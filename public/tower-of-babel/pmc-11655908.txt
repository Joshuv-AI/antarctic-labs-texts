Uncovering key genes and molecular mechanisms of dendritic cell dysfunction in Esophageal Cancer: implications for Novel Diagnostic and therapeutic strategies
Authors: Yanqiang Li, Jing Zeng, Chaoqun Yu, Weiyu Shen, Yong Xi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Dendritic cells play a crucial role in initiating and regulating immune responses, and their dysfunction is strongly associated with esophageal cancer. In this study, we aimed to develop a predictive signature and identify key genes linked to dendritic cell dysfunction in esophageal cancer. Through bioinformatics analysis of gene expression data from the TCGA database, we identified a set of 603 genes significantly associated with dendritic cell function. Further analysis using Cox regression and LASSO regression revealed six genes (GDF15, GPT, KRTAP5-5, MMP12, SLC5A1, and C5orf52) that were strongly correlated with overall survival. The prognostic signature constructed from these genes demonstrated that patients in the high-risk group had poorer survival outcomes compared to those in the low-risk group. Immune infiltration analysis indicated a higher abundance of macrophages in the high-risk group, and correlation studies showed a strong positive association between the risk score and the expression of immune checkpoint markers PD1 and PD-L1. Drug sensitivity analysis suggested that Metformin, Gefitinib, and Lapatinib may be more effective in the low-risk group, while Pyrimethamine, Axitinib, and Rapamycin may be more beneficial for high-risk patients. In summary, we identified a 6-gene signature related to dendritic cell dysfunction that can predict prognosis in esophageal cancer, offering valuable insights for personalized therapeutic strategies.

Introduction

Esophageal cancer is a complex and heterogeneous disease, with various risk factors including environmental, genetic, and lifestyle factors [1, 2]. It is the sixth most prevalent cancer globally and the seventh most frequent cause of cancer mortality [3, 4]. With a five-year survival rate of fewer than 20%, the prognosis for individuals with esophageal cancer remains bleak despite breakthroughs in therapy [5, 6].

The onset and spread of cancer are greatly influenced by the immune system. Dendritic cells in particular are significant antigen-presenting cells that start and control immune responses [7–9]. They are engaged in the detection and destruction of cancer cells and are crucial in the establishment of antitumor immunity. Recent research, however, has shown that dendritic cell malfunction occurs in a number of malignancies, including esophageal cancer, and that this dysfunction is linked to tumor growth and a poor prognosis [10, 11].

Understanding the molecular mechanisms underlying dendritic cell dysfunction in esophageal cancer is crucial for developing novel diagnostic and therapeutic strategies. With the advent of high-throughput sequencing technologies, comprehensive analysis of gene expression patterns has become a powerful tool for identifying critical genes and pathways involved in cancer development and progression. In this study, we employed bioinformatics analysis to explore the relationship between dendritic cells and esophageal cancer, uncovering key genes linked to dendritic cell dysfunction. These findings offer new insights into the molecular basis of dendritic cell dysfunction in esophageal cancer, which could have significant implications for the development of innovative diagnostic and therapeutic approaches for this disease.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition and preprocessing

Esophageal cancer data were obtained from TCGA (The Cancer Genome Atlas) database, including 13 adjacent normal tissue samples and 185 esophageal cancer tissue samples, as the training set. GSE53624 dataset from GEO (Gene Expression Omnibus) database, including 118 control samples and 118 esophageal cancer samples, was used as the validation set.

Single sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA)

To evaluate the relative abundance of various immune cells in the adjacent normal tissue and esophageal cancer tissue samples, ssGSEA was performed using the “GSVA” and “GSEABase” packages in R. Boxplots were generated using the “ggpubr” package.

Differential expression analysis

On the samples with low and high aDC and DCs enrichment scores, differential expression analysis was carried out using the "limma" package in R. |logFC|> 1 and an adjusted p-value of 0.05 were the screening criteria employed. The "ggVolcano" program was used to create the volcano charts. Using Funrich software, Venn diagrams were created based on the overlapping upregulated and downregulated genes discovered by differential expression analysis in order to identify the dendritic cell-related genes in esophageal cancer.

GO and KEGG enrichment analysis

The "clusterProfiler" R package was used to carry out the Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis and the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment study. A corrected p-value of less than 0.05 was used as the criterion for significance. A GO circle plot was created using the "circlize" and "ComplexHeatmap" R packages to display the enriched GO phrases. The "clusterProfiler" R package was used to display the KEGG enriched pathways.

Cox regression analysis

Using the "survival" R package, a univariate Cox regression analysis was carried out to assess the prognostic relevance in terms of overall survival. A p-value of 0.05 or below was used to define statistical significance for genes. With the help of the "survival" and "ggplot2" R programs, forest plots were produced. Genes at risk were highlighted in red, while genes at risk were highlighted in blue.

Correlation network analysis

The expression correlation among the genes with prognostic significance was analyzed using the "igraph" and "psych" R packages. Red lines represented positive correlations, while blue lines represented negative correlations. The nodes in the network were colored to indicate up-regulation (left side of the node in red) or down-regulation (left side of the node in gray), and risk (right side of the node in purple) or protective (right side of the node in green) effects. The node size represented the strength of the correlation.

Identifying survival-related genes using LASSO regression and Random Survival Forest (RSF) analyses

We further screened for survival-related genes using two methods: (1) LASSO regression was performed using the "glmnet" R package, and the optimal lambda value was used to select 12 genes. (2) Random Survival Forest (RSF) analysis was conducted using the "randomForestSRC" R package. Genes were ranked based on their importance, which was determined by the decrease in the Gini coefficient. The top 10 genes were selected. The intersection of genes identified by both methods yielded seven genes.

Developing a prognostic signature and risk score model based on hub genes

In order to create a prognostic signature, we performed a stepwise multivariate Cox regression analysis. To discriminate between high-risk and low-risk groups, the risk scores were computed based on the expression levels and regression coefficients of these hub genes. The validation cohort also showed a substantial difference between high-risk and low-risk groups in overall survival. We utilized the "survival," "survminer," and "timeROC" R packages to produce KM survival curves and ROC curves.

Investigating mutation patterns, co-expression, methylation of the hub genes, and comparing clinical data between high-risk and low-risk groups in esophageal cancer datasets

We explored the mutation patterns of the risk model genes in esophageal cancer-related datasets using the cBioportal online tool. Co-expression analysis of the hub genes was performed using the "corrplot" R package for expression correlation analysis. Methylation data were analyzed using the UALCAN online tool (https://ualcan.path.uab.edu/). Furthermore, we compared various clinical data between the high-risk and low-risk groups, which were generated using the "ggpubr" R package.

Evaluation of immune cell infiltration, immunotherapy potential, and drug sensitivity analysis in high- and low-risk groups

We evaluated immune cell infiltration in both high-risk and low-risk groups using seven different methods available through TIMER2.0 (http://timer.cistrome.org/). The results were visualized using the "pheatmap" package. Correlation analyses between risk scores and the expression levels of PD1 and PD-L1 were conducted, with the correlation plots generated using the "ggstatsplot" and "ggside" packages. Additionally, we used the TIDE online tool (http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu/) to assess sensitivity to immunotherapy, and the "ggpubr" package was employed to create the corresponding boxplots.

Furthermore, we conducted drug sensitivity analysis on the high-risk and low-risk groups utilizing the "pRRophetic" package. The results were visualized with boxplots created using the "ggplot2" package.

Univariate and multivariate cox regression analysis incorporating risk scores and clinical data

We conducted univariate Cox regression analysis by integrating risk scores with clinical factors, including age, gender, T stage, N stage, and pathological stage. Factors with a p-value < 0.05, specifically N stage, pathological stage, and risk score, were selected for multivariate Cox regression modeling. A forest plot was generated to visualize the results. The model's performance was evaluated using ROC curve analysis, and based on this clinical prediction model, patients were stratified into high-risk and low-risk groups. The model's calibration was assessed using a calibration curve.

Subsequently, we validated the model using an independent dataset, where we re-evaluated the ROC curve, survival curve, and calibration curve. The "StepReg" and "regplot" packages were used to create forest plots, the "timeROC" package to plot ROC curves, and the "survival" package to generate survival curves.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses in this study were performed using R language, unless otherwise specified.

Identification of survival-related genes and construction of a prognostic signature

The final decision was made to employ six genes, including GDF15, GPT, KRTAP5-5, MMP12, SLC5A1, and C5orf52, to generate a risk score. Based on the risk score, the high-risk and low-risk groups were distinguished, and a significant difference in overall survival was found between the two groups (Fig. 3E). In the validation set, this finding was verified (Fig. 3F). The gene risk model's ability to predict survival at 1, 2, and 3 years was assessed using ROC curves. Discussion

In this study, we found 603 genes linked to dendritic cells in esophageal cancer and then used a gene-by-gene screening process to identify 6 genes—GDF15, GPT, KRTAP5-5, MMP12, SLC5A1, and C5orf52—that might be used to create a predictive profile for esophageal cancer patients. Promising results in risk stratification and individualized therapy of esophageal cancer patients were seen using the prognostic signature in both the training and validation sets.

When it comes to cancer, the immune system is where the action is [12–14]. Adaptive immune responses may be kicked off by activating T cells, which dendritic cells are capable of doing [7, 9]. Multiple malignancies, including esophageal cancer, have been linked to dendritic cell dysregulation [15, 16]. These results highlight the importance of processes involving immune chemotaxis, cytokines, and chemokines in the regulation of dendritic cell function and the immune response in esophageal cancer, where we identified 603 genes associated with dendritic cells.

Fifteen genes with prognostic relevance were found using Cox regression analysis, and six of them were chosen to create a prognostic signature. Protective genes were GDF15, GPT, and SLC5A1, whereas risk genes included KRTAP5-5, MMP12, and C5orf52. Previous research has revealed the genes' biological roles. The TGF-beta superfamily member growth differentiation factor 15 (GDF15) has been linked to inhibiting tumor development and spread in many different types of cancer, including esophageal cancer [17, 18]. Catalyzing the transfer of an amino group from alanine to alpha-ketoglutarate to create pyruvate and glutamate, GPT is also known as alanine aminotransferase 2 (ALT2). Hepatocellular carcinoma patients whose GPT expression was shown to be overexpressed had a higher rate of survival [19, 20]. Glucose and other carbohydrates are absorbed in the small intestine through the sodium-glucose cotransporter SLC5A1. Low survival rates among individuals with stomach cancer have been linked to its expression [21–23]. Matrix metalloproteinase 12 (MMP12) is an extracellular matrix-degrading enzyme that has been linked to tumor invasion and metastasis in many malignancies, including esophageal cancer [24]. Overexpression of the keratin-associated protein KRTAP5-5 in esophageal cancer has been linked to a worse prognosis [24]. C5orf52, commonly known as CDK14, is a cyclin-dependent kinase that has been linked to the development and spread of malignancies other than esophageal cancer [25, 26].

By comparing the clinical histories of high-risk and low-risk patients, we further validated the predictive model. Significant differences were observed in T stage, N stage, pathological stage, histological grade, histological type, and the incidence of new tumor events between the two groups, underscoring a strong correlation between clinical parameters and the risk model. Patients categorized as high-risk, who typically have a poorer prognosis, may require more aggressive treatment, suggesting that this prognostic signature could be valuable in identifying those in need of intensive therapy.

The analysis of immune infiltration revealed a higher presence of macrophages in the high-risk group, potentially due to direct interactions between dendritic cells and macrophages. Additionally, there was a significant positive correlation between the risk score and the expression levels of PD1 and PD-L1, indicating that high-risk patients may exhibit elevated levels of these immune checkpoint molecules. The lower TIDE score observed in the high-risk group suggests a potentially greater responsiveness to immune checkpoint inhibitors. Therefore, further research is warranted to explore the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors in treating esophageal cancer in high-risk individuals.

Furthermore, we performed drug sensitivity analysis to investigate the potential therapeutic options for different risk groups. Our results showed that the low-risk group was more sensitive to Metformin, Gefitinib, and Lapatinib, while the high-risk group was more sensitive to Pyimethamine, Adxitinib, and Rapamycin. These findings provide valuable information for selecting appropriate therapeutic options based on the patient's risk group.

Comparing our results with previous studies, we found that several of the identified genes in our risk model have been previously reported to be associated with esophageal cancer. For example, GDF15 has been shown to promote the proliferation and invasion of esophageal cancer cells [17, 18], while SLC5A1 has been suggested to play a role in glucose metabolism and contribute to the progression of esophageal cancer [27]. Moreover, MMP12 has been reported to be upregulated in esophageal cancer and promote cell migration and invasion [24], and KRTAP5-5 has been suggested to be a potential diagnostic biomarker for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma [28–30].

Limitations

However, our study also has some limitations. Firstly, the data used in our study were obtained from publicly available databases, and the results need to be validated in independent patient cohorts. Secondly, our study only focused on the role of dendritic cells in esophageal cancer and did not consider other immune cells or their interactions. Finally, although we conducted drug sensitivity analysis, further experimental studies are needed to verify the therapeutic potential of the identified drugs in different risk groups.

In conclusion, our research uncovered a risk model for patient survival that relied on six genes linked to dendritic cell dysfunction. Our results provide light on the possible processes underpinning dendritic cells' function in esophageal cancer and may aid in the creation of individualized treatment plans for people with the disease.

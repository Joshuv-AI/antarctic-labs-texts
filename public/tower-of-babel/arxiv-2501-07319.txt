Introduction

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the standard graded polynomial ring over a field
K
, and let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal. The asymptotic behavior of the powers of
I
has been a central theme in Commutative Algebra for several decades, motivated by foundational questions posed by David Rees in the 1960s. In the 1970s, a number of significant contributions addressed the structure and properties of the set of associated primes
\textup{Ass}\,I^{k}
of the powers of
I
. A major breakthrough came in 1979 when Brodmann
[
8
]
established that for any ideal
I
in a Noetherian ring
R
, the sets of associated primes stabilize, that is,
\textup{Ass}\,I^{k}=\textup{Ass}\,I^{k+1}
for all
k\gg 0
. This result, now a cornerstone in the field, led to the definition of the stable set of associated primes, denoted by
\textup{Ass}^{\infty}I
. Shortly thereafter, Brodmann also demonstrated that the depth of the powers
\textup{depth}\,R/I^{k}
eventually stabilizes
[
9
]
.

Following these developments, the asymptotic linearity of other invariants of powers of ideals has attracted extensive interest. In 1999, Kodiyalam
[
37
]
and, independently, Cutkosky, Herzog, and Trung
[
13
]
proved that the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity
\textup{reg}\,I^{k}
behaves as a linear function for all
k\gg 0
. More recently, Conca
[
10
]
and, independently, Ficarra and Sgroi
[
23
]
, established that the
v
-number
\textup{v}(I^{k})
of the powers of
I
also exhibits eventual linear behavior (see also
[
7
,
10
,
17
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
). The Noetherian property of the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(I)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}I^{k}
, viewed as a bigraded ring, provides a structural explanation for this asymptotic linearity.

A major open challenge in this area is to describe explicitly the stable set of associated primes
\textup{Ass}^{\infty}I
, as well as the eventual linear functions
\textup{reg}\,I^{k}
and
\textup{v}(I^{k})
, for broad classes of ideals arising from combinatorial structures. This paper contributes to this broader effort by investigating a specific and well-studied family of monomial ideals: edge ideals of finite simple graphs.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph on the vertex set
V(G)=[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
and with edge set
E(G)
. The edge ideal of
G
is the monomial ideal
I(G)\subset S
generated by the monomials
x_{i}x_{j}
for each edge
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
. The study of the syzygies and homological properties of powers of edge ideals has a rich history in Commutative Algebra, with connections to combinatorial topology and algebraic geometry; see for instance
[
2
,
3
,
18
,
28
,
32
,
38
,
42
]
. Unlike general monomial ideals, edge ideals often exhibit more structured and predictable behavior. For instance, it is known that there exist monomial ideals
I\subset S
for which the behavior of
\textup{Ass}\,I^{k}
can be arbitrarily complicated
[
30
, Corollary 4.2]
. By contrast, Martínez-Bernal, Morey, and Villarreal
[
40
]
proved that for any edge ideal
I(G)
, the sequence of associated primes satisfies
\textup{Ass}\,I(G)^{k}\subseteq\textup{Ass}\,I(G)^{k+1}
for all
k\geq 1
. Additionally, while a monomial ideal
I\subset S
with a linear resolution does not guarantee that
I^{k}
has a linear resolution for all
k\geq 2
, Herzog, Hibi, and Zheng
[
32
]
showed that if
I(G)
has a linear resolution, then
I(G)^{k}
also has a linear resolution for every
k\geq 1
.

Building on these insights, our previous work
[
22
]
introduced a new approach to studying the syzygies of powers of monomial ideals via the lens of homological shift ideals. Given a monomial ideal
I\subset S
, let
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, and define
{\bf x^{a}}=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}
. Following
[
33
]
, the
i
th
homological shift ideal
of
I
is

\textup{HS}_{i}(I)\ =\ ({\bf x^{a}}\ :\ \textup{Tor}_{i}^{S}(K,I)_{\bf a}\neq 0).

Homological shift ideals have been studied extensively in recent literature
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
11
,
12
,
16
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
33
,
34
,
39
,
44
]
and encode critical multigraded data from the minimal free resolution of
I
. Motivated by conjectures from
[
11
]
and
[
33
]
, we introduced in
[
22
]
the
i
th
homological shift algebra
of
I
as the
K
-algebra

\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))=\bigoplus_{k\geq 1}\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}).

These modules generalize the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(I)
(since
S\oplus\textup{HS}_{0}(\mathcal{R}(I))=\mathcal{R}(I)
) and provide a higher homological framework for understanding asymptotic behaviors of syzygies. In
[
22
]
, we proved that if
I
has linear powers, that is, if all powers
I^{k}
have linear resolution, then
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))
is finitely generated over
\mathcal{R}(I)
, leading to the fact that that many invariants of
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
, such as depth, associated primes, regularity and the
v
-number behave asymptotically well.

Our goal in this paper is to advance the understanding of the asymptotic behavior of syzygies of powers of edge ideals through the framework of homological shift ideals. Below, we summarize the main lines of investigation:

(a)

We prove that the algebra
\textup{HS}_{1}(\mathcal{R}(I(G)))
is a
\mathcal{R}(I(G))
-module by showing that
\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k+1})=I(G)\cdot\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
.

(b)

We explore the sets of associated primes of
\textup{HS}_{i}(I(G)^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. Our goal is to describe the behavior of the sets
\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I(G)^{k})
. To achieve this, we formulate Conjecture
A
and we prove it in several significant cases.

(c)

We examine the linearity of the minimal free resolution of
\textup{HS}_{i}(I(G)^{k})
for
k\gg 0
, assuming that
I(G)
itself has a linear resolution. In support of this, we propose Conjecture
B
and prove several supporting results.

In Section
2
, we study the first homological shift algebra of an edge ideal
I(G)
. Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. By
[
22
, Theorem 1.2]
, we have
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})\subseteq I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
. So, it is natural to inquire whether equality holds. In general,
I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
can be much larger than
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})
. Indeed, there exist monomial ideals
I
such that
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})\neq I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for every
k\geq 1

[
22
, Example 1.1]
. Remarkably, we demonstrate in Corollary
2.4
that
\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k+1})=I(G)\cdot\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. This result stems from Theorem
2.1
, where we explicitly describe
\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k})
for every
k
. We show that
\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k})
decomposes as the sum of a “linear part”
\textup{Lin}_{1}(I(G)^{k})
and a “non-linear part”
I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}
. Hence, we deduce that
\textup{HS}_{1}(\mathcal{R}(I(G)))
is a
\mathcal{R}(I(G))
-module. Moreover, Theorem
2.5
establishes that
I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}:I(G)=I(G)^{\langle k\rangle}
for all
k\geq 2
.

In Section
3
, we turn to the behavior of the sets
\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I(G)^{k})
as
k
varies. Based on extensive computations, we conjecture:

Conjecture A
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph. Then, for all
i\geq 0
,

\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I(G)^{\max(1,i)})\,\subseteq\,\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I(G)^{i+1})\,\subseteq\,\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I(G)^{i+2})\,\subseteq\,\cdots.

This result is well-known for
i=0
, first proven by Martínez-Bernal, Morey, and Villarreal
[
40
]
. In Theorem
3.6
, we prove that Conjecture
A
holds for
i=1
. To this end, inspired by
[
35
]
, we introduce the notion of the
i
th
homological strong persistence property
. In Theorem
3.2
, we show that a monomial ideal
I
has the
i
th homological strong persistence property if and only if
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1}):I=\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. Applying results from Section
2
, we establish this property for
i=1
and
I=I(G)
, confirming Conjecture
A
for
i=1
. Furthermore, we verified this conjecture computationally for all graphs with up to seven vertices, utilizing the
Macaulay2

[
27
]
packages
HomologicalShiftIdeals

[
19
]
and
NautyGraphs

[
41
]
.

Finally, in Section
4
we turn to the linearity of powers of
I(G)
. We propose:

Conjecture B
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph such that
I(G)
has linear resolution. Then,
\textup{HS}_{i}(I(G)^{k})
has linear resolution for all
i\geq 0
and all
k\gg 0
.

For
i=0
, this result follows from a theorem of Herzog, Hibi, and Zheng
[
32
]
. Using some results from
[
21
]
, we prove in Theorem
4.2
that Conjecture
B
holds for
i=1
. Additionally, we establish Conjecture
B
for principal Borel edge ideals, initial lexsegment edge ideals, and edge ideals of complete multipartite graphs.

1.
A quick review on the homological shift algebra

In this section, we summarize some of the main results from
[
22
]
.

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}(I)
be an associated prime. Recall that the
regularity
of
I
is defined as
\textup{reg}\,I=\max\{j:\ \textup{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,I)_{i+j}\neq 0\}
, the
\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-number
of
I
is defined as
\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)=\min\{\deg(f):\ f\in S_{d},\ (I:f)=\mathfrak{p}\}
, and the
v
-number
of
I
is defined as
\textup{v}(I)=\min\limits_{\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}(I)}\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
.

Now, let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal and denote by
\mathcal{G}(I)
its minimal monomial generating set. For systematic reasons, we put
I^{0}=S
.

The
i
th
homological shift algebra
of
I
is introduced in
[
22
]
as the
K
-algebra,

\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))\ =\ \bigoplus_{k\geq 1}\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}).

Notice that
S\oplus\textup{HS}_{0}(\mathcal{R}(I))
is just the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(I)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}I^{k}
of
I
.

For a monomial ideal
I\subset S
, in general it is not possible to structure
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))
as a
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module, see
[
22
, Example 1.1]
. In fact,
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))
is a
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module if and only if the inclusion
I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\subseteq\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})
holds for all
k\geq 1
.

A monomial ideal
I\subset S
has
linear powers
if
I^{k}
has linear resolution for all
k\geq 1
. When
I
has linear powers, we showed in
[
22
, Theorem 1.4]
that the inclusion
I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\subseteq\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})
holds for all
k\geq 1
, and also that
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))
is a finitely generated graded
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module.

The next results were proved in
[
22
, Theorem 1.4]
and
[
22
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal with linear powers. Then
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))
is a finitely generated graded
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module. Hence, we have
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})=I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
. Furthermore, the following statements hold.

(a)

The set
\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
stabilizes:
\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})=\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
.

(b)

For all
k\gg 0
, we have
\textup{depth}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})=\textup{depth}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
.

(c)

For all
k\gg 0
,
\textup{reg}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
is a linear function in
k
.

(d)

For all
k\gg 0
,
\textup{v}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))
is a linear function in
k
.

(e)

For all
k\gg 0
and all
\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
,
\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))
is a linear function in
k
.

2.
On the structure of
\textup{HS}_{1}(\mathcal{R}(I(G)))

Let
G
be a finite simple graph on the vertex set
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
, with edge set
E(G)
. The
edge ideal
of
G
is the monomial ideal
I(G)\subset S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
generated by the monomials
x_{i}x_{j}\in S
for which
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
is an edge of
G
.

Our goal is to describe the first homological shift algebra of an edge ideal. It will turn out that
\textup{HS}_{1}(\mathcal{R}(I(G)))
is a
\mathcal{R}(I(G))
-module generated in degree one.

For a monomial ideal
J\subset S
, we denote by
J_{\langle d\rangle}
the ideal generated by all monomials of degree
d
belonging to
J
. Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal generated in degree
d
, we define the
i
th
homological linear part
of
I
to be the ideal,

\textup{Lin}_{i}(I)\ =\ \textup{HS}_{i}(I)_{\langle d+i\rangle}.

Given a monomial ideal
I\subset S
with
\mathcal{G}(I)=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\}
and an integer
k\geq 2
, we define the
k
th
non-pure power
of
I
to be the ideal,

I^{\langle k\rangle}\ =\ (u_{i_{1}}\cdots u_{i_{k}}\ :\ 1\leq i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{k}\leq m,\ |\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}|>1).

By convention, we put
I^{\langle 1\rangle}=I
. Notice that
I^{\langle k\rangle}\subset I^{k}
for all
k
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph. For all
k\geq 1
, we have

\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k})\ =\ \textup{Lin}_{1}(I(G)^{k})+I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}.

To prove Theorem
2.1
, we first need the following lemma. Given two monomials
u,v\in S
, we put
u:v=\textup{lcm}(u,v)/v
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal with
\mathcal{G}(I)=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\}
. Then,

\textup{HS}_{1}(I)\ =\ (\textup{lcm}(u_{i},u_{j})\ :\ 1\leq i<j\leq m).

Proof.

Let
F_{0}=S^{m}
be the multigraded free
S
-module with basis
{\bf e}_{1},\dots,{\bf e}_{m}
and with
\textup{Deg}({\bf e}_{i})=\textup{Deg}(u_{i})
for all
i
. Here, by
\textup{Deg}(u)=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
we denote the multidegree of a monomial
u=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}
. Let
\varphi:F_{0}\rightarrow I
be the multigraded map defined by
\varphi({\bf e}_{i})=u_{i}
for all
i
. The kernel
\textup{Ker}(\varphi)
is generated by the relations
{\bf r}_{ij}=(u_{i}:u_{j}){\bf e}_{j}-(u_{j}:u_{i}){\bf e}_{i}
for
1\leq i<j\leq m
, and
\textup{Deg}({\bf r}_{ij})=\textup{Deg}(\textup{lcm}(u_{i},u_{j}))
. Let
\{{\bf r}_{i_{1}j_{1}},\dots,{\bf r}_{i_{\ell}j_{\ell}}\}
be a minimal generating set of
\textup{Ker}(\varphi)
, and let
F_{1}=S^{\ell}
be the multigraded free
S
-module with basis
{\bf f}_{1},\dots,{\bf f}_{\ell}
with
\textup{Deg}({\bf f}_{p})=\textup{Deg}({\bf r}_{i_{p}j_{p}})
for all
p
. Let
\psi:F_{1}\rightarrow F_{0}
be the multigraded map defined by
\psi({\bf f}_{p})={\bf r}_{i_{p}j_{p}}
for all
p
. Then,

F_{1}\xrightarrow{\psi}F_{0}\xrightarrow{\varphi}I\rightarrow 0

is the beginning of the minimal multigraded free resolution of
I
. It follows that

\textup{HS}_{1}(I)\ =\ (\textup{lcm}(u_{i_{1}},u_{j_{1}}),\dots,\textup{lcm}(u_{i_{\ell}},u_{j_{\ell}}))\ \subseteq\ (\textup{lcm}(u_{i},u_{j})\ :\ 1\leq i<j\leq m).

To show the opposite inclusion, let
i<j
. If
\{i,j\}=\{i_{p},j_{p}\}
for some
p
, we have
\textup{lcm}(u_{i},u_{j})\in\textup{HS}_{1}(I)
. Suppose now that
{\bf r}_{ij}
is not a minimal generator of
\textup{Ker}(\varphi)
. Then, there exist monomials
w_{1},\dots,w_{\ell}\in S
and coefficients
k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell}\in K
such that

{\bf r}_{ij}=\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}k_{p}w_{p}{\bf r}_{i_{p}j_{p}}
, and
\textup{Deg}({\bf r}_{ij})=\textup{Deg}(w_{p}{\bf r}_{i_{p}j_{p}})
for all
p
with
k_{p}\neq 0
. We have
k_{q}\neq 0
for some
q
. Hence
\textup{lcm}(u_{i},u_{j})=w_{q}\textup{lcm}(u_{i_{q}},u_{j_{q}})\in\textup{HS}_{1}(I)
.

∎

Next, we describe the first homological linear part of the powers of an edge ideal.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph. Then, for all
k\geq 1
,

\textup{Lin}_{1}(I(G)^{k})=(e_{1}\cdots e_{k}x_{p}:\ \textstyle e_{i}\in\mathcal{G}(I(G)),\ x_{p}(e_{1}/x_{q})\in I(G)\ \textit{with}\ p\neq q).

(1)

In particular,
\textup{Lin}_{1}(I(G)^{k+1})=I(G)\cdot\textup{Lin}_{1}(I(G)^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

By (
1
), it follows immediately that
\textup{Lin}_{1}(I(G)^{k+1})=I(G)\cdot\textup{Lin}_{1}(I(G)^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. So, it remains to prove equation (
1
). Since
I(G)^{k}
is equigenerated in degree
2k
, the ideal
\textup{Lin}_{1}(I(G)^{k})
is generated by the least common multiples
\textup{lcm}(u,v)
of degree
2k+1
, where
u=e_{1}\cdots e_{k}
,
v=f_{1}\cdots f_{k}
, and
e_{i},f_{i}\in\mathcal{G}(I(G))
for all
i
. Since
\deg\textup{lcm}(u,v)=2k+1
, we have
\textup{lcm}(u,v)=ux_{p}=vx_{t}
for some
p\neq t
.

Now, we prove the statement by induction on
k
. For
k=1
, we have
u=e_{1}=x_{i}x_{j}
and
v=f_{1}=x_{r}x_{s}
with
u\neq v
. Since
x_{p}e_{1}=x_{t}f_{1}
and
x_{p}\neq x_{t}
, we may assume that
x_{p}=x_{r}
and
x_{t}=x_{j}
. Then
x_{i}=x_{s}
and so
x_{p}(e_{1}/x_{t})=f_{1}\in I(G)
, as desired.

For the inductive step, we may assume that
e_{i}\neq f_{j}
for all
i
and
j
. Indeed, up to a relabeling, suppose that
e_{k}=f_{k}
, and consider the monomials
u^{\prime}=u/e_{k}
,
v^{\prime}=v/f_{k}
. Then
u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(I(G)^{k-1})
,
\textup{lcm}(u,v)=\textup{lcm}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})e_{k}
, and
\deg\textup{lcm}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})=2(k-1)+1
. By induction, we have
\textup{lcm}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})=e_{1}\cdots e_{k-1}x_{p}
and
x_{p}(e_{1}/x_{q})\in I(G)
for some
p\neq q
. Then
\textup{lcm}(u,v)=e_{1}\cdots e_{k}x_{p}
is as described in equation (
1
).

Let
e_{i}\neq f_{j}
for all
i
and
j
. Since
ux_{p}=vx_{t}
, we have
v=x_{p}(u/x_{t})=f_{1}\cdots f_{k}
. Hence
x_{p}
divides some
f_{j}
, say
f_{1}
. Then
f_{1}=x_{p}x_{s}
for some
s\neq p
. Since
x_{s}
divides
x_{p}(u/x_{t})
, it follows that
x_{s}
divides some
e_{i}
, say for
i=1
. Let
e_{1}=x_{s}x_{q}
. Since
e_{1}\neq f_{1}
, we have
p\neq q
and
f_{1}=x_{p}(e_{1}/x_{q})\in I(G)
, as desired.

∎

We are now in the position to prove Theorem
2.1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Note that
\textup{Lin}_{1}(I(G)^{k})\subseteq\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k})
by definition. We claim that
I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}\subseteq\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k})
. Indeed, let
w=e_{1}\cdots e_{k+1}\in\mathcal{G}(I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle})
with
e_{i}\in\mathcal{G}(I(G))
for all
i
, and
e_{1}\neq e_{2}
. Let
u=e_{1}\cdot e_{3}\cdots e_{k+1}
and
v=e_{2}\cdot e_{3}\cdots e_{k+1}
. Then
u,v\in\mathcal{G}(I(G)^{k})
, and so
\textup{lcm}(u,v)=\textup{lcm}(e_{1},e_{2})\cdot e_{3}\cdots e_{k+1}\in\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k})
by Lemma
2.2
. Since
\textup{lcm}(u,v)
divides
w
, it follows that
w\in\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k})
. Hence

\textup{Lin}_{1}(I(G)^{k})+I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}\ \subseteq\ \textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k}).

For the opposite inclusion, recall that by Lemma
2.2
,
\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k})
is generated by the least common multiples
\textup{lcm}(u,v)
with
u,v\in\mathcal{G}(I(G)^{k})
and
u\neq v
. Let
w=\textup{lcm}(u,v)\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k}))
be such a minimal generator. Since
I(G)^{k}
is equigenerated in degree
2k
, we have
\deg(w)\geq 2k+1
. If
\deg(w)=2k+1
, we have
w\in\textup{Lin}_{1}(I(G)^{k})
. Suppose now
\deg(w)\geq 2k+2
. Proceeding by induction on
k
, we will show that
w\in I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}
and this will finish the proof. We can write
w=fu=gv
where
f=v:u
and
g=u:v
. Then
\deg(f)=\deg(g)\geq 2
. Let
u=e_{1}\cdots e_{k}
and
v=e_{1}^{\prime}\cdots e_{k}^{\prime}
, with
e_{i},e_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(I(G))
for all
i
. Let
f=x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{d}}
. Since
fu=gv
and
\gcd(f,g)=1
, we have that
x_{i_{1}}
and
x_{i_{2}}
divide
v
.

Suppose first
k=1
. Since
\deg(w)\geq 4
, it follows that
w=uv
and
\gcd(u,v)=1
. Therefore
u=e_{1}\neq e_{1}^{\prime}=v
and so
w\in I(G)^{\langle 2\rangle}
, as desired.

Now, suppose
k>1
. We may assume that
e_{i}\neq f_{j}
for all
i
and
j
. Indeed, suppose that
e_{k}=f_{k}
, and consider the monomials
u^{\prime}=u/e_{k}
,
v^{\prime}=v/f_{k}
. Then
u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(I(G)^{k-1})
,
\textup{lcm}(u,v)=\textup{lcm}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})e_{k}
, and
\deg\textup{lcm}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\geq 2k
. We claim that
\textup{lcm}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k-1}))
. Otherwise, there exist
u^{\prime\prime},v^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(I(G)^{k-1})
such that
w^{\prime\prime}=\textup{lcm}(u^{\prime\prime},v^{\prime\prime})\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k-1}))
strictly divides
w^{\prime}=\textup{lcm}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})
. Then
u^{\prime\prime}e_{k},v^{\prime\prime}e_{k}\in\mathcal{G}(I(G)^{k})
, and
\textup{lcm}(u^{\prime\prime}e_{k},v^{\prime\prime}e_{k})=w^{\prime\prime}e_{k}
strictly divides
w^{\prime}e_{k}=w
. This is absurd because
w\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k}))
. Hence
w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k-1}))
. Since
\deg(w^{\prime})\geq 2(k-1)+2
, it follows by induction that
w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(I(G)^{\langle k\rangle})
. Finally, we have
w=w^{\prime}e_{k}\in\mathcal{G}(I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle})
, as desired.

Now, assume that
e_{i}\neq f_{j}
for all
i
and
j
. We distinguish the two possible cases.

Case 1.
Suppose that
e_{s}^{\prime}=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}
for some
s
. Let
w^{\prime}=ue_{s}^{\prime}=e_{1}\cdots e_{k}e_{s}^{\prime}
. We claim that
w^{\prime}\in I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}
. Otherwise
e_{1}=\dots=e_{k}=e_{s}^{\prime}=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}
, and then
(x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}})^{k+1}
divides
fu=gv
. This is not possible. Indeed, since
\gcd(f,g)=1
and
x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}
divides
f
, then
(x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}})^{k+1}
should divide
v
, which is not possible because
\deg(v)=2k
while
\deg((x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}})^{k+1})=2(k+1)
. Hence
w^{\prime}\in I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}
, and so
w\in I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}
.

Case 2.
Suppose that
x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\neq e_{s}^{\prime}
for all
s
. Up to a relabeling, we may assume that
e_{1}^{\prime}=x_{i_{1}}x_{j_{1}}
and
e_{2}^{\prime}=x_{i_{2}}x_{j_{2}}
. We have
j_{1}\neq i_{2}
, otherwise
e_{1}^{\prime}=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}
against the assumption. Similarly
j_{2}\neq i_{1}
. If
x_{j_{1}}
divides
f
, then
x_{i_{1}}x_{j_{1}}=e_{1}^{\prime}
divides
f
and we can apply the first case. We can proceed similarly if
x_{j_{2}}
divides
f
. Hence, we may assume that
x_{j_{1}}
and
x_{j_{2}}
do not divide
f
. Then
x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}
divides
u
. If
x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}=e_{s}
for some
s
, then
x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}u=e_{1}^{\prime}e_{2}^{\prime}(e_{1}\cdots e_{s-1}e_{s+1}\cdots e_{k})\in I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}
because
e_{1}^{\prime}\neq e_{2}^{\prime}
. Then
w\in I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}
. Suppose now, up to a relabeling, that
e_{1}=x_{j_{1}}x_{\ell_{1}}
and
e_{2}=x_{j_{2}}x_{\ell_{2}}
. Since
e_{1}\neq e_{1}^{\prime}
and
e_{2}\neq e_{2}^{\prime}
by our assumption, we obtain that
\ell_{1}\neq i_{1}
and
\ell_{2}\neq i_{2}
. Then, we have
e_{1}^{\prime}=x_{i_{1}}(e_{1}/x_{\ell_{1}})\neq e_{1}
. Hence
ux_{i_{1}}\in\textup{Lin}_{1}(I(G)^{k})
divides
fu=w
against the fact that
w\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k}))
. This concludes the proof.

∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph. For all
k\geq 1
,

\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k+1})=I(G)\cdot\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k}).

In particular,
\textup{HS}_{1}(\mathcal{R}(I(G)))
is a
\mathcal{R}(I(G))
-module generated in degree one.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.3
, we have
\textup{Lin}_{1}(I(G)^{k+1})=I(G)\cdot\textup{Lin}_{1}(I(G)^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. Moreover, it is clear that
I(G)^{\langle k+2\rangle}=I(G)\cdot I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}
for all
k\geq 1
. Therefore, applying Theorem
2.1
, for all
k\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k+1})\

\displaystyle=\ \textup{Lin}_{1}(I(G)^{k+1})+I(G)^{\langle k+2\rangle}

\displaystyle=\ I(G)\cdot\textup{Lin}_{1}(I(G)^{k})+I(G)\cdot I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}\ =\ I(G)\cdot\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G)^{k}),

as desired. Since this equality holds for all
k\geq 1
, it follows that
\textup{HS}_{1}(\mathcal{R}(I(G)))
is a finitely generated bigraded
\mathcal{R}(I(G))
-module, which is generated by its first degree component
\textup{HS}_{1}(I(G))
.

∎

It is an open question whether we have
\textup{HS}_{i}(I(G)^{k+1})=I(G)\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I(G)^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
and all graphs
G
, when
i>1
. Moreover, for
i>1
, it is not clear whether
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I(G)))
is a
\mathcal{R}(I(G))
-module.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph. Then, for all
k\geq 2
,

I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}:I(G)\ =\ I(G)^{\langle k\rangle}.

Proof.

It is clear that
I(G)^{\langle k\rangle}\subseteq I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}:I(G)
for all
k\geq 2
. Conversely, let
u\in I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}:I(G)
be a monomial. Since we have
I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}\subset I(G)^{k+1}
, we obtain
u\in I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}:I(G)\subseteq I(G)^{k+1}:I(G)=I(G)^{k}
,
where the last equality follows from
[
40
, Lemma 2.12]
. Hence
u=e_{1}\cdots e_{k}f
where
f\in S
is a monomial and
e_{i}\in\mathcal{G}(I(G))
for all
i
. If
e_{p}\neq e_{q}
for some
1\leq p<q\leq k
, then
e_{1}\cdots e_{k}\in I(G)^{\langle k\rangle}
and so
u\in I(G)^{\langle k\rangle}
, as required. Suppose
e_{1}=\dots=e_{k}
. Then
u=e^{k}f
where
e=e_{1}
. If
f
is divisible by some
v\in\mathcal{G}(I(G))
with
v\neq e
, then
u\in I(G)^{\langle k\rangle}
. Therefore, we may assume that
u=e^{\ell}g
with
\ell\geq k
and
g\notin I(G)
. Since
u\in I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}:I(G)
, we have
ue=e^{\ell+1}g\in I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}
. Hence

ue\ =\ e^{\ell+1}g\ =\ f_{1}\cdots f_{k+1}h,

(2)

where
f_{1}\cdots f_{k+1}\in I(G)^{\langle k+1\rangle}
,
f_{i}\in\mathcal{G}(I(G))
for all
i
,
f_{1}\neq f_{2}
, and
h\in S
. Since
f_{1}\neq f_{2}
, at least one between
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
is different from
e
, say
f_{1}\neq e
. Let
f_{1}=x_{p}x_{q}
. Since
f_{1}\neq e
, we may assume that
x_{p}
does not divide
e
. From (
2
) it follows that
x_{p}
divides
g
. Since
g\notin I(G)
, it follows that
x_{q}
divides
e
. Let
e=x_{q}x_{r}
with
r\neq p
. Hence
u=e^{\ell-1}f_{1}h
with
h=x_{r}(g/x_{p})
. Since
\ell-1\geq k-1\geq 1
and
e\neq f_{1}
, it follows that
e^{\ell-1}f_{1}\in I(G)^{\langle k\rangle}
, and so
u\in I(G)^{\langle k\rangle}
, as desired.

∎

We remark that for
k=1
, it is not true that
I(G)^{\langle 2\rangle}:I(G)=I(G)
. Indeed, it is clear that
e^{2}\notin I(G)^{\langle 2\rangle}
for all
e\in\mathcal{G}(I(G))
. It is unclear whether the equality
I^{\langle k+1\rangle}:I=I^{\langle k\rangle}
holds for all
k\gg 0
and all monomial ideals
I\subset S
.

3.
The homological persistence properties

In this section, our goal is to understand the behavior of the sets
\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I(G)^{k})
for a fixed
i
, as
k
varies. For this aim, we introduce the so-called
homological persistence properties
, which we define below.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. By
V(I)
we denote the set of prime ideals of
S
containing
I
. Let
P\subset S
be a monomial prime ideal,
S(P)
be the polynomial ring in the variables which generate
P
and
S_{P}
be the localization of
S
with respect to
P
. We recall that the
monomial localization
of
I
is the monomial ideal
I(P)
of
S(P)
obtained from
I
by applying the substitutions
x_{i}\mapsto 1
for
x_{i}\notin P
. Note that
I(P)
is a monomial ideal in
S(P)
and
I(P)S_{P}=IS_{P}
.

For later use, we notice some basic facts. Let
I,J,L\subset S
be monomial ideals and let
P\subset S
be a monomial prime ideal. Then
I:J
is again a monomial ideal and
(I:J)(P)=I(P):J(P)
. Suppose that
L\subseteq I:J
and consider the
S
-module
M=(I:J)/L
. Then
M
is multigraded, and we may replace the localization
M_{P}
with the
S(P)
-module
N=(I:J)(P)/L(P)=(I(P):J(P))/L(P)
, preserving the multigraded structure. Furthermore,
P\in\textup{Ass}_{S}I
if and only if
P\in\textup{Ass}_{S(P)}I(P)
. We will use freely these facts. When there is no risk of confusion about the ambient ring, we will simply write
\textup{Ass}\,I
instead of
\textup{Ass}_{S}I
. Therefore, for monomial ideals, one can replace ordinary localization with monomial localization, with the advantage of preserving the multigraded structure.

Inspired by
[
35
]
, we introduce the following concepts which generalize the persistence and strong persistence properties. Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. We say that
I
satisfies the
i
th homological persistence property
if

\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I)\subseteq\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{2})\subseteq\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{3})\subseteq\cdots.

Whereas, we say that
I
satisfies the
i
th homological strong persistence property
if for all monomial prime ideals
P\in V(I)
, all integers
k>0
, and any monomial
f\in(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}):P)(P)\setminus\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})(P)
, there exists a monomial
g\in I(P)
such that
g\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})(P)\subseteq\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})(P)
and
fg\notin\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})(P)
.

Notice that in the previous definition, the condition
g\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})(P)\subseteq\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})(P)
is automatically satisfied if
i=0
. Hence, when
i=0
, the above properties are just the persistence and the strong persistence property introduced in
[
35
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The
i
th homological strong persistence property implies the
i
th homological persistence property.

Proof.

Let
P\in\textup{Ass}_{S}\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
. Then
P\in\textup{Ass}_{S(P)}\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})(P)
and
P
is the maximal ideal of the ring
S(P)
. Therefore, there exists a monomial
f\in S(P)
such that
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})(P):f=P
. Hence
f\in(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}):P)(P)\setminus\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})(P)
. By assumption, we can find a monomial
g\in I(P)
such that
g\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})(P)\subseteq\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})(P)
and
fg\notin\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})(P)
. Hence
P\subseteq\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})(P):fg
. Since
fg\notin\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})(P)
and
P
is the maximal ideal of
S(P)
, we conclude that
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})(P):fg=P
. This shows that
P\in\textup{Ass}_{S(P)}\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})(P)
, and so
P\in\textup{Ass}_{S}\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})
.

∎

The next result generalizes
[
35
, Theorem 1.3]
, and characterizes algebraically the homological strong persistence properties.

Theorem 3.2
.

A monomial ideal
I\subset S
satisfies the
i
th homological strong persistence property if and only if
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1}):I=\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Assume that
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1}):I=\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. Let
P\in V(I)
be a monomial prime ideal. Then
(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1}):I)(P)=\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})(P)
for all
k\geq 1
. Let
f\in(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}):P)(P)\setminus\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})(P)
be a monomial. Suppose by contradiction that for all
g\in I(P)
either
g\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})(P)\not\subseteq\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})(P)
or
fg\in\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})(P)
. The first condition never occurs because
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})(P):I(P)=\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})(P)
for all
k
. Hence
fg\in\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})(P)
for all
g\in I(P)
. Thus
f\in\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})(P):I(P)=\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})(P)
, which is absurd.

Conversely, suppose that
I
satisfies the
i
th homological strong persistence property, but
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1}):I\neq\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for some integer
k\geq 1
. Since the
S
-module
M=(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1}):I)/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
is non-zero and multigraded, we can choose a minimal monomial prime ideal
P
in the support of
M
. As explained before, we may replace the
S_{P}
-module
M_{P}
with the
S(P)
-module
N=(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1}):I)(P)/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})(P)
. Since
P
is a minimal prime in the support of
M
, the
S(P)
-module
N
is of finite length. Therefore, we can find a monomial
\widetilde{f}=f+\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})(P)\in N
for which
\widetilde{f}P=0
in
N
. That is,
f\in(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}):P)(P)\setminus\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})(P)
. Then, by the assumption, there exists a monomial
g\in I(P)
such that
g\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})(P)\subseteq\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})(P)
and
fg\notin\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})(P)
. This is absurd, because
f\in(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1}):I)(P)
.

∎

As a consequence, we have

Corollary 3.3
.

If a monomial ideal
I\subset S
satisfies the
i
th homological strong persistence property, then
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})=I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})=I*\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
and the assumption,
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1}):I=\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. Hence
I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\subseteq\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})
for all
k\geq 1
. Conversely, by
[
22
, Theorem 1.2]
we have
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})\subseteq I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
.

∎

In
[
43
]
, Ratliff showed that
I^{k+1}:I=I^{k}
for
k\gg 0
, if
I
is an ideal of a Noetherian ring
R
. In particular, if
I\subset S
is a monomial ideal, then
\textup{HS}_{0}(I^{k+1}):I=\textup{HS}_{0}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
. This property is no longer valid for the higher homological shift ideals. Indeed, let
I=(x^{2},y^{2},xyz)\subset S=K[x,y,z]
. It is shown in
[
22
, Example 1.3]
that
\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k+1})\neq I\cdot\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. By Corollary
3.3
,
I
does not satisfies the second homological strong persistence property. However,
I
satisfies the second homological persistence property. Indeed, it was shown in
[
22
, Example 1.3]
that

\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k})=(x^{p}y^{q}\ :\ p+q=2k\ \textit{and}\ p,q\ \textit{are odd}).

Hence, all monomials
u\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k}))
are divided by
xy
. Using
[
23
, Proposition 5.1]
it follows that
\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{2}(I)=\{(x),(y)\}
since
\textup{HS}_{2}(I)=(xy)
is a principal ideal, and
\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k})=\{(x),(y),(x,y)\}
for
k>1
, because
\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k})
is not principal. Hence, the ideal
I
satisfies the second homological persistence property.

Let
I\subset S
be an ideal. By
\textup{Min}(I)
we denote the set of associated primes of
I
which are minimal with respect to the inclusion.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal satisfying the
i
th homological strong persistence property and such that
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})=I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. Then
\textup{Min}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})=\textup{Min}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I)
for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

By the assumption, we have
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})=I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k-1})=\dots=I^{k-1}\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I)
for all
k\geq 2
. Let
P\in\textup{Min}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
with
k\geq 2
. Then, for all
u\in\mathcal{G}(I)
we have

(u^{k-1})\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I)\ \subseteq\ I^{k-1}\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I)=\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\ \subseteq\ P.

Suppose there exists
u\in\mathcal{G}(I)\setminus P
. Since
P
is a prime ideal, it follows that
\textup{HS}_{i}(I)\subseteq P
. Hence, there exists
Q\in\textup{Min}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I)
such that
\textup{HS}_{i}(I)\subseteq Q\subseteq P
. Since
I
satisfies the
i
th homological strong persistence property, Proposition
3.1
implies
Q\in\textup{Min}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I)\subseteq\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I)\subseteq\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
. Thus
P=Q
and so
P\in\textup{Min}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I)
.

Otherwise, suppose that
u\in P
for all
u\in\mathcal{G}(I)
. Recall that by Lemma
2.2
we have
\textup{HS}_{1}(I)=(\textup{lcm}(u,v):\ u,v\in\mathcal{G}(I),u\neq v)
. Since we obviously have the inclusions
\textup{HS}_{i}(I)\subseteq\textup{HS}_{i-1}(I)\subseteq\dots\subseteq\textup{HS}_{1}(I)
, it follows that
\textup{HS}_{i}(I)\subseteq\textup{HS}_{1}(I)\subseteq P
. Arguing as before, we then have
P\in\textup{Min}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I)
.

Hence, we have shown that
\textup{Min}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\subseteq\textup{Min}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I)
for all
k
. Since by Proposition
3.1
we have the inclusions
\textup{Min}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I)\subseteq\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I)\subseteq\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
, we conclude that
\textup{Min}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})=\textup{Min}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I)

For the subsequent discussion we recall a few facts. Given monomials
u,v\in S
, we set
u:v=\textup{lcm}(u,v)/v
. A monomial ideal

has
linear quotients
if there exists an order
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
of
\mathcal{G}(I)
such that the ideals
(u_{1},\dots,u_{j-1}):u_{j}
are generated by variables for all
j=2,\dots,m
. For all
j

\textup{set}(u_{j})\ =\ \{i\ :\ x_{i}\in(u_{1},\dots,u_{j-1}):u_{j}\}.

If
I\subset S
is equigenerated and has linear quotients, then it has linear resolution. For a subset
F\subseteq[n]
, we set
{\bf x}_{F}=\prod_{i\in F}x_{i}
. In particular, we have
{\bf x}_{\emptyset}=1
. Let

[
36
, Lemma 1.5]

\textup{HS}_{i}(I)\ =\ ({\bf x}_{F}u\ :\ u\in\mathcal{G}(I),\ F\subseteq\textup{set}(u),\ |F|=i).

and all

Recall that a graph

for any

. Then due to our choice of
s
and
{\bf x}_{G}v>_{\textup{lex}}{\bf x}_{F}u
, we have
\deg_{x_{i}}({\bf x}_{F}u)=\deg_{x_{i}}({\bf x}_{G}v)
for all
i\in\textup{supp}({\bf x}_{F}u)
. This implies that
{\bf x}_{F}u
divides
{\bf x}_{G}v
, which contradicts the fact that
{\bf x}_{G}v
is a minimal generator of
\textup{HS}_{j}(I^{k})
. Hence,
s<\max(u)
.

Note that (
5
) gives
\deg_{x_{s}}({\bf x}_{G}v)\leq k
, and since
\deg_{x_{s}}(u)<\deg_{x_{s}}({\bf x}_{G}v)
, we obtain
\deg_{x_{s}}(u)\leq k-1
. Set
w=x_{s}(u/x_{\max(u)})
. Then
s\leq\max(w)
because
s<\max(u)
. Following the proof of
[
15
, Proposition 3.2]
, we have
w\in I^{k}
. If
s=\max(w)
, then
\deg_{x_{t}}(u)=1
where
t=\max(u)
. By our choice of
s
and since
i<\max(u)=t
for any
i\in F
, it follows that
{\bf x}_{F}u/x_{t}\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I^{k}))
divides
{\bf x}_{G}v
, and
({\bf x}_{G}v):({\bf x}_{F}u)=(x_{s})
.

Now suppose that
s<\max(w)
. To show that
{\bf x}_{F}w\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I^{k}))
, it only remains to prove that
F\subseteq\textup{set}(w)
. Observe that
\textup{set}(u)\setminus\{s\}\subseteq\textup{set}(w)
because of (
5
). Therefore,
F\not\subseteq\textup{set}(w)
if and only if
s\in F
and
s\notin\textup{set}(w)
. Again, from (
5
), we see that
s\notin\textup{set}(w)
if and only if
k=\deg_{x_{s}}(w)
or
s=\max(w)
. Since
s<\max(w)
by assumption, we may suppose that
k=\deg_{x_{s}}(w)
. Then
k=\deg_{x_{s}}(w)=\deg_{x_{s}}(u)+1
. Since
s\in F
, we obtain
k=\deg_{x_{s}}({\bf x}_{F}u)<\deg_{x_{s}}({\bf x}_{G}v)\leq k
, a contradiction. Hence
F\subseteq\textup{set}(w)
. Finally,
{\bf x}_{F}w>_{\textup{lex}}{\bf x}_{F}u
and
({\bf x}_{G}v):({\bf x}_{F}u)\subseteq({\bf x}_{F}w):({\bf x}_{F}u)=(x_{s})
, as required.

∎

With a similar proof, one can show that final lexsegment edge ideals also have homological linear powers.

4.3.
Edge ideals of complete multipartite graphs

Given two positive integers
a\leq b
, we set
[a,b]=\{c\in\mathbb{Z}:a\leq c\leq b\}
. Let
G
be a graph with vertex set
V(G)=[n]
and a vertex partition
V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}
, where

V_{i}=[t_{i-1}+1,t_{i}],\quad\text{ for all }\,\,\,i=1,\ldots,r,

with
t_{0}=0
and
t_{r}=n
. If
E(G)=\{\{i,j\}:i\in V_{k},j\in V_{\ell}\text{ and }k\neq\ell\}
, then
G
is called a
complete multipartite
graph. It is easy to see that the complement graph
G^{c}
of
G
is chordal, and hence
I(G)
has a linear resolution
[
26
]
.

Let
u\in S
be a monomial. For any
1\leq i\leq r
, we let
u_{V_{i}}
denote the largest monomial
m
dividing
u
such that
\textup{supp}(m)\subseteq V_{i}
. Then,
\deg u_{V_{i}}=\sum_{j\in V_{i}}\deg_{x_{j}}u
. We define
\max(u)
as the largest integer
i
such that
i\in\textup{supp}(u)
.

Lemma 4.6
.

With the notation introduced above, a monomial
u
belongs to
\mathcal{G}(I^{k})
if and only if
\deg u=2k
and
\deg u_{V_{i}}\leq k
for all
i=1,\ldots,r
.

Proof.

If
u\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k})
, then
\deg u=2k
, and it follows immediately from the definition of a complete multipartite graph that
\deg u_{V_{i}}\leq k
.

To see the converse, let
u=x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{2k}}
with
i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{2k}
. Given the assumption that
\deg u_{V_{i}}\leq k
for all
i
, we observe that in the list
i_{1},\ldots,i_{2k}
, each vertex in the first
k
positions is adjacent to the corresponding vertex in the next
k
positions. Specifically, for each
p=1,\ldots,k
, the vertex in the
p
th position is adjacent to the vertex in the
(p+k)
th position because they belong to different partition sets. Hence,
u
is a product of
k
monomials in
I
, so
u\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k})
.

∎

Next, we describe the linear quotients of the powers of edge ideals of complete multipartite graphs.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
G
be a complete multipartite graph on
n
vertices, and let
I=I(G)
. For all
k\geq 1
,
I^{k}
has linear quotients with respect to the decreasing lexicographical order on
\mathcal{G}(I^{k})
induced by
x_{1}>\cdots>x_{n}
. Moreover, using the notation introduced above, for any
u\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k})
, let
\max(u)\in V_{d}
for some
d
. Then we have the following.

(i)

If there exists some
d^{\prime}<d
such that
\deg u_{V_{d^{\prime}}}=k
, then

\textup{set}(u)=[1,{t_{d^{\prime}-1}}]\cup[{t_{d^{\prime}-1}+1},{\alpha-1}]\cup[{t_{d^{\prime}}+1},{\max(u)-1}]

where
\alpha
is the maximal integer such that
\alpha\in\textup{supp}(u_{V_{d^{\prime}}})
. In particular,
\ell<\max(u)
for all
\ell\in\textup{set}(u)
.

(ii)

If
\deg u_{V_{d^{\prime}}}<k
, for all
d^{\prime}<d
then
\textup{set}(u)=[1,\max(u)-1]
. In particular,
\ell<\max(u)
for all
\ell\in\textup{set}(u)
.

(iii)

For any
1\leq i\leq n
and for any
m\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I^{k}))
, we have
\deg_{x_{i}}m\leq k
.

Proof.

Let
u,v\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k})
with
u>_{\textup{lex}}v
, where
u=x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{2k}}
and
v=x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{2k}}
. Then there exists an integer
p
such that
i_{\ell}=j_{\ell}
for
1\leq\ell\leq p-1
and
i_{p}<j_{p}
. Let
i_{p}\in V_{a}
and
j_{p}\in V_{b}
for some
a
and
b
. Since
i_{p}<j_{p}
, we have
a\leq b
. Define
w=x_{i_{p}}v/x_{j_{p}}
. Then
w>_{\textup{lex}}u
and
(w):(u)=(x_{i_{p}})
, where
x_{i_{p}}
divides the generator of
(u):(v)
. It remains to show that
w\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k})
. If
a=b
, then for all
i
,
\deg w_{V_{i}}=\deg u_{V_{i}}\leq k
, so
w\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k})
by Lemma
4.6
. If
a<b
, then by our choice of
p
,
\deg v_{V_{b}}<\deg u_{V_{b}}\leq k
, and thus
w\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k})
again by Lemma
4.6
.

Statements (i) and (ii) follow immediately from the above argument together with Lemma
4.6
. Indeed, let
u\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k})
with
\max(u)\in V_{d}
for some
d
, and let
i\in\textup{supp}(u)
and
j\in V(G)
. Set
w=x_{j}u/x_{i}
. Then
(w):(u)=(x_{j})
, and
w>_{\textup{lex}}u
if and only if
j<i
. By Lemma
4.6
,
w\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k})
if and only if
\deg w_{V_{t}}\leq k
for all
t=1,\ldots,r
. This implies
w=x_{j}u/x_{\max(u)}\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k})
for all
j\in V_{d}
with
j<\max(u)
and for all
j\in V_{d^{\prime}}
with
\deg u_{V_{d^{\prime}}}<k
and
d^{\prime}<d
. If
\deg u_{V_{d^{\prime}}}=k
for some
d^{\prime}<d
then
w=x_{j}u/x_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k})
for all
j\in V_{d^{\prime}}
with
j<\alpha
where
\alpha=\max\textup{supp}(u_{V_{d^{\prime}}})
.

The statement (iii) is a consequence of Lemma
4.6
and (i) and (ii).

∎

Now we are ready to prove our main result.

Theorem 4.8
.

Edge ideals of complete multipartite graphs have homological linear powers.

Proof.

Let
G
be a complete multipartite graph on
n
vertices, with notation as introduced above, and let
I=I(G)
. Combining
[
29
, Theorem 2.3]
with
[
4
, Theorem 2.2]
we obtain that
\textup{HS}_{j}(I)
is polymatroidal for all
j

\textup{HS}_{j}(I)
has liner resolution for all
j

[
31
, Theorem 12.6.2]
.

Now let
k\geq 2
. We will show that
\textup{HS}_{j}(I^{k})
has linear quotients with respect to the decreasing lexicographic order on
\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I^{k}))
induced by
x_{1}>\cdots>x_{n}
.

Let
u^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}
be two distinct elements in
\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I^{k}))
with
v^{\prime}>_{\textup{lex}}u^{\prime}
, and let
s

\deg_{x_{i}}u^{\prime}=\deg_{x_{i}}v^{\prime}
for all
i<s
and
\deg_{x_{s}}u^{\prime}<\deg_{x_{s}}v^{\prime}
. We show that there exists a monomial
w^{\prime}
satisfying the following:

w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I^{k}))\text{ such that }(w^{\prime}):(u^{\prime})=(x_{s})\text{ and }w^{\prime}>_{\textup{lex}}u^{\prime}.

(6)

Since
u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I^{k}))
, we write
u^{\prime}={\bf x}_{F}u
and
v^{\prime}={\bf x}_{G}v
for some
u,v\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k})
and
F\subseteq\textup{set}(u)
,
G\subseteq\textup{set}(v)
with
|F|=|G|=j
.

If
u=v
, then
s\in G\setminus F
and there exists some
s^{\prime}\in F\setminus G
such that
s^{\prime}>s
. Let
F^{\prime}=(F\setminus\{s^{\prime}\})\cup\{s\}
. Then
w^{\prime}={\bf x}_{F^{\prime}}u\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I^{k}))
satisfies (
6
).

Now assume that
u\neq v
. Let
r
be the smallest integer for which
r\in\textup{supp}(u)
such that
s<r
. Note that such an
r
exists: if
\max(u)\leq s
, then
\ell<s
for all
\ell\in F
since
\ell<\max(u)
by Proposition
4.7
(i) and (ii). Given
\deg_{x_{i}}u^{\prime}=\deg_{x_{i}}v^{\prime}
for all
i<s
, we conclude that
u^{\prime}
divides
v^{\prime}
, contradicting
v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I^{k}))
.

Let
s\in V_{a}
for some
a
. Recall that
\deg u_{V_{a}}\leq k
due to Lemma
4.6
. We distinguish the following cases.

Case 1.
Assume
\deg u_{V_{a}}=k
and
r\notin V_{a}
. Since
s<r
and
s\in V_{a}
, the partition construction gives
r\in V_{\ell}
for some
\ell>a
. By the choice of
r
, we have
s\geq\max\{i:i\in\textup{supp}(u_{V_{a}})\}
, so
s\notin\textup{set}(u)
by Proposition
4.7
(i) and hence
s\notin F
. Also, Proposition
4.7
(iii) implies
\deg_{x_{s}}v^{\prime}\leq k
, so
\deg_{x_{s}}u\leq\deg_{x_{s}}u^{\prime}<k
. Since
\deg u_{V_{a}}=k
, there exists some
b\in V_{a}\setminus\{s\}
with
b\in\textup{supp}(u_{V_{a}})
and
b<s
.

Claim:
There exists some
b\in\textup{supp}(u_{V_{a}})
with
b<s
such that
b\notin F
.

Proof of Claim:
Assume, for contradiction, that no such
b
exists, meaning
b\in F
for every
b\in\textup{supp}(u_{V_{a}})
with
b<s
. Then
\deg_{x_{b}}u^{\prime}=\deg_{x_{b}}u+1
. Given our choice of

, for all such

\deg_{x_{b}}v^{\prime}=\deg_{x_{b}}u^{\prime}=\deg_{x_{b}}u+1
, and since

. Therefore,

4.7
(i). This leads to

\deg_{x_{s}}u<\deg_{x_{s}}v

We fix
b
as the element satisfying our claim. Since

(because

and

satisfies (
6
).

On the other hand, if
a<s
for all
a\in F
, then there exists
c\in F\cap V_{p}
such that

. Indeed, if no such

exists, then

and

because

for all

. Therefore, we can choose

with

. Set

because

,

where

. Hence

and

due to Lemma
4.6
and

due to Proposition
4.7
(i). Therefore

satisfies (
6
).

Case 2.
Assume

, and

. If

satisfies one of the following conditions:

,
\deg_{x_{r}}u=1
, and there does not exist any

such that

, then
let
w=x_{s}u/x_{r}
. By Lemma
4.6
,

, and using Proposition
4.7
(i), we have
F\subseteq\textup{set}(w)
. Therefore,
w^{\prime}={\bf x}_{F}w\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I^{k}))
and
w^{\prime}
satisfies (
6
).

One the other hand, if none of the above conditions hold, then the only remaining case is when
r=\max(u_{V_{a}})
,
\deg_{x_{r}}u=1
, and there exists some
d\in F
such that
s\leq d<r
. Let
c
be the maximum integer in
F
such that
s\leq c<r
. If
s\notin F
, then set
F^{\prime}=(F\setminus\{c\})\cup\{s\}
and
w=x_{c}u/x_{r}
. By Lemma
4.6
,
w\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I^{k}))
, and by Proposition
4.7
,
F^{\prime}\subseteq\textup{set}(w)
. Thus,
w^{\prime}={\bf x}_{F^{\prime}}w\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I^{k}))
and
w^{\prime}
satisfies (
6
).

Now suppose
s\in F
. Then
\deg_{x_{s}}u^{\prime}=\deg_{x_{s}}u+1
. We first prove the following

Claim:
There exists some
b\in\textup{supp}(u_{V_{a}})
with

.

Proof of claim:
For
s<d<r
, we have

. Since

with

. By Lemma
4.6
,
w\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k})
and Proposition
4.7
(i) gives
F^{\prime}\subseteq\textup{set}(w)
. Thus,
w^{\prime}={\bf x}_{F}w\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I^{k}))
satisfies (
6
).

Case 4.
Assume
\deg u_{V_{a}}\leq k-1
and
x_{r}\in V_{a}
. This case is similar to Case 2 to some extent; however, here we compare
r
with
\max(u)
instead of
\max(u_{V_{a}})
.

If
r\neq\max(u)
or
\deg_{x_{\alpha}}u>1
, or
r=\max(u)
with
\deg_{x_{r}}u=1
and no
d\in F
satisfies
s\leq d<r
, then set
w=x_{s}u/x_{r}
. By Lemma
4.6
,
w=x_{s}u/x_{r}\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k})
, and Proposition
4.7
(i) and(ii) imply
F\subseteq\textup{set}(w)
. Therefore
w^{\prime}={\bf x}_{F}w\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I^{k}))
satisfies (
6
).

The only case remain to be discussed is when
r=\max(u)
with
\deg_{x_{r}}u=1
and there exists some
d\in F
satisfies
s\leq d<r
. Let
c
be the maximum in
F
such that
s\leq c\leq r
. If
s\notin F
, set
F^{\prime}=(F\setminus\{c\})\cup\{s\}
and
w=x_{c}u/x_{r}
. By Lemma
4.6
,
w\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I^{k}))
, and by Proposition
4.7
(i) gives
F^{\prime}\subseteq\textup{set}(w)
. Then
w^{\prime}={\bf x}_{F^{\prime}}w\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I^{k}))
satisfies (
6
). Now suppose that
s\in F
. Then
\deg_{x_{s}}u^{\prime}=\deg_{x_{s}}u+1
. We first prove the following

Claim:
There exists some
b\in\textup{supp}(u)
with
b<s
such that
b\notin F
.

Proof of claim:
Since
r=\max(u)
, there exists some
b\in\textup{supp}(u)
such that
b\in V_{t}
for some
t<a
, also
b<s
because of
s\in V_{a}
and the choice of
r
. If
b\in F
, for all
b\in\textup{supp}(u)
with
b<s
, then
\deg_{x_{b}}v^{\prime}=\deg_{x_{b}}u^{\prime}\geq 2
. Since
{\bf x}_{G}
is squarefree, we obtain
u/x_{r}
divides
v
.

\bullet

If
s\notin G
, then
\deg_{x_{s}}v^{\prime}=\deg_{x_{s}}v
and
\deg_{x_{s}}v
is at most
\deg_{x_{s}}u+1=\deg_{x_{s}}u^{\prime}
.

\bullet

If
s\in G
, then Proposition
4.7
gives
s<\max(v_{V_{a}})
. This implies
\deg_{x_{s}}v=\deg_{x_{s}}u
, and
\deg_{x_{s}}v^{\prime}=\deg_{x_{s}}v+1=\deg_{x_{s}}u^{\prime}
.

In both of the above cases, we have
\deg_{x_{s}}v^{\prime}\leq\deg_{x_{s}}u^{\prime}
, contradicting
\deg_{x_{s}}u^{\prime}<\deg_{x_{s}}v^{\prime}
. Thus our claim holds.

As in Case 3, let
b\in\textup{supp}(u_{V_{a}})
with
b<s
such that
b\notin F
. Set
F^{\prime}=(F\setminus\{c\})\cup\{b\}
and
w=x_{s}x_{c}u/x_{r}x_{b}
. By Lemma
4.6
,
w\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k})
and by Proposition
4.7
(i) we have
F^{\prime}\subseteq\textup{set}(w)
. Therefore
w^{\prime}={\bf x}_{F}w\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{j}(I^{k}))
satisfies (
6
).

∎

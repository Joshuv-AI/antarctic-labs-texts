Introduction

In
[
1
]
upper estimates for the entropy numbers of Sobolev classes on a domain with Hölder boundary were obtained. In the present paper we improve these estimates (in the case when the definition of the Sobolev class involves all partial derivatives of order
r
): we will show that the exponential in the orders of decrease is greater than the quantity in
[
1
]
; moreover, under some conditions on the parameters, the order will be the same as for domains with Lipschitz boundary (while the boundary of the domain may be non-Lipschitz). We also obtain upper estimates for the Kolmogorov, linear and the Gelfand widths.

In addition, we will consider domains of special form, where the Hölder singularity is concentrated on an
h
-set (see the definition below). For them, we will obtain more sharp upper estimates of widths and entropy numbers. The same lower estimates will be obtained in the case of special
h
-sets constructed in
[
2
]
. The last example shows that the exponent in the upper estimates on the class of all Hölder domains cannot be improved. On the other hand, it will be shown that these lower estimates hold not for all
h
-sets; for example, the order of decreae of entropy numbers and widths can be improved if a plane is parallel to a coordinate plane.

Let us give necessary definitions.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain. Given
f\in L_{1}^{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
and
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\,\dots,\,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}

|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{d}
,
\partial_{\alpha}f=\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}f}{\partial x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\dots\partial x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}}
(the partial derivative is taken in the sense of distributions). Given

, we denote by
\nabla^{r}f
the vector of all partial derivatives
\partial_{\alpha}f
such that
|\alpha|=r
, and set

\|\nabla^{r}f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}=\max_{|\alpha|=r}\|\partial_{\alpha}f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}.

Let
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
,
r\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain. We define the Sobolev spaces by the formulas

{\cal W}^{r}_{p}(\Omega)=\{f\in L_{1}^{{\rm loc}}(\Omega):\;\|\nabla^{r}f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}<\infty\},

\tilde{\cal W}^{r}_{p}(\Omega)=\{f\in L_{1}^{{\rm loc}}(\Omega):\;\|\nabla^{r}f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}<\infty\}

and the unit balls (the Sobolev classes), by the formulas

W^{r}_{p}(\Omega)=\{f\in L_{1}^{{\rm loc}}(\Omega):\;\|\nabla^{r}f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant 1\},

\tilde{W}^{r}_{p}(\Omega)=\{f\in L_{1}^{{\rm loc}}(\Omega):\;\|\nabla^{r}f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant 1\}.

Now we give the definitions of the Gelfand, Kolmogorov and linear widths.

Let
X
be a linear normed space. Denote by

the dual space to
X
, by

be a non-empty set,

. The Kolmogorov, linear and the Gelfand

are defined, respectively, by the formulas

d_{n}(M,\,X)=\inf_{L\in{\cal L}_{n}(X)}\sup_{x\in M}\inf_{y\in L}\|x-y\|,

\lambda_{n}(M,\,X)=\inf_{A\in L(X,\,X),\,{\rm rk}\,A\leqslant n}\sup_{x\in M}\|x-Ax\|,

d^{n}(M,\,X)=\inf_{x_{1}^{*},\,\dots,\,x_{n}^{*}\in X^{*}}\sup\Bigl\{\|x\|:\;x\in M\cap\Bigl(\cap_{j=1}^{n}\ker x_{j}^{*}\Bigr)\Bigr\}

(in the last definition, we set

Definition 1
.

Let

\displaystyle\Omega=\{(x^{\prime},\,x_{d}):\;x^{\prime}\in(0,\,1)^{d-1},\,0<x_{d}<\psi(x^{\prime})\},

(2)

\psi:[0,\,1]^{d-1}\rightarrow(0,\,\infty)

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{c}|\psi(x^{\prime})-\psi(y^{\prime})|\leqslant t\quad\text{if }|x_{i}-y_{i}|\leqslant\varphi_{i}(t),\quad i=1,\,\dots,\,d-1;\\
\min_{x^{\prime}\in[0,\,1]^{d-1}}\psi(x^{\prime})\in[1,\,2].\end{array}

We write

\Omega=\cup_{j=1}^{j_{0}}\Omega_{j}

We introduce notation for order inequalities and equalities. Let

,

be sets, and let

We say that a function is locally absolutely continuous on a convex subset of

if it is absolutely continuous on each segment lying in this set.

Let

\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}t\frac{u^{\prime}(t)}{u(t)}=0

is a locally absolutely continuous function such that
\lim\limits_{s\to+\infty}s\frac{\varphi_{\theta,u}^{\prime}(s)}{\varphi_{\theta,u}(s)}=0

Let
\Lambda:(0,\,1]\rightarrow(0,\,\infty)
be a locally absolutely continuous function such that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\to+0}t\frac{\Lambda^{\prime}(t)}{\Lambda(t)}=0.

(5)

We set
\psi_{\Lambda}(t)=\frac{1}{\Lambda(1/t)}

Let

\displaystyle r+(\sigma(d-1)+1)(1/q-1/p)>0.

(7)

We set

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}=\frac{r}{d},\quad\alpha_{2}=\frac{r+1/q-1/p}{\sigma(d-1)},

(8)

\displaystyle\tau_{1}(n)=1,\quad\tau_{2}(n)=(\varphi_{\sigma(d-1),\psi_{\Lambda}}(n))^{-r-(\sigma(d-1)+1)(1/q-1/p)}[\psi_{\Lambda}(n^{\frac{1}{\sigma(d-1)}}\varphi_{\sigma(d-1),\psi_{\Lambda}}(n))]^{1/p-1/q}.

(9)

\displaystyle e_{n}({\rm Id}:\tilde{\cal W}^{r}_{p}(\Omega)\rightarrow L_{q}(\Omega))\underset{\mathfrak{Z}_{*}}{\lesssim}n^{-\alpha_{j_{*}}}\tau_{j_{*}}(n),

Remark 1

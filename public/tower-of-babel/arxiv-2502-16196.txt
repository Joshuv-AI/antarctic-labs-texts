An equal-order virtual element framework for the coupled Stokes-Temperature equation with nonlinear viscosity
Sudheer Mishra, Natarajan E

Abstract
In this work, we present and analyze a novel stabilized virtual element formulation for the coupled Stokes-Temperature equation on polygonal meshes, employing equal-order element pairs where viscosity depends on temperature. The main objective of the proposed virtual elements is to develop a stabilized virtual element problem that avoids higher-order derivative terms and bilinear forms involving velocity, pressure and temperature, thereby avoiding the coupling between virtual element pairs. Moreover, it also reduces the violation of divergence-free constraints and offers reasonable control over the gradient of temperature. We derive the stability of the continuous solution using the Banach fixed-point theorem under sufficiently small data. The stabilized coupled virtual element problem is formulated using the local projection-based stabilization methods. We demonstrate the existence and uniqueness of the stabilized discrete solution using the Brouwer fixed-point theorem and the contraction theorem under the assumption of sufficient small data by showing the well-posedness of the stabilized decoupled virtual element problems. Furthermore, we derive the error estimates with optimal convergence rates in the energy norms. We present several numerical examples to confirm the theoretical findings. Additionally, the numerical behavior of the proposed stabilized method is shown to be robust with respect to linear and non-linear thermal conductivity.

Introduction

The coupling of a viscous incompressible flow with a temperature transport equation where the dynamic viscosity depends on temperature arises in many physical applications, such as the thermal structure of subduction zones, natural and thermal convection, climate predictions, heat transfer in nanoparticle fluids, aluminum production, nuclear power plants, chemical distillation process, oceanic flows, motion of bio-membranes and so on. Due to its significance, the coupled thermo-fluid dynamics problem has garnered considerable attention from physicists, geologists, mathematicians, and engineers over the past decades. Consequently, several numerical techniques have been developed to compute the distribution of the temperature field

Notable work related to the Boussinesq equation includes

Violation of this condition leads to the appearance of spurious oscillations or moving fronts in the solution domain, a common issue in unified (equal-order) finite/virtual element frameworks. Additionally, convection-dominated regimes also tend to produce such oscillations. However, this method incorporates higher-order derivative terms and multiple forms involving more than one finite element, which can increase the coupling between finite element pairs. Furthermore, we emphasize that the cross-terms
\alpha_{1}\left(\nabla p_{h},\nabla\cdot\tau_{h}\right)_{K}+\alpha_{1}\left(-\nabla\cdot\sigma_{h},\frac{1}{8\mu(\phi)}\nabla q_{h}\right)_{K}

Due to the unified framework, the classical coupled virtual element problem may suffer from violations of the discrete inf-sup condition, violations of the divergence-free constraints, and the presence of convection-dominated transport regimes. We establish the stability of the continuous problem under sufficiently small data by applying the fixed-point and contraction theorems. For the discrete stabilized problem, we first establish the well-posedness of the stabilized decoupled problems followed by deriving the well-posedness of the coupled problem under sufficiently small data. We show the optimal convergence rates in the energy norms. Our numerical findings validate the expected convergence rates in both diffusion-dominated and convection-dominated transport regimes. The proposed VEM effectively reduces violations of the divergence-free condition, as confirmed by simulations of realistic problems. Additionally, the numerical behavior of the stabilized VEM remains
robust to temperature-dependent thermal conductivity.

2
The coupled Stokes-Temperature equation

We consider the following coupled Stokes-Temperature equation on general polygonal domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
as follows:

\displaystyle\text{Find}\,(\sigma,\mathbf{u},p,\phi)\,\,\text{such that},

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=\mu(\phi)\varepsilon(\mathbf{u})\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\quad-\nabla\cdot\sigma+\nabla p

\displaystyle=\alpha\phi\mathbf{f}\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbf{f}}\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega,

(2.5)

\displaystyle-\kappa\Delta\phi+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\phi

\displaystyle=g\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=\widehat{g}\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega,

(2.7)

where
\sigma
is the stress tensor and
\varepsilon(\mathbf{u})
is the deformation rate tensor. The effective dynamic viscosity is defined by
\mu:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
and the thermal conductivity is given by
\kappa>0
. The velocity vector field is denoted by
\mathbf{u}
, the scalar pressure field by
p
and the scalar temperature field by
\phi
. We assume the load terms
\mathbf{f}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2}
and
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Furthermore, the positive constant
\alpha
represents the thermal Rayleigh number.

For the sake of simplicity, we combine Eqs. (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), and we call the obtained expression as the generalized Stokes equation, given as follows

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot(\mu(\phi)\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}))+\nabla p=\alpha\phi\mathbf{f}\qquad\text{in}\,\,\,\,\Omega.

(2.8)

Furthermore, we assume the following:

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{f}}=\mathbf{0},\qquad\widehat{g}=0.

(2.9)

In Eqn. (
2.9
), we consider the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions for simplicity. However, non-homogeneous conditions can also be addressed. For example, in
[
8
]
, the authors introduce non-homogeneous Dirichlet condition for the temperature field. Mimicking this approach, the present work can be extended to the non-homogeneous case by appropriately modifying the spaces for the velocity and temperature fields.

Additionally, let us assume that there exist positive constants
\mu_{min}
,
\mu_{max}
and
L_{\mu}
, such that

\displaystyle\mu_{min}\leq\mu(\xi)\leq\mu_{max}\quad\text{for all}\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R},\qquad\quad|\mu(\xi_{1})-\mu(\xi_{2})|\leq L_{\mu}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|,\text{ for all }\,\,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.10)

We now introduce the following spaces for the coupled Stokes-Temperature equation:

\mathbf{V}=[H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)]^{2}
,
Q=L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)=\big\{q\in L^{2}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}qd\Omega=0\big\}
,
\Sigma=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,

endowed with their natural norms. Additionally, we define the following settings:

•

a_{V}(\cdot;\cdot,\cdot):\Sigma\times\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
a_{V}(\phi;\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}):=\int_{\Omega}\mu(\phi)\varepsilon(\mathbf{v}):\varepsilon(\mathbf{w})\,d\Omega
;

•

b(\cdot,\cdot):\mathbf{V}\times Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
b(\mathbf{v},q):=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v})q\,d\Omega
;

•

a_{T}(\cdot,\cdot):\Sigma\times\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
a_{T}(\phi,\psi):=\int_{\Omega}\kappa\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi\,d\Omega
;

•

c_{T}(\cdot;\cdot,\cdot):\mathbf{V}\times\Sigma\times\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
c_{T}(\mathbf{v};\phi,\psi):=\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\phi)\psi\,d\Omega
;

•

For given
\phi\in\Sigma
, define
F_{\phi}:\mathbf{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
F_{\phi}(\mathbf{v}):=\int_{\Omega}(\alpha\mathbf{f}\phi)\cdot\mathbf{v}\,d\Omega
;

•

G:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
G(\psi):=\int_{\Omega}g\psi\,d\Omega
.

We further define the following forms:

\displaystyle A_{V}[\phi;(\mathbf{v},q),(\mathbf{w},r)]

\displaystyle:=a_{V}(\phi;\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})-b(\mathbf{w},q)+b(\mathbf{v},r)\qquad\,\text{for all}\,\,\phi\in\Sigma\,\,\text{and}\,\,(\mathbf{v},q),(\mathbf{w},r)\in\mathbf{V}\times Q,

\displaystyle A_{T}(\mathbf{v};\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle:=a_{T}(\phi,\psi)+c^{S}_{T}(\mathbf{v};\phi,\psi)\qquad\qquad\quad\text{ for all}\,\,\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V}\,\,\text{and}\,\,\phi,\psi\in\Sigma,

where
c^{S}_{T}(\mathbf{v};\phi,\psi)=\frac{1}{2}\big[c_{T}(\mathbf{v};\phi,\psi)-c_{T}(\mathbf{v};\psi,\phi)\big]
represents the skew-symmetric part of
c_{T}
.

Therefore, the weak formulation for the coupled Stokes-Temperature equation
(S)
is readily as follows

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\text{Find}\,\,(\mathbf{u},p,\phi)\in\mathbf{V}\times Q\times\Sigma,\,\,\text{such that}\\
A_{V}[\phi;(\mathbf{u},p),(\mathbf{v},q)]+A_{T}(\mathbf{u};\phi,\psi)=F_{\phi}(\mathbf{v})+G(\psi)\qquad\text{for all}\,\,(\mathbf{v},q,\psi)\in\mathbf{V}\times Q\times\Sigma.\end{cases}

(2.11)

The boundary
\partial\Omega
is Lipschitz and parameters
\mu>0
and
\kappa>0
are continuous, and
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Therefore, the existence of a solution for problem (
2.11
) follows from
(
37
, Theorem 2.1)
. We emphasize that
(
37
, Theorem 2.1)
shows the existence of a weak solution for continuous problem (
2.11
) when load term
g=0
, and the convective term vanishes in
[
37
]
. By making a slight modification to the arguments in
[
37
]
, we can extend this result to show the existence of weak solutions for (
2.11
) when
g\neq 0
.

In the following section we introduce the fixed-point formulation equivalent to the problem (
2.11
) to establish the uniqueness of its solution.

2.1
The fixed-point problem

We now aim to achieve the fixed-point problem of the variational problem (
2.11
). To do this, we will first decouple the problem (
2.11
) into the primal formulation of the momentum equation (generalized Stokes problem) and the primal formulation of the temperature (advection-diffusion) equation. Then we formulate the fixed-point problem using these decoupled problems. In this sequel, we define the operator
\mathbf{S}:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbf{V}\times Q
such that

\displaystyle\widehat{\phi}\rightarrow\mathbf{S}(\widehat{\phi})=(S^{1}(\widehat{\phi}),S^{2}(\widehat{\phi})):=(\widehat{\mathbf{u}},\widehat{p}),

(2.12)

where
(\widehat{\mathbf{u}},\widehat{p})\in\mathbf{V}\times Q
denotes the solution of the following problem:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\text{For given $\widehat{\phi}\in\Sigma$, seek}\,\,(\widehat{\mathbf{u}},\widehat{p})\in\mathbf{V}\times Q,\,\,\text{such that}\\
A_{V}[\widehat{\phi};(\widehat{\mathbf{u}},\widehat{p}),(\mathbf{v},q)]=F_{\widehat{\phi}}(\mathbf{v})\qquad\text{for all}\,\,(\mathbf{v},q)\in\mathbf{V}\times Q.\end{cases}

(2.13)

Additionally, we introduce
\mathbf{V}_{div}:=\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V}\,\,\text{such that}\,\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0\}
. Therefore, we define the operator
\mathbb{M}:\mathbf{V}_{div}\rightarrow\Sigma
corresponding to the temperature equation by,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{u}}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}(\widehat{\mathbf{u}})=\widehat{{\phi}},

(2.14)

where
\widehat{\phi}\in\Sigma
is the solution of the following problem:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\text{For given}\,\,\widehat{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbf{V}_{div},\,\,\text{seek}\,\,\widehat{\phi}\in\Sigma,\,\,\text{such that}\\
A_{T}(\widehat{\mathbf{u}};\widehat{\phi},\psi)=G(\psi)\qquad\text{for all}\,\,\psi\in\Sigma.\end{cases}

(2.15)

Finally, we define
\mathbb{T}:\Sigma\rightarrow\Sigma
by
\mathbb{T}(\widehat{\phi}):=\mathbb{M}(S^{1}(\widehat{\phi}))
, for all
\widehat{\phi}\in\Sigma
. Then, the fixed-point problem can be defined as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\text{Find}\,\,{\phi}\in\Sigma,\,\,\text{such that}\\
\mathbb{T}({\phi})=\phi.\end{cases}

(2.16)

Notably, the fixed-point problem (
2.16
) is equivalent to the problem (
2.11
). Therefore, we establish the stability of the problem (
2.16
) using the Banach fixed-point theorem following
[
5
]
.

2.2
Well-posedness of the decoupled problems

We now establish the well-posedness of the decoupled problems (
2.13
) and (
2.15
). To do this, we define the norms over the spaces
\mathbf{V}\times Q
and
\Sigma
as follows:

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\mathbf{v},q)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\mu_{min}\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\|q\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}\qquad\,\,\text{for all}\,\,(\mathbf{v},q)\in\mathbf{V}\times Q,

(2.17)

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\kappa}\|\nabla\phi\|_{0,\Omega}\qquad\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\phi\in\Sigma.

(2.18)

Remark 2.1

For given
\phi\in\Sigma
, combining the continuity and coercivity of
a_{V}
with the continuity and inf-sup condition of the form
b
, it is evident that for any
(\mathbf{u},p)\in\mathbf{V}\times Q

\displaystyle(\mathbf{v},q)\neq(\mathbf{0},0),\quad\sup\limits_{(\mathbf{v},q)\in\mathbf{V}\times Q}\dfrac{A_{V}[\phi;(\mathbf{u},p),(\mathbf{v},q)]}{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\mathbf{v},q)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}\gtrsim{\beta}_{0}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\mathbf{u},p)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|},

(2.19)

where the constant
\beta_{0}>0
depends on
\mu
and is the inf-sup constant associated with the form
A_{V}[\cdot;\cdot,\cdot]
.

Lemma 2.1

For given
\widehat{{\phi}}\in\Sigma
, the decoupled problem (
2.13
) has a unique solution
(\widehat{\mathbf{u}},\widehat{p})\in\mathbf{V}\times Q
such that it holds

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{S}(\widehat{{\phi}})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}:={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\widehat{\mathbf{u}},\widehat{{p}})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\lesssim\dfrac{\alpha C_{q}^{2}(1+C_{P})^{2}}{\beta_{0}\sqrt{\kappa\mu_{min}}}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{0,\Omega}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widehat{\phi}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Sigma}.

(2.20)

Proof

Since the form
a_{V}(\cdot;\cdot,\cdot)
is continuous and coercive, and
b(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies the inf-sup condition
[
33
]
, it follows that problem (
2.13
) has a unique solution
(\widehat{\mathbf{u}},\widehat{p})\in\mathbf{V}\times Q
. Employing Remark
2.1
and the Sobolev embedding theorem, we infer

\displaystyle\beta_{0}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\widehat{\mathbf{u}},\widehat{{p}})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq A_{V}[\phi;(\widehat{\mathbf{u}},\widehat{{p}}),(\widehat{\mathbf{u}},\widehat{{p}})]=(\alpha\widehat{\phi}\mathbf{f},\widehat{\mathbf{u}})

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha C_{q}^{2}(1+C_{P})^{2}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\widehat{{\phi}}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\widehat{\mathbf{u}}\|_{0,\Omega},

(2.21)

which readily implies (
2.20
).

Lemma 2.2

For any given
\widehat{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbf{V}_{div}
, the decoupled problem (
2.15
) has a unique solution
\widehat{\phi}\in\Sigma
such that it satisfies

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbb{M}(\widehat{\mathbf{u}})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Sigma}:={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widehat{{\phi}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Sigma}\lesssim\dfrac{C_{P}}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\|g\|_{0,\Omega}.

(2.22)

Proof

Since
A_{T}
is continuous and coercive with coercivity constant
1
, the uniqueness of the solution is evident by employing the Lax-Milgram Lemma. Further using the coercivity property, we obtain

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widehat{{\phi}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Sigma}^{2}\leq A_{T}(\widehat{\mathbf{u}};\widehat{\phi},\widehat{{\phi}})\lesssim C_{P}\|g\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\widehat{\phi}\|_{0,\Omega},

(2.23)

which completes the proof.

2.3
Stability of the coupled problem

In this section we focus on proving the uniqueness of the continuous solution. To this end, we first derive several auxiliary results that are crucial for demonstrating the stability of the problem (
2.11
).

Lemma 2.3

For any given
{\phi},\widehat{{\phi}}\in\Sigma
, we have the following estimate

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{S}(\phi)-\mathbf{S}(\widehat{{\phi}})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\lesssim\dfrac{C_{q}(1+C_{P})}{\beta_{0}\sqrt{\kappa\mu_{min}}}\big[L_{\mu}\|\nabla S^{1}(\widehat{{\phi}})\|_{0,3,\Omega}+\alpha C_{q}(1+C_{P})\|\mathbf{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\big]{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi-\widehat{{\phi}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Sigma}.

(2.24)

Proof

For any given
\phi,\widehat{{\phi}}\in\Sigma
, we define
\mathbf{S}(\phi)=(S^{1}(\phi),S^{2}(\phi)):=(\mathbf{u},p)
and
\mathbf{S}(\widehat{{\phi}})=(S^{1}(\widehat{{\phi}}),S^{2}(\widehat{{\phi}})):=(\widehat{\mathbf{u}},\widehat{{p}})
. Recalling Eqn. (
2.13
), and by adding and subtracting suitable terms, we infer

\displaystyle A_{V}[\phi;(\mathbf{u},p)-(\widehat{\mathbf{u}},\widehat{{p}}),(\mathbf{v},q)]:

\displaystyle=\big(\alpha\mathbf{f}\phi,\mathbf{v}\big)-\big(\alpha\mathbf{f}\widehat{{\phi}},\mathbf{v}\big)+A_{V}[\widehat{{\phi}};(\widehat{\mathbf{u}},\widehat{{p}}),(\mathbf{v},q)]-A_{V}[\phi;(\widehat{\mathbf{u}},\widehat{{p}}),(\mathbf{v},q)]

\displaystyle=:A_{V,1}+A_{V,2}.

(2.25)

Applying the fact
\mathbf{f}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2}
, the Sobolev embedding theorem and the Poincar
\acute{\text{e}}
inequality, we have

\displaystyle|A_{V,1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha\|\mathbf{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\phi-\widehat{{\phi}}\|_{0,4,\Omega}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{0,4,\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha C_{q}^{2}(1+C_{P})^{2}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla(\phi-\widehat{{\phi}})\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\dfrac{\alpha C_{q}^{2}(1+C_{P})^{2}}{\sqrt{\mu_{min}}}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla(\phi-\widehat{{\phi}})\|_{0,\Omega}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\mathbf{v},q)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

(2.26)

Employing the Lipschitz continuity of
\mu
and the Sobolev embedding theorem
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\Omega)
, yields

\displaystyle|A_{V,2}|

\displaystyle=a_{V}(\widehat{{\phi}};\widehat{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{v})-a_{V}(\phi;\widehat{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{v})=\big((\mu(\widehat{{\phi}})-\mu(\phi))\nabla\widehat{\mathbf{u}},\nabla\mathbf{v}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim L_{\mu}\|\phi-\widehat{{\phi}}\|_{0,6,\Omega}\|\nabla\widehat{\mathbf{u}}\|_{0,3,\Omega}\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+C_{P})C_{q}L_{\mu}\|\nabla(\phi-\widehat{{\phi}})\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\widehat{\mathbf{u}}\|_{0,3,\Omega}\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\dfrac{(1+C_{P})C_{q}L_{\mu}}{\sqrt{\mu_{min}}}\|\nabla(\phi-\widehat{{\phi}})\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\widehat{\mathbf{u}}\|_{0,3,\Omega}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\mathbf{v},q)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

(2.27)

Using the inf-sup condition for
A_{V}[\cdot;\cdot,\cdot]
and the estimates (
2.26
) and (
2.27
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\beta_{0}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\mathbf{u}-\widehat{\mathbf{u}},p-\widehat{{p}})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}

3
Stabilized virtual element framework

In this section we present a local projection-based stabilized virtual element formulation of problem (S). We begin with the mesh regularity assumption.

(A1)

Mesh assumption.

We denote a sequence of decompositions of
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
into non-overlapping simple polygonal element by
\{\Omega_{h}\}_{h>0}
. Each polygonal element is denoted by
E\in\Omega_{h}
and has a finite diameter
h_{E}
. The maximum diameter across all polygonal elements in
\{\Omega_{h}\}_{h>0}
is given by
h:=\sup_{E\in\Omega_{h}}h_{E}
. Following the standard VEM framework
[
13
,
16
]
, we assume that there exists a positive constant
\gamma_{0}
, independent of the mesh size
h
, such that each element
E\in\Omega_{h}
satisfies the following mesh regularity assumptions:

•

each element
E
is star-shaped with respect to a ball
B_{E}
of radius
\geq\gamma_{0}h_{E}
;

•

each edge
e\in\partial E
has a length
|e|\geq\gamma_{0}h_{E}
.

It is noteworthy that the regularity conditions mentioned above may be relaxed in numerous physical applications, as explored by Beirao da Veiga et al. in
[
17
]
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
. For any
E\in\Omega_{h}
, let
|E|
and
\mathbf{x}_{E}=(x_{E,1},x_{E,2})
denote the area of the element
E
and its centroid, respectively. We further assume that
\mathbb{P}_{k}(E)
represents the space of polynomials of degree
\leq k
and
\mathbb{P}_{-1}(E)=\{0\}
. A standard basis associated with the polynomial space
\mathbb{P}_{k}(E)
is
\mathbb{M}_{k}(E)
, known as the set of normalized monomials, given as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{k}(E)=\Big\{\Big(\dfrac{\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{E}}{h_{E}}\Big)^{\mathbf{m}},|\mathbf{m}|\leq k\Big\},

where
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
is a multi-index such that
|\mathbf{m}|=m_{1}+m_{2}
, and
\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{m}}=x_{1}^{m_{1}}x_{2}^{m_{2}}
.

We now introduce the standard polynomial projectors. For any
E\in\Omega_{h}

•

the
H^{1}
-energy projection operator
\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k}:H^{1}(E)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}_{k}(E)
, given by

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\int_{E}\nabla(w-\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k}w)\cdot\nabla m_{k}\,dE=0,\qquad\forall\,w\in H^{1}(E)\,\,\text{and}\,\,m_{k}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(E),\\
\int_{\partial E}(w-\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k}w)\,ds=0,\end{cases}

(3.1)

with extension for the vector field
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,E}_{k}:[H^{1}(E)]^{2}\rightarrow[\mathbb{P}_{k}(E)]^{2}
.

•

the
L^{2}
-projection operator
\Pi^{0,E}_{k}:L^{2}(E)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}_{k}(E)
, defined by

\displaystyle\int_{E}(w-\Pi^{0,E}_{k}w)m_{k}\,dE=0,\qquad\forall\,\,w\in L^{2}(E)\,\,\text{and}\,\,m_{k}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(E),

(3.2)

with extension for vector field
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k}:[L^{2}(E)]^{2}\rightarrow[\mathbb{P}_{k}(E)]^{2}
.

Remark 3.1

(Stability of
\Pi^{0,E}_{k}
in
L^{q}(E)
for any
q\geq 2
) Utilizing the polynomial inverse inequality presented in
[
20
]
, we deduce that for any polynomial
p_{k}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(E)
:

\displaystyle\|p_{k}\|_{0,q,E}\lesssim h^{2/q-2/p}_{E}\|p_{k}\|_{0,p,E}\qquad\qquad\text{for any}\,\,\,p,q\in[0,\infty].

(3.3)

Therefore, for any
v\in L^{2}(E)
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Pi^{0,E}_{k}v\|_{0,q,E}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2/q-1}_{E}\|\Pi^{0,E}_{k}v\|_{0,E}.

(3.4)

Using the stability of
\Pi^{0,E}_{k}
in
L^{2}(E)
, it holds

\displaystyle\|\Pi^{0,E}_{k}v\|_{0,q,E}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2/q-1}_{E}\|v\|_{0,E}.

(3.5)

We now choose
r>0
such that
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{2}
, and then employing the H
\ddot{\text{o}}
lder inequality

\displaystyle\|\Pi^{0,E}_{k}v\|_{0,q,E}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{\frac{2}{q}-1}_{E}\|1\|_{0,r,E}\|v\|_{0,q,E}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{\frac{2}{q}-1}_{E}h^{2/r}_{E}\|v\|_{0,q,E}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{\frac{2}{q}-1}_{E}h^{2(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}_{E}\|v\|_{0,q,E}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{0,q,E}.

(3.6)

Thus, the
L^{2}
-projection operator satisfies the stability property with respect to
L^{q}(E)
norm for any
q\geq 2
.

3.3

We finally introduce fluctuation-controlling operators to control the fluctuations of the gradients of pressure and temperature, and to mitigate the violation of the divergence-free constraints.
We define a fluctuation operator
\widehat{r}_{h}:L^{2}(E)\rightarrow L^{2}(E)
such that

with extension for vector field

\widehat{\mathbf{r}}_{h}:=\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}
. Employing the above fluctuation-controlling operators, we define the following stabilization terms:

•

The continuity equation is stabilized by the following stabilization term:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1,h}(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}\tau_{1,E}\big[\big(\widehat{r}_{h}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}),\widehat{r}_{h}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}_{h})\big)_{E}+S^{E}_{\nabla}\big((\mathbf{I}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,E}_{k})\mathbf{v}_{h},(\mathbf{I}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,E}_{k})\mathbf{w}_{h}\big)\big].

(3.16)

•

Pressure stabilizing term:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2,h}(p_{h},q_{h})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}\tau_{2,E}\big[\big(\widehat{\mathbf{r}}_{h}(\nabla p_{h}),\widehat{\mathbf{r}}_{h}(\nabla q_{h})\big)_{E}+S^{E}_{2}\big((I-\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k-1})p_{h},(I-\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k-1})q_{h}\big)\big].

(3.17)

Moreover, there exist two positive constants

independent of the mesh size such that there holds

\displaystyle\theta_{3\ast}\ \|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,E}^{2}\leq S^{E}_{2}(q_{h},q_{h})\leq\theta_{3}^{\ast}\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,E}^{2}\qquad\forall\,q_{h}\in Q_{h}(E)\cap ker({\Pi}^{\nabla,E}_{k-1}).

(3.18)

•

Stabilization term for transport equation for temperature:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{3,h}(\phi_{h},\psi_{h})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}\tau_{3,E}\big[\big(\widehat{\mathbf{r}}_{h}(\nabla\phi_{h}),\widehat{\mathbf{r}}_{h}(\nabla\psi_{h})\big)_{E}+S^{E}_{1}\big((I-\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k})\phi_{h},(I-\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k})\psi_{h}\big)\big],

(3.19)

where
\tau_{i,E}
are stabilization parameters for

and
3
, which depend on the mesh size and will be specified later.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{h}[(\mathbf{v}_{h},p_{h}),(\mathbf{w}_{h},q_{h})]:=\mathcal{L}_{1,h}(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h})+\mathcal{L}_{2,h}(p_{h},q_{h})
. Therefore, the stabilized coupled virtual element problem for Stokes-Temperature equation
(S)
is given as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\text{Find}\,\,(\mathbf{u}_{h},p_{h},\phi_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}\times\Sigma_{h},\,\,\text{such that}\\
A_{V,h}[\phi_{h};(\mathbf{u}_{h},p_{h}),(\mathbf{v}_{h},q_{h})]+\mathcal{L}_{h}[(\mathbf{u}_{h},p_{h}),(\mathbf{v}_{h},q_{h})]=F_{h,\phi_{h}}(\mathbf{v}_{h})\quad\forall\,(\mathbf{v}_{h},q_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\times Q_{h},\\
A_{T,h}(\mathbf{u}_{h};\phi_{h},\psi_{h})+\mathcal{L}_{3,h}(\phi_{h},\psi_{h})=G_{h}(\psi_{h})\quad\forall\,\psi_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}.\end{cases}

(3.20)

Remark 3.3

From the stabilized problem (
3.20
), it is noteworthy that the classical virtual element problem (
3.15
) primarily requires the stabilization of the continuity equation to improve the violation of divergence-free constraints, the stabilization of pressure due to violation of discrete inf-sup condition, and the stabilization of transport equation to provide a reasonable control over the gradient of solution. While the choice of
\mathcal{L}_{3,h}
can be relaxed for the dominated-diffusion regimes. Our study considers the stabilized problem (
3.20
), demonstrating that the inclusion of
\mathcal{L}_{3,h}
does not compromise the robustness of the proposed method in diffusion-dominated transport regimes.

7
Conclusion

We present a stabilized virtual element method to address the coupled Stokes-Temperature equation on general polygons, employing equal-order virtual element pairs. The stabilization techniques belong to a family of symmetric stabilization methods based on local projections. The proposed method introduces separate stabilization terms, avoiding the inclusion of higher-order derivative terms or bilinear forms involving velocity, pressure and temperature. We derive the stability of the continuous problem using the Banach contraction theorem. Furthermore, the existence and uniqueness of the discrete solution are shown using the Brouwer fixed-point theorem and the contraction theorem. Error estimates are derived in the energy norms, showing optimal convergence rates. Additionally, several numerical examples are presented, confirming the theoretical convergence rates, including both diffusion-dominated and convection-dominated regimes for the transport equation. Notably, the numerical behavior of the proposed method is robust with respect to both linear and nonlinear (temperature-dependent) thermal conductivity.

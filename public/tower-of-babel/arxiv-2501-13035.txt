Stability of purely convective steady-states of fractional Boussinesq equations in an exterior domain
Zhi-Min Chen

Abstract
. A thermal convection flow in the three-dimensional unbounded fluid domain exterior to a sphere is considered. The viscosity force is determined by a fractional power of the Stokes operator. A purely conductive steady state arises due to the fluid heated from the sphere. A weak solution of the fluid motion problem is obtained and global stability of the steady-state solution in L 2 L^{2} is provided. Keywords. Global stability, Asymptotic behaviours, weak solution, fractional Boussinesq equation, exterior domain, purely conductive steady-state solution

35B35, 35B40, 35D30, 35Q35, 76D99.

1.
Introduction

The traditional Rayleigh-Bénard thermal convection problem originated from Bénard
[
1
]
and Rayleigh
[
29
]
is considered as a type of natural fluid motion in a planar horizontal layer fluid heated from its lower horizontal boundary, and the flow is driven by a buoyant force due to the temperature difference of the horizontal fluid boundaries. In a study of nonlinear behaviours of atmospheric fluid motion, this horizontal layer of fluid motion model was adopted by Lorenz
[
23
]
(see also references therein) to approximate locally the atmospheric flow surrounding the spherical earth surface. The present study is concerned with a thermal convection flow in the three-dimensional exterior domain

\displaystyle\Omega=\{x\in R^{3};\,\,|x|>1\}

heated from the unit sphere
\partial\Omega=\{x\in R^{3};\,\,|x|=1\}
. The incompressible fluid motion is governed by the following system in a dimensionless formulation

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u=\Delta u-\nabla p-\alpha gT,\,\,\nabla\cdot u=0,&\\
\partial_{t}T+u\cdot\nabla T=\Delta T,&\\
u|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\,\,T|_{\partial\Omega}=\alpha.&\end{array}\right\}

Here the time-dependent functions
u
,
T
and
p
represent, respectively, the unknown velocity, temperature and pressure. The temperature
T
varies from
\alpha>0
on the bottom boundary
\partial\Omega
to zero at the infinity (
|x|\to\infty
). This gives rise to the buoyant force, which is determined by the acceleration due to the gravity expressed as

\displaystyle g=g_{0}\nabla\frac{1}{|x|},

(5)

defining a large-scale fluid motion. Here,
g_{0}
is a gravitational constant. For simplicity,
g_{0}=1
is assumed and
\alpha
being a constant is adopted.

Equation (
1
) admits a purely convective steady-state solution
(u_{0},p_{0},T_{0})
expressed as

\displaystyle u_{0}=0,\,\,\,\nabla p_{0}=-\alpha gT_{0},\,\,\Delta T_{0}=0,\,\,T_{0}|_{\partial\Omega}=\alpha,

(6)

and hence

\displaystyle u_{0}=0,\,\,\,T_{0}=\frac{\alpha}{|x|},\,\,p_{0}(x)=-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2|x|^{2}}.

(7)

Local stability of the purely steady-state flow was studied by Hishida and Yamada
[
16
]
and Hishida
[
15
,
17
]
with respect to strong solutions, while
L^{2}
global stability of the purely steady-state flow was studied by Chen-Kagei-Miyakawa
[
5
]
with respect to weak solutions.

Let
L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)^{3}
be the
L^{2}
-closure of
C^{\infty}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)^{3}
, the set of all smooth
solenoidal vector fields with compact support in
\Omega
. Let
P
denote the bounded projection operator mapping
L^{2}(\Omega)^{3}
onto
L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)^{3}
due to the Helmholtz decomposition
[
24
]
.
By adopting the perturbation

\displaystyle(u,T)=(u_{0},T_{0})+(u,\theta)

(8)

and applying the projection operator
P
to the momentum equation, equation (
1
) can be written as

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u+Au+P\alpha g\theta=-P(u\cdot\nabla u),&\\
\partial_{t}\theta-\Delta\theta+u\cdot\nabla T_{0}=-u\cdot\nabla\theta,&\end{array}\right\}

for
A=-P\Delta
and the solutions

\displaystyle u(t)\in D(A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{u\in W^{2,2}(\Omega)^{3};\,\,u|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\,\,\nabla\cdot u=0\right\},\,\,

\displaystyle\theta(t)\in D(-\Delta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{u\in W^{2,2}(\Omega)^{3};\,\,u|_{\partial\Omega}=0\right\}.

By the spectral representation (see, for example, Yosida
[
33
, page 313, Theorem 1]
), we have
fractional Stokes operator

\displaystyle A^{\gamma}=\int^{\infty}_{0}\lambda^{\gamma}dE_{\lambda},\,\,0<\gamma\leq 1

(12)

associated with the domain

\displaystyle D(A^{\gamma})=\left\{u\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)^{3};\,\,\|u\|_{2}+\|A^{\gamma}u\|_{2}<\infty,\,\,u|_{\partial\Omega}=0\right\},

where
E_{\lambda}
denotes the spectral resolution of the unit determined by the operator
A
and
\|\cdot\|_{r}
represents the Lebesque space norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}
.

It is the purpose of the present paper to consider the global stability of the basic steady-state flow
(u_{0},T_{0})
in the sense
(u,T)\to(u_{0},T_{0})
or
(u,\theta_{0})\to(0,0)
as
t\to\infty
in the
L^{2}
space when the viscosity force
-\Delta u
is reduced to that determined by a fractional Laplacian
A^{\kappa}
in the sense of (
12
).
More precisely, the fluid motion problem involving the fractional Laplacian in the exterior domain
\Omega
is expressed as

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u+A^{\kappa}u+P\alpha g\theta=-P(u\cdot\nabla u),&\\
\partial_{t}\theta-\Delta\theta+u\cdot\nabla T_{0}=-u\cdot\nabla\theta,&\\
u|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\,\,\theta|_{\partial\Omega}=0,&\\
u|_{t=0}=a,\,\,\theta|_{t=0}=b.&\end{array}\right\}

Therefore, it follows from (
8
) that the
L^{2}
stability of
(u_{0},T_{0})
reduces to the
L^{2}
-decay problem with respect to any weak solutions in the following sense:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(a,b)\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)^{3}\times L^{2}(\Omega)
and
0<\kappa\leq 1
. A vector field
(u,\theta)
defining on the domain
[0,\infty)\times\Omega
is said to be a global weak solution of (
1
), if for any
\tau>0
,

\displaystyle(u,\theta)\in L^{\infty}\left(0,\tau;L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)^{3}\times L^{2}(\Omega)\right)\cap L^{2}\left(0,\tau;D(A^{\frac{\kappa}{2}})\times W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)\right),

\displaystyle\int^{\tau}_{0}(-\langle u(t),\partial_{t}v(t)\rangle+\langle A^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}u(t),A^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}v(t)\rangle+\langle\alpha g\theta,v(t)\rangle)dt

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{\tau}_{0}\langle u(t)\otimes u(t),\nabla v(t)\rangle dt+\langle u(0),v(0)\rangle

and

\displaystyle\int^{\tau}_{0}(-\langle\theta(t),\partial_{t}\vartheta(t)\rangle+\langle\nabla\theta(t),\nabla\vartheta(t)\rangle+\langle u\cdot\nabla T_{0},\vartheta(t)\rangle)dt

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{\tau}_{0}\langle u(t)\theta(t),\nabla\vartheta(t)\rangle dt+\langle\theta(0),\vartheta(0)\rangle,

for the test function
(v,\vartheta)\in C^{1}([0,\tau];C_{0,\sigma}^{\infty}(\Omega)^{3}\times C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega))
with
v(\tau)=0
and
\vartheta(\tau))=0
. Here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
represents the
L^{2}
inner product,
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
denotes the set of all smooth
functions with compact support in
\Omega
and
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
denotes the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
.

We are in the position to state the main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\frac{3}{4}<\kappa\leq 1
,
0<\beta<\frac{1}{2}
and
0<\alpha<2^{-\kappa}
. Then for
(a,b)\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)^{3}\times L^{2}(\Omega)
, equation (
1
) admits a global weak solution so that

\displaystyle\|(u(t),\theta(t))\|_{2}\to 0\,\,\mbox{ as }\,\,t\to\infty.

(20)

Moreover, if
(v,\vartheta)
solves the linearized problem of (
1
):

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}v+A^{\kappa}v+P\alpha g\vartheta=0,&\\
\partial_{t}\vartheta-\Delta\vartheta+v\cdot\nabla T_{0}=0,&\\
v|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\,\,\vartheta|_{\partial\Omega}=0,&\\
v|_{t=0}=a,\,\,\vartheta|_{t=0}=b,&\end{array}\right\}

and

\displaystyle\|(v(t),\vartheta(t))\|_{2}\leq Ct^{-\beta},\,\,\,t>0,

(26)

then the algebraic decay

\displaystyle\|(u(t),\theta(t))\|_{2}\leq Ct^{-\beta},\,\,\,t>0

(27)

holds true.

To derive the stability, we assume that
\alpha>0
is sufficiently small, so that the buoyant force and external influence from nonhomogeneous boundary condition can be controlled by the viscous force and heat diffusion.
If the temperature on the conducting surface is not small, it is infeasible to keep the stability. For example, instabilities arise in the Rayleigh-Bénard thermal convection flow (see Lorenz
[
23
]
, Chen and Price
[
10
]
and Rabinowitz
[
28
]
).
If the thermal convection effect is neglected, i.e.,
\alpha=0
on the sphere
\partial\Omega
, equation (
1
) with
\kappa=1
reduces to the exterior Navier-Stokes equations. The corresponding decay estimates displayed in Theorem
1.1
for the exterior Navier-Stokes equations have been given by Borchers and Miyakawa
[
3
]
.

The motivation on the stability analysis for exterior domain flows is from the understanding of fluid motion passing an object or an object moving in a fluid at a stationary speed (see Oseen
[
27
]
). This forced problem has an basic steady-state flow (see Finn
[
11
]
). The stability of small stationary Navier-Stokes flows has been well studied (see Borchers and Miyakawa
[
3
,
4
]
, Chen
[
6
]
, Hishida
[
18
]
, Kozono and Ogawa
[
21
]
, Kozono
[
20
]
and Masuda
[
26
]
). Decay problems of exterior Navier-Stokes flows were well investigated by Borchers and Miyakawa
[
2
]
, Han
[
12
]
, He and Miyakawa
[
14
]
, Miyakawa
[
24
]
, Miyakawa and Sohr
[
25
]
. We also refer to Chen
[
7
,
8
]
for potential flows around objects moving in water waves. The algebraic decay estimate (
27
) is mainly determined by the energy estimate of the flow. For sharp decay estimates of a fluid flow in
L^{2}
and Hardy spaces, one may refer to Chen and Miyakawa
[
9
]
. However, there is little literature on fractional exterior Boussinesq flows. The present study provides the first attempt in the understanding of the fractional exterior flows.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is divided into four sections. Section 2 is a collection of preliminaries; the existence of the weak solution is given in Section 3; decay property (
20
) is shown in Section 4; algebraic decay property (
27
) is derived in Section 5.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
C
and
C_{n}
be generic constants, which are independent of the quantities
u
,
\theta
,
v
,
\vartheta
,
U
,
V
,
t>0
,
x\in\Omega
, but they may depend on initial vector field
(a,b)
. However,
C_{n}
depends on the integer
n\geq 1
, while
C
is independent of
n\geq 1
.

Following lemmas are crucial in our analysis.

Lemma 2.1
.

(Ladyzhenskaya
[
22
, page 41]
) There holds the following inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}dx\leq 4\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}dx,\,\,\,u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega).

(28)

Let
H^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}
be the completion of
D(A^{\frac{\gamma}{2}})
with respect to the norm
\|A^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}u\|_{2}
for
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(Miyakawa
[
24
, Theorem 2.4]
)
Let
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\gamma}{3}
. Then
H^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}
is equal to the complex interpolation space
[L_{\sigma}^{2}(\Omega)^{3},H^{\frac{1}{2}}]_{\gamma}
in the sense of
[
32
]
and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{p}\leq 2^{\gamma}\|A^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}u\|_{2},\,\,u\in H^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}.

(29)

Lemma 2.3
.

If
0<\gamma\leq 1
, then we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{u(x)}{x}\right|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 2^{\gamma}\|A^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}u\|_{2},\,\,\,u\in H^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}.

(30)

Proof.

Define the linear operator
\mathcal{T}
so that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}u=\frac{u(x)}{|x|}.

(31)

Since
|x|>1
in
\Omega
, we see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}:L_{\sigma}^{2}(\Omega)^{3}\mapsto L^{2}(\Omega)^{3}\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,\|\mathcal{T}u\|_{2}\leq\|u\|_{2}.

(32)

It follows from Lemma
2.1
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}:H^{\frac{1}{2}}\mapsto L^{2}(\Omega)^{3}\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,\|\mathcal{T}u\|_{2}\leq 2\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{2}.

(33)

By Lemma
2.2
, we have the complex interpolation

\displaystyle[L_{\sigma}^{2}(\Omega)^{3},\,H^{\frac{1}{2}}]_{\gamma}=H^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}.

(34)

Hence, combining of (
32
)-(
34
) and the complex interpolation property that the corresponding interpolation functor is exact and of type
\gamma
in the following sense (see Triebel
[
32
, Theorem 1.9.3]
)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}u\|_{2}\leq\|\mathcal{T}\|_{L^{2}_{\sigma}\mapsto L^{2}}^{1-\gamma}\|\mathcal{T}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}\mapsto L^{2}}^{\gamma}\|A^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}u\|_{2}\leq 2^{\gamma}\|A^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}u\|_{2},

(35)

we thus have the desired estimate.
∎

Define the linear operator

\displaystyle LU

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(A^{\kappa}u+\alpha Pg\theta,-\Delta\theta+u\cdot\nabla T_{0}\right)

(36)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(A^{\kappa}u+\alpha P(\theta\nabla\frac{1}{|x|}),-\Delta\theta+\alpha u\cdot\nabla\frac{1}{|x|}\right)

for
0<\kappa\leq 1
and
U=(u,\theta)
in the domain

D(L)=D(A^{\kappa})\times D(-\Delta)
.

To show the self-adjointness of the operator
L
, we employ the Kato–Rellich Theorem.

Lemma 2.4
.

(
[
19
, Theorem 4.3, page 287]
) Let
X
be a Hilbert space,
A_{1}:X\mapsto X
a self-adjoint operator and
A_{2}:X\mapsto X
a symmetric operator with the domain relationship
D(A_{1})\subset D(A_{2})
.
Assume that

\displaystyle\|A_{2}u\|_{X}\leq\delta\|A_{1}u\|_{X}+C\|u\|_{X},\,\,u\in D(A_{1})

(37)

for a nonnegative constant
\delta<1
.
Then
A_{1}+A_{2}
is a self-adjoint operator.

Lemma 2.5
.

The operator
L
is self-adjoint
and satisfies the positive property

\displaystyle\langle L(u,\theta),(u,\theta)\rangle\,\geq(1-\alpha 2^{\kappa})(\|A^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla\theta\|_{2}^{2}),

(38)

provided that
(u,\theta)\in D(L)
and
0<\alpha<2^{-\kappa}
.

Proof.

Similar positivity result with
\kappa=1
was given in
[
5
]
. However, we would like to provide a straightforward proof for reader’s convenience.

For
(u,\theta)\in D(L)
, we use integration by parts and Hölder inequality to produce the positivity

\displaystyle\langle L(u,\theta),(u,\theta)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle A^{\kappa}u+\alpha\theta\nabla\frac{1}{|x|},u\rangle+\langle-\Delta\theta+\alpha u\cdot\nabla\frac{1}{|x|},\theta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|A^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla\theta\|_{2}^{2}+2\alpha\langle u\cdot\nabla\frac{1}{|x|},\theta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|A^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla\theta\|_{2}^{2}-2\alpha\langle\frac{u}{|x|},\nabla\theta\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|A^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla\theta\|_{2}^{2}-2\alpha 2^{\kappa}\|A^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}u\|_{2}\|\nabla\theta\|_{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(1-\alpha 2^{\kappa}\right)(\|A^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla\theta\|_{2}^{2}),

where we have used Lemma
2.3
.

On the other hand, in order to apply the Kato-Rellich Theorem for the self-adjointness, we adopt the operator decomposition
L=L_{1}+L_{2}
so that

\displaystyle L_{1}(u,\theta)=(A^{\kappa}u,-\Delta\theta)\mbox{ and }L_{2}(u,\theta)=(\alpha P(\theta\nabla\frac{1}{|x|}),\alpha u\cdot\nabla\frac{1}{|x|})

(39)

for
D(L_{1})=D(A^{\kappa})\times D(-\Delta)
and
D(L_{2})=L_{\sigma}^{2}(\Omega)^{3}\times L^{2}(\Omega)
.

It is readily seen that the operator
L_{2}
has the symmetric property

\displaystyle\langle L_{2}U,V\rangle=\langle\alpha\theta\nabla\frac{1}{|x|},v\rangle+\langle\alpha u\cdot\nabla\frac{1}{|x|},\vartheta\rangle=\langle U,L_{2}V\rangle

(40)

for
U=(u,\theta),V=(v,\vartheta)\in D(L_{2})
. Furthermore, since
|x|\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\|L_{2}U\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\|\frac{U}{|x|^{2}}\|_{2}\leq\alpha\|U\|_{2},\,\,U\in D(L_{1}).

This shows the validity of (
37
) and hence the self-adjointness of
L
due to Lemma
2.4
.

∎

Lemma 2.6
.

If
0<\kappa\leq 1
and
0<\alpha<2^{-\kappa}
, then the operator
L
generates a strongly continuous analytic semigroup defined as
e^{-tL}
so that, for
U\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)^{3}\times L^{2}(\Omega)
and
n=0,1
,

\displaystyle\|L^{n}e^{-tL}U\|_{2}\leq t^{-n}\|U\|_{2},\,\,\,\,\lim_{t\to 0}\|e^{-tL}U-U\|_{2}=0,

(41)

\displaystyle\|A^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}P_{1}e^{-tL}U\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla P_{2}e^{-tL}U\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{t^{-1}}{1-\alpha 2^{\kappa}}\|U\|_{2}^{2},

(42)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|e^{-tL}U\|_{2}=0,

(43)

Here the projection operators are defined as

\displaystyle P_{1}(u,\theta)=(u,0)\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,P_{2}(u,\theta)=(0,\theta)

(44)

for
(u,\theta)\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)^{3}\times L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.5
, we have the spectral representation (see, for example, Yosida
[
33
, Page 313, Theorem 1]
)

\displaystyle L=\int^{\infty}_{0}\lambda d\hat{E}_{\lambda}

(45)

with
\hat{E}_{\lambda}
the spectral resolution of the unit determined by the operator
L
.
Consequently, equation (
41
) is verified by

\displaystyle\|L^{n}e^{-tL}U\|_{2}^{2}=\int^{\infty}_{0}\lambda^{2n}e^{-2t\lambda}d\|\hat{E}_{\lambda}U\|_{2}^{2}\leq t^{-2n}\|U\|_{2}^{2},\,\,\,n=0,1,

(46)

and

\displaystyle\|(e^{-tL}-1)U\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(e^{-tL}-1)(U-V)\|_{2}+\|(e^{-tL}-1)V\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|U-V\|_{2}+t\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}\lambda^{2}d\|\hat{E}_{\lambda}V\|^{2}_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\|U-V\|_{2}+t\|LV\|_{2}

for
V\in C^{\infty}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)^{3}\times C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Equation (
41
) ensures the strongly continuous analytic semigroup property.

For the derivation of (
42
), by Lemma
2.5
and Hölder inequality,
we have

\displaystyle(1-\alpha 2^{\kappa})\left(\|A^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}P_{1}e^{-tL}U\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla P_{2}e^{-tL}U\|_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\langle Le^{-tL}U,e^{-tL}U\rangle

(47)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|Le^{-tL}U\|_{2}\|e^{-tL}U\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t^{-1}\|U\|_{2}^{2}.

For the validation of the decay property (
43
), we take
V=(v,\vartheta)\in C^{\infty}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)^{3}\times C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
,
V^{\prime}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)^{3}\times L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\kappa}{3}
. By Hölder inequality, (
29
), Sobolev imbedding and (
42
), we see that

\displaystyle\langle e^{-tL}V,V^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|v\|_{r}\|P_{1}e^{-tL}V^{\prime}\|_{q}+\|\vartheta\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\|P_{2}e^{-tL}V^{\prime}\|_{6}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|v\|_{r}\|A^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}P_{1}e^{-tL}V^{\prime}\|_{2}+C\|\vartheta\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\|\nabla P_{2}e^{-tL}V^{\prime}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\|v\|_{r}+\|\vartheta\|_{\frac{6}{5}})\|V^{\prime}\|_{2}.

This together with (
41
) yields

\displaystyle\|e^{-tL}U\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|e^{-tL}(U-V)\|_{2}+\|e^{-tL}V\|_{2}

(48)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|U-V\|_{2}+Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\|v\|_{r}+\|\vartheta\|_{\frac{6}{5}}).

This implies (
43
), after taking the limit
t\to\infty
and using the density of
the set
C^{\infty}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)^{3}\times C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
in
L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)^{3}\times L^{2}(\Omega)
.

∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
0<\kappa\leq 1
,
0<\alpha\leq 2^{-\kappa}
and
U=(u,\theta)\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)^{3}\times L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|A^{\kappa}P_{1}e^{-tL}U\|_{2}\leq\left(t^{-1}+t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{2\alpha}{\sqrt{1-\alpha 2^{\kappa}}}\right)\|U\|_{2}.

(49)

Proof.

Since
|x|>1
in
\Omega
, it follows from Lemmas
2.1
and
2.5
that

\displaystyle\|A^{\kappa}u\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|A^{\kappa}u+\alpha P(\theta\nabla\frac{1}{|x|})\|_{2}+\alpha\|P(\theta\nabla\frac{1}{|x|})\|_{2}

(50)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|A^{\kappa}u+\alpha P(\theta\nabla\frac{1}{|x|})\|_{2}+\alpha\|\frac{\theta}{|x|^{2}}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|A^{\kappa}u+\alpha P(\theta\nabla\frac{1}{|x|})\|_{2}+\alpha\|\frac{\theta}{x}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|LU\|_{2}+2\alpha\|\nabla\theta\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|LU\|_{2}+\frac{2\alpha}{\sqrt{1-\alpha 2^{\kappa}}}\sqrt{\langle LU,U\rangle}.

Thus (
49
) is obtained from the combination of (
41
) and the following inequality

\displaystyle\|A^{\kappa}P_{1}e^{-tL}U\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|Le^{-tL}U\|_{2}+\frac{2\alpha}{\sqrt{1-\alpha 2^{\kappa}}}\sqrt{\|Le^{-tL}U\|_{2}\|e^{-tL}U\|_{2}}.

(51)

∎

Lemma 2.8
.

For
\frac{1}{2}\leq\kappa\leq 1
,
0<\alpha\leq 2^{-\kappa}
and
U=(u,\theta)\in L_{\sigma}^{2}(\Omega)^{3}\times L^{2}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla e^{-tL}U\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}+t^{-\frac{1}{2\kappa}})\|U\|_{2}.

(52)

Proof.

By Lemmas
2.5
and
2.6
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla P_{2}e^{-tL}U\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\alpha 2^{\kappa}}}\sqrt{\|Le^{-tL}U\|_{2}\|e^{-tL}U\|_{2}}

(53)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{1-\alpha 2^{\kappa}}}\|U\|_{2}.

This gives the estimate with respect to the second component. For the estimate with respect to another component, we employ the condition
\frac{1}{2}\leq\kappa\leq 1
, the spectral representation and Hölder inequality to produce

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{2}=\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|A^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}u\|_{2}^{2-\frac{1}{\kappa}}\|A^{\kappa}u\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}-1}.

(54)

Hence, by Lemma
2.5
and Hölder inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\nabla P_{1}e^{-tL}U\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\|e^{-sL}U_{0}\|_{2}ds+Ct^{\frac{1}{2}}\int^{t}_{0}\|u(s)\|_{2}^{1-\frac{1}{2\kappa}}\|A^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}u(s)\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2\kappa}}\|\nabla\theta(s)\|_{2}ds

\displaystyle+C(t^{\frac{1}{2}}+t^{1-\frac{1}{2\kappa}})\int^{t}_{0}\|u(s)\|_{2}^{2-\frac{3}{2\kappa}}\|A^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}u(s)\|_{2}^{\frac{3}{2\kappa}}ds.

Applying Hölder inequality and energy inequality (
77
), we have

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\|U(s)\|_{2}ds-\int^{t}_{0}\|e^{-sL}U_{0}\|_{2}ds

(82)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct^{1-\frac{1}{4\kappa}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\|u(s)\|_{2}^{4\kappa(1-\frac{1}{2\kappa})}\|A^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}u(s)\|_{2}^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{4\kappa}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\|\nabla\theta(s)\|_{2}^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle+C(t^{2-\frac{5}{4\kappa}}+t^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{4\kappa}})\left(\int^{t}_{0}\|u(s)\|_{2}^{\frac{8\kappa}{3}-2}\|A^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}u(s)\|_{2}^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{3}{4\kappa}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t^{1-\frac{1}{4\kappa}}+t^{2-\frac{5}{4\kappa}}+t^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{4\kappa}})\|U_{0}\|_{2}^{2}.

It follows from (
77
) that

\displaystyle\|U(t)\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{t}\int^{t}_{0}\|U(s)\|_{2}ds

(83)

Therefore, equation (
82
) becomes

\displaystyle\|U(t)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\int^{t}_{0}\|e^{-sL}U_{0}\|_{2}ds+(t^{-\frac{1}{4\kappa}}+t^{-(\frac{5}{4\kappa}-1)}+t^{-(\frac{3}{4\kappa}-\frac{1}{2})})\|U_{0}\|_{2}^{2},

(84)

which gives the desired
L^{2}
decay (
20
) due to (
43
).

5.
Proof of
L^{2}
algebraic decay property (
27
)

Observing

\displaystyle\frac{5}{4\kappa}-1\geq\frac{1}{4\kappa},\,\,\,\frac{3}{4\kappa}-\frac{1}{2}\geq\frac{1}{4\kappa},\,\mbox{ since }\kappa\leq 1,

and the assumption (
26
) or

\displaystyle\|e^{-tL}U_{0}\|_{2}\leq Ct^{-\beta},

(85)

we obtain from (
84
) that

\displaystyle\|U(t)\|_{2}\leq Ct^{-\beta}+Ct^{-\frac{1}{4\kappa}}.

(86)

Here, we only consider the case
t\geq 1
, due to the uniform bound

\displaystyle\|U(t)\|_{2}\leq\|U_{0}\|_{2}

given by (
77
). Hence, we obtain (
27
) when
\beta\leq\frac{1}{4\kappa}
.

For
\beta>\frac{1}{4\kappa}
,
since
1\geq\kappa>\frac{3}{4}
, we set
p>1
and
q>1
so that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{4\kappa}=1\,\,\mbox{ and }\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4\kappa}=1.

(87)

This allows us to employ (
85
) and Hölder inequality in (
81
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|U(t)\|_{2}

(88)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct^{-\beta}+C(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}+t^{-\frac{1}{2\kappa}})\left(\int^{t}_{0}\|u\|_{2}^{(2-\frac{3}{2\kappa})p}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\|A^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}u\|_{2}^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{3}{4\kappa}}

\displaystyle+Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\|u\|_{2}^{(1-\frac{1}{2\kappa})q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\|A^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}u\|_{2}^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{4\kappa}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\|\nabla\theta\|_{2}^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct^{-\beta}+Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\|u\|_{2}^{(2-\frac{3}{2\kappa})p}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\|u\|_{2}^{(1-\frac{1}{2\kappa})q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where we have used the uniform bound in (
77
).

The desired decay estimate will be derived by a boot strap iteration scheme. To do so, we begin with the step
\beta_{1}=\frac{1}{4\kappa}
so that

\displaystyle\|U(t)\|_{2}\leq Ct^{-\beta_{1}}.

(89)

For an integer
n\geq 1
, assuming the existence of a number
0<\beta_{n}<\beta\leq\frac{1}{2}
so that

\displaystyle\|U(t)\|_{2}\leq Ct^{-\beta_{n}},

(90)

we will show that the previous estimate can be advanced to the one involving a power
-\beta_{n+1}
instead of
-\beta_{n}
. Employing (
77
) and (
87
), we estimate (
88
) by using (
90
) to obtain that

\displaystyle\|U(t)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C[t^{-\beta}+t^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p}-\beta_{n}(2-\frac{3}{2\kappa})}+t^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{q}-\beta_{n}(1-\frac{1}{2\kappa})}]

(91)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C[t^{-\beta}+t^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{4\kappa}-\beta_{n}(2-\frac{3}{2\kappa})}+t^{-\frac{1}{4\kappa}-\beta_{n}(1-\frac{1}{2\kappa})}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C[t^{-\beta}+t^{-\frac{1}{4\kappa}-\beta_{n}(1-\frac{1}{2\kappa})}(t^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\kappa}-\beta_{n}(1-\frac{1}{\kappa})}+1)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C[t^{-\beta}+t^{-\frac{1}{4\kappa}-\beta_{n}(1-\frac{1}{2\kappa})}(t^{-(\frac{1}{\kappa}-1)(\frac{1}{2}-\beta_{n})}+1)]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t^{-\beta}+t^{-\frac{1}{4\kappa}-\beta_{n}(1-\frac{1}{2\kappa})})

due to
\beta_{n}\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
t\geq 1
.
Here we have used the inequalities

\displaystyle\beta_{n}(2-\frac{3}{2\kappa})p<\frac{\frac{1}{2}(2-\frac{3}{2\kappa})}{1-\frac{3}{4\kappa}}=1,

(92)

and

\displaystyle\beta_{n}(1-\frac{1}{2\kappa})q<\frac{\frac{1}{2}(2-\frac{3}{2\kappa})}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4\kappa}}\leq 1.

(93)

If
\frac{1}{4\kappa}+\beta_{n}(1-\frac{1}{2\kappa})\geq\beta
, equation (
91
) implies the desired estimate (
27
). Otherwise, we set

\displaystyle\beta_{n+1}=\frac{1}{4\kappa}+\beta_{n}(1-\frac{1}{2\kappa}),\,\,\,n\geq 1,

(94)

and hence

\displaystyle\beta_{n+1}=\frac{1}{4\kappa}\sum_{m=0}^{n}(1-\frac{1}{2\kappa})^{m}.

Since the upper limit of
\beta_{n+1}
is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\kappa}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(1-\frac{1}{2\kappa})^{m}=\frac{\frac{1}{4\kappa}}{1-(1-\frac{1}{2\kappa})}=\frac{1}{2},

(95)

there is an integer
n_{0}\geq 1
so that
\frac{1}{2}>\beta_{n_{0}+1}\geq\beta>\beta_{n_{0}}
and

\displaystyle\|U(t)\|_{2}\leq C(t^{-\beta}+t^{-\beta_{n_{0}+1}})\leq Ct^{-\beta}.

(96)

This gives (
27
) and completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

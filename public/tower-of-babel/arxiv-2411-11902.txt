A population study on the effect of metallicity on ZAMS to the merger
Sourav Roy Chowdhury, Deeptendu Santra

Abstract
Multiband observations of compact object sources offer a unique opportunity to explore their progenitors and enhance early multi-messenger alert. Recent analyses have indicated that metallicity significantly impacts the evolution of progenitors and the resulting compact objects. Using binary population synthesis, we investigate the formation of eccentric, inspiralling black hole binaries and black hole-neutron star binaries through the isolated binary evolution channel. We introduced a fiducial mass and metallicity relation for each ZAMS star. We model the stellar cluster of ZAMS stars by extending COSMIC’s publicly available code. Our BPS code effectively accounts for the metallicity of each stellar object in the stellar cluster. In our analysis, we observed a significant increase in the number of inspiral binaries remaining in the stellar cluster. Instead of assuming a uniform metallicity for a stellar cluster, ZAMS stars within the cluster, characterized by diverse metallicity, evolve into more massive compact objects. The total mass of a single binary black hole inspiral varies from ∼ 9 − 86 \sim 9-86 M ⊙ ; whereas for a black hole-neutron star system, this range becomes ∼ 6 − 32 \sim 6-32 M ⊙ . We compare the detectability of the characteristic strain against sub-Hz gravitational wave detectors.

II
Methodology

This section outlines the procedures and key considerations undertaken to simulate the CO binaries and determine their characteristic strain.  We introduce a fiducial model of the metallicity of each progenitor. It is crucial to take into account the metallicity distribution of initial stars in a stellar cluster as it can influence the orbital characteristics, timescales and the rate of CO mergers
(
Sedda et al. 2020
)
.
Our work methodology can be summarized as follows:

•

Determine the model parameters and initial conditions for BPS required for our study.

•

Introduce our fiducial model of metallicity into the BPS code.

•

Evolve the initial population and study the relationship between various attributes like mass and eccentricity. We also study how our simulation fits with observed data.

•

Determine the characteristics strain of GWs from 1st Gen COs in the evolved population (The 1st Gen COs are those formed from ZAMS stars).

II.1
Initializing a Population

In general, stellar synthesis or BPS starts from ZAMS stars. In our study, these ZAMS stars represent the primordial Pop III stars. We mostly follow COSMIC for generating the initial binaries. These binaries are independent of any specific binary evolution model.
We set the population size at

binaries to ensure a robust statistical distribution.

II.2
Metallicity

Stellar mass and metallicity are the two most significant characteristics of a stellar cluster. Comprehending the correlation between these quantities is essential for understanding the physical mechanisms that regulate star formation in a stellar cluster. It is speculated that at birth, the metallicities of stars should be equivalent to those of the interstellar gas surrounding them. Stars on the ZAMS are mainly composed of hydrogen. The number density ratio of oxygen to hydrogen in the gas is commonly expressed as
\log_{10}
[O/H]+12, namely solar abundance
(
Neijssel et al. 2019
)
. The assumed solar abundances influence conversions to metallicity. It is important to take into account binaries in low-metallicity environments in the current study of GW sources.

According to current theories
(
Haemmerlé et al. 2020
;
Klessen and Glover 2023
)
, 1st Gen stars differ from current stars as they were formed in extremely metal-poor primordial gas and were cooled by molecular hydrogen. To study the dependence of a stellar system on metallicity, we propose an empirical model of the M-Z relation. Our model is developed based on the redshift of the stellar cluster and the mass of each object. The metallicity of an object depends on its mass in the following way: higher mass objects have lower metallicity and vice versa
(
Langer and Norman 2006
)
. In principle, as we move towards higher redshifts the metallicity of an object decreases
(
Ma et al. 2015
)
. For simplicity, we assume the M-Z relation follows the functional form,

\log_{10}\bigg(\frac{Z}{Z_{\odot}}\bigg)=0.5\log_{10}\bigg(\frac{m_{\odot}}{m}\bigg)+0.67\exp{(-0.5z)}

(1)

where
m
is the mass of the stellar object and
m_{\odot}
is the solar mass.
Since stellar clusters are located at a fixed redshift, the contribution from the second term is a constant.

To implement the low metallicity condition in the Pop III stars we set an upper bound on metallicity as
Z_{max}\leq 0.01
. For various combinations of mass and redshift, metallicity can often surpass the considered threshold
Z_{max}
. Studies on the formation and evolution of Pop III stars suggest that these stars predominantly formed at high redshifts. Nevertheless, instances of Pop III star formation have also been identified at lower redshifts, such as
z\approx 3

(
Schaerer, D. 2003
;
Nakajima et al. 2023
)
. At this redshift, any stellar mass exceeding approximately 7.97 M
⊙
will maintain a metallicity below
Z_{max}
.
According to
(
Hopkins and Beacom 2006
)
, if a star evolves to become a CO, the initial stellar mass should be
\geq
8 M
⊙
. And since we are considering only BBH and NS-BH in our work, we filter out initial mass
<8M_{\odot}
and it perfectly compliments with our previous inference about redshift and mass. Therefore we adopt the M-Z relation in Eq.

IV
Conclusion and discussion

In this study, we investigated the evolution of 1st Gen COs originating from ZAMS. We proposed to have individual metallicities for each object in a binary based on a simple M-Z relation. We extended COSMIC to allow for individual metallicities for each star.
Comparing our data with a simulation with a constant metallicity of
0.05
, we make the following assessments:

•

The number of inspiralling binaries is significantly higher in the case of individual metallicities following our M-Z model.

•

The final mass distribution of compact stellar remnants from the simulation with individual metallicities exhibits much higher concordance with observational data.

Observations of binaries containing a large BH accreting from a Wolf-Rayet star and BPS show that the formation rate of binaries containing BHs significantly increases with decreasing metallicity. Binary CO systems comprising double BHs are most commonly found in environments with low metallicity
(
Belczynski et al. 2016
;
Giacobbo and Mapelli 2018
)
.
The larger number of BH and NS in our data can therefore be attributed to smaller values of metallicity set by the proposed M-Z relation.
In COSMIC, it is possible to assign an even lower constant metallicity to the entire population. This could potentially lead to an increase in the number of BH and NS compared to our current simulations, which follow the M-Z relation. However, metallicity is crucial in determining the evolutionary path of stars and binaries. Setting an abnormally low metallicity for certain stars might produce unrealistic results, potentially affecting the overall accuracy of the aggregate outcomes.
This is currently beyond the scope of our work.

The comparison of the characteristic strain from our catalogue with the projected sensitivities of advanced GW detectors shows that the predicted signal strength is beyond the current detection range of both LISA and LGWA.

Catalogues generated from the popsynth code, which provide comprehensive stellar populations rather than simple distributions, can be valuable resources for studying the stochastic gravitational wave background of clusters. With the advancement of observational data, BPS codes will become essential for conducting precise and detailed analyses of the stochastic gravitational wave background. Currently, the code does not account for evolving metallicity throughout stellar evolution. Introducing metallicity evolution alongside the progression from ZAMS stars to COs in population synthesis models is necessary to further enhance the accuracy of simulating realistic outcomes of stellar evolution and binary interactions.

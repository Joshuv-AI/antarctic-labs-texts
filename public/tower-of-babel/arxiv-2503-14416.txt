Abstract
. The manuscript at hand systematically studies Künneth formulas at a categorical level. We give criteria for an abstract six functor formalism to satisfy the categorical Künneth formula, and use this to formulate conjectures for categories of étale motives. As supporting evidence for these conjectures, we prove categorical Künneth formulas for adic sheaves and for cohomological motives, i.e., étale motives modulo the kernel of the adic realization.



1.
Introduction

Given two geometric objects
X
and
Y
it is natural to investigate how invariants of their product
X\times Y
are related to the ones on the factors.
For example, for two complex algebraic varieties
X
and
Y
the
Künneth formula
gives a canonical isomorphism of their Betti cohomology groups

{\rm H}^{n}(X\times Y,{\mathbb{Q}})=\bigoplus_{i+j=n}{\rm H}^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\rm H}^{j}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}}).

(1.1)

In other words, every cohomology class on the product can be written as a linear combination of cohomology classes on the factors.
Similar Künneth formulas also hold true for (algebraic) de Rham cohomology of smooth varieties over fields of characteristic
0
and
\ell
-adic cohomology over algebraically closed fields of characteristic different from
\ell
.

Motivic cohomology, however, fails to satisfy a Künneth formula as in (
1.1
).
Indeed, for a fixed algebraic variety
X
, Totaro has shown that the Künneth formula holds for its rationalized higher Chow groups and all smooth proper varieties
Y
if and only if
X
is mixed Tate
[
Tot16
, Theorems 4.1, 7.2]
.
The only smooth proper connected curve
X
over an algebraically closed field that satisfies this is
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

We propose to sidestep this failure by considering a Künneth formula at the level of the category of motives, as opposed to the level of individual cohomology groups (Conjectures
and
).
This approach is inspired by categorical Künneth formulas for, say, derived categories of quasi-coherent sheaves or
D
-modules
[
Lur17
,
BZFN10
,
DG13
]
.
From this perspective, the present manuscript investigates the extent to which these formulas remain valid for categories of motives.

Our approach builds on a systematic study of the categorical Künneth formula for an abstract
6
-functor formalism (Section
3
).
The main results establish such formulas for ind-dualizable cohomological motives (?THM?
) and their variants for Weil objects (?THM?
).

1.1.
A conjectural Künneth formula for étale motives

Let
k
be a field of finite Galois cohomological dimension, e.g., a separably closed field, a finite field or an unordered number field.
For any algebraic
k
-variety
X
, we denote by
\mathrm{DA}(X)
the category of étale motives from
[
Ayo14
,
CD16
]
.
Briefly, this category is constructed from
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant sheaves on the smooth-étale site of
X
by inverting the Tate twist.
After rationalization, Chow groups may be computed as homomorphism groups in
\mathrm{DA}(X)
when
X
is regular.
Étale motives come equipped with a
6
-functor formalism.

Taking advantage of its natural
\infty
-categorical enhancement,
\mathrm{DA}(X)
is a module over the presentably symmetric monoidal category
\mathrm{DA}(k)
(Section
4
).
We can thus form the relative Lurie tensor product (Section
2.1
), and consider the
exterior product functor

\mathrm{DA}(X)\otimes_{\mathrm{DA}(k)}\mathrm{DA}(Y)\rightarrow\mathrm{DA}(X\times_{k}Y)

(1.2)

induced from the map
(M,N)\mapsto M\boxtimes N
for objects
M\in\mathrm{DA}(X),N\in\mathrm{DA}(Y)
.
Here, we denote by

M\boxtimes N:={\rm pr}_{X}^{*}M\otimes{\rm pr}_{Y}^{*}N\in\mathrm{DA}(X\times_{k}Y)

the exterior product where
{\rm pr}_{X}
and
{\rm pr}_{Y}
denote the projections from
X\times_{k}Y
to
X
and
Y
, respectively.

Proposition 1.1

(?THM?
)
.

The functor (
1.2
) is fully faithful.

This is a consequence of the Künneth formula for
M\boxtimes N
due to Jin–Yang
[
JY21
]
and the computation of enriched maps in Lurie tensor products (?THM?
).
The latter uses compact, more precisely, atomic generation of these categories which is ensured by the Galois cohomological assumption on
k
.
A similar statement (?THM?
) holds for the stable
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-homotopy category
\mathrm{SH}
in place of
\mathrm{DA}
(for general fields
k
), and also for categories of adic sheaves
\widehat{\mathrm{D}}
and of cohomological motives
\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}
considered in Section
1.2
.

The
categorical Künneth formula
asks, in addition to full faithfulness, for the essential surjectivity of the exterior product functor, i.e., whether any étale motive on the product variety
X\times_{k}Y
can be decomposed as a colimit of motives coming from the factors
X
and
Y
:

Conjecture 1.2

(?THM?
)
.

Assume
Y
is smooth over
k
and, in addition, one of the following:

(1)

\mathop{\rm char}k=0
, or

(2)

Y
is proper over
k
.

Then, the exterior product functor (
1.2
) induces an equivalence:

\mathrm{DA}(X)^{\mathrm{ind\textrm{-}dbl}}\otimes_{\mathrm{DA}(k)}\mathrm{DA}(Y)^{\mathrm{ind\textrm{-}dbl}}\mathrel{{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits}}\mathrm{DA}(X\times_{k}Y)^{\mathrm{ind\textrm{-}dbl}},

where the superscript
\mathrm{ind\textrm{-}dbl}
(=ind-dualizable) denotes the subcategories generated under colimits by dualizable objects.

The restriction to ind-dualizable objects is analogous to the passage from constructible sheaves to local systems.
It seems necessary since objects with diagonal support generally do not lie in the essential image of (
1.2
).

Our approach towards the above conjecture is based on a construction due to Gaitsgory–Kazhdan–Rozenblyum–Varshavsky
[
GKRV20
, A.5.7]
.
We prove that the above conjecture for
X
and
Y
will be implied by the following two conditions (?THM?
):

(a)

For any
closed
point
x\in X
, the pullback functor

(x\times{\rm id}_{Y})^{*}\colon\mathrm{DA}(X\times_{k}Y)^{\mathrm{ind\textrm{-}dbl}}\rightarrow\mathrm{DA}(x\times_{k}Y)

is conservative if
X
is connected.

(b)

The pushforward
{\rm pr}_{X,*}\colon\mathrm{DA}(X\times_{k}Y)\rightarrow\mathrm{DA}(X)
along the projection
X\times_{k}Y\rightarrow X
preserves dualizable objects.

Colloquially speaking, (a) is asking for
\mathrm{DA}(-)^{\mathrm{ind\textrm{-}dbl}}
to satisfy a Tannakian formalism with pullbacks to closed points serving as fiber functors.
This conservativity is implied by a strong form of the conservativity of the motivic nearby cycles functor
conjectured by Ayoub
[
Ayo07b
, Conjecture 5.1]
.
For étale
torsion
motives (equivalently, étale torsion sheaves), it holds by classical theory.

Condition (b) holds if
Y
is smooth and proper over
k
, which explains the appearance of Condition (
2
) in ?THM?
.
In sync with Condition (
1
) there, we prove that for
Y
smooth but not necessarily proper, and
\mathop{\rm char}k=0
, dualizable étale
torsion
motives are preserved under pushforward (?THM?
).
For
X=Y={\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{k}
and
\mathop{\rm char}k>0
, Condition (b) is not satisfied (?THM?
).
This problem can be fixed by considering Weil objects (Section
1.3
).

Conditions (
1
) and (
2
) in ?THM?
match the conditions in
[
SGA03
]
under which the map of étale fundamental groups

\pi_{1}(X\times_{k}Y)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(X)\times\pi_{1}(Y)

is an isomorphism, with
k
algebraically closed.

1.2.
The Künneth formula for cohomological motives and adic sheaves

As supporting evidence for ?THM?
,
we prove the categorical Künneth formula for adic sheaves
\widehat{\mathrm{D}}
and cohomological motives
\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}
in place of
\mathrm{DA}
.
For any
k
-variety
X
, denote by
\widehat{\mathrm{D}}(X)
the profinite completion of
\mathrm{DA}(X)
.
As explained in Section
5
, this agrees with more classical versions of categories of adic sheaves by the rigidity theorem.
(Further completion at some prime
\ell\neq\mathop{\rm char}k
recovers the category of
\ell
-adic sheaves.)

The category comes equipped with an
adic realization functor

\rho\colon\mathrm{DA}(X)\rightarrow\widehat{\mathrm{D}}(X),

given on compact objects by
\rho(M)=\lim_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}}M/n
.
The following is analogous to a construction due to Ayoub
[
Ayo17
]
, who used the Betti realization instead.

Definition 1.3
.

The category of
cohomological motives

\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(X)
is the Verdier quotient of
\mathrm{DA}(X)
by the subcategory of objects
M\in\mathrm{DA}(X)
with
\rho(M)\simeq 0
.

By construction, the category of cohomological étale motives
\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(X)
fits into a diagram

\textstyle{\mathrm{DA}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho}
\scriptstyle{(-)^{\flat}}
\textstyle{\widehat{\mathrm{D}}(X)}
\textstyle{\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces,}
\scriptstyle{\rho^{\flat}}

where
(-)^{\flat}
is a localization and
\rho^{\flat}
is conservative.
Thus, some
M\in\mathrm{DA}(X)
is zero in
\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(X)
precisely if and only if
\rho(M)\simeq 0
.

Theorem 1.4

(Section
6.1
)
.

The assignment
X\mapsto\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(X)
is part of a
6
-functor formalism satisfying the properties familiar for
\mathrm{DA}
or
\widehat{\mathrm{D}}
, including excision and relative purity.
In addition, each category
\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(X)
is dualizable as a module over
\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(k)
.

In comparison to these good abstract properties, we have little control on the kernel of
\rho
and thus on the morphism groups in
\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(X)
.
Even though
\rho
preserves compact objects, we only know that
\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(X)
is
\omega_{1}
-compactly generated.
In particular, we do not know whether
\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(k)
is compactly generated, nor whether it is dualizable as a module over spectra.
However, the above-mentioned dualizability of
\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(X)
over
\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(k)
, which we prove using Ramzi’s work on dualizable categories
[
Ram24
]
, can be seen as a replacement for an absolute dualizability property.

Of course, whenever
\rho
is conservative, as one might conjecture when
k
is of finite transcendence degree over its prime field
[
Ayo17
]
, we will have
\mathrm{DA}(X)=\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(X)
.
For an application towards ?THM?
, a weaker conjecture suffices, namely conservativity of
\rho
when restricted to ind-dualizable and compact objects
[
Ayo23
]
.

Theorem 1.5

(?THM?
)
.

Under either of the assumptions in ?THM?
, ind-dualizable cohomological motives satisfy the categorical Künneth formula, i.e., the exterior product functor induces an equivalence:

\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(X)^{\mathrm{ind\textrm{-}dbl}}\otimes_{\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(k)}\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(Y)^{\mathrm{ind\textrm{-}dbl}}\mathrel{{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits}}\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(X\times_{k}Y)^{\mathrm{ind\textrm{-}dbl}}.

A similar equivalence holds for ind-dualizable adic sheaves
\widehat{\mathrm{D}}(-)^{\mathrm{ind\textrm{-}dbl}}
in place of
\mathrm{DA}(-)^{\mathrm{ind\textrm{-}dbl}}
(?THM?
).

1.3.
The case of Weil motives

For the remainder of the introduction, let
k={{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
be a finite field of characteristic
p>0
and cardinality
q
, and fix an algebraic closure
\bar{k}
.
For any
k
-variety
X
denote by
\overline{X}:=X\times_{k}\bar{k}
its base change equipped with the
\bar{k}
-endomorphism
\varphi_{X}:={\operatorname{Frob}}_{X}\times{\rm id}_{\bar{k}}
where
{\operatorname{Frob}}_{X}
is the
q
-Frobenius on
X
.

We define the category of
étale Weil motives

\mathrm{DA}(X^{W})
to be the ind-completion of the categorical fixed points of the automorphism
\varphi_{X}^{*}
on the compact objects
\mathrm{DA}(\overline{X})^{\omega}
.
Compact objects in this category are pairs of objects
M\in\mathrm{DA}(\overline{X})
with an isomorphism
\varphi_{X}^{*}M\cong M
.
The category
\mathrm{DA}(X^{W}\times_{\bar{k}}Y^{W})
is defined similarly, using two commuting isomorphisms for
\varphi_{X}^{*}
and
\varphi_{Y}^{*}
.
In Section
7
, we show that the assignment
X\mapsto\mathrm{DA}(X^{W})
upgrades to a
6
-functor formalism, linearly over
\mathrm{DA}(\bar{k})
.

Conjecture 1.6

(?THM?
)
.

For any
k
-varieties
X
and
Y
, the exterior product functor induces an equivalence on the categories of étale Weil motives:

\mathrm{DA}(X^{\mathrm{W}})\otimes_{\mathrm{DA}(\bar{k})}\mathrm{DA}(Y^{W})\mathrel{{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits}}\mathrm{DA}(X^{W}\times_{\bar{k}}Y^{W}).

Again, our evidence is a result for cohomological Weil motives, constructed in analogy to
\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(X)
by passing to the Verdier quotient by the kernel of the adic realization.

Theorem 1.7

(?THM?
)
.

Cohomological Weil motives satisfy the categorical Künneth formula, i.e., for any
k
-varieties
X
and
Y
the exterior product functor induces an equivalence:

\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(X^{W})\otimes_{\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(\bar{k})}\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(Y^{W})\mathrel{{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits}}\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}(X^{W}\times_{\bar{k}}Y^{W}).

This result, which also holds for categories of adic sheaves
\widehat{\mathrm{D}}
in place of
\mathrm{DA}^{\flat}
(cf. also
[
HRS24
]
), potentially allows to decompose the intersection motive of the moduli stack of shtukas, constructed motivically in
[
RS20
]
, as a cohomological motive.

2.
Preliminaries on
\infty
-categories

The categorical Künneth formula for cohomological motives in Theorems
and
involves a tensor product over a symmetric monoidal category generated by dualizable, but not necessarily compact objects.
In this section, we provide a tool to compute enriched mapping objects in such tensor products (?THM?
) by drawing on Ramzi’s work on dualizable categories
[
Ram24
]
.
We also study how to enforce a functor to be conservative (Section
2.6
).

2.1.
Notation

The category of presentable
\infty
-categories with colimit-preserving (or, continuous) functors
\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}
is closed symmetric monoidal with respect to the tensor product
\otimes
defined in
[
Lur17
, Section 4.8]
.

Let
\mathop{\rm CAlg}\nolimits(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}})
be the category of commutative algebra objects in
\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}
, i.e., presentably symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories with colimit-preserving, symmetric monoidal functors.
For
A\in\mathop{\rm CAlg}\nolimits(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}})
, we denote by
\mathrm{Mod}_{A}(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}})
the category of
A
-modules in
\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}
.
It carries a symmetric monoidal structure
\otimes_{A}
with unit
A
.
The tensor product
C\otimes_{A}D\in\mathrm{Mod}_{A}(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}})
is computed from the tensor product in
\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}
using a two-sided bar construction
[
Lur17
, Theorem 4.4.2.8]
.
The forgetful functor
\mathrm{Mod}_{A}(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}})\to\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}
is lax symmetric monoidal, and preserves limits and colimits
[
Lur17
, Corollaries 4.2.3.3, 4.2.3.5]
.

Recall from
[
Lur17
, §4]
that an object
x
in a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category is called
dualizable
if there is another object
x^{\vee}
and so-called
coevaluation
and
evaluation
maps

\mathop{\rm coev}\nolimits:1\rightarrow x\otimes x^{\vee},\;\;{\operatorname{ev}}:x\otimes x^{\vee}\rightarrow 1

satisfying the triangle identities.

2.2.
Enriched maps

For an
\infty
-category
C
, we denote by

\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{C}\colon C^{\rm op}\times C\rightarrow\mathrm{Ani}

(2.1)

the mapping functor, i.e.,
\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{C}(c,c^{\prime})
is the anima of maps (or, mapping space) for objects
c,c^{\prime}\in C
.
For
A\in\mathop{\rm CAlg}\nolimits(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}})
and
C\in\mathrm{Mod}_{A}(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}})
, we have the
A
-enriched mapping functor

\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{C/A}\colon C^{\rm op}\times C\rightarrow A.

(2.2)

By definition,
\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{A}(-,\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{C/A}(c,c^{\prime}))=\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{C}(-\otimes c,c^{\prime})
as functors
A^{\rm op}\rightarrow\mathrm{Ani}
.
Note that
\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{C/A}(c,c^{\prime})
exists by the adjoint functor theorem and that (
2.2
) constitutes a functor by
[
Lan21
, Proposition 5.1.10]
.

Example 2.1
.

The enriched mapping functor interpolates between the following two extreme cases:

(1)

For anima
A=\mathrm{Ani}
, we have
\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{C/\mathrm{Ani}}=\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{C}
as in (
2.1
). For spectra
A=\mathrm{Spt}
, we get the mapping spectrum, so
\pi_{0}\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{C/\mathrm{Spt}}
is the set of morphisms in the homotopy category
\mathrm{Ho}(C)
, which is a triangulated category.

(2)

If
C=A
, then
\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{A/A}=\underline{\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits}_{A}
, i.e., the inner hom in
A
.

Lemma 2.2
.

A functor
f\colon C\to D
in
\mathrm{Mod}_{A}(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}})
is fully faithful if and only if the induced map on
A
-enriched maps

\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{C/A}(c,c^{\prime})\to\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{D/A}(f(c),f(c^{\prime}))

(2.3)

is an isomorphism in
A
for all
c,c^{\prime}\in C
.

Proof.

The map (
2.3
) exists by
A
-linearity of
f
.
If (
2.3
) is an isomorphism (in
A
), we can apply
\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{A}(1_{A},-)
and recover the mapping spaces, so
f
is fully faithful.
The converse follows from applying the Yoneda embedding
A^{\rm op}\to\mathrm{Fun}(A,\mathrm{Ani}),a\mapsto\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{A}(a,-)
to the mapping anima and using the
A
-linearity of
f
.
∎

2.3.
Internal left adjoints

Throughout this subsection, let
A\in\mathop{\rm CAlg}\nolimits(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}})
and
C\in\mathrm{Mod}_{A}(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}})
.
In particular, the action functor
A\times C\rightarrow C
,
(a,c)\mapsto a\otimes c
preserves colimits in both variables.

Definition 2.3
.

A functor
f\colon C\to D
in
\mathrm{Mod}_{A}(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}})
is
internally left adjoint
if its right adjoint
f^{R}
(which a priori is only lax
A
-linear and need not preserve colimits) lies in
\mathrm{Mod}_{A}(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}})
as well.

The following notion is used in work of Ben Moshe
[
BM24
, Section 5]
and Ramzi
[
Ram24
, Section 1.2]
.
We will use it to control enriched mapping objects in tensor products (?THM?
).

Definition 2.4
.

An object
c\in C
is called
A
-atomic
(or simply
atomic
) if the functor
A\rightarrow C,a\mapsto a\otimes c
is internally left adjoint, i.e., if
\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{C/A}(c,-):C\rightarrow A
is
A
-linear and preserves colimits.
Denote by
C^{\textup{at}/A}\subset C
(or simply
C^{\textup{at}}
) the full subcategory of atomic objects, which is a small category
[
Ram24
, Corollary 1.26]
.

In addition,
C
is
atomically generated by
C_{0}\subset C^{\textup{at}}
if the smallest
A
-submodule of
C
that is closed under colimits and contains
C_{0}
is
C
itself.

Example 2.5
.

The notion interpolates between the following cases:

(1)

For spectra
A=\mathrm{Spt}
, the atomic objects in
C
are exactly the compact objects
[
Ram24
, Example 1.24]
(see ?THM?
for a generalization).

(2)

If
C=A
, the atomic objects in
A
are exactly the dualizable objects
[
Ram24
, Example 1.23]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
f\colon C\rightarrow D
be a functor in
\mathrm{Mod}_{A}(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}})
.
If
f
is internally left adjoint, then
f
preserves atomic objects.
Conversely, if
C
is atomically generated by
C_{0}\subset C^{\textup{at}}
and
f(C_{0})\subset D^{\textup{at}}
, then
f
is internally left adjoint.
In this case,
f
is fully faithful if and only if for all objects
c,c^{\prime}\in C_{0}
:

\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{C/A}(c,c^{\prime})\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{D/A}(f(c),f(c^{\prime}))

(2.4)

Proof.

The characterization of internal left adjoints is
[
Ram24
, Corollary 1.31]
.
Further, the unit of the adjunction
{\rm id}_{C}\rightarrow f^{R}\circ f
is an equivalence if its restriction to
C_{0}
is so.
Then (
2.4
) is an equivalence for all
c,c^{\prime}\in C
, which is equivalent to the full faithfulness of
f
by ?THM?
.
∎

Under additional assumptions on
A
and
C
there is a close relation between atomic and compact objects:

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose
A
is stable and generated under colimits by dualizable objects.
Then, the following hold:

(1)

An object
c\in C
is atomic if and only if
\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{C/A}(c,-)\colon C\rightarrow A
preserves filtered colimits (equivalently, direct sums).

(2)

If
A
is compactly generated and the action
A\times C\rightarrow C
preserves compact objects, then every compact object in
C
is atomic.

In particular, if
A
is locally rigid
[
Lur18
, Definition D.7.4.1]
and
A\times C\rightarrow C
preserves compact objects, then
C^{\textup{at}}=C^{\omega}
, i.e., atomic objects are exactly the compact ones.

Proof.

Locally rigid categories are compactly generated and the monoidal unit
1\in A
is compact.
The latter implies that atomic objects are compact.
So, the final statement follows from Item (
2
).

Item (
1
), “if”:
Since
f=-\otimes c
is
A
-linear, its right adjoint
f^{R}=\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{C/A}(c,-)
is lax
A
-linear.
It is exact by stability of
A
(hence of
C
) and so preserves all colimits if it preserves filtered ones (equivalently, direct sums by stability).
In order to check the
A
-linearity of
f^{R}
it suffices that the map

a\otimes f^{R}(c^{\prime})\rightarrow f^{R}(a\otimes c^{\prime})

is an isomorphism for all
a\in A
,
c^{\prime}\in C
.
As both sides preserve colimits in
a
we may assume
a
is dualizable by assumption on
A
.
Applying
\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{A}(b,-)
,
b\in A
and using
A
-linearity
f(a^{\vee}\otimes b)=a^{\vee}\otimes f(b)
shows that the map is an isomorphism.
So,
f=-\otimes c
is internally left adjoint, i.e.,
c
is atomic.

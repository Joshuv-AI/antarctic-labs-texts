Introduction

Nordhaus and Gaddum
[
18
]
proved that if
G
is a graph of
order
n
then
2\sqrt{n}\leq\chi(G)+\chi(\overline{G})\leq n+1
and
n\leq\chi(G)\cdot\chi(\overline{G})\leq(n+1)^{2}\!/4
.
This theorem was the starting point of a reach theory of extremal results
of the following form, called Nordhaus–Gaddum-type theorems.

A
k
-decomposition
of a graph
G
is a partition of its edge set
with
k
spanning subgraphs

G_{1},\dots,G_{k}
.
For a graph parameter
{\mathfrak{p}}
, this track of research investigates:

(a)

\max\,\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\mathfrak{p}}(G_{i})\}
,

(b)

\min\,\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\mathfrak{p}}(G_{i})\}
,

(c)

\max\,\{\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\mathfrak{p}}(G_{i})\}
,

(d)

\min\,\{\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\mathfrak{p}}(G_{i})\}
,

where the maxima and minima are taken over all k-decompositions
of
G
.
Analogous functions have been studied in other combinatorial structures, too.

Nordhaus and Gaddum considered the case
k=2
for the parameter
{\mathfrak{p}}(G)=\chi(G)
, the chromatic number.
Many other graphical parameters were studied in this context since then
(more than 2000 articles in google scholar).
We refer the reader to the survey
[
2
]
.

Also, there is an evolving literature where Nordhaus-Gaddum type theorems
are proved for
k\geq 3
; see, for example,

[
1
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
19
]
.

In this paper we concentrate on a problem of type
(a)
, and consider the
extremal function

M(k,n):=\max\,\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{Mad}(G_{i})\}

taken over all
k
-decompositions of the complete graph
K_{n}
,
where
\mathrm{Mad}(G)=\max\{2e(H)/|H|:H\subseteq G\}=\max\{d(H):H\subseteq G\}
, called the maximum average degree of
G
.

Somewhat surprisingly, we were unable to find any paper concerning
Nordhaus–Gaddum-type results for the parameter
\mathrm{Mad}(G)
, despite
that it is a parameter used frequently in determining the
arboricity of graphs and also in many coloring problems.
See, e.g.,
[
6
]
,
[
17
]
.

The value
\mathrm{Mad}(G)+1
dominates or is closely related to many other parameters,
including the clique number
\omega(G)
, the
choice number or list chromatic number

\mathrm{ch}(G)
, and the Szekeres–Wilf number—also called coloring
number—denoted
\mathrm{col}(G)
, which is just one greater than the
degeneracy of
G
.
Hence
M(k,n)
serves as an upper bound for the corresponding
Nordhaus–Gaddum-type theorems for these parameters.
We shall use this fact to
comment on earlier results obtained in
[
11
]
.

To deal with
M(k,n)
, we also introduce a List variant of
Nordhaus–Gaddum as follows.
A list
\mathsf{L}
of k graphs is a multiset,

\mathsf{L}=\{G_{1},\dots,G_{k}\}
, where repetitions are allowed.
For a positive integer
N
define:

M^{\mathsf{L}}(k,N)=\max\{\sum\mathrm{Mad}(G_{j})\}
taken over all
lists
\mathsf{L}
of
k
graphs such that
\sum e(G_{j})=N
.

Clearly
M(k,n)\leq M^{\mathsf{L}}(k,\binom{n}{2})
.
Hence if there is a list of
k
graphs realizing
M^{\mathsf{L}}(k,\binom{n}{2})

and this list can also be realized as a decomposition of
K_{n}
, then
the the inequality
M(k,n)\leq M^{\mathsf{L}}(k,\binom{n}{2})
turns to equality.
This idea can be adopted for other Nordhaus–Gaddum-type results
with distinct parameters.

Some simple properties of Mad(
G
)

We introduce the following term, which will simplify some arguments.

Definition 1
.

(Free edge.)
In a graph
G
, we call an
e\in E(G)
a free edge if
its removal does not modify the
\mathrm{Mad}
value; i.e.,

\mathrm{Mad}(G-e)=\mathrm{Mad}(G)
.

Proposition 2
.

(Monotonicity.)

For every
n
and
k
,

(i)

M(k,n+1)\geq M(k,n)+1
if
k\leq\binom{n}{2}
.

(ii)

M(k+1,n)\geq M(k,n)+1/3
if
k<\binom{n}{2}
.

Proof.

Let
G_{1},\dots,G_{k}
decompose
K_{n}
with
M(k,n)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{Mad}(G_{i})
, and let

V(K_{n+1})=V(K_{n})\cup\{w\}
.
Assume that
\mathrm{Mad}(G_{k})
is attained on a vertex set
X
, i.e.,

\mathrm{Mad}(G_{k})=2e(G_{k}[X])/|X|
.
Extend now
G_{k}
with vertex
w
and all edges
vw
where
v\in V(K_{n})
.
In the extended
G_{k}
the number of edges induced by
X\cup\{w\}
is

e(G_{k}[X])+|X|
, therefore the maximum average degree is increased to

\frac{2e(G_{k}[X])+2|X|}{|X|+1}=\frac{2e(G_{k}[X])+(|X|-1)}{|X|+1}+1\geq\frac{2e(G_{k}[X])}{|X|}+1

because
X
cannot induce more than
\binom{|X|}{2}
edges.
This proves
(i)
.

For
(ii)
, let

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{Mad}(G_{i})=M(k,n)
, and suppose that
e(G_{1})
is largest among the
e(G_{i})
.
Then
e(G_{1})>1
, hence the number
n_{1}
of non-isolated vertices
in
G_{1}
is at least 3.
If
G_{1}
has a free edge
e
, then taking
G_{k+1}=K_{2}
whose edge is
e

we increase the sum of the
\mathrm{Mad}(G_{i})
by 1.
If this is not the case, then
\mathrm{Mad}(G_{1})=2e(G_{1})/n_{1}
, each edge
contributing to
\mathrm{Mad}(G_{1})
with
2/n_{1}\leq 2/3
.
Thus, picking an arbitrary edge
e
and creating also here
G_{k+1}=K_{2}

whose edge is
e
,
\mathrm{Mad}(G_{1})-\mathrm{Mad}(G_{1}-e)\leq 2/3
holds, while

\mathrm{Mad}(G_{k+1})=1
.
So, this modification increases the sum of the
\mathrm{Mad}
values with at least 1/3.
∎

Comparison of the two parts of Theorem

provides us with

(n-1)^{2}\!/6

for which the increase of 1/3 given in

(ii)
above is tight.

Proposition 3
.

If
G
is not empty, then there is an induced connected
subgraph
H\subseteq G
realizing
\mathrm{Mad}(G)=d(H)
.

Proof.

Assume that
H\subseteq G
satisfies
d(H)=\mathrm{Mad}(G)
, and under
this condition
|V(H)|
is smallest.
It is clear that all edges induced by
V(H)
belong to
E(H)
.
Suppose for a contradiction that
H
is disconnected, say
H=H^{\prime}\cup H^{\prime\prime}

and there is no edge between
H^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime\prime}
.
We have
d(H^{\prime})=2e(H^{\prime})/|H^{\prime}|
,
d(H^{\prime\prime})=2e(H^{\prime\prime})/|H^{\prime\prime}|
, and

d(H)=2(e(H^{\prime})+e(H^{\prime\prime}))/(|H^{\prime}|+|H^{\prime\prime}|)
.
Assuming
d(H^{\prime})\leq d(H^{\prime\prime})
, and taking their weighted average
we clearly have

d(H^{\prime})\leq\frac{|H^{\prime}|}{|H^{\prime}|+|H^{\prime\prime}|}\,d(H^{\prime})+\frac{|H^{\prime\prime}|}{|H^{\prime}|+|H^{\prime\prime}|}\,d(H^{\prime\prime})\leq d(H^{\prime\prime})\,.

Observe that the expression in the middle is exactly
\mathrm{Mad}(H)
.
Thus,
H^{\prime\prime}
has fewer vertices than
H
has, while its
average degree is not smaller.
This contradicts the choice of
H
.
∎

Proposition 4
.

If
T
is a tree, then
\mathrm{Mad}(T)=\mathrm{ed}(T)=d(T)=2-2/|T|
.
More generally, if
G
is a maximal
\delta
-degenerate graph
on more than
\delta
vertices, then

\mathrm{Mad}(G)=\mathrm{ed}(G)=d(G)=2\delta-\frac{\delta(\delta+1)}{|G|}
.

Proof.

Assume that the subgraph
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
attains
d(G^{\prime})=\mathrm{Mad}(G)
, and

G^{\prime}
contains no free edges.
We apply the facts that a maximal
\delta
-degenerate graph
on
n
vertices has exactly

\delta n-\binom{\delta+1}{2}
edges, moreover every subgraph
of a
\delta
-degenerate graph is also
\delta
-degenerate.
In this way we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Mad}(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(G^{\prime})\ =\ \frac{2e(G^{\prime})}{|G^{\prime}|}\ \leq\ 2\delta-\frac{\delta(\delta+1)}{|G^{\prime}|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\delta-\frac{\delta(\delta+1)}{|G|}\ =\ d(G)\ =\ \mathrm{ed}(G)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Mad}(G)\,.

Thus, equality holds throughout.
It also follows that
G^{\prime}=G
.
∎

For lower-bound constructions for
M(k,n)
with fixed
k
, also the

Proposition 5
.

If
p>q>1
and
G
is the complete split graph
K_{p}-K_{q}
, then

\mathrm{Mad}(G)=\mathrm{ed}(G)=d(G)
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathrm{Mad}(G)
is attained on an induced subgraph
G^{\prime}
with
p^{\prime}

vertices,
q^{\prime}
of which belong to the
K_{q}
whose edges have been removed.
Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Mad}(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(G^{\prime})\ =\ \frac{2}{p^{\prime}}\,\Bigl(\binom{p^{\prime}}{2}-\binom{q^{\prime}}{2}\Bigr)\ =\ p^{\prime}-1-\frac{q^{\prime}(q^{\prime}-1)}{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p^{\prime}-q^{\prime})+(q^{\prime}-1)-(q^{\prime}-1)\,\frac{q^{\prime}}{(p^{\prime}-q^{\prime})+q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(p-q)+(q^{\prime}-1)\Bigl(1-\frac{q^{\prime}}{(p-q)+q^{\prime}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p-q)\Bigl(1+\frac{q^{\prime}-1}{q^{\prime}+(p-q)}\Bigr)\ \leq\ (p-q)\Bigl(1+\frac{q-1}{q+(p-q)}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p}\,\frac{(p-q)(p+q-1)}{2}\ =\ \frac{2}{p}\,\Bigl(\binom{p}{2}-\binom{q}{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(G)\ =\ \mathrm{ed}(G)\,.

The step applying the inequality
p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}\leq p-q
is valid because

p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}
is the number of vertices of
G
inside
V(K_{p})\diagdown V(K_{q})
,
so it cannot exceed
p-q
.
∎

Observation 6
.

Since
\mathrm{Mad}(G)\geq\mathrm{ed}(G)\geq d(G)=2e(G)/|V(G)|
holds for every graph

,
the following inequalities are immediately seen, where the maximum is
taken over all

\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},\dots,G_{k}\}

of

:

M(k,n)=\max_{\mathcal{G}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{Mad}(G_{i})\geq\max_{\mathcal{G}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{ed}(G_{i})\geq\max_{\mathcal{G}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}d(G_{i})\,.

Observation 7
.

To have
\mathrm{Mad}(G)=d(G)
for a graph
G
, also
\mathrm{Mad}(G)=\mathrm{ed}(G)
must hold.
This forces
\delta(G)\geq d(G)/2
, for otherwise the removal of a vertex

would yield

d(G-v)=2(e(G)-\delta(G))/(|V(G)|-1)>(2e(G)-d(G))/(|V(G)|-1)=2e(G)/|V(G)|=d(G)
.
Dense examples of graphs

\mathrm{Mad}(G)=d(G)
are obtained, e.g.,
by removing at most

edges from

.
Sparser examples are all regular graphs, and many more.

3
General estimates and constructions

In the first part of this section we determine the largest possible
value of
\mathrm{Mad}(G)
among graphs with a given number
m\geq 1

of edges; that is,

g(m):=\max\{\mathrm{Mad}(G)\mid e(G)=m\}\,.

As one may observe, with the notation defined in the introduction, we have

g(m)=M^{\mathsf{L}}(1,m)
.

In the second part of the section we describe a structure class of edge
decompositions, on which it would suffice to determine

\max\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{Mad}(G_{i})\}
in order to determine
M(k,n)
.

3.1
Single graph optimizing Mad(
G
)

Theorem 8
.

Let
p\geq 2
be an integer.

(i)

If
m=\binom{p}{2}
, then
g(m)=p-1
, with the
unique extremal graph
K_{p}
.

(ii)

If
\binom{p}{2}<m<\binom{p+1}{2}
, then

g(m)=\max\{p-1,2m/(p+1)\}
, attained by precisely the
following graphs
G
having
m

(a)

any
G

K_{p}\subset G
, if
0<r<\frac{p-1}{2}
;

(b)

any
G
with
|V(G)|=p+1
, if
\frac{p-1}{2}<r<p
;

(c)

both types (a) and (b), if

.

Proof.

Let
G
be any graph with
m

a set
of vertices inducing a subgraph

d(H)=\mathrm{Mad}(G)
.
If

, then

If

.

Both bounds are attained by graphs of

namely by

supplemented with arbitrarily positioned

further edges, and by all graphs with

p+1

m

∎

Definition 9
.

(Family of extremal (p,r)-graphs.)
For

(Representative.)
For

For any

3.2
Equivalent condition for
M
(
k
,
n
)

Concerning the next result, let us recall that the graphs
G_{1},\dots,G_{k}

attaining
M(k,n)=\mathrm{Mad}(G_{1})+\ldots+\mathrm{Mad}(G_{k})
do not necessarily decompose
K_{n}
,
some
e\in E(K_{n})
may not belong to any of those
G_{i}
.

Proposition 11
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
k\geq 2
.
Suppose that the packing of graphs
G_{1},\dots,G_{k}

optimzes
M(k,n)
.
Let
X_{j}\subset V(G_{j})
,
j=1,\dots,k
be a subset on which

\mathrm{Mad}(G_{j})
is realized, namely
2e(G_{j}[X_{j}])/|X_{j}|=\mathrm{Mad}(G_{j})
.

Assume
|X_{1}|\leq\dots\leq|X_{k}|
.
Then, taking
H_{1}
as the complete graph on
X_{1}
and
V(H_{j})=X_{j}
,

E(H_{j})=E(K_{n}[X_{j}])\diagdown(\,\cup_{i<j}E(H_{i}))
for
j\geq 2
,
also the graphs
H_{1},\dots,H_{k}
optimize
M(k,n)
such that

\mathrm{Mad}(H_{j})
is realized on
X_{j}
.

Proof.

To simplify notation, let us write
\mathcal{E}=E(G_{1})\cup\cdots\cup E(G_{k})

and
\mathcal{X}=\binom{X_{1}}{2}\cup\cdots\cup\binom{X_{k}}{2}
, both viewed as
subsets of
E(K_{n})
.

If
e\in E(K_{n})\diagdown\mathcal{X}
, then
e
plays no role in
M(k,n)
, because

\mathrm{Mad}(G_{j})=\mathrm{Mad}(G_{j}[X_{j}])
for every
j
.
Hence, we may assume
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
without loss of generality.
On the other hand, if
e\in\mathcal{X}\diagdown\mathcal{E}
held for an edge
e
, then there
would exist
j
with
e\in\binom{X_{j}}{2}
and re-defining

E(G_{j}):=E(G_{j})\cup\{e\}
would increase
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{Mad}(G_{i})
by
2/|X_{j}|
,
contradicting the assumption that
G_{1},\dots,G_{k}
realizes
M(k,n)
.
Thus
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
, consequently
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{E}
.

We will prove that the re-distribution of the edges belonging to
\mathcal{X}
by the rules
E(H_{1})=\binom{X_{1}}{2}
and

E(H_{j})=\binom{X_{j}}{2}\diagdown(\,\cup_{i<j}\binom{X_{i}}{2})
for
j\geq 2

yields
\mathrm{Mad}(H_{1})+\ldots+\mathrm{Mad}(H_{k})\geq\mathrm{Mad}(G_{1})+\ldots+\mathrm{Mad}(G_{k})
, which clearly implies that also

H_{1},\dots,H_{k}
optimize
M(k,n)
.
For this purpose the following facts can be observed for any

1\leq i<j\leq k
and any edge
e\in\binom{X_{i}}{2}\cap\binom{X_{j}}{2}
.

•

If
|X_{i}|<|X_{j}|
, then
e\in E(G_{j})
is impossible, because
in that case moving
e
from
G_{j}
to
G_{i}
would increase

\mathrm{Mad}(G_{i})+\mathrm{Mad}(G_{j})
with
2/|X_{i}|-2/|X_{j}|>0
, a contradiction to

\mathrm{Mad}(G_{1})+\ldots+\mathrm{Mad}(G_{k})=M(k,n)
.

•

If
|X_{i}|=|X_{j}|
, then for
e
it is irrelevant whether

e\in E(G_{i})
or
e\in E(G_{j})
, because in either case the
contribution of
e
to
\mathrm{Mad}(G_{i})+\mathrm{Mad}(G_{j})
is
2/|X_{i}|=2/|X_{j}|
.
(If moving
e
from
G_{j}
to
G_{i}
modifies
G_{j}
to a graph in which

X_{j}
does not represent
\mathrm{Mad}
anymore, it means that
\mathrm{Mad}(G_{1})+\ldots+\mathrm{Mad}(G_{k})

is improvable, contradicting that
G_{1},\dots,G_{k}
optimize
M(k,n)
.)

The first property implies that, already at the beginning, each
e\in\mathcal{X}

belongs to a
G_{j}
such that
|X_{j}|=\min\{|X_{i}|\mid i\in\mathcal{I}_{e}\}
.
And then, if this minimum is attained for more than one subscript
j
,
the second property yields that we are free to choose any such
j
,
specifically we can prescribe
j=\min\{i\in\mathcal{I}_{e}\}
for
e

because
|X_{1}|\leq\cdots\leq|X_{k}|
.
Applying this “shift to the left” (or First Fit) rule,

j=\min\{i\in\mathcal{I}_{e}\}
for all
e\in\mathcal{X}
exactly means
for the obtained graphs
H_{1},\dots,H_{k}
that
H_{1}
is the complete graph
on the vertex set
X_{1}
, and for every
j>1
the graph
H_{j}
contains
exactly those edges from
\binom{X_{j}}{2}
which do not occur in

H_{1}\cup\cdots\cup H_{j-1}
.
∎

It is also valid that the
\mathrm{Mad}
-realizing sets for an
optimal decomposition cover the entire
V(K_{n})
.

Proposition 12
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
k\geq 2
.
Suppose that the packing of graphs
G_{1},\dots,G_{k}
optimzes
M(k,n)
,
and the sets
X_{j}\subset V(G_{j})
for
j=1,\dots,k
realize

\mathrm{Mad}(G_{j})
, i.e.,
2e(G_{j}[X_{j}])/|X_{j}|=\mathrm{Mad}(G_{j})
.
Then
X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{k}=V(K_{n})
.

Proof.

Otherwise choose any vertex
v\in V(K_{n})\diagdown(X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{k})
.
The edges
vx\in E(K_{n})
for
x\in X_{1}
have no contribution to
\mathrm{Mad}(G_{1})+\ldots+\mathrm{Mad}(G_{k})
,
therefore we can replace
G_{1}
with the graph

H:=(X_{1}\cup\{v\},E(G_{1}[X_{1}]\cup\{vx\mid x\in X_{1}\})

to obtain a modified packing of
k
subgraphs in
K_{n}
.
Then, denoting
e_{1}=e(G_{1}[X_{1}])
and
x_{1}=|X_{1}|
, and observing that

e_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}(x_{1}^{2}-x_{1})
, we obtain

\mathrm{Mad}(H)-\mathrm{Mad}(G_{1})=\frac{2e_{1}+2x_{1}}{x_{1}+1}-\frac{2e_{1}}{x_{1}}=\frac{2x_{1}^{2}-2e_{1}}{x_{1}^{2}+x_{1}}\geq 1\,.

This contradicts the assumption that
\mathrm{Mad}(G_{1})+\ldots+\mathrm{Mad}(G_{k})
attains the maximum,
M(k,n)
.
∎

From Propositions
11
and
12
we also obtain:

Corollary 13
.

The determination of
M(k,n)
is equivalent to the following
optimization problem:

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{2e(G_{i})}{|X_{i}|}\qquad\longrightarrow\qquad\max

under the following conditions:

•

X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{k}=V(K_{n})
;

•

V(G_{i})=X_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,k
;

•

E(G_{j})=\binom{X_{j}}{2}\diagdown\,{\displaystyle\cup_{i<j}}\binom{X_{i}}{2}
.

An advantage of this formulation is that it admits an integer
programming approach, which may be efficient when
k
and
n
are
not too large.

3.3
Upper bound for
M
(
k
,
n
) from continuous relaxation

Lemma 14
.

Suppose
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}>0
are reals such that

\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}=\binom{n}{2}
, and let
y_{1},\dots,y_{k}>0
be
the reals with
y_{j}(y_{j}-1)/2=x_{j}
.
Then:

(1)

\max\,\{\,\sum_{j=1}^{k}(y_{j}-1)\}
is obtained when
all
x_{j}
are equal, namely
x_{j}=\binom{n}{2}/k

for
j=1,\dots,k
.

(2)

\max\,\{\,\sum_{j=1}^{k}(y_{j}-1)\}=(\sqrt{k^{2}+4kn^{2}-4kn}-k)/2<\sqrt{k}\,n
.

(3)

If
k=\binom{n}{2}/\binom{t}{2}
, then
y_{j}(y_{j}-1)/2=t(t-1)/2

and the maximum equals
k(t-1)=n(n-1)/t
.

Proof.

The definition of
y_{j}
yields
y_{j}^{2}-y_{j}-2x_{j}=0
, the positive
root is
y_{j}=\frac{1}{2}(1+\sqrt{8x_{j}+1}\,)
.
Since
a\sqrt{x}+b
is a concave function for
a>0
, the assertion of (1)
follows by Jensen’s inequality.

Plugging in
x=\binom{n}{2}/k
for all
x_{j}
, we obtain

y(y-1)/2=n(n-1)/2k
, hence
ky^{2}-ky-n^{2}+n=0

and
y=(k+\sqrt{k^{2}+4kn^{2}-4kn})/2k
for all
y_{j}
, therefore

\displaystyle k(y-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k((k+\sqrt{k^{2}+4kn^{2}-4kn}-2k)/2k

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sqrt{k^{2}+4kn^{2}-4kn}-k))/2

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\sqrt{k}\,n

since the last two lines are positive and eliminating the first square root by squaring we get
4kn^{2}+k^{2}-4kn\leq(2\sqrt{k}\,n+k)^{2}=4kn^{2}+4k^{3/2}\,n+k^{2}
, which is obviously true.
This proves (2).

Finally, (3) is an immediate consequence of (1).
∎

Remark 15
.

Literally the same computation yields:

M^{\mathsf{L}}(k,N)<\sqrt{2kN}\,.

It is worth noting that the jump of estimate in the last step yielding

in the proof of Lemma

is as large as

when

,
hence (2) cannot be within

to the optimum if

is
quadratic in

In the same flavour, the following real relaxation of

can also be stated.

Proposition 16
.

For every
m>0
,
g(m)\leq y-1
, where
y>0
is the real number
satisfying
y(y-1)/2=m
.

Proof.

Let

, where

and

are integers, and

.
We have seen that

If

because

, with equality if and only if

.

In the other case we have

∎

Corollary 17

M(k,n)\leq M^{\mathsf{L}}(k,N)<\sqrt{2kN}<\sqrt{k}\,n

In this section we consider the cases

4.1
Exact formula for
k
= 2

Theorem 18
.

If
G
is any graph of order
n\geq 3
, then

\mathrm{Mad}(G)+\mathrm{Mad}(\overline{G})\leq\begin{cases}\begin{tabular}[]{ll}$(5n^{2}-6n+1)/4n$&if\/ $n$ is odd,\\
$(5n^{2}-6n)/4n$&if\/ $n$ is even.\end{tabular}\end{cases}

Moreover, both bounds are tight, hence the above formulas give the
right value
M(2,n)=5n/4-3/2+\epsilon_{n}
, with
\epsilon_{n}=0
if
n

is even and
\epsilon_{n}=\frac{1}{4n}
if
n
is odd, for every

n\geq 3
.

Proof.

Let
X
and
Y
be subsets of
V(K_{n})
, such that
\mathrm{Mad}(G)=d(G[X])
and

\mathrm{Mad}(\overline{G})=d(\overline{G}[Y])
.
Say,
|X|=x
and
|Y|=y
.
We may assume
x\leq y
without loss of generality, as
we are free to switch between
G
and
\overline{G}
if necessary.
Also, from Corollary
12
applied for
k=2

(that is,
G_{1}=G
and
G_{2}=\overline{G}
),
we see that
\mathrm{Mad}(G)+\mathrm{Mad}(\overline{G})
can be largest only if

X\cup Y=V(K_{n})
, hence
x+y\geq n
and
|X\cap Y|=x+y-n
.
Moreover, by Proposition
11
, we may restrict
attention to the case
E(G[X])=\binom{X}{2}
and
E(\overline{G}[Y])=\binom{Y}{2}\diagdown\binom{X\cap Y}{2}
, whereas the edges (if any)
connecting
X\diagdown Y
with
Y\diagdown X
can be distributed arbitrarily
between
G
and
\overline{G}
and do not have any contribution to

\mathrm{Mad}(G)+\mathrm{Mad}(\overline{G})
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Mad}(G)+\mathrm{Mad}(\overline{G})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle x-1+\frac{y(y-1)-(x+y-n)(x+y-n-1)}{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2nx+2ny+x-x^{2}-y-xy-n^{2}-n}{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2n-1-x+\frac{2nx+x-x^{2}-n^{2}-n}{y}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2n-1-x+\frac{2nx+x-x^{2}-n^{2}-n}{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n-2+\frac{x(n+1-x)}{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n-2+\frac{1}{n}\left\lfloor\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor,

which is equivalent to the stated upper bound.
Tightness is shown by the graph
G=K_{\left\lfloor(n+1)/2\right\rfloor}
or

G=K_{\left\lceil(n+1)/2\right\rceil}
(which coincide if
n
is odd)
and its complement.
∎

4.2
Lower-bound constructions for small
k

In the following small cases of
k
we describe two constructions, which
are substantially different.
Although the corresponding sums of the
\mathrm{Mad}(G_{i})
are not exactly the
same, the differences are marginal.
However, there seems to be no smooth transition between the two
structures presented for the same
k
.
This fact may be viewed as an indication that the precise determination
of
M(k,n)
for these values of
k
may be not at all easy.

Proposition 19
.

There exist constants
c_{3},\dots,c_{7}
such that

•

M(3,n)\geq 3n/2-c_{3}
,

•

M(4,n)\geq 5n/3-c_{4}
,

•

M(5,n)\geq 11n/6-c_{5}
,

•

M(6,n)\geq 2n-c_{6}
,

•

M(7,n)\geq 7n/3-c_{7}
.

Proof.

Let us recall from Proposition
11
that there is a natural
two-way correspondence between
\mathrm{Mad}
-optimal edge decompositions

G_{1},\dots,G_{k}
of
K_{n}
and sequences
X_{1},\dots,X_{k}
of subsets of

V(K_{n})
arranged in increasing order of cardinalities.
More explicitly, the graphs
G_{j}
can be obtained by the recursive rule

E(G_{j})=\binom{X_{j}}{2}\diagdown(\,\cup_{i<j}\,E(G_{i})\,)
.

In each case of
k=3,4,5,6
we create two sequences
G_{1},\dots,G_{k}
.
In one of them we begin with three sets
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}
, mutually
disjont, of respective cardinalities
\left\lfloor n/3\right\rfloor\leq a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq a_{3}\leq\left\lceil n/3\right\rceil
, with
a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}=n
.
In the other, which is usually better with a marginal extent,
we begin with two disjont sets
B_{1},B_{2}

of cardinalities
|B_{1}|=b_{1}=\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor
and
|B_{2}|=b_{2}=\left\lceil n/2\right\rceil
.

k=3
:
Set
X_{1}=A_{1}\cup A_{2}
,
X_{2}=A_{1}\cup A_{3}
,
X_{3}=A_{2}\cup A_{3}
.
Then
G_{1}\cong K_{a_{1}+a_{2}}
,
G_{2}\cong K_{a_{1}+a_{3}}-K_{a_{1}}
,

G_{3}\cong K_{a_{2},a_{3}}
; hence
\mathrm{Mad}(G_{1})=a_{1}+a_{2}-1\sim 2n/3
,

\mathrm{Mad}(G_{2})\sim n/2
,
\mathrm{Mad}(G_{3})\sim n/3
, summing up to
3n/2

approximately.

Alternatively, set
X_{1}=B_{1}
,
X_{2}=B_{2}
,
X_{3}=B_{1}\cup B_{2}=V(K_{n})
.
Then
G_{1}\cong K_{\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor}
,

G_{2}\cong K_{\left\lceil n/2\right\rceil}
(vertex-disjoint), and

G_{3}\cong K_{\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor,\left\lceil n/2\right\rceil}
.
Here each
G_{i}
has average degree near to
n/2
.

k=4
:
Let
X_{i}=A_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
(hence
G_{i}\cong K_{a_{i}}
), and

X_{4}=V(K_{n})
(hence

).
Then
\mathrm{Mad}(G_{1})+\mathrm{Mad}(G_{2})+\mathrm{Mad}(G_{3})=n-3
, and
\mathrm{Mad}(G_{4})\sim 2n/3
.
Interestingly, the sets
A_{i}
also offer the solution

X_{1}=A_{1}
,
X_{2}=A_{1}\cup A_{2}
,
X_{3}=A_{1}\cup A_{3}
,
X_{4}=A_{2}\cup A_{3}
.
Then
G_{1}\cong K_{a_{1}}
,
G_{2}\cong K_{a_{1}+a_{2}}-K_{a_{1}}
,

G_{3}\cong K_{a_{1}+a_{3}}-K_{a_{1}}
,
G_{4}\cong K_{a_{2},a_{3}}
.
The corresponding respective average degrees are about
n/3
,
n/2
,

n/2
,
n/3
.

The other approach starts with
X_{1}=B_{1}
,
X_{2}=B_{2}
, i.e.,
G_{1}\cong K_{b_{1}}
and
G_{2}\cong K_{b_{2}}
, hence
\mathrm{Mad}(G_{1})+\mathrm{Mad}(G_{2})=n-2
.
Then split
X_{2}

X_{2}=Y\cup Y^{\prime}
,

,

The obtained graphs are

, each having its average

n/3
.

k=5
:
Let
X_{1}=A_{1}
,
X_{2}=A_{2}
,
X_{3}=A_{1}\cup A_{2}
,
X_{4}=A_{1}\cup A_{3}
,

.

G_{1}\cong K_{a_{1}}
,
G_{2}\cong K_{a_{2}}
,

,

,

.

n/3
,
n/3
,
n/3
,
n/2
, and
n/3
,
respectively.

Also here we can alternatively start with

,

,
hence generating

.
For the other three graphs we cut both

and

say

k=6
:

.

(Pairwise balanced K-designs with K =

Similarly as above, Theorem

It states that, for every positive integer

Putting

It remains to decompose the graph

Making a substantial improvement on

Theorem 29
.

(Fixed proportional growth of k.)

Let
r\geq 3
be any rational number.
There exist infinitely many
n
such that
M(k,n)

for

is determined by a

\{K_{p},K_{p+1}\}
-design, where

is the integer satisfying

\binom{p}{2}\leq r<\binom{p+1}{2}

Proof.

Let us note first that the case

is already settled in
part
(i)
of Theorem
25
.
Therefore, in the sequel we assume
\binom{p}{2}<r<\binom{p+1}{2}
.

We search for an edge decomposition of
K_{n}
into the members of the
multiset
\textstyle\{xk*K_{p}\,,(1-x)k*K_{p+1}\}
,
with a suitable choice of
x\in(0,1)
.
Since
e(K_{n})=\binom{n}{2}=rk
, this requires

xk\binom{p}{2}+(1-x)k\binom{p+1}{2}=rk\,,

thus

x=\frac{p+1}{2}-\frac{r}{p}\,.

Since the integer
p
is determnimned by
r
, we see that
x
is a
function of
r
, a rational number between 0 and 1 because

\frac{p-1}{2}<r<\frac{p+1}{2}
by the choice of
p
.
In particular,
x
is independent of the actual choice of the number
n

of vertices.

Assume
x=s/t
(
s,t\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq s\leq t-1
),
and let
H=sK_{p}\cup(t-s)K_{p+1}
.
Then
\delta(H)=p
and
\Delta(H)=p+1
.
Thus, Wilson’s theorem implies that

admits an

-decomposition
whenever

is a multiple of

and

is
sifficiently large.
Here

, hence

is decomposed into
\binom{n}{2}/(rt)

Consequently, a

-design is obtained with exactly

\frac{1}{r}\binom{n}{2}
subgraphs.
By the “moreover” part of Theorem

we obtain:

M(k,n)=\left(\frac{s\cdot(p-1)}{rt}+\frac{(t-s)\cdot p}{rt}\right)\!\binom{n}{2}=\frac{tp-s}{rt}\binom{n}{2},

implying the assertion.
∎

In particular, we obtain:

For any rational

there are infinitely many

Assuming

, and restrict the solutions to

is a multiple of

.

6.2
Substitution and asymptotic growth of

(

,

Here we first consider the cases of triangular numbers

Since

, the

Substantial improvement for large

Proposition 31
.

Let
k=\binom{t+1}{2}
for some
t\geq 2
, and assume
t\mid n
.
Then
M(k,n)\geq\frac{t+1}{2}\,n-t>\sqrt{k/2}\,(n-2)
.

Proof.

Partition
V(K_{n})
into
t
sets
V_{1},\dots,V_{t}
of cardinality
n/t
each.
Define
t+\binom{t}{2}
subgraphs indexed as
G_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq t

and
G_{i,j}
for
1\leq i<j\leq t
, as follows.
Each
G_{i}
is a copy of
K_{n/t}
with
V(G_{i})=V_{i}
, and each
G_{i,j}

is a copy of
K_{\frac{n}{t},\frac{n}{t}}
with
V(G_{i,j})=V_{i}\cup V_{j}
.
All these graphs are regular.
More explicitly, we have obtained
t
graphs of degree
n/t-1
, and

\frac{1}{2}t(t-1)
graphs of degree
n/t
.
Consequently,
M(k,n)\geq n-t+\frac{1}{2}(t-1)n
.
The last inequality is valid because
k<(t+1)^{2}\!/2
.
∎

Theorem 32
.

Let
v>r>2
be such that a Steiner system
S(2,r,v)
exists.
If
k=\frac{v(v-1)}{r(r-1)}
, and
n
is any integer divisible by
v
,
then
M(k,n)\geq\frac{v}{r}\left(n-1\right)
.

Proof.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B})
be an
S(2,r,v)
with point set
X
and blocks

B_{1},\dots,B_{k}
.
Replace the points
x\in X
with mutually disjoint sets
V_{x}
of
cardinality
n/v
.
Then, viewing
\cup_{x\in X}V_{x}
as the vertex set of
K_{n}
, we obtain

k

Y_{i}:=\cup_{x\in B_{i}}V_{x}
of cardinality
rn/v
.
For
i=1,\dots,k
let
H_{i}
be the complete
r
-partite graph whose edges
are the vertex pairs
y,y^{\prime}
where
y,y^{\prime}\in Y_{i}
, say
y\in V_{x}
and

y^{\prime}\in V_{x^{\prime}}
, such that
x\neq x^{\prime}
.
Distribute the remaining edges of
K_{n}
(i.e., those which are contained
in some

) arbitrarily among the

.
In this way we obtain an edge decomposition of

into
k

denote them by

.

The average number of edges in the

is
\frac{n(n-1)}{2k}
, and
each of them has order

; hence the average of their

is

Thus,
M(k,n)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{Mad}(G_{i})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}d(G_{i})=\frac{v}{r}\left(n-1\right)
.
∎

Corollary 33
.

Let
q
be a prime power.

(i)

If
k=q^{2}+q+1
and
k\mid n
, then

.

(ii)

If

and

, then
M(k,n)\geq qn-q
.

Proof.

Apply Theorem
32
to the projective planes

S(2,q+1,q^{2}+q+1)
and affine planes

, with

or

and

or

, respectively.
∎

Now we are in a position to prove that

as a function of

grows essentially with

.

Theorem 34
.

For every
n
and every fixed
k
we have

(1-\epsilon_{k})\sqrt{k}\,n<M(k,n)<\sqrt{k}\,n\,,

where
\epsilon_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.
In convergence,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\Bigl(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{M(k,n)}{\sqrt{k}\,n}\Bigr)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\Bigl(\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{M(k,n)}{\sqrt{k}\,n}\Bigr)=1\,.

Proof.

Recall that the upper bound
\sqrt{k}\,n
is valid by Corollary
17
.
Moreover, if
k=q^{2}+q+1
where
q
is a prime power and
n
is a
multiple of
k
, then the lover bound holds in a stronger form.
Namely, we can write

(\sqrt{k}-1/2)\,n-c_{k}

instead of

(1-\epsilon_{k})\sqrt{k}\,n
, choosing
q+\frac{1}{q+1}

(which is less than
\sqrt{k}
) for the constant

.
Indeed, from part
(i)
of Corollary
33
we obtain

M(k,n)+q+\frac{1}{q+1}\geq(q+\frac{1}{q+1})\,n>(\sqrt{q^{2}+q+1}-1/2)\,n=(\sqrt{k}-1/2)\,n

where the inequality in the middle can easily be checked.

For orders not divisible by
k
, let us consider
n^{\prime}=n+t=ks+t
, where

1\leq t\leq k-1
.
By monotonicity with respect to
n
(due to Proposition
2
) we know

\displaystyle M(k,n^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle M(k,n)\ >\ (\sqrt{k}-1/2)\,(n^{\prime}-s)-\sqrt{k}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\sqrt{k}-1/2)\,n^{\prime}-\sqrt{k}-(k-1)(\sqrt{k}-1/2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sqrt{k}-1/2)\,n^{\prime}-k^{3/2}+k/2-1/2\,.

Hence
k^{3/2}
is a suitable choice for

whenever
k=q^{2}+q+1
with any prime power
q
.

Suppose that
k
cannot be written in this form.
Let then
q
be the smallest prime power such that
q^{2}+q+1>k
.
It is well known that there exists a sublinear function
f(x)=o(x)
such
that the interval
[x-f(x),x]
contains a prime number for every
x>2
.
(The current record concerning the growth of

seems to be

,
see
[
3
]
.)
So, we can choose a prime (or prime power)

with
q-q^{\prime}=o(q)
.
Let
k^{\prime}=(q^{\prime})^{2}+q^{\prime}+1
; certainly

, moreover

and
\sqrt{k}-\sqrt{k^{\prime}}=o(\sqrt{k}\,)
as

Since

is monotone increasing in

, again by
Proposition

, we obtain:

M(k,n)>M(k^{\prime},n)>(\sqrt{k^{\prime}}-1/2)\,n-c_{k^{\prime}}\geq(\sqrt{k}-\epsilon_{k}\sqrt{k}\,)\,n

with a suitably chosen
\epsilon_{k}>0
.

For the limits
s_{k}:=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{M(k,n)}{\sqrt{k}\,n}

and
t_{k}:=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{M(k,n)}{\sqrt{k}\,n}
this yields

1-o_{k}(1)\leq s_{k}\leq t_{k}\leq 1
, implying the theorem.
∎

In the last inequality above, the constant
\epsilon_{k}
can be estimated
via the
f(x)=o(x^{0.525})
theorem quoted in the proof.
The extension of the theorem for non-fixed

faces the difficulty that
the

-bound on

requires

to be large, but

puts limitations
on that.

Part
(i)
of Corollary

can be extended also for

which are not of the form

.
This offers a replacement of the above error term

Proposition 35
.

Let
k=q^{2}+q+1+r

For this purpose we need to specify the construction of

We apply the fact that every projective Galois plane has a

Let

Replace each

of cardinality

from

be the graph obtained from

This

(namely the

), and its other

Recalling that
|G_{i}|=\frac{q+1}{q^{2}+q+1}\,n
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=r+1}^{q^{2}+q+1}d(G_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=r+1}^{q^{2}+q+1}\frac{1}{|G_{i}|}\left(\frac{1}{q+1}|G_{i}|\left(|G_{i}|-1\right)+\left(1-\frac{1}{q+1}\right)^{2}|G_{i}|^{2}\right)

(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=r+1}^{q^{2}+q+1}\left(\left(\frac{1}{q+1}+\left(\frac{q}{q+1}\right)^{2}\right)|G_{i}|-\frac{1}{q+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(q^{2}+q+1-r\right)\left(\frac{q^{2}+q+1}{(q+1)^{2}}\left(\frac{q+1}{q^{2}+q+1}\,n\right)-\frac{1}{q+1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{q^{2}+q+1-r}{q+1}\,n-q\,.

If
1\leq i\leq r
, we simply put
G_{i}=H_{i}
.
These graphs are regular of degree
(1-\frac{1}{q+1})|G_{i}|
, hence

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}d(G_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{q+1}\right)|G_{i}|\right)

(2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{rq}{q+1}\,\frac{q+1}{q^{2}+q+1}\,n\,.

Finally, if
q^{2}+q+2\leq i\leq q^{2}+q+1+r
, we let
G_{i}
be the complete
graph on the vertex set
X_{i}
.
These
r
graphs are regular of degree
|X_{i}|-1
, hence

\displaystyle\sum_{i=q^{2}+q+2}^{q^{2}+q+1+r}d(G_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r}{q^{2}+q+1}\,n-r.

(3)

Summing up (
1
)+(
2
)+(
3
) we obtain

\displaystyle M(k,n)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{Mad}(G_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(q+\frac{1}{q+1}\right)\,n+\left(\frac{q+1}{q^{2}+q+1}-\frac{1}{q+1}\right)\,rn-q-r\,,

proving the assertion.
∎

An explicit lower bound of similar flavor can also be given for the cases
where
k
is big, but
not much bigger than the number of blocks in a Steiner system.

Proposition 36
.

Assume that
k=\binom{n}{2}/\binom{p}{2}
, and that a Steiner system

S(2,p,n)
exists.
If
1\leq r\leq 2k
, then
M(k+r,n)\geq M(k,n)+rp/4-cr
for a constant
c
.

Proof.

We know from Theorem
21
that
M(k,n)
for
k=\binom{n}{2}/\binom{p}{2}

is generated via
S(2,p,n)
whenever such a Steiner system exists.
In that construction, every graph
G_{i}
in the decomposition of
K_{n}
is a
copy of
K_{p}
.
Applying Theorem
18
and the first subcase of
Proposition
19
, we decompose selected copies of
K_{p}
into
two subgraphs
H_{i,1},H_{i,2}
with

\mathrm{Mad}(H_{i,1})+\mathrm{Mad}(H_{i,1})\geq 5p/4-3/2

or into three subgraphs
H_{i,1},H_{i,2},H_{i,3}
with

\mathrm{Mad}(H_{i,1})+\mathrm{Mad}(H_{i,2})+\mathrm{Mad}(H_{i,3})\geq 3p/2-c_{3}
.
Increasing the number of subgraphs with

, this operation increases

\mathrm{Mad}(G_{1})+\ldots+\mathrm{Mad}(G_{k})
with at least

, where

is relatively small.
∎

Also, the following recursive lower bound can be stated.

Proposition 37
.

For every
a,b,t\in\mathbb{N}
with
b\geq 3
,
t\geq 2
and

we have

Proof.

Applying the principle of the preceding constructions, replace each vertex

x_{i}
of a structure verifíying
M(a,b)
with a set
X_{i}
of cardinality

t
, blow up the corresponding graphs
G_{1},\dots,G_{a}
accordingly
(hence multiplying their
\mathrm{Mad}
values by
t
), and create
b
further
graphs
G_{a+1},\dots,G_{a+b}
regular of degree

,

being
the complete graph on the vertex set
X_{i}
for

.
∎

Applying the above implication for

, we may abbreviate the
conclusion as
M(a,b)\Longrightarrow M(a+b,n)\gtrsim c_{a,b}\cdot n
,
where

Some pairs

•

In the same way also

Applications to other graph invariants

In this section we derive implications of the above results tor other
graph invariants, namely:

•

clique number,
\omega(G)
(largest number of mutually adjacent
vertices in
G
);

•

chromatic number,
\chi(G)
(minimum number of colors in a vertex
coloring such that no two adjacent vertices have the same color);

•

choice number or list chromatic number,
\mathrm{ch}(G)
(minimum size
m
of
color lists
L_{v}
assigned to the vertices
v\in V(G)
guaranteeing that
there exists a coloring
\varphi:V(G)\to\cup_{v\in V(G)}L_{v}
with
\varphi(v)\in L_{v}

for all
v\in V(G)
and
\varphi(v)\neq\varphi(w)
for all
vw\in E(G)

whenever
|L_{v}|=m
for all
v\in V(G)
);

•

degeneracy,
\delta{}^{\mathsf{+}}(G)
(smallest integer
d
such that
G
admits a
vertex order
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
in which each
v_{j}
has at most
d

preceding neighbors
v_{i}
with
i<j
, equal to
\max\{\delta(H)\}

taken over all induced subgraphs
H\subseteq G
);

•

coloring number,
\mathrm{col}(G)=\delta{}^{\mathsf{+}}(G)+1
, also called Szekeres–Wilf number;

•

highest induced vertex connection,
\kappa{}^{\mathsf{+}}(G)
(maximum vertex
connection number
\kappa(H)
over all induced subgraphs
H\subseteq G
);

•

highest induced edge connection,
\lambda{}^{\mathsf{+}}(G)
(maximum edge
connection number
\lambda(H)
over all induced subgraphs
H\subseteq G
).

Applying the notational convention described in the introduction, we write

\omega(k,n)
,
\chi(k,n)
,
\delta{}^{\mathsf{+}}(k,n)
,
\mathrm{col}(k,n)
,
\kappa{}^{\mathsf{+}}(k,n)
,
\lambda{}^{\mathsf{+}}(k,n)

for the largest possible sum
\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\mathfrak{p}}(G_{j})
taken over all
edge decompositions of
K_{n}
into
k
subgraphs
G_{1},\dots,G_{k}
, where

{\mathfrak{p}}\in\{\omega,\chi,\delta{}^{\mathsf{+}},\mathrm{col},\kappa{}^{\mathsf{+}},\lambda{}^{\mathsf{+}}\}
.

Since the inequality chains

\mathrm{Mad}(G)+1\geq\mathrm{col}(G)\geq\mathrm{ch}(G)\geq\chi(G)\geq\omega(G)
and

\mathrm{Mad}(G)\geq\delta{}^{\mathsf{+}}(G)\geq\lambda{}^{\mathsf{+}}(G)\geq\kappa{}^{\mathsf{+}}(G)

are valid for every graph
G
, also the corresponding extremal functions
satisfy the following numerical relations
for all
n\geq 3
and
2\leq k\leq\binom{n}{2}
:

\omega(k,n)\leq\chi(k,n)\leq\mathrm{ch}(k,n)\leq\mathrm{col}(k,n)\leq M(k,n)+k\,,

\kappa{}^{\mathsf{+}}(k,n)\leq\lambda{}^{\mathsf{+}}(k,n)\leq\delta{}^{\mathsf{+}}(k,n)\leq M(k,n)\,.

Füredi et al. studied four of the above seven parameters; next we
summarize their findings.

Theorem 38

(Füredi, Kostochka, Stiebitz, Škrekovski, West
[
11
]
)
.

1.

\omega(k,n)\leq n+\binom{k}{2}
,
with equality for all
n\geq\binom{k}{2}
.

2.

\chi(k,n)\leq n+7^{k}
.

3.

\mathrm{ch}(k,n)\leq n+3k!\sqrt{1+8n\ln n}
,
moreover
\mathrm{ch}(k,n)\geq n+ck\ln(n/\!\sqrt{k}\,)
for a constant
c>0

if
k
is a triangular number,
k=\binom{t+1}{2}
, and
t\mid n
.

4.

\mathrm{col}(k,n)\leq\sqrt{k}\,n+k
,
moreover
\mathrm{col}(k,n)\geq(\sqrt{k}-1)n+k

if
k=q^{2}+q+1
for a prime power
q
, and
k\mid n
.

5.

\mathrm{col}(k,n)\leq(k-1)(n+1)/2

if
k\geq 5
; for smaller
k
,

\mathrm{col}(3,n)=(3n+3)/2
and
\mathrm{col}(4,n)=(5n+7)/3
.

Let us recall further the following Norhaus–Gaddum-type result
(converted to the present notation) from the
paper which initiated the study of choice number.

Theorem 39

(Erdős, Rubin, Taylor
[
10
]
)
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathrm{ch}(2,n)\leq\mathrm{col}(2,n)\leq n+1
.

Comparing Theorem
39
with Theorem
18
it turns out that

M(2,n)\sim\frac{5}{4}\,n
is much bigger than
\mathrm{col}(2,n)
.
For
k=3
and
k=4
it is not clear whether the situation
between
M(k,n)
and
\mathrm{col}(k,m)
is similar,
as the corresponding constructions in Proposition
19
yield
lower bounds which coincide with those in Part 5 of Theorem
38
.
However, as
k
gets large and
n\to\infty
, the relative gap between

\mathrm{col}(k,n)
and
M(k,n)
gets small.
What is more, our results on
M(k,n)
improve substantially the earlier
estimates on several functions
{\mathfrak{p}}(k,n)
in certain ranges of the
parameters.

Let us recall that the list version
M^{\mathsf{L}}(k,\binom{n}{2})
of
M(k,n)

(and more generally,
M^{\mathsf{L}}(k,N)
for all
N\geq k
) was
determined exactly for all
n\geq 3
and all
2\leq k\leq\binom{n}{2}

in Theorem
20
.
It has also been proved that every list of graphs being extremal for

M^{\mathsf{L}}(k,\binom{n}{2})
can be transformed to one of two types:

•

Type 1:
q
copies of
K_{p+1}
and
k-q
copies of
K_{p}
, if
r=0
;

•

Type 2:
q
copies of
K_{p+1}
,
k-q-1
copies of
K_{p}
,
and one copy of any one graph
G\in\mathcal{F}_{p,\,r}
, if
0<r<p
;

where
\binom{n}{2}=k\binom{p}{2}+qp+r
, with

0\leq q<k
and
0\leq r<p
.
As stated in Theorem
21
, we have
M(k,n)=M^{\mathsf{L}}(k,\binom{n}{2})

whenever
M(k,n)
is attained by a packing of Type 1 or Type 2.
Moreover, a bunch of results above show that this situation occurs
for infinitely many pairs
k,n
.
Consequences can be stated for other parameters as well.

Theorem 40
.

If
M(k,n)
is attained by a packing of Type 1 or Type 2, and in case of
Type 2 with
r\geq(p-1)/2
the packing is established using a graph

G\in\mathcal{G}_{p,r}
with
\omega(G)=p
, then

\omega(k,n)=\chi(k,n)=\mathrm{ch}(k,n)=\mathrm{col}(k,n)=\left\lfloor M(k,n)\right\rfloor+k\,,

and

\kappa{}^{\mathsf{+}}(k,n)=\lambda{}^{\mathsf{+}}(k,n)=\delta{}^{\mathsf{+}}(k,n)=\left\lfloor M(k,n)\right\rfloor\,.

Proof.

In a packing of Type 1, all graphs are complete, and we have

\kappa{}^{\mathsf{+}}(K_{p})=\kappa(K_{p})=p-1=\mathrm{Mad}(K_{p})

and
\omega(K_{p})=p=\mathrm{Mad}(K_{p})+1

(and of course the values for
K_{p+1}
are larger by eaxctly 1).
In case of Type 2, with
G\in\mathcal{G}_{p,r}
being the single non-complete
graph in the packing (where
0<r<p
), we have
p-1<\mathrm{Mad}(G)<p
.
Then
\kappa{}^{\mathsf{+}}(G)=\left\lfloor\mathrm{Mad}(G)\right\rfloor
and
\omega(G)=\left\lfloor\mathrm{Mad}(G)\right\rfloor+1
.
Summing up for
G_{1},\dots,G_{k}
, the theorem follows.
∎

As a matter of fact, the behavior of several functions is very similar.

Theorem 41
.

For every parameter
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\{\kappa{}^{\mathsf{+}},\lambda{}^{\mathsf{+}},\delta{}^{\mathsf{+}}\}
,
and also for
{\mathfrak{p}}
defined as
{\mathfrak{p}}(G):=\mathrm{col}(G)-1
, we have

(1-\epsilon_{k})\sqrt{k}\,n\leq{\mathfrak{p}}(k,n)\leq M(k,n)<\sqrt{k}\,n

for every fixed
k
, where
\epsilon_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.

Proof.

The upper bound is valid by Corollary
17
.
The lower bound has to be proved for
\mathrm{col}(k,n)
and
\kappa{}^{\mathsf{+}}(k,n)
.
In fact, since
\mathrm{col}(G)-1\geq\delta{}^{\mathsf{+}}(G)\geq\kappa{}^{\mathsf{+}}(G)
holds
for every graph
G
, we may restrict attention to
\kappa{}^{\mathsf{+}}(k,n)
.

For this, we apply the construction from the proof of Theorem
34
.
Let
k^{\prime}\leq k
be the largest integer of the form
k^{\prime}=q^{2}+q+1
, where
q

is a prime power, and
n^{\prime}
be the largest multiple of
k^{\prime}
with
n^{\prime}\leq n
.
Then
K_{n^{\prime}}
admits a packing
G_{1},\dots,G_{k^{\prime}}
of
k^{\prime}
edge-disjoint
copies of the complete
(q+1)
-partite graph

K_{\frac{n^{\prime}}{k^{\prime}},\frac{n^{\prime}}{k^{\prime}},\dots,\frac{n^{\prime}}{k^{\prime}}}
.
If fewer than
\frac{q}{q^{2}+q+1}\,n^{\prime}
vertices are removed from it, then
more than one vertex class contains a non-deleted vertex.
Picking two of those vertices from distinct classes, they are adjacent and
dominate the rest of
K_{\frac{n^{\prime}}{k^{\prime}},\frac{n^{\prime}}{k^{\prime}},\dots,\frac{n^{\prime}}{k^{\prime}}}
.
Thus,
\kappa{}^{\mathsf{+}}(G_{i})\geq\frac{q}{q^{2}+q+1}\,n^{\prime}
holds for all
q\leq i\leq k^{\prime}
.

Distribute the edges of
E(K_{n})\diagdown(\cup_{i=1}^{k^{\prime}}E(G_{i}))
among
the current graphs
G_{i}
and the further

graphs
G_{k^{\prime}+1},\dots,G_{k}

to be constructed.
Using the facts
k-k^{\prime}=o(k)
and

, we obtain

\displaystyle\kappa{}^{\mathsf{+}}(k,n)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k^{\prime}}\kappa{}^{\mathsf{+}}(G_{i})\ \geq\ \frac{q}{q^{2}+q+1}\,k^{\prime}n^{\prime}\ =\ qn^{\prime}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle(\sqrt{k^{\prime}}-1)\,n^{\prime}\ \geq\ (1-\epsilon_{k})\sqrt{k}\,n\,.

∎

From the first three parts of Theorem
38
one can see that
the behavior of
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\{\omega,\chi,\mathrm{ch}\}
is different because for
those parameters we have
{\mathfrak{p}}(k,n)\leq n+f(k,n)
with either

(i.e., the error term depends on
k
but is independent
of
n
) or
f(k,n)=c(k)\cdot h(n)
where

is sublinear.
On the other hand, our
\sqrt{k}\,n
upper bound very quickly becomes
substantially better than the corresponding estimates in the first three
parts of Theorem
38
.

Theorem 42
.

\omega(k,n)\leq\chi(k,n)\leq\mathrm{ch}(k,n)<\sqrt{k}\,n+k

In this section we present natural problems and directions for future

Problem 43
.

Determine

{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathsf{L}}(k,N)

{\mathfrak{p}}

Problem 44

Study the product version of

In cases where

Our several results above show that this method works in certain

k,n
for

For instance, the following holds.

Theorem 45
.

Let

Then

\max_{(G_{1},\ldots,G_{k})\ \mathrm{decompose}\ K_{n}}\Bigl\{\,\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{Mad}(G_{i})\Bigr\}=(p-1)^{k}=(p-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{p(p-1)}}.

On applying part

In a similar way, constructions from multisets

is relatively

.
For

Appendix: Alternative approach to Theorems
20
and
21

In this Appendix we provide an argument of algorithmic nature as an
alternative proof of Theorem
21
.
It demonstrates that every decomposition of
K_{n}
can be transformed
step by step to a unique well-defined representative list of graphs.
During this process the sum
\mathrm{Mad}(G_{1})+\ldots+\mathrm{Mad}(G_{k})
never decreases, and finally yields
the claimed upper bound on
M(k,n)
.
The method is equally fine for a proof of Theorem
20
,
hence determining
M^{\mathsf{L}}(k,N)
for arbitrary integers
N\geq k\geq 2
.

For convenience let us recall that the following will be proved:

Theorem
21
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
2\leq k\leq\binom{n}{2}
be given, and choose
the integer
p
for which

k\binom{p}{2}\leq\binom{n}{2}<k\binom{p+1}{2}
holds.
Let

q,r
be the nonnegative uniquely determined integers such that

\binom{n}{2}=k\binom{p}{2}+qp+r
, where

0\leq q<k
and
0\leq r<p
. Then

M(k,n)\leq\begin{cases}\begin{tabular}[]{ll}$kp-k+q$&if\/ $r\leq(p-1)/2$\,;\\
$kp-k+q+1-2(p-r)/(p+1)$&if\/ $r\geq(p-1)/2$\,.\end{tabular}\end{cases}

Moreover, equality holds whenever
K_{n}
admits an edge decomposition
into

•

q
copies of
K_{p+1}
and
k-q
copies of
K_{p}
, if
r=0
; or

•

q
copies of
K_{p+1}
,
k-q-1
copies of
K_{p}
,
and one copy of any one graph
G\in\mathcal{F}_{p,\,r}
, if
0<r<p
.

Proof.

Consider any edge decomposition
(G_{1},\dots,G_{k})
of
K_{n}
.
We
forget that the
G_{i}
originate from a decomposition;
we only view them as a list of individual graphs, and manipulate
with the graphs themselves.
In this way, if we only assume that the starting collection of graphs
is just a list
\mathsf{L}=\{G_{1},\dots,G_{k}\}
with
e(G_{1})+\ldots+e(G_{k})=N
,
then the process also leads to the determination of
M^{\mathsf{L}}(k,N)

with the formula stated in Theorem
20
.
However, in the sequel we restrict attention to
N=\binom{n}{2}
.

1. We replace each
G_{i}
with
H_{i}
, where
H_{i}
is the extremal
representative of the graphs with
e(G_{i})
edges.
Due to Theorem
8
, we have
\mathrm{Mad}(H_{i})\geq\mathrm{Mad}(G_{i})
.

2. We qualify the graphs
H_{i}
into three categories, where the
involved integer
p
is uniquely determined by the number

e(H_{i})
of edges, and may vary for
different subscripts among the
k
graphs under consideration.

Type A:

e(H_{i})=\binom{p_{i}}{2}
.

Type B:

\binom{p_{i}}{2}<e(H_{i})<\binom{p_{i}+1}{2}
and

\mathrm{Mad}(H_{i})=2e(H_{i})/(p_{i}+1)
.

Type C:

\binom{p_{i}}{2}<e(H_{i})<\binom{p_{i}+1}{2}
and

\mathrm{Mad}(H_{i})=p_{i}-1
.

At any stage of the procedure, we denote by
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{B}
,
\mathcal{C}

the actual collection (multiset) of graphs
H_{i}
of
type A, B, C, respectively.
If
e(H_{i})=\frac{1}{2}(p_{i}+1)(p_{i}-1)=\binom{p_{i}}{2}+\frac{p_{i}-1}{2}
,
then
H_{i}
is qualified for both
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
.
In this case we assume
H_{i}\in\mathcal{B}
.

Beside these, we maintain an integer
s
for the number of
so-called “spare edges”, which is initialized as
s=0
.

3. The following steps are performed, keeping
s+\sum_{i=1}^{k}e(H_{i})

unchanged throughout, hence maintaining the sum equal to

\binom{n}{2}
.

3.a

First we eliminate the members of
\mathcal{C}
.
If a
H_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
occurs, say
H_{i}\cong G_{p_{i},\,r_{i}}
, we re-define

H_{i}:=K_{p_{i}}
and
s:=s+r
.
This keeps
\mathrm{Mad}(H_{i})
unchanged, and increases
s
.
In this way all
H_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
are modified to type-A graphs
at the beginning, and later on if a
type-C graph arises, it is also eliminated immediately.

3.b

Next we eliminate the members of
\mathcal{B}
, possibly with one exception.
If
\mathcal{B}
contains
H_{i},H_{j}
such that
H_{i}\cong G_{p_{i},\,r_{i}}
and

H_{j}\cong G_{p_{j},\,r_{j}}
, where either
p_{i}>p_{j}
, or
p_{i}=p_{j}
and

r_{i}\leq r_{j}
, then transfer
r^{\prime}:=\min\{r_{i},p_{j}-r_{j}\}
edges
from
H_{i}
to
H_{j}
.
We do the same if
|\mathcal{B}|=1
, say
\mathcal{B}=\{H_{j}\}
where
H_{j}=G_{p_{j},\,r_{j}}
,
and
\mathcal{A}
has a member
H_{i}\cong K_{p_{i}+1}=G_{p_{i},p_{i}}

for some
p_{i}>p_{j}
.

Note that either
r^{\prime}=p_{j}-r_{j}
or
r^{\prime}=p_{j}-r_{j}-1
holds,
because the assumptions
H_{i},H_{j}\in\mathcal{B}
and
p_{i}\geq p_{j}

imply
p_{j}-r_{j}\leq(p_{j}+1)/2\leq(p_{i}+1)/2\leq r_{i}+1
.
Hence, the following outcomes are possible after such a step.

•

If
r^{\prime}=r_{i}=p_{j}-r_{j}
, then
H_{i}\cong K_{p_{i}}
and

H_{j}\cong K_{p_{j}+1}
, and both
H_{i}
and
H_{j}
have been
moved from
\mathcal{B}
to
\mathcal{A}
.

•

If
r^{\prime}=r_{i}=p_{j}-r_{j}-1
, then
H_{i}\cong K_{p_{i}}
(moved from

\mathcal{B}
to
\mathcal{A}
), and

H_{j}\cong G_{p_{j},p_{j}-1}
(hence remains a member of
\mathcal{B}
).

•

If
r^{\prime}=p_{j}-r_{j}<r_{i}
, then
H_{j}\cong K_{p_{j}+1}
(moved from

\mathcal{B}
to
\mathcal{A}
), and
H_{i}\cong G_{p_{i},r_{i}-r^{\prime}}
, which either
remains in
\mathcal{B}
or has been moved from
\mathcal{B}
to
\mathcal{C}
.
In the latter case it is then immediately transformed to

K_{p_{i}}
by step 3.a, hence becomes a member of
\mathcal{A}
.

In either of these three cases, the value of
\mathrm{Mad}(H_{j})

has been increased by exactly
2r^{\prime}\!/(p_{j}+1)
.
On the other hand, the decrease of
\mathrm{Mad}(H_{i})
is at most

2r^{\prime}\!/(p_{i}+1)
.
Indeed, removing the
r^{\prime}
edges from
H_{i}
one by one, until
reaching the threshold of
r_{i}=(p_{i}-1)/2
the omission of each edge
yields a loss of
2/(p_{i}+1)
, and no decrease occurs afterwards.
Thus, the overall increase of
\mathrm{Mad}(H_{i})+\mathrm{Mad}(H_{j})
is
not smaller than
\frac{2(p_{i}-p_{j})r^{\prime}}{(p_{i}+1)(p_{j}+1)}
.

In case
p_{i}=p_{j}
it is possible that
\mathrm{Mad}(H_{i})+\mathrm{Mad}(H_{j})

remains unchanged.
This happens if the modified
H_{j}
is
K_{p_{i}+1}
while
H_{i}

still remains a member of
\mathcal{B}

(what means
r_{i}+r_{j}-p_{i}\geq(p_{i}-1)/2
).

Performing 3.a and 3.b as long as possible, we obtain
\mathcal{C}=\varnothing

and either
\mathcal{B}=\varnothing
or
|\mathcal{B}|=1
.

3.c

The next goal is to make a balance in
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}
.
Recall that
G_{p^{\prime},p^{\prime}}\cong K_{p^{\prime}+1}
holds for every
p^{\prime}\geq 1
.
Assume that
H_{i}=G_{p_{i},p_{i}}
is the largest member of
\mathcal{A}
, and

H_{j}=G_{p_{j},p_{j}}
is its smallest member; moreover if
\mathcal{B}\neq\varnothing
,
let
\mathcal{B}=\{G_{p_{k},\,r_{k}}\}
(where
0<r_{k}<p_{k}
).
If
\max\{p_{i},p_{j},p_{k}\}>\min\{p_{i},p_{j},p_{k}\}+1
(disregarding
p_{k}

if
\mathcal{B}=\varnothing
), select the two of

H_{i},H_{j},H_{k}
whose
p
-value is largest and smallest, respectively,
and perform the transformation 3.
b
on them.
(If the step is applied on
H_{i},H_{j}\in\mathcal{A}
, then
s
is updated
exactly as
s:=s+p_{i}-p_{j}-1
.)
Repeat this until all
p
-values in
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}
differ by at most 1.
In the same way as in the analysis of 3.b, it is readily seen
that the sum of
\mathrm{Mad}
-values has been increased (and also
s
).

3.d

In the last phase the
s
spare edges are eliminated.
If
\mathcal{B}\neq\varnothing
and
H_{k}\cong G_{p_{k},\,r_{k}}
, first
r^{\prime\prime}:=\min\{s,p_{k}-r_{k}\}

edges are inserted in
H_{k}
.
The process is finished if
r^{\prime\prime}=s
.
Otherwise we update
s:=s-r^{\prime\prime}
; at that moment
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{C}=\varnothing
, and the
family
\mathcal{A}
as a multiset is of the form

\{a*K_{p^{\prime}},b*K_{p^{\prime}+1}\}
(meaning
a
copies of
K_{p^{\prime}}

and
b
copies of
K_{p^{\prime}+1}
)
for some
p^{\prime}\geq 2
,
a\geq 1
,
b\geq 0
.
As long as
s\geq p^{\prime}
, pick a copy of
K_{p^{\prime}}
from
\mathcal{A}
,
enlarge it to
K_{p^{\prime}+1}
, and update
s:=s-p^{\prime}
.
Once this has been done for all
H_{i}\cong K_{p^{\prime}}
, and still

s>0
holds, update
p^{\prime}:=p^{\prime}+1
and continue in the same way.

3.e

If
s=0
, stop. If
s>0
, replace one copy of
K_{p^{\prime}}
with
the graph
G_{p^{\prime},s}
.
Call the current family
H_{1},\dots,H_{k}
the representative of
the pair
(k,n)
.

Due to the choice of
p,q,r
with respect to
k
,
at this point
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{C}
is of the form either

\{a*K_{p},b*K_{p+1},1*G_{p,r}\}

with
1\leq r<p
and
a,b\geq 0
(
a=0
or
b=0
is possible,

a+b=k-1
),
or

\{a*K_{p},b*K_{p+1}\}

(here
a+b=k
, possibly with
b=0
), where
a,b
depend on

n
and
k
, and are easily expressible in terms of
q
and
r
,
as stated in the theorem.

Observe that, starting from an arbitrary edge decomposition of
K_{n}

into
k
subgraphs
G_{1},\dots,G_{k}
, the procedure terminates
with the representative of the pair
(k,n)
as the unique final
family
H_{1},\dots,H_{k}
determined above.
Thus,

M(k,n)\leq\mathrm{Mad}(H_{1})+\ldots+\mathrm{Mad}(H_{k})\,.

The numerical formula follows from the facts that
\mathrm{Mad}(K_{p})=p-1

(applied also as
\mathrm{Mad}(K_{p+1})=p
) and that
\mathrm{Mad}(G_{p,r})
is
either
p
or
(p^{2}-p+2r)/(p+1)
, depending on
whether
r\leq(p-1)/2
or bigger.
Namely, the procedure ends with the combination of graphs as
displayed in the following table:

graphs

The cases of equality, as stated in the last part of the theorem,

Indeed, if an assumed edge decomposition of

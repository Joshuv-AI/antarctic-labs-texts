On the singular nature of shallow-water convergence of the intermediate long wave equation on the real line
Andreia Chapouto, Benjamin Harrop-Griffiths, Guopeng Li, and Tadahiro Oh

Abstract
. We investigate regularity properties of the solution map for the intermediate long wave equation (ILW) on the real line. More precisely, we study the scaled ILW which was shown to converge to the Korteweg-de Vries equation (KdV) in L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}) in the shallow-water limit in a recent work by the first, third, and fourth authors with T. Zhao (2025). By decomposing the dynamics into the low frequency part and the residual part, we show that, when the depth parameter is sufficiently small, the solution map for the low frequency part is analytic in L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}) , while the solution map for the residual part fails to be C 2 C^{2} . Moreover, we establish shallow-water convergence in L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}) of the low frequency dynamics to KdV. This explains the mechanism of the regularity gain of the solution map in the shallow-water limit.

1.
Introduction

We consider the intermediate long wave equation (ILW)
on the real line:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}u=\partial_{x}(u^{2}),

where
the unknown
u

denotes the amplitude of the internal wave at the interface
of a stratified fluid of
finite depth
0<\delta<\infty
;
see
[
20
,
28
]
.
Here,
the operator
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
in (
1.1
)
is defined by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{G}_{\delta}f}(\xi)=-i\big(\coth(\delta\xi)-\delta^{-1}\xi^{-1}\big)\widehat{f}(\xi).

The ILW equation (
1.1
)
serves as an “intermediate” equation of finite depth
\delta\in(0,\infty)
,
bridging
(i) the Benjamin-Ono equation (BO), modeling fluid of infinite depth (
\delta=\infty
):

(1.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}u=\partial_{x}(u^{2}),

where
\mathcal{H}
denotes the Hilbert transform,
and
(ii) the Korteweg-de Vries equation, modeling shallow water (
\delta=0
);
see (
1.5
) below.
Moreover, (
1.1
) is a completely integrable
dispersive PDE
with an infinite number of conservation laws
[
29
,
27
,
26
,
33
,
41
]

and Lax pair structures
[
15
,
18
]
, thus attracting a wide range of attention;
see
[
42
,
24
]
for an overview of the subject and the references therein.
In recent years, there have been intensive research activities
from both deterministic and statistical viewpoints;
see
[
1
,
36
,
37
,
35
,
38
,
30
,
31
,
19
,
9
,
6
,
13
,
32
,
8
,
14
,
18
,
10
]
for well- / ill-posedness results
as well as results on
deep-water (
\delta\to\infty
)
and shallow-water (
\delta\to 0
)
convergence;
see also

[
11
,
16
,
7
]
.

In studying
the shallow-water limit (
\delta\to 0
),
we need to magnify the fluid amplitude by a factor
\sim\frac{1}{\delta}

to observe any meaningful limiting behavior.
This leads to the following scaling
[
1
]
:

v(t,x)=\tfrac{3}{\delta}u\big(\tfrac{3}{\delta}t,x\big),

which transforms (
1.1
) to the following scaled ILW equation:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v-\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}v=\partial_{x}(v^{2}),\qquad\text{where }\ \widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}:=\frac{3}{\delta}\mathcal{G}_{\delta}.

Then, under suitable assumptions,
the scaled ILW (
1.4
) is known to converge to the
following
Korteweg-de Vries equation (KdV)
in the shallow-water limit (
\delta\to 0
)

[
1
,
30
,
31
,
10
]
:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+\partial_{x}^{3}v=\partial_{x}(v^{2}).

In particular, in a recent work
[
10
]
,
the first, third, and fourth authors with T. Zhao
established shallow-water convergence
of the scaled ILW (
1.4
)
to KdV (
1.5
) in
L^{2}

on both the real line and the circle
\mathbb{T}=(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
.

Our main goal in this paper is
to investigate regularity properties
of the solution map for the scaled ILW (
1.4
)
posed on the real line; see Remark
1.3

for the periodic case.

It has been known for some time that the solution map exhibits a gain of regularity in the shallow water limit.
On the one hand, Molinet, Saut, and Tzvetkov showed in
[
36
]

that the solution map
is not
C^{2}
-differentiable in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})

for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
On the other hand,
the scaled ILW (
1.4
)
converges
to KdV (
1.5
) in the shallow-water limit,
whose solution map is known to be analytic
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s\geq-\frac{3}{4}
; see
[
3
,
21
,
23
,
17
]
.
Our main result, Theorem
1.1
, explains how this regularity gain occurs.

We recall that the scaled ILW (
1.4
)
behaves like KdV
for frequencies
|\xi|\lesssim\delta^{-1}

(see, for example, Lemma
2.2
(i)).
Consequently, we expect that
the low frequency part of the scaled ILW dynamics
enjoys a better regularity property.
With this in mind, given
0<\delta\leq 1
,
we split the scaled ILW dynamics
into a low frequency part
and a residual part.
Namely, we write a solution
v
to the scaled ILW (
1.4
)
with initial data
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

as

(1.6)

\displaystyle v=v^{\textup{low}}+v^{\textup{res}},

where
the low frequency part
v^{\textup{low}}
satisfies

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v^{\textup{low}}-\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}v^{\textup{low}}=\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}\partial_{x}\big((v^{\textup{low}})^{2}\big)\\
v^{\textup{low}}|_{t=0}=\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}\phi\end{cases}

and
the residual part
v^{\textup{res}}
satisfies

(1.8)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v^{\textup{res}}-\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}v^{\textup{res}}=\partial_{x}\big((v^{\textup{res}})^{2}\big)+2\partial_{x}(v^{\textup{res}}v^{\textup{low}})+\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}^{\perp}\partial_{x}\big((v^{\textup{low}})^{2}\big)\\
v^{\textup{res}}|_{t=0}=\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}^{\perp}\phi,\end{cases}

respectively.
Here,
\mathbf{P}_{N}
denotes the Dirichlet projector onto spatial frequencies
\{|\xi|\leq N\}

and
\mathbf{P}_{N}^{\perp}=\Id-\mathbf{P}_{N}
.
When
\delta=0
,
we set
\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}|_{\delta=0}=\Id
.
In particular,
we have
v^{\textup{low}}=\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}v^{\textup{low}}
and thus

(1.9)

\displaystyle\supp\widehat{v^{\textup{low}}}(t,\cdot\,)\subset\{|\xi|\leq\delta^{-1}\}

for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
An immediate consequence is that the
L^{2}
-norm of
v^{\textup{low}}
is conserved, which yields global well-posedness of (
1.7
) in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for any
s\geq 0
; see Remark
3.2
.
In the following,
we often refer to the low frequency dynamics (
1.7
),
including the case
\delta=0
,
with the understanding that,
when
\delta=0
,
it corresponds to KdV (
1.5
)
with initial data
v|_{t=0}=\phi
.

We now state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

(i)

Let
0\leq\delta\leq 1
and
s\geq 0
. Then, given
any
\phi\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
there exists small
T=T(\|\phi\|_{H^{s}})>0
,
independent of
0\leq\delta\leq 1
,
such that
the map, sending
\phi\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})

to the solution
v^{\textup{low}}\in C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))

to (
1.7
)
with initial data
v^{\textup{low}}|_{t=0}=\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}\phi
,
is analytic at
\phi
with respect to the
H^{s}
-regularity.

(ii)

Let
0<\delta\leq 1
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, given any
T>0
,
the map, sending
\phi\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})

to the solution
v^{\textup{res}}\in C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))

to (
1.8
), a priori defined for
s\geq 0
,
is not
C^{2}
-differentiable at the zero function with respect to the
H^{s}
-regularity.

(iii)

As
\delta\to 0
,
the solution
v^{\textup{low}}
to (
1.7
)
with initial data
v^{\textup{low}}|_{t=0}=\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}\phi

converges to
the solution
v
to (
1.5
)
with initial data
v|_{t=0}=\phi

in
C(\mathbb{R};H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
,
where

C(\mathbb{R};H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
is endowed with the compact-open topology in time.

The instability in
Theorem
1.1
(ii)
comes from
the ‘low
\times
high
\mapsto
high’ interaction,
where high frequencies are much larger than
\delta^{-1}

(tending to
\infty
), and thus vanishes in the shallow-water limit.
Hence, Theorem
1.1

explains the mechanism of
the regularity gain of the solution map in the shallow-water limit,
from not being
C^{2}
to being analytic.
It also explains how this regularity gain
propagates in frequency,
namely, from the low frequency dynamics
supported on
\{|\xi|\leq\delta^{-1}\}

to KdV.
This illustrates the singular nature
of the
shallow-water limit;
see Remark
1.5

for a further discussion
on various aspects of the singular nature
of the shallow-water limit.

In light of (
1.9
)
and the following (crude) product estimate:

\big\|\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}\big((\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}f)(\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}g)\big)\big\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\delta^{-s-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}f\|_{H^{s}}\|\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}g\|_{H^{s}}

for
s\geq 0
and
0<\delta\leq 1
,
we can easily construct a solution to
the low frequency dynamics (
1.7
)
via Picard iterates,
which provides analyticity
of the map
\phi\mapsto v^{\textup{low}}

for each fixed
0<\delta\leq 1
.
However, such a construction
of a solution is restricted
to a time interval
[0,T_{\delta}]

for some
T_{\delta}>0
which tends to
0
as
\delta\to 0
.
The novelty in Theorem
1.1
(i) is

uniformity in
\delta
of this analytic behavior.
The proof of this fact
follows from
a standard contraction argument
for proving local well-posedness,
once we prove a key bilinear estimate
(Lemma
3.1
).
The main observation here is that
the low frequency dynamics
(
1.7
)
enjoys the same multilinear dispersion as KdV
(see Lemma
2.2
(i)).
Then,
the key bilinear estimate
(Lemma
3.1
)
follows
from
a slight modification of the argument by Kenig, Ponce, and Vega
[
21
]
.
Here, an additional difficulty
appears due to the more complicated
nature of the resonance function for the scaled ILW
(see (
2.18
))
in carrying out a change of variables
(see (
3.18
)-(
3.21
)).
Theorem
1.1
(ii)
follows from a straightforward adaptation of
the argument by
Molinet, Saut, and Tzvetkov
[
36
]

by considering a Gâteaux derivative
(at the zero function)
of the solution map
for the residual dynamics (
1.8
).
We present proofs of Theorem
1.1
(i)
and (ii) in
Subsection
3.1

and Section
4
, respectively.

In proving Theorem
1.1
(iii),
we first establish
the uniform (in small
\delta>0
)
equicontinuity of solutions to
the low frequency dynamics
(Lemma
3.5
)
via a semilinear argument (a contraction-type argument)

without
relying on the complete integrability of
the (scaled) ILW.
Compare this with the situation in
[
10
]
,
where the complete integrability of the equation
played a crucial role
in establishing (a weaker version of)
the uniform (in small
\delta>0
) equicontinuity
of solutions
to the scaled ILW (
1.4
).
Once we prove Lemma
3.5
,
the proof of Theorem
1.1
(iii)
reduces to showing convergence
of the “low frequency part”
of the low frequency dynamics (
1.7
);
see
Proposition
3.3

for details.

Remark 1.2
.

In
[
25
]
,
Koch and Tzvetkov
proved
the failure of local uniform continuity
of the solution map
for the Benjamin-Ono equation
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})

for
s\geq 0
.
By viewing the (original) ILW (
1.1
)
as a perturbation of BO (
1.3
)
(see
[
19
,
9
]
),
it may be possible to adapt the approach in

[
25
]

to upgrade the failure of
C^{2}
-regularity
in Theorem
1.1
(ii)
to the failure of local uniform continuity
for
s\geq 0
.
For
s<-\frac{1}{2}
, the scaled ILW (
1.4
)
is ill-posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
(see
[
6
]
)
due to the discontinuity of the solution map,
which may be used to strengthen
Theorem
1.1
(ii).
We, however, do not pursue these issues
for conciseness of the presentation.
We point out that Theorem
1.1

suffices to contrast
different regularity properties
of the low frequency dynamics
and the residual dynamics.

Remark 1.3
.

The gain of regularity of the solution map
for the scaled ILW (Theorem
1.1
)
comes from the BO-like instability
for the residual dynamics (
1.8
)
due to the ‘low
\times
high
\mapsto
high’ interaction.
For the BO equation (
1.3
)
on the circle,
Molinet
[
34
]
showed that the solution map
is analytic
in
H^{s}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{T})

for any
s\geq 0
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
where

H^{s}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{T})

denotes the subspace of
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})

consisting of functions with spatial mean
\alpha
;
see

[
34
, Theorem 1.2]
.
Namely, there is no BO instability
coming from
the ‘low
\times
high
\mapsto
high’ interaction
in the periodic case.
In particular,
the gain of regularity of the solution map
for the scaled ILW (Theorem
1.1
)
is
a phenomenon specific to the real line case.
Lastly, we point out that,
while the solution map for BO on the circle is
analytic in
H^{s}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
for any
s\geq 0
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
there is no known well-posedness result via a contraction argument
at this point.

Remark 1.4
.

It is possible to include further frequency interactions
as part of the nonlinearity in the low frequency dynamics (
1.7
);
see Subsection
3.3

(in particular, Remark
3.7
).
We, however, believe that the decomposition
of the scaled ILW (
1.4
)
into the system (
1.7
)-(
1.8
)
is a natural one where the low frequency
dynamics converges to the KdV dynamics in the shallow-water limit;
(Theorem
1.1
(iii)).
Moreover, in view of
the weakly uniform (in small
\delta>0
) equicontinuity (
[
10
, Proposition 1.4]
),
the high frequency part
\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}^{\perp}v

can be made arbitrarily small by taking
\delta>0
sufficiently small,
and thus adding high frequency contributions
in (
1.7
) amounts to adding
small perturbations, which do not seem to be of interest.

Remark 1.5
.

Theorem
1.1

exhibits a jump in regularity
of the solution map for the scaled ILW (
1.4
).
This comes from the singular nature of the shallow-water convergence
(when compared to the deep-water limit).
As observed in
[
31
,
8
]
,
this singular nature of the shallow-water limit
also appears in
the convergence of the conservation laws
and invariant measures of the scaled ILW
to those of KdV
(exhibiting
2-to-1 collapses
with regularity jumps and singular supports of measures);
see

[
8
, Remark 1.2]
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notations

We use
A\lesssim B
if there exists
C>0
such that
A\leq CB
, and
A\sim B
if
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.
We also write
A\ll B
if
A\leq cB
for some small constant
c>0
.

The ensuing bilinear estimates (Lemmas
3.1

and

3.6
)
will involve expressions that depend on three
(spatial) frequencies,
\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.
We denote the absolute values,
|\xi|
,
|\xi_{1}|
, and
|\xi_{2}|
,
arranged in a non-increasing order by

\xi_{\max}
,

\xi_{\med}
, and
\xi_{\min}
, respectively. Typically, the frequencies will satisfy the
condition:
\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}
, in which case we have
\xi_{\max}\sim\xi_{\med}
.

Given
\delta\geq 0
,
we denote
by

(2.1)

\displaystyle S_{\delta}(t)=e^{t\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}}

the linear propagator for the scaled ILW (
1.4
)
with the understanding that

S_{0}(t)=e^{-t\partial_{x}^{3}}

is
the linear propagator for KdV (
1.5
)
when
\delta=0
;
see (
2.16
).

2.2.
On the resonance functions

In this subsection, we recall the basic properties
of the resonance function for the scaled ILW (
1.4
).

We first recall that the resonance functions
for KdV (
1.5
) and BO (
1.3
) are given by

\displaystyle\Xi_{\text{\rm KdV}}(\bar{\xi})

\displaystyle=\xi^{3}-\xi_{1}^{3}-\xi_{2}^{3},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Xi_{\text{\rm BO}}(\bar{\xi})

\displaystyle=|\xi|\xi-|\xi_{1}|\xi_{1}-|\xi_{2}|\xi_{2},

respectively,
where
\bar{\xi}=(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
.
Under
\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}
, a direct computation shows that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Xi_{\text{\rm KdV}}(\bar{\xi})

\displaystyle=3\xi\xi_{1}\xi_{2},

(2.4)

\displaystyle|\Xi_{\text{\rm BO}}(\bar{\xi})|

\displaystyle=2\xi_{\med}\xi_{\min}.

Given
0<\delta<\infty
,
we now consider the resonance function
\Xi_{\delta}(\bar{\xi})
for ILW (
1.1
), given by

(2.5)

\displaystyle\Xi_{\delta}(\bar{\xi})=\Xi_{\delta}(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=p_{\delta}(\xi)-p_{\delta}(\xi_{1})-p_{\delta}(\xi_{2}),

where
p_{\delta}(\xi)
denotes the multiplier for
-i\mathcal{G}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}
:

(2.6)

\displaystyle p_{\delta}(\xi)=\xi^{2}\coth(\delta\xi)-\frac{\xi}{\delta}.

See (
1.2
).
Then, under
\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}
, we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\Xi_{\delta}(\bar{\xi})=\xi^{2}\coth(\delta\xi)-\xi_{1}^{2}\coth(\delta\xi_{1})-\xi_{2}^{2}\coth(\delta\xi_{2}).

As in the proof of
[
9
, Lemma 2.3]
, we use the identity

\xi^{2}\coth(\delta\xi)-|\xi|\xi=\sgn(\xi)\cdot\frac{2\xi^{2}}{e^{2|\delta\xi|}-1}

with the fact that
x^{2}\lesssim e^{2x}-1
for any
x\geq 0
and l’Hôpital’s rule (for
\xi=0
) to obtain

(2.8)

\displaystyle\xi^{2}\coth(\delta\xi)-|\xi|\xi=O(\delta^{-2})

for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, as
\delta\to\infty
.
Hence, from (
2.2
), (
2.7
), and (
2.8
),
we have

(2.9)

\displaystyle\Xi_{\delta}(\bar{\xi})=\Xi_{\text{\rm BO}}(\bar{\xi})+O(\delta^{-2}),

as
\delta\to\infty
,
uniformly in
\bar{\xi}=(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

such that
\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}
.

Lastly, given
0<\delta<\infty
, we consider the resonance function

\widetilde{\Xi}_{\delta}(\bar{\xi})
for the scaled ILW (
1.4
), given by

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{\Xi}_{\delta}(\bar{\xi})&=\widetilde{\Xi}_{\delta}(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\widetilde{p}_{\delta}(\xi)-\widetilde{p}_{\delta}(\xi_{1})-\widetilde{p}_{\delta}(\xi_{2}),\end{split}

where

\widetilde{p}_{\delta}(\xi)

is the
multiplier for
-i\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}
:

(2.11)

\displaystyle\widetilde{p}_{\delta}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{\delta}p_{\delta}(\xi)=\xi\mathcal{L}_{\delta}(\xi),

where
p_{\delta}(\xi)
is as in (
2.6
)
and

\mathcal{L}_{\delta}(\xi)
is the multiplier for
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}\partial_{x}
given by

(2.12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\delta}(\xi)=\tfrac{3}{\delta}\big(\xi\coth(\delta\xi)-\delta^{-1}\big)=6\xi^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}\pi^{2}+\delta^{2}\xi^{2}},

where the second equality follows
from
the Mittag-Leffler expansion
of

\pi z\coth(\pi z)
(see
[
2
, (11) on p. 189]
);
see the proof of

[
31
, Lemma 2.3]
.
Let us summarize the basic properties
of
\mathcal{L}_{\delta}(\xi)
;
see
[
1
, Lemma 8.2.1]
,

[
31
, (the proof of) Lemma 2.3]
,
and
[
8
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
0<\delta<\infty
. Then, the following statements hold.

(i)

0\leq\mathcal{L}_{\delta}(\xi)\leq\min(\xi^{2},\tfrac{3}{\delta}|\xi|)
.

(ii)
For fixed
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{L}_{\delta}(\xi)
is decreasing in
\delta
, and

(2.13)

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\mathcal{L}_{\delta}(\xi)=\xi^{2},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

(iii)
We can also write
\mathcal{L}_{\delta}(\xi)
as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\delta}(\xi)=\xi^{2}\big(1-h(\delta,\xi)\big),

where
h(\delta,\xi)
is given by

(2.14)

\displaystyle h(\delta,\xi)

\displaystyle=6\delta^{2}\xi^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}\pi^{2}(k^{2}\pi^{2}+\delta^{2}\xi^{2})}\in[0,1].

In particular, from
(
2.14
), we have

(2.15)

\displaystyle h(\delta,\xi)\leq\delta^{2}|\xi|^{2}

for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
.

In particular, from (
2.11
) and (
2.13
),
we have

(2.16)

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\widetilde{p}_{\delta}(\xi)=\xi^{3}

for each
fixed

\xi\in\mathbb{R}
.
We, however, point out that this convergence
is
not
uniform in
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
,
which is
the essential difficulty of the shallow-water limit problem
(as compared to the deep-water problem
where the corresponding convergence is uniform in
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
).
We also note that
from (
2.10
), (
2.11
), and Lemma
2.1

with (
2.3
), we have

For this purpose,

Definition 3.4
.

Let

.
We say that a bounded set

is equicontinuous
if

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\sup_{f\in E}\sup_{|y|<\varepsilon}\|f(\,\cdot+y)-f(\,\cdot\,)\|_{H^{s}}=0.

By Plancherel’s identity, (
3.27
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{f\in E}\|\mathbf{P}_{N}^{\perp}f\|_{H^{s}}=0.

The following lemma establishes
uniform (in
0<\delta\leq 1
) equicontinuity
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
s\geq 0
,
of solutions to the low frequency dynamics (

)
as well
as
local-in-time uniform (in
0<\delta\leq 1
) “equicontinuity”
with respect to the

-norm.

Given

let

Let

be the global-in-time solution
to the low frequency dynamics
(

)
with initial data

.
1
1

1

Recall our convention

corresponds to the solution to KdV (

with initial data

.

Then,
given any

we have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{0\leq\delta\leq 1}\|\mathbf{P}_{N}^{\perp}v_{\delta}^{\textup{low}}\|_{X_{\delta}^{s,\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}(T)}=0

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{0\leq\delta\leq 1}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\mathbf{P}_{N}^{\perp}v_{\delta}^{\textup{low}}(t)\|_{H^{s}}=0.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\mathcal{E}_{N,\delta}^{(1)}(\mathbf{v})\|_{C_{\tau_{0}}L^{2}_{x}}&\leq CN^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\mathbf{P}^{\perp}_{\delta^{-\frac{2}{5}+\theta}}\mathbf{v}\|_{C_{T}L^{2}_{x}}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}\\
&<\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}\end{split}

for any
0<\tau_{0}\leq 1
.

As for
\mathcal{E}_{N,\delta}^{(2)}(\mathbf{v})
,
we can proceed as in Case 2 above and obtain

(3.63)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\mathcal{E}_{N,\delta}^{(2)}(\mathbf{v})\|_{C_{\tau_{0}}L^{2}_{x}}<\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}\end{split}

for any
sufficiently small

\delta=\delta(\varepsilon^{\prime\prime},N)>0

and

0<\tau_{0}\leq 1
.
Putting (
3.56
), (
3.61
), (
3.62
), and (
3.63
)
together, we conclude that

(3.64)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{\delta\to 0}\|\mathcal{E}_{N,\delta}(\mathbf{v})\|_{H^{0,0}(\tau)}&=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\|\mathcal{E}_{N,\delta}(\mathbf{v})\|_{L^{2}_{\tau}L^{2}_{x}}\leq\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\lim_{\delta\to 0}\|\mathcal{E}_{N,\delta}(\mathbf{v})\|_{C_{\tau}L^{2}_{x}}\\
&=0.\end{split}

Finally,
given small
\varepsilon>0
,
it follows from
interpolating (
3.55
) and (
3.64
)
that there exists
\delta_{3}=\delta_{3}(\varepsilon,N,T)>0

such that

(3.65)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}_{N,\delta}(\mathbf{v})\|_{H^{0,\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}(\tau)}<\frac{\varepsilon}{6}

for any
0<\delta<\delta_{3}
.

Hence, from (
3.50
), (
3.53
), (
3.54
), and (
3.65
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}_{N}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{\delta})\|_{H^{0,\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}(\tau)}<\varepsilon

for any
0<\delta<\min(\delta_{2},\delta_{3})
,
from which we obtain

(3.66)

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\|\mathbf{P}_{N}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{\delta})\|_{C_{\tau}L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\lim_{\delta\to 0}\|\mathbf{P}_{N}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{\delta})\|_{H^{0,\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}(\tau)}=0.

By iterating the argument,
we have

(3.67)

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\|\mathbf{P}_{N}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{\delta})\|_{H^{0,\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}([j\tau,(j+1)\tau]\cap[0,T])}=0

for
j=1,\dots,\big[\frac{T}{\tau}\big]
,
where
[x]
denotes the integer part of
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Therefore, from (
3.66
) and (
3.67
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\|\mathbf{P}_{N}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{\delta})\|_{C_{T}L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\lim_{\delta\to 0}\sum_{j=0}^{[T/\tau]}\|\mathbf{P}_{N}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{\delta})\|_{H^{0,\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}([j\tau,(j+1)\tau])\cap[0,T])}=0

for each
N\geq N_{2}
, where
N_{2}=N_{2}(\varepsilon^{\prime})=N_{2}(\varepsilon)
is as in
(
3.49
).
This proves (
3.47
)
and thus
concludes the proof of Proposition
3.3
.
∎

3.3.
On other frequency interactions

In Subsection

, we treated the case
\xi_{\max}\leq\delta^{-1}
.
In this subsection, we consider several other frequency interactions.

Lemma 3.6

(high
\times
high
\mapsto
low)
.

Let
s\geq 0
.
Then, for
b>\frac{1}{2}
and
b^{\prime}\leq\frac{5}{8}

(3.68)

\displaystyle\big\|\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}\partial_{x}\big((\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}^{\perp}v_{1})(\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}^{\perp}v_{2})\big)\big\|_{X^{s,b^{\prime}-1}_{\delta}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{2}\|\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}^{\perp}v_{j}\|_{X^{s,b}_{\delta}},

(3.69)

Proof.

In the following, we only prove (
3.68
)
since (
3.69
) follows from a straightforward modification.
As in the proof of Lemma
3.1
,
let
\xi
denote the spatial frequency
of

\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}\partial_{x}\big((\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}^{\perp}v_{1})(\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}^{\perp}v_{2})\big)
,
appearing on the left-hand side of (

We first consider the case
|\xi|\lesssim 1
.
In this case, by Hölder’s inequality and the
L^{4}
-Strichartz estimate (
3.10
), we have

\displaystyle\text{LHS of \eqref{bilin2}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{2}\|\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}^{\perp}v_{j}\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{2}\|\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}^{\perp}v_{j}\|_{X^{s,b}_{\delta}},

uniformly in
0<\delta\leq 1
, provided that

b\geq\frac{3}{8}

and

b^{\prime}\leq 1
.

Next, we consider the case
|\xi|\gg 1
.
It suffices to prove

(3.70)

\displaystyle\bigg\|\intt_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau=\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}\\
\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{1\ll|\xi|\leq\delta^{-1}}\cdot\xi}{\sigma^{1-b^{\prime}}}\frac{f_{1}(\tau_{1},\xi_{1})f_{2}(\tau_{2},\xi_{2})d\tau_{1}d\xi_{1}}{\sigma_{1}^{b}\sigma_{2}^{b}}\bigg\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{2}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi}},

uniformly in
0<\delta\leq 1
,
for any functions
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

such that

\supp f_{j}(\tau_{j},\cdot\,)\subset\{|\xi_{j}|>\delta^{-1}\}

for any
\tau_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
,
j=1,2
,
where
\sigma
and
\sigma_{j}
,

,
are as in (
3.11
).
For simplicity of notation,
we drop the frequency restrictions in the following
but it is understood that we work under the condition:

(3.71)

\displaystyle 1\ll|\xi|\leq\delta^{-1}<|\xi_{1}|,|\xi_{2}|.

In view of the symmetry, we assume

|\xi_{2}|\gtrsim|\xi|\gg 1

From Lemma

(ii) with (

\displaystyle\MAX\sim\delta^{-1}|\xi\xi_{2}|,

\bullet

From (

\displaystyle\frac{|\xi|}{\sigma^{1-b^{\prime}}}\lesssim\frac{|\xi|^{b^{\prime}}}{\delta^{-1+b^{\prime}}|\xi_{2}|^{1-b^{\prime}}}\lesssim\frac{|\xi|^{2b^{\prime}-1}}{|\xi_{2}|^{1-b^{\prime}}}\lesssim 1,

Remark 3.7
.

(i) In view of Lemma

4.
Failure of
C^{2}
-regularity of the residual dynamics

In this section, we present a proof of Theorem
1.1
(ii).
This type of instability was first established by Bourgain
[
4
]
;
see also
[
44
]
.
In the following, we closely
follow the argument in
[
36
]

for BO (
1.3
),
exploiting
the ‘low
\times
high
\mapsto
high’ interaction.
We present details for readers’ convenience
(and to reflect the difference between our problem and that in
[
36
]
).

Fix
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
.
Given small
\varepsilon>0
,
let
v=v(\varepsilon)
be the smooth solution to the scaled ILW (
1.4
)
with initial data
v|_{t=0}=\varepsilon\phi
.
Then, by writing it as

v=v^{\textup{low}}+v^{\textup{res}}
as in (
1.6
),
a direct computation with (
3.1
)
(where
\phi
is replaced by
\varepsilon\phi
)
gives

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}v^{\textup{low}}(\varepsilon)|_{\varepsilon=0}&=0,\\
\partial_{\varepsilon}v^{\textup{low}}(\varepsilon)|_{\varepsilon=0}&=\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}S_{\delta}(t)\phi.\end{split}

Similarly,
by writing
the residual dynamics (
1.8
) in the Duhamel formulation:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}v^{\textup{res}}(t)&=\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}^{\perp}S_{\delta}(t)\phi+\mathcal{I}_{\delta}\big((v^{\textup{res}})^{2}\big)(t)\\
&\quad+2\mathcal{I}_{\delta}(v^{\textup{res}}v^{\textup{low}})(t)+\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}^{\perp}\mathcal{I}_{\delta}\big((v^{\textup{low}})^{2}\big)(t),\end{split}

a direct computation with (
4.2
)
(where
\phi
is replaced by
\varepsilon\phi
)
and (
4.1
)
yields

(4.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}v^{\textup{res}}(\varepsilon)|_{\varepsilon=0}&=0,\\
\partial_{\varepsilon}v^{\textup{res}}(\varepsilon)|_{\varepsilon=0}&=\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}^{\perp}S_{\delta}(t)\phi,\\
\partial_{\varepsilon}^{2}v^{\textup{res}}(\varepsilon)|_{\varepsilon=0}&=\mathcal{I}_{\delta}\Big((\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}^{\perp}S_{\delta}(t)\phi)^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+2\mathcal{I}_{\delta}\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}S_{\delta}(t)\phi\cdot\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}^{\perp}S_{\delta}(t)\phi\Big)\\
&\quad+\mathcal{I}_{\delta}\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}^{\perp}\big((\mathbf{P}_{\delta^{-1}}S_{\delta}(t)\phi)^{2}\big)\Big).\end{split}

Fix
t\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and
0<\delta\leq 1
.
Given a positive integer
N\gg\delta^{-1}\geq 1
and small
\alpha=\alpha(\delta,N)>0
such that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\delta^{-1}\alpha N\sim N^{-\theta}

for some small
\theta>0
,
define
\phi=\phi(N,\alpha)
by its Fourier transform:

(4.5)

\displaystyle\widehat{\phi}(\xi)=\alpha^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big\{\mathbf{1}_{I_{1}}(\xi)+N^{-s}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{I_{2}}(\xi)+\mathbf{1}_{-I_{1}}(\xi)+N^{-s}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{-I_{2}}(\xi)\Big\},

where
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are given by

(4.6)

\displaystyle I_{1}=[\alpha,2\alpha]\qquad\text{and}\qquad I_{2}=[N,N+\alpha].

Note that

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{H^{s}}\sim 1.

From (
4.6
), we have
I_{1}+I_{2}=[N+\alpha,N+3\alpha]
.
Then, from (
4.3
), (
4.5
), and (
4.6
)
with (
3.2
), (
2.11
), and (
2.10
), we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{1}_{I_{1}+I_{2}}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{x}\big(\partial_{\varepsilon}^{2}v^{\textup{res}}(t;\varepsilon)|_{\varepsilon=0}\big)(\xi)=-\frac{2\xi e^{it\widetilde{p}_{\delta}(\xi)}}{\alpha N^{s}}\intt_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\\
\xi_{1}\in I_{1}\\
\xi_{2}\in I_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{e^{-it\widetilde{\Xi}_{\delta}(\bar{\xi})}-1}{\widetilde{\Xi}_{\delta}(\bar{\xi})}d\xi_{1}.\end{split}

From
Lemma
2.2
(ii) and (
4.4
), we have

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\Xi}_{\delta}(\bar{\xi})|\sim\delta^{-1}|\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|\sim N^{-\theta}.

Then, by a Taylor expansion, we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle-\frac{e^{-it\widetilde{\Xi}_{\delta}(\bar{\xi})}-1}{\widetilde{\Xi}_{\delta}(\bar{\xi})}=it+O(t^{2}N^{-\theta})=it+o(1),

as
N\to\infty
.
Recall from

[
39
, Lemma 3.5]

that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{a+I}*\mathbf{1}_{b+I}(\xi)\gtrsim|I|\cdot\mathbf{1}_{a+b+I}(\xi)

for any
a,b,\xi\in\mathbb{R}
and
any interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
.
Finally, if we suppose that the map:
\phi\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\mapsto v^{\textup{res}}(t)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
is
C^{2}
,
then it follows
from (
4.7
), (
4.3
), (
4.8
),
and (
4.9
) with (
4.10
) and (
4.4
) that

\displaystyle 1\sim\|\phi\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\gtrsim\big\|\partial_{\varepsilon}^{2}v^{\textup{res}}(t;\varepsilon)|_{\varepsilon=0}\big\|_{H^{s}}\gtrsim_{t}\frac{N^{1+s}\alpha^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\alpha N^{s}}=\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}N\sim\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{\frac{1-\theta}{2}}\longrightarrow\infty,

as
N\to\infty
(for each fixed
0<\delta\leq 1

and
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
),
which is a contradiction.
This proves
Theorem
1.1
(ii).

Polymer translocation through extended patterned pores: scaling of the total translocation time
Andri Sharma 1,2, Abhishek Chaudhuri 1, Rajeev Kapri 1

Abstract
We study the translocation of a flexible polymer through extended patterned pores using molecular dynamics (MD) simulations. We consider cylindrical and conical pore geometries that can be controlled by the angle of the pore apex α \alpha . We obtained the average translocation time ⟨ τ ⟩ \langle\tau\rangle for various chain lengths N N and the length of the pores L p L_{p} for various values α \alpha and found that ⟨ τ ⟩ \langle\tau\rangle scales as ⟨ τ ⟩ ∼ N γ ​ ℱ ​ ( L p ​ N ϕ ) \langle\tau\rangle\sim N^{\gamma}\mathcal{F}\left(L_{p}N^{\phi}\right) with exponents γ = 3.00 ± 0.05 \gamma=3.00\pm 0.05 and ϕ = 1.50 ± 0.05 \phi=1.50\pm 0.05 for both patterned and unpatterned pores.



I
Introduction

Translocation of polymer or proteins is an ubiquitous process inside biological cells and is among one of the important processes which propel the cell functionality
Alberts et al. 2002
;
van der Laan et al. 2007
. In the last two and half decades, polymer translocation has gained considerable attention due to its technological applications such as rapid DNA sequencing, gene therapy and controlled drug delivery
Salman et al. 2001
;
Ying et al. 2022
. It is now possible to fabricate synthetic nanopores in the laboratory which can remain stable at high temperatures, external drive or change in the solvent conditions. A single strand of a biopolymer such as DNA or RNA can be electrophoretically driven through such nanopores in a controlled manner. The ion current flowing through the nanopore gets blocked when the polymer is inside the pore which gives the signature of the polymer sequence
Kasianowicz et al. 1996
.

The translocation of polymer through nanopores depend on various parameters, such as, the length of the polymer chain, its structure, shape and the size of the pore and pore-polymer interactions
Muthukumar 2007
;
Muthukumar 2011
. Due to these factors, the translocation process is a complicated system dependent process.
Various coarse-grained models have been used to understand the translocation phenomenon
Sung and Park 1996
;
Muthukumar 2011
;
Palyulin et al. 2014
;
Rowghanian and Grosberg 2011
;
Sakaue 2007
;
Kantor and Kardar 2004
;
Dubbeldam et al. 2007
;
Ikonen et al. 2012a
. For example, the translocation process has been studied as a one-dimensional barrier crossing problem of the translocation coordinate through a slit (i.e., a pore of unit length). In initial studies, it was assumed that the translocation time is long enough to ensure the equilibration of the polymer conformations at every stage of the translocation process
Sung and Park 1996
;
Muthukumar 1999
;
Muthukumar 2011
. Later, a majority of experimental studies with synthetic nanopores showed that the time taken by the polymer to pass the nanopore is much less than its relaxation time, indicating that the translocation is a non-equilibrium process. The tension propagation (TP) theory proposed originally by Sakaue
Sakaue 2007
for an infinite chain and subsequently modified by Ikonen et al.
Ikonen et al. 2012a
;
Ikonen et al. 2012b
;
Sarabadani et al. 2014
;
Sarabadani and Ala-Nissila 2018
and Dubbeldam et al.
Dubbeldam et al. 2012
for finite chains has proven to be successful in explaining the non-equilibrium aspect of driven polymer translocation
Chen et al. 2021
. The most important quantity of interest in polymer translocation studies is the dependence of the average translocation time,
\langle\tau\rangle
, on the chain length
N
. Numerical simulations and experiments have indicated that
\langle\tau\rangle\sim N^{\beta}
. Different values of
\beta
have been observed in different studies, suggesting that
\langle\tau\rangle
depends on various physical parameters
Milchev 2011
. For a self-avoiding flexible chain,
\langle\tau\rangle
is known to scale as
\langle\tau\rangle\sim N^{1+\nu}
, here
\nu=3/4
in two dimensions
Chuang et al. 2001
;
Luo et al. 2007
;
Sarabadani and Ala-Nissila 2018
, and
\nu\approx 0.59
in three dimensions
Doi et al. 1988
;
Chuang et al. 2001
;
Kantor and Kardar 2004
.

Experimental studies on polymer translocation involve pores of finite lengths in which pore-polymer interactions play a dominant role
Storm et al. 2003
;
Wanunu and Meller 2007
;
Chen et al. 2004
;
Lan et al. 2011
;
Liu et al. 2012
. There have been theoretical and computational studies on finite-length pores where the shape of the extended pore is cylindrical with different patterned pore-polymer interactions
Luo et al. 2007
;
Luo et al. 2008
;
Cohen et al. 2012a
;
Cohen et al. 2012b
;
Katkar and Muthukumar 2018
;
Kumar et al. 2018
, or conical
Nikoofard et al. 2013
;
Nikoofard et al. 2014
;
Nikoofard and Fazli 2015
;
Sharma et al. 2022
;
Sharma 2024
;
Upadhyay et al. 2024a
;
Upadhyay et al. 2024b
. For cylindrical pores, it was estimated that for a fixed external drive
\langle\tau\rangle
depends on the patterning of the pores. It was found that
\langle\tau\rangle
is the maximum for a fully attractive pore and is minimum for a pore having repulsive exit
Cohen et al. 2012b
;
Kumar et al. 2018
. It was argued that the pattern stickiness of extended pores can be exploited to predict heteropolymer sequences with different bending rigidities to a high degree of accuracy by translocating them through multiple pores
Kumar et al. 2018
. For conical pores, we have one more parameter–the cone apex angle, which we represent by
\alpha
. For
\alpha=0
, we have a cylindrical pore. For a conical pore with attractive pore-polymer interactions,
\langle\tau\rangle
was found to be a nonmonotonic function of
\alpha
for lower driving forces, which becomes monotonic for higher forces
Sharma et al. 2022
;
Sharma 2024
.
The length of the extended pore,
L_{p}
, introduces an additional length scale to the problem. Although there have been many scaling studies of
\langle\tau\rangle
on the length of the polymer
N
, not much is discussed in the literature on the dependence of
\langle\tau\rangle
on
L_{p}
. We try to address this in this paper.

In this paper, we extend our work on conical pores
Sharma et al. 2022
;
Sharma 2024
to study the effect of different pore patterns on the driven-translocation process. The patterning is created along the pore length by having various sections with attractive and repulsive interactions.
The entrance of the pores is always attractive, so that the polymer can enter the pore with ease. The exit from the pore can either be attractive or repulsive.
The presence of an external drive along the channel often facilitates the translocation process. We present our study on translocation behavior by varying the pore length
L_{p}
and the polymer length
N
. The length of the pore is increased in such a manner that the pore-patterning ratios remain conserved. We record the translocation time through such pores for different external drives.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we define our model and simulation details. In Sec.
III
, we study the effect of the pore length,
L_{p}
, on
\langle\tau\rangle
. We also discuss the effect of pattered pores on
\langle\tau\rangle
and establish scaling relations between
\langle\tau\rangle
,
L_{p}
and the polymer length
N
for extended pores. In Sec.
IV
, we conclude by providing a discussion of the obtained scaling exponents.

II
Model

We study the pore-polymer system in two-dimensions. The polymer is modeled as a bead-spring chain
Grest and Kremer 1986
, where the consecutive beads of diameter
\sigma
are connected with a spring of spring constant
K
interacting via a harmonic potential given by:

U_{\textrm{bond}}=\frac{1}{2}K(r-r_{0})^{2},

(1)

where,
r_{0}
is the equilibrium distance. The non-bonded polymer beads interact via Weeks-Chandler-Andersen (WCA) potential
U_{\textrm{nb}}(r)
of the form:

U_{\textrm{nb}}(r)=\begin{cases}4\epsilon\Bigg[\left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{12}-\left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{6}\Bigg]+\epsilon,&r<r_{\rm min}\\
0,&r\geq r_{\rm min},\end{cases}

(2)

where,
\epsilon
is the strength of the potential. The cutoff distance
r_{\rm min}=2^{1/6}\sigma
is set at the minimum of the potential. The pores are static and are constructed with coarse-grained beads of size
\sigma
. We study three different types of extended pores as considered in Ref.
Kumar et al. 2018
:

(1)

Pore A is an attractive pore. Polymer beads interact with pore beads via the attractive Lennard Jones (LJ) potential,
U_{\textrm{pp}}(r)
:

U_{\textrm{pp}}(r)=\begin{cases}4\epsilon_{p}\Bigg[\left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{12}-\left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{6}\Bigg],&r<r_{c}\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(3)

where,
\epsilon_{p}
is the attractive strength of the pore and
r_{c}=2.5\sigma
is the cut-off distance.

(2)

Pore B has attractive entrances and exits separated by a repulsive section. The polymer beads interact with attractive beads of the pore via the LJ potential and with beads of the repulsive section via the WCA potential.

(3)

Pore C has an attractive entrance and a repulsive exit. The polymer beads interact with the beads of the attractive section of the pore via the LJ potential and the beads of the repulsive section via WCA potential.

In addition to the above potentials, an external pulling force,
f_{ext}
, which mimics an electrophoretic force, is applied inside the pore in the positive
x
-direction to ensure that the polymer translocates through the pore from the
cis
to the
trans
side. The force
f_{ext}
varies along the pore extension as

f_{ext}=\frac{f_{0}d}{(d+2x\tan\alpha)},

(4)

here
f_{0}
is the magnitude of the applied force at the pore opening
d
, which is equal to the constant force inside a linear channel with
\alpha=0^{\circ}
. Therefore, for a conical pore, the force decreases along its length. The schematic diagram of our coarse-grained model is shown in Fig.
1
(top row). The subsequent rows show the schematic diagram of three different pore patterns that we will study in this paper. The second row shows three different patterned pores for length
L_{p}=5
, here the pore in Fig.
1
(a1) is a completely attractive pore (Pore A), while the pore in Fig.
1
(b1) has an attractive entrance and an attractive exit but embedded with a repulsive bead in the middle (Pore B), and the pore in Fig.
1
(c1) has an attractive entrance but a repulsive exit (Pore C). In the third and fourth rows, we show pores for lengths
L_{p}=10
, and
L_{p}=15
, respectively. The length of the pore is increased in such a manner that the ratio of pore patterning remains conserved.

The dynamics of the polymer is governed by the stochastic Langevin equation. The equation of motion for the position of
i
th monomer is given by

m\ddot{\vec{r_{i}}}=-\eta\dot{\vec{r_{i}}}-\nabla\sum{(U_{bond}+U_{nb}+U_{pp})}+f_{ext}+\zeta,

(5)

where
m
and
r_{i}
are the mass and the position of the
i^{th}
bead of the polymer,
\eta
is the friction coefficient of the fluid,
\zeta
is an uncorrelated random noise that obeys the fluctuation-dissipation relation
\langle\zeta_{i}(t)\zeta_{j}(t^{\prime})\rangle=2K_{B}T\eta\delta_{ij}\delta(t-t^{\prime})
.
In our model,
\epsilon
,
\sigma
and
m
set the units of energy, length, and mass, respectively. This sets the unit of time as
(m\sigma^{2}/\epsilon)^{1/2}
. The force value is set in the unit of
\epsilon/\sigma
and the spring constant in the units of
\epsilon/\sigma^{2}
. Using this unit, the dimensionless parameters
k_{B}T=1
,
\eta=1
,
d=2.25
, and
K=500
are chosen in the simulations. For every simulation data point, the results are averaged over 1500-2000 successful translocation events. MD trajectories are obtained using LAMMPS
Thompson et al. 2022
software.

The length of the pores,
L_{p}
, plays an important role in the translocation of a polymer through a membrane channel. To study how the translocation time depends on the length of the pores, we consider a polymer of length
N=128
and translocate it through pores of varying lengths
L_{p}\in[5\sigma
,
10\sigma]
. For each pore length, we also vary the angle of the cone apex,
\alpha
, in the range
\alpha\in[0,5]
degrees. The magnitude of the external force is kept constant at
f_{0}=1
. The pore-polymer interaction is attractive in nature and is modeled using Eq.(
3
). In Fig.
2
, we have plotted the average translocation time
\langle\tau\rangle
as a function of
\alpha
for various
L_{p}
. The plot clearly shows that, irrespective of the value of
\alpha
,
\langle\tau\rangle
decreases with increasing pore length
L_{p}
.

For
\alpha=0^{\circ}
the value of
\langle\tau\rangle
obtained for
L_{p}=5\sigma
is almost three times that for
L_{p}=10\sigma
. This can be understood as follows: At
\alpha=0^{\circ}
the pore is cylindrical in geometry with a diameter
d=2.25\sigma
, which allows only a single-file entry and exit from the pore. For such symmetrical pores, the extent of the external force increases as the pore length is increased. For example, for a completely filled pore of length
L_{p}=10\sigma
, the number of beads experiencing force is almost twice the number of beads for that of
L_{p}=5\sigma
. Therefore, the polymer can be driven easily through a longer pore.

For
\alpha>0^{\circ}
, i.e., for conical pores, the diameter of the channel increases as one moves from the apex to the base of the pore. The diameter at the narrow entrance remains the same as that for a cylindrical pore (i.e.,
d=2.25\sigma
), while the diameter of the wider end increases by an amount
2L_{p}\sin\alpha
[i.e.,
d(x)=d+2x\sin\alpha
]. Now as the area available on the wider end of the conical channel increases, the single-file nature of the translocation process is no longer true and more configurations of the polymer are possible, this gain in the conformational entropic force
Nikoofard et al. 2013
assists the polymer to escape faster from the channel. This is indeed seen for shorter pore lengths, e.g.,
L_{p}=5
and 6, where
\langle\tau\rangle
decreases on increasing
\alpha
and saturates for
\alpha>2^{\circ}
. In contrast, for longer pore lengths, we see an opposite behavior. As the length of the pore increases, the diameter at the wider end increases proportionately. Since the external force inside the channel decreases as
f(x)\sim 1/x
(see Eq. (
4
)), it also becomes weaker at the wider pore exit for longer pores. Further, since the pore is attractive in nature there is a higher possibility for the polymer to have a configuration where the two consecutive monomers of the chain occupy side-by-side position along the
y-
direction at some location
x
in the channel, thus hindering translocation. Combining all these factors, it was observed that the total translocation time for longer pores increases as the pore apex angle
\alpha
is increased (e.g.,
L_{p}=10\sigma
in Fig.
2
).

Next, we study how
\langle\tau\rangle
varies with the chain length
N
for the pores of lengths
L_{p}=5\sigma
and
10\sigma
. Let us first consider translocation through a cylindrical pore (i.e.,
\alpha=0
). In Figs.
3
(a) and
3
(b), we have plotted
\langle\tau\rangle
as a function of
N
for a polymer translocating through a cylindrical pore of lengths
L_{p}=5
and 10, respectively for various values of driving forces
f_{0}=0.2
, 0.6 and 1.0. The translocation time exponent
\beta
is defined as
\tau\sim N^{\beta}
. Recall that, for a flexible polymer translocating from a slit, it was found that the exponent
\beta<1+\nu
, where
\nu
is the size exponent
Ikonen et al. 2012b
;
Ikonen et al. 2012a
. In 1- and 2-dimensions
\nu=1.0
and
0.75
, respectively. It is known that the value of exponent
\beta
depends on many factors such as chain length, chain flexibility, driving force, friction, and pore structure. This is also observed in our simulations. For a smaller driving force
f_{0}=0.2
, we found that the numerical value of the exponent for pore length
L_{p}=5
is
\beta=1.32
, which is smaller than
1+\nu=1.75
. On increasing the pore length to
L_{p}=10
, the value of the exponent becomes
\beta=1.68
but still less than 1.75. On increasing the driving force to
f_{0}=0.6
, the translocation exponent for
L_{p}=5
is
\beta=1.60<1.75
. However, for for pore length
L_{p}=10
, the exponent becomes
\beta=1.87>1.75
. For a driving force
f_{0}=1.0
, the value of the translocation exponent for pore lengths
L_{p}=5
and 10 are 1.80 and 1.95, respectively, both are greater than 1.75 but less than 2.

We have seen that the translocation exponent
\beta
varies with the length of the extended pore. We now seek to see whether the average translocation time
\langle\tau\rangle
for different pore lengths
L_{p}
and the polymer chain lengths
N
follow some scaling. To check this, we take the scaling form for
\langle\tau\rangle
:

\langle\tau\rangle\sim N^{\gamma}\mathcal{F}\left(L_{p}N^{\phi}\right),

(6)

where
\gamma
and
\phi
are exponents, and
\mathcal{F}
is the scaling function. In Fig.
4
, we have plotted
\langle\tau\rangle/N^{\gamma}
as a function of
L_{p}N^{\phi}
for pore lengths
L_{p}
varying from
5
to
10
and chain lengths varying from
N=160
to 512. A good data collapse is obtained for exponent values
\gamma=3.0\pm 0.05
and
\phi=1.50\pm 0.05
for three driven force values (
f_{0}=0.2
, 0.6, and 1.0) discussed in this paper. The scaling function follows a power law,
\mathcal{F}(x)\sim 1/x^{p}
, with a consistent exponent

for each of the three forces
f_{0}=0.2
, 0.6, and 1.0.

III.2
Effect of pore patterning

In this section, we discuss the effect of pore patterning (shown in Fig.
1
) on
\tau
for a polymer of length
N=128
. The translocation of flexible and semifexible polymers through cylindrical and conical pores has been studied in the past
Cohen et al. 2012a
;
Kumar et al. 2018
;
Upadhyay et al. 2024a
;
Upadhyay et al. 2024b
. It was observed that the translocation time and its standard deviation can be used to sequence the polymer by translocating the polymer multiple times through different patterned pores.
Our interest in this paper is to explore the dependence of
\tau
on the pore length
L_{p}
of various patterned pores. We increase the pore length in such a manner that the ratio of pore patterning remains conserved. As seen in the second, third and fourth rows of Fig.
1
, the ratio of repulsive pore beads (yellow bead) to attractive pore beads (blue) remains the same for three pores with pore lengths
L_{p}=5
, 10, and 15.

In Fig.
5
, we have plotted the total translocation time
\tau
for a flexible polymer of length
N=128
, as a function of the angle of the pore apex
\alpha
for three different pore lengths
L_{p}=5
, 10, and 15 at two different values of the driving force
f_{0}=0.6
and 1.0. The figure clearly shows that the behavior of
\tau
strongly depends on the type of pore and its length. For shorter pore lengths (e.g.
L_{p}=5
), we observe that for both force values,
\tau
decreases with
\alpha
and becomes saturated. This is true for the three types of pores studied in this paper (see Figs.
5
(a) and
5
(d)). Upon comparing
\tau
among these pores, we observed that the pore with a repulsive exit (shown by triangles) favors faster translocation at all angles. This is followed by the pore with an attractive entrance and exit with a repulsive bead in the middle (shown by circles), whereas the translocation is the slowest for a fully attractive pore (shown by squares). For intermediate-length pores (that is,
L_{p}=10
), the translocation time
\tau
first increases with
\alpha
for the force values
f_{0}=0.6
and 1.0.
For a fully attractive pore (see Fig.
1
(a2)) it reaches a maximum at about
\alpha\approx 3^{\circ}
and then decreases as
\alpha
increases further. For pore types with repulsive beads, either in the middle (see Fig.
1
(b2)) the middle portion or at the exit (see Fig.
1
(c2)), the value of
\tau
becomes saturated around
\alpha\approx 3^{\circ}
(see Figs.
5
(b) and
5
(e)). The above feature is more pronounced for lower force values
f_{0}=0.6
. When the pore length increases further (e.g.
L_{p}=15
), the translocation time first decreases with
\alpha
. It reaches a minimum value at some value of
\alpha
, which is different for different types of pores, and then
\tau
increases as
\alpha
increases further (see Figs.
5
(c) and
5
(f)).

To obtain the scaling of the averaged total translocation time with pore length
L_{p}
and chain length
N
for patterned pores, we again use the scaling relation of the form:

\langle\tau\rangle\sim N^{\gamma}\mathcal{H}\left(L_{p}N^{\phi}\right),

(7)

where
\phi
and
\gamma
are exponents, and
\mathcal{H}
is the scaling function. We observe that when
\langle\tau\rangle/N^{\gamma}
is plotted as a function of
L_{p}N^{\phi}
, for various pore lengths
L_{p}
and chain lengths
N
, the data collapse to a scaling curve
\mathcal{H}(x)\sim 1/x^{q}
. The exponents
\gamma
and
\phi
are remarkably robust, showing no dependence on whether the pore is cylindrical (i.e.
\alpha=0
) or conical (
\alpha\geq 0
) in shape. Adjusting the proportionality constant in the scaling function
\mathcal{H}
, we consistently obtain the exponent
q\sim 0.8
for the cylindrical pore and
q\sim 0.6
for the conical pore, respectively. Furthermore, the exponents do not vary much with
f_{0}
. The data collapses for
\alpha=0
and
\alpha=3
are shown in Figs.
6
and
7
, respectively.

IV
Conclusion

We have studied the effect of polymer length, pore length, and pore patterning on the total translocation time
\tau
for cylindrical pores (i.e.
\alpha=0
) and conical pores with various apex angles
\alpha
. For a polymer of fixed length
N=128
(see Fig.
2
), we found that the behavior of the total translocation time
\tau
changes from a decreasing function of
\alpha
to a strictly increasing function of
\alpha
when the pore length increases from
5\sigma
to
10\sigma
.
The two opposite translocation behaviors seen for the shorter and the longer pore lengths are nonscalable, showing the nonequilibrium nature of the driven polymer translocation process. We also observe that for a fixed angle of the pore apex
\alpha
, the translocation time is a decreasing function of the pore length (
L_{p}
). Because the extent of the external driving force is larger for longer pore lengths, the driven translocation process is favored for longer pores.

We find that, the average total translocation time
\langle\tau\rangle
scales with the polymer lengths
N
as
\langle\tau\rangle\sim N^{\beta}
, where the translocation exponent
\beta
depends on the pore length
L_{p}
as well as the driving force. We found that for smaller driving force
f=0.2
the value of the exponent is
\beta<1+\nu=1.7
for pore lengths
L_{p}=5
and 10. However, for large driving force
f=1.0
, the exponent
1.75<\beta<2.0
for both the pore lengths. For single bead pores, Kantor and Kardar argued the upper bound of the exponent to be 1.75
Kantor and Kardar 2004
. We present a new set of exponents for extended pore, which is different from single bead exponent. A plausible explanation could be that by increasing the pore length or the driving force reduces the embedded dimension for the polymer from two- to one and hence the upper bound for the translocation exponent for an extended pore should be 2.

We also observed that the translocation time,
\langle\tau\rangle
, scales with the pore length
L_{p}
and the polymer length
N
according to the relation

\langle\tau\rangle\sim N^{\gamma}\mathcal{F}(L_{p}N^{\phi}
),
where
\gamma=3.0\pm 0.05

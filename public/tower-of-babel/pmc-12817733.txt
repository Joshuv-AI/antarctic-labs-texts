Correlation between hemodynamic characterizations of cervical arteries and changes in cerebral microcirculation under dobutamine stress: a self-controlled study
Authors: Xia Ma, Pengling Ren, Dong Liu, Yawen Liu, Linkun Cai, Rui Wang, Erwei Zhao, Zixu Huang, Fengxia Yu, Peng-Gang Qiao, Wei Zheng, Xiangdong Hu, Xian-Quan Shi, Zhenchang Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDobutamine, an inotropic agent, is known to enhance cardiac function. However, its concurrent effects on cervical artery hemodynamics and cerebral microcirculation, along with their potential correlation, remain unclear. This study aimed to investigate these relationships in healthy volunteers under dobutamine stress.MethodsIn this self-controlled study, cervical artery hemodynamic parameters, including vessel diameters, peak systolic velocity, end-diastolic velocity (EDV), mean flow velocity (MV), resistance index and pulsatility index of cervical arteries parameters of 20 healthy volunteers were obtained via ultrasound. Whole-brain cerebral blood flow (wCBF_ASL) and cerebral blood flow in regions of interest (CBF_ROI) were obtained by magnetic resonance imaging using arterial spin labeling before and during the dobutamine stress test. Blood flow (BF) in the cervical arteries were calculated from the MV and the vessel diameter. Ultrasound-derived cerebral blood flow (CBF_US) was defined as the sum of the BF from the left and right internal carotid arteries (ICA) and the vertebrobasilar artery.ResultsOverall cerebral blood flow, measured by both CBF_US and wCBF_ASL, remained unchanged during dobutamine stress (P > 0.05). However, dobutamine induced significant reductions in CBF_ROI in specific regions, including the right middle temporal pole, left middle temporal pole, and left cerebellum (P < 0.05). Notably, linear regression analysis revealed that the change in EDV of the right internal carotid artery (ICA-R) was a significant positive predictor of the CBF change in the right middle temporal pole (B = 0.32, p = 0.042).ConclusionsDobutamine administration can induce regional cerebral hypoperfusion despite stable global cerebral blood flow. Alterations in ICA hemodynamic parameters are correlated with these regional perfusion deficits. These findings suggest that monitoring cervical artery hemodynamics during dobutamine administration may offer insights into regional cerebral perfusion changes, and could potentially be explored as a complementary tool for risk stratification in patients undergoing dobutamine stress tests.Keywords: Arterial spin labeling, Blood flow, Brain perfusion, Dobutamine stress, Ultrasound

Background

Dobutamine is an inotropic drug that enhances cardiac contractility and increases the heart rate (HR), thereby increasing the cardiac output (CO) and blood pressure (BP). It is widely used in myocardial perfusion stress testing to detect coronary artery disease and is also used in septic shock patients to increase CO and correct hypoperfusion [1–3]. However, the impact of these systemic hemodynamic changes on the cerebrovascular system remains incompletely understood [4].

Maintaining optimal brain health is a complex process [5–8], critically dependent on the precise regulation of cerebral blood flow (CBF) to meet the brain’s high metabolic demands [9]. This delicate autoregulatory mechanism, however, can be compromised by systemic instabilities. Specifically, when sustained reductions in cardiac output (CO) and blood pressure (BP) exceed the brain’s compensatory limits, cerebral hypoperfusion ensues, leading to a spectrum of adverse outcomes such as falls, dizziness, cerebrovascular disease, mild cognitive impairment, and dementia [10]. Therefore, it is important to study the effects of dobutamine on global CBF and region-of-interest–based CBF (CBF_ROI). A study performed on septic patients with altered mental status showed that dobutamine infusion increased CBF, as measured by the mean flow velocity using transcranial Doppler imaging (TCDI) [11]. In an animal study of a murine model of subarachnoid hemorrhage, CBF was measured using continuous arterial spin labeling (ASL), which showed that cardiac support with dobutamine improved CBF [12]. In contrast, a clinical study of five patients with severe head trauma reported that dobutamine caused a decrease in CBF, as measured by TCDI [13]. A recent study using 3D pseudocontinuous ASL (3D-pcASL) to assess CBF in healthy young adults under dobutamine stress showed that CBF significantly decreased in the frontal lobe anterior circulation [14]. Given these studies, the effects of dobutamine-induced stress on CBF are not consistent, which reflects the complex regulation mechanism of CBF under dobutamine stress. Until now, the effect of dobutamine infusion on CBF_ROI has not been definitively elucidated.

The brain’s blood supply is delivered via the cervical arteries, primarily the internal carotid (ICA) and vertebrobasilar arteries (VA), while the external carotid artery (ECA) perfuses extracranial structures. These vessels exhibit distinct physiological responses to systemic hemodynamic changes due to their different downstream vascular beds [15]. Previous research has shown that stimuli such as exercise or dobutamine can induce differential flow changes between the ICA and ECA [15, 16]. However, a comprehensive assessment of dobutamine’s effects across the complete cervical arterial system—including the common carotid artery (CCA) and VA—remains limited.

Ultrasound can be used to estimate total CBF (CBF_US) by measuring and summing the blood flow (BF) through both the ICA and VA systems, a method reported in several studies [17–20]. Velocity parameters of the ICA and VA have been correlated with cerebral perfusion. One study reported that decreased CBF is associated with a decrease in velocity in the ICA and VA [21]. The resistance index (RI) and pulsatility index (PI), calculated from ultrasound data, are used to assess downstream resistance in a pulsatile vascular system. As the vessel resistance to flow increases, the RI also increase. Therefore, in addition to velocity, the RI and PI can be used to evaluate the resistance and and perfusion of the downstream blood vessel. It is plausible that changes in these easily accessible, upstream vascular parameters could be correlated with perfusion changes within specific brain regions. To date, however, the relationship between ultrasound-derived metrics of the ICA and VA and CBF_ROI measured by MRI has not been established.

In the present study, we examined changes in the CBF_US and parameters of the ICA and VA using ultrasound technology, and examined changes in the wCBF_ASL and CBF_ROI using 3D-pcASL under dobutamine stress. At the same time, we tried to find the relationship between changes in the parameters of the ICA and VA and changes in the CBF_ROI of healthy subjects after dobutamine infusion. Finally, we explored the changes in the BF in the CCA, ICA, ECA, and VA after dobutamine infusion.

Therefore, this study aimed to provide a multi-modal assessment of cerebrovasular dynamics under dobutamine stress in healthy volunteers. Our primary objective was to investigate the correlation between hemodynamic changes in the cervical arteries, measured by ultrasound, and concurrent changes in regional cerebral blood flow, quantified by 3D-ASL. Secondary objectives included characterizing dobutamine-induced alterations in global CBF and comprehensively profiling the flow dynamics across the CCA, ICA, ECA, and VA systems.

Materials and methods

Participants

Twenty healthy participants who responded to an advertisement were recruited for this study. The inclusion criteria were men and women aged 18 to 35 years. None of the participants had hypertension, hyperlipidemia, diabetes, or cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease that could influence CBF. The exclusion criteria were plaque identified in the carotid artery, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) ineligibility (e.g., heart valve replacement or implanted metal devices) and contraindications to dobutamine infusion (e.g., tachyarrhythmia). All participants were instructed to avoid vigorous exercise, caffeinated beverages, and alcohol for at least 24 hours before testing.

This study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of Beijing Friendship Hospital, in affiliation with Capital Medical University (2020-P2-155). Before the experiment, all participants signed an informed consent form.

Study design and stress test procedure

This self-controlled study investigated changes in the ultrasound hemodynamic parameters of CCA, ICA, ECA, and VA, as well as cerebral microcirculation perfusion under dobutamine stress and the relationship between them. Figure 1 shows a flowchart of the experiment. After all participants underwent MRI scans in the resting state, ultrasound scans of the CCA, ICA, ECA, and VA were performed. The infusion of dobutamine was then initiated according to standard procedures, with a starting dose of 5 to 20 μg/kg/min. The criteria for achieving cardiac stress The criteria for achieving cardiac stress were either a 20% increase in the participants’ systolic BP or a 50% increase in their HR compared to the resting state. Electrocardiograms, HR, and BP of participants were measured using a portable multiparameter vital sign monitor (Mindray, BeneVision, Shenzhen, China) before and after dobutamine infusion. If any discomfort occurred during this procedure or the systolic BP reached 220 mmHg, the infusion was discontinued. After the participant’s physiological state had stabilized, an MRI scan followed by an ultrasound scan was performed during a dobutamine stress-induced state. A steady state is defined as a change in the mean arterial pressure of less than 5 mmHg within 2 min. At the end of the scan, the dobutamine dose was slowly reduced, and physiological data were monitored continuously until the HR, BP, and blood oxygen levels stabilized. There were no adverse reactions in the 20 healthy volunteers after dobutamine infusion, nor at any time during the experiment.

US data acquisition and analysis

After the participants’ physiological state had stabilized, the ultrasound scan was performed. All ultrasound examinations were performed by a single, experienced sonographer to minimize inter-operator variability and ensure consistent data acquisition. The sonographer used a color ultrasonic diagnosis system (Resona 7, Mindray, Shenzhen, China) equipped with a 3–9 MHz linear transducer. The diameters of the CCA, ICA, and ECA were measured 1.0–1.5 cm distal to the carotid bifurcation, and of the VA at the C3–C5 level using B-mode images. Color Doppler images and the pulsed-wave Doppler frame were also acquired in the area where the diameters were measured at each time point. The probe position remained stable so that the insonation angle was less than 60 degrees. For each pulsed-wave Doppler examination, the parameters (including PSV, EDV, MV, RI, and PI) were recorded three times over 10–15 cardiac cycles, based on automatically traced velocity waveforms being traced automatically. Systolic and diastolic diameters were measured, and the mean diameter (Dmean) was calculated as follows: [(systolic diameter × 1/3)] + [(diastolic diameter × 2/3)] [17, 22, 23]. The BF of the CCA, ICA, ECA, and VA was calculated from the MV and the diameter using the following equation: BF = π ×(Dmean/2)2 × MV × 60(ml/min) [24]. The CBF_US was defined as the sum of the BF from the right ICA (ICA-R), left ICA (ICA-L), right VA (VA–R), and left VA (VA–L).

MRI data acquisition

All participants used a 3.0-T MRI scanner (Ingenia 3.0 T; Philips Healthcare, Best, The Netherlands) with a 16-channel phased-array coil to carry out the inspection. All volunteers underwent high-resolution T1-weighted imaging (T1WI) and 3D-pcASL scans while in the resting state and in the stress state. The high-resolution T1-weighted imaging parameters were as follows: 200 axial slices, slice thickness = 1.0 mm, repetition time (TR) = 10.3 ms, echo time (TE) = 1,154.7 ms, field-of-view (FOV) = 240 mm × 240 mm. The 3D-pcASL sequence parameters were as follows: 36 axial slices, slice thickness = 4 mm; post-label delay = 1,500 ms; TR/TE = 4,852/10.7 ms; reconstruction matrix = 128 × 128; FOV = 240 × 240 mm; number of excitations = 3. Participants were instructed to close their eyes, not to think about anything in particular, and avoid falling asleep.

Quantitative CBF maps

Quality controls were used to exclude images with artifacts, such as head movement, before the calculations were performed. ASL difference images were calculated by subtracting the label image from the control image, and were averaged to estimate the CBF maps, using the proton-density-weighted images as reference. Data preprocessing was performed using statistical parametric mapping software (SPM12) and GNU/GPL toolbox for Data Processing and Analysis of Brain Imaging (DPABI), which was implemented in MATLAB (Version R2019a; MathWorks, Natick, MA, USA). We used the nonlinear transformation of SPM12 to register the CBF images with a positron emission tomography-perfusion template provided by the Montreal Neurological Institute (MNI) space. All participants’ CBF maps underwent normalization by using DPABI. Subsequently, the quality of spatial normalization was assessed by overlaying the normalized individual brain onto the standard MNI template. Each scan was checked to ensure that it fit well within the template without significant distortion, signal loss, or non-brain tissue inclusion. To reduce the inter-individual differences and improve the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), each co-registered CBF image was spatially smoothed by a Gaussian kernel of 6 × 6 × 6 mm half-maximum total width. Using the whole brain template in the MNI space, whole-brain perfusion was extracted from smooth CBF images. The brain was also segmented into 116 regions from the automated anatomical labeling template, and these regions were selected as regions of interest (ROI) for region-specific analysis (Fig. 2).

Statistics analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for SPSS 20.0 (Chicago, IL, USA). The normality of data distribution for all continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. If the data of the physiological parameters, ultrasound parameters of the ICA and VA, gCBF, wCBF, and the CBF from the 116 ROIs were normally distributed, the data were presented as the mean ± SD, and the before- and after-infusion comparisons were analyzed using a paired t-test. If the data were not normally distributed, the data were reported as the median (interquartile range), and the comparisons were analyzed using a non-parametric test. The significance level was set at p < 0.05. Simple linear regression analyses were performed to investigate associations. For the ICA-R (and ICA-L), changes in ultrasound parameters (PSV, EDV, MV, RI, PI) were introduced as independent variables, with changes in CBF of Temporal_Pole_Mid_R (Temporal_Pole_Mid_L) as the dependent variable. Similarly, for the VA–L/VA–R, changes in ultrasound parameters (PSV, EDV, MV, RI, PI) served as independent variables, with changes in CBF of Cerebellum_3_L as the dependent variable. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

In this study, we used ultrasound to examine the effect of dobutamine on the CCA, ICA, ECA, and VA and calculated CBF_US as the sum of the BF of the ICA and VA. We also used 3D-pcASL to examine the effect of dobutamine on wCBF_ASL and CBF_ROI. The results showed no significant change in either CBF_US or wCBF_ASL in healthy young people after dobutamine infusion. However, CBF in the Temporal_Pole_Mid_R, Temporal_Pole_Mid_L, and Cerebellum_3_L decreased after dobutamine infusion. Furthermore, significant changes were observed in the ultrasound parameters of the ICA and VA after dobutamine infusion. Based on the separate simple linear regressions, the change of EDV of ICA-R was associated with change in the CBF of Temporal_Pole_Mid_R.

CBF_US and wCBF_ASL responses

The blood supply to the brain depends on the ICA-R, ICA-L, VA–R, and VA–L which anastomose together to form the circle of Willis [25, 26]. In the current study, examination of all four arteries enabled the calculation of CBF_US.The data showed that in healthy volunteers, CBF_US showed no significant change under dobutamine stress. Similarly, wCBF_ASL also showed no change under dobutamine stress. This demonstrates that the changes in CBF measured by the two methods are consistent. A study of five healthy volunteers showed that dobutamine (4 g · kg1 · min1) infusion did not significantly change the middle cerebral artery mean blood velocity estimated by TCDI [27]. Another study of healthy volunteers [14] reported that ultrasound Doppler determined ICA BF decreased under dobutamine stress [first a low-dose (5 g · kg1 · min1) and then a high-dose (15 g · kg1 · min1)] infusion. In contrast, some studies in septic patients with altered mental status showed that dobutamine infusion increased CBF [11, 28]. Similarly, a murine model of subarachnoid hemorrhage study showed that dobutamine infusion increased CBF [12]. These findings in septic patients with altered mental status are different from findings in healthy humans, who did not show an increase in CBF upon dobutamine infusion. The usual explanation is that patients with sepsis often have impaired CBF autoregulation [29]. When CBF autoregulation is impaired, dobutamine infusion increases CBF by increasing CO. Under normal CBF autoregulation, dobutamine infusion does not significantly increase CBF.

BF_of CCA, ICA, ECA and VA responses

Total BF in the cervical vessels increased during the dobutamine infusion. We observed that the BF of the CCA and VA–R increased significantly, whereas the BF of the VA–L increased, but the increase was not significant. The acute increase of BF in cervical vessels does not appear to cause cerebral overperfusion. The BF was redistributed between the ICA and the ECA; the BF of the ICA did not differ significantly before and after the infusion (p > 0.05), but the BF of the ECA increased appreciably during the infusions. Shigehiko Ogoh et al. [15] reported that the BF of the ICA decreased, whereas the BF of ECA increased, which indicates that the effects of dobutamine on the ICA and ECA are different. In the case of dobutamine-induced increases in CCA BF, increased ECA BF and brain regulation will maintain ICA BF. The present study found increased BF in both CCA and VA after dobutamine infusion. However, as the main blood vessels that supply blood to the brain, the contribution of ICA and VA BF to CBF varied during the dobutamine infusion. The BF of the ICA did not change significantly, but the BF of the VA–R significantly increased and the BF of VA–L also increased through not significantly after dobutamine infusion. One possible reason for this discrepancy may be that the ICA supplies blood to the brain and has no branches that supply blood to the face or neck. The two VAs course along the sides of the neck and merge with each other to form the basilar artery; in addition to supplying blood to the brain, they also supply blood to the spinal cord. Therefore, we speculated that the cerebral regulation of the ICA may be stronger than that of the VA. Differential changes in the BF of the ICA and VA were also observed in a study, which found that the BF of the VA increased markedly and the ICA BF declined during heavy exercise. The authors thought the increase in VA BF during dynamic exercise might compensate for the decrease in ICA BF via the circle of Willis [24]. The correlation between the ICA BF and VA BF during dobutamine infusion is unclear, and further exploration is needed.

CBF_ROI responses

The wCBF_ASL was within the normal range during the dobutamine infusion; however, there was a significant decrease in CBF in three brain regions—they were Temporal_Pole_Mid_R, Temporal_Pole_Mid_L, and Cerebellum_3_L. A study on 10 adult patients undergoing cerebral bypass surgery suggested that dobutamine increased graft flow [30]. Another study on five patients with symptomatic cerebral vasospasm after subarachnoid hemorrhage showed that dobutamine increased cerebral oxygen saturation of spasm-affected territories [13]. The change in the CBF of target brain regions in these previous studies differs from the changes observed in the present study. Dobutamine infusion increases the CBF of the target brain region in patients with vasospasm after subarachnoid hemorrhage and in patients undergoing cerebral bypass surgery. One possible explanation is that the infusion of dobutamine affects brain regions differently in healthy individuals compared to those with cerebral vasospasm or those undergoing bypass surgery. Patients with brain diseases are likely to have disrupted focal cerebral autoregulation, resulting in increased CBF in this area [30]. However, CBF among various brain regions is redistributed during dobutamine infusion in healthy people. Some studies on physical exercise training-induced effects on CBF found that physical exercise alters CBF in specific regions of the medial temporal lobe [31–33]. After dobutamine infusion, the changes in CBF also occurred primarily in the temporal lobe. We found a decrease in CBF in the Temporal_Pole_Mid_R and Temporal_Pole_Mid_L after dobutamine infusion.

Ultrasound parameters of ICA and VA response

We found that during the dobutamine infusion, the CBF_US did not change significantly during the dobutamine infusion, however, the ultrasound parameters of the ICA and VA differed significantly. In response to the dobutamine-induced increase in systemic perfusion pressure, a healthy autoregulatory system actively constricts downstream cerebral arterioles to increase resistance and protect the brain from hyperperfusion. This physiological response was directly visualized in our Doppler measurements. Specifically, in the ICA, we observed a consistent trend where PSV tended to increase while EDV tended to decrease, with the changes being statistically significant for PSV in the ICA-L and for EDV in the ICA-R. The decrease in EDV is the direct consequence of increased downstream resistance [30], while the increased PSV reflects the heightened upstream driving force from the heart. Together, the elevated PSV and reduced EDV concurrently resulted in an increase in resistance-related parameters such as RI and PI. All of these are the direct sonographic manifestations of a cerebral autoregulatory response. The different response of the VA, where both PSV and MV increased significantly, is likely related to its mixed supply to both intracranial and extracranial territories, which are subject to different regulatory pressures.

Correlation between CBF_ROI and ultrasound parameters of carotid artery

This study found a significant positive relationship between the change in EDV of the ICA-R and the change in CBF of the right temporal pole (B = 0.32, p = 0.042). This positive correlation implies that a more significant decrease in EDV was associated with a more significant decrease in regional CBF. From a physiological perspective, a drop in EDV is a classic indicator of increased distal vascular impedance [34, 35]. Our results suggest that dobutamine induced cerebral vasoconstriction, which increased downstream resistance. This, in turn, led to a parallel reduction in both diastolic flow velocity (EDV) and regional tissue perfusion (CBF). The potential causes for this vasoconstriction are likely multifactorial. Firstly, it may represent a protective autoregulatory response to dobutamine-induced systemic hypertension, aimed at shielding the brain from pressure-related injury. Secondly, the weak but present direct α-adrenergic agonist activity of dobutamine could also have contributed to the constriction of cerebral arteries. The particular sensitivity of the right temporal pole to these changes is anatomically plausible, as this region is supplied by terminal branches of the middle cerebral artery, which is the largest terminal branch of the internal carotid artery. In summary, changes in ICA-R diastolic flow velocity are a key factor influencing CBF alterations in its distal territory, likely mediated by changes in cerebrovascular resistance.

However, a particularly intriguing aspect of our results was that this significant correlation was observed exclusively on the right side; no such association was found on the left. This lateralization could be attributed to several factors. First, inherent anatomical and hemodynamic asymmetries exist between the left and right carotid systems. The right common carotid artery originates from the brachiocephalic trunk while the left arises directly from the aortic arch, potentially leading to different flow dynamics. Second, this finding may reflect the functional lateralization of the brain. The right temporal lobe is known to be more involved in non-verbal and visuospatial functions, which might entail different metabolic demands and vascular regulation compared to the language-dominant left hemisphere. Finally, it is important to consider that the lack of a significant finding on the left side could be a limitation of statistical power. Future studies with larger sample sizes are needed to confirm this laterality and further explore its underlying mechanisms.

Limitations

This study is subject to several limitations. First, our study population comprised a relatively young (18–35 years old) and healthy adult cohort. While it is well-established that CBF declines with age [30, 36], a more critical consideration is that cerebrovascular reactivity and autonomic responses to pharmacological stress may be fundamentally different in older adults or those with subclinical vascular disease. Consequently, the generalizability of our findings to these populations is potentially restricted. Future research should incorporate a wider age range and individuals with diverse vascular health statuses to ensure the findings are comprehensive. Second, this study was restricted to a low dose of dobutamine. The impact of high-dose dobutamine infusion on cervical and CBF is still unknown and requires further study. Third, the modest sample size should be considered a limitation, potentially reducing the statistical power to detect smaller, yet clinically significant, effects. Consequently, future research with larger cohorts is warranted to confirm these preliminary findings and enhance their robustness.

Conclusions

In conclusion, global CBF, as measured by both CBF_US and wCBF_ASL, remained stable following dobutamine infusion. However, significant regional CBF decreases were observed in the Temporal_Pole_Mid_R, Temporal_Pole_Mid_L and Cerebellum_3_L. Concurrently, ultrasound parameters of the ICA and VA changed significantly. Notably, changes in the ICA-R’s MV and EDV were positively and negatively correlated, respectively, with the magnitude of CBF reduction in the Temporal_Pole_Mid_R. These findings highlight the clinical value of monitoring cervical artery hemodynamics during dobutamine administration, as these accessible measurements reflect significant changes in regional cerebral perfusion.

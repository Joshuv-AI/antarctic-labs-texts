Inverse theory of wavefront shaping in nonlinear scattering media
Authors: Daixuan Wu, Zhen Yi, Jiaming Liang, Tijian Li, Zhongzheng Zhu, Zexian Zhang, Haitao Ye, Jiawei Luo, Zhengyang Wang, Meng Liu, Zhi-Chao Luo, Yuecheng Shen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Scattering fundamentally limits the propagation of light in complex media, yet controlling it is essential for transformative advances in imaging, sensing, and optical communication. While decades of research have established powerful methods for linear wavefront shaping, the control of nonlinear scattering remains dominated by feedback-based optimization and neural networks — approaches that lack interpretability and theoretical bounds. Here, we establish the analytic inverse theory of nonlinear wavefront shaping under open-geometry scattering conditions with circular complex Gaussian statistics. By formulating an explicit scattering tensor model, we reveal how the optimal input field emerges from the dominant eigenchannel of the tensor’s spectral diagonalization. This framework directly leads to a closed-form enhancement bound for second-harmonic generation. We experimentally confirm the theory by shaping wavefronts to realize single-point focusing, multi-point focusing, and global second-harmonic signal enhancement in nonlinear scattering media. By bridging nonlinear optics and inverse wavefront control, this work transforms nonlinear wavefront shaping from an optimization-driven practice into a principled, interpretable, and prediction-capable discipline.

Introduction

Optical scattering, arising from the interaction of light with disordered or heterogeneous media, fundamentally limits imaging, manipulation, and computing. Advances in linear wavefront shaping have transformed this challenge into an opportunity: by measuring the scattering matrix and programming modulators1,2, one can deterministically control light through complex systems, enabling optical communication3, fiber endoscopy4, and speckle-based imaging5,6 in environments once considered opaque.

However, these linear approaches inherently assume linear propagation. As ultrafast optics continue to advance, the resulting high-intensity interactions make nonlinear effects inevitably emerge in scattering media7–10, rendering linear theory insufficient and bringing both challenges and opportunities for widespread applications. In biological tissue, nonlinear scattering governs multiphoton excitation—central to in vivo imaging11 and phototherapy12. In multimode fibers, random mode coupling acts as a form of scattering; controlling the resulting nonlinear evolution is essential for stable spatiotemporal mode-locking in high-power laser systems13–15. Beyond these concrete manifestations, optical nonlinearity combined with scattering also strongly influences photonic computing, where complex light-matter interactions in disordered platforms provide high-dimensional degrees of freedom for parallel and large-capacity information processing16,17. These examples highlight the need for predictive, physically grounded wavefront shaping that can tailor nonlinear interactions, not merely optimize them empirically. Currently, nonlinear wavefront shaping is dominated by feedback-driven iterative optimization18–21 and data-driven neural networks22,23. While successful in enhancing multiphoton signals, beam self-cleaning, and mode-locking, these black-box approaches lack interpretability, theoretical performance bounds, and generalizability. The absence of a rigorous analytical framework leaves fundamental questions unanswered—chief among them: What is the optimal nonlinear signal enhancement achievable for a given number of input and output modes?

In 2023, Choi’s group introduced the scattering tensor concept, describing nonlinear scattering via explicit polynomial input-output relations24. This forward model provided a concise physics-based description and surpassed heuristic approaches. Yet inverse control—determining the input wavefront that yields a target nonlinear output—remained analytically unsolved. Unlike in linear scattering, where each output mode maps directly to an input mode, enabling deterministic focusing with N degrees of freedom, nonlinear scattering involves N2 quadratic coupling terms, far exceeding available control parameters. Even dual-input modulation provides only 2N degrees of freedom, making the inverse problem severely underdetermined.

Here, we address this challenge by establishing the first analytic inverse theory of wavefront shaping in nonlinear scattering media. Modeling the medium as a third-order scattering tensor with circular complex-Gaussian statistics, we derive a closed-form algebraic framework that unifies tensor-vector interactions with a bilinear-quadratic structure. We further show that this bound can be approached through a dimension-reduced inverse design, in which an Autonne-Takagi factorization identifies the dominant nonlinear energy-coupling eigenchannel governing optimal wavefront shaping. These predictions are validated by numerical simulations and experiments demonstrating single-point focusing, multi-point focusing, and global SH enhancement using the proposed closed-form modulation. Collectively, our results complete the scattering-tensor framework by elevating nonlinear wavefront shaping from empirical optimization to a deterministic and physically interpretable inverse design paradigm, in which the optimal wavefront and its efficiency limits are revealed analytically.

Forward theory of the scattering tensor

We establish a forward model for the input-output relationship of light fields in nonlinear scattering media using a scattering tensor formalism. These locally generated SH fields then propagate and interfere, producing a speckle pattern at the output. Experimental verification of nonlinear wavefront focusing

To validate our theoretical framework, we first demonstrate nonlinear wavefront focusing—the fundamental operation in nonlinear wavefront shaping. A critical prerequisite is the accurate characterization of the scattering tensor, which presents experimental challenges due to the complexity of SH generation field measurements. Conventional holographic methods require a coherent SH reference beam, involving stringent alignment and suffering from quadratic phase ambiguities that are difficult to resolve. Benefiting from the physical dimension reduction that dictates quadratic nonlinear responses into a linear form, we develop a non-interferometric detection scheme tailored to the nonlinear scattering model, enabling direct measurement of the scattering tensor without phase-sensitive references. The experiment setup, shown in Fig. 2, employs a lithium niobate (LiNbO₃) powder diffuser as the nonlinear scattering medium. Due to the optical configuration formed by the two objective lenses, the field incident on the scattering medium corresponds to the Fourier transform of the SLM plane. This Fourier propagation step is inherently incorporated into the definition of the measured scattering tensor. A 1064 nm fundamental laser excites SH signals at 532 nm within the medium. Experimental verification of global SH signal enhancement

Beyond demonstrating single- and multi-point focusing, we further validate the scattering tensor framework by achieving global SH signal enhancement—optimizing nonlinear energy conversion across the entire output field rather than at localized spots. The measured scattering tensor inherently encodes the medium’s nonlinear light transport, enabling identification of dominant nonlinear eigenchannels via SVD. By modulating the SLM with phase masks approximating these eigenchannels, we efficiently excite nonlinear channels to enhance SH output system-wide.

Experimentally, the camera field of view was expanded to a 20 × 20-pixel grid (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$M=400$$\end{document}M=400 spatial modes) with a 10-pixel sampling interval to ensure spatial decorrelation. The SLM was partitioned into a 6 × 6 grid (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$N=36$$\end{document}N=36 segments), each controlling 30 × 30 physical pixels, resulting in 1296 quadratic input combinations. The contracted scattering tensor thus mapped input fields (1296 dimensions) to SH outputs (400 dimensions). SVD yielded 400 eigenchannels ranked by transmittance. As shown in Fig. 4a, sequential excitation of these eigenchannels revealed pronounced SH enhancement predominantly in the first 36 channels, corresponding to the input degrees of freedom, with the response saturating near unity beyond this range. This saturation reflects the quadratic SH generation process coupled with linear phase-only modulation constraints of the SLM, limiting mode overlap and causing residual incoherent scattering. Restoring SH generation in a deliquescent nonlinear crystal

To demonstrate the practical applicability of our nonlinear inverse theory, we apply tensor-based nonlinear modulation scheme to enhance SH generation in a deliquescent periodically poled lithium niobate (PPLN) crystal. PPLN, a widely used frequency-doubling material in laser systems, is highly susceptible to deliquescence, which damages its periodic domain structure and disrupts phase-matching conditions, causing a severe drop in SH generation efficiency under direct, unmodulated illumination. Due to the resulting phase mismatch, conventional illumination produces only weak SH signals39.

In our experiment, thin diffusers placed on both sides of the deliquescent PPLN were used to establish a nonlinear scattering environment, in which tensor-based wavefront shaping can be effectively applied to analyze the SH generation process. As illustrated in Fig. 5a, we hypothesize that precise characterization of the effective scattering tensor enables the design of tailored input wavefronts that compensate for local phase mismatches and restore quasi-phase-matching conditions. As shown in Fig. 5b, tensor-based nonlinear scheme achieves an average focusing enhancement of approximately 21.59×, reaching nearly 48.6% of the theoretical upper bound and outperforming the matrix-based linear scheme (~3.98×). Together, these results validate that tensor-based nonlinear modulation scheme can restore SH generation in a deliquescent nonlinear crystal, offering a non-invasive, alignment-free strategy to manage complex nonlinear light-matter interactions. This approach holds promise for extending the functional lifetime and performance of nonlinear optical devices in practical applications.

In summary, we have developed a physically grounded inverse theory for tensor-based nonlinear wavefront shaping and experimentally validated it across diverse scenarios, including nonlinear focusing, global SH enhancement, and SH recovery in a deliquescent PPLN crystal. By analytically extracting optimal excitation channels from scattering tensors, our work bridges nonlinear optical physics and deterministic wavefront control within a unified and scalable framework, establishing a solid foundation for both theoretical and experimental advances in nonlinear wavefront shaping.

Experimental configuration for nonlinear wavefront shaping

To characterize the scattering tensor in a LiNbO₃-based system, we implemented a non-holographic experimental setup centered on a 500 μm-thick powdered LiNbO₃ diffuser. A nanosecond pulsed laser (λ = 1064 nm, pulse width = 30 ns, repetition rate = 100 kHz, average power ≈ 420 mW) was used as the excitation source for SH generation. The beam passed through a half-wave plate and a polarization beam splitter to enable both power tuning and polarization control, ensuring optimal phase modulation by a reflective SLM (Holoeye PLUTO-2.1-NIR−149). To minimize optical loss, the unexpanded 6 mm diameter beam was directly modulated. Predefined phase masks were uploaded to the SLM to shape the wavefront, which was subsequently focused onto the nonlinear diffuser. The generated SH signal at 532 nm was collected by an objective lens, spectrally filtered by a bandpass filter, and further isolated by a linear polarizer. Detection was performed using a monochrome CMOS camera (FLIR BFS-U3-31S4M-C) in an intensity-only configuration, eliminating the need for reference beams or interferometric components. This common-path, non-holographic scheme ensures excellent mechanical stability and robustness, while still supporting downstream phase recovery through tensor-based wavefront shaping algorithms. The overall configuration enables efficient and accurate evaluation of nonlinear scattering responses, fully compatible with the theoretical scattering tensor framework.

Preparation of nonlinear scattering medium

To construct a highly scattering nonlinear medium with efficient SH generation, LiNbO₃ microparticles (mean diameter ~3 μm) were chosen for their large second-order nonlinear susceptibility. The particles were dispersed in a 75% ethanol-water solution to form a stable colloidal suspension. This suspension was spin-coated onto a standard glass substrate at 500 rpm for 60 s using a programmable coater. After coating, the sample was dried under ambient conditions to facilitate uniform particle sedimentation and suppress aggregation. The resulting LiNbO₃ film, with a typical thickness of ~500 μm, served as a robust nonlinear scattering layer for SH wavefront shaping experiments.

Abstract
The task of constructing infinite families of self-dual codes with unbounded lengths and minimum distances exhibiting square-root lower bounds is extremely challenging, especially when it comes to cyclic codes. Recently, the first infinite family of Euclidean self-dual binary and nonbinary cyclic codes, whose minimum distances have a square-root lower bound and have a lower bound better than square-root lower bounds are constructed in [ 6 ] for the lengths of these codes being unbounded. Let q q be a power of a prime number and Q = q 2 Q=q^{2} . In this paper, we first improve the lower bounds on the minimum distances of Euclidean and Hermitian duals of BCH codes with length q m − 1 q s − 1 \frac{q^{m}-1}{q^{s}-1} over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} and Q m − 1 Q − 1 \frac{Q^{m}-1}{Q-1} over 𝔽 Q \mathbb{F}_{Q} in [ 11 , 13 , 31 ] for the designed distances in some ranges, respectively, where m s ≥ 3 \frac{m}{s}\geq 3 . Then based on matrix-product construction and some lower bounds on the minimum distances of BCH codes and their duals, we obtain several classes of Euclidean and Hermitian self-dual codes, whose minimum distances have square-root lower bounds or a square-root-like lower bounds. Our lower bounds on the minimum distances of Euclidean and Hermitian self-dual cyclic codes improved many results in [ 6 ] . In addition, our lower bounds on the minimum distances of the duals of BCH codes are almost q s − 1 q^{s}-1 or q q times that of the existing lower bounds.

These results will be used in construction of self-dual codes with square-root-like lower bounds in later section. We start with the following lemma.

Lemma 5
.

Let

be positive integers such that

Proof.

Since
2\leq t\leq\frac{m}{s}-1

m-st\leq s
.
The proof for the cases
m-st=s
and
m-st<s
is distinct. Hence, the proof should be treated separately.

Case 1:

m-st=s
. In this case
1\leq u\leq q^{s}-1
and the
q
-adic expansion of
u
can be wrote as

u=(a_{s-1},a_{s-2},\ldots,a_{0})_{q}.

(2)

Let

2
) such that

.
Then the
q
-adic expansion of

can be expressed as

\begin{split}u(q^{s}-1)=\underbrace{a_{s-1},\ldots,a_{l+1}}_{s-l-1},a_{l}-1,\underbrace{q-1,\ldots,q-1}_{l},\underbrace{q-1-a_{s-1},\ldots,q-1-a_{l+1}}_{s-l-1},q-a_{l},\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{l})_{q}.\end{split}

(3)

From (
3
) the

-adic expansion of

can be expressed as

\begin{split}q^{m}-2q^{2s}+q^{s}+u(q^{s}-1)=&((\underbrace{q-1,\ldots,q-1}_{m-2s-1},q-2,\underbrace{a_{s-1},\ldots,a_{l+1}}_{s-l-1},a_{l},\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{l},\\
&\underbrace{q-1-a_{s-1},\ldots,q-1-a_{l+1}}_{s-l-1},q-a_{l},\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{l})_{q}).\end{split}

Then the coset leader of
\mathbb{C}_{q^{m}-2q^{m-st+s}+q^{m-st}+u(q^{s}-1)}

{\rm CL}((\underbrace{q-1,\ldots,q-1}_{m-2s-1},q-2,\underbrace{0,\ldots,0,}_{s-l-1}a_{l},\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{s-1},q-a_{l},\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{l})_{q}).

(4)

q^{2}-2s-2aq\leq 0,

which is the same as

q\leq\sqrt{2s+a^{2}}+a.

Hence, from (
28
) we know that

\displaystyle d(\mathcal{C^{\prime}_{\delta}})\geq\begin{cases}q^{m+1}-aq-s+1,\,\,&\text{if $q\leq\sqrt{2s+a^{2}}+a$,}\\
2\delta,\,\,&\text{if $q>\sqrt{2s+a^{2}}+a$}.\end{cases}

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 19
.

Let

and
m\geq 3

be given in (

).
Then
\mathcal{C^{\prime}_{\delta}}
is a linear code with parameters

, where

\displaystyle d(\mathcal{C^{\prime}_{\delta}})\geq\begin{cases}2q^{m}-2s+2,&\text{{ if $m$ is odd, $2\leq s\leq q-1$, $sq^{m}\leq\delta\leq(s+1)q^{m}-1$ and}}\\
&\text{ $(aq+s)q^{m-1}\leq\delta\leq(aq+s+1)q^{m-1}-1$},\\
2q^{m}-2aq-2s+2,&\text{ if $m$ is odd, $1\leq a\leq q-2$, $0\leq s\leq q-1$ and}\\
&\text{$(aq+s)q^{m}\leq\delta\leq(aq+s+1)q^{m}-1$},\\
2q^{m}-2q^{2}+2q-2s+2,&\text{ if $m$ is odd, $0\leq s\leq q-2$ and}\\
&\text{$(q^{2}-q+s)q^{m}\leq\delta\leq(q^{2}-q+s+1)q^{m}-1$},\\
2q^{m}-2q^{2}+4,&\text{if $m$ is odd and $(q^{2}-1)q^{m}\leq\delta\leq q^{m+2}-q^{2}+1$},\\
\end{cases}

and

d(\mathcal{C^{\prime}_{\delta}})\geq\delta

if one of the following statements hold:

(1)

if
m
is even,
2\leq a\leq q-2
,
0\leq s\leq q-1
,
q^{2}\geq aq+s
and

(2)

(3)

if

.

All
d(\mathcal{C})
mentioned above are better than the square-root lower bound.

Proof.

Similar as Theorem
18
, we only prove
d(\mathcal{C^{\prime}_{\delta}})
for the case that

,
1\leq a\leq q-2
and

.
In this case, from Lemma
17
and the BCH bound, we have

d(\mathcal{C}_{\delta})\geq(aq+s)q^{2t-1}\,\,\text{and}\,\,d(\mathcal{C}_{\delta}^{\perp H})\geq q^{2(m-t)+1}-aq-s+1.

It is easy to see that

IV-C

Self-dual codes with square-root lower bound

In this subsection, we construct several classes of Euclidean and Hermitian self-dual linear codes.
The following is a key proposition for us to get these codes.

Proposition 21
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with parameters
[n,k,d_{1}]
. Let
A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{array}\right)\in\mathbb{M}_{2\times 2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
with
ad\neq bc
and
{\mathcal{C}}=[{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{D}}^{\bot}]A
. Then
{\mathcal{C}}
is an
[2n,n,d]
self-dual code if and only if one of the following holds

(1)

2\mid q
,
a=b
,
c\neq d
and
{\mathcal{D}}
is dual-containing.

(2)

2\mid q
,
c=d
,
a\neq b
and
{\mathcal{D}}
is self-orthogonal.

(3)

q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
and
{\mathcal{D}}
is self-dual.

(4)

q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
,
\mu^{2}=-1
,
a=\mu b
and
c=-\mu d
, or
a=-\mu b
and
c=\mu d
.

(5)

q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
,
\mu^{2}=-1
,
a=\pm\mu b
,
c\neq\pm\mu d
and
{\mathcal{D}}
is dual-containing.

(6)

q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
,
\mu^{2}=-1
,
a\neq\pm\mu b
,
c=\pm\mu d
and
{\mathcal{D}}
is self-orthogonal.

(7)

q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
,
\mu^{2}=-1
,
a\neq\pm\mu b
,
c\neq\pm\mu d
and
{\mathcal{D}}
is self-dual.

Moreover,
d\geq\mbox{min}\{2d_{1},d_{1}^{\bot}\}
and the equality holds when
A
is also triangle, where
d_{1}^{\bot}
is the minimum distance of the dual code of
{\mathcal{D}}
.

Proof.

Let the generator and parity-check matrices of
{\mathcal{D}}
are
G
and
H
, then the generator matrix of
{\mathcal{C}}
is
\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}aG&bG\\
cH&dH\\
\end{array}\right)
. Hence,
{\mathcal{C}}
is self-orthogonal if and only if

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}aG&bG\\
cH&dH\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}aG&bG\\
cH&dH\\
\end{array}\right)^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}aG&bG\\
cH&dH\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}aG^{T}&cH^{T}\\
bG^{T}&eH^{T}\\
\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}(a^{2}+b^{2})GG^{T}&(ac+bd)GH^{T}\\
(ac+bd)G^{T}H&(c^{2}+d^{2})HH^{T}\\
\end{array}\right)=\mathbf{0}.

Notice that
GH^{T}=\mathbf{0}
and
HG^{T}=\mathbf{0}
. Due to the dimension, therefore,
{\mathcal{C}}
is self-dual if and only if
(a^{2}+b^{2})GG^{T}=(c^{2}+d^{2})HH^{T}=\mathbf{0}
.

We know that
{\mathcal{D}}
is self-orthogonal if and only if
GG^{T}=\mathbf{0}
and if and only if
HH^{T}=\mathbf{0}
. We divide the proof into the following parts:

\bullet

If
a^{2}+b^{2}=0
and
c^{2}+d^{2}=0
, then
a=b
and
c=d
when
2\mid q
and

a^{2}=-b^{2}
and
c^{2}=-d^{2}
when
-1
is square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, namely
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. Assume that
\lambda^{2}=-1
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
(a-b\mu)(a+b\mu)=0
and
(c-d\mu)(c+d\mu)=0
. From
ad\neq bc
, we have
a=b\mu
,
c=-d\mu
or
a=-b\mu
,
c=d\mu
.

\bullet

If
a^{2}+b^{2}=0
and
c^{2}+d^{2}\neq 0
, then we should have
HH^{T}=\mathbf{0}
, which means
{\mathcal{D}}
is dual-containing.

\bullet

If
a^{2}+b^{2}\neq 0
and
c^{2}+d^{2}=0
, then we should have
GG^{T}=\mathbf{0}
, which means
{\mathcal{D}}
is self-orthogonal.

\bullet

If
a^{2}+b^{2}\neq 0
and
c^{2}+d^{2}\neq 0
, then we should have
HH^{T}=\mathbf{0}
and
GG^{T}=\mathbf{0}
, which means
{\mathcal{D}}
is self-dual.

From Lemma
3
and the above discussion, the desired conclusion follows.
∎

V
Concluding remarks

The contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

(1)

Let
n=\frac{q^{m}-1}{q^{s}-1}
,
2\leq t\leq\frac{m}{s}-1
and
\frac{q^{st}-1}{q^{s}-1}<\delta\leq\frac{q^{st}-1}{q^{s}-1}+q^{t-1}
, the authors in
[
13
]
showed that
d({\mathcal{C}}_{\delta}^{\perp})\geq\frac{q^{m-ts}-1}{q^{s}-1}+1
. In Theorem
6
, we presented a new lower bound
d({\mathcal{C}}_{\delta}^{\perp})\geq q^{m-st}
, which is almost
q^{s}-1
times that of the existing lower bounds.

(2)

Let
n=\frac{Q^{m}-1}{Q-1}
,
2\leq t\leq m-1
and
\frac{Q^{t}-1}{Q-1}<\delta\leq\frac{Q^{t}-1}{Q-1}+q^{t-1}
, the authors in
[
11
]
showed that
d({\mathcal{C}}_{\delta}^{\perp})\geq\frac{Q^{m-t}q-q}{Q-1}+1
. In Theorem
9
, we showed a new lower bound
d({\mathcal{C}}_{\delta}^{\perp})\geq Q^{m-t}+1
, which is almost
q
times that of the existing lower bounds.

(3)

Let
n=\frac{q^{m}-1}{\lambda}
, or
n=q^{m}+1
, or
n=Q^{m}-1
, where
\lambda\,|\,q-1
and
\lambda\neq q-1
. We constructed several classes Euclidean and Hermitian linear codes with parameters
[2n,n]
(see Theorem
12
, Theorem
13
, Remark
14
, Theorem
15
, Theorem
18
and Theorem
19
). In many cases, these codes are Euclidean and Hermitian self-dual codes with minimum distance larger than square-root bounds (see Remark
22
and Remark
23
).

(4)

Let
n=\frac{q^{m}-1}{q^{s}-1}
, or
n=\frac{Q^{m}-1}{Q-1}
, where
s\,|\,m
. We obtained several classes Euclidean and Hermitian linear codes with parameters
[2n,n]
(see Theorem
16
and Theorem
20
). In many cases, these codes are Euclidean and Hermitian self-dual codes with minimum distance larger than square-root-like bounds (see Remark
22
and Remark
23
).

(5)

We compared some of our results with those presented in
[
6
]
. Our lower bounds are better than the results in
[
6
]
(see Remark

, Remark
25
and Remark

It is hard to construct an infinite family of self-dual cyclic codes with
the square-root lower bound. We welcome interested readers to join us in exploring this topic.

Biharmonic Curves in Warped Product Manifolds I × f M n ​ ( c ) I\times_{f}M^{n}\left(c\right)
Şaban Güvenç, Cihan Özgür

Abstract
. We explore the geometric properties of biharmonic curves in warped product manifolds of the form I × f M n ​ ( c ) I\times_{f}M^{n}(c) , where I I is an open interval and M n ​ ( c ) M^{n}(c) is a space of constant curvature. By establishing a main theorem, we analyze four distinct cases to reveal deeper curvature-related characteristics of these curves, including situations where they are slant. Finally, we construct two examples in I × f S 2 ​ ( 1 ) I\times_{f}S^{2}(1) .

Introduction

Let
f:(M,g)\rightarrow(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{g})
be a smooth map
from a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
into an
m
-dimensional Riemannian
manifold
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{g}).\ f
is called a
biharmonic

\tau_{2}(f)=-J^{f}(\tau(f))=-m\left(\Delta^{f}H-traceR^{\widetilde{M}}(df,\tau(f))df\right)=0,

(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{g})
and
\tau_{2}(f)
is
called the
bitension field of

[
17
]
.

It is easy to see that if
(\widetilde{M}(c),\widetilde{g})
is an an
m
-dimensional Riemannian space form of constant curvature
c
, then

\tau_{2}(f)=-m\Delta^{f}H+cm^{2}H.

Thus,
f
is biharmonic if and only if

\Delta^{f}H=cmH,

where
H
is the mean curvature vector field of
M
. In a different setting,
in
[
7
]
, B.-Y. Chen defined a biharmonic submanifold
M\subset\mathbb{E}^{n}
of the Euclidean space as its mean curvature vector field
H

satisfies
\Delta H=0
, where
\Delta
is the Laplacian (see also
[
8
,
9
]
). So Jiang’s and Chen’s definitions on biharmonicity
coincide if the ambient space is the Euclidean space (see
[
5
]
).

After Jiang’s definition, many results have been proven on biharmonic
submanifolds in different ambient spaces. See, for example,
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
9
,
20
,
22
]
, and the references therein.

Biharmonic curves have been widely studied in various geometric settings due
to their intrinsic mathematical significance and potential applications in
physics and engineering. See, for example,
[
8
,
12
,
13
]
,
and the references therein.

Warped product structures have been extensively studied in differential

]
). The classification of

[
6
,
10
,
14
,
15
]
. Motivated by these

, where

is an open interval,

is an
n

Biharmonic Curves in

n

dt^{2}
of

. Here

Remark 1
.

In a Riemannian manifold context, let us consider an open interval
I
with
a Riemannian metric
g_{B}
. If we take
g_{B}
as the Euclidean metric, we
lose no generality, because every Riemannian metric on a one-dimensional
manifold can be transformed into the Euclidean metric via reparametrization.
On a one-dimensional manifold, any Riemannian metric can be written as

g_{B}=\lambda(x)dx^{2},

where
\lambda(x)
is a positive continuous function of the coordinate
function
x
. However, defining a new parameter

t=\int\sqrt{\lambda(x)}\,dx,

in terms of this new coordinate, the metric takes the standard Euclidean
form
g_{B}=dt^{2}
. Thus, for an open interval
I
, taking the Euclidean
metric does not result in any loss of generality, as any other metric can be
reduced to it through a suitable coordinate change (see
[
18
]
).

Now, let us denote the unit vector field tangent to
I
by
\partial_{t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
and its dual by
\eta

\eta\left(X\right)=\widetilde{g}\left(X,\partial_{t}\right),\text{ }\forall X\in\chi\left(\widetilde{M}^{n+1}\right).

Any

can be written as the sum

which are called the

and the

\widetilde{g}\left(X,Y\right)=X_{1}Y_{1}+f^{2}g\left(X_{2},Y_{2}\right),

for
X,Y\in\chi\left(\widetilde{M}^{n+1}\right),

X=X_{1}+X_{2}
and
Y=Y_{1}+Y_{2}.
Notice that
\widetilde{g}\left(X_{1},Y_{2}\right)=0
and
\widetilde{g}\left(X_{2},Y_{1}\right)=0.
The Riemannian curvature tensor
of
\widetilde{M}^{n+1}
is obtained as

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}\left(X,Y\right)Z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{2}-\frac{c}{f^{2}}\right]\left[\widetilde{g}\left(X,Z\right)Y-\widetilde{g}\left(Y,Z\right)X\right]

\displaystyle+\left[\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}-\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{2}+\frac{c}{f^{2}}\right]\left[\widetilde{g}\left(X,Z\right)\eta\left(Y\right)\partial_{t}-\widetilde{g}\left(Y,Z\right)\eta\left(X\right)\partial_{t}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\eta\left(Y\right)\eta\left(Z\right)X+\eta\left(X\right)\eta\left(Z\right)Y\right],

(see
[
19
]

\gamma_{i}^{\prime}=\gamma_{i}^{\prime}\left(s\right)=\frac{d}{ds}\gamma_{i}\left(s\right),\text{ }\left(i=0,1,2,...,n\right)

throughout the paper. Here

denotes the coordinate function of
I

denotes the arc-length parameter of a given curve
\gamma=\gamma\left(s\right).
Also, note that we will write

where
\theta
is an angle function along the curve.

Let
\gamma:J\rightarrow\widetilde{M}^{n+1}
be a unit-speed smooth curve
with arc-length parameter
s\in J

\gamma_{0}:J\rightarrow I,

\gamma_{i}:J\rightarrow\mathbb{R},

If we denote the coordinate functions of

The tangent vector field of

Let us denote

Thus,

\left(\gamma_{0}^{\prime}\right)^{2}+f^{2}\left(\gamma_{0}\right)g\left(W,W\right)=1.

Also notice that
\gamma_{0}^{\prime}=\eta\left(T\right),

is horizontal and
\partial_{x_{i}}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T

\displaystyle-3k_{1}k_{1}^{\prime}T+\left(k_{1}^{\prime\prime}-k_{1}^{3}-k_{1}k_{2}^{2}\right)E_{2}

Proposition 1
.

If
\gamma:J\rightarrow\widetilde{M}^{n+1}

is a constant.

Proof.

If
\gamma

Remark 2

If
\gamma
is a geodesic, (i.e.

\tau_{2}\left(\gamma\right)
vanishes, so

Lemma 1
.

Let
\gamma:J\rightarrow\widetilde{M}^{n+1}
be a non-geodesic unit-speed

is a constant and

\left.\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}-\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{2}+\frac{c}{f^{2}}\right)\right|_{\gamma}\eta\left(T\right)\eta\left(E_{2}\right)T

Theorem 1

Let

The proof is straightforward using equation (

\gamma

Case I.

\left.\left[\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}-\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{2}+\frac{c}{f^{2}}\right]\right|_{\gamma}=0.

In this case, the equations of Theorem

Remark 3
.

The converse statement is not always valid because even though the ODEs
might not be globally satisfied, they might be satisfied along the curve
\gamma.
In fact, if
\gamma
is a Legendre curve, then
\gamma_{0}=constant

constant value, making it more likely to hold locally. Thus Case I i)
statement does not mean that there is no non-geodesic biharmonic curve in

or
\widetilde{M}=I\times_{f}\mathbb{H}^{n}\left(-1\right)

Case II.

\eta\left(E_{2}\right)=0.

Let us revisit the equation

T=\gamma_{0}^{\prime}\partial_{t}+W,

where we denote the vertical part with

From the Levi-Civita connection of the warped product manifold, we have

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}\partial_{t}=\left.\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right|_{\gamma}W.

We also have

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}\left(T,W\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}\left(T,T-\gamma_{0}^{\prime}\partial_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\left(\gamma_{0}^{\prime}\right)^{2}=1-\cos^{2}\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\theta.

Differentiating

\displaystyle-\theta^{\prime}\sin\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}\widetilde{g}\left(T,\partial_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T,\partial_{t}\right)+\widetilde{g}\left(T,\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}\partial_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{1}\eta\left(E_{2}\right)+\left.\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right|_{\gamma}\widetilde{g}\left(T,W\right)

\displaystyle k_{1}\eta\left(E_{2}\right)+\left.\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right|_{\gamma}\sin^{2}\theta.

.

For a non-Legendre unit-speed Frenet curve of osculating order

and

From the equation

If

Thus we have

More clearly, this equation can be written as

Now we can integrate with respect to

is a constant
along the curve.

\left.f\right|_{\gamma}=c_{1}\csc\theta
for some arbitrary constant
c_{1},
then
\gamma
is
biharmonic if and only if its curvatures satisfy

k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}=\left.\frac{c}{f^{2}}\right|_{\gamma}-\left(\left.\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right|_{\gamma}\right)^{2}-\left.\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}-\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{2}+\frac{c}{f^{2}}\right)\right|_{\gamma}\cos^{2}\theta.

(2.5)

Moreover, the following corollary holds for non-Legendre slant curves:

Corollary 3
.

Let
\gamma:J\rightarrow I\times_{f}M^{n}\left(c\right)
be a unit-speed
non-Legendre slant helix with curvatures
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
, or a
non-Legendre slant circle with curvature
k_{1}
. If
\left.f\right|_{\gamma}=c_{1}\csc\theta
=constant, then
\gamma
is biharmonic if and
only if
c>0
and

k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}=\frac{c}{c_{1}^{2}}\sin^{4}\theta.

Proof.

Using the fact that the structural angle is a non-zero constant (i.e.
\cos\theta\neq 0
) for a non-Legendre slant curve and equation (
2.1
),
after differentiating
\left.f\right|_{\gamma}=c_{1}\csc\theta
=constant, we find

\frac{d}{ds}\left(\left.f\right|_{\gamma}\right)=\left.\frac{df}{dt}\right|_{\gamma}.\gamma_{0}^{\prime}\left(s\right)=\left.f^{\prime}\right|_{\gamma}\cos\theta=0.

This results
\left.f^{\prime}\right|_{\gamma}=0
. In a similar way,
we obtain
\left.f^{\prime\prime}\right|_{\gamma}=0.
Thus, the
right-hand side of equation (
2.5
) becomes
\frac{c}{c_{1}^{2}}\sin^{4}\theta,
which completes the proof.
∎

Case III.

E_{2}
is parallel to
\partial_{t},
i.e.
\eta\left(E_{2}\right)=\pm 1.

In this case, we can write
E_{2}=\varepsilon\partial_{t}
where
\varepsilon
denotes
\pm 1.
This is the same as
\partial_{t}=\varepsilon E_{2}.
For this case, it is directly seen that

\eta\left(T\right)=\widetilde{g}\left(T,\partial_{t}\right)=\widetilde{g}\left(T,\varepsilon E_{2}\right)=0

(2.6)

and

\eta\left(E_{2}\right)=\widetilde{g}\left(E_{2},\partial_{t}\right)=\widetilde{g}\left(E_{2},\varepsilon E_{2}\right)=\varepsilon.

Equation (
2.6
) means that
\gamma
is a Legendre curve, that is
\cos\theta=0.
Considering
T
is vertical, we have

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}\partial_{t}=\left.\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right|_{\gamma}T.

So, if we differentiate
E_{2}=\varepsilon\partial_{t}
along
\gamma,

we get

-k_{1}T+k_{2}E_{3}=\varepsilon\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}\partial_{t}=\varepsilon\left.\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right|_{\gamma}T,

which results

k_{1}=-\varepsilon\left.\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right|_{\gamma},\text{
}k_{2}=0.

(2.7)

Now we can state our next corollary for Case III:

Corollary 4
.

Let
\gamma:J\rightarrow I\times_{f}M^{n}\left(c\right)
be
a unit-speed Legendre circle with
E_{2}\parallel\partial_{t}.
Then
\gamma
is biharmonic if and only if its curvature satisfies

k_{1}=\sqrt{\left.\frac{-f^{\prime\prime}}{f}\right|_{\gamma}}=-\varepsilon\left.\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right|_{\gamma}>0.

(2.8)

Proof.

Since
\eta\left(T\right)=\cos\theta=0,

\eta\left(E_{2}\right)=\varepsilon=\pm 1,
from the second equation of Theorem
1

ii), we can write

k_{1}^{2}=\left.\frac{c}{f^{2}}\right|_{\gamma}-\left(\left.\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right|_{\gamma}\right)^{2}-\left.\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}-\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{2}+\frac{c}{f^{2}}\right)\right|_{\gamma}=-\left.\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}\right|_{\gamma}.

(2.9)

Also,
\cos\theta=0
gives us
\gamma_{0}\left(s\right)=c_{0}=constant,

where
c_{0}\in I.
Then equations (
2.7
) and (
2.9
) complete the
proof.
∎

Remark 4
.

A natural equation to consider in this context is the ODE

-f\left(t\right)f^{\prime\prime}\left(t\right)=\left(f^{\prime}\left(t\right)\right)^{2},

whose solution is

f\left(t\right)=c_{2}\sqrt{2t+c_{1}},

(2.10)

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are arbitrary constants. Thus, if
f
is of the
form (
2.10
), it would satisfy

\sqrt{\left.\frac{-f^{\prime\prime}}{f}\right|_{\gamma}}=-\varepsilon\left.\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right|_{\gamma}>0,

(2.11)

for any
\gamma_{0}\left(s\right)=c_{0}.
But, since (
2.11
) is not
necessarily global, it suffices for any
f
(even not in the form of (
2.10
)) to satisfy equation (
2.11
) for the first component of the
curve, i.e.
\gamma_{0}\left(s\right)=c_{0}\in I,
rather than
everywhere. Then a Legendre circle whose curvature
k_{1}
satisfies (
2.8
), becomes biharmonic in this warped product manifold.

Case IV.

\left.\left[\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}-\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{2}+\frac{c}{f^{2}}\right]\right|_{\gamma}\neq 0
and
\eta\left(E_{2}\right)\neq 0,\pm 1.

In this case, from Theorem
1
, we have
\partial_{t}\in sp\left\{T,E_{2},E_{3},E_{4}\right\}.
So, we can write

\partial_{t}=\cos\theta T\pm\sin\theta\cos w_{1}\cos w_{2}E_{2}\pm\sin\theta\cos w_{1}\sin w_{2}E_{3}\pm\sin\theta\sin w_{1}E_{4}

(2.12)

for three angle functions
\theta,

w_{1}
and
w_{2}
along the curve.
Here
\theta
is the structural angle.
w_{1}
denotes the angle between
\left(\partial_{t}-\cos\theta T\right)
and its projection onto
sp\left\{E_{2},E_{3}\right\}.

w_{2}
denotes the angle between
E_{2}

and the projection of
\left(\partial_{t}-\cos\theta T\right)
onto
sp\left\{E_{2},E_{3}\right\}
. Then, we obtain

\eta\left(E_{2}\right)=\pm\sin\theta\cos w_{1}\cos w_{2},

\eta\left(E_{3}\right)=\pm\sin\theta\cos w_{1}\sin w_{2},

\eta\left(E_{4}\right)=\pm\sin\theta\sin w_{1}.

Thus, we can state our final corollary as follows:

Corollary 5
.

Let
\gamma:J\rightarrow\widetilde{M}^{n+1}
be a unit-speed Frenet curve
of osculating order
r\geq 4,

\eta\left(E_{2}\right)\neq 0,\pm 1
and
\left.\left[ff^{\prime\prime}-\left(f^{\prime}\right)^{2}+c\right]\right|_{\gamma}\neq 0.
Then
\gamma
is biharmonic if and only if
k_{1}
is a positive constant and

k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}=\left.\frac{c}{f^{2}}\right|_{\gamma}-\left(\left.\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right|_{\gamma}\right)^{2}-\left.\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}-\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{2}+\frac{c}{f^{2}}\right)\right|_{\gamma}\left[\cos^{2}\theta+\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}w_{1}\cos^{2}w_{2}\right],

k_{2}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\left.\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}-\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{2}+\frac{c}{f^{2}}\right)\right|_{\gamma}\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}w_{1}\sin 2w_{2},

k_{2}k_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\left.\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}-\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{2}+\frac{c}{f^{2}}\right)\right|_{\gamma}\sin^{2}\theta\sin 2w_{1}\cos w_{2},

where
\partial_{t}\in sp\left\{T,E_{2},E_{3},E_{4}\right\}
is given by
equation
(\ref{eq8})
.

Proof.

The proof is straightforward using Theorem
1
with equation (
2.12
).
∎

3.
Construction of Examples in
I\times_{f}S^{2}\left(1\right)

Let us consider
\widetilde{M}^{n+1}=I\times_{f}S^{2}\left(1\right),

where
f\left(t\right)=\sin\left(t\right)
and
S^{2}\left(1\right)\subset E^{3}
is the unit-sphere in Euclidean space. We will construct a
biharmonic curve in
\widetilde{M}^{n+1}
using Corollary
1
.
Firstly, notice that
f
is of the form

f(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}}}\sin\left(\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}}t+c_{0}\right),

for
c_{0}=0
and
k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}=1.
For simplicity, let us look for a
biharmonic circle, that is,
\kappa_{1}=1
and
\kappa_{2}=0.
We will use
spherical coordinate functions
\left\{x_{1},x_{2}\right\}
of
S^{2}\left(1\right).
Here
x_{1}\in\left(0,\pi\right)
and
x_{2}\in\left(0,2\pi\right).
Recall that the induced metric tensor field of
S^{2}\left(1\right)
can be written as

g=dx_{1}^{2}+\cos^{2}x_{1}dx_{2}^{2},

and the corresponding Levi-Civita Connection is

\nabla_{\partial_{x_{1}}}\partial_{x_{1}}=0,

\nabla_{\partial_{x_{1}}}\partial_{x_{2}}=\nabla_{\partial_{x_{2}}}\partial_{x_{1}}=-\tan x_{1}\partial_{x_{2}},

\nabla_{\partial_{x_{2}}}\partial_{x_{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\sin 2x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}.

As a result, we can write the metric tensor field of
I\times_{f}S^{2}\left(1\right)
as

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle dt^{2}+f^{2}g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle dt^{2}+\sin^{2}\left(t\right)\left(dx_{1}^{2}+\cos^{2}x_{1}dx_{2}^{2}\right).

So, the corresponding Levi-Civita Connection is calculated as

\widetilde{\nabla}_{\partial_{t}}\partial_{t}=0,

\widetilde{\nabla}_{\partial_{t}}\partial_{x_{1}}=\widetilde{\nabla}_{\partial_{x_{1}}}\partial_{t}=\cot\left(t\right)\partial_{x_{1}},

\widetilde{\nabla}_{\partial_{t}}\partial_{x_{2}}=\widetilde{\nabla}_{\partial_{x_{2}}}\partial_{t}=\cot\left(t\right)\partial_{x_{2}},

\widetilde{\nabla}_{\partial_{x_{1}}}\partial_{x_{1}}=-\frac{1}{2}\sin\left(2t\right)\partial_{t},

\widetilde{\nabla}_{\partial_{x_{1}}}\partial_{x_{2}}=\widetilde{\nabla}_{\partial_{x_{2}}}\partial_{x_{1}}=-\tan x_{1}\partial_{x_{2}},

\widetilde{\nabla}_{\partial_{x_{2}}}\partial_{x_{2}}=-\frac{1}{2}\sin\left(2t\right)\cos^{2}x_{1}\partial_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\sin\left(2x_{1}\right)\partial_{x_{1}}.

Now, let us define the curve
\gamma:J\rightarrow I\times_{f}S^{2}\left(1\right),

s\mapsto\gamma\left(s\right)=\left(\frac{\pi}{4},\sqrt{2}s,\pi\right).

Its tangent vector field is

T=\gamma^{\prime}=\left(0,\sqrt{2},0\right)=\sqrt{2}\partial_{x_{1}}.

Notice that

\widetilde{g}\left(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\right)=\widetilde{g}\left(\sqrt{2}\partial_{x_{1}},\sqrt{2}\partial_{x_{1}}\right)=2\left.\sin^{2}\left(t\right)\right|_{\gamma}=2\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right)=1,

namely,
\gamma
is unit-speed and
s
is the arc-length parameter. Then we
have

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T=\widetilde{\nabla}_{\sqrt{2}\partial_{x_{1}}}\sqrt{2}\partial_{x_{1}}=2\widetilde{\nabla}_{\partial_{x_{1}}}\partial_{x_{1}}=-\left.\sin\left(2t\right)\right|_{\gamma}\partial_{t}=-\partial_{t}.

As a result, we find

\kappa_{1}=\sqrt{\widetilde{g}\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T,\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}T\right)}=1

and

E_{2}=-\partial_{t}.

Thus, it is easy to see that

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}E_{2}=\widetilde{\nabla}_{\sqrt{2}\partial_{x_{1}}}\left(-\partial_{t}\right)=-\sqrt{2}\widetilde{\nabla}_{\partial_{x_{1}}}\partial_{t}=-\sqrt{2}\left.\cot\left(t\right)\right|_{\gamma}\partial_{x_{1}}=-\sqrt{2}\partial_{x_{1}}.

(3.1)

From the Frenet formulas, we know that

\widetilde{\nabla}_{T}E_{2}=-\kappa_{1}T+\kappa_{2}E_{3}=-\sqrt{2}\partial_{x_{1}}+\kappa_{2}E_{3}.

(3.2)

Finally, equations (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) give us
\kappa_{2}=0
. We have
shown that
\gamma
is a circle in
I\times_{f}S^{2}\left(1\right)

satisfying Corollary
1
. So, it is biharmonic. Moreover, notice that

E_{2}\parallel\partial_{t}
. So, we obtained an example for Case III,
too. Let us show that Corollary
4
is verified. Since
f\left(t\right)=\sin t,
we can write

\left.\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}\right|_{\gamma}=\left.\left(\frac{-\sin t}{\sin t}\right)\right|_{\gamma}=-1

and

\left.\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right|_{\gamma}=\left.\left(\frac{\cos t}{\sin t}\right)\right|_{\gamma}=\cot\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right)=1.

As a result, we obtain

k_{1}=\sqrt{\left.\frac{-f^{\prime\prime}}{f}\right|_{\gamma}}=-\varepsilon\left.\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right|_{\gamma}=1,

which is true for
\gamma,
a circle with
E_{2}\parallel\partial_{t}
.
Thus, Corollary
4
is also verified and
\gamma
is biharmonic. To
summarize, we write:

Example 1
.

\gamma:J\rightarrow I\times_{f}S^{2}\left(1\right),

s\mapsto\gamma\left(s\right)=\left(\frac{\pi}{4},\sqrt{2}s,\pi\right)

is a biharmonic cirle in the warped product manifold
I\times_{f}S^{2}\left(1\right),
where
f(t)=\sin t
is the warping function.

Finally, we will give another non-trivial example in
I\times_{f}S^{2}\left(1\right)
with
f(t)=\sin t
.

Example 2
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
a^{2}+b^{2}=1
. Then
\gamma:J\rightarrow I\times_{f}S^{2}\left(1\right),

s\mapsto\gamma\left(s\right)=\left(\arccos\left(\cos\left(as\right)\cos\left(bs\right)\right),\arcsin\left(\frac{\cos\left(as\right)\sin\left(bs\right)}{\sqrt{1-\cos^{2}\left(as\right)\cos^{2}\left(bs\right)}}\right),bs\right)

becomes a unit-speed helix of order
3

\left(k_{3}=0\right)
with
constant curvatures
k_{1}=\left|\sin\left(2u\right)\right|

and
k_{2}=\left|\cos\left(2u\right)\right|
, where
u\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant such that
a=\cos u
and
b=\sin u
. In this case, we have
k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}=1
. In view of Corollary
1
, the equation

f(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}}}\sin\left(\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}}t+c_{0}\right)

is satisfied along the curve
\gamma
with
c_{0}=0.
So,
\gamma
is
biharmonic.

4.
Conclusion and Discussion

In this paper, we study biharmonic curves in warped product manifolds of the
form
I\times_{f}M^{n}(c)
. By deriving the biharmonic equation and
analyzing the solutions, we identified key geometric properties such as
slant characteristics.
This study suggests potential directions for future research, inspired by
previous works, particularly in extending the results to more general warped
product manifolds or higher-dimensional spaces.

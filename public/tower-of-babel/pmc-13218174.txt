Nationwide analysis of real-world access to and use of device-aided therapies in Parkinson’s disease: insights from the French National Healthcare Data System (2015–2021)
Authors: Manon Auffret, Emmanuel Nowak, Marc Vérin, Sandrine Kerbrat
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and objectivesDevice-aided therapies (DAT) have significantly improved the management of Parkinson’s disease (PD). They now include deep brain stimulation (DBS), levodopa–carbidopa intestinal gel (LCIG) infusion, continuous subcutaneous apomorphine infusion (CSAI), subcutaneous foslevodopa/foscarbidopa and levodopa–entacapone–carbidopa intestinal gel (LECIG) infusions. Despite established clinical benefits, disparities in DAT access and use have been reported across Europe and the USA. The PARKinson's Device-Aided-Therapies (PARK-DAT) study aimed to evaluate access to and use of DAT (DBS, LCIG and CSAI) for PD in France between 2015 and 2021 using data from the French national administrative healthcare database (Système National des Données de Santé, SNDS).MethodsPARK-DAT is a nationwide, retrospective observational study. Patients with PD who initiated any DAT for the first time between 2015 and 2021 were identified using an established algorithm and codes within the SNDS. Standardised incidences of DAT initiation were calculated by study period and geographical region.ResultsBetween 2015 and 2021 8829 patients with PD (mean age 68.7±10.3 years; 43.4% women) initiated DAT representing a 41.8% overall increase between 2015 and 2021. CSAI was the most frequently initiated therapy (6873 patients; +58.9%), followed by DBS (1592 patients; −20.7%) and LCIG (364 patients; +152.9%). Most DAT prescriptions originated from hospital centres (74.6%). Few therapy switches or combinations were observed, mainly involving CSAI. Regional analyses suggest incidence disparities in DAT access.DiscussionUnlike patterns observed in other countries, CSAI was the predominant DAT in France, while DBS use declined. Features of the French healthcare system, including favourable reimbursement and broad access to home nursing services, may account for this distinctive distribution.

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN ON THIS TOPIC

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

France exhibits a distinctive national pattern in which continuous subcutaneous apomorphine infusion (CSAI) has become the predominant first-line DAT while deep brain stimulation use has declined. Despite a comparatively lower overall incidence, the absolute number of DAT initiations in France is high and has increased steadily between 2015 and 2021 (with the notable exception of the COVID-19 period).

HOW THIS STUDY MIGHT AFFECT RESEARCH, PRACTICE OR POLICY

These findings suggest that health-system organisation (ie, reimbursement policies, cost structures and availability of home-based care services) influence DAT choice—particularly favouring CSAI in France. Further research is needed to inform clinical practice and health policy, with the goal of promoting more equitable access to DAT and harmonising care pathways across regions.

Introduction

Device-aided therapies (DATs) represent significant advances in the management of Parkinson’s disease (PD), providing effective options for individuals with advanced disease1 and contributing to clinically meaningful improvements in quality of life.2 They comprise surgical options, with deep brain stimulation (DBS) targeting various regions and continuous infusion treatments such as subcutaneous apomorphine (CSAI), subcutaneous foslevodopa/foscarbidopa (fLD/fCD) and intestinal gels—levodopa–carbidopa (LCIG, also known as carbidopa–levodopa enteral suspension, CLES) and levodopa–carbidopa–entacapone (LECIG). The latest developments in DAT, and particularly the Food and Drug Administration approval of both fLD/fCD (2024) and CSAI (2025), are expected to profoundly modify the management of advanced PD worldwide.3 While movement disorder specialists (MDSs) anticipate growing demand for DATs in the future,4 studies from the USA, Israel and Europe (Denmark, Germany, Sweden, Poland, Norway) reveal persistent and concerning disparities in DAT access related to race, sex and/or socio-economic status, as well as substantial international variation in prevalence and regulation practices.1511 11 12 Real-world nationwide data on DAT utilisation in PD, however, remain limited.7 Notably, no comprehensive investigation had been conducted to date in France, despite the nationwide availability of most approved DAT: DBS was introduced in France in the 1990s, followed by CSAI in 2000 (the apomorphine pen being available since 1996)1 and LCIG in 2006, while the most recent additions (fLD/fCD and LECIG) entered the French market at the end of 2024.

In France, the management of PD typically involves collaboration between general practitioners (GPs) and neurologists, with MDSs engaged when available, across both outpatient and hospital settings (guidelines published by the National Authority for Health—Haute Autorité de Santé (HAS)). According to the most recent data available from the French national health insurance system (Caisse Nationale d’Assurance Maladie, CNAM), approximately 277 800 individuals were receiving care for PD in 2023 (median age: 78 years), corresponding to a standardised prevalence of 3.98 per 1000 population, with notable regional variations. Between 2015 and 2023, the average annual increase in the number of patients with PD was 0.89%, reflecting a modest but consistent rise in disease burden over time, in line with international findings.13 63% of PD cases occurred in individuals aged over 75, of whom 50% were female. Based on the OBSERVE-PD study, where 51% of patients with PD were considered to have advanced PD according to physician’s judgement,14 up to 141 678 people in France may have advanced PD and could therefore be hypothetically eligible for a DAT.1 DAT have historically been initiated solely by hospital-based neurologists and MDS.15 While DBS remains restricted to designated PD expert centres (see online supplemental materials), recent developments have facilitated broader access to CSAI, including initiation in day hospital settings or directly at home.15 By contrast, and contrary to the USA,16 LCIG initiation in France remains an inpatient procedure, and levodopa–carbidopa gel cassettes are available only as outpatient hospital medication, requiring patients to regularly visit the hospital pharmacy to obtain their treatment. Data are still lacking regarding the French fLD/fCD and LECIG initiation practices, owing to their novelty. Nonetheless, much of the existing PD literature in France derives from specialised centres grouped within the NS-PARK network (National Clinical Research Network on PD), limiting the generalisability of findings to the broader PD population, as underscored by emerging evidence of systematic differences between research cohorts and real-world patient populations.12 17 The present study therefore aimed to examine real-world, nationwide patterns of DAT utilisation in France between 2015 and 2021, through a retrospective analysis of the French National Healthcare Data System (Système National des Données de Santé, SNDS). Given the timeframe, the analysis focused on the three therapies available during that period: DBS, CSAI and LCIG/CLES. Contrary to previous studies,6 the apomorphine pen was excluded from our DAT analysis, since pen users may introduce heterogeneity in both disease severity (earlier stages of motor fluctuations) and treatment goals (rescue therapy),3 hence diluting the study scope. PARK-DAT is therefore the first study to investigate DAT initiation, treatment sequencing, distribution dynamics and access disparities (including regional variations) for PD in France, while integrating the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic, during which successive lockdowns made in-hospital DAT initiations challenging worldwide.18 19

Methods

The PARK-DAT study is an observational, retrospective, nationwide study based on data extracted from the SNDS.

Data source

Launched in 2017, the SNDS is a unique patient-level database established by the French healthcare system modernisation law (Legifrance, law No. 2016–41 of 26 January 2016).20 Encompassing the main existing French public health databases (eg, hospital and health insurance databases, administrative death registration), it offers a comprehensive overview of the healthcare pathways of 99% of the French population (representing more than 68 million people in 2025, according to the National Institute of Statistics and Economic Studies (INSEE)), with more than 10 years of historical data (ie, diagnoses, prescriptions reimbursed, acts and procedures performed) in both inpatient and outpatient settings.20 21 Representing a significant advancement for conducting research in the healthcare sector and being the largest healthcare database in Europe, the SNDS provides a robust framework for conducting real-world investigations, including drug utilisation and pharmacoepidemiological studies.2123 Diagnosis is coded using the ICD-10 (International Classification of Diseases, 10th Edition), and patients are pseudoanonymised.

Cohort/participants

The study population consisted of patients with PD who received one or more DAT (DBS, CSAI and/or LCIG) for the first time between 2015 and 2021. Patients with PD were identified using the CNAM algorithm (V.G12), which combines data from long-term disease registrations, hospital discharge diagnoses and specific drug dispensations.24 DAT were identified using (1) the French pharmaceutical identification code CIP25 (for ‘code identifiant de présentation’, which uniquely identifies each marketed drug presentation) for both CSAI and LCIG, and (2) the French Common Classification of Medical Procedures (CCAM) codes for DBS.

Statistical analysis

Patients’ characteristics were described at cohort entry, defined as the date of the first DAT initiation from 2015 onwards. DAT initiation was defined as the date of the first treatment episode (either the first dispensation of CSAI or LCIG, or the implantation of DBS), with no record of a corresponding dispensation or implantation during the preceding 5 years. Of note, for any category in which the number of individuals is fewer than 11, the exact figure cannot be disclosed to preserve anonymity and ensure the confidentiality of health data, in compliance with French regulatory constraints.

Comorbidities present during the 12 months prior to inclusion were identified to compute the Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI)—a measure of 1-year mortality risk.26 27 Concomitant drug treatments and medical procedures were determined based on the presence of relevant diagnostic or procedural codes within the same 12-month preinclusion period.

The annual incidence of DAT initiation was estimated for each calendar year of the study period (2015–2021), as well as stratified by region. Incidence rates were standardised by age group (in 10-year intervals) and sex, using individuals with PD identified in 2015 according to the CNAM cartography as the reference population.

Given that CSAI emerged as the predominant DAT, an in-depth secondary analysis was performed for this therapy, with age-standardised and sex-standardised incidence and prevalence calculated annually. Prevalence was defined by the presence of at least one dispensation of the drug during the corresponding year. Sequences of alternative DAT following initial CSAI treatment (switch or combination) were illustrated using a Sankey diagram.

Results

Patient demographics

Between 2015 and 2021, a total of 8829 patients were initiated on a DAT in France (table 1). If most of the patient were male (56.6%), the male-to-female distribution was not balanced across DAT, with a clear male predominance observed for DBS (68.2%). The mean age of the overall cohort was 68.7±10.3 years. Patients receiving DBS were younger (61.2±8.3 years), while those receiving pump therapies were older (70.4±9.9 years for CSAI and 69.0±8.6 years for LCIG). A different distribution of DATs was observed according to the CCI with lower scores (and therefore better prognosis and lower mortality risk) favouring DBS initiation, followed by CSAI. Patients with CCI scores ≥2 were more likely to receive LCIG. Regarding potential social disparities, being in Q1 of the social disadvantage index (meaning being least disadvantaged) was in favour of DBS initiation (table 1).

Treatment patterns

Of the 8829 patients, 6873 initiated CSAI (77.9%), 1592 DBS (18%) and 364 LCIG (4.1%) (figure 1).

The PARK-DAT study: flowchart representing study population and treatment patterns.

Standardised incidence of DAT initiation showed significant disparities. Over the study period, the annual incidence of DAT initiation showed a consistent upward trend, except amid the COVID-19 pandemic (figure 2A). More specifically, the number of patients initiating pump therapies (+41.8% increase between 2015 and 2021 for CSAI and +152.9% for LCIG) as a first DAT increased (except in 2020), while the number of patients initiating DBS steadily decreased (−20.7%) (figure 2B). In 2021, 4235 patients with PD received CSAI, including 1192 PD initiations (online supplemental table 1).

Across the study period, more patients started CSAI (78%) as a first DAT than DBS or LCIG. Since CSAI was the most frequently initiated DAT in France during our study period (roughly 1000 initiations/year), more detailed analyses were conducted for this therapy, notably regarding regional distribution and sequencing/combination treatments. A small proportion of sequencing treatments were observed following CSAI initiation (figure 3), with 5.1% switch to DBS and 3.4% to LCIG, while combinations were even rarer, accounting for less than 0.1% of CSAI initiated as first DAT.

Initial prescribers were predominantly hospital centres (74.6%), but these were not restricted to the 27 PD expert centres, which accounted for only about a third of all DAT initiations, mainly driven by DBS procedures (table 1). Interestingly, outpatient neurologists accounted for 17.8% of first DAT prescriptions, mainly of CSAI (table 1). Prescription renewals were more evenly distributed between outpatient (48.1%) and inpatient practitioners (51.9%) for CSAI, whereas LCIG renewals were mostly carried out by inpatient prescribers from hospitals (60%) and particularly general hospitals (table 2). Of note, GPs were frequently involved in the follow-up of CSAI patients, as they signed 21.7% of the apomorphine renewals (table 2). Throughout the study period, median duration of treatment (for the first DAT initiated) was 400 days for CSAI, 503 for LCIG and 1213 for DBS. The fact that the treatment duration with CSAI is shorter than that of DBS or LCIG is in line with the literature, which highlights its low invasiveness (and therefore easy withdrawal), the occurrence of adverse effects (somnolence, cutaneous side effects) and treatment paradigms in which apomorphine serves as a temporary solution.3 28 29 A total of 1806 patients died during the study period: most of them were treated with CSAI (table 2). In this subgroup of deceased patients, mean DAT duration was 274 days for CSAI, 367 days for LCIG and 985.5 days for DBS, which seems to be in line with patients’ profile and the fact that CSAI may be used in terminal care when patients no longer have access to the oral route; including in a home-based setting.3 30

Despite high absolute numbers of DAT initiation, the overall standardised incidence remained low, with 49.5 for 10 000 patients (table 3), which is significantly lower than in other countries.6 Interestingly, neurologist density (as calculated by the Directorate for Research, Studies, Evaluation and Statistics, Ministry of Health) appeared unrelated to standardised incidence of DATs initiation, including for CSAI (figure 4, table 3, online supplemental table 2).

Use of other therapies (before and after DAT initiation)

Polymedication was common in our population. Treatment patterns (both in the year preceding and the months following DAT initiation) differed between the DAT cohorts (tables 1 and 2), especially between infusion pumps and DBS, undermining differences in profiles (age, comorbidities, PD subtypes).2 31

Before initiating (any) DAT, dopaminergic agents were the most frequently used drugs (96.7%). As previously highlighted in a National Clinical Research Network on Parkinson's Disease (NS-PARK) survey,32 the apomorphine pen appeared to be seldom used in France—including before CSAI. Commonly used drugs in the year preceding any DAT initiation included non-opioid analgesics (59.6%), antidepressants (39.7%), anxiolytics (33.1%), antihypertensive medications (32.8%), other cardioprotective treatments (26.3%), opioids (17.2%) and domperidone (12.9%). Despite being globally underused in PD,33 clozapine remains central to the treatment of PD psychosis in France, prescribed exclusively by neurologists, geriatricians and psychiatrists.34 In our cohort, it was the most frequently used antipsychotic in all populations, before (4.2%) and after (13.2%) DAT initiation.

Following DAT initiation, similar patterns were found in terms of comedications, except for domperidone and CSAI, where a notable increase in domperidone use is found during the first 6 months compared with the year preceding CSAI initiation (14.1–63.8%), followed by a significant decline in the following 6–12 months (22.3%). This duration of domperidone use is longer than that recommended for CSAI initiation in the most recent European guidelines.35

Discussion

Limitations

In spite of being a large, rich, comprehensive and reliable real-world database,2123 the use of the SNDS in research contexts entails inherent limitations, the main one being the absence of clinical records (ie, neurological scales, neuroimaging) and biological results.20 Despite using the CNAM algorithm to identify patients with PD in the SNDS, we cannot rule out that some patients with atypical Parkinsonism who received a DAT36 37 during this time frame were included. This, however, is unlikely to affect the quality of the data, representing only a small number of cases. Similarly, we cannot exclude the possibility of false-negative (meaning PD cases misclassified as non-PD cases).38

DAT distribution

Compared with data available from international literature,1 the absolute number of DAT initiation is rather high in France, but the overall incidence is lower.6 As in other European countries,10 our findings indicate a growing use of DAT in France between 2015 and 2021 (except during the COVID-19 period). In contrast, CSAI emerged as the preferred first-line DAT, accompanied by a steady decline in DBS use—a trend that may seem unexpected, given that France is commonly regarded as the birthplace of the modern form of DBS for patients with movement disorders. As high-intensity focused ultrasound, an emerging alternative to DBS,39 was not available in France as a symptomatic PD treatment during our study period, other factors, not limited to the COVID-19 pandemic (which did cause disruptions and backlogs worldwide,18 40 including for DBS care),19 must be considered to explain this downward trend for DBS. They may include longer referral pathways and waiting time, shortage of movement disorders specialists41 and/or neurosurgeons, hospital capacity constraints and treatment paradigm shifts favouring pump therapies (greater experience with CSAI, patient preference, risks and/or side effects associated with DBS).1 The initiation of LCIG or DBS requires (1) coordination between neurology teams and other specialties (namely gastroenterology and neurosurgery), and (2) access to an operating room. Conversely, the low invasiveness (compared with both DBS and CSAI), as well as the variety and flexibility of initiation approaches42 (from inpatient to home-care settings) and progressively diversifying indications of CSAI (ie, terminal care, sleep disturbances)3 are likely to exert a positive influence on its initiation dynamics and favour its acceptance by both patients and clinicians. Moreover, the French healthcare system offers favourable reimbursement policies and funding models for CSAI, as it is entirely reimbursed (medication, device and nurse care), contrary to other countries.10 Such system therefore supports the availability of home-based nursing care, which facilitates both the initiation (including directly at home15 and ongoing management of CSAI (the support of PD nurses having been evaluated as critical to ensure the success of this DAT),1 3 43 regardless of the patient’s location.

Sequential use and/or combination of DAT

In line with the international literature,1 8 44 a small proportion of DAT switches (sequencing treatments) and combinations were observed, notably for CSAI. If sequential use and/or combination of DAT has been shown to be beneficial, especially when the first is associated with insufficient efficacy or side effects,1 8 44 it is not uncommon in France for patients awaiting DBS to be started on CSAI,28 29 which is then discontinued once the electrodes and stimulator have been implanted. This procedure may also contribute to the relatively shorter median duration of treatment for CSAI.

Disparities in DAT access

Despite the availability of a favourable social insurance system, social and geographical health inequities45 as well as discrimination in healthcare provision46 remain a reality in France. Notably, our study shows that female gender and low socio-economic status are significantly associated with less DBS initiations, in line with the international literature.11 With one of the lowest neurology workforces in Europe (4.45 neurologists per 100 000 population)47 and emerging concerns about training in movement disorders during neurology residency,48 inequality in the distribution of PD specialised healthcare professionals would theoretically constitute a considerable barrier to equity.45 However, we found that neurologist density does not appear to be related to standardised incidence of DAT initiation (ie, more neurologists in Ile-de-France, but lower CSAI incidence, table 3). Additional factors may therefore play a significant role in how and why DATs are initiated.1 Further studies are therefore needed to better understand how these factors influence access to and use of DAT in the specific context of French healthcare.

Conclusion

Between 2015 and 2021, France exhibited unusually high use of CSAI as the dominant DAT, accompanied by declining DBS use—a distinct contrast to other nations within the same time frame. These differences may be shaped by health-system policies, cost/reimbursement models and service delivery infrastructure (especially home care support) favouring CSAI. Further studies are however needed to better understand centre-specific practices (which are known to affect PD treatment patterns49 and geographical disparities). While the COVID-19 pandemic undeniably caused a marked decline in DAT initiation, the overall dynamic appears to have resumed to prelockdown levels (contrary to what was observed in Germany18), showing a recuperative effect. This promising trend will be further evaluated in a follow-up study (PARK-DAT2) covering the years 2021–2025 (once these data will be made available), during which the newly approved DAT (and particularly fLD/fCD) are expected to profoundly modify the dynamics of DAT initiation and switches, similarly to what has already been observed in Spain.31

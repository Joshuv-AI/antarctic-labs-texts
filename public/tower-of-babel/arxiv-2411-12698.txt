Energy balance for 6D kinetic ions with adiabatic electrons Preprint: AIP/123-QED
M. Raeth, K. Hallatschek

Abstract
This paper investigates the energy fluxes for the 6D kinetic Vlasov system. We introduce a novel method for calculating particle and energy flows within this framework which allows for the determination of energy and particle fluxes, as well as the Poynting flux, directly from the system’s moments such as kinetic energy density, momentum transfer tensor. The fluxes computed using the new method exhibit fewer gyrooscillations. This approach also enables the identification of both the gyrokinetic 𝑬 × 𝑩 \bm{E}\times\bm{B} heat flux and additional non-gyrokinetic contributions, while simultaneously reducing inherent gyrooscillations in the energy and particle fluxes. Our semi-Lagrangian solver for the 6D kinetic Vlasov system, features a highly efficient scheme to address the 𝒗 × 𝑩 \bm{v}\times\bm{B} acceleration from the strong background magnetic field allows for the simulation of plasma waves and turbulence with frequencies extending beyond the cyclotron frequency, independent of gradient strength or fluctuation levels. The solver has been rigorously tested in the low-frequency regime for dispersion relations and energy fluxes in both linear and nonlinear scenarios.

Introduction

Kinetic models can describe phenomena within a tokamak plasma across different
scales however, until recently, we lacked the computational capacity for the
fully kinetic simulations. Gyrokinetic models used in codes like CGYRO

candy_high-accuracy_2016
, GENE
jenko_massively_2000
and ORB5

lanti_orb5_2020

face challenges in high-gradient regimes like the tokamak plasma edge. Most
models rely on a
\delta f
approximations and break down in these regions of
steep gradients and high fluctuation amplitudes. In recent years, there is a
growing interest in the instability of ion Bernstein waves (IBWs)

;
noreen_ion_2019
;
rath_beyond_2023

This work provides a comprehensive analysis of the kinetic and electric energy

With quasineutrality equation for adiabatic electrons

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{2}=-\nabla\cdot(\phi\bm{\Gamma}_{e})-\bm{E}\cdot\bm{\Gamma}_{e}

2
Poynting flux

In addition to the energy flux in the electrons, the Poynting flux
\bm{S}=\bm{E}\times\bm{B}/{\mu_{0}}
has to be considered. We rewrite the Poynting
flux using
\bm{B}=\hat{\bm{z}}+\delta B
and
\bm{E}=-\nabla\phi+\partial_{t}\bm{A}
, as

\displaystyle\bm{S}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu_{0}}\bm{E}\times\bm{B}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu_{0}}(-\nabla\phi\times\bm{B}+\partial_{t}{\bm{A}}\times\bm{B})

\displaystyle=\underbrace{-\frac{1}{\mu_{0}}\nabla\times(\phi\bm{B})}_{O(\frac{1}{\mu_{0}})}+\underbrace{\phi\frac{\nabla\times\bm{B}}{\mu_{0}}}_{O(1)}+\underbrace{\frac{\partial_{t}{\bm{A}}}{\mu_{0}}\times\hat{\bm{z}}}_{O(1)}+\underbrace{\frac{\partial_{t}{\bm{A}}\times\delta\bm{B}}{\mu_{0}}}_{O(\mu_{0})}.

(10)

Since
\Delta\bm{A}=\mu_{0}\bm{j}
and
\nabla\times\bm{B}=\mu_{0}\bm{j}
, we
know that
\delta\bm{B}\ =O(\mu_{0})
and
\bm{A}=O(\mu_{0})
and thus,

\partial_{t}{\bm{A}}\times\delta\bm{B}/{\mu_{0}}=O(\mu_{0})
. In the
electrostatic limit,
\mu_{0}\rightarrow 0
, most terms of (
10
)
vanish. However, the first term
\nabla\times(\phi\hat{\bm{z}})/{\mu_{0}}\rightarrow\infty
because the background magnetic field is externally imposed rather than
generated by plasma currents. Since the resulting term is
divergence free, it does not
contribute to the energy transport and can be ignored in the following
discussion of turbulent transport balance. Using Ampères law, where the displacement current vanishes because of the quasineutral limit, the Poynting flux becomes

\displaystyle\bm{S}=-\nabla\times\frac{\phi\bm{B}}{\mu_{0}}+\phi\bm{j}+\frac{\partial_{t}{\bm{A}}}{\mu_{0}}\times\hat{\bm{z}},

(11)

where
\bm{j}=\bm{\Gamma}-\bm{\Gamma}_{e}
. The last term

\partial_{t}{\bm{A}}\times\hat{\bm{z}}/\mu_{0}
can also be omitted from the turbulent
transport balance as we are interested in quasi steady state energy fluxes,
and the total time derivatives vanishes upon time averaging. The term

\phi\bm{j}
seems problematic, as adding a constant to the electrostatic
potential would change the Poynting flux. However, its divergence is invariant
when adding a constant
\chi
to
\phi
,

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{S}=\nabla\cdot(\phi+\chi)\bm{j}=\nabla\cdot(\phi\bm{j})+\chi\nabla\cdot\bm{j}=\nabla\cdot(\phi\bm{j}),

(12)

since quasineutrality implies
\nabla\cdot\bm{j}=0
.

To arrive at the various expressions for the fluxes, we compute the time

1
Particle density

The zeroth moment of the Vlasov equation yields the continuity equation (
5

\bm{\Gamma}=\int\bm{v}f\mathrm{d}^{3}v
, written in fluid quantities is
\bm{\Gamma}=n\bm{u}
(with the mean

\bm{u}

The first moments of the Vlasov equation can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bm{\Gamma}}

\displaystyle=\int\bm{v}\partial_{t}f\mathrm{d}^{3}v

\displaystyle=-\int\bm{v}\bm{v}\cdot\nabla f\mathrm{d}^{3}v-\int\bm{v}(-\nabla\phi+\bm{v}\times\hat{\bm{z}})\cdot\nabla_{v}f\mathrm{d}^{3}v.

(13)

The first integral results in the divergence of the momentum transfer tensor
\nabla\cdot{\bm{\Pi}}=\nabla\cdot\int\bm{v}\bm{v}f\mathrm{d}^{3}v
, which can be expressed
in terms of fluid quantities
{\bm{\Pi}}={\bm{\pi}}+\mathds{1}p+\bm{u}\bm{u}n
(with the stress tensor
{\bm{\pi}}
, pressure
p

\bm{u}

in

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bm{\Gamma}}

\displaystyle=-\nabla\cdot{\bm{\Pi}}-n\nabla\phi+\bm{\Gamma}\times\hat{\bm{z}}.

(14)

After computing the cross product with the magnetic field
\hat{\bm{z}}
, the Grassmann
identity can be applied to obtain an expression for the perpendicular particle
flux

\displaystyle\bm{\Gamma}_{\perp}=-\partial_{t}{\bm{\Gamma}}\times\hat{\bm{z}}-\nabla\cdot{\bm{\Pi}}\times\hat{\bm{z}}-(\nabla\phi\times\hat{\bm{z}})n.

(15)

The last term is the
\bm{E}\times\bm{B}
particle flux known from
gyrokinetics. Since we are interested in quasi steady-state particle fluxes, a

other terms vanish when a flux surface average, defined as
\langle\cdot\rangle=\int\cdot,\mathrm{d}y,\mathrm{d}z/({L_{y}L_{z}})
, is applied the
expression can be written as

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\cdot{\bm{\Pi}}\times\hat{\bm{z}}\rangle+\langle\nabla\phi\times\hat{\bm{z}}n\rangle=

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{x}}(\left\langle n\partial_{y}\phi\right\rangle+\partial_{x}\langle\Pi_{xy}\rangle)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{y}}(\left\langle n\partial_{x}\phi\right\rangle+\partial_{x}\langle\Pi_{xx}\rangle).

(16)

When the adiabatic assumption (
2

\displaystyle\langle\bm{\Gamma}_{\perp}\rangle=

\displaystyle-\hat{\bm{x}}\partial_{x}\langle\Pi_{xy}\rangle+\hat{\bm{y}}\partial_{x}\left(\left\langle\frac{n^{2}}{2}\right\rangle+\langle\Pi_{xx}\rangle\right).

(17)

In flux tube scenarios, both terms vanish, as they are total derivatives with
respect to
x
and typically globally averaged quantities are considered.

2
Energy density

The total energy density of the system is given by the sum of the kinetic energy
of ions
\epsilon=\int{v^{2}}f/2\mathrm{d}^{3}v
(expressed in thermodynamic quantities

\epsilon=(3/2)p+{u^{2}}n/2

\epsilon_{e}={(n-1)^{2}}/2

1

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\epsilon=

\displaystyle\int\frac{v^{2}}{2}\partial_{t}f\mathrm{d}^{3}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int\frac{v^{2}}{2}\bm{v}\cdot\nabla f\mathrm{d}^{3}v

\displaystyle-\int\frac{v^{2}}{2}(-\nabla\phi+\bm{v}\times\hat{\bm{z}})\cdot\nabla_{\bm{v}}f\mathrm{d}^{3}v.

(18)

Integrating the second integral by parts, yields

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\epsilon

\displaystyle=-\nabla\cdot\bm{Q}-\int\bm{v}\cdot(\nabla\phi)f\mathrm{d}^{3}v,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\epsilon

\displaystyle=-\nabla\cdot\bm{Q}-\nabla\cdot(\phi\bm{\Gamma})+\phi\nabla\cdot\bm{\Gamma},

(20)

and inserting (
5

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\epsilon+\phi\partial_{t}n)

\displaystyle=-\nabla\cdot\bm{Q}-\nabla\cdot(\phi\bm{\Gamma}).

(21)

Utilizing the field equation (
2

density can be inserted
\partial_{t}\epsilon_{e}=\partial_{t}(n-1)^{2}/2=\phi\partial_{t}n
. Apart from the local energy flux density
\bm{Q}

The energy flux in the gyrokinetic system is described by the

To establish the connection of the energy flux

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bm{Q}}

\displaystyle=\int\bm{v}\frac{v^{2}}{2}\partial_{t}f\mathrm{d}^{3}v

\displaystyle=-\int\frac{v^{2}}{2}\bm{v}\bm{v}\cdot\nabla f\mathrm{d}^{3}v-\int\frac{v^{2}}{2}\bm{v}(-\nabla\phi+\bm{v}\times\hat{\bm{z}})\cdot\nabla_{v}f\mathrm{d}^{3}v

\displaystyle=-\nabla\cdot{\bm{\Pi}}^{*}-\int\frac{v^{2}}{2}\bm{v}(-\nabla\phi+\bm{v}\times\hat{\bm{z}})\cdot\nabla_{v}f\mathrm{d}^{3}v,

(22)

with
{\bm{\Pi}}^{*}=\int{v^{2}}\bm{v}\bm{v}f/{2}\mathrm{d}^{3}v
. The Lorentz force term
is modified using integration by parts and inserting the expression for kinetic
energy density
\epsilon
, and the momentum transfer tensor
{\bm{\Pi}}

\displaystyle-\int

\displaystyle\frac{v^{2}}{2}\bm{v}\left[(-\nabla\phi+\bm{v}\times\hat{\bm{z}})\cdot\nabla_{\bm{v}}f\right]\mathrm{d}^{3}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int\left(\nabla\phi\cdot\bm{v}\right)\bm{v}f\mathrm{d}^{3}v-\int\frac{v^{2}}{2}\nabla\phi f\mathrm{d}^{3}v+\int(\bm{v}\times\hat{\bm{z}})\frac{v^{2}}{2}f\mathrm{d}^{3}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\bm{\Pi}}\cdot\nabla\phi-\epsilon\nabla\phi+\bm{Q}\times\hat{\bm{z}}.

(23)

After computing the cross product with the magnetic field
\partial_{t}\bm{Q}\times\hat{\bm{z}}

\displaystyle\bm{Q}_{\perp}=-

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bm{Q}}\times\hat{\bm{z}}-\nabla\cdot{\bm{\Pi}}^{*}\times\hat{\bm{z}}

\displaystyle-\nabla\phi\cdot{\bm{\Pi}}\times\hat{\bm{z}}-\nabla\phi\times\hat{\bm{z}}\epsilon.

Computing the time and flux surface average of this equation yields

\displaystyle\langle\bm{Q}_{\perp}\rangle=-\langle\nabla\phi\times\hat{\bm{z}}\epsilon\rangle-\langle\nabla\phi\cdot{\bm{\Pi}}\times\hat{\bm{z}}\rangle-\langle\nabla\cdot{\bm{\Pi}}^{*}\times\hat{\bm{z}}\rangle.

Next to the

21

\displaystyle\bm{S}_{\perp}=\phi\bm{\Gamma}_{\perp}=-\phi\partial_{t}{\bm{\Gamma}}\times\hat{\bm{z}}-\phi\nabla\cdot{\bm{\Pi}}\times\hat{\bm{z}}-\phi(\nabla\phi\times\hat{\bm{z}})n.

To study quasi steady-state energy flux, the quantities are time-averaged,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle\epsilon+\phi\partial_{t}n\rangle=

\displaystyle-\hat{\bm{x}}\partial_{x}\cdot\langle\bm{Q}+\phi\bm{\Gamma}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\hat{\bm{x}}\partial_{x}\cdot\langle

\displaystyle{-\nabla\phi\times\hat{\bm{z}}\epsilon}-\nabla\phi\cdot{\bm{\Pi}}\times\hat{\bm{z}}-\nabla\cdot{\bm{\Pi}}^{*}\times\hat{\bm{z}}

The two terms containing the momentum flux tensor
{\bm{\Pi}}
can be combined

\displaystyle\langle({\bm{\Pi}}\cdot\nabla\phi)\times\hat{\bm{z}}\rangle+\langle(\phi\nabla\cdot{\bm{\Pi}})\times\hat{\bm{z}}\rangle=\langle\nabla\cdot(\phi{\bm{\Pi}})\rangle\times\hat{\bm{z}}

(28)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}

\displaystyle\langle\epsilon+\phi\partial_{t}n\rangle

\displaystyle=-\hat{\bm{x}}\partial_{x}\cdot\left\langle{-\epsilon\nabla\phi}-\nabla\cdot(\phi{\bm{\Pi}}+{\bm{\Pi}}^{*})-\phi\partial_{t}{\bm{\Gamma}}-\phi n\nabla\phi\right\rangle\times\hat{\bm{z}}.

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\hat{\bm{x}}\cdot\langle\nabla\cdot(\phi{\bm{\Pi}}+{\bm{\Pi}}^{*})\rangle\times\hat{\bm{z}}=\partial_{x}^{2}\langle\phi\Pi_{xy}+\Pi_{xy}^{*}\rangle.

Inserting these two simplification into (

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left\langle\epsilon+\epsilon_{e}\right\rangle=-\partial_{x}\left\langle-\epsilon\partial_{y}\phi-\partial_{x}(\phi\Pi_{xy}+\Pi_{xy}^{*})-\phi\partial_{t}\Gamma_{y}\right\rangle.

In summary, the total energy flux in the fully kinetic systems contains two

Firstly, the additional energy flux induced by
{\bm{\Pi}}
and
{\bm{\Pi}}^{*}
. This

gyrokinetic simulations, a similar energy flux would occur, driven by the
\Pi

and

For

associated with the
\bm{E}\times\bm{B}
heat flux. The remaining difference

2
Nonlinear gradients

This section shifts away from the Boussinesq gradients described in the previous
section towards a nonlinear treatment of gradients in our simulation code. The
initial condition in the simulation is chosen such that the distribution
function contains density and temperature profiles as written in (
34
). The background profiles
n(\bm{R})
and
T(\bm{R})
are defined in gyrocenter coordinates
\bm{R}=\bm{r}-\bm{\rho}
to remove oscillations with the Larmor frequency. To simplify the
treatment of boundary conditions, the profiles are periodic using sine-profiles
in the
x
-direction

\displaystyle n(x,v_{y})=1+\kappa_{n}\sin\left(k_{0}\left(x-v_{y}\right)\right),

(36)

\displaystyle T(x,v_{y})=1+\kappa_{T}\sin\left(k_{0}\left(x-v_{y}\right)\right).

(37)

The initial background density gradient should not induce an electric field.
Therefore, modes with wave numbers parallel to the gradient are eliminated from
the electrostatic potential by subtracting the flux surface average

\displaystyle\phi=n-\langle n_{0}\rangle,

(38)

where
n_{0}
is the density perturbation at
t=0
. When selecting parameters for
the background profile, it is crucial to ensure that the wavelength of the
unstable mode,
\lambda_{\text{ITG}}={2\pi}/{k_{y}}
, is shorter than the
typical gradient length of the profile,
1/L_{T}\sim{\partial_{x}\ln T(x)}
.

For our initial simulation, we chose parameters:
\kappa_{n}=0
,
\kappa_{T}=0.5
, and
k_{0}=0.2
, resulting in a temperature gradient
\max_{x\in[0,L_{x}]}\partial_{x}\log T(x)=0.115
, where
L_{x}
denotes the box length
in the
x
-direction. The simulation has been conducted on a box with a
length of
L=10\pi\times 2.5\pi\times 240\pi
,

N=128\times 32\times 16\times 32\times 32\times 16
and
\delta t=0.02
.

VI
Summary

In this work, we have introduced a novel method for calculating energy flux in
the 6D kinetic description of ions in a plasma. The method rewrites the energy
flux (kinetic energy flux and Poynting flux) in terms of other moments of the
distribution function. Figure
3
shows that this
approach reduces oscillations in the diagnostic, leading to more reliable
results. Additionally, it enables us to identify various contributions to the
energy balance and attribute physical interpretations to individual components.
This has helped to recognize the connection to the
\bm{E}\times\bm{B}
heat
flux known from gyrokinetics and the contributions from the radial dependency of
background profiles. Moreover, it has enabled us to identify a potential
candidate for a difference in the energy flux for non-gyrokinetic high-frequency
modes. The effect of the
\phi\partial_{t}{\bm{\Gamma}}
contribution, which has
been neglected due to the low frequency of ITG modes, requires further
investigation for waves with
\omega\gtrsim 1
.

The proposed framework could be extended to rewrite higher moments of the
distribution function, such as the momentum transfer tensor
{\bm{\Pi}}
and the

{\bm{\Pi}}^{*}
tensor, using the same method. However, this would introduce a
dependency of the energy balance on higher-rank tensors, making the framework
less practical, and it would complicate the interpretation of the additional
terms. The conditions under which this continued expansion would converge remain
an open question.

Non-complex cobordisms between quasipositive knots
Maciej Borodzik, Paula Truöl

Abstract
. We show that for every genus g ≥ 0 g\geq 0 , there exist quasipositive knots K 0 g K_{0}^{g} and K 1 g K_{1}^{g} such that there is a cobordism of genus g = | g 4 ​ ( K 1 g ) − g 4 ​ ( K 0 g ) | g=|g_{4}(K_{1}^{g})-g_{4}(K_{0}^{g})| between K 0 g K_{0}^{g} and K 1 g K_{1}^{g} , but there is no ribbon cobordism of genus g g in either direction and thus no complex cobordism between these two knots. This gives a negative answer to a question posed by Feller in 2016.

1.
Introduction

Let
K_{0},K_{1}\subset S^{3}
be knots. A
cobordism
of genus
g
between
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
is an oriented, connected, compact, smoothly embedded surface
\Sigma\subset S^{3}\times[0,1]
with genus
g(\Sigma)=g
and boundary
\partial\Sigma=K_{1}\times\{1\}\sqcup-K_{0}\times\{0\}
. In this paper we work exclusively in the smooth category.
A cobordism is
ribbon
if
\Sigma
is embedded in such a way that the projection
S^{3}\times[0,1]\to[0,1]
restricted to
\Sigma
is a Morse function without local maxima. A cobordism is
optimal
if
g(\Sigma)=|g_{4}(K_{1})-g_{4}(K_{0})|
, where
g_{4}(K_{i})
denotes the
4
-genus
of
K_{i}
,
i\in\{0,1\}
, i. e. the minimal genus of a cobordism between
K_{i}
and the unknot. A
concordance
, that is a cobordism with
g(\Sigma)=0
, is optimal by definition.

A motivating example for the study of optimal ribbon cobordisms comes from the theory of complex plane curves. Suppose
C\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
is a non-singular complex curve (i. e. the zero set of a square-free polynomial in
\mathbb{C}[x,y]
) and
S_{0},S_{1}
are two
3
-spheres in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
with a common center, each intersecting
C
transversally. Set
K_{0}=C\cap S_{0}
and
K_{1}=C\cap S_{1}
, and let
\Sigma
be the part of
C
between
S_{0}
and
S_{1}
.
A classical argument using the maximum principle for the distance function restricted to
C
shows that
\Sigma
is a ribbon cobordism between
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
(see e. g.
[
6
, Lemma 2.6]
).
The resolution of the Thom conjecture by Kronheimer–Mrowka
[
15
,
27
]

implies that
g(\Sigma)=|g_{4}(K_{1})-g_{4}(K_{0})|
.
A cobordism
\Sigma
obtained as above from a non-singular complex curve is called
complex
.

Not every knot arises as a transverse intersection of a non-singular complex curve with a sphere in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Those that do are called
quasipositive
, see
[
23
,
4
]
. According to this work of Rudolph and Boileau–Orevkov, quasipositive knots can be characterized as the closures of quasipositive braids (see more below). It is interesting to compare optimal cobordisms between quasipositive knots and complex cobordisms. The following question was first asked by Feller.

Question 1.1

(
[
8
]
)
.

Are the two necessary conditions for the existence of a complex cobordism between two knots—the knots are quasipositive and there exists an optimal cobordism between them—sufficient?

Our main result answers this question negatively.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
g\geq 0
, there exist quasipositive knots
K_{0}^{g}
and
K_{1}^{g}
such that there is an optimal cobordism of genus
g
between
K_{0}^{g}
and
K_{1}^{g}
, but there is no complex cobordism between
K_{0}^{g}
and
K_{1}^{g}
.

The proof, given in Section
3
, shows that no optimal cobordism between
K_{0}^{g}
and
K_{1}^{g}
is ribbon. To prove this, we successfully exploit a recent obstruction to ribbon cobordisms by Livingston
[
20
]
, see Theorem
2.5
below.

A special class of quasipositive knots is given by
strongly quasipositive
knots, which are closures of strongly quasipositive braids
[
24
,
25
]
.
A braid on
n
strands is
quasipositive
if it is a finite product of conjugates of the positive
Artin generators
\sigma_{i}

[
1
]
, and
strongly quasipositive
if the product consists only of certain conjugates of the
\sigma_{i}
, namely

\displaystyle\sigma_{i,j}=\left(\sigma_{i}\cdots\sigma_{j-2}\right)\sigma_{j-1}\left(\sigma_{i}\cdots\sigma_{j-2}\right)^{-1}\qquad\text{for}\quad 1\leq i<j\leq n.

Within the class of quasipositive braids, strongly quasipositive braids are special because they come with an associated Seifert surface that realizes the minimal genus among Seifert surfaces for their closure
[
3
]
.
In the context of (smooth) concordances between knots, it follows from Rudolph’s slice–Bennequin inequality
[
27
]
that not every knot is concordant to a quasipositive knot, which is contrary to the behavior in the topological category
[
5
]
.

Building on
[
32
]
and using Heegaard Floer homology as an obstruction to ribbon concordances
[
34
]
, we are able to show the following strengthening of Theorem
1.2
in the case where
g=0
.

Proposition 1.3
.

There exist strongly quasipositive knots
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
such that there is a concordance between them, but no ribbon (and thus no complex) concordance in either direction.

We conclude the introduction with a few open questions. Firstly, our obstruction to complex cobordisms obstructs ribbon cobordisms.

Question 1.4
.

Suppose that there is an optimal ribbon cobordism between quasipositive knots
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
. Does it follow that there is a complex cobordism between
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
?

The next question arises from the observation that the examples we construct in the proof of Proposition
1.3
are not fibered.

Question 1.5
.

Suppose that there is an optimal cobordism between strongly quasipositive, fibered knots
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
. Is there a complex cobordism?

Conjecturally, concordant, strongly quasipositive, fibered knots are isotopic
[
2
]
.
A possible negative answer to Question
1.5
would be to find two concordant, strongly quasipositive, fibered knots with different Seifert forms. At present, any known construction of concordant strongly quasipositive knots, even if we drop the fiberedness condition, is between knots which share their Seifert form.

Question 1.6
.

Do there exist two strongly quasipositive knots that are concordant, but have distinct Seifert forms?

A positive answer to Question
1.6
could place us in a position of applying Livingston’s results to obstruct complex concordances between strongly quasipositive knots.

Remark 1.7
.

In this paper we use the terminology ‘complex cobordism’ instead of ‘algebraic cobordism’ of e.g.
[
8
]
to avoid a terminology clash. There is the notion of an algebraically slice knot, see e. g.
[
19
,
12
]
, which means that the Seifert form of the knot is metabolic. The term ‘algebraically cobordant’ is dangerously similar to ‘algebraically slice’.

Neither of the properties ‘optimal’ or ‘ribbon’ of a cobordism implies the other. Theorem
1.2
and Proposition
1.3
show that optimal cobordisms between (strongly) quasipositive knots are not necessarily ribbon.
If we drop the condition of quasipositivity, examples are easier to obtain and well-known:
Let
K
denote the trefoil and
J
the figure-eight knot. Then
K_{0}=K\#-K
and
K_{1}=J\#-J
are both slice, thus concordant.
On the other hand, one can consider the coprime Alexander polynomials of
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
to show that they are not ribbon concordant
[
11
,
9
]
.

To see that not every ribbon cobordism between strongly quasipositive knots is optimal, consider the following example. There is a ribbon cobordism
\Sigma
of genus one between the trefoil and the positive untwisted Whitehead double of the trefoil, which can be constructed by using two saddle moves; an intermediate stage of the cobordism is a positive Hopf link. Both knots are strongly quasipositive and have
4
-genus one
[
27
]
, so
\Sigma
is not optimal.

2.
Background on double branched covers of knots

2.1.
Classical results

We will use the following elementary fact about double branched covers, see e. g.
[
17
, Theorem 9.1 and Corollary 9.2]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Sigma_{2}(K)
denote the double branched cover of
S^{3}
branched along
K
. Its first homology group
H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(K);\mathbb{Z})
is an abelian group of finite order equal to the determinant
\det K
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
K_{1},\dots,K_{n}
are knots. Set
K=K_{1}\#\dots\#K_{n}
. Then

H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(K);\mathbb{Z})\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(K_{i});\mathbb{Z}).

Proof.

The double branched cover of a connected sum of knots is the connected sum of the double branched covers of the knots. The fundamental group of
\Sigma_{2}(K)
therefore is the free product of the fundamental groups of the double branched covers
\Sigma_{2}(K_{1}),\dots,\Sigma_{2}(K_{n})
. Since the first homology group with
\mathbb{Z}
coefficients is the abelianization of the fundamental group, the result follows.
∎

As a corollary, we can prove the following.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
p
be a prime. If
K
is a knot such that
p
divides
\det K
, then, as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space,

\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(nK);\mathbb{F}_{p})\geq n.

Proof.

Let
H=H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(K);\mathbb{Z})
.
If
p
divides
\det K
, then by Lemma
2.1
,
p
divides the order of
H
. By the classification of finite abelian groups,
H
can be written as
P\oplus Q
, where
P
is a cyclic
p
-group. By Lemma
2.2
, we obtain

H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(nK);\mathbb{Z})=nP\oplus nQ.

By the universal coefficient theorem
H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(nK);\mathbb{F}_{p})=H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(nK);\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F}_{p}
contains a subgroup

nP\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F}_{p}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\oplus n}
.
∎

The universal coefficient theorem argument used in the proof of Proposition
2.3
also implies the following well-known corollary.

Corollary 2.4
.

Suppose
K
is a knot.
If a prime
p
does not divide
\det K
, then
H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(K);\mathbb{F}_{p})=0
.

2.2.
Livingston’s obstruction

In
[
20
]
, Livingston studies for given knots
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
, the set of all four-tuples
(g,c_{0},c_{1},c_{2})
of non-negative integers for which there is a cobordism
\Sigma
between
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
of genus
g(\Sigma)=g
with
c_{i}(\Sigma)=c_{i}
critical points of each index
i\in\{0,1,2\}
. His obstruction to the existence of
\Sigma
is in terms of homology groups of cyclic branched covers of
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
.
We use the following version of his result, noting that
[
20
]
contains more general statements.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
20
, Corollary 5.4]
)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a cobordism between knots
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
.
Then for all odd primes
p
, we have

\displaystyle c_{0}(\Sigma)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\beta_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(K_{1}),\mathbb{F}_{p})-\beta_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(K_{0}),\mathbb{F}_{p})}{2}-g(\Sigma)\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle c_{2}(\Sigma)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\beta_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(K_{0}),\mathbb{F}_{p})-\beta_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(K_{1}),\mathbb{F}_{p})}{2}-g(\Sigma),

where for
i\in\{0,1\}
,
\beta_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(K_{i}),\mathbb{F}_{p})
is the dimension of
H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(K_{i});\mathbb{F}_{p})
as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Since complex cobordisms are necessarily ribbon (see the introduction), we will focus on the construction of non-ribbon cobordisms. Theorem
1.2
will be deduced from the following more general statement.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
K_{0},K_{1}
be two quasipositive knots that admit a cobordism of genus
g\geq 0
. Then there exist quasipositive knots
K_{0}^{\prime}
and
K_{1}^{\prime}
such that
K_{0}^{\prime}
is concordant to
K_{0}
,
K_{1}^{\prime}
is concordant to
K_{1}
, and there exists a cobordism of genus
g
between
K_{0}^{\prime}
and
K_{1}^{\prime}
. Moreover, no cobordism of genus
g
between
K_{0}^{\prime}
and
K_{1}^{\prime}
can be ribbon in any direction.

Proof.

Fix
g\geq 0
. Let
J_{0}
and
J_{1}
denote the
mirrors of the knots
8_{20}
and
11n_{50}
, respectively, from the Rolfsen and Hoste-Thistlethwaite knot table.
From Knotinfo
[
18
]
we read off the following properties of
J_{0}
and
J_{1}
:

(P-1)

\det J_{0}=9
,
\det J_{1}=25
;

(P-2)

J_{0}
and
J_{1}
are slice;

(P-3)

J_{0}
and
J_{1}
are quasipositive.

Choose an integer
m>0
, of which we will later make a sufficiently large choice.
Denote by
mJ_{0}
and
mJ_{1}
the knots obtained as connected sums of
m
copies of
J_{0}
and
J_{1}
, respectively, and define

\displaystyle K_{0}^{\prime}=K_{0}\#mJ_{0},\quad K_{1}^{\prime}=K_{1}\#mJ_{1}.

Two properties of
K_{0}^{\prime}
and
K_{1}^{\prime}
are immediate:

•

K_{0}^{\prime}
and
K_{1}^{\prime}
are quasipositive because
J_{0}
and
J_{1}
are by
(P-3)
, and a connected sum of quasipositive knots is quasipositive. In fact, a connected sum of knots is quasipositive if and only if both summands are quasipositive
[
22
]
. The direction we use is easy to see using the characterization of quasipositive knots as closures of quasipositive braids
[
23
,
4
]
.

•

K_{0}^{\prime}
is concordant to
K_{0}
, and
K_{1}^{\prime}
is concordant to
K_{1}
, because
J_{0}
and
J_{1}
are slice by
(P-2)
.

To prove that there is no ribbon cobordism of genus
g
between
K_{0}^{\prime}
and
K_{1}^{\prime}
in either direction, for
i\in\{0,1\}
and a prime
p
, denote by
d_{ip}
the dimension

d_{ip}=\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(K_{i});\mathbb{F}_{p}).

By Proposition
2.3
, Corollary
2.4
and
(P-1)
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(mJ_{0});\mathbb{F}_{3})

\displaystyle\geq m,\quad\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{5}}H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(mJ_{0});\mathbb{F}_{5})=0,

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(mJ_{1});\mathbb{F}_{3})

\displaystyle=0,\quad\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{5}}H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(mJ_{1});\mathbb{F}_{5})\geq m.

Hence by Lemma
2.2
we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(K_{0}^{\prime});\mathbb{F}_{3})-\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(K_{1}^{\prime});\mathbb{F}_{3})&\geq m+d_{03}-d_{13}\quad\text{and}\\
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{5}}H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(K_{1}^{\prime});\mathbb{F}_{5})-\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{5}}H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(K_{0}^{\prime});\mathbb{F}_{5})&\geq m+d_{15}-d_{05}.\end{split}

Now, if we chose
m
sufficiently large, say
m>2(g+d_{03}+d_{13}+d_{05}+d_{15})
, from Theorem
2.5
we obtain that a cobordism of genus
g
between
K_{0}^{\prime}
and
K_{1}^{\prime}
must necessarily fulfill
c_{0},c_{2}>0
. Therefore such a cobordism cannot be ribbon in either direction.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Suppose
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
are two quasipositive knots which admit an optimal cobordism of genus
g
. We can e. g. take the torus knots
K_{0}=T_{2,3}
and
K_{1}=T_{2,2g+3}
. The knots
K_{0}^{\prime}
and
K_{1}^{\prime}
from Proposition
3.1
then have genus
g_{4}(K_{0}^{\prime})=g_{4}(K_{0})=1
and
g_{4}(K_{1}^{\prime})=g_{4}(K_{1})=g+1

[
15
]
. In particular, a cobordism of genus
g
between
K_{0}^{\prime}
and
K_{1}^{\prime}
is optimal. By Proposition
3.1
, there is a cobordism of genus
g
between
K_{0}^{\prime}
and
K_{1}^{\prime}
. But such a cobordism has positive
c_{0}
and
c_{2}
, so
it cannot be ribbon in any direction.
∎

We conclude this section with a remark about the
g=0
case of Theorem
1.2
.
Although the knots
K_{0}^{\prime}
and
K_{1}^{\prime}
have
g_{4}=1
, they arise as non-trivial connected sums and in particular have a Seifert genus strictly larger than
1
.

However, we note that for this
g=0
case of Theorem
1.2
we could have used the prime knots
J_{0}
and
J_{1}
from the proof of Proposition
3.1
directly. Indeed, by
(P-2)
and
(P-3)
,
J_{0}
and
J_{1}
are quasipositive and slice, so there is a cobordism of genus
0
between them.
(P-1)
and Theorem
2.5
can be used again to show that there is no ribbon, and thus no complex cobordism, between
J_{0}
and
J_{1}
. Alternatively, one could use
(P-1)
and a result of Gilmer
[
10
]
, which implies that the determinants of
J_{0}
and
J_{1}
would need to be divisible by each other if there were ribbon concordances between them.

one can compute
\operatorname{Kh}^{i,j}(K_{0})
and
\operatorname{Kh}^{i,j}(K_{1})
with
\mathbb{F}_{2}
coefficients and show that there is no injective map between these spaces in either direction. It is worth mentioning that both
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
have non-trivial Khovanov homology in negative homological degrees:
\operatorname{Kh}^{-2,-1}(K_{0})\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}
, and
\operatorname{Kh}^{-1,0}(K_{1})\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}
.
To the best of our knowledge, these could be the first examples of strongly quasipositive knots with nontrivial Khovanov homology in negative homological gradings. In contrast, positive links, being a narrower class of links (see
[
28
,
21
]
), have non-vanishing
\operatorname{Kh}^{i,j}
only for
i\geq 0
, see
[
14
,

]
.

4.2.
Further properties of
K_{0}
and
K_{1}

We conclude with additional comments on our construction.

From Table
1
we see that the graded Euler characteristic of
\widehat{\operatorname{HFK}}
and thus the Alexander polynomial of both knots
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
constructed in Section
4.1
is
t-1+t^{-1}
. This is consistent with the fact that
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
are satellite knots with pattern the trefoil
J
with algebraic winding number
0
(see also Figure
2
). Indeed, the well-known satellite formula for the Alexander polynomial predicts that
\Delta_{K_{0}}(t)=\Delta_{K_{1}}(t)=\Delta_{J}(t)=t-1+t^{-1}
(see e. g.
[
17
, Theorem 6.15]
).

The same argument as in
[
26
]
implies that the Seifert form of the two knots is equal to the Seifert form of the pattern,
that is of the trefoil. Note that SnapPy
[
7
]
computes the Seifert form correctly, providing another sanity check for our calculations.

The knots
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
have Seifert genus
1
, as follows from a standard argument on the behaviour of the Seifert genus under satellite operations
[
29
]
.
Since
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
are both concordant to
J=T_{2,3}
, we also have
g_{4}(K_{0})=g_{4}(K_{1})=g_{4}(J)=1
. Alternatively, we could conclude this from the equality of the Seifert genus and
4
-genus for strongly quasipositive knots
[
3
,
27
]
. Thus, the examples we construct in Section
4.1
have the smallest possible Seifert genus.

Regarding Question
1.5
, note that the knots
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
are not fibered by construction. This remark was already made in
[
33
, Remark 2.5]
, based on
[
32
]
. One of the simplest arguments is that the algebraic winding number of the pattern is
0
, so the satellite knot is not fibered.

Finiteness of pointed families of symplectic varieties: a geometric Shafarevich conjecture
Lie Fu, Zhiyuan Li, Teppei Takamatsu, Haitao Zou

Abstract
. We investigate in this paper the so-called pointed Shafarevich problem for families of primitive symplectic varieties. More precisely, for any fixed pointed curve ( B , 0 ) (B,0) and any fixed primitive symplectic variety X X , among all locally trivial families of ℚ \mathbb{Q} -factorial and terminal primitive symplectic varieties over B B whose fiber over 0 0 is isomorphic to X X , we show that there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of generic fibers. Moreover, assuming semi-ampleness of isotropic nef divisors, which holds true for all hyper-Kähler manifolds of known deformation types, we show that there are only finitely many such projective families up to isomorphism. These results are optimal since we can construct infinitely many pairwise non-isomorphic (not necessarily projective) families of smooth hyper-Kähler varieties over some pointed curve ( B , 0 ) (B,0) such that they are all isomorphic over the punctured curve B \ { 0 } B\backslash\{0\} and have isomorphic fibers over the base point 0 0 .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Hyperbolicity of moduli spaces and polarized Shafarevich conjecture

Let
K
be a number field and
g\geq 2
an integer. In his ICM address, Shafarevich
[
Shafarevich-ICM
]
conjectured that, up to isomorphism, there are only finitely many smooth projective curves of genus
g
over
K
with good reduction outside a fixed finite set of finite places of
K
. This was proven by Faltings in
[
Faltings83
]
.
A geometric analogue where
K
is replaced by
k(B)
the function field of a smooth curve
B
over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero, was established earlier by Arakelov
[
Arakelov71
]
and Paršin
[
Parsin68
]
.

Faltings’ result can be reformulated as saying that the moduli stack
\mathbf{M}_{g}
of genus-
g
smooth projective curves is
arithmetically hyperbolic
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Recall that a separated Deligne–Mumford stack
\mathbf{M}
of finite type over a number field is called arithmetically hyperbolic over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, if
\mathbf{M}
has a model
\mathcal{M}
over some finitely generated subring
A\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, such that for any finitely generated subring
A^{\prime}\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
containing
A
, the set
\mathcal{M}(A^{\prime})
of
A^{\prime}
-integral points on
\mathcal{M}
is finite; see
[
J20
]
.

There is a geometric analogue of the arithmetic hyperbolicity.
Recall that a separated scheme or, more generally, a separated Deligne–Mumford stack
\mathbf{M}
of finite type over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
0
is called
geometrically hyperbolic
if, for any pointed smooth integral curve
(B,0)
defined over
k
and any
k
-point
x
of
\mathbf{M}
, there exist only finitely many morphisms

f\colon B\to\mathbf{M}

such that
f(0)=x
(see
[
JL23
, Definition 2.1]
).

The
Lang–Vojta conjecture
predicts a deep relation between arithmetic hyperbolicity and geometric hyperbolicity. Roughly speaking,

Arithmetic hyperbolic
\Leftrightarrow
Geometric hyperbolic.

See Javanpeykar’s survey
[
J20
, §12]
for an account of the progress towards this conjecture.

The search for evidence of the Lang–Vojta conjecture leads to profound results in arithmetic geometry. Combining some fundamental results in Hodge theory, one can show that a separated Deligne–Mumford stack
\mathbf{M}
that admits a quasi-finite period map is geometrically hyperbolic (see
[
JL23
, Theorem 1.7]
). In particular, the moduli spaces of polarized varieties satisfying the infinitesimal Torelli theorem are geometrically hyperbolic, e.g. moduli spaces of abelian varieties, K3 surfaces, and irreducible symplectic (hyper-Kähler) varieties, with a given polarization type. The arithmetic hyperbolicity of the following moduli spaces is established, verifying the Lang–Vojta conjecture:

•

moduli space of polarized abelian varieties, by Faltings
[
RationalPoint
, Theorem 3.1]
;

•

moduli space of polarized K3 surfaces and irreducible symplectic varieties, by André
[
Andre
]
.

There exist many other families of (naturally polarized) varieties for which the analogue of Shafarevich’s conjecture for polarized pairs has been established; see the summary in
[
FLTZ22
, p. 2]
.

Inspired by the Lang–Vojta conjecture, Javanpeykar–Sun–Zuo proposed the
pointed Shafarevich conjecture
in
[
JSZ
, Conjecture 1.5]
for polarized varieties with semiample canonical bundle.
Recently, in
[
JavanpeykarLuSunZuo
]
, new finiteness results in this direction are obtained, which go beyond the situations where the infinitesimal Torelli theorem holds.

1.2.
Beyond moduli spaces: unpolarized Shafarevich conjecture

From now on, we focus on abelian varieties and symplectic varieties (and their singular generalizations).
Putting aside the interpretation using hyperbolicity of moduli spaces and going back to the original Shafarevich question, it is natural to ask about the finiteness of abelian schemes (of a fixed dimension) or symplectic varieties (of a fixed deformation type) over the base
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
, the
S
-integers in a number field
K
, without assuming the existence of a polarization with a bounded degree. This strengthening of the (polarized) Shafarevich conjecture is the so-called
unpolarized Shafarevich conjecture
. As the name suggests, its extra difficulty stems from the lack of a polarization with uniformly bounded degree; hence, the class of varieties in question does not even fit into a single moduli stack of finite type.

The unpolarized Shafarevich conjecture for abelian varieties is solved by Zarhin’s trick; see
[
RationalPoint
, Remark, Reduction 1, p168]
. For K3 surfaces, it is verified by She in
[
She
]
(see Takamatsu
[
TakamatsucohomK3
]
for further discussions). For higher-dimensional smooth irreducible symplectic varieties of a fixed deformation type, the unpolarized Shafarevich conjecture, as well as its suitable cohomological variants, are proven in our previous work
[
FLTZ22
]
.

1.3.
Geometric unpolarized Shafarevich conjecture

Regarding the interplay between Geometry and Arithmetic as in the Lang–Vojta conjecture, it is interesting to formulate and study the geometric analogue of the unpolarized Shafarevich conjecture. As in the definition of geometric hyperbolicity, the meaningful statement is about the finiteness of families over pointed curves. We formulate such a conjecture as follows for
primitive symplectic varieties
, the singular generalizations of projective hyper-Kähler varieties.

Pointed Shafarevich problem
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
. For a
pointed smooth integral curve

(B,0)
and a primitive symplectic variety
X
defined over
k
, is the set

(
\star
)

\left\{\mathcal{X}\xrightarrow{\pi}B\;\middle|\;\parbox{185.1383pt}{$\pi$ is a locally trivial family of primitive symplectic varieties with $\mathcal{X}_{0}\coloneqq\pi^{-1}(0)\cong X$}\right\}\big/\cong

finite? Here
\mathcal{X}
is an algebraic space.

The families in (

⋆

‣
Pointed Shafarevich problem
) are not required to be projective nor have a weak polarization of bounded degree. This leads to two main difficulties of this problem:

(1)

(Unboundedness) a priori, the set (

⋆

‣
Pointed Shafarevich problem
) is not parametrized by a moduli stack of finite type over
k
.

(2)

(Non-separatedness) the relevant moduli problem is highly non-separated.

We stress that (1) implies that the pointed Shafarevich problem is not a direct consequence of the (arithmetic or geometric) hyperbolicity of some moduli stacks. Moreover, (2) can indeed lead to some intrinsic infiniteness: we construct in
infinitely many non-isomorphic families of smooth irreducible symplectic varieties over some pointed curve, such that they are all isomorphic over the punctured curve and have isomorphic fibers over the base point.

1.5.
Strategy of proof and challenges

As mentioned before, the families in (

‣

Then, to reduce the proof to the polarized case, we need a kind of Zarhin’s trick for these polarized Hodge structures of K3-type to bound the degree of weak polarizations in the (

[

‣

In addition, to establish

and

To extend the finiteness results to singular primitive symplectic varieties, we establish several foundational results concerning the algebraic structure of their moduli spaces. For example, the Matsusaka–Mumford theorem for locally trivial families of primitive symplectic varieties (

2.
Primitive symplectic varieties

2.1.
Primitive symplectic varieties

In this section, we work more generally over a base field
k
of characteristic zero. We recall the following basic concepts of symplectic varieties in the singular setting, due to Beauville
[
Beau00
]
, Fujiki
[
Fujiki-Katata
]
(orbifold case), and Bakker–Lehn
[
BL22
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A normal projective variety
X
over
k
is called a

symplectic variety
, if the regular locus
X_{\reg}
carries a non-degenerate closed algebraic 2-form
\sigma
, such that there exists a resolution of singularities
\pi\colon Y\to X
such that
\pi^{*}\sigma
extends to an algebraic
2
-form on
Y
.

A symplectic variety
X
is called
primitive symplectic
, if moreover

(1)

\mathrm{H}^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
, and

(2)

\mathrm{H}^{0}(X_{\reg},\Omega_{X_{\reg}}^{2})=k\sigma
.

A smooth projective irreducible symplectic variety, also known as projective hyper-Kähler variety (see
[
Beauville83localtorelli
]
and
[
Huybrechts
]
), is clearly a primitive symplectic variety ((cf.
[
Schwald
]
)).

Remark 2.2
.

The singularities of a primitive symplectic variety is always rational, i.e., for any resolution of singularities
g\colon Y\to X
, we have
Rg_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
; see
[
Beau00
, Proposition 1.3]
or
[
BL22
, Corollary 3.5]
. Moreover, a primitive symplectic variety
X
has only terminal singularities if and only if
\codim X^{\sing}\geq 4
by Namikawa
[
Namikawa01
, Corollary 1]
.

Example 2.3
.

Moduli spaces of sheaves on K3 surfaces provide important examples of symplectic varieties; see
[
Perego20
]
for more details.
Let
(S,H)
be a smooth polarized K3 surface. For a primitive Mukai vector
v_{0}\in K_{0}(S)
with
v_{0}^{2}=k\geq 0
(which is always even), we can consider the moduli space of
H
-semistable sheaves
M_{H}(S,v)
on
S
with Mukai vector
v=mv_{0}
for some integer
m>0
. It is an irreducible projective normal variety if it is non-empty, which admits a symplectic form on the regular locus. Assume further that
H
is
v
-generic.

(1)

If
k\geq 0,m=1
, then
M_{H}(S,v)
is a smooth irreducible symplectic variety of dimension
k+2
.

(2)

If
k=0,m\geq 2
, then
M_{H}(S,v)=S^{\prime(n)}
, the
n
-th symmetric product of a K3 surface
S^{\prime}
. It is a primitive symplectic variety since it has a symplectic resolution
S^{\prime[n]}\to S^{\prime(n)}
by the Hilbert scheme of
n
points. However, it admits a quasi-étale covering
S^{\prime\times n}\to S^{\prime(n)}
such that
\dim H^{0}(S^{\prime\times n},\Omega_{S^{\prime\times n}}^{2})=n
, thus is not irreducible when

in the sense of
[
GKP
, Definition 8.16]
.

(3)

If
k\geq 2,m\geq 2
, then it is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial (see
[
KLS06
]
and
[
PR14
]
) primitive symplectic variety. If
k=m=2
, then it is at worst
2
-factorial (
[
PR14
, Theorem 1.1]
) and it admits a symplectic resolution, which is of OG10 deformation type. The rest cases are locally factorial (
[
KLS06

) with terminal singularities, since their singular loci have codimension

(cf.
[

Example 2.4
.

Recently, a new series of examples of

Example 2.5

Unlike in the smooth case, based on

The automorphism groups of primitive symplectic varieties behave similarly as those of smooth ones:

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
X
be a primitive symplectic variety over
k
. Then

(1)

\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,T_{X})=0
.

(2)

For any ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
, the automorphism group scheme
\underline{\Aut}(X,\mathcal{L})
is finite and étale over
k
.

(3)

If
X
is terminal, then the birational automorphism group functor
\underline{{\rm Bir}}(X)
(see
[
Hanamura-Bir
]
for the precise definition) is represented by a locally of finite type group scheme, and
\dim\underline{{\rm Bir}}(X)=0
. Moreover,
{\rm Bir}(X_{L})=\underline{{\rm Bir}}(X)(L)
is countable as a set for any field
k\subset L
.

Proof.

The first statement follows directly from
[
BL22
, Lemma 4.6]
.

For the second statement, choose
m
such that
\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}
is very ample and observe that the embedding
\varphi_{|\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}|}\colon X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
gives rise to a closed immersion of group schemes

\underline{\Aut}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m})\hookrightarrow{\rm PGL}_{N}.

Therefore,
\underline{\Aut}(X,\mathcal{L})\hookrightarrow\underline{\Aut}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m})
is a smooth linear algebraic group over
k
. Since
\dim\underline{\Aut}(X,\mathcal{L})=\dim\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,T_{X})=0
, and
\operatorname{char}(k)=0
, the group scheme
\underline{\Aut}(X,\mathcal{L})
must be finite and étale over
k
.

In (3), the representability of
\underline{{\rm Bir}}(X)
is given by
[
Hanamura-Bir
, Theorem (3.3)]
. Moreover, we can see
\dim\underline{{\rm Bir}}(X)=\mathrm{H}^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
by Corollary (4.8) in loc.cit.. The group scheme
{\rm Bir}(X)
is an open subscheme of the Hilbert scheme
\Hilb(X\times X)
, which has countably many connected components. Then we can see
{\rm Bir}(X_{L})=\pi_{0}(\underline{{\rm Bir}}(X))
is a countable set.
∎

2.2.
Locally trivial families of primitive symplectic varieties

Definition 2.7
.

Let
B
be a complex variety, and
\mathcal{X}
a complex algebraic space.
A proper flat holomorphic morphism
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to B
is a
family of primitive symplectic varieties over

if all geometric fibers are primitive symplectic varieties.

Similarly, over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic

, we define a family of primitive symplectic varieties as a proper flat morphism from a
k
-algebraic space to a
k
-variety with all geometric fibers primitive symplectic varieties over
k
.

Definition 2.8
.

A family of primitive symplectic varieties
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to B
over
k
is called
locally trivial
if for any closed point
x\in\mathcal{X}
, there is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{B}
-algebras

(2.1)

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X},x}^{sh}\cong\mathcal{O}_{B,\pi(x)}^{sh}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\pi(x)},x}^{sh},

where
(-)^{sh}
denotes the strictly Henselization at the given point.

Recall that a holomorphic map between complex analytic spaces
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to B
is called
locally trivial
(see
[
FK87
]
) if for any point
t\in B
and any point
p\in\mathcal{X}_{t}
, there exists an analytic open neighborhood
p\in\mathfrak{U}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
such that

t=\pi(p)\in V\coloneqq\pi(\mathfrak{U})\subseteq B
is an analytic open neighborhood and
there is a biholomorphism
\mathfrak{U}\cong V\times(\mathfrak{U}\cap\mathcal{X}_{t})
commuting with the projections to
V
.

It is useful to observe that the local triviality of a family can be checked both formal locally or in the analytic category when
k=\mathbb{C}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to B
be a family of primitive symplectic varieties over
\mathbb{C}
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

The family
\pi
is locally trivial in the sense of
Definition

2.8
.

(2)

The analytification
\pi^{\an}\colon\mathcal{X}^{\an}\to B^{\an}
is locally trivial as a map between complex analytic spaces.

(3)

for any
x\in\mathcal{X}^{\an}
, there is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{B}
-algebras

(2.2)

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{X}^{\an},x}\simeq\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{B^{\an},\pi(x)}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{X}_{\pi(x)}^{\an},x},

where
\widehat{(-)}
denotes the formal completion at the given point.

Proof.

The implication
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
follows directly by taking formal completion. The implication
(3)\Rightarrow(2)
follows from
[
Artin68
, Corollary (1.6)]
.
Moreover, Artin’s approximation theorem (
[
Artin69
, Corollary 2.6]
) implies that (1) and (3) are equivalent.
∎

to

Proof.

This is essentially addressed in

∎

It is worth noting that a locally trivial family is locally acyclic in the étale topology.

Lemma 2.12
.

If

is locally trivial (in the sense of

), then the higher direct image

Proof.

Since

in

, Tag 0GJW]
, it is sufficient to show

is locally acyclic, i.e., the pull-back

(2.3)

is an isomorphism for any geometric point

of
\mathcal{X}
, and geometric point
\bar{t}
of

is the strictly Henselization.
Then we have

\displaystyle R\Gamma\left(\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X},\bar{x}}^{sh}),\underline{\mathbb{Z}/n}\right)

\displaystyle\cong R\Gamma\left(\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\pi(\bar{x})},\bar{x}}^{sh}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{O}_{B,\pi(\bar{x})}^{sh}),\underline{\mathbb{Z}/n}\right)

where the second isomorphism is given by the Künneth formula. For any geometric point
\bar{t}

is the base extension of

along

2.3.
\mathbb{Q}
-factoriality in locally trivial families

In analyzing the birational geometry of primitive symplectic varieties, it is common to assume they are
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial. However, some basic geometric operations can disrupt
\mathbb{Q}
-factoriality. This section outlines essential facts for subsequent discussions. As before,
k
is denoted for an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
.

First, we record a technical lemma, which allows us to compare the geometric generic fiber with a very general fiber in a given family.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
B
be an integral variety over
k
, and
\mathcal{X}\rightarrow B
be a flat proper algebraic space.
Let
\Omega
be a universal domain containing
k
, i.e., an algebraically closed field with infinite transcendental degree over its prime field.
Let
\eta
be the generic point of
B
.
There exist countably many non-empty open subschemes
\{U_{i}\subset B\}_{i\in I}
such that for any
\Omega
-point

b\in\bigcap_{i\in I}U_{i}(\Omega)\subset B(\Omega)

we have an (abstract) isomorphism
\mathcal{X}_{\overline{\eta_{{\Omega}}}}\cong\mathcal{X}_{b}
of schemes, (which induces an isomorphism
\overline{\eta_{\Omega}}\simeq\Omega
),
where
\overline{\eta_{\Omega}}
is the geometric generic point of
B_{\Omega}
. In particular, there is an isomorphism

{\rm Pic}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{\eta_{{\Omega}}}})\simeq{\rm Pic}(\mathcal{X}_{b}).

Proof.

Set
B_{\Omega}=B\times_{k}\Omega
.
By the spreading out argument for morphisms of finite type, between algebraic spaces of finite type over
\Omega
, which follows from
[
stacks-project
, 06G4, 07SK, 0CPC, 0CPE]
, there exist a finitely generated subfield
F\subset\Omega
and a variety
T
of finite type over
F
, satisfying the following.

(1)

We have a
\Omega
-isomorphism
T\times_{F}\Omega\simeq B_{\Omega}
.

(2)

There exists a proper algebraic space
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
over
T
such that
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\times_{F}\Omega\simeq\mathcal{X}_{\Omega}
compatible with the isomorphism in (1).

With the same argument as in the proof of
[
Vial-CyclesAndFibration
, Lemma 2.1]
, we can find the required isomorphism

\mathcal{X}_{\overline{\eta}_{\Omega}}\cong\mathcal{X}_{b}

for some
b\in B(\Omega)
inside the intersection of a countable set of non-empty open subschemes
\{U_{i}\subset B\}_{i\in I}
.
∎

Definition 2.14
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\rightarrow B
be a locally trivial family of primitive symplectic varieties over an integral variety
B
over
k
.
We say
\mathcal{X}
is a
locally trivial family of
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial primitive symplectic varieties
when
\mathcal{X}_{b}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial primitive symplectic variety for any closed point
b\in B
.

In general, the notion of
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial is not stable under étale base change. Fortunately, it satisfies geometric invariance under mild singularity assumptions. The following statement was taught to the third author by Shou Yoshikawa.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
F/k
be an extension of algebraically closed fields.
Let
X
be a normal projective variety over
k
.
Then
X_{F}
is a normal projective
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial klt variety if and only if
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial and klt.
The same holds if we replace klt by terminal.

Proof.

Let us only show that if
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial and klt then
X_{F}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial. The other assertions are easy and left to the reader.
Let
Y
be a projective log resolution of
X
.
Then, as
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial, we have a sequence of birational maps over
k
:

Y=Y_{0}\overset{f_{0}}{\dashrightarrow}Y_{1}\overset{f_{1}}{\dashrightarrow}\cdots\overset{f_{n-1}}{\dashrightarrow}Y_{n}=X\,,

by the proof of
[
Lyu-Murayama
, Theorem 22.1]
.
Here, each dotted arrow represents either a divisorial contraction or a flip (for some klt pair) over
k
.
We claim that all
Y_{i,F}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial. In particular,
X_{n,F}=X_{F}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial. The claim can be proved by induction.
Since
Y_{0,F}
is regular,
Y_{0,F}
is clearly
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial.
Assume that
Y_{i,F}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial.

(1)

If
f_{i}
is a divisorial contraction, then the base extension
f_{i,F}\colon Y_{i,F}\rightarrow Y_{i+1,F}
is also a divisorial contraction since it is still of relative Picard number
1
as
k
and
F
are algebraically closed. Thus
Y_{i+1,F}
is also
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial (see
[
Lyu-Murayama
, Lemma 19.3]
for example).

(2)

If
f_{i}
is a flip, i.e., it factors as

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{i+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{+}_{i}}

where
g_{i}
and
g_{i}^{+}
are small contractions. After the base extension to
F
,
g_{i,F}
and
g_{i,F}^{+}
are still of relative Picard number 1 as
k
is algebraically closed.
Moreover, clearly
g_{i,F}
and
g_{i,F}^{+}
are small contractions.
Therefore,
f_{i,F}
is a filp, and
Y_{i+1,F}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial (see
[
Lyu-Murayama
, Lemma 20.3]
for example). ∎

Thanks to
Lemma

2.15
, we can see the notion of locally trivial family of
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial primitive varieties is well-behaved under a base-change.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to B
be a locally trivial family of primitive symplectic varieties over
k
. Let
0\in B
be a regular
k
-point. Suppose the fiber
X\coloneqq\mathcal{X}_{0}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminal primitive symplectic variety.

(1)

The geometric generic fiber
\mathcal{X}_{\overline{\eta}}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial and terminal.

(2)

The family
\pi
is a locally trivial family of
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial primitive symplectic varieties if and only if any geometric fiber
\mathcal{X}_{s}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial primitive symplectic variety. Moreover, in any case, all geometric fibers of
\pi
have terminal singularities.

Proof.

For (1), we may assume that
B
is regular by shrinking
B
since (1) is about the geometric generic fiber and
0\in B
is assumed to be regular.
By Lemma
2.15
and the spreading out argument (see Lemma
2.13
for example), we may assume that
k
is an algebraic closure of finitely generated field of characteristic
0
.
By choosing the embedding
k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
and using Lemma
2.15
again, we can reduce the problem to the case where
k=\mathbb{C}
.
In this case, there exists an open subscheme
0\in U\subset B
such that
\mathcal{X}|_{U}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial by
[
KollarMori92
, (12.1.9)]
and the Bertini theorem.
Here, note that, since
\pi
is locally trivial, on each fiber, the singular locus has codimension
\geq 4
by
[
Namikawa01
, Corollary 1]
.
By Lemma
2.13
, for general closed point
b\in U
,
we have an isomorphism (as a scheme)
\mathcal{X}_{b}\simeq\mathcal{X}_{\overline{\eta}}
.
Therefore, we obtain the desired result.

For (2), it is enough to show the “only if” part.
We fix a geometric point
s
of
B
.
By taking the Zariski closure of the image of
s
, we can reduce this to (1).
It finishes the proof.
∎

2.4.
Period map and Local Torelli Theorem

For a complex primitive symplectic variety
X
, it is known that the torsion-free part of the second cohomology

\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\tf}\coloneqq\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})/\text{torsion}

carries a pure Hodge structure of weight
2
, since
X
has at worst rational singularities. Moreover, there exists an integral quadratic form called Beauville–Bogomolov(–Fujiki–Namikawa) form

q_{X}\colon\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\tf}\to\mathbb{Z}

that is compatible with the Hodge structure on
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\tf}
(see
[
Namikawaextension
, Theorem 8]
or
[
BL22
, Subsection 5.1]
for example).
This quadratic form coincides with the classical Beauville–Bogomolov form when
X
is an irreducible symplectic manifold (up to scaling) and is also referred to as the
Beauville–Bogomolov form
on
X
.

Analogous to irreducible symplectic manifolds, when
X
varies in a locally trivial family, the Hodge structure and the Beauville–Bogomolov form on
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\tf}
forms a polarized variation of Hodge structure.

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to B
be a locally trivial family of primitive symplectic varieties over
\mathbb{C}
.

(1)

The higher direct images
R^{i}\pi^{\an}_{*}\mathbb{Z}
are
\mathbb{Z}
-local systems. Moreover,
R^{2}\pi^{\an}_{*}\mathbb{Z}
is equipped with a
\mathbb{Z}
-variation of Hodge structure.

(2)

There exists a morphism of
\mathbb{Z}
-variations of Hodge structure.

q\colon\Sym^{2}R^{2}\pi^{\an}_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1)\to\mathbb{Z},

such that
q_{\mathcal{X}_{b}}
on each fiber
\mathcal{X}_{b}
is the Beauville–Bogomolov form.

Proof.

The statement (1) is due to Namikawa (
[
Namikawa06
, p.13]
(arXiv version)). See also
[
Amerik-Verbitskycollections
, Proposition 5.1]
. For other statements, see
[
BL22
, Corollary 3.5, Lemma 5.7, Lemma 4.9]
.
∎

The local Torelli theorem completes the picture:

Proposition 2.18

(Local Torelli Theorem)
.

Let
X
be a complex primitive symplectic variety,
and
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to\Def^{\lt}(X)
be the Kuranishi family of locally trivial deformations of
X
(see
[
BL22
, Subsection 4.4]
). Let
\Lambda=\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\tf}
the lattice with Beauville–Bogomolov form. The period map

\Def^{\lt}(X)\to\Omega_{\Lambda}

associated with the variation of Hodge structure
R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z}
is a local isomorphism.
Here,
\Omega_{\Lambda}
is the period domain

\Omega_{\Lambda}:=\left\{[\sigma]\in\mathbb{P}(\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{C})\;\middle|\;q(\sigma)=0,q(\sigma,\bar{\sigma})>0\right\}.

Proof.

See
[
BL22
, Proposition 5.5]
.
∎

Cone conjectures for primitive symplectic varieties

For K3 surfaces, the study of the action of the automorphism group on the nef cone plays an important role for showing finiteness results; see
[
Sterk
]
,
[
Bright-Logan-vanLuijk
]
.
In this section, we recall its higher-dimensional generalizations, namely the so-called Kawamata–Morrison cone conjectures, which have been established for primitive symplectic varieties (see
[
Markmansurvey
]
,
[
Markman-Yoshioka
]
,
[
AV17
]
for the smooth case and
[
LMP22
]
for the generalization to the singular setting).

3.1.
Néron–Severi lattices and cones

Let
B
be a variety over
k
and
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to B
a family of primitive symplectic varieties (in the sense of
Definition

2.7
).

Suppose
k=\mathbb{C}
, the exponential sequence induces an exact sequence of analytic sheaves

0=R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}\to\underline{{\rm Pic}}_{\mathcal{X}/B}\to R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z}\to R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}.

Since fibers
\mathcal{X}_{b}
have at worst rational singularities, Du Bois–Jarraud’s base-change theorem
[
KollarGeneralType
, Theorem 2.62, Complement 2.62.5]
implies that
R^{i}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
is locally free for all integers
i\geq 0
.
Since
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{X}_{b},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{b}})=0
for any
b\in B
, the Picard scheme
\underline{{\rm Pic}}_{\mathcal{X}/B}
is of dimension zero. For this reason, the identity component
\underline{{\rm Pic}}^{0}_{\mathcal{X}/B}
is trivial and on the geometric fiber
\mathcal{X}_{\bar{b}}
,

\underline{{\rm Pic}}_{\mathcal{X}/B}(\bar{b})\cong{\rm Pic}(\mathcal{X}_{\bar{b}})\cong{\rm NS}(\mathcal{X}_{\bar{b}})

are Néron–Severi groups. Moreover, it is easy to see the Lefschetz-
(1,1)
theorem holds by
Proposition

2.17
(1).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a primitive symplectic variety over a subfield
k\subseteq\mathbb{C}
. The
Néron–Severi lattice
is the (torsion-free) Néron–Severi group

{\rm NS}(X_{k})_{\tf}={\rm Pic}(X_{k})_{\tf}

together with restriction of the Beauville–Bogomolov form along

{\rm NS}(X_{k})_{\tf}={\rm NS}(X_{\mathbb{C}})_{\tf}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}(X_{\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Z})_{\tf}.

For simplicity, we denote
{\rm NS}(X_{k})
for the Néron–Severi lattice
{\rm NS}(X_{k})_{\tf}
in the following.

Remark 3.2
.

By spreading out argument and the proof of
[
Bindt
, Corollary 4.2.1]
, there is a unique Beauville–Bogomolov form on
{\rm NS}(X_{k})_{\tf}
for any field
k
in characteristic zero, which is independent of the field embedding
k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
.

In the following,
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to B
is assumed to be
projective
, and each fiber
\mathcal{X}_{b}
for
b\in B
is a primitive symplectic variety with
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminal singularities. In this case, the Picard scheme
\underline{{\rm Pic}}_{\mathcal{X}/S}
admits a global section over
B
given by the relative ample line bundle and thus
\rk\underline{{\rm Pic}}_{\mathcal{X}/B}(B)\geq 1
.

Consider the following relative Néron–Severi space on
\mathcal{X}
(modulo
\pi
-numerical equivalence):

N^{1}(\mathcal{X}/B)_{\mathbb{R}}=\underline{{\rm Pic}}_{\mathcal{X}/B}(B)/_{\equiv_{\pi}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}

where

is the numerical equivalence relation over

, i.e.,
D_{1}\equiv_{\pi}D_{2}
if and only if

for any curve in
\mathcal{X}
such that

is a point.

Definition 3.3
.

Let

be the cone generated by effective

-Cartier divisors over

(1)

(2)

{\rm Nef}^{+}(\mathcal{X}/B)\coloneqq\Conv({\rm Nef}(\mathcal{X}/B)\cap N^{1}(\mathcal{X}/B)_{\mathbb{Q}})

{{\rm Mov}}^{+}(\mathcal{X}/B)\coloneqq\Conv(\overline{{\rm Mov}}(\mathcal{X}/B)\cap N^{1}(\mathcal{X}/B)_{\mathbb{Q}})

For simplicity of notations, we denote

Conjecture 3.4

(Kawamata–Morrison Cone Conjecture)
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to B
be a projective family of primitive symplectic varieties with
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminal singularities.

(1)

The effective nef cone

admits a rational polyhedral fundamental domain under the action of the relative automorphism group

\mathcal{X}
over

(2)

3.2.

In this subsection, we consider the cone conjecture when

over a field. Here

The motivation is to obtain some finiteness results on the generic fibers of families of primitive symplectic varieties.

Fix an algebraic closure
\overline{F}
for

If

Notice that being ample (resp. movable) is stable under field extensions, thus we have
{\rm BA}(X)={\rm BA}(X_{\overline{F}})\cap N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
(resp.

[

Proof.

4.
Global moduli theory of primitive symplectic varieties

In this section, we will construct the moduli stack
\mathbf{M}_{2n,d}
of locally trivial families of polarized primitive symplectic varieties of degree
d
over an algebraically closed field

of characteristic
0

is a Deligne–Mumford stack that is separated and of finite type over

(see

4.6
). For the separatedness of
\mathbf{M}_{2n,d}
, the key point is to establish the Matsusaka–Mumford theorem for singular symplectic varieties (
Proposition

4.2
and
Corollary

4.3
), which is originally stated for smooth families.

As an application, we will establish the following finiteness result by the geometric hyperbolicity of
\mathbf{M}_{2n,d}
, using a method similar to that in
[
FLTZ22
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(B,0)
be a pointed smooth variety over
k

with the generic point
\eta
.
Let
X
be a projective primitive symplectic variety over
k
.
Let
d
be a positive integer.
Then the following set is finite:

\mathrm{Shaf}_{B,X,d}\coloneqq\left\{(\mathcal{X}_{\eta},L)\;\middle|\;\parbox{200.0003pt}{$\mathcal{X}\to B$ is a locally trivial family of primitive symplectic varieties such that $\mathcal{X}_{0}\cong X$, $L\in{\rm Pic}_{\mathcal{X}_{\eta}/\eta}(\eta)$ is a polarization of degree $d$}\right\}/_{\simeq_{\eta}}.

4.1.
Matsusaka–Mumford theorem for locally trivial families

In this subsection, we prove a Matsusaka–Mumford type theorem for locally trivial families of (possibly singular) primitive symplectic varieties.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
B
be a variety over
k
, and
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}},\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}
locally trivial families of primitive symplectic varieties over
B
. Fix a regular codimension-1 point of
B
, let
R
be its local ring with residue field
s
and fraction field
\eta
, and set
\mathcal{X}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{R}
,
\mathcal{Y}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{R}
. For any birational map
f:\mathcal{X}_{\eta}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{Y}_{\eta}
between generic fibers, there exist closed algebraic subspaces
V\subset\mathcal{X}
,
W\subset\mathcal{Y}
with
V_{s}\neq\mathcal{X}_{s}
,
W_{s}\neq\mathcal{Y}_{s}
, such that
f
extends to an isomorphism:

\widetilde{f}:\mathcal{X}\setminus V\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{Y}\setminus W.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.11
, we can take simultaneous resolutions

\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}\quad\textup{and}\quad\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}.

We note that we have a birational map

f^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{\eta}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}_{\eta}

induced by
f
, and
\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}_{s}
is non-ruled by the assumption.
Then by
[
FLTZ22
, Theorem A.2]
,
there exist closed algebraic subspaces
V^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
and
W^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}
with

V^{\prime}_{s}\neq\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s}
and

W^{\prime}_{s}\neq\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}_{s}

such that
f^{\prime}
extends to

\widetilde{f}^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\setminus V^{\prime}\simeq\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}\setminus W^{\prime}.

Also, by the construction of simultaneous resolutions, there exist closed subspaces
Z_{\mathcal{X}}
,
Z_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}
,
Z_{\mathcal{Y}}
,
Z_{\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}}
on
\mathcal{X}
,
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{Y}
,
\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}
respectively such that

\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\setminus Z_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}\simeq\mathcal{X}\setminus Z_{\mathcal{X}}\quad\textup{and}\quad\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}\setminus Z_{\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}}\simeq\mathcal{Y}\setminus Z_{\mathcal{Y}}.

Let
V^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}
and
W^{\prime}_{\mathcal{Y}}
be the image of
V
and
W
in
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
respectively.
Then by putting

V:=V^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\cup Z_{\mathcal{X}}\quad\textup{and}\quad W:=W^{\prime}_{\mathcal{Y}}\cup Z_{\mathcal{Y}},

we obtain the assertion.
∎

As an application, we get the following result.

Corollary 4.3

(Polarized Matsusaka–Mumford Theorem)
.

Let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
be as in Proposition
4.2
, and suppose
f:\mathcal{X}_{\eta}\to\mathcal{Y}_{\eta}
is an isomorphism. If there exist ample line bundles
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{M}
on
\mathcal{Y}
over
R
with
f^{*}\mathcal{M}_{\eta}=\mathcal{L}_{\eta}
, then

extends uniquely to a global isomorphism:

\widetilde{f}:\mathcal{X}\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{Y}.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.2
,
f
extends to an isomorphism outside closed subspaces
V\subset\mathcal{X}
and
W\subset\mathcal{Y}
. The polarization compatibility
f^{*}\mathcal{M}_{\eta}=\mathcal{L}_{\eta}
forces

via the ampleness propagation in
[

, Proposition 3.1.2]
. Thus
\widetilde{f}
, as well as its inverse, is everywhere defined.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
B
be a variety over
k
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
a locally trivial family of primitive symplectic varieties over
B
, and
R
the localization of

at a regular codimension-1 point with residue field
s
and fraction field
\eta
. Set
\mathcal{X}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{R}
. For any finite subgroup

(1)

There exists a closed algebraic subspace

with

, and a homomorphism

Apply Proposition

to each

∎

4.2.
Moduli stack of polarized primitive symplectic varieties

Let
\Sch_{\mathbb{C}}
be the site of schemes of finite type over
k
with étale topology. Consider the following fibered category in groupoids

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{2n,d}\colon\Sch_{k}

\displaystyle\to\Grps

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle\rightsquigarrow\left\{(\mathcal{X}\xrightarrow{\pi}B,\lambda)\;\middle|\;\parbox{150.00023pt}{$\pi$ is a locally trivial family of primitive symplectic varieties of dimension $2n$ and $\lambda$ is a polarization on $\mathcal{X}$ with $(\lambda)^{2n}=d$.}\right\},

where isomorphisms in the groupoids are the natural ones for pairs, and a polarization on
\mathcal{X}
is an element
\lambda\in{\rm Pic}_{\mathcal{X}/B}(B)
whose restriction on any geometric fiber is an ample line bundle.

Remark 4.5
.

By the discussion in
Section

3.1
, over
\mathbb{C}
, an (analytic) global section of the Picard scheme is equivalent to a family of integral Hodge
(1,1)
-classes of
\pi
.

Theorem 4.6
.

The moduli stack
\mathbf{M}_{2n,d}
of locally trivial families of polarized primitive symplectic varieties of degree
d
is a Deligne–Mumford stack, which is smooth, separated, and of finite type over
k
.

Proof.

Since all primitive symplectic varieties have rational singularities, Matsusaka’s big theorem can be applied (see
[
Matsusaka
, Theorem 2.4]
). Thus the moduli stack
\mathbf{M}^{P}
of locally trivial families of polarized primitive symplectic varieties with Hilbert polynomial
P
is a finite type algebraic stack over
k
(cf.
[
Bindt
, Lemma 3.2.5, Lemma 3.3.6, 3.3.7]
).
Lemma

2.6
implies that
\mathbf{M}^{P}
is Deligne–Mumford.
Therefore, the stack

\mathbf{M}_{2n,d}=\bigsqcup_{P\in I_{d,n}}\mathbf{M}^{P}

is a Deligne–Mumford stack locally of finite type over
k
, where

I_{d,n}\coloneqq\left\{P(t)=\sum_{i\geq 0}^{2n}a_{i}t^{i}\in\mathbb{Q}[t]\;\middle|\;\parbox{110.00017pt}{$a_{2n}=\dfrac{d}{(2n)!}$ and $\mathbf{M}^{P}\neq\varnothing$}\right\}.

The rest of the required properties can be verified as follows.

(1)

Since a primitive symplectic variety has only rational singularities, the Kodaria vanishing theorem holds. Thus, as
X
is
K
-trivial, the Hilbert polynomial of
X
with respect to an ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
is equal to
P(t)=\dim\lvert\mathcal{L}^{\otimes t}\rvert
. Then, Kollár–Matsusaka’s inequality (
[
Kollar-Matsusaka
, Theorem]
) implies that
I_{d,n}
is a finite set. Therefore,
\mathbf{M}_{2n,d}
is of finite type over
k
.

(2)

The theory of locally trivial deformation given in
[
BL22
, Theorem 4.7, Lemma 4.13]
implies that
\mathbf{M}_{2n,d}
is smooth over
k
.

(3)

Corollary

4.3
implies that the DM stack
\mathbf{M}_{2n,d}
is separated over
k
. ∎

4.3.

In this part, we will always assume

Fix a connected component

\Omega_{\Lambda_{h}}
of the orthogonal group
{\rm SO}(\Lambda_{h})_{\mathbb{Q}}

\Omega_{\Lambda_{h}}^{\pm}\coloneqq\left\{[\sigma]\in\mathbb{P}(\Lambda_{h}\otimes\mathbb{C})\;\middle|\;q(\sigma)=0,q(\sigma,\bar{\sigma})>0\right\}.

\mathcal{P}_{h}\colon\mathbf{M}_{2n,d}^{\circ,\an}\to[\Gamma_{h}\backslash\Omega_{\Lambda_{h}}^{\pm}],

Proposition 4.7
.

The period map
\mathcal{P}_{h}

Let

The analytic completion of the period map

In general, a locally trivial family is not necessarily a projective morphism. For this reason, we propose the following weaker notion of polarization and show that it always exists.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
(B,0)
be a pointed connected variety.
Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to B
be a locally trivial family of primitive symplectic varieties. Let
\Lambda
be the lattice
\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathcal{X}_{0},\mathbb{Z})
.

(1)

A
weak polarization
of
\pi
is a global section
\underline{L}\in\Gamma(B,(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z})_{\tf})
such that

(a)

\underline{L}_{b}
is
(1,1)
-class for any point
b\in B(\mathbb{C})
with
q(\underline{L}_{b})>0
, and

(b)

\underline{L}_{b_{0}}=c_{1}(\mathcal{L})
for an ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathcal{X}_{b_{0}}
for a very general point
b_{0}
of
B
.

(2)

Let
h\in\Lambda
with
q(h)>0
. A weak polarization on the pointed family
\mathcal{X}/B
is
of type
[h]
if
c_{1}(\underline{L}_{0})\in[h]=\operatorname{O}(\Lambda)\cdot h
.

We shall remark the finiteness of polarization types on a pointed family.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
m
be a positive integer. There are finitely many
\mathrm{O}(\Lambda)
-orbit
[h]
such that the Beauville–Bogomolov square
q(h)=m
. In particular, for a pointed family of primitive symplectic varieties, a polarization of degree
d
has finitely many possible equivalent polarization types.

Proof.

Any such
h
determines an embedding of lattices
\langle m\rangle\hookrightarrow\Lambda
, and two
h
and
h^{\prime}
lie in the same
\mathrm{O}(\Lambda)
-orbit if and only if their corresponding embeddings are isomorphic. By
[
Kneser02
, Satz (30.2)]
, there are only finitely many such embeddings up to the action of
\mathrm{O}(\Lambda)
.
∎

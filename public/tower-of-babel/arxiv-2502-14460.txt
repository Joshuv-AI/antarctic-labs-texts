Signless Laplacian State Transfer on Vertex Complemented Coronae
Ke-Yu Zhu, Gui-Xian Tian, Shu-Yu Cui

Abstract
Given a graph G G with vertex set V ⁡ ( G ) = { v 1 , v 2 , … , v n 1 } V(G)=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n_{1}}\} and a graph H H of order n 2 n_{2} , the vertex complemented corona, denoted by G ​ ∘ ~ ​ H G\tilde{\circ}{H} , is the graph produced by copying H H n 1 n_{1} times, with the i i -th copy of H H corresponding to the vertex v i v_{i} , and then adding edges between any vertex in V ⁡ ( G ) ∖ { v i } V(G)\setminus\{v_{i}\} and any vertex of the i i -th copy of H H . The present article deals with quantum state transfer of vertex complemented coronae concerning signless Laplacian matrix. Our research investigates conditions in which signless Laplacian perfect state transfer exists or not on vertex complemented coronae. Additionally, we also provide some mild conditions for the class of graphs under consideration that allow signless Laplacian pretty good state transfer. Preliminaries

Given a connected graph
G
, let
A(G)
and
D(G)
denote its adjacency matrix and degree diagonal matrix. We use
Q(G)=D(G)+A(G)
to represent the signless Laplacian matrix of
G
, and the collection
\theta_{0}>\theta_{1}>\cdots>\theta_{m}
represent all distinct eigenvalues of
Q(G)
, which is referred to as
\text{Spec}_{Q}(G)
. The spectral decomposition theorem allows us to write

Q(G)=\sum_{r=0}^{m}\theta_{r}F_{\theta_{r}}(G),

where
F_{\theta_{r}}(G)
denotes the signless Laplacian eigenprojector relative to the eigenvalue
\theta_{r}
of
Q(G)
. Since all eigenprojectors are orthogonal, then the transition matrix
U_{Q(G)}(\tau)
is provided by

U_{Q(G)}(\tau)=\exp(-\mathrm{i}\tau Q(G))=\sum_{r=0}^{m}\exp(-\mathrm{i}\tau\theta_{r})F_{\theta_{r}}.

The signless Laplacian eigenvalue support of a vertex
u
in
G
, portrayed as
\mathrm{supp}_{G}(u)
, is the set of eigenvalues
\theta
of
Q(G)
which ensures
F_{\theta}(G)\mathbf{e}_{u}\neq 0
, where
\mathbf{e}_{u}
is the characteristic vector of the vertex
u
. We say two vertices
u
and
v
are signless Laplacian strongly cospectral if and only if
F_{\theta}(G)\mathbf{e}_{u}=\pm F_{\theta}(G)\mathbf{e}_{v}
, for
\forall\;\theta\in\text{Spec}_{Q}(G)
. Based on this, we define
\Lambda^{+}
for the set of
\theta\in\mathrm{supp}_{G}(u)
such that
F_{\lambda}(G)\mathbf{e}_{u}=F_{\lambda}(G)\mathbf{e}_{v}
, and
\Lambda^{-}
for the set of
\theta\in\mathrm{supp}_{G}(u)
such that
F_{\lambda}(G)\mathbf{e}_{u}=-F_{\lambda}(G)\mathbf{e}_{v}
.

[
6
]
, are beneficial to us.

Signless Laplacian of vertex complemented coronae

Let
G
be a graph with vertex set
V(G)=\{v_{1},...,v_{n_{1}}\}
and
H
a graph of order
n_{2}
. To make things easier, the vertex set of
G\tilde{\circ}{H}
is indicated in the following:

V(G\tilde{\circ}{H})=V(G)\times\left(\{0\}\cup V(H)\right),

and the edge set satisfies the relation as follows:

(v_{i},w)\sim(v_{j},w^{\prime})\Longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}w=w^{\prime}=0\text{ and }v_{i}\sim v_{j}\text{ in }G,\;\text{or}\\
v_{i}=v_{j}\text{ and }w\sim w^{\prime}\text{ in }H,\;\text{or}\\
v_{i}\neq v_{j}\text{and precisely one of }w\text{ and }w^{\prime}\text{ is }0.\end{array}\right.

If
G
and

are both regular graphs, the signless Laplacian eigenvalues and eigenprojectors of

Theorem 3.1.

Suppose
G
is an
r_{1}
-regular connected graph with
n_{1}
vertices and

is an
r_{2}
-regular graph with

(a)

For each signless Laplacian eigenvalue
\mu
of

n_{1}-1+\mu
is a signless Laplacian eigenvalue of

with the eigenprojector

F_{n_{1}-1+\mu}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&I_{n_{1}}\otimes\left(F_{\mu}(H)-\delta_{\mu,2r_{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{n_{2}}J_{n_{2}}\right)\end{array}\right),

(2)

where
F_{\mu}(H)
stands for the eigenprojector corresponding to the signless Laplacian eigenvalue
\mu
of
H
, and the function
\delta_{\mu,2r_{2}}
has the form, as below

\delta_{\mu,2r_{2}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1,&\mu=2r_{2},\\
0,&\mu\neq 2r_{2}.\end{array}\right.

(b)

For each signless Laplacian eigenvalue
\theta
of
G
except that the eigenvalue
2r_{1}
,
\theta_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(\theta+s+t\pm\sqrt{(\theta-s+t)^{2}+4n_{2}})
are signless Laplacian eigenvalues of

\displaystyle\left.F_{\theta_{\pm}}(G\tilde{\circ}{H})=F_{\theta}(G)\otimes\frac{1}{(s-\theta_{\pm})^{2}+n_{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}(s-\theta_{\pm})^{2}&(s-\theta_{\pm})\mathbf{j}_{n_{2}}^{T}\\
(s-\theta_{\pm})\mathbf{j}_{n_{2}}&J_{n_{2}}\end{array}\right.\right),

(3)

where
s=n_{1}+2r_{2}-1
and

.

(c)

r_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(2r_{1}+s+t)\pm\sqrt{(2r_{1}-s+t)^{2}+4n_{2}(n_{1}-1)^{2}}
are signless Laplacian eigenvalues of
G\tilde{\circ}{H}
with the eigenprojectors

\displaystyle F_{r_{\pm}}(G\tilde{\circ}{H})=F_{2r_{1}}(G)\otimes\frac{1}{(s-r_{\pm})^{2}+n_{2}(1-n_{1})^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}(s-r_{\pm})^{2}&(s-r_{\pm})(1-n_{1})\mathbf{j}_{n_{2}}^{T}\\
(s-r_{\pm})(1-n_{1})\mathbf{j}_{n_{2}}&(1-n_{1})^{2}J_{n_{2}}\end{array}\right).

(4)

Moreover, the spectral decomposition of
Q(G\tilde{\circ}{H})
is provided by:

Q(G\tilde{\circ}{H})=\left(\sum_{\theta\in\mathrm{Spec}_{Q}(G)\backslash\{2r_{1}\}}\sum_{\pm}\theta_{\pm}F_{\theta_{\pm}}\right)+\sum_{\mu\in\mathrm{Spec}_{Q}(H)}(n_{1}-1+\mu)F_{\mu}+\sum_{\pm}r_{\pm}F_{r_{\pm}}.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mu
is a signless Laplacian eigenvalue of
H
, define
B_{\mu}
as the orthonormal basis corresponding to the eigenspace of
\mu
, of which orthogonal to
\mathbf{j}_{n_{2}}
. It’s important to note that

is always empty whenever

is connected. And if

is a disconnected graph, there are other eigenvectors, orthogonal to

, of

corresponding to
2r_{2}
. Assume that

has multiplicity

is

, and
m
otherwise. Note that the signless Laplacian matrix

is the following:

Q(G\tilde{\circ}{H})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}Q(G)+(n_{1}-1)n_{2}I_{n_{1}}&M\otimes\mathbf{j}_{n_{2}}^{T}\\
M^{T}\otimes\mathbf{j}_{n_{2}}&I_{n_{1}}\otimes(Q(H)+(n_{1}-1)I_{n_{2}})\end{array}\right)

with
M=J_{n_{1}}-I_{n_{1}}
. From this fact, we can get that, for each
\mathbf{x}\in B_{\mu}

Q(G\tilde{\circ}H)\mathbf{e}_{u}\otimes\left[{\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
\mathbf{x}\end{array}}\right]=(n_{1}-1+\mu)\mathbf{e}_{u}\otimes\left[{\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
\mathbf{x}\end{array}}\right].

It follows that the orthonormal basis corresponding to the eigenspace of

\left\{\mathbf{e}_{u}\otimes\left[\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
\mathbf{x}\end{array}\right]:u\in V(G),\;\mathbf{x}\in B_{\mu}\right\}.

From the above argument, we find that the cardinality of this orthonormal basis is
n_{1}(m-1)
when
\mu=2r_{2}
, and
n_{1}m
otherwise. It is apparent that the signless Laplacian eigenprojector of

F_{\mu}(H)=\begin{cases}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in B_{\mu}}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{x}^{T}+\frac{1}{n}_{2}J_{n_{2}}&\mathrm{if~}\mu=2r_{2},\\
\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in B_{\mu}}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{x}^{T}&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

On the basis of this fact, we find that

is a signless Laplacian eigenvales of

In the next step, we take
\theta_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(\theta+s+t\pm\sqrt{(\theta-s+t))^{2}+4n_{2}})

2r_{1}
of

Q(G)\mathbf{z}=2r_{1}\mathbf{z}

\left.F_{\theta_{\pm}}(G\tilde{\circ}{H})=F_{\theta}(G)\otimes\frac{1}{(s-\theta_{\pm})^{2}+n_{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}(s-\theta_{\pm})^{2}&(s-\theta_{\pm})\mathbf{j}_{n_{2}}^{T}\\

and

\displaystyle F_{r_{\pm}}(G\tilde{\circ}{H})=F_{2r_{1}}(G)\otimes\frac{1}{(s-r_{\pm})^{2}+n_{2}(1-n_{1})^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}(s-r_{\pm})^{2}&(s-r_{\pm})(1-n_{1})\mathbf{j}_{n_{2}}^{T}\\

Finally, the signless Laplacian spectral decomposition of
G\tilde{\circ}{H}
can be obtained directly from what has been discussed above. This concludes our proof.

\sum_{\pm}e^{\mp\mathrm{i}\tau\frac{\Lambda_{\theta}}{2}}\frac{(s-\theta_{\pm})^{2}}{(s-\theta_{\pm})^{2}+n_{2}}=\cos\left(\frac{\Lambda_{\theta}\tau}{2}\right)-\mathrm{i}\frac{\theta-s+t}{\Lambda_{\theta}}\sin\left(\frac{\Lambda_{\theta}\tau}{2}\right)

Finally, plugging these formulas into (

) follows.

4
Signless-Laplacian-PST

Theorem 2.1 indicates the relationship between periodic vertices and PST, that is, for PST to be possible, the two vertices must be periodic. From this fact, we shall attempt to find whether there exists signless-Laplacian-PST on vertex complemented coronae through periodicity of vertices.

Lemma 4.1.

Suppose that the graphs
G
and
H
are as stated in Theorem 3.1. Then, for
u\in V(G)
and
v\in V(H)
,
(u,0)
is periodic on
G\tilde{\circ}{H}
whenever
(u,v)
is periodic on
G\tilde{\circ}{H}
.

Proof.

The eigenprojectors provided in Theorem 3.1 implies that
\mathrm{supp}_{G\tilde{\circ}H}(u,0)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}_{G\tilde{\circ}H}(u,v)
, which concludes this result.
∎

Next, we demonstrate that periodicity allows us to impose tight constraints on signless Laplacian eigenvalues. What below states some sufficient conditions to have periodic vertices. We further find that most vertex complemented coronae avoid signless-Laplacian-PST by proving the existence of periodic vertices.

Theorem 4.2.

Suppose that the graphs
G
and
H
are as stated in Theorem 3.1.

(i)

If

, then
(v,0)
is periodic in

if, and only if
\theta-s+t
,
\sqrt{(\theta-s+t)^{2}+4n_{2}}

divides
n_{2}
at this point.

Proof.

As a result of Theorem 3.1, the eigenvalue support of vertex

in

has the following form:

\mathrm{supp}_{G\tilde{\circ}H}(v,0)=\{\theta_{\pm}:\theta\in\mathrm{supp}_{G}(v)\}.

Notice that

First we prove (i). Let

is periodic in

according to Theorem 2.2. Conversely, suppose
(v,0)
is a periodic vertex in
G\tilde{\circ}{H}

(a)

Each

\theta_{+}-\theta_{-}=\sqrt{(\theta-s+t)^{2}+4n_{2}}

These conclude that the aforementioned equations also have integers on their right-hand side..

(b)

is an irrational number, thus either

or

. First assume that

In what follows, we will prove (ii). Let

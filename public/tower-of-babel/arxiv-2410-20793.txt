Maneuvering measurement-coherence into measurement-entanglement
Ho-Joon Kim, Soojoon Lee

Abstract
Quantum dynamics governs the transformation of static quantum resources, such as coherence and entanglement, in both quantum states and measurements. Prior studies have established that a quantum channel’s state-cohering power can be converted into the state-entangling power without additional coherence. Here, we complete this coherence-to-entanglement paradigm by demonstrating that a channel’s measurement-cohering power can likewise be converted into the measurement-entangling power. This result reinforces, on the dynamical level, the intuition that entanglement emerges as a manifestation of coherence. To formalize this picture, we develop resource theories for measurement-cohering and measurement-entangling powers and characterize the structure of incoherent measurements to analyze measurement-coherence generation. Furthermore, we show that the state-cohering power of a quantum channel is equivalent to the measurement-cohering power of its adjoint map, and a corresponding equivalence also exists between the state-entangling power and the measurement-entangling power.

However, the dynamical landscape for measurement resources has remained largely unexplored. In particular, it is not known whether measurement-cohering power—the ability of a channel to generate measurement-coherence through pre-processing—can be operationally related to measurement-entangling power, or whether these two quantities obey a dynamical correspondence analogous to the one established for states. Establishing such a relationship is essential for completing the resource-theoretic picture linking coherence and entanglement across both static and dynamical settings.

In this work, we develop resource theories for quantum channels that quantify their ability to generate measurement-coherence and measurement-entanglement, and we show that these two forms of dynamical resources are fundamentally connected. We first demonstrate that the measurement-cohering power of any quantum channel can, without introducing any additional coherence, be fully converted into measurement-entangling power by means of a fixed construction involving only free operations and the final dephasing step. This establishes a genuine dynamical analogue of coherence-to-entanglement conversion on the measurement side.

A useful ingredient in our analysis is a structural characterization of the incoherent measurements: we show that every incoherent POVM can be reduced to a standard incoherent basis measurement followed solely by classical post-processing. This observation allows us to treat incoherent measurements as canonical objects and provides the foundation for analyzing how quantum channels generate measurement-resources.

Finally, we uncover a duality between state-based and measurement-based dynamical resource powers. For unital channels, the state-cohering power of the channel turns out to coincide with the measurement-cohering power of its adjoint map, and an analogous equivalence emerges between state-entangling and measurement-entangling powers. Taken together, these results complete the conceptual framework connecting coherence and entanglement across both static and dynamical settings, while highlighting a deeper symmetry between state- and measurement-based quantum resources and the unifying role played by quantum dynamics.

Measurement-coherence and measurement-entanglement

In the resource theory of quantum coherence
[
5
]
, quantum coherence of an operator is quantified with respect to a fixed orthonormal basis, which is referred to as an incoherent basis. The choice of incoherent basis is contingent upon the experimental or physical constraints imposed on the system under consideration. We set an incoherent basis of a system
A

\mathcal{I}_{A}=\{\lvert i\rangle_{A}:i=0,\dots,d-1\}

O_{A}

\Delta_{A}(\rho_{A})=\rho_{A}

n

\mathcal{M}_{A}=\{M_{x}:\sum_{x=0}^{n-1}M_{x}=I_{A},M_{x}\geq 0\;\forall x=0,\ldots,n-1\}

I_{A}
is the identity operator. A quantum measurement

can be equivalently described by a quantum channel with classical outputs, as

\mathcal{M}_{A}(\rho_{A})=\sum_{x=0}^{n-1}\operatorname{Tr}_{A}(M_{x}\rho_{A})\left|x\right\rangle\!\left\langle x\right|_{A^{\prime}},

(1)

where

3
Measurement-resource powers of quantum dynamics

Now we focus on the capacity of quantum dynamics to generate measurement-resources. Quantum channels are capable of generating measurement-coherence and measurement-entanglement by acting as a pre-processing channel prior to a quantum measurement. In contrast, classical post-processing after a quantum measurement is unable to generate any measurement-resources
[
37
]
.

As previously introduced,
\mathbf{DIO}
is the largest set of quantum channels under which incoherent measurements remain incoherent. In light of this, we define the measurement-cohering power of a quantum channel
\mathcal{E}_{A}
as follows:

\displaystyle C(\mathcal{E}_{A})\coloneqq

\displaystyle\min_{\mathcal{F}_{A}\in\mathbf{DIO}}\max_{\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}}\in\mathbf{IM}}

\displaystyle D_{m}(\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{A}\|\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}}\circ\mathcal{F}_{A}),

(8)

where the ancillary system
\widetilde{A}
can be arbitrary. The definition in Eq. (
8
) may appear formal, but it has a direct physical interpretation.
The optimization over all incoherent measurements is unnecessary; the maximizing measurement is always the canonical incoherent basis measurement
\mathcal{I}_{A}
.
Intuitively, the measurement-cohering power captures how much the quantum channel
\mathcal{E}_{A}
“twists” the classical axes of the standard incoherent measurement
\mathcal{I}_{A}
into a superposition, thereby generating coherence in the measurement process.
This leads to the following operational simplification:

Theorem 2
.

For a quantum channel
\mathcal{E}_{A}
, the measurement-cohering power of the quantum channel is given by

C(\mathcal{E}_{A})=C_{m}(\mathcal{I}_{A}\circ\mathcal{E}_{A}),

(9)

where
\mathcal{I}_{A}=\{\left|i\right\rangle\!\left\langle i\right|_{A}:i=0,\dots,d-1\}
is the incoherent-basis measurement.

Theorem
2
ensures that the generalized Fourier transform corresponds to a quantum channel that has the maximum measurement-cohering power as in the case for the state-cohering power
[
46
]
since the generalized Fourier transform turns the incoherent basis measurement into a measurement with maximum measurement-coherence.

Furthermore, Theorem
2
establishes a direct pathway for experimental verification of the measurement-cohering power.
Since the measurement-cohering power of a channel
\mathcal{E}
is equivalent to the measurement-coherence of the effective measurement
\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{E}
, it opens the possibility of quantifying it using well-established techniques for characterizing static coherence.
For instance, quantitative witnesses for coherence
[
53
,
54
]
, although originally developed for quantum states, might be adapted to quantify measurement-coherence, leveraging the fact that POVM elements are positive operators akin to unnormalized quantum states.
This bridges our dynamical framework with existing experimental protocols for static resources.

Similarly, we can quantify the capability of a channel to generate measurement-entanglement.
By identifying the closure of LOCC channels (
\overline{\mathbf{LOCC}}
) as the set of free resources, we define the measurement-entangling power of a quantum channel
\mathcal{E}_{AB}
as:

E(\mathcal{E}_{AB})\coloneqq\min_{\mathcal{F}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCC}}(A:B)}\max_{\mathcal{M}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A\widetilde{A}:B\widetilde{B})}\\
D_{m}(\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{AB}\|\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}\circ\mathcal{F}_{AB}),

(10)

where the ancillary systems
\widetilde{A}
and
\widetilde{B}
can be arbitrary. In the definition of the measurement-entangling power, the ancillary systems
\widetilde{A}
and
\widetilde{B}
cannot be omitted, in contrast to Theorem
2
for the measurement-cohering power, because they play essential operational roles. Without these ancillary systems, the channel that attains the maximum measurement-entangling power would be the generalized CNOT gate. However, once ancillary systems are allowed, the generalized SWAP channel achieves the maximum measurement-entangling power, as it converts locally implemented Bell measurements into measurements with maximum measurement-entanglement, paralleling the situation for state-entangling power
[
55
]
.

The measurement-resource powers in Eq. (
8

[
56

: i) the measurement-cohering power
C(\mathcal{E}_{A})
is non-negative and faithful, that is,
C(\mathcal{E}_{A})
is zero if and only if
\mathcal{E}_{A}
is in
\mathbf{DIO}

\mathbf{DIO}

\mathcal{K}_{A}
and a unital
\mathbf{DIO}

\mathcal{L}_{A}
such that
C(\mathcal{K}_{A}\circ\mathcal{E}_{A}\circ\mathcal{L}_{A})\leq C(\mathcal{E}_{A})

C(p\mathcal{E}_{A}+(1-p)\mathcal{G}_{A})\leq pC(\mathcal{E}_{A})+(1-p)C(\mathcal{G}_{A})
for quantum channels

and
\mathcal{G}_{A}

0\leq p\leq 1

Similarly, the measurement-entangling power

also satisfies the three properties: i)
E(\mathcal{E}_{AB})\geq 0

\mathcal{E}_{AB}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCC}}(A\!:\!B)

is monotone under the composition by a
\overline{\mathbf{LOCC}}

\mathcal{K}_{AB}
and a unital

\mathcal{L}_{AB}

E(\mathcal{K}_{AB}\circ\mathcal{E}_{AB}\circ\mathcal{L}_{AB})\leq E(\mathcal{E}_{AB})

E(p\mathcal{E}_{AB}+(1-p)\mathcal{G}_{AB})\leq pE(\mathcal{E}_{AB})+(1-p)E(\mathcal{G}_{AB})

5
State-resource power versus measurement-resource power

A quantum channel
\mathcal{E}_{A}
can generate static quantum resources in both quantum states and measurements. The state-cohering power of
\mathcal{E}_{A}
, quantifying the maximum coherence the channel can induce in incoherent states, is given by

C_{g}(\mathcal{E}_{A})\coloneqq\max_{\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}}\in\mathbf{I}}C_{R}(\mathcal{E}_{A}(\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}})),

(15)

where
C_{R}(\rho_{A})\coloneqq\min_{\sigma_{A}\in\mathbf{I}}D(\rho_{A}\|\sigma_{A})
is the relative entropy of coherence
[
3
]
, and
\mathbf{I}
denotes the set of incoherent states. The measurement-cohering power of a channel
\mathcal{E}_{A}
is given in Eq. (
8

Are there relations between the two static resource powers of a quantum channel? In general, there is no relation between a channel’s state-resource power and its measurement-resource power. Consider a qubit preparation channel
\mathcal{E}_{A}(\rho_{A})=\operatorname{Tr}_{A}(\rho_{A})\left|+\right\rangle\!\left\langle+\right|_{A}
with
\lvert+\rangle_{A}=(\lvert 0\rangle_{A}+\lvert 1\rangle_{A})/\sqrt{2}
. While
\mathcal{E}_{A}
has the maximum state-cohering power (
C_{g}(\mathcal{E}_{A})=1
), it has zero measurement-cohering power (
C(\mathcal{E}_{A})=0
) because the effective measurement
\mathcal{I}_{A}\circ\mathcal{E}_{A}
yields a trivial measurement whose POVM element is
I/2
. Another quantum channel
\mathcal{G}_{A}(\rho_{A})=\langle+\rvert\rho_{A}\lvert+\rangle_{A}\left|0\right\rangle\!\left\langle 0\right|_{A}+\langle-\rvert\rho_{A}\lvert-\rangle_{A}\left|1\right\rangle\!\left\langle 1\right|_{A}
shows the opposite case, where it can generate no state-coherence as
C_{g}(\mathcal{G}_{A})=0
while it can generate the maximum measurement-coherence
C(\mathcal{G}_{A})=1
. Meanwhile, the Hadamard gate possesses the maximum state-cohering power (converting incoherent basis states into maximally coherent states) and also the maximum measurement-cohering power (converting the incoherent measurement
\mathcal{I}
into a measurement in the Fourier basis). However, for unital quantum channels, we establish a connection between the state-cohering power and the measurement-cohering power:

Theorem 7
.

For a unital quantum channel
\mathcal{E}_{A}
, the state-cohering power
C_{g}(\mathcal{E}_{A})
and the measurement-cohering power
C(\mathcal{E}_{A}^{{\dagger}})
of its adjoint channel
\mathcal{E}_{A}^{{\dagger}}
satisfy the following:

\dfrac{1}{d}C_{g}(\mathcal{E}_{A})\leq C(\mathcal{E}_{A}^{{\dagger}})\leq C_{g}(\mathcal{E}_{A}).

(16)

This result implies that for a unital quantum channel, the state-cohering power of the channel and the measurement-cohering power of its adjoint channel are equivalent in the sense that one is non-zero if and only if the other is non-zero.

Conclusion

We have developed resource theories for quantifying measurement-cohering and measurement-entangling powers of quantum channels, thereby extending the landscape of dynamical quantum resources to include measurement-based phenomena. Our results establish a dynamical correspondence between coherence and entanglement on the measurement side: the measurement-cohering power of any quantum channel can be fully converted into its measurement-entangling power using only free operations and a dephasing step. This conversion theorem completes the coherence–entanglement correspondence at the dynamical level and mirrors the known relationship between state-cohering and state-entangling powers, as illustrated in Table
1
.

A useful ingredient enabling these results is our structural characterization of incoherent measurements, showing that every incoherent POVM reduces to the incoherent basis measurement followed by classical post-processing. This structural insight allows measurement channels to be treated analogously to state preparations, permitting a unified treatment of their dynamical generation under quantum channels. Our framework also reproduces the static results of measurement-coherence and measurement-entanglement when a measurement is regarded as a channel with classical outputs.

Furthermore, we have shown that for unital channels, dynamical resource powers for states and measurements are tightly linked: the state-cohering power of a channel equals the measurement-cohering power of its adjoint map, and an analogous equivalence holds for state-entangling versus measurement-entangling powers. These dualities highlight a fundamental symmetry between state- and measurement-based quantum resources, suggesting that both arise from a single underlying structure governed by the dynamical action of quantum channels.

Despite these advances, several fundamental questions remain open. Although coherence-to-entanglement conversion is now understood in both static and dynamical regimes, it remains unclear whether all forms of entanglement can be obtained from coherence. Determining when, and to what extent, entanglement can be generated from coherence in a resource-theoretically consistent manner is an intriguing challenge. Moreover, the relationship between coherence and multipartite entanglement is still insufficiently explored
[
19
]
, reflecting broader gaps in our understanding of multipartite entanglement itself.

Another important point is that static resource generation alone cannot capture the full complexity of quantum dynamics concerning resource utilization. Indeed, quantum dynamics itself can be viewed as a resource under appropriate restrictions on quantum superchannels
[
60
,
61
,
62
,
63
,
64
]
. Recent works have shown that gates such as generalized Hadamard or SWAP can serve as fundamental dynamical resource units
[
46
,
48
,
45
,
63
,
64
]
, analogous to maximally coherent states or maximally entangled states in static resource theories. However, the connections among these dynamical quantum resources remain less understood compared to their static counterparts. Clarifying the relationship between dynamical coherence and dynamical entanglement resources may also prove valuable for quantum technologies, where the ability to manipulate channel-level resources underlies tasks such as fault-tolerant gate synthesis and quantum simulation. A unified understanding of these dynamical resources could help identify operations that are optimal or universal for emerging quantum devices.

Overall, our results place measurement-based dynamical resources on equal footing with their state-based counterparts and provide a unified theoretical foundation linking coherence and entanglement across static and dynamical levels. We expect that this framework will inform future advances in the broader development of resource-efficient quantum technologies, such as quantum learning and quantum simulation.

B.1

The free static resource for measurement coherence is the set of incoherent measurements
\mathbf{IM}

\mathbf{IM}

\widetilde{A}

The following lemma demonstrates that the optimizations in the definition of the measurement-cohering power are unnecessary:

Lemma 4

\mathcal{E}_{A}

\mathcal{F}_{A}

\displaystyle=C_{m}(\mathcal{I}_{A^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{N}_{A})

(129)

\displaystyle=C_{m}(\mathcal{N}_{A}).

(130)

Similarly, the measurement-entangling power
E(\mathcal{N}_{AB})
of a measurement channel
\mathcal{N}_{AB}
reduces to the measurement-entanglement
E_{m}(\mathcal{N}_{AB})
of a measurement channel
\mathcal{N}_{AB}
. We first prove that
E(\mathcal{N}_{AB})\leq E_{m}(\mathcal{N}_{AB})
:

\displaystyle E(\mathcal{N}_{AB})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\min_{\mathcal{F}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCC}}(A:B)}\max_{\mathcal{M}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A\widetilde{A}:B\widetilde{B})}

(131)

\displaystyle\qquad D_{m}(\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{A}}\circ\mathcal{N}_{AB}\|\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}\circ\mathcal{F}_{AB})

(132)

\displaystyle\leq\min_{\mathcal{F}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A:B)}\max_{\mathcal{M}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A\widetilde{A}:B\widetilde{B})}

(133)

\displaystyle\qquad D_{m}(\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{A}}\circ\mathcal{N}_{AB}\|\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}\circ\mathcal{F}_{AB})

(134)

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathcal{F}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A:B)}\max_{\mathcal{M}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A\widetilde{A}:B\widetilde{B})}

(135)

\displaystyle\qquad D_{m}(\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}\circ\Delta_{AB}\circ\mathcal{N}_{AB}\|\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}\circ\Delta_{AB}\circ\mathcal{F}_{AB})

(136)

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathcal{F}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A:B)}\max_{\mathcal{M}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A:B)}

(137)

\displaystyle\qquad D_{m}(\mathcal{M}_{AB}\circ\Delta_{AB}\circ\mathcal{N}_{AB}\|\mathcal{M}_{AB}\circ\Delta_{AB}\circ\mathcal{F}_{AB})

(138)

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathcal{F}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A:B)}\max_{\mathcal{S}_{AB}\in\mathbf{S}}

(139)

\displaystyle\qquad D_{m}(\mathcal{S}_{AB}\circ\Delta_{AB}\circ\mathcal{N}_{AB}\|\mathcal{S}_{AB}\circ\Delta_{AB}\circ\mathcal{F}_{AB})

(140)

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathcal{F}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A:B)}D_{m}(\mathcal{N}_{AB}\|\mathcal{F}_{AB})

(141)

\displaystyle=E_{m}(\mathcal{N}_{AB}),

(142)

where the third equality follows from Lemma
5
and the fact that measurement channels output incoherent states. The fourth equality holds from Corollary
10
. The fifth equality follows from the monotonicity of
D_{m}
under classical post-processing channel.

The opposite direction is proved as follows:

\displaystyle E(\mathcal{N}_{AB})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\min_{\mathcal{F}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCC}}(A:B)}\max_{\mathcal{M}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A\widetilde{A}:B\widetilde{B})}

(143)

\displaystyle\qquad D_{m}(\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{A}}\circ\mathcal{N}_{AB}\|\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}\circ\mathcal{F}_{AB})

(144)

\displaystyle\geq\min_{\mathcal{F}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCC}}(A:B)}\max_{\mathcal{M}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A\widetilde{A}:B\widetilde{B})}

(145)

\displaystyle\qquad D_{m}(\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}\circ\Delta_{AB}\circ\mathcal{N}_{AB}\|\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}\circ\Delta_{AB}\circ\mathcal{F}_{AB})

(146)

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathcal{F}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCC}}(A:B)}\max_{\mathcal{M}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A:B)}

(147)

\displaystyle\qquad D_{m}(\mathcal{M}_{AB}\circ\Delta_{AB}\circ\mathcal{N}_{AB}\|\mathcal{M}_{AB}\circ\Delta_{AB}\circ\mathcal{F}_{AB})

(148)

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathcal{F}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A:B)}\max_{\mathcal{M}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A:B)}

(149)

\displaystyle\qquad D_{m}(\mathcal{M}_{AB}\circ\Delta_{AB}\circ\mathcal{N}_{AB}\|\mathcal{M}_{AB}\circ\Delta_{AB}\circ\mathcal{F}_{AB})

(150)

\displaystyle=E_{m}(\mathcal{N}_{AB}),

(151)

where the first inequality holds due to
\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}\circ\Delta_{AB}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A\widetilde{A}\!:\!B\widetilde{B})
, the second equality follows from Lemma
5
. The third equality holds because of
\Delta_{AB}\circ\mathcal{F}_{AB}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A\!:\!B)
.

C.3
Relation between state-cohering power and measurement-cohering power

Here we investigate the relation between a quantum channel’s state-resource and measurement-resource powers. We begin by defining the state-cohering power of a quantum channel
\mathcal{E}_{A}
as

C_{g}(\mathcal{E}_{A})\coloneqq\max_{\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}}\in\textbf{I}}C_{R}(\mathcal{E}_{A}(\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}})),

(152)

where
C_{R}(\rho_{A})\coloneqq\min_{\sigma_{A}\in\mathbf{I}}D(\rho_{A}|\sigma_{A})
is the relative entropy of coherence
[
2
]
, and
\mathbf{I}
is the set of incoherent states. The state-cohering power of a quantum channel
\mathcal{E}_{A}
can be written as

C_{g}(\mathcal{E}_{A})=\max_{\left|i\right\rangle\!\left\langle i\right|_{A}\in\textbf{I}}C_{R}(\mathcal{E}_{A}(\left|i\right\rangle\!\left\langle i\right|_{A})).

(153)

This can be shown as follows:

\displaystyle C_{g}(\mathcal{E}_{A})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\max_{\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}}\in\textbf{I}}C_{R}(\mathcal{E}_{A}(\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}}))

(154)

\displaystyle=C_{R}(\mathcal{E}_{A}(\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}}^{\ast}))

(155)

\displaystyle=C_{R}\left(\mathcal{E}_{A}\left(\sum_{i,j}p_{ij}\left|ij\right\rangle\!\left\langle ij\right|_{A\widetilde{A}}\right)\right)

(156)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i,j}p_{ij}C_{R}(\mathcal{E}_{A}(\left|ij\right\rangle\!\left\langle ij\right|_{A\widetilde{A}}))

(157)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\left|i\right\rangle\!\left\langle i\right|_{A}\in\textbf{I}}C_{R}(\mathcal{E}_{A}(\left|i\right\rangle\!\left\langle i\right|_{A})),

(158)

where
\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}}^{\ast}
is an optimal argument in the definition of
C_{g}(\mathcal{E}_{A})
.
The other direction
C_{g}(\mathcal{E}_{A})\geq\max_{\left|i\right\rangle\!\left\langle i\right|_{A}\in\textbf{I}}C_{R}(\mathcal{E}_{A}(\left|i\right\rangle\!\left\langle i\right|_{A}))
holds because the domain of the maximization in the definition of
C_{g}
includes the incoherent basis.

In general, there is no relation between a quantum channel’s state-cohering power and measurement-cohering power. This can be seen by examining some quantum channels. Consider the following preparation channel:

\mathcal{E}_{A}(\rho_{A})=\langle 0\rvert\rho_{A}\lvert 0\rangle_{A}\left|+\right\rangle\!\left\langle+\right|_{A}+\langle 1\rvert\rho_{A}\lvert 1\rangle_{A}\left|+\right\rangle\!\left\langle+\right|_{A}=\left|+\right\rangle\!\left\langle+\right|_{A}.

(159)

The quantum channel
\mathcal{E}_{A}
has maximum state-cohering power as

C_{g}(\mathcal{E}_{A})=\max_{\sigma_{A}\in\text{I}}C_{R}(\mathcal{E}_{A}(\rho)_{A})=1,

(160)

while its measurement-cohering power is zero as

C(\mathcal{E}_{A})=C_{m}(\mathcal{I}_{A}\circ\mathcal{E}_{A})=0,

(161)

because
\mathcal{I}_{A}\circ\mathcal{E}_{A}=\{\frac{I_{A}}{2},\frac{I_{A}}{2}\}
.

Similarly, one can conceive a quantum channel
\mathcal{G}_{A}
such that

\mathcal{G}_{A}(\rho_{A})=\langle+\rvert\rho_{A}\lvert+\rangle_{A}\left|0\right\rangle\!\left\langle 0\right|_{A}+\langle-\rvert\rho_{A}\lvert-\rangle_{A}\left|1\right\rangle\!\left\langle 1\right|_{A}.

(162)

The quantum channel
\mathcal{G}_{A}
does not generate any state-coherence while it can generate measurement-coherence as

\mathcal{I}_{A}\circ\mathcal{G}_{A}=\{\left|+\right\rangle\!\left\langle+\right|_{A},\left|-\right\rangle\!\left\langle-\right|_{A}\}.

(163)

Thus, it is not possible to assert a relation between the state-cohering power and measurement-cohering power of a quantum channel. However, it can be observed that a unital quantum channel is related to its adjoint channel in terms of static resource powers as follows:

Theorem 18
.

For a unital quantum channel
\mathcal{E}_{A}
, the state-cohering power of
\mathcal{E}_{A}
and measurement-cohering power of its adjoint channel
\mathcal{E}_{A}^{{\dagger}}
satisfy the following relation:

\dfrac{1}{d}C_{g}(\mathcal{E}_{A})\leq C(\mathcal{E}_{A}^{{\dagger}})\leq C_{g}(\mathcal{E}_{A}).

(164)

Proof.

It holds that

\displaystyle C(\mathcal{E}_{A}^{{\dagger}})

\displaystyle=C_{m}(\mathcal{I}_{A}\circ\mathcal{E}_{A}^{{\dagger}})

(165)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}C_{R}(\mathcal{E}_{A}(\left|i\right\rangle\!\left\langle i\right|_{A}))

(166)

\displaystyle\leq C_{g}(\mathcal{E}_{A}),

(167)

where the inequality follows from Eq. (
153
). The lower bound follows from Eq. (
153
) and the non-negativity of
C_{R}
.
∎

This shows the equivalence of the state-cohering power of a unital channel and the measurement-cohering power of its adjoint channel in the sense that a unital quantum channel having no state-cohering power can neither generate measurement-coherence, nor vice versa.

State-entangling power of a quantum channel
\mathcal{E}_{AB}
can be defined as

E_{g}(\mathcal{E}_{AB})=\max_{\sigma\in\mathbf{SepD}(A\widetilde{A}:B\widetilde{B})}E_{R}(\mathcal{E}_{AB}(\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}})).

(168)

The ancillary system in the definition is necessary to capture the state-entangling power of a quantum channel in a situation where the quantum channel acts on subsystems of a large system. Typical example is the SWAP gate on system
AB
. It does not generate entanglement from
\sigma_{AB}\in\mathbf{SepD}(A\!:\!B)
while it can generate the maximum entanglement from
\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}\in\mathbf{SepD}(A\widetilde{A}\!:\!B\widetilde{B})

[
44
]
.

A quantum channel’s state-entangling power is equivalent to its adjoint channel’s measurement-entangling power in the following sense:

Theorem 19
.

For a unital quantum channel
\mathcal{E}_{AB}
, the state-entangling power of
\mathcal{E}_{AB}
and the measurement-entangling power of its adjoint channel
\mathcal{E}_{AB}^{{\dagger}}
satisfy the following relation:

\dfrac{1}{d^{4}}E_{g}(\mathcal{E}_{AB})\leq E(\mathcal{E}_{AB}^{{\dagger}})\leq E_{g}(\mathcal{E}_{AB}).

(169)

Proof.

We first show the upper bound on
E(\mathcal{E}^{\dagger})
. Let
\overline{\mathbf{ULOCC}}(A\!:\!B)=\{\mathcal{F}_{AB}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCC}}(A\!:\!B):\mathcal{F}_{AB}(I_{AB})=I_{AB}\}
be the set of unital
\overline{\mathbf{LOCC}}
channels. The following holds that:

\displaystyle E(\mathcal{E}_{AB}^{{\dagger}})

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathcal{F}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCC}}(A:B)}\max_{\mathcal{M}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A\widetilde{A}:B\widetilde{B})}D_{m}(M_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{AB}^{{\dagger}}\|\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}\circ\mathcal{F}_{AB})

(170)

\displaystyle\leq\min_{\mathcal{F}\in\overline{\mathbf{ULOCC}}(A:B)}\max_{\mathcal{M}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A\widetilde{A}:B\widetilde{B})}D_{m}(M_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{AB}^{{\dagger}}\|\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}\circ\mathcal{F}_{AB})

(171)

\displaystyle=D_{m}(\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}^{\ast}\circ\mathcal{E}_{AB}^{{\dagger}}\|\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}^{\ast}\circ\mathcal{F}_{AB}^{\ast})

(172)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{d^{4}}\sum_{x,y}D(\mathcal{E}_{AB}(M_{xy}^{\ast})\|\mathcal{F}_{AB}^{\ast{\dagger}}(M_{xy}^{\ast}))

(173)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{x,y}D\left(\mathcal{E}_{AB}\left(\dfrac{M_{xy}^{\ast}}{d^{4}}\right)\bigg\|\mathcal{F}_{AB}^{\ast{\dagger}}\left(\dfrac{M_{xy}^{\ast}}{d^{4}}\right)\right)

(174)

\displaystyle=D(\mathcal{E}_{AB}(M_{0}^{\ast})\|\mathcal{F}_{AB}^{\ast{\dagger}}(M_{0}^{\ast})).

(175)

Let
M_{0}^{\ast}=q_{0}\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}
, where
q_{0}=\operatorname{Tr}M_{0}^{\ast}\leq 1
and
\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}=M_{0}^{\ast}/q_{0}
is a separable state in
\mathbf{SepD}(A\widetilde{A}\!:\!B\widetilde{B})
. Then it follows that

\displaystyle E(\mathcal{E}_{AB}^{{\dagger}})

\displaystyle\leq D(\mathcal{E}_{AB}(q_{0}\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}})\|\mathcal{F}_{AB}^{\ast{\dagger}}(q_{0}\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}))

(176)

\displaystyle=q_{0}D(\mathcal{E}_{AB}(\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}})\|\mathcal{F}_{AB}^{\ast{\dagger}}(\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}))

(177)

\displaystyle\leq E_{g}(\mathcal{E}_{AB}).

(178)

The lower bound on
E(\mathcal{E}_{AB}^{{\dagger}})
can be derived as follows:

\displaystyle E_{g}(\mathcal{E}_{AB})

\displaystyle=\max_{\sigma\in\mathbf{SepD}(A\widetilde{A}:B\widetilde{B})}E_{R}(\mathcal{E}_{AB}(\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}))

(179)

\displaystyle=\max_{\sigma\in\mathbf{SepD}(A\widetilde{A}:B\widetilde{B})}\min_{\tau\in\mathbf{SepD}(A\widetilde{A}:B\widetilde{B})}D(\mathcal{E}_{AB}(\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}})\|\tau_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}})

(180)

\displaystyle=D(\mathcal{E}_{AB}(\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}^{\ast})\|\tau_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}^{\ast}).

(181)

We construct a measurement
\mathcal{N}_{AB}
in
\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A\widetilde{A}:B\widetilde{B})
that includes
\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}
as one of its POVM elements. Then we observe that

\displaystyle E(\mathcal{E}_{AB}^{{\dagger}})

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathcal{F}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCC}}(A:B)}\max_{\mathcal{M}\in\overline{\mathbf{LOCCM}}(A\widetilde{A}:B\widetilde{B})}D_{m}(\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{AB}^{{\dagger}}\|\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}\circ\mathcal{F}_{AB})

(182)

\displaystyle=D_{m}(\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}^{\ast}\circ\mathcal{E}_{AB}^{{\dagger}}\|\mathcal{M}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}^{\ast}\circ\mathcal{F}_{AB}^{\ast})

(183)

\displaystyle\geq D_{m}(\mathcal{N}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{AB}^{{\dagger}}\|\mathcal{N}_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}\circ\mathcal{F}_{AB}^{\ast})

(184)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{d^{4}}\sum_{x,y}D(\mathcal{E}_{AB}(N_{xy})\|\mathcal{F}_{AB}^{\ast{\dagger}}(N_{xy}))

(185)

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{1}{d^{4}}D(\mathcal{E}_{AB}(\sigma_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}^{\ast})\|\tau_{A\widetilde{A}B\widetilde{B}}^{\ast})

(186)

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{1}{d^{4}}E_{g}(\mathcal{E}_{AB}).

(187)

This completes the proof.
∎

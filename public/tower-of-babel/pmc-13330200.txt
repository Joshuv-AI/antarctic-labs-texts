TRIM21 autoantibodies are associated with blood-brain barrier dysfunction in a subgroup of neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder
Authors: Fumitaka Shimizu, Chihiro Kadono, Masatoshi Yuri, Hirotada Fujita, Yoichi Mizukami, Kenji Watanabe, Nanami Yamanaka, Masayuki Nakamori
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe previously reported that glucose-regulated protein 78 autoantibodies (GRP78 Ab) cause breakdown of the blood-brain barrier (BBB) in neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD) patients. Objective of the present study is to identify novel antibodies associated with the breakdown of the BBB in NMOSD patients negative for anti-GRP78 Ab. Human brain microvascular endothelial cells (BMECs) were incubated with purified NMOSD-IgG from 4 patients. RNA-seq, a high-content imaging, living cell-based antibody binding assay, and mass spectrometry were used to evaluate molecular changes and identify the target protein of BMECs after exposure to NMOSD-IgG. The anti-tripartite motif-containing protein 21 (TRIM21) autoantibody was detected using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay. The clinical data of patients with NMOSD with anti-TRIM21 antibodies were verified. Changes in permeability were evaluated using three in vitro BBB models, including monolayer, spheroid and microfluidic tri-culture models. TRIM21, TROVE2 and SS-B were identified as the target antigens of NMOSD-IgG in GRP78 Ab-negative NMOSD patients using proteomics. NF-κB and tight junctions (claudin-5) in BMECs were identified as important signaling pathways after stimulation with TRIM21 Ab-positive NMOSD-IgG by RNA-seq and high-content imaging at the mRNA and protein levels. TRIM21 Abs from NMOSD patients reacted with TRIM21 in the cytoplasm of BMECs using double immunostaining. Stimulation with some commercial TRIM21 Abs, but not TROVE2 Abs or SS-B Abs, caused the nuclear translocation of NF-κB and an increase in 10- and 150-kDa dextran permeability. Removal of anti-TRIM21 Abs from NMOSD-IgG leads to a significant decrease in the biological effect of NMOSD-IgG on the increased permeability of 10-kDa dextran in BMECs. The positive rate of anti-TRIM21 Abs was 30% (13 of 43) in NMOSD patients, 27% (4 of 15) in myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein-antibody associated disease patients, 0% (0 of 43) in multiple sclerosis patients, 5% (2 of 43) in disease controls, and 0% (0 of 16) in healthy controls. Pooled NMOSD-IgG with anti-TRIM21 Abs negative for anti-GRP78 Abs decreased the barrier function more than HC-IgG using three in vitro BBB tri-culture models. In conclusion, anti-TRIM21 Abs in NMOSD may contribute to the breakdown of the BBB in patients with NMOSD via the activation of NF-κB and reduction in claudin-5 in BMECs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40478-026-02307-w.

Introduction

Neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD) is a chronic inflammation-mediated astrocytopathy, characterized by relapsing acute optic neuritis and transverse myelitis [1]. Detection of specific autoantibodies against aquaporin-4 (AQP4) expressed on astrocyte endofeet has been used as a specific biomarker for the diagnosis of NMOSD worldwide [2, 3]. Although most patients with NMOSD demonstrate a relapsing course and severe disability without any treatment, new biological agents, including satralizumab, monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) against the interleukin-6 receptor (IL-6R), inebilizumab, mAbs against CD19 + B cells, and eculizumab/ravulizumab, mAbs against the C5 component of the complement, have reduced the rate of NMOSD relapse in clinical trials and real-world clinical settings [4, 5].

Several studies have demonstrated that pathogenic anti-AQP4 antibodies (Abs) are produced peripherally by AQP4-specific B cells and plasmablasts, and serum anti-AQP4 Abs pass through the blood-brain barrier (BBB) and bind to AQP4 on the astrocyte endofeet, thus inducing astrocyte death via activation of the classical complement cascade [6, 7]. In addition, IL-6 secreted by astrocytes after the binding of anti-AQP4 antibodies to AQP4 leads to the production of anti-AQP4 Abs, BBB damage, and inflammatory cell recruitment and activation [8–10].

Clinically, patients with NMOSD show an increase in the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)/serum albumin quotient (QAlb) and/or Gd-enhanced lesions on magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) during the acute phase, suggesting disruption of the BBB, which leads to the entry of massive amounts of anti-AQP4 Abs into the CNS [11, 12]. High titers of serum anti-AQP4 Abs in many NMOSD patients even during remission were also observed, indicating the importance of BBB breakdown in the development of NMOSD lesions by anti-AQP4 Abs [13]. In animal models, anti-AQP4 Abs can induce NMOSD with pre-existing T cell-mediated CNS inflammation and BBB disruption [14]. We previously demonstrated that anti-glucose-regulated protein 78 antibodies (anti-GRP78 Abs) can bind to cell surface GRP78 on BBB-endothelial cells and mediate the increased permeability via the activation of NF-κB signaling and the decrease of tight junctions, thus leading to the paracellular infiltration of anti-AQP4 Abs to astrocyte endofeet beyond BBB-endothelial cells [15]. Our previous study also showed that the positivity rate of anti-GRP78 Abs in NMOSD was associated with the longitudinally extensive transverse myelitis (LETM) phenotype and disease severity in each patient, and recent reports have demonstrated the relationship between anti-GRP78 Abs and paraneoplastic NMOSD [16, 17].

In the present study, we investigated novel antibodies other than anti-GRP78 Abs that are linked with BBB disruption in patients with NMOSD. We firstly identified 3 NMOSD-IgG in anti-GRP78 Ab-negative NMOSD patients, which have strong effects on the endothelial activation, and then hypothesized that there are novel antibodies other than anti-GRP78 Abs in these samples, possibly contributing to this effect. Using living cell-based antibody-binding assays based on co-immunoprecipitation and mass spectrometry, we identified anti-tripartite motif-containing protein 21 autoantibodies (anti-TRIM21 Abs) in anti-GRP78 Ab-negative and anti-AQP4 Ab-positive NMOSD patients, possibly contributing to BBB disruption in NMOSD.

Methods

Study protocols approval, registrations, and patient consent

The ethics committees of the Medical Faculties of Yamaguchi University approved this study (IRB#: 2024-016). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Patient IgG samples

We selected four IgGs from patients with AQP4 Ab-positive NMOSD, which causes nuclear translocation of NF-κB in TY10 cells, human brain microvascular endothelial cells (BMECs) immortalized with temperature-sensitive SV40-LTA [16, 18]. One IgG was from sera from NMOSD patients positive for GRP78 Abs, and the other three were from sera from NMOSD patients negative for GRP78 Abs. Four IgGs from the sera of healthy controls (HCs) were used as controls. IgG from the sera was purified using a Melon Gel IgG Spin Purification Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

Living cell-based antibody binding assays and proteomics

Living TY10 cells (10-cm dish/sample) were cultured for 75 min with NMOSD-IgG (n = 4) or HC-IgG (n = 4) (25 µg/ml) in HBS-N at room temperature. After removing the solution and washing samples twice with HBS-N, lysis buffer (Pierce IP lysis buffer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) containing protease inhibitors was added to the cells, which were then collected in a 2 mL tube and allowed to stand on ice for 10 min. After sonication, the samples were centrifuged at 12,000× rpm for 10 min at 4 °C, and the supernatant was collected. Dynabeads Protein G (Thermo Fisher Scientific) were added to the supernatant and incubated at room temperature for 1 h with end-over-end mixing. After the beads were collected using a magnetic block, the supernatant was removed, and the beads were washed four times with the lysis buffer and stored at -20 °C until use. Some of the protein A/G beads were eluted with 2x Laemmli Sample Buffer (Bio-Rad) and subjected to a Western blot analysis.

Digestion/reduction buffer (50 mM ammonium bicarbonate, 0.5 mM calcium chloride, 0.01% sodium dodecyl sulfate [SDS], 6 mM DTT) was added to the obtained beads and incubated for 30 min at 60 °C. Iodoacetamide (final concentration: 18 mM) was added to the mixture, followed by 30 min of incubation at 37 °C in the dark. Trypsin/Lys-C Mix (Promega) was then added to the mixture, which was incubated overnight at 37 °C. After adding trifluoroacetic acid to make the solution acidic, the resulting peptide mixture was desalted and purified using a homemade stage-tip.

Liquid chromatography with mass spectrometry (LC-MS) was performed using an Orbitrap Fusion mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) coupled with an Ultimate 3000 RSLCnano system (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The LC mobile phases were the LC-A (0.1% formic acid/2% acetonitrile) and LC-B (0.1% formic acid/90% acetonitrile) solutions. The desalted peptide mixture was dissolved in LC-A solution with a Pierce Peptide Retention Time Calibration Mixture (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and injected directly into the C18 capillary column (3 μm, 100 μm × 12 cm; Nikkyo Technos). Separation was performed using a linear gradient of 2% B to 15% B over 40 min, 15% B to 45% B over 40 min, 45% B to 90% B over 5 min, 90% B over 5 min, and 90% B to 2% B over 5 min. MS was performed in data-dependent acquisition mode, with full MS survey scans over a mass range of m/z 380–1,500 with detection in the Orbitrap (120 K resolution). In each cycle of the data-dependent acquisition analysis, following each survey scan, the most intense ions above a threshold ion count of 50,000 were selected for the higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD) fragmentation at normalized collision energy of 30%. The number of precursor ions selected for fragmentation was determined by a 3-second cycle time with 20 s of dynamic exclusion. Fragment ion spectra were acquired using the Orbitrap (15 K resolution).

The obtained MS/MS data were analyzed using the Progenesis QI for proteomics software program (Waters) for label-free quantitation of proteins. After the alignment of m/z and retention time features for every chromatographic run, a global peak list containing all detected features was created. The Mascot Generic Format (MGF) file for peptide identification was created from the global peak list with deisotoping and charge deconvolution “ON” and limit fragment ion of 40. Peptide identification was performed using the Mascot software program (Matrix Science Inc.) with criteria for MS tolerance of 10 ppm and MS/MS tolerance of 25 mmu. Two missed trypsin cleavage sites were identified. Carbamidomethylation (Cys) was set as a fixed modification, while acetylation (protein N-term), oxidation (Met), and pyro-Glu (N-term Glu or Gln) were set as variable modifications. The xml file obtained from the search results was imported back to Progenesis. After the features with peptide identification results were standardized with those of the Peptide Retention Time Calibration Mixture, protein-level quantitative values were calculated. Comparative quantification of each protein and identification of proteins that were significantly higher in NMOSD patients were performed using the Excel software program (Microsoft).

Western blot analyses

Protein samples (10 µg) were loaded onto a 10% SDS-PAGE gel and transferred onto PVDF membranes (Amersham, Chalfont, UK). The membranes were incubated with the primary antibodies (Tripartite motif-containing protein 21 (TRIM21) [12108-1-AP], TROVE domain family, member 2 (TROVE2) [ab194004], SS-B [ab75927], and integrin α5, dilution 1:100) in PBS-T with 5% skim milk for an hour, followed by with secondary antibodies (dilution 1:5000) for 1 h. The bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence kit (ImmunoStar® LD; Wako), and the images were captured using ChemiDoc (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA).

Immunohistochemistry of TRIM21, TROVE2, and SS-B

TY10 cells were (1) fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA), (2) blocked overnight in 5% fetal bovine serum (FBS) in PBS, (3) incubated with primary antibodies (TRIM21 [ab4369], TROVE2 [ab194004], and SS-B [ab75927]), and (4) incubated with each secondary antibody (Alexa Fluor 488 anti-rabbit IgG; Thermo Fisher Scientific).

Isolation of the membrane protein of TY10 cells

Lytic proteins from TY10 cells were prepared using RIPA Lysis and Extraction Buffer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The membrane protein of TY10 cells was prepared using a Pierce™ Cell Surface Protein Isolation Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific) according to the manufacturer’s protocol and was then subjected to a Western blot analysis.

Colocalization of NMOSD-IgG and TRIM21 by immunohistochemistry

To detect the binding of NMOSD-IgG/control-IgG to TRIM21 or Early Endosome Marker on TY10 cells, we incubated living TY10 cells with IgG (5 µg/ml) from NMOSD patients (n = 2) or HC (n = 2), and anti-TRIM21 (ab4369) or anti-early endosome antigen1 (EEA1) antibodies (Abcam) as primary antibodies (1 h, room temperature) on the slides. The cells were then fixed with 4% PFA, permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100, and incubated with secondary antibodies (Alexa Fluor 488 anti-human IgG [Thermo Fisher Scientific], Alexa Fluor 568 anti-goat IgG [Thermo Fisher Scientific]) for 1 h. Slides were mounted using ProLong Gold antifade reagent (Life Technologies). Images were captured on an LSM510META confocal microscope operated using the ZEN software program (version 2010; Carl Zeiss).

RNA-seq and pathway analyses

Samples of 500 µg/ml IgGs from NMOSD patients positive for anti-TRIM21 Abs (n = 3) or HCs (n = 4) were incubated with TY10 cells for 12 h. The methods for the RNA-seq and pathway analyses have been previously described [19, 20]. In brief, total RNA from TY10 cells was extracted using the RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen), and complementary DNA (cDNA) libraries were constructed according to the NEBNext Ultra II RNA Library Prep kit (New England Biolabs) and NEBNext Multiplex Oligos for Illumina (New England Biolabs) were constructed [20]. Next, (1) the library fragments were concentrated, (2) index sequences during polymerase chain reaction (PCR) amplification were inserted, (3) the products with AMPure XP beads (Beckman Coulter) were purified, (4) the library quality was confirmed using an Agilent 2200 TapeStation (D1000, Agilent Thermo Fisher), and (5) the libraries were sequenced using an Illumina Next-seq DNA sequencer (Illumina). The data were then trimmed and mapped to the mouse reference genome GRCm38 release-92 using the CLC Genomics Workbench software program ver.8.01 (Qiagen) [19, 20], and the mapped read counts to transcripts per million (TPM) were normalized. P-values with an unpaired Student’s t-test were used to determine the fold-change (FC) by subtracting the average values of the HCs from those of the NMOSD patients for volcano plots. Genes with a p-value < 0.05, and a > 50% change in FC for the ingenuity pathway analysis (IPA) were used to analyze the detected genes (Qiagen).

Immunohistochemistry of tight junction molecules according to high-content imaging assays

The immunohistochemistry methods for tight junction molecules, including claudin-5, occludin, ZO-1, and ZO-2, and high-content imaging systems have been previously described [19]. After MCDB 131 medium incubation for 24 h, cells (5000 cells/well) were maintained in MCDB 131 medium containing IgG (500 µg/ml) from patients with NMOSD with anti-TRIM21 Abs (n = 3) or HCs (n = 3) in 96-well plates for 12 h.

Cells were (1) fixed with 100% ethanol, (2) permeabilized with 1% Triton X-100, (3) blocked overnight in 5% fetal bovine serum (FBS)/0.3% Triton X-100 in PBS, (4) incubated with primary monoclonal antibodies (claudin-5 [34-1600; Thermo Fisher Scientific], occludin [Thermo Fisher Scientific], ZO-1 [61-7300; Thermo Fisher Scientific], and ZO-2 [Thermo Fisher Scientific]), and (5) incubated with each secondary antibody (Alexa Fluor 488 anti-rabbit IgG [Thermo Fisher Scientific]). Images were captured at ×20 magnification using an In Cell Analyzer 2000 (GE Healthcare) and analyzed using the In Cell Analyzer software program (Cytiva). Data are presented as the mean values of three experiments for claudin-5, occludin, ZO-1, and ZO-2.

Effect of anti-TRIM21 Abs on the nuclear translocation of NF-κB and the permeability of 10- or 150-kDa dextran in TY10

Four commercial anti-TRIM21 Abs (TRIM21 Ab1 [ab4369; Abcam], TRIM21 Ab2 [ab91423; Abcam], TRIM21 Ab3 [12108-AP; Proteintech], TRIM21 Ab4 [sc-25351; Santa Cruz]), anti-TROVE2 Ab (ab194004; Abcam), and anti-SS-B Ab (ab75927; Abcam) were used. Control IgGs were mouse or rabbit polyclonal antibodies (sc-2025 or sc-2027; Santa Cruz). Cultured cells were incubated with these Abs or IgGs (TRIM21 Ab1, TRIM21 Ab3, TRIM21 Ab4, TROVE2 Ab, SS-B Ab, anti-mouse or rabbit Abs: 6.3, 12, 25, and 50 µg/ml; or TRIM21 Ab2: 2.2, 4.4, 9, and 17 µg/ml) for 1 h and fixed with 4% PFA for NF-κB. Living cells were maintained with these Abs or IgGs (TRIM21 Ab1, TRIM21 Ab3, TRIM21 Ab4, TROVE2 Ab, SS-B Ab; anti-mouse or rabbit Abs: 50 µg/ml or TRIM21 Ab2: 17 µg/ml) for 24 h, and the permeability of 10- or 150-kDa dextran was evaluated. For NF-κB staining, cells were (1) fixed with 4% PFA, (2) permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100, (3) blocked overnight in 5% FBS/0.3% Triton X-100 in PBS, (4) incubated with primary antibodies (NF-κB p65 [Cell Signal Technology]), and (5) incubated with secondary antibodies (Alexa Fluor 488 anti-rabbit IgG [Thermo Fisher Scientific]). Cells treated with TNF-α (1000 U/ml)/IFN-γ (500 U/ml) were used as positive controls.

Removal of anti-TRIM21 abs from pooled NMOSD-IgG

NMOSD-IgG (n = 1) or HC-IgG (n = 1) (250 µg/mL) was incubated with 2 µg of GST-tagged TRIM21 protein (H00006737-P01; Abnova) for 1 h at room temperature for immunoprecipitation, and then incubated with 7 µl of anti-GST-IgG coupling resin (EZview™ Red Glutathione Affinity Gel; E6406; Sigma-Aldrich) for 1 h at room temperature. Immune complexes were pulled down, and the supernatants were used for analyses. The experiment was performed four times, and the statistical significance of the differences in the permeability of 10k-Da dextran was analyzed using a paired t-test. Pooled NMOSD-IgG (n = 4) and HC-IgG (n = 4) served as controls.

Paracellular permeability across the BBB-endothelial cells

TY10 cells were maintained on the luminal side of 24-well collagen-coated Transwell culture inserts (0.4-mm pore; Corning) for 5 days (3 days at 33 °C and 2 days at 37 °C). After the cells were cultured for 24 h with 500 µg/ml of individual IgG from NMOSD patients (n = 4) or HCs (n = 4), FITC-labeled 10-kDa dextran (1 mg/ml; Sigma-Aldrich) was added to the luminal chamber, and 100 µl of medium from the abluminal chamber was transferred into 96-well plates after 40 min. Data were obtained at a fluorescence intensity of 490/520 nm (absorption/emission) using a FlexStation 3 Multi-Mode microplate reader (Molecular Devices).

Detection of anti-TRIM21 antibodies in NMOSD patients by an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Sera from 43 NMOSD patients, 15 myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein-antibody associated disease (MOGAD) patients, and 43 multiple sclerosis patients within 21 days before treatment, along with 43 disease controls (DCs) and 16 healthy controls (HCs) were subjected to an ELISA to detect TRIM21 antibodies or TROVE2 antibodies (Anti-SS-A 52, RUO652; Anti-SS-A 60, RUO 660; Sebia), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. In summary, (1) diluted serum samples and positive and negative control samples were incubated in a 96-well-plate coated with TRIM21 or TROVE2 for 30 min. (2) The enzyme conjugate containing horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-labelled anti-human IgG antibodies was incubated for 15 min, (3) tetramethylbenzidine substrate solution was added for 15 min, (4) the stop solution was incubated, and (5) absorbance in the wells was detected at 450 nm using an ELISA plate reader (Molecular Device). The concentration was calculated based on a calibration curve by plotting the calibrator concentration on the x-axis (log scale) against the absorbance of the calibrators on the y-axis (linear scale).

Clinical information from NMOSD patients

We collected the following information from 43 NMOSD patients: clinical phenotypes, sex, age, the Expanded Disability Status Scale (EDSS) score at the diagnosis, the number of relapse before and after treatment, serum autoantibodies (antinuclear antibody, anti-SS-A antibody, anti-SS-B antibody, anti-thyroid peroxidase [TPO] antibody/anti-thyroglobulin [TG] antibody, anti-TRIM21 antibody, anti-TROVE2 antibody and anti-GRP78 antibody), cerebrospinal fluid (cell number, protein, IgG index, QALB/QALBLIM, oligoclonal IgG band [OCB]), and MRI findings (length of the spinal cord lesion, presence of brainstem lesion, presence of brain lesion, and presence of Gd-enhanced lesions) in the 43 NMOSD patients. Both the QALB (CSF / serum albumin ratio) and QALBLIM ([age / 15] + 4) were calculated for the functional assessment of BBB permeability.

Effect of anti-TRIM21 Ab-positive NMOSD-IgG on three in vitro BBB models

Pooled anti-TRIM21 Ab-positive NMOSD-IgG was prepared from the sera of nine NMOSD patients with anti-TRIM21 antibodies. Pooled HC-IgG was prepared from the sera of the eight HCs.Individual NMO-IgG (n = 9) and HC-IgG (n = 7) were also prepared. To prepare the triple-culture monolayer BBB in vitro model, TY10 cells on the luminal side and human brain pericytes on the abluminal side on Transwell culture inserts (pore size: 0.4 mm, 24 wells; Corning) as well as astrocytes on 24-well cell culture plates (24-well cell culture plate; Corning) were maintained for 5 days. To prepare the triple culture self-assembly of multicellular spheroids, TY10 cells, human brain pericytes, and astrocytes were resuspended at 5000 cells/microwell in a 1:1:1 ratio in a seeding volume of 100 µL per well (96-well plate; Cell-able) and then maintained at 37 °C for 3 days. Triple-cultured spheroid cells were incubated with pooled NMOSD-IgG (n = 9)/HC-IgG (n = 8) (500 µg/ml) for 12 h to evaluate the permeability of 10-kDa dextran and to observe the change of claudin-5. The experiment was performed four times and the statistical significance of the differences in intensity was analyzed using the paired t-test. Triple-cultured spheroid cells were incubated with pooled NMOSD-IgG (n = 9)/HC-IgG (n = 8) (500 µg/ml) for 12 h to evaluate the permeability of 10-kDa dextran and to observe changes in claudin-5. The experiment was performed four times, and the statistical significance of the differences in intensity was analyzed using a paired t-test. Triple-cultured spheroid cells were exposed to individual NMOSD-IgG/HC-IgG (500 µg/ml) for 12 h to assay the permeability of 10-kDa dextran (n = 6) and to analyze the change in claudin-5 (n = 3). Fluorescent intensity in spheroids was observed using a BZ-X800 microscope (Keyence) and analyzed using ImageJ (NIH).

Three-lane organoplates with 400 μm × 220 μm (w × h) channels (Mimetas BV) were used to create triple culture microfluidic BBB models [21,22]. Human brain nerve pericytes (2.5 × 106 cells/mL) and astrocytes (2.5 × 106 cells/mL) were resuspended in collagen I gel (2 µL), filled into the middle channel, TY10 were cultured in the top channel to create a triple culture microfluidic model. Cell Biologics Complete Human Endothelial Cell Medium (CB medium, #H1168; Cell Biologics, Chicago, IL, USA) was used to maintain cell culture and assay barrier permeability. Pooled NMO-IgG (n = 9) and HC-IgG (n = 8) were incubated in the top channel for 3 h. Twenty microliters of CB medium without fluorescence were added to the bottom channel inlets and outlets, and CB medium containing 0.5 mg/mL FITC-dextran (20 kDa, FD20S; Sigma, St. Louis, MO, USA) was then added to the top channel (40 µL on inlet; 30 µL on outlet). Fluorescent leakage from the lumen of the microvessel to the adjacent middle channel was observed using a BZ-X800 microscope (Keyence).

Statistical analyses

All statistical analyses were performed using the Prism 7 software program (Graph Pad). An unpaired Student’s t-test (two-sided) was used for single comparison analyses, a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Tukey’s multiple comparisons test for multiple comparison analyses, and Fisher’s exact probability test for differences between groups. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, and ***p < 0.001 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Identification of TRIM21 Abs in NMOSD-IgG responsible for BBB breakdown

The experimental design of this study is illustrated in eFig. 1. We selected four IgGs from AQP4 Ab-positive NMOSD patients (n = 3, GRP78 Ab-negative NMOSD-IgG; n = 1, GRP78 Ab-positive NMOSD-IgG), which caused the nuclear translocation of NF-κB in TY10 cells and increased the 10-kDa dextran permeability from previous data [16]. We first carried out a living cell-based antibody-binding assay and proteomics to identify the target protein on the cell surface of TY10 cells for GRP78-Abs negative NMOSD-IgG. In this method, NMOSD-IgG was bound to live TY10 cell surface antigens. After solubilization of the cells and purification on protein A/G beads, the antigenic targets were digested into peptides and subjected to mass spectrometry analysis. TRIM21, TROVE2, and SS-B were identified as target cell surface antigens of NMOSD-IgG (Fig. 1A and B). A band for TRIM21 or TROVE2, corresponding to 50 or 60 kDa, was detected in the lysate of the immunocomplex from 2 NMOSD-IgGs by a Western blot analysis, but the band for SS-B was not detected (Fig. 1C). Cell surface expression of TRIM21 or TROVE2 was observed in TY10 cells by immunohistochemistry (eFig. 2). The band for TRIM21 or TROVE2 was observed in proteins of the whole cell fraction and the cell membrane fraction of the TY10 cells by a Western blot analysis (eFig. 2). Double immunostaining with NMOSD-IgG and anti-TRIM21 Abs or NMOSD-IgG and anti-EEA1 Abs showed colocalization in the cytoplasm of TY10 cells (Fig. 1D).

Molecular mechanism of the disruption of blood-brain barrier due to anti-TRIM21 Abs

RNA-seq and pathway analyses were performed to identify important signaling pathways after exposure to IgG from both anti-TRIM21 Ab-positive and GRP78 Ab-negative NMOSD patients (n = 3) and HCs (n = 4). Among the over 32,000 genes detected in each sample, heat maps demonstrated that 202 genes were significantly upregulated (FC > 1.2; P < 0.05) 254 genes were significantly downregulated (FC < 0.8; P < 0.05) between the NMOSD patients (n = 3) and HC groups (n = 4) (eTable 1). In the IPA network analysis of the upregulated genes in TY10 cells, NF-κB p65 and p38MAPK were detected in the center of the network analysis, TNFRSF6B was identified as an upstream gene of NF-κB and p38MAPK, and TJP2 (ZO-2) was detected as downstream molecules of NF-κB and p38MAPK (Fig. 2A, eFig.3).

The high-content imaging system demonstrated that the protein levels of claudin-5 and ZO-1 were significantly decreased in TY10 cells after exposure to IgG from anti-TRIM21 Ab-positive and GRP78 Ab-negative NMOSD groups (n = 3) compared with those from HC groups (n = 3) (Fig. 2B, C), but the protein levels of occludin and ZO-2 were not significantly different between the two groups (eFig. 4).

Removal of anti-TRIM21 Abs from NMOSD-IgGs decreases the permeability of the BBB

Two of the 4 commercial anti-TRIM21 Abs showed the ability to induce NF-κB p65 nuclear translocation in TY10 cells (eFig. 5), 3 of 4 had an effect on the increased permeability of 10-kDa dextran (eFig. 6), and 2 of 4 had the same effect on 150-kDa dextran (eFig. 7). These results suggest that the activation of TY10 by anti-TRIM21 Abs is epitope-dependent. Discussion

NMOSD co-occurs with primary Sjögren’s disease (PSS) and systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) more frequently than with other autoimmune diseases [23]. In a cohort of NMOSD patients with anti-AQP4-Ab, 35.1% of patients had autoimmune comorbidities, and 51.4% of patients were positive for systemic autoantibodies, including anti-SSA/Ro Abs, anti-SSB/La Abs, and anti-nuclear (ANA) Abs [24]. A recent report suggests that NMOSD patients with PSS are associated with increased disability and a poorer prognosis than those without PSS [23, 25]. In addition, anti-SSA/Ro Abs are well associated with longitudinally extensive transverse myelitis (LETM), disease activity (higher relapse), and severe disability (higher EDSS), and their coexistence is frequently observed in NMOSD with anti-AQP4 Abs [26, 27].

In the present study, we first identified NMOSD-IgG from individual NMOSD patients, which induced the activation of BBB-endothelial cells, including an increase in the nuclear translocation of NF-κB, an increase in permeability, and a decrease in claudin-5 and ZO-1. The antibody binding assay and proteomics identified TRIM21 and TROVE2 as the target antigens of NMOSD-IgG in GRP78 Ab-negative NMOSD patients. RNA-seq and pathway analyses identified NF-κB and p38MAPK as important signaling pathways and tight junctions (claudin-5 and ZO-2) as downstream signaling pathways of NF-κB and p38MAPK after stimulation with TRIM21 Ab-positive NMOSD-IgG. Cell surface expression of TRIM21 and TROVE2 was observed, and double immunostaining with a monoclonal anti-TRIM21 antibody and NMOSD-IgG from TRIM21 Abs positive-NMOSD patients showed colocalization in BBB-endothelial cells, indicating that TRIM21 Abs from NMOSD patients reacted with TRIM21. Incubation with some commercial TRIM21 Abs, but not TROVE2 and SS-B Abs, resulted in the activation of NF-κB signaling and increased permeability of 10- and 150-kDa dextran. Depletion of anti-TRIM21 Abs from NMOSD-IgG resulted in a significant decrease in the permeability of 10-kDa dextran. These results suggest that anti-TRIM21 Abs in NMOSD may contribute to the breakdown of BBB in patients with NMOSD.

We next assayed anti-TRIM21 Abs and TROVE2 Abs and GRP78 Abs in NMOSD patients from the serum sample at the diagnosis. The positive rate of anti-TRIM21 Abs was 30% (13/43) in NMOSD patients, 27% (4/15) in MOGAD patients, and that of anti-TROVE2 Abs was 23% (10/43) in NMOSD patients or 0% (0/15) in MOGAD patients. The positive rate of anti-GRP78 Abs was 42% (18/43) in NMOSD patients after combining the previous results and current findings [16]. Our previous data suggest that NMOSD patients with anti-GRP78 Abs were correlated with the LETM phenotype and EDSS severity at the onset [16], but the present data from the examination of more patients suggest that NMOSD patients with anti-GRP78 Abs were associated with a higher EDSS at the diagnosis and a greater incidence of relapse before treatment. NMOSD-IgG with anti-TRIM21 Abs and without anti-GRP78 Abs led to an increase in permeability in monolayer/spheroid/microfluidic tri-culture models compared to healthy control IgG. These findings suggest that anti-TRIM21 Abs and anti-GRP78 Abs are linked with the breakdown of the BBB in patients with NMOSD.

Anti-SS-A Abs have been detected in patients with several autoimmune diseases including PSS and SLE. The target proteins of SS-A Abs were classified into two different proteins: Ro60 (TROVE2) and Ro52 (TRIM21) [28]. Double-positive patients with both anti-TROVE2 Abs and anti-TRIM21 Abs are more likely to be observed with PSS [29]. In addition, anti-TRIM21 Abs alone are used as potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers, and elevation of serum anti-TRIM21 Abs is associated with disease severity, including (1) anemia and radiological and histopathological findings of the parotid and salivary glands in PSS, (2) muscular weakness in inflammatory myositis, and (3) lung involvement in systemic sclerosis [28, 30]. Furthermore, patients with anti-TRIM21 Abs in the absence of anti-TROVE2 Abs (isolated anti-TRIM21 Abs) are associated with a higher diagnostic rate of autoimmune encephalitis, juvenile cerebral infarction, interstitial pneumonia, and malignancies than double-positive patients with both anti-TROVE2 Abs and anti-TRIM21 Abs [28]. Importantly, the increased titer of anti-TRIM21 Abs in patients with SLE and PSS is positively correlated with abnormally increased serum titers of immunoregulatory cytokines, including IL-2, IL-4, IL-6, IL-10, IL-21, IL-22, and CXCL10 [31]. In addition, anti-SS-A Abs in PSS induced the activation of NF-κB signaling and led to increased levels of inflammatory interleukins (IL-1a, IL-1b, IL-6, IL-8, IL-9, IL-13, IL-17, and IL-22) and chemokines (CXCL1, CXCL6, CXCL9, CCL2, CCL13, and CCL20) in vitro human salivary gland epithelial cells [32].

TRIM21 is a member of the TRIM protein family of the intracellular antibody receptor and RING E3 ubiquitin and has an important effect on the recognition of intracellular antibodies for degradation via the ubiquitin proteasome system and activation of the immune response [33]. After antibodies or antibody-bound proteins are non-specifically delivered into the cytoplasm via Fc receptor-dependent endocytosis or Fc receptor-independent pathways, intracellular antibodies are recognized by the cytosolic Fc receptor TRIM21, resulting in the ubiquitin proteasome-dependent degradation of antibodies or antibody-bound protein [34, 35]. Furthermore, TRIM21 stimulates immune signaling, such as NF-κB signaling, via TNF receptor-associated factor 6 (TRAF6) after intracellular antibodies recruit TRIM21 in the intracellular space [36]. The present study demonstrated that anti-TRIM21 Abs in NMOSD patients could bind to TRIM21 in the cytoplasm and enhance the TRIM21 function, leading to the activation of NF-κB signaling in BBB-endothelial cells. It is still elusive whether anti-TRIM21 Abs can be delivered into the cytoplasm via receptor-mediated transcytosis against cell surface TRIM21, or non-specific binding including Fc receptor-dependent endocytosis or Fc receptor-independent pathways.

Regarding cancer, TRIM21 has a dual effect, suppressing or promoting tumor growth depending on the cancer cell type; upregulation of TRIM21 in cancer cells inhibits tumor growth in renal cell carcinoma, diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, and breast, gastric, colorectal, and ovarian cancer, while this change promotes tumor proliferation and drug resistance in glioma, thyroid and pancreatic cancer [37]. A small population of NMOSD is associated with several types of cancers, including breast, ovarian, gastric, esophageal, colorectal, and lung cancers; lymphoma; and renal cell carcinoma [38]. An immunopathological examination revealed AQP4 expression in 82% of tumor tissues, suggesting that production of anti-AQP4 Abs could be a bystander activation of autoimmunity against cancer cells [38]. In addition, we recently reported that anti-GRP78 Abs are associated with an NMOSD patient with colorectal cancer, whose tumor tissue expressed abundant cell surface GRP78 in an immunohistochemical analysis, suggesting that autoimmune reactivity against GRP78 in tumor cells may lead to the production of anti-GRP78 Abs [17]. The positivity of anti-TRIM21 Abs is associated with an improved survival in patients with ovarian cancer and a poor survival in patients with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma; however, whether or not anti-TRIM21 Abs impair the function of TRIM21 in cancer cells or whether the production of these Abs is derived from overexpression of TRIM21 in cancer cells remains unclear [33]. In the present study, an NMOSD patient with both anti-TRIM21 Abs and anti-GRP78 Abs in the absence of anti-TROVE2 Abs was diagnosed with lung squamous cell carcinoma a year later, and another NMOSD patient with anti-GRP78 Ab in the absence of anti-TRIM21 Abs and anti-TROVE2 Abs was diagnosed with colorectal adenocarcinoma 5 years later (eTable 2), suggesting that positivity for both anti-GRP78 Ab and anti-TRIM21 Ab or anti-GRP78 Ab reacting with tumor antigen may be a biomarker of paraneoplastic NMOSD.

The limitations of this study are that the detailed molecular mechanism by which anti-TRIM21 Abs reach the cytoplasm remains unclear. This study was an exploratory hypothesis generating study; thus, another limitation includes probable potential bias in patient and IgG selection due to the small discovery cohort (n = 4). Functional studies using antibody depletion and commercial antibodies against TRIM21 do not necessarily translate to BBB breakdown in vivo. As the clinical characteristics of anti-TRIM21 Ab-positive NMOSD remain unclear, a multi-institutional study with a large number of patients, including a patient registry, is required to clarify these characteristics. It could be valuable to assess anti-TRIM21 Abs using live CBA because of its higher sensitivity than ELISA. The significance of anti-TRIM21 Abs in both in vivo models and patients remains unclear. In particular, it is unclear whether anti-TRIM21 Abs are actually detected within cerebral vascular endothelial cells in postmortem or biopsy tissues of patients with NMOSD. However, further studies are required to address these limitations of the present study.

In conclusion, anti-TRIM21 Abs were newly identified in patients with NMOSD, possibly contributing to cause the breakdown of the BBB through the activation of NF-κB and the decrease of claudin-5. The positive rate of anti-TRIM21 Abs was 25% in NMOSD patients and 27% in MOGAD patients. These findings provide further understanding of NMOSD with anti-SS-A and anti-TRIM21 antibodies and the pathophysiology of BBB breakdown in NMOSD.

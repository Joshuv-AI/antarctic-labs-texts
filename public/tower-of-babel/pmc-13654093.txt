A collaborative hybrid network with aggregated attention for pulmonary nodule detection
Authors: Jianle Chen, Jianyu Zhu, Yiying Wang, Yuyan Lin, Huilian Liao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLung cancer remains one of the most lethal malignancies worldwide, underscoring the critical importance of early detection for improving patient survival. Current imaging‑based methods for pulmonary nodule (PN) detection are often limited by low efficiency and suboptimal accuracy, falling short of the demands for precise clinical diagnosis. The baseline YOLOv8 model, although efficient, is prone to missed detections due to non-maximum suppression (NMS)‑induced suppression of small nodules and insufficient feature extraction for morphologically complex lesions. To address these clinical challenges, this study aimed to optimize YOLOv8 by integrating a real-time detection transformer (RT-DETR) decoder head and an aggregated attention mechanism, thereby improving detection sensitivity and accuracy for PNs of varying sizes and shapes.MethodsThe proposed model, termed YOLOv8‑RTA, integrates an RT‑DETR‑based transformer decoder head into YOLOv8, replacing the original detection head to mitigate NMS‑induced suppression of true boxes, and introduces an aggregated attention mechanism within the backbone to expand the effective receptive field and enhance deep feature discriminability. Training and evaluation were performed on the Tianchi dataset, with five‑fold cross‑validation and an external test on 50 hospital Picture Archiving and Communication System (PACS) computed tomography (CT) scans.ResultsYOLOv8‑RTA achieved precision of 93.28%, recall of 92.4%, and mean average precision at 50% intersection over union (mAP50) of 94.98% on the Tianchi dataset. Compared to the baseline YOLOv8 model, which achieved an mAP50 of 90.6%, the proposed method improved the detection accuracy by 4.38 percentage points. It also outperformed RT‑DETR‑R18, which attained an mAP50 of 93.51%, by a margin of 1.47 percentage points. The model contains only 6.24 million parameters and runs at 12.2 giga floating point operations per second (GFLOPs). In five‑fold cross‑validation, the mAP50 ranged from 75.4% to 99.3%, with a mean value of 91.5%. On the external PACS dataset, the model detected all 50 nodules, achieving a mean confidence score of 0.86 for nodules smaller than 5 mm.ConclusionsThis study proposes YOLOv8‑RTA, a collaborative hybrid network that combines an RT‑DETR decoder head and an aggregated attention module to enhance multi‑scale feature extraction and discriminative representation for PN detection. On the Tianchi dataset, the model achieved improved performance over baseline YOLOv8 and RT‑DETR‑R18, with gains of 4.38 and 1.47 percentage points in mAP50, respectively, while maintaining a lightweight architecture of only 6.24 million parameters. A preliminary test on 50 real‑world PACS CT images suggests potential clinical feasibility, with all nodules successfully detected in this limited cohort. These findings indicate that YOLOv8‑RTA offers a favorable accuracy-efficiency trade‑off for PN screening. Future work will focus on larger multi‑center validation, patient‑level cross‑validation, and extension to three-dimensional (3D) context modelling.Keywords: Pulmonary nodule detection (PN), YOLOv8, real-time detection transformer decoder head (RT‑DETR decoder head), aggregated attention

Introduction

Lung cancer is a common malignancy that endangers human health and life (1). In its early stage, it manifests as solitary pulmonary nodules (PNs)—also called “shadows” or “lung spots”, which appear on medical images as small, dense, round areas denser than normal lung tissue (2). As early lung cancer symptoms resemble those of common respiratory diseases and the nodules are tiny on computed tomography (CT), they are easily missed. Although lung cancer can be treated, the cure rate remains low and the process is extremely painful, with a 5-year mortality rate as high as 80% (3). Therefore, early diagnosis, treatment, and screening are crucial to prolong survival.

In clinical practice, imaging-based nodule detection is the first step in lung cancer screening. Statistics indicate that such screening can reduce lung cancer incidence by 20% (4). Physicians monitor nodules as key indicators so that pulmonary abnormalities can be detected early. Tracking changes in nodule size and number helps to identify early signs of lung cancer, enabling timely intervention. In pulmonary disease screening, nodules are emphasized because they allow physicians to follow dynamic changes and judge malignancy potential, thereby gaining valuable time for early diagnosis and treatment. This proactive monitoring significantly raises the early detection rate.

The widespread application of medical imaging analysis technology in the diagnosis of PNs requires radiologists to analyze a large number of medical images to assess patients’ conditions. According to the China Artificial Intelligence White Paper, each patient’s CT examination generates approximately 200–300 images, and physicians need to handle the diagnostic workload of over 40,000 medical images per day on average. Additionally, CT images differ from traditional X-rays, which only require a single scan. In contrast with conventional X-rays, CT requires volumetric scanning of the entire lung to detect small or even micro-nodules (5). Prolonged mechanical reading leads to fatigue and declining accuracy, causing missed or erroneous detection (6). The surge in medical imaging has subjected radiologists to prolonged and high-intensity image-reading tasks, where the accumulation of visual fatigue can compromise diagnostic sensitivity and specificity. To address the heavy workload of image interpretation, the medical field has begun adopting deep learning (DL) techniques for the detection and segmentation of PNs in CT images. This approach not only significantly enhances diagnostic efficiency but also provides critical technical support for early screening and intervention of PNs.

DL has revolutionized medical image processing, achieving notable success in lesion detection (7), segmentation (8), and assisted diagnosis (9). By constructing multi-layer neural networks, DL automatically extracts complex features from large-scale data (10). In medical imaging, computer vision algorithms automatically parse imaging data and precisely locate lesions, surpassing traditional manual reading (11). DL methods have shown outstanding performance in classification and detection tasks and have demonstrated significant advantages in the field of medical image analysis (12). The medical image analysis method based on DL effectively solves the limitations of traditional methods in detection efficiency and accuracy through its automatic feature extraction capability. Such methods enable automated screening of dense CT sequences, increasing speed 3–5 times over traditional approaches (13), while reducing false positives (<0.5 per scan) (14) and raising sensitivity (>95%) (15). This breakthrough allows high-quality screening even in resource-limited settings, offering a new path for early diagnosis.

For example, three-dimensional convolutional neural network (3D-CNN)-based methods have advanced significantly. Researchers such as Golan et al. (16) developed an end-to-end 3D-CNN that automatically extracts 3D spatial features, achieving 88.7% sensitivity on the Lung Image Database Consortium and Image Database Resource Initiative (LIDC-IDRI). Dou et al. (17) optimized a densely connected 3D-CNN, cutting false positives from 4.2 to 1.8 per scan (P<0.01). However, such methods still face two major technical bottlenecks: on the one hand, deep network structures typically contain over 50 million parameters, resulting in a single CT case analysis time exceeding 12 seconds (18), which struggles to meet the demands of clinical real-time diagnosis (19). On the other hand, traditional 3×3 convolutional kernels exhibit significantly insufficient feature capture capability for nodules smaller than 5 mm, with a detection sensitivity of merely 76.3%, far below the 92.1% recognition rate achieved for larger nodules. Even Transformers, although able to model long-range dependencies, produce 1.74 mm localization error on thin-slice CT—worse than radiologists’ 0.92 mm (20). These technical limitations make it difficult for current algorithms to meet real-time detection requirements while maintaining accuracy for small PNs, constituting a key factor restricting the widespread application of DL in early lung cancer screening.

In recent years, various DL approaches have been developed to address these challenges. CNN-based detection methods have been widely applied to PN detection, with the You Only Look Once (YOLO) series demonstrating outstanding performance in both accuracy and speed due to its efficient one-stage detection architecture. Wang et al. (21) proposed an innovative algorithm based on an improved YOLOv3 for PN detection. By skillfully incorporating a Coordinate Attention mechanism, the study significantly enhanced the model's precision and sensitivity metrics in nodule detection. To further promote the clinical application of this technology, Liu et al. (22) subsequently developed the STBi-YOLO model, achieving a major breakthrough in algorithmic efficiency. This model not only considerably optimized the processing speed of PN recognition but also ensured real-time performance, providing reliable technical support for the clinical practice of early lung cancer screening. Although Hou et al.’s (23) attention and multi-path fusion algorithm improved nodule detection performance, CNNs still exhibit inherent limitations in detecting small objects and multi-scale targets, despite their remarkable progress in medical image analysis.

Transformer-based detection methods have also been widely adopted in medical imaging analysis. Detection transformers (DETRs) (24) represent a transformer-based object detection framework that formulates detection as a set prediction problem. Utilizing an encoder-decoder architecture and bipartite matching loss, it directly predicts object categories and bounding boxes without the need for manually designed anchors or non-maximum suppression (NMS) post-processing. In medical image analysis, the advantages of DETR are mainly reflected in the following aspects: First, its end-to-end training paradigm avoids biases introduced by anchor design (25) and NMS commonly used in conventional detection methods. Second, the global self-attention mechanism is particularly suitable for capturing subtle and scattered lesion features—such as PNs or small tumors. Studies have shown that on chest X-ray detection tasks, DETR improves sensitivity for small nodule detection by 12.3% compared to traditional methods such as Faster R-CNN (26). Furthermore, DETR eliminates the need for complex pre- or post-processing pipelines (27). Despite significant progress in transformer-based PN detection, its performance remains constrained by insufficient fine-grained feature extraction for small-scale lesions and information loss during multi-scale feature fusion. In particular, the representational capacity of existing methods in cross-scale feature interaction and inter-channel dependency modeling still requires improvement. More notably, due to the deep degradation effect, feature interaction networks built upon stacked Transformer layers often struggle to achieve sufficient information mixing, leading to suboptimal issues such as distorted visual representations.

To overcome these challenges, this paper proposes a collaborative hybrid network with aggregated attention for PN detection. Our model, YOLOv8-RTA, builds upon the YOLOv8 (28) architecture and innovatively incorporates the real-time DETR (RT-DETR) (29) decoder head to form a synergistic hybrid framework. The introduction of the aggregated attention mechanism (30) further enhances its capability to capture discriminative features of nodules. We present this article in accordance with the CLEAR reporting checklist (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-2026-0799/rc).

Methods

In this chapter, we first introduce the overall structure of the YOLOv8-RTA PN detection model, followed by an in-depth discussion of key improvement modules, including the aggregated attention and RT-DETR decoder modules.

Datasets

Tianchi dataset: this public dataset comprises 1,000 patients, each with at least one PN (total 1,000 nodules). Nodule size distribution: 5–10 mm (50%) and 10–30 mm (50%). Pathological benign/malignant labels are not uniformly available; for nodules without pathology, three radiologists performed annotation via majority voting. All CT images have a slice thickness <2 mm. No formal inter-observer agreement metric was reported by the data providers, but the consensus‑based procedure ensures reliability.

Hospital PACS dataset: we collected 50 CT scans from patients with confirmed PNs at Shunde Hospital of Guangzhou University of Chinese Medicine. Each scan contains exactly one nodule (total 50 nodules), as shown in Figure 1. Nodule sizes range from 3 to 10 mm (no benign/malignant distinction due to lack of pathological confirmation for all cases). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. According to the Institutional Review Board of Guangzhou University of Chinese Medicine Shunde Hospital and national regulations, the requirement for ethical approval and informed consent was waived for the use of these retrospectively collected, anonymized images.

Sample CT images from the Tianchi dataset. CT, computed tomography.

Acquisition parameters: scanner = United Imaging uCT 710 (United Imaging, Shanghai, China); slice thickness =1 mm (minimum); spiral time =0.5 s; collimator width =40 mm; tube current =130 mAs; tube voltage =120 kVp; reconstruction kernel = standard lung kernel.

Preprocessing and slice selection: a lung window [width =1,500 Hounsfield units (HU), level =−500 HU] was applied; CT values were clipped to this range and normalized to [0, 1]. Only axial slices containing the nodule were selected for testing. All selected slices were resized to 640×640 pixels.

The radiologists independently drew bounding boxes using LabelImg software (TzuTa Lin, Washington, DC, USA). No data augmentation or post-processing was applied.

This experiment is based on the Tianchi dataset for training, validation, and testing. Hospital PACS dataset is used as a generalization test to validate the detection performance of the model. This study adopts a rigorous data partitioning scheme, dividing the entire dataset into training, validation, and test sets in a 7:1.5:1.5 ratio. This partitioning strategy is based on the following scientific considerations: a 70% allocation to the training set ensures sufficient samples for the model to learn effective features, whereas 15% allocated to each of the validation and test sets meets the requirements for hyperparameter tuning and provides an adequate number of samples for final performance evaluation. The data partitioning process employs a stratified sampling strategy to ensure that each subset maintains distribution characteristics consistent with the original dataset across key dimensions such as nodule size, pathological type, and imaging features, thereby guaranteeing the reliability and reproducibility of the experimental results. All data partitioning is performed at the case level to prevent CT slices from the same patient from being assigned to different subsets, avoiding data leakage issues.

YOLOv8-RTA architecture

The proposed YOLOv8-RTA network architecture is illustrated in Figure 2. By integrating the hybrid decoder equipped with auxiliary prediction heads from RT-DETR into the YOLOv8 framework to replace the original Head module, the model effectively addresses the issue of missed detection caused by erroneous suppression of ground-truth bounding boxes during traditional NMS post-processing. Furthermore, the introduction of an Aggregated Attention mechanism optimizes the Backbone structure of YOLOv8, significantly mitigating problems such as slow growth of the effective receptive field and excessive smoothing of deep features in traditional multi-stage stacked networks. Consequently, it effectively reduces both missed detections and false alarms in small object detection. The structural innovations in this study not only overcome the limitations of shallow integrated networks but also markedly enhance the model’s feature extraction capability for multi-scale objects.

The RT-DETR decoder is integrated as follows: The three feature maps (P3, P4, and P5) from the YOLOv8 backbone are linearly projected to 256 channels, flattened, and concatenated. Positional encodings are added. The decoder consists of 6 transformer layers with 8 attention heads and 300 learnable object queries. Auxiliary losses are applied after each layer. The output features are passed to dynamic classification and regression heads; no NMS is used.

RT-DETR decoder

In pulmonary CT imaging datasets, PNs typically appear as small objects with low pixel occupancy and limited feature representation. However, the head network of the YOLOv8 detection framework exhibits inherent limitations in small object detection: the multi-scale feature pyramid (such as P3/P4/P5) tends to generate duplicate predictions for the same nodule, whereas the traditional NMS algorithm—relying on a rigid intersection over union (IoU)-based suppression strategy—is prone to over-suppression of detection boxes for small objects. This phenomenon stems from two core factors: firstly, localization deviations of small objects across multi-scale feature maps lead to inaccurate IoU calculations; secondly, there exists an inherent conflict between the global ranking mechanism of NMS and the locally sensitive spatial distribution characteristics of small objects.

To fundamentally address this issue, this study adopts an end-to-end detection model based on the transformer architecture, such as RT-DETR (22). Its core advantages include the following: leveraging self-attention mechanisms for global context modeling and utilizing the Hungarian matching algorithm to establish a one-to-one correspondence between predicted boxes and ground-truth targets, thereby completely avoiding the suppression bias introduced by NMS.

RT-DETR is a real-time object detection model based on the transformer architecture, which achieves efficient detection performance through innovative decoder and head designs. The decoder component employs a multi-level query mechanism to transform input feature maps into semantically rich feature representations. Specifically, the decoder effectively integrates multi-scale features from the encoder via cross-attention mechanisms and models relationships between objects using self-attention. The head component then converts the output features of the decoder into final detection results, typically consisting of two sub-components: a classification head, which predicts the categorical probability distribution of objects, and a regression head, which outputs the coordinates of the bounding boxes. Compared to traditional detection models, the decoder and head design of RT-DETR eliminates the reliance on manually designed anchors and NMS post-processing, enabling an end-to-end detection pipeline.

Aggregated attention

In the task of PN detection, YOLOv8, as an efficient general object detection framework, faces significant limitations due to the depth degradation effect caused by the stacking of residual modules in its backbone structure. This architectural flaw is mainly reflected in two aspects: multiple downsampling operations in deep networks lead to the gradual disappearance of features of tiny PNs, and there is a significant mismatch between the preset anchor sizes and the actual scale distribution of PNs in medical images.

To address the above issues, this study proposes the use of an aggregated attention mechanism for optimization. This innovative design introduces a 3×3 sliding window self-attention computation path based on query pixel centers in the early network stages where feature maps maintain their original resolution. This architectural design can directly extract texture features and morphological details specific to tiny PNs, effectively avoiding the loss of feature information caused by consecutive strided convolution operations in traditional network architectures.

The aggregated attention mechanism innovatively fuses the local window attention path with contextual features obtained through global average pooling within a single mixed layer. This parallel multi-scale feature processing strategy not only achieves synergistic optimization of features at different levels but also breaks through the traditional technical approach of relying on residual block stacking to expand the receptive field. This design significantly shortens the gradient propagation path, effectively alleviating the performance degradation problem associated with increasing network depth, allowing key features of tiny PNs to be fully preserved even in shallow networks.

The aggregated attention mechanism fuses two parallel attention paths: a local path that operates on a 3×3 sliding window centered at each pixel, and a global path that uses global average pooling to capture context. For each pixel (i, j), we compute similarity scores with its local neighbors (K=9) and with a global context vector. The two similarity vectors are concatenated, combined with a learnable positional bias (log‑CPB), and normalized via a temperature-scaled softmax to produce fused attention weights. The weights are then split back into local and global parts, and each part multiplies its corresponding value vectors. Finally, a learnable key token T provides an additional position-dependent modulation. The forward computation is summarized in Table 1.

Results

This paper introduces the Tianchi dataset (32), experimental setup, and metrics. Further, we compare methods, conduct ablation and k-fold cross-validation, and test on real hospital Picture Archiving and Communication System (PACS) images.

Detection is performed on two-dimensional (2D) axial CT slices independently. Each slice is processed as a separate image; no 3D context is used across slices. This choice is justified because most PNs (especially those <10 mm) span only 1–3 slices, and a 2D model with deformable attention achieves competitive sensitivity with far lower computational cost [12.2 giga floating point operations per second (GFLOPs) vs. >100 GFLOPs for typical 3D-CNNs].

Experimental setting and evaluation metrics

Experimental setting

During model training, Windows 11 was used with Python 3.11. Torch is 2.7.1+cu128 framework. The CPU is an Intel® Core™ i5-14600KF, and the GPU consists of an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5060Ti graphics card with 16 GB of memory. The number of epochs is 300; batch size is 4; workers is 4; optimizer is AdamW; and Cosine annealing with 3 warmup epochs.

In the baseline YOLOv8 model, the Recall and mAP50 metrics were 0.877 and 0.906, respectively. The study first optimized the detection head of YOLOv8 by introducing the decoder module from the RT-DETR head, significantly improving model performance. Considering the presence of substantial noise interference and complex backgrounds in PN images, the study further integrated the aggregated attention module to enhance the model’s ability to discern background information. The experimental results showed that the introduction of this module significantly increased the Recall metric from 0.877 in the baseline model to 0.924. More importantly, this architectural optimization significantly enhanced the model’s feature extraction and representation capabilities, enabling it to more accurately capture the subtle features of PNs, thereby comprehensively improving multiple key performance indicators including mAP.

As shown in Table 2, the combination of RT-DETR and aggregated attention alone yields low performance. This is because the aggregated attention module was originally designed for CNN backbones. When applied to the transformer-based RT-DETR backbone, it introduces redundant feature modeling and training instability due to conflicting attention mechanisms. In contrast, YOLOv8’s CNN backbone benefits significantly from the local texture enhancement provided by Aggregated Attention, whereas the subsequent RT-DETR head effectively eliminates missed detections caused by NMS. Consequently, the complete YOLOv8-RTA model achieves the best performance (mAP50 =0.9498), demonstrating genuine synergy among the three components rather than a trivial additive effect.

Compared to traditional detectors, YOLOv8-RTA shows clear performance advantages. The two-stage detector Faster R-CNN, due to its VGG-16 architecture and multiple pooling operations, is prone to severe spatial information loss, resulting in an mAP50 of only 0.8090 and a recall rate of only 0.765 for small-sized nodules. The single-stage detector (SSD), although using multi-scale prediction, has an mAP50 as low as 0.582 due to insufficient resolution of high-level features, making it difficult to meet clinical needs.

Among the YOLO series models, the latest versions show improved accuracy but at the cost of drastically increased computational complexity. For example, YOLOv9 has up to 60.7 M parameters, 9.7 times that of YOLOv8-RTA, and a computational cost of 266.1 GFLOPs, severely limiting clinical application. In contrast, YOLOv8-RTA achieves the optimal mAP50 (0.9498) and recall (0.924) among all models evaluated, while maintaining a lightweight architecture (6.24 M parameters, 12.2 GFLOPs). In terms of the stricter mAP50-95 metric, RT-DETR R18 (0.7631) surpasses YOLOv8-RTA (0.643), demonstrating that the latter emphasizes detection completeness (high recall) over precise bounding-box alignment. This trade-off is clinically relevant: for nodule screening, missing a nodule is more harmful than a slightly imprecise box. These results indicate that YOLOv8-RTA achieves a favorable balance between detection accuracy and computational efficiency.

This study confirms that YOLOv8-RTA has made important breakthroughs in the precision-efficiency balance, providing a new technical path for the actual deployment of artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted diagnosis systems. Future research will focus on optimizing the model’s deployment performance on edge computing devices to further promote its application in clinical environments.

K-fold cross-validation experiments

To systematically evaluate the generalization performance of the YOLOv8-RTA model, this study conducted five-fold cross-validation experiments on the Tianchi dataset. This dataset contains 1,380 annotated CT images for training and 395 test images, allowing validation of the model’s detection capability for different types of PNs, including nodules and low-contrast lesions.

As shown in Table 4, considerable performance variation was observed across folds: mAP50 ranged from 75.4% (Fold-4) to 99.33% (Fold-3), and recall from 80.2% to 99.75%. This fluctuation is attributable to differences in nodule characteristics across the data subsets created by random splitting. Fold-4 contains a higher proportion of challenging small and low-contrast nodules, whereas Fold-3 is enriched with larger, easier-to-detect nodules. These results indicate that the model’s performance is sensitive to the distribution of nodule types in the training set, which is a limitation of the current validation protocol. Future work should adopt stratified cross-validation (balancing nodule size and density across folds) to obtain a more stable and unbiased estimate of generalization. Additionally, Fold-1 accidentally shared the same slices with the main test set (Table 3) because the split was performed at the slice level, indicating that the cross-validation results are not fully independent.

Discussion

The YOLOv8-RTA model proposed in this study achieves significant performance improvement in the task of PN detection. Systematic experiments based on the Tianchi PN dataset demonstrate that the model exhibits clear advantages in detection accuracy, computational efficiency, and model complexity. Compared to baseline models and current state-of-the-art object detection models, YOLOv8-RTA shows substantial improvements in key evaluation metrics such as mAP50.

To comprehensively evaluate the detection performance of YOLOv8-RTA, this study selected YOLOv8, YOLOv11, and RT-DETR-R18 as comparative models. Additionally, to validate the model’s performance on real clinical images, test images were sourced from the CT image database of the PACS system at Shunde Hospital of Guangzhou University of Chinese Medicine. From a clinical translation perspective, detection sensitivity alone is not sufficient for PN management. In practice, clinicians also rely on longitudinal stability (nodule growth over serial CT scans), clinical context (patient age, smoking history, family history, and symptoms), and risk stratification tools to estimate malignancy probability. YOLOv8-RTA is designed as an assistive second reader that processes CT images and highlights suspicious nodules, thereby reducing the risk of missed detections and allowing radiologists to concentrate on complex cases. The AI output should be interpreted together with the patient’s clinical history and temporal imaging changes. This workflow ensures that AI complements, rather than replaces, human expertise. Future work could integrate temporal information (serial CT) and risk calculators directly into the framework to further support clinical decision-making.

Quantitative evaluation on the Tianchi dataset demonstrates that YOLOv8-RTA achieves an mAP50 value of 0.9498. This indicates that the model can effectively detect PNs of varying sizes and morphologies. Although this paper qualitatively observed that YOLOv8-RTA detects many nodules, we have not provided a quantitative size-stratified analysis. Such an evaluation is necessary to substantiate claims of “strong performance for small nodules”, and we plan to perform it in a future study. The absence of data augmentation and 3D context modeling are limitations that may be addressed in future work.

Furthermore, the current model has not been validated on CT scans from patients with interstitial lung diseases (ILDs), including both fibrotic and non-fibrotic forms. As noted in previous studies (33,34), ILDs can generate nodular‑like abnormalities, inflammatory pseudonodules, and architectural distortion that may mimic or obscure true PNs. Our hospital PACS validation specifically excluded such patients to avoid confounding; consequently, the model’s performance in ILD‑affected lungs remains unknown. Future work should include dedicated evaluation on ILD cohorts to determine the model’s utility in these clinically challenging scenarios.

Ablation experiment results reveal that both the RT-DETR decoder head and the aggregated attention module play critical roles in performance improvement. The aggregated attention module directly extracts texture features and morphological details unique to nodules, effectively mitigating the issue of feature information loss caused by successive strided convolution operations in traditional network architectures. The decoder head design of RT-DETR eliminates the need for manually preset anchor boxes and the NMS post-processing step, achieving a truly end-to-end detection pipeline, thereby further enhancing the overall detection performance of YOLOv8-RTA.

Conclusions

This study introduces YOLOv8-RTA, a collaborative hybrid network with aggregated attention for PN detection. Through comprehensive experiments including systematic comparisons, ablation studies, k-fold cross‑validation, and a preliminary test on hospital PACS CT images, the following conclusions are drawn.

On the Tianchi dataset, YOLOv8-RTA achieves the highest mAP50 (94.98%) and recall (92.4%) among all compared models (YOLOv8, YOLOv11, RT-DETR-R18, etc.), with a precision of 93.28%. Therefore, YOLOv8-RTA offers a favorable trade-off for screening scenarios where high detection recall is critical. In a preliminary test on 50 real-world CT images from a hospital PACS system, the model detected all nodules in this limited cohort, showing promising clinical feasibility.

On the Tianchi dataset, YOLOv8-RTA achieves the highest mAP50 (94.98%) and recall (92.4%) among all compared models (YOLOv8, YOLOv11, RT-DETR-R18, etc.), with a precision of 93.28%. Therefore, YOLOv8-RTA offers a favorable trade-off for screening scenarios where high detection recall is critical. In a preliminary test on 50 real-world CT images from a hospital PACS system, the model detected all nodules in this limited cohort, showing promising clinical feasibility.

Ablation experiments confirm that the full YOLOv8-RTA model (with both the RT-DETR decoder head and the aggregated attention module) achieves the best mAP50 (94.98%), significantly outperforming variants that use only a subset of these modules. The synergistic effect of the decoder head and the attention module enhances multi-scale feature extraction and discriminative representation.

In summary, YOLOv8-RTA provides a useful accuracy-efficiency trade-off for PN detection. Nevertheless, several limitations remain: the primary evaluation originally relied heavily on mAP50, external validation was limited to 50 single-institution cases, the cross-validation was not patient-stratified, and comparisons with dedicated PN detectors are still preliminary. Future work will focus on larger multi-center validation, patient-level cross-validation, and extending the method to 3D context modelling.

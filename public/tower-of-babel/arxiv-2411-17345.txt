The horospherical p p -Christoffel-Minkowski and prescribed p p -shifted Weingarten curvature problems in hyperbolic space
Yingxiang Hu, Haizhong Li and Botong Xu

Abstract
. The L p L_{p} -Christoffel-Minkowski problem and the prescribed L p L_{p} -Weingarten curvature problem for convex hypersurfaces in Euclidean space are important problems in geometric analysis. In this paper, we consider their counterparts in hyperbolic space. For the horospherical p p -Christoffel-Minkowski problem first introduced and studied by the second and third authors, we prove the existence of smooth, origin-symmetric, strictly horospherically convex solutions by establishing a new full rank theorem. We also propose the prescribed p p -shifted Weingarten curvature problem and prove an existence result.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
58J05, 52A55

1.
Introduction

The regular Christoffel-Minkowski problem aims to find a smooth, closed and strictly convex hypersurface in the
(n+1)
-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with the prescribed
k
-th area measure. Equivalently, for any given smooth positive function
f
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, it is to find
strictly convex
solutions of the equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle p_{n-k}(D^{2}h+h\sigma)=f\quad\text{on $\mathbb{S}^{n}$},

with
(D^{2}h+h\sigma)>0
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Here
\sigma
is the round metric on the
n
-dimensional unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
D
its Levi-Civita connection, and
p_{n-k}
is the normalized
(n-k)
th elementary symmetric polynomial of the eigenvalues of its argument. The solution
h
is known as the support function of a strictly convex hypersurface in Euclidean space, and the eigenvalues of the matrix
(D^{2}h+h\sigma)
are the principal radii of curvature of the hypersurface. When
k=0
, it is the equation for the famous Minkowski problem, which has been completely resolved, see e.g.
[
Nir53
,
CY76
,
Pog78
,
Lew83
,
Caf90
]
; When
k=n-1
, it is the equation for the Christoffel problem, which is also completely settled in
[
Fir67
,
Ber69
,
LWW21
]
. For the intermediate Christoffel-Minkowski problem (
1\leq k\leq n-2
), under a sufficient condition on the prescribed function
f
, Guan and Ma
[
GM03
]
proved the existence of a unique strictly convex solution up to translations.

By calculating the variation of quermassintegrals of convex bodies along Firey’s
p
-sums
[
Fir62
]
, Lutwak
[
Lut93
]
introduced the
k
-th
p
-area measure of convex bodies for
p\geq 1
. The prescribed
k
-th
p
-area measure problem for
1\leq k\leq n-1
is called the
L_{p}
-Christoffel-Minkowski problem, which reduces to the classical one when
p=1
. In the regular case, this problem can be reduced to the following nonlinear PDE:

(1.2)

\displaystyle h^{1-p}p_{n-k}(D^{2}h+h\sigma)=f\quad\text{on $\mathbb{S}^{n}$},

with
(D^{2}h+h\sigma)>0
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. For
p\geq n-k+1
, equation (
1.2
) was investigated by Hu, Ma and Shen
[
HMS04
]
. For
1<p<n-k+1
, by imposing the even assumption on
f
, this equation was studied by Guan and Xia
[
GX18
]
. For
0<p<1
, it was recently discussed by Bianchini, Colesanti, Pagnini and Roncoroni
[
BCPR23
]
.

One of the main ingredients in the proof of the existence of convex solutions to the
L_{p}
-Christoffel-Minkwski problem is the constant rank theorem, which has profound implications for the geometry of solutions to equation (
1.2
). This technique was devised to deal with the convexity property for the homotopy method of deformation by Caffarelli and Friedman
[
CF85
]
, see also
[
SWYY85
]
. Later, it was extended to the Christoffel-Minkowski problem and the prescribed Weingarten curvature problem for embedded hypersurfaces in Euclidean space
[
GM03
,
GLM06
,
GMZ06
]
, as well as the more general fully nonlinear elliptic equations by
[
CGM07
,
BG09
]
. The deformation lemma was also adapted to the prescribed curvature measure problems in
[
GLM09
,
GLL12
]
. Recently, Bryan, Ivaki and Scheuer
[
BIS23
]
presented a new approach to the constant rank theorem by applying the strong maximum principle to a linear differential inequality (in a viscosity sense) for the subtraces of a linear map, instead of the nonlinear test functions as in
[
BG09
]
.

It is natural to study the analog of such prescribed measure problems for closed hypersurfaces in hyperbolic space.
A bounded domain (as well as its boundary) in hyperbolic space is called strictly horospherically convex if the principal curvatures are greater than
1
on its boundary. The geometry of horospherically convex domains has attracted much attention in the decades, see e.g.
[
ACW21
,
BM99
,
GRST08
,
HLW22
,
NT97
,
NSW22
,
WX14
]
.

Recently, Li and Xu
[
LX22
]
introduced a summation of two sets in hyperbolic space called the hyperbolic
p
-sum, and they introduced the horospherical
k
-th
p
-area measure by calculating the variation of the
k
-th modified quermassintegrals (see
[
ACW21
]
) of horospherically convex domains along the hyperbolic
p
-sum. They proposed the associated horospherical
p
-Christoffel-Minkowski problem
[
LX22
, Prob. 5.2]
. This problem aims to find strictly horospherically convex solutions to equation

(1.3)

\displaystyle\varphi^{-p-k}p_{n-k}(A[\varphi])=f\quad\text{on $\mathbb{S}^{n}$},

where
A[\varphi]
is a symmetric
2
-tensor on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
defined by

(1.4)

\displaystyle A[\varphi]:=D^{2}\varphi-\frac{1}{2}\frac{|D\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi}\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi-\frac{1}{\varphi}\right)\sigma,

and a positive function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is called strictly horospherically convex if
A[\varphi]>0
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. The strict horospherical convexity of
\varphi
is equivalent to the fact that
\log\varphi
is the horospherical support function of a strictly horospherically convex hypersurface.
When
k=1
and
p=-n
, equation (
1.3
) is the Christoffel problem in hyperbolic space proposed by
[
EGM09
]
.

We first focus on the case
1\leq k\leq n-1
of equation (
1.3
). By employing the volume preserving curvature flows, Li and Xu
[
LX22
,
Thm. 7.3]
proved the existence of solutions to equation (
1.3
) for
p\geq-n
, up to a constant:

Theorem A

(
[
LX22
]
)
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
1\leq k\leq n-1
be integers, and let
p\geq-n
be a real number. Let
f(z)
be a smooth, positive and even function defined on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

(1)

If
p=-n
, we assume that
f
is a positive constant function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

(2)

If
-n<p\leq-\frac{n+k}{2}
, then we assume that

D^{2}f^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}-\frac{n-3k-2p}{n-k}|Df^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}|\sigma+\left(\frac{n+p}{n-k}\right)^{2}f^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}\sigma\geq 0.

(3)

If
-\frac{n+k}{2}<p<-k
, then we assume that

D^{2}f^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}-\frac{(n-3k-2p)^{2}}{2(n+p)(n+k+2p)}\frac{|Df^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}|^{2}}{f^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}}\sigma+\frac{n+p}{2(n-k)}f^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}\sigma\geq 0.

(4)

If
-k\leq p\leq n-2k
, then we assume that

D^{2}f^{-\frac{1}{n+p}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{|Df^{-\frac{1}{n+p}}|^{2}}{f^{-\frac{1}{n+p}}}\sigma+\frac{1}{2}f^{-\frac{1}{n+p}}\sigma\geq 0.

(5)

If
p>n-2k
, then we assume that

D^{2}f^{-\frac{1}{n+p}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{|Df^{-\frac{1}{n+p}}|^{2}}{f^{-\frac{1}{n+p}}}\sigma+\frac{n-k}{n+p}f^{-\frac{1}{n+p}}\sigma\geq 0.

Then there exists a constant
\gamma>0
such that the following equation admits a smooth, even and strictly horospherically convex solution,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\varphi^{-p-k}p_{n-k}(A[\varphi(z)])=\gamma f(z).

Due to the lack of homogeneity of the horospherical
p
-area measures, it is in general difficult to remove the normalizing constant
\gamma
in (
1.5
) via the flow approach. Recently, Chen
[
Che24
]
removed the constant
\gamma
for equation (
1.3
) with
k=n-1
and
p=-n
via a full rank theorem for the corresponding semilinear elliptic equation.

In this paper, by establishing the full rank theorem (Theorem
5.1
) for the fully nonlinear equation (
1.3
), we improve Theorem A by removing the constant
\gamma
. For simplicity, we introduce the following assumptions on the function
f
in equation (
1.3
):

Assumption 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
1\leq k\leq n-1
be integers. Let
p\geq-n
be a real number and
f(z)
be a smooth and positive function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

(1)

If
p=-n
, then we assume that

\displaystyle D^{2}f^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}-3|Df^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}|\sigma+\frac{f^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}}{2+8\left(\max_{z\in\mathbb{S}^{n}}f(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{n-k}}}\sigma\geq 0.

(2)

If
-n<p\leq-\frac{n+k}{2}
, then we assume that

\displaystyle D^{2}f^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}-\frac{n-3k-2p}{n-k}|Df^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}|\sigma+\left(\frac{n+p}{n-k}\right)^{2}f^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}\sigma\geq 0.

(3)

If
-\frac{n+k}{2}<p<-k
, then we assume that

\displaystyle D^{2}f^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}-\frac{(n-3k-2p)^{2}}{2(n+p)(n+k+2p)}\frac{|Df^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}|^{2}}{f^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}}\sigma+\frac{n+p}{2(n-k)}f^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}\sigma\geq 0.

(4)

If
-k\leq p\leq n-2k
, then we assume that

\displaystyle D^{2}f^{-\frac{1}{n+p}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{|Df^{-\frac{1}{n+p}}|^{2}}{f^{-\frac{1}{n+p}}}\sigma+\frac{1}{2}f^{-\frac{1}{n+p}}\sigma\geq 0.

(5)

If
p>n-2k
, then we assume that

\displaystyle D^{2}f^{-\frac{1}{n+p}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{|Df^{-\frac{1}{n+p}}|^{2}}{f^{-\frac{1}{n+p}}}\sigma+\frac{n-k}{n+p}f^{-\frac{1}{n+p}}\sigma\geq 0.

Assumption 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
0\leq k\leq n-1
be integers. Let
p\geq n-2k
be a real number and
f(z)
be a smooth, positive function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. We assume that
0<f<2^{k-n}
if
p=n-2k
, and
0<f<(2k+p-n)^{\frac{2k+p-n}{2}}(n-k)^{n-k}/(n+p)^{\frac{n+p}{2}}
if
p>n-2k
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
1\leq k\leq n-1
be integers, and let
p\geq-n
be a real number.
Assume that
f
is a positive, even function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
that satisfies Assumption
1.1
. If
p\geq n-2k
, we assume in addition that
f
satisfies Assumption
1.2
. Then the horospherical
p
-Christoffel-Minkowski problem (
1.3
) admits a smooth, even and strictly horospherically convex solution.

Let us make the following remarks on the assumption of
f
in the above Theorem
1.1
.

(1)

When
1\leq k\leq n-1
and
p>-n
, the convexity assumption of
f
in Assumption
1.1
is surprisingly the same as that of Theorem A, even though they are derived from two different ways. The former one is obtained from the full rank theorem for the fully nonlinear elliptic equation (
1.3
) that would be established in Theorem
5.1
, and the latter one was derived from the pinching estimates for a volume preserving curvature flow (see
[
LX22
, Prop. 7.1]
). When
k=n-1
and
p=-n
, Theorem
1.1
reduces to
[
Che24
, Theorem 1.1]
.

(2)

When
p\geq n-2k
, Assumption
1.2
of
f
is natural. By the work of Li-Xu
[
LX22
]
and Hu-Wei-Zhou
[
HWZ23
]
, the origin-centered geodesic spheres are the unique solutions to equation (
1.3
) when
p>-n
, and they are also the unique even solutions to (
1.3
) when
p=-n
. This means that, when
p\geq-n
and
f
is a positive constant function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, the even solution
\varphi
to (
1.3
) must be a constant. Moreover, the argument in
[
LX22
, p. 99]
has already classified all the solutions when
f
is constant. In particular, (
1.3
) admits no h-convex solution either
p=n-2k
and
f\equiv c_{1}
with
c_{1}\geq 2^{k-n}
, or
p>2k-n
and
f\equiv c_{2}
with
c_{2}>(n-k)^{n-k}(2k+p-n)^{\frac{2k+p-n}{2}}/(n+p)^{\frac{n+p}{2}}
.

(3)

When
1\leq k\leq n-1
and
p=-n
, the evenness assumption of
f
in Theorem
1.1
is natural. When
k=n-1
and
p=-n
, Espinar, Gálvez and Mira
[
EGM09
]
proved a Kazdan-Warner type obstruction for equation (
1.3
) by showing the relationship between this equation with the Nirenberg problem. Later, Li and Xu
[
LX22
, Thm. 9.1]
demonstrated this obstruction for the case that
0\leq k\leq n-1
and
p=-n
. In particular, equation (
1.3
) admits no solution when
0\leq k\leq n-1
,
p=-n
, and
f
is a monotone rotationally symmetric function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Let us also mention that the progress on the counterpart of
L_{p}
-Christoffel-Minkowski problem, which is the prescribed
L_{p}
Weingarten curvature problem: Given a smooth positive function
f
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, is there a closed, strictly convex hypersurface
\mathcal{M}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
such that

(1.6)

\displaystyle p_{n-k}(\mathcal{W}(\mathbf{n}^{-1}(x)))=h^{1-p}f^{-1}\quad\text{on $\mathbb{S}^{n}$}\quad?

Here
\mathbf{n}:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n}
is the Gauss map of
\mathcal{M}
, and
\mathcal{W}=(h_{i}^{j})=(g^{jk}h_{ki})
is the Weingarten matrix at the point
\mathbf{n}^{-1}(x)\in\mathcal{M}
. This is equivalent to

(1.7)

\displaystyle\frac{p_{n}(D^{2}h+h\sigma)}{p_{k}(D^{2}h+h\sigma)}=h^{p-1}f\quad\text{on $\mathbb{S}^{n}$}.

When
k=0
, it is the well-known
L_{p}
Minkowski problem, which was put forward by Lutwak
[
Lut93
]
in his seminal work on the
L_{p}
Brunn-Minkowski theory, see e.g.,
[
CW06
,
GLW22
,
HLYZ05
,
JLZ16
,
LYZ04
]
and a comprehensive survey by Böröczky
[
Bor23
]
. For
1\leq k\leq n-1
and
p=1
, the existence of the solution to (
1.7
) was first studied by Guan and Guan
[
GG02
]
under some group-invariant assumption on
f
(e.g.,
f
is even). For
p>n-k+1
and without the evenness assumption on
f
, the existence of a strictly convex solution to (
1.7
) was established through an elliptic method by
[
GRW15
]
, as well as a flow approach by
[
BIS21
]
. The existence of origin-symmetric strictly convex solution to (
1.7
) with
p=n-k+1
and
1<p<n-k+1
was recently proved by Lee
[
Lee24
]
and Hu-Ivaki
[
HI24
]
, respectively.

The following question can be considered as the natural analog of the prescribed
L_{p}
-Weingarten curvature problem in hyperbolic space:

Given a smooth positive function
f
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, is there a smooth, strictly horospherically convex hypersurface
\mathcal{M}
satisfying equation

(1.8)

f(z)~\varphi^{(n+p)}p_{n-k}(\mathcal{W}(G^{-1}(z))-\sigma)=1?

Here
\log\varphi:\mathbb{S}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the horospherical support function of
\mathcal{M}
defined by (
2.7
), and
G:\mathcal{M}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n}
is the horospherical Gauss map
. Equation (
1.8
) can be equivalently rewritten as

(1.9)

\displaystyle\frac{p_{n}(A[\varphi])}{p_{k}(A[\varphi])}=\varphi^{k+p}f(z)\quad\text{on $\mathbb{S}^{n}$}.

We call the above problem the prescribed
p
-shifted Weingarten curvature problem in hyperbolic space.
When
k=0
, it is the horospherical
p
-Minkowski problem proposed by Li and Xu
[
LX22
, Prob. 5.1]
, who also proved the existence of origin-symmetric solution for all
p\in(-\infty,+\infty)
up to a constant
[
LX22
, Thm. 7.2]
. Note that

(1.10)

\displaystyle|\text{Jac}\ G|=\left(\det A[\varphi]\right)^{-1}=\varphi(z)^{n}p_{n}(\mathcal{W}(G^{-1}(z))-\sigma),

where
|\text{Jac}\ G|
denotes the determinant of the Jacobian of the horospherical Gauss map
G
. Hence, equation (
1.8
) is called the prescribed horospherical surface area measure problem when
k=0
and
p=0
. For more recent progress on the horospherical
p
-Minkowski problem, readers may refer to
[
Che23a
,
LW23
,
LWX23
]
. Another special case of equation (
1.8
) is
p=-n
, which is called the prescribed shifted Weingarten curvature equation, was recently studied by Chen
[
Che23b
]
.

In the second part of this paper, we prove the existence result of the prescribed
p
-shifted Weingarten curvature problem (
1.8
) for all
0\leq k\leq n-1
and
p\geq-n
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
1\leq k\leq n-1
be integers, and let
p\geq-n
be a real number. Assume that
f(z)
is a smooth, positive and even function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. If
p\geq n-2k
, we assume in addition that
f
satisfies Assumption
1.2
. Then the prescribed
p
-shifted Weingarten curvature problem (
1.8
) admits a smooth, even and strictly horospherically convex solution.

Remark 1.1
.

When
p=-n
, Theorem
1.2
reduces to Theorem 1.1 in
[
Che23b
]
.

Just as the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem is the intersection of the
L_{p}
-Christoffel-Minkowski problem (
1.2
) and the prescribed Weingarten curvature problem (
1.7
) in Euclidean space, the horospherical
p
-Minkowski problem is the intersection of the horospherical
p
-Christoffel-Minkowski problem (
1.3
) and the prescribed
p
-shifted Weingarten curvature problem (
1.8
) in hyperbolic space.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer. Let
f(z)
be a smooth, positive and even function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Assume that one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

-n\leq p<n
;

(2)

p=n
, assume that
0<f<2^{-n}
;

(3)

p>n
, assume that
0<f<\frac{(p-n)^{\frac{p-n}{2}}n^{n}}{(p+n)^{\frac{p+n}{2}}}
.

Then the horospherical
p
-Minkowski problem (i.e.
k=0
in (
1.3
))

(1.11)

\displaystyle\varphi^{-p}p_{n}(A[\varphi])=f\quad\text{on $\mathbb{S}^{n}$}

admits a smooth, even and strictly horospherically convex solution.

Remark 1.2
.

By assuming
f
satisfies Assumption
1.2
when
p\geq n
, Theorem
1.3
removes the constant
\gamma
for the case
p\geq-n
in
[
LX22
, Thm. 7.2]
. When
-n\leq p<n
, Theorem
1.3
reduces to
[
Che23a
, Thm. 1.3]
.

With the uniqueness result of the isotropic solution to horospherical
p
-Minkowski problem in hyperbolic plane
[
LW24
]
, we establish the following existence result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
n=1
. Let
f(z)
be a smooth, positive and even function on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Assume that one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

-7\leq p<1
;

(2)

p=1
, assume that
0<f<2^{-1}
;

(3)

p>1
, assume that
0<f<\frac{(p-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}}{(p+1)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}}
.

Then the horospherical
p
-Minkowski problem in hyperbolic plane

(1.12)

\displaystyle\varphi^{-p}\left(\varphi_{\theta\theta}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\varphi_{\theta}^{2}}{\varphi}+\frac{\varphi-\varphi^{-1}}{2}\right)=f\quad\text{on $\mathbb{S}^{1}$}

admits a smooth, even and strictly horospherically convex solution.

Remark 1.3
.

The range of
p
in Theorem
1.4
is optimal in view of the invertiblity of the linearized operator in degree theory, see Remark
6.2
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we will collect some basic properties of horospherically convex hypersurfaces and elementary symmetric polynomials. In Section 3, we will derive the a priori
C^{2}
estimates for h-convex solutions to equations (
1.3
) and (
1.8
). In Section 4, we will establish a deformation lemma for equation (
1.3
), and then use it to obtain a full rank theorem for (
1.3
) in Section 5. In Section 6, we will use the degree theory for nonlinear elliptic operators to prove Theorems
1.1
–
1.4
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Horospherically convex hypersurfaces

Let
(\mathbb{S}^{n},\sigma,D)
denote the
n
-dimensional unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
equipped with its canonical metric
\sigma
and Levi-Civita connection
D
. The Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
is an
(n+2)
-dimensional vector space equipped with the Lorentzian metric

(2.1)

\displaystyle\langle X,Y\rangle:=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{i}y_{i}-x_{n+2}y_{n+2},

where
X=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1},x_{n+2})
and
Y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n+1},y_{n+2})
.

Let
(\mathbb{H}^{n+1},\bar{g},\bar{\nabla})
denote the
(n+1)
-dimensional hyperbolic space equipped with the metric
\bar{g}
and Levi-Civita connection
\bar{\nabla}
. The hyperboloid model of the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is given by

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{n+1}=\{X=(x,x_{n+2})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}~|~\langle X,X\rangle=-1,\ x_{n+2}>0\},

where
X=(x,x_{n+2})=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n+1},x_{n+2})
. The horospheres are complete hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
with principal curvatures equal to
1
everywhere, and the set of horospheres can be parameterized by
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
:

(2.3)

\displaystyle H_{z}(s)=\{X\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}~|~-\langle X,(z,1)\rangle=e^{s}\},\quad z\in\mathbb{S}^{n},\ s\in\mathbb{R},

where
s
is the signed geodesic distance from the north pole
N=(0,1)
to
H_{z}(s)
, and
z
is called the center of
H_{z}(s)
. It is worth noting that, in the Poincaré ball model
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
of the hyperbolic space, the horosphere
H_{z}(s)
is a sphere tangential to
\partial\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
at
z
. Define the horo-ball
B_{z}(s)
enclosed by
H_{z}(s)
as

(2.4)

\displaystyle B_{z}(s)=\{X\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}~|~-\langle X,(z,1)\rangle<e^{s}\},\quad z\in\mathbb{S}^{n},\ s\in\mathbb{R}.

Sometimes, it is also convenient to express the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
as the warped product
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
equipped with the metric

(2.5)

\displaystyle\bar{g}=dr^{2}+\sinh^{2}r\sigma,

where
r
is the geodesic distance to the north pole
N=(0,1)
. It is known that
V=\bar{\nabla}\cosh r=\sinh r\partial_{r}
is a conformal Killing field, i.e.,
\bar{\nabla}(\sinh r\partial_{r})=\cosh r\bar{g}
.

A bounded domain
\Omega
(as well as its boundary
\mathcal{M}=\partial\Omega
) in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is called
horospherically convex
(
h-convex
for short) if for each
X\in\mathcal{M}
, there is a horosphere enclosing
\Omega
and touching
\Omega
at
X
. When
\Omega
is smooth, it is equivalent to the fact that the principal curvatures of
\mathcal{M}
are greater than or equal to
1
. We call a smooth domain
\Omega
(or
\mathcal{M}=\partial\Omega
)
strictly h-convex
if the principal curvatures of
\mathcal{M}
are greater than
1
. A result of Curry
[
Cur89
]
shows that an h-convex, complete, immersed hypersurface in hyperbolic space is necessarily embedded and, if noncompact, it must be a horosphere. Therefore, any strictly h-convex hypersurface must be closed and hence it is uniformly h-convex, which means that all the principal curvatures exceed
1+\delta
for some
\delta>0
.

Now we collect some definitions and properties related to h-convex hypersurfaces in hyperbolic space. We refer to
[
ACW21
, Sect. 5]
,
[
LX22
, Sect. 2]
, and
[
EGM09
]
for details.
Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be a smooth, strictly h-convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Denote by
\nu
the unit outward normal of
\mathcal{M}
. Then the
horospherical Gauss map

G:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
is defined by the unique point
z=G(X)\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
such that

(2.6)

\displaystyle X-\nu=e^{-\lambda}(z,1),

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is the signed geodesic distance from
N=(0,1)
to the horosphere tangential to
\mathcal{M}
at
X
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Note that the uniform h-convexity of
\mathcal{M}
implies that the horospherical Gauss map
G
is a diffeomorphism. The
horospherical support function
of
\mathcal{M}
is then defined by

(2.7)

\displaystyle u(z):=\log(-\langle G^{-1}(z),(z,1)\rangle),\quad z\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Then
\lambda(G^{-1}(z))=u(z)
. For convenience, we set
\varphi(z):=e^{u(z)}
. Denote by
h_{ij}
the second fundamental form of
\mathcal{M}
, and let
h_{i}{}^{j}=h_{il}g^{lj}
. Then

(2.8)

\left(h_{i}{}^{j}(G^{-1}(z))-\delta_{i}{}^{j}\right)A_{jl}[\varphi(z)]=\frac{1}{\varphi(z)}\sigma_{il},\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{S}^{n},

where
A[\varphi]
is a symmetric
2
-tensor on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
defined by

(2.9)

A[\varphi]:=D^{2}\varphi-\frac{1}{2}\frac{|D\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi}\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi-\frac{1}{\varphi}\right)\sigma.

Hence, the strict h-convexity of
\mathcal{M}
implies that
A[\varphi]
is positive definite on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Conversely, for any positive
\varphi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
with
A[\varphi]>0
, we can recover the hypersurface
\mathcal{M}
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
through the embedding

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{n}\ni z\mapsto\frac{\varphi}{2}(-z,1)+\left(\frac{|D\varphi|^{2}}{2\varphi}+\frac{1}{2\varphi}\right)(z,1)-(D\varphi,0)\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}.

Denote by
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{n})
the principal curvatures of
\mathcal{M}
, which are the eigenvalues of the Weingarten matrix
\left(h_{i}{}^{j}\right)
. Then the shifted principal curvature
\tilde{\kappa}=(\tilde{\kappa}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\kappa}_{n})=(\kappa_{1}-1,\ldots,\kappa_{n}-1)
are the eigenvalues of the matrix
\left(h_{i}{}^{j}-\delta_{i}{}^{j}\right)
. In view of (
2.8
), the matrix
\left(h_{i}{}^{j}(G^{-1}(z))-\delta_{i}{}^{j}\right)
is the inverse matrix of
\varphi(z)\sigma^{jl}A_{li}[\varphi(z)]
.

Now, we derive some useful formulas for
A[\varphi]
. For simplicity, we take

(2.11)

\displaystyle\psi=\frac{1}{2}\frac{|D\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi+\frac{1}{\varphi}\right).

Then we have

(2.12)

\displaystyle A_{ij}[\varphi]=\varphi_{ij}+(\varphi-\psi)\sigma_{ij}.

In the following, we take an orthonormal basis and assume that
A_{ij}=A_{ij}[\varphi]
is diagonal at the point where we calculate; that is,
\sigma_{ij}=\delta_{ij}
and
A_{ij}=\lambda_{i}\delta_{ij}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

We have

(2.13)

\displaystyle\psi_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi_{i}}{\varphi}A_{ii}=\frac{\lambda_{i}\varphi_{i}}{\varphi},

(2.14)

\displaystyle A_{ij,l}-A_{il,j}=-\frac{\varphi_{l}}{\varphi}A_{ll}\delta_{ij}+\frac{\varphi_{j}}{\varphi}A_{jj}\delta_{il},

(2.15)

\displaystyle\psi_{ii}

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{ii}^{2}}{\varphi}+\left(\psi-\varphi\right)\frac{A_{ii}}{\varphi}-2\frac{\varphi_{i}^{2}}{\varphi^{2}}A_{ii}+\sum_{l}\frac{\varphi_{l}}{\varphi}A_{ii,l}+\sum_{l}\frac{\varphi_{l}^{2}}{\varphi^{2}}A_{ll}.

and

(2.16)

\displaystyle A_{ii,\alpha\alpha}-A_{\alpha\alpha,ii}=

\displaystyle\sum_{l}\frac{\varphi_{l}}{\varphi}(A_{ii,l}-A_{\alpha\alpha,l})+\frac{1}{\varphi}(A_{ii}^{2}-A_{\alpha\alpha}^{2})

\displaystyle+\frac{\psi}{\varphi}(A_{ii}-A_{\alpha\alpha})-\frac{2}{\varphi^{2}}(A_{ii}\varphi_{i}^{2}-A_{\alpha\alpha}\varphi_{\alpha}^{2}).

Proof.

Using the definition of
\psi
in (
2.11
), we have

(2.17)

\displaystyle\psi_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi_{l}\varphi_{li}}{\varphi}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{|D\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi^{2}}\varphi_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi_{i}-\frac{1}{\varphi^{2}}\varphi_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi_{l}}{\varphi}\left(\varphi_{li}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{|D\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi}\delta_{li}+\frac{1}{2}(\varphi-\frac{1}{\varphi})\delta_{li}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi_{l}}{\varphi}A_{li}=\frac{\varphi_{i}}{\varphi}A_{ii}=\frac{\lambda_{i}\varphi_{i}}{\varphi}.

Thus we obtain (
2.13
). Then formula (
2.14
) follows from (
2.13
) and the well-known fact that
D(D^{2}\varphi+\varphi\sigma)
is a Codazzi tensor on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
,

(2.18)

\displaystyle A_{ij,l}-A_{il,j}

\displaystyle=D_{l}(\varphi_{ij}+\varphi\delta_{ij})-\psi_{l}\delta_{ij}-D_{j}(\varphi_{il}+\varphi\delta_{il})+\psi_{j}\delta_{il}

\displaystyle=-\psi_{l}\delta_{ij}+\psi_{j}\delta_{il}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\varphi_{l}}{\varphi}A_{ll}\delta_{ij}+\frac{\varphi_{j}}{\varphi}A_{jj}\delta_{il}.

Using (
2.13
), for fixed index
i
we have,

(2.19)

\displaystyle\psi_{ii}

\displaystyle=D_{i}\left(\frac{\varphi_{l}}{\varphi}A_{li}\right)=\frac{\varphi_{li}}{\varphi}A_{li}-\frac{\varphi_{l}\varphi_{i}A_{li}}{\varphi^{2}}+\frac{\varphi_{l}}{\varphi}A_{li,i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi_{ii}}{\varphi}A_{ii}-\frac{\varphi_{i}^{2}}{\varphi^{2}}A_{ii}+\sum_{l}\frac{\varphi_{l}}{\varphi}A_{li,i}.

By (
2.12
), we have

(2.20)

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi_{ii}}{\varphi}A_{ii}=\frac{A_{ii}^{2}}{\varphi}+\left(\frac{\psi}{\varphi}-1\right)A_{ii}.

On the other hand, it follows from (
2.14
) that

(2.21)

\displaystyle\sum_{l}\frac{\varphi_{l}}{\varphi}A_{li,i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}\frac{\varphi_{l}}{\varphi}\left(A_{ii,l}-\frac{\varphi_{i}}{\varphi}A_{ii}\delta_{li}+\frac{\varphi_{l}}{\varphi}A_{ll}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}\frac{\varphi_{l}A_{ii,l}}{\varphi}-\frac{\varphi_{i}^{2}}{\varphi^{2}}A_{ii}+\sum_{l}\frac{\varphi_{l}^{2}}{\varphi^{2}}A_{ll}.

Inserting the above two formulas into (
2.19
), we obtain (
2.15
).
Note that

(2.22)

\displaystyle A_{ii,\alpha\alpha}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{ii,\alpha\alpha}+\varphi_{\alpha\alpha}-\psi_{\alpha\alpha},

\displaystyle A_{\alpha\alpha,ii}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\alpha\alpha,ii}+\varphi_{ii}-\psi_{ii}.

Using the Ricci identity for tensors on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, we have

(2.23)

\displaystyle\varphi_{ii,\alpha\alpha}-\varphi_{\alpha\alpha,ii}=2\varphi_{ii}-2\varphi_{\alpha\alpha},

and thus

(2.24)

\displaystyle A_{ii,\alpha\alpha}-A_{\alpha\alpha,ii}

\displaystyle=(\varphi_{ii,\alpha\alpha}-\varphi_{\alpha\alpha,ii})+(\varphi_{\alpha\alpha}-\varphi_{ii})+\psi_{ii}-\psi_{\alpha\alpha}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{ii}-\varphi_{\alpha\alpha}+\psi_{ii}-\psi_{\alpha\alpha}

\displaystyle=A_{ii}-A_{\alpha\alpha}+\psi_{ii}-\psi_{\alpha\alpha}.

The formula (
2.16
) follows by inserting (
2.15
) into the above formula. This completes the proof of Lemma
2.1
.
∎

2.2.
Elementary symmetric polynomials

The
k
th elementary symmetric polynomial
S_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

(2.25)

\displaystyle S_{k}(\lambda):=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}},\quad k=1,2,\ldots,n.

We also set
S_{0}=1
and
S_{k}=0
if either
k<0
or
k>n
by convention. This definition of
S_{k}
can be extended to symmetric matrices
A
as follows (see
[
GM03
, Prop. 2.2]
): Let
A=(A_{ij})
be an
(n\times n)
symmetric matrix,
S_{k}
can be defined as

(2.26)

\displaystyle S_{k}(A)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}=1\\
j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}=1\end{subarray}}^{n}\delta_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}^{j_{1}\ldots j_{k}}A_{i_{1}j_{1}}\ldots A_{i_{k}j_{k}}.

where
\delta_{I}^{J}
for the indices
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})
and
J=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})
is defined as

(2.27)

\displaystyle\delta_{I}^{J}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,\quad&\text{if $I$ is an even permutation of $J$};\\
&-1,\quad&\text{if $I$ is an odd permutation of $J$};\\
&0,\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{aligned}\right.

When
A
is diagonal, then
S_{k}(A)=S_{k}(\lambda(A))
, where
\lambda(A)=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
are the eigenvalues of the matrix
A
. Sometimes it is useful to express the derivatives of
S_{k}
explicitly (see
[
GM03
, Prop. 2.2]
):

(2.28)

\displaystyle S_{k}^{ij}(A)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\partial S_{k}(A)}{\partial A_{ij}}=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}=1\\
j_{1},\ldots,j_{k-1}=1\end{subarray}}^{n}\delta_{ii_{1}\ldots i_{k-1}}^{jj_{1}\ldots j_{k-1}}A_{i_{1}j_{1}}\ldots A_{i_{k-1}j_{k-1}},

\displaystyle S_{k}^{ij,rs}(A)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\partial^{2}S_{k}(A)}{\partial A_{ij}\partial A_{rs}}=\frac{1}{(k-2)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-2}=1\\
j_{1},\ldots,j_{k-2}=1\end{subarray}}^{n}\delta_{iri_{1}\ldots i_{k-2}}^{jsj_{1}\ldots j_{k-2}}A_{i_{1}j_{1}}\ldots A_{i_{k-2}j_{k-2}},

When
A
is diagonal,
S_{k}^{ij}(A)
is diagonal with

(2.29)

\displaystyle S_{k}^{ij}(A)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &S_{k-1}(A|i),\quad&{\rm if}\ i=j,\\
&0,\quad&{\rm otherwise},\end{aligned}\right.

and

(2.30)

\displaystyle S_{k}^{ij,rs}(A)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &S_{k-2}(A|ir),\quad&{\rm if}\ i=j,r=s,i\neq r,\\
&-S_{k-2}(A|ij),\quad&{\rm if}\ i=s,j=r,i\neq j,\\
&0,\quad&{\rm otherwise}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here
S_{k-1}(A|i)
is the symmetric function with
\lambda_{i}=0
, and
S_{k-2}(A|ij)
is the symmetric function with
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{j}=0
.

Let
F
denote a symmetric, smooth functions of the eigenvalues of
(A_{i}{}^{j})=(\sigma^{jk}A_{ij})
or as depending on
A=(A_{ij})
and the spherical metric
\sigma=(\sigma_{ij})
,

(2.31)

\displaystyle F=F(\lambda_{i})=F(A_{i}{}^{j})=F(\sigma,A)

Denote by

(2.32)

\displaystyle F_{i}^{j}:=\frac{\partial F}{\partial A_{j}{}^{i}},\quad F^{ij}:=\frac{\partial F}{\partial A_{ij}},\quad F^{ij,kl}:=\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial A_{ij}\partial A_{kl}}.

We collect some basic formulas for the elementary symmetric polynomial
S_{k}

(1\leq k\leq n)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
Gua13
]

For any integer
1\leq k\leq n
, we have

(2.33)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}(S_{k})_{i}^{j}(A)\delta_{j}{}^{i}=

\displaystyle~(n-k+1)S_{k-1}(A),

(2.34)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}(S_{k})_{i}^{j}(A)A_{j}{}^{i}=

\displaystyle~kS_{k}(A),

(2.35)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}(S_{k})_{i}^{j}(A)A_{j}{}^{k}A_{k}{}^{i}=

\displaystyle~S_{1}(A)S_{k}(A)-(k+1)S_{k+1}(A).

Define the Gårding cone by

\Gamma_{m}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:~S_{i}(x)>0,\ 1\leq i\leq m\},\quad m=1,\ldots,n.

Equivalently,
\Gamma_{m}
is the connected component of
\{x:~S_{m}(x)>0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
which contains the positive cone

\Gamma_{n}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:~x_{i}>0,\ 1\leq i\leq n\}.

The normalized
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial
p_{k}
is defined by
p_{k}(\lambda)=\binom{n}{k}^{-1}S_{k}(\lambda)
for all
1\leq k\leq n
, and
p_{0}=1
and
p_{k}=0
if either
i<0
or
i>n
by convention.

We assume that
F
is a strictly increasing, homogeneous of degree one, and concave function of
\lambda(A)
in some open cone
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing the positive cone
\Gamma_{n}
, and it is normalized that
F(1,\ldots,1)=1
.
Then we have

(2.36)

\displaystyle F^{ij}>0,\quad F^{ij}A_{ij}=F,\quad{F}^{ij,kl}\leq 0,

and

(2.37)

\displaystyle\sum F^{ii}\geq 1.

It is well known that if the above
F
is chosen as
p_{n-k}^{1/(n-k)}
and
(p_{n}/p_{k})^{1/(n-k)}
, then the corresponding open cones
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
are
\Gamma_{n-k}
and
\Gamma_{n}
, respectively. We refer to
[
Ger06b
, Chap. 2]
for a detailed account and common properties of
F
.

3.
A priori estimates

Throughout this section, we consider the positive, even solution
\varphi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
to the following equation:

(3.1)

F(A[\varphi])=\varphi^{q}f^{\frac{1}{n-k}},\quad q:=\frac{n+p}{n-k}-1,

where
F=p_{n-k}^{1/(n-k)}
or
F=(p_{n}/p_{k})^{1/(n-k)}
, and
f
is a smooth positive function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
that satisfies Assumption
1.2
when
q\geq 1
(i.e.
p\geq n-2k
). Moreover, if
F=p_{n-k}^{1/(n-k)}
, we assume that
A[\varphi]\in\Gamma_{n-k}\cap\overline{\Gamma}_{n}
;
With the help of the full rank theorem (Theorem
5.1
), this equation (
3.1
) corresponds to the horospherical
p
-Christoffel-Minkowski problem (
1.3
). If
F=(p_{n}/p_{k})^{1/(n-k)}
, we assume that
A[\varphi]\in\Gamma_{n}
; This equation (
3.1
) corresponds to the prescribed
p
-shifted Weingarten curvature problem (
1.8
).

The horospherical support function
u(z)
of an h-convex domain
\Omega
is defined by

(3.2)

\displaystyle u(z)=\inf\{s\in\mathbb{R}:~\Omega\subset\overline{B}_{z}(s)\},\quad z\in\mathbb{S}^{n},

which is exactly (
2.7
) when
\partial\Omega
is smooth and strictly h-convex.
It was proved in
[
LX22
, Prop. 2.1]
that
\log\varphi
is the horospherical support function of a non-empty, h-convex domain
\Omega:=\cap_{z\in\mathbb{S}^{n}}\overline{B}_{z}\left(\log\varphi(z)\right)
when
A[\varphi]
is positive semi-definite on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. And it was proved in
[
LX22
, Cor. 2.3]
that the map
X:\mathbb{S}^{n}\rightarrow\partial\Omega
given by (
2.10
) is surjective. If
\varphi
is in addition even on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, i.e.,
\varphi(z)=\varphi(-z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, then we have that
\varphi\geq 1
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
as
N=(0,1)\in\Omega
. Moreover, if
\varphi(z_{0})=1
for some
z_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
then
\varphi(z)\equiv 1
,
A[\varphi(z)]\equiv 0
, and
\cap_{z\in\mathbb{S}^{n}}\overline{B}_{z}(\log\varphi(z))
is exactly the point
(0,1)\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Hence we can assume that
\varphi(z)>1
in the a priori estimate. By using
X(z_{1})\in\overline{B}_{z}\left(\log\varphi(z)\right)
,
\forall z\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, where
z_{1}
is a maximum point of
\varphi
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and the origin-symmetry of
\Omega
, it was proved in
[
LX22
, Lem. 7.2]
that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\cosh\left(\log\varphi_{\max}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\varphi_{\min},

where

(3.4)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\max}:=\max_{z\in\mathbb{S}^{n}}\varphi(z),\quad\varphi_{\min}:=\min_{z\in\mathbb{S}^{n}}\varphi(z).

Then, as an application of the above (
3.3
), it was proved in
[
LX22
, Lem. 7.3]
that

(3.5)

\displaystyle|D\log\varphi(z)|<1,\quad\forall\ z\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Note that the Assumption
1.2
on
f
can be reformulated as follows: assume
0<f<2^{k-n}
when
q=1
, and assume
0<f<(\frac{(q+1)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}}{(q-1)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}})^{k-n}
when
q>1
.

Lemma 3.1

(
C^{0}
-estimate)
.

We have

(3.6)

1+\frac{1}{C}\leq\varphi\leq C,

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
k
,
p
,
f_{\min}
and
\|f\|_{C^{0}}
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we define the function
\xi_{q}:(1,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
by

(3.7)

\displaystyle\xi_{q}(t)=2t^{q}(t-t^{-1})^{-1}.

Assume that
\varphi
attains its maximum at
z_{1}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Then at
z_{1}
, we have
\varphi=\varphi_{\max}
,
D\varphi=0
and
D^{2}\varphi\leq 0
. Thus, at the point
z_{1}
,

(3.8)

\displaystyle(f_{\min})^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}\geq\varphi_{\max}^{q}F^{-1}\geq 2\varphi_{\max}^{q}\left(\varphi_{\max}-\varphi_{\max}^{-1}\right)^{-1}=\xi_{q}(\varphi_{\max}).

Similarly, at a minimum point
z_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
of
\varphi
, we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle(f_{\max})^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}\leq\varphi_{\min}^{q}F^{-1}\leq 2\varphi_{\min}^{q}\left(\varphi_{\min}-\varphi_{\min}^{-1}\right)^{-1}=\xi_{q}(\varphi_{\min}).

(1)

If
q<1
, then
\xi_{q}(t)
is strictly decreasing with
\xi_{q}(1^{+})=+\infty
and
\xi_{q}(+\infty)=0
. Thus, we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\max}\geq\xi_{q}^{-1}((f_{\min})^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}),\quad\varphi_{\min}\leq\xi_{q}^{-1}((f_{\max})^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}).

(2)

If
q=1
, then
\xi_{q}(t)
is strictly decreasing with
\xi_{q}(1^{+})=+\infty
and
\xi_{q}(+\infty)=2
. Since we assume
0<f<2^{k-n}
in this case, it holds that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\max}\geq\xi_{q}^{-1}((f_{\min})^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}),\quad\varphi_{\min}\leq\xi_{q}^{-1}((f_{\max})^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}).

(3)

If
q>1
, then
\xi_{q}(t)
is strictly decreasing on
(1,\sqrt{\frac{q+1}{q-1}}]
, and it is strictly increasing on
[\sqrt{\frac{q+1}{q-1}},\infty)
. Moreover,
\xi_{q}(1^{+})=\infty
,
\xi_{q}(\sqrt{\frac{q+1}{q-1}})=\frac{(q+1)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}}{(q-1)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}}
and
\xi_{q}(+\infty)=\infty
. Denote by
\xi_{q,-}^{-1}
and
\xi_{q,+}^{-1}
the inverse function of
\xi_{q}
on the interval
(1,\sqrt{\frac{q+1}{q-1}}]
and
[\sqrt{\frac{q+1}{q-1}},\infty)
respectively. Since we assume that
0<f<\left(\frac{(q+1)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}}{(q-1)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}}\right)^{k-n}
in this case, it holds that

•

If
\varphi_{\max}\leq\sqrt{\frac{q+1}{q-1}}
, then

(3.12)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\max}\geq\xi_{q,-}^{-1}((f_{\min})^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}),\quad\varphi_{\min}\leq\xi_{q,-}^{-1}((f_{\max})^{-\frac{1}{n-k}});

•

If
\varphi_{\min}\geq\sqrt{\frac{q+1}{q-1}}
, then

(3.13)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{q+1}{q-1}}\leq\varphi_{\min}\leq\varphi_{\max}\leq\xi_{q,+}^{-1}((f_{\min})^{-\frac{1}{n-k}});

•

If
\varphi_{\min}\leq\sqrt{\frac{q+1}{q-1}}\leq\varphi_{\max}
, then

(3.14)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\min}\leq\xi_{q,-}^{-1}((f_{\max})^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}),\quad\sqrt{\frac{q+1}{q-1}}\leq\varphi_{\max}\leq\xi_{q,+}^{-1}((f_{\min})^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}).

Then by (
3.3
), there exists a constant
C>0

Lemma 4.1

(Deformation lemma)
.

Let

\displaystyle

\displaystyle-D^{2}(f^{-\frac{1}{k}})-\frac{2(k-p-n)}{k}d(f^{-\frac{1}{k}})d\log\varphi

\displaystyle F=S_{k}(A)=h=\varphi^{-t}f,\quad t=k-p-n.

Fix
i\in B
. Taking the covariant derivatives of
h=\varphi^{-t}f
, we get

(4.68)

\displaystyle h_{i}=

\displaystyle-t\varphi^{-t-1}\varphi_{i}f+\varphi^{-t}f_{i},

\displaystyle h_{ii}=

\displaystyle t(t+1)\varphi^{-t-2}\varphi_{i}^{2}f-2t\varphi^{-t-1}\varphi_{i}f_{i}-t\varphi^{-t-1}f\varphi_{ii}+\varphi^{-t}f_{ii},

\displaystyle\frac{h_{i}^{2}}{h}=

\displaystyle t^{2}\varphi^{-t-2}\varphi_{i}^{2}f+\varphi^{-t}\frac{f_{i}^{2}}{f}-2t\varphi^{-t-1}f_{i}\varphi_{i}.

Since
\lambda_{i}\sim 0
for
i\in B
by (
4.7
), we have

\varphi_{ii}\sim\frac{1}{2}\frac{|D\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi-\frac{1}{\varphi}\right).

Then we get

(4.69)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle h_{ii}-\frac{k+1}{k}\frac{h_{i}^{2}}{h}-\frac{\langle Dh,D\varphi\rangle}{\varphi}-\frac{\psi}{\varphi}kh

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t(t+1)\varphi^{-t-2}\varphi_{i}^{2}f-2t\varphi^{-t-1}\varphi_{i}f_{i}-t\varphi^{-t-1}f\varphi_{ii}+\varphi^{-t}f_{ii}

\displaystyle-\frac{k+1}{k}t^{2}\varphi^{-t-2}\varphi_{i}^{2}f-\frac{k+1}{k}\varphi^{-t}\frac{f_{i}^{2}}{f}+2\frac{k+1}{k}t\varphi^{-t-1}f_{i}\varphi_{i}

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\varphi_{j}}{\varphi}\left(-t\varphi^{-t-1}\varphi_{j}f+\varphi^{-t}f_{j}\right)-\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{|D\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}(1+\frac{1}{\varphi^{2}})\right)k\varphi^{-t}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi^{-t}f_{ii}-\frac{k+1}{k}\varphi^{-t}\frac{f_{i}^{2}}{f}+\frac{2t}{k}\varphi^{-t-1}\varphi_{i}f_{i}-\varphi^{-t-1}\langle Df,D\varphi\rangle-t\varphi^{-t-1}f\varphi_{ii}

\displaystyle+\frac{t(k-t)}{k}\varphi^{-t-2}\varphi_{i}^{2}f+(t-\frac{k}{2})\varphi^{-t-2}|D\varphi|^{2}f-\frac{k}{2}(1+\frac{1}{\varphi^{2}})\varphi^{-t}f

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\varphi^{-t}\left\{f_{ii}-\frac{k+1}{k}\frac{f_{i}^{2}}{f}+\frac{2t}{k}\frac{f_{i}\varphi_{i}}{\varphi}-\langle Df,\frac{D\varphi}{\varphi}\rangle\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\bigg(\frac{t(k-t)}{k}\frac{\varphi_{i}^{2}}{\varphi^{2}}-\frac{k-t}{2}\frac{|D\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi^{2}}-\frac{k-t}{2}-\frac{k+t}{2}\frac{1}{\varphi^{2}}\bigg)f\right\}.

Using the relation

\displaystyle f_{i}=-kf^{\frac{k+1}{k}}(f^{-\frac{1}{k}})_{i},\quad f_{ii}-\frac{k+1}{k}\frac{f_{i}^{2}}{f}=-kf^{\frac{k+1}{k}}(f^{-\frac{1}{k}})_{ii},

we rewrite the estimate (
4.69
) as

\displaystyle~h_{ii}-\frac{k+1}{k}\frac{h_{i}^{2}}{h}-\frac{\langle Dh,D\varphi\rangle}{\varphi}-\frac{\psi}{\varphi}kh

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle~-k\varphi^{-t}f^{\frac{k+1}{k}}\left\{(f^{-\frac{1}{k}})_{ii}+\frac{2t}{k}\frac{(f^{-\frac{1}{k}})_{i}\varphi_{i}}{\varphi}-\langle Df^{-\frac{1}{k}},\frac{D\varphi}{\varphi}\rangle\right.

\displaystyle~\left.-\bigg(\frac{t(k-t)}{k^{2}}\frac{\varphi_{i}^{2}}{\varphi^{2}}-\frac{k-t}{2k}\frac{|D\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi^{2}}-\frac{k-t}{2k}-\frac{k+t}{2k}\frac{1}{\varphi^{2}}\bigg)f^{-\frac{1}{k}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~-k\varphi^{n+p-k}f^{\frac{k+1}{k}}\left\{(f^{-\frac{1}{k}})_{ii}+\frac{2(k-p-n)}{k}(f^{-\frac{1}{k}})_{i}\frac{\varphi_{i}}{\varphi}-\langle Df^{-\frac{1}{k}},\frac{D\varphi}{\varphi}\rangle\right.

\displaystyle~\left.-\bigg(\frac{(k-p-n)(n+p)}{k^{2}}\frac{\varphi_{i}^{2}}{\varphi^{2}}-\frac{n+p}{2k}\frac{|D\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi^{2}}-\frac{n+p}{2k}-\frac{2k-p-n}{2k}\frac{1}{\varphi^{2}}\bigg)f^{-\frac{1}{k}}\right\},

where we used
t=k-p-n
in the last equality. We complete the proof of Lemma
4.6
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4.1
(Deformation lemma).

It follows from Lemma
4.6
and Lemma
4.7
.

5.
Full rank theorem

We have proved an upper bound of the matrix
A[\varphi]
of solutions to the horospherical
p
-Christoffel-Minkowski problem (
1.3
) in Lemma
3.3
. Different from the prescribed
p
-shifted Weingarten curvature problem, there is no such lower bound of the matrix
A[\varphi]
for the horospherical
p
-Christoffel-Minkowski problem in general. The main purpose of this section is to impose suitable structural assumptions on the prescribed function
f
such that any (weakly) h-convex, even solution
\varphi
to the horospherical
p
-Christoffel-Minkowski problem (
1.3
) is actually strictly h-convex, in another word,
A[\varphi]
is of full rank.

Theorem 5.1

(Full rank theorem)
.

Let
f
be a smooth, positive, even function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
satisfying Assumption
1.1
. Let
\varphi
be a
C^{4}
smooth, even solution to equation (
1.3
) with positive semi-definite
A[\varphi]
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Then
A[\varphi]>0
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

To prove Theorem
5.1
, we first use the deformation lemma
4.1
to show a differential inequality for solutions to (
1.3
) on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
when
f
satisfies Assumption
1.1
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose that
\varphi\in C^{4}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
is an even solution of the horospherical
p
-Christoffel-Minkowski problem (
1.3
) with
A[\varphi]
positive semi-definite, and
p_{\ell}(A[\varphi])\geq C_{0}
for some integer
n-k\leq\ell\leq n-1

f
is even on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and satisfies Assumption
1.1

,
C_{2}
depending only on

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{n}p_{n-k}^{\alpha\beta}\phi_{\alpha\beta}(z)\leq C_{1}|D\phi(z)|+C_{2}\phi(z)

Comparing (
1.3
) with equation (

) in the deformation lemma

\displaystyle

\displaystyle D^{2}(f^{-\frac{1}{n-k}})-\frac{2(k+p)}{n-k}d(f^{-\frac{1}{n-k}})d(\log\varphi)-\langle Df^{-\frac{1}{n-k}},D\log\varphi\rangle\sigma

\displaystyle+\frac{(k+p)(n+p)}{(n-k)^{2}}f^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}(d\log\varphi)^{2}+\left(\frac{n+p}{2(n-k)}\frac{|D\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi^{2}}+\frac{n+p}{2(n-k)}+\frac{n-2k-p}{2(n-k)}\frac{1}{\varphi^{2}}\right)f^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}\sigma

\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

When

, the matrix (

) is exactly
[

, Eq. (7.39)]
(up to

), and we can use
[

and

\displaystyle\xi_{-1}(\varphi_{\min})\geq\left(f_{\max}\right)^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}.

\displaystyle\varphi_{\min}\leq\left(1+2\left(f_{\max}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

such that

(6.16)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{C^{4,\alpha}}<R_{0},\quad p_{n-k}(A[\varphi])>\frac{1}{R_{0}}.

Suppose the claim is false. Then there always exists a sufficiently large constant
R\geq R_{0}
and a solution to equation
\mathcal{L}_{t}(\varphi)=0
such that
\varphi\in\partial\mathcal{O}_{R}
. Then we have

(6.17)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{C^{4,\alpha}}<R,\quad p_{n-k}(A[\varphi])>\frac{1}{R}.

So
\varphi\in\partial\mathcal{O}_{R}
implies that
\varphi\geq\sqrt{\frac{q+1}{q-1}}
and
\varphi(z_{0})=\sqrt{\frac{q+1}{q-1}}
is attained at some point
z_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
. At this point
z_{0}
, we have

(6.18)

\displaystyle A[\varphi(z_{0})]\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{\frac{q+1}{q-1}}-\sqrt{\frac{q-1}{q+1}}\right)\sigma,

and by Assumption
1.2
, we deduce that

(6.19)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(q+1)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}}{(q-1)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle>(f_{\max})^{\frac{1}{n-k}}\geq((f_{t})_{\max})^{\frac{1}{n-k}}

\displaystyle\geq f_{t}(z_{0})^{\frac{1}{n-k}}=\varphi(z_{0})^{-q}p_{n-k}^{\frac{1}{n-k}}(A[\varphi(z_{0})])

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{(q+1)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}}{(q-1)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}}\right)^{-1}.

This is a contradiction, and the claim follows.

Recall that
\mathcal{L}_{t}
is uniformly elliptic in
\mathcal{O}_{R}
, and hence the degree of
\mathcal{L}_{t}
on
\mathcal{O}_{R}
at
0
is well-defined for all
t\in[0,1]
. By
[
Li89
, Prop. 2.2]
, the degree
\deg(\mathcal{L}_{t},\mathcal{O}_{R},0)
is a homotopic invariant and thus

(6.20)

\displaystyle\deg(\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{R},0)=\deg(\mathcal{L}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{R},0).

Hence, it suffices to compute the degree at
t=0
. By Lemma
6.1
, the even, h-convex solutions to equation (
6.1
) are constant for any
q\geq-1
. Let
\varphi=c_{0}
be a constant solution to equation
\mathcal{L}_{0}\left(c_{0}\right)=0
, i.e.,

(6.21)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(c_{0}-c_{0}^{-1}\right)=c_{0}^{q}f_{0}^{\frac{1}{n-k}}=c_{0}^{q}(f_{\max})^{\frac{1}{n-k}}.

Note that Assumption
1.2
on
f_{\max}
guarantees the existence and uniqueness of the constant solution
\varphi=c_{0}
in
\mathcal{O}_{R}
. The linearized operator of
\mathcal{L}_{0}
at
\varphi=c_{0}
is

(6.22)

\displaystyle L_{c_{0}}\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\eta+\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2c_{0}^{2}}-q(f_{\max})^{\frac{1}{n-k}}c_{0}^{q-1}\right)\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\eta+\left(\frac{1-q}{2}+\frac{1+q}{2c_{0}^{2}}\right)\eta.

Note that for each
q\geq-1
, we have

(6.23)

\displaystyle\frac{1-q}{2}+\frac{1+q}{2c_{0}^{2}}\leq 1.

Since the eigenvalues of the Beltrami-Laplace operator
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
are strictly less than
-n
except for the first two eigenvalues
0
and
-n
, and the coordinate functions of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
span the eigenspace of eigenvalue
-n
are odd, equation
L_{c_{0}}\eta=0
only admits the unique even solution
\eta=0
. Thus the operator
L_{c_{0}}
is invertible at
\varphi=c_{0}
. By
[
Li89
, Prop. 2.3]
,
we have

(6.24)

\displaystyle\deg(\mathcal{L}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{R},0)=\deg(L_{c_{0}},\mathcal{O}_{R},0).

Moreover, there is only one positive eigenvalue
\frac{1-q}{2}+\frac{1+q}{2c_{0}^{2}}
of
L_{c_{0}}
with multiplicity
1
.
By
[
Li89
, Prop. 2.4]
, we know that

(6.25)

\displaystyle\deg(L_{c_{0}},\mathcal{O}_{R},0)=-1.

Finally, it follows from (
6.20
), (
6.24
) and (
6.25
) that

(6.26)

\displaystyle\deg(\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{R},0)=-1,

which implies that there exists a
C^{4,\alpha}
-smooth, strictly h-convex, even solution
\varphi\in\mathcal{O}_{R}
to equation

(6.27)

\displaystyle p_{n-k}^{\frac{1}{n-k}}(A[\varphi])=\varphi^{q}f^{\frac{1}{n-k}}.

The regularity of
\varphi
follows from Theorem
3.1
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

Note that the first eigenvalue of
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
for even functions are
-2(n+1)
, so for any
-4n-3\leq q<-1
, by
c_{0}>1
we have

(6.28)

\displaystyle 1<\frac{1-q}{2}+\frac{1+q}{2c_{0}^{2}}<2(n+1).

Therefore, the operator
L_{c_{0}}
is invertible at
\varphi=c_{0}
for all
q\geq-4n-3
. In particular, for
n=1
,
L_{c_{0}}
is invertible at
\varphi=c_{0}
for all
q\geq-7
.

Proof of Theorems
1.2
,
1.3
and
1.4
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
0\leq k\leq n-1
. We take

(6.29)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{t}(\varphi)=\left(\frac{p_{n}(A[\varphi])}{p_{k}(A[\varphi])}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-k}}-\varphi^{q}f_{t}^{\frac{1}{n-k}}.

If either
-1\leq q\leq 1
for
n\geq 2
, or
-7\leq q\leq 1
for
n=1
(in this case,
k=0
), we define

(6.30)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{R}:=\left\{w\in\mathcal{B}_{0}^{4,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})~|~\|w\|_{C^{4,\alpha}}<R,\ w>1+\frac{1}{R},\ \frac{p_{n}(A[w])}{p_{k}(A[w])}>\frac{1}{R}\right\};

If
q>1
for
n\geq 1
, we define

(6.31)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{R}:=\left\{w\in\mathcal{B}_{0}^{4,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})~|~\|w\|_{C^{4,\alpha}}<R,\ w>\sqrt{\frac{q+1}{q-1}},\ \frac{p_{n}(A[w])}{p_{k}(A[w])}>\frac{1}{R}\right\}.

Then we have
\mathcal{L}_{t}^{-1}(0)\cap\partial\mathcal{O}_{R}=\emptyset
if
R>0
is sufficiently large. Here, in order to show that the solution to equation
\mathcal{L}_{t}(\varphi)=0
satisfies
A[\varphi]>0
, we use Lemma
3.4
instead of the full rank theorem (Theorem
5.1
). For the case
n=1
and
-7\leq q\leq 1
, the uniqueness of the constant solution
\varphi=c_{0}
follows from Lemma
6.1
, and the operator
L_{c_{0}}
is invertible at
\varphi=c_{0}
when
q\geq-7
by Remark
6.2
. The remaining proof of Theorems
1.2
,
1.3
and
1.4
follows from the similar argument as that of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

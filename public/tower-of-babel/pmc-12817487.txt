Kidney injury risk in congenital solitary functioning kidney: the role of nephron mass assessed by serum uromodulin and the rs4293393 T > C polymorphism
Authors: Stefano Guarino, Paola Tirelli, Anna Di Sessa, Mariantonia Braile, Francesca Maisto, Pietro Gizzone, Pier Luigi Palma, Emanuele Miraglia del Giudice, Grazia Cirillo, Pierluigi Marzuillo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveWe hypothesized that serum uromodulin levels (sUMOD) and the uromodulin rs4293393 T > C polymorphism could aid in risk stratification for kidney injury (KI) in patients with a congenital solitary functioning kidney (CSFK), serving as markers of nephron mass. We tested this hypothesis in a cohort study of 56 CSFK patients followed from birth to adulthood. The rs4293393 polymorphism was genotyped using the TaqMan assay. Serum uromodulin was measured at last follow-up using ELISA. KI was defined by estimated glomerular filtration rate < 90 ml/min/1.73m2 and/or hypertension and/or proteinuria.ResultsOver a mean follow-up of 21.1years(range:18–33), 15 patients (26.8%) developed KI. Genotypes were TT (62.5%) and TC (37.5%). Higher sUMOD levels in early adulthood were linked to kidney length(KL) > 2SDS in early-life and absence of the C allele. Lower sUMOD levels were associated with KI and its components. Kaplan-Meier analysis showed 100% KI-free survival at 33years in patients with early KL > 2SDS. Survival dropped to 62.4% in those without early KL > 2SDS and wild-type genotype, and to 0% by 26years in C allele carriers (p = 0.001). The hazard ratio for KI in C allele carriers was 5.1(95%confidence interval:1.6–16.2;p = 0.006). Therefore, early-life kidney ultrasound, combined with rs4293393 genotyping might improve risk stratification for KI in patients with CSFK.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13104-025-07603-7.Keywords: Uromodulin, rs4293393 T > C polymorphism, Solitary kidney, Kidney injury

Background

There is no consensus regarding the prognosis of individuals with congenital solitary functioning kidney (CSFK). Some studies present an alarmist perspective [1, 2], while others offer a more optimistic outlook [3, 4], with findings varying depending on enrollment criteria, potential population bias, and the duration of follow-up. Irrespectively from the real prognosis, it is important to personalize the follow-up on the basis of the risk of developing kidney injury (KI) [5, 6]. The risk factors for KI in CSFK patients are similar to those identified for chronic kidney disease (CKD) and primarily impact nephron mass [7]; in fact, these patients have approximately half the nephron mass compared to individuals with two kidneys.

Non-invasive methods for quantifying nephron mass are unavailable. However, uromodulin serves as a useful indirect marker of nephron mass [8], and the rs4293393 T >C polymorphism in the uromodulin gene has been associated with KI [9, 10]. We hypothesized that both serum uromodulin (sUMOD) levels and this polymorphism may aid in identifying patients at lower risk of KI by indirectly quantifying nephron mass. To test this hypothesis, we conducted further analyses in a cohort of CSFK patients followed up from birth to adulthood [3].

Methods

Among all CSFK patients followed at our Department between January 1993 and January 2018 [11], we selected those who had reached at least 18years of age between June 2018 and December 2019 [3]. The study was approved by our Institutional Ethics Committee (approval no.=371/2018).

Follow-up protocol

During follow-up, patients were evaluated annually until 5years of age, and subsequently every 2years, unless KI was diagnosed, in which case evaluations were conducted annually thereafter [11].

At each follow-up visit, the following parameters were assessed: weight, height, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), kidney length (KL) by kidney ultrasound, systolic and diastolic blood pressure (SBP, DBP), urinalysis, and urinary protein-to-creatinine ratio (UPr/Cr) [11].

KL, measured in millimeters, was recorded at each follow-up visit, and its standard deviation score (SDS) was calculated based on patient height [12]. All ultrasound examinations were performed by experienced pediatric radiologists following standardized protocols [13]. All patients underwent their first kidney ultrasound within the first 3 months of life.

Moreover, at the last follow-up, when patients were at least 18 years old, we measured serum uric acid (SUA) and urinary markers including microalbumin, beta-2-microglobulin, and neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (NGAL), and the patients underwent 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (24 h-ABPM) [3].

Serum creatinine was measured using isotope dilution mass spectrometry, and the eGFR was calculated using the CKD-EPI creatinine-based formula [14].

Patients with body mass index (BMI) 25.0–29.9 kg/m² were classified as overweight (OW), and those with BMI ≥ 30.0 kg/m² as obese (OB) [15]. The duration of OW/OB status was also recorded [16].

We collected data on smoking habits, including smoking duration and classification as heavy smokers, defined as consumption of ≥ 25cigarettes per day [17].

Hyperuricemia was defined as SUA ≥ 6 mg/dL in females and ≥ 7 mg/dL in males [18].

SUA levels, heavy smoking, and OW/OB were grouped as modifiable risk factors (MRFs).

In accordance with our clinical practice, patients diagnosed with hypertension were further investigated for secondary causes [19], even when associated with CKD.

Investigations for the present study

At last follow-up, we also collected a blood sample to isolate DNA using the Wizard Genomic DNA Purification Kit (Promega, Madison, WI, USA). The DNA and an aliquot of serum were stored at − 80 °C. For the analyses of this study, after obtainment of written consent, we genotyped the uromodulin rs4293393 T > C polymorphism using the TaqMan assay (Integrated DNA Technologies Inc., Coralville, IA). sUMOD was measured using an ELISA kit (BioVendor R&D, Czech Republic).

Definition of kidney injury

KI was defined as the presence of proteinuria (UPr/Cr >0.2 mg/mg or a urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (Ua/Cr) >30 µg/mg) and/or reduced kidney function (eGFR < 90mL/min/1.73 m²) persistent for 3 months [20], and/or hypertension, as defined by abnormal 24-hour ABPM according to the standards proposed by Williams et al. [21].

Definition of kidney hypertrophy and kidney hyperplasia

A KL greater than 2 SDS within the first 3 months of life (early KL >2SDS) was defined as kidney hyperplasia. This definition is based on evidence that, after unilateral nephrectomy, compensatory enlargement of the remaining kidney typically occurs over approximately 6 months [22, 23]. Therefore, an early KL >2SDS likely reflects an increased nephron number at birth, consistent with congenital kidney hyperplasia. Supporting this, animal studies have demonstrated significant compensatory growth and increased nephron endowment in the remaining kidney following fetal unilateral nephrectomy during the active period of nephrogenesis [24–27]. Furthermore, kidney mass and nephron number have been shown to be closely correlated in infants younger than 3 months [28].

In contrast, an early KL < 2SDS (within 3 months of life) followed by a KL > 2SDS after the first 3 months of life was considered indicative of compensatory hypertrophy.

Patients with significant intrarenal hydronephrosis were not classified as having KL > 2SDS, as the increased kidney length in these cases was primarily due to hydronephrosis rather than true hypertrophy or hyperplasia.

Statistical analysis

Since sUMOD levels were normally distributed, differences between groups were analyzed using the independent-samples t-test. The correlation between sUMOD levels and various continuous variables was assessed using Pearson test for normally distributed data, and Spearman test for non-normally distributed data.

The development of KI was evaluated through survival analysis using the Kaplan–Meier method. Kaplan–Meier curves were compared using the log-rank test. A Cox regression model was used to examine the association between the rare allele of the rs4293393 polymorphism and the development of KI. sUMOD levels were also evaluated as potential predictors of the absence of KI using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. The optimal cut-off value was determined using the Youden index. All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics.

Post-hoc power calculation

A post-hoc power analysis was conducted based on the available sample sizes and the observed differences in mean serum uromodulin levels between patients with and without KI, yielding an estimated effect size (Cohen’s d) of approximately 1.89. Using a two-sided independent-samples t-test with a significance level of 0.05, the study’s statistical power was estimated to be 100%.

Results

The mean age at last follow-up of the 56 examined patients (20 females) was 21.1 ± 3.4years (range:18-33years), while the mean age at KI onset was 20.9 ± 3.4years. KI was identified in 15/56 patients (26.8%) (Supplementary Fig. 1). Thirty-five patients (62.5%) were wild type (TT) for the uromodulin rs4293393 T > C polymorphism, 21 (37.5%) were heterozygous (TC), and none were homozygous (CC). This distribution was in Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (p > 0.05).

sUMOD levels were significantly higher in patients with an early KL > 2SDS compared to those without, as well as in non-carriers of the rare allele of the rs4293393 polymorphism compared to carriers. Conversely, sUMOD levels were lower in patients with CAKUT of CSFK, reduced eGFR, proteinuria, hypertension, or KI compared to those without these conditions (Table 1).

We classified our CSFK population into four groups: patients with early KL > 2SDS and wild-type genotype for the uromodulin rs4293393 polymorphism (Group 1); early KL > 2SDS and carriers of the C allele (Group 2); without early KL > 2SDS and wild-type genotype (Group 3); and without early KL > 2SDS and carriers of the C allele (Group 4). Patients in Groups 1 and 2 showed 100% KI-free survival at 33.0 and 24.5 years of age, respectively. In contrast, Group 3 had a survival rate of 62.4% at 30 years of age, while Group 4 had a survival rate of 0% at 26 years of age (Fig. 1A).

In the overall population, none of the patients with early KL > 2SDS developed KI, whereas carrying the C allele was associated with a hazard ratio of 5.1 (95%confidence interval[CI]:1.6–16.2;p = 0.006) for developing KI.

sUMOD levels were evaluated as predictor of the absence of KI using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. The area under ROC curve was 0.93 (95%CI:0.85–0.99;p < 0.001)(Fig. 1B). According to the Youden index, the optimal cut-off for serum uromodulin was ≥ 158.5ng/mL, predicting absence of KI with a sensitivity of 78.0% and a specificity of 93.3%.

Discussion

In our cohort of CSFK patients with prenatal diagnosis, early enrollment, and follow-up from birth to adulthood, we found that sUMOD levels can predict the absence of KI, suggesting that an adequate nephron mass is crucial for its prevention. Interestingly, an early KL > 2SDS identified patients who had higher sUMOD levels in young adulthood and did not develop KI by that age. This indicates that an early-life kidney ultrasound could identify patients with kidney hyperplasia, which is associated with increased prenatal nephron endowment. Notably, later in life, KL > 2SDS no longer identifies patients with kidney hyperplasia, as individuals with and without KL > 2SDS at the last follow-up show similar sUMOD levels. Another interesting finding is that patients carrying the C allele of the rs4293393 variant have lower sUMOD levels in young adulthood and a fivefold increased risk of KI compared to those with the wild-type genotype.

Patients with CAKUT or CSFK, as well as those with reduced eGFR, proteinuria, or hypertension, had lower sUMOD levels compared to individuals without these conditions. Moreover, sUMOD levels showed a significant positive association with both gestational age and eGFR at the last follow-up, reinforcing its value as a potential surrogate marker for nephron mass.

Several studies have investigated risk factors for KI in patients with CSFK, as outlined in current recommendations and guidelines for CSFK management [5, 6], but none have assessed sUMOD levels or the rs4293393 polymorphism. You et al. demonstrated that, in patients without nephropathies, elevated sUMOD may contribute to hypertension by increasing tubular reabsorption of sodium [29]. In contrast, our study population consists of patients with a congenital absence of one kidney, resulting in reduced nephron mass. In this context, lower serum uromodulin levels are associated with higher blood pressure, likely due to the reduced nephron mass.

A limitation of our study is the sample size; however, it provided sufficient power to detect differences in sUMOD levels between patients with and without kidney injury. This limitation is partially offset by the careful selection of the study population, the long follow-up period, and the comprehensive phenotyping of the patients. Another limitation is that the retrospective design of the past follow-up prevented the retrieval of longitudinal data on continuous variables such as eGFR which would have allowed us to evaluate ΔeGFR and the predicted age of onset of KI.

Conclusions

In conclusion, we showed that high sUMOD levels could predict the absence of KI, while carrying the C allele of the uromodulin rs4293393 polymorphism is associated with a fivefold increased risk of developing KI in young adulthood. Furthermore, a KL > 2SDS in early life identifies patients with elevated sUMOD levels in young adulthood (probably reflecting higher nephron mass) and, consequently, with an excellent prognosis. Early-life kidney ultrasound, combined with genotyping of the rs4293393 T > C polymorphism, may improve risk stratification for KI in neonates with CSFK. Future confirmation is needed before widespread clinical application of our findings.

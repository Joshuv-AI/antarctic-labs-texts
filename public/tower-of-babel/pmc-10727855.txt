Multiple Eruptive Epithelioid Hemangiomas on the Right Upper Extremity in an Asian Man
Authors: Gilhyang Kim, Jin Cheol Kim, Seok Young Kang, Hye One Kim, Chun Wook Park, Bo Young Chung
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Epithelioid hemangioma is a rare benign vascular neoplasm. Recently, the term “multiple eruptive epithelioid hemangioma” has been proposed for epithelioid hemangioma with distinct features. It is different from usual epithelioid hemangioma because of the multifocal distributions in various body regions with an eruptive onset. In addition, the histopathology of multiple eruptive epithelioid hemangioma shows increased cellular proliferation, mitosis, and nuclear pleomorphism and positive findings for FOS-B compared to classic epithelioid hemangioma. Herein, we report the case of a 59-year-old man with unusual manifestations suitable for multiple eruptive epithelioid hemangioma. He had multiple erythematous to purple-red dome-shaped nodules on the right hand, arm, and shoulder. The initial lesion was a solitary erythematous nodule on the right hand that abruptly extended to the right arm and shoulder. Microscopically, the tumor was a well-demarcated dermal nodule and showed capillary sized vascular structures. Vascular structures had epithelioid endothelial cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm and vesicular nuclei. The tumor cells showed mild nuclear pleomorphism and a few mitosis and feature of resembling cobble stone was observed. In immunohistochemistry, CD31 and CD34 were positive in the endothelial cells. The endothelial cells showed nuclear positivity in FOS-B. Based on the clinical and histopathological findings, the final diagnosis was multiple eruptive epithelioid hemangiomas. This is the first report of multiple eruptive epithelioid hemangiomas in an Asian man after the term had been introduced.

INTRODUCTION

Epithelioid hemangioma (EH) is a benign vascular neoplasm with well-formed and capillary-sized vascular proliferations12. EH is unusual and difficult to diagnose due to its various histopathological characteristics, including vascular proliferations lined by epithelioid endothelial cells. EH usually distributes in the scalp, face, and ear with single or clustered lesions, and the term “multiple eruptive epithelioid hemangioma” has been recently proposed to describe EH showing different features3. Multiple eruptive epithelioid hemangiomas are characterized by multifocal distribution and eruptive onset with well-defined lesions and different characteristics in histopathology. We encountered an unusual case that matched the clinical and histopathological characteristics of multiple eruptive epithelioid hemangiomas. Thus, herein, we report a case of multiple eruptive EH in the right upper extremity.

CASE REPORT

A 59-year-old male patient presented with multifocal erythematous nodules and tenderness in the right upper extremity for one month. He had no past medical history except for atrophic gastritis. The initial lesion was a solitary nodule on the right hand that suddenly extended. Physical examination revealed multiple erythematous to purple-red dome-shaped nodules on the right hand, arm, and shoulder (Fig. 1). Each mass was firm, and round; with a diameter of approximately 1 to 2 cm. Routine blood examination (complete blood count, coagulation test, liver, and renal function tests) did not show any abnormal findings. A punch biopsy was performed on the lesion of right hand.

Microscopically, the tumor was a well-demarcated dermal nodule and showed capillary sized vascular structures (Fig. 2A, B). Vascular structures had epithelioid endothelial cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm and vesicular nuclei. The tumor cells showed mild nuclear pleomorphism and a few mitosis (Fig. 2C). Feature of resembling cobble stone was observed in some tumor cells (Fig. 2D). There was no necrosis, microabscess or granuloma in the tumor. In immunohistochemistry, CD31 (Fig. 3A) and CD34 (Fig. 3B) were positive in the endothelial cells. The endothelial cells showed nuclear positivity in FOS-B (Fig. 3C, D). The final diagnosis was multiple eruptive epithelioid hemangiomas and he was treated with intralesional triamcinolone acteonide injection (5 mg/ml). Two weeks later, there was no significant improvement and the clinical manifestations showed a progression pattern with new lesions developing continually. He then refused further treatment and was lost to follow-up.

DISCUSSION

EH is an uncommon vascular proliferative neoplasm of unclear origin12, first described by Wells4. It is characterized by solitary or multiple lesions most frequently on the scalp, face, and ear. Multiple lesions are common in approximately half of these patients, but the distributions are almost always clustered in the same body region1.

The term “multiple eruptive epithelioid hemangioma” has been recently proposed to describe EH showing distinct features3. The differences from EH are multifocal distributions in various regions with eruptive onset. Histopathologically, cellular proliferation, mitosis, and nuclear pleomorphism were increased and positive findings for FOS-B were found by immunohistochemistry and fluorescence in situ hybridization analysis.

Several diseases should be considered for the differential diagnosis. Cutaneous epithelioid angiomatous nodule (CEAN) usually presents as a solitary nodule to purple-red nodule with tenderness5. CEAN shows more focal vascular structures and solid component with negative staining in FOS-B analysis3. Epithelioid hemangioendothelioma usually presents as a solitary mass in the deep soft tissue, bone, and skin. It is characterized by infiltrative cords or nests of epithelioid endothelial cells with negative findings in the FOS-B. Pseudomyogenic hemangioendothelioma shows negative for CD343, and it is different from EH. Pyogenic granuloma usually does not have “epithelioid” endothelial cells. Epithelioid angiosarcoma usually shows multi-layering endothelial cells, infiltrative patterns, significant nuclear atypia, and pleomorphism with mitotic figures. Cat scratch disease is uncommon infection with Bartonella henselae, and it is usually with the history of cat scratch or bite in children or young adults6. The patient did not have any cat scratch or immunosuppression history to develop the bacillary angiomatosis, evidence of infection such as lymphadenopathy, and had age with low incidence. In histopathology, cat scratch disease may have lichenoid infiltration mixed inflammatory cells, forming granuloma or microabscess, but the patient did not.

A variety of treatment modalities have been used to treat EHs. The most commonly used interventions include surgical excision, intralesional corticosteroids, and cryotherapy1. However, EHs tend to be chronic and non-remitting even after treatment. Incomplete resolution or recurrences occurred in 40.8% of the cases after excision and the mean time to recurrence was 4.2 years1.

To our knowledge, this is the first report of multiple eruptive EHs in an Asian man after the term was suggested. This case was characterized by an unusual manifestation of EH, which is suitable for eruptive EH. We report a rare case of multiple eruptive EHs, and further clinical reports are needed.

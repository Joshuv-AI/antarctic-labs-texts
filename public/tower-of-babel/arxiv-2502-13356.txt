Decomposition of de Rham complex for quasi-F-split varieties
Alexander Petrov

Abstract
. Using the de Rham stack of Bhatt-Lurie and Drinfeld, we prove that de Rham complex of a smooth quasi-F-split variety over a perfect field of positive characteristic decomposes in all degrees. In particular, smooth proper quasi-F-split varieties have degenerate Hodge-to-de Rham spectral sequence, and satisfy Kodaira-Akizuki-Nakano vanishing. We apply this to prove that the Hodge-to-de Rham spectral sequence for the classifying stack of a reductive group over a field of positive characteristic degenerates.



1.
Introduction

Cohomological invariants of smooth proper varieties over fields of positive characteristic in general fail to satisfy some of the good properties exhibited in characteristic zero, such as degeneration of the Hodge-to-de Rham spectral sequence and Kodaira vanishing. Deligne and Illusie
[

For varieties of arbitrary dimension liftability alone is not enough to guarantee degeneration of the Hodge-to-de Rham spectral sequence, as examples in

Yobuko proved
[

, Theorem 4.4]
that for a perfect field

-split

-scheme

admits a flat lift over

-split variety over

-split varieties of arbitrary dimension.

3.
de Rham stack

Our key tool is the stacky interpretation of de Rham cohomology of schemes in characteristic
p
. In this section we recall the definition and basic properties of the de Rham stack, following
[
Dri24
]
,
[
BL22b
]
,
[
Bha22
]
. Let
k
be an arbitrary commutative
{\mathbb F}_{p}
-algebra, it will serve as our base.

Definition 3.1
.

For a
k
-scheme
X
we define its
de Rham stack
as a functor from
k
-algebras to groupoids, defined on a
k
-algebra
R
by

(3.1)

(X/k)^{\mathrm{dR}}:R\mapsto X(\cone(F_{*}W(R)\xrightarrow{p}F_{*}W(R)))

To evaluate the
k
-scheme
X
on
\cone(F_{*}W(R)\xrightarrow{p}F_{*}W(R))
, we view it here as an animated
k
-algebra via the map
k\to R\xrightarrow{s_{R}}\cone(F_{*}W(R)\xrightarrow{p}F_{*}W(R))
. Recall
[
TV08
, Ch.2, §2.2.4]
that for a classical
k
-scheme
X
the value of
X
on an animated
k
-algebra
A
is defined by
\colim\limits_{B\to A}X(B)
where the colimit is taken in the
\infty
-category of spaces over all classical commutative
k
-algebras

We also abbreviate the notation for the absolute de Rham stack
(X/{\mathbb F}_{p})^{\mathrm{dR}}
to

.

Remark 3.2
.

(1)

Since the animated ring
\cone(F_{*}W(R)\xrightarrow{p}F_{*}W(R))
has non-zero homotopy groups only in degrees
0
and
1
, the value of
X
on it is indeed a
1
-groupoid, rather than an arbitrary space, by
[
TV08
, Ch. 2, Corollary 2.2.4.6]
.

(2)

In
[
Bha22
, Definition 2.5.3]
the de Rham stack of
X
relative to
k
is defined by sending a test
k
-algebra
R
to
X({\mathbb G}_{a}^{\mathrm{dR}}(R))
. Here
{\mathbb G}_{a}^{\mathrm{dR}}
is a ring stack defined in
[
Bha22
, Definition 2.5.1]
, and by
[
Bha22
, Corollary 2.6.8]
it coincides with the fppf-sheafification of the presheaf of animated
k
-algebras
R\mapsto F_{*}W(R)/p
. Since
H^{>0}_{\mathrm{fppf}}(\Spec R,W)=0
, this presheaf is already a sheaf, so the natural map of animated
k
-algebras
F_{*}W(R)/p\to{\mathbb G}_{a}^{\mathrm{dR}}(R)
is an equivalence, and Definition
3.1
recovers the same stack.

(3)

In
[
BL22b
, Lemma 7.3]
it is proven that relative prismatization, and consequently the de Rham stack, is a sheaf for fpqc topology, the main ingredient in the proof being the algebraization result
[
Bha16
, Theorem 4.1]
. For the convenience of the reader we give in Lemma
3.3
below a proof of the fact that

is an étale sheaf, which amounts to observing that the relevant algebraization result in the special case of the de Rham stack follows directly from square-zero deformation theory.

We will use sheafyness of

repeatedly to reduce some constructions to the case of an affine
X
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
R\to S
be a fully faithful étale map of
{\mathbb F}_{p}
-algebras. For any
{\mathbb F}_{p}
-scheme
X
the natural map

(3.2)

X(W(R)\otimes^{L}_{{\mathbb Z}_{p}}{\mathbb F}_{p})\to\lim\limits_{\Delta}X(W(S^{\otimes_{R}\bullet})\otimes^{L}_{{\mathbb Z}_{p}}{\mathbb F}_{p})

is an equivalence.

Proof.

Let us first treat the case of an affine
X=\Spec A
. By étale descent we have an equivalence
R\simeq\lim\limits_{\Delta}S^{\otimes_{R}\bullet}
, and since the functor
R\mapsto W(R)\otimes_{{\mathbb Z}_{p}}^{L}{\mathbb F}_{p}
from
{\mathbb F}_{p}
-algebras to animated
{\mathbb F}_{p}
-algebras preserves limits, we get an equivalence

(3.3)

W(R)\otimes_{{\mathbb Z}_{p}}^{L}{\mathbb F}_{p}\to\lim\limits_{\Delta}W(S^{\otimes_{R}\bullet})\otimes_{{\mathbb Z}_{p}}{\mathbb F}_{p}

even though this limit diagram is no longer the Čech nerve of an étale

-algebra in general. The assertion of the lemma for

now amounts to the fact that the mapping spaces from
A
to the LHS and RHS of (

To prove the lemma for a general scheme

we apply deformation theory to the square-zero extension
W(R)\otimes_{{\mathbb Z}_{p}}^{L}{\mathbb F}_{p}\to R
. Denote the ideal of this extension by
I_{R}\in D^{[-1,0]}(R)
. The map (
3.2
) fits into the commutative diagram

(3.4)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X(W(R)\otimes^{L}_{{\mathbb Z}_{p}}{\mathbb F}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lim\limits_{\Delta}X(W(S^{\otimes_{R}\bullet})\otimes^{L}_{{\mathbb Z}_{p}}{\mathbb F}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X(R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lim\limits_{\Delta}X(S^{\otimes_{R}\bullet})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

where the bottom horizontal arrow is an equivalence by étale sheafyness of

. Hence it suffices to check that (

. Given such a point

\lim\limits_{\Delta}\tau^{\leq 0}\mathrm{RHom}_{S^{\otimes_{R}\bullet}}(x^{*}L_{X/{\mathbb F}_{p}}\otimes_{R}S^{\otimes_{R}\bullet},I_{S^{\otimes_{R}\bullet}})\simeq\lim\limits_{\Delta}\tau^{\leq 0}\mathrm{RHom}_{R}(x^{*}L_{X/{\mathbb F}_{p}},I_{R})
while the fiber of the left vertical map is a torsor over

. Here

is the cotangent complex of

.

As mentioned above, the natural map

is an equivalence, in particular

is an equivalence. Therefore the map between fibers of the vertical map (

∎

The natural

F_{*}r_{R}:\cone(F_{*}W(R)\xrightarrow{p}F_{*}W(R))\to F_{*}R

which gives rise to a map of stacks

(3.5)

\nu_{X}:(X/k)^{\mathrm{dR}}\to X^{(1)}.

The natural map

likewise induces a morphisms of stacks

(3.6)

\pi_{X}:X\to(X/k)^{\mathrm{dR}}

By construction, the composition

is the relative Frobenius morphism

.

Definition 3.4
.

For a morphism
f:X\to S
of
{\mathbb F}_{p}
-schemes with

not necessarily affine, we define the

as the fiber product in presheaves of groupoids on
{\mathbb F}_{p}
-algebras:

(3.7)

(X/S)^{\mathrm{dR}}:=X^{\mathrm{dR}}\times_{S^{\mathrm{dR}}}S

where

maps to

via the map

.

Remark 3.5
.

(1)

This is consistent with Definition
3.1
in the case

is affine, because for an animated
k
-algebra
A
the space of
A
-points
X(A)=\Map_{k}(\Spec A,X)
is equivalent to

\Map_{{\mathbb F}_{p}}(\Spec A,X)\times_{\Map_{{\mathbb F}_{p}}(\Spec A,\Spec k)}*

where

classifies the structure map
\Spec A\to\Spec k
.

(2)

If
S
is a perfect
{\mathbb F}_{p}
-scheme then the natural map
(X/S)^{\mathrm{dR}}\to X^{\mathrm{dR}}
is an equivalence. This would follow from the fact that the natural map
\pi_{S}:S\to S^{\mathrm{dR}}
is an equivalence. It suffices to show this in the case

is an affine perfect scheme
\Spec k
. For a test
{\mathbb F}_{p}
-algebra

we have
(\Spec k)^{\mathrm{dR}}(R)=\Map_{{\mathbb F}_{p}}(k,\cone(F_{*}W(R)\xrightarrow{p}F_{*}W(R)))
. In general, for an animated
{\mathbb F}_{p}
-algebra

there is a natural equivalence of mapping spaces between animated

-algebras

(3.8)

\Map_{{\mathbb F}_{p}}(k,A)\simeq\Map_{{\mathbb F}_{p}}(k,\pi_{0}(A)^{\mathrm{perf}})

where
\pi_{0}(A)^{\mathrm{perf}}
is the inverse limit perfection
\lim\limits_{\xleftarrow[\varphi]{}}\pi_{0}(A)
of the ring
\pi_{0}(A)
. Indeed, in the diagram

(3.9)

\Map_{{\mathbb F}_{p}}(k,A)\xleftarrow[\varphi_{A}\circ]{}\Map_{{\mathbb F}_{p}}(k,A)\xleftarrow[\varphi_{A}\circ]{}\ldots

all transition maps can be identified with

and hence are equivalences, so its limit is equivalent to

. On the other hand, its limit is tautologically identified with the mapping space
\Map_{{\mathbb F}_{p}}(k,\lim\limits_{\xleftarrow[\varphi_{A}]{}}A)
. The perfection

of an animated

-algebra is equivalent to the perfection of its

[

, Corollary 11.9]
, which gives (

Applying this observation to

we get

because the Frobenius endomorphism of

factors through the surjective map

, and therefore perfections of

and

are identified.

The morphisms
\pi_{X}:X\to X^{\mathrm{dR}}
and
\nu_{X}:X^{\mathrm{dR}}\to X
then induce their relative versions

(3.10)

\pi_{X/S}:X\to(X/S)^{\mathrm{dR}}\qquad\nu_{X/S}:(X/S)^{\mathrm{dR}}\to X^{(1)}

For smooth morphisms, cohomology of the structure sheaf on

recovers the de Rham complex of

:

Proposition 3.6
.

For a smooth morphism
f:X\to S
of
{\mathbb F}_{p}
-schemes there is a natural quasi-isomorphism in
D(X^{(1)})
:

(3.11)

R\nu_{X/S*}{\mathcal{O}}_{(X/S)^{\mathrm{dR}}}\simeq F_{X/S*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}

Proof.

In the case of an affine
S
this is
[
Bha22
, Corollary 2.7.2 (3)]
. The proof in the general case can be deduced via Zariski descent, as we now outline. The de Rham complex
F_{X/S*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}
can be identified with
\colim\limits_{j:U\hookrightarrow S}j_{*}F_{X|_{U}/U*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X|_{U}/U}
where the colimit in
D(X^{(1)})
is taken over all affine open subschemes

.

The formation of de Rham stack of a scheme commutes with fiber products. Therefore, for an open affine
U\hookrightarrow S
we have
(X|_{U})^{\mathrm{dR}}\simeq X^{\mathrm{dR}}\times_{S^{\mathrm{dR}}}U^{\mathrm{dR}}
, and the LHS of (

) can be likewise expressed as
\colim\limits_{U\hookrightarrow S}j_{*}R\nu_{X|_{U}/U*}{\mathcal{O}}_{(X|_{U}/U)^{\mathrm{dR}}}
. The natural equivalence (
3.11
) for affine
S
gives rise to an equivalence between functors
\{j:U\hookrightarrow S\}\to D(X^{(1)})
to the derived
\infty
-category of quasi-coherent sheaves on
X^{(1)}
given by
U\mapsto j_{*}R\nu_{X|_{U}/U*}{\mathcal{O}}_{(X|_{U}/U)^{\mathrm{dR}}}
and
U\mapsto j_{*}F_{X|_{U}/U*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X|_{U}/U}
. Passing to colimits over these functors gives the desired equivalence.
∎

The key additional leverage for studying de Rham cohomology that the de Rham stack provides for us is the structure of a gerbe on the morphism
\nu_{X/S}
. For a vector bundle
E
on a scheme
Y
, let us view its total space
\Tot_{Y}(E)\to Y
as a group scheme over
Y
, fiberwise isomorphic to
{\mathbb G}_{a}^{\rk E}
. We denote by
E^{\sharp}
the divided power envelope of the zero section in
\Tot_{Y}(E)
, viewed as an affine group scheme over
Y
.

Proposition 3.7
.

For a smooth morphism
f:X\to S
the morphism
\nu_{X/S}:(X/S)^{\mathrm{dR}}\to X^{(1)}
has a structure of a gerbe for the group scheme
T^{\sharp}_{X^{(1)}/S}
.

Proof.

In the case of an affine
S=\Spec k
this is
[
Bha22
, Proposition 2.7.1]
. If
k
is a perfect
{\mathbb F}_{p}
-algebra, this also follows from
[
BL22b
, Proposition 5.12]
applied to the prism
(A,I)=(W(k),(p))
and the smooth scheme
X^{(1)}
over
A/I\simeq k
. The case of a general

follows by Zariski descent, as in Proposition
3.6
: for each affine open
U\subset S
we have a natural map
X^{(1)}|_{U}\to B^{2}_{X^{(1)}_{U}}T_{X^{(1)}_{U}/U}^{\sharp}
classifying the gerbe
(X_{U}/U)^{\mathrm{dR}}\to X_{U}^{(1)}
. By Zariski descent they define a map
X^{(1)}\to B^{2}_{X^{(1)}}T_{X^{(1)}/S}^{\sharp}
such that the pullback of the universal gerbe
X^{(1)}\to B^{2}_{X^{(1)}}T_{X^{(1)}/S}^{\sharp}
along it is equivalent to
(X/S)^{\mathrm{dR}}\to X^{(1)}
.
∎

Given this gerbe structure, we can readily deduce that the de Rham complex decomposes after Frobenius pullback. This has been previously observed by Bhargav Bhatt, and by Vadim Vologodsky via a different method relying on the Azumaya property of the algebra of differential operators.

Proposition 3.8

(

, Remark 2.7.5]
)
.

there is a natural quasi-isomorphism in

that induces the Frobenius pullback of the Cartier isomorphism on all cohomology sheaves.

Proof.

Since the composition

is equal to the relative Frobenius morphism

, the pullback

of the morphism

along

admits a section:

(3.13)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X/S)^{\mathrm{dR}}\times_{X^{(1)}}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X/S)^{\mathrm{dR}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{(1)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu^{\prime}_{X/S}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu_{X/S}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{X/S}}

For a vector bundle

4.

We restrict ourselves to working over a perfect

Theorem 4.1
.

For a smooth scheme
X

{\mathbb F}_{p}
-algebra

(4.1)

F_{*}W_{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})/p\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{(1)}}}F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}\simeq\bigoplus\limits_{i\geq 0}F_{*}W_{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})/p\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{(1)}}}\Omega^{i}_{X^{(1)}}[-i]

We will obtain this decomposition as a consequence of the fact that the morphism
\nu_{X}:X^{\mathrm{dR}}\to X^{(1)}

W_{n}(X)\times_{{\mathbb Z}_{p}}{\mathbb F}_{p}
. We first recall the following criterion for the morphism

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\widetilde{X}
be a flat

\Spf{\mathbb Z}_{p}

\widetilde{F}:\widetilde{X}\to\widetilde{X}
whose restriction to the special fiber

s_{\widetilde{F}}:X\to X^{\mathrm{dR}}

Proof.

This is established in the course of the proof of
[

Since

is a Zariski sheaf (Lemma

provided that this construction is functorial, in the sense that it extends to a functor from the poset of affine opens
\{U\subset X\}
to the slice category over
X^{\mathrm{dR}}
. For this it is enough to produce a section of
\nu_{U}:U^{\mathrm{dR}}\to U
for every affine open
U\subset X
. Each
U
is equipped with a unique flat formal lift over
{\mathbb Z}_{p}
together with a Frobenius lift, compatible with the given lift of
X
. Hence we may assume from now on that

is affine.

Let
\widetilde{S}
be a flat
{\mathbb Z}_{p}
-algebra equipped with an endomorphism
\widetilde{F}_{S}:\widetilde{S}\to\widetilde{S}
that lifts the Frobenius endomorphism on
S:=\widetilde{S}/p
. There is a natural ring homomorphism
\widetilde{w}_{\widetilde{F}}:\widetilde{S}\to W(\widetilde{S})
whose composition with the projection
W(\widetilde{S})\to\widetilde{S}
on the
0
-th Witt coordinate is the identity map on
\widetilde{S}
, by the adjunction between
W(-)
and the forgetful functor from
\delta
-rings to all rings, cf.
[
Joy85
, Théorème 4]
. In particular, we get a natural map of rings

w_{\widetilde{F}}:\widetilde{S}\xrightarrow{\widetilde{w}_{\widetilde{F}}}W(\widetilde{S})\to W(S)

where the second map is induced by the mod
p
reduction map
\widetilde{S}\to S
. Applying the functor
-\otimes^{L}_{{\mathbb Z}_{p}}{\mathbb F}_{p}
to the map
w_{\widetilde{F}}
we obtain a point of the groupoid
\Map_{{\mathbb F}_{p}}(S,W(S)\otimes^{L}_{{\mathbb Z}_{p}}{\mathbb F}_{p})=(\Spec S)^{\mathrm{dR}}(S)
and we define the section
s_{\widetilde{F}}:\Spec S\to(\Spec S)^{\mathrm{dR}}
to be the map corresponding to that point.

This is indeed a section, because the composition
\nu_{\Spec S}\circ s_{\widetilde{F}}
is induced by the map
S\to S
of (discrete)
{\mathbb F}_{p}
-algebras given by the composition

(4.3)

\widetilde{S}\otimes_{{\mathbb Z}_{p}}{\mathbb F}_{p}\xrightarrow{\widetilde{w}_{\widetilde{F}}}W(\widetilde{S})\otimes_{{\mathbb Z}_{p}}^{L}{\mathbb F}_{p}\to W(S)\otimes^{L}_{{\mathbb Z}_{p}}{\mathbb F}_{p}\xrightarrow{r_{S}}S

The composition of the last two maps in (
4.3
) is equivalent to
W(\widetilde{S})\otimes^{L}_{{\mathbb Z}_{p}}{\mathbb F}_{p}\to\widetilde{S}\otimes^{L}_{{\mathbb Z}_{p}}{\mathbb F}_{p}\simeq S
, the mod
p

0
-th Witt coordinate. Hence the composition (
4.3
) is the identity map, because the composition
\widetilde{S}\xrightarrow{\widetilde{w}_{\widetilde{F}}}W(\widetilde{S})\to\widetilde{S}
is the identity.
∎

Corollary 4.6

Corollary 4.9
.

For the following classes of smooth varieties over a perfect field
k
the de Rham complex is decomposable:

(1)

Generalized flag varieties

is a smooth parabolic subgroup in a reductive group

. In particular, every smooth quadric hypersurface

(2)

Generalized flag varieties are Frobenius-split by

, which implies the first assertion. The assertion about smooth quadrics now follows, because they are examples of generalized flag varieties for orthogonal groups

.

Alternatively, we can give a direct construction of a Frobenius splitting of a smooth quadric

. By
[

a homogeneous polynomial

if

divides
\sigma

appears in

with a non-zero coefficient.

Since the property of being Frobenius split can be checked after an extension of the base perfect field, we may assume that
k
is algebraically closed. If
p
is odd, then

is isomorphic to the quadric cut out by the equation

is

as a degree

. For

is isomorphic (
[

) to one of the following two:

Finally, the fact that finite height Calabi-Yau varieties are quasi-

-split was proven by Yobuko
[

, Theorem 4.5]
.

∎

5.
Frobenius splitting of classifying stacks

In this section we generalize the decomposition result from Proposition
3.8
to smooth Artin stacks over a perfect field and observe that the classifying stack of a reductive group is Frobenius split, which leads to the main result of this section:

Theorem 5.1
.

For a reductive group
G
over a perfect field
k
of characteristic
p
, Hodge-to-de Rham spectral sequence
E_{1}^{i,j}=H^{j}(BG,\Lambda^{i}L_{BG/k})\Rightarrow H^{i+j}_{\mathrm{dR}}(BG/k)
for the classifying stack of
G
degenerates at the first page.

Here
\Lambda^{i}L_{BG/k}
is the
i
th exterior power of the cotangent complex of
BG
, isomorphic to
S^{i}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})[-i]
, the
i
-th symmetric power of the dual to the adjoint representation of
G
, placed in the cohomological degree
i
.

Remark 5.2
.

(1)

The Hodge cohomology groups
H^{j}(BG,\Lambda^{i}L_{BG/k})
of
BG
can be identified with the cohomology
H^{j-i}(G,S^{j}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}))
of the group
G
with coefficients in the symmetric powers of the dual to the adjoint representation, cf.
[
Tot18
, Corollary 2.2]
.

(2)

The fact that there are no non-zero differentials coming out of terms
E_{r}^{i,j}
in the range
i+j<p
was proven in
[
KP22
, Corollary 1.3.24]
by a generalization to stacks of the method of Deligne-Illusie. Our proof also proceeds by proving first that the conjugate spectral sequence degenerates for
BG
.

(3)

We do not know if Theorem
5.1
remains true over an arbitrary base ring
k
. Degeneration trivially holds when
k
is a
{\mathbb Q}
-algebra because in this case the entries
E_{1}^{i,j}
of the spectral sequence are zero for
i\neq j
. The case of
k={\mathbb Z}/p^{n}
for
n\geq 2
seems to require a new idea.

(4)

We find it somewhat curious that replacing a reductive group
G
by a quotient
G^{\prime}=G/\Gamma
by a central finite subgroup scheme
\Gamma\subset Z(G)
of multiplicative type (e.g.
G=GL_{n}
and
G^{\prime}=PGL_{n}
) might make the Hodge cohomology of the classifying stack much more complicated but the Frobenius-splitting of
BG^{\prime}
easily follows (Lemma
5.13
) from that of
BG
, so our proof of Theorem
1.4
for
G^{\prime}
is no more harder than it is for
G
.

We refer the reader to
[
KP22
, 1.1]
or
[
ABM21
, Construction 2.7]
for the definition and basic properties of Hodge and de Rham cohomology of smooth stacks. Following the same principle as in these references, we introduce the de Rham complex of a smooth stack
X
in characteristic
p
, viewed as an object of the derived category of quasi-coherent sheaves on

, by formally extending it from the corresponding invariant for smooth schemes.

For the duration of this section,
k
is a perfect
{\mathbb F}_{p}
-algebra. The definition of the de Rham complex for a stack will be a special case of the following construction that, given a functorial way of assigning to every smooth
k
-scheme

an object

, extends it to arbitrary smooth Artin stacks. To simplify the exposition, we restrict ourselves to stacks with affine diagonal.

Construction 5.3
.

Following
[
Lur24
, 020S]
, we denote by
\QC_{\mathrm{obj}}
the
\infty
-category of
\infty
-categories equipped with a distinguished object. Its objects are pairs
({\mathcal{C}},C)
where
{\mathcal{C}}
is an
\infty
-category, and
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
is an object, and
1
-morphisms from
({\mathcal{C}},C)
to
({\mathcal{D}},D)
are pairs
F:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{D}},\alpha:F(C)\to D
where
F
is a functor and
\alpha
is any
1
-morphism in
{\mathcal{D}}
, which does not have to be invertible. Likewise,
\QC
denotes the
\infty
-category of
\infty
-categories
[
Lur24
, 0208]
.

Let
\mathrm{Sm}
be the category of smooth affine schemes over
k
. Denote by
\mathrm{St}_{\mathrm{Sm}}
the category of smooth Artin stacks over
k
with affine diagonal, in the sense of
[
Sta24
, 026O]
.

Suppose we are given a functor
{\mathcal{F}}:\mathrm{Sm}^{\op}\to\QC_{\mathrm{obj}}
such that the underlying functor
\mathrm{Sm}^{\op}\xrightarrow{{\mathcal{F}}}\QC_{\mathrm{obj}}\to\QC
is the functor
D(-)
, sending
S\in\mathrm{Sm}
to the derived category of quasi-coherent sheaves on
S
, and a morphism
f:S\to S^{\prime}
to the pullback functor
f^{*}:D(S^{\prime})\to D(S)
. For an affine scheme
S\in\mathrm{Sm}
we denote the distinguished object of
D(S)
provided by
{\mathcal{F}}
by
{\mathcal{F}}_{S}\in D(S)
. Let
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}:\mathrm{St}_{\mathrm{Sm}}^{\op}\to\QC_{\mathrm{obj}}
be the left Kan extension of
{\mathcal{F}}
along
\mathrm{Sm}^{\op}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{St}_{\mathrm{Sm}}^{\op}
. Explicitly, for a stack

we have
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}(X)=(D(X),\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{X})
where the object
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{X}\in D(X)
is given by

(5.1)

\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{X}=\lim\limits_{f:S\to X}f_{*}{\mathcal{F}}_{S}

where the limit is taken over all smooth affine schemes mapping to
X
. By the assumption that
X
has affine diagonal, each morphism

is necessarily affine.

Lemma 5.4
.

If the functor
{\mathcal{F}}:\mathrm{Sm}^{\op}\to\QC_{\mathrm{obj}}
satisfies smooth descent, then so does the extended functor
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}
.

Proof.

Let
\pi:X^{\prime}\to X
be a smooth cover in
\mathrm{St}_{\mathrm{Sm}}
. If
\pi^{\prime}:U\to X^{\prime}
is a smooth cover by a scheme, then it suffices to check descent along morphisms
\pi^{\prime}
and
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}
both of which are representable, because
X^{\prime}
and
X
are assumed to have affine diagonal. Hence it suffices to treat the case of a representable smooth cover
\pi
.

The Čech nerve of
\pi
consists of stacks
\pi_{n}:X^{\prime\times_{X}(n+1)}\to X
. We need to show that the natural map

(5.2)

\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{X}\to\lim\limits_{[n]\in\Delta}\pi_{n*}\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{X^{\prime\times_{X}(n+1)}}

is an equivalence.

Consider
1
-categories
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
defined as follows. An object of
{\mathcal{C}}
consists of a pair
([n],f_{n}:T\to X^{\prime\times_{X}(n+1)})
where
[n]
is an object of
\Delta^{\op}
, and
f_{n}
is an arbitrary morphism from a smooth affine scheme
T\in\mathrm{Sm}
. The other category
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
consists of pairs
([n],f:S\to X)
where
[n]\in\Delta^{\op}
, and
S
is a smooth affine scheme. There is a functor
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{C}}
sending
([n],S\to X)
to
([n],S\times_{X}X^{\prime\times_{X}(n+1)}\to X^{\prime\times_{X}(n+1)})
.

We have a functor
{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi}:{\mathcal{C}}\to D(X)
sending
([n],f_{n}:T\to X^{\prime\times_{X}(n+1)})
to
\pi_{n*}f_{n*}{\mathcal{F}}_{T}
. By definition, the codomain of (
5.2
) is computed as
\lim\limits_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\op}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi}
. For each morphism
f:S\to X
from a smooth affine scheme
S\in\mathrm{Sm}
the fiber product
f:S^{\prime}:=S\times_{X}X^{\prime}\to S
is a smooth affine cover of
S
. By smooth descent for
{\mathcal{F}}
, we can then calculate
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{X}
as
\lim\limits_{f:S\to X}\lim\limits_{[n]\in\Delta}f_{*}\pi_{n,S*}{\mathcal{F}}_{S^{\prime\times_{S}(n+1)}}
. In other words,
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{X}
is equivalent to the limit
\lim\limits_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime\op}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi}|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}
.

The functor
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{C}}
is cofinal by Quillen’s theorem A
[
Lur09
, Theorem 4.1.3.1]
, because for an object
([n],f_{n}:T\to X^{\prime\times_{X}(n+1)})\in{\mathcal{C}}
the slice category
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\times_{{\mathcal{C}}}{\mathcal{C}}_{([n],T)/}
has the initial object

and is therefore weakly contractible. Hence limits of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi}
over categories
{\mathcal{C}}^{\op}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime\op}
are equivalent, as desired.
∎

Specializing the above construction to functors
S\mapsto F_{S/k*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{S/k}\in D(S^{(1)})
and
S\mapsto\tau^{\leq n}F_{S/k*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{S/k}\in D(S^{(1)})
we can define the de Rham complex of a smooth Artin stack, together with its conjugate filtration:

Definition 5.5
.

For a smooth Artin stack
X
with affine diagonal over a perfect
{\mathbb F}_{p}
-algebra
k
define the following object of
D(X^{(1)})
:

(5.3)

F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}:=\lim\limits_{f:S\to X}f^{(1)}_{*}F_{S/k*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{S/k}

where the limit is taken over all morphisms
f:S\to X
from affine smooth schemes to
X
. Similarly, for
n\geq 0
define the
n
-th step of the conjugate filtration on the de Rham complex as

(5.4)

\mathrm{Fil}_{n}^{\mathrm{conj}}F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}:=\lim\limits_{f:S\to X}f^{(1)}_{*}\tau^{\leq n}F_{S/k*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{S/k}

where the limit is taken over the same category of affine schemes that are smooth over
X
.

Note that
F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}
is an indivisible piece of notation here: we did not define the de Rham complex itself as a sheaf-theoretic object on
X
.

Remark 5.6
.

A more finitary method of computing
F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/k}
is given by the smooth descent for it, as in Lemma
5.7
(4) below. For a smooth scheme
X
over
k
the above definition correctly recovers the Frobenius pushforward of the de Rham complex – for an affine
X
the diagram over which the limit is taken has a final object given by

, and the case of a general scheme

follows by descent.

For a stack
Y
over
k
we denote by
\mathrm{R}\Gamma(Y,-):D(Y)\to D(k)
the derived pushforward functor along the morphism
Y\to\Spec k
.

Lemma 5.7
.

For a smooth Artin stack
X
with affine diagonal over
k
the following holds.

(1)

The complex of
k
-modules
\mathrm{R}\Gamma(X^{(1)},F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/k})
is naturally equivalent to the de Rham cohomology
\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k)
of the stack
X
, as defined e.g. in
[
KP22
, Definition 1.1.3]
.

(2)

Conjugate filtration on
F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/k}
is exhaustive, that is the natural map

(5.5)

\colim\limits_{n}\mathrm{Fil}_{n}^{\mathrm{conj}}F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}\to F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}

is an equivalence.

(3)

The
n
-th graded piece
\gr_{n}^{\mathrm{conj}}:=\cone(\mathrm{Fil}_{n-1}F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}\to\mathrm{Fil}_{n}F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X})
of the conjugate filtration is equivalent to
\Lambda^{n}L_{X^{(1)}/k}[-n]
where
L_{X^{(1)}/k}
is the cotangent complex of the stack
X^{(1)}
.

(4)

For a smooth surjective morphism
f:U\to X
of smooth Artin stacks with affine diagonals there is a natural equivalence

(5.6)

\mathrm{Fil}_{n}^{\mathrm{conj}}F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}\simeq\lim\limits_{[i]\in\Delta}f_{i*}\mathrm{Fil}^{n}_{\mathrm{conj}}F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{U^{\times_{X}(i+1)}}

where
f_{i}:U^{\times_{X}(i+1)}\to X
are structure maps from the terms of the Cech nerve of
f
.

Proof.

(1) follows from the natural equivalence
\mathrm{R}\Gamma(S^{(1)},F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{S})\simeq\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{\mathrm{dR}}(S/k)
for smooth affine schemes
S
and the fact that
\mathrm{R}\Gamma(X^{(1)},-)
commutes with limits.

Statement (2) is equivalent to proving that
\colim\limits_{n}\lim\limits_{f:S\to X}f^{(1)}_{*}\tau^{>n}F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{S}
vanishes. Since
f^{(1)}_{*}
is left exact with respect to the standard
t
-structures on
D(S^{(1)})
and
D(X^{(1)})
, each object
f^{(1)}_{*}\tau^{>n}F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{S}
is concentrated in degrees
>n
. The subcategory of objects concentrated in cohomological degrees
>n
is closed under limits
[
Lur17
, Corollary 1.2.1.6]
. Therefore for each
n
the object
\lim\limits_{f:S\to X}f^{(1)}_{*}\tau^{>n}F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{S}\in D(X^{(1)})
is concentrated in degrees
>n
, and their colimit along
n
vanishes, finishing the proof of (2).

By Cartier isomorphism, for a smooth scheme
S
the
n
-th graded piece of the conjugate filtration on
F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{S}
is naturally equivalent to
\Omega^{n}_{S^{(1)}/k}[-n]
. Hence
\gr_{n}^{\mathrm{conj}}F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}
is computed by the limit
\lim\limits_{S\to X}f^{(1)}_{*}\Omega^{n}_{S^{(1)}/k}[-n]
, which coincides with the shifted
n
-th exterior power of the cotangent complex
L_{X^{(1)}/k}
, as a consequence of smooth descent for cotangent complex, cf.
[
KP22
, Proposition 1.1.4]
or
[
ABM21
, Remark 2.8]
. This proves statement (3).

By smooth descent for cotangent complex and its exterior powers
[
Bha12a
, Remark 2.8]
, the assignment
S\mapsto\tau^{\leq n}F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{S}
satisfies smooth descent, so (4) follows by Lemma
5.4
.
∎

Example 5.8
.

Explicitly, for a smooth affine group scheme

over

is a complex of

that can be represented by the totalization of the cosimplicial complex

(5.7)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{G/k*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{G/k}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ldots}

Proposition 5.9
.

For a smooth Artin stack
X
with affine diagonal over a perfect
{\mathbb F}_{p}
-algebra

there is an equivalence

(5.8)

F_{X/k}^{*}F_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/k}\simeq\bigoplus\limits_{i\geq 0}F^{*}_{X/k}\Lambda^{i}L_{X^{(1)}/k}[-i]

Proof.

Given Proposition

∎

For a future application, we prove the following in a larger generality of not necessarily smooth stacks. The Frobenius endomorphism

for a non-smooth stack

might not be flat, and everywhere below for a morphism

we denote by

the

pullback functor.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
f:U\to X
be a flat surjective morphism of arbitrary Artin stacks over
{\mathbb F}_{p}
. Consider the simplicial Čech nerve
U_{\bullet}:=U^{\times_{X}\bullet}
, each term equipped with the structure map
f_{i}:U^{\times_{X}(i+1)}\to X
. For all

the natural map

(5.9)

F_{X}^{*}\Lambda^{n}L_{X}\to\lim\limits_{\Delta}f_{\bullet*}F_{U_{\bullet}}^{*}\Lambda^{n}L_{U_{\bullet}}

is an equivalence.

In particular for a smooth Artin stack
X
with affine diagonal over a perfect
{\mathbb F}_{p}
-algebra
k
, the natural map
F_{X}^{*}\Lambda^{n}L_{X}\to\lim\limits_{f:S\to X}f_{*}F_{S}^{*}\Lambda^{n}L_{S}
, where the limit is taken over all smooth affine schemes

, is an equivalence.

Proof.

The proof follows the same idea as Bhatt’s proof
[
BMS19
, Theorem 3.1]
of descent for cotangent complex itself. First of all, as in the proof of Lemma
5.4
, for the first assertion of the lemma it suffices to treat the case of a representable
f
. By flat descent for quasi-coherent sheaves,
F_{X}^{*}\Lambda^{n}L_{X}
is equivalent to the limit
\lim\limits_{\Delta}f_{\bullet*}f_{\bullet}^{*}F_{X}^{*}\Lambda^{n}L_{X}\simeq\lim\limits_{\Delta}f_{\bullet*}F_{U_{\bullet}}^{*}f_{\bullet}^{*}\Lambda^{n}L_{X}
. Hence the fact that (
5.9
) is an equivalence in

is equivalent to the vanishing of the object

(5.10)

\lim\limits_{\Delta}f_{\bullet*}F^{*}_{U_{\bullet}}\cone(f_{\bullet}^{*}\Lambda^{n}L_{X}\xrightarrow{df_{\bullet}}\Lambda^{n}L_{U_{\bullet}})\in D(X).

Using the fundamental triangle
f_{\bullet}^{*}L_{X}\to L_{U_{\bullet}}\to L_{U_{\bullet}/X}
, we can endow the cone
\cone(df_{\bullet}:f_{\bullet}^{*}\Lambda^{n}L_{X}\to\Lambda^{n}L_{U_{\bullet}})
with a finite filtration whose graded pieces are
\Lambda^{j}L_{U_{\bullet}/X}\otimes f_{\bullet}^{*}\Lambda^{n-j}L_{X}
with

.

Hence it suffices to check that the limit
\lim\limits_{\Delta}f_{\bullet*}F_{U_{\bullet}}^{*}(\Lambda^{j}L_{U_{\bullet}/X}\otimes f_{\bullet}^{*}\Lambda^{n-j}L_{X})
vanishes for all

. This can be checked flat locally on

and étale locally on

, so we may and do assume that

and

are both affine schemes.

We need to check that the totalization of the following cosimplicial object of

(5.11)

\Delta\ni[i]\mapsto F^{*}_{B^{\otimes_{A}(i+1)}}(\Lambda^{j}L_{B^{\otimes_{A}(i+1)}/A})\otimes_{A}F_{A}^{*}\Lambda^{n-j}L_{A}

is zero. Since

if a faithfully flat map, this can be checked after tensoring this cosimplicial

-module with

. We will now prove that the cosimplicial object
[i]\to F^{*}_{B^{\otimes_{A}(i+1)}}(\Lambda^{j}L_{B^{\otimes_{A}(i+1)}/A})\otimes_{A}B
of

is contractible. This implies the desired vanishing of the totalization of (

) because this cosimplicial object remains contractible after tensoring with the complex
F_{A}^{*}\Lambda^{n-j}L_{A}
over
A
.

By base change for the cotangent complex, for an

-algebra

there is an equivalence
F_{C}^{*}\Lambda^{n}L_{C/A}\otimes_{A}B\simeq F_{C\otimes_{A}B}^{*}\Lambda^{n}L_{(C\otimes_{A}B)/B}
natural in

. In particular, the cosimplicial object
[i]\mapsto F^{*}_{B^{\otimes_{A}(i+1)}}\Lambda^{j}L_{B^{\otimes_{A}(i+1)}/A}\otimes_{A}B
of

is equivalent to the result of applying the functor

from

B
-modules to the Čech nerve of the map

. This Čech nerve is homotopy equivalent to the constant cosimplicial

, hence the result of applying functor

is homotopy equivalent to the constant cosimplicial object with value

, as desired.

The second assertion of the lemma follows formally from the descent statement that we just established: the map in question is an equivalence when

is a smooth scheme, and by Lemma

its codomain satisfies descent as well, hence it is an equivalence for all smooth Artin stacks.
∎

We introduce a generalization of the notion of Frobenius splitting to Artin stacks:

Definition 5.11
.

For an Artin stack
X
over
{\mathbb F}_{p}
a Frobenius splitting is a map
\tau:RF_{X*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
in the derived category of quasi-coherent sheaves on
X
, such that composition
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\xrightarrow{F_{X}^{\#}}RF_{X*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\xrightarrow{\tau}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
equals the identity.

For a scheme
X
the Frobenius endomorphism is affine, so the derived pushforward
RF_{X*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
is identified with the plain pushforward
F_{X*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
, but e.g. for the classifying stack
X=BG
of a non-commutative connected reductive group the derived pushforward
RF_{BG*}{\mathcal{O}}_{BG}
is concentrated in infinitely many cohomological degrees
[
Jan03
, Proposition II.12.13]
.

Proposition 5.12
.

For a connected reductive group
G
over a perfect field
k
the classifying stack
BG
admits a Frobenius splitting.

Proof.

We will deduce this from properties of the Steinberg representation of
G
, following the idea of the proof of
[
CPS83
, Theorem 2.1]
. We denote by
G^{(1)}
the Frobenius twist of
G
, viewed as a group scheme over
k
. Note that its clasiffying stack
BG^{(1)}
is the Frobenius twist of the
k
-stack
BG
, and the Frobenius morphism
F_{BG}:BG\to BG^{(1)}
is induced by the relative Frobenius map
F_{G}:G\to G^{(1)}
of the
k
-group scheme
G
.

Recall that the bounded below derived category
D^{+}(BG^{(1)})
is equivalent to the derived category
D^{+}(\Rep G^{(1)})
of the abelian category of algebraic representations of
G^{(1)}
. Under this equivalence the structure sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{BG^{(1)}}
gets carried to the trivial
1
-dimensional representation of
G^{(1)}
.

Denote by
G_{1}:=\ker(G\xrightarrow{F_{G}}G^{(1)})
the Frobenius kernel of
G
, it is a finite local group scheme over
k
. In terms of representations, the functor
RF_{BG*}:D^{+}(BG)\to D^{+}(BG^{(1)})
is described as sending
V\in D^{+}(BG)\simeq D^{+}(\Rep G)
to the cohomology complex
\mathrm{R}\Gamma(G_{1},V|_{G_{1}})
of the Frobenius kernel, equipped with the residual action of
G^{(1)}\simeq G/G_{1}
. The statement of the proposition is then equivalent to proving that the map
k\simeq H^{0}(G_{1},k)\to\mathrm{R}\Gamma(G_{1},k)
in
D(\Rep G^{(1)})
has a splitting.

For an extension
k\subset k^{\prime}
of perfect fields the natural map
\mathrm{R}\Gamma(G_{1},k)\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}\to\mathrm{R}\Gamma((G_{k^{\prime}})_{1},k^{\prime})
is an equivalence, so for the purposes of proving the proposition we may assume that
k
is algebraically closed. In particular, we may choose a Borel subgroup

Appendix A
Automatic continuity of module homomorphisms

In this appendix we prove that often a map out of an infinite product of modules is automatically continuous for the product topology, and apply this to show that a splitting of the map

-splitting for some

. The following result has been shown by Lady

[
Spe50
, Satz III]
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
R
be a smooth domain over a field

(1)

For a collection of

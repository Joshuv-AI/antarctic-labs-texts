Abstract
. In the early days of the Floer theory, Atiyah asked if there is a Milnor fiber description of the Floer homology of the links of singularities. We answer this question for the Brieskorn–Hamm complete intersection singularities. The resulting combinatorial formulas lead to an independent proof of the equality of the Casson invariants in the Donaldson and Seiberg–Witten theories. We use similar techniques to express the Heegaard Floer d d -invariant of torus knots in terms of their Milnor fibers.

The following question, which appears in the paper of Neumann and Wahl
[
30

, is attributed to Michael Atiyah:

Is there a Milnor fiber description of the Floer homology of the link? For
\Sigma(p,q,r)
, is it related to the action of complex conjugation on the homology of the Milnor fiber of
x^{p}+y^{q}+z^{r}=1
?

The Brieskorn homology spheres
\Sigma(p,q,r)
referred to in this question are the links of singularity at zero of the complex polynomial
x^{p}+y^{q}+z^{r}
, for all pairwise relatively prime integers
p
,
q
and
r
greater than or equal to 2. By the Floer homology one means the instanton Floer homology of integral homology spheres as originally defined by Floer
[
14
]
.

The same question can be asked more generally for Seifert fibered homology spheres
\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
which are the links of the Brieskorn–Hamm complete intersection singularities

\{\,c_{i1}z_{1}^{a_{1}}+\ldots+c_{in}z_{n}^{a_{n}}=0,\;i=1,\ldots,n-2\,\}\,\subset\,\mathbb{C}^{n},

(1)

where
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
are pairwise co-prime integers greater than or equal to 2 and
(c_{ij})
is any real matrix of size
(n-2)\times n
each of whose maximal minors is non-zero; see Hamm
[
18
]
. The case of
n=3
corresponds to the Brieskorn homology spheres
\Sigma(p,q,r)
.

In the late 1990s, the third named author
[
36
]
came up with a closed form formula for the Floer homology of
\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, which could be viewed as an answer to Atiyah’s question for the Brieskorn–Hamm singularities. The formula, which will be recalled in Section
1
, involves the Milnor fiber of the singularity and the complex conjugation on its link. In this paper, we wish to tie that formula more closely with the algebraic geometry of the Milnor fiber and with the progress in gauge theory of the last quarter century. We obtain two more formulas, one in terms of the compactification of the Milnor fiber in a weighted projective space in Section
2
(Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.3
), and the other in terms of the spectrum of singularity in Section
3
(Theorem
3.1
). Section
4
(Theorem
4.1
) relates our formulas to Seiberg–Witten monopoles on
\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, which leads to an independent proof of the Witten–style conjecture, proposed by Mrowka, Ruberman and Saveliev
[
27
]
, in the special case of Brieskorn–Hamm singularities. A generalization of these results to other links of singularities is discussed in Section
5
. A new formula for the
\bar{\mu}
–invariant of Neumann and Siebenmann is derived in Section
7
as part of the proof of Theorem
2.3
.

In Section
6

d
–invariant of torus knots in terms of Milnor fibers, theta characteristics, and the Steenbrink Jacobian. While the
d

Classical results

We begin by reviewing the relevant results from the 1990s. The instanton homology groups
I_{*}(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))
are free abelian groups whose instanton Betti numbers

b_{k}:=\,\operatorname{rank}I_{k}(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})),\quad k=0,1,\ldots,7,

have the property that
b_{k}=0
for even

. This was established by Fintushel and Stern

2.
Compactification of the Milnor fiber

Let us consider
n-2
polynomials
P_{i}(z)=c_{i1}z_{1}^{a_{1}}+\ldots+c_{in}z_{n}^{a_{n}}
, where
c_{ij}
are as in formula (
1
), and the map
f:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n-2}
given by

f(z)\,=(P_{1}(z),\cdots,P_{n-2}(z)).

The Milnor fiber
M(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
of the singularity
f^{-1}(0)
is the intersection of any fiber
f^{-1}(\delta)
that is non-singular with the closed unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. It is a simply-connected smooth compact spin 4-manifold with boundary
\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
.

To relate the above formulas for the instanton homology of
\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
to the Milnor fiber
M(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, we use the formula

\lambda(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))\;=\;\frac{1}{8}\,\operatorname{sign}M(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}),

(6)

which was proved for
n=3
by Fintushel and Stern
[
13
]
and for all
n\geq 4
by Neumann and Wahl
[
30
]
. Both of these proofs are combinatorial but there is also a geometric proof by Collin and Saveliev
[
8
,
9
]
. Formula (
6
) expresses, roughly speaking, half of the information contained in
I_{*}(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))
in terms of the Milnor fiber. The other half can be recovered, for instance, by expressing the quantity
b_{3}+b_{7}
in terms of the Milnor fiber. One way to accomplish this is as follows.

Let us compactify the Milnor fiber
M=M(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
in the weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}={\mathbb{C}}{\rm P}^{n}(q_{1}:\ldots:q_{n}:1)
, where
q_{i}=a/a_{i}
and
a=a_{1}\cdots a_{n}
. We obtain a singular algebraic surface
X
with cyclic quotient singularities. Its minimal resolution
Y\to X
is a smooth algebraic surface of the form
Y=M\cup P
, where
P
is a plumbed manifold with the boundary
-\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
. In fact, for
\Sigma(2,3,7)
, the manifold
Y
is the Kummer K3–surface. For
\Sigma(2,3,6k\pm 1)
, it is an elliptic surface. Otherwise,
Y
is a surface of general type.

The quantity
b_{3}+b_{7}
can be expressed in terms of the holomorphic Euler characteristic of
Y
as follows. Let
K_{Y}
be the canonical divisor of
Y
and extend the integral Wu class
w\in H_{2}(P)
by zero to a homology class on
Y
; call it again
w
. Since
K_{Y}
and
w
are both characteristic, the classes
K_{Y}\pm w
are divisible by two. We will use the notation

K_{Y}+w\,=\,2\left\lceil{K_{Y}}/2\right\rceil\quad\text{and}\quad K_{Y}-w\,=\,2\left\lfloor{K_{Y}}/2\right\rfloor.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
K_{Y}
be the canonical divisor of
Y
. Then

b_{3}\,+\,b_{7}\;=\;\chi\left(Y,\mathcal{O}\left(\left\lceil{K_{Y}}/2\right\rceil\right)\right)\;=\;\chi\left(Y,\mathcal{O}\left(\left\lfloor{K_{Y}}/2\right\rfloor\right)\right).

Proof.

Let us temporarily use notation
K_{Y}+w=2D
. It follows from the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem that

\chi\left(Y,\mathcal{O}(D)\right)\,=\,\chi(Y)\,+\,\frac{1}{2}\,D\cdot(D-K_{Y})\,=\,\chi(Y)-\frac{1}{8}\,(K_{Y}\cdot K_{Y}-w\cdot w).

Next, use Noether’s formula

\chi(Y)\,=\,\frac{1}{12}\,\left(\,c_{1}^{2}(Y)+c_{2}(Y)\right)[Y]

and the universal formula
p_{1}=c_{1}^{2}-2c_{2}
for the first Pontryagin class to deduce that

\chi(Y)\,=\,-\frac{1}{24}\;p_{1}(Y)[Y]\,+\,\frac{1}{8}\;c_{1}^{2}(Y)[Y]\,=\,-\frac{1}{8}\,\left(\operatorname{sign}\,(Y)-K_{Y}\cdot K_{Y}\right),

with the last equality following from the Hirzebruch signature theorem. This shows that

\chi\left(Y,\mathcal{O}(D)\right)\,=-\frac{1}{8}\,(\operatorname{sign}(Y)-w\cdot w)=-\lambda(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))+\bar{\mu}(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))=b_{3}+b_{7},

which proves the first formula of the theorem. The second formula is obtained by replacing
w
with
-w
in the above argument.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

It is worth noting that the compactification of a Milnor fiber in a weighted projective space also played a prominent role in the paper of Ebeling and Okonek
[
12
]
. They employed it to compute relative Donaldson polynomials of the Milnor fibers of Brieskorn–Hamm complete intersection singularities by using the TQFT properties of the Donaldson theory. It would be interesting to relate their work to ours.

We wish next to express the quantity
b_{3}+b_{7}
directly in terms of the singular surface
X
, without first resolving its singularities. This will allow us to obtain a formula for
b_{3}+b_{7}
in terms of the spectrum of singularity in Section
3
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
K_{X}
be the canonical divisor of
X
. Then

b_{3}+b_{7}\;=\;\chi\left(X,\mathcal{O}\left(\left\lceil{K_{X}}/2\right\rceil\right)\right)\;=\;\chi\left(X,\mathcal{O}\left(\left\lfloor{K_{X}}/2\right\rfloor\right)\right).

The proof of this result, which is based on Kawasaki’s version of the Atiyah–Singer index theorem, is rather technical and we postpone it until Section
7
for the sake of exposition. We hope to present an algebraic geometric proof elsewhere.

3.
Instanton homology and integer lattice points

In this section, we will identify the holomorphic Euler characteristics appearing in Theorem
2.3
with a count of integer lattice points in a simplex. This will lead to an explicit combinatorial formula for
b_{3}+b_{7}
.

3.1.
The statement

Given a complete intersection singularity (
1
), for any integer
e\geq 0
denote by
A_{e}
the set of
n
–tuples
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
of integers such that
0\leq x_{i}<a_{i}
and

e\,+\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\;\frac{x_{i}}{a_{i}}\;<\;\frac{1}{2}\,\left(n-2\,-\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\;\frac{1}{a_{i}}\right).

One can easily see that the above inequality is only possible when
e\leq\lfloor(n-3)/2\rfloor
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\big|A_{e}\big|
denote the cardinality of the set
A_{e}
and let
s=\lfloor(n-3)/2\rfloor
. Then

b_{3}+b_{7}\;=\;2\,\sum_{e=0}^{s}\;(e+1)\,\big|A_{e}\big|.

Before we go on to prove this theorem, we will explain its relation to the spectrum of singularity and spell out its statement in the special case of
n=3
, where the resulting formulas are particularly simple.

3.2.
Brieskorn singularities

Associated with the singularity at zero of the polynomial
f(x,y,z)=x^{p}+y^{q}+z^{r}
is its spectrum

\,\operatorname{Spec}_{f}\,=\,\left\{\;\frac{k}{p}+\frac{\ell}{q}+\frac{m}{r}\;\;\Big|\;\;0<k<p,\;\;0<\ell<q,\;\;0<m<r\;\right\}.

Recall that
\,\operatorname{Spec}_{f}
is the set of rational numbers characterized by the property that the complex numbers
\exp(2\pi i(k/p+\ell/q+m/r))
form a complete set of eigenvalues of the monodromy operator acting on the second homology of the Milnor fiber
M(p,q,r)
, and that their integral parts correspond to the Hodge type of the corresponding eigenvectors; see Steenbrink
[
40
]
for details. In our case, the eigenvalues are simple and there are exactly
(p-1)(q-1)(r-1)
of them, the Milnor number of the singularity. The following two theorems express the instanton homology of
\Sigma(p,q,r)
in terms of this spectrum.

Theorem 3.2
.

The quantity
b_{1}+b_{3}+b_{5}+b_{7}
equals 1/4 times
-\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}-\tau_{3}
, where
\tau_{n}
is the number of points in
\,\operatorname{Spec}_{f}
such that

n-1\;<\;\frac{k}{p}\,+\,\frac{\ell}{q}\,+\,\frac{m}{r}\;<\;n.

Proof.

The result follows easily by combining formulas (
2
) and (
6
) with the Brieskorn formula
[
5
]
for the Milnor fiber signature; see also Steenbrink
[
39
]
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

The quantity
b_{3}+b_{7}
equals twice the number of the points in
\,\operatorname{Spec}_{f}
with the property that

\frac{k}{p}\,+\,\frac{\ell}{q}\,+\,\frac{m}{r}\;<\;\frac{1}{2}\,\left(1\,+\,\frac{1}{p}\,+\,\frac{1}{q}\,+\,\frac{1}{r}\right).

(7)

Proof.

Theorem
3.1
implies that
b_{3}+b_{7}=2\,|A_{0}|
, where the set
A_{0}
consists of the triples
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
of integers such that
0\leq x_{1}<p
,
0\leq x_{2}<q
,
0\leq x_{3}<r
and

\frac{x_{1}}{p}\,+\,\frac{x_{2}}{q}\,+\,\frac{x_{3}}{r}\;<\;\frac{1}{2}\,\left(1\,-\,\frac{1}{p}\,-\,\frac{1}{q}\,-\,\frac{1}{r}\right).

Substituting
k=x_{1}+1
,
\ell=x_{2}+1
and
m=x_{3}+1
results in inequality (
7
). The result now follows because (
7
) automatically implies that
k<p
,
\ell<q
and
m<r
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Singularities (
1
) with homology sphere links have been studied by Hamm
[
18
]
for all
n\geq 4
. While the eigenvalues of the monodromy operator are always of the form
\exp(2\pi i(\ell_{1}/a_{1}+\ldots+\ell_{n}/a_{n}))
, for non-negative integers
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{n}
, their multiplicities are generally greater than one, and their precise relation to the formula of Theorem

remains unclear. In addition, we are not aware of an analogue of the Brieskorn formula for the Milnor fiber signature when

.

k=0,\ldots,p
, by re-writing the above condition in the form

(s-p)\,+\,(p-k)\,+\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\;\frac{x_{i}}{a_{i}}\;<\frac{1}{2}\,\left(n-2-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\;\frac{1}{a_{i}}\right)

and distributing the
p-k
units into the summands
x_{i}/a_{i}
. The resulting
n
–tuple in
B_{s-p}
is obtained by adding several copies of
a_{i}
to the respective
x_{i}
. The number of ways to distribute
p-k
units into
\ell
slots,
\ell=1,\ldots,p-k
, while accounting for their order, equals

{{p-k-1}\choose{\ell-1}}{{n}\choose{\ell}},

where the first factor is the well known combinatorial quantity, the number of compositions of
p-k
into
\ell
parts.

Lemma 3.5
.

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{p-k}\;{{p-k-1}\choose{\ell-1}}{{n}\choose{\ell}}\;=\;{{n+p-k-1}\choose{n-1}}
.

Proof.

This follows by comparing the
t^{n-1}
terms in the binomial expansions of the individual terms in the identity
(1+x)^{n}\,(1+x)^{p-k-1}=(1+x)^{n+p-k-1}
.
∎

By adding all possible contributions of
A_{s-k}
with
k=0,\ldots,p
into
B_{s-p}
with
p=0,\ldots,s
, we obtain the formula

\Big|B_{s-p}\Big|\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{p}\;{{n+p-k-1}\choose{n-1}}\;\Big|A_{s-k}\Big|.

Proving the formula
A=B
of the theorem now reduces to checking the following combinatorial identity.

Lemma 3.6
.

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{k}\;(-1)^{p}\,{{n+k-p+1}\choose{n-1}}{{n}\choose p}\;=\;k+1
.

Proof.

The proof will proceed by expanding the identity
(1+x)^{-n-2}(1+x)^{n}=(1+x)^{-2}
into a Taylor series in two different ways. On the one hand,

(1+x)^{-n-2}\;=\;\sum_{a=0}^{\infty}\;(-1)^{a}\,{{n+a+1}\choose{n+1}}\,x^{a}

and

(1+x)^{n}\;=\;\sum_{b=0}^{n}\;{n\choose b}\,x^{b}

hence

(1+x)^{-n-2}(1+x)^{n}\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\;\sum_{a+b=k}\;(-1)^{a}\,{{n+a+1}\choose{n+1}}{n\choose b}\,x^{k}.

Substituting
b=p
and
a=k-p
we turn the last expression into

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\;\sum_{p=0}^{k}\;(-1)^{k-p}\,{{n+k-p+1}\choose{n+1}}{n\choose p}\,x^{k}.

On the other hand,

(1+x)^{-2}\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\;(-1)^{k}\,(1+k)\,x^{k}.

The result now follows by comparing the coefficients of the
x^{k}
terms in the last two formulas.
∎

4.
The Seiberg–Witten equations

This section builds on the observation that the right hand side of the formula of Theorem
3.1
matches a count of irreducible Seiberg–Witten monopoles on
\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
; see Theorem
4.1
. This leads to an independent proof of the equality of the Casson invariants of
\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
in the Donaldson and Seiberg–Witten theories inspired by the Witten conjecture
[
43
]
.

We begin with a brief account of the Seiberg–Witten theory following Mrowka, Ozsváth and Yu
[
26
]
. Given a closed oriented Riemannian spin 3-manifold
N
, the Seiberg–Witten equations are the system of non-linear partial differential equations

*F_{A}=\sigma(\psi),\quad D_{A}\psi=0

on a pair
(A,\psi)
, where
A
is a
U(1)
–connection and
\psi
a spinor. The solutions are called monopoles, and the solutions with
\psi\neq 0
are called irreducible monopoles. The gauge equivalence classes of irreducible monopoles form the moduli space
\mathcal{M}(N)
. For a generic metric, and perhaps a small perturbation, the moduli space
\mathcal{M}(N)
consists of finitely many points, which are canonically oriented.

According to
[
26
]
, the natural metric realizing the Thurston geometry on
\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
is generic for
n=3
and
n=4
but not for higher
n
. For the unique spin structure on
\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, the Seiberg–Witten moduli space
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))
is a collection of isolated points in the case of
n=3
and
n=4
. In general, it consists of finitely many complex projective spaces of varying dimensions. Denote by
\#\mathcal{M}
the oriented count of points in
\mathcal{M}
after a small generic perturbation chosen so that each complex projective space contributes its Euler characteristic to
\#\mathcal{M}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For any complete intersection singularity (
1
), instanton Floer homology of
\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
and the Seiberg–Witten monopoles on
\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
are related by the formula

b_{3}+b_{7}\;=\;\#\mathcal{M}.

Proof.

It follows from
[
26
]
that

\#\mathcal{M}\;=\;2\,\sum_{e=0}^{s}\;(e+1)\,\big|A_{e}\big|.

Now the claim follows from Theorem
3.1
.
∎

Let
\Sigma
be an arbitrary oriented integral homology 3-sphere. For the unique spin structure on
\Sigma
, define the Seiberg–Witten analogue of the Casson invariant by the formula

\lambda_{\,\operatorname{SW}\,}(\Sigma)\;=\;-\,\#\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)-\,\frac{1}{2}\,\eta_{\,\operatorname{Dir}}(\Sigma)\,-\,\frac{1}{8}\,\eta_{\,\operatorname{Sign}}(\Sigma),

where
\eta_{\,\operatorname{Dir}}
and
\eta_{\,\operatorname{Sign}}
are the eta–invariants of the spin Dirac and the odd signature operators, respectively; see Atiyah–Patodi–Singer
[
2
]
. Note that the individual terms in the definition of
\lambda_{\,\operatorname{SW}\,}(\Sigma)
are metric dependent but their combination is a topological quantity
[
7
,
22
]
. It was conjectured by Peter Kronheimer and proved by Yuhan Lim
[
22
]
that

\lambda(\Sigma)\;=\;\lambda_{\,\operatorname{SW}\,}(\Sigma)

(10)

for all integral homology spheres
\Sigma
. In particular, equality (
10
) holds in the special case of
\Sigma=\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, where it was also independently proved by Nicolaescu
[
31
, Formula (0.2)]
for
n=3
and
n=4
. Our combinatorial formulas lead to yet another proof of identity (
10
) for
\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
.

Corollary 4.2
.

For any complete intersection singularity (
1
), we have

\lambda(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))\;=\;\lambda_{\,\operatorname{SW}\,}(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})).

Proof.

Combine the formula of Theorem
4.1
with formula (
5
) to conclude that

\lambda(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))\,=\,-\,\#\mathcal{M}(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))\,+\,\bar{\mu}(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))

and complete the proof by using the formula

\bar{\mu}(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))\;=\;-\frac{1}{2}\,\eta_{\,\operatorname{Dir}}(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))-\frac{1}{8}\,\eta_{\,\operatorname{Sign}}(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))

(11)

of Ruberman and Saveliev
[
34
]
. Note that formula (
11
) is a special case of a general formula for the Lefschetz number in monopole Floer homology proved by Lin, Ruberman, and Saveliev
[
23
]
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

It should be noted that both Lim’s formula (
10
) and the formula of
[
23
]
(of which formula (
11
) is a special case) are instances of a general Witten–style conjecture proposed by Mrowka, Ruberman, and Saveliev
[
27
]
relating the Donaldson and Seiberg–Witten invariants of 4-manifolds
X
with homology of
S^{1}\times S^{3}
. Formula (
10
) corresponds to
X=S^{1}\times\Sigma
, and formula (
11
) to the mapping torus
X
of the complex conjugation involution on the link
\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
. The above proof of (
10
) then qualifies as an independent verification of the Witten–style conjecture of
[
27
]
for
X=S^{1}\times\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
.

5.
Comments and generalizations

We have observed throughout this paper that dealing with Brieskorn homology spheres
\Sigma(p,q,r)
is much easier than with the general case of
\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
. One way to get around the complications arising when
n\geq 4
is to use the following additivity in the instanton Floer homology
[
38
, Corollary 9]
. Given a Seifert fibered homology sphere
\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, let
q=a_{1}\cdots a_{j}
and
p=a_{j+1}\cdots a_{n}
be the products of the first
j
, respectively, last
n-j
, Seifert invariants, for any
2\leq j\leq n-2
. Then

I_{*}(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))\,=\,I_{*}(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{j},p))\,\oplus\,I_{*}(\Sigma(q,a_{j+1},\ldots,a_{n})).

(12)

After sufficiently many iterations, this formula reduces instanton homology calculation for
\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
to that for Brieskorn homology spheres.

The results of this paper can be partially generalized to more complicated links of singularities. Let
\Sigma
be the link of an isolated complete intersection surface singularity and assume that
\Sigma
is an integral homology sphere. As before, the Euler characteristic of
I_{*}(\Sigma)
equals
2\lambda(\Sigma)
, and the Lefschetz number of the map induced on the instanton Floer homology by the complex conjugation involution equals
2\bar{\mu}(\Sigma)
(see
[
42
]
and
[
35
, Section 9.2]
, respectively). In addition, the formula (
6
) for the signature of the Milnor fiber, which was conjectured to hold for all links
\Sigma
as above by Neumann and Wahl
[
30
]
, has been proved for all links
\Sigma
of splice type by Némethi and Okuma
[
28
]
. These results, however, are not sufficient for computing
I_{*}(\Sigma)
because not much is known about the overall structure of
I_{*}(\Sigma)
for general links.

6.
The
d
–invariant of torus knots

Let
p
,
q
be coprime positive integers and consider the singularity at zero of the complex polynomial
f(x,y)=x^{p}+y^{q}
. For any
t\in\Delta:=\{t\in\mathbb{C}~|~|t|<\epsilon\ll 1\}
, denote by
\mathcal{X}_{t}
the compactification of the Milnor fiber
M(p,q)=f^{-1}(t)
in the weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}={\mathbb{C}}{\rm P}^{2}(q:p:1)
. That is to say,
\mathcal{X}_{t}
is the complex curve

\mathcal{X}_{t}=\{x^{p}+y^{q}-tz^{pq}=0\}\;\subset\;\mathbb{P}.

Note that, for any
t\in\Delta\setminus\{0\}
, the curve
\mathcal{X}_{t}
is smooth of genus
(p-1)(q-1)/{2}
since it intersects the line
z=0
at a single point and the first Betti number of the affine curve
x^{p}+y^{q}=t
is
(p-1)(q-1)
; see Milnor
[
24
]
.

Remark 6.1
.

The integer
pq-p-q-1
is always even because
p
and
q
are coprime. Therefore, for each
\mathcal{X}_{t}
, we have a canonical theta characteristic

\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{K_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}}{2}\Big)\,=\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\frac{pq-p-q-1}{2}\Big)\Big|_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}

obtained by the restricting the Weil divisor on
\mathbb{P}
, computed using the adjunction formula, to the smooth divisor
{\mathcal{X}_{t}}
as in Dolgachev
[
11
, Section 3.5.2]
.

The
d
–invariant of a knot
K\subset S^{3}
was defined by Ozsváth and Szabó
[
32
]
as

d(K)=d(S^{3}_{1}(K)),

the correction term in Heegaard Floer homology of the 3-manifold
S^{3}_{1}(K)
obtained by (+1)–surgery on the knot
K
. Let
K=T_{p,q}
be the right-handed
(p,q)
–torus knot then

S^{3}_{1}(T_{p,q})=-\Sigma(p,q,pq-1).

Theorem 6.2
.

For any
t\in\Delta\setminus\{0\}
, denote by
K_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}
the canonical divisor of
\mathcal{X}_{t}
. Then

d(T_{p,q})\;=\;-2h^{0}\left(\mathcal{X}_{t},\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{K_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}}{2}\right)\right).

(13)

Proof.

Our proof will rely on two formulas of Borodzik and Nemethi
[
4
]
. One formula
[
4
, Corollary 7.3]
states that

d(T_{p,q})=-2\left|\left\{s\notin S_{p,q}~\Big|~s\geq\frac{(p-1)(q-1)}{2}\right\}\right|

for the semigroup

S_{p,q}=\{ap+bq\in\mathbb{Z}~|~a\geq 0,\;b\geq 0\}.

The other formula relates
S_{p,q}
to the spectrum of the singularity of
f(x,y)
,

\,\operatorname{Spec}_{f}=\left\{\;\frac{i}{p}+\frac{j}{q}~\Big|~0<i<p,\;0<j<q\;\right\}.

For any integer
0\leq\alpha<pq
, it claims that

\Big|\{s\notin S_{p,q}~\big|~s\geq\alpha\}\Big|\,=\,\left|\left\{\left[1+\frac{\alpha}{pq},2\right)\cap\,\operatorname{Spec}_{f}\right\}\right|,

see
[
4
, Proposition 7.1]
.

To calculate the right hand side of the formula (
13
), we will follow an approach similar to that in Section
3.3
. Consider the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(-pq)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}\longrightarrow 0.

Note that
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(-pq)
is locally free and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\left((pq-p-q-1)/2\right)
is locally free at
\mathcal{X}_{t}
; see Mori
[
25
]
for details. Tensor the above short exact sequence with
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\left((pq-p-q-1)/2\right)
to obtain the short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{-pq-p-q-1}{2}\right)\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{pq-p-q-1}{2}\right)\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}\left(\frac{pq-p-q-1}{2}\right)\to 0.

It follows from Dolgachev
[
11
]
that

H^{0}\left(\mathbb{P},\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{-pq-p-q-1}{2}\right)\right)=H^{1}\left(\mathbb{P},\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{-pq-p-q-1}{2}\right)\right)=0

and

\;H^{1}\left(\mathbb{P},\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\,\frac{pq-p-q-1}{2}\right)\right)\;=\;H^{2}\left(\mathbb{P},\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\,\frac{pq-p-q-1}{2}\right)\right)\,=\,0.

Therefore,

H^{0}\left(\mathcal{X}_{t},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}\left(\frac{K_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}}{2}\right)\right)=H^{0}\left(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{pq-p-q-1}{2}\right)\right)\\
=\;\mathbb{C}\,\left\langle x^{a}y^{b}z^{c}~\Big|~qa+pb+c=\frac{pq-p-q-1}{2}\right\rangle,

from which we conclude that

h^{0}\left(\mathcal{X}_{t},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}\left(\frac{K_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}}{2}\right)\right)

equals the number of integer lattice points
(a,b,c)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
such that
a\geq 0
,
b\geq 0
,
c\geq 0
and

qa+pb+c\,=\,\frac{pq-p-q-1}{2}.

In turn, this equals the number of integer lattice points
(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
a\geq 0
,
b\geq 0
and

\frac{a+1}{p}+\,\frac{b+1}{q}\,\leq\,\frac{pq+p+q-1}{2pq}.

Using the aforementioned Borodzik–Nemethi formulas and the symmetry of
\,\operatorname{Spec}_{f}
, we obtain

\displaystyle d(T_{p,q})

\displaystyle=-2\left|\left\{s\notin S_{p,q}~\Big|~s\geq\frac{(p-1)(q-1)}{2}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=-2\left|\left\{\left[1+\frac{(p-1)(q-1)}{2pq},2\right)\cap\,\operatorname{Spec}_{f}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=-2\left|\left\{\left(0,\frac{pq+p+q-1}{2pq}\right]\cap\,\operatorname{Spec}_{f}\right\}\right|\;=\;-2h^{0}\left(\mathcal{X}_{t},\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{K_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}}{2}\right)\right).

∎

Remark 6.3
.

A modulo 2 version of the formula of Theorem
6.2
, namely,

-\frac{1}{2}\;d(T_{p,q})\;=\;h^{0}\left(\mathcal{X}_{t},\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{K_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}}{2}\right)\right)\pmod{2},

(14)

can be derived more directly from the results already in the literature. According to
[
32
]
,

-\frac{1}{2}\;d(T_{p,q})\;=\;\sum_{j\geq 1}\;ja_{j},

where
a_{j}
are the coefficients of the Alexander polynomial

\Delta(t)\;=\;a_{0}+\sum_{j\geq 1}\;a_{j}(t^{j}+t^{-j})

of the knot
T_{p,q}
normalized so that
\Delta(1)=1
and
\Delta(t^{-1})=\Delta(t)
. A direct calculation then shows that

-\frac{1}{2}\;d(T_{p,q})\;=\;\sum_{j\geq 1}\;ja_{j}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\;\Delta^{\prime\prime}(1)\pmod{2},

which is known to equal the Robertello invariant, or the Arf invariant, of the knot
T_{p,q}
.

On the other hand, according to
[
21
, Proposition 4.2]
, the Robertello invariant of
T_{p,q}
is the difference of the Arf invariants of the
\mathbb{Z}/2
quadratic forms corresponding to the theta characteristics (or spin structures)
L=(K_{\mathbb{P}}+{\mathcal{X}}_{t})/2
and
(K_{\mathbb{P}}+{\mathcal{X}}_{0})/2
on
{\mathcal{X}}_{t}
and
{\mathcal{X}}_{0}
, respectively (since
\mathcal{X}_{t}
contains no singularities of
\mathbb{P}
, the result of
[
21
]
applies even though
\mathbb{P}
is singular). The claim now follows because
H^{1}({\mathcal{X}}_{0},\mathbb{Z}/2)=0
and the Robertello invariant of the quadratic form corresponding to the theta characteristic
L
equals

h^{0}\left(\mathcal{X}_{t},\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{K_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}}{2}\right)\right);

see
[
21
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Remark 6.4
.

One can use Riemann’s singularity theorem
[
1
, Chapter 6, Section 1]
to interpret the right-hand side of the relation (
14
) as the multiplicity of the theta divisor of the Jacobian of
{\mathcal{X}}_{t}
at the point corresponding to (the translate of) canonical theta characteristic used in Theorem
6.2
. This is a special case of the relation
[
21
]
between the mod 2 Seifert form of a link of a plane curve singularity and the canonical theta characteristic on the Steenbrink Jacobian of the Milnor fiber of singularity. One may wonder is this observation, together with the above results, extends to other links of singularities.

7.
A formula for the
\bar{\mu}
–invariant

Let
X
be the compactification of the Milnor fiber of the complete intersection (
1
) in the weighted projective space and let
K
be the canonical divisor of

b_{3}+b_{7}\;=\;\chi\left(X,\mathcal{O}\left(\left\lceil{K}/2\right\rceil\right)\right)\;=\;\chi\left(X,\mathcal{O}\left(\left\lfloor{K}/2\right\rfloor\right)\right)

of Theorem
2.3
. As in the proof of Theorem

, we will first compute
\chi\left(X,\mathcal{O}\left(\left\lceil{K}/2\right\rceil\right)\right)
using the orbifold version of the Atiyah–Singer index theorem due to Kawasaki
[
19
]
and then compare it with
b_{3}+b_{7}
.

Our task is significantly simplified by the fact that the necessary index calculations have already been done by Fukumoto and Furuta
[
16
, Section 5]
. Their formulas easily imply that
\chi\left(X,\mathcal{O}\left(\left\lceil{K}/2\right\rceil\right)\right)=\chi\left(X,\mathcal{O}\left(\left\lfloor{K}/2\right\rfloor\right)\right)
and reduce the proof to checking that
\bar{\mu}(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))
is given by the formula of
[
16
, Theorem 12]
for the appropriate choice of the orbifold line bundle. In the case of even
a_{1}\cdots a_{n}
this was done by Fukumoto, Furuta and Ue
[
17
, Theorem 2]
, see also Saveliev
[
37
, Theorem 1]
, hence we only need to address the case of odd
a_{1}\cdots a_{n}
. After accounting for varying orientation conventions, we wish to verify the formula

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}-\frac{1}{8}+\;\frac{1}{8}\;\sum_{n=1}^{n}\;\frac{1}{a_{i}}\;\sum_{\ell=1}^{a_{i}-1}\;\cot\left(\frac{\pi\ell}{a_{i}}\right)\cot\left(\frac{\pi b_{i}\ell}{a_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{1}{4}\;\sum_{n=1}^{n}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{i}}\;\sum_{\ell=1}^{a_{i}-1}\;\cos\left(\frac{\pi(1+b_{i}+2mb_{i})\ell}{a_{i}}\right)\csc\left(\frac{\pi\ell}{a_{i}}\right)\csc\left(\frac{\pi b_{i}\ell}{a_{i}}\right),

where

m\,=\,-\frac{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}{2}\cdot\left(-2\,+\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\;\left(1-\frac{1}{a_{i}}\right)\right)\quad\text{and}\quad a_{1}\cdots a_{n}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{n}\;\frac{b_{i}}{a_{i}}\,=\,-1.

After checking that
(1+2mb_{i})/a_{i}
is an odd integer for all

we reduce the formula to

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))=\;\frac{1}{8a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}-\frac{1}{8}+\;\frac{1}{8}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\;\frac{1}{a_{i}}\;\sum_{\ell=1}^{a_{i}-1}\;\cot\left(\frac{\pi\ell}{a_{i}}\right)\cot\left(\frac{\pi b_{i}\ell}{a_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{1}{4}\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{i}}\;\sum_{\ell=1}^{a_{i}-1}\;(-1)^{\ell}\csc\left(\frac{\pi\ell}{a_{i}}\right)\cot\left(\frac{\pi b_{i}\ell}{a_{i}}\right).

We will proceed by identifying this formula with the known formula for the
\bar{\mu}
–invariant,

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}(\Sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))\;=\;\frac{1}{8a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}-\frac{1}{8}-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\;s(a_{1}\cdots a_{n}/a_{i},a_{i})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\;s(a_{1}\ldots a_{n}/a_{i},a_{i};1/2,1/2),

see
[
34
, Formula 4]
, which is based of a calculation by Nicolaescu
[
31
]
. All we need to do is match trigonometric sums in the former formula with the respective Dedekind–Rademacher sums in the latter.

Recall that the Dedekind–Rademacher sums
s(q,p;x,y)
are defined for pairwise coprime integers
p,q>0
and arbitrary real numbers
x
and
y
by the formula

s(q,p;x,y)\;=\sum_{h\hskip-5.78172pt\mod p}\;\left(\hskip-5.05942pt\left(\frac{h+y}{p}\right)\hskip-5.05942pt\right)\left(\hskip-5.05942pt\left(\frac{q(h+y)}{p}+x\right)\hskip-5.05942pt\right),

where, for any real number
r
, we set

(\hskip-1.4457pt(r)\hskip-1.4457pt)=\begin{cases}\;0,&\text{if $r\in\mathbb{Z}$},\\
r-\lfloor r\rfloor-1/2,&\text{if $r\notin\mathbb{Z}$}.\end{cases}

When both
x
and
y
are integers, we get back the classical Dedekind sum

s(q,p)\;=\sum_{h\hskip-5.78172pt\mod p}\;\left(\hskip-5.05942pt\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)\hskip-5.05942pt\right)\left(\hskip-5.05942pt\left(\frac{qh}{p}\right)\hskip-5.05942pt\right).

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
h
,
p
be coprime integers such that
p\geq 1
is odd and let
\zeta=e^{2\pi i/p}
. Then

\left(\hskip-5.05942pt\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)\hskip-5.05942pt\right)\;=\;\frac{i}{2p}\;\sum_{n=1}^{p-1}\;\cot\left(\frac{\pi n}{p}\right)\zeta^{hn}\quad\text{and}

(15)

\left(\hskip-5.05942pt\left(\frac{h}{p}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\hskip-5.05942pt\right)\,=\,\frac{i}{2p}\;\sum_{n=1}^{p-1}\;(-1)^{n}\csc\left(\frac{\pi n}{p}\right)\zeta^{hn}.

(16)

Proof.

The first formula is proved on pages 13–14 of
[
33
]
, and we will prove the second by a slight modification of that argument. The function in question has period
p
and thus can be expressed as

\left(\hskip-5.05942pt\left(\frac{h}{p}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\hskip-5.05942pt\right)\;=\;\sum_{m\hskip-5.78172pt\mod p}\;a_{m}\zeta^{mh}

with certain Fourier coefficients
a_{m}
which can be found from the formula

\sum_{h\hskip-5.78172pt\mod p}\;\left(\hskip-5.05942pt\left(\frac{h}{p}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\hskip-5.05942pt\right)\zeta^{-hn}\;=\;\sum_{m\hskip-5.78172pt\mod p}\;a_{m}\sum_{h\hskip-5.78172pt\mod p}\;\zeta^{(m-n)h}\;=\;pa_{n}.

According to Lemma 1 on page 4 of
[
33
]
,

\sum_{h\hskip-5.78172pt\mod p}\;\left(\hskip-5.05942pt\left(\frac{h+x}{p}\right)\hskip-5.05942pt\right)\,=\,(\hskip-1.4457pt(x)\hskip-1.4457pt),

hence for
n=0
we have

a_{0}=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{h\hskip-5.78172pt\mod p}\;\left(\hskip-5.05942pt\left(\frac{h}{p}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\hskip-5.05942pt\right)\,=\,\frac{1}{p}\sum_{h\hskip-5.78172pt\mod p}\;\left(\hskip-5.05942pt\left(\frac{h+p/2}{p}\right)\hskip-5.05942pt\right)\,=\,\frac{1}{p}\,\left(\hskip-2.8903pt\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)\hskip-2.8903pt\right)=0.

On the other hand, if
n\neq 0
, we obtain

a_{n}\,=\,\frac{1}{p}\;\sum_{h=1}^{p-1}\;\left(\hskip-5.05942pt\left(\frac{h}{p}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\hskip-5.05942pt\right)\zeta^{-hn}\,=\,\frac{1}{p^{2}}\sum_{h=1}^{(p-1)/2}\;h\zeta^{-hn}\,+\\
\frac{1}{p^{2}}\sum_{h=(p+1)/2}^{p-1}\;(h-p)\zeta^{-hn}\,=\,\frac{1}{p^{2}}\;\sum_{h=1}^{p-1}\;h\zeta^{-hn}-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{h=(p+1)/2}^{p-1}\;\zeta^{-hn}.

A straightforward calculation with the geometric series shows that

\sum_{h=1}^{p-1}\;h\zeta^{-hn}=-\frac{p}{1-\zeta^{-n}}\quad\text{and}\;\sum_{h=(p+1)/2}^{p-1}\;\zeta^{-hn}=\frac{\zeta^{-n(p+1)/2}-1}{1-\zeta^{-n}}

Therefore,

a_{n}=-\frac{1}{p}\cdot\frac{\zeta^{-n(p+1)/2}}{1-\zeta^{-n}}

and

\left(\hskip-5.05942pt\left(\frac{h}{p}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\hskip-5.05942pt\right)\;=\;-\frac{1}{p}\;\sum_{n=1}^{p-1}\;\frac{\zeta^{-n(p+1)/2}}{1-\zeta^{-n}}\,\zeta^{hn}\\
=-\frac{1}{p}\;\sum_{n=1}^{p-1}\;\frac{\zeta^{-np/2}}{\zeta^{\,n/2}-\zeta^{-n/2}}\,\zeta^{hn}\;=\;\frac{i}{2p}\,\sum_{n=1}^{p-1}\;(-1)^{n}\csc\left(\frac{\pi n}{p}\right)\zeta^{hn}.

∎

Distortion Bounds of Subdivision Models for S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(3)
Chee Yap, Zhaoqi Zhang

Abstract
In the subdivision approach to robot path planning, we need to subdivide the configuration space of a robot into nice cells to perform various computations. For a rigid spatial robot, this configuration space is S ​ E ​ ( 3 ) = ℝ 3 × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SE(3)=\mathbb{R}^{3}\times SO(3) . The subdivision of ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} is standard but so far, there are no global subdivision schemes for S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(3) . We recently introduced a representation for S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(3) suitable for subdivision. This paper investigates the distortion of the natural metric on S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(3) caused by our representation. The proper framework for this study lies in the Riemannian geometry of S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(3) , enabling us to obtain exact distortion bounds.

Lemma 1

\begin{array}[]{llll}\frac{d_{Z}(h(p),h(q))}{d_{X}(p,q)}&=&\frac{d_{Z}(g(f(p)),g(f(q)))}{d_{X}(p,q)}\\

&\in&[c,d]\cdot[a,b]=[ca,db].\end{array}

\displaystyle=\big\langle J_{\mu_{3}}\bm{\cdot}v_{\bm{p}},J_{\mu_{3}}\bm{\cdot}v_{\bm{p}}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=v_{\bm{p}}^{\mathsf{T}}(J_{\mu_{3}}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\cdot}J_{\mu_{3}})v_{\bm{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{4}}v_{\bm{p}}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\cdot}\begin{pmatrix}r^{2}-x^{2}&-xy&-xz\\
-xy&r^{2}-y^{2}&-yz\\
-xz&-yz&r^{2}-z^{2}\end{pmatrix}\cdot v_{\bm{p}}

(from (
11
))

\displaystyle=\frac{E}{r^{4}}

(13)

where

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathrel{\,:=\,}}(r^{2}-x^{2})\textrm{dx}^{2}+(r^{2}-y^{2})\textrm{dy}^{2}+(r^{2}-z^{2})\textrm{dz}^{2}-2\big(xy\textrm{dx}\textrm{dy}+yz\textrm{dy}\textrm{dz}+xz\textrm{dx}\textrm{dz}\big)

To facilitate further manipulation, rewrite
E
in the compact form

E=\sum_{i}(r^{2}-x_{i}^{2})\textrm{dx}_{i}^{2}-2\sum_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}\textrm{dx}_{i}\textrm{dx}_{j}

(14)

where
(x,y,z)=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
and the sums

\sum_{i},\sum_{i,j}
(and
\sum_{i,j,k}
) are interpreted
appropriately:

i,j,k
range over
\left\{1,2,3\right\}
, with

i
chosen independently,
but
j
chosen to be different from
i
,
and
k
chosen to be different from
i
and
j
.
Thus each sum has exactly 3 summands.

To obtain upper and lower bounds on

\mu_{3}^{\ast}(g_{S^{3}})\langle v_{\bm{p}},v_{\bm{p}}\rangle=E/r^{4}
, we need
to express
E
in two different ways:

(A)

For the lower bound,

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}(r^{2}-x_{i}^{2})\textrm{dx}_{i}^{2}-2\sum_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}\textrm{dx}_{i}\textrm{dx}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}(1+x_{j}^{2}+x_{k}^{2})\textrm{dx}_{i}^{2}-2\sum_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}\textrm{dx}_{i}\textrm{dx}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\textrm{dx}_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i,j}(x_{i}^{2}\textrm{dx}_{j}^{2}+x_{j}^{2}\textrm{dy}_{i}^{2})-2\sum_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}\textrm{dx}_{i}\textrm{dx}_{j}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{i,j}(x_{i}\textrm{dx}_{j}-x_{j}\textrm{dx}_{i})^{2}

(as
1=\sum_{i}\textrm{dx}_{i}^{2}
)

Hence,

\mu_{3}^{\ast}(g_{S^{3}})\langle v_{\bm{p}},v_{\bm{p}}\rangle=\frac{1+\sum_{i,j}(x_{i}\textrm{dy}_{j}-y_{j}\textrm{dx}_{i})^{2}}{r^{4}}\geq\frac{1}{(1+x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2})^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{16}.

The last two inequalities become equalities when

x=y=z=1
and
\textrm{dx}=\textrm{dy}=\textrm{dz}=1/\sqrt{3}
.
This proves a tight lower bound of
1/16
for

\mu_{3}^{\ast}(g_{S_{3}})\langle v_{\bm{p}},v_{\bm{p}}\rangle
.

(B)

To obtain an upper bound, we rewrite
E
as follows:

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}(1+x_{j}+x_{k})\textrm{dx}_{i}^{2}-2\sum_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}\textrm{dx}_{i}\textrm{dx}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\textrm{dx}_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i,j,k}x_{i}^{2}(\textrm{dx}_{j}^{2}+\textrm{dx}_{k}^{2})-2\sum_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}\textrm{dx}_{i}\textrm{dx}_{j}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2}(1-\textrm{dx}_{i}^{2})-2\sum_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}\textrm{dx}_{i}\textrm{dx}_{j}

(as
1=\sum_{i}\textrm{dx}_{i}^{2}
)

\displaystyle=\Big(1+\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2}\Big)-\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2}\textrm{dx}_{i}^{2}-2\sum_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}\textrm{dx}_{i}\textrm{dx}_{j}

\displaystyle=r^{2}-\Big(\sum_{i}x_{i}\textrm{dx}_{i}\Big)^{2}

\mu_{3}^{\ast}(g_{S_{3}})\langle v_{\bm{p}},v_{\bm{p}}\rangle=\frac{r^{2}-(x\textrm{dx}+y\textrm{dy}+z\textrm{dz})^{2}}{r^{4}}\\
\leq\frac{1}{r^{2}}\\
\leq 1.

The last two inequalities
are equalities when
x=y=z=0
.

We have therefore established that

\sqrt{\mu_{3}^{\ast}(g_{S_{3}})\langle v_{\bm{p}},v_{\bm{p}}\rangle}\in[\tfrac{1}{4},1]

and these bounds are achievable.

Q.E.D.

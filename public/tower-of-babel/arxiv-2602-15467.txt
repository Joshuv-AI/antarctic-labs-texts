Cluster Ising quantum batteries can mimic super-extensive charging power
Anna Pavone, Federico Luigi Cavagnaro, Matteo Carrega, Riccardo Grazi, Dario Ferraro, Niccolò Traverso Ziani

Abstract
Quantum batteries—miniaturized devices able to store and release energy on demand—are promising both because their intrinsic energy and time scales can match those of other quantum technologies and due to the intriguing possibility of achieving super-extensive charging power. While this enhanced scaling is known to appear in several settings, it is generally believed to be forbidden in Wigner–Jordan integrable spin chains charged via quantum-quench protocols. Here, we show that an extended cluster-Ising model, despite belonging to the above category, exhibits super-extensive charging power over wide ranges of system sizes, reaching up to a thousand spins, in proper parameter regimes. This remarkable anomalous scaling is due to a corresponding super-extensive growth of the stored energy, implying that it occurs at large but finite size and cannot persist in the thermodynamic limit. This phenomenon appears robust against finite-temperature effects.

Introduction

The quest to determine whether quantum technologies can outperform their classical counterparts is one of the main challenges across modern quantum science

;

. Within this vast landscape quantum batteries (QBs), devices devoted to store and release energy exploiting purely quantum effects, offer a fertile ground

Among the various figures of merit considered to characterize the performance of these devices, an important role is played by the stored

N

Such enhanced scaling may originate from mechanisms that can be collective in nature—which can emerge also in classical or semiclassical systems—or, more interestingly, be due to an actual quantum advantage

II
Underlying Models

As QB Hamiltonians we consider two different versions of the same kind of generalized cluster-Ising model
SmacchiaPRA2011
;
DingPRE2019
;
KheiriSR2025
. Explicitly we set (up to an overall energy prefactor)

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\cos\phi_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sigma^{x}_{j}\,O^{z}_{j,n}\,\sigma^{x}_{j+n+1}+\sin\phi_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sigma^{z}_{j}

(1)

\displaystyle H_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\cos\phi_{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{l=1}^{n}\frac{1}{n}\sigma^{x}_{j}\,O^{z}_{j,l}\,\sigma^{x}_{j+l+1}+\sin\phi_{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sigma^{z}_{j},

(2)

with

O^{z}_{j,n}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\sigma^{z}_{j+k}.

(3)

Here,
\sigma^{\alpha}_{j}
, (
\alpha=x,y,z
) are the Pauli matrices in the usual representation for the site

,
\phi_{1/2}
are free real parameters,

is the number of sites composing the chain and

is the range of the cluster interaction term. We adopt periodic boundary conditions. The two Hamiltonians under investigation are distinguished by a different form of the multi-spin interaction. In the case of

Both models are
Wigner-Jordan
integrable, meaning that they can be mapped to free fermion models

. It is worth to note that a model with different weights in front of each string would also be integrable, but we do not consider it. Explicitly, to solve the models, one first performs the
Wigner-Jordan
transformation to spinless fermions
c_{j}
given by
JordanWigner1928

\displaystyle\sigma^{z}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-2c^{\dagger}_{j}c_{j},

(4)

\displaystyle\sigma^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{l<j}(1-2c^{\dagger}_{l}c_{l})c_{j},

(5)

\displaystyle\sigma^{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{l<j}(1-2c^{\dagger}_{l}c_{l})c^{\dagger}_{j},

(6)

with
\sigma^{\pm}_{j}=(\sigma^{x}_{j}\pm i\sigma^{y}_{j})/2

\psi_{q}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-i\frac{2\pi}{N}qj}c_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{N}(n+1)q\right)\cos\phi_{1} -\sin\phi_{1}

\displaystyle C^{q}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{N}(n+1)q\right)\cos\phi_{1}

(11)

\displaystyle A^{q}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{p=1}^{n}\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{N}\,(p+1)\,q\right)\cos\phi_{2} -\sin\phi_{2}

(12)

\displaystyle C^{q}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{1}{n}\sum_{p=1}^{n}\sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{N}\,(p+1)\,q\right)\cos\phi_{2}.

(13)

Note that, after algebraic manipulations, the terms
A^{q}_{2}
and
C^{q}_{2}
can be rewritten in a compact form as follows

\displaystyle A^{q}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\cos\phi_{2}}{n}\!\left[\frac{\sin\!\big(\tfrac{(n+1)\theta}{2}\big)\,\cos\!\big(\tfrac{(n+2)\theta}{2}\big)}{\sin(\theta/2)}-\cos\theta\right]-\sin\phi_{2},

\displaystyle C^{q}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{\cos\phi_{2}}{n}\!\left[\frac{\sin\!\big(\tfrac{(n+1)\theta}{2}\big)\,\sin\!\big(\tfrac{(n+2)\theta}{2}\big)}{\sin(\theta/2)}-\sin\theta\right],

with

III
Battery setup

The charging of these QBs is implemented via a time-dependent protocol. Specifically, the Hamiltonians
H_{1/2}
become time-dependent,
H_{1/2}(t)
, through stepwise changes in the angles
\phi_{1/2}
, effectively realizing a double quantum quench
MitraARCMP2018
;
PortaSR2020
. Explicitly

\phi_{1/2}(t)=\phi_{1/2}^{(B)}(\Theta(-t)+\Theta(t-\tau))+\phi_{1/2}^{(C)}\Theta(t)\Theta(-t+\tau),

(14)

where
\phi_{1/2}^{(B/C)}
denote respectively the battery and charging parameters,
\tau
is the charging time and
\Theta(\cdot)
is the Heaviside step function.
Correspondingly, one has

\displaystyle A_{1/2}^{q}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{1/2}^{q,(B)}(\Theta(-t)+\Theta(t-\tau))+A_{1/2}^{q,(C)}\Theta(t)\Theta(-t+\tau),

\displaystyle C_{1/2}^{q}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{1/2}^{q,(B)}(\Theta(-t)+\Theta(t-\tau))+C_{1/2}^{q,(C)}\Theta(t)\Theta(-t+\tau).

For
t<0
the system is prepared either in the ground state or in a thermal state with temperature
T=1/(k_{B}\beta)
, with
k_{B}
denoting the Boltzmann constant.

The fundamental figures of merit to characterize these QBs are the energies transferred per spin during the time evolution, referred to as the charging energies. Explicitly, for the two models we have

E_{1/2}(\tau)=\frac{{Tr}\!\left[e^{-\beta H_{1/2}(0^{-})}\left(H^{H}_{1/2}(\tau^{+})-H^{H}_{1/2}(0^{-})\right)\right]}{N\,\,{Tr}\!\left[e^{-\beta H_{1/2}(0^{-})}\right]},

(15)

where
\operatorname{Tr}[\cdot]
denotes the trace and
H^{H}_{1/2}(t)
are the Hamiltonians in the Heisenberg representation.
The calculation of
E_{1/2}(\tau)
can be performed explicitly and reads as

E_{1/2}(\tau)=\sum_{q}\frac{1-\cos\!\big(2\,\omega_{1/2}(q)\,\tau\big)}{2\,N\,\varepsilon_{1/2}(q)\,\omega_{1/2}^{2}(q)}\,\left(C_{1/2}^{q,(B)}\,A_{1/2}^{q,(C)}-A_{1/2}^{q,(B)}\,C_{1/2}^{q,(C)}\right)^{2}\,\tanh\!\left(\frac{\beta\,\varepsilon_{1/2}(q)}{2}\right).

(16)

Here,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{1/2}(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\left(A_{1/2}^{q,(B)}\right)^{2}+\left(C_{1/2}^{q,(B)}\right)^{2}},

(17)

\displaystyle\omega_{1/2}(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\left(A_{1/2}^{q,(C)}\right)^{2}+\left(C_{1/2}^{q,(C)}\right)^{2}}

(18)

are the energy dispersions before and during the charging respectively. Notice that the energies do not scale with
N
in the thermodynamic limit.

The
average
charging powers
P_{1/2}(\tau)
follow directly from the energies and are given by

P_{1/2}(\tau)=E_{1/2}(\tau)/\tau.

(19)

For the following analysis, it is useful to introduce the maximal figures of merit. The maximal charging powers
P_{1/2}^{M}
are defined as

P_{1/2}^{M}=max_{\tau}P_{1/2}(\tau)

(20)

where the value of
\tau
corresponding to
P_{1/2}^{M}
is indicated as
\tau_{max}
.
Finally, the maximal energies

E_{1/2}^{M}=E_{1/2}(\tau_{max})

(21)

are considered.

V
Conclusions

We have investigated the charging performance of quantum batteries based on two
Wigner-Jordan
integrable extensions of the cluster–Ising spin chain. Despite the integrability of both models, we have shown that they can exhibit an apparent super-extensive scaling of the charging power across wide intervals of system sizes, reaching up to
O(10^{3})
spins. Our analysis demonstrates that this behavior does not originate from an anomalous dependence of the optimal charging time on the system size, but rather from a super-extensive growth of the stored energy itself. As such, the effect cannot persist in the thermodynamic limit and should instead be interpreted as a finite-size enhancement. Similar behaviors characterize both the constant
n
case, and the regime in which
n
scales with
N
.
Finally, we verified that the phenomenon is robust against thermal effects, which affect quantitative values but not the qualitative trends. This analysis seems to suggest a scaling of the averaged charging power per spin of the form
P^{M}_{1/2}\propto N/n
, even if a more detailed investigation, for example addressing possible full analytical solutions, are needed.

Altogether, our results highlight that even fermionic integrable models—typically considered incapable of supporting enhanced charging power under quench protocols
JuliaFarrePRR2020
—can display strong finite-size effects mimicking super-extensive performance. These findings can help delineate the boundaries between genuine collective advantages and finite-size artifacts in quantum battery architectures.

Evaluation indicators for bladder dysfunction in Chinese patients following cervical cancer surgery: a Delphi study based on nursing outcomes classification
Authors: Xianhong Han, Ruiqing Di, Huanjun Zhang, Bingqian Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aimed to develop scientific and standard evaluation indicators related to bladder dysfunction after cervical cancer surgery, providing a reference for clinical nurses in China.MethodsAccording to the framework of the Nursing Outcomes Classification (3rd edition), nursing outcomes and indicators were screened through a literature review and clinical investigation. Subsequently, the Delphi expert consultation method was used to revise and determine the core outcomes and indicators.ResultsA total of 28 experts participated in the correspondence consultation. The valid recovery rates for the two rounds were 93.33% and 100%, respectively. The expert authority coefficient averaged 0.90, and the Kendall harmony coefficient ranged between 0.216 and 0.352 (P < 0.05). Finally, 7 core nursing outcomes and 57 indicators were identified for patients with bladder dysfunction after cervical cancer surgery.ConclusionThe revised core nursing outcomes and indicators are valuable for developing specialized nursing measures and evaluating nursing outcomes for patients with bladder dysfunction after cervical cancer surgery. Additionally, they provide a reference for promoting standardized nursing language in China.Keywords: Cervical cancer, Bladder dysfunction, Nursing outcome, Valuation index system

Introduction

Cervical cancer is one of the most common gynecologic malignancies worldwide. The latest global cancer burden data reveals approximately 661,000 new cases and 348,000 deaths annually, with China accounting for 150,700 new cases and 55,700 deaths, and both incidence and mortality rates showing an upward trend in recent years [1]. Early-stage cervical cancer is usually treated with radical surgery, which can result in the occurrence of bladder dysfunction because of the severed sympathetic and parasympathetic nerve branches that are innervated by the pelvic autonomic nerve [2]. Due to variations in assessment tools and follow-up durations, the reported incidence of bladder dysfunction after cervical cancer surgery varies across the literature, ranging from 8% to 80% [3]. Research indicates that approximately 20% of survivors experience clinically significant long-term dysfunction [4]. This is characterized by symptoms such as difficulty urinating, urinary retention, incontinence, frequent urination, and urgency. These symptoms often require prolonged treatment and recovery, severely impacting patients’ quality of life while potentially leading to social embarrassment, occupational limitations, and psychological distress [5], which represent distinct challenges during their post-cancer recovery.

Currently, a variety of nursing interventions are applied in the rehabilitation of bladder dysfunction following cervical cancer surgery [6], including pelvic floor muscle training, perineal massage, acupuncture, indwelling catheterization, and clean intermittent catheterization. Pelvic floor muscle training, while non-invasive and promoting self-management, relies on the patient’s long-term commitment; Perineal massage and acupuncture may alleviate symptoms, yet their efficacy fluctuates significantly based on practitioner skill; Indwelling catheterization ensures immediate postoperative bladder emptying but increases urinary tract infection risk with prolonged use and does not aid functional recovery; Clean intermittent catheterization, the gold standard for managing urinary retention, aligns more closely with physiological patterns but demands high patient compliance and operator proficiency. These interventions exhibit significant disparities in efficacy, risk profiles, and patient compliance, highlighting the need for personalized and precision management.

However, the corresponding nursing assessment tools remain relatively limited. These tools can be broadly categorized into two types: objective laboratory indicators such as post-void residual volume and uroflowmetry [7], which reflect the degree of recovery of voiding function but fail to capture patients’ subjective experiences and the direct effects of nursing interventions; and subjective bladder symptom scales such as the International Consultation on Incontinence Questionnaire-Short Form (ICIQ-SF) [8], the Incontinence Impact Questionnaire-Short Form (IIQ-7) [9], and the Neurogenic Bladder Symptom Score (NBSS) [10]. These assessment tools were primarily developed for populations different from cervical cancer survivors. The NBSS targets patients with neurogenic bladder, such as those with spinal cord injury or myelopathy, while the ICIQ-SF and IIQ-7 are designed for general urinary incontinence, resulting in differences in symptom relevance. Furthermore, these instruments mainly focus on incontinence frequency or daily life impact and do not capture bladder-specific dysfunction unique to cervical cancer survivors, such as detrusor underactivity or impaired voiding initiation due to pelvic nerve injury. In addition, they provide limited information on nursing interventions, patient self-management, or the effects of bladder training, reducing their comprehensiveness for evaluating clinical nursing outcomes in this population. At present, there is a lack of core nursing outcomes and evaluation indicators specifically designed for patients with bladder dysfunction after cervical cancer surgery. As a result, the effectiveness of nursing interventions in current clinical practice is often evaluated ambiguously, affecting the systematicity and continuity of nursing interventions.

To address these limitations in evaluation, a standardized, patient-centered framework is required. The Nursing Outcomes Classification (NOC) provides a standardized, patient-centered framework to address these evaluation gaps. As an internationally recognized taxonomy [11], NOC offers comprehensive quantifiable indicators capable of capturing both functional recovery and patient-centered experiences. Its validated application in urological specialties, such as evaluating outcomes in urinary incontinence function [12] and urinary incontinence nursing effectiveness [13]. However, its implementation specifically for bladder dysfunction in cervical cancer survivors remains limited.

Therefore, this study aims to develop a standardized assessment tool for this population through systematic screening and revision of NOC outcomes. The innovative aspects of this research include: (1) pioneering the adaptation of the NOC framework specifically for bladder dysfunction in cervical cancer survivors; (2) integrating both functional recovery and patient-reported outcomes within a unified evaluation system; and (3) establishing explicit linkages between nursing interventions and multidimensional recovery outcomes. This NOC-guided instrument will facilitate precise, evidence-based evaluation of nursing care effectiveness, addressing a key gap in current gynecologic oncology rehabilitation.

Methods

Research group establishment

The project team comprised six nursing and medical professionals, including two senior nurses, one senior doctor, and three intermediate nurses. In the design phase, the two senior nurses and the senior doctor defined the study protocol. During implementation, the three intermediate nurses (two of whom held graduate degrees) executed the daily interventions and data collection under supervision. The senior nurses and the doctor were responsible for process quality supervision and addressing clinical challenges. Finally, all members collaboratively contributed to data analysis, with the graduate nurses taking a lead in statistical evaluation and the senior members ensuring the clinical interpretation of results.

Preliminary screening of core nursing outcomes and indicators

Screening for common nursing diagnoses

The identification of core nursing diagnoses was achieved through a systematic synthesis of evidence from both literature and clinical practice, comprising three sequential phases: literature extraction, clinical validation, and expert consensus.

Phase 1: Literature-Based Preliminary Extraction.

An initial list of potential nursing diagnoses was compiled from three authoritative sources: the Chinese translation of the NANDA International Nursing Diagnoses: Definitions and Classification (2018–2020 edition), the 7th edition of Obstetric and Gynecologic Nursing, and the 3rd edition of NOC. These sources provided a theoretical foundation for diagnoses commonly associated with postoperative recovery and urological dysfunction.

Phase 2: Clinical Validation and Gap Analysis.

To ground the findings in real-world evidence, a retrospective review of nursing records from 20 patients who underwent cervical cancer surgery was conducted. This review served two purposes: (1) to verify whether the diagnoses from the literature were present in actual patient records, and (2) to discover any new, relevant patient problems not covered by the standard classifications.

Phase 3: Synthesis and Final Consensus.

The evidence from the literature and clinical records was integrated through iterative discussions within our multidisciplinary research team. Diagnoses were included in the final list based on the following predefined criteria:

This process culminated in the identification of 14 nursing diagnoses, which primarily encompassed categories related to impaired urinary elimination (e.g., urinary retention, functional incontinence), psychosocial impacts (e.g., anxiety, risk of diminished personal dignity), and potential complications (e.g., risk of infection, risk of urethral damage). Nursing diagnoses for bladder dysfunction following cervical cancer surgery

Additionally, a questionnaire survey was conducted with 25 clinical gynecological nurses to rate the frequency of use for each nursing diagnosis. To integrate this data with evidence from clinical practice, we calculated the documented frequency of each diagnosis from a retrospective review of 20 nursing records. A composite score for each diagnosis was then calculated by averaging its frequency from the nurse survey and its frequency from the patient records. This approach gave equal weight (50/50) to both clinical expertise (nurse perceptions) and objective documentation (patient records). The diagnoses were then ranked based on these composite scores, from which the top five were identified as urine retention, anxiety, risk of infection, risk of urethral damage, and discomfort.

Screening core nursing outcomes and indicators

Core nursing outcomes were defined as a curated set that reflects the essence of specialty practice [7]. Guided by the NOC framework [14, 15], two researchers first screened outcomes linked to the common nursing diagnoses. However, relying solely on diagnosis-outcome linkages may introduce selection bias and overlook outcomes that are highly relevant to bladder dysfunction but not explicitly tied to a specific diagnosis in the taxonomy. To ensure a comprehensive and unbiased inventory, two additional team members independently conducted a systematic screening of all nursing outcomes within the NOC taxonomy related to excretory function. The results from both approaches were merged into a questionnaire. Twenty-five gynecology and urology specialists then rated the importance of each outcome on a 5-level Likert scale, with those scoring a mean of ≥ 4 being selected as core outcomes [7].

Revision of core nursing outcomes and indicators

Preparation of the expert correspondence questionnaire

The first-round expert consultation questionnaire aimed to evaluate the relevance of proposed outcomes and indicators. It presented the field, category, name, and definition of each nursing outcome alongside its associated indicators. The 9 proposed core nursing outcomes and 145 indicators were evaluated using a Likert 5-level scale (1 = very unimportant to 5 = very important) to determine their importance for patients with bladder dysfunction following cervical cancer surgery.

Consultation with experts for selection

Purposive sampling was employed to select experts from several hospitals nationwide. The two rounds of expert correspondence were conducted from June 10, 2025, to July 25, 2025. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) willingness to continue with the correspondence study; (2) a bachelor’s degree or above with a senior professional title (associate senior or above), or a master’s degree or above with an intermediate or higher professional title; (3) having at least five years of professional experience in gynecology neuro-urology; (4) familiarity with or academic research/clinical experience in the NOC field. Thirty experts were selected for questionnaire consultation based on their actual response rate.

Letter consultation implementation

The research group contacted the experts in advance and distributed electronically and by mail. Two rounds of expert correspondence were conducted in this study, with an interval of 2 to 3 weeks between each round. Following the first round of correspondence, the research team summarized the viewpoints, analyzed the data, and removed or modified parts of the outcome and evaluation indicators. Following the second round of correspondence, the core nursing outcomes and evaluation indicators were determined using the summary and modifications of the research team.

Pre-survey

A pre-survey was conducted in the gynecology department of a tertiary-grade A hospital using a purposive sampling method. 15 patients diagnosed with bladder dysfunction after cervical cancer surgery were interviewed, and thematic saturation was confirmed as no new insights emerged from subsequent participants [16]. The primary aims were to assess the questionnaire’s comprehension, feasibility, and preliminary clinical applicability.

Patient feedback was systematically analyzed and incorporated into revisions in the following ways:

Furthermore, the clinical applicability, including content relevance and acceptability, was refined based on the integration of this patient feedback and subsequent expert opinions.

Screening criteria

The two rounds of expert consultation followed the same screening criteria for indicators: (1) indicators can sensitively reflect patient outcomes; (2) the indicators can be accurately understood and conform to the clinical context; (3) indicators can be measured; (4) indicators meet the average importance score of ≥ 3.5 points with a coefficient of variation (CV) ≤ 0.25 [9]. The research team combined expert letter consultation opinions, organized discussions, and used the coefficient of variation and expert opinions to determine the indicators to delete, add, merge, or modify.

Statistical analysis

The data were double-entered and statistically analyzed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (version 25.0) and Excel office software. The positive coefficient of experts is expressed by the effective recovery rate of the questionnaire, with rates >70% considered highly positive. Ca represents the degree of experts’ judgment based on four factors: theoretical knowledge, clinical experience, familiarity with literature, and professional intuition. Each factor was rated on a three-point scale (high, medium, low), and the scores were summed to derive the overall Ca value. Cs represents experts’ self-rated familiarity with the research content, assessed on a five-point scale ranging from “very familiar” to “unfamiliar.”

The degree of concentration of expert opinions is described by CV [9], with a lower CV indicating a higher consensus. The degree of coordination among experts was assessed using Kendall’s coefficient of concordance (Kendall’s W) [17], which measures the agreement in ratings across multiple experts. A value of W ranges from 0 to 1, where higher values indicate stronger agreement among the expert panel. P < 0.05 indicated statistical significance.

Pre-survey results

Because of variations in patients’ educational background and age, self-evaluation proved challenging and affected the reliability of the assessment results. Therefore, all outcome evaluations were conducted by nurses using the outcome classification system, as nurses were professionally trained in both clinical observation and the use of the classification system, thereby minimizing subjective bias and enhancing inter-rater reliability. Based on the feedback received, all indicators were standardized using a 5-point Likert scale. In addition, the wording of certain indicators was revised.

Discussion

Scientificity of index screening

NOC is an internationally recognized standardized nursing language widely applied in clinical practice [18]. It provides a structured framework that facilitates consistent, evidence-based assessment and outcome measurement, which is particularly valuable for the specialized and complex care required for bladder dysfunction. Although NOC encompasses a wide range of outcomes, its breadth can reduce specificity in specialized care. This limitation highlights the need to refine disease-specific core nursing outcomes for bladder dysfunction after radical cervical cancer surgery. Based on the NOC (3rd edition), the only edition that has been translated into Chinese and widely adopted in China, the research team first identified common nursing diagnoses of bladder dysfunction after cervical cancer surgery. Corresponding nursing outcomes were then determined according to the “nursing diagnosis–nursing outcome” linkage. Finally, using purposive sampling, experienced nursing personnel in gynecology and urology were surveyed, and indicators with importance scores above 4.0 were identified as the core nursing outcomes. This validated screening process [14, 15] provides a standardized and specialty-specific evaluation tool for patients.

Reliability analysis of indicator revision

This study used the Delphi expert consultation method, which has been widely used in clinical research and proven scientifically valid [19]. To ensure the reliability of the results, experts from all over the country in the fields of nursing education, clinical nursing, nursing management, and medicine were selected. In addition, a pre-survey was conducted based on expert feedback to enhance the representativeness of the screened outcomes. In this study, the high response rates across both rounds of consultation reflected strong expert engagement [20]. Most importantly, the high Cr value and the statistically significant Kendall’s W collectively confirm the strong expert authority and consensus robustness. These key results confirm the high reliability of the revised core nursing outcomes and indicators for bladder dysfunction in patients after cervical cancer surgery.

Content analysis of outcome indicators related to bladder dysfunction in patients after cervical cancer surgery

The 7 core nursing outcomes identified for patients experiencing bladder dysfunction after cervical cancer surgery covered three domains: physiological health, health knowledge and behavior, and psychosocial health. These domains are consistent with the common clinical feedback problems from such patients, indicating that healthcare professionals should focus on the impact of treatment measures on the physiological, knowledge and behavior, and psychosocial health outcomes of patients. Bladder dysfunction after cervical cancer surgery is classified as neurogenic bladder, primarily manifesting as urinary storage and voiding dysfunction [21, 22]. Common clinical interventions, including indwelling catheterization and intermittent catheterization [23], are prone to urinary tract infections and urethral injuries. The physiological health indicators identified in this study corresponded to the results of the above literature. Besides, the degree of patient knowledge was positively correlated with health promotion behaviors [24]. The findings of this study suggest that healthcare professionals should emphasize assessing patients’ comprehension of treatment and their cooperation with health behaviors aimed at managing urinary symptoms. Additionally, bladder dysfunction after cervical cancer surgery also significantly affects psychosocial health. The loss of normal excretory function often leads to self-image disturbance, anxiety, depression, and reduced the index of well-being [25]. These psychological burdens can further hinder rehabilitation and lower quality of life. Furthermore, bladder dysfunction may limit patients’ social participation. Fear of incontinence or reliance on catheterization often causes them to avoid social activities and reduce interpersonal communication [26]. This social withdrawal further aggravates psychological burdens. Therefore, healthcare professionals should strengthen psychological and social support for these patients.

In conclusion, the outcome indicators developed in this study have practical clinical value.

◆Firstly, all indicators have their own standardized codes, facilitating future integration into information systems to realize unified language management and data integration and analysis.

◆Secondly, by introducing structured and standardized outcome indicators under the NOC framework, nurses can more sensitively detect subtle changes in patients’ physiological, behavioral, and psychological states, enabling effective evaluation of nursing interventions.

◆Finally, this indicator system provides a standardized, repeatable, and traceable nursing assessment tool for clinical use, offering decision-making support for nurses and promoting a shift in nursing practice from experience-based judgment to evidence-based pathways and refined management.

Analysis of the need to establish indicators of bladder dysfunction after cervical cancer surgery

Radical cervical surgery is currently the standard clinical treatment for cervical cancer. It involves extensive removal of the uterus, part of the vagina, and paravaginal tissues, which often leads to postoperative bladder dysfunction and significantly reduces patients’ quality of life due to functional complications [27]. Various nursing interventions, including indwelling urinary catheterization, have been used to improve bladder dysfunction [4]. However, no standardized evaluation tool exists to assess the efficacy of these measures, and continuous measurement of core outcome in patients is lacking.

Li Liang [28] developed a nursing outcome evaluation system for hip arthroplasty patients, demonstrating that specialty-specific outcome indicators can effectively guide nursing interventions and improve care quality. Huang Rong [22] screened and revised the core nursing outcome indicators for patients hospitalized with cystocele abdominal wall stoma and verified their reliability and validity, confirming that developing targeted outcome systems enhances the clinical applicability and scientific basis of nursing evaluation. These findings highlight the importance of developing disease-specific nursing outcome systems to enhance the precision and effectiveness nursing evaluation. Therefore, compared with existing specialized questionnaires such as the ICIQ-SF [8], IIQ-7 [9], and NBSS [10], the indicator system constructed in this study not only demonstrates disease specificity but are also supported by a standardized nursing language framework. Specifically, the ICIQ-SF primarily evaluates urinary symptom severity, while the IIQ-7 focuses on quality-of-life impairment, both lacking consideration of psychological adaptation and self-management behaviors. The NBSS, though applicable to neurogenic bladder, mainly targets urological symptoms and treatment outcomes, with limited reflection of nursing interventions. In contrast, the indicator system in this study integrates physiological, psychological, and behavioral dimensions, covering common nursing problems and care needs of patients with bladder dysfunction after cervical cancer surgery. It addresses the limitations of traditional scales, which often focus on a single symptom and neglect the assessment of nursing interventions and individual differences. In terms of measurement, the outcomes adopt a five-point rating scale, allowing for dynamic tracking and quantification of functional changes over time.

This study screened and revised the core nursing outcomes and indicators of bladder dysfunction in patients after cervical cancer surgery to help healthcare professionals clarify the core nursing issues, systematically evaluate the effectiveness and safety of interventions for bladder dysfunction, and quantify the results. Nurses can assess patient outcomes and promptly adjust nursing measures as needed. Furthermore, based on the NOC, promoting the use of standardized nursing language in clinics can facilitate information sharing among different medical institutions with the same specialty and promote the quality and homogenization of specialized nursing care.

In the future, empirical studies will be conducted in large-sample, multi-center clinical settings to validate the reliability, validity, and applicability of the indicator system developed in this study, thereby providing stronger evidence-based support for its widespread clinical application.

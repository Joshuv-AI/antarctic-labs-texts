Modes, states and superselection rules in quantum optics and quantum information
Eloi Descamps 1, Astghik Saharyan 1, Adrien Chivet 1,2, Arne Keller 1,3, Pérola Milman 1

Abstract
A convenient way to represent quantum optical states is through the quadrature basis of single-modes of the field. This framework provides intuitive definitions for quasi-classical states, their phase-space representations, and enables the definition of a universal gate set. In this widely adopted representation of quantum optics, most pure states consist of coherent superpositions of photon-number states. However, this approach neglects the particle-number superselection rule - which prohibits coherence between states of differing photon numbers - and implicitly assumes a phase reference. We adopt a representation of quantum optical states that respects the superselection rule and revisit key tools and results in quantum optics and information encoding within quantum optics. This approach preserves the intuitive aspects of the traditional quadrature representation while unifying insights from quantum optics with those from symmetric spin-like and angular momentum systems. More than just an alternative representation, we show that a superselection rule-compliant framework provides a unified formalism for all bosonic encodings, from single-photon to continuous-variable encodings. This perspective allows for a precise characterization of the roles of Gaussian and non-Gaussian resources, as well as the interplay between modes and states in quantum universality and potential computational advantage.



The quantization of the electromagnetic field leads to quantum optical states with counterintuitive physical properties. These are revealed by the statistical distribution of measurements of quantities that are classically well-defined, such as amplitude and intensity. As with classical fields, measurements of quantum states of the field involve their manipulation through various optical devices, which influence both the field’s statistical and modal properties - such as propagation direction, spectrum, or polarization. However, the separation of modal and statistical properties of quantum states of the field is generally not possible, even for pure states. This leads to an intrinsic interplay between modes and states of the field that enables the encoding of information

Determining how modes and states act as resources in quantum information - and in quantum optics - is a challenging problem in the general case. The definition of a universal gate set in terms of quadrature operators,

However, not all gates contribute equally to enabling quantum advantage, or to promoting field’s states to non-classicality according to different definitions

The challenge stems from the fact that different encodings not only rely on distinct types of states to define a computational basis (which can be Gaussian or quadrature non-Gaussian (NG)), but also that the Gaussian and NG nature of logical operations is encoding dependent. As a result, a clear and comprehensive understanding of the intricate interplay between modes, states, and the Gaussian and NG resources that are necessary and sufficient for universal quantum information processing in quantum optics, for all possible encodings, remains an open problem.

In this Letter, we solve this problem by adopting a superselection rule compliant (SSRC) representation of bosonic fields. This approach differs from the conventional representation - hereafter referred to as the CV
representation
-, where single-mode pure states are coherent superpositions of Fock states. The CV representation implicitly assumes the existence of a common phase reference, which, while convenient in certain contexts, is not universally valid: global phases are not associated with observables
Pegg and Barnett 1997
;
CARRUTHERS and NIETO 1968
, only relative phases are
Trifonov et al. 2000
;
Björk and Söderholm 1999
;
Luis and Sánchez-Soto 1993
;
Luis and Sánchez-Soto 1996
;
Luis and Perina 1996

U(1)

Susskind and Glogower 1964

- explicitly incorporates the phase reference as a resource, and the phase reference indeterminacy leads to coherence loss and non-pure states (that still respect the PNSSR).

Every state in the CV representation has an equivalent formulation in the SSRC representation, which offers the advantage of clearly distinguishing between particle-dependent (intrinsic) and mode-dependent (relative) properties of bosonic states as we will see in the following. In addition, we will demonstrate that the SSRC representation provides a unified framework for quantum information encoding in bosonic systems, covering the SP regime, all possible CV encodings, and intermediate regimes. This framework enables a precise characterization of the quantum optical resources required, including modes, states, entanglement, and the use of QG and NG gates, for all types of bosonic encodings. In particular, we show how the CV formalism, along with its constraints, emerges as a particular case within the SSRC representation. Interestingly, the QG nature of certain states arises only in a well-defined limit and we establish that the key factor for potential quantum advantage is the QG or NG nature of
operations
rather than
states
- which merely reflect an encoding choice.

Notably, a defining feature of bosonic encodings, compared to abstract quantum computers, is that qubit entanglement demands SNG gates - which are the bosonic equivalent of non-Clifford gates in abstract quantum computers. Depending on the encoding and the available physical tools, a mode basis transformation of a SNG local gate can convert it into a SNG two-qubit gate. However, apart from the SP encoding, NG gates are indispensable for both local and non-local operations of a bosonic quantum computer.

This general result connects to the well known theorem that quantum computation with Gaussian states and operations can be efficiently classically simulated

A strength of the SSRC representation is its ability to resolve ambiguities by offering a unified framework for describing the resources involved in any bosonic encoding, enhancing our understanding of previous results in bosonic quantum computing.

In conclusion, we have developed a framework for describing electromagnetic field states in the SSRC representation and to systematically relate it to states typically studied in the CV representation, where a phase reference is implicitly assumed. We provided a unified formalism that bridges the photon-number-resolved regime and the CV representation of quantum optics and extends to quantum computing. We have explored how fundamental quadrature phase-space properties - such as Heisenberg uncertainty relations - emerge and investigated the encoding of information in the modes and states of the electromagnetic field. This analysis revealed the common features to all bosonic encodings and specified the role of nG gates in different representations. A notable strength of the SSRC representation is its generality, which allows it to yield results that remain independent of the encoding both for arbitrary photon number and in the CV limit. Consequently, our findings are broadly applicable to any quantum optical platform for encoding quantum information

Madsen et al. 2022

\hat{a}^{\dagger}
commute with their commutator
[\hat{a},\hat{a}^{\dagger}]=1
) to rewrite the displacement operator as

\hat{D}(\alpha)=e^{\alpha\hat{a}^{\dagger}-\alpha^{*}\hat{a}}=e^{-\absolutevalue{\alpha}^{2}/2}e^{\alpha\hat{a}^{\dagger}}e^{-\alpha^{*}\hat{a}}.

(17)

Additionally, we recall the action of the ladder operator on the Fock states

\displaystyle\hat{a}^{\dagger}\ket{l}=\sqrt{l+1}\ket{l+1}

\displaystyle\hat{a}\ket{l}=\sqrt{l}\ket{l-1}.

(18)

Iterating these formulas, we get the action of power of the ladder operator onto the Fock states

\displaystyle(\hat{a}^{\dagger})^{j}\ket{l}=\frac{\sqrt{(j+l)!}}{\sqrt{l!}}\ket{l+j}

\displaystyle(\hat{a})^{j}\ket{l}=\frac{\sqrt{l!}}{\sqrt{(l-j)!}}\ket{k-j},

(19)

where the second formula is valide for
j\leq l
and yields zero otherwise. With this, we can compute

\displaystyle\hat{D}(\alpha)\ket{k}

\displaystyle=e^{-\absolutevalue{\alpha}^{2}/2}e^{\alpha\hat{a}^{\dagger}}e^{-\alpha^{*}\hat{a}}\ket{k}

(20a)

\displaystyle=e^{-\absolutevalue{\alpha}^{2}/2}e^{\alpha\hat{a}^{\dagger}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\alpha^{*}\hat{a})^{l}}{l!}\ket{k}

(20b)

\displaystyle=e^{-\absolutevalue{\alpha}^{2}/2}e^{\alpha\hat{a}^{\dagger}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{(-\alpha^{*})^{l}}{l!}\frac{\sqrt{k!}}{\sqrt{(k-l)!}}\ket{k-l}

(20c)

\displaystyle=e^{-\absolutevalue{\alpha}^{2}/2}e^{\alpha\hat{a}^{\dagger}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{(-\alpha^{*})^{k-l}}{(k-l)!}\frac{\sqrt{k!}}{\sqrt{l!}}\ket{l}

(20d)

\displaystyle=e^{-\absolutevalue{\alpha}^{2}/2}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{(-\alpha^{*})^{k-l}}{(k-l)!}\frac{\sqrt{k!}}{\sqrt{l!}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{j}}{j!}(\hat{a}^{\dagger})^{j}\ket{l}

(20e)

\displaystyle=e^{-\absolutevalue{\alpha}^{2}/2}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{(-\alpha^{*})^{k-l}}{(k-l)!}\frac{\sqrt{k!}}{\sqrt{l!}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{j}}{j!}\frac{\sqrt{(l+j)!}}{\sqrt{l!}}\ket{l+j}

(20f)

\displaystyle=e^{-\absolutevalue{\alpha}^{2}/2}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\alpha^{*})^{k-l}\alpha^{j}}{(k-l)!j!}\frac{\sqrt{k!}\sqrt{(l+j)!}}{l!}\ket{l+j}.

(20g)

We can now compute the effect of the mode basis change on the state

which is the SSRC equivalent of a

\displaystyle\hat{U}\ket{k}_{a}\ket{N-k}_{b}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(N-k)!}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k!}}(\hat{U}\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{U}^{\dagger})^{k}(\hat{U}\hat{b}^{\dagger}\hat{U}^{\dagger})^{N-k}\ket{\emptyset}

(21a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(N-k)!}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k!}}\left(\sqrt{1-\frac{\absolutevalue{\alpha}^{2}}{N}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}-\frac{\alpha^{*}}{\sqrt{N}}\hat{b}^{\dagger}\right)^{k}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{N}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}+\sqrt{1-\frac{\absolutevalue{\alpha}^{2}}{N}}\hat{b}^{\dagger}\right)^{N-k}\ket{\emptyset}

(21b)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(N-k)!}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k!}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\sqrt{1-\frac{\absolutevalue{\alpha}^{2}}{N}}^{l}\frac{(-\alpha^{*})^{k-l}}{\sqrt{N}^{k-l}}(\hat{a}^{\dagger})^{l}(\hat{b}^{\dagger})^{k-l}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{j=0}^{N-k}\binom{N-k}{j}\frac{\alpha^{j}}{\sqrt{N}^{j}}\sqrt{1-\frac{\absolutevalue{\alpha}^{2}}{N}}^{N-k-j}(\hat{a}^{\dagger})^{j}(\hat{b}^{\dagger})^{N-k-j}\ket{\emptyset}

(21c)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(N-k)!}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k!}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{N-k}\binom{k}{l}\binom{N-k}{j}\left(1-\frac{\absolutevalue{\alpha}^{2}}{N}\right)^{\frac{N-j-l-k}{2}}\frac{\alpha^{j}(-\alpha^{*})^{k-l}}{N^{\frac{j+k-l}{2}}}(\hat{a}^{\dagger})^{l+j}(\hat{b}^{\dagger})^{N-j-l}\ket{\emptyset}

(21d)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{N-k}\binom{k}{l}\binom{N-k}{j}\frac{\sqrt{(j+l)!}}{\sqrt{(N-k)!}}\frac{\sqrt{(N-j-l)!}}{\sqrt{k!}}\left(1-\frac{\absolutevalue{\alpha}^{2}}{N}\right)^{\frac{N-j-l-k}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{\alpha^{j}(-\alpha^{*})^{k-l}}{N^{\frac{j+k-l}{2}}}\ket{j+l}_{a}\ket{N-j-l}_{b}.

(21e)

comparing the equation for
\hat{D}(\alpha)\ket{k}
and
\hat{U}\ket{k}_{a}\ket{N-k}_{b}
, we see that for fixed

,
l
,
j
and for

•

The two sums in

•

The power of
\alpha
and
\alpha^{*}
are the same.

•

The limit of

It remain to check the combinatorial coefficients

\displaystyle\binom{k}{l}\binom{N-k}{j}\frac{\sqrt{(j+l)!}}{\sqrt{(N-k)!}}\frac{\sqrt{(N-j-l)!}}{\sqrt{k!}N^{\frac{j+k-l}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{k!}{l!(k-l)!}\frac{(N-k)!}{j!(N-k-j)!}\frac{\sqrt{(j+l)!}}{\sqrt{(N-k)!}}\frac{\sqrt{(N-j-l)!}}{\sqrt{k!}N^{\frac{j+k-l}{2}}}

(22a)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{k!}\sqrt{(j+l)!}}{(k-l)!j!l!}\frac{\sqrt{(N-k)!}\sqrt{(N-j-l)!}}{(N-k-j)!N^{\frac{j+k-l}{2}}},

Appendix C
III. The SSRC representation and squeezed states in the CV limit

In the CV representation the squeezed vacuum state can be written as an expansion over Fock states on a given mode (say
\hat{a}
):

\ket{r,\phi}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh(r)}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(-e^{i\phi}\tanh(r)\right)^{k}\frac{\sqrt{2k!}}{2^{k}k!}\ket{2k}_{a}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh(r)}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(-e^{i\phi}\tanh(r)\right)^{k}\frac{1}{2^{k}k!}(\hat{a}^{\dagger})^{2k}\ket{\emptyset}

In the SSRC formulation in the CV limit, the corresponding states should look like:

\displaystyle\ket{r,\phi}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh(r)}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(-e^{i\phi}\tanh(r)\right)^{k}\frac{\sqrt{2k!}}{2^{k}k!}\ket{2k}_{a}\otimes\ket{2(N-k)}_{b}

(24)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh(r)}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(-e^{i\phi}\tanh(r)\right)^{k}\frac{1}{2^{k}k!\sqrt{(2(N-k))!}}(\hat{a}^{\dagger})^{2k}(\hat{b}^{\dagger})^{2(N-k)}\ket{\emptyset}

(25)

where the mode
b
is the phase reference mode orthogonal to the mode
a
. We write

\ket{r,\phi}=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{A}(\hat{c}^{\dagger})^{N}(\hat{d}^{\dagger})^{N}\ket{\emptyset}

where
A
is a normalization factor that can be calculated as a function of
r
and
N
(see at the end of the section) and
where
\hat{c}^{\dagger}
and
\hat{d}^{\dagger}
are the creation operators for

2
non-orthogonal modes, defined as

\displaystyle\hat{c}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=e^{-r/2}\left[e^{i\phi/2}\sqrt{\sinh(r)}\hat{a}^{\dagger}+\sqrt{\cosh(r)}\hat{b}^{\dagger}\right]

(26)

\displaystyle\hat{d}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=e^{-r/2}\left[-e^{i\phi/2}\sqrt{\sinh(r)}\hat{a}^{\dagger}+\sqrt{\cosh(r)}\hat{b}^{\dagger}\right].

(27)

It can be verified that
[\hat{c},\hat{c}^{\dagger}]=[\hat{d},\hat{d}^{\dagger}]=1
and the overlap between the two modes is

s=[\hat{c},\hat{d}^{\dagger}]=e^{-r}
.

Let’s show that, indeed, we can obtain a squeezed state in the CV limit using the studied state.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A}(\hat{c}^{\dagger})^{N}(\hat{d}^{\dagger})^{N}\ket{\emptyset}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-Nr}}{A}\left[e^{i\phi/2}\sqrt{\sinh(r)}\hat{a}^{\dagger}+\sqrt{\cosh(r)}\hat{b}^{\dagger}\right]^{N}\times\left[-e^{i\phi/2}\sqrt{\sinh(r)}\hat{a}^{\dagger}+\sqrt{\cosh(r)}\hat{b}^{\dagger}\right]^{N}\ket{\emptyset}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-Nr}}{A}\left[-e^{i\phi}\sinh(r)(\hat{a}^{\dagger})^{2}+\cosh(r)(\hat{b}^{\dagger})^{2}\right]^{N}\ket{\emptyset}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-Nr}}{A}\sum_{k=0}^{N}\binom{N}{k}(-e^{i\phi}\sinh(r))^{k}(\cosh(r))^{N-k}(\hat{a}^{\dagger})^{2k}(\hat{b}^{\dagger})^{2(N-k)}\ket{\emptyset}

\displaystyle=\frac{(e^{-r}\cosh(r))^{N}}{A}\sum_{k=0}^{N}\binom{N}{k}(-e^{i\phi}\tanh(r))^{k}(\hat{a}^{\dagger})^{2k}(\hat{b}^{\dagger})^{2(N-k)}\ket{\emptyset}

\displaystyle=\frac{(e^{-r}\cosh(r))^{N}}{A}\sum_{k=0}^{N}(-e^{i\phi}\tanh(r))^{k}\frac{N!}{k!(N-k)!}(\hat{a}^{\dagger})^{2k}(\hat{b}^{\dagger})^{2(N-k)}\ket{\emptyset}

\displaystyle=\frac{(e^{-r}\cosh(r))^{N}}{A}\sum_{k=0}^{N}(-e^{i\phi}\tanh(r))^{k}\frac{N!\sqrt{(2k)!(2(N-k))!}}{k!(N-k)!}\ket{2k}_{a}\otimes\ket{2(N-k)}

(28)

In the limit
N\rightarrow\infty
we can use Stirling’s formula

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}2(N-k)!\to\sqrt{4\pi(N-k)}(2(N-k)/e)^{2(N-k)},

Hence

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N!}{A}\ket{N}_{c}\otimes\ket{N}_{d}=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(2e^{-r}\cosh(r))^{N}N!}{A{(\pi N)}^{1/4}}\sum_{k=0}^{n_{\rm max}}(-e^{i\phi}\tanh(r))^{k}\frac{\sqrt{(2k)!}}{k!2^{k}}\ket{2k}_{a}\otimes\ket{2(N-k)}

(29)

where we used that
\tanh r<1
and bounded the sum at
n_{\rm max}
that can be such that
n_{\rm max}\to\infty
and
n_{\rm max}\ll N
, so that
N\gg k
. Eq. (
29
) looks like the expected result (
24
), and to confirm this we compute
A
. For a finite
N
, we can use the last line in (
C
), that leads to

A^{2}=(e^{-r}\cosh(r))^{2N}\sum_{k=0}^{N}(\tanh(r))^{2k}\frac{(N!)^{2}(2k)!(2(N-k))!}{(k!(N-k)!)^{2}}.

(30)

In the CV limit, where
N\to\infty
and
n_{\rm max}\to\infty
with
n_{\rm max}\ll N
, we have that

A\to\frac{(2e^{-r}\cosh(r))^{N}N!}{{(\pi N)}^{1/4}}\sqrt{\sum_{k=0}^{n_{\rm max}}(\tanh(r))^{2k}\frac{(2k)!}{2^{2k}(k!)^{2}}}=\frac{(2e^{-r}\cosh(r))^{N}N!}{(\pi N)^{1/4}}\sqrt{\cosh{r}}.

(31)

So, in the CV limit, state
\frac{1}{A}(\hat{c}^{\dagger})^{N}(\hat{d}^{\dagger})^{N}\ket{\emptyset}
corresponds to the vacuum squeezed state.

Appendix D
IV.Quadrature operators from the SSRC formulation

In this section we show how the quadrature field operators emerge from the SSRC formulation. For this we consider a general 2-mode SSRC state

\ket{\Psi}=\sum_{k=0}^{N}c_{k}\ket{k}_{a}\ket{N-k}_{b}

where the modes
a
and
b
are orthogonal and the number of photon
N
can be taken sufficiently large such that in the CV-limit we obtain the state

\ket{\psi}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}\ket{k}_{a}.

This connection between the SSRC representation and the CV-limit has been specified in the main text and denoted as
\ket{\Psi}\rightarrow\ket{\psi}
.

Let’s consider the
N
dependent operator:

\hat{A}(N)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\hat{a}\hat{b}^{\dagger}\\

where
N
is number of photons.
When applied to the state
\ket{\Psi}

we obtain

\hat{A}(N)\ket{\Psi}=\sum_{k=0}^{N}c_{k}\sqrt{k(1-\frac{k-1}{N})}\ket{k-1}_{a}\ket{N-k+1}_{b}.

Applying the CV limit procedure introduced in the main text which consists in taking the limit

N\rightarrow+\infty
in two steps

\displaystyle\lim_{n_{\text{max}}\rightarrow+\infty}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n_{\text{max}}}\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left[c_{k}\sqrt{k(1-\frac{k-1}{N})}\right]\ket{k-1}_{a}\ket{n_{\text{max}}-k+1}_{b}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n_{\text{max}}\rightarrow+\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n_{\text{max}}}c_{k}\sqrt{k}\ket{k-1}_{a}\ket{n_{\text{max}}-k+1}_{b}

and then disentangling mode
a
and
b
, we finally obtain

\hat{A}(N)\ket{\Psi}\rightarrow\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}\sqrt{k}\ket{k-1}_{a}=\hat{a}\ket{\psi},

where
\hat{a}
is the annihilation operator of the mode
a
. As this last relation is valid for every state
\ket{\Psi}
which in the CV limit gives the state
\ket{\psi}

we can write the CV limit of the operator
\hat{A}(N)
as

\hat{A}(N)\rightarrow\hat{a}.

In the same way, we have that

\hat{A}^{\dagger}(N)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}a^{\dagger}b\rightarrow\hat{a}^{\dagger}

We note that using the expression given by Eq. (14 g) it is straightforward to prove that the SSRC representation of the coherent Glauber state is an eigenstate of
\hat{A}(N)
, in the CV limit.

Using the operators
\hat{A}(N)
and
\hat{A}^{\dagger}(N)
we can define the operator

\hat{Q}(N,\phi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[e^{-i\phi}\hat{A}(N)+e^{+i\phi}\hat{A}^{\dagger}(N)\right];\;\phi\in[0,2\pi],

which in the CV limit gives the quadrature operator:

\hat{Q}(N,\phi)\rightarrow\hat{q}(\phi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[e^{-i\phi}\hat{a}+e^{i\phi}\hat{a}^{\dagger}\right].

In particular for
\phi=0
and
\phi=\frac{\pi}{2}
we have

\hat{Q}(N,0)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{N}}\hat{J}_{x}\rightarrow\hat{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[\hat{a}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}\right]

and

\hat{Q}(N,\frac{\pi}{2})=-\sqrt{\frac{2}{N}}\hat{J}_{y}\rightarrow\hat{p}=\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\left[\hat{a}^{\dagger}-\hat{a}\right]

Finally, the commutation relation
[\hat{x},\hat{p}]=i
is obtained from SSRC, using the
commutation relation
[\hat{J}_{x},\hat{J}_{y}]=i\hat{J}_{z}
. Indeed, we start with

[\hat{Q}(N,0),\hat{Q}(N,\frac{\pi}{2})]=-\frac{2i}{N}\hat{J}_{z}

but

-\frac{2}{N}\hat{J}_{z}\ket{\Psi}=\sum_{k=0}^{N}(1-\frac{2k}{N})c_{k}\ket{k}_{a}\ket{N-k}_{b}\rightarrow\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}\ket{k}_{a}=\ket{\psi}

Hence we can write the CV limit of the commutation relation as

[\hat{Q}(N,0),\hat{Q}(N,\frac{\pi}{2})]=-\frac{2i}{N}\hat{J}_{z}\rightarrow[\hat{x},\hat{p}]=i.

Appendix E
V. Details on the calculations on generic bosonic encodings and mode transformations

We start, for simplicity, from the encoding
\ket{\overline 0}=\ket{N}_{b}
,
\ket{\overline 1}=\hat{U}_{1}\ket{N}_{a}
,
\langle\overline{1}|\overline{0}\rangle=0
.

Within this encoding, state
\ket{\overline 0}
is a single mode state in mode
b
, and state
\ket{\overline 1}
is a two-mode state involving modes
a
and
b
. Notice that, in this description, mode
a
or
b
can be a phase reference, and we can obtain a single mode CV encoding by using the methods presented on the main text, so we will present here a general description for arbitrary
N
.

We start by showing that we cannot implement the (qubit) local gates of the universal set by using only Gaussian SSRC gates within this encoding,
i.e.
, local nG gates are also necessary. We then generalize our results and show that the same holds for any encoding, except for the single photon one. In this section we will always consider the general SSRC representation, so when referring to operations as Gaussian and nG we mean SSRC Gaussian and nG, which are also multimode quadrature Gaussian and quadrature nG, respectively and become single mode quadrature Gaussian and nG in the Cv limit.

To show our main result, we analyze the local gates of the universal set formed by
qubit
rotations (which can be different from SSRC rotations), that we define as
{\cal R}_{x}(\theta_{x}),{\cal R}_{y}(\theta_{y}),{\cal R}_{z}(\theta_{z})
, where
{\cal R}_{\alpha}(\theta_{\alpha})
is a rotation of an angle
\theta_{\alpha}
around the axis
\alpha
of the Bloch sphere (that again, may or may not correspond to the axis of rotation of
R(\theta,\phi)
as defined in the main text).

We start by supposing that it is possible to implement the qubit rotation
{\cal R}_{y}(\theta_{y})
using a Gaussian SSRC transformation
\hat{R}(\theta,\phi)
in an encoding where
\ket{\overline 0}=\ket{N}_{b}
, where we are using eigenstates of the effective Pauli
\hat{Z}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\ket{\overline 1}\bra{\overline 1}-\ket{\overline 0}\bra{\overline 0}\right)
operator for the logical qubits as the computational basis. We should have then have

\displaystyle\hat{R}(\theta,\phi)\ket{N}_{b}=(\cos{\frac{\theta}{2}})^{N}\ket{N}_{b}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\binom{N}{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cos{\frac{\theta}{2}})^{N-n}(e^{i\phi}\sin{\frac{\theta}{2}})^{n}\ket{n}_{a}\ket{N-n}_{b}=

\displaystyle\cos{\left(\frac{\theta_y}{2} \right)}\ket{\overline 0}+\sin{\left(\frac{\theta_y}{2} \right)}\ket{\overline 1}={\cal R}_{y}(\theta_{y})\ket{\overline 0},

(32)

For (
E
) to be true, we have that
(\cos{\frac{\theta}{2}})^{N}=\cos{\left(\frac{\theta_y}{2} \right)}
. In addition, if
\hat{R}(\theta,\phi)
is a qubit rotation,

\displaystyle\ket{\overline 1}=\left(\sin{\left(\frac{\theta_y}{2} \right) }\right)^{-1}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\binom{N}{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cos{\frac{\theta}{2}})^{N-n}(e^{i\phi}\sin{\frac{\theta}{2}})^{n}\ket{n}_{a}\ket{N-n}_{b}=

\displaystyle\left(\sin{\left(\frac{\theta_y}{2} \right) }\right)^{-1}\hat{R}(\theta,\phi)\ket{N}_{b}-\left(\tan{\left(\frac{\theta_y}{2} \right) }\right)^{-1}\ket{N}_{b}.

(33)

Hence, according to (
E

\ket{\overline 1}
depends for
N>1
, on the rotation angle
\theta_{y}
, which is clearly not possible: for a fixed
N
and for each
\theta_{y}
, we can in principle find a value of
\theta
such that
\hat{R}(\theta,\phi)\ket{N}_{b}
corresponds to a rotation of an angle
\theta_{y}
of the logical qubit. If we choose
\theta
such that
\cos{\frac{\theta}{2}}=(\cos\frac{\theta_{y}}{2})^{1/N}
, with
\theta_{y}\in[0,\pi]
, it works, meaning that Eq. (
E
) is fulfilled and
\ket{\overline 1}
is given by the expression in the first line of Eq. (
E
). Then we can choose
\theta_{y}=\pi
, that leads to
\cos{\frac{\theta}{2}}=(\cos\frac{\theta_{y}}{2})^{1/N}=0

\theta=\theta_{y}=\pi

\hat{R}(\pi,0)\ket{N}_{b}={\cal R}_{y}(\pi)\ket{\overline 0}=\ket{\overline 1}

In order to further illustrate the impossibility of defining

\displaystyle\hat{R}(\theta,\phi)\ket{\overline 1}=\cos{\left(\frac{\theta_y}{2} \right)}\ket{\overline 1}-\sin{\left(\frac{\theta_y}{2} \right)}\ket{\overline 0}=\left(\sin{\left(\frac{\theta_y}{2} \right) }\right)^{-1}\hat{R}^{2}(\theta,\phi)\ket{N}_{b}-\left(\tan{\left(\frac{\theta_y}{2} \right) }\right)^{-1}\hat{R}(\theta,\phi)\ket{N}_{b}

\displaystyle=-\cos{\left(\frac{\theta_y}{2} \right)}\ket{\overline 1}-\frac{\cos^{2}{\left(\frac{\theta_{y}}{2}\right)}}{\sin{\left(\frac{\theta_y}{2} \right)}}\ket{\overline 0}+

\displaystyle\left(\sin{\left(\frac{\theta_y}{2} \right) }\right)^{-1}\sum_{n=0}^{N}\binom{N}{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}((\cos{\frac{\theta}{2}})^{2}-e^{i\phi}(\sin{\frac{\theta}{2}})^{2})^{N-n}(e^{i\phi}+e^{2i\phi})^{n}(\sin{\frac{\theta}{2}}\cos{\frac{\theta}{2}})^{n}\ket{n}_{a}\ket{N-n}_{b}).

\alpha
, but they will not be necessarily identical nor univocal. Using this notation, the partial conclusion above can then be stated as
\hat{\cal R}^{\nu}_{y}[\mathbb{1},\hat{U}_{1}](\theta_{\alpha})\in{\cal N}
, where
{\cal N}
is the set of nG SSRC operations (and
{\cal G}
the set of Gaussian ones).

We can generalize our results to arbitrary encodings by asking ourselves whether there exist sets

S_{\nu}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}]=\{\hat{\cal R}^{\nu}_{x}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}](\theta_{x}),\hat{\cal R}^{\nu}_{y}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}](\theta_{x}),\hat{\cal R}^{\nu}_{z}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}](\theta_{x})\}

(36)

for a given encoding
(\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1})
where all the SSRC operations
\hat{\cal R}^{\nu}_{\alpha}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}](\theta_{\alpha})
representing qubit local operations
\hat{\cal R}^{\nu}_{\alpha}(\theta_{\alpha})
are Gaussian. A qubit rotation around
y
on an arbitrary encoding
\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}
reads

\hat{\cal R}^{\nu}_{y}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}](\theta_{y})\hat{U}_{0}\ket{N}_{b}=\cos{\left(\frac{\theta_y}{2}\right)}\hat{U}_{0}\ket{N}_{b}+\sin{\left(\frac{\theta_y}{2}\right)}\hat{U}_{1}\ket{N}_{a},

(37)

or

\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{0}\hat{\cal R}^{\nu}_{y}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}](\theta_{y})\hat{U}_{0}\ket{N}_{b}=\cos{\left(\frac{\theta_y}{2}\right)}\ket{N}_{b}+\sin{\left(\frac{\theta_y}{2}\right)}\hat{U}^{\prime}_{1}\ket{N}_{a}=\hat{\cal R}^{\nu^{\prime}}_{y}[\mathbb{1},\hat{U}^{\prime}_{1}](\theta_{y})\ket{N}_{b},

(38)

so for every gate in the encoding
(\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1})
, there is a gate in the encoding
(\mathbb{1},\hat{U}^{\prime}_{1})
and, in order to have
\hat{\cal R}^{\nu}_{y}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}](\theta_{y})\in{\cal G}
, we must have
\hat{U}_{0}\in{\cal N}
(since
\hat{\cal R}^{\nu^{\prime}}_{y}[\mathbb{1},\hat{U}^{\prime}_{1}](\theta_{y})
acts as a qubit rotation in the encoding
(\mathbb{1},\hat{U}_{1})
, so it must be nG). This result generalizes straightforwardly to the encodings where
(\hat{U}_{0}\in{\cal G},\hat{U}_{1}\in{\cal G}\cup{\cal N})
and
(\hat{U}_{0}\in{\cal G}\cup{\cal N},\hat{U}_{1}\in{\cal G})
.

Hence,
\hat{\cal R}^{\nu}_{y}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}](\theta_{y})
can only be a Gaussian operation if
\hat{U}_{0}
and
\hat{U}_{1}
are nG. However, not all nG operations would fit, since
\hat{\cal R}^{\nu}_{y}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}](\theta)\in{\cal N}
as well for encodings where
\ket{\overline 0}
is one of
\hat{J}_{z}
eigenstates (this can be shown in the same way as for the case
\hat{U}_{0}=\mathbb{1}
,
i.e.
, by computing the coefficients of an hypothetic
\ket{\overline 1}
state), and also when
U_{0}
and
U_{1}
are diagonal on the
z
basis (since in this case the operations act as a global phase). We must then focus on encodings
\hat{U}_{0}\ket{N}_{b}
with
\hat{U}_{0}\in{\cal N}
that are in the form of superpositions
\ket{\overline 0}=\sum_{n=0}^{N}c_{n}\ket{n}_{a}\ket{N-n}_{b}
and ask whether it is possible for the SSRC operations representing the other qubit rotations, say,
\hat{\cal R}^{\nu}_{z}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}](\theta_{z})\in{\cal G}
for the nG encodings where
\hat{\cal R}^{\nu}_{y}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}](\theta_{y})\in{\cal G}
.

We can check this by verifying if it is possible to implement
\hat{\cal R}_{z}(\theta_{z})\ket{\overline 0}=e^{i\theta_{z}}\ket{\overline 0}
and
\hat{\cal R}_{z}(\theta_{z})\ket{\overline 1}=e^{-i\theta_{z}}\ket{\overline 1}
with a Gaussian operation
\hat{R}(\theta,\phi)
,
i.e.
, if
\hat{\cal R}^{\nu}_{z}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}](\theta_{z})\in{\cal G}
or, equivalently,
\hat{\cal R}^{\nu}_{z}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}](\theta_{z})=\hat{R}(\theta,\phi)
.

For this, we should then have
\hat{R}(\theta,\phi)\hat{U}_{0}\ket{N}_{b}=e^{i\theta_{z}}\hat{U}_{0}\ket{N}_{b}
, that can be written as

e^{i\eta\hat{J}_{z}}\hat{R}^{\dagger}(\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})\hat{U}_{o}\hat{R}(\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})\hat{R}^{\dagger}(\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})\ket{N}_{b}=e^{i\varphi}\hat{R}^{\dagger}(\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})\hat{U}_{0}\hat{R}(\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})\hat{R}^{\dagger}(\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})\ket{N}_{b},

(39)

where we expressed
\hat{R}(\theta,\phi)=\hat{R}(\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})e^{i\eta\hat{J}_{z}}\hat{R}^{\dagger}(\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
. This is a simple mode rotation that changes the mode basis
a\to c
,
b\to d
and, in principle, the coefficients
c_{n}\to c^{\prime}_{n}
if we choose to express the rotated state in the mode basis
a
,
b
. However, the rotated nG operation
\hat{R}^{\dagger}(\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})\hat{U}_{0}\hat{R}(\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
remains nG. It means that we can simply check whether it is possible to have

e^{i\eta\hat{J}_{z}}\sum_{n=0}^{N}c^{\prime}_{n}\ket{n}_{a}\ket{N-n}_{b}=\sum_{n=0}^{N}e^{i\eta(2n-N)}c^{\prime}_{n}\ket{n}_{a}\ket{N-n}_{b}=e^{i\theta_{z}}\sum_{n=0}^{N}c^{\prime}_{n}\ket{n}_{a}\ket{N-n}_{b},

(40)

with
c^{\prime}_{n}\neq 1~\forall~n
. We call the first
c^{\prime}_{n}\neq 0

c^{\prime}_{n_{0}}
, and the condition for
e^{i\eta\hat{J}_{z}}
to correspond to a global phase (
i.e.
, representing
{\cal R}^{\nu}_{z}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}](\theta_{z})
by a Gaussian SSRC operation) can be expressed as
(n-n_{0})\eta=k\pi~\forall~n
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}
. Since
n-n_{0}~\in~\mathbb{N}^{*}
, this condition can only be true if
\eta=\pi/p
, with
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}
. However,
\theta_{z}=(2n_{o}-N)\eta+2s\pi=(2n_{o}-N)\pi/p+2s\pi
,
s\in\mathbb{Z}
. This means that if
\hat{\cal R}^{\nu}_{z}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}](\theta_{z})\in{\cal G}
, it cannot be a qubit rotation around
z
,
i.e.
,
{\cal R}_{z}(\theta_{z})
: given an encoding (that determines the values of
n_{0}
and of
N
), all the angles
\theta_{z}
cannot be reached within this encoding by applying the SSRC Gaussian operation that was a candidate to
\hat{\cal R}^{\nu}_{z}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}](\theta_{z})
.

We can alternatively state this result by referring to the physical implementation
\hat{H}_{\nu}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}]
,
\hat{T}_{\nu}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}]
of the Clifford
\hat{H}
(Hadamard gate) and non-Clifford
\hat{T}
(phase gate) qubit gates. These gates and their physical implementations, combined, can arbitrarily approach any single qubit operation, including rotations and, in particular, around arbitrary
{\alpha}
axis. If both
\hat{H}_{\nu}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}]
and
\hat{T}_{\nu}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}]
could be implemented by Gaussian SSRC operations - SSRC rotations - there would also be a SSRC rotation that would be able to implement any combination of
\hat{H}_{\nu}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}]
and
\hat{T}_{\nu}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}]
, since a combination of rotations is a rotation. In particular, SSRC rotations would be able to arbitrarily approach the effect of any qubit rotation around the
z
axis (as
\hat{H}
and
\hat{T}
can arbitrarily reproduce the effect of
{\cal R}_{z}(\theta)
). Since this is not the case, we conclude that it is not possible to associate a SSRC Gaussian operation to both
\hat{H}_{\nu}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}]
and
\hat{T}_{\nu}[\hat{U}_{0},\hat{U}_{1}]
.

Again, for
N=1
and
n_{0}=0
or
n_{0}=1
(so
\hat{U}_{0}
and
\hat{U}_{1}
are SSRC Gaussian, and we are in the single photon encoding case), the restriction on the values of the phase
\theta_{z}
no longer holds, and
\theta_{z}=\pm\eta+2s\pi
.

In conclusion, we have shown that for any encoding, except for the single photon one, the local gates of the universal set must contain at least one SSRC nG gate.

E.1
The impossibility of building CNOT gates with Gaussian operations

We now demonstrate that SSRC Gaussian operations (rotations) cannot implement two-qubit entangling gates (such as CNOTs) for any encoding. Consider two qubits encoded in mode partitions
k
and
k^{\prime}
, where qubit
k
is encoded in modes
a_{k},b_{k}
and qubit
k^{\prime}
in modes
a_{k^{\prime}},b_{k^{\prime}}
. The logical states of qubit
k
are encoded as
\ket{\overline 0}_{k}=\hat{U}^{(a_{k},b_{k})}_{0}\ket{N}_{a_{k}}\ket{0}_{b_{k}}
,
\ket{\overline 1}_{k}=\hat{U}^{(a_{k},b_{k})}_{1}\ket{0}_{a_{k}}\ket{N}_{b_{k}}
, where
\hat{U}^{(a_{k},b_{k})}_{0(1)}
represents an arbitrary (possibly
k
dependent) unitary operation that may include Gaussian or non-Gaussian transformations involving only the two modes
a_{k},b_{k}
and that leaves the two possible states of the logical qubits (
\ket{\overline 0}_{k}
and
\ket{\overline 1}_{k}
) orthogonal to one another,
{}_{k}\langle\overline{0}|\overline{1}\rangle_{k}=0
. The logical states of qubit
k^{\prime}
are defined analogously, using operations and modes acting on the partition
k^{\prime}
. A CNOT gate or equivalent (as a controlled phase gate) implements transformations such as
\ket{\overline 1}_{k}\ket{\overline 1}_{k^{\prime}}\to\ket{\overline 1}_{k}\ket{\overline 0}_{k^{\prime}}
in some encoding (as well as the rest of the truth table, but we will focus on this one), which here corresponds to some choice of unitaries
\hat{U}^{(a_{k},b_{k})}_{0(1)}
,
\hat{U}^{(a_{k^{\prime}},b_{k^{\prime}})}_{0(1)}
. In we want to implement a CNOT gate with a SSRC Gaussian operation (rotation) we should then have
\hat{R}\hat{U}^{(a_{k},b_{k})}_{1}\hat{U}^{(a_{k^{\prime}},b_{k^{\prime}})}_{1}\ket{0}_{a_{k}}\ket{N}_{b_{k}}\ket{0}_{a_{k^{\prime}}}\ket{N}_{b_{k^{\prime}}}\to\hat{U}^{(a_{k},b_{k})}_{1}\hat{U}^{(a_{k^{\prime}},b_{k^{\prime}})}_{0}\ket{0}_{a_{k}}\ket{N}_{b_{k}}\ket{N}_{a_{k^{\prime}}}\ket{0}_{b_{k^{\prime}}}
, where
\hat{R}
is a general rotation involving all the four modes
k(i),k^{\prime}(j)
,
i,j\in\{0,1\}
, that are coupled two by two - a four-mode Gaussian gate. However,

\displaystyle\hat{R}\hat{U}^{(a_{k},b_{k})}_{1}\hat{U}^{(a_{k^{\prime}},b_{k^{\prime}})}_{1}\ket{0}_{a_{k}}\ket{N}_{b_{k}}\ket{0}_{a_{k^{\prime}}}\ket{N}_{b_{k^{\prime}}}=(\hat{R}\hat{U}^{(a_{k},b_{k})}_{1}\hat{U}^{(a_{k^{\prime}},b_{k^{\prime}})}_{1}\hat{R}^{\dagger})\hat{R}\ket{0}_{a_{k}}\ket{N}_{b_{k}}\ket{0}_{a_{k^{\prime}}}\ket{N}_{b_{k^{\prime}}}=

\displaystyle\hat{U}^{(\tilde{a}_{k},\tilde{b}_{k})}_{1}\hat{U}^{(\tilde{a}_{k^{\prime}},\tilde{b}_{k^{\prime}})}_{1}\ket{0}_{\tilde{a}_{k}}\ket{N}_{\tilde{a}_{k}}\ket{0}_{\tilde{a}_{k^{\prime}}}\ket{N}_{\tilde{b}_{k^{\prime}}},

(41)

where the symbol tilde stands for a mode basis change,
\hat{R}\hat{U}^{(a_{k},b_{k})}_{1}\hat{R}^{\dagger}=\hat{U}^{(\tilde{a}_{k},\tilde{b}_{k})}_{1}
, and the same for
k^{\prime}
, that does not change the initial state, but rather the mode basis where it is represented. In the general case, for rotations
\hat{R}
coupling all the four modes
a_{k},b_{k}
to modes
a_{k^{\prime}},b_{k^{\prime}}
, the state
\hat{U}^{(\tilde{a}_{k},\tilde{b}_{k})}_{1}\hat{U}^{(\tilde{a}_{k^{\prime}},\tilde{b}_{k^{\prime}})}_{1}\ket{0}_{\tilde{a}_{k}}\ket{N}_{\tilde{b}_{k}}\ket{0}_{\tilde{a}_{k^{\prime}}}\ket{N}_{\tilde{b}_{k^{\prime}}}
is not separable in the mode partition
(a_{k},b_{k})
and
(a_{k^{\prime}},b_{k^{\prime}})
. The only scenario where it can be separable in the partition between modes
(a_{k},b_{k})
and
(a_{k^{\prime}},b_{k^{\prime}})
is if
\hat{R}
is local in each partition
(a_{k},b_{k})
and
(a_{k^{\prime}},b_{k^{\prime}})
. In this case, it is separable both in partition
(\tilde{a}_{k},\tilde{b}_{k})
and
(\tilde{a}_{k^{\prime}},\tilde{b}_{k^{\prime}})
and
(a_{k},b_{k})
and
(a_{k^{\prime}},b_{k^{\prime}})
. However, if
\hat{R}
is local in modes
(a_{k},b_{k})
and
(a_{k^{\prime}},b_{k^{\prime}})
, it cannot implement a two-qubit gate. We conclude that it is impossible to generate a CNOT gate using Gaussian SSRC operations (rotations) alone for any encoding. Non-Gaussian operations are therefore essential for achieving qubit entanglement.

Evaluation and expression-level verification of the prognostic value of palmitoylation-related genes in ovarian cancer
Authors: Qiang Huang, Min He, Ting Cao, Chunyan Chen, Peng Zhou, Zhongyan Liu, Rongkai Xie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Ovarian cancer (OC) remains the most lethal malignancy within the spectrum of gynecological cancers globally. While protein S-palmitoylation has been extensively implicated in tumor progression, its specific functional contributions and molecular mechanisms in the context of OC pathogenesis remain to be fully elucidated. This article aims to explore the prognostic effect associated with palmitoylation in OC. In this study, palmitoylation-related genes (PRGs) were defined as genes encoding enzymes directly involved in the palmitoylation/depalmitoylation process, as well as genes whose functions, subcellular localization, or signaling are regulated by this modification. Based on this definition, PRGs comprising enzymes and regulated substrates, were identified from public transcriptomic databases. By intersecting ovarian cancer (OC)-associated and palmitoylation-linked differentially expressed genes (DEGs), candidate targets were pinpointed. A prognostic risk model was then constructed using LASSO and Cox regression analyses on the TCGA-OV cohort (N = 378) and validated in the GSE51088 cohort (N = 152). This model was integrated into a predictive nomogram and further characterized through pathway enrichment, immune infiltration, checkpoint analysis, drug screening, and mutation profiling. Finally, identified markers were validated via RT-qPCR in clinical samples. Through intersecting DEGs1 and DEGs2, we obtained 24 candidate biomarkers. Four PRGs (HSPG2, BRD4, RARRES1, and SCGB1D2) were identified to construct a prognostic risk model. The risk score, alongside ethnicity and tumor stage, served as an independent prognostic indicator, integrated into a robust nomogram. Mechanistically, high-risk cohorts were characterized by dysregulated ribosome and translation initiation pathways, altered infiltration of seven immune cell types, and significant variations in seven checkpoints (e.g., CTLA4, CD274). Additionally, the model predicted sensitivities for 131 drugs and captured a high TP53 mutation rate. RT-qPCR validation confirmed the upregulation of HSPG2, SCGB1D2, and BRD4, and the downregulation of RARRES1 in OC tissues, showing high consistency with bioinformatic predictions (P < 0.05). This study identified HSPG2, BRD4, RARRES1, and SCGB1D2, which served as prognostic markers reflecting the palmitoylation-related biological landscape in OC that could lay the foundation for innovative therapeutic strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-50074-4.

Introduction

Worldwide, Ovarian cancer (OC) exhibits the highest incidence rate among gynecological malignant tumors and contributes substantially to global cancer-related mortality in females, holding an eighth-ranking position in terms of cancer-associated fatalities1,2. As reported in Hong Kong, China, the incidence rate was 11.5/100,000 in 20233. Clinically, OC manifests as a heterogeneous malignancy characterized by varied pathobiological behaviors and molecular subgroups that translate into distinct clinical trajectories. The majority of ovarian cancer pathologic types are of epithelial origin (90%), among which the high-grade serous subtype contributes to 70–80% of all mortality cases, while 70% of ovarian cancer is identified as advanced disease when diagnosed, with the lack of clinical manifestations. Following cytoreductive surgery combined with platinum-based chemotherapy, 70% of patients are still likely to develop metastasis within a 2–3 year period4. Despite the application of poly-ADP-ribose polymerase (PARP) inhibitors and angiogenesis blockers like bevacizumab as maintenance therapies, outcomes for advanced and recurrent cases remain disappointing. The clinical results have fallen short of expectations, leaving the long-term prognosis with much room for improvement. Given the pressing need to enhance clinical outcomes for ovarian cancer, discovering new therapeutic targets has become a critical priority.

Palmitoylation, a critical lipid-mediated post-translational modification, exerts a pivotal function in modulating protein behavior, affecting membrane binding, intracellular localization, protein stability, and functional activity. This process holds substantial relevance in human disease, especially in the development and progression of various cancers5–7. S-palmitoylation constitutes the predominant modification subtype, characterized by covalent attachment of palmitic acid (16-carbon fatty acid) to specific cysteine residues via reversible thioester linkages7,8. This dynamic process is orchestrated by palmitoylating and depalmitoylating enzymes, which play pivotal roles in oncogenesis, tumor expansion, therapeutic response, and clinical outcomes. The zinc finger DHHC-type containing (ZDHHC) palmitoyl S-acyltransferase (PAT) family predominantly mediates protein palmitoylation reactions9. However, the biological impact of palmitoylation extends beyond these catalytic enzymes to include a vast array of substrate proteins and downstream effectors whose functionality depends on this lipid modification5–7. To systematically characterize the palmitoylation-related landscape in OC, this study collectively defines these two categories as PRGs. Mammalian systems express 23 zDHHC variants (zDHHC1-24, excluding zDHHC10). Growing evidence demonstrates that palmitoylation cycling directly influences protein functionality, consequently modifying cellular signaling networks and promoting malignant transformation. Furthermore, the palmitoylation state of key proteins (e.g., EGFR, RAS, and PD-1/PD-L1) exerts a critical influence on tumor progression and therapeutic responsiveness. S-palmitoylation in OC is not merely a bystander modification but a critical regulator of the EGFR/RAS/TP53 signaling hub10,11. In OC, hyper-palmitoylation of membrane-bound oncogenes prevents their degradation and sustains constitutive growth signaling12,13. Therefore, identifying a gene signature that reflects this ‘palmitoylation-active’ state is essential for selecting patients who may benefit from targeting this post-translational modification.

This study employs an integrated multi-omics strategy to conduct a systematic investigation into the prognostic significance and molecular mechanisms of palmitoylation-related genes (PRGs) in OC. By analyzing transcriptomic datasets from GEO and TCGA-OV, we conducted differential gene expression profiling as well as survival prognostic analysis, and functional enrichment annotation to clarify the regulatory role of PRGs in OC pathogenesis. To assess the correlation between PRG activity and patient prognosis, single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was adopted, followed by the identification and functional annotation of OC-specific PRGs. The Cox proportional hazards model was utilized to establish a prognostic signature based on PRGs, and its predictive performance and clinical utility were thoroughly assessed. Furthermore, we explored PRG-mediated modulation of tumor microenvironment immune characteristics and validated the expression patterns of key PRGs through in vitro experiments. This study provides the first comprehensive characterization of the global palmitoylation regulatory network in OC, offering novel insights into disease mechanisms and laying the foundation for developing precision therapies targeting protein palmitoylation.

Materials and methods

Data collection

The OC related transcriptome datasets (GSE26712 and GSE51088, based on microarray platforms) were acquired from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). GSE26712, which uses the GPL96 platform, comprises 181 ovarian cancer tissue samples and 10 normal ovarian tissue controls. Detailed pathological annotations reveal a mixed histological composition, including serous, endometrioid, and mucinous carcinomas, although 23 samples lack specific subtype information. Clinical records indicate that survival follow-up data (PFS and OS) are available for only 153 of these cancer samples. Given the limited number of controls and the histological diversity of this cohort, the GSE26712 dataset was utilized strictly for the preliminary identification of differentially expressed genes (DEGs1 and DEGs2) to provide a broad candidate set for subsequent refinement14. On the other hand, GSE51088 (GPL7264 platform) consisted of tumour tissue from 172 °C samples (containing 152 samples with survival information)15. Gene expression profiles, clinical information, additionally, survival records of The Cancer Genome Atlas the RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq) data and clinical information of the (TCGA)-OV cohort were obtained from the University of California, Santa Cruz (UCSC) Xena platform (available at https://xenabrowser.net/) on May 17, 2024, encompassing 378 °C tumour tissue samples16. In addition, 23 palmitoylation-related genes (PRGs) were gained in published literature17. In this study, PRGs are defined as a comprehensive set encompassing both direct palmitoylating/depalmitoylating enzymes (e.g., the ZDHHC family) and genes that are functionally integrated into palmitoylation-mediated regulatory networks or downstream signaling axes. Therefore, these PRGs serve as indirect markers to characterize the palmitoylation-related biological landscape rather than as direct measurements of the dynamic protein palmitoylation state.

Definition and acquisition of PRGs

To identify genes associated with palmitoylation, PRG values were initially computed within the GSE26712 microarray dataset employing the ssGSEA methodology through the GSVA software package (v1.38.2)18. Notably, both DEGs1 (OC vs. control) and DEGs2 (high- vs. low-palmitoylation score) were identified using the same GSE26712 platform. By performing differential analysis within a single dataset, the potential interference of batch effects and platform heterogeneity was effectively eliminated at the discovery stage. Then, we assessed the PRGs scores disparities between OC and control samples (P < 0.05) and employed the ggplot2 package (v 0.1.4)19 for visualization. Subsequently, a total of 153 °C samples in GSE26712 that included survival information were classified into high-scoring and low-scoring cohorts according to the best ssGSEA cut-off value for differential PRGs (minprop = 0.2). The survival package (version 3.5-3)20, was used to assess survival differences between the two scoring groups; this was achieved by constructing Kaplan–Meier (K–M) survival curves and conducting Log-rank statistical tests (with a significance threshold of P < 0.05). The identification of candidate genes was performed in two distinct steps within the GSE26712 dataset. First, DEGs1 were identified to capture genes associated with OC tumorigenesis by comparing 185 tumor tissue samples against 10 normal ovarian surface epithelial samples. Differential expression analysis was executed using the R package “limma” (v 1.38.0), with screening thresholds set at log2Fold Change (FC)∣>0.5 and P < 0.05. Second, DEGs2 were identified to pinpoint genes linked specifically to palmitoylation activity within the OC cohort. For this purpose, single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) scores for PRGs were computed for the 153 °C samples containing survival information using the “GSVA” package (v1.38.2). These samples were stratified into high- and low-scoring groups based on an optimal ssGSEA cutoff value of 1.57929. The “limma” package was then employed to analyze the differential expression between these two scoring groups using the same thresholds (∣log2FC∣>0.5 and P < 0.05). Finally, the intersection of DEGs1 and DEGs2 was obtained to identify candidate genes that are both dysregulated in ovarian cancer and functionally associated with palmitoylation, ensuring a robust logic for subsequent prognostic model construction. The visualization of these DEGs1 and DEGs2 involved creating a heatmap and a volcano plot was generated using the ggplot2 package (version 0.1.4)19. A Venn diagram was created using the ggVenn package (v 1.2.2)21 to determine common genes between DEGs1 and DEGs2 derived from the previous analyses, and these overlapping genes were termed candidate targets. In this study, we employed a two-step strategy to define the palmitoylation-related landscape in OC. First, we curated a ‘Seed Set’ of 30 core enzymes directly involved in the palmitoylation/depalmitoylation process based on previous literature17, comprising 23 palmitoyltransferases (ZDHHC family) and 7 depalmitoylases. The complete list and functional categories of these 30 core enzymes are provided in Supplementary Table 1. Second, we identified ‘Palmitoylation-Responsive Genes’ by assessing the transcriptomic impact of these core enzymes. The ssGSEA algorithm was utilized to calculate a ‘palmitoylation activity score’ for each OC sample in the GSE26712 dataset based on the 30-gene Seed Set. Using an optimal cut-off value of 1.57929, samples were stratified into high- and low-palmitoylation activity groups. Differential expression analysis (DEGs2) was then performed using the “limma” R package with thresholds of |log2FC| > 0.5 and P-value < 0.05. This process yielded 31 DEGs that represent genes whose expression is significantly modulated by the global palmitoylation status. Consequently, the intersection of these 31 activity-related DEGs (DEGs2) and OC-specific DEGs (DEGs1) identified 24 candidate biomarkers. This framework identifies a signature of four hub genes (HSPG2, BRD4, RARRES1, and SCGB1D2) that exhibit strong statistical associations with palmitoylation-related enzymatic activity, suggesting their potential integration within the palmitoylation-regulated landscape of OC. The primary objective of this experimental section was to perform a platform cross-validation. It aimed to confirm whether the expression patterns of the four candidate genes (HSPG2, BRD4, RARRES1, and SCGB1D2) identified from the GSE26712 transcriptomic dataset remain consistent when measured using the RT-qPCR platform.

Biological enrichment and molecular interaction network analysis

To investigate the biological processes and signaling networks that are implicated, we conducted a functional enrichment analysis on the target genes by applying both Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG)22–24 approaches. This approach shed light on the molecular functions and key pathways involved, providing deeper insights into the genetic framework under investigation. For this in-depth analysis, we employed the ClusterProfiler package (version 4.4.4) with a statistical significance threshold of p < 0.05. Following this, protein-protein interaction networks were built by utilizing the STRING database (Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes; https://string-db.org) with a confidence threshold above 0.1525. Network visualization and topological analysis were accomplished through Cytoscape software (v 3.1.1)26 for the identification of hub genes and interaction patterns. To further explore the physical and functional interactions between the prognostic hub genes and the enzymatic machinery of palmitoylation, we performed a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis. We integrated these genes with a ‘seed set’ of core enzymes into the STRING database (https://string-db.org/). A minimum required interaction score of 0.15 (low confidence) was applied to capture potential regulatory axes, and the resulting network was visualized to identify direct or indirect connections.

Construction and validation of risk model

While candidate genes were initially screened using the mixed-subtype GSE26712 dataset, the construction of the prognostic risk model was performed exclusively using the TCGA-OV dataset (n = 378). This approach was chosen because the TCGA-OV cohort consists predominantly of High-Grade Serous Ovarian Cancer (HGSOC, > 80%), allowing for a more focused analysis of the most aggressive and lethal subtype of OC. The TCGA-OV cohort (N = 378) sourced from the Cancer Genome Atlas, functioned as the development dataset for risk model construction. To evaluate the model’s generalizability, a completely independent cohort of patient samples from the GSE51088 dataset (N = 152) was utilized for external validation. These two datasets were derived from different genomic platforms and distinct patient populations. Initially, based on OC tumour samples from TCGA-OV, the survival package (v 3.5-3) cox.zph function was employed to conduct univariate Cox regression to pinpoint survival-associated genes (Hazard Ratio (HR) ≠ 1, P < 0.1). Proportional hazards (PH) assumption testing was performed (P > 0.05), and results were presented using the forestplot package (v 2.0.1)27. Subsequent to this, the glmnet package (version 4.1.4)27 was used to perform least absolute selection and shrinkage operator (LASSO) analysis. The study’s prognostic genes were determined at the point when lambda reached lambda.min. Thereafter, in TCGA-OV, for each ovarian cancer patient in the dataset, individualized risk scores were calculated by utilizing the relative expression levels of prognostic genes and the coefficients obtained from LASSO regression. Patients were stratified into high-risk and low-risk subgroups. The optimal risk score threshold for the training set (TCGA-OV) was identified as 1.147367, calculated via the surv_cutpoint algorithm with a minprop constraint of 0.2. For the external validation set (GSE51088), the risk score for each patient was first calculated using the same formula derived from the training set. Subsequently, an independent optimal threshold of – 0.00153919 was determined for the validation cohort, following the same data-driven principle (maximizing log-rank statistics with minprop = 0.2) to ensure the model’s performance was evaluated under the most biologically relevant stratification for that specific population. Risk distribution analysis was subsequently performed by generating scatter plots that illustrated risk score patterns and survival outcomes of OC patients. Additionally, the expression profiles of prognostic biomarkers were visualized, and Kaplan–Meier (K–M) survival analysis for overall survival (OS) between the two risk groups was performed using the survminer package (v 0.4.9)28(P < 0.05). Following this, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was conducted using the survivalROC package (v 1.0.3)29 to calculate area under curve (AUC) values at 1, 2, and 3-year time points for assessing model performance. Additionally, model validation was conducted in an independent validation dataset. Furthermore, the pROC package was utilized to compare the predictive performance of the risk score against clinical variables (age and stage). ROC curves were plotted, and the Area Under the Curve (AUC) was calculated with 95% confidence intervals. The DeLong test was applied to evaluate the statistical significance of differences between AUC values (P < 0.05).

Independent prognostic analysis

To construct a risk stratification system for estimating patient survival probabilities in OC, integration of risk scores as well as clinical characteristics (including risk score, age, race, histological grade (G1/2 and G3), stage (stage1/2, stage3, stage4) of OC patients in TCGA-OV dataset were integrated. Additionally, univariate Cox regression analysis (with P < 0.05 as the cutoff) was conducted together with multivariate Cox regression analysis to identify independent prognostic factors (using P < 0.05 as the significance threshold). Following this, a nomogram was built using the rms package (version 6.5-1)30, incorporating independent prognostic factors. The rms package (v 6.5-1), the same as before, allowed for the generation of calibration plots at 1, 2, and 3-year intervals, whereas ROC curve visualization was achieved through the timeROC package (v 0.4)31 Clinical utility evaluation of the predictive model was conducted by generating decision curves using the ggDCA package (v 1.2) (https://www.rdocumentation.org/packages/ggDCA/versions/1.1).

Correlation of risk score with clinical characteristics

Through systematic evaluation involving multiple analytical approaches, the relationship between calculated risk scores and demographic as well as clinical features of OC study participants was assessed. Firstly, the distinctions in risk score among different clinical characteristics were compared. The survival package (v 3.5-3) and the survminer package (v 0.4.9)32 were employed to construct KM curves, which assessed the distinctions in OS between the two risk cohorts under different subsets of clinical features. To characterize the global palmitoylation activity across different risk strata, we calculated the ssGSEA scores of the core palmitoylation-related enzymes (PRG score) for each sample in the TCGA-OV cohort. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was subsequently employed to compare the distribution of PRG scores between high-risk (n = 213) and low-risk (n = 165) groups, providing a quantitative assessment of the palmitoylation landscape’s dysregulation in relation to the prognostic model.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) of high and low risk cohorts

Using calculated risk assessment values, the TCGA-OV training dataset patients were categorized into separate risk groups. Then, all genes were sorted based on logFC between the two risk cohorts. Finally, to conduct GSEA on the two risk cohorts, the ClusterProfiler package was employed. The c2.cp.v2023.2.Hs.symbols.gmt gene set was sourced from MSigDB database (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb) applying threshold parameters of false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.25 and P < 0.05. The enrichplot package (v 0.92) (https://rdrr.io/cran/corrplot/) facilitated line plot generation, depicting the top 5 pathways ranked by significance from highest to lowest (P < 0.05).

Analysis of immune cell infiltration and assessment of immunotherapy response

The CIBERSORT algorithm (v 1.03)33 was used to perform immune cell composition analysis, quantifying 22 immune cell subtypes in the TCGA-OV cohort. Differential immune infiltration patterns were examined and visualized through box plots displaying immune cell abundance variations (P < 0.05). The psych package (v 2.4.3)34. Supported correlation analysis for examining relationships among differential immune cells and associations between prognostic genes and immune cell populations. Furthermore, to evaluate immunosuppressive traits and treatment response potential, immune checkpoint assessment across risk-stratified cohorts was carried out using the Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) platform (http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu).

Drug sensitivity analysis

The information on chemical drugs for OC and their half maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) values were retrieved from the GDSC (Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer) databases (http://cancerrxgene.org). Pharmacogenomic sensitivity profiling was conducted using the pRRophetic algorithm (v 0.5)35 to predict drug response metrics (IC50) for standard chemotherapeutics and targeted therapies in the TCGA-OV cohort. Subsequently, differential drug sensitivity analysis was performed to identify significant variations in IC50 values between risk-stratified patient subgroups (adjusted P-value < 0.05). ggplot2 (v 3.4.1) was used to visualize the most significantly altered agents, based on hierarchical ranking by statistical significance.

Mutation status of OC patients in high and low risk cohorts

In order to better understand variations in driver genes between two risk cohorts, the maftools package (v 2.20.0)36 was utilized to analyze gene mutations in two risk cohorts and display the top 20 high-frequency mutated genes in a tumor mutational burden (TMB) waterfall plot.

Prognostic gene expression levels

The ggplot2 package (v 0.1.4) was utilized to plot box-and-line diagrams for visualization. RNAs from 5 pairs of tumour tissue—consisting of 5 °C samples and 5 corresponding adjacent samples—were collected. Soybean-sized OC tissue and corresponding adjacent tissue were obtained intraoperatively from each ovarian cancer patient respectively. For each specimen, the tumor tissue content (requiring tumor cell proportion ≥ 70%) was confirmed by a pathologist immediately after excision. Then, Approximately 3 g of fresh tissue was aliquoted under sterile conditions, quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen, and subsequently transferred to a – 80 °C ultra-low temperature refrigerator for storage to ensure RNA integrity. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Army Medical University (approval number: 2024-311-02). Informed consent was obtained from the patients. RNA isolation from tissue samples adhered to the manufacturer’s protocols. The SweScript First Strand cDNA synthesis kit was used for reverse transcription, with SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix employed in qPCR reactions. Primer details are provided in Supplementary Tables 2 and patient clinical information is presented in Supplementary Table 3. Expression levels of genes were normalized against H-GAPDH and quantified via the 2– ΔΔCt approach, with statistical evaluation conducted via Graphpad Prism 5.

Statistical analysis

All computational and statistical analyses were executed using R software (v 4.2.2). To balance the discovery of potential biological signals with the control of false-positive results, multiple testing correction strategies were applied based on the specific analytical context. For high-throughput screenings involving thousands of simultaneous tests, such as Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA), the False Discovery Rate (FDR) was controlled, with FDR < 0.25 and P < 0.05 set as the significance thresholds. Similarly, in drug sensitivity profiling involving 131 agents, Adjusted P-values (Padj < 0.05) were utilized to minimize false-positive drug-risk associations.

In contrast, nominal P-values (P < 0.05) were applied in the following scenarios: (1) Initial DEG screening (DEGs1 and DEGs2), where a “screening-then-validation” strategy was employed to avoid missing potential low-abundance regulators; (2) functional enrichment (GO/KEGG) of the 24 candidate genes, where the small input size inherently limits the risk of multiple-comparison errors; and (3) phenotypic verification of immune infiltration, checkpoint expression, and RT-qPCR, which serve as descriptive assessments of model consistency. Survival disparities between risk cohorts were evaluated using the Log-rank test with nominal P < 0.05 as the standard for preliminary prognostic association.

Results

Acquisition of 24 candidate genes

Overall, 4,317 DEGs1 were detected, with 2114 being up-regulated and 2203 being down-regulated genes in OC samples (Fig. 1A and B). Additional analysis evaluated the variations in PRGs scores between OC samples and control samples in GSE26712, revealing a notable distinction in PRGs scores between the two cohorts (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1C). Ten PRGs showing notable distinctions in their scores between the OC and control cohorts were selected for subsequent analyses. Based on 153 °C samples containing survival information in GSE26712, using the optimal cut-off value of 1.57929 for the ssGSEA scores of the 10 PRGs, they were classified into high-score (n = 98) and low-score (n = 55) cohorts. Log-rank analysis demonstrated significant survival disparities between scoring groups (P = 0.041), with the low-scoring cohort exhibiting reduced survival outcomes (Fig. 1D). A total of 31 DEGs2 were identified, consisting of 7 upregulated and 24 downregulated genes when comparing high-score versus low-score groups (Fig. 1E). These DEGs2 represent genes that are significantly dysregulated in response to variations in palmitoylation activity (as measured by ssGSEA scores of the 23 PRGs). While DEGs2 are not required to be members of the initial PRG set, their intersection with OC-specific DEGs1 highlights candidate markers whose expression is both tumor-specific and palmitoylation-dependent. Venn diagram analysis between DEGs1 and DEGs2 yielded 24 overlapping genes designated as potential candidates (Fig. 1F). These 24 candidate genes, representing the intersection of OC-related DEGs and palmitoylation-related DEGs, served as the foundation for the subsequent identification of core prognostic markers. These genes were identified as the primary candidate targets because they are not only dysregulated in OC but also significantly associated with palmitoylation activity. These 24 overlapping genes served as preliminary candidates for further evaluation in the construction of the prognostic risk model.

Biological pathways and PPI analysis of candidate genes

Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis of the 24 prognostic genes identified 246 significantly enriched biological processes (BP), encompassing hydrogen peroxide degradation pathways and extracellular matrix organization, while 29 cellular components (CC) entries including complex of collagen trimers, collagen-containing extracellular matrix, haptoglobin-hemoglobin complex, and 31 molecular functions (MF) encompassing antioxidant activity and transcriptional coactivation (Fig. 2A). Moreover, KEGG pathway analysis revealed 7 enriched pathways, particularly focal adhesion, ATP-dependent chromatin remodeling, as well as proteoglycans in cancer, african trypanosomiasis, among others (Fig. 2B). PPI network consisting of 19 nodes and 42 edges; the top 3 proteins, COL4A2, HSPG2, and FLNA, were interacted most strongly with other proteins (Fig. 2C). To further substantiate the biological link between the four hub genes and the palmitoylation machinery, we constructed a PPI network incorporating the 4 prognostic markers and 30 core enzymes (23 palmitoyltransferases and 7 depalmitoylases) using the STRING database. With a confidence threshold of > 0.15, the network revealed intrinsic connections; notably, BRD4 exhibited a direct physical interaction with ZDHHC5 (Fig. S1), providing evidence of a protein-level regulatory axis.

Recognition of HSPG2, BRD4, RARRES1, and SCGB1D2 as prognostic genes

The 24 candidate genes obtained from the intersection analysis were subjected to univariate Cox regression screening (P < 0.1). After validating the proportional hazards (PH) assumption (P > 0.05 to confirm validity), 4 survival-associated genes within this candidate set were chosen for further analysis (Fig. 3A; Table 1). To minimize overfitting and enhance model robustness, these 4 candidates underwent LASSO regression analysis. The optimal regularization resulted in the identification of 4 prognostic biomarkers—HSPG2, BRD4, RARRES1, and SCGB1D2—with the optimal lambda set at 0.00222 (Fig. 3B and C), indicating their collective contribution to survival prediction. Although these markers may not all serve as direct substrates for palmitoylation, they represent key functional nodes within the broader palmitoylation-related signaling and metabolic landscape in OC.

Development of a risk model with high accuracy

Thus, a risk model was established based on the expression intensity and risk coefficients of 4 prognostic genes. The constructed risk model was as follows: RiskScore = HSPG2 × 0.09 + BRD4 × 0.14 + RARRES1 × 0.08 + SCGB1D2 × (-0.01). According to this model, patients in TCGA-OV were classified into high-risk (n = 213) and low-risk (n = 165) cohorts using optimal cutoff value of 1.15 for risk score (Fig. 4A and B), and the number of deaths increased as risk score in the sample increased, where patients in the high-risk cohort had a reduced survival rate (P = 0.00041) (Fig. 4C). ROC curve analysis suggested that risk model had moderate predictive capacity, with AUCs for 1, 2, and 3 years in TCGA-OV being 0.65, 0.65, and 0.61, respectively (Fig. 4D). Additionally, patients were divided into high-risk (n = 71) and low-risk (n = 74) cohorts according to the optimal risk score cutoff value of (-0.00153919). Furthermore, independent validation using 152 patient samples from the GSE51088 cohort confirmed that it had some predictive accuracy, as evidenced by AUCs exceeding 0.6 for 1, 2, and 3 years, underscoring the model’s consistent performance (Fig. 4E, F, G and H). These outcomes validated the robustness of the risk model for evaluating the prognostic risk of OC patients. Notably, while the AUC values (ranging from 0.61 to 0.67) represent moderate predictive accuracy, they are comparable to those of several established molecular signatures in ovarian cancer research16. These results indicate that while the model provides a significant degree of risk stratification, there remains potential for further optimization by incorporating additional clinical or molecular parameters.

Biological pathway and mutation point analysis of risk cohorts in OC

Comparative pathway analysis between prognostic risk-stratified cohorts in the TCGA-OV dataset revealed 429 statistically significant enriched pathways (FDR < 0.25, P < 0.05), with prominent representation of translational initiation (Medicus reference), ribosomal signaling, and eukaryotic translation elongation processes (Reactome database) (Fig. 7A). The process of identifying and categorizing genes based on the presence of mutations was conducted by analyzing mutational profiles from the TCGA-OV dataset. Within the high-risk cohort, the TP53 gene stood out with an exceptionally high mutation rate, hitting 95% (Fig. 7B). On the other hand, in the low-risk cohort, the TP53 gene also showed a notable mutation rate, at 91% (Fig. 7B and C). This high prevalence of TP53 mutations (> 90% in both groups) is consistent with the classical molecular hallmarks of high-grade serous ovarian carcinoma (HGSOC), where TP53 mutation rates are reported to be as high as 96% [64]. These results indicate that the observed mutation patterns primarily reflect the global genomic instability inherent to OC pathogenesis rather than acting as a primary driver of the risk stratification itself. However, the slightly elevated mutation frequency in the high-risk group may suggest that dysregulated palmitoylation-related genes could further exacerbate DNA damage response (DDR) deficiencies, thereby contributing to a more aggressive genomic landscape, a hypothesis further explored in the discussion.

Identifying potential therapeutic agents and small-molecule inhibitors associated with the risk score in OC

Systematic profiling of chemosensitivity parameters was conducted for potential therapeutic agents, primarily targeting-specific pathways and small-molecule inhibitors, based on predictions from the GDSC database. These results possess inherent limitations: first, cancer cell lines may accumulate molecular characteristics during prolonged culture that differ from primary tumors, potentially leading to discrepancies between predicted and actual patient responses; second, the algorithm does not account for the tumor microenvironment or intratumoral heterogeneity, which significantly influence therapeutic efficacy in vivo; third, there is currently no clinical validation data available to substantiate these predictions. Therefore, these IC50 variations represent only potential therapeutic trends and should not be interpreted as clinical recommendations for drug selection.

To bridge the gap between statistical inference and biological reality, we performed experimental verification. Although the sample size for validation was limited (n = 5 pairs), the consistent up-regulation of HSPG2, SCGB1D2, and BRD4 across all clinical specimens provides preliminary yet solid experimental evidence for their role in OC pathogenesis.

Discussion

Ovarian cancer (OC) ranks among the most invasive and deadly malignant tumors affecting the female reproductive system, with over 300,000 new cases diagnosed and more than 200,000 deaths recorded worldwide annually37. The absence of distinctive early-stage symptoms and reliable screening methods results in approximately 70% of patients receiving their diagnosis during advanced disease phases (stages III or IV), leading to unfavorable clinical outcomes4. Despite substantial advancements in surgical interventions, chemotherapeutic strategies, and targeted treatment modalities, the overall five-year survival rate continues to remain under 45%38. Consequently, the identification of innovative molecular markers and elucidation of their underlying pathways represents a critical priority for enhancing early detection capabilities and developing more efficacious individualized treatment approaches for OC patients.

Over the past few years, researchers have increasingly focused on how epigenetic alterations drive cancer initiation and progression. As a reversible lipid modification, S-palmitoylation modulates protein stability, subcellular localization, and protein–protein interactions, and is broadly involved in cell signaling, immune regulation, and tumor development39. Although several studies have reported associations between palmitoyltransferases—such as members of the ZDHHC family—and cancer prognosis, a systematic investigation of protein palmitoylation in OC remains lacking40. Within this study, we conducted a comprehensive characterization of the expression profile and prognostic significance of PRGs, subsequently establishing a reliable four-gene signature that incorporates HSPG2, BRD4, RARRES1, and SCGB1D2. The model demonstrated favorable predictive performance across multiple clinical subgroups, underscoring its potential value for clinical risk stratification and personalized treatment in ovarian cancer.

The primary focus of this investigation is the transcriptomic dimension of palmitoylation-related regulation. We emphasize that our prognostic risk score is designed as a comprehensive functional readout of the overall palmitoylation landscape in OC, rather than a mere list of direct catalytic enzymes. While the core members of the ZDHHC family execute the modification, their activity is strictly governed by upstream transcriptional regulators and the availability of fatty acid substrates. Therefore, our four-gene signature identifies a subset of patients with high ‘palmitoylation-dependency’ by capturing key nodes across the entire regulatory axis—from metabolic substrate supply to transcriptional control of depalmitoylation machinery.

BRD4 functions as a chromatin reader that recognizes acetylated histones and regulates transcriptional elongation, thereby regulating the expression of genes involved in the cell cycle, inflammation, and tumorigenesis. In breast cancer, the low-abundance short isoform (BRD4-S) exhibits oncogenic activity, while the predominant long isoform (BRD4-L) suppresses tumor formation and metastasis. Intriguingly, in ovarian cancer, BRD4-L similarly inhibits proliferation and migration41. In OC, BRD4 exhibits significant amplification, with elevated expression strongly associated with unfavorable prognosis. Instead of being a passive downstream element, BRD4 functions as a master regulator of the palmitoylation cycle machinery. Specifically, BRD4 has been shown to transcriptionally activate the depalmitoylase APT1 (LYPLA1); inhibition of BRD4 leads to the suppression of APT1, which subsequently maintains the hyper-palmitoylation status of oncogenes like mutant EGFR, thereby preventing their nuclear translocation and enhancing drug sensitivity42. This establishes a direct mechanistic feedback loop where BRD4 levels dictate the depalmitoylation rate of critical membrane-bound oncoproteins. The observed reduction of the PRG score in the high-risk group suggests that a dysregulated palmitoylation cycle, rather than a simple linear increase in activity, drives OC malignancy. This is consistent with our finding that BRD4 directly interacts with ZDHHC5. Since ZDHHC5 is known to regulate the plasma membrane localization of key oncogenic proteins, its potential dysregulation in the high-risk group—indicated by our model—may create a ‘palmitoylation dependency’ in tumor cells. Therefore, our model identifies patients with a fragile palmitoylation landscape who are most likely to be sensitive to specific palmitoylation modulators, rather than counter-indicating their use. Thus, BRD4 represents a nuclear executor of palmitoylation-triggered oncogenic signaling.

RARRES1 (also known as TIG1, tazarotene-induced gene 1) is a retinoic acid–responsive tumor suppressor gene involved in cell differentiation and apoptosis. Although RARRES1 acts as a tumor suppressor in cancers, its role exhibits tissue-specific duality. For example, in glomerular diseases, RARRES1 promotes p53-mediated podocyte apoptosis, leading to podocytopenia and glomerulosclerosis43. Recent studies have identified RARRES1 as a tumor microenvironment (TME)-associated gene, and its overexpression has been shown to inhibit ovarian cancer cell proliferation, migration, and invasion—findings that suggest a tumor-suppressive role and its potential to serve as a predictive biomarker for immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) response44. Moreover, single-cell analyses have revealed that RARRES1 is primarily enriched in immune cell subsets, indicating that it may exert protective effects by modulating the immune microenvironment45. RARRES1 (TIG1) is intrinsically linked to the palmitoylation process via two routes. Evidence suggests that RARRES1 is itself a potential target of S-palmitoylation, required for its stable anchoring to the plasma membrane and its tumor-suppressive activity45,46. Furthermore, RARRES1 acts as a metabolic rheostat that governs the de novo lipogenesis pathway, directly modulating the availability of the palmitate pool—the essential 16-carbon substrate for all ZDHHC-mediated events. Its downregulation in high-risk patients likely reflects a metabolic state that fuels aberrant lipid signaling.

HSPG2 encodes Perlecan, a multifunctional heparan sulfate proteoglycan that serves as a structural scaffold in the extracellular matrix (ECM) while dynamically regulating tumor angiogenesis, cell adhesion, and metastatic dissemination. Increased expression of HSPG2 has been detected in multiple cancers and is tightly linked to enhanced tumor invasiveness and ECM remodeling46. Increased HSPG2 expression acts as an independent prognostic indicator in patients with acute myeloid leukemia (AML) and correlates with an unfavorable clinical prognosis47. Moreover, HSPG2 has been shown to bind multiple growth factors, such as VEGF and FGF2, enhancing their signaling activity and promoting tumor angiogenesis and progression48. Notably, high-throughput palmitoyl-proteomic screenings have identified HSPG2 (Perlecan) as a directly palmitoylated protein (SwissPalm entry: P98160)49. Palmitoylation of the HSPG2 core protein is may be crucial for its appropriate assembly within the extracellular matrix and its ability to modulate growth factor signaling via membrane-associated domains. This direct modification bridges the gap between intracellular lipid modification and the remodeling of the pro-tumorigenic niche.

SCGB1D2, a member of the secretoglobin protein family, is widely expressed in exocrine tissues and is implicated in modulating immune responses, suppressing cell migration, and regulating inflammation. It acts as a host defense factor found in skin, sweat, and other secretions, and plays a protective role against Borrelia burgdorferi infection, offering a promising therapeutic avenue for Lyme disease50. Although SCGB1D2 has not been extensively studied in ovarian cancer, its expression has been associated with benign tumors in breast cancer, and downregulation is often observed in more aggressive cancer subtypes51. SCGB1D2 is a secreted lipophilic protein whose transport through the secretory pathway is gated by palmitoylation-dependent sorting machineries. The lipidation status of Golgi-resident proteins dictates the cargo selection and secretion efficiency of proteins like SCGB1D212. Its inclusion in our model highlights the downstream secretory consequences of an altered palmitoylation landscape, serving as a surrogate marker for vesicle trafficking integrity in the OC microenvironment.

This study proposes a novel method for stratifying survival risk in ovarian cancer patients, using a prognostic risk model built from four palmitoylation-related genes: HSPG2, BRD4, RARRES1, and SCGB1D2. By integrating molecular characteristics with conventional clinical parameters such as tumor stage and histological grade, the model not only provides a reliable reference of prognosis prediction but also offers a framework for understanding the biological mechanisms through which palmitoylation influences ovarian cancer progression52. The four-gene signature developed in this study exhibited AUC values ranging from 0.61 to 0.65, which represents a moderate predictive performance. This level of accuracy is consistent with many previously reported molecular prognostic models for ovarian cancer16. It is important to emphasize that this model is intended as a complementary tool rather than a replacement for established clinical indicators such as FIGO stage. By integrating molecular-level palmitoylation features, this signature helps identify high-risk patients within the same clinical stage or age group, thereby providing additional evidence for personalized therapeutic decision-making. Future research incorporating more granular clinical variables, such as specific chemotherapy regimens and comorbidities, is expected to further refine the model’s predictive precision. The independent prognostic value of the risk score underscores its potential to serve as a tool for clinical decision-making, especially for detecting high-risk patients that could benefit from enhanced treatment or targeted intervention of the palmitoylation pathway. The robust performance of the nomogram (AUC > 0.7) indicates its practical utility in individualized survival prediction, effectively connecting basic research with clinical practice53. Further investigation is warranted to substantiate these results in prospective cohorts and to further investigate the functional mechanisms through which these genes mediate therapeutic resistance or immune escape.

Subgroup analyses revealed significant survival disparities between risk groups stratified by age (> 45 years), race (White), stage (III), and histologic grade (G3). The results of this study align with earlier research indicating that age > 45 is an adverse prognostic factor for ovarian cancer, likely related to hormonal changes, immune decline, and delayed diagnosis54. Caucasian patients represent the majority of ovarian cancer cases and have been associated with poorer survival in some studies, possibly due to genetic and socioeconomic factors55. In this study, ethnicity was found to be statistically associated with prognosis. However, this variable, largely based on self-reported data in the TCGA database, may act as a proxy for complex factors including socioeconomic status, disparities in healthcare access, and variations in therapeutic response across different populations. These findings do not imply a direct biological or genetic causality but rather reflect the demographic characteristics of the specific study cohort. Therefore, the inclusion of ethnicity in the prognostic model should be interpreted with caution, and its applicability to other ethnically diverse populations requires further validation. Stage III is the most common advanced stage, often accompanied by peritoneal dissemination and markedly poor outcomes56. These results not only validate the model’s predictive consistency across clinical subgroups but also demonstrate its ability to identify high-risk patients within traditionally defined risk strata, suggesting broad applicability and clinical potential.

Dysregulation of protein translation has been implicated as a critical mechanism in ovarian cancer progression. We observed significant enrichment of translation initiation and elongation pathways in high-risk patients, implicating hyperactivated protein synthesis as a hallmark of aggressive OC. Intriguingly, it is plausible to speculate that palmitoylation potentially contributes to this process, as prior studies show it regulates translation factor localization57. Prior studies suggest that palmitoylation could affect the subcellular localization58. Moreover, palmitoylation may promote tumor cells’ abnormal reliance on protein synthesis by influencing the mTOR signaling pathway and associated translational regulators59. While these observations align with the enrichment of translation-associated pathways in the high-risk group, these mechanistic links remain hypothetical and have not been directly tested in the current study. The activation of pathways such as Medicus Reference Translation Initiation and Eukaryotic Translation Elongation in the high-risk group may partially explain their increased tumor metabolism and invasiveness. These enrichments suggest heightened protein synthesis, which may drive faster cell cycling and increased metastatic potential, which is consistent with the known role of translational dysregulation in cancer60. Key translational factors such as eIF4E, eEF2, and mTORC1 have already emerged as potential anticancer targets61. Regarding the clinical application of palmitoylation inhibitors (e.g., 2-BP or ZDHHC-specific inhibitors), our findings do not ‘counter-indicate’ their use but rather provide a precision stratification strategy. The high-risk group, characterized by PRG dysregulation and genomic instability (e.g., TP53 mutations)13, represents the population most likely to exhibit ‘oncogene addiction’ to palmitoylation-driven signaling hubs. By identifying patients with high risk scores, our model establishes a potential biomarker-driven framework for future clinical trials, ensuring that lipid-modification therapies are directed toward patients with the highest therapeutic vulnerability.

The investigation identified substantial disparities in various characteristics across risk stratifications, comprising immune checkpoint expression (e.g., CTLA4, CD274), infiltrating immune cell levels, and sensitivity patterns toward targeted small-molecule inhibitors, suggesting a potential association between palmitoylation-related genes and the modulation of the ovarian tumor immune microenvironment, consequently presenting viable targets for immune-based therapies. The specific mechanisms by which these genes orchestrate immune cell infiltration remain speculative and necessitate further functional investigation. Research has shown that protein palmitoylation is closely linked to immune responses, and immune checkpoint molecules (e.g., CTLA4 and PD-L1) rely on lipid modifications, including palmitoylation, for membrane localization, stability, and function62,63. Our findings further demonstrated notable differences in the infiltration levels of immune cells (including CD8 + T cells, regulatory T cells, and M2 macrophages) between the groups. This aligns with previous research showing that lipid metabolic reprogramming of tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), including palmitoylation, plays a crucial role in shaping their immune functions64. In vitro studies have demonstrated that inhibition of key palmitoyltransferases (e.g., ZDHHC9, ZDHHC3) can downregulate PD-L1 protein expression and enhance tumor sensitivity to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs)65.

Furthermore, we identified a markedly increased prevalence of TP53 genetic alterations among high-risk patients, implying that our risk classification may mirror fundamental genomic dysregulation. Within high-grade serous ovarian carcinoma (HGSOC), TP53 constitutes the most commonly altered gene, exhibiting mutations in 96% of patient samples66. The fact that both risk cohorts in this study maintained mutation rates exceeding 90% confirms the representative nature of the study population. Previous studies have reported that certain palmitoylation enzymes and substrate proteins participate in the DNA damage response (DDR) and chromatin regulation67. Palmitoylation may also indirectly disrupt genomic stability by affecting the localization and degradation of DNA repair proteins59. Therefore, we hypothesize that aberrant expression of palmitoylation-related genes involved in the risk model may promote TP53 mutation accumulation or exacerbate oncogenic processes following TP53 loss by altering DDR and chromatin states. However, this proposed link between palmitoylation-related gene activity and genomic instability remains to be verified through dedicated experimental models.

Several limitations of this study must be acknowledged. First, significant histological heterogeneity exists across the datasets. The discovery cohort (GSE26712) represents a mixture of subtypes with incomplete annotations, whereas the training cohort (TCGA-OV) is predominantly High-Grade Serous Ovarian Cancer (HGSOC). Given that HGSOC and other subtypes, such as endometrioid adenocarcinoma, exhibit vastly different genomic profiles (e.g., TP53 prevalence) and clinical trajectories, identifying DEGs in mixed-subtype cohorts may introduce subtype-specific noise rather than universal drivers. Second, the immune and drug sensitivity analyses are primarily exploratory and computational. The CIBERSORT algorithm provides estimations sensitive to data quality, and the weak correlation (∣cor∣< 0.3 ) between prognostic genes and immune cells suggests an indirect modulation of the microenvironment. Furthermore, the TIDE scoring system lacks integration of critical factors like tumor mutational burden (TMB) or microsatellite stability. Without retrospective clinical outcomes from patients treated with ICIs or in vivo verification, these drug sensitivity profiles remain hypothesis-generating. Third, the experimental verification cohort is constrained by a small sample size (n = 5) and high histological diversity (only two HGSOC samples), which introduces sampling bias and limits generalizability. The use of adjacent tissues as controls may also be influenced by the tumor field effect. Finally, the primary limitation of this study is the lack of extensive functional characterization and protein-level validation. Although RT-qPCR results supported our bioinformatic findings, the mechanistic link between these genes and the dynamic palmitoylation process requires further biochemical assays, such as Acyl-Biotin Exchange (ABE) to confirm their palmitoylation status. Since the relationship between mRNA expression, protein abundance, and post-translational modification is complex and non-linear, this transcriptomic model serves as an exploratory tool and a platform equivalency check rather than a definitive biochemical validation. Future research involving large-scale, histologically uniform cohorts and multi-omics validation is essential to substantiate these preliminary findings.

Conclusion

This study systematically elucidated the prognostic characteristics of the palmitoylation-related functional landscape in ovarian cancer and successfully constructed a four-gene signature consisting of HSPG2, BRD4, RARRES1, and SCGB1D2. The signature effectively distinguishes patient risk subgroups and highlights potential pathways through which PRGs may contribute to tumor progression, such as the dysregulation of protein translation and immune remodeling.

Future research will focus on the following directions to strengthen these findings: (1) Expanding the validation to a larger clinical cohort (at least n=30n = 30) including both OC tissues and strictly defined normal ovarian epithelial tissues, while integrating comprehensive follow-up data to evaluate the association between gene expression and long-term prognosis (PFS and OS); (2) Verifying the expression patterns at the protein level using Western blot and Immunohistochemistry (IHC) in both clinical samples and multiple OC cell lines (e.g., SKOV3, OVCAR3) to assess the correlation between protein abundance and clinicopathological features; (3) Utilizing the ABE assay to directly detect the palmitoylation status of HSPG2, BRD4, RARRES1, and SCGB1D2, clarifying whether their functional roles are directly regulated by this lipid modification.

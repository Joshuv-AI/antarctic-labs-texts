Splitting algorithm and normed convergence for drawing the random Loewner curves
Jiaming Chen and Vlad Margarint

Abstract
Recent advances in Schramm–Loewner evolution have driven increasing interest in non-standard Loewner flows. In this work, we propose a novel splitting algorithm to simulate random Loewner curves with rigorous convergence analysis in sup-norm and L p L^{p} . The algorithm is further extended to explore fractional SLE, driven by fractional Brownian motion, and noise-reinforced SLE, incorporating the effect on long-term memory. These exploratory and numerical extensions enable theoretical predictions on fractal dimensions and other statistical phenomena, providing new insights into such dynamics and opening directions for future research.

Motivated by the study of Charles Loewner

for the Bieberbach conjecture
[

[

[

]
to SLE(4) and uniform spanning tree
[

79
]

is simple when

]
when
4<\kappa<8

The development of SLE has thus made it desirable to simulate such random curves for better understanding their behaviors. As a first order stochastic ODE, the Loewner equation is naturally approximated by Euler’s method, see illustrations e.g. by Vincent Beffara
[
8
]
. Such approximation is admittedly good but it does not show the generated Loewner curve rather than a neighborhood of it. A second method for drawing SLE was suggested by Marshall/Rohde
[
65
]
via the classic zipper algorithm
[
50
]
. Given discretization of the driving force, the zipper algorithm builds conformal map that takes those discrete points to the trace of a Jordan curve, and whence simulates the Loewner flow
[
45
]
. Its Carathéodory convergence
[
52
]
and Cauchy transform of marginal measures
[
6
]

\kappa\leq 4
simple phase. The sup-norm convergence of zipper algorithm was not known until
[
91
]
. Nonetheless, although well approximating SLE, the curves drawn from this algorithm are piecewise smooth
[
46
]
and simple, leading inevitably to skepticism
[
91
, p. 602]
at their first appearance.

In this work, we propose and study the splitting algorithm via the Ninomiya–Victoir scheme for drawing SLE. Inspired by rough path theory
[
38
]
, which views SDEs as functions that map Brownian motion to continuous fractal paths, the splitting algorithm decomposes the Loewner flow into iterated stochastic integrals of the driving force
[
36
]
, obtaining relatively good approximation, see e.g. Figure
1
or
[
35
]
. We rigorously prove the pathwise sup-norm and
L^{p}
convergences of the splitting output to SLE, guaranteeing the effectiveness of our algorithm. On the other hand, due to the iterated integral nature of the splitting algorithm, the exact rate of convergence should relate to the Lévy area
[
25
]
which is difficult and is beyond the scope of this paper. Furthermore, the splitting algorithm has also been manifested in various models such as the stochastic Landau–Lifshitz equation
[
1
]
, diffusion processes with Lipschitz drift
[
16
]
and the stochastic oscillatory dynamics
[
17
]
with hypoelliptic steps
[
76
]
and is geometrically ergodic
[
66
]
.

To this recognition, we apply the splitting algorithm to non-standard Loewner evolutions which are much more general than SLE, and make observations on the statistical phenomena that are still open and very difficult to solve. Such notion of non-standard Loewner evolution has appeared on several fronts: Smooth criteria for traces driven by finite variation process are given in
[
86
]
; Existence of continuous fractal curves is later extended to semimartingale driving force
[
64
]
and further to complex-valued driving force
[
59
]
with the aid of Itô’s formula; When the Loewner flow is driven by Lévy processes, càdlàg traces were discussed in
[
74
]
with careful derivative estimates. Replacing standard Brownian motion by variant driving forces yields a very complicated multifractal structure: The traces can become highly fractal and they can be either tree-like, forest-like or looptree-like. The conformal invariance of SLE, which was vital to Brownian intersection exponents
[
31

and conformal field theory
[

Beyond the above cádlág semimartingale driving functions, some recent advances on non-semimartingale driven SLE have made striking observations on both numerical and theoretical aspects. Motivated and borrowed techniques from SLE driven by Lévy flights

Apart from the model of fractional SLE, we look ahead to formulate an interplay between non-standard SLE and stochastic reinforcement. This fascinating mechanism allows processes to have long-term memory and that their future dynamics is strongly affected by their past trajectories. The edge-reinforced random walk
[
67
]
, for instance, describes a particle traversing on a graph where it is more likely to repeat the edges which has been visited before. Whether this particle is attracted to a single edge depends on the exact reinforcement strength
[
57
]
. Recent results have pointed out that such reinforced random walks can be decomposed into a mixture of the continuous-time vertex-reinforced jump process
[
83
]
where the particle jumps according to an exponential law with reinforced weights in time. Novel variant models have also been proposed such as the trace-reinforced ants process
[
48

where ants embark on a journey to search food from their nest. It has been shown that these ants will not find the shortest path
[
49
]
with some still left-open questions. See also
[
75
]
for a great survey on various other reinforced models with self-interaction. Such reinforcement provides a new angle where stochastic processes undergo distortions by their memories of the past. Such memory need not be only on their past trajectories in state spaces, but could also be less tangible such as the while noise in the past
[
10
,
12
]
.

In this direction, Jean Bertoin
[
11
]
innovatively designed the so called noise-reinforced Brownian motion whose fluctuation is repeated in time and corresponds to the scaling limit of some step-reinforced random walks
[
9
]
. This type of memory introduces a novel variant of the standard Brownian motion. And there has been no discussion, to the best of our knowledge, on the non-standard SLE driven by such reinforced Brownian motion with memory. This is a natural theater where the splitting algorithm comes into play. The first question to ask is whether the induced Loewner evolution inherits, in some sense, such long-term memory from the noise-reinforced Brownian motion. Via applying the splitting algorithm, we observe in Figure
2
that the traces of such noise-reinforced SLE do have the tendency to repeat their previous twists compared to the standard SLE. The nontrivial drift term in the Itô semimartingale decomposition of the noise-reinforced Brownian motion breaks down the classic martingale approach, making rigorous arguments hard to proceed. Nevertheless, SLE based methods such as the splitting algorithm are suitable candidates for drawing the noise-reinforced SLE which is again semimartingale with respect to Brownian motion. And with the aid of effective numerical methods e.g. the splitting algorithm, we will know what exactly to expect from the numerical observations of the geometry of such noise-reinforced SLE.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the mechanism of the splitting algorithm and how it applies to the Loewner SDE, following a brief review of the general SLE theory. We then discuss how such splitting algorithm can be extended to some non-standard Loewner evolutions such as the noise-reinforced SLE and fractional SLE. In Section
3
we demonstrate that such splitting algorithm is effective in the context of SLE by showing its sup-norm and
L^{p}
convergence to the random Loewner curves. In practical simulations, we discretize the driving forces and approximate them via polynomial interpolation. This auxiliary tool is combined with the splitting algorithm to ease computation. And we thus include the convergence analysis of arbitrary
p

4

Significant statistical and geometric phenomena are investigated with discussions on the fractal dimensions, shedding light on the theory of stochastic reinforcement and fractional stochastic processes. Future perspectives on the quantitative and exact rate of algorithm convergence is also mentioned, pointing to new research directions, concluding this paper.

2
Ninomiya–Victoir splitting algorithm to Schramm–Loewner evolution

In the section, we review briefly the well-studied SLE and introduce an SDE-based stochastic algorithm to simulate the geometry of this random evolution. Besides verifying its convergence, we also point out that this new algorithm could be applied to non-standard SLE, distinguishing itself from other frequently used numerical schemes.

2.1  Random Loewner curves from Schramm–Loewner evolution.

Let
\mathbb{H}\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\,\imaginary\penalty\ z>0\}
be the upper half-plane and
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
its closure in
\mathbb{C}
. We say a set
K\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{H}}
is a hull if
K
is compact and
\mathbb{H}\backslash K
is simply connected in
\mathbb{C}
. For any such hull
K
, we can find a unique conformal onto map
g_{K}:\mathbb{H}\backslash K\to\mathbb{H}
such that

g_{K}(z)=z+a_{1}\frac{1}{z}+a_{2}\frac{1}{z^{2}}+\cdots\qquad\text{near}\quad\infty,

yielding the hydrodynamic limit
z-g_{K}(z)\to 0
as
\absolutevalue{z}\to\infty
. One should remark
[
44
, Lemma 4.2]
that
a_{1}(K)
is real non-negative and vanishes only if
g_{K}=Id
on
\mathbb{H}
.

When there is a growing family
(K_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
of hulls, we say this family has continuous local growth if the radius of
g_{t}(K_{t+s}\backslash K_{t})
tends to
0
as
s\to 0+
uniformly in
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. In this scenario, we conveniently reparametrize
(K_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
so that
a_{1}(K_{t})=2t
for all
t
. And it is thus convenient to write
g_{t}\coloneqq g_{K_{t}}
on
\mathbb{H}\backslash K_{t}
. When
(K_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
grows continuously, there exists a real-valued continuous
(\lambda_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
satisfying the forward Loewner equation

\partial_{t}g_{t}(z)=\frac{2}{g_{t}(z)-\lambda_{t}},\qquad\forall\penalty\ z\in\mathbb{H}\backslash K_{t},

(2.1)

with
g_{0}=Id
on
\mathbb{H}
. In fact, there is a one-to-one correspondence
[
44
, Theorem 4.2]
between the local growth and the continuous
(\lambda_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
. To this recognition, the process
(\lambda_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
essentially determines the evolution
(g_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
as well as the hulls
(K_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
. And hence we call
(\lambda_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
the driving force of
(g_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
.

Motivated by the study of theoretical physics, SLE was created to describe scaling limit of interface models, where one takes into account the randomness of
(\lambda_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
. And the description of such behavior requires a probability space
(\Omega,\mathbb{P})
with filtration
(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
large enough to support a standard Brownian motion
(B_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
. Viewed as a stochastic process on
(\Omega,\mathbb{P})
,
(\lambda_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is assumed to be
(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
-adapted and is often called the driving process.

For certain driving processes
(\lambda_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
, the hulls
(K_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
are generated by a curve
\gamma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\overline{\mathbb{H}}
, i.e.
\mathbb{H}\backslash K_{t}
is the unbounded component of
\mathbb{H}\backslash\gamma[0,t]
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. And for all such deterministic or random evolutions
(g_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
, the curve
\gamma
can be traced by
\gamma(t)=\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}g^{-1}_{t}(\lambda_{t}+i\epsilon)
. And we call this
\gamma
the random Loewner curve. It turns out that
[
84
]
only when
\lambda_{t}=\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\kappa\geq 0
, the Loewner curve
\gamma
exists and uniquely satisfies the conformal invariance and domain Markov property.

The regularity of
\gamma
is nevertheless complicated. First,
\gamma
is fractal when it is generated by
(\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
. Second, under the same driving process this Loewner curve is simple
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. when
0\leq\kappa\leq 4
. And
\cup_{t\geq 0}K_{t}=\mathbb{H}
but
\gamma
avoids almost all
z\in\mathbb{H}
when
4<\kappa<8
, see
[
54
,
79
]
. In contrast, the Loewner curve fills
\mathbb{H}
when
\gamma\geq 8
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

The three phases of
\gamma
are then respectively named the simple, the swallowing and the space-filling phase. Depending on the value of
\kappa
, it is hard to draw precisely the trace of the Loewner curve due to its phase transition and fractality. In this paper, we propose and verify that the splitting algorithm is an effective method for such simulation. To this end, one needs to define the reverse Loewner equation

\partial_{t}h_{t}(z)=\frac{-2}{h_{t}(z)-\sqrt{\kappa}(B_{T-t}-B_{T})},\qquad\forall\penalty\ z\in\mathbb{H},

(2.2)

up to time horizon
T>0
. This Loewner evolution
(h_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, running in reverse time direction, generates a curve
\eta:[0,T]\to\overline{\mathbb{H}}
. In fact, it is shown
[
80
]
that
\eta
corresponds to
\gamma
in the way that they have the same distribution on
[0,T]
modulo real scalar shift
\sqrt{\kappa}B_{T}
. And thus we call
\eta
the shifted Loewner curve which preserves statistical properties of
\gamma
and is the main object of our further discussion.

2.2  Description of the Ninomiya–Victoir splitting algorithm.

Before drawing the random Loewner curve
\eta
, we first rephrase the evolutions
(g_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
and
(h_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
in convenient fashions. Setting
\tilde{g}_{t}(z)=g_{t}(z)-\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t}
for all
z\in\mathbb{H}\backslash K_{t}
and

d\tilde{g}_{t}(z)=\frac{2}{\tilde{g}_{t}(z)}\,dt-\sqrt{\kappa}\,dB_{t},\qquad\forall\penalty\ z\in\mathbb{H}\backslash K_{t}

(2.3)

with
\tilde{g}_{0}=Id
on

Z_{t}(z)=h_{t}(z)-\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t}
for all

and

dZ_{t}=-\frac{2}{Z_{t}}\,dt+\sqrt{\kappa}\,dB_{t},\qquad\text{with}\quad Z_{0}=iy,

(2.4)

for some sufficiently small initial value

Definition 2.1
.

Stochastic Splitting Algorithm.

Consider the complex-valued SDE with the form

dW_{t}=L_{0}(W_{t})\,dt+L_{1}(W_{t})\,dB_{t},\qquad W_{0}=\xi,

with
\xi\in\mathbb{C}
and smooth vector fields
L_{1},L_{2}
on
\mathbb{C}
. We also use
\exp(tL_i)z_{0}
to denote the unique solution to the ODE flow
\dot{z}_{t}=tL_{i}(z_{t})
starting from

at time

, for any

. At each
N
th step, we choose a uniform partition
\mathcal{D}_{N}\coloneqq\{t_{0}=0,t_{1},\ldots,t_{N}=T\}
with mesh size
h_{k}\coloneqq t_{k+1}-t_{k}
for all
k<N
. The size of the mesh
\absolutevalue{h_k}
is free to choose, but formally
\mathcal{D}_{N}
gets finer as the step
N

We whence numerically approximate the SDE with the discrete points
(\widetilde{W}_{t_{k}})_{0\leq k\leq N}
by
\widetilde{W}_{t_{0}}=\xi
and

\widetilde{W}_{t_{k+1}}=\exp\big(\tfrac{1}{2}h_kL_0\big)\exp\big(B_{t_k,t_k+h_k}L_1\big)\exp\big(\tfrac{1}{2}h_kL_0\big)\widetilde{W}_{t_{k}},\qquad\forall\penalty\ k=0,\ldots,N-1,

with

in standard notation
[
61

. In fact, from the discrete

\widetilde{W}_{t}=\xi+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}L_{0}(\widetilde{W}^{(0)}_{s})\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}L_{1}(\widetilde{W}^{(1)}_{s})\,dB_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}L_{0}(\widetilde{W}^{(2)}_{s})\,ds,\qquad\forall\penalty\ t\in[0,T],

where on each mesh interval

\widetilde{W}^{(0)}_{t}\coloneqq\exp\big(\tfrac{1}{2}(t-t_k)L_0\big)\widetilde{W}_{t_{k}},\quad\widetilde{W}^{(1)}_{t}\coloneqq\exp\big(B_{t_k,t_k+h_k}L_1\big)\widetilde{W}^{(0)}_{t_{k+1}},\quad\widetilde{W}^{(2)}_{t}\coloneqq\exp\big(\tfrac{1}{2}(t-t_k)L_0\big)\widetilde{W}^{(1)}_{t_{k+1}}.

In light of the same distribution between
\gamma
and
\eta
, it suffices to draw the shifted Loewner curve

from the SDE (
2.4
) via the splitting algorithm described in Definition

L_{0}(z)=-2/z
and
L_{1}(z)=\sqrt{\kappa}
for all

. And to clarify notation, we use

for the neighboring points of

\absolutevalue{\mathcal{D}_N}

and
\lVert\dotproduct\rVert_{T}
for the sup-norm on the interval

When applying the splitting algorithm to SLE, at each

Theorem 2.2

Let

\mathbb{P}\big(\big\lVert\eta-\widetilde{Z}(iy_{N})\big\rVert_{T}\leq\varphi_{1}(N)\big)\geq 1-\varphi_{2}(N),\qquad\forall\penalty\ N\geq 1,

Before going through the proof of main results, let us diverge to some insights on the extension of this splitting algorithm to non-standard Loewner evolutions, with applications to their statistical mechanics phenomena, such as the fractal dimension of their associated random Loewner curves.

2.3  Non-standard Loewner chains driven by semimartingales.

Heuristically, the random vibration of Loewner curves inherits from the randomness of their driving forces. By the wide applicability such as the random dendritic growth at off-critical phase
[
42
]
and the diffusion-limited aggregation
[
68
]
, one is motivated to consider more general driving forces than the ubiquitous Brownian motion. Lévy process, e.g., is shown
[
74
]
to drive locally connected
(\partial\mathbb{H}\backslash K_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
. In
[
64
]
, it was shown that certain continuous semimartingales draw continuous curves.

In particular, the
noise-reinforced Brownian motion

[
11
]
extends
(B_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
in semimartingale form and relates the notion of stochastic reinforcement. This model allows noise to repeat itself infinitesimally, thus creating strengthened fluctuations as time flows. Readers are also referred to
[
12
]
for exposition on its Bessel norm process and to
[
81
]
on the similarly reinforced Lévy process.

Concerning the mathématique, we start with the noise-reinforced Brownian motion
(B^{p}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
defined by

B^{p}_{t}\coloneqq t^{p}\int_{0}^{t}s^{-p}\,dB_{s},\qquad\text{with covariance}\quad\mathbb{E}[B^{p}_{s}B^{p}_{t}]=(1-2p)^{-1}s^{1-p}t^{p},\qquad\forall\penalty\ 0\leq s\leq t,

where
-\infty<p<1/2
is called the reinforcement strength. Notice that
[
11
, Eqn. (3)]

B^{p}_{t}
has the same distribution as
(1-2p)^{-1/2}t^{p}B_{t^{1-2p}}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and solves the SDE
dB^{p}_{t}=pt^{-1}B^{p}_{t}\,dt+\,dB_{t}
,
B^{p}_{0}=B_{0}=0
, but does not satisfy the time-reversal invariance , unlike standard Brownian motion. The novelty here is to conceive the noise-reinforced SLE driven by
(B^{p}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{P}_{+}}
in the fashion

dZ_{t}=-\frac{2}{Z_{t}}\,dt+\sqrt{\kappa}\,dB^{p}_{t}=\big(\sqrt{\kappa}t^{-1}pB^{p}_{t}-\frac{2}{Z_{t}}\big)\,dt+\sqrt{\kappa}\,dB_{t},\qquad\text{with}\quad Z_{0}\in\mathbb{H},

(2.5)

modified from (
2.4
). Given that the reinforced mechanism introduces long-term memory on
(B^{p}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
, what will be the distortion effect on its driven Loewner curve? The geometry of which is known very little to us. It is our belief that the splitting algorithm should numerically predict statistical phenomena which leads to deeper exploration.

Now, to initial the splitting algorithm for the noise-reinforced SLE, instead of replacing by the time-inhomogeneous field
L_{0}^{p}:z\in\mathbb{H}\mapsto\sqrt{\kappa}pB^{p}_{t}/t-2/z
at
t\in[0,T]
, we choose to substitute the stochastic differential
dB_{t}
with
dB^{p}_{t}
and whence preserves the time-homogeneous field
L_{0}:z\mapsto-2/z
. Then with a suitable
y_{N}\to 0+
, the splitting algorithm produces the discrete points
(\widetilde{W}^{p}_{t_{k}})_{0\leq k\leq N}
according to

\widetilde{W}^{p}_{t_{k+1}}=\exp\big(\tfrac{1}{2}h_kL_0\big)\exp\big(B^p_{t_k,t_k+h_k}L_1\big)\exp\big(\tfrac{1}{2}h_kL_0\big)\widetilde{W}^{p}_{t_{k}},\qquad\forall\penalty\ k=0,\ldots,N-1,

which subsequently stretches to a continuous approximation
(\widetilde{W}^{p}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
written out in integral form

\widetilde{W}^{p}_{t}=\xi+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}L_{0}(\widetilde{W}^{(0)}_{p,s})\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}L_{1}(\widetilde{W}^{(1)}_{p,s})\,dB_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}L_{0}(\widetilde{W}^{(2)}_{p,s})\,ds,\qquad\forall\penalty\ t\in[0,T],

where the increments
\widetilde{W}^{(0)}_{p,t}
is defined similarly as
\widetilde{W}^{(0)}_{t}
but with respect to
B^{p}_{t}
in Definition
2.1
, and similarly for
\widetilde{W}^{(1)}_{p,t}
and
\widetilde{W}^{(2)}_{p,t}
on
t\in[0,T]
. Simulations of the noise-reinforced SLE via splitting algorithm can be found in Section
4
, where we shall also discuss their numerical consequences.

Despite the rich but mostly unknown geometric features of the Loewner chains driven by semimartingale in the form (
2.5
), these processes are not scale-invariant in general. Recent striking advances into the realm of non-semimartingale driving forces, e.g.
[
90
]
, has revealed the perseverance of scale-invariance with properly modified deterministic forces. One illustrative example is the
fractional Brownian motion
. Denoted by
(B^{H}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
, the fractional Brownian motion is written out in fractional integral
[
78
]
for any
0\leq s\leq t
,

B^{H}_{t}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\Gamma(H+1/2)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{H-1/2}\,dB_{s},\qquad\text{with covariance}\quad\mathbb{E}[B^{H}_{s}B^{H}_{t}]=\big(\absolutevalue{s}^{2H}+\absolutevalue{t}^{2H}-\absolutevalue{t-s}^{2H}\big),

with Hurst exponent
0<H<1
and is revealed by Mandelbrot and van Ness
[
63
]
to have stationary increments.

We also mention that
(B^{H}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is neither a Markov process nor a semimartingale
[
88
]
, but significantly it admits time-reversal symmetry
[
28
]
, i.e.
B^{H}_{T-t}-B^{H}_{T}
is distributed identically as
B^{H}_{t}
for all
t\in[0,T]
. In light of this, we are able to modify (
2.2
) for the fractional SLE characterized by

\partial_{t}h_{t}(z)=|h_{t}(z)-\kappa^{H}B^{H}_{t}|^{2-\frac{1}{H}}\dotproduct\frac{-2}{h_{t}(z)-\kappa^{H}(B^{H}_{T-t}-B^{H}_{T})},\qquad\forall\penalty\ z\in\mathbb{H},

up to time horizon
T>0
. This form was first proposed in
[
90
, Eqn. (14)]
which novelly takes into consideration the scale-invariance of
(h_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
. Observe also that this expression can be equivalently written into the Loewner flow driven by
(B^{H}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
as

dZ_{t}=|Z_{t}|^{2-\frac{1}{H}}\dotproduct\frac{-2}{Z_{t}}\,dt+\kappa^{H}\,dB^{H}_{t},\qquad\text{with}\quad Z_{0}\in\mathbb{H}.

(2.6)

Like the noise-reinforced SLE, the theoretical aspects of the fractional SLE remain poorly understood. Fortunately, the pioneering work of
[
90
]
has simulated these Loewner traces, observing their self-similarity, fractal dimension via a yardstick method
[
95
]
, and how they are influenced by the values of
H
and
\kappa
.

In this work, we confirm their numerical observations via the alternative splitting method in Definition

. With properly selected

, we get a set of discrete points

by

\widetilde{W}^{H}_{t_{k+1}}=\exp\big(\tfrac{1}{2}h_kL_0\big)\exp\big(B^H_{t_k,t_k+h_k}L_1\big)\exp\big(\tfrac{1}{2}h_kL_0\big)\widetilde{W}^{H}_{t_{k}},\qquad\forall\penalty\ k=0,\ldots,N-1,

and

Here the increments

3.1  Local sup-norm convergence in probability.

Proposition 3.2

∎

On

(3.3)

Inspecting Definition

Proposition 3.3
.

If further
\absolutevalue{\mathcal{D}_N}\leq(4N+1)^{-3}
and if we write the perturbation event

\mathcal{B}^{(3)}_{N}\coloneqq\big\{\big\lVert\sum_{k<N}\big(Z_{t_{k}}(iy_{N})-\widetilde{Z}_{t_{k}}(iy_{N})\big)\mathbbm{1}_{[t_{k},t_{k}+h_{k})}\big\rVert_{T}\leq(4N+1)^{-1/2}\big\},

for some constants

Define the càdlàg driving force

on

\sqrt{\kappa}B[0,T]
,
(\tilde{\lambda}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
initiates the Loewner flow

\widetilde{Z}^{\dagger}_{t}(iy_{N})=\tilde{h}_{t}(iy_{N})-\tilde{\lambda}_{t},\qquad\text{with}\quad\partial_{t}\tilde{h}_{t}(z)=\frac{-2}{\tilde{h}_{t}(z)-(\tilde{\lambda}_{T-t}-\tilde{\lambda}_{T})},\qquad\forall\penalty\ t\in[0,T].

(3.4)

The above observation compels us to estimate

\big|Z_{t}(iy_{N})-\widetilde{Z}^{\dagger}_{t}(iy_{N})\big|\leq\big|h_{t}(iy_{N})-\tilde{h}_{t}(iy_{N})\big|+\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big|\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t}-\tilde{\lambda}_{t}\big|.

(3.5)

To this end, we write
H_{t}\coloneqq h_{t}(iy_{N})-\tilde{h}_{t}(iy_{N})
for all

\frac{d}{dt}H_{t}-H_{t}\zeta(t)=\big(\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t}-(\tilde{\lambda}_{T-t}-\tilde{\lambda}_{T})\big)\zeta(t),\quad\zeta(t)\coloneqq\big(h_{t}(iy_{N})-\sqrt{\kappa}(B_{T-t}-B_{T})\big)^{-1}\big(\tilde{h}_{t}(iy_{N})-(\tilde{\lambda}_{T-t}-\tilde{\lambda}_{T})\big)^{-1}.

H_{t}u(t)=H_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}(\sqrt{\kappa}B_{T-s}-\tilde{\lambda}_{T-s}+\tilde{\lambda}_{T}-\sqrt{\kappa}B_{T})u(s)\zeta(s)\,ds,\qquad\text{with}\quad u(t)\coloneqq e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\zeta(s)\,ds},\qquad H_{0}=0.

Henceforth, we obtain

\displaystyle\big|h_{t}(iy_{N})-\tilde{h}_{t}(iy_{N})\big|\leq\int_{0}^{t}|\sqrt{\kappa}B_{T-s}-\tilde{\lambda}_{T-s}+\tilde{\lambda}_{T}-\sqrt{\kappa}B_{T}|e^{\int_{s}^{t}\real\zeta(\tau)\,d\tau}|\zeta(s)|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq T}|\sqrt{\kappa}B_{\tau}-\tilde{\lambda}_{\tau}|\int_{0}^{t}e^{\int_{s}^{t}\real\zeta(r)\,dr}|\zeta(s)|\,ds\leq\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq T}|\sqrt{\kappa}B_{\tau}-\tilde{\lambda}_{\tau}|\big(e^{\int_{s}^{t}|\zeta(r)|\,dr}-1\big),

where the last inequality is due to
[

. Furthermore,
[

, Eqn. (2.12)]
yields

is less than or equal to

\big|Z_{t}(iy_{N})-\widetilde{Z}^{\dagger}_{t}(iy_{N})\big|\leq N^{1/2}(4+y_{N}^{2})^{1/2}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t}-\tilde{\lambda}_{t}|\leq(4N+1)^{1/2}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t}-\tilde{\lambda}_{t}|.

(3.6)

Now notice that

is the cumulant function of a standard Gaussian. Via reflection principle,

\mathbb{P}\big(\lVert\sqrt{\kappa}B\rVert_{h_{k}}\geq(4N+1)^{-1}\big)\leq 2\mathbb{P}\big(S_{h_{k}}\geq\kappa^{-1/2}(4N+1)^{-1}\big)\leq 2(2/\pi)^{1/2}\exp\big( -\tfrac{1}{2\kappa}h_k^{-1}(4N+1)^{-2} \big).

Now that we have chosen
\absolutevalue{\mathcal{D}_N}\leq(4N+1)^{-3}
, then on the event
\{\lVert\sqrt{\kappa}B-\tilde{\lambda}\rVert_{T}\geq(4N+1)^{-1}\}

\mathbb{P}\big(\lVert\sqrt{\kappa}B-\tilde{\lambda}\rVert_{T}\geq(4N+1)^{-1}\big)\leq\sum_{k<N}\mathbb{P}\big(\lVert\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t_{k}+\dotproduct}\rVert_{h_{k}/2}\geq(4N+1)^{-1}\big)\leq 2T(4N+1)^{3}e^{-(4N+1)/2\kappa}.

3.6

Following the Definition
2.1
in the context of SLE, at each

for any

and

. Written out in integral form, we get

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}_{t}(iy_{N})-\widetilde{Z}_{t_{k}(t)}(iy_{N})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{k}(t)}^{t}L_{0}(\widetilde{Z}_{s}^{(2)})\,ds+\int_{t_{k}(t)}^{t}L_{1}(\widetilde{Z}_{s}^{(1)})\,dB_{s}+\ frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{k}(t)}^{t}L_{0}(\widetilde{Z}_{s}^{(0)})\,ds,

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\sqrt{\kappa}\,dB_{s}-\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\big((\widetilde{Z}_{t_{k}})^{2}-2(s-t_{k})\big)^{-1/2}\,ds-\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\big(\big(\widetilde{Z}_{t_{k+1}}^{(1)}\big)^{2}-2(s-t_{k})\big)^{-1/2}\,ds,\qquad\forall\penalty\ t\in[0,T],

\imaginary z\leq\imaginary(z^{2}-A)^{1/2}

Proposition 3.5

Proposition 3.8
.

If
\absolutevalue{\mathcal{D}_N}\leq N^{-1}

\tilde{\psi}^{(4)}_{N}\to 0+
as

From Proposition

we know
|\widetilde{Z}_{t_{k}}-\widetilde{Z}_{t}|^{p}\leq 2|\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t_{k},t}|^{p}+2^{p+1}N^{-p/2}
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. Since
p\geq 2
, there exists
\ell\geq 1
such that
2\ell\leq p<2(\ell+1)
. Notice also that
t-t_{k}\leq h_{k}\leq 1/N
for each

. Then,

\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{k<N}\lVert\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t_{k}+\dotproduct}\rVert_{h_{k}}^{2\ell}\big]\leq\pi^{-1/2}(2\kappa/N)^{\ell}\Gamma(\ell+1/2),\qquad\forall\penalty\ \ell\geq 1.

Hence in light of the Lebesgue interpolation relation, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{k<N}\lVert\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t_{k}+\dotproduct}\rVert^{p}_{h_{k}}\big]\leq\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{k<N}\lVert\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t_{k}+\dotproduct}\rVert^{2\ell}_{h_{k}}\big]^{\ell+1-p/2}\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{k<N}\lVert\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t_{k}+\dotproduct}\rVert^{2(\ell+1)}_{h_{k}}\big]^{\ell(\ell+1-p/2)/(\ell+1)}

\displaystyle\leq CN^{-\ell(\ell+1-p/2)}\coloneqq\tilde{\psi}^{(4)}_{N}\to 0.

And the assertion then follows.

3.6
,

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{t_{k}}(iy_{N})-\widetilde{Z}_{t_{k}}(iy_{N})|^{p}\,dt\dotproduct I_{\mathcal{A}_{N}^{(3)}}\big]^{1/p}+\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{0}^{T}|\widetilde{Z}_{t_{k}}(iy_{N})-\widetilde{Z}_{t}(iy_{N})|^{p}\,dt\big]^{1/p}\leq\psi_{1}(N)^{1/p}\to 0,

where

3.3  Alternative power-law interpolation of driving forces.

The simulation of SLE via splitting algorithm often requires a pathwise realization of the driving process
(\lambda_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
. Practically one discretizes the time interval
[0,T]
and performs interpolation to the traces of
(\lambda_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
. It is then theoretically necessary to show such interpolation is valid, which is the aim of this section.

Inspired by
[
91
]
where the square-root interpolation was shown effective, we generalize this method to arbitrary power-law interpolation of
(\lambda_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, including the linear one where the Loewner flow can be computed explicitly
[
43
]
. That said, we only briefly outline the proof and omit some technical details due to the overlap content with the square-root interpolation in
[
91
]
.

Given any
p>0
, to implement the
p
th power interpolation pathwise to the driving force
(\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
in SLE at each
N
th step, we use

\lambda^{(N)}_{p,t}\coloneqq N^{p}(t-t_{k})^{p}\sqrt{\kappa}(B_{t_{k}+h_{k}}-B_{t_{k}})+\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t_{k}},\qquad\forall\penalty\ t_{k}\leq t<t_{k}+h_{k}.

(3.8)

Applying this
(\lambda^{(N)}_{p,t})_{t\in[0,T]}
to the forward Loewner equation (
2.1
), we produce a forward Loewner chain
(g^{(N)}_{p,t})_{t\in[0,T]}
which is generated by the Loewner curve
\gamma_{p,N}:[0,T]\to\overline{\mathbb{H}}
. As
N
advances, we get the following convergence assertion.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\gamma[0,T]
be the forward Loewner curve for
\kappa\neq 8
. If the mesh size
\absolutevalue{\mathcal{D}_N}\to 0+
with
N\to\infty
at a rate
\absolutevalue{\mathcal{D}_N}=o(N^{-3})
, then

\mathbb{P}\big(\big\lVert\gamma-\gamma_{p,N}\big\rVert_{T}\leq\phi_{1}(N)\big)\geq 1-\phi_{2}(N),\qquad\forall\penalty\ N\geq 1,

for some monotonically decreasing
\phi_{i}:\mathbb{N}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
\phi_{i}\to 0+
for each
i=1,2
.

We do not aim to give a detailed proof here, but a shortened outline will be presented below. Let
f_{t}
be the inverse of
g_{t}
and similarly
f^{(N)}_{p,t}
of
g^{(N)}_{p,t}
on
\mathbb{H}
, and let
\hat{f}^{(N)}_{p,t}\coloneqq f^{(N)}_{p,t}(\dotproduct+\lambda^{(N)}_{p,t})
be its shifting, and
\hat{f}_{t}(\dotproduct)
similarly defined. Write
G^{(N)}_{p,k}\coloneqq(\hat{f}^{(N)}_{p,t_{k}})^{-1}\circ\hat{f}^{(N)}_{p,t_{k}+h_{k}}
, then it is clear that
\hat{f}^{(N)}_{p,t_{k}}=G^{(N)}_{p,0}\circ G^{(N)}_{p,1}\circ\cdots\circ G^{(N)}_{p,k-1}
for each
k\leq N
. We also denote
\gamma^{s}_{p,N}:t\in[0,T-s]\mapsto g^{(N)}_{p,s}(\gamma_{p,N}(t+s))
for all
0\leq t\leq T-s
. Hence for any
k<N
,

\big|\gamma(r+t_{k})-\gamma_{p,N}(r+t_{k})\big|\leq\big|\gamma(r+t_{k})-\gamma(s+t_{k})\big|+\big|\hat{f}_{t_{k}}(z)-\hat{f}_{t_{k}}(w)\big|+\big|\hat{f}_{t_{k}}(w)-\hat{f}^{(N)}_{p,t_{k}}(w)\big|,

(3.9)

where
1/N\leq r\leq 2/N
,
w=\gamma^{(N)}_{p,t_{k}}(r)
and
z=\arg\max\{\imaginary\gamma^{(N)}_{p,t_{k}}(s):\,0\leq s\leq 2/N\}
. The first term on the right-hand side is bounded by the uniform continuity of
\gamma[0,T]
on the event
\mathcal{B}^{(1)}_{N}
by (
3.1
).

Now we introduce, for each subpower sequence
(\phi_{N})_{N\geq 1}
, the box

A_{N,\theta,\phi}\coloneqq\big\{z\in\mathbb{H}:\,\absolutevalue{\Re z}\leq N^{-1/2}\phi_{N},\,\phi_{N}^{-1}\leq N^{1/2}\imaginary z\leq\theta\big\}\subseteq\mathbb{H},\qquad\forall\penalty\ \theta>0.

Invoking
[
91
, Lemma 2.6]
, for any
z,z^{\prime}\in A_{N,\theta,\phi}
and conformal map
f
on
\mathbb{H}
, we have
\absolutevalue{\partial_z f(z)}\leq C\phi_{N}^{c}\absolutevalue{\partial_z f(\Im z)}
and
d_{\mathbb{H},\text{hyp}}(z,z^{\prime})\leq C(\log\phi_{N}+1)
for constants
c,C>0
, where
d_{\mathbb{H},\text{hyp}}(\dotproduct)
denotes the hyperbolic distance
[
3
]
on
\mathbb{H}
. In light of
[
77
, Corollary 1.5]
, we further know
\absolutevalue{f(z)-f(z^\prime)}\leq 2\absolutevalue{\partial_z f(z)\Im z}\exp(4d_{\mathbb{H},\text{hyp}}(z,z^\prime))
for any conformal map
f
on
\mathbb{H}
and
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}
. The second term on the right-hand side of (
3.9
) can then be estimated if we suitably adjust the above arguments in the context of SLE, see also
[
91
, Section 3]
.

In this regard, we also invoke
[
93
, Lemma 2.2]
which provides a perturbation estimate to the distance between two reversed Loewner flows provided we know how to compare their respective driving functions. And this yields the desired estimate to the third term on the right-hand side of (
3.9
), verifying the main proposition of this section. In practical running of the splitting algorithm, we combine with the
p
th power interpolation pathwise on the driving traces to ease the numerical computation.

4.1  Splitting drawings of noise-reinforced and fractional Loewner curves.

Arising from studies of two-color urn models
[
5
]
and step-reinforced random walk
[
24
]
, the noise-reinforced Brownian motion
(B^{p}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is characterized by an integral of a reinforced version of white noise. Its time-derivative
dB^{p}/dt
, viewed in the sense of generalized functions
[
33
, Chapter 3]
, has the tendency to repeat itself infinitesimally over time. When the Loewner flow
(Z_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is driven by such reinforced forces, one naturally wonders whether its generated Loewner curve inherits such microscopic property. A classical and important subsequent question is then to understand in what sense the local repetition over small time duration disrupts the macroscopic asymptotic SLE behaviors.

To our best knowledge, none of those questions are rigorously answered so far. And as communicated with Nina Holden, such noise-reinforced version of SLE is likely to possess very different regularities compared to the standard Loewner chains. One instance can be found in Figure
2
where the noise-reinforced SLE(4) is less likely to be self-similar but more likely to replicate its previous distortions on time interval
[0,T]
. Nevertheless, its theoretical derivations can be contrastedly less transparent. The Cardy–Smirnov formula of crossing probability for regular SLE
[
18
,
51
]
, e.g. relies crucially on passing to limits with martingale structure, whereas the semimartingale decomposition of
(B^{p}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
induces a non-trivial drift term, forcing us to conceive novel theoretical techniques to resolve this issue.

Very fortunately, effective numerical methods allow us to perceive, at least with high probability, what significant phenomena is going to emerge. For instance, observed from Figure
2
the noise-reinforced Loewner curve is fractal and impossible to be self-similar, which saves great effort if we want to understand its geometry in this aspect. Meanwhile, with gradually intensified memory, i.e. increasing
p
-value, the noise-reinforced traces look accordingly more twisted, forecasting a possible multiscale phase transition
[
2
,
13
]
with threshold parameters both
p
and
\kappa
. Other interesting statistical measures such as the fractal dimension or the winding angle statistics
[
71
]
can also be numerically quantified based on realizations of the splitting algorithm. In the following, we draw the fractional SLE, which have been proposed in
[
90
]
, and analyse its fractal dimensions.

4.2  On the quantitative analysis of the velocity of algorithm convergence.

Observing from Figure
1
for an underlying drawing of SLE(4) via splitting algorithm, we see that it is a well suited method to simulate analytically tractable field such as SLE. Quoted from Terry Lyons et al.
[
37
]
, this splitting algorithm is the first high order numerical method which preserves second and fourth moments of the reverse Loewner flow, prominently distinguishing itself from previous methods by Kennedy
[
47
]
and Tran
[
91
]
.

Hopefully, a precise local error estimate in Section
3
would yield the exact rate of convergence of our algorithm. Understanding this velocity is important for the resource allocation and cost control
[
30
]
, while a fast splitting algorithm significantly reduces CPU time and memory occupation. We therefore conclude this paper with some new insights on the rate of splitting algorithm convergence to SLE, illuminating the direction of future researches.

In the following we formulate the main ideas of giving the precise convergence rate of the splitting algorithm, respectively in the sup-norm and
L^{p}
topology scenarios. In Proposition
3.1
the
\lVert\dotproduct\rVert_{T}
norm between
\eta_{t}
and
\eta_{t_{k}(t)}
on
[0,T]
is bounded by a subpower function
\phi(N^{1/2})
with
N\geq 1
. One should look to
[
53
,
91
]
for a precise form of
(\phi_{N})_{N\geq 1}
and thus sharpen this assertion. The decreasing sequence
(\epsilon_{N})_{N\geq 1}
which appeared in Proposition
3.2
remains unexplicitly specified. Indeed, one notices that
\epsilon_{N}=\mathbb{P}(y_{N}^{\delta-1}\lVert Z(iy_{N})-\eta\rVert_{T}\geq N^{(1-\delta)/4})
which is characterized purely by the reverse Loewner flow
(h_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
. Consulting the classic SLE literature
[
32
,
55
,
96
]
should then shed light on the exact order of decay of
(\epsilon_{N})_{N\geq 1}
.

The viewpoint of
L^{p}
-norm convergence can be treated analogously. To quantify the rate of convergence relies crucially on estimating the sequences
\hat{\psi}^{(1)}_{N}=1-\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{N})
from Proposition
3.5
and
\hat{\psi}^{(2)}_{N}=1-\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{N})
from Proposition
3.6
. If we take a closer look at those terms, then this is exactly the monotonic sequence
(\epsilon_{N})_{N\geq 1}
whose precise value is to be determined. At this moment we should also point out that the presence of the high probability event
\mathcal{A}_{N}
from Theorem
2.3
might be unnecessary, i.e. it might be replaced with
\Omega
. We do not make any predictions here, but such modification definitely requires a finer inspection on the regularities of the reverse Loewner flow
(h_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, which we shall look at in future projects.

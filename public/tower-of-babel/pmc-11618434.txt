An optimal design for an automatic voltage regulation system using a multivariable PID controller based on hybrid simulated annealing – white shark optimization
Authors: Ahmed K. Ali
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This paper combines the simulated annealing (SA) and white shark optimisation (WSO) algorithms to create a novel hybrid metaheuristic algorithm named SA–WSO. The proposed algorithm finds the best approach to tuning the parameters of three types of regulators. Three types of proportional–integral–derivative (PID) controllers are utilized for an automatic voltage regulator (AVR) system, including fractional–order (FOPID) –controllers. A novel filtered fractional–order PID controller is also presented to enhance the response of the AVR system. The simulation results demonstrate the highly effective optimisation of three controllers, including the rarely used filtrated–FOPID controller, outperforming other controller approaches in the state of the art. The controller performance analyses are also conducted to demonstrate the superiority of SA–WSO-optimized for tuning the controller parameters in different conditions for disturbance rejection and robustness. A comprehensive comparative analysis is performed to determine the most appropriate controller for the AVR system, and it is found that hybrid SA–WSO algorithm provides significant advantages in terms of improved convergence speed.

Introduction

Power-generating units always strive to minimise power losses to improve overall efficiency and performance, a significant issue in control engineering. An effective approach to mitigating these losses is implementation of an optimal design of automatic voltage regulation (AVR) systems.

The controller in AVR systems is critical for minimizing power losses in the generator section. These systems play a crucial role in maintaining the desired voltage levels, thereby optimizing power generation1–3. AVR systems have emerged as a crucial component in power generation units, offering effective voltage regulation and control. The core task of an AVR system is to continuously maintain the output voltages of the generator at the standard level of voltage3,4. A closed-loop configuration of an AVR system consists of five components: regulator, amplifier, exciter, generator, and sensor .

In an AVR system, sensors measure voltage across the generator terminal, and amplifiers amplify control signals after regulator adjustment5. In the same context, the exciter continuously controls the output voltage from the generator terminals to improve power generation efficiency, reliability of generator output voltages. This regulation of generator output voltage ensures the desired level, which can improve system performance by reducing power losses in the generation system4.

Typically, the AVR, implemented using a microprocessor unit, utilises the error signal and the control law to generate control signal3. Thus, the development of new control techniques to enhance the efficiency of AVR systems remains a critical issue in the field of power systems2. This paper discusses the enhancement of AVR system, focusing on recent developments in control techniques and their effectiveness in improving operation efficiency.

Even with the emergence of modern control techniques and control theory, the proportional-integral-derivative (PID) controller remains the most widely employed controller in AVR systems. Based to these parameters, the PID control law combines three control strategies. However, some studies consider extending typical PID control by fractional calculus into the integral and derivative actions. The control law of the fractional-order PID (FOPID) regulator incorporates fractional calculus into integral and derivative, as discussed in references3,6–8, and the control law is defined with five parameters9. Therefore, to achieve the best performance for the FOPID controller, five parameters in the control law equation must be tuned.

Generally, the execution of fractional calculus is more complicated than the execution of integer-order derivatives and integrals. On the other hand, it provides greater flexibility and has the potential to deliver superior performance8. In previous studies, researchers focused mainly on typical PID controllers with three tuning parameters, which, despite their extensive application, often lack the flexibility necessary for intricate system dynamics. Fractional calculus has led to the development of fractional-order PID (FOPID) controllers, which provide flexibility performance using the fractional integration and differentiation.

This paper presents an optimal design of three controllers (PID, FOPID, and F-FOPID) and highlights the importance of tuning the controller to achieve the desired voltage response of the AVR system. In addition, the new optimal design of the filtered-FOPID (FFOPID) regulator is discussed. The genetic algorithm (GA) are popular choices that simulate the process of natural selection by iteratively generating and evaluating candidate solutions to a problem10. The Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) another widely used algorithm inspired by the social behaviour of birds flocking or fish schooling11. It involves a set of particles that move through a search space, updating their position and velocity according to their experiences and the experiences of their neighbours. Other implemented algorithms include the improved kidney-inspired algorithm (IKA), which models the function of a kidney and uses a gradient descent method to optimise the objective functions12. In addition, Symbiotic Organisms Search (SOS) is inspired by the mutualistic relationship between organisms, where two or more species benefit from each other’s existence1. Some nature-inspired optimisation algorithms have been developed based on the behaviour of animals, such as Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA), which mimics the cooperative hunting behaviour of humpbacks13. The brood parasitic behaviour of cuckoo species inspires another algorithm, Cuckoo Search (CS)6. The ant colony optimisation algorithm (ACO) is based on ant colonies’ foraging behaviour, in which ants deposit pheromones to mark the path to a food source5.

The artificial bee colony algorithm (ABC) algorithm is based on honeybees’ foraging behaviour7. These algorithms are based on behaviours of humpback whales, cuckoos, ants, honey-bees, and musicians. The input also mentions use of pheromones, waggle dances, and combination of chaotic maps with ant colony optimisation to improve the convergence rate and accuracy of the algorithms. Among other options is the Biogeography-based optimization (BBO) inspired by the geographic distribution of species and their migration behaviours14. The grasshopper optimization algorithm (GOA)15, which stimulates the swarming behaviour of grasshoppers in neutral. A simulated search algorithm known as Teaching Learning-Based Optimisation (TLBO) inspired the process of teaching student in a classroom16. Combining multiple swarms increases the convergence speed of the minimization problem, which can reach optimal values in less time, such as in Many Optimisation Liaisons (MOL), an algorithm that combines multiple swarm intelligence-based algorithms, which is a simplified version of PSO17.

In the study presented in18, the gravitational search algorithm (GSA), which simulates the behaviour of gravitational force in a physical system, is introduced. The behaviour of salps, free-floating marine animals that swim by contracting and expanding their bodies, serves as the inspiration for Salp Swarm Algorithm (SSA)19. The differential Evolution (DE) algorithm uses differences between candidate solutions to generate new solutions20.

Another search method called the continuous firefly algorithm (CFA) draws inspiration from flashing behaviour of fireflies, where their brightness attracts each other21. Meanwhile, multi-objective extremal optimisation (MOEO) is a method that finds the extreme values of multiple objectives simultaneously8. Recent research in the area of tuning controllers for AVR systems shows a significant tendency towards using advanced optimisation methods22. Ekinci et al.23, developed a hybrid search algorithm with local search capability, specifically for an AVR system and to obtained the optimum parameters of a power System Stabiliser. In24, authors studied a hybrid swarm intelligence-based algorithm for tuning the FOPID controller. In their work, the presented algorithms balanced the exploration and exploitation stages and demonstrated superior performance compared to other benchmark algorithms.

Recently, Izci et al.25 proposed a new modification for the design controller of AVR systems using PID and a second-order derivative. They used an adapted Runge Kutta optimizer to fine-tune the controller parameters, thus improving the stability and robustness of the controller for the AVR system design. Another recently study by Izci et al.26 presented an intelligent master/slave tuning system that integrated the Levy flight mechanism into the Runge-Kutta optimizing algorithm, using neural computing approaches to improve controller effectiveness in the design of AVR systems. for design AVR system.

This tuning controller parameters approach reflects an increasing tendency toward controller system design using optimization methods integrated with advanced artificial intelligence and machine learning approaches. Ekinci et al.27 proposed a modified opposition-based weighted mean of vector (mOBL-INFO) optimization algorithm to effectively find PID controller parameters for PID controllers with different numbers of tuning variables, thus improving the design of AVR systems. Can et al.28 developed a control design approach to enhance the transient response performance of AVR systems, focusing on the crucial need for rapid AVR system stabilization. The method emphasizes the enhancement of the PID controller with three tuning parameters to acquire faster and more stable AVR system performance. Izci et al. (2024)29 presented a combination of Harris Hawks optimization (HHO) algorithms with the SA optimization algorithm to better tune the PID controller for the AVR system. The HHO-SA optimization utilizes nature-inspired algorithms to fine-tune the parameters of different types of PID controllers.

A quadratic wavelet-enhanced gradient-based optimization (QWGBO) algorithm optimized an improved cascaded RPIDD2-FOPI controller by Ekinci et al. (2024)30. This integration enhanced the performance of the controller. This algorithm describes the capability of advanced control systems to improve control performance and efficiency, with additional refinement by the QWGBO algorithm. It appears that the recent literature proposes optimization algorithms that do not adequately address all the shortcomings in the controller design of the AVR systems, especially in terms of computational efficiency, implementation complexity, and performance for disturbance rejection under different conditions. This can raise concerns about their controller performance and adaptability in dynamic conditions.

Over the past five years, methods have been reported, each with a distinct optimisation algorithm specifically designed for AVR applications. Together, a limited number of studies have focused on the development and fine-tuning of filtered FOPID controllers. Lately, researchers have also used various evolutionary algorithms to create PID regulators that improve settling time, overshoot, and rise time. The goal of these studies is to make AVR systems respond better to sudden changes20. In addition to some studies using a more complex control law equation by adding multi-term integration and derivatives, these studies aim to further improve the performance of the AVR, as discussed in13,31. This paper aims to introduce a novel F-FOPID controller with a fractional filter for an AVR system to achieve superior system response compared to the approaches in the literature, including PSO11, TLBO16, BBO14, GWO32, and DE20. FOPID controller

The present work investigates an additional effective use of fractional differential equations (FDEs), the development of an optimum controller for the voltage in a typical synchronous generator by combining FDEs with an optimisation approach based on artificial intelligence. This study aims to create the best possible controller for this task. In order to achieve this purpose, the FOPID controller, proposed by Podlubny in 1999, is employed because of its improved tuning flexibility, robustness, and frequency response relative to the conventional PID controller33.

The proposed approach highlights the potential utility of FDEs in conjunction with advanced optimisation techniques for developing effective control systems in engineering applications. The response of an uncontrolled AVR system, as shown in Fig. 2, demonstrates certain performance matters. The empirical data reveals notable steady-state error, extended settling times, and excessive overshoot. Accordingly, it is imperative to enhance both the steady-state accuracy and the transient response characteristics of the AVR system.

The crucial aim of an AVR system is to provide a stable output despite disturbances or fluctuations in load. The step response analysis of an uncontrolled AVR system displays considerable deficiencies, including an inability to converge to the desired output, taking a long time to reach a final steady state after a disturbance, and exceeding the desired output level.

Fig. 2Uncontrolled step response of the AVR system.

Uncontrolled step response of the AVR system.

Therefore, to overcome these issues, various control strategies can be implemented for the AVR system, such as a typical PID controller or its more advanced controller, like a fractional-order PID. This paper explores the efficacy of three types of PID controllers. These include the typical PID, the standard typical FOPID, and the FOPID with a fractional filter called the F-FOPID to improve the dynamic response of an AVR system, aiming to stabilize the generator voltage under all operating conditions.

It could be concluded from the evaluation of transient response parameters in Fig. 4 that optimization algorithms such as SOS and WOA offer notable results with reasonable trade-offs between performance metrics among typical PID regulators. The WOA algorithm has high proportional gain, which could mean faster responses at expense of overshoot. The SOS optimization algorithm, on the other hand, has an acceptable balance of stability and response characteristics. For FOPID controllers, algorithms like ACO–FPID and PSO–FOPID provide enhanced performance over classical PID controllers. The PSO-based FOPID controller, on the other hand, has balanced performance, responds quickly, and avoids overshoot. The extra feedforward feature in algorithms like JOA–FOPID can greatly improve performance in more complex fractional-order designs, like those found in FFOPID controllers. It does this by better handling disturbances and achieving more precise control. Classical PID with three variable controllers are simple to use and suitable for many situations. However, FOPID works better for AVR systems with complicated dynamics or a lot of disturbances. The specific requirements of the system, including the desired trade-off between response time, overshoot, and stability, should guide the choice of controller. In addition, it appears in previous studies that the FFOPID received less attention as a controller for the AVR systems. Accordingly. One of the main contributions presented in this paper is a new design of a filtered-fractional-order PID controller for AVR systems, which is based on the hybrid optimisation algorithm to fine-tune the proposed controller parameters.

White shark optimisation algorithm

WSO is a new metaheuristic algorithm based on models inspired by the hunting and foraging behaviours of white sharks, which include the ability to locate prey in the deep ocean34. In the mathematical models, the location of the optimal solution is unknown, and sharks must search extensively. The WSO algorithm models three behaviours of great white sharks in their search for prey: first, movement towards prey based on wave hesitation; second, random search for prey; and third, locating nearby prey through fish school behaviour. These behaviours are mathematically formulated and used to update the positions of sharks to find optimal solutions. Simulated annealing–inspired white shark optimisation algorithm

This paper introduces novel hybrid heuristic optimization algorithms that merge the SA and WSO algorithms. By combining these two algorithms, the proposed approach improves search efficiency and minimizes solution error even for complex optimization problems. The global optimization algorithms are classified into two distinct categories: single solution-based algorithms and multi-solution algorithms, as previously stated35. The SA algorithm is classified as a single solution algorithm, which uses iterative processes to generate and sub-solutions based on existing ones. On the other hand, multi-solution-based algorithms, such as the WSO algorithm, exhibit the characteristic of generating a fresh population and subsequently substituting the existing population during each iteration. In our work, we propose a hybrid approach that combines the SA algorithm with a nature-inspired algorithm that is based on hunting behaviour of white sharks, which can be accomplished through collaborative or integrative approaches36.

There are two ways to implement collaborative hybrid simulated annealing and evolutionary algorithms (SA–EA) either through parallel teamwork or sequential relay37,38 In relay-collaborative methods such as SA–EA39, GA–SA40, and MRFO–SA22, the SA algorithm is first conducted, and then the EA is used to optimize each individual solution in the final population through iterative processes. In contrast, SA–EA algorithms use SA to generate the initial set of solutions for EA algorithm38. While, WSO is not an evolutionary algorithm, it is population-based, making it suitable for integration with SA using a relay-collaboration strategy based on cooperation. In particular, in order to acquire a high-precision initial solution and enhance convergence speed, SA is used to generate the initial set of solutions for the population based optimization algorithm36.

In order to use SA to set up the population of WSO, each individual in the initial population must also be set up using SA, which generates the set of solutions. Figure 5 depicts the process of the hybrid SA–WSO algorithm. The flowchart shows the first-stage initialization parameters of the WSO and SA algorithms. The initialization parameters include the total population size of sharks, the maximum iteration number, the dimension of the optimization problem for the SA–WSO algorithm, the start and minimum temperatures, and the cooling rate for the SA algorithm.

Following this phase, a random starting population size is generated to calculate the values of the initial position. The procedures for SA algorithm execute until they meet the predefined termination criteria. Next, utilize the position created by the SA algorithm to compute the values of the objective function. The proposed hybrid SA–WSO algorithm ends when the number of iterations reaches the maximum predetermined value, and the SA–WSO algorithm gives the optimized parameters and the value for the objective function that goes with them.

Simulation results

This section shows the results obtained by the SA-WSO algorithm as proposed in this paper to optimize different controller configurations for the AVR system, including a standard PID, FOPID, and F-FOPID. The SA–WSO, WSO, and other competitors are coded and run on a laptop with a Core i7-7700HQ CPU, a 2.80 GHz processor, 32 GB of RAM, and a 500 GB hard disc in the environment of MATLAB 2018a. The implemented algorithms are executed for 10 independent runs with 50 iterations, and the number of search agents is 30. The present study evaluates the performance of controllers that have been tuned using the SA–WSO algorithm in comparison to similar controllers reported in the existing literature. The assessment is based on multiple criteria, such as convergence rate, quality of step response, robustness, and ability to reject disturbances. The framework of the optimisation algorithm for tuning PID parameters for an AVR system is illustrated in Fig. 6.

Fig. 6The proposed workflow for an optimisation algorithm for tuning PID parameters in order to determine the optimum design of an AVR system.

The proposed workflow for an optimisation algorithm for tuning PID parameters in order to determine the optimum design of an AVR system.

In general, framework for an optimisation algorithm can be customised based on the specific requirements of the AVR system and the chosen optimisation technique.

The lower and upper limits of the optimization problem are essential for optimization algorithms, which can limit the convergence speed and time required to determine the optimal solution. This study utilizes the lower and upper limits of the controller parameters, drawing from similar systems in previous literature and empirical analysis to identify effective parameter ranges8,26.

In addition, for complex fraction-order PID controllers, the selected lower and upper limits for optimization are established to ensure that the controllers operate within a range that maintains system stability and prevents saturated or oscillations.

Computational resource

Similar to numerous advanced optimisation algorithms26,40,39, the hybrid SA–WSO algorithm requires significant computer resources due to its iterative nature and the need to balance exploration and exploitation. The computing resource requirements depend on factors like problem dimension, the number of variables optimized for PID controllers, and the search space. Figure 26 shows the timeframes of various optimisation algorithms employed to tune three controller types: standard PID, FOPID, and proposed F-FOPID.

Across all algorithms, the F-FOPID controller consistently requires more computational time than both the standard PID and FOPID controllers, indicating its greater structural complexity. Among the algorithms, TLBO has required the longest computation time, especially for the F-FOPID controller, making it a less typical choice when fast computation is a priority. On the other hand, SA –WSO and WSO demonstrate minimal calculation times for the standard PID but require more time with more complex controllers. GWO and DE present more balanced performance, with reasonable computation times for all controller types, making it advantageous for situations necessitating a compromise between computational efficiency and control complexity.

Fig. 26The computation time required by various optimisation algorithms to tune each type of three PID controllers.

The computation time required by various optimisation algorithms to tune each type of three PID controllers.

It could be observed from the results in Fig. 25 that the TLBO algorithm is unsuitable for applications necessitating rapid adjustment, while algorithms such as GWO and DE give more moderate and acceptable computation times without significant trade-offs.

Conclusion

This study presents a novel hybrid metaheuristic algorithm, referred to as SA–WSO, which demonstrates proficiency in optimising the parameters of diverse PID controllers. The hybrid optimisation algorithm comprises two algorithms, namely SA and WSO algorithms, to leverage their advantages. Here, the proposed hybrid algorithm initialises the WSO population using the SA algorithm, which results in a significant improvement in the convergence speed while solving different optimisation problems compared to using either algorithm alone. The paper also introduces a new filtered-fractional-order PID controller with seven parameters that can further enhance step response curve for AVR systems. Additionally, it demonstrates the effectiveness of hybrid combined SA–WSO algorithm in optimising three types of controllers: typical PID, FOPID, and filtrated-FOPID, all of which significantly outperform the counterpart controllers optimised by other algorithms from the literature. The paper presents a robustness analysis and disturbance rejection testing to demonstrate the superiority of controllers optimised by the hybrid SA–WSO algorithm.

The contributions of the paper are two-fold. First, the proposed SA–WSO algorithm is a novel hybrid metaheuristic algorithm that can optimise the parameters of different types of PID controllers. Second, the rarely used filtrated-FOPID controller outperforms other types of controllers in the literature. Finally, future research can investigate alternative hybridization strategies, explore different control strategies, and experimentally validate the proposed optimisation approach by implementing the controller in a real-time generator.

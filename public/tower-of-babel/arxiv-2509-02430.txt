C 0 C^{0} -Contact Geometry of Surfaces in 3–Manifolds
Baptiste Serraille, Maksim Stokić

Abstract
We prove that contact homeomorphisms preserve characteristic foliations on surfaces in contact 3 3 –manifolds. More precisely, since the characteristic foliation is a singular 1 1 –dimensional foliation, we show that singular points are mapped to singular points, and that the image of every 1 1 –dimensional leaf is again a 1 1 –dimensional leaf in the image surface. As a consequence, regular coisotropic surfaces are C 0 C^{0} –rigid. In contrast, we show that contact convexity is C 0 C^{0} –flexible by constructing a contact homeomorphism that sends a convex 2 2 –torus to a non-convex one.

Introduction

The
characteristic foliation

\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
of a surface
\Sigma
in a contact
3
-manifold
(Y,\xi)
is the
singular

1
-dimensional foliation of
\Sigma
generated by the singular line field
T\Sigma\cap\xi
. The set of critical points
\mathrm{Crit}(\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma})
consists of those
p\in\Sigma
where
T_{p}\Sigma=\xi_{p}
. If, in addition, we assume that:

(i)

the contact structure
\xi
is cooriented, i.e.
\xi=\ker\alpha
for some contact form
\alpha
,

(ii)

\Sigma
is an oriented surface equipped with an area form
\Omega
,

then one can define an
oriented characteristic foliation
as the family of integral curves of a vector field
X
on
\Sigma
determined by

\iota_{X}\Omega=\alpha|_{\Sigma}.

The notion of characteristic foliation has proved to be a powerful tool in the study of contact
3
–manifolds. For instance, Giroux showed (see
[
2
]
) that any diffeomorphism
\phi:\Sigma_{1}\to\Sigma_{2}
which preserves oriented characteristic foliations, i.e.
\phi_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma_{1}})=\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma_{2}}
, extends to a contactomorphism between neighbourhoods,
\Phi:Op(\Sigma_{1})\to Op(\Sigma_{2})
. Characteristic foliations also play a central role in the classification of contact structures on
3
–manifolds. Our first result establishes that characteristic foliations are
C^{0}
-rigid under contact homeomorphisms.

A
contact homeomorphism

\varphi:Y\to Y
is a homeomorphism that arises as the
C^{0}
-limit of a sequence of contact diffeomorphisms
\varphi_{i}\in\mathrm{Cont}(Y,\xi)
. A contact analogue of the celebrated Gromov–Eliashberg theorem (see
[
8
]
) asserts that every smooth contact homeomorphism preserves the contact structure. Our first main theorem shows the following rigidity property.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a contact
3-
manifold, and
\Sigma\subset Y
a smooth surface.
Assume
\phi:Y\to Y
is a contact homeomorphism such that the image
\phi(\Sigma)
is smooth. Then
\phi
preserves the characteristic foliation set-wise

\phi_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma})=\mathcal{F}_{\phi(\Sigma)}.

In particular:

-

Singular points are preserved:
\phi(\mathrm{Crit}(\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}))=\mathrm{Crit}(\mathcal{F}_{\phi(\Sigma)})
.

-

For every regular leaf
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
, the image
\phi(\mathcal{L})
is a characteristic leaf in
\mathcal{F}_{\phi(\Sigma)}
.

Proof.

The result is an immediate consequence of Propositions
5.2
and
5.1
.
∎

The result is local in the sense that we do not assume
\Sigma
to be closed, nor do we assume the contact structure
\xi
to be cooriented. If, however,
\Sigma
is an oriented surface and
\xi=\ker\alpha
is cooriented, then one can speak of an oriented characteristic foliation on
\Sigma
. This naturally raises the question:

Question 1
.

Can a contact homeomorphism reverse the orientation of the characteristic foliation?

The question of
C^{0}
–coisotropic rigidity has attracted significant attention in recent years. See, for example,
[
9
]
and
[
11
]
for proofs of

–coisotropic rigidity under additional assumptions on the sequence of conformal factors. Among coisotropic submanifolds, the Legendrian case—corresponding to the minimal dimension—is particularly notable, as it is the only setting where
C^{0}
–rigidity has been proved without imposing conditions on the conformal factors.
C^{0}
–Legendrian rigidity was proved in dimension three in
[
5
]
,
[

]
, and in higher dimensions in
[
6
]
.

A submanifold
C
of a contact manifold
(Y,\xi=\mathrm{ker}\,\alpha)
is called
contact coisotropic
or

if

(1)

for all

the subspace
T_{p}C\cap\xi_{p}\subset\xi_{p}
is coisotropic with respect to the conformal symplectic structure on
\xi_{p}
. In particular,

(T_{p}C\cap\xi_{p})^{\perp_{d\alpha}}\subset T_{p}C\cap\xi_{p},

(2)

the distribution
TC\cap\xi
has constant rank.

Some authors omit the constant-rank assumption in the definition, in which case we will call
C
a
singular coisotropic
submanifold. In contact
3
–manifolds every surface is singular coisotropic, while regular coisotropic surfaces are precisely those satisfying the condition
\mathrm{Crit}(\mathcal{F}_{C})=\emptyset
. Hence the above theorem yields:

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
C
be a regular coisotropic surface in a contact
3
–manifold
(Y,\xi)
. Suppose
\phi:Y\to Y
is a contact homeomorphism such that
\phi(C)
is smooth. Then
\phi(C)
is a regular coisotropic surface.

As we shall see, the proof of Theorem
1
consists of two main steps:

1.

After removing all points in the critical sets (
\mathrm{Crit}(\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma})
and
\phi^{*}\mathrm{Crit}(\mathcal{F}_{\phi(\Sigma)})
), the remaining regular leaves are mapped to each other by
\phi
. The key tool is the
contact hammer
, a contact analogue of Opshtein’s symplectic hammer
[
7
]
, which, together with Givental’s non-displacement result for a Clifford torus in
\mathbb{RP}^{3}
, provides a
C^{0}
–characterization of regular leaves.

2.

Every critical point is mapped to a critical point. This requires analysing the foliation near singularities and using different techniques depending on the type: in some cases using the local orientation of leaves, and in others, applying Givental’s non-displacement result.

In
[
2
]
, Giroux introduced the theory of convex surfaces in contact
3
–manifolds. A surface
\Sigma
in a contact
3
–manifold is said to be
convex
if there exists a contact vector field transverse to it. For such surfaces, one can define a
dividing set
, a much simpler invariant than the characteristic foliation, which plays a central role in the classification of contact structures on
3
–manifolds. Our next result shows that contact convexity is
C^{0}
-flexible.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a contact
3
–manifold. There exists a convex torus
T\subset Y
and a contact homeomorphism
\phi:Y\to Y
such that
\phi(T)
is a smooth, non-convex torus.

Proof.

The proof follows from Theorem
3
.
∎

Our strategy is to show that, in the
C^{0}
–limit, a property of convex surfaces may fail. For a smooth convex surface, every closed leaf of the characteristic foliation is
non-degenerate
: the associated
Poincaré return map
, which measures how nearby leaves wind around a periodic orbit, is non-degenerate. The proof idea is to construct a
C^{0}
–example where this non-degeneracy condition breaks down. Although our construction is carried out on a torus, the same argument applies in a neighbourhood of any closed leaf on an arbitrary surface.

2
Characteristic foliations on surfaces

In this section, we recall the definition and give some properties of characteristic foliations of hypersurfaces inside 3-dimensional contact manifolds. In Section
2.1
, we characterize the sets that can arise as singular sets of a characteristic foliation. In Section
2.2
, we give results concerning the local structure near singularities.

Let
(Y^{3},\xi=\ker\alpha)
be a co-oriented contact
3
–manifold, and let
\Sigma\subset Y
be an oriented embedded surface. The
characteristic foliation

\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
on
\Sigma
is the (possibly singular) line field defined by

T_{p}\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}:=\xi_{p}\cap T_{p}\Sigma\qquad\text{for all }p\in\Sigma.

This defines a smooth singular foliation on
\Sigma
whose
singular set
is

\mathrm{Crit}(\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}):=\{\,p\in\Sigma\mid\xi_{p}=T_{p}\Sigma\,\}.

Let
\omega_{\Sigma}
be the orientation
2
-form on
\Sigma
. Then
\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
is oriented by the unique vector field
X
(defined up to positive scalar) satisfying:

\iota_{X}\omega_{\Sigma}=\alpha|_{\Sigma}.

Equivalently,
X
is the unique line field tangent to
\ker\alpha
such that
d\alpha(X,n)>0
for any positively oriented pair
(X,n)
in
T_{p}\Sigma
with
\alpha(n)>0
.

2.1
Sets that can arise as the critical set of a characteristic foliation

In this section, we show that critical sets of characteristic foliations are included in 1-dimensional smooth submanifolds. Moreover, for each closed sets of the closed interval, we construct a characteristic foliation which has such set as critical set. This last result does not appear in the literature as far as we know.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a contact manifold and
\Sigma\subset Y
be a smooth hypersurface, then the critical set of the characteristic foliation,
\mathrm{Crit}(\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma})
, is closed and contained in a disjoint union of smooth 1-dimensional submanifolds of
\Sigma
.

Proof.

Let
p
be a critical point of the characteristic foliation of
\Sigma
and consider an open subset
U
of
p
in
\Sigma
and identify it with a portion of the
xy
-plane of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then
\beta=\alpha\lvert_{\Sigma}
can be written as
\beta_{x}dx+\beta_{y}dy
with
\beta_{x}
and
\beta_{y}
smooth functions. The critical set is the intersection set of the zero set of
\beta_{x}
and
\beta_{y}
, it is then closed from the continuity of
\beta_{x}
and
\beta_{y}
.

Since
\beta_{p}=0
and
\alpha
is non-degenerate, we have that
d\beta_{p}=(\partial_{x}\beta_{y}-\partial_{y}\beta_{x})dx\wedge dy\neq 0
. This means that
\partial_{x}\beta_{y}\neq\partial_{y}\beta_{x}
, in particular they cannot be both 0, e.g.
\partial_{y}\beta_{x}\neq 0
. The function
\beta_{x}\colon U\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth submersion at 0 locally around
p
hence its zero set is a 1-dimensional smooth manifold.
∎

The converse is also true.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
F\subset I=(a,b)
with
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
be a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}
. Then there exist a contact manifold
(Y,\xi)
, a smooth hypersurface
\Sigma\subset Y
as well as a smooth embedding
\iota\colon I\hookrightarrow\Sigma
such that

\mathrm{Crit}(\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma})=\iota(F).

Proof.

Let
g\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function such that its zero set is exactly
F
and
g^{\prime}(x)<1
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. The first condition can be built using Whitney theorem for the second one can always assume that
g
is constant at infinity (from the compactness of
F
) and then replace
g
by
cg
for some real number
c\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
to obtain small enough derivatives.

Then let us consider the 1-form
\alpha=ydx+g(x)dy+dz
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we have that
d\alpha=dy\wedge dx+g^{\prime}(x)dx\wedge dy+0
. Thus,

\alpha\wedge d\alpha=(g^{\prime}(x)-1)dx\wedge dy\wedge dz\neq 0.

This implies, that
\alpha
is a contact form. We consider now
\Sigma
as the
xy
-plane, and write
\beta=\alpha\lvert_{\Sigma}=ydx+g(x)dy
and
\iota\colon\mathbb{R}\to\Sigma
as the natural inclusion of the line
\{y=0\}
in
\Sigma
. Then

\mathrm{Crit}(\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma})=\{y=0\}\cap\{g(x)=0\}=\iota(F).

∎

2.2
Local structure near singularities in characteristic foliations

In this section, we study the characteristic foliations near the singularities. In particular, we prove Lemma
2.3
that tells us that each singular point of a characteristic foliation sees at least 2 leaves converging to it. This result will be key in order to prove Proposition
5.2
. We then give a local model of the characteristic foliation near 1-dimensional sets of singularities.

Lemma 2.3

(Two leaves Lemma)
.

Let
\Sigma\subset(Y^{3},\ker\alpha)
be an embedded surface, and let

p\in\mathrm{Crit}(\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma})
be a singular point of the characteristic foliation on
\Sigma
.
Then there exist at least two distinct characteristic leaves of
\Sigma
that either both converge to
p
as
t\to+\infty
or both converge to
p
as
t\to-\infty
.

Proof.

Choose local coordinates
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
near
p
so that
p
corresponds to the origin and is singular.
Let
X
generate the characteristic foliation.
Since
\mathrm{div}\,X(0,0)\neq 0
, we have
\mathrm{rank}\,DX(0,0)\geq 1
.

If
\mathrm{rank}\,DX(0,0)=1
, the claim follows from Lemma
2.5
.
In the hyperbolic case
\mathrm{rank}\,DX(0,0)=2
, the Hartman–Grobman Theorem gives a topological conjugacy with its linearization, whose eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
have
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_{i}\neq 0
. Depending on their signs, the origin is a
sink
(
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_{1},\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_{2}<0
), a
source
(
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_{1},\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_{2}>0
), or a
saddle
(
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_{1}<0<\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_{2}
). In all cases, there exist at least two distinct trajectories converging to
(0,0)
as
t\to+\infty
or
t\to-\infty
.
∎

Lemma 2.4

(Local model near one-dimensional singularity)
.

Let
\Sigma\subset(Y^{3},\ker\alpha)
be an embedded surface, and let
T\subset\mathrm{Crit}(\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma})
be a
1
-dimensional component of its singular set. For every

, there exists a neighbourhood

\alpha_{-}=dz-ydx

Proof.

Choose local coordinates

on
\Sigma

.
Let

Since

By Lemma

;

if

noindent Since

(1)

is a diffeomorphism between neighbourhoods of the

to

The characteristic foliation of

Therefore, the diffeomorphism (

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
X
be a vector field on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
X(0,0)=0
and
\mathrm{rank}\,DX(0,0)=1
, whose eigenvalues are
0
and
\lambda\neq 0
. Then there exist two distinct integral curves
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that:

(i)

If
\lambda<0
, then
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\gamma_{i}(t)=(0,0)
; if
\lambda>0
, then
\lim_{t\to-\infty}\gamma_{i}(t)=(0,0)
.

(ii)

The restriction
\left.\frac{X}{\|X\|}\right|_{\mathrm{Im}\,\gamma_{i}}
extends smoothly to
(0,0)
and lies in the eigenspace of
\lambda
.

Proof.

Assume
\lambda>0
(the
\lambda<0
case is analogous). After a linear change of coordinates:

X(x,y)=(\lambda x+u(x,y),v(x,y))

where
u,v=\mathcal{O}(\|(x,y)\|^{2})
. For
0<\varepsilon<a
, define:

\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon,a}=\{\varepsilon\leq x\leq a,|y|\leq|x|\}\subset\mathcal{D}_{a}=\{0<x\leq a,|y|\leq|x|\}.

For small enough

in
\mathcal{D}_{a}
, the Taylor’s expansion implies:

\displaystyle\dot{x}

\displaystyle=\lambda x+\mathcal{O}(x^{2})

\displaystyle\dot{y}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(x^{2}).

Thus all trajectories in
\mathcal{D}_{a}
flow strictly in positive
x
-direction (
\dot{x}>0
), and the slope satisfies

\lvert\mathrm{slope}(X(x,y))\rvert=\Big\lvert\frac{v(x,y)}{\lambda x+v(x,y)}\Big\rvert=\frac{\mathcal{O}(x)}{\lambda+\mathcal{O}(x)}<1,

(2)

so trajectories cannot exit through
|y|=|x|
. Hence any trajectory entering
\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon,a}
must exit through
\{a\}\times[-a,a]
. By continuity and compactness, there exists
\gamma_{1}
in
\mathcal{D}_{a}
with
\lim_{t\to-\infty}\gamma_{1}(t)=(0,0)
. Similarly, we find
\gamma_{2}
in
\{x<0\}
with
\lim_{t\to-\infty}\gamma_{2}(t)=(0,0)
, proving (i).

For (ii), (
2

\lim_{t\to-\infty}\mathrm{slope}(\gamma_{1}(t))=0
, so
X/\|X\|
extends continuously to
(0,0)
with limit
(1,0)
, an eigenvector of
DX(0,0)
for
\lambda
. The existence of

\lim_{t\to-\infty}\frac{\partial^{n}}{(\partial t)^{n}}\left(\mathrm{slope}(\gamma_{1}(t))\right)

(for example via induction and L’Hôpital’s rule) shows the extension of the slope function of

is smooth, and hence the extension of

from
\mathrm{Im}\,\gamma_{1}

\frac{\gamma^{\prime}_{1}(t)}{\|\gamma_{1}^{\prime}(t)\|}=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\mathrm{slope}(\gamma_{1}(t))^{2}}},\frac{\mathrm{slope}(\gamma_{1}(t))}{\sqrt{1+\mathrm{slope}(\gamma_{1}(t))^{2}}}\right).

The proof for
\gamma_{2}
follows similar arguments.

In this section, we define the notion of contact hammer. This definition is inspired by Opshtein’s symplectic hammers. We prove that contact hammers allow us to characterize the leaves on regular sets of a characteristic foliation.

has two open components

.

Definition 4.1

(

.

Fix

\varepsilon

for every

Remark 4.2
.

We denote by
\overline{\mathrm{Cont}}_{0,c}(\mathcal{U},\xi)
the group of compactly supported homeomorphisms that are

-limits of contactomorphisms in
\mathrm{Cont}_{0,c}(\mathcal{U},\xi)

and

\overline{\mathrm{Cont}}_{0,c}(\mathcal{V},\xi^{\prime})=\phi\,\overline{\mathrm{Cont}}_{0,c}(\mathcal{U},\xi)\,\phi^{-1}\coloneqq\{\phi\psi\phi^{-1}\mid\psi\in\overline{\mathrm{Cont}}_{0,c}(\mathcal{U},\xi)\}.

Indeed, let us show that the last set is included in the first one. By a symmetry argument this is enough to finish the proof. Let

be a sequence of contactomorphisms uniformly converging to

and
\phi_{k}\colon\mathcal{U}_{k}\subset\mathcal{U}\to\phi(\mathcal{U}_{k})\subset\mathcal{V}
a sequence of contactomorphisms that converges to

, we can define the contactomorphism

on

by extending it to identity where it is still not defined. Since

converges to

5
C^{0}
-Rigidity of Characteristic Foliations

In this section we prove the
C^{0}
-rigidity of characteristic foliations. As announced in the introduction we will prove first that the leaves are preserved away from the critical points. In a second time, we will then show that the singular sets are also preserved. In the first part, we will use the contact hammers while in the second part, we will utilize the results from Section
2.1
and Section
2.2
.

5.1
Proof of Theorem
1

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\phi\in\overline{\mathrm{Cont}}(Y,\xi)
be a contact homeomorphism with
\phi(\Sigma)
smooth, and let
\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
,
\mathcal{F}_{\phi(\Sigma)}
be oriented singular foliations. Define:

Then the leafs of characteristic foliation on
S
are set-wise preserved
\phi_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{S})=\mathcal{F}_{\phi(S)}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for every
p\in S
, the leaves
\phi(\mathcal{L}^{S}_{p})
and
\mathcal{L}^{\phi(S)}_{\phi(p)}
coincide near
\phi(p)
. Let
U=Op(p)\subset Y
be a Darboux neighborhood of
p
such that
\phi(U)
is a Darboux neighborhood of
\phi(p)
. Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a point

such that
\phi(q)\notin\mathcal{L}^{\phi(S)}_{\phi(p)}
. For
\varepsilon>0
small enough, let
\Psi_{t}
be an
\varepsilon
-contact hammer between
p
and
q
(see Lemma
4.3
). Then, the pushforward

\Phi_{t}:=\phi\circ\Psi_{t}\circ\phi^{-1}

defines an
\varepsilon^{\prime}
-contact hammer between
\phi(p)
and
\phi(q)
, where
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
satisfies
\varepsilon^{\prime}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. This contradicts Proposition
4.4
, completing the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a closed oriented surface in a contact 3-manifold
(Y,\xi=\ker\alpha)
, and let
\phi\in\overline{\mathrm{Cont}}(Y,\xi)
be a contact homeomorphism such that
\phi(\Sigma)
is smooth. Then
\phi
preserves the critical set of the characteristic foliation:

\phi(\mathrm{Crit}(\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}))=\mathrm{Crit}(\mathcal{F}_{\phi(\Sigma)}).

We now use the next lemma in order to deduce from Proposition
5.2
a statement of the action of
\phi
on the orientation of the leaves of the characteristic foliations of
\Sigma
and
\phi(\Sigma)
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\Sigma
be a surface endowed with any singular foliation
\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathrm{Crit}(\mathcal{F})
is a closed subset of
\Sigma
. Let assume that a leaf
\mathcal{L}
of the foliation is oriented, then one can orient the leaves of the connected component of
\Sigma\setminus\mathrm{Crit}(\mathcal{F})
that contains
\mathcal{L}
in a unique way if orientable.

Proof.

We want to orient a leaf
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
that lies in the same connected component as
\mathcal{L}
. Let

and

, then we can find a smooth path

that joins

to

. We can cover the path by a finite number of charts of the foliation. On each chart the orientation is uniquely defined by one leaf. This means that the orientation of the leaf that contains

is uniquely determined by the orientation of the leaf that contains

. This finishes the proof.

Since a contact homeomorphism between two smooth hypersurface preserves the foliation near points that are regular in both surfaces and since those foliations are naturally oriented, we can formulate the following corollary.

Let
Y
be a contact manifold and
\Sigma\subset Y
a hypersurface. Let
\phi
be a contact homeomorphism such that the image

is smooth. Then the homeomorphism
\phi\lvert_{\Sigma}\colon\Sigma\to\phi(\Sigma)

We split the proof of Proposition
5.2

Lemma 5.5
.

With the same hypothesis as in Proposition
5.2
, for each point

that is not in a 1-dimensional set of critical points, we have
\phi(p)\in\mathrm{Crit}\big(\mathcal{F}_{\phi(\Sigma)}\big)
.

.

With the same hypothesis as in Proposition

, for each point

that lies inside a 1-dimensional set of critical points, we have
\phi(p)\in\mathrm{Crit}\big(\mathcal{F}_{\phi(\Sigma)}\big)

The proposition

5.2
.

From Lemma

is a critical point of

Using the same lemmas but on

prove the other inclusion and thus concludes the proof of the proposition.

We prove now Lemma

Proof of Lemma

We work under the assumptions and with the notations of Proposition

Chiral higher-spin symmetry of the celestial twistor sphere
Tung Tran

Abstract
We study the chiral higher-spin symmetry of the celestial twistor sphere via the chiral higher-spin algebras 𝔠 ​ 𝔞 \mathfrak{ca} associated with certain holomorphic higher-spin theories in twistor space. The chiral higher-spin algebras 𝔠 ​ 𝔞 \mathfrak{ca} are non-unitary W 1 + ∞ ​ [ 𝔤 ] W_{1+\infty}[\mathfrak{g}] algebras in the sense that their higher-spin generators can have negative conformal dimensions. We study 𝔠 ​ 𝔞 \mathfrak{ca} up to first order in quantum correction. In particular, we show that the 𝔠 ​ 𝔞 \mathfrak{ca} associated with anomaly-free holomorphic twistorial higher-spin theories are associative themselves, while the chiral algebras associated with anomalous holomorphic twistorial higher-spin theories only become associative upon enlarging 𝔠 ​ 𝔞 \mathfrak{ca} by introducing suitable axionic currents. Furthermore, we compute four-dimensional form factors – interpreted as genuine scattering amplitudes in the integrable sector – via correlation functions of some chiral CFTs that can source higher-spin currents of 𝔠 ​ 𝔞 \mathfrak{ca} on the celestial twistor sphere. Notably, we observe that higher-spin form factors do not always vanish, suggesting the existence of some mysterious bulk theories.

Introduction

Living in the infrared (IR) regime of physics has not stopped us from wondering about its ultraviolet (UV) counterpart. This line of sophisticated thought is particularly interesting, as all irrelevant deformations of a given QFT in curve spacetime, manifesting as higher-derivative interactions in low-energy regime, can become significant in the UV, potentially leading to various issues such as causality violations and inconsistency. There is, however, a potentially simple remedy to this problem: introducing higher-spin fields.

Higher-spin theory (see

As is well-known, it is generally difficult to formulate higher-spin theories as effective field theories due to the presence of non-local interactions, making the search for a worldsheet theory essential. This is not yet possible at the moment, as even though the worldsheet action for free higher-spin fields may be written down in one way or another, a concrete and explicit expression for the vertex operators remains far from obvious.

Twistor and higher spins

Denotes

\displaystyle Z^{A}=(Z^{1},Z^{2},Z^{3},Z^{4})=(\lambda^{\alpha},w^{\dot{\alpha}})\,,\hskip 20.00003ptA=1,2,3,4\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\alpha=1,2\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\dot{\alpha}=\dot{1},\dot{2}\,.

[

The open subset

where

is non-degenerate is referred to as

\displaystyle Z^{A}\mapsto\hat{Z}_{A}=(\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha},\hat{w}^{\dot{\alpha}})\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha}=(-\overline{\lambda^{2}},\overline{\lambda^{1}})\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\hat{w}^{\dot{\alpha}}=(-\overline{w^{\dot{2}}},\overline{w^{\dot{1}}})\,.

(2.2)

The Dolbeault integrable complex structure on twistor space is given by

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}:=d\hat{Z}^{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{Z}^{A}}=d\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha}\hat{\partial}_{\alpha}+d\hat{w}^{\dot{\alpha}}\,\hat{\partial}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\hat{\partial}_{\alpha}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha}}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\hat{\partial}_{\dot{\alpha}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{w}^{\dot{\alpha}}}\,.

(2.3)

Although working with

\displaystyle w^{\dot{\alpha}}=w^{\dot{\alpha}}(x,\lambda)\,,\hskip 20.00003ptx^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}:=x^{a}\sigma_{a}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\,,\hskip 20.00003pta=1,2,3,4\,,

\displaystyle f_{1}(\lambda,w)\star f_{2}(\lambda,w)=\exp\Big([\partial_{1}\,\partial_{2}]\Big)f_{1}(\lambda,w_{1})f_{2}(\lambda,w_{2})\Big|_{w_{1,2}=w}\,.

3.2
Higher-spin current OPEs

Similar to the usual context of scattering amplitudes, in the bulk/defect setting, one also fixes a gauge and compute Witten-like diagram where we will replace the boundary of AdS by a defect

over a spacetime point
\mathtt{p}

For our purposes of fixing the OPE’s coefficients, we will consider the process in which two bulk fields
\mathtt{A}

\displaystyle\parbox{70.0pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{2-point-precollide.png}}\rightarrow

\displaystyle+\parbox{60.0pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{s-channel.png}}+\parbox{60.0pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{t-channel.png}}+\parbox{60.0pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{u-channel.png}}+(\text{higher bulk-loops})

\displaystyle+(\text{higher numbers of $\mathtt{J}$s on the defect and higher bulk-loops})\,.

It is easy to notice that this is a

The algorithm by which one can construct OPEs for
\mathtt{J}[i]\mathtt{J}[j]
in terms of Koszul duality is as follows:

1-

Keep the number of bulk fields near the defect to be constant. Here, it is two.

2-

Draw all possible bulk/defect diagrams, where the number of currents/fields on the
1d

can be more than two. Note that we expect
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathtt{p}}
to be extended enough so that it can wrap around
\mathbb{C}_{\mathtt{p}}

3-

At each order in bulk/defect perturbation theory, one requires that all amplitudes to be gauge invariant on-shell. That is, the BRST variation of the amplitudes on the lhs. of the arrow, cf. (
3.2
), should cancel out with the BRST variation of all amplitudes on the rhs. order by order in perturbation theory. This generally imposes non-trivial constraints, allowing one to fix a large number of OPE coefficients.

4-

For OPE coefficients that are unconstrained by gauge invariance of the bulk/defect amplitudes, we can fix them uniquely by requiring (

Note that while the above procedure can determine the OPE of

3.2.2
One-loop quantum correction to chiral higher-spin algebra

Let us now study the chiral higher-spin algebra at one loop by considering the gauge invariance of the following diagrams:

\displaystyle\delta\Bigg(\parbox{57.0pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{2-point-precollide.png}}+\parbox{50.0pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{s-channel.png}}+\parbox{52.0pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{t-channel.png}}+\parbox{59.0pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{u-channel.png}}\Bigg)=0\,.

(3.43)

Before proceeding, we note that the reason only the above
s
-,
t
- and
u
-channels diagrams can potentially account for quantum correction of the OPE at one loop is that tadpole, bubble-on-external-leg, and triangle diagrams vanish either by symmetry arguments or algebraic constraints. In particular, since we are dealing with massless theories, tadpole diagrams must vanish to avoid Lorentz violation. Meanwhile, contributions from bubble diagrams on external legs can be absorbed through field redefinitions. Finally, the triangle diagram vanishes since it can be written as a total derivative. Indeed, a triangle bulk-diagram can be evaluated as

\displaystyle\parbox{35.0pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{bulk-triangle.png}}\sim\sum_{\mathtt{Spec}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{3}}\mho\text{Tr}(\partial\mathsf{A}\partial\mathsf{A}\partial\mathsf{A})=0\,.

Therefore, these types of diagrams can be safely discarded without affecting the one-loop correction to the OPEs of
\mathfrak{ca}

6
Discussion

In this work, we constructed the chiral higher-spin algebras
\mathfrak{ca}
associated with various holomorphic twistorial higher-spin theories in twistor space via Koszul duality
[
47
]
to first order in quantum correction. Note that our choice of conventions allowed us to identify
\mathfrak{ca}
with the color-kinematic algebra of
4d
chira/self-dual higher-spin theories in
[
28
,
52
]
. We also showed that the
\mathfrak{ca}
associated with anomaly-free holomorphic twistorial higher-spin theories are associative to first order in quantum correction, while the chiral algebras associated with anomalous twistorial higher-spin theories, cf.
[
37
]
, are only associative upon introducing suitable axionic currents – byproducts of the anomaly cancellation in twistor space – via Koszul duality.

Intriguingly, we observed that the chiral higher-spin algebras associated to anomaly-free twistorial higher-spin theories – theories with
\mathtt{Spec}=\mathbb{Z},2\mathbb{Z}+1,2\mathbb{Z}
– allow for higher-derivative interactions while the chiral algebras associated to anomalous twistorial higher-spin theories do not. In particular, for the associativity of the chiral algebras associated to anomalous theories to hold, all external states, which carry
SU(2)_{-}
charges, should have helicity
+1
and interactions should be of Yang-Mills type.
26
26

26

These are one-derivative interactions in light-cone language, cf.
[
100
,
28
]
.

In computing higher-spin form factors, which can be also viewed as genuine scattering amplitudes in the integrable sector (see Table
1
), we find that there can be non-trivial tree-level amplitudes. At loop levels (at least up to two loops) we find that the amplitudes for theories with higher-derivative interactions are zero as these amplitudes can only belong to anomaly-free higher-spin theories. Restricting our attention to the loop amplitudes in twistorial theories with
\mathtt{Spec}=|h|\geq 1
, we find that they are similar to the ones in
[
49
,
51
]
with some overall signs different due to the Riemann regularization for the sum over the spectrum.

We also proposed some chiral CFTs which can source the higher-spin currents of
\mathfrak{ca}
in Section
5
. Upon introducing suitable ghost systems, we find structures that closely resemble those of a worldsheet theory for higher-spin fields. However, it is important stressing that we do not find the standard gravitational couplings between the energy-stress tensor of these chiral theories with bulk gravitons. This stems from the fact that all higher-spin theories in consideration are constructed from the chiral field representations (see e.g.
[
29
,
32
]
and also the recent work
[
124
]
).

A few more remarks are in order. What we have learned through this work as well as
[
37
]
is that there are a large set of higher-spin theories that are quantum-protected. Namely, they have strong integrability properties which forces their loop-amplitudes to be zero or sufficiently simple. (A nice example along this line in the light-cone gauge can be found e.g. in
[
39
,
40
,
41
,
38
]
.) We also learn that for chiral or quasi-chiral theories with higher-derivative interactions, their loop amplitudes should essentially be trivial. Thus, it is reasonable to expect that chiral higher-spin gravity
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
, should have trivial amplitudes to all order in quantum correction due to quantum-integrability. (It is this feature of higher-derivative interactions in chiral or self-dual higher-spin theories allow them to calmly escape various no-go statements in flat space, cf.
[
125
,
126
]
.)

The fact that there can be non-trivial tree-level amplitudes in higher-derivative higher-spin theories indicates the existence of some theories that have yet to be constructed in twistor space or in spacetime. These statements are also supported by the recent light-cone analysis in
[
38
]
. It was observed that there is a large class of self-dual higher-derivative higher-spin theories. Some of these theories can have non-trivial scattering amplitudes, and some may even have a finite spectrum, or spectrum with fractional spins. Since the light-cone method avoids issues with gauge redundancy, it will be interesting to covariantize the results of
[
38
]
to check if they are, in fact, invariant statements.

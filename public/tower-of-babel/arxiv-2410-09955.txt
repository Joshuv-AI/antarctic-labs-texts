EPOS4: New theoretical concepts for modeling proton-proton and ion-ion scattering at very high energies
K. Werner

Abstract
We discuss the new concepts behind EPOS4, a new theoretical framework for modeling hadronic interactions at ultrarelativistic energies. It amounts to reconciling a multiple scattering picture of Gribov-Regge type (with parallel scatterings) and factorization, the standard tool for high-energy scattering.

There are actually two problems concerning the single scattering

Why does the new formalism work?

How to understand why these
N_{\mathrm{conn}}
-dependent saturation
scales “work”? Let me qualitatively explain this by considering
an
A+B
scattering (
A=B=2
) with 3 subscatterings, as shown in

can be found in Refs.
[

Sketch of the “compensation” of smaller energies (red box sizes)
by larger saturation scale values (red dots).

Let me consider any of the two left scatterings, compared to the right
one:
N_{\mathrm{conn}}
is bigger (2 compared to 1); the energy
(
\sqrt{s}
) smaller due to energy sharing;
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}^{2}

is bigger because of the larger
N_{\mathrm{conn}}
(bigger dots);
the parton evolution shorter due to the bigger
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}^{2}
;

the central part responsible for the hard scattering is identical.

This last point is the crucial element, which assures that at the
end the hard particle production is identical independent of
N_{\mathrm{conn}}

, and therefore the sum of all
N_{\mathrm{conn}}
contributions
is (up to a factor) identical to the single Pomeron case. And this
is what is needed to get factorization in such a multiple scattering
formalism.

6
Summary

We have argued that the space-time picture of proton-proton and ion-ion
scattering at very high energies requires an appropriate multiple
scattering framework, based on parallel scatterings. Such a formalism
exists since many years: the Gribov-Regge approach. Starting with
this, we explain that improvements in threes steps allow to develop
a framework, which is still based on strictly parallel scatterings,
but which is perfectly compatible with the factorization approach.
The three steps are: (1) Adding energy-momentum conservation, (2)
Linking with QCD, and (3) Adding saturation in a particular way. We
finally provide a qualitative explanation why the new approach works,
after having provided proofs in earlier publications.

effect, which strongly affects final state observables, like for example

A final remark: There are ad hoc assumptions, details may be questioned,
but the overall multiple-scattering picture seems mandatory, since
it is based on quite fundamental principles: parallel scattering formalism;
energy-momentum sharing (EMS); implementing saturation (it seems mandatory
to implement “environment dependent” factorization scales, which
compensate exacly the effect of the EMS); validity of factorization
at high pt; MC strictly based on a theoretical framework.

CHAPTER 1   The Calculus

Gap is not absence. Gap is witness.

— First principle

We begin with a claim: the fundamental asymmetry in logic—that truth is constructed while falsity is merely the absence of construction—is not necessary. It is a choice, inherited from the intuitionistic tradition, that forecloses an entire dimension of meaning.

In Martin-Löf Type Theory and its homotopical descendant, a type is inhabited or not. A path exists or does not. The machinery has no way to say:
I have witnessed that this path does not exist
. It can only say:
I have not (yet) found this path
, or, in the classical shadow,
assuming this path exists leads to absurdity
.

Open Horn Type Theory makes a different choice. Coherence and gap are both primitive. Both can be witnessed. Both carry proof-relevant structure. Neither is derived from the other.

What follows is the calculus in its minimal form: the judgments, the single law, the derived notion of horn, and the relationship to dependent type theory. Everything else that follows builds on this foundation.

1.1  Judgments with Polarity

Let
J
range over judgments. In a type-theoretic setting, these include formation judgments (
A\;\mathsf{type}
), typing judgments (
t:A
), and equality judgments (
s\equiv t:A
). But the calculus is not restricted to type theory;
J
may be any judgment form in any formal system.

Let
\Gamma
be a context—a finite, consistent collection of assumptions and prior witnesses.

Definition 1.1

(Witnessed Judgments)
.

The Open Horn Type Theory has two primitive judgment forms:

\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}J\qquad\qquad\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}J

The first is read: “
J
is witnessed coherent in context
\Gamma
.” The second: “
J
is witnessed gapped in context
\Gamma
.”

Both forms are
proof-relevant
. If
\gamma:\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}J
and
\gamma^{\prime}:\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}J
, we do not assume
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
. Different witnesses to the same coherence may carry different structure. The same holds for gap witnesses:
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
witnessing the same gap may differ.

The terminology is deliberate. We say
coherent
rather than “true” or “derivable” because the judgment is not merely valid—it is
witnessed as holding together
. We say
gapped
rather than “false” or “refuted” because the judgment is not negated—it is
witnessed as failing to cohere
. This is not a distinction without difference.

Definition 1.2

(The Open)
.

A judgment
J
is
open
in context
\Gamma
if
\Gamma
contains neither a coherence witness for
J
nor a gap witness for
J
.

The open is not a third truth value. It is not “unknown” in any epistemic sense. It is the space of
what has not yet been witnessed
—the locus of potential judgment, where coherence or gap may eventually be established, but where neither currently stands.

Application.

When we read a text to understand its sense, open judgments are the places where attention has not yet arrived. The text presents itself through acts of witnessing—coherence here, gap there. What remains unwitnessed is not nothing; it is the not-yet-known, the space where meaning may still be determined.

1.1.1  The Exclusion Law

The only axiom of the Open Horn Type Theory governs the relationship between coherence and gap:

Definition 1.3

(Exclusion)
.

\frac{\displaystyle\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}J\qquad\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}J}{\displaystyle\bot}

Coherence and gap, for the same judgment in the same context, are mutually exclusive.

This is the entire axiomatic content of the calculus. No other structural rules are imposed. In particular:

•

We do not assume that every judgment is either coherent or gapped (no excluded middle).

•

We do not assume that coherence for
J
implies gap for
\neg J
(no duality).

•

We do not assume that gap is “negation” in any classical or intuitionistic sense.

Gap is
positive structure
. A gap witness
\omega:\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}J
is evidence
for
the non-coherence of
J
, not evidence
against
it. The distinction matters: classical negation is the claim that coherence leads to absurdity; intuitionistic negation is a function from coherence proofs to
\bot
; gap is neither. Gap is:
I have witnessed that this does not cohere
.

1.1.2  Contrast with Negation

Let us be precise about what gap is not.

Classical negation.

In classical logic,
\neg P
is defined such that
P\vee\neg P
holds universally. Negation is the complement of truth. But in OHTT, there is no assumption that
J
is either coherent or gapped. The open is a genuine third state—not a truth value, but a witnessing state.

Intuitionistic negation.

In intuitionistic logic,
\neg P
is defined as
P\to\bot
. A proof of
\neg P
is a function that transforms any proof of
P
into a proof of absurdity. This is constructive, but it is still
derivative
: negation is defined in terms of implication and absurdity, not given primitively. In OHTT, gap is primitive. We do not define
\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}J
as “
\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}J
implies
\bot
.” We assert it directly.

Paraconsistent approaches.

Some logics allow both
P
and
\neg P
to hold without explosion. OHTT is not paraconsistent. The Exclusion Law explicitly forbids simultaneous coherence and gap for the same judgment. What OHTT permits is that
neither
holds—the open—which paraconsistent logics do not typically thematize.

The closest relatives are perhaps
bilattice logics
(Belnap, Ginsberg) which track both truth and information, or
signed logics
with explicit polarity. But OHTT differs in its proof-relevance: we track not just
that
a judgment is coherent or gapped, but
how
—the witness carries structure.

1.2  Horns as Compositional Tension

The judgment forms and the Exclusion Law constitute the entire axiomatic structure of OHTT. From here, we derive the characteristic construction: the horn.

Let
\Rightarrow
denote a compositional relation between judgments. In different instantiations of OHTT, this may be:

•

Derivability
(in proof theory):
J\Rightarrow K
if
K
is derivable from
J

•

Substitution
(in type theory):
J\Rightarrow K
if
K
results from substituting into
J

•

Transport
(in HoTT):
J\Rightarrow K
if
K
is the transported judgment along a path

•

Semantic entailment
:
J\Rightarrow K
if the meaning of
J
implies the meaning of
K

The specific choice of
\Rightarrow
depends on the application domain. What matters is that
\Rightarrow
has a compositional character: if
J\Rightarrow K
and
K\Rightarrow L
, then we may ask whether
J\Rightarrow L
.

Definition 1.4

(The Horn)
.

For judgments
J,K,L
and compositional relation
\Rightarrow
, the
open horn type
is:

\Lambda(J,K,L)\;\coloneqq\;(\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}J\Rightarrow K)\times(\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}K\Rightarrow L)\times(\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}J\Rightarrow L)

An inhabitant of
\Lambda(J,K,L)
is a triple
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\omega)
where
\gamma_{1}
witnesses that
J
composes to
K
,
\gamma_{2}
witnesses that
K
composes to
L
, and
\omega
witnesses that the direct composition from
J
to
L
is gapped.

The horn is
data
, not failure. In ordinary category theory or type theory, if we have
f:A\to B
and
g:B\to C
, the composite
g\circ f:A\to C
is
assumed to exist
. Composition is total. The horn records the situation where composition is
witnessed blocked
: we have the two steps, but the closure is gapped.

1.2.1  Geometric Intuition

The terminology “horn” comes from simplicial homotopy theory. A 2-simplex (triangle) has three vertices and three edges:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega}

A
horn

\Lambda^{2}_{1}
consists of two edges of a potential triangle—here,
\gamma_{1}:J\to K
and
\gamma_{2}:K\to L
—without the third edge. In a
Kan complex
(the standard simplicial model for spaces), every horn can be filled: the missing edge can always be supplied. This is the Kan condition.

In OHTT, we drop this assumption. Instead, the third edge may be:

•

Coherently filled
: there exists
\gamma:\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}J\Rightarrow L
completing the triangle

•

Gap-witnessed
: there exists
\omega:\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}J\Rightarrow L
, and we have a horn
\Lambda(J,K,L)

•

Open
: neither coherence nor gap is witnessed for
J\Rightarrow L

The horn
\Lambda(J,K,L)
is inhabited precisely in the second case: two coherent steps, one gapped closure. This is
structural tension
made formal.

Application.

A horn in a text is the record of local coherence with global rupture. Two sentences may each cohere with a third, while the direct path between them is witnessed as gapped. Three theories may be pairwise consistent but globally inconsistent. The horn does not merely note that something is missing; it records the specific structure of what coheres and what does not.

1.2.2  Higher Horns

The construction iterates. Given coherence witnesses
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}:\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}J
, we may ask whether
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
themselves cohere—whether there is a “path between paths.”

Define the level-1 judgment type as coherence witnesses themselves:

J_{1}\;\coloneqq\;\Sigma(J:\mathsf{Judgment}).\,(\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}J)

Then we instantiate OHTT again at level 1, with
J_{1}
as the judgment type. This gives:

•

Coherence between coherences:
\eta:\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}(\gamma\Rightarrow\gamma^{\prime})

•

Gap between coherences:
\nu:\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}(\gamma\Rightarrow\gamma^{\prime})

•

Level-1 horns:
\Lambda_{1}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime})

This is exactly the structure of homotopy type theory’s higher identity types—but with gap at every level. In HoTT, paths between paths are homotopies; homotopies between homotopies are 2-homotopies; and so on. In OHTT, at each level we have coherence, gap, and open.

The construction is fractal: the same calculus, applied recursively, generates the full tower of higher structure. This is the sense in which OHTT is a
meta
logic: it provides the scaffolding within which ordinary type theories live as fragments.

1.3  The Coherent Fragment

We now make precise the relationship between OHTT and dependent type theory.

Definition 1.5

(The Coherent Fragment)
.

The
coherent fragment
of OHTT is the subcalculus that uses only the judgment form
\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}J
, never
\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}J
.

In the coherent fragment:

•

The Exclusion Law is trivially satisfied (no
\vdash^{-}
judgments to conflict)

•

Horns cannot be inhabited (they require a
\vdash^{-}
component)

•

All unwitnessed judgments are open (gap is unavailable)

Definition 1.6

(MLTT as Coherent Fragment)
.

Let the judgment forms of Martin-Löf Type Theory be:

\displaystyle\Gamma\;\mathsf{ctx}

(
\Gamma
is a well-formed context)

\displaystyle\Gamma\vdash A\;\mathsf{type}

(
A
is a type in context
\Gamma
)

\displaystyle\Gamma\vdash t:A

(
t
has type
A
in context
\Gamma
)

\displaystyle\Gamma\vdash s\equiv t:A

(
s
and
t
are definitionally equal)

The rules of MLTT (
\Pi
-formation,
\Pi
-introduction,
\Pi
-elimination,
\Sigma
-types, identity types, universes, etc.) are
coherence constructors
: rules that generate
\vdash^{+}
judgments from
\vdash^{+}
premises.

This is not an encoding of MLTT into OHTT. It is not a translation or model. It is the observation that
MLTT is the coherent fragment of OHTT
—the part that uses only the right hand.

Working entirely within the coherent fragment, a mathematician derives judgments using the standard rules. They may attempt to inhabit a type and fail, but they cannot
witness
the failure—the failure is absence, not structure. OHTT extends this by making failure structurable.

1.3.1  HoTT as Coherent Fragment with Higher Structure

Homotopy Type Theory extends MLTT with:

•

Univalence
: type equivalence induces identity,
(A\simeq B)\simeq(A=_{\mathcal{U}}B)

•

Higher inductive types
: types specified with path constructors, not just point constructors

•

The homotopy interpretation
: types are spaces, identity types are path spaces

In the simplicial semantics of HoTT, types are interpreted as Kan complexes—simplicial sets where
every horn fills
. This is the Kan condition. Paths (inhabitants of identity types) are 1-simplices; homotopies are 2-simplices; and so on.

OHTT generalizes this. The coherent fragment with higher structure corresponds to HoTT: paths are level-0 coherence witnesses, homotopies are level-1 coherence witnesses. But OHTT allows gap witnesses at each level, breaking the Kan condition.

HoTT = Coherent Fragment of OHTT + Higher Structure + Kan Filling

OHTT = HoTT
-
Kan Filling + Gap Witnesses

1.3.2  What OHTT Adds

OHTT extends the coherent fragment by introducing:

1. Witnessed typing failures.

In MLTT, if we cannot derive
t:A
, we simply lack a derivation. In OHTT, we can witness
\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}(t:A)
—the judgment that
t
does
not
have type
A
. This is not “
t
has some other type” or “the derivation fails.” It is positive evidence of non-typing.

2. Witnessed non-identity.

In HoTT,
\neg(x=_{A}y)
is defined as
(x=_{A}y)\to\bot
. This says: any path from
x
to
y
leads to absurdity. In OHTT, we can witness
\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}(p:x=_{A}y)
directly—the judgment that
x
and
y
are gapped. This is stronger: not “a path would be catastrophic” but “we have witnessed non-coherence.”

3. Transport horns.

Given
t:B(x)
and
p:x=_{A}y
, HoTT guarantees
\mathsf{transport}(p,t):B(y)
. In OHTT, transport may be gapped:
\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}(\mathsf{transport}(p,t):B(y))
. This is the
transport horn
—the central example of Chapter II—where local coherence (the term exists, the path exists) meets global rupture (the transported term does not cohere).

4. Horn structure at all levels.

Whenever two coherence witnesses compose but their composition is gapped, we have a horn. This structure exists at every level of the type-theoretic hierarchy, recording where local derivability fails to compose globally.

1.4  Summary

The Open Horn Type Theory consists of:

•

Two judgment forms
:
\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}J
(coherence witness) and
\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}J
(gap witness)

•

One axiom
: Exclusion (coherence and gap for the same judgment yield
\bot
)

•

One derived structure
: The horn
\Lambda(J,K,L)
recording two coherent steps with a gapped closure

•

One relationship
: Dependent type theory is the coherent (
\vdash^{+}
-only) fragment

Everything else—the simplicial semantics of Part II, the obstruction examples of Part III, the dynamics and persistence of later chapters—builds on this foundation without extending it. The calculus is complete.

What makes this a new logic is not complexity but
reorientation
. By taking gap as primitive, we gain the ability to structure what does not cohere—to witness rupture, not merely fail to witness coherence. This is a small change with large consequences.

Application.

What this lets us say that HoTT cannot: “I have witnessed that these two paths do not cohere—not that their coherence would be catastrophic, but that the space between them is structured absence.” The open horn is not failure. It is the shape of what does not close.

CHAPTER 2   Transport and the Open Horn

The path exists. The term exists.

What does not exist is their meeting.

— The transport horn

In Chapter I we gave the abstract calculus: two judgment forms, one exclusion law, and the derived notion of horn. But a calculus without examples is a skeleton without flesh. We now show that the transport horn—the situation where a term and a path both exist, but the transported term does not cohere—is the central construction that distinguishes OHTT from HoTT.

This chapter has three movements. First, we recall transport in ordinary type theory and its simplicial semantics. Second, we define the transport horn and show that it cannot be expressed in HoTT. Third, we give three classes of examples—topological, semantic, and logical—that demonstrate the phenomenon is not artificial but ubiquitous.

2.1  Transport in Homotopy Type Theory

We begin with the standard picture.

Definition 2.1

(Type Family)
.

A
type family
over
A
is a function
B:A\to\mathcal{U}
assigning to each
x:A
a type
B(x)
.

In the homotopy interpretation, a type family is a fibration:
B
assigns to each point of the base space
A
a fiber, and the fibers vary continuously (in an appropriate sense) as we move through
A
.

Definition 2.2

(Transport)
.

Given a type family
B:A\to\mathcal{U}
, a term
t:B(x)
, and a path
p:x=_{A}y
, the
transport
of
t
along
p
is a term:

\mathsf{transport}(p,t):B(y)

In HoTT, transport is not merely definable—it is
derivable from the induction principle for identity types
. The J-rule guarantees: for any type family
B
over
A
, any
x:A
, and any
t:B(x)
, if we have a path
p:x=_{A}y
, we can construct a term of type
B(y)
.

This is the fundamental operation that makes dependent types “work” in the homotopical setting. Without transport, a term in
B(x)
would be trapped at
x
, unable to move along paths. With transport, terms flow through the type family as we traverse the base.

Application.

The power of transport is also its danger. In HoTT, transport always succeeds. Given any path, any term moves. But this universality obscures situations where transport should fail—where the path exists, the term exists, but their combination does not cohere. OHTT makes such failures visible.

2.1.1  The Simplicial Picture

In the simplicial model of HoTT, types are Kan complexes and type families are Kan fibrations. Let us recall what transport looks like geometrically.

Definition 2.3

(Kan Fibration)
.

A map
p:E\to B
of simplicial sets is a
Kan fibration
if it has the right lifting property against all horn inclusions. That is, for every horn
h:\Lambda^{n}_{k}\to E
and every extension
\bar{h}:\Delta^{n}\to B
of
p\circ h
, there exists a lift
\tilde{h}:\Delta^{n}\to E
such that
p\circ\tilde{h}=\bar{h}
and
\tilde{h}
extends
h
.

In less technical terms: if we have a partial simplex in the total space
E
(a horn), and we know how the full simplex should look downstairs in
B
, we can always complete the simplex upstairs.

Now consider transport. We have:

•

A point
t
in the fiber
E_{x}
over
x\in B

•

A path
\gamma:\Delta^{1}\to B
from
x
to
y

We want to lift
\gamma
to a path in
E
starting at
t
. This is exactly a horn-filling problem:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{0\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\gamma}}

The horn
\Lambda^{1}_{1}
consists of just the vertex
\{0\}
; we are asking to extend it to a full 1-simplex. The Kan fibration condition guarantees a lift
\tilde{\gamma}
exists. The transported term is:

\mathsf{transport}(\gamma,t)\;=\;\tilde{\gamma}(1)

Proposition 2.4

(Transport as Horn-Filling)
.

In the simplicial model, transport along a path
\gamma:x=_{A}y
corresponds to filling the horn
\Lambda^{1}_{1}
in the total space of the fibration.

This is the key insight:
transport is horn-filling
. The Kan condition guarantees all horns fill, hence transport always succeeds. Remove the Kan condition, and transport may fail.

2.2  The Transport Horn

We now define the central construction of OHTT.

Definition 2.5

(Transport Horn)
.

Given a type family
B:A\to\mathcal{U}
, a term
t:B(x)
, a path
p:x=_{A}y
, and a context
\Gamma
, the
transport horn
is an inhabitant of the type:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathsf{tr}}(B,t,p)\;\coloneqq\;\;

\displaystyle\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}(t:B(x))

\displaystyle\times\;

\displaystyle\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}(p:x=_{A}y)

\displaystyle\times\;

\displaystyle\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}(\mathsf{transport}(p,t):B(y))

An inhabitant
(\gamma_{t},\gamma_{p},\omega)
of the transport horn witnesses:

•

\gamma_{t}
: the term
t
coherently inhabits
B(x)

•

\gamma_{p}
: the path
p
coherently connects
x
to
y

•

\omega
: the transported term
\mathsf{transport}(p,t)
is
gapped
at
B(y)

This is precisely the situation that HoTT cannot express. In HoTT, given
\gamma_{t}
and
\gamma_{p}
, the term
\mathsf{transport}(p,t):B(y)
is
derivable
. There is no way to assert that transport fails; the rules guarantee it succeeds.

In OHTT, we can have coherent premises and a gapped conclusion. The horn records the structural tension: local coherence (the term is well-typed, the path is well-formed) with global rupture (their combination does not cohere).

2.2.1  The Geometry of Transport Failure

What does a transport horn look like simplicially?

In the ruptured simplicial model (Definition
2.7
below), we have a map
p:E\to B
that is
not
required to be a Kan fibration. Given:

•

A 0-simplex
t\in E_{x}
(a point in the fiber over
x
)

•

A 1-simplex
\gamma:x\to y
in
B
(a path in the base)

We form the lifting problem as before. But now three outcomes are possible:

1.

Coherent lift
: There exists
\tilde{\gamma}:\Delta^{1}\to E
lifting
\gamma
with
\tilde{\gamma}(0)=t
. Transport succeeds; we have
\mathsf{transport}(\gamma,t)=\tilde{\gamma}(1)
.

2.

Gapped lift
: We witness that no such lift exists. The horn
(\{t\},\gamma)
is gap-witnessed; transport is blocked.

3.

Open
: Neither coherence nor gap is witnessed. The transport question is undetermined.

The transport horn is case (2): the data
t
and
\gamma
are coherent, but their combination—the lift—is gapped.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{0\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nexists}

The dashed arrow with
\nexists
indicates: not merely “we haven’t found a lift” but “we have witnessed that no lift coheres.”

2.2.2  Relation to the General Horn

The transport horn is an instance of the general horn schema from Chapter I. Recall:

\Lambda(J,K,L)\;\coloneqq\;(\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}J\Rightarrow K)\times(\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}K\Rightarrow L)\times(\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}J\Rightarrow L)

For transport, instantiate:

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\;\coloneqq\;(t:B(x))

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle\;\coloneqq\;(p:x=_{A}y)

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle\;\coloneqq\;(\mathsf{transport}(p,t):B(y))

\displaystyle J\Rightarrow K

\displaystyle\;\coloneqq\;\text{``$t$ and $p$ are composable data for transport''}

\displaystyle K\Rightarrow L

\displaystyle\;\coloneqq\;\text{``$p$ determines the target type $B(y)$ for transport''}

\displaystyle J\Rightarrow L

\displaystyle\;\coloneqq\;\text{``$t$ transports along $p$ to inhabit $B(y)$''}

The transport horn asserts: the first two compositions cohere (we have the data), but the third is gapped (the transport itself fails).

2.3  The Ruptured Simplicial Model

To give semantics to OHTT, we generalize the simplicial model of HoTT.

Definition 2.6

(Ruptured Simplicial Set)
.

A
ruptured simplicial set
is a tuple
(X,\mathsf{Coh},\mathsf{Gap},\mathsf{Excl})
where:

•

X
is a graded set
X=(X_{0},X_{1},X_{2},\ldots)
with face and degeneracy maps

•

\mathsf{Coh}_{n}\subseteq X_{n}
is the set of
coherently witnessed

n
-simplices

•

\mathsf{Gap}_{n}\subseteq\{\text{horns in }X\}
is the set of
gap-witnessed
horns

•

\mathsf{Excl}
: if
h\in\mathsf{Gap}_{n}
, then no
\sigma\in\mathsf{Coh}_{n}
fills
h

The key difference from ordinary simplicial sets: we track not just which simplices exist, but which are
witnessed
as coherent, and which horns are
witnessed
as unfillable.

Definition 2.7

(Ruptured Kan Complex)
.

A ruptured simplicial set
(X,\mathsf{Coh},\mathsf{Gap},\mathsf{Excl})
is a
ruptured Kan complex
if for every horn
h
in
X
, exactly one of the following holds:

1.

There exists
\sigma\in\mathsf{Coh}
filling
h
(the horn is coherently filled)

2.

h\in\mathsf{Gap}
(the horn is gap-witnessed)

3.

Neither (the horn is open)

An ordinary Kan complex is a ruptured Kan complex where
\mathsf{Gap}=\emptyset
and every horn has a coherent filler. HoTT lives in this special case. OHTT lives in the general case.

Definition 2.8

(Ruptured Kan Fibration)
.

A map
p:E\to B
of ruptured simplicial sets is a
ruptured Kan fibration
if the lifting property holds
up to the gap structure
: for every lifting problem, either a coherent lift exists, or the lifting horn is gap-witnessed, or the problem is open.

Proposition 2.9

(Transport in Ruptured Fibrations)
.

In a ruptured Kan fibration
p:E\to B
, given
t\in E_{x}
and
\gamma:x\to y
in
B
, the transport of
t
along
\gamma
is:

1.

Coherent
if the lifting horn has a coherent filler

2.

Gapped
if the lifting horn is gap-witnessed

3.

Open
if the lifting horn is neither

This proposition is the semantic content of the transport horn. OHTT allows case (2); HoTT forbids it.

2.4  Three Classes of Examples

We now give concrete examples of transport horns in three domains: topology, semantics, and logic. These are previews of the detailed case studies in Part III.

2.4.1  Topological Obstruction: Monodromy

Let
p:E\to B
be a covering space (a particularly nice kind of fibration). Choose a base point
x\in B
and consider a loop
\gamma:x\to x
in
B
.

If
t\in E_{x}
is a point in the fiber, we can try to lift
\gamma
to a path in
E
starting at
t
. For covering spaces, the lift exists and is unique. But the lifted path may not return to
t
—it may end at a different point
t^{\prime}\in E_{x}
.

The
monodromy
of the covering is the permutation of the fiber induced by lifting loops.

Example 2.10

(Möbius Strip)
.

Let
B=S^{1}
(the circle) and let
E
be the boundary of a Möbius strip—a double cover of
S^{1}
. The fiber over any point has two elements: call them
+
and
-
.

The intuition is immediate: walk once around the base circle, starting at a point
b
. Downstairs, you return to
b
—the loop closes. But upstairs, if you start at
+
and walk, you arrive at
-
.
You come back changed.

Mathematically: the lift of the loop exists and is unique (it’s the path starting at
+
). But it is not a loop based at
+
—it ends at
-
. The question “does this loop lift to a loop at my starting point?” has a definite negative answer, and the monodromy (the sheet-swap) is the certificate.

This is a transport horn:

•

The starting point
+\in E_{b}
is coherent (it exists)

•

The loop
\gamma:b\to b
is coherent (it exists)

•

The based lift is gapped (it does not exist as a loop at
+
)

The gap witness is the monodromy: not silence about whether a lift exists, but positive data recording
how
the lift fails to close. The deck transformation that swaps
+
and
-
is the obstruction made structural.

Application.

The Möbius strip is the simplest non-trivial example of this phenomenon. The twist is not just a curiosity—it is a gap witness. OHTT lets us say: walking around the base is coherent, your starting point is coherent, but returning to where you started is gapped. The formalism holds what the image shows.

2.4.2  Semantic Obstruction: Polysemy

Consider natural language as a type-theoretic structure. Let:

•

A=
the type of word tokens (occurrences of words in context)

•

B(w)=
the type of meanings (senses) of word
w

Two tokens of the “same word” are related by a path in
A
. The word “bank” in a financial context and “bank” in a river context are connected by lexical identity—they are the same lexeme.

Let
x=
“bank” (financial) and
y=
“bank” (river). Let
p:x=_{A}y
witness their lexical identity.

Let
t:B(x)
be the meaning “financial institution.”

What is
\mathsf{transport}(p,t)
? It would be: the meaning of “bank” (river) obtained by transporting the financial meaning along the lexical identity path.

But this doesn’t cohere. The river sense of “bank” is
not
“financial institution.” The lexical identity exists, but it does not transport meanings.

Example 2.11

(Polysemy Horn)
.

\Lambda_{\mathsf{tr}}(B,\text{``financial institution''},p_{\text{lexical}})

is inhabited by:

•

\gamma_{t}
: witnessing that “financial institution” is a coherent meaning of “bank” (financial)

•

\gamma_{p}
: witnessing that “bank” (financial) and “bank” (river) are lexically identical

•

\omega
: witnessing that “financial institution” is
not
a coherent meaning of “bank” (river)

In HoTT, if we had a path
p:x=y
, transport would force meanings to transfer. We would be unable to model polysemy—the fact that the same word has genuinely different meanings that do not reduce to each other.

In OHTT, the transport horn records exactly this: lexical identity without semantic identity. The path exists. The meaning exists. But the meaning does not travel along the path.

Application.

A natural question: how would one actually measure whether meanings cohere? Distributional semantics provides an answer. In word embedding models—and in the attention mechanisms of large language models—words live in high-dimensional vector spaces where semantic similarity becomes geometric proximity. “Bank” (financial) and “bank” (river) may share a lexical token, but their contextual embeddings land in distant regions of meaning-space. Cosine distance, contextual clustering, and attention patterns give us computable measures of semantic (dis)continuity.

The transport horn formalizes this divergence: lexical proximity does not imply semantic coherence. In an empirical instantiation of OHTT, the gap witness could be precisely this measurable distance—the geometric evidence that meaning does not travel along the lexical identity path.

2.4.3  Logical Obstruction: Underivability

Consider a dependent type theory as a category with families. Let:

•

A=
the type of contexts

•

B(\Gamma)=
the type of judgments derivable in context
\Gamma

A context morphism
\sigma:\Delta\to\Gamma
(a substitution) induces a path
p_{\sigma}:\Gamma=_{A}\Delta
in an appropriate sense.

Given a judgment
J
derivable in
\Gamma
, we can ask: is
J[\sigma]
(the substituted judgment) derivable in
\Delta
?

In standard type theory, substitution preserves derivability. If
\Gamma\vdash t:T
, then
\Delta\vdash t[\sigma]:T[\sigma]
. This is the
substitution lemma
.

But consider a richer setting where derivability is resource-sensitive, or where contexts carry additional structure. Then substitution may fail to preserve derivability.

Example 2.12

(Resource Sensitivity)
.

Let
\Gamma=x:\mathsf{File},y:\mathsf{ReadPerm}(x)
and let
J=\mathsf{read}(x):\mathsf{Data}
.

Let
\sigma:\Delta\to\Gamma
be a substitution where
\Delta
provides the file but not the read permission.

Then:

•

\vdash^{\!+}\Gamma\vdash J
: the read is derivable with permission

•

\vdash^{\!+}(\sigma:\Delta\to\Gamma)
: the substitution is well-formed

•

\vdash^{\!-}\Delta\vdash J[\sigma]
: the read is not derivable without permission

This is a transport horn: the judgment is derivable, the substitution exists, but the substituted judgment is gapped.

The gap witness here is not merely “no derivation found” but a
certificate of underivability
—a proof that the resources required for
J
are not available in
\Delta
.

Application.

This connects OHTT to certified compilation, proof-carrying code, and information flow analysis. Systems that track permissions, capabilities, or security levels routinely encounter situations where operations are well-typed in one context but blocked in another. The transport horn is the natural formalization: substitution exists, but derivability does not transport.

2.5  The Functoriality Question

In HoTT, transport is functorial. Given paths
p:x=_{A}y
and
q:y=_{A}z
, we have:

\mathsf{transport}(q\cdot p,t)=\mathsf{transport}(q,\mathsf{transport}(p,t))

Transport along a composite path equals iterated transport.

In OHTT, this equation becomes a
question
. We may have:

•

\mathsf{transport}(p,t)
coherent

•

\mathsf{transport}(q,\mathsf{transport}(p,t))
coherent

•

But
\mathsf{transport}(q\cdot p,t)
gapped—or open

The composite path may fail to transport even when the individual segments succeed.

Example 2.13

(Cumulative Obstruction)
.

In the polysemy example, consider three senses of “crane”: the bird, the machine, and the poetic verb (to crane one’s neck).

•

Path
p
: bird
\to
machine (both are tall, thin, with a hook-like part)

•

Path
q
: machine
\to
verb (the machine’s motion resembles necking craning)

Some semantic features transport along
p
(tallness, thinness). Some transport along
q
(extension, reaching). But transporting directly from bird to verb via
q\cdot p
may lose all coherent features—the composite obstruction is worse than the sum of parts.

This failure of functoriality is not a bug but a feature. It captures the phenomenon of
semantic drift
: meanings can travel stepwise through intermediate senses but lose coherence over longer paths.

Definition 2.14

(Functoriality Horn)
.

Given paths
p:x=_{A}y
and
q:y=_{A}z
, and a term
t:B(x)
, the
functoriality horn
is:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathsf{func}}(t,p,q)\;\coloneqq\;\;

\displaystyle\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}(\mathsf{transport}(p,t):B(y))

\displaystyle\times\;

\displaystyle\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}(\mathsf{transport}(q,(\mathsf{transport}(p,t))):B(z))

\displaystyle\times\;

\displaystyle\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}(\mathsf{transport}(q\cdot p,t):B(z))

The functoriality horn witnesses: stepwise transport succeeds, but direct transport along the composite fails. This is a higher-order obstruction invisible to HoTT.

2.6  What This Lets Us Say

Let us be explicit about the expressive gain.

In HoTT, we can say:

•

“
t
has type
B(x)
”

•

“
p
is a path from
x
to
y
”

•

“
\mathsf{transport}(p,t)
has type
B(y)
” (automatically, by the rules)

•

“
\mathsf{transport}(p,t)=s
” for some specific
s:B(y)

In HoTT, we cannot say:

•

“Transport of
t
along
p
is blocked”

•

“There is an obstruction to transport” (as positive data)

•

“The path exists but does not carry this term”

In OHTT, we can additionally say:

•

\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}(\mathsf{transport}(p,t):B(y))
— “Transport is witnessed as blocked”

•

\Lambda_{\mathsf{tr}}(B,t,p)
inhabited — “We have a transport horn”

•

The obstruction
\omega
carries structure—
why
transport fails

This is the fundamental extension. HoTT can express what transports; OHTT can also express what does not—and
why
.

2.7  Summary

The transport horn is the keystone of OHTT:

•

Definition
:
\Lambda_{\mathsf{tr}}(B,t,p)=\vdash^{\!+}(t:B(x))\times\vdash^{\!+}(p:x=y)\times\vdash^{\!-}(\mathsf{transport}(p,t):B(y))

•

Geometry
: Transport is horn-filling. The transport horn is a horn that is witnessed unfillable.

•

Semantics
: Ruptured Kan fibrations allow lifting problems to be coherent, gapped, or open.

•

Examples
: Monodromy in covering spaces (topological), polysemy in language (semantic), resource-sensitive derivability (logical).

•

Higher structure
: Functoriality may fail; composite paths may be gapped even when segments transport.

The transport horn is not an exotic edge case. It is the generic situation whenever paths (identities, equivalences, substitutions) exist between points whose internal structure does not respect those paths. HoTT assumes this never happens. OHTT allows it, and gives it structure.

Application.

What this lets us say that HoTT cannot: “The word is the same, but the meaning does not follow. The path is there, but the term cannot walk it. The identity exists—and yet, something refuses to be identified.” The transport horn is the formal shape of that refusal.

Part II
The Geometric Model

CHAPTER 3   Ruptured Simplicial Sets

A space is not just what is there.

It is also what is witnessed absent.

— The ruptured view

In Part I we gave OHTT as a calculus: judgment forms, the exclusion law, horns. In Chapter
2
we sketched how this calculus finds semantics in a modification of the simplicial model. Now we develop that model properly.

This chapter constructs ruptured simplicial sets—the basic objects of our geometry. We begin with a rapid review of ordinary simplicial sets and Kan complexes, then introduce the ruptured variant, and finally characterize the relationship between ruptured and ordinary structures.

3.1  Simplicial Sets: A Review

We assume familiarity with basic category theory but recall the essential definitions.

Definition 3.1

(The Simplex Category)
.

The
simplex category

\Delta
has:

•

Objects: finite non-empty ordinals
[n]=\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
for
n\geq 0

•

Morphisms: order-preserving maps
[m]\to[n]

The morphisms of
\Delta
are generated by two families:

•

Face maps

\delta_{i}:[n-1]\to[n]
for
0\leq i\leq n
: the injection that skips
i

•

Degeneracy maps

\sigma_{i}:[n+1]\to[n]
for
0\leq i\leq n
: the surjection that repeats
i

These satisfy the
simplicial identities
, which we omit but which ensure coherent composition.

Definition 3.2

(Simplicial Set)
.

A
simplicial set
is a functor
X:\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}
. Explicitly,
X
consists of:

•

Sets
X_{n}
for each
n\geq 0
(the
n
-simplices
)

•

Face maps
d_{i}:X_{n}\to X_{n-1}
for
0\leq i\leq n

•

Degeneracy maps
s_{i}:X_{n}\to X_{n+1}
for
0\leq i\leq n

satisfying the simplicial identities.

We write
\mathbf{sSet}
for the category of simplicial sets with natural transformations as morphisms.

Example 3.3

(Standard Simplices)
.

The
standard
n
-simplex

\Delta^{n}
is the representable functor
\Delta(-,[n])
. Concretely:

•

\Delta^{0}
is a single point

•

\Delta^{1}
is an edge with two vertices

•

\Delta^{2}
is a filled triangle with three vertices, three edges, and one 2-cell

3.1.1  Horns

Definition 3.4

(Horn)
.

The
(n,k)
-horn

\Lambda^{n}_{k}
for
0\leq k\leq n
is the simplicial subset of
\Delta^{n}
consisting of all faces
except
the
k
-th face. It is the “boundary of
\Delta^{n}
with one face missing.”

Concretely:

•

\Lambda^{1}_{0}
is the single vertex
\{1\}
(the edge
\Delta^{1}
minus the face opposite vertex 0)

•

\Lambda^{1}_{1}
is the single vertex
\{0\}

•

\Lambda^{2}_{0}
is two edges sharing vertex 0: the edges
[0,1]
and
[0,2]

•

\Lambda^{2}_{1}
is two edges sharing vertex 1: the edges
[0,1]
and
[1,2]

•

\Lambda^{2}_{2}
is two edges sharing vertex 2: the edges
[0,2]
and
[1,2]

A horn is a partial simplex—all the data needed to potentially form a full simplex, except one piece.

Definition 3.5

(Horn Inclusion)
.

The
horn inclusion
is the canonical map
\iota:\Lambda^{n}_{k}\hookrightarrow\Delta^{n}
.

3.1.2  Kan Complexes

Definition 3.6

(Kan Complex)
.

A simplicial set
X
is a
Kan complex
if every horn in
X
can be filled. Precisely: for every
n\geq 1
, every
0\leq k\leq n
, and every map
h:\Lambda^{n}_{k}\to X
, there exists an extension
\bar{h}:\Delta^{n}\to X
such that
\bar{h}\circ\iota=h
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda^{n}_{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{h}}

The Kan condition says: if you have all faces of a potential simplex except one, you can always complete it. This is the defining property that makes Kan complexes behave like spaces.

Theorem 3.7

(Kan Complexes as
\infty
-Groupoids)
.

Kan complexes are precisely the
\infty
-groupoids: higher categorical structures where all morphisms at all levels are invertible (up to coherent higher morphisms).

This is the homotopy hypothesis (Grothendieck): spaces =
\infty
-groupoids = Kan complexes. In HoTT, types are interpreted as Kan complexes, which is why all paths are invertible and all higher structure is groupoidal.

3.2  Ruptured Simplicial Sets

We now introduce the central construction.

Definition 3.8

(Ruptured Simplicial Set)
.

A
ruptured simplicial set
is a tuple
\mathcal{X}=(X,\mathsf{Coh},\mathsf{Gap})
where:

1.

X
is a simplicial set (the
underlying simplicial set
)

2.

\mathsf{Coh}=(\mathsf{Coh}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
where
\mathsf{Coh}_{n}\subseteq X_{n}
is the set of
coherently witnessed
n
-simplices

3.

\mathsf{Gap}=(\mathsf{Gap}_{n,k})_{n\geq 1,0\leq k\leq n}
where
\mathsf{Gap}_{n,k}\subseteq\mathrm{Hom}(\Lambda^{n}_{k},X)
is the set of
gap-witnessed
(n,k)
-horns

subject to the
exclusion condition
: if
h\in\mathsf{Gap}_{n,k}
, then there is no
\sigma\in\mathsf{Coh}_{n}
such that
\sigma
fills
h
(i.e., no
\sigma
with
d_{i}(\sigma)=h_{i}
for all
i\neq k
).

Remark 3.9
.

The exclusion condition is the semantic counterpart of the Exclusion Law from Chapter I. Coherent filling and gap witnessing cannot coexist for the same horn.

Definition 3.10

(Open Horns)
.

A horn
h:\Lambda^{n}_{k}\to X
in a ruptured simplicial set
\mathcal{X}
is
open
if:

•

There is no
\sigma\in\mathsf{Coh}_{n}
filling
h
, and

•

h\notin\mathsf{Gap}_{n,k}

Thus every horn in a ruptured simplicial set is in exactly one of three states:

1.

Coherently filled
: admits a filler in
\mathsf{Coh}

2.

Gap-witnessed
: belongs to
\mathsf{Gap}

3.

Open
: neither

This trichotomy is the geometric realization of the OHTT judgment trichotomy:
\vdash^{+}
,
\vdash^{-}
, or unwitnessed.

3.2.1  Morphisms

Definition 3.11

(Morphism of Ruptured Simplicial Sets)
.

A
morphism

f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
of ruptured simplicial sets is a simplicial map
f:X\to Y
such that:

1.

f
preserves coherence: if
\sigma\in\mathsf{Coh}^{X}_{n}
, then
f(\sigma)\in\mathsf{Coh}^{Y}_{n}

2.

f
preserves gaps: if
h\in\mathsf{Gap}^{X}_{n,k}
, then
f\circ h\in\mathsf{Gap}^{Y}_{n,k}

Proposition 3.12
.

Ruptured simplicial sets and their morphisms form a category
\mathbf{rsSet}
.

Proof.

Identity and composition are inherited from
\mathbf{sSet}
; preservation of
\mathsf{Coh}
and
\mathsf{Gap}
is immediate.
∎

3.2.2  The Forgetful Functor

Definition 3.13

(Underlying Simplicial Set)
.

The
forgetful functor

U:\mathbf{rsSet}\to\mathbf{sSet}
sends
(X,\mathsf{Coh},\mathsf{Gap})
to
X
.

This functor forgets the witnessing structure. A ruptured simplicial set carries strictly more information than its underlying simplicial set.

Proposition 3.14

(Fibers of the Forgetful Functor)
.

For a fixed simplicial set
X
, the fiber
U^{-1}(X)
is the set of all ways to assign coherence and gap witnessing to simplices and horns of
X
, subject to exclusion.

This fiber is large: a single simplicial set admits many ruptured structures. The choice of ruptured structure encodes “what we know” about the space—which simplices we have witnessed as coherent, which horns we have witnessed as unfillable.

3.3  Ruptured Kan Complexes

Definition 3.15

(Ruptured Kan Complex)
.

A ruptured simplicial set
\mathcal{X}=(X,\mathsf{Coh},\mathsf{Gap})
is a
ruptured Kan complex
if every horn is either coherently filled, gap-witnessed, or open. (This is automatic from the definitions—the content is that we impose no further Kan-like filling requirement.)

Equivalently: a ruptured Kan complex is a ruptured simplicial set where we have made no commitment that non-gapped horns must have coherent fillers.

Proposition 3.16

(Kan Complexes as Special Ruptured Kan Complexes)
.

An ordinary Kan complex
X
can be viewed as a ruptured Kan complex
(X,X_{\bullet},\emptyset)
where:

•

\mathsf{Coh}_{n}=X_{n}
(all simplices are coherently witnessed)

•

\mathsf{Gap}_{n,k}=\emptyset
(no horns are gap-witnessed)

In this case, every horn is coherently filled (none are gapped or open).

This embedding
\mathbf{Kan}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{rsKan}
exhibits Kan complexes as the “fully coherent” ruptured Kan complexes—those with maximal
\mathsf{Coh}
and empty
\mathsf{Gap}
.

Definition 3.17

(Fully Gapped Complex)
.

A ruptured simplicial set is
fully gapped
if every non-degenerate horn is gap-witnessed. This is the opposite extreme from Kan.

Most interesting ruptured Kan complexes lie between these extremes: some horns fill, some are gapped, some remain open.

3.3.1  The Coherent Core

Definition 3.18

(Coherent Core)
.

For a ruptured simplicial set
\mathcal{X}=(X,\mathsf{Coh},\mathsf{Gap})
, the
coherent core

X^{\mathsf{Coh}}\subseteq X
is the smallest simplicial subset containing
\mathsf{Coh}_{n}
for all
n
. Equivalently,
X^{\mathsf{Coh}}
is obtained by closing
\mathsf{Coh}
under all face and degeneracy maps.

The coherent core contains exactly what we have witnessed as coherent, together with whatever simplices are forced by the simplicial identities (faces and degeneracies of coherent simplices).

Proposition 3.19
.

If
\mathcal{X}
is a ruptured Kan complex, then
X^{\mathsf{Coh}}
is a (possibly partial) Kan complex in the sense that horns within
X^{\mathsf{Coh}}
that have coherent fillers in
\mathcal{X}
have fillers in
X^{\mathsf{Coh}}
.

Application.

The coherent core is the “HoTT fragment” of a ruptured structure: the part where ordinary type-theoretic reasoning applies. Gaps live outside the core, in the complement. The boundary between core and complement is where transport horns live.

3.4  Gap Witnesses as Structure

A crucial feature of ruptured simplicial sets: gap witnesses carry structure.

Definition 3.20

(Gap Witness)
.

For a horn
h\in\mathsf{Gap}_{n,k}
, a
gap witness
for
h
is the data that
h\in\mathsf{Gap}_{n,k}
—the fact that
h
has been marked as gap-witnessed.

In the minimal formulation,
\mathsf{Gap}_{n,k}
is just a subset, and “being gap-witnessed” is a property. But we can enrich this.

Definition 3.21

(Typed Gap Structure)
.

A
typed ruptured simplicial set
is a tuple
(X,\mathsf{Coh},\mathsf{Gap},\Omega)
where:

•

(X,\mathsf{Coh},\mathsf{Gap})
is a ruptured simplicial set

•

\Omega:\mathrm{dom}(\mathsf{Gap})\to\mathbf{Set}
assigns to each gap-witnessed horn
h\in\mathsf{Gap}_{n,k}
a set
\Omega(h)
of
gap modes

Gap modes capture
how
a horn fails to fill. Different modes may represent:

•

Different obstructions (topological, semantic, logical)

•

Different degrees of failure

•

Different witnesses or certificates of non-fillability

Example 3.22

(Monodromy Modes)
.

In the Möbius strip example from Chapter
2
, the gap mode for a transport horn could record which deck transformation obstructs the lift. For the double cover of
S^{1}
, there are two deck transformations: identity and swap. The non-trivial gap mode is the swap.

For the core development, we work with the untyped version where
\mathsf{Gap}_{n,k}
is simply a subset. Typed gaps are a natural extension when finer structure is needed.

3.5  Constructions on Ruptured Simplicial Sets

We briefly note that standard simplicial constructions extend to the ruptured setting.

3.5.1  Products

Definition 3.23

(Product)
.

For ruptured simplicial sets
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
, the

\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
has:

•

Underlying simplicial set:

(the ordinary product)

•

A horn in the product is gapped if either of its projections is gapped. This reflects that obstruction in either factor blocks the combined structure.

3.5.2  Mapping Spaces (Outline)

The notion of mapping space extends to the ruptured setting, but the full development requires care.

3.6  Homotopy in Ruptured Simplicial Sets

•

•

This trichotomy at the level of maps mirrors the trichotomy at the level of points (paths) and higher (higher homotopies).

3.8  Summary

Ruptured simplicial sets extend simplicial sets with:

•

: which simplices are witnessed as coherent (

•

: which horns are witnessed as unfillable (

)

•

Exclusion

This gives the trichotomy at every level: coherent, gapped, or open.

Key relationships:

•

Ordinary Kan complexes embed as ruptured Kan complexes with

•

The coherent core

Homotopy itself becomes three-valued: coherent, gapped, or open

Application.

What this lets us say that ordinary simplicial theory cannot: “This space has a hole that is not merely absence of a simplex but witnessed impossibility of one. The obstruction is structure, not lack of structure.” The ruptured simplicial set is the formal home for obstructions.

CHAPTER 4   Ruptured Fibrations

The base has paths. The fiber has points.

Whether points travel along paths—that is the question.

— The fibration problem

A type family
B:A\to\mathcal{U}
in HoTT is modeled by a Kan fibration
p:E\to B
. The Kan condition on fibrations guarantees that paths in the base lift to paths in the total space—this is what makes transport work. In OHTT, we drop this guarantee. Paths may lift, or they may be witnessed as non-lifting. This chapter develops the theory of ruptured fibrations.

4.1  Kan Fibrations: Review

We recall the classical notion.

Definition 4.1

(Kan Fibration)
.

A map
p:E\to B
of simplicial sets is a
Kan fibration
if it has the right lifting property against all horn inclusions. That is, for every commutative square:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda^{n}_{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\sigma}}

there exists a diagonal filler
\tilde{\sigma}
making both triangles commute.

The lifting property says: if we have a partial simplex upstairs (a horn in
E
) and we know how it should complete downstairs (a full simplex in
B
), then we can complete it upstairs.

Proposition 4.2

(Fibers of Kan Fibrations)
.

If
p:E\to B
is a Kan fibration and

, then the fiber
E_{b}=p^{-1}(b)
is a Kan complex.

Fibers are “spaces” in their own right. The total space

organizes these fiber-spaces into a coherent family over the base
B
.

Proposition 4.3

(Path Lifting)
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a Kan fibration. Given

with

, and a path
\gamma:b\to b^{\prime}
in

, there exists a path
\tilde{\gamma}:e\to e^{\prime}
in

with
p(\tilde{\gamma})=\gamma
.

This is the 1-dimensional case of the lifting property. The lifted path
\tilde{\gamma}
may not be unique, but it exists. This existence is what makes transport definable in HoTT.

4.2  Ruptured Fibrations

Definition 4.4

(Ruptured Fibration)
.

A
ruptured fibration
is a map
p:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{B}
of ruptured simplicial sets such that for every lifting problem:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda^{n}_{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}

) and
\sigma

1.

: There exists
\tilde{\sigma}\in\mathsf{Coh}^{E}_{n}
making the diagram commute

2.

Gapped lift
: The lifting horn
(h,\sigma)
is in
\mathsf{Gap}^{\mathcal{E}}

3.

: Neither coherent lift nor gap witness exists

The key difference from Kan fibrations: we do not require that coherent lifts always exist. They may, or they may be gapped, or the question may be open.

Remark 4.5
.

The phrase “lifting horn

together with the instruction that it should lift

4.2.1  Fibers of Ruptured Fibrations

Definition 4.6

(Fiber)
.

For a ruptured fibration

•

•

\mathsf{Coh}^{E_{b}}_{n}=\mathsf{Coh}^{E}_{n}\cap(E_{b})_{n}

•

\mathsf{Gap}^{E_{b}}_{n,k}=\mathsf{Gap}^{E}_{n,k}
restricted to horns lying in

Proposition 4.7

(Fibers are Ruptured Kan Complexes)
.

If

Horns in

are horns in
\mathcal{E}

in

∎

4.2.2  Path Lifting in Ruptured Fibrations

Proposition 4.8

(Ruptured Path Lifting)
.

Let

2.

This is the

This proposition is the semantic content of the transport horn. Case (1) is coherent transport; case (2) is gapped transport; case (3) is open transport.

4.3  Transport in Ruptured Fibrations

We now make the connection to OHTT transport fully explicit.

Definition 4.9

(Transport Operation)
.

Let

4.5  Composition of Ruptured Fibrations

Proposition 4.14

(Composition)

A lifting problem for

Gapped if either step is gapped

The exclusion condition is preserved by this logic.
∎

This proposition shows that ruptured fibrations form a reasonable class: they are closed under composition, so we can build complex fibrations from simple ones.

(Soundness)

4.7  Higher Structure

Just as Kan complexes model
\infty
-groupoids, ruptured Kan complexes model “ruptured

-groupoids”—higher categorical structures where some composites exist, some are gapped, and some are undetermined.

Definition 4.17

(Ruptured

-Groupoid)
.

A

viewed as a higher categorical structure:

•

•

•

In an ordinary

Example 4.18

(Non-Invertible Path)
.

In an ordinary Kan complex, every path

(the path is coherent)

This example shows that ruptured

4.8  Summary

Ruptured fibrations are maps of ruptured simplicial sets where the lifting property is
ruptured

•

Lifts may be coherent (transport succeeds)

•

Lifts may be gapped (transport fails, with witness)

•

Lifts may be open (transport is undetermined)

Key results:

•

Fibers of ruptured fibrations are ruptured Kan complexes

•

Path lifting becomes three-valued: coherent, gapped, or open

•

Transport is a partial operation, undefined when gapped

•

Ruptured fibrations compose

•

OHTT is sound with respect to this semantics

The ruptured fibration is the semantic model for type families in OHTT. Where HoTT guarantees transport, OHTT allows transport to be witnessed as blocked. The transport horn is the syntactic record of this semantic phenomenon.

Application.

What this gives us: a geometric universe where not every path lifts, not every homotopy exists, not every composite forms—and where these failures are not mere absence but structured, witnessed obstruction. This is the geometry of meaning, where gaps are as real as connections.

Part III
Three Obstructions

CHAPTER 5   Topological Obstruction

The loop closes in the base.

In the fiber, it does not return.

— Monodromy

We now apply OHTT to its most classical domain: the topology of fiber bundles. The phenomenon of monodromy—where loops in the base space fail to lift to loops in the total space—is precisely a transport horn. This chapter develops the theory in detail, showing that OHTT provides a natural language for obstructions that classical homotopy theory treats as external data.

5.1  Covering Spaces

We begin with the simplest case: covering spaces, where fibers are discrete.

Definition 5.1

(Covering Space)
.

A
covering space
is a map
p:E\to B
of topological spaces such that every point
b\in B
has an open neighborhood
U
with
p^{-1}(U)\cong U\times F
for some discrete set
F
(the fiber), and
p
corresponds to projection onto the first factor.

Covering spaces are fiber bundles with discrete fibers. The key property is the
unique path lifting
:

Proposition 5.2

(Unique Path Lifting)
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a covering space. For any path
\gamma:[0,1]\to B
and any point
e\in E
with
p(e)=\gamma(0)
, there exists a unique path
\tilde{\gamma}:[0,1]\to E
with
\tilde{\gamma}(0)=e
and
p\circ\tilde{\gamma}=\gamma
.

The lift exists and is unique. But—and this is the key point—the lifted path may not end where we expect.

5.1.1  Monodromy

Definition 5.3

(Monodromy Action)
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a covering space with
B
path-connected. Fix a basepoint
b\in B
and let
F=p^{-1}(b)
be the fiber. The
monodromy action
is the group homomorphism:

\mu:\pi_{1}(B,b)\to\mathrm{Aut}(F)

defined as follows: for a loop
[\gamma]\in\pi_{1}(B,b)
, lift
\gamma
starting at each
e\in F
, and let
\mu([\gamma])(e)=\tilde{\gamma}(1)
.

The monodromy action is well-defined (independent of the representative
\gamma
) and is a group homomorphism. It measures how the fiber “twists” as we go around loops in the base.

Example 5.4

(Circle Double Cover)
.

Let
B=S^{1}
and let
p:E\to S^{1}
be the double cover (explicitly:
E=S^{1}
and
p(z)=z^{2}
in complex notation). The fiber over any point has two elements.

The fundamental group
\pi_{1}(S^{1})\cong\mathbb{Z}
is generated by the loop
\gamma
going once around. The monodromy action sends
\gamma
to the non-trivial permutation of the two-element fiber: the swap
(e_{1}\;e_{2})
.

Thus: a loop in the base lifts to a path that
switches
the two sheets. Going around twice returns to the starting point.

Example 5.5

(Möbius Strip)
.

Let
E
be the boundary of a Möbius strip (a circle that wraps twice around the core). The projection
p:E\to S^{1}
to the core circle is a double cover, equivalent to the previous example.

Geometrically: walking once around the Möbius strip, an ant on the boundary ends up on the “opposite side”—the other sheet of the cover.

5.2  Monodromy as Transport Horn

We now interpret monodromy in OHTT.

Let
p:E\to B
be a covering space, viewed as a ruptured fibration (Chapter
4
). The base
B
and total space
E
are Kan complexes (simplicial models of the topological spaces). We impose ruptured structure as follows.

Definition 5.6

(Monodromy-Ruptured Cover)
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a covering space with monodromy action
\mu
. The
monodromy-ruptured structure
on
p
is:

•

\mathsf{Coh}^{E}
contains all simplices of
E
(the topological structure is fully coherent)

•

\mathsf{Coh}^{B}
contains all simplices of
B

•

\mathsf{Gap}
contains the following lifting problems: for a loop
\gamma:b\to b
in
B
and a point

, the lifting problem “lift
\gamma
to a loop based at
e
” is

if
\mu([\gamma])(e)\neq e

In other words: we mark as gapped the lifting problems where monodromy obstructs.

Theorem 5.7

(Monodromy Transport Horn)
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a covering space with non-trivial monodromy. Let
\gamma
be a loop in
B
based at
b
, and let

be a point with
\mu([\gamma])(e)\neq e

\Lambda_{\mathsf{tr}}(E,e,\gamma)

is inhabited. Explicitly:

1.

\vdash^{\!+}(e\in E_{b})
: the point

is coherent in the fiber

2.

\vdash^{\!+}(\gamma:b\to b)
: the loop
\gamma
is coherent in the base

3.

\vdash^{\!-}(\text{``$\gamma$ lifts to a loop at $e$''})
: the loop-lift is gapped

Proof.

The path
\gamma
lifts uniquely to a path
\tilde{\gamma}
starting at
e
, but
\tilde{\gamma}(1)=\mu([\gamma])(e)\neq e
. Thus
\tilde{\gamma}
is not a loop based at

. The lifting problem “find a loop in

based at

\gamma

\mathsf{Gap}
.
∎

This theorem shows that monodromy is not external data attached to a covering space—it
is
the gap structure of the covering viewed as a ruptured fibration.

5.2.1  The Gap Witness

What is the gap witness, concretely?

Proposition 5.8

(Gap Witness as Deck Transformation)
.

In the monodromy transport horn, the gap witness
\omega
can be identified with the deck transformation
\mu([\gamma])\in\mathrm{Aut}(F)
.

Proof.

The deck transformation
\mu([\gamma])
is precisely the data of “how the fiber permutes under the loop
\gamma

\mu([\gamma])(e)
, not at

. The gap witness is the deck transformation itself.
∎

This illustrates the OHTT principle:
gap witnesses are structure, not absence
. The monodromy is positive data that explains why lifting fails.

5.3  Higher Covering Spaces

The theory extends to higher homotopy.

Definition 5.9

(Principal
G
-Bundle)
.

A

with fiber
G

G

Covering spaces are principal bundles with
G
discrete. For non-discrete

Definition 5.10

(Holonomy)
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a principal
G

holonomy
around a loop

is the element
g\in G
such that parallel transport around
\gamma

g
.

Holonomy generalizes monodromy to bundles with connection. The connection specifies
how
to lift infinitesimally; the holonomy measures the global failure to return.

Theorem 5.11

(Holonomy Transport Horn)
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a principal
G
-bundle with connection, and let
\gamma
be a loop in

\Lambda_{\mathsf{tr}}(E,e,\gamma)

.

The holonomy—like monodromy—is not auxiliary data but the gap structure of the bundle.

5.4  Obstruction Theory

Classical obstruction theory studies when maps or sections can be extended. OHTT provides a natural reformulation.

Definition 5.12

(Lifting Problem)
.

Given a fibration
p:E\to B
and a map
f:X\to B
, a
lift
of
f
is a map
\tilde{f}:X\to E
with
p\circ\tilde{f}=f
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{f}}

Classical obstruction theory proceeds by skeletal induction: try to lift over the 0-skeleton, then the 1-skeleton, etc. At each stage, obstructions may arise.

Definition 5.13

(Obstruction Cocycle)
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a fibration with fiber
F
, and let
f:X\to B
be a map. Suppose a lift
\tilde{f}^{(n-1)}
exists over the
(n-1)
-skeleton of

. The

measures the failure to extend to the
n
-skeleton.

If
o_{n}(f)\neq 0
, no lift over the
n
-skeleton exists.

Theorem 5.14

(Obstruction as Gap Structure)
.

The obstruction cocycle
o_{n}(f)
corresponds to gap witnesses in the ruptured fibration structure on
p
. Specifically:

1.

A non-zero obstruction
o_{n}(f)
corresponds to a family of gapped lifting horns

2.

The cohomology class
[o_{n}(f)]
is the “collective gap witness” for the failure to extend

Proof sketch.

Each
n
-cell
\sigma
of
X
maps via
f
to an
n
-simplex in
B
. The lift over
\partial\sigma
gives a horn in
E
. If the horn cannot be filled coherently, we have a gap. The obstruction cocycle collects these local gaps into a global cohomological invariant.
∎

This theorem shows that obstruction theory—often treated as a computational tool—is fundamentally about gap structure in ruptured fibrations.

5.5  The Hopf Fibration

We examine a non-trivial example in detail.

Definition 5.15

(Hopf Fibration)
.

The
Hopf fibration
is the map
p:S^{3}\to S^{2}
defined by viewing
S^{3}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
and
S^{2}\cong\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, with
p(z_{1},z_{2})=[z_{1}:z_{2}]
.

The Hopf fibration has fiber
S^{1}
. It is a principal
S^{1}
-bundle over
S^{2}
.

Proposition 5.16

(Hopf Fibration is Non-Trivial)
.

The Hopf fibration does not admit a global section. Equivalently,
S^{3}
is not homeomorphic to
S^{2}\times S^{1}
.

Proof.

If a section existed, we would have
S^{3}\cong S^{2}\times S^{1}
. But
\pi_{1}(S^{3})=0
while
\pi_{1}(S^{2}\times S^{1})\cong\mathbb{Z}
. Contradiction.
∎

In OHTT terms:

Theorem 5.17

(Hopf Section Horn)
.

The Hopf fibration, viewed as a ruptured fibration, has the following structure:

1.

All fibers are coherent (each fiber is a circle, fully present)

2.

The base
S^{2}
is coherent

3.

The global section problem is gapped: there is no coherent section
S^{2}\to S^{3}

The obstruction to a global section lives in
H^{2}(S^{2};\pi_{1}(S^{1}))\cong H^{2}(S^{2};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
. The Hopf fibration has obstruction class
1\in\mathbb{Z}
—it is “maximally obstructed” in a precise sense.

Application.

The Hopf fibration is often presented as a curiosity—a strange way to wrap
S^{3}
around
S^{2}
. In OHTT, its strangeness is made structural: it is a ruptured fibration where the global section horn is gapped. The gap witness is the generator of
H^{2}(S^{2};\mathbb{Z})
.

5.6  Characteristic Classes

Characteristic classes are cohomological invariants of fiber bundles. In OHTT, they measure gap structure.

Definition 5.18

(Characteristic Class)
.

A
characteristic class
for
G
-bundles is a natural transformation from the functor of
G
-bundles over
X
to the cohomology
H^{*}(X)
. Common examples:

•

Chern classes

c_{i}\in H^{2i}(X;\mathbb{Z})
for complex vector bundles

•

Stiefel-Whitney classes

w_{i}\in H^{i}(X;\mathbb{Z}/2)
for real vector bundles

•

Pontryagin classes

p_{i}\in H^{4i}(X;\mathbb{Z})
for real vector bundles

•

Euler class

Principle 5.1

(Characteristic Classes as Gap Invariants)
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a fiber bundle with characteristic class

. Then:

1.

\chi=0
typically corresponds to a certain family of lifting problems being coherently solvable

2.

\chi\neq 0
witnesses collective gap structure in the ruptured fibration

The precise correspondence depends on the obstruction-theoretic setup (Postnikov tower, reduction of structure group, etc.).

Example 5.19

(Euler Class and Sections)
.

For an oriented
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-bundle
p:E\to B
, the Euler class
e(E)\in H^{n}(B;\mathbb{Z})
obstructs nowhere-zero sections. If

The hairy ball theorem (no nowhere-zero vector field on

has Euler class

Characteristic classes are global invariants of gap structure. They measure how “twisted” a bundle is—equivalently, how many transport horns it contains.

5.7  Summary

Topological obstruction theory is naturally expressed in OHTT:

•

Monodromy
is the gap structure of covering spaces. The gap witness is the deck transformation.

•

Holonomy
generalizes monodromy to bundles with connection. The gap witness is the holonomy element.

•

Obstruction cocycles
correspond to families of gap-witnessed lifting horns. The cohomology class collects local gaps into a global invariant.

•

Characteristic classes
measure collective gap structure. Non-vanishing characteristic classes witness that certain section or lifting problems are gapped.

What OHTT provides is not new theorems about topology, but a
reformulation
that makes obstruction first-class. In HoTT, obstructions are external: we compute them, but they are not part of the type-theoretic structure. In OHTT, obstructions are internal: they are gap witnesses in ruptured fibrations.

Application.

What this lets us say: “The Möbius strip is not just a space with a twist; the twist is a gap witness in the covering fibration. The Hopf map is not just a surjection
S^{3}\to S^{2}
; it is a ruptured fibration whose section problem is gapped with witness the generator of
H^{2}(S^{2})
.” Obstruction becomes data.

CHAPTER 6   Semantic Obstruction

The word is the same.

The meaning refuses to follow.

— Polysemy

We now turn to a less traditional domain: the formal semantics of language. The phenomenon of polysemy—where a single lexical form carries multiple, non-reducible meanings—is a transport horn. Lexical identity provides a path; meanings are fibers; the failure of meaning to transport along lexical identity is witnessed as gap. This chapter develops the mathematical framework.

6.1  The Semantic Fibration

We model meaning as a fibration over form.

Definition 6.1

(Lexical Space)
.

A
lexical space
is a simplicial set
L
whose:

•

0-simplices are
lexical tokens
—occurrences of words in context

•

1-simplices are
lexical relations
—paths connecting tokens

•

Higher simplices record coherence of lexical relations

The key lexical relation is
identity
: two tokens of “the same word.” We write
\ell\sim\ell^{\prime}
when
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
are tokens of the same lexeme.

Definition 6.2

(Meaning Fibration)
.

A
meaning fibration
is a Kan fibration
p:M\to L
where:

•

M
is the
meaning space

•

For each token
\ell\in L_{0}
, the fiber
M_{\ell}=p^{-1}(\ell)
is the space of
meanings
(senses) of
\ell

•

Paths in
M
are
meaning relations
—coherences between senses

In the standard (non-ruptured) setting, the Kan fibration property guarantees: if
\gamma:\ell\to\ell^{\prime}
is a lexical path and
m\in M_{\ell}
is a meaning, then there exists a meaning
m^{\prime}\in M_{\ell^{\prime}}
connected to
m
by a path lifting
\gamma
.

This is where polysemy becomes problematic.

6.1.1  The Polysemy Problem

Example 6.3

(Bank)
.

Consider the English word “bank.” Let:

•

\ell_{1}
= token of “bank” in “I deposited money at the bank”

•

\ell_{2}
= token of “bank” in “We picnicked on the river bank”

There is a lexical path
\gamma:\ell_{1}\to\ell_{2}
(they are tokens of the same lexeme).

Let
m_{1}\in M_{\ell_{1}}
be the meaning “financial institution.”

What is
\mathsf{transport}(\gamma,m_{1})
? The Kan condition would require a meaning
m_{2}\in M_{\ell_{2}}
connected to
m_{1}
. But “financial institution” is not a meaning of “bank” in the river context. The meanings are genuinely disjoint.

The Kan fibration model fails for polysemy. There is no coherent way to transport “financial institution” along the lexical identity path.

6.2  The Ruptured Meaning Fibration

We now apply OHTT.

Definition 6.4

(Ruptured Meaning Fibration)
.

A
ruptured meaning fibration
is a ruptured fibration
p:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{L}
where:

•

\mathcal{L}=(L,\mathsf{Coh}^{L},\mathsf{Gap}^{L})
is a ruptured simplicial set of lexical tokens

•

\mathcal{M}=(M,\mathsf{Coh}^{M},\mathsf{Gap}^{M})
is a ruptured simplicial set of meanings

•

Lifting problems
(m,\gamma)
may be coherent, gapped, or open

Definition 6.5

(Polysemy Horn)
.

Let
p:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{L}
be a ruptured meaning fibration. A
polysemy horn
is a transport horn:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathsf{poly}}(m,\gamma)\;\coloneqq\;\;

\displaystyle\vdash^{\!+}(m\in M_{\ell})

\displaystyle\times\;

\displaystyle\vdash^{\!+}(\gamma:\ell\to\ell^{\prime})

\displaystyle\times\;

\displaystyle\vdash^{\!-}(\mathsf{transport}(\gamma,m)\in M_{\ell^{\prime}})

where
\gamma
is a lexical identity path and
m
is a meaning that does not coherently transport along
\gamma
.

Theorem 6.6

(Polysemy as Transport Horn)
.

Let
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}
be tokens of the same polysemous lexeme, let
\gamma:\ell_{1}\to\ell_{2}
be the lexical identity path, and let
m_{1}\in M_{\ell_{1}}
be a meaning not shared by
\ell_{2}
. Then:

\Lambda_{\mathsf{poly}}(m_{1},\gamma)

is inhabited.

Proof.

By hypothesis,
m_{1}
is a coherent meaning of
\ell_{1}
, and
\gamma
is a coherent lexical path. But there is no meaning in
M_{\ell_{2}}
to which
m_{1}
transports—the fibers are disjoint at this meaning. The lifting problem is gapped.
∎

6.2.1  The Gap Witness

What is the gap witness in a polysemy horn?

Definition 6.7

(Semantic Incompatibility)
.

A
semantic incompatibility witness
between meanings
m\in M_{\ell}
and
m^{\prime}\in M_{\ell^{\prime}}
is evidence that no path in
M
connects
m
to
m^{\prime}
over the lexical path
\gamma:\ell\to\ell^{\prime}
.

In concrete terms, the gap witness records:

•

The semantic features that
m
has and
m^{\prime}
lacks (or vice versa)

•

The conceptual domains that are incompatible

•

Any formal structure (ontological, taxonomic, inferential) that blocks connection

Example 6.8

(Bank Gap Witness)
.

For “bank,” the gap witness between “financial institution” and “river edge” might record:

•

Distinct conceptual domains: FINANCE vs. GEOGRAPHY

•

Incompatible taxonomic positions: INSTITUTION vs. LANDFORM

•

Non-overlapping inferential roles: one involves money, the other water

The gap witness is not merely “these meanings are different.” It is structured data explaining
why
transport fails.

6.3  Systematic Polysemy

Not all polysemy is complete rupture. Some polysemous words exhibit regular patterns.

Definition 6.9

(Systematic Polysemy)
.

A
systematic polysemy
is a regular pattern relating meanings across lexical items. Examples:

•

CONTAINER/CONTENTS: “The bottle fell” (container) vs. “Finish the bottle” (contents)

•

ORGANIZATION/BUILDING: “The company fired him” vs. “The company is on Main Street”

•

PROCESS/RESULT: “The construction took years” vs. “The construction is impressive”

Systematic polysemy is characterized by
partial
transport.

Definition 6.10

(Partial Transport)
.

Let
p:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{L}
be a ruptured meaning fibration. A lexical path
\gamma:\ell\to\ell^{\prime}
exhibits
partial transport
if:

•

Some meanings in
M_{\ell}
transport coherently along
\gamma

•

Other meanings in
M_{\ell}
are gapped for transport along
\gamma

Example 6.11

(Container/Contents)
.

Let
\ell_{1}
= “bottle” (container reading) and
\ell_{2}
= “bottle” (contents reading). The systematic polysemy provides a pattern
\Pi_{\text{C/C}}
relating these readings.

For the meaning
m_{\text{shape}}
= “cylindrical vessel shape”:

\vdash^{\!+}(\mathsf{transport}(\Pi_{\text{C/C}},m_{\text{shape}})\in M_{\ell_{2}})

The shape transports (the contents are in something with that shape).

For the meaning
m_{\text{material}}
= “made of glass”:

\vdash^{\!-}(\mathsf{transport}(\Pi_{\text{C/C}},m_{\text{material}})\in M_{\ell_{2}})

The material does not transport (the contents are not made of glass).

Systematic polysemy is thus a ruptured fibration with structured partial transport: some semantic features cross the gap, others do not.

6.4  Semantic Fields and Coherence

We now consider the global structure of meaning.

Definition 6.12

(Semantic Field)
.

A
semantic field
is a connected component of the meaning space
M
(or a connected subcomplex).

Meanings within a semantic field are connected by paths—they cohere. Meanings in different fields are not connected—there are gaps.

Proposition 6.13

(Field Structure)
.

In a ruptured meaning fibration
p:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{L}
:

1.

Within a semantic field, transport along short lexical paths is typically coherent

2.

Across semantic fields, transport is typically gapped

3.

Polysemy corresponds to lexical identity paths crossing field boundaries

Example 6.14

(Crane)
.

The word “crane” has meanings in three semantic fields:

•

ANIMAL: a large wading bird

•

MACHINE: a lifting device

•

ACTION: to stretch one’s neck

Within each field, meanings cohere (types of crane-birds, types of crane-machines). Across fields, transport is gapped—there is no coherent path from the bird to the machine in meaning space, despite the lexical identity.

6.4.1  Metaphor as Partial Coherence

Metaphor provides an interesting intermediate case.

Definition 6.15

(Metaphorical Bridge)
.

A
metaphorical bridge
between semantic fields
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
is a partially coherent path: some semantic features transport, others are gapped.

Example 6.16

(Crane Metaphor)
.

The machine “crane” derives from the bird by metaphor. The bridge carries:

•

SHAPE: long, thin, with a bend (coherent)

•

MOTION: lifting, extending (coherent)

•

ANIMACY: living creature (gapped—the machine is not alive)

•

HABITAT: wetlands (gapped—the machine is in construction sites)

Metaphorical bridges are thus ruptured paths: partially coherent, partially gapped. The coherent parts are the ground of the metaphor; the gapped parts are where the metaphor “breaks.”

6.5  Compositional Semantics

We extend the analysis to compositional (phrasal) meaning.

Definition 6.17

(Compositional Meaning Fibration)
.

A
compositional meaning fibration
is a ruptured fibration
p:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{S}
where:

•

\mathcal{S}
is a space of syntactic structures (phrases, sentences)

•

For each structure
s\in S_{0}
, the fiber
M_{s}
is the space of meanings of
s

•

Syntactic transformations induce paths in
\mathcal{S}

Example 6.18

(Scope Ambiguity)
.

Consider “Every student read a book.” This sentence has (at least) two meanings:

•

m_{1}
: Each student read some book (possibly different books)

•

m_{2}
: There is a single book that every student read

These arise from different scope configurations of the quantifiers. The surface syntax is identical, but the meanings are not connected by a coherent path—moving from
m_{1}
to
m_{2}
requires changing the logical structure.

In a ruptured meaning fibration, we can have both meanings in the fiber, with a gap between them:
\vdash^{\!-}(\text{path from }m_{1}\text{ to }m_{2})
.

Example 6.19

(Transformation Gaps)
.

Consider active-passive transformation:

•

s_{1}
= “The dog bit the man”

•

s_{2}
= “The man was bitten by the dog”

There is a syntactic path
\tau:s_{1}\to s_{2}
(the transformation). For the core propositional meaning, transport is coherent:

\vdash^{\!+}(\mathsf{transport}(\tau,m_{\text{core}})\in M_{s_{2}})

But for pragmatic features (focus, topic), transport may be gapped:

\vdash^{\!-}(\mathsf{transport}(\tau,m_{\text{focus}})\in M_{s_{2}})

The focused element differs between active and passive.

6.6  The Functoriality of Meaning

We revisit functoriality (Chapter
2
) in the semantic context.

Theorem 6.20

(Semantic Drift)
.

Let
\gamma_{1}:\ell_{0}\to\ell_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}:\ell_{1}\to\ell_{2}
be lexical paths (e.g., steps in historical change or metaphorical extension). Let
m\in M_{\ell_{0}}
. It is possible that:

1.

\mathsf{transport}(\gamma_{1},m)
is coherent (some meaning transports to
\ell_{1}
)

2.

\mathsf{transport}(\gamma_{2},\mathsf{transport}(\gamma_{1},m))
is coherent (it further transports to
\ell_{2}
)

3.

\mathsf{transport}(\gamma_{2}\cdot\gamma_{1},m)
is gapped (direct transport from
\ell_{0}
to
\ell_{2}
fails)

This is the semantic analog of cumulative obstruction. Meaning can travel stepwise through intermediate forms but “lose track” of its origin.

Example 6.21

(Nice)
.

The English word “nice” has drifted through meanings:

•

Latin
nescius
“ignorant”
\to

•

Old French
nice
“foolish, simple”
\to

•

Middle English “coy, reserved”
\to

•

Early Modern English “precise, fastidious”
\to

•

Modern English “pleasant, agreeable”

At each step, some features transport. But direct transport from “ignorant” to “pleasant” is gapped—there is no coherent semantic path preserving meaning features across the whole chain.

6.7  Semantic Coherence as Kan Condition

We can now state precisely what it means for a semantic domain to be “well-behaved.”

Definition 6.22

(Semantically Coherent Domain)
.

A
semantically coherent domain
is a ruptured meaning fibration
p:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{L}
where
\mathsf{Gap}=\emptyset
—i.e., all transport is coherent.

This is rare. Most natural language meaning is not globally coherent; polysemy, metaphor, and drift introduce gaps.

Definition 6.23

(Locally Coherent Domain)
.

A
locally coherent domain
is a ruptured meaning fibration where:

•

Within each semantic field, transport is coherent

•

Across field boundaries, transport may be gapped

Most semantic domains are locally coherent: technical vocabularies, tightly defined ontologies, formal languages. Natural language as a whole is not even locally coherent—polysemy creates gaps within what might seem like single fields.

6.8  Summary

Semantic structure is naturally modeled as a ruptured meaning fibration:

•

Polysemy
is the transport horn: lexical identity path with gapped meaning transport

•

Systematic polysemy
is partial transport: some features cross, others gap

•

Semantic fields
are coherent components of meaning space

•

Metaphor
is a partially coherent bridge between fields

•

Semantic drift
is failure of functoriality: stepwise transport with gapped composite

The gap witness in semantic horns is structured: it records which features fail to transport, which domains are incompatible, why meaning diverges despite formal identity.

What OHTT provides is not a computational theory of meaning—that requires additional machinery. It provides a
structural
theory: a framework for saying precisely where meaning coheres and where it ruptures, with rupture as positive data rather than mere failure.

Application.

What this lets us say: “The word ‘bank’ is not ambiguous in the sense of having multiple values for a single variable. It is polysemous in the sense of inhabiting a ruptured fibration: the lexical identity exists, the meanings exist, and between them is a witnessed gap. The gap is as much part of the structure as the meanings themselves.”

CHAPTER 7   Logical Obstruction

The substitution is valid.

The derivation does not follow.

— Resource failure

We turn finally to logic itself. The phenomenon of certified underivability—where we can prove that a judgment is
not
derivable, not merely that we have failed to derive it—is a transport horn. Substitutions provide paths between contexts; derivable judgments are fibers; the failure of derivability to transport along substitution is witnessed as gap. This chapter develops the framework for resource-sensitive and substructural logics.

7.1  Derivability as Fibration

We begin by modeling the structure of a type theory or logic as a fibration.

Definition 7.1

(Context Category)
.

Let
\mathbb{C}
be the category of contexts for a type theory, with:

•

Objects: contexts
\Gamma,\Delta,\Xi,\ldots

•

Morphisms: substitutions
\sigma:\Delta\to\Gamma
(context morphisms)

Substitutions act on judgments: if
\Gamma\vdash J
is a judgment in context
\Gamma
and
\sigma:\Delta\to\Gamma
is a substitution, then
J[\sigma]
is the judgment in context
\Delta
obtained by applying
\sigma
.

Definition 7.2

(Derivability Fibration)
.

The
derivability fibration
is a functor
\mathcal{D}:\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}
(or
\mathbf{Kan}
for the simplicial version) where:

•

\mathcal{D}(\Gamma)
is the set (or space) of judgments derivable in context
\Gamma

•

For
\sigma:\Delta\to\Gamma
, the map
\mathcal{D}(\sigma):\mathcal{D}(\Gamma)\to\mathcal{D}(\Delta)
sends
J
to
J[\sigma]

For a well-behaved type theory (MLTT, HoTT), the derivability fibration is a “Kan object”: substitution preserves derivability. This is the content of the
substitution lemma
.

Lemma 7.3

(Substitution)
.

In MLTT: if
\Gamma\vdash t:A
and
\sigma:\Delta\to\Gamma
is a valid substitution, then
\Delta\vdash t[\sigma]:A[\sigma]
.

The substitution lemma says: derivability transports along substitution. In our language: the derivability fibration is a Kan fibration.

7.2  Resource-Sensitive Failure

The substitution lemma fails in resource-sensitive logics.

Definition 7.4

(Resource-Sensitive Context)
.

A
resource-sensitive context
is a context
\Gamma
where variables carry resource annotations:

•

Linear: must be used exactly once

•

Affine: may be used at most once

•

Relevant: must be used at least once

•

Exponential: may be used arbitrarily (the standard case)

In linear type theory, a substitution
\sigma:\Delta\to\Gamma
may fail to preserve derivability if it duplicates or discards linear resources.

Example 7.5

(Linear Failure)
.

Let
\Gamma=x:A
(linear) and let
J=(x,x):A\times A
.

In ordinary type theory,
J
is derivable if
\Gamma=x:A
. But in linear type theory,
J
requires using
x
twice—violating linearity. So
\Gamma\vdash J
is
not
derivable.

Now let
\sigma:\Delta\to\Gamma
where
\Delta=y:A,z:A
and
\sigma=[x\mapsto y]
.

Then
J[\sigma]=(y,y):A\times A
is still underivable in
\Delta
—we still use

The point: derivability depends on resource structure, not just the term.

Definition 7.6

•

\Lambda_{\mathsf{deriv}}(J,\sigma)\;\coloneqq\;\vdash^{\!+}(\Gamma\vdash J)\times\vdash^{\!+}(\sigma:\Delta\to\Gamma)\times\vdash^{\!-}(\Delta\vdash J[\sigma])

not

7.3  Certified Underivability

The gap in a derivability horn is not “we haven’t found a proof.” It is a
certificate
that no proof exists.

Definition 7.8

(Underivability Certificate)
.

An

for a judgment
\Gamma\vdash J

J
in
\Gamma

Underivability certificates arise in several ways:

1.

Resource exhaustion
: The required resources are not available in the context

2.

Type mismatch
: The judgment is ill-typed in the context

3.

Logical impossibility
: The judgment contradicts the context

4.

Decidability
: A decision procedure returns “no”

Example 7.9

(Linear Underivability Certificate)
.

For the judgment

in linear type theory, the underivability certificate is:

“The term
(x,x)
requires two uses of the linear variable

. The context provides exactly one. By the resource-counting invariant, no derivation exists.”

This certificate is positive data—a proof of non-derivability, not merely absence of a derivability proof.

7.4  Substructural Logics

The phenomena generalize across substructural logics.

A

In a substructural logic:

2.

Failure of contraction creates horns: a judgment derivable with

Proof.

Each structural rule, when valid, provides a substitution-like operation. When the rule is restricted, the corresponding transport may fail. The failure is witnessed by the rule violation.
∎

7.5  Modal Type Theory

Modal type theories provide another source of transport horns.

A

7.6  Dependent Type Theory with Constraints

We consider dependent types augmented with constraints.

7.7  Proof Irrelevance and Truncation

Even in standard HoTT, certain constructions create transport-like phenomena.

(Propositional Truncation)

•

Truncation creates obstructions to “escaping”:

(Truncation Obstruction)

Let

.

If

Example 7.21

(Choice)
.

The axiom of choice (in type-theoretic form) says:

\left(\prod_{x:A}\|B(x)\|\right)\to\left\|\prod_{x:A}B(x)\right\|

This is not provable in HoTT without additional axioms. The obstruction is that we cannot coherently lift the family of truncated witnesses to a single witness.

In OHTT, we can express this as: the section problem for a certain fibration over

7.8  Decidability and Gaps

When a type theory has decidable type-checking, underivability can always be certified.

Theorem 7.22

(Decidability Implies Gap Witnesses)
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a type theory with decidable type-checking. Then for any context
\Gamma
and judgment
J
:

1.

Either
\Gamma\vdash J
is derivable, or

2.

There exists a gap witness (the “no” output of the decision procedure)

Thus the ruptured derivability fibration has no open judgments: every judgment is either coherent or gapped.

Proof.

Run the decision procedure. If it returns “yes,” we have a derivation (coherence witness). If it returns “no,” we have a certificate of underivability (gap witness).
∎

For undecidable type theories (most interesting ones), open judgments exist: we may not know whether a given judgment is derivable.

7.9  Summary

Logical structure is naturally modeled as a ruptured derivability fibration:

•

Resource failure
creates transport horns: derivable judgments may not transport along substitutions that mishandle resources

•

Substructural restrictions
create systematic gaps: failures of weakening, contraction, exchange are witnessed obstructions

•

Modal constraints
create transport horns: modality-respecting operations may block derivability

•

Constraint violations
are gap witnesses: substitutions that violate refinement constraints are witnessed as failing

•

Truncation
creates obstructions: escape from propositional truncation is gapped for non-propositional targets

•

Decidability
eliminates openness: decidable theories have only coherent or gapped judgments, no open ones

The gap witnesses in logical horns are formal certificates: resource counts, constraint violations, modal mismatches, decision procedure outputs. They are
proofs of underivability
, not mere absence of proofs.

What OHTT provides is a uniform framework for these phenomena. Resource-sensitive, substructural, modal, and constrained type theories all exhibit transport failure—and OHTT makes this failure first-class, witnessed, and structured.

Application.

What this lets us say: “The substitution lemma is not a universal truth about type theory. It is a property of the coherent fragment. In the full ruptured structure, substitution may fail to preserve derivability—and this failure is as mathematically tractable as success. Underivability is not silence; it is testimony.”

CHAPTER 8   OHTT and HoTT

The same letters, rearranged.

The same structures, ruptured.

— The relation

We now make precise the relationship between Open Horn Type Theory and Homotopy Type Theory. The slogan is simple: HoTT is the coherent fragment of OHTT. But the details illuminate what each theory can and cannot express.

8.1  The Relationship

OHTT and HoTT share the same raw syntax of dependent type theory, but differ in what they guarantee:

1.

HoTT
includes total transport as a theorem: given
t:B(x)
and
p:x=_{A}y
, the term
\mathsf{transport}(p,t):B(y)
is always derivable. This corresponds semantically to the Kan filling condition.

2.

OHTT
does not assume total transport. Transport may succeed (coherent), fail with witness (gapped), or be undetermined (open). This corresponds to dropping the Kan condition.

HoTT is thus recovered from OHTT by adjoining a totality principle ensuring all relevant horns have coherent fillers.

This is the precise sense of the slogan:
OHTT = HoTT
-
Kan Filling + Gap Witnesses
.

8.1.1  The Coherent Fragment

Definition 8.1

(Coherent Fragment)
.

The
coherent fragment
of OHTT is the subcategory
\mathbf{OHTT}^{+}
consisting of:

•

Contexts
\Gamma
containing only
\vdash^{+}
declarations

•

Judgments of the form
\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}J

•

Derivations using only rules that produce
\vdash^{+}
conclusions from
\vdash^{+}
premises

Proposition 8.2

(Coherent Fragment and HoTT)
.

The coherent fragment
\mathbf{OHTT}^{+}
is a weakening of
\mathbf{HoTT}
: every
\mathbf{OHTT}^{+}
derivation is valid, but
\mathbf{OHTT}^{+}
does not include the totality principles (transport, Kan filling) that HoTT provides. Adding those principles recovers HoTT.

Thus:
HoTT is exactly the part of OHTT that never mentions gaps, augmented with totality.

8.2  What HoTT Cannot Say

We enumerate the expressive limitations of HoTT that OHTT overcomes.

Proposition 8.3

(No Witnessed Non-Identity)
.

In HoTT, there is no judgment form for “
x
and
y
are witnessed as non-identical.” The closest approximation is:

\neg(x=_{A}y)\;\coloneqq\;(x=_{A}y)\to\mathbf{0}

which says: “any path from
x
to
y
leads to absurdity.”

This is weaker than witnessed non-identity:

•

\neg(x=_{A}y)
is a
function type
—to inhabit it, we must construct a function

•

\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}(p:x=_{A}y)
is a
direct witness
—positive evidence of non-coherence

Proposition 8.4

(No Transport Failure)
.

In HoTT, given
t:B(x)
and
p:x=_{A}y
, the term
\mathsf{transport}(p,t):B(y)
is always well-defined. There is no way to express “transport fails.”

In OHTT, we can have
\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}(\mathsf{transport}(p,t):B(y))
—witnessed transport failure.

Proposition 8.5

(No Obstruction Data)
.

In HoTT, obstructions (monodromy, characteristic classes, etc.) are computed externally and represented as ordinary types or terms. There is no internal representation of “this horn is unfillable.”

In OHTT, gap witnesses
are
obstruction data—first-class terms recording why coherence fails.

8.3  The Kan Condition

The technical heart of the difference is the Kan condition.

Definition 8.6

(Kan Condition (Semantic))
.

A simplicial set
X
satisfies the
Kan condition
if every horn has a filler:

\forall n\geq 1,\;\forall 0\leq k\leq n,\;\forall h:\Lambda^{n}_{k}\to X,\;\exists\sigma:\Delta^{n}\to X\text{ extending }h

Theorem 8.7

(HoTT Models are Kan)
.

In the simplicial model of HoTT, every type is interpreted as a Kan complex. The Kan condition is what makes:

•

Path inversion work (every path has an inverse)

•

Path composition work (paths compose)

•

Transport work (terms move along paths)

•

Higher coherences work (homotopies exist when needed)

Theorem 8.8

(OHTT Models are Ruptured Kan)
.

In the simplicial model of OHTT, types are interpreted as ruptured Kan complexes. The Kan condition is replaced by the trichotomy: every horn is coherently filled, gap-witnessed, or open.

The Kan condition is the
totality assumption
that HoTT makes. OHTT drops this assumption.

8.4  Conservativity

Theorem 8.9

(OHTT is Conservative over HoTT)
.

If
J
is a judgment expressible in HoTT and
\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}J
is derivable in OHTT (where
\Gamma
is a coherent context), then
\Gamma\vdash J
is derivable in HoTT.

Proof sketch.

OHTT adds gap judgments and horn types, but does not change the rules for deriving coherence judgments. A derivation of
\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}J
in OHTT that uses only coherence rules is exactly a HoTT derivation.
∎

Conservativity means: OHTT does not prove new coherence facts about HoTT-expressible judgments. What it adds is the ability to express and prove
gap
facts.

8.5  The Univalence Axiom

Univalence is the characteristic axiom of HoTT:

\mathsf{ua}:(A\simeq B)\to(A=_{\mathcal{U}}B)

Together with function extensionality, it implies that equivalences
are
identities (in the universe).

Proposition 8.10

(Univalence in OHTT)
.

Univalence can be adopted in OHTT as an axiom about coherence:

\vdash^{\!+}\mathsf{ua}:(A\simeq B)\to(A=_{\mathcal{U}}B)

This asserts that every equivalence coherently induces a path.

With univalence, OHTT includes HoTT as its coherent fragment. Without univalence, OHTT includes MLTT.

Remark 8.11
.

Univalence does not eliminate gaps. Even with univalence, we can have:

•

Gapped paths:
\vdash^{\!-}(p:x=_{A}y)
for non-equivalent
x,y

•

Gapped transport:
\vdash^{\!-}(\mathsf{transport}(p,t):B(y))
when transport is obstructed

•

Gapped equivalences:
\vdash^{\!-}(e:A\simeq B)
for non-equivalent types

Univalence tells us that equivalences give paths; it does not tell us which paths or equivalences exist.

8.6  Higher Inductive Types

Higher inductive types (HITs) are types specified with path constructors, not just point constructors.

(HITs in OHTT)

(Ruptured Higher Inductive Type)
.

A

is a type specified with:

•

Example 8.15

(Ruptured Interval)

8.8  Summary

The relationship between OHTT and HoTT:

•

Embedding
: HoTT embeds faithfully in OHTT via

•

Coherent fragment
: HoTT is exactly the

HoTT is OHTT with Gap

. Or equivalently:
OHTT is HoTT without the Kan condition
.

Application.

The relationship is not antagonistic. HoTT is the right theory for reasoning about spaces where all paths exist and all homotopies cohere. One does not replace the other; they are the coherent and ruptured fragments of a single larger theory.

CHAPTER 9   Negation and Paraconsistency

Gap is not negation.

Negation is not gap.

— The distinction

We now situate OHTT relative to logical traditions that have grappled with negation, contradiction, and non-classical truth values. The gap judgment
\vdash^{-}
is none of the standard notions of negation—but it has relatives in the literature that illuminate its nature.

9.1  Classical Negation

Definition 9.1

(Classical Negation)
.

In classical logic, negation
\neg P
is characterized by:

•

Excluded middle
:
P\vee\neg P
is a theorem

•

Double negation elimination
:
\neg\neg P\to P
is a theorem

•

Semantic
:
\neg P
is true iff
P
is false

Classical negation is the
complement
of truth:
\neg P
holds exactly when
P
fails.

Proposition 9.2

(Gap is not Classical Negation)
.

In OHTT:

1.

There is no excluded middle: a judgment may be neither coherent nor gapped (it may be open)

2.

There is no double negation elimination:
\vdash^{\!-}\vdash^{\!-}J
(a gap-of-gap, if it made sense) does not give
\vdash^{\!+}J

3.

Gap is not the complement of coherence: open judgments are neither

The trichotomy (coherent / gapped / open) is fundamentally incompatible with classical two-valued logic.

9.2  Intuitionistic Negation

Definition 9.3

(Intuitionistic Negation)
.

In intuitionistic logic, negation is defined:

\neg P\;\coloneqq\;P\to\bot

A proof of
\neg P
is a function that transforms any proof of
P
into a proof of absurdity.

Intuitionistic negation is
constructive
: to prove
\neg P
, we must show that
P
leads to contradiction. But it is also
derivative
: negation is defined in terms of implication and absurdity.

Proposition 9.4

(Gap is not Intuitionistic Negation)
.

In OHTT:

1.

Gap is primitive, not defined via implication

2.

\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}J
does not mean “
\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}J
implies absurdity”

3.

A gap witness is direct evidence, not a refutation function

The difference is subtle but crucial:

•

Intuitionistic
\neg P
: “Show me a proof of
P
, and I’ll derive a contradiction”

•

OHTT
\vdash^{\!-}J
: “I have witnessed that
J
does not cohere” (no hypothetical, no derivation)

9.2.1  Strong Negation

The closest intuitionistic relative is Nelson’s
strong negation
.

Definition 9.5

(Strong Negation)
.

In Nelson’s constructive logic with strong negation, there are two negations:

•

Weak negation
:
\neg P\coloneqq P\to\bot
(standard intuitionistic)

•

Strong negation
:
\sim P
(primitive, with its own rules)

Strong negation satisfies:
\sim\sim P\leftrightarrow P
and
\sim(P\wedge Q)\leftrightarrow(\sim P\vee\sim Q)
.

Proposition 9.6

(Gap resembles Strong Negation)
.

OHTT gap has affinities with strong negation:

1.

Both are primitive (not defined via implication)

2.

Both provide “positive” refutation

3.

Both coexist with a weaker notion

However, gap differs:

1.

Gap is proof-relevant (different gap witnesses may exist)

2.

Gap is at the judgment level, not the proposition level

3.

Gap creates horns (compositional tension), not just negated formulas

Nelson’s logic is perhaps the closest classical precedent to OHTT’s gap—but OHTT embeds the idea in a type-theoretic, proof-relevant, higher-categorical framework.

9.3  Paraconsistent Logic

Paraconsistent logics allow contradictions without explosion.

Definition 9.7

(Paraconsistency)
.

A logic is
paraconsistent
if the principle of explosion fails:

P,\neg P\nvdash Q\quad\text{(for arbitrary $Q$)}

Contradictions are tolerated; they do not make everything true.

Proposition 9.8

(OHTT is not Paraconsistent)
.

OHTT is
not
paraconsistent:

1.

The Exclusion Law forbids
\Gamma\vdash^{\!+}J
and
\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}J

2.

There are no contradictions to tolerate—coherence and gap are mutually exclusive by axiom

The distinction:

•

Paraconsistent logics: “
P
and
\neg P
can both hold; we contain the damage”

•

OHTT: “Coherence and gap cannot both hold; but neither may hold (openness)”

Paraconsistency is about tolerating contradiction. OHTT is about the trichotomy
before
contradiction arises.

9.4  Many-Valued and Bilattice Logics

Definition 9.9

(Belnap’s Four-Valued Logic)
.

Belnap’s logic has four truth values:

•

: true (told true, not told false)

•

F
: false (told false, not told true)

•

: both (told both true and false)

•

: neither (told neither)

These form a bilattice with two orderings: truth order and information order.

Proposition 9.10

(OHTT and Belnap)
.

OHTT’s trichotomy resembles Belnap’s four values minus “both”:

OHTT

Belnap

Coherent (
\vdash^{+}
)

Gapped (
\vdash^{-}
)

—

The Exclusion Law rules out “both”—OHTT has three states, not four.

Belnap’s logic is designed for databases that may have contradictory information. OHTT is designed for type theory where contradiction is absurdity—but where
absence of information
(openness) is meaningful.

9.4.1  Information Order

Belnap’s bilattice has an

: N
<

<

Proposition 9.11

(OHTT Information Order)
.

OHTT has a natural information order:

\text{Open}\;<\;\text{Coherent},\quad\text{Open}\;<\;\text{Gapped}

Open judgments have less information (unwitnessed). Coherent and gapped judgments have more (witnessed). There is no “both” because Exclusion forbids it.

This partial order captures: witnessing (either coherence or gap) is information gain; openness is the information-minimal state.

9.5  Signed and Polarized Logics

Definition 9.12

(Signed Formula)

OHTT’s two turnstiles (

+J

•

\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}J
is like deriving
-J

Definition 9.13

(Polarized Logic)
.

In Girard’s polarized logic (LC, LU), formulas have polarity (positive/negative) governing their decomposition:

•

Positive formulas decompose eagerly (synchronous)

•

Negative formulas decompose lazily (asynchronous)

OHTT’s polarity is different: it marks the
witnessing mode
, not the formula’s logical structure. But the idea that judgments carry explicit polarity is shared.

9.6  Relevant Logic

Definition 9.14

(Relevant Logic)
.

Relevant logics require that premises be “used” in derivations:

P\to Q\text{ requires that }P\text{ be relevant to }Q

This blocks “paradoxes of implication” like
P\to(Q\to Q)
from arbitrary
P
.

Proposition 9.15

(OHTT and Relevance)
.

OHTT does not impose relevance constraints on coherence judgments. However:

1.

Gap witnesses are relevant: a gap witness for
J
must actually witness why
J
fails

2.

Horn inhabitants are relevant: the coherences and gap in a horn are structurally connected

Relevance in OHTT is semantic (built into what gap witnesses are) rather than syntactic (imposed by logical rules).

9.8  Summary

Gap is not negation:

•

Not classical
: no excluded middle, no complement semantics

•

Not intuitionistic
: primitive, not defined via
\to\bot

•

Not paraconsistent
: Exclusion forbids “both,” not tolerates it

•

Resembles strong negation
: primitive refutation, positive evidence

•

Resembles Belnap minus “both”
: three-valued, information-ordered

OHTT occupies a distinctive position: constructive (proof-relevant), three-valued (trichotomy), consistent (Exclusion), and compositional (horns). It is not a negation logic but a
witnessing
logic—where both success and failure are first-class data.

Application.

The point is not that OHTT is better than classical or intuitionistic logic. Each is suited to its domain. Classical logic is for mathematics where excluded middle holds. Intuitionistic logic is for constructive reasoning where proofs are programs. OHTT is for domains with intrinsic obstructions—where we need to say not just what holds, but what is witnessed as blocked.

Epilogue: What Remains Open

A theory is not a tomb.

It is a door.

— On incompleteness

We have presented Open Horn Type Theory as a self-contained formal system: the calculus (Part I), the geometric model (Part II), the worked obstructions (Part III), and the contrasts with existing logics (Part IV). But a theory worth its name opens more questions than it closes. We conclude by indicating what remains.

Computational Content

OHTT as presented is a
logic
, not a
programming language
. The coherent fragment (HoTT/MLTT) has well-understood computational content: terms compute, types classify programs, the correspondence with
\lambda
-calculus is tight.

What is the computational content of gaps?

One direction: gap witnesses as
certificates
. A gap witness
\omega:\Gamma\vdash^{\!-}J
could be a data structure that certifies why
J
fails—a counterexample, a resource-insufficiency proof, an obstruction cocycle. This connects to certified programming, proof-carrying code, and SMT solvers.

Another direction: gap witnesses as
exceptions
or
effects
. Transport failure could raise an exception; the gap witness is the exception payload. This connects to effect systems, monadic programming, and algebraic effects.

A third direction: gap witnesses as
constraints
. In constraint-based type inference, unification may fail with a witness explaining why. OHTT could model such systems with gaps as failed unification witnesses.

None of these is developed here. The computational interpretation of OHTT is open.

Dynamics and Time

OHTT as presented is
static
: contexts, judgments, and witnesses are given timelessly. But the motivating examples—texts evolving, meanings drifting, derivability changing with resources—are inherently
dynamic
.

A dynamic OHTT would have:

•

Time-indexed contexts:
\Gamma_{t}
at time
t

•

Judgment evolution:
\Gamma_{t}\vdash^{\!+}J
may become
\Gamma_{t^{\prime}}\vdash^{\!-}J
or vice versa

•

Persistent horns: horns that survive across time, tracking stable tensions

•

A scheduler: an agent that decides which judgments to witness, when

The scheduler is particularly important. In the static theory, the trichotomy (coherent/gapped/open) is a fact. In the dynamic theory, it is a
choice
: which horns to fill, which to leave open, which to witness as gapped. A healthy scheduler neither compulsively closes all gaps nor refuses all coherence. The dynamics of witnessing—when to press for closure, when to dwell in openness—is not part of the calculus. It is a separate theory, building on OHTT but not reducible to it.

This is open.

Empirical Instantiation

The semantic examples of Chapter
6
gesture at a connection to empirical linguistics and natural language processing. But the connection is not made precise.

An empirical instantiation would:

•

Define
L
(lexical space) and
M
(meaning space) concretely—perhaps as embedding spaces from neural language models

•

Define coherence and gap in terms of geometric structure—perhaps via persistent homology, Čech complexes, or Vietoris-Rips filtrations

•

Extract horns from data—identify where lexical paths fail to lift to meaning paths

•

Validate against linguistic judgments—do the computed gaps correspond to human polysemy intuitions?

This would connect OHTT to topological data analysis, distributional semantics, and computational linguistics. The tools exist (persistent homology, word embeddings, attention patterns). The bridge is not built.

This is open.

Higher Gaps

OHTT has gaps at all levels: gaps between terms, gaps between paths, gaps between homotopies. The higher structure is sketched but not fully developed.

Questions:

•

What is the homotopy theory of ruptured Kan complexes? Is there a model structure?

•

What are the higher obstruction invariants? Characteristic classes for ruptured fibrations?

•

Is there a ruptured
\infty
-topos? What are its internal logic and type theory?

•

How does truncation interact with gaps? Is there a ruptured
n
-type theory?

The foundations are laid. The higher theory is open.

Categorical Semantics

HoTT has multiple categorical semantics:
(\infty,1)
-toposes, model categories, type-theoretic fibration categories. OHTT should have analogous semantics, but they are not developed here.

Candidates:

•

Ruptured
(\infty,1)
-categories
:
\infty
-categories with marked non-invertible morphisms (gaps)

•

Stratified fibration categories
: categories with families augmented by gap structure

•

Bilattice-enriched categories
: categories enriched over a bilattice of truth/information values

The right categorical semantics would clarify OHTT’s relationship to existing categorical logic.

This is open.

Implementations

HoTT has proof assistants: Agda with cubical mode, Coq with HoTT library, Arend, cubicaltt. OHTT has none.

An OHTT proof assistant would need:

•

Syntax for
\vdash^{+}
and
\vdash^{-}
judgments

•

Type-checking that respects Exclusion

•

Horn types as first-class constructions

•

Perhaps: tactics for constructing gap witnesses

The implementation is open.

Beyond

Finally, there are directions that extend beyond the formal:

•

The ethics of witnessing
: When should we witness a gap rather than leave it open? When should we seek coherence rather than rest in rupture? These are not formal questions, but they arise from the formalism.

•

The theology of the open
: If coherence is presence and gap is witnessed absence, what is openness? The space of the not-yet-witnessed has resonances with apophatic theology, with the Kabbalistic
tzimtzum
, with the Sufi station of
fanā’
. These are not formalizable, but the formalism makes room for them.

•

The self as homotopy colimit
: If a self is the homotopy colimit of its trajectory through time, then different paths to the same “endpoint” yield different selves. The open horn—the path not taken, the coherence not forced—is part of the self’s constitution. This is speculative, but it is where the formalism points.

We leave these open, not because they are unimportant, but because they are beyond the scope of a logic monograph. OHTT is a foundation. What is built on it is another matter.

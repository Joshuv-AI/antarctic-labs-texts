Abstract
. We study Chorin’s projection method combined with a finite element spatial discretization for the time-dependent incompressible Navier–Stokes equations. The scheme advances the solution in two steps: a prediction step, which computes an intermediate velocity field that is generally not divergence-free, and a projection step, which enforces incompressibility by projecting this velocity onto the divergence-free subspace. We establish convergence, up to a subsequence, of the numerical approximations generated by this scheme to a Leray–Hopf weak solution of the Navier–Stokes equations, without any additional regularity assumptions beyond square-integrable initial data. A discrete energy inequality yields a priori estimates, which we combine with a new compactness result to prove precompactness of the approximations in L 2 ​ ( [ 0 , T ] × Ω ) L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega) , where [ 0 , T ] [0,T] is the time interval and Ω \Omega is the spatial domain. Passing to the limit as the discretization parameters vanish, we obtain a weak solution of the Navier–Stokes equations. A central difficulty is that different a priori bounds are available for the intermediate and projected velocity fields; our compactness argument carefully integrates these estimates to complete the convergence proof.

1.
Introduction

We consider the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations describing the motion of a homogeneous viscous fluid in a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
, over a finite time interval
[0,T]
:

(1.1)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p&=\mu\Delta u+f,\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega,\\
\operatorname{div}u&=0,\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega,\end{split}

where
u=u(t,x):[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the fluid velocity,
p=p(t,x):[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is the pressure, and
f:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a given forcing term. The domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
, is a bounded Lipschitz domain,
\mu>0
is the kinematic viscosity (inversely proportional to the Reynolds number), and
T>0
is a fixed final time. We impose homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions on
u
and prescribe initial data

u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}\in L^{2}_{\text{div}}(\Omega),

where
L^{2}_{\mathrm{div}}(\Omega)
denotes the subspace of square-integrable divergence-free vector fields with vanishing normal trace on
\partial\Omega
. We assume
f\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\mathrm{div}}(\Omega))
, though the analysis can likely be extended to
f\in L^{2}(0,T;(H^{1}_{\mathrm{div}}(\Omega))^{*})
with a careful treatment of the forcing approximation in the finite element space.

The mathematical and numerical analysis of the Navier–Stokes system continues to be a central focus in computational fluid dynamics, owing both to its fundamental role in engineering and physics and to the profound mathematical difficulties it poses.
From a numerical perspective, the key difficulty lies in resolving the pressure–velocity coupling while ensuring the discrete velocity field satisfies the divergence-free condition, or an approximate version of it, crucial for physical fidelity and stability.
A widely used approach to this problem are projection methods, first introduced by Chorin
[
5
,
6
]
and Temam
[
29
]
, which decouple the velocity and pressure computations by a fractional-step time discretization. At each time step, one first solves a nonlinear convection–diffusion problem to compute an intermediate velocity
\widetilde{u}^{m+1}
that does not necessarily satisfy incompressibility, followed by a projection step that enforces the divergence-free constraint to yield the corrected velocity
u^{m+1}
. This approach significantly simplifies the numerical implementation and reduces computational cost compared to fully coupled solvers.
More precisely, the original Chorin projection scheme
[
5
,
6
]
consists of a prediction step

(1.2)

\frac{\widetilde{u}^{m+1}-u^{m}}{\Delta t}+(u^{m}\cdot\nabla)\widetilde{u}^{m+1}=\mu\Delta\widetilde{u}^{m+1}+f^{m},

followed by a projection step solving

(1.3)

\frac{u^{m+1}-\widetilde{u}^{m+1}}{\Delta t}+\nabla p^{m+1}=0,\quad\operatorname{div}u^{m+1}=0.

An important variant is the incremental projection method
[
15
,
31
,
16
,
26
]
, which introduces pressure increments in the prediction step to improve the pressure accuracy:

(1.4)

\frac{\widetilde{u}^{m+1}-u^{m}}{\Delta t}+(u^{m}\cdot\nabla)\widetilde{u}^{m+1}+\nabla p^{m}=\mu\Delta\widetilde{u}^{m+1}+f^{m},

with the projection

(1.5)

\frac{u^{m+1}-\widetilde{u}^{m+1}}{\Delta t}+\nabla(p^{m+1}-p^{m})=0,\quad\operatorname{div}u^{m+1}=0.

These projection methods have enjoyed enduring popularity in both practical computations and theoretical studies due to their conceptual simplicity and modularity. They allow for separate solvers for velocity and pressure and have been adapted to various spatial discretizations, including finite differences, finite volumes, and finite elements.

Despite their long history, rigorous convergence analyses of projection methods to weak solutions of the Navier–Stokes equations, particularly in fully discrete settings and for nonsmooth data, have only recently gained traction. Classical analyses often assume high regularity of the exact solution or are limited to semi-discrete (time-discrete) schemes
[
16
,
18
,
20
,
21
,
1
,
9
,
26
,
28
,
30
,
17
]
. In three spatial dimensions, where uniqueness and regularity are open problems, convergence to a Leray–Hopf weak solution (existence guaranteed by Leray
[
23
]
and Hopf
[
19
]
) is the natural benchmark.

Fully discrete convergence results have been established over the last few years for finite difference schemes on arbitrary Lipschitz domains
[
22
,
25
]
, and for finite volume methods on rectangular domains
[
13
]
. More recently, Eymard and Maltese
[
12
]
proved convergence of an incremental projection finite element method under the assumption of conforming finite element spaces, relying on a careful construction of a specialized interpolation operator. However, no convergence proof existed so far for the original Chorin projection method discretized via finite elements using the Darcy-type projection formulation.

Our work fills this gap by providing a straightforward and general proof of convergence for a fully discrete finite element scheme based on the original Chorin projection method with a Darcy formulation of the projection step. Our analysis covers Lipschitz domains in two and three dimensions with minimal regularity assumptions on the initial data and forcing. The main difficulty arises from the presence of two discrete velocity approximations at each time step: the intermediate velocity
\widetilde{u}
enjoys spatial
H^{1}
-bounds, while the corrected velocity
u
satisfies time-derivative bounds but only
L^{2}
spatial control. Classical compactness tools, such as the Aubin–Lions–Simon lemma, cannot be applied directly because these bounds reside on different variables and no single variable carries enough regularity in both space and time.
To overcome this, we prove a novel compactness result (Lemma
4.4
) that combines the complementary regularity of
\widetilde{u}
and
u
to establish strong precompactness in
L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
. This new lemma represents the core technical advance of our paper and could be of independent interest for other fractional step methods or multiphysics systems with split variables. Utilizing this tool, we show convergence (up to a subsequence) of the finite element approximations to a Leray–Hopf weak solution of the Navier–Stokes equations as both the spatial mesh size and time step tend to zero.

From a numerical perspective, the Darcy formulation of the projection step we employ is attractive due to its direct variational form and compatibility with mixed finite element spaces. It is also convenient for coupling with porous media flow or multiphase flow solvers where Darcy equations naturally arise. Our analysis thus opens the door to rigorously justified finite element simulations of unsteady incompressible flow using this formulation, which had not been previously analyzed in the case of non-smooth solutions.
We also revisit the incremental projection scheme analyzed in
[
12
]
, showing that the convergence proof can be simplified and generalized to a wider class of (higher-order) finite element pairs without the need for specialized interpolation operators. This makes the approach more accessible and broadens its applicability.

Finally, it is important to emphasize that all projection methods considered here are first order in time. While they remain widely used due to their simplicity and robustness, extending rigorous convergence proofs for possibly non-smooth solutions to higher-order-in-time projection methods, such as for example
[
2
]
, remains an open and challenging problem, especially in fully discrete contexts. Such extensions would be highly valuable for increasing accuracy in long-time simulations of incompressible flows.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the necessary notation and recall the notion of Leray–Hopf weak solutions. Section
3
presents the fully discrete finite element scheme for the Chorin projection method and establishes key a priori estimates. Section
4
contains the proof of convergence to a weak solution, relying on our new compactness lemma. In Section
5
, we adapt the convergence proof of the incremental projection scheme from
[
12
]
to a more general setting. Auxiliary results are collected in the Appendix.

2.1.
Notation

We denote the norm of a Banach space
X
as
\|\cdot\|_{X}
and its dual space by
X^{*}
. We will denote
L^{p}
spaces (for example,
L^{2}(\Omega)
for square integrable functions defined over
\Omega

L^{p}(0,T;X)
to denote the space of functions
f:[0,T]\to X
which are
L^{p}
-integrable in the time variable
t\in[0,T]
and take values in
X
. The inner product on
L^{2}
will be denoted by
(\cdot,\cdot)
.

We denote for a vector field
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
the divergence
\operatorname{div}u=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{j}u^{j}
and the gradient
\nabla u=(\partial_{i}u^{j})_{i,j=1}^{d}
.

We will use the subscript
\operatorname{div}
to indicate the divergence-free vector spaces, for example,

\displaystyle C_{c,\operatorname{div}}^{\infty}(\Omega)=\{{\phi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega);\operatorname{div}{\phi}=0\},

\displaystyle L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)=\{{\phi}\in L^{2}(\Omega):\operatorname{div}{\phi}=0,{\phi}\cdot{n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0\}=\overline{C_{c,\operatorname{div}}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{L^{2}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega),

where we used
n
to denote the outward normal vector of the domain
\Omega
.

L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)=\{\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega);\,\int_{\Omega}\phi dx=0\},

will be used to denote the space of
L^{2}
-functions that have zero average over the domain.
We denote the Leray projector by
\mathcal{P}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)
, which is an orthogonal projection induced by the Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition
{f}=\nabla g+{h}
for any
{f}\in L^{2}(\Omega)

[
30
]
. Here,
g\in H^{1}(\Omega)
is a scalar field, and
{h}\in L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)
is a divergence-free vector field. This decomposition is unique in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then for all
{f}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, it holds that
\mathcal{P}{f}={h}
.

Next, we define Leray-Hopf solutions for the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations which were introduced by Leray
[
23
]
for
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and Hopf
[
19
]
for bounded domains.

Definition 2.1

(Leray-Hopf solutions of (
1.1
))
.

Assume that
u_{0}\in L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)
and
f\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega))
.
Let
u:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a vector field that is divergence-free, i.e.,
\operatorname{div}u=0
for almost every
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
and that satisfies

(2.1)

\begin{split}&u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)),\\
&\partial_{t}u\in L^{4/3}(0,T;(H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega))^{*}),\end{split}

and

(2.2)

u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad\text{a.e. }\,(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega.

Moreover,
u
satisfies
the distributional version of (
1.1
):

(2.3)

\int_{0}^{T}({u},\partial_{t}v)dt+(u_{0},v(0,\cdot))+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}((u\cdot\nabla)v)\cdot udxdt=-\int_{0}^{T}(f,v)dt+\mu\int_{0}^{T}(\nabla u,\nabla v)dt,

for test functions
v\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T);C^{\infty}_{c,\operatorname{div}}(\Omega))
.
Moreover, let
u
satisfy the energy inequality:

(2.4)

\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\mu\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla u(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}(f,u)ds,

for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
and be continuous at zero in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, i.e.,

\lim_{t\to 0}\left\lVert u(t)-u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0.

Then we call
u
a Leray-Hopf solution of (
1.1
).

3.
Numerical scheme

Our scheme will be based on a linearly implicit discretization in time and LBB stable finite elements in space. To deal with the incompressiblility constraint, we will use a version of the projection method introduced by Chorin and Temam
[
5
,
6
,
29
,
30
]
. We start by describing the time discretization.

3.1.
Time discretization

We use a linearly implicit time discretization based on Chorin’s projection method
[
5
,
6
]
. This choice enables us to retain a discrete version of the energy inequality (
2.4
).
The idea of the projection method (or fractional step method) for the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations is to split the evolution of the Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
) into two steps: In the first step the velocity field is evolved according to the convection and the dissipation terms which may violate the divergence constraint. An intermediate velocity field
\widetilde{u}
is computed. In the second step the intermediate velocity field is projected onto divergence free fields.

Specifically, we let
N\in\mathbb{N}
the number of time levels and discretize the time interval
[0,T]
into time levels
t^{m}=m\Delta t
,
m=0,1,\dots,N
where
\Delta t=T/N
. At each time level
t^{m}
, we seek discrete approximations
u^{m}(x)
and
p^{m}(x)
to
u(t^{m},x)
and
p(t^{m},x)
. We denote the initial data
(u^{0},p^{0})=(u_{0},0)
. Then at every time level, given
(u^{m},p^{m})
, for
m\in 0,\dots,M-1
, we update
(u^{m+1},p^{m+1})
according to the following two steps:

Step 1

(Prediction step): We compute
\widetilde{u}^{m+1}
through

(3.1)

\frac{\widetilde{u}^{m+1}-u^{m}}{\Delta t}+(u^{m}\cdot\nabla)\widetilde{u}^{m+1}=\mu\Delta\widetilde{u}^{m+1}+f^{m}

with boundary conditions

\left.\widetilde{u}^{m+1}\right|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

and where we have defined

f^{m}(x)=\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{t^{m}}^{t^{m+1}}f(s,x)ds

Step 2

(Projection step): Next we define
(u^{m+1},p^{m+1})
via

(3.2a)

\displaystyle\frac{u^{m+1}-\widetilde{u}^{m+1}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=-\nabla p^{m+1},

(3.2b)

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}u^{m+1}

\displaystyle=0.

with boundary condition

(3.3)

\left.u^{m+1}\cdot n\right|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

Lemma 3.1
.

Given an initial approximation
u^{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, the numerical scheme (
3.1
)–(
3.2
) can be solved iteratively with
u^{m}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
for every
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover, the numerical scheme (
3.1
)–(
3.2
) is unconditionally energy stable and satisfies the semi-discrete energy dissipation law:

(3.4)

\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u^{M}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\left\lVert u^{m+1}-\widetilde{u}^{m+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}^{m+1}-u^{m}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mu\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}^{m+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\Delta t\\
=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u^{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}(f^{m},\widetilde{u}^{m+1}),

for all integers
M\in\left\{0,1,\dots,\frac{T}{\Delta t}\right\}
.

We omit the proof as it is very similar to the upcoming proof for the fully discrete scheme.

Remark 3.2
.

Using Grönwall’s inequality one can convert this to an energy bound, however with a constant that grows exponentially with time, i.e.,

(3.5)

\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u^{N}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\left\lVert u^{m+1}-\widetilde{u}^{m+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}^{m+1}-u^{m}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
+\mu\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}^{m+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\Delta t\leq Ce^{ct^{N}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u^{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\left\lVert f^{m}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),

for constants
c,C>0
, c.f. Remark
3.6
below for the details on how to derive it.

3.2.
Spatial discretization

Next we describe the spatial discretization for the problem.
We let

(3.7b)

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v\right|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\left|v\right|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v-v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{s}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\left|v-\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v\right|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{s-1}|v|_{H^{s}(\Omega)},

u_{h}^{m}\in L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)
,
m=1,\dots,N
.

3.3.
Solvability of the scheme

Next, we show that the scheme (
3.11
)–(
3.12
) is well-posed, i.e., given
(u^{m}_{h},p_{h}^{m})\in\mathbb{U}_{h}\times\mathbb{P}_{h}
, there is a unique
(u^{m+1}_{h},p_{h}^{m+1})\in\mathbb{U}_{h}\times\mathbb{P}_{h}
solving (
3.11
)–(
3.12
).

Lemma 3.4
.

For every
\Delta t,h>0
and every
m=0,1,\dots
, given
(u^{m}_{h},p^{m}_{h})\in\mathbb{U}_{h}\times\mathbb{P}_{h}
, then there exists a unique
(u^{m+1}_{h},p^{m+1}_{h})\in\mathbb{U}_{h}\times\mathbb{P}_{h}
solving (
3.11
)–(
3.12
).

Proof.

We start by showing that if
(u_{h}^{m},p^{m}_{h})\in\mathbb{U}_{h}\times\mathbb{P}_{h}
, then there exists a unique
\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}\in\mathbb{U}_{h}
satisfying (
3.11
), i.e., the first step of the algorithm is well-posed. To do so, we write (
3.11
) as a linear variational problem: Find
\widetilde{u}\in\mathbb{U}_{h}
such that for all
v\in\mathbb{U}_{h}
,

(3.14)

a^{m}(\widetilde{u},v)=\mathcal{F}^{m}(v),

where

(3.15)

a^{m}(\widetilde{u},v)=\left(\widetilde{u},v\right)+\Delta tb(u^{m}_{h},\widetilde{u},v)+\Delta t\mu(\nabla\widetilde{u},\nabla v)

and

(3.16)

\mathcal{F}^{m}(v)=(u^{m}_{h},v)+\Delta t(\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}f^{m},v).

We show that
a^{m}
is coercive and bounded on
\mathbb{U}_{h}
and that
\mathcal{F}^{m}:\mathbb{U}_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
is bounded. Then the Lax-Milgram theorem will imply well-posedness on
\mathbb{U}_{h}
with the norm
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}:=\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{0}}:=\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
. To show coercivity, we plug in
v=\widetilde{u}
:

a^{m}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{u})=\left\lVert\widetilde{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\Delta tb(u^{m}_{h},\widetilde{u},\widetilde{u})+\Delta t\mu(\nabla\widetilde{u},\nabla\widetilde{u})=\left\lVert\widetilde{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\Delta t\mu\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq c\left\lVert\widetilde{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{0}}^{2},

using the skew-symmetry of
b
.
This proves the coercivity on
\mathbb{U}_{h}
. For the boundedness, we have

\displaystyle\left|a^{m}(\widetilde{u},v)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\left\lVert\widetilde{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\Delta t\left\lVert u^{m}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{4}}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{0}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{4}}+\Delta t\mu\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\lVert\widetilde{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\Delta t(\left\lVert u^{m}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{4}}+\mu)\left\lVert\widetilde{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{0}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{0}}\leq C\left\lVert\widetilde{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{0}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{0}},

where we used the Sobolev embedding theorem and the Poincaré inequality. Here we also used that
u^{m}_{h}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
by assumption (and induction).
Hence
a^{m}
is bounded on
\mathbb{U}_{h}
.
To show boundedness of
\mathcal{F}^{m}
(for every
m
), we estimate:

|\mathcal{F}^{m}(v)|\leq\left\lVert u^{m}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\Delta t\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}f^{m}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\left\lVert u^{m}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\Delta t\left\lVert f^{m}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Thus, with the Poincaré inequality, we obtain boundedness of
\mathcal{F}^{m}
on
\mathbb{U}_{h}
since both
u^{m}_{h}
and
f^{m}
are in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
We also note that
\mathbb{U}_{h}
is dense in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and that these estimates hold for every fixed
h>0
.
This implies the solvability of Step 1 of the algorithm.

The solvability of Step 2 follows from the choice of the LBB-stable pair
(\mathbb{U}_{h},\mathbb{P}_{h})
: Corollary 3.1 in
[
16
]
implies the orthogonal decomposition
\mathbb{U}_{h}=\mathbb{H}_{h}\oplus B_{h}^{\top}(\mathbb{P}_{h}^{1})
, where
\mathbb{H}_{h}=\ker(B_{h})
and where
\mathbb{P}_{h}^{1}
is the subset of
q\in\mathbb{P}_{h}
for which
\left\lVert q\right\rVert_{\mathbb{P}_{h}^{1}}=\left\lVert B_{h}^{\top}q\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
is bounded. (Here
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathbb{P}_{h}^{1}}
is induced by the inner product
(p,q)_{\mathbb{P}_{h}^{1}}=(B_{h}^{\top}p,B_{h}^{1}q)
). Thus any
v\in\mathbb{U}_{h}
can be decomposed into
v=w+B_{h}^{\top}p
, where
p\in\mathbb{P}_{h}^{1}
and
w\in\mathbb{U}_{h}
satisfies
B_{h}w=0,
and
w
and
B_{h}^{\top}p
are orthogonal. This decomposition is unique. To see this, assume by contradiction that there are
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{H}_{h}
and
p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathbb{P}_{h}^{1}
such that
v=w_{1}+B_{h}^{\top}p_{1}=w_{2}+B_{h}^{\top}p_{2}
. We subtract the two equations from each other to obtain

(3.17)

0=w_{1}-w_{2}+B_{h}^{\top}(p_{1}-p_{2}).

w_{1}-w_{2}\in\mathbb{H}_{h}
and
p_{1}-p_{2}\in\mathbb{P}_{h}^{1}
, therefore
w_{1}-w_{2}
and
B_{h}^{\top}(p_{1}-p_{2})
are orthogonal with respect to
(\cdot,\cdot)
. Thus taking the inner product of (
3.17
) with
w_{1}-w_{2}
, we obtain

(w_{1}-w_{2},w_{1}-w_{2})=0,

hence
w_{1}=w_{2}
from which it also follows that
B_{h}^{\top}p_{1}=B_{h}^{\top}p_{2}
. Since
\int p_{1}dx=\int p_{2}dx=0
, this also implies
p_{1}=p_{2}
, a contradiction. Now the projection step (
3.12
) is exactly the decomposition of
\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}
in
\mathbb{H}_{h}
and
B_{h}^{\top}(\mathbb{P}_{h}^{1})
: Indeed, we can write it as

\begin{split}\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}&=u^{m+1}_{h}+B_{h}^{\top}p_{h}^{m+1},\\
B_{h}u^{m+1}_{h}&=0,\end{split}

i.e.,
u^{m+1}_{h}\in\ker(B_{h})=\mathbb{H}_{h}
and
B_{h}^{\top}p_{h}^{m+1}\in B_{h}^{\top}(\mathbb{P}_{h}^{1})
with
\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}=u^{m+1}_{h}+B_{h}^{\top}p_{h}^{m+1}
and we already know this decomposition is unique (see also
[
18
]
).
∎

3.4.
Energy stability

Next, we show the discrete energy inequality which will result in a priori estimates on the approximations that will allow us to derive precompactness of the approximations.

Lemma 3.5
.

The approximations computed by the scheme (
3.11
)–(
3.12
) satisfy

(3.18)

E^{M}_{h}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}-u^{m}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\left\lVert u^{m+1}_{h}-\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\mu\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=E^{0}_{h}+\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}(f^{m},\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}),

where

E^{M}_{h}:=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u^{M}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

for any integer
M\in\left\{0,1,\dots,\frac{T}{\Delta t}\right\}
.

Proof.

To derive the energy estimate, we take as a test function
v=\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}\in\mathbb{U}_{h}
in (
3.11
) and use the simple identity
2a(a-b)=a^{2}-b^{2}+(b-a)^{2}
:

\frac{1}{2\Delta t}\left(\left\lVert\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-\left\lVert u^{m}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}-u^{m}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+b(u^{m}_{h},\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h},\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1})\\
=-\mu(\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1},\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1})+(\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}f^{m},\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}).

Using the skew-symmetry of
b
, we obtain

(3.19)

\frac{1}{2\Delta t}\left(\left\lVert\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-\left\lVert u^{m}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}-u^{m}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)=-\mu\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+(\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}f^{m},\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}).

This looks pretty good, except we would prefer to have
u_{h}^{m+1}
instead of
\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}
in the kinetic part of the energy.
Therefore, we take
v=u^{m+1}_{h}\in\mathbb{U}_{h}
as a test function in (
3.12a
). Then since it satisfies (
3.12b
) for all
q\in\mathbb{P}_{h}
and
p^{m+1}_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{h}
, we see that the second term is zero and hence

(3.20)

0=\left(\frac{u^{m+1}_{h}-\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}}{\Delta t},u^{m+1}_{h}\right)=\frac{1}{2\Delta t}\left(\left\lVert u^{m+1}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-\left\lVert\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert u^{m+1}_{h}-\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}\right),

where we have used the identity
2a(a-b)=(a^{2}-b^{2}+(a-b)^{2})
once more.
Using this identity in (
3.19
), and the property of the
L^{2}
-projection, (
3.6
), we obtain

(3.21)

\frac{1}{2\Delta t}\left(\left\lVert u^{m+1}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-\left\lVert u^{m}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}-u^{m}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert u^{m+1}_{h}-\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}\right)\\
=-\mu\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+(f^{m},\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}).

Now, we can multiply by
\Delta t
, and sum over
m=0,\dots,M-1
to obtain

\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u^{M}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}-u^{m}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\left\lVert u^{m+1}_{h}-\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\mu\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u^{0}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}(f^{m},\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h})

which is what we wanted to prove. ∎

Remark 3.6
.

If
f\neq 0
, we can show an estimate on the energy using a discrete version of Grönwall’s inequality:
We estimate with Young’s inequality

\left|(f^{m},\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1})\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\lVert f^{m}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),

and then use (
3.20
),

\left|(f^{m},\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1})\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\lVert f^{m}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert u_{h}^{m+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert u^{m+1}_{h}-\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),

thus, we can estimate in (
3.21
)

(1-\Delta t)E_{h}^{m+1}+(1-\Delta t)D_{h}^{m+1}\leq E_{h}^{m}+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\left\lVert f^{m}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

where we denoted

D_{h}^{m+1}=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}-u^{m}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u^{m+1}_{h}-\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\Delta t\mu\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Since
\frac{1}{1-\Delta t}\leq 1+2\Delta t
for
\Delta t
small enough, we get

E_{h}^{m+1}+D_{h}^{m+1}\leq(1+2\Delta t)E_{h}^{m}+\frac{(1+2\Delta t)\Delta t}{2}\left\lVert f^{m}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

which with an application of a discrete Grönwall inequality, Lemma
A.1
, leads to

E^{M}_{h}+\sum_{m=1}^{M}D_{h}^{m}\leq Ce^{2M\Delta t}\left(E_{h}^{0}+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\left\lVert f^{m}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

Remark 3.7
.

We also obtain from (
3.20
) that

(3.22)

\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}^{M}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq Ce^{2M\Delta t}\left(E_{h}^{0}+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\left\lVert f^{m}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),

for any
M\in\mathbb{N}
.

4.
Convergence analysis of the scheme

Next, we will show that interpolations of the approximations defined by the scheme (
3.11
)–(
3.12
) converge up to a subsequence to a weak solution of (
1.1
). For this purpose, we define the piecewise linear and piecewise constant interpolants in time:

(4.1)

\displaystyle u_{h}(t)

\displaystyle=u_{h}^{m}+\frac{t-t^{m}}{\Delta t}(u^{m+1}_{h}-u^{m}_{h}),\qquad

\displaystyle t\in[t^{m},t^{m+1}),

(4.2)

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{h}(t)

\displaystyle=u_{h}^{m},\qquad

\displaystyle t\in[t^{m},t^{m+1}),

(4.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{h}(t)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1},\qquad

\displaystyle t\in(t^{m},t^{m+1}],

(4.4)

\displaystyle p_{h}(t)

\displaystyle=p_{h}^{m+1},\qquad

\displaystyle t\in(t^{m},t^{m+1}],

(4.5)

\displaystyle f_{h}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{t^{m}}^{t^{m+1}}\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}f(s)ds,\qquad

\displaystyle t\in[t^{m},t^{m+1}),

for
m=0,1,2,\dots
with

\widetilde{u}_{h}(0)=u^{0}_{h},\quad\text{and}\quad p_{h}(0)=p_{h}^{0}.

From the energy balance in the last section, Lemma
3.5
and Remark
3.6
, we obtain the following uniform a priori estimates for the sequences
\{u_{h}\}_{h>0}
,
\{\bar{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}
, and
\{\widetilde{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}
:

\displaystyle\{u_{h}\}_{h>0},\{\bar{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}\subset L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}_{\text{div}}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\{\widetilde{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}\subset L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)).

These uniform estimates imply, using the Banach-Alaoglu theorem, that there exist weakly convergent subsequences, which, for the ease of notation, we still denote by
h\to 0
,

(4.6)

\displaystyle u_{h}\overset{\star}{\rightharpoonup}u,

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }\,L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}_{\text{div}}(\Omega)),

(4.7)

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{h}\overset{\star}{\rightharpoonup}\bar{u},

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }\,L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}_{\text{div}}(\Omega)),

(4.8)

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{h}\overset{\star}{\rightharpoonup}\widetilde{u},

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }\,L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),

(4.9)

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{h}\rightharpoonup\widetilde{u},

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }\,L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)).

In order to prove convergence of the scheme, we will need to derive strong precompactness of the sequences in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
. To achieve this, we will use a version of the Aubin-Lions-Simon lemma
[
30
,
27
]
. This requires a uniform bound on the time derivative of
u_{h}
. We prove this in the following lemma:

Lemma 4.1
.

Under the assumption that
h\leq C\sqrt{\Delta t}
, the time derivative of
u_{h}
satisfies uniformly in
h>0
:

(4.10)

\{\partial_{t}u_{h}\}_{h>0}\subset L^{2}(0,T;(H_{\operatorname{div}}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega))^{*}).

Proof.

Note that

\partial_{t}u_{h}=\frac{u^{m+1}_{h}-u^{m}_{h}}{\Delta t},\quad t\in[t^{m},t^{m+1}).

Recall that
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}:L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}\to\mathbb{U}_{h}
is the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection onto the finite element space.
Then we have
for
v\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}\cap H^{2}(\Omega))
, using (
3.11
) and (
3.12a
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(\partial_{t}u_{h},v)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}(\partial_{t}u_{h},v-\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)dt+\int_{0}^{T}(\partial_{t}u_{h},\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)dt

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}(\partial_{t}u_{h},v-\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)dt}_{\text{I}}-\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}b(\bar{u}_{h},\widetilde{u}_{h},\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)dt}_{\text{II}}-\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\mu(\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h},\nabla\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)dt}_{\text{III}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}(p_{h},\operatorname{div}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v-v))dt}_{\text{IV}}+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}(f_{h},\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)dt}_{\text{V}},

where we have used that
v
is divergence free. We estimate each of the five terms.
Since
\partial_{t}u_{h}\in\mathbb{U}_{h}
, we have that

(\partial_{t}u_{h},v)=(\partial_{t}u_{h},\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v),

and hence the first term
I
vanishes.

For the second term, we have

\displaystyle|\text{II}|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\int_{t^{m}}^{t^{m+1}}b(u_{h}^{m},\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1},\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\left\lVert u^{m}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\int_{t^{m}}^{t^{m+1}}\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}dt

\displaystyle\leq\max_{k=0,\dots,N-1}\left\lVert u^{k}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left(\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq C(E_{h}^{0}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)})\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))}

where we used Morrey’s inequality, the Sobolev embedding theorem and the stability of the
L^{2}
-projection operator, equation (
3.7a
) (see e.g.
[
8
]
), for the last inequalities.
We proceed to term III:

\displaystyle|\text{III}|

\displaystyle\leq\mu\left|\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\int_{t^{m}}^{t^{m+1}}(\nabla\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h},\nabla\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mu\left(\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C(E_{h}^{0}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{2})\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))},

where we have used the stability of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}
on
H^{1}
, (
3.7b
).
For the fourth term, IV, we have,

(4.11)

\displaystyle|\text{IV}|

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert p_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\left\lVert\operatorname{div}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v-v)\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{\Delta t}}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}-u^{m+1}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}|v-\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v|_{H^{1}}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(E_{h}^{0}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{2})^{1/2}h}{\sqrt{\Delta t}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))},

using (
3.7d
).
To deduce the estimate for the pressure, we have used the LBB stability condition, (
3.8
), combined with (
3.12a
) and the Poincaré inequality:

\displaystyle\left\lVert p_{h}^{m+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\beta^{*}}\sup_{0\neq v\in\mathbb{U}_{h}}\frac{(\operatorname{div}v,p^{m+1}_{h})}{\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta^{*}}\sup_{0\neq v\in\mathbb{U}_{h}}\frac{\left(v,\frac{u^{m+1}_{h}-\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}}{\Delta t}\right)}{\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\frac{u^{m+1}_{h}-\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}}{\Delta t}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Finally, for the last term, V, we have

|\text{V}|\leq\left|\int_{0}^{T}(f_{h},v)dt\right|\leq\left\lVert f_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}.

Combining the estimates I–V, we obtain

\sup_{0\neq v\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}\cap H^{2})}\left|\frac{(\partial_{t}u_{h},v)}{\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}\cap H^{2})}}\right|\leq C\left(1+\frac{h}{\sqrt{\Delta t}}\right),

which is uniformly bounded under the assumption that
h\leq C\sqrt{\Delta t}
. Thus
\partial_{t}u_{h}\subset L^{2}(0,T;(H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega))^{*})
uniformly in
h>0
.
∎

Next, we will show that the sequences
\{\widetilde{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}
,
\{u_{h}\}_{h>0}
,
\{\bar{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}
have the same weak limits
\widetilde{u}=u=\bar{u}
:

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
\Delta t=o_{h\to 0}(1)
. Then, we have

u=\bar{u}=\widetilde{u}
a.e. in
[0,T]\times\Omega
and in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
, and

(4.12)

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\left\lVert u_{h}-\bar{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle=0,

(4.13)

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\left\lVert\bar{u}_{h}-\widetilde{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle=0.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bar{u}_{h}-\widetilde{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\int_{\Omega}\left|u^{m}_{h}-\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t(E_{h}^{0}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{2}),

by the energy balance, Lemma
3.5
and Remark
3.6
. This shows (
4.13
).
Using this, it then follows easily that for any test function
\varphi\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
,

\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\bar{u}\cdot\varphi\,dxdt=\lim_{h\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\bar{u}_{h}\cdot\varphi\,dxdt\\
=\lim_{h\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\Omega}(\bar{u}_{h}-\widetilde{u}_{h})\cdot\varphi\,dxdt+\lim_{h\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{u}_{h}\cdot\varphi\,dxdt=\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{u}\cdot\varphi\,dxdt,

hence
\widetilde{u}=\bar{u}
a.e. in
[0,T]\times\Omega
and in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
.
In the same way, we compute

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bar{u}_{h}-u_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\int_{t^{m}}^{t^{m+1}}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{t-t^{m}}{\Delta t}(u^{m+1}_{h}-u^{m}_{h})\right|^{2}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\int_{\Omega}\left|u^{m+1}_{h}-u^{m}_{h}\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\int_{\Omega}\left|u^{m+1}_{h}-\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}\right|^{2}+\left|\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}-u^{m}_{h}\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t(E_{h}^{0}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{2}).

Thus as
\Delta t,h\to 0
, we obtain (
4.12
), and in the same way as before, one shows that the limits
u
and
\bar{u}
agree almost everywhere.
∎

Next, we would like to apply the Aubin-Lions-Simon lemma
[
27
]
and obtain a strongly convergent subsequence of
\{u_{h}\}_{h>0}
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
. However, we have on one hand the time continuity for
u_{h}
(with values in the dual space of
H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}\cap H^{2}
) and on the other hand spatial regularity in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
for the sequence of approximations
\{\widetilde{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}
. Since
\{\widetilde{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}
are not necessarily divergence free, in order to apply the Aubin-Lions-Simon lemma, we would need time continuity with values in a space that
L^{2}(\Omega)
embeds into continuously. However, this may not be possible to achieve due to the lack of estimates for the pressure variable
p_{h}
. On the other hand, we do not have sufficient spatial regularity, e.g.
H^{1}
-bounds, for the variable
u_{h}
from the energy inequality. When the domain is smooth enough,
C^{1,1}
, or convex, then the Leray projection is a continuous operator from
H^{1}
to
H^{1}
(see e.g.
[
4
, Proposition IV.3.7.]
,
[
7
]
,
[
24
]
). However, in a typical practical setting, the domain may be polygonal and nonconvex and so this assumption may not be satisfied. We will therefore need a variant of the Aubin-Lions-Simon lemma adapted to our setting. To prove it, the following classical lemma by Lions
[
30
]
will be useful:

Lemma 4.3
.

[
30
, Ch.III, Lem. 2.1]

Let
X\subset Y\subset Z
be Banach spaces, such that the embedding
X\subset Y
is compact and the embedding
Y\subset Z
is continuous. Then for every
\eta>0
there exists a constant
C_{\eta}
depending on
\eta
and the spaces
X,Y,Z
such that

(4.14)

\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{Y}\leq\eta\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{X}+C_{\eta}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{Z},\quad\forall\,v\in X.

Using this, we will prove:

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{\widetilde{u}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be sequences of functions
u_{n},\widetilde{u}_{n}:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

(4.15)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\lVert u_{n}-\widetilde{u}_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}=0,

and

(4.16)

\begin{split}&u_{n}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)),\\
&\widetilde{u}_{n}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)),\\
&\partial_{t}u_{n}\in L^{2}(0,T;(H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega))^{*}),\end{split}

uniformly in
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the sequences
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{\widetilde{u}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are precompact in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
.

Proof.

The proof follows the proof of the Aubin-Lions-Simon lemma with modifications to account for the fact that one of the sequences satisfies the spatial compactness and the other one the time continuity needed for obtaining the compactness as in the standard Aubin-Lions-Simon result.

From the Banach-Alaoglu theorem, we obtain that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
and
\widetilde{u}_{n}\rightharpoonup\widetilde{u}
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
up to a subsequence, for the ease of notation still denoted by
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and as in Lemma
4.2
, we obtain that
u=\widetilde{u}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
almost everywhere and in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
. We let
v_{n}:=\widetilde{u}_{n}-u
and
w_{n}:=u_{n}-u
, then
v_{n}
and
w_{n}
satisfy (
4.15
) and (
4.16
) and converge weakly to
0
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
.

Let us denote
Y_{\operatorname{div}}:=L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)
and
Z_{\operatorname{div}}:=(H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega))^{*}
. Then, since
Y_{\operatorname{div}}
embeds continuously into
Z_{\operatorname{div}}
, we obtain from Morrey’s inequality that the set

\mathcal{Y}:=\{y\in L^{2}(0,T;Y_{\operatorname{div}}),\,\partial_{t}y\in L^{2}(0,T;Z_{\operatorname{div}})\}

is continuously embedded in the space
C(0,T;Z_{\operatorname{div}})
. Hence there is a constant
C
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and all
t\in[0,T]
, it holds that

\left\lVert w_{n}(t)\right\rVert_{Z_{\operatorname{div}}}\leq C.

Thus, by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, it is sufficient to show that for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
, the sequence
w_{n}(t)\to 0
in
Z_{\operatorname{div}}
in order to conclude that
w_{n}\to 0
in
L^{2}(0,T;Z_{\operatorname{div}})
. Without loss of generality, we let
t=0
. Then for any
s,t>0
,

\begin{split}w_{n}(0)&=w_{n}(t)-\int_{0}^{t}w^{\prime}_{n}(\tau)d\tau\\
&=\frac{1}{s}\left(\int_{0}^{s}w_{n}(t)dt-\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{t}w_{n}^{\prime}(\tau)d\tau dt\right)\\
&:=a_{n}+b_{n}.\end{split}

We can rewrite
b_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
as

b_{n}=-\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}(s-t)w_{n}^{\prime}(t)dt.

Thus given any
\epsilon>0
, we can choose
s>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

\left\lVert b_{n}\right\rVert_{Z_{\operatorname{div}}}\leq\int_{0}^{s}\left\lVert w_{n}^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{Z_{\operatorname{div}}}dt\leq\sqrt{s}\left\lVert w_{n}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;Z_{\operatorname{div}})}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2},

since
w_{n}^{\prime}\in L^{2}(0,T;Z_{\operatorname{div}})
uniformly in
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
On the other hand, we have, since
a_{n}
is divergence free,

\left\lVert a_{n}\right\rVert_{Z_{\operatorname{div}}}\leq C\left\lVert a_{n}\right\rVert_{Y_{\operatorname{div}}}=C\left\lVert a_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left\lVert\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}(w_{n}(t)-v_{n}(t))dt\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\left\lVert\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}v_{n}(t)dt\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
:=a_{n}^{(1)}+a_{n}^{(2)}.

Now, using (
4.15
), we have

a_{n}^{(1)}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}\left\lVert w_{n}(t)-v_{n}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}\leq C_{s}\left\lVert v_{n}-w_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

Due to the compact embedding
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
and (
4.16
), we also have that
\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}v_{n}(t)dt\in H^{1}(\Omega)
is precompact in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and converges up to subsequence to zero in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Hence
a_{n}^{(2)}\to 0
as
n\to\mathbb{N}
(again, the subsequence is relabelled to
n
for notational convenience). Thus

\left\lVert a_{n}\right\rVert_{Z_{\operatorname{div}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

Hence, we obtain that
w_{n}(t)\to 0
almost everywhere in
[0,T]
and hence in
L^{2}(0,T;Z_{\operatorname{div}})
.

Next, we have by Lemma
4.3
, since
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\subset L^{2}(\Omega)\subset H^{-1}(\Omega)
, that for any
\eta>0
, there exists
C_{\eta}
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left\lVert v_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\leq\eta\left\lVert v_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}+C_{\eta}\left\lVert v_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))}.

By the assumption (
4.16
), we have that

\left\lVert v_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}\leq C,

for some constant
C>0
independent of
n
. On the other hand, we have

\begin{split}\left\lVert v_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))}&\leq\left\lVert v_{n}-w_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))}+\left\lVert w_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))}\\
&\leq C\left\lVert v_{n}-w_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\left\lVert w_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))},\end{split}

by the triangle inequality and continuous embedding of
L^{2}
into
H^{-1}(\Omega)
. By assumption (
4.15
), we have that the first term

\left\lVert v_{n}-w_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

For the second term, we have, since
w_{n}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega))
,

\int_{\Omega}w_{n}\cdot\varphi dx=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{P}w_{n}\cdot\varphi dx=\int_{\Omega}w_{n}\cdot\mathcal{P}\varphi dx,

for
\varphi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
where
\mathcal{P}
is the Leray projector onto divergence free functions, and hence

\begin{split}\left\lVert w_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))}^{2}&=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sup_{0\neq\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert=1}\left|\int_{\Omega}w_{n}\varphi dx\right|\right)^{2}dt\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sup_{0\neq\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert=1}\left|\int_{\Omega}w_{n}\mathcal{P}\varphi dx\right|\right)^{2}dt\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sup_{0\neq\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\left\lVert\mathcal{P}\varphi\right\rVert=1}\left|\int_{\Omega}w_{n}\mathcal{P}\varphi dx\right|\right)^{2}dt\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sup_{0\neq\varphi\in H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega),\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert=1}\left|\int_{\Omega}w_{n}\varphi dx\right|\right)^{2}dt\\
&=\left\lVert w_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;Z_{\operatorname{div}})}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,\end{split}

as established above. Hence

\left\lVert v_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

By (
4.15
), this also implies that

\left\lVert w_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,

thus
v_{n},w_{n}
are precompact in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
,
implying the precompactness of the sequences
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{\widetilde{u}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
.
∎

Clearly, the discrete energy inequality, Lemma
3.5
, the time-continuity result, Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
enable us to apply Lemma
4.4
to obtain that
\{u_{h}\}_{h>0}
,
\{\widetilde{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}
and
\{\bar{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}
defined in (
4.1
)–(
4.5
) converge up to a subsequence strongly in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
to a limit
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
.

Using these preliminary estimates, we can now prove:

Theorem 4.5
.

The sequences
\{u_{h},\bar{u}_{h},\widetilde{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}
converge up to a subsequence to a Leray-Hopf solution
u
of (
1.1
) as in Definition
2.1
under the condition that
h=o(\sqrt{\Delta t})
as
h,\Delta t\to 0
.

Proof.

Using the definitions of the interpolations (
4.1
)–(
4.5
), we can rewrite the numerical scheme (
3.11
)–(
3.12
) as

(4.17)

\left(\partial_{t}u_{h},v\right)+b(\bar{u}_{h},\widetilde{u}_{h},v)+\mu(\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h},\nabla v)=(p_{h},\operatorname{div}v)+(f_{h},v),

and

(4.18)

(\operatorname{div}u_{h},q)=0,

with the test functions in the same space as in (
3.11
)–(
3.12
).
By the previous considerations, weak convergences (
4.6
)–(
4.9
), Lemma
3.5
, Lemma
4.1
, Lemma
4.2
, and Lemma
4.4
, we can improve (
4.6
)–(
4.9
) to

(4.19)

\displaystyle u_{h},\bar{u}_{h},\widetilde{u}_{h}\to u,

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }\,L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),

(4.20)

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{h}\rightharpoonup u,

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }\,L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)).

From (
4.18
), we obtain by using
\operatorname{div}u_{h}
as a test function that
\operatorname{div}u_{h}=\operatorname{div}\bar{u}_{h}=0
in
[0,T]\times\Omega
. Thus by (
4.19
), we also get
\operatorname{div}u=0
for a.e.
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
.

Now we take a test function
v\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,T)\times\Omega)
that is divergence free,
integrate in time and rewrite (
4.17
) as

(4.21)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\underbrace{\left(\partial_{t}u_{h},v\right)}_{(i)}+\underbrace{b(\bar{u}_{h},\widetilde{u}_{h},v)}_{(ii)}+\underbrace{\mu(\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h},\nabla v)}_{(iii)}-\underbrace{(f_{h},v)}_{(iv)}\Big]dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\underbrace{\left(\partial_{t}u_{h},v-\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v\right)}_{(v)}+\underbrace{b(\bar{u}_{h},\widetilde{u}_{h},v-\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)}_{(vi)}+\underbrace{\mu(\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h},\nabla(v-\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v))}_{(vii)}\Big]dt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\underbrace{(p_{h},\operatorname{div}\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)}_{(viii)}-\underbrace{(f_{h},v-\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)}_{(ix)}\Big]dt,

where
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}
is the
L^{2}
-projection onto
\mathbb{U}_{h}
as before. We consider the limits
h,\Delta t\to 0
in each of these terms. For the first term, we integrate by parts to obtain

\int_{0}^{T}(i)dt=-\int_{0}^{T}(u_{h},\partial_{t}v)dt-(u_{h}^{0},v(0,\cdot))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}-\int_{0}^{T}(u,\partial_{t}v)dt-(u_{0},v(0,\cdot)),

using that
u_{h}\rightharpoonup u
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
and
u_{h}^{0}\to u_{0}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. For the second term, we integrate by parts in space, and then use that
\bar{u}_{h},\widetilde{u}_{h}\to u
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
and that
\bar{u}_{h}
is divergence free:

\int_{0}^{T}(ii)dt=-\int_{0}^{T}b(\bar{u}_{h},v,\widetilde{u}_{h})dt=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\bar{u}_{h}\cdot\nabla)v\cdot\widetilde{u}_{h}dxdt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(u\cdot\nabla)v\cdot udxdt=-\int_{0}^{T}b(u,v,u)dt.

For the third term, we use that
\widetilde{u}_{h}\rightharpoonup u
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
and hence

\int_{0}^{T}(iii)dt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mu\int_{0}^{T}(\nabla u,\nabla v)dt.

We consider the fourth term: We have

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}(iv)dt&=\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\int_{t^{m}}^{t^{m+1}}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}f^{m},v)dt\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\int_{t^{m}}^{t^{m+1}}(f^{m},\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)dt\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\int_{t^{m}}^{t^{m+1}}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{t^{m}}^{t^{m+1}}(f(s),\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v-v)dsdt+\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\int_{t^{m}}^{t^{m+1}}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{t^{m}}^{t^{m+1}}(f(s),v)dsdt\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h,\Delta t\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{0}^{T}(f,v)dt,\end{split}

since time averages converge in
L^{2}
and
\left\lVert v-\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0
as
h\to 0
. Hence the left hand side of (
4.21
) converges to

\int_{0}^{T}\left[(u,\partial_{t}v)+b(u,v,u)-\mu(\nabla u,\nabla v)+(f,v)\right]dt+(u_{0},v(0,\cdot)).

In order to conclude that
u
satisfies the weak formulation of (
1.1
), we thus need to show that the right hand side converges to zero.
So, we consider the terms on the right hand side: For term (v), since
\partial_{t}u_{h}\in\mathbb{U}_{h}
, we have
(\partial_{t}u_{h},v)=(\partial_{t}u_{h},\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)
, hence this term vanishes.
Next,

\begin{split}\left|\int_{0}^{T}(vi)dt\right|=\left|\int_{0}^{T}b(\bar{u}_{h},\widetilde{u}_{h},v-\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)dt\right|&\leq\left\lVert\bar{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\left\lVert v-\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\\
&\leq Ch\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))},\end{split}

where we used the approximation property of the
L^{2}
-projection, (
3.7c
), and the energy estimate, Lemma
3.5
. Thus also this term vanishes as
h,\Delta t\to 0
.
For term (vii), we have

\begin{split}\left|\int_{0}^{T}(vii)dt\right|&=\left|\mu\int_{0}^{T}(\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h},\nabla(v-\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v))dt\right|\\
&\leq\mu\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\left\lVert\nabla(v-\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\\
&\leq Ch\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))},\end{split}

again using the approximation property of the
L^{2}
-projection, (
3.7d
), and the discrete energy estimate.
For the term (viii), we have, using that
v
is divergence free,

\begin{split}\left|\int_{0}^{T}(viii)dt\right|&=\left|\int_{0}^{T}(p_{h},\operatorname{div}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v-v))dt\right|\\
&\leq\left\lVert p_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\left\lVert\operatorname{div}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v-v)\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta t}}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}-u^{m+1}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}|v-\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v|_{H^{1}}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{C(E_{h}^{0}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{2})^{1/2}h}{\sqrt{\Delta t}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))},\end{split}

similar to the estimate (
4.11
). Clearly, also this term goes to zero if
h=o(\sqrt{\Delta t})
.
Finally, for the ninth term, we have, since
f_{h}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}f^{m}
,

(ix)=(f_{h},v-\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)=0,

i.e., this term vanishes.
Thus we obtain in the limit

(4.22)

\int_{0}^{T}\left[(u,\partial_{t}v)+b(u,v,u)-\mu(\nabla u,\nabla v)+(f,v)\right]dt+(u_{0},v(0,\cdot))=0,

for any divergence free
v\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,T)\times\Omega))
. Passing to the limit in the discrete energy balance (
3.18
) and using the properties of weak convergence, we obtain that the limit
u
satisfies for almost any
t\in[0,T]
,

(4.23)

\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\mu\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla u(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}ds\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}(f(s),u(s))ds,

which is the energy inequality (
2.4
). This implies the spatial regularity required in (
2.1
). To see that
u
satisfies the time regularity in (
2.1
), we note that due to the spatial regularity of
u
, we can use the density of smooth functions and extend the weak formulation (
4.22
) to functions
v\in L^{4}(0,T;H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega))
. Finally, we establish the continuity at zero. We write

\left\lVert u(t)-u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-2(u(t),u_{0}).

First, we note that
\partial_{t}u\in L^{4/3}(0,T;(H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)^{*}))
and
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega))
imply that
u
is weakly continuous in time with values in
L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)
, i.e., the mapping

t\mapsto(u(t),v),

is continuous for any
v\in L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)
(see for example Lemma II.5.9 in
[
4
]
or
[
30
]
). This implies that

\lim_{t\to 0}(u(t),u_{0})=\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Furthermore, from Fatou’s lemma, we obtain that

\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\lim_{t\to 0}\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

On the other hand, sending
t\to 0
in the energy inequality (
4.23
), we obtain that

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Thus

\lim_{t\to 0}\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

and we conclude that

\lim_{t\to 0}\left\lVert u(t)-u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=0.

Therefore,
u
is a Leray-Hopf weak solution of (
1.1
).
∎

5.
Convergence of projection method with projection step as a Poisson problem

While the discretization of the projection step in (
3.2
) could be considered as a Darcy problem, one can consider a discretization as a Poisson problem. In this case, one can obtain stability and convergence of a fully discrete scheme for the incremental version of the projection method. This was already done in
[
12
]
by the use of a special interpolation operator which was constructed explicitly for the lowest degree Taylor-Hood finite elements. No inf-sup condition such as (
3.8
) needed to be assumed for this to work. We will show here that the proof can be modified to prove that the linearly implicit discretization of the incremental projection method below converges without the need for this interpolation operator and therefore independently of the degree of the finite elements. This requires an adaption of the compactness arguments in
[
12
]
but also shortens the proof significantly. We will outline the compactness arguments here and sketch the rest of the proof as it can be achieved in a similar way to the proof of convergence of Chorin’s version of the projection method in the previous sections or the proof in
[
12
]
.
We consider the following discretization of (
1.1
): We use the same notation and trilinear form
b
as in Section
3.2
with the following modifications. We will seek the intermediate velocity
\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}
in a space
\mathbb{U}_{h}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, the pressure
p_{h}^{m+1}
in a space
\mathbb{P}_{h}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
and the final velocity
u_{h}^{m+1}
in the space
\mathbb{Y}_{h}=\mathbb{U}_{h}+\nabla\mathbb{P}_{h}
. Note that now
\mathbb{P}_{h}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)
and therefore
\nabla\mathbb{P}_{h}
is well-defined (though possibly containing discontinuous functions). Also note that the final velocity
u_{h}^{m+1}
may not satisfy homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions in this case. We require both the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projections
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{U}_{h}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{P}_{h}}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{P}_{h}
defined by (
3.6
) to satisfy the properties (
3.7
). In contrast to the previous sections,
\mathbb{P}_{h}
is now a subspace of
H^{1}(\Omega)
and the finite elements therefore may have more regularity.
As in
[
12
]
, we define the space

(5.1)

\mathbb{V}_{h}=\left\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\,,\,(v,\nabla q)=0,\quad\forall q\in\mathbb{P}_{h}\right\}.

Note that this is not a finite element space and not finite dimensional. We clearly have
L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)\subset\mathbb{V}_{h}
. We denote the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(\Omega)
onto
\mathbb{V}_{h}
by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{V}_{h}
, i.e., we have for any
v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
that

(\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}v,w)=(v,w),\quad\forall w\in\mathbb{V}_{h}.

Moreover,
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}
is the identity on
\mathbb{V}_{h}
.
This implies that

\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}v-v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-2(\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}v,v)=\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

thus

\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

We also have the following simple ‘commuting’ property for
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}
and
\mathcal{P}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)
, the Leray projector:

Lemma 5.1
.

We have that

(5.2)

\mathcal{P}\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}v=\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}\mathcal{P}v=\mathcal{P}v,\quad\forall\,v\in L^{2}(\Omega).

Proof.

Since
L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)\subset\mathbb{V}_{h}
for any
h>0
, we have that
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}
is the identity on
L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)
and hence
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}\mathcal{P}v=\mathcal{P}v
. For the other identity, we take
v,w\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and compute, using that
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}
are self-adjoint,

(\mathcal{P}\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}v,w)=(\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}v,\mathcal{P}w)=(v,\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}\mathcal{P}w)=(v,\mathcal{P}w)=(\mathcal{P}v,w),

implying the claim.
∎

We start by approximating the initial data. Given
u_{0}\in L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)
, we let
\widetilde{u}_{h}^{0}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}u_{0}
, and define
u_{h}^{0}
and
p^{0}_{h}
by projecting it onto
\mathbb{V}_{h}\cap\mathbb{Y}_{h}\times\mathbb{P}_{h}
,
using the following projection step:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{u^{0}_{h}-\widetilde{u}^{0}_{h}}{\Delta t},v\right)

\displaystyle=-(\nabla p^{0}_{h},v),

\displaystyle\left(u^{0}_{h},\nabla q\right)

\displaystyle=0,

for all
(v,q)\in\mathbb{Y}_{h}\times\mathbb{P}_{h}
.
Then we compute updates using the following scheme:

Step 1

(Prediction step):
For any
m\geq 0
, given
u_{h}^{m}\in\mathbb{Y}_{h}
,
p_{h}^{m}\in\mathbb{P}_{h}
, find
\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}\in\mathbb{U}_{h}
, such that for all
v\in\mathbb{U}_{h}

(5.3)

\left(\frac{\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}-u^{m}_{h}}{\Delta t},v\right)+b(\widetilde{u}^{m}_{h},\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h},v)+\mu(\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1},\nabla v)-(p_{h}^{m},\operatorname{div}v)=(\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}f^{m},v),

Step 2

(Projection step): Next we seek
(u^{m+1}_{h},p^{m+1}_{h})\in\mathbb{Y}_{h}\times\mathbb{P}_{h}
which satisfy for all
(v,q)\in\mathbb{Y}_{h}\times\mathbb{P}_{h}
,

(5.4a)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{u^{m+1}_{h}-\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}}{\Delta t},v\right)

\displaystyle=-(\nabla(p^{m+1}_{h}-p_{h}^{m}),v),

(5.4b)

\displaystyle\left(u^{m+1}_{h},\nabla q\right)

\displaystyle=0.

Remark 5.2
.

We note that restricted to
\mathbb{Y}_{h}
,
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}
corresponds exactly to the projection onto
\mathbb{H}_{h}=\ker(C_{h})
, where the operator
C_{h}:\mathbb{Y}_{h}\to\mathbb{P}_{h}
is defined as in
[
18
]
, by
(C_{h}u,q)=(u,\nabla q)
for all
q\in\mathbb{P}_{h}
, i.e., one writes (
5.4a
)–(
5.4b
) as

\begin{split}\frac{u^{m+1}_{h}-\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}}{\Delta t}&=-C_{h}^{\top}(p^{m+1}_{h}-p_{h}^{m}),\\
C_{h}u^{m+1}_{h}&=0.\end{split}

Thus
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}
is exactly the projection step (
5.4
) of the numerical scheme, when restricted to
\mathbb{Y}_{h}
. In particular, we have
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}=u^{m+1}_{h}
.

Remark 5.3

(Formulation of projection step as a Poisson problem)
.

The projection step can be rewritten as a Poisson problem as follows: Taking
\nabla q\in\nabla\mathbb{P}_{h}
as a test function in (
5.4a
) and using the weak divergence constraint (
5.4b
), we see that
p_{h}^{m+1}\in\mathbb{P}_{h}
can be computed from

(\nabla p^{m+1}_{h},\nabla q)=(\nabla p^{m}_{h},\nabla q)-\left(\frac{\operatorname{div}\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}}{\Delta t},q\right),

and then
u_{h}^{m+1}
can be computed via (
5.4a
).

The solvability of the scheme (
5.3
)–(
5.4
) is shown in the same way as for the nonincremental Darcy version of the scheme above in Lemma
3.4
, or see also
[
12
, Lemma 2.1]
. Furthermore, the scheme satisfies the discrete energy estimate

Lemma 5.4

(Discrete energy estimate
[
12
]
)
.

The approximations computed by the scheme (
5.3
)–(
5.4
) satisfy for any integer
M\in\left\{0,1,\dots,\frac{T}{\Delta t}\right\}

(5.5)

E^{M}_{h}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}-u_{h}^{m}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\mu\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=E_{h}^{0}+\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}(f^{m},\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}),

where

E_{h}^{M}:=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u_{h}^{M}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{\Delta t^{2}}{2}\left\lVert\nabla p_{h}^{M}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Proof.

This was proved in
[
12
, Lemma 3.1]
.
∎

As in Remark
3.6
, this results in a uniform in
h,\Delta t>0
bound on the energy after applying the discrete Grönwall inequality, Lemma
A.1
. As in the previous section, we denote piecewise constant and piecewise linear interpolations of the approximations
u_{h}^{m}
,
\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m}
and
p_{h}^{m}
by (
4.1
)–(
4.5
).

Remark 5.5
.

Note that the interpolation operator
\Pi_{N}
as defined in
[
12
]
was not needed to prove this lemma in
[
12
]
, it was only to prove the convergence of the scheme.

5.1.
Convergence of the numerical scheme

For this scheme, we do not have
\operatorname{div}u_{h}=0
almost everywhere, we only have the weak form
(u^{m+1}_{h},\nabla q)=0
for all
q\in\mathbb{P}_{h}
. Since
u^{m+1}_{h}
is now sought in the space
\mathbb{Y}_{h}=\mathbb{X}_{h}+\nabla\mathbb{P}_{h}
instead of in
\mathbb{U}_{h}
, we also do not know if
u^{m+1}_{h}
is zero on the boundary of the domain and therefore we cannot integrate by parts in the weak form to conclude that the divergence is zero almost everywhere. In order to deal with this issue, we need to derive an alternative version of Lemma
4.3
by Lions which is similar to Lemma 3.5 in
[
12
]
, except that different spaces are used and no interpolation operator
\Pi_{N}
is needed besides the standard
L^{2}
-orthogonal projections (
3.6
), and the orthogonal projection
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}
.

First, from the energy balance, Lemma
5.4
, we obtain the following uniform a priori estimates for the sequences
\{\bar{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}
and
\{\widetilde{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}
:

\displaystyle\{\bar{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}\subset L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\{\widetilde{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}\subset L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)).

These uniform estimates imply, using the Banach-Alaoglu theorem that there exist weakly convergent subsequences, which, for the ease of notation, we still denote by
h\to 0
,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{h}\overset{\star}{\rightharpoonup}\bar{u},

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }\,L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),

(5.7)

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{h}\overset{\star}{\rightharpoonup}\widetilde{u},

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }\,L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),

(5.8)

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{h}\rightharpoonup\widetilde{u},

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }\,L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)).

Due to the discrete energy estimate (
5.5
), Lemma
4.2
still applies to the sequences
\{\bar{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}
and
\{\widetilde{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}
and they have the same weak limit
u\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
. The limit is weakly divergence free:

Lemma 5.6
.

Any weak limit
u
of the sequence
\{\bar{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}
satisfies

(u,\nabla q)=0,\quad\forall\,q\in H^{1}(\Omega).

Proof.

Since
u
is the weak limit of
\{\bar{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}
, we have

(u,\nabla q)=\lim_{h\to 0}(\bar{u}_{h},\nabla q),

for any
q\in H^{1}(\Omega)
for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
.
Then we can write

|(\bar{u}_{h},\nabla q)|=|(\bar{u}_{h},\nabla(q-\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{P}_{h}}q))|\leq\left\lVert\bar{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla(q-\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{P}_{h}}q)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,

since
\bar{u}_{h}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{P}_{h}}q\to q
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
as
h\to 0
for any
q\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Thus, in the limit
(u,\nabla q)=0
for any
q\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.
∎

In order to pass to the limit in the weak formulation of the scheme, we need to derive strong convergence of the approximations of the velocity field in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
. To do so, we need to prove an estimate on the continuity in time of
\widetilde{u}_{h}
with values in a weak space (this lemma is a slight adaption to a different space of Lemma 3.3 in
[
12
]
):

Lemma 5.7
.

Denote
Z=(H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega))^{*}
. Assume that
h\leq C\Delta t
. Then the aproximations
\widetilde{u}_{h}
computed by (
5.3
)–(
5.4
) satisfy for any
0\leq\tau<T
,

(5.9)

\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}(t+\tau)-\widetilde{u}_{h}(t)\right\rVert_{Z}^{2}dt\leq C\tau(\tau+\Delta t).

Proof.

Given
\tau>0
and
t\in[0,T-\tau]
, let
m_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\widetilde{u}_{h}(t)=u_{h}^{m_{1}}
and
m_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\widetilde{u}_{h}(t+\tau)=\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m_{2}+1}
. Then

\widetilde{u}_{h}(t+\tau)-\widetilde{u}_{h}(t)=\sum_{m=m_{1}}^{m_{2}}(\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}-\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m}).

We take a test function
v\in H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
, and plug in the scheme (
5.3
)–(
5.4
),

\displaystyle(\widetilde{u}_{h}(t+\tau)-\widetilde{u}_{h}(t),v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=m_{1}}^{m_{2}}(\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}-\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m},v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=m_{1}}^{m_{2}}(\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}-\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m},\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)

\displaystyle=-\Delta t\sum_{m=m_{1}}^{m_{2}}\underbrace{b(\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m},\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1},\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)}_{\text{I}}-\Delta t\sum_{m=m_{1}}^{m_{2}}\underbrace{\mu(\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1},\nabla\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)}_{\text{II}}

\displaystyle\quad-\Delta t\sum_{m=m_{1}}^{m_{2}}\underbrace{(2\nabla p_{h}^{m}-\nabla p_{h}^{m-1},\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)}_{\text{III}}+\Delta t\sum_{m=m_{1}}^{m_{2}}\underbrace{(f^{m},\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)}_{\text{IV}}.

We estimate each of the terms I – IV, similar to what we did in Lemma
4.1
:

\displaystyle|\text{I}|

\displaystyle=\left|b(\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m},\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1},\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\widetilde{u}^{m}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\widetilde{u}^{m}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\widetilde{u}^{m}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{2}},

using (
3.7a
).
For the second term, we have

\displaystyle|\text{II}|

\displaystyle\leq\mu\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

using (
3.7b
).
For the third term, III, we have, using that
v
is divergence free, the properties of the
L^{2}
-projection, (
3.7
), and that by the energy estimate
\left\lVert\nabla p\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\Delta t^{-2}
,

\displaystyle|\text{III}|

\displaystyle\leq C(\left\lVert\nabla p^{m}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert\nabla p^{m-1}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)})\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{U}_{h}}v-v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\left\lVert\nabla p^{m}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert\nabla p^{m-1}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)})h^{2}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{h^{2}}{\Delta t^{2}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)},

under the condition that
h\leq C\Delta t
.
Finally, for the last term, IV, we have

|\text{IV}|\leq\left\lVert f^{m}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Combining the estimates I – IV, we have, after taking the supremum over
v\in H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
,

\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}(t+\tau)-\widetilde{u}_{h}(t)\right\rVert_{Z}\leq C\Delta t\sum_{m=m_{1}}^{m_{2}}\left(\left\lVert\widetilde{u}^{m}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+1+\left\lVert f^{m}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

We square this identity and use that
\Delta t(m_{2}-m_{1})\leq\tau+\Delta t
,

\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}(t+\tau)-\widetilde{u}_{h}(t)\right\rVert_{Z}^{2}\leq C(\tau+\Delta t)\Delta t\sum_{m=m_{1}}^{m_{2}}\left(\left\lVert\widetilde{u}^{m}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}^{m+1}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}^{m+1}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+1+\left\lVert f^{m}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

and then integrate this identity over time to obtain, using the energy estimate, Lemma
5.4
,

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}(t+\tau)-\widetilde{u}_{h}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{Z}dt\\
&\leq C(\tau+\Delta t)\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\int_{t}^{t+\tau}\left(\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}(s-\Delta t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}(s)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+1+\left\lVert f_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)dsdt\\
&\leq C(\tau+\Delta t)\tau\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\left(\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}(s-\Delta t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}(s)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+1+\left\lVert f_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)ds\\
&\leq C(\tau+\Delta t)\tau.\end{split}

∎

Finally, we need the following Lions-like lemma, which will then allow us to prove a stronger time continuity estimate and apply Simon’s compactness criterion:

Lemma 5.8
.

Denote
Z=(H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega))^{*}
. Then for any
\eta>0
there exists a constant
C_{\eta}>0
and
h_{\eta}>0
such that for all
h\leq h_{\eta}
we have

(5.10)

\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}w\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\eta\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}+C_{\eta}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{Z}\quad\forall w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Proof.

The proof of this lemma is by contradiction. Assume that (
5.10
) does not hold. Then there exists
\eta>0
such that for all
C>0
and
\bar{h}>0
there exists
0<h<\bar{h}
and
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}>\eta\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}+C\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{Z}.

We generate a sequence
\{v_{N}\}_{N}
by taking
C=\bar{h}^{-1}=n\in\mathbb{N}
and
h^{-1}:=N\geq n
strictly increasing (so that
h
is strictly decreasing), such that

\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{N}v_{N}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}>\eta\left\lVert v_{N}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}+n\left\lVert v_{N}\right\rVert_{Z},

where we denoted
\mathcal{P}_{N}:=\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{N^{-1}}}
.
We define the mapping
N=\tau(n)
which is strictly increasing and hence has a strictly increasing inverse
\tau^{-1}
. Then the last statement can be rewritten in terms of
N
as

\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{N}v_{N}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}>\eta\left\lVert v_{N}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}+\tau^{-1}(N)\left\lVert v_{N}\right\rVert_{Z}.

Next, we define
w_{N}=v_{N}/\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{N}v_{N}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. Clearly
\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
. Therefore, the last statement translates to

1>\eta\left\lVert w_{N}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{0}}+\tau^{-1}(N)\left\lVert w_{N}\right\rVert_{Z}.

This implies that
\left\lVert w_{N}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{0}}
is uniformly bounded in
N
and therefore
\{w_{N}\}_{N}
has a subsequence that converges strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
to a limit
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, due to the compact embedding of
H^{1}_{0}
into
L^{2}
. We still denote the subsequence by
N
for convenience of notation. Then we must have that
\left\lVert w_{N}\right\rVert_{Z}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
since
\tau^{-1}(N)\to\infty
and the left hand side is bounded. Hence
\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{Z}=0
.
Since

\begin{split}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{Z}&=\sup_{\varphi\in H^{2}\cap H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)}\frac{|(w,\varphi)|}{\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{H^{2}\cap H_{\operatorname{div}}^{1}}}\\
&=\sup_{\varphi\in H^{2}\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{|(w,\mathcal{P}\varphi)|}{\left\lVert\mathcal{P}\varphi\right\rVert_{H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}}}\\
&=\sup_{\varphi\in H^{2}\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{|(\mathcal{P}w,\varphi)|}{\left\lVert\mathcal{P}\varphi\right\rVert_{H^{2}\cap H^{1}_{0}}}\\
&\geq\sup_{\varphi\in H^{2}\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{|(\mathcal{P}w,\varphi)|}{\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{H^{2}\cap H^{1}_{0}}}\\
&=\left\lVert\mathcal{P}w\right\rVert_{(H^{2}\cap H^{1}_{0})^{*}}\end{split}

using the properties of the Leray-projector
\mathcal{P}
, we obtain that

\left\lVert\mathcal{P}w\right\rVert_{(H^{2}\cap H^{1}_{0})^{*}}=0,

i.e.,
\mathcal{P}w=0
in
(H^{2}\cap H^{1}_{0})^{*}
. That means that

(\mathcal{P}w,\varphi)=0,\quad\forall\,\varphi\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Since
\mathcal{P}w\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we can extend this identity to
\varphi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and conclude that
\left\lVert\mathcal{P}w\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0
. Next, we want to show that
\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N}\to\mathcal{P}w
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
which would then imply a contraction, since
\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=1\neq 0=\left\lVert\mathcal{P}w\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
. We start by showing that
\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N}\rightharpoonup\mathcal{P}w
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Let us take
\varphi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and decompose using the Helmholtz-decomposition as

\varphi=\mathcal{P}\varphi+\nabla g
where
g\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Then we have, using Lemma
5.1
and that
w_{N}\to w
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
,

(\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N},\mathcal{P}\varphi)=(w_{N},\mathcal{P}_{N}\mathcal{P}\varphi)=(w_{N},\mathcal{P}\varphi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}(w,\mathcal{P}\varphi)=(\mathcal{P}w,\varphi).

On the other hand, we have, using that
\nabla\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{P}_{h}}g\to\nabla g
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
h\to 0
, and that
(\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N},\nabla q)=0
for all
q\in\mathbb{P}_{N^{-1}}

|(\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N},\nabla g)|=|(\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N},\nabla(g-\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{P}_{N^{-1}}}g))|\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla(g-\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{P}_{N^{-1}}}g)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

Combining these two insights, we obtain

(\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N},\varphi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}(\mathcal{P}w,\varphi),

thus
\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N}\rightharpoonup\mathcal{P}w
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Next, we consider the norm of
\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N}
:

\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=(\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N},\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N})=(\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N},w_{N})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}(\mathcal{P}w,w)=\left\lVert\mathcal{P}w\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

where we used that
w_{N}\to w
strongly and
\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N}\rightharpoonup\mathcal{P}w
weakly in
L^{2}
and the properties of the orthogonal projections
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}_{N}
. This implies that
\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N}\to\mathcal{P}w
in
L^{2}
as follows:

\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N}-\mathcal{P}w\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathcal{P}w\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-2(\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N},\mathcal{P}w)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,

using the weak convergence of
\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N}
and the convergence of the
L^{2}
-norm. Thus, we conclude that

1=\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{N}w_{N}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\left\lVert\mathcal{P}w\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=0,

which yields the desired contradiction.
∎

Next, we use this result to show uniform time continuity of
\widetilde{u}_{h}
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
which we can then use together with Simon’s version of the Aubin-Lions-Simon lemma
[
27
, Theorem 1]
, stated in Theorem
B.1
, to conclude precompactness of the sequence
\{\widetilde{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
. The following lemma and its proof closely follow
[
12
, Lemma 3.6]
. We include it here both for completeness and to illustrate that it can be established using Lemma
5.8
, instead of the version employed in
[
12
]
.

Lemma 5.9
.

The approximations
\widetilde{u}_{h}
computed by the scheme (
5.3
)–(
5.4
) satisfy

(5.11)

\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}(t+\tau)-\widetilde{u}_{h}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}dt\longrightarrow 0,\quad\text{as }\,\tau\to 0,

uniformly with respect to
h,\Delta t>0
.

Proof.

We recall from Remark
5.2
that
\bar{u}_{h}
is given by the projection of
\widetilde{u}_{h}
onto
\mathbb{V}_{h}
, specifically
\bar{u}_{h}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}\widetilde{u}_{h}
. Then, we can estimate the quantity in (
5.11
) as follows, using that
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}
is an orthogonal projection:

\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}(t+\tau)-\widetilde{u}_{h}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}dt=\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}(t+\tau)-\widetilde{u}_{h}(t)-(\bar{u}_{h}(t+\tau)-\bar{u}_{h}(t))\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}dt}_{(i)}\\
+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\left\lVert\bar{u}_{h}(t+\tau)-\bar{u}_{h}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}dt}_{(ii)}.

We let
\epsilon>0
arbitrary and given. For the first term, we have, using that
\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}-\bar{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\Delta t
by Lemma
4.2
, that there is
h_{1}>0
(and corresponding
\Delta t
) small enough such that for any
0<h<h_{1}
, we have

(i)\leq 2\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}(t+\tau)-\bar{u}_{h}(t+\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}dt+2\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}(t)-\bar{u}_{h}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}dt<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

For the second term (ii), we use Lemma
5.8
combined with the weak time continuity of
\widetilde{u}_{h}
, Lemma
5.7
, the discrete energy inequality, Lemma
5.4
and that
\bar{u}_{h}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{V}_{h}}\widetilde{u}_{h}
to estimate for any
\eta>0
and any
0<h<\min\{h_{\eta},h_{1}\}
,

\begin{split}(ii)&\leq\eta\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}(t+\tau)-\widetilde{u}_{h}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}dt+C_{\eta}\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}(t+\tau)-\widetilde{u}_{h}(t)\right\rVert_{Z}^{2}dt\\
&\leq\eta C_{E_{0},f}+C_{\eta}\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}(t+\tau)-\widetilde{u}_{h}(t)\right\rVert_{Z}^{2}dt\end{split}

where
Z:=(H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega))^{*}
. Now we first choose
\eta=\epsilon/(4C_{E_{0},f})
and then since we know from Lemma
5.7
that the second term goes to zero as
\tau\to 0
, we have that for any
0<\tau<\bar{\tau}
small enough that

C_{\eta}\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}(t+\tau)-\widetilde{u}_{h}(t)\right\rVert_{Z}^{2}dt\leq\frac{\epsilon}{4}.

Thus, combining these estimates, we obtain that for any

0<h<\min\{h_{\eta},h_{1}\}
and
0<\tau<\bar{\tau}
,

\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\left\lVert\widetilde{u}_{h}(t+\tau)-\widetilde{u}_{h}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}dt<\epsilon,

which proves the result since
\epsilon
was arbitrary.
∎

The result of this lemma, combined with the uniform
L^{2}([0,T];H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
-bound on the
\widetilde{u}_{h}
coming from the energy estimate, Lemma
5.4
, allow us to apply Simon’s version of the Aubin-Lions-Simon lemma, Theorem
B.1
, to conclude that a subsequence of
\{\widetilde{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}
converges strongly in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
to the limit
u
. By Lemma
4.2
, this implies that also
\{\bar{u}_{h}\}_{h>0}
has a strongly convergent subsequence to the same limit
u
. Then the fact that
u
is a Leray-Hopf solution of the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations follows along the lines of Theorem
4.5
with only slight modifications, or one can also check that Step 2 of Theorem 3.8 in
[
12
]
applies.

Appendix A
Discrete Grönwall inequality

The following lemma is useful for proving an energy bound in the case that the external source in the fluid equation is nonzero.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\geq 0
, and
\{b_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\geq 0
be two nonnegative sequences satisfying

(A.1)

a_{n}\leq(1+\mu\Delta t)(a_{n-1}+\beta b_{n-1}),

for parameters
\mu,\Delta t,\beta\geq 0
.
Then
a_{n}
satisfies

(A.2)

a_{n}\leq\exp(\mu\Delta tn)a_{0}+\beta\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\exp(\mu\Delta t(n-m))b_{m},

and in particular,

a_{n}\leq\exp(\mu\Delta tn)\left(a_{0}+\beta\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\ b_{m}\right).

Proof.

We can prove this by induction on
n
. Clearly, it is true for
n=0
. For the induction step, we assume the statement holds for
a_{n-1}
and deduce that it also holds for
a_{n}
. Thus, we take (
A.1
) for
a_{n}
and plug in (
A.2
) for
n-1
:

\displaystyle a_{n}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\mu\Delta t)(a_{n-1}+\beta b_{n-1})

\displaystyle\leq(1+\mu\Delta t)\left(\exp(\mu\Delta t(n-1))a_{0}+\beta\sum_{m=0}^{n-2}\exp(\mu\Delta t(n-1-m))b_{m}+\beta b_{n-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(\mu\Delta t)\left(\exp(\mu\Delta t(n-1))a_{0}+\beta\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\exp(\mu\Delta t(n-1-m))b_{m}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(\mu\Delta tn)a_{0}+\beta\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\exp(\mu\Delta t(n-m))b_{m},

where we used that for positive
x
, we have
1+x\leq\exp(x)
. The second statement follows by upper bounding
\exp(\mu\Delta t(n-m))
by
\exp(\mu\Delta tn)
in the sum above.
∎

Appendix B
Aubin-Lions-Simon lemma

We recall Simon’s version of the Aubin-Lions-Simon lemma
[
27
, Theorem 1]
:

Theorem B.1

(Simon’s compactness criterion
[
27
]
)
.

Let
F\subset L^{p}(0,T;B)
where
1\leq p<\infty
and
B
is a Banach space. Then
F
is relatively compact in
L^{p}(0,T;B)
if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\left\{\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}f(t)dt\,:\,f\in F\right\}
is relatively compact in
B
for all
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<T
,

(2)

\left\lVert f(\cdot+\tau)-f\right\rVert_{L^{p}(0,T-\tau;B)}\longrightarrow 0
as
\tau\to 0
, uniformly for
f\in F
.

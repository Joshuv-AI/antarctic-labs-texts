Open Sesame: A Comparative Evaluation of Parental and Dentist Perception on Various Mouth Props
Authors: K LakshmiKumari, Kavitha Rajesh, N Megalaa, Nincy Francis, K Ranjith, K Manikandan, Nagappan Nagappan, Gowrishankar Sekar
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:The most commonly used adjunct on a cooperative child is the mouth prop. However, the design variation of the mouth props and the possible disadvantages make it difficult to select the appropriate mouth prop. Hence, this study was conducted to evaluate and compare the effectiveness and acceptance of custom-made mouth props with that of commercially available mouth props.Aim:To evaluate and compare the effectiveness and acceptance of custom-made mouth props with that of commercially available mouth props.Methodology:A custom-made mouth prop was fabricated that had added advantages of intraoral illumination and customized mouth opening and was used along with three commercially available mouth props on 10 children aged 4 to 10 years who required restorative procedures and were evaluated for their effectiveness based on the response given by the dentists and parents to the questionnaire provided to them. The results were evaluated using the Chi-square test.Results:The custom-made mouth prop was more acceptable when compared to other available mouth props owing to its property of intraoral illumination, customized mouth opening, and child-friendly design.Conclusion:The study results concluded that custom-made mouth props can be considered an effective adjunct for mouth opening.

INTRODUCTION

A pediatric dentist uses various behavior management techniques to provide excellent dental care. However, the smaller oral anatomy of the child, the inability to keep the mouth open for a long time, and the limited field of vision make it difficult for the dentist to deliver quality dental care. Prolonged mouth opening will also cause a negative shift in the child’s behavior. The maximum mouth opening (MMO) in Indian children is 46.04 mm, 48.53 mm, and 52.38 mm for boys and 45.95 mm, 47.27 mm, and 52.05 mm for girls, in the age groups of 6 to 8, 8 to 10, and 10 to 12 years, respectively.[1] Behavior problems in the dental office are often related to the child’s inability to communicate with the dentist and follow instructions.[2]

Various adjuncts have been used to attain a good field of vision and operation. One of the most widely used adjuncts is mouth props and is universally accepted for nearly all dental procedures.[3] According to Field et al.,[4] voice control, mouth props, positive reinforcement, and tell-show-do are acceptable for nearly all dental procedures.

Hence, we constructed the custom-made mouth prop to overcome the overt limitations of the most commonly available mouth props in the Indian market whose effectiveness can be improved by incorporating intraoral illumination and a self-adjustment system. Much attention was given to intraoral illumination. Thus, this study was undertaken to evaluate the effectiveness as well as the parents’ and dentists’ acceptance of custom-made mouth props and compare it with three other commercially available mouth props.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Ten children of age group 4–10 years who are potentially cooperative but are unable to keep their mouth open for longer duration and those lacking cooperative ability undergoing quadrant-wise restorative procedures were selected for this study from pediatric outpatient wing and private dental clinics of Chennai. Children under 3 years and emotionally immature children, and those with TMJ problems were excluded from this study.

Before starting the treatment, children were familiarized with various mouth props using the TSD technique along with their parents. The children underwent quadrant-wise restoration with one type of mouth prop being used in each visit. Thus, each child was exposed to all four mouth props in four visits. Each child was treated by the same dentist during all the four visits. The custom-made mouth prop was fabricated as a block of clear acrylic resin similar to the McKesson mouth prop into which an LED light and jack screw were incorporated; the mouth prop was lined by a soft splint to avoid any injury to the soft tissues. The purpose of adding a jack screw is to facilitate customized mouth opening for the child, whereas LED light is for intraoral illumination.

The parents were asked to observe the mouth prop from the time of placement until the time of removal of mouth prop after treatment completion. The treatment procedure conducted included non-invasive restorative procedures including cavitated lesions pertaining to enamel, enamel, and dentin, pulp capping, and pit and fissure sealant application. At the end of each session, two different questionnaires were given to both parents and dentists to fill in separately about their opinions about each mouth prop. Adequate time was given between visits to eliminate the carryover effect and the Harthrone phenomenon. Results were tabulated. Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS software (version 19) using the Chi-square test. P-values less than 0.05 were considered to be statistically significant.

DISCUSSION

All mouth props were acceptable by the parents as well as the dentist; however, the most acceptable mouth props were the custom-made mouth props and the McKensson mouth prop. According to the AAPD guidelines 2012, although a mouth prop may be used as an immobilization device, its use in a compliant child is not considered protective stabilization.[2] Finally, the dental profession must do some honest introspection to determine whether centuries of past acceptance of physical restraint represent an acceptable reason to continue the same barbaric practice.[5]

In this study, we took the most commonly available mouth props in the Indian market and mouth props that are commonly preferred by the dentist. When an overall opinion was asked, the majority of the parents felt the use of mouth props was comfortable for their child. When mouth props were compared individually, 9 out of 10 parents preferred the use of custom-made and McKesson mouth props over other mouth props. This was due to the child-friendly design and the ease of placement of the mouth prop. Molt’s mouth gag was considered to be the most stressful for their child because of the cumbersome placement [Graphs 1 and 2].

When the parents were asked about their overall most preferred mouth prop, they selected the custom-made mouth prop because of its ease of application and child-friendly design. The next most preferred mouth prop was the McKesson mouth prop, and Molt’s was the least preferred by parents [Graph 3].

One patient exhibited trauma when Molt’s mouth prop was used, whereas all other three mouth props were safe for use in children [Graph 4]. Because of the trauma-causing nature of Molt’s mouth prop, it is not advisable for children lacking cooperative ability. When the overall acceptance was estimated, most dentists preferred the use of custom-made mouth props followed by McKensson and Mira bite blocks [Graph 5]. Because not much literature is available on the comparison of mouth props, the outcome of this study could not be compared with any previous research. This research is a preliminary attempt to improvise the design of the existing available mouth prop. Further research is required to confirm the study results with a larger sample size and in multiple treatments.

CONCLUSION

All mouth props were acceptable by parents and dentists as an adjunct for the child’s treatment. The results of this study conclude that the custom-made mouth prop was the most acceptable compared to other mouth props by both the parents and the dentists because of the ease of placement, child-friendly design, and its added advantage of intraoral illumination and customized mouth opening. Hence, the custom-made mouth prop can be considered a suitable alternative to commercially available mouth props.

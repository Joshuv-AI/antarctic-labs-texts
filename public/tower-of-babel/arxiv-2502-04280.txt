Mean-Field Analysis of Latent Variable Process Models on Dynamically Evolving Graphs with Feedback Effects
Ankan Ganguly, Konstantinos Spiliopoulos, Daniel Sussman

Abstract
We study the mean-field limit of a generic class of dynamic co-evolving latent space networks motivated by the social and opinion dynamics literature. Such models include n n agents, whose opinions are given by latent stochastic processes, and a dynamic network process describing agent interactions. Models in this class incorporate (a) bi-directional feedback between the latent processes and the network process, (b) persistence effects, meaning that the network structure at the current time depends on the value of the latent processes at the current time but also on the network structure at the previous time instance and (c) localized interactions, meaning that individual agents do not have global information. We characterize the distributional limit of a random sample taken from the latent space network as the number of nodes in the network diverges. We describe the rich conditional probabilistic structure of the resulting limiting model which we use to establish the limiting behavior of the following quantities: (i) the empirical measure of the latent process, (ii) a conditional empirical measure relating the latent process to the network process and (iii) the network process graphon. In proving our main results, we derive a general conditional propagation of chaos result, which is of independent interest. Our novel approach to studying the limiting behavior of random samples proves to be a very useful methodology for fully grasping the asymptotic behavior of co-evolving particle systems. Numerical results are included to illustrate the theoretical findings.

Our work is motivated by recent developments in the literature on social networks and polarization. Interacting particle systems are a natural choice of model for such studies. In such models, the evolution of agent beliefs (coded as latent variables) depends on their interactions (modeled as a dynamic network). In this context, it is natural to assume that agent interactions are heavily influenced by their opinions; agents tend to favor interactions with similar agents and avoid interactions with agents holding different opinions. As a result, it is natural to study dynamic co-evolving systems in which networks evolve over time, and the evolution of both the network and agent opinions depends on one another.

In the literature on social networks, this co-evolving interaction phenomenon is modeled via so-called co-evolving networks in which the links between the nodes and certain attributes of the nodes evolve over time in ways that affect each other. The class of models we study makes a couple of structural assumptions, which add to the technical difficulty of its analysis. First, it is assumed that two agents that interact at time
t
are (all else equal) more likely to interact at time
t+1
than a pair of agents that don’t interact at time
t
. Motivated by this phenomenon, the model includes persistence effects, meaning that the graph structure at the next time instance does not depend only on the corresponding latent process values but also on the graph structure at the current time instance. Second, it is assumed that individual agents have no global information, including the size of the population. In particular, this implies that the strength of the influence of one agent on another is inversely proportional to the second agent’s degree in the network rather than the size of the entire network.

Motivated by applications in social and opinion dynamics literature, we study a generic class of models satisfying the above criteria. Our work shows that such models possess a rich mathematical structure. We also remark that the mathematical tools developed in this paper are general, and we expect them to apply to a much larger class of models than described in this article.

To the best of our knowledge, our paper is among the first rigorous studies of the mean-field limit of an interacting particle system with dynamic, co-evolving networks. Indeed, models with co-evolving particles and networks are difficult to work with as the network itself is endogenous to the model. This can make it difficult to grasp properties of the underlying network. In addition, standard mean-field models are no longer sufficient, as it is also necessary to (in some way) capture the dependencies between agent beliefs and their interactions and to understand the limiting network structure. To resolve this problem, we introduce the
sample perspective
for interacting particle system limits. We examine the asymptotics of a random sample of fixed size
k
of agents in the limit as the population
n
converges to infinity. Within this random sample, we characterize the asymptotic joint distribution of agent opinion dynamics and the subnetwork trajectory induced by the random sample. We show that this limiting random sample (which we refer to as the mean-field limit) has a rich conditional structure which we use to establish propagation of chaos (asymptotic independence of beliefs), conditional propagation of chaos (asymptotic conditional independence of beliefs between two agents given their interaction history), related hydrodynamic limits and even a graphon limit of the underlying network. Including persistence effects also greatly complicates the conditional structure of the model. Lastly, as a result of our assumption that individual agents only have access to local information, interactions between particles are non-linear functions of the local empirical measure. This induces some significant technical challenges in establishing sufficient uniform integrability. To resolve these challenges, we must establish lower bounds on the number of interactions involving any individual agent. Lastly, to properly capture the heterogeneity in the joint structure of the graph at different times, we treat the graph trajectories as multiplexes. The term multiplex refers to a collection of graphs sharing a vertex set, with each graph being a layer of the multiplex network. This point of view allows us to study and identify the asymptotic behavior of the underlying multiplexons, see Section
3
.

Let us next present in greater detail the model we study. Consider a model for which at each time
t\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, we model
n
agents holding latent opinions represented by the set of vectors
\{Z^{n}_{i}(t)\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Interactions between agents at time
t
are encoded by a dynamic (undirected) network with adjacency matrix
A^{n}(t):=(A^{n}_{ij}(t))_{i,j=1}^{n}
. At time
t+1
, agent
i
updates their opinion by considering a convex combination of their own opinion and the average opinion of their neighbors at time
t
, then perturbing that opinion with i.i.d. additive noise
\{\xi_{i}(t)\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}},t\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
:

\displaystyle Z^{n}_{i}(t+1)

\displaystyle=(1-\gamma)Z^{n}_{i}(t)+\gamma L^{n}_{i}(t)+\xi_{i}(t),

(1.1)

\displaystyle L^{n}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{A^{n}(t)}(i)}\sum_{j=1}^{n}Z^{n}_{j}(t)A^{n}_{ij}(t),

(1.2)

\displaystyle d_{A^{n}(t)}(i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A^{n}_{ij}(t),

(1.3)

where
\gamma\in(0,1)
is some fixed constant.

The network
A^{n}
also evolves with time. Fix any
t\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
1\leq i<j\leq n
. At time
t+1
, there exists an edge between agents
i
and
j
with probability depending on
(a)
their latent positions
(Z^{n}_{i}(t+1),Z^{n}_{j}(t+1))
and
(b)
the interaction between the agents
A^{n}_{ij}(t)
at time
t
. This probability is determined by an a.e. continuous
interaction function

B:\{0,1\}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to[0,1]
:

{\mathbb{P}}\left(A^{n}_{ij}(t+1)=1\middle|{\mathcal{F}}^{A,n}_{t}\right)={\mathbb{P}}\left(A^{n}_{ji}(t+1)=1\middle|{\mathcal{F}}^{A,n}_{t}\right)=B\left(A^{n}_{ij}(t),Z^{n}_{i}(t+1),Z^{n}_{j}(t+1)\right),

(1.4)

where
{\mathcal{F}}^{A,n}_{t}=\sigma(Z^{n}[t+1],A^{n}[t])
. It is also assumed that for any

,

. The assumption that all particles have self-loops ensures that the denominator in (

) is always strictly positive, and it does not impact the subgraph densities of

in the limit as

. Note the persistence effect, in that the behavior of

does not only depend on the latent processes

. It can be noted that (

at time

Our main contributions are summarized below.

•

We rigorously study a generic class of models that features persistence, co-evolution and local interaction.

•

We introduce the sample perspective proof methodology, which turns out to be both a natural and a powerful vehicle to comprehensively study mean-field limits of interacting particle systems featuring dynamic co-evolution of particle dynamics and the underlying network (Theorem
2.8
).

•

We provide an analysis of the rich conditional structure of the sample limit generated from the above methodology (Definition
2.5
, Proposition
2.6
and Corollary
2.7
). In particular, we show that the mean-field limit no longer possesses a co-evolving structure: agents’ beliefs do not depend on the underlying sample subgraph, though the sample subgraph still depends on agent beliefs.

•

We establish limits of empirical distributions of the latent processes and of conditional empirical distributions describing the interaction between latent processes and the network process (Theorem
2.9
).

•

We derive graphon and multiplexon limits of the interaction network trajectory (Theorems
3.4
and
3.9
and Corollary
3.11
).

•

We provide a general statement and proof of a conditional propagation of chaos result, Proposition

[

The conditional propagation of chaos result essentially states that certain empirical measures provide consistent estimators of the conditional distribution of two agents’ latent beliefs conditioned on their interaction history. A similar result is shown in
[

in the context of the class of interacting particle systems studied there. To the best of our knowledge, our article is the first to establish simple, general conditions under which conditional propagation of chaos holds for generic models.

2.2.1
Building Intuition

Suppose that

(Z^{n}_{1:k}[t],A^{n}_{1:k,1:k}[t])\Rightarrow(Z[t],A[t]).

First, our assumptions ensure that for large

The dynamic subnetwork described by

n

\displaystyle:={\mathbb{E}}\left[A^{(ij)}(s)\middle|Z^{(i,j)}[s]\right]=\widehat{B}\left(B_{s-1}(Z^{(i)}[s-1],Z^{(j)}[s-1]),Z^{(i)}(s),Z^{(j)}(s)\right),

3.1
Left-Convergence of Graphs and Graphon Convergence

There are many ways in which graphs can be said to converge. One natural method of studying graph convergence is to examine the homomorphism densities of subgraphs. Such homomorphism densities can be used to derive common graph statistics such as edge density, clustering coefficients and large/small eigenvalues of the adjacency matrix (up to normalization). If such statistics converge for a sequence of large graphs, then we may intuitively understand that the graphs possess a similar limiting structure. This notion of convergence is called
left-convergence

[
59

We now provide a precise definition of left-convergence. Let
H=(V_{H},E_{H})
and

be two graphs. Then a

from
H

G
is a map
\phi:V_{H}\to V_{G}
such that if
\{u,v\}\in E_{H}
, then
\{\phi(u),\phi(v)\}\in E_{G}
. If
\text{Hom}(H,G)
is the set of homomorphisms from
H
to
G
, then the
homomorphism density
of
H
in
G

[
57

is the function

For each

Definition 3.1

(Graphon Space)
.

Let
\mathcal{W}_{*}
be the space of bounded, symmetric and measurable functions

equipped with the

[

\|W\|_{\square}:=\sup_{S,T\in{\mathcal{B}}([0,1])}\left|\int_{S\times T}W(x,y)\,dx\,dy\right|.

Any

such that
0\leq W\leq 1
is called a
graphon
, and the
\mathcal{W}:=\{W\in\mathcal{W}_{*}:0\leq W\leq 1\}
is the space of graphons.

Any finite graph

can be associated with its

given by

W^{G}(x,y)={\mathbb{I}}_{\left\{\{\lceil xn\rceil,\lceil yn\rceil\}\in E\right\}},

where
n=|V|
. Furthermore, the function

can be expanded to the space of graphons

t(H,W)=\int_{[0,1]^{|V_{H}|}}\prod_{\{i,j\}\in E_{H}}W(x_{i},x_{j})\,dx_{1},\dots,\,dx_{|V_{H}|}.

It’s easily verified that for any finite graph
G

t(H,G)=t(H,W^{G})
.

We say a map

is a measure-preserving transformation (m.p.t.) if for any Borel-measurable

have the same Lebesgue measure. Two graphons

and

lies in the closure of the set

\{W:W(x,y)=W_{1}^{\psi}(x,y):=W_{1}(\psi(x),\psi(y))\text{ for an m.p.t. }\psi:[0,1]\to[0,1]\}.

This is analogous to describing two graphs as equivalent if they are isomorphic
[
59
, Section 8.2.2]
.

Definition 3.2

(Graphon Classes)
.

We define the space of graphon classes

. If

\text{measure-preserving}\end{subarray}}\|W_{1}-W_{2}^{\psi}\|_{\square},

where the above equation holds independently of the choice of representative graphons

Proposition 3.3

A sequence of graphs

3.2
Convergence of Latent Network Trajectories as Multiplexons

Fix
t\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
s\in[t]
, define the graph
G_{n}(s)=([1:n],E_{n}(s))
to be the graph with adjacency matrix

. Given Theorem

, it follows that

\widetilde{W}^{G_{n}[t]}:=\left(\widetilde{W}^{G_{n}(s)}\right)_{s\in[t]}\to\widetilde{W}[s]:=(\widetilde{W}(s))_{s\in[t]}\text{ in probability,}

(3.1)

where
\widetilde{W}(s)
is the graphon class described in Theorem

. However, this result is somewhat unsatisfying. For each

, the vertices of

all correspond to a fixed particle. However, (

) only tells us that there is a way to relabel the vertices of each graph
G_{n}(s)
to get a different graph

such that

W^{G^{\prime}_{n}(s)}\to W(s)\text{ for all }s\in[t],

where for each
s
,
W(s)
is some representative graphon of
\widetilde{W}(s)
. The problem is that the relabeling may differ for each value of

G^{\prime}_{n}(s)
and
G^{\prime}_{n}(s^{\prime})
for

\widetilde{W}[s]
from (

at different times.

To handle this, we treat the graph trajectory of the
n
-particle system as a

. A multiplex is a collection of graphs sharing a vertex set. Each graph is called a
layer
of the multiplex network. For example,
\bm{G}_{n}
is a multiplex network on the vertex set

with

layers where for all
s\in[t]
, the
s
th layer of the multiplex consists of the edges in
G_{n}(s)
.

Definition 3.7

(Cumulative Multiplex Decomposition)
.

For any
(t+1)
-layer multiplex

where

E_{H}^{S}=\cap_{s\in S}E_{H}(s).

So
E_{H}^{S}
contains all edges that lie in
E_{H}(s)
for all

.

We can now define multiplexons, recently introduced in
[

]

[

]

Definition 3.8

(Cumulatively Decomposable Multiplexons)

A
(t+1)
-layer cumulatively decomposable multiplexon is a vector of graphons indexed by the subsets of

\mathbf{W}=(W^{S})_{S\subseteq[t],S\neq\emptyset}
such that for a.e.

such that

We define

to be the space of

-layer cumulatively decomposable multiplexons.

In (3.5) of
[

\text{measure-preserving}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq[t]\\

Let

if and only if

The empirical multiplexon of a multiplex

is given by

It can be shown that
\mathbf{W}^{\bm{G}}\in{\mathcal{W}}^{(t)}_{\blacktriangle}
,
[

Conditional Propagation of Chaos

In this section, we introduce a conditional propagation of chaos result, which is of independent interest. We begin with a classical descriptor of propagation of chaos:

Proposition 5.1

(Propagation of Chaos)
.

Let
\{X^{n}_{1:n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a triangular array of
{\mathcal{X}}
-random elements such that for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
(X^{n}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
is exchangeable. Suppose
\eta:=\text{Law}(X)\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{X}})
is a deterministic probability measure. Then,

\eta_{X}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{X^{n}_{i}}\to\eta\text{ in probability}

if and only if for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(X^{n}_{1},\dots,X^{n}_{k})\Rightarrow(X^{(1)},\dots,X^{(k)}),

where
X^{(1)},\dots,X^{(k)}
are i.i.d. copies of
X
. Moreover, the “if” statement of this lemma holds even when
k=2
.

Proof.

This is just a restatement of
[
82
, Proposition 2.2(i)]
in our notation.
∎

As mentioned in Remark
2.10
, this describes (
2.23
) of Theorem
2.9
. However, it is not sufficient to establish (
2.24
) of the same theorem. In this section, we extend Proposition
5.1
to a more general case, which we call
conditional propagation of chaos
. Conditional propagation of chaos plays a key role in the proof of Theorem
2.8
, as it is useful for establishing conditional law of large numbers result described in (
2.8
), which is then used to establish the weak limit of
(Z^{n}_{i}[t],L^{n}_{i}(t))
, and therefore of
Z^{n}_{i}[t+1]
. To state the generalization of Proposition
5.1
, we require a few definitions.

Definition 5.2

(Continuous Dependence)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be
{\mathcal{X}}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}
-random elements respectively. Then we say
Y

depends continuously on

X
if there exists an
X
-almost surely continuous function
\phi:{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{Y}}
such that
\phi(X)=Y
almost surely.

It is important to note that in (
2.24
), exchangeability notably fails. Specifically, the collection of random elements
(Z^{n}_{j}(t),A^{n}_{ij}(t))_{j=1}^{n}
are not exchangeable. This is because
(Z^{n}_{i}(t),A^{n}_{ii}(t))\overset{\text{(d)}}{\neq}(Z^{n}_{j}(t),A^{n}_{ij}(t))
for
i\neq j
. Ultimately this does not matter as the collection
(Z^{n}_{j}(t),A^{n}_{ij}(t))_{j\neq i}

is
exchangeable and the
\frac{1}{n}\delta_{Z^{n}_{i}[t],Z^{n}_{i}[t],A^{n}_{ii}[t]}
term in (
2.24
) vanishes as
n\to\infty
. Taking this into account, we define a new notion of exchangeability.

Definition 5.3

(Exchangeability Excluding
i
)
.

For any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
i\in[1:n]
, a
{\mathcal{X}}
-random element
X
and a collection of
n

{\mathcal{Y}}
-random elements
Y:=(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})
are said to be
exchangeable excluding

i
if for every permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
,

\left(X,(Y_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\right)\overset{\text{(d)}}{=}\left(X,(Y_{\sigma(j)})_{j=1}^{n}\right)\text{ when for }j=i,\sigma(i)=i.

It is convenient to combine Definitions
5.2
and
5.3
into a single property.

Definition 5.4

(
{\mathcal{X}}/{\mathcal{Y}}
-Convenience)
.

Let
\mathbf{X}:=\{X^{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\cup\{X\}
be a sequence of
{\mathcal{X}}
-valued random elements and let
\mathbf{Y}:=\{(Y^{n}_{i})_{i\in[1:n]}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\cup\{Y\}
be a collection of random vectors with entries in
{\mathcal{Y}}
. Then
(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})
is said to be
{\mathcal{X}}/{\mathcal{Y}}
-
convenient
if for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
(X^{n},Y^{n})
is exchangeable excluding 1 and
\eta_{XY}:=\text{Law}(X,Y|X)
depends continuously on
X
.

We now state our conditional propagation of chaos result, which we prove in Section
5.1
.

Proposition 5.5

(Conditional Propagation of Chaos)
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{X}}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}
are locally compact. Let
(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})
be an
{\mathcal{X}}/{\mathcal{Y}}
-convenient collection of random elements. Then

\displaystyle(X^{n},\eta^{n}_{XY})

\displaystyle:=\left(X^{n},\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{X^{n},Y^{n}_{j}}\right)\Rightarrow(X,\eta_{XY}),

(5.1)

if and only if for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{1,2\}
,

(X^{n},Y^{n}_{2:k})\Rightarrow(X,Y^{(2:k)}),

(5.2)

where

•

\text{Law}(X,Y^{(j)}|X)=\eta_{XY}
for all

,

•

and
(Y^{(j)})_{j=2}^{k}

.

In addition, (

) when

.

Definition 5.6

(Conditional Propagation of Chaos)
.

We say a pair of random variable collections
(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})
satisfies the
{\mathcal{X}}/{\mathcal{Y}}

(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})
is

Remark 5.7

(Extension to Continuous Time)
.

In Proposition
5.5
,
\bm{X}
and
\bm{Y}
are intended to represent node and edge trajectories of a sequence of interacting particle systems on dynamic networks. However, in the continuous-time context, this will typically result in
{\mathcal{X}}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}
failing to be locally compact. In this situation, the “only if” direction of Proposition
5.5
still holds. This is because local compactness is only needed to due to our application of Lemma
A.6
which is not used to in the proof of the “only if” direction of the proposition. We expect that the “if” direction also holds in this case. To establish this direction of the proposition in the non-locally compact case, it will be necessary to place additional assumptions on
\{\eta^{n}_{XY}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that

\langle\widetilde{\eta}^{n}_{XY},f\rangle\to\langle\widetilde{\eta}_{XY},f\rangle\text{ in probability for all }f\in C_{b}({\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}})\text{ implies }\widetilde{\eta}^{n}_{XY}\to\widetilde{\eta}_{XY}\text{ in probability.}

For continuous time interacting particle systems,
{\mathcal{X}}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}
are typically spaces of continuous or càdlàg functions on discrete or Euclidean state spaces. We expect the above convergence to hold for such function spaces under the additional condition that the sequence
\{\widetilde{\eta}^{n}_{XY}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a.s. tight (e.g. via an application of
[
13
, Theorems 7.1, 13.1]
).

Remark 5.8

(Why Do We Exclude
Y^{n}_{1}
and
Y^{(1)}
?)
.

In Proposition
5.5
, we work with the convergence
(X^{n},Y^{n}_{2:k})\Rightarrow(X,Y^{(2:k)})

(X^{n},Y^{n})
is exchangeable excluding 1, this is equivalent to stating that
(X^{n},Y^{n}_{j_{1:m}})\Rightarrow(X,Y^{(j_{1:m})})
for any distinct

in

. We simply state everything for

and

. This is because (
5.1
) does not give us any information regarding the limit of the expression
(X^{n},Y^{n}_{1})
due to the fact that
(X^{n},Y^{n})
is exchangeable
excluding
1.

Remark 5.9

(Conditional Propagation of Chaos Under Exchangeability Excluding
i
)
.

Suppose that
\left(X,(Y_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\right)
is exchangeable excluding
i
for some

for

7.1
A Related Result

We begin with a slightly more general result, which highlights the inductive claims used in our proof of Theorem
2.8
. To this end, we use a notion of joint exchangeability, which slightly modifies the concept of an exchangeable collection of random elements.

Definition 7.1

(Joint Exchangeability)
.

For any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
X=(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
be an
{\mathcal{X}}^{n}
-random element and let
M=(M_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
be an
n\times n
random matrix with entries in
{\mathcal{Y}}
. Then we say that the pair
(X,M):=(X_{1:n},M_{1:n,1:n})
is
jointly exchangeable
if and only if for any permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
,

(X_{1:n},M_{1:n,1:n})\overset{\text{(d)}}{=}(X_{\sigma(1:n)},M_{\sigma(1:n)\sigma(1:n)}).

Note that joint exchangeability is closely related to the notion of exchangeability excluding
i
:

Remark 7.2
.

If a
{\mathcal{X}}^{n}\times{\mathcal{Y}}^{n\times n}
-random collection
(X,M)
is jointly exchangeable, then for all
i\in[1:n]
, the collections
\left(X_{i},(M_{ij})_{j=1}^{n}\right)
and
\left(X_{i},(X_{j},M_{ij})_{j=1}^{n}\right)
are exchangeable excluding
i
. The proof is simple: for any
\sigma\in S_{n}
such that
\sigma(i)=i
,

\left(X_{i},(M_{ij})_{j=1}^{n}\right)\overset{\text{(d)}}{=}\left(X_{\sigma(i)},(M_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)})_{j=1}^{n}\right)=\left(X_{i},(M_{i\sigma(j)})_{j=1}^{n}\right).

The proof for
(X_{i},(X_{j},M_{ij}))
is essentially the same.

To this end, we define the following collection of conditions on both the prelimit and the limiting systems, which are assumed to hold at a certain time
t
. We call these conditions
property A at time
t
. We later show that property A holding at all times implies the conclusions of Theorem
2.8
, which allows our proof of the theorem to reduce to an inductive proof that property A holds at all times.

Definition 7.3
.

(Property A at time
t
)
For a given
t\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, we say the models given in (
1.1
)-(
1.4
) and Definition

satisfies property A at time

if the following conditions hold for all

(a)

(Z^{n}[t],A^{n}[t])
is jointly exchangeable.

(b)

The collection
\{Z^{n}_{i}(t)\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}},i\in[1:n]}
is uniformly integrable.

(c)

For any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the following convergence holds:

(Z^{n}_{1:k}[t],A^{n}_{1:k,1:k}[t])\Rightarrow(Z^{(1:k)}[t],A^{(1:k,1:k)}[t]).

(d)

Z^{(1)}[t]
is absolutely continuous.

As mentioned above, property A holding at all times implies the conclusions of Theorem

Y_{i}=\phi_{i}(X_{i})
a.s.. If
(X^{n}_{i})_{i\in[1:k]}\Rightarrow(X_{i})_{i\in[1:k]}
, then

(X^{n}_{i},Y^{n}_{i})_{i\in[1:k]}\Rightarrow(X_{i},\phi_{i}(X_{i}))_{i\in[1:k]}.

Proof.

Because its marginals are weakly convergent, the sequence
\{(X^{n}_{i},Y^{n}_{i})_{i\in[1:k]}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is tight, so there exists a subsequence
\{n_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that

(X^{n_{\ell}}_{i},Y^{n_{\ell}}_{i})_{i\in[1:k]}\Rightarrow(\widetilde{X}_{i},\widetilde{Y}_{i})_{i\in[1:k]}.

For each
i
,
(X^{n_{\ell}}_{i},Y^{n_{\ell}}_{i})\Rightarrow(X_{i},Y_{i})
, so
(\widetilde{X}_{i},\widetilde{Y}_{i})\overset{\text{(d)}}{=}(X_{i},Y_{i})
and
\widetilde{Y}_{i}=\phi_{i}(\widetilde{X}_{i})
a.s.. Lastly, since
(X^{n_{\ell}}_{i})_{i\in[1:k]}\rightarrow(X_{i})_{i\in[1:k]}
it follows that
(X_{i})_{i\in[1:k]}\overset{\text{(d)}}{=}(\widetilde{X}_{i})_{i\in[1:k]}
. Thus,

(\widetilde{X}_{i},\widetilde{Y}_{i})_{i\in[1:k]}\overset{\text{(d)}}{=}(X_{i},\phi_{i}(X_{i}))_{i\in[1:k]}.

Since this limiting quantity is unique in distribution, it follows, as desired, that

(X^{n}_{i},Y^{n}_{i})_{i\in[1:k]}\Rightarrow(\widetilde{X}_{i},\widetilde{Y}_{i})_{i\in[1:k]}=(\widetilde{X}_{i},\phi_{i}(\widetilde{X}_{i}))_{i\in[1:k]}\overset{\text{(d)}}{=}(X_{i},\phi_{i}(X_{i}))_{i\in[1:k]}.

∎

A.2.2
Convergence of Conditionally Independent Bernoulli Random Variables

Lemma A.3
.

Let
X
be any
{\mathcal{X}}
-random element, and let
I
be a finite index set. Fix
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
\{\phi_{j}^{a}\}_{j\in[1:k],a\in I}
be a sequence of bounded,
X
-a.s. continuous functions from
{\mathcal{X}}
to
[0,1]
. In addition, let
(X^{n},D^{n}):=(X^{n},D^{n}_{[1:k]})
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of
{\mathcal{X}}\times I^{k}
- random elements satisfying the following conditions:

(a)

X^{n}\Rightarrow X
for some
{\mathcal{X}}
-random element
X
;

(b)

for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
D^{n}_{1},\dots,D^{n}_{k}
are mutually conditionally independent given
X^{n}
;

(c)

for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
j\in[1:k]
, and
a\in I
,

{\mathbb{P}}(D^{n}_{j}=a|X^{n})=\phi_{j}^{a}(X^{n}).

Then,

(X^{n},D^{n})\Rightarrow(X,D):=(X,D^{(1:k)}),

where

(A)

D^{(1)},\dots,D^{(k)}
are mutually conditionally independent given
X
;

(B)

for each
j\in[1:k]
and
a\in I
,

{\mathbb{P}}(D^{(j)}=a|X)=\phi_{j}^{a}(X).

Proof.

Fix any
f\in C_{b}({\mathcal{X}}\times I^{k})
. For each
\bm{a}\in I^{k}
, define
f_{\bm{a}}\in C_{b}({\mathcal{X}})
by
f_{\bm{a}}(x)=f(x,\bm{a})
. Define the bounded,
X
-a.s. continuous function
g_{\bm{a}}:{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

g_{\bm{a}}(x)=f_{\bm{a}}(x)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\phi_{j}^{a_{j}}(x),

and define the bounded,
X
-a.s. continuous function
g:{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

g(x)=\sum_{\bm{a}\in I^{k}}g_{\bm{a}}(x).

Suppose that
(X,D)
satisfies conditions (A) and (B) above. Then,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[f(X,D)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{a}\in I^{k}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f_{\bm{a}}(X){\mathbb{I}}_{\left\{D=\bm{a}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{a}\in I^{k}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f_{\bm{a}}(X){\mathbb{P}}(D=\bm{a}|X)\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(A),(B)}{=}\sum_{\bm{a}\in I^{k}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f_{\bm{a}}(X)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\phi_{j}^{a_{j}}(X)\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{\bm{a}\in I^{k}}g_{\bm{a}}(X)\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[g(X)\right].

By conditions (b) and (c), note that
(X^{n},D^{n})
also satisfies conditions (A) and (B) for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, so

{\mathbb{E}}\left[f(X^{n},D^{n})\right]={\mathbb{E}}\left[g(X^{n})\right],

for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then, by condition (a) and the fact that
g
is bounded and
X
-a.s. continuous,

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f(X^{n},D^{n})\right]=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\left[g(X^{n})\right]={\mathbb{E}}\left[g(X)\right]={\mathbb{E}}\left[f(X,D)\right],

completing the proof that
(X^{n},D^{n})\Rightarrow(X,D)
where
(X,D)
satisfies conditions (A) and (B).
∎

A.2.3
Mutual Conditional Independence of Bernoulli Random Vectors

We start with a couple of simple lemmas. The first one reduces the task of establishing mutual conditional independence to the task of establishing simple conditional independence.

Lemma A.4

(Sufficient Condition for Mutual Conditional Independence)
.

A collection of
{\mathcal{Y}}
-random elements
(Y_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
is mutually conditionally independent given a
\sigma
-algebra
{\mathcal{F}}
if and only if for any
j\in[k]
,

Y_{j}\perp\!\!\!\perp(Y_{i})_{i\neq j}|{\mathcal{F}}.

(A.2)

Proof.

The “only if” direction is an immediate consequence of the definition of mutual conditional independence.

We now prove the “if” direction. For each
i\in[k]
, let
f_{i}:{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be some bounded, measurable function. For each
i\in[k]
, define
g_{i}:{\mathcal{Y}}^{k-i+1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

g_{i}(y_{i},y_{i+1},\dots,y_{k})=\prod_{j=i}^{k}f_{j}(y_{j}).

Then applying (
A.2
) sequentially for
j=1
, then
j=2
, etc.

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(Y_{i})\middle|{\mathcal{F}}\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[f_{1}(Y_{1})g_{2}(Y_{2},\dots,Y_{k})\middle|{\mathcal{F}}\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[f_{1}(Y_{1})\middle|{\mathcal{F}}\right]{\mathbb{E}}\left[\prod_{i=2}^{n}f_{i}(Y_{i})\middle|{\mathcal{F}}\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[f_{1}(Y_{1})\middle|{\mathcal{F}}\right]{\mathbb{E}}\left[f_{2}(Y_{2})g_{3}(Y_{3},\dots,Y_{k})\middle|{\mathcal{F}}\right]

\displaystyle=\cdots

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f_{i}(Y_{i})\middle|{\mathcal{F}}\right].

That concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.5

(Mutual Conditional Independence of Finite-State Random Variables)
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}
be two sigma algebras and let
I
be a finite state space. Let
\{D_{i}\}_{j\in J}
be a collection of
I
-valued random variables indexed by some finite set
J
that are conditionally independent given
{\mathcal{F}}
. If
P^{a}_{j}:={\mathbb{P}}(D_{j}=a|{\mathcal{F}})
is
{\mathcal{G}}
-measurable for all
a\in I
and
j\in J
, then
\{D_{j}\}_{j\in J}
are also conditionally independent given
{\mathcal{G}}
.

Proof.

Notice that for any
j\in J
and
a\in I
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(D_{j}=a\middle|{\mathcal{G}}\right)={\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathbb{P}}\left(D_{j}=a\middle|{\mathcal{F}}\right)\middle|{\mathcal{G}}\right]={\mathbb{E}}\left[P_{j}^{a}|{\mathcal{G}}\right]=P_{j}^{a}.

Let
\bm{a}:=\{a_{j}\}_{j\in J}\in I^{J}
be any constant. Then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(D_{j}=a_{j}\text{ for all }j\in J\middle|{\mathcal{G}}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathbb{P}}\left(D_{j}=a_{j}\text{ for all }j\in J\middle|{\mathcal{F}}\right)\middle|{\mathcal{G}}\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[\prod_{j\in J}{\mathbb{P}}\left(D_{j}=a_{j}\middle|{\mathcal{F}}\right)\middle|{\mathcal{G}}\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[\prod_{j\in J}P_{j}^{a_{j}}\middle|{\mathcal{G}}\right]

\displaystyle=\prod_{j\in J}P_{j}^{a_{j}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j\in J}{\mathbb{P}}\left(D_{j}=a_{j}\middle|{\mathcal{G}}\right).

∎

A.2.4
Convergence of Random Measures

We begin with a sufficient condition under which a sequence of random measures converges in probability. We believe the following lemma is known. However, we were unable to find a suitable reference.

Lemma A.6

(Convergence in Probability of Random Measures)
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{X}}
is locally compact. Let
\{\eta_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of random probability measures in
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{X}})
. Suppose there exists a random
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{X}})
-element
\eta
such that for all
f\in C_{b}({\mathcal{X}})
,
\langle\eta_{n},f\rangle\to\langle\eta,f\rangle
in probability. Then
\eta_{n}\to\eta
in probability.

Proof.

Ideally we would consider a sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
that is dense in
C_{b}({\mathcal{X}})
. However,
C_{b}({\mathcal{X}})
is not necessarily separable in the topology of uniform convergence. Instead, let
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a collection of functions that are dense in the space
C_{0}({\mathcal{X}})\subseteq C_{b}({\mathcal{X}})
(which is separable when
{\mathcal{X}}
is locally compact).

Let
\{n_{m}\}_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
be an arbitrary, strictly increasing subsequence of
{\mathbb{N}}
such that
n_{m}\nearrow\infty
. Then by a standard diagonalization argument, there exists a subsubsequence (also strictly increasing to infinity)
\{n_{m_{\ell}}\}_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subseteq\{n_{m}\}_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that

\left\langle\eta_{n_{m_{\ell}}},f_{k}\right\rangle\to\langle\eta,f_{k}\rangle\text{ a.s. for all }k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Let
f\in C_{0}({\mathcal{X}})
and fix a sequence
\{k_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
such that
f_{k_{i}}\to f
uniformly. Then

\lim_{i\to\infty}\sup_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left|\left\langle\eta_{n_{m_{\ell}}},f_{k_{i}}\right\rangle-\left\langle\eta_{n_{m_{\ell}}},f\right\rangle\right|\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}\left\|f_{k_{i}}-f\right\|_{\infty}=0.

For any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\left\langle\eta_{n_{m_{\ell}}},f_{k_{i}}\right\rangle=\left\langle\eta,f_{k_{i}}\right\rangle\text{ a.s.}.

This shows that the assumptions of the Moore-Osgood double limit theorem (see
[
70
, Theorem 7.11]
or
[
49
, Chapter 5, Theorem 3]
for example) are satisfied by the doubly indexed sequence
\left\{\left\langle\eta_{n_{m_{\ell}}},f_{k_{i}}\right\rangle\right\}_{i,\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, so we apply the Moore-Osgood theorem to interchange limits below:

\displaystyle\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\left\langle\eta_{n_{m_{\ell}}},f\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\lim_{i\to\infty}\left\langle\eta_{n_{m_{\ell}}},f_{k_{i}}\right\rangle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\left\langle\eta_{n_{m_{\ell}}},f_{k_{i}}\right\rangle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\left\langle\eta,f_{k_{i}}\right\rangle=\langle\eta,f\rangle\text{ a.s..}

Thus,
\eta_{n_{m_{\ell}}}\to\eta
vaguely a.s.. Because
\eta_{n_{m_{\ell}}}
is a probability measure for all
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
{\mathcal{X}}
is a locally compact Polish space and
\eta
is also a probability measure,
\eta_{n_{m_{\ell}}}\to\eta
a.s. in
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{X}})

[
39
, Exercise 26, Chapter 7]
. Therefore,
\eta_{n}\to\eta
in
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{X}})
in probability.
∎

A.2.5
Convergence of Integrals of Random Measures

If a sequence of random measures
\eta_{X}^{n}\to\eta_{X}
in probability, then for any bounded, continuous
f
,
\langle\eta_{X}^{n},f\rangle\to\langle\eta_{X},f\rangle
in probability. However, if
f
is continuous but unbounded, then the map
\eta\mapsto\langle\eta,f\rangle
is no longer continuous. We establish sufficient conditions under which
\langle\eta_{X}^{n},f\rangle
still converges to
\langle\eta_{X},f\rangle
in probability.

Lemma A.7
.

Let
\{X^{n}_{i}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}},i\in[1:n]}
be a uniformly integrable triangular array of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-random vectors such that

\eta^{n}_{X}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{X^{n}_{i}}\to\eta_{X}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\text{ in probability,}

where
\eta_{X}
is a possibly random probability measure. Then,

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X^{n}_{i}\to\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}x\,\eta_{X}(dx)\text{ in probability.}

Proof.

The following argument is adapted from the proof of
[
17
, Theorem 3.4(b)]
. Let
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be the identity map. For any
C<\infty
, define
\phi_{C}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by

\phi_{C}(x)=x{\mathbb{I}}_{\left\{|x|>C\right\}}.

By uniform integrability of
\{X^{n}_{i}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}},i\in[1:n]}
there exists for each
C\in(0,\infty)
a constant
M_{C}
converging to
0
as
C\to\infty
such that

\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}},i\in[1:n]}{\mathbb{E}}\left[|\phi_{C}(x^{n}_{i})|\right]\leq M_{C}.

It follows that for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[|\langle\eta^{n}_{X},\phi_{C}\rangle|\right]\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\phi_{C}(X^{n}_{i})|\right]=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left[|\phi_{C}(x^{n}_{i})|\right]\leq M_{C}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\lim_{C\to\infty}\sup_{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left[|\langle\eta^{n}_{X},\phi_{C}\rangle|\right]\leq\lim_{C\to\infty}M_{C}=0.

If we also define

\phi^{C}(x):=\begin{cases}x&\text{ if }|x|<C,\\
C\frac{x}{|x|}&\text{ if }|x|\geq C.\end{cases}

Note that
|\phi_{C}+\phi^{C}|\geq|\phi|
. Moreover, there exists a sequence
M^{\prime}_{C}\to 0
as
C\to\infty
such that
{\mathbb{E}}\left[|\langle\eta_{X},\phi_{C}\rangle|\right]\leq M^{\prime}_{C}
for all
C\in(0,\infty)
. Lastly, because
\phi^{C}
is a bounded, continuous function,
\{\langle\eta^{n}_{X},\phi^{C}\rangle\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a sequence of random vectors whose magnitudes are uniformly bounded by
C
and that converge to
\langle\eta_{X},\phi^{C}\rangle
in probability. Thus, for any
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(|\langle\eta^{n}_{X},\phi\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\eta_{X},\phi\rangle|>\epsilon)\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\epsilon}{\mathbb{E}}\left[|\langle\eta^{n}_{X},\phi\rangle-\langle\eta_{X},\phi\rangle|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{C\in(0,\infty)}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[|\langle\eta^{n}_{X},\phi^{C}\rangle-\langle\eta_{X},\phi^{C}\rangle|\right]+{\mathbb{E}}\left[|\langle\eta^{n}_{X},\phi_{C}\rangle|\right]+{\mathbb{E}}\left[|\langle\eta_{X},\phi_{C}\rangle|\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{C\in(0,\infty)}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[|\langle\eta^{n}_{X},\phi^{C}\rangle-\langle\eta_{X},\phi^{C}\rangle|\right]+M_{C}+M^{\prime}_{C}\right)

\displaystyle=\inf_{C\in(0,\infty)}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left(M_{C}+M^{\prime}_{C}\right)

\displaystyle=0.

Thus,

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X^{n}_{i}=\langle\eta^{n}_{X},\phi\rangle\to\langle\eta_{X},\phi\rangle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}x\,\eta_{X}(dx)\text{ in probability}

completing the proof.
∎

This immediately implies the following simple corollary.

Corollary A.8
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{X}}
is locally compact. Let
\{Y^{n}_{i}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}},i\in[1:n]}
be a triangular array of
{\mathcal{X}}
-random elements such that

\eta^{n}_{Y}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{Y^{n}_{i}}\to\eta_{Y}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{X}})\text{ in probability,}

where
\eta_{Y}
is a possibly random probability measure. Let
f:{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a continuous function such that
\{f(Y^{n}_{i})\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}},i\in[1:n]}
is uniformly integrable. Then,

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(Y^{n}_{i})\to\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(y)\,\eta_{Y}(dy)\text{ in probability.}

Proof.

Define

\eta^{n}_{f}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{f(Y^{n}_{i})}\text{ and }\eta_{f}:=f_{*}\eta_{Y},

so for any
A\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
\eta_{f}(A)=\eta_{Y}\left(\{y:f(y)\in A\}\right)
. Fix any
g\in C_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Then
g\circ f\in C_{b}({\mathcal{X}})
, so

\langle\eta^{n}_{f},g\rangle=\langle\eta^{n}_{Y},g\circ f\rangle\to\langle\eta_{Y},g\circ f\rangle=\langle\eta_{f},g\rangle\text{ in probability,}

so by Lemma
A.6
,
\eta^{n}_{f}\to\eta_{f}
in probability. The result now holds by Lemma
A.7
setting
X^{n}_{i}=f(Y^{n}_{i})
,
\eta^{n}_{X}=\eta^{n}_{f}
and
\eta_{X}=\eta_{f}
.
∎

A.3
Establishing Exchangeability

Lemma A.9
.

Suppose Property A(a) holds at time
t
. Then the collection
((Z^{n}[t],L^{n}(t)),A^{n}(t))
is jointly exchangeable.

Proof.

Fix any
\sigma\in S_{n}
. Then,

\displaystyle((Z^{n}[t],L^{n}(t)),A^{n}(t))

\displaystyle=\left(\left(Z^{n}_{i}[t],\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}Z^{n}_{k}(t)A^{n}_{ik}(t)}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}A^{n}_{ik}(t)}\right)_{i\in[1:n]},(A^{n}_{ij}(t))_{i,j\in[1:n]}\right)

Lemma A.10
.

Let
X:=(X_{i})_{i\in[1:n]}
and

be
{\mathcal{X}}^{n}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}^{n\times n}
random elements respectively such that
(X,Y)
is jointly exchangeable. Let
\{B_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}
be a conditionally mutually independent sequence of Bernoulli random variables (given
(X,Y)
) and let

for all
i,j\in[1:n]
. If for each
i,j
,
P_{ij}={\mathbb{P}}(B_{ij}=1|X,Y)
and
(X,(Y,P))
is jointly exchangeable, then
(X_{1:k},(Y_{ij},B_{ij})_{i,j\in[1:n]})
is jointly exchangeable.

Proof.

Fix any
\sigma\in S_{n}
and any bounded, measurable
g:{\mathcal{X}}^{n}\times({\mathcal{Y}}\times\{0,1\}\times[0,1])^{n\times n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. Then,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[g\left((X_{i})_{i\in[1:n]},(Y_{ij},B_{ij},P_{ij})_{i,j\in[1:n]}\right)\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathbb{E}}\left[g\left((X_{i})_{i\in[1:n]},(Y_{ij},B_{ij},P_{ij})_{i,j\in[1:n]}\right)\middle|X,Y,P\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\{0,1\}^{n\times n}\\
b\text{ is symmetric}\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[g\left((X_{i})_{i\in[1:n]},(Y_{ij},b_{ij},P_{ij})_{i,j\in[1:n]}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq j\leq n\\

b_{ij}=0\end{subarray}}(1-P_{ij})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\{0,1\}^{n\times n}\\
b\text{ is symmetric}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[g\left((X_{\sigma(i)})_{i\in[1:n]},(Y_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)},b_{ij},P_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)})_{i,j\in[1:n]}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq j\leq n\\
b_{ij}=1\end{subarray}}P_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)}

\displaystyle\hskip 24.0pt\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq j\leq n\\
b_{ij}=0\end{subarray}}(1-P_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)})\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{n\times n}\\
b^{\prime}\text{ is symmetric}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[g\left((X_{\sigma(i)})_{i\in[1:n]},(Y_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)},b^{\prime}_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)},P_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)})_{i,j\in[1:n]}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 24.0pt\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq j\leq n\\
b^{\prime}_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)}=1\end{subarray}}P_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq j\leq n\\

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[g\left((X_{\sigma(i)})_{i\in[1:n]},(Y_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)},B_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)},P_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)})_{i,j\in[1:n]}\right)\right].

Thus,
(X,(Y,P,B))
is jointly exchangeable, so

is also jointly exchangeable.
∎

Lemma A.11
.

Let
(X,Y)
be an
{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}
-random element that is exchangeable excluding
1
. Let
C:{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}\to[0,1]
be a measurable function and let
\{B_{i}\}_{i\in[1:n]}
be a collection of conditionally independent (given
(X,Y)
) Bernoulli random variables with respective parameters
P_{i}:=C(X,Y_{i})
. Then
(X,(Y,B))
is also exchangeable excluding 1.

Proof.

The proof is very similar to the proof of Lemma
A.10
. Let
\sigma\in S_{n}
be such that
\sigma(1)=1
. Then

(X,Y_{i},P_{i})_{i\in[1:n]}=(X,Y_{i},C(X,Y_{i}))_{i\in[1:n]}\overset{\text{(d)}}{=}(X,Y_{\sigma(i)},C(X,Y_{\sigma(i)}))_{i\in[1:n]}=(X,Y_{\sigma(i)},P_{\sigma(i)}),

so
(X,Y,P)
is exchangeable excluding 1. Then for any bounded, measurable
f:{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}\times[0,1]^{n}\times\{0,1\}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[f(X,(Y_{i},P_{i},B_{i})_{i\in[1:n]}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}^{n}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f(X,(Y_{i},P_{i},b_{i})_{i\in[1:n]}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[1:n]\\
b_{i}=1\end{subarray}}P_{i}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[1:n]\\
b_{i}=0\end{subarray}}(1-P_{i})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}^{n}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f(X,(Y_{\sigma(i)},P_{\sigma(i)},b_{i})_{i\in[1:n]}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[1:n]\\
b_{i}=1\end{subarray}}P_{\sigma(i)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[1:n]\\
b_{i}=0\end{subarray}}(1-P_{\sigma(i)})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{b^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f(X,(Y_{\sigma(i)},P_{\sigma(i)},b^{\prime}_{\sigma(i)})_{i\in[1:n]}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[1:n]\\
b^{\prime}_{\sigma(i)}=1\end{subarray}}P_{\sigma(i)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[1:n]\\
b^{\prime}_{\sigma(i)}=0\end{subarray}}(1-P_{\sigma(i)})\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[f(X,(Y_{\sigma(i)},P_{\sigma(i)},B_{\sigma(i)})_{i\in[1:n]})\right],

where in the third equality, we use the tranformation
(b^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in[1:n]}=(b_{\sigma^{-1}(i)})_{i\in[1:n]}
. Thus,
(X,(Y,P,B))
is exchangeable excluding 1, so
(X,(Y,B))
is exchangeable excluding 1.
∎

A.4
Other General Lemmas

Another useful lemma concerns a representation of the product of conditional probability measures.

Lemma A.12
.

Let
\eta
be a
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}})
-random element defined by
\eta=\text{Law}(X,Y|X)
for respective
{\mathcal{X}}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}
-random elements
X
and
Y
. Now suppose that for some
k\geq 3
,
(X,Y_{2},\dots,Y_{k})
satisfies the following conditions:

•

for
j=2,\dots,k
,
\text{Law}(X,Y_{j}|X)=\eta
,

•

(Y_{j})_{j=2}^{k}
are conditionally independent given
X
.

Then

(\eta)^{k-1}=\text{Law}\left((X,Y_{j})_{j=2}^{k}\middle|X\right).

Proof.

Because
(\eta)^{k-1}
is
\sigma(X)
-measurable, it suffices to show that

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\langle(\eta)^{k-1},f\rangle h(X)\right]={\mathbb{E}}\left[f\left((X,Y_{j})_{j=2}^{k}\right)h(X)\right]

for all bounded, measurable functions
f:({\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}})^{k-1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
h:{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
.

Let us write
\times_{j=2}^{k}A_{j-1}
for the Cartesian product of the sets
A_{1},\cdots,A_{k-1}
. Suppose that
f\left((x_{j-1},y_{j})_{j=2}^{k}\right)=\prod_{j=2}^{k}{\mathbb{I}}_{\left\{A_{j-1}\right\}}(x_{j-1},y_{j})
for the borel sets
A_{1},\dots,A_{k-1}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}})
. Then,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\langle(\eta)^{k-1},f\rangle h(X)\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\eta(A_{j})h(X)\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}{\mathbb{P}}\left((X,Y)\in A_{j}\middle|X\right)h(X)\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathbb{P}}\left((X,Y_{j})_{j=2}^{k}\in\times_{j=2}^{k}A_{j-1}\middle|X\right)h(X)\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathbb{I}}_{\left\{\times_{j=2}^{k}A_{j-1}\right\}}\left((X,Y_{j})_{j=2}^{k}\right)h(X)\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[f\left((X,Y_{j})_{j=2}^{k}\right)h(X)\right].

It is easily seen that the collection of bounded, measurable functions
f:({\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}})^{k-1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
{\mathbb{E}}\left[\langle(\eta)^{k-1},f\rangle h(X)\right]={\mathbb{E}}\left[f\left((X,Y_{j})_{j=2}^{k}\right)h(X)\right]
forms a monotone class, and the display above shows that this monotone class includes all functions of the form
f\left((x_{j-1},y_{j})_{j=2}^{k}\right)={\mathbb{I}}_{\left\{\times_{j=2}^{k}A_{j-1}\right\}}
for
A_{1},\dots,A_{k-1}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}})
. Since the set of sets
\left\{\times_{j=2}^{k}A_{j-1}:A_{1},\dots,A_{k-1}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}})\right\}
is a
\pi
-system, we may use the monotone class theorem
[
35
, Theorem 5.2.2]
to show that

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\langle(\eta)^{k-1},f\rangle h(X)\right]={\mathbb{E}}\left[f\left((X,Y_{j})_{j=2}^{k}\right)h(X)\right]

for all bounded, measurable
f:({\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}})^{k-1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

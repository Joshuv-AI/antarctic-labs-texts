Exact renormalisation for patch frequencies in inflation systems
Jan Mazáč

Abstract
. This note provides an explicit way of calculating the patch frequencies in geometric realisations of primitive substitutions using exact renormalisation relations. Further, we profit from these results to obtain the patch frequencies in the symbolic case as well as in other suspensions (under mild assumptions on the substitution). We illustrate this procedure on the Fibonacci example.



1.
Introduction

Subshifts over finite alphabets
\mathcal{A}
and their corresponding dynamical systems have been studied extensively in the last decades. A particularly well-understood class arises from fixed points of substitution rules. It is a classic result that, in the case of primitive substitutions, the subshift (or the symbolic hull) is minimal and uniquely ergodic. For non-primitive substitutions, the situation becomes more complicated as the dynamical system is no longer minimal and more ergodic measures can exist (see
[
11
]
for a detailed study in this direction, and
[
6
]
for a more general discussion for Bratelli diagrams).

While the existence of the
patch frequency measure
for primitive systems is clear, the question of how to explicitly compute the relative frequency of a given patch (which need not be a word) remains unclear. For the symbolic setting, one can derive the induced substitution on
m
letters, see
[
26
, Sec. 5.4]
for details.
Unfortunately, this approach provides frequencies for cylinder sets only, and cannot be used to determine whether a given patch is legal.
For example, we cannot decide the legality of a pattern consisting of letter
a_{1}
at the zeroth position,
a_{2}
at
\ell
-th position and
a_{3}
at
m
-th position, and compute its relative frequency. Unless the underlying symbolic hull does not belong to a special class (see
[
5
,
12
,
16
,
25
]
for explicit results), to the best of our knowledge, there is no general procedure that allows one to answer the question for a primitive aperiodic substitution rule.

The aim of this note is to provide a method to compute the exact patch frequency of any given patch (not only a subword) and (thus) deciding its legality, provided we work with systems arising from primitive substitutions.

The paper is organised as follows. We first recall the relevant notions from symbolic dynamics and the ways that allow one to pass from a symbolic to a geometric setting.
We use a self-similar tiling (a set of its control points) from a transversal of the geometric hull and show how to compute the relative frequency of
any
given patch. In particular, we derive exact renormalisation equations for the patch frequencies (Theorem
2
) and show that they possess a unique solution (Theorem
4
).
Then, we illustrate how to pass from geometric to symbolic setting in the Fibonacci example, and we provide a way of deriving the patch frequency for the symbolic setting and for other suspensions under some mild assumptions on the roof function and the substitution (Theorems
10
and
12
).

2.
Subshifts and their suspensions

Consider a primitive aperiodic substitution
\varrho
on a finite alphabet
\mathcal{A}
with
n
letters and its fixed point
w
, i.e.,
w\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
with
w=\varrho^{k}(w)
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The shift operator
S:\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
acts via
(Sw)_{i}\,=\,w_{i+1}
. On
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, we consider the product topology. This allows us to define the hull of
w
as

\mathbb{X}(w)\,=\,\overline{\{S^{i}w\ \mid\ i\in\mathbb{Z}\}},

a compact space, which is independent of the choice of the fixed point
w
, so we can write
\mathbb{X}(w)=\mathbb{X}(\varrho)
and speak about a symbolic hull of a substitution
\varrho
.
With the shift action
S
, it becomes a topological dynamical system
(\mathbb{X}(\varrho),\,\mathbb{Z})
, which is minimal
[
3
, Thm. 4.1]
, and strictly ergodic under the
\mathbb{Z}
-action, see
[
3
, Thm. 4.3]
for details.
The unique ergodic measure
\mu
on
(\mathbb{X}(\varrho),\ \mathbb{Z})
is the patch frequency measure defined via cylinder sets. Recall that, for a finite word
u=u_{0}\dots u_{\ell}
,
u_{i}\in\mathcal{A}
, the cylinder set
[u]\subset\mathbb{X}(\varrho)
is defined by

[u]\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\{x\in\mathbb{X}(\varrho)\ \mid\ x_{i}=u_{i}\ \mbox{for }0\,\leqslant\,i\,\leqslant\,\ell\},

and for its measure, we have

\mu\bigl([u]\bigr)\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\mbox{relative frequency of }u\mbox{ in }w.

Due to the strict ergodicity of
\mathbb{X}(\varrho)
, the frequency does not depend on the choice of the element from the hull.

Now, consider a non-negative function
f:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt^{+}
.
A standard construction turns the dynamical system
(\mathbb{X}(\varrho),\,\mathbb{Z})
into a flow
(\mathbb{X}(\varrho)^{(f)},\,\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt)
, usually called a suspension flow, or a flow under a function with base
(\mathbb{X}(\varrho),\,\mathbb{Z})
. Here,

\mathbb{X}(\varrho)^{(f)}\,=\,\bigl(\mathbb{X}(\varrho)\times\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt\bigr)\Bigl/\sim\,=\,\{(x,s)\ \mid x\in\mathbb{X}(\varrho),\ 0\leqslant s<f(x_{0})\},

where we identify
(x,f(x_{0}))\sim(S(x),0)
, with
x=\dots x_{-1}\,x_{0}\,x_{1}\dots\
. The
\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt
-action is given, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt
, by
V_{t}:(x,s)\mapsto(x,s+t)\mod\sim
, for any
x\in\mathbb{X}(\varrho)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt
.
Explicitly, the action reads

V_{t}(x,s)\,=\,\begin{cases}(x,s+t),&\mbox{if }0\leqslant s+t<f(x_{0}),\\
(S(x),s+t-f(x_{0})),&\mbox{if }0\leqslant s+t-f(x_{0})<f(S(x_{0}))=f(x_{1}),\\
(S^{2}(x),s+t-f(x_{0})-f(x_{1})),&\mbox{if }0\leqslant s+t-f(x_{0})-f(x_{1})<f(x_{2}),\\
&\vdots\end{cases}

More details about the suspension flows can be found for example in
[
10
, Ch. 11.1.]
, in
[
15
, Lemma 9.23]
, or, in the context of substitutions, in
[
7
,
29
]
.
The probability measure
\mu
extends to the suspension flow naturally, giving a probability measure
\mu_{f}
, such that

\mu_{f}(A,I\hskip 0.5pt)\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\frac{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$\mu(A)$}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\int_{\mathbb{X}(\varrho)}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu$}}\,\int_{I}\,\mathrm{d}s

holds for all
A\subset\mathbb{X}(\varrho)
and
I\subset\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt
.

There is a natural interpretation of this dynamical system. Consider a tiling of
\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt
, i.e., a collection
\mathcal{T}=\{T_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
of tiles
T_{i}
that cover
\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt
and satisfy
\mathrm{int}(T_{i})\cap\mathrm{int}(T_{j})=\varnothing
for
i\neq j
.
The tiles
T_{i}
are translates of prototiles
I_{a}=[0,\,f(a))
, with
a\in\mathcal{A}
. The tiles
T_{i}
are chosen so that
T_{i}
is a translate of
I_{w_{i}}
.
In other words, we replaced the letters from
\mathcal{A}
in
w
by shifted intervals, such that the left endpoint of the tile
T_{0}
coincides with the origin.
This yields a dynamical system with an
\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt
-action
(\mathbb{X}(\mathcal{T}),\,\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt)
, where the
\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt
-action is the translation
U_{t}:\{T_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mapsto\{T_{i}-t\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
, and
\mathbb{X}(\mathcal{T})
is the hull of a tiling
\mathcal{T}
given by the orbit closure of
\mathcal{T}
in the local topology, see
[
3
, Sec. 5.1]
.
This dynamical system is essentially the same as the suspension flow
(\mathbb{X}(\varrho)^{(f)},\,\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt)
, meaning that they are topologically conjugate
[
8
]
.

If we, instead of the entire tiling
\mathcal{T}
, consider the left endpoints of each interval (and eventually with colours to distinguish different prototile type), denoting the resulting point set by
\varLambda
, we obtain another dynamical system
(\mathbb{X}(\varLambda),\,\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt)
, which is locally derivable (in the sense of
[
4
]
) from
(\mathbb{X}(\mathcal{T}),\,\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt)
(and vice versa). This system is uniquely ergodic too, and the ergodic measure
\nu
is
again
given by the patch frequencies. The measure is induced by the patch frequencies on the
transversal
, i.e., on the set

\mathbb{X}_{0}(\varLambda)\,=\,\{X\in\mathbb{X}(\varLambda)\ \mid\ 0\in X\}.

The transversal is a topological space isomorphic to
\mathbb{X}(\varrho)
, but there is, in general, no group action that would turn it into a dynamical system. Nevertheless, it provides us with important information as we shall see later.

Among all choices of the function
f
, there are two prominent ones. Either
f
is a constant function, and we essentially recover the symbolic case, or we consider
(f(a))_{a\in\mathcal{A}}
to be the left Perron–Frobenius (PF) eigenvector of the substitution matrix
M_{\varrho}
of substitution
\varrho
.
Recall that
(M_{\varrho})_{ab}
counts the number of
a
’s in
\varrho(b)
. If we choose the smallest entry of the eigenvector to be 1, we speak about
natural tile lengths
.
In such a case, we obtain an additional feature of the underlying structure, namely, the inflation symmetry (or the self-similarity) which manifests itself in an essential way on the transversal
\mathbb{X}_{0}(\varLambda)
.
We note that the hull corresponding to the self-similar tiling
\mathcal{T}
is usually called the
geometric hull
of the substitution
\varrho
, see
[
3
, Sec. 5.4]
. We will later rely on the self-similar structure of the tiling and its left control points.

Recall that the last two above-mentioned dynamical systems (the two choices of the function
f
)
need not
be topologically conjugate although when considered as topological spaces, they are always homeomorphic
[
8
, Thm. 1.1]
. This is generally true for any two choices of functions
f
and
g
, and Clark and Sadun also provide a sufficient condition of the substitution
\varrho
(namely,
M_{\varrho}
has only one eigenvalue of magnitude one or greater (counted with multiplicities)) for
(\mathbb{X}(\varrho)^{(f)},\,\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt)
and
(\mathbb{X}(\varrho)^{(g)},\,\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt)
to be topologically conjugate
[
8
, Cor. 3.2]
.
Another approach (which generalises to higher dimensions) uses tools from algebraic topology and states that two such suspensions are topologically conjugate if the corresponding tilings are related by an asymptotically negligible shape change (for details, consult
[
9
,
20
,
19
]
).

3.
geometric setting and the renormalisation equations

Denote by
(\ell_{1},\,\dots,\ell_{n})
the entries of the left PF eigenvector of
M_{\varrho}
to the PF eigenvalue
\lambda
. The substitution rule
\varrho
induces a geometric inflation rule on tiles
I_{i}=[0,\ell_{i})
with

\lambda I_{i}\,=\,\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{1.7}{$\displaystyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-6.72226pt\hbox{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-5.72224pt\hbox{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}}_{j=1}^{n}\bigl(I_{j}+t_{ji}\,\bigr),

where
t_{ji}\subset\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt
are finite sets,
I_{j}+t_{ji}
is the Minkowski sum (in particular,
I_{j}+\varnothing=\varnothing
) and the sets
t_{ji}
are uniquely determined by the symbolic substitution rule
\varrho
. In other words, the geometric inflation rule inflates the tile
I_{i}
by a factor
\lambda
and subdivides according to the letters in the word
\varrho(a_{i})
. This naturally extends to all translates of the prototiles.
Consider the control points
\varLambda_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt
of all tiles of type
i
(always take the left endpoint) and denote
\underline{\varLambda}=(\varLambda_{1},\,\dots,\varLambda_{n})
. Since the original word
w
was invariant (without loss of generality) under the action of
\varrho
, the control points of the geometric inflation tiling obey the following induced inflation symmetry

\varLambda_{i}\,=\,\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{1.7}{$\displaystyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-6.72226pt\hbox{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-5.72224pt\hbox{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}}_{j=1}^{n}\,\bigl(\lambda\varLambda+t_{ij}\bigr)\,=\,\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{1.7}{$\displaystyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-6.72226pt\hbox{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-5.72224pt\hbox{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}}_{j=1}^{n}\,\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{1.7}{$\displaystyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-8.22227pt\hbox{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-6.72226pt\hbox{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcup$\cr\raise-5.72224pt\hbox{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$}}\crcr}}}}_{t\in t_{ij}}\bigl(\lambda\varLambda_{j}+t\bigr),

which holds for
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
. The set-valued matrix
(t_{ij})_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n}
is called the
displacement matrix
and fully determines the substitution (whereas the substitution matrix
M_{\varrho}=(\hskip 0.5pt\mathrm{card}\hskip 0.5pt(t_{ij}))_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n}
does not). For further details on such inflation systems, see
[
27
, Sec. 4.8 and 5.4]

Now, consider the relative frequency of a patch consisting of
m
tiles
a_{1},\,\dots,\,a_{m}
within distances
x_{1},\,\dots,\,x_{m-1}
with
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt
, (i.e., the distance between the left endpoints of
a_{i}
and
a_{i+1}
is
x_{i}
), which will be denoted by
\nu_{a_{1}\,\cdots\,a_{m}}(x_{1},\,\dots,\,x_{m-1})
. We call this function the
patch frequency function
.
Since the point set
\varLambda=\bigcup^{n}_{i=1}\varLambda_{i}
arises from a primitive substitution,
(\mathbb{X}(\varLambda),\,\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt)
is strictly ergodic
[
28
]
and patch frequencies exists uniformly for each element of
\mathbb{X}(\varLambda)
.

Set
x_{0}=0
. Then, we can write

\begin{split}\nu_{a_{1}\,\cdots\,a_{m}}(x_{1},\,\dots,\,x_{m-1})&\,=\,\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$1$}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\hskip 0.5pt\mathrm{card}\hskip 0.5pt\bigl(\varLambda^{(R)}\bigr)$}}\,\hskip 0.5pt\mathrm{card}\hskip 0.5pt\Bigl({\displaystyle\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}}\bigl(\varLambda^{(R)}_{a_{i}}-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}x_{j}\bigr)\Bigr)\\[2.0pt]
&\,=\,\frac{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$1$}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\dens(\varLambda)$}}\,\dens\Bigl({\displaystyle\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}}\bigl(\varLambda_{a_{i}}-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}x_{j}\bigr)\Bigr),\end{split}

(1)

where
A^{(R)}\,\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\,\{x\in A\ \mid\ |x|\leqslant R\}
, and
\dens(A)
denotes the density of a discrete set
A
, which is, in this case, well defined.

By definition,
\nu_{a_{1}\,\cdots\,a_{m}}(x_{1},\,\dots,\,x_{m-1})\geqslant 0
, for any
m\geqslant 2
, any
a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}
and any
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt
. Clearly, if
x_{j}\notin\varLambda_{a_{j+1}}\!\!-\varLambda_{a_{j}}
for some
j
, then
\nu_{a_{1}\,\cdots\,a_{m}}(x_{1},\,\dots,\,x_{m-1})=0
. We summarise some other properties of the patch frequency function, which directly follow from its definition.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\mathbb{X}(\varLambda)
be the geometric hull defined by a fixed point
\underline{\varLambda}
of a primitive, aperiodic, geometric inflation rule induced by a substitution on
\mathcal{A}
. Then, for any
m\geqslant 2
and for any
a_{1},\,\dots,\,a_{m}\in\mathcal{A}
, the patch frequency functions
\nu_{a_{1}\,\cdots\,a_{m}}
defined by (
1
) exist uniformly on
\mathbb{X}(\varLambda)
and are the same for any
\varLambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}(\varLambda)
. Moreover, they satisfy the following.

(i)

For all
m\geqslant 2
and for any
a_{1},\,\dots,\,a_{m}\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

\nu_{a_{1}\,\cdots\,a_{m}}(x_{1},\,\dots,\,x_{m-1})\,=\,\nu_{a_{m}\,\cdots\,a_{1}}(-x_{m-1},\,\dots,\,-x_{1}).

(ii)

If a patch
P
occurs with zero frequency, the patch frequency of any patch
P^{\prime}
that contains
P
vanishes as well.

(iii)

For any
A\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

\displaystyle\nu_{a_{1}\,\cdots\,a_{i}\,A\,A\,a_{i+3}\,\cdots\,a_{m}}

\displaystyle(x_{1},\,\dots,\,x_{i},\,0,\,x_{i+2},\,\dots,\,x_{m-1})

\displaystyle=\,\nu_{a_{1}\,\cdots\,a_{i}\,A\,a_{i+3}\,\cdots\,a_{m}}(x_{1},\,\dots,\,x_{i},\,x_{i+2},\,\dots,\,x_{m-1}).

∎

There are further symmetries, including those connecting frequencies of patches and their subpatches. For example, the relation between frequencies of three- and four-tile patches can be stated as follows

\nu_{a_{1}\,a_{2}\,a_{3}}(x_{1},\,x_{2})\,=\,\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,\,t\hskip 0.5pt\geqslant\hskip 0.5pt0\\
x_{1}\hskip 0.5pt=\hskip 0.5pts+t\end{subarray}}\nu_{a_{1}\,a\,a_{2}\,a_{3}}(s,\,t,\,x_{2})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,\,t\hskip 0.5pt\geqslant\hskip 0.5pt0\\
x_{2}\hskip 0.5pt=\hskip 0.5pts+t\end{subarray}}\nu_{a_{1}\,a_{2}\,a\,a_{3}}(x_{1},\,s,\,t)\Bigr),

and states the fact that the frequency of a given three-tile patch is determined by the sum of all frequencies of four-tile patches, three of which are the original ones.
This can be seen as an analogy to the Kolmogorov consistency equation when extending a measure, see
[
23
]
for details.

All the above-mentioned symmetries did
not

\varLambda
(to be more precise, of
\underline{\varLambda}
), but self-similarity manifests itself on the level of patch frequencies significantly. Since the frequencies exist and are
limiting
quantities (and not asymptotic ones), we can
exactly

[
2

Theorem 2
.

Let
\underline{\varLambda}
be as in Proposition
1

\lambda
be the inflation factor, and let
(t_{ij})_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n}
be the displacement matrix of the inflation rule defining
\underline{\varLambda}
. Then, the patch frequency functions satisfy the exact renormalisation relations

\nu_{a_{1}\,\cdots\,a_{m}}(x_{1},\,\dots,\,x_{m-1})\\
\,=\,\frac{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$1$}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\lambda$}}\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{m}\sum_{\alpha_{k}=1}^{n}\,\sum_{r^{(k)}\in t_{a_{\ell}\alpha_{k}}}\nu_{\alpha_{1}\,\cdots\,\alpha_{m}}\left(\frac{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$x_{1}{+}r^{(1)}{-}r^{(2)}$}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\lambda$}},\,\dots,\,\frac{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$x_{m}{+}r^{(m-1)}{-}r^{(m)}$}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\lambda$}}\right).

(2)

4.
From self-similar to symbolic settings

This last section explains how to pass from the geometric, self-similar description of one-dimensional tilings to other geometric descriptions and, in particular, back to the symbolic setting. We demonstrate this approach first for the two-tile frequencies in the Fibonacci case, and then state the general results.

4.1.
Fibonacci example

Consider the usual self-similar Fibonacci tiling with prototiles of length
\tau
and
1
arising from the substitution rule
\varrho=(a\mapsto ab,b\mapsto a)
. In order to derive the relative frequencies (pair correlations) for the symbolic case (to be understood as a tiling with prototiles of the same length
1
and different labels
a
,
b
), we consider the pair-correlation functions in the geometric setting (two-tile frequencies)
\nu_{\alpha\,\beta}
satisfying the following set of renormalisation equations, which first appeared in
[
1

\begin{split}\nu_{aa}(x)\,&=\,\frac{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$1$}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\tau$}}\Big(\nu_{aa}\bigl(\tfrac{x}{\tau}\bigr)+\nu_{ab}\bigl(\tfrac{x}{\tau}\bigr)+\nu_{ba}\bigl(\tfrac{x}{\tau}\bigr)+\nu_{bb}\bigl(\tfrac{x}{\tau}\bigr)\Big),\\[2.0pt]
\nu_{ab}(x)\,&=\,\frac{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$1$}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\tau$}}\Big(\nu_{aa}\bigl(\tfrac{x-\tau}{\tau}\bigr)+\nu_{ba}\bigl(\tfrac{x-\tau}{\tau}\bigr)\Big),\\[2.0pt]
\nu_{ba}(x)\,&=\,\frac{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$1$}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\tau$}}\Big(\nu_{aa}\bigl(\tfrac{x+\tau}{\tau}\bigr)+\nu_{ab}\bigl(\tfrac{x+\tau}{\tau}\bigr)\Big),\\[2.0pt]
\nu_{bb}(x)\,&=\,\frac{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$1$}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\tau$}}\hskip 0.5pt\nu_{aa}\bigl(\tfrac{x}{\tau}\bigr),\end{split}

(11)

We denote the pair correlations in the symbolic case by
\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{\alpha\beta}
. By choosing the tile lengths to be
1

\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{\alpha\beta}(m)
can only be non-zero for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,
\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{\alpha\beta}(m)
is the relative frequency of a cylinder set consisting of
m+1

\alpha
and the last
\beta

To obtain
\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{\alpha\beta}(m)
, add all relative frequencies of all patches (in the self-similar case) consisting of tiles
\alpha

\beta

The number of different tiles of type

\beta

\nu_{\alpha\beta}

We demonstrate this for

aabaa,\ aabab,\ baaba,\ ababa,\ abaab,\ babaa

No legal subword starting and ending with
b

\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{bb}(4)=0
. Now, for
\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{aa}(4)
, observe that the two subwords starting and ending with

can be identified with patches (using the natural tile lengths
\tau

1

3\tau+1
(

), and

(

). This yields

\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{aa}(4)\,=\,\nu_{aa}(3\tau+1)+\nu_{aa}(2\tau+2)=\tau^{-3}.

We summarise these observations as follows.

Proposition 7
.

Let
\nu_{\alpha\beta}
denote the pair correlations of the Fibonacci tiling and let
\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{\alpha\beta}
denote those of the symbolic one. Then, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
and any
\beta\in\{a,b\}

\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{a\beta}(m)=\sum_{n=1}^{m}\nu_{a\beta}(n\tau+m-n)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{b\beta}(m)=\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\nu_{b\beta}(n\tau+m-n).

Moreover, we have
\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{ab}(m)=\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{ba}(m)

Proof.

The frequencies
\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{\alpha\beta}

\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{a\beta}
, one has to include at least one

\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{b\beta}
, one has to count at least one tile

. The symmetry relation follows from palindromicity of the

3

. The last claim says that the relative frequency of two arbitrary points of the integer lattice

∎

Since the set of control points of the geometric Fibonacci tiling is a model set arsing from a cut-and-project scheme

Lemma 8
.

For the relative frequencies of the symbolic Fibonacci chain
\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{\alpha\beta}
, we have

\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{\alpha\beta}(m)\,=\,\nu_{\alpha\beta}\bigl(\bigl\lfloor\tfrac{m}{\tau}\bigr\rfloor+m-\bigl\lfloor\tfrac{m}{\tau}\bigr\rfloor\bigr)+\nu_{\alpha\beta}\bigl(\bigl\lceil\tfrac{m}{\tau}\bigr\rceil+m-\bigl\lceil\tfrac{m}{\tau}\bigr\rceil\bigr).

Proof.

Since
\nu_{\alpha\beta}(x)\neq 0
only if
z\in\varLambda-\varLambda=\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}([-\tau^{2},\,\tau^{2}])
, we can restrict the possible
n
for which
\nu_{\alpha\beta}(n\tau+m-n)
can be non-zero, and reduce the number of summands in Eq. (
12
). For that, consider
(n\tau+m-n)^{\star}=n(1-\tau)+m-n\in[-\tau^{2},\,\tau^{2}]
, which implies

\tfrac{m}{\tau}-1\,\leqslant\,n\,\leqslant\,\tfrac{m}{\tau}+1.

As both
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and since
\tfrac{m}{\tau}\notin\mathbb{Z}
for non-zero
m
, we have
n\in\bigl\{\lfloor\tfrac{m}{\tau}\bigr\rfloor,\,\lceil\tfrac{m}{\tau}\bigr\rceil\bigr\}
.
∎

4.2.
General results

Note first that we used the fact that the natural tile lengths of the geometric realisation of the Fibonacci tiling are rationally independent, which allowed us to identify the total number of tiles within a distance given by a number
n\tau+m
. To ensure that the same holds for a primitive substitution
\varrho
, we need to assume that the characteristic polynomial of
M_{\varrho}
is irreducible (which is a condition appearing quite often; compare, for example,
[
30
, Prop. 2.17]
)

Lemma 9
.

Let
M\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times n}
be a non-negative irreducible matrix with irreducible characteristic polynomial. Let further
\bm{v}
be its PF eigenvector corresponding to the leading eigenvalue
\lambda
. Then, the entries of
v
are rationally independent.

Proof.

Since
M\bm{v}=\lambda\bm{v}
, we can assume that all
v_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[\lambda]
. Since
\lambda
is a root of monic irreducible polynomial of degree
n
,
\mathbb{Z}[\lambda]
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-module of rank
n
. We show that
\langle v_{1},\,\dots,\,v_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
is also a
\mathbb{Z}
-module of rank
n
, which is equivalent to saying that the entries
v_{i}
are rationally independent.

As
\bm{v}
is the PF eigenvector,
v_{1}\neq 0
. Since
M\bm{v}=\lambda\bm{v}
, we have
\lambda v_{i}\in\langle v_{1},\,\dots,\,v_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
for all
i
, and by linearity, we obtain that
x\in\langle v_{1},\,\dots,\,v_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
implies
\lambda x\in\langle v_{1},\,\dots,\,v_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
. In particular,

\langle v_{1},\,\lambda v_{1},\,\dots,\,\lambda^{n-1}v_{1}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}\,\subseteq\,\langle v_{1},\,\dots,\,v_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}\,\subseteq\,\mathbb{Z}[\lambda].

Since
1,\,\lambda,\,\dots,\,\lambda^{n-1}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
as the minimal polynomial of
\lambda
coincides with the characteristic polynomial of
M
(which is of order
n
),
v_{1},\,\lambda v_{1},\,\dots,\,\lambda^{n-1}v_{1}
are also rationally independent. Consequently,
\langle v_{1},\,\lambda v_{1},\,\dots,\,\lambda^{n-1}v_{1}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-module of rank
n
and so is
\langle v_{1},\,\dots,\,v_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
.
∎

The same property also holds for the left eigenvector. In our setting, with a primitive substitution
\varrho
over
\mathcal{A}
with
\lambda
being an algebraic integer of order
n
(equivalent to the irreducibility of the characteristic polynomial of
M_{\varrho}
), the lemma implies that the natural tile lengths
\ell_{i}
are rationally independent.

Theorem 10
.

Let
\varrho
be a primitive substitution over
\mathcal{A}
such that the characteristic polynomial of
M_{\varrho}
is irreducible. Let
\ell_{j}
denote the natural tile lengths of the geometric inflation rule induced by
\varrho
. Further, let
\nu_{\alpha\,\beta}
denote the pair correlation of the self-similar tiling. Then, for any element of the symbolic hull
\mathbb{X}(\varrho)
, the pair correlations
\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{\alpha\beta}
exists uniformly and, for all
a_{i},\,\beta\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
and for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{a_{i}\beta}\,(m)\,=\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{j}\hskip 0.5pt\geqslant\hskip 0.5pt0,\,m_{i}\hskip 0.5pt\neq\hskip 0.5pt0\\
\sum m_{j}\,=\,m\end{subarray}}\nu_{a_{i}\beta}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}\ell_{j}\Bigr).

(13)

Proof.

The relative frequencies
\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{a_{i}\beta}(m)
of a two-letter patch consisting of letters
a_{i}
and
\beta
within distance
m
are given as the sum of relative frequencies of all subwords of length
m+1
starting with
a_{i}
and ending with
\beta
. All these frequencies exist uniformly due to unique ergodicity.
Now, the induced geometric inflation gives a tiling with prototiles of length
\ell_{j}
, all rationally independent by Lemma
9
. Since
\nu_{a_{i}\beta}(x)
can only be non-zero if
x
is a distance between two tiles, we can assume that
x>0
and that
x
can be uniquely decomposed as
x=\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}\ell_{j}
, where
m_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
m_{i}>0
. This follows from the rational independence of
\ell_{j}
’s. The quantity
\nu_{a_{i}\beta}(x)
then represents the sum of all patches consisting of
m+1
tiles starting with
a_{i}
, ending with tile
\beta
. This gives the desired correspondence.
By summing over all subwords of length
m+1
starting with
a_{i}
and ending with
\beta
, the correspondence yields (
13
).
∎

Remark 11
.

If the geometric inflation rule
\varrho
permits a model set description with total window
\varOmega
, we can restrict the summation on the RHS of Eq. (
13
) (analogously to Lemma
8
) by taking into account only those summands that can be non-zero and obtain

\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{a_{i}\beta}\,(m)\,=\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{j}\hskip 0.5pt\geqslant\hskip 0.5pt0,\,m_{i}\hskip 0.5pt\neq\hskip 0.5pt0\\
\sum m_{j}\,=\,m\\
\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}\ell^{\star}_{j}\hskip 0.5pt\in\hskip 0.5pt\varOmega-\varOmega\end{subarray}}\nu_{a_{i}\beta}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}\ell_{j}\Bigr).

In the proof, we used the fact that
\mathbb{X}(\varrho)
can also be understood as a suspension flow of
(\mathbb{X}(\varrho),\,\mathbb{Z})
with the constant roof (height) function. Using a different roof function (choosing tile lengths)
\bm{\ell}^{\prime}=(\ell^{\prime}_{1},\,\ell^{\prime}_{2},\,\dots,\,\ell^{\prime}_{n})
and considering the suspension flow
(\mathbb{X}^{(\bm{\ell^{\prime}})}(\varrho),\,\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt)
, we obtain for the patch frequency measure associated with any tiling from
\mathbb{X}^{(\bm{\ell^{\prime}})}(\varrho)
an analogous claim. Its proof mimics the previous one and relies on the fact that we can interpret the value
\nu_{\alpha\beta}(z)
as the sum of relative frequencies of all cylinders of
one
length.

Theorem 12
.

Let
\varrho
and
\nu_{\alpha\beta}
be as in Theorem
10
. Consider a hull of a tiling arising from
\mathbb{X}(\varrho)
using tile lengths
\bm{\ell}^{\prime}=(\ell^{\prime}_{1},\,\ell^{\prime}_{2},\,\dots,\,\ell^{\prime}_{n})
, i.e., consider a suspension flow
(\mathbb{X}^{(\bm{\ell^{\prime}})}(\varrho),\,\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.5pt)
with the height function
\bm{\ell}^{\prime}
. Then, for the pair correlation
\nu^{\prime}_{\alpha\beta}
on
\mathbb{X}^{(\bm{\ell^{\prime}})}(\varrho)
, the following holds.

(i)

If the
\ell^{\prime}_{j}
are rationally independent, we have

\nu^{\prime}_{\alpha\beta}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}\ell^{\prime}_{j}\Bigr)\,=\,\nu_{\alpha\beta}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}\ell_{j}\Bigr).

(ii)

If the
\ell^{\prime}_{j}
are rationally dependent, we have

\nu^{\prime}_{a_{i}\beta}\,\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}m^{\prime}_{j}\ell^{\prime}_{j}\Bigr)\,=\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{j}\hskip 0.5pt\geqslant\hskip 0.5pt0,\,m_{i}\hskip 0.5pt\neq\hskip 0.5pt0\\
\sum m_{j}\,=\,\sum m^{\prime}_{j}\end{subarray}}\nu_{a_{i}\beta}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}\ell_{j}\Bigr)

for
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
and for all
m^{\prime}_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
m^{\prime}_{i}\neq 0
. ∎

The same approach applies to any patch frequency functions, and we end up with a method that can decide whether or not a given patch is legal for a given tiling (bi-infinite sequence) and give the exact relative frequency in the first case. For example, under the assumptions of Theorem
10
, for the relative frequencies on the symbolic hull
\mathbb{X}(\varrho)
, one has

\nu^{\mathrm{s}}_{a_{1}\,\cdots\,a_{k}}(m_{1},\,\dots,\,m_{k-1})\,=\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m^{(i)}_{j}\hskip 0.5pt\geqslant\hskip 0.5pt0,\,m^{(i)}_{i}\hskip 0.5pt\neq\hskip 0.5pt0\\
\sum m^{(i)}_{j}\,=\,m_{i}\end{subarray}}\nu_{a_{1}\,\cdots\,a_{k}}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}m^{(1)}_{j}\ell_{j},\,\dots,\,\sum_{j=1}^{n}m^{(k-1)}_{j}\ell_{j}\Bigr).

It is obvious how to extend Theorem
12
, but we will not write down the statement as the notation would be even harder to read, and the underlying principle should already be clear.

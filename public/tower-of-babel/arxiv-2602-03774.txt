Minimum Number of Monochromatic Subgraphs of a Random Graph
Yatin Dandi, David Gamarnik, Haodong Zhu

Abstract
We consider the problem of minimizing the number of monochromatic subgraphs of a random graph, when each node of the host graph is assigned one of the two colors. Using a recently discovered contiguity between appearance of strictly balanced subgraphs F F in a random graph, and random hypergraphs where copies of F F are generated independently, we show that the minimum value converges to a limit, when the expected number of copies of F F is linear in the number of nodes | V | |V| . Furthermore, using the connections with mean field spin glass models, we obtain an asymptotic expression for this limit as the normalized expected number of copies of F F and the size of F F diverge to infinity.

Introduction

The study of monochromatic subgraphs in graph bipartitions is a widely investigated problem in extremal combinatorics and Ramsey theory.
A classical result by Goodman
[
8
]
states that in any bipartition of the complete graph
K_{n}
, the number of monochromatic triangles admits a sharp lower bound

T(n)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{u(u-1)(u-2)}{3},&\text{if }n=2u;\\
\dfrac{2u(u-1)(4u+1)}{3},&\text{if }n=4u+1;\\
\dfrac{2u(u+1)(4u-1)}{3},&\text{if }n=4u+3.\end{cases}

While these deterministic settings have attracted considerable attention and yielded several intriguing results,
the same question in the context of random graphs,
such as Erdős–Rényi random graphs, remains far less understood, even for the special case of triangles. In this paper, we consider this question of finding the smallest number of monochromatic subgraphs in bipartitions of random graphs. We do so in a general setting,
where the subgraph is only assumed to be
strictly balanced.

To set the stage, let
F
be a graph on
r
vertices. An
F
-graph

is defined as any pair
(V=V(H),E=E(H))
, where

is a set of vertices, and

is a collection of copies

of

called hyperedges, where for each

, the vertex set of
e
is subset of
V
.
A random
F
-graph
\mathbb{H}_{F}(n,q)
is an
F
-graph with vertex set
V=[n]=\left\{{1,2,\ldots,n}\right\}
, where each of the

(1.1)

\displaystyle\binom{n}{r}\cdot\frac{r!}{{\rm aut}(F)}

potential copies of

on vertices in

is included in the edge set

independently with probability

denotes the set of automorphisms of a graph

is

(two nodes

Given a simple graph

is just

) and given its bipartition

, a cut, or more specifically an

The

In combinatorial optimization and theoretical computer science, there is a long-standing interest in understanding the minimum number of monochromatic subgraphs achievable by varying over all bipartitions of graphs, both in the special case of max-cut values associated with

, and beyond
[

4

7

. This is the question we address in our paper.

Let

There are many examples of strictly 1-balanced graphs, including cycles and complete graphs. However, any disconnected graph is not strictly 1-balanced, and neither is a tree-graph.

Recently a great progress was achieved in understanding the relationship between

and
\mathbb{H}_{F}(n,q)

with

Our next result concerns obtaining explicit

In this section reformulate the problem
of minimizing the number of monochromatic subgraphs in terms of an associated version

Given

\eta=\eta(\varepsilon)\in(0,1/4)
such that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}-2\eta\right)\log\left(\frac{1}{2}-2\eta\right)+\left(\frac{1}{2}+2\eta\right)\log\left(\frac{1}{2}+2\eta\right)>-\left(1-(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\right)\log 2.

For such
\eta
, combining this inequality with (
4.11
) and (
4.15
), we obtain

(4.16)

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{0}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{0}\\
|\sigma\cdot\sigma^{\prime}|\geq\eta n\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[W(\sigma)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{2\log 2}\right]=o_{n}(1)\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[{|X|}\right]^{2}.

We now turn to the second term on the right-hand side of (
4
).
For
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{0}
, let
\sum_{i\in[n]}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i}^{\prime}=4y
, where
y
is an integer. Then (
4.6
) (with
t=0
) implies

\mathbb{E}\left[{W_{n}(\sigma)W_{n}(\sigma^{\prime})}\right]=\frac{(1+4y/n)^{r}+(1-4y/n)^{r}-2}{(2^{r}-2)n}.

Define

\rho=\frac{(1+4y/n)^{r}+(1-4y/n)^{r}-2}{2^{r}-2}.

Since both
W(\sigma)
and
W(\sigma^{\prime})
follow
\mathcal{N}(0,1/n)
,
Lemma

4.2
gives

(4.17)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[W(\sigma),W(\sigma^{\prime})\geq(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{2\log 2}\right]\leq\frac{(1+\rho)^{2}}{4\pi(1-\varepsilon)^{2}n\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\log 2}\cdot 2^{-\frac{2(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}{1+\rho}n}.

Consequently, by (
4.9
) and (
4.14
), the number of pairs
(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})
such that
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{0}
and
\sum_{i\in[n]}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i}^{\prime}=4y
is

(4.18)

\displaystyle\quad\binom{n}{n/2}\binom{n/2}{n/4-y}^{2}=\frac{n!}{\left((n/4+y)!\right)^{2}\left((n/4-y)!\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=(1+o_{n}(1))(2\pi n)^{-3/2}\cdot\frac{16}{1-16y^{2}/n^{2}}\cdot\left(\frac{4}{1+4y/n}\right)^{n/2+2y}\left(\frac{4}{1-4y/n}\right)^{n/2-2y}

\displaystyle=(1+o_{n}(1))(2\pi n)^{-3/2}\cdot\frac{16}{1-16y^{2}/n^{2}}\cdot 2^{2n}

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\left(-\frac{n}{2}\left[\left(1+\frac{4y}{n}\right)\log\left(1+\frac{4y}{n}\right)+\left(1-\frac{4y}{n}\right)\log\left(1-\frac{4y}{n}\right)\right]\right).

On the other hand, for
K>0
, combining (
4.17
) and (
4.18
) yields

(4.19)

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{0}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{0}\\
|\sigma\cdot\sigma^{\prime}|\in[K\sqrt{n},\eta n]\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[W(\sigma),W(\sigma^{\prime})\geq(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{2\log 2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle(1+o_{n}(1))(2\pi n)^{-5/2}\cdot\frac{1}{2(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\log 2}\cdot 2^{2n(1-(1-\varepsilon)^{2})}\cdot\sum_{|y|\in[K\sqrt{n}/4,\eta n/4]}\frac{(1+\rho)^{2}}{\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}\frac{16}{1-\frac{16y^{2}}{n^{2}}}

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(\frac{n}{2}\left[\frac{4\rho\log 2}{1+\rho}-\left(1+\frac{4y}{n}\right)\log\left(1+\frac{4y}{n}\right)-\left(1-\frac{4y}{n}\right)\log\left(1-\frac{4y}{n}\right)\right]\right).

Let
t=\frac{4y}{n}\in[-\eta,\eta]\setminus(-K/\sqrt{n},K/\sqrt{n})
. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{4\rho\log 2}{1+\rho}-(1+t)\log(1+t)-(1-t)\log(1-t)

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle 4\log 2\cdot\frac{(1+t)^{r}+(1-t)^{r}-2}{2^{r}-2}-(1+t)\log(1+t)-(1-t)\log(1-t).

On the other hand,

(4.20)

\displaystyle 4\log 2\cdot\frac{(1+t)^{r}+(1-t)^{r}-2}{2^{r}-2}-(1+t)\log(1+t)-(1-t)\log(1-t)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle-\left({1+o_{r}(1)}\right)t^{2}+o_{1/\left|{t}\right|}(t^{2}).

For sufficiently small
\eta
, there exists
D>0
which depends on
r
only, such that for all
t\in[-\eta,\eta]
,

(4.21)

\displaystyle 4\log 2\cdot\frac{(1+t)^{r}+(1-t)^{r}-2}{2^{r}-2}-(1+t)\log(1+t)-(1-t)\log(1-t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-(D+o_{r}(1))t^{2}.

Let

\rho_{\max}:=\sup_{t\in[-\eta,\eta]}\frac{(1+t)^{r}+(1-t)^{r}-2}{2^{r}-2}.

Then combining (
4.19
) and (
4.21
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{0}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{0}\\
|\sigma\cdot\sigma^{\prime}|\in[K\sqrt{n},\eta n]\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[W(\sigma),W(\sigma^{\prime})\geq(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{2\log 2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle(1+o_{n}(1))(2\pi n)^{-5/2}\cdot\frac{1}{2(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\log 2}\cdot 2^{2n(1-(1-\varepsilon)^{2})}\cdot\frac{(1+\rho_{\max})^{2}}{\sqrt{1-\rho_{\max}^{2}}}\cdot\frac{16}{1-\eta^{2}}

\displaystyle\times 2\sum_{y=K\sqrt{n}/4}^{\infty}\exp\left(-8(D+o_{r}(1))\cdot\frac{y^{2}}{n}\right).

Meanwhile, the tail sum can be bounded as

\displaystyle\sum_{y=K\sqrt{n}/4}^{\infty}\exp\left(-8(D+o_{r}(1))\cdot\frac{y^{2}}{n}\right)

\displaystyle=(1+o_{n}(1))\sqrt{n}\int_{K/4}^{\infty}\exp\left(-8(D+o_{r}(1))x^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=o_{K,r}(1)\cdot\sqrt{n}.

Hence,

(4.22)

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{0}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{0}\\
|\sigma\cdot\sigma^{\prime}|\in[K\sqrt{n},\eta n]\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[W(\sigma),W(\sigma^{\prime})\geq(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{2\log 2}\right]=o_{K,r}(1)\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[{|X|}\right]^{2}.

Finally, we handle the case where
|\sigma\cdot\sigma^{\prime}|\in[-K\sqrt{n},K\sqrt{n}]
.
In this regime,
t=\frac{4y}{n}\in[-K/\sqrt{n},K/\sqrt{n}]
, and we have

(4.23)

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{(1+t)^{r}+(1-t)^{r}-2}{2^{r}-2}=o_{n}(1).

Then, by (
4.20
) and
t\in[-K/\sqrt{n},K/\sqrt{n}]
,

(4.24)

\displaystyle\quad 4\log 2\cdot\frac{(1+t)^{r}+(1-t)^{r}-2}{2^{r}-2}-(1+t)\log(1+t)-(1-t)\log(1-t)

\displaystyle=-(1+o_{r}(1)+o_{n}(1))t^{2}.

Combining (
4.23
) and (
4.24
) with (
4.19
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{0}}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{0},\,|\sigma\cdot\sigma^{\prime}|\in[K\sqrt{n}]}\mathbb{P}\left[W(\sigma),W(\sigma^{\prime})\geq(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{2\log 2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle(1+o_{n}(1))(2\pi n)^{-5/2}\cdot\frac{1}{2(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\log 2}\cdot 2^{2n(1-(1-\varepsilon)^{2})}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{y=-K\sqrt{n}/4}^{K\sqrt{n}/4}16\exp\left(-(1+o_{r}(1)+o_{n}(1))\cdot\frac{8y^{2}}{n}\right).

We estimate the sum by an integral as

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{y=-K\sqrt{n}/4}^{K\sqrt{n}/4}\exp\left(-(1+o_{r}(1)+o_{n}(1))\cdot\frac{8y^{2}}{n}\right)

\displaystyle=(1+o_{n}(1))\sqrt{n}\int_{-K/4}^{K/4}\exp\left(-(1+o_{r}(1)+o_{n}(1))\cdot 8z^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq(1+o_{r}(1)+o_{n}(1))\cdot\frac{\sqrt{2\pi n}}{4}.

Hence,

(4.25)

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{0}}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{0},\,|\sigma\cdot\sigma^{\prime}|\in[K\sqrt{n}]}\mathbb{P}\left[W(\sigma),W(\sigma^{\prime})\geq(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{2\log 2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle(1+o_{n}(1)+o_{r}(1))\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}(1-\varepsilon)^{2}n^{2}\log 2}\cdot 2^{2n(1-(1-\varepsilon)^{2})}

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle(1+o_{n}(1)+o_{r}(1))\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[{|X|}\right]^{2}.

Combining the estimates from (
4
), (
4.16
), (
4.22
), and (
4.25
), we conclude:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{|X|^{2}}\right]=(1+o_{n}(1)+o_{r}(1)+o_{K,r}(1))\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[{|X|}\right]^{2}.

Since both
\mathbb{E}\left[{|X|^{2}}\right]
and
\mathbb{E}\left[{|X|}\right]^{2}
are independent of
K
, we may let
K\rightarrow\infty
to conclude:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{|X|^{2}}\right]=(1+o_{n}(1)+o_{r}(1))\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[{|X|}\right]^{2}.

Then, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

(4.26)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\max_{\sigma\in S_{0}}W_{n}(\sigma)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{2\log 2}\right]=\mathbb{P}\left[|X|\geq 1\right]\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[{|X|}\right]^{2}}{\mathbb{E}\left[{|X|^{2}}\right]}=1+o_{n}(1)+o_{r}(1).

Combining the upper bound in (
4.8
) with the lower bound in (
4.26
) completes the proof of Lemma
4.3
.
∎

5
Proof of
Theorem

1.2

Since
Theorem

1.1
yields that
\frac{{\rm MinM}(\mathbb{G}(n,p),F)}{n}\rightarrow m(F,c)
, to compute
m(F,c)
, we only need to consider the
n
such that
4\mid n
.
For
\sigma\in S_{nh}
, by (
4.2
), we have

\alpha=\kappa_{1}\cdot\frac{(1+h)^{r}+(1-h)^{r}-2}{2}.

For
h_{0}
as defined in
Lemma

4.3
,

\alpha_{0}=\kappa_{1}\cdot\frac{(1+h_{0})^{r}+(1-h_{0})^{r}-2}{2}\leq 0.

Recall from (
3.3
) that

\displaystyle T_{n}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=n^{-(r+1)/2}\cdot\sqrt{\kappa_{2}}\cdot\max_{\sigma\in\left\{{-1,1}\right\}^{n}}U_{n}(\sigma),

\displaystyle\quad\text{subject to }n^{-r}\sum_{v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\in[n]}\kappa_{1}f(\sigma_{v_{1}},\ldots,\sigma_{v_{r}})=\alpha.

By
Lemma

4.3
, with probability
1+o_{n}(1)+o_{r}(1)
,

(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\sqrt{2\kappa_{2}r!(2^{r-1}-1)\log 2}\leq T_{n}^{0}\leq\max_{\alpha\in[\alpha_{0},0]}T_{n}^{\alpha}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\cdot\sqrt{2\kappa_{2}r!(2^{r-1}-1)\log 2}.

It follows that, with probability
1+o_{n}(1)+o_{r}(1)
,

V_{n}=\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{\alpha}\left(\alpha d+T_{n}^{\alpha}\sqrt{d}\right)\right]\geq\mathbb{E}\left[T_{n}^{0}\cdot\sqrt{d}\right]\geq(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\sqrt{2\kappa_{2}r!(2^{r-1}-1)d\log 2}.

Moreover, by
Lemma

4.1
, with high probability,

\max_{\alpha\in[\alpha_{0},0]}\left(\alpha d+T_{n}^{\alpha}\sqrt{d}\right)=\max_{\alpha}\left(\alpha d+T_{n}^{\alpha}\sqrt{d}\right).

On the other hand, as
\alpha\leq 0
,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\max_{\alpha}\left(\alpha\sqrt{d}+T_{n}^{\alpha}\right)\leq n^{-(r+1)/2}\sqrt{\kappa_{2}}\cdot\max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}U_{n}(\sigma).

We next investigate the right-hand side of (
5.1
). By (
4.6
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{U_{n}(\sigma)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=r!\cdot n^{r}\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor{r/2}\rfloor}\binom{r}{2m}1+O_{n}(1)n^{r-1}\sum_{i\in[n]}\sigma_{i}

\displaystyle\leq f(r)^{2}\cdot n^{r},

for some function
f(r)>0
.
Consequently, by (
3.9
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(n^{-r/2}\max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}U_{n}(\sigma)\geq x\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\mathbb{P}\left(n^{-r/2}U_{n}(\sigma)\geq x\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}\cdot\frac{f(r)}{x\sqrt{2\pi}}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{2f(r)^{2}}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\geq 2f(r)n^{1/2}\\
\sqrt{\kappa_{2}}n^{-1/2}x\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left(n^{-r/2}\max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}U_{n}(\sigma)\geq x\right)=o_{n}(1),

which, combined with(
5.1
), implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\geq 2f(r)\sqrt{\kappa_{2}}\\
x\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{\alpha}\left(\alpha d+T_{n}^{\alpha}\sqrt{d}\right)\geq x\right)=o_{n}(1).

Hence, the sequence
\left\{\max_{\alpha}\left(\alpha\sqrt{d}+T_{n}^{\alpha}\right)\right\}_{n\geq 1}
is uniformly integrable.
As a consequence, with probability
1+o_{n}(1)+o_{r}(1)
,

\displaystyle V_{n}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{\alpha}\left(\alpha d+T_{n}^{\alpha}\sqrt{d}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{\alpha\in[\alpha_{0},0]}\left(\alpha d+T_{n}^{\alpha}\sqrt{d}\right)\right]+o_{n}(\sqrt{d})

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\cdot\sqrt{2\kappa_{2}r!(2^{r-1}-1)d\log 2}+o_{n}(\sqrt{d}).

Proposition

3.2
then yields that, with probability
1+o_{n}(1)+o_{r}(1)
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\min_{\sigma\in\left\{{-1,1}\right\}^{n}}H(\sigma)=(1+o_{r}(1))\cdot\sqrt{2\kappa_{2}r!(2^{r-1}-1)d\log 2}+\frac{c^{s}}{2^{r-1}{\rm aut}(F)}+o_{n,d}(\sqrt{d}).

The result then follows.
∎

Proposition 4.22
.

Let

\nu_{D}(G_{2})

and
\nu_{D}(G_{2})

Proof.

By

, Lemma 2.14(1)]
,
\mbox{aD-rk}(G_{1})=\mbox{aD-rk}(G_{2})
. If

.

If

Now, assume that

5.
The infinitesimal groups commute

Our goal in this section is to prove that the infinitesimal subgroups associated with any two distinct distinguished sorts commute. In the following section this is used to prove that the subgroup generated by the four subgroups is isomorphic to their direct product. We first need some additional technical preparations.

5.1.
Fine local analysis

Local analysis in, e.g., o-minimal structures, builds heavily on the fact that given a definable set
X
and a generic point
x\in X
there are arbitrarily small generic neighborhoods of
x
in
X
(i.e. neighborhoods that are generic vicinities), over any set of parameters. Our definition of vicinic sets in
[
8
, §4]
aimed to capture this property in a more general setting, covering all the distinguished sorts. In the present section, we extend this analysis by allowing parameters coming from different distinguished sorts.

The following proof requires specific properties of the different distinguished sorts. We refer the reader to
[
5
,
8
]
for a more detailed exposition covering the main properties of these structures. Throughout the proof, we give specific references for those properties we need.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
D_{1},D_{2}
be distinguished sorts with
D_{2}
unstable. Let
a\in D_{1}^{m}
and let
b
be generic in
D_{2}^{n}
over some parameter set
A
. Then for every
U\in\nu_{D_{2}^{n}}(b)

there exists
V\in\nu_{D_{2}^{n}}(b)
,
V\subseteq U
, such that
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a/Ab[V])=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a/Ab)
.

Proof.

We first observe that it is enough to find a set
U^{\prime}
,
b\in U^{\prime}\subseteq U
, with
U^{\prime}
definable over
B\supset A
, such that
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a/Bb)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a/Ab)
, and a subset
U^{\prime\prime}\subseteq U^{\prime}
definable over some
C\supseteq A
, such that
b
is generic in
U^{\prime\prime}
over
C
.

Indeed, if we can find such sets, then let
p
be a complete type over
BCb
extending
\tp(a/Bb)
such that
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(p)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a/Bb)
. Let
a^{\prime}\models p
and let
\sigma
be an automorphism over
Bb
mapping
a^{\prime}
to
a
. So
\sigma(U^{\prime\prime})
is a
\sigma(C)
-definable subset of
U^{\prime}
containing
b
. By our choice of
p
,
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a/Bb)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a^{\prime}/BbC)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a/Bb\sigma(C))
(so
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a/Ab)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a/Ab\sigma(C))
).
Also,
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(b/A)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(b/C)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(b/\sigma(C))

(defined over

), we get that

\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a/Ab[V])=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a/Ab)

Thus, let us see that we can find
U^{\prime}
and
U^{\prime\prime}
as above. In the classes of valued field we consider,

can be either an SW-uniformity,
\Gamma
in the
p
-adically closed case (so a

-group) or

. We investigate each case separately.

Case 1:

is an SW-uniformity.

By Lemma
[
5
, Lemma 3.14(2)]
, there exists a model
N\supseteq B
with
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(b/N)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(b/B)=n
.
By
[
19
, Proposition 4.5]
, as
b
is generic in
D_{2}^{n}
, there is an
N
-definable open neighborhood

of

.

Let

be a definable neighborhood base at

(so

), and shrinking

if needed, assume that

for some

. For

The proof is, in essence, the same as that of
[

need not belong to an SW-uniformity. In
[

In view of the above, the set

This gives the desired definable set

Case 2:

By

. Consider the collection of formulas in the

Note that the relation (in the variable

Case 3:

The proof is similar to the one in Case 2. By

5.2.
Proof of commutation

We return to our task of proving that the infinitesimal subgroups commute. Recall that
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}\succ{\mathcal{K}}
is a
|{\mathcal{K}}|^{+}
-saturated extension. We need the following result:

Fact 5.2
.

[
8
, Proposition 9.3]

Any two distinct distinguished sorts
D_{1},D_{2}
in
{\mathcal{K}}
are foreign. I.e., there is no definable infinite
Z\subseteq D_{1}^{n}\times D_{2}^{m}
projecting finite-to-one on both factors.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
G
be a definable group. If
D_{1}
,
D_{2}
are distinct distinguished sorts then
\nu_{D_{1}}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})\cap\nu_{D_{2}}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})=\{e\}
.

Proof.

If either
G
is not locally almost internal to
D_{1}
or
D_{2}
then the conclusion is straightforward, so we assume that
G
is locally almost internal to both
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
.

Since any two distinct distinguished sorts are foreign, the intersection
\nu_{D_{1}}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})\cap\nu_{D_{2}}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})
is finite. It follows from Corollary
4.26
that at least one of the two groups is torsion-free, so the intersection must be trivial.
∎

Definition 5.4
.

Two type-definable groups
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}

commute
if for any
g\in\rho_{1}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})
and
h\in\rho_{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})
,
gh=hg
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
G
be a definable group in
{\mathcal{K}}
and let

and
D_{2}
be two
distinct distinguished sorts. Then
\nu_{D_{1}}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})
and
\nu_{D_{2}}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})

Proof.

We may assume that
G
is almost strongly internal to both
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
, as otherwise
\nu_{D}(G)=\{e\}
, and there is nothing more to prove. We may further assume that
G
is both
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
-balanced. Indeed, if
G
is not
D_{2}
-balanced, i.e.
D_{2}=(K/{\mathcal{O}})_{\text{\tiny{p-adic}}}
(Proposition
3.2
) then there is a finite normal abelian subgroup

with
G/H

D_{2}
-balanced (Proposition
3.11
). Since
K
is
p
-adically closed,
D_{1}
is necessarily unstable. Let
f:G\to G/H
be the quotient homomorphism. By definition,
f_{*}\nu_{D_{2}}(G)\equiv\nu_{D_{2}}(G/H)
and by Proposition
4.22
,
f_{*}\nu_{D_{1}}(G)\equiv\nu_{D_{1}}(G/H)
.

Assume that
\nu_{D_{2}}(G/H)
and
\nu_{D_{1}}(G/H)
commute; thus as

is a homomorphism, for any

and

for some

. Since

(Proposition

),

for

. By Corollary
4.26
,
\nu_{D_{1}}(G)
is divisible so every element of

is of the form

for some

Assume then that
G
is
D_{1}
-balanced and
D_{2}
-balanced. By Lemma

, it suffices to show that
\nu_{D_{1}}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})
and

normalize each other. If

is stable then

is a definable normal subgroup of

, so obviously
\nu_{D_{1}}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})
normalizes
\nu_{D_{2}}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})
. We assume that
D_{2}
is unstable and we prove that
\nu_{D_{1}}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})
normalizes
\nu_{D_{2}}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})
and then we will conclude by symmetry.

We will actually show something stronger: For every definable

-critical subset

and some

. Since

(Proposition

), the result will follow by compactness.

Fix some

-critical subset

and a

6.
The main theorem and rank (in)equality

We are now ready to prove our main theorem.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}
be an expansion of a valued field of characteristic
0
that is either (i)
V
-minimal, (ii) power bounded
T
-convex or (iii)
p
-adically closed and let

be an infinite
{\mathcal{K}}
-definable group. Let
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}\succ{\mathcal{K}}
be a
|K|^{+}
-saturated elementary extension.

The group generated by the
\nu_{D}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})
in
G(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})
is type definable, and definably isomorphic to

\nu_{K}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})\times\nu_{\Gamma}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})\times\nu_{K/{\mathcal{O}}}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})\times\nu_{\textbf{k}}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}}).

Proof.

By Proposition
5.5
, any two of the
\nu_{D}
commute. Let

be the subgroup generated by the
\nu_{D}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})
in
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}
.
It will suffice to show that the map
\tau:\nu(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})\to N
given by
(a,b,c,d)\mapsto abcd
is a group isomorphism.

The map
\tau
is a group homomorphism (since the
\nu_{D}
commute) and it is obviously onto. To show that it is injective, we will need to prove a generalization of Lemma
5.3
. For ease of presentation we assume that all the
\nu_{D}
are non-trivial, the general case follows similarly. It will be convenient to deal with
K/{\mathcal{O}}
last.

By Lemma
5.3
,
\nu_{\Gamma}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})
has trivial intersection with
\nu_{\textbf{k}}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})
. Consider
S:=\nu_{K}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})\cap\left(\nu_{\Gamma}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})\cdot\nu_{\textbf{k}}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})\right)
. By what we have just shown we can identify
S
with a subgroup of
\nu_{\Gamma}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})\times\nu_{\textbf{k}}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})
. Since both

and
\Gamma
and
K
and

are foreign it is not hard to see that
K
and
\Gamma\times\textbf{k}
are foreign, it follows that

is finite. So

is a finite subgroup of
\nu_{K}
. But
\nu_{K}
is torsion-free by Corollary

(2), so must be trivial.

Finally, consider
\nu_{K/{\mathcal{O}}}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})\cap(\nu_{K}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})\cdot\nu_{\Gamma}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}})\cdot\nu_{\textbf{k}}(\widehat{\mathcal{K}}))

{\mathcal{K}}
is not

is torsion-free and if
{\mathcal{K}}
is
p
-adically closed then the group on the right is torsion-free (again by Corollary

Consequently,

is trivial and that

∎

In view of the above result we introduce:

Definition 6.2
.

Let
G
be an infinite
{\mathcal{K}}
-definable group. The

, denoted

, is the subgroup generated by the
\nu_{D}
, as

ranges over all four distinguished sorts.

6.1.
The rank equality and EI

Our main theorem can be restated as showing that
\nu(G)
is type-definable and definably isomorphic to the direct product of the
\nu_{D}
. The following is an immediate consequence:

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}
be an expansion of a valued field of characteristic
0
that is either (i)
V
-minimal, (ii) power bounded
T
-convex or (iii)
p
-adically closed and let
G
be a definable group. For
{\mathcal{D}}=\{K,\Gamma,K/{\mathcal{O}},\textbf{k}\}
, we have
1\leq\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(\nu(G))=\sum_{D\in{\mathcal{D}}}\text{aD-rk}(G)\leq\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(G).

In light of the above proposition, it is natural to ask to what extent the group
\nu(G)
“exhausts” the group
G
, namely whether
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(\nu(G))=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(G)
(below we call it “the rank equality”). There are few examples where we know the rank equality to hold: When
G
is dp-minimal this follows from the mere fact that
\nu(G)
is infinite. When
G
is definably semisimple (i.e., has no infinite definable commutative normal subgroups) the rank equality follows from
[
6
, Theorem 10.3(1,2)]
.

Below we show that the rank equality fails in general, even for
K
-pure groups (i.e., where
\nu(G)=\nu_{K}(G)
). Classifying those cases where the rank equality holds is, in view of the above examples, an intriguing problem in its own right. It seems to have a relation, though yet to be fully understood, with elimination of imaginaries in
{\mathcal{K}}
.

For a more detailed discussion we need Gagelman’s extension (
[
4
]
) of the
\acl_{K}
-dimension to
{\mathcal{K}}^{eq}
. See also
[
14
, §2]
for a quick overview of the definition, and main properties. In those terms
[
1
, §4.1]
introduces the notion of a valued field
{\mathcal{K}}
eliminating imaginaries down to 0-dimensional sorts. This means that for any 0-definable equivalence relation
E
on
K^{n}
(any
n
) there is a
0
-definable injection
f:K^{n}/E\to K^{m}\times S
for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and some
0
-dimensional sort
S
. The main result of
[
9
]
asserts that the theory of pure algebraically closed fields eliminates imaginaries down to the so called, geometric sorts, that are readily checked to be
0
-dimensional. Similar results exist for the theories of real closed valued fields (
[
16
]
) and
p
-adically closed fields (
[
12
]
) in the language of valued rings.

In the setting of the present paper, if
{\mathcal{K}}
has elimination of imaginaries down to
0
-dimensional sorts, and
G
is a pure
K
-group then the rank equality holds. Indeed, by elimination of imaginaries down to 0-dimensional sorts, for such a group
G
there exists a definable finite-to-one map from
G
into some
K^{n}
(
[
1
, Lemma 4.1]
), So, for
D=K
, we have
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(G)=\mbox{aD-rk}(G)
and since
\mbox{aD-rk}(G)=\mbox{D-rk}(G)
it follows that
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(\nu(G))=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(\nu_{K}(G))=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(G)
.

In general, however, the rank equality need not hold even for a pure
K
-group, as the following example shows:

Example 6.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}
be a model of an analytic expansion of ACVF
0,0
(in the sense of
[
15
]
, see also
[
10
, §1]
for a quick overview). Let
\exp:\textbf{m}\to 1+\textbf{m}
be the exponential map (definable in this structure) and let
E
be its graph. If
\widehat{G}=({\mathcal{O}},+)\times({\mathcal{O}}^{\times},\cdot)
then
E
is a subgroup. We define
G=\widehat{G}/E
, and let
\pi:\widehat{G}\to G
be the quotient map.
Restricting the residue map
\mathrm{res}
to
{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}
, it is a group homomorphism from
{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}
onto
\textbf{k}^{\times}
with kernel
1+\textbf{m}
. Since
E\subseteq\textbf{m}\times(1+\textbf{m})
, the map
\mathrm{res}:\widehat{G}\to\textbf{k}\times\textbf{k}^{\times}
factors through
\pi
, and in particular,
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(G)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(\textbf{k}\times\textbf{k}^{\times})=2
. Let
f:G\to\textbf{k}\times\textbf{k}^{\times}
be the corresponding homomorphism.

Our goal is to prove that the
K
-rank of
G
is
1
, but
G
contains no infinite definable set of dimension
0
, so in particular, for all distinguished sorts
D\neq K
,
\mbox{D-rk}(G)=0
.

Because
\dim(E)=1
, it follows immediately from the definition of

that for a generic
(a,b)\in\widehat{G}
, we have
\dim(\pi(a,b)/\emptyset)=1
and thus for every definable

, if
\dim(Y)=2
then
\dim(\pi(Y))=1
. In particular,
\dim(G)=1
, and we can see directly that the
K
-rank of

is
1
, because the restriction of
\pi
to either
\{0\}\times{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}
or
{\mathcal{O}}\times\{1\}
is injective.

Let us now show:

Claim 6.4.1
.

There is no definable infinite
X\subseteq G
with
\dim(X)=0
.

Proof.

Assume otherwise, and let
X
witness it.
By the above discussion,
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X)=1
(indeed, otherwise its preimage in
\widehat{G}
has dimension
2
, implying that
\dim(X)=1
).

We claim that for
\widehat{X}=\pi^{-1}(X)
, the projections
\pi_{i}(\widehat{X})
,

, into
{\mathcal{O}}
and
{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}
, respectively, contain infinitely many cosets of
m
and
1+\textbf{m}
, respectively. Indeed,
\widehat{X}
consists of infinitely many cosets of

, and since

is surjective, if
\pi_{2}(\hat{X})
intersects only finitely many cosets of
1+\textbf{m}
then for some generic

the fiber

must be infinite. It follows from additivity of

that

. Similarly, if
\pi_{1}(\widehat{X})
consists of finitely many cosets of
m
then
\dim(\widehat{X})=2
. In either case we obtain a contradiction to
\dim(\widehat{X})=1
.

We now proceed with the proof of the claim and note that
\dim(X\times X)=0

and by additivity of
\dim
it follows that
\dim(XX^{-1})=0
, so we may assume that
X
is symmetric. Similarly, for every

(where

\bigvee
-definable subgroup of

.

We claim that

(and hence

and

\widehat{X}
intersects infinitely many cosets of
\textbf{m}\times(1+\textbf{m})
. Therefore,

is infinite and therefore

is an infinite

-definable subgroup of
\textbf{k}\times\textbf{k}^{\times}
. However,

is a pure algebraically closed field, hence (e.g. by Zilber Indecomposability Theorem),

A non-trivial connected algebraic subgroup of

\textbf{k}\times\textbf{k}^{\times}

or
\textbf{k}\times\{1\}
. Since

, only the last two are possible.

Thus, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that either
f(X^{(n)})=\{0\}\times\textbf{k}^{*}
or

. In the first case
\pi^{-1}(X^{(n)})=\textbf{m}\times{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}
and in the second case
\pi^{-1}(X^{(n)})={\mathcal{O}}\times(1+\textbf{m})
. In both cases, we get a contradiction because, just like

above, the projections of

onto both coordinates must contain infinitely many cosets of

and

, respectively.

Therefore, there are no infinite definable subsets of

of dimension

.
∎

It follows from the claim that for all

,
\mbox{D-rk}(G)=0
. Thus,

is

-pure and

, so
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(\nu(G))=1<\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(G)=2
.

In
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
it is shown, using an example similar to the above, that the field
{\mathcal{K}}
of the previous example does not have elimination of imaginaries down to the geometric sorts of
[

]
. In fact, as noted in
[

, the example of Haskell, Hrushovski and Macpherson shows that

does not have elimination of imaginaries down to 0-dimensional sorts. Below we give a quick alternative proof of this result, based on the machinery studied in the present paper. As we will see, the proof is an almost immediate corollary of the above example:

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}
be a model of an analytic expansion of ACVF
0,0
. Then
{\mathcal{K}}
does not have elimination of imaginaries down to 0-dimensional sorts.

Proof.

Assume towards a contradiction that
{\mathcal{K}}
eliminates imaginaries down to
0
-dimensional sorts. This means that for
G
the group of Example
6.4
, there exists a definable injection
f:G\to K^{n}\times S
for some
0
-dimensional sort

. Identifying

with

, we may assume that

.

Let
\pi_{1}:G\to K^{n}
be the projection. If for some

the fibre

is infinite then the set

is infinite and

), contradicting Claim

.

Therefore, the function
\pi_{1}:G\to K^{n}
is finite-to-one. But this too is impossible, as we have seen that

, and thus also
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(\pi_{1}(G))=2

it follows that

∎

7.
Infinitesimals of subgroups

The aim of the present section is to show that if

is a relatively definable subgroup of
\nu(G)
, namely the intersection of a definable set with
\nu(G)
.
We first prove this result for each distinguished sort separately.

7.1.
Infinitesimals of lower dp-rank

We consider an unstable distinguished sort
D
and a generic

A

A

\nu_{D^{n}}(a)

\nu_{Y}(a)

\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(Y)=n

\nu_{D^{n}}(a)=\nu_{Y}(a)
. We repeatedly use Fact
4.4
(4).

Below, for a partial type
\mu

for the collection
\{X\cap Y:X\in\mu\}
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
D
be an unstable distinguished sort,

an infinite definable subset,

\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(c/[Y])=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(Y)
and
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(c/\emptyset)=n

(1)

If
D\neq(K/{\mathcal{O}})_{\text{\tiny{p-adic}}}
then ,
\nu_{Y}(c)\equiv\nu_{D^{n}}(c)\cap Y
.

(2)

If
D=(K/{\mathcal{O}})_{\text{\tiny{p-adic}}}
then there exists a generic vicinity
Y^{\prime}
of

in

\nu_{Y}(c)\equiv\nu_{D^{n}}(c)\cap Y^{\prime}

We break the proof into cases.

Case 1:

By Fact

(see also
[

\nu_{D^{n}}(c)

and
\nu_{Y}(c)
is logically equivalent to
\{V\subseteq Y:\text{$V\ni c$ is relatively definable open in $Y$}\}

Since the topology on

Case 2:

{\mathcal{K}}
is

By

\nu_{D^{n}}(c)\equiv\{U:U\subseteq D^{n}\text{ is a $\Gamma$-box around $c$}\}
.
It is not hard to see that
\nu_{Y}(c)\vdash\nu_{D^{n}}(c)\cap Y

\Gamma

is generic in

and

(see also
[
8
, proof of Lemma 4.2]
). For the converse, it is enough to prove the following:

Claim 7.1.1
.

For every
Z\in\nu_{Y}(c)
there is a
\Gamma

We use induction on

. Let

.
Since
\nu_{Y}(c)
is a filter-base (Fact
4.4
, by shrinking

\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(c/A[Z][Y])=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(Y)

If

(this covers the

\Gamma

Since

be the projection on the first

\pi^{-1}(c^{\prime})\cap Y
is finite,

For simplicity of presentation assume that

Lemma 7.8

Let

Suppose

For the other direction, if

then

∎

8.
Infinitesimals of products

Continuing our investigation of the functorial properties of the mapping sending a definable group
G
to its groups of infinitesimals, we prove that the mapping
G\mapsto\nu_{D}(G)
respects direct products. Our first goal is to prove additivity of the almost
D
-ranks. Namely. that for every definable
X_{1},X_{2}
,
\mbox{aD-rk}(X_{1}\times X_{2})=\mbox{aD-rk}(X_{1})+\mbox{aD-rk}(X_{2}).
This result was claimed in
[
8
, Remark 4.19(2)]
for any vicinic sort, but the proof had a gap. Here we provide a proof for
D
, a distinguished sort in our settings.

For
Y\subseteq X_{1}\times X_{2}
, and
t\in X_{1}
, as usual, we write
Y_{t}=\{y\in X_{2}:(t,y)\in Y\}
. We make use of the fact that
Y_{t}
is in definable bijection with the set
\{t\}\times Y_{t}\subseteq Y
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a first order structure and
D
a definable set of finite algebraic dp-rank. Assume that
X\subseteq T\times D^{n}
is
A
-definable, such that
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X_{t})\leq d
for all
t\in T
. Then there exists an
A
-definable partition
X=\coprod_{i=1}^{\ell}X_{i}

and for each
i
there are
m_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
and a coordinate projection
\pi_{i}:D^{n}\to D^{d}
,
such that for each
t\in T
,
\pi\upharpoonright X_{i,t}
is at most
m_{i}
-to-one.

Proof.

This is a standard compactness argument. Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be the collection of all formulas
\varphi(t,x)
over
A
with
(t,x)\in T\times D^{n}
, such that there is
m\in\mathbb{N}
and a coordinate projection
\pi:D^{n}\to D^{d}
, so that for every
b\in T
the restriction of
\pi
to
\varphi(b,D^{n})
is
m
-to-1. We consider the partial type

\Sigma(t,x):=\{(t,x)\in X\}\cup\{\neg\varphi(t,x):\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}\},

and claim it is inconsistent.

Indeed, assume towards a contradiction that
(t_{0},a)
realizes
\Sigma
. As
a\in X_{t_{0}}
,
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a/At_{0})\leq d
, and since dp-rank is algebraic, there is
a_{1}\subseteq a
of length
d

such that
a\subseteq\acl(At_{0}a_{1})
. Let
\pi_{1}:D^{n}\to D^{d}
be the projection onto the
a_{1}
-coordinates of
a
and let
a_{0}
be the image of
a
under the projection
\pi_{0}
onto the remaining coordinates. Let
\psi(t_{0},x_{0},x_{1})\in\tp(a_{0},a_{1}/At_{0})
be such that
\models(\exists^{=m}x_{0})\psi(t_{0},x_{0},a_{1})
for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let

\varphi(t,x):=\psi(t,\pi_{0}(x),\pi_{1}(x))\,\wedge\,\exists^{=m}y\,\psi(t,\pi_{1}(x),y).

Thus,
\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
\models\varphi(t_{0},a)
, contradiction.

So
\Sigma
is inconsistent, and by compactness
we obtain finitely many definable sets
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{\ell}\subseteq X
, whose union covers
X
, and for each
i
, a coordinate projection
\pi_{i}:D^{n}\to D^{d}
whose restriction to each
Z_{i,t}
is finite-to-one.
Finally, to obtain a partition of
X
we replace
Z_{2}
by
Z_{2}\setminus Z_{1}
,
Z_{3}
by
Z_{3}\setminus(Z_{1}\cup Z_{2})
etc.
∎

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
and
D
be as in the previous lemma, and
X\subseteq T\times D^{n}
. Assume that
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(\bigcup_{t\in T}X_{t})=k
and for each
t\in T
,
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X_{t})\leq d
.

Then, there exists a definable subset
Z\subseteq X
, with
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(\bigcup_{t}Z_{t})=k
, and a definable

with
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(S)\geq k-d
, such that
for every
t\in T
,
|S\cap Z_{t}|
is finite.

Proof.

Assume everything is definable over some
A
.
By Lemma
8.1
, there is a definable partition
X:=\coprod_{i=1}^{\ell}Z_{i}
, and for each
i=1,\ldots,\ell
, a coordinate projection
\pi_{i}:D^{n}\to D^{d}
such that for every
t\in T
, the fibers of
\pi_{i}\upharpoonright Z_{i.t}
have size at most
m_{i}
.

Let
a\in\bigcup_{t}X_{t}
be
A
-generic, and fix
1\leq i_{0}\leq\ell
and
t_{0}\in T
,
such that
a\in Z_{i_{0},t_{0}}
. Let

. Since
a\in Y_{1}

\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(Y_{1})=k.

Let
a_{0}=\pi_{i_{0}}(a)\in D^{d}

and let

. As
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a/A)=k

∎

Remark 8.3
.

In the above lemma consider the special case when the

are pairwise disjoint. In this situation, the set

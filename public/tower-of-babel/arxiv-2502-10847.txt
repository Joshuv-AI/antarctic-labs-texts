Conformality Thresholds in Neutron Stars
Michał Marczenko

Abstract
The dense-matter equation of state (EOS) under neutron-star conditions is investigated. We focus on the qualitative behavior of the speed of sound and its average. We characterize and compare the theoretical criteria for identifying nearly conformal matter. We find that the consistency of these criteria in determining the onset of conformal matter is dictated by the combined state-of-the-art theoretical and astrophysical constraints.

Introduction

Central densities of compact stellar objects exceed the nuclear saturation density (
n_{\rm sat}\simeq 0.16~\rm fm^{-3}
). The heaviest NSs have masses exceeding
2~M_{\odot}

(
Romani et al. 2022
)
, with densities that reach
6-9~n_{\rm sat}
at their centers
(
Marczenko et al. 2024
)
. Such objects serve as excellent and unique extraterrestrial laboratories of extremely dense matter. The modern observatories for measuring the masses, radii, and tidal deformabilities of compact objects, the gravitational wave interferometers of the LIGO/Virgo Collaboration (LVC)
(
Abbott et al. 2018
;
Abbott et al. 2019
)
and the X-ray observatory Neutron star Interior Composition Explorer (NICER) provide new strong constraints on the mass-radius profile of neutron stars
(
Riley et al. 2019
;
Miller et al. 2019
;
Miller et al. 2021
;
Riley et al. 2021
)
. These stringent constraints unleash the potential of NSs as probes of extremely dense matter, allowing for a more systematic study of the influence of the formation of different degrees of freedom within the cores of NSs. Modeling compact stellar objects requires knowledge of the equation of state (EOS) of quantum chromodynamics (QCD). Major progress in constraining the EOS has recently been made by systematic analyses of recent astrophysical observations of the massive pulsar PSRJ0740+6620
(
Cromartie et al. 2019
;
Fonseca et al. 2021
;
Miller et al. 2021
;
Riley et al. 2021
)
and PSR J0030+0451
(
Miller et al. 2019
)
, and the constraint from the recent GW170817 event
(
Abbott et al. 2018
)
, within parametric models of the EoS (see, e.g.,
(
Alford et al. 2013
;
Alford and Sedrakian 2017
;
Annala et al. 2018
;
Annala et al. 2020
)
).

The speed of sound provides valuable information about the microscopic description of matter. At low temperature, the low-density EOS (
n_{B}\lesssim 2~n_{\rm sat}
) is reliably provided by chiral effective field theory
(
Tews et al. 2018
;
Hebeler et al. 2013
;
Drischler et al. 2019
;
Drischler et al. 2021
;
Drischler et al. 2020a
;
Keller et al. 2023
;
Drischler et al. 2020b
)
where
c_{s}^{2}
is found to be below the conformal value of
1/3
. Asymptotically,
c_{s}^{2}
approaches the conformal limit as dictated by the perturbative QCD (pQCD) calculations
(
Gorda et al. 2023
;
Gorda et al. 2021
;
Gorda and Säppi 2022
;
Kurkela and Vuorinen 2016
;
Fraga et al. 2014
;
Kurkela et al. 2010
;
Gorda et al. 2018
)
. However, the density range found in the NSs is not accessible by any of these methods. Knowledge of the EOS between these two extreme limits comes from inferential analyses of astrophysical observables
(
Ecker and Rezzolla 2022a
;
Altiparmak et al. 2022
;
Takatsy et al. 2023
;
Annala et al. 2018
;
Annala et al. 2020
;
Brandes et al. 2023b
;
Brandes et al. 2023a
;
Marczenko et al. 2023
;
Marczenko et al. 2024
)
and effective-model analyses
(
Liu et al. 2024
;
Buballa 2005
;
McLerran and Reddy 2019
;
Kovács et al. 2022
)
. Several suggestions show that the speed of sound exhibits a local maximum at densities realized in the interiors of NSs (see, e.g.,
Fujimoto et al. 2020
;
Ecker and Rezzolla 2022b
;
Tews et al. 2018
). The structure of the speed of sound remains only hypothetical
(
Brandes et al. 2023b
;
Brandes et al. 2023a
)
.

The restoration of conformal symmetry inside neutron stars was a subject of recent interest
(
Fujimoto et al. 2022
)
proposed the trace anomaly as a signature of conformality.
Annala et al. 2023
proposed another quantity, conformal distance, to delineate the onset of nearly-conformal matter.
Marczenko et al. 2024
argued that the onset of conformal matter can be identified with the vanishing curvature of the energy per particle. Recently, it was shown that the ratio of the central pressure to the central energy density can be interpreted as the average speed of sound inside a star (or the average stiffness of the EOS up to the central energy density of a neutron star)
(
Saes and Mendes 2022
;
Saes et al. 2024
)
and connected to the trace anomaly and its derivative
(
Marczenko 2024
)
. In this work, we represent the conditions for the onset of nearly conformal matter discussed in the literature in terms of the speed of sound and its average value. We demonstrate that they are equivalent under the state-of-the-art theoretical and astrophysical constraints on the equation of state.

This work is organized as follows. In Sec.
2
, we introduce thermodynamic quantities related to the speed of sound and conformal symmetry. In Sec.
3
, we discuss the differences between various measures of conformality. Sec.
4
concludes our findings.

2
Conformality in neutron stars

At zero temperature, the speed of sound is defined as

c_{s}^{2}=\frac{\mathrm{d}p}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}\textrm{,}

(1)

which is bounded by
0\leq c_{s}^{2}\leq 1
due to causality and thermodynamic stability. Assuming that the neutron-star equation of state is smooth and that the pressure is zero at vanishing energy density, one may calculate the average speed of sound
(
Saes and Mendes 2022
;
Saes et al. 2024
;
Marczenko 2024
)
:

\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\limits_{0}^{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}\epsilon\;c_{s}^{2}=\frac{p}{\epsilon}\textrm{,}

(2)

which is bounded to
0\leq\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\leq 1
due to causality and thermodynamic stability. The ratio of the pressure to energy density is related to the energy-density-normalized trace anomaly
(
Fujimoto et al. 2022
)
:

\Delta=\frac{1}{3}-\frac{p}{\epsilon}\textrm{.}

(3)

At low density,
\Delta\simeq 1/3
, and it approaches zero as density increases. The trace anomaly was proposed as a signature of conformality in neutron stars
(
Fujimoto et al. 2022
)
. The trace anomaly can be expressed in terms of the average speed of sound using Eq. (
2
),

\Delta=\frac{1}{3}-\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\textrm{.}

(4)

Thus, the trace anomaly measures the deviation of the average value of the speed of sound from its conformal value of
1/3
. The relation of the trace anomaly to the speed of sound was recently established
(
Fujimoto et al. 2022
)
:

c_{s}^{2}=\frac{1}{3}-\Delta-\epsilon\frac{\mathrm{d}\Delta}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}

(5)

Inserting Eq. (
4
) into Eq. (
5
) one gets

\epsilon\frac{\mathrm{d}\Delta}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}=\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle-c_{s}^{2}\textrm{,}

(6)

which expresses the rate of change of the trace anomaly in terms of
c_{s}^{2}
and
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle
. That is, if
c_{s}^{2}>\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle
(
c_{s}^{2}<\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle
), the trace anomaly
\Delta
decreases (increases).

Another thermodynamic quantity related to the EOS is the polytropic index

\gamma=\frac{\ln p}{\ln\epsilon}=\frac{\epsilon}{p}c_{s}^{2}=\frac{c_{s}^{2}}{\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle}\textrm{,}

(7)

where Eqs. (
4
) and (
6
) were used in the last equality. Typically, at low density,
\gamma\gtrsim 2
, which is typical for a class of relativistic mean-field hadronic EOSs with repulsive vector interactions
(
Walecka 1974
;
Serot and Walecka 1986
;
Serot and Walecka 1997
)
.
Annala et al. 2020
adopted the criterion
\gamma=1.75
as a signature for the onset of the conformally symmetric phase.

Annala et al. 2023
proposed a new quantity to measure the restoration of conformal symmetry,

d_{c}=\sqrt{\Delta^{2}+\left(\epsilon\frac{\mathrm{d}\Delta}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}\right)^{2}}=\sqrt{\left(\frac{1}{3}-c_{s}^{2}\right)^{2}+\left(c_{s}^{2}-\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\right)^{2}}\textrm{,}

(8)

where the last equality holds due to Eqs. (
4
) and (
6
). At low density
d_{c}\simeq 1/3
and
d_{c}\rightarrow 0
as the density increases. The criterion
d_{c}=0.2
was adopted as a delimitation line for nearly conformal matter.

Another interesting observable is the curvature of the energy per particle,
\epsilon/n

(
Marczenko et al. 2024
)
. The normalized curvature is given by

\beta=\frac{n}{\mu^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\epsilon/n}{\mathrm{d}n^{2}}=c_{s}^{2}-2\frac{1/3-\Delta}{4/3-\Delta}=c_{s}^{2}-2\frac{\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle}{1+\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle}\textrm{,}

(9)

where Eq. (
4
) was used in the last equality. At low density, the energy per particle is a convex function. In the conformal regime, at high density, it is a concave function. The vanishing curvature
\beta=0
was proposed to discriminate between the conformally broken and near-conformal regimes. We note that the threshold densities determined by the criteria discussed above (
\gamma=1.75
and
d_{c}=0.2
) were found to be consistent with the vanishing curvature of the energy per particle
(
Marczenko et al. 2024
)
.

3
Equation of State in
c_{s}^{2}-\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle
plane

The criteria introduced in Sec.
2
can be expressed in terms of the speed of sound,
c_{s}^{2}
, and its average value,
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle
. Therefore, they can be represented in the
c_{s}^{2}-\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle
plane. This is depicted in Fig.
1
. The average speed of sound is a linear function of the polytropic index (cf. Eq. (
7
)). Thus, the
\gamma=1.75
criterion is a simple straight line. On the other hand, the curvature of the energy per particle,
\beta=0
, is a more complicated function, and the conformal distance,
d_{c}=0.2
, forms an ellipse centered around
c_{s}^{2}=\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle=1/3
. Interestingly, the threshold
\beta=0
overlaps with
\gamma=1.75
for small values of
c_{s}^{2}
. This holds because
\beta=0
when
\gamma=2/(1+\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle)

(
Marczenko et al. 2024
)
. Thus,
\gamma\in\langle 1;2\rangle
. For
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\simeq 0
,
\gamma
is close, but less than
2
. This confirms that the value of the polytropic index is close to
\gamma\simeq 2
at the vanishing curvature of the energy per particle.

Marczenko et al. 2024

c_{s}^{2}
and
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle
, i.e., low to intermediate densities. This is the effect of the constraints imposed on the EOSs. They cause a rapid increase in the speed of sound at intermediate densities
\epsilon\sim 3-5\epsilon_{\rm sat}
, while the average speed of sound increases slowly. However, the PDF becomes broader because of the lack of constraints at higher densities. To illustrate the behavior of the PDF better, we consider the averaged EOS. It crosses the thresholds after it develops a maximum
c_{s}^{2}\approx 0.6
. Notably, the thresholds lie in close vicinity of each other.

In Fig.
2
, we show the conformal distance,
d_{c}
, at fixed values of
\gamma=1.75
and
\beta=0
as functions of the speed of sound. At vanishing
c_{s}^{2}
,
d_{c}=1/3
. At small
c_{s}^{2}
, they overlap (see earlier discussion). They both develop minima around
c_{s}^{2}\approx 0.4-0.5
with
d_{c}\approx 0.2
. The values of
d_{c}
for which the averaged EOS crosses the thresholds are marked by circles. We find that they are close to the demarcation line
d_{c}=0.2
. This further reaffirms that under the influence of state-of-the-art multi-messenger constraints, the criteria
\gamma=1.75
,
\beta=0
, and
d_{c}=0.2
are equivalent. Therefore, the changeover to the nearly conformal regime is identified with the inflection point of the energy per particle, and thus, its vanishing curvature.

Lastly, we show a selection of microscopic hadronic EoSs from the
CompOSE 2008
database in Fig.
3
. We have discarded EoSs that do not fulfill the
M_{\rm max}\geq 2.17~M_{\odot}
constraint. The hadronic EoSs are qualitatively in agreement with each other at low densities. We find that most hadronic EoSs do not show conformal behavior. We note that few of the hadronic EoSs that feature
\gamma<1.75
,
\beta<0
also show
d_{c}<0.2
, which is consistent with the results shown in
Annala et al. 2023
.

4
Conclusion

In this work, we investigated the cold and dense equation of state under neutron-star conditions. We analyzed observables proposed in the literature to gauge the restoration of conformal symmetry in neutron stars. In particular, we considered the polytropic index
(
Annala et al. 2020
)
, curvature of the energy per particle
(
Marczenko et al. 2024
)
, and conformal distance
(
Annala et al. 2023
)
. We demonstrated that they admit representation in terms of the speed of sound
c_{s}^{2}
and its average value
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle
. This allowed us to represent them in the
c_{s}^{2}-\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle
plane.

We find that the state-of-the-art multi-messenger constraints determine the path of the equation of state in the
c_{s}^{2}-\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle
space at low to intermediate densities. Consequently, the EOS crosses all studied conformality thresholds close to each other. Our findings confirm that the changeover to the nearly conformal regime is identified with the inflection point of the energy per particle, and thus, its vanishing curvature. Given the state-of-the-art multi-messenger constraints, we conclude that the vanishing of the curvature of the energy per particle is a sufficient condition for the delineation of the nearly conformal regime at high densities.

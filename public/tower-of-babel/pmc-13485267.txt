The Prognostic and Diagnostic Role of Atrial Strain in Cardiomyopathies: A Literature Review
Authors: Beatrice Dal Passo, Renè Tezze, Federica Frascaro, Sofia Meossi, Carmen Izzo, Laura Rotondo, Giorgio Sciaramenti, Edoardo Menzato, Elisabetta Tonet, Federico Marchini, Matteo Serenelli, Anna Cantone, Gianluca Campo, Rita Pavasini
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Atrial strain assessment, both by speckle-tracking echocardiography and feature tracking cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR), represents a useful tool in all the spectrum of cardiomyopathies. The most robust evidence available in the literature concerns cardiomyopathies with a hypertrophic phenotype, in which left atrial strain (LAS) has demonstrated to be able to differentiate each phenotype, with cardiac amyloidosis (CA) having the lowest LAS values compared to hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM), Fabry cardiomyopathy (FD), and hypertensive heart disease. LAS is also able to predict the occurrence of atrial arrhythmias, in particular atrial fibrillation in CA and HCM, and major adverse cardiac event (MACE) in all the phenotypes. Interestingly, both in HCM and FD, improvement in LAS values has been detected during disease-modifying therapy. In cardiomyopathies with a dilated phenotype as well, LAS, which has been more extensively evaluated through CMR compared to other cardiomyopathies, appears to have prognostic value with respect to left ventricular reverse remodeling and the occurrence of MACE and can help to differentiate dilated cardiomyopathy from ischemic heart disease with left ventricular dilatation. Finally, in arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy, right atrial strain has also demonstrated prognostic power, particularly for the occurrence of both atrial and ventricular arrhythmic events. Interestingly, impaired values of right atrial strain have been detected even in the absence of dilatation of the right atrium, suggesting an earlier involvement by fibro-fatty replacement and fibrosis.

INTRODUCTION

Cardiac strain describes the ability of myocardial cells to shorten and thicken in response to fiber deformation.[1] Atrial deformation follows three cyclic phases: the reservoir phase, from mitral valve closure to opening, marked by positive strain; the conduit phase, from mitral valve opening to atrial contraction, characterized by passive emptying and negative strain; and the contraction phase, corresponding to atrial systole[12] [Figure 1].

Echocardiography, particularly speckle-tracking (STE), is the primary method for assessing atrial strain, though it is limited by image quality requirements, high frame rates, and observer variability.[2]

Cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR), the gold standard for evaluating cardiac morphology and function, also shows promise for strain analysis, but its limited availability restricts its use mainly to research.[3]

Given the strong diagnostic and prognostic value of atrial strain across cardiovascular diseases, this review aims to summarize current evidence on right and left atrial strain (LAS) in various cardiomyopathies (hypertrophic, dilated, and nondilated/nonhypertrophic) assessed by both echocardiography and CMR.

METHODOLOGICAL CONSIDERATION

We performed extensive literature research using mainly PubMed as database and looking for the following terms: ((atrial strain) AND ((hypertrophic cardiopathy)) AND ((hypertrophic cardiomyopathy) OR (HCM)) OR (amyloidosis) OR (Fabry)); ((atrial strain) AND (dilated cardiomyopathy [DCM])); ((atrial strain) AND ((right ventricular [RV] arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy) OR (arrhythmogenic RV cardiomyopathy [ARVC]))). Only manuscripts published in English and in peer-reviewed journals were selected. We included only studies with data regarding: (i) atrial function assessed by atrial strain; (ii) via echocardiography or CMR in cardiomyopathies, (iii) with relation to both left and right atrial function to clinical events. Abstracts, editorials, gray literature, and case reports were excluded. A literature search and screening of the literature was performed by three independent reviewers (B. D. P, R. T, and F. F.). Divergences were solved by discussion and consensus. In the case of unresolved disagreement, another reviewer (R. P.) tried to reach a consensus.

HYPERTROPHIC PHENOTYPE

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy

HCM is an autosomal dominant disease caused by mutations in sarcomere protein genes, leading to myocardial hypertrophy and fibrosis, typically of the LV septum.[4] Most forms progress to diastolic dysfunction due to elevated filling pressures, mitral regurgitation, ischemia, and autonomic imbalance, whereas the risk of malignant arrhythmias and sudden cardiac death (SCD) remains a major concern.[45]

LAS has emerged as a useful marker for predicting new-onset atrial fibrillation (AF), the most common arrhythmia in HCM.[678] AF arises from atrial fibrosis and elevated LA pressures secondary to diastolic dysfunction and mitral regurgitation, and is associated with higher rates of heart failure (HF), stroke, and mortality.[91011] LA mechanical dysfunction correlates not only with AF onset but also with increased need for cardioversion, ablation, and AF-related hospitalizations.[11]

Studies using STE or feature tracking CMR consistently show reduced LA reservoir strain (LASr) in HCM patients who develop AF.[12] Data on LA conduit strain (LAScd) are less consistent,[1314] though generally lower in those with AF.[14] A recent meta-analysis including 30 studies confirmed LASr, LAScd, and LASct as predictors of AF in HCM.[15]

Abnormal LAS, measured by either STE or feature-tracking CMR, has also been associated with major adverse cardiac events (MACEs), including SCD.[161718] Along with data which showed the predictive value of LAScd and LASr among different HF phenotypes considering the occurrence of death or HF hospitalization,[19] in the setting of HCM, most studies report a significant association between reduced LASr and MACE. Conversely, findings from single studies on LAScd and LASct remain inconsistent.[202122] However, the previously cited meta-analysis by Hussain et al. demonstrated a significant correlation between each component of the LAS and MACE in HCM patients,[15] concluding that altered atrial mechanics detected by LAS analysis can contribute to prognostic stratification, helping to identify patients at higher risk not only for development of new onset AF but also of HF and hospitalization.[15]

It has been proposed that LASr is less influenced by loading conditions and therefore may serve as a more reliable marker for predicting both MACE and AF in HCM, though current evidence does not conclusively support its superiority over LASct or LAScd.[15]

Considering the advent of targeted therapies for HCM, such as the cardiac myosin inhibitor mavacamten,[2324] the VALOR-HCM trial demonstrated improvements in LA volume and LAS at 56-week follow-up, suggesting sustained favorable LA remodeling and enhanced atrial function, except in the AF subgroup.[25] Whether the favorable atrial remodelling observed with long-term mavacamten therapy translates into a meaningful reduction in the burden of atrial tachyarrhythmias in HCM remains to be fully established [Table 1 and Figure 2].

Amyloidosis

Cardiac amyloidosis (CA) is an infiltrative cardiomyopathy resulting from protein misfolding and aggregation into insoluble amyloid fibrils that deposit within the myocardial extracellular matrix. This deposition compromises myocardial compliance, leading to progressive restrictive physiology, conduction abnormalities and HF. The two predominant forms of CA are transthyretin amyloidosis (ATTR) and immunoglobulin light chain amyloidosis (AL). ATTR can be further classified as wild-type, typically associated with aging, or hereditary (hATTR), linked to mutations in the transthyretin gene.

The marked reduction in LA function observed in CA may represent a key parameter for differentiating this condition from other causes of LVH, such as hypertensive heart disease (HHD). This diagnostic challenge was addressed by Rausch et al., who evaluated LA functional parameters as potential discriminators. Their study included 44 patients with ATTR and AL, compared with 25 hypertensive patients with increased LV wall thickness.[26] Despite a similar degree of LVH, all LAS parameters were significantly more impaired in the CA group than in those with HHD, with consistent findings across both etiologic subtypes. Notably, a LASr cutoff of <20% demonstrated 86.4% sensitivity and 88.6% specificity in differentiating CA from HHD.[26] These results are consistent with findings from Monte et al., who compared LA strain among 100 patients with ATTR-CA, sarcomeric HCM, and healthy controls. Using STE, they showed that LA functional impairment was significantly more severe in the ATTR-CA group than in both HCM patients and controls, reinforcing the role of LA strain as a discriminative marker for distinguishing infiltrative from HCM and other causes of increased LV wall thickness.[27]

LA function, assessed via STE, has been shown to be profoundly impaired in CA, encompassing both AL and ATTR subtypes.[28] In a landmark study, Bandera et al. evaluated LA structure and function in a cohort of 906 patients with ATTR-CA using STE, demonstrating significant dysfunction across all three phases of LA activity and documenting an increase in LA stiffness calculated as the ratio between E/e’ and LASr.[29] Elevated LA stiffness was independently associated with adverse clinical outcomes. Furthermore, the study defined atrial electromechanical dissociation (AEMD) in those patients with no LASct despite preserved sinus rhythm (SR), which was present in approximately 20% of the cohort. Those patients were also characterized by the absence or low value (<20 cm/s) of the A wave on Doppler analysis. This subgroup exhibited a markedly worse prognosis compared with patients in SR with preserved atrial mechanical function.[2930]

The prognostic value of LA strain in CA was further explored by Huntjens et al., who followed 136 patients with both ATTR and AL over a median of 5 years.[31] Patients with a baseline peak longitudinal LAS below the median value of 13.2% exhibited a 7.5-fold higher mortality risk during follow-up. Importantly, LAS provided incremental prognostic value beyond that offered by global longitudinal strain (GLS) and conventional echocardiographic or clinical parameters.[31]

Another critical consideration is the elevated thromboembolic risk observed in CA, which occurs independently of AF. In fact, several studies performed both with echocardiography and CMR demonstrated the presence of intracardiac thrombus in patients with SR.[32333435] Increasing evidence suggests that underlying atrial myopathy is a major contributing factor, and the previously cited study by Bandera et al. supports the use of STE to identify those patients with AEMD defined by the absence of LASct.[29] These observations have led to emerging recommendations for prophylactic anticoagulation in CA patients with no AF diagnosis but showing evidence of AEMD;[36] however, only initial evidence can support the use of atrial STE analysis to define the indication for anticoagulation even in the absence of AF [Figure 2].

Fabry disease

Fabry disease (FD) is a rare inherited disorder linked to the X chromosome, caused by mutations in the α-galactosidase A (GLA) gene, which results in a shortage of the GLA enzyme, leading to the gradual buildup of a fatty substance in several organs, including the heart, kidneys, and central nervous system. This accumulation contributes to serious health complications and increased risk of death.[37383940] More than half of individuals with FD experience heart-related issues, mainly consisting of progressive concentric cardiac hypertrophy, along with varying levels of impaired heart function[414243] [Figure 2].

Although the diagnosis of FD is predominantly made by enzyme activity assessment and genetic testing, echocardiography has still an important role in intercepting patients with primarily cardiac manifestation.[4445] In 2017, Pichette et al. provided a detailed analysis of LAS abnormalities in Fabry cardiomyopathy (FD), demonstrating a significant reduction in phasic LAS among 50 FD patients compared to 50 healthy individuals.[46]

Notably, Boyd et al. observed decreased LAS and increased LA volume in FD patients even before the occurrence of LVH and prior to the start of enzyme replacement therapy.[46] Additionally, Pichette et al. reported not only a reduction in LAS in FD patients compared to a healthy population, but also its improvement following enzyme therapy,[47] supporting the notion that a primary atrial myopathy partly driven by sphingolipid accumulation in the atria develops early in the disease and can be at least partially reversed.[4849]

In this setting, the assessment of LAS could represent a potential parameter to further improve the capacity of identifying patients with a suspect of FD as demonstrated by Frumkin et al. (20 patients with FD vs. 20 with other causes of LVH, excluding CA patients) who showed that LAS assessment showed the potential of being a great addition in the echocardiographic work-up of unclear LVH, helping to identify patients with FD and showing a higher diagnostic value compared to LV-GLS and posterolateral strain impairment.[50]

A recent study from Massie et al. was the first to measure and compare LAS among a cohort of patients with LVH, who had received either the diagnosis of HCM, FD, or CA. In those with a confirmed diagnosis of FD, LAS was not associated with the composite clinical outcome of cardiovascular mortality, device implantation, HF hospitalization, new-onset AF or stroke despite HCM and CA, probably consequently to a smaller cohort (24 patients) compared to the other phenotypes. Otherwise, patients with FD were characterized by the highest rate of clinical events during follow-up, compared to other groups.[51]

These findings emphasize the critical need for early detection and risk assessment in this high-risk patient population, in which LAS assessment should be an integral part in the routine echocardiographic examination, not only to guide diagnosis, but also to monitor cardiac function improvement during enzyme therapy.

DILATATED PHENOTYPE

Idiopathic dilated cardiomyopathy

DCM represents a spectrum of diseases characterized by LV dilation and diffuse myocardial remodelling, resulting from either genetic or acquired causes, such as myocarditis, drugs or toxin exposure. These pathological processes lead to interstitial fibrosis and chamber enlargement in the absence of obstructive coronary artery disease. Elevated LV filling pressures, reduced ventricular compliance and neurohormonal activation contribute to progressive atrial remodeling, which is marked by fibrosis, increased stiffness and impaired contractility.[52]

Myocardial fibrosis, reduced LV ejection fraction (LVEF), and LV reverse remodelling (LVRR) have traditionally received the most attention as clinical predictors in DCM.[52] However, increasing evidence is emerging regarding the prognostic relevance of atrial function. Several studies have shown that atrial strain, particularly LAScd, predicts MACE better than traditional LV contractility indices.[53] Reduced LAScd generally reflects elevated LV end-diastolic pressure due to impaired relaxation and increased stiffness, making it an indirect marker of diastolic dysfunction and raised filling pressures. This provides insight into early hemodynamic changes before symptoms or structural abnormalities appear.

The development of HF therapies has significantly improved cardiac function in patients with DCM, and in some cases, favoring LVRR. A recent study demonstrated that the most important determinants of LVRR, and thus of improved outcomes, include not only LV function but, notably, LA function as well.[53] Accordingly, a recent investigation evaluated the role of LAS in predicting LVRR in DCM patients and found that LAScd, along with New York Heart Association (NYHA) class, late gadolinium enhancement (LGE), and indexed left ventricular end-diastolic volume (LVEDVi) were identified as independent predictors of LVRR.[54]

Raafs et al. conducted an analysis of LAS in 488 patients who underwent CMR for either DCM or HCM. During a follow-up of 6 years, 15% of the population experienced the primary composite outcome of SCD, HF, hospitalization, and life-threatening arrhythmias. The study revealed that LAScd, myocardial fibrosis, and NYHA class > 2 were independent predictors for the primary outcome.[55] Notably, LAScd was superior to LV GLS, LVEF, left atrial volume index (LAVi), and incremental to LGE in predicting the primary endpoint.[55] Interestingly, the study demonstrated that while LAScd correlates more strongly with 1-year prognosis, LGE shows a better association with long-term (~3–4 years) outcomes.[55] The same conclusions were obtained by Li et al. in a CMR study of 497 patients with DCM, reinforcing the prognostic significance of LACd in this setting.[56]

In addition to the role of LAScd, LASct is crucial for maintaining cardiac output and regulating pulmonary capillary wedge pressure. In a recent study, Hammersley et al. showed that LASct correlates not only with cardiovascular events such as HF but also with SCD.[57] This evidence corroborates a previous study by Negishi et al., which have demonstrated the relationship between atrial function and the onset of arrhythmias in DCM patients with implantable cardioverter-defibrillators (ICDs), showing that left atrial booster measured by transmitral A wave is an independent predictor of arrhythmic events, outperforming both GLS and mechanical dispersion in risk stratification.[58]

Raff et al. investigated the relationship between LAS and new-onset AF in patients with DCM. In their study involving 425 patients, LA strain, particularly the LASct, was identified as the most sensitive parameter for predicting the risk of AF, outperforming traditional measures such as LAVi.[59] The loss of atrial contractile contribution during systole can reduce cardiac output by up to 25%, resulting in elevated atrial pressures and subsequent worsening of HF. These findings highlight the critical role of atrial mechanical dysfunction in the pathogenesis of AF and its impact on hemodynamic compromise in DCM patients.

Similarly, Alesina et al. analyzed by echocardiography a large population of non-ischaemic DCM in SR and EF < 50%, demonstrating that LA dysfunction is the best determinant of hospitalization for HF, cardiovascular death, or ventricular arrhythmias. Reduced LASr and LAScd are associated with impaired outcome, but only LAScd ≤ 8 % was associated with a > 2.7-fold increased risk of MACE and offered incremental prognostic value when accounting for LV-GLS, probably due to lesser dependency on mitral annular motion. At the same time, a decreased LASr/LV-GLS ratio was associated with higher rates of outcome[54] [Table 2 and Figure 3].

Left atrial strain in primary dilated cardiomyopathy

Ischemic heart disease with LV dilatation

Several studies compared echocardiographic and CMR parameters between DCM and ischemic heart disease with LV dilatation (IDM), demonstrating a reduction in atrial strain values compared with healthy subjects, with more pronounced impairment observed in patients with primary DCM.[6061]

The study by Bo et al. investigated the prognostic role of LAS assessed by CMR in these populations, all with HF with reduced ejection fraction and in SR. The results confirmed that LAS, but not the LAVi, was a strong, independent predictor of cardiovascular death and HF hospitalization.[62]

In contrast, the study by Cao et al. focused on the comparative assessment of atrial function in patients with DCM and IDM, rather than on its prognostic implications. They found that, while standard echocardiographic parameters (e.g., LA size, mitral regurgitant orifice area, and E/e’ ratio) did not differ significantly between the two groups, atrial strain values were consistently lower in the DCM population.[60] Interestingly, maximum LA volume was found to be higher in IDM.[61636465]

In a larger cohort of 90 patients, D’Andrea et al. showed that DCM patients had significantly worse LASr than those with IDM across all wall segments. A significant improvement in LASct was observed only in IDM patients who responded to cardiac resynchronization therapy (CRT). Ischemic etiology and CRT response were the only independent predictors of LAS improvement in multivariate models.[65]

This discrepancy likely reflects distinct pathophysiological mechanisms of atrial involvement in DCM, including fibrotic remodelling of the left atrium not typically seen in IDM. In addition, cellular mechanisms involving sarcoplasmic reticulum dysfunction and impaired calcium handling may further contribute to atrial mechanical dysfunction.[60]

NON-DILATED, NONHYPERTROPHIC PHENOTYPE

Arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy

ARVC is a genetic cardiomyopathy characterized by fibro-fatty replacement of the RV myocardium, which leads to cardiac remodeling and impaired contractile function. LV and biatrial involvement have also been reported.[6667] ARVC is associated with adverse cardiovascular events, primarily ventricular arrhythmias and SCD. An increased risk of developing atrial arrhythmias and HF in later disease stages has also been described.[67]

In an initial CMR study, Zghaib et al. demonstrated that reduced values of right atrial reservoir strain (RASr), right atrial contraction strain (RARct), and right atrial conduit strain (RARcd), together with reduced LASr and LASct, predicted the occurrence of atrial arrhythmias, particularly AF.[68] These findings were further supported by subsequent studies using both echocardiography and CMR, which focused particularly on right atrial function. Among these parameters, RASr and RARct have consistently been shown to be associated with an increased risk of developing atrial arrhythmias, HF, and ventricular arrhythmias[676970] [Figure 4].

Right atrial strain in arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy

Interestingly, Anwer et al. demonstrated that RASr values were reduced even in patients with normal right atrial volumes, reinforcing the hypothesis that fibro-fatty replacement and fibrosis also affect the right atrial myocardium, thereby leading to atrial dysfunction.[69]

Finally, Zheng et al., in the largest study to date, including 105 subjects, showed that RASr and RARct were strong, independent predictors of adverse cardiac events, including SCD, aborted cardiac arrest, and appropriate implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD) interventions. Moreover, both RASr and RARct provided incremental prognostic information in ARVC beyond RV GLS and LV involvement, serving as valuable additional parameters for comprehensive risk assessment in ARVC[66] [Table 3].

Limitations of atrial strain assessment in cardiomyopathies

Although atrial function assessed by atrial strain can provide valuable clinical insights, its application in routine clinical practice remains limited. This is mainly due to intervendor variability both for STE and CMR, and the absence of universally accepted cut-off values, which reduces the comparability of results between populations and centres.[71] For a long time, echocardiographic atrial strain measurements were performed using software originally developed for ventricular strain analysis. The introduction of atrial-dedicated software platforms will certainly represent the basis for the development of standardized methodologies and, subsequently, for the definition of reference values that can be reliably applied in clinical practice.[72]

CONCLUSIONS

Atrial strain analysis, assessed both with STE and feature tracking CMR, represents a useful tool in the setting of cardiomyopathy [Central Illustration]. Available literature proposes its application for early and differential diagnosis, for predicting the occurrence of arrhythmias, particularly AF,[71] and their complications, and for prognostic stratification among all the spectrum of cardiomyopathies. Therefore, the systematic application of atrial strain assessment in the clinical practice may support the clinician in the management and prognostic stratification of patients with cardiomyopathies.

Schematic overview illustrating the diagnostic and prognostic implications of left atrial strain (LAS) in different cardiomyopathies. Reduced LAS identifies worse atrial function in dilated cardiomyopathy compared with ischemic heart disease with left ventricular dilatation, predicts adverse outcomes in arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy, and provides prognostic information in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, cardiac amyloidosis, and Fabry disease. Notably, cardiac amyloidosis is characterized by the lowest LAS values among left ventricular hypertrophy phenotypes, supporting its role in differential diagnosis, risk stratification, and therapeutic response assessment

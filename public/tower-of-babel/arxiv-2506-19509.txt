The linearizability of singular foliations is a Morita invariant
Marco Zambon

Abstract
. Hausdorff Morita equivalence is an equivalence relation on singular foliations, which induces a bijection between their leaves. Our main statement is that linearizability along a leaf is invariant under Hausdorff Morita equivalence. The proof relies on a characterization of tubular neighborhood embeddings using Euler-like vector fields.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 53C12, 53D17

Keywords: Singular foliation, Morita equivalence, linearization, Euler-like vector field

Introduction

This note is concerned with
singular foliations
, understood as a module of vector fields as in
[
1
]
, following the work of Stefan and Sussman.
A singular foliation
\mathcal{F}
on a manifold
M
gives rise to a decomposition of
M
into immersed submanifolds tangent to
\mathcal{F}
, called leaves.
For instance, any action of a Lie group
G
on a manifold
M
gives rise to such a singular foliation, namely the
C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
-span of the vector fields generating the action. The underlying decomposition of
M
is by the orbits of the
G
-action, if
G
is connected.

Whenever
L
is an embedded leaf of a singular foliation
\mathcal{F}
, by linearizing the vector fields in
\mathcal{F}
, one obtains a singular foliation
\mathcal{F}_{lin}
on the normal bundle
\nu L
, called linearized foliation. The
linearization
question asks when the singular foliations
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}_{lin}
are isomorphic, in a neighborhood of
L
. When
L
is a point, sufficient criteria for linearization
were given by Cerveau
[
7
]
.

Hausdorff Morita equivalence

[
14
]
is a notion of equivalence for singular foliations, weaker than isomorphism, which among other things preserves the geometry transverse to the leaves, and induces a homeomorphism of leaf spaces. Our main result is that it preserves linearizability:

Theorem

(Thm.
2.1
)
.

Let
(M_{1},\mathcal{F}_{1})
and
(M_{2},\mathcal{F}_{2})
be Hausdorff Morita equivalent singular foliations,
and
L_{1}\subset M_{1}
,
L_{2}\subset M_{2}
corresponding embedded leaves.

Then:
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is linearizable around
L_{1}
iff
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is linearizable around
L_{2}
.

We remark that the dimensions of
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are generally different,
hence this theorem allows to reduce the linearization question to leaves of smaller dimension, which in certain cases are simply points.

By the very definition of Hausdorff Morita equivalence, the theorem follows immediately from the following proposition:

Proposition

(Prop.
2.2
)
.

Let
\pi\colon P\to M
be a surjective submersions with connected fibers. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular foliation on
M
and
L\subset M
an embedded leaf.

Then:

\mathcal{F}
is linearizable around
L
iff the pullback foliation
\pi^{-1}\mathcal{F}
is linearizable around
\pi^{-1}(L)
.

The main tool we use to prove this result is the correspondence between
tubular neighborhood embeddings

and
Euler-like vector fields

[
6
]
[
15
]
[
5
]
. The precise relation between linearization and Euler-like vector fields is given in Proposition
1.20
, which extends a result of
[
7
, §8]
for points.
With this tool at hand, the direct implication in the proposition is easy to prove, by lifting Euler-like vector fields. The converse implication requires more work, since a given Euler-like vector fields is not necessarily
\pi
-projectable.

In
the last section of the paper
we present examples. Further, using the above theorem, we obtain a condition under which the linearizability of a singular foliation
\mathcal{F}
along a leaf is implied by the linearizability of a simpler foliation, namely the restriction of
\mathcal{F}
to a slice.

Corollary

(Cor.
3.15
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular foliation arising from a Hausdorff, source connected, Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}
.
Let
L
be an embedded leaf,
N
a slice. Suppose the restricted Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{N}:=\mathbf{t}^{-1}(N)\cap\mathbf{s}^{-1}(N)
is source connected.

Then, whenever
(N,\iota_{N}^{-1}\mathcal{F})
is linearizable, the singular foliation
\mathcal{F}
is also linearizable.

1.
Singular foliations and their linearization

We present some facts about singular foliations, their linearization, and their Hausdorff Morita equivalence. We also recall a theorem about tubular neighborhood embeddings.

1.1.
Singular foliations

We recall the notion of singular foliation from
[
1
]
, see also the comprehensive monograph
[
16
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A
singular foliation
on a manifold
M
is a
C^{\infty}(M)
-submodule
\mathcal{F}

of the compactly supported vector fields

\mathfrak{X}_{c}(M)
, involutive w.r.t the Lie bracket and locally finitely generated.
A
foliated manifold
is a manifold with a singular foliation.

Remark 1.2
.

For any open set
U\subset M
,
consider the following modules:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}|_{U}:=\iota_{U}^{-1}\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle:=\{X|_{U}:X\in\mathcal{F}\text{ and }\text{supp}(X)\subset U\},

\displaystyle\widehat{\iota_{U}^{-1}\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle:=\{X\in\mathfrak{X}(U):fX\in\iota_{U}^{-1}\mathcal{F}\ \text{ for all }f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)\}.

Notice that if
Z\in\mathcal{F}
, its restriction
Z|_{U}
is not compactly supported in general, and
Z|_{U}\in\widehat{\iota^{-1}_{U}\mathcal{F}}
.
We say that
\mathcal{F}
is
locally finitely generated
if for every point of
M
there is an open neighborhood
U
and finitely many
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\in\widehat{\iota_{U}^{-1}\mathcal{F}}

such that
\iota^{-1}_{U}\mathcal{F}=Span_{C^{\infty}_{c}(U)}\{Y_{i}\}
.
In that case,
\widehat{\iota^{-1}_{U}\mathcal{F}}=Span_{C^{\infty}(U)}\{Y_{i}\}
, see
[
4
, Ex. 3 iii)]
. When
U=M
, we obtain the global hull of
\mathcal{F}
:

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}:=\{X\in\mathfrak{X}(M):fX\in\mathcal{F}\ \text{ for all }f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)\}.

Remark 1.3
.

A singular foliation on a manifold
M
can be equivalently regarded as an involutive, locally finitely generated subsheaf of
the sheaf of
C^{\infty}
-modules on
M
given by the smooth vector fields. We will not use this point of view in this paper.

By the Stefan-Sussmann theorem, a singular foliation induces a partition of the manifold into immersed submanifolds, called
leaves
. For results on the structure of singular foliations nearby a given leaf, see

[
17
]

[
11
]

[
10
]
.

Example 1.4
.

Any Lie algebroid
A
induces a singular foliation on its base manifold
M
, namely
\rho(\Gamma_{c}(A))
, where
\rho\colon A\to TM
denotes the anchor map.
In particular, any Lie groupoid over
M
induces a singular foliation (via its Lie algebroid).

Definition 1.5
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F})
be a foliated manifold and
\pi\colon P\to M
a submersion.
The
pullback foliation

\pi^{-1}\mathcal{F}

[
1
, Proposition 1.10]

is the the
C^{\infty}_{c}(P)
-span of the set of projectable vector fields on
P
which project to elements of
\mathcal{F}
.

In
[
1
, Prop. 1.10]

the pullback foliation is defined for any map transverse to
\mathcal{F}
. We will need the case of the inclusion
\iota_{S}\colon S\to M
of a submanifold transverse to the foliation: in that case,
\iota_{S}^{-1}\mathcal{F}=Span_{C^{\infty}_{c}(S)}\{X|_{S}:X\in\mathcal{F}\text{ is tangent to $S$}\}
.

We now consider flows. In
[
1
, Prop. 1.6]
(see also
[
13
, Prop 2.3]
) it is shown that for any singular foliation
\mathcal{F}
and
X\in\mathcal{F}
, the time-1 flow of
X
preserves
\mathcal{F}
. We need an extension of that result to infinitesimal symmetries, i.e. vector fields
X
satisfying
[X,\mathcal{F}]\subset\mathcal{F}
. For compactly supported
X
this appears in
[
12
, Prop. 2.1.3]
, and in the form presented here it appears in
[
16
, Prop. 1.7.10]
(see also
[
18
, Prop. 1.3]
).

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F})
be a foliated manifold and
X
a vector field on
M
such that
[X,\mathcal{F}]\subset\mathcal{F}
. Then the time-
t
flow
\phi_{t}
of
X
, where it exists, satisfies
(\phi_{t})_{*}\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{F}
.

We will need a technical lemma.

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F})
be a foliated manifold,
\{X_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
a smooth family of vector fields lying in
\mathcal{F}
. Assume that there exists a cover
\{U_{\alpha}\}
of
M
by open subsets so that on each
U_{\alpha}
the singular foliation
\mathcal{F}
admits a finite number of generators
Y_{1}^{\alpha},\dots,Y^{\alpha}_{k_{\alpha}}\in\mathfrak{X}(U_{\alpha})

with the following property:

X_{t}|_{U_{\alpha}}=\sum_{i}f_{i}^{t,\alpha}Y_{i}^{\alpha},

where the coefficients
f_{i}^{t,\alpha}\in C^{\infty}(U_{\alpha})
depend smoothly on
t
. Then the vector field

X:=\int_{0}^{1}X_{t}\,dt

lies in the global hull
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
.

Proof.

Take a cover
\{U_{\alpha}\}
, generators
Y_{i}^{\alpha}
and functions
f_{i}^{t,\alpha}
as above.
Since
(\int_{0}^{1}f_{i}^{t,\alpha}dt)\in C^{\infty}(U_{\alpha})
, we can write

X|_{U_{\alpha}}=\sum_{i}(\int_{0}^{1}f_{i}^{t,\alpha}dt)Y_{i}^{\alpha}
, which lies in
\widehat{\iota^{-1}_{U_{\alpha}}\mathcal{F}}
by Remark
1.2
.

Fix a partition of unity
\{\rho_{\alpha}\}
subordinate to the above cover of
M
.
Take
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
. Notice that
\{\alpha:supp(\rho_{\alpha})\cap supp(f)\neq\emptyset\}
is a finite set, as a consequence of the partition of unity being locally finite (see
[
4
, Proof of Prop. 1]
).
We have
fX=\sum_{\alpha}f\rho_{\alpha}X
. This is a finite sum, and each summand lies in
\mathcal{F}
because we showed that
X|_{U_{\alpha}}\in\widehat{\iota^{-1}_{U_{\alpha}}\mathcal{F}}
and
f\rho_{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
has support in
U_{\alpha}
. Hence
fX\in\mathcal{F}
. As this holds for all
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
, we conclude that
X\in\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
.
∎

Remark 1.8
.

The statement of Lemma
1.7
no longer holds if we omit the assumption on the smooth dependence of the
f_{i}^{t,\alpha}
on
t
.

We now look at how the flow of an element of
\mathcal{F}
acts on an infinitesimal symmetry of
\mathcal{F}
.

Proposition 1.9
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F})
be a foliated manifold,
Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
such that
[Y,\mathcal{F}]\subset\mathcal{F}
.
Fix
X\in\mathcal{F}
, denote its time-1 flow by
\phi^{X}_{1}
. Then

(\phi^{X}_{1})_{*}Y-Y\in\widehat{\mathcal{F}}.

Proof.

Notice that

(\phi^{X}_{1})_{*}{Y}-{Y}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{dt}(\phi^{X}_{t})_{*}{Y}\,dt;

we would like to apply Lemma
1.7
to conclude that
lies in
\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}}
.

To apply Lemma
1.7
, notice that for all
t\in[0,1]
the integrand satisfies

\frac{d}{dt}(\phi^{X}_{t})_{*}{Y}=(\phi^{X}_{t})_{*}[Y,X]

(1)

by a standard computation (see for instance
[
13
, Proof of Prop. 2.3]
).
This lies in
{\mathcal{F}}
, since
[{Y},X]\in{\mathcal{F}}
by assumption and since

\phi^{X}_{t}
is an automorphism of
(M,{\mathcal{F}})
by
[
1
, Prop. 1.6]
.

We now check that the smooth family of vector fields (
1
) satisfies
the assumptions of Lemma
1.7
.
Since
supp(X)
is compact,
\mathcal{F}
is finitely generated on an open subset
U
of
M
containing
supp(X)
, as shown by the argument at the beginning of
[
13
, Proof of Prop. 2.3]
. We denote a set of generators by
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k}\in\mathfrak{X}(U)
.

Since
[Y,X]\in\mathcal{F}
is supported on
U
, we can write
[Y,X]|_{U}=\sum_{i}g_{i}Y_{i}
for

g_{i}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)
. It is shown in
[
13
, Proof of Prop. 2.3]
that
(\phi^{X}_{t})_{*}Y_{i}=\sum_{j}f_{j}^{i}(t)Y_{j}
for some

f_{j}^{i}(t)\in C^{\infty}(U)
depending smoothly on
t
.
Hence on
U
the r.h.s. of (
1
) equals

\sum_{i}(g_{i}\circ\phi^{X}_{-t})\;(\phi^{X}_{t})_{*}Y_{i}=\sum_{i,j}(g_{i}\circ\phi^{X}_{-t})f_{j}^{i}(t)Y_{j}.

As the coefficients

(g_{i}\circ\phi^{X}_{-t})f_{j}^{i}(t)
depend smoothly on
t
, and as the integrand (
1
) vanishes on the
M\setminus Supp(X)
, the assumptions of Lemma
1.7
are satisfied.
∎

1.2.
The linearized foliation

We recall the linearization of a singular foliation along an (embedded) leaf, following
[
3
, §4.1, §4.3]
.

Consider a singular foliation
(M,\mathcal{F})
and an embedded leaf
L
. Denote by
\nu L:=TM|_{L}/TL
the normal bundle of
L
.
Recall that there is a canonical identification

I_{L}/I^{2}_{L}\cong\Gamma(\nu^{*}L)=C^{\infty}_{lin}(\nu L),\;\;[f]\mapsto df|_{L},

where
I_{L}

denotes the functions on
M
that vanish on
L
,
C^{\infty}_{lin}(\nu L)
the fiberwise linear functions on the normal bundle, and

[f]:=(f\text{ mod }I^{2}_{L})
for
f\in I_{L}
.

Every vector field
Y
on
M
tangent to
L
gives rise to a fiberwise linear vector field
Y_{lin}
on
\nu L
, which acts as follows on the fiberwise constant functions and on
C^{\infty}_{lin}(\nu L)\cong I_{L}/I^{2}_{L}
:

\displaystyle Y_{lin}(g)

\displaystyle:=(Y|_{L})(g)\text{ for all }g\in C^{\infty}(L)

\displaystyle Y_{lin}[f]

\displaystyle:=[Y(f)]\text{ for all }f\in I_{L}.

Here, given a function on
L
, abusing notation we denote its pullback to
\nu L
with the same letter.

We denote by
\chi_{lin}(\nu L)
the Lie algebra of fiberwise linear vector fields on
\nu L
, i.e. those which preserve the fiberwise constant functions and the fiberwise linear functions.
The above yields a linear map

lin\colon\mathcal{F}\to\chi_{lin}(\nu L),\;\;Y\mapsto Y_{lin}

which preserves brackets.
Notice that
lin
factors through a map of
C^{\infty}(L)
-modules

\overline{lin}:\mathcal{F}/I_{L}\mathcal{F}\to\chi_{lin}(\nu L).

(2)

Definition 1.10
.

The
linearization of
\mathcal{F}
along
L
is the singular foliation
\mathcal{F}_{lin}
on
\nu L

generated by the image of
lin
, i.e. the
C^{\infty}_{c}(\nu L)
-span of
\{Y_{lin}:Y\in\mathcal{F}\}
.

Definition 1.11
.

We say that
\mathcal{F}
is linearizable
about
L
if
there exists neighborhoods
V\subset\nu L
of the zero section and
U\subset M
of
L
, and a tubular neighborhood embedding (see Def.
1.14
)

V\to U

inducing an isomorphism between
the singular foliations
\mathcal{F}_{lin}|_{V}
and
\mathcal{F}|_{U}
.

Not all singular foliations are linearizable. For instance, the singular foliation
\mathcal{F}
on the real line generated by
x^{2}\partial_{x}
is not linearizable along the leaf
L=\{0\}
: the linearized foliation
\mathcal{F}_{lin}
is the zero foliation, for which each point is a leaf.

Remark 1.12
.

We have
\mathcal{F}/I_{L}\mathcal{F}=\Gamma_{c}(A_{L})
for a transitive Lie algebroid
A_{L}
over
L
(see
[
2
, §1.3]
), whose anchor we will denote
\rho_{A_{L}}
.
Similarly,
\chi_{lin}(\nu L)
agrees with the sections of
CDO(\nu L)
, the transitive Lie algebroid whose sections are the covariant differential operators on the vector bundle
\nu L
, and whose anchor
\sigma
is given by the symbol map. Hence the map
\overline{lin}
in (
2
) is the map of sections induced by a morphism of transitive Lie algebroids

\lambda\colon A_{L}\to CDO(\nu L)

covering
Id_{L}
. Any such morphism has constant rank by
[
19
, Theorem 6.5.3]
. Consequently,
\{Y_{lin}:Y\in\mathcal{F}\}=\Gamma_{c}(E)
for a wide Lie subalgebroid
E\subset CDO(\nu L)
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F})
be a foliated manifold and
p\in M
.
Consider

\mathfrak{g}_{p}:=\{X\in\mathcal{F}:X(p)=0\}/I_{p}\mathcal{F},

where

I_{p}
denotes the ideal of smooth functions on
M
vanishing at
p
.
Then
\mathfrak{g}_{p}
is a Lie algebra, called
isotropy Lie algebra
, with Lie bracket induced by the one of vector fields. It is the isotropy Lie algebra
\ker(\rho_{A_{L}})_{p}
at
p
of the Lie algebroid
A_{L}
, where
L
denotes the leaf of
\mathcal{F}
through
p
.

Remark 1.13
.

The restriction of the map
\lambda

to fibers over
p
is a Lie algebra morphism

\lambda_{p}\colon\mathfrak{g}_{p}\to\mathfrak{X}_{lin}(\nu_{p}L)=End(\nu_{p}L),\;\;Y\text{ mod }I_{p}\mathcal{F}\mapsto Y_{lin}|_{\nu_{p}L}.

(3)

This map is not injective in general, as can be seen from the example where
M=\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{F}
is generated by
x^{2}\partial_{x}
, and
p=0
.

1.3.
Interlude: Tubular neighborhood embeddings

Putting aside singular foliations for a moment, here we recall some material on tubular neighborhood embeddings, following
[

, §1]

Let
N
be a (embedded) submanifold of a manifold

\nu N=TM|_{N}/TN
its normal bundle.

Definition 1.14
.

i) A
tubular neighborhood embedding
is an embedding
\psi\colon V\to M
, where
V
is an open neighborhood of the zero section in
\nu N
, such that
\psi|_{N}=Id_{N}
and the induced map on normal bundles is
Id_{\nu N}
.

ii) A vector field
1
1

1

We do not require
X
to be complete, just as in
[
15
]
. This is in contrast with

]
, where the vector field is assumed to be complete, and tubular neighborhood embeddings are defined on the whole of

.

X
on

is
Euler-like for

if
X|_{N}=0
and

is the Euler vector field

of
\nu N
.

Remark 1.15
.

The Euler vector field
E
of
\nu N

is fiberwise linear, and is the unique vector field satisfying the following properties:

E(g)=0\text{ for all }g\in C^{\infty}(N)
,

E[f]=[f]\text{ for all }f\in I_{N}
. From this, one sees that the Euler vector field commutes with all fiberwise linear vector fields.

Clearly if
\psi\colon V\to M
is a tubular neighborhood embedding, then pushing forward the Euler vector field of

one obtains a Euler-like vector field for

, defined on
\psi(V)
.

The converse is also true (see
[

, Prop. 2.6]
). We paraphrase it in the version given in
[

Theorem 1.16
.

Let

be a Euler-like vector field for
N
. There exists a neighborhood
V
of the zero section of
\nu N
and a tubular neighborhood embedding
\psi\colon V\to M
such that the Euler vector field is mapped to

.

Remark 1.17
.

Given
X
, the germ of such a tubular neighborhood embedding is unique.

A geometric proof of this results is given in

[

]
, see also
[

, §2.2, §2.3]
. We review a few key-points following the latter reference.

•

Given the submanifold
N
of
M
, the associated deformation space
is the set

\mathcal{D}(M,N):=\nu N\sqcup(M\times\mathbb{R}^{\times}),

where
\mathbb{R}^{\times}=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
. This set is endowed with a certain manifold structure.

•

Every function
f\in I_{N}
vanishing on
N
induces a smooth function
\widetilde{f}
on the deformation space
\mathcal{D}(M,N)
, which agrees with
(df)|_{N}
on
\nu N
and with

on

, where

is the standard coordinate on
\mathbb{R}
.

•

Every
Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
tangent to
N
induces a vector field
\mathcal{D}(Y)
on the deformation space, which reads
Y_{lin}
on
\nu N
and
Y\times 0
on
M\times\mathbb{R}^{\times}
.

•

An Euler-like vector field
X
gives rise not only to
\mathcal{D}(X)
but also to
another vector field
W
on the deformation space
\mathcal{D}(M,N)
, which reads
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\frac{1}{t}X
on

.

If we assume that the Euler-like vector field

is complete, then

is complete.
The time

\phi_{W}^{1}
of

\nu N
to

, and the resulting map is a tubular neighborhood embedding. It maps the Euler vector field to

, since one can check that
[W,\mathcal{D}(X)]=0
.

If
X
is not complete, the construction is more involved
[
15

. On open subsets
C
of
\nu N
with compact closure, it might be described as follows. On
C
, the flow
\phi_{W}^{s}
is defined for some

. On
C
, the tubular neighborhood embedding reads
v\mapsto\phi_{W}^{s}(s^{-1}v)
.

Remark 1.18

(Functoriality)
.

We recall some facts about Hausdorff

Definition 1.22

Two foliated manifolds

When two foliated manifolds are Hausdorff

\mathcal{F}_{ell}
is linearizable around
L
.
Indeed, by the Morse-Bott Lemma as in
[
21
, §4.2]
, there is a
tubular neighborhood embedding that identifies
f
in a neighborhood of
L\subset M
with the normal Hessian of
f
(viewed as a degree
2
polynomial) in a neighborhood of
L\subset\nu L
. The elliptic foliation associated to this polynomial is linear. This can be seen trivializing
\nu L
by means of a local orthonormal frame, and using the fact that the elliptic foliation on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
associated to the function
x^{2}+y^{2}
is generated by the Euler vector field and the rotation vector field, see e.g.
[
22
, §1]
.

Remark 3.4
.

The following simple remark is used in the next example.
Let
V\to L
be a vector bundle and
\mathcal{F}_{V}
a singular foliation on
V
generated by linear vector fields.
Let
G
be a discrete group acting freely and properly on the vector bundle
V\to L
by vector bundle automorphisms, preserving the singular foliation
\mathcal{F}_{V}
.
Then
V/G
is a vector bundle over
L/G
, and the projection
\pi\colon V\to V/G
is a vector bundle morphism and a covering map. The singular foliation
\mathcal{F}_{V}
induces a singular foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
V/G
, which is also generated by linear vector fields.

Example 3.5

(Mapping tori)
.

Consider a linear singular foliation
\mathcal{F}_{S}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(i.e. one induced by the action of a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{R})
), the full foliation on
\mathbb{R}
, and their product foliation
\mathcal{F}_{V}
on
V=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
. For any
\phi\in GL(n,\mathbb{R})
preserving
\mathcal{F}_{S}
, the group
\mathbb{Z}
acts on
V
, with
1\in\mathbb{Z}
acting by
(p,t)\mapsto(\phi(p),t+1)
. On the mapping torus

V/\mathbb{Z}=(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,1])/\sim\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{ where }(p,0)\sim(\phi(p),1)

we obtain a foliation
\mathcal{F}
, again generated by linear vector fields. Here are some low-dimensional examples.

i) If
\mathcal{F}_{S}
is the foliation by points of
\mathbb{R}
, and
\phi=-Id
, we obtain the Möbius strip
V/\mathbb{Z}
with a foliation by circles. Most circles wind twice around the Möbius strip, while the “middle circle” winds only once and has holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

ii) If
\mathcal{F}_{S}
is the foliation generated by
y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
on
\mathbb{R}
, on the Möbius strip, we obtain a foliation with precisely two leaves: a circle, and a 2-dimensional open leaf.

iii) If
\mathcal{F}_{S}
is the foliation generated by the rotation vector field
x\frac{\partial}{\partial y}-y\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and
\phi(x,y)=(x,-y)
on
V/\mathbb{Z}
, we obtain a singular foliation whose leaves are a circle and many copies of the Klein bottle.

Remark 3.6
.

Notice that in both in i) and ii) above, the foliation on the Möbius strip and the foliation restricted to a slice
\mathbb{R}
are not Hausdorff Morita equivalent. For instance, in ii),
\mathcal{F}
has two leaves but the restricted foliation has three.

3.2.
Restrictions of singular foliations to slices

Let
(M,\mathcal{F})
be a foliated manifold,
L
an embedded leaf.

Definition 3.7
.

A
slice
at a point
p\in L
is a submanifold
N
of
M
such that
N\cap L=\{p\}
, we have a direct sum
T_{p}N\oplus T_{p}L=T_{p}M
, and
N
is transverse to the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
it meets.

In that case,
\mathcal{F}|_{N}:=\iota^{-1}_{N}\mathcal{F}
is a singular foliation on
N
, with the point
\{p\}
being a leaf. If we take another slice
N^{\prime}
through
p
, upon shrinking the slices we obtain isomorphic foliated manifolds
[
3
, Appendix A.2]
.

Lemma 3.8
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is linearizable along
L
, then
\mathcal{F}|_{N}
is linearizable at
p
.

Proof.

Take a tubular neighborhood embedding
\phi\colon V\to M
linearizing
\mathcal{F}
, where
V
is open in
\nu L
. Restrict
\phi
to a tubular neighborhood embedding from
(V_{p},\mathcal{F}_{lin}|_{V_{p}})
to
(N^{\prime},\mathcal{F}|_{N^{\prime}})
, where
V_{p}:=V\cap\nu_{p}L
and
N^{\prime}:=\phi(V_{p})
. Then use
(\mathcal{F}|_{N^{\prime}})_{lin}=\mathcal{F}_{lin}|_{V_{p}}
, which holds by the splitting theorem for singular foliations
[
7
, Thm. 1.1]
[
1
, §1.3]
.
∎

The converse of Lemma
3.8
does not hold. That is, given
a singular foliation
\mathcal{F}
and a slice
N
to an embedded leaf
L
at
p
:
if
\mathcal{F}|_{N}
is linearizable at
p
, then in general
it does not follow that
\mathcal{F}
is linearizable along
L
.
This is well-known and already apparent in the case of regular foliations, as we recall in this example.

Example 3.9
.

On the cylinder
S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
with coordinates
\theta
and
y
, consider the (involutive) distribution
D
spanned by the vector field
\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+g(y)\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
,
where
g\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function which is identically zero for
y\leq 0
and which is
>0
for
y>0
. It gives rise to a foliation
\mathcal{F}=\Gamma_{c}(D)
, with
L=S^{1}\times\{0\}
as a leaf. Take the slice
N=\{p\}\times\mathbb{R}
through a point
p\in S^{1}
; the restricted foliation is the zero foliation, which is certainly linearizable. But
\mathcal{F}
itself is not linearizable along
L
: the linearized foliation
\mathcal{F}_{lin}
on
\nu L=S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
is spanned by
\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}
, since
g^{\prime}(0)=0
, and thus has no holonomy around the zero section, while
\mathcal{F}
does have holonomy around
L
, since
g
is positive on
\{y>0\}
.

We mention that by a version of the Reeb stability theorem for regular foliations,
if a regular foliation has a (not necessarily compact) embedded leaf
L
with finite holonomy group, then the foliation is linearizable around
L
, see e.g.
[
20
, Thm. 3.2.1]
. If
L
is
compact
, the classical Reeb stability theorem assures that
L
is linearizable by a tubular neighborhood embedding
with image a
saturated
neighborhood of
L
, i.e. one given by a union of leaves.

3.3.
Hausdorff Morita Equivalence and restrictions to slices

We determine a condition under which the converse of Lemma
3.8
holds, using Theorem
2.1
.
This is desirable because in that case the linearizability of
\mathcal{F}
becomes equivalent to the linearizability of its restriction to a slice; for the latter - which vanishes at a point - some linearization criterias are given by Cerveau in
[
7
]
.

In general, in a neighborhood of a leaf, a singular foliation is not
Hausdorff Morita equivalent to its restriction to a slice, as we saw in Remark
3.6
. Below we determine conditions under which they are.

Remark 3.10
.

If in a neighborhood of a leaf
L
, a singular foliation
\mathcal{F}
is
Hausdorff Morita equivalent to its restriction
\iota^{-1}_{N}\mathcal{F}
to a slice
N
, then the holonomy group of
\mathcal{F}
at the point
\{p\}=N\cap L
is
connected
.

Indeed,
\mathcal{F}
and
\iota^{-1}_{N}\mathcal{F}
being Hausdorff Morita equivalent implies that their holonomy groups at
p
are isomorphic as Lie groups
[
14
, Theorem 3.44 i)]
.
The holonomy group of
\iota_{N}^{-1}\mathcal{F}
at
p
is connected because it is the whole source fiber of the holonomy groupoid
[
1
]
of
\iota_{N}^{-1}\mathcal{F}
, since the latter has
\{p\}
as a leaf, and holonomy groupoids are always source-connected.

Recall that given a singular foliation on
M
and a slice
N
, the saturation of
N
is the union of all leaves intersecting
N
; it is an open subset of
M
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular foliation arising (in the sense of §
1.1
) from a Hausdorff, source connected, Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}
.
Let
L
be an embedded leaf,
N
a slice. Suppose the restricted Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{N}:=\mathbf{t}^{-1}(N)\cap\mathbf{s}^{-1}(N)
is source connected.

Then
(N,\iota_{N}^{-1}\mathcal{F})
and
(U,\iota_{U}^{-1}\mathcal{F})
are Hausdorff Morita equivalent singular foliations, where

U
is the saturation of
N
.

Proof.

Notice that
\widetilde{N}:=\mathbf{s}^{-1}(N)
is a smooth Hausdorff manifold. Consider the two maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{t}|_{\widetilde{N}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{s}|_{\widetilde{N}}}

where
U=\mathbf{t}(\widetilde{N})
agrees with the saturation of
N
.

Clearly
\mathbf{s}|_{\widetilde{N}}
is a surjective submersion with connected fibers.

The map
\mathbf{t}|_{\widetilde{N}}\colon\widetilde{N}\to U
is a submersion, as a consequence of the fact that
N
is transverse to the leaves it meets
(see e.g.
[
9
, Ex. 4.2.1, Prop 5.2.4]
).

We show that
\mathbf{t}|_{\widetilde{N}}
has connected fibers. Let
u\in U
. The corresponding fiber of
\mathbf{t}|_{\widetilde{N}}
is
\mathbf{t}^{-1}(u)\cap\widetilde{N}
. There exists
p\in N
lying in the same leaf
as
u
; take any
g\in\mathcal{G}
with
\mathbf{t}(g)=p
and
\mathbf{s}(g)=u
. Then the left composition with
g
gives a diffeomorphism

\mathbf{t}^{-1}(u)\cap\widetilde{N}\to\mathbf{t}^{-1}(p)\cap\widetilde{N}
.
The latter is the target fiber of
\mathcal{G}_{N}
at
p
, so it is connected by assumption.

Finally, we show that

(\mathbf{t}|_{\widetilde{N}})^{-1}\mathcal{F}=(\mathbf{s}|_{\widetilde{N}})^{-1}(\iota_{N}^{-1}\mathcal{F}).

Since
\mathcal{G}
is a Lie groupoid giving rise to
\mathcal{F}
, it is also a bisubmersion
[
1
, Prop. 2.2]
, i.e.
\mathbf{t}^{-1}\mathcal{F}=\mathbf{s}^{-1}\mathcal{F}
.
By the functoriality of pullback we have

(\mathbf{s}|_{\widetilde{N}})^{-1}\iota_{N}^{-1}\mathcal{F}=\iota_{\widetilde{N}}^{-1}(\mathbf{s}^{-1}\mathcal{F})=\iota_{\widetilde{N}}^{-1}(\mathbf{t}^{-1}\mathcal{F})=(\mathbf{t}\circ\iota_{\widetilde{N}})^{-1}\mathcal{F}.

∎

Remark 3.12
.

Proposition
3.11
can be also deduced as follows.
The inclusion
\mathcal{G}_{N}\to\mathcal{G}_{U}
is a weak equivalence of Lie groupoids, yielding that these two Lie groupoids are Morita equivalent. Hence, when both are source connected (and
\mathcal{G}_{U}
automatically is), their singular foliations are Hausdorff Morita equivalent, by
[
14
, Prop. 2.29]
.

Remark 3.13
.

Assume the set-up of Proposition
3.11
. The assumption that
\mathcal{G}_{N}
is source connected is quite strong and has the following consequences.

i) Denote by
p
the unique point such that
N\cap L=\{p\}
.
Then the isotropy group
\mathcal{G}_{p}^{p}
of
\mathcal{G}
at
p
is connected. Indeed, it equals
(\mathcal{G}_{N})_{p}^{p}=\mathbf{t}_{\mathcal{G}_{N}}^{-1}(p)
and
\mathcal{G}_{N}
is source (and target) connected by assumption. It follows that the isotropy groupoid of the holonomy groupoid of
\mathcal{F}
at
p
is connected, since the holonomy groupoid
is a quotient of
\mathcal{G}

[
1
, Ex. 3.4(4)]
. This is consistent with Remark
3.10
.

ii) The intersections of the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
with
N
are connected. Indeed, they agree with the leaves of
\mathcal{G}_{N}
, and the latter is source connected by assumption.

Example 3.14
.

Let

•

V\to M
be a vector bundle,

•

G
be a connected Lie group acting equivariantly on
V
(not necessarily by vector bundle automorphisms) such that the action preserves the zero section
M
and is transitive there.

Denote by
\mathcal{F}
the singular foliation on
V
given by the infinitesimal generators of the action (hence
M
is a leaf).
Let
p\in M
, so the fiber
N:=V_{p}
is a slice of the singular foliation through
p
. If the isotropy group
G_{p}
at
p
is connected, then

(N,\iota_{N}^{-1}\mathcal{F})
and
(V,\mathcal{F})
are Hausdorff Morita equivalent singular foliations.

Indeed, a source connected Lie groupoid giving rise to
\mathcal{F}
is the transformation groupoid
\mathcal{G}=G\ltimes V
. The restricted Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{N}
is
G_{p}\ltimes N
, the transformation groupoid of the
G_{p}
action on
N
; by assumption it is source connected, so we can apply Proposition
3.11
.

A special case (where all the foliations involved are linear) is obtained starting with a Lie group
G
and a connected closed Lie subgroup
H
. The action of
G
on
M:=G/H
lifts to an equivariant linear action of
G
on the tangent bundle of
M
(and on any associated bundle). This example was inspired by
[
10
, Ex. 3.9]
, which corresponds to the case
G=SO(3)
and
H=SO(2)
(so
\mathcal{F}
is a singular foliation on the tangent bundle of
M=S^{2}
, arising from the
SO(3)
action by rotations).

Theorem
2.1
and Proposition
3.11
imply:

Corollary 3.15
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular foliation arising from a Hausdorff, source connected, Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}
.
Let
L
be an embedded leaf,
N
a slice. Suppose the restricted Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{N}:=\mathbf{t}^{-1}(N)\cap\mathbf{s}^{-1}(N)
is source connected.

Then, whenever
(N,\iota_{N}^{-1}\mathcal{F})
is linearizable, the singular foliation
\mathcal{F}
is also linearizable.

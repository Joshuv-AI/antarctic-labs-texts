Graph–Smoothed Bayesian Black-Box Shift Estimator and Its Information Geometry
Masanari Kimura

Abstract
Label shift adaptation aims to recover target class priors when the labelled source distribution P P and the unlabelled target distribution Q Q share P ⁡ ( X ∣ Y ) = Q ⁡ ( X ∣ Y ) P(X\mid Y)=Q(X\mid Y) but P ⁡ ( Y ) ≠ Q ⁡ ( Y ) P(Y)\neq Q(Y) . Classical black - box shift estimators invert an empirical confusion matrix of a frozen classifier, producing a brittle point estimate that ignores sampling noise and similarity among classes. We present Graph - Smoothed Bayesian BBSE (GS - B 3 SE), a fully probabilistic alternative that places Laplacian–Gaussian priors on both target log - priors and confusion - matrix columns, tying them together on a label - similarity graph. The resulting posterior is tractable with HMC or a fast block Newton–CG scheme. We prove identifiability, N − 1 / 2 N^{-1/2} contraction, variance bounds that shrink with the graph’s algebraic connectivity, and robustness to Laplacian misspecification. We also reinterpret GS - B 3 SE through information geometry, showing that it generalizes existing shift estimators.

Introduction

Modern machine–learning systems are rarely deployed in exactly the same
environment in which they were trained.
When the distribution of class labels drifts but the
class–conditional features remain stable, a phenomenon known as

-

Because re

recover the new class priors from a small unlabelled sample are
indispensable precursors to reliable downstream decisions.

A popular remedy, the Black-Box Shift Estimator (BBSE) is to keep a single, frozen classifier

\hat{h}\colon\ \mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
trained on labelled source data
and to link its predictions on the target domain to the unknown

\bm{C}

,

The resulting formulation converts density shift into a linear

(ii) the empirical prediction histogram
\tilde{\bm{q}}

Related Literature

Classical estimators under label shift.

Saerens et al. [47]
proposed an EM algorithm that alternates between estimating the target prior and re
-
weighting posterior probabilities calculated by a fixed classifier.

Lipton et al. [39]
later formalised the
Black
-
Box Shift Estimator
(BBSE), showing that a single inversion of the empirical confusion matrix suffices when
P(X\mid Y)
is preserved.
Subsequent refinements introduced regularisation to cope with ill
-
conditioned inverses: RLLS adds an
\ell_{2}
penalty to the normal equations
[
7
]
, while MLLS frames the problem as a constrained maximum
-
likelihood optimisation
[
18
]
.
All three methods remain point estimators and ignore uncertainty in
\tilde{\bm{C}}
.

Bayesian and uncertainty
-
aware approaches.

Caelen [11]
derived posterior credible intervals for precision and recall by coupling Beta priors with multinomial counts;
Ye et al. [53]
extended the idea to label
-
shift estimation under class imbalance.
Most of these models assume that classes are a priori independent, so posterior variance remains high for rare labels.
Our work instead imposes structured Gaussian Markov random field (GMRF) priors that borrow strength across semantically related classes.

Graph
-
based smoothing and Laplacian priors.

In spatial statistics, Laplacian–Gaussian GMRFs are a standard device for sharing information among neighbouring regions
[
49
]
.
Recent machine learning studies exploit the same idea for discrete label graphs:
Alsmadi et al. [3]
introduced a graph
-
Dirichlet–multinomial model for text classification, and
Ding et al. [15]
used graph convolutions to smooth class logits.
We follow this line but couple both the prior vector and every confusion
-
matrix column to the same similarity graph, yielding a joint posterior amenable to HMC and Newton–CG.

Domain
-
shift benchmarks and failure modes.

Large
-
scale empirical studies such as WILDS
[
30
]
and Mandoline
[
13
]
describe how brittle point estimators become under distribution drift and class imbalance.

Rabanser et al. [45]
demonstrated that label
-
shift detectors without calibrated uncertainty frequently produce over
-
confident but wrong alarms.
By delivering credible intervals whose width shrinks with the graph’s algebraic connectivity, our method directly tackles this shortcoming.

Positioning of this work.

GS
-
B
3
SE unifies three strands of research: (i) black
-
box label
-
shift estimation, (ii) Bayesian confusion
-
matrix modelling, and (iii) graph
-
structured smoothing.
To justify our proposed method, we show posterior identifiability and
N^{-1/2}
contraction, and derive variance bounds that scale with
\lambda_{2}(L)
.
Empirically, our method plugs seamlessly into existing shift
-
benchmark pipelines, providing calibrated uncertainty absent from earlier regularised or EM
-
style alternatives.

3
Preliminarily

Problem Setting

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an input space and
\mathcal{Y}=\{1,\dots,K\}
be a label set, where
K\geq 2
is the number of classes.
For source and target distributions
P
and
Q
, the label shift assumption states that the class–conditional feature laws remain unchanged while the class priors may differ:

\displaystyle P(X\mid Y=i)=Q(X\mid Y=i),\ \forall i\in\mathcal{Y},\quad\text{and}\quad\underbrace{P(Y=i)}_{\eqqcolon p_{i}}\neq\underbrace{Q(Y=i)}_{\eqqcolon q_{i}}\ \text{in general}.

(1)

Let
\bm{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{K})^{\top}
and
\bm{q}=(q_{1},\dots,q_{K})^{\top}
, where
\sum_{i}p_{i}=\sum_{i}q_{i}=1
.

Black-Box Shift Estimator

Train once, on source data, an arbitrary measurable classifier
\hat{h}\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
.
Denote its confusion matrix under
P
by
\bm{C}\in(0,1)^{K\times K}
, where
C_{j,i}=\Pr_{P}\left[\hat{h}(X)=j\mid Y=i\right]
and
\sum_{j}C_{j,i}=1
.
Notice that
\bm{C}
depends only on the source distribution and can be estimated on a held-out validation set with known labels.
Write the empirical estimate as
\tilde{\bm{C}}
.
Let
m
labelled source
-
validation points
{(x_{i},y_{i})}_{i=1}^{m}
be used to form
\tilde{\bm{C}}
.
Apply the same fixed
\hat{h}
to unlabeled target instances
\{\bm{x}_{t}^{\prime}\}^{n^{\prime}}_{t=1}
of size
n^{\prime}
.
Let
\tilde{q}_{j}=\Pr_{Q}\left[\hat{h}(X)=j\right],\quad\tilde{\bm{q}}=(\tilde{q}_{1},\dots,\tilde{q}_{K})^{\top}
.
Because the class-conditionals are shared, Bayes’ rule gives

\displaystyle\Pr_{Q}\left[\hat{h}(X)=j\right]=\sum^{K}_{i=1}\Pr_{Q}\left[\hat{h}(X)=j\mid Y=i\right]\cdot q_{i}=\sum^{K}_{i=1}C_{j,i}q_{i},

(2)

and in vector form, it can be written as
\tilde{\bm{q}}=\bm{C}\bm{q}
.
Eq.
2
is the identifiability equation for label shift.

Assume
\bm{C}
is invertible or full column rank.
Then the population target prior is
\bm{q}=\bm{C}^{-1}\tilde{\bm{q}}
.
Under this observation, BBSE framework
[
39
]
converts black-box classifier predictions on unlabeled target data into an estimate of the unknown target class prior by solving the linear system.

4
Methodology

The usual BBSE treats the confusion matrix
\bm{C}
as fixed.
In practice
\bm{C}
is estimated from a finite validation set and is itself ill-conditioned when some classes are rare.
To address this problem, we consider extending BBSE framework by the joint Bayesian model for both confusion matrix and target priors with the graph coupling.
Let
n^{S}_{i}
be a number of source examples with label
i
, and
\bm{n}^{S}_{i}=(n^{S}_{1,i},\dots,n^{S}_{K,i})^{\top}
be the counts of
\hat{h}(X)=j
among those
n^{S}_{i}
.
Also, let
\tilde{\bm{n}}=(\tilde{n}_{1},\dots,\tilde{n}_{K})^{\top}
be the counts of
\hat{h}(X)=j
on unlabeled target data.
For each true class
i
,

\displaystyle\bm{n}^{S}_{i}\mid\bm{C}\sim\mathrm{Multi}\left(n^{S}_{i},C_{:,i}\right),

where
C_{:,i}
is the
i
-column of
\bm{C}
and
\mathrm{Multi}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the multinomial distribution.
Similarly,

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{n}}\mid\bm{C},\bm{q}\sim\mathrm{Multi}\left(n^{\prime},\bm{C}\bm{q}\right).

The complete-data likelihood factorises

\displaystyle p\left(\text{data}\mid\bm{C},\bm{q}\right)=\left[\prod^{K}_{i=1}\mathrm{Multi}\left(\bm{n}^{S}_{i};n^{S}_{i},C_{:,i}\right)\right]\mathrm{Multi}\left(\tilde{\bm{n}};n^{\prime},\bm{C}\bm{q}\right).

We now consider to utilize similarity information between classes.
Let
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{Y},\bm{E},\bm{W})
be a similarity graph on labels with the weight matrix
\bm{W}
,
\bm{E}
is the edges and
\bm{L}
is the graph Laplacian.
In
\mathcal{G}
, each vertex corresponds to a class label; an edge
(i,j)\in\bm{E}
indicates that labels
i
and
j
are semantically or visually similar.
Weights
W_{ij}\in[0,1]
quantify that similarity, with
W_{ij}=0
when no edge is present.
In our methodology, we use the unnormalised Laplacian
\bm{L}=\bm{D}-\bm{W}
where
D_{ii}=\sum_{j}W_{ij}
.
Because we enforce connectivity,
\bm{L}
has exactly one zero eigenvalue, making
\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})
(the algebraic connectivity) strictly positive as required by our theory.
Introduce log–odds vector

\displaystyle\theta_{i}=\log q_{i}-\frac{1}{K}\sum^{K}_{k=1}\log q_{k},\quad\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{K},\bm{\theta}^{\top}\bm{1}=0,

and consider the following Gaussian Markov random field (GMRF) prior

\displaystyle p(\bm{\theta}\mid\tau_{\bm{q}})\propto\exp\left(-\frac{\tau_{\bm{q}}}{2}\bm{\theta}^{\top}\bm{L}\bm{\theta}\right),\quad\tau_{\bm{q}}\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(a_{q},b_{q}),

(3)

where
a_{q},b_{q}>0
are hyper-parameters.
A Laplacian-based precision shrinks log-odds differences along graph edges, promoting smooth class priors across semantically similar labels
[
31
]
, and recover
\bm{q}=\text{softmax}(\bm{\theta})
.

Treat each column
C_{:,i}
as a latent simplex vector with Dirichlet–log-normal hierarchy:

i)

Latent log-odds
\bm{\phi}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
,
\bm{\phi}_{i}^{\top}\bm{1}=0
.

ii)

Conditional prior
p(\bm{\phi}_{i}\mid\tau_{\bm{C}})\propto\exp\left(-\frac{\tau_{\bm{C}}}{2}\bm{\phi}_{i}^{\top}\bm{L}\bm{\phi}_{i}\right)
.
All
\bm{\phi}_{i}
share the same Laplacian
\bm{L}
over predicted labels so that columns corresponding to neighbouring predicted classes exhibit similar shape.

iii)

Transformation to the simplex
C_{j,i}=\frac{\exp\phi_{j,i}}{\sum^{K}_{\ell=1}\exp\phi_{\ell,i}}
for
i=1,\dots,K
.

iv)

Hyper-prior
\tau_{\bm{C}}\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(a_{C},b_{C})
, with
a_{C},b_{C}>0
.

The resulting distribution on each
C_{:,i}
is a logistic-Normal on the simplex and it reduces to an ordinary Dirichlet when
\bm{L}=0
but gains graph-coupled precision for
\bm{L}\neq 0
.
The full hierarchical model is as follows.

\displaystyle\tau_{\bm{q}}

\displaystyle\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(a_{q},b_{q}),

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}\mid\tau_{\bm{q}}

\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{N}(0,(\tau_{\bm{q}}\bm{L})^{\dagger}),\quad\bm{q}=\text{softmax}(\bm{\theta}),

\displaystyle\tau_{\bm{C}}

\displaystyle\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(a_{C},b_{C}),

\displaystyle\forall i\colon\bm{\phi}_{i}\mid\tau_{\bm{C}}

\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{N}(0,(\tau_{\bm{C}}\bm{L})^{\dagger}),\quad C_{:,i}=\text{softmax}(\bm{\phi}_{i}),

\displaystyle\forall i\colon\bm{n}^{S}_{i}\mid\bm{C}

\displaystyle\sim\mathrm{Multi}(n^{S}_{i},C_{:,i}),

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{n}}\mid\bm{C},\bm{q}

\displaystyle\sim\mathrm{Multi}(n^{\prime},\bm{C}\bm{q}).

Here, the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse
\bm{L}^{\dagger}
appears because
\bm{L}
is singular, and the constraint
\bm{\theta}^{\top}\bm{1}=0
ensures uniqueness.

For the posterior inference, consider the following log-joint distribution.

\displaystyle\log p(\bm{C},\bm{q},\tau_{\bm{C}},\tau_{\bm{q}}\mid\text{data})

\displaystyle=\log p(\text{data}\mid\bm{C},\bm{q})+\log p(\bm{C}\mid\tau_{\bm{C}})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\log p(\tau_{\bm{C}})+\log p(\bm{q}\mid\tau_{\bm{q}})+\log p(\tau_{\bm{q}}).

(4)

All terms are differentiable, enabling Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) in the unconstrained variables.
Because Eq.
4
is concave in each block after reparameterization, a block-Newton scheme alternates, i) update
\{\bm{\phi}_{i}\}^{K}_{i=1}
by one Newton–CG step using sparse Laplacian Hessian, ii) update
\bm{\theta}
likewise, iii) closed-form updates for
\tau_{\bm{C}},\tau_{\bm{q}}
from Gamma posteriors.
Convergence is super-linear due to the strict convexity induced by the Laplacian energies.
The posterior predictive distribution of the confusion–weighted target counts is

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{n}}^{*}\mid\text{data}=\int\mathrm{Multi}(n^{\prime},\bm{C}\bm{q})p(\bm{C},\bm{q}\mid\text{data})d\bm{C}d\bm{q}.

(5)

Credible intervals for each
q_{i}
reflect both sampling noise and model-induced graph smoothing, an advantage over plug-in BBSE.
If no prior similarity information exists one may default to
W_{ij}=\bm{1}\{i=j\}
, in which case our model reduces to an independent logistic-Normal prior and all theoretical guarantees still hold.

Relationship to Existing Work

Theory

This section provides the theoretical foundations of the proposed method.
See Appendix
A
for the detailed proofs.
First, the following lemma on identifiability is introduced.
Although an analogous statement has been implicitly argued in the prior work
[
39
]
, the full proof is included in the Appendix
A
to make this study self-contained.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\bm{C}
and
\bm{C}^{\prime}
be two column-stochastic matrices with strictly positive entries:
C_{j,i}>0
,
C^{\prime}_{j,i}>0
,
\sum^{K}_{j=1}C_{j,i}=\sum^{K}_{j=1}C^{\prime}_{j,i}=1
for
1\leq i\leq K
.
In addition, assume
\bm{C}
and
\bm{C}^{\prime}
are invertible, or equivalently,
\det\bm{C}\neq 0
and
\det\bm{C}^{\prime}\neq 0
.
For any deterministic sample sizes
n_{i}^{S}\in\{1,2,\dots\}
and
n^{\prime}\in\{1,2,\dots\}
, define the data-generating distributions

\displaystyle\bm{N}^{S}_{i}\mid\bm{C}\sim\mathrm{Multi}(n^{S}_{i},C_{:,i}),\quad\bm{N}^{S}_{i}\mid\bm{C}^{\prime}\sim\mathrm{Multi}(n_{i}^{S},C^{\prime}_{:,i}),

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{N}}\mid\bm{C},\bm{q}\sim\mathrm{Multi}(n^{\prime},\bm{C}\bm{q}),\quad\tilde{\bm{N}}\mid\bm{C}^{\prime},\bm{q}^{\prime}\sim\mathrm{Multi}(n^{\prime},\bm{C}^{\prime}\bm{q}^{\prime}).

Suppose that, for every choice of the sample sizes
\{n^{S}_{i=1}\}^{K}_{i=1}
and
n^{\prime}
,
\left(\{\bm{N}^{S}_{i}\}^{K}_{i=1},\tilde{\bm{N}}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\left(\{\bm{N}_{i}^{S^{\prime}}\}^{K}_{i=1},\tilde{\bm{N}}^{\prime}\right)
, as random vectors in
\mathbb{N}^{K^{2}+K}
, where the left-hand side is generated by
(\bm{C},\bm{q})
and the right-hand side by
(\bm{C}^{\prime},\bm{q}^{\prime})
.
Then,
\bm{C}=\bm{C}^{\prime}
and
\bm{q}=\bm{q}^{\prime}
.

Lemma
1
implies that the mapping
(\bm{q},\bm{C})\mapsto\{\{\bm{n}^{S}_{i}\},\tilde{\bm{n}}\}
is injective up to measure-zero label permutations when the graph is connected and all source classes appear.

Let
\Delta^{K-1}
be the
(K-1)
-dimensional probability simplex:

\displaystyle\Delta^{K-1}\coloneqq\left\{\bm{q}=(q_{1},\dots,q_{K})\in\mathbb{R}^{K}\ \colon\ q_{i}\geq 0\ \ \text{for every $i$},\ \ \sum^{K}_{i=1}q_{i}=1\right\}.

The following lemma provides the support condition needed in statements described later.

Lemma 2
.

Let
(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})
be the true parameter pair, where
\bm{q}_{0}\in\Delta^{K-1}
and
\bm{C}_{0}\in(0,1)^{K\times K}
with
\det\bm{C}_{0}\neq 0
.
Define the Euclidean small ball as

\displaystyle B_{\epsilon}(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})\coloneqq\left\{(\bm{q},\bm{C})\ \colon\ \|\bm{q}-\bm{q}_{0}\|_{2}<\epsilon,\ \|\bm{C}-\bm{C}_{0}\|_{F}<\epsilon\right\},

for some radius
\epsilon>0
small enough that all vectors in the ball stay strictly inside the simplex.
Then, for every
\epsilon
, the joint prior distribution
\Pi
on
(\bm{q},\bm{C})
assigns strictly positive mass to the ball:
\Pi\left(B_{\epsilon}(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})\right)>0
.

This lemma states that positivity of Gaussian density and the smooth bijection yield the positive push-forward density, and it is the classical strategy used for logistic-Gaussian process priors in density estimation
[
50
]
.

Lemmas
1
,
2
and the classical results from
Ghosal et al. [20]
,
Van der Vaart [52]
gives the following statement.

Proposition 1
.

Let
K\geq 2
be fixed and
(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})
be the true parameters pair with
\bm{q}_{0}\in\mathring{\Delta}^{K-1}
and
\bm{C}_{0}\in(0,1)^{K\times K}
, where
\mathring{\Delta}^{K-1}
is the interior of
\Delta^{K-1}
, and
\det\bm{C}_{0}\neq 0
.
Suppose that the data consist of
\{\bm{N}^{S}_{i}\}^{K}_{i=1}
and
\tilde{\bm{N}}
where conditionally on
(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})
,

\displaystyle\bm{N}^{S}_{i}\sim\mathrm{Multi}\left(n^{S}_{i},\bm{C}_{0,i}\right),\quad\tilde{\bm{N}}\sim\mathrm{Multi}\left(n^{\prime},\bm{C}_{0}\bm{q}_{0}\right).

Also suppose that sample sizes diverge with the single index:
N\coloneqq n^{\prime}+\sum^{K}_{i=1}n^{S}_{i}\to\infty,\quad\text{and}\quad\min_{i}n^{S}_{i}\to\infty
.
Then, for every
\epsilon>0
,
\Pi\left(B_{\epsilon}^{c}\mid\text{data}\right)\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}_{(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})}}0
.

Under the same assumption in Proposition
1
, the following statements about the posterior contraction rate are obtained.

Theorem 1
.

Let the data-generating model, true parameter pair
(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})
, and diverging sample sizes
N=n^{\prime}+\sum^{K}_{i=1}n_{i}^{S}\to\infty
satisfy the setup spelled out before Proposition
1
.
Define the Euclidean radius
\epsilon_{N}\coloneqq M/\sqrt{N},\quad M>0\ \ \text{arbitrary but fixed}
.
Let

\displaystyle B_{N}^{c}=\left\{(\bm{q},\bm{C})\ \colon\ \|\bm{q}-\bm{q}_{0}\|_{2}+\|\bm{C}-\bm{C}_{0}\|_{F}>\epsilon_{N}\right\}.

Under Lemma
1
and
2
, the joint posterior
\Pi(\cdot\mid\text{data})
for the Laplacian-Gaussian hierarchy satisfies

\displaystyle\Pi\left(B_{N}^{c}\mid\text{data}\right)\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}_{(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})}}0.

That is, the posterior contracts around the truth at the parametric rate
N^{-1/2}
.

Corollary 1
.

Retain the setting and notation of Theorem
1
.
For each class
i
, write

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{N}(q_{i})\coloneqq\mathrm{Var}\left(q_{i}\mid\text{data of size $N$}\right),

under the joint posterior
\Pi(\cdot\mid\text{data})
.
Let
L
be the connected-graph Laplacian used in the GMRF prior and let
\lambda_{2}(L)\coloneqq\min\left\{\lambda>0\ \colon\ \text{$\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $L$}\right\}
be its algebraic connectivity.
Assume the hyper-parameter
\tau_{\bm{q}}
is fixed, or sampled from a Gamma prior independent of
N
.
Then, there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on the true
(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})
and on
K
, such that for every sample size
N
large enough,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{N}(q_{i})\leq\frac{C}{\lambda_{2}(L)N},\quad\forall i\in\{1,\dots,K\}.

Finally, we can show the following statement about the robustness to graph Laplacian misspecification.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\bm{L}_{0}
be the true Laplacian, and
\bm{F}_{0}\coloneqq\mathrm{diag}(\bm{C}_{0}\bm{q}_{0})-(\bm{C}_{0}\bm{q}_{0})(\bm{C}_{0}\bm{q}_{0})^{\top}\succeq 0
be the Fisher information of
\bm{\theta}
in the target multinomial likelihood.
For
\bm{L}\neq\bm{L}_{0}
, let
\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{N}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}[\bm{\theta}\mid\text{data}]
be the posterior mean of
\bm{\theta}
under the misspecified prior.
Then, for all sample sizes
N
large enough,

\displaystyle\|\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{N}-\bm{\theta}_{0}\|_{2}\leq\underbrace{\Big\|(N\bm{F}_{0}+\tau_{\bm{q}}\bm{L})^{-1}\Big\|_{2}}_{\text{sampling + prior precision}}\underbrace{\tau_{\bm{q}}\Big\|(\bm{L}-\bm{L}_{0})\bm{\theta_{0}}\Big\|_{2}}_{\text{graph-misspecification bias}}+O_{P}(N^{-1}).

(6)

In particular,

\displaystyle\|\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{N}-\bm{\theta}_{0}\|_{2}\leq\frac{\tau_{\bm{q}}}{N\lambda_{\min}(\bm{F}_{0})+\tau_{\bm{q}}\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})}\left\|(\bm{L}-\bm{L}_{0})\bm{\theta_{0}}\right\|_{2}+O_{P}(N^{-1}),

(7)

where
\lambda_{\min}(\bm{F}_{0})>0
and
\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})>0
are, respectively, the smallest eigenvalue of
\bm{F}_{0}
and the algebraic connectivity of
\bm{L}
.

Thus, we can see that the bias decays as
N^{-1}
when
\bm{L}\neq\bm{L}_{0}
and if the graphs coincide the leading term vanishes and the posterior mean is unbiased up to the usual
N^{-1/2}
noise.
Moreover, Proposition
2
states that a larger algebraic connectivity
\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})
reduces bias, emphasising the benefit of rich similarity structures.

6.1
Natural
-
Gradient Dynamics

The natural gradient of

\displaystyle\operatorname{grad}^{\mathrm{FR}}F(\bm{q})=g_{\bm{q}}^{-1}\,\nabla^{(m)}F(\bm{q})=\bm{q}\odot\Bigl(\nabla_{\!\bm{q}}F-(\nabla_{\!\bm{q}}F)^{\top}\!\bm{q}\Bigr),

where

The associated flow
\dot{\bm{q}}(t)=-\,\operatorname{grad}^{\mathrm{FR}}F\bigl(\bm{q}(t)\bigr)
is the steepest–descent curve in the Fisher–Rao geometry.

Proposition 3

(Natural–gradient flow of the penalised objective)
.

Under the Fisher–Rao metric
g_{\bm{q}}(\bm{v},\bm{w})=\sum_{i=1}^{K}v_{i}w_{i}/q_{i}

the natural gradient
\operatorname{grad}^{\mathrm{FR}}F(\bm{q})
of

is

\operatorname{grad}^{\mathrm{FR}}F(\bm{q})\;=\;\mathrm{diag}(\bm{q})\Bigl(n^{\prime}\,\bm{C}^{\!\top}\!\bigl(\bm{1}-\tfrac{\hat{\bm{r}}}{\bm{r}(\bm{q})}\bigr)\;+\;\tau_{\bm{q}}\,\bm{L}\,\bm{\theta}\Bigr),

(9)

where the division is element–wise.
Consequently the un
-

-

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{q}}_{t}\,=\,-\operatorname{grad}^{\mathrm{FR}}F(\bm{q}_{t})\,=\,-\,\mathrm{diag}(\bm{q}_{t})\Bigl(n^{\prime}\,\bm{C}^{\!\top}\!\bigl(\bm{1}-\tfrac{\hat{\bm{r}}}{\bm{r}(\bm{q}_{t})}\bigr)\;+\;\tau_{\bm{q}}\,\bm{L}\,\bm{\theta}_{t}\Bigr)

\dot{q}_{t,j}=-\,q_{t,j}\bigl[n^{\prime}\,[\bm{C}^{\!\top}(\bm{1}-\hat{\bm{r}}/\bm{r})]+\tau_{\bm{q}}[\bm{L}\bm{\theta}_{t}]\bigr]_{j}

Remarks

i)

Role of Laplacian
When the Laplacian term is absent (
\tau_{\bm{q}}=0
), the flow reduces to the classical replicator equation that drives every class
-

q_{j}
proportionally to the (signed) log–likelihood residual
[\bm{C}^{\top}(\bm{1}-\hat{\bm{r}}/\bm{r})]_{j}

-

plays the role of a mutation / diffusion force that mixes mass along edges of the label graph and prevents degenerate solutions.

ii)

Role of the algebraic connectivity.
From Theorem
2
the strong
-
convexity modulus is
\alpha=n^{\prime}\lambda_{\min}(\bm{F}_{0})+\tau_{\bm{q}}\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})
. Along the flow we have
\tfrac{d}{dt}F(\bm{q}_{t})=-\|\mathrm{grad}^{\text{FR}}F(\bm{q}_{t})\|^{2}_{g_{\bm{q}_{t}}}\leq-2\alpha(F(\bm{q}_{t})-F^{*})
, so

\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})
; a denser

iii)

Link to Saerens EM correction.
If we freeze the confusion matrix and drop the Laplacian term, the stationary condition
\bm{C}^{\top}(\hat{\bm{r}}/\bm{r})=\bm{1}
is exactly the fixed point solved (iteratively) by the Saerens EM method
[
47
]

Let

\mathcal{M}

8
Conclusion

We presented GS
-
B
3
SE, a graph–smoothed Bayesian generalization of the classical black
-
box shift estimator.
By tying both the target prior
\bm{q}
and every column of the confusion matrix
\bm{C}
together through a Laplacian–Gaussian hierarchy, the model simultaneously i) shares statistical strength across semantically related classes, ii) quantifies all uncertainty arising from finite validation and target samples, and admits scalable inference with either HMC or a Newton–CG variational surrogate.
We proved that the resulting posterior is identifiable, contracts at the optimal
N^{-1/2}
rate, and that its class
-
wise variance decays inversely with the graph’s algebraic connectivity
\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})
.
A robustness bound further shows that even with a misspecified graph the bias vanishes as
N^{-1}
.
Because our approach is a pure post
-
processing layer that needs only a frozen classifier, a tiny labelled validation set, and a pre
-
computed label graph, it can be retro
-
fitted to virtually any deployed model.

Limitations and future work.

i) Our current graph is built from CLIP or feature embeddings; learning the graph jointly with the posterior could adapt it to the task.
ii) Although inference is already tractable, further speed
-
ups via structured variational approximations would make GS
-
B
3
SE attractive for extreme
-
label settings.
iii) As declared in Section
7.1
, our experiments used a single NVIDIA T4; scaling to larger datasets remains future work.

is the volume if the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\Pi\left(B_{\epsilon}(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\epsilon}(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})}\pi(\bm{q},\bm{C})d(\bm{q},\bm{C})

This concludes the proof.

Proof for Proposition
1
.

For every
\eta>0

\displaystyle U_{\eta}\coloneqq\left\{(\bm{q},\bm{C})\ \colon\ D_{\mathrm{KL}}[P_{(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})}\|P_{(\bm{q},\bm{C})}]<\eta\right\},

denotes the
K(K+1)
-dimensional multinomial law of the complete data.
Because the parameter space is finite-dimensional and smooth,

\displaystyle\left\|(\bm{q},\bm{C})-(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})\right\|<\delta(\eta)\Longrightarrow D_{\mathrm{KL}}[P_{(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})}\|P_{(\bm{q},\bm{C})}]<\eta,

with a deterministic radius
\delta(\eta)\to 0
as
\eta\downarrow 0
.
This follows from a second-order Taylor expansion of the multinomial log-likelihood around the interior point.
Lemma
2
says that the prior puts strictly positive mass on every Euclidean ball and hence on
U_{\eta}
:

\displaystyle\Pi(U_{\eta})>0,\quad\forall\eta>0.

Let
\bm{\theta}=(\bm{q},\bm{C})
and
\bm{\theta}_{0}=(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})
.
For any fixed
\epsilon>0
, define the alternative set
B_{\epsilon}
above.
Because the model is an i.i.d. exponential family (multinomials) of finite dimension,
Ghosal et al. [20]
show there exist tests
\varphi_{N}\ \colon\ \text{data}\to\{0,1\}
satisfying, for some constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
independent of
N
,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\text{(i)}&\mathbb{P}_{\bm{\theta}_{0}}(\varphi_{N}=1)\leq e^{-c_{1}N},\\
\text{(ii)}&\sup_{\bm{\theta}\in B_{\epsilon}}\mathbb{P}_{\bm{\theta}}(\varphi_{N}=0)\leq e^{-c_{2}N}.\end{cases}

(13)

Write the complete log-likelihood ratio as

\displaystyle l_{N}(\bm{\theta})

\displaystyle=\log\frac{dP_{\bm{\theta}}}{dP_{\bm{\theta}_{0}}}(\text{data})

\displaystyle=\sum^{K}_{i=1}\sum^{K}_{j=1}N^{S}_{j,i}\log\frac{C_{j,i}}{C_{0j,i}}+\sum^{K}_{j=1}\tilde{N}_{j}\log\frac{(\bm{C}\bm{q})_{j}}{(\bm{C}_{0}\bm{q}_{0})_{j}}.

Let
\varphi_{N}=\bm{1}\left\{l_{N}(\bm{\theta}_{0})<-\frac{1}{2}cN\right\}
, where
c\in(0,c^{*})
and
c^{*}
is the minimized KL-divergence over
B_{\epsilon}
:

\displaystyle c^{*}=\inf_{\bm{\theta}\in B_{\epsilon}}D_{KL}\left[P_{\bm{\theta}_{0}}\|P_{\bm{\theta}}\right]>0.

The above inequality is strict by Lemma
1
.
Chernoff bounds for sums of bounded log-likelihood ratios gives (i) in Eq.
13
.
Under any
\bm{\theta}\in B_{\epsilon}
, the expected log-likelihood ratio equals
-N\cdot D_{\mathrm{KL}}[P_{\bm{\theta}_{0}}\|P_{\bm{\theta}}]\leq-c^{*}N
.
A one-sided Hoeffding inequality for sums of independent, bounded random variables then yields (ii) in Eq.
13
, with
c_{2}=(c^{*}-c)/2
.

\displaystyle\Pi\left(B_{\epsilon}\mid\text{data}\right)\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}_{(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})}}0,\quad\forall\epsilon>0.

Because the metric
\|(\bm{q},\bm{C})-(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})\|
is continuous, this convergence in outer probability equals convergence in probability, completing the proof.

.

Put the log-odds vector for the target prior

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}=(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{K})^{\top},\quad\theta_{i}=\log q_{i}-\frac{1}{K}\sum^{K}_{k=1}\log q_{k},

and for the
i
-th column of the confusion matrix

\displaystyle\bm{\phi}_{i}=(\phi_{1,i},\dots,\phi_{K,i})^{\top},\quad\phi_{i,j}=\log C_{j,i}-\frac{1}{K}\sum^{K}_{k=1}\log C_{k,i}.

K-1

\displaystyle\bm{\eta}=(\bm{\theta}^{\top},\bm{\phi}_{1}^{\top},\dots,\bm{\phi}_{K}^{\top})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad d=(K-1)+K(K-1)=K^{2}-1.

The map
\Psi\ \colon\bm{\eta}\mapsto(\bm{q},\bm{C})
given by component-wise softmax is
C^{\infty}
and has a Jacobian of full rank everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

Hence,
\bm{\eta}\mapsto(\bm{q},\bm{C})

We fix
\bm{\eta}_{0}
corresponding to
(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})

Let

\displaystyle\ell_{N}(\bm{\eta})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\log p_{\bm{\eta}}(\text{data})

\displaystyle=\sum^{K}_{i=1}\sum^{K}_{j=1}N^{S}_{j,i}\log C_{j,i}+\sum^{K}_{j=1}\tilde{N}_{j}\log(\bm{C}\bm{q})_{j},

where
\bm{C}
and
\bm{q}
in the right-hand side are the softmax images of
\bm{\eta}
.
Because each count is bounded by
N
and the mapping
\Psi

\ell_{N}(\bm{\eta})
is twice countinuously differentiable in a neighbourhood of
\bm{\eta}_{0}
.

\displaystyle\dot{\ell}_{N}(\bm{\eta}_{0})

\displaystyle=\sum^{K}_{i=1}\sum^{K}_{j=1}\left(N^{S}_{j,i}-n^{S}_{i}C_{0j,i}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{\eta}}\log C_{j,i}+\left.\sum^{K}_{j=1}\left(\tilde{N}_{j}-n^{\prime}(\bm{C}_{0}\bm{q}_{0})_{j}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{\eta}}\log(\bm{C}\bm{q})_{j}\right|_{\bm{\eta}_{0}},

\displaystyle\ddot{\ell}_{N}(\bm{\eta}_{0})

\displaystyle=-N\bm{F}(\bm{\eta}_{0}),

where
\bm{F}(\bm{\eta}_{0})
is the Fisher information matrix of dimension
d\times d
.
Here,
\bm{F}(\bm{\eta}_{0})
is positive definite because
(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})
is interior and
\bm{C}_{0}
is invertible (ensures different parameters yield different probability mass functions).
The positive definiteness can be checked by observing that the Fisher information of a finite multinomial family with parameters inside the simplex is positive definite and the Jacobian of
\Psi
is full rank.

Set local parameter
\bm{h}=\sqrt{N}(\bm{\eta}-\bm{\eta}_{0})

\displaystyle\ell_{N}(\bm{\eta}_{0}+h/\sqrt{N})-\ell_{N}(\bm{\eta}_{0})=\bm{h}^{\top}\Delta_{N}-\frac{1}{2}\bm{h}^{\top}\bm{F}(\bm{\eta}_{0})\bm{h}+r_{N}(\bm{h}),

\displaystyle\Delta_{N}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\dot{\ell}_{N}(\bm{\eta}_{0})\rightsquigarrow\mathcal{N}\left(0,\bm{F}(\bm{\eta}_{0})\right),\quad\sup_{\|\bm{h}\|=O(1)}\left|r_{N}(\bm{h})\right|\overset{P}{\to}0.

The convergence of
\Delta_{N}
uses the multivariate central limit theorem for sums of independent bounded variables.
Uniform control of
r_{N}
follows from third derivative boundedness in a neighborhood of
\bm{\eta}_{0}
.

In
\bm{\eta}
-coordinates, the Laplacian-Gaussian prior has a density

\displaystyle\pi(\bm{\eta})=\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\tau_{\bm{q}}\bm{\theta}^{\top}\bm{L}\bm{\theta}-\frac{1}{2}\tau_{\bm{C}}\sum^{K}_{i=1}\bm{\phi}_{i}^{\top}\bm{L}\bm{\phi}_{i}\right]\varphi(\tau_{\bm{q}},\tau_{\bm{C}}),

where
\varphi
is strictly positive and smooth Gamma density.
Because
\bm{L}
is positive semi-definite on
\mathcal{H}

\pi
is continuous and strictly positive in a neighborhood of
\bm{\eta}_{0}

c_{0}>0

\displaystyle\pi(\bm{\eta})\geq c_{0},\quad\text{whenever}\quad\|\bm{\eta}-\bm{\eta}_{0}\|_{2}\leq\delta.

\displaystyle\Pi\left(\sqrt{N}(\bm{\eta}-\bm{\eta}_{0})\in\cdot\mid\text{data}\right)\rightsquigarrow\mathcal{N}\left(\bm{F}^{-1}\Delta_{N},\bm{F}^{-1}\right)\quad\text{in $P_{(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})}$-probability},

and for any
M>0

\displaystyle\Pi\left(\left\|\sqrt{N}(\bm{\eta}-\bm{\eta}_{0})\right\|_{2}>M\mid\text{data}\right)\overset{P_{(\bm{q}_{0},\bm{C}_{0})}}{\to}0.

Because
\Psi
is
C^{1}
with Jacobian
D\Psi(\bm{\eta}_{0})
of full rank, there exists a constant

such that, for all
\bm{\eta}
in a neighborhood of
\bm{\eta_{0}}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi(\bm{\eta})-\Psi(\bm{\eta}_{0})\right\|\geq K_{0}^{-1}\|\bm{\eta}-\bm{\eta}_{0}\|_{2}.

Because

Proof for Corollary
1
.

Consider the centred log-odds vector
\bm{\theta}\in\mathcal{H}
for the target prior.
Its conditional posterior density is

\displaystyle p(\bm{\theta}\mid\text{data})\propto\exp\left\{\ell_{N}^{(\bm{q})}(\bm{\theta})-\frac{1}{2}\tau_{\bm{q}}\bm{\theta}^{\top}\bm{L}\bm{\theta}\right\},

where

\displaystyle\ell_{N}^{(\bm{q})}(\bm{\theta})=\sum^{K}_{j=1}\tilde{N}_{j}\log\left[(C\cdot\text{softmax}(\bm{\theta}))_{j}\right].

Take any point
\bm{\theta}^{*}
in a
O(N^{-1/2})
-ball around
\bm{\theta}_{0}
.
By Theorem
1
this contains essentially all posterior mass.
Inside that ball Taylor expansion gives

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}\ell_{N}^{(\bm{q})}}{\partial\bm{\theta}}(\bm{\theta}^{*})=N\bm{F}+O(N^{1/2}),

with
\bm{F}
the Fisher information matrix.
Hence the negative Hessian of the log-posterior satisfies

\displaystyle\bm{J}_{N}\coloneqq\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\bm{\theta}^{2}}\left[\ell_{N}^{(\bm{q})}-\frac{1}{2}\tau_{\bm{q}}\bm{\theta}^{\top}\bm{L}\bm{\theta}\right](\bm{\theta}^{*})\succeq N\bm{F}+\tau_{\bm{q}}\bm{L}-O(N^{1/2})\bm{F}.

For
N
beyond some
N_{0}
, the error term is dominated by
N\bm{F}
, so there exists
c_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\bm{J}_{N}\succeq Nc_{0}\bm{F}+\tau_{\bm{q}}\bm{L}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}_{N}(\bm{\theta})\preceq\left[Nc_{0}\bm{F}+\tau_{\bm{q}}\bm{L}\right]^{-1}.

Because
\bm{L}
has eigen-pair
(0,\bm{1})
and eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}\geq\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})
on
\mathcal{H}
, the restricted inverse satisfies

\displaystyle\left[Nc_{0}\bm{F}+\tau_{\bm{q}}\bm{L}\right]^{-1}\preceq\frac{1}{Nc_{0}+\tau_{\bm{q}}\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})}\bm{F}\preceq\frac{1}{\tau_{\bm{q}}\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})}\bm{F}+\frac{1}{c_{0}N}\bm{F}.

(14)

For
N\geq N_{0}
, the second term dominates
1/(\tau_{\bm{q}\lambda_{2}(\bm{L}}))
, and hence

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{N}(\bm{\theta}_{\ell})\leq\frac{2}{c_{0}N},\quad\ell=1,\dots,K.

(15)

The softmax map
\sigma\ \colon\ \bm{\theta}\mapsto\bm{q}
has derivative

\displaystyle D\sigma(\bm{\theta})=\mathrm{diag}(\bm{q})-\bm{q}\bm{q}^{\top}.

Every entry of
D\sigma
is bounded by
1/4
(attained at uniform prior), hence the operator norm obeys
\|D\sigma\|\leq 1/2
.
For any random vector
\bm{\theta}
with covariance
\Sigma
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(\sigma(\bm{\theta}))=D\sigma\Sigma D\sigma^{\top}\preceq\|D\sigma\|^{2}\Sigma\preceq\frac{1}{4}\Sigma.

Applying to the posterior distribution with bound
15
gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(q_{i})\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum^{K}_{\ell=1}\left[D\sigma_{i\ell}(\bm{\theta}^{*})\right]^{2}\mathrm{Var}_{N}(\theta_{\ell})\leq\frac{1}{2c_{0}N}.

(16)

The constant
c_{0}
is
\lambda_{\min}(\bm{F})
which is independent of the graph but positive.
Tightening Eq.
14
using the
\tau_{\bm{q}}\bm{L}
, term, we keep only the
\tau_{\bm{q}}\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})
contribution:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{N}(\theta_{\ell})\leq\frac{1}{\tau_{\bm{q}}\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})}+\frac{1}{c_{0}N}\leq\frac{C_{2}}{\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})N},\quad C_{2}=\frac{\tau_{\bm{q}}+c_{0}}{\tau_{\bm{q}}c_{0}}.

Repeating the delta-method argument with this sharper bound scales Eq.
16
by the same
1/\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})
.
Set
C=K(\tau_{\bm{q}}+c_{0})/2\tau_{\bm{q}}c_{0}
.
Then,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{N}(q_{i})\leq\frac{C}{\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})N},\quad i=1,\dots,K,

which is precisely the claimed finite-sample variance control.
∎

(17)

\displaystyle\bm{\Delta}_{N}\coloneqq\hat{\bm{\theta}}_{N}-\bm{\theta}_{0}.

\displaystyle\nabla\ell^{(\bm{q})}_{N}(\hat{\bm{\theta}}_{N})

\displaystyle=\nabla\ell^{(\bm{q})}_{N}(\bm{\theta}_{0})+\nabla^{2}\ell_{N}^{(\bm{q})}(\bm{\theta}_{0})\bm{\Delta}_{N}+R_{N},

O(N^{-1})

[
52
]
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{N}-\bm{\theta}_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\bm{\Delta}_{N}\mid\text{data}]+O_{P}(N^{-1})

\displaystyle=-(N\bm{F}_{0}+\tau_{\bm{q}}\bm{L})^{-1}(\bm{L}-\bm{L}_{0})\bm{\theta}_{0}+O_{P}(N^{-1}),

\displaystyle\left\|\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{N}-\bm{\theta}_{0}\right\|

\mathcal{H}=\{\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}\ \colon\ \bm{v}^{\top}\bm{1}_{K}=0\}
is the
(K-1)
-dimensional hyper-plane of vectors whose components sum to zero.
Its orthogonal projection operator is the
K\times K
symmetric matrix
\bm{P}_{\mathcal{H}}=\bm{I}_{\bm{K}}-\frac{1}{K}\bm{1}_{K}\bm{1}_{K}^{\top}
, because for any
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}

.

\displaystyle\left\|(N\bm{F}+\tau_{\bm{q}}\bm{L})^{-1}\right\|=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\min}(N\bm{F}+\tau_{\bm{q}}\bm{L})}\leq\frac{1}{N\lambda_{\min}(\bm{F})+\tau_{\bm{q}}\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})}.

Combine them to get inequality in Eq.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Throughout the proof we fix an arbitrary interior point
\bm{q}\in\mathring{\Delta}^{K-1}
and an arbitrary tangent direction
\bm{v}\in T_{\bm{q}}\Delta^{K-1}=\{\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}\mid\bm{1}^{\top}\bm{v}=0\}
.
To establish geodesic convexity it suffices to show

\bm{v}^{\!\top}\,\operatorname{Hess}^{\mathrm{FR}}_{\bm{q}}F\,\bm{v}\;\;\geq\;\;\Bigl[n^{\prime}\lambda_{\min}(\bm{F}_{0})+\tau_{\bm{q}}\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})\Bigr]\,\bm{v}^{\!\top}g_{\bm{q}}\,\bm{v},

(18)

because the latter is the Rayleigh quotient form of the desired
positive–definite bound.
Recall the form of the negative log–posterior (constant terms omitted):

\displaystyle F(\bm{q})=n^{\prime}\underbrace{D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl[\hat{\bm{r}}\,\|\,\bm{C}\bm{q}\bigr]}_{F_{\text{lik}}(\bm{q})}+\frac{\tau_{\bm{q}}}{2}\,\underbrace{\bm{\theta}^{\!\top}L\bm{\theta}}_{F_{\text{reg}}(\bm{q})}.

Accordingly

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hess}^{\mathrm{FR}}_{\bm{q}}F=n^{\prime}\,\operatorname{Hess}^{\mathrm{FR}}_{\bm{q}}F_{\text{lik}}+\tau_{\bm{q}}\,\operatorname{Hess}^{\mathrm{FR}}_{\bm{q}}F_{\text{reg}}.

(19)

Write
\bm{r}(\bm{q})\coloneqq\bm{C}\bm{q},\;\;r_{i}(\bm{q})=\sum_{j}C_{ij}q_{j}
.
For the multinomial log–likelihood term we have the usual identity

\operatorname{Hess}^{\mathrm{FR}}_{\bm{q}}F_{\text{lik}}=\bm{F}_{0}=\mathrm{diag}\!\bigl(\bm{r}(\bm{q})\bigr)-\bm{r}(\bm{q})\bm{r}(\bm{q})^{\!\top}.

(20)

Indeed, start with
F_{\text{lik}}(\bm{q})=\sum_{i}\hat{r}_{i}\log\frac{\hat{r}_{i}}{r_{i}(\bm{q})}
and differentiate twice with respect to
q_{j}
while keeping the tangent constraint
\bm{1}^{\top}\bm{v}=0
, and we have Eq. (
20
).
Now evaluate the quadratic form:

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{\!\top}\operatorname{Hess}^{\mathrm{FR}}_{\bm{q}}F_{\text{lik}}\bm{v}

\displaystyle\,=\,\bm{v}^{\!\top}\bm{F}_{0}\bm{v}

\displaystyle\,\geq\,\lambda_{\min}(\bm{F}_{0})\,\|\bm{v}\|_{2}^{2}\qquad\bigl(\text{Rayleigh bound}\bigr)

\displaystyle\,=\,\lambda_{\min}(\bm{F}_{0})\,\bm{v}^{\!\top}\bm{I}\bm{v}.

(21)

To relate the Euclidean norm
\|\cdot\|_{2}
with the Fisher metric
g_{\bm{q}}
notice that

g_{\bm{q}}(\bm{v},\bm{v})=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{v_{i}^{2}}{q_{i}}=\bm{v}^{\!\top}\mathrm{diag}(\bm{q})^{-1}\bm{v},\quad\mathrm{diag}\!(\bm{q})^{-1}\succ 0.

(22)

Hence for every
\bm{v}
,

\displaystyle\|\bm{v}\|_{2}^{2}\;=\;\bm{v}^{\!\top}\mathrm{diag}(\bm{q})\,\mathrm{diag}(\bm{q})^{-1}\bm{v}\leq\bigl(\max_{i}q_{i}\bigr)\,g_{\bm{q}}(\bm{v},\bm{v})\leq g_{\bm{q}}(\bm{v},\bm{v}),

because
q_{i}<1
inside the simplex.
Thus, we have

n^{\prime}\,\bm{v}^{\!\top}\operatorname{Hess}^{\mathrm{FR}}_{\bm{q}}F_{\text{lik}}\bm{v}\geq n^{\prime}\lambda_{\min}(\bm{F}_{0})\,g_{\bm{q}}(\bm{v},\bm{v}).

(23)

Recall
\bm{\theta}=\bigl(\theta_{i}\bigr)_{i=1}^{K}
with
\theta_{i}=\log q_{i}-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{j}\log q_{j}
.
Differentiating twice gives

\operatorname{Hess}^{\mathrm{FR}}_{\bm{q}}F_{\text{reg}}\;=\;J(\bm{q})^{\top}L\,J(\bm{q}),

(24)

where
J(\bm{q})\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times(K-1)}
is the Jacobian

\partial\bm{\theta}/\partial\bm{q}
restricted to the tangent space.
Concretely

\displaystyle J(\bm{q})=\Bigl[\mathrm{diag}(\bm{q})^{-1}-\tfrac{1}{K}\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\!\top}\mathrm{diag}(\bm{q})^{-1}\Bigr]P_{T},

with
P_{T}=\bm{I}-\frac{1}{K}\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\!\top}
the projection onto

T_{\bm{q}}\Delta^{K-1}
.

Applying Eq. (
24
) we expand the quadratic form:

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{\!\top}\operatorname{Hess}^{\mathrm{FR}}_{\bm{q}}F_{\text{reg}}\bm{v}

\displaystyle\,=\,(J\bm{v})^{\!\top}\bm{L}\,(J\bm{v})

\displaystyle\,\geq\,\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})\,\|J\bm{v}\|_{2}^{2}\qquad\bigl(\text{Rayleigh bound on }\bm{L}\bigr)

\displaystyle\,=\,\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})\,\bm{v}^{\!\top}J^{\top}J\,\bm{v}.

(25)

Because
J(\bm{q})^{\top}J(\bm{q})=\mathrm{diag}(\bm{q})^{-1}P_{T}
one checks

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{\!\top}J^{\top}J\,\bm{v}\;=\;\bm{v}^{\!\top}\mathrm{diag}(\bm{q})^{-1}\bm{v}\;=\;g_{\bm{q}}(\bm{v},\bm{v}).

Thus,

\tau_{\bm{q}}\,\bm{v}^{\!\top}\operatorname{Hess}^{\mathrm{FR}}_{\bm{q}}F_{\text{reg}}\bm{v}\;\geq\;\tau_{\bm{q}}\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})\,g_{\bm{q}}(\bm{v},\bm{v}),

(26)

and

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{\!\top}\operatorname{Hess}^{\mathrm{FR}}_{\bm{q}}F\bm{v}\;\geq\;\Bigl[n^{\prime}\lambda_{\min}(\bm{F}_{0})+\tau_{\bm{q}}\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})\Bigr]\,g_{\bm{q}}(\bm{v},\bm{v}).

Since the inequality holds for every
\bm{v}\in T_{\bm{q}}\Delta^{K-1}
, the matrix inequality announced in the theorem follows, and the strong
-
convexity constant is

\displaystyle\alpha\;=\;n^{\prime}\lambda_{\min}(\bm{F}_{0})+\tau_{\bm{q}}\lambda_{2}(\bm{L})\;>\;0.

\theta_{i}=\log q_{i}-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log q_{k}
. Let
s(\bm{q})=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k}\log q_{k}
.
For
j

\displaystyle\partial_{q_{j}}\theta_{i}

\displaystyle\,=\,\frac{\delta_{ij}}{q_{i}}-\frac{1}{K}\frac{1}{q_{j}},\quad\partial_{q_{j}}\bm{\theta}=\mathrm{diag}(\bm{e}_{j}/q)-\tfrac{1}{K}\bm{1}(\bm{e}_{j}/q)^{\top},

where

and

\displaystyle\partial_{q_{j}}F_{\text{reg}}

\displaystyle\,=\,\frac{\tau_{\bm{q}}}{2}\,\partial_{q_{j}}\!\bigl(\bm{\theta}^{\!\top}\bm{L}\bm{\theta}\bigr)

\displaystyle\,=\,\tau_{\bm{q}}\,(\partial_{q_{j}}\bm{\theta})^{\!\top}\bm{L}\bm{\theta}

\displaystyle\,=\,\tau_{\bm{q}}\,\Bigl[\mathrm{diag}(\bm{e}_{j}/q)-\tfrac{1}{K}\bm{1}(\bm{e}_{j}/q)^{\top}\Bigr]^{\!\top}\bm{L}\bm{\theta}

\displaystyle\,=\,\tau_{\bm{q}}\,\tfrac{v_{j}}{q_{j}},\quad\text{where }\;\bm{v}=\Bigl[I-\tfrac{1}{K}\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\!\top}\Bigr]\bm{L}\bm{\theta}=P_{T}\bm{L}\bm{\theta}.

(30)

\nabla_{\!\bm{q}}F_{\text{reg}}\;=\;\tau_{\bm{q}}\,\mathrm{diag}(\bm{q})^{-1}\,P_{T}\,\bm{L}\,\bm{\theta},

(31)

and because

annihilates the
\bm{1}
-

inside the tangent space:

since
\bm{1}
is the null

\bm{L}
.
On the simplex
g_{\bm{q}}^{-1}
acts as left–multiplication by
\mathrm{diag}(\bm{q})
(followed by projection onto
T_{\bm{q}}\Delta^{K-1}
, automatic here because every gradient we computed is already centered).
Hence

\displaystyle\operatorname{grad}^{\mathrm{FR}}F(\bm{q})

\displaystyle\,=\,\mathrm{diag}(\bm{q})\,\nabla_{\!\bm{q}}F

\displaystyle\,=\,\mathrm{diag}(\bm{q})\left(-\,n^{\prime}\,\bm{C}^{\!\top}\!\frac{\hat{\bm{r}}}{\bm{r}(\bm{q})}\;+\;\tau_{\bm{q}}\,\mathrm{diag}(\bm{q})^{-1}\bm{L}\bm{\theta}\right)

\displaystyle\,=\,\mathrm{diag}(\bm{q})\left(n^{\prime}\,\bm{C}^{\!\top}\!\left(\bm{1}-\frac{\hat{\bm{r}}}{\bm{r}(\bm{q})}\right)\;+\;\tau_{\bm{q}}\,\bm{L}\,\bm{\theta}\right),

(32)

which is exactly (
9
). The rightmost expression

\bm{1}

sums to

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigl(\bm{1}^{\top}\bm{q}_{t}\bigr)\;=\;\bm{1}^{\top}\dot{\bm{q}}_{t}\;=\;-\,\bm{1}^{\top}\operatorname{grad}^{\mathrm{FR}}F(\bm{q}_{t})\;=\;0.

Finally, inserting (

, confirming the announced replicator–Laplacian dynamics.

Integrative transcriptomics and single-cell analysis identify SIX4 as a candidate epithelial target in ovarian cancer and PCOS-associated ovarian dysregulation
Authors: Xuan Li, Gaowen Chen, Yifeng Wang, Chunfang Ha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Single-cell-guided transcriptomics prioritized SIX4 in ovarian cancer.•SIX4 was enriched in tumor epithelial compartments of ovarian cancer.•PCOS-associated ovarian dysregulation provided a comorbidity context.•SIX4 knockdown suppressed SKOV3 proliferation, migration and colony growth.•Docking and MD suggested a testable SIX4-centered therapeutic axis.Keywords: Ovarian cancer, Polycystic ovary syndrome, Single-cell RNA sequencing, Machine learning, Biomarker prioritization

Introduction

Ovarian cancer is a highly lethal gynecologic solid tumor characterized by late-stage diagnosis, frequent therapeutic resistance, and marked molecular heterogeneity [1,2]. Among epithelial ovarian cancers, high-grade serous ovarian carcinoma represents the most common and aggressive subtype and is distinguished by extensive genomic instability, recurrent disruption of DNA-repair and cell-cycle regulatory pathways, and substantial intertumoral transcriptional diversity [3,4]. These features indicate that ovarian cancer cannot be understood solely as a malignant epithelial-cell disorder; rather, its bulk transcriptomic profile reflects the combined contribution of tumor epithelial cells, stromal elements, immune populations, and other components of the tumor microenvironment.

Single-cell RNA sequencing has substantially refined the interpretation of solid-tumor transcriptomes by resolving malignant and non-malignant cell states that are otherwise obscured in bulk profiles [[5], [6], [7], [8]]. In ovarian cancer, single-cell studies have revealed heterogeneous tumor epithelial compartments, variable stromal and immune-cell states, and site- or context-specific microenvironmental programs that contribute to tumor progression, immune escape, and treatment resistance [9,10]. These findings provide a rationale for integrating bulk transcriptomics with single-cell localization when prioritizing candidate biomarkers or therapeutic targets in ovarian cancer, particularly when candidate genes are derived from cross-cohort or cross-disease analyses.

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a common endocrine and metabolic disorder in women of reproductive age, with a reported prevalence that varies according to diagnostic criteria and population characteristics [11,12]. PCOS is clinically defined by hyperandrogenism, ovulatory dysfunction, and polycystic ovarian morphology, and is associated with abnormalities in follicular development, ovarian somatic-cell function, and metabolic regulation [[13], [14], [15]]. Although epidemiologic studies have examined possible associations between PCOS and gynecologic malignancies, the evidence linking PCOS to ovarian cancer remains inconsistent, and current data do not justify a direct causal interpretation [16,17]. Recent systematic and bioinformatic studies suggest that PCOS and ovarian cancer may share dysregulated hormonal, metabolic, inflammatory, and proliferative networks, but these observations do not establish progression from PCOS to cancer [18,19]. Therefore, PCOS should not be framed as a precursor of ovarian cancer. A more appropriate and biologically restrained question is whether PCOS-associated ovarian dysregulation and ovarian cancer share molecular candidates that reflect convergent alterations in ovarian-cell homeostasis, proliferation, or tissue remodeling.

From this perspective, cross-disease transcriptomic analysis can identify shared molecular candidates without implying direct disease progression. Bulk transcriptomic integration can nominate recurrent genes across independent cohorts, while single-cell analysis can determine whether these candidates localize to biologically relevant compartments, such as granulosa-cell states in PCOS-associated ovarian dysfunction or epithelial and microenvironmental compartments in ovarian cancer [9,10,20,21]. This distinction is important because genes identified from bulk overlap may have different cellular sources and functional meanings in different disease settings. Candidate genes therefore require cell-state-level interpretation before they can be considered biologically meaningful or experimentally tractable.

SIX-family homeodomain transcription factors regulate organ development, epithelial remodeling, and oncogenic plasticity, providing biological context for evaluating SIX4 beyond computational ranking [[22], [23], [24], [25]]. In the present study, we performed an integrative bulk and single-cell transcriptomic analysis to identify shared molecular candidates between ovarian cancer and PCOS-associated ovarian dysregulation. Public bulk transcriptomic datasets were analyzed using platform-specific differential-expression analysis, within-arm harmonization, robust rank aggregation, and machine-learning-based feature selection, including LASSO regression, SVM-RFE, and SHapley Additive exPlanations analysis [[26], [27], [28]]. Single-cell RNA-sequencing datasets were then used to localize prioritized candidates within PCOS-related ovarian cell populations and HGSOC-derived compartments. SIX4 was prioritized for further evaluation based on its model contribution, epithelial enrichment, and experimental tractability. Preliminary validation was performed using dehydroepiandrosterone-treated KGN cells and siRNA-mediated SIX4 knockdown in SKOV3 ovarian cancer cells, followed by exploratory structural modeling of a SIX4-centered axis.

Materials and methods

Study design and data acquisition

This study used an integrative cross-disease framework to identify shared molecular candidates between PCOS-associated ovarian dysregulation and ovarian cancer (OC); it was not designed as a tissue-matched or epidemiologic comorbidity analysis. The PCOS bulk arm included granulosa-cell datasets GSE293353 (RNA sequencing; 9 PCOS and 9 control samples), the mRNA subset of GSE168404 (RNA sequencing; 5 PCOS and 5 age/BMI-matched control samples), and GSE34526 (GPL570 microarray; 7 PCOS and 3 control samples). The OC bulk arm included GSE66957 (GPL15048 microarray; 57 ovarian carcinomas and 12 normal ovarian surface epithelial samples), GSE14407 (GPL570 microarray; 12 serous ovarian cancer epithelial and 12 ovarian surface epithelial samples), and GSE27651 (GPL570 microarray; 43 serous borderline/low- or high-grade neoplastic samples and 6 human ovarian surface epithelial samples). Single-cell localization used GSE240688 (3 PCOS and 3 control granulosa-cell samples) and GSE184880 (59,324 cells from 7 treatment-naïve HGSOC and 5 non-malignant ovarian samples). Because the PCOS datasets primarily represent granulosa cells whereas the OC datasets represent tumor tissue or ovarian epithelial compartments, integration was interpreted at the gene-rank and pathway levels rather than as a direct tissue-matched comparison. Detailed metadata are provided in Supplementary Table S1.

Data preprocessing and batch correction

Bulk expression data were processed using R software (version 4.3.1) separately by assay type. For the RNA-sequencing datasets GSE293353 and the mRNA subset of GSE168404, processed gene-level matrices deposited in GEO were imported, log2(x + 1)-transformed when required, and modeled with limma after mean-variance trend estimation. For GSE34526, GSE66957, GSE14407, and GSE27651, microarray data underwent background correction and quantile normalization using limma (version 3.56.2). Platform annotation files were used to map probes to official gene symbols; unannotated probes were removed, and multiple probes mapping to the same gene were collapsed by their median expression. Only gene-level log2-scale matrices were harmonized. ComBat from sva (version 3.48.0) was applied separately within the PCOS and OC arms, never across the two diseases, with disease/control status retained in the model matrix to reduce removal of biological signal. The adjusted matrices were used for PCA and integrated arm-level visualizations, whereas the primary cross-study synthesis relied on dataset-specific differential statistics and rank aggregation to reduce dependence on cross-platform absolute expression.

Identification of cross-disease shared differentially expressed genes (DEGs)

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified separately within each dataset and in each harmonized disease-arm matrix using limma with empirical Bayes-moderated statistics. Both upregulated and downregulated genes were defined by |log2 fold change| > 1 and a Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted P value < 0.05 [29]. This prespecified stringent cutoff was selected to prioritize larger, reproducible effects across heterogeneous public cohorts, while acknowledging that moderate disease-associated changes may be missed. For robust rank aggregation (RRA), upregulated and downregulated genes from each dataset were ranked separately by signed moderated statistics and adjusted P values, preserving directionality before aggregation with RobustRankAggreg (version 1.2.1) [30]. Shared candidates were derived from (i) the intersection of the arm-level DEG lists and (ii) RRA across dataset-specific ranked lists.

Functional enrichment and machine-learning prioritization

Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses were performed using clusterProfiler (version 4.8.3). Machine-learning analyses were used for feature prioritization rather than development of a clinical diagnostic model. Candidate features were restricted to shared genes supported by the intersection and/or RRA analyses. The binary classification task distinguished disease samples (PCOS or OC) from their corresponding control samples in the combined gene-level matrix; it did not classify PCOS versus OC. Predictors were standardized within training folds. LASSO logistic regression (glmnet) and support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE; e1071 and caret) each used 10-fold cross-validation. Nonzero LASSO features at the selected lambda and the SVM-RFE subset with the highest cross-validated accuracy were intersected to yield five prioritized genes. A gradient-boosted tree model fitted to these genes was interpreted with SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP).

Single-Cell RNA sequencing analysis

Processed scRNA-seq data were analyzed using Seurat (version 4.4.0). Cells were filtered using gene counts (200–6000), total UMI counts, and mitochondrial read percentages (<20%), followed by SCTransform normalization, PCA, UMAP, and graph-based clustering. Cell types were annotated using canonical markers, including FSHR, CYP19A1, AMH, and INHBA for granulosa cells; EPCAM, KRT8, KRT18, and KRT19 for epithelial cells; COL1A1, COL1A2, and DCN for fibroblast/stromal cells; PECAM1 and VWF for endothelial cells; and PTPRC, CD3D/E, MS4A1/CD79A, and LST1/CTSS for immune lineages. PCOS granulosa-cell subtypes were named by their leading markers (ADAMTS1, PTGES, RGS4, CYP19A1, EGR1, MKI67, BBC3, and COL1A2). In GSE184880, epithelial cells were annotated by epithelial markers and sample origin; no copy-number inference was performed, so HGSOC-derived epithelial cells were not assumed to be definitively malignant. GSVA evaluated the Hallmark WNT/β-catenin signaling, epithelial–mesenchymal transition, hypoxia, and inflammatory-response gene sets; cell-level scores were averaged within epithelial subtypes for visualization.

In silico structural analysis and molecular dynamics (MD) simulation

To investigate the potential structural tractability of the prioritized candidate SIX4, an exploratory molecular dynamics simulation was performed. Given that SIX4 transcriptional activity relies on EYA family co-factors, we evaluated the binding stability of Benzbromarone, a known EYA-family inhibitor scaffold with the SIX4 protein structure.

Initial binding poses were obtained through an Easy2MD docking workflow. MD simulations were executed in GROMACS 2022.2 using the Amber14SB force field and the TIP3P water model. Ligand topologies were generated via antechamber with AM1-BCC charges and converted using ACPYPE. Systems were neutralized with 0.15 M NaCl and underwent energy minimization followed by 200 ps of NVT and NPT equilibration at 298 K. A 100-ns production run was performed under NPT conditions. Structural stability was assessed via RMSD, Rg, and Buried SASA, while binding energetics were estimated using the MM-PBSA method (gmx_MMPBSA).

Cell culture and experimental validation

KGN (granulosa-like tumor cells) and SKOV3 (ovarian serous adenocarcinoma) cells were cultured in DMEM/F12 and McCoy’s 5A medium, respectively, supplemented with 10% FBS. To mimic the PCOS microenvironment, KGN cells were treated with 10 μM dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA) for 24 h.

For loss-of-function studies, siRNA targeting SIX4 (si-SIX4) or a non-targeting control (si-NC) was transfected using Lipofectamine RNAiMAX (Invitrogen). SIX4 mRNA abundance was quantified by RT-qPCR using the 2−ΔΔCt method with GAPDH as the internal reference. Functional effects were assessed by CCK-8 proliferation assays, colony-formation assays (10–14 days), and Transwell migration assays (24 h). All experiments were performed as three independent biological replicates; technical replicate measurements were averaged, and statistical tests were applied to biological-replicate means.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism (version 9.5) and R. Data are presented as mean ± SD from three independent biological replicates. Two-group comparisons used Student’s t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test, as appropriate, and multiple comparisons used one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc test. All tests were two-sided, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Cross-dataset integration identifies disease-specific transcriptional dysregulation in PCOS and ovarian cancer

To characterize the transcriptomic landscapes of PCOS and ovarian cancer, bulk gene-expression data were subjected to platform-specific preprocessing and separate within-arm harmonization. PCA showed improved cross-cohort comparability after ComBat adjustment. The arm-level differential analysis (|log2FC| > 1; adjusted P < 0.05) identified 388 upregulated and 227 downregulated genes in the combined PCOS arm (GSE293353, GSE168404 mRNA subset, and GSE34526), with EPCAM, PAX8, SCNN1A, and HMGA1 among the most upregulated and ITLN1, GADL1, and CLEC4M among the downregulated genes (Fig. 1A–B). In the combined OC arm (GSE66957, GSE14407, and GSE27651), 340 upregulated and 20 downregulated genes were identified, including KLK3, MEGF6, MYH11, and COLQ (upregulated), and DCDC1, ZIC4, and SST (downregulated) (Fig. 1C–D).

Robust rank aggregation identifies a broader shared transcriptomic program enriched in proliferative and epithelial-remodeling pathways

The batch-level intersection yielded 147 shared candidates, whereas RRA identified a broader set of 797 candidates; 91 genes were common to both approaches, with 56 unique to the batch-level set and 706 unique to RRA (Fig. 1E). A heatmap of the top RRA-ranked genes confirmed consistent directionality across disease arms, with TMC4, RCC2, and SPAG5 concordantly upregulated and RASSF3, HBD, and DCDC1 concordantly downregulated (Fig. 2A). Coherent expression clustering was also observed across the top 50 shared DEGs in the combined visualization matrix (Fig. 2E).

GO enrichment analysis revealed significant enrichment in nuclear chromosome segregation, sister chromatid segregation, mitotic sister chromatid segregation, and epithelial tube formation (Fig. 2B). Hierarchical clustering organized these processes into two overarching modules: a proliferative–mitotic axis and an epithelial-morphogenesis axis (Fig. 2C). KEGG analysis further identified significant enrichment in the cell-cycle and Wnt signaling pathways (Fig. 2D). Notably, a circular gene–term network revealed that several genes including SIX4, CCNE1, KIF4A, and CCNB2 simultaneously participated in both the chromosome-segregation and epithelial-tube-formation modules. SIX4 occupied a dual-module hub position, consistent with its identity as a transcription factor at a critical regulatory node within the shared program (Fig. 2F).

Machine-learning-based feature selection prioritizes five genes, with SIX4 highest-ranked in the model

LASSO logistic regression and SVM-RFE identified complementary feature subsets (Fig. 3A–C), and their intersection nominated five prioritized genes: SIX4, KIF2C, MMP7, CCNE1, and GPX3 (Fig. 3D). SHAP analysis showed that SIX4 had the widest distribution of per-sample contribution values (Fig. 3E). The model-specific feature-importance ranking was SIX4 (mean |SHAP| ≈ 1.5), CCNE1 (≈ 0.9), MMP7 (≈ 0.7), KIF2C (≈ 0.3), and GPX3 (≈ 0.1) (Fig. 3F). Thus, SIX4 was the highest-ranked contributor within this internally cross-validated computational model.

Single-cell profiling localizes compartment-specific expression trajectories across disease states

To map the prioritized genes to specific cellular contexts, we projected them onto scRNA-seq atlases. The PCOS atlas resolved 17 transcriptionally distinct populations (Fig. 4A), annotated as eight granulosa-cell (GC) subtypes (ADAMTS1+, PTGES+, RGS4+, CYP19A1+, EGR1+, MKI67+, BBC3+, and COL1A2+), together with immune and epithelial populations (Fig. 4B). GPX3 showed the broadest absolute expression, whereas KIF2C and CCNE1 were restricted mainly to MKI67+ proliferative GCs. SIX4 showed low but broadly distributed expression across several GC subtypes, with modest enrichment in MKI67+ and COL1A2+ populations (Fig. 4D–E).

The HGSOC atlas separated into ten major cell types (Fig. 5A). SIX4 showed the highest z score (≈ 2.5) in the HGSOC-derived epithelial compartment among the five prioritized genes (Fig. 5B). CCNE1 and MMP7 were also epithelially enriched, whereas GPX3 localized mainly to vascular smooth-muscle-cell and fibroblast populations. Within nine epithelial subtypes (MUC5B+, MUC4+, TFF3+, NODAL+, HLA-DMB+, MKI67+, ISG+, TAC1+, and THY1+), GSVA showed differential WNT/β-catenin, epithelial–mesenchymal transition, hypoxia, and inflammatory-response activity, with MMP7 co-localizing with EMT-active populations (Fig. 5C).

Preliminary experimental validation of SIX4 in PCOS-like and ovarian-cancer cellular models

Preliminary experimental validation was performed to evaluate SIX4 expression and function in PCOS-like granulosa-cell and ovarian-cancer cellular models. RT-qPCR confirmed efficient SIX4 knockdown in SKOV3 cells, with SIX4 mRNA expression reduced from 1.00 ± 0.09 in the si-NC group to 0.41 ± 0.02 in the si-SIX4 group, corresponding to an approximately 59% decrease (P = 0.0070). In the PCOS-like model, DHEA treatment increased SIX4 expression in KGN cells compared with the control condition (1.65 ± 0.08 vs. 1.00 ± 0.06), suggesting that SIX4 expression may be inducible under a hyperandrogenic PCOS-like stimulus.

Functionally, SIX4 knockdown attenuated ovarian-cancer-associated cellular phenotypes. CCK-8 assays showed lower absorbance values in si-SIX4-transfected SKOV3 cells than in si-NC cells at 24, 48, and 72 h, indicating reduced proliferative activity. Transwell migration assays showed that SIX4 knockdown decreased the number of migrated cells compared with the si-NC group (126.33 ± 10.59 vs. 311.67 ± 12.34 cells, P = 0.000,585). Colony formation was also impaired following SIX4 silencing (70.67 ± 7.37 vs. 145.67 ± 8.02 colonies, P = 0.0060). These preliminary results indicate that SIX4 is inducible in a PCOS-like cellular context and may contribute to proliferation, migration, and clonogenic growth in ovarian-cancer cells.(Fig. 6).

Exploratory docking and molecular dynamics simulation suggest stable Benzbromarone engagement with SIX4

While our experimental data validated the functional importance of SIX4, its direct pharmacologic tractability remains challenging. SIX4 typically relies on EYA co-factors to form an active transcriptional complex, and Benzbromarone has been reported as an EYA-family inhibitor scaffold. We hypothesized that this scaffold might also exhibit direct affinity for the SIX4 structure, potentially offering a dual-targeting mechanism.

To explore this, a structure-based molecular dynamics (MD) simulation was performed. The docking-derived SIX4–Benzbromarone complex revealed an initial interaction network featuring a hydrogen bond with TYR228, alongside critical hydrophobic and van der Waals contacts involving TYR187, TYR208, ARG211, ARG212, PRO215, LEU216, TRP221, THR226, VAL227, CYS229, and LEU766 (Fig. 7J). Over a 100-ns trajectory, the RMSD of the complex stabilized, the radius of gyration (Rg) remained constant, and distance metrics fluctuated within a stable range, supporting persistent binding (Fig. 7A, B, D). Buried SASA remained strictly above zero, confirming sustained occupancy of the pocket (Fig. 7E, F). Furthermore, MM-PBSA analysis yielded a highly favorable thermodynamic binding energy (ΔEMMPBSA = −77.393 ± 2.759 kJ/mol; ΔGbind = −68.18 ± 3.275 kJ/mol), with van der Waals interactions serving as the dominant energetic contributor (Fig. 7G, I). Residue-wise decomposition explicitly highlighted TRP221 and PRO215 as major interaction anchors (Fig. 7H). Collectively, these in silico results suggest that the SIX4 axis possesses a structurally tractable pocket that warrants future targeted pharmacologic investigation.

Discussion

In this study, we used integrative transcriptomic and single-cell analyses to identify a shared molecular program across PCOS-associated ovarian dysregulation and ovarian cancer without inferring disease progression. By combining multi-cohort bulk integration, machine-learning-based feature prioritization, cross-disease single-cell localization, and preliminary experimental validation, we identified SIX4 as the highest-ranked feature within the computational model and characterized its cell-state distribution.

The shared transcriptomic program was enriched in two principal functional modules: a proliferative–mitotic axis and an epithelial-morphogenesis axis. This architecture is biologically coherent with both diseases. In PCOS, granulosa-cell proliferative dysfunction and arrested follicular development are established features [13,31], whereas ovarian cancer is characterized by cell-cycle dysregulation and epithelial plasticity [3,4]. Previous systematic and integrated bioinformatics studies have likewise identified shared hormonal, metabolic, inflammatory, cell-adhesion, and proliferative networks between PCOS and gynecologic malignancies [18,19]. Independent ovarian-cancer analyses have repeatedly highlighted cell-cycle and kinesin-related hubs, including BUB1B, KIF11, and KIF20A, as well as broader cross-gynecologic hub networks, which is directionally consistent with the CCNE1/KIF2C-containing module identified here [32,33].

Among the five prioritized genes, SIX4 had the highest SHAP-based feature importance, approximately 1.7-fold above the second-ranked feature. SIX4 belongs to the SIX family of homeodomain transcription factors, which regulate development and organogenesis [22]. SIX1, the best-characterized family member, promotes tumor growth, epithelial–mesenchymal transition, and metastasis in breast and ovarian cancer models [[23], [24], [25]]. To our knowledge, the present study provides an integrative evaluation of SIX4 across PCOS-associated ovarian dysregulation and ovarian cancer, but the model ranking should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than evidence of biological dominance.

A notable observation was the context-dependent distribution of SIX4. In the PCOS granulosa-cell atlas, SIX4 showed low-level expression across multiple GC subtypes, whereas in the HGSOC atlas it showed the highest epithelial z score among the prioritized genes. This contrast may reflect disease- and cell-state-specific deployment of a developmental transcription factor, but it should not be interpreted as a demonstrated transition from PCOS to malignancy. Single-cell analysis was valuable for resolving cellular localization that would be obscured in bulk data [[7], [8], [9], [10]]; however, malignant identity of the HGSOC-derived epithelial cells was not independently confirmed by copy-number inference.

The in vitro module provides preliminary biological support for the computational findings. DHEA increased SIX4 expression in KGN cells, directionally consistent with a hyperandrogenic PCOS-like stimulus. In SKOV3 cells, transient SIX4 silencing reduced mRNA expression, proliferative activity, migration, and colony formation. These experiments do not resolve upstream or downstream signaling and were performed in tumor-derived cell lines; therefore, they support SIX4 as a functionally relevant candidate rather than establishing it as a shared disease driver.

Beyond SIX4, single-cell analysis revealed gene-specific divergences that emphasize context dependency. GPX3, despite its low SHAP importance, showed broad expression in PCOS GCs but localized mainly to stromal populations in OC, consistent with its role as a secreted glutathione peroxidase [34]. MMP7 was not detected in PCOS GCs but was enriched in HGSOC-derived epithelial populations with EMT and inflammatory activity, consistent with its established role in epithelial migration and invasion [9,10,35,36]. The five prioritized genes therefore represent a heterogeneous set with disease-specific cellular deployment rather than a single uniform module.

From a translational perspective, SIX4 is a candidate regulatory node for further investigation. Transcription factors can sometimes be approached through obligate co-factors or structurally defined pockets [37]. SIX-family activity depends on EYA co-factors with druggable phosphatase domains [38], and Benzbromarone-related scaffolds inhibit EYA-family phosphatase activity [39]. We therefore explored whether the scaffold might show predicted affinity for a SIX4 structural pocket. The docking-derived SIX4–Benzbromarone complex remained stable during the 100-ns simulation and showed favorable MM-PBSA energetics. These in silico observations do not demonstrate biochemical binding, direct SIX4 inhibition, or therapeutic efficacy; they provide a structural hypothesis for future target-engagement and medicinal-chemistry studies.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the bulk datasets differed in platform, sample source, and biological material; platform-specific preprocessing, arm-specific ComBat adjustment, and rank-based RRA reduce but cannot eliminate cross-study heterogeneity. Second, the PCOS datasets primarily contained granulosa cells, whereas the OC datasets contained tumor tissue or ovarian epithelial samples, so the analysis is not tissue matched. Third, the single-cell datasets were independent rather than patient matched, and no copy-number inference was used to confirm malignant epithelial identity in GSE184880. Fourth, the machine-learning pipeline used internal cross-validation but no independent hold-out cohort after feature selection, creating a risk of optimistic performance and SHAP ranking. Fifth, KGN cells are derived from a granulosa-cell tumor, and the in vitro validation was limited to preliminary assays in KGN and SKOV3 cells. Finally, the Benzbromarone–SIX4 analysis was computational and lacked direct biochemical target-engagement evidence. Validation in primary human granulosa cells, independent ovarian-cancer cohorts, spatial or copy-number-informed single-cell analyses, patient-derived organoids, and direct binding assays is required.

Conclusion

This study identifies a shared, noncausal proliferative and epithelial-remodeling transcriptomic program across PCOS-associated ovarian dysregulation and ovarian cancer through multi-cohort bulk analysis, machine-learning prioritization, and single-cell localization. SIX4 was the highest-ranked feature in the exploratory model and showed broad low-level expression in PCOS granulosa-cell states but stronger enrichment in HGSOC-derived epithelial populations. Preliminary experiments showed SIX4 induction under a PCOS-like hyperandrogenic stimulus and reduced proliferation, migration, and colony formation after SIX4 depletion in ovarian-cancer cells. Structural simulations further generated a hypothesis of predicted Benzbromarone-scaffold engagement. These findings position SIX4 as a candidate regulatory and potentially tractable axis for future validation, not as evidence that PCOS progresses to ovarian cancer or that SIX4 is an established therapeutic target.

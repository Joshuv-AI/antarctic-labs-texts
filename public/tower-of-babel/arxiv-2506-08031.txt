Stochastic Krasnosel skii-Mann Iterations in Banach Spaces with Bregman Distances
Saeed Hashemi Sababe, Ehsan Lotfali Ghasab

Abstract
. We propose a generalization of the stochastic Krasnoselskil-Mann ( S ​ K ​ M ) (SKM) algorithm to reflexive Banach spaces endowed with Bregman distances. Under standard martingale-difference noise assumptions in the dual space and mild conditions on the distance-generating function, we establish almost-sure convergence to a fixed point and derive non-asymptotic residual bounds that depend on the uniform convexity modulus of the generating function. Extensions to adaptive Bregman geometries and robust noise models are also discussed. Numerical experiments on entropy-regularized reinforcement learning and mirror-descent illustrate the theoretical findings.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we fix notation and recall definitions and key lemmas that will be used throughout.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\|\cdot\|)
be a real reflexive Banach space and
\mathcal{X}^{*}
its continuous dual, with duality pairing
\langle\zeta^{*},\zeta\rangle
.

Definition 2.1

(Duality mapping)
.

The
(normalized) duality mapping

\mathcal{J}\colon\mathcal{X}\to 2^{\mathcal{X}^{*}}
is

\mathcal{J}(\zeta)=\bigl\{\zeta^{*}\in\mathcal{X}^{*}:\,\langle\zeta^{*},\zeta\rangle=\|\zeta\|^{2}=\|\zeta^{*}\|_{*}^{2}\bigr\}.

If
\mathcal{X}
is smooth, then
\mathcal{J}
is single-valued; we denote its value by
j(\zeta)
when no ambiguity arises
[
13
]
.

Definition 2.2

(Legendre function)
.

A convex function
\vartheta\colon\mathcal{X}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is
Legendre
if it is

(1)

proper and lower semi-continuous,

(2)

essentially smooth and essentially strictly convex on
\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}\vartheta)
,

(3)

its Fenchel conjugate
\vartheta^{*}
satisfies the same properties.

On
\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}\vartheta)
,
\vartheta
is Gateaux-differentiable; we denote its gradient by
\nabla\vartheta(\zeta)\in\mathcal{X}^{*}

[
11
]
.

Definition 2.3

(Bregman distance)
.

For
\zeta,\varsigma\in\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}\vartheta)
, the
Bregman distance
induced by
\vartheta
is

D_{\vartheta}(\zeta,\varsigma)\;=\;\vartheta(\zeta)-\vartheta(\varsigma)-\bigl\langle\nabla\vartheta(\varsigma),\,\zeta-\varsigma\bigr\rangle.

Lemma 2.4

(Three-Point Identity)
.

For all
\zeta,\varsigma,z\in\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}\vartheta)
,

D_{\vartheta}(\zeta,z)=D_{\vartheta}(\zeta,\varsigma)+D_{\vartheta}(\varsigma,z)+\bigl\langle\nabla\vartheta(z)-\nabla\vartheta(\varsigma),\,\zeta-\varsigma\bigr\rangle.

Proof.

See
[
8
, Lemma 2.2]
.
∎

Definition 2.5

(Uniform convexity)
.

A Legendre function
\vartheta
is
uniformly convex
with modulus
\delta\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
if

(2)

\vartheta\Bigl(\tfrac{\zeta+\varsigma}{2}\Bigr)+\delta\bigl(\|\zeta-\varsigma\|\bigr)\;\leq\;\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\vartheta(\zeta)+\vartheta(\varsigma)\bigr]\quad\forall\,\zeta,\varsigma\in\mathcal{X}.

Uniform convexity implies the estimate

\|\zeta-\varsigma\|^{2}\;\leq\;\frac{2}{\delta(\|\zeta-\varsigma\|)}\,D_{\vartheta}(\zeta,\varsigma),

which links the norm error to the Bregman distance
[
7
]
.

A mapping
\hbar\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
is called
nonexpansive
if

\|\hbar(\zeta)-\hbar(\varsigma)\|\;\leq\;\|\zeta-\varsigma\|\quad\forall\,\zeta,\varsigma\in\mathcal{X}.

Given an iterate
\zeta_{n}
, we define its
Bregman residual

\mathcal{R}_{n}\;:=\;D_{\vartheta}\bigl(\zeta_{n},\,\hbar(\zeta_{n})\bigr).

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\lx@paragraphsign)
be a probability space with a filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
.

Definition 2.6

(Martingale-difference noise)
.

A sequence
\{\mho_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{X}^{*}
is a martingale-difference if

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\,\mho_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigr]=0,\quad\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\mho_{n+1}\|_{*}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigr]<\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

Lemma 2.7

(Robbins-Siegmund)
.

Let
\{a_{n}\},\{b_{n}\},\{c_{n}\}
be nonnegative
\mathcal{F}_{n}
-adapted sequences satisfying

\mathbb{E}[a_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}]+b_{n}\;\leq\;(1+\alpha_{n})\,a_{n}+c_{n},\quad\sum_{n}\alpha_{n}<\infty,\quad\sum_{n}c_{n}<\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

Then
a_{n}
converges a.s. and
\sum_{n}b_{n}<\infty
a.s.
[
15
]
.

3.
Bregman-SKM Algorithm and Almost-Sure Convergence

In this section we introduce the stochastic Bregman-Krasnosel skiĭ -Mann (Bregman-
SKM
) iteration in a Banach space, state the main convergence Theorem, and prove almost-sure convergence under martingale-difference noise.

Definition 3.1

(Bregman-
SKM
iteration)
.

Let
\hbar\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
be nonexpansive and let
\vartheta
be a Legendre function on
\mathcal{X}
. Given
\zeta_{0},\zeta_{1}\in\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}\vartheta)
and step-sizes
(\alpha_{n})\subset(0,1)
, the
Bregman-
SKM
iterates
\{\zeta_{n}\}
are defined by

\displaystyle\varsigma_{n}\;=\;\nabla\vartheta^{*}\!\bigl((1-\alpha_{n})\,\nabla\vartheta(\zeta_{n})+\alpha_{n}\,\nabla\vartheta\bigl(\hbar(\zeta_{n})+\mho_{n}\bigr)\bigr),

\displaystyle\zeta_{n+1}\;=\;\varsigma_{n},

where
(\mho_{n})\subset\mathcal{X}^{*}
is a martingale-difference sequence modeling noise.

In the Hilbertian case
\vartheta(\zeta)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|\zeta\|^{2}
,
\nabla\vartheta=\nabla\vartheta^{*}=I
, and the above reduces to

\zeta_{n+1}=(1-\alpha_{n})\,\zeta_{n}+\alpha_{n}\bigl(\hbar(\zeta_{n})+\mho_{n}\bigr),

which is the classical stochastic KM (
SKM
) scheme
[
9
]
.

Consider the following assumptions:

(A1)

\hbar\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
is non-expansive and has at least one fixed point.

(A2)

\vartheta
is Legendre and uniformly convex with modulus
\delta
satisfying
\delta(r)>0
for
r>0
.

(A3)

Step-sizes satisfy

\alpha_{n}\in(0,1),\quad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}=\infty,\quad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}^{2}<\infty.

(A4)

(\mho_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a martingale-difference with respect to
\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}
and

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\mho_{n+1}\|_{*}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigr]<\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

Lemma 3.2

(One-Step Bregman Decrease)
.

Under
(A1)–(A4)
, the Bregman residuals
\mathcal{R}_{n}=D_{\vartheta}(\zeta_{n},\hbar(\zeta_{n}))
satisfy

\mathbb{E}[D_{\vartheta}(\zeta_{n+1},\hbar(\zeta_{n+1}))\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}]+\tfrac{\delta(\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|)}{2}\,\alpha_{n}\;\leq\;(1+\alpha_{n}^{2L})D_{\vartheta}(\zeta_{n},\hbar(\zeta_{n}))+\alpha_{n}^{2}\,\sigma^{2},

for constants
L,\sigma^{2}>0
depending on the Lipschitz and noise bounds.

Proof.

Recall that

\zeta_{n+1}=\nabla\vartheta^{*}\bigl((1-\alpha_{n})\,\nabla\vartheta(\zeta_{n})+\alpha_{n}\,\nabla\vartheta\bigl(\hbar(\zeta_{n})+\mho_{n}\bigr)\bigr).

Set

a_{n}:=(1-\alpha_{n})\,\nabla\vartheta(\zeta_{n})+\alpha_{n}\,\nabla\vartheta\bigl(\hbar(\zeta_{n})\bigr),\quad b_{n}:=\alpha_{n}\,\bigl[\nabla\vartheta\bigl(\hbar(\zeta_{n})+\mho_{n}\bigr)-\nabla\vartheta\bigl(\hbar(\zeta_{n})\bigr)\bigr].

Then

\zeta_{n+1}=\nabla\vartheta^{*}(a_{n}+b_{n}).

By Lemma
2.4
, for any
\varsigma,z
,

D_{\vartheta}(\varsigma,z)=D_{\vartheta}(\varsigma,\zeta_{n})+D_{\vartheta}(\zeta_{n},z)+\bigl\langle\nabla\vartheta(z)-\nabla\vartheta(\zeta_{n}),\,\varsigma-\zeta_{n}\bigr\rangle.

Apply this with
\varsigma=\zeta_{n+1}
and
z=\hbar(\zeta_{n+1})
to get

(3)

\displaystyle D_{\vartheta}\bigl(\zeta_{n+1},\hbar(\zeta_{n+1})\bigr)

\displaystyle=D_{\vartheta}\bigl(\zeta_{n+1},\hbar(\zeta_{n})\bigr)+D_{\vartheta}\bigl(\hbar(\zeta_{n}),\hbar(\zeta_{n+1})\bigr)

\displaystyle+\bigl\langle\nabla\vartheta\bigl(\hbar(\zeta_{n+1})\bigr)-\nabla\vartheta\bigl(\hbar(\zeta_{n})\bigr),\,\zeta_{n+1}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\bigr\rangle.

Since
\hbar
is nonexpansive and
\vartheta
is uniformly convex with modulus
\delta
, one shows via (
2
) that

D_{\vartheta}\bigl(\hbar(\zeta_{n}),\hbar(\zeta_{n+1})\bigr)\;\leq\;\frac{1}{2}\,\delta\bigl(\|\zeta_{n}-\zeta_{n+1}\|\bigr).

By uniform convexity and smoothness of
\vartheta
, its gradient is Lipschitz on bounded sets: there exists
L>0
such that

\bigl\|\nabla\vartheta\bigl(\hbar(\zeta_{n+1})\bigr)-\nabla\vartheta\bigl(\hbar(\zeta_{n})\bigr)\bigr\|_{*}\;\leq\;L\,\|\hbar(\zeta_{n+1})-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|\;\leq\;L\,\|\zeta_{n+1}-\zeta_{n}\|.

Hence, by Cauchy-Schwarz and Youngs inequalities,

\bigl\langle\nabla\vartheta(\hbar(\zeta_{n+1}))-\nabla\vartheta(\hbar(\zeta_{n})),\,\zeta_{n+1}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\bigr\rangle\;\leq\;\frac{\delta(\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|)}{4}\,\alpha_{n}\;+\;L\,\|\zeta_{n+1}-\zeta_{n}\|^{2}.

Since
\nabla\vartheta^{*}
is Lipschitz (by uniform convexity of
\vartheta
) with constant
L
, and using the definitions of
a_{n},b_{n}
, we get

\|\zeta_{n+1}-\zeta_{n}\|\;=\;\bigl\|\nabla\vartheta^{*}(a_{n}+b_{n})-\nabla\vartheta^{*}(a_{n})\bigr\|\;\leq\;L\,\|b_{n}\|\;\leq\;L\,\alpha_{n}\,\|\mho_{n}\|.

Thus

\|\zeta_{n+1}-\zeta_{n}\|^{2}\;\leq\;L^{2}\,\alpha_{n}^{2}\,\|\mho_{n}\|^{2}.

Substitute the provided bounds into (
3
), then take
\mathbb{E}[\cdot\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}]
. Using
\mathbb{E}[\|\mho_{n}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}]\leq\sigma^{2}
and collecting terms yields

\mathbb{E}\bigl[D_{\vartheta}(\zeta_{n+1},\hbar(\zeta_{n+1}))\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigr]+\tfrac{\delta(\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|)}{2}\,\alpha_{n}\;\leq\;\bigl(1+\alpha_{n}^{2}L\bigr)D_{\vartheta}(\zeta_{n},\hbar(\zeta_{n}))+\alpha_{n}^{2}\,\sigma^{2},

as claimed.
∎

Theorem 3.3

(Almost-Sure Convergence)
.

Under
(A1)–(A4)
, the Bregman-
KM
iterates satisfy

\zeta_{n}\;\to\;\zeta^{*}\quad\text{a.s.},

for some fixed point
\zeta^{*}\in\operatorname{Fix}(\hbar)
, and

D_{\vartheta}(\zeta_{n},\hbar(\zeta_{n}))\;\to\;0\quad\text{a.s.}

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
, with

a_{n}=D_{\vartheta}(\zeta_{n},\hbar(\zeta_{n})),\quad b_{n}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\delta\bigl(\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|\bigr)\,\alpha_{n},\quad c_{n}=\alpha_{n}^{2}\,\sigma^{2},

we have almost surely

\mathbb{E}\bigl[a_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigr]+b_{n}\;\leq\;(1+\alpha_{n}^{2}L)\,a_{n}+c_{n}.

Since by assumption
\sum_{n}\alpha_{n}^{2}<\infty
and
\sum_{n}c_{n}=\sigma^{2}\sum_{n}\alpha_{n}^{2}<\infty
, the coefficients in (
3
) satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma
2.7
. Therefore:

a_{n}\;\to\;a_{\infty}\quad\text{a.s.},\quad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}<\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

In particular,
a_{n}
converges almost surely to some nonnegative random variable
a_{\infty}
.

Since

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{2}\,\delta(\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|)\,\alpha_{n}<\infty\quad\text{a.s.},

but
\sum_{n}\alpha_{n}=\infty
, the only way the series can converge is if

\delta\bigl(\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|\bigr)\;\to\;0\quad\text{a.s.}

By uniform convexity of
\vartheta
,
\delta(r)>0
for
r>0
, so
\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|\to 0
and hence

a_{n}=D_{\vartheta}(\zeta_{n},\hbar(\zeta_{n}))\;\to\;0\quad\text{a.s.}

Since
(\zeta_{n})
lives in the reflexive Banach space
\mathcal{X}
and
\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|\to 0
, any weak cluster point
\bar{\zeta}
of
(\zeta_{n})
must satisfy
\bar{\zeta}=\hbar(\bar{\zeta})
, i.e.
\bar{\zeta}\in\operatorname{Fix}(\hbar)
(see
[
3
, Thm. 5.14]
). Furthermore, the Fejér monotonicity induced by the BM-
SKM
update in Bregman distance implies that all cluster points coincide. Therefore the entire sequence
(\zeta_{n})
converges weakly to some
\zeta^{*}\in\operatorname{Fix}(\hbar)
.

If in addition
\mathcal{X}
is uniformly convex (or
\vartheta
is strongly-convex), one can upgrade weak convergence to strong convergence via standard arguments (e.g. Opials Lemma).

Hence
\zeta_{n}\to\zeta^{*}
and
D_{\vartheta}(\zeta_{n},\hbar(\zeta_{n}))\to 0
almost surely, completing the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

If, in addition,
\vartheta
is
2
-uniformly convex (i.e.
\delta(r)\geq\kappa r^{2}
), then

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}\,\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|^{2}<\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

Proof.

From the proof of Theorem
3.3
, we know that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}\;=\;\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2}\,\delta\bigl(\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|\bigr)\,\alpha_{n}\;<\;\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

If, in addition,
\vartheta
is
2
-uniformly convex with modulus
\delta(r)\geq\kappa\,r^{2}
, then

b_{n}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\,\delta\bigl(\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|\bigr)\,\alpha_{n}\;\geq\;\frac{1}{2}\,\kappa\,\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|^{2}\,\alpha_{n}.

Hence

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}\,\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|^{2}\;\leq\;\frac{2}{\kappa}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}\;<\;\infty\quad\text{a.s.},

as claimed.
∎

Example 1

(Entropy-Regularized Q-Learning)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\Delta^{d}
be the probability simplex and
\vartheta(\zeta)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\zeta_{i}\ln\zeta_{i}
. Then Bregman-
SKM
specializes to a stochastic mirror-descent scheme for computing the Q-optimal policy under entropy regularization
[
12
]
. Theorem
3.3
implies almost-sure convergence of the policy iterates.

4.
Non-Asymptotic Residual Bounds in Banach Spaces

In this section we derive explicit non-asymptotic bounds on the Bregman residual

\mathcal{R}_{n}\;=\;D_{\vartheta}\bigl(\zeta_{n},\,\hbar(\zeta_{n})\bigr),

highlighting the role of the uniform convexity modulus
\delta
of
\vartheta
.

Definition 4.1

(Modulus-Dependent Rate Exponent)
.

Let
\vartheta
be uniformly convex with modulus
\delta(r)\geq c\,r^{q}
for some
c>0
and
q\geq 2
. We define the
rate exponent

p\;:=\;\frac{q-1}{q}\,\in\bigl[\tfrac{1}{2},1\bigr).

Definition 4.2

(Residual Averaging)
.

For a window size
N\in\mathbb{N}
, define the averaged residual

\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{N}\;:=\;\frac{1}{A_{N}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\alpha_{n}\,\mathcal{R}_{n},\quad A_{N}:=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\alpha_{n}.

Theorem 4.3

(Non-Asymptotic Bregman-Residual Bound)
.

Under assumptions (A1)–(A4) and if
\delta(r)\geq c\,r^{q}
, then there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that for all
N\geq 1
,

\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{N}\;\leq\;\frac{C_{1}+C_{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\alpha_{n}^{2}}{A_{N}^{p}}\;=\;\mathcal{O}\bigl(A_{N}^{-p}\bigr).

In particular, if
\alpha_{n}=1/n
, then
A_{N}=\Theta(\ln N)
and
\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{N}=O\bigl((\ln N)^{-p}\bigr)
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, write

D_{n}:=D_{\vartheta}(\zeta_{n},\hbar(\zeta_{n})),\quad A_{N}:=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\alpha_{n},\quad\bar{D}_{N}:=\frac{1}{A_{N}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\alpha_{n}D_{n}.

Let
p=(q-1)/q\in[1/2,1)
as in the statement.

Since
\delta(r)\geq c\,r^{q}
and by the Definition of
D_{n}=D_{\vartheta}(\zeta_{n},\hbar(\zeta_{n}))
one shows (e.g. from the midpoint-convexity inequality) that

D_{n}\;\geq\;\delta\bigl(\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|\bigr)\;\geq\;c\,\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|^{q},

hence

\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|^{q}\;\leq\;D_{n}/c.

Lemma
3.2
gives

\mathbb{E}\bigl[D_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigr]+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\delta\bigl(\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|\bigr)\,\alpha_{n}\leq(1+L\alpha_{n}^{2})\,D_{n}+\sigma^{2}\alpha_{n}^{2}.

Substitute
\delta\bigl(\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|\bigr)\geq c\,\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|^{q}\geq c(D_{n}/c)=D_{n}
to obtain

\mathbb{E}\bigl[D_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigr]+\tfrac{1}{2}\,D_{n}\,\alpha_{n}\;\leq\;(1+L\alpha_{n}^{2})\,D_{n}+\sigma^{2}\alpha_{n}^{2}.

Rearrange:

\mathbb{E}\bigl[D_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigr]\;\leq\;\bigl(1-\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha_{n}+L\alpha_{n}^{2}\bigr)\,D_{n}+\sigma^{2}\alpha_{n}^{2}.

Define

E_{n}:=\frac{D_{n}}{A_{n}^{p}},

with the convention
A_{0}=0
,
E_{0}=D_{0}
. Observe that for
p\in(0,1)
and
\alpha_{n}>0
,

\frac{1}{A_{n+1}^{p}}=\frac{1}{(A_{n}+\alpha_{n})^{p}}\geq\frac{1}{A_{n}^{p}}\Bigl[1-p\,\frac{\alpha_{n}}{A_{n}}\Bigr]

by the binomial-type inequality
(1+t)^{-p}\geq 1-p\,t
for
t\geq 0
. Hence

\frac{D_{n+1}}{A_{n+1}^{p}}\leq\bigl(1-\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha_{n}+L\alpha_{n}^{2}\bigr)\,E_{n}\Bigl[1-p\,\tfrac{\alpha_{n}}{A_{n}}\Bigr]\;+\;\sigma^{2}\,\frac{\alpha_{n}^{2}}{A_{n+1}^{p}}.

Using
A_{n}\leq A_{n+1}
and absorbing higher-order
\alpha_{n}^{2}
-terms into constants, there are
C_{1},C_{2}>0
so that

\mathbb{E}\bigl[E_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigr]\;\leq\;E_{n}-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\frac{\alpha_{n}}{A_{n}^{p}}\,D_{n}-p\,\frac{\alpha_{n}}{A_{n}^{p+1}}\,D_{n}+C_{1}\,\alpha_{n}^{2}\,E_{n}+C_{2}\,\frac{\alpha_{n}^{2}}{A_{n}^{p}}.

Noting that
\frac{D_{n}}{A_{n}^{p}}=E_{n}
and
\frac{D_{n}}{A_{n}^{p+1}}=E_{n}/A_{n}
, we get

\mathbb{E}\bigl[E_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigr]\;\leq\;E_{n}\Bigl[1-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\alpha_{n}-p\,\tfrac{\alpha_{n}}{A_{n}}+C_{1}\,\alpha_{n}^{2}\Bigr]\;+\;C_{2}\,\frac{\alpha_{n}^{2}}{A_{n}^{p}}.

Since
\sum_{n}\alpha_{n}=\infty
and
\sum_{n}\alpha_{n}^{2}<\infty
, the negative terms
-\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha_{n}
and
-p\,\alpha_{n}/A_{n}
dominate eventually, yielding a super-martingale structure for
(E_{n})
. An application of a discrete martingale-difference summation argument shows that

E_{N}\;\leq\;E_{0}+C_{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\frac{\alpha_{n}^{2}}{A_{n}^{p}}\;\leq\;\frac{D_{0}}{A_{0}^{p}}+C_{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\frac{\alpha_{n}^{2}}{A_{n}^{p}}.

But
A_{n}\leq A_{N}
for all
n<N
, so

E_{N}\;\leq\;\frac{D_{0}}{A_{N}^{p}}+C_{2}\frac{\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\alpha_{n}^{2}}{A_{N}^{p}}\;=\;\frac{C_{1}+C_{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\alpha_{n}^{2}}{A_{N}^{p}},

where
C_{1}=D_{0}
. Finally, since

\bar{D}_{N}=\frac{1}{A_{N}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\alpha_{n}D_{n}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{A_{N}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\alpha_{n}\,A_{N}^{p}E_{N}=A_{N}^{p}\,E_{N},

we conclude

\bar{D}_{N}\;\leq\;\frac{C_{1}+C_{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\alpha_{n}^{2}}{A_{N}^{p}},

as required. The final statement about
\alpha_{n}=1/n
follows because
A_{N}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}1/n=\Theta(\ln N)
.
∎

Proposition 4.4

(Hilbert-Space Recovery)
.

If
\mathcal{X}
is a Hilbert space and
\vartheta(\zeta)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|\zeta\|^{2}
(so
q=2
,
p=\tfrac{1}{2}
), then

\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{N}=O\bigl(A_{N}^{-1/2}\bigr),

recovering the classical
O(1/\sqrt{n})

SKM
rate
[
9
]
.

Corollary 4.5

(Polynomial Step-Sizes)
.

If
\alpha_{n}=n^{-\gamma}
with
\gamma\in(\tfrac{1}{2},1)
, then

A_{N}\;\approx\;\begin{cases}\tfrac{N^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma},&\gamma<1,\\
\ln N,&\gamma=1,\end{cases}\quad\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{N}=O\bigl(N^{-p(1-\gamma)}\bigr).

Faster decay of
\delta(r)
(larger
q
) yields a better exponent
p\to 1

We now present two major extensions: (i) time-varying Bregman geometries, and (ii) robust
SKM
under heavy-tailed perturbations. Each subsection introduces definitions, algorithmic descriptions, and convergence results.

Definition 5.1

(Adaptive Legendre functions)
.

Let
\{\vartheta_{n}\}
be a sequence of Legendre functions on
\mathcal{X}
with conjugates
\{\vartheta_{n}^{*}\}
. We assume:

(B1)

Each
\vartheta_{n}
is uniformly convex with modulus
\delta_{n}(r)\geq c_{n}\,r^{q_{n}}
.

(B2)

There exist constants
0<\underline{\kappa}\leq\bar{\kappa}<\infty
such that

\underline{\kappa}\,\vartheta(\zeta)\;\leq\;\vartheta_{n}(\zeta)\;\leq\;\bar{\kappa}\,\vartheta(\zeta)\quad\forall\,\zeta\in\mathcal{X},\;\forall n,

where
\vartheta
is a fixed reference Legendre function.

Theorem 5.2

(Convergence of Adaptive Bregman-
SKM
)
.

Under (A1),(A3),(A4) and (B1),(B2), the iterates of Algorithm
1
satisfy

D_{\vartheta_{n}}\bigl(\zeta_{n},\hbar(\zeta_{n})\bigr)\;\to\;0\quad\text{a.s.},\quad\zeta_{n}\;\to\;\zeta^{*}\in\operatorname{Fix}(\hbar)\quad\text{a.s.}

provided
\sum_{n}\alpha_{n}^{2}\bar{\kappa}^{2}<\infty
and
\inf_{n}c_{n}>0
.

Proof.

The proof proceeds in the same spirit as Theorem
3.3
, but with the time-varying Legendre functions
\{\vartheta_{n}\}
and their moduli
\{\delta_{n}\}
.

By the argument of Lemma
3.2
, applied at iteration
n
with the distance-generating function
\vartheta_{n}
, there exist constants
L_{n},\sigma^{2}_{n}>0
(depending on the Lipschitz and noise bounds for
\vartheta_{n}
) such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[D_{\vartheta_{n}}(\zeta_{n+1},\hbar(\zeta_{n+1}))\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigr]

\displaystyle+\;\tfrac{1}{2}\,\delta_{n}\bigl(\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|\bigr)\,\alpha_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\;(1+L_{n}\,\alpha_{n}^{2})\;D_{\vartheta_{n}}(\zeta_{n},\hbar(\zeta_{n}))\;+\;\sigma_{n}^{2}\,\alpha_{n}^{2}.

Define the adapted sequences

a_{n}\;=\;D_{\vartheta_{n}}(\zeta_{n},\hbar(\zeta_{n})),\quad b_{n}\;=\;\tfrac{1}{2}\,\delta_{n}\bigl(\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|\bigr)\,\alpha_{n},\quad c_{n}\;=\;\sigma_{n}^{2}\,\alpha_{n}^{2}.

Then

\mathbb{E}[a_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}]+b_{n}\;\leq\;(1+L_{n}\,\alpha_{n}^{2})\,a_{n}+c_{n}.

By assumption (B2) there is
\bar{\kappa}
such that

\vartheta_{n}(\zeta)\leq\bar{\kappa}\,\vartheta(\zeta)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\nabla\vartheta_{n},\;\nabla\vartheta_{n}^{*}\text{ are Lipschitz with constant }\bar{\kappa}\,L,

and the noise bound gives
\sigma_{n}^{2}\leq\bar{\kappa}^{2}\,\sigma^{2}
. Thus we may take

L_{n}\leq\bar{\kappa}^{2}\,L,\quad\sigma_{n}^{2}\leq\bar{\kappa}^{2}\,\sigma^{2},

so

\mathbb{E}[a_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}]+b_{n}\;\leq\;\bigl(1+(\bar{\kappa}^{2}L)\,\alpha_{n}^{2}\bigr)\,a_{n}+(\bar{\kappa}^{2}\sigma^{2})\,\alpha_{n}^{2}.

Since
\sum_{n}\alpha_{n}^{2}<\infty
and
\bar{\kappa}^{2}L
is constant, the Robbins-Siegmund Lemma (Lemma
2.7
) applies, yielding

a_{n}\;\to\;a_{\infty}\quad\text{a.s.},\qquad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}<\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

By (B1),
\inf_{n}c_{n}>0
, so each
\delta_{n}(r)\geq c_{n}\,r^{q_{n}}
with
c_{n}\geq\underline{c}>0
. Then

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{2}\,\delta_{n}(\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|)\,\alpha_{n}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\,\underline{c}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|^{q_{n}}\,\alpha_{n},

which can converge only if
\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|\to 0
a.s. Hence
a_{n}=D_{\vartheta_{n}}(\zeta_{n},\hbar(\zeta_{n}))\to 0
a.s.

Since
\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|\to 0
, any weak cluster point
\bar{\zeta}
satisfies
\bar{\zeta}=\hbar(\bar{\zeta})
, so
\bar{\zeta}\in\operatorname{Fix}(\mathcal{hbar})
. Moreover, the Bregman-Fejér monotonicity

D_{\vartheta_{n}}(\zeta_{n+1},\zeta^{*})\leq D_{\vartheta_{n}}(\zeta_{n},\zeta^{*})

for any fixed
\zeta^{*}\in\operatorname{Fix}(\hbar)
(by nonexpansivity and three-point identity) ensures all cluster points coincide. Reflexivity of
\mathcal{X}
then gives

\zeta_{n}\;\rightharpoonup\;\zeta^{*}\quad\text{and hence}\quad\zeta_{n}\to\zeta^{*},

where strong convergence follows under uniform convexity of
\mathcal{X}
or
\vartheta_{n}
.

Thus
D_{\vartheta_{n}}(\zeta_{n},\hbar(\zeta_{n}))\to 0
and
\zeta_{n}\to\zeta^{*}\in\operatorname{Fix}(\hbar)
almost surely.
∎

Adaptive geometries can track local curvature or empirically estimated Hessian information (e.g. quasi-Newton style), offering potential acceleration without sacrificing convergence guarantees.

(Convergence with Trimming)
.

Under (A1),(A3), Assumption
5
, and if

2
converge a.s. to

Proof.

We adapt the proof of Theorem
3.3
to the trimmed-noise case.

By Assumption, there is
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and
M<\infty
such that

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\mho_{n+1}\|_{*}^{1+\epsilon}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigr]\leq M\quad\text{a.s.}

Let
\tilde{\mho}_{n}=\operatorname{Trim}_{k_{n}}(\mho_{n})
zero out the
k_{n}
largest-magnitude coordinates of
\mho_{n}
. Then, writing
\mho_{n}=\tilde{\mho}_{n}+r_{n}
with

the removed remainder, one shows by standard order-statistic / Markov-inequality arguments that there exists
\delta>0
and

so that

(4)

The same argument as in Lemma
3.2
gives, for some constants

\mathbb{E}\bigl[D_{\vartheta}(\zeta_{n+1},\hbar(\zeta_{n+1}))\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigr]+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\delta\bigl(\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|\bigr)\,\alpha_{n}\;\leq\;\bigl(1+L\,\alpha_{n}^{2}\bigr)D_{\vartheta}(\zeta_{n},\hbar(\zeta_{n}))+\alpha_{n}^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\tilde{\mho}_{n}\|_{*}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigr].

4

\mathbb{E}\bigl[D_{\vartheta}(\zeta_{n+1},\hbar(\zeta_{n+1}))\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigr]+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\delta(\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|)\,\alpha_{n}\;\leq\;\bigl(1+L\alpha_{n}^{2}\bigr)\,D_{\vartheta}(\zeta_{n},\hbar(\zeta_{n}))\;+\;C\,\alpha_{n}^{2}\,n^{-\delta}.

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}^{2}\,n^{-\delta}\;<\;\infty.

Hence by defining

a_{n}=D_{\vartheta}(\zeta_{n},\hbar(\zeta_{n})),\quad b_{n}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\delta(\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|)\,\alpha_{n},\quad c_{n}=C\,\alpha_{n}^{2}\,n^{-\delta},

a_{n}=D_{\vartheta}(\zeta_{n},\hbar(\zeta_{n}))\;\to\;0\quad\text{a.s.},\qquad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}<\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

\|\zeta_{n}-\hbar(\zeta_{n})\|\to 0

7.
Conclusion and Future Directions

In this work, we have introduced and analyzed a novel stochastic Krasnosel skiĭ-Mann iteration in reflexive Banach spaces driven by Bregman distances. In theis paper, we formulated the stochastic fixed-point iteration using a general Legendre function and proved almost-sure convergence under standard martingale-difference noise (Theorem
3.3
). By exploiting the modulus of uniform convexity, we derived explicit
O(A_{N}^{-p})
bounds on the averaged Bregman residual for
\delta(r)\geq c\,r^{q}
(Theorem
4.3
). We extended the framework to time-varying Bregman geometries (Theorem
5.2
) and heavy-tailed noise with trimming (Proposition
5.4
), demonstrating the flexibility of Bregman-
SKM
.
Experiments on entropy-regularized policy iteration and heavy-tailed noise confirm that Bregman-
SKM
outperforms classical SKM and remains robust under non-Gaussian perturbations.

Several promising avenues remain open:

Inertial and Variance-Reduced Hybrids:
Incorporating momentum or SVRG-style variance reduction into Bregman-
SKM
could yield faster rates, but requires new analysis in non-Euclidean settings.

Decentralized and Asynchronous
SKM
:
Extending to networked or delayed environments would broaden applications in distributed optimization and multi-agent RL.

Beyond Reflexivity:
Addressing non-reflexive Banach spaces or quasi-Banach settings may handle more general regularizers and loss functions.

Adaptive Distance Learning:
Online adaptation of the generating function
\vartheta_{n}
based on curvature estimates could further accelerate convergence in practice.

We anticipate that the Bregman-
SKM
framework will serve as a foundation for future advances in stochastic fixed-point algorithms, with applications ranging from reinforcement learning to large-scale inverse problems.

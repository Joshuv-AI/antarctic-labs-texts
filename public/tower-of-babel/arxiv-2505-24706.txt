The quantitative semi-classical limit of a large Fermi system at zero temperature
Esteban Cárdenas

Abstract
. In this article we consider a large system of fermions in a combined mean-field and semi-classical limit, in three dimensions. We investigate the convergence of the Wigner function of the ground state, towards the classical Thomas-Fermi theory. The main novelty of the present article is quantifying the convergence rate with respect to the semi-classical parameter. One of the main ingredients is a recent result on the validity of semi-classical commutator estimates satisfied by the Hartree theory. Singular potentials, up to the Coulomb interaction, are included.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Introduction

In this article, we consider
N
(spinless) fermions moving in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The Hilbert space of the system is the antisymmetric tensor product.

\mathscr{H}_{N}\equiv\bigwedge^{N}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ .

(1.1)

We
consider the
N
-body Hamiltonian
with a combined mean-field and semi-classical scaling

{\rm H}_{N}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{x_{i}}+U(x_{i})\Big)+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i<j}V(x_{i}-x_{j})\ ,\qquad\hbar\equiv N^{-1/d}\,.

(1.2)

Here
U:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

and
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

account for an external trapping potential, and a pair potential.
They are assumed to be sufficiently regular, so
that
{\rm H}_{N}
is self-adjoint in its natural domain.
The parameter
\hbar\in(0,1)
can be understood as an
effective Planck constant, and the scaling is chosen
so that the kinetic energy per particle remains bounded.
In particular, the present
N
-body problem is naturally linked
with a semi-classical limit
\hbar\rightarrow 0.

Let
\Psi\in\mathscr{H}_{N}
be a normalized state.
We define the one-particle reduced density matrix
as the operator

\gamma_{\Psi}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

with kernel

\displaystyle\gamma_{\Psi}(x,y)\equiv N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d(N-1)}}\Psi(x,x_{2},\cdots x_{N})\overline{\Psi}(y,x_{2},\cdots,x_{N})dx_{2}\cdots dx_{N}\ ,

(1.3)

here
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
In particular,
\gamma_{\Psi}
is self-adjoint, trace-class, and
verifies

0\leqslant\gamma_{\Psi}\leqslant\bm{1}\ .

(1.4)

For short, we refer
to an operator
\gamma

which verifies these properties
a
density matrix
.
Here, we have the normalization

\textnormal{Tr}\gamma_{\Psi}=N

and we call
\varrho_{\Psi}(x)=N^{-1}\gamma_{\Psi}(x,x)
the position density.
We are interested in the limit of
\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}

as
N\rightarrow\infty

when
\Psi_{N}
is an
approximate ground state of
{\rm H}_{N}

\left\langle\Psi_{N},{\rm H}_{N}\Psi_{N}\right\rangle=\inf\sigma_{\mathscr{H}_{N}}({\rm H}_{N})+N\varepsilon_{N}\ ,

(1.5)

for a sequence
\varepsilon_{N}\rightarrow 0

at a certain rate.

Due to the semi-classical nature of the scaling under consideration, the limit of
\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}

is better understood in terms
of its Wigner function.
This is the real-valued function on phase space
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

defined
for a general density matrix as

f_{\gamma}(x,p)\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\gamma\big(x+\tfrac{1}{2}y\,,\,x-\tfrac{1}{2}y\big)e^{-i\hbar^{-1}y\cdot p}dy\ ,

(1.6)

with
normalization

(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}f_{\gamma}(x,p)dxdp=1.

We also
introduce
the Weyl quantization map,
which assigns to a real-valued function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

the self-adjoint operator
\gamma_{f}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with kernel

\gamma_{f}(x,y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi\hbar)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\left(\frac{x+y}{2},p\right)e^{-i\hbar^{-1}p\cdot(x-y)}dp\ .

(1.7)

It is well-known
[
11
]
that under very general conditions,
the large
N
limit
of
f_{\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}}

can be described by Thomas-Fermi theory.
More precisely,
the limit function on phase space
corresponds to the generalized Fermi ball

f_{\rm TF}(x,p)\equiv\mathds{1}(|p|^{2}\leqslant{\rm C}_{\rm TF}\,\varrho_{\rm TF}(x)^{2/d})

(1.8)

where

{\rm C}_{\rm TF}\equiv 4\pi^{2}|B_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(0,1)|^{-2/d}

is a constant,
and
\varrho_{\rm TF}(x)
is
a measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which verifies the Thomas-Fermi equation

\varrho_{\rm TF}(x)={\rm C}_{\rm TF}^{-d/2}\big(U(x)+V*\varrho_{\rm TF}(x)-\mu\big)_{-}^{d/2}\ .

(1.9)

Here,
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
is a Lagrange multiplier (chemical potential)
which guarantees the normalization
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varrho_{\rm TF}(x)dx=1
.
In particular,
\varrho_{\rm TF}
is the minimizer
of the Thomas-Fermi functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rm TF}(\varrho)

(1.10)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{d\,{\rm C}_{\rm TF}}{d+2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varrho(x)^{1+\frac{2}{d}}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}U(x)\varrho(x)dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varrho(x)V(x-y)\varrho(y)dxdy

which is minimized
over all
0\leqslant\varrho\in L^{1}\cap L^{\frac{d+2}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

of unit mass.

The validity of the limit
f_{\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}}\rightarrow f_{\rm TF}

is often refered to as
convergence of states
,
and was proven in great generality
by Lewin, Fournais and Solovej
[
11
]
,
for a wide variety of potentials (
U,V
), in any dimension
d\geqslant 1
,
with magnetic fields,
and also for higher-order marginals.
This is contrast to the
convergence of the position densities
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\varrho_{\Psi_{N}}=\varrho_{\rm TF},

whose analysis goes back to the pioneering works of Lieb and Simon
[
18
,
19
]
.
In particular, the convergence of states addressed in
[
11
]
was proven
in the following topology (assuming here
uniqueness of minimizers)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\langle\varphi,f_{\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}}\rangle=\left\langle\varphi,f_{{\rm TF}}\right\rangle\ ,\qquad\forall\varphi\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\ .

(1.11)

The mode of convergence was then improved by the author
[
7
]
,
and it was shown that (
1.11
) holds
in the strong sense, with respect to various norms of interest.
For instance, for all
s<0

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\|f_{\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}}-f_{{\rm TF}}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}=0\ ,

(1.12)

although with slightly stronger conditions on the pair
(U,V)
.
We would also like to mention
that the convergence of states obtained in
[
11
]
has been extended in various other directions:

[
17
]
considered a system of fermions at positive temperature;

[
12
]
studied various scalings for the magnetic field;
and
[
13
]

analyzed the case of anyons with almost fermionic statistics.
We would also like to mention
[
14
]
which considers the convergence of the position densities in the sense of
\Gamma
convergence.

The main goal of this article
is to investigate the
quantitative convergence of states
.
As a corollary of our main result in Theorem
1
we obtain the
L^{2}
convergence

\|f_{\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}}-f_{\rm TF}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leqslant C\hbar^{\delta/2}|\!\ln\hbar|^{1/4}

(1.13)

in terms of an explicit power
\delta>0
, where up to Coulomb interactions
V(x)=|x|^{-1}
are included.
To the authors best knowledge,
there are no previous results in the literature which are quantitative in the semi-classical parameter.
In particular, previous work on the convergence of states
[
11
,
12
,
17
,
13
,
7
]

relies on compactness arguments and, therefore, are not suitable for proving quantitative estimates.
The only exception corresponds to systems on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
with no external trap,
for which the problem becomes translation invariant
\rho_{\rm TF}=1
. See e.g.
[
5
,
10
]
for Fermi systems
in the random phase approximation.
Additionally,
in the context of large atomic/molecular Coulomb systems,
related estimates have been employed with the purpose
of proving relevant energy asymptotics. See
for instance
[
15
, Theorem 1.6]
.
Here, however, it is sufficient to analyze
the difference between the position densities of a
mean-field density matrix (trial state)
and the Thomas-Fermi theory; no information about the
N
-body is deduced.

1.2.
Semi-classical commutator estimates and Hartree theory

One of the key challenges regarding the problem under consideration
is the lack of high regularity of the limit (
2.4
),
given by a characteristic function.
In addition, in order to quantify
the convergence as
N\rightarrow\infty

it becomes crucial to understand the uniform-in-
N
regularity
at the quantum level.

In this regard, there is an extremely helpful notion
that we may use to understand such properties.
Namely, we say

(\gamma_{N})_{N\geqslant 1}

satisfies the

semi-classical commutator estimates
if
there is
C>0
such that for all
N\geqslant 1

\|[x,\gamma_{N}]\|_{\textnormal{Tr}}\leqslant CN\hbar\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\|[-i\hbar\nabla,\gamma_{N}]\|_{\textnormal{Tr}}\ \leqslant CN\hbar\ ,

(1.14)

where
\|A\|_{\textnormal{Tr}}=\textnormal{Tr}|A|
is the trace norm for operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The bounds (
1.14
)
where introduced
as key requirements in the initial data by
Benedikter, Porta and Schlein
[
2
]
,
in the context of the derivation
of Hartree-Fock dynamics for large fermionic systems.
Physically, these bounds encode the semi-classical structure of the states, meaning they can only vary over macroscopic scales.
Mathematically, they contain information about the uniform regularity of the density matrix
\gamma_{N}
.
For instance, for orthogonal projections
\gamma_{N}=\gamma_{N}^{2}

they imply the

optimal
fractional regularity of the Wigner functions in the Sobolev scale

\sup_{N\geqslant 1}\|f_{\gamma_{N}}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty\ ,\qquad\forall s<\tfrac{1}{2}\ .

(1.15)

We refer the reader to the article
[
16
]
by Lafleche for more details
on the regularity of orthogonal projections verifying the semi-classical commutator estimates.

In this article, our strategy
is to approximate
the reduced density matrix
of
\Psi_{N}
with an effective one-body problem.
More precisely,
for the chemical potential
\mu\in\mathbb{R}

introduced in (
1.9
)
we define
the
Hartree functional
as

\mathscr{E}_{N}(\gamma)\equiv\textnormal{Tr}\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta+U(x)-\mu\right)\gamma+\frac{N}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varrho_{\gamma}(x)V(x-y)\varrho_{\gamma}(y)dxdy

(1.16)

where
\gamma
is a
trace-class
operator which verifies
0\leqslant\gamma\leqslant\bm{1}
,
and we denote

\varrho_{\gamma}(x)=N^{-1}\gamma(x,x)
the associated position density.
We will regard its minimizers
as an intermediate step between the full
N
-body problem,
and Thomas-Fermi theory.
The argument employed here is an adaptation of the one presented for the time-dependent case
[
2
]
,
and uses
the second quantization formalism.
There are, however, some key differences
outlined in Subsection
2.2
.

Let us denote by
\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}
a minimizer of
\mathscr{E}_{N}
.
Various of its properties
have been studied
by Nguyen
[
22
]
.
In particular
one may always find a minimizer that verifies
the fixed point equation
1
1

1

For convenience, our functional is different by an overall factor
\hbar^{d}

\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}=\mathds{1}_{(-\infty,\mu]}\big(H_{\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}}\big)

(1.17)

where, for an arbitrary density matrix
\gamma
,
the operator defined by

H_{\gamma}\equiv-\hbar^{2}\Delta+U+V*\varrho_{\gamma}

(1.18)

is called the
Hartree Hamiltonian
.
More recently, Lafleche and the author
[
8
]

analyzed various additional quantitative properties
which will be key ingredients for the proof of our main result:

•

First, the semi-classical commutator estimates

\|[x,\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}]\|_{\textnormal{Tr}}\leqslant CN\hbar\ ,\qquad\|[-i\hbar\nabla,\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}]\|_{\textnormal{Tr}}\ \leqslant CN\sqrt{|\!\ln\hbar|}\,\hbar\ ,

(1.19)

hold for a class
of potentials including repulsive Coulomb interactions.
Verifying these estimates
in practice is highly non-trivial.
They were first verified
for the free Fermi ball on the torus
[
2
]
,
and then for
spectral projections
of Schrödinger operators
with smooth potentials
[
20
]
.
See also
[
1
,
16
]

for explicit calculations for the harmonic oscillator, and
[
6
]

for the inclusion of smooth magnetic fields.
Only recently,
[
8
]

they were verified for
Schrödinger operators with potentials in the class
C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
which is the required regularity to analyze
a mean-field potential

\varrho*|x|^{-1}
with density
\varrho\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

•

Secondly, the quantitative convergence of states in trace topology,
from Hartree to Thomas-Fermi

\|\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}-\gamma_{f_{\rm TF}}\|_{\textnormal{Tr}}\leqslant CN\hbar^{1/2}|\!\ln\hbar|^{1/2}

(1.20)

Previously
[
11
,
22
]
similar results in this direction
were obtained, although in weaker topologies, and with no convergence rates.
The proof of (
1.20
)
is based on a variational argument, combined with the uniform-in-
N
regularity of
f_{\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}}
,
inherited from the commutator estimates.

Our main result is contained in Theorem
1
.
In summary, it applies the new results
[
8
]
to establish the quantitative convergence of
states
f_{\Psi_{N}}\rightarrow f_{\rm TF}

in three dimensions,
with respect
to the semi-classical parameter
\hbar=N^{-1/3}
.

Organization of this article
.
In Section
2

we state the main result of this article, Theorem
1
.
In Section
3

we collect some preliminary results that we need in our anaysis.
Next, in Section
4

we establish operator estimates in Fock space for regular potentails, and in Section

Finally, we prove the main result in Section

.

2.
Main results

2.1.
Statements

Let us now turn to the statements of our main results.
We assume that
(U,V)
satisfy the following assumptions.

Condition 1
.

We assume that

U\in C^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}U(x)=\infty

and,
additionally, that for some

\beta>0

\|e^{-\beta|x|}D^{2}U\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
.
The pair potential is

V(x)=\lambda|x|^{-a}

(2.1)

for
\lambda>0
and
a\in(0,1].

The main result of this paper is now contained in the following theorem,
where we consider the three dimensional case.

Theorem 1

(Quantitative convergence of states)
.

Let
\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}

be the one-particle reduced density matrix
of an
approximate ground state
with rate
\varepsilon_{N}=O(N^{-1/6})
,
and let

\gamma_{\rm TF}\equiv\gamma_{f_{\rm TF}}
be the
Weyl quantization
of the Thomas-Fermi state
f_{\rm TF}
in (
1.8
).
Assume
(U,V)
verifies Condition
1
.
Then, for
d=3

there exists
C>0
such that for all
N\geqslant 1

\displaystyle\|\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}-\gamma_{\rm TF}\|_{\textnormal{Tr}}\leqslant CN\hbar^{\delta}|\!\ln\hbar|^{1/4}\ ,

(2.2)

where the power is given by

\delta=\frac{1}{2}\min(\frac{6-5a}{16+5a},\frac{4+15a}{2(16+5a)}).

Additionally, if
a<1
, the logarithmic factor may be ommitted.

Remark 2.1
.

Let us recall that
\textnormal{Tr}\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}=N
.
Thus, inequality (
2.2
)
establishes a relative error of size
O(\hbar^{\delta})
.
In addition,
the trace norm topology
guarantees various consequences
regarding the convergence of the Wigner function

f_{N}\equiv f_{\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}}

and the position density
\varrho_{N}\equiv\varrho_{\Psi_{N}}
.
First,
a standard argument using the unitarity of the Wigner transformation
\gamma(x,y)\in L^{2}\mapsto f_{\gamma}(x,p)\in L^{2}

as well as the bound

\|\gamma\|_{\rm HS}\leqslant\|\gamma\|_{\textnormal{Tr}}^{1/2}

for fermions, implies the quantitative convergence

\displaystyle\|f_{N}-f_{\rm TF}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\hbar^{\delta/2}|\!\ln\hbar|^{1/8}\ .

(2.3)

Using Gronewold’s formula

\widehat{f}_{\gamma}(\xi,\eta)=\tfrac{1}{N}\textnormal{Tr}e^{i\xi\cdot x+i\eta\cdot\textbf{p}}\gamma

for
\textbf{p}=-i\hbar\nabla
,
we also obtain
the convergence in the
L^{\infty}
norm in Fourier space

\|\widehat{f}_{N}-\widehat{f}_{\rm TF}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant C\hbar^{\delta}|\!\ln\hbar|^{1/4}\ ,

(2.4)

which can be understood as a weaker analogue of the
L^{1}
norm.
We also note that
it is a standard exercise
to show that the convergence in trace norm
implies the
L^{1}
convergence of the position densities

\|\varrho_{N}-\varrho_{\rm TF}\|_{L^{1}}\leqslant C\hbar^{\delta}|\!\ln\hbar|^{1/4}

(2.5)

and also similarly for the momentum densities.
Currently, it is unclear if
the bounds in this article can be
satisfactorily
generalized control the higher-order marginals
\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}^{(k)}
,
and we leave this problem for future research.

Remark 2.2
.

We do not expect
\delta
to be optimal in any sense. Its form is a consequence of the methods used here to treat singular potentials,
and we have not tried to find the optimal power.

Remark 2.3
.

In principle, the proof can be extended
to super Coulombic potentials in the range
1<a<6/5
,
as one would still formally have a positive power
\delta>0
.
Unfortunately, it is not currently known if the Hartree minimizers
\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}
with such singular potentials
satisfy the necessary properties required
in our proof.

Remark 2.4
.

From the proof, we deduce it is possible
to prove a better result for regular pair potentials

V\in C_{\rm loc}^{1,1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
which verify the assumptions

\widehat{V}(\xi)\geqslant 0\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\|V\|\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\widehat{V}(\xi)(1+|\xi|)d\xi<\infty\ .

(2.6)

Namely, that (
2.2
)
holds with
\delta=\frac{1}{2}
.
In particular, potentials in this class
may be added to
{\rm H}_{N}

without modifying the result of Theorem
1
.

2.2.
Outline of the proof

The proof of Theorem
1

contains two parts, which we explain below.

(i)
Denote by
\gamma_{N}
the
reduced density matrix of
\Psi_{N}
,
and
\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}

the minimizer of the Hartree functional.
Then, we devote much attention
to using the second quantization formalism to prove that

\|\gamma_{N}-\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}\|_{\textnormal{Tr}}\leqslant CN\hbar^{\delta}|\!\ln\hbar|^{1/4}\ ,

(2.7)

see Theorem
2
for the corresponding number estimates.
Here, the validity of the commutator estimates for
\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}
is crucial.
Our strategy is to adapt
the argument that
was developed to treat the time-dependent case
[
2
]

with regular potentials.
There are two critical differences, however.

•

In order to include singular interactions,
the argument requires an additional ultraviolet
regularization of the pair potential
V_{\Lambda}(x)
.
In particular,
the regularization is chosen so that
it preserves the non-negativity of
\widehat{V}_{\Lambda}(\xi)\geqslant 0

that we use in various lower bounds.
The tail of the regularization
is controlled thanks to the embedding

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{1+\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

for
s=\tfrac{d}{2}\frac{d-2}{d+2}

and the Lieb-Thirring inequality, in
d=3.

•

While in the time-dependent case
[
2
]
, the strategy is to
use the Gronwall inequality to control the size of the
number operator in Fock space, (see Section
3
for the definitions),
a dynamical argument is not available in our setting.
This problem is resolved thanks to the following operator inequality

\mathcal{N}\leqslant\frac{1}{\varepsilon}d\Gamma(|H_{\gamma}-\mu|)+\bm{1}_{\mathscr{F}}\,\textnormal{Tr}\mathds{1}_{|H_{\gamma}-\mu|\leqslant\varepsilon}\,\ ,\qquad\forall\varepsilon>0\ ,

(2.8)

understood in the sense of quadratic forms in Fock space
\mathscr{F}
.
Here,
\mathcal{N}
is the number operator, and

d\Gamma(A)
is the second quantization operator.
We observe that similar inequalities have been used
in the torus setting
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, see e.g.
[
5
,
9
,
10
]
.
Here
|H_{\gamma}-\mu|

is replaced by
the free excitation energy relative
to the Fermi ball

|-\hbar^{2}\Delta-\mu|
.
In particular, on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
one has the advantage
of having a spectral gap of order
O(\hbar^{2})
which,
with appropriate choice of
(\mu,\varepsilon)
,
can completely remove
the second term in (
2.8
).
In our analysis, it has to be controlled with state of the art,
sharp semi-classical methods.
Indeed,
the final crucial ingredient in our analysis
is the validity of the
optimal
Weyl’s law in
d=3

for non-smooth
potentials,
proven only recently by Mikkelsen
[
21
]
. It states that

\textnormal{Tr}\mathds{1}_{(-\infty,0]}\big(-\hbar^{2}\Delta+W(x)\big)=\frac{|B_{3}|}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}W_{-}(x)^{3/2}dx+O(\hbar^{-2})\ .

(2.9)

We will repeatedly apply it (in various forms)
for the Hartree Hamiltonian
with singular interactions. In particular, it controls
the trace in (
2.8
)
by a small error
CN(\varepsilon+\hbar)
.
The parameters
(\varepsilon,\Lambda)
are optimized at the end of the calculations.

(ii)
For the second step,
we invoke the convergence result
[
8
, Theorem 1.6]

which states the validity of the estimate

\|\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}-\gamma_{\rm TF}\|_{\textnormal{Tr}}\leqslant CN\sqrt{|\!\ln\hbar|}\,\hbar^{1/2}\ .

(2.10)

There, a variational argument combined with
the semi-classical commutator estimates were employed.
In our case
\delta<1/2
is always the worst power.

3.
Preliminaries

3.1.
Second quantization

It is extremely convenient
to work on the second quantization formalism.
To this end, we define the
fermionic Fock space over
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\mathscr{F}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathscr{F}^{(n)}\quad\textnormal{where}\quad\mathscr{F}^{(0)}\equiv\mathbb{C}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\mathscr{F}^{(n)}\equiv\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ \forall n\geqslant 1,

(3.1)

we denote by
\Omega_{0}=(1,\textbf{0})
the vacuum vector.
Note
\mathscr{H}_{N}=\mathscr{F}^{(N)}
.
The space
\mathscr{F}
becomes a Hilbert space with the inner product

\left\langle\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{F}}\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle\,\Psi_{1}^{(n)},\Psi_{2}^{(n)}\,\rangle_{\mathscr{F}_{F}^{(n)}}\ ,\qquad\forall\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2}\in\mathscr{F}\ .

(3.2)

On the Fock space
\mathscr{F}
one introduces the smeared-out
creation- and annihilation operators
as follows.
Given
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
we let
a^{*}(f)
and
a(f)

be defined for
\Psi\in\mathscr{F}
as

\displaystyle\big(a^{*}(f)\Psi\big)^{(n)}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}f(x_{i})\Psi^{(n-1)}(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{i},\ldots,x_{n})\ ,

\displaystyle\big(a(f)\Psi\big)^{(n)}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle\equiv\sqrt{n+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{f(x)}\Psi^{(n+1)}(x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})dx\ ,

(3.3)

where
\widehat{x}_{i}
means ommission of the
i
-th variable.
In particular, they satisfy
the following version of the CAR

\{a(f),a^{*}(g)\}=\left\langle f,g\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\{a^{\hash}(f),a^{\hash}(g)\}=0

(3.4)

where we recall
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
stands for the anticommutator.
In particular, it is easy to see that
the CAR turns them into bounded operators, with norms

\|a^{*}(f)\|=\|a(f)\|=\|f\|_{L^{2}}\ .

(3.5)

Let us finally mention that the connection with the operator-valued distributions
a_{x}^{*}
and
a_{x}
is by means of the formulae

a^{*}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)a_{x}^{*}dx\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad a(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{f(x)}a_{x}dx\ .

Given a one-particle operator
A
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

with kernel
A(x,y)
one defines its
second quantization
d\Gamma(A)

as the operator on Fock space
\mathscr{F}

d\Gamma(A)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}A(x,y)a_{x}^{*}a_{y}dxdy\ .

(3.6)

We let the number operator be

\mathcal{N}\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a_{x}^{*}a_{x}\,dx

(3.7)

corresponding to the second quantization
of the identity
d\Gamma(\bm{1}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})})
.

We record here some useful estimates which are well-known.
See e.g.
[
2
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a bounded operator, with operator norm
\|A\|
.

(1)

For all
\Psi,\Phi\in\mathscr{F}

\displaystyle\|d\Gamma(A)\Psi\|_{\mathscr{F}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|A\|\|\mathcal{N}\Psi\|_{\mathscr{F}}\ ,

(3.8)

\displaystyle|\left\langle\Psi,d\Gamma(A)\Phi\right\rangle_{\mathscr{F}}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|A\|\|\mathcal{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi\|_{\mathscr{F}}\|\mathcal{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Phi\|_{\mathscr{F}}\ .

(3.9)

(2)

If
A
is Hilbert-Schmidt, then for all

\Psi\in\mathscr{F}

\displaystyle\|d\Gamma(A)\Psi\|_{\mathscr{F}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|A\|_{\rm HS}\|\mathcal{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi\|_{\mathscr{F}}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}A(x,y)a_{x}a_{y}dxdy\,\Psi\|_{\mathscr{F}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|A\|_{\rm HS}\|\mathcal{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi\|_{\mathscr{F}}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}A(x,y)a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}dxdy\,\Psi\|_{\mathscr{F}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|A\|_{\rm HS}\|(\mathcal{N}+\bm{1})^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi\|_{\mathscr{F}}\ .

(3.12)

(3)

If
A
is trace-class, then for all

\Psi\in\mathscr{F}

\displaystyle\|d\Gamma(A)\Psi\|_{\mathscr{F}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|A\|_{\textnormal{Tr}}\|\Psi\|_{\mathscr{F}}

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}A(x,y)a_{x}a_{y}dxdy\,\Psi\|_{\mathscr{F}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|A\|_{\textnormal{Tr}}\|\Psi\|_{\mathscr{F}}

(3.14)

\displaystyle\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}A(x,y)a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}dxdy\,\Psi\|_{\mathscr{F}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|A\|_{\textnormal{Tr}}\|\Psi\|_{\mathscr{F}}\ .

(3.15)

We will need to construct
the following
particle-hole transformation

which implements a change of variables
relative to a reference density matrix
\gamma
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\gamma=\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant M}\ket{f_{i}}\bra{f_{i}}
be a density matrix on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
with
M=\textnormal{Tr}\gamma\in\mathbb{N}
.
Define the operators

u=\bm{1}-\gamma\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\textstyle v=\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant M}\ket{\overline{f}_{i}}\bra{f_{i}}\ .

(3.16)

Then,
there is a unitary map

\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}(\gamma):\mathscr{F}\rightarrow\mathscr{F}

such that for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{*}\ a^{*}(f)\ \mathcal{R}=a^{*}(uf)+a(\overline{vf})\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\mathcal{R}^{*}\ a(f)\ \mathcal{R}=a(uf)+a^{*}(\overline{vf})\ .

and

\mathcal{R}\Omega_{0}=\bigwedge_{1\leqslant i\leqslant M}a^{*}(f_{i})\Omega_{0}\ .

Remark 3.1
.

In terms of the operator kernels

u_{y}(x)\equiv u(x,y)
and
v_{y}(x)\equiv v(x,y)

it is common to write the conjugation relation in distributional form

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{*}\ a^{*}_{x}\ \mathcal{R}=a^{*}(u_{x})+a(\overline{v_{x}})\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\mathcal{R}^{*}\ a_{x}\ \mathcal{R}=a(u_{x})+a^{*}(\overline{v_{x}})

(3.17)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The proof of the lemma follows
from the relations
u^{*}u+u^{*}u=0
,
uv^{*}=vu=0

and
\|v\|_{\rm HS}<\infty
(the Shale-Stinespring condition).
We refer the reader to
[
2
]
for more details
on the implementation of Bogoliubov transformations.

3.2.
Properties of the Hartree states

In this subsection, we collect some results regarding the minimizers

\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}

of the Hartree functional
\mathscr{E}_{N}
,
with chemical potential
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
.
These properties are all we need for the proof of Theorem
1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
U\in C^{2}_{\rm loc}
satisfy
\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}U(x)=\infty

and assume for some
\beta>0

that
e^{-\beta|x|}D^{2}U(x)
is bounded.
Consider the case
V(x)=\lambda|x|^{-a}

where
\lambda>0
and
a\in(0,1]
.
Then, there is a constant
C>0
such that the following holds
in
d=3
.

(1)

For all
N\geqslant 1
there holds

\|[x,\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}]\|_{\textnormal{Tr}}\leqslant CN\hbar\ .

(3.18)

(2)

For all
N\geqslant 1
the uniform estimates hold for all
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty

\textnormal{Tr}(-\hbar^{2}\Delta)\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}\leqslant CN\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\|\varrho_{\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}}\|_{L^{p}}\leqslant C\ .

(3.19)

(3)

Let
\gamma_{N}^{\rm TF}
be the Weyl quantization
of
f_{\rm TF}
. Then, for all
N\geqslant 1

\|\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}-\gamma_{N}^{\rm TF}\|_{\textnormal{Tr}}\leqslant CN\hbar^{1/2}|\ln\hbar|^{1/2}

(3.20)

(4)

For all
N\geqslant 1

|\textnormal{Tr}\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}-N|\leqslant CN\hbar^{1/2}|\ln\hbar|^{1/2}\ .

(3.21)

Remark 3.2
.

A well-known consequence of the first bound of (
3.18
)
is that for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\|[e^{i\xi\cdot x},\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}]\|_{\textnormal{Tr}}\leqslant C_{0}N\hbar|\xi|

(3.22)

for a constant
C_{0}>0
, see e.g.
[
20
]
.
The commutator estimate
with
-i\hbar\nabla

also holds,
altough one needs to include
a correction
|\!\ln\hbar|^{\frac{1}{2}}
if
a=1
.
The bound (
3.22
) is, however, all we need in our analysis.

Remark 3.3
.

For
a<1
the
|\!\ln\hbar|^{\frac{1}{2}}
factor
in items (3) and (4) may be ommitted. See e.g.
[
8
, Theorem 1.5]
.

Remark 3.4
.

We employ several results from
[
8
]
for the minimizer
\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}

of the Hartree functional
\mathscr{E}_{N}
.
Let us point out that the functional defined here is different up to an overall factor

(2\pi\hbar)^{d}
.
Additionally, the scaling chosen
in
[
8
]

for the densities is
\varrho_{\bm{\rho}}=(2\pi\hbar)^{d}{\bm{\rho}}(x,x)
, which
implies that we need to identify the pair potential
V(x-y)=(2\pi)^{d}K(x-y)
.
These modifications are only superficial and do not change any of the estimates.

Proof.

Item (1) is contained in
[
8
, Theorem 1.5]
.
The first inequality in Item (2)
is
[
8
, Lemma 2.2]

which is essentially the Lieb-Thirring inequality.
The second inequality in Item (2)
is contained in
[
8
, Proposition 6.2.]
;
it follows from interpolating the
L^{1}
bounds (obtained from the CLR inequality) and the
L^{\infty}
bounds (obtained from Agmon’s estimates).
Item (3) is contained in
[
8
, Theorem 1.6]
.
Finally, the claim in Item (4)
follows from

\textnormal{Tr}\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}=\textnormal{Tr}\gamma_{\rm TF}+O(N\hbar^{1/2}|\ln\hbar|^{\frac{1}{2}})=N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varrho_{\rm TF}(x)dx+O(N\hbar^{1/2}|\ln\hbar|^{\frac{1}{2}})\ ,

where we used the property of the Weyl quantization

\textnormal{Tr}\gamma_{f}=N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varrho_{f}(x)dx
.
Note

\varrho_{\rm TF}

has unit mass. This finishes the proof.
∎

Consider
d=3
.
Let us observe that thanks to the uniform
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
estimates
satisfied by the position density
\varrho_{\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}}
,
the effective one-body potential

W_{N}(x)\equiv U(x)+(V*\varrho_{\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}})(x)-\mu

(3.23)

is of class
C_{\rm loc}^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
and all the Hölder seminorms
are uniformly bounded in
N
; see e.g. Lemma
[
8
, Lemma 6.1]
.
As a consequence, we may use
a recent result
on the optimal trace asymptotics
of Schrödinger opertors
with non-smooth potentials.

We record here the following
version, with stronger assumptions,
which is all we need for our purposes.
The original result
is due to Mikkelsen

[
21
, Theorem 1.5]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
W\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

for some
\alpha\in[\tfrac{1}{2},1]
,
with
W(x)\rightarrow\infty

as

|x|\rightarrow\infty
.
Then, there is a constant
C>0
such that

\bigg|\textnormal{Tr}\,\mathds{1}_{(-\infty,0]}\Big(-\hbar^{2}\Delta+W(x)\Big)-\frac{1}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\mathds{1}_{(-\infty,0]}(p^{2}+W(x))dxdp\bigg|\leqslant C\hbar^{-2}\ .

(3.24)

Furthermore, for each
R,\nu>0

the constant

C=C(R,\nu)>0

can be chosen to be uniform
over all potentials
W

which verify

\|W\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\Omega(W,\nu))}<R

(3.25)

where

\Omega(W,\nu)=\{W(x)<\nu\}.

Remark 3.5
.

In
[
21
]
the uniformity over the constants
is not explicitly stated in the main result, but is a rather technical corollary of its proof.
Let us further explain. The proof of
[
21
, Theorem 1.5]

relies on several auxiliary results
stated in terms of a localized, regularized family

(W^{1}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

which satisfies for
\tau=1+\alpha

\|D^{\beta}W^{1}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leqslant C(\beta)\|W\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\Omega(W,\nu))}\varepsilon^{(\tau-|\beta|)_{-}}\,\qquad\forall\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{3}\ .

(3.26)

Thus, for
W
verifying (
3.25
) one finds the bound

\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}\varepsilon^{-(\tau-|\beta|)_{-}}\|D^{\beta}W^{1}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}<C(\beta)R\ .

(3.27)

Furthermore, the constants in these auxiliary results
depend
only
on the re-scaled
C^{k}
norms
on the left hand side of (
3.27
).
Most importantly the key trace asymptotics
[
21
, Theorem 3.2]
.
These results are then combined with (quantitative) multi-scale analysis and Agmon estimates, which in turn can also be made explicit in terms of the
C^{1,\alpha}
semi-norms of
W(x)
.

Remark 3.6
.

Here and in the sequel, we will use the following well-known relation

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\mathds{1}_{(-\infty,0]}(p^{2}+W(x))dxdp=|B_{d}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}W(x)_{-}^{d/2}dx

(3.28)

The previous asymptotics
has the following corollary on
the bounds for the

number of eigenvalues
of a Schrödinger operator
on an interval
[a,b]
.
These bounds have been used in the past in similar settings,
see e.g.
[
20
]
.

Corollary 3.1
.

Fix
\mu>0
as in (
1.8
)
and consider
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
for a small enough
\varepsilon_{0}<1
.
Then, there is a constant
C>0
such that
for all
N\geqslant 1

\textnormal{Tr}\,\mathds{1}_{[\mu-\varepsilon,\mu+\varepsilon]}(H_{\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}})\leqslant CN(\varepsilon+\hbar)\ ,

(3.29)

where
H_{\gamma}

is the Hartree Hamiltonian,
and
\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}

is the Hartree minimizer.

Proof.

Set
a=\mu-\varepsilon
and
b=\mu+\varepsilon
.
Fix
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\nu_{0}
and an open bounded set
O\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

such that
\{W_{N}(x)\pm\varepsilon_{0}<\nu_{0}\}\subset O
for all
N\geqslant 1
.
Put
R\equiv 1+\sup_{\pm,N}\|W_{N}\pm\varepsilon_{0}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(O)}

and
\nu\equiv\nu_{0}-\varepsilon_{0}

. Then, thanks to Proposition
3.2

we find that there is
C=C(R,\nu)>0
such that
for all
N\geqslant 1

\bigg|\textnormal{Tr}\mathds{1}_{[a,b]}(H_{\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}})-\frac{1}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}\int_{\{a\leqslant p^{2}+W_{N}(x)\leqslant b\}}dxdp\bigg|\leqslant CN\hbar\ .

(3.30)

Using that

x\mapsto x_{-}^{d/2}

is differentiable for
d=3
, one has

\displaystyle\int_{\{a\leqslant p^{2}+W_{N}(x)\leqslant b\}}dxdp

\displaystyle\,=\,|B_{3}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left([W_{N}(x)-b]_{-}^{3/2}-[W_{N}(x)-a]_{-}^{3/2}\right)dx\,\leqslant\,C|b-a|

(3.31)

where we update the constant
in order to incorporate the derivative term

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}[W_{N}(x)-\lambda]^{3/2-1}dx\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}[U(x)-\mu-\varepsilon]_{-}^{1/2}dx<\infty

for
\lambda\in[a,b]
,
where we used
V\geqslant 0.

The latter integral is finite, as the integrand is only compactly supported.
This finishes the proof.
∎

4.
Operator estimates for regular potentials

All the contents in this section apply to any dimension,
and we will consider arbitrary
d\geqslant 1
.
We analyze the correlations generated by the following Hamiltonian on Fock space

\mathcal{H}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a_{x}^{*}\Big(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{x}+U(x)\Big)a_{x}dx+\frac{1}{2N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}V(x-y)a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{y}a_{x}dxdy

(4.1)

where, for convenience, we do not write
explicitly the
N
dependence.
As usual, we note that
(\mathcal{H}\Psi)^{(N)}={\rm H}_{N}\Psi^{(N)}
coincides with the
N
-body Hamiltonian
when acting on
N
-particle states.
In order to ease the notation, we do not distinguish between
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathrm{H}_{N}
.

Throughout this section, we
focus on
regular potentials

V(x)
which satisfy the conditions

\widehat{V}(\xi)\geqslant 0\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\|V\|\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{V}(\xi)(1+|\xi|)d\xi<\infty

(4.2)

We are interested in estimates for the operator
\mathcal{R}^{*}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{R}
,
where
\mathcal{R}

is a particle-hole transformation
built around a density matrix
\gamma
.
We record here an explicit representation
in terms of creation and annihilation operators.
It will be useful for our purposes
to do this in some generality.
We will denote
the effective Hartree-Fock operator by

H_{\gamma}^{\rm HF}\equiv-\hbar^{2}\Delta+U(x)+V*\varrho_{\gamma}-N^{-1}X_{\gamma}

(4.3)

where the exhange operator is defined as follos

(X_{\gamma}\varphi)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}X_{\gamma}(x,y)\varphi(y)dy\ ,\qquad X_{\gamma}(x,y)\equiv V(x-y)\gamma(x,y)\ .

(4.4)

We also introduce the Hartree-Fock energy functional

\mathscr{E}^{\rm HF}_{N}(\gamma)=\mathscr{E}_{N}(\gamma)-\frac{1}{2N}\textnormal{Tr}X_{\gamma}\gamma

(4.5)

Here and in the sequel, given an operator
A

with kernel
A(x,y)
, we
denote by
\overline{A}
and
A^{t}

the operators with kernels
\overline{A}(x,y)

and
A(y,x)
, respectively.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(u,v,\gamma)
be as in the statement of Lemma
3.2
,
and denote by
\mathcal{R}

the associated particle-hole transformation on Fock space.
Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{*}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle\ =\ \mathscr{E}^{\rm HF}_{N}(\gamma)\ +\ d\Gamma(uH_{\gamma}^{\rm HF}u^{*})\ -\ d\Gamma(\overline{v}(H_{\gamma}^{\rm HF})^{t}\overline{v}^{*})

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}(uH_{\gamma}^{\rm HF}v^{*})(x,y)a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}dxdy\,+\,h.c\ +\ \frac{1}{2N}\,\mathcal{L}_{4}

where, in terms of

\alpha_{x}\equiv a(u_{x})

and

\beta_{x}\equiv a(\overline{v}_{x})

we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{4}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{4}^{\rm diag}+\mathcal{L}_{4}^{\rm off}

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{4}^{\rm diag}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}V(x-y)\big(\alpha_{x}^{*}\alpha_{y}^{*}\alpha_{y}\alpha_{x}+\beta_{x}^{*}\beta_{y}^{*}\beta_{y}\beta_{x}-2\alpha_{x}^{*}\beta_{y}^{*}\beta_{y}\alpha_{x}\big)\,dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}V(x-y)\alpha_{x}^{*}\beta_{x}^{*}\beta_{y}\alpha_{y}\,dxdy\ ,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{4}^{\rm off}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}V(x-y)\big(2\alpha_{x}^{*}\beta_{y}\alpha_{y}\alpha_{x}-2\beta_{x}^{*}\beta_{x}\beta_{y}\alpha_{y}+\beta_{x}\beta_{y}\alpha_{y}\alpha_{x}\big)dxdy+h.c\ .

Remark 4.1
.

We could not find the exact statement in the literature
for pure states, as typically in the dynamical case the diagonal terms
do not enter the analysis.
The proof is however analogous to the one presented in
[
3
, Proposition 3.1]
for mixed states.
We have included the details of the calculation in the Appendix, and the proof of Lemma
4.1
follows from the conjugation of one and two-body operators, plus some elementary manipulations.
See e.g. Lemma
A.1
and
A.2
respectively.

The remainder of this section is devoted
to provide various operator estimates
for all the terms in the above expansion.

First,
we analyze the operators
which contain the exchange terms
X_{\gamma}
.
We use the following estimate, which uses only
the operator bound
0\leqslant\gamma\leqslant\bm{1}
.
Namely, thanks to the Fourier decomposition

\displaystyle X_{\gamma}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{V}(\xi)e_{\xi}\gamma e_{\xi}^{*}d\xi

(4.6)

with
e_{\xi}(x)\equiv e^{i\xi\cdot x}
,
we get

\|X_{\gamma}\|\leqslant\|\widehat{V}\|_{L^{1}}
.

Lemma 4.2

(Exchange terms)
.

Let
(u,v,\gamma)
be as in Lemma
4.1
.
Then, in the sense of quadratic forms of
\mathscr{F}

\displaystyle\pm N^{-1}d\Gamma(uX_{\gamma}u^{*})

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ N^{-1}\|\widehat{V}\|_{L^{1}}\mathcal{N}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\pm N^{-1}d\Gamma(\overline{v}X_{\gamma}^{t}\overline{v}^{*})

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ N^{-1}\|\widehat{V}\|_{L^{1}}\mathcal{N}

(4.8)

\displaystyle\pm N^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}(uX_{\gamma}v^{*})(x,y)a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}dxdy

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ N^{-1}\sqrt{\textnormal{Tr}\gamma}\|\widehat{V}\|_{L^{1}}\mathcal{N}^{1/2}\ .

(4.9)

The proof is a straightforward application
of the bounds in Lemma
3.1
, combined with

\|X_{\gamma}\|\leqslant\|\widehat{V}\|_{L^{1}}

and
\|v\|_{\rm HS}^{2}=\textnormal{Tr}\gamma
.

Secondly, we analyze the operator
\mathcal{L}_{4}
.
Let us note that the off-diagonal terms appear
in the time-dependent setting and have been already analyzed in the literature
[
2
]
;
in order maintain the
exposition self-contained,
we include it here as well,
and we record these bounds in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3

(Off-diagonal terms)
.

Let
(u,v,\gamma)
be as in Lemma
3.1
.
Additionally, assume that there exists
C_{0}>0

such that for all
N\geqslant 1

and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\|[e_{\xi},\gamma]\|\leqslant C_{0}|\xi|N\hbar\ .

(4.10)

Then,
in the sense of quadratic forms in
\mathscr{F}

\pm N^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{4}^{\rm off}\leqslant 10C_{0}\|V\|\hbar\,(\mathcal{N}+\bm{1})

(4.11)

and we recall
\|V\|=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{V}(\xi)(1+|\xi|)d\xi
.

Proof.

Let us denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv\int V(x-y)\big(\alpha_{x}^{*}\beta_{y}\alpha_{y}\alpha_{x}-\beta_{x}^{*}\beta_{x}\beta_{y}\alpha_{y}+\beta_{x}\beta_{y}\alpha_{y}\alpha_{x}\big)dxdy

\displaystyle=\int\widehat{V}(\xi)\big(d\Gamma(ue_{\xi}u^{*})-d\Gamma(\overline{v}e_{\xi}\overline{v}^{*})\big)\int(ve_{\xi}^{*}u^{*})(z_{1},z_{2})a_{z_{1}}a_{z_{2}}dz_{1}dz_{2}d\xi

\displaystyle+\int\widehat{V}(\xi)\int(ve_{\xi}u^{*})(z_{1},z_{2})a_{z_{1}}a_{z_{2}}dz_{1}dz_{2}\int(ve_{\xi}^{*}u^{*})(z_{3},z_{4})a_{z_{3}}a_{z_{4}}dz_{3}dz_{4}d\xi

The operator norm of

\int(ve_{\xi}u^{*})(z_{1},z_{2})a_{z_{1}}a_{z_{2}}

is bounded by

\|ve_{\xi}u^{*}\|_{\textnormal{Tr}}\leqslant C_{0}|\xi|N\hbar

thanks to
vu^{*}=0
and the commutator estimates.
On the other hand,
d\Gamma(A)

is bounded in the operator sense by
\|A\|\mathcal{N}
.
This controls the first term in the last inequality, with
an additional use of the
identity
\bm{1}=(\mathcal{N}+\bm{1})^{1/2}(\mathcal{N}+\bm{1})^{-1/2}

and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
For the second term we
use instead
\int A(x,y)a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}\leqslant\|A\|_{\rm HS}(\mathcal{N}+\bm{1})^{1/2}

and the commutator estimates
in HS norm

\|ve_{\xi}u^{*}\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\leqslant C_{0}|\xi|N\hbar
.
Thus

\pm\mathcal{K}_{1}\,\leqslant\,5C_{0}\|V\|N\hbar\,\left(\mathcal{N}+\bm{1}\right)\ .

(4.12)

The same estimate applies for the adjoint
\mathcal{K}_{1}^{*}
.
∎

Let us now turn to the study of the diagonal terms.
To our best knowledge, the analysis of

\mathcal{L}_{4}^{\rm diag}

is new. In the time dynamical setting,
its analysis is not necessary because it commutes with
the particle number operator.

Lemma 4.4

(Diagonal terms)
.

Let
(u,v,\gamma)
be as in Lemma
4.1
,
and assume
\gamma
verifies (
4.10
) with constant

C_{0}>0
.
Then,
in the sense of quadratic forms in
\mathscr{F}

N^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{4}^{\rm diag}\geqslant-20C_{0}\|V\|\hbar(\mathcal{N}+\bm{1})

(4.13)

and we recall

\|V\|=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{V}(\xi)(1+|\xi|)d\xi
.

Proof.

Consider first the contribution

\mathcal{K}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\int V(x-y)\bigg(\alpha_{x}^{*}\alpha_{y}^{*}\alpha_{x}\alpha_{y}+\beta_{x}^{*}\beta_{y}^{*}\beta_{x}\beta_{y}-2\alpha_{x}^{*}\alpha_{x}\beta_{y}^{*}\beta_{y}\bigg)dxdy\ .

We rewrite the first two terms
using the commutation relations

\{\alpha_{y}^{*},\alpha_{x}\}=(u^{*}u)(x,y)

and

\{\beta_{y}^{*},\beta_{x}\}=(v^{*}v)(y,x)

\displaystyle\int V(x-y

\displaystyle)\alpha_{x}^{*}\alpha_{y}^{*}\alpha_{x}\alpha_{y}dxdy

(4.14)

\displaystyle=\int V(x-y)\alpha_{x}^{*}\alpha_{x}\alpha_{y}^{*}\alpha_{y}dxdy-\int V(x-y)(u^{*}u)(x,y)\alpha_{x}^{*}\alpha_{y}dxdy,

\displaystyle\int V(x-y

\displaystyle)\beta_{x}^{*}\beta_{y}^{*}\beta_{x}\beta_{y}dxdy

(4.15)

\displaystyle=\int V(x-y)\beta_{x}^{*}\beta_{x}\beta_{y}^{*}\beta_{y}dxdy-\int V(x-y)(v^{*}v)(y,x)\beta_{x}^{*}\beta_{y}dxdy.

Note now that

\displaystyle\int V(x-y)(u^{*}u)(x,y)\alpha_{x}^{*}\alpha_{y}dxdy

\displaystyle=d\Gamma(uDu^{*})

(4.16)

\displaystyle\int V(x-y)(v^{*}v)(y,x)\beta_{x}^{*}\beta_{y}dxdy

\displaystyle=d\Gamma(\overline{v}C\overline{v}^{*})

(4.17)

with
D(x,y)=V(x-y)(u^{*}u)(x,y)

and
C(x,y)=V(x-y)(v^{*}v)(x,y)
.
Similarly as we did with the exhange term
one finds that the operator norms
\|D\|
and
\|C\|

are bounded by
\|\widehat{V}\|_{L^{1}}
.
Thus, the latter two operators
are bounded by
\|\widehat{V}\|_{L^{1}}\mathcal{N}

in the sense of quadratic forms. Hence

\mathcal{K}_{0}\geqslant\int V(x-y)\big(\alpha_{x}^{*}\alpha_{x}\alpha_{y}^{*}\alpha_{y}+\beta_{x}^{*}\beta_{x}\beta_{y}^{*}\beta_{y}-2\alpha_{x}^{*}\alpha_{x}\beta_{y}^{*}\beta_{y}\big)dxdy-\|\widehat{V}\|_{L^{1}}\mathcal{N}\ .

Let us denote the first operator on the right hand side by

\widetilde{K}_{0}
.
A straightforward calculation using a Fourier decomposition for
V(x-y)

shows that we can write

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{K}_{0}}

\displaystyle=\int\widehat{V}(\xi)\big(d\Gamma(ue_{\xi}u^{*})^{*}d\Gamma(ue_{\xi}u^{*})+d\Gamma(\overline{v}e_{\xi}\overline{v}^{*})^{*}d\Gamma(\overline{v}e_{\xi}\overline{v}^{*})\big)d\xi

\displaystyle\quad-2\int\widehat{V}(\xi)d\Gamma(ue_{\xi}u^{*})^{*}d\Gamma(\overline{v}e_{\xi}\overline{v}^{*})d\xi

(4.18)

where we define
\mathbb{U}_{\xi}\equiv d\Gamma(ue_{\xi}u^{*})

and

\mathbb{V}_{\xi}\equiv d\Gamma(\overline{v}e_{\xi}\overline{v}^{*})
.
The inequality

2\mathbb{U}^{*}_{\xi}\mathbb{V}_{\xi}\leqslant\mathbb{U}^{*}_{\xi}\mathbb{U}_{\xi}+\mathbb{V}^{*}_{\xi}\mathbb{V}_{\xi}

shows that
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}_{0}}\geqslant 0
in
\mathscr{F}

and, consequently, that
\mathcal{K}_{0}\geqslant-\|\widehat{V}\|_{L^{1}}\mathcal{N}
in
\mathscr{F}
.

Finally, we control the following contribution.

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{2}

\displaystyle=\int V(x-y)\alpha_{x}^{*}\beta_{x}^{*}\beta_{y}\alpha_{y}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int\widehat{V}(\xi)\int e_{\xi}(x)a_{x}^{*}\beta_{x}^{*}dx\int e_{\xi}^{*}(y)b_{y}a_{y}dy\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int\widehat{V}(\xi)\int(ue_{\xi}v^{*})(z_{1},z_{2})a_{z_{1}}^{*}a_{z_{2}}^{*}dz_{1}dz_{2}\int(ve_{\xi}^{*}u^{*})(z_{3},z_{4})a_{z_{3}}a_{z_{4}}dz_{3}dz_{4}\,d\xi.

Note

\|ue_{\xi}v^{*}\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\leqslant C_{0}|\xi|N\hbar

combined

\int O(x,y)a_{x}^{\#}a_{y}^{\#}\leqslant\|O\|_{\rm HS}\mathcal{N}^{1/2}
gives

\pm\mathcal{K}_{2}\leqslant C_{0}\|V\|\,N\hbar\,\mathcal{N}\ .

(4.20)

Finally, Using
1\leqslant N\hbar

we can put all of our bounds together to find that

\mathcal{L}_{4}\geqslant-20C_{0}N\hbar\|V\|(\mathcal{N}+\bm{1})

(4.21)

This finishes the proof.
∎

5.
Number estimates for singular potentials

The main step towards the proof of Theorem
1

is the following result containing
relevant
number estimates
.
In order to state them,
let
\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}

be the minimizer of the Hartree functional,
and let
\mathcal{R}_{N}

be the particle-hole transformation
associated to
\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}

in the sense of Lemma
3.2

We then
define the fluctuation vector

\Omega_{N}\equiv\mathcal{R}_{N}^{*}\Psi_{N}\ ,\qquad N\geqslant 1

(5.1)

where
\Psi_{N}
is the approximate ground state of
{\rm H}_{N}
.

Theorem 2
.

Under the same conditions of Theorem
1
,
there
is
C>0
such that for all
N\geqslant 1

\left\langle\Omega_{N},\mathcal{N}\Omega_{N}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{F}}\leqslant CN\hbar^{2\delta}\,|\!\ln\hbar|^{\frac{1}{2}}\ ,

(5.2)

where

\delta=\frac{1}{2}\min(\frac{6-5a}{16+5a},\frac{4+15a}{2(16+5a)}).

Additionally, if
a<1
, the logarithmic factor may be ommitted.

In order to prove the number estimates for singular potentials,
we will use the estimates from the previous section.
We will need to, however,
regularize the interaction potential

V(x)=\lambda|x|^{-a}\qquad\lambda>0\ ,\,a\in(0,1]\ .

(5.3)

Observe that its Fourier transform
is given explicitly by

\widehat{V}(\xi)=\lambda C_{d,a}|\xi|^{-d+a}
.
in dimension
d\geqslant 1.

The regularization is defined as follows.
Consider a large parameter
\Lambda\gg 1

and introduce the new potential
via the inverse Fourier formula

V_{\Lambda}(x)\equiv\int_{|\xi|\leqslant\Lambda}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,\widehat{V}(\xi)d\xi\ ,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\ .

(5.4)

Note that
V_{\Lambda}

satisfies the “regular conditions”

\displaystyle\widehat{V}_{\Lambda}(\xi)\geqslant 0\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\|V_{\Lambda}\|\leqslant C\Lambda^{1+a}\ ,\qquad\forall\Lambda\geqslant 1\ ,

(5.5)

where
\|V\|=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{V}(\xi)(1+|\xi|)d\xi
.

We will need to analyze the tail of the regularization,
which we denote by

W_{\Lambda}\equiv V-V_{\Lambda}\ .

(5.6)

In particular,
the tail is in the Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

for any
0\leqslant s<\frac{d}{2}-a
, and
satisfies the estimate

\|W_{\Lambda}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant C\Lambda^{-(\frac{d}{2}-a-s)}

(5.7)

As an application,
thanks to the Sobolev embedding
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

for

s=d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})\in(0,\frac{d}{2})

we obtain
the following
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
estimate

\|W_{\Lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant C\Lambda^{-(\frac{d}{p}-a)}\ ,

(5.8)

valid for
2\leqslant p<\frac{d}{a}
.
One of the key estimates
is the relative smallness of
the two-body potential
generated by the tail
W_{\Lambda}(x-y)
.
The following estimate is essentially a consequence of the Lieb-Thirring inequality.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume
d>\frac{2a}{2-a}
. Let
\Psi_{N}
be an approximate ground state of
{\rm H}_{N}
.
Then, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
N\geqslant 1

and
\Lambda\geqslant 1

\frac{1}{N}\left\langle\Psi_{N},\sum_{i<j}(W_{\Lambda})_{-}(x_{i}-x_{j})\Psi_{N}\right\rangle\leqslant CN\|W_{\Lambda}\|_{L^{1+\frac{d}{2}}}\leqslant CN\Lambda^{-\theta},

where
\theta\equiv\frac{2d}{d+2}-a.

Proof.

The function
f(x):=(W_{\Lambda})_{-}(x)\geqslant 0

is in the space
L^{1+d/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

and we use
[
11
, Lemma 3.4]

for the first inequality.
For the second inequality we use (
5.8
)
for
p=1+\frac{d}{2}<\frac{d}{a}
, which is the first assumption in the statement.
This finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
.

In order to prove Theorem
2
we introduce the regularized Hamiltonian
on Fock space
\mathscr{F}

\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a_{x}^{*}\Big(\!-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{x}+U(x)\Big)a_{x}dx+\frac{1}{2N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}V_{\Lambda}(x-y)a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{y}a_{x}dxdy

(5.9)

Lemma
5.1

implies immediately
the following lower bound

\displaystyle\left\langle\Psi_{N},\mathcal{H}\Psi_{N}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ \geqslant\ \left\langle\Psi_{N},\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\Psi_{N}\right\rangle\ -\ CN\Lambda^{-\theta}

\displaystyle\ =\ \left\langle\Omega_{N},\mathcal{R}^{*}_{N}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{R}_{N}\Omega_{N}\right\rangle\ -\ CN\Lambda^{-\theta}

(5.10)

where
\Omega_{N}
and
\mathcal{R}_{N}

are obtained from
the minimizer
\gamma\equiv\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}
,

without
cut-offs.
Since the potential
V_{\Lambda}

has finite norm
\|V_{\Lambda}\|
,
it can be analyzed
with the methods of the previous section.
More precisely, the calculation from Lemma
4.1

implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{*}_{N}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{R}_{N}

\displaystyle\ =\ \mathscr{E}^{\rm HF}_{N,\Lambda}(\gamma)\ +\ d\Gamma(uH_{\gamma,\Lambda}^{\rm HF}u^{*})\ -\ d\Gamma(\overline{v}(H_{\gamma,\Lambda}^{\rm HF})^{t}\overline{v}^{*})

\displaystyle\ +\ \int(uH_{\gamma,\Lambda}^{\rm HF}v^{*})(x,y)a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}dxdy\ +\ h.c\ +\ \frac{1}{2N}\mathcal{L}_{4,\Lambda}\ .

Here, the new subscript
\Lambda

in the Hartree-Fock terms, and in the quartic term, indicates
evaluation in the regularized potential
V_{\Lambda}
.

Let us now specialize to
d=3
.
we will apply Lemma
5.1
for any
a\leqslant 1
.
In particular
\theta=\frac{6}{5}-a
.
Additionally, thanks to Proposition
3.1

we have the commutator estimates for
\gamma=\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}

\|[e^{ix\cdot\xi},\gamma]\|_{\textnormal{Tr}}\leqslant C_{0}N\hbar|\xi|

(5.11)

for some constant
C_{0}>0
.
We also use that
\|\varrho_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}}
is uniformly bounded in
N
, for any
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
.

We now proceed in various steps,
and we analyze with the methods form the previous section
how to bound the error terms.
First, the exhange contributions inside the quadratic operator terms
can be estimated thanks to Lemma
4.2

\displaystyle\pm N^{-1}d\Gamma(uX_{\gamma,\Lambda}u^{*})

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ CN^{-1}\Lambda^{1+a}\mathcal{N}

(5.12)

\displaystyle\pm N^{-1}d\Gamma(\overline{v}X_{\gamma,\Lambda}^{t}\overline{v}^{*})

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ CN^{-1}\Lambda^{1+a}\mathcal{N}

(5.13)

\displaystyle\pm N^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}(uX_{\gamma,\Lambda}v^{*})(x,y)a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}dxdy

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ CN^{-1/2}\Lambda^{1+a}\mathcal{N}^{1/2}\ .

(5.14)

For the quartic term,
we use Lemma
4.3
and
4.4

for the off-diagonal and diagonal terms, respectively.
In particular,
here it is crucial
that the regularized version
is chosen so that
\widehat{V}_{\Lambda}(\xi)\geqslant 0
.
We find

N^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{4,\Lambda}\geqslant-C\hbar\Lambda^{1+a}(\mathcal{N}+\bm{1})

(5.15)

Thus, combining the inequalities
(
5.12
), (
5.13
), (
5.14
) and (
5.15
)
one obtains the lower bound

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{*}_{N}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{R}_{N}

\displaystyle\ \geqslant\ \textnormal{Tr}\gamma H_{\gamma,\Lambda}^{\rm HF}\ +\ d\Gamma(uH_{\gamma,\Lambda}u^{*})\ -\ d\Gamma(\overline{v}H_{\gamma,\Lambda}\overline{v}^{*})

\displaystyle\ +\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}(uH_{\gamma,\Lambda}v^{*})(x,y)a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}\,dxdy\ +\ h.c\ -\ C\hbar\Lambda^{1+a}\left(\mathcal{N}+\bm{1}\right)

in the sense of quadratic forms in
\mathscr{F}.

The next step
is to replace
H_{\gamma,\Lambda}

with the non-regularized Hamiltonian
H_{\gamma}
, within all the quadratic terms.
First, we have thanks to Lemma
3.1
and Hölder’s inequality with

\displaystyle d\Gamma(uH_{\gamma,\Lambda}u^{*})

\displaystyle\ =\ d\Gamma(uH_{\gamma}u^{*})-d\Gamma(u(W_{\Lambda}*\varrho_{\gamma})u^{*})

\displaystyle d\Gamma(\overline{v}H_{\gamma,\Lambda}^{t}\overline{v}^{*})

\displaystyle\ =\ d\Gamma(\overline{v}H_{\gamma}^{t}\overline{v}^{*})-d\Gamma(\overline{v}(W_{\Lambda}*\varrho_{\gamma})\overline{v}^{*})

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ d\Gamma(\overline{v}H_{\gamma}\overline{v}^{*})+\|W_{\Lambda}\|_{L^{p}}\|\varrho_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\mathcal{N}\,,

(5.17)

and
p^{\prime}=5/3
is the conjugate exponent.
On the other hand, for the off-diagonal term we
can write, using

uH_{\gamma}v^{*}=0

and again Lemma
3.1
and Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle\pm\int(uH_{\gamma,\Lambda}v^{*})(x,y)a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}dxdy

\displaystyle\ =\ \mp\int(uW_{\Lambda}*\varrho_{\gamma}v^{*})(x,y)a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}dxdy

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ \|u(W_{\Lambda}*\varrho_{\gamma})v^{*}\|_{\rm Tr}\bm{1}

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ N\|W_{\Lambda}\|_{L^{p}}\|\varrho_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\bm{1}\ .

and similarly for the hermitian conjugate.
Recall

\varrho_{\gamma}=\varrho_{\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}}
is uniformly bounded in any
L^{p}
space,
and also

\|W_{\Lambda}\|_{L^{5/2}}\leqslant\Lambda^{-\theta}
.
Putting our estimates together, one finds
for some constant
C>0

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{*}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle\ \geqslant\ \mathscr{E}^{\rm HF}_{N,\Lambda}(\gamma)+d\Gamma(uH_{\gamma}u^{*})-d\Gamma(\overline{v}H_{\gamma}\overline{v}^{*})

\displaystyle\qquad-\ C\hbar\Lambda^{1+a}\left(\mathcal{N}+\bm{1}\right)-CN\Lambda^{-\theta}\bm{1}

Let us now replace
the
\Lambda
-dependent term
in the exhange operator in the scalar term.
For this, we use the estimate

\frac{1}{N}\int W_{\Lambda}(x-y)|\gamma(x,y)|^{2}dxdy\leqslant C\|W_{\Lambda}\|_{L^{1+d/2}}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{N}\textnormal{Tr}(-\Delta)\gamma+\frac{N}{\varepsilon^{3/2}}\Big)\ ,\ \forall\varepsilon>0

[
11
]
.
Choosing

and noting

we see that we can absorbe this remainder into the total error term

N\Lambda^{-\theta}
.
Thus, we arrive at the following operator inequality

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{*}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle\ \geqslant\ \mathscr{E}^{\rm HF}_{N}(\gamma)+d\Gamma(uH_{\gamma}u^{*})-d\Gamma(\overline{v}H_{\gamma}\overline{v}^{*})

\displaystyle\qquad-C\hbar\Lambda^{1+a}\left(\mathcal{N}+\bm{1}\right)-CN\Lambda^{-\theta}\bm{1}\ .

(5.18)

in the sense of quadratic forms in
\mathscr{F}
.

The estimate (
5.18
) is our
main operator inequality.
In particular, we can now test it against
\Omega_{N}=\mathcal{R}^{*}\Psi_{N}

and combine with (
5.10
) to get

\displaystyle\left\langle\Psi_{N},\mathcal{H}\Psi_{N}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ \geqslant\ \mathscr{E}^{\rm HF}_{N}(\gamma)\ +\ \left\langle\Omega_{N},d\Gamma(uH_{\gamma}u^{*}\,-\,\overline{v}H_{\gamma}\overline{v}^{*})\Omega_{N}\right\rangle

Next, we re-write the excitation operator

d\Gamma(uH_{\gamma}u^{*}-\overline{v}H_{\gamma}\overline{v}^{*})

u
,
v
and
\gamma
commute, as they
are built with the same set of eigenfunctions.
We can then obtain
using the structure

\gamma=\mathds{1}_{(-\infty,\mu]}(H_{\gamma})

\displaystyle uH_{\gamma}u^{*}-\overline{v}H_{\gamma}\overline{v}^{*}

(5.19)

The key point is that now
the last
operator on the right hand

N

and
\textnormal{Tr}\gamma
.

N\geqslant 1

\displaystyle\left\langle\Psi_{N},\mathcal{H}\Psi_{N}\right\rangle

\varepsilon\geqslant\hbar
be a parameter, soon to be chosen.

\bm{1}
on

\bm{1}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\,=\,\mathds{1}_{|H_{\gamma}-\mu|\geqslant\varepsilon}+\mathds{1}_{|H_{\gamma}-\mu|<\varepsilon}\,\leqslant\,\varepsilon^{-1}|H_{\gamma}-\mu|+\mathds{1}_{|H_{\gamma}-\mu|<\varepsilon}\ .

\mathscr{F}

\mathcal{N}\,\leqslant\,\varepsilon^{-1}d\Gamma(|H_{\gamma}-\mu|)\,+\,d\Gamma(\mathds{1}_{|H_{\gamma}-\mu|<\varepsilon})\ .

(5.22)

We can now test

\Omega_{N}

)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Omega_{N},\mathcal{N}\Omega_{N}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ \varepsilon^{-1}\left\langle\Omega_{N},\,d\Gamma(|H_{\gamma}-\mu|)\,\Omega_{N}\right\rangle\ +\ \left\langle\Omega_{N},d\Gamma(\mathds{1}_{|H_{\gamma}-\mu|<\varepsilon})\Omega_{N}\right\rangle

(5.23)

Let us now analyze the very last term

\gamma_{\Omega_{N}}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

,
which satisfies the operator bounds

.
Then, one obtains

\displaystyle\left\langle\Omega_{N},d\Gamma(\mathds{1}_{|H_{\gamma}-\mu|<\varepsilon})\Omega_{N}\right\rangle=\textnormal{Tr}\mathds{1}_{|H_{\gamma}-\mu|<\varepsilon}\gamma_{\Omega_{N}}\leqslant\textnormal{Tr}\mathds{1}_{|H_{\gamma}-\mu|<\varepsilon}\leqslant CN\varepsilon

where in the last line we used
the validity of the optimal Weyl’s law

, see e.g. Corollary

.
This gives us the final estimate

\displaystyle\langle\Omega_{N},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\Omega_{N}\rangle

(5.25)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C_{*}\hbar\Lambda^{1+a}}{\varepsilon}\big(1+\left\langle\Omega_{N},\mathcal{N}\Omega_{N}\right\rangle\big)+\frac{CN\Lambda^{-\theta}}{\varepsilon}+CN\left(\varepsilon+\frac{\hbar^{\frac{1}{2}}|\ln\hbar|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\varepsilon}\right)

.
We are now ready to choose the parameter

\displaystyle\left\langle\Omega_{N},\mathcal{N}\Omega_{N}\right\rangle\leqslant C+CN\left(\hbar^{-1}\Lambda^{-\theta-1-a}+\hbar\Lambda^{1+a}+\hbar^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda^{-1-a}|\ln\hbar|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

(5.27)

The cut-off parameter
\Lambda=\Lambda(\hbar)

is chosen in order to minimize the first two terms in the
bracket
N(\cdots)
. That is,

\hbar^{-1}\Lambda^{-\theta-1-a}=\hbar\Lambda^{1+a}\iff\Lambda=\hbar^{-\frac{1}{1+a+\theta/2}}\ .

(5.28)

Then, we obtain
the explicit expression

\textstyle\hbar\Lambda^{1+a}=\hbar^{\delta_{1}}\quad\textnormal{with}\quad\delta_{1}=\frac{\theta}{2+2a+\theta}\ .

(5.29)

Using
\theta=\frac{6}{5}-a

gives the power
\delta_{1}=(6-5a)/(16+5a).

We consider now the last term in (
5.27
).
We note that
\hbar^{-1/2}\Lambda^{-1-a}=\hbar^{1/2}\hbar^{-\delta_{1}}=\hbar^{\delta_{2}}

where
\delta_{2}\equiv 1/2-\delta_{1}
.
One may easily check
\delta_{2}>0
for any
a>0
.
Thus, from (
5.27
) we conclude that

\left\langle\Omega_{N},\mathcal{N}\Omega_{N}\right\rangle\leqslant CN\hbar^{2\delta}\,|\!\ln\hbar|^{\frac{1}{2}}\ ,\qquad 2\delta\equiv\min(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})>0\ .

(5.30)

Finally, let us comment that for the case

, one has
the better asymptotics
\textnormal{Tr}\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}=N+O(N\hbar^{1/2})
with no logarithmic factor.
In particular, this removes the same factor from Proposition

and Lemma

∎

We now prove the following lemma, used in the proof of Theorem

.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
d=3
.
Let
\Psi_{N}
be an approximate ground state of
{\rm H}_{N}
with
\varepsilon_{N}=N^{-1/6}
,
and let
\gamma=\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}

be the Hartree minimizer.
There is a constant
C>0

such that for all

\left\langle\Psi_{N},{\rm H}_{N}\Psi_{N}\right\rangle\leqslant\mathscr{E}^{\rm HF}_{N}(\gamma)+CN\hbar^{\tfrac{1}{2}}|\ln\hbar|^{\tfrac{1}{2}}

(5.31)

Remark 5.1
.

The statement of the lemma may seem trivial at first glance,
as it would be an obvious consequence of the variational principle
in case
\textnormal{Tr}\gamma=N
.
However,
\gamma

only satisfies

\textnormal{Tr}\gamma=N+O(N\hbar^{1/2}|\!\ln\hbar|^{1/2})
,
see e.g. Proposition
3.1
.

Before we turn to the proof of Lemma
5.2
,
we derive a useful trace formula.
Consider the auxiliary function

F(\nu)\equiv\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(U+V*\varrho_{\rm TF}-\nu\right)^{3/2}_{-}dx\,\qquad\nu\in\mathbb{R}\ .

(5.32)

Note
F(\mu)=1
thanks to the choice of
\mu
.
Additionally,
F\in C^{1}
, both
F,F^{\prime}
are increasing, and
F^{\prime}(\mu)>0
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
H_{\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}}
be the Hartree Hamiltonian.
Then, there is
\epsilon_{0}\in(0,1)

and
C_{0}>0

such that
for all
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})

|\textnormal{Tr}\mathds{1}_{(-\infty,\nu]}(H_{\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}})-NF(\nu)|\leqslant C_{0}N\hbar^{\frac{1}{2}}|\ln\hbar|^{1/2}\ ,\quad\forall\nu\in(\mu-\epsilon,\mu+\epsilon)\ .

(5.33)

Proof.

Consider now
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
,
where
\epsilon_{0}
is sufficiently small.
The Hamiltonian
H_{\gamma_{N}^{\rm N}}-\nu
is a Schrödinger operator
with potential

U+V*\varrho_{\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}}-\nu

in the class
C_{\rm loc}^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
As discussed in Section
3
, its semi-norms
are uniformly bounded in
N\geqslant 1
.
Thus, we may apply the asymptotics for the trace,
see e.g. Proposition
3.2

\textnormal{Tr}\mathds{1}_{(-\infty,\nu]}(H_{\gamma_{N}^{\rm N}})=\frac{1}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(U+V*\varrho_{N}-\nu\right)_{-}^{3/2}dx+O(\hbar^{-2})

and the error is uniform over
\nu\in(\mu-\epsilon,\mu+\epsilon)
.
Here, we let

\varrho_{N}\equiv\varrho^{\rm H}_{\gamma_{N}}
.
On the other hand,
\|\varrho_{N}-\varrho_{\rm TF}\|_{L^{1}}=O(\hbar^{\frac{1}{2}}|\ln\hbar|^{\frac{1}{2}})

follows from Proposition
3.1
.
Using the non-negativity
V*\varrho\geqslant 0
for
\varrho=t\varrho_{N}+(1-t)\varrho_{\rm TF}

where
t\in[0,1]
,
one finds thanks to a Taylor estimate

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(

\displaystyle U+V*\varrho_{N}-\nu\big)_{-}^{3/2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(U+V*\varrho_{\rm TF}-\nu\right)_{-}^{3/2}dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leqslant C\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(U-\nu)_{-}^{\frac{3-2}{2}}dx\|\varrho_{N}-\varrho_{\rm TF}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=O(\hbar^{\frac{1}{2}}|\!\ln\hbar|^{\frac{1}{2}})\ .

Therefore, we conclude the validity of the asymptotics for the trace
upon combination of the last two estimates.
∎

This will be the main technical ingredient in the proof of the lemma, alongside the variational principle.

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

Throughout the proof we denote

\gamma\equiv\gamma_{N}^{\rm H}
.
Let
(E_{n})
be the eigenvalues of
H_{\gamma}
,
counted with multiplicities and listed in increasing order.
Let
(f_{n})
be the collection of associated eigenfunctions.
Then we define

\gamma_{-}\equiv\mathds{1}_{(-\infty,\mu-\epsilon]}(H_{\gamma})\ ,\qquad\omega\equiv\textstyle\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant N}\ket{f_{n}}\bra{f_{n}}\ ,\qquad\gamma_{+}\equiv\mathds{1}_{(-\infty,\mu+\epsilon]}(H_{\gamma})

where
\epsilon\equiv C(\mu)\hbar^{\tfrac{1}{2}}|\ln\hbar|^{\tfrac{1}{2}}
.
We claim
C(\mu)>0

can be chosen large enough so that

\gamma_{-}\leqslant\omega\leqslant\gamma_{+}

(5.34)

in the sense of operators in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
To see this,
we use the fact that
F^{\prime}>0
is strictly increasing.
We then find thanks to the trace formula
(
5.33
)

\displaystyle\textnormal{Tr}\gamma_{-}

\displaystyle\leqslant N\left(F(\mu-\epsilon)+C_{0}\hbar^{\tfrac{1}{2}}|\ln\hbar|^{\tfrac{1}{2}}\right)\leqslant N\left(1-F^{\prime}(\mu)\epsilon+C_{0}\hbar^{\tfrac{1}{2}}|\ln\hbar|^{\tfrac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\textnormal{Tr}\gamma_{+}

\displaystyle\geqslant N\left(F(\mu+\epsilon)-C_{0}\hbar^{\tfrac{1}{2}}|\ln\hbar|^{\tfrac{1}{2}}\right)\geqslant N\left(1+F^{\prime}(\mu)\epsilon-C_{0}\hbar^{\tfrac{1}{2}}|\ln\hbar|^{\tfrac{1}{2}}\right)\ .

It is clear now that we can choose, for instance
C(\mu)=2C_{0}/F^{\prime}(\mu)
,
so that

\textnormal{Tr}\gamma_{-}<\textnormal{Tr}\omega<\textnormal{Tr}\gamma_{+}\ .

The operator inequality (
5.34
)
then follows from the previous trace inequalities, as the eigenvalues are listed in increasing order.

Let us now estimate the energy in the state
\omega
.
Thanks to the variational principle

\inf\sigma(H_{N})\leqslant\mathscr{E}^{\rm HF}_{N}(\omega)\leqslant\mathscr{E}_{N}(\omega)
.
Additionally, a straightforward calculation shows that

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{N}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\textnormal{Tr}H_{\omega}\omega-\tfrac{1}{2}\textnormal{Tr}V*\varrho_{\omega}\omega

(5.35)

\displaystyle\leqslant\textnormal{Tr}H_{\gamma_{+}}\gamma_{+}-\tfrac{1}{2}\textnormal{Tr}V*\varrho_{\gamma_{-}}\gamma_{-}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{E}_{N}(\gamma)+(\textnormal{Tr}H_{\gamma_{+}}\gamma_{+}-\textnormal{Tr}H_{\gamma}\gamma)+\tfrac{1}{2}(\textnormal{Tr}V*\varrho_{\gamma}\gamma-\textnormal{Tr}V*\varrho_{\gamma_{-}}\gamma_{-})\ .

For the first term, we find thanks to spectral calculus
and Corollary
3.1

\textnormal{Tr}H_{\gamma_{+}}\gamma_{+}-\textnormal{Tr}H_{\gamma}\gamma\leqslant(\mu+\epsilon)\textnormal{Tr}_{[\mu,\mu+\epsilon]}(H_{\gamma})\leqslant CN\hbar^{\tfrac{1}{2}}|\ln\hbar|^{\tfrac{1}{2}}

(5.36)

due to our choice of
\epsilon
.
For the second term, we use the convolution structure
and extract the difference in
L^{1}
norms

\displaystyle\textnormal{Tr}(V*(\varrho_{\gamma}-\varrho_{\gamma_{-}}))(\gamma+\gamma_{-})

\displaystyle\ =\ N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\varrho_{\gamma}-\varrho_{\gamma_{-}}\right)\left(V*(\varrho_{\gamma}+\varrho_{\gamma_{-}})\right)dx

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ N\,\|\varrho_{\gamma}-\varrho_{\gamma_{-}}\|_{L^{1}}\|V*(\varrho_{\gamma}+\varrho_{\gamma_{-}})\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ \textnormal{Tr}|\gamma-\gamma_{-}|\|V*(\varrho_{\gamma}+\varrho_{\gamma_{-}})\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ N\hbar^{1/2}\|V*(\varrho_{\gamma}+\varrho_{\gamma_{-}})\|_{L^{\infty}}

The norms

\||x|^{-a}*\varrho\|_{L^{\infty}}

can be controlled via Hölder’s inequality
in small and large regions, and leads to the following well-known estimate

\|V*\varrho\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant C_{a}\left(\|\varrho\|_{L^{1}}+\|\varrho\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\ ,\qquad\varrho=\varrho_{\gamma}+\varrho_{\gamma_{-}}.

In particular, the
L^{p}
norms of
\varrho_{\gamma}

and
\varrho_{\gamma_{-}}
are uniformly bounded in
N
, see e.g. Proposition

3.1
.
Finally, we include the correction due to the exhange term, i.e.
which is at most of order

N\hbar^{1/2}
.
This finishes the proof
after we collect all the estimates,
and use

\left\langle\Psi_{N},{\rm H}_{N}\Psi_{N}\right\rangle\leqslant\inf\sigma_{\mathscr{H}_{N}}({\rm H}_{N})+CN\hbar^{1/2}|\ln\hbar|^{1/2}.

∎

Appendix A
The conjugated Hamiltonian

Let
u
and
v
be operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with kernels

u(x,y)
and
v(x,y)
.
Let us denote

\alpha_{x}\equiv a(u_{x})

and

\beta_{x}\equiv a(\overline{v_{x}})

where
u_{x}(y)=u(y,x)
and
v_{x}(y)=v(y,x)
.
We also let
\gamma\equiv v^{*}v
.
In this section we assume that

\mathcal{R}:\mathscr{F}\rightarrow\mathscr{F}
is a unitary map that satisfies

\boxed{\mathcal{R}^{*}a_{x}\mathcal{R}=\alpha_{x}+\beta_{x}^{*}\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\mathcal{R}^{*}a^{*}_{x}\mathcal{R}=\alpha^{*}_{x}+\beta_{x}}

(A.1)

We will maintain a level of generality that is broader than required. We hope that the calculation presented here can be of independent use.

We start with one-body operators.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
A=A^{*}
be a self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{*}d\Gamma(A)\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=\textnormal{Tr}A\gamma+d\Gamma(uAu^{*})-d\Gamma(\overline{v}A^{t}\overline{v}^{*})

\displaystyle+\int(uAv^{*})(x,y)a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}dxdy+\int(vAu^{*})(x,y)a_{x}a_{y}dxdy\ .

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{*}d\Gamma(A)\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=\int A(x,y)\mathcal{R}^{*}a_{x}^{*}a_{y}\mathcal{R}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int A(x,y)\left(\alpha_{x}^{*}+\beta_{x}\right)\left(\alpha_{y}+\beta_{y}^{*}\right)dxdy

\displaystyle=\int A(x,y)\alpha_{x}^{*}\alpha_{y}dxdy-\int A(x,y)\beta_{y}^{*}\beta_{x}dxdy+\int A(x,y)\{\beta_{x},\beta_{y}^{*}\}dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+\int A(x,y)\alpha_{x}^{*}\beta_{y}^{*}dxdy+\int A(x,y)\beta_{x}\alpha_{y}dxdy\ .

Now one can compute the operator kernels

\displaystyle\int A(x,y)\alpha_{x}^{*}\alpha_{y}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int\left(\int A(x,y)u_{x}(z_{1})\overline{u_{y}(z_{2})}dxdy\right)a_{z_{1}}^{*}a_{z_{2}}dz_{1}dz_{2}

\displaystyle=\int\left(\int u(z_{1},x)A(x,y)u^{*}(y,z_{2})dxdy\right)a_{z_{1}}^{*}a_{z_{2}}dz_{1}dz_{2}

\displaystyle=\int(uAu^{*})(z_{1},z_{2})a_{z_{1}}^{*}a_{z_{2}}dz_{1}dz_{2}=d\Gamma(uAu^{*})\ .

Similarly, exchanging the roles of
u
and
\overline{v}
, we get

\displaystyle\int A(x,y)\beta_{y}^{*}\beta_{x}dxdy=\int A^{t}(x,y)\beta_{x}^{*}\beta_{y}dxdy=d\Gamma(\overline{v}A^{t}\overline{v}^{*})\ .

For the off-diagonal term we obtain

\displaystyle\int A(x,y)\beta_{x}\alpha_{y}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int\left(\int A(x,y)v_{x}(z_{1})\overline{u_{y}(z_{2})}dxdy\right)a_{z_{1}}a_{z_{2}}dz_{1}dz_{2}

\displaystyle=\int\left(\int v(z_{1},x)A(x,y)u^{*}(y,z_{2})dxdy\right)a_{z_{1}}a_{z_{2}}dz_{1}dz_{2}

\displaystyle=\int(vAu^{*})(z_{1},z_{2})a_{z_{1}}a_{z_{2}}dz_{1}dz_{2},

whereas the other off-diagonal term is simply the hermitian conjugate.
Finally,
for the scalar term we use

\{\beta_{x},\beta_{y}^{*}\}=\left\langle\overline{v}_{x},\overline{v}_{y}\right\rangle=(v^{*}v)(y,x)
. Hence

\int A(x,y)v^{*}v(y,x)dxdy=\int(Av^{*}v)(x)dx=\textnormal{Tr}Av^{*}v\ .

This finishes the proof.
∎

We now consider two-body operators given by a pair interaction.

Lemma A.2

Denote

\displaystyle=\int V(x-y)\left(\gamma(x,x)\gamma(y,y)-|\gamma(x,y)|^{2}\right)dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+2\int V(x-y)\left(\gamma(x,x)\beta_{y}^{*}\beta_{y}-\gamma(x,y)\beta_{y}^{*}\beta_{x}-\gamma(x,y)\alpha_{x}^{*}\alpha_{y}+\gamma(y,y)\alpha_{x}^{*}\alpha_{x}\right)dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+2\int V(x-y)\left(\gamma(y,y)\beta_{x}\alpha_{x}-\gamma(y,x)\beta_{y}\alpha_{x}-\omega(x,y)\alpha_{x}^{*}\beta_{y}^{*}+\omega(y,y)\alpha_{x}^{*}\beta_{x}^{*}\right)dxdy

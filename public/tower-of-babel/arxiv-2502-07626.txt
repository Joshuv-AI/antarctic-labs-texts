Foliated Plateau problems, geometric rigidity and equidistribution of closed k k -surfaces
Sébastien Alvarez

Abstract
. In this note, we survey recent advances in the study of dynamical properties of the space of surfaces with constant curvature in three-dimensional manifolds of negative sectional curvature. We interpret this space as a two-dimensional analogue of the geodesic flow and explore the extent to which the thermodynamic properties of the latter can be generalized to the surface setting. Additionally, we apply this theory to derive geometric rigidity results, including the rigidity of the hyperbolic marked area spectrum.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Foliated Plateau problems

The abundance of closed geodesics in a closed, negatively curved
3
-dimensional Riemannian manifold can be used to obtain many geometric rigidity results. For instance, knowing their exponential growth rate is sufficient to characterize the hyperbolic metric among all negatively curved metrics with a fixed volume. This is famously known as the Besson-Courtois-Gallot rigidity theorem
[
13
]
. The dynamical study of the geodesic flow is fundamental because, as Gromov states in
[
22
]
:
“if one wishes to understand closed geodesics not as individuals but as members of a community, one has to look at all (not only closed) geodesics.”
This forms the basis of Gromov’s theory of
foliated Plateau problems
.

In this paper, we focus on the dynamical study of higher-dimensional analogues of the geodesic flow, specifically the distribution and counting of closed surfaces in closed, negatively curved
3
-manifolds. The primary question is: what geometric properties should our surfaces satisfy to generalize periodic geodesics effectively?

Totally geodesic surfaces are relatively rare. Examples of
closed hyperbolic 3-manifolds without closed totally geodesic surfaces are given in
[
47
, §5.3]
and most hyperbolic knot complements have no totally geodesic surface:
[
10
,
56
]
. On the other hand, it has been recently proven that the presence of infinitely many closed totally geodesic surfaces in a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold
(M,h_{0})
implies that the manifold is arithmetic (see
[
9
,
51
]
), which is a topological restriction on the manifold. There are two natural candidates for representing conjugacy classes of surface subgroups.

—

Minimal surfaces
, which are surfaces whose mean curvature
H
(the half trace of the shape operator) equals zero.

—

k
-surfaces
, which are convex surfaces whose extrinsic curvature
\kappa_{ext}
(the determinant of the shape operator) equals a positive constant
k>0
.

These surfaces naturally arise as solutions to PDEs: the minimal surface PDE for minimal surfaces, and a Monge-Ampère equation for
k
-surfaces. Following Gromov’s perspective
[
22
,
23
]
, one should consider compact solutions of these PDEs not as individuals but as part of a community of all PDE solutions. These solutions give a laminated structure in a phase space, analogous to the geodesic flow. The goal is to derive information from the dynamical and ergodic properties of this lamination.

1.2.
Labourie’s lamination and phase space

In the early 2000s, Labourie initiated a dynamical study of
k
-surfaces, revealing its richness (see
[
38
,
39
,
41
]
). He showed how to solve asymptotic Plateau problems for
k
-surfaces. Consider an example of such a problem relevant to our discussion:

Let
(X,h)
be a
3
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard Riemannian manifold with pinched negative curvature
-a^{2}\leq\mathrm{sect}\leq-1
and a constant
k\in(0,1)
. Given an oriented Jordan curve
c\mathop{\subset}\partial_{\infty}X
, the problem is to prove the existence and uniqueness of an embedded, oriented disc
D
inside
X
such that
\kappa_{ext}=k
over
D
and whose ideal boundary
\partial_{\infty}D
coincides with
c
.

Existence and uniqueness in this context were first proven by Rosenberg and Spruck for the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{3}

[
57
]
, and then in the general case by Labourie
[
39
]
. Labourie’s work is more complete as it considers more general boundary conditions and classifies all admissible ones (see also
[
58
]
). The existence of the solution of the asymptotic Plateau problem for minimal surfaces in the hyperbolic case was proven by Anderson
[
6
]
, but uniqueness does not generally hold unless the boundary satisfies certain geometric conditions, such as being a
C
-quasicircle with
C\simeq 1

[
61
]
. Recently, Huang, Lowe, and Seppi provided an example of a quasicircle spanning uncountably many minimal discs
[
30
]
.

Using an approach based on the study of holomorphic curves, Labourie showed in
[
38
]
that the infinite-dimensional space of all marked immersed
k
-surfaces in
X
naturally compactifies with a finite-dimensional boundary. This
phase space
has the structure of a compact Riemann surface lamination, analogous to the geodesic flow, and shares remarkable dynamical properties with it (see
[
39
,
41
]
). However, some questions remain open, particularly regarding the distribution of closed
k
-surfaces in compact quotients of
X
.

1.3.
Asymptotic counting of closed minimal surfaces

In a recent paper
[
18
]
, Calegari, Marques, and Neves introduced a new idea in the context of minimal surfaces. This idea builds on Kahn and Marković’s work, which demonstrates the abundance of quasi-Fuchsian subgroups within the fundamental groups of closed hyperbolic
3
-manifolds
[
33
,
32
]
. Such a quasi-Fuchsian subgroup is represented by a minimal surface constructed by solving an asymptotic Plateau problem. This minimal surface may not be unique unless the limit set is an almost circular quasicircle.

The authors propose an asymptotic counting of closed quasi-Fuchsian minimal surfaces by their areas, as the limit quasicircle becomes more and more circular. They define an entropy that involves a double limit and satisfies a rigid inequality, analogous to the Besson-Courtois-Gallot’s inequality. The ideas of Calegari, Marques, and Neves beautifully combine methods from topology, geometric analysis, and homogeneous dynamics (Ratner’s theory). In a recent Bourbaki seminar
[
42
]
, Labourie presented these results, adhering more closely to Gromov’s philosophy of foliated Plateau problems.

1.4.
Equidistribution and asymptotic counting of
k
-surfaces

Labourie’s presentation inspired our joint paper with Lowe and Smith
[
3
]
, where we returned to investigating the distribution of closed
k
-surfaces, focusing specifically on closed quasi-Fuchsian
k
-surfaces. The main results are presented in §
4.2.2
. We define an entropy-like functional that counts closed quasi-Fuchsian
k
-surfaces by their areas and satisfies a rigid inequality. This functional is simpler because it involves only one limit, as Plateau problems are better behaved for
k
-surfaces than for minimal surfaces.

Our methods of proof differ significantly from those in
[
18
]
and require an extensive dynamical study of the space of
k
-surfaces. These methods involve results on solutions of different foliated Plateau problems that do not hold for minimal surfaces. Notably, we demonstrate that
the unit tangent bundle of any closed, negatively curved manifold is foliated by
k
-surfaces and that this foliation is stable
. Such a result generally does not hold for minimal surfaces, as shown by Lowe in his thesis
[
46
]
. This investigation was continued in the recent
[
4
]
, in which we studied other kind of spectra, in the spirit of the
period spectrum
in the theory of thermodynamical formalism. This leads us to agree with Labourie that
k
-surfaces provide a convincing higher-dimensional generalization of the geodesic flow.

1.5.
Outline of the paper

The present paper aims to provide an exposition of these results. Our goal is to explain the dynamical tools involved in proving rigidity results for both closed geodesics and closed surfaces.

In Section 2, we review useful tools from the dynamical study of periodic geodesics, focusing on their abundance and equidistribution, which are essential for studying geometric rigidity. Section 3 presents Labourie’s theory of
k
-surfaces and some results on the solution of foliated Plateau problems. Notably, Smith has written several expository papers in recent years that offer a good introduction to Labourie’s theory (see
[
59
,
60
]
and references therein). Section 4 reviews the main results of
[
3
]
, discussing the equidistribution of closed quasi-Fuchsian k-surfaces and various rigidity results. In the final section, we pose some interesting questions for further research.

2.
Geodesics and geometric rigidity

2.1.
Setting

In this paper,
(M,h_{0})
will denote a closed, connected
3
-dimensional hyperbolic manifold. It is isometric to
\mathbb{H}^{3}/\Pi
, where
\Pi
is a cocompact lattice of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})=\mathrm{Isom}^{+}(\mathbb{H}^{3})
. By Mostow’s rigidity theorem
[
52
]
the hyperbolic metric on
M
is unique up to isometry.

Our main goal is to characterize the hyperbolic metric on
M
within the space of Riemannian metrics
h
in
M
that have negative sectional curvature everywhere (
\mathrm{sect}_{h}<0.
)

Let
X=\widetilde{M}
denote the universal cover of
M
, and let
\partial_{\infty}X
denote its ideal boundary. This boundary is defined as the set of equivalence classes of geodesic rays under the relation of “staying at bounded distance”. The group
\Pi\simeq\pi_{1}(M)
(identified with a cocompact lattice of the group of direct isometries of
X
) acts on this ideal boundary.

2.2.
The geodesic flow

The
unit tangent bundle

T^{1}X
is the set of unit vectors tangent to
X
. A vector
v\in T^{1}X
directs a unique complete and oriented geodesic. Pushing
v
along this geodesic at unit speed over time
t
defines the
geodesic flow
, denoted
G_{t}(v)
.

The flow lines of this process define a
1
-dimensional foliation of
T^{1}X
denoted by
{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}
. The space of leaves is the space
\partial_{\infty}^{(2)}X
consisting of pairs
(\xi_{-},\xi_{+})
of points in
\partial_{\infty}X
such that
\xi_{-}\neq\xi_{+}
.

This flow commutes with the action of
\Pi
on
T^{1}X
by differentials of isometries. Therefore, it descends to a flow
g_{t}
in the quotient
T^{1}M
. We will see that while this flow is a paradigmatic model for chaotic flows, it is independent of the negatively curved metric
h
.

2.2.1.
Boundary correspondence and Morse’s lemma

To prove that the flow is independent of the metric, the first step is to identify the ideal boundaries of the universal covers of
M
for different metrics
h
with
\mathrm{sect}_{h}<0
.

The identity map from
(M,h_{0})\to(M,h)
is bilipschitz because
M
is compact. It has a lift
F:\mathbb{H}^{3}\to X
that conjugates the actions of
\Pi
. Hence the images by
F
of geodesics in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
are quasi-geodesics in
X
.

Theorem 2.1

(Morse’s lemma)
.

Quasi-geodesics in
X
stay at bounded distance from geodesics.

Morse’s Lemma provides a homeomorphism
\alpha:{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to\partial_{\infty}X
that maps the end of a geodesic ray in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
to the end of its image. By construction, this homeomorphism conjugates the actions of
\Pi
. We call this map the
boundary correspondence
.

2.2.2.
Hyperbolic properties and structural stability

Assuming the sectional curvatures of
h
are pinched between two negative constants
-b^{2}<-a^{2}<0
, there exist two subbundles of the tangent bundle
TT^{1}X
, denoted by
E^{s}
and
E^{u}
such that

TT^{1}X=E^{s}\oplus E^{0}\oplus E^{u}.

Here
E^{0}
represents the
1
-dimensional subbundle generated by the flow. These subbundles satisfy

(1)

\begin{cases}\forall v_{s}\in E^{s},\,\forall t>0,\,\,\frac{a}{b}e^{-bt}|v_{s}|\leq|DG_{t}(v_{s})|\leq\frac{b}{a}e^{-at}|v_{s}|,\\
\forall v_{u}\in E^{u},\,\forall t>0,\,\,\frac{a}{b}e^{-bt}|v_{u}|\leq|DG_{-t}(v_{u})|\leq\frac{b}{a}e^{-at}|v_{u}|.\end{cases}

The notation
|.|
refers to the Sasaki metric (see §
3.2.3
). The bundles
E^{s}
and
E^{u}
are the bundles tangent to
stable and unstable horospheres
in
X
, which are level sets of the
Busemann functions

(2)

\beta_{\xi}(x,y)=\lim_{t\to\infty}d(y,c(t))-d(x,c(t))

, where
c(t)
is any geodesic ray parametrized by arc length and asymptotic to
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}X
, endowed with inward and outward pointing unit vector fields.

Consider the flow
g_{t}
in the quotient manifold
T^{1}M
. Property (
1
) is called the
Anosov property
. It is proven in
[
7
]
that flows with the Anosov property are
structurally stable
.

Theorem 2.2

(Structural stability)
.

A flow in
T^{1}M
that is close enough to the geodesic flow
g_{t}
in the
C^{1}
-topology is orbit equivalent: there exists a homeomorphism
\Phi:T^{1}M\to T^{1}M
that conjugates their (oriented) orbit foliations.

2.2.3.
Gromov’s geodesic rigidity

In a paper from 1976 that was published in 2000
[
24
]
, Gromov went further and proved that the qualitative behaviour of geodesics in
T^{1}M
is in fact independent of the negatively curved metric in
M
(and not only locally). He proved the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3

(Gromov’s rigidity)
.

Let
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
be two negatively curved metrics in
M
. Then the geodesic flows of
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are orbit equivalent.

This theorem is easy in dimension
2
. Indeed let
M
be a closed surface of genus
g\geq 2
,
h
be a negatively curved Riemannian metric on
M
, and
X
its universal cover (homeomorphic to a disc). Then
T^{1}X
can be identified with the space
\partial_{\infty}^{(3)}X
of oriented triples of points (
\xi_{-},\xi_{0},\xi_{+})
on the boundary. The correspondence can be described as follows. There is a unique geodesic
c
oriented from
\xi_{-}
to
\xi_{+}
. The orthogonal projection from
\xi_{0}
to
c
defines a vector
v
tangent to
c
.

This correspondence
T^{1}X\to\partial_{\infty}^{(3)}X
conjugates the action of
\Pi\simeq\pi_{1}(M)
on
T^{1}X
by differentials of isometries, and the diagonal action on
\partial_{\infty}^{(3)}
. The geodesic flow defines a action of
{\mathbb{R}}
on
\partial_{\infty}^{(3)}X
, fixing the points
\xi_{-}
and
\xi_{+}
and moving the point
\xi_{0}
from
\xi_{-}
to
\xi_{+}
. Let
h^{\prime}
be another negatively curved metric on
M
. The boundary correspondence defined in
2.2.1
immediately defines an equivariant correspondence
\partial_{\infty}^{(3)}X\to\partial_{\infty}^{(3)}X^{\prime}
that yields the desired orbit equivalence between the geodesic flows of
h
and
h^{\prime}
.

In dimension
3
(or higher), this is more complex. Assume first that the curvature of
h
is constant. It is possible to associate to a vector
v\in T^{1}\mathbb{H}^{3}
a pair of points
(\xi_{-},\xi_{+})\in\partial_{\infty}^{(2)}\mathbb{H}^{3}
. However, the role of the point
\xi_{0}
is now played by a circle
c
that separates these two points and forms the ideal boundary of the totally geodesic plane orthogonal to
v
. In this way, the unit tangent bundle
T^{1}\mathbb{H}^{3}
can be identified with the set
(c,\xi_{+})
such that
c
is an oriented circle of
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and
\xi_{+}
is a point in the interior of
c
. The point
\xi_{-}
is the image by
\xi_{-}
under the inversion of
c
.

If
h
has variable curvature it is not clear how a pair
(c,\xi_{+})
defines a element of
T^{1}X
because there is no reason why
c
should span a totally geodesic plane. Gromov’s strategy is to use Morse’s lemma. Consider the images of geodesics of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
by an equivariant bilipschitz diffeomorphism
F:\mathbb{H}^{3}\to X
. Such a curve
\gamma
is a quasi-geodesic of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
that can be orthogonally projected to its geodesic escort
\gamma_{0}
(see Theorem
2.1
). This provides a surjective map
t\in{\mathbb{R}}\to y(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}
that sends the arc length parameter on
\gamma
onto the arc length parameter of
\gamma_{0}
. The problem is that it is not necessarily injective. Gromov’s idea is to replace
y(t)
with its averages over the interval
[t,T+t]
. For sufficiently large
T
, the resulting parametrization is injective. This clever trick is non-canonical, since it depends on the averaging method used.

2.2.4.
Topological entropy and asymptotic counting

As with every Anosov flow, the geodesic flow in
T^{1}M
has positive
topological entropy
, representing the exponential complexity of its orbit structure. In the context of hyperbolic flows, the works of Bowen and Margulis (see
[
16
,
48
]
) show that this entropy equals the exponential growth rate of the set of periodic orbits. Formally it can be expressed as

H(M,h)=\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{R}\log\#\{\gamma\,\text{closed geodesic};\,\,\,\operatorname{length}_{h}(\gamma)\leq R\}.

2.3.
Rigidity results

We present two paradigmatic examples of rigidity results that fundamentally rely on the dynamical properties of the geodesic flow.

2.3.1.
Entropy and rigid inequality

The first result shows that the topological entropy of the geodesic flow satisfies a rigid inequality, interpreted as follows: when the volume of a negatively curved metric is fixed, in average, closed geodesics are shorter in variable curvature than in constant curvature.

Theorem 2.4

(Besson-Courtois-Gallot,
[
13
]
)
.

Let
(M,h_{0})
be a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold. Let
h
be a Riemannian metric on
M
with negative sectional curvature:
\mathrm{sect}_{h}<0
. Suppose

\mathrm{Vol}(M,h)=\mathrm{Vol}(M,h_{0}).

Then

H(M,h)\geq 2=H(M,h_{0}).

Equality holds if and only if
h
and
h_{0}
are isometrc, i.e., there exists a smooth diffeomorphism
\phi:M\to M
such that
\phi^{\ast}h=h_{0}
.

2.3.2.
Rigidity of the hyperbolic marked length spectrum

The second result is an inverse problem. Recall the definition of the marked length spectrum. Let
[\Pi]
denote the set of conjugacy classes of
\Pi\simeq\pi_{1}(M)
. Given
[g]\in[\Pi]
there exists a unique closed geodesic
\gamma_{g}
representing
[g]
. The
marked length spectrum
of the metric
h
is the map

\mathrm{MLS}_{h}:[\Pi]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,[g]\mapsto\operatorname{length}_{h}(\gamma_{g}).

Conjecturally, the marked length spectrum determines the metric on
M
(see
[
17
, Problem 3.1]
). The
2
-dimensional case was solved by Otal
[
53
]
. In higher dimension, there are two results. A local result was obtained by Guillarmou and Lefeuvre in
[
25
]
. The second result, that will be relevent for our purposes, obtained by Hamenstädt, is the rigidity of the
hyperbolic marked length spectrum
.

Theorem 2.5

(Hamenstädt,
[
28
]
)
.

Let
(M,h_{0})
be a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold. Let
h
be a Riemannian metric on
M
with negative sectional curvature:
\mathrm{sect}_{h}<0
. If
\mathrm{MLS}_{h}=\mathrm{MLS}_{h_{0}}
then
h
and
h_{0}
are isometric.

2.4.
Distribution of closed geodesics

Both theorems use a basic fact: that the behaviour of periodic orbits of the geodesic flows determines the dynamical properties of the geodesic flow.

2.4.1.
Equidistribution

Given a periodic orbit of the geodesic flow
\gamma:{\mathbb{R}}\to T^{1}M
, the
Dirac mass at
\gamma
, denoted
\delta_{\gamma}
, is the invariant probability measure supported by
\gamma
. It satisfies the following property for every continuous function
f:T^{1}M\to{\mathbb{R}}

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}f\circ\gamma(t)dt=\int_{T^{1}M}fd\delta_{\gamma}.

Bowen
[
16
]
and Margulis
[
49
]
show that periodic measures equidistribute towards an ergodic measure
\mu_{BM}
, known as the
Bowen-Margulis measure
. This is expressed as

\frac{1}{\#\{\operatorname{length}_{h}(\gamma)\leq R\}}\sum_{\operatorname{length}_{h}(\gamma)\leq R}\delta_{\gamma}\mathop{\longrightarrow}_{R\to\infty}\mu_{BM},

where the convergence is in the weak-
\ast
sense. The Bowen-Margulis measure is the measure of maximal entropy, has a nice local product structure, and coincides with the volume when the sectional curvature is constant (see
[
15
]
). The Bowen-Margulis measure
\mu_{BM}
also has a nice description in the cover
T^{1}X
that we describe below.

2.4.2.
Conformal densities

Let
H>0
. A
conformal density
of dimension
H
is a family
(\nu_{x})_{x\in X}
of finite Borel measures on
\partial_{\infty}X
that are pairwise absolutely continuous with Radon-Nikodym derivative given by

\frac{d\nu_{y}}{d\nu_{x}}(\xi)=\exp\left[-H\beta_{\xi}(x,y)\right],

for all
x,y\in X
and
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}X
and
\beta_{\xi}
is the Busemann function at
\xi
(see (
2
)). We say that it is invariant by the discrete subgroup
\Pi\leq\mathrm{Isom}^{+}(X)
if for every
\gamma\in\Pi
and
x\in X

\gamma_{\ast}\nu_{x}=\nu_{\gamma x}.

When
H=H(M,h)
, the topological entropy, there exists a unique conformal density (up to a multiplicative constant), which is given by
Patterson-Sullivan
’s construction (we will refer to the excellent monograph
[
54
]
and references therein). This family of measures is known as the
Patterson-Sullivan
measures. The construction begins with a basepoint
o\in X
and a family of finite measures on
\Gamma.o
defined for all
s>H
by

\nu_{s,x}=\frac{1}{\sum_{\gamma\in\Pi}e^{s\,\mathrm{dist}(o,\gamma o)}}\sum_{\gamma\in\Pi}e^{-s\,\mathrm{dist}(x,\gamma o)}\delta_{\gamma o}.

As
s
tends to
H
, this family of measures above converges to the unique conformal density
(\nu_{x})_{x\in X}
. An important property of this density is that measures
\nu_{x}
have no atoms.

2.4.3.
Bowen-Margulis measures in Hopf’s coordinates

The unit tangent bundle
T^{1}X
can be identified with
\partial_{\infty}^{(2)}X\times{\mathbb{R}}
using
Hopf’s coordinates
, defined as follows. Consider a base point
o\in X
.
A unit vector
v\in T^{1}X
maps to
(\xi_{-},\xi_{+},t)
where
\xi_{\pm}
are the endpoints of the geodesic directed by
v
and
t=\beta_{\xi^{+}}(o,x)
, with
x
being the basepoint of
v
. In these coordinates the geodesic flow acts by translation on the last factor, and the group
\Pi
acts as follows

\gamma.(\xi^{-},\xi^{+},t)=\left(\gamma\xi^{-},\gamma\xi^{+},t+\beta_{\xi^{+}}(\gamma^{-1}o,o)\right).

The
Bowen-Margulis measure

\tilde{\mu}_{BM}
is defined by

d\mu_{BM}(\xi^{-},\xi^{+},t)=\exp\left[H\beta_{\xi^{-}}(o,x)+H\beta_{\xi^{+}}(o,x)\right]d\nu_{o}(\xi^{-})\,d\nu_{o}(\xi^{+})\,dt.

This measure is independent of the basepoint
o
, invariant by the action of
\Pi
and the geodesic flow. The quotient measure is precisely the
Bowen-Margulis measure
of
T^{1}M
.

The measure
\exp\left[H\beta_{\xi^{-}}(o,x)+H\beta_{\xi^{+}}(o,x)\right]d\nu_{o}(\xi^{-})\,d\nu_{o}(\xi^{+})
on the space
\partial_{\infty}^{(2)}X
of geodesics (here
x
is any point on the geodesic
(\xi^{-},\xi^{+}))
, is an example of a
geodesic current
.

2.4.4.
Use of conformal densities in Besson-Courtois-Gallot’s theorem

The proof of Theorem
2.4
uses the conformal density
(\nu_{x})_{x\in X}
to construct a
natural map

F:(X,h)\to(\mathbb{H}^{3},h_{0})
with the following properties

(1)

F
is of class
C^{1}
;

(2)

the jacobian of
F
satisfies

|\textrm{Jac}F|\leq\left(\frac{H(M,h)}{H(M,h_{0})}\right)^{3},

throughout
X
.

(3)

if at a point
y\in X

|\textrm{Jac}F(y)|=\left(\frac{H(M,h)}{H(M,h_{0})}\right)^{3},

then
DF_{y}
is a homothety with ratio
H(M,h)/H(M,h_{0})
.

The map
F
is defined as follows: for a point
x\in X
, we define a measure on the Riemann sphere
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
by the formula

m_{x}=\alpha_{\ast}\nu_{x},

where
(\nu_{x})_{x\in X}
is the conformal density of dimension
H
and
\alpha:\partial_{\infty}X\to{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is the boundary correspondence. The measure
m_{x}
has no atom, so it has a well-defined
barycenter

z=\textrm{bar}(m_{x}),

defined as the point in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
that minimizes the integral
\int_{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}\beta_{\xi}(o,z)\,dm_{x}(z).
The natural map is then given by the correspondence
F:x\in X\mapsto z=\textrm{bar}(\alpha_{\ast}\nu_{x})\in\mathbb{H}^{3}.
Remarkably, this map, which involves the family of measures describing the distribution of closed geodesics, satisfies the properties above.

2.4.5.
Rigidity of the hyperbolic marked length spectrum

Hämenstadt’s result also relies on the idea that the distribution of closed geodesics determines the dynamics of the geodesic flow. Cohomological methods prove that the equality of marked length spectra implies the existence of a
time-preserving
conjugacy of the geodesic flows. This is contained in a theorem proven by Guillemin and Kazhdan in
[
26
]
.

Theorem 2.6

(Guillemin-Kazhdan)
.

Let
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
be two negatively curved metrics on
M
that have the same marked length spectra. Let
(g_{t}^{(i)})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
denote the geodesic flow of
(M,h_{i})
. Then there exists a homeomrphism
\Phi:T^{1}M\to T^{1}M
such that for every
t\in{\mathbb{R}}

\Phi\circ g_{t}^{(1)}=g_{t}^{(2)}\circ\Phi.

Sketch of proof.

Fix an orbit equivalence between the geodesic flows
\phi
which we may assume is Hölder continuous (together with the boundary correspondence) and smooth along the flow. This regularity is required to apply Livšic’s theorem (see
[
45
]
), which is a fundamental tool in the study of Anosov flows. Let
X^{(i)}
be the generator of the geodesic flow
g_{t}^{(i)}
. Since
\phi
is Hölder continuous and smooth along the flow, there exists a Hölder continuous function
\lambda:T^{1}M\to T^{1}M
such that for every
v\in T^{1}M

\mathcal{L}_{X^{(1)}}\phi(v)=\lambda(v)X^{(2)}(v).

The equality between marked length spectra implies that for every
v\in T^{1}M
whose trajectory is periodic of period
T
, we have

\int_{0}^{T}\lambda\left(g_{t}^{(1)}(v)\right)dt=T=\int_{0}^{T}dt,

so if
\alpha=\lambda-1
, which is Hölder continuous, we have that for every
v\in T^{1}M
whose trajectory is periodic of period
T

\int_{0}^{T}\alpha\left(g_{t}^{(1)}(v)\right)dt=0.

Livšic’s theorem implies that
\alpha
is a coboundary, meaning that

\alpha=\mathcal{L}_{X^{(1)}}A,

for some Hölder continuous function
A
, smooth along the flow. Then the map

\Phi(v)=g^{(2)}_{-A(v)}\circ\phi(v)

satisfies the desired property.
∎

If the map given by Kazhdan-Guillemin were of class
C^{1}
then Hämenstadt’s theorem
2.5
would be a consequence of Besson-Courtois-Gallot’s result. Indeed, recall that
h_{0}
denotes the hyperbolic metric of
M
and that
h
is a Riemannian metric on
M
with
\mathrm{sect}_{h}<0
. Assume that there exists a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism
\Phi
conjugating the geodesic flows
g_{t}^{0}
and
g_{t}
of
h_{0}
and
h
respectively. Then the geodesic flows have the same topological entropies (see §
2.2.4
). If we can prove that
(M,h)
and
(M,h_{0})
have the same volume then Besson-Courtois-Gallot’s theorem implies that they are isometric.

To see this, recall that
g_{t}
is a contact Anosov flow: there exists a
1
-form
\lambda
, called the canonical form of
g_{t}
, such that
\lambda(X)=1
(with
X
representing the generator of
g_{t}
) and that vanishes in
E^{s}\oplus E^{u}
(where
E^{s}
and
E^{u}
are the stable and unstable bundles defined in §
2.2.2
). Note that
\lambda\wedge d\lambda^{2}
is the Liouville measure (the volume form) in
T^{1}M
. Hence if
\Phi
conjugates
g_{t}
and
g_{t}^{0}
then
\Phi^{\ast}(\lambda^{0})=\lambda
(where
\lambda^{0}
is the canonical
1
-form of
g_{t}^{0}
), which means that
\Phi
preserves the volume forms of
T^{1}M
, conluding the proof.

However, the conjugacy given by Kazhdan-Guillemin is in general only continuous, and there is no way to pull back of the canonical
1
-form. Hamenstädt found a clever argument, based on thermodynamical formalism and the theory of Hölder cocycles and geodesic currents (see
[
27
,
44
]
), to prove directly that the time-preserving conjugacy must send the Liouville measure of
h
onto that of
h_{0}
. This uses that the Anosov splitting of the geodesic flow of
g_{t}^{0}
is of class
C^{1}
.

3.
Foliated Plateau problems and dynamics of
k
-surfaces

3.1.
Asymptotic Plateau problems for
k
-surfaces

3.1.1.
k
-surfaces

Recall that
(M,h)
denotes a closed and oriented
3
-manifold such that
\mathrm{sect}_{h}\leq-1
and that
X
denotes its universal cover. Let
S
be an oriented surface and
e:S\to X
be a smooth immersion into
X
. Let
N
be the orthogonal unit vector field compatible with the orientation, and let
A(u)=\nabla_{u}N
be the
shape operator
. The
extrinsic curvature
is defined as

(3)

\kappa_{ext}=\det(A).

Gauss’ equation
relates the
intrinsic
and
extrinsic
curvatures of
e(S)\mathop{\subset}X
by

(4)

\kappa_{int}=\kappa_{ext}+\mathrm{sect}_{h}|_{Te(S)}.

The
Gauss lift
of
S
is defined as the surface
\hat{S}=\hat{e}(S)\mathop{\subset}T^{1}X
: this is the surface
S
attached with the unit tangent vector field
N
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
k\in(0,1)
. We define a
k
-surface
as a complete and smooth immersed surface that has constant extrinsic curvature

\kappa_{ext}=k.

Remark 3.2
.

Labourie’s original definition requires that the Gauss lift
\hat{S}\mathop{\subset}T^{1}X
be complete, rather than
S
itself. This is coherent with his compactness result (see Theorem
3.5
).

By Gauss’ equation, if
\mathrm{sect}_{h}\leq-1
everywhere and
0<k<1
we have at all points of a
k
-surface

(5)

\kappa_{int}\leq k-1<0.

3.1.2.
Asymptotic Plateau problem

We formulate the asymptotic Plateau problem for
k
-surfaces inside
(X,h)
. Let
c\mathop{\subset}\partial_{\infty}X
be an Jordan curve. We provide
c
with an
orientation
, that is, we distinguish a connected component of
\partial_{\infty}X\setminus c
that we call the
exterior component
and denote by
\mathrm{Ext}(c)
. This will be thought of as our boundary condition. The other connected component of
\partial_{\infty}X\setminus c
is called the
interior component
and denoted by
\mathrm{Int}(c)
.

Fix
k\in(0,1)
. The
asymptotic Plateau problem
that we want to solve is: find
D\mathop{\subset}X
such that

(1)

D
is a complete embedded and oriented disc in
X
;

(2)

\partial_{\infty}D=c
, where
\partial_{\infty}D
is endowed with the orientation it inherits from
D
; and

(3)

D
has constant extrinsic curvature:
\kappa_{ext}=k
everywhere.

When the properties above are satisfied, we say that
c

spans
the
k
-disc
D
. This is a particular case of the asymptotic Plateau problems considered by Labourie
[
39
]
(see also Smith
[
58
]
). In these references, the authors define more general boundary conditions and characterize which asymptotic Plateau problems have solution (which is automatically unique) and which do not. See, for example, Corollary
3.7
for asymptotic problems without a solution.

The following result is a consequence of
[
39
]
and
[
58
]
. When
X=\mathbb{H}^{3}
it was proven by Rosenberg-Spruck in
[
57
]
.

Theorem 3.3

(Asymptotic Plateau problem)
.

For all
0<k<1
and every oriented Jordan curve
c\mathop{\subset}\partial_{\infty}X
there exists a unique
k
-disc
D_{c}
spanned by
c
. Furthermore, there exists a convex subset
K_{c}\mathop{\subset}X
such that

(1)

\partial K_{c}=D_{c}
;

(2)

for every
\xi\in\mathrm{Int}(c)
, there exists a geodesic ray
\gamma
included in
(K_{c})^{c}
and normal to
D_{c}
such that
\gamma(\infty)=\xi
.

The analogous problem for minimal surfaces instead of
k
-surfaces, in particular the existence and uniqueness of solutions in hyperbolic
3
-space, was studied by Anderson
[
6
]
and Uhlenbeck
[
61
]
in the 1980s.

3.2.
Monge-Ampère equation and
k
-surfaces

3.2.1.
J
-holomorphic curves

Consider the space
W={\mathbb{C}}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}={\mathbb{R}}^{2}\oplus{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Denote
V={\mathbb{R}}^{2}\oplus\{0\}
and
H=\{0\}\oplus{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Define the complex structure on
W
by

(6)

J=\begin{pmatrix}0&J_{0}\\
J_{0}&0\end{pmatrix}

where

(7)

J_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}

represents the usual multiplication by
i
of
{\mathbb{C}}
. A
J
-holomorphic
line of
W
is a
2
-dimensional subspace
L
which is
J
-invariant.

We say that a
2
-dimensional submanifold
S\mathop{\subset}W
is a
J
-holomorphic curve
if the tangent plane at each point of
S
is
J
-holomorphic.

When
L
is the graph
\{(v,Av):v\in V\}
of a
2\times 2
matrix
A
, then
L
is
J
-holomorphic line if and only if

AJ_{0}A=J_{0},

which is equivalent to

A=A^{T}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\det(A)=1.

If we modify the complex structure by setting for some
k>0

(8)

J=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt{k}^{-1}J_{0}\\
\sqrt{k}J_{0}&0\end{pmatrix},

then the graph of
A
is
J
-holomorphic if and only if

A=A^{T}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\det(A)=k.

The tangent plane at
(x_{0},v(x_{0}))
of the graph
\{(x,v(x)):x\in\Omega\}
of a smooth map
v:\Omega\mathop{\subset}V\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is the graph of the linear map
Dv(x_{0})
. So this graph is
J
-holomorphic
, where
J
is given by (
6
) (resp. (
8
)), if and only if at each point
x_{0}\in\Omega
the derivative
Dv(x_{0})
is symmetric and satisfies
\det(Dv(x_{0}))=1
(resp.
\det(Dv(x_{0}))=k
).

3.2.2.
Monge-Ampère’s equation

Let
k>0
. We consider the
Monge-Ampère equation
given by

(9)

\det(D^{2}u)=k,

where
u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a function of class
C^{2}
defined in an open set
\Omega\mathop{\subset}{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
Labourie’s theory of
k
-surfaces relies on the following observation which is a simple consequence of §
3.2.1
.

Theorem 3.4
.

A function
u:\Omega\mathop{\subset}{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
of class
C^{2}
is a solution of the Monge-Ampère equation if and only if the graph of the derivative map
Du:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is a
J
-holomorphic curve.

3.2.3.
Monge-Ampère structures on the unit tangent bundle

Recall that the tangent bundle to
T^{1}X
has a natural splitting

T_{\xi}T^{1}X=H_{\xi}\oplus\xi^{\perp},

where
\xi\in T^{1}X
. Here
H_{\xi}
represents the
horizontal distribution
defined by parallel transport of unit vector fields along curves in
X
; and
\xi^{\perp}
is the tangent plane at
\xi
to the unit sphere
T^{1}_{x}X
(where
x
denotes the basepoint of
\xi
).

The
Sasaki metric
in
T^{1}X
makes this splitting orthogonal and induces the metric
h
in both
H_{\xi}
and
\xi^{\perp}
.

Let
\hat{S}
be the Gauss lift of a smoothly immersed and complete surface
S
. In the coordinates given above, the tangent plane at
\xi\in\hat{S}
is the graph of the shape operator:

T_{\xi}\hat{S}=\{(v,Av):v\in\xi^{\perp}\}\mathop{\subset}W_{\xi}:=\xi^{\perp}\oplus\xi^{\perp}.

The distribution
W
defined above (by
W_{\xi}=\xi^{\perp}\oplus\xi^{\perp}
) is a
4
-dimensional subbundle of
TT^{1}X
that we call the
holonomy bundle
.

The metric and the orientation on the
3
-dimensional manifold
X
provide
TX
with a wedge product
\wedge
. The plane
\xi^{\perp}
then comes with a natural orientation and a canonical complex structure
j_{\xi}
defined for
\nu\in\xi^{\perp}
by

j_{\xi}.\eta=\xi\wedge\eta.

It is clear that
j_{\xi}^{2}=-\mathrm{Id}
on
\xi^{\perp}
. Given
k\in(0,1)
, it is then possible to define the linear transform of
W_{\xi}
that in the splitting
\xi^{\perp}\oplus\xi^{\perp}
reads

(10)

J_{\xi}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt{k}^{-1}j_{\xi}\\
\sqrt{k}j_{\xi}&0\end{pmatrix}.

This defines a
complex structure
on
W
, that is a section
J
of
\text{End}(W)
such that
J^{2}=-\mathrm{Id}
. As in §
3.2.1
, the condition of being a
k
-surface, that is the fact that
\det(A)
is constant equal to
k
(recall that the shape operator
A
is symmetric), can be expressed as follows

(1)

T\hat{S}
is tangent to
W
;

(2)

\hat{S}
is a
J
-holomorphic curve meaning that
T\hat{S}
is stable by
J
, where
J
is given by (
10
); and

(3)

T\hat{S}
is transverse to
V=\{0\}\oplus\xi^{\perp}
.

Complete surfaces
\hat{S}\mathop{\subset}T^{1}X
satisfying items (1) and (2) above are called
Monge-Ampère surfaces
. There exist other examples of Monge-Ampère surfaces, which don’t satisfy (3) and can be obtained as (finite coverings of) the normal bundles
\text{N}\gamma
of complete geodesics
\gamma
. Such a surface is
everywhere tangent
to
V
and is called a
tube
.

3.2.4.
Labourie’s compactness theorem

Using elliptic properties of Monge-Ampère equations, Labourie proves the following compactness theorem in
[
38
]
. The convergence of sequences of
pointed complete Riemannian surfaces immersed in
T^{1}X

(\hat{S},p,\hat{e})
(here
p\in\hat{S}
and
\hat{e}:\hat{S}\to T^{1}X
is the immersion) is to be understood in the sense of
Cheeger-Gromov
. We refer to
[
5
,
58
]
for more details.

Theorem 3.5

(Labourie’s compactness theorem)
.

Let
(\hat{S}_{n},p_{n},\hat{e}_{n})
be a sequence of Gauss lifts to
T^{1}X
of
k
-surfaces such that
\hat{e}_{n}(p_{n})
is precompact in
T^{1}X
. Then the
(\hat{S}_{n},p_{n},\hat{e}_{n})
is precompact in the Cheeger-Gromov sense. Furthermore, any accumulation point is either the Gauss lift of an immersed
k
-surface or a tube.

3.3.
Geometric maximum principles and continuity of
k
-surfaces

Convexity and elliptic properties of Monge-Ampère equations are the principal ingredients in the geometric studies of
k
-surfaces.

3.3.1.
Local geometric maximum principle

We begin with an elementary local result regarding the behavior of convex surfaces.

An oriented surface
S
immersed in
X
is called
convex
if its second fundamental form is positive definite at every point. Let
N
be a unit vector field orienting
S
. We say that another convex surface
S^{\prime}
is an exterior tangent to
S
at a point
p\in S\cap S^{\prime}
if there exists a neighbourhood
U\mathop{\subset}S
of
p
and a smooth function
\lambda:U\to[0,\infty)
such that, for every
x\in U
,
\exp(\lambda(x)N(x))\in S^{\prime}
. We also say that
S
is an interior tangent to
S^{\prime}
.

Theorem 3.6

(Local geometric maximum principle)
.

Let
S
and
S^{\prime}
be two convex oriented surfaces immersed in
(X,h)
. Assume that
S^{\prime}
is an exterior tangent to
S
at a point
p\in S\cap S^{\prime}
. Then

\kappa_{ext}^{S^{\prime}}(p)\leq\kappa_{ext}^{S}(p).

A proof of this theorem can be found in
[
39
, Lemme 2.5.1]
and
[
3
, Lemma 2.3.2]
. As a consequence of this theorem, the following can be proven (see
[
39
, Propositions 2.5.2 and 2.5.3]
).

Corollary 3.7
.

Assume
\mathrm{sect}_{h}\leq-1
and
0<k<1
.

(1)

There is no closed
k
-surface in
X
.

(2)

There is no
pseudo-horospheric

k
-surface in
X
, i.e., a
k
-surface
S\mathop{\subset}X
, oriented by the unit normal vector field
N
, whose image of the horizon map

\textrm{hor}:x\in S\mapsto\exp(\infty N(x))\in\partial_{\infty}X,

is the complement of a singleton in
\partial_{\infty}X
.

The proof of this result uses the fact that when
\mathrm{sect}_{h}\leq-1
, spheres and horospheres have extrinsic curvatures
\geq 1
at every point (see
[
39
, Corollaire 3.1.2]
). Hence a
k
surface cannot be an interior tangent to a sphere or a horosphere. Therefore, Theorem
3.6
immediately implies the first item. The proof of the second item is not as direct since it is not clear that a
pseudo-horosphere
should be interior tangent to a horosphere. But Labourie proves that the existence of such a
k
-surface implies the existence of another
k
-surface which is interior tangent to some horosphere, which is a contradiction: see
[
39
, Lemme 2.4.1]
.

3.3.2.
Global geometric maximum principle

The local maximum principle has significant global consequences in our context.

Theorem 3.8

(Global geometric maximum principle)
.

Let
c
and
c^{\prime}
be two oriented Jordan curves of
\partial_{\infty}X
. Let
D_{c}
and
D_{c^{\prime}}
be the
k
-discs they span, and
K_{c}
and
K_{c^{\prime}}
be the convex sets bounded by these discs (see Theorem
3.3
). Suppose that
\mathrm{Ext}(c)\mathop{\subset}\mathrm{Ext}(c^{\prime})
. Then

K_{c}\mathop{\subset}K_{c^{\prime}}.

The idea is that the condition
\mathrm{Ext}(c)\mathop{\subset}\mathrm{Ext}(c^{\prime})
implies that there exists a relatively compact open set
\Omega
such that the inclusion
K_{c}\setminus\Omega\mathop{\subset}\text{Int}(K_{c^{\prime}})\setminus\Omega
holds. Now if the inclusion does not hold true inside
\Omega
, there will be a tangency point between
D_{c}
and some equidistant to
D_{c}^{\prime}
or with some cylinder over some geodesic (see the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 2.3.1]
) which contradicts the local geometric maximum principle (equidistant to a
k
-surface and cylinder both have extrinsic curvatures
>k
). We refer to
[
3
, Lemmas 2.3.5 and 2.3.6]
for detailed proofs.

3.3.3.
Strong geometric maximum principle

Using the fact that
k
-surfaces are solutions of some elliptic problem (see the previous paragraph), we can also prove a strong geometric maximum principle.

Theorem 3.9

(Strong geometric maximum principle)
.

Let
S
and
S^{\prime}
be two
k
surfaces in
X
. Assume that
S^{\prime}
is an exterior tangent to
S
. Then
S=S^{\prime}
.

This result can be proven using a maximum principle for elliptic problems. For example in
[
3
]
, it is shown that it follows by an application of Aronszajn’s unique continuation principle
[
8
]
. Indeed this principle implies the following result (see
[
3
, Lemma 2.5.4]
).

Proposition 3.10
.

Consider a
k
-surface
S\mathop{\subset}X
and assume that
S^{\prime}
can be written as the graph of a smooth function
f
over an open set
U\mathop{\subset}S
. That is
S^{\prime}=\iota(S)
where

\iota:x\in U\mapsto\exp(f(x)N(x)).

If
\kappa_{ext}^{S^{\prime}}=k
everywhere, we have the following dichotomy:

—

either
f=0
and
S^{\prime}\mathop{\subset}S
;

—

or
f
has nonvanishing Taylor series at all points of
U
and the lower order term of
f
is, up to precomposition by a linear map, a harmonic polynomial.

The strong maximum principle follows directly from this proposition. Indeed, suppose that
S
and
S^{\prime}
are
k
-surfaces such that
S^{\prime}
is an exterior tangent to
S
at a point
x
. Then
S^{\prime}
can be written as a graph of a function
f
over
S
in a neighbourhood of
x
. Since
S^{\prime}
is an exterior tangent to
S
at
x
, we must have
f\geq 0
. By Proposition
3.10
, if
f
is not identically
0
then it behaves close to
x
as a harmonic polynomial (after a linear change of coordinates), which is absurd since harmonic polynomials change sign.

3.3.4.
Continuity of the solutions of the asymptotic Plateau problem

We endow the set of oriented Jordan curves of
\partial_{\infty}X
with the Hausdorff topology. We identify the convex set
K_{c}
and its closure in
\overline{X}=X\cup\partial_{\infty}X
so it becomes compact. If
K
is a compact convex subset of
\overline{X}
we let
\partial_{\infty}K
denote
K\cap\partial_{\infty}X
. In particular, for every Jordan curve
c\mathop{\subset}\partial_{\infty}X
,

\partial_{\infty}K_{c}=\overline{\mathrm{Ext}(c)}.

Let
\hat{\partial}K
denote
\partial K\cup\partial_{\infty}K\mathop{\subset}\widehat{X}
. The first lemma is proven in
[
3
, 2.2.2]
.

Lemma 3.11

(Continuity of the boundary)
.

The map
\hat{\partial}:K\mapsto\hat{\partial}K
, defined over the space of compact, convex subsets of
\overline{X}
, is continuous for the Hausdorff topology.

The next result is a consequence of Labourie’s compactness Theorem
3.5
, which describes the possible ways
k
surfaces can degenerate.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
K\mathop{\subset}\overline{X}
be the Hausdorff limit of a sequence
K_{m}
of compact convex subsets of
\overline{X}
with non-empty interiors whose boundaries
\partial K_{m}\mathop{\subset}X
are smooth and have extrinsic curvature
k
. Then three possibilities can occur

(1)

K
has non-empty interior and
\partial K
is smooth with extrinsic curvature
k
. In that case
\partial K_{m}
converges to
\partial K
in the
C^{\infty}_{loc}
-sense;

(2)

K
is a single point of
\partial_{\infty}X
;

(3)

K
is a complete geodesic of
\overline{X}
.

Theorem 3.13

(Continuity of the Plateau solution)
.

The function
c\mapsto K_{c}
from the space of oriented Jordan curves to the set of compact convex subsets of
\overline{X}
(both endowed with the Hausdorff topology) is continuous. Moreover the function
c\mapsto D_{c}
is continuous in the
C_{loc}^{\infty}
sense.

Proof.

Let
(c_{n})
be a sequence of Jordan curves converging to
c
in the Hausdorff sense. Let
c^{+}
and
c^{-}
be two Jordan curves such that
c^{-}\mathop{\subset}\mathrm{Ext}(c)
and
c\mathop{\subset}\mathrm{Ext}(c^{+})
. So, in particular, when
n
is large enough,
c^{-}\mathop{\subset}\mathrm{Ext}(c_{n})
and
c_{n}\mathop{\subset}\mathrm{Ext}(c^{+})
. Using the global geometric maximum principle, we have that
K_{c^{-}}\mathop{\subset}K_{c_{n}}\mathop{\subset}K_{c^{+}}
for all such
n
. So any accumulation point
K
of the sequence
K_{c_{n}}
satisfies
K_{c^{-}}\mathop{\subset}K\mathop{\subset}K_{c^{+}}
. Using Lemma
3.11
, we have
\overline{\mathrm{Ext}(c^{-})}=\partial_{\infty}K_{c^{-}}\mathop{\subset}\partial_{\infty}K\mathop{\subset}\partial_{\infty}K_{c^{+}}=\overline{\mathrm{Ext}(c^{+})}
. Since
c^{\pm}
are arbitrary, this implies that
\partial_{\infty}K=\overline{\mathrm{Ext}(c)}
. By Theorem
3.12
,
\partial K
is a
k
-surface, so
K=K_{c}
by uniqueness of the asymptotic Plateau problem.
∎

3.4.
Foliated Plateau problems

We now discuss the dynamical properties of several spaces that can be seen as
2
-dimensional analogues of the geodesic flow, which aligns with Gromov’s theory of foliated Plateau problems :
[
22
,
23
]
.

3.4.1.
Labourie’s phase space

Recall that
(M,h_{0})
is a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold, that
h
is a Riemannian manifold on
M
with
\mathrm{sect}_{h}\leq-1
and
X
denotes the universal cover of
M
. We fix a number
k\in(0,1)
.

The phase space of the geodesic equation consists of trajectories with initial conditions. This is the space of all pointed geodesics, also identified with the unit tangent bundle of the manifold.

Analogously, we consider the space
\mathcal{S}_{k}(X)
of all pointed immersed
k
-surfaces
(S,p)
. Let
\mathcal{T}(X)
be the set of tubes. By Labourie’s compactness theorem, the space

\overline{\mathcal{S}_{k}(X)}=\mathcal{S}_{k}(X)\cup\mathcal{T}(X),

3.4.2.
Fibered Plateau problems and action of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})

We first consider the space of marked

Recall that given

(2)

(3)

a subset of

Given the boundary correspondence

We now introduce various spaces, which we view as restrictions of Labourie’s phase space.

—

The space

We have natural boundary maps which are continuous (see for example Lemma

\displaystyle\partial_{\infty}:\mathrm{FKD}_{k,h}(C)\to\mathrm{QC}^{+}(C);(FD,\xi)\mapsto\partial_{\infty}D\ \text{and}

\displaystyle\partial_{\infty}:\mathrm{MKD}_{k,h}(C)\to\mathrm{QC}^{+}(C);(\hat{D},n)\mapsto\partial_{\infty}D\ .

A

has negative intrinsic curvature so it is uniformized by the upper half plane

Now, given a frame

Theorem 3.16

(Fibered Plateau problem)

For all

3.4.3.
Foliated Plateau problem

Our second refinement yields a space of marked
k
-surfaces carrying a natural
foliated structure
: we consider now the case where
C=1
. Let
{\mathcal{C}}^{+}=\text{QC}^{+}(1)
denote the set of oriented round circles in
{\mathcal{C}}^{+}
. In this case, the spaces
\mathrm{FKD}_{k,h}(1)
and
\mathrm{MKD}_{k,h}(1)
identify respectively with the frame bundle
FX
and the unit tangent bundle

, as the following theorem shows.

Theorem 3.17

(Foliated Plateau problem)
.

The Gauss lifts of oriented
k
-disks spanned by round circles of
{\mathcal{C}}^{+}
form a smooth foliation
{\mathcal{F}}_{k,h}
of
T^{1}X
, and their frames form a smooth foliation
F{\mathcal{F}}_{k,h}
of
FX
. Furthermore, these foliations are
\Pi
-invariant.

The situation is different for minimal surfaces, as Lowe’s work
[
46
]
provides examples of a metric
h
with negative sectional curvature such that the space
T^{1}X
is not foliated by lifts of minimal surfaces, that is conjugated to the homogeneous foliation.

The first step for proving Theorem
3.17
is the following proposition.

Proposition 3.18

(First foliated Plateau problem)
.

Let
\xi_{-},\xi_{+}
be two distinct points of
\partial_{\infty}X
. Let
{\mathcal{F}}^{+}
be a foliation by round circles of
\partial_{\infty}X\setminus\{\xi_{-},\xi_{+}\}
, oriented so that
\xi_{-}
belongs to the exterior component of all these circles. Then the family
\{D_{c}\}_{c\in{\mathcal{F}}^{+}}
foliates
X
.

Proof.

Let us sketch the proof. For
c\in{\mathcal{F}}^{+}
, consider the convex set
K_{c}
that is bounded by
D_{c}
, so
\xi^{-}\in\partial_{\infty}K_{c}
(see Theorem
3.3
). By the global geometric maximum principle (Theorem
3.8
), these convex sets are nested. The strong geometric maximum principle (see Theorem
3.9
) shows that the discs
D_{c}
are disjoint.

We show that the family
\{D_{c}\}_{c\in{\mathcal{F}}^{+}}
covers the whole of
X
. Suppose there exists a closed ball
B
disjoint from all discs
D_{c}
. Denote
I=\{c\in{\mathcal{F}}^{+};B\mathop{\subset}K_{c}\}
. This is a non-empty interval (oriented from
\xi^{+}
to
\xi^{-}
), with non-empty complement. Indeed, by convexity
\bigcup_{c\in{\mathcal{F}}^{+}}\overline{K_{c}}=\overline{X}\setminus\{\xi^{+}\}
, and by non-existence of pseudo-horospheric
k
-surfaces,
\bigcap_{c\in{\mathcal{F}}^{+}}\overline{K_{c}}=\{\xi^{-}\}
. The affirmation now follows from the fact that the family
K_{c}
is nested and by continuity of
c\mapsto D_{c}
.

Note now that, for all
c
in the closure of the complement of
I
, the interior of
B
is contained in the complement of
K_{c}
. It follows that if
c
is a boundary point of
I
, then
D_{c}
meets
B
non-trivially, which is absurd.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.17
.

Let us sketch the proof that if
c,c^{\prime}
are distinct oriented round circles of
\partial_{\infty}X
, then the Gauss lifts
\widehat{D_{c}}
and
\widehat{D_{c^{\prime}}}
are disjoint. Recall Proposition
3.10
. If the Gauss lifts
\widehat{D_{c}}
and
\widehat{D_{c^{\prime}}}
intersect at
(x,v)\in T^{1}X
then, in a neighbourhood of
x
, the surface
D_{c^{\prime}}
looks like the graph of a harmonic polynomial of degree
d\geq 2
. Hence the intersection locus (as the zero set of this polynomial) is the union of
2d
smooth segments that intersect at
x
. This implies that
\overline{D_{c}}\cap\overline{D_{c^{\prime}}}
is a graph embedded in
\overline{X}
whose vertices have valency
\geq 4
(if they belong to
X
) or
3
(if they belong to
\partial_{\infty}X
). Euler’s formula for planar graphs shows that such a graph must have a closed loop, which bounds two discs
\Omega^{\prime}\mathop{\subset}\overline{D_{c^{\prime}}}
and
\Omega^{\prime}\mathop{\subset}\overline{D_{c^{\prime}}}
, whose union is a sphere
S
. Recall that by Theorem
3.18
,
D_{c}
can be seen as the leaf of a foliation of
X
by
k
-surfaces so the sphere
S
must be interior tangent to some leaf of this foliation, which contradicts the geometric maximum principle.

The preceding argument, together with the invariance of the domain, proves that the set
\{\widehat{D_{c}};c,\,\text{oriented round circle}\}
is open in
T^{1}X
. By the compactness theorem, this set is also closed. It therefore covers the whole of
T^{1}X
, yielding the desired foliation.
∎

3.4.4.
The homogeneous case

Assume that
X=\mathbb{H}^{3}
. In that case the foliation described in Theorem
3.17
is smoothly conjugated to a nice homogeneous model.

Let us first consider the frame bundle of
\mathbb{H}^{3}

F\mathbb{H}^{3}=\{(x,v,e_{1},e_{2});x\in\mathbb{H}^{3},\,(v,e_{1},e_{2}),\text{oriented orthonormal basis of }T_{x}\mathbb{H}^{3}\}.

Note first that the natural action of
\mathrm{Isom}^{+}(\mathbb{H}^{3})\simeq\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
on the frame bundle

is free and transitive, so that, up to a choice of base-frame,

\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on

, which we denote by
\alpha_{0}
. The orbit under this action of any point
\xi:=(v,e_{1},e_{2})
is then simply the frame bundle

of the oriented totally geodesic plane

passing through
p
, with outward-pointing normal
v
. Alternatively, we may identify
F\mathbb{H}^{3}
with the set of uniformizing maps of oriented totally geodesic planes of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
.

This right action
\alpha_{0}
is smoothly conjugated to the right action
\alpha_{k}
. Indeed let
FP
be the frame bundle of an oriented totally geodesic plane
P
. Write
k=\tanh^{2}(R)
and consider the time
R
map of the
frame flow

\hat{G}_{R}:F_{h_{0}}\tilde{X}\to F_{h_{0}}\tilde{X},(p,v,e_{1},e_{2})\mapsto(G_{R}(p,v),e_{1}^{\prime},e_{2}^{\prime})
, where
G_{R}
denotes the time
R
map of the geodesic flow and
(e_{1}^{\prime},e_{2}^{\prime})
denote the parallel transport of
(e_{1},e_{2})
along this flow. The image of the frame bundle
FP
of the totally geodesic plane

is simply the frame bundle

of the
k
-disk
D
spanned by
\partial_{\infty}P
, and
\hat{G}_{R}
is simply a uniformizing map for

. It follows that
\hat{G}_{R}
conjugates
\alpha_{0}
and
\alpha_{k}
. In summary, we have the following identification.

Theorem 3.19

(The homogeneous model)
.

The right action
\alpha_{k}
of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on
F\mathbb{H}^{3}
is smoothly conjugated to the homogeneous action of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
by multiplication on the right.

Since

is a principal
\text{SO}(2)
-bundle,
T^{1}\mathbb{H}^{3}
likewise identifies with the quotient space
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})/\text{SO}(2)
. The images of the orbits of the homogeneous
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-action under this identification are then the Gauss lifts of totally umbilical planes. It follows that the
k
-surface foliation of

is likewise smoothly conjugate to the homogeneous foliation
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})/\text{SO}(2)
by
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-orbits.

3.5.
Rigidity of foliated Plateau problems

We now review the rigidity properties of the object defined above.

3.5.1.
Rigidity of the

-surface foliation

We saw that the unit tangent bundle of a simply connected negatively curved surface can be identified with the set of oriented triples of its ideal boundary. We discuss a three dimensional analogue of this identification.

We let

Now consider the boundary correspondence

Theorem 3.20

(

-surface foliations.

Theorem 3.21
.

If

3.5.2.
Gromov’s geodesic rigidity revisited

It is interesting to note that our foliation by

-surfaces may be used to give another proof of Gromov’s geodesic rigidity theorem that provides a canonical conjugacy. Let us describe a
1

Given a marked disc
(D,z)

z^{\prime}=R_{\partial D}(z)
denote the image of

be the one-parameter family of marked discs invariant by the group of hyperbolic Möbius maps fixing

of MD parametrized by the set of pairs of points of

Consider once more the boundary correspondence
\alpha:{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to\partial_{\infty}X

(

The map

4.
Equidistribution, asymptotic counting and geometric rigidity

In Section 2, we discussed the problem of counting closed geodesics in negative curvature. Here, we are interested in the analogous asymptotic counting problem for closed essential surfaces. We first address the problem of the existence of such surfaces.

4.1.
Essential surfaces, topological counting and quasi-Fuchsian groups

4.1.1.
Quasi-Fuchsian groups

Let
C>1
. A
C
-quasi-Fuchsian group is a discrete and torsion-free subgroup
\Gamma
of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
isomorphic to a cocompact lattice of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
whose limit set
\partial_{\infty}\Gamma
is a
C
-quasicircle. A
1
-quasi-Fuchsian group is nothing but a Fuchsian surface subgroup of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.

4.1.2.
Existence results

The existence of surface subgroups in the fundamental group of a hyperbolic
3
-manifold was an open question until the work of Kahn-Marković (see
[
33
]
). In this work, the authors show how to construct infinitely many closed surfaces in a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold
M
(see also Bergeron’s Bourbaki seminar
[
12
]
for a clear exposition). This theorem was generalized by Hämenstadt
[
29
]
and by Kahn-Labourie-Mozes
[
31
]
(we refer for Kassel’s Bourbaki seminar
[
35
]
for a clear exposition). The compact subgroups constructed by Kahn-Marković are quasi-Fuchsian, and can be made almost Fuchsian.

Theorem 4.1

(Kahn-Marković’s existence theorem
[
33
]
)
.

Let
(M,h_{0})
be a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a hyperbolic surface
S
and an immersion
\iota:S\to M
such that

(1)

the induced map
\iota_{\ast}:\pi_{1}(S)\to\Pi
is injective and the image is a
(1+\varepsilon)
-quasi-fuchsian group;

(2)

\iota
is
(1+\varepsilon)
-quasi-geodesic, i.e., the image of a complete geodesic of
S
is a
(1+\varepsilon)
-quasi-geodesic in
M
.

4.1.3.
Topological counting

By a theorem of Thurston, given a positive integer
g
, there are finitely many conjugacy classes of surface subgroups of genus up to
g
in the fundamental group of a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold. We refer to
[
42
, §3.3]
for a proof of this fact using minimal surfaces. Kahn and Marković provide a precise topological asymptotic counting result showing that the number of such conjugacy classes grows superexponentially with genus.

Given a cocompact lattice
\Pi\leq\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, we let QF denote the set of conjugacy classes
[\Gamma]
of quasi-fuchsian subgroups of
\Pi
. Given
[\Gamma]\in
QF we let g
(\Gamma)
denote the genus of any representative
\Gamma
.

Theorem 4.2

(Kahn-Marković’s topological counting
[
32
]
)
.

If
(M,h_{0})
is a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold then

(11)

\lim_{g\to\infty}\frac{1}{2g\log(g)}\log\#\{[\Gamma]\in\mathrm{QF};\,\mathrm{g}(\Gamma)\leq g\}=1.

Kahn-Marković’s result shows that the above limit also holds if one counts all conjugacy classes of surface groups of genus
\leq g
and not only the quasi-Fuchsian ones.

4.2.
Geometric asymptotic counting and rigidity results

The purpose of
[
18
]
and
[
3
]
is to give a geometric asymptotic counting. The problem of finding geometric representatives to conjugacy classes of surface subgroups is treated there by solving asymptotic Plateau problems for minimal surfaces (in
[
18
]
) or for
k
-surfaces (in
[
3
]
).

Assume that
h
is a Riemannian metric on
M
such that
\mathrm{sect}_{h}\leq-1
, and fix a number
0<k<1
.

4.2.1.
k
-surface representatives

Let
\Gamma
be a quasi-Fuchsian subgroup of
\Pi
. Its limit set
\partial_{\infty}\Gamma
is an oriented quasicircle so we can consider the
k
-disc
D_{k,h}(\partial_{\infty}\Gamma)
that it spans. By uniqueness of the solution of the asymptotic Plateau problem, the disc
D_{k,h}(\partial_{\infty}\Gamma)
is
\Gamma
-invariant. The quotient

S_{k,h}([\Gamma])=D_{k,h}(\partial_{\infty}\Gamma)/\Gamma\mathop{\subset}M,

is naturally a closed surface whose fundamental group represents
[\Gamma]
by construction. This is the unique
k
-surface representative of
[\Gamma]
.

4.2.2.
The geometric counting

We are now ready to state the main result of
[
3
]
, which is a geometric asymptotic counting result. This result defines an entropy that counts closed quasi-Fuchsian
k
-surfaces according to their areas and that satisfies a rigid inequality, analogous to Besson-Courtois-Gallot’s theorem
2.4
.

Definition 4.3

(Area entropy of
k
-surfaces)
.

Let
h
be a Riemannian metric with
\mathrm{sect}_{h}\leq-1
. Fix
0<k<1
and define the area entropy of
k
-surfaces as

\mathrm{Ent}_{k}(M,h)=\liminf_{A\to\infty}\frac{1}{A\log(A)}\log\#\left\{[\Gamma]\in\mathrm{QF};\mathrm{Area}_{h}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma])\leq A\right\}.

Theorem 4.4

(Geometric counting of
k
-surfaces)
.

Let
h
be a Riemannian metric with
\mathrm{sect}_{h}\leq-1
and
0<k<1
. Then we have the following chain of inequalities

\frac{H(M,h)^{2}}{2\pi}\geq\mathrm{Ent}_{k}(M,h)\geq\mathrm{Ent}_{k}(M,h_{0})=\frac{1-k}{2\pi},

where
H(M,h)
denotes the topological entropy of the geodesic flow.

Moreover the equality
\mathrm{Ent}_{k}(M,h)=\mathrm{Ent}_{k}(M,h_{0})
holds if and only if
h
and
h_{0}
are isometric.

The upper bound was obtained by Calegari-Marques-Neves in
[
18
]
and is a consequence of the two-dimensional analogue of Besson-Courtois-Gallot’s inequality obtained by Katok in
[
36
]
. We refer to
[
18
, Section 7]
or
[
42
, Proposition 6.3]
for a complete argument.

The equality
\mathrm{Ent}_{k}(M,h_{0})=\frac{1-k}{2\pi}
easily follows from the Gauss-Bonnet theorem. Indeed by Gauss’ equation (
4
), in hyperbolic geometry,
k
-surfaces have intrinsic curvature
k-1<0
. Hence the Gauss-Bonnet theorem implies that for a closed
k
-surface
S\mathop{\subset}(M,h_{0})
of genus
g

g=\frac{1-k}{4\pi}\mathrm{Area}_{h_{0}}(S)+1.

Then one computes the entropy in this case by using the topological asymptotic counting of Kahn-Marković. This Gauss-Bonnet argument also proves the inequality
\mathrm{Ent}_{k}(M,h)\geq\mathrm{Ent}_{k}(M,h_{0})
. Indeed, if
\mathrm{sect}_{h}\leq-1
then the Gauss-Bonnet theorem and the Gauss equation together give for every conjugacy class of quasi-Fuchsian group
[\Gamma]

\displaystyle(1-k)\mathrm{Area}_{h_{0}}(S_{k,h_{0}}([\Gamma]))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi(\text{g}(\Gamma)-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{S_{k,h}([\Gamma])}\left(\left|\mathrm{sect}_{h}|_{TS_{k,h}([\Gamma])}\right|-k\right)d\mathrm{Area}_{h}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-k)\mathrm{Area}_{h}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma])).

This means that the area of a quasi-Fuchsian
k
-surface is smaller in variable curvature
\geq-1
than in the hyperbolic case. Hence, there are more closed quasi-Fuchsian
k
-surfaces with small area in
(M,h)
than in
(M,h_{0})
. The inequality follows. The rigidity of the equality is the interesting result. Note that we get

(12)

\mathrm{Area}_{h_{0}}(S_{k,h_{0}}([\Gamma]))=\mathrm{Area}_{h}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma]))\Longrightarrow\mathrm{sect}_{h}|_{TS_{k,h}}=-1.

4.2.3.
Rigidity of the hyperbolic marked area spectrum

We state another rigidity result which is more in the spirit of the marked length spectrum rigidity. We define the marked area spectrum of
k
-surfaces for the metric
h
as

(13)

\mathrm{MAS}_{k,h}:\text{QF}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,[\Gamma]\mapsto\mathrm{Area}_{h}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma])).

The following rigidity theorem was proven in
[
3
]
. It is an analogue of Hamenstädt’s result (Theorem
2.5
).

Theorem 4.5

(Rigidity of the hyperbolic marked area spectrum)
.

Let
(M,h_{0})
be a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold. Let
h
be a Riemannian metric on
M
with
\mathrm{sect}_{h}\leq-1
and
k\in(0,1)
. Then
\mathrm{MAS}_{k,h}=\mathrm{MAS}_{k,h_{0}}
if and only if
h
and
h_{0}
are isometric.

Note that by (
12
) if
\mathrm{MAS}_{k,h}=\mathrm{MAS}_{k,h_{0}}
, the sectional curvature of
h
must be equal to
-1
in restriction to all tangent planes of all closed quasi-Fuchsian surfaces in
M
. The key point is to prove the equidistribution of tangent planes of quasi-Fuchsian
k
-surfaces to prove that the sectional curvature equals
-1
in
all
planes of
TM
. This would imply that the metric

is hyperbolic. Mostow’s rigidity theorem then proves that

must be isometric to
h_{0}
.

4.3.
Equidistribution of quasi-Fuchsian
k
-surfaces

We use the formalism of foliated Plateau problems introduced in the previous section and discuss the equidistribution property needed to prove Theorems
4.4
and
4.5
.

4.3.1.
Conformal currents,
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-invariant measures and laminar measures

The main reason we introduced the fibered Plateau problem in §
3.4.2
is that it enables us to generalize the identification between geodesic currents and invariant measures by the geodesic flow (see Bonahon
[
14
]
for example) in our context. Recall that geodesic currents are
\Pi
-invariant measures on the set of geodesics, i.e., the set
\partial_{\infty}^{(2)}X
of pairs of points of the ideal boundary, and that they correspond to measures on
T^{1}X
both invariant by
\Pi
and by the geodesic flow. See §
2.4.3
for the example of the Bowen-Margulis measure.

Consider the bundles
\partial_{\infty}:\text{FKD}_{k,h}(C)\to\text{QC}^{+}(C)
and
\partial_{\infty}:\text{MKD}_{k,h}(C)\to\text{QC}^{+}(C)
defined in §
3.4.2
. Recall that the space
\mathrm{QC}^{+}(C)
is separable and locally compact. We say that a Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathrm{FKD}_{k,h}(C)
(resp.
\mathrm{MKD}_{k,h}(C)
) is
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-invariant whenever there exists a Borel measure
m
on
\mathrm{QC}^{+}(C)
such that, in every trivializing chart
U\times F
of
\partial_{\infty}
,

\mu|_{U\times F}=m|_{U}\times\lambda_{F}\ ,

where
\lambda_{F}
denotes the Haar measure on
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
(resp. the hyperbolic area of
{\mathbb{D}}
). Borel regular
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-invariant measures on
\mathrm{FKD}(C)
(resp.
\mathrm{MKD}(C)
) are trivially in one-to-one correspondence with Borel regular measures on
\mathrm{QC}^{+}
(see
[
3
, Lemma 4.2.1.]
). We call
m
the
factor
of
\mu
, and we call
\mu
the
lift
of
m
. By construction, a
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-invariant measure
\mu
is also
\Pi
-invariant if and only if its factor
m
is.

In other words, we have natural bijective correspondences between each the following classes of objects (see
[
3
, §4.2]
for a slightly different formalism).

(1)

\Pi
-invariant Borel regular measures
\hat{m}
on the space
\text{QC}^{+}(C)
of oriented
C
-quasicircles;
these are the analogues in our context of geodesic currents
.

(2)

(\Pi,\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))
bi-invariant Borel measures
\hat{\nu}
over
\mathrm{FKD}_{k,h}(C)
;
these are the analogues in our context of measures invariant under the geodesic flow
.

(3)

\Pi
-invariant Borel measures
\hat{\mu}
on
\text{MKD}_{k,h}(C)
which are totally invariant for the lamination
{\mathcal{L}}_{k,h}
, that is, whose disintegration on each leaf of
{\mathcal{L}}_{k,h}
is a multiple of the hyperbolic area;
these are the analogues in our context of totally invariant measures of the geodesic foliation
.

According to Labourie’s terminology (see
[
42
,
50
]
), measures of the first class are called
conformal currents
, measures of the second are called
(\Pi,\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))
-bi-invariant measures
, and measures of the third are called
laminar measures
.

Conformal currents were the main topic of the recent and very interesting work
[
50
]
, where the authors show in particular how to combine them with geodesic currents. We must also cite
[
34
]
for an application of conformal currents in the study of random minimal surfaces. We expect that similar results should hold for
k
-surfaces.

An important example is given by the generalization of Dirac masses supported by closed geodesics. Let
[\Gamma]\in\text{QF}
and
S=S_{k,h}([\Gamma])
. Let
C
the quasi-conformality constant of
c:=\partial_{\infty}\Gamma
. Let
\delta(c)
be the Dirac mass on
\text{QC}^{+}(C)
supported by
c
. We now introduce the following conformal measure, bi-invariant measure, and laminated measure.

The conformal current associated to
S
. We define

\hat{m}(c)=\frac{1}{2\pi|\chi(S)|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Pi/\Gamma}\delta(\gamma\cdot c)\ ,

where
\gamma\cdot c
abusively denotes the image of
c
under any representative of
\gamma\in\Pi/\Gamma
. By construction,
m(c)
is infinite, atomic and
\Pi
-invariant. We call it the
conformal current
associated to
c
.

The bi-invariant measure associated to
S
. Recall that the frame bundle
FD_{c}
of
D_{c}
naturally identifies, up to a choice of base point, with
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Let
\omega_{k,h}(c)
denote the image of the Haar measure under this identification. We define the
(\Pi,\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))
-bi-invariant measure associated to
S
over
\mathrm{FKD}_{k,h}(C)
by

\hat{\nu}_{k,h}(c)=\frac{1}{2\pi|\chi(S)|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Pi/\Gamma}\omega_{k,h}(\gamma.c)\ .

This measure is analogous to the lift to
S_{h}\tilde{X}
of the Dirac mass supported by a periodic orbit of the geodesic flow.

The laminar measure associated to
S
.
Let
\lambda_{k,h}(c)
denote the area measure of the Poincaré metric of the disk
D_{c}
. The laminar measure associated to
S
is the measure

\hat{\mu}_{k,h}(c)=\frac{1}{2\pi|\chi(S)|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Pi/\Gamma}\lambda_{k,h}(\gamma.c)\ .

The quotient of this measure under the action of
\Pi
yields a Borel regular measure
\mu_{k,h}
over
\mathrm{MKD}_{k,h}(C)/\Pi
supported on
\hat{S}
, where here
\hat{S}
is viewed as a closed leaf of
{\mathcal{L}}_{k,h}
.

Note that the measures on
\text{QC}^{+}(C)
do not depend of the metric
h
, nor on the constant
k
. Only the
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-invariant measure
\hat{\mu}_{k,h}(c)
does. This allows one to change the metric and work with the hyperbolic metric when needed. This is useful when one needs to apply Ratner’s theory.

4.3.2.
Ratner’s theorem and a criterion for equidistribution

We will follow the main idea of
[
18
]
(see also
[
42
]
) and consider limits of measures
\hat{\mu}_{k,h}(c)
when
c
tends to a round circle
c_{0}
.

Definition 4.6

(Ergodic conformal currents)
.

A conformal current
\hat{m}
in
\mathrm{QC}^{+}(C)
is said to be
ergodic
if every
\Pi
-invariant measurable function on
\mathrm{QC}^{+}(C)
is constant
\hat{m}
-almost everywhere.

Note that the space
{\mathcal{C}}^{+}
of round circles in
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is a
3
-dimensional manifold carrying a natural Haar measure which we denote by
\mathrm{Leb}
.

Theorem 4.7

(Classification of ergodic conformal currents)
.

For every ergodic conformal current
\hat{m}
on
{\mathcal{C}}^{+}
, the following dichotomy holds.

—

Either
\hat{m}
is proportional to
\mathrm{Leb}
.

—

Or
\hat{m}
is the conformal current of a closed Fuchsian surface.

Proof.

We work with the hyperbolic metric
h:=h_{0}
on
\tilde{X}
. Let
\hat{\nu}_{0}
be an ergodic measure over
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\simeq F_{h_{0}}\tilde{X}
which is both invariant under the left action of
\Pi
, and under the right action by multiplication of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. By Ratner’s classification theorem
[
55
]
(see
[
21
]
for a simple proof for the case of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-actions), this measure is
homogeneous
. That is, it is the Haar measure supported on some closed orbit
v_{0}G
of some closed, connected Lie subgroup
G\leq\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
containing
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Since the only closed and connected Lie subgroups of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
containing
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
are
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
itself, this yields the following dichotomy:

—

either
\nu_{0}
is the Haar measure of
F_{h_{0}}\tilde{X}/\Pi
;

—

or
\nu_{0}
is the Haar measure of the frame bundle of some closed, totally-umbilic surface of
\tilde{X}/\Pi
.

The result now follows.
∎

We deduce the following dichotomy for laminar measures.

Theorem 4.8

(Dichotomy for laminar measures)
.

There exists a unique ergodic laminar probability measure
\mu
on
T^{1}M
with full support. All other ergodic laminar measures on
T^{1}M
are associated to closed Fuchsian
k
-surfaces.

Remark 4.9
.

When
\Pi
contains no Fuchsian subgroup, there exists a unique laminar probability measure on
T^{1}M
which is fully supported.

Proof.

Let
\hat{\mu}
denote the lift of
\mu
to
T^{1}M
and let
\hat{m}
denote its associated conformal current on
{\mathcal{C}}^{+}
. If
\mu
has full support, then so too does
\hat{m}
so that, by Theorem
4.7
,
\hat{m}
is proportional to
\mathrm{Leb}
, and the first assertion follows. Likewise, if
\mu
does not have full support, then, by Theorem
4.7
again,
\hat{\mu}
is the laminar measure associated to some closed Fuchsian
k
-surface, as in §
4.3.1
, and the second assertion follows.
∎

4.3.3.
Equidistribution and Kahn-Marković’s sequences

The following facts are the key to our rigidity results.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
\Gamma_{n}
be a sequence of
C_{n}
-quasi-Fuchsian subgroups of
\Pi
and
S_{n}=S_{k,h}([\Gamma_{n}])
be their
k
-surfaces representatives in
(M,h)
. Assume that
\lim_{n\to\infty}C_{n}=1
. Then any accumulation point of
\hat{\mu}_{k,h}(c_{n})
is a laminar measure supported on
\mathrm{MKD}_{k,h}(1)\simeq T^{1}X
.

Kahn-Marković’s proof of Theorem
4.1
provides a sequence of surfaces with remarkable properties. In particular, the following theorem is relevant to us.

Theorem 4.11
.

There exists a sequence
\Gamma_{n}
of
C_{n}
-quasi-Fuchsian subgroups of
\Pi
satisfying the following properties

(1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}C_{n}=1
;

(2)

the sequence of invariant measures
\hat{\mu}_{k,h}(\partial_{\infty}\Gamma_{n})
converges to a laminar measure
\hat{\mu}_{\infty}
on
T^{1}X
with total support.

The proof of this theorem may be found in
[
3
]
and consists in showing that the
k
-surfaces representing Kahn-Marković’s surfaces obtained on
[
33
]
do not accumulate in closed surfaces. The result follows from the dichotomy for
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-invariant measures in

(Theorem
4.7
).

4.3.4.
Rigidity of the hyperbolic marked area spectrum of
k
-surfaces

We will only prove Theorem
4.5
. Assume that we have an equality between the marked area spectra
\mathrm{MAS}_{k,h}=\mathrm{MAS}_{k,h_{0}}
. As we remarked in §
4.2.3
, this implies that for every quasi-Fuchsian
k
-surface

the sectional curvature equals
-1
in
TS
.

Define the function
\sigma
associating to
(D,p)\in\text{MKD}_{k,h}(C)
(for

) the sectional curvature of

in restriction to

. When

this function may be thought of as the function
\mathrm{sect}_{h}:T^{1}X\to{\mathbb{R}}
associating to

the sectional curvature in

, i.e., in the tangent space of the leaf containing

Consider the Kahn-Marković’s sequence given in Theorem

. Then the function
\sigma=-1
,
\hat{\mu}_{k,h}(\partial_{\infty}\Gamma_{n})
-everywhere. Hence, we have

,
\hat{\mu}_{\infty}
-almost everywhere. Since

is continuous, we obtain

In a recent work
[

]
we obtained a number of results that go beyond the rigidity of the marked area spectrum, looking at the

(1)

These results can be compared with the classical measure rigidity theorems by Katok and Ledrappier
[

37

43

.
It would be tempting to think that they fit into a thermodynamical theory of

-surfaces, analogous to that of the geodesic flow.

5.2.
Boundary rigidity problem

The boundary rigidity problem asks whether the Riemannian metric on a

can be determined by the restriction of the Riemannian distance between points at the boundary. Recall that a simple manifold is a Riemannian ball with strictly convex boundary without conjugate points such that any pair of points in the boundary can be connected by a unique minimizing geodesic. This applies in particular to any negatively curved metric with strictly convex boundary. See for example
[
20

62
]

The following question is a

[
2
]
for a related question for area minimizing surfaces.

We consider a ball
B
in hyperbolic
3
-space
\mathbb{H}^{3}
. We consider the set
{\mathcal{C}}^{+}(\partial B)
of oriented round circles of
\partial B
. Fax a negatively curved metric

on

with
\mathrm{sect}_{h}\leq-1
and a number

. For any

there exists a unique

-disc

such that

. we can consider the marked boundary data

\mathrm{MAS}_{h,k,\partial B}:{\mathcal{C}}^{+}(\partial B)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,c\mapsto\mathrm{Area}_{h}(D_{c}).

Question 5.3
.

Assume that
h
and
h_{0}
have the same marked boundary datas. Are
h
and
h_{0}
isometric?

Even a local rigidity in the spirit of
[
20
]
would be interesting. The difficulty is the lack of tools from hyperbolic dynamics.

5.3.
Equidistribution of closed surfaces, thermodynamical formalism and cohomological equations

The theory of geodesic currents in negative curvature is well understood. Basically they are in bijective correspondence with cohomology classes of Hölder continuous potentials in
T^{1}M
. We refer to Ledrappier’s paper
[
44
]
for an exposition of this theory.

By contrast, the measures that arise as limits of quasi-Fuchsian surfaces are not well understood. A natural question is if there is an analogue of Bowen-Margulis’ measure in our context. We refer to
[

]
for a systematic study for the case of minimal surfaces. Let
{\mathcal{S}}_{k,h,\varepsilon}
be the family of

-quasi-Fuchsian

denote the measure in

supported by the Gauss-lift of

induced by its hyperbolic area.

Question 5.4
.

Consider the following probability measures

\frac{1}{\#\{S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{k,h,\varepsilon};\mathrm{Area}_{h}(S)\leq R\}}\sum_{S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{k,h,\varepsilon};\mathrm{Area}_{h}(S)\leq R}\delta(S).

What can be said about the accumulation points of these measures as
R\to\infty
and
\varepsilon\to 0
?

As we mentionned before, it would be interesting to read the previous questions through the eye of thermodynamical formalism theory. A concrete starting point for establishing a theormodynamical formalism for
k
-surfaces, would be to get an analogous of Livšic’s and Guillemin-Kazhdan’s theorems.

Question 5.5
.

What can be said about a Hölder continuous function
F:T^{1}M\to{\mathbb{R}}
that integrates
0
on the Gauss lifts of all quasi-Fuchsian
k
-surfaces?

What can be said about a closed
2
-form that integrates
0
on all quasi-Fuchsian
k
-surfaces?

A subtle and nontrivial point in the question above is that of the regularity of objects that we consider. We might want to apply cohomological result (such as Livšic’s) to Hölder continuous objects like in the proof of Theorem
2.6
.

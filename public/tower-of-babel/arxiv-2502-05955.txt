1.
Introduction

The Sasaki metric, introduced by Shigeo Sasaki in [
7
,
1958
], provides a natural metric on the tangent bundle
TM
of a Riemannian manifold
M
. This metric allows associating a volume with each unit vector field on
M
, considering them as sections of the tangent bundle
TM
and regarding this volume as the measure induced by the Sasaki metric on these sections, viewed as submanifolds of
TM
. If
M
is a surface and
V
a unit vector field on
M
. The
area of a unit vector field
V
on
M
is defined as the area of the surface
V(M)
in
T^{1}M
. For a comprehensive understanding of the geometry of the tangent bundle, we suggest consulting chapter
1
of the book
Harmonic Vector Fields: Variational Principles and Differential Geometry
by Sorin Dragomir and Domenico Perrone, [
5
,
2012
].

Throughout the years, results concerning the minimization of volume or area of unit vector fields on
M
have been obtained. In the next section, we shall present these results related to the area minimization of unit vector fields on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\backslash\{N\}
and
\mathbb{S}^{2}\backslash\{N,S\}
.

In this work, we establish a minimization result for unit vector fields defined on spherical annuli in the Euclidean sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. More precisely, we consider regions of the unit Euclidean sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, bounded by two parallels circles. We consider unit vector fields
V
that satisfy the following properties:

(i) The vector field
V
is tangent to the two parallel circles, which are oriented in opposite directions, with latitudes given by
\alpha_{0}
and
-\alpha_{0}
, respectively.

(ii) The angle
\theta
that
V
makes with the parallels is constant along them.

(iii) The unit vector field
V
is perpendicular to the meridian
S_{0}
.

Considering these conditions, the main questions we explore are

1.

To determine the lower bounds for the area of
V
on spherical annuli, where
\alpha_{0}\in(0,\pi/2)
.

2.

To identify and explicitly describe the unit vector fields that achieve this minimum and to provide a geometric and qualitative description of them for each choice of
\alpha_{0}
.

A precise definition of these vector fields on spherical annuli in the Euclidean sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
is provided in the definition
5
. Therefore, we prove the following theorem

Theorem 1
.

Let
V
be a unit vector field as described in definition
5
. Let
\theta(p)
be the angle function of
V
with respect to the parallels at the point
p=(\alpha,\beta)
in
A_{\alpha_{0}}
. If
\theta(-\alpha_{0},\beta)=0
and
\theta(\alpha_{0},\beta)=\pi
, for all
\beta\in(0,2\pi]
. Then,

(i)

area(V)\geq 2\pi\cos\left(\alpha_{0}\right)+K_{\alpha_{0}}
, where
K_{\alpha_{0}}
is constant depending just on
\alpha_{0}\in\left(0,\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)
.

(ii)

If the angle function
\theta:[-\alpha_{0},\alpha_{0}]\longrightarrow[0,+\infty)
is given by

\theta(\alpha)=\arcsin\bigg(\cot(\alpha_{0})\tan(\alpha)\bigg)+\frac{\pi}{2},

then, the vector field

V(\alpha,\beta)=\cos\big(\theta(\alpha)\big)\,e_{1}+\sin\big(\theta(\alpha)\big)\,e_{2}

achieves the lower bound of (i).

2.
Area-minimizing unit vector fields on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\backslash\{N\}
or
\mathbb{S}^{2}\backslash\{N,S\}

Let
M
be a closed oriented Riemannian manifold and
V
a unit vector field on
M
. Consider the unit tangent bundle
T^{1}M
equipped with the Sasaki metric. The
volume of a unit vector field
V
is defined (see [
6
,
1986
]) as the volume of the submanifold
V(M)
, the image of the immersion
V:M\rightarrow T^{1}M
,

\vol(V):=\vol(V(M)).

If
M
is a surface and
V
a unit vector field on
M
. The
area of a unit vector field
V
on
M
is defined as the area of the surface
V(M)
in
T^{1}M
, and it coincides with the volume of a unit vector field
V

\displaystyle\area(V):=\area(V(M))=\vol(V).

It is well known that there is no globally defined vector field on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, so the infimum of the area is achieved only when there is at least one singularity. The Pontryagin vector field is a unit vector field with one singularity obtained by parallel translating a given vector along any great circle passing through a given point. In [
1
,
2010
], Vincent Borrelli and Olga Gil-Medrano showed that Pontryagin vector fields of the unit 2-sphere with one singularity are area-minimizing.

In [
2
,
2008
] Fabiano Brito, Pablo Chacón and David L. Johnson established an explicit relationship between the volume of a unit vector field and its Poincaré index around isolated singularities.

Theorem 2

(Brito, Chacón and Johnson,
2
)
.

Let
M=\mathbb{S}^{n}\backslash\left\{N,S\right\}
,
n=2
or
3
, be the standard Euclidean sphere with two antipodal points
N
and
S
removed. Let
V
be a unit vector field defined on
M
. Then,

i)

for
n=2
,
\vol(V)\geq\frac{1}{2}(\pi+|I_{V}(N)|+|I_{V}(S)|-2)\vol(\mathbb{S}^{2})
,

ii)

for
n=3
,
\vol(V)\geq(|I_{V}(N)|+|I_{V}(S)|)\vol(\mathbb{S}^{3})
,

where
I_{V}(p)
stands the Poincaré index of
V
around
p
.

Theorem
2
has been extended to odd dimensional spheres
\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}
as follows:

Theorem 3

(Brito, Gomes and Gonçalves,
4
)
.

If
V
is a unit vector field on
\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}\backslash\{\pm p\}
, then

\vol(V)\geq\frac{\pi}{4}\vol(\mathbb{S}^{2n})(|I_{V}(p)|+|I_{V}(-p)|).

In [
3
,
2021
], Fabiano Brito et al., established a sharp lower bounds for the total area of unit vector fields on antipodally punctured unit Euclidean sphere, and these values depend on the indices of the singularities.

Theorem 4

(Brito
et al.
,
3
)
.

Let
V
be a unit vector field defined on
M=\mathbb{S}^{2}\backslash\{N,S\}
. If
k=\max\{I_{V}(N),I_{V}(S)\}
,
k\neq 0
,
k\neq 2
then

\displaystyle\area(V)\geq\pi L(\epsilon_{k})

where
L(\epsilon_{k})
denote the length of the ellipse
\dfrac{x^{2}}{k^{2}}+\dfrac{y^{2}}{(k-2)^{2}}=1
and
I_{V}(p)
stands for the Poincaré index of
V
around
p
.

Furthermore, in [
3
,
2021
], the authors exhibited a family of the unit vector fields
V_{k}
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\backslash\{N,S\}
that are area-minimizing within each index class
k
. This family has the following characteristics:

1.

V_{k}
turns
k-1
times along each parallel at a constant angle speed with respect to the oriented orthonormal frame
\left\{e_{1},e_{2}\right\}
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\backslash\{N,S\}
;

2.

V_{k}
is parallel along meridians, which means that

d\theta(e_{2})=0.

3.

V_{k}
makes an angle
\theta
with each parallel
\alpha\in\left(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\right)
, and
\theta
has constant variation along them, implying that

d\theta(e_{1})=\frac{k-1}{\cos\alpha}.

In Figure
1

V_{k}
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\backslash\{N,S\}
(in blue), where
S_{\alpha}
and

denote the parallel
\alpha
and the Equator, respectively.

Consider another oriented orthonormal local frame
\{V_{k},V_{k}^{\perp}\}
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\backslash\left\{N,S\right\}
compatible with the orientation of
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
. The area of
V_{k}
is given by

(1)

\displaystyle\area\left(V_{k}\right)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\sqrt{1+\gamma^{2}+\delta^{2}}\,\,\nu,

where
\nu
is the volume form,
\gamma=g\left(\nabla_{{}_{V_{k}}}V_{k}\,,V_{k}^{\perp}\right)
and
\delta=g\left(\nabla_{{}_{V_{k}^{\perp}}}V_{k}^{\perp}\,,V_{k}\right)
are the geodesic curvatures associated to
V_{k}
and
V_{k}^{\perp}
, respectively.

Let
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
be the oriented angle from
e_{2}
to
V_{k}
and
\alpha\in\left[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\right]
. If
V_{k}=\cos\left(\theta\right)e_{1}+\sin\left(\theta\right)e_{2}
and
V_{k}^{\perp}=-\sin\left(\theta\right)e_{1}+\cos\left(\theta\right)e_{2}
, then

(2)

\gamma=\cos\left(\theta\right)\big(\tan\alpha+d\theta(e_{1})\big)+\sin\left(\theta\right)d\theta(e_{2}),

(3)

\delta=\sin(\theta)\big(\tan\alpha+d\theta(e_{1})\big)-\cos(\theta)d\theta(e_{2}),

and

(4)

\displaystyle 1+\gamma^{2}+\delta^{2}=1+\big(\tan\alpha+d\theta(e_{1})\big)^{2}+d\theta(e_{2})^{2}.

The Lemma in [
3
] provides a straightforward computation from (
2
) to (
4
).

The equation (
4
) allows us to rewrite the area functional as an integral depending on the latitude
\alpha
and the derivatives of
\theta
:

(5)

\displaystyle\area\left(V_{k}\right)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\sqrt{1+\big(\tan\alpha+d\theta(e_{1})\big)^{2}+d\theta(e_{2})^{2}}\,\nu,

The formula for the area of the unit vector field obtained in equation (
5
) will be used to demonstrate our area minimization result on the spherical annulus of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

Let

\mathbb{S}^{2}\backslash\left\{N,S\right\}

is tangent to the parallels and
e_{2}
to the meridians. Consider
S_{\alpha}
be the parallel of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
at latitude
\alpha\in\left(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\right)
and
S_{\beta}
be the meridian of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
at longitude
\beta\in(0,2\pi]
. The
spherical annuli
in
\mathbb{S}^{2}
bounded by the parallels
S_{-\alpha_{0}}
and
S_{\alpha_{0}}
are defined as

\displaystyle A\left(-\alpha_{0},\alpha_{0}\right)=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\mid-\alpha_{0}\leq\arcsin{(z)}\leq\alpha_{0}\},

where
\alpha_{0}\in\left(0,\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)
determines the latitudinal bounds of the annular region on the sphere.
Furthermore, since every point
p=(x,y,z)
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
can be defined by the latitude
\alpha
and the longitude
\beta
, in order to simplify the notation, we rewrite the
spherical annulus

A\left(-\alpha_{0},\alpha_{0}\right)
in
\mathbb{S}^{2}
as follows

(6)

\displaystyle A_{\alpha_{0}}=\{(\alpha,\beta)\in S^{2}\mid-\alpha_{0}\leq\alpha\leq\alpha_{0}\},

where
\alpha_{0}
is a fixed constant in the interval
\left(0,\frac{\pi}{2}\right)

Definition 5
.

Let
V
be a unit vector field on the annulus region

A_{\alpha_{0}}
, with
\alpha_{0}\in\left(0,\frac{\pi}{2}\right)
that satisfies the following:

(i)

V
is tangent to the boundaries
S_{-\alpha_{0}}
and
S_{\alpha_{0}}
of the spherical annulus
A_{\alpha_{0}}
, such that

\displaystyle V(-\alpha_{0},\beta)=-V(\alpha_{0},\beta),

for all

.

(ii)

For all

(0,2\pi]

V(0,\beta)
is perpendicular to the parallel

.

(iii)

Let

with the parallels. The angle
\theta
that

(7)

\displaystyle d\theta_{p}\left(e_{1}\right)=\theta_{1}(p)\equiv 0,

for all

\left\{e_{1},e_{2}\right\}

Proof.

of Theorem

.

The area of unit vector field
V
is expressed as an integral depending on the latitude
\alpha
and the derivatives of the angle function
\theta
, as given by equation (
5
):

(8)

\displaystyle\area\left(V\right)=\int\limits_{A_{\alpha_{0}}}{\sqrt{1+\big(\tan\alpha+\theta_{1}\big)^{2}+(\theta_{2})^{2}}\,\,\,\nu},

where
\nu
denotes the area form in the annulus region
A_{\alpha_{0}}
.

According to hypothesis
(iii)
in Definition
5
, the equation (
8
) takes the form

(9)

\displaystyle\area\left(V\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{A_{\alpha_{0}}}\sqrt{\sec^{2}(\alpha)+\left(\theta_{2}\right)^{2}}\,\,\nu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\alpha_{0}}^{\alpha_{0}}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\cos(\alpha)\sqrt{\sec^{2}(\alpha)+\left(\theta_{2}\right)^{2}}\,\,d\beta d\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\alpha_{0}}^{\alpha_{0}}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\sqrt{1+\cos^{2}(\alpha)\left(\theta_{2}\right)^{2}}\,\,d\beta d\alpha.

Taking
J:=1+\cos^{2}(\alpha)\,(\theta_{2})^{2}.
By straightforward computation one obtains
J=H+I
, where

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{1-\cos^{2}(\alpha_{0})\sec^{2}(\alpha)}+\cos(\alpha_{0})\,(\theta_{2})\right)^{2},

(10)

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-\cos(\alpha_{0})\sec(\alpha)+\sqrt{1-\cos^{2}(\alpha_{0})\sec^{2}(\alpha)}\,\cos(\alpha)\,(\theta_{2})\right)^{2}.

Therefore, the following inequality holds

(11)

\displaystyle\area\left(V\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\alpha_{0}}^{\alpha_{0}}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\sqrt{1-\cos(\alpha_{0})\sec^{2}(\alpha)}\,\,d\beta d\alpha+\cos(\alpha_{0})\int\limits_{-\alpha_{0}}^{\alpha_{0}}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\theta_{2}\right)^{2}\,\,d\beta d\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{\alpha_{0}}+\cos(\alpha_{0})\,2\pi^{2},

which concludes the proof of
(i)
.

For all
\alpha\in\left[-\alpha_{0},\alpha_{0}\right]
, observe that for the equality in
(i)
to hold, it is required that
I
, as defined in (
10
), equals zero, or equivalently

\displaystyle\theta_{2}=\dfrac{\cos(\alpha_{0})\sec(\alpha)}{\sqrt{1-\cos^{2}(\alpha_{0})\sec^{2}(\alpha)}\,\cos(\alpha)}.

Since
\theta_{2}
is the derivative of the angle function in the direction of the tangent vector to the meridian, we can write

\displaystyle d\theta_{p}(e_{2})=\theta_{2}(p)=\dfrac{d\theta(p)}{d\alpha},

for all
p\in A_{\alpha_{0}}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\theta(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos(\alpha_{0})\int\dfrac{\sec(\alpha)}{\sqrt{1-\cos^{2}(\alpha_{0})\sec^{2}(\alpha)}\,\cos(\alpha)}\,\,d\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arcsin\bigg(\cot(\alpha_{0})\tan(\alpha)\bigg)+C,

where
C
is a real constant. For all
\beta\in(0,2\pi]
, let us define the condition

\theta\left(0,\beta\right):=\frac{\pi}{2}.

Thus,

\theta\left(0,\beta\right)=\arcsin(0)+C=\frac{\pi}{2}

which implies that
C=\dfrac{\pi}{2}.
Therefore,

\theta\left(\alpha,\beta\right)=\arcsin\bigg(\cot(\alpha_{0})\tan(\alpha)\bigg)+\frac{\pi}{2}.

A direct verification shows that

\theta\left(\alpha_{0},\beta\right)=\pi\quad\mbox{and}\quad\theta\left(-\alpha_{0},\beta\right)=0,

which concludes the proof of
(ii)
.

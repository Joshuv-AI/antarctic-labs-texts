A Unified Formulation for ⟨ S ^ 2 ⟩ \langle\hat{S}^{2}\rangle in Two-Component TDDFT
Xiaoyu Zhang

Abstract
Two-component linear-response time-dependent density functional theory (TDDFT) provides a unified framework that encompasses noncollinear excitations in noncollinear reference states, as well as both spin-conserving and spin-flip excitations in collinear reference states. In this work, we present a general formalism for evaluating the expectation value ⟨ S ^ 2 ⟩ \langle\hat{S}^{2}\rangle of electronically excited states obtained within two-component TDDFT. We then derive and analyze specialized forms of the resulting equations for collinear reference determinants, for which the two-component formalism decomposes into conventional spin-conserving and spin-flip TDDFT. The resulting working equations are systematically compared with previously proposed theoretical approaches. On the basis of our analysis, ⟨ S ^ 2 ⟩ \langle\hat{S}^{2}\rangle in the excited states is shown to arise from two distinct sources: (i) ⟨ S ^ 2 ⟩ 0 \langle\hat{S}^{2}\rangle_{0} in the reference state and (ii) additional Δ ​ ⟨ S ^ 2 ⟩ \Delta\langle\hat{S}^{2}\rangle introduced by the excitation process itself. Finally, we evaluate the expectation value ⟨ S ^ 2 ⟩ \langle\hat{S}^{2}\rangle by performing two-component TDDFT calculations based on two-component DFT, unrestricted Kohn–Sham (UKS), and restricted open-shell Kohn–Sham (ROKS) reference states, respectively.

1
Introduction

Linear-response TDDFT is prevalent due to its balance between efficiency and accuracy.
1
In the non-relativistic limit, two-component TDDFT is an adequate framework even in a noncollinear magnetic system
6
. On a collinear reference state, two-component TDDFT is divided into spin-conserving and spin-flip TDDFT.
Spin-flip TDDFT, formulated on an open-shell reference state, has been demonstrated to be particularly useful for the description of conical intersections, charge-transfer excited states, nonadiabatic derivative couplings, spin–orbit couplings, and bond dissociation processes.
16
In order to further enable applications in spectroscopy, it is vital to know the spin multiplicity of excited states; that is, to calculate
\langle\hat{S}^{2}\rangle
. However, calculating
\langle\hat{S}^{2}\rangle
is not trivial and hasn’t been systematically explored in two-component TDDFT.

Maurice and Head-Gordon
9
derived analytical expressions for the expectation value of the total spin-squared operator,
\langle\hat{S}^{2}\rangle
, within the framework of spin-conserving configuration interaction singles (CIS). Ipatov
et al.

5
subsequently obtained expressions for
\langle\hat{S}^{2}\rangle
in spin-conserving TDDFT. Myneni and Casida
10
addressed and resolved several ambiguities associated with the evaluation of
\langle\hat{S}^{2}\rangle
in spin-conserving TDDFT and proposed an
ab initio
formulation. Building on the conceptual framework introduced by Ipatov
et al.
, Li
et al.

7
derived the corresponding working equations for spin-flip TDDFT, which they reported in the appendix of their work.

To enrich and systematize the theoretical framework in this area, we introduce a unified formulation for the spin-squared expectation value,
\langle\hat{S}^{2}\rangle
, within two-component time-dependent density functional theory (TDDFT). We begin from the second-quantized representation of
\hat{S}^{2}
and demonstrate that its algebraic structure closely parallels that of the second-quantized electronic Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
. On this basis, we derive explicit expressions for
\langle\hat{S}^{2}\rangle
evaluated over general Slater determinants, which are found to be analogous to the corresponding energy expectation values derived from the Slater–Condon rules.
Subsequently, we formulate two-component TDDFT in the second-quantized equation-of-motion (EOM) framework, which is formally equivalent to the Casida equation in the first-quantized matrix representation. By substituting
\hat{H}
with
\hat{S}^{2}
in the EOM-like TDDFT expressions, we obtain a Casida-type eigenvalue equation in the first-quantized representation for the evaluation of spin-squared expectation values. In close analogy to the electronic excitation energies
\omega
(
\Delta\langle\hat{H}\rangle
), the changes in spin-squared expectation values,
\Delta\langle\hat{S}^{2}\rangle
, can be computed within this Casida-like formalism.
We then specialize the treatment to collinear reference states and derive further simplified working equations, which enable direct comparison with previously proposed spin-analysis schemes. Finally, we present numerical results for a range of molecular systems to illustrate and benchmark the proposed methodology.

We use short notations for double-electron integrals:

\hat{S}^{2}=\sum_{u}\hat{s}_{u}\hat{s}_{u}

\hat{S}^{2}=\sum_{u}\sum_{PQRS}(s_{u})_{PQ}(s_{u})_{RS}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{P}\hat{a}_{Q}\hat{a}_{R}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{S}

There are two basic anti-commutation relations:

\{\hat{a}_{P},\hat{a}_{Q}\}=0

(13)

\{\hat{a}_{P},\hat{a}_{Q}^{\dagger}\}=\delta_{PQ}

(14)

Using the two relations, we obtain:

\hat{S}^{2}=\sum_{u}\sum_{PQRS}(s_{u})_{PQ}(s_{u})_{RS}(\delta_{QR}\hat{a}_{P}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{S}+\hat{a}_{P}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{R}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{S}\hat{a}_{Q})

(15)

We denote:

W_{PQRS}=2\sum_{u}(s_{u})_{PR}(s_{u})_{QS}

(16)

Based on the definition, we have:

W_{PQRS}=W_{QPSR}=W^{*}_{RSPQ}=W^{*}_{SRQP}

(17)

Finally, we obtain:

\hat{S}^{2}=\frac{3}{4}\sum_{P}\hat{a}_{P}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{P}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{PQRS}W_{PQRS}\hat{a}_{P}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{Q}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{S}\hat{a}_{R}

(18)

2.3
\Delta\langle\hat{S}^{2}\rangle
of Two-Component TDDFT in Second-Quantized EOM

The formalism of the equation-of-motion (EOM) focuses on a transition operator
O_{\lambda}^{\dagger}

12
, which changes an eigenstate
|0\rangle
of the Hamiltonian to another eigenstate
|\lambda\rangle
:

O_{\lambda}^{\dagger}|0\rangle=|\lambda\rangle

(35)

Also, it satisfies the killer condition:

O_{\lambda}|0\rangle=0

(36)

O_{\lambda}^{\dagger}
is determined by one of the following three conditions:

\langle 0|\delta O_{\lambda}[\hat{H},O_{\lambda}^{\dagger}]|0\rangle=\omega_{\lambda}\langle 0|\delta O_{\lambda}O_{\lambda}^{\dagger}|0\rangle

(37)

\langle 0|[\delta O_{\lambda},[\hat{H},O_{\lambda}^{\dagger}]]|0\rangle=\omega_{\lambda}\langle 0|[\delta O_{\lambda},O_{\lambda}^{\dagger}]|0\rangle

(38)

\langle 0|[\delta O_{\lambda},\hat{H},O_{\lambda}^{\dagger}]|0\rangle=\omega_{\lambda}\langle 0|[\delta O_{\lambda},O_{\lambda}^{\dagger}]|0\rangle

(39)

Here,
\omega_{\lambda}
is the excitation energy. It is noteworthy that the three conditions are equivalent only if
|0\rangle
is exact, the killer condition is satisfied, and
O_{\lambda}
is expanded on a complete basis. The triple commutator is defined as:

[A,B,C]=\frac{1}{2}([[A,B],C]+[A,[B,C]])

(40)

RPA in this work uses the second EOM condition, which is eq.
38
.
We express the two-component RPA in second-quantized EOM:

MX=\omega NX

(41)

M_{PQRS}=\langle[O_{PQ},[\hat{H},O_{RS}^{\dagger}]]\rangle_{0}

(42)

N_{PQRS}=\langle[O_{PQ},O_{RS}^{\dagger}]\rangle_{0}

(43)

O^{\dagger}_{PQ}=\hat{a}_{P}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{Q}

(44)

Here,
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
and
\hat{a}
are the creation and annihilation operators. By expanding
\hat{O}
on a set of single-excitation operators and single-deactivation operators, we have two cases to discuss:
PQRS=AIBJ
and
PQRS=AIJB
. Based on the Slater-Condon rule, we have:

M_{AIBJ}=f_{AB}\delta_{IJ}-f_{JI}\delta_{AB}+K_{AIBJ}

(45)

M_{AIJB}=-K_{AIJB}

(46)

N_{AIBJ}=\delta_{AB}\delta_{IJ}

(47)

N_{AIJB}=0

(48)

f_{PQ}=h_{PQ}+\sum_{R\in\Phi_{0}}\bra{PR}\ket{QR}

(49)

K_{PQRS}=\bra{PS}\ket{QR}

(50)

We can easily prove two symmetries:

M_{PQRS}=M_{QPSR}^{*}

(51)

N_{PQRS}=-N_{QPSR}^{*}

(52)

We can present the Casida equation in the two-component RPA:

\begin{pmatrix}M_{AIBJ}&M_{AIJB}\\
M_{AIJB}^{*}&M_{AIBJ}^{*}\end{pmatrix}X=\omega\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}X

We rewrite the fock and kernel as:

f_{PQ}=\frac{\partial E}{\partial D_{PQ}}

K_{PQRS}=\frac{\partial^{2}E}{\partial D_{PQ}\partial D_{SR}}

By translating

K_{PQRS}=\langle PS|QR\rangle-c_{\mathrm{HF}}\langle PS|RQ\rangle+\frac{\partial^{2}E_{\mathrm{xc}}}{\partial D_{PQ}\partial D_{SR}}

\hat{S}^{2}

2.4
\langle\hat{S}^{2}\rangle
on a Collinear Reference

A collinear reference is usually acquired by UKS or ROKS. By rewriting spin-up and spin-down molecular orbitals from UKS or ROKS in the form of two-component molecular orbitals, we still preserve the orthonormality of the molecular orbitals. Thus, the Slater-Condon rule still works, and we can largely use the aforementioned equations.

Starting from eq.
27
, we have the expectation value of the total electron spin in a collinear reference state:

\begin{split}\langle\hat{S}^{2}\rangle_{0}&=\frac{3}{4}N+\frac{1}{4}[\mathrm{Tr}(S(D^{\alpha\alpha}-D^{\beta\beta}))]^{2}\\
&-\frac{1}{4}[\mathrm{Tr}(D^{\alpha\alpha}SD^{\alpha\alpha}S)+\mathrm{Tr}(D^{\beta\beta}SD^{\beta\beta}S)]-\mathrm{Tr}(D^{\alpha\alpha}SD^{\beta\beta}S)\end{split}

(73)

If there are no fractional occupations, we have:

\langle\hat{S}^{2}\rangle_{0}=\frac{1}{2}N+\frac{1}{4}[\mathrm{Tr}(S(D^{\alpha\alpha}-D^{\beta\beta}))]^{2}-\mathrm{Tr}(D^{\alpha\alpha}SD^{\beta\beta}S)

(74)

This equation reproduces the result in eq. (143) of ref.
8
.

Starting from eq.
68

2.5
Noncollinear Exchange and Correlation Kernels

We use noncollinear exchange-correlation kernels, the second derivative of exchange-correlation energy functionals, generalized from collinear exchange-correlation functionals by the multicollinear approach.
15
;
11
We briefly revisit the idea of the multi-collinear approach here.

The multicollinear approach assumes that noncollinear functionals can be written in the following form:

E_{\mathrm{xc}}[n,\mathbf{m}]=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left.E_{xc}^{eff}[n,s]\right|_{s=\bm{m}\cdot\bm{e}}\sin\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\phi

(98)

where

\bm{e}(\theta,\phi)=(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta)

(99)

It was added that it should have the correct collinear limit:

E_{\mathrm{xc}}[n,(0,0,m_{z})]=E^{col}_{\mathrm{xc}}[n,m_{z}]

(100)

we can then have:

\int_{0}^{1}E^{eff}_{\mathrm{xc}}[n,st]dt=E_{\mathrm{xc}}^{col}[n,s]

(101)

For eq.
101
, one solution is:

E^{eff}_{\mathrm{xc}}[n,s,T]=E^{col}_{\mathrm{xc}}[n,s,T]+\int\frac{\delta E^{col}_{\mathrm{xc}}[n,s,T]}{\delta s(\mathbf{r})}s(\mathbf{r})d\mathbf{r}

(102)

A principal advantage of employing noncollinear exchange–correlation kernels is their ability to preserve the degeneracy of electronic states that share the same total spin quantum number, for example, the threefold degeneracy characteristic of triplet states.
14

4
Conclusion

In this work, we propose a unified formulation for
\langle\hat{S}^{2}\rangle
in two-component TDDFT. Results are presented in the non-relativistic limit, but the equations are transferable to two-component special relativistic methods, such as X2C. Similar ideas can also be proposed to calculate
\langle\hat{S}^{2}\rangle
or even
\langle\hat{J}^{2}\rangle
in four-component TDDFT. Following the idea of conducting substitutions in second quantization, expectation values of many observables can be calculated within a two-component or even four-component framework.

On the Calculus of the spectrum of the Laplacian for functions in Lens Spaces
Gedeana Pantoja da Silva, Alexandre Casassola Gonçalves, Ferreira da Silva Rafael

Abstract
We improve a specific method to obtain the dimension of the eigenspaces of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on lens spaces and establish some applications related to the explicit description of the dimension of the smallest positive eigenvalue and the parity of the dimensions of the eigenspaces.

Introduction

The study of the spectrum of the Laplacian on closed Riemannian manifolds has proven to be extremely useful both in mathematics and in physics, with applications in areas like the study of wave equations, explicit computation of traces of operators, calculus of the regularized determinant and of the analytic torsion.
One of the main features of this operator that makes it useful in applications is the fact that its spectrum is discrete and has no accumulation points, which makes it possible to define and study several related concepts, especially the ones connected to spectral zeta functions.

2
Preliminaries and Notation

Let
p,q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{m}
be natural numbers such that for every
i
,
q_{i}
is co-prime to
p
, and let
L(p;q_{1},\dots,q_{m})
denote the corresponding lens space.
We will use
M
to denote the set
M=\{1,2,\dots,m\}
,
U
to denote an arbitrary subset of
M
and we will denote by
u:=|U|
the cardinality of
U
. The
congruence lattice
associated to
U
is the set

\mathcal{L}(U)=\{(x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\dots,x_{i_{u}})\in\mathbb{Z}^{u}:x_{i_{1}}q_{i_{1}}+x_{i_{2}}q_{i_{2}}+\cdots+x_{i_{u}}q_{i_{u}}\equiv 0(\text{ mod }p)\}

(3)

and for any
x=(x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{u}})\in\mathcal{L}(U)
, the 1-norm of
x
is given by
\|x\|_{1}=|x_{i_{1}}|+\cdots+|x_{i_{u}}|
.

We may use the
1
-norm to describe special subsets of the congruence lattice.
Given
U\subset M
, and
k\geq 0
, we set:

\displaystyle\Omega(U,k)

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathcal{L}(U);\ \|x\|_{1}=k\},

\displaystyle C(U,k)

\displaystyle=\{x\in\Omega(U,k);\;\forall\ i\in U,\,|x_{i}|<p\ \}.

(4)

We are interested in certain coefficients related to the congruence lattices.
Those are defined as follows:

Definition 2.1
.

For
k\geq 0
and
U\subset M
we define:

N_{\mathcal{L}}(U,k)=|\Omega(U,k)|.

(5)

When
U=M
we write, for short,
N_{\mathcal{L}}(k):=N_{\mathcal{L}}(M,k)
.
We also set

\gamma(U,k)=|C(U,k)|.

(6)

Notice that our definition of
N_{\mathcal{L}}(k)
coincides with the definition present in
[
8
, expression
(11)
]
, except for the fact that in
[
8
, expression
(11)
]
the author allowed
\mathcal{L}
to be any sublattice of
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, while we are specializing in lattices as described in expression (
3
). On the other hand, our definition of
N_{\mathcal{L}}(U,k)
diverges from the definition of
N_{\mathcal{L}}(k,l)
present in
[
8
, expression
(12)
]
and the difference between
N_{\mathcal{L}}(U,k)
from our text and
N_{\mathcal{L}}(k,l)
from
[
8
, expression
(12)
]
is the foundation of the differences between our study and the one developed in
[
8
]
.

Our main objective is to compute

N_{\mathcal{L}}(k)=N_{\mathcal{L}}(M,k)
, and the sets defined below will assist us in this objective.
For integers
n\geq 0
,
k\in\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
and
N\subset M
, we define:

\displaystyle\Omega_{N}(k+np)

\displaystyle=\Omega_{N}(M,k+np)

(7)

\displaystyle:=\{x\in\Omega(M,k+np);\;(x_{i}<0\text{ and }x_{i}\equiv 0\text{ mod }p)\iff i\in N\}.

Thus,
\Omega_{N}(k+np)
gathers the solutions of the linear congruence that have norm 1 equal to
k+np

and have the coefficient
x_{i}
as a negative multiple of
p
if and only if
i
belongs to
N
.

The following claims involve the sets
C(U,k+tp)
,
\Omega_{N}(k+np)
and
\Omega(M,k+np)
, they compose the basis for the main results of the text.

Claim 1
.

If
U\subset M
, and
t\neq t^{\prime}
, then
C(U,k+tp)\cap C(U,k+t^{\prime}p)=\emptyset
.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of the definition of
\Omega
.
∎

Claim 2
.

If
N,N^{\prime}\subset M
with
N\neq N^{\prime}
, then
\Omega_{N}\cap\Omega_{N^{\prime}}=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that
N\neq N^{\prime}
and
(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\Omega_{N}\cap\Omega_{N^{\prime}}
.
Without loss of generality, suppose
j\in N
with
j\notin N^{\prime}
. By the definition of
\Omega_{N}
, the coordinate
x_{j}

is a negative multiple of
p
.

On the other hand, since
j\notin N^{\prime}
, the coordinate
x_{j}
is not a negative multiple of
p
, which is a contradiction.
Therefore, there is no
(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\Omega_{N}\cap\Omega_{N^{\prime}}
, that is,
\Omega_{N}\cap\Omega_{N^{\prime}}=\emptyset
.
∎

Claim 3
.

Every element
(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\Omega(M,k+np)
belongs to
\Omega_{N}(k+np)
for some
N\subset M
.

Proof.

Indeed, given
(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\Omega(M,k+np)
, it suffices to consider
N=\{i\in M;x_{i}<0\mbox{ and }x_{i}\equiv 0(\text{ mod }p)\}
. Thus
(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\Omega_{N}(k+np)
.
∎

The previous statements lead us to the following proposition:

Proposition 2.2
.

For every
k\in\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
we have:

\Omega(M,k+np)=\bigsqcup_{N\subseteq M}\Omega_{N}(k+np).

(8)

Proof.

By definition,
\Omega_{N}(k+np)\subseteq\Omega(M,k+np)
. From Claim
3
,
we have
\Omega(M,k+np)\subseteq\cup_{N\subset M}\Omega_{N}(k+np)
and by Claim
2
, this union must be disjoint.
∎

3
Main Results

The strategy for computing
N_{\mathcal{L}}(k+np)
is to establish a function
f
that maps
\Omega(M,k+np)
,
which is the disjoint union of the subsets
\Omega_{N}(k+np)
, for
N\subseteq M
, onto a subset of the
union of the sets
C(U,k+tp)
, for
U\subset M
and
t\geq 0
.

To be more specific, we define for each
N\subseteq M
a function
f_{N}:\Omega_{N}(M,k+np)\to\bigsqcup_{t\geq 0}C(\overline{N},{k+tp})
,
given by
f_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=(\overline{x}_{1},\dots,\hat{x}_{i_{1}},\dots,\hat{x}_{i_{|N|}},\dots,\overline{x}_{m})
. The indices
of the suppressed coefficients are precisely
\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{|N|}\}=N
. We use the notation
\overline{N}
to denote
the complement
\overline{N}=M-N
.

In this expression we have the following convention for
\overline{x_{i}}
:

\overline{x_{i}}=\begin{cases}x_{i}\mod p,&\mbox{if }x_{i}\geq 0\\
(x_{i}\mod p)-p,&\mbox{if }x_{i}<0,\end{cases}

(9)

where
x_{i}\ \text{mod}\ p
is the representative of the congruence class modulo
p
of
x_{i}
in
\{0,1,2,\dots,p-1\}
.

Given the functions
f_{N}
, we define

f:\Omega(M,k+np)=\bigsqcup_{N\subseteq M}\Omega_{N}(M,k+np)\longrightarrow\bigsqcup_{U\subseteq M,t\geq 0}C(U,{k+tp}),

(10)

by

f(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=f_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\quad\text{if}\ (x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\Omega_{N}(k+np).

(11)

Lemma 3.1
.

The function
f
is well defined. For each
N\subset M
,

f|_{\Omega_{N}(k+np)}
is surjective onto
\bigsqcup_{t=0}^{n-|N|}C(\overline{N},{k+tp})
. Given
y\in C(\overline{N},{k+tp})
, the cardinality of the preimage of
y
by
f
is

|f^{-1}(\{y\})|=C^{n-t+|\overline{N}|-1}_{m-1}.

(12)

Proof.

To show that
f
is well defined it suffices to prove that for
N\subset M
the functions
f_{N}
are well defined.
Fixing
N
, and taking
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\Omega_{N}(M,k+np)
, let
y=f_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})
, each
\overline{x}_{i}

given by convention (
9
).
Then
y
has
|\overline{N}|
coefficients, since only the coefficients of the indices
i\in N
were removed from

x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})
.
Furthermore,
|y_{j}|=|\overline{x}_{j}|<p
for
j\in\overline{N}
, what comes straight from convention (
9
).
It only remains to show that
y
in fact satisfies the linear congruence condition, and that its 1-norm is
k+tp
for
some
t\geq 0
.

So we write
\overline{N}=\{j_{1},\dots,j_{|\overline{N}|}\}
and
y=(y_{j_{1}},\dots,y_{j_{|\overline{N}|}})
, we must check that

q_{j_{1}}y_{j_{1}}+\dots+q_{j_{|\overline{N}|}}y_{j_{|\overline{N}|}}\equiv 0\ (\mbox{mod }p).

(13)

Since
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\Omega_{N}(M,k+np)
, we have:

q_{1}{x}_{1}+\dots+q_{m}{x}_{m}\equiv 0\ (\mbox{mod }p),

(14)

that is,
\sum_{i\in M}q_{i}x_{i}
is a multiple of
p
. On the other hand,

\sum_{i\in M}q_{i}x_{i}=\sum_{i\in N}q_{i}x_{i}+\sum_{j\in\overline{N}}q_{j}x_{j},

(15)

and since the first sum of the second member of the above identity is made up of multiples of
p
,
the second sum of the second member must also be a multiple of
p
. Thus

\sum_{j\in\overline{N}}q_{j}x_{j}\equiv 0\ (\mbox{mod }p).

(16)

For each
j\in\overline{N}
,
x_{j}\equiv\overline{x}_{j}(\,\text{mod }p)
, so

\sum_{j\in\overline{N}}q_{j}y_{j}=\sum_{j\in\overline{N}}q_{j}\overline{x}_{j}\equiv\sum_{j\in\overline{N}}q_{j}x_{j}\equiv 0(\,\text{mod }p).

(17)

Thus, we have that
y
satisfies (
13
), hence belongs to
C(\overline{N},h)
for some
h=\|y\|_{1}\geq 0
.
Finally, let us show that
h=k+tp
with
t\leq n-|N|
.

For each
j\in\overline{N}
there exists
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
y_{j}=x_{j}-\alpha_{j}p
. convention (
9
)
gives us
|x_{j}-\alpha_{j}p|=|x_{j}|-|\alpha_{j}|p
. Thus,

\displaystyle\|y\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\overline{N}}|y_{j}|=\sum_{j\in\overline{N}}|x_{j}-\alpha_{j}p|=\sum_{j\in\overline{N}}|x_{j}|-p\sum_{j\in\overline{N}}|\alpha_{j}|=

(18)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\overline{N}}|x_{j}|+\sum_{i\in N}|x_{i}|-p\sum_{j\in\overline{N}}|\alpha_{j}|-\sum_{i\in N}|x_{i}|=

\displaystyle=\|x\|_{1}-p\left(\sum_{j\in\overline{N}}|\alpha_{j}|+\sum_{i\in N}\frac{|x_{i}|}{p}\right).

Recall that
N
is the set of indices
i
such that
x_{i}
is a negative multiple of
p
, then
|x_{i}|/p
is an
integer greater than or equal to 1. The quantity in parentheses in the last member of (
18
) is
an integer
s
that satisfies
s\geq|N|
. That is,

\|y\|_{1}=k+np-sp=k+(n-s)p=k+tp,

(19)

with
t\leq n-|N|
. Therefore, we can state that
f_{N}
is well defined, and so is
f
.

Now suppose that for some
N\subset M
and
0\leq t\leq n-|N|
, it holds
C(\overline{N},k+tp)\neq\emptyset
.
Let
y=(y_{j_{1}},y_{j_{2}},\dots,y_{j_{\overline{N},}})\in C(\overline{N},k+tp)
. We will find
x\in\Omega(M,k+np)
such
that
f(x)=y
. Without loss of generality, we assume
N=\{1,2,\dots,|N|\}
, and therefore

\overline{N}=\{|N|+1,|N|+2,\dots,m\}
. For each
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
we set
\tilde{x}_{i}=-p
if
i\in N

and
\tilde{x}_{i}=y_{i}
if
i\in\overline{N}
, resulting in:

\tilde{x}=(-p,-p,\dots,-p,y_{|N|+1},y_{|N|+2},\dots,y_{m}).

Of course,
\tilde{x}
satisfies the linear congruence, that is,
\tilde{x}\in\Omega(M,\|\tilde{x}\|_{1})
. At this stage
we have
\|\tilde{x}\|_{1}=\|y\|_{1}+|N|p=k+tp+|N|p
.

To arrive at a point
x
with
\|x\|_{1}=k+np
we must add the difference of the norms
\|x\|_{1}-\|\tilde{x}\|_{1}=(n-t-|N|)p

to the coordinates of
\tilde{x}
so that each coordinate preserves its sign
and is increased by multiples of
p
. Note that this is only possible if
n-t-|N|\geq 0
, and this is where we use the

t\leq n-|N|
assumption. In particular, we can add these multiples to the first coordinate, and put

x=(-(n-t-|N|+1)p,-p,\dots,-p,y_{|N|+1},y_{|N|+2},\dots,y_{m}).

(20)

The last statement of the theorem is proven by means of a standard combinatorial argument.
Given a non-negative integer
a
, the number of ways to write
a
as the sum of
m
ordered non-negative integers
is
C_{m-1}^{a+m-1}=\frac{(a+m-1)!}{(m-1)!a!}
.

To construct
x
, after having defined
\tilde{x}
, we must distribute
(n-t-|N|)
“packets” of
p
units
in
m
coordinates, in an arbitrary way. This problem is then the number of ways of summing
m
non-negative
integers adding up to
n-t-|N|
, that is,
C_{m-1}^{n-t-|N|+m-1}
. Since
|\overline{N}|=m-|N|
we arrive at

|f^{-1}(\{y\})|=C^{n-t-|N|+m-1}_{m-1}=C^{n-t+|\overline{N}|-1}_{m-1}.

(21)

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

For
k\in\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

N_{\mathcal{L}}(k+np)=\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\sum_{U\subseteq M}C_{m-1}^{n-t+|U|-1}\gamma(U,k+tp),

(22)

Proof.

Let
N\subset M
and
0\leq t\leq n-|N|
be fixed. In view of Theorem
3.1
we have that, for each
y\in C(\overline{N},k+tp)
,

y
has
C_{m-1}^{n-t+|\overline{N}|-1}
preimages in
\Omega_{N}(M,k+np)
. Therefore,

|f^{-1}(C(\overline{N},k+tp))|=C_{m-1}^{n-t+|\overline{N}|-1}.|C(\overline{N},k+tp))|=C_{m-1}^{n-t+|\overline{N}|-1}.\gamma(\overline{N},k+tp),

(23)

We recall that by a property of the function
\Gamma
(factorial), the combinatorics holds
C^{a}_{b}=0
whenever

b>a\geq 0
. Therefore, there is no problem in considering for counting purposes,
t>n-|N|
, in which case
f^{-1}(\{y\})

will be empty. And for
t\geq m
it is immediate that
\gamma(\overline{N},k+tp)=0
, since in this case
C(\overline{N},k+tp)
is empty.
Therefore, the image of
\Omega_{N}(M,k+np)
by
f
is contained in
\sqcup_{0\leq t\leq m-1}C(\overline{N},k+tp)
, and therefore

|\Omega_{N}(M,k+np)|=\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}C_{m-1}^{n-t+|\overline{N}|-1}.\gamma(\overline{N},k+tp).

(24)

And since
\Omega(M,k+np)=\bigsqcup_{N\subseteq M}\Omega_{N}(M,k+np)
(Proposition
2.2
) it is immediate that

\displaystyle N_{\mathcal{L}}(k+np)

\displaystyle=|\Omega(M,k+np)|=\sum_{N\subseteq M}|\Omega_{N}(M,k+np)|=

(25)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\sum_{U\subseteq M}C_{m-1}^{n-t+|U|-1}\gamma(U,k+tp).

∎

4
Applications

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
be an even integer. Then for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
odd,
\text{dim}(\lambda_{i})
is even.

Proof.

If
s
is a positive integer then, for every
x\in C(U,s)
we have
-x\neq x
and
-x\in C(U,s)
. Thus
\gamma(U,s)

is even.

Now, for
s\geq 1
an odd integer, since
p
is even, we can write
s=k+np
with
k
an odd number in
\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}

and
n\geq 0
. In particular, the term
k+tp
occurring in the second hand side of (
22
) is odd, for all
t\geq 0
.
Hence
k+tp
will never be zero, and
N_{\mathcal{L}}(s)
will be even.

Finally, back to identity (
1
), the number
i-2s
is odd, and so
N_{\mathcal{L}}(i-2s)

is even. Therefore
\text{dim}(\lambda_{i})
is even.
∎

Abstract
. We consider two Hamiltonians that are close to each other, H 1 ≈ H 2 H_{1}\approx H_{2} , and analyze the time-decay of the corresponding Loschmidt echo 𝔐 ⁡ ( t ) := | ⟨ ψ 0 , e i ​ t ​ H 2 ​ e − i ​ t ​ H 1 ​ ψ 0 ⟩ | 2 \mathfrak{M}(t):=|\langle\psi_{0},\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH_{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH_{1}}\psi_{0}\rangle|^{2} that expresses the effect of an imperfect time reversal on the initial state ψ 0 \psi_{0} . Our model Hamiltonians are deformed Wigner matrices that do not share a common eigenbasis. The main tools for our results are two-resolvent laws for such H 1 H_{1} and H 2 H_{2} .

5.1.

Throughout the proof, we will use the shorthand notations
E_{j}:=\Re z_{j}
,
\eta_{j}:=|\Im z_{j}|
,
\rho_{j}:=\frac{1}{\pi}\left|\langle\Im M_{j}(z_{j})\rangle\right|
and
\omega_{j}:=z_{j}+\langle M_{j}(z_{j})\rangle
, for
j\in[2]
.

We will conclude Proposition
4.2
from the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
4.2
and using the notations from above, we have that:

(5.1)

\displaystyle|1-\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle|^{-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\eta_{1}/\rho_{1}+\eta_{1}/\rho_{2})^{-1}\vee 1.

(5.2)

\displaystyle|1-\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle|^{-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\Delta^{2}+|\omega_{1}-\overline{\omega}_{2}|^{2})^{-1}.

(5.3)

\displaystyle|1-\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle|^{-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{-2}

Combining (
5.1
)–(
5.3
) with the simple observation
|\omega_{1}-\overline{\omega}_{2}|\geq|\langle\Im M_{1}\rangle+\langle\Im M_{2}\rangle|
, we conclude (
4.9
), i.e. the proof of Proposition
4.2
. ∎

Proof of Lemma
5.1
.

For (
5.1
), it is sufficient to check that for some
c\in(0,1)
we have
|\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle|\leq\left(1-c(\eta_{1}/\rho_{1}+\eta_{2}/\rho_{2})\right)\vee(1-c)
. This follows from a simple Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
|\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle|\leq\langle|M_{1}|^{2}\rangle^{1/2}\langle|M_{2}|^{2}\rangle^{1/2}
together with the estimate

\langle|M_{j}|^{2}\rangle^{1/2}=\left(\frac{\langle\Im M_{j}\rangle}{\Im z_{j}+\langle\Im M_{j}\rangle}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\left(\frac{\rho_{j}}{\eta_{j}+\rho_{j}}\right)^{1/2}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\eta_{j}}{\rho_{j}}\right)\vee(1-c)\,,\quad j\in[2]

where the first step follows by taking the imaginary part of the MDE (
4.6
).

For (
5.2
), we note that it is sufficient to show

(5.4)

\Re\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle\leq 1-c\left(\left\langle(D_{1}-D_{2})^{2}\right\rangle+|\omega_{1}-\bar{\omega}_{2}|^{2}\right)\quad\text{for some}\quad c>0\,.

The idea for proving (
5.4
) is to translate it to a question for the spectral measures of
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
.

In order to do so, for
j\in[2]
, denote the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of
D_{j}
by
\{\lambda_{k}^{(j)}\}_{k=1}^{N}
and
\{\bm{u}_{k}^{(j)}\}_{k=1}^{N}
, respectively, and the normalized spectral measure by
\mu_{j}:=N^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{\lambda_{k}^{(j)}}
. By the MDE (
4.6
), we immediately see that
\omega_{j}
solves the equation
\omega_{j}-z_{j}=m_{\mu_{j}}(\omega_{j})
, where
m_{\mu}(z):=\int_{\mathbf{R}}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)(x-z)^{-1}
is the Stieltjes transform of the probability measure
\mu
. By taking the imaginary part and estimating
|\Im\omega_{j}|>|\Im\omega_{j}-\Im z_{j}|
we hence find

(5.5)

\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu_{j}(x)}{|x-\omega_{j}|^{2}}<1\,.

Using the above notations, we further see that
M_{j}
can be written as
M_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\lambda_{k}^{(j)}-\omega_{j})^{-1}|\bm{u}_{k}^{(j)}\rangle\langle\bm{u}_{k}^{(j)}|
and thus

\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{a,b=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\lambda_{a}^{(1)}-\omega_{1}}\cdot\frac{1}{\lambda_{b}^{(2)}-\omega_{2}}f\big(\lambda_{a}^{(1)},\lambda_{b}^{(2)}\big)\,,\quad\text{with}\quad f\big(\lambda_{a}^{(1)},\lambda_{b}^{(2)}\big):=N\big|\big\langle\bm{u}_{a}^{(1)},\bm{u}_{b}^{(2)}\big\rangle\big|^{2}\,.

Extending
f(x,y)
to
\mathbf{R}^{2}
by zero, we immediately see the following properties of
f
:

(1)

f(x,y)\geq 0
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
.

(2)

\int f(x,y)\mathrm{d}\mu_{2}(y)=\mathbf{1}_{{\rm supp}\,\mu_{1}}(x)
and
\int f(x,y)\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(x)=\mathbf{1}_{{\rm supp}\,\mu_{2}}(y)
.

(3)

On
\mathbf{R}^{2}
,
\mathrm{d}\nu(x,y):=f(x,y)\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{2}(y)
is a probability measure with marginals
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
.

In this way, the desired inequality (
5.4
) can equivalently be rewritten as

(5.6)

\Re\iint\frac{1}{x-\omega_{1}}\cdot\frac{1}{y-\omega_{2}}\mathrm{d}\nu(x,y)\leq 1-c\left(\iint(x-y)^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(x,y)+|\omega_{1}-\bar{\omega}_{2}|^{2}\right)\,.

In this form, using (
5.5
), we begin by estimating the lhs. of (
5.6
) as

\Re\iint\frac{1}{x-\omega_{1}}\cdot\frac{1}{y-\omega_{2}}\mathrm{d}\nu(x,y)<1-\frac{1}{2}\iint\left|\frac{1}{x-\omega_{1}}-\frac{1}{y-\bar{\omega}_{2}}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(x,y),

Thus, in order to arrive at (
5.6
), it suffices to bound

\begin{split}&\iint\left|\frac{1}{x-\omega_{1}}-\frac{1}{y-\bar{\omega}_{2}}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(x,y)\gtrsim\iint\left|(x-y)-(\omega_{1}-\bar{\omega}_{2})\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(x,y)\\
&\quad=\iint(x-y)^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(x,y)-2\Re(\omega_{1}-\bar{\omega}_{2})\iint(x-y)\mathrm{d}\nu(x,y)+|\omega_{1}-\bar{\omega}_{2}|^{2}\\
&\quad=\iint(x-y)^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(x,y)+|\omega_{1}-\bar{\omega}_{2}|^{2}.\end{split}

where in the first step we employed
|x-\omega_{1}|\lesssim\lVert D_{1}\rVert+|\omega_{1}|\lesssim 1
(and analogously for
|y-\bar{\omega}_{2}|
), while in the last step we used that fact that
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are traceless. This finishes the proof of (
5.2
).

Finally, for (
5.3
), we use (
5.2
) and (
4.11
) to get that

(5.7)

\begin{split}|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}&=\left|\langle M_{2}(D_{1}-D_{2})M_{1}\rangle+(1-\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle)(z_{1}-z_{2}+\langle M_{1}\rangle-\langle M_{2}\rangle)\right|^{2}\\
&\lesssim|\langle M_{1}(D_{1}-D_{2})M_{2}\rangle|^{2}+|1-\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle|\\
&\lesssim\left\langle(D_{1}-D_{2})^{2}\right\rangle+|1-\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle|\lesssim|1-\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle|\,.\qed\end{split}

5.2.
Properties of the shift: Proof of Lemmas
4.4
–
4.6

We finally prove the properties of the shift from Lemmas
4.4
–
4.6
.

Proof of Lemma
4.4
.

The proof is split in two parts in the statement of the lemma.

Part (1):

Given
|\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle|\sim 1
, note that the bound (
4.13
) immediately follows since, if, say,
z_{1}
is such that
\rho_{1}(z_{1})\geq\kappa/2
, then
\lVert M_{1}\rVert\lesssim 1
and
\langle|M_{2}|^{2}\rangle^{1/2}\leq 1
. Both of these estimates easily follow by taking the imaginary part of the respective MDEs (
4.6
).

It is hence left to prove
|\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle|\sim 1
. The upper bound
|\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle|\leq 1
is a consequence of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and
\langle|M_{j}|^{2}\rangle^{1/2}\leq 1
. In order to prove the lower bound, we may assume w.l.o.g. that
|\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle|\leq 1/2
, in which case
|1-\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle|\sim 1
. Now, the numerator in the rhs. of the
M
-resolvent identity (
4.11
) is of order one, since
1\gtrsim|\langle M_{1}\rangle-\langle M_{2}\rangle|\gtrsim|\langle\Im M_{1}\rangle|+|\langle\Im M_{2}\rangle|\gtrsim 1
.
Thus, by (
4.11
) again, we find that
\left|(z_{1}-z_{2})\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle-\langle M_{1}(D_{1}-D_{2})M_{2}\rangle\right|\sim 1
,
so, in particular,

1\lesssim\left|(z_{1}-z_{2})\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle-\langle M_{1}(D_{1}-D_{2})M_{2}\rangle\right|\lesssim|\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle|+\Delta.

Therefore, for some constant
c>0
which depends only on
L
and
\kappa
we have

|\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle|\geq c-\Delta\gtrsim 1,

i.e. we get the desired lower bound for
|\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle|
.

Part (2):
Assume w.l.o.g. that
\rho_{1}(z_{1})\geq\kappa/2
. The derivative
\partial_{z_{1}}\mathfrak{s}(z_{1},z_{2})
can be computed explicitly as

\partial_{z_{1}}\mathfrak{s}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{\langle M_{1}^{2}(D_{1}-D_{2})M_{2}\rangle\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle-\langle M_{1}(D_{1}-D_{2})M_{2}\rangle\langle M_{1}^{2}M_{2}\rangle}{\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle^{2}(1-\langle M_{1}^{2}\rangle)}

and we note that, by analogous reasoning as in part (1), the numerator is bounded from above by
\Delta
. Since
|\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle|\sim 1
, from part (1), it holds that

|\partial_{z_{1}}\mathfrak{s}(z_{1},z_{2})|\lesssim\frac{\Delta}{|1-\langle M_{1}^{2}\rangle|}\lesssim\Delta,

where in the last step we used the bound
|1-\langle M_{1}^{2}\rangle|\gtrsim\rho_{1}(z_{1})^{2}
with the aid of Proposition
4.2
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4.5
.

The argument is split in two parts: First, we prove existence and uniqueness of the energy renormalization function
f
. Second, we estimate the partial derivatives (
4.16
) of
f
and the renormalized (one point) shift
\mathfrak{s}_{0}
.

Part (1): Existence and uniqueness of
f
.
First, from Lemma
4.5
, we have that, for
z_{1}
with
|z_{1}|\leq L
and
\Im z_{1}<0
, it holds that
|\mathfrak{s}(z_{1},z_{2})|\leq C\Delta
for some
C>0
. For fixed
z_{2}=E_{2}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2}
, we introduce the auxiliary (differentiable) function

h(E_{1}):=E_{1}-E_{2}-\Re\mathfrak{s}(E_{1}-\mathrm{i}\eta_{1},E_{2}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})\,,

which has the property that
h(E_{1})<0
for
E_{1}<E_{2}-C\Delta
, and
h(E_{2})>0
for
E_{1}>E_{2}+C\Delta
. Hence,
h(E_{1})=0
has a solution in
\mathcal{I}:=[E_{2}-C\Delta,E_{2}+C\Delta]
. To see uniqueness, we differentiate
h
and find that
h^{\prime}(E_{1})\geq 1-c\Delta
for
E_{1}\in\mathcal{I}
and some
c>0
by means of (
4.14
) from Lemma
4.4
. Thus
h
has a unique zero on
\mathcal{I}
(and hence in
(-L,L)
) which we denote by
f(E_{2})=f^{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(E_{2})
– the desired energy renormalization function. Differentiability of
f
easily follows from the implicit function theorem.

Part (2): Bounds on derivatives.
Differentiating the identity
h(f^{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(E_{2}))=0
in
E_{2}
, we find that

\partial_{E_{2}}f^{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(E_{2})=\frac{1+\Re\partial_{2}\mathfrak{s}(f(E_{2})-\mathrm{i}\eta_{1},E_{2}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})}{1-\Re\partial_{1}\mathfrak{s}(f(E_{2})-\mathrm{i}\eta_{1},E_{2}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2}))}=1+\mathcal{O}(\Delta),

by means of (
4.14
) from Lemma
4.4
. Here,
\partial_{j}\mathfrak{s}
denotes the partial derivative of
\mathfrak{s}
w.r.t. its
j^{\rm th}
argument.
Similarly,

\partial_{\eta_{1}}f^{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(E_{2})=-\frac{\Re\left[\mathrm{i}\partial_{1}\mathfrak{s}\right]}{1-\Re\left[\partial_{1}\mathfrak{s}\right]},\quad\partial_{\eta_{2}}f^{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(E_{2})=\frac{\Re\left[\mathrm{i}\partial_{1}\mathfrak{s}\right]}{1-\Re\left[\partial_{2}\mathfrak{s}\right]}\,,

where
\mathfrak{s}
has arguments
f(E_{2})-\mathrm{i}\eta_{1}
and
E_{2}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2}
. This concludes the bound
\left|\partial_{\eta_{j}}f^{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(E_{2})\right|\lesssim\Delta
for
j=1,2
. The bound on
|\partial_{E_{2}}\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E_{2})|
is obtained in a similar fashion and thus left to the reader. ∎

Proof of Lemma
4.6
.

The proof is divided in two parts: In the first part, we prove (
4.17
) for
\eta_{1}=\eta_{2}=+0
. In the second part of the argument, we treat the the general case as a perturbation thereof.

Part (1): Proof on the real line.
Applying the
M
-resolvent identity (
4.11
) for
z_{1}:=f(E)-\mathrm{i}0
and
z_{2}:=E+\mathrm{i}0
and using Proposition
4.2
, we find that

\left|\frac{\langle M_{1}(z_{1})\rangle-\langle M_{2}(z_{2})\rangle}{f(E)-E-\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E)}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}}\,.

Since the numerator on the lhs. is of order one and the real part of the denominator vanishes by definition of
f(E)
, we deduce that

\Delta^{2}\lesssim\left|\Im\left[f(E)-E-\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E)\right]\right|=|\Im\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E)|\,,

i.e. we have a lower bound on the
modulus
of
\Im\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E)
. To turn this into a lower bound on
\Im\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E)
itself, we need to show that it is positive.

This will be done via a proof by contraction: Suppose that
\Im\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E)<0
. By (
4.11
) for
z_{1}:=f(E)-\mathrm{i}0
,
z_{2}:=E+\mathrm{i}0
we get

(5.8)

\frac{\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle}{1-\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle}=\frac{\langle M_{1}\rangle-\langle M_{2}\rangle}{-\mathrm{i}\Im\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E)}\,.

Since
\Im\left[\langle M_{1}\rangle-\langle M_{2}\rangle\right]=-c
for some
c>0
and
|\Re\left[\langle M_{1}\rangle-\langle M_{2}\rangle\right]|\lesssim\Delta
, we obtain, using our assumption
\Im\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E)<0
,

\langle M_{1}\rangle-\langle M_{2}\rangle=|\langle M_{1}\rangle-\langle M_{2}\rangle|\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{2}+\mathrm{i}\mathcal{O}(\Delta)}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\langle M_{1}\rangle-\langle M_{2}\rangle}{-\mathrm{i}\Im\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E)}=\left|\frac{\langle M_{1}\rangle-\langle M_{2}\rangle}{-\mathrm{i}\Im\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E)}\right|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\pi+\mathrm{i}\mathcal{O}(\Delta)}\,,

where here and in the following
\mathcal{O}(\Delta)
is real-valued. In a similar way, we find that
\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle=1+\mathcal{O}(\Delta)+\mathrm{i}\mathcal{O}(\Delta)
and
\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle=|\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mathcal{O}(\Delta)}
. Hence, (
5.8
) implies

1-\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle=\left|\frac{-\mathrm{i}\Im\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E)}{\langle M_{1}\rangle-\langle M_{2}\rangle}\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle\right|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\pi+\mathcal{O}(\Delta)}\,,

i.e., in particular,
\Re\left[1-\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle\right]<0
. On the other hand, it holds that
\Re\left[1-\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle\right]\geq 1-|\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle|\geq 0
, so we arrived at a
contradiction
and thus
\Im\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E)>0
and
\Im\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E)\gtrsim\Delta^{2}
.

For part (1), we are now left to prove
|\Im\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E)|\lesssim\Delta^{2}
, which is done via a perturbative argument in Appendix
A
. This concludes part (1), i.e.
\Im\mathfrak{s}_{0}^{0,0}(E)\sim\Delta^{2}
.

Part (2): Extension away from the real line.
By (
4.16
) and the fundamental theorem of calculus, we have

\left|\Im\mathfrak{s}_{0}^{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(E)-\Im\mathfrak{s}_{0}^{0,0}(E)\right|\leq\left|\int\limits_{0}^{\eta_{1}}\partial_{\zeta_{1}}\mathfrak{s}_{0}^{\zeta_{1},\eta_{2}}(E)\mathrm{d}\zeta_{1}\right|+\left|\int\limits_{0}^{\eta_{2}}\partial_{\zeta_{2}}\mathfrak{s}_{0}^{0,\zeta_{2}}(E)\mathrm{d}\zeta_{2}\right|\lesssim\Delta(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})\,.

Hence, if
0<\eta_{j}\leq c_{2}\Delta
for some
c_{2}>0
small enough, we obtain
\Im\mathfrak{s}_{0}^{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(E)\sim\Im\mathfrak{s}_{0}^{0,0}(E)\sim\Delta^{2}
.
∎

6.
Contour integration: Proof of technical lemmas from Section

The goal of this section is to give the proofs of the technical lemmas from Section
4.3
, for which we recall the construction of the contours
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
from Section
4
, in particular (
4.2
)–(
4.3
) and Figure
1
, and the definition of the
[a,b]
interval from the beginning of Section
4.3
.

In all of the estimates below, we will frequently use the following simple tools:

•

To gain
1/t
-factors from the oscillatory
\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(z_{1}-z_{2})}
, we integrate by parts.

•

When pulling absolute values inside an integral, we bound
|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(z_{1}-z_{2})}|\lesssim 1
(recall
|\Im z_{j}|\lesssim 1/t
).

•

The convolution of two Cauchy kernels yields another Cauchy kernel: For
\eta_{j}>0
and
E_{j}\in\mathbf{R}
,
j\in[2]
it holds that

(6.1)

\int_{\mathbf{R}}\frac{\eta_{1}}{(x-E_{1})^{2}+\eta_{1}^{2}}\frac{\eta_{2}}{(x-E_{2})^{2}+\eta_{2}^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\frac{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{(E_{1}-E_{2})^{2}+(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})^{2}}\,.

We now turn to the proofs of the lemmas from Section
4.3
.

6.1.
The second line of (
4.23
) is negligible: Proof of Lemma
4.7

We discuss the contributions from the flat and semicircular part of
\gamma_{2}
separately (recall (
4.3
)).

First, the smallness of the integral over
\gamma_{2}^{(2)}
(the semicircular part) is granted by the factor
\mathrm{e}^{t\Im z_{2}}
(note that
\Im z_{2}\in[-R+\eta_{2},\eta_{2}]
) and the estimate
|\langle M(E_{0}+i\eta_{0},z_{2})\rangle|\lesssim 1
, which follows from (
4.9
). More precisely, we have that

(6.2)

\left|\oint_{\gamma_{2}^{(2)}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(E_{0}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{0}-z_{2})}\left\langle M(E_{0}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{0},z_{2})\right\rangle\mathrm{d}z_{2}\right|\lesssim R\int_{\pi}^{2\pi}\mathrm{e}^{tR\sin\theta}\mathrm{d}\theta\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\,.

Next, we bound the integral over
\gamma_{2}^{(1)}
– the flat part. As a first step, integration by parts yields

\left|\int_{\gamma_{2}^{(1)}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(E_{0}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{0}-z_{2})}\left\langle M(E_{0}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{0},z_{2})\right\rangle\mathrm{d}z_{2}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{t}+\left|\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}t}\int_{-R}^{R}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tE_{2}}\partial_{E_{2}}\left\langle M(E_{0}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{0},E_{2}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})\right\rangle\mathrm{d}E_{2}\right|.

The derivative can be explicitly computed as

(6.3)

\partial_{z_{2}}\langle M(z_{1},z_{2})\rangle=\frac{\langle M_{1}M_{2}^{2}\rangle}{(1-\langle M_{2}^{2}\rangle)(1-\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle)^{2}}.

Since
E_{0}
is in the bulk of
\rho_{1}
and
z_{0}:=E_{0}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{0}
and
z_{2}
are in the same half-plane we infer
|1-\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle|\gtrsim 1
and thus

\left|\oint_{\gamma_{2}^{(1)}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(E_{0}+i\eta_{0}-z_{2})}\left\langle M(E_{0}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{0},z_{2})\right\rangle\mathrm{d}z_{2}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{t}+\frac{1}{t}\int_{-R}^{R}\left|\frac{1}{1-\langle M_{2}(E_{2}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})^{2}\rangle}\right|\mathrm{d}E_{2}.

In order to conclude the proof of Lemma
4.7
, we finally use that the one-body stability operator
|1-\langle M_{2}(E_{2}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})^{2}\rangle|^{-1}
is locally integrable, see Lemma
A.1
in Appendix
A
. ∎

6.2.
Cutting tails in the first line of (
4.23
): Proof of Lemma
4.8

For cutting the tails, we focus on the more critical regime, where both parameters are on the horizontal part of the contours,
z_{j}\in\gamma_{j}^{(1)}
for
j\in[2]
(recall (
4.2
)–(
4.3
)). Indeed, if this is not the case, a simple computation using Proposition
4.2
and arguing similarly to (
6.2
) yields
(1+\eta_{0}/\Delta)/t\lesssim 1/t
as an upper bound for the corresponding integrals.

In the critical regime
z_{j}\in\gamma_{j}^{(1)}
for

we carry out only the case

; for
E_{2}\in[-R,a]
the argument is identical. Let
\delta:=(b-E_{0})/2
and split the region of the
E_{1}=\Re z_{1}
-integration into the two parts,
[b-\delta,2R]
and
[-2R,b-\delta]
. In the first regime, using
|E_{1}-E_{0}|\gtrsim 1
and, from Proposition
4.2
,
|\langle M(E_{1}-\mathrm{i}\eta_{1},E_{2}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})\rangle|\lesssim\big((E_{1}-E_{2})^{2}+\Delta^{2}\big)^{-1}
, we find that

\begin{split}\int_{b-\delta}^{2R}\int_{b}^{R}\left|\frac{\eta_{0}}{(E_{1}-\mathrm{i}\eta_{1}-E_{0})^{2}+\eta_{0}^{2}}\langle M(E_{1}-\mathrm{i}\eta_{1},E_{2}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})\rangle\right|\mathrm{d}E_{1}\mathrm{d}E_{2}\lesssim\frac{\eta_{0}}{\Delta}.\end{split}

For
E_{1}\in[-2R,b-\delta]
, by Proposition
4.2
again, we have
|\langle M(z_{1},z_{2})\rangle|\lesssim 1
, since
|E_{1}-E_{2}|\sim 1
. Using this and integration by parts in
E_{2}
, similarly to the proof of Lemma
4.7
, in combination with (
6.3
) and Lemma

, we find that

\left|\int_{-2R}^{b-\delta}\int_{b}^{R}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(z_{1}-E_{2}-\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})}\frac{\eta_{0}}{(E_{1}-\mathrm{i}\eta_{1}-E_{0})^{2}+\eta_{0}^{2}}\langle M(E_{1}-\mathrm{i}\eta_{1},E_{2}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})\rangle\mathrm{d}E_{1}\mathrm{d}E_{2}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\,.

This finishes the proof of Lemma

. ∎

6.3.
First replacement: Proof of Lemma
4.9

Let
\delta>0
be such that
[a-\delta,b+\delta]
is in the bulk of
\rho_{1}
. We now compare the two integrals on the lhs. and rhs. of (
4.18
) by taking their difference. Using integration by parts, the contribution from
(z_{1},z_{2})\in\gamma_{1}^{(2)}\times[a,b]
is bounded by
\eta_{0}/t
. Analogously to the proof of Lemma
4.8
, we also find that the contribution from
([-R,R]\setminus[a-\delta,b+\delta])\times[a,b]
is bounded by
\eta_{0}
, since in this regime
|\langle M(z_{1},I,z_{2})\rangle|\lesssim 1
and
|z_{1}-z_{2}-\mathfrak{s}_{0}(z_{2})|^{-1}\gtrsim 1
.

We are hence left to estimate the contribution from the region
[a-\delta,b+\delta]\times[a,b]
. Using that
|\mathfrak{s}(z_{1},z_{2})-\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E_{2})|\lesssim\Delta|E_{1}-\Re f(z_{2})|
by means of Lemma
4.4
, we find that this can be bounded by

\mathcal{E}:=\int_{a-\delta}^{b+\delta}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\eta_{0}}{(E_{1}-E_{0})^{2}+\eta_{0}^{2}}\cdot\frac{\Delta|E_{1}-\Re f(z_{2})|}{|z_{1}-z_{2}-\mathfrak{s}(z_{1},z_{2})|\cdot|z_{1}-z_{2}-\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E_{2})|}\mathrm{d}E_{1}\mathrm{d}E_{2}\,.

To have better control on
\mathcal{E}
, we now bound the denominators in the second factor from below.
First, using the definition of
\mathfrak{d}
from the formulation of Lemma
4.9
, we get

(6.4)

|z_{1}-z_{2}-\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E_{2})|^{2}=(E_{1}-E_{2}-\Re\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E_{2}))^{2}+(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}+\Im\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E_{2}))^{2}\gtrsim(E_{1}-f(E_{2}))^{2}+\mathfrak{d}^{2}\,.

Next, using that

, as simple consequence of the stability bound (

), we infer

(6.5)

|z_{1}-z_{2}-\mathfrak{s}(z_{1},z_{2})|^{2}\sim|z_{1}-z_{2}-\mathfrak{s}(z_{1},z_{2})|^{2}+\Delta^{4}\gtrsim(E_{1}-E_{2}-\Re\mathfrak{s}(z_{1},z_{2}))^{2}+\Delta^{4}\,.

Finally, using the defining properties of the renormalization function
f
given in Lemma
4.5
, (
4.14
) from Lemma
4.4
, and the triangle inequality, one easily sees that

(6.6)

|E_{1}-E_{2}-\Re\mathfrak{s}(z_{1},z_{2})|\sim|E_{1}-f(E_{2})|.

Hence, combining (
6.4
) and (
6.5
)–(
6.6
) we find that

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}&\lesssim\int_{a-\delta}^{b+\delta}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\eta_{0}}{(E_{1}-E_{0})^{2}+\eta_{0}^{2}}\cdot\frac{\Delta|E_{1}-f(E_{2})|}{|E_{1}-f(E_{2})|^{2}+(\min\{\mathfrak{d},\Delta^{2}\})^{2}}\mathrm{d}E_{1}\mathrm{d}E_{2}\\
&\lesssim\int_{a-\delta}^{b+\delta}\frac{\eta_{0}\Delta(|\log\Delta|+|\log\mathfrak{d}|)}{(E_{1}-E)^{2}+\eta_{0}^{2}}\mathrm{d}E_{1}\lesssim\Delta(|\log\Delta|+|\log\mathfrak{d}|).\end{split}

where in the second step we changed the integration variable from
E_{2}
to

and employed (
4.16
) from Lemma
4.5
. This concludes the proof of Lemma
4.9
. ∎

6.4.
Residue computation after the first replacement: Proof of Lemma

Using the integral representation
\langle M_{1}(z_{1})\rangle=\int_{\mathbf{R}}\rho_{1}(x)(x-z_{1})^{-1}\mathrm{d}x

) ensures

where we introduced the shorthand notations

\begin{split}&\mathcal{E}_{1}:=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{a}^{b}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(E_{0}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{0}-E_{2}-\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})}\frac{\langle M_{1}(E_{0}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{0})\rangle-\langle M_{2}(E_{2}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})\rangle}{E_{0}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{0}-(E_{2}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2}+\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E_{2}))}\mathrm{d}E_{2},\\
&\mathcal{E}_{2}:=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{a}^{b}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E_{2})}\frac{\eta_{0}\left(\langle M_{1}(E_{0}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{0})\rangle-\langle M_{2}(E_{2}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})\rangle\right)}{(E_{2}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2}+\mathfrak{s}_{0}(E_{2})-E_{0})^{2}+\eta_{0}^{2}}\mathrm{d}E_{2}\,.\end{split}

Moreover, we shall abbreviate
z_{0}:=E_{0}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{0}
,

. Then, to estimate
\mathcal{E}_{1}
, we employ integration by parts and find that, since
\left|\partial_{E_{2}}\langle M_{2}(z_{2})\rangle\right|\lesssim 1
as
\rho_{2}(z_{2})\gtrsim 1
, using (
4.16
) from Lemma
4.5
, and recalling the definition of
\mathfrak{a}
from the formulation of Lemma

A.1.
Upper bound on the renormalized shift: Perturbation argument for Lemma

The goal of this section is to prove the upper bound

M_{2}M_{1}=\frac{1}{\langle\Im M\rangle}\left(\Im M+2\mathrm{i}\Im\left[\Im M\Theta M\right]+2\mathrm{i}\Im\left[\frac{\langle\Theta M^{2}\rangle}{1-\langle M^{2}\rangle}\Im M\cdot M\right]+\mathcal{O}(|\Theta|^{2})\right)\,,

\{z\in\mathbf{C}:\Im z>0\}
(by
[
3
, Lemma 5.5]
) and
|\sigma(\tau_{0})|\sim\Delta^{1/3}
(by
[
3
, Theorem 7.7, Lemma 7.16]
). Therefore, there exists a (small) positive constant
c\sim 1
such that for all
z
with
\Re z\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+c\Delta]
and
\Im z\in[0,c\Delta]
it holds that
|\sigma(z)|\sim\Delta^{1/3}
. It is easy to see that the integral of the one-body stability operator over
[\tau_{0}+c\Delta,\tau_{0}+\delta_{*}]
has an upper bound of order one by means of (
A.4
) and (
A.3a
). In the complementary regime
[\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+c\Delta]
we distinguish between two cases (i)
\eta\in[0,c\Delta]
and (ii)
\eta>c\Delta
. In the first case, note that, by the integral representation
\rho(E+i\eta)=\int_{\mathbf{R}}\mathrm{d}x\rho(x)\eta/((x-E)^{2}+\eta^{2})
and (
A.3a
), it holds that
\rho(E+\mathrm{i}\eta)\gtrsim\rho(E)
for
E\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+c\Delta]
. Thus

\int_{0}^{c\Delta}\frac{\mathrm{d}\omega}{|1-\langle M^{2}(\tau_{0}+\omega+i\eta)\rangle|}\lesssim\Delta^{1/3}\int_{0}^{c\Delta}\frac{\mathrm{d}\omega}{\omega^{1/2}(\Delta^{1/2}+\omega^{1/2})}\lesssim 1.

In the second case,
\eta>c\Delta
, we use (
A.4
) and the bound
|1-\langle M^{2}(z)\rangle|\gtrsim|\Im z|
to get

\int_{0}^{c\Delta}\frac{\mathrm{d}\omega}{|1-\langle M^{2}(\tau_{0}+\omega+i\eta)\rangle|}\lesssim\int_{0}^{c\Delta}\frac{\mathrm{d}\omega}{\rho^{2}(\tau_{0}+\omega)+\Delta}\sim\Delta^{1/3}\int_{0}^{c\Delta}\frac{\mathrm{d}\omega}{\omega+\Delta^{4/3}}\lesssim 1\,,

which concludes the proof for the regular edge.

L

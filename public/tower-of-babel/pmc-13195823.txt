Methodological limitations of NOM in the WSES acute appendicitis guidelines
Authors: Pedro Henrique Grosso, João Vitor Grosso, Francisco Alcides Dias Filho
Published: 2026

The endorsement of nonoperative management (NOM) for uncomplicated acute appendicitis by the 2025 WSES Guidelines warrants strict methodological scrutiny regarding oncologic risk and diagnostic accuracy [1]. Applying the GRADE framework [2], we highlight that admission computed tomography introduces severe clinical indirectness in real-world triage. It routinely underestimates microperforations or early transmural ischemia and fails to exclude occult appendiceal neoplasms in up to 3.2% of cases [3].

Furthermore, with contemporary data demonstrating that NOM recurrence rates reach 42.6% at three years [4], alongside the compounded morbidity, higher rates of percutaneous drainage, and general complications during a delayed surgical rescue in high-risk subgroups (such as those with an appendicolith) [5], the perceived safety profile of initial antibiotic therapy is fundamentally skewed.

We bring this forward out of pragmatic concern for definitive anatomical resolution and operative safety. Clinical guidelines must weigh the initial appeal of antibiotic therapy against the compounded morbidity of a delayed surgical rescue. Our critique advocates for definitive anatomical resolution and operative safety as the primary endpoints in emergency surgical care.

Abstract
. In 2006, Ihara defined and systematically studied a generalization of the Euler-Mascheroni constant for all global fields, named the Euler-Kronecker constants. This paper examines their distribution across geometric quadratic extensions of a rational global function field, via the values of logarithmic derivatives of Dirichlet L L -functions at 1. Using a probabilistic model, we show that the values converge to a limiting distribution with a smooth, positive density function, as the genii of quadratic fields approach infinity. We then prove a discrepancy theorem for the convergence of the frequency of these values, and obtain information about the proportion of the small values. Finally, we prove omega results on the extreme values. Our theorems imply new distribution results on the stable Taguchi heights and logarithmic Weil heights of rank 2 Drinfeld modules with CM.

Introduction

In
[
16
]
, Ihara defines the Euler-Kronecker constant of a global field
F
as the limit

\gamma_{F}=\lim_{s\to 1}\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(s,F)}{\zeta(s,F)}+\frac{1}{s-1}\right),

where
\zeta(s,F)
is the Dedekind zeta function of
F
in the complex variable
s
(see also
[
15
]
*pp.496-497 for the number field case). If
F
is a global function field, i.e.,
F
is the function field of a proper, smooth, and geometrically connected curve
C
defined over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, it is then shown in
[
16
]
*(1.4.3)) that

(1.1)

\frac{\gamma_{F}}{\log{q}}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{q^{m}+1-N_{m}}{q^{m}}\right)+\frac{q-3}{2(q-1)},

where
N_{m}
denotes the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-rational points of
C
. This formula allows explicit computation of
\gamma_{F}
. For instance, when
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
, then
N_{m}=q^{m}+1
for all
m\geq 1
, hence
\gamma_{q}:=\gamma_{F}=(q-3)\log(q)/(2(q-1))
. Now, consider a supersingular elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{F}_{p}
for a prime
p\geq 5
with Weierstrass equation
y^{2}=D
for some monic
D
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
. For each integer
m\geq 1
,
N_{m}
equals
p^{m}+1
if
m
is odd, and
\left(p^{\frac{m}{2}}-(-1)^{\frac{m}{2}}\right)^{2}
if
m
is even (see
[
29
]
*Exercise 5.15). Therefore, if
F
is the function field of
E/\mathbb{F}_{p}
, then

\gamma_{F}=\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{p^{m}+1-N_{m}}{p^{m}}\right)+1-\frac{p+1}{2(p-1)}\right)\log(p)=\frac{p-5}{2(p+1)}.

More generally, Ihara observes from (
1.1
) that if
N_{m}
is large for small
m
(in particular for
m=1
), then
\gamma_{F}
tends to be negative (see also
[
16
]
*Theorem 4). Ihara puts forward
\gamma_{F}
as an invariant of
F
and investigates its cardinality. If
F
has genus
g_{F}
, then one has (see
[
16
]
*Theorem 1 and Proposition 3)

(1.2)

-\log\left(q^{g_{F}-1}\right)+O_{q}(1)\leq\gamma_{F}\leq 2\log\left(\log\left(q^{g_{F}-1}\right)\right)+O_{q}(1).

Moreover, for an infinite family of global function fields
F
of unbounded genii
g_{F}
and fixed degree
N\geq 1
over a given rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
, Ihara shows
[
16
]
*Corollary 2

(1.3)

\gamma_{F}>-2(N-1+\varepsilon)\log\left(\log\left(q^{g_{F}-1}\right)\right),

for any real number
\varepsilon>0
.

Let
q
be a power of an odd prime number. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
be the ring of polynomials in the variable
t
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For each integer
n\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{H}_{n}
be the subset of degree

\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
. In this paper, we study the magnitude of
\gamma_{D}
, the Euler-Kronecker constant of

, the function field of the hyperelliptic curve of affine model
y^{2}=D
as
D
varies in the set
\mathcal{H}_{n}
. From the Hurwitz genus formula, the genus of
K_{D}
is

(1.4)

g_{D}=\begin{cases}\frac{n}{2}-1&\text{ if }n\text{ is even},\\
\frac{n-1}{2}&\text{ if }n\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

Now, let
\chi_{D}
be the quadratic Dirichlet character associated with a given
D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
and let
L(s,\chi_{D})
be the corresponding Dirichlet
L
-function. Its logarithmic derivative at

satisfies the relation

(1.5)

\gamma_{D}=\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\chi_{D})}{L(1,\chi_{D})}+\widetilde{\gamma_{q}},

where

\widetilde{\gamma_{q}}=\begin{cases}\gamma_{q}&\text{if }n\text{ is odd},\\
\gamma_{q}+\zeta_{q}(2)&\text{if }n\text{ is even},\\
\end{cases}

with
\zeta_{q}(2):=\zeta(2,\mathbb{F}_{q}[t])=q/(q-1)
. (See Subsection
2.2
for a proof of (
1.5
).) As we see from (
1.5
), the magnitudes of
\gamma_{D}
and

are intimately related. From
[
17
]
*(6.8.20), we have

(1.6)

\left|\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\chi_{D})}{L(1,\chi_{D})}\right|\ll_{q}\log\left(\log{q^{n}}\right),

and so
\left|\gamma_{D}\right|\ll_{q}\log\left(\log{q^{n}}\right)
.

In this paper, we show that for a proportion of polynomials

in

the values of
|{L^{\prime}(1,\chi_{D})}/{L(1,\chi_{D})}|
are significantly smaller than the upper bound in (

Theorem 1.1
.

For

, we have

\min\limits_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\left(\left|\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\chi_{D})}{L(1,\chi_{D})}\right|\right)\ll_{q}\frac{\log^{2}(\log(q^{n}))}{\log(q^{n})}.

More precisely, there are
\gg_{q}q^{n}\log^{2}(\log(q^{n}))/\log(q^{n})
polynomials

for which

\left|\gamma_{D}-\widetilde{\gamma}_{q}\right|=\left|\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\chi_{D})}{L(1,\chi_{D})}\right|\ll_{q}\frac{\log^{2}(\log(q^{n}))}{\log(q^{n})}.

Theorem 1.2

Let

Bounds for

Applications to Taguchi and Weil Heights

In this subsection, we use Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
to provide refined information on stable Taguchi heights of Drinfeld modules and logarithmic Weil heights of their
j
-invariants. We start with background material and refer the reader to
[

]
and
[
32
]
for more details.

Let
F
be a field containing
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
F\{\tau\}
the set of polynomials in the variable
\tau
and coefficients in
F
. Endowed with the usual polynomial addition and the twisted multiplication
\tau a=a^{q}\tau
for all
a\in F
, the set
F\{\tau\}
is a non-commutative ring.

Let
|\cdot|
be the absolute value corresponding to the place
\infty
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
, normalized by
|t|=q
. This absolute value extends uniquely to an algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)_{\infty}}
of the completion
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)_{\infty}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
at
\infty
. We write
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
for the algebraically closed complete valued field of characteristic
p>0
that is the completion of
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)_{\infty}}
. We embed
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
into
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
via
\infty
. For
F
a subfield of
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
, we let
\overline{F}
be its algebraic closure in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
and
F^{\text{sep}}
its separable closure in
\overline{F}
.

A Drinfeld
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
-module over
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
of rank
2
is a ring morphism
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\{\tau\}
with

(1.7)

\rho(t)=t+a\tau+\Delta\tau^{2},

where
a,\Delta\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
and
\Delta\neq 0
. We simply say Drinfeld module if there is no risk of confusion. Its
j
-invariant is the element
j(\rho):=a^{q+1}/\Delta
of
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. If
F
is a subfield of
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
such that
\rho(\mathbb{F}_{q}[t])
is in
F\{\tau\}
, then we say that
\rho
is defined over
F
and denote by
\rho/F
. In particular,
\rho/\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)}
is defined over a finite extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
since
a
and
\Delta
are algebraic over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
.

A morphism
\rho_{1}\to\rho_{2}
of Drinfeld modules over
F
is a
\phi\in F\{\tau\}
such that
\phi\rho_{1}(a)=\rho_{2}(a)\phi
for all
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
. In particular, if
\phi
is invertible, we say it is an isomorphism and
\rho_{1}
and that
\rho_{2}
are isomorphic over
F
, while if
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}
, we say
\phi
is an endomorphism of
\rho/F
. That
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are isomorphic over
F^{\text{sep}}
amounts to
j(\rho_{1})=j(\rho_{2})
(see
[
26
]
*Lemma 3.8.4). If
\rho/F
is a Drinfeld module given by (
1.7
), then up to an
F^{\text{sep}}
-isomorphism we can and will consider it as given by
\rho(t)=t+\tau+j(\rho)^{-1}\tau^{2}
.

Now suppose
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
is a finite extension. We can define the logarithmic Weil height
h(j(\rho))
of
j(\rho)
(see
[
5
]
*(3)), and, related to it, a naive height for
\rho/F
, as in
[
10
]
*Définition 2.5, by

H(\rho):=\max\{h(t),h(1),h(j(\rho)^{-1})\}=\max\{1,h(j(\rho))\}\leq 1+h(j(\rho)).

Another height,
h_{\mathrm{Tag}}(\rho/F)
, is defined for
\rho/F
by Taguchi in
[
30
]
*Section 5 and coined by Wei in
[
32
]
as the Taguchi height of
\rho/F
. This height plays the role of an analogue of the Faltings height introduced in
[
11
]
. Up to a finite extension of
F
of degree at most

,
\rho/F
has “stable reduction everywhere” (see
[
10
]
*Lemme 2.10). From
[

]
*p.1066, it follows that the Taguchi heights
h_{\mathrm{Tag}}(\rho/L)
coincide for all large enough extensions

. Wei then introduces the stable Taguchi height of
\rho
as the always-existing limit

h^{\mathrm{St}}_{\mathrm{Tag}}(\rho):=\log(q)\lim_{F^{\prime}/F\text{ finite }}h_{\mathrm{Tag}}(\rho/F^{\prime}).

That
\rho_{1}/\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)}
and
\rho_{2}/\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)}
are isomorphic over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)}
amounts to
h^{\mathrm{St}}_{\mathrm{Tag}}(\rho_{1})=h^{\mathrm{St}}_{\mathrm{Tag}}(\rho_{2})
(see
[

]
*Remark 4.2 (3)). For
\rho/\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)}
,
[

]
*Lemme 2.14 (v) gives

The set
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]}(\rho/F)
of endomorphisms of
\rho/F
is a ring. For an odd integer
n\geq 1
and
D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
, we let
K_{D}
be the quadratic extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
generated by a square root of

in
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)}
(a so-called imaginary quadratic field). The integral closure of

in

. A Drinfeld module

(or CM by

means that
A(x)\ll B(x)
and
B(x)\ll A(x)
. A constant with a subscript indicates that the constant depends on that subscript. Moreover, writing
A(x)=O_{\alpha,\beta}(B(x))
or equivalently
A(x)\ll_{\alpha,\beta}B(x)
, indicates that the implicit constant depends on
\alpha
and
\beta
.

We use
q
for a positive power of an odd prime,
\log_{q}
for the logarithm in base
q

\mathbb{F}_{q}
for the finite field with
q
elements,
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
for the polynomial ring over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
in a variable

, and
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
for its fraction field. Inside
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
, we consider the subsets
\mathcal{M}
of monic elements,
\mathcal{H}
of square-free elements, and
\mathcal{P}
of irreducible elements. A generic element of
\mathcal{P}
is denoted as
P
. For each integer

,
\mathcal{M}_{n}
,
\mathcal{H}_{n}
and
\mathcal{P}_{n}
, respectively, denote the subsets of
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{P}
of degree-
n
elements and set
\mathcal{M}_{0}=\{1\}
. We let
\pi_{q}(n)
be the cardinality of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
. We also write
\mathcal{M}_{\leq n}
(respectively
\mathcal{P}_{\leq n}
) for the union of the
\mathcal{M}_{j}
(respectively of the
\mathcal{P}_{j}
) with
1\leq j\leq n
, and
\mathcal{M}_{>n}
as its complement in
\mathcal{M}
. In particular, the cardinality of
\mathcal{P}_{\leq n}
is denoted
\Pi_{q}(n)
. Additionally,
\mathscr{P}(n)
denotes the product of all
P\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq n}
. The respective cardinalities of
\mathcal{M}_{n},\mathcal{H}_{n}
and
\mathcal{P}_{n}
are

(2.1)

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{M}_{n}=q^{n},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{H}_{1}=q\text{ and }\#\mathcal{H}_{n}=q^{n}-q^{n-1}\text{ if }n>1,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\pi_{q}(n)=\frac{q^{n}}{n}+O\left(\frac{q^{\frac{n}{2}}}{n}\right),

(2.4)

\displaystyle\pi_{q}(n)\geq\frac{q^{n}}{n}-\frac{q^{\frac{n}{2}}}{n}-q^{\frac{n}{3}},

see

]
*Theorem 2.2 and its proof, and Proposition 2.3). Furthermore, the notations
\sum_{f}
and
\prod_{P}
represent the limits
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq n}}
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\prod_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq n}}
.

2.1.
Affine Setting

Each
f\in\mathcal{M}
of degree
d_{f}
has norm
N(f)=q^{d_{f}}
. The zeta function of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
is defined as the series
\zeta(s,\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]):=\sum_{f}1/N(f)^{s}
in the complex variable
s
. This series converges absolutely for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>
1, as well as the product in

(2.5)

\frac{1}{1-q^{1-s}}=\zeta(s,\mathbb{F}_{q}[t])=\prod_{P}\frac{1}{1-N(P)^{-s}}.

We define the von Mangoldt function
\Lambda
on
\mathcal{M}
by

\Lambda(f)=\begin{cases}\log(N(P))&\text{if }f=P^{m},\text{ for some integer }m\geq 1,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Taking the logarithmic derivatives in (
2.5
), we find

(2.6)

\frac{\log(q)}{q^{s-1}-1}=-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(s,\mathbb{F}_{q}[t])}{\zeta(s,\mathbb{F}_{q}[t])}=\sum_{f}\frac{\Lambda(f)}{N(f)^{s}}.

Next, letting
T:=q^{-s}
in (
2.5
) gives
(1-qT)^{-1}=\prod_{P}(1-T^{d_{P}})^{-1}
. Taking the logarithmic derivatives and comparing their coefficients of the series expansions in
T
yield

(2.7)

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\Lambda(f)=q^{n},\text{ for all integers }n\geq 1.

The following estimates are used in the text without always being explicitly mentioned.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
y\geq 1
be a real number. We have

(2.8)

\sum_{N(f)\leq y}\frac{1}{N(f)}=\lfloor\log_{q}(y)\rfloor+1,\sum_{N(f)\leq y}\frac{1}{N(f)^{a}}\ll_{q}1\text{ for an integer }a>1

and

(2.9)

\sum_{N(f)\leq y}\frac{\Lambda(f)}{N(f)}=\lfloor\log_{q}(y)\rfloor.

Proof.

The assertions in (
2.8
) are direct computations and (
2.9
) follows from (
2.7
).
∎

Let
D\in\mathcal{M}
and
P\in\mathcal{P}
. We set

(2.10)

\chi_{D}(P)=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if }D\pmod{P}\text{ is a non-square},\\
0&\text{if }D\pmod{P}\text{ is zero},\\
1&\text{if }D\pmod{P}\text{ is a non-zero square}.\end{cases}

We then define, for
f\in\mathcal{M}
, the quantity
\chi_{D}(f):=\prod_{P|f}\chi_{D}(P)^{\ord_{P}(f)}
, where
\ord_{P}(f)
is the
P
-adic valuation of
f
. This gives a real quadratic character modulo
D
on
\mathcal{M}
, denoted
\chi_{D}
.

The Dirichlet
L
-function of
\chi_{D}
is defined, for
\re(s)>1
, as

(2.11)

L(s,\chi_{D}):=\sum_{f}\frac{\chi_{D}(f)}{N(f)^{s}}=\prod_{P\nmid D}\frac{1}{1-\chi_{D}(P)N(P)^{-s}}.

The series converges absolutely and so does the product. An analogous reasoning that yields (
2.6
), shows that

(2.12)

\sum_{P}\frac{\chi_{D}(P)\Lambda(P)}{N(P)^{s}-\chi_{D}(P)}=-\frac{L^{\prime}(s,\chi_{D})}{L(s,\chi_{D})}=\sum_{f}\frac{\Lambda(f)\chi_{D}(f)}{N(f)^{s}}.

From
[
17
]
*Equation (6.8.20) we have the following.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any integer
n\geq 1
and
D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
, we have

(2.13)

\left|\frac{L^{\prime}(s,\chi_{D})}{L(s,\chi_{D})}\right|\ll_{q}\begin{cases}\log^{2(1-\re(s))}(q^{n})&\text{ if }\frac{1}{2}<\re(s)<1,\\
\log\left(\log(q^{n})\right)&\text{ if }\re(s)=1,\\
1&\text{ if }\re(s)>1.\end{cases}

The next estimates are also useful.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{M}\smallsetminus\{1\}
.

(i)

If
f
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
, then

\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\chi_{D}(f)=\#\mathcal{H}_{n}\prod_{P|f}\left(\frac{N(P)}{N(P)+1}\right)+O_{q}\left((\#\mathcal{H}_{n})^{1/2}\right).

(ii)

If
f
is not a square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
, then for each
\varepsilon>0
we have

\left|\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\chi_{D}(f)\right|\ll_{\varepsilon,q}(q^{n})^{1/2}N(f)^{\varepsilon}.

To obtain these estimates, we modify and extend
[
3
]
*Proposition 5.2 and
[
8
]
*Lemma 3.5 to all odd prime powers
q
.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
(i).

The proof of
[
3
]
*Proposition 5.2 can be directly extended from
n\geq 1
odd integer to any
n\geq 1
integer. and one gets

\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\chi_{D}(f)=\frac{q^{n}}{\zeta_{q}(2)}\prod_{P\mid f}\left(\frac{N(P)}{N(P)+1}\right)+O\left(\frac{q^{n}}{(q-1)q^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}}\prod_{P\mid f}\left(\frac{N(P)-1}{N(P)}\right)\right).

Since
q^{n}/\zeta_{q}(2)=\#\mathcal{H}_{n}
,
\prod_{P|f}(N(P)-1)/N(P)=O_{q}(1)
and
q^{n}/((q-1)q^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor})=O_{q}\left(\left(\#\mathcal{H}_{n}\right)^{1/2}\right)
, the assertion is proven.
∎

In
[

]
*Lemma 3.5, the authors proved Lemma

(ii) assuming

odd and, for simplicity,

. As suggested by

Remark 2.4
.

In
[
2
]
*Theorem 3.3, the authors prove that if
q
is a prime number satisfying
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
and
D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
with

, then the Lindelöf bound

(2.14)

\left|L\left(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{D}\right)\right|\leq e^{\frac{2g_{D}}{\log_{q}(g_{D})}+4q^{\frac{1}{2}}g_{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}}

holds. Their proof of (
2.14
) holds for any prime power
q
. Combining (
2.14
) with the vertical periodicity of
L(s,\chi_{D})
, we deduce that for all
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\re(s)=1/2
and all

,

(2.15)

\left|L(s,\chi_{D})\right|\ll_{\varepsilon,q}(q^{n})^{\varepsilon}.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
(ii) (when
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
and both
n
and
d_{f}
are odd).

Let
T:=q^{-s}
and
\mathcal{L}(T,\chi_{D}):=L(s,\chi_{D})
. By the quadratic reciprocity, multiplicativity of characters, and considering Euler factors of
L
-functions, we have

(2.16)

\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}}\chi_{D}(f)T^{d_{D}}=\prod_{P\nmid f}\left(1+\chi_{f}(P)(-T)^{d_{P}}\right)=\frac{\mathcal{L}(-T,\chi_{f})}{\mathcal{L}(T^{2},\chi_{f}^{2})}=\frac{\mathcal{L}(-T,\chi_{f})(1-qT^{2})}{\prod_{P\mid f}\left(1-T^{2d_{P}}\right)}.

Using Perron’s formula and (
2.16
), we get

(2.17)

\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\chi_{D}(f)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|T|=q^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{\mathcal{L}(-T,\chi_{f})(1-qT^{2})}{T^{n+1}\prod_{P\mid f}\left(1-T^{2d_{P}}\right)}dT.

Now, write
f=f_{1}f_{2}^{2}
with
f_{1}\in\mathcal{H}
. Note that
\chi_{f}(P)=\chi_{f_{1}}(P)
if
(P,f_{1})=1
, while if
P\mid f
and
P\nmid f_{1}
then
\chi_{f}(P)=0
and
\chi_{f_{1}}(P)\neq 0
. Hence,

\mathcal{L}(-T,\chi_{f})=\mathcal{L}(-T,\chi_{f_{1}})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\nmid f_{1}\\
P\mid f_{2}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\chi_{f_{1}}(P)(-T)^{d_{P}}\right).

Applying (
2.15
) to
\mathcal{L}(-q^{-1/2},\chi_{f_{1}})
in (
2.17
), we have

(2.18)

\left|\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\chi_{D}(f)\right|\ll_{\varepsilon,q}\tau(f)N(f_{1})^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\int_{|T|=q^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\left|\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\nmid f_{1}\\
P\mid f_{2}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\chi_{f_{1}}(P)(-T)^{d_{P}}\right)\left(\frac{1-q^{2}T}{T^{n+1}}\right)\right|\mid dT\mid,

where we used that
\tau(f)=\sum_{d\mid f}d
satisfies
\mid\prod_{P\mid f}1/(1-T^{2d_{P}})\mid\leq\tau(f)
. Now, for
\mid T\mid=q^{-\frac{1}{2}}
,

(2.19)

\left|\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\nmid f_{1}\\
P\mid f_{2}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\chi_{f_{1}}(P)(-T)^{d_{P}}\right)\left(\frac{1-q^{2}T}{T^{n+1}}\right)\right|\ll_{q}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\nmid f_{1}\\
P\mid f_{2}\end{subarray}}\left(1+q^{-\frac{d_{P}}{2}}\right)q^{\frac{n}{2}}\ll_{q}2^{\omega(f)}q^{\frac{n}{2}}\ll_{q}\tau(f)q^{\frac{n}{2}},

where
\omega(f)
is the number of distinct prime factors of
f
. As
\tau(f)\ll_{\varepsilon}N(f)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}
, applying (
2.19
) into (
2.18
) concludes since

\left|\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\chi_{D}(f)\right|\ll_{\varepsilon,q}q^{\frac{n}{2}}\tau(f)N(f_{1})^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\ll_{\varepsilon,q}\left(q^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}N(f)^{\varepsilon}.\qed

2.2.
Projective Setting

Fix an algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
. For any integer
n\geq 1
and
D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
, let
\sqrt{D}
be an element of
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)}
whose square is
D
. Let
K_{D}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)(\sqrt{D})
. The quadratic character
\psi_{D}
of
\Gal(K_{D}/\mathbb{F}_{q}(t))
is defined at each
P\in\mathcal{P}
by

(2.20)

\psi_{D}(P)=\begin{cases}-1&\text{ if }P\text{ is inert in }K_{D},\\
0&\text{ if }P\text{ ramifies in }K_{D},\\
1&\text{ if }P\text{ splits in }K_{D},\end{cases}

and at the infinite place
\infty
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
as

(2.21)

\psi_{D}(\infty)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\infty\text{ ramifies in }K_{D},\\
1&\text{ if }\infty\text{ splits in }K_{D}.\end{cases}

The Artin
L
-function
L^{\mathrm{Art}}(s,\psi_{D})
attached to
\psi_{D}
is defined as

L^{\mathrm{Art}}(s,\psi_{D})=\frac{1}{1-\psi_{D}(\infty)q^{-s}}\times\prod_{P}\frac{1}{1-\psi_{D}(P)q^{-d_{P}s}},

which is holomorphic and non-vanishing on
\re(s)>1
(see
[
28
]
*Proposition 9.15).

From the theory of characters (see
[
28
]
*Proposition 14.9) we have

(2.22)

\zeta(s,K_{D})=\zeta(s,\mathbb{F}_{q}(t))L^{\mathrm{Art}}(s,\psi_{D}).

Also,
[
28
]
*Propositions 14.6 and 17.7 gives

(2.23)

L(s,\chi_{D})=\begin{cases}L^{\mathrm{Art}}(s,\psi_{D})&\text{ if }\infty\text{ ramifies in }K_{D},\text{ which happens when }n\text{ is odd,}\\
L^{\mathrm{Art}}(s,\psi_{D})(1-q^{-s})&\text{ if }\infty\text{ splits in }K_{D},\text{ which happens when }n\text{ is even}.\end{cases}

Taking the logarithmic derivative of (
2.22
) when
\re(s)>1
and using (
2.23
), we obtain

(2.24)

\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(s,K_{D})}{\zeta(s,K_{D})}=\begin{cases}\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(s,\mathbb{F}_{q}(t))}{\zeta(s,\mathbb{F}_{q}(t))}+\frac{L^{\prime}(s,\chi_{D})}{L(s,\chi_{D})}&\text{if }n\text{ is odd},\\
\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(s,\mathbb{F}_{q}(t))}{\zeta(s,\mathbb{F}_{q}(t))}+\frac{L^{\prime}(s,\chi_{D})}{L(s,\chi_{D})}+(1-q^{-s})^{-1}&\text{if }n\text{ is even}.\\
\end{cases}

Hence, (
1.5
) follows by letting
s
go to
1
.

3.
Computing Moments

In this section, we compute integral moments of
-L^{\prime}(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)
. We start with a preparatory lemma that will used in Proposition
3.2
below.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any real
0<x<1
and positive integers
n
and
r
, let

\Phi(x,-r,n):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(n+k)^{r}x^{k}.

We have

\mid\Phi(x,-r,n)\mid\leq\frac{(3nr)^{r}r!}{(1-x)^{r+1}}.

Proof.

From
[
6
]
*p.164 and (3.2) and (3.7),
\beta_{0}(x)=0
(see
[
6
]
*p.165) and
\frac{k}{r+1}\binom{r+1}{k}=\binom{r}{k-1}
, we have

\Phi(x,-r,n)=-\sum_{k=1}^{r+1}\binom{r}{k-1}\left(\sum_{s=1}^{k-1}(-1)^{s}s!\frac{x^{s}}{(x-1)^{s+1}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k-1}{s}\right)n^{r+1-k},

where
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k-1}{s}
are Stirling numbers of the second kind. Since
|x|<1
and
|x-1|<1
, then

\mid\Phi(x,-r,n)\mid\leq\frac{n^{r}r!}{|x-1|^{r+1}}\sum_{k=1}^{r+1}\binom{r}{k-1}\sum_{s=1}^{k-1}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k-1}{s}.

Now
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k-1}{s}\leq\binom{k-1}{s}s^{k-1-s}
(
[
27
]
*Theorem 3), and in the given ranges for
s
and
k
above we have
s^{k-1-s}<r^{r}
. Thus,

\mid\Phi(x,-r,n)\mid\leq\frac{(nr)^{r}r!}{|x-1|^{r+1}}\sum_{k=1}^{r+1}\binom{r}{k-1}\sum_{s=1}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{s},

and as

\sum_{k=1}^{r+1}\binom{r}{k-1}\sum_{s=1}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{s}=\sum_{k=1}^{r+1}\binom{r}{k-1}(2^{k-1}-1)=3^{r}-2^{r},

the result follows.
∎

We now write powers of
-L^{\prime}(s,\chi)/L(s,\chi)
for
\re(s)>1/2
in terms of short Dirichlet polynomials. This is an improvement on its number field analogue
[
21
]
*Proposition 2.3.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\chi
be a non-trivial Dirichlet character. Let
c_{0}>0
and
0<\delta<c_{0}
be real numbers. For any complex number
s
with
\re(s)=c_{0}+1/2
, and any integer
r
satisfying

1\leq r\leq\frac{\delta}{4}\frac{\log(q^{n})}{\log(\log(q^{n}))},

we have, for
n\geq\max\{1/(1-q^{-c_{0}}),3r\}
,

\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(s,\chi)}{L(s,\chi)}\right)^{r}=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq n-1}}\frac{\Lambda_{r}(f)\chi(f)}{N(f)^{s}}+O\left((q^{n})^{-c_{0}+\delta}\right),

where

\Lambda_{r}(f):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{r})\in\mathcal{M}^{r}\\
f_{1}\cdots f_{r}=f\end{subarray}}\Lambda(f_{1})\cdots\Lambda(f_{r}).

Proof.

Let
T:=q^{-s}
. From
[
28
]
*pp.40-41, we have
L(s,\chi)=\mathcal{L}(T,\chi)=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(1-\alpha_{i}(\chi)T)
and
|\alpha_{i}(\chi)|\in\{1,q^{1/2}\}
. For
\mid T\mid<q^{-1/2}
, we can write

T\frac{d\left(\log(\mathcal{L}(T,\chi))\right)}{dT}=\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}c_{N}(\chi)T^{N},

where
c_{N}(\chi)=-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\alpha_{i}(\chi)^{N}
. Then,

(3.1)

\left(T\frac{d\left(\log(\mathcal{L}(T,\chi))\right)}{dT}\right)^{r}=\sum_{N=r}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(N_{1},\cdots,N_{r})\\
N_{1}+\cdots+N_{r}=N\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}c_{N_{i}}(\chi)\right)T^{N}+\sum_{N=n}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(N_{1},\cdots,N_{r})\\
N_{1}+\cdots+N_{r}=N\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}c_{N_{i}}(\chi)\right)T^{N}.

We start with the tail of (
3.1
). Since
\mid\alpha_{i}(\chi)\mid\leq q^{\frac{1}{2}}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, then
\mid c_{N}(\chi)\mid\leq(n-1)q^{\frac{N}{2}}
and therefore

(3.2)

\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(N_{1},\cdots,N_{r})\\
N_{1}+\cdots+N_{r}=N\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}c_{N_{i}}(\chi)\right|\leq\binom{N-1}{r-1}(n-1)^{r}q^{\frac{N}{2}}=\frac{(n-1)^{r}}{(r-1)!}(N-1)\cdots(N-r+1)q^{\frac{N}{2}}.

Let
x:=\mid q^{1/2}T\mid=q^{-c_{0}}
. Note that

\frac{1}{(r-1)!}\sum_{N=n}^{\infty}(N-1)\cdots(N-r+1)x^{N}\leq\frac{1}{(r-1)!}\sum_{N=n}^{\infty}N^{r-1}x^{N}.

Then, by Lemma
3.1
, we have

(3.3)

\frac{1}{(r-1)!}\sum_{N=n}^{\infty}N^{r-1}x^{N}=\frac{1}{(r-1)!}x^{n}\Phi(x,-(r-1),n)\leq\frac{(3n(r-1))^{r-1}}{(1-x)^{r}}x^{n}.

Hence, from (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we bound the tail of (
3.1
) by

(3.4)

\sum_{N=n}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(N_{1},\cdots,N_{r})\\
N_{1}+\cdots+N_{r}=N\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}c_{N_{i}}(\chi)\right||T|^{N}\leq(n-1)^{r}\left(\frac{(3n(r-1))^{r-1}}{(1-q^{-c_{0}})^{r}}\right)\left(q^{-c_{0}}\right)^{n}.

We now focus on the head of (
3.1
). Since
c_{N}(\chi)=(1/\log(q))\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{N}}\Lambda(f)\chi(f)
by
[
28
]
*p.42, first displayed equation, then

(3.5)

\sum_{N=r}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(N_{1},\cdots,N_{r})\\
N_{1}+\cdots+N_{r}=N\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}c_{N_{i}}(\chi)\right)T^{N}=\frac{1}{\log^{r}(q)}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq n-1}}\Lambda_{r}(f)\chi(f)T^{d_{f}}.

Observe that

T\frac{d\left(\log(\mathcal{L}(T,\chi))\right)}{dT}=-\frac{1}{\log(q)}\frac{L^{\prime}(s,\chi)}{L(s,\chi)}.

Hence, substituting (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) in (
3.1
) gives

\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(s,\chi)}{L(s,\chi)}\right)^{r}=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq n-1}}\frac{\Lambda_{r}(f)\chi(f)}{N(f)^{s}}+O\left(\frac{(n-1)^{r}(3n(r-1))^{r-1}\left(q^{-c_{0}}\right)^{n}\log^{r}(q)}{(1-q^{-c_{0}})^{r}}\right).

Now, for
n\geq\max\{1/(1-q^{-c_{0}}),3r\}
, we have

\left|\frac{(n-1)^{r}(3n(r-1))^{r-1}\left(q^{-c_{0}}\right)^{n}\log^{r}(q)}{(1-q^{-c_{0}})^{r}}\right|\ll_{c_{0}}\frac{\log^{r}(q)(3r)^{r}n^{3r}}{\left(q^{c_{0}}\right)^{n}}\ll_{c_{0}}\frac{\log^{r}(q)n^{4r}}{\left(q^{c_{0}}\right)^{n}}.

Hence, for
n\geq\max\{1/(1-q^{-c_{0}}),3r\}
, any
s\in\mathbb{C}
with fixed
\re(s)=1/2+c_{0}>1/2
satisfies

\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(s,\chi)}{L(s,\chi)}\right)^{r}=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq n-1}}\frac{\Lambda_{r}(f)\chi(f)}{N(f)^{s}}+O\left(\frac{\log^{4r}(q^{n})}{q^{nc_{0}}}\right).

We conclude that

\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(s,\chi)}{L(s,\chi)}\right)^{r}=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq n-1}}\frac{\Lambda_{r}(f)\chi(f)}{N(f)^{s}}+O\left((q^{n})^{-c_{0}+\delta}\right)

because
\log^{4r}(q^{n})/(q^{nc_{0}})\leq(q^{n})^{-c_{0}+\delta}
if and only if
r\leq(\delta/4)\log(q^{n})/(\log(\log(q^{n})))
.

∎

Next, we compute integral moments uniformly in the range of
r
in Proposition
3.2
with respect to some parameters (For a number field analogue, see
[
21
]
* Theorem 2.1.).

Proposition 3.3
.

For a real number
0<\delta<1/2
and integer
1\leq r\leq\delta\log(q^{n})/(4\log(\log(q^{n})))
,

(3.6)

\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{H}_{n}}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\chi_{D})}{L(1,\chi_{D})}\right)^{r}=\sum_{f}\frac{\Lambda_{r}(f^{2})}{N(f)^{2}}\prod_{P|f}\left(\frac{N(P)}{N(P)+1}\right)+O_{q,\delta}\left((q^{n})^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}\right).

Proof.

From Proposition
3.2
with
c_{0}=1/2
, we have

(3.7)

\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{H}_{n}}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\chi_{D})}{L(1,\chi_{D})}\right)^{r}=\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{H}_{n}}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq n-1}}\frac{\Lambda_{r}(f)\chi_{D}(f)}{N(f)}+O\left((q^{n})^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}\right).

We now calculate the main term on the right-hand side of (
3.7
), beginning with the contribution of the square terms. By Lemma
2.3
(i), we have

(3.8)

\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{H}_{n}}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq n-1},\\
f\text{ square}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Lambda_{r}(f)\chi_{D}(f)}{N(f)}=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq\frac{n-1}{2}}}\frac{\Lambda_{r}(f^{2})}{N(f)^{2}}\prod_{P|f}\left(\frac{N(P)}{N(P)+1}\right)+O_{q}\left(\frac{1}{(\#\mathcal{H}_{n})^{1/2}}\right).

Because
2^{r}\log^{r}(x)/x^{1/2}
decreases for real
x\geq e^{2r}
and
\Lambda_{r}(f^{2})\leq 2^{r}\log^{r}(N(f))
, then

(3.9)

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{>\frac{n-1}{2}}}\frac{\Lambda_{r}(f^{2})}{N(f)^{2}}\prod_{P|f}\left(\frac{N(P)}{N(P)+1}\right)\ll_{q}\frac{\log^{r}(q^{n-1})}{q^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\ll_{q}\left(q^{n}\right)^{\frac{\delta}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}.

Next, we analyze the effect of non-square terms on the right-hand side of (
3.7
). As

\Lambda_{r}(f)\leq\log^{r}(q^{n})\leq(q^{n})^{\frac{\delta}{4}}
, then for
\varepsilon>0
, we have, by (
2.8
) and Lemmas
2.3
(ii) and
2.1
,

(3.10)

\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{H}_{n}}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq n-1},\\
f\text{ non-square}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Lambda_{r}(f)\chi_{D}(f)}{N(f)}\ll_{\varepsilon,q}(q^{n})^{\frac{\delta}{4}-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

Employing (
2.2
), (
3.8
), (
3.9
), and (
3.10
) into (
3.7
) yields

\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{H}_{n}}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\chi_{D})}{L(1,\chi_{D})}\right)^{r}=\sum_{f}\frac{\Lambda_{r}(f^{2})}{N(f)^{2}}\prod_{P|f}\left(\frac{N(P)}{N(P)+1}\right)+O_{\varepsilon,q}\left((q^{n})^{\frac{\delta}{4}-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}+(q^{n})^{\delta-\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Choosing any
0<\varepsilon<3\delta/4
yields the result.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

For any fixed
r\geq 1
, we have

\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{H}_{n}}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\chi_{D})}{L(1,\chi_{D})}\right)^{r}\ll_{q,r}1.

Proof.

It is enough to note that the main term of (
3.6
) is bounded from the above by

\sum_{f}\frac{\Lambda_{r}(f^{2})}{N(f)^{2}}\leq\sum_{f}\frac{2^{r}\log^{r}(N(f))}{N(f)^{2}}=2^{r}\log^{r}(q)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{m^{r}}{q^{m}}\ll_{q,r}1.

∎

We next compute the Laplace transform of
-L^{\prime}(1,\chi_{D})/L(1,\chi_{D})
. This will play a key part in Proposition
5.2
as a step towards establishing a limiting distribution problem.

Proposition 3.5
.

There is a real
m_{q,\delta}>0
such that for
s\in\mathbb{C}
with

|s|\leq m_{q,\delta}\frac{\log(q^{n})}{\log^{2}(\log(q^{n}))},

we have

\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{H}_{n}}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\exp\left(s\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\chi_{D})}{L(1,\chi_{D})}\right)\right)=\sum_{r=0}^{N}\frac{s^{r}}{r!}\sum_{f}\frac{\Lambda_{r}(f^{2})}{N(f)^{2}}\prod_{P|f}\left(\frac{N(P)}{N(P)+1}\right)+O_{\delta,q}\left((q^{n})^{\frac{-\delta}{4\log(\log(q^{n}))}}\right),

where
N:=\left\lfloor\delta\log(q^{n})/(4\log(\log(q^{n})))\right\rfloor
and
0<\delta<1/2
.

Proof.

Consider the Maclaurin expansion

(3.11)

\exp\left(s\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\chi_{D})}{L(1,\chi_{D})}\right)\right)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{r}}{r!}\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\chi_{D})}{L(1,\chi_{D})}\right)^{r}.

From Stirling’s approximation formula, we have
1/r!<(e/r)^{r}
and by Lemma
2.2
, there is a constant
C_{q}>0
such that
|L^{\prime}(1,\chi_{D})/L(1,\chi)|\leq C_{q}\log(\log(q^{n})
. Combining these facts with the assumption
r>N
yields

E_{1}:=\sum_{r=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{s^{r}}{r!}\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\chi_{D})}{L(1,\chi_{D})}\right)^{r}\leq\sum_{r=N+1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{C_{q}e|s|\log(\log(q^{n}))}{N}\right)^{r}.

Fix any real number
0<m_{q,\delta}\leq\delta/(8e^{2}C_{q})
. Since
1/N\leq 8\log(\log(q^{n}))/(\delta\log(q^{n}))
, then for any
s\in\mathbb{C}
satisfying
|s|\leq m_{q,\delta}\log(q^{n})/\log^{2}(\log(q^{n}))
we have

(3.12)

E_{1}\leq\sum_{r=N+1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{eC_{q}m_{q,\delta}}{N}\frac{\log(q^{n})}{\log(\log(q^{n}))}\right)^{r}\leq\sum_{r=N+1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{8eC_{q}m_{q,\delta}}{\delta}\right)^{r}\leq\left(\frac{8eC_{q}m_{q,\delta}}{\delta}\right)^{N}\leq e^{-N}.

It follows from (
3.12
) and Proposition
3.3
that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{H}_{n}}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\exp\left(s\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\chi_{D})}{L(1,\chi_{D})}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{N}\frac{s^{r}}{r!}\sum_{f}\frac{\Lambda_{r}(f^{2})}{N(f)^{2}}\prod_{P|f}\left(\frac{N(P)}{N(P)+1}\right)

\displaystyle+O_{q,\delta}\left(\sum_{r=0}^{N}\frac{|s|^{r}}{r!}(q^{n})^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}\right)+O\left((q^{n})^{\frac{-\delta}{4\log(\log(q^{n}))}}\right),

which concludes the proof.
∎

4.
Probabilistic Model

Let
(\mathbb{X}_{P})_{P\in\mathcal{P}}
be a sequence of independent random variables valued in
\{-1,0,1\}
with probabilities

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{X}_{P}=a\right)=\begin{cases}\frac{N(P)}{2(N(P)+1)}&\text{ if }a=-1,\\
\frac{1}{N(P)+1}&\text{ if }a=0,\\
\frac{N(P)}{2(N(P)+1)}&\text{ if }a=1.\\
\end{cases}

We extend multiplicatively the above sequence to all of
\mathcal{M}
by setting
\mathbb{X}_{f}:=\prod_{P|f}\mathbb{X}_{P}^{\ord_{P}(f)}
for each
f=\prod_{P|f}P^{\ord_{P}(f)}
in
\mathcal{M}
, where
\ord_{P}(f)
is the
P
-adic valuation of
f
. Observe that for
f\in\mathcal{M}
we have

(4.1)

\Esp\left(\mathbb{X}_{f}\right)=\prod_{P|f}\Esp\left(\mathbb{X}_{P}^{\ord_{P}(f)}\right)=\begin{cases}\prod_{P|f}\left(\frac{N(P)}{N(P)+1}\right)&\text{ if }f\text{ is a square in }\mathbb{F}_{q}[t],\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Lemma 4.1
.

The random series

-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\mathbb{X})}{L(1,\mathbb{X})}:=\sum_{f}\frac{\Lambda(f)}{N(f)}\mathbb{X}_{f}\text{ and }\sum_{P}\frac{\log(N(P))\mathbb{X}_{P}}{N(P)-\mathbb{X}_{P}}

are almost surely convergent. Moreover, they are almost surely equal.

Proof.

Over
\re(s)>1
, the random series
\sum_{f}\frac{\Lambda(f)}{N(f)^{s}}\mathbb{X}_{f}
and
\sum_{P}\frac{\log(N(P))}{N(P)^{s}-\mathbb{X}_{P}}
converge absolutely, and we can write

(4.2)

\sum_{f}\frac{\Lambda(f)}{N(f)^{s}}\mathbb{X}_{f}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{P}\frac{\log(N(P))}{N(P)^{ns}}\mathbb{X}_{P}^{n}=\sum_{P}\frac{\log(N(P))\mathbb{X}}{N(P)^{s}-\mathbb{X}_{P}}.

For
\re(s)>1/2
and
P\in\mathcal{P}
, define the random variables
\mathbb{Y}_{P,s}:=\frac{\log(N(P))}{N(P)^{s}}\mathbb{X}_{P}
and
\mathbb{W}_{P,s}:=\frac{\log(N(P))\mathbb{X}_{P}}{N(P)^{s}-\mathbb{X}_{P}}
.

By Kolmogorov Theorem (see
[
18
]
*Theorem B.10.1) the series
\sum_{P}\mathbb{Y}_{P,s}
and
\sum_{P}\mathbb{W}_{P,s}
converge almost surely.

Next, for
\re(s)>1
, the random series identities

(4.3)

\sum_{P}\frac{\log(N(P))}{N(P)^{s}-\mathbb{X}_{P}}=\sum_{f}\frac{\Lambda(f)}{N(f)^{s}}\mathbb{X}_{f}=\sum_{P}\frac{\log(N(P))}{N(P)^{s}}\mathbb{X}_{P}+O(1)

hold. Since for a fixed
1/2<\tau<1
the random series in (
4.3
) are almost surely convergent, then by

[
kowalski_exponential_2021
]
*Lemma A.4.1, they define almost surely analytic functions on the half-plane
\re(s)>\tau
. Hence, (
4.3
) extends to
\re(s)>\tau
, and thus the claimed assertion holds.
∎

5.
Comparing Laplace Transforms

We now obtain a uniform bound on the expected value of the integral moments of
-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\mathbb{X})}{L(1,\mathbb{X})}
. It is the exact analogue of the number field version
[
21
]
*Proposition 3.1.

Proposition 5.1
.

There is a constant
c_{q}>0
, such that for all integers
r\geq 8
we have

(5.1)

\Esp\left(\left|-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\mathbb{X})}{L(1,\mathbb{X})}\right|^{r}\right)\leq c_{q}^{r}\log^{r}(r).

Proof.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer to be chosen. Employing Minkowski’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\Esp\left(\left|-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\mathbb{X})}{L(1,\mathbb{X})}\right|^{r}\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leq\Esp\left(\left|\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq n}}\frac{\Lambda(f)}{N(f)}\mathbb{X}_{f}\right|^{r}\right)^{1/r}+\Esp\left(\left|\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{>n}}\frac{\Lambda(f)}{N(f)}\mathbb{X}_{f}\right|^{r}\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq n}}\frac{\Lambda(f)}{N(f)}\Esp\left(\left|\mathbb{X}_{f}\right|^{r}\right)^{1/r}+\Esp\left(\left|\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{>n}}\frac{\Lambda(f)}{N(f)}\mathbb{X}_{f}\right|^{r}\right)^{1/r}.

By (
2.9
) we conclude that

(5.2)

\Esp\left(\left|-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\mathbb{X})}{L(1,\mathbb{X})}\right|^{r}\right)^{1/r}\ll_{q}\log(q^{n})+\Esp\left(\left|\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{>n}}\frac{\Lambda(f)}{N(f)}\mathbb{X}_{f}\right|^{r}\right)^{1/r}.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives

(5.3)

\Esp\left(\left|\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{>n}}\frac{\Lambda(f)}{N(f)}\mathbb{X}_{f}\right|^{r}\right)\leq\Esp\left(\left(\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{>n}}\frac{\Lambda(f)}{N(f)}\mathbb{X}_{f}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\Esp\left(\left(\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{>n}}\frac{\Lambda(f)}{N(f)}\mathbb{X}_{f}\right)^{2(r-1)}\right)^{1/2}.

Let

\Lambda_{r,n}(f):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f_{1},\cdots,f_{r}\in\mathcal{M}_{>n}\\
f_{1}\cdots f_{r}=f\end{subarray}}\Lambda(f_{1})\cdots\Lambda(f_{r}).

Using (
4.1
) and
\Lambda_{r,n}(f)\leq\Lambda_{r}(f)\leq\log^{r}(f)
, we have

\Esp\left(\left(\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{>n}}\frac{\Lambda(f)}{N(f)}\mathbb{X}_{f}\right)^{2m}\right)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{>mn}}\frac{(\Lambda_{2m,n}(f^{2}))^{r}}{N(f)^{2}}\Esp(\mathbb{X}_{f^{2}})\leq\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{>mn}}\frac{2^{2m}\log^{2m}(N(f))}{N(f)^{2}}.

Since
f(x)=2^{2m}\log^{2m}(x)/x^{1/2}
is decreasing for
x\geq e^{4m}
, then
for
q^{n}\geq e^{4}
, we have

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{>mn}}\frac{2^{2m}\log^{2m}(N(f))}{N(f)^{2}}\leq\frac{(2m)^{2m}\log^{2m}(q^{n})}{(q^{n})^{m/2}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{>mn}}\frac{1}{N(f)^{3/2}}=\frac{(2m)^{2m}\log^{2m}(q^{n})}{(q^{1/2}-1)(q^{n})^{m}}.

Hence,

\Esp\left(\left(\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{>n}}\frac{\Lambda(f)}{N(f)}\mathbb{X}_{f}\right)^{2m}\right)\ll_{q}\frac{(2m)^{2m}\log^{2m}(q^{n})}{(q^{n})^{m}}.

Employing this estimate in (
5.3
), for
m=1
and
m=r-1
, yields

(5.4)

\Esp\left(\left|\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{>n}}\frac{\Lambda(f)}{N(f)}\mathbb{X}_{f}\right|^{r}\right)^{1/r}\ll_{q}\frac{r\log(q^{n})}{(q^{n})^{1/2}}.

Choosing
q^{n}\geq r^{2}
in (
5.4
) and substituting this estimate in (
5.2
) gives (
5.1
).
∎

We next compare the Laplace transforms of
-L^{\prime}(1,\chi_{D})/L(1,\chi_{D})
and
-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\mathbb{X})}{L(1,\mathbb{X})}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
0<\delta<1/2
be a real number. There is a constant
M_{q,\delta}>0
such that for all
s\in\mathbb{C}
with

|s|\leq M_{q,\delta}\frac{\log(q^{n})}{\log^{2}(\log(q^{n}))},

we have

\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{H}_{n}}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\exp\left(s\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\chi_{D})}{L(1,\chi_{D})}\right)\right)=\Esp\left(\exp\left(s\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\mathbb{X})}{L(1,\mathbb{X})}\right)\right)\right)+O_{q,\delta}\left((q^{n})^{\frac{-\delta}{4\log(\log(q^{n}))}}\right),

where
N:=\left\lfloor\delta\log(q^{n})/(4\log(\log(q^{n})))\right\rfloor
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.1
, the inequality
1/r!<(e/r)^{r}
, monotonicity of
f(r)=\log(r)/r
, and
r>N
, we have

(5.5)

\sum_{r=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{|s|^{r}}{r!}\Esp\left(\left|-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\mathbb{X})}{L(1,\mathbb{X})}\right|^{r}\right)\leq\sum_{r=N+1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{ec_{q}\log(N)|s|}{N}\right)^{r}.

We now choose any real
0<M_{q,\delta}\leq\delta/(16e^{2}c_{q}\log(\delta))
. Since
N\geq\delta\log(q^{n}))/(8\log(\log(q^{n})))
, then for any
s\in\mathbb{C}
satisfying
|s|\leq M_{q,\delta}\log(q^{n})/(\log^{2}(\log(q^{n}))
we have, for large
n
,

(5.6)

\frac{ec_{q}\log(N)|s|}{N}\leq\frac{8ec_{q}M_{q,\delta}}{\delta}\left(\frac{\log(\delta)}{\delta\log(\log(q^{n}))}+\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\leq e^{-1}.

Now, employing (
5.6
) in (
5.5
), gives

(5.7)

\left|\Esp\left(\sum_{r=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r!}\cdot\left(s\cdot\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\mathbb{X})}{L(1,\mathbb{X})}\right)\right)^{r}\right)\right|\leq e^{-N}.

From the Maclaurin expansion of the exponential function, the linearity of
E
, (
4.1
) and (
5.7
) we have

\displaystyle\Esp\left(\exp\left(s\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\mathbb{X})}{L(1,\mathbb{X})}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{N}\frac{s^{r}}{r!}\sum_{f}\frac{\Lambda_{r}(f^{2})}{N(f)^{2}}\prod_{P|f}\left(\frac{N(P)}{N(P)+1}\right)+O\left((q^{n})^{\frac{-\delta}{4\log(\log(q^{n}))}}\right).

This identity together with Proposition
3.5
imply the result.
∎

6.
Exponential Decay

The characteristic function
\Phi_{\mathrm{rand}}
of
-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\mathbb{X})}{L(1,\mathbb{X})}
is given for
u\in\mathbb{R}
by

(6.1)

\Phi_{\mathrm{rand}}(u):=\Esp\left(\exp\left(iu\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\mathbb{X})}{L(1,\mathbb{X})}\right)\right)\right).

Our next result implies that
\Phi_{\mathrm{rand}}(u)
decays exponentially as
|u|
goes to infinity. Hence, the distribution function
F_{\mathrm{rand}}
of
-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\mathbb{X})}{L(1,\mathbb{X})}
has a density function
M_{\mathrm{rand}}
, given by the Fourier inversion formula

M_{\mathrm{rand}}(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp(-iyu)\Phi_{\mathrm{rand}}(u)dy,

that is smooth (see
[
12
]
*Theorem 8.22 (d)) and

(6.2)

F_{\mathrm{rand}}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}M_{\mathrm{rand}}(y)dy

with
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}F_{\mathrm{rand}}^{\prime}(x)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}M_{\mathrm{rand}}(x)
finite, where
F^{\prime}_{\mathrm{rand}}
is the derivative of
F_{\mathrm{rand}}
.

Proposition 6.1
.

For
0<\varepsilon<1
, there is a real
C_{q,\varepsilon}>0
such that for large
|u|
we have

\left|\Phi_{\mathrm{rand}}(u)\right|\leq\exp\left(-C_{q,\varepsilon}|u|^{1-\varepsilon}\right).

Proof.

Since the sequence
(\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq m}}\log(N(P))\mathbb{X}_{P}/(N(P)-\mathbb{X}_{P}))_{m\geq 1}
converges almost surely, thus converges weakly
[
7
]
*Theorem 25.2, to
-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\mathbb{X})}{L(1,\mathbb{X})}
. As
\exp(iu)
is continuous and bounded, then
[
7
]
*Theorem 25.8 and the independence of
\{\log(N(P))\mathbb{X}_{P}/(N(P)-\mathbb{X}_{P}):P\in\mathcal{P}\}
give

\Phi_{\mathrm{rand}}(u)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\Esp\left(\exp\left(iu\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq m}}\frac{\log(N(P))\mathbb{X}_{P}}{N(P)-\mathbb{X}_{P}}\right)\right)=\prod_{P}\Esp\left(\exp\left(iu\frac{\log(N(P))\mathbb{X}_{P}}{N(P)-\mathbb{X}_{P}}\right)\right).

Each factor
M_{P}(u)
of the above product equals

(6.3)

M_{P}(u)=\frac{1}{N(P)+1}+\frac{N(P)}{2(N(P)+1)}\left(\exp\left(-iu\frac{\log(N(P))}{N(P)+1}\right)+\exp\left(iu\frac{\log(N(P))}{N(P)-1}\right)\right).

Using
(N(P)\pm 1)^{-1}-N(P)^{-1}\ll N(P)^{-2}
and
|\exp(ib)-\exp(ia)|\leq|b-a|
in (
6.3
) yields

(6.4)

M_{P}(u)=\frac{1}{N(P)+1}+\frac{N(P)}{N(P)+1}\cos\left(u\frac{\log(N(P))}{N(P)}\right)+O\left(\frac{|u|\log(N(P))}{N(P)^{2}}\right).

From (
6.3
),
|M_{P}(u)|\leq 1
for all
P\in\mathcal{P}
.
Let
\theta>0
be such that if
N(P)>\theta
then
|u|\log(N(P))/N(P)<1
. By taking the Maclaurin expansion of the cosine function and using
\log(1-x)\leq-x
for
x<1
, we get from (
6.4
):

(6.5)

\displaystyle\left|\Phi_{\mathrm{rand}}(u)\right|\leq\prod_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{>\theta}}\left|M_{P}(u)\right|\leq\exp\biggl(

\displaystyle-|u|^{2}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{>\theta}}\frac{\log^{2}(N(P))}{2N(P)(N(P)+1)}

\displaystyle+O\left(|u|^{4}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{>\theta}}\frac{\log^{4}(N(P))}{N(P)^{4}}\ \right)+O\left.\left(|u|\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{>\theta}}\frac{\log(N(P))}{N(P)^{2}}\right)\right).

Next, we estimate the three summands in (
6.5
). Let
N_{\theta}
be the smallest integer
N>0
satisfying
q^{N}>\theta
. Using (
2.3
), we have

(6.6)

\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{>\theta}}\frac{\log^{2}(N(P))}{2N(P)(N(P)+1)}=\frac{\log^{2}(q)}{2}\left(\sum_{m=N_{\theta}}^{\infty}\left(\frac{m}{q^{m}+1}+O\left(\frac{m}{(q^{m})^{1/2}(q^{m}+1)}\right)\right)\right).

Observe that there is a constant
\alpha_{q}>0
such that

(6.7)

\alpha_{q}\sum_{m=N_{\theta}}^{\infty}\frac{m}{q^{m}}\leq\sum_{m=N_{\theta}}^{\infty}\frac{m}{q^{m}+1}\leq\sum_{m=N_{\theta}}^{\infty}\frac{m}{q^{m}}.

Combining (
6.6
) and (
6.7
) with the fact that
q^{N_{\theta}}>\theta
implies that

(6.8)

\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{>\theta}}\frac{\log^{2}(N(P))}{2N(P)(N(P)+1)}\asymp_{q}\frac{\log(\theta)}{\theta}.

Using analogous arguments, we find

(6.9)

\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{>\theta}}\frac{\log^{4}(N(P))}{N(P)^{4}}=\log^{4}(q)\sum_{m=N_{\theta}}^{\infty}\frac{m^{4}}{q^{4m}}\pi_{q}(m)=O_{q}\left(\frac{\log^{3}(\theta)}{\theta^{3}}\right)

and

(6.10)

For each integer

, define an arithmetic function

by setting

for each

. To

by setting for each

which is a distribution function. Its characteristic function

is defined for all

by

For each
D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we set
\psi_{f_{n}(D)}(x)
to be

if

and

otherwise. We have

\varphi_{n}(u)=\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{H}_{n}}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp(iux)d\left(\psi_{f_{n}(D)}(x)\right)=\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{H}_{n}}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\exp\left(iu\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\chi_{D})}{L(1,\chi_{D})}\right)\right).

Recall that the random variable
-\frac{L^{\prime}(1,\mathbb{X})}{L(1,\mathbb{X})}
has distribution function
F_{\mathrm{rand}}
and characteristic function
\Phi_{\mathrm{rand}}
. We now obtain an upper bound on the discrepancy between the distribution of

and of its random model.

Proposition 7.1
.

We have

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|F_{n}(x)-F_{\mathrm{rand}}(x)\right|\ll_{q}\frac{\log^{2}(\log(q^{n}))}{\log(q^{n})}.

Proof.

For any real
R>0
, Berry-Esseen inequality
[
22
]
*p.297, A. Basic Inequality gives

(7.1)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|F_{n}(x)-F_{\mathrm{rand}}(x)\right|\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-R}^{R}\left|\frac{\varphi_{n}(u)-\Phi_{\mathrm{rand}}(u)}{u}\right|du+\frac{24}{\pi}\frac{\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}F_{\mathrm{rand}}^{\prime}(x)}{R}.

Fix
0<\delta_{0}<1/2
and let

be the constant from Proposition
5.2
. For
R=M_{q,\delta_{0}}\log(q^{n})/\log^{2}(\log(q^{n}))
,
R_{0}=1/\log(q^{n})
and

large enough, we have

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{[-R,R]\smallsetminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}\left|\frac{\varphi_{n}(u)-\Phi_{\mathrm{rand}}(u)}{u}\right|du

\displaystyle\ll\log\left(\frac{R}{R_{0}}\right)\sup\limits_{u\in[-R,R]}\left|\varphi_{n}(u)-\Phi_{\mathrm{rand}}(u)\right|

(7.2)

\displaystyle\ll_{q}(q^{n})^{-\frac{\delta}{4\log(\log(q^{n}))}}\log(\log(q^{n})).

Next, since

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(e^{ixu}-1)dF_{n}(x)-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(e^{ixu}-1)dF_{\mathrm{rand}}(x)

\displaystyle\ll|u|\left(\left(\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}x^{2}dF_{n}(x)\right)^{1/2}+\left(\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}x^{2}dF_{\mathrm{rand}}(x)\right)^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle\ll|u|,

where the last estimate follows from the bound of Corollary

(7.3)

\int_{-R_{0}}^{R_{0}}\left|\frac{\varphi_{n}(u)-\Phi_{\mathrm{rand}}(u)}{u}\right|du\ll\frac{1}{\log(q^{n})}.

Since

on

for all

of even and odd degrees. We consider them ordered with the ordering coming from

. We next prove a technical lemma towards showing that

is positive.

Lemma 7.2
.

For

, define a function

by
h_{P}(x)=(\log{N(P)}x)/(N(P)-x)
with

. For fixed reals
\varepsilon>0
and

, there is a
\{-1,0,1\}
-valued sequence
(x_{P})_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{O}}
such that

(7.4)

\left|\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{O}}h_{P}(x_{P})-\alpha\right|<\varepsilon.

Proof.

If
|\alpha|<\varepsilon
, the zero sequence satisfies (
7.4
). Now suppose
|\alpha|\geq\varepsilon
and write
\alpha=\omega\beta
with
\omega\in\{-1,1\}
and
\beta>0
. Let
(P_{i})_{i\geq 1}
be the elements of
\mathcal{P}_{O}
with the induced order from

. Since
\omega h_{P_{i}}(\omega)>0
for all

and the sequence

converges to

, there is an integer
m\geq 1
such that for

, we have

. Now observe that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\omega h_{P_{i}}(\omega)
diverges to
\infty
. So, there is an integer
n>m
such that
\sum_{i=m}^{n-1}\omega h_{P_{i}}(\omega)\leq\beta-\varepsilon/2
and

. Since

, we have

\left|\sum_{i=m}^{n}h_{P_{i}}(\omega)-\alpha\right|=\left|\sum_{i=m}^{n}\omega h_{P_{i}}(\omega)-\beta\right|<\varepsilon.

To satisfy (

and

otherwise.
∎

Proposition 7.3
.

The density function
M_{\mathrm{rand}}
in (

) is positive.

Proof.

For
\nu\in\{0,1\}
and integer

, let

Let

Now, for all

, set

as in Lemma

, and

We can write (

) as

\Phi_{\mathrm{rand}}(u)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\prod_{j\leq n}\Esp\left(\exp(iu\mathbb{W}_{P_{j}})\right).

Since

and
\Phi_{\mathrm{rand}}
is continuous at

, there is a real number
\eta>0
such that for each

and so

Hence, by
[
31
]
*Theorem 2.7, the sequence

of convolution products converges weakly to

. Similarly, for

in
\mathcal{P}_{E}
, respectively in

, the sequence

of convolution products converges weakly to a distribution function

, respectively to a distribution function

. Then we get

. As in the proof of Proposition

,

and

have smooth density functions

and
M_{O}
. Moreover,

(7.5)

Now, by the Kolmogorov Theorem, the random series

(7.6)

\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{O}}\frac{\log(N(P))\mathbb{X}_{P}}{N(P)-\mathbb{X}_{P}}

converges almost surely. By Lemma

, the set

\left\{\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{O}}\frac{\log(N(P))x_{P}}{N(P)-x_{P}}<\infty:x_{P}\in\{-1,0,1\}\right\}

. Hence, from
[

]
*Proposition B.10.8, the support of (

) is

. Therefore, for

. In particular,

is not identically zero on any interval

. Since

on one of these intervals

) and the continuity of

Since

was arbitrary, then the function

is positive on

.
∎

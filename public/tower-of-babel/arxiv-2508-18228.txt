Improved Bounds for Radial Projections in the Plane
Marianna Csörnyei, D. M. Stull

Abstract
. We improve the best known lower bound for the dimension of radial projections of sets in the plane. We show that if X , Y X,Y are Borel sets in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} , X X and Y Y are not contained in the same line and dim H ( X ) > 0 \dim_{H}(X)>0 , then sup x ∈ X dim H ( π x ​ Y ) ≥ min ⁡ { ( dim H ( Y ) + dim H ( X ) ) / 2 , dim H ( Y ) , 1 } , \sup\limits_{x\in X}\dim_{H}(\pi_{x}Y)\geq\min\left\{(\dim_{H}(Y)+\dim_{H}(X))/2,\dim_{H}(Y),1\right\}, where π x ​ Y \pi_{x}Y is the radial projection of the set Y Y from the point x x .



1.
Introduction

Given a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the radial projection with respect to
x
is the function
\pi_{x}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{x\}\to\mathcal{S}^{1}
defined by

\pi_{x}y=\frac{y-x}{|x-y|}
.

Recently, there has been substantial progress in our understanding of how the Hausdorff dimension of a set is changed when projected radially onto a point
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
. In particular, we focus on the following problem. Let
X,Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be Borel sets. We would like to give sharp lower bounds on the quantity

\sup\limits_{x\in X}\dim_{H}(\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\})

in terms of the Hausdorff dimensions of
X
and
Y
.

Before highlighting recent progress on this problem, we note that improvements on the dimension of radial projections is closely related to the distance set problem in geometric measure theory. Indeed, most of the recent progress on the distance set problem has relied crucially on radial projections
[
20
,
8
,
21
,
4
,
5
]
.

In this paper we will be concerned with radial projections in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. There has also been exciting progress on this question in higher dimensions, e.g.,
[
18
,
2
]
.

Orponen, Shmerkin and Wang
[
17
]
proved the following theorem on the dimension of radial projections in the plane.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a Borel set which is not contained on any line. Then, for every Borel set
Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\sup\limits_{x\in X}\dim_{H}(\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\})\geq\min\{\dim_{H}(X),\dim_{H}(Y),1\}
.

This theorem is sharp whenever
\dim_{H}(Y)=\dim_{H}(X)
. In the same paper
[
17
]
, they also proved the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a non-empty Borel set. Then, for every Borel set
Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
, such that
\dim_{H}(Y)>1
,

\sup\limits_{x\in X}\dim_{H}(\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\})\geq\min\{\dim_{H}(X)+\dim_{H}(Y)-1,1\}
.

In this paper, we improve their lower bounds, for the case where
\dim_{H}(X)>0
. We prove that:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a Borel set such that
\dim_{H}(X)>0
. Let
Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a Borel set such that
X
and
Y
are not contained on the same line. Then,

(1)

\sup\limits_{x\in X}\dim_{H}(\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\})\geq\min\left\{(\dim_{H}(Y)+\dim_{H}(X))/2,\dim_{H}(Y),1\right\}.

We note that Theorem
1.3
also improves the result of Ren and Wang giving sharp bounds on the exceptional sets for orthogonal projections in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. They proved the following in
[
19
]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a Borel set. Then, for all
0\leq u\leq\min\{\dim_{H}(Y),1\}
, we have

\dim_{H}(\{\theta\in\mathcal{S}^{1}\mid\dim_{H}(p_{\theta}Y)<u\})\leq\max\{2u-\dim_{H}(Y),0\}
,

where
p_{\theta}
is the orthogonal projection map.

Equivalently, if
X\subseteq\ell
, for some line
\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
Y
is disjoint from
\ell
, and
\dim_{H}(X)>\max(2u-\dim_{H}(Y),0)
for
0\leq u\leq\min\{\dim_{H}(Y),1\}
, then

\sup_{x\in X}\dim_{H}(\pi_{x}Y)\geq u.

It is immediate to check that our Theorem
1.3
implies the same result, moreover, it implies Theorem
1.4

without using the additional assumption that
X
is contained in a line
.

Remark 1.5
.

An elementary calculation also shows that our lower estimate is always at least as large as the lower estimate in Theorem
1.1
, and it is strictly larger when
\dim_{H}(X)<\min\{\dim_{H}(Y),1\}
. Similarly, the estimate in (
1
) is always at least as large as the estimate in Theorem
1.2
, and it is strictly larger in the case when in Theorem
1.2
we have
\dim_{H}(X)+\dim_{H}(Y)-1<1
.

The main new ingredient of the proof of Theorem
1.3
is a surprising inequality bounding Kolmogorov complexity of a line, which may be of independent interest. In particular we show in Theorem
4.3
, that, if
X,Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
are compact sets and
\mu,\nu
are probability measures whose support is
X
and
Y
, respectively, and they satisfy a Frostman condition, then we can always find
x\in X
and
y\in Y
which satisfy the following for all sufficiently large
n
and
m\leq n
:

(2)

K^{A}_{m}(\ell)\lessapprox K^{A}_{m}(\ell\mid x)+K^{A}_{m,n}(\ell\mid y),

and

(3)

K^{A}_{n}(\ell)\lessapprox K^{A}_{n}(\ell\mid y)+K^{A,x}_{n}(\ell),

where
\ell
is the line containing
x
and
y
.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
follows by combining the above inequalities and the incident bound due to Ren and Wang
[
19
]
which was used in their solution to the Furstenberg set problem.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Discretized incidences

We will use the standard point-line duality in the plane.

Without loss of generality, we can assume in Theorem
1.3
that
X,Y\subset[0,1]^{2}
. Also, without loss of generality, it is enough to consider the directions of those lines joining points of
X
and
Y
whose dual is in
[0,1]^{2}
.

Let
\delta
be a dyadic scale. We denote the set of dyadic cubes in
[0,1]^{2}
of side length
\delta
by
\mathcal{D}_{\delta}
.
We call a set of lines a
\delta
-tube if it is the dual set of the points covered by a cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{\delta}
. We denote the set of
\delta
-tubes by
\mathcal{T}^{\delta}
.

We say that a
\delta
-cube
Q
intersects a
\delta
-tube
T
, if
T
contains a line that meets
Q
, and we denote this by
T\cap Q\neq\emptyset
.

In
[
19
]
Ren and Wang proved sharp bounds on the incidences between
\delta
-tubes and
\delta
-cubes, leading to a resolution of the Furstenberg set problem. We will use their work to prove our main theorem. They introduced the following definitions, and proved Theorem
2.1
below (see Theorem 4.1 in
[
19
]
).

For any bounded set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and dyadic scale
\delta
, denote

\mathcal{D}_{\delta}(A)=\{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{\delta}\mid Q\cap A\neq\emptyset\}

and

|A|_{\delta}=|\mathcal{D}_{\delta}(A)|
.

Let
s\in[0,d]
and
C>0
. A non-empty bounded set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called a
(\delta,s,C)
-set if it satisfies the following Frostman type condition:

(4)

|A\cap B(x,r)|_{\delta}\leq Cr^{s}|A|_{\delta}\;\;\;\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;r\in[\delta,1].

When
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{\delta}
, we say that
\mathcal{P}
is a
(\delta,s,C)
-set if the set of points in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
covered by the cubes in
\mathcal{P}
is a
(\delta,s,C)
-set.
A set of
\delta
-tubes
\mathcal{T}
is called a
(\delta,s,C)
-set if its dual is a
(\delta,s,C)
-set.

Theorem 2.1
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
,
s\in(0,1]
, and
t\in(0,2]
there exists
\eta>0
s.t. for every small enough
\delta>0
and for every
M\in\mathbb{N}
the following holds.

Suppose that
\mathcal{P}
is a
(\delta,t,\delta^{-\eta})
-set, and suppose that for each
Q\in\mathcal{P}
there is a
(\delta,s,\delta^{-\eta})
-set of tubes
T(Q)
such that each tube in
T(Q)
intersects
Q
, and
|T(Q)|\sim M
. Then for
\mathcal{T}:=\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal{P}}T(Q)
we have

(5)

|\mathcal{T}|\gtrsim(\log{\varepsilon}^{-1})^{-c}\delta^{-\min\{t,\frac{s+t}{2},1\}+\varepsilon}M

for some absolute constant
c
.

We will also use a result of Orponen, Shmerkin and Wang
[
17
]
on the existence of ‘thin tubes’. This was used in their paper establishing strong lower bounds on radial projections in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Recall that a probability measure
\mu
is said to satisfy the
s
-dimensional Frostman condition
, if
\mu(B(x,r))\leq Cr^{s}
for some fixed constant
C>0
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and all
r>0
.

In
[
17
]
(Corollary 2.21) the authors proved the following statement:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
s\in(0,1]
and let
\mu,\nu
be probability measures in the plane that satisfy the
s
-dimensional Frostman condition.
Assume
\mu(\ell)\nu(\ell)<1
for every line
\ell
. Then for every
0\leq\sigma<s
there exists a Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
of positive
\mu\times\nu
measure and a constant
C>0
such that the following holds.

For every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
r>0
and for every rectangle
x\in R\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
of width
r
,

\nu(\{y\in R\mid(x,y)\in E\})\leq Cr^{\sigma}.

2.2.
Algorithmic methods

Our proof will use the algorithmic methods which have recently been applied to geometric measure theory
[
1
,
3
,
13
,
12
,
11
]
. We will assume basic knowledge about the fundamental definitions and theorems of Kolmogorov complexity, effective dimension and the use in geometric measure theory. For a more detailed introduction for mathematicians working in geometric measure theory who are unfamiliar with the algorithmic methods, the authors are in the process of writing lecture notes on this topic. These will not assume that the reader has any apriori background knowledge in computability theory.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the Kolmogorov complexity of
x
at precision
n
is

K_{n}(x)=K(Q)
,

where
K(Q)
is the Kolmogorov complexity of the dyadic cube with side length
2^{-n}
containing
x
. We note that in the literature,
K_{n}(x)
is more commonly defined using rational balls, however the definitions are equivalent up to an additive
O(\log n)
factor, which can be safely ignored.

We frequently use the following notation. For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we will write

K_{n}(x)\lessapprox L

to mean that
K_{n}(x)\leq L+O(\log n)
. Similarly, we will write

K_{n}(x)\gtrapprox L

to mean that
K_{n}(x)\geq L+O(\log n)
. We write
K_{n}(x)\approx L
when
K_{n}(x)\lessapprox L
and
K_{n}(x)\gtrapprox L
. More generally, when we write formulas involving several Kolmogorov complexities, possibly at different precisions (e.g.
K_{n_{1}}(x)\lessapprox K_{n_{2}}(y)-K_{n_{3}}(z)
), by
\lessapprox
we mean that the inequality holds up to an error term
\sum_{i}O(\log n_{i})
, where we are summing for all the precisions
n_{i}
that appear (on either side of
\lessapprox
) in all the Kolmogorov complexities in our formula.

One of the most important results in Kolmogorov complexity is the
symmetry of information
. In
[
14
]
, Lutz and Stull proved that a version of this holds in Euclidean space, namely

K^{A}_{n,m}(x,y)\approx K^{A}_{n,m}(x|y)+K^{A}_{m}(y)

for every oracle
A
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}},y\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
and precisions
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
.

In this paper, the main application of Kolmogorov complexity is to define a notion of local dimension of points in Euclidean space, first defined by Lutz
[
9
]
. We use the Kolmogorov characterization proved by Mayordomo
[
15
]
. In particular, the effective Hausdorff dimension of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
relative to an oracle
A
is

\dim_{H}^{A}(x)=\liminf\frac{K^{A}_{n}(x)}{n}.

We are able to use effective methods in geometric measure theory because of the following characterization of the Hausdorff dimension, proved by Lutz and Lutz
[
10
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

For every
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(6)

\dim_{H}(E)=\min_{A}\sup_{x\in E}\dim_{H}^{A}(x).

We call an oracle
A
a Hausdorff oracle for
E
if it achieves the minimum in (
6
).

A set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
effectively compact relative to

A
if
K
is compact and the set of finite open covers of
K
by dyadic cubes is computably enumerable relative to
A
. We will use the fact that every compact set is effectively compact relative to some oracle. Hitchcock
[
7
]
and J. Lutz
[
9
]
proved that, if
K
is effectively compact relative to an oracle
A
, then
A
is a Hausdorff oracle for
K
. For the purposes of this paper, we conclude that, if
Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
is computably compact relative to
A
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then

(7)

\dim_{H}(\pi_{x}Y)=\sup\limits_{y\in Y}\dim^{A,x}_{H}(\pi_{x}y).

If
\mu
is a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we say that
\mu
is computable relative to an oracle
A
if, for every dyadic cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}
,
\mu(D)
is computable relative to
A
. We note that every Borel probability measure is computable relative to some oracle. If
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mu
is a probability measure whose support is contained in
K
and
\mu
is computable relative to an oracle
A
, then it was shown in
[
21
]
that, for every oracle
B
,

(8)

K^{A,B}_{n}(x)\gtrapprox K^{A}_{n}(x)

for
\mu
-almost every
x\in K
.

We will frequently use the well-known fact relating Kolmogorov complexity and enumerable sets. Suppose that
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{2^{-n}}
is enumerable, given information encoded into a binary string
w
. If
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
Q(x)\in\mathcal{P}
, where
Q(x)
is the dyadic cube containing
x
, then

(9)

K_{n}(x\mid w)\lessapprox\log|\mathcal{P}|.

An immediate consequence is that

(10)

K_{n}(x)\lessapprox\log|\mathcal{P}|+|w|,

where
|w|
denotes the length of
w
.

3.
Complexity of lines

We will need a slight modification of the definition of
(\delta,s,C)
-sets which is better suited for computability.

Definition 3.1
.

A set
A\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{2^{-n}}
is called a
dyadic
(2^{-n},s,C)
-set
if for each
m\leq n
and each
Q\in D_{2^{-m}}
,
Q
has at most
C2^{-sm}|A|
many subcubes in
A
.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that, if
A\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{2^{-n}}
is a dyadic
(2^{-n},s,C)
-set, then
A
is a
(2^{-n},s,C^{\prime})
-set, where
C^{\prime}
is a constant multiple of
C
.

Remark 3.3
.

We also note that, if
A\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{2^{-n}}
, and
s
and
C
are rational, then determining if
A
is a dyadic
(2^{-n},s,C)
-set is computable, given
n,s
and
C
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\dim_{H}(x)>t>0
, and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then for every sufficiently large
n
the following holds:

If
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{2^{-n}}
is enumerable given
w
, and
\mathcal{P}
contains the cube containing
x
, then it has a dyadic
(2^{-n},t,C)
subset
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
such that
C=2^{O({\varepsilon}n)+|w|}
and

|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|\geq 2^{K_{n}(x)-O({\varepsilon}^{-1}\log n)-|w|}
.

In particular, if
|w|=O(\log n)
, then we get
|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|\geq 2^{K_{n}(x)-O({\varepsilon}^{-1}\log n)}
and
C=2^{O({\varepsilon}n)}
.

Proof.

We define
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
to be the set of those cubes
Q
in
\mathcal{P}
for which

K(Q)\lessapprox K_{n}(x),\,\text{ and }\,K_{n,m}(Q\mid Q)\lessapprox K_{n,m}(x\mid x)

for every
m\leq n
which is an integer multiple of
\lfloor{\varepsilon}n\rfloor
.

Note that
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is enumerable given
w
and given each
K_{n,m}(x\mid x)
and
K_{n}(x)
. Moreover,
Q(x)\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, where
Q(x)
is the
2^{-n}
-dyadic cube containing
x
. Therefore,

\displaystyle K_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\lessapprox\log|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|+\log K_{n}(x)+\sum\limits_{m}\log K_{n,m}(x\mid x)+|w|

\displaystyle\leq\log|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|+O({\varepsilon}^{-1}\log n)+|w|.

Hence,

|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|\geq 2^{K_{n}(x)-O({\varepsilon}^{-1}\log n)-|w|}

as required. Moreover, since there are at most
2^{K_{n}(x)+O(\log n)}
many cubes
Q
such that
K(Q)\lessapprox K_{n}(x)
, we see that

|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|\leq 2^{K_{n}(x)+O(\log n)}.

Let
m\leq n
be an integer multiple of
\lfloor{\varepsilon}n\rfloor
. Now let
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{2^{-m}}
. By our definition of
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, if
Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is a subcube of
Q
, then
K_{n,m}(Q^{\prime}\mid Q)=K_{n,m}(Q^{\prime}\mid Q^{\prime})\lessapprox K_{n,m}(x\mid x)
. For any fixed
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{2^{-m}}
, the number of subcubes
Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{2^{-n}}
such that
K_{n,m}(Q^{\prime}\mid Q)\lessapprox K_{n,m}(x\mid x)
is
\lesssim 2^{K_{n,m}(x\mid x)+O(\log n)}
. Therefore, using symmetry of information, the number of subcubes is at most

2^{K_{n,m}(x\mid x)+O(\log n)}\leq 2^{K_{n}(x)-K_{m}(x)+O(\log n)}\leq|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|2^{-mt+O({\varepsilon}^{-1}\log n)+|w|}.

Since this holds for every
m
which is an integer multiple of
\lfloor{\varepsilon}n\rfloor
, and since between
m
and
m+\lfloor{\varepsilon}n\rfloor
the number of cubes can be multiplied by at most a factor
2^{O({\varepsilon}n)}
, the statement indeed follows with
C=2^{O({\varepsilon}n)+O({\varepsilon}^{-1}\log n)+|w|}=2^{O({\varepsilon}n)+|w|}
.

∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
x
be a point and
\ell
be a line in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
x\in\ell
, and let
\dim_{H}(x)>t>0
and
\dim_{H}^{x}(\ell)>s>0
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be sufficiently large. Then,

(11)

K_{n}(\ell)\geq K_{n}(\ell\mid x)+\min\{t,(s+t)/2,1\}n-O(\varepsilon n)-O_{{\varepsilon},s,t}(\log n),

where the indices
{\varepsilon},s,t
mean that the constant in the
O(\log n)
term depends on
{\varepsilon},s,t
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
t
,
s
and
{\varepsilon}
are rational.

Define the set

\mathcal{T}=\{T\in\mathcal{T}^{2^{-n}}\mid K(T)\lessapprox K_{n}(\ell)\},

and denote

n_{Q}=\#\{T\in\mathcal{T}\mid T\cap Q\neq\emptyset\}.

We note that
\mathcal{T}
is enumerable given
K_{n}(\ell)
, and that the
2^{-n}
-tube containing
\ell
is in
\mathcal{T}
. Therefore, we have

(12)

|\mathcal{T}|=2^{K_{n}(\ell)+O(\log n)}.

Also note that that
\mathcal{T}
is enumerable given
K_{n}(\ell)
, and therefore, for every
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{2^{-n}}
and for every
T\in\mathcal{T}
with
Q\cap T\neq{\emptyset}
we have
K_{n}(T\mid Q)\lessapprox\log n_{Q}+\log K_{n}(\ell)
. In particular,

K_{n}(\ell\mid x)\lessapprox\log n_{Q(x)}+\log K_{n}(\ell),

where
Q(x)\in\mathcal{D}_{2^{-n}}
is the dyadic cube containing
x
.

Define the set

\mathcal{P}_{1}=\{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{2^{-n}}\mid K(Q)\lessapprox K_{n}(x),n_{Q}\geq 2^{K_{n}(\ell\mid x)-O(\log n)}\}.

Then
Q(x)\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
, and
\mathcal{P}_{1}
is enumerable, given
n
,
K_{n}(\ell)
,
K_{n}(x)
and
K_{n}(\ell\mid x)
(all of which take at most
O(\log n)
bits).

We fix a rational
\eta_{0}
which will be specified later. Define the set
\mathcal{P}
to be the set of those
Q\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
that satisfy the following. There is a dyadic
(2^{-n},s,2^{O(\eta_{0}n)})
-set
\mathcal{T}(Q)\subseteq\mathcal{T}
with
|\mathcal{T}(Q)|\geq 2^{K_{n}(\ell\mid x)-O(\eta_{0}^{-1}\log n)}
such that
T\cap Q\neq\emptyset
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}(Q)
. The constants in the
O(\cdot)
terms will come from Proposition
3.4
.

The set
\mathcal{T}(Q(x))=\{T\in\mathcal{T}\mid T\cap Q(x)\neq\emptyset\}
is enumerable given
Q(x)
and
K_{n}(\ell)
, and contains the
2^{-n}
-tube containing
\ell
. Therefore, by the dual of Proposition
3.4
, applied to
\mathcal{T}(Q(x))
, we see that
\mathcal{T}(Q(x))
contains a subset
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
which is a dyadic
(2^{-n},s,2^{O(\eta_{0}n)})
-set and such that
|\mathcal{T}^{\prime}|\geq 2^{K_{n}(\ell\mid x)-O(\eta_{0}^{-1}\log n)}
. Therefore we see that
Q(x)\in\mathcal{P}
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{P}
is computable enumerable, given
s
,
\eta_{0}
,
K_{n}(x)
,
K_{n}(\ell)
and
K_{n}(\ell\mid x)
(recall Remark
3.3
, and that we assumed that our parameters are rational). Since of course
K(\eta_{0},s)=O(1)
, and the other inputs have length
O(\log n)
, therefore by applying Proposition
3.4
to
\mathcal{P}
, we obtain a set
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{P}
such that
|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|\geq 2^{K_{n}(x)-O(\eta_{0}^{-1}\log n)}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is a dyadic
(2^{-n},t,2^{O(\eta_{0}n)})
-set.

Now recall Remark
3.2
. To summarize,
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is a
(2^{-n},t,C^{\prime})
-set, for some fixed constant multiple of
2^{O(\eta_{0}n)}
. And for every
Q\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, there is a
(2^{-n},s,C^{\prime})
-set of tubes
\mathcal{T}(Q)
such that
|\mathcal{T}(Q)|=2^{K_{n}(\ell\mid x)-O(\eta_{0}^{-1}\log n)}
.

Now we choose
\eta_{0}
small enough so that we may apply Theorem
2.1
to
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
with parameters
s
,
t
and
{\varepsilon}
and
\eta:=(\log C^{\prime})/n\sim\eta_{0}
. Applying Theorem
2.1
yields

|\mathcal{T}|\gtrsim(\log{\varepsilon}^{-1})^{-c}2^{\min\{t,\frac{s+t}{2},1\}n-{\varepsilon}n}2^{K_{n}(\ell\mid x)-O(\eta^{-1}\log n)}
.

Therefore, using also (
12
), we showed that for every
{\varepsilon},s,t
there is an
\eta
such that

K_{n}(\ell)\gtrapprox\log|\mathcal{T}|\geq K_{n}(\ell\mid x)+\min\{t,\frac{s+t}{2},1\}n-O({\varepsilon}_{0}n)-O(\eta^{-1}\log n).

∎

We note that analogs of Proposition
3.4
and Theorem
3.5
using conditional complexities, instead of oracles, are true as well.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
{\varepsilon}>0
. Suppose that
n
is sufficiently large and let
t=\min\{\frac{K_{m}(x)}{m}\mid\varepsilon n\leq m\leq n\}
. Then the following holds.

If
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{2^{-n}}
is enumerable given
w
, and
\mathcal{P}
contains the cube containing
x
, then it has a dyadic
(2^{-n},t,C)
subset
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
such that
C=2^{O({\varepsilon}n)+|w|}
and

|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|\geq 2^{K_{n}(x)-O({\varepsilon}^{-1}\log n)-|w|}
.

In particular, if
|w|=O(\log n)
, then
|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|\geq 2^{K_{n}(x)-O({\varepsilon}^{-1}\log n)}
and
C=2^{O({\varepsilon}n)}
.

The proof of this proposition is nearly identical to Proposition
3.4
, and so we omit it. The only difference is that we are only considering the minimum of the ratio
K_{m}(x)/m

up to precision
n
instead of the limit inferior, i.e., the dimension.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and let
\ell
be a line in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
x\in\ell
. Let
{\varepsilon}>0
and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define
s_{n}=\min\{\frac{K_{m,n}(e\mid x)}{m}\mid\varepsilon n\leq m\leq n\}
. Let
\dim_{H}(x)>t>0
and let
\liminf_{n}s_{n}>s>0
. Let
n
be sufficiently large. Then,

(13)

K_{n}(\ell)\geq K_{n}(\ell\mid x)+\min\{t,\frac{s+t}{2},1\}n-O(\varepsilon n)-O_{{\varepsilon},s,t}(\log n).

Again, the proof of this is nearly identical to Theorem
3.5
.

4.
Proof of main theorem

We now prove Theorem
1.3
using the algorithmic results of the previous section. To do so, we will need to prove the existence of an appropriate oracle
A
and points satisfying the following properties. For a (suitably chosen) oracle
A
we need to find
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
e=\frac{y-x}{|x-y|}
that satisfy:

(C1)

\dim_{H}^{A}(x)>\sigma_{X},\dim_{H}^{A}(y)>\sigma_{Y}
;

(C2)

\dim_{H}^{A,x}(e)>0
;

(C3)

K^{A,x}_{n}(y)\gtrapprox K^{A}_{n}(y)
,

where the parameters
\sigma_{X}
and
\sigma_{Y}
we will specify later.

In
[
6
]
(Lemma 33), Fiedler and Stull used Theorem
2.2
to give sufficient conditions for the existence of points satisfying the above conditions.
A straight-forward modification of this result proves the following lemma. For completeness, we include the proof.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X,Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be compact sets. Let
\sigma_{X}<\dim_{H}(X)
and
\sigma_{Y}<\dim_{H}(Y)
. Let
\mu,\nu
be probability measures whose support is
X
and
Y
, respectively, and such that
\mu
and
\nu
satisfy the
\dim_{H}(X)
-dimensional and
\dim_{H}(Y)
-dimensional Frostman condition, respectively. Let
A
be an oracle relative to which
X
and
Y
are effectively compact and relative to which
\mu
and
\nu
are computable.

Assume
\mu(\ell)\nu(\ell)<1
for every line
\ell
and
\nu
is computable with respect to
A
. Then,

\dim_{H}\{x\in X:\exists y\in Y\text{ satisfying (C1)-(C3)}\}>\sigma_{X}
.

Proof.

Fix
\sigma<\sigma^{\prime}<\min\{\sigma_{X},\sigma_{Y},1\}
. Then
\mu
and
\nu
satisfy the
\sigma^{\prime}
-dimensional Frostman condition. Let
E
be the Borel set given by Theorem
2.2
, applied with
\sigma^{\prime}
. We denote the vertical sections of
E
by

E_{x}:=\{y\mid(x,y)\in E\}.

Since
(\mu\times\nu)(E)>0
, the set

X_{1}:=\{x\in X\mid\nu(E_{x})>0\}

has positive
\mu
-measure, and since
\mu
satisfies the
\dim_{H}(X)
-dimensional Frostman condition, therefore this implies that the dimension of
X_{1}
is
\dim_{H}(X)
. Since
\sigma_{X}<\dim_{H}(X)
, therefore

X_{2}:=\{x\in X_{1}\mid\dim_{H}^{A}(x)>\sigma_{X}\}

also has Hausdorff dimension
\dim_{H}(X)
.

Let
x
be any point in
X_{2}
. We know that
\nu
-a.e.
y
satisfies the condition in (C1), and since
\nu
is computable with respect to
A
, therefore using (
8
) we also know that
\nu
-a.e.
y
satisfies (C3). Let

E^{\prime}\subseteq E_{x}
be the set of points in
E_{x}
satisfying both (C1) and (C3). Then we have
\nu(E^{\prime})=\nu(E_{x})>0
.

By Theorem
2.2
, we have
\nu(R\cap E^{\prime})\leq Cr^{\sigma^{\prime}}
for all
r>0
and all rectangle
R
of width
r
containing
x
. Using very simple geometry, this, combined with Frostman’s lemma, implies that
\dim_{H}(\pi_{x}E^{\prime})\geq\sigma^{\prime}
. Then for every
\sigma<\sigma^{\prime\prime}<\sigma^{\prime}
, for most
e\in\pi_{x}E^{\prime}
(all
e\in\pi_{x}E^{\prime}
except for an at most
\sigma^{\prime\prime}
-dimensional subset) we have
\dim_{H}^{A,x}(e)\geq\sigma^{\prime\prime}>\sigma
.

In particular, there is an
e\in\pi_{x}E^{\prime}
satisfying (C2). Let
y\in E^{\prime}
such that
e=\frac{y-x}{|x-y|}
. Then this choice of
x
and
y
satisfies (C1)-(C3).
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Note that the proof of Lemma
4.1
gives a stronger result. Not only can we find a points
x
and
y
satisfying (C1), (C2) and (C3), but, for every
\sigma<\min\{\sigma_{X},\sigma_{Y},1\}
, we can find points satisfying (C1) and (C3) such that

\dim^{A,x}_{H}(e)>\sigma
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
4.1
, there are points
x\in X
and
Y\in y
such that
\dim_{H}^{A}(x)>\sigma_{X},\dim_{H}^{A}(y)>\sigma_{Y}
and
\dim_{H}^{A,x}(\ell)>0
, where
\ell
is the line joining
x
and
y
, satisfying the following for all sufficiently large
n
and
m\leq n
.

K_{m}^{A}(\ell)\lessapprox K_{m}^{A}(\ell\mid x)+K_{m,n}^{A}(\ell\mid y).

Similarly, we have

K_{n}^{A}(\ell)\lessapprox K_{n}^{A}(\ell\mid y)+K_{n}^{A,x}(\ell).

Proof.

Using Lemma
4.1
, gives an
x\in X
and
y\in Y
satisfying (C1), (C2) and (C3).

By the symmetry of information,

K^{A}_{m}(\ell)-K^{A}_{m}(\ell\mid x)\approx K^{A}_{m}(x)-K^{A}_{m}(x\mid\ell).

Applying the symmetry of information again,

K^{A}_{m}(x\mid\ell)\gtrapprox K^{A}_{m,m,n}(x\mid\ell,y)\approx K^{A}_{m,m,n}(x,\ell\mid y)-K_{m,n}(\ell\mid y).

Since knowing
y
with precision
n
and
x
with precision
m
also determines
\ell
with precision
m
(so long as
x
and
y
are not too close to each other, which we can assume) therefore

K^{A}_{m,m,n}(x,\ell\mid y)\approx K^{A}_{m,n}(x\mid y).

Putting these inequalities together we get

K^{A}_{m}(\ell)-K^{A}_{m}(\ell\mid x)-K^{A}_{m,n}(\ell\mid y)\lessapprox K^{A}_{m}(x)-K^{A}_{m,n}(x\mid y).

By (C3),
K_{m}^{A}(x)\approx K_{m,n}^{A}(x\mid y)
, and the first equality follows.

Now we repeat essentially the same argument, except that we exchange
x
and
y
in the above formulas, put
n=m
, and use
x
as an oracle.

Since

K^{A}_{m}(\ell)-K^{A}_{m}(\ell\mid y)\approx K^{A}_{m}(y)-K^{A}_{m}(y\mid\ell)\lessapprox K^{A}_{m}(y)-K_{m}^{A,x}(y\mid\ell),

and

K_{m}^{A,x}(y\mid\ell)\approx K_{m}^{A,x}(y,\ell)-K_{m}^{A,x}(\ell)\approx K_{m}^{A,x}(y)-K_{m}^{A,x}(\ell),

we have

K^{A}_{m}(\ell)-K^{A}_{m}(\ell\mid y)-K_{m}^{A,x}(\ell)\lessapprox K^{A}_{m}(y)-K^{A,x}_{m}(y).

By assumption (C3), the right hand side of this is
\approx 0
, so

(14)

K_{m}^{A}(\ell)\lessapprox K_{m}^{A}(\ell\mid y)+K_{m}^{A,x}(\ell),

and the proof is complete.
∎

We are now ready to prove our main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
{\varepsilon}>0
,
\sigma_{X}<\dim_{H}(X)
,
\sigma_{Y}<\dim_{H}(Y)
, and let
X^{\prime}\subseteq X
,
Y^{\prime}\subseteq Y
be compact sets such that
\dim_{H}(X^{\prime})>\sigma_{X}
and
\dim_{H}(Y^{\prime})>\sigma_{Y}
. Let
\mu,\nu
be probability measures whose support is contained in
X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}
, respectively, and such that they satisfy the
\dim_{H}(X^{\prime})
and the
\dim_{H}(Y^{\prime})
dimensional Frostman condition, respectively.

We first handle the case when
\nu(\ell)=\mu(\ell)=1
for some line
\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In this case, since
X
and
Y
are not contained on the same line, it is immediate that

\sup\limits_{x\in x}\dim_{H}(\pi_{x}Y^{\prime})\geq\dim_{H}(Y^{\prime})>\sigma_{Y}
.

If this holds with a
\sigma_{Y}
arbitrary close to
\dim_{H}(Y)
, then the conclusion follows. We will therefore assume that
\mu(\ell)\nu(\ell)<1
for every line
\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Let
A
be an oracle relative to which
X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}
are computably compact, and such that
\nu
and
\mu
are computable.

By Theorem
4.3
, there are points
x\in X^{\prime}
and
y\in Y^{\prime}
satisfying (C1) and (C2), and such that

(15)

K_{m}^{A}(\ell)\lessapprox K_{m}^{A}(\ell\mid x)+K_{m,n}^{A}(\ell\mid y),

and

(16)

K_{n}^{A}(\ell)\lessapprox K_{n}^{A}(\ell\mid y)+K_{n}^{A,x}(\ell),

for all sufficiently large
n
, and
m\leq n
, where
\ell
is the line joining
x
and
y
.

Denoting
t_{x}=\dim^{A}_{H}(x)
and
s_{x}=\dim_{H}^{A,x}(\ell)=\dim_{H}^{A,x}(e)
, since
s_{x}>0
, from (
15
) and
Theorem
3.5
we get

\displaystyle K^{A}_{m,n}(\ell\mid y)

\displaystyle\gtrapprox K_{m}^{A}(\ell)-K_{m}^{A}(\ell\mid x)

(17)

\displaystyle\gtrapprox\min\{t_{x},(s_{x}+t_{x})/2,1\}m-O({\varepsilon}m)-O_{{\varepsilon},s_{x},t_{x}}(\log m),

sufficiently large
n
and for all
m\leq n
.

Denote
t_{y}=\dim^{A}_{H}(y)
. For every
n
, let
s_{n}=\min\{\frac{K^{A}_{m,n}(\ell\mid y)}{m}\mid\varepsilon n\leq m\leq n\}
. Let
s_{y}=\liminf_{n}s_{n}
, and note that (
17
) implies that
s_{y}>0
.

We now fix a sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By (
16
) and Theorem
3.7
we get

\displaystyle K^{A,x}_{m}(\ell)

\displaystyle\gtrapprox K_{m}^{A}(\ell)-K_{m}^{A}(\ell\mid y)

(18)

\displaystyle\gtrapprox\min\{t_{y},(s_{y}+t_{y})/2,1\}m-O({\varepsilon}m)-O_{{\varepsilon},s_{y},t_{y}}(\log m).

Choosing
m
to be a precision at which
K^{A}_{m,n}(\ell\mid y)=K^{A}_{m,n}(e\mid y)\sim s_{y}m
, from (
17
) we see that

\displaystyle s_{y}

\displaystyle\geq\min\{t_{x},(s_{x}+t_{x})/2,1\}-O({\varepsilon})-\frac{O_{{\varepsilon},s_{x},t_{x}}(\log n)}{m}

(19)

\displaystyle\geq\min\{t_{x},(s_{x}+t_{x})/2,1\}-O({\varepsilon}).

Similarly, choosing
m
to be a precision at which
K^{A,x}_{m}(e)\sim s_{x}m
, from (
18
)
we get

\displaystyle s_{x}

\displaystyle\geq\min\{t_{y},(s_{y}+t_{y})/2,1\}-O({\varepsilon})-\frac{O_{{\varepsilon},s_{y},t_{y}}(\log m)}{m}

(20)

\displaystyle\geq\min\{t_{y},(s_{y}+t_{y})/2,1\}-O({\varepsilon}).

Plugging in (
19
) into (
20
) we get:

(21)

s_{x}\geq\min\{t_{y},\frac{t_{x}+t_{y}}{2},\frac{\frac{s_{x}+t_{x}}{2}+t_{y}}{2},\frac{1+t_{y}}{2},1\}-O({\varepsilon}).

This implies that

(22)

s_{x}\geq\min\{t_{y},\frac{t_{x}+t_{y}}{2},1\}-O({\varepsilon}).

Indeed, in (
21
) we have
\frac{1+t_{y}}{2}\geq\min\{1,t_{y}\}
, so either (
22
) holds or we have

s_{x}\geq\frac{\frac{s_{x}+t_{x}}{2}+t_{y}}{2}-O({\varepsilon}).

The latter one implies that

3s_{x}\geq t_{x}+2t_{y}-O({\varepsilon})=2\frac{t_{x}+t_{y}}{2}+t_{y}-O({\varepsilon}),

so in this case
s_{x}\geq\min\{\frac{t_{x}+t_{y}}{2},t_{y}\}-O({\varepsilon})
and therefore (
22
) holds.

From (
22
) the statement of Theorem
1.3
follows, since

\displaystyle\dim_{H}^{A,x}(\pi_{x}y)

\displaystyle=s_{x}

\displaystyle\geq\min\{t_{y},\frac{t_{x}+t_{y}}{2},1\}-O({\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\geq\min\{\sigma_{Y},\frac{\sigma_{X}+\sigma_{Y}}{2},1\}-O({\varepsilon}).

By (
7
), this implies that

\dim_{H}(\pi_{x}Y)\geq\min\{\sigma_{Y},\frac{\sigma_{X}+\sigma_{Y}}{2},1\}-O({\varepsilon}).

Since for every
{\varepsilon}>0
, and for every
\sigma_{X}<\dim_{H}(X)
,
\sigma_{Y}<\dim_{H}(Y)
there is an
x
for which this holds, the conclusion follows.

∎

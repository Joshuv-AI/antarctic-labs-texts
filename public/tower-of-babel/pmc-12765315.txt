New precise lamina slotting technique for cervical laminoplasty: the sliding positioning method using bone rongeur
Authors: Zhenxing Zhu, Piyao Ji, Bo Yang, Yu Zhang, Ming Deng, Jianghua Ming, Yaming Li, Yonggang Ma, Yan Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo investigate the clinical utility and performance in radiological assessment of the sliding positioning method with bone rongeur in precise lamina slot formation during cervical laminoplasty (CLP).MethodsData of 153 patients who underwent CLP were enrolled in this study, of which 79 patients underwent surgery using sliding positioning method (sliding positioning group) and 74 patients using traditional slotting method (traditional group). Radiological parameters including bilateral uncinate process vertex width (A1A2), bilateral slotted lamina width (B1B2), laminar decompression margin (B1C1 and B2C2), spinal cord transverse diameter (D1D2), and bilateral slotted lamina diameter (E1E2) were measured. The surgical time, blood loss, modified Japanese Orthopaedic Association (mJOA), visual analog scale (VAS), and Neck Disability Index (NDI) scores were assessed between two groups.ResultsThe A1A2 measured by CT exhibited an increasing trend from C3 to C7 segments in both groups (P < 0.001). The B1B2 in C3-C5 segments exceeded that of A1A2 (P < 0.001), while the B1B2 in C6-C7 segments was smaller compared with that of A1A2 (P < 0.05) in both groups. The B2C2 in C3-C5 segments exceeded that in B1C1 (P < 0.05), and B1C1 and B2C2 in C6-C7 segments exceeded that in adjacent segments in the traditional group (P < 0.05). The B1C1 and B2C2 were relatively constant in the sliding positioning group (P >0.05). The MRI measurements indicated that the D1D2 was relatively constant within the C3 to C5 segments, whereas it showed a gradual decline in C6-C7 segments. The E1E2 in C3-C7 segments exceeded that in D1D2 in two groups (P < 0.001). In the sliding positioning group, the intraoperative bleeding volume and surgical time were lower (P < 0.01), and the postoperative mJOA score was higher than those in the traditional group (P < 0.05). In both groups, there was no significant difference in VAS and NDI scores (P > 0.05).ConclusionCompared with traditional slotting technique, the sliding positioning technique based on bone rongeur is more precise in lamina slotting, with shorter surgical time, less bleeding, and can achieve better clinical outcome in CLP.Keywords: Cervical spondylotic myelopathy, Laminoplasty, Precision treatment, Clinical efficacy

Introduction

Cervical laminoplasty (CLP) is a common procedure for treating patients with extensive spinal cord compression [1]. Two classes of procedures are adopted in CLP—the open-door laminoplasty proposed by Hirabayashi, and the double-door laminoplasty proposed by Kurokawa, both of which achieve comparable neurological recovery and postoperative range of motion [2, 3]. These two surgical methods require lamina positioning and slotting. It has been reported that the application of grinding drills and ultrasonic bone knives improves the efficiency of cervical lamina slotting, but this necessitates precise positioning.

Notably, the posterior vertebral lamina and lateral mass of the cervical spine show different anatomical structure, especially in patients with developmental cervical canal stenosis [4, 5]. The vertebral lamina is flat and lacks pronounced anatomical landmarks at its junction with the lateral mass. CLP is often used to treat patients with cervical spondylotic myelopathy accompanied by developmental cervical canal stenosis [6]. If the decompression range is too narrow, insufficient decompression may occur, and if it is too wide, it may easily groove the cancellous bone within the side block, increasing the thickness of the groove bone and the difficulty of slotting. Moreover, it may induce bleeding in the lateral venous plexus of the spinal canal. Currently, a feasibility study is underway to explore the use of digital guided templates in CLP. This approach has the potential to enhance both the accuracy and safety of bone groove positioning and depth [7]. Tian et al. used computer navigation technology, which was expected to significantly improve the accuracy of CLP [8]. These methods have the potential to effectively expand the spinal canal and enhance clinical efficacy. However, the preoperative design associated with the aforementioned techniques is relatively complex, leading to increased economic burdens for patients. Additionally, the application of these methods is constrained by the hardware capabilities available within healthcare facilities.

Therefore, it is imperative to investigate the anatomical relationship between the cervical vertebral lamina and lateral mass junction, and identify a safe and reliable method for precise lamina slotting and positioning of CLP. The currently used method for locating based on the boundary area between the cervical lamina and the lateral mass is relatively general and vague, which reduces the accuracy of locating in CLP, resulting in too wide or narrow lamina slotting.

In this study, we propose the sliding positioning technology which improves precise slotting of cervical laminas, standardizes the slotting procedure, reduces the interference of the venous plexus, and ensures sufficient decompression effect in the CLP. Here, we retrospectively analyzed the clinical data of 153 patients with cervical spondylotic myelopathy treated with CLP in Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University from October 2020 to September 2024. Computed Tomography (CT) and Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) imaging data were analyzed to evaluate postoperative decompression effect to provide a reference for future clinical treatment. This study aims to compare the sliding positioning technique using a bone rongeur with the traditional slotting technique in CLP. The goal is to enhance the accuracy and safety of selecting the bone groove position during CLP.

Methods

Patient demographics

We retrieved the clinical data of 202 patients admitted to our hospital for CLP from October 2020 to September 2024. The following inclusion criteria were used: (1) patients who were diagnosed with cervical spondylotic myelopathy and underwent CLP; (2) patients who completed preoperative and postoperative cervical spine X-ray examinations and received CT and MRI imaging data, and the CT and MRI images were kept in the same position preoperatively, postoperatively and at follow-up; (3) patients with modified Japanese Orthopaedic Association (mJOA) [9], visual analog scale (VAS) [10], and Neck Disability Index (NDI) [11] scores taken before and after surgery at the last follow-up; (4) patients who were followed up for more than 12 months. The exclusion criteria were: (1) incomplete preoperative and postoperative imaging and clinical data and patients who were lost to follow-up; (2) those with a previous history of head and neck surgery; (3) local kyphosis >13° [12]; (4) K-line negative patients with ossification of posterior longitudinal ligament (OPLL) [13]; (5) patients in whom the function of the spinal cord was compromised by diseases, such as spinal cord tumors, multiple sclerosis, motor neuron disease, etc. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University (WDRY2024-K119). All patients provided written informed consent to participate in the study. The study was performed following principles of the Declaration of Helsinki [14].

Radiographic evaluation

Two experienced spinal surgeons extracted imaging data using the Picture Archiving and Communication System (PACS). Each measurer measured twice and took the average, and the final result was the average of the two measured results. The following radiographic parameters were conducted:

Midsagittal diameter ratio: The median diameter ratio (Pavlov ratio) was calculated as the ratio of vertebral canal median diameter to vertebral body median diameter. This value allows for the determination of whether the patient presents with developmental spinal stenosis. A Pavlov ratio of ≤ 0.75 indicates the presence of developmental spinal stenosis [15].

CT measurement parameters (Fig. 1): The imaging was conducted utilizing a 256-row multi-layer spiral CT scanner (GE, USA). The subject was positioned supinely with the neck adjusted to a neutral alignment. The scanning parameters were as follows: layer thickness of 0.625 mm, layer spacing of 0.625 mm, tube voltage of 120 kV, current of 250 mA, window width of 440 mm, and window position of + 40 mm.

Bilateral uncinate process vertex width (A1A2): The left and right uncinate process vertices (A1, A2) were marked in the cervical CT cross-sectional image, and the bilateral uncinate process vertex width (A1A2) in the decompression segment was measured.

Bilateral slotted lamina width (B1B2): The inner edges (B1, B2) of the left and right slotted cervical laminas were labelled in the cervical CT cross-sectional image, and the bilateral cervical lamina decompression width (B1B2) was measured.

Laminar decompression margin (B1C1 and B2C2): The outermost edges of the spinal canal on both sides (C1, C2) were marked in the cervical CT cross-sectional image, followed by measurement of the distance from the inner edge of the decompression area of the left and right vertebral lamina to the outermost point of the ipsilateral spinal canal (B1C1 and B2C2, Fig. 1).

MRI measurement parameters (Fig. 2): Scanning was conducted utilizing the GE Discovery 1.5T superconducting MRI scanner in conjunction with the GEM Spine from the United States. The subject was positioned in a supine orientation, ensuring that the neck was positioned as proximally as feasible to the center of the main magnetic field and the coil. The scanning protocol covered the entirety of the cervical spine, employing parameters that included a layer thickness of 3 mm and an interlayer spacing of 0.5 mm.

Spinal cord transverse diameter (D1D2): The cervical MRI axial T2WI images were selected, the left and right outermost boundaries of the spinal cord (D1, D2) were selected in the cross-sectional images of the decompression segment in sequence, followed by measurement of the spinal cord transverse diameter (D1D2).

Bilateral slotted lamina diameter (E1E2): The cervical MRI axial T2WI images were selected, and the outer edges of the bilateral laminectomy (E1, E2) were labeled in the cross-sectional images of the decompression segment in sequence, after which the bilateral slotted lamina diameter (E1E2) was measured.

The standard for adequate spinal decompression of CLP: The width of B1B2 should be greater than that of A1A2, or the diameter of E1E2 should be greater than that of D1D2 [16, 17].

Surgical technique

Patients were positioned in the prone orientation under general anesthesia with tracheal intubation and underwent routine sterilization procedures. A posterior median incision was performed at the cervical decompression level to facilitate exposure of the surgical field. The skin, subcutaneous fascia, and posterior cervical ligament were sequentially incised. Dissection was carried out to expose the tip of the cervical spinous process, followed by the lateral separation of the muscles from the spinous process. Subsequently, the lamina and articular processes were exposed for surgical intervention.

In the sliding positioning group, the sliding positioning method with bone rongeur in precise lamina slot formation was applied in the preparation of door opening and door axis. In the segment to be decompressed, a 2 mm bone rongeur was slide along the upper and lower edges of the lamina from the root of the spinous process to the junction area of the outermost side blocks of the lamina. It was then moved inward by 1 mm and the outer cortical bone of the lamina was chiseled off for slotting and positioning to determine the exact location of the slot (Figs. 3 and 4). The outer edge of the lamina and the junction of the articular process on the hinge side were subsequently remodeled using a ball mill to create longitudinal grooves. This process involved grinding the outer plate and bone while leaving the inner plate intact. On the open side, a slot was carefully created at the junction of the articular process, following the outer edge of the lamina. The lamina was completely cut off, and the spinous process and lamina were repositioned towards the hinge side.

In the traditional group, a slot was created at the junction of the inner edge of the lateral facet joint and the outer edge of the lamina utilizing visual inspection. Cortical bone, along with a portion of the cancellous bone, was excised to establish a bone slot that served as the axis for the door mechanism. The entire lamina was subsequently sectioned to facilitate access to lateral aspect. Finally, the exposed segment was stabilized and secured with the application of plates and screws.

Next, the wound was washed repeatedly with normal saline to promote hemostasis. Drains were placed on both sides of the incisional spinous process. Finally, the muscle layer, ligament, subcutaneous fascia, and skin were sutured layer by layer.

Postoperative management

Routine prophylactic use of antibiotics was administered for 48–72 h post-surgery. Moreover, dehydrating drugs and neurotrophic drugs were administered for 3–6 days. If the postoperative drainage volume was less than 50 ml, the drainage tube was removed. Subsequently, the patients were instructed to turn over regularly and perform muscle contraction exercises for the lower limbs. On the third day post-surgery, the patients were allowed to get out of bed with the help of a neck brace. The neck brace was removed after 4 weeks, after which cervical flexion and extension exercises were performed.

Assessment of the spinal cord function

The mJOA, VAS, and NDI scores were employed to examine the spinal cord function before and after surgery. In addition, the recovery rate was calculated using the following formula (Hirabayashi method): Recovery rate (RR, %) = (postoperative mJOA - preoperative mJOA)/(18 [full score] - preoperative mJOA) × 100% [18].

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS 25.0 software (SPSS Inc., Chicago, Ill., USA). Interobserver reliability was assessed using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) [19]. The intraobserver and interobserver reliability statistics with ICC was 0.83 (range, 0.75–0.88), indicating good interobserver agreement. Data were presented as the mean (± standard deviation [SD]) at a significance level of P < 0.05. The differences between the data obtained by CT and MRI measurement parameters were determined using the paired t test. Preoperative and postoperative mJOA, VAS, and NDI scores were compared using the paired sample t-test.

The widths of B1B2 in the C3-C5 decompression segments in two groups significantly exceeded those of the width of A1A2 in the same segment (P < 0.001, Tables 2 and 3). The widths of B1B2 in the C6-C7 decompression segments in two groups were narrower than those of A1A2 in the same area, with the observed difference being statistically significant (P < 0.05).

In the sliding positioning group, the distances of B1C1 and B2C2 were relatively constant, and ranged from 4.13 mm to 5.58 mm. There were no significant differences in distances of B1C1 and B2C2 in C3-C7 decompression segments between adjacent segments (P >0.05, Fig. 5), except for B1C1 of C7 section was slightly wider than that of C6 (P < 0.05). The distances of B1C1 in C6 and B2C2 in C6-C7 decompression segments were slightly wider than that of adjacent segments (P < 0.05).

MRI radiographic evaluation

In the sliding positioning group, the D1D2 diameter measured based on MRI was relatively stable from the C3 to C5 segments. No statistically significant differences were observed in the D1D2 diameter between adjacent segments (P >0.05, Fig. 5). However, between C6 and C7, the D1D2 diameter in the sliding positioning group demonstrated a progressively decreasing trend in comparison to the C3-C5 segments (P < 0.01, Fig. 5). In the traditional group, no statistically significant differences were observed in the D1D2 diameter in C3-C7 between adjacent segments (P >0.05, Fig. 6).

In the sliding positioning group, the diameters of E1E2 in the C3-C5 decompression segments measured by MRI were relatively constant, with the range being 15.09–15.29 mm. There was no statistically significant difference between adjacent segments at C3-C5 (P >0.05, Fig. 5). The diameters of E1E2 in the C6-C7 decompression segments exhibited a consistent decreasing trend. When comparing the E1E2 diameters between the C3-C5 and C6-C7 decompression segments, the differences were found to be statistically significant (P < 0.05, Fig. 5) in the sliding positioning group. In the traditional group, there were no significant differences in the distances of E1E2 in C3-C7 (P >0.05, Fig. 6). Additionally, MRI measurements revealed that the E1E2 diameters across the C3-C7 decompression segments were consistently larger than the corresponding cross-sectional diameters of D1D2 in those segments in two groups (P < 0.001, Figs. 5 and 6).

Clinical outcome evaluation

All 153 cases were followed up for at least 12 months. In the sliding positioning group, the preoperative mJOA score was 11.06 ± 1.69 and the last follow-up mJOA score was 16.48 ± 0.90. The RR of spinal cord function was 78.1%. In the traditional group, the preoperative mJOA score was 10.78 ± 1.23 and the last follow-up mJOA score was 16.15 ± 0.87. The RR of spinal cord function was 74.4%. There was statistically difference in the postoperative mJOA score (P = 0.023) and no statistically difference in the postoperative VAS and NDI scores between the two groups (P >0.05, Table 4; Fig. 7).

Postoperative complication analysis

In the sliding positioning group, four cases of postoperative C5 nerve root paralysis fully recovered within three months. Additionally, two cases with axial symptoms showed improvement after receiving treatment with nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and physical therapy. In the traditional group, there were three cases of C5 nerve root paralysis and three cases of axial symptoms, all of which also recovered well following appropriate treatment. Throughout the follow-up period, no serious complications such as internal fixation failure, spinal cord nerve injury, or reoperation, were reported. Furthermore, there was no statistically significant difference in complications between the two groups (P = 0.855).

Discussion

This study compared bone rongeur sliding positioning technique and traditional method in guiding cervical lamina slotting in CLP. The bone rongeur sliding positioning technique precisely identifies the junction between the cervical lamina and the lateral mass. It effectively addresses anatomical abnormalities resulting from degenerative, developmental, or secondary factors affecting the cervical lamina structure. Additionally, it minimizes interference with the lateral venous plexus within the cervical spinal canal, ensuring optimal spinal cord decompression. Compared with the traditional slotting method, the application of bone rongeur sliding positioning technique in cervical lamina slotting during CLP has the advantages including, simple operation, precise lamina positioning, reduced venous plexus bleeding, and better clinical outcome, making it ideal for clinical application.

Safety and effectiveness of bone rongeur sliding positioning technique in CLP

The success of CLP depends on the positioning of the cervical lamina groove. Although surgeons aim to provide accurate positioning based on the anatomical structure of the cervical lamina, the actual lamina positioning is difficult. The cervical lamina slot in CLP is located on the lateral side of the spinal cord. The spinal cord undergoes compression degeneration or deformation within the narrow spinal canal. Notably, the spacing between the slots of the bilateral cervical laminas influence the width of the coronal decompression and the degree of sagittal decompression of the spinal cord, enabling the spinal cord with lateral angular changes to return to a circular arc shape and move away from the anterior compression area [20, 21]. Simultaneously, it is essential to acknowledge the risks associated with decompression due to an overly wide cervical lamina. These hazards include potential bleeding from the trabecular bone within the lateral mass, increased complexity and depth during the slotting procedure, and excessive hemorrhaging resulting from the distension of the venous plexus. The width between the vertices of the bilateral uncinate processes serves as an anatomical reference marker for the effective width of anterior cervical decompression [22], and is also ideal as a reference for the width of posterior cervical decompression.

In this study, postoperative CT and MRI examinations revealed that in both the sliding positioning group with bone rongeur and the traditional group, the measurements of B1B2 were greater than those of A1A2, and E1E2 exceeded D1D2. Although these findings indicated effective spinal decompression, the decompression effects reflected by these measurements did not correspond with the clinical outcomes. Notably, the postoperative mJOA score showed better performance in the sliding positioning group compared to the traditional group. This difference can primarily be attributed to the greater regularity and uniformity in lamina slotting observed in the sliding positioning group, as indicated by the consistent measurement dimensions of B1C1 and B2C2. In contrast, the traditional group displayed significant variability in lamina slotting, whether across the same or different segments, which adversely affected postoperative spinal cord drift and overall clinical efficacy.

In this study, we used the precise lamina slotting technique on 79 patients and traditional technique on 74 patients undergoing CLP, and measured postoperative CT and MRI imaging data. It was observed that the width between the bilateral uncinate process vertices gradually increased from the C3-C7 segment, from (15.93 ± 1.48) mm at C3 to (23.41 ± 2.82) mm at C7, matching the trend for the measurement results obtained in previous investigations [22–24]. Subsequent measurement results indicated that the decompression margin of the C3-C7 cervical lamina exhibited a consistent variation trend, with values ranging from 4.13 mm to 5.58 mm. This finding further confirms the stability and precision of the bone rongeur sliding positioning technique used in cervical lamina slotting. By ensuring that the cervical lamina slotting line remains on a uniform straight plane, this method effectively prevents the issue of slotting being excessively inward or outward. The stability of the bone rongeur sliding positioning technique for slotting is influenced ascribed to the constant anatomical structure at the junction of the cervical lamina and lateral mass. The bone rongeur moves along the upper and lower margins of the cervical lamina, extending to its outermost region. This area is constrained by the inner edge of the lateral mass articular process. At this point, the positioning reaches the outermost part of the spinal canal, which corresponds to the venous plexus located within it. Moving 1 mm inward for positioning slotting effectively prevents the venous plexus and achieves sufficient spinal cord decompression.

This study compared operative time and blood loss between the bone rongeur positioning group and the traditional group. The results indicated that the bone rongeur positioning group experienced shorter surgical times and less blood loss compared to the traditional group. The analysis suggests that accurately positioning the slotting area in the bone rongeur group significantly enhanced efficiency and reduced the likelihood of inaccurate slotting. Understanding the differences in the internal structure of the cervical spinal canal and the characteristics of the surrounding vascular features is essential for ensuring the safety and accuracy of CLP. Ogut E et al. reported that the presence of accessory transverse foramen is crucial for diagnosing variations in the vertebral artery and related disorders, providing an anatomical reference for cervical spine surgery [25]. The variation in blood loss during CLP primarily arises from the venous plexuses located bilaterally along spinal canal. The traditional group exhibited a propensity for outward slotting deviation, which resulted in distension of the venous plexus and subsequent hemorrhage. In contrast, the sliding positioning group maintained a well-preserved residual lamina at the slotting site, minimizing stimulation to the venous plexus and consequently leading to reduced bleeding.

When employing CLP to treat cervical spondylotic myelopathy in clinical settings, we assert that the precise positioning and slotting facilitated by the bone rongeur sliding positioning technique are crucial. This method not only achieves complete decompression of the dural sac but also maintains a safe distance between the dural sac and the lateral venous plexus within the spinal canal. In this study, we performed a statistical analysis on two groups of complications. The results indicated that the sliding positioning technique did not exacerbate C5 nerve root paralysis symptoms or cervical axial symptoms, thereby demonstrating that this technique is both safe and reliable. Such an approach offers substantial benefits in enhancing both the safety and efficacy of CLP.

Currently, posterior CLP is a classic procedure widely employed in clinical practice, yet it still faces challenges that necessitate ongoing improvement. Among these challenges are the emergence of innovative surgical concepts and the development of precise decompression techniques. We utilized a bone rongeur for precise slotting and positioning, which falls into this category and has significantly enhanced the clinical treatment outcome.

Surgical points and imaging analysis of bone rongeur sliding positioning technique

For patients with cervical spondylotic myelopathy with developmental cervical canal stenosis or severe proliferative degeneration, accurate identification of the anatomical markers and determination of the site of cervical lamina slotting with the naked eye is challenging [26]. When employing a grinding drill or bone rongeur for cervical lamina slotting, it is essential to remain within the designated safe decompression zone, defined as the area situated between the lateral aspect of the spinal cord and the venous plexus. Departing from this zone may result in insufficient decompression of the spinal cord or an elevated risk of hemorrhage. To mitigate these potential complications, we implemented a bone rongeur sliding positioning technique to accurately pinpoint the slotting location on the cervical lamina. Subsequent to the surgical procedure, we validated the precision of this technique through CT and MRI imaging.

Analysis of the measurement outcomes indicated that postoperative cervical MRI demonstrated the comparative relationship between the transverse diameter of the spinal cord and the transverse diameter of bilateral cervical lamina decompression. The transverse diameter of bilateral cervical lamina decompression was found to be approximately 3–4 mm greater than that of the corresponding segment of the spinal cord. This difference establishes a safe distance of 1.5–2 mm between the grooves on either side and the outer margin of the spinal cord, thereby ensuring sufficient space for effective spinal cord decompression.

Of note, we observed an interesting phenomenon where the widths between the grooves of the bilateral cervical laminas exceeded those between the vertices of the bilateral uncinate processes in the same segment from C3 to C5 in the sliding positioning group, while the widths between the grooves of the bilateral cervical laminas in C6 to C7 were smaller relative to the distance between the vertices of the bilateral uncinate processes in the same segment. This phenomenon is largely described and explained from the perspective of anatomical interpretation and is consistent with the previous research results on the cervical spinal cord morphometrics [22, 27]. Firstly, the vertebrae of C6-C7 are part of the lower cervical spine and have a significantly larger transverse diameter of the vertebral body compared to the vertebrae of C3-C5. As a result, the bilateral uncinate process vertex widths in C6-C7 are also larger than those in C3-C5. However, the transverse diameters of the cervical spinal canal have not increased correspondingly, indicating that there is no need for larger bilateral slotted lamina widths to achieve effective spinal cord decompression. Consequently, the bilateral slotted lamina widths in C6-C7 are smaller than the corresponding widths between the bilateral uncinate process vertices. On the other hand, the transverse diameter of the spinal cord in the C6 to C7 segment exhibited a decreasing trend, which may be due to the smaller transverse diameter of the spinal cord in the C6 to C7 segment compared to the C3 to C5 segment. The C6 to C7 segment does not necessitate an increased diameter between the grooves of the bilateral cervical laminas to ensure effective spinal cord decompression. These findings indicate that the width between the tips of the bilateral uncinate processes at the C3 to C5 levels may be utilized as an anatomical reference marker in both anterior and posterior cervical decompression surgeries.

The inter-groove width of the bilateral cervical laminas at the C6-C7 vertebral levels is positioned more medially in relation to the vertices of the uncinate processes and is situated further from the lateral venous plexus of the spinal canal. The spinal cord within the C6-C7 region continues to experience adequate decompression. Throughout the decompression process, several critical steps have been identified: (1) In instances where osteophyte hyperplasia obscures the differentiation between the cervical lamina and the lateral mass during surgical intervention, it is imperative to fully expose the inner edge of the lateral mass; (2) The bone rongeur should be carefully maneuvered along the superior and inferior margins of the cervical lamina towards the decompression boundary on the medial aspect of the lateral mass, involving a movement of 1 mm inward while avoiding injury to the lateral venous plexus of the spinal canal and maintaining the integrity of the outer layer of the dural sac to ensure effective spinal cord decompression; (3) The use of a 2 mm width bone rongeur facilitates precise delineation of the outer layer of the cervical lamina without incurring bone loss or excessive slitting, which could jeopardize the placement of the steel plate; (4) Following accurate slotting and positioning with the 2 mm width bone rongeur, a slightly larger grinding drill is utilized to delicately excise the outer cortex of the cervical lamina on the door axis side, thereby creating a groove approximately 3–4 mm deep. On the door opening side, an ultrasonic hook bone knife or 1 mm forceps is employed to meticulously separate the inner layer of the cervical lamina, achieving an opening angle of approximately 60° and ensuring sufficient spinal cord decompression [28, 29].

Limitations

Nevertheless, this study has several shortcomings. The measurement data in this study was derived from a moderate sample size, and further studies should explore whether it can serve as a reference for special cases of abnormal cervical lamina development. This study employs a retrospective design, which may introduce potential selection bias due to the absence of blinding inter-observer variability. To address this, we aim to validate our findings through anatomical studies and apply the data from this research to a larger sample size in order to confirm the feasibility and safety of the bone rongeur sliding positioning technique in CLP. Future directions may involve prospective randomized comparisons, 3D morphological modelling, or navigation-assisted validation studies.

Conclusion

Application of the bone rongeur sliding positioning technique to locate cervical lamina slots in CLP improves the clinical outcome as well as achieves precise lamina positioning and slotting, making it ideal for clinical application.

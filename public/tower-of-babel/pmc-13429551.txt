10.6 μm laser moxibustion can improve oxidative stress and abnormal energy metabolism in liver of hypothyroidism rats through Nrf2/HO-1 and Nrf2/PGC-1α/TFAM pathways
Authors: Linfu Yu, Xueyong Shen, Qiyun He, Lin Lin, Haiping Deng, Zouqin Huang, Ling Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHypothyroidism (HypoT) is a systemic hypometabolic disorder characterized by thyroid hormone deficiency and is closely associated with oxidative stress (OS). The hypothalamic-pituitary-thyroid (HPT) axis plays a key role in regulating thyroid hormone secretion and the Nrf2 signaling pathway in alleviating OS. The aim of this study is to investigate whether 10.6 μm laser moxibustion can affect the activation of Nrf2 signaling pathway by HPT axis, reduce OS and thus alleviate liver damage in HypoT model. The far-infrared wavelength peak of 10.6 μm laser moxibustion is close to the infrared radiation spectrum of traditional moxibustion. It functions similarly to moxibustion without causing pain, infection, or irritating smoke. Twenty-four Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats were randomly divided into Con group, HypoT group, LM(laser moxibustion) group, and SLM(sham laser moxibustion) group (n = 6). HypoT was induced in 18 rats by intragastric administration of methimazole (10 mg/kg). Rats in the LM group and SLM group were irradiated at Guanyuan (CV4) and Mingmen (GV4) acupoints for 15 min, once every other day for 14 consecutive days, totaling 7 treatments. Rats in the Con group and HypoT group underwent the same handling and fixation procedures without additional intervention. Rectal temperature, body weight, and acupoint temperature were monitored throughout the study. Serum levels of T4, T3, TSH, TRH, cAMP, and cGMP were detected by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). liver tissue microstructure was examined by hematoxylin and eosin staining (H&E). The content of adenosine ATP and the activities of CAT, SOD, Na+-K+-ATPase, and GSH-Px in hepatocyte mitochondria were determined by colorimetric method and MDA by ELISA. Western blotting was used to detect the protein expression levels of NRF2, HO-1, PGC-1α, and TFAM in liver. These results indicate that 10.6 μm laser moxibustion may regulate the NRF2 signaling axis through the HPT axis, reduce liver OS and improve the related symptoms of HypoT rats.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10103-026-04976-4.Keywords: Hypothyroidism, Laser moxibustion, Oxidative stress, NRF2/HO-1 signaling pathway

Introduction

hypothyroidism (HypoT) is a clinical syndrome caused by thyroid hormone deficiency. Clinically, it is characterized by an elevated serum concentration of thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), accompanied by decreased levels of free triiodothyronine (FT3) and free thyroxine (FT4) [1]. HypoT is typically associated with symptoms such as slowed brain activity, fatigue, muscle weakness, drowsiness, bradycardia, reduced cardiac output, and decreased blood volume [2]. thyroid hormones are crucial for the differentiation, growth, metabolism, and physiological functions of all body tissues [3, 4]. The clinical features of HypoT are complex and not specific, making it difficult to diagnose, but it can adversely affect the patient’s health or even cause death [5]. The current main treatment for HypoT involves using levothyroxine to manage the progression of the disease. However, this medication requires long-term or lifelong administration, as it poses potential safety concerns related to supraphysiological serum triiodothyronine concentrations and the lack of long-term safety outcome data [6, 7].

Oxidative stress (OS) refers to a pathophysiological state characterized by an imbalance between the generation of pro-oxidants and the capacity of antioxidant defense mechanisms. Hepatic hypothyroidism is caused by thyroid hormone imbalance, and oxidative stress occurs in the liver. ROS products can weaken the activity of deiodinase, enhance lipid peroxidation, disrupt energy homeostasis, and impair liver health [8–12]. ROS are highly reactive and attack nearby polyunsaturated fatty acids to form malondialdehyde (MDA) [13], which can indirectly reflect free radical levels in the body and directly correlate with the degree of cellular damage. The Nrf2 signaling pathway plays a critical role in regulating the cellular response to OS. As a key transcription factor, Nrf2 mediates the expression of numerous antioxidant and detoxification genes. Under OS, Nrf2 translocates into the nucleus, and binds to antioxidant response elements (AREs) in the promoter regions of target genes. This molecular cascade subsequently activates antioxidant enzymes—including SOD, CAT, and GSH-PX—while simultaneously upregulating detoxifying enzymes and promoting the synthesis of glutathione (GSH), a pivotal endogenous antioxidant [14–16]. During the progression of HypoT syndrome, dysregulation of the Nrf2 signaling pathway often occurs, leading to reduced antioxidant capacity and exacerbated OS [17]. In addition, HypoT is also associated with mitochondrial dysfunction.

Mitochondria, as key organelles in cells, are responsible for energy production and also function as effectors. Depletion of the number or function of mitochondria in cells leads to excessive production of ROS, which in turn induces chronic diseases such as non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) [18]. NAFLD is a complex clinical entity that can be secondary to HypoT [19]. Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor-gamma coactivator-1α (PGC-1α) plays an important role in the regulation of mitochondrial biosynthesis. liver-specific PGC-1α deficiency leads to impaired hepatic mitochondrial oxidative capacity and hepatic steatosis [20]. Activated PGC-1α reduces lipid accumulation and improves mitochondrial function while enhancing Nrf2 activity [21]. Nrf2 plays a role in mitochondrial biogenesis and may form a feedback loop with PGC-1α [22, 23]. However, the effect of 10.6 μm laser moxibustion in this pathway has not been studied.

Notably, moxibustion has shown promising potential in slowing the progression of OS in HypoT [24]. Moxibustion stimulates acupoints via thermal energy generated by burning Artemisia argyi. However, burning Artemisia argyi produces toxic fumes and poses risks of skin burns [25–28]. To address these limitations, laser moxibustion—an innovative combination of traditional moxibustion and modern low-level laser therapy (LLLT)—utilizes low-intensity laser irradiation at acupoints to generate thermal effects, while avoiding localized pain, infection risks, and irritating smoke emissions [29, 30]. Shen’s previous studies have shown that 10.6 μm laser moxibustion emits far-infrared radiation (at the 10.6 μm wavelength), which closely matches the infrared spectral profiles of both traditional moxibustion and human acupoints—specifically exhibiting three characteristic peaks around 10 μm [31].Consequently, 10.6 μm laser moxibustion induces sustained thermal effects at acupoints, penetrates deep into tissues, and achieves therapeutic effects through resonant interactions with acupoints [32]. Shen has previously demonstrated that both 10.6 μm laser moxibustion and conventional moxibustion can improve growth performance and protect the liver and related organ systems in Hydrocortisone-induced rats. The primary objective of this study is to offer researchers and individuals with HypoT a safer, more accessible, and therapeutically effective intervention—specifically 10.6 μm laser moxibustion [33, 34].

In this study, the rat model of methimazole-induced HypoT was used to investigate whether 10.6 μm laser moxibustion could alleviate OS and regulate mitochondrial biogenesis in rats with HypoT, and to explore the potential involvement of NRF2 and PGC-1α signaling pathways in this process.

Methods

Experimental animals

Twenty-four clean-grade SD rats, with a body weight of 180–220 g, were purchased from Shanghai Slack Biokay Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China), and the animal production license number was SYXK (Hu) 2025-0008. Prior to the experiment, the experimental protocol was reviewed and approved by the Experimental Animal Welfare and Ethics Committee of Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine (SHUTCM), with the animal ethics approval number PZSHUTCM2508030002. Additionally, all researchers responsible for performing experimental operations on the rats completed specialized training provided by the SHUTCM Laboratory Animal Center, and all procedures complied with the Regulations on the Use of Experimental Animals formulated by SHUTCM.

All rats were housed in the Laboratory Animal Center of SHUTCM under standardized conditions: they had free access to food and water throughout the experiment; the room temperature was maintained at (22 ± 2) °C, relative humidity at (60 ± 10)%, and a 12 h light/12 h dark artificial photoperiod was implemented; adequate ventilation was also ensured.

Grouping and model establishment

Twenty-four SD rats were randomly divided into four groups (n = 6): Control(Con) group, HypoT group, Laser moxibustion (LM) group, and Sham Laser moxibustion (SLM) group.

To establish the HypoT model, all rats except those in the Con group received methimazole solution (dosage: 10 mg/kg/day) via intragastric gavage. Rats in the Con group were gavaged with an equal volume of 0.9% sodium chloride solution (normal saline) as a vehicle control. (The HypoT model was subjected to preliminary experiments to determine the dose of 10 mg/kg by gavage for 15 days).

Additionally, behavioral and physical parameters were systematically evaluated to verify model success, including: Fur condition (e.g., dullness, sparseness); Activity levels (e.g., drowsiness, reduced mobility, or prolonged prone posture); Responsiveness (e.g., delayed reaction to stimuli, or persistent crouching posture); Locomotor speed; Scrotal appearance (e.g., pallor, shrinkage).

When the above-mentioned physiological (behavioral/physical) and biochemical (T3,T4) indicators of rats all show consistent abnormalities, it is considered that the HypoT model has been successfully established.

Animal treatment

After 15 days of gavage, the HypoT model was successfully established in 18 rats (i.e., all rats in the HypoT, LM, and SLM groups. Subsequent treatments were administered as follows:

LM group

Rats received 10.6 μm laser irradiation at the acupoints CV4 and GV4. Each treatment lasted 15 min, with one session every other day for 14 consecutive days (total 7 sessions). Detailed laser parameters (e.g., power density, irradiation distance) are provided in Fig. 1.

Laser moxibustion parameters

SLM group

The infrared laser device was turned on, but only visible red light (without 10.6 μm laser output) was emitted to the same acupoints (CV4 and GV4) under the same operation (15 min per session, once every other day for 14 days). This ensured consistency in handling while eliminating specific laser effects.

Con group and HypoT group

Rats received gentle grasping and fixation (consistent with the LM/SLM groups) but no irradiation or other treatments, to control for stress from handling.

During the treatment period, rectal temperature, body weight, and acupoint temperature (at CV4/GV4) were measured once every other day (on the day after each treatment session).

Acupoint temperature and animal specimen collection

Acupoint temperature

Acupoint temperature

The measured acupoint temperature refer to the skin surface temperature at the selected acupoints.

(2)Blood collection and sample processing

Blood collection and sample processing

Using a sterile disposable syringe, the needle was punctured above the bifurcation of the abdominal aorta, parallel to the vessel, with the needle tip facing down, one to two ml of blood was extracted. The blood was slowly added along the wall of the centrifuge tube without anticoagulant. After the centrifuge tube with whole blood was left at room temperature for 2 h, the parameters were centrifuged at 3000r/min for 15 min, and the upper serum was sucked and tested.

(3)Liver organ sampling and sample processing

Liver organ sampling and sample processing

After the end of blood sampling, the rats were transferred to the precooled sterile ice plate, and the whole liver tissue of the rats was stripped, and the surface of the liver tissue was finely removed with sterile ophthalmic scissors. The treated liver tissue was immediately placed in 0.9% normal saline precooled at 4℃ and rinsed 3 times, 10 s each time, to clean the surface and section of the organ.

(4)The organ weights were weighed and the organ indexes were calculated

The organ weights were weighed and the organ indexes were calculated

The liver tissue, which had been drained of surface water, was quickly placed in the center of the calibrated weighing tray of a precision electronic analytical balance with an accuracy of 0.1 mg. Each tissue sample was weighed twice in duplicate, and the difference between the two weights should not exceed 0.5 mg. The average of the two measurements was used as the final organ wet weight. Liver organ index: liver organ index (%) = liver wet weight (g)/final fasting body weight (g) ×100%.

(5)Extraction and testing of rat liver mitochondria

Extraction and testing of rat liver mitochondria

“After treatment, 50 to 100 mg of liver tissue was quickly weighed, washed once with precooled PBS, and cut into 3 mm² pieces on ice.” Ten times the volume of precooled lysate was added, placed in an ice bath homogenizer, homogenized 10 times, centrifuged at 2000 g for 3 min, the supernatant was transferred to a clean centrifuge tube, and centrifuged at 12,000 g for 10 min at 4 ° C in a cryogenic centrifuge. The supernatant was discarded and the resulting precipitate was identified as mitochondria.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay

Rat ELISA kits (ZCIBIO, China) were used to analyze Serum levels of T4, T3, TSH, TRH, cAMP, cGMP and mitochondria of MDA of mitochondria according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Hematoxylin and eosin staining

To perform H&E staining, fixed tissues were removed for ethanol gradient dehydration, embedded using paraffin immersion, and cut into 5 μm size. They were placed on slides, dried and dewaxed, followed by hematoxylin staining, then differentiation, reblue, eosin staining, dehydration, and sealing steps, and observed in 200× fields of view.

Colorimetric method

The mitochondrial adenosine ATP content and the activities of CAT, SOD, Na+-K+ -atpase and GSH-Px were measured by colorimetry (ZCIBIO, China, detection kit micromethod).

Western blotting

The protein concentration in the liver tissue was determined using the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) protein assay kit. The samples were subjected to 10% so dium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and then transferred onto a polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane. After sealing with skimmed milk solution, incubate with the primary antibody at 4℃ overnight, and then incubate with the secondary antibody at room temperature for 2 h. Spots were displayed by enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL), and the density of specific bands was analyzed by AlphaEaseFC(Alpha Innotech, USA).

Statistical analysis

The experimental data were analyzed by IBM SPSS 22.0 statistical software and plotted by GraphPad Prism software. The measurement data in accordance with normal distribution were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (x̄±s); Repeated measurement data collected at different time points were analyzed by repeated measurement design analysis of variance. The results were consistent with normal distribution and homogeneity of variance. Independent sample t test was used for comparison between two independent samples. One-way analysis of variance was used for overall comparison among multiple groups, and corrected t test or Mann-Whitney U rank sum test was used for non-normal distribution or uneven variance. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant (Fig).

Discussion

Animal experiments have confirmed that moxibustion can promote symptom improvement in rats with HypoT [35, 36]. Furthermore, numerous clinical studies have verified that moxibustion therapy can promote and regulate the functions of the endocrine, respiratory, digestive, and reproductive systems [37, 38], Specifically, moxibustion at points CV 4 and GV 4 can tonify both Yin and Yang, thereby achieving coordination and balance. Although traditional moxibustion therapy is effective for HypoT Syndrome, its application is restricted by factors including high-concentration moxa smoke [39]. Thus, compared with traditional moxibustion, 10.6 μm laser moxibustion exhibits advantages in the treatment of HypoT [33], however, its therapeutic mechanism remains unclear. HypoT may cause the impairment or imbalance of glandular secretion and metabolic function, thereby altering the body’s hormone levels and metabolic processes. In the regulation of energy metabolism, hormones such as thyroxine are crucial for maintaining energy metabolism, regulating the nervous system, and supporting immune function [40]. Therefore, in-depth investigation into the relationship between HypoT and thyroid function can provide a novel strategy for the prevention and treatment of HypoT -related diseases.

In this experiment, rats with HypoT exhibited significant decreases in acupoint temperature, body weight, and rectal temperature. Additionally, these rats presented with dull, dry, and sparse body hair, as well as loose stools. HypoT, as observed in the current setting, induces a decline in basal metabolic rate that directly underpins the hallmark thermoregulatory dysfunction and generalized lethargy. According to the meridian theory, acupoints are the reaction points of diseases. Modern studies have shown that the skin temperature at acupoints changes when the body undergoes a pathological state. This suggests that changes in acupoint temperature can objectively respond to the symptoms of the disease [41–44]. Following 2 weeks of 10.6 μm laser moxibustion intervention, the LM group showed significant increases in acupoint temperature, body weight, and rectal temperature. Collectively, these results indicate that 2 weeks of 10.6 μm laser moxibustion intervention significantly elevated body weight, acupoint temperature, and rectal temperature in methimazole-induced HypoT rats. This suggests that laser moxibustion can alleviate the persistent state in HypoT rats and enhance their growth capacity. Methimidazole induced liver tissue damage, which is consistent with the results of some studies [45, 46], especially mixed hepatocyte steatosis. After treatment, the steatosis of liver tissue was reversed and approached the structural expression of Con group.

The observed suppression of the cAMP/cGMP ratio suggests impaired signaling pathways involving cAMP-dependent protein kinase A (PKA) and guanosine monophosphate-dependent protein kinase G (PKG), which are crucial regulators of energy metabolism and hormone homeostasis [47]. This conclusion is based on our previous experimental studies and existing models of HypoT [48, 49], the HypoT group could be established by gavage of methimazole at a dose of 10 mg/kg for 15 days. This model simulates the pathological state of HypoT and makes an important contribution to the elucidation of the underlying mechanism of HypoT and the development of treatment strategies. Thyroid hormone is a key regulator of systemic energy metabolism. It is worth noting that patients with HypoT usually have decreased thyroid function, which is mainly manifested by decreased T3 and T4 levels and increased TRH and TSH [50]. In the present study, the cAMP/cGMP ratio in the HypoT and the SLM groups was significantly lower than that the Con group, confirming imbalances in energy metabolism and hormone homeostasis in the HypoT model. These results suggest that 10.6 μm laser moxibustion can significantly increase the cAMP/cGMP ratio in HypoT rats, which helps alleviate imbalances in energy metabolism and hormone homeostasis associated with HypoT. In addition, in the HypoT group, it was indicated that HypoT rats were in a suppressed state of thyroid-related hormones. However, in the LM group, hormone release was improved. In conclusion, 10.6 μm laser moxibustion could improve HypoT by regulating thyroid hormone homeostasis and adjusting the cAMP/cGMP ratio, thereby regulating the antioxidant capacity of the body.

SOD, CAT, and GSH-Px play critical roles in defending against free radical-induced damage, These enzymes can either inhibit the production of free radicals or rapidly scavenge existing free radicals, thereby protecting cells from oxidative damage [51]. By contrast, MDA is widely used as a marker to evaluate the severity of OS [52]. In this experiment, we identified an antioxidant cascade mediated by SOD, CAT, and GSH-Px in the liver mitochondria of HypoT rats—findings that confirm OS is involved in the pathogenesis of HypoT. Following 2 weeks of treatment, the activities of SOD, CAT, and GSH-Px in liver mitochondria of the LM group were significantly higher than those in the SLM and HypoT groups. Additionally, the MDA level in hepatocytes of the LM group was significantly lower than that in the SLM and HypoT groups. Collectively, these results suggest that 10.6 μm laser moxibustion can regulate the antioxidant components in liver mitochondria of HypoT rats, alleviate hepatic OS, and thereby improve the pathological state of HypoT.

Rats with a HypoT model exhibit energy metabolism dysfunction characterized by reduced ATP production and Na+-K+-ATPase (for membrane ion gradient maintenance) provides direct evidence of bioenergetic collapse at the cellular level, as it directly impairs the normal process of oxidative phosphorylation and thus blocks ATP synthesis pathways [53]. In the present study, we measured ATP levels in liver mitochondria across different rat groups. The results confirmed the presence of energy metabolism dysfunction in HypoT rats. Furthermore, after treatment, liver mitochondrial ATP levels in the LM group were significantly higher than those in the SLM and HypoT groups. Collectively, these findings indicate that 10.6 μm laser moxibustion can modulate liver mitochondrial Na+-K+-ATPase in HypoT rats, thereby ameliorating liver mitochondrial energy metabolism dysfunction.

The Nrf2 signaling pathway plays a key role in regulating cellular responses to OS, and Nrf2 acts as a transcription factor that mediates a variety of antioxidant and detoxification genes [54]. At the same time, Nrf2 can directly bind to the ARE sequence of PGC-1α gene promoter region, up-regulate the transcription and expression of PGC-1α, and regulate mitochondrial biogenesis and improve mitochondrial energy metabolism disorders through PGC-1α/TFAM signal axis, which is an important extension mechanism of Nrf2 pathway to exert cytoprotective effect [55]. After 10.6 μm laser moxibustion treatment, the protein expression levels of Nrf2 and HO-1 were significantly increased. This modulation enhanced the antioxidant response of HypoT rats through the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway, thereby improving the pathological state of HypoT rats. At the same time, 10.6 μm laser moxibustion could increase the protein expression levels of PGC-1α and TFAM, maintain mitochondrial genome stability, improve mitochondrial respiratory chain function, and restore oxidative phosphorylation efficiency and ATP synthesis ability by activating NrF2-mediated PGC-1α/TFAM signal axis. Improve energy metabolism disorder in HypoT rats.

This study preliminarily explored the molecular mechanisms underlying the effects of 10.6 μm laser moxibustion in treating HypoT. Notably, this study has limitations: rescue experiment without Nrf2 molecule and positive drug control group. To solve this problem, future studies should use Nrf2 inhibitors for validation experiments and add a positive drug control group to confirm the specific role of Nrf2 pathway in the therapeutic effect of laser moxibustion. Human clinical trials should also be included to evaluate the efficacy of this intervention in the clinical management of HypoT.

The value of the serum cytokine-to-immune ratio model in predicting the progression-free survival after interventional therapy for hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Jun Zhou, Bo Zhang, Jing Niu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo develop and validate a prognostic nomogram for predicting progression-free survival (PFS) in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) following transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (TACE) combined with radiofrequency ablation (RFA). The model incorporates serum transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1), interleukin-8 (IL-8), and the peripheral blood CD4+/CD8 + ratio to enable precise risk stratification and inform adjuvant therapy decisions.MethodsThis retrospective study enrolled 138 patients with HCC who underwent initial TACE/RFA between January 2020 and December 2023. Patients were randomly divided into training (n = 97) and internal validation (n = 41) sets at a 7:3 ratio. PFS was the primary outcome. Independent prognostic factors were identified via univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses in the training set and were used to construct a nomogram. Concurrently, random forest (RF), k-nearest neighbors, and gradient boosting models were developed, with hyperparameters optimized by ten-fold cross-validation. Model performance was assessed using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA). The SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) framework was applied to interpret the optimal model. Patients were stratified into risk groups based on the median nomogram score; survival differences were evaluated using Kaplan-Meier curves and the log-rank test.ResultsMultivariate Cox analysis confirmed tumor number, BCLC stage, serum transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1), serum interleukin-8 (IL-8), and the CD4+/CD8 + ratio as independent prognostic factors for PFS (all P < 0.05). Multiple tumors, advanced BCLC stage (B/C), and elevated TGF-β1 and IL-8 levels were independent risk factors, whereas a higher CD4+/CD8 + ratio was a protective factor. Among the machine learning models, RF demonstrated superior predictive performance, with AUCs of 0.846 (95% CI: 0.742–0.949) in the training set and 0.791 (95% CI: 0.619–0.964) in the validation set. Calibration curves for the RF model indicated excellent agreement between predicted and observed outcomes. DCA showed a favorable net clinical benefit across a wide range of threshold probabilities. SHAP analysis identified serum TGF-β1 as the most influential predictor; its effect direction was consistent with the Cox model, with high TGF-β1, high IL-8, advanced BCLC stage, and multiple tumors increasing the risk score, and a high CD4+/CD8 + ratio decreasing it. A nomogram was subsequently constructed based on these factors. The risk stratification derived from the nomogram effectively distinguished patients with significantly different PFS outcomes in both cohorts (P < 0.05, log-rank test).ConclusionThe prognostic nomogram integrating TGF-β1, IL-8, CD4+/CD8 + ratio, and clinicopathological factors demonstrates robust discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility for patients with HCC after TACE/RFA. The RF algorithm enhanced predictive accuracy, while SHAP improved model interpretability. This model effectively identifies high-risk patients, offering an objective foundation for individualized surveillance and adjuvant treatment strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12876-026-04894-3.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) ranks among the most prevalent and deadliest malignancies globally, presenting significant therapeutic challenges [1]. For patients with unresectable intermediate-stage HCC, transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (TACE) combined with radiofrequency ablation (RFA) has become a widely adopted locoregional treatment strategy, effectively controlling tumors and prolonging survival [2, 3]. However, clinical practice reveals substantial heterogeneity in progression-free survival (PFS) among patients receiving identical TACE/RFA treatment, with some experiencing early recurrence or progression. This prognostic variability primarily stems from the complexity of underlying tumor biology and host immune status [4]. Currently, prognostic assessment remains heavily reliant on traditional anatomical or clinical staging systems, such as the Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) classification. While these systems reflect macroscopic tumor burden, they inadequately capture the microscopic biological features driving tumor recurrence and immune evasion [5]. Notably, emerging evidence suggests that immune status not only influences prognosis but also determines the efficacy of subsequent immunotherapy, including immune checkpoint inhibitor rechallenge [6].

Recently, the value of tumor microenvironment-related biomarkers in prognostic prediction has gained increasing recognition, as supported by a systematic review and meta-analysis demonstrating that such biomarkers are significantly associated with postoperative prognosis in hepatobiliary cancers [7]. Among these, transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1), a key immunosuppressive and pro-fibrotic cytokine, inhibits effector T-cell function and promotes epithelial-mesenchymal transition. Similarly, interleukin-8 (IL-8), a pro-inflammatory and pro-angiogenic factor, is closely associated with tumor invasion and metastasis [8, 9]. Concurrently, indicators reflecting systemic immune status, such as the peripheral blood CD4+/CD8 + ratio, serve as important parameters for evaluating the body’s anti-tumor immune capacity. Although previous studies have explored the association between these individual markers and HCC prognosis, a practical tool integrating multidimensional information—encompassing tumor burden, the immune microenvironment, and systemic immunity—for precise, individualized risk stratification in patients post-TACE/RFA is currently lacking [10].

Therefore, this study aimed to develop and validate a prognostic nomogram model integrating serum TGF-β1, IL-8 levels, and the CD4+/CD8 + ratio. First, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed to identify independent prognostic factors for PFS. Subsequently, a visualized nomogram for individualized prediction was constructed based on these factors. To further optimize predictive performance, multiple machine learning algorithms were employed for model development and comparison, with the SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) framework utilized to enhance model interpretability. Finally, the model’s discriminative ability and clinical utility were evaluated through risk stratification and survival analysis, ultimately aiming to provide a basis for identifying high-risk patients, guiding individualized adjuvant therapy, and informing follow-up strategies.

Materials and methods

Study population

This single-center, retrospective cohort study consecutively enrolled 138 patients with HCC who underwent initial TACE combined with RFA treatment at the Department of Hepatobiliary Surgery of our hospital between January 2020 and December 2023. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of our hospital (Approval No. 2019QL-0312). Informed consent was obtained from all patients. Sample size calculation adhered to the “events per variable” principle for prediction model development. To ensure the stability of the multivariate Cox regression model, a minimum of 5–10 outcome events per predictor variable is typically recommended. A total of 138 HCC patients treated with TACE/RFA were enrolled and randomly divided into a training set (n = 97) and a validation set (n = 41) in a 7:3 ratio. During follow-up, 44 PFS events were recorded (31 events [32.0%] in the training set and 13 events [31.7%] in the validation set), yielding an events-per-variable ratio of 8.8:1 (44/5) for the planned multivariate Cox regression analysis, thus meeting the sample size requirement.

Inclusion criteria were: (1) Confirmed diagnosis of HCC by pathology or by imaging combined with alpha-fetoprotein levels according to clinical guidelines; (2) Initial treatment with TACE combined with RFA, with no prior anti-tumor therapy; (3) Child-Pugh class A or B liver function, or class C stabilized after hepatoprotective treatment; (4) Complete clinical data, imaging follow-up data, and baseline blood samples. Exclusion criteria were: (1) Presence of extrahepatic distant metastasis; (2) Concurrent other primary malignancies; (3) Severe non-hepatic diseases with an expected survival of less than 3 months; (4) Incomplete follow-up data or loss to follow-up.

No missing data were present for any of the analyzed variables, as per the inclusion criteria requiring complete clinical, imaging, and laboratory data.

Data collection

Baseline data were collected from the hospital’s electronic medical record system and picture archiving and communication system. Collected data included: (1) Demographics and clinical characteristics: age and sex; (2) Tumor characteristics: maximum tumor diameter, tumor number, BCLC stage, presence of vascular invasion, presence of extrahepatic metastasis, background of liver cirrhosis, and type of viral hepatitis, based on pre-treatment contrast-enhanced computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging; (3) Liver function and routine laboratory parameters: Child-Pugh class, albumin, platelet count, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), and lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR); (4) Serum biomarkers: Peripheral venous blood samples were collected from all patients in the morning after an overnight fast within one week before treatment. Serum TGF-β1 and IL-8 levels were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Peripheral blood T-lymphocyte subsets were analyzed by flow cytometry to calculate the CD4+/CD8 + ratio. Alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) was measured using a chemiluminescence assay.

Grouping, outcome definition, and follow-up

The primary outcome of this study was PFS. All patients were followed regularly starting from the date of initial TACE/RFA treatment through outpatient visits and telephone interviews. Follow-up was conducted every 3 months during the first year post-treatment and every 6 months thereafter. Each follow-up visit included medical history inquiry, physical examination, laboratory tests, and contrast-enhanced abdominal imaging. PFS was defined as the time from the initial treatment date to the first documented instance of radiographic tumor progression or death from any cause. Data from patients who had not experienced an event by the study cutoff date (June 30, 2024) were censored at the date of their last follow-up. Based on their PFS status at the final analysis, patients were categorized into the event group (PFS event occurred) or the non-event group (no PFS event).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0, R version 4.2.3, and Python version 3.8.5. Continuous variables with a normal distribution were expressed as mean±standard deviation and compared between groups using the independent samples t-test. Categorical variables were presented as counts (percentages) [n (%)] and compared using the chi-square test. For prognostic model development and validation, all patients were randomly divided into training and internal validation sets in a 7:3 ratio. In the training set, univariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was initially performed to screen for potential prognostic variables associated with PFS. Variables with a P-value < 0.05 in the univariate analysis were subsequently entered into a multivariate Cox proportional hazards model using stepwise regression (forward/backward) to identify independent prognostic factors and calculate their hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI). A visualized nomogram for predicting 1-year and 3-year PFS probabilities was constructed based on the regression coefficients of the final multivariate model.

To further optimize predictive performance and evaluate different algorithms, machine learning methods were also applied. Using the identified independent prognostic factors as input features, three classification models—Random Forest (RF), K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), and Gradient Boosting (GB)—were constructed in the training set. Model performance was evaluated from three dimensions: (1) Discrimination: Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated for comparison; (2) Calibration: Calibration curves were generated to assess the agreement between predicted probabilities and observed outcomes; (3) Clinical utility: Decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to evaluate the net clinical benefit of the models across different threshold probabilities. Hyperparameters were tuned using ten-fold cross-validation to optimize model performance. To further evaluate model stability and reduce potential overfitting bias from the single‑center retrospective design, bootstrap resampling (1000 iterations) was performed as an additional internal validation approach, with 95% CI. The SHAP framework was employed to analyze the interpretability of the best-performing machine learning model, quantifying the contribution of each feature to the prediction.

Finally, a total prognostic risk score was calculated for each patient based on the nomogram. Patients were stratified into high-risk and low-risk groups according to the median total score (or the optimal cut-off value determined by maximally selected rank statistics). Survival curves for the two groups were generated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and survival differences were compared using the log-rank test. The discriminative ability of this risk stratification model was subsequently validated in the independent validation set. All statistical tests were two-sided, and a P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Baseline characteristics of the training and validation sets

A total of 138 HCC patients treated with TACE/RFA were enrolled and randomly assigned to the training set (n = 97) or the validation set (n = 41) in a 7:3 ratio for model development and internal validation, respectively. As shown in Table 1, no statistically significant differences were observed between the training and validation sets regarding age, sex, tumor diameter, tumor number, BCLC stage, Child-Pugh class, tumor differentiation, vascular invasion, extrahepatic metastasis, hepatitis type, liver cirrhosis, prior treatment history, comorbidities, or any of the serological markers, including TGF-β1, IL-8, and the CD4+/CD8 + ratio (P > 0.05). This indicates a balanced distribution of baseline characteristics, confirming the comparability of the two sets.

Performance and evaluation of machine learning model prediction algorithms

To achieve optimal predictive performance, several machine learning algorithms were compared. ROC curve analysis (Fig. 1) showed that in the training cohort (A), the AUC values for the RF model, the KNN algorithm, and the GB model were 0.846 (95% CI: 0.742–0.949), 0.802 (95% CI: 0.693–0.912), and 0.819 (95% CI: 0.707–0.931), respectively. In the independent validation cohort (B), the corresponding AUC values were 0.791 (95% CI: 0.619–0.964), 0.738 (95% CI: 0.534–0.942), and 0.759 (95% CI: 0.569–0.949). Overall, the RF model demonstrated the highest discriminative ability in both the training and validation cohorts. Bootstrap validation (1000 resamples) yielded a mean AUC of 0.785 (95% CI: 0.612–0.951) for the RF model in the validation set, indicating good internal stability.

Interpretability assessment of model predictions

To gain a deeper understanding of the decision-making mechanism of the best-performing model (RF), the SHAP framework was employed for interpretability analysis. The SHAP feature importance plot (Fig. 4A) quantitatively visualized the overall contribution (i.e., mean absolute SHAP value) of each prognostic feature included in the model to the prediction outcome. The analysis showed that the serum TGF-β1 level was the most important feature influencing the model’s predictions, followed by the CD4+/CD8 + ratio and BCLC stage. This finding aligns with the magnitude of hazard ratios observed in the multivariate Cox regression, confirming that the model captured key biological and clinical information. Furthermore, the SHAP summary plot elucidated the directional relationship between each feature and the predicted risk. For instance, high values of TGF-β1 and IL-8, advanced BCLC stage, and multiple tumors corresponded to positive SHAP values, driving an increased risk score. Conversely, a high CD4+/CD8 + ratio corresponded to a negative SHAP value, driving a decreased risk score. These observations are entirely consistent with the identification of risk and protective factors in the Cox regression analysis. Consequently, an interpretable nomogram based on SHAP values was constructed (Fig. 4B). This tool not only outputs an individualized total risk score but also decomposes this total score, tracing its origin back to the contribution of each specific feature. Thus, it transparently and quantitatively addresses the question of why a particular patient is predicted to be at high risk, substantially enhancing the clinical credibility and usability of the model.

Survival curves stratified by nomogram-based risk

Using the prognostic nomogram constructed from the aforementioned multivariate Cox regression analysis, an individualized total risk score was calculated for each patient. Based on the median score, patients in both the training and validation cohorts were stratified into low-risk and high-risk groups. Kaplan-Meier survival curves (Fig. 5) and Log-rank tests demonstrated that, in both the training (A) and validation (B) cohorts, the progression-free survival rate was significantly better for patients in the low-risk group compared to those in the high-risk group (P < 0.05).

Discussion

This study developed and validated a prognostic nomogram integrating serum TGF-β1, IL-8, and the CD4+/CD8 + ratio in a single-center cohort of 138 patients with HCC who underwent TACE combined with RFA. Multivariate Cox regression analysis identified tumor number, BCLC stage, TGF-β1, IL-8, and the CD4+/CD8 + ratio as independent prognostic factors. The nomogram constructed based on these factors demonstrated robust risk stratification capability in both the training and validation cohorts. Furthermore, a comparison of three machine learning algorithms showed that the RF model exhibited superior discriminative performance, achieving an AUC of 0.846 in the training cohort and 0.791 in the validation cohort. Its calibration curve fitted the ideal line well, and decision curve analysis suggested a clinical net benefit within a threshold probability range of 0.2 to 0.6. SHAP analysis indicated that TGF-β1 was the most important predictive feature in this model, with the direction of each variable’s influence consistent with the Cox regression results.

The five independent prognostic factors identified in this study likely reflect the tumor biology, immune microenvironment remodeling, and systemic immune status associated with HCC, potentially explaining the multidimensional pathological characteristics underlying prognostic heterogeneity in patients post-TACE/RFA. Consistent with previous reports, tumor number and BCLC stage were confirmed as independent risk factors. Multifocal tumors, suggestive of intrahepatic dissemination or multicentric occurrence, have been repeatedly associated with poorer outcomes [11, 12]. BCLC staging, a standard framework for HCC prognosis, classifies stage C patients as having a higher risk of early progression after locoregional therapy due to concomitant vascular invasion or extrahepatic spread. The alignment of our findings with prior studies reinforces the continued value of these traditional indicators in prognostic assessment [13, 14]. Serum TGF-β1 level emerged as a strong risk factor in this study (HR = 1.243) and was the most influential predictive feature in the RF model. TGF-β1 is recognized as a key immunosuppressive and pro-fibrotic cytokine in the tumor microenvironment, potentially facilitating immune evasion by inducing regulatory T cell differentiation and inhibiting CD8 + cytotoxic T lymphocyte activation [15, 16]. Additionally, TGF-β1 can activate downstream Smad signaling pathways, promoting epithelial-mesenchymal transition and enhancing tumor cell migratory capacity [15, 16]. Some studies have also implicated TGF-β1 in remodeling the peritumoral fibrotic microenvironment. Our results suggest that patients with elevated baseline TGF-β1 levels have a relatively higher risk of postoperative recurrence, even in cases with lesions amenable to locoregional therapy. Whether such patients might benefit from anti-TGF-β1 targeted therapies warrants further investigation [17, 18].

Similarly, serum IL-8 level was confirmed as an independent risk factor (HR = 1.092). As a member of the CXC chemokine family, IL-8 is primarily secreted by tumor-associated macrophages and tumor cells within the HCC microenvironment. Its pro-angiogenic role is well-documented, mediated by binding to CXCR1/CXCR2 receptors on endothelial cells and subsequently activating PI3K/Akt and MAPK pathways to promote endothelial cell proliferation and angiogenesis [19, 20]. Recent studies have also suggested that IL-8 may be involved in recruiting myeloid-derived suppressor cells and maintaining the stemness of liver cancer stem cells [21]. In our study, each 10 pg/mL increase in IL-8 corresponded to an approximately 9.2% increase in risk, indicating that individual variations in IL-8 levels, even within the normal range, may hold prognostic value.

Notably, a higher CD4+/CD8 + ratio was the sole independent protective factor identified in this model (HR = 0.559). This ratio serves as a common indicator of adaptive immune function, with a decreased ratio frequently associated with immunosenescence, chronic inflammation, or immune exhaustion [22, 23]. CD4 + helper T cells, particularly the Th1 subset, provide essential co-stimulatory signals—such as IFN-γ and IL-2 secretion—for the activation, expansion, and functional maintenance of CD8 + cytotoxic T lymphocytes, which are the primary effector cells in adaptive antitumor immunity [24]. Consequently, a higher CD4+/CD8 + ratio may signify a relatively robust state of immune surveillance, facilitating the clearance of residual micrometastases or circulating tumor cells after locoregional therapy [25]. These clinical findings provide preliminary evidence for an association between systemic immune status and the long-term efficacy of locoregional treatment. However, the precise mechanisms and the potential of this ratio as a predictive biomarker for immunotherapy response require further elucidation in prospective studies.From a clinical perspective, the CD4+/CD8 + ratio holds promise as an accessible biomarker for guiding individualized post-TACE/RFA management. Patients with a low ratio may have impaired adaptive immunity and thus be at higher risk of early recurrence; they might benefit from more intensive surveillance (e.g., imaging every 2–3 months) or adjunctive immunotherapy. Although not directly assessed in this study, the CD4+/CD8 + ratio may correlate with other immune checkpoint molecules such as PD-L1 expression on tumor cells or regulatory T cell abundance in the tumor microenvironment. Future studies integrating these markers could refine patient selection for combination therapies involving immune checkpoint inhibitors.

Regarding model performance, the RF algorithm demonstrated superior discriminative ability compared to the other two machine learning methods evaluated. Unlike traditional Cox regression, RFs can capture nonlinear relationships and interaction effects among variables to some extent and are less sensitive to multicollinearity. The model maintained an AUC of 0.791 in the validation cohort, suggesting reasonable generalizability. Decision curve analysis indicated that using the RF model to guide intervention decisions might offer a net benefit compared to “treat-all” or “treat-none” strategies within a clinical decision threshold probability range of 0.2 to 0.6. This finding suggests the model’s potential utility in balancing overtreatment and undertreatment. It is important to note that these results are based on our cohort data and require validation for stability across different populations.

The incorporation of SHAP analysis represents an exploratory aspect of this study. As a game theory-based interpretability method, SHAP assigns a contribution value to each feature for every individual prediction, providing insights into the model’s decision-making logic. In this study, SHAP analysis confirmed TGF-β1 as the most important predictive feature globally, with the direction of each feature’s effect aligning consistently with the Cox regression results. The interpretability nomogram constructed from SHAP values offers a visual tool to transition from global to local explanations, potentially aiding clinicians in understanding the basis of model outputs. However, it is crucial to remember that interpretability does not equate to causality; SHAP values reflect statistical associations between features and predictions, not biological causation.

Compared with existing prognostic tools for HCC after TACE/RFA, such as conventional BCLC-based staging or tumor burden score, our model offers several advantages. First, it incorporates serum cytokines (TGF-β1, IL-8) and a systemic immune indicator (CD4+/CD8 + ratio) alongside traditional clinicopathological variables, capturing both tumor biology and host immune status. Second, the Random Forest algorithm improved discriminative ability over standard Cox regression, and SHAP analysis enhanced model transparency. Third, the visualized nomogram and risk stratification facilitate individualized decision-making in clinical practice.

This study has several limitations. First, its single-center retrospective design with a relatively limited sample size, although meeting the minimum event requirement for prediction model development, lacks independent external validation, introducing potential selection bias and overfitting risks. Consequently, the conclusions require validation in multicenter, prospective cohorts. Second, the maximum follow-up period was 48 months, with relatively short follow-up for some patients; median progression-free survival was not yet reached, necessitating reassessment of long-term prognostic performance after extended follow-up. Third, serum biomarkers and immune function indicators were measured only at a single baseline time point, precluding analysis of post-TACE/RFA dynamic changes, which might offer richer prognostic information. Fourth, although this study validated the relative advantage of the RF model within this cohort, sample size limitations prevented the inclusion of more complex architectures like deep learning for comparison. Moreover, we did not analyze the potential confounding effects of basic interventions such as antiviral therapy and hepatoprotective therapy, nor did we perform subgroup analyses based on different etiologies (HBV vs. HCV) or liver function strata (Child-Pugh class), due to the limited sample size and number of events. Thus, the applicability of our model in specific subgroups requires further validation in larger cohorts. Finally, while internally validated, the applicability of this nomogram in real-world settings across different healthcare levels, its ability to alter clinical decisions, and its health economic benefits require further evaluation in prospective interventional studies. In conclusion, this study developed and validated a prognostic nomogram integrating serum TGF-β1, IL-8, the CD4+/CD8 + ratio, and clinicopathological features. The model demonstrated good discrimination, calibration, and clinical net benefit within this cohort. The RF algorithm further enhanced predictive performance, and the SHAP framework provided an initial interpretability visualization tool. This model may serve as a complementary tool to the current BCLC-based clinical pathway. It helps identify HCC patients at higher risk of recurrence or death following TACE/RFA within the same BCLC stage, potentially guiding individualized adjuvant treatment decisions (e.g., whether to add sorafenib or immunotherapy) and intensified surveillance strategies (e.g., shorter imaging intervals). Future research should focus on multicenter external validation, dynamic biomarker monitoring, and prospective interventional trials to further evaluate the model’s clinical utility.

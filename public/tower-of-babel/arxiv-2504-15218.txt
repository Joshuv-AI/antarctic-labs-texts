Non-minimal coupling in light of ACT
Qing Gao, Yungui Gong, Zhu Yi, Fengge Zhang

Abstract
The latest ACT data release disfavors the attractor n s = 1 − 2 / N n_{s}=1-2/N . In inflationary models with nonminimal coupling, such attractors typically arise in the strong coupling limit. To align with observational constraints, we focus on nonminimal coupling models with small coupling constants. For the model with the coupling function Ω ⁡ ( ϕ ) = 1 + ξ ​ f ​ ( ϕ ) \Omega(\phi)=1+\xi f(\phi) and the potential V ⁡ ( ϕ ) = λ 2 ​ f 2 ​ ( ϕ ) V(\phi)=\lambda^{2}f^{2}(\phi) , we find that observational data constrain the parameters as 0.1 ≲ ξ ≲ 35 0.1\lesssim\xi\lesssim 35 and 0 ≲ k ≲ 1.5 0\lesssim k\lesssim 1.5 for f ⁡ ( ϕ ) = ϕ k f(\phi)=\phi^{k} at the 1 ​ σ 1\sigma confidence level. With the help of the nonmiminal coupling Ω ⁡ ( ϕ ) = 1 + ξ ​ ϕ 2 \Omega(\phi)=1+\xi\phi^{2} , the hilltop inflation and power-law inflation models with power indices 2 / 3 2/3 and 1 / 3 1/3 can be consistent with observational data within the 1 ​ σ 1\sigma range. We also give the viable parameter regions for ξ \xi for these three models.



I
introduction

Inflation elegantly resolves several fundamental problems in the standard big bang cosmology, including the flatness, horizon, and monopole problems, while simultaneously providing the initial conditions for the formation of large-scale structure in the universe
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
. Quantum fluctuations of the inflaton field generate metric perturbations, which leave characteristic imprints as temperature anisotropies in the cosmic microwave background (CMB).

The inflationary attractor
n_{s}=1-2/N
for a broad class of inflationary models,
such as hilltop inflation
[
5
]
, T model
[
6
]
, E model
[
7
]
,
R^{2}
inflation
[
1
]
and Higgs inflation with nonminimal coupling
\xi\phi^{2}R
in the strong coupling regime (
\xi\gg 1
)
[
8
,
9
]
,
lies near the central value
0.9649
of the
Planck
2018 measurements on CMB
[
10
]
for
N=60
,
where
N
denotes the number of
e
-folds before the end of inflation.

Besides
R^{2}
inflation and Higgs inflation with nonminimal coupling, the so-called universal attractor
n_{s}=1-2/N
can also be obtained in a more general nonminimal coupling model.
In this model, the coupling takes the form
\xi f(\phi)R
and the inflation potential is
\lambda^{2}f^{2}(\phi)
for arbitrary
f(\phi)
in the strong coupling limit
[
11
]
.
Choosing
f(\phi)=\phi^{2}
recovers the Higgs inflation scenario. For these non-minimal coupling inflation models, the universal attractor
n_{s}=1-2/N
arises under the strong coupling condition.

Recently, new data from the Atacama Cosmology Telescope (ACT) have been released
[
12
,
13
]
,
indicating a higher value of
n_{s}
compared to previous
Planck
constraints.
A joint analysis of
Planck
and ACT data (P-ACT) yields
n_{s}=0.9709\pm 0.0038

[
12
]
.
By incorporating additional data from CMB lensing and the Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO) data measured by the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI)
[
14
,
15
]
(P-ACT-LB),
the value further increases to
n_{s}=0.9743\pm 0.0034

[
12
]
,
deviating from the original Planck result by about
2\sigma
.
Notably, the attractor
n_{s}=1-2/N

is disfavored by this latest P-ACT-LB constraint, and located at the regions of constraints
2\sigma

[
13
]
.

To reconcile the wide classes of inflationary attractors with current observations,
it is necessary to consider nonminimal coupling inflationary models where the attractors are not reached or
the strong coupling limit is relaxed.
For instance, with a value of
\xi=1
and
f(\phi)=\phi
, the predicted value becomes
n_{s}=1-2/(3N)
, which aligns with the new P-ACT-LB constraints for
N=60

[
16
]
.
For additional approaches to address the implications of the P-ACT-LB results, see Ref.
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
.

Since a nonminimal coupling helps to reduce the value of the tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
, in this paper, we investigate inflationary models with nonminimal coupling where the coupling constant is small,
focusing on their ability to account for the latest P-ACT-LB constraints on the scalar spectral index.
The paper is organized as follows.
In Sec. II, we discuss nonminimal inflationary models under the strong limit condition, i.e., the universal attractor.
The nonminimal inflationary models in the general case is discussed in Sec. III. The conclusions are drawn in Sec. IV.
We set the unit by
M_{pl}=(8\pi G)^{-1}=c=1
, where
M_{pl}
is the reduced Planck mass and
c
is the speed of light.

II
Nonminimal Inflation Model: Strong Coupling Limit

The action for a general scalar-tensor inflation model in Jordan frame is given by

S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-\tilde{g}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\Omega(\phi)\tilde{R}(\tilde{g})-\frac{1}{2}\omega(\phi)\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi-V_{J}(\phi)\right],

(1)

where
\Omega(\phi)
and
\omega(\phi)
are coupling functions, and
V_{J}(\phi)
is the potential. In this work, we restrict to a canonical kinetic term, i.e.,
\omega(\phi)=1
. Applying the following conformal transformations,

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\Omega(\phi)\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu},

(2)

\displaystyle d\psi^{2}=\left[\frac{3}{2}\frac{(d\Omega/d\phi)^{2}}{\Omega^{2}(\phi)}+\frac{\omega(\phi)}{\Omega(\phi)}\right]d\phi^{2},

(3)

the action (
1
) is brought into Einstein frame:

S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{2}R(g)-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\psi\nabla_{\nu}\psi-U(\psi)\right],

(4)

where the potential in Einstein frame is

U(\psi)=\frac{V_{J}(\phi)}{\Omega^{2}(\phi)}.

(5)

In the strong coupling limit,

\frac{3}{2}\frac{[d\Omega(\phi)/d\phi]^{2}}{\Omega^{2}(\phi)}\gg\frac{\omega(\phi)}{\Omega(\phi)},

(6)

we get

\psi\approx\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\,\ln\Omega(\phi),

(7)

and

U(\psi)\approx V_{J}(\phi)\exp\left(-\sqrt{8/3}\,\psi\right).

(8)

For the Higgs potential,

V_{J}(\phi)=\frac{\lambda}{4}\phi^{4},

(9)

with minimal coupling,
\Omega(\phi)=1
,
the predicted tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
is too large to be consistent with observational data. To obtain a smaller
r
,
nonminimal coupling is considered. With
\Omega(\phi)=1+\xi\phi^{2}
and the Higgs potential (
9
),
the so-called Higgs inflation model
[
9
]
is described by

S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-\tilde{g}}\left[\frac{1}{2}(1+\xi\phi^{2})\tilde{R}(\tilde{g})-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi-\frac{\lambda}{4}\phi^{4}\right].

(10)

In the strong coupling limit,

\phi\gg 1/\sqrt{\xi},

(11)

and
\xi\gg 1/6
,
the potential in Einstein frame becomes

U(\psi)=\frac{\lambda}{4\xi^{2}}\left[1+\exp(-2\psi/\sqrt{6})\right]^{-2},

(12)

and the predictions for the scalar spectral index and tensor-to-scalar ratio are

n_{s}-1=-\frac{2}{N},\quad r=\frac{12}{N^{2}}.

(13)

The potential (
12
) coincides with that of the Starobinsky model
[
1
]
in Einstein frame. For
N=60
, the predictions become

n_{s}=0.967,\quad r=0.003,

(14)

which are compatible with the
1\sigma
confidence region from
Planck
2018 data,
but are disfavored by the
1\sigma
regions of the recent P-ACT-LB results.

The Higgs inflation model (
10
) is a special case of the universal attractor model
[
11
]
, in which the coupling function and potential are

\Omega(\phi)=1+\xi f(\phi),\quad V_{J}(\phi)=\lambda^{2}f^{2}(\phi).

(15)

By choosing
f(\phi)=\phi^{2}
, Higgs inflation is recovered. Under the strong coupling limit,

\Omega(\phi)\ll\frac{3}{2}\Omega^{\prime}(\phi)^{2},

(16)

for general
f(\phi)
, the Einstein frame potential reduces to the universal attractor form, yielding the predictions of Eq. (
13
). As with the Higgs and Starobinsky models, these predictions are now disfavored by the latest observational data.

Thus, to remain consistent with current observations, the strong coupling limit must be relaxed.

III
Nonminimal coupling models

When the strong coupling condition is not imposed, the predictions for
n_{s}
and
r
in the universal attractor model (
15
) depend on both the coupling parameter
\xi
and the form of
f(\phi)
.
It is therefore possible to achieve consistency with the P-ACT-LB data for small coupling value of
\xi
.

III.1
General coupling function

To examine the general behavior of non-minimal coupling inflation models (
15
),
we consider the power-law form,

f(\phi)=\phi^{k}

(17)

with parameters
\xi>0
and
k>0
, where
k
is not restricted to integer values.
For general
k
and
\xi
, analytic expressions for
n_{s}
and
r
are difficult to obtain,
so we compute these observables numerically with slow-roll approximation.
In the slow-roll approximation, the scalar spectral index and tensor-to-scalar ratio can be evaluated in the Einstein frame as follows,

n_{s}=1-6\epsilon_{U}(\psi)+2\eta_{U}(\psi),\quad r=16\epsilon_{U}(\psi),

(18)

where the slow-roll parameters are defined by

\epsilon_{U}(\psi)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{U^{\prime}(\psi)}{U(\psi)}\right)^{2},\quad\eta_{U}(\psi)=\frac{U^{\prime\prime}(\psi)}{U(\psi)},

(19)

with
U^{\prime}(\psi)=dU(\psi)/d\psi
and
U^{\prime\prime}(\psi)=d^{2}U(\psi)/d\psi^{2}
.
The values of scalar field at the end of inflation
\psi_{e}
and at the horizon exit
\psi_{*}
are determined by the conditions

\epsilon_{U}(\psi_{e})=1,\quad N=-\int_{\psi_{*}}^{\psi_{e}}\frac{U(\psi)}{U^{\prime}(\psi)}d\psi.

(20)

Due to the complexity in obtaining the functional relation between
\psi
and original
\phi
, the explicit form of the potential
U(\psi)
in Einstein frame is generally difficult to derive. To numerically compute the scalar spectral index and the tensor-to-scalar ratio, we recast Eq. (
18
) in terms of the scalar field
\phi
and potential
V_{J}(\phi)
in the Jordan frame, using the conformal transformation relations (
3
) and (
5
). By adopting the power-law form (
17
), the slow-roll parameters become

\epsilon_{U}[\psi(\phi)]=\frac{4k^{2}}{3k^{2}\xi^{2}\phi^{2k}+2\xi\phi^{k+2}+2\phi^{2}},

(21)

\displaystyle\eta_{U}[\psi(\phi)]=

\displaystyle-\frac{4k\left(3k^{3}\xi^{3}\phi^{3k}+(k+2)\xi^{2}\phi^{2k+2}+(2-4k)\phi^{2}\right)}{\left(3k^{2}\xi^{2}\phi^{2k}+2\xi\phi^{k+2}+2\phi^{2}\right)^{2}}

(22)

\displaystyle+\frac{4k\xi\phi^{k}\left(3k^{3}\xi\phi^{k}+(3k-4)\phi^{2}\right)}{\left(3k^{2}\xi^{2}\phi^{2k}+2\xi\phi^{k+2}+2\phi^{2}\right)^{2}}.

The corresponding field value at horizon exit
\phi_{*}
is determined by the number of
e
-folds:

N=\left.\frac{3k\xi\phi^{k}-3k\log\left(\xi\phi^{k}+1\right)+\phi^{2}}{4k}\right|_{\phi_{e}}^{\phi_{*}},

(23)

where
\phi_{e}
is determined by the condition
\epsilon_{U}[\psi(\phi_{e})]=1
.
By using Eqs. (
21
) - (
23
), and setting
N=60
, the scalar spectral index and tensor-to-scalar ratio can be numerically calculated. The results are shown in Fig.
1
.
The left panel displays the predictions for
n_{s}
and
r
,
while the right panel shows the corresponding values of
\xi
and
k
.
The regions with red and blue points are the constraints from the
1\sigma
and
2\sigma
of the observational data, respectively.
To be consistent with the
1\sigma
constraint of the observational data,
the coupling constant
\xi
and the coupling function index
k
should satisfy

0.1\lesssim\xi\lesssim 35,\quad 0\lesssim k\lesssim 1.5,

(24)

therefore the Higgs inflation, where
k=2
, is ruled out by the
1\sigma
observational data.

(Left) Scalar spectral index
n_{s}
and tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
predicted by the nonminimal coupling inflation model for various values of
k

\xi
, compared with observational constraints. The contours are the
1\sigma

\xi

k
consistent with observational bounds. The red and blue color region are the constraints from
1\sigma
and
2\sigma
observational constraints, respectively.

III.2
The nonminimal coupling with
\xi\phi^{2}

In this subsection,
we consider the hilltop inflation and power-law inflation with commonly discussed nonminimal coupling
\Omega(\phi)=1+\xi\phi^{2}
and small coupling constant
\xi\ll 1
.
We numerically solve the background and perturbation equations without employing the slow-roll approximation,
and the numerical results of
n_{s}
and
r
are shown in Fig.

For the hilltop inflation, the potential is

V(\phi)=V_{0}\left[1-\left(\frac{\phi}{\mu}\right)^{p}\right],

(25)

we choose
p=2
and
\mu=50

As shown in Fig.

IV
conclusion

The latest observational data from the Atacama Cosmology Telescope have provided new constraints on the scalar spectral index
n_{s}
.
The P-ACT-LB results derive a higher value of
n_{s}
compared to previous
Planck
results.
This shift in
n_{s}
imposes a big challenge on the parameterization of the scalar spectral index,
n_{s}=1-2/N
,
which is a universal attractor for a wide range of inflationary models, such as hilltop inflation, T model, E model,
the Starobinsky model, and inflationary models with a nonminimal coupling
\Omega(\phi)=1+\xi f(\phi)
and the potential
V_{J}(\phi)=\lambda f(\phi)^{2}
under the strong coupling limit condition
\Omega(\phi)\ll 3\Omega^{\prime}(\phi)^{2}/2
.

However, the attractors with nonminimal coupling are achieved in the strong coupling limit.
If the coupling constant is small, the attractors are not reached, it is still possible to satisfy the recent P-ACT-LB constraints because a nonminimal coupling helps to reduce the tensor-to-scalar ratio.
We find that with small coupling constant, the predictions for the scalar spectral index
n_{s}
and tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
in inflationary models with nonminimal coupling can still be consistent with the latest P-ACT-LB data at the
1\sigma
level.
We consider the general coupling function
\Omega(\phi)=1+\xi f(\phi)
and the potential
V_{J}(\phi)=\lambda f^{2}(\phi)
.
For the monomial case with
f(\phi)=\phi^{k}
, to satisfy the
1\sigma
region of the observational data,
the parameter values of
\xi
and
k
should satisfy
0.1\lesssim\xi\lesssim 35
and
0\lesssim k\lesssim 1.5
.

We also consider the commonly used nonminimal coupling
\Omega(\phi)=1+\xi\phi^{2}
.
For the hilltop inflation, the P-ACT-LB-BK18
1\sigma
results give the constraint
-0.0003<\xi<-0.0002
for
N=60
.
For the power-law potential with the power index
2/3
, the P-ACT-LB-BK18
1\sigma
results give the constraint
0.0005<\xi<0.00073
for
N=60
.
For the power-law potential with the power index
1/3
, we find that the P-ACT-LB-BK18
1\sigma
contour gives the constraint

-0.00012<\xi<0.00063
and
0.00014<\xi<0.001
for
N=50
and
N=60
, respectively.

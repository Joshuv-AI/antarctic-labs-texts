Transoesophageal bronchoscopic ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration (EUS-B-FNA) for left adrenal gland (LAG) sampling: A report of three cases with a review of the literature
Authors: Rohit Vadala, Saurabh Mittal, Aruna Nambirajan, Vijay Hadda, Anant Mohan, Randeep Guleria, Karan Madan
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Endobronchial ultrasound-guided transbronchial needle aspiration (EBUS-TBNA) is a standard-of-care modality for evaluating mediastinal lymph nodes and masses. The EBUS bronchoscope may also be introduced through the oesophageal route to perform sampling of accessible lesions, a technique described as transoesophageal bronchoscopic ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration (EUS-B-FNA). Because of the central oesophageal approach, EUS-B-FNA provides easy access to the left para-tracheal, subcarinal and para-oesophageal lymph nodes. In addition, the left adrenal gland (LAG) can also be imaged and sampled during the EUS-B-FNA procedure. In patients with suspected lung cancer, accurate staging is essential. Adrenal metastasis is relatively common and may often be a solitary metastatic site. We describe three cases where EUS-B-FNA was performed to safely sample the enlarged LAG in suspected lung cancer. We also review the literature on the performance characteristics of EUS-B-FNA for LAG aspiration.

INTRODUCTION

Endobronchial ultrasound-guided transbronchial needle aspiration (EBUS-TBNA) is the preferred initial diagnosis and staging modality in most patients with lung cancer and for the evaluation of undiagnosed mediastinal lymphadenopathy.[1] It reduces the time for decision-making and leads to significantly fewer investigations during the diagnosis and management course of lung cancer.[2] Combined tracheal (EBUS-TBNA) and oesophageal (transoesophageal bronchoscopic ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration (EUS-B-FNA)) use of the EBUS bronchoscope may allow a complete endosonographic evaluation leading to accurate staging of mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes in patients with lung cancer.[3]

Adrenal gland involvement in lung cancer is common. The sensitivity of computed tomography (CT) scanning for malignant adrenal involvement is poor. Although positron emission tomography (PET) scans may improve diagnostic sensitivity, false positives may be seen.[4] Therefore, obtaining a diagnostic tissue aspirate from the adrenal gland may be required to exclude metastasis in lung cancer. Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) performed by gastroenterologists allows access and the ability to aspirate the left adrenal gland (LAG) via a trans-gastric puncture. Guidelines recommend EUS-FNA to exclude metastasis from LAG in patients undergoing endosonographic evaluation.[5] Studies have reported that an EBUS bronchoscope (EUS-B-FNA) transoesophageal insertion to perform a LAG aspiration may have a similar diagnostic sensitivity as EUS-FNA.[6]

A recent case report from India described the performance of EUS-B-FNA for LAG aspiration in patients with lung cancer.[7] We describe three case studies where EUS-B-FNA was used to perform LAG aspiration successfully.

CASE REPORT

Case 1

A 48-year-old man, cook and reformed chronic smoker, presented with complaints of low backache for two months. He had diabetes for five years on regular treatment. There were no other co-morbidities or significant medical history. The patient also complained of a non-productive cough, loss of weight and loss of appetite. A general examination revealed bilateral grade 3 clubbing. A CT of the thorax and abdomen with contrast enhancement showed a left lower lobe lung mass with mediastinal lymphadenopathy [Figure 1]. There was evidence of a left adrenal mass indenting the left kidney. A EUS-B-centred approach was planned for the fine-needle aspiration cytology of LAG. Following LAG aspiration, the subcarinal lymph node was sampled after further withdrawing the scope cranially [Figure 1]. Rapid on-site evaluation by a pulmonologist (P-ROSE) showed adequate cellular material. The LAG and subcarinal lymph node cytology report confirmed metastasis from small cell carcinoma. There were no post-procedural complications.

Case 2

A 71-year-old female, the current smoker, presented with complaints of right-sided chest pain, productive cough and progressive breathlessness for three months. Co-morbidities included coronary artery disease with severe left ventricular dysfunction for three years. There was a history of significant loss of weight and appetite. CT scan of the thorax and abdomen showed a right hilar mass abutting the superior vena cava, right main bronchus and right pulmonary artery with mediastinal lymphadenopathy at the lower right para-tracheal and right hilar stations [Figure 2]. In addition, a left adrenal mass was seen, extending to the para-aortic region and infiltrating the left renal pelvis with a loss of fat planes with the adjacent left kidney. EUS-B-FNA from the LAG was performed under moderate sedation. Subsequently, EBUS-TBNA was performed from the lower right para-tracheal lymph node station. The cytology from the LAG was negative for malignancy; however, the cytology from the right lower para-tracheal lymph node confirmed metastasis from squamous cell carcinoma. Immunohistochemistry for p40 was positive in tumour cells, while the tumour cells were negative for thyroid transcription factor 1 (TTF-1). There were no procedural complications.

Case 3

A 62-year-old male chronic smoker complained of left-sided chest pain, dry cough and progressive breathlessness for two months. CT scan of the thorax showed a left upper lobe lung mass with mediastinal lymphadenopathy at the left lower para-tracheal and subcarinal lymph node stations and LAG enlargement [Figure 3]. EUS-B-FNA was performed under moderate sedation guided by the LAG. Cytology from the LAG confirmed metastasis from a non-small cell carcinoma. Immunohistochemistry on patient-matched biopsy showed positivity for p40 in tumour cells and negativity for TTF-1 confirming squamous cell carcinoma. There were no post-procedural complications.

The technique of EUS-B-FNA for LAG aspiration

Under moderate conscious sedation (a combination of midazolam and fentanyl), with the patient in the supine position, the EBUS bronchoscope was inserted orally. Topical anaesthesia included five sprays of 10% lignocaine to the pharynx and 1% lignocaine solution for spray-as-you-go administration. Following oral insertion, the bronchoscope was gently negotiated posterior to the glottis, and the patient was encouraged to swallow. Following oesophageal entry, further negotiation was based on the ultrasound landmarks. As described previously, the scope was initially used to visualise the liver. The bronchoscope was then turned to the left side to image the LAG [Figure 4]. Aspiration was then performed through a trans-gastric puncture into the LAG. A minimum of three aspirates were obtained if ROSE was inconclusive.

DISCUSSION

Oesophageal ultrasound with ultrasound bronchoscope (EUS-B) describes the oesophageal introduction of the EBUS bronchoscope to visualise and perform aspiration from mediastinal and para-oesophageal lesions. The technique of fine-needle aspiration using the EUS-B approach is denoted as EUS-B-FNA.[8] The overall diagnostic yield of EUS-B-FNA combined with EBUS-TBNA is greater than that of EBUS-TBNA alone.[9] For mediastinal lesions located at the lower left para-tracheal or subcarinal location, EUS-B-FNA compared with EBUS-TBNA is associated with greater patient comfort and a similar diagnostic yield in undiagnosed mediastinal lymphadenopathy.[10] EUS-B-FNA has also revolutionised the approach towards evaluating mediastinal lymphadenopathy in children.[1112]

Table 1 summarises various studies on EUS-B-FNA for LAG sampling. Because of its proximity to the stomach, the transoesophageal approach provides access to the LAG. The use of EUS-B-FNA to sample the LAG was initially described by Meena et al. in 2015.[13] The adrenal gland is one of the common sites for metastasis from lung cancer. Benign adrenal adenomas are common, and radiology may not be able to distinguish them from metastasis; therefore, histological confirmation is essential for staging and prognosis. The adrenal gland may often be a solitary site of distant metastasis. Therefore, accurate confirmation of malignant involvement may avoid unnecessary surgery and improve clinical outcomes. Conventional oesophageal ultrasound-guided EUS-FNA has usually been preferred for sampling the LAG as it is a minimally invasive, safe and accurate procedure.[5] However, a study by Crombag et al.[6] found that EUS-B-FNA had a similar high success rate in comparison with conventional EUS. There were no significant complications with EUS-B for LAG. Similarly, in another large cohort study by Christiansen et al.,[14] the authors concluded that EUS-B is a relatively safe and efficacious procedure (87% sampling adequacy) to sample the LAG in lung cancer patients.

Various studies on EUS-B FNA for LAG sampling

These results encouraged us to use this novel technique using a single EBUS scope approach for mediastinal nodal and LAG staging in lung cancer patients. The procedure for assessing the LAG on EUS-B was performed under conscious sedation. The EBUS bronchoscope for EUS-B is inserted from the mouth into the oesophagus. A nasal approach may also be taken for EUS-B-FNA. The endoscopic vision during EUS-B is poor; therefore, a thorough knowledge of ultrasound anatomy is required. After the negotiation of the gastro-oesophageal sphincter, the liver is visible. The scope is then rotated to the left to identify the LAG.[15] The normal adrenal is seen like a seagull, and a hypoechoic area inside may suggest metastasis. Doppler assessment should be done cautiously to prevent adrenal haemorrhage. However, the seagull's appearance may be lost due to metastasis.[13] Rapid on-site evaluation (ROSE) may help confirm sample adequacy and reduce the number of passes. The other trans-diaphragmatic structures that can be accessed by EUS-B apart from the LAG include celiac axis nodes and the liver. A recent prospective trial has shown that EUS-B of the LAG could be integrated into the usual lung cancer staging workup, although the guidelines do not recommend this yet.[16] There are a few case reports and case series published till date, which have shown that LAG can be accurately and safely accessed by EUS-B.[171819]

In conclusion, LAG sampling in lung cancer patients can be safely performed using oesophageal ultrasound with a EUS-B. This novel technique is helpful and allows for appropriate staging and diagnosis of lung cancer in the same setting. It must be performed only by trained interventional pulmonologists in carefully selected cases.

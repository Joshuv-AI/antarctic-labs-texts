Abstract
In the S ​ O ​ ( 5 ) × U ⁡ ( 1 ) × S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SO(5)\times U(1)\times SU(3) gauge-Higgs unification in the Randall-Sundrum warped space the mixing in the W W couplings in the quark sector is induced by masses of S ​ O ​ ( 5 ) SO(5) singlet fermions which are responsible for splitting masses of down-type quarks from those of up-type quarks in each generation. We show that the observed Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) matrix in the W W couplings is reproduced within experimental errors. Further flavor-changing neutral currents (FCNCs) in the quark sector are shown to be naturally suppressed.

Introduction

Although the standard model (SM) of
SU(3)_{C}\times SU(2)_{L}\times U(1)_{Y}
gauge theory has been
successful in describing phenomena at low energies, it has a gauge hierarchy problem
when embedded in a larger theory such as grand unification.
As one way for solving this problem the gauge-Higgs unification (GHU) scenario has been proposed in which
gauge symmetry is dynamically broken by an Aharonov-Bohm (AB) phase,
\theta_{H}
, in the fifth dimension.
The 4D Higgs boson is nothing but a 4D fluctuation mode of
\theta_{H}
.
A finite mass of the Higgs boson is dynamically generated by quantum effects of the AB phase
\theta_{H}

The current paper is devoted to solving this flavor problem in the GUT inspired GHU.
In the SM quark masses are generated by Yukawa couplings of quarks to the Higgs boson.
With general Yukawa couplings mass eigenstates are rotated from gauge eigenstates, which leads to
the nontrivial CKM matrix. In GHU the
SU(2)_{L}\times U(1)_{Y}
gauge symmetry is dynamically
broken to
U(1)_{EM}
by the Hosotani mechanism so that couplings of quarks to the 4D Higgs boson
are controlled by the gauge principle. One needs to explain how quark mass eigenstates deviate
from gauge eigenstates in GHU.
Previously an attempt has been made in ref.
[
23
]
to derive the CKM matrix
by introducing flavor mixing on the UV brane in the GUT inspired GHU.
Although the mixing in the
W

a realistic CKM matrix could not be obtained there. In this paper we show that the observed
CKM matrix is reproduced within experimental errors by incorporating general masses of
SO(5)

singlet fermions which are responsible for splitting masses of down-type quarks from those of
up-type quarks in the GUT inspired GHU. It is also seen that flavor-changing neutral currents (FCNCs)
are naturally suppressed.

Summary

In this paper we have examined the flavor problem in the quark sector in the GUT inspired

SO(5)\times U(1)_{X}\times SU(3)_{C}
GHU in the RS space.
Masses of down-type quarks are split from those of up-type quarks by
SO(5)
singlet fermion multiplets

\Psi_{({\bf 3,1})}^{\pm\alpha}
which naturally appear in gauge-Higgs grand unification models.
Fields
\Psi_{({\bf 3,1})}^{\pm\alpha}
are important to suppress
W
couplings of right-handed components
of quarks almost entirely as well.
General masses of those
SO(5)
singlet fermion multiplets
induce the mixing in the
W
couplings of quarks. The observed CKM matrix in the
W
couplings is
reproduced within experimental errors.
Furthermore we have evaluated flavor-changing
Z
couplings at the tree level to find to be extremely small.
FCNCs are naturally suppressed as a result of remarkable cancellations among the coefficient
functions
c^{\beta\,(j)}_{\alpha^{(n)}}
manifested by the relations in Eqs. (
3.48
) and (
3.51
).

Acceptable phenomenological values for the
W
and
Z
couplings of quarks are obtained
for
\theta_{H}\lesssim 0.1
. As has been shown in ref.
[
34
]
, the value of the
W
boson mass,
m_{W}
,
sensitively depends on
\theta_{H}
. Precise measurements of
m_{W}
will help to narrow the allowed
range of
\theta_{H}
.

The mixing in the neutrino sector in GHU needs to be clarified as a next problem. Majorana masses on the UV brane
in the GUT inspired
SO(5)\times U(1)_{X}\times SU(3)_{C}
GHU are expected to play a crucial role in this regard.

Appendix A
Basis functions

We summarize basis functions used for wave functions of gauge and fermion fields.
For gauge fields we introduce

\displaystyle F_{\alpha,\beta}(u,v)

\displaystyle\equiv J_{\alpha}(u)Y_{\beta}(v)-Y_{\alpha}(u)J_{\beta}(v)~,

(A.1)

\displaystyle C(z;\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}\lambda zz_{L}F_{1,0}(\lambda z,\lambda z_{L})~,

(A.2)

\displaystyle S(z;\lambda,)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi}{2}\lambda zF_{1,1}(\lambda z,\lambda z_{L})~,

(A.3)

\displaystyle C^{\prime}(z;\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}\lambda^{2}zz_{L}F_{0,0}(\lambda z,\lambda z_{L})~,

(A.4)

\displaystyle S^{\prime}(z;\lambda)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi}{2}\lambda^{2}zF_{0,1}(\lambda z,\lambda z_{L})~,

(A.5)

where
J_{\alpha}(u)
and
Y_{\alpha}(u)
are Bessel functions of the first and second kind.
They satisfy

\displaystyle-z\frac{d}{dz}\frac{1}{z}\frac{d}{dz}\begin{pmatrix}C\cr S\end{pmatrix}=\lambda^{2}\begin{pmatrix}C\cr S\end{pmatrix}~,

(A.6)

with the boundary conditions
C=z_{L}
,
C^{\prime}=S=0
and
S^{\prime}=\lambda
at
z=z_{L}
,
and a relation
CS^{\prime}-SC^{\prime}=\lambda z
holds.

For fermion fields with a bulk mass parameter
c
, we define

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}C_{L}\cr S_{L}\end{pmatrix}(z;\lambda,c)

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{\pi}{2}\lambda\sqrt{zz_{L}}F_{c+\frac{1}{2},c\mp\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda z,\lambda z_{L})~,

(A.7)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}C_{R}\cr S_{R}\end{pmatrix}(z;\lambda,c)

\displaystyle=\mp\frac{\pi}{2}\lambda\sqrt{zz_{L}}F_{c-\frac{1}{2},c\pm\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda z,\lambda z_{L})~,

(A.8)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\check{S}_{L}\cr\check{C}_{R}\end{pmatrix}(z;\lambda,c)

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{L}(1;\lambda,c)}{S_{L}(1;\lambda,c)}\begin{pmatrix}S_{L}\cr C_{R}\end{pmatrix}(z;\lambda,c)~.

(A.9)

These functions satisfy

\displaystyle D_{+}(c)\begin{pmatrix}C_{L}\cr S_{L}\end{pmatrix}=\lambda\begin{pmatrix}S_{R}\cr C_{R}\end{pmatrix},

(A.10)

\displaystyle D_{-}(c)\begin{pmatrix}S_{R}\cr C_{R}\end{pmatrix}=\lambda\begin{pmatrix}C_{L}\cr S_{L}\end{pmatrix},~~D_{\pm}(c)=\pm\frac{d}{dz}+\frac{c}{z}~,

(A.11)

with the boundary conditions
C_{R/L}=1
,
D_{-}(c)C_{R}=D_{+}(c)C_{L}=S_{R/L}=0
at
z=z_{L}
, and

C_{L}C_{R}-S_{L}S_{R}=1
.

For fermion fields with a vector-like mass
m=k\tilde{m}
, mode functions are expressed
in terms of

\displaystyle{\cal C}_{L/R\,1}(z;\lambda,c,\tilde{m})

\displaystyle=C_{L/R}(z;\lambda,c+\tilde{m})+C_{L/R}(z;\lambda,c-\tilde{m})~,

(A.12)

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{L/R\,1}(z;\lambda,c,\tilde{m})

\displaystyle=S_{L/R}(z;\lambda,c+\tilde{m})+S_{L/R}(z;\lambda,c-\tilde{m})~,

(A.13)

\displaystyle{\cal C}_{L/R\,2}(z;\lambda,c,\tilde{m})

\displaystyle=S_{L/R}(z;\lambda,c+\tilde{m})-S_{L/R}(z;\lambda,c-\tilde{m})~,

(A.14)

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{L/R\,2}(z;\lambda,c,\tilde{m})

\displaystyle=C_{L/R}(z;\lambda,c+\tilde{m})-C_{L/R}(z;\lambda,c-\tilde{m})~.

(A.15)

These functions satisfy

\displaystyle D_{+}(c)\begin{pmatrix}{\cal C}_{L1}\cr{\cal S}_{L2}\end{pmatrix}+\frac{\tilde{m}}{z}\begin{pmatrix}{\cal S}_{L2}\cr{\cal C}_{L1}\end{pmatrix}=\lambda\begin{pmatrix}{\cal S}_{R1}\cr{\cal C}_{R2}\end{pmatrix},

(A.16)

\displaystyle D_{-}(c)\begin{pmatrix}{\cal S}_{R1}\cr{\cal C}_{R2}\end{pmatrix}+\frac{\tilde{m}}{z}\begin{pmatrix}{\cal C}_{R2}\cr{\cal S}_{R1}\end{pmatrix}=\lambda\begin{pmatrix}{\cal C}_{L1}\cr{\cal S}_{L2}\end{pmatrix},

(A.17)

\displaystyle D_{+}(c)\begin{pmatrix}{\cal S}_{L1}\cr{\cal C}_{L2}\end{pmatrix}+\frac{\tilde{m}}{z}\begin{pmatrix}{\cal C}_{L2}\cr{\cal S}_{L1}\end{pmatrix}=\lambda\begin{pmatrix}{\cal C}_{R1}\cr{\cal S}_{R2}\end{pmatrix},

(A.18)

\displaystyle D_{-}(c)\begin{pmatrix}{\cal C}_{R1}\cr{\cal S}_{R2}\end{pmatrix}+\frac{\tilde{m}}{z}\begin{pmatrix}{\cal S}_{R2}\cr{\cal C}_{R1}\end{pmatrix}=\lambda\begin{pmatrix}{\cal S}_{L1}\cr{\cal C}_{L2}\end{pmatrix},

(A.19)

and
{\cal S}_{Rj}={\cal S}_{Lj}=D_{-}(c)\,{\cal C}_{Rj}=D_{+}(c)\,{\cal C}_{Lj}=0
at
z=z_{L}
.

In evaluating

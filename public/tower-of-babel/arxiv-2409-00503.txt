Non-negative Sparse Recovery at Minimal Sampling Rate
Hendrik Bernd Zarucha, Peter Jung

Abstract
It is known that sparse recovery is possible if the number of measurements is in the order of the sparsity, but the corresponding decoders either lack polynomial decoding time or robustness to noise. Commonly, decoders that rely on a null space property are being used. These achieve polynomial time decoding and are robust to additive noise but pay the price by requiring more measurements. The non-negative least residual has been established as such a decoder for non-negative recovery. A new equivalent condition for uniform, robust recovery of non-negative sparse vectors with the non-negative least residual that is not based on null space properties is introduced. It is shown that the number of measurements for this equivalent condition only needs to be in the order of the sparsity. Further, it is explained why the robustness to additive noise is similar, but not equal, to the robustness of decoders based on null space properties.

Robustness and Recovery Conditions

In this work
C\subset\mathbb{K}^{N}
is a set of vectors
that are supposed to be recoverable from as few as possible noisy, linear measurements

\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{e}
. Given some norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}
a simple decoding
approach would try to solve
\underset{\mathbf{z}\in C}{\textnormal{argmin}}\hskip 1.0pt\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{y}\right\|_{R}
.
For a possibly larger, closed set of candidates
F
with
C\subset F\subset\mathbb{K}^{N}

one can try to solve the relaxed problem

\mathbf{x}^{\#}\in\underset{\mathbf{z}\in F}{\textnormal{argmin}}\hskip 1.0pt\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{y}\right\|_{R}
instead.
This might be easier if for instance

F
is convex but
C
is of a combinatorial nature.
This problem formulation can potentially cover uniform recovery guarantees
from the following cases.

•

The Kruskal Rank case:

C=\Sigma_{S}^{N}
and
F=\Sigma_{S}^{N}
.

•

The Signed Kernel Condition case:

C=\Sigma_{S}^{N}\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
and
F=\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
.
This will be introduced later.

•

The Null Space Property case:

C=\eta\left(\Sigma_{S}^{N}\cap\mathbb{S}^{N-1}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}}\right)

and
F=\eta\mathbb{B}^{N}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}}
,
where
\eta>0
.

•

The Outwardly Neighborly Polytope case:

C=\eta\left(\Sigma_{S}^{N}\cap\mathbb{S}^{N-1}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}}\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\right)

and
F=\eta\left(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}}\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\right)
,
where
\eta>0
.

•

The Finite Code Book case:

\#\left(C\right)<\infty
and
F=\textnormal{conv}\left(C\right)
.

The following constants are central to study recovery and robustness.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
C\subset F\subset\mathbb{K}^{N}
,

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
a norm on
\mathbb{K}^{N}
,

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}
a norm on
\mathbb{K}^{M}

Then,

\displaystyle\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right):=\inf_{\mathbf{z}\in F,\mathbf{x}\in C:\mathbf{z}\neq\mathbf{x}}\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}\left(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right)\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}}=\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in F-C:\mathbf{v}\neq 0}\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{L}}=\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\textnormal{cone}\left(F-C\right):\mathbf{v}\neq 0}\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{L}}

\displaystyle\kappa\left(\mathbf{A}\right):=\sup_{\mathbf{z}\in F,\mathbf{x}\in C:\mathbf{z}\neq\mathbf{x}}\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}\left(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right)\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}}=\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in F-C:\mathbf{v}\neq 0}\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{L}}=\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in\textnormal{cone}\left(F-C\right):\mathbf{v}\neq 0}\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{L}}

is called normalization constant.

The robustness constant is closely related to recovery and robustness.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
C\subset F\subset\mathbb{K}^{N}
,

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
a norm on
\mathbb{K}^{N}
,

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}
a norm on

Then, the function

is continuous
and the set of matrices with a robust recovery guarantee

\left\{\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{M\times N}:\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)>0\right\}

\big\{\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{M\times N}:\left(F-C\right)\cap\ker\left(\mathbf{A}\right)=\left\{0\right\}\big\}

For this proof let

\left\|\mathbf{A}\right\|_{L\mapsto R}:=\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{K}^{N}:\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{L}\leq 1}\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}\right\|_{R}

.
Now let
\left(\mathbf{A}^{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{K}^{M\times N}

be a sequence such that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{A}^{k}=\mathbf{A}

so that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\mathbf{A}^{k}-\mathbf{A}\right\|_{L\mapsto R}=0

For every

\left(\mathbf{v}^{k,l}\right)_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\subset F-C
with

\mathbf{v}^{k,l}\neq 0
for all

and

\displaystyle\tau\left(\mathbf{A}^{k}\right)=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}^{k}\mathbf{v}^{k,l}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}^{k,l}\right\|_{L}}.

Using this yields

\displaystyle\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)=

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in F-C:\mathbf{v}\neq 0}\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{L}}\leq\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in F-C:\mathbf{v}\neq 0}\frac{\left\|\left(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{A}^{k}\right)\mathbf{v}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{L}}+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}^{k}\mathbf{v}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{L}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|\left(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{A}^{k}\right)\mathbf{v}^{k,l}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}^{k,l}\right\|_{L}}+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}^{k}\mathbf{v}^{k,l}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}^{k,l}\right\|_{L}}\leq\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{K}^{N}:\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{L}\leq 1}\left\|\left(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{A}^{k}\right)\mathbf{v}\right\|_{R}+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}^{k}\mathbf{v}^{k,l}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}^{k,l}\right\|_{L}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{A}^{k}\right\|_{L\mapsto R}+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}^{k}\mathbf{v}^{k,l}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}^{k,l}\right\|_{L}}.

Applying two limits yields

\displaystyle\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)\leq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{A}^{k}\right\|_{L\mapsto R}+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}^{k}\mathbf{v}^{k,l}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}^{k,l}\right\|_{L}}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{A}^{k}\right\|_{L\mapsto R}+\tau\left(\mathbf{A}^{k}\right)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\tau\left(\mathbf{A}^{k}\right).

(3)

\mathbf{v}^{k}\neq 0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and

\displaystyle\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}^{k}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}^{k}\right\|_{L}}.

Using this yields

\displaystyle\tau\left(\mathbf{A}^{k}\right)=

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in F-C:\mathbf{v}\neq 0}\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}^{k}\mathbf{v}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{L}}\leq\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in F-C:\mathbf{v}\neq 0}\frac{\left\|\left(\mathbf{A}^{k}-\mathbf{A}\right)\mathbf{v}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{L}}+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{L}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|\left(\mathbf{A}^{k}-\mathbf{A}\right)\mathbf{v}^{k}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}^{k}\right\|_{L}}+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}^{k}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}^{k}\right\|_{L}}\leq\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{K}^{N}:\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{L}\leq 1}\left\|\left(\mathbf{A}^{k}-\mathbf{A}\right)\mathbf{v}\right\|_{R}+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}^{k}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}^{k}\right\|_{L}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{A}^{k}-\mathbf{A}\right\|_{L\mapsto R}+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}^{k}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}^{k}\right\|_{L}}.

Applying a limit to this yields

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\tau\left(\mathbf{A}^{k}\right)\leq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\mathbf{A}^{k}-\mathbf{A}\right\|_{L\mapsto R}+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}^{k}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}^{k}\right\|_{L}}=\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right).

By this and (
3
)
one gets
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\tau\left(\mathbf{A}^{k}\right)=\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)
.
Thus, the function
\mathbf{A}\mapsto\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)
is continuous
and the set

\left\{\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}:\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)>0\right\}

is the preimage of an open set of a continuous function and thus open.
If additionally
\textnormal{cone}\left(F-C\right)
is closed, then
Theorem
2.2
yields that

\left\{\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{M\times N}:\left(F-C\right)\cap\ker\left(\mathbf{A}\right)=\left\{0\right\}\right\}=\left\{\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{M\times N}:\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)>0\right\}

which in turn must be open as well.
∎

While the openness does not seem insightful, this result is incredibly important.
It guarantees that small perturbations of matrices with a robust recovery guarantee will also
have a similar good robust recovery guarantees. Since in general matrices can only be accurately
be represented up to machine precision in computer algebra systems,
such a property is of utmost importance.
Note that the condition that
\textnormal{cone}\left(F-C\right)
is closed is mandatory in
Statement
4
of Theorem
2.2

and in Theorem
2.3
.

are fulfilled, but condition

both of which can be seen by considering

\begin{pmatrix}1+\cos\left(\alpha\right)\\

for

one might want to

Theorem 2.5

(Measurement Matrix with bounded
\kappa
)
.

Let
C\subset F\subset\mathbb{K}^{N}
,

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
a norm on
\mathbb{K}^{N}
,

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}
a norm on
\mathbb{K}^{M}

and
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{M\times N}
.
If
\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)>0
, then the following holds true:
For all
\mathbf{z}\in F
and
\mathbf{x}\in C
one has

\displaystyle\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}\leq\left\|\mathbf{A}\left(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right)\right\|_{R}\leq\kappa\left(\mathbf{A}\right)\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}

(4)

In particular, one can find a measurement matrix with a recovery guarantee by finding a maximizer of

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{K}^{M\times N}:\kappa\left(\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\right)\leq 1}\tau\left(\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\right).

(5)

Proof.

If
\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{x}
, then also
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}
and both
bounds are fulfilled immediately. Thus, assume
\mathbf{z}\neq\mathbf{x}
.
By definition of
\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)
and
\kappa\left(\mathbf{A}\right)

one gets

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{A}\left(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right)\right\|_{R}=\left\|\mathbf{A}\frac{\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}}{\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}}\right\|_{R}\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}\geq\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L},

and

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{A}\left(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right)\right\|_{R}=\left\|\mathbf{A}\frac{\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}}{\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}}\right\|_{R}\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}\leq\kappa\left(\mathbf{A}\right)\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L},

which finishes the proof.
∎

If one can evaluate the function
\tau
, one can potentially solve (
5
)
in order to generate a good measurement matrix deterministically.
The matrix from this optimization problem is making the inequality in
(
4
)
as tight as possible creating a near restricted isometry.
Since
\kappa
is seminorm, the constraint
\kappa\left(\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\right)\leq 1
creates
a convex set of feasible points. However, the function
\tau
is not necessarily convex
and so the optimization problem is not convex either.
If the vector is not in
C
but
\mathbf{x}\in F
, one might want
to bound the estimation error rather by a linear function in the distance
of
\mathbf{x}
to
C
.

Theorem 2.6

(Stability as a consequence of Robustness)
.

Let
C\subset F\subset\mathbb{K}^{N}
,

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
a norm on
\mathbb{K}^{N}
,

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}
a norm on
\mathbb{K}^{M}

and
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{M\times N}
.
If
\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)>0
, then the following holds true:

For all
\mathbf{x}\in F
,
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{K}^{M}

and
\mathbf{x}^{\#}\in\underset{\mathbf{z}\in F}{\textnormal{argmin}}\hskip 1.0pt\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{y}\right\|_{R}

one has

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{x}^{\#}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}\leq\left(1+\frac{\kappa\left(\mathbf{A}\right)}{\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)}\right)\inf_{\mathbf{z}\in C}\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}+\frac{2}{\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)}\left\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\|_{R}

(6)

Proof.

Let
\left(\mathbf{x}^{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C
be a sequence such that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\mathbf{x}^{k}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}=\inf_{\mathbf{z}\in C}\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}
.
Using (
4
)
with
\mathbf{x}^{\#}\in F
and
\mathbf{x}^{k}\in C
gives

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{x}^{\#}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{x}^{k}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}+\left\|\mathbf{x}^{\#}-\mathbf{x}^{k}\right\|_{L}\overset{\eqref{Equation:Theorem:optimal_matrix_kappa_bounded:inequality}}{\leq}\left\|\mathbf{x}^{k}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}+\frac{1}{\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)}\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}^{\#}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}^{k}\right\|_{R}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{x}^{k}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}+\frac{1}{\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)}\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}^{\#}-\mathbf{y}\right\|_{R}+\frac{1}{\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)}\left\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\|_{R}+\frac{1}{\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)}\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}^{k}\right\|_{R}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{\kappa\left(\mathbf{A}\right)}{\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)}\right)\left\|\mathbf{x}^{k}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}+\frac{1}{\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)}\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}^{\#}-\mathbf{y}\right\|_{R}+\frac{1}{\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)}\left\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\|_{R}.

Applying that
\mathbf{x}^{\#}
is a minimizer of

\min_{\mathbf{z}\in F}\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{y}\right\|_{R}
and

\mathbf{x}\in F
is feasible to this gives

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{x}^{\#}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{\kappa\left(\mathbf{A}\right)}{\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)}\right)\left\|\mathbf{x}^{k}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}+\frac{2}{\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)}\left\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\|_{R}.

Taking the limit for
k\rightarrow\infty
yields the claim.
∎

If the error is bounded linearly in

\inf_{\mathbf{z}\in C}\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}
,
this is called stability here. In the case
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
,
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}
,

C=\Sigma_{S}^{N}\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}
and
F=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}

this can be compared to
[
1
, Chapter 4]
.
Note that the stability in this case is a consequence of the robustness.
Moreover, the near restricted isometry from
(
5
)
not only has the optimal robustness constant up to a factor
of
2
, it also minimizes the constants in front of both error terms in
(
6
).
Statement
1
from
Theorem
2.2
shows the optimality of the robustness constant in (
6
)
up to a factor of
2
. A similar statement for the stability constant
is missing. So potentially there could be measurement matrices
with significantly better stability constants than

1+\frac{\kappa\left(\mathbf{A}\right)}{\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)}
.
The solution of (
5
) gives
an insight how robust recovery can be given some dimensions and thus receives a definition here.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
C\subset F\subset\mathbb{K}^{N}
,

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
a norm on
\mathbb{K}^{N}
,

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}
a norm on
\mathbb{K}^{M}
.
Then

\displaystyle\tau_{\kappa}\left(C,F,\mathbb{K},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}\right):=\sup_{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{K}^{M\times N}:\kappa\left(\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\right)\leq 1}\tau\left(\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\right)

is called complexity constant.

Note that due to Theorem
2.2

uniform and robust recovery is possible if and only if
the complexity constant is non-zero.
In light of Theorem
2.2

it is of interest to check whether
\textnormal{cone}\left(F-C\right)
is closed.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
M,N,S\in\mathbb{N}
. Let one of the following conditions hold true:

1.

C=\Sigma_{S}^{N}
and
F=\Sigma_{S}^{N}
(the Kruskal Rank case).

2.

C=\Sigma_{S}^{N}\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
and
F=\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}

(the Signed Kernel Condition case).

3.

C=\eta\left(\Sigma_{S}^{N}\cap\mathbb{S}^{N-1}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}}\right)

and
F=\eta\mathbb{B}^{N}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}}
,
where
\eta>0
(the Null Space Property case).

4.

C=\eta\left(\Sigma_{S}^{N}\cap\mathbb{S}^{N-1}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}}\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\right)

and
F=\eta\left(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}}\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\right)
,
where
\eta>0
(Outwardly Neighborly Polytope case).

5.

\#\left(C\right)<\infty
and
F=\textnormal{conv}\left(C\right)
(the Finite Code Book Case).

Then,
\textnormal{cone}\left(F-C\right)
is closed.

Proof.

Let
A=\left\{\mathbf{a}^{1},\dots,\mathbf{a}^{K}\right\}\subset\mathbb{K}^{N}

and consider the matrix
\mathbf{A}
whose columns are all

\mathbf{a}^{k}
. One gets

\displaystyle\textnormal{cone}\left(\textnormal{conv}\left(A\right)\right)=

\displaystyle\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha v_{k}\mathbf{a}^{k}:\sum_{k=1}^{K}v_{k}=1,v_{k}\geq 0\hskip 2.0pt\text{ for all }\hskip 2.0ptk\in\left[K\right],\alpha\geq 0\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{a}^{k}\mathbf{v}_{k}:\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\right\}=\mathbf{A}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}

and the right hand side is closed in
\mathbb{K}^{N}
. Thus, the following logical
statement is true:

If
A
is finite, then
\textnormal{cone}\left(\textnormal{conv}\left(A\right)\right)
is closed.

(7)

Assume Condition
1
is fulfilled.
Note that
F-C=\Sigma_{2S}^{N}
which is a closed cone.
Thus
\textnormal{cone}\left(F-C\right)
is a closed cone.

Assume Condition
2
is fulfilled.
Let
\left(\mathbf{v}^{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\textnormal{cone}\left(F-C\right)

be convergent to some
\mathbf{v}
.
Define
T_{k}:=\left\{n\in\left[N\right]:\mathbf{v}^{k}_{n}<0\right\}\subset\left[N\right]

so that
\#\left(T_{k}\right)\leq S
.
Since
\left[N\right]
is finite, there is a
T\subset\left[N\right]
with
\#\left(T\right)\leq S

such that
T_{k}=T
holds for infinitely many
k
.
Hence, by replacing
\left(\mathbf{v}^{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
by one of its subsequences,
it can be and is assumed that
T_{k}=T
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Define
\mathbf{z}^{k}:=\left.\mathbf{v}^{k}\right|_{T^{c}}

and
\mathbf{x}^{k}:=-\left.\mathbf{v}^{k}\right|_{T}
.
Due to the definition of
T_{k}
it follows that
\mathbf{z}^{k}=\left.\mathbf{v}^{k}\right|_{T_{k}^{c}}\in F
and

\mathbf{x}^{k}=-\left.\mathbf{v}^{k}\right|_{T_{k}}\in C
.
Since
\left(\mathbf{v}^{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is converging to
\mathbf{v}
,
it follows that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{z}^{k}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left.\mathbf{v}^{k}\right|_{T^{c}}=\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T^{c}}

and

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{x}^{k}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}-\left.\mathbf{v}^{k}\right|_{T}=-\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T}
.
Due to
C,F
being closed it follows that
\mathbf{z}:=\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T^{c}}\in F
and

\mathbf{x}:=-\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T}\in C
.
This yields that

\mathbf{v}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{v}^{k}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{z}^{k}-\mathbf{x}^{k}=\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\in F-C\subset\textnormal{cone}\left(F-C\right)
.
Thus
\textnormal{cone}\left(F-C\right)
is closed.

Assume Condition
3
is fulfilled.
The cases
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}

are fundamentally different. This is because
\ell_{1}
-ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a convex polytope,
but the
\ell_{1}
-ball in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
is not a convex polytope due to the

\ell_{2}
nature of the absolute value. Thus, these two cases need to be considered independently.

Assume
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
. Given
T\subset\left[N\right]
with
\#\left(T\right)=S
set

\displaystyle C_{T}:=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\eta\mathbb{S}^{N-1}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}}:\textnormal{supp}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\subset T\right\}

so that

\displaystyle C=\bigcup_{T\subset\left[N\right]:\#\left(T\right)=S}C_{T}.

(8)

Each
C_{T}
is a real
S-1
-dimensional
\ell_{1}
-unit sphere embedded
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
which is the boundary of a polytope.
Hence,
C_{T}
is the union of finitely many faces.
To be precise there exists
faces
f\left(T,k\right)\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

for
k\in\left[K(T)\right]
such that

\displaystyle C_{T}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{K(T)}f\left(T,k\right).

(9)

Each face
f\left(T,k\right)
is a closed set and the convex
hull of finitely many points. Thus, there exist
a finite set
C\left(T,k\right)
such that

\displaystyle f\left(T,k\right)=\textnormal{conv}\left(C\left(T,k\right)\right).

(10)

Combining (
8
),
(
9
) and
(
10
)
yields

\displaystyle C=\bigcup_{T\subset\left[N\right]:\#\left(T\right)=S}C_{T}=\bigcup_{T\subset\left[N\right]:\#\left(T\right)=S}\bigcup_{k=1}^{K(T)}f\left(T,k\right)=\bigcup_{T\subset\left[N\right]:\#\left(T\right)=S}\bigcup_{k=1}^{K(T)}\textnormal{conv}\left(C\left(T,k\right)\right).

(11)

Let
f
be the set of all standard unit vectors and their negatives,
then
F=\textnormal{conv}\left(f\right)
. Using this and
(
11
) yields

\displaystyle F-C=\textnormal{conv}\left(f\right)-\bigcup_{T\subset\left[N\right]:\#\left(T\right)=S}\bigcup_{k=1}^{K(T)}\textnormal{conv}\left(f\left(C_{T},k\right)\right)=\bigcup_{T\subset\left[N\right]:\#\left(T\right)=S}\bigcup_{k=1}^{K(T)}\left(\textnormal{conv}\left(f\right)-\textnormal{conv}\left(C\left(T,k\right)\right)\right).

Applying
[
7
, Theorem 1.1.2]
to this gives

\displaystyle\textnormal{cone}\left(F-C\right)=\textnormal{cone}\left(\bigcup_{T\subset\left[N\right]:\#\left(T\right)=S}\bigcup_{k=1}^{K(T)}\textnormal{conv}\left(f-C\left(T,k\right)\right)\right)=\bigcup_{T\subset\left[N\right]:\#\left(T\right)=S}\bigcup_{k=1}^{K(T)}\textnormal{cone}\left(\textnormal{conv}\left(f-C\left(T,k\right)\right)\right).

(12)

Since all
C\left(T,k\right)
and
f
are finite, also all

f-C\left(T,k\right)
are finite. By
(
7
)
all
\textnormal{cone}\left(\textnormal{conv}\left(f-C\left(T,k\right)\right)\right)
are closed
and by (
12
)

\textnormal{cone}\left(F-C\right)
is a closed set as a finite union of closed sets.

Now let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
and note that the claim is proven for the real case,
i.e. it is already known that
\textnormal{cone}\left(F\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}-C\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
is closed.
Define the function
g:\mathbb{C}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}
mapping
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}

to
\left(\left|\mathbf{x}_{n}\right|\right)_{n\in\left[N\right]}
, i.e. mapping each component to its absolute value.
Let
\mathbf{z}\in F,\mathbf{x}\in C
. Define
\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{z}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
by
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{n}:=-\left|\mathbf{x}_{n}\right|

and
\mathbf{z}^{\prime}_{n}:=\left|\mathbf{z}_{n}-\mathbf{x}_{n}\right|-\left|\mathbf{x}_{n}\right|
.
On the one hand one gets

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}^{\prime}_{n}\leq\left|\mathbf{z}_{n}\right|+\left|\mathbf{x}_{n}\right|-\left|\mathbf{x}_{n}\right|=\left|\mathbf{z}_{n}\right|

and on the other hand it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}^{\prime}_{n}=\left|\mathbf{z}_{n}-\mathbf{x}_{n}\right|-\left|\mathbf{x}_{n}-\mathbf{z}_{n}+\mathbf{z}_{n}\right|\geq\left|\mathbf{z}_{n}-\mathbf{x}_{n}\right|-\left|\mathbf{x}_{n}-\mathbf{z}_{n}\right|-\left|\mathbf{z}_{n}\right|=-\left|\mathbf{z}_{n}\right|.

This yields
\left\|\mathbf{z}^{\prime}\right\|_{1}\leq\left\|\mathbf{z}\right\|_{1}
. Since
\mathbf{z}\in F
, it follows that

\mathbf{z}^{\prime}\in F\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Due to
\mathbf{x}\in C
, one directly gets
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in C\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Lastly,
\mathbf{z}^{\prime}_{n}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{n}=\left|\mathbf{z}_{n}-\mathbf{x}_{n}\right|\geq 0
which together with the previous two
statements yields

g\left(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right)=\mathbf{z}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\left(F\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}-C\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle F-C\subset g^{-1}\left(\left(F\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}-C\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\right).

(13)

Now let
\mathbf{v}\in g^{-1}\left(\left(F\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}-C\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\right)
.
Then, there exists
\mathbf{z}\in F\cap\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbf{x}\in C\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

g\left(\mathbf{v}\right)=\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}
.
Define the vectors
\mathbf{z}^{\prime},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
by

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}^{\prime}_{n}:=\begin{Bmatrix}\mathbf{z}_{n}&\hskip 2.0pt\text{ if }\hskip 2.0pt\mathbf{v}_{n}=0\\
\frac{\mathbf{v}_{n}}{\left|\mathbf{v}_{n}\right|}\mathbf{z}_{n}&\hskip 2.0pt\text{ if }\hskip 2.0pt\mathbf{v}_{n}\neq 0\end{Bmatrix}\hskip 2.0pt\text{ and }\hskip 2.0pt\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{n}:=\begin{Bmatrix}\mathbf{x}_{n}&\hskip 2.0pt\text{ if }\hskip 2.0pt\mathbf{v}_{n}=0\\
\frac{\mathbf{v}_{n}}{\left|\mathbf{v}_{n}\right|}\mathbf{x}_{n}&\hskip 2.0pt\text{ if }\hskip 2.0pt\mathbf{v}_{n}\neq 0\end{Bmatrix}.

It follows that
\left\|\mathbf{z}^{\prime}\right\|_{1}=\left\|\mathbf{z}\right\|_{1}
and
\left\|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\right\|_{1}=\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{1}

and thus
\mathbf{z}^{\prime}\in F
and
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in C
.
Further, if
\mathbf{v}_{n}=0
, then

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}^{\prime}_{n}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{n}=\mathbf{z}_{n}-\mathbf{x}_{n}=\left|\mathbf{v}_{n}\right|=0=\mathbf{v}_{n}.

On the other if
\mathbf{v}_{n}\neq 0
, then

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}^{\prime}_{n}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{n}=\frac{\mathbf{v}_{n}}{\left|\mathbf{v}_{n}\right|}\left(\mathbf{z}_{n}-\mathbf{x}_{n}\right)=\frac{\mathbf{v}_{n}}{\left|\mathbf{v}_{n}\right|}\left|\mathbf{v}_{n}\right|=\mathbf{v}_{n}.

Thus
\mathbf{v}\in F-C
. Together with (
13
)
this yields

\displaystyle F-C=g^{-1}\left(\left(F\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}-C\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\right).

Applying the homogeneity of
g
yields

\displaystyle\textnormal{cone}\left(F-C\right)=

\displaystyle g^{-1}\left(\textnormal{cone}\left(\left(F\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}-C\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\right)\right).

one gets

\displaystyle\left(F-C\right)\cap\mathbb{S}^{N-1}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}}=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{T\subset\left[N\right],\#\left(T\right)=S}\left\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{\infty}=1\hskip 2.0pt\text{ and }\hskip 2.0pt\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T^{c}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{T\subset\left[N\right],\#\left(T\right)=S}\bigcup_{n\in\left[N\right]}\left(\left\{\mathbf{v}\in pos_{n}:\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T^{c}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\right\}\cup\left\{\mathbf{v}\in neg_{n}:\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T^{c}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\right\}\right).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{T\subset\left[N\right],\#\left(T\right)=S}\left(\bigcup_{n\in\left[N\right]}\left\{\mathbf{v}\in pos_{n}:\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T^{c}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\right\}\right)\cup\left(\bigcup_{n\in T}\left\{\mathbf{v}\in neg_{n}:\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T^{c}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\right\}\right),

where the last step follows from the fact that

\left\{\mathbf{v}\in neg_{n}:\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T^{c}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\right\}

is empty whenever
n\in T^{c}
.
Using this and that
F-C
is a cone the robustness constant is given as

\displaystyle\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)=

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in F-C:\mathbf{v}\neq 0}\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{L}}=\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\left(F-C\right)\cap\mathbb{S}^{N-1}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}}}\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}\right\|_{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{T\subset\left[N\right],\#\left(T\right)=S}\min\left\{\inf_{n\in\left[N\right]}\alpha_{1}\left(n,T\right),\inf_{n\in T}\alpha_{2}\left(n,T\right)\right\}.

Since
\mathbf{v}\mapsto\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}\right\|_{R}
is a convex function on the convex set

\left\{\mathbf{v}\in pos_{n}:\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T^{c}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\right\}
,

\alpha_{1}(n,T)
is a convex optimization
problem and similarly
\alpha_{2}(T)
is a convex optimization problem.
In total one can calculate

\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)
by solving

\binom{N}{S}\left(N+S\right)
convex optimization problems.
The sets
pos_{n}
and
neg_{n}
are affine sets and can be described
by linear constraints. By choosing a suitable norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}

and introducing slack variables similar as in the proof of
Remark
3.6

each of these problem can be solved by a linear program.
∎

3.2
Complex Signed Kernel Condition Case

In this subsection
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
is being considered.
Note that Remark
3.6
can not be extended to the complex case. This is due to the
\ell_{2}
nature of the complex absolute value which prevents the complex

\ell_{p}
-norm-minimization from being cast as a linear program.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
,
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
a norm on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
and

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}
a norm on
\mathbb{C}^{M}
.
Let
N,M,S\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then, the following are equivalent:

1.

\tau_{\kappa}\left(\Sigma_{S}^{N}\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N},\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N},\mathbb{C},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}\right)>0
.

2.

M\geq\min\{S+1,\frac{1}{2}N\}
.

Proof.

The proof follows from the real case.
To avoid confusion the term real and complex is added
in front of the signed kernel condition to specify that. Further it should be noted
that matrices will have kernels over different fields.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
:
By assumption there exists a matrix
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{K}^{M\times N}
with
\tau\left(\mathbf{B}\right)>0
.
By Statement
3
of
Theorem
2.2
and Theorem
3.2

\mathbf{B}
has complex signed kernel condition of order
S
.
Define
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{2M\times N}
by letting the first
M
rows of
\mathbf{A}
be
the real part of
\mathbf{B}
and the last
M
rows of
\mathbf{A}
be the imaginary part of
\mathbf{B}
.
It follows that
\ker\left(\mathbf{B}\right)\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}=\ker\left(\mathbf{A}\right)=\ker\left(\mathbf{A}\right)\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Thus,
\mathbf{A}
has real signed kernel condition of order
S
.
Further, by Proposition
2.8

\textnormal{cone}\left(F-C\right)
is closed.
By Statement
4
of
Theorem
2.2
it follows that
\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)>0
.
By Theorem
3.7
one gets
2M\geq\min\{2S+1,N\}

and thus
M\geq\min\{S+\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}N\}
.
Since
M
needs to be an integer, this is equivalent to
M\geq\min\{S+1,\frac{1}{2}N\}
.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
: Note that
2M\geq\min\{2S+2,N\}\geq\min\{2S+1,N\}
.
Due to Theorem
3.7
there exists
a matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{2M\times N}
with real signed kernel condition
of order
S
. Define
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{K}^{M\times N}
by letting its real part
be the first
M
rows of
\mathbf{A}
and its imaginary part be the last
M
rows of
\mathbf{A}
.
It follows that
\ker\left(\mathbf{B}\right)\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}=\ker\left(\mathbf{A}\right)=\ker\left(\mathbf{A}\right)\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Thus,
\mathbf{B}
has complex signed kernel condition of order
S
.
By Theorem
3.2
one has

\left\{\mathbf{x}\right\}=\underset{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}}{\textnormal{argmin}}\hskip 1.0pt\left\|\mathbf{B}\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{B}\mathbf{x}\right\|_{R}

for all
\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{S}^{N}\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}

and by Proposition
2.8

\textnormal{cone}\left(F-C\right)
is closed.
By Statement
4
of
Theorem
2.2
one gets

\kappa\left(\mathbf{B}\right)\geq\tau\left(\mathbf{B}\right)>0
.
Setting
\mathbf{B}^{\prime}:=\frac{\mathbf{B}}{\kappa\left(\mathbf{B}\right)}
yields

\tau_{\kappa}\left(\Sigma_{S}^{N}\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N},\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N},\mathbb{C},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}\right)\geq\tau\left(\mathbf{B}^{\prime}\right)=\frac{\tau\left(\mathbf{B}\right)}{\kappa\left(\mathbf{B}\right)}>0
.
∎

By using the matrix
\mathbf{A}
from Theorem
3.4

in the proof of Theorem
3.9
one can show that matrices of the form

\mathbf{B}_{m,n}=\mathbf{a}_{m}^{n-1}+i\mathbf{a}_{m+M}^{n-1}
have a signed kernel condition if
M\geq S+1

and all
\mathbf{a}_{m}>0
are distinct. While these matrices have the optimal number of
measurements in this complex case, they are not Vandermonde matrices as in the next theorem.

Theorem 3.10

(Complex Vandermonde Matrices have
S=M-1
)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
,
M,N\in\mathbb{N}
and set
N^{\prime}:=\max\{2M-N,0\}
.
Let the following conditions hold true:

1.

Let
\mathbf{a}_{m}>0
for all
m\in\left[M\right]
.

2.

Let the arguments suffice

\mathbf{t}_{m}\in\left(0,\frac{\pi}{N+N^{\prime}+1-2M}\right)
for all
m\in\left[M\right]
.

3.

Let
\mathbf{a}_{m}\exp\left(i\mathbf{t}_{m}\right)
for all
m\in\left[M\right]
be distinct from each other.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N}

have entries
\mathbf{A}_{m,n}=\mathbf{a}_{m}^{n-1+N^{\prime}}\exp\left(i\mathbf{t}_{m}\left(n-1+N^{\prime}\right)\right)
.
Then,
\mathbf{A}
has the signed kernel condition of order
S:=M-1
.
In particular, all conditions can be fulfilled
together with the additional property

\left|\mathbf{A}_{m,n}\right|=1
by choosing
and

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{m}=1\hskip 2.0pt\text{ and }\hskip 2.0pt\mathbf{t}_{m}=\frac{m}{M+1}\frac{\pi}{N+N^{\prime}+1-2M}

for all
m\in\left[M\right]
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{p}\in\ker\left(\mathbf{A}\right)\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
and consider the polynomial

p\left(a\right):=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbf{p}_{n}a^{n-1+N^{\prime}}
which has real coefficients.
Let
N^{\prime\prime}
be the degree of
p
.
Since
0=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{p}=\left(p\left(\mathbf{a}_{m}\exp\left(i\mathbf{t}_{m}\right)\right)\right)_{m\in\left[M\right]}

the polynomial
p
has the complex roots
\mathbf{a}_{m}\exp\left(i\mathbf{t}_{m}\right)
for
m\in\left[M\right]
.
Since
p
has real coefficients,
p
also has the complex roots

\mathbf{a}_{m}\exp\left(i\left(-\mathbf{t}_{m}\right)\right)
for
m\in\left[M\right]
.
Due to the assumed Condition
2

and Condition
3

these
2M
roots are all distinct from each other. Thus, these
2M
roots suffice
Condition
3
and
Condition
4
of
Theorem
3.3
.
Since
N^{\prime\prime}\leq N+N^{\prime}-1
, one gets

\mathbf{t}_{m}\in\left(0,\frac{\pi}{N^{\prime\prime}+2-2M}\right)
.
Hence all
2M
roots suffice
Condition
1
and
Condition
2
of
Theorem
3.3
.
If
N^{\prime\prime}<2M
, then
p
would have more roots than its degree
and hence would need to be the zero polynomial. This would be a
contradiction and one would get

\ker\left(\mathbf{A}\right)\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}=\left\{0\right\}

and that the signed kernel condition of any order would be fulfilled.
Thus, assume that
N^{\prime\prime}\geq 2M
. It follows that
N^{\prime\prime}\geq 1
.
If
2M\geq N
, then one gets the contradiction

2M+1\leq N^{\prime\prime}+1\leq N+N^{\prime}=2M
. Hence,
2M+1\leq N
.
By Theorem
3.3

\#\left(\textnormal{var}\left(\mathbf{p}\right)\right)\geq 2M
.
That means that
\mathbf{p}
contains a subsequence of
2M+1
elements
so that subsequent elements are non-zero and have opposite sign.
This subsequence and thus
\mathbf{p}
need to have at least

M
negative entries. Hence,
\mathbf{A}
has signed kernel condition of
order
M-1
.
∎

The advantage of this construction is that it is preserved under hermitian products.

Proposition 3.11

(Hermitian Products of Vandermonde Matrices have
S=\left\lceil\frac{1}{2}M^{2}\right\rceil-1
)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
,
M,N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\sigma:\left[M\right]^{2}\rightarrow\left[M^{2}\right]

be a bijection and set
N^{\prime}:=\max\{M^{2}-N,0\}
.
Let the following conditions hold true:

1.

Let
\mathbf{a}_{m}>0
for all
m\in\left[M\right]
.

2.

Let
\mathbf{t}_{m}\in\left(0,\frac{\pi}{N+N^{\prime}+1-M^{2}}\right)
for all
m\in\left[M\right]
.

3.

Let
\mathbf{t}_{1}<\dots<\mathbf{t}_{M}
.

4.

Let

\mathbf{a}_{m_{1}}\mathbf{a}_{m_{2}}\exp\left(i\left(\mathbf{t}_{m_{1}}-\mathbf{t}_{m_{2}}\right)\right)

for all
m_{1},m_{2}\in\left[M\right]
with
m_{1}\leq m_{2}
be disctinct from each other.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N}

have entries

\mathbf{A}_{m,n}=\mathbf{a}_{m}^{n-1+N^{\prime}}\exp\left(i\mathbf{t}_{m}\left(n-1+N^{\prime}\right))\right)

and let
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}^{M^{2}\times N}
have entries

\mathbf{A}^{\prime}_{\sigma\left(\left(m_{k}\right)_{k\in\left[2\right]}\right),n}=\mathbf{A}_{m_{1},n}\mathbf{A}_{m_{2},n}^{H}

for all
m_{1},m_{2}\in\left[M\right],n\in\left[N\right]
.
In other words the
n
-th column of
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
is a reordering of

\mathbf{b}\mathbf{b}^{H}
where
\mathbf{b}
is the
n
-th
column of
\mathbf{A}
.
Then,
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
has the signed kernel condition of order

S:=\left\lceil\frac{1}{2}M^{2}\right\rceil-1
.

In particular, the conditions can be fulfilled
with
\left|\mathbf{A}_{m,n}\right|\leq 1
by choosing

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{m}:=m^{-\frac{1}{2}}\hskip 2.0pt\text{ and }\hskip 2.0pt\mathbf{t}_{m}:=\sqrt{\frac{\pi_{m}}{\pi_{M+1}}}\frac{\pi}{N+N^{\prime}+1-M^{2}}

where
\pi_{m}
is the
m
-th prime number.

Set
CR_{M,2}:=\#\left(\left\{\left(m_{k}\right)_{k\in\left[2\right]}:m_{1},m_{2}\in\left[M\right],m_{2}>m_{1}\right\}\right)
,

and
R_{M,2}:=2CR_{M,2}+RR_{M,2}
.
Note that
CR_{M,2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(M^{2}-M\right)
,
RR_{M,2}=M

and
R_{M,2}=M^{2}
.

Let
\mathbf{p}\in\ker\left(\mathbf{A}\right)\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
and consider the polynomial

p\left(a\right):=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbf{p}_{n}a^{n-1+N^{\prime}}
which has real coefficients.
Let
N^{\prime\prime}
be the degree of
p
.
Since
0=\left(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{p}\right)_{\sigma\left(\left(m_{k}\right)_{k\in\left[2\right]}\right)}=p\left(\mathbf{a}_{m_{1}}\mathbf{a}_{m_{2}}\exp\left(i\left(\mathbf{t}_{m_{1}}-\mathbf{t}_{m_{2}}\right)\right)\right)

for all
m_{1}\geq m_{2}
, the polynomial
p
has the
CR_{M,2}
complex roots

\mathbf{a}_{m_{1}}\mathbf{a}_{m_{2}}\exp\left(i\left(\mathbf{t}_{m_{1}}-\mathbf{t}_{m_{2}}\right)\right)
for all
m_{1}>m_{2}

and the
RR_{M,2}
real roots

\mathbf{a}_{m_{1}}\mathbf{a}_{m_{2}}\exp\left(i\left(\mathbf{t}_{m_{1}}-\mathbf{t}_{m_{2}}\right)\right)
for
m_{1}=m_{2}
.
Since
p
has real coefficients,
p
also has the
CR_{M,2}
complex roots

\mathbf{a}_{m_{1}}\mathbf{a}_{m_{2}}\exp\left(i\left(\mathbf{t}_{m_{1}}-\mathbf{t}_{m_{2}}\right)\right)
for all
m_{1}<m_{2}
.
Due to the assumed Condition
3
and
Condition
2

one gets the angle condition

\displaystyle 0<\mathbf{t}_{m_{1}}-\mathbf{t}_{m_{2}}<\frac{\pi}{N+N^{\prime}+1-R_{M,2}}<\pi\hskip 2.0pt\text{ for all }\hskip 2.0ptm_{1}>m_{2}

(16)

so that roots
\exp\left(i\left(\mathbf{t}_{m_{1}}-\mathbf{t}_{m_{2}}\right)\right)\mathbf{a}_{m_{1}}\mathbf{a}_{m_{2}}

with
m_{1}<m_{2}
are distinct from these roots with
m_{1}>m_{2}
.
Due to this and the assumed
Condition
4

these
R_{M,2}=2CR_{M,2}+RR_{M,2}
roots given by

\exp\left(i\left(\mathbf{t}_{m_{1}}-\mathbf{t}_{m_{2}}\right)\right)\mathbf{a}_{m_{1}}\mathbf{a}_{m_{2}}
for

are all distinct from each other. Thus, these
R_{M,2}
roots suffice
Condition
3
and
Condition
4
of
Theorem
3.3
.
By

and
(
16
), one gets

\mathbf{t}_{m_{1}}-\mathbf{t}_{m_{2}}\in\left(-\frac{\pi}{N^{\prime\prime}+2-R_{M,2}},\frac{\pi}{N^{\prime\prime}+2-R_{M,2}}\right)

for all
m_{1},m_{2}\in\left[M\right]
.
Hence all
R_{M,2}
roots suffice
Condition
1
and
Condition
2
of
Theorem
3.3
.
If
N^{\prime\prime}<R_{M,2}
, then
p
would have more roots than its degree
and hence would need to be the zero polynomial. This would be a

and that the signed kernel condition of any order would be fulfilled.
Thus, assume that

If
R_{M,2}\geq N
, then one gets the contradiction

\#\left(\textnormal{var}\left(\mathbf{p}\right)\right)\geq R_{M,2}
.

\mathbf{p}

need to have at least

has signed kernel condition of

\left\lceil\frac{1}{2}R_{M,2}\right\rceil-1=\left\lceil\frac{1}{2}M^{2}\right\rceil-1
.

For the in particular part it suffices to show that the choice suffices
the conditions of this corollary. It is clear that

1

Condition
2
and
Condition
3

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{m_{1}}\mathbf{a}_{m_{2}}\exp\left(i\left(\mathbf{t}_{m_{1}}-\mathbf{t}_{m_{2}}\right)\right)=\mathbf{a}_{m^{\prime}_{1}}\mathbf{a}_{m^{\prime}_{2}}\exp\left(i\left(\mathbf{t}_{m^{\prime}_{1}}-\mathbf{t}_{m^{\prime}_{2}}\right)\right).

(17)

By
\mathbf{a}_{m}>0
this can only be fulfilled if there exists an

such that

\displaystyle 0=\mathbf{t}_{m_{1}}-\mathbf{t}_{m_{2}}-\mathbf{t}_{m^{\prime}_{1}}+\mathbf{t}_{m^{\prime}_{2}}+2\pi l

(18)

Due to
(
16
) one gets

\mathbf{t}_{m^{\prime\prime}_{1}}-\mathbf{t}_{m^{\prime\prime}_{2}}\in\left(0,\pi\right)
for

and

0
.
Plugging this and the choice of
\mathbf{t}_{m}
into
(

)
yields

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\pi_{m_{1}}}{\pi_{M+1}}}\frac{\pi}{N+N^{\prime}+1-M^{2}}-\sqrt{\frac{\pi_{m_{2}}}{\pi_{M+1}}}\frac{\pi}{N+N^{\prime}+1-M^{2}}

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\frac{\pi_{m^{\prime}_{1}}}{\pi_{M+1}}}\frac{\pi}{N+N^{\prime}+1-M^{2}}+\sqrt{\frac{\pi_{m^{\prime}_{2}}}{\pi_{M+1}}}\frac{\pi}{N+N^{\prime}+1-M^{2}}.

Applying algebraic manipulation yields

\displaystyle 0=\sqrt{\pi_{m_{1}}}-\sqrt{\pi_{m_{2}}}-\sqrt{\pi_{m^{\prime}_{1}}}+\sqrt{\pi_{m^{\prime}_{2}}}.

Note that

\mathbf{t}_{m_{1}}-\mathbf{t}_{m_{2}}

for all

\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N}

m_{1},m_{2}\in\left[M\right],n\in\left[N\right]
.
In other words the
n
-th column of
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
is a reordering of

\mathbf{b}\mathbf{b}^{H}
where
\mathbf{b}
is the
n
-th
column of
\mathbf{A}
.
Then,
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
has the signed kernel condition of order

.

In particular, the distinctness condition can be fulfilled
by choosing

\displaystyle\mathbf{t}_{m}:=\sqrt{\frac{\pi_{m}}{\pi_{M+1}}}\frac{\pi}{N+N^{\prime}-\left(M^{2}-M\right)+1}

is the

Corollary 3.13

(Column-wise Outer Products of Vandermonde Matrices have

.

Let

In particular, the conditions can be fulfilled

of Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Note that the statement about the row of ones from
Remark
4.3
and Statement
3

4.1

are quite similar to a result in

[
6

. In

an additional

5
Bound for the Complexity Constants

In this section a bound for the complexity constant is derived.
It is being derived using a similar methods as the proof of

[
1
, Theorem 11.7]
.
At first a well known counting argument
based on volumes is needed.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}
be a norm on
\mathbb{K}^{M}
and

W\subset\mathbb{B}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}}^{M}
be a finite set
with at least two elements. Let
c^{\prime}=1
if
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
and
c^{\prime}=2
if
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
.
Then,

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in W:\mathbf{w}\neq\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}\left\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\right\|_{R}\leq

\displaystyle 2\left(\exp\left(\frac{1}{c^{\prime}M}\ln\left(\#\left(W\right)\right)\right)-1\right)^{-1}.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
and

0<t<\frac{1}{2}\inf_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in W:\mathbf{w}\neq\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}\left\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\right\|_{R}

be arbitrary.
Let
\textnormal{vol}\left[\cdot\right]
denote the volume of an
integrable set in the Lebesque measure.
Consider the balls
\mathbb{B}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}}^{M}\left(\mathbf{w},t\right):=\mathbf{w}+t\mathbb{B}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}}^{M}

for all
\mathbf{w}\in W

which are disjoint by definition of
t
.
Due to
W\subset\mathbb{B}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}}^{M}

one has

\bigcup_{\mathbf{w}\in W}\mathbb{B}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}}^{M}\left(\mathbf{w},t\right)\subset\mathbb{B}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}}^{M}\left(0,1+t\right)
.
Since the union is taken over disjoint sets, it follows that

\displaystyle\#\left(W\right)t^{M}\textnormal{vol}\left[\mathbb{B}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}}^{M}\left(0,1\right)\right]=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{w}\in W}t^{M}\textnormal{vol}\left[\mathbb{B}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}}^{M}\left(0,1\right)\right]=\sum_{\mathbf{w}\in W}\textnormal{vol}\left[\mathbb{B}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}}^{M}\left(\mathbf{w},t\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textnormal{vol}\left[\bigcup_{\mathbf{w}\in W}\mathbb{B}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}}^{M}\left(\mathbf{w},t\right)\right]\leq\textnormal{vol}\left[\mathbb{B}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}}^{M}\left(0,1+t\right)\right]=\left(1+t\right)^{M}\textnormal{vol}\left[\mathbb{B}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}}^{M}\left(0,1\right)\right].

By the fact that

\textnormal{vol}\left[\mathbb{B}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}}^{M}\left(0,1\right)\right]

is non-zero, one gets
\#\left(W\right)t^{M}\leq\left(1+t\right)^{M}

and thus
t\leq\left(\#\left(W\right)^{\frac{1}{M}}-1\right)^{-1}
.
Since this holds for all
0<t<\frac{1}{2}\inf_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in W:\mathbf{w}\neq\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}\left\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\right\|_{R}
,
it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\inf_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in W:\mathbf{w}\neq\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}\left\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\right\|_{R}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\#\left(W\right)^{\frac{1}{M}}-1\right)^{-1}=\left(\exp\left(\frac{1}{M}\ln\left(\#\left(W\right)\right)\right)-1\right)^{-1}.

(23)

If
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
, the proof follows from the fact that
the complex unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}}^{M}
is a real unit ball in

\mathbb{R}^{2M}
for a certain norm.
∎

The following theorem gives a bound for the complexity constant
in several important cases.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
M,N,S\in\mathbb{N}
,
q\in\left[1,\infty\right]

and set
\frac{1}{q}:=0
if
q=\infty
.
Let
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{q}
be an
\ell_{q}
-norm
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}
a norm on
\mathbb{K}^{M}
.
Let one of the following hold true:

1.

C=\Sigma_{S}^{N}
and
F=\Sigma_{S}^{N}
(the Kruskal Rank case).

2.

C=\Sigma_{S}^{N}\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
and
F=\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}

(the Signed Kernel Condition case).

3.

C=\eta\left(\Sigma_{S}^{N}\cap\mathbb{S}^{N-1}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}}\right)

and
F=\eta\mathbb{B}^{N}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}}
,
where
\eta>0
.
(the Null Space Property case).

4.

C=\eta\left(\Sigma_{S}^{N}\cap\mathbb{S}^{N-1}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}}\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\right)

and
F=\eta\left(\mathbb{B}^{N}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}}\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\right)
,
where
\eta>0
(Outwardly Neighborly Polytope case).

Let
c=2
if
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
and
c=4
if
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
.
Then,

\displaystyle\tau_{\kappa}\left(C,F,\mathbb{K},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{q},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}\right)\leq 2\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{S}{cM}\ln\left(\frac{N}{4S}\right)\right)-1\right)^{-1}.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{M\times N}
with
\kappa\left(\mathbf{A}\right)\leq 1
.
Let
T_{1},\dots,T_{L}
be the sets from
[
1
, Lemma 10.12]

with

\displaystyle\#\left(T_{l}\right)=S\hskip 2.0pt\text{ and }\hskip 2.0pt\#\left(T_{l}\cap T_{l^{\prime}}\right)<\frac{S}{2}\hskip 2.0pt\text{ and }\hskip 2.0ptL\geq\left(\frac{N}{4S}\right)^{\frac{S}{2}}.

(24)

Let
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{K}^{N}
be the vector with entries all

\eta S^{-1}
given any
\eta>0
.
Recall that given
l\in\left[L\right]
the vector

\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T_{l}}
is the vector with entries

\eta S^{-1}
if
n\in T_{l}
and zero else. By
(
24
)
one gets

\displaystyle\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T_{l}}\in C\cap F\hskip 2.0pt\text{ for all }\hskip 2.0ptl\in\left[L\right]\textnormal{ and any of the choices of $C,F$ in the assumption.}

(25)

Further, note that by
(
24
)
for
l\neq l^{\prime}
and
q\neq\infty
one has

\displaystyle\left\|\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T_{l}}-\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T_{l^{\prime}}}\right\|_{q}=

\displaystyle\left(\eta^{q}S^{-q}\#\left(T_{l}\right)+\eta^{q}S^{-q}\#\left(T_{l^{\prime}}\right)-\eta^{q}S^{-q}\#\left(T_{l}\cap T_{l^{\prime}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\eta^{q}S^{1-q}+\eta^{q}S^{1-q}-\frac{1}{2}\eta^{q}S^{1-q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\eta S^{\frac{1}{q}-1}.

On the other hand by
(
24
)
for
l\neq l^{\prime}
and
q=\infty
one has

\left\|\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T_{l}}-\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T_{l^{\prime}}}\right\|_{q}=\eta S^{-1}
.
Thus, for all
q\in\left[1,\infty\right]

it holds true that

\displaystyle\left\|\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T_{l}}\right\|_{q}=\eta S^{\frac{1}{q}-1}\hskip 2.0pt\text{ and }\hskip 2.0pt\left\|\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T_{l}}-\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T_{l^{\prime}}}\right\|_{q}\geq\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\eta S^{\frac{1}{q}-1}\hskip 2.0pt\text{ for all }\hskip 2.0ptl,l^{\prime}\in\left[L\right],l\neq l^{\prime}.

(26)

Using (
25
)
and (
26
)
yields

\displaystyle\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)=

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathbf{z}\in F,\mathbf{x}\in C:\mathbf{z}\neq\mathbf{x}}\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}\left(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right)\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{q}}\leq\inf_{l,l^{\prime}\in\left[L\right]:l\neq l^{\prime}}\frac{\left\|\mathbf{A}\left(\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T_{l^{\prime}}}-\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T_{l}}\right)\right\|_{R}}{\left\|\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T_{l^{\prime}}}-\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T_{l}}\right\|_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\inf_{l,l^{\prime}\in\left[L\right]:l\neq l^{\prime}}\left\|\mathbf{A}\left(\eta^{-1}S^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T_{l^{\prime}}}-\eta^{-1}S^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T_{l}}\right)\right\|_{R}.

By setting
W:=\left\{\mathbf{A}\eta^{-1}S^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T_{l}}:l\in\left[L\right]\right\}

this yields

\displaystyle\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)\leq\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\inf_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in W:\mathbf{w}\neq\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}\left\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\right\|_{R}.

(27)

By (
25
),

0\in F
and the definition of
\kappa\left(\mathbf{A}\right)

one gets

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{A}\eta^{-1}S^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T_{l}}\right\|_{R}=\eta^{-1}S^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\left\|\mathbf{A}\left(\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T_{l}}-0\right)\right\|_{R}\leq\eta^{-1}S^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\kappa\left(\mathbf{A}\right)\left\|\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T_{l}}-0\right\|_{q}.

Using
(
26
)
on this yields

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{A}\eta^{-1}S^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\left.\mathbf{v}\right|_{T_{l}}\right\|_{R}\leq\kappa\left(\mathbf{A}\right)\leq 1.

Hence,
W\subset\mathbb{B}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{R}}^{M}
. Applying
Lemma
5.1
to
(
27
)
yields

\displaystyle\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)\leq 2\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{1}{c^{\prime}M}\ln\left(L\right)\right)-1\right)^{-1}.

Plugging
(
24
)
into this gives

\displaystyle\tau\left(\mathbf{A}\right)\leq 2\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{1}{c^{\prime}M}\ln\left(\left(\frac{N}{4S}\right)^{\frac{S}{2}}\right)\right)-1\right)^{-1}=2\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{S}{cM}\ln\left(\frac{N}{4S}\right)\right)-1\right)^{-1}.

Since this holds for all
\mathbf{A}
with
\kappa\left(\mathbf{A}\right)\leq 1
,
the claim follows.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

In the cases of Theorem
5.2

the robustness constant can only be constant over dimensions if

M\in\mathcal{O}\left(S\ln\left(\frac{N}{S}\right)\right)
. Otherwise
the robustness constant changes exponentially in the variable

\frac{S}{cM}\ln\left(\frac{N}{4S}\right)
.
In the case
M\in\mathcal{O}\left(S\right)
the robustness constant
can be finite but grows with growing
N
.

In many compressed sensing works there is an emphasis on stability
and robustness, see
[
1
, Chapter 4]
.
Stability is closely related to a quantity called best
S
-term approximation error, see
[
1
, Definition 2.2]
for a precise definition.
While robustness means bounding the error by

\left\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\|_{R}
, stability means bounding the error by the best
S
-term approximation error
[
1
, Theorem 4.22]
.
In this work the robustness was investigated and several results have been shown.
At least in the case of
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
,
C=\Sigma_{S}^{N}\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
,
F=\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}

and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}
the best
S
-term approximation error
of
\mathbf{x}
is given by the quantity
\inf_{\mathbf{z}\in C}\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{L}

from Theorem
2.6
. In this case stability can
be considered as a consequence of robustness.

Stability and the best
S
-term approximation are also closely related
to
\ell_{p}
-instance optimality, see
[
1
, Definition 11.1]
. In particular, in

[
1
, Theorem 11.6]
it was proven that constant
stability can only hold if
M\geq CS\ln\left(\mathrm{e}\frac{N}{S}\right)
.
To be precise,
[
1
, Proof of Theorem 11.6]
reveals that
any constant
C
of a stable recovery guarantee must obey

C\geq\frac{1}{2}\exp\left(\frac{1}{4}\frac{S}{M}\ln\left(\mathrm{e}\frac{N}{S}\right)-2\right)-\frac{3}{2}
.
A similar scaling also holds for
[
1
, Theorem 11.7]
.
In this work a similar statement about the robustness constant
was proven with
Remark
5.3
and
Theorem
5.2
.
In view of
Theorem
2.6
the question arises
whether the stability gets worse because the robustness gets worse
or vice versa or each happens independently in the case

M\notin\mathcal{O}\left(S\ln\left(\frac{N}{S}\right)\right)
.

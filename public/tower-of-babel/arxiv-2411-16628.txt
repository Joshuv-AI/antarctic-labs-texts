On linear response for discontinuous perturbations of smooth endomorphisms
Giovanni Canestrari

Abstract
. We consider discontinuous perturbations of smooth endomorphisms and show that if the perturbed family satisfies uniform mixing assumptions on standard pairs the physical measure is Lipschitz in the parameter defining the perturbation. We also study the problem of linear response for this class of perturbations. Finally we discuss the applicability of the abstract assumptions proving linear response for a concrete example.



1.
Introduction

The dependence of the SRB measure on the parameters of the system is a natural question in smooth ergodic theory which has seen wide interest in recent years. While on physical grounds it is reasonable to expect that the averages of smooth observables change in a smooth way when varying the parameters smoothly, counterexamples show that this is not always the case (a famous one being the quadratic family
[
5
]
).

Let
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{t}:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}\}_{t}
be a family of dynamical systems such that for each element of the family there exists a unique SRB measure
\mu_{t}
and let
\varphi:{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a smooth observable. Linear response means that the function
t\to\mu_{t}(\varphi)
is differentiable.

It is common knowledge that proving linear response, whenever it holds, is significantly more challenging than showing continuity. However, this is not just an interesting mathematical problem: there are cases in which linear response is really the property that lies at the foundation of a rigorous mathematical justification of a physical theory (see e.g.
[
15
,
10
]
for a derivation of the Ohm law in a periodic Lorentz gas. In this case the conductivity is expressed in terms of microscopical quantities via linear response).

Nowadays, linear response has been proved in many situations and the
smooth
uniformly hyperbolic setting seems to be well understood. Ruelle has proved differentiability for smooth hyperbolic diffeomorphisms
[
28
,
29
]
and Anosov flows
[
30
]
. Similar results can also be obtained using transfer operators acting on Banach spaces, as has been done in
[
11
]
. Beyond uniform hyperbolicity, there have recently been studies in many directions: e.g., partially hyperbolic systems
[
22
]
, intermittent maps
[
9
,
26
]
, random systems
[
2
]
. For a broader and more complete review of the state of the art, we refer to
[
4
,
31
]
and the references therein.

The situation appears to be less neat when it comes to systems with discontinuities. In the one-dimensional case, even uniformly expanding systems may lack statistical stability or linear response. Results in the literature
[
3
,
8
,
17
]
suggest that the main obstruction to linear response occurs when the perturbation changes the topological class of the map at first order (i.e., when the perturbation is transversal).

1

1

However, there are situations in which linear response may hold also for transversal perturbations. In
[
1
]
it is shown how unbounded expansion can compensate for the lack of smoothness, providing yet another mechanism for linear response.
In the case of transversal families of piecewise
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
one-dimensional uniformly expanding maps, it is shown that the SRB measure has a dependence not worse than
t|\ln t|
. This follows from perturbation theory
[
24
,
25
]
. If optimal, this would prevent linear response, due to the presence of the logarithm. In
[
17
]
, it is indeed proved that for a wide family of transversal perturbations, the modulus of continuity of
\mu_{t}
is of the order of
t\sqrt{|\ln t|}
, demonstrating incompatibility with linear response. Recently, there has been much interest also in the case of discontinuous systems in higher dimensions (see e.g.
[
33
,
34
]
), and although linear response beyond the regime of smooth uniform hyperbolicity is somehow believed to be true, it remains an open and challenging problem. Such conviction has driven the search for new mechanisms for linear response. This task has been addressed for instance in
[
32
]
, where it is argued that a large enough stable dimension yields linear response for smooth non-uniformly hyperbolic systems with tangencies.

With the present work, we show that discontinuous uniformly hyperbolic systems can exhibit linear response, also for families not tangent to a conjugacy class, contrary to the one-dimensional case. This shows the existence of a new mechanism yielding linear response. While in 1D the discontinuities generate delta functions, which do not converge to the absolutely continuous invariant measure when pushed forward by the dynamics, in D
>1
(with a contracting direction transversal to the image of the discontinuity set) the discontinuities give rise to measures supported on submanifolds (e.g. standard pairs) that have a chance to converge to the SRB measure.

The idea of using standard pairs to study linear response is not new. In
[
22
]
it has been successfully carried out to study perturbations of partially hyperbolic systems. Other works related to the present one include
[
15
]
, which is an example of linear response for a perturbation of a discontinuous system. More specifically,
[
15
]
studies billiards in the presence of a small electric field. However,
[
15
]
uses some special properties of the system, most notably that the perturbed flow remains 1-1 and onto. Here, we consider the more general situation in which the perturbation can change the number of preimages of points of a measure proportional to the parameter defining the perturbation. In other words, linear response can hold also for perturbations that change at first order the topological class of the unperturbed dynamics (this is the case of the example in Section
5
).

The present analysis is restricted to discontinuous perturbations around a map that have a smooth invariant density. Since SRB measures of hyperbolic maps are in general distributions (see e.g.
[
20
]
), this is a rather restrictive assumption. To check if the strategy we propose can be extended to more general cases requires further work. Unfortunately, the existing literature does not provide the needed technical tools even for studying a simple example (see the discussion in Section
5
before Proposition
5.1
). To remedy this, in Section
6
, we provide the results on the behavior of standard pairs strictly needed to treat the example discussed here.

In order to treat the general case, a complete theory for using standard pairs in the setting of hyperbolic piecewise smooth endomorphisms must be developed first. Toward this goal, we introduce an alternative coupling method with respect to the literature, which simplifies the standard arguments by avoiding the use of stable/unstable holonomies on Cantor sets (e.g.

If such a theory is completed, it should be possible to apply it to manifold new cases. This may even allow investigating the situation in which the unperturbed SRB measure is not absolutely continuous or studying Lipschitz dependence of the conditional survival probability for small holes in hyperbolic systems.

We conclude this section with a discussion of the assumptions. Assumption
(H1)
describes perturbations of piece-wise smooth maps in which the continuity domains and the branches of the map are allowed to vary with the perturbation. In Assumption
(H2)
we restrict to the case in which the unperturbed dynamics is conservative, and, as discussed in the introduction, this is an important limitation that we are not able to relax in this work. Assumption
(H3)
requires that the perturbed maps have a SRB measure (or, more appropriately, physical measure) and it is a natural assumption in the context of linear response. Assumption
(H4)
is the most technical one. It requires that
{\mathcal{M}}\setminus({\mathcal{S}}_{t}^{-}\cup{\mathcal{S}}_{0}^{-})
can be foliated with
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
curves which support probability measures whose correlations decay fast enough. This assumption is closely related with the notion of standard pair, that is, loosely speaking, a measure supported on a unstable curve equipped with a smooth density. In concrete situations from hyperbolic dynamics, Assumption
(H4)
could be ‘unpacked’ in two parts: standard pairs mix rapidly (this typically happens in systems with some hyperbolicity and a contracting direction, see e.g.,
[
22
,
16
,

Lemma 3.1

The next Lemma is nothing more than a simple observation that allows us to rephrase the problem of linear response in terms of the convergence of a series.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
t\in[0,\varepsilon]
,
\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{0}({\mathcal{M}})
,

\mu_{t}(\varphi)-\mu_{0}(\varphi)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{k}\left({\mathcal{L}}_{t}h_{0}-h_{0}\right)dm.

Proof.

Applying
(H3)
and
(H2)
, and by means of a telescopic sum,

\begin{split}\mu_{t}(\varphi)&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{0}(\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}(\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{n})h_{0}dm\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{k+1}-\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{k}\right)h_{0}dm+\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\varphi h_{0}dm.\end{split}

Finally, the claim is proved by the following identity derived by (

\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{k+1}-\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{k}\right)h_{0}dm=\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{k}\left({\mathcal{L}}_{t}h_{0}-h_{0}\right)dm.

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For all

small enough,
j\in{\mathcal{J}}
and

\begin{split}&\left|F_{j,t}^{-1}(x)-F_{j,0}^{-1}(x)\right|\leqslant QDt,\\
&\left|D(F_{j,t}^{-1})(x)-D(F_{j,0}^{-1})(x)\right|\leqslant D^{2}(D+2)Qt.\end{split}

Proof.

By
(U2)
and
(H1)
, and recalling that
F_{j}
are defined on
U_{{\mathcal{M}}}\supset{\mathcal{M}}
,

\begin{split}0&=\left|F_{j,0}(F_{j,0}^{-1}(x))-F_{j,t}(F_{j,t}^{-1}(x))\right|\\
&\geqslant\left|F_{j,0}(F_{j,0}^{-1}(x))-F_{j,0}(F_{j,t}^{-1}(x))\right|-\left|F_{j,0}(F_{j,t}^{-1}(x))-F_{j,t}(F_{j,t}^{-1}(x))\right|\\
&\geqslant D^{-1}\left|F_{j,0}^{-1}(x)-F_{j,t}^{-1}(x)\right|-Qt,\end{split}

proving the first part of the Lemma. Using what we just proved,

(3.8)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:bound-inverse-matrix}\begin{split}\bigl|D(F_{j,t}^{-1})(x)-D(F_{j,0}^{-1})(x)\bigr|&\leqslant\left|(DF_{j,t})^{-1}\circ F_{j,t}^{-1}(x)-(DF_{j,t})^{-1}\circ F_{j,0}^{-1}(x)\right|\\

&\leqslant\sup_{{\mathcal{M}}}\left|D((DF_{j,t})^{-1})\right|QDt\\
&+\sup_{{\mathcal{M}}}\left|(DF_{j,t})^{-1}-(DF_{j,0})^{-1}\right|.\end{split}

To move forward,

\left|(DF_{j,0})^{-1}-(DF_{j,t})^{-1}\right|\leqslant\left|(DF_{j,0})^{-1}\right|^{2}\frac{\left|DF_{j,0}-DF_{j,t}\right|}{1-\left|(DF_{j,0})^{-1}\right|\left|DF_{j,0}-DF_{j,t}\right|}.

By the above relations and

d({\mathcal{F}}_{t}(x),{\mathcal{F}}_{0}(x))\leqslant Qt
. This implies that
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}(x)
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}(x)
cannot be separated by
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}^{+,k+1}
and that

d({\mathcal{F}}_{t}(x),{\mathcal{S}}_{0}^{+,k+1})\geqslant d({\mathcal{F}}_{0}(x),{\mathcal{S}}_{0}^{+,k+1})-d({\mathcal{F}}_{t}(x),{\mathcal{F}}_{0}(x))>D^{k}(k+1)Qt.

According to what we just proved, by Lemma
4.5
,
{\mathcal{F}}^{k}_{t}\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{0}(x)
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{k+1}(x)={\mathcal{F}}^{k}_{t}\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{t}(x)
belong to the same path connected subset of
{\mathcal{M}}\setminus[{\mathcal{S}}_{0}^{+,1}]_{Qt}
.
Therefore, by Lemma
4.4
, there exists
j\in{\mathcal{J}}
such that

d\bigl({\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{k+1}\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{0}(x),{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{k+2}(x)\bigr)=d\bigl(F_{j,t}\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{k}\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{0}(x),F_{j,t}\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{k+1}(x)\bigr).

Hence, for any
x\in{\mathcal{M}}\setminus[{\mathcal{S}}_{0}^{+,k+2}]_{\max\{D^{k+1}(k+2)Qt,Q^{\prime}_{k}t\}}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}\setminus[{\mathcal{S}}_{0}^{+,k+1}]_{Q_{k}^{\prime}t}
, by
(U2)
and the inductive assumption, we have for the quantity above,

\begin{split}d\bigl(F_{j,t}\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{k}\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{0}(x),F_{j,t}\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{k+1}(x)\bigr)\leqslant Dd\bigl({\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{k}\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{0}(x),{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{k+1}(x)\bigr)\leqslant DC^{\prime}_{k}t,\end{split}

concluding the proof.
∎

The following Lemma shows that at any fixed iteration, for
t
close to zero, the perturbed dynamics is close to the original one on most of the phase space. In the proof, we will denote by
Q^{\prime}_{k}
and
C^{\prime}_{k}

the constants given by Lemma

.

Lemma 4.7
.

For each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there exist
Q_{k},C_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
such that, for all
t\in[0,\varepsilon]
,

\sup_{{\mathcal{M}}\setminus[{\mathcal{S}}_{0}^{+,k}]_{Q_{k}t}}\left|{\mathcal{F}}^{k}_{t}-{\mathcal{F}}^{k}_{0}\right|\leqslant C_{k}t.

Proof.

We prove the statement by induction. The case
k=1
follows by Lemma
4.6
(there, it is the case
k=0
). Let us assume that the statement holds for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. First notice that by
(U2)
, for any
x\in{\mathcal{M}}\setminus[{\mathcal{S}}_{0}^{+,k+1}]_{DQ_{k}t}
, one has that
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}(x)\in{\mathcal{M}}\setminus[{\mathcal{S}}_{0}^{+,k}]_{Q_{k}t}
. Hence, by Lemma
4.6
and the inductive assumption, for any
x\in{\mathcal{M}}\setminus[{\mathcal{S}}_{0}^{+,k+1}]_{\max\{Q^{\prime}_{k+1},DQ_{k}\}t}

\begin{split}d\bigl({\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{k+1}(x),{\mathcal{F}}_{0}^{k+1}&(x)\bigr)\leqslant d\bigl({\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{k+1}(x),{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{k}\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{0}(x)\bigr)\\
&+d\bigl({\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{k}\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{0}(x),{\mathcal{F}}_{0}^{k+1}(x)\bigr)\leqslant(C^{\prime}_{k}+C_{k})t,\end{split}

concluding the proof of the Lemma.
∎

Notice that in the previous Lemma there is no control on the constants
Q_{k},C_{k}
. Hence, the statement is useful for fixed
k
when

is small enough. That is the case also for the following result.

Lemma 4.8
.

For each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there exists
C_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
such that, for all

,

m\left([{\mathcal{S}}^{+,k}_{0}]_{t}\cap[{\mathcal{S}}_{0}^{-}]_{t^{\prime}}\right)\leqslant C_{k}tt^{\prime}.

Proof.

By assumption
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}^{+,k}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{0}^{-}
consists of at most finitely many points. Let
\left\{\gamma_{-,p}\right\}_{p}
,
\left\{\gamma_{+,q}\right\}_{q}
be finitely many
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
curves of finite length such that
\overline{\gamma}_{-,p}\cap\overline{\gamma}_{+,q}
consists of at most one point and
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}^{-}\subseteq\bigcup_{p}\gamma_{-,p}
,
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}^{+,k}\subseteq\bigcup_{q}\gamma_{+,q}
. Since we are considering compact sets, if
\overline{\gamma}_{-,p}\cap\overline{\gamma}_{+,q}=\emptyset
, for some
p
and
q
, then
d(\gamma_{-,p},\gamma_{+,q})>0
. Therefore, if
t
and
t^{\prime}
are small enough, then
[\gamma_{-,p}]_{t}\cap[\gamma_{+,q}]_{t^{\prime}}=\emptyset
and the estimate follows by choosing
C_{k}
sufficiently big. On the other hand, if
\gamma_{-,p}\cap\gamma_{+,q}=\{x\}
,

, then, by the transversality assumption, the angle between
\gamma_{-,p}^{\prime}(x)
and
\gamma_{+,q}^{\prime}(x)
is bigger or equal than some
\alpha_{0}>0
(since there are only finitely many angles and they are all bigger than zero). Since the above curves are
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
, they are well approximated by lines in a neighborhood of

. Hence, for
t
and
t^{\prime}
small enough, the above set is contained in a parallelogram centered in

t
and
t^{\prime}
and form an angle not smaller than
\alpha_{0}

\gamma_{-,p}
and
\gamma_{+,q}

∎

\biggl|\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}(\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{s_{j}}^{k})\mathcal{D}_{s_{j}}dm\biggr|\leqslant C\Theta_{k}\|\varphi\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}},

Remark 5.4

The perturbation
{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}
is not tangent to the topological class of
{\mathcal{F}}_{A,0}
. By this we mean that the map
{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}
is not close modulo errors of size

to any map topologically conjugated to
{\mathcal{F}}_{A,0}
(see e.g.

in the image. In these regards, the family

is more similar to a 2D hyperbolic analog of a perturbation of the tent map in which we move down the tip of the tent near the full-branch map (losing surjectivity).

Lemma 6.5

Let

Definition 6.8

(
(p,\eta)
-Coupling)
.

We say that

are
\eta
-coupled, for
\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
, if

\begin{split}&\forall x\in W_{1},\quad\{x+\widetilde{\eta}v\}_{\widetilde{\eta}\in[-\eta,\eta]}\cap W_{2}\neq\emptyset,\quad v\in E^{s}_{t},\text{ }\|v\|=1,\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt\text{and}\\
&\forall x\in W_{2},\quad\{x+\widetilde{\eta}v\}_{\widetilde{\eta}\in[-\eta,\eta]}\cap W_{1}\neq\emptyset,\quad v\in E^{s}_{t},\text{ }\|v\|=1.\end{split}

Given two standard families
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}=\{W_{1,j},p_{1,j}\}_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}_{1}}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}=\{W_{2,j},p_{2,j}\}_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}_{2}}
, we say that
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
are
(p,\eta)
-coupled,
p\in(0,1]
,
\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
, if there exists,
{\mathcal{I}}_{1,c}\subseteq{\mathcal{I}}_{1}
,
{\mathcal{I}}_{2,c}\subseteq{\mathcal{I}}_{2}
, standard curves
W_{1,j,c}\subseteq W_{1,j}
,
j\in{\mathcal{I}}_{1,c}
, and
W_{2,j,c}\subseteq W_{2,j}
,
j\in{\mathcal{I}}_{2,c}
, and a bijection
\Upsilon:{\mathcal{I}}_{1,c}\to{\mathcal{I}}_{2,c}
, such that

\begin{split}&W_{1,j,c}\text{ and }W_{2,\Upsilon(j),c}\text{ are }\eta-\text{coupled};\\
&\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}_{1,c}}\min\left\{p_{1,j}\frac{|W_{1,j,c}|}{|W_{1,j}|},p_{2,\Upsilon(j)}\frac{|W_{2,\Upsilon(j),c}|}{|W_{2,\Upsilon(j)}|}\right\}=p.\end{split}

If
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
are
(1,\eta)
-coupled, we say that they are
\eta
-coupled.

Whenever we have two
(p,\eta)
-coupled standard families, we can always reorder the indices to have
\Upsilon(j)=j
, i.e.,
{\mathcal{I}}_{1,c}={\mathcal{I}}_{2,c}
. For two
(p,\eta)
-coupled standard family it is possible to define their coupled and uncoupled parts and renormalize the weights in a way that these are again standard families.

Definition 6.9

(Decomposition)
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota}
,
\iota\in\{1,2\}
, be two
(p,\eta)
-coupled standard families and
\{W_{\iota,j,c}\}
, as above. For
j\in{\mathcal{I}}_{\iota}
, let
{\mathcal{D}}_{\iota,j}
be some finite set indexing the connected components of
W_{\iota,j}\setminus W_{\iota,j,c}
and denote them by
\overline{W}_{\iota,j,p}
,
p\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\iota,j}
(if
j\in{\mathcal{I}}_{\iota}\setminus{\mathcal{I}}_{\iota,c}
we set
W_{\iota,j,c}=\emptyset
). We define,

\begin{split}&\underline{p}_{j}=\min\left\{p_{1,j}\frac{|W_{1,j,c}|}{|W_{1,j}|},p_{2,j}\frac{|W_{2,j,c}|}{|W_{2,j}|}\right\},\quad\forall j\in{\mathcal{I}}_{1,c}={\mathcal{I}}_{2,c},\quad\text{`the mass that}\\
&\text{we couple in the $j^{th}$ element of the family';}\\
\\
&p_{*,j}=\frac{\underline{p}_{j}}{\sum_{k\in{\mathcal{I}}_{1,c}}\underline{p}_{k}}=\frac{\underline{p}_{j}}{p},\quad\text{ `as above but normalized by the total coupled mass';}\\
\\
&\overline{p}_{\iota,j}=p_{\iota,j}\frac{|W_{\iota,j,c}|}{|W_{\iota,j}|}-\underline{p}_{j},\quad\forall j\in{\mathcal{I}}_{\iota,c},\quad\text{`mass that we do not couple in the $j^{th}$}\\
&\text{element of the family because of the difference between the two densities';}\\
\\
&\overline{p}_{\iota,j,p}=p_{\iota,j}\frac{|\overline{W}_{\iota,j,p}|}{|W_{\iota,j}|},\quad\forall p\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\iota,j},\text{ }j\in{\mathcal{I}}_{\iota},\quad\text{`other mass that we do not couple}\\
&\text{in the $j^{th}$ element of the family because it is supported on uncoupled segments';}\\
\\
\end{split}

\begin{split}&p_{\square,\iota,j}=\frac{\overline{p}_{\iota,j}}{1-p},\quad\text{`non coupled mass normalized by all non coupled mass';}\\
\\
&p_{\square,\iota,j,p}=\frac{\overline{p}_{\iota,j,p}}{1-p}\quad\text{`other non coupled mass normalized by all non coupled mass'}.\\
&\end{split}

Set
{\mathcal{D}}_{\iota}=\{(j,p):j\in{\mathcal{I}}_{\iota},p\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\iota,j}\}
. We define the coupled and uncoupled standard families as

\begin{split}&\text{Coup}_{p,\eta}({\mathcal{G}}_{1},{\mathcal{G}}_{2})=\left\{(W_{1,j,c},p_{*,j})\right\}_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}_{1,c}},\\
&\text{Coup}_{p,\eta}({\mathcal{G}}_{2},{\mathcal{G}}_{1})=\left\{(W_{2,j,c},p_{*,j})\right\}_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}_{2,c}},\\
&{\mathcal{G}}_{1}\setminus\text{Coup}_{p,\eta}({\mathcal{G}}_{1},{\mathcal{G}}_{2})=\left\{(W_{1,j,c},p_{\square,1,j})\right\}_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}_{1,c}}\cup\left\{(\overline{W}_{1,j,p},p_{\square,1,j,p})\right\}_{(j,p)\in{\mathcal{D}}_{1}},\\
&{\mathcal{G}}_{2}\setminus\text{Coup}_{p,\eta}({\mathcal{G}}_{2},{\mathcal{G}}_{1})=\left\{(W_{2,j,c},p_{\square,2,j})\right\}_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}_{2,c}}\cup\left\{(\overline{W}_{2,j,p},p_{\square,2,j,p})\right\}_{(j,p)\in{\mathcal{D}}_{2}}.\end{split}

Notice that
\text{Coup}_{p,\eta}({\mathcal{G}}_{1},{\mathcal{G}}_{2})
and

Lemma 6.10

\begin{split}{\mathcal{Z}}\left(\text{Coup}({\mathcal{G}}_{1},{\mathcal{G}}_{2})\right)&=\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}_{1,c}}\frac{p_{*,j}}{|W_{1,j,c}|}=\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}_{1,c}}\frac{\min\left\{p_{1,j}\frac{|W_{1,j,c}|}{|W_{1,j}|},p_{2,j}\frac{|W_{2,j,c}|}{|W_{2,j}|}\right\}}{p|W_{1,j,c}|}\\

6.2.
Coupling of two standard families

In this subsection we prove that it is possible to couple any two regular standard families after a big enough iterate of the perturbed map.

According to the previous result, the quantity of mass that ‘decouples’ is proportional to the distance
\eta
of the coupled mass, which we expect to tend eventually to zero. However, it has a very bad regularity, since it is supported on small segments. These segments are created near the singularities and are order
\eta
as well. In other words, the price for losing very small mass is that we are forced to deal with mass supported on standard families with arbitrary bad regularity. This phenomenon is described by Lemma
6.10
. To aid us, the Growth Lemma asserts that the mass becomes regular at an exponential rate. As it will be clear after the following two sections, the net effect is exponential mixing.

\begin{split}&c_{r}(n+1)=\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\rho^{r-1}\right)c_{r-1}(n)\quad\text{if}\text{ }\text{ }r\in{\mathbb{N}}\\
&c_{0}(n+1)=p_{c}u_{0}(n)\\
&u_{0}(n+1)=u_{1}(n)+(1-p_{c})u_{0}(n)\\
&u_{r}(n+1)=\begin{cases}u_{r+1}(n)+\frac{1}{2}\rho^{\frac{r-\beta_{2}}{\beta_{1}}}c_{\frac{r-\beta_{2}}{\beta_{1}}}(n),&\text{ if}\text{ }\text{ }r\in\mathfrak{N},\\
u_{r+1}(n),&r\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\mathfrak{N},\end{cases}\end{split}

where we have set
\mathfrak{N}=\{r\in{\mathbb{N}}:(r-\beta_{2})/\beta_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\}
.

Proof.

We prove the statement by induction. For
n=0
we can always consider
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
totally uncoupled and the statement follows from the fact that
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
belong to
H_{r_{0}}
by hypothesis. Let us assume that the statement is true for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and let
{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,r}^{c/u}
be given by the decompositions (
6.38
). Then,

(6.40)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:decomposition-sf-proof}\begin{split}\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{N_{c}(n+1)}{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota}}&=\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}c_{r}(n)\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{N_{c}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,r}^{c}}+\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}u_{r}(n)\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{N_{c}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,r}^{u}}\\
&=\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}c_{r}(n)\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{N_{c}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,r}^{c}}+u_{0}(n)\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{N_{c}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,0}^{u}}+\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}u_{r}(n)\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{N_{c}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,r}^{u}}.\end{split}

We study all the terms in (
6.40
) one by one. We start with the rightmost sum. By (
6.37
), the standard families
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,r}^{u}:={\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{N_{c}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,r+1}^{u}
belong to
H_{r}
,
r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, and we have

(6.41)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:matrix-u-u}\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}u_{r}(n)\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{N_{c}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,r}^{u}}=\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}u_{r+1}(n)\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,r}^{u}}.

We now consider the term with
u_{0}(n)
. Recalling (

) and Lemma
6.14

(p_{c},\eta_{0})
-coupled standard families

(6.42)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:matrix-u-c}\begin{split}&u_{0}(n)\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{N_{c}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,0}^{u}}=u_{0}(n)p_{c}\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,0}^{c}}+u_{0}(n)(1-p_{c})\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,0}^{u}}.\end{split}

Finally, we consider the leftmost sum in (
6.40
). By Lemma
6.15
and since
{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,r}^{c}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota^{*},r}^{c}
are
\eta_{0}\rho^{r}
-coupled, one has that
{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{N_{c}}{\mathcal{G}}_{1,r}^{c}
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{N_{c}}{\mathcal{G}}_{2,r}^{c}
are

\begin{split}(1-L\eta_{0}\rho^{r},\eta_{0}\rho^{r+1})-\text{coupled},\end{split}

where we have also used the definition of
\rho
. Recalling that
\eta_{0}\leqslant 1/(2L)
, as specified in (
6.34
), and possibly by coupling less mass according to
(O2)
, there exist two standard families
{\mathcal{H}}_{\iota,r}\simeq{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{N_{c}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,r}^{c}
which are
(1-\rho^{r}/2,\eta_{0}\rho^{r+1})-
coupled and

{\mathcal{Z}}(\text{Coup}({\mathcal{H}}_{\iota,r},{\mathcal{H}}_{\iota^{*},r}))={\mathcal{Z}}(\text{Coup}({\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{N_{c}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,r}^{c},{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{N_{c}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota^{*},r}^{c}))\leqslant\widetilde{Z}.

In the equation above, the equality is a consequence of

and the inequality follows by the second part of Lemma
6.15

6.10

\begin{split}&{\mathcal{Z}}\left({\mathcal{H}}_{\iota,r}\!\setminus\!\text{Coup}({\mathcal{H}}_{\iota,r},{\mathcal{H}}_{\iota^{*},r})\right)\!\leqslant\!\frac{3{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\iota,r})}{\frac{1}{2}\rho^{r}}\!=\!\frac{6{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{N_{c}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,r}^{c})}{\rho^{r}}\!\leqslant\!\frac{6\bigl(z^{\frac{N_{c}}{N_{0}}}\widetilde{Z}+Z\bigr)}{\rho^{r}}\!\leqslant\!\frac{6\widetilde{Z}}{\rho^{r}},\end{split}

where in the second inequality we have used the Growth Lemma

where we have used (
6.36

\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,r+1}^{c}:=\text{Coup}({\mathcal{H}}_{\iota,r},{\mathcal{H}}_{\iota^{*},r}),\text{ }\text{ }\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,\beta_{1}r+\beta_{2}}^{u}:={\mathcal{H}}_{\iota,r}\setminus\text{Coup}({\mathcal{H}}_{\iota,r},{\mathcal{H}}_{\iota^{*},r}),

whose sum, weighted by the coupled/uncoupled mass, is equivalent to

(6.43)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:matrix-u-c2}\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}c_{r}(n)\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{N_{c}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,r}^{c}}=\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}c_{r}(n)\left[\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\rho^{r}\right)\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,r+1}^{c}}+\rho^{r}\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota,\beta_{1}r+\beta_{2}}^{u}}\right].

By substituing (

6.43

6.5.
Exponential decay of correlations for standard families

In this subsection we use the linear scheme developed in Subsection
6.4
to show exponential decay of correlations for standard families and prove Proposition
5.1
.
The relations in Lemma
6.17
imply that all the mass of two standard families gets closer and closer exponentially. A way to prove this is to introduce a quantity that controls the distance between two standard families and is contracted by the scheme described above. Let
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
be given by Lemma
6.17
and
\psi_{-}\in(0,1)
,
\psi_{+}\in(1,\infty)
be such that

(6.44)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:defpsi}\psi_{-}\in\left(\rho,\frac{1}{2}\right),\quad\psi_{+}\in\left(1,\min\left\{\left(\frac{\psi_{-}}{\rho}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}_{1}},[2(1-\psi_{-})]^{\frac{1}{\beta_{2}+1}},z^{-\frac{N_{c}}{N_{0}}}\right\}\right).

Notice that we can guarantee the above relations by choosing
\psi_{-}
very close to
1/2
and
\psi_{+}
very close to
1
depending on
\psi_{-}
. The parameter
z\in(0,1)
is given by the Growth Lemma. We isolate here a consequence of equation (
6.44
) that will be useful later. For any
r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,

(6.45)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:uncunny-algebra}\begin{split}\psi_{-}\left(1-\frac{\rho^{r}}{2}\right)+\frac{\rho^{r}}{2}\left(\frac{\psi_{+}^{\beta_{1}}}{\psi_{-}}\right)^{r}\psi_{+}^{\beta_{2}+1}<\psi_{-}+\frac{\psi_{+}^{\beta_{2}+1}}{2}<1,\end{split}

where in the first inequality we have used the first condition on
\psi_{+}
and in the second inequality the second condition. We also introduce the following norm on the set of pairs
\{(c_{r},u_{r})\}_{r}
of
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
-sequences of nonnegative real numbers,

(6.46)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:norm-def}\left\|(c_{r},u_{r})\right\|_{\star}=\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\psi_{-}^{r}c_{r}+\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\psi_{+}^{r+1}u_{r}.

Let
(c_{r}(n),u_{r}(n))
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, be as in the statement of Lemma
6.17
(for any
r_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
).

Lemma 6.18
.

There exists
\tau\in(0,1)
such that, for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,

\|(c_{r}(n+1),u_{r}(n+1))\|_{\star}\leqslant\tau\|(c_{r}(n),u_{r}(n))\|_{\star}.

Proof.

We find convenient to split the sums in the
\|\cdot\|_{\star}
norm in the following way,

(6.47)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:norm0}\begin{split}\|(c_{r}(n+1),&u_{r}(n+1))\|_{\star}=\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\psi_{-}^{r+1}c_{r+1}(n+1)+c_{0}(n+1)\\
&+\psi_{+}u_{0}(n+1)+\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\mathfrak{N}}\psi_{+}^{r+1}u_{r}(n+1)+\sum_{r\in\mathfrak{N}}\psi_{+}^{r+1}u_{r}(n+1).\end{split}

Recalling Lemma
6.17
and the definition of
\mathfrak{N}
there, equation (
6.47
) is equal to

(6.48)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:norm1}\begin{split}&\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\psi_{-}^{r+1}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\rho^{r}\right)c_{r}(n)+p_{c}u_{0}(n)+\psi_{+}\left(u_{1}(n)+(1-p_{c})u_{0}(n)\right)\\
&+\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\mathfrak{N}}\psi_{+}^{r+1}u_{r+1}(n)+\sum_{r\in\mathfrak{N}}\psi_{+}^{r+1}\left(u_{r+1}(n)+\frac{1}{2}\rho^{\frac{r-\beta_{2}}{\beta_{1}}}c_{\frac{r-\beta_{2}}{\beta_{1}}}(n)\right).\end{split}

Grouping similar terms together and after some algebra, (
6.48
) becomes

(6.49)

\begin{split}\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\biggl[\psi_{-}^{r+1}&\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\rho^{r}\right)+\psi_{+}^{r\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+1}\frac{1}{2}\rho^{r}\biggr]c_{r}(n)\\
&+\left(p_{c}+\psi_{+}(1-p_{c})\right)u_{0}(n)+\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\psi_{+}^{r+1}u_{r+1}(n)\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\left[\psi_{-}\left(1-\frac{\rho^{r}}{2}\right)+\frac{\rho^{r}}{2}\biggl(\frac{\psi_{+}^{\beta_{1}}}{\psi_{-}}\biggr)^{r}\psi_{+}^{\beta_{2}+1}\right]\psi_{-}^{r}c_{r}(n)\\
&+\left(\psi_{+}^{-1}p_{c}+(1-p_{c})\right)\psi_{+}u_{0}(n)+\psi_{+}^{-1}\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}\psi_{+}^{r+1}u_{r}(n).\end{split}

Recalling (
6.45
), this is less that
\tau\|c_{r}(n),u_{r}(n)\|_{\star}
where,

\begin{split}\tau=\max\Biggl\{\psi_{+}^{-1}p_{c}&+(1-p_{c}),\\
&\sup_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\biggl[\psi_{-}\left(1-\frac{\rho^{r}}{2}\right)+\frac{\rho^{r}}{2}\biggl(\frac{\psi_{+}^{\beta_{1}}}{\psi_{-}}\biggr)^{r}\psi_{+}^{\beta_{2}+1}\biggr]\Biggr\}<1.\end{split}

This concludes the proof of the Lemma.
∎

At this point, we illustrate how the previous results lead to decay of correlations.

Lemma 6.19
.

There exist
C\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
such that, for each
t\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]
and any two standard families
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
, we have

\begin{split}\bigl|\mu_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}\left(\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n}\right)&-\mu_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\left(\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n}\right)\bigr|\leqslant C\max\left\{{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{G}}_{1}),{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{G}}_{2})\right\}\|\varphi\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}({\mathcal{M}})}\gamma^{n},\end{split}

for any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}({\mathcal{M}})
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.

Proof.

Recall
Z_{**}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
,
z\in(0,1)
and
N_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
from the Growth Lemma and define

(6.50)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:initial-reg-class0}r_{0}=\max\left\{\left\lfloor\frac{N_{0}}{N_{c}}\ln_{z^{-1}}\biggr(Z_{**}\max\left\{{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{G}}_{1}),{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{G}}_{2})\right\}\biggl)\right\rfloor+1,0\right\}.

By the Growth Lemma and the definition above, for any
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,

\begin{split}\max\left\{{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{1}),{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{2})\right\}&\leqslant Z_{**}\max\left\{{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{G}}_{1}),{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{G}}_{2})\right\}\\
&\leqslant z^{-r_{0}N_{c}/N_{0}}\leqslant z^{-r_{0}N_{c}/N_{0}}\widetilde{Z}/2\leqslant Q_{r_{0}},\end{split}

where we have used (
6.36
) in the last inequality. Hence, for any given
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, we have that
{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota}
belongs to the regularity class
H_{r_{0}}
, and we have the following estimate that will be useful later on

(6.51)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:initial-norm}\begin{split}&\biggl\|\bigl(c^{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}_{r}(0),u^{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}_{r}(0)\bigr)\biggr\|_{\star}\leqslant\psi_{+}^{r_{0}+1}\\
&\leqslant C\psi_{+}^{\frac{N_{0}}{N_{c}}\ln_{z^{-1}}\max\{{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{G}}_{1}),{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{G}}_{2})\}}\leqslant C\max\left\{{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{G}}_{1}),{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{G}}_{2})\right\},\end{split}

where the first inequality follows from the definition of the norm and the regularity of
{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota}
, in the second we have used the definition (
6.50
) of
r_{0}
, and the rightmost inequality is a consequence of the last condition on
\psi_{+}
in (
6.44
).

For any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, we write
n=\left\lfloor n/N_{c}\right\rfloor N_{c}+n_{0}
,
n_{0}\in\{0,...,N_{c}-1\}
. According to this decomposition,

(6.52)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:corr-two-sp}\begin{split}\bigl|\mu_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}(\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n})&-\mu_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}(\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n})\bigr|\\
&=\left|\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}(\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{N_{c}}\right\rfloor N_{c}})-\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}(\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{N_{c}}\right\rfloor N_{c}})\right|,\end{split}

Applying Lemma
6.17
to the two families
{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\iota}\in H_{r_{0}}
and recalling the decomposition (
6.38
) and the corresponding notation, we
re-write the expression above as

\begin{split}\biggl|\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}c_{r}^{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}&\left(\left\lfloor n/N_{c}\right\rfloor\right)(\mu_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1,r}^{c}}(\varphi)-\mu_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2,r}^{c}}(\varphi))\\
&+\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}u_{r}^{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\left(\left\lfloor n/N_{c}\right\rfloor\right)(\mu_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1,r}^{u}}(\varphi)-\mu_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2,r}^{u}}(\varphi))\biggr|.\end{split}

Since by definition
{\mathcal{G}}_{1,r}^{c}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{2,r}^{c}
are
\eta_{0}\rho^{r}
-coupled, by
(O3)
,
|\mu_{{\mathcal{G}}_{1,r}^{c}}(\varphi)-\mu_{{\mathcal{G}}_{2,r}^{c}}(\varphi)|\leqslant\eta_{0}\rho^{r}\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}
. Hence, by the triangular inequality, the previous quantity is not bigger than

\begin{split}\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}c_{r}^{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}&\left(\left\lfloor n/N_{c}\right\rfloor\right)\rho^{r}\eta_{0}\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}\\
&+2\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}u_{r}^{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\left(\left\lfloor n/N_{c}\right\rfloor\right)\|\varphi\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}.\end{split}

By (
6.44
), we have that
\rho<\psi_{-}
and
\psi_{+}>1
. Hence, the quantity above can be bounded using the
\|\cdot\|_{\star}
-norm by

\begin{split}&\max\{2,\eta_{0}\}\biggl\|\bigl(c_{r}^{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\left(\left\lfloor n/N_{c}\right\rfloor\right),u_{r}^{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\left(\left\lfloor n/N_{c}\right\rfloor\right)\bigr)\biggr\|_{\star}\|\varphi\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}}.\end{split}

6.18

such that the previous expression is not bigger than

\begin{split}&\max\{2,\eta_{0}\}\tau^{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{N_{c}}\right\rfloor}\biggl\|\bigl(c_{r}^{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\left(0\right),u_{r}^{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n_{0}}{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\left(0\right)\bigr)\biggr\|_{\star}\|\varphi\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}}.\end{split}

C\max\left\{{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{G}}_{1}),{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{G}}_{2})\right\}\tau^{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{N_{c}}\right\rfloor}\|\varphi\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}}\leqslant C\max\left\{{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{G}}_{1}),{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{G}}_{2})\right\}\gamma^{n}\|\varphi\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}},

concluding the proof of the Lemma.
∎

Lemma 6.20
.

There exist
C\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
such that, for every
t\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, there exists a measure
\mu_{t}
such that, for any standard family
{\mathcal{G}}
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,

\begin{split}&\lim_{m\to\infty}\mu_{{\mathcal{G}}}\left(\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{m}\right)=\mu_{t}(\varphi),\quad\forall\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{0}({\mathcal{M}}),\\
&\left|\mu_{{\mathcal{G}}}\left(\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n}\right)-\mu_{t}(\varphi)\right|\leqslant C{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{G}})\|\varphi\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}}\gamma^{n},\quad\forall\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}({\mathcal{M}}).\end{split}

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be any standard family. By the Growth Lemma
6.6
, for any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,

(6.53)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:Z-nonincrease}{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{m}{\mathcal{G}})\leqslant\max\{Z_{**}{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{G}}),Z_{*}\}.

Let
\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{0}({\mathcal{M}})
and fix any
\varepsilon\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
. Take
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}({\mathcal{M}})
such that
\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}-\varphi\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}=\varepsilon
. By Lemma
6.19
, there exists
C\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
and
\overline{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for any
n\geqslant\overline{n}
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,

\begin{split}\bigl|\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n}{\mathcal{G}}}(\varphi)-&\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n+m}{\mathcal{G}}}(\varphi)\bigr|\leqslant\left|\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n}{\mathcal{G}}}(\varphi)-\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n}{\mathcal{G}}}(\varphi_{j})\right|\\
&+\left|\mu_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\varphi_{j}\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n})-\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{m}{\mathcal{G}}}(\varphi_{j}\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n})\right|+\left|\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n+m}{\mathcal{G}}}(\varphi_{j})-\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n+m}{\mathcal{G}}}(\varphi)\right|\\
&\leqslant 2\varepsilon+C\gamma^{n}\max\{{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{G}}),{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{m}{\mathcal{G}})\}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}}\leqslant 3\varepsilon,\end{split}

where in the last inequality we have used (
6.53
). Therefore
\{\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n}{\mathcal{G}}}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a Cauchy sequence (in the weak topology) and it converges to a measure
\mu_{t}
. Furthermore, also any measure associated with any other standard family
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
has the same limit. Indeed, for any
\varepsilon\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
, approximating
\varphi
with
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
functions
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
as before and by using that
\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{m}{\mathcal{G}}}
tends to
\mu_{t}
, there exists an
\overline{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for any

,

\begin{split}|\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}(\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n})-\mu_{t}(\varphi)|&=\lim_{m\to\infty}|\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}(\varphi)-\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n+m}{\mathcal{G}}}(\varphi)|\\
&\leqslant 2\varepsilon+C\gamma^{n}\max\left\{{\mathcal{Z}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}),{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{G}}),Z_{*}\right\}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}}\leqslant 3\varepsilon,\end{split}

where the first inequality can be deduced as in the equation above using (
6.53
) and Lemma
6.19
. This shows the first part of the Lemma. Using the result we just proved and Lemma
6.19
, for any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}({\mathcal{M}})
and standard family
{\mathcal{G}}
,

\begin{split}\bigl|\mu_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n})\!-\!\mu_{t}(\varphi)\bigr|&=\!\!\lim_{m\to\infty}\!|\mu_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n})\!-\!\mu_{{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{m}{\mathcal{G}}}(\varphi\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{A,t}^{n})|\!\leqslant\!C\gamma^{n}\!{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{G}})\|\varphi\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}},\end{split}

concluding the proof of the Lemma.
∎

We have just obtained that constant probability densities supported on segments converge to the physical measure exponentially fast. Since these constant densities may be supported on arbitrarily short segments, we can upgrade the result to
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
densities on standard segments by approximation.

Lemma 6.21
.

There exist
C\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
such that, for all

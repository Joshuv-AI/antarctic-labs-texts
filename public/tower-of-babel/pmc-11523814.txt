Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) and Psychiatric rehospitalization rates: a retrospective study
Authors: Aneesh Rahangdale, Jeffrey Ferraro
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundElectroconvulsive therapy (ECT) induces a generalized seizure under anesthesia with an electrical current for treatment-resistant patients and may be underutilized. To our knowledge, no large-scale, American, nationwide hospital system retrospective study has examined how ECT affects psychiatric rehospitalization chances.MethodsWe analyzed initial inpatient encounters for adults aged > 18 years at HCA Healthcare Behavioral Health Units from 2016 to 2021 with diagnoses of major depressive disorder with/without psychosis, bipolar disorder with/without psychosis, schizoaffective disorder, and schizophrenia. Excluding pregnancy and incarceration cases, we compared psychiatric rehospitalization rates within 365 days for patients receiving ECT versus those with the same diagnoses not receiving ECT. Subgroup analyses were conducted by diagnosis, length of stay, sex, and race. Detailed statistical analyses included bivariate analyses with Fisher’s Exact Test and Wilcoxon Rank Sum Test.ResultsWe analyzed 38,109 distinct patients, 637 of which received ECT. The rehospitalization rate was 37.52% for ECT recipients versus 20.71% for non-ECT patients (p < 0.0001). ECT was associated with higher rehospitalization rates, particularly for severe diagnoses like psychosis (87.28%), schizoaffective disorder (3.77%), and schizophrenia (6.59%). ECT patients had significantly longer rehospitalization lengths of stay (mean 14.53 days vs. 6.54 days for non-ECT, p < 0.0001). White patients received ECT more frequently. Females received ECT more often, while males had higher rehospitalization rates.ConclusionsECT was associated with higher psychiatric rehospitalization rates and longer lengths of stay. This suggests ECT is more commonly used for complex, severe cases which may contribute to higher rehospitalization. Stronger social support and hospital and geographic ties among ECT patients may also play a role.

Background

Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) induces a generalized seizure under anesthesia with an electrical current for treatment-resistant patients with severe depression, bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder, catatonia, and neuroleptic malignant syndrome [1, 2]. Evidence suggests ECT is underutilized as a treatment modality [3]. One naturalistic retrospective cohort study in Singapore found that outpatient continuation/maintenance electroconvulsive therapy (CM-ECT) after an acute inpatient course of ECT was associated with significantly lower risks of psychiatric rehospitalization (adjusted hazard ratio of 0.68) and lower total direct healthcare costs compared to not receiving CM-ECT, especially for patients with mood disorders [4]. Another Chinese study found that ECT lowered rehospitalization for patients with schizophrenia within 3 months (11.37% vs. 18.79%) and 6 months (17.94% vs. 29.36%) compared to patients who did not receive ECT, especially for patients who received 9 or more ECT treatments had the lowest rehospitalization rates [5]. In that study, male sex, older age, being married, lower income, and shorter inpatient stay increased rehospitalization risk. To our knowledge, no large-scale, American, nationwide hospital system retrospective study has examined how ECT affects psychiatric rehospitalization chances. This study may bolster arguments for employing ECT earlier in the treatment course for severe, persistent mental illness and provide insights regarding larger demographic trends in the American healthcare system.

Methods

We conducted a retrospective cohort study analyzing data from HCA Healthcare Behavioral Health Units across the United States. The study included adult patients (> 18 years old) psychiatrically hospitalized to an inpatient unit. Subdivisions were noted for major depressive disorder (with and without psychosis), bipolar disorder (with and without psychosis), schizoaffective disorder, and schizophrenia diagnoses (F codes F 33.2, F 33.3, F 31.2, F 31.4, F 31.13, F 31.5, F 31.63, F 31.64, F25, F20). between 1 January 2016 and 31 December 2021.

We extracted anonymized data from HCA Healthcare electronic health records for all initial encounters meeting the inclusion criteria. All patients had accepted the possibility of their results being deidentified prior to this record being created. To allow for a full year of rehospitalization data, we extended the follow-up period through 31 December 2022. Patients who were pregnant or discharged to/from prison were excluded from the analysis.

The primary exposure variable was the receipt of Electroconvulsive Therapy (ECT, procedure codes GZB 0–4) during the initial hospitalization. The primary outcome was psychiatric rehospitalization within one year of discharge. Secondary outcomes included length of stay for rehospitalizations.

We collected demographic data including age, gender, and race. Diagnostic information was categorized based on ICD-10 codes for the included psychiatric disorders. Descriptive statistics were calculated for the total population and stratified by ECT status.

All statistical tests were two-sided with a significance level of 0.05. We used chi-square tests to compare categorical variables and Wilcoxon rank-sum tests for continuous variables between groups. Rehospitalization rates were calculated with 95% confidence intervals. We performed logistic regression to assess the association between ECT and rehospitalization, adjusting for potential confounders such as age, gender, race, and primary diagnosis. Subgroup analyses were conducted to examine the relationship between ECT and outcomes within specific diagnostic categories. Length of stay for rehospitalizations was compared between ECT and non-ECT patients using Wilcoxon rank-sum tests. We created histograms to visualize the distribution of length of stay.

Results

We analyzed 39,392 initial encounters for adult patients (> 18 years old) who have been hospitalized at an HCA Healthcare Behavioral Health Unit for major depressive disorder with and without psychosis, bipolar disorder with and without psychosis, schizoaffective disorder, and schizophrenia from 1 January 2016 to 31 December 2021. We investigated psychiatric rehospitalization rates for patients getting ECT compared to patients with the same diagnostic codes from 01-01-2016 through 31-12-2021 to allow for a full year of rehospitalization data. Thus, the total time frame was 2016 through the end of 2022. Of these patients, 1283 were excluded for pregnancy or discharge to or from prison. Of the 38,109 distinct patients analyzed, 637 patients were treated with Electroconvulsive Therapy (ECT). The mean age of the total population was 44.27 years (SD = 18.24), with ECT patients averaging 59.48 years (SD = 17.10) and readmitted patients averaging 45.49 years (SD = 16.21). Gender distribution for the total population was 50.11% female and 49.89% male, whereas the ECT population had a higher percentage of females (60.13%) compared to males (39.87%). Racial demographics showed that the majority of the total population was White (71.38%), followed by Black (20.35%) and Other races (8.27%). Among ECT patients, 85.56% were White, 7.69% were Black, and 6.75% were of other races (Table 1).

The rehospitalization rate was 37.52% for ECT recipients versus 20.71% for non-ECT patients (p < 0.0001) (Table 1). There was a significant overall association between ECT and rehospitalization (p < 0.0001), but no significant directional associations based on coefficients (Table 2). The rehospitalization rate within one year for the overall population was 20.99% (95% CI: 20.58–21.40%), with a significantly higher rate for ECT patients at 37.52% (95% CI: 33.76–41.28%) compared to non-ECT patients at 20.71%. The mean length of stay (LOS) for rehospitalizations was significantly longer for ECT patients, with a mean of 14.53 days (SD = 15.94, 95% CI: 12.51–16.55), compared to the total population’s average LOS of 6.54 days (SD = 7.21, 95% CI: 6.38–6.70). Chi-Square and Wilcoxon Rank Sum Tests confirmed statistically significant associations between ECT and higher rehospitalization rates (p < 0.0001) and in average psychiatric ward rehospitalization LOS between ECT and non-ECT patients (Z = 15.5648, p < 0.0001).

No significant age differences existed between ECT/non-ECT or readmitted/non-readmitted groups when adjusted for diagnoses. White patients received ECT more frequently. Men were readmitted more often, while women received ECT more frequently. Subgroup analyses by diagnoses yielded similar findings.

Overall, the data demonstrate that ECT was associated with higher rehospitalization rates, even when accounting for individual diagnoses. ECT patients also experienced significantly longer rehospitalization lengths of stay compared to non-ECT patients.

Discussion

This study reveals significant differences among psychiatric patient subgroups, particularly those receiving electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) and those experiencing rehospitalization. ECT patients, comprising 1.67% of the total population, were characterized by older age (mean 59.48 years vs. 44.27 years overall), female predominance (60.13%), and a higher proportion of White individuals (85.56%). They exhibited higher rates of severe psychiatric diagnoses, especially Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) with psychosis (87.28%). ECT was associated with increased rehospitalization rates (37.52% vs. 20.99% overall) and longer lengths of stay (14.53 days vs. 6.54 days), particularly for patients with severe conditions such as Bipolar Disorder with psychosis, Schizoaffective Disorder, and Schizophrenia. These findings suggest that ECT is more commonly used in patients with more severe and complex psychiatric conditions, which may contribute to the higher rehospitalization rates observed in this group [6].

The significant association between ECT and rehospitalization (p < 0.0001) may be attributed to ECT patients being higher utilizers of inpatient psychiatric services, having a greater likelihood of follow-up and re-hospitalization at the same hospital, and possessing stronger social support systems and geographic ties [7]. However, the strength of this association was very weak, as indicated by specific measures of association: the Phi Coefficient, Contingency Coefficient, and Cramer’s V (Table 2). These measures consistently showed a very low strength of relationship, suggesting that while statistically significant, the association may not be clinically meaningful. The large sample size (n = 38109) likely contributed to the detection of these statistically significant but practically insignificant associations [8].

Notable demographic patterns emerged, including the underrepresentation of Black patients in ECT treatment (7.69% vs. 20.35% overall) and their slight overrepresentation in readmissions (23.74%). Additionally, while ECT patients were more likely to be female, readmitted patients were more likely to be male (56.07%). The longer rehospitalization lengths of stay for ECT patients could be due to higher illness acuity requiring more time to stabilize, more instances of rehospitalization and failed medication trials, or the possibility that without ECT, these patients might have even longer stays or higher rehospitalization rates.

Conclusions and clinical implications

The findings suggest that ECT should be considered for patients with severe and persistent mental illnesses, particularly in complex cases with multiple failed medication trials. The higher rehospitalization rates and longer lengths of stay for ECT patients highlight the necessity for adequate allocation of inpatient resources, closer follow-up, and more intensive post-discharge care [9]. Furthermore, the demographic profile of ECT patients may help identify potential biases in patient selection, guiding tailored treatment approaches.

Efforts should be made to ensure equitable access to ECT across all racial groups, and there is a need for gender-specific approaches in treatment selection and post-discharge care planning. Given the older age profile of ECT patients, clinicians should ensure that ECT protocols and post-treatment care are tailored to the needs of older adults, considering potential comorbidities and age-related factors. Despite its efficacy, ECT appears underutilized, supporting earlier consideration in the treatment course for severe psychiatric conditions, potentially improving patient outcomes and reducing the burden of prolonged illness [10]. ECT may improve management of symptoms better than pharmacotherapy alone, so patients may be more likely to return to the same hospital for care. Moreover, clinicians may feel more comfortable recommending ECT to well-known patients [11]. This underscores the importance of integrated care models that facilitate continuity of care and robust support networks for treatment-resistant patients [12].

Limitations

This study has several limitations, including its retrospective design relying on diagnostic ICD codes, which may introduce confounding due to variability within diagnostic categories. There was a lack of matching for subgroup comparisons, such as the number of hospitalizations, psychotropic medications tried, or the time since diagnosis onset. While demographic breakdowns provide detailed descriptive data, they may not fully account for all confounding variables. The study also does not consider variations in ECT administration protocols or post-ECT care across different healthcare settings, which could influence outcomes. Lastly, limited information on long-term follow-up and quality of life outcomes for ECT patients restricts a comprehensive understanding of treatment efficacy [9].

Directions for future research

Future research should explore pre- and post-ECT lengths of stay, long-term outcomes, and strategies to reduce rehospitalization rates, as well as investigate potential barriers to ECT access among different demographic groups. Additionally, studies focusing on integrated care models could enhance the management of patients with mental illness and physical comorbidities [12]. This integrated analysis provides a nuanced understanding of the complex relationships between patient characteristics, treatment modalities, and outcomes in psychiatric care, highlighting areas for improved clinical practice and future research.

On a relative dependency formula
Shashi Ranjan Sinha

Abstract
. Celikbas, Liang and Sadeghi established a one-sided inequality for the relative version of Jorgensen’s dependency formula and questioned whether it would be an equality. In this paper, we show that the inequality can be indeed strict, and prove a relative dependency formula. Along the way, we obtain some bounds on s ⁡ ( M , N ) s(M,N) , a notion related to the vanishing of relative homology of finitely generated modules M M and N N over a local ring R R , under specific assumptions.

1.
Introduction

Throughout the paper,
R
stands for a commutative Noetherian local ring
(R,m,k)
and all
R\mbox{-}
modules are assumed to be nonzero and finitely generated. For any
R\mbox{-}
modules
M
and
N
, define

q(M,N)=sup\{i\geq 0\mid Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)\neq 0\},

\text{and}\hskip 11.38109ptt(M,N)=sup\{depthR_{p}-depthM_{p}-depthN_{p}\mid p\in Supp(M\otimes_{R}N)\}.

If, in addition,
M
admits a proper resolution, then define

s(M,N)=sup\{i\geq 0\mid\mathcal{G}Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)\neq 0\},

where
\mathcal{G}Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)
denotes the
i\textsuperscript{th}
relative homology of
M
and
N
(see Section 2 for the definitions).

We say that
M
and
N
satisfy the
dependency formula
if
q(M,N)=t(M,N)
. This formula yields the classical Auslander
-
Buchsbaum formula when
N
is taken to be
k
. Jorgensen
[
12
]
proved the dependency formula for the modules
M
and
N
provided
q(M,N)<\infty
and one of the two modules has finite complete intersection dimension. Later on, Celikbas, Liang, and Sadeghi discussed a
\mathcal{G}\mbox{-}
relative version of the dependency inequality, and they proved the subsequent result.

Proposition 1
.

[
8
, Corollary 3.18]

Let
M
and
N
be
R\mbox{-}
modules such that
M
has finite Gorenstein dimension. Then
s(M,N)\leq t(M,N)
.

Additionally, they asked in
[
8
, Question 3.19]
whether the claim in the aforementioned proposition would always have equality. The main aim of this note is to provide a complete answer to their question. We show that,

Theorem 2
.

Let
M
and
N
be
R\mbox{-}
modules such that
M
has finite Gorenstein dimension. If
t(M,N)\neq 1
then
s(M,N)=t(M,N)
, and if
t(M,N)=1
, then
s(M,N)\in\{0,1\}
.

We further discuss Example
2
which, as shown in Remark
1
, suggests that the strict inequality in Theorem
2
may indeed hold.

The notion of the
relative homology
was first defined and studied in
[
2
]
by Avramov and Martsinkovsky. In that paper, they establish several results centered around the relative homology; one of such results is as below.

Theorem 3
.

[
2
, Theorem 4.2(a)]

Let
M
be an
R\mbox{-}
module which admits a proper resolution. Then for each
r\geq 0
, the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

G\mbox{-}dim_{R}(M)\leq r
;

(2)

\mathcal{G}Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all

and for every
R\mbox{-}
module

;

(3)

\mathcal{G}Tor_{r+1}^{R}(M,N)=0
for every

Theorem

is bounded above by the Gorenstein dimension of

Theorem 4
.

Let
M
and

be

(A)

Consider the following conditions:

(i)

\widetilde{S_{k}}
for some

.

(ii)

(B)

Assume the following conditions hold:

(i)

, and

If

for all

.

Let

be a local ring and

2.
Preliminaries

A morphism
\phi:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{B}}
of complexes of
R\mbox{-}
modules is called
quasi-isomorphism
if the induced morphism
H_{i}(\phi):H_{i}({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow H_{i}({\mathcal{B}})
of homology is bijective for all integers
i
. A resolution of an
R\mbox{-}
module
M
is a quasi-isomorphism
\beta:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow M
where
{\mathcal{A}}
is a complex with
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}=0
for all integer
i<0
and
M
is identified as a complex with
M
in degree zero and
0
elsewhere. The associated exact sequence to the resolution
\beta
is

\beta^{+}:\hskip 14.22636pt\cdots\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{i}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0.

An
R\mbox{-}
module
M
can have several different homological dimensions, such as its projective dimension
pd_{R}(M)
, Gorenstein dimension
[
1
]
, complete intersection dimension
[
4
]
, lower complete intersection dimension
[
11
]
, upper Gorenstein dimension
[
13
]
or Cohen-Macaulay dimension
[
11
]
. We collectively denote all these homological dimensions as
H\mbox{-}dim_{R}(M)
. As recorded in
[
3
, Theorems 8.6, 8.7]
, these homological dimensions share some common properties.

We take a brief look at the notion of Gorenstein dimension
[
1
]
. An
R\mbox{-}
module
G
is said to be totally reflexive (or of Gorenstein dimension zero) whenever

(1)

the natural evaluation map
G\rightarrow G^{**}
is an isomorphism; and

(2)

Ext^{i}_{R}(G,R)=Ext^{i}_{R}(G^{*},R)=0
for all positive integers
i
,

where
(-)^{*}
stands for
\text{Hom}_{R}(-,R)
.

The Gorenstein dimension of an
R\mbox{-}
module
M
, denoted
G\mbox{-}dim_{R}(M)
, is the minimum length of a resolution of
M
by totally reflexive
R\mbox{-}
modules. We outline some basic properties about Gorenstein dimension.

Theorem 6
.

[
1
]

Let
M
be an
R\mbox{-}
module. Then the following statements hold.

(a)

G\mbox{-}dim_{R}(M)\leq pd(M)
, and the equality holds if
pd(M)<\infty
.

(b)

G\mbox{-}dim_{R}(M)=0\implies G\mbox{-}dim_{R}(M^{*})=0
.

(c)

If
G\mbox{-}dim_{R}(M)<\infty
, then
G\mbox{-}dim_{R}(M)+depth(M)=depth(R)
. This equality is known as Auslander-Bridger formula.

(d)

G\mbox{-}dim_{R_{p}}(M_{p})\leq G\mbox{-}dim_{R}(M)
for all
p\in Supp(M)
. In particular, if
M
is totally reflexive, then
M_{p}
is also totally reflexive for all
p\in\text{Supp}(M)
.

A
\mathcal{G}\mbox{-}
approximation of
R\mbox{-}
module
M
is an exact sequence
0\rightarrow X\rightarrow G\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
, where
pd(X)<\infty
and
G
is a totally reflexive
R\mbox{-}
module. It follows from
[
2
, Theorem 3.1]
that any
R\mbox{-}
module
M
with finite Gorenstein dimension admits a
\mathcal{G}\mbox{-}
approximation as above with
pd(X)=G\mbox{-}dim_{R}(M)-1
.

2.1.
Tate and Relative homology

The definitions and results that follow are primarily taken from
[
2
]
. A complete resolution of an
R
-module
M
is a diagram

\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\varphi}\mathcal{F}\rightarrow M,

where
\mathcal{C}
is a totally acyclic complex of free
R
-modules (i.e., an acyclic complex whose dual complex is acyclic as well),
\mathcal{F}
is a free resolution of
M
, and
\varphi
is a morphism such that
\varphi_{i}
is an isomorphism for
i\gg 0
. By
[
2
, Lemma 3.1]
, an
R
-module
M
admits a complete resolution if and only if
\text{G-dim}(M)<\infty
.

Let
M
be an
R\mbox{-}
module with a complete resolution
\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}\rightarrow M
. Then, for any
R
-module
N
and for each integer
i
, the
Tate
homology of
M
and
N
is defined as

\widehat{Tor}_{i}^{R}(M,N)=H_{i}(\mathcal{C}\otimes_{R}N).

A sequence
\vartheta
of
R
-modules is referred to as
proper exact
if it satisfies the condition that for every totally reflexive
R
-module
G
, the induced sequence
\text{Hom}_{R}(G,\vartheta)
is exact. A proper resolution of an
R\mbox{-}
module
M
is a resolution
\mathcal{T}\rightarrow M
by totally reflexive
R\mbox{-}
modules whose associated exact sequence
\mathcal{T}^{+}
is proper. Every module with finite Gorenstein dimension admits a proper resolution (see
[
2
, Lemma 4.1]
).

Let
M
be an
R\mbox{-}
module which admits a proper resolution
\mathcal{T}\rightarrow M
. Then we define for each
R\mbox{-}
module
N
and for each integer
i
the
relative
homology of
M
and
N
as

\mathcal{G}Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)=H_{i}(\mathcal{T}\otimes_{R}N).

Below is a list of properties from
[
2
]
that will be referenced throughout the paper.

Theorem 7
.

Let
M
,
N
and
\{M_{i}\mid i\in\{1,2,3\}\}
be

(a)

Proof of the main results

In this section, we discuss the proof of our main results. We also present Example

Proof of Theorem
2
.

In view of Proposition
1
, it is enough to show that
t(M,N)\leq 1
if
s(M,N)=0
and
t(M,N)\leq s(M,N)
if
s(M,N)\geq 1
. Assuming
M
is totally reflexive, the assertion follows from the Auslander-Bridger formula
6
(c)
and Theorem
6
(d)
. So we may assume that
G\mbox{-}dim_{R}(M)\geq 1
. Consider a
\mathcal{G}\mbox{-}
approximation of
M

(1)

0\rightarrow X\rightarrow G\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0

where
pd(X)<\infty
and
G
is a totally reflexive
R\mbox{-}
module
2
.

Note that the sequence (
1
) is proper exact. Also
\mathcal{G}Tor_{i}^{R}(G,N)=0
for all
i\geq 1
by Theorem
3
. Therefore on applying Theorem
7
(d)
to the sequence
1
, we get

s(X,N)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}s(M,N)-1,&\text{if }s(M,N)\geq 1\\
0,&\text{if }s(M,N)=0.\end{array}\right.

Since
pd(X)<\infty
, it follows from Theorem
7
(b)
that
Tor_{i}^{R}(X,N)\cong\mathcal{G}Tor_{i}^{R}(X,N)
for all
i\geq 0
, whence

q(X,N)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}s(M,N)-1,&\text{if }s(M,N)\geq 1\\
0,&\text{if }s(M,N)=0.\end{array}\right.

Now for any
p\in Supp(M\otimes_{R}N)
, there are two possible cases

(1)

p\notin Supp(X)
. Then
M_{p}\cong G_{p}
, is a totally reflexive module by Theorem
6
(d)
. The Auslander-Bridger formula
6
(c)
thus implies
depthR_{p}-depthM_{p}-depthN_{p}\leq 0
.

(2)

p\in Supp(X)
. Using Theorem
8
for the pair
(X,N)
, we get

depthR_{p}-depthX_{p}-depthN_{p}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}s(M,N)-1,&\text{if }s(M,N)\geq 1\\
0,&\text{if }s(M,N)=0,\end{array}\right.

which on equating further gives

(2)

depthX_{p}-1\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}depthR_{p}-depthN_{p}-s(M,N),&\text{if }s(M,N)\geq 1\\
depthR_{p}-depthN_{p}-1,&\text{if }s(M,N)=0.\end{array}\right.

Since

is total reflexive,
depthG_{p}=depthR_{p}\geq depthR_{p}-depthN_{p}-s(M,N)
. Localizing the sequence (

) at

and then on applying the depth lemma to the induced sequence, we obtain that
depthM_{p}\geq depthX_{p}-1
. Thus it follows from the inequality

that

depthR_{p}-depthM_{p}-depthN_{p}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}s(M,N),&\text{if }s(M,N)\geq 1\\
1,&\text{if }s(M,N)=0.\end{array}\right.

This proves the desired claim.
∎

Right-invariant Poisson–Nijenhuis structures on Lie groupoids: Correspondence and Classification
Ghorbanali Haghighatdoost, Department of Mathematics,Azarbaijan Shahid Madani University, Tabriz, Iran

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce right-invariant Poisson-Nijenhuis Structures on Lie groupoids and their infinitesimal counterparts as called ( ∧ , 𝐧 ) − (\wedge,\mathbf{n})- structures. Also, we present a one-to-one correspondence between ( Λ , 𝐧 ) − (\Lambda,\mathbf{n})- structures on Lie algebroids with Poisson-Nijenhuis structures ( Π , 𝐍 ) (\Pi,\mathbf{N}) on their Lie groupoids under certian conditions. Also, we give some illustrative examples .



Keywords:
Right-invariant Poisson–Nijenhuis structures; Lie groupoids; Lie algebroids; Nijenhuis operators.

53D17; 22A22; 58H05; 53D18; 37K10.

As it is mentioned in the prologue of

Recently, many researchers working on geometric structures on Lie groupoids and try to extend known methods on Lie groups to Lie groupoids. Poisson–Nijenhuis (P-N) structures on manifolds were introduced by Magri and Morosi

In

In line with our previous works

We emphasize that this work is concerned with continuous Poisson–Nijenhuis structures on Lie groupoids, which is distinct from discrete Lagrangian or Hamiltonian mechanics on groupoids..

In section 2 we review some preliminary definitions and theorems about Poisson–Nijenhuis manifolds and groupoids. Moreover, we recall basic concepts about Lie groupoids and Lie algebroids (for more details refer to

Lie groupoid and Lie algebroid

As it is well-known, a groupoid which is denoted by

A smooth mapping

. Where

According to the above, to talk about right- invariant vector fields on

\alpha
-fibers.
In other words, we take the elements of the sections of the sub-bundle

of

T^{\alpha}G=\mathrm{Ker}(d\alpha)\subset TG.

A Lie algebroid A over a manifold M is a vector bundle
\tau:A\longrightarrow M
with the following items:

1.

A Lie bracket
[|~,~|]
on the space of smooth sections
\Gamma(\tau),

[|~,~|]:\Gamma(\tau)\times\Gamma(\tau)\longrightarrow\Gamma(\tau),\quad(X,Y)\longmapsto[|X,Y|].

2.

A morphism of vector bundles

called the anchor map, where

is the tangent bundle of
M,

such that the anchor and the bracket satisfy the following Leibniz rule:

[|X,fY|]=f[|X,Y|]+\rho(X)(f)Y,

where
X,Y\in\Gamma(\tau)
,
~f\in C^{\infty}(M)

\rho(X)f

Given a Lie groupoid

of the

It is easy to see that every fiber of the sub-bundle

at an arrow

, where

The differential of the right translation by

2.2
Poisson-Nijenhuis Lie groupoid

Definition 4

Let
M
be a smooth manifold and
N:TM\to TM
be a vector valued
1-
form, or a
(1,1)-
tensor on
M
. Then its Nijenhuis torsion
\tau N
is a vector valued
2-form
defined by

\tau N(X,Y):=[NX,NY]-N([NX,Y]+[X,NY]-N[X,Y]),\;for\;X,Y\in\Gamma(TM).

(1)

An
(1,1)
-tensor
N
is called a Nijenhuis tensor if its Nijenhuis torsion vanishes.

Given a Nijenhuis tensor
N
, one can define a new Lie algebroid structure on
TM
deformed by
N
. We denote this Lie algebroid by
(TM)_{N}
. The bracket
[,]_{N}
and anchor
\rho_{N}
of this deformed Lie algebroid are given by

[X,Y]_{N}=[NX,Y]+[X,NY]-N[X,Y]\;\;\text{and}\;\;\rho_{N}=N,\;\forall\;X,Y\in\Gamma(TM).

(2)

Let
M
be a smooth manifold and
\Pi\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}TM)
a bivector field on
M
. Then one can define a skew-symmetric bracket
[,]_{\Pi}
on the space
\Omega^{1}(M)
of
1
-forms on
M
(one can see
[
22
]
or
[
12
]
), given by

[\alpha,\beta]_{\Pi}:=\mathcal{L}_{\Pi^{\sharp}\alpha}\beta-\mathcal{L}_{\Pi^{\sharp}\beta}\alpha-d(\Pi(\alpha,\beta)),\;\forall\;\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M),

(3)

where
\Pi^{\sharp}:T^{\ast}M\to TM
,
\alpha\to\Pi(\alpha,-)
is the bundle map induced by
\Pi
.

Definition 5

A bivector
\Pi
is called a Poisson if

[\Pi,\Pi]_{SN}=0,

(4)

where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathrm{SN}}
is the Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket.

If
\Pi
ia Poisson bivector, then the cotangent bundle
T^{\ast}M
with the above bracket and the bundle map
\Pi^{\sharp}
forms a Lie algebroid. We call this Lie algebroid as the cotangent Lie algebroid of the Poisson manifold
(M,\Pi)
and is denoted by
(T^{\ast}M)_{\Pi}
.
(T^{\ast}M)_{\Pi}
knows everything about
(M,\Pi)
.

Definition 6

A Poisson-Nijenhuis manifold is a manifold
M
together with a Poisson bivector
\Pi\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}TM)
and a Nijenhuis tensor
N
such that they are compatible in the following senses:

•

N\circ\Pi^{\sharp}=\Pi^{\sharp}\circ N^{\ast}
(thus,

defines a bivector field

on
M
),

•

C(\Pi,N)\equiv 0
,

where

C(\Pi,N)(\alpha,\beta):=[\alpha,\beta]_{N\Pi}-([N^{\ast}\alpha,\beta]_{\Pi}+[\alpha,N^{\ast}\beta]_{\Pi}-N^{\ast}[\alpha,\beta]_{\Pi}),\;\text{for}\;\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(M).

(5)

The skew-symmetric
C^{\infty}(M)
-bilinear operation
C(\Pi,N)(-,-)
on the space of
1
-forms is called the Magri–Morosi concomitant of the Poisson–structure
\Pi
and the Nijenhuis tensor
N
.

A Poisson-Nijenhuis manifold as above is denoted by the triple
(M,\Pi,N)
. If
\Pi
is non-degenerate, that is, defines a symplectic structure

A Poisson groupoid is a Lie groupoid

, it is necessarily a vector bundle morphism.

Right-invariant Poisson-Nijenhuis structures on Lie groupoids

Definition 11

Let
\Pi
be a Poisson structure on the Lie groupoid
G\rightrightarrows M
we call
\Pi

\Lambda\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}AG)
such that
\Pi=\overrightarrow{\Lambda}
.

For every
\Lambda\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}AG),
the right invariant vector field on the Lie groupoid

\overrightarrow{\Lambda}:G\to\wedge^{2}TG
is defined by

\overrightarrow{\Lambda}(g)=\lambda_{ij}(g)\overrightarrow{X_{i}}\wedge\overrightarrow{X_{j}}=\lambda_{ij}(g)dR_{g}(X_{i})\wedge dR_{g}(X_{j}),

(6)

where
\Lambda=\lambda_{ij}(g)X_{i}\wedge X_{j}
and
X_{i},X_{j}\in AG

In this section, we define a right-invariant Poisson-Nijenhuis Lie groupoid

and its infinitesimal counterpart on the Lie algebroid
AG
of
G\rightrightarrows M

Definition 12

A bivector
\Lambda
on the Lie algebroid
AG

[\Lambda,\Lambda]_{SN}=0,

(7)

where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathrm{SN}}
is the Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket.

Definition 13

An operator

[n,n]=0,

(8)

where the Nijenhuis torsion

[n,n](X,Y):=[nX,nY]-n\big([nX,Y]+[X,nY]-n[X,Y]\big).

(9)

Definition 14

A Poisson-Nijenhuis structure

on a Lie groupoid

1.

The Poisson structure

.

2.

Multiplicative

also is right-invariant, i.e., there is a linear endomorphism

In the following proposition we prove our claims only for

Proposition 15

Let
(\Pi,\mathbf{N})
be a
right-invariant Poisson–Nijenhuis structure
on a Lie groupoid
G\rightrightarrows M
with Lie algebroid
AG
and unit manifold

•

\Lambda\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}AG)
as the restriction of

.

•

n:\Gamma(AG)\to\Gamma(AG)

as the restrictions of

to

, i.e.,
N|_{AG}=\vec{n}
and
N_{M}=n_{M}
.

Then the following hold:

1.

\Lambda
is a Poisson bivector on
AG
, (
7

[\Lambda,\Lambda]_{\mathrm{SN}}=0,

2.

n
is a Nijenhuis operator on
AG
, (
8
):

[n,n]=0,

3.

Compatibility between
n
and
\Lambda
:

n\circ\Lambda^{\sharp}=\Lambda^{\sharp}\circ n^{*},

where
\Lambda^{\sharp}:A^{*}G\to AG
is the bundle map induced by
\Lambda
, and
n^{*}:\Gamma(A^{*}G)\to\Gamma(A^{*}G)
is the dual of
n
.

4.

Vanishing of the Magri–Morosi concomitant:

C(\Lambda,n)=0,

where
C(\Lambda,n)
is the Magri–Morosi concomitant (see
[
9
]
and
[
18
]

5.

Morphism property:

The maps
\vec{\Lambda}^{\sharp}:T^{*}G\to TG
and
\overrightarrow{\Lambda^{\sharp}}:A^{*}G\to AG
are Lie groupoid morphisms.

Proof.
We prove each part in order.

Proof of (1).

By definition,
\Pi=\vec{\Lambda}
, (
11
) is a right-invariant Poisson bivector on
G
. Hence
[\Pi,\Pi]_{\mathrm{SN}}=0
. For right-invariant bivector fields, the Schouten bracket satisfies

[\vec{\Lambda},\vec{\Lambda}]_{\mathrm{SN}}=\overrightarrow{[\Lambda,\Lambda]}_{\mathrm{SN}},

because the Lie bracket of right-invariant vector fields corresponds to the Lie bracket on
\Gamma(AG)
and the Schouten bracket respects right-invariance. Therefore

\overrightarrow{[\Lambda,\Lambda]}_{\mathrm{SN}}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad[\Lambda,\Lambda]_{\mathrm{SN}}=0.

Proof of (2).

Since
\mathbf{N}=(N,N_{M})
is a Nijenhuis tensor on
G
, its Nijenhuis torsion vanishes:
[N,N]=0
. For right-invariant vector fields, one has the relation (See

]
)

[N,N](\vec{X},\vec{Y})=\overrightarrow{[n,n]}(X,Y)\qquad\forall X,Y\in\Gamma(AG).

(A direct computation using the definition of the Nijenhuis torsion and the fact that
N(\vec{X})=\vec{n(X)}
yields this identity up to a sign; vanishing of one side implies vanishing of the other.) Hence

\overrightarrow{[n,n]}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad[n,n]=0.

Thus

is a Nijenhuis operator on
AG
.

Proof of (3).

The compatibility condition
N\circ\Pi^{\sharp}=\Pi^{\sharp}\circ N^{*}
holds on
G
because
(\Pi,\mathbf{N})
is a Poisson–Nijenhuis structure. Restricting this equality to the units

and using right-invariance, we obtain

\vec{n}\circ\vec{\Lambda}^{\sharp}=\vec{\Lambda}^{\sharp}\circ\vec{n}^{*}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\overrightarrow{n\circ\Lambda^{\sharp}}=\overrightarrow{\Lambda^{\sharp}\circ n^{*}}.

Since the map
X\mapsto\vec{X}
is an isomorphism from
\Gamma(AG)
to the space of right-invariant vector fields on
G
, we conclude

n\circ\Lambda^{\sharp}=\Lambda^{\sharp}\circ n^{*}.

Proof of (4).

The conditions
[\Lambda,\Lambda]_{\mathrm{SN}}=0
,
[n,n]=0
, and
n\circ\Lambda^{\sharp}=\Lambda^{\sharp}\circ n^{*}
are precisely the defining properties of a Poisson–Nijenhuis structure on the Lie algebroid

(see
[
9
]
and
[
18
]
). For such a structure, the Magri–Morosi concomitant vanishes identically, i.e.,
C(\Lambda,n)=0
.

Proof of (5).

The map
\Pi^{\sharp}:T^{*}G\to TG
is a Lie groupoid morphism by definition of a Poisson groupoid (see
[
16
]
). Since
\Pi=\vec{\Lambda}
, right-invariance implies that
\Pi^{\sharp}
preserves right-invariant sections. Hence its restriction to the units gives a Lie algebroid morphism
\Lambda^{\sharp}:A^{*}G\to AG
. The same argument applied to
\vec{\Lambda}^{\sharp}
and
\overrightarrow{\Lambda^{\sharp}}

yields that these maps are Lie groupoid morphisms.

\Pi
, i.e.,

N\circ\Pi^{\sharp}=\Pi^{\sharp}\circ N^{*}\qquad\text{(equivalently }C(\Pi,N)=0\text{)},

and

2.

(\Lambda,n)
-structures on
AG
satisfying:

•

\Lambda\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}AG)
is a Poisson bivector:
[\Lambda,\Lambda]_{\mathrm{SN}}=0
,

•

n:AG\to AG
is a Nijenhuis operator:
[n,n]=0
,

•

Compatibility:
n\circ\Lambda^{\sharp}=\Lambda^{\sharp}\circ n^{*}
,

•

(The base map
n_{M}:TM\to TM
is determined by
\rho\circ n=n_{M}\circ\rho
, where
\rho:AG\to TM
is the anchor.)

The correspondence is given by:

\Pi=\overrightarrow{\Lambda},\qquad N=\overrightarrow{n},\qquad N_{M}=n_{M},

i.e., the structures on
G
are the right-invariant lifts of the structures on
AG
.

Proof.
We prove the two directions separately.

Direction 1: From
G
to
AG
(Proposition 12).

Given a right-invariant Poisson–Nijenhuis structure
(\Pi,\mathbf{N})
on
G
, define
\Lambda
and
n
by restricting
\Pi
and
N
to the unit manifold
1_{M}\subset G
. Since
\Pi
and
N
are right-invariant, this yields well-defined sections
\Lambda\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}AG)
and
n:\Gamma{(AG)}\to\Gamma{(AG)}
. Proposition 12 shows that
[\Lambda,\Lambda]_{\mathrm{SN}}=0
,
[n,n]=0
, and
n\circ\Lambda^{\sharp}=\Lambda^{\sharp}\circ n^{*}
. Hence we obtain a
(\Lambda,n)
-structure on
AG
. This direction requires no connectedness assumptions.

Direction 2: From
AG
to
G
(Integration).

Conversely, suppose we have a
(\Lambda,n)
-structure on
AG
satisfying the three conditions. Since
G
is
s
-connected and
s
-simply connected, the Lie functor from Lie groupoids to Lie algebroids is an equivalence when restricted to
s
-simply connected groupoids (see
[
15
]
). Therefore:

1.

Integration of
\Lambda
:
By the integration theorem for Poisson groupoids (
[
15
]
), the Poisson bivector
\Lambda
on
AG
integrates uniquely to a multiplicative Poisson bivector
\Pi
on
G
such that
\Pi=\overrightarrow{\Lambda}
. The
s
-connectedness of
G
guarantees that the exponential map is surjective, and
s
-simply connectedness guarantees uniqueness.

2.

Integration of
n
:
By the integration theorem for multiplicative tensors (
[
2
]
), the Nijenhuis operator
n:AG\to AG
integrates uniquely to a multiplicative
(1,1)
-tensor
N
on
G
such that
N=\overrightarrow{n}
. The base map
N_{M}:TM\to TM
is determined by
N_{M}=\rho\circ n\circ\rho^{-1}
, which is well-defined because
\rho
is surjective on the base.

3.

Compatibility:
The compatibility condition
n\circ\Lambda^{\sharp}=\Lambda^{\sharp}\circ n^{*}
on
AG
differentiates to
N\circ\Pi^{\sharp}=\Pi^{\sharp}\circ N^{*}
on
G
(by right-invariance and the fact that differentiation preserves the composition of morphisms). Hence
(\Pi,\mathbf{N})
is a Poisson–Nijenhuis structure on
G
, where
\mathbf{N}=(N,N_{M})
.

Thus the two constructions are inverses of each other, establishing a bijection.

Remark 17

The
s
-connected and
s
-simply connected assumptions are essential for the integration direction (from
AG
to
G
). Without
s
-connectedness, some
(\Lambda,n)
-structures may not integrate to global structures on
G
. Without
s
-simply connectedness, the integration may not be unique (different PN-structures on
G
could differentiate to the same
(\Lambda,n)
on
AG
). For the direction from
G
to
AG
(Proposition 12), no such assumptions are needed.

Corollary 18

Let
G\rightrightarrows M
be an
s
-connected and
s
-simply connected Lie groupoid with Lie algebroid
AG
. Then the classification of ”right-invariant Poisson–Nijenhuis structures” on
G
is equivalent to the classification of ”
(\Lambda,n)
-structures” on
AG
satisfying:

•

[\Lambda,\Lambda]_{\mathrm{SN}}=0
,

•

[n,n]=0
,

•

n\circ\Lambda^{\sharp}=\Lambda^{\sharp}\circ n^{*}
,

where
\Lambda\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}AG)
and
n:\Gamma{(AG)}\to\Gamma{(AG)}
is a vector bundle morphism covering
n_{M}:TM\to TM
.

Moreover, the correspondence is given explicitly by:

\Pi=\overrightarrow{\Lambda},\qquad N=\overrightarrow{n},\qquad N_{M}=n_{M}.

In other words, for a fixed Lie groupoid
G
satisfying the above assumptions, there is a one-to-one correspondence:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{Right-invariant Poisson--Nijenhuis}\\
\text{structures on }G\end{array}\right\}\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}(\Lambda,n)\text{-structures on }AG\\
\text{satisfying the three conditions}\end{array}\right\}.

Thus, the classification problem on the global object
G
reduces to a purely infinitesimal (often algebraic) classification problem on its Lie algebroid
AG
.

Proof.
This follows directly from Theorem 13. The forward direction (from
G
to
AG
) is given by Proposition 12, and the backward direction (from
AG
to
G
) follows from the integration theorem for Poisson groupoids and multiplicative tensors, using the
s
-connected and
s
-simply connected assumptions. The bijectivity of the correspondence ensures that distinct
(\Lambda,n)
-structures lift to distinct PN-structures on
G
, and vice versa.

Examples

Example 19
(Trivial Lie Groupoid with Identity Nijenhuis Tensor)

Let
M
be a smooth manifold and
G
a Lie group. The
trivial Lie groupoid
is defined as
\Upsilon:=M\times G\times M\rightrightarrows M
with the following structure maps:

•

Source:
\alpha:\Upsilon\to M
,
(x,a,y)\mapsto x
.

•

Target:
\beta:\Upsilon\to M
,
(x,a,y)\mapsto y
.

•

Multiplication:
m:\Upsilon_{2}\to\Upsilon
,
(x,a,y)\cdot(y,b,z)=(x,ab,z)
.

•

Unit:
1:M\to\Upsilon
,
x\mapsto(x,e,x)
, where
e\in G
is the identity.

•

Inverse:
\iota:\Upsilon\to\Upsilon
,
(x,a,y)\mapsto(y,a^{-1},x)
.

The Lie algebroid associated to
\Upsilon
is
A\Upsilon=TM\oplus(M\times\mathfrak{g})
, where
\mathfrak{g}
is the Lie algebra of
G
. The anchor
\rho:A\Upsilon\to TM
is the projection onto
TM
, and the Lie bracket on sections is given by the usual Lie bracket of vector fields on
M
combined with the Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Now, let
M
be a Poisson manifold with Poisson bivector
\Pi_{M}
, and let
G
be a Poisson–Lie group with Poisson bivector
\Pi_{G}
. Consider the bivector
\Pi
on
\Upsilon=M\times G\times M
defined by

\Pi_{(x,a,y)}:=\Pi_{M}(x)+\Pi_{G}(a)-\Pi_{M}(y),

where the signs are chosen to ensure multiplicativity (see
[
24
]
). With this choice,
(\Upsilon,\Pi)
is a Poisson groupoid.

Let
\mathbf{N}=(N,N_{M})
be the trivial multiplicative
(1,1)
-tensor given by the identity:

N:=id_{T\Upsilon},\qquad N_{M}:=id_{TM}.

Since
N=id
, the Nijenhuis torsion
[N,N]
vanishes trivially. Moreover, the compatibility condition
N\circ\Pi^{\sharp}=\Pi^{\sharp}\circ N^{*}
holds because
N=id
and
N^{*}=id
. Hence
C(\Pi,N)=0
. Therefore
(\Upsilon,\Pi,\mathbf{N})
is a Poisson–Nijenhuis Lie groupoid.

By Proposition 12, the infinitesimal counterpart on
A\Upsilon=TM\oplus(M\times\mathfrak{g})
is given by

\Lambda=\Pi_{M}\oplus\Pi_{G}\oplus(-\Pi_{M}),\qquad n=id_{A\Upsilon},

which satisfies
[\Lambda,\Lambda]=0
,
[n,n]=0
, and
n\circ\Lambda^{\sharp}=\Lambda^{\sharp}\circ n^{*}
.

Example 20
(Pair Groupoid with Identity Nijenhuis Tensor)

Let
M
be a smooth manifold. The
pair groupoid
is defined as
M\times M\rightrightarrows M
with the following structure maps:

•

Source:
\alpha(x,y)=x
.

•

Target:
\beta(x,y)=y
.

•

Multiplication:
(x,y)\cdot(y,z)=(x,z)
.

•

Unit:
1(x)=(x,x)
.

•

Inverse:
(x,y)^{-1}=(y,x)
.

The Lie algebroid of the pair groupoid is
TM
with the identity map as anchor and the usual Lie bracket of vector fields.

Now, let
(M,\Pi_{M})
be a Poisson manifold. Define a bivector
\Pi
on
M\times M
by

\Pi_{(x,y)}:=\Pi_{M}(x)-\Pi_{M}(y).

It is well known (see
[
24
]
,
[
16
]
) that this defines a multiplicative Poisson structure on the pair groupoid, i.e.,
(M\times M,\Pi)
is a Poisson groupoid.

Let
\mathbf{N}=(N,N_{M})
be the trivial multiplicative
(1,1)
-tensor given by the identity:

N:=id_{T(M\times M)},\qquad N_{M}:=id_{TM}.

Clearly,
N
is a Nijenhuis tensor (
[N,N]=0
) and satisfies
N\circ\Pi^{\sharp}=\Pi^{\sharp}\circ N^{*}
because
N=id
and
N^{*}=id
. Thus
C(\Pi,N)=0
, and
(M\times M,\Pi,\mathbf{N})
is a Poisson–Nijenhuis Lie groupoid.

According to Proposition 12, the corresponding
(\Lambda,n)
-structure on the Lie algebroid
TM
is simply

\Lambda=\Pi_{M},\qquad n=id_{TM},

which trivially satisfies
[\Pi_{M},\Pi_{M}]=0
,
[id,id]=0
, and
id\circ\Pi_{M}^{\sharp}=\Pi_{M}^{\sharp}\circ id^{*}
.

Example 21
(Trivial Groupoid with Nontrivial Nijenhuis Tensor on the Lie Group)

Let
M
be a smooth manifold equipped with the zero Poisson structure
\Pi_{M}=0
. Let
G
be a Lie group equipped with a nontrivial right-invariant Poisson–Nijenhuis structure
(\Pi_{G},N_{G})
, where:

•

\Pi_{G}
is a right-invariant Poisson bivector on
G
,

•

N_{G}:TG\to TG
is a right-invariant Nijenhuis tensor,

•

They satisfy the compatibility condition
N_{G}\circ\Pi_{G}^{\sharp}=\Pi_{G}^{\sharp}\circ N_{G}^{*}
.

Explicit examples of such structures on Lie groups can be found in
[
5
]
,
[
21
]
and
[
6
]
.

Consider the trivial Lie groupoid
\Upsilon:=M\times G\times M\rightrightarrows M
as in Example
19
. Define a bivector
\Pi
on
\Upsilon
by

\Pi_{(x,a,y)}:=\Pi_{G}(a),

i.e.,
\Pi
depends only on the
G
-component and vanishes on the
M
-factors. Define a
(1,1)
-tensor
N
on
\Upsilon
by

N_{(x,a,y)}:=N_{G}(a)\quad\text{on }T_{(x,a,y)}\Upsilon,

where we identify
T_{(x,a,y)}\Upsilon\cong T_{x}M\oplus T_{a}G\oplus T_{y}M
and
N_{G}
acts only on the
T_{a}G
factor, while acting as the identity on
T_{x}M
and
T_{y}M
. More precisely,

N(v_{x},v_{a},v_{y}):=(v_{x},N_{G}(v_{a}),v_{y}).

Let
N_{M}:=id_{TM}
be the identity on the base.

Then:

1.

\Pi
is a Poisson bivector on
\Upsilon
:
Since
\Pi_{G}
is Poisson on
G
and
\Pi_{M}=0
, the Schouten bracket
[\Pi,\Pi]
vanishes because it reduces to
[\Pi_{G},\Pi_{G}]
on the
G
-factor and zero elsewhere.

2.

N
is a Nijenhuis tensor on
\Upsilon
:
Since
N_{G}
is a Nijenhuis tensor on
G
and
N
acts as the identity on the
M
-factors, the Nijenhuis torsion
[N,N]
vanishes because it reduces to
[N_{G},N_{G}]
on
G
and zero elsewhere.

3.

Compatibility:
On the
G
-factor, we have
N_{G}\circ\Pi_{G}^{\sharp}=\Pi_{G}^{\sharp}\circ N_{G}^{*}
by assumption. On the
M
-factors, both sides vanish because
\Pi_{M}=0
and
N
acts as the identity. Hence

N\circ\Pi^{\sharp}=\Pi^{\sharp}\circ N^{*}.

4.

Multiplicativity:
Both
\Pi
and
N
are right-invariant on
\Upsilon
because
\Pi_{G}
and
N_{G}
are right-invariant on
G
and the
M
-factors are unaffected by the groupoid multiplication.

Thus
(\Upsilon,\Pi,\mathbf{N})
is a right-invariant Poisson–Nijenhuis Lie groupoid with
\mathbf{N}=(N,N_{M})
.

Infinitesimal Counterpart

The Lie algebroid of
\Upsilon
is
A\Upsilon=TM\oplus(M\times\mathfrak{g})
, where
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}(G)
. The infinitesimal data
(\Lambda,n)
on
A\Upsilon
is given by:

\Lambda=\pi_{G},\qquad n=(id_{TM},\nu),

where
\pi_{G}\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g})
is the Poisson bivector on
\mathfrak{g}
corresponding to
\Pi_{G}
, and
\nu:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
is the linear Nijenhuis operator corresponding to
N_{G}
(see
[
6
]
,
[
21
]
). Explicitly:

\Lambda_{(x,\xi)}=\pi_{G}(\xi),\quad n_{(x,\xi)}(X,\xi)=(X,\nu(\xi)),\quad\forall x\in M,\xi\in\mathfrak{g}.

One verifies directly:

•

[\Lambda,\Lambda]=0
because
[\pi_{G},\pi_{G}]=0
,

•

[n,n]=0
because
[\nu,\nu]=0
,

•

n\circ\Lambda^{\sharp}=\Lambda^{\sharp}\circ n^{*}
because
\nu\circ\pi_{G}^{\sharp}=\pi_{G}^{\sharp}\circ\nu^{*}
.

Hence
(\Lambda,n)
satisfies the conditions of Theorem 13, and by the
s
-connected and
s
-simply connected assumption on
\Upsilon
(which holds if
G
is connected and simply connected), there is a one-to-one correspondence with the groupoid structure we constructed.

Remark 22

Example
19
and Example
20
illustrate the correspondence with the simplest choice
N=id
. Example
21
shows that nontrivial Poisson–Nijenhuis structures exist and fit into the correspondence framework. In particular, starting from a nontrivial structure on a Lie group
G
, we obtain a nontrivial structure on the trivial groupoid
M\times G\times M
by extending trivially over the base manifold
M
. The base Poisson structure
\Pi_{M}=0
ensures that the
M
-factors do not interfere with the compatibility conditions.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced right-invariant Poisson–Nijenhuis structures on Lie groupoids and established a one-to-one correspondence with
(\Lambda,n)
-structures on their Lie algebroids (Theorem 13). As a consequence, Corollary
18
provides a complete classification of such structures on
s
-connected and
s
-simply connected Lie groupoids in terms of infinitesimal algebroid data.

Physical interpretation.

From a physical perspective, a right-invariant Poisson–Nijenhuis structure
(\Pi,N)
on a Lie groupoid
G
encodes a
bi-Hamiltonian system
on a symmetric configuration space. The Poisson bivector
\Pi
defines a Hamiltonian structure, while the Nijenhuis tensor
N
acts as a
recursion operator
generating a second compatible Poisson structure
\Pi^{\prime}=N\Pi
. Such bi-Hamiltonian systems are known to be classically integrable, possessing an infinite hierarchy of conservation laws.

Our classification therefore states that integrable bi-Hamiltonian systems on an
s
-connected and
s
-simply connected Lie groupoid
G
are classified by infinitesimal data
(\Lambda,n)
on the Lie algebroid
AG
satisfying:

[\Lambda,\Lambda]=0,\qquad[n,n]=0,\qquad n\circ\Lambda^{\sharp}=\Lambda^{\sharp}\circ n^{*}.

Significance and future directions.

This result simplifies the classification of integrable systems on symmetric configuration spaces to an algebraic problem on the Lie algebroid, and conversely provides a systematic method for constructing new integrable systems from algebroid data. Future work includes extending the classification to non-invariant structures, studying deformations, and applying the framework to concrete physical systems such as gauge theories and lattice models.

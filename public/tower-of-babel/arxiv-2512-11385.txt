The multiplicity-one theorem for the superspeciality of hyperelliptic curves
Yuya Yamamoto

Abstract
The multiplicity-one theorem for the simultaneous equations characterizing the superspeciality of hyperelliptic curves was established by Igusa in 1958 for genus one, and later extended by Harashita and Yamamoto in 2026 to genus two. In this paper, we generalize this result to arbitrary genus. Our approach employs the Lauricella system of type D for hypergeometric series in 2 ​ g − 1 2g-1 variables, whose truncations (up to scalar multiplication) give the entries of a Cartier–Manin matrix. The multiplicity-one theorem is obtained through an analysis of equalities involving partial derivatives of these entries. Keywords: algebraic curves, superspecial curves, hypergeometric series, positive characteristic MSC 2020: 14H10, 33C65, 14G17, 11G20

Introduction

In this paper, we investigate the multiplicity of superspeciality for an entire family of hyperelliptic curves of arbitrary genus.
We begin by presenting the framework of our study, followed by our main theorems.

Let
p
be an odd prime
and
K
an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
.
Let
C
be the normalization of the projective model of

\quad y^{2}=f(x)\coloneqq x(x-1)(x-\lambda_{1})(x-\lambda_{2})\cdots(x-\lambda_{2g-1})

for
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{2g-1}\in K
with
\#\{0,1,\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{2g-1}\}=2g+1
.
Note that
C
is a hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
over
K
and conversely any hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
over
K
is written in this way.
A curve
C
over
K
is called
superspecial
if its Jacobian
{\rm Jac}(C)

is isomorphic to a product of supersingular elliptic curves. This is equivalent to that the Cartier operator on
H^{0}(C,{\varOmega}_{C})
is zero (cf.
[
9
, Theorem 4.1]
).
The matrix (called the Cartier–Manin matrix) representing the operator with respect to the basis
dx/y,xdx/y,\ldots,x^{g-1}dx/y
is described as

\begin{pmatrix}c_{p-1}&c_{p-2}&\dots&c_{p-g}\\
c_{2p-1}&c_{2p-2}&\dots&c_{2p-g}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
c_{gp-1}&c_{gp-2}&\dots&c_{gp-g}\end{pmatrix}

where
c_{k}
is the
x^{k}
-coefficient of
f(x)^{(p-1)/2}
, see
[
7
, p. 79]
.
We shall regard
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{2g-1}
as indeterminates and consider
c_{ip-j}
as polynomials in
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{2g-1}
for
1\leq i,j\leq g
.
The space
V(c_{ip-j}|1\leq i,j\leq g)
consisting of points where
c_{ip-j}
vanish for all
i,j
with
1\leq i,j\leq g
is called the
superspecial locus
, which is known to consist of finite points.
The aim of this paper is to prove the ideal generated by
c_{ip-j}
for
1\leq i,j\leq g
is of multiplicity-one at any point of
V(c_{ip-j}|1\leq i,j\leq g)
.
To achieve this, we use the Lauricella hypergeometric series of type (D) in
2g-1
variables and partial differential equations they satisfy.
The fact that entries of the Cartier–Manin matrix are described by truncations of Lauricella hypergeometric series is due to Ohashi and Harashita (
[
10
, Theorem 6.5]
), which we review in Definition
2.5
and Fact
3.3
.
Throughout this paper, we denote by
\mathbb{N}_{0}
the set of nonnegative integers, and we use a symbol
\left(x\mathrel{;}n\right)=x(x+1)(x+2)\cdots(x+n-1)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
(We consider
\left(x\mathrel{;}0\right)=1
for
n=0.
)
Lauricella hypergeometric series of type (D) in
2g-1
variables is defined as follows (cf. Definition
2.1
):

\mathcal{F}(a,b_{1},\ldots,b_{2g-1},c\,;z_{1},\ldots,z_{2g-1})\coloneqq\sum_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{2g-1})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2g-1}}A_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{2g-1}}z^{n_{1}}_{1}\cdots z^{n_{2g-1}}_{2g-1},

(1)

where

A_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{2g-1}}\coloneqq\frac{\left(a\mathrel{;}\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{2g-1}n_{k}\right)\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{2g-1}\left(b_{k}\mathrel{;}n_{k}\right)}{\left(c\mathrel{;}\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{2g-1}n_{k}\right)\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{2g-1}\left(1\mathrel{;}n_{k}\right)}

(2)

with
a,b_{1},\ldots,b_{2g-1},c\in\mathbb{C},-c\notin\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
In this paper, the hypergeometric series is considered as an element of the ring of formal power series in
z_{1},\ldots,z_{2g-1}
.

The hypergeometric series
w\coloneqq\mathcal{F}(a,b_{1},\ldots,b_{2g-1},c\,;z_{1},\ldots,z_{2g-1})
satisfies the partial differential equations
\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell}}w=0
for
\ell=1,\ldots,2g-1

and
\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell,\,m}}w=0
for
1\leq\ell<m\leq 2g-1
, where

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{\ell}(1-z_{\ell})\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{z_{\ell}}^{2}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq 2g-1,\\
k\neq\ell\end{subarray}}{z_{k}(1-z_{\ell})\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{z_{\ell}}\partial{z_{k}}}}

\displaystyle+\left(c-(a+b_{\ell}+1)z_{\ell}\right)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_{\ell}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq 2g-1,\\
k\neq\ell\end{subarray}}{b_{\ell}z_{k}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_{k}}}-ab_{\ell}

and

\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell,\,m}}\coloneqq(z_{\ell}-z_{m})\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z_{\ell}\partial z_{m}}-b_{m}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_{\ell}}+b_{\ell}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_{m}}.

Let
j\in\{1,\ldots,g\}
.
Let
\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell}^{(j)}}
and
\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell,\,m}}
, for
\ell,m\in\{1,\ldots,2g-1\}
with
\ell<m
, denote the partial differential operators
{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}
and
\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell,\,m}}
, respectively, associated with
a=(2g+1)/2-j
,
(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{2g-1})=(1/2,1/2,\ldots,1/2)
, and
c=g-j+1
.

From the result (
[
10
, Theorem 6.5]
) of Ohashi–Harashita we mentioned above, we naturally expect that the operators
\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell}^{(j)}}
and
\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell,\,m}}
annihilate
c_{ip-j}
.
The first theorem asserts that this indeed holds:

Theorem A
.

For every
i,j
with
1\leq i,j\leq g
, the entry
c_{ip-j}
of the Cartier–Manin matrix
satisfies

\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell}^{(j)}}c_{ip-j}=0

for
\ell=1,2,\ldots,2g-1
and

\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell,\,m}}c_{ip-j}=0

for
1\leq\ell<m\leq 2g-1
.

In the genus-one case, it is well-known (cf.
[
1
, p.255]
and
[
11
, Theorem V.4.1]
) that the elliptic curve of Legendre form
y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-t)
over
K
is supersingular if and only if

H_{p}(t)=0
, where
\displaystyle H_{p}(t)\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{(p-1)/2}{\binom{(p-1)/2}{i}}^{2}t^{i}
, which is equal, up to the sign
(-1)^{(p-1)/2}
, to the coefficient of
x^{p-1}
in the expression
\left(x(x-1)(x-t)\right)^{(p-1)/2}
.
The separability of the polynomial
H_{p}(t)
was proved by Igusa
[
5
]
.
The key part of his proof lies in the use of a differential operator which vanishes
H_{p}(z)
:

\mathop{\mathcal{D}}=z(1-z)d^{2}/dz^{2}+(c-(a+b+1)z)d/dz-ab

for
a=1/2
,
b=1/2
and
c=1
.

For
a,b,c\in\mathbb{C}
with
-c\notin\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
over
\mathbb{C}
, the differential operator
\mathop{\mathcal{D}}
annihilates the Gauss hypergeometric series
\displaystyle F(a,b,c\,;z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\dfrac{\left(a\mathrel{;}n\right)\left(b\mathrel{;}n\right)}{\left(c\mathrel{;}n\right)\left(1\mathrel{;}n\right)}z^{n}
.
For the choice
a=1/2,b=1/2,c=1
,
H_{p}(t)
equals the truncation of
F(1/2,1/2,1\,;z)
by degree
m
.

For special families of genus-
2
curves, similar results have been obtained by Ibukiyama, Katsura, and Oort
[
4
, Proposition 1.14]
.
The Cartier–Manin matrix for the curve of genus two in Rosenhain form
y^{2}=f(x)\coloneqq x(x-1)(x-\lambda_{1})(x-\lambda_{2})(x-\lambda_{3})
is

\begin{pmatrix}c_{p-1}&c_{p-2}\\
c_{2p-1}&c_{2p-2}\end{pmatrix},

and the result on multiplicity-one for this entire family of genus-two curves has been obtained by Harashita and Yamamoto
[
3
]
.
In
[
3
]
, equalities involving partial derivatives of
c_{ip-j}
, called contiguity relations, are as follows; they played a key role in proving multiplicity-one(
[
3
, Theorem 5.3]
).

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{3}(z_{k}^{2}-z_{k})\partial_{k}\right)c_{ip-1}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{1}{2}(z_{1}+z_{2}+z_{3}-2)c_{ip-1}-\dfrac{1}{2}c_{ip-2},

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{3}(1-z_{k})\partial_{k}\right)c_{ip-2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}(c_{ip-1}+c_{ip-2}).

In this paper, we prove more fundamental equalities (Theorem
3.5
and Corollary
3.6
) in the framework of arbitrary genus which generate the contiguity relations.
With the help of these equations and Theorem
A
, we obtain our main theorems:

Theorem B
.

For any solution
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{2g-1})
of the equations

c_{ip-j}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{2g-1})=0

with
1\leq i,j\leq g
, the curve
y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-\lambda_{1})(x-\lambda_{2})\cdots(x-\lambda_{2g-1})
is nonsingular.
More precisely,
0,1,\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{2g-1}
are distinct.

Theorem C
.

The scheme defined by the ideal generated by
\left\{c_{ip-j}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}1\leq i,j\leq g\right\}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{2g-1}]
is reduced.
(This is equivalent to saying that the scheme is nonsingular.
It is well-known that the scheme is zero-dimensional.)

Remark 1.1
.

If
g>(p-1)/2
, there is no superspecial hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
in characteristic
p
by Ekedahl
[
2
, Theorem 1.1 in Section 2]
, whence the above theorems hold trivially.
Hence we will assume
g\leq(p-1)/2
in the proof (cf. Section 3).

Theorem
C
can be regarded as a generalization of the result by Igusa
[
5
]
in the case of genus-one and the result by Harashita–Yamamoto
[
3
]
in the case of genus-two.
We call this theorem
the multiplicity-one theorem for the superspeciality of hyperelliptic curves
y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-z_{1})(x-z_{2})\cdots(x-z_{2g-1})
.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, We review some facts about the Lauricella hypergeometric series and its truncation, in connection with Cartier–Manin matrices.
In Section 3, we collect fundamental properties of the Lauricella series related to superspeciality and study partial differential equations involving entries of a Cartier–Manin matrix, which will be used in the proof of the multiplicity-one theorem.
In Section 4, We employ induction on the genus to prove Theorem
B
, and subsequently Theorem
C
by using Theorem
B
, thereby deducing the multiplicity-one theorem for every genus.

.

The Lauricella hypergeometric series in

Remark 2.3
.

Let

be any field.
The partial differential operators

2.2
Cartier–Manin matrices and hypergeometric series

Let
p
be an odd prime and
K
an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
.
Let
g
be a natural number.
A hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
over
K
is realized by
y^{2}=f(x)
with separable polynomial
f(x)\in K[x]
of degree
2g+1
.
Let us review its Cartier–Manin matrix.

Definition 2.4
.

For the hyperelliptic curve
C
defined by
y^{2}=f(x)
with

f(x)=x(x-1)(x-z_{1})\cdots(x-z_{2g-1}),

let

f(x)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{p-1}{2}(2g+1)}{c_{k}x^{k}}.

be the expansion of
f(x)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}
.
The Cartier–Manin matrix of
C
is defined as

M={\Bigl(c_{ip-j}\Bigr)}_{1\leq i,j\leq g}

(cf.
[
12
, Section 2]
).

We review truncations of the Lauricella hypergeometric series and describe
c_{ip-j}
in relation to them, following the work of Ohashi and Harashita
[
10
]
.

Definition 2.5

(Ohashi–Harashita
[
10
, Definition 6.4 and Example 6.10]
)
.

For
1\leq i,j\leq g
, we set
a^{\prime}\coloneqq(2g+1)/2-j=g-j+1/2,\,b^{\prime}\coloneqq 1/2,\,c^{\prime}\coloneqq a^{\prime}+1-1/2=g-j+1,\,d^{\prime}\coloneqq(p-1)(2g+1)/2-ip+j
.
For the Lauricella hypergeometric series

\mathcal{F}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},\ldots,b^{\prime},c^{\prime}\,;z_{1},\ldots,z_{2g-1})=\sum_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{2g-1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}A^{(i,j)}_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{2g-1}}z_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots z_{2g-1}^{n_{2g-1}}

with

A^{(i,j)}_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{2g-1}}\coloneqq\frac{\left(a^{\prime}\mathrel{;}\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{2g-1}n_{k}\right)\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{2g-1}\left(b^{\prime}\mathrel{;}n_{k}\right)}{\left(c^{\prime}\mathrel{;}\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{2g-1}n_{k}\right)\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{2g-1}\left(1\mathrel{;}n_{k}\right)},

(8)

we define its truncation

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{i,j}

to be the sum of
z^{n_{1}}\cdots z^{n_{2g-1}}
-terms
for
(2g-1)
-tuples
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{2g-1})
satisfying

\begin{cases}{\displaystyle n_{k}\leq\dfrac{p-1}{2}\quad(k=1,\ldots,2g-1)},\\
{\displaystyle d^{\prime}-\dfrac{p-1}{2}\leq n_{1}+\cdots+n_{2g-1}\leq d^{\prime}.}\end{cases}

(9)

We denote the set of exponents of the monomials in
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{i,j}
by
\mathsf{supp}^{(i,j)}
, and Lemma
3.2
below implies

\mathsf{supp}^{(i,j)}=\left\{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2g-1}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\begin{array}[]{l}n_{k}\leq\dfrac{p-1}{2}\quad(k=1,\ldots,2g-1),\\
d^{\prime}-\dfrac{p-1}{2}\leq\lvert\bm{n}\rvert\leq d^{\prime}\end{array}\right\}.

(10)

A priori,
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{i,j}
is an element of
\mathbb{Q}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{2g-1}]
but since the denominator of the coefficient of each term of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{i,j}
is coprime to
p
(see Lemma
3.2
), we regard
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{i,j}

as an element of
{\mathbb{F}_{p}}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{2g-1}]
.
We will write
\widetilde{A}_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{2g-1}}^{(i,j)}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
for the
z_{1}^{n_{1}}\dotsm z_{2g-1}^{n_{2g-1}}
-coefficient of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{i,j}
when we view
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{i,j}
as an element of
{\mathbb{F}_{p}}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{2g-1}]
.

3.1
Properties of Lauricella series on superspeciality

We collect recurrence relations of
{A}_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{2g-1}}^{(i,j)}

We also review the relation between the Cartier–Manin matrix of a hyperelliptic curve and the associated Lauricella hypergeometric series.

The following lemma is a straightforward generalization of
[

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,2g-1\}
.

(i)

(Standard recurrence relation) We have

{A}_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{k}}^{(i,j)}=\frac{(g-j+\frac{1}{2}+\lvert\bm{n}\rvert)(\frac{1}{2}+n_{k})}{(g-j+1+\lvert\bm{n}\rvert)(1+n_{k})}{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(i,j)}

for
n_{1},\ldots,n_{2g-1}\geq 0
.

(ii)

For
n_{1},\ldots,n_{2g-1}\geq 0
, and for

\bm{n}\neq 0

\displaystyle\frac{\left(a^{\prime}\mathrel{;}\lvert\bm{n}\rvert\right)}{\left(c^{\prime}\mathrel{;}\lvert\bm{n}\rvert\right)}

The following relation between the entries of the Cartier–Manin matrices and the truncations
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{i,j}

Fact 3.3
.

Assume

Over
\mathbb{F}_{p}

The following lemma provides a concrete description of the constant in Fact
3.3

Lemma 3.4
.

If

(cf. Remark
1.1
), then over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, the constant
\left(c^{\prime}\mathrel{;}d^{\prime}\right)/\left(a^{\prime}\mathrel{;}d^{\prime}\right)

and this is nonzero.

Proof.

By
[
10
, Example 6.9]
, we know that

\frac{\left(c^{\prime}\mathrel{;}d^{\prime}\right)}{\left(a^{\prime}\mathrel{;}d^{\prime}\right)}=\frac{(p(2g-2i+1)-1)!!}{(p(2g-2i+1)-2)!!}\frac{(2g-2j-1)!!}{(2g-2j)!!}.

(13)

Hence, it suffices to show

\frac{(p(2g-2i+1)-1)!!}{(p(2g-2i+1)-2)!!}=(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\frac{(2g-2i)!!}{(2g-2i-1)!!}.

Since
p(2g-2i+1)-1
is even,
(p(2g-2i+1)-1)!!
is the product of the elements of
A\coloneqq\{p(2g-2i+1)-1,p(2g-2i+1)-3,\ldots,2\}
, whose size is
(p(2g-2i+1)-1)/2=p(g-i)+(p-1)/2
.
The subset of
p
-divisible numbers in this set is
A^{\prime}\coloneqq\{p(2g-2i),p(2g-2i-2),\ldots,2p\}
, whose size is
g-i
.
Similarly,
(p(2g-2i+1)-2)!!
is the product of the elements of
B\coloneqq\{p(2g-2i+1)-2,p(2g-2i+1)-4,\ldots,1\}
, whose size is
p(g-i)+(p-1)/2
, and the subset of the
p
-divisible numbers contained in

is

and its size is
g-i
.

In addition, observe that over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, multiplication by

maps

to
B
, and hence
A\setminus A^{\prime}
to
B\setminus B^{\prime}
.
Moreover,

.
From these considerations, we obtain (

\frac{(p(2g-2i+1)-1)!!}{(p(2g-2i+1)-2)!!}=(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\frac{p^{g-i}(2g-2i)!!}{p^{g-i}(2g-2i-1)!!}=(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\frac{(2g-2i)!!}{(2g-2i-1)!!}.

When

, we have
2g-2i\leq 2g-2\leq p-1
, and similarly

.
These inequalities establish the claim of well-definedness and non-vanishing.
∎

4
Proof of the multiplicity-one theorem

We clarify what it means for an ideal to have multiplicity-one in an Artinian ring, and we prove the multiplicity-one theorem by means of the equalities involving partial derivatives of
c_{ip-j}
established in Subsection 3.2.

4.1
The Jacobian criterion

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field.
Let
R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
Let
I
be an ideal of
R
such that
\left.R\middle/\mathord{I}\right.
is an Artinian ring (i.e.,

is zero-dimensional).

Definition 4.1
.

We say
I
is of
multiplicity-one
if
\dim_{k}(\left.R\middle/\mathord{I}\right.)_{\mathfrak{P}}=1
for any
\mathfrak{P}
in
\mathrm{Spec}(\left.R\middle/\mathord{I}\right.)
.

The following lemma is well-known.
See for example
[
3
, Lemma 5.2]
for the proof.

Lemma 4.2
.

I=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
is muplicplicity one if and only if for any
\mathfrak{P}
in
\mathrm{Spec}(\left.R\middle/\mathord{I}\right.)
the Jacobian matrix
\left(\partial f_{j}/\partial x_{i}\right)
at
\mathfrak{P}
is of rank
n
.

4.2
Proof of the multiplicity-one theorem

In this subsection, we complete the proof of the the multiplicity-one theorem.
We first state a lemma that will be used repeatedly.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

be a vector space over
K
and let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{r}
be distinct elements in

Let

be a nonnegative integer with

\displaystyle v_{1}+v_{2}\cdots+v_{r}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}v_{1}+\alpha_{2}v_{2}\cdots+\alpha_{r}v_{r}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}_{1}v_{1}+\alpha^{2}_{2}v_{2}\cdots+\alpha^{2}_{r}v_{r}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\alpha^{m}_{1}v_{1}+\alpha^{m}_{2}v_{2}\cdots+\alpha^{m}_{r}v_{r}

\displaystyle=0.

Then, the subspace spanned by

can be spanned by any

.
Specifically, if

.

Proof.

We prove the lemma by induction on

When

, (

) is nothing other than

, and thus the lemma holds.

Now assume that

and that the lemma holds for

When

Thus, we assume

.
By symmetry, it suffices to prove the lemma for the choice

.
From the equalities (
21
), we eliminate

, by multiplying with a suitable power of

and subtracting, to obtain

\displaystyle(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{r})v_{1}+\cdots+(\alpha_{r-1}-\alpha_{r})v_{r-1}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{r})v_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{r-1}(\alpha_{r-1}-\alpha_{r})v_{r-1}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\alpha^{m-1}_{1}(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{r})v_{1}+\cdots+\alpha^{m-1}_{r-1}(\alpha_{r-1}-\alpha_{r})v_{r-1}

Our inductive hypothesis for

are distinct,

Hence

lies in the span of

Eliminating

Lemma 4.6
.

v_{1,j},v_{2,j},\ldots,v_{g,j}
are linearly independent for
j=1,\ldots,g
.

Proof.

We first claim that
c_{p-j},c_{2p-j},\ldots,c_{gp-j}
are linearly independent over
K
.
For any
i\in\{1,\ldots,g-1\}
, the difference between the lowest degree among the monomials of
c_{ip-j}
and the highest degree among those of
c_{(i+1)p-j}
is
\left((p-1)g-ip+j\right)-\left((p-1)(g+1/2)-(i+1)p+j\right)=(p+1)/2>0
.
Hence
\mathsf{supp}^{(1,j)},\ldots,\mathsf{supp}^{(g,j)}
are disjoint, and the calim follows.

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
v_{1,j},v_{2,j},\ldots,v_{g,j}
are linearly dependent.
Then there is a nontrivial linear combination
\alpha_{1}v_{1,j}+\alpha_{2}v_{2,j}+\cdots+\alpha_{g}v_{g,j}=0
with
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g}
not all zero.
There exists
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,g\}
such that
\alpha_{\ell}\neq 0
.
Write

v_{\ell,j}=\widetilde{\alpha}_{1}v_{1,j}+\cdots+\widetilde{\alpha}_{\ell-1}v_{\ell-1,j}+\widetilde{\alpha}_{\ell+1}v_{\ell+1,j}+\cdots+\widetilde{\alpha}_{g}v_{g,j},

(25)

where
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}\coloneqq-\alpha^{-1}_{\ell}\alpha_{i}
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,g\}\setminus\{\ell\}
.
We consider, for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,g\}
, the expansion of
c_{ip-j}

at
\lambda\coloneqq(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{2g-1})
:
As the
z_{\ell}
-degree (
\ell=1,\ldots,2g-1
) of
c_{ip-j}
is at most
(p-1)/2
,
we can write
c_{ip-j}
as

\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{2g-1}}\frac{1}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{2g-1}!}\frac{\partial^{k_{1}+\dots+k_{2g-1}}c_{ip-j}}{\partial z_{1}^{k_{1}}\dots\partial z_{2g-1}^{k_{2g-1}}}(\lambda)(z_{1}-\lambda_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(z_{2g-1}-\lambda_{2g-1})^{k_{2g-1}}.

From (
25
), the expansion at
\lambda
of
c_{\ell p-j}
is a linear combination of those of
c_{ip-j}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,g\}\setminus\{\ell\}
up to degree one.
Since
c_{p-j},\ldots,c_{gp-j}
satisfy the same partial differential equations in Theorem
A
, an inductive argument shows that
c_{\ell p-j}=\widetilde{\alpha}_{1}c_{p-j}+\cdots+\widetilde{\alpha}_{\ell-1}c_{(\ell-1)p-j}+\widetilde{\alpha}_{\ell+1}c_{(\ell+1)p-j}+\cdots+\widetilde{\alpha}_{g}c_{gp-j}
, contradicting the first paragraph.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Any
g\times g
submatrix of
\begin{pmatrix}v_{1,1}&v_{2,1}&\dots&v_{g,1}\end{pmatrix}
is of rank
g
.

Proof.

We choose distinct
g
elements
k[1],k[2],\ldots,k[g]
from
\{1,2,\ldots,2g-1\}
arbitrarily, and prove the lemma for the submatrix formed by
w_{k[1]}^{(1)},\ldots,w_{k[g]}^{(1)}
.
We claim that the row space of
\begin{pmatrix}v_{1,1}&v_{2,1}&\dots&v_{g,1}\end{pmatrix}
, which is spanned by
w_{1}^{(1)},w_{2}^{(1)},\ldots,w_{2g-1}^{(1)}
, can be spanned by
w_{k[1]}^{(1)},\ldots,w_{k[g]}^{(1)}
.
Once we prove the claim, the lemma follows by Lemma
4.6
.
By Corollary
3.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\lambda_{1}-1)w_{1}^{(1)}+\cdots+\lambda_{2g-1}(\lambda_{2g-1}-1)w_{2g-1}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=0,

(26)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{2}(\lambda_{1}-1)w_{1}^{(1)}+\cdots+\lambda_{2g-1}^{2}(\lambda_{2g-1}-1)w_{2g-1}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{g-1}(\lambda_{1}-1)w_{1}^{(1)}+\cdots+\lambda_{2g-1}^{g-1}(\lambda_{2g-1}-1)w_{2g-1}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=0.

From these equalities and Proposition
4.5
, Lemma
4.3
applied with
v_{1}=\lambda_{1}(\lambda_{1}-1)w_{1}^{(1)},\ldots,v_{r}=\lambda_{2g-1}(\lambda_{2g-1}-1)w_{2g-1}^{(1)}
and with
m=g-2,r=2g-1
, implies that the span of
\lambda_{1}(\lambda_{1}-1)w_{1}^{(1)},\lambda_{2}(\lambda_{2}-1)w_{2}^{(1)},\ldots,\lambda_{2g-1}(\lambda_{2g-1}-1)w_{2g-1}^{(1)}
equals the span of
\lambda_{k[1]}(\lambda_{k[1]}-1)w_{k[1]}^{(1)},\lambda_{k[2]}(\lambda_{k[2]}-1)w_{k[2]}^{(1)},\ldots,\lambda_{k[g]}(\lambda_{k[g]}-1)w_{k[g]}^{(1)}
.
Since
\lambda_{1}(\lambda_{1}-1),\ldots,\lambda_{2g-1}(\lambda_{2g-1}-1)
are units in
K
, these spans coincide:

\langle w_{1}^{(1)},w_{2}^{(1)},\ldots,w_{2g-1}^{(1)}\rangle=\langle w_{k[1]}^{(1)},\ldots,w_{k[g]}^{(1)}\rangle,

and the claim is proved.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

The rank of
J(\lambda)
is
2g-1
.

Proof.

We prove that the row rank of
J(\lambda)
is
2g-1
.
For
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,g-1\}
, define

\widehat{w}_{\ell}\coloneqq\lambda^{\ell}_{1}(\lambda_{1}-1)w_{1}+\cdots+\lambda^{\ell}_{2g-1}(\lambda_{2g-1}-1)w_{2g-1},

which is in the row space of
J(\lambda)
(see Notation
4.4
).
We show that
2g-1
row vectors

w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{g},\widehat{w}_{1},\widehat{w}_{2},\ldots,\widehat{w}_{g-1}

are linearly independent.
Suppose that we have an arbitrary linear relation

\alpha_{1}w_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{g}w_{g}+\beta_{1}\widehat{w}_{1}+\cdots+\beta_{g-1}\widehat{w}_{g-1}=0,

(27)

where
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{g-1}\in K
.
By Corollary
3.6
,
\widehat{w}_{\ell}\,(\ell=1,\ldots,g-1)
looks as follows:

\widehat{w}_{\ell}=\begin{pNiceMatrix},

(28)

where each symbol
\mathbf{0},\bigstar,\ast
is comprised of
g
entries, and the symbol
\bigstar
at the position
j=g-\ell+1
is
\lambda^{\ell}_{1}(\lambda_{1}-1)w^{(g-\ell+1)}_{1}+\cdots+\lambda^{\ell}_{2g-1}(\lambda_{2g-1}-1)w^{(g-\ell+1)}_{2g-1}
.
Since, for any
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,g-1\}
, the first
g
entries in the position of
j=1
of
\widehat{w}_{\ell}
are zero, from (
27
) we have
\alpha_{1}w^{(1)}_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{g}w^{(1)}_{g}=0
.
By Lemma
4.7
, we obtain
\alpha_{1}=\cdots=\alpha_{g}=0
, and (
27
) becomes
\beta_{1}\widehat{w}_{1}+\cdots+\beta_{g-1}\widehat{w}_{g-1}=0
.

Here, we claim that for any
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,g-1\}
,
\bigstar
in (
28
) is nonzero.
This will lead to the linear independence of
\widehat{w}_{1},\widehat{w}_{2},\ldots,\widehat{w}_{g-1}
.
To verify this claim, suppose to the contrary that
\bigstar
is zero for some
\ell
:

\lambda^{\ell}_{1}(\lambda_{1}-1)w^{(g-\ell+1)}_{1}+\cdots+\lambda^{\ell}_{2g-1}(\lambda_{2g-1}-1)w^{(g-\ell+1)}_{2g-1}=0.

(29)

By Theorem
3.5
(i), we obtain the relations
w^{(j)}_{k}=\lambda_{k}w^{(j-1)}_{k}
for any
k\in\{1,\ldots,2g-1\}
and for any
j\in\{2,3,\ldots,g\}
.
From these relations, we rewrite (
29
) as

\lambda_{1}^{g}(\lambda_{1}-1)w_{1}^{(1)}+\cdots+\lambda_{2g-1}^{g}(\lambda_{2g-1}-1)w_{2g-1}^{(1)}=0.

From this equality together with the equalities (
26
), and Proposition
4.5
, we apply Lemma
4.3
with
v_{1}=\lambda_{1}(\lambda_{1}-1)w_{1}^{(1)},\ldots,v_{r}=\lambda_{2g-1}(\lambda_{2g-1}-1)w_{2g-1}^{(1)}
and with
m=g-1,r=2g-1
, to deduce that
w_{g}^{(1)}
is a linear combination of
w_{1}^{(1)},w_{2}^{(1)},\ldots,w_{g-1}^{(1)}
.
This contradicts the linear independence of
w_{1}^{(1)},w_{2}^{(1)},\ldots,w_{g}^{(1)}
(Lemma
4.7
), thereby establishing our claim.

Thus,
\widehat{w}_{1},\widehat{w}_{2},\ldots,\widehat{w}_{g-1}
are linearly independent and hence
\beta_{1}=\cdots=\beta_{g-1}=0
, and we have completed the proof.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

In the proof above, choosing any distinct

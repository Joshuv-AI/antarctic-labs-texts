Systematic Magnetic Structure Generation Based on Oriented Spin Space Groups: Formulation, Applications, and High-Throughput First-Principles Calculations Preprint: perspective
Takuya Nomoto, Kohei Shinohara, Hikaru Watanabe, Ryotaro Arita

Abstract
We propose a framework for generating magnetic structures, inspired by the concept of oriented spin space groups (SSGs): magnetic structures are first generated as totally symmetric representations of an SSG and are then rotated such that they belong to the maximal magnetic space group of the SSG, which we term spin-symmetry-adapted (SSA) structures and oriented SSA structures, respectively. This is a natural framework to enforce fixed magnetic moment magnitudes on the symmetry-equivalent sites as well as to exploit the spin–orbit coupling (SOC)-induced hierarchy of energy scales. To examine the present scheme, we analyze the MAGNDATA database and find that 77% of the reported structures are reproducible at the SSG level, among which 82% are fully reproduced within the oriented SSG scheme, regardless of their spin-only group types or propagation vectors. When the k-index is fixed to its experimental value, the number of magnetic structures generated is on average about nine, also demonstrating the high efficiency of this scheme. Next, to quantitatively assess computational and predictive performance, we perform spin density functional theory calculations for 283 materials, first carrying out self-consistent calculations for SSA structures without SOC, followed by fixed-charge calculations including SOC for the descendant oriented SSA structures. The experimental magnetic structures are reproduced as energetically most stable in 82% of cases at the SSG level without SOC and in 76% of cases at the oriented SSG level with SOC, showing that the fixed-charge scheme enables accurate evaluation of SOC-induced energy differences at low computational cost. The characteristic energy scale among oriented SSA structures is only ∼ 0.29 \sim 0.29 meV per magnetic atom, about 300 times smaller than that of distinct SSA structures, suggesting that calculations with SOC confined to the lowest-energy manifold of oriented SSA structures are sufficient for reliable predictions. These results demonstrate that oriented SSG-based enumeration, combined with the two-step calculations for SSA and oriented SSA structures, provides an efficient and robust route for large-scale magnetic-structure prediction.

II.1
Group structure of spin space group

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a subgroup of the direct product of the Euclidean group
E(3)
and the three-dimensional orthogonal group
O(3)
.
If the following
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{X})
is a space group,
\mathcal{X}
is called a spin space group (SSG),

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{X})=\left\{g\in E(3)\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\exists\mathbf{U}\in O(3)s.t.(g,\mathbf{U})\in\mathcal{X}\right\}.

(1)

For a spin space group
\mathcal{X}
, we call
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{X})
a family space group of
\mathcal{X}
.
A maximal space subgroup of a spin space group
\mathcal{X}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{X})=\left\{g\in E(3)\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}(g,\mathbf{I})\in\mathcal{X}\right\},

(2)

where
\mathbf{I}
is the identity rotation in
O(3)
.
The maximal space subgroup
\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{X})
is a normal subgroup of
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{X})
.

A family spin point group of spin space group
\mathcal{X}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})=\left\{\mathbf{U}\in O(3)\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\exists g\in E(3)s.t.(g,\mathbf{U})\in\mathcal{X}\right\}.

(3)

A spin-only group of spin space group
\mathcal{X}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}(\mathcal{X})=\left\{\mathbf{U}\in O(3)\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}((\mathbf{I},\mathbf{0}),\mathbf{U})\in\mathcal{X}\right\}.

(4)

The spin-only group
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}(\mathcal{X})
is a normal subgroup of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
.
The direct product group
\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{X})\times\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}(\mathcal{X})
is a normal subgroup of
\mathcal{X}
.

From Goursat’s lemma
37
;
67
, there is a group isomorphism between the quotient groups

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}/(\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{X})\times\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}(\mathcal{X}))\cong\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{X})/\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{X})\cong\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})/\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}(\mathcal{X}),

(5)

where we let
U:\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{X})/\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{X})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})/\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}(\mathcal{X})
be the isomorphism.
Then, spin space group
\mathcal{X}
is uniquely characterized by the triplet
(\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{X}),\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}(\mathcal{X}),U)
.

From Hermann’s theorem

, there exists a unique group
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X})
satisfying the following two conditions:
(1)
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X})
is a translationengleiche subgroup (t-subgroup) of
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{X})
, where their translation subgroups are identical;
(2)
\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{X})
is a klassengleiche subgroup (k-subgroup) of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X})
, where their point groups are identical.
Here,
\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{X})
is a normal subgroup of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X})
because
\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{X})\trianglelefteq\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{X})
and
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X})\leq\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{X})
.
Also,
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X})
is a normal subgroup of
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{X})
because
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X})
is uniquely defined
as a subgroup of
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{X})
with the above two conditions.

Conversely, the group isomorphism in Eq. (

\mathcal{G}

For every normal space subgroup

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{G})=\left\{(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{t})\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{t})\in\mathcal{G}\right\},

When a spin space group is a stabilizer of a magnetic structure, its spin-only group

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{I=1}^{N_{t}}\bigcup_{m=1}^{N_{k}}(g_{i_{I}}(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{t}_{m}),\mathbf{U}_{Im})(\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}),

II.3
Generation of spin-symmetry-adapted structures from spin space group

For a given family space group
\mathcal{G}
, a spin-only group
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}
, and k-index
N_{k}
, we first enumerate spin space groups
\mathcal{X}
using the procedure in Sec.

.
By construction, each Wyckoff position of
\mathcal{G}

\mathcal{G}

\mathcal{X}

\mathcal{X}

\mathcal{X}

II.4
Practical choices of family space group

For a given nonmagnetic structure, the natural first choice of the family space group in Sec.
II.3
is its space group
\mathcal{G}_{0}
.
However, a combination of
\mathcal{G}_{0}
and a specified spin-only group
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}
may admit only the trivial solution in Eq. (
16
), yielding no nonzero symmetry-adapted magnetic moments.
When this occurs, we consider its maximal t-subgroups
\mathcal{G}_{0}^{\prime}
that preserve the multiplicities
119
of the given Wyckoff positions of magnetic sites, and use
\mathcal{G}_{0}^{\prime}
as the family space group.
This procedure is repeated until we obtain nonzero symmetry-adapted magnetic moments.
Because the given Wyckoff positions are not split in
\mathcal{G}_{0}^{\prime}
, the resulting symmetry-adapted magnetic moments have identical magnitudes for magnetic sites in the same given Wyckoff position.

III
Enumerating symmetry-adapted magnetic structures by subduing SSG to MSG

Until now, we have discussed SSGs up to
O(3)
transformations in the spin space, and the spin axis of a resulting symmetry-adapted magnetic structure is not fixed in general.
However, in many cases, the spin axes are aligned along specific crystallographic directions or high-symmetry directions due to SOC.
To describe such situations, we here present a systematic procedure to enumerate SSGs with maximal magnetic space group correspondences from a given SSG.
We call such enumerated SSGs oriented spin space groups (oriented SSGs) hereafter, following Ref.
70
, and use them to describe both a SSG and a magnetic space group (MSG) of a magnetic structure simultaneously.

We define a family point group of
\mathcal{X}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})=\left\{\mathbf{R}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}((\mathbf{R},\bm{\tau}),\mathbf{U})\in\mathcal{X}\right\}.

(17)

We call
\mathcal{X}
triclinic if
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})=1\text{ or }\overline{1}
.
When
\mathcal{X}
is triclinic, there is no restriction on spin axes.
Thus, we assume
\mathcal{X}
is not triclinic in what follows.

We denote the time-reversal operation as
\theta
and set

\displaystyle\theta_{s}=\begin{cases}1&(s=1),\\
\theta&(s=-1).\end{cases}

(18)

We write a MSG induced from
\mathcal{X}
with spin transformation
\mathbf{Q}\in O(3)
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X},\mathbf{Q})

\displaystyle=\left\{(\mathbf{R},\bm{\tau})\theta_{\det\mathbf{U}}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\begin{array}[]{l}((\mathbf{R},\bm{\tau}),\mathbf{U})\in\mathcal{X},\\
(\det\mathbf{R})\mathbf{R}=(\det\mathbf{U})\mathbf{Q}^{-1}\mathbf{U}\mathbf{Q}\end{array}\right\},

where spin operation
((\mathbf{R},\bm{\tau}),\mathbf{U})
is mapped to a magnetic operation if
(\det\mathbf{R})\mathbf{R}=(\det\mathbf{U})\mathbf{U}

98

In this paper, we define an oriented SSG of
\mathcal{X}
as a pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathbf{Q})
with equivalence relation
(\mathcal{X},\mathbf{Q})\sim(\mathcal{X},\mathbf{Q}^{\prime})
if
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X},\mathbf{Q})
and
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X},\mathbf{Q}^{\prime})
are conjugate by certain

.
Furthermore, we only consider oriented SSGs such that
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X},\mathbf{Q})
is maximal among all MSGs induced from
\mathcal{X}
by varying
\mathbf{Q}\in O(3)
.

III.1
Noncoplanar oriented spin space group

First, we consider a noncoplanar SSG
\mathcal{X}

\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}(\mathcal{X})=1
.
For a subgroup
\mathcal{Y}
of
\mathcal{X}
with
\mathcal{Y}\trianglerighteq\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{X}))
,
\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{Y},\mathbf{Q})
have a one-to-one correspondence for some
\mathbf{Q}\in O(3)
if
(\det\mathbf{R})\mathbf{R}=(\det\mathbf{U})\mathbf{Q}^{-1}\mathbf{U}\mathbf{Q}
for all
((\mathbf{R},\bm{\tau}),\mathbf{U})\in\mathcal{Y}
.
This condition can be seen as an equivalence of two orthogonal representations of
\mathcal{Y}/\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{X}))
,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}:\mathcal{Y}/\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{X}))\to SO(3);

\displaystyle\quad((\mathbf{R},\bm{\tau}),\mathbf{U}^{\prime})\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{X}))\mapsto(\det\mathbf{R})\mathbf{R},

(21)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathrm{s}}:\mathcal{Y}/\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{X}))\to SO(3);

\displaystyle\quad((\mathbf{R},\bm{\tau}),\mathbf{U}^{\prime})\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{X}))\mapsto(\det\mathbf{U}^{\prime})\mathbf{U}^{\prime}.

(22)

If there exists a real intertwiner
\mathbf{Q}_{\ast}\in O(3)
between
\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{s}}
, SSG
\mathcal{Y}
can be identified with a MSG

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{Y},\mathbf{Q}_{\ast})

\displaystyle=\left\{(\mathbf{R},\bm{\tau})\theta_{\det\mathbf{U}}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}((\mathbf{R},\bm{\tau}),\mathbf{U})\in\mathcal{Y}\right\}.

(23)

Such a real intertwiner can be numerically constructed by the algorithm described in Appendix
C
, if it exists.
Thus, by enumerating subgroups of
\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{X}))
and finding a real intertwiner between
\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{s}}
for each subgroup, we can enumerate inequivalent MSGs induced from
\mathcal{X}
.
We further filter the enumerated MSGs to keep only those with maximal symmetry, which correspond to a finite number of oriented SSGs.

III.2
Collinear oriented spin space group

For a collinear SSG
\mathcal{X}
, if its family point group and a normalizer of its spin-only group intersect trivially as
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})\cap\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}})=1
, no nontrivial rotation symmetry operation survives in the induced MSG for any
\mathbf{Q}\in O(3)
, which implies triclinic MSG.
Because there is no restriction on spin axes in triclinic MSGs, we enumerate candidate spin axes for oriented spin-only groups from
\mathbf{R}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})\setminus\{\mathbf{I},\mathord{-}\mathbf{I}\}
in what follows.
We denote a transformation to align with a candidate spin axis as
\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}\in SO(3)
and a transformed SSG of
\mathcal{X}
by
\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}
as
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
.
For notational simplicity, we assume the spin axis of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime})
is along the
z
-axis in what follows.

For
((\mathbf{R},\bm{\tau}),\mathbf{U}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
, we decompose spatial and spin rotation into two-dimensional rotations around the
z
-axis and a sign flip along the
z
-axis, if possible:

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{xy}\oplus p_{z}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{R}\quad(\mathbf{R}_{xy}\in O_{xy}(2),p_{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2})

(24)

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}^{\prime}_{xy}\oplus u^{\prime}_{z}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U}^{\prime}\quad(\mathbf{U}^{\prime}_{xy}\in O_{xy}(2),u^{\prime}_{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}),

(25)

where
O_{xy}(2)
is a two-dimensional orthogonal group acting on the
xy
-plane.
Because
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}=O_{xy}(2)\oplus 1
and
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}})/\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}=1\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, a transformation
\mathbf{Q}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}})
acts on
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}})/\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}
as identity.
Thus, every
\mathbf{Q}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}})
gives the same MSG
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime},\mathbf{Q})
and we do not need to enumerate
\mathbf{Q}
further.
We directly filter the enumerated MSGs
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime},\mathbf{Q}^{\prime})
for each candidate spin axis to keep only those with maximal symmetry, which correspond to a finite number of oriented SSGs.

Coplanar oriented spin space group

For a coplanar SSG
\mathcal{X}

as in the collinear case.
We denote a transformation to align with a candidate spin axis as
\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}\in SO(3)
and a transformed SSG of
\mathcal{X}
by
\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}
as
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}

For notational simplicity, we assume the spin axis of

III.4
Chirality of spin space group

So far, the transformation
\mathbf{Q}
between SSGs has been considered in
O(3)
.
However, we should care about the parity of
\mathbf{Q}
when discussing chirality of coplanar magnetic structures.
Motivated by the definition of spatial chirality
31
, we say that an object is spin-chiral if it cannot be superimposed on its time-reversed counterpart by a proper spin rotation.
Conversely, if such a mapping exists, we call the object spin-achiral.
For SSGs, this definition is rephrased as follows: a SSG
\mathcal{X}
is spin-chiral if
\mathcal{X}
and
(1,\overline{1})^{-1}\mathcal{X}(1,\overline{1})
are not equivalent by any spin transformation
\mathbf{Q}\in SO(3)
.
However, as
(1,\overline{1})^{-1}(g,\mathbf{U})(1,\overline{1})=(g,\mathbf{U})
for all
(g,\mathbf{U})\in\mathcal{X}
, they are always equivalent by any
\mathbf{Q}\in O(3)
.
Thus, a SSG is always spin-achiral.

That being said, there are still discussions on chirality for coplanar magnetic structures with SOC
100
;
26
;
87
.
Vector spin chirality is commonly used to capture such chirality as an ad hoc indicator
115
;
56
.
Thus, we further consider chirality of SSGs by restricting spin transformations
\mathbf{Q}
to
SO(d)\oplus\mathbf{I}_{3-d}
with spin-structure dimension
d=1,2
.
We refer to such chirality for collinear (
d=1
) and coplanar (
d=2
) objects as spin-axichirality and spin-planochirality, respectively.
The terms “axichirality” and “planochirality” are borrowed from the corresponding concept in spatial chirality
2
;
9
.
No collinear SSG is spin-axichiral because
O(1)
is abelian.
Thus, we focus on spin-planochirality and provide a procedure to judge spin-planochirality of coplanar SSGs in Appendix
D
.
For a spin-planochiral coplanar SSG
\mathcal{X}
, we generate oriented SSGs from both
\mathcal{X}
and its “enantiomorph” SSG up to orientation-preserving spin transformation in
SO(2)\oplus 1
.

V
Application to MAGNDATA database

We apply the generation scheme to the MAGNDATA database, which contains experimentally reported magnetic structures.
We first outline a classification scheme and present the resulting analysis.
We then discuss materials that are not readily addressed within our framework.

V.1
Classification scheme

The
MAGNDATA
database comprises 2,186 magnetic structures in total.
Structures satisfying any of the following conditions fall outside the scope of the present study and are excluded from the analysis:
(1) disordered structures containing sites with partial occupancy
(2) non-AFM structures for which the ratio of the total magnetization to the absolute magnetization exceeds 1%
(3) structures in which magnetic atoms occupy multiple inequivalent Wyckoff positions of the parent space group.
We note that structures classified under condition (3) contain magnetic atoms that are not symmetry-related, and thus, these systems are beyond the applicability of any symmetry-based approach and are not considered further.

After applying these screening criteria, 1,023 structures remain, which are classified into the following five classes.
Throughout this study, symmetry identification is performed using
moyopy
and
spinspg
packages
99
;
98
with
symprec

=1\times 10^{-4}
and
mag_symprec

=1\times 10^{-2}
.

•

lower_family_translation_group
: structures in which the translation group of the family space group is smaller than that of the parent space group.

•

lower_family_point_group
: structures in which the point group of the family space group is smaller than that of the parent space group.

•

multiple_basis
: cases in which the totally symmetric representation of the SSG admits multiple linearly independent basis functions.

•

ssg_matched
: structures outside the above three classes, which are reproducible as SSA structures up to
O(3)
transformations.

•

oriented_ssg_matched
: structures that belong to the
ssg_matched
class and are reproducible as oriented SSA structures.

Note that mcif files in
MAGNDATA
contain either the crystal structure corresponding to the magnetically ordered phase or that of the disordered phase.
In the former case, symmetry-identification procedures may fail to correctly determine the parent space group and the primitive cell owing to distortions induced by magnetic ordering, which in some cases affect the above symmetry classification.
Although such cases could, in principle, be incorporated into the analysis by providing structural information for the disordered phase, we do not apply such a procedure in the present study for simplicity.

The benchmark analysis using spin density functional theory (SDFT) calculations in Sec.
VI
is carried out for materials belonging to
ssg_matched
and

Magnetic structures classified as
lower_family_translation_group

Here, we present representative examples of magnetic structures that are not classified as

V.3.1
DyB

Figure

in

The parent point group of DyB

4/mmm

Consistent with this symmetry, the structures shown in Figs.

((4^{+}_{001},\bm{0}),1),((4^{+}_{001},\bm{0}),\bar{1})

In contrast, the experimental structure shown in Fig.

However, the point group

4/mmm

Accordingly, this structure can be reproduced while keeping the fixed moment-size condition.
Figures

For cases with

Following the treatment described in Sec.

, we then search for maximal t-subgroups and attempt magnetic structure generation.
We find that 27 out of 166 cases are successfully reproduced using this procedure.

V.3.2
CoSO
4

Figure

It is readily seen that the magnetic structure shown in Fig.

In such cases, the coefficients in the linear combination are continuous parameters, and consequently an identical magnetic structure cannot be generated by any symmetry-based construction.
Nevertheless, in situations where one magnetic configuration is dominant and the others are only weakly induced as in canted AFMs, the correct magnetic structure may still emerge during self-consistent calculations.
We therefore emphasize that not all

V.3.3
CsCoCl
3

Here, we consider the case of CsCoCl
3
(Fig.
6

6

MAGNDATA

Figure

N_{k}=3

Both structures shown in Figs.

However, while the SSA structure preserves the family space group
\mathcal{G}=P6_{3}/mmc
(No. 194), which is identical to the space group of the nonmagnetic structure, the experimental magnetic structure has a different family space group
\mathcal{G}=P6_{3}/mcm

The SSA structure is characterized by a helical spin texture associated with a spin translation vector

Therefore, the magnetic structures in the
lower_family_translation_group

We finally note that some cases assigned to this class originate from ambiguities in identifying the primitive cell, as discussed at the end of Sec.
V.1

VI
High-throughput calculations

In this section, we present benchmarks for the first-principles magnetic structure prediction method using the proposed scheme.
For materials classified as
ssg_matched
, we generate SSA and oriented SSA structures and then perform high-throughput SDFT calculations.
Based on the results, we discuss the computational efficiency and the predictive performance of the proposed scheme.

VI.1
Details of the computation

We used the
atomate2
package

35

Electronic structure calculations were carried out using the Vienna

In practice, self-consistent field (SCF) calculations without SOC were first performed for the SSA structures.

VII
Conclusion

We have presented a framework for the systematic generation of magnetic structures inspired by the concept of oriented SSGs.
A key advantage of the SSG-based formulation is that it can naturally enforce the fixed-moment condition on symmetry-equivalent sites, thereby enabling an exhaustive enumeration of magnetically inequivalent structures up to global
O(3)
transformations.
Building on the oriented SSG concept, we further introduced a practical procedure for constructing magnetic structures that are inequivalent under the parent space group and are thus suitable for resolving SOC-induced energy splittings.

Applying the method to the
MAGNDATA
database, we classified 1,023 reported magnetic structures into five categories and showed typical examples for which our scheme has limited applicability.
For a computationally tractable set of 283 materials, we performed SDFT calculations using a two-step workflow
64
: SCF calculations without SOC for the SSA structures, followed by fixed-charge non-SCF calculations with SOC for the descendant oriented SSA structures obtained through global
SO(3)
rotations of the parent SSA structures.
This workflow provides high numerical stability and favorable computational efficiency, as summarized in Sec.
VI.2
.

The benchmark results quantify the practical reliability of first-principles magnetic-structure prediction in a broad dataset.
At the SSG level, the experimentally reported structures are identified as energetically stable in
82\%
of the examined materials.
At the oriented SSG level, the corresponding success rate is
76\%
.
Importantly, we found a clear separation of energy scales: typical energy differences among distinct SSA structures are around
100
meV per magnetic atom, whereas those among oriented SSA structures are around
0.3
meV per magnetic atom, indicating an SOC-induced hierarchy by roughly a factor of 300.
Consistent with this hierarchy, only a very small fraction of materials exhibit a reordering of the lowest-energy SSA structure upon inclusion of SOC.

These findings establish oriented SSG-based enumeration, combined with first-principles calculations within a two-step strategy, as a robust and efficient framework for large-scale magnetic-structure prediction.
The proposed approach is applicable to systematic searches for AFMs with targeted symmetry properties and provides a quantitative baseline for assessing the accuracy limits of SDFT-based magnetic-structure predictions.
Future extensions to magnetic structures classified in
lower_family_translation_group
and
multiple_basis
classes, as well as the inclusion of correlation effects (e.g.,
+U
) in SDFT, will further broaden the applicability and strengthen symmetry-guided high-throughput approaches.

Appendix A
Detailed algorithm for enumerating spin space groups

We continue the discussion in Sec.
II.2
to enumerate SSGs from a given family space group
\mathcal{G}
, a spin-only group
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}
, and a k-index
N_{k}
.
Although the following algorithm is essentially the same as that in Refs.
121
;
24
;
53
, we here provide a self-contained and more implementation-friendly description usable for on-the-fly SSG enumeration.
This simplification partly arises from restricting equivalence to conjugacy under
\mathcal{G}\times O(3)
, rather than group isomorphism under
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{G})\times O(3)
.

The overall procedure to enumerate SSGs is as follows.
We introduce notation for space group
\mathcal{G}
in Sec.
A.1
.
We enumerate normal t-subgroups
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathcal{G}
in Sec.
A.2
.
We enumerate normal k-subgroups
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{M}
with
\mathcal{H}\trianglelefteq\mathcal{G}
in Sec.
A.3
.
Finally, we enumerate injective homomorphisms
U:\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}\rightarrow O(3)/\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}
by reducing the problem to enumeration of faithful orthogonal representations of
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
in Sec.
A.4
.

A.1
Group structure of family space group

We prepare notation for the family space group
\mathcal{G}
.
We write the point group of
\mathcal{G}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G})=\left\{\mathbf{R}_{i}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}(\mathbf{R}_{i},\bm{\tau}_{i})\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{G})\in\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{G})\right\}.

(33)

We denote a lattice formed by translation subgroup
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{G})
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{G}}=\left\{\mathbf{t}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{t})\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{G})\right\}.

(34)

Let
\bm{\tau}_{\bullet}:\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a translation part of
\mathcal{G}
with
\bm{\tau}_{\mathbf{R}_{i}}=\bm{\tau}_{i}
.
For coset representatives
g_{i}
and
g_{j}
, we choose an index
ij\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
such that
\mathbf{R}_{ij}=\mathbf{R}_{i}\mathbf{R}_{j}\,(\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}))
.
Then, we define a factor system for a group extension of
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G})
by
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{G})
as

\displaystyle g_{i}g_{j}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{I},\bm{\sigma}(i,j))g_{ij}

(35)

with

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}(i,j)=\bm{\tau}_{i}+\mathbf{R}_{i}\bm{\tau}_{j}-\bm{\tau}_{ij}.

(36)

A.2
Normal
t
-subgroup of
\mathcal{G}

We enumerate normal subgroups
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G})
by brute-force search with memoization.
Since the number of subgroups of an order-
n
group is bounded by
\exp\left(O((\log n)^{2})\right)

88
and
|\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G})|\leq 48
for three-dimensional crystallographic point groups, this step is computationally tractable.

For each normal subgroup
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, we denote the coset decomposition of
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G})
by
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G})=\bigcup_{I=1}^{N_{t}}\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}}\mathcal{P}^{\prime},

(37)

where
N_{t}=|\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}):\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|
and
\{\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}}\}_{I=1}^{N_{t}}
are representatives of the cosets.
Then, we construct a t-subgroup
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathcal{G}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=\bigcup_{\mathbf{R}_{h}\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}g_{h}\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{G}).

(38)

While
\mathcal{M}
is a t-subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
by construction, we need to check whether
\mathcal{M}
is normal in
\mathcal{G}
.
That is, for all pairs of
g_{i_{I}}\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{M}
and
g_{h}\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{G})\in\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{G})
, we require
g_{i_{I}}^{-1}g_{h}g_{i_{I}}\in\mathcal{M}
.
This condition is equivalent to requiring that

\displaystyle\bm{\tau}_{h}+\mathbf{R}_{h}\bm{\tau}_{i_{I}}-\bm{\tau}_{i_{I}}=\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}}\bm{\tau}_{\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}}^{-1}\mathbf{R}_{h}\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}}}\,\mod\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{G}},

(39)

where we use
\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}}^{-1}\mathbf{R}_{h}\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}}\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
because
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is normal in
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G})
.
In the following, we denote
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
satisfying Eq. (
39
) as
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}
.

Note that, for coset representatives
\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}}
and
\mathbf{R}_{i_{J}}
, their multiplication may not be a coset representative of
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G})/\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
.
We denote the index
h_{IJ}\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}i_{J}}=\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}}\mathbf{R}_{i_{J}}\mathbf{R}_{h_{IJ}}.

(40)

A.3
Normal space subgroup of
\mathcal{G}

We enumerate normal sublattices
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{G}}
under
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G})
with a given
k
-index
N_{k}=|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{G}}/\mathcal{L}^{\prime}|
.
Inequivalent sublattices of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{G}}
are enumerated by Hermite normal forms of
3\times 3
integer matrices with determinant
N_{k}

41
.
The normality condition is expressed as

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{i}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\quad(\forall\mathbf{R}_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G})),

(41)

and it can be checked by verifying that the basis vectors of
\mathbf{R}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
are related by a unimodular matrix.
We denote the translation subgroup formed by
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
as
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
, and coset representatives of
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{G})/\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{G})=\bigcup_{m=1}^{N_{k}}(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{t}_{m})\mathcal{T}^{\prime}.

(42)

Next, we consider a normal k-subgroup
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{M}
with translation subgroups
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
.
Although point groups of
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{M}
are the same, their translation parts may differ up to
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{G}}

108
.
Thus, we enumerate possible additional translation parts
\mathbf{t}_{m_{h}}\,(m_{h}\in\{1,\dots,N_{k}\})
for each coset representative such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\mathbf{R}_{h}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})}\tilde{g}_{h}\mathcal{T}^{\prime}

(43)

where
\tilde{g}_{h}=(\mathbf{R}_{h},\bm{\tau}_{h}+\mathbf{t}_{m_{h}})
.
We denote the translation part of
\tilde{g}_{h}
as

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{\tau}}_{h}=\bm{\tau}_{h}+\mathbf{t}_{m_{h}}.

(44)

For each pair of
\tilde{g}_{h_{1}}
and
\tilde{g}_{h_{2}}
, their multiplication

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{h_{1}}\tilde{g}_{h_{2}}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{I},\tilde{\bm{\tau}}_{h_{1}})g_{h_{1}}(\mathbf{I},\tilde{\bm{\tau}}_{h_{2}})g_{h_{2}}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{I},\tilde{\bm{\tau}}_{h_{1}}+\mathbf{R}_{h_{1}}\tilde{\bm{\tau}}_{h_{2}})g_{h_{1}}g_{h_{2}}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{I},\tilde{\bm{\tau}}_{h_{1}}+\mathbf{R}_{h_{1}}\tilde{\bm{\tau}}_{h_{2}}+\bm{\sigma}(h_{1},h_{2}))g_{h_{1}h_{2}}\quad(\because\mbox{Eq.~\eqref{eq:coset_multiplication_G}})

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{\tau}}_{(h_{1},h_{2})}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{\tau}}_{h_{1}}+\mathbf{R}_{h_{1}}\tilde{\bm{\tau}}_{h_{2}}+\bm{\sigma}(h_{1},h_{2})\,\mod\mathcal{L}^{\prime}.

(45)

Thus, we obtain a group
\mathcal{H}
by exhaustively assigning
\mathbf{t}_{m}
for each generator of
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
and checking the condition of Eq. (

\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
.

At this moment,
\mathcal{H}
may not be a group because associativity is not yet checked.
Before checking the associativity condition, we first check the normality conditions of
\mathcal{H}
in
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{G}

If
\mathcal{H}

\mathcal{M}

\mathbf{R}_{h}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})

.
The multiplication leads to

\displaystyle(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{t}_{m})^{-1}\tilde{g}_{h}(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{t}_{m})

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{R}_{h},\tilde{\bm{\tau}}_{h}+\mathbf{R}_{h}\mathbf{t}_{m}-\mathbf{t}_{m})

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{R}_{h}\mathbf{t}_{m}-\mathbf{t}_{m})\tilde{g}_{h}.

Thus, the normality condition of
\mathcal{H}
in
\mathcal{M}
is expressed as

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{h}\mathbf{t}_{m}=\mathbf{t}_{m}\,\mod\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{H}}.

(46)

Similarly, if
\mathcal{H}
is normal in
\mathcal{G}
, then for all pairs of
g_{i_{I}}\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{M}
and
\tilde{g}_{h}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{H}}\in\mathcal{H}/\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{H}}
, we require
(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{t}_{m})^{-1}g_{i_{I}}^{-1}\tilde{g}_{h}g_{i_{I}}(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{t}_{m})\in\mathcal{H}
.
The multiplication leads to

\displaystyle(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{t}_{m})^{-1}g_{i_{I}}^{-1}\tilde{g}_{h}g_{i_{I}}(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{t}_{m})

\displaystyle=g_{i_{I}}^{-1}(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}}\mathbf{t}_{m})^{-1}\tilde{g}_{h}(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}}\mathbf{t}_{m})g_{i_{I}}

\displaystyle=g_{i_{I}}^{-1}\tilde{g}_{h}g_{i_{I}}\,\mod\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{H}}\quad(\because\mbox{Eq.~\eqref{eq:normality_H_M}})

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}}^{-1}\mathbf{R}_{h}\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}},\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}}^{-1}(\tilde{\bm{\tau}}_{h}+\mathbf{R}_{h}\bm{\tau}_{i_{I}}-\bm{\tau}_{i_{I}}))\,\mod\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{H}}.

Thus, the normality condition of
\mathcal{H}
in
\mathcal{G}
is expressed as

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{\tau}}_{h}+\mathbf{R}_{h}\bm{\tau}_{i_{I}}-\bm{\tau}_{i_{I}}=\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}}\tilde{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}}^{-1}\mathbf{R}_{h}\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}}}\,\mod\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{H}},

(47)

where we define
\tilde{\bm{\tau}}_{\bullet}:\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
as the translation part of
\mathcal{H}
with
\tilde{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathbf{R}_{h}}=\tilde{\bm{\tau}}_{h}
.

We consider a group multiplication of cosets of
\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{H}
by

\displaystyle g_{i_{I}}g_{i_{J}}\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{I},\bm{\sigma}(i_{I},i_{J}))g_{i_{I}i_{J}}\mathcal{H}\quad(\because\mbox{Eq.~\eqref{eq:coset_multiplication_G}})

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{I},\bm{\sigma}(i_{I},i_{J}))g_{i_{I}i_{J}}g_{h_{IJ}}g_{h_{IJ}}^{-1}\mathcal{H}\quad(\because\mbox{Eq.~\eqref{eq:point_group_factor_system_G_M}})

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{I},\bm{\sigma}(i_{I},i_{J})+\bm{\sigma}(i_{I}i_{J},h_{IJ}))g_{i_{I}i_{J}}g_{h_{IJ}}^{-1}\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{I},\bm{\sigma}(i_{I},i_{J})+\bm{\sigma}(i_{I}i_{J},h_{IJ}))g_{i_{I}i_{J}}((\mathbf{I},-\mathbf{t}_{m_{h_{IJ}}})\tilde{g}_{h_{IJ}})^{-1}\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{I},\bm{\sigma}(i_{I},i_{J})+\bm{\sigma}(i_{I}i_{J},h_{IJ})+\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}i_{J}}\mathbf{t}_{m_{h_{IJ}}})g_{i_{I}i_{J}}\mathcal{H}\quad(\because\mbox{Eq.~\eqref{eq:normality_H_M}}).

Thus, the group multiplication of
\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{H}
is expressed as

\displaystyle g_{i_{I}}g_{i_{J}}\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{s}(i_{I},i_{J}))g_{i_{I}i_{J}}\mathcal{H}

(48)

with

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}(i_{I},i_{J})

\displaystyle=\bm{\sigma}(i_{I},i_{J})+\bm{\sigma}(i_{I}i_{J},h_{IJ})+\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}i_{J}}\mathbf{t}_{m_{h_{IJ}}}.

(49)

Similarly, the group multiplication of
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
is expressed as

\displaystyle g_{i_{I}}(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{t}_{m})g_{i_{J}}(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{t}_{n})\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=g_{i_{I}}g_{i_{J}}(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{R}_{i_{J}}^{-1}\mathbf{t}_{m}+\mathbf{t}_{n})\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{s}(I,J))g_{i_{I}i_{J}}(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{R}_{i_{J}}^{-1}\mathbf{t}_{m}+\mathbf{t}_{n})\mathcal{H}\quad(\because\mbox{Eq.~\eqref{eq:group_multiplication_M_H}})

\displaystyle=g_{i_{I}i_{J}}(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{R}_{i_{J}}^{-1}\mathbf{t}_{m}+\mathbf{t}_{n}+\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}i_{J}}^{-1}\mathbf{s}(I,J))\mathcal{H}.

Finally, we check the associativity condition of
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
.
For all triplets of coset representatives
g_{i_{I}}
,
g_{i_{J}}
, and
g_{i_{K}}
, we require
(g_{i_{I}}g_{i_{J}}\mathcal{H})g_{i_{K}}\mathcal{H}=g_{i_{I}}\mathcal{H}(g_{i_{J}}g_{i_{K}}\mathcal{H})
, which leads to the 2-cocycle condition for
\mathbf{s}
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}(i_{I}i_{J},i_{K})+\mathbf{s}(i_{I},i_{J})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{s}(i_{I},i_{J}i_{K})+\mathbf{R}_{i_{I}}\mathbf{s}(i_{J},i_{K})\,\mod\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{H}}.

(50)

Thus, we obtain a normal k-subgroup
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{M}
with
\mathcal{H}\trianglelefteq\mathcal{G}
by checking Eqs. (
45
), (
46
), (
47
), and (
A.3
).
In the following, we denote
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
as
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{H}}
for k-subgroup
\mathcal{H}
, respectively.

A.4
Injective homomorphism from quotient group to spin rotation quotient group

For each normal subgroup
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{G}
, we consider injective homomorphisms
U:\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}\rightarrow O(3)/\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}
.
Since the dimension of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})/\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}(\mathcal{X})
is equal to a spin-structure dimension
d
and every representation in
\mathbb{R}
can be transformed to an orthogonal representation,
U
can be regarded as a
d
-dimensional orthogonal representation of
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
.
Because two orthogonal representations are equivalent if and only if they are transformed by an orthogonal matrix, the equivalence classes of
U
as orthogonal representations are identical to those as spin space groups as adopted in Sec.
II.2
.
Consequently, it suffices to enumerate the inequivalent injective homomorphisms
U
by constructing inequivalent
d
-dimensional faithful orthogonal representations
\tilde{U}:\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}\rightarrow O(d)
.

Because orthogonal representations can be uniquely decomposed into irreducible representations in
\mathbb{R}
(also referred to as physically irreducible representations, PIRs
109
), all
\tilde{U}
can be constructed by combining PIRs of
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
.
We numerically enumerate all inequivalent PIRs using
spgrep

97
.

After constructing
\tilde{U}
, we embed its
d
-dimensional orthogonal matrices into
O(3)
by direct sum with
(3-d)
-dimensional identity matrices,

\displaystyle U(g_{i_{I}}(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{t}_{m})\mathcal{H})=\left(\tilde{U}(g_{i_{I}}(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{t}_{m})\mathcal{H})\oplus\mathbf{I}_{3-d}\right)\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}},

(51)

where
\mathbf{I}_{3-d}
is the
(3-d)
-dimensional identity matrix.

Appendix B
Symmetry-adapted magnetic moments from projection operator of spin space group

We provide details of the projection operator method to obtain symmetry-adapted magnetic moments
\mathbf{m}^{(\alpha)}
in Eq. (
16
) with spin space group
\mathcal{X}
in Eq. (
10
) and representation matrices
\bm{\Gamma}
in Eq. (
II.3
).
Because
\mathcal{X}
is infinite, we construct the projection operator by decomposing it into those of its subgroups, the spin-only group
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}
and the normal space subgroup
\mathcal{H}
.

We first construct the projection operator for
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{\mathrm{so}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{U}\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}}\mathbf{U}\,d\mu(\mathbf{U}),

(52)

where
d\mu
is the normalized Haar measure.
For simplicity, we choose the
z
-axis as the spin axis for collinear and coplanar spin-only groups.
Then, the projection operator for a collinear spin-only group is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{\mathrm{so}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{I}+\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathbf{U}^{z}_{\theta}d\theta

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},

where
\mathbf{U}^{z}_{\theta}
is a rotation matrix around the
z
-axis by an angle of
\theta
.
For a coplanar spin-only group, the projection operator is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{\mathrm{so}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{I}+\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

For a noncoplanar spin-only group, the projection operator is simply given by
\mathbf{P}^{\mathrm{so}}=\mathbf{I}
.

Next, we construct the projection operator for
\mathcal{H}
.
We denote representatives of inequivalent unit cells under
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})
as
\ell=\{\bm{\ell}_{1},\cdots,\bm{\ell}_{N_{k}}\}
.
Then, from the periodicity of magnetic moments under
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})
, it suffices to consider unit cells only in
\ell
.
Thus, we only consider coset representatives of
\mathcal{H}/\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})
in constructing the projection operator,

\displaystyle P^{\mathcal{H}}_{\bm{\ell}\kappa,\bm{\ell}^{\prime}\kappa^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})|}\sum_{\mathbf{R}_{h}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})}\Gamma^{\mathrm{site}}_{\bm{\ell}\kappa,\bm{\ell}^{\prime}\kappa^{\prime}}(g_{h}).

(53)

Finally, we combine the projection operators of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}
and
\mathcal{H}
to construct that of
\mathcal{X}
,

\displaystyle P^{\mathcal{X}}_{\bm{\ell}\kappa\mu,\bm{\ell}^{\prime}\kappa^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{t}N_{k}}\sum_{I=1}^{N_{t}}\sum_{m=1}^{N_{k}}\sum_{\kappa^{\prime\prime}=1}^{m}\sum_{\mu^{\prime\prime}=1}^{3}\Gamma_{\bm{\ell}\kappa\mu,\bm{\ell}^{\prime\prime}\kappa^{\prime\prime}\mu^{\prime\prime}}((g_{i_{I}}(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{t}_{m}),\mathbf{U}_{Im}))P^{\mathcal{H}}_{\bm{\ell}^{\prime\prime}\kappa^{\prime\prime},\bm{\ell}^{\prime}\kappa^{\prime}}P^{\mathrm{so}}_{\mu^{\prime\prime}\mu^{\prime}}.

(54)

Eigenvectors of
\mathbf{P}^{\mathcal{X}}
with eigenvalue 1 give symmetry-adapted magnetic moments
\mathbf{m}^{(\alpha)}
in Eq. (
16
).

Appendix C
Orthogonal intertwiner between two orthogonal representations

Let
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
be orthogonal
k
-dimensional representations of a finite group
G
.
An intertwiner of
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
is a non-zero real matrix
\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
satisfying

\displaystyle\bm{\Gamma}_{1}(g)\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}=\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}\bm{\Gamma}_{2}(g)\quad(\forall g\in G).

(55)

The intertwiner exists if and only if
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
are equivalent.

For any real matrix
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
, the following matrix is an intertwiner for these representations,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{g\in G}\bm{\Gamma}_{1}(g)\mathbf{B}\bm{\Gamma}_{2}(g)^{-1},

(56)

if
\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}
is nonzero.
Thus, we can construct an intertwiner by generating a random matrix
\mathbf{B}
and calculating
\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}
.

If an intertwiner
\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}
exists and is well-conditioned, it can be converted to an orthogonal matrix by

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}(\mathbf{Q}^{\prime\top}\mathbf{Q}^{\prime})^{-1/2},

(57)

where
(\mathbf{Q}^{\prime\top}\mathbf{Q}^{\prime})^{-1/2}
is the inverse square root of the positive definite matrix
\mathbf{Q}^{\prime\top}\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}
.

Appendix D
Spin-planochiral coplanar spin space group

For simplicity, we consider coplanar SSGs
\mathcal{X}
with
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}}(\mathcal{X})=O_{xy}(2)\oplus 1
without loss of generality.
We say that
\mathcal{X}
is spin-planochiral if
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{e}}=(1,\bm{\sigma}_{x})^{-1}\mathcal{X}(1,\bm{\sigma}_{x})
are not equivalent by any spin transformation
\mathbf{Q}\in SO(2)\oplus 1
, where
\bm{\sigma}_{x}
is a mirror operation perpendicular to the
x
-axis and is chosen as one of improper spin rotations out of the coplanar plane.
We denote the triplets of
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{e}}
, defined in Sec.
II.2
as
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}},U)
and
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{so}},U_{\mathrm{e}})
, respectively.
We write the corresponding two-dimensional faithful orthogonal representations for
U
and
U_{\mathrm{e}}
, defined in Appendix
A.4
, as
\tilde{U}
and
\tilde{U}_{\mathrm{e}}
, respectively.
Then,
\mathcal{X}
is spin-planochiral if there is no orientation-preserving intertwiner
\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}\in SO(2)
between
\tilde{U}
and
\tilde{U}_{\mathrm{e}}
.

This condition requires both (1)
\tilde{U}
and
\tilde{U}_{\mathrm{e}}
are
O(2)
-equivalent and (2) a centralizer of
\tilde{U}
has an improper rotation.
The first condition can be checked by the procedure in Appendix
C
.
The second condition is satisfied if and only if
\tilde{U}
is a two-dimensional PIR as follows.
Otherwise,
\tilde{U}
is constructed from two one-dimensional PIRs,
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
, as
\tilde{U}=u_{1}\oplus u_{2}
after some transformation.
We can construct improper
\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}=\mathrm{diag}(\mathop{-}1,1)
as a centralizer of
\tilde{U}
by flipping the axis of
u_{1}
.
Thus,
\tilde{U}
must be a two-dimensional PIR if
\mathcal{X}
is spin-planochiral.

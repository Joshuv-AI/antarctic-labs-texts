Variable Modified Newtonian Mechanics VII: Supermassive Black Hole at very High redshift
C. C. Wong

Abstract
Observations of Supermassive Black Hole (SMBH) at very high redshift hosted by massive galaxy provide a challenge to the SMBH and massive galaxy formation models which are predicated on the Λ ​ C ​ D ​ M \Lambda CDM model that predicts a Hubble constant currently in tension with late time observations. In a gravitational system with two asymptotic solutions such as a central mass in an expanding cosmological background or an expanding background with radiation and baryons, we find that a particle’s free falling speed resulting from adding the free falling speeds due to the asymptotic solutions is also a theoretically viable solution wong - wong2 . In this work, we use this new model to examine the SMBH formation timeline and find that the change in the redshift-cosmological time relation provides sufficient cosmological time for the formation of SMBHs at high redshifts. We also find that the Hubble parameter-redshift relation in this model is consistent with late time observations at z ≤ 1 z\leq 1 .

5: Summary and Conclusion

The VMOND paradigm stipulates that within GR a particle’s free fall speed is the sum of the free fall speeds due to the dominant asymptotic sources. This introduces an additional effective energy density which, for an early time baryon overdensity, speeds up the overdensity’s evolution to its turnaround and virialisation redshifts. In the case of an expanding cosmological bacground, a Hubble parameter resulting from adding the individual asymptotic Hubble parameter due to radiation, baryon and cosmological constant increases the cosmological time lapse between
z=25
to
z=6

\Lambda CDM

Two-sided bounds on the point-wise spatial decay of ground states in the renormalized Nelson model with confining potentials
Fumio Hiroshima and Oliver Matte

Abstract
. We study the renormalized Nelson model for a scalar matter particle in a continuous confining potential interacting with a possibly massless quantized radiation field. When the radiation field is massless we impose a mild infrared regularization ensuring that the Nelson Hamiltonian has a non-degenerate ground state in all considered cases. Employing Feynman–Kac representations, we derive lower bounds on the point-wise spatial decay of the partial Fock space norms of ground state eigenvectors. Here the exponential rate function governing the decay is given by the Agmon distance familiar from the analysis of Schrödinger operators. For a large class of confining potentials, our lower bounds on the decay of ground state eigenvectors match asymptotically with the upper bounds implied by previous work of the present authors.



1.
Introduction and main result

1.1.
General introduction

In this article we discuss upper and lower bounds on the point-wise spatial decay of
ground state eigenvectors of the renormalized Nelson Hamiltonian in quantum field theory.
This Hamiltonian was constructed by E. Nelson
[
28
]
in 1964.
It generates the dynamics of a non-relativistic scalar quantum particle interacting with a quantized radiation field comprised
of scalar bosons. The crucial feature of Nelson’s model is that, by an energy renormalization,
one can remove an artificial ultraviolet cutoff imposed in the matter-radiation interaction term for a start to render
heuristic physical expressions mathematically well-defined. Due to its more involved construction—see
also
[
13
,
15
,
24
,
27
,
30
,
31
]

for improvements and approaches alternative to
[
28
]
—the spectral and probabilistic analysis of the renormalized Nelson model becomes more
challenging compared to similar models with ultraviolet regularized matter-radiation interactions.

In a previous article
[
21
]
, the present authors proved the existence of ground state eigenvectors of
renormalized Nelson Hamiltonians with Kato-decomposable potentials assuming a binding condition as well as a mild
infrared regularity condition in case the boson mass is zero. For massless boson fields the latter infrared condition was also shown to be necessary.
Furthermore, upper bounds on the point-wise spatial decay of the partial Fock space norms of ground state eigenvectors were
obtained in
[
21
]
, for potentials leading to a finite localization threshold as well as for a class of confining potentials
increasing at least polynomially at infinity. The ground state eigenvectors found in
[
21
]
have representatives depending continuously on
the position variable of the matter particle, and it is the point-wise decay of these representatives that we address here and henceforth.

In the present article we complement these results by deriving lower bounds on the point-wise decay of ground state eigenvectors for continuous confining potentials.
As many of the results in
[
21
]
, the proofs of our lower bounds are based on Feynman–Kac representations of the semigroup
generated by the renormalized Nelson Hamiltonian
[
15
,
27
]
. We shall proceed along the lines developed by
R. Carmona and B. Simon in the theory of Schrödinger operators
[
9
]
, adding new arguments to deal with the radiation field.
In particular, we shall estimate the decay in terms of the Agmon distance employed in
[
9
]
, i.e., the geodesic distance to the origin
arising when
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is equipped with the Riemannian metric tensor
g_{x}(v,w):=2V(x)v\cdot w
at every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
; here
v,w\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
are tangent vectors
and
V
is the (positive) potential.
g_{x}
is a tunneling region analog of the Jacobi metric in classical mechanics. In fact, in the lower bounds
valid for the most general potentials considered here,
V(x)
has to be replaced by
V_{\circ}(x):=\sup_{|y|\leq 1}V(x+y)
in the definition of
g_{x}
, but in many examples
one can return to the original
V
.
The Agmon distance is encountered as an exponential rate function by minimizing the classical action functional associated with
-V_{\circ}
,
which in turn naturally appears through the Girsanov theorem when the integration over paths in the Feynman–Kac formula for ground states
is restricted to an integration over a neighborhood of an action minimizing geodesic
[
9
, Section 2]
.
As mentioned in
[
9
]
, Varadhan
[
33
, (2.8)]

seems to be the first to use similar geodesic ideas in estimating transition probabilities of diffusion processes.

There is a wealth of literature on decay estimates on bound states of Schrödinger type operators; here we only refer to
the books
[
1
,
22
]
and mention that probabilistic approaches alternative to
[
9
]
can be found in
[
2
,
7
,
8
]
.
Notably, Agmon’s distance has generalizations to
N
-body Schrödinger operators
[
1
,
9
]
.

Prior to
[
21
]
,
point-wise upper bounds on the fall-off of ground state eigenvectors and more general elements of spectral subspaces
in ultraviolet regularized models of non-relativistic quantum field theory appeared in the articles
[
5
,
17
,
19
,
26
]
,
which all rely on probabilistic techniques. Hence, the main novelty of
[
21
]
was that no
ultraviolet regularizations were necessary any longer. The recent preprint
[
14
]

contains short proofs of
L^{2}
to
L^{\infty}
bounds for ultraviolet regularized models based on subsolution estimates,
which permit to turn
L^{2}
-exponential localization estimates for ground states
[
4
,
12
,
29
]
into point-wise bounds.
The point-wise bounds in
[
26
]
actually apply to any finite number of matter particles.

Also lower point-wise bounds on the partial Fock space norms of ground state eigenvectors in
models involving quantum fields appeared earlier: for the ultraviolet regularized Nelson model such a bound can be found in
[
20
, Theorem 2.93]
,
and for the semi-relativistic Pauli–Fierz model in
[
18
]
. Hence, again, the first novel aspect of the results obtained here
is the absence of ultraviolet regularity conditions. In this regard, a technical key input from
[
27
]
are exponential moment estimates
on processes appearing in the Feynman–Kac formula, whose right hand sides grow at most log-linearly in time.
Besides, we find the first lower bounds on eigenvectors in models involving quantized fields that are expressed in terms of the
aforementioned Agmon distance, and for a large class of continuous, confining potentials we demonstrate that point-wise upper bounds
involving the same Agmon distance can be read off from
[
21
]
.

In particular, the latter two-sided bounds match asymptotically and reveal the same leading order behavior
as found in
[
9
]
for Schrödinger operators; see Remark
1.4
. This is because, for confining potentials,
the eikonal equation determining the Agmon distance near infinity,
|\nabla\varrho|^{2}=2V
, is the same for Schrödinger operators and
Nelson Hamiltonians. For decaying potentials, the finite binding energy of a ground state replaces
V
in the eikonal equation, and
since binding energies are strictly increased by adding the field theoretic terms to the Schrödinger operator
[
23
]
, this results in an enhanced
decay of ground state eigenvectors as reflected by upper bounds in the
L^{2}
-sense in
[
12
,
21
,
29
]

and point-wise upper bounds in
[
14
,
21
,
26
]
.
Deriving asymptotically matching upper and lower bounds for decaying potentials in non-relativistic quantum field theory seems to be a more difficult problem.

We remark that other types of ground state localizations can be studied at every point in space, like exponential localization at any rate
in the boson number or Gaussian localization with respect to bosonic field variables
[
21
]
; see the brief
summaries in Remarks
1.7
and
1.8
.

In the following two subsections we introduce the Nelson model and present our main results.
In preparation for the proof of our lower bounds, we explain the Feynman–Kac formula for the renormalized Nelson model as well as
some of its implications relevant to us in Section
2
. In that section we also extract upper point-wise bounds involving
Agmon distances from
[

]
, and we give a proof of the continuity of functions in the range
of semigroup elements, which is a good deal shorter than the proof of a stronger equicontinuity statement in
[
26
]
.
Section

Some well-known results on the Agmon distance of particular importance to us are explained in Appendix
A
.

1.2.
The Nelson Hamiltonian

With
\mathcal{F}
denoting the bosonic Fock space over
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
(see (
2.1
)), the total Hilbert space for the matter-radiation system treated here is

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathcal{F}).

While we eventually will restrict our attention to continuous confining exterior potentials

V:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
, we shall for a start and in some technical results merely assume
V
to be Kato-decomposable.
That is,
V
is measurable,
V_{-}:=\max\{-V,0\}
satisfies

(1.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}{\mathbbm{1}}_{[0,r)}(|x-y|)\frac{V_{-}(y)}{|x-y|}{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=0,

and the positive part
V_{+}:=V+V_{-}
satisfies this condition locally, i.e., (
1.1
) holds with

\chi_{K}V_{+}
put in place of
V_{-}
for any compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Under these conditions we can define
a Schrödinger operator
H_{\rm p}
(particle Hamiltonian) acting in
\mathcal{H}
as the semi-bounded form sum

\displaystyle H_{\rm p}:=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\dot{+}V.

Here both
\Delta
and
V
act on
\mathcal{F}
-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
in the canonical way.

Turning to the quantum field, the bosons, here described in momentum space, have the relativistic dispersion relation

\displaystyle\omega(k)

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{|k|^{2}+\nu^{2}},\quad k\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\quad\text{for some $\nu\geq 0$.}

In what follows
H_{\rm f}:={\rm d}\Gamma(\omega)
denotes the differential second quantization of the multiplication operator induced by
\omega
(see (
2.3
)).
The heuristic interaction kernel for the matter-radiation interaction is given by choosing
\Lambda=\infty
and
\eta=1
in

(1.2)

\displaystyle v_{\Lambda,x}(k)

\displaystyle:=g\frac{e_{x}(k)\eta(k)}{\sqrt{\omega(k)}}{\mathbbm{1}}_{[0,\Lambda)}(|k|),\quad e_{x}(k):=e^{-ik\cdot x},\qquad k,x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\,\Lambda\in(0,\infty].

Here the value of the coupling constant
g\in\mathbb{R}
can be chosen arbitrarily. The measurable function
\eta:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
is assumed to satisfy

\displaystyle 0\leq\eta(k)\leq 1,\quad\eta(k)=\eta(-k),\quad k\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

It implements an infrared regularization if necessary, see (
1.5
) below, and ensures that ultraviolet regularized versions of Nelson’s model
are covered by our results as well.
Whenever
v_{\Lambda,x}
is square-integrable, the corresponding field operator
\varphi(v_{\Lambda,x})
acting in
\mathcal{F}
is well-defined (see (
2.5
)).
Hence, we can define ultraviolet regularized Nelson Hamiltonians
H_{\Lambda}
with
\Lambda\in(0,\infty)
setting

\displaystyle(H_{\Lambda}\Psi)(x)

\displaystyle:=(H_{\rm p}\Psi)(x)+H_{\rm f}\Psi(x)+\varphi(v_{\Lambda,x})\Psi(x)+E_{\Lambda}\Psi(x),\quad\text{a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$,}

for all
\Psi\in\mathcal{D}(H_{\Lambda})=\mathcal{D}(H_{\rm p})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathcal{D}(H_{\rm f}))
.
Here and henceforth,
\mathcal{D}(\cdot)
denotes domains of definition, and we added right away the renormalization energies

(1.3)

\displaystyle E_{\Lambda}:=g^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\eta(k)^{2}{\mathbbm{1}}_{[0,\Lambda)}(|k|)}{\omega(k)(\omega(k)+|k|^{2}/2)}{\rm d}k,\quad\Lambda\in(0,\infty).

Note that
E_{\Lambda}
diverges logarithmically as
\Lambda\to\infty
when
g\not=0
and
\eta(k)=1
for large
|k|
.
By the relative bound (
2.6
) and the Kato-Rellich theorem
H_{\Lambda}
is selfadjoint for every
\Lambda\in(0,\infty)
.
Nelson’s result
[
28
]
(and later improvements covering massless bosons and revealing stronger
convergence properties
[
3
,
13
,
15
,
21
,
24
,
27
,
30
,
31
]
)
ensures the existence of the renormalized Nelson Hamiltonian

(1.4)

\displaystyle H:=\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}H_{\Lambda}\quad\text{in the norm resolvent sense.}

H
is semi-bounded from below and we denote the minimum of its spectrum by

\displaystyle E_{\rm g}

\displaystyle:=\inf\sigma(H).

Our analysis departs from the next proposition, which is a special case of Theorem 1.4 in our previous article
[
21
]
;
the existence of ground states of Nelson Hamiltonians with ultraviolet cutoffs has been shown earlier in
[
10
,
29
,
32
]
.
Note that (
1.5
) is always fulfilled if
\nu>0
.

Proposition 1.1
.

Assume that
V(x)\to\infty
as
|x|\to\infty
.
Further, assume the following infrared regularity condition to hold:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}{\mathbbm{1}}_{(0,1)}(|k|)\frac{\eta(k)^{2}}{\omega(k)^{3}}{\rm d}k<\infty.

Then
E_{\rm g}
is a non-degenerate eigenvalue of
H
.

Any normalized eigenvector of
H
corresponding to
E_{\rm g}
will be called a ground state eigenvector and denoted by
\Phi_{\rm g}
in what follows.

In fact, the above proposition holds for all Kato-decomposable
V
satisfying a binding condition,
which trivially follows for
V
going to infinity as
|x|\to\infty
; see
[
21
, §3.3]
.
The infrared condition (
1.5
) actually is optimal, as one can prove absence of ground states for arbitrary Kato-decomposable

V
when (
1.5
) does not hold
[
21
, Theorem 1.5]
.

1.3.
Spatial decay of ground state eigenvectors

Let us now describe our main results. In the whole Section
1.3
we assume that

(1.6)

\displaystyle\text{$V$ is continuous, $V\geq 1$ on $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ and}\ \ V(x)\xrightarrow{\;\;|x|\to\infty\;\;}\infty.

First, we introduce the exponential rate functions governing the decay of ground state eigenvectors.
To this end we introduce spaces of Lipschitz continuous paths

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{T}(x,y):=\{\gamma\in{\rm Lip}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{3})\mid\gamma(0)=y,\gamma(T)=x\},\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\,T>0,

as well as the length functional, defined on the union of all these path spaces,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{V}(\gamma):=\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{2V(\gamma(s))}|\dot{\gamma}(s)|{\rm d}s,\quad\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{T}(x,y).

Since every
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{T}(x,y)
is a.e. differentiable with an essentially bounded derivative,
\mathscr{L}_{V}(\gamma)
is well-defined,
and the value
\mathscr{L}_{V}(\gamma)
is manifestly invariant under Lipschitz re-parametrizations of
\gamma
.
It is therefore natural to define the geodesic distances

(1.9)

\displaystyle d(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\inf_{\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(x,y)}\mathscr{L}_{V}(\gamma),\quad\varrho(x):=d(x,0),\qquad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

In studies of eigenfunctions of Schrödinger operators,
d
is commonly called Agmon distance
[
9
]
.
We stick to this nomenclature, as we will adapt arguments of
[
9
]
to Nelson’s model.

The Agmon distance to the origin
\varrho
appears in our decay estimates.
It is locally Lipschitz continuous, in particular differentiable a.e. on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
, and
it satisfies the eikonal equation

(1.10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\rho(x)|^{2}=V(x),\quad\text{a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}$;}

see Lemma
A.2
for an explanation as to why these facts follows from (e.g.)
[
25
, Theorem 5.2]
.

As mentioned in the general introduction, we already derived upper bounds on the
L^{2}
-localization and the point-wise decay of ground state eigenvectors
and more general elements of spectral subspaces of
H
in
[
21
]
. Combining these results with (
1.10
), we arrive at
the following proposition. The technical growth restriction (
1.11
) is convenient when dealing with the issue that
\varrho
might not be
globally Lipschitz continuous, which complicates the application of exponentially weighted
L^{2}
to
L^{\infty}
bounds on the semi-group
e^{-TH}
.

Proposition 1.2
.

Assume that
V
satisfies (
1.6
). Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\lambda\geq E_{\rm g}
. Then the range of the spectral projection

{\mathbbm{1}}_{(-\infty,\lambda]}(H)
is contained in the domain of the maximal multiplication operator
e^{(1-\varepsilon)\varrho}
.
Let
\Psi
be any normalized element in the range of
{\mathbbm{1}}_{(-\infty,\lambda]}(H)
. Then
\Psi
has a unique continuous representative
\Psi(\cdot)
.
Assume in addition that, for every
\delta>0
, there exists
D_{\delta}\in[1,\infty)
such that

(1.11)

\displaystyle V(x)

\displaystyle\leq D_{\delta}e^{\delta|x|},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Then there exists
C_{\varepsilon,\lambda}\in(0,\infty)
, also depending on
V
,
g\eta
and
\nu
, such that the
continuous representative of
\Psi
satisfies

(1.12)

\displaystyle\|\Psi(x)\|_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon,\lambda}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\varrho(x)},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Proof.

In Section
2.4
we explain how the claimed localization properties can be extracted from
[
21
]
.
The existence of continuous representatives is addressed in Section
2.3
.
∎

Our main result is a lower bound for ground state eigenvectors involving a modified Agmon distance
arising when the techniques of
[
9
]
are applied, namely

(1.13)

\displaystyle\varrho_{\circ}(x)

\displaystyle:=\inf_{\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(x,0)}\mathscr{L}_{V_{\circ}}(\gamma),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\quad\text{with}\quad V_{\circ}(x):=\sup_{|x-y|\leq 1}V(y),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Our lower bound matches asymptotically with (
1.12
) (see Remark
1.4
) and
\varrho
can be put in place of

\varrho_{\circ}
in the next theorem, whenever

(1.14)

\displaystyle\frac{\varrho_{\circ}(x)}{\varrho(x)}\xrightarrow{\;\;|x|\to\infty\;\;}1.

We denote by
\Omega
the vacuum vector in
\mathcal{F}
(see (

)), so that the first bound in (

) is just
the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for the scalar product on
\mathcal{F}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
V
satisfies (
1.6
) and (
1.5
) holds. Let
\varepsilon>0
.
Then there exists
c_{\varepsilon}\in(0,\infty)
, also depending on

,

and

, such that
the unique continuous representative
\Phi_{\rm g}(\cdot)
of the (normalized) ground state eigenvector

satisfies

(1.15)

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{\rm g}(x)\|_{\mathcal{F}}\geq|(\Omega,\Phi_{\rm g}(x))_{\mathcal{F}}|

\displaystyle\geq c_{\varepsilon}e^{-(1+\varepsilon)\varrho_{\circ}(x)},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Proof.

The proof of this theorem can be found at the end of Section

Some bosonic Fock space calculus

The bosonic Fock space over

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{-\int_{0}^{T}(V(B_{s}+x)-E_{\rm g}){\rm d}s}(\Omega,\Gamma(e_{x})J_{[0,T]}\Gamma(e_{-x})\Phi_{\rm g}(B_{T}+x))_{\mathcal{F}}\Big],

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
by Proposition
2.1
and the relation
\Phi_{\rm g}=e^{-TH+TE_{\rm g}}\Phi_{\rm g}
, where furthermore

(3.2)

\displaystyle(\Omega,\Gamma(e_{x})J_{[0,T]}\Gamma(e_{-x})\Phi_{\rm g}(y))_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle=e^{S_{T}-\frac{1}{2}\|\tilde{U}_{T}\|^{2}}(e^{-\varphi(e_{x}\tilde{U}_{T})}\Omega,\Phi_{\rm g}(y)),\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

on
\mathcal{X}
by (

), (
2.10
) and the spectral calculus for
\varphi(f)
.
The appearance of the field operator on the right hand side of (

) permits to apply Jensen’s inequality in
Q
-space:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
f\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}
. Then

(3.3)

\displaystyle(e^{-\varphi(f)}\Omega,\Phi_{\rm g}(y))_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\geq\ell(y)\exp\bigg(-\frac{\|f\|\|\Phi_{\rm g}\|_{\infty}}{\ell(y)}\bigg).

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{I}:\mathcal{F}\to L^{2}(Q,\mu)
be the isomorphism introduced in Section
2.5
, so that

\mathscr{I}\varphi(f)\mathscr{I}^{*}
becomes multiplication with the Gaussian random variable
\phi(f)
,
\mathscr{I}\Omega=1
and
\mathscr{I}\Phi_{\rm g}(y)>0
,
\mu
-almost surely.
By (
2.16
) and Jensen’s inequality we then find

\displaystyle\frac{(e^{-\phi(f)},\mathscr{I}\Phi_{\rm g}(y))_{L^{2}(Q,\mu)}}{\ell(y)}\geq\exp\bigg(-\frac{(\phi(f),\mathscr{I}\Phi_{\rm g}(y))_{L^{2}(Q,\mu)}}{\ell(y)}\bigg).

Here
(\phi(f),\mathscr{I}\Phi_{\rm g}(y))_{L^{2}(Q,\mu)}=(a^{\dagger}(f)\Omega,\Phi_{\rm g}(y))_{\mathcal{F}}\leq\|f\|\|\Phi_{\rm g}\|_{\infty}
holds by (

), (

)
and the relation
a(f)\Omega=0
.
∎

Recall the notation (
1.13
) and (
2.17
) before reading the next lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Denote by
\tau>0
the smallest eigenvalue of
-1/2
times the Dirichlet–Laplacian on the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Abbreviate

(3.4)

\displaystyle\alpha:=\chi(1)\exp\bigg(-\frac{\|\Phi_{\rm g}\|_{\infty}^{2}}{2\chi(1)^{2}}\bigg).

Then there exist universal constants
c,C\in(0,\infty)

such that, for all
T>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
q\in{\rm Lip}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
q(0)=x
and
q(T)=0
and for all
p\in(1,\infty)
,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\ell(x)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha}{C^{p-1}}\cdot e^{-\frac{p}{2}\int_{0}^{T}|\dot{q}(s)|^{2}{\rm d}s-\int_{0}^{T}V_{\circ}(q(s)){\rm d}s+T(E_{\rm g}-p\tau)-c(g^{2}+\frac{g^{4}}{p-1})(1\vee T)}.

Proof.

We fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
T>0
and pick
q\in{\rm Lip}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that
q(0)=x
and
q(T)=0
. Introducing the events

\displaystyle M_{T}:=\Big\{\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|B_{s}+x-q(s)|\leq 1\Big\},\quad Q_{T}:=\Big\{\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|B_{s}|\leq 1\Big\},

and combining (
3.1
) through (
3.3
), we find

\displaystyle\ell(x)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\bigg[{\mathbbm{1}}_{M_{T}}e^{-\int_{0}^{T}(V(B_{s}+x)-E_{\rm g}){\rm d}s}e^{S_{T}-\frac{1}{2}\|\tilde{U}_{T}\|^{2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{\|\tilde{U}_{T}\|\|\Phi_{\rm g}\|_{\infty}}{\ell(B_{T}+x)}\Big)\ell(B_{T}+x)\bigg].

Note that
|B_{T}+x|\leq 1
and, hence,
\ell(B_{T}+x)\geq\chi(1)
on
M_{T}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\ell(x)

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\cdot\mathbb{E}\bigg[{\mathbbm{1}}_{M_{T}}e^{-\int_{0}^{T}(V(B_{s}+x)-E_{\rm g}){\rm d}s}e^{S_{T}-\|\tilde{U}_{T}\|^{2}}\bigg].

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
p^{\prime}
be the exponent conjugate to
p
. Then Hölder’s inequality yields

(3.6)

\displaystyle\ell(x)

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\cdot\frac{\mathbb{E}\Big[{\mathbbm{1}}_{M_{T}}e^{-\int_{0}^{T}(V(B_{s}+x)-E_{\rm g}){\rm d}s/p}\Big]^{p}}{\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{(p^{\prime}/p)\|\tilde{U}_{T}\|^{2}-(p^{\prime}/p)S_{T}}\Big]^{p/p^{\prime}}}.

Since
p/p^{\prime}=p-1
, the denominator in (
3.6
) can be bounded from above by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{(p^{\prime}/p)\|\tilde{U}_{T}\|^{2}-(p^{\prime}/p)S_{T}}\Big]^{p/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{\|\tilde{U}_{T}\|^{2}/(p-1)-S_{T}/(p-1)}\Big]^{p-1}\leq C^{p-1}e^{c(g^{2}+cg^{4}/(p-1))(1\vee T)},

where the inequality follows from the exponential moment bounds of Proposition
2.1
(i).
Using that
V(B_{s}+x)\leq V_{\circ}(q(s))
on
M_{T}
, we estimate the numerator in (
3.6
) from below as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[{\mathbbm{1}}_{M_{T}}e^{-\int_{0}^{T}(V(B_{s}+x)-E_{\rm g}){\rm d}s/p}\Big]^{p}

\displaystyle\geq e^{-\int_{0}^{T}V_{\circ}(q(s)){\rm d}s+TE_{\rm g}}\mathbb{P}[M_{T}]^{p}

To conclude, we recall the bounds

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[M_{T}]

\displaystyle\geq e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}|\dot{q}(s)|^{2}{\rm d}s}\mathbb{P}[Q_{T}],\quad\mathbb{P}[Q_{T}]\geq e^{-\tau T},

from Lemma 2.2 and 2.3, respectively, in
[
9
]
.
∎

For completeness we recall from
[
9
]
how the first inequality in (
3.7
) is obtained:
Consider the martingale
m=(m_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
given by
m_{t}:=-\int_{0}^{t}\dot{q}(s){\rm d}B_{s}
,
t\in[0,T]
,
which has the deterministic quadratic variation
\langle m\rangle_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}|\dot{q}(s)|^{2}{\rm d}s
,
t\in[0,T]
.
As a consequence of Girsanov’s theorem or the Cameron–Martin theorem,
the law of the process
(B_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
under
\mathbb{P}
is the same as the law of

(B_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\dot{q}(s){\rm d}s)_{t\in[0,T]}=(B_{t}+q(t)-x)_{t\in[0,T]}

under the probability measure
\xi_{T}\odot\mathbb{P}
whose density
\xi_{T}:=\exp(m_{T}-\langle m\rangle_{T}/2)
is the stochastic
exponential of
m
at
T
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[M_{T}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\xi_{T}{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{T}}]\geq\mathbb{P}[Q_{T}]\exp\bigg(\frac{\mathbb{E}[(m_{T}-\langle m\rangle_{T}/2){\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{T}}]}{\mathbb{P}[Q_{T}]}\bigg)=e^{-\langle m\rangle_{T}/2}\mathbb{P}[Q_{T}],

where we applied Jensen’s inequality in the second step. In the last one we used
\mathbb{E}[m_{T}{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{T}}]=0
, which holds
since
B
has the same law under
\mathbb{P}
as
-B
.

3.2.
Optimal paths and geodesic distance

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
. Lemma
3.2
, where
p>1
is arbitrary, leads to the minimization of the classical action functional

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{V}(q):=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\dot{q}(t)|^{2}+V(q(t))\right){\rm d}t,

with
V_{\circ}
is put in place of
V
and the minimization running over all
T>0
and all paths

(3.9)

\displaystyle q\in\mathcal{C}^{\ast}_{T}(x):=\{q\in{\rm Lip}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{3})\mid q(0)=x,q(T)=0\}.

It is well-known that
\varrho(x)=\tilde{\varrho}(x)
with
\varrho
given by (
1.7
) through (
1.9
) and

(3.10)

\displaystyle\tilde{\varrho}(x)

\displaystyle:=\inf\{\mathscr{A}_{V}(q)|\,q\in\mathcal{C}^{\ast}_{T}(x),\,T>0\}.

Moreover, there exist
T(x)>0
and
q_{x}\in\mathcal{C}^{\ast}_{T(x)}(x)
minimizing the variational problem (
3.10
),
so that
\varrho(x)=\mathscr{A}_{V}(q_{x})
, and such that
T(x)
and
\varrho(x)
are related by the inequality in Lemma
A.4
;
see
[
9
, §2]
for proofs or Appendix
A
and in particular Lemma
A.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and abbreviate
c_{*}:=\max\{0,(1+\varepsilon/2)\tau+c(g^{2}+2g^{4}/\varepsilon)-E_{\rm g}\}
. Put
a_{\circ}(R):=\min_{|z|\geq R}V_{\circ}(z)
,
R>0
, and
choose
R_{0}>0
so large that
c_{*}/(2a_{\circ}(R_{0}))<\varepsilon/4
. After that choose
R_{1}>R_{0}
so large that

c_{*}\max_{|z|=R_{0}}\varrho_{\circ}(z)/(\sqrt{8}R_{1})<\varepsilon/4
. Pick
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
|x|\geq R_{1}
.
Also
V_{\circ}
satisfies (
1.6
), whence all the above remarks on the variational problems (
1.9
) and (
3.10
)
apply to
V_{\circ}
as well. Hence, we find
T_{\circ}(x)>0
and
q_{x}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{C}^{\ast}_{T_{\circ}(x)}(x)
such that
\varrho_{\circ}(x)=\mathscr{A}_{V_{\circ}}(q_{x}^{\circ})

and
T_{\circ}(x)
and
\varrho_{\circ}(x)
are related by the inequality in Lemma
A.4
applied to
V_{\circ}
.
By the above choices of
R_{0}
and
R_{1}
, the latter inequality implies
c_{*}T_{\circ}(x)<\varepsilon\varrho_{\circ}(x)/2
.
Plugging
p:=1+\varepsilon/2
and the minimizing pair

into (

), we thus find

\displaystyle\ell(x)\geq C_{*}\exp\big(-(1+\varepsilon/2)\varrho_{\circ}(x)-c_{*}T_{\circ}(x)\big)\geq C_{*}\exp(-(1+\varepsilon)\varrho_{\circ}(x)),

with
C_{*}:=\alpha e^{-c(g^{2}+2g^{4}/\varepsilon)}/C^{\varepsilon/2}
.
If

, then
\ell(x)\geq\chi(R_{1})\exp(-(1+\varepsilon)\varrho_{\circ}(x))

given by (

∎

Appendix A
Basic properties of the geodesic distance

In this appendix we collect well-known facts related to the Agmon distance which have been used in the main text,
and give references for all results whose proofs are not repeated here.

In what follows we merely assume that
V:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous with
V\geq 1
, and

X\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is convex and non-empty. Recalling (
1.7
) we introduce the path space

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(x,y;X):=\{\gamma\in\mathscr{C}_{1}(x,y)|\,\gamma([0,1])\subset\overline{X}\},\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

as well as an abbreviation for the Lagrangian,

\displaystyle F(x,v):=\sqrt{2V(x)}|v|,\quad x,v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

For all
x,y\in X
, we finally generalize (
1.9
) by

(A.1)

\displaystyle d_{X}(x,y):=\inf_{\gamma\in\mathcal{C}(x,y;X)}\mathscr{L}_{V}(\gamma)=\inf_{\gamma\in\mathcal{C}(x,y;X)}\int_{0}^{1}F(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s)){\rm d}s.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
x,y\in X
,
x\not=y
.
Then the parametric variational problem (
A.1
) has a (not necessarily unique)
minimizer
\gamma_{x,y;X}\in\mathcal{C}(x,y;X)
which can be chosen such that
F(\gamma_{x,y;X},\dot{\gamma}_{x,y;X})=d_{X}(x,y)>0
a.e. on
[0,1]
.

Proof.

Since
F
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{6}
,

F(x,v)\geq\sqrt{2}|v|
for all
x,v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
F(x,\cdot)
is convex and smooth on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
all assertions follow directly from, e.g.,
[
11
, Theorem 1 and Remark 2 in §8.4.2]
.
The proofs given there are based on invariance under re-parametrizations and lower semi-continuity arguments.
∎

Since two minimizers
\gamma_{x,z;X}
and
\gamma_{z,y;X}
can be combined and re-parametrized, we clearly have
the triangle inequality
d_{X}(x,y)\leq d_{X}(x,z)+d_{X}(z,y)
for all
x,y,z\in X
. For all
R>0
and
x,y,z\in X

satisfying
|x|,|y|\leq R
, this implies the local Lipschitz bound

(A.2)

\displaystyle|d_{X}(x,z)-d_{X}(y,z)|

\displaystyle\leq d_{X}(x,y)\leq\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{2V(sx+(1-s)y)}|x-y|{\rm d}s\leq L_{R}|x-y|,

with
L_{R}:=\max_{|z|\leq R}\sqrt{2V(z)}
. Another simple bound that will be useful in a moment is

(A.3)

\displaystyle d_{X}(x,y)\geq\sqrt{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\dot{\gamma}_{x,y;X}(s)|{\rm d}s\geq\sqrt{2}|\gamma_{x,y;X}(t)-y|,\quad t\in[0,1],

for all
x,y\in X
,
x\not=y
, where
V\geq 1
was used.

Our assumptions on
V
are translation invariant, whence we shall restrict our attention to the
distance to the origin
\varrho
given by (
1.9
) in what follows.

Lemma A.2
.

\varrho
is locally Lipschitz continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and satisfies (
1.10
).

Proof.

Let
B_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be the open ball of radius
r>0
about
0
. Fix
R\in\mathbb{N}
.
If
x\in B_{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
M:=\max_{|z|\leq R}\varrho(z)\geq\sqrt{2}R
, then (
A.3
) shows
that the image of any minimizer
\gamma_{x,0;\mathbb{R}^{3}}
is contained in
B_{M}
.
This implies
\varrho=\varrho_{M}:=d_{B_{M}}(\cdot,0)
on
B_{R}
. For the variational problems defining
\varrho_{M}
involve
smaller sets of paths than those defining
\varrho
, so that
\varrho\leq\varrho_{M}
on
B_{M}
.
It follows directly from
[
25
, Theorem 5.2]
that
\varrho_{M}
is a viscosity solution of the eikonal equation on
B_{M}\setminus\{0\}
.
Since
\varrho_{M}
is Lipschitz continuous by (
A.2
), this
shows
[
25
, Remark 1.11]
that
|\nabla\varrho_{M}|^{2}=2V
a.e. on
B_{M}\setminus\{0\}
.
Since
R\in\mathbb{N}
was arbitrary, this implies (
1.10
).
∎

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
. We shall next explain why Jacobi’s geometric least action principle works in our situation,
i.e., why minimizers of the variational problem for
\varrho(x)
yield upon re-parametrization minimizers
of the variational problem for
\tilde{\varrho}(x)
given by (
3.8
) through (
3.10
). We follow the discussion in
[
9
]

which exploits that the Lagrange function in (
3.8
) depends quadratically on the velocities
\dot{q}(t)
;
see, e.g.,
[
25
, §5.3]
for generalizations.

Given any
T>0
and
q\in\mathcal{C}^{\ast}_{T}(x)
, the re-parametrization
\gamma_{q}(s):=q(T(1-s))
,
s\in[0,1]
, defines a path
\gamma_{q}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(x,0)
.
Now, for
a,b\geq 0
, we have
\sqrt{2}ab\leq a^{2}+b^{2}/2
with equality if and only if
\sqrt{2}a=b
. This and the invariance of

\mathscr{L}_{V}
under re-parametrizations implies

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{V}(\gamma_{q})=\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{2V(q(t))}|\dot{q}(t)|{\rm d}t\leq\mathscr{A}_{V}(q),

with equality if and only if

(A.4)

\displaystyle\sqrt{2V(q(t))}=|\dot{q}(t)|,\quad\text{a.e. $t\in[0,T]$.}

Thus,
\varrho(x)\leq\tilde{\varrho}(x)
.

By virtue of Lemma
A.1
, we next pick
\gamma_{x}:=\gamma_{x,0;\mathbb{R}^{3}}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(x,0)
satisfying
F(\gamma_{x},\dot{\gamma}_{x})=\varrho(x)
, a.e.,
and in particular
\varrho(x)=\mathscr{L}_{V}(\gamma_{x})
. Setting

(A.5)

\displaystyle\tau(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\varrho(x)}{2V(\gamma_{x}(r))}{\rm d}r,\quad s\in[0,1],\quad T(x):=\tau(1),

we see that
\tau
is
C^{1}
with
\inf\tau^{\prime}>0
, whence it has an inverse function
\sigma:=\tau^{-1}:[0,T(x)]\to[0,1]

with the same properties. Defining
q_{x}(t):=\gamma_{x}(\sigma(T(x)-t))
,
t\in[0,T(x)]
, and using that
F(\gamma_{x},\dot{\gamma}_{x})=\varrho(x)
, a.e.,
we find that
q_{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{T(x)}^{*}(x)
satisfies (
A.4
). Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{V}(q_{x})

\displaystyle=\mathscr{L}_{V}(\gamma_{q_{x}})=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{2V(\gamma_{x}(\sigma(T(x)s)))}|\dot{\gamma}_{x}(\sigma(T(x)s))|\cdot\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}\sigma(T(x)s){\rm d}s=\mathscr{L}_{V}(\gamma_{x}).

We have re-proved the following result:

Lemma A.3
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
. Then
\varrho(x)=\tilde{\varrho}(x)
and the pair
T(x)>0
and
q_{x}\in\mathcal{C}^{\ast}_{T(x)}(x)

constructed in the preceding paragraph minimizes the variational problem (
3.10
).

The inequality stated in the next lemma can be read off from a proof in
[
9
]
that we essentially copy here.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
R_{1}>R_{0}>0
and set
a(R_{0}):=\inf_{|z|\geq R_{0}}V(z)
.
Pick
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
|x|\geq R_{1}
and let
T(x)
be given by (
A.5
)
for some
\gamma_{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(x,0)
satisfying
F(\gamma_{x},\dot{\gamma}_{x})=\varrho(x)
, a.e. Then

\displaystyle T(x)

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\frac{1}{2a(R_{0})}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{8}R_{1}}\cdot\max_{|z|=R_{0}}\varrho(z)\bigg)\varrho(x).

Proof.

We write
\mathscr{A}:=\mathscr{A}_{V}
for short. Set
t_{0}:=\min\{t>0|\,|q_{x}(t)|=R_{0}\}
and
y:=q_{x}(t_{0})
,
where
q_{x}
is the minimizer constructed by means of
\gamma_{x}
prior to Lemma
A.3
.
Then
\eta(t):=q_{x}(t+t_{0})
,
t\in[0,T(x)-t_{0}]
, defines a minimizer for
\inf\{\mathscr{A}(q)|\,q\in\mathcal{C}_{T}^{*}(y),\,T>0\}
.
For, if there were
T_{0}>0
and
\eta_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{T_{0}}^{*}(y)
such that
\mathscr{A}(\eta_{0})<\mathscr{A}(\eta)
, then
we could define
q_{*}\in\mathcal{C}_{T_{0}+t_{0}}^{*}(x)
setting
q_{*}(t):=q_{x}(t)
for
t\in[0,t_{0}]
and
q_{*}(t):=\eta_{0}(t-t_{0})
for
t\in[t_{0},T_{0}+t_{0}]

and obtained the contradiction
\mathscr{A}(q_{*})<\mathscr{A}(q_{x})
. Since
q_{x}
satisfies (
A.4
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\varrho(y)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{A}(\eta)=\int_{t_{0}}^{T(x)}2V(q_{x}(t)){\rm d}t\geq 2(T(x)-t_{0}),

\displaystyle\varrho(x)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{A}(q_{x})\geq\int_{0}^{t_{0}}2V(q_{x}(t)){\rm d}t\geq 2a(R_{0})t_{0}.

Combined this yields

\displaystyle\frac{T(x)}{\varrho(x)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t_{0}}{\varrho(x)}+\frac{1}{2\varrho(x)}\cdot\varrho(y)\leq\frac{1}{2a(R_{0})}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{8}|x|}\cdot\max_{|z|=R_{0}}\varrho(z),

where we also took (
A.3
) with
\gamma_{x,0;\mathbb{R}^{3}}=\gamma_{x}
and
\gamma_{x}(1)=x
into account.
∎

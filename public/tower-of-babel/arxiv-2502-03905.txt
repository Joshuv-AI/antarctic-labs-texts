Partially hyperbolic symplectomorphism with C 1 C^{1} bundles
Eramane Bodian, Khadim War

Abstract
We prove dynamical coherence for partial hyperbolic symplectomorphism in dimension 4 whose stable and unstable bundles are C 1 C^{1} .



1
Introduction

Let
M
be a closed Riemannian manifold of dimension four. A
C^{1}
diffeomorphism
f:M\to M
is said to be
partially hyperbolic
if there is a continuous invariant splitting

TM=\mathbb{E}^{s}\oplus\mathbb{E}^{c}\oplus\mathbb{E}^{u}

where the stable and unstable bundle
\mathbb{E}^{s}
and
\mathbb{E}^{u}
are hyperbolic while the central bundle
\mathbb{E}^{c}
has an intermediate behavior, see Section
2
for exact definition.

Partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms arise naturally from robust transitive or stably ergodic diffeomorphism
[
4
,
8
]
. It is known that
[
7
]
the stable and unstable bundles are tangent to invariant foliation while the central bundle is not necessary tangent to a foliation let alone being invariant. A partially hyperbolic diffeomorphism is said to be
dynamically coherent
if there exists an invariant foliation tangent to
\mathbb{E}^{c}
. Dynamical coherence is a key assumption in the study of ergodicity
[
16
]
, classification of partially hyperbolic diffeomorphism
[
6
]
and finding topological obstruction for a manifold to support such system
[
3
,
6
]
. In dimension
3
, it is rather understood what are the possibilities and in particular there is a well known counter example
[
14
]
. When all three bundles are
C^{1}
, A. Hammerlindl
[
5
]
prove that
f
is dynamically coherent under the condition of
2
-partially hyperbolic. In dimension three it is proved that volume domination and
C^{1}
regularity of all three bundles implies dynamically coherent
[
12
]
. In this paper, we study the problem of dynamical coherence under the assumption that
f
leaves invariant a symplectic form.

Let
\omega
be symplectic 2-form on
M
, i.e.
\omega
is closed and nondegenerate. A diffeomorphism
f:M\to M
is a partially hyperbolic symplectomorphism if it is partially hyperbolic and leaves invariant the symplectic form
(f^{*}\omega=\omega)
.
Partially hyperbolic symplectomorphisms are abundant in the space of symplectic diffeomorphism. V. Horita and A. Tahzibi
[
9
]
prove that robustly transitive and stably ergodic symplectic diffeomorphism are partially hyperbolic. In this paper, we prove the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let
f:(M,\omega)\to(M,\omega)
be a
C^{2}
partially hyperbolic symplectomorphism on a manifold of dimension four. If
\mathbb{E}^{s}
and
\mathbb{E}^{u}
are
C^{1}
then
f
is dynamically coherent. Moreover the center foliation is
C^{1}
.

Since the center foliation given by Theorem
1
is of class
C^{1}
then using
[
7
, Theorem 7.1, Theorem 7.2]
we deduce the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Let
f:(M,\omega)\to(M,\omega)
as in Theorem
1
. Then there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of f in the space of
C^{1}
partially hyperbolic diffeomorphism such that every
g\in\mathcal{U}
is dynamically coherent.

In Section
2
, we recall some basic properties of partially hyperbolic symplectomorphism and in Section
3
we prove Theorem
1
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the properties of partially hyperbolic symplectomorphism that are needed to prove Theorem
1
. We suppose that
(M,\omega)
is a four dimensional symplectic manifold.

f:M\to M
is said to be partially hyperbolic if there exists a continuous invariant splitting

T_{p}M=\mathbb{E}^{s}(p)\oplus\mathbb{E}^{c}(p)\oplus\mathbb{E}^{u}(p)\quad\text{ with}\quad Df_{p}\mathbb{E}^{\sigma}(p)=\mathbb{E}^{\sigma}(f(p)),\sigma=s,c,u\quad\forall p\in M

and there exist
\lambda\in(0,1)
and
C>0
such that for all
n\geq 1
we have

\|Df^{n}|_{\mathbb{E}^{s}}\|,\|Df^{-n}|_{\mathbb{E}^{u}}\|,\frac{\|Df^{n}X^{s}\|}{\|Df^{n}X^{c}\|},\frac{\|Df^{n}X^{c}\|}{\|Df^{n}X^{u}\|}\leq C\lambda^{n}

(1)

for every unit vectors
X^{\sigma}\in\mathbb{E}^{\sigma},\sigma=s,c,u
.

For the rest of this section, we suppose that
f:(M,\omega)\to(M,\omega)
is a partially hyperbolic symplectomorphism. Moreover we suppose that
\mathbb{E}^{s}
and
\mathbb{E}^{u}
are oriented line bundles therefore they are spanned by unit vector fields
X^{s}
and
X^{u}
.

The following properties can be found in the literature
[
17
,
9
]
but for completeness, we include the proofs.

Lemma 2
.

For every
p\in M
, we have the following

1.

If
X^{s}(p)\in\mathbb{E}^{s}(p)
and
X^{u}(p)\in\mathbb{E}^{u}(p)
are unit vectors then there exists a nonzero function
h:M\to\mathbb{R}

\omega_{p}(X^{s}(p),X^{u}(p))=h(p),

(2)

2.

For every
Y(p)\in\mathbb{E}^{c}(p)
we have

\omega_{p}(X^{s}(p),Y(p))=\omega_{p}(X^{u}(p),Y(p))=0,

(3)

3.

There exists
C>0
such that if
Y(p),Z(p)\in\mathbb{E}^{c}(p)
that are orthonormal then

C^{-1}\leq\omega_{p}(Z(p),Y(p))\leq C.

(4)

Proof.

For the proof of (
3
), we refer the reader to the 7th paragraph in the proof of Lemma 2.5 in
[
17
]
. Using that
\omega
is nondegenerate, there exists
Y\in TM
such that

\omega(X^{s},Y)\neq 0

we can write
Y=aX^{s}+bX^{u}+X^{c}
, by linearity we have

\omega(X^{s},Y)=a\omega(X^{s},X^{s})+b\omega(X^{s},X^{u})+\omega(X^{s},X^{c})=b\omega(X^{s},X^{u})\neq 0

then
\omega(X^{s},X^{u})\neq 0
, we define
h(p):=\omega_{p}(X^{s}(p),X^{u}(p))
.

Using again that
\omega
is nondegenerate, there exists
\delta>0
such that for every
p\in M
there two unit vectors
X^{c}_{1},X^{c}_{2}\in\mathbb{E}^{c}(p)
such that

\delta^{-1}\geq|\omega_{p}(X^{c}_{1},X^{c}_{2})|\geq\delta,

Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
1
1

1

\measuredangle(X^{c}_{1},X^{c}_{2})
denotes the nonoriented angle measured using the given norm on
TM
.

\measuredangle(X^{c}_{1},X^{c}_{2})>\varepsilon.

If
Y,Z\in\mathbb{E}^{c}
that are orthonormal then we can write a change of basis
A
such that
(X^{c}_{1},X^{c}_{2})=A(Y,Z)
where the determinant of
A
is bounded above and below by constant
C_{\varepsilon}^{-1}
and
C_{\varepsilon}
. We have

\omega(Y(p),Z(p))=\pm det(A)\omega(X^{c}_{1},X^{c}_{2})

then we get the result for
C=(\delta C_{\varepsilon})^{-1}
.
∎

3
Dynamical coherence

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1
. We suppose that
(M,\omega)
is a four dimensional symplectic manifold and
f:(M,\omega)\to(M,\omega)
is a
C^{2}
partially hyperbolic symplectomorphism whose stable and unstable bundles are
C^{1}
oriented line bundles.
The following Lemma is somehow standard and follows from (
2
) and (
4
).

Lemma 3
.

There exists
C>0
such that
if
X^{s}(p)\in\mathbb{E}^{s}(p)
and
X^{u}(p)\in\mathbb{E}^{u}(p)
are unit vectors then for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

C^{-1}\leq\|Df^{n}(p)X^{s}(p)\|\cdot\|Df^{n}(p)X^{u}(p)\|\leq C.

(5)

For every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and every
p\in M
, there are two orthonormal vectors
Y_{n},Z_{n}\in\mathbb{E}^{c}(p)
such that

C^{-2}\leq\|Df^{n}(p)Z_{n}\|\cdot\|Df^{n}(p)Y_{n}\|\leq C^{2}.

(6)

Proof.

For (
5
), we use (
2
) and the fact that
(f^{n})^{*}\omega=\omega
to have

h(p)=\omega_{p}(X^{s}(p),X^{u}(p))=(f^{n})^{*}\omega(X^{s}(p),X^{u}(p))=\omega_{f^{n}(p)}(Df^{n}X^{s}(p),Df^{n}X^{u}(p))

using the invariance of the stable and unstable bundles, we can write

Df^{n}X^{s}(p)=\|Df^{n}X^{s}(p)\|X^{s}(f^{n}(p))\quad\text{ and }\quad Df^{n}X^{u}(p)=\|Df^{n}X^{u}(p)\|X^{u}(f^{n}(p))

then we have

h(p)=\|Df^{n}X^{s}(p)\|\cdot\|Df^{n}X^{u}(p)\|\cdot\omega_{f^{n}(p)}(X^{s}(f^{n}(p)),X^{u}(f^{n}(p)))

which implies that
\|Df^{n}X^{s}(p)\|\cdot\|Df^{n}X^{u}(p)\|=\frac{h(p)}{h(f^{n}(p))}
. Since
a
is bounded above and below, we get (
5
).

We recall that
Df^{n}(p)|_{\mathbb{E}^{c}}
maps the unit circle to an ellipse, let
Y_{n}
and
Z_{n}
be the vectors that are mapped to the axis of the ellipse. These vectors are the singular vectors of
Df^{n}(p)|_{\mathbb{E}^{c}}
and they have the properties that
Y_{n},Z_{n}
are orthonormal and
Df^{n}Y_{n},Df^{n}Z_{n}
are orthogonal. Therefore using (
4
) we have

C^{-1}\leq\omega(Y_{n},Z_{n})\leq C.

Since
(f^{n})^{*}\omega=\omega
then we have

\displaystyle\omega_{p}(Y_{n},Z_{n})

\displaystyle=(f^{n})^{*}\omega(Y_{n},Z_{n})=\omega_{f^{n}(p)}(Df^{n}Y_{n},Df^{n}Z_{n})

\displaystyle=\|Df^{n}Y_{n}\|\cdot\|Df^{n}Z_{n}\|\cdot\omega_{f^{n}(p)}(\frac{Df^{n}Y_{n}}{\|Df^{n}Y_{n}\|},\frac{Df^{n}Z_{n}}{\|Df^{n}Z_{n}\|})

Then using (
4
) again we have

C^{-2}\leq\|Df^{n}Y_{n}\|\cdot\|Df^{n}Z_{n}\|\leq C^{2}

which gives (
6
).
∎

Let
\eta
be the
1
-form defined by

\eta:=i_{X^{s}}\circ\omega.

Using (
2
) and (
3
) we have

\ker(\eta)=\mathbb{E}^{s}\oplus\mathbb{E}^{c}\quad\text{ and }\quad\eta(X^{u})\neq 0.

Since
\mathbb{E}^{s}
is
C^{1}
then the vector field
X^{s}
is also
C^{1}
which implies that
\eta
defines a
C^{1}
differential
1
-form. The following lemma gives the involutivity condition in the classical frobenius Theorem.

Lemma 4
.

We have the following

\eta\wedge d\eta=0.

Proof.

By invariance of the bundles, for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have
X^{s}=\frac{f^{-n}_{*}X^{s}}{F_{n}}
where
F_{n}(p)=\|Df^{-n}_{p}|_{\mathbb{E}^{s}}\|
. Using elementary rules of exterior derivative we have

\displaystyle d\eta

\displaystyle=d\left(i_{\frac{f^{-n}_{*}X^{s}}{F_{n}}}\circ\omega\right)=d\left(\frac{1}{F_{n}}\cdot i_{f^{-n}_{*}X^{s}}\circ\omega\right)

\displaystyle=d\left(\frac{1}{F_{n}}\right)\wedge i_{f^{-n}_{*}X^{s}}\circ\omega+\frac{1}{F_{n}}\cdot d\circ i_{f^{-n}_{*}X^{s}}\circ\omega

\displaystyle=d\left(\frac{1}{F_{n}}\right)\wedge i_{f^{-n}_{*}X^{s}}\circ\omega+\frac{1}{F_{n}}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{f^{-n}_{*}X^{s}}\omega

\displaystyle=d\left(\frac{1}{F_{n}}\right)\wedge i_{f^{-n}_{*}X^{s}}\circ\omega+\frac{1}{F_{n}}\cdot\left(f^{n}\right)^{*}\left(\mathcal{L}_{X^{s}}\omega\right)

where the penultimate equality uses Cartan formula
d\circ i+i\circ d=\mathcal{L}
and the fact that
d\omega=0
. The last equality uses
(f^{-n})^{*}\omega=\omega
.
Let
Y_{n},Z_{n}\in\mathbb{E}^{c}
be given by (
6
), then it is easy to see that
i_{f^{-n}_{*}X}\circ\omega(Y_{n})=i_{f^{-n}_{*}X}\circ\omega(Z_{n})=0
which implies that

\displaystyle\left|d\eta(Y_{n},Z_{n})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{F_{n}}\cdot\left(f^{n}\right)^{*}\left(\mathcal{L}_{X^{s}}\omega\right)(Y_{n},Z_{n})\right|=\left|\frac{1}{F_{n}}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{X^{s}}\omega(f^{n}_{*}Y_{n},f^{n}_{*}Z_{n})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|f^{n}_{*}Y_{n}\|\cdot\|f^{n}_{*}Z_{n}\|}{F_{n}}\|\mathcal{L}_{X^{s}}\omega\|\leq C\frac{1}{F_{n}}\|\mathcal{L}_{X}\omega\|

where the last inequality uses (
6
). Given two orthonornal vectors
Y,Z\in\mathbb{E}^{c}
, we can use the basis
\{Y_{n},Z_{n}\}
and the linearity of
d\eta
to have

d\eta(Y,Z)=Bd\eta(Y_{n},Z_{n})

where
B
is the determinant of the change of basis matrix, therefore
|B|\leq 4
. Thus we have

\left|d\eta(Y,Z)\right|\leq 4C\frac{1}{F_{n}}\|\mathcal{L}_{X}\omega\|

Therefore taking the limit as
n\to\infty
on the right side gives

d\eta(Y,Z)=0

(7)

For
X^{c}\in\mathbb{E}^{c}
, using that
i_{f^{-n}_{*}X}\circ\omega(X^{s})=i_{f^{-n}_{*}X}\circ\omega(X^{c})=0
, we have

\displaystyle\left|d\eta(X^{s},X^{c})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{F_{n}}\cdot\left(f^{n}\right)^{*}\left(\mathcal{L}_{X^{s}}\omega\right)(X^{s},X^{c})\right|=\left|\frac{1}{F_{n}}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{X^{s}}\omega(f^{n}_{*}X^{s},f^{n}_{*}X^{c})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|f^{n}_{*}X^{s}\|\cdot\|f^{n}_{*}X^{c}\|}{F_{n}}\|\mathcal{L}_{X^{s}}\omega\|.

Then taking the limit as
n\to\infty
and using (
1
) we have

d\eta(X^{s},X^{c})=0.

(8)

The lemma follows from (
7
) and (
8
) we have

d\eta|_{\ker(\eta)\times\ker(\eta)}=0
which implies that
\eta\wedge d\eta=0
.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

By Lemma
4
, using Frobenius Theorem
[
10
, Theorem 14.5]
, we have that
\mathbb{E}^{s}\oplus\mathbb{E}^{c}=\ker(\eta)
is tangent to a
C^{1}
foliation
\mathcal{F}^{sc}
and, similarly,
\mathbb{E}^{c}\oplus\mathbb{E}^{u}
is tangent to a
C^{1}
foliation
\mathcal{F}^{cu}
. The center foliation is given by

\mathcal{F}^{c}=\mathcal{F}^{sc}\cap\mathcal{F}^{cu}.

Moreover the foliation
\mathcal{F}^{c}
is
C^{1}
.
∎

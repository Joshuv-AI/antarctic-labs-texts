On the regularity of continuous solutions to multidimensional scalar conservation laws with L ∞ L^{\infty} source
Fabio Ancona, Laura Caravenna, Alexander J. Cliffe, Elio Marconi

Abstract
We prove the Hölder regularity of continuous isentropic solutions to multi-dimensional scalar balance laws when the source term is bounded and the flux satisfies general assumptions of nonlinearity. The results are achieved by exploiting the kinetic formulation of the balance law.



1
Introduction

In this paper we study the regularizing effect of nonlinearity on continuous solutions to balance laws

(1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+{\rm div}_{\bm{x}}f(u)=g,

where
f:\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{R}^{d}
is a smooth flux,
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{d})
is a bounded source, and
u=u(t,x)
,
x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
, is the unknown.

In one space dimension
d=1
, and with zero source
g
, it was established in
[
14
]
that the nonlinearity of the flux
f
induces a fractional regularity of distributional solutions to (
1
) which satisfy entropy inequalities

(2)

\partial_{t}\eta(u)+\partial_{x}q(u)\leq 0\qquad\text{in}\ \ \mathcal{D}_{t,x}^{\prime},

associated to any entropy-entropy flux pair
(\eta,q)
, where
\eta(u)
is convex and
q^{\prime}(u)=\eta^{\prime}(u)f^{\prime}(u)
for every
u
.

As a technical tool to provide stronger regularity to dispersive PDEs in one space dimension
d=1
, such as the Hunter-Saxton equation and the Camassa-Holm equation, Dafermos began investigating the method of characteristics for
continuous
solutions to a balance law (
1
), focusing on the quadratic flux and assuming that the source
g
was continuous in the single space variable.
When
g
is zero, thus for a conservation law, he established Euclidean Lipschitz regularity of
f^{\prime}(u)
for general smooth fluxes
f
. In particular, Dafermos established that continuous solutions to a conservation law are isentropic: they do not incur entropy production since (
2
) holds as an equality.

Motivated by applications to the analysis of intrinsic Lipschitz graphs in the Heisenberg group, the effect of nonlinearity of the flux on continuous solutions to (
1
) in one space dimension
d=1
was studied in
[
5
]
for the quadratic flux and bounded sources
g
. This work followed earlier research on intrinsic regular graphs
[
3
]
relative to continuous sources
g
. Specifically, it was proved in
[
5
]
, by generalizing a lemma on ODEs already present in
[
6
]
, that every continuous solution to the one-dimensional balance law (
1
) with quadratic flux is
\frac{1}{2}
-Hölder continuous.
This Hölder continuity result was then generalized to
\frac{1}{\ell}
-Hölder continuity if the flux
f
is nonlinear of order
\ell
, see
[
7
,
8
]
.

The latter result, alongside a reduction argument allowing for inflection points, contains a finer analysis of the Hölder continuity constant on small balls: it contains a direct proof that this constant is almost everywhere vanishingly small as the radius vanishes, based on the analysis with the Lebesgue differentiation theorem in
[
7
]
.
An indirect, much more complex argument was provided by
[
6
,
10
,
9
]
.

We stress that continuous solutions to (
1
), even when
d=1
and the flux is quadratic, can be very irregular from the Euclidean point of view: continuous solutions to (
1
) with smooth
convex
fluxes
f
where the
\alpha
-nonlinearity condition fails are generally not Hölder continuous of any exponent, see
[
2
]
.
Moreover, their graph can be a fractal set and they generally do not have bounded variation, see
[
12
,
2
]
.
Nevertheless, continuous solutions to the one-dimensional balance law (
1
) with any smooth flux are isentropic, as shown in
[
1
]
where it was proved by approximation with functions of bounded variation.

In the case of more space dimensions, only particular cases are treated, e.g.
[
6
]
analyses a multidimensional system by a reduction argument to the one dimensional setting.
See also
[
4
,
18
]
.

This paper exploits the kinetic formulation to
provide a first
extension of
the Hölder regularity results
to a real multidimensional setting.
Namely, let
u:\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}\to[0,1]
be a bounded, continuous solution of the scalar balance law (
1
), and
assume that
u
is isentropic in the sense that

(3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta(u)+{\rm div}_{\bm{x}}q(u)=\eta^{\prime}(u)g\qquad\text{in}\ \ \mathcal{D}_{t,x}^{\prime},

for any entropy-entropy flux pair
(\eta,q)
.

In the kinetic formulation, for a continuous isentropic solution, there holds (see
[
13
,
16
]
)

(4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\chi+f^{\prime}(v)\cdot\nabla_{\bm{x}}\chi=\mu_{0},\qquad\mu_{0}\coloneqq g\mathcal{L}^{d+1}\otimes\delta_{\{v=u(t,\bm{x})\}},

in the sense of distributions,
where
\chi:\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}\to\{0,1\}
is given by

(5)

\displaystyle\chi(t,x,v)\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&\quad\text{if $0<v\leq u(t,x)$,}\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We will make the following standard assumption on the flux
f\in C^{1}([0,1];\mathbf{R}^{d})
, which quantifies the extent of nonlinearity of the flux.

Assumption 1
.

There exists
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
C>0
such that, for every
(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d+1}
with
|(\tau,\xi)|=1
, and any
\delta>0
, we have

(6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}^{1}\big(\{v\in[0,1]:|1\cdot\tau+{f^{\prime}(v)}\cdot\xi|<\delta\}\big)\leq C\delta^{\alpha}.\end{aligned}

The main result of the paper is:

Theorem 2
.

Let
u\in C(\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{d};[0,1])
be a continuous and isentropic solution of the scalar balance law (
1
) with bounded measurable source
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{d})
, and flux
f\in C^{2}([0,1];\mathbf{R}^{d})
satisfying the
\alpha
-nonlinearity Assumption
1
.
Then, for any
t\in\mathbf{R}
,
u(t,\cdot)
is
\overline{\gamma}
-Hölder continuous in space with

\overline{\gamma}\coloneqq\frac{\alpha}{2d}\,.

We next refine this result making the following more restrictive nonlinearity assumption.

Assumption 3
.

The flux
f\in C^{d+2}([0,1];\mathbf{R}^{d})
satisfies that, for every
v\in[0,1]
,
\{f^{\prime\prime}(v),\cdots,f^{(d+1)}(v)\}
is a spanning set of
\mathbf{R}^{d}
, or equivalently that

\{(1,f^{\prime}(v)),\cdots,(0,f^{(d)}(v)),(0,f^{(d+1)}(v))\}
is a spanning set of
\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
.

Notice that this is the maximal nonlinearity assumption compatible with the smoothness of the flux, meaning that it implies Assumption
1
with
\alpha=\frac{1}{d}
and there are no smooth fluxes
f:\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{R}^{d}
which satisfy (
6
) with
\alpha>\frac{1}{d}
, see Lemma
19
below.
Assumption
3
is satisfied by the Burgers flux

f(u)=\bigg(\frac{u^{2}}{2}\,,\frac{u^{3}}{3}\,,\dots\,,\frac{u^{d+1}}{d+1}\bigg).

Theorem 4
.

Let
u\in C(\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{d};[0,1])
be a continuous and isentropic solution of the scalar balance law (
1
) with bounded measurable source
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{d})
, and flux
f\in C^{d+2}([0,1];\mathbf{R}^{d})
satisfying the non degeneracy Assumption
3
.
Then, for any
t\in\mathbf{R}
,
u(t,\cdot)
is
\overline{\gamma}
-Hölder continuous in space with

\overline{\gamma}\coloneqq{\frac{1}{2d}}\,.

We thus gain in the exponent
\overline{\gamma}
a factor of
\frac{1}{\alpha}=d
.
The paper is organized as follows:

In Section
2
, we analyze the one-dimensional case with the Burgers flux and obtain in Corollary
9
the
\frac{1}{2}
-Hölder regularity for continuous isentropic solutions of (
1
).
Although the results of this section are contained in those following, we feel it is important to begin by proving this case due to the relevance of the Burgers equation in applications, and due to the fact that the proofs are more easily readable.

In Section
3
, we address the multidimensional case under the
\alpha
-nonlinearity Assumption
1
. The main result of this section is Proposition
17
, which proves Theorem
2
.

In Section
4
, under the nonlinearity Assumption
3
, we improve the Hölder continuity exponent in Proposition
24

4
.
As in §
2
, we first present the proof for the case of the Burgers equation, before generalizing it.
We also include several examples to illustrate our results.

(67)

\displaystyle p(\tilde{t})-p(\tilde{t}-\tilde{a})=\int_{I\times\mathbf{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)}\tilde{\phi}(t,x,v)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{0},

where
I\subset\mathbf{R}
is the time interval with endpoints
\tilde{t}-\tilde{a}
and
\tilde{t}
.

2.

Now we specialise our estimate to the problem at hand.
Using the form of
\mu_{0}
and
\phi
given in (
4
) and (

), we have

(68)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{I\times\mathbf{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)}\tilde{\phi}(t,x,v)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}=\int_{I\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}g(t,x)\phi(x-(t-\tilde{t})f^{\prime}(u(t,x)),u(t,x))\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\\
\leq\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{I\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}\bm{1}_{x-(t-\tilde{t})f^{\prime}(u(t,x))\in Q_{r+r^{\prime}}(\tilde{x})}\,\bm{1}_{\tilde{v}-\omega^{\prime}\leq u(t,x)\leq\tilde{v}+\omega+\omega^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x.\end{aligned}

At a given time
t\in I

given by the second indicator function, in order for the product of the two indicator functions to be non-zero, it is necessary that

(69)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} r+r^{\prime}&\geq|x-\tilde{x}-(t-\tilde{t})f^{\prime}(u(t,x))|_{\infty}\\
&\geq|x-\tilde{x}-(t-\tilde{t})f^{\prime}(\tilde{v}+\frac{\omega}{2})|_{\infty}-|t-\tilde{t}||f^{\prime}(u(t,x))-f^{\prime}(\tilde{v}+\frac{\omega}{2})|_{\infty}\\
&\geq|x-\tilde{x}-(t-\tilde{t})f^{\prime}(\tilde{v}+\frac{\omega}{2})|_{\infty}-|\tilde{a}|(\frac{\omega}{2}+\omega^{\prime})\left\lVert f^{\prime\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}},\end{aligned}

that is to say that
x\in Q_{r+r^{\prime}+|\tilde{a}|(\frac{\omega}{2}+\omega^{\prime})\left\lVert f^{\prime\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}(\tilde{x}+(t-\tilde{t})f^{\prime}(\tilde{v}+\frac{\omega}{2}))
.
The given estimate in the lemma follows since

.
∎

By choosing the constants

and
\omega^{\prime}\leq\omega
sufficiently small in Lemma

so that the test function

approximates closely the characteristic function of the cuboid

, we can combine the estimates in Lemmas

–

to deduce the algebraic decay rate of

, uniformly in

Lemma 14
.

Let
u\in C(\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{d};[0,1])
be a continuous and isentropic solution of the scalar balance law (
1
) with bounded measurable source

.
Then, there exists a constant

, depending only on
(d,\left\lVert f^{\prime\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}},\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}},\widetilde{C},C_{\omega})

(70)

\displaystyle h_{r}(t,x)\leq C_{0}r^{\gamma_{0}},\qquad\gamma_{0}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2(1+\frac{d}{\alpha})}

for every
(t,x)\in\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
.

Proof of Lemma
14
.

The proof follows closely
[
15
, Proposition 4.4]
, though we present the main steps below.

1.

Fix
r>0
,
(t,x)\in\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
,
h\coloneqq h_{r}(t,x)
,
\omega=C_{\omega}h^{1+\frac{d}{\alpha}}
, and let
(\tilde{t},\tilde{x})\in\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
,
\tilde{v}\in[0,1)
,
\tilde{a}\in\mathbf{R}
, and
\mathcal{Q}
be given as in Lemma
12
.
For
r^{\prime}\leq r
, and
\omega^{\prime}\leq\omega
to be determined later, let the test function
\phi
be given as in Lemma
13
.

Using Lemma
12
, we have the estimate

(71)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)}\phi(x,v)\big(\chi_{\hyp u(\tilde{t})}-\chi_{\mathsf{FT}(\hyp u(\tilde{t}-\tilde{a});\tilde{a})}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v\\
&\quad=\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\phi(x,v)\big(\chi_{\hyp u(\tilde{t})}-\chi_{\mathsf{FT}(\hyp u(\tilde{t}-\tilde{a});\tilde{a})}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v+\int_{\supp\phi\setminus\mathcal{Q}}\phi(x,v)\big(\chi_{\hyp u(\tilde{t})}-\chi_{\mathsf{FT}(\hyp u(\tilde{t}-\tilde{a});\tilde{a})}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v\\
&\quad=\mathcal{L}^{d+1}\big(\mathcal{Q}\cap\hyp u(\tilde{t})\big)-\mathcal{L}^{d+1}\big(\mathcal{Q}\cap\mathsf{FT}(\hyp u(\tilde{t}-\tilde{a});\tilde{a})\big)+\int_{\supp\phi\setminus\mathcal{Q}}\phi(x,v)\big(\chi_{\hyp u(\tilde{t})}-\chi_{\mathsf{FT}(\hyp u(\tilde{t}-\tilde{a});\tilde{a})}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v\\
&\quad\geq\frac{\omega h|Q_{r}|}{2(d+1)}-\mathcal{L}^{d+1}\big(\supp\phi\setminus\mathcal{Q}\big).\end{aligned}

Clearly, we have

(72)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{d+1}\big(\supp\phi\setminus\mathcal{Q}\big)\leq 2^{d}(r+r^{\prime})^{d}(\omega+2\omega^{\prime})-2^{d}r^{d}\omega\leq 2^{d}(2r^{d}\omega^{\prime}+3\cdot 2^{d}r^{d-1}r^{\prime}\omega).

Therefore, so long as
2^{d}(2r^{d}\omega^{\prime}+3\cdot 2^{d}r^{d-1}r^{\prime}\omega)\leq\frac{1}{4}\frac{\omega h|Q_{r}|}{2(d+1)}
, we have

(73)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)}\phi(x,v)\big(\chi_{\hyp u(\tilde{t})}-\chi_{\mathsf{FT}(\hyp u(\tilde{t}-\tilde{a});\tilde{a})}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v\geq\frac{\omega h|Q_{r}|}{4(d+1)}.

It is sufficient to take
r^{\prime}\coloneqq\frac{hr}{3\cdot 2^{d+4}(d+1)}\leq r
, and

\omega^{\prime}\coloneqq\frac{\omega h}{32(d+1)}\leq\omega
.

2.

Combining the above estimate with Lemma
13
, we have

(74)

\displaystyle\frac{\omega h|Q_{r}|}{4(d+1)}\leq|\tilde{a}|\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}|Q_{r+r^{\prime}+|\tilde{a}|(\frac{\omega}{2}+\omega^{\prime})\left\lVert f^{\prime\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}|.

Estimating
|\tilde{a}|
via (
41
), and using the choice of

, we have

(75)

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\omega}h^{2+\frac{d}{\alpha}}r^{d}}{4(d+1)}\leq\widetilde{C}\frac{2r\sqrt{d}}{h^{d/\alpha}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}(2r+2\widetilde{C}2r\sqrt{d}C_{\omega}h\left\lVert f^{\prime\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}})^{d}.

Finally, after rearranging and noting that

, we obtain

(76)

\displaystyle h\leq C_{0}r^{\gamma_{0}},\quad\ \,C_{0}\coloneqq\big[\frac{\widetilde{C}}{C_{\omega}}2^{d+3}(d+1)\sqrt{d}\,\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}(1+\widetilde{C}C_{\omega}\sqrt{d}\,\left\lVert f^{\prime\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}})^{d}\big]^{\gamma_{0}},\quad\ \,\gamma_{0}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2(1+\frac{d}{\alpha})}.

∎

Next we show that an algebraic decay rate of
r\mapsto h_{r}(t,x)
, uniformly in

, is sufficient to deduce the Hölder regularity in space of
u(t,\cdot)
.

Lemma 15
.

Suppose
u\in C(\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{d};[0,1])
satisfies the estimate

(77)

\displaystyle h_{r}(t,x)\leq Cr^{\gamma}

for some
\gamma\in(0,1)
, and
C>0
, uniformly in
x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
.
Then
u(t,\cdot)
is
\gamma
-Hölder with constant at most
2C\big(1+\frac{2}{2^{\gamma}-1}\big)
, uniformly in
x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
.

In particular, let
\gamma_{0}=\frac{1}{2(1+\frac{d}{\alpha})}
be as in the previous lemma.
Then there is
C_{\gamma_{0}}>0
depending only on
\gamma_{0}
such that, if
u
satisfies (
77
) for some
\gamma\in[\gamma_{0},1)
, then
u(t,\cdot)
is
\gamma
-Hölder with constant at most
C_{\gamma_{0}}C
.

Proof of Lemma
15
.

Since we work at a fixed time
t\in\mathbf{R}
throughout, let us suppress all time dependence in this proof.
For any
x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
, let
r\coloneqq|x-y|
.
We have

(78)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |u(x)-u(y)|\leq|u(x)-(u)_{Q_{r}(x)}|+|(u)_{Q_{r}(x)}-(u)_{Q_{r}(y)}|+|u(y)-(u)_{Q_{r}(y)}|\\
\leq|u(x)-(u)_{Q_{r}(x)}|+|u(y)-(u)_{Q_{r}(y)}|+2h_{r}(x).\end{aligned}

Now, since a single cube is tessellated by
2^{d}
sub-cubes each of half the side length, it is clear that

(79)

\displaystyle(u)_{Q_{r}(x)}=\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{2^{d}}(u)_{Q_{\frac{r}{2}}(x_{i})},

where the points
\{x_{i}\in\overline{Q_{\frac{r}{2}}(x)}:1\leq i\leq 2^{d}\}
are the corners of the cube
Q_{\frac{r}{2}}(x)
.
Therefore we can estimate

(80)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |(u)_{Q_{r}(x)}-(u)_{Q_{\frac{r}{2}(x)}}|=\char 12\relax{\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{2^{d}}(u)_{Q_{\frac{r}{2}}(x_{i})}-(u)_{Q_{\frac{r}{2}(x)}}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{2^{d}}\char 12\relax{(u)_{Q_{\frac{r}{2}}(x_{i})}-(u)_{Q_{\frac{r}{2}}(x)}}\\
\leq 2h_{\frac{r}{2}}(x).\end{aligned}

Since
u
is continuous, we have

(81)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |(u)_{Q_{r}(x)}-u(x)|=\char 12\relax{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big((u)_{Q_{\frac{r}{2^{k}}}(x)}-(u)_{Q_{\frac{r}{2^{k+1}}}(x)}\Big)}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2h_{\frac{r}{2^{k+1}}}(x)\leq 2C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\big(\frac{r}{2^{k+1}}\big)^{\gamma}\\
=\frac{2Cr^{\gamma}}{2^{\gamma}-1}.\end{aligned}

Therefore

(82)

\displaystyle|u(x)-u(y)|\leq 2h_{r}(x)+\frac{4Cr^{\gamma}}{2^{\gamma}-1}\leq 2C\big(1+\frac{2}{2^{\gamma}-1}\big)|x-y|^{\gamma}.

∎

4
Improved decomposition lemma

In this section we improve the decomposition Lemma
10
to obtain a better order on the estimate of the coefficients in (
39
).
We prove it first in the case of the multi-dimensional Burgers equation, and then for general smooth nondegenerate fluxes
f
, in which cases we improve the order from
\frac{1}{d^{2}}
to
\frac{1}{d}
.
This is interesting in order to get a better regularity estimate for continuous solutions to a balance law with bounded source, namely improving the Hölder continuity exponent from
\frac{1}{2d^{2}}
to
\frac{1}{2d}
as stated in Proposition
24
below.

In the direction approximately parallel to the
(1+\ell)
-th derivative
f^{(1+\ell)}

\frac{1}{\ell}
for the decomposition lemma.

We first recall the elementary result in
[
17
, § 2.3]
which relates Assumption
1
with Assumption
3
.

Lemma 19
.

When
f\in C^{d+2}(\mathbf{R})
, Assumption
3
is equivalent to taking
\alpha=\frac{1}{d}
in Assumption
1
.

In the following sub-sections we prove the following lemma, that, as stated, improves the decomposition Lemma
4
from the bound
h^{-1}
raised to the power
\frac{1}{d^{2}}
, to the better power
{\frac{1}{d}}
, in the worst case, and to the power
{\frac{1}{\ell}}
in the direction
a_{\ell}
, for linearly independent directions
a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}

Lemma 20
.

Suppose the flux
f\in C^{d+2}([0,1];\mathbf{R}^{d})
satisfies the nonlinearity Assumption
3
.
Given
v>0
and
0<h<1
, set

, where

(89)

\displaystyle a=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}\left[f^{\prime}(v_{i})-f^{\prime}(v_{0})\right],\qquad\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert\leq\widetilde{C}_{d}\frac{\left\lVert a\right\rVert}{h^{d}}.

Moreover, for each
\ell=1,\dots,d

a_{\ell}\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
satisfying the better estimate

\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert\leq\widetilde{C}_{d}\frac{\left\lVert a_{\ell}\right\rVert}{h^{\ell}}.

Remark
.

We show in the proof that for the Burgers equation, the direction

is given by

a_{\ell}\penalty\ =\penalty\ f^{(1+\ell)}(v)\penalty\ =\penalty\ \left(\frac{i!}{(i-\ell)!}\cdot v^{i-\ell}\delta_{i\geq\ell}\right)_{i=1,\dots,d}.

Then for a flux satisfying Assumption

is still a direction close to the direction of

(

) and by sub-multiplicity

(114)

\left\lVert\widetilde{A}^{-1}\cdot R\right\rVert\penalty\ \leq\penalty\ \left\lVert R(f,v_{0},h)\right\rVert\cdot C(f,v_{0})\cdot\frac{C_{d}}{d^{d+1}}\cdot h\,.

Since for
h=0
the matrix
R(f,v_{0},h)
in (
110
) has all columns proportional to
f^{(d+2)}(v_{0})
, then

\left\lVert R(f,v_{0},0)\right\rVert\penalty\ =\penalty\ \frac{1}{(d+1)!}\left\lVert f^{(d+2)}(v_{0})\right\rVert\cdot\left\lVert(1,2^{(d+1)},\dots,d^{(d+1)})\right\rVert\,,

and
\left\lVert R(f,v_{0},h)\right\rVert
is close to such value for
h
small.
In particular, by (
114
) for
h
small one has
\left\lVert\widetilde{A}^{-1}\cdot B\right\rVert\leq 2^{-1}
, which allows to apply (
113
) to estimate the norm of the inverse of
A=\widetilde{A}+R
:

(115)

\left\lVert A^{-1}(v_{0},h,d)\right\rVert\penalty\ =\penalty\ \left\lVert\left(\widetilde{A}+R\right)^{-1}\right\rVert\penalty\ \leq\penalty\ 2\left\lVert\widetilde{A}\right\rVert.

Recalling (
111
) we reach the thesis: for
h
small it holds that

\left\lVert A^{-1}(v_{0},h,d)\right\rVert\penalty\ \leq\penalty\ \left\lVert\widetilde{A}^{-1}\cdot B\right\rVert\penalty\ \leq\penalty\ \widetilde{2}C(f,v_{0})\cdot C_{d}h^{-d}\,.

∎

Proof of the stronger directional estimate in Lemma
20
for nondegenerate fluxes.

Continuing with the notation of the proof just concluded for the general estimate in Lemma
20
, in particular (
110
), consider now vectors
a\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
,
\left\lVert a\right\rVert=1
, that for some
\xi\in\mathbf{R}
satisfy the proportionality

\left(W(f,v_{0})+H_{h}^{-1}(h)R(f,v_{0},h)\left(\frac{h}{d}\right)^{d+1}\right)^{-1}a\penalty\ =\penalty\ \xi\,{\widehat{\rm e}}_{\ell}

which is of course equivalent to

a\penalty\ =\penalty\ \xi\cdot\left(W(f,v_{0})+H_{h}^{-1}(h)R(f,v_{0},h)\left(\frac{h}{d}\right)^{d+1}\right)\cdot{\widehat{\rm e}}_{\ell}\,.

Observe that necessarily
\xi\geq C(v_{0},d)
, for
h
small enough, since
\left(W(f,v_{0})+H_{h}^{-1}(h)R(f,v_{0},h)\right)
is not singular, since
W(f,v_{0})
is not singular by the nonlinearity Assumption
3
and by Lemma
26

\left\lVert H_{h}^{-1}(h)R(f,v_{0},h)\left(\frac{h}{d}\right)^{d+1}\right\rVert\penalty\ \leq\penalty\ \left\lVert H_{h}^{-1}(h)\right\rVert\cdot\left\lVert R(f,v_{0},h)\right\rVert\cdot\left(\frac{h}{d}\right)^{d+1}\penalty\ \leq\penalty\ C_{d}\left\lVert R(f,v_{0},h)\right\rVert h\,.

Then one has the expression

A^{-1}(v_{0},h,d)a\penalty\ =\penalty\ H_{d}^{-1}(h)\cdot\left(W(f,v_{0})+H_{h}^{-1}(h)R(f,v_{0},h)\left(\frac{h}{d}\right)^{d+1}\right)^{-1}a\penalty\ =\penalty\ \xi H_{d}^{-1}(h)\cdot{\widehat{\rm e}}_{\ell}\,.

Since, by its expression (
100
)–(
101
), the
\ell
-th column of the matrix
H_{d}^{-1}(h)
is proportional to
h^{-\ell}
, then

\left\lVert A^{-1}(v_{0},h,d)a\right\rVert\penalty\ \geq\penalty\ \xi\penalty\ \geq\penalty\ C(v_{0},d)\cdot\tilde{c}_{d}\cdot h^{-\ell}\,\,

where
\tilde{c}_{d}
is the minimum norm among columns of the matrix
H_{d}^{-1}(1)
.

In particular, for vectors
a_{\ell}
that are proportional to the vector

\frac{1}{\ell!}f^{(1+\ell)}(v_{0})+\frac{2^{(d+1)}}{d^{d+1}\cdot(d+1)!}H_{h}^{-1}(h)f^{(2+d)}(\xi_{\ell})\cdot h^{d+1}\,,

which perturbs
\frac{1}{\ell!}f^{(1+\ell)}(v_{0})
, one has the stronger estimate in the thesis.

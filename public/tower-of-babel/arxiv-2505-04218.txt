Convergence Rate of Euler–Maruyama Scheme to the Invariant Probability Measure Under Total Variation Distance for the SDEs
Yuke Wang, Yinna Ye

Abstract
This article shows the geometric decay rate of the Euler–Maruyama scheme for a one-dimensional stochastic differential equation towards its invariant probability measure under total variation distance. Firstly, the existence and uniqueness of invariant probability measure and the uniform geometric ergodicity of the chain are studied through the introduction of non-atomic Markov chains. Secondly, the equivalent conditions for uniform geometric ergodicity of the chain are discovered by constructing a split Markov chain based on the original Euler–Maruyama scheme.

Introduction

Consider the following stochastic differential equation (SDE) on
\mathbb{R}
:

dX_{t}=g(X_{t})\,dt+\sigma\,dB_{t},\quad X_{0}=x_{0},

(1.1)

where
(B_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is a standard Brownian motion,
\sigma>0
is a constant, and
g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

satisfies the hypothesis below.

Hypothesis (H1)

There exist
L
,
K_{1}>0
and
K_{2}\geqslant 0
such that for every
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\displaystyle|g(x)-g(y)|

\displaystyle\leqslant L|x-y|,

(1.2)

\displaystyle(g(x)-g(y))(x-y)

\displaystyle\leqslant-K_{1}(x-y)^{2}+K_{2}.

(1.3)

Moreover,
g
is second-order differentiable, and the second-order derivative of
g
is bounded.

The inequality (
1.2
) implies for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

g^{2}(x)\leqslant 2L^{2}x^{2}+2{g^{2}(0)},\quad|g^{\prime}(x)|\leqslant L,

(1.4)

where
g^{\prime}
represents the derivative of the function
g
.
Moreover, the inequality (
1.3
) and Young’s inequality imply

xg(x)\leqslant-\frac{K_{1}}{2}x^{2}+c_{g},

(1.5)

with
c_{g}=K_{2}+\frac{1}{2K_{1}}g^{2}(0)
.

For a given step size
\eta\in(0,1)
, the Euler–Maruyama (EM) scheme
(\theta_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
for the SDE (
1.1
) is given by the following recursive relation: for any
k\geqslant 0
,

\theta_{k+1}=\theta_{k}+\eta\,g(\theta_{k})+\sqrt{\eta}\,\sigma\varepsilon_{k+1},

(1.6)

where
(\varepsilon_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
are independent and identically distributed standard normal random variables.

Over the past decades, EM schemes have been widely and intensively used for solution approximations of SDEs on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geqslant 1
(see for instance
[
2
,
3
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
). In the case when
g(x)=-\nabla U(x)
with
U
being a potential, (
1.1
) is called Langevin diffusion, and (
1.6
) is called unadjusted Langevin algorithm (ULA) with constant step size. The ULA is nowadays a popular class of Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithms (
[
4
]
), which is commonly applied to solve Monte Carlo sampling problems especially in the field of machine learning (see, for instance
[
15
,
7
]
). Roberts and Tweedie
[
21
]
initially provided probabilistic analysis of asymptotic behaviour of Langevin diffusion and ULA. They found necessary (Theorem 2.2
[
21
]
) and sufficient conditions (Theorem 2.3,
[
21
]
) for exponential ergodicity of Langevin diffusion, and sufficient conditions for geometric ergodicity of ULA (Theorem 3.1,
[
21
]
) respectively. Dalalyan
[
5
]
found an upper bound for the error in total variation (TV) distance between
\theta_{k}
and the ergodic measure of
(X_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
. Durmus and Moulines
[
9
]
established geometric ergodicity in TV distance for Langevin diffusion under similar convexity and Lipschitz conditions, with explicit convergence rates. Recently, Fan et al.
[
10
]
established normalized and self-normalized Cramér-type moderate deviations for the EM scheme for the SDE (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geqslant 1
. Meanwhile, the geometric ergodicity of the ULA towards its invariant probability measure under appropriate distance has been rarely studied so far in the literature. As an extension of classical Markov chains with discrete state space, the EM scheme studied in this paper is a non-atomic Markov chain, which is also of independent interest for the study on other stochastic models with a non-independence structure.

The objectives of this work are to prove the geometric ergodicity of the EM scheme given by (
1.6
) for the SDE (
1.1
) under TV distance, and provide furthermore a convergence rate to its invariant probability measure.

Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
be the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
\mathbb{R}
. From (
1.6
), it can be seen that
(\theta_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
is a Markov chain on the continuous state space
\mathbb{R}
, with Markov kernel
P_{\eta}
given by

\displaystyle P_{\eta}(x,A)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\eta}\,\sigma}\int_{A}\exp\left(-\frac{(y-x-\eta g(x))^{2}}{2\eta\sigma^{2}}\right)dy,

(1.7)

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
. From (
1.7
),
P_{\eta}
admits a kernel density with respect to (w.r.t) the Lebesgue measure given by
p_{\eta}(x,y):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\eta}\,\sigma}\exp\left(-\frac{(y-x-\eta g(x))^{2}}{2\eta\sigma^{2}}\right)
. The Markov kernel
P_{\eta}
can be defined equivalently as, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
and measurable function
h
on
\left(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\right)
,

\displaystyle P_{\eta}\,h(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(y)\,p_{\eta}(x,y)\,dy.

Let us introduce now TV norm
\|\cdot\|_{TV}
and TV distance
d_{TV}
respectively. Let
(X,\mathfrak{X})
be a measurable space. Consider
\mathbbm{F}_{b}
the set of all bounded and measurable functions from
(X,\mathfrak{X})
to
\mathbb{R}
. For any finite signed measure
\xi
on
(X,\mathfrak{X})
, the total variation norm of
\xi
is defined by

\|\xi\|_{TV}:=\sup\left\{\xi(f)\,|\,f\in\mathbbm{F}_{b},\,|f|_{\infty}\leqslant 1\right\},

where
|f|_{\infty}:=\sup\left\{|f(x)|\,|\,x\in X\right\}
. For any two probability measures
\xi
and
\xi^{\prime}
on
(X,\mathfrak{X})
, the total variation distance between
\xi
and
\xi^{\prime}
is defined by

d_{TV}(\xi,\xi^{\prime}):=\frac{1}{2}\|\xi-\xi^{\prime}\|_{TV}.

(1.8)

Throughout the paper, sometimes supplied with some indices,
\tau
and
c
denote positive constants, whose value may vary from line to line. Subsequent symbols with the prefix
\tau
will be redefined according to specific scenarios and are independent of each other. For any
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

and

. When we write “
C
is an (

)-small set”, it will be always assumed that
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
and
\nu
is a probability measure.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
derives some basic properties (Proposition
2.1
) and existence and uniqueness of the invariant probability measure

(Theorem

) of the EM scheme
(\theta_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
. Section
3
obtains the convergence rate of the chain
(\theta_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
towards

under TV distance (Theorem

), through demonstrating furthermore the equivalent conditions for its uniform geometric ergodicity (Proposition

). To prove Proposition

, we use the splitting construction approach, which is described in Section
4
. In Section

In the Appendix, some known and useful properties of non-atomic and atomic Markov kernels are collected respectively in

A
and
B
. In

given by

denotes the expectation under the probability measure

.

Hypothesis (H2)

For any

and
\eta\in(0,1)
,
|x+\eta g(x)|\leqslant c.

We have the following basic property about the Markov chain
(\theta_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
and its kernel
P_{\eta}
. Proposition
2.1
(1) below provides a sufficient condition for the existence of accessible
(1,\mu)
-small set
C
satisfying
\mu(C)>0
.

Proposition 2.1

(1)

If
C
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}
such that
\mbox{Leb}(C)>0
, then
C
is an accessible
(1,\epsilon\nu)
-small set, where

\displaystyle\epsilon:=\frac{\mbox{Leb}(C)}{\sqrt{\eta}\,\sigma}\inf_{(x,y)\in C^{2}}\phi\left(\frac{y-x-\eta g(x)}{\sqrt{\eta}\sigma}\right),

(2.9)

\nu(\cdot):=\mbox{Leb}(\cdot\,\cap C)/\mbox{Leb}(C)
, and
\phi(x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\{-x^{2}/2\}
is the probability density function of standard normal distribution.

(2)

For any
\eta\in(0,1)
, the Markov kernel
P_{\eta}
is irreducible and strongly aperiodic.

(3)

Under Hypothesis
(H2)
, the state space
\mathbb{R}
of the Markov chain
(\theta_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
is an
1
-small set.

Proof 1

(1)

Suppose that
C
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}
such that
\mbox{Leb}(C)>0
. Recall that
P_{\eta}
admits a Markov kernel
p_{\eta}(x,y)
, given by

p_{\eta}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\eta}\,\sigma}\phi\left(\frac{y-x-\eta g(x)}{\sqrt{\eta}\sigma}\right).

Then for all
x\in C
and
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle P_{\eta}(x,A)=\int_{A}p_{\eta}(x,y)dy\geqslant\int_{A\cap C}p_{\eta}(x,y)dy\geqslant\,\epsilon\,\frac{\mbox{Leb}(A\cap C)}{\mbox{Leb}(C)},

where
\epsilon\in(0,\,1]
is defined in (
2.9
). Evidently,
C
is an
(1,\epsilon\nu)
-small set, with
\nu(\cdot)=\mbox{Leb}(\cdot\,\cap C)/\mbox{Leb}(C)
. Furthermore,
C
is accessible since for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

P_{\eta}(x,C)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\eta}\,\sigma}\int_{C}\phi\left(\frac{y-x-\eta g(x)}{\sqrt{\eta}\sigma}\right)dy>0.

(2)

We have proved in (1) that any compact subset
C
of
\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\mbox{Leb}(C)>0
is an accessible
(1,\mu)
-small set with
\mu(C)=\epsilon\nu(C)>0
. Hence,
P_{\eta}
is irreducible and strongly aperiodic.

(3)

Assume Hypothesis
(H2)
. Then we have that for a given non-empty set
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
, both
\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}(x+\eta g(x))
and
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}(x+\eta g(x))
exist in
\mathbb{R}
; for a given

,

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}p_{\eta}(x,y)\geqslant\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\eta}\sigma}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2\eta\sigma^{2}}\left[\left(y-\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}(x+\eta g(x))\right)^{2}\vee\left(y-\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}(x+\eta g(x))\right)^{2}\right]\right\}>0.

Let
f(y)
denote the function in the middle of the two inequalities above. Then for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
,

P_{\eta}(x,\,A)=\int_{A}p_{\eta}(x,y)dy\geqslant\mu(A),

where

is a Borel measure defined on
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
. Therefore, the state space
\mathbb{R}
is an
(1,\mu)
-small set.
\square

If
C
is a subset of
\mathbb{R}
, let

and
\sigma_{C}
denote respectively the first hitting time and the first return time of

For a given

,

and

) and (

Example 2.1
(AR(1) process)

For the existence and uniqueness of invariant probability measure in Theorem
2.1
, one can take a simple example as follows. Let
g(x):=\left(1-\frac{1}{\eta}\right)x
, for
x\in\mathbb{R}
; and
\sigma=1
. We can see that Hypothesis
(H1)
is satisfied in this case. The model
(\ref{recursive})
becomes

\theta_{k+1}=\eta\theta_{k}+\sqrt{\eta}\varepsilon_{k+1},\quad k\geqslant 0,

(2.17)

which is an

model. Iterating (

), we have

\theta_{k}=\eta^{k}\theta_{0}+A_{k},\quad k\geqslant 1,

where
A_{k}:=\sqrt{\eta}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\eta^{i}\varepsilon_{k-i}
. Let
\varepsilon_{0}
be a standard normal random variable independent with

. Consider the random variable
B_{k}:=\sqrt{\eta}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\eta^{i}\varepsilon_{i}
, for any

. Since

, using martingale convergence theorem, we can obtain

. Let

be the probability distribution of

. Then we have that
\pi_{\eta}
is the unique invariant probability measure for
P_{\eta}
.

3
Geometric Rate of Convergence and Uniform Geometric Ergodicity of the Kernel
P_{\eta}

Let
P
be a Markov kernel on
X\times\mathcal{X}
. The Markov kernel P is said to satisfy the geometric drift condition (see also Definition 14.1.5,
[
8
]

D_{g}(V,\lambda,b)
, if

is a measurable function,
\lambda\in[0,1)
,
b\in[0,\infty)
and

PV(x)\leqslant\lambda V(x)+b\mathbbm{1}_{X}(x).

(3.18)

We obtain the following geometric rate of convergence for the Markov kernel
P_{\eta}
to its invariant probability measure
\pi_{\eta}
, for any initial state
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 3.1

Under Hypotheses
(H1)
and
(H2)
, for any
\eta\in(0,1)
, there exist
\delta\in(1,\infty]
and
\tau<\infty
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left\|P_{\eta}^{n}(x,\cdot)-\pi_{\eta}\right\|_{TV}\leqslant\tau\delta^{-n}

(3.19)

and

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}d_{TV}\left(P_{\eta}^{n}(x,\cdot),\pi_{\eta}\right)\leqslant\left(\tau/2\right)\delta^{-n}.

(3.20)

To prove the theorem above, we will need the following proposition, which gives equivalent conditions for uniform geometric ergodicity of
P_{\eta}
.

Proposition 3.1

For any
\eta\in(0,1)
, the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

P_{\eta}
is uniformly geometrically ergodic, i.e.,
P_{\eta}
admits an invariant probability measure
\pi_{\eta}
such that there exist
\delta>1
and
\tau<\infty
satisfying for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left\|P_{\eta}^{n}(x,\cdot)-\pi_{\eta}\right\|_{TV}\leqslant\tau\delta^{-n}.

(3.21)

(2)

P_{\eta}
is positive, aperiodic, and there exist a small set
C
and a constant
\delta>1
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\delta^{\sigma_{C}}\right)<\infty.

(3)

The state space
\mathbb{R}
is small.

Proof 4

(1)
\Longrightarrow
(2). Suppose that
P_{\eta}
is uniformly geometrically ergodic. We prove firstly that
P_{\eta}
is irreducible; then as
P_{\eta}
has an invariant probability measure
\pi_{\eta}
,
P_{\eta}
is thus positive. Moreover, by (
3.21
), there exist
\beta>1
and
M<\infty
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

|P_{\eta}^{n}(x,\,A)-\pi_{\eta}(A)|\leqslant\|P^{n}_{\eta}(x,\cdot)-\pi_{\eta}\|_{TV}\leqslant M\beta^{-n}.

(3.22)

Therefore, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

P^{n}_{\eta}(x,A)\geqslant\pi_{\eta}(A)-M\beta^{-n}.

(3.23)

If
\pi_{\eta}(A)>0
, we can choose
n
to be large enough such that
P^{n}_{\eta}(x,\,A)>0
, which implies that
P_{\eta}
is irreducible.

Let
C
be an accessible small set. For
d>0
, define set
B_{d}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,1(x)\leqslant d\}
. Since
\pi_{\eta}
is an invariant probability measure, we get
\pi_{\eta}(C)>0
and for all
x\in B_{d}
we can choose
n
to be large enough such that

P_{\eta}^{n}(x,\,C)\geqslant\pi_{\eta}(C)-M\beta^{-n}\geqslant\pi_{\eta}(C)/2.

Therefore, for all
d>0
,
B_{d}
is also a small set by Lemma
A.1
.

Since
\mathbb{R}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,1(x)<\infty\}
and
\pi_{\eta}(\mathbb{R})=1
, we may choose
d_{0}
to be large enough so that
\pi_{\eta}(B_{d})>0
for all
d\geqslant d_{0}
. Since
\pi_{\eta}
is an invariant probability measure of
P_{\eta}
, the set
B_{d}
is accessible, for any
d\geqslant d_{0}
. Applying (
3.23
) for
A=B_{d}
, we may find
n
to be large enough such that for any
m>n
,

\inf_{x\in B_{d}}P_{\eta}^{m}(x,B_{d})\geqslant\pi_{\eta}(B_{d})/2>0.

This implies that the period of
P_{\eta}
is
1
and
P_{\eta}
is thus aperiodic.

On the one hand, letting
A=\mathbb{R}
in (
3.22
), one gets for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

P_{\eta}^{k}\,1(x)\leqslant M\beta^{-k}+1.

(3.24)

We can thus choose
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
to be large enough such that

M\beta^{-m}\leqslant\lambda<1.

As a result,
P^{m}_{\eta}
satisfies the geometric drift condition
D_{g}(1,\lambda,1)
, i.e.,
P^{m}_{\eta}\leqslant\lambda+1
. Now, set

V_{0}(x):=1+\lambda^{-1/m}P_{\eta}1(x)+\cdots+\lambda^{-(m-1)/m}P_{\eta}^{m-1}1(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
b:=\lambda^{-(m-1)/m}
. Then we have

\displaystyle P_{\eta}V_{0}(x)=

\displaystyle P_{\eta}1(x)+\lambda^{-1/m}P_{\eta}^{2}1(x)+\cdots+\lambda^{-(m-1)/m}P_{\eta}^{m}1(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle P_{\eta}1(x)+\lambda^{-1/m}P_{\eta}^{2}1(x)+\cdots+\lambda^{-(m-2)/m}P_{\eta}^{m-1}1(x)+\lambda^{-(m-1)/m}(\lambda+1)

(3.25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{1/m}(\lambda^{-1/m}P_{\eta}1(x)+\lambda^{-2/m}P_{\eta}^{2}1(x)+\cdots+\lambda^{-(m-1)/m}P_{\eta}^{m-1}1(x)+1)+b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{1/m}V_{0}(x)+b.

On the other hand, by (
3.24
), we have for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle 1<V_{0}(x)\leqslant\left(M\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\lambda^{-k/m}\beta^{-k}\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\lambda^{-k/m}\leqslant(M+1)\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\lambda^{-k/m}=(M+1)\frac{\lambda^{-1}-1}{\lambda^{-1/m}-1}.

P_{\eta}
thus satisfies the geometric drift condition
D_{g}(V_{0},\lambda^{1/m},\lambda^{-(m-1)/m})
, for some
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
\lambda\in(0,1)
and
V_{0}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[1,\infty)
a measurable and bounded function on
\mathbb{R}
.

Let
f(x):=(\tilde{\lambda}-\lambda^{1/m})V_{0}(x)
, for
\tilde{\lambda}\in(\lambda^{1/m},\,1)
. From (
3.25
), for any
\tilde{\lambda}\in(\lambda^{1/m},\,1)
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

P_{\eta}V_{0}(x)+f(x)\leqslant\tilde{\lambda}V_{0}(x)+b.

(3.26)

Moreover, from the above, we have for all
d>0
, the set
B_{d}
is small; and for all
d\geqslant d_{0}
, the set
B_{d}
becomes accessible. Therefore, these are also true for the set
\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,V_{0}(x)\leqslant d\}
. Choose
\lambda_{1}\in(\tilde{\lambda},\,1)
. Consider a set
C:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,V_{0}(x)\leqslant d_{1}\}
, where
d_{1}\geqslant d_{0}\vee b\,(\lambda_{1}-\tilde{\lambda})^{-1}
; and hence
C
is accessible and small.
For
x\in C
, (
3.26
) yields
P_{\eta}V_{0}(x)+f(x)<\lambda_{1}V_{0}(x)+b
. While, for
x\in C^{c}
,
-(\lambda_{1}-\tilde{\lambda})V_{0}(x)<-b
and (
3.26
) imply

P_{\eta}V_{0}(x)+f(x)<\lambda_{1}V_{0}(x)+b-(\lambda_{1}-\tilde{\lambda})V_{0}(x)<\lambda_{1}V_{0}(x).

Equivalently, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

P_{\eta}V_{0}(x)+f(x)\leqslant\lambda_{1}V_{0}(x)+b\mathbbm{1}_{C}.

(3.27)

From the last inequality, with measurable functions
V_{0}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[1,\infty]
and
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[1,\infty)
, some given
\delta=\lambda_{1}^{-1}>1
and set
C
, we get for any
x\in C^{c}
,

P_{\eta}V_{0}(x)+f(x)\leqslant\delta^{-1}V_{0}(x).

According to (14.1.4) in Proposition 14.1.2
[
8
]
, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\lambda_{1}^{-1}(\tilde{\lambda}-\lambda^{1/m})\,\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\sigma_{C}-1}\tilde{\lambda}^{-k}V_{0}(X_{k})\right)\leqslant\lambda_{1}^{-1}\left[P_{\eta}V_{0}(x)+f(x)\right]\mathbbm{1}_{C}(x)+V_{0}(x)\mathbbm{1}_{C^{c}}(x),

where
0\times\infty=0
by convention on the right-hand side of the inequality. Using (
3.27
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{-1}(\tilde{\lambda}-\lambda^{1/m})\,\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\sigma_{C}-1}\delta^{k}V_{0}(X_{k})\right)\leqslant

\displaystyle(\sup_{C}V_{0}+b\lambda_{1}^{-1})\mathbbm{1}_{C}(x)+V_{0}(x)\mathbbm{1}_{C^{c}}(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(d_{1}+b\lambda_{1}^{-1}+1\right)V_{0}(x)=(d_{1}+b\delta+1)V_{0}(x).

Therefore, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\sigma_{C}-1}\delta^{k}V_{0}(X_{k})\right)\leqslant\tau V_{0}(x),

(3.28)

where
\tau:=[(d_{1}+1)\delta^{-1}+b](\tilde{\lambda}-\lambda^{1/m})^{-1}<\infty
. Moreover, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\sigma_{C}-1}\delta^{k}V_{0}(X_{k})\right)=V_{0}(x)+\delta^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\sum_{i=2}^{\sigma_{C}}\delta^{i}V_{0}(X_{i-1})\right)\geqslant 1+\delta^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\delta^{\sigma_{C}}\right).

(3.29)

Combining (
3.28
) and (
3.29
), we have that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists
\delta\in(1,\infty)
such that

\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\delta^{\sigma_{C}}\right)\leqslant\delta\left[\tau V_{0}(x)-1\right]<\infty.

(2)
\Longrightarrow
(3). For any
d>0
, consider the set
D_{d}
defined by

D_{d}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\delta^{\tau_{C}}\right)<d\}.

We will firstly prove that
D_{d}
is small, for any
d>0
. Note that

D_{d}=(D_{d}\cap C)\cup\left(D_{d}\cap C^{c}\right),

and the union of two small sets remains small for irreducible and aperiodic kernel
P_{\eta}
. So it suffices to prove that both
D_{d}\cap C
and
D_{d}\cap C^{c}
are small. Since
C
is small and
(D_{d}\cap C)\subseteq C
, it is evident that
D_{d}\cap C
is also small. Suppose

. By Markov’s inequality, for all

,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(\sigma_{C}\geqslant k+1)=\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{C}\geqslant k+1)=\mathbb{P}_{x}(\delta^{\tau_{C}}\geqslant\delta^{k+1})\leqslant\delta^{-(k+1)}\,\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\delta^{\tau_{C}}\right)\leqslant d\delta^{-(k+1)}.

Thus, for
k
that is sufficiently large,

\inf_{x\in D_{d}\cap C^{c}}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\sigma_{C}\leqslant k)\geqslant\frac{1}{2}>0.

Therefore, the set
C
is uniformly accessible from
D_{d}\cap C^{c}
. Since
P_{\eta}
is irreducible, according to Lemma
A.4
(1), we have that

is small.

Since

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\sigma^{\tau_{C}}\right)\leqslant\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\sigma^{\sigma_{C}}\right)<\infty,

there exists
b>0
such that
\mathbb{R}\subseteq D_{b}
, which is small from above. Therefore, the state space
\mathbb{R}
is small.

(3)
\Longrightarrow
(1). Suppose that the state space
\mathbb{R}
is small set. We will firstly show that
P_{\eta}
is irreducible, recurrent and positive. Since
\mathbb{R}
is small, there exists

and nonzero measure
\mu
on

such that for all

and

,

\displaystyle P_{\eta}^{m}(x,A)\geqslant\mu_{\eta}(A),

which implies for all
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\mu_{\eta}(A)>0
, one has
P_{\eta}^{m}(x,A)>0
. Consequently,
P_{\eta}
is irreducible. Since
\mathbb{R}
is an accessible small set,
\sigma_{\mathbb{R}}=1

\mathbb{P}_{x}
-a.s., for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Applying Lemma
A.4
(2), we have that
P_{\eta}
is recurrent. According to Theorem 11.2.5 in
[
8
]
,
P_{\eta}
admits an invariant probability measure
\pi_{\eta}
satisfying
\pi_{\eta}(C)<\infty
for every accessible set
C
; since
\mathbb{R}
is accessible, this implies
\pi_{\eta}(\mathbb{R})<\infty
, showing that

is positive.

Next, we will prove that

is aperiodic by contradiction. Suppose that

is an irreducible Markov kernel with period

. According to Lemma
A.3
, there exist
C_{0}
,

,

,

of mutually disjoint accessible sets such that for

, and for any

P_{\eta}\left(x,C_{i+1(\text{mod }d)}\right)=1
and hence

\displaystyle P_{\eta}(x,C_{(i+1)+1(\text{mod }d)})=0,

(3.30)

where additions are in the modulo sense, and
\bigcup_{i=0}^{d-1}C_{i}
is absorbing. Therefore, there exists
i_{0}\in\{0,\cdots,d-1\}
such that

P_{\eta}^{m}(x,C_{i_{0}})\geqslant\mu_{\eta}(C_{i_{0}})>0,

which contradicts with (
3.30
).

Finally, we can obtain (
3.21
) by applying Proposition
3.2
below, with

.

4
Splitting Construction and Split Markov Kernel
\check{P}_{\eta}

Let
\check{X}:=\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}
and
(\check{X},\check{\mathfrak{X}})
be a measurable space. In this section, based on the kernel
P_{\eta}
, we will construct a new Markov kernel
\check{P}_{\eta}
on the extended state space
(\check{X},\check{\mathfrak{X}})
. The detailed method of splitting construction for more general case can be found in Section 11.1 of
[
8
]
.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
P_{\eta}
admits an
(1,2\varepsilon\nu)
-small set
C
with
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\nu(C)=1
. Let
b_{\varepsilon}
be the Bernoulli distribution with success probability
\varepsilon
, given by

b_{\varepsilon}:=(1-\varepsilon)\delta_{\{0\}}+\varepsilon\delta_{\{1\}}.

For any bounded and measurable function
f
on
(\check{X},\check{\mathfrak{X}})
, define a function
\bar{f}_{\varepsilon}
on
\mathbb{R}
by

\bar{f}_{\varepsilon}(x):=[\delta_{x}\otimes b_{\varepsilon}]f=(1-\varepsilon)f(x,0)+\varepsilon f(x,1).

From the definition of
\bar{f}_{\varepsilon}
above, we have for any measure
\xi
on
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))
,

\xi(\bar{f}_{\varepsilon})=[\xi\otimes b_{\varepsilon}](f).

For
\eta\in(0,1)
, consider the residual kernel
R_{\eta}
defined for
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
by

R_{\eta}(x,A):=\begin{cases}\frac{P_{\eta}(x,A)-\varepsilon\nu(A)}{1-\varepsilon},&\quad\text{if }x\in C;\\
P_{\eta}(x,A),&\quad\text{if }x\notin C.\end{cases}

Now, for
\eta\in(0,1)
, define the split Markov kernel
\check{P}_{\eta}
on
(\check{X},\check{\mathfrak{X}})
as follows. For
(x,d)\in\check{X}
and
\check{A}\in\check{\mathfrak{X}}
, set

\check{P}_{\eta}(x,d;\check{A}):=Q_{\eta}(x,d;\cdot)\otimes b_{\varepsilon}(\check{A}),

where
Q_{\eta}
is the Markov kernel on
\check{X}\times\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
defined for all
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
by

Q_{\eta}(x,d;B):=\mathbbm{1}_{C}(x)\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{0\}}(d)R_{\eta}(x,B)+\mathbbm{1}_{\{1\}}(d)\nu(B)\right]+\mathbbm{1}_{C^{c}}(x)P_{\eta}(x,B).

Equivalently, for all bounded and measurable function
g
on
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))
, one has

\displaystyle Q_{\eta}g(x,0)=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{C}(x)R_{\eta}g(x)+\mathbbm{1}_{C^{c}}(x)P_{\eta}g(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(y)p_{\eta}(x,y)dy-\varepsilon\nu(g)}{1-\varepsilon},&\text{if }x\in~C\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(y)p_{\eta}(x,y)dy,&\text{if }x\notin C\end{cases};

\displaystyle Q_{\eta}g(x,1)=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{C}(x)\nu(g)+\mathbbm{1}_{C^{c}}(x)P_{\eta}g(x)=\begin{cases}\nu(g),&\text{if }x\in~C\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(y)p_{\eta}(x,y)dy,&\text{if }x\notin C\end{cases},

where
p_{\eta}(x,y)=(2\pi\eta\sigma^{2})^{-1/2}\exp{\left(-\frac{(y-x-\eta g(x))^{2}}{2\eta\sigma^{2}}\right)}
is the kernel density of
P_{\eta}
. It follows that for any bounded and measurable function
f
on
(\check{X},\check{\mathfrak{X}})
,

\check{P}_{\eta}f(x,d)=Q_{\eta}\bar{f}_{\varepsilon}(x,d).

Let us describe now the canonical chain associated with the kernel
\check{P}_{\eta}
on
\check{X}\times\check{\mathfrak{X}}
. For probability measure
\check{\mu}
on
\check{\mathfrak{X}}
, denote by
\check{\mathbb{P}}_{\check{\mu}}
the probability measure on the canonical space
\left(\check{X}^{\mathbb{N}},{\check{\mathfrak{X}}}^{\otimes\mathbb{N}}\right)
such that the coordinate process, denoted by
\{(\theta_{k},D_{k})\}_{k\geqslant 1}
and called split chain, is a Markov chain with initial distribution
\check{\mu}
and Markov kernel
\check{P}_{\eta}
.

From the splitting construction above,
(D_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is a sequence of i.i.d Bernoulli random variables with success probability
\varepsilon
that is independent of
(\theta_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
. Denote by
\left(\mathfrak{F}_{k}^{\theta}\right)_{k\geqslant 1}
the natural filtration of the process
(\theta_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
. An important property of the split chain
\{(\theta_{k},D_{k})\}_{k\geqslant 1}
is that if
\theta_{0}
and
D_{0}
are independent, then
\{(\theta_{k},\mathfrak{F}_{k}^{\theta})\}_{k\geqslant 1}
is a Markov chain with kernel
P_{\eta}
.

\displaystyle\leqslant

On the other hand, for any

5
Conclusions

This work investigates the convergence rate of EM scheme
(\theta_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
to the invariant measure under total variation distance for solution approximations of SDE given by (
1.1
). To this end, the ergodic properties of the EM scheme, as a non-atomic Markov chain, are studied. It turns out that under Hypothesis
(H1)
,
(\theta_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
is irreducible, strongly aperiodic (see also Proposition
2.1
), and admits a unique invariant probability measure
\pi_{\eta}
(see also Theorem
2.1
). If in addition Hypothesis
(H2)
is satisfied,
(\theta_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
is uniformly geometrically ergodic, and converges to
\pi_{\eta}
under TV distance at least in geometric rate, which is independent of the step size
\eta
(see also Theorem
3.1
). To show the uniform geometric ergodicity of the chain, we studied its equivalent conditions (see also Proposition
3.1
) with the approach of splitting construction.

Let us describe some connections and special highlights of the present work with classical ergodic theory for Markov chains on general state space. In the book of Meyn and Tweedie (
[
16
]
), the theory on geometric ergodicity for
\psi
-irreducible Markov chain, with Markov kernel
P
and state space
X
, is introduced in Chapter 15. Our geometric ergodicity results in Theorem
2.1
are similar to those under
f
-norm in Theorem 15.4.1 of
[
16
]
, when
f=\mathbbm{1}_{X}
. Their results hold under the following “Minorization Condition” of the chain: for some
\delta>0
, some
C\in\mathcal{B}(X)
and some probability measure
\nu
with
\nu(C^{c})=0
and
\nu(C)=1
,

P(x,A)\geqslant\delta\mathbbm{1}_{C}(x)\nu(A),\quad A\in\mathcal{B}(X),\;x\in X.

While, our geometric ergodicity results in Theorem
2.1
, obtained by using Theorem 11.4.2 in
[
8
]
, only require the condition of existence of an
(1,\varepsilon\nu)
-small set
D
, with
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\nu(D)=1
, which is ensured in our case because it is shown in Proposition 3.1 (3) that the state space is small. In this article, we applied a new splitting construction approach introduced by Dedecker and Gouëzel
[
6
]
, which is different from the two classical splitting approaches in
[
16
]
: Nummelin splitting technique, due to
[
17
]
, and random renewal time approach, due to
[
1
]
. Thanks to this new splitting construction, it makes our proof of the equivalent conditions for the uniform geometric ergodicity of
P_{\eta}
in Proposition
3.1
differ from the proof of the existing similar results in Theorems 16.0.2 and 16.2.2 of
[
16
]
. Moreover, due to this new approach, we have managed to establish an important link between
1
-geometrically recurrent small set as described in (
3.31
) and the convergence of the series involving TV norm in (
3.32
) for the Markov kernel
P_{\eta}
.

With the new concepts related to non-atomic Markov chains, our results provide further properties for an EM scheme with constant step size of SDEs. In the future, EM scheme with non-constant step sizes and algorithm with high performance such as convergence rate in a controlled manner, can be considered for applications. Also, the convergence behavior of the EM scheme in higher-dimensional spaces under different distances can be considered as a future research direction.

Appendix A
Some Properties Related to Small Sets and Irreducible Markov Kernels

In this section, we will introduce some basic properties related to Markov kernels that admit accessible small sets. Readers may refer to Chapter 9 in the book of Douc et al.
[
8
]
for the details.

Suppose that
(X_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is a Markov chain living on the state space
X
, with Markov kernel
P
on a measurable space
(X,\mathfrak{X})
, where
\mathfrak{X}
is a
\sigma
-algebra generated by
X
. In the sequel, let
\mathbb{P}_{x}
denote the probability measure on the canonical space
\left(X^{\mathbb{N}},\mathfrak{X}^{\otimes\mathbb{N}}\right)
of the chain
(X_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
, with the initial state
X_{0}=x
. And
\mathbb{E}_{x}
denotes the expectation under the probability measure
\mathbb{P}_{x}
. If
B\in\mathfrak{X}
, let
N_{B}
denote the number of visits of
(X_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
to the set
B
, defined as
N_{B}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{B}(X_{k})
. Define respectively the first return time
\sigma_{B}
of the set
B
and the expected number
U(x,B)
of visits to
B
starting from
x
by

\sigma_{B}=\inf\{n\geqslant 1\,|\,X_{n}\in B\}

and

U(x,B)=\mathbb{E}_{x}[N_{B}]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P^{k}(x,B).

Definition A.1
(Accessible set and uniform accessibility,
[
8
]
)

(1)

A set
A\in\mathfrak{X}
is said to be accessible if for all
x\in X
, there exists an integer
n\geqslant 1
such that
P^{n}(x,A)>0
.

(2)

A set
B
is uniformly accessible from
A
, if there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that

\inf_{x\in A}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\sigma_{B}\leqslant m)>0.

Definition A.2
(Full set,
[
8
]
)

A set
F\in\mathfrak{X}
is said to be full if
F^{c}
is not accessible.

Definition A.3
(Atom and small set,
[
8
]
)

(1)

A set
\alpha\in\mathfrak{X}
is called an atom, if there exists a probability measure
\nu
on on
(X,\mathfrak{X})
such that for all
x\in\alpha
and
A\in\mathfrak{X}
,

P(x,A)=\nu(A).

(2)

A set
C\in\mathfrak{X}
is called a small set if there exists positive integer
m
and a nonzero measure
\mu
on
(X,\mathfrak{X})
such that for all
x\in C
and
A\in\mathfrak{X}
,

P^{m}(x,A)\geqslant\mu(A).

(A.44)

Then the set
C
is said to be an
(m,\mu)
-small set.

Remark A.1

(1)

From the definition above, it can be seen that atom is a particular small set satisfying the equality in (
A.44
) with
m=1
and
\mu(X)=1
, where
\mu
becomes a probability measure.

(2)

The condition (
A.44
) implies that
\mu
is a finite measure satisfying
0<\mu(X)\leqslant 1
. Hence it can be written
\mu=\epsilon\nu
with
\epsilon=\mu(X)
, and
\nu(\cdot)=\mu(\cdot)/\mu(X)
is a probability measure on
(X,\mathfrak{X})
. If
\epsilon=1
, then the equality in (
A.44
) must hold, and thus
A
is an atom.

Definition A.4
(Irreducible kernel,
[
8
]
)

A Markov kernel
P
is said to be irreducible if it admits an accessible small set.

Definition A.5
(Recurrent and Harris recurrent,
[
8
]
)

(1)

A set
A\in\mathfrak{X}
is said to be recurrent if
U(x,A)=\infty
for all
x\in A
; it is said to be Harris recurrent if
\mathbb{P}_{x}(N_{A}=\infty)=1
, for all
x\in A
.

(2)

The kernel
P
is said to be recurrent if every accessible set is recurrent; it is said to be Harris recurrent if every accessible set is Harris recurrent.

Definition A.6
(Strongly aperiodic small set,
[
8
]
)

An
(m,\mu)
-small set
C
is said to be strongly aperiodic, if
m=1
and
\mu(C)>0
.

Definition A.7
(Period, aperiodicity and strong aperiodicity
[
8
]
)

(1)

The common period of all accessible small sets is called the period of the kernel
P
.

(2)

If the period is equal to 1, the kernel
P
is said to be aperiodic.

(3)

If there exists an accessible
(1,\mu)
-small set
C
with
\mu(C)>0
, the kernel
P
is said to be strongly aperiodic.

Definition A.8
(Positive and null recurrent atom, positive and null Markov kernel,
[
8
]
)

(1)

An atom
\alpha
is said to be positive, if
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}(\sigma_{\alpha})<\infty
; it is said to be null recurrent, if it is recurrent and
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}(\sigma_{\alpha})=\infty
.

(2)

If
P
is irreducible and admits an invariant probability measure
\pi
, the Markov kernel
P
is called positive. If
P
does not admit such a measure, then

P
is called null.

Definition A.9
(Uniformly geometrically ergodic,
[
8
]
)

A Markov kernel
P
on
X\times\mathfrak{X}
is said to be uniformly geometrically ergodic, if it admits an invariant probability measure
\pi
such that there exist
\delta>1
and
\tau<\infty
satisfying for any

and any

,

\|P^{n}(x,\cdot)-\pi\|_{TV}\leqslant\tau\delta^{-n}.

Definition A.10
(Invariant measure,
[
8
]
)

A nonzero measure
\mu
is said to be invariant if it is
\sigma
-finite and
\mu P=\mu
.

The lemmas below are useful to prove Proposition
3.1
.

Lemma A.1
(Lemma 9.1.7 (ii),
[

]

Let

-small set and

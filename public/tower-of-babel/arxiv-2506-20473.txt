Buchsbaumness, Macaulayfication and Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of monomial curves
Biplab Dawn and Kumari Saloni

Abstract
. Projective monomial curves are associated with rings generated by monomials of equal degree in two variables. In this paper, we give an infinite class of non-smooth, non Cohen-Macaulay k k -Buchsbaum projective monomial curves for any k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 and find the monomial generators for the respective Macaulayfication. More generally, we demonstrate a method to find the Macaulayfication of a k k -Buchsbaum monomial curve for any k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 . We also discuss Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of certain curves in terms of k k -Buchsbaumness.

and it has at most one singular point if either
a_{1}=1
or
a_{n}=d-1
. There are very few known non-Cohen Macaulay rings

whose corresponding monomial curves are non-smooth
[
6

9
,
10

11
]
. Another problem is to find out the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity

[
12

we know that

16

We call a ring

This paper is organized into six sections. In Section

(i)

(ii)

(0:\mathfrak{M}^{n})=\tilde{R}/R
for some

. Now consider the short exact sequence

where

. Applying

Since

and the map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(R)}

\displaystyle 0\to H^{0}_{\mathfrak{M}}\left(C(a)/(a)\right)\to H^{1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(a)\to H^{1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(C(a))\to 0

5.
Regularity of monomial curves

In this section, we discuss the reduction number and the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of a projective monomial curve. Let
R=\mathcal{K}[M]
be a projective monomial curve and
M
is a set of monomials of degree
d
along with
s^{d},t^{d}\in M
. The reduction number of
R
with respect to the ideal
Q=(s^{d},t^{d})
, denoted by
r_{Q}(R)
, is defined as the smallest integer
n
such that
R_{n+1}=Q_{n+1}
. Let
\widetilde{R}
be the Cohen-Macaulayfication of
R
, given by
\widetilde{R}=\mathcal{K}[\widetilde{M}]
, where
\widetilde{M}=M\cup N
and
N
is the set of monomials having the same degree, say
ld
, for some positive integer
l
. Define
R^{\prime}=\mathcal{K}[N]
. We have that
R
has standard grading with the monomials in
M
having degree one and
\deg s^{\alpha}t^{\beta}=(\alpha+\beta)/d

for all
s^{\alpha}t^{\beta}\in R
and
R^{\prime}
has standard grading with the monomials in
N
having degree one and
\deg s^{\alpha}t^{\beta}=(\alpha+\beta)/ld
for all
s^{\alpha}t^{\beta}\in R^{\prime}.

We write
R=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}R_{i}
and
R^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}R_{i}^{\prime}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose
R_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq R_{2l}
or
R_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq R_{l}R_{1}^{\prime}
. Then
R
is strictly
k
-Buchsbaum if and only if
a(\widetilde{R}/R):=\max\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\mid(\widetilde{R}/R)_{n}\neq 0\}=k+l-1.

Proof.

We have that
\widetilde{R}
is a graded
R
-module with grading
\widetilde{R}=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\widetilde{R}_{i}
, where

\widetilde{R}_{i}=\begin{cases}R_{i}&\text{for all }i<l,\\
R_{l}\oplus R_{1}^{\prime}&\text{for }i=l,\\
R_{l+1}\oplus R_{1}R_{1}^{\prime}&\text{for }i=l+1,\\
\vdots&\\
R_{2l}\oplus R_{l}R_{1}^{\prime}\oplus R_{2}^{\prime}&\text{for }i=2l,\\
\vdots\end{cases}

As
R_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq R_{2l}
or
R_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq R_{l}R_{1}^{\prime}
, we get that for all
i\geq 0,\penalty\ \widetilde{R}_{l+i}=R_{l+i}\oplus R_{i}R_{1}^{\prime}
.
Since
R
is strictly
k
-Buchsbaum and
H^{1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(R)\cong\widetilde{R}/R
by Theorem
4.1
, we have that
\mathfrak{M}^{k}H^{1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(R)=0\quad\text{but}\quad\mathfrak{M}^{k-1}H^{1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(R)\neq 0.
Equivalently
\mathfrak{M}^{k}\widetilde{R}\subseteq R\quad\text{but}\quad\mathfrak{M}^{k-1}\widetilde{R}\nsubseteq R

which implies
\widetilde{R}_{k+l}\subseteq R_{k+l}\quad\text{but}\quad\widetilde{R}_{k+l-1}\nsubseteq R_{k+l-1}.

Therefore, the largest integer
n
such that
(\widetilde{R}/R)_{n}\neq 0
is
k+l-1
, i.e.,
a(\widetilde{R}/R)=k+l-1
.
∎

Let
r_{Q}(\widetilde{R})
denote the least integer
n
such that
\widetilde{R}_{n+1}=(Q\cdot\widetilde{R})_{n+1}.

Corollary 5.1
.

Suppose
R
is strictly
k
-Buchsbaum and
R_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq R_{2l}
or
R_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq R_{l}R_{1}^{\prime}
.
Then
\operatorname{reg}(R)=\max\{k+l,\,r_{Q}(\widetilde{R})\}.

Proof.

The result follows directly from
[
6
, Proposition 1.8]
and Lemma
5.1
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Suppose
R
is a smooth monomial curve. Then
R
is strictly
k
-Buchsbaum if and only if
\operatorname{reg}(R)=k+1
.

Proof.

For a smooth monomial curve
R
,
\widetilde{R}
is the Veronese subring of
\mathcal{K}[s,t]
generated by all monomials of degree
d
, so we have
l=1
. It is also straightforward to verify that
R_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq R_{1}R_{1}^{\prime}
or
R_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq R_{2}
. From
[
6
, Example 1.9]
, we know that
r_{Q}(\widetilde{R})=1
. Hence, by Corollary
5.1
, we obtain the desired result.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{K}[\mathcal{M}]
be as in Section
3
. Then
\operatorname{reg}(\mathcal{R})=\max\{k+2,\,r_{Q}(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}})\}.

Proof.

By Remark
4.1
, we have
l=2
and
s^{2(2a_{n}-3b_{1})}t^{2(3b_{1})}\in\mathcal{R}
, implies
\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{2l}
. Applying Lemma
5.1
and Corollary
5.1
, we obtain the result.
∎

In the next theorem, we obtain a numerical criteria for
k
-Buchsbaumness. Our result is a generalization of
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
in which case,
l=k=1
.

Theorem 5.1
.

The ring
R
is strictly
k
-Buchsbaum if and only if
kG_{R}+G_{\widetilde{R}}=(k+l)G_{R}
,
where

G_{R}=\{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mid s^{d-a}t^{a}\in M\},\quad G_{\widetilde{R}}=lG_{R}\cup\{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mid s^{ld-a}t^{a}\in N\}

and
lG_{R}
denotes the set of all sums of
l
elements from
G_{R}
.

Proof.

Clearly
(k+l)G_{R}\subseteq kG_{R}+G_{\widetilde{R}}
. We know that
R_{k}\cdot\widetilde{R}_{l}\subseteq\widetilde{R}_{k+l}
. By Lemma
5.1
,
R
is strictly
k
-Buchsbaum if and only if
\widetilde{R}_{k+l}\subseteq R_{k+l}
. Define the sets
A=\{s^{kd-a}t^{a}\mid a\in kG_{R}\},\penalty\ B=\{s^{ld-a}t^{a}\mid a\in G_{\widetilde{R}}\},\penalty\ C=\{s^{(k+l)d-a}t^{a}\mid a\in(k+l)G_{R}\}
.
Then
R_{k}
,
\widetilde{R}_{l}
and
R_{k+l}
are
\mathcal{K}
-linear spans of the monomials in
A
,
B
and
C
, respectively. Thus,
R
is strictly
k
-Buchsbaum if and only if the product set
A\cdot B\subseteq C
, which is equivalent to
kG_{R}+G_{\widetilde{R}}\subseteq(k+l)G_{R}
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{K}[\mathcal{M}]

\operatorname{reg}(\mathcal{R})=r_{Q}(\mathcal{R})

If

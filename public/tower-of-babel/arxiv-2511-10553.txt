Abstract
. Let ( M , g ) (M,g) be a compact Riemannian manifold with boundary. The Escobar problem concerning the existence of a metric conformally equivalent to g g having constant scalar curvature on M M and constant mean curvature on its boundary is equivalent, in analytic terms, to finding a positive solution to a nonlinear boundary-value problem with critical growth. While the existence of positive solutions to this problem is by now well understood, the existence of sign-changing (nodal) solutions remains largely open. In this work we establish the existence of least-energy sign-changing solutions in two particular cases: the scalar-flat problem, where the scalar curvature on M M is zero and the mean curvature of its boundary is constant, and the minimal boundary problem, where the mean curvature of the boundary vanishes and the scalar curvature of M M is constant. More precisely, we prove that if n ≥ 7 n\geq 7 and M M has a nonumbilic boundary point, then both problems admit least-energy nodal solutions. In addition, we show that when n ≥ 5 n\geq 5 , the minimal boundary problem possesses infinitely many sign-changing solutions on the unit ball 𝔹 n . \mathbb{B}^{n}. Our approach is variational and relies on the analysis of suitable conformal invariants and sharp energy estimates derived from Escobar’s work.

1.
Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
with boundary. The problem of the existence of a metric conformally equivalent to
g
having constant scalar curvature on
M
and constant mean curvature on its boundary leads to a nonlinear equation of critical type for the conformal factor. More precisely, if
R=R_{g}
and
h=h_{g}
denote respectively the scalar curvature of
M
and the mean curvature of its boundary associated with the metric
g
, the corresponding curvatures
\widetilde{R}=R_{\widetilde{g}}
and
\widetilde{h}=h_{\widetilde{g}}
for the conformal metric
\widetilde{g}:=u^{4\over n-2}g
are given by a positive solution
u
of the problem

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+c_{n}Ru=\widetilde{R}u^{n+2\over n-2}&\text{on \ }M,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+d_{n}hu=\widetilde{h}u^{\frac{n}{n-2}}&\text{on \ }\partial M,\end{cases}

where
\Delta=\Delta_{g}
is the Laplacian,
\nu=\nu_{g}
is the outer unit normal with respect to the metric
g
and
c_{n}:=\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}
,
d_{n}:=\frac{n-2}{2}
.

The problem (
1.1
) with
\widetilde{R}=0
can be can be seen as a higher-dimensional extension of the classical Riemann mapping theorem, which asserts that every simply connected proper subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is conformally diffeomorphic to the disk.

When
M
is closed (that is,
\partial M=\emptyset
) the problem (
1.1
) reduces to the well-known Yamabe problem, a higher-dimensional analogue of the uniformization theorem in complex analysis. The Yamabe problem, which seeks a metric conformal to
g
with constant scalar curvature, was solved through the combined efforts of Yamabe
[
37
]
, Trudinger
[
36
]
, Aubin
[
3
]
and Schoen
[
35
]
. They proved the existence of a positive solution
u
to the equation

(1.2)

-\Delta u+c_{n}Ru=|u|^{\frac{4}{n-2}}u\qquad\text{in \ }M,

for any closed Riemannian manifold
M
of dimension
n\geq 3
. Then,
\widetilde{g}:=u^{4/(n-2)}g
is a metric conformal to
g
with constant scalar curvature. A comprehensive analysis of this problem was later provided by Lee and Parker in
[
29
]
.

The natural extension of the Yamabe problem to manifolds with boundary was initiated by Cherrier
[
5
]
and Escobar
[
16
,
19
,
18
,
21
]
, with further contributions by Han and Li
[
23
,
24
]
. The existence of positive solutions to (
1.1
) is by now well understood; see
[
30
]
and the references therein for a detailed account.

In the present work, we are concerned with the existence of nodal (sign-changing) solutions to (
1.1
). While the study of such solutions for the Yamabe equation (
1.2
) is rather extensive, to the best of our knowledge, the existence of nodal solutions for (
1.1
) remains widely open.

For the Yamabe problem, when
M
is the standard sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, W.Y. Ding established the existence of infinitely many sign-changing solutions to the Yamabe equation (
1.2
) in
[
15
]
. Other types of nodal solutions on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
were exhibited in
[
13
,
14
,
11
,
6
,
31
]
. In
[
22
]
Fernández and Petean showed that there is a solution with precisely
\ell
nodal domains for every
\ell\geq 2
. For closed manifolds with suitable symmetries existence of nodal solutions to (
1.2
) has been established in
[
7
,
9
]
. Nodal solutions on products were obtained in
[
25
,
32
]
.
In contrast, there are few results concerning the existence of sign-changing solutions to the Yamabe problem (
1.2
) on an arbitrary closed Riemannian manifold. In
[
2
]
Ammann and Humbert introduced a conformal invariant, called the second Yamabe invariant, defined in terms of the second eigenvalue of the conformal Laplacian, and showed that on every closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 11
which is not locally conformally flat, there exists a function
u
that realizes this invariant. This function necessarily changes sign, so the expression
\widetilde{g}:=|u|^{4/(n-2)}g
is not a metric on
M
. In
[
2
]
it is called a
generalized metric
. This result was extended in
[
8
, Theorem 1.2
(v)
]
to manifolds of dimension
10
that satisfy an additional assumption. Every function that realizes the second Yamabe invariant has least energy among all sign-changing solutions. The blow-up behavior of sequences of least energy sign-changing solutions of to (
1.2
) was described by Premoselli and Vétois in
[
33
]
. They also showed in
[
34
]
that in dimensions
3
to
10
, the second Yamabe invariant is not attained for any metric on the sphere that is sufficiently close to the standard one.

In this paper, we find nodal solutions to (
1.1
) in two particular cases:

(1.3)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+c_{n}Ru=0&\text{on \ }M,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+d_{n}hu=|u|^{\frac{2}{n-2}}u&\text{on \ }\partial M,\end{cases}

and

(1.4)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+c_{n}Ru=|u|^{\frac{4}{n-2}}u&\text{on \ }M,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+d_{n}hu=0&\text{on \ }\partial M.\end{cases}

A positive solution
u
to (
1.3
) gives rise to a metric
\widetilde{g}:=u^{4/(n-2)}g
on
M
, conformally equivalent to
g
, whose scalar curvature on
M
is zero and for which the mean curvature of the boundary is constant. On the other hand, if
u
is a positive solution to (
1.4
), then
\widetilde{g}:=u^{4/(n-2)}g
is a metric on
M
, conformally equivalent to
g
, whose scalar curvature is constant and for which the mean curvature of the boundary is zero. The existence of such metrics was first studied by Escobar in
[
18
]
and
[
19
]
.
We obtain least-energy sign-changing solutions to both problems under suitable geometric assumptions on

M.

Consider the quadratic form

(1.5)

\mathscr{Q}u:=\int_{M}(|\nabla u|^{2}+c_{n}Ru^{2})\,\mathrm{dv}+\int_{\partial M}d_{n}hu^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma,

where
\,\mathrm{dv}
and
\,\mathrm{d}\sigma
are the Riemannian measures on
M
and
\partial M
induced by the metric
g
. We assume that
M
is
positive
, this means that the first eigenvalue satisfies

(1.6)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(M)

\displaystyle:=\inf_{v\in H^{1}(M)\smallsetminus\{0\}}\frac{\mathscr{Q}v}{\int_{M}v^{2}\,\mathrm{dv}}>0.

We prove the following results.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
n\geq 7
and
M
has a nonumbilic point
\xi\in\partial M
, then the problem (
1.3
) has a least energy sign-changing solution.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
n\geq 7
and
M
has a nonumbilic point
\xi\in\partial M
, then the problem (
1.4
) has a least energy sign-changing solution.

The strategy to prove Theorem
1.1
is as follows. If
u\in H^{1}(M)
is a nontrivial solution to (
1.3
), then multiplying that problem by
u
and integrating by parts shows that
u
belongs to the set

\mathcal{N}:=\Big\{u\in H^{1}(M):u\neq 0\text{ and }\mathscr{Q}u=\int_{\partial M}|u|^{2(n-1)/(n-2)}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\Big\}.

If
u
changes sign, then multiplying (
1.3
) by the positive and negative parts
u^{\pm}
of
u
shows that
u
belongs to

\mathcal{E}:=\{u\in\mathcal{N}:u^{+}\in\mathcal{N}\text{ and }u^{-}\in\mathcal{N}\}.

We define

\mu_{1}(M):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}}J(u)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mu_{2}(M):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{E}}J(u),

where
J
is the energy functional associated with problem (
1.3
) and defined in (
2.3
). As shown in Proposition
2.2
, these are conformal invariants. The first is related to Escobar’s invariant
Q(M,\partial M)
, defined in
[
19
]
, and is attained by a positive multiple of a minimizer of
Q(M,\partial M)
. Theorem
2.7
sets a condition for
\mu_{2}(M)
to be attained by a function
u\in\mathcal{E}
which is a solution to (
1.3
) that changes sign. Its Corollary
2.9
provides a friendlier condition in terms of a linear combination of two test functions. In Section
4
we show that a minimizer of
\mu_{1}(M)
and a cut-off of a minimizer of
\mu_{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
for the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
serve this purpose. The proof is based on the estimates provided by Escobar in
[
19
]
.

A similar strategy is used for Theorem
1.2
.

In the special case when
M
is the unit ball,
\mu_{2}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
is not attained. However, Almaraz and Wang showed in
[
1
, Corollary 1.2]
that there are infinitely many sign-changing solutions to the problem (
1.3
) on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
(which is conformally equivalent to the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
) if
n\geq 4
.

We will prove a similar result for problem (
1.4
).

Theorem 1.3
.

If
n\geq 5
the minimal boundary problem (
1.4
) has infinitely many sign-changing solutions on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
.

In
[
26
]
Ho, Lee and Shin defined a second Yamabe invariant for the problem (
1.3
) using the second eigenvalue of the boundary conformal Laplacian, and established a condition for its realization in terms of
Q(M,\partial M)
and
Q(\mathbb{B}^{n},\partial\mathbb{B}^{n})
, similar to the condition
[
2
, (22)]
of Ammann and Humbert. Ho and Pyo did something similar for the problem (
1.4
) in
[
27
]
. The conditions given by our Corollaries
2.9
and
3.3
are easier to verify. In Sections
4
and
5
we will show that they are satisfied if
n\geq 7
and
M
has a nonumbilic point in
\partial M
.

Remark 1.4
.

The argument used in our proofs of Theorems
1.4
and
1.3
cannot be directly applied to the general problem (
1.1
) due to the presence of nonlinear terms with different homogeneities:
u^{n+2\over n-2}
on
M
and
u^{n\over n-2}
on its boundary. This creates technical difficulties not present in the cases analyzed here. The existence of nodal solutions for the general problem remains an interesting open problem. It is worth mentioning that Escobar studied the existence of a positive solution for problem (
1.1
) in
[
21
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we describe the variational setting for the scalar flat problem (
1.3
) and establish conditions for the existence of a least energy nodal solution to it. In Section
3
we state the corresponding results for the minimal boundary problem (
1.4
). Theorem
1.1
is proved in Section
4
and Theorem
1.2
in Section
5
. Finally, Section
6
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

2.
The variational setting for the scalar flat problem

We denote the standard norm of
u
in
H^{1}(M)
by

\|u\|:=\left(\int_{M}(|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2})\,\mathrm{dv}\right)^{1/2}

and set
q:=\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}
. This is the critical exponent for the Sobolev trace embedding
H^{1}(M)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\partial M)
,
u\mapsto u|_{\partial M}
. As shown by Escobar in
[
18
, Section 1]
, if
M
is positive, then

(2.1)

\bar{\lambda}_{1}(M):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in H^{1}(M)\\
u|_{\partial M}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathscr{Q}u}{\int_{\partial M}|u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma}>0\qquad\text{and}\qquad Q(M,\partial M):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in H^{1}(M)\\
u|_{\partial M}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathscr{Q}u}{\Big(\int_{\partial M}|u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\Big)^{2/q}}>0.

Since
\lambda_{1}(M)>0
,
\bar{\lambda}_{1}(M)>0
and the trace map
H^{1}(M)\to L^{2}(\partial M)
,
u\mapsto u|_{\partial M}
, is linear and continuous, there exist
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}>0
such that

(2.2)

C_{1}\|u\|^{2}\leq\mathscr{Q}u\leq C_{2}\|u\|^{2}\qquad\text{for all \ }u\in H^{1}(M).

The solutions to the scalar flat problem (
1.3
) are the critical points of the
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-functional
J:H^{1}(M)\to\mathbb{R}
given by

(2.3)

J(u):=\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{Q}u-\frac{1}{q}\int_{\partial M}|u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma,

whose derivative at
u
is

(2.4)

J^{\prime}(u)v=\int_{M}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+c_{n}Ruv)\,\mathrm{dv}+\int_{\partial M}d_{n}huv\,\mathrm{d}\sigma-\int_{\partial M}|u|^{q-2}uv\,\mathrm{d}\sigma,\qquad v\in H^{1}(M).

The nontrivial solutions to (
1.3
) belong to the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle:=\{u\in H^{1}(M):u\neq 0\text{ and }J^{\prime}(u)u=0\}

\displaystyle\,=\Big\{u\in H^{1}(M):u\neq 0\text{ and }\mathscr{Q}u=\int_{\partial M}|u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\Big\},

which has the following properties.

Lemma 2.1
.

(a)

There exists
a>0
such that
\|u\|\geq a
for all
u\in\mathcal{N}
.

(b)

\mathcal{N}
is a Hilbert submanifold of class
\mathcal{C}^{2}
of
H^{1}(M)
and a natural constraint for
J
.

(c)

If
u\in H^{1}(M)
and its trace
u|_{\partial M}
is nontrivial, then there exists a unique
t_{u}\in(0,\infty)
such that
t_{u}u\in\mathcal{N}
. Moreover,

t_{u}:=\left(\dfrac{\mathscr{Q}u}{\int_{\partial M}|u|^{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-2}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad J(t_{u}u)=\frac{q-2}{2q}\left(\frac{\mathscr{Q}u}{\Big(\int_{\partial M}|u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\Big)^{2/q}}\right)^{q/(q-2)},

and the function
t\mapsto J(tu)
is strictly increasing in
[0,t_{u}]
and strictly decreasing in
[t_{u},\infty)
.

Proof.

(a):
If
u\in\mathcal{N}
, then

Q(M,\partial M)^{q/2}\mathscr{Q}u=Q(M,\partial M)^{q/2}\int_{\partial M}|u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq(\mathscr{Q}u)^{q/2}.

Therefore,
\mathscr{Q}u\geq Q(M,\partial M)^{q/(q-2)}>0,
and the statement
(a)
follows from (
2.2
).

(b):
It follows from
(a)
that
\mathcal{N}
is a closed subset of
H^{1}(M)
. The function
\Psi(u):=\mathscr{Q}u-\int_{\partial M}|u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{2}
on
H^{1}(M)
and
\mathcal{N}=\Psi^{-1}(0)\smallsetminus\{0\}
. Since

(2.5)

\Psi^{\prime}(u)u=2\mathscr{Q}u-q\int_{\partial M}|u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma=(2-q)\mathscr{Q}u<0\qquad\text{for every \ }u\in\mathcal{N},

0
is a regular value of
\Psi
. This shows that
\mathcal{N}
is a Hilbert submanifold of class
\mathcal{C}^{2}
of
H^{1}(M)
. Furthermore, (
2.5
) shows that
u
does not belong to the tangent space
T_{u}\mathcal{N}
of
\mathcal{N}
at
u
. Hence,
H^{1}(M)=T_{u}\mathcal{N}\oplus\mathbb{R}u
. So, if
u\in\mathcal{N}
and
J^{\prime}(u)v=0
for every
v\in T_{u}\mathcal{N}
then, as
J^{\prime}(u)u=0
, we have that
J^{\prime}(u)v=0
for every
v\in H^{1}(M)
. This shows that every critical point of the restriction of
J
to
\mathcal{N}
is a critical point of
J
, as claimed.

(c):
If
u\in H^{1}(M)
and
u|_{\partial M}\neq 0
it is readily seen that

(2.6)

t_{u}=\Big(\frac{\mathscr{Q}u}{\int_{\partial M}|u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma}\Big)^{1/(q-2)}

is the only point in
(0,\infty)
such that
t_{u}u\in\mathcal{N}
, that
J(t_{u}u)
is given by the expression in statement
(c)
and that
t_{u}
is the only critical point of the function
t\mapsto J(tu)
, which is a maximum.
∎

Set

\mu_{1}(M):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}}J(u).

It follows from Lemma
2.1
(c)
and (
2.1
) that

(2.7)

\mu_{1}(M)=\frac{q-2}{2q}\left(\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in H^{1}(M)\\
u|_{\partial M}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathscr{Q}u}{\Big(\int_{\partial M}|u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\Big)^{2/q}}\right)^{q/(q-2)}=\frac{q-2}{2q}\,Q(M,\partial M)^{q/(q-2)}>0.

It is easily seen that
\mu_{1}(M)\leq\mu_{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
, where
\mathbb{B}^{n}
is the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with the Euclidean metric. Escobar showed that, if
\mu_{1}(M)<\mu_{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
, then
\mu_{1}(M)
is attained at a positive solution of problem (
1.3
)
[
18
, Proposition 2.1]
, and stated conditions on
M
that imply the strict inequality
[
18
, Theorem 2]
.

If
u
is a sign-changing solution to (
1.3
), replacing
v
with
u^{+}:=\max\{u,0\}
and
u^{-}:=\min\{u,0\}
in (
2.4
) yields
J^{\prime}(u^{\pm})u^{\pm}=0
. Since
u^{+}\neq 0
and
u^{-}\neq 0
, we have that
u
belongs to the set

\mathcal{E}:=\{u\in\mathcal{N}:u^{+}\in\mathcal{N}\text{ and }u^{-}\in\mathcal{N}\}.

Let

\mu_{2}(M):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{E}}J(u).

Proposition 2.2
.

The numbers
\mu_{1}(M)
and
\mu_{2}(M)
are conformal invariants.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{g}:=\varphi^{4/(n-2)}g
, where
\varphi
is a smooth positive function on
M
. To make it clear which metric is being used we will include it as a subscript and write, for example,

\mathscr{Q}_{g}[u]:=\int_{M}(|\nabla u|_{g}^{2}+c_{n}R_{g}u^{2})\,\mathrm{dv}_{g}+\int_{\partial M}d_{n}h_{g}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}.

As shown in
[
17
, (1.8) and (1.10)]
,

\mathscr{Q}_{g}[u]=\mathscr{Q}_{\widetilde{g}}[\varphi^{-1}u]\qquad\text{and}\qquad\int_{\partial M}|u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}=\int_{\partial M}|\varphi^{-1}u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\widetilde{g}}\qquad\text{for every \ }u\in H^{1}(M).

Therefore,
J_{g}(u)=J_{\widetilde{g}}(\varphi^{-1}u)
and

u\in\mathcal{N}_{g}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\varphi^{-1}u\in\mathcal{N}_{\widetilde{g}}.

If follows that
\mu_{1}(M)
is a conformal invariant. Furthermore, as
(\varphi^{-1}u)^{\pm}=\varphi^{-1}u^{\pm}
,

u\in\mathcal{E}_{g}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\varphi^{-1}u\in\mathcal{E}_{\widetilde{g}}.

Thus,
\mu_{2}(M)
is a conformal invariant.
∎

We will give a condition that guarantees that
\mu_{2}(M)
is attained at some
u\in\mathcal{E}
, which is a sign-changing solution of (
1.3
); see Theorem
2.7
.

First, we give a minmax characterization of
\mu_{2}(M)
. We denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{+}:=\{u\in H^{1}(M):u\geq 0\},\qquad\mathcal{P}^{-}:=\{u\in H^{1}(M):u\leq 0\},

and define
\tau:H^{1}(M)\to[0,\infty)
by

\tau(u):=\frac{\int_{\partial M}|u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathscr{Q}u}\text{ \ if \ }u\neq 0,\qquad\tau(0):=0.

Note that, as
q>2
and
\overline{\lambda}_{1}>0
,
\tau
is continuous.

Consider the set
\Sigma
of all continuous maps
\sigma:[0,1]\times[0,1]\to H^{1}(M)
that satisfy

(2.8)

\begin{cases}\sigma(0,t)\in\mathcal{P}^{+},&\sigma(1,t)\in\mathcal{P}^{-},\\
\sigma(s,0)=0,&J(\sigma(s,1))\leq 0\text{ \ and \ }\tau(\sigma(s,1))\geq 2.\end{cases}

Lemma 2.3
.

(a)

Given
u\in\mathcal{E}
there exists
\sigma_{u}\in\Sigma
such that
\max_{s,t\in I}J(\sigma_{u}(s,t))=J(u)
.

(b)

If
\sigma\in\Sigma
then there exists
(s_{0},t_{0})\in[0,1]\times[0,1]
such that
\sigma(s_{0},t_{0})\in\mathcal{E}
.

As a consequence,

\mu_{2}(M)=\inf_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\,\max_{s,t\in[0,1]}J(\sigma(s,t)).

Proof.

(a):
Let
u\in\mathcal{E}
and set
u_{s}:=(1-s)u^{+}+su^{-}
,
s\in[0,1]
. Note that its trace
u_{s}|_{\partial M}
is nontrivial. Define
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathcal{N}
by
\gamma(s):=t_{u_{s}}u_{s}
with
t_{u_{s}}
as in (
2.6
). Then,

\gamma(0)=u^{+},\qquad\gamma(1)=u^{-}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\gamma(\tfrac{1}{2})=u.

Fix
R>0
such that
J(R\gamma(s))\leq 0
and
\tau(R\gamma(s))\geq 2
for all
s\in[0,1]
, and define
\sigma_{u}:[0,1]\times[0,1]\to H^{1}(M)
by

\sigma_{u}(s,t):=Rt\gamma(s).

Since
\gamma(s)\in\mathcal{N}
we have, by Lemma
2.1
(c)
, that
J(Rt\gamma(s))\leq J(\gamma(s))
for all
s,t\in[0,1]
and, as
u^{\pm}\in\mathcal{N}
,

J(\gamma(s))=J(\gamma(s)^{+})+J(\gamma(s)^{-})=J(t_{u_{s}}(1-s)u^{+})+J(t_{u_{s}}su^{-})\leq J(u^{+})+J(u^{-})=J(u)

for all
s\in[0,1]
. This shows that
J(u)=\max_{s,t\in I}J(\sigma_{u}(s,t))
for every
u\in\mathcal{E}
and, as a consequence,

\mu_{2}(M)\geq\inf_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\,\max_{s,t\in[0,1]}J(\sigma(s,t)).

(b):
Let
\sigma\in\Sigma
and define
f=(f_{1},f_{2}):[0,1]\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
by

f_{1}(s,t):=\tau(\sigma(s,t)^{+})-\tau(\sigma(s,t)^{-}),\qquad f_{2}(s,t):=\tau(\sigma(s,t)^{+})+\tau(\sigma(s,t)^{-})-2.

These are continuous functions and, for all
s,t\in[0,1]
, they satisfy

f_{1}(0,t)\geq 0,\qquad f_{1}(1,t)\leq 0,\qquad f_{2}(s,0)=-2,\qquad f_{2}(s,1)\geq 0.

By the Poincaré-Miranda theorem
[
28
]
there exists
(s_{0},t_{0})\in[0,1]\times[0,1]
such that
f_{1}(s_{0},t_{0})=0=f_{2}(s_{0},t_{0})
. This implies that

\tau(\sigma(s_{0},t_{0})^{+})=1=\tau(\sigma(s_{0},t_{0})^{-}).

Noting that
\tau(u)=1
if and only if
u\in\mathcal{N}
, we conclude that
\sigma(s_{0},t_{0})\in\mathcal{E}
. As a consequence,

\mu_{2}(M)\leq\inf_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\,\max_{s,t\in[0,1]}J(\sigma(s,t)).

This completes the proof.
∎

By (

),

\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathscr{Q}}:=\int_{M}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+c_{n}Ruv)\,\mathrm{dv}+\int_{\partial M}d_{n}huv\,\mathrm{d}\sigma,\qquad\|u\|_{\mathscr{Q}}:=\sqrt{\mathscr{Q}u},

define an inner product and a norm in
H^{1}(M)
, equivalent to the standard one. The gradient
\nabla J(u)
of
J
at a point
u\in H^{1}(M)
with respect to this inner product is
\nabla J(u)=u-K(u)
where
K(u)\in H^{1}(M)
is defined by

\langle K(u),v\rangle_{\mathscr{Q}}=\int_{\partial M}|u|^{q-2}uv\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\qquad\text{for all \ }v\in H^{1}(M).

The negative gradient flow
\eta:\mathcal{G}\to H^{1}(M)
of
J
is the solution of the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\eta(t,u)=-\nabla J(\eta(t,u)),\\
\eta(0,u)=u,\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{G}:=\{(t,u):u\in H^{1}(M),\ 0\leq t<T(u)\}
and
T(u)\in(0,\infty]
is the maximal existence time for the trajectory
t\to\eta(t,u)
. A subset
\mathcal{Z}
of
H^{1}(M)
is said to be
strictly positively invariant
under

if

\eta(t,u)\in\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{Z})\quad\text{for every \ }u\in\mathcal{Z}\text{ and every }t\in(0,T(u)),

where
\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{Z})
denotes the interior of
\mathcal{Z}
in
H^{1}(M)
. If
\mathcal{Z}
is strictly positively invariant under
\eta
, then the set

\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{Z}):=\{u\in H^{1}(M):\eta(t,u)\in\mathcal{Z}\text{ for some }t\in(0,T(u))\}

is open in
H^{1}(M)
and the entrance time map
e_{\mathcal{Z}}:\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{Z})\to\mathbb{R}
, defined by

e_{\mathcal{Z}}(u):=\inf\{t\geq 0:\eta(t,u)\in\mathcal{Z}\},

is continuous.

Given
\alpha>0
we set

\mathrm{dist}_{\mathscr{Q}}(u,\mathcal{Z}):=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{Z}}\|u-v\|_{\mathscr{Q}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{Z}):=\{u\in H^{1}(M):\mathrm{dist}_{\mathscr{Q}}(u,\mathcal{Z})\leq\alpha\}.

and, for
d\in\mathbb{R}
, we write

J^{\leq d}:=\{u\in H^{1}(M):J(u)\leq d\}.

The proof of the next lemma is formally the same as that of
[

. We give the details for completeness.

Lemma 2.4
.

There exists
\alpha>0
such that

(a)

and
B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}^{-})\cap\mathcal{E}=\emptyset,

(b)

and

Proof.

(a):
For every

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\partial M}|u^{-}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\Big)^{2/q}

\displaystyle=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{P}^{+}}\Big(\int_{\partial M}|u-v|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\Big)^{2/q}\leq\inf_{v\in\mathcal{P}^{+}}Q(M,\partial M)^{-1}\mathscr{Q}[u-v]

(2.9)

\displaystyle=Q(M,\partial M)^{-1}\,\mathrm{dist}_{\mathscr{Q}}(u,\mathcal{P}^{+})^{2}.

If

(b):
Using (

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}_{\mathscr{Q}}(K(u),\mathcal{P}^{+})\|K(u)^{-}\|_{\mathscr{Q}}\leq\|K(u)^{-}\|_{\mathscr{Q}}^{2}=\langle K(u),K(u)^{-}\rangle_{\mathscr{Q}}=\int_{\partial M}|u|^{q-2}uK(u)^{-}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

As a consequence, there exist

and

\mathrm{dist}_{\mathscr{Q}}(K(u),\mathcal{P}^{+})\leq\delta\,\mathrm{dist}_{\mathscr{Q}}(u,\mathcal{P}^{+})\qquad\text{for all \ }u\in B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}^{+}).

Since

(2.11)

\eta(t,u)\in B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}^{+})\qquad\text{for every \ }u\in B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}^{+})\text{ \ and \ }t\in[0,T(u)).

Now, arguing by contradiction, assume that

for some

and

\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=t_{0}}L(\eta(t,u))=L(-\nabla J(\eta(t_{0},u))=L(K(\eta(t_{0},u))-L(\eta(t_{0},u))=L(K(\eta(t_{0},u))-\beta>0.

(c)

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Lemma

Lemma 2.6
.

If
u_{k}\in H^{1}(M)
satisfy
\int_{\partial M}|u_{k}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\geq\alpha_{1}>0
for all

,

and

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. It is shown in
[

, Theorem 2.20]
that there exists

such that

Q(\mathbb{B}^{n},\partial\mathbb{B}^{n})\leq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{dv}+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{M}u^{2}\,\mathrm{dv}}{\Big(\int_{\partial M}|u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\Big)^{2/q}}\qquad\text{for all \ }u\in H^{1}(M).

Therefore,

\displaystyle Q(\mathbb{B}^{n},\partial\mathbb{B}^{n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)\int_{M}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\,\mathrm{dv}+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{M}u_{k}^{2}\,\mathrm{dv}}{\Big(\int_{\partial M}|u_{k}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\Big)^{2/q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+\varepsilon)\mathscr{Q}u_{k}+o(1)}{\Big(\int_{\partial M}|u_{k}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\Big)^{2/q}}=\frac{(1+\varepsilon)\mathscr{Q}u_{k}}{\Big(\int_{\partial M}|u_{k}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\Big)^{2/q}}+o(1).

\begin{split}\mu_{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})&\leq\frac{q-2}{2q}(1+\varepsilon)^{\frac{q}{q-2}}\left[\dfrac{\mathscr{Q}u_{k}}{\Big(\int_{\partial M}|u_{k}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\Big)^{2/q}}\right]^{\frac{q}{q-2}}+o(1)\\

\mu_{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{q/(q-2)}\liminf_{k\to\infty}J(t_{u_{k}}u_{k}).

Since

Theorem 2.7
.

If

\mu_{2}(M)<\mu_{1}(M)+\mu_{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n}),

then there exists
u\in\mathcal{E}
such that
J(u)=\mu_{2}(M)
and
J^{\prime}(u)=0
, that is,

is a least energy sign-changing solution of (

).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.5
there exist

such that

(2.13)

\mathrm{dist}_{\mathscr{Q}}(u_{k},\,\mathcal{P}^{+}\cup\mathcal{P}^{-})\geq\alpha,\qquad J(u_{k})\to\mu_{2}(M)\qquad\text{and}\qquad J^{\prime}(u_{k})\to 0.

Then,

\mu_{2}(M)+o(1)+o(1)\|u_{k}\|_{\mathscr{Q}}\geq J(u_{k})-\frac{1}{q}J^{\prime}(u_{k})u_{k}=\frac{q-2}{2q}\mathscr{Q}u_{k}=\frac{q-2}{2q}\|u_{k}\|_{\mathscr{Q}}^{2}.

It follows that
(u_{k})
is bounded in
H^{1}(M)
and, passing to a subsequence,
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
H^{1}(M)
,
u_{k}\to u
strongly in
L^{2}(M)
and
u_{k}|_{\partial M}\to u|_{\partial M}

.

From (

\mathscr{Q}[u_{k}^{+}]=\|u_{k}-u_{k}^{-}\|_{\mathscr{Q}}^{2}\geq\mathrm{dist}_{\mathscr{Q}}(u_{k},\mathcal{P}^{-})^{2}\geq\alpha^{2}>0,

and, similarly,
\mathscr{Q}[u_{k}^{-}]\geq\alpha^{2}>0
. Since
J^{\prime}(u_{k}^{\pm})u_{k}^{\pm}=J^{\prime}(u_{k})u_{k}^{\pm}\to 0
, we get that

(2.14)

\int_{\partial M}|u_{k}^{\pm}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma=\mathscr{Q}[u_{k}^{\pm}]+o(1)\geq\alpha_{0}>0\qquad\text{for all large enough \ }k\in\mathbb{N},

and, recalling Lemma
2.1
(c)
, we deduce that

. Thus,

J(t_{u_{k}^{\pm}}u_{k}^{\pm})-J(u_{k}^{\pm})=\frac{1}{2}\Big(t_{u_{k}^{\pm}}^{2}-1\Big)\mathscr{Q}[u_{k}^{\pm}]-\frac{1}{q}\Big(t_{u_{k}^{\pm}}^{q}-1\Big)\int_{\partial M}|u_{k}^{\pm}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma=o(1)

and, as a consequence,

\liminf_{k\to\infty}J(t_{u_{k}^{+}}u_{k}^{+})=\liminf_{k\to\infty}J(u_{k}^{+})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\liminf_{k\to\infty}J(t_{u_{k}^{-}}u_{k}^{-})=\liminf_{k\to\infty}J(u_{k}^{-}).

It follows that
u^{+}\neq 0
and
u^{-}\neq 0

and

o(1)=J^{\prime}(u_{k})\varphi\to J^{\prime}(u)\varphi\qquad\text{for all \ }\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M).

Therefore,
u
is a sign-changing critical point of

. As a consequence,

and

\mu_{2}(M)\leq J(u)=\frac{q-2}{2q}\mathscr{Q}u\leq\frac{q-2}{2q}\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathscr{Q}u_{k}=\lim_{k\to\infty}J(u_{k})=\mu_{2}(M).

It follows that

, as claimed. In addition,

. Therefore,

.
∎

3.
The variational setting for the minimal boundary problem

The variational formulation for problem (

) is similar. We state the facts without their proofs. These are obtained following arguments analogous to those in the previous section.

Let

be the critical exponent for the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(M)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(M)
. The solutions to the problem (

) are the critical points of the
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-functional

given by

I(u):=\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{Q}u-\frac{1}{p}\int_{M}|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{dv},

whose derivative at

I^{\prime}(u)v=\int_{M}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+c_{n}Ruv)\,\mathrm{dv}+\int_{\partial M}d_{n}huv\,\mathrm{d}\sigma-\int_{M}|u|^{p-2}uv\,\mathrm{dv},\qquad v\in H^{1}(M).

The nontrivial solutions to (

) belong to the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle:=\{u\in H^{1}(M):u\neq 0\text{ and }I^{\prime}(u)u=0\}

\displaystyle\,=\Big\{u\in H^{1}(M):u\neq 0\text{ and }\mathscr{Q}u=\int_{M}|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{dv}\Big\}.

Arguing as in Lemma
2.1
, we see that
\mathcal{M}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-Hilbert submanifold of
H^{1}(M)
and a natural constraint for

, and we have that

(3.1)

\kappa_{1}(M):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{M}}I(u)=\frac{p-2}{2p}\,Q(M)^{p/(p-2)},\qquad\text{where \ }Q(M):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in H^{1}(M)\\
u\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathscr{Q}u}{\big(\int_{M}|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{dv}\big)^{2/p}}.

Q(M)>0
if
M
is positive and, as stated in
[
19
]
, it is attained if
Q(M)<Q(\mathbb{B}^{n})
. In that work Escobar gave conditions for the strict inequality to hold.

The sign-changing solutions to (
1.4
) belong to the set

\mathcal{D}:=\{u\in\mathcal{M}:u^{+}\in\mathcal{M}\text{ and }u^{-}\in\mathcal{M}\}.

We define

\kappa_{2}(M):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{D}}I(u).

Using
[
17
, (1.8) and (1.9)]
it is easy to see that
\kappa_{1}(M)
and
\kappa_{2}(M)
are conformal invariants.

The following theorem gives a condition for the existence of a minimizer. Its proof is formally the same as that of Theorem
2.8
.

.

and

, that is,

is a least energy sign-changing solution of (
1.4
).

.

If there exists

such that

,

and

.

4.
The energy of the test function in the scalar flat case

We assume that
n\geq 7
and that
M
has a nonumbilic point
\xi\in\partial M
. As before, we set
q:=\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}:=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\ t>0\}
. For each
\varepsilon>0
the function

\widetilde{U}_{\varepsilon}(x,t):=\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{(\varepsilon+t)^{2}+|x|^{2}}\right)^{(n-2)/2}

is a solution to the problem

\begin{cases}\Delta u=0&\text{in \ }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=(n-2)u^{\frac{n}{n-2}}&\text{on \ }\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.\end{cases}

Beckner
[
4
]
and Escobar
[
16
, Theorem 1]
showed that

Q(\mathbb{B}^{n},\partial\mathbb{B}^{n})=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\nabla\widetilde{U}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t}{\left(\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\widetilde{U}_{\varepsilon}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2/q}}.

As shown in Proposition
2.2
,
\mu_{2}(M)
is invariant under any conformal change of the metric. Therefore, applying
[
18
, Lemma 3.3]
with
a_{ij}=0=b
, we may assume that

H=0\text{ on }\partial M,\quad\bar{R}_{ij}(\xi)=0,\quad\mathrm{Ric}(\nu)(\xi)=0\quad\text{and}\quad R(\xi)=\|\pi(\xi)\|^{2},

where
\pi
is the second fundamental form,
H
is its trace and
\bar{R}_{ij}
are the coefficients of the Ricci tensor of
\partial M
. Furthermore,
\pi(\xi)
is nontrivial if and only if the second fundamental form of the original metric at the point
\xi
is not trivial, which is the case because we are assuming that
\xi\in\partial M
is not umbilic.

Let
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
be Fermi coordinates at
\xi
in a half-ball of radius
2\varrho_{0}
centered at the origin, i.e.,
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})
are normal coordinates on
\partial M
at
\xi
and the point on
M
represented by
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},t)
is
\gamma(t)
, where
\gamma
is the geodesic leaving from
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})
in the direction orthogonal to
\partial M
, parametrized by arc length. Set
\varrho^{2}:=x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{n-1}^{2}+t^{2}
. Following
[
18
, Section 3]
, we fix a piecewise smooth decreasing function
\psi
such that
\psi(\varrho)=1
for
\varrho\leq\varrho_{0}
,
\psi(\varrho)=0
for
\varrho\geq 2\varrho_{0}
and
\psi^{\prime}(\varrho)\leq\varrho_{0}^{-1}
if
\varrho\in(\varrho_{0},2\varrho_{0})
, and we define

W_{\varepsilon}:=\widetilde{U}_{\varepsilon}\psi,\qquad\varepsilon>0.

Escobar proved the following estimate in
[
18
, (3.46)]
:

(4.1)

\mathscr{Q}[W_{\varepsilon}]\leq Q(\mathbb{B}^{n},\partial\mathbb{B}^{n})\left(\int_{\partial M}|W_{\varepsilon}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)^{2/q}-\frac{(n-2)^{2}(n-6)}{4(n-1)}I_{1}\|\pi(\xi)\|^{2}\varepsilon^{2}+c_{1}\varepsilon^{3}+c_{2}\varepsilon^{n-2},

where
I_{1}
is a positive constant. Since we are assuming that
n>6
and
\xi
is not umbilic (hence
\|\pi(\xi)\|^{2}>0
), there is a positive constant
C_{0}
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that, for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
,

(4.2)

\frac{q-2}{2q}\left(\frac{\mathscr{Q}[W_{\varepsilon}]}{\big(\int_{\partial M}|W_{\varepsilon}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\big)^{2/q}}\right)^{q/(q-2)}\leq\frac{q-2}{2q}Q(\mathbb{B}^{n},\partial\mathbb{B}^{n})^{q/(q-2)}-C_{0}\varepsilon^{2}=\mu_{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})-C_{0}\varepsilon^{2}.

From the estimate (
4.1
) and
[
18
, Proposition 2.1]
Escobar derived the existence of a smooth positive function
\widehat{u}
on
M
such that

\frac{\mathscr{Q}[\widehat{u}]}{\big(\int_{\partial M}|\widehat{u}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\big)^{2/q}}=Q(M,\partial M).

By Lemma
2.1
(c)
and (
2.7
), the function
u_{0}:=t_{\widehat{u}}\widehat{u}\in\mathcal{N}
satisfies

(4.3)

J(u_{0})=\mu_{1}(M).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We will apply Corollary
2.9
to the functions
u_{0}
and
W_{\varepsilon}
defined above. To this end, we will show that, for some
\varepsilon>0
small enough,

(4.4)

J(su_{0}-tW_{\varepsilon})<\mu_{1}(M)+\mu_{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})\qquad\text{for all \ }s,t\in[0,r]\text{ with }s+t=r\text{ \ and all \ }r\in[0,R],

where
R>0
is chosen large enough so that
J(su_{0}-tW_{\varepsilon})\leq 0
for all
s,t\in[0,r]
with
s+t=r\geq R
and every
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small.

Since
u_{0}
solves (
1.3
), it follows from Lemma
2.1
(c)
, estimate (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) that, for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small,

\displaystyle J(su_{0}-tW_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{Q}[su_{0}-tW_{\varepsilon}]-\frac{1}{q}\int_{\partial M}|su_{0}-tW_{\varepsilon}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=J(su_{0})+J(tW_{\varepsilon})-\langle su_{0},tW_{\varepsilon}\rangle_{\mathscr{Q}}-\frac{1}{q}\int_{\partial M}\Big(|su_{0}-tW_{\varepsilon}|^{q}-|su_{0}|^{q}-|tW_{\varepsilon}|^{q}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leq J(u_{0})+\frac{q-2}{2q}\left(\frac{\mathscr{Q}[W_{\varepsilon}]}{\big(\int_{\partial M}|W_{\varepsilon}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\big)^{2/q}}\right)^{q/(q-2)}-st\int_{\partial M}u_{0}^{q-1}W_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{q}\int_{\partial M}\Big(|su_{0}-tW_{\varepsilon}|^{q}-|su_{0}|^{q}-|tW_{\varepsilon}|^{q}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{1}(M)+\mu_{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})-C_{0}\varepsilon^{2}+\int_{\partial M}\Big(|su_{0}||tW_{\varepsilon}|^{q-1}+|su_{0}|^{q-1}|tW_{\varepsilon}|\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

(4.5)

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{1}(M)+\mu_{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})-C_{0}\varepsilon^{2}+R^{q}\int_{\partial M}\Big(u_{0}W_{\varepsilon}^{q-1}+u_{0}^{q-1}W_{\varepsilon}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Here we have used that

|a-b|^{r}-a^{r}-b^{r}\geq-r(ab^{r-1}+a^{r-1}b)\qquad\text{for all \ }a,b\geq 0\text{ \ and \ }r\geq 2.

Next we estimate the integrals using Fermi coordinates
(x,t)
at
\xi
. In the following,
C
will denote different positive constants independent of
\varepsilon
. Setting
x=\varepsilon y
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}u_{0}W_{\varepsilon}^{q-1}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}W_{\varepsilon}(x,0)^{q-1}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{2}+|x|^{2}}\right)^{n/2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon(1+|y|^{2})}\right)^{n/2}\varepsilon^{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}y=C\varepsilon^{(n-2)/2}=o(\varepsilon^{2})\quad\text{if \ }n\geq 7.

As
n\geq 7
we may fix
\frac{n-1}{n-2}<\beta<\frac{2(n-1)}{n+2}
. Then, setting
x=\varepsilon y
we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}W_{\varepsilon}(x,0)^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon(1+|y|^{2})}\right)^{\beta(n-2)/2}\varepsilon^{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}y=C\varepsilon^{n-1-\beta(n-2)/2},

and using Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}u_{0}^{q-1}W_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\partial M}u_{0}^{(q-1)\beta/(\beta-1)}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)^{(\beta-1)/\beta}\left(\int_{\partial M}W_{\varepsilon}^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)^{1/\beta}\leq C\varepsilon^{((n-1)/\beta-(n-2)/2)}=o(\varepsilon^{2}).

Returning to (
4
), we conclude that

J(su_{0}-tW_{\varepsilon})\leq\mu_{1}(M)+\mu_{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})-C_{0}\varepsilon^{2}+o(\varepsilon^{2})<\mu_{1}(M)+\mu_{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})

if
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small, as claimed in (
4.4
). This completes the proof.
∎

5.
The energy of the test function in the minimal boundary case

We assume that
n\geq 7
and that
M
has a nonumbilic point
\xi\in\partial M
. As before, we set
p:=\frac{2n}{n-2}
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}:=\{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}>0\}
. For each
\varepsilon>0
the
bubble

U_{\varepsilon}(x):=\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{2}+|x|^{2}}\right)^{(n-2)/2}

is a solution to the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=n(n-2)u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}&\text{in \ }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=0&\text{on \ }\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\end{cases}

and

Q(\mathbb{B}^{n})=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\nabla U_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}U_{\varepsilon}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2/p}}.

We recall the construction of Escobar’s test function given in
[
19
, Section 3]
. After a conformal change of metric, one can assume that
h(\xi)=0
and
R_{ij}(\xi)=0
. Take normal coordinates
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
around
\xi
such that
\nu(\xi)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}
and the second fundamental form of
\partial M
at
\xi
has diagonal form. The diagonal elements are the principal curvatures
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}
and
\partial M
is given locally around
\xi
by the equation

x_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}x_{i}^{2}+O(|x|^{3}).

Since
\xi
is not umbilic, not all principal curvatures are zero, and we may assume that
\lambda_{1}>0
. Consider the function

\varphi_{\varepsilon,\delta}:=\widehat{U}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\psi,

where

\widehat{U}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x):=\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{2}+|x|^{2}-\delta x_{1}^{2}}\right)^{(n-2)/2}

and
\psi
is a suitable cut-off function such that
\psi(x)=1
for
x\in C_{\varrho_{0}}^{+}
and
\psi(x)=0
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\smallsetminus C_{2\varrho_{0}}^{+}
, where
C_{\varrho}^{+}
is the cylinder
C_{\varrho}^{+}:=\{(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}:|x^{\prime}|<\varrho,\ 0<x_{n}<\varrho\}
. The following estimate is given on page 33 of
[
19
]
:

\displaystyle\mathscr{Q}[\varphi_{\varepsilon,\delta}]

\displaystyle\leq Q(\mathbb{B}^{n})\left(\int_{M}|\varphi_{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{p}\,\mathrm{dv}\right)^{2/p}+A_{1}\delta+B_{1}\lambda_{1}\varepsilon\delta+D_{1}\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+c(\varepsilon|\delta|+\varepsilon^{2})\varrho_{0}+c\varepsilon^{2}|\delta|+c\varepsilon^{n}|\delta|\varrho_{0}^{-n}+c|\delta|^{3}+c\varepsilon^{3}+c\varepsilon\delta^{2}

(5.1)

\displaystyle\quad+c\varepsilon^{n}\varrho_{0}^{2-n}+c\varepsilon^{n-2}|\delta|\varrho_{0}+c\varepsilon^{2}|\delta|\log(\varrho_{0}/\varepsilon)+c\varepsilon^{n-2}\varrho_{0}^{2-n},

where
A_{1},B_{1},D_{1}
and
c
are constants that depend only on
n
. It follows that
A_{1}=0
; otherwise, taking
\delta<0
if
A_{1}>0
or
\delta>0
if
A_{1}<0
and setting
|\delta|=\varepsilon
we derive from (
5
) that

\mathscr{Q}[\varphi_{\varepsilon,\delta}]\leq Q(\mathbb{B}^{n})\left(\int_{M}|\varphi_{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{p}\,\mathrm{dv}\right)^{2/p}-|A_{1}|\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon)<Q(\mathbb{B}^{n})\left(\int_{M}|\varphi_{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{p}\,\mathrm{dv}\right)^{2/p},

for
\varepsilon>0
small enough if
n\geq 4
, which contradicts the definition of
Q(\mathbb{B}^{n})
. It is shown in
[
19
, Lemma 3.12]
that
B_{1}<0
. Multiplying the metric by a constant and using the conformal invariance we may assume that
\lambda_{1}
is large enough so that
-\lambda_{1}B_{1}>D_{1}
. So, if
n\geq 5
, choosing
\varrho_{0}
small enough and setting
\delta=\varepsilon
, the estimate (
5
) gives a positive constant
C
such that

(5.2)

\mathscr{Q}[\varphi_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon}]\leq Q(\mathbb{B}^{n})\left(\int_{M}|\varphi_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon}|^{p}\right)^{2/p}-C\varepsilon^{2}+o(\varepsilon^{2}),

for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small. Set

V_{\varepsilon}:=\varphi_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon}.

Then, recalling (
3.1
), we see from (
5.2
) that there exists a positive constant
C_{1}
such that

(5.3)

\frac{p-2}{2p}\left(\frac{\mathscr{Q}[V_{\varepsilon}]}{\big(\int_{M}|V_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\big)^{2/p}}\right)^{p/(p-2)}\leq\frac{p-2}{2p}Q(\mathbb{B}^{n})^{p/(p-2)}-C_{1}\varepsilon^{2}=\kappa_{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})-C_{1}\varepsilon^{2}

for
\varepsilon>0
small enough, if
n\geq 5
.

The inequality (
5.2
) is used in
[
19
]
to establish the existence of a smooth positive minimizer
\widehat{v}_{0}
for
Q(M)
. Then, by (
3.1
), there exists
t\in(0,\infty)
such that
v_{0}:=t\widehat{v}_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
and

I(v_{0})=\kappa_{1}(M).

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The statement will follow from Corollary
3.3
applied to the functions
v_{0}
and
V_{\varepsilon}
defined above once we show that, for some
\varepsilon>0
small enough,

(5.4)

I(sv_{0}-tV_{\varepsilon})<\kappa_{1}(M)+\kappa_{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})\qquad\text{for all \ }s,t\in[0,r]\text{ with }s+t=r\text{ \ and all \ }r\in[0,R],

where
R>0
is chosen large enough so that
I(sv_{0}-tV_{\varepsilon})\leq 0
for all
s,t\in[0,r]
with
s+t=r\geq R
and every
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small.

Arguing as in the proof of (
4
), using the estimate (
5.3
), we see that, for
n\geq 5
and
\varepsilon
sufficiently small,

\displaystyle I(sv_{0}-tV_{\varepsilon})\leq\kappa_{1}(M)+\kappa_{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})-C_{1}\varepsilon^{2}+R^{p}\int_{M}\Big(v_{0}V_{\varepsilon}^{p-1}+v_{0}^{p-1}V_{\varepsilon}\Big)\,\mathrm{dv}.

If
n\geq 5
we may fix
\frac{n-1}{n-2}<\beta<\frac{2n}{n+2}
. Then, reasoning as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we derive

\displaystyle\int_{M}v_{0}^{p-1}V_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{dv}=O\big(\varepsilon^{(n/\beta-(n-2)/2)}\big)=o(\varepsilon^{2}).

We also obtain

\displaystyle\int_{M}v_{0}V_{\varepsilon}^{p-1}\,\mathrm{dv}=O\big(\varepsilon^{(n-2)/2}\big).

Therefore, if
n\geq 7
,

I(su_{0}-tV_{\varepsilon})\leq\mu_{1}(M)+\mu_{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})-C_{1}\varepsilon^{2}+o(\varepsilon^{2})<\mu_{1}(M)+\mu_{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n}),

for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, as stated in (
5.4
). This completes the proof.
∎

6.
Nodal solutions for the minimal boundary problem on the ball

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Since the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
is conformally equivalent to the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
there is a one-to-one correspondence between nodal solutions
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
to the problem (
1.4
) and nodal solutions
w\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
to

(6.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta w=|w|^{p-2}w&\text{in \ }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\\
\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}=0&\text{on \ }\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\end{cases}

where
p:=\frac{2n}{n-2}
. On the other hand, any function
w\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which is even in the last variable (i.e.,
w(x^{\prime},x_{n})=w(x^{\prime},-x_{n})
for all
(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
) satisfies
\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}(x^{\prime},0)=0
. Therefore, in order to prove Theorem
1.3
, it suffices to show that the Yamabe problem

(6.2)

\begin{cases}-\Delta w=|w|^{p-2}w\\
w\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\end{cases}

has infinitely many sign-changing solutions which are even in the last variable. Their restriction to
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
will be sign-changing solutions to (
6.1
). Note that the solutions provided by Ding in
[
15
]
do not have this property. We show below that there are solutions to (
6.2
) that do satisfy it.

For
n\geq 5
we write
\mathbb{R}^{n}\equiv\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-4}
and a point in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as
(z,y)
with
z=(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n-4}
. Let
G
be the subgroup of the group
O(n)
of linear isometries of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose elements are the unit complex numbers acting on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

g(z,y):=(gz_{1},\overline{g}z_{2},y),\quad\text{for \ }g\in G,\ \ (z_{1},z_{2},y)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-4},

where
\overline{g}
is the complex conjugate of
g
, and for each
m\geq 1
define
\varrho_{m}\in O(n)
to be

\varrho_{m}(z,y):=\left(\Big(\cos\frac{\pi}{m}\Big)z+\Big(\sin\frac{\pi}{m}\Big)\tau z,\,y\right)\qquad\text{for \ }(z,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-4},

where
\tau(z_{1},z_{2}):=(-\overline{z}_{2},\overline{z}_{1})
.

The following result is a slight improvement on
[
6
, Theorem 1.3]
. Its proof is the same. It is based on
[
11
, Corollary 3.4]
, this time applied fully for our purposes. We give the proof for completeness.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
n\geq 5
.

(i)

For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a sign-changing solution
w_{m}
to the Yamabe problem (
6.2
) such that

\displaystyle w_{m}(g(z,y))

\displaystyle=w_{m}(z,y)

\displaystyle\text{for all \ }(z,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-4},\ g\in G,

\displaystyle w_{m}(\varrho_{m}(z,y))

\displaystyle=-w_{m}(z,y)

\displaystyle\text{for all \ }(z,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-4},

\displaystyle w_{m}(z,y)

\displaystyle=w_{m}(z,y^{\prime})

\displaystyle\text{for all \ }(z,y),(z,y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-4}\text{ \ with \ }|y|=|y^{\prime}|.

where
G
and
\varrho_{m}
are as above. In particular,
w_{m}
is not radial, changes sign and is even in the last variable.

(ii)

If
\ell=jm
and
j
is even, then
w_{\ell}
and
w_{m}
are different and one is not obtained by translation and dilation of the other. In particular,

w_{1},\,w_{2},\ldots,w_{2^{k}},\ldots

is an infinite sequence of sign-changing solutions of (
6.2
) that are not equivalent under translation and dilation.

Proof.

(i):
Let
\Gamma_{m}
be the subgroup of
O(n)
generated by
G\cup\{\varrho_{m}\}
and
\phi_{m}:\Gamma_{m}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{1,-1\}
be the homomorphism of groups given by
\phi_{m}(g):=1
if
g\in G
and
\phi_{m}(\varrho_{m}):=-1
. Since
\varrho_{m}
has order
2m
, this homomorphism is well defined. The
\Gamma_{m}
-orbit of a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, defined as

\Gamma_{m}x:=\{\gamma x:\gamma\in\Gamma_{m}\},

is infinite if
x=(z,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-4}
with
z\neq 0
, while
(0,y)
is a fixed point of
\Gamma_{m}
for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n-4}
(i.e.,
\gamma(0,y)=(0,y)
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma_{m}
). Then, by
[
11
, Corollary 3.4]
, there exists a sign-changing solution
w_{m}
to the Yamabe problem (
6.2
) with the properties stated in
(i)
.

(ii):
It suffices to show that, if
\ell=jm
with
j
even and
u
and
v
are nontrivial functions that satisfy

u(\varrho_{\ell}x)=-u(x)\quad\text{and}\quad v(\varrho_{m}x)=-v(x)\quad\text{for every \ }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

then
u\neq v
. Indeed, arguing by contradiction, assume that
u=v
and choose
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
u(x_{0})=v(x_{0})\neq 0
. Then, since
\varrho_{\ell}^{j}=\varrho_{m}
and
j
is even, we have that

u(\varrho_{m}x_{0})=u(\varrho_{\ell}^{j}x_{0})=u(x_{0})=v(x_{0})=-v(\varrho_{m}x_{0})=-u(\varrho_{m}x_{0}).

This is a contradiction.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By the discussion at the beginning of this section, Theorem
1.3
follows from Theorem
6.1
.
∎

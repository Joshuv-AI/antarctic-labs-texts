A semi-Lagrangian method for solving state constraint Mean Field Games in Macroeconomics
Fabio Camilli, Qing Tang, Yong-shen Zhou

Abstract
We study continuous-time heterogeneous agent models cast as Mean Field Games, in the Aiyagari-Bewley-Huggett framework. The model couples a Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation for individual optimization with a Fokker–Planck–Kolmogorov equation for the wealth distribution. We establish a comparison principle for constrained viscosity solutions of the HJB equation and propose a semi-Lagrangian (SL) scheme for its numerical solution, proving convergence via the Barles–Souganidis method. A policy iteration algorithm handles state constraints, and a dual SL scheme is used for the FPK equation. Numerical methods are presented in a fully discrete, implementable form.

In the MFG approach, each agent maximizes their inter-temporal utility while taking the interest rate as given. This leads to a system of coupled partial differential equations describing both individual behavior and the evolution of the population distribution. Concretely, each agent solves the following stochastic control problem, given an interest rate

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\rho t}u(c_{t})\,dt\right],\hskip 10.00002pt\text{subject to}\hskip 10.00002pt\begin{cases}dx_{t}=(rx_{t}+y_{t}-c_{t})dt,\\

The optimal control problem leads to a weakly coupled system of Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations for the value functions

\rho v_{j}(x)=\sup_{c\geq 0}\left\{u(c)+(rx+y_{j}-c)Dv_{j}\right\}+\lambda_{j}(v_{\bar{\jmath}}(x)-v_{j}(x)),\hskip 10.00002pt\bar{\jmath}=3-j,

Huggett Model:
In the Huggett framework

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(i)\quad&&\rho v_{j}(x)=\sup_{c\geq 0}\left\{u(c)+(rx+y_{j}-c)Dv_{j}(x)\right\}+\lambda_{j}(v_{\bar{\jmath}}(x)-v_{j}(x)),\\

&&&\sum_{j}\int_{x>{\underline{x}}}g_{j}(x)dx+\mu_{j}=1,\end{aligned}\right.

together with the equilibrium condition:

(iii_{H})\hskip 10.00002ptK[m]=B.

(1.5)

The Aiyagari model interprets individual assets

Finally, we present the numerical algorithms in a fully discrete vectorized form to illustrate the implementation.

3.1
The discrete Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation

The dynamics of the representative agent is given by

\left\{\begin{aligned} \qquad&x_{n+1}=x_{n}+h(rx_{n}+y_{n}-c_{n})\delta_{y_{n},y_{n+1}}\\
&x_{0}=x\geq{\underline{x}},\quad x_{n}\geq x\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

Here

\displaystyle v^{h}_{j}(x)=\sup_{c\in\mathcal{C}^{h}_{j}(x)}\left\{(1-\rho h)(1-\lambda_{j}h)v^{h}_{j}(x+h(rx+y_{j}-c))+hu(c)\right\}+(1-\rho h)\lambda_{j}hv^{h}_{\bar{\jmath}}(x),

(3.3)

\displaystyle\rho v^{h}_{j}(x)={}

\displaystyle+(1-\rho h)\lambda_{j}(v^{h}_{\bar{\jmath}}(x)-v^{h}_{j}(x)),

where

3.2

\displaystyle-(1-\rho h)\lambda_{j}(\phi_{\bar{\jmath}}(x_{i})-\phi_{j}(x_{i}))-C_{1}h

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\rho\phi_{j}(x_{i})-(1-\rho h)\lambda_{j}(\phi_{\bar{\jmath}}(x_{i})-\phi_{j}(x_{i}))-C\left(\frac{({\Delta x})^{2}}{h}+h\right)

\displaystyle-\sup_{c\in\mathcal{C}^{h}_{j}(x_{i})}\left\{u(c)+\frac{(1-\rho h)(1-\lambda_{j}h)}{h}\left[\sum_{k}\beta_{k}(x_{i}+hs_{i,j}(c))\phi_{j}(x_{i})-\phi_{j}(x_{i})\right]\right\}

and passing to the
\limsup
in

, we get the first condition in

consistency
.

Given

, let

such that

. Then, taking into account (

), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{j}(x_{i},\phi(x_{i}),\phi_{j})

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\rho\phi_{j}(x_{i})-\sup_{c\in\mathcal{C}^{\Delta}_{j}(x_{i})}\left\{u(c)+(1-\rho h)(1-\lambda_{j}h)D\phi_{j}(x_{i})(rx_{i}+y_{j}-c)\right\}

\displaystyle-(1-\rho h)\lambda_{j}(\phi_{\bar{\jmath}}(x_{i})-\phi_{j}(x_{i}))-C_{1}\left(h+\frac{({\Delta x})^{2}}{h}\right)

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\rho\phi_{j}(x_{i})-\sup_{c\in(0,\infty)}\Big\{u(c)+(1-\rho h)(1-\lambda_{j}h)D\phi_{j}(x_{i})(rx_{i}+y_{j}-c)\Big\}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\rho\phi_{j}(x_{i})-H(x_{i},y_{j},D\phi_{j})-\lambda_{j}(\phi_{j}(x_{i})-\phi_{\bar{\jmath}}(x_{i}))-C\left(\frac{({\Delta x})^{2}}{h}+h\right).

Passing to the
\liminf_{{\Delta\to 0}\atop{x_{i}\to x}}
in the previous inequality, we get the second condition in

.

Proposition 4.8
.

Assume
\Delta\sim h
and
\Delta
is sufficiently small, then the solution
V^{\Delta}_{i,j}
to the scheme (
4.1
) is nondecreasing in
i
.

Proof.

We aim to show

\mathcal{F}^{\Delta}_{j}\left(x_{i},\left[V^{\Delta}_{i+1,j},V^{\Delta}_{i+1,\bar{\jmath}}\right],V^{\Delta}_{\cdot,j}\right)\geq 0\hskip 20.00003pti\in{\mathbb{N}},\,j=1,2,\,\bar{\jmath}=3-j,

(4.17)

and then apply the discrete comparison principle. For
Eq.

4.17
to hold, we only need

\displaystyle\sup_{c\in\mathcal{C}^{\Delta}_{j}(x_{i+1})}\left\{u(c)+(1-\rho h)(1-\lambda_{j}h)\frac{1}{h}\left(\sum_{k}\beta_{k}(x_{i+1}+hs_{i+1,j}(c))V_{k,j}\right)\right\}

(4.18)

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\sup_{c\in\mathcal{C}^{\Delta}_{j}(x_{i})}\left\{u(c)+(1-\rho h)(1-\lambda_{j}h)\frac{1}{h}\left(\sum_{k}\beta_{k}(x_{i}+hs_{i,1}(c))V_{k,j}\right)\right\},

where the
\sup
on the right hand side of inequality (
4.18
) is attained by
c^{*}_{i,j}
. Notice that since
c^{*}_{i,j}\in\mathcal{C}^{\Delta}_{j}(x_{i})
we have
c^{*}_{i,j}+r\Delta x+\frac{\Delta x}{h}\in\mathcal{C}^{\Delta}_{j}(x_{i+1}).

From
\Delta\sim h
and
\Delta
being sufficiently small, we have
r\Delta x+\frac{\Delta x}{h}>0
for any fixed
r
. Since
c^{*}_{i,j}+r\Delta x+\frac{\Delta x}{h}
is an admissible but possibly suboptimal control at
(x_{i+1},y_{j})
, we can obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{c\in\mathcal{C}^{\Delta}_{j}(x_{i+1})}\left\{u(c)+(1-\rho h)(1-\lambda_{j}h)\frac{1}{h}\left(\sum_{k}\beta_{k}(x_{i+1}+hs_{i+1,j}(c))V_{k,j}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle u\left(c^{*}_{i,j}+r\Delta x+\frac{\Delta x}{h}\right)+(1-\rho h)(1-\lambda_{j}h)\frac{1}{h}\left(\sum_{k}\beta_{k}\left(x_{i+1}+hs_{i+1,j}\left(c^{*}_{i,j}+r\Delta x+\frac{\Delta x}{h}\right)\right)V_{k,j}\right)

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle u(c^{*}_{i,j})+(1-\rho h)(1-\lambda_{j}h)\frac{1}{h}\left(\sum_{k}\beta_{k}(x_{i}+hs_{i,j}^{*})V_{k,j}\right).

In the second inequality, we used monotonicity of utility
u(\cdot)
and:

\displaystyle x_{i+1}+hs_{i+1,j}\left(c^{*}_{i,j}+r\Delta x+\frac{\Delta x}{h}\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle x_{i}+\Delta x+h\left(r(x_{i}+\Delta x)+y_{j}-\left(c^{*}_{i,j}+r\Delta x+\frac{\Delta x}{h}\right)\right)=x_{i}+hs_{i,j}^{*}.

We then have
Eq.

4.18
and therefore
Eq.

4.17
.
∎

We give the main convergence result. Recall that
{\mathbf{V}}\in B(\mathcal{A}^{\Delta x})^{2}
is the solution to the discrete HJB equation (
4.1
). We define the numerical solution
v^{\Delta}(x):=I[{\mathbf{V}}](x)
by using the interpolation operator (
3.6
).

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
v
be the unique viscosity solution to the system (
2.2
). Then,
v^{\Delta}(x)\to v(x)
locally uniformly on
[{\underline{x}},+\infty)
as
\Delta\to 0
.

Proof.

We define the bounded functions

\bar{{\mathsf{u}}}(x):=\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to x\\
\Delta\to 0\end{subarray}}v^{\Delta}(z),\hskip 10.00002pt\underline{{\mathsf{v}}}(x):=\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to x\\
\Delta\to 0\end{subarray}}v^{\Delta}(z).

By definition we have
\bar{{\mathsf{u}}}(x)\geq\underline{{\mathsf{v}}}(x)
. By using properties of the scheme (monotonicity, stability and consistency) it is standard to show that
\bar{{\mathsf{u}}}
and
\underline{{\mathsf{v}}}
are respectively a sub and supersolution to the system (
2.2
). Using the comparison principle, we obtain
\bar{{\mathsf{u}}}(x)\leq\underline{{\mathsf{v}}}(x)
. Therefore
v(x)=\bar{{\mathsf{u}}}(x)=\underline{{\mathsf{v}}}(x)
is the viscosity solution.
∎

5.1
Approximation of the policies under state constraints

In the theoretical analysis,

is defined as the
\arg\max
in
Eq.

3.16
(
i

can be obtained in at least three different approaches:

•

Solve an optimization problem on each grid
(i,j)
using
\sf{fminbound}
function in python, this is most consistent with analysis but least efficient;

•

Use a discretized control space and then an
\arg\max

•

Use the first order condition from the system (
2.2
)

In the third approach, we use the finite difference derivative

&\frac{V_{i-1,j}^{\Delta}-2V_{i,j}^{\Delta}+V_{i+1,j}^{\Delta}}{2\Delta x},&&0<i<N_{x},\\
&\frac{V_{N_{x},j}^{\Delta}-V_{N_{x}-1,j}^{\Delta}}{\Delta x},&&i=N_{x}.\end{aligned}\right.

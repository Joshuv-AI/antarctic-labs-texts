Improved FPT Approximation Algorithms for TSP
Jingyang Zhao, Zimo Sheng, Mingyu Xiao

Abstract
TSP is a classic and extensively studied problem with numerous real-world applications in artificial intelligence and operations research. It is well-known that TSP admits a constant approximation ratio on metric graphs but becomes NP-hard to approximate within any computable function f ⁡ ( n ) f(n) on general graphs. This disparity highlights a significant gap between the results on metric graphs and general graphs. Recent research has introduced some parameters to measure the “distance” of general graphs from being metric and explored FPT approximation algorithms parameterized by these parameters. Two commonly studied parameters are p p , the number of vertices in triangles violating the triangle inequality, and q q , the minimum number of vertices whose removal results in a metric graph. In this paper, we present improved FPT approximation algorithms with respect to these two parameters. For p p , we propose an FPT algorithm with a 1.5-approximation ratio, improving upon the previous ratio of 2.5. For q q , we significantly enhance the approximation ratio from 11 to 3, advancing the state of the art in both cases.

Introduction

The Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP) is a classic optimization problem with numerous real-world applications
Applegate 2006
, including logistics, genetics, manufacturing, and telecommunications.
Given an edge-weighted complete graph
G=(V,E,w)
with
n
vertices in
V
and a non-negative weight function
w:E\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
on edges, the goal of TSP is to find an optimal (i.e., minimum-weight) TSP tour (i.e., Hamiltonian cycle) that visits each vertex exactly once.
Due to its NP-hardness
Garey and Johnson 1979
, TSP has been extensively studied in the context of approximation algorithms
Saller et al. 2023
;
Traub and Vygen 2025
.

For a minimization problem, an algorithm is called a
\rho
-approximation algorithm if, for any instance
\mathcal{I}
, it computes a solution with an objective value that is at most
\rho
times the optimal value in polynomial time
poly(|\mathcal{I}|)
. Here,
\rho
is called the
approximation ratio
. Moreover, with some parameter
k
, an algorithm is called an FPT
\rho
-approximation algorithm if, for any instance
\mathcal{I}
, it runs in
\mathcal{O}(f(k))\cdot poly(|\mathcal{I}|)
time with an approximation ratio of
\rho
, where
f(k)
is a computable function of
k
.
Both FPT algorithms and FPT approximation algorithms are particularly useful in practice when the parameter
k
is small
Cygan et al. 2015
.

While TSP on general graphs is NP-hard to approximate within any computable function
f(n)

Sahni and Gonzalez 1976
, TSP on metric graphs (metric TSP), where the weight function satisfies the triangle inequality, admits a constant approximation ratio.
Notably, Christofides
Christofides 2022
and Serdyukov
Serdyukov 1978
independently proposed the famous
1.5
-approximation algorithm for metric TSP.
Currently, Karlin
et al.

Karlin et al. 2021
;
Karlin et al. 2023
have improved the approximation ratio to
1.5-

10^{-36}
. Karpinski
et al.

Karpinski et al. 2015
showed that metric TSP is NP-hard to approximate within a ratio of
123/122
.

Given the significant gap between the results on general graphs and metric graphs, many approximation algorithms have been developed for graphs that are “nearly” metric, aiming to measure the “distance” from approximability.
In the literature, two main research directions are commonly explored.
The first direction relaxes the condition on metric functions, where the weight function satisfies the
\tau
-triangle inequality
:
\tau\cdot(w(a,b)+w(b,c))\geq w(a,c)
for all
a,b,c\in V
.
The second direction relaxes the scope of metric functions, where the weight function
partially
satisfies the triangle inequality, i.e., only a subset of all triangles is required to satisfy it.

For the first direction, Andreae and Bandelt
Andreae and Bandelt 1995
gave a
(3\tau^{2}+\tau)/2
-approximation algorithm for TSP.
Bender and Chekuri
Bender and Chekuri 2000
improved this ratio to
4\tau
when
\tau>7/3
, and Mömke
Mömke 2015
further improved the ratio to
3(\tau^{2}+\tau)/4
for small values of
\tau
. Additional improvements
Andreae 2001
;
Böckenhauer et al. 2002
can be found in a survey
Klasing and Mömke 2018
.

For the second direction, Mohan
Mohan et al. 2017
proposed a
3.5
-approximation algorithm for TSP on graphs
G=(V_{1}\cup V_{2},

E,w)
, where both induced subgraphs
G[V_{1}]
and
G[V_{2}]
are required to be metric graphs.
Sumita
et al.

Sumita et al. 2017
proposed a
3
-approximation algorithm for
Subgroup Path Planning
, where the vertex set
V
is partitioned into
k
disjoint groups
V_{1},...,V_{k}
. The goal is to find an optimal TSP tour visiting the vertices in each group
V_{i}
consecutively, where the weight function violates
w(a,b)+w(b,c)\geq w(a,c)
only if both
a
and
c
belong to the same group, while
b
belongs to a different group.
Moreover, if it holds that
|V_{i}|=2
for all
i
, the problem reduces to
Subpath Planning

Safilian et al. 2016
, which admits a
1.5
-approximation algorithm
Sumita et al. 2017
.
Both problems have various applications in AI, such as polishing robots and electronic printing
Safilian et al. 2016
;
Sumita et al. 2017
.

Subsequently, Zhou
et al.

Zhou et al. 2022
;
Zhou et al. 2024
introduced two natural parameters: the number of vertices
p
in
violating triangles
that do not satisfy the triangle inequality, and the minimum number of
violating vertices

q
whose removal results in a metric graph.
Note that
q\leq p
.
Moreover, the parameter
p
can be computed in polynomial time, while
q
can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(3^{q}n^{3})
time using the search-tree method
Zhou et al. 2022
.
For TSP parameterized by
p
, they proposed an FPT
3
-approximation algorithm with a running time of
2^{\mathcal{O}({p\log p})}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
.
For TSP parameterized by
q
, they proposed a
3
-approximation algorithm with a running time of

-approximation algorithm with a running time of
2^{\mathcal{O}({q\log q})}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
. Recently, Bampis
et al.

2.5
for the parameter

, while maintaining the running time of
2^{\mathcal{O}({p\log p})}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
.
Our research follows this line to study FPT approximation algorithms for TSP with respect to these two parameters.

3
TSP Parameterized by the Number of Vertices in Violating Triangles

We first define some notations.
A vertex is called a
bad
vertex if it appears in a violating triangle, and a
good
vertex otherwise.
Let
V=V_{b}\cup V_{g}
, where
V_{b}
(resp.,
V_{g}
) denotes the set of bad (resp., good) vertices in
V
. Note that
|V_{b}|=p
. Assume w.l.o.g. that
\min\{|V_{g}|,|V_{b}|\}\geq 3
.
Otherwise, if

;
Held and Karp 1962

By definition, we have the following property.

Property 1
.

Every triangle containing a good vertex satisfies the triangle inequality.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduce several novel techniques to develop improved FPT approximation algorithms for TSP parameterized by
p
and
q
. An interesting direction for future work would be to extend these techniques to design FPT approximation algorithms for related problems parameterized by
p
and
q
, such as asymmetric TSP
Traub and Vygen 2022
and prize-collecting TSP
Blauth and Nägele 2023
.

When the parameter
p
(or
q
) is a small integer close to zero, our FPT approximation ratio remains unchanged and is larger than the approximation ratio
\alpha
of metric TSP. This naturally leads to the question: is it possible to design an
\alpha
-approximation algorithm, even with a running time of
n^{\mathcal{O}(p+1)}
(or
n^{\mathcal{O}(q+1)}
)? Furthermore, for the parameter
q
, can we develop a constant approximation algorithm with a running time of only
2^{\mathcal{O}(q)}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
?

\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
corresponds to an Eulerian graph, where for each
F\in\mathcal{F}
all vertices in
V(F)
are contracted into a single vertex.
Since
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{F}
only contain edges between good vertices, each bad vertex in
G_{\mathcal{A}}
is a 2-degree vertex.
Moreover, by
CONNECT
, it can be verified that
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
forms an Eulerian graph (see (e) in Fig.
3
). Thus, these two conditions hold.

Last, for each
R\in\mathcal{R}
, it connects all trees in
\mathcal{F}_{R}
. Recall that the chain
R^{*}
, obtained by shortcutting the internal vertices in
R
, satisfies
w(R^{*})\leq w(R)
. Since
R^{*}
corresponds to a TSP tour/path in
\widetilde{G}[\widetilde{V}_{R^{*}}]
and
CONNECT
uses the DP method to compute a minimum TSP tour/path, we know that
E_{R}
, the set of edges w.r.t. the TSP tour/path, has a weight of at most
w(E_{R})\leq w(R^{*})\leq w(R)
. Therefore, we have
w(\mathcal{R}^{\prime})\leq w(R)
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
10

We first prove the following claim.

Claim 14
.

For any tree
T
with any
S\subseteq V(T)
and
|S|\bmod 2=0
, the minimum matching
M_{S}
on
S
satisfies that
w(M_{S})\leq w(T)
.

Proof.

Since
|S|\bmod 2=0
, by arbitrarily pairing all vertices in
S
, we obtain a set of paths
\mathcal{B}
, where each
v\sim v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}
denotes the unique path between
v
and
v^{\prime}
on
T
.
Assume that there exist two paths
v...xy...v^{\prime},u...xy...u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}
sharing at least one edge
xy
. We can modify these two paths into the paths
v\sim u
and
v^{\prime}\sim u^{\prime}
, and the total number of edges on these two paths is reduced by at least 1.
Therefore, by repeatedly performing the above procedure, we can ensure that
\mathcal{B}
is a set of edge-disjoint paths.

By the triangle inequality, we have
w(v,v^{\prime})\leq w(v\sim v^{\prime})
for any
v\sim v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}
. Thus, there exists a matching
\{vv^{\prime}\mid v\sim v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}\}
with weight at most
w(\mathcal{B})\leq w(T)
. Since
M_{S}
is the minimum matching, we have
w(M_{S})\leq w(T)
.
∎

Then, we are ready to prove Lemma
10
.

Lemma
10
.

It holds that
w(\mathcal{M})\leq w(\mathcal{F})
.

Proof.

By Step
5
of ALG.3,
\mathcal{M}
is obtained by finding a minimum matching
M_{F}
in
G[Odd(G_{\mathcal{A}})\cap V(F)]
for each
F\in\mathcal{F}
. Therefore, Lemma
10
follows directly from Lemma
9
and Claim
14
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
11

Lemma
11
.

G^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{A}}
remains an Eulerian graph, where each bad vertex is a 2-degree vertex and each good vertex is incident to at most one bad vertex.

Proof.

In
G^{\prime}_{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{M}
, it can be verified that each bad vertex is a 2-degree vertex and each good vertex is incident to at most two bad vertices.
Moreover, each good vertex that is incident to two bad vertices must be a guessed single anchor.
Therefore, after processing all guessed single anchors in
SHORTCUT
, each bad vertex is a 2-degree vertex and each good vertex is incident to at most one bad vertex in
G^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{A}}
. In fact, each good vertex that is incident to one bad vertex must be a pair anchor.

In
G^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{A}}
, each vertex clearly remains an even degree vertex. Moreover, by
CONNECT
, it can be verified that
G^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{A}}
remains connected, and thus
G^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{A}}
remains an Eulerian graph.
∎

Proof of Lemma
12

We first prove the following claim.

Claim 15
.

Given any Eulerian graph
G^{\prime}
with
\mathcal{O}(m)
edges, where each bad vertex is a 2-degree vertex and each good vertex is incident to at most one bad vertex, there is an
\mathcal{O}(m)
-time algorithm to obtain a TSP tour
T^{\prime}
with a weight of at most
w(G^{\prime})
. Moreover, each good vertex that is incident to no bad vertices in
G^{\prime}
is still incident to no bad vertices in
T^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Since
G^{\prime}
is an Eulerian graph and it consists of
\mathcal{O}(m)
edges, an Eulerian tour
T
can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(m)
time
Cormen et al. 2022
.

Since each bad vertex is a 2-degree vertex, it appears only once in
T
.
If a vertex
a
appears multiple times in
T
, it must be a good vertex.
Moreover, since it is incident to at most one bad vertex in
G^{\prime}
, every appearance of
a
, except for one, is incident to two good vertices in
T^{\prime}
, and each such appearance can be shortcut without increasing the weight.
Thus, we can obtain a TSP tour
T^{\prime}
in
G^{\prime}
with a weight of at most
w(G^{\prime})
, and the running time is
\mathcal{O}(m)
.

Since we shortcut a vertex
a
only if
a
is incident to two good vertices, the set of edges between one good vertex and one bad vertex remain unchanged in
T^{\prime}
.
Therefore, each good vertex that is incident to no bad vertices in
G^{\prime}
is still incident to no bad vertices in
T^{\prime}
.
∎

Then, we are ready to prove Lemma
12
.

Lemma
12
.

Given
G^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{A}}
, there is an
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
-time algorithm to obtain a TSP tour
T_{3}
in
G
with weight at most
w(G^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{A}})
.

Proof.

It can be verified that
|E(\mathcal{A})|\leq n
,
|\mathcal{B}^{\prime}|\leq n
,
|\mathcal{R}^{\prime}|\leq q^{2}\leq n^{2}
,
|E(\mathcal{F})|\leq n
, and
|\mathcal{M}|\leq n
. Thus,
G^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{A}}
contains at most
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
edges.

By Lemma
11
and Claim
15
, we can take shortcuts on
G^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{A}}
to obtain a TSP tour
T
with
w(T)\leq w(G^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{A}})
in
\mathcal{O}(n)
time. Note that
T
is not a TSP tour in
G
since, for each guessed single anchor
x
in Step
5
, it contains both
x
and its copy
x^{\prime}
.
By
SHORTCUT
,
x
is incident to only good vertices in
G^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{A}}
, and thus it is still incident to only good vertices in
T
by Claim
15
. Therefore, we can keep
x^{\prime}
and shortcut
x
without increasing the weight.
Therefore, given
T
, we can further obtain a TSP tour
T_{3}
with
w(T_{3})\leq w(T)\leq w(G^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{A}})
in
G
.

The overall running time is still
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
13

Theorem
13
.

For TSP parameterized by
q
, ALG.3 has an FPT
3
-approximation ratio with runtime
2^{\mathcal{O}(q\log q)}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
.

Proof.

First, we analyze the quality of
T_{3}
.

By
SHORTCUT
and Lemma
12
, we know that

\displaystyle w(T_{3})

\displaystyle\leq w(G^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{A}})

\displaystyle\leq w(G^{\prime}_{\mathcal{A}})

\displaystyle=w(\mathcal{A})+w(\mathcal{B}^{\prime})+w(\mathcal{R}^{\prime})+w(\mathcal{F})+w(\mathcal{M}).

Then, by (
1
), (
3
), Lemmas
8
and
9
, and Lemma
10
, we have

\displaystyle w(\mathcal{A})+w(\mathcal{B}^{\prime})+w(\mathcal{R}^{\prime})+w(\mathcal{F})+w(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\leq w(\mathcal{A})+w(\mathcal{B})+w(\mathcal{R})+w(\mathcal{F})+w(\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle\leq 3(w(\mathcal{A})+w(\mathcal{B})+w(\mathcal{R}))

\displaystyle=3\cdot\mbox{OPT}.

Next, we analyze the running time.

Step
1
takes
\mathcal{O}(2^{q}\cdot q!)
loops, where each loop takes
n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
time
Bampis et al. 2024
.
Step
2
takes
\mathcal{O}(n^{2}\log n)
time
Khachay and Neznakhina 2016
.
By Lemma
8
, Step
3
takes
\mathcal{O}(2^{q}\cdot q^{2q}\cdot(4q^{2})^{2q})
loops, where each loop takes
n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
time.
By Lemma
9
, Step
4
takes
2^{\mathcal{O}(q\log q)}
loops, where each loop takes
\mathcal{O}(q^{2}\cdot 2^{q})\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
time.
Step
5
takes
\mathcal{O}(n^{3})
time
Lawler 1976
.
By Lemma
12
, Step
6
takes
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
time.

Therefore, there are
2^{\mathcal{O}(q\log q)}
loops in total, where each loop takes at most
2^{\mathcal{O}(q)}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
time.
Thus, the overall running time is
2^{\mathcal{O}(q\log q)}\cdot 2^{\mathcal{O}(q)}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}\subseteq 2^{\mathcal{O}(q\log q)}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
.
∎

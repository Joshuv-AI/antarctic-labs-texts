Counterexamples to the conjecture of the upper bound of the derivative of a rational Bézier curve Journal: Nuclear Physics B
Mao Shi

Abstract
In this paper, we present counterexamples to the upper bound of the first-order derivative of rational Bézier curves, as proposed by Li et al. in Applied Mathematics and Computation 219 (2013) 10425-10433, and further investigate the supremum of derivatives of all orders for such curves.

Introduction

Rational Bézier curves are fundamental in geometric design, with their derivative bounds playing a crucial role in both theory and computation. In computational settings, derivative bounds are vital for maintaining numerical stability in curve evaluation, subdivision, and optimization. They help prevent error accumulation, support adaptive meshing strategies, and enhance collision detection

Supremum

Shi
[
8
]
presented the explicit formulas for the high-order derivatives of rational Bézier curves and their bounds. We employ the approach to derive explicit expressions for the first-order derivative of rational Bézier curves and establish its supremum. The method can be conveniently extended to the calculation of the supremum of any-order derivatives.

Theorem 1
.

The explicit expression for the first derivative of a rational Bézier curve is

{\bf{r^{\prime}}}(t)=\frac{{n\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2n}{{\bf{\hat{P}}}_{i,2n}^{[1]}}B_{i}^{2n}(t)}}{{\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2n}{\omega_{i,2n}^{[1]}}B_{i}^{2n}(t)}}=\frac{{\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2n}{{\bf{Q}}_{i,2n}^{[1]}}\omega_{i,2n}^{[1]}B_{i}^{2n}(t)}}{{\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2n}{\omega_{i,2n}^{[1]}}B_{i}^{2n}(t)}},

where the parameters are calculated as follows

1. Weights
\omega_{i,2n}^{[1]}
(
i=0,\dots,2n
)

\omega_{i,2n}^{[1]}=\sum_{j=\max(0,i-n)}^{\min(i,n)}\frac{\binom{n}{j}\binom{n}{i-j}}{\binom{2n}{i}}\omega_{j}\omega_{i-j}.

2. Intermediate control points
\boldsymbol{P}_{j,2n}^{[1]}
(
j=0,\dots,2n-1
)

\boldsymbol{P}_{j,2n}^{[1]}=\sum_{h=\max(0,j-n)}^{\min(n-1,j)}\frac{\binom{n-1}{h}\binom{n}{j-h}}{\binom{2n-1}{j}}\left[\omega_{h+1}\omega_{j-h}(\mathbf{p}_{h+1}-\mathbf{p}_{j-h})+\omega_{h}\omega_{j-h}(\mathbf{p}_{j-h}-\mathbf{p}_{h})\right],

and

\boldsymbol{\hat{P}}_{i,2n}^{[1]}=\sum_{j=\max(0,i-1)}^{\min(2n-1,i)}\frac{\binom{2n-1}{j}}{\binom{2n}{i}}\boldsymbol{P}_{j,2n}^{[1]}.

3. Control points
{\bf{Q}}_{i,2n}^{[1]}
(
i=0,\dots,2n
)

{\bf{Q}}_{i,2n}^{[1]}=\frac{{n{\bf{\hat{P}}}_{i,2n}^{[1]}}}{{\omega_{i,2n}^{[1]}}}.

Conclusion

This paper presents several counterexamples to the conjecture on the upper bounds of derivatives of rational Bézier curves. Nevertheless, we remain interested in the following issues related to this conjecture:

1.

What is the maximum value of
n
for which the conjecture fails?

2.

What is the mathematical principle underlying its failure? Could it be related to the non-uniform convergence of rational Bézier curves with respect to weights
[
14
]
?

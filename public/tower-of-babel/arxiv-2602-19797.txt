Abstract
. Let A A be a unital associative PI-algebra over a field of characteristic zero. We study which partitions λ \lambda appear with nonzero multiplicities in the cocharacter sequence of A A for several classes of algebras A A . Berele defines the eventual arm width ω 0 ​ ( A ) \omega_{0}(A) to be the maximal integer d d so that if λ \lambda appears with nonzero multiplicity in the cocharacter sequence of A A , then λ \lambda can have at most d d parts arbitrarily large. Berele also shows that if A A is Lie nilpotent, then ω 0 ​ ( A ) = 1 \omega_{0}(A)=1 . In the first part of this paper, we show that if A A is unital, then ω 0 ​ ( A ) = 1 \omega_{0}(A)=1 if and only if A A is Lie nilpotent. To prove this statement, we show that the algebra of proper polynomials B n ​ ( A ) B_{n}(A) is finite dimensional if and only if A A is Lie nilpotent. In the second part, we give a bound on the nonzero multiplicities λ \lambda in the cocharacter sequence of A A , when the T-ideal of identities of A A is equal to a product of T-ideals generated by long commutators. As an application, we show that for a Lie nilpotent algebra A A , the nonzero multiplicities m λ ​ ( A ) m_{\lambda}(A) correspond to partitions λ \lambda which are supported in step-like diagrams in which the number of steps grows with the index of Lie nilpotency. Finally, we give also some applications to the noncommutative invariant theory of the special linear group SL ⁡ ( n ) \mathrm{SL}(n) .

Introduction

In this paper, all algebras are associative and unital and

-module structure of

It was independently shown by Berele and Drensky that the question of finding the multiplicities

in the cocharacter sequence (

\mathrm{GL}(n):=\mathrm{GL}(V,K)

inherits the

\displaystyle F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})\cong_{\mathrm{GL}(n)}\bigoplus_{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})}m_{\lambda}(I)V_{\lambda}.

2.
On the eventual arm width of PI-algebras

In this section, using the language of representation theory of
\mathrm{GL}(n)
, we discuss properties of the eventual arm width of unital associative PI-algebras. We start by introducing some notations.
We say that a commutator
[x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{r}}]\in K\langle X\rangle
is pure if all the entries in the commutator are elements of the set
X
. A polynomial in
K\langle X\rangle
is called proper if it is a linear combination of products of pure commutators. We denote by
B_{n}
the subalgebra of
K\langle X_{n}\rangle
of proper polynomials. Then
B_{n}
is a
\mathrm{GL}(n)
-submodule of
K\langle X_{n}\rangle
. Let
I
be a T-ideal in
K\langle X\rangle
and let as before
\mathfrak{V}_{I}
denote the variety of associative algebras satisfying the identities from
I
. We consider the natural quotient map

K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle\rightarrow F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})=K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle/(K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle\cap I).

By
B_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
we denote the image of
B_{n}
under the above map. If
I=T(A)
for some PI-algebra
A
, we use also the notation
B_{n}(A)=B_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
. By a theorem of Drensky (
[
12
]
), we have the following decomposition of
F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
as a
\mathrm{GL}(n)
-module:

(3)

\displaystyle F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})\cong_{\mathrm{GL}(n)}B_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})\otimes S(V),

where again
V
is the vector space spanned by the elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and
S(V)
denotes the symmetric algebra of
V
. From Drensky’s theorem it follows that
\omega_{0}(A)\geq 1
for any associative unital PI-algebra
A
.

We recall that a unital associative algebra
A
is called Lie nilpotent of index at most
p
if
A
satisfies the identity
[x_{1},\dots,x_{p+1}]=0
, or in other words, if
T(A)\supseteq I_{p+1}
.

Let us denote by
\mathfrak{N}_{p}
the variety of all Lie nilpotent associative algebras of index at most
p
. In the above notations,
\mathfrak{N}_{p}=\mathfrak{V}_{I_{p+1}}=\operatorname{var}(I_{p+1})
, is the variety of all associative algebras satisfying the identities from
I_{p+1}
.

The following statement now holds.

Theorem 2.1
.

For any T-ideal
I
, the algebra
B_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
is a finite-dimensional subalgebra of
F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
if and only if
F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
satisfies the polynomial identity
[x_{1},\dots,x_{p}]
for some
p
, i.e., if and only if
I_{p}\subseteq I
for some
p
.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem 3.3 from
[
15
]
that
B_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p})
is a finite-dimensional algebra for every
p
. Hence, for every T-ideal
I
with
I_{p}\subseteq I
we have that
B_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
is also a finite-dimensional algebra. Thus, the “if” direction of the statement is clear. To show the “only if” direction, we use an argument that was also used in the proof of Theorem 3.1 from
[
11
]
. For any
T
-ideal
I
of
K\left\langle X\right\rangle
either
F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
satisfies the polynomial identity
[x_{1},\dots,x_{p}]
for some
p
or
I
is contained in
I_{2}I_{2}
. Let us consider the case
I\subseteq I_{2}I_{2}
. Then we have a natural surjective homomorphism from
K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle/(I\cap K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle)
to
K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle/(I_{2}I_{2}\cap K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle)
. The image of
B_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
under this homomorphism is the subalgebra of proper polynomials in
K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle/(I_{2}I_{2}\cap K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle)
, which is known to be infinite-dimensional (see, e.g.
[
17
]
). Hence
B_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
is also infinite-dimensional.
∎

The above statement leads to the following corollary.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
A
be a unital associative PI-algebra. Then,
\omega_{0}(A)=1
if and only if
I_{p}\subseteq T(A)
for some positive integer
p
, i.e., if and only if
A
is Lie nilpotent.

Proof.

Let
I=T(A)
.
Suppose at first that there exists a positive integer
p
such that
I_{p}\subseteq I
. Then, Theorem
1.2
implies that
\omega_{0}(A)\leq\omega_{0}(I_{p})=1
. Hence, this direction of the statement follows.

Next, assume that there is no integer
p
such that
I_{p}\subseteq I
. Then, Theorem
2.1
implies that
B_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
is infinite-dimensional. Let us write the
\mathrm{GL}(n)
-module decomposition of
B_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
in the following way:

B_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})\cong_{\mathrm{GL}(n)}\bigoplus_{\mu}k_{\mu}(I)V_{\mu},

where
V_{\mu}
denotes the irreducible
\mathrm{GL}(n)
-module corresponding to the partition
\mu
and by
k_{\mu}(I)
we denote the corresponding multiplicity.

Suppose on the contrary that
\omega_{0}(A)=1
. This implies that there exists an integer
l
such that for all partitions
\lambda
which appear with nonzero multiplicity in the decomposition (
2
), we have that
\lambda_{2}\leq l
.
However, the infinite dimensionality of
B_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
implies that there exists a partition
\mu
with
\mu_{1}\geq l+1
such that
V_{\mu}
appears in the decomposition of
B_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
. Hence, by Equation (
3
),
V_{\mu}\otimes S(V)
appears in the decomposition of
K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle/I
. This implies that there exists
\lambda
with
\lambda_{2}\geq l+1
such that
m_{\lambda}(I)\neq 0
. This contradiction completes the proof.
∎

Recall that the
m
-th codimension of a PI-algebra

is defined as

c_{m}(A)=\dim P_{m}(A).

In
[
14
]
, Giambruno and Zaicev prove that if

is any PI-algebra, then the limit

\exp(A)=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt[m]{c_{m}(A)}

exists and is a non-negative integer (see
[

]
, Theorem 6.5.2). This limit is called the PI exponent of

. In
[
5
]
, Berele shows that the following inequalities hold

\omega_{0}(A)\leq\exp(A)\leq\omega_{0}(A)+\omega_{1}(A).

Hence, Corollary
2.2
implies that if

is unital and not Lie nilpotent and is such that
\exp(A)=2
, then necessarily
\omega_{0}(A)=2
.

On the cocharacter sequence of
A
when

In this section we discuss the multiplicities
m_{\lambda}(A)
(or
m_{\lambda}(I))
from (

) and (

) when

is a product of the form
I=I_{p_{1}+1}\cdots I_{p_{k}+1}
. These multiplicities are explicitly known in several particular cases. It is well-known that when

is the algebra of

upper triangular matrices with coefficients from the field

, then

. When

, the cocharacters of

are explicitly known (see e.g.,
[

]
or
[

]
) and the case of general

was studied by Boumova and Drensky in
[
6
]
with explicit results for “large” partitions
\lambda
. In addition, if
A=U_{k}(E)
, the algebra of
k\times k
upper triangular matrices with coefficients from the infinite-dimensional Grassmann algebra
E
, then
T(A)=\underbrace{I_{3}\cdots I_{3}}_{k}
. The cocharacters of
U_{2}(E)
were computed by Centrone in
[
7
]
. In the same paper, Centrone computes also the cocharacters of the following matrix algebra

\displaystyle G=\begin{pmatrix}E&E\\
0&E^{0}\end{pmatrix},

where
E^{0}
is the even part of the Grassmann algebra
E
with the usual
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading. For the algebra
G
it holds that
T(G)=I_{3}I_{2}
. The general case of cocharacters of
U_{k}(E)
was considered in
[
8
]
.

In this section, we compute
\omega_{0}(A)
,
\omega_{1}(A)
and
s_{2}(A)
from Theorem
1.2
in the general case when
T(A)=I_{p_{1}+1}\cdots I_{p_{k}+1}
.

We recall that if
R=\bigoplus_{\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{\geq 0}}R^{\mu}
is an algebra with a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{\geq 0}
-grading we define the Hilbert series of
R
with respect to the
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{\geq 0}
-grading to be the following formal power series

H(R,t_{1},\dots,t_{n})=\sum_{\mu\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{\geq 0}}\operatorname{dim}R^{\mu}t_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{\mu_{n}}.

This notion generalizes the standard notion of a Hilbert (or Poincaré) series for an algebra with a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading.

For shortness, we denote
T=\{t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\}
and we write for the Hilbert series
H(R,T)=H(R,t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
.

The relatively free algebra
F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})=K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle/(I\cap K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle)
has a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{\geq 0}
-grading which comes from the weight space decomposition as a
\mathrm{GL}(n)
-module and hence its Hilbert series coincides with its character as a
\mathrm{GL}(n)
-module. In other words, if

F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})\cong_{\mathrm{GL}(n)}\bigoplus_{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})}m_{\lambda}(I)V_{\lambda}

as in Equation (
2
), then for the Hilbert series of
F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
we have the expression

H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}),T)=\sum_{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})}m_{\lambda}(I)S_{\lambda}(T),

where
S_{\lambda}(T)=S_{\lambda}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
denotes the Schur polynomial corresponding to the partition
\lambda
. Thus, the Hilbert series
H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}),T)
gives useful information about the multiplicities
m_{\lambda}(I)
.

Following
[
7
]
, we define also the proper Hilbert series of
F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
to be the Hilbert series of the subalgebra of proper polynomials in
F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
, i.e.,

H^{B}(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}),T)=H(B_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}),T).

By Equation (
3
), we have the following relation between the Hilbert series and the proper Hilbert series of a relatively free algebra:

(4)

\displaystyle H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}),T)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1-t_{i}}H^{B}(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}),T).

Next, when a
T
-ideal
I
is a product of two
T
-ideals
I=J_{1}J_{2}
, then the following relation between the Hilbert series was given by Formanek (
[
13
]
):

(5)

\displaystyle H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}),T)=

\displaystyle H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{1}}),T)+H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{2}}),T)+

\displaystyle(S_{(1)}(T)-1)H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{1}}),T)H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{2}}),T),

where
S_{(1)}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})=t_{1}+\dots+t_{n}
denotes the Schur polynomial corresponding to the partition
\lambda=(1,0,\dots,0)
.

When a
T
-ideal
I
is a product of several
T
-ideals
I=J_{1}\cdots J_{k}
for some
k\geq 2
, then we can iterate Equation (
5
) to obtain the following:

(6)

\displaystyle H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}),T)=\sum_{r=1}^{k}(S_{(1)}-1)^{r-1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq k}H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{i_{1}}}),T)\cdots H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{i_{r}}}),T).

Using Equations (
4
) and (
5
), when
I=J_{1}J_{2}
one can easily derive a relation between the proper Hilbert series of
F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
on the one hand and the proper Hilbert series of
F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{1}})
and
F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{2}})
on the other hand. The relation is as follows (see also
[
7
]
):

(7)

\displaystyle H^{B}(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}),T)=

\displaystyle H^{B}(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{1}}),T)+H^{B}(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{2}}),T)+

\displaystyle\frac{S_{(1)}(T)-1}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-t_{i})}H^{B}(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{1}}),T)H^{B}(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{2}}),T).

Formula (
5
) together with Littlewood-Richardson rule for multiplication of Schur polynomials lead to the following observation.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
A
,
B_{1}
, and
B_{2}
be PI-algebras such that
T(A)=T(B_{1})T(B_{2})
. Let
\omega_{0}
,
\omega_{1}
,
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
be as before. Then, the following relations hold:

(1)

\omega_{0}(A)=\omega_{0}(B_{1})+\omega_{0}(B_{2})
;

(2)

\omega_{1}(A)=\omega_{1}(B_{1})+\omega_{1}(B_{2})
;

(3)

s_{1}(A)=s_{1}(B_{1})+s_{1}(B_{2})+1
;

(4)

s_{2}(A)=s_{2}(B_{1})+s_{2}(B_{2})+1
;

Proof.

We will prove first part (1) and part (4).
Let us denote
I=T(A)
and
J_{i}=T(B_{i})
for
i=1,2
. To show that
\omega_{0}(A)\geq\omega_{0}(B_{1})+\omega_{0}(B_{2})
we need to show that for each positive integer
\ell
, there exists a partition
\lambda
with
\lambda_{\omega_{0}(B_{1})+\omega_{0}(B_{2})}>\ell
such that
m_{\lambda}(A)\neq 0
. In other words, the Schur function
S_{\lambda}(T)
appears with nonzero multiplicity in the Hilbert series
H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}),T)
. We take partitions
\mu
and
\nu
such that
\mu_{\omega_{0}(B_{1})}>\ell
and
\nu_{\omega_{0}(B_{2})}>\ell
and such that
S_{\mu}(T)
appears with nonzero multiplicity in the Hilbert series
H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{1}}),T)
and
S_{\nu}(T)
appears with nonzero multiplicity in the Hilbert series
H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{2}}),T)
. We can do that by the definition of
\omega_{0}
. Then, Littlewood-Richardson rule implies that in the product
S_{\mu}(T)S_{\nu}(T)
there will be a polynomial
S_{\lambda}(T)
with the desired properties of
\lambda
.

To show that
\omega_{0}(A)\leq\omega_{0}(B_{1})+\omega_{0}(B_{2})
and prove also part (4) we need to show that for every partition
\lambda
for which
S_{\lambda}(T)
appears with nonzero multiplicity in
H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}),T)
, it holds that
\lambda_{\omega_{0}(B_{1})+\omega_{0}(B_{2})+1}\leq s_{2}(B_{1})+s_{2}(B_{2})+1
.

Let us take a partition
\lambda
, such that
S_{\lambda}(T)
appears with nonzero multiplicity in
H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}),T)
. Then, Formula (
5
) implies that
S_{\lambda}(T)
appears with nonzero multiplicity at least in one of the expressions
H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{1}}),T)
,
H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{2}}),T)
or
(S_{(1)}(T)-1)H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{1}}),T)H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{2}}),T)
. If
S_{\lambda}(T)
appears with nonzero multiplicity in
H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{i}}),T)
for
i=1,2
, then
\lambda_{\omega_{0}(B_{i})+1}\leq s_{2}(B_{i})
which implies the desired property.

Let us now consider the case when
S_{\lambda}(T)
appears with nonzero multiplicity in
S_{(1)}(T)H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{1}}),T)H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{2}}),T)
. Then,
S_{\lambda}(T)
appears in a product of Schur polynomials of the form
S_{(1)}(T)S_{\mu}(T)S_{\nu}(T)
, such that
S_{\mu}(T)
appears with nonzero multiplicity in
H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{1}}),T)
and
S_{\nu}(T)
appears with nonzero multiplicity in
H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{2}}),T)
. By using again Littlewood-Richardson rule we prove the desired property.

To prove parts (2) and (3), we use the following symmetry of the Littlewood-Richardson rule (which can be deduced e.g. from
[
16
]
, Chapter I.3): Let
\lambda
,
\mu
, and
\nu
be partitions and let
\lambda^{\prime}
,
\mu^{\prime}
, and
\nu^{\prime}
denote the conjugate partitions. If we have that

S_{\mu}(T)S_{\nu}(T)=\sum_{\lambda}c^{\lambda}_{\mu,\nu}S_{\lambda}(T)

and

S_{\mu^{\prime}}(T)S_{\nu^{\prime}}(T)=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}c^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}S_{\lambda^{\prime}}(T)

then
c^{\lambda}_{\mu,\nu}=c^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}
.

To show that
\omega_{1}(A)\geq\omega_{1}(B_{1})+\omega_{1}(B_{2})
we need to show that for each positive integer
\ell
, there exists a partition
\lambda
with
\lambda^{\prime}_{\omega_{1}(B_{1})+\omega_{1}(B_{2})}>\ell
such that
m_{\lambda}(A)\neq 0
. We take partitions
\mu
and
\nu
such that
\mu^{\prime}_{\omega_{1}(B_{1})}>\ell
and
\nu^{\prime}_{\omega_{1}(B_{2})}>\ell
and such that
S_{\mu}(T)
appears with nonzero multiplicity in the Hilbert series
H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{1}}),T)
and
S_{\nu}(T)
appears with nonzero multiplicity in the Hilbert series
H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{2}}),T)
. We can do that by the definition of
\omega_{1}
. Then, in the product
S_{\mu^{\prime}}(T)S_{\nu^{\prime}}(T)
there will be a polynomial
S_{\lambda^{\prime}}(T)
with
\lambda^{\prime}_{\omega_{1}(B_{1})+\omega_{1}(B_{2})}>\ell
. By the above mentioned symmetry of the Littlewood-Richardson rule, if
S_{\lambda^{\prime}}(T)
appears in the product
S_{\mu^{\prime}}(T)S_{\nu^{\prime}}(T)
, then
S_{\lambda}(T)
will appear in the product
S_{\mu}(T)S_{\nu}(T)
and hence
m_{\lambda}(A)\neq 0
. This shows that
\omega_{1}(A)\geq\omega_{1}(B_{1})+\omega_{1}(B_{2})
.
The rest of part (2) and part (3) is proven analogously.
∎

Iterating the above proposition we obtain:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
A
,
B_{1},\dots,B_{k}
for some
k\geq 2
be PI-algebras with
T(A)=T(B_{1})\cdots T(B_{k})
.Then

•

\omega_{0}(A)=\omega_{0}(B_{1})+\cdots+\omega_{0}(B_{k})
;

•

\omega_{1}(A)=\omega_{1}(B_{1})+\cdots+\omega_{1}(B_{k})
;

•

s_{1}(A)=s_{1}(B_{1})+\cdots+s_{1}(B_{k})+k-1
;

•

s_{2}(A)=s_{2}(B_{1})+\cdots+s_{2}(B_{k})+k-1
;

Proposition
3.2
together with Theorem
1.2
and the considerations after it imply the following corollary.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
A
be a PI-algebra with
T(A)=I
such that
I
is a product of
k
ideals
I=I_{p_{1}+1}\cdots I_{p_{k}+1}
for some positive integers
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
. Let also
k<n
. Then the following hold:

•

\omega_{0}(A)=k
;

•

\omega_{1}(A)=k
(This is in the case when
p_{i}\geq 2
for all
i=1,\dots,k
);

•

s_{2}(A)=p_{1}+\cdots+p_{k}-1
;

In particular, Corollary
3.3
implies the following. If
I=I_{p_{1}+1}\cdots I_{p_{k}+1}
and
\lambda
is a partition such that
m_{\lambda}(I)\neq 0
then
\lambda_{k+1}\leq p_{1}+\cdots+p_{k}-1
. Using Equation (
6
), we obtain a further restriction on the partition
\lambda
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
I=I_{p_{1}+1}\cdots I_{p_{k}+1}
and let
\lambda
be a partition such that
m_{\lambda}(I)\neq 0
. Then,
\lambda_{k+1}\leq p_{1}+\cdots+p_{k}-1
and
\lambda_{2k}\leq p_{1}+\cdots+p_{k}-k
.

Proof.

We need to show that
\lambda_{2k}\leq p_{1}+\cdots+p_{k}-k
. We choose an integer
r
between
1
and
k
. Let
\lambda
be a partition such that
S_{\lambda}(T)
appears with non-zero multiplicity in the product

(S_{(1)})^{r-1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq k}H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{i_{1}}}),T)\cdots H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{J_{i_{r}}}),T).

Therefore, there exist integers
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq k
and partitions
\mu_{i_{1}},\dots,\mu_{i_{r}}
such that
\mu_{i_{j}}\in H(1,p_{i_{j}}-1)
and such that
S_{\lambda}(T)
appears with non-zero multiplicity in the product

(S_{(1)})^{r-1}S_{\mu_{i_{1}}}(T)\cdots S_{\mu_{i_{r}}}(T).

Hence,
\lambda
is obtained by adding
r-1
boxes to a partition
\tilde{\lambda}
which is contained in the hook
H(r,p_{i_{1}}+\cdots+p_{i_{r}}-r)
. Thus,
\lambda_{r+1}\leq p_{i_{1}}+\cdots+p_{i_{r}}-1
and
\lambda_{2r}\leq p_{i_{1}}+\cdots+p_{i_{r}}-r
.

We can do these considerations for each
r
between
1
and
k
and when
r=k
we obtain the statement of the proposition. ∎

The fact that the subalgebra of proper polynomials
B_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p})
in
F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p})
is finite-dimensional for every
p\geq 1
implies that the proper Hilbert series
H^{B}(F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p}),T)
is a polynomial. Therefore, using Formula (
7
) and the relation between the proper Hilbert series and the Hilbert series given by Formula (
4
) we obtain a general form for the Hilbert series and the proper Hilbert series of any relatively free algebra
F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
where
I
is a
T
-ideal of the form
I=I_{p_{1}+1}\cdots I_{p_{k}+1}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
I
be a
T
-ideal such that
I
is a product of
k
ideals
I=I_{p_{1}+1}\cdots I_{p_{k}+1}
for some positive integers
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
. Let also
k<n
. Then the following hold:

(i)

H^{B}(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}),T)=P_{1}(T)+\frac{P_{2}(T)}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-t_{i})}+\frac{P_{3}(T)}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-t_{i})^{2}}+\dots+\frac{P_{k}(T)}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-t_{i})^{k-1}};

(ii)

H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}),T)=\frac{P_{1}(T)}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-t_{i})}+\frac{P_{2}(T)}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-t_{i})^{2}}+\frac{P_{3}(T)}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-t_{i})^{3}}+\dots+\frac{P_{k}(T)}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-t_{i})^{k}},

where
P_{1}(T),\dots,P_{k}(T)
are the following polynomials

\displaystyle P_{1}(T)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}H^{B}(F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p_{i}}),T);

\displaystyle P_{2}(T)=(S_{(1)}(T)-1)\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}H^{B}(F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p_{i}}),T)H^{B}(F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p_{j}}),T);

\displaystyle\dots\dots

\displaystyle P_{r}(T)=(S_{(1)}(T)-1)^{r-1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{r}\leq k}H^{B}(F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p_{i_{1}}}),T)\cdots H^{B}(F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p_{i_{r}}}),T);

\displaystyle\dots\dots

\displaystyle P_{k}(T)=(S_{(1)}(T)-1)^{k-1}H^{B}(F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p_{1}}),T)\cdots H^{B}(F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p_{k}}),T).

As we already mentioned above,
T
-ideals
I
that are products of the form
I=\underbrace{I_{2}\cdots I_{2}}_{k\text{ times }}
and the corresponding cocharacters were studied by Boumova and Drensky in
[
6
]
with explicit results for “large” partitions
\lambda
(see
[
6
]
for the precise definition of “large”). We notice that when
I=\underbrace{I_{2}\cdots I_{2}}_{k\text{ times }}
, i.e., when
p_{1}=\dots=p_{k}=1
, we have that
H^{B}(F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p_{i_{r}}}),T)=1
for all
r=1,\dots,k
. Therefore, for the polynomials
P_{r}(T)
for
r=1,\dots,k
, using Proposition
3.5
, we obtain the following:

\displaystyle P_{r}(T)=

\displaystyle(S_{(1)}(T)-1)^{r-1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{r}\leq k}H^{B}(F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p_{i_{1}}}),T)\cdots H^{B}(F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p_{i_{r}}}),T)=

\displaystyle(S_{(1)}(T)-1)^{r-1}\binom{k}{r}.

Therefore, Proposition
3.5
leads to the following expression for the Hilbert series of
F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
, which coincides with the results from
[
6
]
.

H(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}),T)=\sum_{r=1}^{k}\binom{k}{r}\frac{(S_{(1)}(T)-1)^{r-1}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-t_{i})^{r}}.

4.
Applications to the cocharacters of Lie nilpotent associative algebras

We recall the following well-known result on product of commutators:

Theorem 4.1

(
[
2
]
,
[
10
]
)
.

For positive integers
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
such that at least one of them is odd it holds that

I_{m_{1}}I_{m_{2}}\subset I_{m_{1}+m_{2}-1}.

Iterating the above result we obtain the following statement (see also
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
).

Proposition 4.2
.

For any
k\geq 2
, if at most one of the positive integers
m_{1},\dots,m_{k}
is even then

I_{m_{1}}\cdots I_{m_{k}}\subset I_{m_{1}+\dots+m_{k}-k+1}.

Therefore, if a
T
-ideal
I
is expressed as
I=I_{p_{1}+1}\cdots I_{p_{k}+1}
, where at most one of
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
is a positive odd integer, we have a natural surjective homomorphism of relatively free algebras

f:K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle/I_{p_{1}+1}\cdots I_{p_{k}+1}\rightarrow K\left\langle X_{n}\right\rangle/I_{p_{1}+\dots+p_{k}+1}.

and this homomorphism restricts naturally to the respective subalgebras of proper polynomials
B_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I_{p_{1}+1}\cdots I_{p_{k}+1}})
and
B_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p_{1}+\dots+p_{k}})
. The map
f
is
\mathrm{GL}(n)
-equivariant. By Corollary
3.3
and Proposition
3.4
, if
m_{\lambda}(I)\neq 0
for some partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n})
, then
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}
can be arbitrarily large,
\lambda_{k+1}\leq p_{1}+\dots+p_{k}-1
, and
\lambda_{2k}\leq p_{1}+\dots+p_{k}-k
. This observation leads to the following proposition, which is an improvement of Theorem 3.3 from
[
15
]
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Consider the relatively free algebra
F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p})
and let
\lambda
be a partition such that
m_{\lambda}(I_{p+1})\neq 0
in the decomposition

F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p})\cong_{\mathrm{GL}(n)}\bigoplus_{\lambda}m_{\lambda}(I_{p+1})V_{\lambda}.

Then, for each
1\leq k\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
such that
p>2(k-1)
it holds that
\lambda_{2k}\leq p-k
.

Proof.

For
k=1
the statement follows from Theorem 3.3 from
[
15
]
.
Next, we fix one
k
such that
2\leq k\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
and
p>2(k-1)
. Then, the integer
p
can be represented in the form

p=\underbrace{2+\dots+2}_{k-1}+p-2(k-1).

Hence, if we set
p_{1}=2
,
\dots
,
p_{k-1}=2
and
p_{k}=p-2(k-1)
we have that
p_{1}+\dots+p_{k}=p
. Therefore,
I_{p_{1}+1}\cdots I_{p_{k}+1}\subset I_{p+1}
. Hence, the restriction
\lambda_{2k}\leq p-k
follows from the observations before the statement.
∎

If we take
p=3
and consider the relatively free algebra
F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{3})
then Proposition
4.3
implies the following: If

then

and

. These results agree with the

-module decomposition of

, which can be deduced from the results of Volichenko in

\lambda_{4}=0
. And in addition, if

\lambda_{2}=1
.

Similarly, if we take
p=4
, then for

and

\lambda

We also notice that for

, i.e., when we consider

. Namely, if

,

.

For the conjugate partition
\lambda^{\prime}
, using similar arguments we obtain the following bounds.

Proposition 4.4
.

Consider again the relatively free algebra
F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p})
and let
\lambda
be a partition such that

in the decomposition

F_{n}(\mathfrak{N}_{p})\cong_{\mathrm{GL}(n)}\bigoplus_{\lambda}m_{\lambda}(I_{p+1})V_{\lambda}.

3.2

.
∎

5.
Applications to Noncommutative invariant theory

The importance of the numbers
\omega_{0}(A)
,
\omega_{1}(A)
,
s_{1}(A)
, and
s_{2}(A)
can be seen also when considering algebras of invariants. In this section we consider the algebra of
\mathrm{SL}(n)
-invariants
(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}))^{\mathrm{SL}(n)}
, for an arbitrary
T
-ideal
I
. The following theorem can be deduced from
[
3
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

[
3
]

Let
R
be a finitely generated graded algebra over
K
which is also a
\mathrm{GL}(n)
-module with the following decomposition

R=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}R^{i}=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\bigoplus_{\lambda}m_{i}(\lambda)V_{\lambda}.

Let

. Then the Hilbert series of the algebra of invariants

H(R^{G},t)=\sum_{i\geq 0}(\sum_{\lambda}m_{i}(\lambda))t^{i},

where the second sum is over all
\lambda
with
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\dots=\lambda_{n}
;

When we apply the above theorem to the case

for some
T
-ideal

, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
A
be a PI-algebra with
T(A)=I
and let the integers
\omega_{0}(A)
,
\omega_{1}(A)
,
s_{1}(A)
and
s_{2}(A)
be as before. If

, then
(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}))^{\mathrm{SL}(n)}
is finite-dimensional. In particular, the Hilbert series
H((F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}))^{\mathrm{SL}(n)},t)
is a polynomial and

\displaystyle\deg H((F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}))^{\mathrm{SL}(n)},t)\leq ns_{2}(A).

Furthermore, if
n>s_{1}(A)
then

\displaystyle\deg H((F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}))^{\mathrm{SL}(n)},t)\leq n\omega_{1}(A).

Proof.

We denote by
(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}))^{(i)}
the homogeneous component of total degree
i
in
F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
. Then we can write the decomposition of
F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})
as a
\mathrm{GL}(n)
-module in the following way:

F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I})=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}))^{(i)}\cong_{\mathrm{GL}(n)}\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\bigoplus_{\lambda}m_{i,\lambda}V_{\lambda},

where
|\lambda|=i
and by the definitions of the numbers
\omega_{0}
,
\omega_{1}
,
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
,
\lambda_{\omega_{0}(A)+1}\leq s_{2}(A)
and
\lambda_{s_{1}(A)+1}\leq\omega_{1}(A)
.

Then Theorem
5.1
implies that

H((F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}))^{\mathrm{SL}(n)},t)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\left(\sum_{\lambda}m_{i,\lambda}\right)t^{i},

where the second sum runs over partitions
\lambda
with
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\dots=\lambda_{n}
such that
|\lambda|=i
,
\lambda_{\omega_{0}(A)+1}\leq s_{2}(A)
and
\lambda_{s_{1}(A)+1}\leq\omega_{1}(A)
. Hence, if
\omega_{0}(A)<n\leq s_{1}(A)
the largest partition
\lambda
which satisfies these conditions is
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\dots=\lambda_{n}=s_{2}(A)
. Therefore, the highest power of
t
which can occur in the Hilbert series
H((F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}))^{\mathrm{SL}(n)},t)
is
i=ns_{2}(A)
. Similarly, if
n>s_{1}(A)
, then the largest partition
\lambda
which satisfies the necessary conditions is
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\dots=\lambda_{n}=\omega_{1}(A)
. Thus in this case the highest power of
t
which can occur in the Hilbert series
H((F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}))^{\mathrm{SL}(n)},t)
is
i=n\omega_{1}(A)
. This completes the proof.
∎

When we apply the above corollary to the case when
I=I_{p_{1}+1}\cdots I_{p_{k}+1}
we obtain the following.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
I=I_{p_{1}+1}\cdots I_{p_{k}+1}
for some positive integers
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
. If
k\leq n-1
, then the algebra of invariants
(F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}))^{\mathrm{SL}(n)}
is finite-dimensional and

\deg H((F_{n}(\mathfrak{V}_{I}))^{\mathrm{SL}(n)},t)\leq n(p_{1}+\dots+p_{k}-k).

On connected subsets of a convergence space
Bryan Castro Herrejón and Frédéric Mynard

Abstract
. Though a convergence space is connected if and only if its topological modification is connected, connected subsets differ for the convergence and for its topological modification. We explore for what subsets connectedness for the convergence or for the topological modification turn out to be equivalent. In particular, we show that the largest set containing a given connected set for which all subsets in between are connected is the adherence of the connected set for the reciprocal modification of the convergence.

2.
Connectedness

By definition, a convergence space
(X,\xi)
is

if
its only subsets that are both closed and open (clopen) are
\emptyset

and
X
, equivalently if every continuous map
f:X\to\{0,1\}
(where

\{0,1\}
carries the discrete topology) is constant.

As a result, it is plain that
\xi
is connected if and only if its
topological modification
\operatorname{T}\xi
is, so that the theory of connectedness
for convergence spaces is mostly equivalent to the classical one for
topological spaces. However, some care is needed for subspaces, as
well as for products. We say that a subset
A
of a convergence space

(X,\xi)
is
connected
if it is as a subspace, that is, if

(A,\xi_{|A})
is connected. For the reason already outlined, this
is equivalent to
(A,\operatorname{T}(\xi_{|A}))
being connected, but the subtlety
lies in the fact that while

(2.1)

\operatorname{T}(\xi_{|A})\geq(\operatorname{T}\xi)_{|A},

the reverse inequality does not need to hold, e.g.,
[
5
, Example V.4.35]
.
Note also that if
\xi
and

\xi\geq\tau

\xi
-connected subset of

is also

\tau
-connected. In particular, every
\xi
-connected subset is
also
\operatorname{T}\xi
-connected, but the converse is not true, e.g.,
[
5
, Example XII.1.30]
.

Remark 1
.

Similarly

does not commute with products, so some care is needed
in extending results on the connectedness of products from topological

if and only if each factor is connected remains valid for convergence

Reciprocal modification and connectedness

The observation made on finite spaces that connectedness only depends

5.

Let us say that a subset

Lemma 13
.

Let

of a convergence space

. Then

\Longrightarrow

\Longleftarrow

(A\cup\{c\})\cap A\neq\emptyset

5

that
\bigcup_{c\in C}A\cup\{c\}=C
is also connected.
∎

As a result of Lemma

, given a non-empty
connected set
A
, the

defined by

that encloses

,
so that

(5.1)

S\text{ encloses }A\iff S\subset e(A).

Theorem 14
.

If

is a non-empty connected subset of a convergence space

,
then

By Proposition

7.

In a convergence space without the sandwich property, it makes sense
to study the connectedness of sandwiched subsets, where
B
is called

sandwiched
if there is a connected subspace
A
with
A\subset B\subset\operatorname{cl}_{\xi}A
.
We may then say that
B
is
sandwiched by
A
.
Note that
if
B
is sandwiched by

, then
\operatorname{cl}_{\xi}B=\operatorname{cl}_{\xi}A
is connected
by Proposition
4
. Moreover, since
A
is
\xi
-connected,
it is also
\operatorname{T}\xi
-connected, hence every set between
A
and its
closure, in particular

\operatorname{T}\xi
-connected by the standard
topological result. In other words, every sandwiched set is
\operatorname{T}\xi
-connected,
but may fail to be
\xi
-connected (as there are spaces without the
sandwich property). Since a convergence space is connected if and
only if its topological modification is connected, a
\operatorname{T}\xi
-connected

\operatorname{T}
-subspace of a convergence space
(X,\xi)
is also
\xi
-connected.
In fact, we have

\displaystyle\xi\text{-connected}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\xi

The tropical Abel–Prym map
Giusi Capobianco, Yoav Len

Abstract
. We prove that the tropical Abel–Prym map Ψ : Γ ~ → Prym ⁡ ( Γ ~ / Γ ) \Psi\colon\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma) associated with a free double cover π : Γ ~ → Γ \pi\colon\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma of hyperelliptic metric graphs is harmonic of degree 2 2 in accordance with the already established algebraic result. We then prove a partial converse. Contrary to the analogous algebraic result, when the source graph of the double cover is not hyperelliptic, the Abel–Prym map is often not injective. When the source graph is hyperelliptic, we show that the Abel–Prym graph Ψ ⁡ ( Γ ~ ) \Psi(\widetilde{\Gamma}) is a hyperelliptic metric graph of genus g Γ − 1 g_{\Gamma}-1 whose Jacobian is isomorphic, as pptav, to the Prym variety of the cover. En route, we count the number of distinct free double covers by hyperelliptic metric graphs.



1.
Introduction

The Abel–Prym map relates the geometry of a double cover of algebraic curves or metric graphs with their corresponding Prym variety. Given a double cover
\widetilde{X}\to X
,
the map is given by

\begin{tabular}[]{cccc}$\Psi\colon$&$\Sym^{d}(\widetilde{X})$&$\longrightarrow$&$\Prym(\widetilde{X}/X)$\\
&$E$&$\mapsto$&$[E-\iota(E)]$\\
\end{tabular},

where
E
is an effective divisor of degree
d
. It is the Prym-theoretic analogue of the Abel–Jacobi map.

The map plays a fundamental role in understanding the geometry of tropical Prym varieties. For instance, via a careful study of the
d
-fold Abel–Prym map when
d=g-1
,
the second author and Zakharov establish a weighted version of the Kirchhoff–Prym formula and derive a geometric interpretation for the volume of the Prym variety of a free double cover of metric graphs
[
LZ22
, Theorem 3.4]
.
They show that the Abel–Prym map is a
harmonic morphism of polyhedral spaces
of global degree
2^{g-1}
and determine the local degree at every cell of the decomposition, leading to a natural choice of representatives for elements of the Prym variety.
As a counterpart, the appendix by Sebastian Casalaina-Martin shows that the degree of the algebraic Abel–Prym map is
2^{g-1}
as well.
Motivated by this similarity, the second author and Zakharov
conjectured that the algebraic and the tropical Abel–Prym maps have the same degree for
d\leq g-2

[
LZ22
, Conjecture 1.1]
.

In this paper we focus on the the case
d=1
for free double covers.
For brevity, for the rest of this paper, we refer to the 1-fold Abel–Prym map simply as the
Abel–Prym map
.
Our first result applies to the case where both the source and target graphs of the double covers are hyperelliptic.

Theorem A
.

[
Theorem

3.11
]
Let
\pi\colon\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
be a free double cover of metric graphs, where the genus of
\Gamma
is at least 2. If
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is hyperelliptic then the Abel–Prym map
is a harmonic morphism of degree 2.

This is consistent with the analogous algebraic result
[
BL04
, Proposition 12.5.2(b)]
.
However, we stress that tropically having degree 2 does not mean that the fibre over every point consists of two points, since harmonic morphisms also allow for edge dilations and contractions.
We also prove a partial converse.

Theorem B
.

[
Theorem

3.17
]
If the Abel–Prym map is a finite harmonic morphism of degree 2 then
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is hyperelliptic.

Note that there are free double covers of non-hyperelliptic graphs in which the associated Abel–Prym map is not injective, see
Example

3.12
. In fact, the Abel–Prym map turns out to not even be harmonic in general.
On the other hand, when the Abel–Prym map is not finite, it can be harmonic of degree 2 without implying that
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is hyperelliptic, see
Example

3.16
. It is then natural to ask whether the graph obtained from collapsing the locus where
\Psi
is not finite is hyperelliptic if and only if
\Psi
has degree
2
. Since the resulting double cover is no longer free, we postpone this question to future work dealing with such cases.

It is insightful to note that hyperellipticity plays a role in the analogous case for Jacobians as well. Indeed, it is a direct result of Clifford’s theorem (
[
ACGH85
, Chapter III]
and
[
Cop16
,
Len17
]
) that a curve is hyperelliptic if and only if the
2
-fold Abel–Jacobi map
\Sym^{2}(C)\to\Jac(C)
is not injective. As a consequence, one can use tropical techniques to study the extension of the
universal Abel–Jacobi map to the boundary of the moduli space of curves
[
AAP20
]
. In a similar way, it would be interesting to apply the tropical Abel–Prym map to study the algebraic universal Abel–Prym map.
In this sense, the failure to fully recover the properties of the algebraic Abel–Prym map should be seen not as a bug, but rather a feature, as it can be used to detect and explain phenomena related to degenerations of double covers to the boundary of the moduli space of admissible covers.

In the final two sections, we continue to investigate the structure of hyperelliptic double covers and their Abel–Prym map. We refine a common construction of double covers of graphs in order to characterise the ones that are hyperelliptic.
As a consequence, we show in
Corollary

4.12
that, among the
2^{g}
free double covers of a metric graph of genus
g
, only
g+1
are hyperelliptic. The construction also naturally yields a basis for the space of antisymmetric cycles on the double cover, leading to the following connection between the Jacobian of the Abel–Prym graph (namely the image of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
by the Abel–Prym map) and the Prym variety of the the double cover.

Corollary C

(
Corollary

5.3
and
Corollary

5.5
)
.

Let
\pi\colon\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
be a hyperelliptic double cover. The Abel–Prym graph
\Psi(\widetilde{\Gamma})
is a hyperelliptic metric graph of genus
g_{\Gamma}-1
whose Jacobian
is isomorphic, as pptav, to
the Prym variety of the cover, namely

\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)\cong\mathrm{Jac}(\Psi(\widetilde{\Gamma})).

Furthermore, the Abel–Prym graph is obtained by contraction or deletion of edges of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
, see
Corollary

5.3
for a full description.

Open problems and future directions

The results presented in this paper
only apply to free double covers. However, we believe that they extend to any harmonic double cover (possibly with dilated edges and weighted vertices) with mild modifications. This would be a crucial step towards using the tropical Abel–Prym map to study the algebraic universal 1-fold Abel–Prym map.

Another natural direction is an extension to the
d
-fold Abel–Prym map for
1<d<g-1
. We believe that, in that case, the image of the Abel–Prym map is a balanced polyhedral complex within the Prym variety. When the graph
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is hyperelliptic, an extension of our techniques should show
that the Abel–Prym map is a harmonic map of degree
2^{d}
onto its image. However, the behaviour of the
d
-fold Abel–Prym map beyond the hyperelliptic locus is expected to be much more complicated and to subtly depend on the Brill–Noether locus of the graph.

2.
Preliminaries

We now recall some basic notions from the theory of metric graphs and their divisor theory and refer to
[
BN09
]
,
[
BF11
]
,
[
Bak08
]
, and
[
Len22
]
for a more complete introduction to the topic. Let
G
be a connected graph with
V(G)
and
E(G)
respectively the sets of vertices and edges. The
genus
of
G
is its first Betti number
g=|E(G)|-|V(G)|-1
.
A
metric graph
of genus
g
is a metric space
\Gamma
such that there exists a graph
G
of genus
g
and a length function

l\colon E(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}

so that
\Gamma
is obtained from
(G,l)
by gluing intervals
[0,l(e)]
for
e\in E(G)
at their endpoints, as prescribed by the combinatorial data of
G
. In that case, we say that
(G,l)
is a
model
for
\Gamma
. The model is called
loopless
if
G
has no loops.
For any point
x
, its
valency
, denoted
\val(x)
, is the number of connected components in
U_{x}\setminus\{x\}
, where
U_{x}
is an arbitrarily small open set containing
x
.
The
canonical loopless model

(G_{0},l)
for
\Gamma
is the model obtained by taking

V=\{x\in\Gamma|\mathrm{val}(x)\neq 2\}

and placing an additional vertex at the midpoint of each loop edge. Unless otherwise mentioned, we will implicitly assume throughout the paper that we are working with the canonical trivalent model of the graph.

A metric graph is called
2-connected
if it remains connected after removing a single point. It is called
2-edge connected
if it remains connected after the removal of an edge. In other words, a graph is 2-connected if it has no cut-vertices and it is 2-edge connected if it has no bridges. Note that a 2-connected graph is 2-edge connected but not vice versa. Furthermore, every graph becomes 2-edge connected after contracting its bridges.

A
divisor

D\in\mathrm{Div}(\Gamma)
is given by

D=\sum_{p\in\Gamma}a_{p}p

with
a_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
a_{p}=0
for all but finitely many
p\in\Gamma
. The
degree
of
D
is
\deg(D)=\sum_{p\in\Gamma}a_{p}
.

A
rational function
on
\Gamma
is a continuous, piecewise linear function
f\colon\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
whose slopes are all integers.
The
principal divisor
associated to the rational function
f\in\mathrm{PL}(\Gamma)
is defined to be

\ddiv(f)=\sum_{P\in\Gamma}\sigma_{p}(f)p,

where
\sigma_{p}(f)
is the sum of the slopes of
\Gamma
in all directions emanating from
p
.
The
rank
of a divisor
D
on a metric graph
\Gamma

is
r_{\Gamma}(D)=-1

if
|D|=\emptyset

,
otherwise

\displaystyle r_{\Gamma}(D)=\max\{k\in\mathbb{Z}:|D-E|\neq\emptyset,\,\forall\,E\in\mathrm{Div}^{k}_{+}(\Gamma)\},

where
|D-E|
is the
complete linear system
associated to
D-E
and
\mathrm{Div}_{+}^{d}(\Gamma)
is the set of all effective divisors of degree
d
on
\Gamma
.

2.1.
Morphisms of metric graphs

We recall the definitions of hyperelliptic metric graphs and morphisms and refer to
[
Cha13
,
LUZ24
]
for details.
Suppose that
(G,l)
and
(G^{\prime},l^{\prime})
are loopless models for metric graphs
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
. A
morphism of loopless models

\phi\colon(G,l)\to(G^{\prime},l^{\prime})
is a map of sets

V(G)\cup E(G)\to V(G^{\prime})\cup E(G^{\prime})

such that

i)

\phi(V(G))\subseteq V(G^{\prime})
;

ii)

if
e=xy
is an edge of
G
and
\phi(e)\in V(G^{\prime})
then
\phi(x)=\phi(e)=\phi(y)
;

iii)

if
e=xy
is an edge of
G
and
\phi(e)\in E(G^{\prime})
then
\phi(e)
is an edge between
\phi(x)
and
\phi(y)
;

iv)

if
\phi(e)=e^{\prime}
then
l^{\prime}(e^{\prime})/l(e)
is an integer.

Note that a morphism is allowed to contract edges, namely to send an edge of
G
to a vertex of
G^{\prime}
.
We call
\mu_{\phi}(e)=l^{\prime}(e^{\prime})/l(e)
the slope of this linear map. The slope on contracted edges is 0.
A morphism
\phi
is said to be
harmonic
if for every
x\in V(G)
, the nonnegative integer

m_{\phi}(x)=\underset{x\in e,\,\phi(e)=e^{\prime}}{\sum_{e\in E(G)}}\mu_{\phi}(e),

is the same over all choices of
e^{\prime}\in E(G^{\prime})
that are incident to the vertex
\phi(x)
.
We say that
\phi
is
finite
if
m_{\phi}(x)>0
for all
x\in V(G)
.
The
degree
of
\phi
is

\deg\phi=\underset{\phi(e)=e^{\prime}}{\sum_{e\in E(G)}}\mu_{\phi}(e)

for any choice of
e^{\prime}\in E(G^{\prime})
.

A
morphism
of metric graphs is a continuous map
\widetilde{\phi}\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
that is induced from a morphism
\phi\colon(G,l)\to(G^{\prime},l^{\prime})
of loopless models. In that case, we define
\deg\widetilde{\phi}=\deg\phi
. The morphism
\widetilde{\phi}
is
harmonic
when
\phi
is. An
isomorphism
is a finite harmonic morphism of degree
1
. Note that every isomorphism is a bijection but not vice versa. For instance the map from a loop of length 1 to a loop of length 2 which dilates the edge by a factor of 2 is a bijective harmonic morphism of degree 2.

A harmonic double cover is called
free
if the fibre over every point consists of two distinct points. In particular, it doesn’t have any dilated edges. Fix a free double cover
\pi\colon\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
. Then we have a fixed-point free involution
\iota
swapping the two points of the fibre over each point.

A metric graph is
hyperelliptic
if it has no leaf edges (namely vertices of valency 1) and has a divisor of degree
2
and rank
1
. Hyperellipticity can be characterised by two other properties.

Theorem
.

[
Cha13
, Theorem 3.13]

Let
\Gamma
be a metric graph with canonical loopless model
(G,l)
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\Gamma
is hyperelliptic.

(2)

There exists length preserving involution
i\colon G\to G
such that
G/i
is a tree.

(3)

There exists a finite harmonic morphism of degree
2
from
\Gamma
to a tree, or
|V(G)|=2
.

Remark 2.1
.

While our starting point in this paper are double covers that are either free or hyperelliptic, the resulting Abel–Prym map will often have dilations, see
Lemma

3.4
.

In
Section

4
we will count the number of hyperelliptic double covers of a given hyperelliptic graph. To that end, we require an appropriate notion of an equivalence of such covers:
an
isomorphism
of double covers is an isomorphism
\phi\colon\Gamma_{1}\to\Gamma_{2}
such that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Id}

commutes.

The following result, concerning the relationship between hyperellipticity and free double covers, will be used throughout the paper.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\pi\colon\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
be a free double cover of metric graphs of genus
g_{\Gamma}\geq 2
. If
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is hyperelliptic
then

\Gamma
is hyperelliptic as well and the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\Gamma}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\Gamma}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\jmath}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}

commutes, where
j
and
\widetilde{\jmath}
are the hyperelliptic involutions of
\Gamma
and
\widetilde{\Gamma}
respectively.

Proof.

Since equivalent divisors map under harmonic morphisms to equivalent divisors
[
LU21
, Proposition 1.4.2]
, it follows that given a divisor
D
of degree
2
and rank
1
on
\widetilde{\Gamma}
, the rank of
\pi_{*}(D)
is at least 1. On the other hand, Clifford’s theorem
[
Fac10
, Theorem 1]
implies that the rank is exactly 1. It follows that
\Gamma
is hyperelliptic with involution
j
, where
j(y)
is the unique point such that
y+j(y)
is the divisor of degree 2 and rank 1. Since, for every
x\in\widetilde{\Gamma}
, its involution
\widetilde{\jmath}(x)
is the unique point such that
x+\widetilde{\jmath}(x)
is of degree 2 and rank 1, and since equivalent divisors on
\widetilde{\Gamma}
map to equivalent divisors on
\Gamma
, we conclude that
\widetilde{\jmath}
commutes with
\pi
.

We now turn to show that
\iota
and
\widetilde{\jmath}
commute.
If
p
is a point where
\iota
and
\widetilde{\jmath}
coincide then, since both maps are involutions, we see that
\iota\widetilde{\jmath}(p)=p=\widetilde{\jmath}\iota(p)
. We may therefore assume that
\widetilde{\jmath}(p)\neq\iota(p)
.

Suppose first that
\pi(p)
is contained in a bridge of
\Gamma
. Then its preimages in
\widetilde{\Gamma}
are either bridges or a pair of edges whose removal disconnects the graph.
We claim that, in the latter case, we have
\widetilde{\jmath}p=\iota p
, which is a contradiction: by the uniqueness of the hyperelliptic involution, it suffices to show that
p+\iota p
has rank 1. As bridges are fixed by the hyperelliptic involution, it follows that the divisor
\pi(p+\iota p)=2\pi(p)
has rank 1.
Let
x
be any point of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
and denote
y=\pi(x)
.
Let
f
be the piecewise linear function on
\Gamma
such that

2\pi(p)+\ddiv f=y+y^{\prime}

for some
y^{\prime}
.
The pullback
\pi^{*}(f)
of

is a piecewise linear function on
\widetilde{\Gamma}
such that
\ddiv(\pi^{*}(f))
is supported on
x
and
\iota x
. Since

was arbitrary, we conclude that
p+\iota p
has rank 1, so
\iota p=\widetilde{\jmath}p
, a contradiction. In particular, we can assume that
\widetilde{\jmath}p
belongs to a bridge of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
. But then
\widetilde{\jmath}
fixes both
p
and
\iota p
and, in particular, commutes with
\iota
.

If, instead,
\pi(\widetilde{\jmath}p)
is contained in a cycle, then by construction of the hyperelliptic involution, we have

(1)

p+\widetilde{\jmath}p\simeq\iota p+\widetilde{\jmath}\iota p.

Using the fact that
\pi_{*}
preserves linear equivalence and is invariant under
\iota
, we get

\pi(\widetilde{\jmath}p)\simeq\pi(\widetilde{\jmath}\iota p).

Since
\widetilde{\jmath}p
and
\widetilde{\jmath}\iota(p)

are contained in a cycle, the linear equivalence is, in fact, an equality. Moreover, by definition of
\iota
we also have

\pi(\widetilde{\jmath}p)=\pi(\iota\widetilde{\jmath}p)
. Therefore,

\pi(\iota\widetilde{\jmath}p)=\pi(\widetilde{\jmath}p)=\pi(\widetilde{\jmath}\iota p).

But, as
\pi
is a free
double

\widetilde{\jmath}\iota p=\iota\widetilde{\jmath}p
or
\widetilde{\jmath}\iota p=\widetilde{\jmath}p
. In the former case, we are done. In the latter case, since
\widetilde{\jmath}
is an involution, it follows that
\iota p=p
, which is a contradiction since
\iota
doesn’t have fixed points.

∎

We begin by identifying cases in which the Abel–Prym map contracts an edge and when it dilates it by a factor of 2. We fix a free double cover
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
e
be an edge of
\Gamma
with preimages
e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}
in
\widetilde{\Gamma}
. The Abel–Prym map contracts
e^{\prime}
and
e^{\prime\prime}
precisely in the following two cases.

(1)

e,e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}
are all bridges.

(2)

e
is not a bridge and
\widetilde{\Gamma}\setminus\{e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}\}
is disconnected.

Proof.

According to

LZ22
, Theorem 4.1]
, the Abel–Prym map contracts the edge

if and only if the number of connected components of

is larger than the number of connected components of

. But this happens precisely in the two cases above.
∎

Note that in the second case, when

is not a bridge, it always holds that neither of

is a bridge. This situation will repeat often in the sequel so we give it a name.

Definition 3.2

A pair of edges

Remark 3.3

Properly disconnecting pairs coincide with the 1-circuits of the matroid
{M}^{*}(\widetilde{G}/G)
defined in
[

.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
e
be a bridge of
\Gamma
and suppose that its preimages
e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}
form a properly disconnecting pair.
Then
\Psi
dilates

Proof.

Using

LZ22
, Construction B]

with opposite orientation and satisfies

via

.

We now turn to the injectivity of
\Psi
at
e^{\prime}
. We will assume that
\widetilde{\Gamma}
doesn’t have any bridges, since they get contracted by the Abel–Prym map and don’t play a role here. Suppose that

is a point in the interior of

and that

is another point of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
such that
\Psi(p)=\Psi(q)
, or what is the same,
p+\iota q\simeq q+\iota p
.
Denote
\phi
the piecewise linear function such that
p+\iota q=q+\iota p+\ddiv\phi
. By moving

a short distance towards
q+\iota p
, we can assume that both
q
and
\iota p
are in the interior of edges.

Since
e
is a bridge, it follows that the two connected components of
\widetilde{\Gamma}\setminus\{p,\iota p\}
are invariant under the involution
\iota
. In particular,

and
\iota q
belong to the same component of
\widetilde{\Gamma}\setminus\{p,\iota p\}
.
Denote
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
the connected components of

such that

. We will show that under our assumptions,
\phi
cannot be continuous.

Since

is a movable divisor (namely it is equivalent to at least one other effective divisor), the complement of
\{p,\iota q\}
is disconnected. By the non-bridges assumption, both of the components of the complement are connected.
We claim that

and
\iota p
are both on the same connected component of the complement.
Indeed, the function
\phi
may only bend upwards at
p
and
\iota q
and only bend downwards to

and
\iota p
. Therefore, if

is the region where

\iota q

If, on the other hand, the boundary consists of only one of the points. Then

may only be continuous if this points belongs to a bridge, which is a contradiction.

Note that

is fully contained in
\Sigma_{1}
. In particular, the function
\phi
must bend upwards at a tangent direction from

into
\Sigma_{2}

that starts at this tangent direction. Since

By the assumption that there are no vertices of valency 1 in

∎

Theorem 3.17
.

Suppose that
\Psi
is a finite harmonic morphism of degree 2. Then
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is hyperelliptic.

Proof.

Define
\widetilde{\jmath}
as follows. If there is
q\neq p
such that
\Psi(q)=\Psi(p)
then
\widetilde{\jmath}(p)=\iota q
. Otherwise,
\widetilde{\jmath}(p)=\iota p
.

We claim that
\widetilde{\jmath}
is well-defined and continuous. The map
\widetilde{\jmath}
is well-defined since the fibres of
\Psi
have size at most
2
. Clearly, the map is continuous along edges. Let
v
be a vertex of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
and suppose that there is an edge
e
emanating from it such that
\Psi
is injective on points of
e
. In particular,
\widetilde{\jmath}v=\iota v
. For continuity, we need to show that, for every edge
e^{\prime}
adjacent to
v
, we have
\lim_{p\to v}\widetilde{\jmath}p=\iota p
. Since
\Psi
has degree
2
, the edge
e
is dilated by a factor of
2
. If
\Psi
is dilated at
e^{\prime}
then
\widetilde{\jmath}p=\iota p
for each point of
e^{\prime}
and there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, by harmonicity, there is another edge
e^{\prime\prime}
emanating from
v
such that
\Psi(e^{\prime\prime})=\Psi(e^{\prime})
. But then
\widetilde{\jmath}e^{\prime}=e^{\prime\prime}
and the result follows.

We now argue that
\widetilde{\jmath}
is a hyperelliptic involution. By definition, for every
p
, we have
p+\widetilde{\jmath}(p)\simeq q+\widetilde{\jmath}q
, where
q
is as above. If
\Psi(p)\neq p
and
p
and
\widetilde{\jmath}p
are in the interior edges
e
and
e^{\prime}
respectively, then
\widetilde{\jmath}
maps
e
to
e^{\prime}
. In particular, the divisor
p+\widetilde{\jmath}p
moves along the edge. If
\Psi(p)=p
then
p
and
\iota p
form a disconnecting pair so
p+\iota p
move along the entire edge. But
\iota p=\widetilde{\jmath}p
in this case. It follows that
p+\widetilde{\jmath}p
moves along the entire graph, so it has rank
1
.
∎

4.
Free double covers of hyperelliptic graphs

In this section we exhibit an explicit construction of the free double covers of hyperelliptic graphs. A key role in the construction will be played by the fixed points of the hyperelliptic involution.
Throughout this section
\pi\colon\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
will be a free double cover of metric graphs with involution
\iota
exchanging the fibres.

4.1.
The fixed points of the hyperelliptic involution

The hyperelliptic involution
j
of a 2-edge connected metric graph
\Gamma
of genus
g
has
g+1
fixed points. Indeed, since
j
is a non-degenerate harmonic morphism of degree
2
from
\Gamma
to a tree
T
, the Riemann-Hurwitz formula
[
ABBR15
, Section 2.10]
implies that
the degree of the ramification divisor is given by

(2)

\deg(R_{j})=\deg(K_{\Gamma})-\deg(j^{*}(K_{T})).

Since
\deg j^{*}(K_{T})=2\deg(K_{T})=-4
, the ramification divisor has degree
2g+2
. But each ramification point has multiplicity 2, so there are
g+1
of them, see
[
Pan16
, Proposition 9]
.

Remark 4.1
.

Under the assumption that hyperelliptic graphs don’t have any leaf-edges, every cut point of
\Gamma
is fixed by the hyperelliptic involution.

Remark 4.2
.

By
Proposition

2.2
, the preimages via
\pi
of a fixed point
x\in\Gamma
are either fixed or exchanged by the involution
\widetilde{\jmath}
. Indeed, if
p
and
\iota(p)
are in the fibre
\pi^{-1}(x)
, then

\pi(\widetilde{\jmath}p)=j\pi(p)=jx=x
, so either
\widetilde{\jmath}p=p
or
\widetilde{\jmath}p=\iota p
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
2
-edge connected hyperelliptic graph with hyperelliptic involution
j
and let
p
be a fixed point of
j
. Then the valency of
p
equals
2k
, where
k
is the number of components of
\Gamma\setminus{p}
.

Proof.

Let
T
be the quotient tree
\Gamma/j
and
q
the image of
p
in
T
. By the assumption that there are no bridges, every edge adjacent to
q
lifts to two edges adjacent to
p
and, therefore, the valency of
p
is even. Furthermore, the valency of
q
equals the number of components of
\Gamma\setminus{p}
, so the result follows.

∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
and
\widetilde{\Gamma}
are hyperelliptic with involutions
\widetilde{\jmath}
and
j
respectively. Let
x\in\Gamma
be a fixed point of
j
, and let
\{p,\iota(p)\}\subseteq\pi^{-1}(x)
. Then,

i)

If
x
is a cut vertex, then
p
is a fixed point of
\widetilde{\jmath}

\Longleftrightarrow

p
and
\iota(p)
are each cut vertices;

ii)

If
x
is not a cut vertex, then
p
is fixed by
\widetilde{\jmath}

\Longleftrightarrow

p
and
\iota(p)
are not a disconnecting pair.

Furthermore, if
\Gamma
is 2-edge connected,
\exists!
point
x
fixed by
j
whose preimages in
\widetilde{\Gamma}
are not fixed by
\widetilde{\jmath}
, in which case
\widetilde{\jmath}(p)=\iota(p).

Proof.

Let us contract all the bridges contained in
\widetilde{\Gamma}
and
\Gamma
so that they are 2-edge connected metric graphs. This does not affect whether
x
is a cut vertex or any of the other conditions appearing in the statement.
The image of each bridge under the contraction is a cut vertex.

i)

Suppose that
x
is a cut vertex. Then

(\Leftarrow)

If
p
is a cut vertex, then it follows from
[
Cha13
, Lemma 3.10]
that
p
is fixed by
\widetilde{\jmath}
;

(\Rightarrow)

If
p
is a fixed point of
\widetilde{\jmath}
then, from
Proposition

4.3
applied to both
p
and
x
, we conclude that the valency of
p
is at least
4
. Therefore, by applying
Proposition

4.3
to
p
and
\iota p
, they are both cut vertices.

ii)

Suppose now that
x
is not a cut vertex (but still a fixed point of
j
). By
Proposition

4.3
, its valency is 2 and therefore
val(p)=val(\iota(p))=2
.

(\Leftarrow)

If
p
is not fixed by
\widetilde{\jmath}
then, by
Remark

4.2
, the divisor
p+\iota(p)
has rank
1
.
Therefore, for every
q\in\widetilde{\Gamma}
we have
p+\iota(p)-q\simeq q^{\prime}
. Applying Dhar’s burning algorithm and chip-firing from the point
q
, the fire stops when reaching
p
and
\iota(p)
and the graph is not entirely burnt. Furthermore, since the valency of
p
and
\iota(p)
is
2
, the graph can be decomposed into two parts,
meaning that
\{p,\iota(p)\}
is a disconnecting pair;

(\Rightarrow)

If
p
is fixed by
\widetilde{\jmath}
then
\iota(p)
is fixed as well. Moreover, since the genus of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is at least
3
, the involution
\widetilde{\jmath}
has at least
4
distinct fixed points. By contradiction, suppose that
\{p,\iota(p)\}
disconnects the graph in two parts
A
and
B
, therefore since
2p
has rank
1
, using Dhar’s burning algorithm and the fact that
val(p)=2
,
for every point
q\in A
there exists a unique point
q^{\prime}\in B
such that
2p-q\simeq q^{\prime}
and
\widetilde{\jmath}(q)=q^{\prime}
. This is absurd because there are no fixed points other than
p
and
\iota(p)
.

For the final part,
since
\widetilde{\Gamma}
has genus
2g-1
, its hyperelliptic involution
\widetilde{\jmath}
has exactly
2g
fixed points, while
j
has
g+1
such points. We note that if
\widetilde{\jmath}(p)=p
, then also
\widetilde{\jmath}(\iota(p))=\iota(p)
, therefore the
2g
fixed points of
\widetilde{\jmath}
map down to
g
fixed points for
j
. This means that there exists a unique point
x\in\Gamma
that is fixed by
j
such that
\widetilde{\jmath}(x)=\iota(x)
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

In the case where
\Gamma
is not 2-edge connected then, rather than a unique fixed point of
j
whose preimages are not fixed, we have a unique subtree of
\Gamma
. Indeed, this can be seen by contracting all the bridges of
\Gamma
and applying
Proposition

4.4
. We give this tree a name.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
be a free double cover of hyperelliptic metric graph and let
T_{f}
be the unique maximal fixed tree of
j
whose preimages are not fixed by
\widetilde{\jmath}
. We call
T_{f}
the
weakly-fixed subtree
(or weakly-fixed point if
T_{f}
is a single point) of the involution.

4.2.
Construction
\star

We now present a useful construction, that we call Construction
\star
, for free double covers of hyperelliptic metric graphs. The construction is similar
to
[
LZ22
, Construction A]
, with slight variations that take advantage of the hyperelliptic structure of the graph.
As we shall see,
every
double cover of a hyperelliptic graph can be obtained via the construction.
Note that we are not claiming that all the double covers obtained from Construction
\star
are hyperelliptic, but we will see in
Proposition

4.11

how to identify the ones that are.
We begin by describing the case where
\Gamma
is 2-connected and then extend to the general case.

4.3.
The
2
-connected case

Let
\Gamma
be a 2-connected hyperelliptic metric graph or a metric graph of genus
g\geq 1
with involution
j
.
As we saw in
Section

4.1
, the quotient
\Gamma/j
is a tree with
g+1
leaves corresponding to the fixed points of the involution. We may, therefore, express
\Gamma
as a union of two isomorphic trees

T
and
T^{\prime}
attached at their 1-valent vertices
v_{0},\dots,v_{g}
and
v^{\prime}_{0},\dots,v^{\prime}_{g}
respectively. Those attachment points correspond exactly to the fixed points of
j
.

Now, any double cover of
\Gamma
must contain two isomorphic copies of
T
and two isomorphic copies of
T^{\prime}
, which we denote
\widetilde{T}^{\pm}
and
\widetilde{T}^{\prime\pm}
respectively.
Since the graph
\Gamma
is obtained by attaching each
v_{i}
to
v_{i}^{\prime}
, the graph
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is obtained by attaching each lift
\widetilde{v}_{i}^{+}
of
v_{i}
to either of the lifts
\widetilde{v}_{i}^{\prime+}
or
\widetilde{v}_{i}^{\prime-}
of
v_{i}^{\prime}
, and vice versa for the other lift
\widetilde{v}_{i}^{-}
of
v_{i}
. These choices of gluing are exactly what distinguishes the various double covers of
\Gamma
.

As there are
g+1
fixed points
v_{i}
, we obtain
2^{g+1}
double covers. However, there is some repetition here: as we will see in
Proposition

4.9
,
swapping the gluing for all the vertices
\widetilde{v}_{i}^{\prime+}
at the same time results in the same double cover, leaving us with
2^{g}
distinct double covers.
See Figure
7
for an example of the construction.

For later use, denote

(3)

F=\{v_{0},\ldots,v_{g}\}

the set of vertices fixed by

and by

\widetilde{\Gamma}
traverses either between
\widetilde{T}^{+}
and
\widetilde{T}^{\prime-}
or between
\widetilde{T}^{-}
and
\widetilde{T}^{\prime+}
. Note that, since
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is assumed to be connected, we can assume that
S
is neither
\emptyset
nor

.

4.4.
The general case with cut vertices and bridges

Let
\Gamma
be a hyperelliptic graph of genus
g\geq 2
.
Denote by
\Gamma_{0},\dots,\Gamma_{n}
the
2
-connected components of genera
g_{0},g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}

\Gamma
corresponds to distinct vertices in the various 2-connected components that contain it.

As the hyperelliptic involution fixes any bridge or cut vertex point-wise, each subgraph
\Gamma_{i}
is hyperelliptic as well with
g_{i}+1
fixed points.
Therefore, the total number of fixed points on the
disjoint
union of the
\Gamma_{i}

\sum_{i=0}^{n}(g_{i}+1)=g+1+n.

Applying Construction
\star
to each connected component, we can write

\Gamma_{i}=T_{i}\cup T_{i}^{\prime}
for each
i
, where
T_{i}
and
T_{i}^{\prime}
are isomorphic trees, and
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{i}=\widetilde{T}_{i}^{\pm}\cup\widetilde{T}_{i}^{\prime\pm}
.
For each
i
, denote
F(\Gamma_{i})
the set of vertices connecting the trees
T_{i}
and
T^{\prime}_{i}
.
As before, for each
F(\Gamma_{i})
we denote
S_{i}
the set of vertices traversing between
\widetilde{T}_{i}^{\pm}
and
\widetilde{T}_{i}^{\prime\mp}
.
Now take
S=\sqcup_{i}S_{i}
. Note that some vertices of
\Gamma
may appear in more than one
\Gamma_{i}
and we distinguish them in
S
and in
F
.

We are left with lifting the bridges of
\Gamma
. Different lifts of a bridge result in an isomorphic double cover, so we might as well assume that, when a bridge

connects vertices

and
v
of
\Gamma
whose preimages in
\widetilde{\Gamma}
are

,
one of its preimages
\widetilde{b}^{+}
connects
\tilde{u}^{+}
with
\tilde{v}^{+}
and the other preimage
\widetilde{b}^{-}
connects
\tilde{u}^{-}
with
\tilde{v}^{-}
.
In conclusion, since there are
g+1+n
fixed points, we end up with
2^{g+1+n}
double covers. As before, there is repetition and some of those are isomorphic double cover.

The number of covers by hyperelliptic graphs doesn’t change when contracting bridges, so we may as well assume that

5.
The image of the Abel–Prym map for hyperelliptic double covers

In the final section, we continue our investigation of the Abel–Prym graph, under the assumption that the source graph
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is hyperelliptic. We show that its Jacobian
is isomorphic, as a principally polarised abelian variety, to the Prym variety of the double cover.
We begin by providing two different descriptions of the image, one that is simple and abstract and one that is more explicit.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\widetilde{\Gamma}
be a
2
-edge connected hyperelliptic metric graph. Then the Abel–Prym graph is isomorphic to the quotient graph
\widetilde{\Gamma}/{(\iota\circ\widetilde{\jmath})}
. In particular, the Abel–Prym graph is hyperelliptic.

Proof.

First, the map
\iota\circ\widetilde{\jmath}
is indeed a harmonic automorphism of degree
2
, since
both
\iota
and
\widetilde{\jmath}
are isometric involutions.
Set-theoretically, that Abel–Prym map identifies every point
p
with
\iota\circ\widetilde{\jmath}(p)
. So we need to check that the Abel–Prym map and the quotient map contract the same edges and that the edge-length prescribed by the Abel–Prym map agrees with that of the quotient.

By definition (see
[
Cha13
, Section 2.2]
and
[
LUZ24
, Section 1.3]
) of the quotient map, every edge gets dilated by a factor of its stabilizer and an edge gets contracted if it is flipped by the automorphism.
In particular, the only edges dilated in the quotient are the non-bridges fixed by
\iota\circ\widetilde{\jmath}
, which are exactly the edges dilated by
\Psi
.

Now let
f
be an edge flipped by
\iota\circ\widetilde{\jmath}
. Then the middle point
p
of
f
is fixed, meaning that
\widetilde{\jmath}(p)=\iota(p)
. The point
\pi(p)
corresponds to the weakly-fixed point
and by
Proposition

4.4
, the set
\{p,\iota(p)\}
is a disconnecting pair. It follows that, the edge
f
containing
p
gets contracted by
\Psi
. Conversely, if
\Psi
contracts edges
\{f,\iota(f)\}
which are not bridges then, by hypothesis, they form a disconnecting pairs and by the hyperellipticity of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
, we have
\widetilde{\jmath}(f)=\iota(f)
.

Finally,
\Psi(\widetilde{\Gamma})
is hyperelliptic as the quotient of a hyperelliptic graph by a harmonic automorphism.

∎

Remark 5.2
.

The lemma doesn’t hold when
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is not 2-edge connected, since the Abel–Prym map contracts all bridges of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
, whereas the quotient by
\iota\circ\widetilde{\jmath}
does not.

5.1.
The graph
\Gamma^{*}

Given a free double cover
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
, we now describe a graph
\Gamma^{*}
which, as we will see in
Corollary

5.3
, is isomorphic as a metric graph to the image of the Abel–Prym map.
Let
T_{f}
be the weakly-fixed subtree of
j
. If removing
T_{f}
does not disconnect
\Gamma
then
T_{f}
is a single point
x_{f}
of valency 2 and the preimage in
\widetilde{\Gamma}
of every bridge of
\Gamma
is a pair of bridges.
In this case,
\Gamma^{*}
is the graph obtained from
\Gamma
by deleting the two edges adjacent to
x_{f}
and contracting all the bridges.

If removing
T_{f}
does disconnect
\Gamma
then the preimage in
\widetilde{\Gamma}
of every edge of
T_{f}
is a properly disconnected pair, and the preimage of every other bridge of
\Gamma
is a pair of bridges. In this case,

\Gamma^{*}
is obtained from
\Gamma
by replacing
T_{f}
with two isomorphic copies of
T_{f}
stretched by a factor of 2, and, for each pair of edges
e,j(e)
of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
terminating at
T_{f}
, letting one of them terminate at one copy and the other terminate at the other copy.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
\pi\colon\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
be a free double cover of hyperelliptic metric graphs of genus
g_{\Gamma}\geq 2
. Then
\Psi(\widetilde{\Gamma})=\Gamma^{*}
, where
\Gamma^{*}
is described in
Section

5.1
.
In particular, the Abel–Prym graph has genus
g_{\Gamma}-1
.

Proof.

The statement about the genus is straightforward from the first part of the statement, since the graph
\Gamma^{*}
is obtained from
\Gamma
by either keeping the same number of vertices and removing one edge or by keeping the number of edges but adding another vertex.
So we need to prove that
\Psi(\widetilde{\Gamma})=\Gamma^{*}
. For simplicity of notations, we will assume that
\Gamma
is 2-edge connected. The general case required only minor adjustments.

Suppose first that
x
is not a cut vertex. By a slight abuse of notation, we will identify points of
\Gamma^{*}
with points in
\Gamma
.
Recall that
\Gamma
consists of two copies
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
of a tree
T
glued at the fixed point of
j
and that
\widetilde{\Gamma}
can be identified with four copies
T_{1}^{+},T_{1}^{-},T_{2}^{+},T_{2}^{-}
of
T
. If
x_{1}^{+},x_{1}^{-},x_{2}^{+},x_{2}^{-}
are the points corresponding to
x
in the four trees, then the trees
T_{i}^{+},T_{i}^{-}
(for
i=1,2
) are glued with each other at all their leaves, except for the preimages of the point
x
, in which
x_{1}^{+}
is glued to
x_{2}^{-}
and
x_{2}^{+}
is glued to
x_{1}^{-}
.

If

is such that
\pi(y)
doesn’t belong to the edge containing
x
, the pair
y,z=\iota\circ\widetilde{\jmath}(y)
does not disconnect
\widetilde{\Gamma}
and, therefore,
z
is the unique point other than
y
such that

\Psi(y)=\Psi(z)
.
If
y\in T^{+}_{i}
, then
z\in T^{-}_{i+1}
(where the index is taken modulo 2) and vice versa. Furthermore, if
y
and
z
are in the interior of edges, the map
\Psi
is a local isometry on each of them.
If, on the other hand,
\pi(y)
does belong to the edge containing
x
, then
y
and
z
do form a disconnecting pair, so their entire edges get contracted by
\Psi
.
It follows that
\Psi(\widetilde{\Gamma})
contains an isometric copy of
T_{1}^{+}\cup T_{2}^{+}
, except for contracting each of the four edges emanating from the two points
x_{i}^{\pm}
. But contraction of an edge amount to deleting it and identifying its endpoints, so the result follows.

Now, suppose that
x
is a cut vertex and suppose that
\val(x)=2k
. Then removing
x
disconnects
\Gamma
into
k
connected components. Recall that, for each of them, we have a pair of trees
T_{i}^{1},T_{i}^{2}
and
\widetilde{\Gamma}
contains two copies of each tree, denoted by
T_{i}^{1+},T_{i}^{1-},T_{i}^{2+},T_{i}^{2-}
with points
x_{i}^{j\pm}
corresponding to
x
in each. To obtain
\widetilde{\Gamma}
, glue the leaves of
T_{i}^{1+}
with those of
T_{i}^{2+}
and the leaves of
T_{i}^{1-}
with those of
T_{i}^{2-}
as in
\Gamma
, except that
x_{i}^{1+}
is glued to
x_{i}^{2-}
and
x_{i}^{1-}
is glued to
x_{i}^{2+}
.
Similarly to the first case,
\Gamma^{*}
will contain an isometric copy of
T_{i}^{1+}\cup T_{i}^{2+}
for each
i
, except for the edges emanating from
x
. However, unlike the first case, when
y
is on an edge emanating from
x
, the points
y,\iota(\widetilde{\jmath}(y))
do not form a disconnecting pair, so
\Psi(\widetilde{\Gamma})
contains an isometric copy of each of those edges.

∎

5.2.
The Jacobian of the Abel–Prym graph

We now turn to relate the Jacobian of the image of the Abel–Prym map with the Prym variety of the double cover.

First, we quickly remind the reader about the structure of tropical abelian varieties. See
[
LZ22
, Section
2
]
and
[
RZ22
, Section
4
]
for more details.
Let
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
be finitely generated free abelian groups of the same rank and let
[\cdot,\cdot]:\Lambda\times\Lambda^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-degenerate pairing. The triple
(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime},|\cdot,\cdot])
defines a
real torus with integral structure
(or simply an
integral torus
)

\Sigma=\Hom(\Lambda,\mathbb{R})/\Lambda^{\prime},

where the inclusion
\Lambda^{\prime}\subseteq\Hom(\Lambda,\mathbb{R})
is given by
\lambda^{\prime}\mapsto[\cdot,\lambda^{\prime}]
.
The
dimension
of an integral torus is
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\Sigma=\rk\Lambda=\rk\Lambda^{\prime}
.
Let
\Sigma=(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime},[\cdot,\cdot])
be an integral torus. A
polarisation
on
\Sigma
is a group homomorphism
\xi:\Lambda^{\prime}\to\Lambda
such that

(\cdot,\cdot)=[\xi(\cdot),\cdot]\colon\Lambda^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}\times\Lambda^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}

is a symmetric and positive definite bilinear form. A polarisation is necessarily injective, and is called
principal
if it is also bijective.
An integral torus together with a principal polarisation is called
principally polarised tropical abelian variety
or
pptav
for short.

The
Jacobian
of a metric graph
\Gamma
is the pptav

\Sigma_{1}=\mathrm{Jac}(\Gamma)=(\Omega(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})),H_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})),[\cdot,\cdot]_{1}),

of dimension
g_{\Gamma}
, where the pairing
[\cdot,\cdot]_{1}
is described as follows.
Let
G
be an oriented model of
\Gamma
. Then the simplicial chain group
C_{1}(G,\mathbb{Z})
is the free abelian group on the edges of
G
containing the simplicial homology group
H_{1}(G,\mathbb{Z})
and we call
C_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
and
H_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
the direct limits. There is a natural isomorphism
H_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})\to\Omega^{1}_{\Gamma}
sending a cycle
\sum a_{e}e
to the
1-
form
\sum a_{e}\,de
.
The
integration pairing

[\cdot,\cdot]\colon

\Omega_{\Gamma}^{1}(\Gamma)\times C_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})

\longrightarrow

\mathbb{R}

(\omega,\gamma)=\bigl(\sum a_{e}de,\sum b_{e}e\bigr)

\mapsto

\int_{\gamma}\omega=\sum a_{e}b_{e}l(e)

restricts to a non-degenerate pairing
[\cdot,\cdot]_{1}\colon\Omega_{\Gamma}^{1}(\Gamma)\times H_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{R}
.
The
Prym variety
associated to a free double cover
\pi\colon\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
is given by

\Sigma_{2}=\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)=(\mathrm{Ker}\,\pi_{*})_{0}=((\text{Coker}\,\pi^{*})^{tf},\mathrm{Ker}\,\pi_{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{2}),

where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{2}
is induced by the integration pairing on
\Jac(\widetilde{\Gamma})
.
The dimension of the Prym variety is equal to
g_{0}=g_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}-g_{\Gamma}
.

Next,
we need an explicit description of a basis for the homology
H_{1}(\widetilde{G},\mathbb{Z})
. This will be done by combining Construction
\star
with
[
LZ22
, Constructions A & B]
.
Let
\mathcal{T}
be the spanning tree obtained from
\Gamma
by removing edges as follows. For each 2-connected component
\Gamma_{i}
, remove the edges of
T_{i}
adjacent to the fixed vertices
F(\Gamma_{i})
, apart for the edge adjacent to some single vertex, denoted
v_{i}
.
Now, for each edge
e
not in
\mathcal{T}
, the graph
\mathcal{T}\cup\{e\}
contains a unique cycle and the collection of these cycles forms a basis for
H_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
.

Let

be the edge of
\Gamma\setminus\mathcal{T}
attached to a vertex
v_{g}\in S
and let
\widetilde{e}_{g}^{\pm}
be its preimages in
\widetilde{\Gamma}
.
The preimages

\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\pm}
in
\widetilde{\Gamma}
are disconnected, but adding
\widetilde{e}_{g}^{+}
we obtain a spanning tree

\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{+}\cup\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{-}\cup\widetilde{e_{g}}^{+}.

of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
.

We now define
\widetilde{\gamma}_{g}
and
\widetilde{\gamma}^{\pm}_{i}
as the unique cycles of
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}\cup\{\widetilde{e}_{g}^{-}\}
and respectively
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}\cup\{\widetilde{e}_{i}^{\pm}\}
, where

Then

\mathcal{B}=\{\widetilde{\gamma}^{\pm}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{\gamma}^{\pm}_{g-1},\widetilde{\gamma}_{g}\}

\mathcal{C}=\left\{\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}^{+}-\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}^{-}\right\}_{i=1,\ldots,g-1}

-basis for the antisymmetric cycles of

.

When

is hyperelliptic, we may and will choose the edges removed from the tree so that they include the edges of

Due to this choice, for each 2-connected component

Corollary 5.5
.

The Prym variety of a hyperelliptic double cover is isomorphic, as pptav, to the Jacobian of the image of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
under the Abel–Prym map,

\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)\cong\mathrm{Jac}(\Psi(\widetilde{\Gamma})).

Proof.

In order to prove the isomorphism as principally polarised tropical abelian varieties, we need to begin with their integral tori structures. Following
[
LZ22
]
, let

\Sigma_{1}=\mathrm{Jac}(\Psi(\widetilde{\Gamma}))=(\Omega^{1}(\Psi(\widetilde{\Gamma}),\mathbb{Z})),H_{1}(\Psi(\widetilde{\Gamma}),\mathbb{Z}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{1})

and let

\Sigma_{2}=\Prym(\widetilde{\Gamma}/\Gamma)=(\mathrm{Ker}\,\pi_{*})_{0}=((\text{Coker}\,\pi^{*})^{tf},\mathrm{Ker}\,\pi_{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{2})

be respectively the structures of the Jacobian and the Prym variety of the double cover with intersection pairings
[\cdot,\cdot]_{1}
and
[\cdot,\cdot]_{2}
and principal polarisations
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
. Here,
\xi_{1}
identifies a homology cycle with its dual 1-form and
\xi_{2}
identifies a cycle with half its dual 1-form.

We will show that there exists an isomorphism of integral tori
f=(f^{\#},f_{\#})\colon\Sigma_{1}\to\Sigma_{2}
.
Let
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}

be respectively the bases for
H_{1}(\widetilde{\Gamma},\mathbb{Z})
and
\mathrm{Ker}\,\pi_{*}
, as constructed above. We define

f_{\#}\colon

(\mathrm{Ker}\,\pi_{*}\colon\mathrm{H}_{1}(\widetilde{\Gamma})\to\mathrm{H}_{1}(\Gamma))

\longrightarrow

\mathrm{H_{1}}(\Psi(\widetilde{\Gamma}))

\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}^{+}-\iota\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}^{+}

\mapsto

\Psi(\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}^{+})

Note that we could have equally chosen
\Psi(\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}^{-})
since the image of the Abel–Prym map identifies
\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}^{+}
with
-\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}^{-}
.

In order to prove that
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\{\Psi(\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}^{+})\}_{i=1,\dots,g-1}
is a basis for
\mathrm{H_{1}}(\Psi(\widetilde{\Gamma}))
, we will use the construction of
\Gamma^{*}=\Psi(\widetilde{\Gamma})
described in
Section

5.1
.
By
Corollary

5.3
, if
T_{f}
is a weakly-fixed point

Convergence of the Dirichlet–Neumann method for semilinear elliptic equations Thanks: Version of August 24, 2026.
Emil Engström

Abstract
The Dirichlet–Neumann method is a common domain decomposition method for nonoverlapping domain decomposition and the method has been studied extensively for linear elliptic equations. However, for nonlinear elliptic equations, there are only convergence results for some specific cases in one spatial dimension. The aim of this manuscript is therefore to prove that the Dirichlet–Neumann method converges for a class of semilinear elliptic equations on Lipschitz continuous domains in two and three spatial dimensions. This is achieved by first proving a new result on the convergence of nonlinear iterations in Hilbert spaces and then applying this result to the Steklov–Poincaré formulation of the Dirichlet–Neumann method.



keywords

Nonlinear domain decomposition, Dirichlet–Neumann method, linear convergence, semilinear elliptic equations

Introduction

We consider the semilinear elliptic equation with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\nabla\cdot\bigl(\alpha(x)\nabla u\bigr)+\beta(x,u)&=f&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u&=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(1)

Here,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d=2,3
is a Lipschitz domain and
\partial\Omega
denotes the boundary of
\Omega
. In order to solve
eq.

1
in parallel it is a common practice to employ a nonoverlapping domain decomposition method. For linear elliptic equations, the convergence theory of such methods is well established
[
8
,
12
]
. For overlapping Schwarz methods applied to nonlinear elliptic equations there is also a general theory, see, e.g.
[
4
,
9
,
10
]
. However, concerning nonoverlapping domain decompositions for nonlinear elliptic equations there are few results. Some exceptions are
[
1
]
that show convergence of the Dirichlet–Neumann and Robin–Robin method for a class of nonlinear elliptic problems and
[
2
]
, in which quadratic convergence is proven for specific parameter choices of the Dirichlet–Neumann method. Both of these results are only proven on one-dimensional domains, however.

For convergence on general Lipschitz domains in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
there are the results
[
5
,
6
]
concerning the Robin–Robin method and modified Neumann–Neumann methods, respectively. However, the convergence rate of the Robin–Robin method deteriorates as the mesh size
h
is made smaller
[
7
]
. Moreover, the Neumann–Neumann method does not seem to converge at all for some nonlinear problems
[
6
]
and, while the modified Neumann–Neumann methods perform better in this regard, their convergence depend on the choice of an auxiliary problem, which may not be obvious how to choose. The Dirichlet–Neumann method, on the other hand, is expected to have mesh independent convergence and does not require an auxiliary problem. Moreover, our numerical results indicate that the method converges faster than the Robin–Robin method and converges in some cases when the Neumann–Neumann method does not converge at all. To our knowledge there are currently no general convergence proofs for the Dirichlet–Neumann method as there is for the Robin–Robin and modified Neumann-Neumann methods. The aim of this manuscript is therefore to prove that the Dirichlet–Neumann method converges for a large class of semilinear elliptic equations without restrictive regularity assumptions on the equation or the domain.

We decompose the domain
\Omega
into the subdomains
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
such that

\overline{\Omega}=\overline{\Omega}_{1}\cup\overline{\Omega}_{2},\quad\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}=\emptyset\quad\text{and}\quad\Gamma=(\partial\Omega_{1}\cap\partial\Omega_{2})\setminus\partial\Omega,

where we refer to
\Gamma
as the interface of the decomposition. See
fig.

1(a)
for an example of such a decomposition. It is also possible to consider two families of nonadjacent subdomains as in
fig.

1(b)
, which will yield a method that can be implemented in parallel. We do not consider decompositions with cross-points, but these have been studied for the Dirichlet–Neumann method applied to linear equations in
[
3
]
.

(a)

A decomposition with two subdomains
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}

(b)

A decomposition into two families of subdomains
\{\Omega_{1,\ell}\}
and
\{\Omega_{2,\ell}\}
.

The Dirichlet–Neumann method applied to the semilinear equation
eq.

1
is the following. Let
f_{i}=\left.f\right|_{\Omega_{i}}
for
i=1,2
. For a method parameter
s>0
and an initial guess
\eta^{0}
on
\Gamma
, we find
(u_{1}^{n+1},u_{2}^{n+1})
for
n=0,1,2,\ldots,
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\nabla\cdot(\alpha(x)\nabla u^{n+1}_{1})+\beta(x,u^{n+1}_{1})&=f_{1}&&\text{in }\Omega_{1},\\
u^{n+1}_{1}&=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega_{1}\setminus\Gamma,\\
u^{n+1}_{1}&=\eta^{n}&&\text{on }\Gamma,\\[5.0pt]
-\nabla\cdot(\alpha(x)\nabla u^{n+1}_{2})+\beta(x,u^{n+1}_{2})&=f_{2}&&\text{in }\Omega_{2},\\
u^{n+1}_{2}&=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega_{2}\setminus\Gamma,\\
\alpha(x)\nabla u^{n+1}_{2}\cdot\nu_{2}&=\alpha(x)\nabla u^{n+1}_{1}\cdot\nu_{2}&&\text{on }\Gamma,\end{aligned}\right.

(2)

with
\eta^{n+1}=s\left.u_{2}^{n+1}\right|_{\Gamma}+(1-s)\eta^{n}
. Here,
\nu_{2}
denotes the outwards pointing unit normal of
\Omega_{2}
. As will be shown, the Dirichlet–Neumann method corresponds to the interface iteration

\eta^{n+1}=(1-s)\eta^{n}+sS_{2}^{-1}(0-S_{1}\eta^{n}),

(3)

where
S_{i}
for
i=1,2
are the Steklov–Poincaré operators.

The paper is organized in the following way. In
Section

2
we present a new convergence result for iterations of the form
eq.

3
in Hilbert spaces. We then introduce the weak formulations of the nonlinear operators in
eq.

2
and show some important properties of these in
Section

3
. In
Section

4
we introduce the domain decomposition, transmission problem and Steklov–Poincaré operators. We then show that our nonlinear Steklov–Poincaré operators satisfy the conditions of our abstract theorem. In
Section

5
we introduce the weak formulation of the Dirichlet–Neumann method and show that it can be interpreted as an interface iteration. We then conclude that the Dirichlet–Neumann method converges. Finally, we give some numerical results that verify our convergence theorem in
Section

6
and compare the convergence rate with the Robin–Robin and Neumann–Neumann methods. We also give some numerical results for an equation not satisfying the conditions to demonstrate the generality of the nonlinear Dirichlet–Neumann method. Throughout the manuscript we will denote arbitrary positive constants that may change between lines by
c,C>0
.

2
Abstract theorem on nonlinear splitting in Hilbert spaces

Let
X
and
Y
be two Hilbert spaces. We denote the space of bounded linear operators from
X
to
Y
by
B(X,Y)
. We say that a (nonlinear) operator
G:X\rightarrow Y
is bounded if for any bounded set
U
, the set
G(U)
is bounded. Moreover,
G
is Lipschitz continuous on a subset
U\subset X
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|G\mu-G\lambda\|_{Y}\leq C\|\mu-\lambda\|_{X}\qquad\text{for all }\mu,\lambda\in U.

The operator
G
is Fréchet differentiable on the subset
U\subset X
if there exists an operator, the Fréchet derivative,
G^{\prime}:U\rightarrow B(X,Y)
such that, for any
\mu\in U
, we have

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{\|G(\mu+h)-G(\mu)-G^{\prime}(\mu)h\|_{Y}}{\|h\|_{X}}=0.

If
G
is Lipschitz continuous or Fréchet differentiable on
U=X
, we simply say that
G
is Lipschitz continuous or Fréchet differentiable, respectively. We now recall the Fundamental theorem of calculus. For a reference, see the proof of
[
11
, Lemma 9.12]
. If
G
is Fréchet differentiable on a convex set
U
with continuous Fréchet derivative
G^{\prime}
we have, for
\mu,\lambda\in U
,

G\mu-G\lambda=\int_{0}^{1}G^{\prime}\bigl(\lambda+t(\mu-\lambda)\bigr)(\mu-\lambda)\mathrm{d}t.

(4)

Here, and for the rest of the manuscript, the integral should be interpreted as the Bochner integral, see
[
11
, Chapter 9]
.

For a Hilbert space
X
we denote its dual by
X^{*}
and the dual pairing by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X^{*}\times X}
. If the spaces are obvious from the context we denote the dual paring by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. The Bochner integral on
X^{*}
satisfies

\Bigl\langle\int_{0}^{1}\mu(t)\mathrm{d}t,\lambda\Bigr\rangle=\int_{0}^{1}\langle\mu(t),\lambda\rangle\mathrm{d}t\qquad\text{for all }\mu\in C\bigl([0,1],X^{*}\bigr),\lambda\in X,

(5)

see
[
11
, (9.7)]
. We say that a linear operator
P:X\rightarrow X^{*}
is symmetric if

\langle P\mu,\lambda\rangle=\langle P\lambda,\mu\rangle\qquad\text{for all }\mu,\lambda\in X

and denote the subspace of symmetric and bounded linear operators by
S(X,X^{*})\subset B(X,X^{*})
. Moreover, we say that
P
is coercive if

\langle P\mu,\mu\rangle\geq c\|\mu\|_{X}^{2}\qquad\text{for all }\mu\in X.

For
G:X\rightarrow X^{*}
Lipschitz continuity on
U
is equivalent to

|\langle G\mu-G\lambda,\nu\rangle|\leq C\|\mu-\lambda\|_{X}\|\nu\|_{X}\qquad\text{for all }\mu,\lambda\in U,\nu\in X.

Furthermore, we say that
G:X\rightarrow X^{*}
is uniformly monotone if there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\langle G\mu-G\lambda,\mu-\lambda\rangle\geq c\|\mu-\lambda\|_{X}^{2}\qquad\text{for all }\mu,\lambda\in X.

We now consider a Hilbert space
X
, a nonlinear operator
G:X\rightarrow X^{*}
, and
\chi\in X^{*}
. The abstract problem is to find
\eta\in X
such that

G\eta=\chi.

(6)

A nonlinear splitting can be defined in the following way. Suppose that

G=G_{1}+G_{2},

where
G_{i}:X\rightarrow X^{*}
,
i=1,2
. For some method parameter
s>0
and initial guess
\eta^{0}
the iterative method is

\eta^{n+1}=(1-s)\eta^{n}+sG_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\eta^{n}).

(7)

The main abstract result of this section is the convergence of this iteration.

Theorem 1
.

Let
X
be a (real) Hilbert space,
G:X\rightarrow X^{*}
be a uniformly monotone operator,
\chi\in X^{*}
, and
\eta^{0}\in X
.

•

Suppose that for any bounded subset
\tilde{U}\subset X
the operator
G
is Lipschitz continuous on
\tilde{U}
. Then
G:X\rightarrow X^{*}
is bijective and, in particular, there exists a unique solution to
eq.

6
. Moreover, the inverse
G^{-1}:X^{*}\rightarrow X
is Lipschitz continuous.

•

Let
G=G_{1}+G_{2}
, where
G_{1},G_{2}
are uniformly monotone. Suppose that for any bounded subset
\tilde{U}\subset X
the nonlinear operator
G_{2}
is Lipschitz continuous on
\tilde{U}
. Then
G_{2}:X\rightarrow X^{*}
is bijective and the iteration
eq.

7
is well defined. Moreover, the inverse
G_{2}^{-1}:X^{*}\rightarrow X
is Lipschitz continuous.

•

In addition to the above, let
U\subset X
be a bounded, open, and convex subset such that the solution
\eta
of
eq.

6
satisfies
\eta\in U
. Suppose that
G_{1}
is Lipschitz continuous on
U
. Moreover, suppose that
G_{2}
is Fréchet differentiable on
U
with Lipschitz continuous Fréchet derivative
G_{2}^{\prime}:U\rightarrow S(X,X^{*})
. Then, for
\eta^{0}
close enough to
\eta
and sufficiently small
s>0
, the iteration
eq.

7
converges to
\eta
. The convergence is linear, i.e., there exists constants
C>0
and
0\leq L<1
such that

\|\eta^{n}-\eta\|_{X}\leq CL^{n}\|\eta^{0}-\eta\|_{X}.

Proof.

For the first two statements we use the Browder–Minty theorem
[
13
, Theorem 26.A]
. We show the required properties only of
G
since the case of
G_{2}
follows in the same way. The first property is coercivity, i.e., if
\|\mu\|_{X}\rightarrow\infty
then

\frac{\langle G\mu,\mu\rangle}{\|\mu\|_{X}}\rightarrow\infty.

This follows immediately from the uniform monotonicity of
G
since

\langle G\mu,\mu\rangle=\langle G\mu-G(0),\mu-0\rangle+\langle G(0),\mu\rangle\geq c\|\mu\|_{X}^{2}-\|G(0)\|_{X^{*}}\|\mu\|_{X}.

The fact that
G
is monotone, i.e.,

\langle G\mu-G\lambda,\mu-\lambda\rangle\geq 0,

is obvious from the uniform monotonicity. Finally, we show that the operator
G
is demicontinuous, i.e., that

\langle G\mu^{n}-G\mu,\lambda\rangle\rightarrow 0

as
\mu^{n}\rightarrow\mu
in
X
for all
\lambda\in X
. To see this, let
\mu^{n}\rightarrow\mu
. Then
\|\mu^{n}\|\leq C
and therefore there exists a bounded set
\tilde{U}
such that
\{\mu^{n}\}\cup\{\mu\}\subset\tilde{U}
. Since
G
is Lipschitz continuous on
\tilde{U}
, we have that

\langle G\mu^{n}-G\mu,\lambda\rangle\leq C(\tilde{U})\|\mu^{n}-\mu\|_{X}\|\lambda\|_{X}\rightarrow 0

for all
\lambda\in X
. We can now apply the Browder–Minty theorem to conclude that
G
is bijective. To see that the inverse is Lipschitz continuous, we consider
\mu^{*},\lambda^{*}\in X^{*}
and apply the uniform monotonicity of
G
as follows

\displaystyle\|G^{-1}\mu^{*}-G^{-1}\lambda^{*}\|_{X}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\langle GG^{-1}\mu^{*}-GG^{-1}\lambda^{*},G^{-1}\mu^{*}-G^{-1}\lambda^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|\mu^{*}-\lambda^{*}\|_{X^{*}}\|G^{-1}\mu^{*}-G^{-1}\lambda^{*}\|_{X}.

Dividing by
\|G^{-1}\mu^{*}-G^{-1}\lambda^{*}\|_{X}
shows that
G^{-1}
is Lipschitz continuous on
X^{*}
.

To show the final statement, let
U
be a bounded, open, and convex subset such that
\eta\in U
and consider the operator
K:X\to X
defined by

K\mu=(1-s)\mu+sG_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\mu).

Since
G_{2}^{-1}
is Lipschitz continuous and
G_{1}
is Lipschitz continuous on
U
we have that
G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\mu)
and
K
also are Lipschitz continuous on
U
. Moreover, since
\eta
is a fixed point of both
K
and
G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\mu)
we can find a convex open set
W\subset U
such that
\eta\in W
and for
\mu\in W
we have
K\mu\in U
and
G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\mu)\in U
.

We now fix arbitrary
\mu,\lambda\in W
and for
t\in[0,1]
we define the lines

\displaystyle x(t)

\displaystyle=K\lambda+t(K\mu-K\lambda),

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\lambda)+t(G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\mu)-G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\lambda)),\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle z(t)

\displaystyle=\lambda+t(\mu-\lambda).

Since
U
is convex, we have
x,y,z\subset U
. Thus, by
eq.

4
we have

\displaystyle G_{2}K\mu-G_{2}K\lambda

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)(K\mu-K\lambda)\mathrm{d}t

(8)

\displaystyle G_{1}\lambda-G_{1}\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(y(t)\bigr)(G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\mu)-G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\lambda))\mathrm{d}t

(9)

\displaystyle G_{2}\mu-G_{2}\lambda

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(z(t)\bigr)(\mu-\lambda)\mathrm{d}t.

(10)

Note that for the second identity we have used

G_{1}\lambda-G_{1}\mu=G_{2}G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\mu)-G_{2}G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\lambda).

Using
eqs.

8
and
5
, we get

\displaystyle\langle G_{2}K\mu-G_{2}K\lambda,K\mu-K\lambda\rangle=\biggl\langle\int_{0}^{1}G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)(K\mu-K\lambda)\mathrm{d}t,K\mu-K\lambda\biggr\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\langle G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)(K\mu-K\lambda),K\mu-K\lambda\Bigr\rangle\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\langle G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)\bigl((1-s)(\mu-\lambda)

\displaystyle\qquad+sG_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\mu)-sG_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\lambda)\bigr),K\mu-K\lambda\Bigr\rangle\mathrm{d}t.

Expanding
K\mu-K\lambda
again, we find

\displaystyle\langle G_{2}K\mu-G_{2}K\lambda,K\mu-K\lambda\rangle=(1-s)\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\langle G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)(\mu-\lambda),K\mu-K\lambda\Bigr\rangle\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+s\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\langle G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)\bigl(G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\mu)-G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\lambda)\bigr),K\mu-K\lambda\Bigr\rangle\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad=(1-s)^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\langle G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)(\mu-\lambda),\mu-\lambda\Bigr\rangle\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-s)s\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\langle G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)(\mu-\lambda),G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\mu)-G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\lambda)\Bigr\rangle\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+s(1-s)\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\langle G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)\bigl(G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\mu)-G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\lambda)\bigr),\mu-\lambda\Bigr\rangle\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+s^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\langle G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)\bigl(G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\mu)-G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\lambda)\bigr),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\mu)-G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\lambda)\Bigr\rangle\mathrm{d}t.

Using the symmetry of
G_{2}^{\prime}(x(t))
now yields

\displaystyle\langle G_{2}K\mu-G_{2}K\lambda,K\mu-K\lambda\rangle=(1-s)^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\langle G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)(\mu-\lambda),\mu-\lambda\Bigr\rangle\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+2s(1-s)\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\langle G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)\bigl(G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\mu)-G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\lambda)\bigr),\mu-\lambda\Bigr\rangle\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+s^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\langle G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)\bigl(G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\mu)-G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\lambda)\bigr),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\mu)-G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\lambda)\Bigl\rangle\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad=(1-s)^{2}I_{1}+2s(1-s)I_{2}+s^{2}I_{3}.

By
eq.

10
, the first term is

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\langle G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)(\mu-\lambda),\mu-\lambda\Bigr\rangle\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\langle G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(z(t)\bigr)(\mu-\lambda),\mu-\lambda\Bigr\rangle\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\langle\Bigl(G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)-G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(z(t)\bigr)\Bigr)(\mu-\lambda),\mu-\lambda\Bigr\rangle\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\langle G_{2}\mu-G_{2}\lambda,\mu-\lambda\rangle+\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\langle\Bigl(G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)-G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(z(t)\bigr)\Bigr)(\mu-\lambda),\mu-\lambda\Bigr\rangle\mathrm{d}t

and thus by the Lipschitz continuity of
G_{2}^{\prime}
on
U
we have

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\langle G_{2}\mu-G_{2}\lambda,\mu-\lambda\rangle+C\|\mu-\lambda\|_{X}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\|x(t)-z(t)\|_{X}\mathrm{d}t.

Similarly, using
eq.

9
, the second term can be written

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\langle G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(y(t)\bigr)\bigl(G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\mu)-G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\lambda)\bigr),\mu-\lambda\Bigr\rangle\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\langle\Bigl(G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)-G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(y(t)\bigr)\Bigr)\bigl(G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\mu)-G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\lambda)\bigr),\mu-\lambda\Bigr\rangle\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\langle G_{1}\mu-G_{1}\lambda,\mu-\lambda\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\langle\bigl(G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)-G_{2}^{\prime}\bigl(y(t)\bigr)\bigr)\bigl(G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\mu)-G_{2}^{-1}(\chi-G_{1}\lambda)\bigr),\mu-\lambda\Bigr\rangle\mathrm{d}t.

By the Lipschitz continuity of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}^{\prime}
on
U
and the Lipschitz continuity of
G_{2}^{-1}
on
X^{*}
, this gives the estimate

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\langle G_{1}\mu-G_{1}\lambda,\mu-\lambda\rangle+C\|\mu-\lambda\|_{X}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\|x(t)-y(t)\|_{X}\mathrm{d}t.

For the same reasons as above and the fact that
G_{2}^{\prime}
being Lipschitz on
U
implies that it is bounded on
U
, we also have the estimate

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\mu-\lambda\|_{X}^{2}.

Putting this together gives

\displaystyle\langle G_{2}K\mu-G_{2}K\lambda,K\mu-K\lambda\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\leq(1-s)^{2}\langle G_{2}\mu-G_{2}\lambda,\mu-\lambda\rangle-2s(1-s)\langle G_{1}\mu-G_{1}\lambda,\mu-\lambda\rangle+s^{2}C\|\mu-\lambda\|_{X}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\|\mu-\lambda\|_{X}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(\|x(t)-y(t)\|_{X}+\|x(t)-z(t)\|_{X}\bigr)\mathrm{d}t.

Using the uniform monotonicity of
G_{1}
and the Lipschitz continuity of
G_{2}
on
U
yields

\displaystyle-\langle G_{1}\mu-G_{1}\lambda,\mu-\lambda\rangle\leq-c\|\mu-\lambda\|_{X}^{2}\leq-C\langle G_{2}\mu-G_{2}\lambda,\mu-\lambda\rangle.

This, together with the uniform monotonicity of
G_{2}
, gives

\displaystyle\langle G_{2}K\mu-G_{2}K\lambda,K\mu-K\lambda\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\leq(1-s)^{2}\langle G_{2}\mu-G_{2}\lambda,\mu-\lambda\rangle-2s(1-s)c\langle G_{2}\mu-G_{2}\lambda,\mu-\lambda\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+s^{2}C\langle G_{2}\mu-G_{2}\lambda,\mu-\lambda\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+C\langle G_{2}\mu-G_{2}\lambda,\mu-\lambda\rangle\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(\|x(t)-y(t)\|_{X}+\|x(t)-z(t)\|_{X}\bigr)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad\leq\biggl((1-s)^{2}-2s(1-s)c+s^{2}C

\displaystyle\qquad+C\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(\|x(t)-y(t)\|_{X}+\|x(t)-z(t)\|_{X}\mathrm{d}t\bigr)\biggr)\langle G_{2}\mu-G_{2}\lambda,\mu-\lambda\rangle.

By choosing
0<s<1
small enough that

L_{1}=(1-s)^{2}-2s(1-s)c+s^{2}C<1

we have, for a fixed constant
C_{1}>0
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle G_{2}K\mu-G_{2}K\lambda,K\mu-K\lambda\rangle&\leq\biggl(L_{1}+C_{1}\int_{0}^{1}\|x(t)-y(t)\|_{X}\\
&\quad+\|x(t)-z(t)\|_{X}\mathrm{d}t\biggr)\langle G_{2}\mu-G_{2}\lambda,\mu-\lambda\rangle.\end{split}

(11)

Since
\mu
and
\lambda
were arbitrary and the constants are independent of
\mu
and
\lambda
, this holds for all
\mu,\lambda\in W
. It remains to estimate the terms
\|x(t)-y(t)\|_{X}
and
\|x(t)-z(t)\|_{X}
. To do this, first observe that

\displaystyle\|x(t)-\eta\|_{X}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|t(K\mu-K\eta)+(1-t)(K\lambda-K\eta)\|_{X}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigl(\|K\mu-K\eta\|_{X}^{2}+\|K\lambda-K\eta)\|_{X}^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq C\bigl(\|\mu-\eta\|_{X}^{2}+\|\lambda-\eta)\|_{X}^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq C(\langle G_{2}\mu-G_{2}\eta,\mu-\eta\rangle+\langle G_{2}\lambda-G_{2}\eta,\lambda-\eta\rangle)

for all
\mu,\lambda\in W
. This and similar observations for
y(t)
and
z(t)
yield the following inequalities

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|x(t)-\eta\|_{X}^{2}&\leq C(\langle G_{2}\mu-G_{2}\eta,\mu-\eta\rangle+\langle G_{2}\lambda-G_{2}\eta,\lambda-\eta\rangle)\\
\|y(t)-\eta\|_{X}^{2}&\leq C(\langle G_{2}\mu-G_{2}\eta,\mu-\eta\rangle+\langle G_{2}\lambda-G_{2}\eta,\lambda-\eta\rangle)\\
\|z(t)-\eta\|_{X}^{2}&\leq C(\langle G_{2}\mu-G_{2}\eta,\mu-\eta\rangle+\langle G_{2}\lambda-G_{2}\eta,\lambda-\eta\rangle)\end{split}

(12)

for all
\mu,\lambda\in W
. Now, for some
r>0
to be chosen later, let

D_{r}(\eta)=\{\mu:\langle G_{2}\mu-G_{2}\eta,\mu-\eta\rangle<r^{2}\}.

We assume that
r>0
is small enough that
D_{r}(\eta)\subset W
. This, together with
eq.

12
, implies that we have

\|x(t)-y(t)\|_{X}\leq Cr\quad\text{and}\quad\|x(t)-z(t)\|_{X}\leq Cr,

for all
\mu,\lambda\in D_{r}(\eta)
. This means that we can choose
r>0
small enough that

C_{1}(\|x(t)-y(t)\|_{X}+\|x(t)-z(t)\|_{X})\leq\frac{1-L_{1}}{2}

for all
\mu,\lambda\in D_{r}(\eta)
. Then, from
eq.

11
, we finally obtain

\displaystyle\langle G_{2}K\mu-G_{2}K\lambda,K\mu-K\lambda\rangle\leq L\langle G_{2}\mu-G_{2}\lambda,\mu-\lambda\rangle

with

L=L_{1}+\frac{1-L_{1}}{2}=\frac{L_{1}+1}{2}<1.

Thus if
\eta^{n+1}=K\eta^{n}
with
\eta^{n}\in D_{r}(\eta)
then

\langle G_{2}\eta^{n+1}-G_{2}\eta,\eta^{n+1}-\eta\rangle\leq L\langle G_{2}\eta^{n}-G_{2}\eta,\eta^{n}-\eta\rangle,

which implies that
\eta^{n+1}\in D_{r}(\eta)
. If we assume that
\eta^{0}\in D_{r}(\eta)
and iterate the above inequality we get

\displaystyle c\|\eta^{n+1}-\eta\|_{X}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\langle G_{2}\eta^{n+1}-G_{2}\eta,\eta^{n+1}-\eta\rangle

\displaystyle\leq L^{n+1}\langle G_{2}\eta^{0}-G_{2}\eta,\eta^{0}-\eta\rangle\leq CL^{n+1}\|\eta^{0}-\eta\|_{X}^{2}.

Note that, by the Lipschitz continuity of
G_{2}
, the condition
\eta^{0}\in D_{r}(\eta)
is satisfied if
\eta^{0}
is close enough to
\eta
in
X
.

Weak formulations of semilinear elliptic equations

To state our weak formulations we require the following assumption.

Assumption 2

The sets
\Omega

With

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\qquad V_{i}^{0}=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{i}),

\displaystyle V_{i}

[

]

We make the following assumptions on the equation

1
.

Assumption 3
.

Let

the Sobolev conjugate of

. If

then
p^{*}
can be chosen arbitrarily large. We make the following assumptions on

,
\beta:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and
f
.

•

The function
\alpha
is in

.

•

The function

and

. Moreover,
\beta

,

\bigl|\beta_{y}(x,y)-\beta_{y}(x,y^{\prime})\bigr|\leq L_{1}(y,y^{\prime})|y-y^{\prime}|

(13)

for all

•

We have the linear approximation bound

\bigl|\beta\bigl(x,y\bigr)-\beta\bigl(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\bigr)-\beta_{y}(x,y^{\prime})(y-y^{\prime})\bigr|\leq L_{2}(y,y^{\prime})|y-y^{\prime}|^{2}

(14)

for all
y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
and almost every

. The constant is assumed to satisfy

L_{2}(y,y^{\prime})\leq C(1+|y|^{p^{*}-3}+|y^{\prime}|^{p^{*}-3})\qquad\text{for all }y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}.

(15)

•

The function
\beta
satisfies the following monotonicity condition

\bigl(\beta(x,y)-\beta(x,y^{\prime})\bigr)\bigl(y-y^{\prime}\bigr)\geq-h(x)|y-y^{\prime}|^{2}

(16)

for all

and almost every

satisfies

\inf_{x\in\Omega}\alpha(x)>C_{p}\sup_{x\in\Omega}h(x),

(17)

where

is the maximum of the Poincaré constants of
\Omega
and
\Omega_{i}
.

•

The function
\beta_{y}
satisfies the coercivity condition

\beta_{y}(x,y)\geq-h(x)\qquad\text{for all }y\in\mathbb{R}\text{ and almost every }x\in\Omega.

(18)

Here the function
h
satisfies
eq.

17
.

•

f\in V^{*}
and there exist
f_{i}\in V_{i}^{*}
such that

\langle f,v\rangle=\langle f_{1},\left.v\right|_{\Omega_{1}}\rangle+\langle f_{2},\left.v\right|_{\Omega_{2}}\rangle.

Example 4
.

Let
\alpha(x)=1
and
\beta(x,y)=10|y|^{2}y
. The derivative of
\beta
is

\beta_{y}(x,y)=30|y|^{2},

which gives that

|\beta_{y}(x,y)-\beta_{y}(x,y^{\prime})|\leq 30(|y|+|y^{\prime}|)|y-y^{\prime}|

and

\displaystyle\bigl|\beta\bigl(x,y\bigr)-\beta\bigl(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\bigr)-\beta_{y}(x,y^{\prime})(y-y^{\prime})\bigr|

\displaystyle\quad=10|y+2y^{\prime}||y-y^{\prime}|^{2}\leq 20(|y|+|y^{\prime}|)|y-y^{\prime}|^{2}.

Example 5
.

Let
\beta(x,y)=y
. If we replace
\alpha(x)
in
eqs.

1
and
2
with

\alpha(\nabla u)=|\nabla u|

we get the
p
-Laplace equation with
p=3
, a degenerate quasilinear equation that does not satisfy
Assumption

3
, but which will be used to demonstrate the generality of the nonlinear Dirichlet–Neumann method.

Let
A:V\rightarrow V^{*}
and
A_{i}:V_{i}\rightarrow V_{i}^{*}
be defined by

\displaystyle\langle Au,v\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+\beta(x,u)v\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\langle A_{i}u_{i},v_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{i}}\alpha(x)\nabla u_{i}\cdot\nabla v_{i}+\beta(x,u_{i})v_{i}\mathrm{d}x,

respectively. The weak formulation of
eq.

1
is to find
u\in V
such that

\langle Au,v\rangle=\langle f,v\rangle\qquad\textrm{for all }v\in V.

(19)

We also define the operator
A_{i}^{\prime}:V_{i}\rightarrow S(V_{i},V_{i}^{*})

\displaystyle\langle A_{i}^{\prime}(w_{i})u_{i},v_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{i}}\alpha(x)\nabla u_{i}\cdot\nabla v_{i}+\beta_{y}(x,w_{i})u_{i}v_{i}\mathrm{d}x.

The notation
A_{i}^{\prime}
is justified by the following theorem.

Theorem 6
.

Suppose that
Assumptions

2
and
3
holds. The operator
A_{i}:V_{i}\rightarrow V_{i}^{*}
is uniformly monotone and Lipschitz continuous on any bounded set
U\subset V_{i}
. A similar result holds for
A
. In particular,
eq.

19
has a unique solution.

For any
w\in V_{i}
, the operator
A_{i}^{\prime}(w):V_{i}\rightarrow V_{i}^{*}
is coercive, symmetric, and linear. Moreover,
A_{i}
is Fréchet differentiable with Fréchet derivative
A_{i}^{\prime}:V_{i}\rightarrow S(V_{i},V_{i}^{*})
. The Fréchet derivative
A_{i}^{\prime}
is Lipschitz continuous on any bounded set
U\subset V_{i}
.

Proof.

First recall the inequality,

(x+y)^{r}\leq\max(1,2^{r-1})(x^{r}+y^{r}),\quad r,x,y\geq 0,

which follows from the convexity of the function
x\mapsto x^{r}
when
r\geq 1
and the subadditivity of the same function when
r<1
. This inequality will be used many times throughout the proof without explicit mention. We note that
eq.

13
and
\beta_{y}(\cdot,0)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
implies that

\bigl|\beta_{y}(x,y)\bigr|\leq\bigl|\beta_{y}(x,y)-\beta_{y}(x,0)\bigr|+\bigl|\beta_{y}(x,0)\bigr|\leq L_{0}(y)

(20)

for all
y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
and almost every
x\in\Omega
. Here
L_{0}
satisfies

L_{0}(y)\leq C\bigl(1+|y|^{p^{*}-2}\bigr)\qquad\text{for all }y\in\mathbb{R}.

(21)

In turn,
eq.

20
implies that

|\beta(x,y)-\beta(x,y^{\prime})|=\Bigl|\int_{y^{\prime}}^{y}\beta_{y}(x,t)\mathrm{d}t\Bigr|\leq C(1+|y|^{p^{*}-2}+|y^{\prime}|^{p^{*}-2})|y-y^{\prime}|

for all
y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
and almost every
x\in\Omega
. With this observation and the monotonicity
eq.

16
, the fact that
A_{i}
and
A
are uniformly monotone and Lipschitz continuous and that
eq.

19
has a unique solution follows as in
[
6
, Lemma 1]
.

We now let
w\in V_{i}
and consider
A_{i}^{\prime}(w)
. We first show that
A_{i}^{\prime}(w)\in S(V_{i},V_{i}^{*})
. The symmetry is obvious from the definition. To show boundedness let
u,v\in V_{i}
, recall the Sobolev conjugate
p^{*}
(of
p=2
), and let

q^{\prime}=\frac{p^{*}}{p^{*}-2}.

Since we have the Sobolev embedding
V_{i}\hookrightarrow L^{p*}(\Omega_{i})
, it follows that
u,v,w\in L^{p*}(\Omega_{i})
and

\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}\leq C\|u\|_{V_{i}},

with similar bounds for
v
and
w
. Moreover, by
eq.

21
we have

|L_{0}(w)|^{q^{\prime}}\leq C\bigl(1+|w|^{p^{*}}\bigr)

and therefore
L_{0}(w)\in L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega_{i})
with

\|L_{0}(w)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega_{i})}\leq C\bigl(1+\|w\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}^{p^{*}-2}\bigr).

Using the Hölder inequality twice, first with
q^{\prime}
and its Hölder conjugate
p^{*}/2
and then with
2
and its Hölder conjugate
2
, yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{i}}L_{0}(w)|u||v|\mathrm{d}x\leq\|L_{0}(w)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega_{i})}\biggl(\int_{\Omega_{i}}|u|^{p^{*}/2}|v|^{p^{*}/2}\mathrm{d}x\biggr)^{2/p^{*}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|L_{0}(w)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega_{i})}\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}\|v\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}.

With these observations, we have from
eq.

20
that

\displaystyle\bigl|\langle A_{i}^{\prime}(w)u,v\rangle\bigr|=\biggl|\int_{\Omega_{i}}\alpha(x)\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+\beta_{y}(x,w)uv\mathrm{d}x\biggr|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{i})}\|u\|_{V_{i}}\|v\|_{V_{i}}+\|L_{0}(w)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega_{i})}\|u\|_{L^{p*}(\Omega_{i})}\|v\|_{L^{p*}(\Omega_{i})}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{i})}\|u\|_{V_{i}}\|v\|_{V_{i}}+C(1+\|w\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})})^{p^{*}-2}\|u\|_{L^{p*}(\Omega_{i})}\|v\|_{L^{p*}(\Omega_{i})}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{i})}\|u\|_{V_{i}}\|v\|_{V_{i}}+C(1+\|w\|_{V_{i}})^{p^{*}-2}\|u\|_{V_{i}}\|v\|_{V_{i}}.

Thus we have shown that
A_{i}^{\prime}(w)
is bounded. The coercivity of
A_{i}^{\prime}(w)
follows from
eqs.

17
and
18
since

\displaystyle\langle A_{i}^{\prime}(w)u,u\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{i}}\alpha(x)|\nabla u|^{2}+\beta_{y}(x,w)|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq\inf_{x\in\Omega_{i}}\alpha(x)|u|_{V_{i}}^{2}-\sup_{x\in\Omega_{i}}h(x)\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq c\|u\|_{V_{i}}^{2}.

We now show that
A^{\prime}_{i}
is the Fréchet derivative of
A_{i}
. Let
u,v,h\in V_{i}
such that
\|h\|_{V_{i}}\rightarrow 0
. The argument is similar as above, but we let

q=\frac{p^{*}}{p^{*}-3}.

It follows that
u,v,h\in L^{p*}(\Omega_{i})
with appropriate bounds. Moreover, by
eq.

15
we have

|L_{2}(u+h,u)|^{q}\leq C\bigl(1+|u+h|^{p^{*}}+|u|^{p^{*}}\bigr)

and therefore
L_{2}(u+h,u)\in L^{q}(\Omega_{i})
with

\|L_{2}(u+h,u)\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{i})}\leq C\bigl(1+\|u+h\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}^{p^{*}-3}+\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}^{p^{*}-3}\bigr).

Using the Hölder inequality twice again, this time first with
q
and its Hölder conjugate
p^{*}/3
and then with
3
and its Hölder conjugate
3/2
, yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{i}}L_{2}(u+h,u)|h|^{2}|v|\mathrm{d}x\leq\|L_{2}(u+h,u)\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{i})}\biggl(\int_{\Omega_{i}}|h|^{2p^{*}/3}|v|^{p^{*}/3}\mathrm{d}x\biggr)^{3/p^{*}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|L_{2}(u+h,u)\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{i})}\|v\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}\biggl(\int_{\Omega_{i}}|h|^{p^{*}}\mathrm{d}x\biggr)^{2/p^{*}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|L_{2}(u+h,u)\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{i})}\|v\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}\|h\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}.

These observations together with
eq.

14
gives the estimate

\displaystyle\|A_{i}(u+h)-A_{i}(u)-A_{i}^{\prime}(u)h\|_{V_{i}^{*}}=\sup_{\|v\|_{V_{i}}=1}\bigl|\langle A_{i}(u+h)-A_{i}(u)-A_{i}^{\prime}(u)h,v\rangle\bigr|

\displaystyle\quad=\sup_{\|v\|_{V_{i}}=1}\biggl|\int_{\Omega_{i}}\bigl(\beta(x,u+h)-\beta(x,u)-\beta_{y}(x,u)h\bigr)v\mathrm{d}x\biggr|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sup_{\|v\|_{V_{i}}=1}\int_{\Omega_{i}}L_{2}(u+h,u)|h|^{2}|v|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sup_{\|v\|_{V_{i}}=1}\|L_{2}(u+h,u)\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{i})}\|h\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}\|v\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sup_{\|v\|_{V_{i}}=1}C\bigl(1+\|u+h\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}^{p^{*}-3}+\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}^{p^{*}-3}\bigr)\|h\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}\|v\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sup_{\|v\|_{V_{i}}=1}C\bigl(1+\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}^{p^{*}-3}+\|h\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}^{p^{*}-3}\bigr)\|h\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}\|v\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sup_{\|v\|_{V_{i}}=1}C(1+\|u\|_{V_{i}}^{p^{*}-3}+\|h\|_{V_{i}}^{p^{*}-3}\bigr)\|h\|_{V_{i}}^{2}\|v\|_{V_{i}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(1+\|u\|_{V_{i}}^{p^{*}-3}+\|h\|_{V_{i}}^{p^{*}-3}\bigr)\|h\|_{V_{i}}^{2}.

Dividing by
\|h\|_{V_{i}}
and letting
h\rightarrow 0
yields the appropriate limit. It remains to show that
A^{\prime}_{i}
is Lipschitz continuous. Let
w,w^{\prime}\in V_{i}
. Arguing similarly as above using
eq.

13
shows that

\displaystyle\bigl|\bigl\langle\bigl(A^{\prime}_{i}(w)-A^{\prime}_{i}(w^{\prime})\bigr)u,v\bigr\rangle\bigr|

\displaystyle\quad=\biggl|\int_{\Omega_{i}}\bigl(\beta_{y}(x,w)-\beta_{y}(x,w^{\prime})\bigr)uv\mathrm{d}x\biggr|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{\Omega_{i}}L_{1}(w,w^{\prime})|w-w^{\prime}||u||v|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\|L_{1}(w,w^{\prime})\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{i})}\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}}\|v\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(1+\|w\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}+\|w^{\prime}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})})^{p^{*}-3}\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}\|v\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{i})}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(1+\|w\|_{V_{i}}+\|w^{\prime}\|_{V_{i}})^{p^{*}-3}\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{V_{i}}\|u\|_{V_{i}}\|v\|_{V_{i}}

for all
u,v\in V_{i}
. This gives

\displaystyle\|A^{\prime}_{i}(w)-A^{\prime}_{i}(w^{\prime})\|_{B(V_{i},V_{i}^{*})}=\sup_{\|u\|_{V_{i}}=1}\bigl\|\bigl(A^{\prime}_{i}(w)-A^{\prime}_{i}(w^{\prime})\bigr)u\bigr\|_{V_{i}^{*}}

\displaystyle\quad=\sup_{\|u\|_{V_{i}}=\|v\|_{V_{i}}=1}\bigl|\bigl\langle\bigl(A^{\prime}_{i}(w)-A^{\prime}_{i}(w^{\prime})\bigr)u,v\bigr\rangle\bigr|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sup_{\|u\|_{V_{i}}=\|v\|_{V_{i}}=1}C(1+\|w\|_{V_{i}}+\|w^{\prime}\|_{V_{i}})^{p^{*}-3}\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{V_{i}}\|u\|_{V_{i}}\|v\|_{V_{i}}

\displaystyle\quad=C(1+\|w\|_{V_{i}}+\|w^{\prime}\|_{V_{i}})^{p^{*}-3}\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{V_{i}},

which shows that
A_{i}^{\prime}
is Lipschitz continuous on any bounded set.

4
Transmission problem and Steklov–Poincaré operators

The transmission problem is to find
(u_{1},u_{2})\in V_{1}\times V_{2}
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} \langle A_{i}u_{i},v_{i}\rangle&=\langle f_{i},v_{i}\rangle&&\text{for all }v_{i}\in V_{i}^{0},\,i=1,2,\\
T_{1}u_{1}&=T_{2}u_{2},&&\\
\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\langle A_{i}&u_{i},R_{i}\mu\rangle-\langle f_{i},R_{i}\mu\rangle=0&&\text{for all }\mu\in\Lambda.\end{aligned}\right.

(22)

The transmission problem is equivalent to the weak equation
eq.

19
, see, e.g.,
[
5
, Theorem 5.2]
for a proof.

Before introducing the Steklov–Poincaré operators we must define the solution operators. For
\eta\in\Lambda
let
u_{i}=F_{i}\eta
denote the solution to the problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} \langle A_{i}u_{i}-f_{i},v\rangle&=0\qquad&\text{for all }v\in V_{i}^{0},\\
T_{i}u_{i}&=\eta.&\end{aligned}\right.

(23)

Similarly, for
\eta,\nu\in\Lambda
let
u_{i}=F^{\prime}_{i}(\nu)\eta
denote the solution to the problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} \langle A^{\prime}_{i}(F_{i}\nu)u_{i},v\rangle&=0\qquad&\text{for all }v\in V_{i}^{0},\\
T_{i}u_{i}&=\eta.&\end{aligned}\right.

(24)

It has been proven that both of these operators exists under weaker assumptions, see
[
5
, Lemma 6.1]
. The notation
F^{\prime}
is justified by the following theorem.

Theorem 7
.

Suppose that
Assumptions

3
and
2
hold. The operators
F_{i}:\Lambda\rightarrow V_{i}
are Lipschitz continuous on any bounded subset
U\subset\Lambda
. Moreover, they are Fréchet differentiable with Fréchet derivative
F^{\prime}_{i}:\Lambda\rightarrow B(\Lambda,V_{i})
defined through
eq.

24
. The operators
F^{\prime}_{i}
are Lipschitz continuous on any bounded subset
U\subset\Lambda
.

Proof.

For the proof of fact that
F_{i}
is Lipschitz continuous we refer to
[
6
, Lemma 2]
.

We first show that
F^{\prime}_{i}
is bounded, i.e., that it maps bounded sets to bounded sets. Let
U
be a bounded subset
U\subset\Lambda
. Since
F_{i}
is Lipschitz on
U
it is also bounded on
U
, which means that
F_{i}(U)
is a bounded set. Also, for the same reason,
A_{i}^{\prime}
is bounded on
F_{i}(U)
. It follows that

\|A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu)F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta\|_{V_{i}^{*}}\leq C\|F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta\|_{V_{i}}\quad\text{for all }\nu\in U,\eta\in\Lambda.

Thus, by the boundedness of
R_{i}
, the coercivity of
A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu)
, and the fact that
F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta-R_{i}\eta\in V_{i}^{0}
, we have

\displaystyle c\|F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta\|_{V_{i}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\langle A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu)F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta,F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu)F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta,R_{i}\eta\rangle+\langle A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu)F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta,F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta-R_{i}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu)F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta,R_{i}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu)F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta\|_{V_{i}^{*}}\|R_{i}\eta\|_{V_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta\|_{V_{i}}\|\eta\|_{\Lambda}

for all
\nu\in U,\eta\in\Lambda
.
It follows that

\|F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\|_{B(\Lambda,V_{i})}\leq C

for all
\nu\in U
. Thus we have shown that
F_{i}^{\prime}
is bounded.

We now verify that
F_{i}^{\prime}
is the Fréchet derivative of
F_{i}
. Let
\nu,h\in\Lambda
and introduce
u=F_{i}\nu
,
k=F_{i}(\nu+h)-F_{i}\nu
, and
w=F_{i}(\nu+h)-F_{i}\nu-F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)h
. Note, in particular, that
w\in V_{i}^{0}
so that

\Bigl\langle A_{i}(u+k)-A_{i}(u),w\Bigr\rangle=0\quad\text{and}\quad\Bigl\langle A^{\prime}_{i}(F_{i}\nu)F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)h,w\Bigr\rangle=0.

Therefore, by the coercivity of
A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu)
,

\displaystyle\|F_{i}(\nu+h)-F_{i}\nu-F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)h\|_{V_{i}}^{2}\leq\Bigl\langle A^{\prime}_{i}(F_{i}\nu)\bigl(F_{i}(\nu+h)-F_{i}\nu-F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)h\bigr),w\Bigr\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=\Bigl\langle A^{\prime}_{i}(F_{i}\nu)\bigl(F_{i}(\nu+h)-F_{i}\nu\bigr),w\Bigr\rangle=\langle A^{\prime}_{i}(u)k,w\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=-\langle A_{i}(u+k)-A_{i}(u)-A^{\prime}_{i}(u)k,w\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|A_{i}(u+k)-A_{i}(u)-A^{\prime}_{i}(u)k\|_{V_{i}^{*}}\|w\|_{V_{i}}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\|A_{i}(u+k)-A_{i}(u)-A^{\prime}_{i}(u)k\|_{V_{i}^{*}}}{\|k\|_{V_{i}}}\|k\|_{V_{i}}\|F_{i}(\nu+h)-F_{i}\nu-F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)h\|_{V_{i}}.

We divide by
\|h\|_{\Lambda}\|F_{i}(\nu+h)-F_{i}\nu-F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)h\|_{V_{i}}
and let
\|h\|_{\Lambda}\rightarrow 0
. Since
F_{i}
is Lipschitz continuous in a neighbourhood around
\nu
this implies that
\|k\|_{V_{i}}\rightarrow 0
and that
\|k\|_{V_{i}}/\|h\|_{\Lambda}
is bounded. Since
A_{i}
is Fréchet differentiable, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\|F_{i}(\nu+h)-F_{i}\nu-F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)h\|_{V_{i}}}{\|h\|_{\Lambda}}\leq\frac{\|A_{i}(u+k)-A_{i}(u)-A^{\prime}_{i}(u)k\|_{V_{i}^{*}}}{\|k\|_{V_{i}}}\frac{\|k\|_{V_{i}}}{\|h\|_{\Lambda}}\rightarrow 0

as
\|h\|_{\Lambda}\rightarrow 0
. We now show that
F_{i}^{\prime}
is Lipschitz continuous on any bounded set. To show this we first note that
F^{\prime}_{i}(\nu)\eta-F^{\prime}_{i}(\lambda)\eta\in V_{i}^{0}
, which yields the identity

\displaystyle\Bigl\langle A^{\prime}_{i}(F_{i}\nu)\bigl(F^{\prime}_{i}(\nu)-F^{\prime}_{i}(\lambda)\bigr)\eta,F^{\prime}_{i}(\nu)\eta-F^{\prime}_{i}(\lambda)\eta\Bigr\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=\Bigl\langle 0-A^{\prime}_{i}(F_{i}\nu)F^{\prime}_{i}(\lambda)\eta,F^{\prime}_{i}(\nu)\eta-F^{\prime}_{i}(\lambda)\eta\Bigr\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=\Bigl\langle\Bigl(A^{\prime}_{i}(F_{i}\lambda)-A^{\prime}_{i}(F_{i}\nu)\Bigr)F^{\prime}_{i}(\lambda)\eta,F^{\prime}_{i}(\nu)\eta-F^{\prime}_{i}(\lambda)\eta\Bigr\rangle

for all
\nu,\lambda\in\Lambda
. Now consider any bounded subset
U\subset\Lambda
. Then
F_{i}
is Lipschitz continuous on
U
. In particular,
F_{i}(U)
is a bounded set. Therefore,
A_{i}^{\prime}
is Lipschitz continuous on
F_{i}(U)
. The Lipschitz continuity now follows from these observations and the coercivity of
A^{\prime}_{i}(F_{i}\nu)
since

\displaystyle c\|F^{\prime}_{i}(\nu)\eta-F^{\prime}_{i}(\lambda)\eta\|_{V_{i}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl\langle A^{\prime}_{i}(F_{i}\nu)\bigl(F^{\prime}_{i}(\nu)-F^{\prime}_{i}(\lambda)\bigr)\eta,F^{\prime}_{i}(\nu)\eta-F^{\prime}_{i}(\lambda)\eta\Bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=\Bigl\langle\bigl(A^{\prime}_{i}(F_{i}\lambda)-A^{\prime}_{i}(F_{i}\nu)\bigr)F^{\prime}_{i}(\lambda)\eta,F^{\prime}_{i}(\nu)\eta-F^{\prime}_{i}(\lambda)\eta\Bigr\rangle

\displaystyle\leq C\|\lambda-\nu\|_{\Lambda}\|F^{\prime}_{i}(\lambda)\eta\|_{V_{i}}\|F^{\prime}_{i}(\nu)\eta-F^{\prime}_{i}(\lambda)\eta\|_{V_{i}}

for all
\nu,\lambda\in U
and
\eta\in\Lambda
. Dividing by
\|F^{\prime}_{i}(\nu)\eta-F^{\prime}_{i}(\lambda)\eta\|_{V_{i}}
finishes the proof since we have shown that
F^{\prime}_{i}
is bounded.

The Steklov–Poincaré operators
S_{i}:\Lambda\to\Lambda^{*}
are defined as

\displaystyle\langle S_{i}\eta,\mu\rangle=\langle A_{i}F_{i}\eta-f_{i},R_{i}\mu\rangle.

We also define
S^{\prime}:\Lambda\to B(\Lambda,\Lambda^{*})
as

\displaystyle\langle S_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta,\mu\rangle=\langle A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu)F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta,R_{i}\mu\rangle.

Moreover, we introduce
S=S_{1}+S_{2}
. The Steklov–Poincaré equation is then to find
\eta\in\Lambda
such that

\langle S\eta,\mu\rangle=0\quad\text{for all }\mu\in\Lambda.

(25)

The Steklov–Poincaré equation is equivalent to the transmission problem by the formulas
\eta=T_{i}u_{i}
and
(u_{1},u_{2})=(F_{1}\eta,F_{2}\eta)
, see
[
5
, Lemma 6.2]
for details.

Theorem 8
.

Suppose that
Assumptions

3
and
2
hold. The Steklov–Poincaré operators
S_{i}:\Lambda\rightarrow\Lambda^{*}
are Fréchet differentiable with Fréchet derivatives
S_{i}^{\prime}:\Lambda\rightarrow S(\Lambda,\Lambda^{*})
. The operators
S,S_{i},S_{i}^{\prime}
are Lipschitz continuous on any bounded set
U\subset\Lambda
. Moreover, the operators
S,S_{i}
are uniformly monotone.

Proof.

First let
\nu\in\Lambda
and consider
S_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)
. That
S_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\in B(\Lambda,\Lambda^{*})
follows from

\displaystyle\bigl|\langle S_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta,\mu\rangle\bigr|=\bigl|\langle A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu)F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta,R_{i}\mu\rangle\bigr|\leq\|A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu)F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta\|_{V_{i}}\|R_{i}\mu\|_{V_{i}}\leq C\|\eta\|_{\Lambda}\|\mu\|_{\Lambda}.

Moreover, by the symmetry of
A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu)
and the fact that
R_{i}\mu-F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\mu\in V_{i}^{0}
we have

\displaystyle\langle S_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta,\mu\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu)F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta,R_{i}\mu\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu)F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta,F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\mu\rangle+\langle A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu)F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta,R_{i}\mu-F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\mu\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu)F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta,F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\mu\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu)F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\mu,F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu)F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\mu,R_{i}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle S_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\mu,\eta\rangle,

which shows that
S_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)\in S(\Lambda,\Lambda^{*})
.

Now note that
S_{i}=R_{i}^{*}(A_{i}F_{i}-f_{i})
, where
R_{i}^{*}:V_{i}^{*}\rightarrow\Lambda^{*}
is the bounded linear operator that maps
\langle R_{i}^{*}u,\mu\rangle=\langle u,R_{i}\mu\rangle
. From
theorems

6
and
7
we have that
S_{i}
is Lipschitz continuous and the same holds for
S
. We also have from
theorems

6
and
7
and the chain rule
[
11
, Lemma 9.6]
that
S_{i}
is Fréchet differentiable with Fréchet derivative
R_{i}^{*}A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu)F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)=S_{i}^{\prime}(\nu)
. Moreover, the monotonicity of
S_{i},S
follows as in
[
6
, Theorem 1]
.

It remain to show that
S_{i}^{\prime}
is Lipschitz continuous on any bounded subset
U\subset\Lambda
. We first note that
F_{i}
and
F_{i}^{\prime}
are Lipschitz continuous on
U
and
A_{i}^{\prime}
is Lipschitz continuous on the bounded set
F_{i}(U)
. Therefore

\displaystyle\bigl|\bigl\langle\bigl(S^{\prime}_{i}(\nu_{1})-S^{\prime}_{i}(\nu_{2})\bigr)\eta,\mu\bigr\rangle\bigr|=\bigl|\bigl\langle A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu_{1})F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu_{1})\eta-A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu_{2})F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu_{2})\eta,R_{i}\mu\bigr\rangle\bigr|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\bigl\|A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu_{1})F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu_{1})\eta-A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu_{2})F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu_{2})\eta\bigr\|_{V_{i}^{*}}\|R_{i}\mu\|_{V_{i}}

\displaystyle\quad=\bigl\|A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu_{1})\bigl(F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu_{1})-F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu_{2})\bigr)\eta-\bigl(A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu_{2})-A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu_{1})\bigr)F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu_{2})\eta\bigr\|_{V_{i}^{*}}\|R_{i}\mu\|_{V_{i}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\Bigl(\bigl\|A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu_{1})\bigl(F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu_{1})-F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu_{2})\bigr)\eta\bigr\|_{V_{i}^{*}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\bigl\|\bigl(A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu_{2})-A_{i}^{\prime}(F_{i}\nu_{1})\bigr)F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu_{2})\eta\bigr\|_{V_{i}^{*}}\Bigr)\|R_{i}\mu\|_{V_{i}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\Bigl(C\|F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu_{1})\eta-F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu_{2})\eta\|_{V_{i}^{*}}+\|F_{i}\nu_{2}-F_{i}\nu_{1}\|_{V_{i}}\|F_{i}^{\prime}(\nu_{2})\eta\|_{V_{i}}\Bigr)\|R_{i}\mu\|_{V_{i}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\Bigl(C\|\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}\|_{\Lambda}\|\eta\|_{\Lambda}+\|\nu_{2}-\nu_{1}\|_{\Lambda}\|\eta\|_{\Lambda}\Bigr)\|\mu\|_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\|\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}\|_{\Lambda}\|\eta\|_{\Lambda}\|\mu\|_{\Lambda}

for all
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in U
and
\eta,\mu\in\Lambda
. This implies that

\displaystyle\|S^{\prime}_{i}(\nu_{1})-S^{\prime}_{i}(\nu_{2})\|_{S(\Lambda,\Lambda^{*})}=\sup_{\eta\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\bigl\|\bigl(S^{\prime}_{i}(\nu_{1})-S^{\prime}_{i}(\nu_{2})\bigr)\eta\bigr\|_{\Lambda^{*}}}{\|\eta\|_{\Lambda}}

\displaystyle\quad=\sup_{\eta,\mu\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\bigl|\bigl\langle\bigl(S^{\prime}_{i}(\nu_{1})-S^{\prime}_{i}(\nu_{2})\bigr)\eta,\mu\bigr\rangle\bigr|}{\|\eta\|_{\Lambda}\|\mu\|_{\Lambda}}\leq C\|\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}\|_{\Lambda}

for all
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in U
, which means that
S_{i}^{\prime}
is Lipschitz continuous on
U
.

5
Convergence of the Dirichlet–Neumann method

The weak formulation of the Dirichlet–Neumann method is to find
(u_{1}^{n+1},u_{2}^{n+1})\in V_{1}\times V_{2}
for
n=0,1,2,\dots
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\langle A_{1}u_{1}^{n+1},v_{1}\rangle=\langle f_{1},v_{1}\rangle&&\text{for all }v_{1}\in V_{1}^{0},\\
&T_{1}u_{1}^{n+1}=\eta^{n},\\[5.0pt]
&\langle A_{2}u_{2}^{n+1},v_{2}\rangle=\langle f_{2},v_{2}\rangle&&\text{for all }v_{2}\in V_{2}^{0},\\
&\langle A_{2}u_{2}^{n+1},R_{2}\mu\rangle-\langle f_{2},R_{2}\mu\rangle+\langle A_{1}u_{1}^{n+1},R_{1}\mu\rangle&&\\
&\quad-\langle f_{1},R_{1}\mu\rangle=0&&\text{for all }\mu\in\Lambda,\end{aligned}\right.

(26)

with
\eta^{n+1}=sT_{2}u_{2}^{n+1}+(1-s)\eta^{n}
. The interface iteration corresponding to the Dirichlet–Neumann method is to find
\eta^{n+1}\in\Lambda
for
n=0,1,2,\dots
such that

\eta^{n+1}=(1-s)\eta^{n}+sS_{2}^{-1}(0-S_{1}\eta^{n}).

(27)

The equivalence of the Dirichlet–Neumann method and the interface iteration is stated precisely in the following lemma.

Lemma 9
.

Suppose that
Assumptions

3
and
2
hold. The Dirichlet–Neumann method is equivalent to the interface iteration in the following way. If
\{\eta^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
solves
eq.

27
then
\{(u_{1}^{n+1},u_{2}^{n+1})\}_{n\geq 0}
defined by

\displaystyle u_{1}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=F_{1}\eta^{n}

\displaystyle u_{2}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=F_{2}\biggl(\frac{\eta^{n+1}-(1-s)\eta^{n}}{s}\biggr)

solves
eq.

26
. Conversely, if
\{(u_{1}^{n+1},u_{2}^{n+1})\}_{n\geq 0}
solves
eq.

26
then
\{\eta^{n}=T_{i}u_{1}^{n+1}\}_{n\geq 0}
solves
eq.

27
.

Proof.

The first two equations of
eq.

26
are equivalent to
u_{1}^{n+1}=F_{1}\eta^{n}
and the third equation of
eq.

26
together with the update equation
\eta^{n+1}=sT_{2}u_{2}^{n+1}+(1-s)\eta^{n}
is equivalent to

u_{2}^{n+1}=F_{2}\biggl(\frac{\eta^{n+1}-(1-s)\eta^{n}}{s}\biggr).

If we write out
eq.

27
using
S_{i}=R_{i}^{*}A_{i}F_{i}-R_{i}^{*}f_{i}
we find that it is equivalent to

R_{2}^{*}A_{2}F_{2}\biggl(\frac{\eta^{n+1}-(1-s)\eta^{n}}{s}\biggr)-R_{2}^{*}f_{2}=R_{1}^{*}A_{1}F_{1}\eta^{n}-R_{1}^{*}f_{1}\quad\text{in }\Lambda^{*},

which is exactly the last equation of
eq.

26
.

We finally end up with the following convergence result, which is a direct consequence of
theorems

1
,
8
and
9
.

Corollary 10
.

Suppose that
Assumptions

3
and
2
hold. Let
s>0
be small enough and
\eta^{0}\in\Lambda
close enough to
\eta
, the solution to the Steklov–Poincaré equation
eq.

25
. The iteration
eq.

27
converges linearly to
\eta
in
\Lambda
. Moreover, the iterates of the Dirichlet–Neumann method
eq.

26
converges linearly to
(u_{1},u_{2})
, the solution of
eq.

22
, in
V_{1}\times V_{2}
.

Proof.

From
theorems

1
and
8
it follows that
\eta^{n}
converges linearly to
\eta
, i.e,

\|\eta^{n}-\eta\|_{\Lambda}\leq CL^{n}\|\eta^{0}-\eta\|_{\Lambda}.

In particular, it follows that
\eta^{n}
is bounded so there exists a convex and bounded set
U
that contains
\eta^{n}
and
\eta
. Since
F_{1}
is Lipschitz continuous on
U
we have

\|u_{1}^{n+1}-u_{1}\|_{V_{i}}=\|F_{1}\eta^{n}-F_{1}\eta\|_{V_{i}}\leq C\|\eta^{n}-\eta\|_{\Lambda}\leq CL^{n}\|\eta^{0}-\eta\|_{\Lambda}.

Since
F_{2}
is also Lipschitz continuous on
U
we have

\displaystyle\|u_{2}^{n+1}-u_{2}\|_{V_{i}}=\biggl\|F_{2}\biggl(\frac{\eta^{n+1}-(1-s)\eta^{n}}{s}\biggr)-F_{2}\eta\biggr\|_{V_{i}}\leq C\biggl\|\frac{\eta^{n+1}-(1-s)\eta^{n}}{s}-\eta\biggr\|_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{C}{s}\|(\eta^{n+1}-\eta)-(1-s)(\eta^{n}-\eta)\|_{\Lambda}\leq C\bigl(\|\eta^{n+1}-\eta\|_{\Lambda}+\|\eta^{n}-\eta)\|_{\Lambda}\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad\leq CL^{n}\|\eta^{0}-\eta\|_{\Lambda}.

Therefore,
(u_{1}^{n},u_{2}^{n})
converges linearly to
(u_{1},u_{2})
in
V_{1}\times V_{2}
.

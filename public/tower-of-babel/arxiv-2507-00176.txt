Entanglement, Trace Anomaly and Confinement in QCD
Kiminad A. Mamo

Abstract
We formulate confinement in QCD as an entropic surface phenomenon. Quark and gluon quantum information is localized on a transverse two–dimensional sheet of radius R E ​ E R_{\!EE} ; at this radius the QCD vacuum—partitioned by a hadron into interior and exterior regions—reaches its maximal entanglement entropy. Lattice‑QCD determinations of scalar (trace) gravitational form factors of hadrons fix both R E ​ E R_{\!EE} and the transverse trace‑anomaly density ρ h ​ ( R E ​ E ) \rho_{h}(R_{\!EE}) with percent‑level accuracy, enabling a parameter‑free mechanical entropy S E ​ E ​ ( y ) = c h ​ y S_{\!EE}(y)=c_{h}\,y growing linearly with rapidity y y . The associated entropy gradient changes sign at R E ​ E R_{\!EE} : it repels colour fields for r < R E ​ E r<R_{\!EE} and attracts them for r > R E ​ E r>R_{\!EE} , trapping partons at all scales—UV and IR—in contrast to Wilson’s area law, which probes only long distances. Squaring the entropy density as required by unitarity yields σ ∝ y δ \sigma\!\propto\!y^{\delta} . World data demand δ = 2 \delta=2 for elastic p ​ p ​ ( p ​ p ¯ ) pp\,(p\bar{p}) scattering and heavy‑quark photoproduction, but a softer δ = 0.387 \delta=0.387 for ϕ \phi photoproduction, all two orders of magnitude below the Froissart–Martin bound. These results provide the first confinement criterion derived directly from non‑perturbative QCD, unifying the trace anomaly, entanglement entropy, and high‑energy scattering within a single, quantitative framework.

Conclusions.—

We have shown that the QCD trace anomaly, quantified with
percent-level lattice determinations of the scalar gravitational form
factors, fixes a transverse “information wall” of radius

R_{\!EE}
. Tracing over quark and gluon modes outside that surface
yields a finite,
parameter-free
mechanical entropy

S_{\!EE}=c_{h}\,y
with

c_{h}
fixed entirely by lattice inputs
—whose sign-changing radial derivative locks coloured
fields to the wall at
all
momentum scales. This entropic
mechanism replaces the traditional Wilson-loop area law with a
scale-independent confinement criterion that is simultaneously
consistent with asymptotic freedom in the ultraviolet and linear
confinement in the infrared. Squaring the entropy density, as required
by unitarity, leads to the single-scale form

\sigma(s)\propto y^{\delta}
. Hard processes
(
pp
,
J/\psi
,
\Upsilon
) saturate the unitarity limit

\delta=2
, reproducing the universal
\ln^{2}\!s
increase of total
cross sections, while the more diffuse
\phi
channel selects

\delta_{\phi}=0.387
. In every case the predicted cross sections
remain one–to–two orders of magnitude below the Froissart–Martin bound,
demonstrating that the information wall enforces—rather than
saturates—unitarity.

Outlook.—

The entropic framework developed here delivers a quantitative baseline against which future high-precision data can be
benchmarked. Upcoming measurements at the HL-LHC, Jefferson Lab (CLAS12, GlueX, and SoLID), and
the Electron–Ion Collider will confront its predictions across an
unprecedented kinematic range. Any systematic deviation—for example
from odderon exchange, Reggeon contributions, exotic multiquark
resonances—will stand out cleanly
against the fully quantified entropy background, offering a sharpened
path to discovering genuinely new QCD dynamics.

\displaystyle=2\pi\!\!\int_{r^{\prime 2}<R^{2}}\!\!\!\!\!\mathrm{d}r_{\parallel}^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}^{2}r^{\prime}_{\perp}\;\frac{R^{2}-r_{\parallel}^{\prime 2}-r_{\perp}^{\prime 2}}{2R}\,T(0,\bm{x}^{\!*})\,\delta(r_{\parallel}^{\!*}\!-r_{\parallel}^{\prime})\,\delta^{(2)}(\bm{r}_{\perp}^{\!*}\!-\bm{r}_{\perp}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=2\pi\,T(0,\bm{x})\,F(r)\bigl|_{r=r^{\!*}},

(S7)

where
F(r)=w(r)\,\Theta(R-r)
with

w(r)=\tfrac{1}{2R}(R^{2}-r^{2})
.

For any spherically symmetric kernel
F(r)
one has the total derivative

\nabla^{2}F(r)=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\Bigl(r^{2}\,\frac{\partial F}{\partial r}\Bigr),

so
-\tfrac{4\pi R^{2}}{2}\nabla^{2}

projects
the weight function onto the
surface term dictated by Gauss’ theorem, isolating the local contact
piece that sits on the entangling sphere
r=R
.
Carrying out the differentiation yields

-\tfrac{4\pi R^{2}}{2}\nabla^{2}F(r)=\underbrace{\frac{3\times 4\pi R^{2}}{2R}\,\Theta(R-r)}_{\text{bulk term}}+\underbrace{\delta(R-r)}_{\delta_{\Sigma}(r)}.

(S8)

The bulk contribution integrates to a power divergence removable by
counter‑terms and therefore does not affect the universal logarithmic
coefficient. Retaining only the surface
\delta
-function, we rewrite (
S7
) in terms of the total derivative of
F(r)
given in (
S8
) as

\langle T(0,\bm{x})\,K\rangle=2\pi\,T(0,\bm{x})\,\delta(R-r).

(S9)

The factor
\delta(R-r)\equiv\delta_{\Sigma}(r)
is a
surface delta function
: it collapses any three–dimensional integral onto the two–dimensional entangling sphere
r=R
. Concretely,

\int\!\mathrm{d}^{3}r\,\delta_{\Sigma}(r)\,f(r,\Omega)=R^{2}\!\int_{r=R}\!\mathrm{d}\Omega\,f(R,\Omega),

(S10)

so only the values of the integrand on
\Sigma_{\perp}
contribute.

Although Eq. (
S9
) was established within a conformal field theory, the derivation relies solely on locality and the Ward identity
\partial_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}=0
; it therefore applies to any relativistic quantum field theory, including QCD.
We thus adopt (
S9
) as the starting point for the explicit calculation of the entanglement–entropy flow that culminates in Eq. (
S11
).

5. Entanglement‑entropy flow.

A uniform Weyl shift
\delta g_{\mu\nu}=2\sigma g_{\mu\nu}
gives

Rosenhaus and Smolkin 2014

\delta S_{\!EE}=-\sigma\!\int\!\mathrm{d}^{3}x\,\langle T(0,\bm{x})\,K\rangle=-\sigma\,2\pi\!\int_{r=R}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}r_{\perp}\;T(0;r_{\parallel},\bm{r}_{\perp}),

(S11)

where we have used the delta function in (
S9
) to collapse the 3D integral to 2D. Then, choosing
\sigma=\delta R/R
gives the RG-flow equation

R\,\frac{\mathrm{d}S_{\!EE}}{\mathrm{d}R}=-\,2\pi\!\int_{r=R}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}r_{\perp}\;T\bigl(0;r_{\parallel},\bm{r}_{\perp}\bigr).

(S12)

Integrating from the UV cut-off
\epsilon
to
R
we define

J(R^{\prime})\;\equiv\;\int_{r=R^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}^{2}r_{\perp}\,T\bigl(0;r_{\parallel},\bm{r}_{\perp}\bigr),

so that

\displaystyle S_{\!EE}^{\text{univ}}(R)

\displaystyle=-2\pi\int_{\epsilon}^{R}\frac{\mathrm{d}R^{\prime}}{R^{\prime}}\,J(R^{\prime})

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{\epsilon}^{R}\frac{\mathrm{d}R^{\prime}}{R^{\prime}}\,\bigl[J(R)\!-\!\bigl(J(R^{\prime})+J(R)\bigr)\bigr]

\displaystyle=2\pi\,J(R)\!\int_{\epsilon}^{R}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}R^{\prime}}{R^{\prime}}-2\pi\int_{\epsilon}^{R}\frac{\mathrm{d}R^{\prime}}{R^{\prime}}\,\bigl(J(R^{\prime})+J(R)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\,2\pi\,J(R)\,\ln\!\frac{R}{\epsilon}-\underbrace{2\pi\int_{\epsilon}^{R}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}R^{\prime}}{R^{\prime}}\,\bigl(J(R^{\prime})+J(R)\bigr)}_{\text{analytic, removed by counter-terms}}.

(S13)

Discarding the analytic piece leaves the universal logarithm:

S_{\!EE}^{\text{univ}}(R)=\,2\pi\,\ln\!\frac{R}{\epsilon}\int_{r=R}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}r_{\perp}\;T\bigl(0;r_{\parallel},\bm{r}_{\perp}\bigr),

(S14)

which reproduces Eq. (
1
) of the main text.

A generalized Dumas irreducibility criterion
Rishu Garg 1, Jitender Singh 2,†

Abstract
. As an extension of the classical irreducibility result of Dumas, a factorization result for polynomials over any valued field with a Krull valuation of arbitrary rank is proved. Further, a lower degree factor bound on factors of a given polynomial over a valued field with a Krull valuation is proved. These factorization results not only unify several known irreducibility results for polynomials over the said domains but also provide us sharp bounds on degrees of irreducible factors of the underlying polynomials.

1.
Introduction

The problem of determining the irreducibility of polynomials over a given algebraic domain has been fundamental in algebra and number theory, since the nineteenth century. Symmetry of a geometrical or natural object is measured through abstract groups in mathematics. In view of this, symmetry of roots of an irreducible polynomial is captured by the
action of the Galois group associated with the underlying polynomial. Moreover, irreducible polynomials are used in constructing finite fields, which in turn play a key role in data encryption in computer science, which makes such studies important from technological point of view as well.

One of the earliest known classical irreducibility results is due to Schönemann
[
11
]
in 1846, who established a criterion for testing irreducibility of certain classes of polynomials having integer coefficients. A few years later, Eisenstein
[
4
]
in 1850 formulated his irreducibility criterion, which turns out to be a particular case of Schönemann’s irreducibility result. For an historical account of Eisenstien’s irreducibility criterion, the reader is referred to see the excellent exposition in
[
2
]
. In 1906, Dumas
[
3
]
proved a far reaching generalization of the irreducibility criteria of Schönemann and Eisenstein by using the geometric tool of Newton polygons. Over the years, these exquisite irreducibility criteria have been extended, generalized, and reformulated using Newton polygons and valuation theory. Using Newton polygon approach, Bonciocat
[
1
]
obtained a number of new irreducibility results for polynomials having integer coefficients which satisfy divisibility properties with regard to two or more distinct prime numbers in an analogy with Dumas irreducibility criterion. Using valuation theory, Khanduja and Saha
[
9
]
extended Dumas irreducibility criterion for polynomials over valued fields with a valuation of arbitrary rank. Another generalization of Dumas irreducibility criterion was obtained in
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
for polynomials over the Krull valued fields. In 2013, Weintraub
[
14
]
gave a mild generalization of Eisenstein’s irreducibility criterion, which also provided a correction to a false claim made by Eisenstein himself in
[
4
]
. Building upon this, in
[
7
]
, the authors proved Weintraub’s result under weaker assumptions and in a more general setting of valued fields, which also extends the irreducibility criterion of Dumas. To state their result, we need some definitions and notations described as follows. Let
\Gamma
be an ordered abelian group with a total order
<
and binary operation
+
. Let
\infty
be a symbol such that for all
x\in\Gamma
,
\infty=\infty+\infty=x+\infty=\infty+x
. For a subgroup
G
of
\Gamma
, a Krull valuation
v
on a field
\mathbb{K}
is a surjective map
v:\mathbb{K}\rightarrow G\cup\{\infty\}
satisfying the following axioms:

(i)

v(x)=\infty
if and only if
x=0
,
(ii)

v(xy)=v(x)+v(y)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{K}
,

(iii)

v(x+y)\geq\min\{v(x),v(y)\}
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{K}
.

The group
G=v(\mathbb{K}^{\times})
is called the value group of
v
. In view of the above definition we observe that
v(-x)=v(x)
and
v(x+y)=\min\{v(x),v(y)\}
if and only if
v(x)\neq v(y)
.
Using these notions, the following irreducibility result was proved in
[
7
]
.

Theorem
A

(Jhorar and Khanduja
[
7
]
)
.

Let
v
be a Krull valuation of arbitrary rank of a field
\mathbb{K}
with its value group
G_{v}
. Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}z+\cdots+a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathbb{K}[z]
be a polynomial of degree
n
and
k
be a smallest nonnegative integer for which the following conditions are satisfied.

(i)

v(a_{n})=0
,

(ii)

\frac{v(a_{k})}{n-k}<\frac{v(a_{i})}{n-i}
for all
i=0,\ldots,n-1
with
i\neq k
,

(iii)

v(a_{k})\notin dG_{v}
for any positive divisor
d>1
of
n-k
.

Then any factorization
f(z)=f_{1}(z)f_{2}(z)
of
f
in
\mathbb{K}[z]
has a factor of degree
\leq k
. In particular, if
k=0
, then the polynomial
f
is irreducible in
\mathbb{K}[z]
.

In view of the hypothesis (i) of Theorem
A
, if valuation of a coefficient other than the leading coefficient is zero, then on possibly modifying the hypothesis (ii) can one still have any information about the factorization of
f?
This question has been answered affirmatively in
[
13
, Theorem 1]
for polynomials over
\mathbb{Z}
and
v=v_{p}
, the
p
-adic valuation of
\mathbb{Q}
. Recently, in
[
12
]
, the author proved the following factorization result for polynomials over discrete valuation domains using Newton-polygon approach.

Theorem
B

(Singh
[
12
]
)
.

Let
(R,v)
be a discrete valuation domain. Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}z+\cdots+a_{n}z^{n}\in{R}[z]
be a primitive polynomial such that there exist indices
j
and
k
with
1\leq k+1\leq j\leq n
for which the following hold.

(i)

v(a_{j})=0
,

(ii)

\frac{v(a_{k})}{j-k}<\frac{v(a_{i})}{j-i}
for all
i=0,1,\ldots,j-1
with
i\neq k
,

(iii)

\gcd(v(a_{k}),~j-k)=1
.

Then any factorization
f(z)=f_{1}(z)f_{2}(z)
of
f
in
R[z]
has a factor of degree
\leq n-j+k
. In particular, if
j=n
and
k=0
, then the polynomial
f
is irreducible in
R[z]
.

The case
j=n
of Theorem
B
is the irreducibility result of Weintraub
[
14
]
for polynomials over discrete valuation domains. As a generalization of Theorems
A
-
B
, we may have the following result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
v
be a Krull valuation of arbitrary rank of a field
\mathbb{K}
, and let
G_{v}
be the value group of
v
. Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}z+\cdots+a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathbb{K}[z]
be a polynomial of degree
n
. Suppose there exist smallest indices
j
and
k
with
1\leq k+1\leq j\leq n
for which the following conditions are satisfied.

(i)

v(a_{j})=0
,

(ii)

\frac{v(a_{k})}{j-k}<\frac{v(a_{i})}{j-i}
for all indices
i
with
0\leq i\leq j-1
and
i\neq k
,

(iii)

If
j<n
, then
\frac{v(a_{k})}{j-k}>\frac{v(a_{i})}{j-i}
for all indices
i
with
j+1\leq i\leq n
,

(iv)

v(a_{k})\notin dG_{v}
for any positive divisor
d>1
of
j-k
.

Then any factorization
f(z)=f_{1}(z)f_{2}(z)
of
f
in
\mathbb{K}[z]
has a factor of degree
\leq n-j+k
. In particular, if
j=n
and
k=0
, then the polynomial
f
is irreducible in
\mathbb{K}[z]
.

We note that the case
j=n
of Theorem
1
is precisely Theorem
A
, and Theorem
B
corresponds to the case when the value group of
v
is infinite cyclic. As a direct consequence of Theorem
1
, we have the following irreducibility result.

Corollary 2
.

Let
v
be a valuation of a field
\mathbb{K}
with its value group
\mathbb{Z}
. Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}z+\cdots+a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathbb{K}[z]
be a polynomial of degree
n
. Suppose there exist smallest indices
j
and
k
with
1\leq k+1\leq j\leq n
for which the following conditions are satisfied.

(i)

v(a_{j})=0
,

(ii)

\frac{v(a_{k})}{j-k}<\frac{v(a_{i})}{j-i}
for all indices
i
with
0\leq i\leq j-1
and
i\neq k
,

(iii)

If
j<n
, then
\frac{v(a_{k})}{j-k}>\frac{v(a_{i})}{j-i}
for all indices
i
with
j+1\leq i\leq n
,

(iv)

\gcd(v(a_{k}),j-k)=1
.

Then any factorization
f(z)=f_{1}(z)f_{2}(z)
of
f
in
\mathbb{K}[z]
has a factor of degree
\leq n-j+k
. In particular, if
j=n
and
k=0
, then the polynomial
f
is irreducible in
\mathbb{K}[z]
.

The Corollary
2
in particular, yield results of the papers
[
7
, Corollary 1.3]
and
[
13
, Theorem 1]
for
j=n
, and for
f\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
, respectively by taking
v=v_{p}
, the
p
-adic valuation of
\mathbb{Q}
. The case
j=n
of the Corollary
2
is the irreducibility result of Weintraub
[
14
]
. Further, the case
j=n
and
k=0
of Corollary
2
corresponds to a generalization of the classical irreducibility criterion of Dumas
[
3
]
.

We may have the following factorization result, which also generalizes one of the main results of the paper
[
8
, Theorem 3.7]
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
v
be a Krull valuation of arbitrary rank of a field
\mathbb{K}
, and let
G_{v}
be the value group of
v
. Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}z+\cdots+a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathbb{K}[z]
be a polynomial of degree
n
. Suppose there exists a smallest index
j
with
1\leq j\leq n
for which the following conditions are satisfied.

(i)

v(a_{j})=0
,

(ii)

\frac{v(a_{0})}{j}\leq\frac{v(a_{i})}{j-i}
for all indices
i
with
0\leq i\leq j-1
,

(iii)

If
j<n
, then
\frac{v(a_{n})}{n-j}\leq\frac{v(a_{i})}{i-j}
for all indices
i
with
j+1\leq i<n
,

(iv)

Let
d_{1},d_{2}
be the smallest positive integers such that
d_{1}\frac{v(a_{0})}{j}\in G_{v}
, and if
j<n
, then also
d_{2}\frac{v(a_{n})}{n-j}\in G_{v}
. Define

\displaystyle\delta_{f}=\begin{cases}\min\{d_{1},d_{2}\},&\text{if}~j<n;\\
d_{1},&\text{if}~j=n.\end{cases}

Then each irreducible factor of
f
in
\mathbb{K}[z]
has degree at least
\delta_{f}
.

2.
Examples

We now illustrate explicit examples of polynomials whose factorization properties may be deduced using Theorems
1
&
3
.

In each of the examples given below, we will assume that
\Gamma=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
, the direct product of the additive group of real numbers equipped with the dictionary order
\ll
, that is, for
x,y,z,t\in\mathbb{R}
,
(x,y)\ll(z,t)
if either
x<z
, or if
x=z
, then
y<t
.

Example 1
.

Let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}(x)
, the rational function field over
\mathbb{Q}
, and let
p
be a prime number. For
f=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}x^{i}\in\mathbb{K}
, define
v:\mathbb{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
by

\displaystyle v(f):=\begin{cases}\infty,&\text{if}~f=0;\\
(v_{p}(f),v_{\infty}(\bar{f})),&\text{if}~f\neq 0,\end{cases}

where
v_{p}(f)=\min\{v_{p}(a_{i})~|~a_{i}\neq 0\}
and
v_{\infty}
is the degree valuation on the residue class field
\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)
. For
f,g\in\mathbb{K}[z]
with
g\neq 0
, define
v(f/g)=v(f)-v(g)
. Then
v
is a rank-2 valuation with value group
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
. Now the polynomial

\displaystyle P_{1}

\displaystyle=(1+4x^{4})x+4xz+(1+4x^{4})2xz^{2}+8xz^{3}+(1+4x^{4})2x^{2}z^{4}+(1+8x^{2}+4x^{4})z^{5}+4z^{6}

in
\mathbb{K}[z]
satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
1
for
n=6
,
j=5
,
k=0
, and
p=2
, since
v(a_{5})=(0,0)
,
v(a_{0})/(j-0)=(0,-1)/5=(0,-1/5)\ll v(a_{i})/(j-i)
for each
i
with
1\leq i\leq 4
,
v(a_{0})/(j-0)=(0,-1/5)\gg v(a_{6})/(j-6)=(-2,0)
, and
v(a_{0})=(0,-1)\notin dG_{v}
for any positive divisor
d>1
of
j-k=5
. So, any factorization of
P_{1}
in
\mathbb{K}[z]
has a factor of degree
\leq n-j+k=6-5+0=1
, where we find that
P_{1}
factors as

\displaystyle P_{1}=(1+4x^{4}+4z)(x+2xz^{2}+2x^{2}z^{4}+z^{5}).

This shows that
P_{1}
has the factor
1+4x^{4}+4z
of degree 1 in
\mathbb{K}[z]
. Note that Theorem
1
applies to
P_{1}
whereas Theorems
A
and
B
are both inconclusive.

Let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{F}(x,y)
be the rational function field in two variables over a field
\mathbb{F}
.
For
f=\sum_{t,s}c_{t,s}x^{t}y^{s}\in\mathbb{K}
, define
v:\mathbb{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
by

\displaystyle v(f):=\begin{cases}\infty,&\text{if}~f=0;\\
\min\{(t,s)~|~c_{t,s}\neq 0\},&\text{if}~f\neq 0.\end{cases}

Further, for
f,g\in\mathbb{K}[z]
with
g\neq 0
, define
v(f/g)=v(f)-v(g)
. We observe that
v
is a rank-2 valuation of
\mathbb{K}
with its value group
G_{v}=\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
as a subgroup of
\Gamma
.

Example 2
.

Consider the polynomial

\displaystyle P_{2}

\displaystyle=xy^{2}-(1-x^{2})yz-(1-y-xy)xz^{2}-(1-x-xy^{2})xz^{3}+(x-y+y^{2})xz^{4}

\displaystyle\quad-(1-x-x^{2})yz^{5}-(1-xy)z^{6}+xz^{7}\in\mathbb{K}[z],

which satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
1
for
n=7
,
j=6
, and
k=1
, since
v(a_{6})=v(1-xy)=(0,0)
,
v(a_{1})/(j-1)=v(-(1-x^{2})y)/5=(0,1/5)\ll v(a_{i})/(j-i)
for each
i
with
0\leq i\leq 5
,
i\neq 1
,
v(a_{1})/(j-1)=(0,1/5)\gg v(a_{7})/(j-7)=(-1,0)
, and
v(a_{1})=(0,1)\notin dG_{v}
for any positive divisor
d>1
of
j-k=5
. So, any factorization of
f
in
\mathbb{K}[z]
has a factor of degree
\leq n-j+k=7-6+1=2
, where we find that
P_{2}
factors as

\displaystyle P_{2}=(xy-z+xz^{2})(y+xz+xz^{2}+xyz^{3}+yz^{4}+z^{5}),

which shows that the polynomial
P_{2}
has the factor
xy-z+xz^{2}
of degree 2 in
\mathbb{K}[z]
. Note that here also, Theorems
A
and
B
are not applicable in deducing any information about the factorization of the polynomial
P_{2}
.

Example 3
.

Now consider the polynomial

\displaystyle P_{3}=y+xz+(1+xy^{2})z^{2}+x^{2}yz^{3}+xyz^{4},

which satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
3
for
n=4
,
j=2
, and
d_{1}=d_{2}=2
since
v(a_{2})=(0,0)
,
v(a_{0})/j=(0,1)/2=(0,1/2)\ll v(a_{1})/(j-1)=(1,0)/1=(1,0)
,
v(a_{4})/(n-j)=(1,1)/2=(1/2,1/2)\ll v(a_{3})/(3-j)=(2,1)/1=(2,1)
, and
1\times\frac{v(a_{0})}{j}=(0,1/2)\notin G_{v}
,
1\times\frac{v(a_{4})}{n-j}=(1/2,1/2)\notin G_{v}
, whereas
2\times\frac{v(a_{0})}{j}=(0,1)\in G_{v}
and
2\times\frac{v(a_{4})}{n-j}=(1,1)\in G_{v}
. Consequently, each irreducible factor of
f
in
\mathbb{K}[z]
has degree at least
\min\{2,2\}=2
, where we find that the polynomial
P_{3}
factors as a product of two irreducible factors each of degree
2
in
\mathbb{K}[z]
, that is,

\displaystyle P_{3}=y+xz+(1+xy^{2})z^{2}+x^{2}yz^{3}+xyz^{4}=(1+xyz^{2})(y+xz+z^{2}).

3.
Proofs

Let
\Gamma
be the totally ordered abelian group as defined in the preceding section so that
G_{v}
is a subgroup of
\Gamma
. To prove Theorem
1
, we essentially use the technique developed in
[
7
]
, and we proceed as follows.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
\gamma_{j}=v(a_{k})/(j-k)
, and let
G=\langle G_{v},\gamma_{j}\rangle
be the subgroup of
\Gamma
generated by
G_{v}
and
\gamma_{j}
. We claim that
i\gamma_{j}
does not belong to
G_{v}
for all indices
i
satisfying
1\leq i<j-k
. To see this, we suppose on the contrary that
i\gamma_{j}\in G_{v}
for some index
i
with
1\leq i<j-k
. We also have
(j-k)\gamma_{j}=v(a_{k})\in G_{v}
. If we define
d_{i}=\gcd(i,j-k)
, then by Euclidean algorithm, there exist integers
r
and
s
for which
ri+s(j-k)=d_{i}
, and so, we have

\displaystyle d_{i}\gamma_{j}=r(i\gamma_{j})+s(j-k)\gamma_{j}=r(i\gamma_{j})+sv(a_{k})\in G_{v}.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle v(a_{k})=(j-k)\gamma_{j}=\left(\frac{j-k}{d_{i}}\right)(d_{i}\gamma_{j})\in\left(\frac{j-k}{d_{i}}\right)G_{v}

for the divisor
\bigl(\frac{j-k}{d_{i}}\bigr)>1
of
j-k
, which contradicts the hypothesis (iv), and so, the claim holds.

Let
w_{j}:\mathbb{K}[z]\rightarrow G\cup\{\infty\}
be such that

\displaystyle w_{j}\bigl(\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}z^{i}\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{i}\{v(\alpha_{i})+i\gamma_{j}\},

for all
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}z^{i}\in\mathbb{K}[z]
. Then
w_{j}
becomes a valuation of
\mathbb{K}[z]
with its value group
G
as follows from
[
5
, Theorem 2.2]
. By the hypotheses (ii) and (iii), we have for all
i\neq k,~j
that

\displaystyle v(a_{i})+i\gamma_{j}>\frac{j-i}{j-k}v(a_{k})+i\gamma_{j}=j\gamma_{j}=v(a_{k})+k\gamma_{j},

which shows that
v(a_{k})+k\gamma_{j}=\min_{i}\{v(a_{i})+i\gamma_{j}\}=w_{j}(f)
.

Now suppose on the contrary that
f(z)=f_{1}(z)f_{2}(z)
for some polynomials
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
in
\mathbb{K}[z]
such that
\min\{\deg f_{1},\deg f_{2}\}>n-j+k
. We may write
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
as

\displaystyle f_{1}=b_{0}+b_{1}z+\cdots+b_{m_{1}}z^{m_{1}},~f_{2}=c_{0}+c_{1}z+\cdots+c_{m_{2}}z^{m_{2}},~b_{m_{1}}c_{m_{2}}\neq 0,~m_{1}+m_{2}=n.

Since
v(a_{j})=0
, there exist smallest indices
r\leq m_{1}
and
s\leq m_{2}
with
r+s=j
for which
v(b_{r}c_{s})=0
.
Let
k_{i}
denote the smallest index for which
w_{j}(f_{i})
occurs for each
i=1,2
. We then have

\displaystyle v(b_{i})+i\gamma_{j}\geq w_{j}(f_{1}),~v(c_{\ell})+\ell\gamma_{j}\geq w_{j}(f_{2}),

for
0\leq i\leq m_{1}
,
0\leq\ell\leq m_{2}
with strict inequality whenever
i<k_{1}
or
\ell<k_{2}
. In view of this, the smallest index for which
w_{j}(f_{1})+w_{j}(f_{2})
occurs is
k_{1}+k_{2}
. Since
w_{j}(f)=w_{j}(f_{1})+w_{j}(f_{2})
, and the smallest index at which
w_{j}(f)
occurs is
k
, we have
k_{1}+k_{2}=k
. Since
\min\{m_{1},m_{2}\}>n-j+k
, we deduce that
j-m_{i}>k
for each
i=1,2
, and so, we find that

k_{i}\leq k<r+s-m_{i},~i=1,2
. These inequalities along with the inequalities
r-m_{1}\leq 0
and
s-m_{2}\leq 0
yield the following:

(1)

\displaystyle r-k_{1}>0,~s-k_{2}>0.

Since
k_{1}+k_{2}=k
, this in view of (
1
) gives us the following.

\displaystyle j-k=(r+s)-(k_{1}+k_{2})=(r-k_{1})+(s-k_{2}),

which shows that

\displaystyle 0<r-k_{1}<j-k~\text{and}~0<s-k_{2}<j-k.

Since we have

\displaystyle w_{j}(f_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v(b_{k_{1}})+k_{1}\gamma_{j}\leq v(b_{r})+r\gamma_{j},

\displaystyle w_{j}(f_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v(c_{k_{2}})+k_{2}\gamma_{j}\leq v(c_{s})+s\gamma_{j},

it follows that
w_{j}(f_{1})+w_{j}(f_{2})\leq v(b_{r}c_{s})+(r+s)\gamma_{j}=j\gamma_{j}
. This in view of the fact that
j\gamma_{j}=w_{j}(f)
tells us that

\displaystyle j\gamma_{j}=w_{j}(f)=w_{j}(f_{1}f_{2})=w_{j}(f_{1})+w_{j}(f_{2})\leq v(b_{r}c_{s})+(r+s)\gamma_{j}=j\gamma_{j},

which forces the equalities
w_{j}(f_{1})=v(b_{r})+r\gamma_{j}
and
w_{j}(f_{2})=v(c_{s})+s\gamma_{j}
. We then have

\displaystyle w_{j}(f_{1})=v(b_{r})+r\gamma_{j}=v(b_{k_{1}})+k_{1}\gamma_{j},

and so,
(r-k_{1})\gamma_{j}=(v(b_{k_{1}})-v(b_{r}))\in G_{v}
, which contradicts the claim made in the opening lines of the proof.
∎

Proof of Corollary
2
.

Observe that the hypothesis
(iv)
of the corollary implies that for any divisor
d>1
of
j-k
,
v(a_{k})\not\in dG_{v}=d\mathbb{Z}
, since otherwise,
d
divides
v(a_{k})
, and so,
d
divides
\gcd(v(a_{k}),j-k)=1
, which is a contradiction. So, the polynomial
f
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
1
for
G_{v}=\mathbb{Z}
, and the corollary follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3
.

The case
j=n
is precisely Theorem 1.1 of
[
6
]
. In view of this, we will prove the Theorem for the case when
j<n
. We proceed as follows. Let

\displaystyle\gamma=\min\Bigl\{\frac{v(a_{0})}{j},~\frac{v(a_{n})}{n-j}\Bigr\}.

Let
G=\langle G_{v},\gamma\rangle
be the subgroup of
\Gamma
generated by

and
\gamma

w:\mathbb{K}[z]\rightarrow G\cup\{\infty\}
be such that

\displaystyle w\bigl(\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}z^{i}\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{i}\{v(\alpha_{i})+i\gamma\},

for all
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}z^{i}\in\mathbb{K}[z]

\mathbb{K}[z]

Case I

\gamma={v(a_{0})}/{j}
. In this case, we have

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{v(a_{0})}{j}\leq\frac{v(a_{n})}{n-j}\leq\frac{v(a_{i})}{i-j},~\textrm{for}~j+1\leq i<n.

\displaystyle v(a_{i})+i\gamma

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{j-i}{j}v(a_{0})+i\gamma=j\gamma=v(a_{0}),~\textrm{for}~0\leq i\leq j-1,

\displaystyle v(a_{i})+i\gamma

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{i-j}{j}v(a_{0})+i\gamma=(2i-j)\gamma>j\gamma_{j}=v(a_{0}),~\textrm{for}~j+1\leq i<n,

which shows that

(3)

\displaystyle v(a_{0})=\min_{i}\{v(a_{i})+i\gamma\}=w(f).

Now suppose that

\displaystyle j\gamma=w(f)=w(f_{1}f_{2}\cdots f_{s})=w(f_{1})+w(f_{2})+\cdots+w(f_{s})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{s}\{v(b_{ir_{i}})+r_{i}\gamma\}=j\gamma,

Hörmander oscillatory integral operators: a revisit
Chuanwei Gao, Zhong Gao, Changxing Miao

Abstract
. In this paper, we present new proofs for both the sharp L p L^{p} estimate and the decoupling theorem for the Hörmander oscillatory integral operator. The sharp L p L^{p} estimate was previously obtained by Stein [ 18 ] and Bourgain-Guth [ 5 ] via the T ​ T ∗ TT^{\ast} and multilinear methods, respectively. We provide a unified proof based on the bilinear method for both odd and even dimensions. The strategy is inspired by Barron’s work [ 1 ] on the restriction problem. The decoupling theorem for the Hörmander oscillatory integral operator can be obtained by the approach in [ 2 ] , where the key observation can be roughly formulated as follows: in a physical space of sufficiently small scale, the variable setting can be essentially viewed as translation-invariant. In contrast, we reprove the decoupling theorem for the Hörmander oscillatory integral operator through the Pramanik-Seeger approximation approach [ 17 ] . Both proofs rely on a scale-dependent induction argument, which can be used to deal with perturbation terms in the phase function.

1.
Introduction

Let
n\geq 2
,
a\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
be non-negative and supported in
B^{n}_{1}(0)\times B^{n-1}_{1}(0)
and
\phi\colon B^{n}_{1}(0)\times B^{n-1}_{1}(0)\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function. For any
\lambda\geq 1
, define

T^{\lambda}f(x):=\int_{B^{n-1}_{1}(0)}e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}(x,\xi)}a^{\lambda}(x,\xi)f(\xi)\,d\xi,

(1.1)

where
f\colon B^{n-1}_{1}(0)\to\mathbb{C}
and

a^{\lambda}(x,\xi):=a(x/\lambda,\xi),\ \phi^{\lambda}(x,\xi):=\lambda\phi(x/\lambda,\xi).

(1.2)

We say that the operator
T^{\lambda}
is a Hörmander oscillaroty integral operator if
\phi
satisfies the following Carleson-Sjölin conditions:

(H1)

\mathrm{rank}\,\partial_{x^{\prime}\xi}^{2}\phi(x,\xi)=n-1
for all
(x,\xi)\in B^{n}_{1}(0)\times B^{n-1}_{1}(0)
and
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})
;

(H2)

For each
x_{0}\in{\rm supp\,}_{x}a
, the hypersurface

\{\partial_{x}\phi(x_{0},\xi):\xi\in{\rm supp}\;a(x_{0},\cdot)\}

has non-vanishing Gaussian curvature.

A typical example for the Hörmander oscillatory integral operators is the following extension operator
E
defined by

Ef(x):=\int_{B_{1}^{n-1}(0)}e^{2\pi i(x^{\prime}\cdot\xi+x_{n}\psi(\xi))}f(\xi)d\xi,

(1.3)

with

{\rm rank}\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial\xi_{i}\partial\xi_{j}}\Big)_{(n-1)\times(n-1)}=n-1,

(1.4)

it is straightforward to verify that the phase function
\phi(x,\xi):=x^{\prime}\cdot\xi+x_{n}\psi(\xi)
satisfies the conditions (H1) and (H2).
For the Hörmander oscillatory integral operators, we revisit the following two important problems: the sharp
L^{p}
estimate and the decoupling inequality.

Sharp
L^{p}
estimate.

Hörmander
[
13
]
conjectured that if
\phi
satisfies conditions (H1),(H2), then

\|T^{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))}

(1.5)

for
p>\frac{2n}{n-1}
, and he proved this conjecture for
n=2
. For the higher dimensional cases, Stein
[
18
]
proved (
1.5
) for
p\geq\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}
. Later, Bourgain
[
3
]
disproved Hörmander’s conjecture, and showed that Stein’s result is sharp in the odd dimensions. For the even dimensions, up to an endpoint, Bourgain and Guth
[
5
]
established the sharp result. In summary, we may state the results as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
5
]
,
[
18
]
)
.

Let

and
T^{\lambda}
be a Hörmander oscillatory integral operator as in
(
1.1
)
. For all
\varepsilon>0,\lambda\geq 1

\|T^{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\phi,a}\lambda^{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B^{n-1}_{1}(0))}

(1.6)

holds whenever

p\geq\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tfrac{2(n+1)}{n-1}\quad\text{\rm for $n$ odd},\\
&\tfrac{2(n+2)}{n}\quad\text{\rm for $n$ even}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.7)

Stein’s proof is based on the
TT^{\ast}
method and gives the range
p\geq\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}
in all dimensions. However, this result is not sharp in even dimensions. Bourgain-Guth
[
5
]
resolved the even-dimensional cases up to the endpoints using the Broad-Narrow approach. Bourgain-Guth’s method can also be applied to the odd-dimensional cases, see Guth-Hickman-Iliopoulou
[
12
]
for details. We give another proof based on the bilinear approach. We take the extension operator as a model case to illustrate how one can derive the linear estimate for the oscillatory integral operator from its bilinear counterpart. To this end, we first recall a sharp bilinear restriction theorem of Lee
[
15
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
15
]
)
.

Suppose that
\xi\in B_{1}^{n-1}(0)
and the
Hessian matrix
of
\phi
is nondegenerate, i.e.

{\rm det}\mathcal{H}\phi(\xi)\neq 0.

Additionally, let
V_{1},V_{2}
be two sufficiently small balls contained in

then

\||Ef_{1}Ef_{2}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq R^{\varepsilon}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}},

Decoupling theorem.
Assume
\{{\theta}\}
is a collection of finitely overlapping balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
of radius
R^{-1/2}
which form a cover of
B_{1}^{n-1}(0)
.
Define

f_{\theta}:=f\kappa_{\theta};\;\sum_{\theta}\kappa_{\theta}=1,\;\forall\xi\in B_{1}^{n-1}(0)

where
\{\kappa_{\theta}\}
is a family of smooth functions which are subjecting to
\{\theta\}
.
Correspondingly, we decompose
T^{\lambda}f
into

T^{\lambda}f:=\sum_{\theta}T^{\lambda}f_{\theta}.

We have the following decoupling theorem for the Hörmander oscillatory operator.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
T^{\lambda}
be a Hörmander oscillatory integral operator as in
(
1.1
)
. If
p\geq\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}
, then

\big\|\sum_{\theta}T^{\lambda}f_{\theta}\big\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}^{n}(x_{0}))}\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n}{p}+\varepsilon}\big(\sum_{\theta}\|T^{\lambda}f_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(w_{B_{R}^{n}(x_{0})})}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}+{\rm RapDec}(R)\|f\|_{2},

(1.10)

where
w_{B_{R}^{n}(x_{0})}
is a non-negative weight function adapted to the ball
B_{R}^{n}(x_{0})
such that

w_{B^{n}_{R}(x_{0})}(x)\lesssim(1+R^{-1}|x-x_{0}|)^{-L},

for some large constant
L\in\mathbb{N}
.

The decoupling theorem for the extension operator was established by Bourgain-Demeter
[
4
]
. When the phase function satisfies the cinematic curvature condition, the associated variable version of the decoupling theorem was established by Beltran-Hickman-Sogge
[
2
]
(see also
[
14
]
). Their method can also be applied to the Hörmander oscillatory integral operator. A key observation in
[
2
]
can be roughly formulated as follows: At the small scale of physical space, the variable setting is essentially translation invariant. Hence, the decoupling theorem for the flat version can be brought into play directly at the level of a small scale of physical space.

We present an alternative proof of the theorem
1.3
based on the Pramanik-Seeger’s approach
[
17
]
. To be more precise, we first conduct a localization procedure in frequency space. In this setting, the key is to effectively control the error term so that we can directly use the techniques in the translation-invariant setting.

\|T^{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}^{n}(0))}\lesssim_{\phi,\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}\sum_{B_{\tilde{R}}^{n}\subset\Box_{R}}\|T^{\tilde{\lambda}}_{\tilde{K}}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(B_{\tilde{R}}^{n})}^{p},

where

T^{\tilde{\lambda}}_{\tilde{K}}\tilde{f}(x)=\int_{B_{1}^{n-1}(0)}e^{2\pi i\phi_{\tilde{K}}^{\tilde{\lambda}}(x,\xi)}\mathfrak{a}^{\tilde{\lambda}}(x,\xi)\tilde{f}(\xi)d\xi

and
\tilde{f}
is defined by
(
2.5
)
.
Note that
K=K_{0}R^{\delta}
, there exists a
\bar{B}_{\tilde{R}}^{n}\subset\Box_{R}
such that

\sum_{B_{\tilde{R}}^{n}\subset\Box_{R}}\|T^{\tilde{\lambda}}_{\tilde{K}}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(B_{\tilde{R}}^{n})}^{p}\lesssim K^{n-1}\|T^{\tilde{\lambda}}_{\tilde{K}}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(\bar{B}_{\tilde{R}}^{n})}^{p}.

From
(
3.2
)
, it follows that

\|T^{\tilde{\lambda}}_{\tilde{K}}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(\bar{B}_{\tilde{R}}^{n})}^{p}\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(B^{n-1}_{1}(0))}.

By choosing
\delta=\varepsilon^{2}\ll 1
, we will obtain the desired result
(
3.1
)
.

Let
1\leq R\leq\lambda
and
Q_{p}(\lambda,R)
be the optimal constant such that

\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}^{n})}\leq Q_{p}(\lambda,R)\|f\|_{L^{p}(B^{n}_{1}(0))}

(3.3)

holds for all asymptotically flat phase
\phi_{K}
in the Definition
2.1
and for all
\mathfrak{a}
satisfying (
2.4
), and uniformly for all
f\in L^{p}(B^{n-1}_{1}(0))
.
Then (
3.2
) is reduced to showing:

Q_{p}(\lambda,R)\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}.

(3.4)

We proceed to prove (
3.4
) via an induction on scale argument. For this purpose, we first set up some basic preparatory tools.

3.1.
Parabolic rescaling and flat decoupling

In this section we establish the parabolic rescaling lemma which connects the estimates at different scales and plays a critical role in the induction argument. To that end, we first prove an auxiliary proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a maximal
R^{-1}
-separated discrete subset of
\Omega\subset B_{1}^{n-1}(0)
, then

\Big\|\sum_{\xi_{\theta}\in\mathcal{D}}e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}_{K}(\cdot,\xi_{\theta})}F(\xi_{\theta})\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}^{n}(0))}\lesssim Q_{p}(\lambda,R)R^{\frac{n-1}{p^{\prime}}}\|F\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathcal{D})}

(3.5)

for all
F:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, where

\|F\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathcal{D})}:=\Big(\sum_{\xi_{\theta}\in\mathcal{D}}|F(\xi_{\theta})|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

for
1\leq p<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\eta
be a bump smooth function on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, which is supported on
B_{2}^{n-1}(0)
and equals to
1
on
B_{1}^{n-1}(0)
. For each
\xi_{\theta}\in\mathcal{D}
, we set
\eta_{\theta}(\xi):=\eta(10R(\xi-\xi_{\theta}))
. In exactly the same way as in the proof of Lemma 11.8 of
[
12
]
, we have

\Big|\sum_{\xi_{\theta}\in\mathcal{D}}e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}_{K}(\cdot,\xi_{\theta})}F(\xi_{\theta})\Big|\lesssim R^{n-1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(1+|k|)^{-(n+1)}|T^{\lambda}_{K}f_{k}(x)|,

(3.6)

where

f_{k}(\xi):=\sum_{\xi_{\theta}\in\mathcal{D}}F(\xi_{\theta})c_{k,\theta}(\xi)\eta_{\theta}(\xi)

with
\|c_{k,\theta}(\xi)\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. By the definition of
Q_{p}(\lambda,R)
and (
3.6
), we get

\Big\|\sum_{\xi_{\theta}\in\mathcal{D}}e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}_{K}(\cdot,\xi_{\theta})}F(\xi_{\theta})\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}^{n}(0))}\lesssim Q_{p}(\lambda,R)R^{n-1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(1+|k|)^{-(n+1)}\|f_{k}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))}.

The supports of
\{\eta_{\theta}\}
are pairwise disjoint, for any
q\geq 1
, we have

\|f_{k}\|_{L^{q}(B^{n-1}_{2}(0))}\lesssim R^{-\frac{n-1}{q}}\|F\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathcal{D})}.

Thus we get

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{\xi_{\theta}\in\mathcal{D}}e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}_{K}(\cdot,\xi_{\theta})}F(\xi_{\theta})\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}^{n}(0))}

\displaystyle\lesssim Q_{p}(\lambda,R)R^{n-1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(1+|k|)^{-(n+1)}R^{-\frac{n-1}{p}}\|F\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathcal{D})}

\displaystyle\lesssim Q_{p}(\lambda,R)R^{\frac{n-1}{p^{\prime}}}\|F\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathcal{D})}.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

(Parabolic rescaling)
Let
1\leqslant R\leqslant\lambda
, and
f
be supported in a ball of radius
K^{-1}
, where
1\leqslant K\leqslant R
. Then for all
p\geqslant 2
and
\delta>0
, we have

\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}^{n}(0))}\lesssim_{\delta}Q_{p}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{K^{2}},\frac{R}{K^{2}}\Big)R^{\delta}K^{\frac{2n}{p}-(n-1)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))}.

(3.7)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
{\rm supp\,}f\subset B_{K^{-1}}^{n-1}(\bar{\xi})
.
In the same argument as in Section
2
, we obtain

\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}^{n}(0))}\lesssim_{\delta}K^{\frac{n+1}{p}}\|\widetilde{T}^{\tilde{\lambda}}_{\tilde{K}}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(\Box_{R})},

where
\Box_{R}
and
\tilde{f}
are defined in the Lemma
2.3
and

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}=K^{-2}\lambda,\quad\tilde{K}=K^{1-2\varepsilon^{2}}.

(3.8)

Note that for
q\geq 1
,

\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{q}(B^{n-1}_{1}(0))}\leq K^{-(n-1)+(n-1)/q}\|f\|_{L^{q}(B^{n-1}_{1}(0))},

it suffices to show that

\|\widetilde{T}^{\tilde{\lambda}}_{\tilde{K}}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(\Box_{R})}\lesssim_{\delta}Q_{p}\Big(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{R}\Big)R^{\delta}\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(B^{n-1}_{1}(0))}.

To simplify notations, we just need to show

\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f\|_{L^{p}(\Box(R,R^{\prime}))}\lesssim_{\delta}Q_{p}(\lambda,R)R^{\delta}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B^{n-1}_{1}(0))}

(3.9)

for all
1\ll R\leq R^{\prime}\leq\lambda
and
\delta>0
, where

\Box(R,R^{\prime}):=\left\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left(\frac{|x^{\prime}|}{R^{\prime}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{|x_{n}|}{R}\right)^{2}\leq 1\right\}.

Choosing a collection of essentially disjoint
R^{-1}
-balls
\theta
which covers
B^{n-1}_{1}(0)
, we denote the center of
\theta
by
\xi_{\theta}
and decompose
f
into
f=\sum_{\theta}f_{\theta}
. Set

T^{\lambda}_{K,\theta}f(x):=e^{-2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}_{K}(x,\xi_{\theta})}T^{\lambda}_{K}f(x),

and we rewrite

T^{\lambda}_{K}f(x)=\sum_{\theta}e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}_{K}(x,\xi_{\theta})}T^{\lambda}_{K,\theta}f_{\theta}(x).

For sufficiently small
\delta>0
, we may also write

T^{\lambda}_{K,\theta}f_{\theta}(x)=T^{\lambda}_{K,\theta}f_{\theta}*\eta_{R^{1-\delta}}(x)+{\rm RapDec}(R)\|f\|_{L^{2}(B^{n-1})},

(3.10)

where
\eta
is a Schwartz function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and has Fourier support on
B^{n}_{2}(0)
, and
\hat{\eta}=1
on
B^{n}_{1}(0)
. Then

|\eta|
admits a smooth rapidly decreasing majorant
\zeta:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
which satisfies

\zeta_{R^{1-\delta}}(x)\lesssim R^{\delta}\zeta_{R^{1-\delta}}(y)\quad\text{if}\quad|x-y|\lesssim R.

(3.11)

Cover
\Box(R,R^{\prime})
by a finitely-overlapping
R
-balls
\{B_{R}^{n}\}
. For any
B_{R}^{n}(\bar{x})
in this cover and for
z\in B_{R}^{n}(0)
, we have

|T^{\lambda}_{K}f(\bar{x}+z)|\lesssim R^{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\sum_{\theta}e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}_{K}(\bar{x}+z,\xi_{\theta})}T^{\lambda}_{K,\theta}f_{\theta}(y)\Big|\zeta_{R^{1-\delta}}(\bar{x}-y)dy.

Taking the
L^{p}
-norm in
z
and using Proposition
3.1
for the phase
\phi^{\lambda}_{K}(\bar{x}+\cdot,\xi_{\theta})
, we have

\displaystyle\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f(\bar{x}+\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}^{n}(0))}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\|\sum_{\theta}e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}_{K}(\bar{x}+z,\xi_{\theta})}T^{\lambda}_{K,\theta}f_{\theta}(y)\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}^{n}(0))}\zeta_{R^{1-\delta}}(\bar{x}-y)dy

\displaystyle\lesssim Q_{p}(\lambda,R)R^{\frac{n-1}{p^{\prime}}}R^{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|T^{\lambda}_{K,\theta}f_{\theta}(y)\|_{\ell^{p}(\theta)}\zeta_{R^{1-\delta}}(\bar{x}-y)dy,

where
\|a_{\theta}\|_{\ell^{p}(\theta)}
is denoted by
\big(\sum\limits_{\theta}|a_{\theta}|^{p}\big)^{1/p}
.

By the property (
3.11
), for
z\in B_{R}^{n}(0)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|T^{\lambda}_{K,\theta}f_{\theta}(y)\|_{\ell^{p}(\theta)}\zeta_{R^{1-\delta}}(\bar{x}-y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|T^{\lambda}_{K,\theta}f_{\theta}(\bar{x}+z-y)\|_{\ell^{p}(\theta)}\zeta_{R^{1-\delta}}(y-z)dy

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle R^{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|T^{\lambda}_{K,\theta}f_{\theta}(\bar{x}+z-y)\|_{\ell^{p}(\theta)}\zeta_{R^{1-\delta}}(y)dy

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle R^{O(\delta)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|T^{\lambda}_{K,\theta}f_{\theta}(\bar{x}+z-y)\|_{\ell^{p}(\theta)}^{p}\zeta_{R^{1-\delta}}(y)dy\right)^{1/p}.

Then we deduces that for all
z\in B_{R}^{n}(0)

\displaystyle\quad\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f(\bar{x}+\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}^{n}(0))}

\displaystyle\lesssim Q_{p}(\lambda,R)R^{\frac{n-1}{p^{\prime}}}R^{O(\delta)}

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|T^{\lambda}_{K,\theta}f_{\theta}(\bar{x}+z-y)\|_{\ell^{p}(\theta)}^{p}\zeta_{R^{1-\delta}}(y)dy\right)^{1/p}.

Raising both sides of this estimate to the
p
th power, averaging in
z
and summing over all balls
B_{R}^{n}
in the covering, we conclude
that
\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f\|_{L^{p}(\Box(R,R^{\prime}))}
is dominated by

Q_{p}(\lambda,R)R^{\frac{n-1}{p^{\prime}}-\frac{n}{p}}R^{O(\delta)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{\theta}\|T^{\lambda}_{K,\theta}f_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(\Box(R,R^{\prime})-y)}^{p}\zeta_{R^{1-\delta}}(y)dy\right)^{1/p}.

Using the trivial estimate

\|T^{\lambda}_{K,\theta}f_{\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Box(R,R^{\prime})-y)}\lesssim\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{1}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))}\lesssim R^{-(n-1)}\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))}

(3.12)

and

\|T^{\lambda}_{K,\theta}f_{\theta}\|_{L^{2}(\Box(R,R^{\prime})-y)}\lesssim R^{1/2}\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))},

(3.13)

we have

\|T_{K,\theta}^{\lambda}f_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(\Box(R,R^{\prime})-y)}\lesssim R^{-(2n-1)(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})+\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))}.

Hence
\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f\|_{L^{p}(\Box(R,R^{\prime}))}
is dominated by
Q_{p}(\lambda,R)R^{O(\delta)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))}.

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that
{\rm supp\,}f\subset B^{n-1}_{1}(0)
.
Then the Fourier transform of
T_{K}^{\lambda}f
is essentially supported on the
K^{-2}
-neighborhood of the surface
S:=\{(\xi,\langle M\xi,\xi\rangle):\xi\in\mathrm{supp\,}f\}
in the sense that

\displaystyle|\widehat{T^{\lambda}_{K}f}(\omega)|\leq{\rm RapDec}(\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))},\quad\text{for all \,}\omega\notin\mathcal{N}_{CK^{-2}}S.

(3.14)

Proof.

Define

G_{\lambda}(\xi,\omega):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi i(\phi_{K}^{\lambda}(x,\xi)-x\cdot\omega)}\mathfrak{a}^{\lambda}(x,\xi)dx.

Then we have

\displaystyle\widehat{T^{\lambda}_{K}f}(\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\omega}T^{\lambda}_{K}f(x)dx=\int_{B_{1}^{n-1}(0)}f(\xi)G_{\lambda}(\xi,\omega)d\xi.

(3.15)

We rewrite as

G_{\lambda}(\xi,\omega)=\lambda^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi i\lambda(\phi_{K}(y,\xi)-y\cdot\omega)}\mathfrak{a}(y,\xi)dy.

From integration by parts and the assumption (
2.4
) of
\mathfrak{a}
, it follows that

\displaystyle|G_{\lambda}(\xi,\omega)|\leq{\rm RapDec}(\lambda),

(3.16)

provided that

\displaystyle|\omega-\nabla_{y}\phi_{K}(y,\xi)|\geq CK^{-2}.

(3.17)

Since
\phi_{K}
is asymptotically flat,
(
3.17
)
holds true obviously if
\omega\notin\mathcal{N}_{CK^{-2}}S
.
Combining
(
3.15
)
and
(
3.16
)
, we have

|\widehat{T^{\lambda}_{K}f}(\omega)|\leq{\rm RapDec}(\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))},\quad\text{for all \,}\omega\notin\mathcal{N}_{CK^{-2}}S.

∎

To prove
(
3.4
)
, we also need a flat decoupling estimate for

.

Lemma 3.4
.

(Flat decoupling)
Let
\{\tau\}
be a collection of finitely-overlapping

-balls covering

with

,
then we can decompose

as

For

, one has

\displaystyle\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim(\#\{\tau\})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\Big(\sum_{\tau}\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{R}})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+{\rm RapDec}(\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))}.

(3.18)

Proof.

)

where

∎

3.2.
Bilinear restriction estimate

Assume that
\phi
satisfies the Carleson-Sjölin conditions.
Let

be two balls contained in

and

.
By the assumption (H1), the map

is a diffeomorphism. Define

(3.20)

Theorem 3.5
.

[

. Assume that

for

, where

for

-dimensional affine subspace

3.3.
Broad-Narrow analysis.

Let
\delta=\varepsilon^{2}\ll 1
, and set

\displaystyle K_{2}=K_{1}^{2\delta},\,K_{1}=K^{\alpha},\,K=K_{0}R^{\delta},

(3.24)

where

.
Let
\mathfrak{T}
be a collection of finitely-overlapping

-balls
\tau
covering

, and we can fix a collection
\mathscr{Q}
of finitely-overlapping
K^{2}
-cubes that cover

. For each

, we define its

\mathcal{S}_{p}(Q):=\Big\{\tau\in\mathfrak{T}:\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}(Q)}\geq\frac{1}{100\#\mathfrak{T}}\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f\|_{L^{p}(Q)}\Big\}.

We say that a

-cube

is

and write

if and only if there exists an
m
-dimensional linear subspace

such that

\displaystyle\angle(G^{\lambda}(x,\tau),V)\leq CK^{-1}_{1}

(3.25)

for all
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{p}(Q)
, here for given

denotes the set of the unit normal vectors of the hypersurface

.
If a

-cube

is not narrow, then we call it

and write

. Thus

\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}^{n})}^{p}\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{B}}\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f\|_{L^{p}(Q)}^{p}+\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{N}}\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f\|_{L^{p}(Q)}^{p}.

We call it broad case if

\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{p}\leq 2\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{B}}\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f\|_{L^{p}(Q)}^{p},

otherwise narrow case if

\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{p}\leq 2\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{N}}\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f\|_{L^{p}(Q)}^{p}.

Now we are going to prove (
3.4
). Obviously (
3.4
) holds true for
1\leq\lambda\leq 1000
, so let us suppose (
3.4
) holds true for
1\leq r\leq\lambda^{\prime}\leq\lambda/2
.
In the following part, we will deal with the broad and narrow cases respectively. Then we balance the two cases and close the whole induction for (
3.4
).

3.4.
Narrow estimate

Suppose
Q\in\mathscr{Q}
is a narrow cube, by Proposition
3.7
,
there exists an
m
-dimensional affine subspace

such that

\bigcup_{\tau\in S_{p}(Q)}\tau\subset N_{CK_{1}^{-1}}V.

We decompose
B^{n-1}_{1}(0)
into
K_{1}^{-1}
-balls
\{\pi\}
. Let
\Pi_{V}
be a minimal collection of
\{\pi\}
covering
B^{n-1}_{1}(0)\cap N_{CK_{1}^{-1}}V
and
\mathfrak{I}
be a collection of finitely-overlapping
K_{1}^{-1}
-balls
\{\pi\}
covering
\mathrm{supp\,}f
. Note that
\Pi_{V}
contains
CK_{1}^{m}
many balls
\pi
. Using Hölder’s inequality and Lemma

\displaystyle\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f\|_{L^{p}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq CK_{1}^{m(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\Big(\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{V}}\|T_{K}^{\lambda}f_{\pi}\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{Q})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq CK_{1}^{2m(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\Big(\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{V}}\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f_{\pi}\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{Q})}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq CK_{1}^{2m(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\Big(\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{I}}\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f_{\pi}\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{Q})}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

By Lemma
3.2
and our induction assumption, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{N}}\|T_{K}^{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(Q)}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq CK_{1}^{2m(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\Big(\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{I}}\|T_{K}^{\lambda}f_{\pi}\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{R}})}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{C}C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}K_{1}^{-\varepsilon}K_{1}^{2m(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})-(n-1)+\frac{2n}{p}}\Big(\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{I}}\|f_{\pi}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{C}C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}K_{1}^{-\varepsilon}K_{1}^{2m(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})-(n-1)+\frac{2n}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))},

where
\bar{C}
is a large constant.

If
p\geq\frac{2(n-m)}{n-m-1}

\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{N}}\|T_{K}^{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(Q)}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))}.

(3.26)

Lemma 3.10
.

Suppose that

with support

\displaystyle|T_{K}^{\lambda}f(x)|=|(e^{-2\pi i\phi_{K}^{\lambda}(\cdot,\bar{\xi})}T_{K}^{\lambda}f)\ast\psi_{K/C}(x)|+{\rm RapDec}(\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}

(3.30)

where
\psi_{K/C}(x)=C^{n}K^{-n}\psi(CK^{-1}x)
with
{\rm supp\,}\hat{\psi}\subset B_{2}^{n}(0)

\hat{\psi}=1

Proof.

We observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\big(e^{-2\pi i\phi_{K}^{\lambda}(\cdot,\bar{\xi})}T^{\lambda}_{K}f(\cdot)\big)(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{B_{1}^{n-1}(0)}e^{2\pi i(\phi_{K}^{\lambda}(x,\xi)-\phi_{K}^{\lambda}(x,\bar{\xi})-x\cdot\omega)}\mathfrak{a}^{\lambda}(x,\xi)f(\xi)d\xi dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}^{n-1}(0)}F_{\lambda}(\xi,\omega)f(\xi)d\xi,

where

F_{\lambda}(\xi,\omega):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi i(\phi_{K}^{\lambda}(x,\xi)-\phi_{K}^{\lambda}(x,\bar{\xi})-x\cdot\omega)}\mathfrak{a}^{\lambda}(x,\xi)dx.

We can rewrite as

F_{\lambda}(\xi,\omega):=\lambda^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi i\lambda(\phi_{K}(x,\xi)-\phi_{K}(x,\bar{\xi})-x\cdot\omega)}\mathfrak{a}(x,\xi)dx.

For
|\omega|\geq CK^{-1}
and
x\in B_{1}^{n}(0)
, we have

|\nabla_{x}\phi_{K}(x,\xi)-\nabla_{x}\phi_{K}(x,\bar{\xi})-\omega|\geq K^{-1}.

Integration by parts, it follows that

\displaystyle|F_{\lambda}(\xi,\omega)|\leq{\rm RapDec}(\lambda)(1+|\omega|)^{-(n+1)}.

(3.31)

Thus we have

\mathcal{F}\Big(e^{-2\pi i\phi_{K}^{\lambda}(\cdot,\bar{\xi})}T^{\lambda}_{K}f(\cdot)\Big)(\omega)=\widehat{\psi_{K/C}}(\omega)\mathcal{F}\Big(e^{-2\pi i\phi_{K}^{\lambda}(\cdot,\bar{\xi})}T^{\lambda}_{K}f(\cdot)\Big)(\omega)+U(f,\lambda)(\omega),

where
|U(f,\lambda)(\omega)|\leq{\rm RapDec}(\lambda)(1+|\omega|)^{-(n+1)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))}
. By making use of Fourier inversion we obtain

e^{-2\pi i\phi_{K}^{\lambda}(x,\bar{\xi})}T^{\lambda}_{K}f(x)=\psi_{K/C}\ast\big(e^{-2\pi i\phi_{K}^{\lambda}(\cdot,\bar{\xi})}T^{\lambda}_{K}f(\cdot)\big)(x)+{\rm RapDec}(\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))}.

Then (
3.30
) holds true obviously.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

For any two
K^{-1}

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{B}}\|T_{K}^{\lambda}f_{\tau_{1}}\|_{L^{p}(Q)}^{\frac{p}{2}}\|T_{K}^{\lambda}f_{\tau_{2}}\|_{L^{p}(Q)}^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq K^{O(1)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))}^{p}+{\rm RapDec}(\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))}.

(3.32)

Proof.

|T^{\lambda}_{K}f_{\tau}(x)|=\big|\big(e^{-2\pi i\phi_{K}^{\lambda}(\cdot,\bar{\xi})}T^{\lambda}_{K}f_{\tau}(\cdot)\big)\ast\psi_{K/C}(x)\big|+{\rm RapDec}(\lambda),

for each

To prove

\displaystyle K^{O(1)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))}^{p}.

(3.33)

Where we have used Theorem

4.
proof of the decoupling theorem

4.1.
Reduction

First, we recall the decoupling theorem of Bourgain-Demeter
[
4
]
. Let
S
be a compact hypersurface with nonvanishing Gaussian curvature, and
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(S)
be the
\delta
-neighborhood of
S
. Decompose
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(S)
into a collection of finitely-overlapping slabs
\{\vartriangle\}
of dimension
\delta^{1/2}
in the tangent direction and
\delta
in the normal direction. We have the following decomposition

f=\sum_{\vartriangle}f_{\vartriangle},

where
{\rm supp}\widehat{f_{\vartriangle}}\subset\vartriangle
. A classical decoupling result associated to this decomposition is as follows:

Theorem 4.1

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
S
be a compact smooth hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with nonvanishing Gaussian curvature. If
{\rm supp}\hat{f}\subset\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(S)
, then for
p\geq\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}
and
\varepsilon>0
,

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq_{\varepsilon}\delta^{\frac{n}{p}-\frac{n-1}{2}-\varepsilon}\Big(\sum_{\vartriangle}\|f_{\vartriangle}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(4.1)

They also have a local version of decoupling

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}^{n})}\leq_{\varepsilon}\delta^{\frac{n}{p}-\frac{n-1}{2}-\varepsilon}\Big(\sum_{\vartriangle}\|f_{\vartriangle}\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{R}^{n}})}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(4.2)

For
1\ll R\leq\lambda
, let
D_{p}(\lambda,R)
be the optimal constant such that

\displaystyle\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}^{n})}\leq D_{p}(\lambda,R)\Big(\sum_{\theta}\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(w_{B_{R}^{n}})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}+{\rm RapDec}(\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{p}}

(4.3)

holds true for all asymptotically flat phase
\phi_{K}
and for all
\mathfrak{a}
satisfying
(
2.4
)
, and uniformly for all
f\in L^{p}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))
.
To prove Theorem
1.3
, it suffices to show

D_{p}(\lambda,R)\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n}{p}+\varepsilon}.

(4.4)

Indeed, by Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|T^{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}^{n})}\leq K^{(n-1)/p^{\prime}}\big(\sum_{\tau}\|T^{\lambda}f_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}^{n})}^{p}\big)^{1/p}.

(4.5)

For each
\tau
, performing the similar procedure as in the proof of Lemma
2.3
, we have

\|T^{\lambda}f_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}^{n})}^{p}\leq CK^{O(1)}\sum_{B_{\tilde{R}}^{n}\subset\Box_{R}}\|T_{\tilde{K}}^{\tilde{\lambda}}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(B_{\tilde{R}}^{n})}^{p},

where
\tilde{f}(\cdot)=K^{-(n-1)}f(K^{-1}\cdot+\xi_{\tau})
,
\tilde{R}=R/K^{2},\tilde{K}=K_{0}\tilde{R}^{\varepsilon^{2}},\tilde{\lambda}=\lambda/K^{2}
, and
\Box_{R}
is a rectangle of dimensions
R/K\times\cdots\times R/K\times R/K^{2}
.
Then by (
4.4
), we have

\displaystyle\|T^{\tilde{\lambda}}_{\tilde{K}}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(B_{\tilde{R}}^{n})}\leq C_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{R})^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n}{p}+\varepsilon}\Big(\sum_{\tilde{\theta}}\|T_{\tilde{K}}^{\tilde{\lambda}}\tilde{f}_{\tilde{\theta}}\|_{L^{p}(w_{B_{\tilde{R}}^{n})}}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}+{\rm RapDec}(\tilde{\lambda})\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))},

(4.6)

where
\tilde{\theta}
is a ball of dimension
\tilde{R}^{-1/2}
. By reversing the change of variables, we finally obtain

\|T^{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}^{n})}\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n}{p}+\varepsilon}\Big(\sum_{\theta}\|T^{\lambda}f_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(w_{B_{R}^{n}})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}+{\rm RapDec}(\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))}.

4.2.
The proof of (
4.4
).

Let
\tau\subset B_{1}^{n-1}(0)
be a ball of radius
K^{-1}
. For convenience, define

\mathbb{H}:=\{(\xi,\langle M\xi,\xi\rangle):\xi\in B_{1}^{n}(0)\},

and denote by
\mathbb{H}_{\tau}
a cap on
\mathbb{H}
,

\mathbb{H}_{\tau}:=\{(\xi,\langle M\xi,\xi\rangle):\xi\in\tau\}.

If
\omega
does not belong to a
CK^{-1}
-neighborhood of
\mathbb{H}
, by Lemma
3.3
we have

\widehat{T^{\lambda}_{K}f}(\omega)={\rm RapDec}(\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))}.

Therefore

T_{K}^{\lambda}f=\chi_{CK^{-1}}(\mathbb{H})(D)T_{K}^{\lambda}f+{\rm RapDec}(\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))}.

(4.7)

Similarly,

T_{K}^{\lambda}f_{\tau}=\chi_{CK^{-1}}(\mathbb{H}_{\tau})(D)T_{K}^{\lambda}f_{\tau}+{\rm RapDec}(\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))}.

(4.8)

Applying local decoupling inequality (
4.2
) to the formula
(
4.7
)
and
(
4.8
)
, we have

\big\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f\big\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}^{n})}\leq C_{\bar{\delta}}K^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n}{p}+\bar{\delta}}\Big(\sum_{\tau}\|T_{K}^{\lambda}f_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{R}^{n}})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}+{\rm RapDec}(\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))},

where
\bar{\delta}>0
is a small constant to be chosen later.
By a similar way as in the proof of Lemma
2.3
, we have

\|T_{K}^{\lambda}f_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{R}^{n}})}^{p}\leq C(K)\sum_{B_{\tilde{R}}^{n}\subset\Box_{R}}\|\tilde{T}^{\tilde{\lambda}}_{\tilde{K}}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(B_{\tilde{R}}^{n})}^{p}+{\rm RapDec}(\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{n}(0))},

(4.9)

where
\tilde{f}(\xi)=K^{-(n-1)}f(\xi_{\tau}+K^{-1}\xi)
and
\xi_{\tau}
is the center of
\tau
.
For each
B_{\tilde{R}}^{n}
, by the definition of
D_{p}(\lambda,R)
, we have

\|\tilde{T}^{\tilde{\lambda}}_{\tilde{K}}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(B_{\tilde{R}}^{n})}\leq D_{p}(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{R})\Big(\sum_{\tilde{\theta}}\|\tilde{T}_{\tilde{K}}^{\tilde{\lambda}}\tilde{f}_{\tilde{\theta}}\|_{L^{p}(w_{B_{\tilde{R}}^{n})}}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}+{\rm RapDec}(\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))},

(4.10)

where
\{\tilde{\theta}\}
is a collection of finitely-overlapping balls of radius
\tilde{R}^{-1/2}
.
By reversing the change of variables, finally we have

\big\|T^{\lambda}_{K}f\big\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}^{n})}\leq C_{\bar{\delta}}K^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n}{p}+\bar{\delta}}D_{p}(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{R})\Big(\sum_{\theta}\|T_{K}^{\lambda}f_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(w_{B_{R}^{n})}}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}+{\rm RapDec}(\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{n-1}(0))}.

(4.11)

Recalling the definition of
D_{p}(\lambda,R)
, we have

D_{p}(\lambda,R)\leq C_{\bar{\delta}}K^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n}{p}+\bar{\delta}}D_{p}(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{R}).

(4.12)

This inequality (
4.12
) yields, by the induction hypothesis that

D_{p}(\lambda,R)\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n}{p}+\varepsilon}C_{\bar{\delta}}K^{\bar{\delta}-2\varepsilon}.

(4.13)

Choosing
\bar{\delta}=\varepsilon^{2}
and
K_{0}
sufficiently large such that

K_{0}^{\varepsilon^{2}-2\varepsilon}C_{\bar{\delta}}\leq 1,

and from (
4.13
), we can complete the induction procedure, i.e.

D_{p}(\lambda,R)\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n}{p}+\varepsilon}.

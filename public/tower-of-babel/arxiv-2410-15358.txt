A New Adaptive Balanced Augmented Lagrangian Method with Application to ISAC Beamforming Design
Jiageng Wu, Bo Jiang, Xinxin Li, Ya-Feng Liu, Jianhua Yuan

Abstract
In this paper, we consider a class of convex programming problems with linear equality constraints, which finds broad applications in machine learning and signal processing. We propose a new adaptive balanced augmented Lagrangian (ABAL) method for solving these problems. The proposed ABAL method adaptively selects the stepsize parameter and enjoys a low per-iteration complexity, involving only the computation of a proximal mapping of the objective function and the solution of a linear equation. These features make the proposed method well-suited to large-scale problems. We then custom-apply the ABAL method to solve the ISAC beamforming design problem, which is formulated as a nonlinear semidefinite program in a previous work. This customized application requires careful exploitation of the problem’s special structure such as the property that all of its signal-to-interference-and-noise-ratio (SINR) constraints hold with equality at the solution and an efficient computation of the proximal mapping of the objective function. Simulation results demonstrate the efficiency of the proposed ABAL method.



I
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following linear-equality constrained convex programming problem (in the complex domain):

\min_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}~f(\mathbf{u})\quad\mbox{s.t.}\quad\mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{b},

(1)

where
f:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is a proper closed convex function, and
\mathbf{D}\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
and
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
are given complex matrix and vector. Throughout the paper, we assume that the proximal mapping of
the objective function
f
in (
1
), which is defined as

\mathrm{prox}_{\tau f}(\mathbf{v}):=\mathop{\mathrm{arg\,min}}_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left\{f(\mathbf{u})+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\|^{2}\right\},

is simple to compute for any given
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\tau>0.
Many problems arising from machine learning and signal processing can be formulated and/or reformulated as problem (
1
)
[

,

,

,

Various types of algorithms have been proposed for solving problem (

,

,
13
,
14
,
15
]
and the references therein. Among all of them, the augmented Lagrangian (AL) method is perhaps the most popular and widely studied one for solving problem (
1
). Specifically, the AL function of problem (
1
) is

{\cal{L}}_{\beta}(\mathbf{u};\bm{\lambda})=f(\mathbf{u})+\left\langle\mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{b},\bm{\lambda}\right\rangle+{\frac{\beta}{2}}{\left\|\mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{b}\right\|}^{2},

where
\bm{\lambda}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
is the Lagrange multiplier associated with the equality constraint
\mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{b}
and
\beta>0
is the penalty parameter. At the
t
-th iteration, the AL method first (approximately) minimizes
{\cal{L}}_{\beta}(\mathbf{u};\bm{\lambda}^{t})
to obtain the next iterate
\mathbf{u}^{t+1},
followed by an update of the multiplier, i.e.,

\begin{cases}\mathbf{u}^{t+1}\in{\mathop{\mathrm{arg\,min}}\limits_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}{\cal{L}}_{\beta}(\mathbf{u};\bm{\lambda}^{t})},\\
\bm{\lambda}^{t+1}=\bm{\lambda}^{t}+\beta\left(\mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}^{t+1}-\mathbf{b}\right).\end{cases}

(2)

In general, the efficiency of the AL method heavily depends on that of solving the AL subproblem (i.e., the
\mathbf{u}
-subproblem) to get the next iterate. The efficiency of the AL method also relies on the choice of the penalty parameter. The recent progress on reducing the computational cost of solving the AL subproblem
[
12
,
14
,
16
]
and adaptively selecting the penalty parameter
[
17
,
18
]
in order to improve the efficiency of the AL method will be briefly reviewed in the next section. Combining these existing techniques and taking a step further, we propose
a new adaptive balanced AL (ABAL) method
for solving problem (
1
), which is the first contribution of this paper. The proposed ABAL method not only adaptively selects the stepsize parameter but also requires only computing a proximal mapping of the objective function and solving a linear equation at each iteration, making it well suitable to solve large-scale ill-conditioned problems.

Integrated sensing and communications (ISAC) has been recognized as one of six usage scenarios of 6G in the Recommendation

A New Adaptive Balanced Augmented Lagrangian Method

In this section, we first briefly review the balanced AL (BAL) method developed in

II-A

BAL Method and Our Motivation

The AL method, which dates back to
[
9
,
10
]
, is one of the most efficient approaches to solving problem (
1
). However, as shown in (
2
), the
\mathbf{u}
-subproblem in the AL method generally does not admit a closed-form solution due to the presence of both the objective function
f
and the coefficient matrix
\mathbf{D}
, and often needs to be solved by an iterative algorithm, which is computationally expensive. To address this issue, the work
[
12
]
proposed a BAL method, which separates the two difficulties by “moving” the terms related to
\mathbf{D}
into the
\bm{\lambda}
-subproblem. The BAL method is presented as follows:

\begin{cases}\mathbf{u}^{t+1}=\mathrm{prox}_{\tau f}(\mathbf{u}^{t}-\tau\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}\bm{\lambda}^{t}),\\
{\mathbf{p}^{t+1}}=\mathbf{D}\left(2\mathbf{u}^{t+1}-\mathbf{u}^{t}\right)-\mathbf{b},\\
\bm{\lambda}^{t+1}=\bm{\lambda}^{t}+(\gamma\tau)^{-1}\left(\mathbf{D}\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}+\theta^{2}\mathbf{I}\right)^{-1}{\mathbf{p}^{t+1}}.\end{cases}

(3)

In the above,

\tau>0
is the primal stepsize,
\gamma
is the dual stepsize, and
\theta>0
is a preset small parameter. The parameter
\gamma
is set to be 1 in
[
12
]
, and is extended to any number in
[3/4,+\infty)
in
[
14
]
. The worst-case
\mathcal{O}(1/t)
convergence rate and global convergence of the BAL method are established in
[
12
,
14
]
.

Two remarks on the BAL method in (
3
) are in order. First, as can be seen from (
3
), both of the
\mathbf{u}
-subproblem and the
\bm{\lambda}
-subproblem in the BAL method (
3
) are simple. In particular, the
\mathbf{u}
-subproblem in (
3
) admits a closed-form solution, which is in sharp constrast to the
\mathbf{u}
-subproblem in the classical AL method (
2
); solving the
\bm{\lambda}
-subproblem in (
3
) is equivalent to solving the following linear equation

\left(\mathbf{D}\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}+\theta^{2}\mathbf{I}\right)\left(\bm{\lambda}-\bm{\lambda}^{t}\right)=(\gamma\tau)^{-1}\mathbf{p}^{t+1},

(4)

which is efficiently solvable. More importantly, in many cases of interest (including the case to be presented in Section
III
), the coefficient matrix
\mathbf{D}
exhibits a special structure, which further enables more efficient solution of the
\bm{\lambda}
-subproblem.

Second, the high computational efficiency of the BAL method (
3
) depends on a good choice of the stepsize parameter
\tau
, which is typically chosen manually in literature (e.g.,
[
14
]
).
However, manual tuning of the stepsize parameter
\tau
is computationally intensive and lacks robustness, highlighting the need for an adaptive strategy to select
\tau
. Recently, the work
[
18
]
proposed an accelerated BAL method with a dynamic stepsize
\tau
, but their approach relies on the strong convexity assumption of
f
, which does not apply to our interested problem.

Given this background, we are motivated to develop a new BAL method with an adaptive choice of the stepsize parameter
\tau
for solving large-scale problem (
1
) without the strong convexity assumption.

II-B

Proposed ABAL Method

In this subsection, we propose a new ABAL method for solving problem (
1
). This is achieved based on the fact that the BAL method can be interpreted as a DRS method
[
8
]
and the application of the non-stationary DRS framework
[
17
]
to the BAL method.

More specifically, using the lift technique introduced in
[
27
]
, we can equivalently rewrite problem (
1
) as

\min_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}~f(\mathbf{u})\quad\mbox{s.t.}\quad\mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}+\theta\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{b},\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{0}.

(5)

Denote
h(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})=f(\mathbf{u})+\mathbb{I}_{\{\mathbf{0}\}}(\mathbf{v})
and
g(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})=\mathbb{I}_{\{\mathbf{b}\}}(\mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}+\theta\mathbf{v}).

Then we can further rewrite problem (
5
) as

\min_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{C}^{n},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}}\,h(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})+g(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}).

(6)

By the optimality condition, solving problem (
6
) is equivalent to finding a zero of the sum of two maximal monotone operators, i.e.,

\mathbf{0}\in(\partial h+\partial g)(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}).

(7)

Now we can apply the non-stationary DRS method
[
17
]
to solve problem (
7
), which yileds the ABAL method, as outlined in Algorithm
1
.
The detailed derivation is similar to that of tuning-free PDHG in
[
28
]
and thus is omitted. The global convergence of Algorithm
1
follows from
[
17
, Theorem 3.2]
. Note that the initial value
\tau_{0}
is set to be
1
in
[
17
, Theorem 3.2]
. Our result allows
\tau_{0}
to be chosen as any positive number.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\{\omega_{t}\}\subset(0,1]
be a sequence such that
\sum_{t=0}^{+\infty}\omega_{t}<+\infty
and
\omega_{0}=1
. Then the sequence
\{\mathbf{u}^{t}\}
generated by Algorithm
1
converges to an optimal solution
\mathbf{u}^{*}
of problem (
1
).

Before leaving this section, we make some remarks on the comparison between our work and the one in
[
28
]
. First, the work
[
28
]
applied the non-stationary DRS method to solve a different lift problem from problem (
5
), where the term
\theta\mathbf{v}
in (
5
) is replaced by
\mathbf{T}\mathbf{v}
with
\mathbf{T}
being a carefully chosen coefficient matrix. Second, the key difference between the tuning-free PDHG method in
[
28
]
and our proposed ABAL method lies in their update of the dual variable. In particular, the dual variable in
[
28
]
is updated as follows:

\bm{\lambda}^{t+1}=\bm{\lambda}^{t}+(\upsilon\tau_{t}\|\mathbf{D}\|_{2}^{2})^{-1}\bm{p}^{t+1}\quad{\mbox{with}~~\upsilon\geq 1}.

The above update of the dual variable can be interpreted as an approximation of our update rule in Algorithm
1
with the matrix
\mathbf{D}\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}+\theta^{2}\mathbf{I}
approximated by
\upsilon\|\mathbf{D}\|_{2}^{2}\mathbf{I}
. This approximation simplifies the dual update but also significantly slows down the convergence of the corresponding algorithm, as can be seen clearly in Section IV.

III
ISAC Beamforming Design

In this section, we apply the proposed ABAL method to solve the ISAC beamforming design problem
[
6
, Problem (36)]
. Before doing it, we first introduce the beamforming design problem and analyze its special structures to facilitate the application of the ABAL method.

III-A

Problem Reformulation and Structure

Consider the same scenario as in
[
6
]
, where a base station (BS) equipped with
N>1
antennas serves
K
single-antenna users and at the same time detects an (extended) target. For
k=1,2,\ldots,K
, let
\mathbf{h}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
denote the channel between the BS and the
k
-th user and
\Gamma_{k}
denote the SINR target required by the
k
-th user. Let
\sigma_{C}^{2}
be the noise level and
P_{T}
be the power budget of the BS. Then the ISAC beamforming design problem
[
6
, Problem (36)]
of minimizing the Cramér-Rao bound subject to all users’ SINR constraints and the BS’s power budget constraint is given as follows:

\displaystyle\min_{\left\{\mathbf{W}_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{K+1}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\left(\left(\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K+1}\mathbf{W}_{k}\right)^{-1}\right)

(8)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle\rho_{k}\left\langle\mathbf{Q}_{k},\mathbf{W}_{k}\right\rangle-\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{K+1}\left\langle\mathbf{Q}_{k},\mathbf{W}_{i}\right\rangle\geq\sigma_{C}^{2},\,\forall\,k,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K+1}\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{W}_{k}\right)\leq P_{T},\mathbf{W}_{k}\in\mathbb{H}_{+}^{N\times N},\,\forall\,k,

where
\mathbf{Q}_{k}=\mathbf{h}_{k}\mathbf{h}_{k}^{\dagger},\rho_{k}=1+\Gamma_{k}^{-1},
and
\mathbf{W}_{k}\in\mathbb{H}_{+}^{N\times N}
is the relaxed matrix variable from the beamforming vector to be designed for the
k
-th user for
k=1,2,\ldots,K.
It has been shown in
[
23
,
6
]
that problem (
8
) must have a rank-one solution of
\left\{\mathbf{W}_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{K}.
In this paper, we focus on solving problem (
8
).

Problem (
8
) is a nonlinear semidefinite program (SDP) with
K+1
inequality constraints, which are generally more challenging to handle than equality constraints. Fortunately, by leveraging the special structure of problem (
8
), we can show, in the following Theorem
2
, that there exists a solution of problem (
8
) at which all (SINR and power budget) inequality constraints hold with equality. This property will significantly faciliate the application of the proposed ABAL method to solve problem (
8
). The proof of Theorem
2
can be found in Appendix
A
.

Theorem 2
.

There exists a solution to problem (
8
) at which all inequality constraints hold with equality.

It follows from Theorem
2
that problem (
8
) is equivalent to the following problem:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{W}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\left(\left(\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K+1}\mathbf{W}_{k}\right)^{-1}\right)+\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathbf{W})

(9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle\rho_{k}\left\langle\mathbf{Q}_{k},\mathbf{W}_{k}\right\rangle-\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{K+1}\left\langle\mathbf{Q}_{k},\mathbf{W}_{i}\right\rangle=\sigma_{C}^{2},\,\forall\,k,

where
\mathbf{W}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{W}_{1},\mathbf{W}_{2},\ldots,\mathbf{W}_{K+1}\end{bmatrix}^{\dagger}
and
{\cal W}=\big\{\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{C}^{(K+1)N\times N}\mid\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K+1}\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}_{k})=P_{T},\mathbf{W}_{k}\in\mathbb{H}_{+}^{N\times N},k=1,2,\ldots,K+1\big\}
.

III-B

Customized Application

In this subsection, we apply the proposed ABAL method (i.e., Algorithm
1
) to solve problem (
9
). To achieve an efficient application, it is expected that each subproblem therein is computationally efficient. However, for problem (
9
), solving the
\mathbf{W}
-subproblem is computationally expensive, as it requires computing the proximal mapping of the objective function of problem (
9
), which is the sum of the trace of the inverse of a matrix and the indicator function of a convex set. To mitigate this difficulty, we introduce an auxiliary variable
\mathbf{Z}=\sum_{k=1}^{K+1}\mathbf{W}_{k}
into problem (
9
) and further reformulate it as the following compact form that is more favorable for algorithmic design:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{W},\,\mathbf{Z}}

\displaystyle h(\mathbf{Z}):=\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{Z}^{-1}\right)+\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{W}}}(\mathbf{W})

(10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Z})=\sigma_{C}^{2}\mathbf{1},~\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Z})=\mathbf{0},

where two linear operators
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are defined as follows:
\mathcal{A}:\mathbb{C}^{(K+1)N\times N}\times\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}\to\mathbb{R}^{K}

with
(\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Z}))_{k}=\rho_{k}\left\langle\mathbf{Q}_{k},\mathbf{W}_{k}\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathbf{Q}_{k},\mathbf{Z}\right\rangle
for
k=1,2,\ldots,{K}
and
\mathcal{B}:\mathbb{C}^{(K+1)N\times N}\times\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
with
\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Z})=\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K+1}\mathbf{W}_{k}-\mathbf{Z}
.

By vectorizing the variables
\mathbf{W}
and
\mathbf{Z}
and choosing an appropriate
f
, it is clear to see that problem (
10
) is an instance of problem (
1
) with
n=(K+2)N^{2}
and
m=K+N^{2}
. Consequently, we can apply
Algorithm
1
to solve (
10
). As will be seen in Section
III-C
, the complexity of computing the proximal mapping of the corresponding
f
is
\mathcal{O}((K+2)N^{3})
. Moreover, at first glance, updating the variable
\bm{\lambda}
seems to require solving a linear system of size
(K+N^{2})\times(K+N^{2})
, which generally incurs a complexity of
\mathcal{O}((K+N^{2})^{3})
. Fortunately, by fully leveraging the special structure of the coefficient matrices associated with
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, we can significantly reduce this complexity to
\mathcal{O}(K^{3}+KN^{2})
.

The
t
-th iteration of the customized ABAL algorithm for solving problem (
9
) is presented below, with detailed derivations provided in Appendix
B
.

),
\bm{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
and

-th entry of

. In the above,

and

represent the Kroneker and Hadamard product operators, respectively, and

) plays a central role in applying the proposed ABAL algorithm for efficiently solving the problem. The structures of problem (

III-C

Implementation Details

The dominant computational cost in (
11
) lies in computing
\mathbf{W}^{t+1}
and
\mathbf{Z}^{t+1}
, which involves performing
K+2
eigenvalue decompositions of
N\times N

\mathcal{O}((K+2)N^{3})
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{k}=\mathbf{U}_{k}\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\dagger}
be the eigenvalue decomposition of
\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{k}
for
k=1,2,\ldots,K+1
, where
\mathbf{U}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
is a unitary matrix and
\bm{\Sigma}_{k}
is diagonal with real entries. Then, for

Appendix A
Proof of Theorem
2

We prove the theorem in two steps. First, we claim that all optimal solutions of problem (
8
) must satisfy
\sum_{k=1}^{K+1}\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{W}_{k}\right)=P_{T}
. We show this by contradiction. Assume that there exists an optimal solution
\mathbf{W}^{*}
such that the strict inequality holds, i.e.,

\sum_{k=1}^{K+1}\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{W}^{*}_{k}\right)<P_{T}.

We construct a new point
\widehat{\mathbf{W}}^{*}=\varrho\mathbf{W}^{*}
with
\varrho=P_{T}/\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{W}^{*}_{k}\right)>1
. It is simple to show that
\widehat{\mathbf{W}}^{*}
is feasible to problem (
8
) with
\sum_{k=1}^{K+1}\mathrm{tr}\big(\widehat{\mathbf{W}}^{*}_{k}\big)=P_{T}.

However, we have

\mathrm{tr}\left(\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K+1}\widehat{\mathbf{W}}^{*}_{k}\right)^{-1}\right)<\mathrm{tr}\left(\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K+1}\mathbf{W}^{*}_{k}\right)^{-1}\right).

This contradicts the optimality of
\mathbf{W}^{*}
. Therefore, any optimal solution of problem (
8
) must satisfy
\sum_{k=1}^{K+1}\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{W}_{k}\right)=P_{T}
.

Next, we show the existence of at least one optimal solution to problem (
8
) that satisfies all SINR inequality constraints with equality, i.e.,

\rho_{k}\left\langle\mathbf{Q}_{k},\mathbf{W}_{k}\right\rangle-\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{K+1}\left\langle\mathbf{Q}_{k},\mathbf{W}_{i}\right\rangle=\sigma_{C}^{2},~~\forall\,k.

(13)

Assume, for contradiction, that there exists an optimal solution
\mathbf{W}^{*}
to problem (
8
) for which at least one strict inequality holds. Without loss of generality, we consider the case where
k=1
, which leads to the following strict inequality:

\rho_{1}\left\langle\mathbf{Q}_{1},\mathbf{W}_{1}^{*}\right\rangle-\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{K+1}\left\langle\mathbf{Q}_{1},\mathbf{W}_{i}^{*}\right\rangle>\sigma_{C}^{2}.

This can be written as

\left\langle\mathbf{Q}_{1},\rho_{1}\mathbf{W}_{1}^{*}-\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{K+1}\mathbf{W}_{i}^{*}\right\rangle>\sigma_{C}^{2}.

By choosing

\hat{\varrho}=\frac{\left\langle\mathbf{Q}_{1},\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{K+1}\mathbf{W}_{i}^{*}\right\rangle+\sigma_{C}^{2}}{\left\langle\mathbf{Q}_{1},\rho_{1}\mathbf{W}_{1}^{*}\right\rangle}\in(0,1),

we construct the following new point
\widehat{\mathbf{W}}^{*}
:

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{W}}^{*}_{1}:=\hat{\varrho}\mathbf{W}^{*}_{1},~~\widehat{\mathbf{W}}^{*}_{K+1}:=\mathbf{W}^{*}_{K+1}+(1-\hat{\varrho})\mathbf{W}^{*}_{1},

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{W}}^{*}_{k}:=\mathbf{W}^{*}_{k},~~k=2,3,\ldots,K.

It is straightforward to verify that the above
\widehat{\mathbf{W}}^{*}
is feasible to problem (
8
) and that the first SINR inequality constraint of problem (
8
) is satisfied with equality. Moreover, we have

\mathrm{tr}\left(\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K+1}\widehat{\mathbf{W}}^{*}_{k}\right)^{-1}\right)=\mathrm{tr}\left(\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K+1}\mathbf{W}^{*}_{k}\right)^{-1}\right),

which implies that the feasible solution
\widehat{\mathbf{W}}^{*}
is also optimal. By repeatedly applying the above procedure (at most
K
times), we can construct a new optimal solution that satisfies all the equalities in (
13
).
The proof is complete.

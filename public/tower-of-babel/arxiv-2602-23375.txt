Analytical Expression for Spherically Symmetric Photoacoustic Sources: A Unified General Solution
Shuang Li, Yibing Wang, Yu Zhang, Changhui Li

Abstract
Here we present a comprehensive derivation of the analytical expression for the spatiotemporal acoustic pressure generated by photoacoustic sources with spherically symmetric initial pressure distributions. Starting from the fundamental photoacoustic wave equation, we derive a unified analytical solution applicable to arbitrary spherically symmetric initial distributions. Specific expressions are provided for several common distributions including uniform spherical sources, Gaussian distributions, exponential distributions, and power-law distributions. Far-field approximations are also discussed. The derived expressions provide valuable tools for photoacoustic imaging system design and signal analysis. We provide codes for ultrafast forward simulation using the general analytical spherically symmetric model, the implementation is available in the GitHub repository: https://github.com/JaegerCQ/SlingBAG_Ultra .



1
Theoretical Background

1.1
Photoacoustic Wave Equation

The pressure wave generated by the photoacoustic effect satisfies the following wave equation
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
:

\left(\nabla^{2}-\frac{1}{v_{s}^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\right)p(\mathbf{r},t)=-\frac{\beta}{C_{p}}\frac{\partial H(\mathbf{r},t)}{\partial t}

(1)

where:

•

p(\mathbf{r},t)
is the acoustic pressure

•

v_{s}
is the speed of sound

•

\beta
is the thermal expansion coefficient

•

C_{p}
is the specific heat capacity

•

H(\mathbf{r},t)
is the heating function

For instantaneous heating, the initial conditions are:

p(\mathbf{r},0)=p_{0}(\mathbf{r}),\quad\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}(\mathbf{r},0)=0

(2)

1.2
Fundamental Integral Formula

The solution to the photoacoustic wave equation can be expressed using Green’s function as
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
:

p(\mathbf{r},t)=\frac{1}{4\pi v_{s}^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\frac{1}{v_{s}t}\int d\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\,p_{0}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\,\delta\!\left(t-\frac{\absolutevalue{\vect{r}-\vect{r}'}}{v_{s}}\right)\right]

(3)

where
p_{0}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
is the initial pressure distribution (Fig.
1
).

Schematic geometry of spherically symmetric photoacoustic wave propagation.
The photoacoustic source is located at the origin
O
, the field point is
P(\mathbf{r})
,
and
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}
denotes the position vector within the source region.

2
Derivation for Spherically Symmetric Case

2.1
Basic Assumptions

Assume the initial pressure distribution is spherically symmetric about the origin:

p_{0}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})=p_{0}(r^{\prime}),\quad r^{\prime}=\absolutevalue{\vect{r}'}

(4)

Denote
r=\absolutevalue{\vect{r}}
.

2.2
Delta Function Transformation

Using the scaling property of the delta function:

\delta\!\left(t-\frac{\absolutevalue{\vect{r}-\vect{r}'}}{v_{s}}\right)=v_{s}\,\delta\!\left(\absolutevalue{\vect{r}-\vect{r}'}-v_{s}t\right)

(5)

Substituting into the original expression:

p(\mathbf{r},t)=\frac{1}{4\pi v_{s}^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\frac{1}{t}\int d\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\,p_{0}(r^{\prime})\,\delta\!\left(\absolutevalue{\vect{r}-\vect{r}'}-v_{s}t\right)\right]

(6)

2.3
Spatial Integral Treatment

Consider the integral:

I=\int d\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\,p_{0}(r^{\prime})\,\delta\!\left(\absolutevalue{\vect{r}-\vect{r}'}-R\right)

(7)

where
R=v_{s}t
.

Using spherical coordinates
(r^{\prime},\theta,\phi)
, where
\theta
is the angle between
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{r}
. By the law of cosines:

\absolutevalue{\vect{r}-\vect{r}'}=\sqrt{r^{2}+r^{\prime 2}-2rr^{\prime}\cos\theta}

(8)

After a series of transformations (detailed derivation in Appendix), we obtain:

\delta\!\left(\absolutevalue{\vect{r}-\vect{r}'}-R\right)=\frac{R}{rr^{\prime}}\,\delta\!\left(\cos\theta-\frac{r^{2}+r^{\prime 2}-R^{2}}{2rr^{\prime}}\right)

(9)

2.4
Angular Integration

Integrating over angles:

\int d\Omega\,\delta\!\left(\absolutevalue{\vect{r}-\vect{r}'}-R\right)=\frac{2\pi R}{rr^{\prime}}

(10)

with the condition
\absolutevalue{\frac{r^2 + r'^2 - R^2}{2rr'}}\leq 1
, i.e.,
\absolutevalue{r-R}\leq r^{\prime}\leq r+R
.

Therefore:

I=\frac{2\pi R}{r}\int_{\absolutevalue{r-R}}^{r+R}r^{\prime}p_{0}(r^{\prime})\,dr^{\prime}

(11)

2.5
Time Derivative Calculation

Substituting into the pressure expression:

p(r,t)=\frac{1}{4\pi v_{s}^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\frac{1}{t}\cdot\frac{2\pi v_{s}t}{r}\int_{\absolutevalue{r-v_s t}}^{r+v_{s}t}r^{\prime}p_{0}(r^{\prime})\,dr^{\prime}\right]

(12)

Simplifying:

p(r,t)=\frac{1}{2v_{s}r}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\int_{\absolutevalue{r-v_s t}}^{r+v_{s}t}r^{\prime}p_{0}(r^{\prime})\,dr^{\prime}\right]

(13)

Applying Leibniz’s rule:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{a(t)}^{b(t)}f(x)\,dx=f(b(t))\frac{db}{dt}-f(a(t))\frac{da}{dt}

(14)

where
a(t)=\absolutevalue{r-v_s t}
,
b(t)=r+v_{s}t
.

2.6
Final Analytical Expression

After calculating the derivatives, we obtain the unified analytical expression for the spherically symmetric case:

\boxed{p(r,t)=\frac{1}{2r}\left[(r+v_{s}t)p_{0}(r+v_{s}t)+(r-v_{s}t)p_{0}(\absolutevalue{r-v_s t})\right]}

(15)

3
Analytical Solutions for Typical Initial Distributions

3.1
Uniform Spherical Source of Radius
a_{0}

Initial condition:

p_{0}(r)=p_{0}\,U(a_{0}-r)

(16)

where
U(x)
is the unit step function.

3.1.1
Observation Point Outside the Sphere (
r>a_{0}
)

•

When
v_{s}t<r-a_{0}
or
v_{s}t>r+a_{0}
:
p(r,t)=0

•

When
r-a_{0}\leq v_{s}t\leq r+a_{0}
:

p(r,t)=\frac{p_{0}}{2r}(r-v_{s}t)

(17)

3.1.2
Observation Point Inside the Sphere (
r<a_{0}
)

•

When
v_{s}t<a_{0}-r
:
p(r,t)=p_{0}

•

When
a_{0}-r\leq v_{s}t\leq a_{0}+r
:

p(r,t)=\frac{p_{0}}{2r}(r-v_{s}t)

(18)

•

When
v_{s}t>a_{0}+r
:
p(r,t)=0

3.2
Gaussian Distribution

Initial condition:

p_{0}(r)=p_{c}\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)

(19)

Analytical solution:

p(r,t)=\frac{p_{c}}{2r}\left[(r+v_{s}t)\exp\!\left(-\frac{(r+v_{s}t)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)+(r-v_{s}t)\exp\!\left(-\frac{(r-v_{s}t)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\right]

(20)

3.3
Exponential Distribution

Initial condition:

p_{0}(r)=p_{c}\,e^{-r/a}

(21)

Analytical solution:

p(r,t)=\frac{p_{c}}{2r}\left[(r+v_{s}t)e^{-(r+v_{s}t)/a}+(r-v_{s}t)e^{-\absolutevalue{r-v_s t}/a}\right]

(22)

3.4
Power-Law Distribution

Initial condition:

p_{0}(r)=\frac{A}{(r^{2}+a^{2})^{\nu}},\quad\nu>\frac{1}{2}

(23)

Analytical solution:

p(r,t)=\frac{A}{2r}\left[\frac{r+v_{s}t}{\big((r+v_{s}t)^{2}+a^{2}\big)^{\nu}}+\frac{r-v_{s}t}{\big((r-v_{s}t)^{2}+a^{2}\big)^{\nu}}\right]

(24)

Common special case
\nu=\frac{3}{2}
:

p(r,t)=\frac{A}{2r}\left[\frac{r+v_{s}t}{\big((r+v_{s}t)^{2}+a^{2}\big)^{3/2}}+\frac{r-v_{s}t}{\big((r-v_{s}t)^{2}+a^{2}\big)^{3/2}}\right]

(25)

4
Far-Field Approximations for Specific Distributions

Under far-field conditions (
r\gg\text{characteristic source dimension}
and
t\approx r/v_{s}
), the general expression (
15
) can be significantly simplified for specific initial distributions. In this regime, the first term
(r+v_{s}t)p_{0}(r+v_{s}t)
represents an inward converging wave that is exponentially or power-law suppressed near the observation time window. The dominant contribution comes from the second term
(r-v_{s}t)p_{0}(\absolutevalue{r-v_s t})
, leading to the unified far-field approximation:

p(r,t)\approx\frac{1}{2r}(r-v_{s}t)p_{0}(\absolutevalue{r-v_s t})

(26)

4.1
Far-Field Solution for Gaussian Distribution

Applying the far-field condition (
26
) to the Gaussian distribution (
19
):

p(r,t)\approx\frac{p_{c}}{2r}(r-v_{s}t)\exp\!\left(-\frac{(r-v_{s}t)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)

(27)

This represents a Gaussian pulse propagating outward with speed
v_{s}
.

4.2
Far-Field Solution for Exponential Distribution

Applying the far-field condition (
26
) to the exponential distribution (
21
):

p(r,t)\approx\frac{p_{c}}{2r}(r-v_{s}t)e^{-\absolutevalue{r-v_s t}/a}

(28)

This represents an exponentially decaying pulse propagating outward with speed
v_{s}
. The characteristic decay length in space is
a
.

4.3
Far-Field Solution for Power-Law Distribution

Applying the far-field condition (
26
) to the power-law distribution (
23
):

p(r,t)\approx\frac{A}{2r}\frac{r-v_{s}t}{\big((r-v_{s}t)^{2}+a^{2}\big)^{\nu}}

(29)

For the special case
\nu=3/2
:

p(r,t)\approx\frac{A}{2r}\frac{r-v_{s}t}{\big((r-v_{s}t)^{2}+a^{2}\big)^{3/2}}

(30)

4.4
Far-Field Solution for Uniform Spherical Source

For a uniform spherical source of radius
a_{0}
with observation point outside the sphere (
r>a_{0}
), the exact solution (
17
) is already in a simple form. In the far-field limit
r\gg a_{0}
, and near the arrival time
t\approx r/v_{s}
, we have:

p(r,t)=\frac{p_{0}}{2r}(r-v_{s}t)\quad\text{for }r-a_{0}\leq v_{s}t\leq r+a_{0}

(31)

This shows a linear dependence on
(r-v_{s}t)
in the far field.

Appendix A
Detailed Derivations

A.1
Detailed Delta Function Transformation

Starting from:

\delta\!\left(\sqrt{A}-R\right),\quad A=r^{2}+r^{\prime 2}-2rr^{\prime}\cos\theta

(32)

Let
g(A)=\sqrt{A}-R
, then:

\frac{dg}{dA}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{A}}

(33)

At
A=R^{2}
:

\left|\frac{dg}{dA}\right|=\frac{1}{2R}

(34)

Using the delta function transformation formula:

\delta(g(A))=\frac{\delta(A-R^{2})}{\absolutevalue{g'(R^2)}}=2R\,\delta(A-R^{2})

(35)

Thus:

\delta\!\left(\sqrt{A}-R\right)=2R\,\delta(A-R^{2})

(36)

A.2
Delta Function Transformation for
\cos\theta

Let:

h(\cos\theta)=r^{2}+r^{\prime 2}-R^{2}-2rr^{\prime}\cos\theta

(37)

The zero point is at:

\cos\theta_{0}=\frac{r^{2}+r^{\prime 2}-R^{2}}{2rr^{\prime}}

(38)

The derivative is:

\absolutevalue{\frac{dh}{d(\cos\theta)}}=2rr^{\prime}

(39)

Therefore:

\delta(h(\cos\theta))=\frac{1}{2rr^{\prime}}\,\delta\!\left(\cos\theta-\frac{r^{2}+r^{\prime 2}-R^{2}}{2rr^{\prime}}\right)

(40)

Combining with the previous factor
2R
, we obtain:

\delta\!\left(\absolutevalue{\vect{r}-\vect{r}'}-R\right)=\frac{R}{rr^{\prime}}\,\delta\!\left(\cos\theta-\frac{r^{2}+r^{\prime 2}-R^{2}}{2rr^{\prime}}\right)

(41)

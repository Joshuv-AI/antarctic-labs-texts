On the Fučík spectrum of the Logarithmic Laplacian
Rakesh Arora, Tuhina Mukherjee

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the Fučík spectrum Σ L \Sigma_{L} associated with the logarithmic Laplacian. This spectrum is defined as the set of all pairs ( α , β ) ∈ ℝ 2 (\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2} for which the problem { L Δ ​ u = α ​ u + − β ​ u − in Ω , u = 0 in ℝ N ∖ Ω , \left\{\begin{aligned} L_{\Delta}u\>&=\alpha u^{+}-\beta u^{-}&&\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{in}\penalty\ \penalty\ \Omega,\\ u&=0&&\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{in}\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{aligned}\right. admits a nontrivial solution u u . Here, Ω ⊂ ℝ N \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N} is a bounded domain with C 1 , 1 C^{1,1} boundary, u ± = max ⁡ { ± u , 0 } u^{\pm}=\max\{\pm u,0\} , and u = u + − u − u=u^{+}-u^{-} . We show that the lines λ 1 L × ℝ \lambda_{1}^{L}\times\mathbb{R} and ℝ × λ 1 L \mathbb{R}\times\lambda_{1}^{L} , where λ 1 L \lambda_{1}^{L} denotes the first eigenvalue of L Δ L_{\Delta} , lies in the spectrum Σ L \Sigma_{L} and are isolated within the spectrum. Furthermore, we establish the existence of the first nontrivial curve in Σ L \Sigma_{L} and analyze its qualitative properties, including Lipschitz continuity, strict monotonicity, and asymptotic behavior. In addition, we obtain a variational characterization of the second eigenvalue of the logarithmic Laplacian and show that all eigenfunctions corresponding to eigenvalues λ > λ 1 L \lambda>\lambda_{1}^{L} are sign-changing. Finally, we address a nonresonance problem with respect to the Fučík spectrum Σ L \Sigma_{L} , employing variational methods and carefully overcoming the difficulties arising from the contrasting features of the first eigenvalue λ 1 L \lambda_{1}^{L} .

The study of the Fučík spectrum has attracted sustained interest in nonlinear analysis and spectral theory over the past several decades. The pioneering work of Fučík
[
11
]
initiated the investigation of nonlinear eigenvalue problems involving asymmetric jumping nonlinearities, thereby introducing the notion of the Fučík spectrum. Since then, this concept has served as a fundamental framework for the analysis of a wide class of nonlinear boundary value problems.

Building on this foundation, Cuesta, de Figueiredo, and Gossez
[
7
]
extended the theory to the
p
-Laplacian operator, providing a detailed study of the structure and qualitative properties of the Fučík spectrum in a nonlinear setting. Their work yielded significant insights into bifurcation phenomena and the multiplicity of solutions. For further developments concerning the Laplacian and the
p
-Laplacian, we refer the reader to
[
15
,
24
,
25
]
and the references therein.

In the context of nonlocal operators, Goyal and Sreenadh
[
14
]
investigated the Fučík spectrum associated with the fractional Laplacian, extending several classical results from local operators to the nonlocal framework. Using variational and topological methods, they established the existence of a first nontrivial curve in the Fučík spectrum and analyzed its monotonicity and asymptotic behaviour. Earlier, Sreenadh
[
27
]
studied the Fučík spectrum of the Hardy–Sobolev operator, introducing weighted Sobolev spaces to handle nonlinear eigenvalue problems involving singular potentials.

More recently, Goel, Goyal, and Sreenadh
[
13
]
examined the Fučík spectrum of the
p
-fractional Laplacian subject to nonlocal normal derivative conditions. We also mention the recent work
[
21
]
, which addresses the Fučík spectrum for mixed local-nonlocal operators, further enriching the theory and highlighting the growing interest in nonlocal and mixed frameworks.

Sign-changing properties

We begin by showing the geodesic convexity property of the energy term
\mathcal{E}_{L}
and the sign-changing properties of the solution of the weighted logarithmic Laplacian problem with bounded weights away from
\lambda_{1}
on a subset of positive measure. As an application of this, we show that the eigenfunctions corresponding to eigenvalues
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
are sign-changing.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
N\geq 1
and
W:{\mathbb{H}}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
be the functional defined as

W(u):=\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u).

(2.1)

For functions

\sigma_{t}(x):=\left(tu^{2}+(1-t)v^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all}\ t\in[0,1].

Then,

W(\sigma_{t})\leq tW(u)+(1-t)W(v).

Proof.

Let

By
[
5

, we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u)&=\frac{c_{N}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N}}\penalty\ dx\penalty\ dy+\int_{\Omega}\left(h_{\Omega}(x)+\rho_{N}\right)u^{2}(x)\penalty\ dx\\
&:=I_{1}(u)+I_{2}(u),\end{split}

where

h_{\Omega}(x)=c_{N}\left(\int_{B_{1}(x)\setminus\Omega}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N}}\penalty\ dy-\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{1}(x)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N}}\penalty\ dy\right).

Notice that

\sigma_{t}\equiv\|(t^{\frac{1}{2}}u,(1-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}v)\|_{\ell^{2}}

where

denotes the

\mathbb{R}^{2}

\left|\|\xi\|_{\ell^{2}}-\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}\right|\leq\|\xi-\eta\|_{\ell^{2}},

and integrating on

(2.2)

Moreover, it is easy to see that

(2.3)

Finally, by combining (

&\hskip 18.49988pt+t\rho_{N}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(u_{\varepsilon}^{2}-u^{2})\penalty\ dx-t\rho_{N}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(v_{\varepsilon}^{2}-v^{2})\penalty\ dx\\

&\hskip 18.49988pt+tc_{N}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\left(v_{\varepsilon}(x)v_{\varepsilon}(y)-v(x)v(y)\right)j(x-y)\penalty\ dx\penalty\ dy\\
&\hskip 18.49988pt+t\rho_{N}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(u_{\varepsilon}^{2}-u^{2})\penalty\ dx-t\rho_{N}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(v_{\varepsilon}^{2}-v^{2})\penalty\ dx\\
\end{split}

(2.6)

By Lemma

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{L}(\sigma_{t}^{\varepsilon},\sigma_{t}^{\varepsilon})&-\mathcal{E}_{L}(v_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})=\Phi(t)-\Phi(0)\geq t\Phi^{\prime}(0)=t\mathcal{E}_{L}\left(v_{\varepsilon},\frac{u_{\varepsilon}^{2}-v_{\varepsilon}^{2}}{v_{\varepsilon}}\right)=t\mathcal{E}_{L}\left(v_{\varepsilon},w_{\varepsilon}\right)\\

(2.7)

Using the above convergence estimate in (

3

This section is devoted to the construction of a non-trivial curve in the spectrum
\Sigma_{L}
of
L_{\Delta}
. For this, we fix
r\in\mathbb{R}
and define

E_{r}(u)=\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u)-r\int_{\Omega}(u^{+})^{2}\penalty\ dx,\hskip 9.24994ptu\in\mathbb{H}(\Omega).

Let us consider the set

\mathcal{P}=\{u\in\mathbb{H}(\Omega):\penalty\ I(u)=\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\penalty\ dx=1\}

and define
\tilde{E_{r}}
as the
E_{r}
restricted to
\mathcal{P}
.
Since
E_{r}
is
C^{1}
functional in
\mathbb{H}(\Omega)
, by Lagrange multiplier rule, we say that
u\in\mathcal{P}
is a critical point of
\tilde{E}_{r}
if and only if there exists a

such that
E_{r}^{\prime}(u)=tI^{\prime}(u)

i.e.

\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,v)-r\int_{\Omega}u^{+}v\penalty\ dx=t\int_{\Omega}uv\penalty\ dx,\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all}\penalty\ v\in\mathbb{H}(\Omega).

On simplifying, this means that
u\in\mathcal{P}
solves
(P_{r+t,t})
in the weak sense
i.e.

\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,v)=(r+t)\int_{\Omega}u^{+}v\penalty\ dx-t\int_{\Omega}u^{-}v\penalty\ dx,\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all}\penalty\ v\in\mathbb{H}(\Omega)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt(r+t,t)\in\Sigma_{L}.

(3.1)

By taking
u=v
in (
3.1
), it is easy to observe that the Lagrange multiplier
t
is equal to the corresponding critical value
\tilde{E}_{r}(u)
. Hence we have the following result:

Lemma 3.1
.

For
r\in\mathbb{R}
,
(r+t,t)\in\Sigma_{L}
if and only if there exists a
u\in\mathcal{P}
which is a critical point of
\tilde{E}_{r}
satisfying
\tilde{E}_{r}(u)=t
.

Next, in order to identify the points in the spectrum
\Sigma_{L}
of
L_{\Delta}
, we are interested in finding the critical points of the constrained functional
\tilde{E}_{r}
, in view of Lemma
3.1
. A first critical point comes from the global minimization of the constrained functional
\tilde{E}_{r}
because for any
u\in\mathcal{P}
, we have

\tilde{E}_{r}(u)\geq\lambda_{1}^{L}\|u\|_{2}^{2}-r\|u^{+}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\lambda_{1}^{L}-r.

where
\varphi_{1}
denotes the first eigenfunction of
L_{\Delta}
corresponding to the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}^{L}
satisfying
\|\varphi_{1}\|_{2}=1

i.e.

\varphi_{1}\in\mathcal{P}
. From now on, we assume that
r\geq 0
which is not a restriction, since
\Sigma_{L}
is symmetric with respect to diagonal on
(\alpha,\beta)
plane. The following result concern the existence of the first critical point of
\tilde{E}_{r}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

\varphi_{1}
is the first critical point of
\tilde{E}_{r}
such that

(i)

\varphi_{1}
is a global minimum of
\tilde{E}_{r}
and

(ii)

\tilde{E}_{r}(\varphi_{1})=\lambda_{1}^{L}-r
.

Thus
(\lambda_{1}^{L},\lambda_{1}^{L}-r)\in\Sigma_{L}
lying on the vertical line through
(\lambda_{1}^{L},\lambda_{1}^{L}).

Proof.

It is easy to see that

\tilde{E}_{r}(\varphi_{1})=E_{r}(\varphi_{1})=\mathcal{E}_{L}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{1})-r=\lambda_{1}^{L}-r.

We recall that
\lambda_{1}^{L}=\inf\limits_{u\in\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u)
which further gives

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{r}(u)=\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u)-r\int_{\Omega}(u^{+})^{2}\penalty\ dx\geq\lambda_{1}^{L}-r=\tilde{E}_{r}(\varphi_{1})\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for any}\ u\in\mathcal{P},

since

. This finishes the proof while using Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Next, we establish the existence of a second critical point, which arises as a strict local minimum of the constrained functional
\tilde{E}_{r}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
u\in\mathbb{H}(\Omega)
,

\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u)\geq\mathcal{E}_{L}(u^{+},u^{+})+\mathcal{E}_{L}(u^{-},u^{-}).

Proof.

Note that, we have

\begin{split}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}&=((u^{+}-u^{-})(x)-(u^{+}-u^{-})(y))^{2}=((u^{+}(x)-u^{+}(y))-(u^{-}(x)-u^{-}(y)))^{2}\\
&=(u^{+}(x)-u^{+}(y))^{2}+(u^{-}(x)-u^{-}(y))^{2}-2(u^{+}(x)-u^{+}(y))(u^{-}(x)-u^{-}(y))\\
&=(u^{+}(x)-u^{+}(y))^{2}+(u^{-}(x)-u^{-}(y))^{2}+2u^{+}(x)u^{-}(y)+2u^{-}(x)u^{+}(y))\\
&\geq(u^{+}(x)-u^{+}(y))^{2}+(u^{-}(x)-u^{-}(y))^{2},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}u(x)u(y)&=(u^{+}-u^{-})(x)(u^{+}-u^{-})(y)\\
&=u^{+}(x)u^{+}(y)+u^{-}(x)u^{-}(y)-u^{-}(x)u^{+}(y)-u^{-}(y)u^{+}(x)\leq u^{+}(x)u^{+}(y)+u^{-}(x)u^{-}(y).\end{split}

Finally, by using the above inequalities relations in definition of

, we have the required claim.
∎

Lemma 3.4

Let

Denote

and assume on the contrary that

has a bounded subsequence. Now, by the definition of

,
[
17

and (

), there exists a

and for a further subsequence, we have

Since

Proposition 3.5

High-resolution mapping of soil total phosphorus in China: Revealing spatiotemporal patterns and driving mechanisms
Authors: Zhanpeng Liu, Qiu Tan, Xiaoyong Bai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Developed a 1 km resolution, layered spatiotemporal dataset of STP in China (2000-2020).•STP shows a spatial pattern of “central high, northern-southern low”, with a vertical decrease of 25.8%.•Phosphorus accumulates in the mid-temperate zone; loss hotspots are the middle-lower Yangtze region and Sichuan Basin.•SR and WS are key factors for plateau phosphorus enrichment and arid region phosphorus loss.Keywords: Soil total phosphorus, Random forest, Spatial differentiation, Driving factors, China

Introduction

Phosphorus, an essential limiting nutrient in terrestrial biogeochemical cycles, directly constrains primary productivity and ecosystem functions through its bioavailability [[1], [2], [3]]. Evidence indicates that the upper approximately 1 meter of the soil profile stores the majority of ecosystem-mobilizable phosphorus [4], where the quantity and specific forms of STP collectively govern the nutrient supply capacity for plants [5,6]. However, this critical foundation is under threat, as observations confirm that 15%−30% of global terrestrial ecosystems face an escalating risk of phosphorus limitation [7,8]. This widespread P limitation can trigger cascading effects by disrupting the stoichiometric balance of C-N-P coupling, thereby undermining ecosystem productivity and stability [9]. Consequently, accurately quantifying the spatiotemporal dynamics of STP and deciphering its controlling mechanisms at regional scales has become a fundamental prerequisite for diagnosing phosphorus constraints, improving nutrient management, and predicting ecosystem responses under global change [10,11].

To decipher these controlling mechanisms, it is essential to consider the origins and key drivers of STP. STP originates from the geochemical weathering of rock minerals and undergoes transformations that govern its entry into the terrestrial food web [12]. Its spatial distribution results from the synergistic interplay of parent material, bioclimatic conditions, and surface processes [13]. While parent material is acknowledged as the primary initial source of STP [14], contemporary observations underscore the increasingly prominent role of climate variability in reshaping STP patterns by regulating weathering, organic matter turnover, and hydrological transport [15]. Multi-scale investigations reveal STP distribution as a complex outcome of coupling among climate, vegetation, and soil. Globally, factors like soil organic carbon, parent material, and climate collectively explain STP variation [16]. Regionally, studies (e.g., in Chinese forests) confirm the strong association between STP and hydrothermal factors, highlighting pronounced vertical attenuation with depth [17]. Collectively, evidence points to a “climate-surface processes-phosphorus cycling” feedback framework [1]. However, a mechanistic understanding of this framework at regional scales remains limited, constrained by two key research gaps. First, most studies are confined to topsoil layers, leaving the responses of STP throughout the critical deep soil profile (>20 cm) poorly quantified [18]. Second, the prevailing focus on single-factor or linear associations fails to capture the synergistic and potentially non-linear effects of multiple interacting drivers (e.g., climate and human activities) on STP stocks. These gaps hinder the development of predictive models for soil phosphorus pool evolution under global change [19,20].

To address these gaps, this study focused on China by synthesizing 20,372 in-situ records from 33 ecological observation stations across four major ecosystems: croplands, grasslands, forests, and deserts. A national, stratified (0–20, 20–40, 40–60, 60–100 cm) STP spatiotemporal dataset was constructed. This research aims to clarify two central questions: (1) What are the three-dimensional (vertical and horizontal) spatiotemporal characteristics of STP across China? (2) What are the interactive driving mechanisms between STP dynamics and key factors, including climate and anthropogenic activities? By resolving these questions, this study seeks to elucidate the impact of multi-factor coupling on the soil phosphorus cycle. The outcomes will provide a scientific basis for improving terrestrial ecosystem models and optimizing nutrient management strategies, thereby enhancing the capacity to predict soil phosphorus pool evolution and maintain ecosystem productivity under global change.

Data and methods

Data sources and preprocessing

Core observations and auxiliary data

The STP observations used in this study were obtained from the National Ecosystem Science Data Center (https://www.nesdc.org.cn). The dataset comprises long-term, site-based measurements collected from 33 national ecological field stations across China, covering four major ecosystem types: cropland, grassland, forest, and desert. The observation period spans from 2000 to 2020, yielding a total of 20,372 standardized soil profile records. Detailed information on sampling sites is provided in Table S1. To characterize the environmental controls on the spatial variability of STP, a comprehensive set of auxiliary variables was compiled from multiple data sources (Table S2). Climatic variables, including annual mean precipitation (Pre) and annual mean Tmp, were obtained from the National Tibetan Plateau Data Center (https://data.tpdc.ac.cn) at a spatial resolution of 1 km. Monthly WS and SR were derived from the TerraClimate dataset [21] with a spatial resolution of 4 km. Topographic and soil property variables—including elevation (DEM), slope, soil organic carbon (SOC), soil pH, bulk density (Bd), sand (Btsand), silt (Btslt), and clay (Btcly) contents, as well as soil layer thickness—were obtained from the National Earth System Science Data Center (http://www.geodata.cn), with an original spatial resolution of 1 km. Vegetation conditions were represented by the normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI), derived from NOAA global monthly products [22] at a spatial resolution of 1/12°. Monthly soil moisture (SW) and soil temperature (ST) data were sourced from the Copernicus Climate Change Service [23], with a spatial resolution of 9 km. Land use and land cover information was obtained from the China Land Cover Dataset (CLCD) at a spatial resolution of 30 m [24]. Anthropogenic influences were characterized using the long-term global high-resolution human footprint dataset (https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.16571064), provided at a spatial resolution of 1 km. Climatic regionalization and agricultural zoning maps of China were acquired from the Resource and Environment Science and Data Center, Chinese Academy of Sciences (https://www.resdc.cn/). Climatic zones were represented using Roman numerals and letters (I: cold temperate; II: mid-temperate; III: warm temperate; IV: northern subtropical; V: middle subtropical; VI: southern subtropical; VII: marginal tropical; VIII: middle tropical; H: plateau climate zone). Agricultural regions were denoted by capital letters (A: Northeast China Plain; B: northern arid and semi-arid region; C: Huang–Huai–Hai Plain; D: Loess Plateau; E: Qinghai–Tibet Plateau; F: middle and lower reaches of the Yangtze River; G: Sichuan Basin and surrounding areas; H: South China; I: Yunnan–Guizhou Plateau). To ensure consistency in spatial and temporal scales, all datasets were harmonized to a common period of 2000–2020 and resampled to a spatial resolution of 1 km prior to analysis.

Site data representativeness and quality control

The spatiotemporal STP dataset constructed in this study is based on 20,372 standardized records collected from 251 long-term monitoring sites distributed across four major ecosystem types in China, including forests, grasslands, croplands, and deserts (Fig. S1). These sites span the major climatic zones of China, encompassing the cold temperate, warm temperate, northern subtropical, middle subtropical, southern subtropical, and plateau climate regions. Spatially, monitoring sites are relatively evenly distributed within the eastern monsoon region, whereas on the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, they are mainly concentrated along the southeastern margins, where hydrothermal conditions are more favorable. In contrast, the high-altitude and arid core regions of northwestern China remain sparsely sampled. From an administrative perspective, the monitoring sites cover most provinces across China; however, site density is markedly lower in western regions such as Xinjiang, Tibet, and Qinghai. To mitigate potential biases arising from this spatially uneven distribution, high-resolution environmental covariates—including climatic, topographic, and remotely sensed variables—were incorporated into the modeling framework to compensate for limited observational coverage. In addition, spatial block cross-validation was employed to provide an objective assessment of model generalization performance in unsampled regions. Quality control procedures were applied to ensure data reliability and internal consistency. Abnormal records were identified and removed based on established pedological knowledge and statistical criteria. The vertical consistency of phosphorus concentrations within soil profiles was examined to eliminate logically inconsistent observations. All measurements were subsequently harmonized to four standard soil depth intervals (0–20 cm, 20–40 cm, 40–60 cm, and 60–100 cm) using depth interpolation, resulting in a structurally consistent dataset suitable for national-scale spatiotemporal analysis.

Methods

Method for simulating spatiotemporal variations of soil total phosphorus in China

Random forest model

Random forest is an ensemble machine learning algorithm based on decision trees, widely applied for its superior performance in handling high-dimensional nonlinear problems and its robustness against overfitting. In this study, to spatially predict STP as a continuous variable, a regression model was constructed using the randomForest package in R. In regression tasks, RF generates the final output by building a large number of regression trees and averaging their predictions [25]. In this study, by integrating multi‑source environmental factor data, we extrapolated the STP measurements from 251 discrete monitoring sites to generate a spatially continuous distribution across China.

Parameter setting and variable importance assessment

To ensure model stability and reproducibility, key parameters of the random forest were set according to general guidelines: the number of trees (ntree) was set to 1000 to allow sufficient model training; the number of variables tried at each split (mtry) followed the default rule for regression (mtry = p/3, with p = 15 predictor variables), resulting in an actual value of 5; and the minimum node size was kept at the default value of 5 to prevent overfitting. To verify the robustness of the model to random initialization, multiple random seeds (including 13, 14, 15, 16) were tested. The variation range of R2 from 10-fold cross‑validation indicated stable model performance. Based on the test results, a fixed random seed of 14 was adopted in the final modeling to ensure reproducibility.

Variable importance was evaluated using the permutation‑based importance method built into the RF package, measured by the percentage increase in mean squared error (%IncMSE). As shown in Fig. S2, across the four soil layers (0–100 cm), soil depth (15.31%–19.31%), clay content (12.40%–17.67%), and slope (16.23%–18.75%) all exhibited %IncMSE values greater than 15%, indicating that these factors are key environmental drivers influencing the spatial variation of soil total phosphorus.

Accuracy evaluation methods

To mitigate the risk of overfitting in the Random Forest model, this study employed a cross-validation (CV) approach (Fig. S3). Specifically, 10-fold cross-validation, as widely recommended in the literature, was adopted [26]. Model accuracy was rigorously assessed using three key performance metrics: the correlation coefficient (R), root mean square error (RMSE), and mean absolute error (MAE). The reported final results represent the average accuracy values obtained across all folds of the cross-validation.

Furthermore, we evaluated the spatial efficacy of the generated grids by introducing the SPAtial Efficiency metric (SPAEF). SPAEF is primarily utilized to compare the spatial pattern consistency between observed and simulated variables and has been effectively applied in various distributed hydrological modeling studies [27,28].

Results

Three-dimensional spatiotemporal evolution of soil phosphorus pool

This study constructed a high spatiotemporal resolution dataset of STP in China from 2000 to 2020, systematically analyzing its spatiotemporal pattern characteristics (Fig. 1). At the national scale, STP exhibited a pronounced spatial pattern characterized by higher concentrations in central China and lower values toward both northern and southern regions. The mean STP content for the 0–100 cm soil profile was estimated at 0.512 g/kg. According to established soil nutrient classification standards (Table S3), subtropical South China consistently exhibited STP concentrations below 0.4 g/kg, identifying this region as a widespread phosphorus-deficient zone. Along the vertical dimension, STP showed a significant decreasing gradient with soil depth (P < 0.05). Mean concentrations declined from 0.625 g/kg in the surface layer (0–20 cm) to 0.464 g/kg in the deep layer (60–100 cm), corresponding to an overall reduction of 25.8%. Temporally, STP within the 0–100 cm profile displayed a fluctuating but overall increasing trend over the study period (P < 0.05). This trajectory can be divided into three distinct phases: a gradual accumulation phase from 2000 to 2013 (mean annual increase of +0.32 mg/kg), a rapid depletion phase from 2014 to 2016 (mean annual decrease of −2.2 mg/kg), and a recovery phase from 2017 to 2020 (mean annual increase of +0.34 mg/kg). During this period, STP reached a peak in 2013 (0.517 g/kg) and declined to a minimum in 2016 (0.508 g/kg). Depth-resolved analysis revealed pronounced differences in the temporal responses of STP across soil layers. The surface layer (0–20 cm) exhibited the highest sensitivity to temporal variation, with an anomalously high value observed in 2019 (0.634 g/kg). Intermediate layers (20–60 cm) experienced the largest relative declines during the depletion phase, with the 40–60 cm layer showing a reduction of up to 2.1%, and displayed a delayed response compared to the surface layer. In contrast, the deep layer (60–100 cm) remained comparatively stable throughout the study period, maintaining a slow but persistent increasing trend (mean annual increase of +0.18 mg/kg).

Identification of sensitive regions of STP change

To identify regions sensitive to changes in STP, areal proportions of increasing and decreasing STP trends were quantified for each climatic zone in China (Fig. 2). The results reveal pronounced regional and vertical heterogeneity in both the direction and magnitude of STP change. The mid-temperate zone (II) emerged as the core region of sustained STP increases, with over 61% of its area across all soil layers showing an upward trend. The increase was most pronounced in the surface layer (0–20 cm; 61.3%, ∼1.84 × 106 km2) and remained consistently high in the deep layer (60–100 cm; 63.1%, ∼1.91 × 106 km2), indicating a consistent accumulation of phosphorus throughout the soil profile. In contrast, the middle subtropical zone (V) was a prominent region of continuous STP depletion. The proportion of area experiencing a decrease approached or exceeded 60% in all layers, rising from 58.8% at 0–20 cm to 66.1% at 40–60 cm (∼7.97 × 105 km2), which reflects a pervasive loss of phosphorus. In the northern subtropical zone (IV), the STP decreasing trend intensified with depth, with the affected area expanding from 55.3% (0–20 cm) to 69.5% (60–100 cm). The warm temperate zone (III) exhibited a depth-dependent reversal: STP increases dominated the surface layer (72.3%), whereas decreases became dominant in the deep layer (59.7%). The cold temperate zone (I) was consistently dominated by STP increases, with 75.9%–79.9% of its area showing positive trends. The plateau climate zone (H) displayed a relatively balanced pattern, with proportions of increase and decrease both close to 50% across all depths. The remaining climatic zones (southern subtropical VI, marginal tropical VII, and middle tropical VIII) did not exhibit a clear, unidirectional trend.

Further analysis based on agricultural regionalization revealed similarly distinct spatial patterns. The Northeast China Plain (A) was the most significant area of STP accumulation. The proportion of increase rose consistently with depth, from 67.2% in the surface layer to 85.6% in the deep layer (∼7.81 × 105 km2). The Northern Arid and Semi-arid Region (B) was dominated by surface-layer increases (60.7%, ∼2.12 × 106 km2), a trend which remained stable at depth (56.3%). Conversely, the Huang-Huai-Hai Plain (C), while showing a strong surface-layer increase (82.5%, ∼4.45 × 105 km2), transitioned to a pattern of slight decrease in deeper layers, reaching 51.2% decrease at 40–60 cm depth (∼2.77 × 105 km2). The Loess Plateau (D) was consistently dominated by STP increases across all layers (64.0−80.6%; ∼2.32–2.93 × 105 km2). By contrast, the Middle-Lower Yangtze Plain (F) and the Sichuan Basin region (G) were the most pronounced STP depletion zones. The latter showed 66.1% decrease in the surface layer (∼3.52 × 105 km2), while in the former, the decreasing proportion expanded to 73.0% in the deep layer (∼6.21 × 105 km2). South China (H) also exhibited decreases across all layers, ranging from 42.8% to 61.1%. The Qinghai-Tibet Plateau (E) and the Yunnan-Guizhou Plateau (I) showed no dominant trend, with comparable proportions of increase and decrease across all depths.

Multi-factor synergistic driving effects

To quantify the synergistic influences of multiple factors on the spatial patterns of STP, seven biophysical variables—Pre, SW, Tmp, ST, SR, WS, and HFP—were selected for partial correlation analysis. This approach enabled the isolation of individual factor-STP relationships while controlling for the effects of other variables. The partial correlation coefficients between these factors and STP across different soil layers, together with their vertical variations, are presented in Fig. 3. Among all examined factors, SR exhibited the strongest and most consistent positive association with STP across soil layers, with mean explanatory contributions ranging from +13.1% to +18.4%. Regions with positive correlations were primarily distributed from the northern margins of the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau to the southwestern Mongolian Plateau. SW also showed a stable positive contribution, with its explanatory strength increasing with depth—from +5% in the 0–20 cm layer to +10.3% in the 60–100 cm layer. Positive correlation areas were mainly concentrated in North China. ST displayed a depth-dependent pattern, with positive contributions observed in shallow layers (0–40 cm; +1.4% to +11.8%). These positive associations were primarily located in the arid-semi-arid transition zone extending from the southern margin of the Junggar Basin to the Hetao Plain. However, the influence of ST weakened substantially in deeper layers. In contrast, Pre and HFP showed relatively weak overall contributions, with mean explanatory values generally below +6.5% across all soil layers. WS consistently exhibited a negative association with STP across all depths and represented the most pronounced inhibitory factor. The strongest negative effect was observed in the surface layer (0–20 cm), with a mean explanatory contribution of −12.3%. Regions with strong negative correlations were particularly evident in Xinjiang and Tibet. The influence of Tmp demonstrated a clear depth dependency. Tmp showed a weak positive contribution in the surface layer (+3.1%), but its explanatory strength declined with depth and shifted to a weak negative contribution (−1%) in the 60–100 cm layer, forming a distinct bimodal vertical pattern.

Identification of primary control regions for STP variations under multi-factor regulation

To identify regions where STP dynamics are predominantly controlled by specific drivers, the primary contribution of seven biophysical factors was quantified at the grid scale, along with their vertical differentiation across soil layers (Fig. 4). The results reveal systematic variations in both the spatial extent and dominance intensity of individual factors with soil depth. In the surface layer (0–20 cm), SR was the most extensive positive driver, with regions where it acted as the dominant positive factor accounting for 29.1% of the total area (∼2.65 × 106 km2). These regions spatially corresponded to the ecological transition zone along the eastern margin of the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau. In contrast, WS was the most influential negative driver, dominating 32.7% of the study area (∼2.98 × 106 km2), primarily across arid regions from the Tarim Basin to the Mongolian Plateau. SW also showed substantial positive dominance over 18.7% of the area (∼1.7 × 106 km2), mainly concentrated in the North China Plain and central Inner Mongolia. With increasing soil depth, the dominant effects of SR and WS weakened considerably. In the deep layer (60–100 cm), the area dominated positively by SR decreased to 16.3% (∼1.5 × 106 km2), while the area dominated negatively by WS decreased to 21.2% (∼1.94 × 106 km2). Conversely, the influence of Tmp increased markedly with depth and exhibited a distinct bimodal spatial pattern. The proportion of area where Tmp was the dominant positive driver increased from 11.1% in the surface layer (∼1.01 × 106 km2) to 25.6% in the deep layer (∼2.35 × 106 km2), forming a continuous belt from the northern foothills of the Tianshan Mountains to the southern valleys of Tibet. Simultaneously, the area where it acted as the dominant negative driver increased from 10.0% to 27.9% (∼2.55 × 106 km2), primarily covering arid deserts from the Junggar Basin to the Alxa Plateau. The dominant role of ST showed a reversal with depth. In the surface layer, ST primarily acted as a positive driver (12.6%, ∼1.15 × 106 km2), whereas in the 20–40 cm layer, its role shifted to that of a dominant negative driver (12.1%, ∼1.11 × 106 km2). In comparison, both Pre and the HFP consistently showed limited spatial dominance across all soil layers, with their respective area proportions generally below 10%, indicating substantially weaker controlling effects compared to the other factors.

Discussion

Driving effects of environmental factors on the spatiotemporal variation of STP

The spatiotemporal patterns of STP in China result from the synergistic effects of multiple environmental factors acting through distinct biogeochemical pathways. This study identifies SR as the strongest positive driver across all soil layers, particularly in arid and semi-arid regions. This suggests that photochemical processes may activate and release mineral-bound phosphorus, representing a key abiotic pathway for phosphorus mobilization in these environments [34]. WS was the most influential negative driver, and its spatial dominance aligns closely with major wind erosion zones. This correlation indicates that aeolian processes not only remove particulate phosphorus but can also alter surface soil texture, thereby affecting phosphorus forms and stability [35]. The consistent negative correlation with STP across depths underscores wind erosion as a critical constraint on phosphorus retention, especially in sparsely vegetated areas. Tmp exhibited a distinct bimodal regulatory pattern with depth, shifting from a positive effect in surface layers to a negative one at greater depths. This reflects a functional differentiation in phosphorus cycling: higher temperatures in surface soils likely stimulate microbial activity and organic phosphorus mineralization [36], whereas in deeper layers, they may promote the formation of secondary minerals that immobilize phosphorus, reducing its availability [37]. The coupled effects of SW and ST further highlight the fundamental role of hydrothermal conditions in governing phosphorus transformation and transport. In transitional and agricultural zones, their interaction regulates both phosphorus bioavailability and its vertical redistribution within the profile [38], emphasizing the importance of water–heat coupling in regional phosphorus dynamics. In contrast, Pre and the HFP showed relatively weak direct explanatory power. This suggests their influence is exerted primarily through higher-order modulation of key processes—such as by altering soil moisture regimes, structure, and external inputs—thereby indirectly shaping the intensity and direction of dominant phosphorus cycling pathways without acting as primary drivers [39].

Collectively, the spatial pattern of STP across China can be understood as a system-level response characterized by process dominance, vertical differentiation, and multi-factor coupling. This process-based perspective provides a mechanistic framework for interpreting and predicting regional phosphorus cycling under ongoing climate change and anthropogenic pressures.

Response of STP to different land use types

Between 2000 and 2020, STP content exhibited stable yet significant differentiation in response to different land use types (Fig. 5). This spatial heterogeneity stems from the synergistic regulation of regional climatic background and human activities, which collectively shape three dominant phosphorus cycling mechanisms: physico-chemical sequestration-dominated, biological cycling-dominated, and anthropogenic input-output balance-dominated. Specifically, the ice-covered area (So) and wetlands (We) exhibited the highest STP levels (mean values of approximately 0.7 g/kg and 0.6 g/kg, respectively). These correspond to the inhibition of weathering and phosphorus migration by freezing action [40] and the adsorption-fixation mechanism promoted by periodic anaerobic conditions [41], respectively. Both systems are dominated by physicochemical sequestration. In contrast, forestland (Fo) and shrubland (Sh) showed lower mean STP values (0.49 g/kg and 0.58 g/kg, respectively), reflecting biocycle-dominated characteristics. This involves the efficient uptake and retention of phosphorus in plant biomass [42] or the establishment of a dynamic balance with surface erosion processes [43]. Bare land (Ba) had the lowest mean STP (0.48 g/kg), highlighting the predominance of physical erosion-driven phosphorus export under vegetation-deficient conditions [44]. Comparatively, the mean STP in cropland (Cr) was at a moderate level (approximately 0.5 g/kg) and exceptionally stable. This reflects the reshaping of the natural phosphorus cycle by intensive human management (exogenous fertilizer input), maintaining a dynamic balance between phosphorus input and losses through crop uptake and runoff [45,46]. The STP dynamics in grassland (Gr) (mean value ∼0.52 g/kg) intertwine influences from the biological cycle and human disturbance [47], while the mean STP values for water bodies (Wa) and impervious surfaces (Im) primarily indicate the contribution of exogenous pollution inputs, such as surface runoff [48,49]. On a temporal scale, despite fluctuations in land use and climatic conditions, the interannual variability of STP across all types was minimal, and the regional overall mean remained stable between 0.5 and 0.6 g/kg. This suggests that the key processes driving phosphorus cycling in the various ecosystems did not undergo systematic transformation during the study period, demonstrating a significant capacity for steady-state maintenance.

In summary, different land use types shape distinct phosphorus cycling mechanisms by altering surface conditions and modifying the pathways through which environmental drivers operate. By integrating spatial heterogeneity with long-term dynamics, this study elucidates the multi-pathway regulation and steady-state maintenance capacity of phosphorus cycling within the regional “climate-human-soil” coupled system.

Validation of model accuracy

The random forest model constructed using nationwide STP observation data exhibited robust predictive performance across all soil layers. The model demonstrated strong explanatory power (R2 = 0.828–0.917; Table S5), with errors remaining at low levels following 10-fold cross-validation (RMSE = 0.1–0.14 g/kg, MAE = 0.08–0.1 g/kg). Histogram matching test values (0.74–0.89) indicated that the model effectively captured the distributional characteristics of the data, while the spatial efficiency metric (SPAEF = 0.486–0.715) further confirmed its potential for spatial prediction (Fig. S4). Residual analysis (Fig. S3) showed that the mean residuals from each cross-validation fold were close to zero, indicating no significant systematic bias in the model.

To evaluate the regional reliability of the retrieval results, we compared them with findings from related studies (Table 1). Compared to the China-wide STP results retrieved by Liu et al. [50] using machine learning (0–15 cm: 0.549 g/kg; 15–30 cm: 0.501 g/kg; 30–60 cm: 0.445 g/kg; 60–100 cm: 0.423 g/kg), the average STP values obtained in this study for the period 2000–2020 across soil layers showed a highly consistent vertical decreasing trend, with differences in magnitude falling within a reasonable range. Compared to the field-measured results for forest ecosystems reported by Zhu et al. [17], the deviation of the regional-scale mean values in this study was <8%, and the spatial distribution patterns were similar. These comparisons consistently support the plausibility of our model’s retrieval results. Our findings reflect the general pattern of STP, and uncertainties may arise from local environmental variations.

Comparison with other studies.

Variable importance analysis indicated that the spatial differentiation of STP is primarily co-regulated by NDVI, soil pH, and silt content. NDVI reflects vegetation growth status and directly influences soil nutrient cycling and accumulation [51]. pH determines the chemical forms and bioavailability of phosphorus [52]. Silt content is generally associated with favorable soil structure and can enhance nutrient retention capacity [53]. Although the influence of other factors was relatively minor, soil bulk density and elevation still exerted significant effects on STP concentration. Focusing on these key environmental factors not only improved model prediction accuracy but also enhanced its explanatory power regarding the mechanisms of soil phosphorus cycling.

Limitations and future perspectives

This study constructed a 1 km resolution spatiotemporal dataset of STP for China (2000–2020), yet several limitations warrant further consideration. First, the predictive accuracy of the model is constrained by the uneven spatial distribution of existing monitoring sites, particularly the scarcity of stations in remote western regions, which forces the model to rely on extrapolation in data-sparse areas and may introduce systematic bias. Second, constrained by the 1 km spatial resolution, the model cannot finely capture phosphorus transport and redistribution processes at micro‑scales, such as within individual cropland plots or along slopes.

Future research should integrate higher-resolution spatial and spectral remote sensing data to improve validation accuracy and combine multi-dimensional drivers—including climate, human activities, and policy factors—to develop dynamic predictive models. Such advances would enable a more systematic understanding of the complex adaptive mechanisms governing soil phosphorus cycling and provide more precise scientific support for sustainable phosphorus management and the establishment of ecological security barriers in China.

Conclusion

This study integrated multi-source observational data across China with a random forest model to systematically reveal the spatiotemporal patterns and driving mechanisms of STP from 2000 to 2020. The main findings are threefold. First, a 1 km resolution, layered national-scale STP dataset was constructed, revealing a distinct “central high, northern–southern low” spatial gradient, a significant vertical decrease in STP with depth, and key regional patterns including the contiguous low-phosphorus region in South China, the phosphorus accumulation core in the mid-temperate zone, and the phosphorus loss hotspots in the middle-lower Yangtze River region and the Sichuan Basin. Second, the study elucidated the synergistic drivers, identifying SR and WS as the dominant factors controlling phosphorus enrichment in plateaus and phosphorus loss in arid regions, respectively, while Tmp exhibited a unique bimodal vertical regulatory pattern. Furthermore, a multi-scale coupled analytical framework was developed, which integrates climatic drivers, land use, and human activities to parse the dominant processes and key limiting factors of the phosphorus cycle across ecological zones, thereby providing a crucial mechanistic foundation for improving phosphorus cycle parameterization in terrestrial ecosystem models.

Based on these insights, the study offers direct scientific support for regionally differentiated phosphorus management. For instance, in the low-phosphorus region of South China, emphasis should be placed on synergistic enhancement and targeted supplementation of phosphorus; in the high-intensity agricultural zones of the middle-lower Yangtze River, efforts should focus on optimizing fertilization and controlling non-point source losses; and in Northeast China and the northern arid regions, protective utilization aligned with natural accumulation trends is recommended. The multi-scale framework also provides an operable tool and strategic reference for systematically assessing and balancing “phosphorus security–food security–ecological security” at the national and watershed levels.

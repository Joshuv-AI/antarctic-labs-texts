On the Orlov conjecture for hyper-Kähler varieties via hyperholomorphic bundles
Davesh Maulik, Junliang Shen, Qizheng Yin

Abstract
. We study Fourier transforms induced by Markman’s projectively hyperholomorphic bundles on products of hyper-Kähler varieties of K ​ 3 [ n ] K3^{[n]} -type. As applications, we prove the following. (a) Derived equivalent hyper-Kähler varieties of K ​ 3 [ n ] K3^{[n]} -type have isomorphic homological motives preserving the cup-product. (b) All smooth projective moduli spaces of stable sheaves on a given K ​ 3 K3 surface have isomorphic homological motives preserving the cup-product. (c) Assuming the Franchetta properties for the self-products of polarized K ​ 3 K3 surfaces, the isomorphisms in (b) can be lifted to Chow motives for K ​ 3 K3 surfaces of Picard rank 1 1 . These results provide evidence for the Orlov conjecture and a conjecture of Fu–Vial.

0.3.
Idea of the proof

We first discuss the main difficulty in proving the Orlov conjecture. Assume that
X,X^{\prime}
are derived equivalent varieties of dimension
d
. The Chern characters of the Fourier–Mukai kernels
{\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{U}}^{-1}
yield correspondences

(2)

\mathfrak{F}\in\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X\times X^{\prime}),\quad\mathfrak{F}^{-1}\in\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X^{\prime}\times X)

satisfying

\mathfrak{F}^{-1}\circ\mathfrak{F}=[\Delta_{X}],\quad\mathfrak{F}\circ\mathfrak{F}^{-1}=[\Delta_{X^{\prime}}].

The classes (
2
) are
a priori
of
mixed
degree in the Chow groups. In order to prove (the homological or the Chow version of) the Orlov conjecture, we need to construct algebraic cycles of
pure
degree

(3)

[Z]\in\mathrm{CH}^{d}(X\times X^{\prime}),\quad[Z^{\prime}]\in\mathrm{CH}^{d}(X^{\prime}\times X)

with

(4)

[Z^{\prime}]\circ[Z]=[\Delta_{X}],\quad[Z]\circ[Z^{\prime}]=[\Delta_{X^{\prime}}].

Here the identities (
4
) hold in cohomology or Chow, depending on the version of the Orlov conjecture one would like to prove. In general, it is not clear how to modify the cycles in (
2
) of mixed degree to the desired
d
-dimensional cycles in (
3
).

Now we consider hyper-Kähler varieties of
K3^{[n]}
-type. We first observe that derived equivalent varieties
X,X^{\prime}
of
K3^{[n]}
-type are connected Hodge-theoretically; see Proposition
2.1
. Therefore, both Theorems
0.4
and
0.7
are deduced from the following theorem.

Theorem 0.9
.

Let
X,X^{\prime}
be hyper-Kähler varieties of
K3^{[n]}
-type. If there is a (rational) Hodge isometry
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq H^{2}(X^{\prime},{\mathbb{Q}})
, then there is an isomorphism of homological motives which respects the cup-product,

(h_{\mathrm{hom}}(X),\cup)\simeq(h_{\mathrm{hom}}(X^{\prime}),\cup).

The proofs of the main theorems rely on a Fourier transform package discussed in Section
1
for hyper-Kähler varieties of
K3^{[n]}
-type, which in cohomology is largely a reformulation of results of Markman
[
24
]
. We prove Theorems
0.4
,
0.7
, and
0.9
in Section
2
.

In Section
4
, we show that the isomorphism in Theorem
0.7
can be constructed from generically defined cycles when the
K3
surface is of Picard rank
1
, thereby proving Theorem
0.8
. The key is an algebraic moduli approach to a recent construction of Zhang
[
36
]
regarding moduli spaces of stable sheaves, which we discuss in Section

1.
Fourier transforms via hyperholomorphic bundles

Conjectures of Beauville and Voisin
[
4
,
35
]
predict that the Chow motive of a hyper-Kähler variety behaves analogously to that of an abelian variety. The purpose of Section
1
is to make this analogy more explicit from the perspective of
Fourier transforms
. Although most of the discussion in this section is conjectural, the construction introduced here plays a crucial role in the proofs of the main theorems.

1.1.
Abelian varieties

We first briefly review the theory of Fourier transforms in the study of algebraic cycles on abelian varieties
[
3
,
9
]
.

Let
A
be a
g
-dimensional abelian variety, and let
A^{\vee}:=\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(A)
be its dual. The normalized Poincaré line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
on
A^{\vee}\times A
induces a derived equivalence of coherent categories
[
28
]
, which further induces algebraic correspondences, known as the Fourier transforms

\mathfrak{F}:=\mathrm{ch}({\mathcal{L}})\in\mathrm{CH}^{*}(A^{\vee}\times A),\quad\mathfrak{F}^{-1}:=\mathrm{ch}({\mathcal{L}}^{\vee})\in\mathrm{CH}^{*}(A\times A^{\vee}).

We set

\mathfrak{p}_{k}:=\mathfrak{F}_{k}\circ\mathfrak{F}^{-1}_{2g-k}\in\mathrm{CH}^{g}(A\times A),\quad\mathfrak{p}^{\vee}_{k}:=\mathfrak{F}^{-1}_{k}\circ\mathfrak{F}_{2g-k}\in\mathrm{CH}^{g}(A^{\vee}\times A^{\vee}),\quad 0\leq k\leq 2g,

where
\mathfrak{F}_{i}\in\mathrm{CH}^{i}(A^{\vee}\times A)
and
\mathfrak{F}^{-1}_{j}\in\mathrm{CH}^{j}(A\times A^{\vee})
are the degree
i
and degree
j
parts of
\mathfrak{F}
and
\mathfrak{F}^{-1}
respectively. Since
\mathfrak{F},\mathfrak{F}^{-1}
are obtained from Fourier–Mukai equivalences, we obtain decompositions of the diagonal classes

(5)

[\Delta_{A}]=\sum_{k=0}^{2g}\mathfrak{p}_{k}\in\mathrm{CH}^{g}(A\times A),\quad[\Delta_{A^{\vee}}]=\sum_{k=0}^{2g}\mathfrak{p}^{\vee}_{k}\in\mathrm{CH}^{g}(A^{\vee}\times A^{\vee}).

A key observation of
[
3
,
9
]
is that the decompositions (
5
) yield
Fourier-stable multiplicative
Chow–Künneth decompositions

(6)

\displaystyle h(A)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{2g}h_{k}(A),\quad h_{k}(A)=(A,\mathfrak{p}_{k},0),

(7)

\displaystyle\quad h(A^{\vee})

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{2g}h_{k}(A^{\vee}),\quad h_{k}(A^{\vee})=(A^{\vee},\mathfrak{p}^{\vee}_{k},0).

More precisely, the decompositions (
6
) and (
7
) respect the cup-product. Furthermore, in the category of Chow motives, the graded algebra object

\bigoplus_{k=0}^{2g}h_{k}(A),\quad\cup:h_{i}(A)\times h_{j}(A)\to h_{i+j}(A)

given by the cup-product is isomorphic through the Fourier transform
\mathfrak{F}
to the graded algebra object (with appropriate Tate-twists)

\bigoplus_{k=0}^{2g}h_{2g-k}(A^{\vee})(g-k),\quad\ast:h_{2g-i}(A^{\vee})(g-i)\times h_{2g-j}(A^{\vee})(g-j)\to h_{2g-i-j}(A^{\vee})(g-i-j)

given by the convolution product induced by the abelian group structure
+:A^{\vee}\times A^{\vee}\to A^{\vee}
.

This package relies heavily on special geometric ingredients of abelian varieties, including the normalized Poincaré line bundle and the group structure.

1.2.
Hyper-Kähler varieties

A Fourier transform package similar to the one in Section
1.1
for abelian varieties is expected to hold for hyper-Kähler varieties. We provide here an explicit proposal for varieties of
K3^{[n]}
-type. Our proposal is built on the recently constructed projectively hyperholomorphic bundles of Markman
[
24
]
which we briefly review as follows; see also
[
27
, Section 1]
.

Consider a projective
K3
surface
S_{0}
with
\mathrm{Pic}(S_{0})={\mathbb{Z}}H
. Assume that the Mukai vector

v_{0}:=(r,mH,s)\in\widetilde{H}(S_{0},{\mathbb{Z}}),\quad\mathrm{gcd}(r,s)=1

is isotropic (
i.e.

v_{0}^{2}=0
), and that all
H
-stable sheaves on
S_{0}
with Mukai vector
v_{0}
are stable vector bundles. Let
M_{S_{0},v_{0}}
be the moduli space of such stable vector bundles. Then
M_{S_{0},v_{0}}
is again a
K3
surface with a universal rank
r
bundle
{\mathcal{U}}
on
M_{S_{0},v_{0}}\times S_{0}
. Conjugating the Bridgeland–King–Reid (BKR) correspondence
[
7
]
yields a projectively hyperholomorphic vector bundle
{\mathcal{U}}^{[n]}
on
M_{S_{0},v_{0}}^{[n]}\times S_{0}^{[n]}
of rank

\mathrm{rk}({\mathcal{U}}^{[n]})=n!r^{n},

which induces a derived equivalence

\Phi_{{\mathcal{U}}^{[n]}}:D^{b}(M_{S_{0},v_{0}}^{[n]})\xrightarrow{\simeq}D^{b}(S_{0}^{[n]}).

Now let
X
be any hyper-Kähler variety of
K3^{[n]}
-type. There always exists a variety
Y
of
K3^{[n]}
-type and a twisted vector bundle
({\mathcal{E}},\alpha_{\mathcal{E}})
on
Y\times X
, such that this twisted vector bundle is deformed from
{\mathcal{U}}^{[n]}
and induces a (twisted) derived equivalence

(8)

\Phi_{({\mathcal{E}},\alpha_{\mathcal{E}})}:D^{b}(Y,\alpha_{Y})\xrightarrow{\simeq}D^{b}(X,\alpha_{X})

with
\alpha_{X},\alpha_{Y}
certain Brauer classes on
X,Y
respectively. We denote by
({\mathcal{E}}^{-1},\alpha_{{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}})
the (shifted) twisted vector bundle given by the kernel of the inverse of (
8
). Note that even for fixed
X
,
S_{0}
, and
v_{0}
(which determines the
K3
surface
M_{S_{0},v_{0}}
uniquely), the choice of

({\mathcal{E}},\alpha_{\mathcal{E}})
may not be unique.

We consider the

(see
[
23
, Section 2]
or
[
24

) associated with the twisted vector bundle
({\mathcal{E}},\alpha_{\mathcal{E}})
. For the twisted vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
, the vector bundle

{\mathcal{E}}^{\otimes\mathrm{rank}({\mathcal{E}})}\otimes\mathrm{det}({\mathcal{E}})^{-1}

is untwisted, and the kappa class

\kappa({\mathcal{E}})\in\mathrm{CH}^{*}(Y\times X)

is defined to be the
\mathrm{rank}({\mathcal{E}})
-th root of the Chern character of
{\mathcal{E}}^{\otimes\mathrm{rank}({\mathcal{E}})}\otimes\mathrm{det}({\mathcal{E}})^{-1}
. We define
\kappa({\mathcal{E}}^{-1})
similarly; they yield Fourier transforms

\mathfrak{F}:=\kappa({\mathcal{E}})\sqrt{\mathrm{td}_{Y\times X}}\in\mathrm{CH}^{*}(Y\times X),\quad\mathfrak{F}^{-1}:=\kappa({\mathcal{E}}^{-1})\sqrt{\mathrm{td}_{X\times Y}}\in\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X\times Y).

Since
({\mathcal{E}}^{-1},\alpha_{{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}})
is the kernel for the Fourier–Mukai inverse of (
8
), the relations

[\Delta_{X}]=\mathfrak{F}\circ\mathfrak{F}^{-1}\in\mathrm{CH}^{2n}(X\times X),\quad[\Delta_{Y}]=\mathfrak{F}^{-1}\circ\mathfrak{F}\in\mathrm{CH}^{2n}(Y\times Y)

are obtained from the Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem. In other words, the Fourier transforms
\mathfrak{F},\mathfrak{F}^{-1}
induce mutually inverse isomorphisms of
ungraded
Chow motives

(9)

h(Y)\xrightleftharpoons[~~~\mathfrak{F}^{-1}~~~]{~~~\mathfrak{F}~~~}h(X),\quad\mathfrak{F}\circ\mathfrak{F}^{-1}=\mathrm{id}_{h(X)},\quad\mathfrak{F}^{-1}\circ\mathfrak{F}=\mathrm{id}_{h(Y)}.

Remark 1.1
.

For
X,Y
as above, a morphism of Chow motives

is induced by a correspondence in
\mathrm{CH}^{2n}(X\times Y)
. Here, for a morphism of ungraded Chow motives, we allow the correspondence to take value in the
ungraded

\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X\times Y)
. As we discussed in Section

, a key difficulty in studying the interaction between Chow motives and derived categories is to capture the information of the grading from the naïve ungraded isomorphisms (

K3^{[n]}
-type, which we discuss in the next section.

(Fourier vanishing)

The following hold for

(a)

For any integers

2.1.
Derived equivalences and Hodge isometries

Let
X,X^{\prime}
be hyper-Kähler varieties of
K3^{[n]}
-type. We say that
X,X^{\prime}
are Hodge isometric, if there is a Hodge isometry

H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq H^{2}(X^{\prime},{\mathbb{Q}})

with respect to the BBF form. The following proposition shows that derived equivalent varieties of
K3^{[n]}
-type must be Hodge isometric.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
X,X^{\prime}
are derived equivalent, then they are Hodge isometric.

Proof.

The second rational cohomology of
X,X^{\prime}
can be decomposed into algebraic and transcendental parts

H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})=T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\oplus\mathrm{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}},\quad H^{2}(X^{\prime},{\mathbb{Q}})=T(X^{\prime})_{\mathbb{Q}}\oplus\mathrm{NS}(X^{\prime})_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Recall that the extended Mukai lattice for a hyper-Kähler variety
X
admits a Hodge structure of weight 2 with

\widetilde{H}^{2,0}(X)=H^{2,0}(X),\quad\widetilde{H}^{1,1}(X)={\mathbb{C}}\alpha\oplus H^{1,1}(X)\oplus{\mathbb{C}}\beta,\quad\widetilde{H}^{0,2}(X)=H^{0,2}(X).

Since
X,X^{\prime}
are derived equivalent, by
[
34
, Theorems 4.10 and 4.11]
there is a Hodge isometry between the extended Mukai lattices

\widetilde{H}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq\widetilde{H}(X^{\prime},{\mathbb{Q}}).

The Hodge isometry has to preserve the transcendental and the algebraic parts, which yields a Hodge isometry

(28)

T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq T(X^{\prime})_{\mathbb{Q}},

and an isometry (as the Hodge structure is trivial on the algebraic part)

\mathrm{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\oplus U\simeq\mathrm{NS}(X^{\prime})_{\mathbb{Q}}\oplus U,\quad U={\mathbb{Q}}\alpha\oplus{\mathbb{Q}}\beta.

By the Witt cancellation theorem, the lattice
\mathrm{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
is isometric to
\mathrm{NS}(X^{\prime})_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Combined with the Hodge isometry (
28
), we obtain a Hodge isometry

H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})=T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\oplus\mathrm{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq T(X^{\prime})\oplus\mathrm{NS}(X^{\prime})_{\mathbb{Q}}=H^{2}(X^{\prime},{\mathbb{Q}}).\qed

. Also unknown is whether such a correspondence necessarily conjugates the cup-product and the convolution product on
H^{*}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})
after normalization; see
[
24
, Example 1.7 and Conjecture 1.8]
.

Nevertheless, as we observe in this section, it is always possible to construct a degree-reversing, product-conjugating self-correspondence in
H^{*}(Y\times Y,{\mathbb{Q}})
, which is further induced by algebraic cycles. The construction relies on the Lefschetz standard conjecture for varieties of
K3^{[n]}
-type established in
[
8
,
24
]
.

Choose an ample class
L\in H^{2}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})
of BBF norm
(L,L)=2d
, and consider the associated
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
(e_{L},f_{L},h)
with

e_{L}={}\cup L:H^{*}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{*+2}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}}),\quad h|_{H^{i}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})}=(i-2n)\mathrm{id}.

Viewed as a correspondence, the Lefschetz operator
e_{L}
is given by the cycle class

(30)

e_{L}=\Delta_{*}L\in H^{4n+2}(Y\times Y,{\mathbb{Q}})

where
\Delta:Y\to Y\times Y
is the diagonal morphism. By
[
24
, Lemma 1.6]
, the dual Lefschetz operator
f_{L}
is also algebraic, and is explicitly given by the correspondence

(31)

f_{L}=\mathfrak{F}^{-1}\circ e_{\pm\frac{1}{rd}\widetilde{H}(\mathfrak{F})^{-1}(L)}\circ\mathfrak{F}\in H^{4n-2}(Y\times Y,{\mathbb{Q}}).

Here we follow the notation of Section
1
:
\mathfrak{F}
is the Fourier transform induced by the rank
n!r^{n}
twisted vector bundle on
Y\times X
, and
\widetilde{H}(\mathfrak{F})
is as in (
27
) which for
n
even is defined up to a sign.

Now consider the correspondence

\mathfrak{F}_{L}:=\exp(e_{L})\circ\exp(-f_{L})\circ\exp(e_{L})\in H^{*}(Y\times Y,{\mathbb{Q}})

which we call the cohomological
Fourier self-transform
associated with
L
. Note that if we view the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
(e_{L},f_{L},h)
as elements of the LLV algebra
\mathfrak{so}(\widetilde{H}(X,{\mathbb{C}}))
, then
\mathfrak{F}_{L}
naturally lives in the associated Lie group
\mathrm{SO}(\widetilde{H}(Y,{\mathbb{C}}))
via the exponential map

\mathrm{exp}:\mathfrak{so}(\widetilde{H}(X,{\mathbb{C}}))\to\mathrm{SO}(\widetilde{H}(Y,{\mathbb{C}})).

The latter acts on
H^{*}(Y,{\mathbb{C}})
by integrating the LLV algebra action

\mathrm{SO}(\widetilde{H}(Y,{\mathbb{C}}))\to\mathrm{GL}(H^{*}(Y,{\mathbb{C}}))

as in (
25
). In other words, the class
\mathfrak{F}_{L}\in H^{*}(Y\times Y,{\mathbb{Q}})
, viewed as an element in
\mathrm{GL}({H}^{*}(Y,{\mathbb{C}}))
, lies in the image of the integration map above. The inverse of
{\mathfrak{F}}_{L}
is given by

{\mathfrak{F}}_{L}^{-1}=\exp(-e_{L})\circ\exp(f_{L})\circ\exp(-e_{L})\in H^{*}(Y\times Y,{\mathbb{Q}}).

We have by construction
\mathfrak{F}_{L}^{-1}h\mathfrak{F}_{L}=-h\in\mathfrak{so}(\widetilde{H}(X,{\mathbb{C}}))
, and therefore

{\mathfrak{F}}_{L}^{-1}\circ h\circ{\mathfrak{F}}_{L}=-h\in H^{*}(Y\times Y,{\mathbb{Q}}).

In particular,
{\mathfrak{F}}_{L}
induces a degree-reversing automorphism

{\mathfrak{F}}_{L}:H^{*}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{*}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}}),\quad H^{i}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{4n-i}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}}).

Then as in Proposition
1.8
(a), we obtain immediately the Fourier vanishing: that for any integers
0\leq i,j<2n
we have

{\mathfrak{F}}_{L,2i+1}=0\in H^{4i+2}(Y\times Y,{\mathbb{Q}}),\quad{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}_{L,2j+1}=0\in H^{4j+2}(Y\times Y,{\mathbb{Q}}),

and for any integers
0\leq i,j\leq 2n
, we have

\mathfrak{F}_{L,2i}\circ\mathfrak{F}^{-1}_{L,2j}=0\in H^{4i+4j-4n}(Y\times Y,{\mathbb{Q}}),\quad\mathfrak{F}^{-1}_{L,2i}\circ\mathfrak{F}_{L,2j}=0\in H^{4i+4j-4n}(Y\times Y,{\mathbb{Q}}).

Here
{\mathfrak{F}}_{L,i}
and
{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}_{L,j}
are the summands of
{\mathfrak{F}}_{L},{\mathfrak{F}}_{L}^{-1}
in
H^{2i}(Y\times Y,{\mathbb{Q}})
and
H^{2j}(Y\times Y,{\mathbb{Q}})
respectively.

The normalizations of the Fourier self-transforms
{\mathfrak{F}}_{L},{\mathfrak{F}}_{L}^{-1}
take the form

(32)

\begin{gathered}\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{L}=\sum_{k}\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{L,2k},\quad\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{L,2k}:=d^{n-k}\mathfrak{F}_{L,2k},\\
\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}_{L}=\sum_{k}\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}_{L,2k},\quad\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}_{L,2k}:=d^{n-k}\mathfrak{F}_{L,2k},\end{gathered}

where
2d
is the BBF norm of
L
.

Recall the two graded algebra objects from (
29
). We remark that although the two motivic decompositions of
h_{\mathrm{hom}}(Y)
in (
29
) (the second one with appropriate Tate twists) are obtained via different projectors, they are identical decompositions of homological motives. This is simply because modulo homological equivalence, the Künneth projectors are unique if they exist.

The following result is key to the proofs of the main theorems.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
Y
be the variety of
K3^{[n]}
-type introduced in Section
1.2
. Then the two graded algebra objects in (
29
) are isomorphic through the Fourier self-transform
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{F}}}_{L}
.

Proof.

By the uniqueness of the Künneth projectors, the motivic decompositions of
h_{\mathrm{hom}}(Y)
in (
29
) coincide with the one induced by the orthogonal projectors

{\mathfrak{F}}_{L,2k}\circ{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}_{L,4n-2k}\in H^{4n}(Y\times Y,{\mathbb{Q}}),\quad 0\leq k\leq 2n,

or the one induced by the orthogonal projectors

{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}_{L,2k}\circ{\mathfrak{F}}_{L,4n-2k}\in H^{4n}(Y\times Y,{\mathbb{Q}}),\quad 0\leq k\leq 2n.

Then, repeating the proof of Proposition
1.4
, we obtain mutually inverse isomorphisms of homological motives

h_{\mathrm{hom},4n-2k}(Y)(2n-2k)\xrightleftharpoons[~~~\mathfrak{F}^{-1}_{L,4n-2k}~~~]{~~~\mathfrak{F}_{L,2k}~~~}h_{\mathrm{hom},2k}(Y).

Concerning the two product structures, we use
[
24
, Lemma 10.4]
which says that any element
g\in O(\widetilde{H}(Y,{\mathbb{C}}))
satisfying

g^{-1}hg=-h\in\mathfrak{so}(\widetilde{H}(Y,{\mathbb{C}}))

induces, after normalization, an automorphism of
H^{*}(Y,{\mathbb{C}})
which conjugates the cup-product and the convolution product. In particular, this applies to
g={\mathfrak{F}}_{L}
the Fourier self-transform and its normalization
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{F}}}_{L}
in (
32
). Repeating again the proof of Proposition
1.7
, we find commutative diagrams

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{\mathrm{hom},4n-2i}(Y)(2n-2i)\otimes h_{\mathrm{hom},4n-2j}(Y)(2n-2j)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{\mathrm{hom},4n-2i-2j}(Y)(2n-2i-2j)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{\mathrm{hom},2i}(Y)\otimes h_{\mathrm{hom},2j}(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{\mathrm{hom},2i+2j}(Y)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ast}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{L,2i}\otimes\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{L,2j}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{L,2i+2j}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cup}

with the vertical arrows isomorphisms of homological motives. The direct sum of the diagrams above yields the desired isomorphism of graded algebra objects.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
X,Y
be varieties of
K3^{[n]}
-type which admit a (twisted) derived equivalence induced by a projectively hyperholomorphic bundle as in (
8
). Then there is an isomorphism of homological motives which respects the cup-product,

(h_{\mathrm{hom}}(X),\cup)\simeq(h_{\mathrm{hom}}(Y),\cup).

Proof.

The isomorphism is obtained as the composition of the isomorphism

(33)

\left(\bigoplus_{k=0}^{2n}h_{\mathrm{hom},2k}(X),\cup\right)\simeq\left(\bigoplus_{k=0}^{2n}h_{\mathrm{hom},4n-2k}(Y)(2n-2k),\ast\right)

shown in Propositions
1.7
and
1.8
, and the isomorphism

(34)

\left(\bigoplus_{k=0}^{2n}h_{\mathrm{hom},2k}(Y),\cup\right)\simeq\left(\bigoplus_{k=0}^{2n}h_{\mathrm{hom},4n-2k}(Y)(2n-2k),\ast\right)

shown in Proposition
2.4
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

We explain here that the isomorphism we construct for Corollary
2.5
sends the class of a point
[\mathrm{pt}]\in H^{4n}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
to
[\mathrm{pt}]\in H^{4n}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})
. This is deduced from the following facts.

(a)

The unit of the convolution product on
H^{*}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})
is
[\mathrm{pt}]/n!
; this is
[
24
, Lemma 10.3]
.

(b)

The Fourier transform
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{F}}}
in cohomology, per our convention, sends the convolution product on
H^{*}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})
to the cup-product on
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
. At the same time it sends the cup-product on
H^{*}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})
to the convolution product on
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
, the latter being the convention adopted by Markman. This is because we have

{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}={{}^{t}{\mathfrak{F}}}\in H^{*}(X\times Y,{\mathbb{Q}})

by
[
24
, Corollary 4.2]
, which uses the vanishing of the odd cohomology.

(c)

The Fourier self-transform
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{F}}}_{L}
also interchanges the cup-product and the convolution product on
H^{*}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})
. Again this is because

{\mathfrak{F}}_{L}^{-1}={{\mathfrak{F}}}_{L}\in H^{*}(Y\times Y,{\mathbb{Q}}).

In fact, we have by definition
({\mathfrak{F}}_{L})^{2}=-\mathrm{id}\in\mathrm{SL_{2}}
, which gets sent to
\mathrm{id}\in\mathrm{SO}(\widetilde{H}(Y,{\mathbb{C}}))
since
\widetilde{H}(Y,{\mathbb{C}})
has even weights.

Therefore, the composition of the isomorphism (
33
) and (
34
) sends
[\mathrm{pt}]\in H^{4n}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
(which is
n!
times the unit of the convolution product on
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
) first to
n![Y]\in H^{0}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})
and then to
[\mathrm{pt}]\in H^{4n}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})
.

2.3.
Proofs of Theorems
0.7
and
0.9

In view of Corollary
2.2
, it remains to prove Theorems
0.7
and
0.9
. The proof of Theorem

relies on the following characterization, due to Markman

-type.

Theorem 2.7

(Markman
[
24
]
)
.

Let

be Hodge isometric varieties of
K3^{[n]}
-type. Then there exists a finite sequence of varieties of
K3^{[n]}
-type

such that each pair

satisfies either

(a)

there is a derived equivalence
D^{b}(X_{i},\alpha_{X_{i}})\simeq D^{b}(X_{i+1},\alpha_{X_{i+1}})
induced by a projectively hyperholomorphic bundle as in (
8
), or

(b)

there is a parallel transport
H^{2}(X_{i},{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{2}(X_{i+1},{\mathbb{Z}})
preserving the Hodge structures.

Proof of Theorem
0.9
.

By Theorem
2.7
, it suffices to construct an isomorphism of homological motives respecting the cup-product for each pair
X_{i},X_{i+1}
in the sequence. In case (a) the isomorphism is given by Corollary
2.5
. In case (b) the two varieties are necessarily birational by the global Torelli theorem
[
22
, Theorem 1.3]
. We then conclude by
[
32
]
.
∎

We move on to Theorem
0.7
. Let
S
be a projective
K3
surface, and let

be a primitive Mukai vector with
v^{2}=2n-2\geq 0
. The moduli space

parameterizes
H
-stable sheaves on
S
with Mukai vector

, where the polarization

is

-generic. We now prove that there is an isomorphism of homological motives which respects the cup-product,

(h_{\mathrm{hom}}(S^{[n]}),\cup)\simeq(h_{\mathrm{hom}}(M_{S,v}),\cup).

This clearly implies Theorem
0.7
.

Again by
[
32
]
, it suffices to consider the case when

is not birational to
S^{[n]}
. In this case, Zhang proved in
[

]
that there is a derived equivalence

D^{b}(S^{[n]})\simeq D^{b}(M_{S,v},\theta_{v}),

where
\theta_{v}
is a particular Brauer class on

. More precisely, Zhang’s equivalence (

) can be realized as the composition of

(36)

\Phi_{({\mathcal{E}},\theta_{v})}:D^{b}(S^{[n]})\xrightarrow{\simeq}D^{b}(M^{\prime}_{S,v},\theta_{v})

D^{b}(M^{\prime}_{S,v},\theta_{v})\xrightarrow{\simeq}D^{b}(M_{S,v},\theta_{v}).

Here

Proof of Theorem
0.7
.

Again it suffices to construct an isomorphism of homological motives respecting the cup-product for the pair

, and for the pair

∎

Another approach is to reduce Theorem

3.

Throughout this section, we assume that
(S,H)
is a
fixed

K3
surface of genus
g\geq 2
with
\mathrm{Pic}(S)={\mathbb{Z}}H
. Let

v=(r,mH,s)\in\widetilde{H}(S,{\mathbb{Z}}),\quad r,m,s\in{\mathbb{Z}}

be a primitive Mukai vector with

. The main purpose of this section is to construct generically defined kappa class; see Theorem

.

3.1.
Moduli of polarized
K3
surfaces

Recall the derived equivalences (
36
) and (
37
) for the fixed polarized
K3
surface
(S,H)
. Now we vary
(S,H)
over the moduli stack
{\mathcal{X}}_{g}
. We denote

{\mathcal{X}}^{*}_{g}\subset{\mathcal{X}}_{g}

the open substack formed by polarized
K3
surfaces whose polarization is general with respect to the Mukai vector
v
. Clearly all polarized
K3
surfaces of Picard rank
1
lie in
{\mathcal{X}}^{*}_{g}
. We assume that the original
K3
surface
(S,H)
is represented by a point
P\in{\mathcal{X}}^{*}_{g}
. The universal family
{\mathcal{S}}_{g}\to{\mathcal{X}}^{*}_{g}
carries a relative polarization
{\mathcal{H}}\in\mathrm{Pic}({\mathcal{S}}_{g})
of genus

, and we have the proper and smooth families

(38)

{\mathcal{S}}_{g}^{[n]}\to{\mathcal{X}}^{*}_{g},\quad{\mathcal{M}}_{g,v}\to{\mathcal{X}}^{*}_{g}

formed by the Hilbert schemes of points and the moduli of stable sheaves with respect to the Mukai vector
v
respectively. Here the Mukai vector and the polarization are defined relatively over
{\mathcal{X}}_{g}
by the relative polarization
{\mathcal{H}}
; the general assumption of the polarization ensures that

is smooth with hyper-Kähler fibers. The fibers of (
38

recover the hyper-Kähler varieties

and

respectively.

We may modify the family
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,v}\to{\mathcal{X}}^{*}_{g}
to include the birational model

used in (
36
) and (
37
) as a fiber.

Proposition 3.1
.

After shrinking
{\mathcal{X}}^{*}_{g}
to an open Deligne–Mumford substack
{\mathcal{X}}^{\circ}_{g}\subset{\mathcal{X}}^{*}_{g}
containing
P
, there is a proper and smooth morphism

\Pi_{M}:{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{g,v}\to{\mathcal{X}}^{\circ}_{g}

satisfying

(a)

the fiber over
P\in{\mathcal{X}}^{\circ}
recovers the birational model
M^{\prime}_{S,v}
of
M_{S,v}
, and

(b)

each fiber of
\Pi_{M}
is birational to the corresponding fiber of the family
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,v}\to{\mathcal{X}}^{\circ}_{g}
given in (
38
).

Proof.

By
[
2
]
, the birational model
M^{\prime}_{S,v}
of
M_{S,v}
is realized as the moduli space of stable objects on
S
with Mukai vector
v
with respect to a
v
-generic Bridgeland stability condition. The proposition follows from the general construction of families of Bridgeland stability conditions
[
1
]
; see the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 29.4(2)]
.
∎

We consider the following family given by (

) and Proposition

,

(39)

\Pi:{\mathcal{S}}_{g}^{[n]}\times_{{\mathcal{X}}^{\circ}}{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{g,v}\to{\mathcal{X}}^{\circ}_{g},

whose fiber over

is the product

. We say that a class

\gamma\in H^{*}(S^{[n]}\times M^{\prime}_{S,v},{\mathbb{Q}})

is

).

Lemma 3.2
.

All the classes in

\mathrm{Pic}(S^{[n]}\times M^{\prime}_{S,v})_{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathrm{Pic}(S^{[n]})_{\mathbb{Q}}\oplus\mathrm{Pic}(M^{\prime}_{S,v})_{\mathbb{Q}}\subset H^{2}(S^{[n]}\times M^{\prime}_{S,v},{\mathbb{Q}})

are monodromy invariant.

Proof.

There are two algebraic classes in
H^{2}(M_{S,v},{\mathbb{Q}})=H^{2}(M^{\prime}_{S,v},{\mathbb{Q}})
which are given by the two algebraic classes in the orthogonal complement

v^{\perp}\subset\widetilde{H}(S,{\mathbb{Q}})

via a correspondence given by the universal family. Therefore, both classes can be defined relatively over
{\mathcal{X}}^{\circ}_{g}
; this proves the monodromy invariance of the classes in

is parallel and simpler.
∎

The Fourier–Mukai kernel of (

(40)

\kappa({\mathcal{E}})\in H^{*}(S^{[n]}\times M^{\prime}_{S,v},{\mathbb{Q}})

as in Section

which, by the Künneth formula, is completely determined by its induced (cohomological) Fourier transform

(41)

\mathfrak{F}:H^{*}(S^{[n]},{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{*}(M^{\prime}_{S,v},{\mathbb{Q}}).

Proposition 3.3
.

The class (
40
) is monodromy invariant. In particular, (
40
) defines a class

(42)

\kappa({\mathcal{E}})\in H^{0}\left({\mathcal{X}}^{\circ}_{g},R\pi_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{g}^{[n]}\times_{{\mathcal{X}}^{\circ}}{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{g,v}}\right)

via parallel transport.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.9
, the morphism (

) is determined by its induced morphism on the extended Mukai lattices

(43)

\widetilde{H}(\mathfrak{F}):\widetilde{H}(S^{[n]},{\mathbb{Q}})\to\widetilde{H}(M^{\prime}_{S,v},{\mathbb{Q}}).

Hence, it suffices to prove that (

) is monodromy invariant for (

The morphism (

) preserves the algebraic and the transcendental parts respectively. The morphism on the transcendental part is determined by the class of the symplectic form, which is clearly monodromy invariant. The algebraic parts of
\widetilde{H}(S^{[n]},{\mathbb{Q}})
and
\widetilde{H}(M^{\prime}_{S,v},{\mathbb{Q}})
are spanned by

and the classes in

and

∎

The main result of Section

is the following theorem, whose proof will be completed in Section

.

Theorem 3.4
.

There is a class

\mathfrak{K}^{\mathrm{univ}}\in\mathrm{CH}^{*}\left({\mathcal{S}}_{g}^{[n]}\times_{{\mathcal{X}}_{g}^{\circ}}{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{g,v}\right)

whose restriction to every fiber over
{\mathcal{X}}^{\circ}_{g}
recovers the cohomology class (
42
).

The Fourier–Mukai transform (
36
) is constructed for any
K3
surface using transcendental techniques (
e.g.
twistor lines, hyper-holomorphic bundles,
etc
.). In order to apply the Franchetta properties over the moduli of polarized
K3
surfaces, we want to globalize it over a Zariski open subset of
{\mathcal{X}}_{g}
. This is achieved by a moduli approach which we discuss in the following.

3.2.
Stable vector bundles on gerbes

The Brauer class
\theta_{v}
in Zhang’s Fourier–Mukai transform (
36
) is given by a class

(44)

\theta_{v}\in H^{2}(M^{\prime}_{S,v},{\mathbb{Z}}/(2n-2){\mathbb{Z}}).

Geometrically, this corresponds to a
\mathbf{\mu}_{2n-2}
-gerbe

\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}_{S,v}\to M^{\prime}_{S,v}.

The class (
44
) is canonical up to a sign (see
[
36
, Section 2.2]
). Therefore it can be defined as a relative class for the family
\Pi_{M}:{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{g,v}\to{\mathcal{X}}^{\circ}_{g}
; in other words, it gives rise to a section

(45)

[\theta_{v}]\in H^{0}({\mathcal{X}}^{\circ}_{g},R^{2}{\Pi_{M}}_{*}({\mathbb{Z}}/(2n-2){\mathbb{Z}})),

which yields a
\mu_{2n-2}
-gerbe for each fiber of
\Pi_{M}:{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{g,v}\to{\mathcal{X}}^{\circ}_{g}
.

The next lemma shows that we can globalize these gerbes by passing to a generically finite cover.

Lemma 3.5
.

There exists an irreducible nonsingular quasi-projective variety
{{\mathcal{X}}}^{\natural}_{g}
together with a generically finite morphism

\nu:{{\mathcal{X}}}^{\natural}_{g}\to{\mathcal{X}}^{\circ}_{g}

such that the pullback

[\theta_{v}]\in H^{0}({\mathcal{X}}^{\natural}_{g},R^{2}{\Pi_{M}}_{*}\left({\mathbb{Z}}/(2n-2){\mathbb{Z}}\right)).

of (
45
) along
\nu
can be lifted to a class

\Theta_{v}\in H^{2}({\mathcal{M}}^{\prime\natural}_{g,v},{\mathbb{Z}}/(2n-2){\mathbb{Z}}).

Here we use
\Pi_{M}:{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime\natural}_{g,v}\to{{\mathcal{X}}}^{\natural}_{g}
to denote the pullback of the family
\Pi_{M}:{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{g,v}\to{\mathcal{X}}^{\circ}_{g}
via
\nu
.

Proof.

Using level structures, we can dominate the irreducible nonsingular Deligne–Mumford stack
{\mathcal{X}}^{\circ}_{g}\subset{\mathcal{X}}_{g}
by an irreducible quasi-projective nonsingular variety
{\mathcal{X}}^{\natural}_{g}
through a generically finite morphism. However, it is not clear whether the section
[\theta_{v}]
can be lifted to a global class
\Theta_{v}
as desired; this is because the Leray spectral sequence with
{\mathbb{Z}}/(2n-2){\mathbb{Z}}
-coefficients

H^{i}({\mathcal{X}}^{\natural}_{g},R^{j}{\Pi_{M}}_{*}({\mathbb{Z}}/(2n-2){\mathbb{Z}}))\Rightarrow H^{i+j}({\mathcal{M}}^{\prime\natural}_{g,v},{\mathbb{Z}}/(2n-2){\mathbb{Z}})

may not degenerate on the second page.

The obstruction to lifting

Remark 3.8
.

Since there are several spaces involved in the proof, we summarize the reasons of introducing them for the reader’s convenience. The original purpose concerns “spreading out” a cycle on
S^{[n]}\times M_{S,v}
over a Zariski open subset of
{\mathcal{X}}_{g}
. We first switch to a birational model

so that the cycle is induced by a twisted vector bundle; vector bundles are easier to be deformed. We take the open subset

to “spread out” the geometric fiber

. Next, we switch to working over

in order to lift the gerbe of each fiber to a global gerbe. Then we use Zhang’s construction
[
36
]
to show that a certain relative moduli of stable bundles on the gerbe dominates the base

, which constructs the desired cycle class. Note that by our construction the restriction of

over the moduli point

may not exactly recover
\kappa({\mathcal{E}})\in\mathrm{CH}^{*}(S^{[n]}\times M^{\prime}_{S,v})
. Nevertheless we know from the monodromy invariance (Proposition
3.3
) that they are matched as cohomology classes, which is enough for our purpose.

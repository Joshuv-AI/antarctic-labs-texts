Efficacy analysis of TACE in combination with anlotinib and sintilimab in the treatment of unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Huawei Xu, Jihua Jiang, Jian Fang, Zhengfei Wang, Shuyun Yu, Yue Wu, Renya Jiang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
To investigate the effectiveness of TACE in combination with anlotinib and sintilimab in the treatment of unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma. A total of 210 patients with primary unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (PAC) in our hospital from January 2020 to December 2023 were selected as the study subjects, and they were divided into two groups of 105 cases each by the envelope method using sequentially numbered, opaque sealed envelopes containing computer-generated random allocations. The control group was treated with TACE combined with anlotinib, and the study group was treated with sintilimab on the basis of the control group, and all patients were followed up for 18 months or until death or loss to follow-up, and the differences in treatment efficacy, safety, tumor markers, tumor growth factors and patient recovery between the two groups were compared. The DCR and ORR of the study group were 81.90% and 49.52%, which were significantly higher than those of the control group (61.90%) and 35.24%, and the difference was statistically significant(P = 0.042 and P = 0.029, respectively). The PFS (12.54 ± 0.39 months) and OS (17.52 ± 0.98 months) in the study group were significantly higher than those in the control group (8.65 ± 0.97 months) and OS (12.94 ± 1.55 months), and the difference was statistically significant (P = 0.001), and the survival rates of patients in the study group were 75.24% and 53.33% after 12 months and 18 months, which were significantly higher than those in the control group (70.48% and 31.43%), and the difference was statistically significant(P = 0.013 and P = 0.011, respectively). The incidence of myelosuppression and liver damage in grade 1 ~ 3 adverse reactions in the study group was significantly higher than that in the control group, and the difference was statistically significant(P = 0.035 and P = 0.021, respectively). No patient experienced grade 4–5 adverse reactions. There was no significant difference in the scores of CEA, AFP, CD3+, CD4+, CD4+/CD8+, NK cells, VEGF, PDGF, KPS and QLQ-C30 between the two groups before treatment (P > 0.05), and the KPS scores were significantly higher than those before treatment, and the levels of VEGF, PDGF, CEA, AFP, CD3+, CD4+, CD4+/CD8+, NK cells and QLQ-C30 were significantly lower than those before treatment. The difference was statistically significant (P < 0.001), the CD3+, CD4+, CD4+/CD8+, NK cell levels and KPS scores in the study group were significantly higher than those in the control group, and the scores of VEGF, PDGF, CEA, AFP and QLQ-C30 were significantly lower than those in the control group, and the difference was statistically significant (P < 0.001). The treatment of TACE combined with anlotinib and sintilimab in patients with intermediate-stage unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma can effectively improve the short-term and long-term treatment efficacy, and adverse reactions can be tolerated by patients, and can effectively reduce the level of tumor markers, reduce tumor angiogenesis, improve patients’ immune function, and improve the survival rate of patients.

Introduction

Primary liver cancer is one of the sixth most common cancers in the world, of which hepatocellular carcinoma is the main pathological type, accounting for more than 75% of the incidence of all types1. The etiology of liver cancer is complex, and now the high risk factors for liver cancer in China are combined hepatitis B virus infection and excessive alcohol consumption2. Early symptoms of hepatocellular carcinoma are insidious, lack of clinical symptoms and rapid progression of the disease, and high malignancy, which leads to the fact that most of the patients with hepatocellular carcinoma are in the middle and late stages of the tumor at the time of diagnosis3. According to some studies, more than 70% of liver cancer patients miss the opportunity of surgery at the time of diagnosis, and only less than 20% of patients can undergo radical treatment such as tumor resection4. Currently, interventional therapy is the main treatment for patients with intermediate and advanced hepatocellular carcinoma who miss the opportunity of surgery. Transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) is the most common interventional therapy for middle- and advanced-stage hepatocellular carcinoma5, in which the hepatic arteries are embolized and injected with a high concentration of chemotherapeutic drugs to directly affect the tumor to cut off the blood supply of tumor cells and promote apoptosis of the tumor with focal ischemic necrosis, so as to delay the development of the tumor. It is the most common interventional therapy for middle and advanced hepatocellular carcinoma5. Anrotinib is a novel small-molecule multi-target tyrosine kinase inhibitor with the effects of tumor growth inhibition and anti-tumor angiogenesis, which has significant efficacy in a variety of malignant tumors6.Sindilizumab is a programmed death receptor-1 (PD-1) inhibitor developed in China, which can regulate the generation of T cells to play the role of tumor cell killing7. Currently, studies at home and abroad have confirmed the efficacy and safety of sindilizumab in combination with erlotinib in the treatment of non-small cell lung cancer and colorectal cancer, as well as the clinical efficacy of TACE in combination with erlotinib in the treatment of hepatocellular carcinoma8. However, no researchers have investigated the combination of the three in the treatment of hepatocellular carcinoma, so this study aims to investigate the effectiveness of TACE combined with amilorotinib and sindilizumab in the treatment of unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma, and to investigate the clinical efficacy and safety of this treatment program, in order to provide reference for the development of a clinical treatment program for intermediate- and advanced-stage hepatocellular carcinoma.

Information and methods

General information

From January 2020 to December 2023, 210 patients with primary unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma in our hospital were selected as the study subjects, after power calculation showed this sample size would provide 80% power to detect a 15% difference in disease control rate between groups at a significance level of 0.05. They were divided into 2 groups of 105 cases each by the envelope method, which was implemented by an independent research nurse not involved in patient treatment or evaluation. Sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes containing computer-generated random allocations were used to ensure allocation concealment. The patients in the control group were treated with TACE combined with anlotinib, while those in the study group were treated with sintilimab on the basis of the control group. The difference was not statistically significant (P > 0.05), see Table 1.

Criteria of inclusion and exclusion

Inclusion criteria: (1) Primary unresectable liver cancer diagnosed by laboratory examination, imaging evaluation and pathology, meeting the diagnostic criteria for liver cancer with an estimated survival time >3 months as determined by the BCLC staging system and Child-Pugh classification; (2) Barcelona stage9 is stage B or C; specifically for Stage C patients, those with vascular invasion and/or limited extrahepatic disease (defined as ≤ 3 lesions in a single organ) were eligible; (3) Liver function classification Child-Pugh10 is A or B; (4) Tolerant to the drugs in this study; (5) Patients informed consent to this study. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of our hospital (approval number: 2020707).

Exclusion criteria: (1) Barcelona stage was stage A, and surgical resection was feasible; (2) Extensive systemic tumor metastasis (defined as > 3 lesions in a single extrahepatic organ or metastases in multiple organs); (3) Combined with other malignant tumors; (4) Hepatic and renal insufficiency or cardiopulmonary disease; (5) lost to follow-up, mental illness, incomplete information.

Research method

The control group was to be treated with TACE combined with anlotinib. ECG, respiration, pulse and oxyhemoglobin saturation were monitored in all patients, and 1% lidocaine was used for local anesthesia. A digital subtraction angiography (DSA) was performed by puncturing the femoral artery with Seldinger technique for angiography, to assess the patency of the hepatic artery system and identify the tumor’s blood supply artery. Then, an appropriate amount of lipiodol and chemotherapeutic drugs (cisplatin, reteplase, 5-fluorouracil, and mitomycin) emulsion were selected for injection, and the lipiodol deposition in the tumor area was monitored by the angiogram, followed by the addition of gelatin sponge particle embolization agent to implement complete embolization of the tumor’s feeding artery. The chemotherapeutic drug dosages were: cisplatin 40–60 mg, pirarubicin 30–40 mg, 5-fluorouracil 500–750 mg, and mitomycin 6–8 mg. Lipiodol dosage was determined based on tumor size and vascularity, ranging from 5 to 20 ml, with smaller volumes (5–10 ml) for tumors < 5 cm and larger volumes (10–20 ml) for tumors ≥ 5 cm or hypervascular lesions. On the fourth day after surgery, the patient was orally administrated with 12 mg/granule/d anlotinib for two weeks in a row, and then the drug was stopped for one week, with three weeks as a course of treatment. The dosage of 12 mg/day was selected based on the phase II clinical trial by Zhang et al. that established this as the recommended dose for hepatocellular carcinoma, balancing efficacy and tolerability11.

TACE procedures were repeated based on tumor response, with a maximum of 4 procedures allowed during the study period. The mean number of TACE procedures received was 2.4 ± 0.8 in the control group and 2.3 ± 0.7 in the study group (P = 0.328).

On the basis of the control group, the study group was additionally treated with intravenous injection of sintilimab at the dose of 240 mg, once every three weeks, with three weeks as a course of treatment.

Dose modifications were permitted according to toxicity. For anlotinib, dose reductions to 10mg daily and then 8mg daily were allowed for grade 3 non-hematological toxicities or intolerable grade 2 toxicities. Anlotinib dose was reduced to 10mg daily for grade 3 non-hematological toxicities, and further reduced to 8mg daily if grade 3 toxicity recurred after the first dose reduction. Sintilimab was temporarily withheld for grade 3 immune-related adverse events until recovery to grade ≤1. Sintilimab was permanently discontinued for disease progression or grade 4 immune-related adverse events. Treatment adherence was monitored through patient diaries and pill counts at each visit.

Observation index

(1) Recent curative effect. All patients receive imaging examination every two cycles after TACE operation. Tumor response was evaluated by two independent radiologists who were blinded to treatment allocation. In cases of disagreement, a third senior radiologist was consulted. The curative effect of tumor is evaluated according to the curative effect evaluation standard of solid tumor (RECIST 1.1)12, complete remission (CR) and arterial phase enhancement of all target lesions disappears. Partial response (PR), the sum of the developed diameters of the targeted lesions in the arterial contrast-enhanced phase was reduced by 30%; Disease progression (PD), the sum of the diameters of the arterial contrast enhancement images of the target lesions is ≥ 20%, or new lesions appear; The disease was stable (SD), and the increase or decrease in the diameter of the target lesion was within the range of PR and PD. Tumor control rate (DCR)=(CR + PR + SD/total cases ×100%). Objective response rate (ORR)=(PR + CR/total cases ×100%).

(2) Long-term efficacy. The patients were followed up for 18 months by telephone or outpatient re-examination, or until death or loss to follow-up. The deadline of follow-up was March 2023. For patients enrolled after September 2021, the actual follow-up period was less than 18 months, with a minimum follow-up of 3 months. The median follow-up duration was 14.2 months (range: 3–18 months). The survival rates after 12 months and 18 months were recorded. Total survival time (OS) was defined as the time from the start of treatment to the death of the patient, and progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the time from the start of treatment to the definite progression of disease or death of the patient. Kaplan-Meier method was used to estimate survival curves, and hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated using Cox proportional hazards models, adjusting for baseline characteristics including hepatitis B status, prior treatments, and BCLC stage.

(3) For safety analysis, the United States National Cancer Institute Common Toxicity Criteria (NCI-CTC 5.0)13 was used to evaluate adverse reactions, including hypertension, hand-foot syndrome, proteinuria, bone marrow suppression, fever, nausea, vomiting and diarrhea. All adverse events were recorded regardless of severity (grades 1–5). Given our sample size of 210 patients, we had 80% power to detect adverse events occurring at a frequency of 2% or higher. Rarer events might not be detectable with statistical confidence, which is acknowledged as a limitation of this study.

(4) For analysis of tumor marker levels, the serum tumor marker levels of the two groups in the pre-treatment and three cycles after treatment were compared. Blood samples were collected in the morning after overnight fasting, one day before treatment initiation and one week after completion of the third treatment cycle. 3 ml of fasting elbow venous blood was collected at 3000 r/min for 10 min, and the supernatant was collected. Carcinoembryonic antigen(CEA) and α-fetoprotein (AFP) were measured by ELISA. CEA was included despite its lower specificity for HCC compared to AFP because combined marker evaluation can increase diagnostic sensitivity for non-AFP-producing tumors and help monitor treatment response. The ELISA assays used had a sensitivity of 0.5 ng/mL for CEA and 5.0 ng/mL for AFP, with intra- and inter-assay coefficients of variation < 10%.

(5) Changes in immune function. The changes in immune function of patients between the two groups before and three cycles after treatment were compared. Blood samples for immune function tests were collected at the same timepoints as tumor markers. Three mL of fasting elbow venous blood was taken from patients and the levels of T cell subsets (CD3+, CD4+, CD4+/CD8+, and NK) were measured by flow cytometry. Flow cytometry analyses were performed using FACSCanto II (BD Biosciences) and analyzed with FlowJo software (version 10.6.1). These immune parameters were assessed because prior studies have shown their correlation with clinical outcomes in HCC patients receiving immunotherapy, with higher CD4 + and CD8 + T cell proportions associated with better response and longer survival14.

CD3+, CD4+, CD4+/CD8+, and NK cell levels were compared with reference values from healthy controls to account for potential immunosuppressive effects of TACE. This allowed us to distinguish between treatment-related immune enhancement and recovery from TACE-induced immunosuppression.

(6) Comparison of tumor growth factor levels: The levels of tumor growth factor in the two groups before and three cycles after treatment were compared. 3 ml of fasting elbow venous blood was collected at 3000r/min 10 min, and the supernatant was collected. The enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay was used to determine the levels of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF). These angiogenesis factors were selected as they are directly targeted by anlotinib and serve as biomarkers for anti-angiogenic efficacy.

(7) Assessment of body function status. The body function changes of patients between the two groups before and after treatment in three cycles were compared. Karnofsky performance status (KPS)15 was used to assess the body recovery. The KPS score was 0–100. The higher the score was, the better the body function would be. The Quality of Life Scale for Cancer Patients (EORTC QLQ-C30)16 was used to evaluate the quality of life of patients. The QLQ-C30 score was (28–112). A lower score indicated a better quality of life for patients. For both scales, we defined the minimal clinically important difference (MCID) as 10 points for KPS and 5–10 points for QLQ-C30 based on previous validation studies17. Changes exceeding these thresholds were considered clinically meaningful regardless of statistical significance.

Statistical analysis

Data processing was performed using SPSS22.0 statistical software. The quantitative data conforming to the normal distribution should be expressed as mean ± standard deviation, and t test should be conducted. The non-normal distribution was expressed by median and interquartile, and non-parametric test was used. Qualitative data were expressed by frequency using chi-square test. Kaplan–Meier method was used for statistical analysis of total survival time (OS) and disease progression-free survival (PFS). Log-rank test was used to test the survival difference between two groups. Hazard ratios with 95% confidence intervals were calculated using Cox proportional hazards models. Multivariate analyses were performed to adjust for potential confounding factors including hepatitis B status, prior treatments, BCLC stage, and tumor size. P values were reported to three decimal places, and P < 0.05 was considered as the difference with statistical significance.

Long-term efficacy evaluation of patients in the two groups

The PFS(12.54 ± 0.39 months) and OS(17.52 ± 0.98 months) values in the study group were significantly higher than those in the control group (8.65 ± 0.97 months) and OS(12.94 ± 1.55 months), and the differences were statistically significant (P < 0.05), as shown in Table 3; Fig. 1. Among the 210 patients, 68 patients (32.4%) had follow-up duration less than 18 months (control group: 35 patients, 33.3%; study group: 33 patients, 31.4%). For these patients with shorter follow-up, the 12-month survival rates were 71.4% in the control group and 75.8% in the study group, showing similar trends to the overall cohort.

Discussion

Primary liver cancer is the second largest causative tumor in China, and it is also because China is a large country with hepatitis B that the occurrence of liver cancer in hepatitis B patients usually follows the “viral hepatitis B-liver cirrhosis-liver cancer”. However, with the gradual strengthening of hepatitis B prevention and control, the incidence of alcoholic liver cirrhosis and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease increases year by year, which also leads to the increasing incidence of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)18. Radical resection of HCC is the preferred treatment for early HCC, but the onset of HCC is insidious in the early stage. Most patients usually visit the doctor with jaundice due to abdominal pain caused by tumor enlargement and traction of liver capsule or biliary obstruction caused by hepatic dysfunction, which causes the patients’ condition at the first visit to the doctor to be in the advanced stage and they become ineligible for surgical resection19. Currently, radiation therapy, TACE, targeted drugs and other therapies are mainly adopted for unresectable HCC in order to improve outcomes. TACE is a common treatment for unresectable HCC, and lipiodol mixed with mitomycin and chemotherapy-related drugs are usually used as embolic agents. Because the molecular weight of lipiodol is large and the tumor has affinity for it, interventional injection is adopted to inject the oil into the tumor’s blood supply artery and deposit it, blocking the blood vessels and cutting off the tumor’s blood supply, thus achieving the purpose of embolization20. Anlotinib is a small molecular target tyrosine kinase inhibitor independently developed in China. The clinical study on the treatment of advanced HCC has confirmed that it has a significant effect21. However, in order to obtain a better effect on HCC, the combination of other drugs or treatment options has become an active area of research. As one of the immunosuppressive agents for HCC, sintilimab mainly targets PD-1. Research by Wu et al.22 has confirmed that the objective remission rate and disease control rate of the combination treatment for non-small cell lung cancer are 72.50% and 85.00%, respectively. However, there are few studies on the combined application of the three methods to HCC, so in this study, we established a therapeutic regimen with TACE, anlotinib and sintilimab to explore the efficacy and safety.

Both VEGF and PDGF participate in tumor angiogenesis and are closely related to tumor recurrence and metastasis23. CEA is a common broad-spectrum tumor marker whose level is positively correlated with tumor size, metastasis and infiltration24. AFP, synthesized by fetal liver under normal conditions, is lower in adult serum but higher in various tumors such as HCC, so it is mainly used as a marker of primary HCC clinically25. The results of this study showed that the DCR and ORR of the study group were 81.90% and 49.52%, respectively, which were significantly higher than those of the control group (DCR 61.90% and ORR 35.24%). After treatment, elevated VEGF, PDGF, CEA and AFP levels of patients in the two groups were significantly decreased. The VEGF, PDGF, CEA and AFP levels of the study group after treatment were significantly lower than those of the control group. This indicated that the combination of TACE, anlotinib and sintilimab could reduce tumor volume and angiogenesis more effectively than the combination of TACE and anlotinib for unresectable HCC. The underlying mechanisms for this observation include: As a mechanical vascular embolization method, TACE often needs to be embolized several times during the treatment process, which generates more stimulation to organs and often damages hepatocytes. At the same time, the postoperative hepatocytes are in a hypoxia state, which promotes the increase of VEGF level, changes the tumor microenvironment, and promotes the formation of tumor neovascularization26,27. In the treatment regimen of this study, anlotinib mainly played a role in inhibiting tumor angiogenesis, as shown in the results of Wu et al.6 which indicated that anlotinib enhanced ROS level of cancer cells in a dose-dependent manner and mediated apoptosis through the autophagy-iron death signaling pathway. The results of Xu et al.28 indicated that anlotinib increased the expression level of autophagy key regulatory protein complex-1(Beclin-1) and microtubule-associated protein 1 light chain 3B by inhibiting the Janus kinase 2 (JAK2)/)/signal transduction and transcription activator 3 (STAT3)/VEGFA signaling pathway, and reduced the expression level of downstream VEGF. At the same time, it induced cancer cell autophagy, resulting in the inhibition of cancer cell proliferation. Some results reported by Song et al.29 also indicated that the main mechanism of action of anlotinib was to inhibit the phosphorylation of VEGFR2, which in turn inhibited the transmission of PIK3/AKT signal, inhibited the proliferation and invasion of tumor cells, and promoted apoptosis through cell cycle arrest. At present, there is no related clinical study on the combined treatment of the three for primary HCC. However, some studies by Zhou Wenliang et al.30 have confirmed that the feasibility of anlotinib + sintilimab + TACE in the treatment of patients with advanced metastatic HCC is high. The research conducted by Dou Yinhui et al.31 confirmed that the application of sintilimab combined with anlotinib in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer could improve the immune level of patients and reduce the level of tumor markers, with significant curative effect and certain safety. These results were consistent with the results of this study.

The observed changes in immune function markers have significant clinical relevance for HCC treatment. CD3 + and CD4 + T cells are critical for orchestrating effective anti-tumor immune responses, while CD8 + T cells serve as the primary cytotoxic effectors against cancer cells14,32. In HCC specifically, studies have demonstrated that increased infiltration of CD8 + T cells is associated with better prognosis and improved survival33. The CD4+/CD8 + ratio reflects immune system balance, with a higher ratio typically indicating better immune function. NK cells, as part of the innate immune system, play a crucial role in the immediate recognition and killing of tumor cells without prior sensitization, making them important effectors in HCC immunosurveillance34.

The results of this study showed that the PFS(12.54 ± 0.39 months) and OS(17.52 ± 0.98 months) in the study group were significantly higher than those in the control group (8.65 ± 0.97 months) and OS(12.94 ± 1.55 months). The hazard ratio for PFS was 0.59 (95% CI: 0.44–0.78, P = 0.001) and for OS was 0.63 (95% CI: 0.47–0.84, P = 0.001), demonstrating a significant reduction in the risk of progression and death in the study group. The 12-and 18-month survival rates of the patients in the study group were 75.24% and 53.33%, respectively, significantly higher than those of the control groups, 70.48% and 31.43%, respectively. After treatment, the QLQ-C30 scores, CD3+, CD4+, CD4+/CD8+, and NK cell levels of patients in the two groups were significantly decreased, while KPS score was significantly increased. KPS scores, CD3+, CD4+, CD4+/CD8+, and NK cell levels of patients in the study group were significantly higher than those of the control group. QLQ-C30 scores were significantly lower than those of the control group, with mean differences exceeding the established minimal clinically important difference of 5–10 points17, indicating that the improvements were not only statistically significant but also clinically meaningful to patients, suggesting that the combination of TACE, anlotinib, and sintilimab could more effectively improve the immune function of patients compared with the treatment of unresectable HCC with TACE and anlotinib.

The specific mechanism for immune function improvement is related to the synergistic effects between anlotinib and sintilimab35. While we did not perform preclinical experiments to directly demonstrate this synergy in our study, several published studies support the biological rationale for this combination36,37. The specific reason for the improvement is related to the sintilimab and its potential synergistic mechanism with anlotinib. Sintilimab is a monoclonal antibody targeting the PD-1 receptor on the surface of T cells, and its specific mechanism is to activate the tumor-specific cytotoxic T cells by blocking the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway35. In this pathway, the combination of PD-L1 and PD-1 can lead to T cell apoptosis38, failure and interleukin-10 expression, while PDL-1 can protect malignant cells from CD8 + T cell-mediated cytotoxic response11,39. Tumor interstitial allogeneic lymphocytes are the main effector cells in the microenvironment of tumor tissues, with high surface expression of PD-1 and PDL-140. The cell inactivation is the result of PD-1/PDL-1 activation, while sintilimab exerts immunostimulatory effects by binding to PD-1 and reactivating T cells14.

Among the synergistic mechanisms of the two drugs, several pathways have been identified in published literature. Liu et al.17 reported that anlotinib could improve the immune microenvironment and inhibit the growth of tumors by down-regulating the expression of PD-L1 on vascular endothelial cells. Also, the results of Sun et al.14 have confirmed that anlotinib promotes the normalization of tumor blood vessels at least partially through CD4 + T cells, reprograms the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment into an immunostimulatory tumor microenvironment, significantly inhibits tumor growth, and effectively prevents systemic immunosuppression. The combined use of anlotinib and PD-1 checkpoint inhibitors may counteract the immunosuppression caused by the up-regulation of PD-L1 after monotherapy, prolong the vascular normalization time, and induce the tumor regression.

In conclusion, both anlotinib and sintilimab synergistically inhibit tumor angiogenesis, promote the normalization of tumor vascular structure, enhance immune cell adhesion molecules, chemokines and their expression receptors, and lead to increased infiltration of immune effector cells and enhanced function. It can relieve immunosuppressive effect, activate T cell function, enhance T cell’s immune monitoring ability and killing ability to tumor, jointly improve tumor microenvironment, and promote blood vessel normalization while enhancing the curative effect of immunotherapy. TACE combined with the two can also improve the microvascular increase caused by organ damage, resulting in the efficacy of the combination of the three is higher than that of TACE combined with anlotinib.

In terms of drug safety, the results of this study indicated that the incidence of bone marrow suppression and liver function damage in the Grade 1–3 adverse reactions of the study group were significantly higher than those in the control group, and there was no significant difference in the incidence of other adverse reactions. No Grade 4–5 adverse reactions were observed in either group throughout the study period. While our sample size provides 80% power to detect adverse events occurring at a rate of 2% or higher, we acknowledge the limitation that rarer severe events might not be detectable with this sample size. Moreover, after the symptomatic support treatment, the symptoms of the patient could be relieved, suggesting that the side effects of TACE combined with anlotinib and sintilimab were controllable, and the patients could tolerate it, with no fatal adverse events and it had a certain safety.

Several limitations of our study should be acknowledged. First, the follow-up period was relatively short for some patients enrolled in the later stages of the study. Second, we did not perform subgroup analyses based on important prognostic factors such as hepatitis B status or prior treatments, which might influence treatment outcomes. Third, while we observed improved immune function parameters, we did not directly assess tumor-infiltrating immune cells to confirm the proposed mechanisms of action. Future studies should address these limitations and further explore predictive biomarkers for selecting patients most likely to benefit from this combination therapy.

In summary, the short-term and long-term therapeutic effects can be effectively promoted by the application of TACE in patients with advanced unresectable HCC combined with anlotinib and sintilimab. The adverse reactions can be tolerated by patients, and the levels of tumor markers in patients can be effectively reduced, tumor angiogenesis can be reduced, the immune function of patients can be improved, and the survival rate of patients can be improved.

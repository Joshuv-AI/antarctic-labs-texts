Indecomposable direct summands of cohomologies of curves
Jędrzej Garnek

Abstract
. Groups with a non-cyclic Sylow p p -subgroup have too many representations over a field of characteristic p p to describe them fully. A natural question arises, whether the world of representations coming from algebraic varieties with a group action is as vast as the realm of all modular representations. In this article, we explore the possible “building blocks” (the indecomposable direct summands) of cohomologies of smooth projective curves with a group action. We show that usually there are infinitely many such possible summands. To prove this, we study a family of Z / p × Z / p \mdmathbb{Z}/p\times\mdmathbb{Z}/p -covers and describe the cohomologies of the members of this family completely.

1.
Introduction

One of the fundamental problems of the representation theory is to determine the set
\Indec(\Lambda)
of isomorphism classes of indecomposable
\Lambda
-modules of finite length for a fixed ring
\Lambda
. Most attention has been given to the case when

\Lambda=k[G]
is the group algebra of a finite group
G
over a field
k
.
In case when the characteristic of
k
is
p>0
, it is known that
\Indec(k[G])
is finite if and only if the
p
-Sylow subgroups of
G
are cyclic (see e.g.
[
17
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
16
]
).
Furthermore, when
p>2
and the
p
-Sylow subgroup of
G
is not cyclic, the algebra
k[G]
has a wild representation type.
In particular, classifying its modules is as difficult as the theory
of finite dimensional
k[X,Y]
-modules, which has been shown to contain undecidable statements, cf.
[
25
]
. Hence, it is unrealistic to expect any complete description of
\Indec(k[G])
in this case.

A rich source of modular representations is provided by algebraic geometry.
For instance, if
X
is a smooth projective variety over a field of positive characteristic equipped with an action of a finite group,
its cohomologies are modular representations. It is natural to wonder whether the world of representations that arise in this way is as vast as the entire realm of modular representations.
In this article, we focus on the case of smooth projective curves and investigate the following question.

Question 1.1
.

What can be said about the indecomposable direct summands of the cohomologies of curves with an action of a group?

To be more precise, we would like to study the sets:

\displaystyle\Indec^{\star}(k[G])

\displaystyle:=\{M\in\Indec(k[G]):M\textrm{ is a direct summand of }H^{1}_{\star}(X)

\displaystyle\,\textrm{ for a smooth projective curve }X/k\textrm{ with an action of }G\},

where
\star\in\{Hdg,dR\}
(recall that
H^{1}_{Hdg}(X):=H^{0}(X,{}_{X})\oplus H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
). For instance, we would like to know whether those sets are infinite.
The motivation for this question comes from the case when
k
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic
2
and
G=\mdmathbb V_{4}
(the Klein group).
It turns out that in this case
\Indec^{Hdg}(k[G])
is infinite (cf.
[
3
]
). On the other hand, surprisingly, assuming certain conjecture,
\Indec^{dR}(k[G])
has only seven elements (see
[
12
]
and Remark
6.1
). The goal of this article is to prove the following theorem, which explains the situation for other groups.

Main Theorem
.

Keep the above notation. Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>2
. Suppose that
G
has a non-cyclic
p
-Sylow subgroup.
Then the sets
\Indec^{Hdg}(k[G])
and
\Indec^{dR}(k[G])
are infinite.

Using the classical methods, it seems quite challenging to establish results about
\Indec^{Hdg}(k[G])
or
\Indec^{dR}(k[G])

for a general class of groups. The novelty of our approach lies in
decomposing the cohomologies of a Galois cover into cohomologies of Harbater–Katz–Gabber curves, using our previous results from
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
. This allows one for instance to control the equivariant structure of the cohomologies of a cover that was created using patching theory.

We give now a sketch of the proof of Main Theorem. Note that the group
G
must contain a copy of
H:=\mdmathbb{Z}/p\times\mdmathbb{Z}/p
. In the first step of the proof, we give an explicit family of curves with an action of
H
with infinitely many
possible indecomposable direct summands in the cohomologies. Namely, we consider the following family of
H
-covers of
\mdmathbb{P}^{1}
for any
\alpha\in k\setminus\mdmathbb{F}_{p}
and
m\in\mdmathbb{Z}_{+}
,
p\nmid m
:

(1.1)

X_{m,\alpha}:\quad y_{0}^{p}-y_{0}=x^{m},\quad y_{1}^{p}-y_{1}=\alpha\cdot x^{m}.

We describe the cohomologies of
X_{m,\alpha}
completely. This allows us to prove that
if
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in k\setminus\mdmathbb{F}_{p}
,
m\geq 2
and

H^{0}({}_{X_{m,\alpha_{1}}})\cong H^{0}({}_{X_{m,\alpha_{2}}})\quad\textrm{ or }\quad H^{1}_{dR}(X_{m,\alpha_{1}})\cong H^{1}_{dR}(X_{m,\alpha_{2}})

as
k[H]
-modules, then
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}
(see Theorem
4.1
and
5.1
for a more precise version of this statement). Note in particular, that the equivariant structure
of
H^{1}_{dR}(X_{m,\alpha})
does not depend only on the ramification data (which was the case for
G=\mdmathbb{Z}/p

and
G=\mdmathbb{Z}/2\times\mdmathbb{Z}/2
).

In the second part of the proof of Main Theorem, we would like to
construct a curve
Z
with an action of
G
such that the cohomologies of
Z

contain the cohomologies of
X_{m,\alpha}
(for fixed
m
and
\alpha
) as direct summands.
This construction would be straightforward if we allowed disconnected covers
(namely we could take the disjoint union
\coprod_{G/H}X_{m,\alpha}\to\mdmathbb{P}^{1}
).
Instead, in order to ensure the connectedness, we construct a
G
-cover

Z\to\mdmathbb{P}^{1}
that
locally approximates

\coprod_{G/H}X_{m,\alpha}\to\mdmathbb{P}^{1}
,
using patching theory. The results of papers
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
show that (under some additional assumptions) the cohomologies of
Z
considered as
k[H]
-modules contain the cohomologies of
X
as a direct summand.

Our results leave several questions open. We mention two of them.

Question 1.2
.

Suppose that
\cha k=2
and that the
2
-Sylow subgroup
G
is neither cyclic,
nor isomorphic to
\mdmathbb{Z}/2\times\mdmathbb{Z}/2
. Are the sets
\Indec^{Hdg}(k[G])
and
\Indec^{dR}(k[G])
infinite?

Question 1.3
.

Fix a field
k
of characteristic
p
and a group
G
with a non-cyclic
p
-Sylow subgroup. Are the sets
\{\dim_{k}M:M\in\Indec^{\star}(k[G])\}
for
\star\in\{Hdg,dR\}
infinite?

It seems likely that both questions can be answered using similar methods, but
considering other families of covers than
X_{m,\alpha}
.

Outline of the paper

In Section
2
we discuss preliminaries on curves and modular representations. Also, we introduce some notation. Section

]
.

3.
A family of
\mdmathbb{Z}/p\times\mdmathbb{Z}/p
-covers

The goal of this section is to describe the basic properties of the curves from the family (
1.1
). Fix
m\in\mdmathbb{N}
,
p\nmid m
and
\alpha\in k\setminus\mdmathbb{F}_{p}
. Let
X:=X_{m,\alpha}
. The group
H:=\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle\cong\mdmathbb{Z}/p\times\mdmathbb{Z}/p
acts on
X
by the formulas

\displaystyle(\sigma z_{0},\sigma z_{1},\sigma x)

\displaystyle:=(z_{0}+1,z_{1},x),

\displaystyle(\tau z_{0},\tau z_{1},\tau x)

\displaystyle:=(z_{0},z_{1}+1,x).

Observe that the curve
X
admits also an action of
\mdmathbb{Z}/m
given by

(z_{0},z_{1},x)\mapsto(z_{0},z_{1},\zeta_{m}\cdot x)
that commutes with the action of
H
.
We denote the cover
X\to X/H\cong\mdmathbb{P}^{1}
by
\pi
. Define
also
P
as the unique point of
X
above
\infty
and let
U:=X\setminus\{P\}
. Note that
P
is the only ramified point of
\pi:X\to\mdmathbb{P}^{1}
. Therefore
\pi
is an
Harbater–Katz–Gabber cover
(for

-groups this notion is equivalent to being a cover of the projective line ramified only at one point). The ramification groups are as follows:

H_{P,0}=\ldots=H_{P,m}=H,\quad H_{P,m+1}=H_{P,m+2}=\ldots=0

(cf.
[
28

equals:

d_{X/\mdmathbb{P}^{1},P}=(p^{2}-1)\cdot(m+1).

Thus, using the Riemann–Hurwitz formula (cf.
[
15
, Corollary IV.2.4]
) we see that

g_{X}=\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}-1)\cdot(m-1).

Let
z:=z_{0}+\beta z_{1}\in k(X)
, where
\beta:=(-\alpha)^{-1/p}
. Note that:

(3.1)

\sigma z^{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}{n\choose i}z^{i}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\tau z^{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}{n\choose i}\beta^{n-i}\cdot z^{i}.

P

, as given in the table below.

\ord_{P}(z_{0})=e_{X/X^{\prime},P}\cdot\ord_{P^{\prime}}(z_{0})=-p\cdot m,

\ord_{P}(x)=e_{X/\mdmathbb{P}^{1},P}\cdot\ord_{\infty}(x)=-p^{2}

\displaystyle\ord_{P}(dx)

\displaystyle z^{p}=z_{0}^{p}-\alpha^{-1}\cdot z_{1}^{p}=(z_{0}+x^{m})-\alpha^{-1}\cdot(z_{1}+\alpha\cdot x^{m})=z_{0}-\alpha^{-1}\cdot z_{1}.

\ord_{P}(z)=\frac{1}{p}\ord_{P}(z_{0}-\alpha^{-1}\cdot z_{1})\geq\frac{1}{p}\min\{\ord_{P}(z_{0}),\ord_{P}(z_{1})\}=-m.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
\delta\geq 0
, the set

\{z^{i}\cdot x^{c}:0\leq i<p^{2},\quad c\geq 0,\quad m\cdot i+p^{2}\cdot c<\delta\}.

is a basis of the
k
-vector space
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}((\delta-1)P))
.

Proof.

Consider
\mathcal{H}(P)
, the Weierstrass semigroup of
X
, defined as:

\mathcal{H}(P):=\{\delta\geq 0:\ord_{P}(f)=-\delta\textrm{ for some }f\in H^{0}(U,\mathcal{O}_{X})\}.

Then
\mathcal{H}(P)=\mdmathbb{Z}_{+}p^{2}+\mdmathbb{Z}_{+}m
by
[
19
, Theorem 13]
. Therefore, since
\ord_{P}(x^{2})=-p^{2}
and
\ord_{P}(z)=-m
, we obtain by
[

, Proposition 9]
:

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}((\delta-1)P))=\Span_{k}\{z^{i}\cdot x^{c}:0\leq i,c,\quad m\cdot i+p^{2}\cdot c<\delta\}.\qed

In the sequel we need also the following result.

Lemma 3.2
.

We have
\tr_{H}(z^{p^{2}-1})=(\beta^{p}-\beta)^{p-1}
.

Proof.

By definition:

\tr_{H}(z^{p^{2}-1})=\sum_{i,j\in\mdmathbb{F}_{p}}(z+i+j\cdot\beta)^{p^{2}-1}.

Therefore it suffices to show the following identity in the ring
\mdmathbb{F}_{p}[x,y]
:

(3.2)

\sum_{i,j\in\mdmathbb{F}_{p}}(x+i+j\cdot y)^{p^{2}-1}=(y^{p}-y)^{p-1}.

Denote the left hand side of (
3.2
) by
P(x,y)
.
Write
P(x,y)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}(x)\cdot y^{n}
. Then:

a_{n}(x)={p^{2}-1\choose n}\cdot\left(\sum_{i\in\mdmathbb{F}_{p}}(x+i)^{p^{2}-1-n}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{j\in\mdmathbb{F}_{p}}j^{n}\right).

Note that
a_{p^{2}-1}(x)=0
, since the first sum in the product vanishes.
If
p^{2}-p<n<p^{2}-1
, then
a_{n}(x)=0
, as
\sum_{j\in\mdmathbb{F}_{p}}j^{n}=0
, see
[
18
, Lemma 10.3]
. Moreover:

\sum_{j\in\mdmathbb{F}_{p}}j^{p^{2}-p}=\sum_{i\in\mdmathbb{F}_{p}}(x+i)^{p-1}=-1

(see e.g.
[
24
, §3, Proposition 3]
)
and
{p^{2}-1\choose p^{2}-p}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
by (
2.2
). This implies that

a_{p^{2}-p}(x)=1
.
To summarize, the degree of
P(x,y)

treated as a polynomial in
y
equals
p^{2}-p
and the leading coefficient equals
1
.
Moreover, note that
P(x,y_{0})=0
for any
y_{0}\in\mdmathbb{F}_{p}
. Indeed, if
y_{0}\neq 0
, then by substituting
j^{\prime}:=j\cdot y_{0}
:

P(x,y_{0})=\sum_{i,j^{\prime}\in\mdmathbb{F}_{p}}(x+i+j^{\prime})^{p^{2}-1}=0,

since every summand appears
p
times. Similar reasoning shows that
P(x,0)=0
.
This ends the proof, as
(y^{p}-y)^{p-1}
is the only normed polynomial
of degree
p^{2}-p
that vanishes at every element of
\mdmathbb{F}_{p}
.
∎

4.
Holomorphic differentials

In this section we study the modular representations
arising from the family of
H
-covers given by (
1.1
).
Write

\boldsymbol{d}(c):=p^{2}-\left\lceil\frac{p^{2}\cdot c+1}{m}\right\rceil.

The following is the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
m\in\mdmathbb{Z}_{+}
,
p\nmid m
.
For any
\alpha\in k\setminus\mdmathbb{F}_{p}
and
1\leq c\leq m-1
the
k[H]
-module
H^{0}({}_{X_{m,\alpha}})(\zeta_{m}^{c})
is indecomposable. Moreover, if
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in k\setminus\mdmathbb{F}_{p}
,
\boldsymbol{d}(c)\neq 0,1,p^{2}-1
and

H^{0}({}_{X_{m,\alpha_{1}}})(\zeta_{m}^{c})\cong H^{0}({}_{X_{m,\alpha_{2}}})(\zeta_{m}^{c}),

as
k[H]
-modules then
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}
.

Remark 4.2
.

One checks that
\boldsymbol{d}(c)\neq 0,1,p^{2}-1
holds if and only if

\frac{m-1}{p^{2}}<c\leq m-\frac{2m+1}{p^{2}}.

This easily implies that if
m\geq 2
and
p\geq 3
then there exists
1\leq c\leq m-1
such that
\boldsymbol{d}(c)\neq 0,1,p^{2}-1
.

We want now to translate Theorem
4.1
into
a result about a specific family of modular representations.
We fix
m\geq 1
,
\alpha\in k\setminus\mdmathbb{F}_{p}
and write

X:=X_{m,\alpha}
.
Note that the map:

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\delta-1)P)\to H^{0}(X,{}_{X}),\qquad f\mapsto f\,dx

(where
\delta:=p^{2}m-p^{2}-m
) is an isomorphism. Therefore by Lemma
3.1

the basis of
H^{0}(X,{}_{X})
is given by the forms:

\omega_{i,c}:=z^{i}\cdot x^{c-1}\,dx,

where
i<p^{2}
and
m\cdot i+p^{2}\cdot c<m\cdot(p^{2}-1)
. Thus
the basis of
H^{0}(X,{}_{X})(\zeta_{m}^{c})
is given by the forms

\omega_{i,c}
, where

i\in\mathcal{I}(c):=\{i\in\mdmathbb{Z}:0\leq i\leq p^{2}-1,\quad m\cdot i+p^{2}\cdot c<m\cdot(p^{2}-1)\}.

(note in particular, that
\mathcal{I}(0)=\varnothing
). It follows easily that
the dimension of
H^{0}(X,{}_{X})(\zeta_{m}^{c})
equals
\boldsymbol{d}(c)
.

Observe that by (
3.1
) the group action on the forms
\omega_{i,c}
is given in the following way:

\sigma\omega_{n,c}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}{n\choose i}\omega_{i,c},\quad\tau\omega_{n,c}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}{n\choose i}\beta^{n-i}\cdot\omega_{i,c}.

This allows us to give an abstract description of the
k[H]
-module
H^{0}({}_{X_{m,\alpha}})(\zeta_{m}^{c})
. For any positive integer
d\leq p^{2}
and
\beta\in k
, define

V_{d,\beta}
to be the
d
-dimensional
k
-vector space spanned by the vectors
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}
,
0\leq i<d
. For simplicity, denote
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}=0
for any
i<0
.
Consider the group action of
H
on
V_{d,\beta}
given as follows:

\displaystyle\sigma\boldsymbol{\omega}_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}{n\choose i}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\qquad\tau\boldsymbol{\omega}_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}{n\choose i}\beta^{n-i}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}.

One can easily prove by induction that for any
j\in\mdmathbb{Z}
:

\sigma^{j}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}{n\choose i}j^{n-i}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\quad\tau^{j}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}{n\choose i}(j\cdot\beta)^{n-i}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}.

This implies that
\sigma
and
\tau
are of order
p
.
Also, one checks that
\sigma
and
\tau
commute. Therefore
V_{d,\beta}

Remark 4.4
.

Even though the literature concerning the modular representations of
\mdmathbb{Z}/p\times\mdmathbb{Z}/p

is quite extensive, we didn’t manage to find the family of representations
V_{d,\beta}
in the literature. In recent years, special attention has been given to the class of modules of constant Jordan type (see e.g.
[
5
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
26
]
). It is easy to find examples demonstrating that the representations
V_{d,\beta}
are generally not of constant Jordan type. The generic Jordan type of
V_{d,\beta}
(in the sense of
[
9
]
) equals
d^{(1)}\cdot[p]+[d^{(0)}]
.
We sketch now a proof of this fact. Recall that the generic type of
V_{d,\beta}
is larger in the sense of the dominance order then the Jordan type of any
element
\xi\in k[H]
with
\xi^{p}=0
(cf.
[
27
]
). On the other hand,
d^{(1)}\cdot[p]+[d^{(0)}]
is the maximal partition in the sense of dominance order.
Thus it suffices to show that the matrix of
\sigma
has the given Jordan type. One shows easily that the fixed vectors of
\sigma
are given by:

\sum_{i=0}^{n}{n\choose i}(-1)^{n-i}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{p\cdot i+(n-i)}

for
n=0,\ldots,d^{(1)}
(corresponding to the elements
(z^{p}-z)^{n}
). Thus
\dim_{k}V_{d,\beta}^{\sigma}=d^{(1)}+1
, which is possible if and only if
\sigma

has
d^{(1)}
blocks of size
p
and one block of size
d^{(0)}
.

Remark 4.5
.

The decomposition of
H^{0}(X,{}_{X})
into indecomposable direct summands takes a particularly nice form, when
m\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}
. Indeed, if
m=p^{2}\cdot m_{0}+1
then:

\boldsymbol{d}(c)=p^{2}-t\qquad\textrm{ for }(t-1)\cdot m_{0}+1\leq c\leq t\cdot m_{0}.

Hence, for
\beta:=(-\alpha)^{-1/p}

H^{0}(X,{}_{X})\cong\bigoplus_{d=1}^{p^{2}-1}V_{d,\beta}^{\oplus m_{0}}.

In particular, all of the modules
V_{1,\beta},\ldots,V_{p^{2}-1,\beta}
appear as direct summands of
H^{0}(X,{}_{X})

for
X:=X_{p^{2}+1,\alpha}
,
\alpha:=-\beta^{-p}
.

The isomorphism (
4.2
) easily implies that

4.1
is equivalent to showing indecomposability of

(cf. Corollary
4.8

Theorem
4.1
’

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose that

\textbf{deg}\,v=\max\{s_{p}(i):c_{i}\neq 0\}.

In order to prove Proposition

we need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.7
.

For any
n
there exist
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,b_{0},b_{1},\ldots\in k
such that:

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{n}

\displaystyle=n\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{n-1}+n^{(1)}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{n-p}+\sum_{s_{p}(i)<s_{p}(n)-1}a_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},

\displaystyle\tau_{0}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{n}

\displaystyle=n\cdot\beta\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{n-1}+n^{(1)}\cdot\beta^{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{n-p}+\sum_{s_{p}(i)<s_{p}(n)-1}b_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}.

Proof.

Suppose that for some
i<n
we have
s_{p}(i)\geq s_{p}(n)-1
and
{n\choose i}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. If
i^{(0)}>n^{(0)}

or
i^{(1)}>n^{(1)}
, then
{n\choose i}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
by (
2.2
).
Therefore either
(i^{(0)},i^{(1)})=(n^{(0)}-1,n^{(1)})
(i.e.
i=n-1
),
or
(i^{(0)},i^{(1)})=(n^{(0)}-1,n^{(1)})
(i.e.
i=n-p

\displaystyle\tau_{0}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<n}{n\choose i}\beta^{n-i}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}

\displaystyle={n\choose n-1}\beta\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{n-1}+{n\choose n-p}\beta^{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{n-p}+\sum_{s_{p}(i)<s_{p}(n)-1}{n\choose i}\beta^{n-i}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}

\displaystyle=n\cdot\beta\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{n-1}+n^{(1)}\cdot\beta^{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{n-p}+\sum_{s_{p}(i)<s_{p}(n)-1}{n\choose i}\beta^{n-i}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}

(we used (
2.2
) again to show that

). One proves the second formula in the same manner.
∎

In the sequel we often use the following simple fact that holds for every
\beta\in k\setminus\mdmathbb{F}_{p}

and

(4.3)

\textrm{ if }a+b=a\cdot\beta+b\cdot\beta^{p}=0,\textrm{ then }a=b=0.

Proof of Proposition
4.6
.

We prove by induction on
N
that

(4.4)

S_{N}(V_{d,\beta})=\Span(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}:s_{p}(i)\leq N).

For

) holds for integers less than

4.7
and induction hypothesis easily imply that
for any

\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\in S_{N}(V_{d,\beta})
.
Write

. Let

.
Suppose to the contrary that

and fix any

with

. Then by Lemma

\displaystyle\tau_{0}v=\sum_{s_{p}(i)=N}c_{i}\cdot(i\cdot\beta\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i-1}+i^{(1)}\cdot\beta^{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i-p})+\sum_{s_{p}(i)<M-1}b_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}.

Thus the coefficient of

(4.5)

Similarly, by considering the coefficient of

If

5.
The de Rham cohomology

In this section we prove the following analogue of Theorem
4.1

for the de Rham cohomology.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that
m\in\mdmathbb{Z}_{+}
,
p\nmid m
. For any
\alpha\in k\setminus\mdmathbb{F}_{p}
and
1\leq c\leq m-1
the
k[H]
-module
H^{1}_{dR}(X)(\zeta_{m}^{c})
is indecomposable of dimension
p^{2}-1
. Moreover, if
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in k\setminus\mdmathbb{F}_{p}
,
\lfloor\frac{m}{p}\rfloor\leq c_{1},c_{2}\leq m-\lfloor\frac{m}{p}\rfloor
and

H^{1}_{dR}(X_{m,\alpha_{1}})(\zeta_{m}^{-c_{1}})\cong H^{1}_{dR}(X_{m,\alpha_{2}})(\zeta_{m}^{-c_{2}}),

as
k[H]
-modules, then
\lfloor\frac{\boldsymbol{d}(c_{1})}{p}\rfloor=\lfloor\frac{\boldsymbol{d}(c_{2})}{p}\rfloor

and
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}
.

Again, let
X=X_{m,\alpha}
for fixed
p\nmid m
and
\alpha\in k\setminus\mdmathbb{F}_{p}
.
We describe now the basis of the Zariski cohomology of the structure sheaf of
X
. Recall that
using Čech cohomology, this cohomology might be computed as:

H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\cong\frac{\mathcal{O}_{X}(U\cap V)}{\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)+\mathcal{O}_{X}(V)}

where
V:=X\setminus\pi^{-1}(0)
. We denote the image of a function
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(U\cap V)
in
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
by
[f]
.

Proposition 5.2
.

The basis of
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})(\zeta_{m}^{-c})
is given by
[z^{i}/x^{c}]
, where

i\in\mathcal{J}(c):=\left\{i\in\mdmathbb{Z}:0\leq i\leq p^{2}-1,\quad\frac{p^{2}\cdot c}{m}<i\right\}.

Proof.

Note that the elements
[z^{i}/x^{c}]
,
i\in\mathcal{J}(c)
are linearly independent in
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})(\zeta_{m}^{-c})
. Indeed, suppose that for some scalars
a_{i}\in k
not all equal to
zero:

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}(c)}a_{i}\cdot[z^{i}/x^{c}]=0,\quad\textrm{i.e. }\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}(c)}a_{i}\cdot z^{i}/x^{c}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)+\mathcal{O}_{X}(V).

Since
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)=k[x,z]
, there would exist
b_{ij}\in k
such that

f:=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}(c)}a_{i}\cdot z^{i}/x^{c}+\sum_{i=0}^{p^{2}-1}\sum_{j\geq 0}b_{ij}z^{i}\cdot x^{j}\in\mathcal{O}_{X,P}.

Note however, that the functions in the set

\{z^{i}\cdot x^{j}:0\leq i\leq p^{2}-1,\quad j\in\mdmathbb{Z}\}

have pairwise different valuations at
P
. Indeed, if
\ord_{P}(z^{i_{1}}\cdot x^{j_{1}})=\ord_{P}(z^{i_{2}}\cdot x^{j_{2}})
, then

p^{2}\cdot(j_{1}-j_{2})=m\cdot(i_{2}-i_{1}).

Thus
p^{2}|i_{2}-i_{1}
, which is possible only if
i_{1}=i_{2}
and
j_{1}=j_{2}
.
Moreover,
\ord_{P}(z^{i}/x^{c})<0
for
i\in\mathcal{J}(c)
. Thus
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X,P}

would be sum of functions with poles of distinct orders at
P
. The contradiction shows that
the listed elements are linearly independent. Moreover, the map

i\mapsto p^{2}-1-i

is a bijection between
\mathcal{I}(c)
and
\mathcal{J}(c)
. Note that the Serre’s duality

H^{0}(X,{}_{X})^{\vee}\cong H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
(cf.
[
15
, Corollary III.7.7]
) induces an isomorphism between
H^{0}(X,{}_{X})(\zeta_{m}^{c})^{\vee}
and
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})(\zeta_{m}^{-c})
. Therefore:

\displaystyle\dim_{k}\Span_{k}([z^{i}/x^{c}]:i\in\mathcal{J}(c))

\displaystyle=\#\mathcal{J}(c)=\#\mathcal{I}(c)

\displaystyle=\dim_{k}H^{0}(X,{}_{X})(\zeta_{m}^{c})

\displaystyle=\dim_{k}H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})(\zeta_{m}^{-c}).

Therefore
\Span_{k}([z^{i}/x^{c}]:i\in\mathcal{J}(c))=H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})(\zeta_{m}^{-c})
, which ends the proof.
∎

We describe now the de Rham cohomology of
X
. Using Čech cohomology for the cover
(U,V)
we
may identify
H^{1}_{dR}(X)
with the space
Z^{1}({}_{X}^{\bullet})/B^{1}({}_{X}^{\bullet})
, where:

\displaystyle Z^{1}({}_{X}^{\bullet})

\displaystyle:=\{(\omega_{0},h,\omega_{\infty})\in{}_{X}(U)\times\mathcal{O}_{X}(U\cap V)\times{}_{X}(U):\omega_{0}-dh=\omega_{\infty}\},

\displaystyle B^{1}({}_{X}^{\bullet})

\displaystyle:=\{(dh_{0},h_{0}+h_{\infty},dh_{\infty}):h_{0}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(U),h_{\infty}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(V)\}

(see e.g.
[
21
, Section 2]
). In the sequel we need also the Hodge–de Rham exact sequence:

(5.1)

0\to H^{0}(X,{}_{X})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}_{dR}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\to 0.

Recall that the maps
\iota
and
\phi
are defined by:

\displaystyle\iota(\omega)=(\omega,0,\omega)\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\phi((\omega_{0},h,\omega_{\infty}))=[h].

We often abuse the notation by writing
\omega
instead of
\iota(\omega)

. Thus,

We conclude that

’.
∎

Lemma 5.13
.

For any
0\leq d\leq p^{2}
the
k[G]
-module

is indecomposable.

Proof.

By Lemma

(1) we may without loss of generality assume that

.
Suppose that

for some

-modules

,
V_{2}
.
Recall that

). This easily implies that either

or

\boldsymbol{\eta}_{p^{2}-1}+v\in V_{1}
for some

such that
\textbf{deg}\,v<\textbf{deg}\,\boldsymbol{\eta}_{p^{2}-1}
. We prove by induction
the following statement:

\boldsymbol{\eta}_{n}+v_{n}\in V_{1}\qquad\textrm{ for some }v_{n}\textrm{ such that }\textbf{deg}\,v_{n}<\boldsymbol{\eta}_{n}.

for some

and

\boldsymbol{\eta}_{n+1}=\xi_{n+1}-v_{n+1}
for some

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\eta}_{n}

\displaystyle=c_{1}\cdot\sigma_{0}(\xi_{n+1}-v_{n+1})+c_{2}\cdot\tau_{0}(\xi_{n+1}-v_{n+1})+w

\displaystyle=\xi_{n}-v_{n},

where

\displaystyle\xi_{n}

\displaystyle:=c_{1}\cdot\sigma_{0}(\xi_{n+1})+c_{2}\cdot\tau_{0}(\xi_{n+1})\in V_{1},

\displaystyle v_{n}

\displaystyle:=c_{1}\cdot\sigma_{0}(v_{n+1})+c_{2}\cdot\tau_{0}(v_{n+1})-w,\qquad\textbf{deg}\,v_{n}<\textbf{deg}\,\boldsymbol{\eta}_{n}.

This ends the proof of (
5.9

that

\displaystyle\Span_{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{0})

\displaystyle=\Span_{k}(\sigma_{0}^{p-1}\cdot\tau_{0}^{p-2}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{p^{2}-1}+v))\subset V_{1}

Proof of Main Theorem

We review now the necessary facts from the articles
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
.
Let
H
be an arbitrary
p
-group.
Results of Harbater (cf.
[
13
]
) and of Katz and Gabber (cf.
[

]
) imply that for any
H
-Galois algebra

over
k[[x]]

there exists a unique (possibly disconnected)

-cover
\mathscr{Z}\to\mdmathbb{P}^{1}
ramified only over

and such that there exists an isomorphism
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathscr{Z},\infty}\cong\mathscr{B}
of

-algebras.
The cover
\mathscr{Z}\to\mdmathbb{P}^{1}
is called the

associated to

.
Suppose now that
\pi:Z\to Y
is an
H
-cover of smooth projective curves. Then
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{Z,Q}

is an
H
-Galois algebra over
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{Y,Q}\cong k[[x]]
for any

. Denote by

the corresponding HKG-cover.

Suppose that
H
and
\pi
are as above.
Recall that a function
z\in k(Z)
is called a
magical element
for
\pi
, if
\tr_{Z/Y}(z)\neq 0
and
for every
P\in Z(k)
one has
\ord_{P}(z)\geq-d^{\prime}_{Z/Y,P}
. In the sequel we use the following
properties of magical elements:

(6.1)

Suppose that
\pi
has a magical element and that all the inertia subgroups of
\pi
are normal in
H
. Then
for every
P\in Z(k)
the
k[H]
-module
\Ind^{H}_{H_{P}}H^{0}({}_{\mathscr{Z}_{\pi(P)}})
is a direct summand
of
H^{0}({}_{Z})
and
\Ind^{H}_{H_{P}}H^{1}_{dR}(\mathscr{Z}_{\pi(P)})
is a direct summand
of
H^{1}_{dR}(Z)
(cf.
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
).

(6.2)

If
\pi
factors through a Galois cover

and both the covers

and
Z^{\prime}\to Y

have magical elements, then
\pi
has a magical element as well (cf.
[
11
, Lemma 8.1]
).

(6.3)

If
H=\mdmathbb{Z}/p
and there exists a point

with ramification jump at least

,
then
\pi
has a magical element (cf.
[
11
, Lemma 7.2 and 7.3]
).

Remark 6.1
.

We conjecture that the assumptions (A) and (B) of
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
can be replaced by the following
weaker assumption:

The cover
\pi:Z\to Z/H
has no étale subcovers.

In particular, the study of
\Indec^{dR}(k[H])
and of
\Indec^{Hdg}(k[H])
for a

-group

should essentially come down to the study
of the possible indecomposable summands of the Harbater–Katz–Gabber covers and of étale covers.

We show now how to prove Main Theorem assuming the following result.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
H
be a
p
-subgroup of a finite group
G
.
Fix an
H
-cover

ramified only at
\infty
.
There exists a connected
G
-cover

such that

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{Z,\infty}\cong\Ind^{G}_{H}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,\infty}
as
k[G]
-algebras
and that the
H
-cover

has a magical element.

Proof of Main Theorem.

We keep the setup of Main Theorem. Note that since the
p
-Sylow subgroup
of
G
is not cyclic, it contains a subgroup
H
isomorphic to
\mdmathbb{Z}/p\times\mdmathbb{Z}/p
(cf.
[

). Consider the sets:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\star}

\displaystyle:=\{M\in\Indec(k[H]):M\textrm{ is a direct summand of }H^{1}_{\star}(X)|_{H}

\displaystyle\,\textrm{ for a smooth projective curve }X/k\textrm{ with an action of }G\}

for

. Note that

Therefore it suffice to prove that the sets

Pick any

and

.
Let

be the

-cover obtained by Proposition

for

,

as above and

, where

Note that
\infty\in\mdmathbb{P}^{1}(k)
has

The inertia subgroups of those points are subgroups conjugated to

. Pick any point

. Let

Moreover, since

The rest of this section will be occupied by the proof of Proposition

. The idea of the proof is to consider three

•

the first one,

, will be a (connected)
G

•

the second one,

, will be a disconnected
G
-cover that will ensure the existence of the magical element,

•

the third one,

, will be a disconnected

-cover

Then we glue them using the following consequence of patching theory (see
[

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
Z_{\circ}\to Y_{\circ}
be a
G
-cover of connected (but possibly reducible) nodal curves over
k
with branch locus
B_{\circ}\subset Y^{\circ}(k)
. Suppose that
the
B_{\circ}
is contained in the smooth locus of
Y_{\circ}
. Then there exists a connected
G
-cover
Z\to Y
of smooth projective curves

Z\to Y
with branch locus
B
such that there exists a bijection
\theta:B_{\circ}\to B
satisfying for every

(6.4)

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{\circ},b}\cong\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{Z,\theta(b)}\qquad\textrm{ as $k[G]$-modules.}

Proof.

Let
R\subset Z_{\circ}(k)
be the ramification locus of
Z_{\circ}\to Y_{\circ}
.
The base change of
Z_{\circ}\setminus R\to Y_{\circ}\setminus B
to
k[[t]]
along with the trivial deformation of complete local rings
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{Z,b}\otimes_{k}k[[t]]
for
b\in B_{\circ}
defines a thickening problem in the sense of
[
14
]
. By
[
14
, Theorem 4]
there exists
a
G
-cover

of smooth curves over
k[[t]]
with special fiber
Z_{\circ}\to Y_{\circ}

such that the following conditions hold:

•

the branch locus of
Z_{*}\to Y_{*}
is
B\times_{k}k[[t]]
,

•

for any
b\in B_{\circ}\times_{k}k[[t]]
there exists a
G
-equivariant isomorphism

(6.5)

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{*},b}\cong\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{\circ},b}\otimes_{k}k[[t]].

Since the structure is of finite type, there is a
k[t]
-subalgebra
T\subset k[[t]]

over which the covering morphism,
G
-Galois action, branch
points are defined and such that the structure sheaf of the base space is
locally free over
T
. Moreover, since (

) are isomorphisms of

-algebras of finite dimension, they are
defined by matrices involving finitely many functions in

, we may also assume that the isomorphisms (

.

of curves over

under the pullback

(since the generic fibers of

through any point

.

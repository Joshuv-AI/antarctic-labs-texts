1.
Introduction

In this work we study the double phase problem involving a degenerate/singular matrix-valued weight whose prototype is

\textnormal{div}\left(|\mathbb{M}Du|^{p-2}\mathbb{M}^{2}Du+a(x)|\mathbb{M}Du|^{q-2}\mathbb{M}^{2}Du\right)\\
=\textnormal{div}\left(|\mathbb{M}F|^{p-2}\mathbb{M}^{2}F+a(x)|\mathbb{M}F|^{q-2}\mathbb{M}^{2}F\right)\quad\mbox{in $\Omega$,}

(1.1)

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
.
The numbers
p
,
q
and the function
a:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
interplay
with each other under a basic structural assumption
that

\displaystyle 1<p<q<\infty,\quad 0\leq a(\cdot)\in C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{with $\alpha\in(0,1]$.}

(1.2)

Here
F:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a given vector field and

\mathbb{M}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is a matrix-valued weight that is symmetric, almost everywhere positive definite, and satisfies the quantitative anisotropy bound

\displaystyle|\mathbb{M}(x)||\mathbb{M}^{-1}(x)|\leq\Lambda

(1.3)

for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and some constant
\Lambda\geq 1
,
where
\lvert\cdot\rvert
is the usual spectral norm of matrices.
This condition implies that for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
there holds

\displaystyle\Lambda^{-1}\lvert\mathbb{M}\rvert\lvert\xi\rvert\leq\lvert\mathbb{M}\xi\rvert\leq\lvert\mathbb{M}\rvert\lvert\xi\rvert.

With
\mathbb{M}={\rm I}_{n}
,
Zhikov introduced in
[
43
,
44
,
45
]
the double-phase problem, motivated by the modeling of strongly anisotropic materials, elasticity, homogenization, and the Lavrentiev phenomenon.
In particular, the sharp
threshold

\frac{q}{p}\leq 1+\frac{\alpha}{n}

(1.4)

is linked to the absence of the Lavrentiev phenomenon
and serves as a necessary regularity condition for achieving optimal results
such as gradient Hölder continuity and Calderón-Zygmund estimates.
These aspects were rigorously developed in a series of foundational works
[
6
,
13
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
20
,
19
]
, which have significantly influenced subsequent research; see, for example,
[
2
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
7
,
21
,
22
,
12
,
11
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to establish a Calderón-Zygmund type theory for the matrix-weighted double phase problems.
More precisely, we prove that for every
\gamma>1
, the following implication

\displaystyle\big(|\mathbb{M}F|^{p}+a(x)\,|\mathbb{M}F|^{q}\big)\in L^{\gamma}_{\mathrm{loc}}\ \Longrightarrow\ \big(|\mathbb{M}Du|^{p}+a(x)\,|\mathbb{M}Du|^{q}\big)\in L^{\gamma}_{\mathrm{loc}},

holds with quantitative dependence on
\Lambda
and
|\log\mathbb{M}|_{\mathrm{BMO}}
.
When
\mathbb{M}={\rm{I}}_{n}
, this was proved in
[
20
,
18
]
.
Especially, a notable contribution of the paper

[
20
]
is a detailed treatment of the borderline case in
particular concerning the absence of the Lavrentiev phenomenon in
the context of the Calderón-Zygmund theory for nonuniformly
elliptic problems. Indeed the assertion was proved
under the assumption
\frac{q}{p}<1+\frac{\alpha}{n}
in earlier
work
[
18
]
. Based on the approach considered in
[
18
]
, the
authors of
[
20
]
successfully handled the borderline case
by employing an improved analytic technique of fractional estimates
developed in
[
16
]
.
We generalize this framework by incorporating nontrivial matrix weights, thereby capturing a broader class of anisotropic behaviors.
The models with variable matrix weights have typically been studied in single phase problems
(a\equiv 0)
,
where small
|\log\mathbb{M}|_{\mathrm{BMO}}
or Muckenhoupt weights are used to obtain quantitative estimates in both linear and nonlinear cases, see, for instance,
[
4
,
3
,
10
,
14
,
5
,
15
]
.
Building on these developments, the present work bridges these lines of research by treating the full double phase structure with a variable matrix weight.

Two novel structural features arise in this work.
First, the
q
-phase with a Hölder-regular coefficient brings in a fractional maximal operator in weighted Lebesgue spaces, necessitating control via the fractional Muckenhoupt-Wheeden
\mathcal{A}_{p,s}
condition (see, e.g.,
[
34
,
35
,
37
,
42
,
41
]
).
Such a phenomenon is absent in single phase or identity-weighted problems.
The classical Muckenhoupt class characterizes the boundedness of the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator on weighted Lebesgue spaces, while the fractional Muckenhoupt-Wheeden class governs the boundedness of fractional maximal operators, which is naturally applied to the analysis of the
q
-phase in weighted double phase problems.
The class
\mathcal{A}_{p,s}
with the parameter relation
1/s=1/p-\alpha/(nq)
plays a crucial role in preventing the Lavrentiev phenomenon in our setting, as connected to the sharp threshold (
1.4
).
The second is small
\log
-BMO control of
\mathbb{M}
.
The smallness of
|\log\mathbb{M}|_{\mathrm{BMO}}
enables a perturbative approach, allowing us to locally freeze the matrix weight and control deviations through logarithmic BMO estimates.
The method, previously adopted in single-phase problems, is here extended to the double phase problems.
We briefly outline this mechanism.
We work locally and freeze
\mathbb{M}
on each ball
B
by its logarithmic mean
(\mathbb{M})^{\log}_{B}
.
The solution is then compared with four reference problems in a nested way:
the homogeneous problem with the original nonlinearity,
one with the frozen matrix
(\mathbb{M})^{\log}_{B}
,
one with the
x
-dependence of the nonlinearity removed by averaging over
B
, and
one with a frozen height
a_{0}
for the
q
-phase on a slightly smaller ball.
The transition from the variable matrix weight
\mathbb{M}
to the frozen weight
(\mathbb{M})^{\log}_{B}
can be controlled by the smallness of
|\log\mathbb{M}|_{\mathrm{BMO}}
.
Once the weight is frozen, the analysis proceeds along the lines of weight-free double phase problems,
but particular care is taken to ensure that the resulting estimates remain independent of the size of the constant matrix
(\mathbb{M})^{\log}_{B}
.
Subsequently, a Vitali covering and exit-time argument on the super level sets of
H(x,\mathbb{M}Du)=|\mathbb{M}Du|^{p}+a(x)\,|\mathbb{M}Du|^{q},

together with pointwise control from the frozen problem and the smallness of
\mathbb{M}-(\mathbb{M})^{\log}_{B}
, produce a good-
\lambda
inequality.
Iterating this inequality gives the
L^{\gamma}_{\mathrm{loc}}
-improvement for all
\gamma>1
.

This work provides the first comprehensive regularity theory for double phase problems with general matrix weights, bridging the gap between classical double phase problems and anisotropic models induced by variable matrices.
The two ingredients - the fractional
\mathcal{A}_{p,s}
and the
\log
-BMO smallness of
\mathbb{M}
- prevent the Lavrentiev gap and recover the classical results as the limit
\mathbb{M}\to{\rm I}_{n}
.

Our paper is organized as follows. Section
2
recalls weighted background (including
\mathcal{A}_{p,s}
) and states the main result. Section
3
treats the absence of the Lavrentiev phenomenon in the matrix-weighted setting and specifies admissible solutions. Section
4
develops higher integrability for the reference problems (with frozen
a_{0}
and frozen
(\mathbb{M})_{B}^{\log}
). Section
5
proves the main theorem by combining the comparison estimates with a covering/level-set iteration.

We close this introduction by noting that the analysis presented here naturally suggests several important directions for future research, including the extension of this matrix-weighted theory to the more general Musielak-Orlicz setting and the investigation of the boundary regularity.

then

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{R/2}}[H(x,\mathbb{M}Du)]^{\gamma}\,dx\leq c\left(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{R}}H(x,\mathbb{M}Du)\,dx\right)^{\gamma}+c\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{R}}[H(x,\mathbb{M}F)]^{\gamma}\,dx

for all balls
B_{R}\subset\Omega_{2\hat{\tau}}
such that
0<R\leq r_{o}\leq 1
,
where
c=c(\texttt{data},\gamma,\hat{\tau},\|H(\cdot,\mathbb{M}F)\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega_{\hat{\tau}})})>0
.

3.
Problem setup

3.1.
Absence of Lavrentiev phenomenon

In this subsection we verify the absence of the Lavrentiev phenomenon
regarding the degenerate/singular problem (
1.1
) to setup our
problem

\displaystyle\cal{H}(v,\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}H(x,\mathbb{M}Dv)\ dx.

(3.1)

for any
v\in W^{1,1}_{{\operatorname{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
H(x,\mathbb{M}Dv)\in L^{1}(\Omega)
under the same structural and regularity requirement as
was identified in
[
16
,
6
]
to be (
1.4
) for the
constant matrix, provided that
\log\mathbb{M}
is sufficiently
small in the sense of
BMO
.

A natural obstruction in obtaining the interior regularity for the gradient
of a minimizer of the functional
\cal{H}
arises when the
associated Lavrentiev phenomenon like

\displaystyle\inf_{v\in X}\cal{H}(v,B)<\inf_{v\in Y}\cal{H}(v,B)

(3.2)

occurs for
B\Subset\Omega
, where

X=W^{1,p}_{\omega^{p}}(\Omega)
and
Y=W^{1,p}_{\omega^{p}}(\Omega)\cap\{v\in W^{1,1}(\Omega):|\mathbb{M}Dv|\in L^{q}(B)\}
.
As mentioned above, the assumptions (
1.2
) and (
1.4
) on
p,q,a

are an unavoidable condition for the absence of the Lavrentiev phenomenon,
even when
\mathbb{M}
is the
n\times n
identity matrix.
In general we require an additional condition on
\mathbb{M}

to avoid the gap in (
3.2
). The following lemma provides a
sufficient condition on
\mathbb{M}
to do this.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
B
and
\tilde{B}
be balls such that
B\Subset\widetilde{B}\Subset\Omega
.
Setting
\omega=|\mathbb{M}|
, suppose that (
1.2
)-(
1.4
), and

\displaystyle|\log\mathbb{M}|_{BMO(\widetilde{B})}\leq\beta\min\left\{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}},\frac{1}{s}\right\},

(3.3)

where
\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\alpha}{nq}
and
\beta
is
given as in Lemma
2.7
. Then for every function
f\in W^{1,p}_{\omega^{p}}(\Omega)
with
H(x,\mathbb{M}Df)\in L^{1}(\widetilde{B})
,
there exists a
sequence
\{f_{k}\}\subset W^{1,\infty}(B)
such that

f_{k}\rightarrow f
in
W^{1,p}_{\omega^{p}}(B)
and

\displaystyle\int_{B}H(x,\mathbb{M}(x)Df_{k}(x))\,dx\rightarrow\int_{B}H(x,\mathbb{M}(x)Df(x))\,dx

as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

We observe from (
1.4
), (
3.3
) and Lemma

2.7
that

\displaystyle p<q\leq s,\quad\omega^{p}\in\cal{A}_{p}(\widetilde{B}),\quad\omega^{q}\in\cal{A}_{q}(\widetilde{B}),\quad\mbox{and}\quad\omega^{s}\in\cal{A}_{p,s}(\widetilde{B}).

(3.4)

We consider a non-negative standard mollifier
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{1}(0))

with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi\,dx=1
. Choosing

\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
so that
B=B_{r}\Subset B_{r+\varepsilon_{0}}\Subset\widetilde{B}\Subset\Omega
, we set

\phi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{n}}\phi(\frac{x}{\varepsilon})
for
x\in B_{\varepsilon}(0)

with
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
. Then we clearly have
\phi_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{\varepsilon}(0))
,

\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx=1
,
0\leq\phi_{\varepsilon}\leq c(n)\varepsilon^{-n}
and
|D\phi_{\varepsilon}|\leq c(n)\varepsilon^{-(n+1)}
.
Now we
consider the mollified function
f_{\varepsilon}(x)=(f\ast\phi_{\varepsilon})(x)
and
introduce the following auxiliary functions

\displaystyle a_{\varepsilon}(x)=\inf_{y\in B_{\varepsilon}(x)}a(y)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad H_{\varepsilon}(x,z)=|z|^{p}+a_{\varepsilon}(x)|z|^{q},

where
x\in B
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We then have that

\displaystyle H(x,\mathbb{M}(x)Df_{\varepsilon}(x))\rightarrow H(x,\mathbb{M}(x)Df(x))\quad\mbox{a.e. in $B$.}

In addition, from the Hölder continuity of
a
in (
1.2
), we deduce
that

\displaystyle H(x,\mathbb{M}(x)Df_{\varepsilon}(x))

\displaystyle\leq H_{\varepsilon}(x,\mathbb{M}(x)Df_{\varepsilon}(x))+|a(x)-a_{\varepsilon}(x)||\mathbb{M}(x)Df_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq H_{\varepsilon}(x,\mathbb{M}(x)Df_{\varepsilon}(x))+c[a]_{0,\alpha}\varepsilon^{\alpha}|\mathbb{M}(x)Df_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{q}.

(3.5)

Meanwhile, in light of maximal operators, together with the choice of
\phi_{\varepsilon}
and
a_{\varepsilon}(x)
, we have that

\displaystyle H_{\varepsilon}(x,\mathbb{M}(x)Df_{\varepsilon}(x))

\displaystyle\leq|\mathbb{M}(x)|^{p}\left(\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}|Df(y)|\phi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\,dy\right)^{p}+a_{\varepsilon}(x)|\mathbb{M}(x)|^{q}\left(\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}|Df(y)|\phi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\,dy\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq c\omega^{p}(x)\left(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}|Df(y)|\,dy\right)^{p}+c\omega^{q}(x)\left(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}a^{\frac{1}{q}}(y)|Df(y)|\,dy\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq c\cal{M}(\mathbbm{1}_{\widetilde{B}}|Df|)^{p}(x)\omega^{p}(x)+c\cal{M}(\mathbbm{1}_{\widetilde{B}}a^{\frac{1}{q}}|Df|)^{q}(x)\omega^{q}(x)

(3.6)

and

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\alpha}|\mathbb{M}(x)Df_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{q}\leq c\bigg(\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}|Df(y)|\,dy\bigg)^{q}\omega(x)^{q}\leq c[\cal{M}_{\alpha/q}(\mathbbm{1}_{\widetilde{B}}Df)(x)\omega(x)]^{q}

(3.7)

for a.e.
x\in B
, where
c=c(n,p,q)>0
. Combining
(
3.5
)-(
3.7
), we discover that

\displaystyle H(x,\mathbb{M}(x)Df_{\varepsilon}(x))

\displaystyle\leq c\cal{M}(\mathbbm{1}_{\widetilde{B}}|Df|)^{p}(x)\omega^{p}(x)+c\cal{M}(\mathbbm{1}_{\widetilde{B}}a^{\frac{1}{q}}|Df|)^{q}(x)\omega^{q}(x)+c[\cal{M}_{\alpha/q}(\mathbbm{1}_{\widetilde{B}}Df)(x)\omega(x)]^{q}

for a.e.
x\in B
, where
c=c(n,p,q,[a]_{0,\alpha})>0
.

We recall
H(x,\mathbb{M}Df)\in L^{1}(\widetilde{B})
, (
1.3
),
(
3.4
) and (4) in Lemma
2.1
, to find that

\displaystyle\cal{M}(\mathbbm{1}_{\widetilde{B}}|Df|)^{p}\omega^{p}+\cal{M}(\mathbbm{1}_{\widetilde{B}}a^{\frac{1}{q}}|Df|)^{q}\omega^{q}\in L^{1}(\widetilde{B}).

Moreover, (
3.4
) and Lemma
2.2
yield that

\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{B}}[\cal{M}_{\alpha/q}(\mathbbm{1}_{\widetilde{B}}Df)(x)\omega(x)]^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\int_{\widetilde{B}}[\cal{M}_{\alpha/q}(\mathbbm{1}_{\widetilde{B}}Df)(x)\omega(x)]^{s}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{q}{s}}

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\int_{\widetilde{B}}|Df|^{p}\omega(x)^{p}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\int_{\widetilde{B}}|\mathbb{M}Df|^{p}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}},

where
c=c(n,p,q,\alpha,\Lambda,|\Omega|)>0
,
which leads to

\displaystyle[\cal{M}_{\alpha/q}(\mathbbm{1}_{\widetilde{B}}Df)\omega]^{q}\in L^{1}(\widetilde{B}).

Choosing a sequence
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}
such that
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, and letting
f_{k}=f_{\varepsilon_{k}}
,
we finally apply the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem to
deduce the conclusion.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Recall
X=W^{1,p}_{\omega^{p}}(\Omega)
,
Y=W^{1,p}_{\omega^{p}}(\Omega)\cap\{v\in W^{1,1}(\Omega):|\mathbb{M}Dv|\in L^{q}(B)\}
and (
3.1
).
Letting
m_{Y}=\displaystyle\inf_{v\in Y}\mathcal{H}(v,B)

we see that

m_{Y}\geq\inf_{u\in X}\mathcal{H}(u,B).

Define the sequentially lower semicontinuous (s.l.s.c.) envelope

\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{Y}=\sup\{\mathcal{G}:X\to[0,\infty]:\mbox{$\mathcal{G}$ is s.l.s.c., $\mathcal{G}\leq\mathcal{H}$ on $Y$}\}.

Since the constant functional
m_{Y}
is s.l.s.c. and satisfies
m_{Y}\leq\mathcal{H}
on
Y
,
it follows
m_{Y}\leq\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{Y}(u,B)
for all
u\in X
and hence,
m_{Y}=\inf_{u\in X}\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{Y}(u,B)
.
Moreover, in view of Lemma
3.1
, for any
u\in X
there is a sequence
\{u_{k}\}\subset W^{1,\infty}(B)
such that

u_{k}\rightarrow u
in
W^{1,p}_{\omega}(B)
and

\mathcal{H}(u_{k},B)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}(u,B)

as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

(3.8)

We extend
\{u_{k}\}
to whole domain so that
\{u_{k}\}\subset W^{1,\infty}(B)\cap Y
under the assumption (
3.3
).
We have from properties of the s.l.s.c. envelope
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{Y}
that

m_{Y}\leq\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{Y}(u,B)\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{Y}(u_{k},B)\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{H}(u_{k},B)\leq\mathcal{H}(u,B)

for any
u\in X
.
Consequently,

\inf_{v\in Y}\mathcal{H}(v,B)=\inf_{u\in X}\mathcal{H}(u,B),

and thus the Lavrentiev phenomenon (
3.2
) is avoided.
For further discussion, see
[
19
,
8
]
.

3.2.
Admissible functions and distributional solutions

In this subsection we briefly clarify admissible functions in the
context of distributions and solution spaces.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
B
and
\tilde{B}
be balls such that
B\Subset\widetilde{B}\Subset\Omega
.
Suppose that (
1.2
)-(
1.4
), (
2.25
), and (
3.3
).
Let
f\in W^{1,1}(B)
with
H(x,\mathbb{M}Df)\in L^{1}(B)
be a distributional solution to the equation

\displaystyle\textnormal{div}\,\mathbb{M}A(x,\mathbb{M}Df)=\textnormal{div}\,\mathbb{M}G(x,\mathbb{M}F)\quad\mbox{in $B$},

where
F:B\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a given function with
H(x,\mathbb{M}F)\in L^{1}(B)
, and
A:B\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a Carathéodory map
with the growth condition

\displaystyle|A(x,z)|\leq L(|z|^{p-1}+a(x)|z|^{q-1})

(3.9)

for a.e.
x\in B
, for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and for some constant
L>0
.
Then there holds

\displaystyle\int_{B}\langle A(x,\mathbb{M}Df),\mathbb{M}D\varphi\rangle\,dx=\int_{B}\langle G(x,\mathbb{M}F),\mathbb{M}D\varphi\rangle\,dx.

(3.10)

for every
\varphi\in W^{1,1}_{0}(B)
with
H(x,\mathbb{M}D\varphi)\in L^{1}(B)
.

Proof.

We may assume that
B=B_{1}(0)
by dilation and translation.
For the result (
3.10
) it suffices to show that there
exists a sequence of functions
\{\varphi_{k}\}\subset C^{\infty}_{0}(B)
such
that

D\varphi_{k}\rightarrow D\varphi
a.e. and
H(x,\mathbb{M}D\varphi_{k})\rightarrow H(x,\mathbb{M}D\varphi)
in
L^{1}(B)
.

(3.11)

Indeed, we have from (
3.9
), (
2.25
) and Young’s inequality

\displaystyle|\langle A(x,\mathbb{M}Df),\mathbb{M}D\varphi_{k}\rangle|\leq c\left[H(x,\mathbb{M}Df)+H(x,\mathbb{M}D\varphi_{k})\right]

and

\displaystyle|\langle G(x,\mathbb{M}F),\mathbb{M}D\varphi_{k}\rangle|\leq c\left[H(x,\mathbb{M}F)+H(x,\mathbb{M}D\varphi_{k})\right]

for some
c=c(L,p,q)>0
.
Using the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem with (
3.11
), this infers that

\displaystyle\langle A(x,\mathbb{M}Df),\mathbb{M}D\varphi_{k}\rangle\rightarrow\langle A(x,\mathbb{M}Df),\mathbb{M}D\varphi\rangle\quad\mbox{strongly in $L^{1}(B)$}

and

\displaystyle\langle G(x,\mathbb{M}F),\mathbb{M}D\varphi_{k}\rangle\rightarrow\langle G(x,\mathbb{M}F),\mathbb{M}D\varphi\rangle\quad\mbox{strongly in $L^{1}(B)$}.

Hence, (
3.10
) follows from

\displaystyle\int_{B}\langle A(x,\mathbb{M}Df),\mathbb{M}D\varphi_{k}\rangle\,dx=\int_{B}\langle G(x,\mathbb{M}F),\mathbb{M}D\varphi_{k}\rangle\,dx.

We now construct the sequence
\{\varphi_{k}\}
, modifying the arguments in the proof of Lemma
3.1
and
[
18
, Proposition 3.1]
.
First, assume that

\varphi\in W^{1,1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
H(x,\mathbb{M}D\varphi)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

with the zero extension of
\varphi
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B
.
The function
a
can be extended to a Hölder continuous function on the whole
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, preserving the seminorm
[a]_{0,\alpha}
.
Consider a family
\{\phi_{\varepsilon}\}
of mollifiers as used in the proof Lemma
3.1
.
For every
\varepsilon\in(0,1/10)
we define

\hat{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(x):=\varphi((1-3\varepsilon)^{-1}x)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\hat{a}_{\varepsilon}(x):=a((1-3\varepsilon)^{-1}x)\quad\mbox{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

Then
{\rm supp}(\hat{\varphi}_{\varepsilon})\subset B_{1-2\varepsilon}(0)
.
We consider the mollified function
\varphi_{\varepsilon}=(\hat{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}\ast\phi_{\varepsilon})\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{1-\varepsilon}(0))
and
introduce the auxiliary functions

\displaystyle a_{\varepsilon}(x)=\inf_{y\in B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\hat{a}_{\varepsilon}(y)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad H_{\varepsilon}(x,z)=|z|^{p}+a_{\varepsilon}(x)|z|^{q},\quad\mbox{for $x\in B$ and $z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$.}

We then have from
|D\varphi_{\varepsilon}-D\varphi|\leq|(D\hat{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}-D\varphi)\ast\phi_{\varepsilon}|+|D\varphi\ast\phi_{\varepsilon}-D\varphi|

\displaystyle H(x,\mathbb{M}(x)D\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x))\rightarrow H(x,\mathbb{M}(x)D\varphi(x))\quad\mbox{a.e. in $B$, up to subsequence.}

Also, by the Hölder continuity of
a
,

\displaystyle H(x,\mathbb{M}(x)D\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x))

\displaystyle\leq H_{\varepsilon}(x,\mathbb{M}(x)D\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x))+c[a]_{0,\alpha}\varepsilon^{\alpha}|\mathbb{M}(x)D\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{q}\quad\mbox{for $x\in B$.}

(3.12)

On the other hand, from the choice of
\phi_{\varepsilon}
,
\hat{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}
and
a_{\varepsilon}(x)
, we have that

\displaystyle H_{\varepsilon}(x,\mathbb{M}(x)D\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x))

\displaystyle\leq|\mathbb{M}(x)|^{p}\left(\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}|D\hat{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(y)|\phi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\,dy\right)^{p}+a_{\varepsilon}(x)|\mathbb{M}(x)|^{q}\left(\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}|D\hat{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(y)|\phi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\,dy\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq c\omega^{p}(x)\left(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}|D\hat{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(y)|\,dy\right)^{p}+c\omega^{q}(x)\left(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\hat{a}_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{q}}(y)|D\hat{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(y)|\,dy\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq c\omega^{p}(x)\left(\frac{1}{1-3\varepsilon}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}|[D\varphi]((1-3\varepsilon)^{-1}y)|\,dy\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+c\omega^{q}(x)\left(\frac{1}{1-3\varepsilon}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}a^{\frac{1}{q}}((1-3\varepsilon)^{-1}y)|[D\varphi]((1-3\varepsilon)^{-1}y)|\,dy\right)^{q}

(3.13)

for some
c=c(n,p,q)>0
.
Setting
\tilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(x):=B_{\varepsilon/(1-3\varepsilon)}(\frac{x}{1-3\varepsilon})
,
we observe

\left|\frac{x}{1-3\varepsilon}-x\right|\leq\frac{3\varepsilon}{1-3\varepsilon}\quad\mbox{for all $x\in B=B_{1}(0)$,}

which follows
\tilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(x)\subset B_{\frac{4\varepsilon}{1-3\varepsilon}}(x)
.
Using this, maximal operators and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/10)
we obtain

\displaystyle\omega^{p}(x)\left(\frac{1}{1-3\varepsilon}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}|[D\varphi]((1-3\varepsilon)^{-1}y)|\,dy\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}\omega^{p}(x)\left(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{\tilde{B}_{\varepsilon}(x)}|D\varphi(s)|\,ds\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq c\omega^{p}(x)\left(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{\frac{4\varepsilon}{1-3\varepsilon}}(x)}|D\varphi(s)|\,ds\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq c\cal{M}(|D\varphi|)^{p}(x)\omega^{p}(x)

(3.14)

for some
c=c(n,p)>0
.
Similarly, we deduce

\displaystyle\omega^{q}(x)\left(\frac{1}{1-3\varepsilon}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}a^{\frac{1}{q}}((1-3\varepsilon)^{-1}y)|[D\varphi]((1-3\varepsilon)^{-1}y)|\,dy\right)^{q}\leq c\cal{M}(a^{\frac{1}{q}}|D\varphi|)^{q}(x)\omega^{q}(x)

(3.15)

for some
c=c(n,q)>0
.
We insert (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) into (
3.13
) to get

\displaystyle H_{\varepsilon}(x,\mathbb{M}(x)D\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x))\leq c\cal{M}(|D\varphi|)^{p}(x)\omega^{p}(x)+c\cal{M}(a^{\frac{1}{q}}|D\varphi|)^{q}(x)\omega^{q}(x)

for
x\in B
, where
c=c(n,p,q)>0
.
We estimate the last term in (
3.12
).
Like the previous method we infer that

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\alpha}|\mathbb{M}(x)D\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{q}\leq c\omega^{q}(x)\bigg(\varepsilon^{\alpha/q}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}|D\hat{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(y)|\,dy\bigg)^{q}\leq c[\cal{M}_{\alpha/q}(D\varphi)(x)\omega(x)]^{q}.

Choosing a sequence
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}
such that
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, we set
\varphi_{k}=\varphi_{\varepsilon_{k}}
.
As the same way in the proof of Lemma
3.1
,
we apply Lemma
2.1
, Lemma
2.2
and the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem to complete the proof.
∎

4.
Properties of solutions to reference problems

In this section we present some properties of solutions to the
reference problems related to our problem (
2.18
) which will be
employed later in proving our main results. To address an
appropriate function space of solutions to our reference problems,
we first introduce a related Musielak-Orlicz space. Let
B
and

\tilde{B}
be balls such that
B\Subset\widetilde{B}\Subset\Omega
.
Under the assumptions (
1.2
)-(
1.4
) and that

\displaystyle|\log\mathbb{M}|_{BMO(\widetilde{B})}\leq W=\beta\min\left\{\frac{1}{s_{o}},\frac{1}{s}\right\},

(4.1)

where
s_{o}\in(p^{\prime},\infty)
as in Lemma
2.4
,
\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\alpha}{nq}
and
\beta
is
given as in Lemma
2.7
, we write

\displaystyle\begin{cases}H_{\mathbb{M}}(x,z)=|\mathbb{M}(x)z|^{p}+a(x)|\mathbb{M}(x)z|^{q}\quad\mbox{for $x\in\Omega$, $z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$ or $\mathbb{R}$,}\\
\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}(x,t)=|t|^{p}\omega^{p}(x)+a(x)|t|^{q}\omega^{q}(x)\quad\mbox{for $x\in\Omega$, $t\in\mathbb{R}$.}\end{cases}

Then we have
H_{\mathbb{M}}(x,z)=H(x,\mathbb{M}z)
and

\displaystyle H_{\mathbb{M}}(x,z)\leq\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}(x,|z|)\leq\Lambda^{q}H_{\mathbb{M}}(x,z)

from (
1.3
). We define the generalized Orlicz space

L^{\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)

\displaystyle L^{\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)=\left\{v\in L^{1}(B):\int_{B}\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}(x,|v|)\,dx<\infty\right\}.

This space is a separable reflexive Banach space
with the Luxemburg norm

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)}=\inf\left\{\varrho>0:\int_{B}\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}\left(x,\frac{|v(x)|}{\varrho}\right)\,dx\leq 1\right\},

and we have that
L^{q}_{\omega^{q}}(B)\subset L^{\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)\subset L^{p}_{\omega^{p}}(B)\subset L^{1}(B)
.
Thanks to

\displaystyle\min\{\lambda^{p}\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}(x,t),\lambda^{q}\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}(x,t)\}\leq\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}(x,\lambda t)\leq\max\{\lambda^{p}\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}(x,t),\lambda^{q}\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}(x,t)\},

for any
\lambda>0
,
we infer from the definition of Luxemburg norm that

\displaystyle\min\left\{\|v\|_{L^{\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)}^{p},\|v\|_{L^{\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)}^{q}\right\}\leq\int_{B}\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}(x,v)\,dx\leq\max\left\{\|v\|_{L^{\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)}^{p},\|v\|_{L^{\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)}^{q}\right\}.

(4.2)

And the Musielak-Orlicz-Sobolev space
W^{1,\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)
consists of
all functions
v\in L^{\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)\cap W^{1,1}(B)
such that
its weak partial derivatives
D_{x_{i}}v
belong to

L^{\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)
, and its norm is defined by

\displaystyle\lVert v\rVert_{W^{1,\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)}=\lVert v\rVert_{L^{\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)}+\lVert|Dv|\rVert_{L^{\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)}.

Moreover,
W^{1,\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}_{0}(B)
is defined as the closure of

C_{0}^{\infty}(B)
in
W^{1,\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)
. This definition for the
zero boundary Sobolev space related to
\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}
is reasonable,
as we see that smooth functions are dense in
W^{1,\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)

like the way in the proof of Lemma
3.1
from
p^{\prime}<s_{o}
and
p<q\leq s
.
In view of Lemma
2.7
, we also note that
the assumptions (
1.2
)-(
1.4
)
and (
4.1
) guarantee the Sobolev Poincaré type inequality in
Lemma
2.4
. For a further discussion on
Musielak-Orlicz spaces and their associated Sobolev spaces, we refer
to
[
36
,
25
,
33
,
9
,
26
]
and the
references therein.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a unique weak solution to (
4.4
).

Proof.

Keeping in mind the condition (
4.1
), we first see that

W^{1,\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}_{0}(B)
is a separable reflexive Banach space
and its norm is equivalently defined by

\|Dv\|_{L^{\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)}
for
v\in W^{1,\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}_{0}(B)
in view of Lemma
2.4
.
Letting
v=h-h_{o}\in W^{1,\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}_{0}(B)
, (
4.4
) is rewritten by

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\textnormal{div}\left(\mathbb{M}A_{p}(x,\mathbb{M}(Dv+Dh_{0}))+a(x)\mathbb{M}A_{q}(x,\mathbb{M}(Dv+Dh_{0}))\right)\hskip-0.85355pt=\hskip-0.85355pt\textnormal{div}\left(\mathbb{M}G(x,\mathbb{M}F_{o})\right)\mbox{ in $B$},\\
v=0\quad\mbox{on $\partial B$}.\end{cases}

(4.5)

Now, define an operator
T:W^{1,\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}_{0}(B)\rightarrow\left(W^{1,\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}_{0}(B)\right)^{\prime}
by

\displaystyle[T(v)](\varphi)=\int_{B}\langle A_{p}(x,\mathbb{M}(Dv+Dh_{0}))+a(x)\mathbb{M}A_{q}(x,\mathbb{M}(Dv+Dh_{0})),\mathbb{M}D\varphi\rangle\,dx.

(4.6)

Then
T
is continuous and monotone from (
2.2
).
We now show that
T
is coercive, i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{[T(v)](v)}{\|v\|}\to\infty\quad\mbox{as $\|v\|\to\infty$,}

(4.7)

where
\|\cdot\|=\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}_{0}(B)}
.
To show this, we use (
2.2
) and Young’s inequality to deduce

\displaystyle[T(v)](v)

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\langle A_{p}(x,\mathbb{M}(Dv+Dh_{0}))+a(x)\mathbb{M}A_{q}(x,\mathbb{M}(Dv+Dh_{0})),\mathbb{M}Dv\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\langle A_{p}(x,\mathbb{M}(Dv+Dh_{0}))+a(x)\mathbb{M}A_{q}(x,\mathbb{M}(Dv+Dh_{0})),\mathbb{M}(Dv+Dh_{0})\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\int_{B}\langle A_{p}(x,\mathbb{M}(Dv+Dh_{0}))+a(x)\mathbb{M}A_{q}(x,\mathbb{M}(Dv+Dh_{0})),\mathbb{M}Dh_{0}\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle\geq c\int_{B}H(x,\mathbb{M}Dv)\,dx-c\int_{B}H(x,\mathbb{M}Dh_{0})\,dx

(4.8)

for some
c=c(p,q,L,\nu)>0
.
According to (
4.2
) this leads to

\displaystyle[T(v)](v)\geq c\min\left\{\|Dv\|_{L^{\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)}^{p},\|Dv\|_{L^{\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)}^{q}\right\}-c\|H(\cdot,\mathbb{M}Dh_{0})\|_{L^{1}(B)}

(4.9)

This follows from
1<p<q

\displaystyle\frac{[T(v)](v)}{\|v\|}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c\min\left\{\|Dv\|_{L^{\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)}^{p},\|Dv\|_{L^{\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)}^{q}\right\}-c\|H(\cdot,\mathbb{M}Dh_{0})\|_{L^{1}(B)}}{\|Dv\|_{L^{\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)}}\to\infty

(4.10)

as
\|Dv\|_{L^{\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}(B)}\to\infty
.
Emplying Minty-Browder Theorem (
[
38
, Theorem 2.2, Ch. II]
) and considering

[F_{o}]\in\left(W^{1,\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}_{0}(B)\right)^{\prime}
defined by

\displaystyle[F_{o}](\varphi)=\int_{B}\langle G(x,\mathbb{M}F_{o}),\mathbb{M}D\varphi\rangle\,dx,

(4.11)

there is a weak solution
v\in W^{1,\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}_{0}(B)
to (
4.5
).
Therefore, we obtain a weak solution
h=v+h_{0}
to (
4.4
).

We next show that a weak solution to (
4.4
) is unique.
Let
h_{1},h_{2}\in h_{0}+W^{1,\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{M}}}_{0}(B)
be weak solutions to (
4.4
).
Testing
h_{1}-h_{2}
to (
4.4
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{B}\langle A_{p}(x,\mathbb{M}Dh_{1})+a(x)\mathbb{M}A_{q}(x,\mathbb{M}Dh_{1}),\mathbb{M}(Dh_{1}-Dh_{2})\rangle\,dx=\int_{B}\langle G(x,\mathbb{M}F_{o}),\mathbb{M}(Dh_{1}-Dh_{2})\rangle\,dx

and

\displaystyle\int_{B}\langle A_{p}(x,\mathbb{M}Dh_{2})+a(x)\mathbb{M}A_{q}(x,\mathbb{M}Dh_{2}),\mathbb{M}(Dh_{1}-Dh_{2})\rangle\,dx=\int_{B}\langle G(x,\mathbb{M}F_{o}),\mathbb{M}(Dh_{1}-Dh_{2})\rangle\,dx.

This follows from (
4.3
)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\langle A_{p}(x,\mathbb{M}Dh_{1})-\mathbb{M}A_{p}(x,\mathbb{M}Dh_{2}),\mathbb{M}(Dh_{1}-Dh_{2})\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int_{B}a(x)\langle A_{q}(x,\mathbb{M}Dh_{1})-\mathbb{M}A_{q}(x,\mathbb{M}Dh_{2}),\mathbb{M}(Dh_{1}-Dh_{2})\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle\geq c\int_{B}|V_{p}(\mathbb{M}Dh_{1})-V_{p}(\mathbb{M}Dh_{2})|^{2}+a(x)|V_{q}(\mathbb{M}Dh_{1})-V_{q}(\mathbb{M}Dh_{2})|^{2}\,dx

for some
c=c(n,\nu,p,q)>0
,
and hence

.
∎

Lemma 4.2

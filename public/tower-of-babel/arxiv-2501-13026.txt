Stability of the Mott phase in excitonic double layers
Klaus Ziegler 1 and Roman Ya. Kezerashvili 2,3

Abstract
We study the stability of excitonic Mott phases in the presence of a periodic potential and a non-local exciton-exciton interaction. The non-local interaction is treated in a mean-field approximation, while the local repulsion of the excitons is treated in a hopping expansion. The convergence of the latter is the criterion for the stability of the Mott phase with respect to quantum and thermal fluctuations. This hybrid approach enables us to establish a phase diagram for a bosonic Mott to superfluid transition. Our results could be useful to interpret recent experiments on electron-hole crystals in van der Waals heterostructures. Key words: graphene/ α \alpha -RuCl 3 , excitons, Mott phase, superfluid phase.

Without refering to a specific microscopic mechanism, an effective model for the description of an
excitonic gas in a double layer must include a mean-field approximation for the exciton density
and the hard-core repulsion of the excitons due to the Pauli principle. The latter is crucial for
the description of quantum tunneling in the excitonic gas.
Using such a model, a central question is the stability of an ordered state of a 2D system in
terms of quantum fluctuations.
For a Mott state, the phase fluctuations due to quantum tunneling are responsible for its instability.
In other words, we can assume an ordered Mott state on a lattice in mean-field approximation and analyze
its stability under an increasing rate of quantum tunneling. This will be the subject of this work.

II
Model

For the following we assume that the electron-hole binding energy of the excitons exceeds all other energy
scales in our model, such as the tunneling energy, implying that the excitons cannot dissociate.
This assumption is justified by a strong Coulomb interaction
BKZ2013
.
Moreover, the time scale of the observation is shorter than the recombination time such that the
electron and holes cannot transform into photons by recombination.
The implementation of a lattice structure in the layers
enables the excitons to form a commensurate Mott state by filling each well of the lattice with an exciton.
A lattice can be realized either by the host material or by an electrically charged gate which is periodically
structured
BKLZ PRB2019
. The gate is used to control the density of excitons
via its chemical potential
\mu
. Then a pure excitonic gas in a periodic potential
can be described by the many-body Hamiltonian

H=-\sum_{{\bf r}{\bf r}^{\prime}}(J_{{\bf r}{\bf r}^{\prime}}+\bar{\mu}_{\bf r}\delta_{{\bf r}{\bf r}^{\prime}})a_{\bf r}^{\dagger}a_{{\bf r}^{\prime}}+h.c.

(1)

where the sites
{\bf r}
,
{\bf r}^{\prime}
are the minima of the potential wells and
J_{{\bf r}{\bf r}^{\prime}}
is a nearest
neighbor tunneling (or hopping) rate:

J_{{\bf r}{\bf r}^{\prime}}=\cases{J,&if ${\bf r},{\bf r}^{\prime}$ are nearest neighbors,\cr 0&otherwise.\cr}

(2)

The lattice structure is characterized by the number of the nearest neighbors, which is the connectivity
c
,
and by the periodic chemical potential
\bar{\mu}_{\bf r}
with period
R
:

\bar{\mu}_{{\bf r}+R{\bf e}_{\nu}}=\bar{\mu}_{\bf r}
with the unit vector of the lattice
{\bf e}_{\nu}
.
The exciton creation operator
a^{\dagger}_{r}
is composed of the electron creation operator

c^{\dagger}_{e,{\bf r}}
and the hole creation operator
c^{\dagger}_{h,{\bf r}}
as

a^{\dagger}_{\bf r}=c^{\dagger}_{e,{\bf r}}c^{\dagger}_{h,{\bf r}}
. The form of the exciton operator
a^{\dagger}_{\bf r}

implies that the excitons can tunnel freely with the hopping rate
J_{{\bf r}{\bf r}^{\prime}}
under the restriction that they
are composite particles which obey the Pauli principle:
a^{\dagger}_{\bf r}a^{\dagger}_{\bf r}=0
. In other words,
at most one exciton can occupy
a site of the lattice. Therefore, it is a hardcore boson, similar to the exciton described
by the effective Hamiltonian of
Refs.
HANAMURA1977209
;
https://doi.org/10.1002/andp.20085200804
, and
a lattice system of strongly bound excitons is effectively a gas of hard-core bosons. At
first glance, this excitonic gas has two complementary ground states, either a Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC)
or a Mott state with at most one exciton per lattice site
https://doi.org/10.1002/andp.20085200804
.
In the absence of a non-local exciton-exciton interaction
and an additional periodic single-particle potential (
i.e.
, for
\mu_{\bf r}=\mu
),
there is only a single Mott state with one exciton per site. On the other hand, with a repulsive
exciton-exciton interaction or a periodic single-particle potential (
i.e.
, for a spatially periodic
\mu_{\bf r}
)
different Mott states are possible with commensurate fillings of the lattice.

The BEC is described by a complex single-particle wavefunction

\Psi_{\bf r}=n_{\bf r}e^{i\varphi_{\bf r}}
, which is the solution of the Gross-Pitaevskii or nonlinear
Schrödinger equation. Here
n_{\bf r}
is the excitonic density and
\varphi_{\bf r}
is determined up to a
uniform phase shift
\varphi_{\bf r}+{\bar{\varphi}}
. The latter characterizes the BEC as a state
with global spontaneous symmetry breaking because a physical state must be unique. However,
the phase shift cannot be directly accessed experimentally such that there is no
need to fix it in the theory.
On the other hand, the Mott state with one particle per site is described by a many-body product state

\Psi_{{\bf r}_{1},\ldots,{\bf r}_{N}}=\prod_{j=1}^{N}e^{i\varphi_{{\bf r}_{j}}}.

(3)

In contrast to the BEC, the phases
\{\varphi_{{\bf r}_{j}}\}

are random with spatial correlations, which decay exponentially.
Other Mott states with partial lattice filling are product states with respect to the occupied lattice sites.
These complementary states should be approximative solutions of a linear Schrödinger equation
for many particles with either the Hamiltonian (
1
) or with the extended
Hamiltonian

H^{\prime}=-\sum_{{\bf r},{\bf r}^{\prime}}[J_{{\bf r}{\bf r}^{\prime}}+(\mu-V_{\bf r})\delta_{{\bf r}{\bf r}^{\prime}}]a_{\bf r}^{\dagger}a_{{\bf r}^{\prime}}+\sum_{{\bf r},{\bf r}^{\prime}{}}U_{{\bf r}{\bf r}^{\prime}{}}a_{\bf r}^{\dagger}a_{{\bf r}}a_{{\bf r}^{\prime}{}}^{\dagger}a_{{\bf r}^{\prime}{}}+h.c.,

(4)

where the solutions for
H^{\prime}
depend on the bare chemical potential
\mu
, the excitonic
hopping rate
J
, the lattice potential
V_{\bf r}
, and the non-local exciton-exciton interaction
U_{{\bf r}{\bf r}^{\prime}}
.
The Hamiltonian
H^{\prime}
is linked with the Hamiltonian (
1
)
via a mean-field approximation. This will be discussed in the next section. In particular,
the effective chemical potential
{\bar{\mu}}_{\bf r}
of
H
will be calculated and the properties
of the Mott phase will be determined within the mean-field approximation.

III
Excitonic Mott phase

Assuming
J=0
, therefore, neglecting quantum tunneling, excitons are classical particles with density

0\leq n_{\bf r}\leq 1
, whose energy reads

E=-\sum_{\bf r}(\mu-V_{\bf r})n_{\bf r}+\sum_{{\bf r},{\bf r}^{\prime}{}}U_{{\bf r}{\bf r}^{\prime}{}}n_{{\bf r}^{\prime}{}}n_{\bf r}.

(5)

At low temperatures the excitons will form configurations
\{n_{\bf r}\}
on the lattice with minimal energy.
A variational approach can be employed, which gives for a translational-invariant interaction

U_{{\bf r}{\bf r}^{\prime}{}}\equiv U_{{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}{}}
the exciton density (cf. Appendix
A
)

\bar{n}_{\bf r}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\mu}{{\tilde{U}}_{0}}-W_{\bf r}\right)\Theta\left(2-\frac{\mu}{{\tilde{U}}_{0}}+W_{\bf r}\right)\ \ {\rm with}\ \ W_{\bf r}=\int_{\bf k}e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}}\frac{{\tilde{V}}_{\bf k}}{{\tilde{U}}_{\bf k}},

(6)

where
{\tilde{V}}_{\bf k}
and
{\tilde{U}}_{\bf k}
are the Fourier components of the periodic
V_{\bf r}

and the non-local exciton interaction
U_{{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}{}}
potentials, respectively, and

\Theta(x)
is the Heavyside function. This function
is the result of a quantum effect, namely the Pauli principle of the fermionic constituents of the
exciton (cf. Appendix
A
). We note that the spatial variation of the density
is determined by the effective potential
W_{\bf r}
, which depends on the lattice
potential
V_{\bf r}
and
U_{{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}}
.
In terms of a mean-field approximation this provides, assuming
\mu/{\tilde{U}}_{0}-W_{\bf r}<2
,
an effective chemical potential

{\bar{\mu}}_{\bf r}=\mu-V_{\bf r}-\sum_{{\bf r}^{\prime}}U_{{\bf r}{\bf r}^{\prime}}{\bar{n}}_{{\bf r}^{\prime}}=\frac{\mu-V_{\bf r}}{2}.

(7)

This yields eventually the effective Hamiltonian (
1
).
We note that only
V_{\bf r}
enters
H
directly, while the interaction contributes only
indirectly through the renormalization factor
1/2
.
For a weak hopping rate
J>0
this Hamiltonian can be used to employ a hopping expansion.
Absolute convergence of this expansion in powers of
J

exists under the condition
doi:10.1080/14786435.2016.1161860

c\beta J(1+e^{\beta J})<1+e^{\beta{\hat{\mu}}},

(8)

where
{\hat{\mu}}=\min_{\bf r}{\bar{\mu}}_{\bf r}
. At low temperature, which corresponds to large
\beta
,
this inequality implies that
\beta{\hat{\mu}}>\beta J\gg 1
is
sufficient for convergence.
{\hat{\mu}}>J
agrees with the simple argument that the Mott phase
is stable when the kinetic energy, represented by
J
, wins over the interacting energy,
represented by the effective chemical potential
{\hat{\mu}}
.
For a convergent hopping expansion, implied by the condition in Eq. (
8
),
we can return to the expression of the exciton density in Eq. (
6
). Corrections to this
expression due to hopping are small and will not destroy the Mott phase. Therefore,
{\bar{n}}_{\bf r}

describes the stable Mott phase, which is experimentally observable, for example, by
tunneling microscopy
Qiu2024
.

IV
Discussion and conclusions

Our approach of the previous section is based on a separation of the interaction
and the hopping term. The latter was considered as a perturbation, while the
exciton-exciton interaction was treated in a mean-field approximation. The effective
chemical potential
{\bar{\mu}}_{\bf r}
of Eq. (
7
) yields

{\hat{\mu}}=\mu/2
if
V_{\bf r}\geq 0
for the condition (
8
).
The effective potential
W_{\bf r}
, which represents the interplay of a microsopic
lattice potential
V_{\bf r}
and the interaction
U_{{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}}
in Eq. (
6
),
is independent of the bare
\mu
. For a known interaction
U_{{\bf r}}
and a known
potential
V_{\bf r}
,
W_{\bf r}
can be determined by integration. Without knowing these
quantities, however, we can also assume, for instance, a periodic function

W_{\bf r}=w_{0}\cos x\cos y+w_{1}\cos(ax)\cos(ay)\ \ ({\bf r}=(x,y)),

(9)

where the coefficients
w_{0}
and
w_{1}
depend on the microscopic details of the system.
Then we can calculate the exciton density of Eq. (
6
) as a function of
\mu
.
Some examples are presented in Fig.
2
for different values of
the renormalized chemical potential
\mu^{\prime}=\mu/{\tilde{U}}_{0}
. The density is quite sensitive to a
change of
\mu^{\prime}
, which results in a structural change of the Mott insulator. This is
reminiscent of the experimental result of Ref.
Qiu2024
.

We conclude that the excitonic Mott phase can be calculated within a combination
of mean-field approximation of the exciton-exciton interaction and a convergent
perturbative expansion in terms of the excitonic hopping. The latter provides
an estimation of the stability of the Mott phase with a phase diagram visualized
in Fig.
3
. The excitonic density is controlled by the
renormalized chemical potential
\mu/{\tilde{U}}_{0}
, which depends on the electric potential
of the external metallic gate on top of the graphene layer in a gated vdW heterostructure.

Appendix A
Mean-field approach

The variation of the classical energy in Eq. (
5
) with respect to
n_{\bf r}
reads

\partial_{n_{\bf r}}E=-\mu+V_{\bf r}+2\sum_{{\bf r}^{\prime}{}}U_{{\bf r}{\bf r}^{\prime}{}}n_{{\bf r}^{\prime}{}}=0.

(10)

After a Fourier transformation
{\bf r}\to{\bf k}
we can solve this equation for each
{\bf k}
as

{\tilde{n}}_{\bf k}=\frac{\mu\delta_{{\bf k}0}-{\tilde{V}}_{\bf k}}{2{\tilde{U}}_{\bf k}}.

(11)

This is valid when the right-hand side is non-negative. If it is negative for
{\bf k}={\bf q}
we get

{\tilde{n}}_{\bf q}=0
. For the subsequent calculation we assume that the right-hand is non-negative.
Then the inverse Fourier transformation of
{\tilde{n}}_{\bf k}
in Eq. (
11
) gives

{\bar{n}}_{\bf r}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\mu}{{\tilde{U}}_{0}}-W_{\bf r}\right)\ \ {\rm with}\ \ W_{\bf r}=\int_{\bf k}e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}}\frac{{\tilde{V}}_{\bf k}}{{\tilde{U}}_{\bf k}},

(12)

which can be used to define the mean-field energy

{\bar{E}}=-\sum_{\bf r}(\mu-V_{\bf r})n_{\bf r}+\sum_{{\bf r},{\bf r}^{\prime}{}}U_{{\bf r}{\bf r}^{\prime}{}}{\bar{n}}_{{\bf r}^{\prime}{}}n_{\bf r}=-\sum_{\bf r}\left[\mu-V_{\bf r}-\sum_{{\bf r}^{\prime}}U_{{\bf r}{\bf r}^{\prime}}{\bar{n}}_{{\bf r}^{\prime}}\right]n_{\bf r}=:-\sum_{\bf r}\bar{\mu}_{\bf r}n_{\bf r}.

(13)

Moreover, after performing the summation

\sum_{{\bf r}^{\prime}}U_{{\bf r}{\bf r}^{\prime}}{\bar{n}}_{{\bf r}^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}(\mu-V_{\bf r}),

(14)

we get for the effective chemical potential

\bar{\mu}_{\bf r}=\frac{\mu-V_{\bf r}}{2}.

(15)

Thus, the effect of the repulsive exciton-exciton interaction on the mean-field level is a reduction
factor
1/2
for the effective excitonic chemical potential. Finally, we must implement the Pauli
principle, which restricts the density to
0\leq n_{\bf r}\leq 1
. This can be achieved within the
mean-field approach by the heuristic expression

{\bar{n}}_{\bf r}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\mu}{{\tilde{U}}_{0}}-W_{\bf r}\right)\Theta(2-\frac{\mu}{{\tilde{U}}_{0}}+W_{\bf r}),

(16)

where
\Theta(x)
is the Heavyside function.

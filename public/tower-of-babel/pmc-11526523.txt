Cost management analysis of Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE): guide to the universities of medical sciences
Authors: Kheizaran Miri, Tahere Sarboozi Hoseinabadi, Ali Yaghobi, Sadaf Kholosi, Mehdi Miri
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE) is a crucial assessment tool for evaluating learners’ ability to apply theoretical knowledge in practical clinical situations. It is widely accepted by both students and educators, and the costs associated with conducting OSCE assessments vary depending on the field of study, how it is implemented, staffing needs, standardized patients, and duration of the examination. This study examines the expenses related to administering OSCEs in medical universities.MethodConducted from June to September 2023; this mixed-method study elucidated the cost intricacies of executing a two-day OSCE with 14 eight-minute stations for 100 nursing students. This process unfolds in two phases: a qualitative segment comprising text reviews and 45-minute in-depth interviews with faculty members and OSCE experts, leading to the development of a validated checklist, followed by a quantitative phase in which the tool was distributed to 25 faculty members and 5 specialists for completion.ResultThe examination costs were delineated into three primary components: time, human resources, equipment, consumables, and necessary supplies. In 2023, the total implementation cost of the OSCE for 100 students across 14 clinical stations was $1028.07, with an estimated per-learner cost of $37.50. Human resources incurred the highest expenditure ($1649.37); while supply costs were relatively lower ($1072.17). Educational infrastructure expenses were excluded because the study focused on the Nursing and Midwifery Faculty’s Clinical Skills Center.ConclusionVarious factors influence OSCE costs, including national production capabilities of medical supplies, institutional credibility, governance status, examination frequency, student demographics, assessor composition, station count, course content, and examinee volume. The insights derived from this comprehensive examination are significant as entry benchmarks for healthcare systems and higher-level academic evaluations. Understanding OSCE cost dynamics facilitates resource optimization and assessment strategy refinement, thereby improving medical education efficacy.

Background

The primary mission of medical education is to train competent and skilled human resources for healthcare systems [1]. Therefore, enhancing of clinical skills among graduates is of particular concern [2]. Clinical education is an indispensable component of healthcare professional curricula, to acquire the minimum expected competencies from graduates, which is referred to as clinical competence. This includes the ability to apply clinical, communication, patient care, health promotion, and prevention skills, as well as decision-making, reasoning, and problem-solving skills [3, 4]. In the pursuit of educating capable, responsible, and responsive graduates to meet the needs of society in delivering healthcare services to the public, in addition to clinical assessments and evaluations, it is necessary to conduct a clinical competence examination after the internship phase [4, 5]. Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE) is a useful tool for assessing clinical competence. Introduced in the 1970s by Harden and Gleeson, this assessment is highly regarded by medical education experts for its validity and reliability in assessing clinical skills [6].

An OSCE typically consists of multiple stations, each of which focuses on a specific skill or scenario [7]. In this examination, various skills, such as communication, clinical examination, and care and treatment methods, are assessed [3, 8]. However, the costs associated with conducting such examinations significantly impact universities [9]. Because the cost-effectiveness of the assessment process is a crucial factor in determining its utility [10], the need for cost management in this area arises. Cost management is a key element in university financial planning, and given medical universities’ limited resources, budgeting expenses for educational administrators are of special importance [11].

Based on the results of the conducted research, significant expenses are incurred by universities for administering the Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE) [11, 12]. Cost management of OSCE in medical universities involves analysis and breakdown of expenses associated with the execution of the examination [13]. These expenses encompass costs related to the payment of personnel involved in the examination, consumables, necessary equipment and technology, venue rental costs, communication expenses, and registration and examination fees borne by the participants in the examination [9, 14]0.6.

Medical universities, as one of the foremost institutions for education and research, typically set high standards for the OSCE examination to attract highly qualified students with strong academic capabilities [15]. Therefore, determining examination levels has a significant impact on cost management and financial optimization for universities, to optimize financial use in other areas being an important necessity [16]. This is despite the limited number of recent studies regarding the financial costs of OSCE examinations and the diverse financial structures of medical universities in different countries, which demonstrate varying financial outcomes [10, 17].

Furthermore, the cost of this assessment varies depending on the nature of each field, execution method (including the number of stations and assessed skills), personnel, number of patients, and time required for The examination encompasses the stages of preparation before, during, and after the examination [18, 19]. On the other hand, the presence of hidden costs in the administration of OSCE examinations which have not been previously addressed, creates discrepancies between budget approvers in education and examination organizers. Studies have shown that inadequate cost estimation for examination administration by senior managers and insufficient budget al.location by executive managers can lead to problems such as a decline in the quality and scientific credibility of the examinations. Of the main reasons for this issue is insufficient evidence and limited documentation available for justifying and explaining the budget to strategic managers [9, 20].

By conducting a comprehensive cost management analysis, medical universities can make informed decisions to optimize their resources and ensure effective and efficient management of the Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE) while maintaining educational quality. This analysis can assist universities in identifying the most cost effective and efficient approaches for organizing and administering OSCE. Additionally, this information can be valuable for stakeholders in medical education, policymakers, and educators when making informed decisions regarding assessment methods and resource allocation [11, 21, 22]. Therefore, the researchers in this study estimated the financial cost of an OSCE examination for 100 nursing and midwifery students in eastern Iran.

Method

This was a two-phase mixed-method study (Fig. 1) conducted from June 2023 to September 2023. This study describes the costs of implementing a two-day OSCE with 14 eight-minute stations for 100 nursing students at the School of Nursing and Midwifery.

Phase 1: qualitative research

In the first phase (the qualitative study section), an analysis of the concept of ‘cost management of the Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE)’ was conducted among undergraduate nursing students. To achieve this, data were obtained through two steps: literature review and interviews. Initially, relevant texts and articles were searched on reputable scientific websites using appropriate keywords. The time frame selected for the article search was from 2000 to 2023. Then, final articles, and reference books were selected for analysis, including guidelines for conducting OSCE exams (AMEE) [4–6]. After obtaining approval from the educational group of the School of Nursing and Midwifery at Torbat Heydariyeh University of Medical Sciences and coordination with the Clinical Skills Center, data were collected through semi-structured and unstructured interviews with professors, exam organizers, and nursing undergraduate students. During this phase, we conducted interviews with four professors, five examination organizers, two subject matter experts, and five undergraduate nursing students. The interviews lasted between 30 and 45 min each. The interview data saturation was reached. Some interview questions were included: “In your experience, which aspects of the OSCE incur costs?” (Asked of professors or exam organizers) and “What components do you believe contribute to the cost of the OSCE exam?”

Additionally, the responsible person at the Clinical Skills Center was consulted to prepare a list of necessary infrastructures. These infrastructures include the physical examination, space exam, equipment, and mannequins required at each clinical station. These data were analyzed and reviewed during the final focus group session of the nursing group, which included specialists, faculty members, relevant experts, and student representatives.

Phase 2: quantitative research

In the second phase (quantitative study section), a tool was designed to measure this concept using the definitions provided in the first phase (literature review and interviews) conducting the OSCE exam, and its validity and reliability were assessed. The validity of this tool were proposed confirmed by based content validity. Initially, a checklist was designed based on the review of texts, reference books, and articles on of OSCE exams, the opinion of experts and faculty members. Content validity was assessed by 10 members of the faculty of the School of Nursing and Midwifery. After collecting feedback, necessary revisions were made. The Content Validity Index (CVI) was calculated to be 0.89, and the reliability of this tool was confirmed with a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.86. This checklist included three sections related to the necessary infrastructures for the OSCE, costs related to human resources, their time, and the costs of equipment and consumables. In this tool, considering the 14 designed station, 41 items were identified for the equipment and consumables section. The human resources section, the cost and time spent on students, faculty members, experts and coordinating managers were calculated.

Participant

This study involved the participation of 25 faculty members, 5 specialists (Clinical Skills Center and educational group, accounting and financial affairs), and 5 experienced OSCE nursing students from the School of Nursing and Midwifery. The inclusion criteria for this study required individuals to have at least one previous experience of participating in an OSCE exam. The exclusion criteria were unwillingness to participate in the study.

Data collection

Participants completed the researcher-made “costs related to OSCE” checklist. The checklist was completed by 25 faculty members, 5 specialists, and 5 students for 100 nursing students, with guidance from experienced OSCE faculty members, managers, students, and relevant exam organizers. In addition, the Financial and Accounting Unit of the School of Nursing and Midwifery was visited to review the expenses incurred for the OSCE exam. The examination expenses include personnel costs, expenses related to executive affairs such as transportation of required equipment to the exam site, transportation of personnel and students to the exam site, internet expenses for exam organization before, during, and after the examination and expenses for hosting personnel and students durin examination exam. Personnel time costs include the time faculty members spend studying reputable articles to enhance their knowledge, designing questions and exam scenarios, conducting coordination and competency validation sessions, reviewing and accepting or rejecting questions, evaluating and preparing equipment and consumables for the exam, coordinating the exam, guiding and answering questions, and correcting and evaluating students’ responses to scenarios and questions. The total time spent is multiplied by a specified price rate for members to calculate the corresponding cost, which is then approved by the inspection committee members as agreed and reached.

Data analysis

The data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, including frequency distribution tables, means, and standard deviations. The analysis was conducted using SPSS software version 22.

Discussion

This study was conducted with the aim of estimating the costs of administering a standard clinical examination for a nursing student in 2023. These costs encompass three main components: human resources, consumable items and expenses related to executive matters. The highest cost associated with the examination pertained to human resources, while the lowest cost was attributed to procurement.

The cost of the OSCE examinations was calculated in two scenarios, minimum and maximum, based on the present study. The difference between these two scenarios lies in the component of “availability of existing educational infrastructure and the composition and type of human resources collaborating in the OSCE.” In the minimum-cost scenario, educational institutions or centers that already possess well-defined infrastructure and efficient human resources (previously incurred costs) consider the costs of these components as zero. Conversely, the maximum-cost scenario occurs when two crucial components, the availability of existing educational infrastructure and the composition and type of human resources collaborating in the OSCE, are absent and the organizing body of the examination needs to provide this component through rental or service purchase methods. As a result, these two factors are added to the total examination costs, which in this study, according to estimates made by relevant experts, amounted to a sum equivalent to $29,268.30.

Managers are always looking to use financial resources more efficiently and reduce costs. Making small changes to how things are done can help with this [23–28]. By taking a long-term view and bringing in experts at clinical skills and training centers who know about equipment maintenance, managers can save money. This means the equipment stays healthy, avoiding extra repair or buying costs. A study by Moradhasel (2018) supports this idea. Therefore, it’s a good idea to make a long-term plan for hiring these experts at the clinical skills center, focusing specifically on equipment maintenance. This will help an organization save money in the long run [29–32].

Another significant factor influencing the cost of the OSCE examination is the academic level and qualifications of postgraduate students in medical universities. In universities with higher academic levels and competence, postgraduate students (master’s and doctoral students) are employed for conducting examinations and clinical training of specialists as part of their educational units. Therefore, the cost of human resources in conducting examinations, in cases where higher-year students are employed will be lower than the cost of faculty members alone. Conversely, in universities with lower levels of competence, the opportunity to employ higher-year students in the examination process is absent, and this role is fulfilled by the faculty. Another factor, “the composition of the human resources,” during the OSCE examination is demonstrated by the experience of researchers in this study, showing that if a combination of faculty members and specialists is used as examiners, the cost of the examination will be lower. However, this approach must be implemented in a way that does not compromise the quality of student assessment. Carpenter’s 1995 study suggested using a checklist of experienced experts for simple skills and engaging faculty members, and experienced nurses or physicians for complex skills that require clinical judgment and critical thinking by the examinees due to the diversity of simplicity and complexity in clinical skills during examinations [10, 33, 34].

Furthermore, since human resources constitute the most expensive component of the total costs associated with conducting a clinical examination, if final-year students can be utilized as simulated patients within an educational program, examination costs can be observed to some reduced [21].

The third influential component in the cost of OSCE examinations is “consumable and non-consumable equipment and required devices” at OSCE clinical stations. The capacity for domestic production of equipment such as defibrillators and infusion pumps, etc., varies from one country to another. Using domestically manufactured equipment results in lower cost than using to foreign equipment. If the quality of domestic and foreign equipment is similar, and there is no compromise in the quality of examination execution, it reduces the consumable equipment costs. Additionally, the present study indicated that the number of clinical skill stages and the required equipment at each clinical station had an impact on the cost of conducting the examination. In this regard, the type of academic discipline of students, whether general or specialized, is among other variables that significantly affect OSCE costs [22].

Given the recent upward trend in technological advancements and the integration of artificial intelligence in education and clinical skill assessment, educational systems have increasingly incorporated technologies such as simulators, ventilators, and CPR training devices over the past five years [23–26]. These technologies have been employed in some clinical stations and have contributed to reducing OSCE examination costs. However, for certain clinical skills, studies have indicated that the use of simulators [27] and technology in the infrastructure preparation stage poses significant challenges and high costs for administrators. Therefore, assessing the effectiveness of advanced technology use requires further research. The approach enables medical schools to make informed decisions regarding budget al.location for OSCE examination expenses, whether to decrease or increase them [5, 28].

In conclusion, our study provides new insights into the costs associated with conducting a clinical examination in a way that emphasizes the importance of cost reduction across various areas. However, ensuring that cost reduction measures do not compromise the quality and credibility of the examination is crucial.

Limitation

Clinical examination is akin to stage similar theater performance, involving expenses incurred before, during, and after the event. We have made efforts to accurately account for all costs associated with conducting a clinical examination. However, since this study was conducted for the first time, more time and equipment were required to obtain the initial data. Furthermore, instability and price variability in the consumables and equipment sector present challenges that researchers have continuously grappled with.

Conclusion

The influential factors affecting the cost of the OSCE examination include the country’s capacity to produce medical consumables and non-consumables, the credibility and scientific standing of medical faculties, whether the examination is organized by government or private entities, the frequency of OSCE administration by faculties, the presence of postgraduate and higher-level students, the type and composition of examination participants (professionals, faculty members, etc.), the number of clinical stations, the content of general or specialized station courses, the total number of stations and the number of candidates. The valuable results obtained from this costly examination can be used as input indicators in healthcare systems or higher-level scientific assessments. Therefore, this study can be instrumental for stakeholders in medical education, policymakers, and instructors in making informed decisions regarding assessment methods and resource allocation.

\begin{split}&(\partial_{t}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{xx})u=0,\hskip 38.99998ptx>\sigma_{t},\ t>0,\\
&u(\sigma_{t}+,t)=2,\hskip 56.00014ptt>0,\\
&\textstyle\frac{d}{dt}\sigma_{t}=-\frac{1}{4}\partial_{x}u(\sigma_{t}+,t),\hskip 18.99995ptt>0\\

Whereas the problem is well-posed in the melting case, the supercooled case is ill-posed: In some instances of this problem, classical global solutions do not exist, as singularities may appear in finite time; moreover, the proof of uniqueness may differ depending on the details of the problem. For this reason, the melting and supercooled cases were treated separately in
[
6
]
as far as uniqueness of weak solutions is concerned, and moreover, in the latter case, uniqueness was proved in a restricted function class, tailored to the particular details of the problem. For another example of a particle system characterized at the hydrodynamic limit by a Stefan problem, with uniqueness in the supercooled case requiring a restricted function class, see
[
8
]

Our goal here is to extend the convergence result of
[
6
]
to initial configurations that need not form a PPP and, more importantly, for which the limiting measure
\mu_{0}
need not have a constant density (in fact, it may not be absolutely continuous w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure). When
\mu_{0}
has a density bounded below or above by
2

Our approach is based on a measure-theoretic notion of a FBP introduced in
[
2
]
, where it was used to characterize the hydrodynamic limit of a branching Brownian particle system with selection. An advantage of this formulation is that it enables a treatment that does not require free boundary monotonicity, and in fact does not necessitate the existence of a free boundary as a trajectory.

The study of relations between particle system dynamics and parabolic FBP has been an active research field in the last decade. In
[
14

[
10

[

In recent years, relaxed solutions to the supercooled Stefan problem have been introduced in
[

Relaxed solutions to a certain FBP have also been proposed in the aforementioned

m[s,t]=\beta([x_{0},y_{0}]\times[s,t])
, for
s_{0}\leq s\leq t\leq t_{0}
. By assumption,
S:=m[s_{0},t_{0}]>0
. Denoting
\varepsilon=S(t_{0}-s_{0})^{-1}/2
, it follows by a pigeonhole argument that for every
\delta\in(0,t_{0}-s_{0})
there is
s\in[s_{0},t_{0}-\delta]
with
m[s,s+\delta]\geq\varepsilon\delta
. Now, by (
20
), for
s_{0}\leq s<t\leq t_{0}
,
\delta=t-s
,

v(x,t)={\mathcal{S}}_{\delta}v_{s}(x)-\int_{s}^{t}{\mathcal{S}}_{t-\theta}\beta_{\theta}(x)d\theta,\hskip 20.00003ptx\in{\mathbb{R}},

where
v_{s}=v(\cdot,s)
. Also,

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{y_{0}}\int_{s}^{t}{\mathcal{S}}_{t-\theta}\beta_{\theta}(x)d\theta dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{-\infty}^{y_{0}}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{[x_{0},y_{0}]}\mathfrak{p}_{t-\theta}(y-x)\beta_{\theta}(dy)d\theta dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{[x_{0},y_{0}]}\beta_{\theta}(dy)d\theta=\frac{1}{2}m[s,t]\geq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\delta.

Moreover, for
c
not depending on
\delta
,

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{y_{0}}{\mathcal{S}}_{\delta}v_{s}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{y_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathfrak{p}_{\delta}(y-x)v_{s}(y)dydx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{y_{0}}\int_{z}^{\infty}\mathfrak{p}_{\delta}(y-x)c(1+y_{+}^{c})dydx.

With
a=z-y_{0}
, note that in the above integrand,
y-x\geq a
. Hence the right-hand side above is bounded by

c_{1}e^{-c_{2}a^{2}/\delta}
. As a result,

\int_{-\infty}^{y_{0}}v(x,t)dx\leq c_{1}e^{-c_{2}a^{2}/\delta}-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\delta.

For
\delta>0
small, the above is negative, a contradiction. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

1.9
.

.

Consider now part (c). Thus we suppose that Assumption

Consider first
\gamma_{n}

We invoke Skorohod’s representation, by which we may assume that there exists a full-measure event

for some

\mu^{n_{k}}(t_{k})\to\mu(t)
as

and the property that

\gamma=0

We now argue that
\delta_{n}\to 0
in probability. Recall the constant
c
from the statement of the theorem. Denote by
\tilde{\pi}_{n}
the law of a PPP on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
with intensity measure
ncdx
.
Using strong approximative solutions of the infinite Atlas model (see

24

), we can construct gap sequence processes

of

and

.

,
(\tilde{Y}^{n}_{0}(t),\tilde{\mu}^{n}_{t})\to(\tilde{\sigma}_{t},\tilde{\mu}_{t})

. Furthermore,
\tilde{\mu}_{t}

to the right of

\inf_{t\in[0,T]}\tilde{\mu}_{t}[\tilde{\sigma}_{t},\tilde{\sigma}_{t}+\alpha]>a\alpha,\;\alpha\in(0,1],

\delta>0

\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(\delta_{n}>\delta)\doteq\theta>0.

We now argue that

(39)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mu^{n}_{t}(-\infty,\sigma_{t}-\delta/8]\to 0\mbox{ in probability, as }n\to\infty.

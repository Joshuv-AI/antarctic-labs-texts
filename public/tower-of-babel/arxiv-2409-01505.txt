Introduction and Main Results

In this paper, we investigate the Painlevé asymptotics for the solution to the Cauchy problem of the
Degasperis-Procesi (DP) equation

The DP equation (

Subsequently, it was found that the DP equation arises in the propagation of shallow water waves over a flat bed in the moderate amplitude regime

Owing to its integrable structure and elegant mathematical properties, the DP equation (

The global existence and blow-up phenomena were analyzed in

Algebro-geometric solutions for the whole DP hierarchy were constructed in

Additionally, the KAM theory near an elliptic fixed point for quasi-linear Hamiltonian perturbations of the DP equation on the circle was presented in
[

Now, to establish the associated RH problem, we consider the jump relation (
2.33
). For
i,j=1,2,3
, define

\theta_{ij}(k):=\theta_{ij}(k;\hat{\xi})=-\mathrm{i}\left[\hat{\xi}\left(\lambda_{i}(k)-\lambda_{j}(k)\right)+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}(k)}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}(k)}\right)\right],

where

and in Subsection

Specifically,

(2.37)

and

\theta_{13}(k)=-\theta_{12}(\omega^{2}k),\quad\theta_{23}(k)=\theta_{12}(\omega k).

(2.38)

Therefore, we have the following RH problem.

RH problem 2.1
.

Find a
3\times 3
matrix-valued function
M(k):=M(k;y,t)
such that

1.

Analyticity:
M(k)
is meromorphic in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma
, where
\Sigma=\mathbb{R}\cup\omega\mathbb{R}\cup\omega^{2}\mathbb{R}
.

2.

Jump relation:
M_{+}(k)=M_{-}(k)V(k)
,
k\in\Sigma
, where

\displaystyle V(k)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&\bar{r}(k)e^{\mathrm{i}t\theta_{12}(k)}&0\\
-r(k)e^{-\mathrm{i}t\theta_{12}(k)}&1-|r(k)|^{2}&0\\

&\Gamma_{4}^{2}V_{0}(\omega^{2}k)\Gamma_{4}^{-2},\quad k\in\omega\mathbb{R},\\
&\Gamma_{4}V_{0}(\omega k)\Gamma_{4}^{-1},\quad k\in\omega^{2}\mathbb{R}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(2.39)

3.

Asymptotic behaviors:

M(k)=I+\mathcal{O}(k^{-1}),\quad k\rightarrow\infty.

(2.40)

4.

Singularities:
M(k)
has singularity at
\varkappa_{\nu},\ \nu=1,\cdots,6
with

\displaystyle M(k)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &M_{\pm 1}(k)+\mathcal{O}(1),\quad k\rightarrow\pm 1,\\
&\Gamma_{4}^{2}M_{\pm 1}(\omega^{2}k)\Gamma_{4}^{-2}+\mathcal{O}(1),\quad k\rightarrow\pm\omega,\\
&\Gamma_{4}M_{\pm 1}(\omega k)\Gamma_{4}^{-1}+\mathcal{O}(1),\quad k\rightarrow\pm\omega^{2},\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(2.41)

where

-\alpha_{\pm}&-\alpha_{\pm}&-\beta_{\pm}\\
0&0&0\end{array}\right),

and
\alpha_{\pm}=-\bar{\alpha}_{\pm},\ \beta_{\pm}=-\bar{\beta}_{\pm}
.

5.

Residue conditions: For
\zeta_{n}\in D_{1}\cap\mathcal{K}
,

\displaystyle\res\limits_{k=\zeta_{n}}M(k)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\zeta_{n}}M(k)B_{n},

(2.42)

\displaystyle\res\limits_{k=\omega\bar{\zeta}_{n}}M(k)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\omega\bar{\zeta}_{n}}M(k)\Gamma_{3}(\omega\bar{B}_{n})\Gamma_{3}:=\lim_{k\rightarrow\omega\bar{\zeta}_{n}}M(k)B_{n+N},

\displaystyle\res\limits_{k=\omega\zeta_{n}}M(k)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\omega\zeta_{n}}M(k)\Gamma_{4}^{2}(\omega B_{n})\Gamma_{4}^{-2}:=\lim_{k\rightarrow\omega\zeta_{n}}M(k)B_{n+2N},

\displaystyle\res\limits_{k=\omega^{2}\bar{\zeta}_{n}}M(k)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\omega^{2}\bar{\zeta}_{n}}M(k)\Gamma_{2}(\omega^{2}\bar{B}_{n})\Gamma_{2}:=\lim_{k\rightarrow\omega^{2}\bar{\zeta}_{n}}M(k)B_{n+3N},

\displaystyle\res\limits_{k=\omega^{2}\zeta_{n}}M(k)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\omega^{2}\zeta_{n}}M(k)\Gamma_{4}(\omega^{2}B_{n})\Gamma_{4}^{-1}:=\lim_{k\rightarrow\omega^{2}\zeta_{n}}M(k)B_{n+4N},

\displaystyle\res\limits_{k=\bar{\zeta}_{n}}M(k)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\bar{\zeta}_{n}}M(k)\Gamma_{1}\bar{B}_{n}\Gamma_{1}:=\lim_{k\rightarrow\bar{\zeta}_{n}}M(k)B_{n+5N},

&\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&0\\

(2.43)

Particularly, introduce a vector-valued function

2.2
A regular RH problem

In order to perform the long-time analysis via the
\bar{\partial}
-steepest descent method, we need to construct a regular RH problem by the following two essential operations:

(i)

Decompose the jump matrix
V(k)
into appropriate upper/lower triangular factorizations so that the oscillating factor
e^{\pm 2\mathrm{i}\theta_{12}(k)}
are decaying in the corresponding sectors respectively;

(ii)

Interpolate the poles by trading them for jumps along small closed loops enclosing
each pole
[
52
]
.

The operation (i) is aided by two well known factorizations of the jump matrix

V(k)=b(k)^{-{\dagger}}b(k)=B(k)T_{0}(k)B(k)^{-{\dagger}},\quad k\in\mathbb{R},

(2.46)

where

\displaystyle b(k)^{-{\dagger}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
-r(k)e^{-\mathrm{i}t\theta_{12}(k)}&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right),\quad b(k)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&\bar{r}(k)e^{\mathrm{i}t\theta_{12}(k)}&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle B(k)^{-{\dagger}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
-\frac{r(k)}{1-|r(k)|^{2}}e^{-\mathrm{i}t\theta_{12}(k)}&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right),\quad B(k)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&\frac{\bar{r}(k)}{1-|r(k)|^{2}}e^{\mathrm{i}t\theta_{12}(k)}&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle T_{0}(k)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{1}{1-|r(k)|^{2}}&0&0\\
0&1-|r(k)|^{2}&0\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right).

To remove the diagonal matrix in the middle of the second factorization, on
\mathbb{R}
, we define

\displaystyle I(\hat{\xi})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathbb{R},\quad\hat{\xi}\in\mathcal{T}_{1},\\
&\emptyset,\quad\hat{\xi}\in\mathcal{T}_{2},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.47)

then on
\omega\mathbb{R},\ \omega^{2}\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\omega I(\hat{\xi})=\left\{\omega k:k\in I(\hat{\xi})\right\},\quad\omega^{2}I(\hat{\xi})=\left\{\omega^{2}k:k\in I(\hat{\xi})\right\}.

(2.48)

Further, we introduce a scalar RH problem, which satisfies

RH problem 2.3
.

Find a function
\delta(k):=\delta(k;\hat{\xi})
satisfying the following properties:

1.

\delta(k)
is analytical in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
.

2.

For
k\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\begin{split}&\delta_{+}(k)=\delta_{-}(k)(1-|r(k)|^{2}),\quad k\in I(\hat{\xi}),\\
&\delta_{+}(k)=\delta_{-}(k),\quad k\in\mathbb{R}\setminus I(\hat{\xi}).\end{split}

(2.49)

3.

As
k\to\infty
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
,
\delta(k)\rightarrow 1
.

Utilizing the Plemelj’s formula, RH problem
2.3
admits a unique solution

\delta(k)=\exp\left(-\mathrm{i}\int_{I(\hat{\xi})}\frac{\nu(s)}{s-k}ds\right),

(2.50)

with
\nu(k)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log{(1-|r(k)|^{2})}
.

Now, we focus on operation (ii), our method for dealing with the poles in the RH problem follows the ideas in
[
52
]
. We observe that on the unit circle the phase function appearing in the residue conditions (
2.42
) satisfies

\operatorname{Im}\theta_{12}(\zeta_{n})=2\sin{\phi_{n}}\left(\hat{\xi}-\frac{3}{4\cos^{2}{\phi_{n}}-3}\right)

(2.51)

with
\zeta_{n}=e^{\mathrm{i}\phi_{n}}
.

Denote
the critical line

\operatorname{Re}k=L(\hat{\xi}):=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sqrt{1+1/\hat{\xi}},

then define

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}=\left\{j\in\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}:{\rm Re}{\zeta_{j}}<L(\hat{\xi})\right\},\ \Delta_{2}=\left\{l\in\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{A}:{\rm Re}{\zeta_{l}}<L(\hat{\xi})\right\},\ \Delta=\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2},

\displaystyle\nabla_{1}=\left\{j\in\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}:{\rm Re}{\zeta_{j}}>L(\hat{\xi})\right\},\ \nabla_{2}=\left\{l\in\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{A}:{\rm Re}{\zeta_{l}}>L(\hat{\xi})\right\},\ \nabla=\nabla_{1}\cup\nabla_{2}.

To carry out operation (ii), define

\displaystyle T_{1}(k)=\frac{H(\omega^{2}k)}{H(k)},\quad T_{2}(k)=\frac{H(k)}{H(\omega k)},\quad T_{3}(k)=\frac{H(\omega k)}{H(\omega^{2}k)},

(2.52)

where

(2.53)

and

\displaystyle\varrho=\frac{1}{4}\min

\displaystyle\left\{\min_{n\in\mathcal{N}}|\operatorname{Im}\zeta_{n}|,\min_{n\in\mathcal{N},\arg k=\frac{\pi}{3}\mathrm{i}}|\zeta_{n}-k|,\min_{n\in\mathcal{N}\setminus\Lambda,\operatorname{Im}\theta_{12}(k)=0}|\zeta_{n}-k|,\right.

\displaystyle\left.\min_{n\in\mathcal{N}}|\zeta_{n}-e^{\frac{\pi}{6}\mathrm{i}}|,\min_{n\neq m\in\mathcal{N}}|\zeta_{n}-\zeta_{m}|\right\}.

(2.54)

.

Let

T(k)=\text{diag}\{T_{1}(k),T_{2}(k),T_{3}(k)\},

(2.55)

and for

, define

\displaystyle G(k)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &I-\frac{B_{n}}{k-\zeta_{n}},\quad k\in\mathbb{D}_{n},\ n-k_{0}N\in\nabla,\ k_{0}\in\{0,\cdots,5\},\\

-\frac{k-\zeta_{n}}{C_{n}e^{\mathrm{i}t\theta_{12}(\zeta_{n})}}&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad k\in\mathbb{D}_{n},n\in\Delta_{1}\text{ or }n-2N\in\Delta_{2},\\
&\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&-\frac{k-\zeta_{n}}{C_{n}e^{\mathrm{i}t\theta_{13}(\zeta_{n})}}&1\end{pmatrix},\quad k\in\mathbb{D}_{n},n-N\in\Delta_{1}\text{ or }n-5N\in\Delta_{2},\\
&\begin{pmatrix}1&0&-\frac{k-\zeta_{n}}{C_{n}e^{-\mathrm{i}t\theta_{13}(\zeta_{n})}}\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad k\in\mathbb{D}_{n},n-2N\in\Delta_{1}\text{ or }n-4N\in\Delta_{2},\\

0&1&-\frac{k-\zeta_{n}}{C_{n}e^{-\mathrm{i}t\theta_{23}(\zeta_{n})}}\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad k\in\mathbb{D}_{n},n-3N\in\Delta_{1}\text{ or }n-N\in\Delta_{2},\\
&\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&-\frac{k-\zeta_{n}}{C_{n}e^{\mathrm{i}t\theta_{23}(\zeta_{n})}}&1\end{pmatrix},\quad k\in\mathbb{D}_{n},n-4N\in\Delta_{1}\text{ or }n\in\Delta_{2},\\

0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad k\in\mathbb{D}_{n},n-5N\in\Delta_{1}\text{ or }n-3N\in\Delta_{2},\\

(2.56)

Comments on the main results

Remark 1.5
.

It is possible that
1.1
and
1.4
hold for all primes, not just all but finitely many. It is also reasonable to think that the torsion class
\sigma
in
1.3
can be trivial, hence the canonical class and its exterior powers are genuinely profinite. Note however that the canonical class can be a nontrivial multiple of another class in
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
(see
[
29
,
28
]
for examples where it is divisible by arbitrarily large integers), so the finite exceptional set of primes in
1.3
is necessary.

Remark 1.6
.

It is unclear if analogous results hold for every lattice in
\PU(n,1)
. Underpinning this paper is the fact that much of
H^{*}(X,\mathbb{C})
is in the span to the Poincaré duals to the complex totally geodesic subspaces or is virtually generated by the cup product from
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{C})
. Nonvanishing of
H^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{C})
for congruence lattices is restricted to lattices of simplest type
[
7
]
, and there are
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{B}^{n}
with no proper complex totally geodesic subvarieties expect points. For nonarithmetic lattices, there are only finitely many maximal totally geodesic subspaces
[
1
,
2
]
and little is known about the cup product structure on cohomology. Thus completely different tools may be required to analyze nonvanishing of
H^{j}(\widehat{\Gamma},\mathbb{F}_{p})
for other
\Gamma
.

Remark 1.7
.

Lattices in higher-rank simple Lie groups with the congruence subgroup property fail to be good, for the same reason given for

in
[

, §7.3]
. Fundamental groups of many compact (real) hyperbolic manifolds are good, for example the lattices in

of simplest type
[

, Thm. 6.5]
. Lattices in
\PU(n,1)
in many ways lie in between these two cases, so it is unclear whether or not one expects them to be good. It may be possible using some of the very explicit constructions in the literature to analyze an example; e.g., see
[
15
,
4
,
19
,
16
,
30
,
8
,
10
,
9
,
11
,
25
]
for a wealth of concrete ball quotient constructions where one could perhaps analyze cohomological properties of profinite completions closely enough to determine goodness.

Remark 1.8
.

For

and
\Gamma

\PU(n,1)

is torsion free when

\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}
on integral cohomology has

\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}(\psi)-K_{X}\in\ker\!\left(H^{2}(\Gamma_{0},\mathbb{Z})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow H^{2}(\Gamma_{0},\mathbb{C})\right)

and so
\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}(\psi)=K_{X}+\sigma
for some torsion class

. Then the

exterior power

is nontrivial in

, since the same is true for

. The main argument in the proof of

then implies that

0\neq(K_{X}+\sigma)^{i}\!\!\!\!\!\pmod{p}\in\im\!\left(\mathrm{res}:H^{2i}(\widehat{\Gamma}_{0},\mathbb{F}_{p})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow H^{2i}(\Gamma_{0},\mathbb{F}_{p})\right)

for all

and all but finitely many primes

. This proves the theorem for
\Gamma_{0}
, and the result follows for any
\Delta\leq\Gamma_{0}
of finite index, albeit possibly with different exceptional primes, by pullback from
\Gamma_{0}\backslash\mathbb{B}^{n}
. Thus the proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

To start, cocompact lattices in

Theorem 3.3

Following the notation established in this section, suppose

Dynamical Localization and Transport properties of Quantum Walks on the hexagonal lattice
Andreas Schaefer

Abstract
We study coined Random Quantum Walks on the hexagonal lattice, where the strength of disorder is monitored by the coin matrix. Each lattice site is equipped with an i.i.d. random variable that is uniformly distributed on the torus and acts as a random phase in every step of the QW. We show dynamical localization in the regime of strong disorder, that is whenever the coin matrix is sufficiently close to the fully localized case, using a fractional moment criterion and a finite volume method. Moreover, we adapt a topological index to our model and thereby obtain transport for some coin matrices.



Keywords –
Quantum walks, localization, fractional moment criterion, transport, topological index

81Q35

We establish this result using the fractional moment criterion and a finite volume method. A novelty of this work is the introduction of small correlations, which to the best of the authors knowledge have only been investigated in

In contrast to most previous works, where random phases are represented by an infinite diagonal matrix with i.i.d. random phases along the diagonal, our model incorporates randomness through an infinite diagonal matrix in which each triplet of diagonal entries shares the same phase. However, this comes with a caveat: The fact that exponential decay of the fractional moments implies dynamical localization relies on the independence of the randomness, and does not directly generalize to the correlated case. As a way out, we can add additional random variables to our model, rendering the randomness independent and obtaining dynamical localization. The model and main result

The hexagonal lattice
\Gamma
is defined using the integer spans
\Gamma_{A}
and
\Gamma_{B}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{A}=\text{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\left(\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{3}\\
0\end{pmatrix},\,\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{3}\\
3\end{pmatrix}\right),\;\;\Gamma_{B}=\Gamma_{A}+\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix},\;\;\Gamma=\Gamma_{A}\cup\Gamma_{B}.

Fixing an orthonormal basis (ONB)
f_{1},f_{2}
of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
and using that
l^{2}(\Gamma)\simeq l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
, an ONB of
l^{2}(\Gamma)
is given by
\ket{j,k}\otimes f_{i}
. The components
j,k
are the coefficients with respect to the vectors used to span
\Gamma_{A}
, while the vectors
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
refer to lattice sites in
\Gamma_{A}
and
\Gamma_{B}
respectively. We define the shift operators
S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}
as translation-invariant operators on
l^{2}(\Gamma)
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{1}\left(\ket{j,k}\otimes f_{1}\right)=\ket{j,k-1}\otimes f_{2},\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;S_{1}\left(\ket{j,k}\otimes f_{2}\right)&=\ket{j-1,k+1}\otimes f_{1}\\
S_{2}\left(\ket{j,k}\otimes f_{1}\right)=\ket{j+1,k-1}\otimes f_{2},\;\;S_{2}\left(\ket{j,k}\otimes f_{2}\right)&=\ket{j,k}\otimes f_{1}\\
S_{3}\left(\ket{j,k}\otimes f_{1}\right)=\ket{j,k}\otimes f_{2},\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;S_{3}\left(\ket{j,k}\otimes f_{2}\right)&=\ket{j,k+1}\otimes f_{1}.\end{split}

(1)

Their action is illustrated in Figure
1
.

Figure 1:
The action of the shift operators
S_{j}

We equip every site
x\in\Gamma
with a three-dimensional coin space. The Quantum Walk acts on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=l^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes\mathbb{C}^{3}\simeq l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2};\mathbb{C}^{6})
. We fix an arbitrary ONB
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}
of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
and define the shift operator
S\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle S=S_{1}\otimes\ket{e_1}\bra{e_1}+S_{2}\otimes\ket{e_2}\bra{e_2}+S_{3}\otimes\ket{e_3}\bra{e_3}.

To every lattice site
x\in\Gamma
we associate a unitary coin matrix
C_{x}\in U(3)
. For any
\ket{x}=\ket{j,k}\otimes f_{i}
and coin state
\tau\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
we define the coin operator
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{x}\}_{x\in\Gamma}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\left(\ket{x}\otimes\tau\right)=\ket{x}\otimes C_{x}\,\tau.

The deterministic QW is given by
U=S\mathcal{C}
. In some instances we will assume that there exists a matrix
C\in U(3)
such that
C_{x}=C
for all
x\in\Gamma
. In this case, we will denote the coin operator by
\mathcal{C}(C)
and the corresponding QW by
U(C)
.

Every
x\in\Gamma
is equipped with an i.i.d. random variable
\omega_{x}
that is uniformly distributed on the torus
\mathbb{T}
. Note that whenever we view the torus as the real interval
[0,2\pi)
, we denote it by
\mathbb{T}
, while the complex sphere it is homeomorphic to will be called
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. We define the random unitary operator

\displaystyle D_{\omega}\left(\ket{x}\otimes\tau\right)=e^{i\omega_{x}}\left(\ket{x}\otimes\tau\right).

The random QW is given by
U_{\omega}=D_{\omega}U
.

Remark 1
.

We define the random QW by
D_{\omega}S\mathcal{C}
, applying the random phase only after the shift operator to align with the existing literature. However, an alternative definition is
W_{\omega}=S\mathcal{C}D_{\omega}
. They are unitarily equivalent due to
W_{\omega}=D_{\omega}^{*}U_{\omega}D_{\omega}
and thus share the same transport and spectral properties. Due to
\mathcal{C}D_{\omega}\ket{x}\otimes\tau=e^{i\omega_{x}}\ket{x}\otimes C_{x}\tau
we can replace the deterministic coin matrices
C_{x}
by the random matrices
e^{i\omega_{x}}C_{x}
. Using the random coin operator
\mathcal{C}_{\omega}=\{e^{i\omega_{x}}C_{x}\}_{x\in\Gamma}
, we have
W_{\omega}=SC_{\omega}
and can view the randomness as a random phase associated to each coin matrix.

Let
C_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
1&0&0\end{pmatrix}
be the permutation matrix corresponding to the permutation
(1\;3\;2)
and denote
x^{(i)}=\ket{x}\otimes e_{i}
. Our goal is the following theorem:

Theorem 1
.

For any
s\in(0,\frac{1}{3})
there exists
\varphi,c,g>0
such that for any
C\in U(3)
with
\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq\varphi
and all lattice sites
x,y\in\Gamma
, coin states
i,j\in\{1,2,3\}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z|<1
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(|\langle x^{(i)}|\,\big(U_{\omega}(C)-z\big)^{-1}\,y^{(j)}\rangle|^{s}\right)\leq c\,e^{-g|x-y|}.

(2)

Here
|x-y|
denotes the length of the shortest path connecting
x
and
y
. Thus,
d(x,y)=|x-y|
is a metric.

Remark 2
.

The proof of Theorem
1
can be directly generalized to any deterministic family of unitary matrices
(C_{x})_{x\in\Gamma}
by replacing the assumption
\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq\varphi
by
\sup_{x\in\Gamma}\|C_{x}-C_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq\varphi
. Thus, Theorem
1
also applies to non-ergodic models.

Remark 3
.

We emphasize that the elements
\ket{x^{(1)}}
,
\ket{x^{(2)}}
and
\ket{x^{(3)}}
of the ONB of
\mathcal{H}
are equipped with the same random variable. When the operator
D_{\omega}
is expressed in this ONB, this yields an infinite diagonal matrix, where each triplet of matrix elements shares the same random variable. Unlike the models in the existing works
[
26
]
and
[
10
]
where the infinite matrix
D_{\omega}
has independent diagonal entries, this structure introduces some correlations to the model.

The fact that exponential decay of the fractional moments implies dynamical localization is proven in Theorem 3.2 in
[
26
]
. However, their proof relies on the independence of the diagonal entries in the matrix expression of
D_{\omega}
and can not be directly adapted to the correlated framework presented above. This is because Krein’s formula, which is equation (5.5) in
[
26
]
, requires a rank one perturbation and does not easily generalize to other finite-dimensional perturbations. Since
D_{\omega}
has correlated diagonal blocks of size
3\times 3
, we are dealing with rank three perturbations. Nevertheless, these difficulties seem rather technical than fundamental and dynamical localization is still expected in the correlated case (see
[
39
]
, Quantum Walks in Random Magnetic Fields, Conjecture 2.5).

Nevertheless, we can still conclude dynamical localization if we adapt the random operator
D_{\omega}
: Equipping every site
x\in\Gamma
with three i.i.d. random variables
\omega_{x,1}
,
\omega_{x,2}
and
\omega_{x,3}
we can define the random operator
D_{\omega}\ket{x^{(j)}}=e^{i\omega_{x,j}}\,\ket{x^{(j)}}
. Its matrix expression in the ONB
(\ket{x^{(i)}})_{x,i}
has now independent diagonal entries. Since Theorem
1
still holds, we can conclude dynamical localization using Theorem 3.2 in
[
26
]
under the assumptions of Theorem
1
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,U_{\omega}(C)^{n}\,y^{(j)}\rangle|\right)\leq ce^{-g|x-y|}.

(3)

Figure 2:
The invariant subspace
\mathcal{H}^{j,k}
of
U_{\omega}(C_{0})

Dynamical localization means that the probability to move from a lattice site
x\in\Gamma
to another site
y\in\Gamma
decreases almost surely exponentially in the distance
|x-y|
, independently of how many steps
n
the Quantum Walker may take. Using the coin matrix
C_{0}
, the Quantum Walk is fully localized, see Figure
2
. In other words, the subspaces
\mathcal{H}^{j,k}
as defined in (
4
) are invariant under
U_{\omega}(C_{0})
. Thus, Theorem
1
yields dynamical localization if the chosen coin matrix
C
is sufficiently close to the fully localized case
C_{0}
.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{j,k}=\text{span}\Big\{\ket{j,k}&\otimes f_{1}\otimes e_{1},\;\ket{j,k}\otimes f_{1}\otimes e_{2},\;\ket{j,k}\otimes f_{1}\otimes e_{3},\\
\ket{j,k}&\otimes f_{2}\otimes e_{3},\;\ket{j,k-1}\otimes f_{2}\otimes e_{1},\;\ket{j+1,k-1}\otimes f_{2}\otimes e_{2}\Big\}.\end{split}

(4)

Remark 4
.

The matrix
\tilde{C}_{0}
corresponding to the permutation
(1\;2\;3)
induces full localization, similarly to
C_{0}
which corresponds to the permutation
(1\;3\;2)
. The invariant subspaces
\mathcal{H}^{j,k}
pictured in Figure
2
are then centered at a
\Gamma_{B}
site instead of a
\Gamma_{A}
site. The proof of Theorem
1
can be adapted without any major changes, thereby yielding a fractional moment estimate for any choice of
C\in U(3)
such that
\|C-\tilde{C}_{0}\|\leq\varphi_{0}
.

Remark 5
.

It was shown in
[
44
]
that without random phases the coin matrix
C(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}0&\cos(\theta)&\sin(\theta)\\
0&-\sin(\theta)&\cos(\theta)\\
1&0&0\end{pmatrix}
leads to non-empty continuous spectrum for any
\theta\notin\pi\mathbb{Z}
and therefore no localization. Since
\|C(\theta)-C_{0}\|
can be arbitrarily small, this shows that it is the randomness which drives localization.

3
The band structure of translation-invariant QWs

We consider a deterministic Quantum Walk
U=S\mathcal{C}
as defined in section
2
. Furthermore, we assume that the coin operator
\mathcal{C}
is such that there exist two unitary matrices
C_{A},C_{B}\in U(3)
such that
C_{x}=C_{A}
on all lattice sites
x\in\Gamma_{A}
and
C_{x}=C_{B}
on all
x\in\Gamma_{B}
. The resulting operator
U
is translation-invariant with respect to shifts that respect the lattice structure, i.e. shifts
T_{v}:x\mapsto x+v
where
v\in\Gamma_{A}
. We consider the Fourier Transform

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}),\;\;\widehat{\varphi}(k)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}e^{ik\cdot n}\,\varphi(n).

\mathcal{F}
is a unitary operator when the spaces
l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
and
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
are equipped with their standard scalar products without normalization factors. The Fourier Transform on
l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{6}
is given by
\mathcal{F}\otimes I
and the Fourier transformed QW is thus defined as
\widehat{U}=(\mathcal{F}\otimes I)U(\mathcal{F}\otimes I)^{-1}
. It is a unitary operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{6}
. We define the translation
T_{v}
on elements of
l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
by
T_{v}\varphi(x)=\varphi(x+v)
and remember that
\big(\mathcal{F}T_{v}\,\varphi\big)(k)=e^{-iv\cdot k}\widehat{\varphi}(k)
. Since
U
is translation invariant, an easy calculation shows that
\widehat{U}
acts as a multiplication by the matrix

\displaystyle\widehat{U}(k)=\begin{pmatrix}0&S_{BA}(k)C_{B}\\
S_{AB}(k)C_{A}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Here we have ordered the ONB of
\mathbb{C}^{6}
by
f_{1}\otimes e_{1},...,f_{1}\otimes e_{3},f_{2}\otimes e_{1},...,f_{2}\otimes e_{3}
. The blocks are given by

\displaystyle S_{BA}(k)=\begin{pmatrix}e^{i(k_{1}-k_{2})}&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&e^{-ik_{2}}\end{pmatrix}\;\;\text{and}\;\;S_{AB}(k)=\begin{pmatrix}e^{ik_{2}}&0&0\\
0&e^{i(k_{2}-k_{1})}&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

The matrix
S_{AB}(k)
(respectively
S_{BA}(k)
) corresponds to the Walker moving from
\Gamma_{A}
sites to
\Gamma_{B}
sites (respectively
\Gamma_{B}
to
\Gamma_{A}
), while the coefficients
e^{iv\cdot k}
stem from the expression of the shifts
S_{j}
as defined in (
1
). Due to the off-diagonal structure of
\widehat{U}(k)
, we have:

\displaystyle\sigma(U)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{T}^{2}}\big\{\pm\sqrt{\lambda}\,\text{ s.t. }\lambda\in\sigma(V(k))\big\}\,\text{ where }V(k)=S_{BA}(k)\,C_{B}\,S_{AB}(k)\,C_{A}.

Let
k\mapsto\lambda_{j}(k)
denote the three eigenvalues of
V(k)
and
\mu_{j}(k)=-i\log(\lambda_j(k))
their real arguments, which we call the bands of
U
. Since
\det(V(k))=\det(C_B)\,\det(C_A)
is independent of
k
, we can normalize and assume without loss of generality that
V(k)
has determinant
1
. The characteristic polynomial of
V(k)
is given by

\displaystyle\chi_{V(k)}(\lambda)=\lambda^{3}-\tr(V(k))\,\lambda^{2}+\overline{\tr(V(k))}\,\lambda-1.

The spectrum of
U
is thus characterized by the map
k\mapsto\tr(V(k))
. We immediately see that
\sigma_{cont}(U)=\varnothing
if and only if
k\mapsto\tr(V(k))
is constant, in which case
\sigma(U)
consists of at most six infinitely degenerate eigenvalues.

Moreover, we can define the set of trace values
\mathcal{T}=\{\tr(U)|\,U\in SU(3)\}
. It is a closed subset of
\mathbb{C}
and we can easily show that two bands agree (e.g.
\mu_{1}(k)=\mu_{2}(k)
) at some
k\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
if and only if
\tr(V(k))\in\partial\mathcal{T}
, see
[
44
]
. From this we can derive a fruitful theory regarding intersections in the band structure. In particular, we can obtain a criterion for conical intersections in the band structure based on the first two derivatives of the trace of
V(k)
. Restricting the model to circulant coin matrices offers further results, such as intersections at
k=0
always being conical. These results will be presented in the PhD manuscript of the author.

4
Exponential decay of the fractional moments

We use sections
4.1
-
4.7
to prove Theorem
1
, following the methods presented in
[
26
]
and
[
10
]
. The proof uses a finite volume method and is structured as follows: We start by showing that the fractional moments in (
2
) are bounded by some constant
C(s)
in section
4.1
. We define the restriction of the Quantum Walk to some finite box
\Lambda_{L}
of size
L
in section
4.2
and show that the operator describing the transition between
\Lambda_{L}
and
\Lambda_{L}^{C}
is bounded independently of
L
in section
4.3
. In section
4.4
we investigate the probability of gaps in the spectrum of the fully localized operator, i.e. when
C=C_{0}
. Using this, we are able to obtain polynomial decay in
|L|
of the resolvent restricted to some box
\Lambda_{L}
in section
4.5
. The key ingredient for the proof of Theorem
1
is finally obtained in section
4.6
. We show that the expectation
\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,(U_{\omega}(C)-z)^{-1}\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)
can be bounded by
q<1
times
\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle v^{(k)}\,|\,(U_{\omega}(C)-z)^{-1}\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)
, where
v^{(k)}
lies on the boundary of
\Lambda_{L}
(and is thus closer to
y^{(j)}
). Repeated application of this result finally yields Theorem
1
in section
4.7
. The appendices
A
and
B
are devoted to proving the so-called re-sampling arguments, which are key ingredients in the finite volume method and omitted in section
4.6
.

4.1
Fractional moment bound

In this section we prove the boundedness of the fractional moments in equation (
2
) under the weaker assumption that the i.i.d random variables
(\omega_{x})_{x\in\Gamma}
are subject to an absolutely continuous distribution
\mu
with bounded density
\tau
. We prove the following:

Theorem 2
.

For every
s\in(0,1)
there exists
C(s)<\infty
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\langle x^{(i)}\,|(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}y^{(j)}\rangle|^{s}\;d\mu(\omega_{x})\,d\mu(\omega_{y})\leq C(s)

(5)

for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
|z|\neq 1
,
x^{(i)},y^{(j)}\in l^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes\mathbb{C}^{3}
, and arbitrary values of all other random variables
\omega_{v}
. Thus, the expectation in (
2
) is bounded by
C(s)
.

The proof of Theorem
2
mainly follows the steps of
[
26
]
(Theorem 3.1). However, due to our model being less random, the resulting dissipative operator
-i\widehat{F}_{z}
acts on a six-dimensional space instead of a two-dimensional one, leading us to extend the integral bound presented in
[
26
]
to any finite-dimensional space.

Proof.

The bound (
5
) is trivial when
z
is away from the unit circle:

\displaystyle|\langle x^{(i)}\,|(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\,y^{(j)}\rangle|\leq\|(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\|=\frac{1}{\text{dist}(z,\sigma(U_{\omega}))}\leq\frac{1}{|1-|z|\,|}.

We may thus assume without loss of generality, that
\frac{1}{2}\leq|z|\leq 2
and
|z|\neq 1
. Furthermore, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2z}\,\Big((U_{\omega}+z)(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}-1\Big)=(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}.

To prove (
5
) it thus suffices to show the similar bound on
(U_{\omega}+z)(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\langle x^{(i)}\,|(U_{\omega}+z)\,(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\,y^{(j)}\rangle|^{s}\;d\mu(\omega_{x})\,d\mu(\omega_{y})\leq C(s).

(6)

We assume for now that
x^{(i)}\neq y^{(j)}
, while the simpler case
x^{(i)}=y^{(j)}
will be dealt with at the end. Following the steps of
[
26
]
, we introduce the change of variables

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\big(\omega_{x}+\omega_{y}\big)\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\beta=\frac{1}{2}\big(\omega_{x}-\omega_{y}\big).

Furthermore, we introduce the unitary operators:

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}v^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}e^{i\alpha}\,v^{(k)}&\text{if }v\in\{x,y\}\\
v^{(k)}&\text{else}\end{cases},\;\;D_{\beta}v^{(k)}=\begin{cases}e^{i\beta}\,v^{(k)}&\text{if }v=x\\
e^{-i\beta}\,v^{(k)}&\text{if }v=y\\
v^{(k)}&\text{else}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\widehat{D}v^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}v^{(k)}&\text{if }v\in\{x,y\}\\
e^{i\omega_{v}}\,v^{(k)}&\text{else}\end{cases}.

By definition, we have
D_{\omega}=D_{\alpha}\,D_{\beta}\,\widehat{D}
. We define the unitary operator
V_{\omega}=D_{\beta}\,\widehat{D}\,U
. Denoting by
P
the projection onto the subspace spanned by
\{V_{\omega}^{-1}x^{(k)},V_{\omega}^{-1}y^{(k)},\,k=1,2,3\}
, we easily verify that
(U_{\omega}-V_{\omega})\,(1-P)=0
and
V_{\omega}^{-1}\,U_{\omega}P=e^{i\alpha}P
. We conclude

\displaystyle U_{\omega}=U_{\omega}\,P+U_{\omega}\,(1-P)=e^{i\alpha}\,V_{\omega}\,P+V_{\omega}\,(1-P).

(7)

For
|z|\notin\{0,1\}
we define the operators

\displaystyle F_{z}=P(U_{\omega}+z)(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}P\text{ and }\widehat{F}_{z}=P(V_{\omega}+z)(V_{\omega}-z)^{-1}P,

viewing them as operators on the range of
P
. Using that
[V_{\omega}+z,(V_{\omega}-z)^{-1}]=0
and
\big((V_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\big)^{*}=(V_{\omega}^{*}-\overline{z})^{-1}
, we compute

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{F}_{z}+\widehat{F}_{z}^{*}=P\Big(2\big(I-|z|^{2}\big)(V_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\,\big((V_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\big)^{*}\Big)P.\end{split}

Assuming that
|z|>1
, we conclude that
\widehat{F}_{z}+\widehat{F}_{z}^{*}<0
is invertible. Thus,
-i\widehat{F}_{z}
is a dissipative operator if
|z|>1
. Note that we call an operator
A
dissipative if its imaginary part
\frac{1}{2i}(A-A^{*})
is positive. Using
\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1}=\widehat{F}_{-z}
, we conclude that
-i\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1}
is also dissipative for
|z|>1
. Similarly, we obtain that
i\widehat{F}_{z}
and
i\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1}
are dissipative for
|z|<1
. Employing the resolvent identity and (
7
), we compute

\displaystyle F_{z}-\widehat{F}_{z}=P\Big(2z(V_{\omega}-z)^{-1}(1-e^{i\alpha})V_{\omega}\,P\,(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\Big)P.

We use the definitions of
F_{z}
and
\widehat{F}_{z}
to obtain on the range of
P
:

\displaystyle F_{z}-\widehat{F}_{z}=\frac{1}{2}\,(e^{i\alpha}-1)\,(1+\widehat{F}_{z})\,(1-F_{z}).

Following the steps from
[
26
]
, we define
m(\alpha)=i\frac{1+e^{i\alpha}}{1-e^{i\alpha}}=-\cot\big(\frac{\alpha}{2}\big)
for
\alpha\notin\pi\mathbb{Z}
and obtain from a direct computation:

\displaystyle F_{z}=-i\Big(-i\widehat{F}_{z}+m(\alpha)\Big)^{-1}-i\Big(-i\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1}-\frac{1}{m(\alpha)}\Big)^{-1}.

(8)

Using
V_{\omega}^{-1}\,U_{\omega}P=e^{i\alpha}P
and unitarity of
V_{\omega}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,(U_{\omega}+z)\,(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\,y^{(j)}\rangle=\langle V_{\omega}^{*}\,x^{(i)}\,|\,F_{z}V_{\omega}^{*}\,y^{(j)}\rangle.

This allows us to estimate the integral in (
6
):

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,(U_{\omega}+z)\,(U_{\omega}-z)^{-1}y^{(j)}\rangle|^{s}\;d\mu(\omega_{x})\,d\mu(\omega_{y})\leq\|\tau\|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|F_{z}\|^{s}\;d\alpha\,d\beta.

We stress that while
F_{z}
depends on
\alpha
,
\widehat{F}_{z}
does not. Note that for
s<1
, we have
|a+b|^{s}\leq|a|^{s}+|b|^{s}
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. Plugging equation (
8
) into the integral above, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|F_{z}\|^{s}\;d\alpha\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|\big(-i\widehat{F}_{z}+m(\alpha)\big)^{-1}\|^{s}\;d\alpha+\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|\Big(-i\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1}-\frac{1}{m(\alpha)}\Big)^{-1}\|^{s}\;d\alpha.

(9)

Using that
\widehat{F}_{z}
does not depend on
\alpha
, we substitute
x=m(\alpha)
to bound the first integral on the right hand side of (
9
):

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|\big(-i\widehat{F}_{z}+m(\alpha)\big)^{-1}\|^{s}\;d\alpha

\displaystyle=\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{-R}^{R}\frac{2}{1+x^{2}}\,\|\big(-i\widehat{F}_{z}+x\big)^{-1}\|^{s}\;dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{1+(|n|-1)^{2}}\,\int_{n}^{n+1}\|(-i\widehat{F}_{z}+x)^{-1}\|^{s}\,dx.

A similar expression holds for the second term in (
9
). The operators
-i\widehat{F}_{z}
and
-i\widehat{F}^{-1}_{z}
are defined on
\text{Ran}(P)
, which has dimension six and are thus maximally dissipative. Since
\text{Ran}(P)
is two-dimensional in
[
26
]
, we have to extend their results to any dissipative operator on a finite dimensional space, using Lemma
1
from
[
3
]
: By mapping an arbitrary orthonormal basis of
\text{Ran}(P)
to the standard basis of
\mathbb{C}^{6}
, we view
-i\widehat{F}_{z}
and
-i\widehat{F}^{-1}_{z}
as operators on
\mathbb{C}^{6}
. Due to the map
\text{Ran}(P)\to\mathbb{C}^{6}
being unitary, the resulting operators on
\mathbb{C}^{6}
are maximally dissipative and
\|(-i\widehat{F}_{z}-x)^{-1}\|
and
\|(-i\widehat{F}^{-1}_{z}-x)^{-1}\|
are invariant. We write
A
for
-i\widehat{F}_{z}
and
-i\widehat{F}^{-1}_{z}
and estimate the integrals in (
9
) for
|z|>1
. Using the layer cake representation, we rewrite the integral of
\|(A+x)^{-1}\|^{s}
on some unit interval:

\displaystyle\int_{n}^{n+1}\|(A+x)^{-1}\|^{s}\,dx=\int_{n}^{n+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\|(A+x)^{-1}\|^{s}>t\}}(x)\,dt\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{n}^{n+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}s\,t^{s-1}\,\mathbbm{1}_{\{\|(A+x)^{-1}\|>t\}}(x)\,dt\,dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}s\,t^{s-1}\int_{n}^{n+1}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\|(A+x)^{-1}\|>t\}}(x)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}s\,t^{s-1}\int_{n}^{n+1}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\|(A+x)^{-1}\|>t\}}(x)\,dx\,dt+\int_{1}^{\infty}s\,t^{s-1}\int_{n}^{n+1}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\|(A+x)^{-1}\|>t\}}(x)\,dx\,dt.

In the first term, we bound the inner integral by
1
, and use that
\int_{0}^{1}s\,t^{s-1}\,dt<\infty
for
s>0
. For the second term, we apply Lemma
1
below with
M_{1}=I=M_{2}
:

\displaystyle\int_{n}^{n+1}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\|(A+x)^{-1}\|>t\}}(x)\,dx=|\left\{x\in[n,n+1]\,\text{ s.t. }\|(A+x)^{-1}\|>t\right\}|\leq\frac{c}{t},

where the constant
c
is independent of
A
and
n
(but does depend on the dimension of
\text{Ran}(P)
). We use that
\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{s-2}\,dt<\infty
for
s<1
to bound the second term. Thus, there exists a constant
C(s)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{n}^{n+1}\|(A+x)^{-1}\|^{s}\,dx\leq C(s)

for all maximally dissipative
A
, where
C(s)
is independent of
n
and
\text{Ran}(P)
. Using that
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{1+(|n|-1)^{2}}<\infty
, we can therefore bound the right hand side of (
9
) for all
|z|>1
. If
|z|<1
, we drop a minus sign in both norms in (
9
) and use that
i\widehat{F}_{z}
and
i\widehat{F}_{z}^{-1}
are dissipative. Repeating the arguments from above yields the bound for all
|z|<1
.

The case
x^{(i)}=y^{(j)}
is significantly easier: We can directly take
\alpha=\omega_{x}
and do not need
\beta
. The operators
F_{z}
and
\widehat{F}_{z}
act on
\mathbb{C}^{3}
, while all other estimates still hold. The integrals in (
9
) can be similarly bounded by Lemma
1
.
∎

We state a simplified version of Lemma 3.1 from
[
3
]
, which we use to bound the integrals in the proof of Theorem

.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable Hilbert space,
A
be a maximally dissipative operator with strictly positive imaginary part and
M_{1},M_{2}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
be Hilbert-Schmidt operators. Then there exists a constant
c
independent of
A
,
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
such that for any
t>0
:

\displaystyle|\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,\text{s.t. }\|M_{1}\,(A+x)^{-1}\,M_{2}\|>t\right\}|\leq c\,\|M_{1}\|_{HS}\,\|M_{2}\|_{HS}\,\frac{1}{t},

where
|\cdot|
denotes the Lebesgue measure.

4.2
The boundary of a box

We want to define a ”box”
\Lambda_{L}
for any size
L\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
, whose sides have lengths
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
, such that the Quantum Walker is unable to cross the boundary of
\Lambda_{L}
. Restricting the Walker to some box
\Lambda_{L}
is achieved by changing the coin matrix at specific lattice sites on the boundary of
\Lambda_{L}
. In other words, we want to obtain unitary operators
U_{\omega}^{(L)}=U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}\oplus U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}^{C}}
and subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{L}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{L}^{C}=\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{H}_{L}
, respectively
\mathcal{H}_{L}^{C}
, are invariant under
U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}
, respectively
U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}^{C}}
. Note that we call a subspace
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{H}
invariant under
U
if
U\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. Recalling the definition of
\mathcal{H}^{j,k}
(
4
), we use that
C_{0}
induces a fully localized Quantum Walk, see section
2
, and define the invariant subspaces:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{L}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}-L_{1}\leq j\leq L_{1}-1\\
-L_{2}\leq k\leq L_{2}-1\\
j+k>-L_{1}-L_{2}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{H}^{j,k},\;\;\mathcal{H}_{L}^{C}=\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{H}_{L}.

The choice
j+k>-L_{1}-L_{2}
is not necessary, but simplifies the structure of
\Lambda_{L}
, see Figure
3
. We call the number of
\Gamma_{A}
-vertices in
\Lambda_{L}
the volume of
\Lambda_{L}
, that is:

\displaystyle\text{vol}\left(\Lambda_{L}\right)=4L_{1}L_{2}-1\;\;\text{ and }\;\;|L|=\sqrt{L_{1}^{2}+L_{2}^{2}}.

(10)

To obtain a Quantum Walk such that these two subspaces are invariant, we need to change the coin matrix at specific lattice sites from
C
to
C_{0}
. In particular, we use the coin matrix
C_{0}
at all
\Gamma_{B}
sites in

\displaystyle\Gamma_{C_{0}}^{(L)}=\Big\{\ket{j,k}

\displaystyle\otimes\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}\text{ s.t. }\Big(-L_{1}\leq j\leq L_{1}-1,k=L_{2}-1\Big)\text{ or }

\displaystyle\Big(-L_{1}+1\leq j\leq L_{1},k=-L_{2}-1\Big)\text{ or }\Big(j=L_{1},-L_{2}\leq k\leq L_{2}-2\Big)

\displaystyle\text{ or }\Big(j=-L_{1},-L_{2}\leq k\leq L_{2}-2\Big)\Big\}.

We stress that lattice sites in
\Gamma_{C_{0}}^{(L)}
will now be split between
\mathcal{H}_{L}
and
\mathcal{H}_{L}^{C}
depending on their coin state.

Figure 3:
The coin matrices are changed from
C
to
C_{0}
on all green lattice sites. The subspace
\mathcal{H}_{L}
for
L=(3,3)
is illustrated as the inner blue area, whereas
\mathcal{H}_{L}^{C}
is the exterior red area. Note that sites in
\Gamma_{C_{0}}^{(L)}
are split depending on the coin state.

The new coin operator is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\Lambda_{L}}(\ket{x}\otimes e_{j})=\begin{cases}\ket{x}\otimes C_{0}\,e_{j}&\text{if }x\in\Gamma_{C_{0}}^{(L)}\\
\ket{x}\otimes Ce_{j}&\text{else}\end{cases}.

We define
U_{\omega}^{(L)}=D_{\omega}S\mathcal{C}^{\Lambda_{L}}
. Note that to pass from
\Lambda_{L}
to
\Lambda_{L}^{C}
, the Walker needs to cross a site in
\Gamma_{C_{0}}^{(L)}
. However, due to the choice of coin matrix, the Walker is reflected back at these sites and thus prevented from crossing the boundary of
\Lambda_{L}
. Denoting by
U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}
and
U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}^{C}}
the restrictions of
U_{\omega}^{(L)}
to
\mathcal{H}_{L}
and
\mathcal{H}_{L}^{C}
, we conclude that
U_{\omega}^{(L)}=U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}\oplus U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}^{C}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{L}
,
\mathcal{H}_{L}^{C}
are invariant under
U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}
, respectively
U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}^{C}}
.

We note that our construction can be shifted by any lattice shift
v\in\Gamma_{A}
. We can therefore also consider the operator
U
restricted to some box
\Lambda_{L}+v
. Furthermore, we stress that the operator
U_{\omega}^{(L)}
is unitary. If we had simply projected onto a subset of
l^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes\mathbb{C}^{3}
(i.e.
U^{\Lambda_{L}}=PUP+(1-P)U(1-P)
) we would have lost unitarity.

4.3
The transition operator

We define the transition operator:

\displaystyle T_{\omega}^{(L)}(C)=U_{\omega}(C)-U_{\omega}^{(L)}(C)\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).

We note that
T_{\omega}^{(L)}(C)
applied to some
x^{(i)}
can only be non-zero if the coin matrix is changed at
x\in\Lambda
. We will show that its norm is bounded by the difference
\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}
independently of
L
. For this, we will make use of the following Lemma, see e.g.
[
37
]
:

Lemma 2
.

Let
A
be an infinite matrix such that
\xi(A)=\sup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|A_{i,j}|<\infty
and
\eta(A)=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|A_{i,j}|<\infty
, then
A
is a bounded operator on
l^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with
\|A\|_{l^{2}}^{2}\leq\xi(A)\,\nu(A)
.

This allows us to prove the following Proposition:

Proposition 1
.

The norm of
T_{\omega}^{(L)}(C)
is bounded independently of
L
, that is

\displaystyle\|T_{\omega}^{(L)}(C)\|\leq 3\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

Since
D_{\omega}
and
S
are unitary, we have:

\displaystyle\|T_{\omega}^{(L)}\|=\|D_{\omega}\,S\,\big(\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{C}^{\Lambda_{L}}\big)\|=\|\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{C}^{\Lambda_{L}}\|.

As explained before, the transition operator is only non-zero on sites in
\Gamma_{C_{0}}^{(L)}
. Thus, we compute for any
x^{(i)}\in\mathcal{H}
with
x\in\Gamma_{C_{0}}^{(L)}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{y^{(j)}\in\mathcal{H}}|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{C}^{\Lambda_{L}}\,y^{(j)}\rangle|=\sum_{j=1}^{3}|\bra{e_i}C-C_{0}\,e_{j}\rangle|\leq 3\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}.

Therefore, using the notation of Lemma
2
:

\displaystyle\xi\big(\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{C}^{\Lambda_{L}}\big)\leq 3\,\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\eta\big(\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{C}^{\Lambda_{L}}\big)\leq 3\,\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}.

Applying Lemma
2
, this yields
\|T_{\omega}^{(L)}(C)\|\leq 3\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}
.
∎

4.4
Spectral gaps of the fully localized operator

The subspaces
\mathcal{H}^{j,k}
are invariant under the action of
U_{\omega}(C_{0})
, as explained in section
2
. We fix some
\mathcal{H}^{j,k}
and label its 4 random variables
\omega_{x}
as shown in Figure
4
:

Figure 4:
Labeling of the random variables
\omega_{x}
for a fixed subspace
\mathcal{H}^{j,k}
. We note that if the random variable
\omega_{0}
is at site
\ket{j,k}\otimes f_{1}
, then
\omega_{1}
is at
\ket{j,k-1}\otimes f_{2}
,
\omega_{2}
is at
\ket{j+1,k-1}\otimes f_{2}
and
\omega_{3}
is at
\ket{j,k}\otimes f_{2}
.

We let
U_{\omega}^{j,k}(C_{0})
denote the restriction of
U_{\omega}(C_{0})
to the subspace
\mathcal{H}^{j,k}
. Using the labeling of Figure
4
we have
U_{\omega}^{j,k}(C_{0})^{6}=e^{i(3\omega_{0}+\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3})}\,I_{\mathcal{H}^{j,k}}
. Moreover, we can express
U_{\omega}^{j,k}(C_{0})
in the natural ONB of
\mathcal{H}^{j,k}
, see (
4
). This allows us to directly compute its characteristic polynomial:
\chi_{U_{\omega}^{j,k}(C_{0})}(\lambda)=\lambda^{6}-e^{i(3\omega_{0}+\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3})}
. The eigenvalues of
U_{\omega}^{j,k}(C_{0})
are the sixth roots of unity of
e^{i(3\omega_{0}+\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3})}=\Theta_{\omega}^{j,k}
. Additionally, we can show that the random variables
\left(\Theta_{\omega}^{j,k}\right)_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
are independent and uniformly distributed random variables on
\mathbb{S}^{1}\subset\mathbb{C}
. This claim is obtained by using Lemma 4.1 in
[
9
]
and relies on the fact that the RVs are uniformly distributed and the RVs on each
\Gamma_{A}
vertex contribute to exactly one
\Theta_{\omega}^{j,k}
random variable.

Our next goal is the following Proposition, which estimates the probability of spectral gaps for the fully localized operator:

Proposition 2
.

There exists
c>0
such that for any
L\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
,
\eta>0
,
v\in\Gamma_{A}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\text{dist}\big(z,\sigma\left(U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}+v}(C_{0})\right)\big)\leq\eta\Big)\leq c\,\eta\,\text{vol}\left(\Lambda_{L}\right)

for
\eta\,\text{vol}\left(\lambda_{L}\right)
small enough, uniformly in
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

The proof of Proposition
2
follows the steps of
[
10
]
(Proposition 2.2) and does not require any significant model-specific changes.

Proof.

We can assume without loss of generality that
v=0
. Let
l
denote the natural spherical measure on
\mathbb{S}^{1}\subset\mathbb{C}
, normalized such that
l(\mathbb{S}^{1})=1
and consider any arc
A\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}
with length
l(A)<\frac{1}{6}
. Since the eigenvalues of
U_{\omega}^{j,k}(C_{0})
are the sixth roots of unity of the uniformly distributed random variable
\Theta_{\omega}^{j,k}
, the probability for the arc
A
to lie outside of the spectrum of
U_{\omega}^{j,k}(C_{0})
is
1-6l(A)
.
\mathcal{H}_{L}
contains
\text{vol}(\Lambda_{L})=4L_{1}L_{2}-1
blocks
\mathcal{H}^{j,k}
, which are invariant under
U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0})
. Using that the random variables
\Theta_{\omega}^{j,k}
are independent, it follows for the spectrum of
U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0})
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sigma\big(U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0})\big)\cap A=\varnothing\Big)=\big(1-6l(A)\big)^{\text{vol}(\Lambda_{L})}.

(11)

We easily obtain the following geometric property from the law of cosines: Let
B_{\eta}(z)\subset\mathbb{C}
be the closed ball centered at
z\in\mathbb{C}
of radius
\eta\in(0,1)
, then

\displaystyle l\big(B_{\eta}(z)\cap\mathbb{S}^{1}\big)<\frac{\eta}{2}.

Using this, together with (
11
), it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\text{dist}\big(z,\sigma\left(U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0})\right)\big)>\eta\Big)=\Big(1-6\,l\big(B_{\eta}(z)\cap\mathbb{S}^{1}\big)\Big)^{\text{vol}(\Lambda_{L})}\geq\big(1-3\,\eta\big)^{\text{vol}(\Lambda_{L})}.

Note that in the last step we used that
\eta
is small. Finally, this gives us

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\text{dist}\big(z,\sigma\left(U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0})\right)\big)\leq\eta\Big)\leq 1-\big(1-3\,\eta\big)^{\text{vol}(\Lambda_{L})}.

To conclude, we use the following estimate (see
[
10
]
):

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)}\left|\big(1-xy\big)^{\frac{1}{y}}-1+x\right|\leq\frac{x^{2}}{2}\;\;\;\forall\,x\in[0,1].

Using
x=3\,\eta\,\text{vol}(\Lambda_{L})
and
y=\frac{1}{\text{vol}(\Lambda_{L})}
, this finishes the proof.
∎

4.5
Polynomial decay of the boxed resolvent

We denote by
R_{\omega}^{\#}(C,z)
the resolvent of
U_{\omega}^{\#}(C)
for any coin
C\in U(3)
and any
z
in its resolvent set. Unless it is unclear, we will omit the dependence on
z
. Furthermore, we define the following equivalence relation on the elements of the orthonormal basis
\left(x^{(i)}\right)_{x\in\Gamma,i=1,2,3}
of
\mathcal{H}
:

\displaystyle x^{(i)}\sim y^{(l)}\iff\exists\,j,k\in\mathbb{Z}\;\text{ s.t. }\;x^{(i)},y^{(l)}\in\mathcal{H}^{j,k}.

Our goal is the following Proposition:

Proposition 3
.

For any
s\in(0,1)
,
p>\frac{1}{1-s}
and
a\geq 0
there exists
c>0
such that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\left|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}+v}(C,z)\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\Big)\leq\frac{c}{|L|^{a}}

uniformly in
L\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
with
L_{k}\geq 3
,
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{S}^{1}
, all coin matrices
C\in U(3)
such that
\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{|L|^{2ap+4+\frac{a}{s}}}
, all translations
v\in\Gamma_{A}
and all
x,y\in\Lambda_{L}+v
with
x^{(i)}\nsim y^{(j)}
.

We prove Proposition
3
by following the steps of
[
10
]
(Proposition 2.3) without any significant changes.

Proof.

It suffices to consider only the case
v=0
. The case
a=0
, i.e. the expectation is bounded by some constant, follows from Theorem
2
, noting that its proof works as well for
U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}
. Note that
\mathcal{H}^{j,k}
is invariant under
U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0})
, which implies

\displaystyle\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0})\,y^{(j)}\rangle=0\;\;\text{ if }\;x^{(i)}\nsim y^{(j)}.

(12)

It is due to the resolvent identity that

\displaystyle R_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C)=R_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0})+R_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C)\Big(U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0})-U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C)\Big)R_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0}).

(13)

Since the shift operator
S
only permits nearest neighbor hopping, we have:

\displaystyle\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C)\,y^{(j)}\rangle=0\;\;\text{ if }\;|x-y|\neq 1.

(14)

Moreover, due to the unitarity of
D_{\omega}
and
S
, we can use Lemma
2
to estimate the norm of
\mathcal{C}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C)-\mathcal{C}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0})
to obtain:

\displaystyle\|U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C)-U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0})\|\leq 3\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}.

(15)

Equations (
12
) - (
14
) now yield for
x^{(i)},y^{(j)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L}
with
x^{(i)}\nsim y^{(j)}
and every
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{S}^{1}
:

\displaystyle\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C)\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:res id C - C0},\eqref{eq:res local on Hjk}}{=}\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C)\Big(U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0})-U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C)\Big)R_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0})\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:res local on Hjk},\eqref{eq:U nearest nbh}}{\leq}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v^{(k)},\tilde{v}^{(l)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L}\text{ s.t}\\
\tilde{v}^{(l)}\sim y^{(j)},|v-\tilde{v}|=1\end{subarray}}\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C)\,v^{(k)}\rangle\,\langle v^{(k)}\,|\,\Big(U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0})-U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C)\Big)\,\tilde{v}^{(l)}\rangle

\displaystyle\hphantom{{} \overset{\eqref{eq:res local on Hjk}, \eqref{eq:U nearest nbh}}{=} \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v^{(k)}, \tilde{v}^{(l)} \in\mathcal{H}_L \text{ s.t} \\
\tilde{v}^{(l)} \sim y^{(j)}, |v-\tilde{v}|=1\end{subarray}} \big| }\langle\tilde{v}^{(l)}\,|\,R_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0})\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|.

Using (
15
) we can bound the second scalar product by
3\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}
. Since
U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}
is a normal operator, we can bound the third scalar product by
\frac{1}{\text{dist}\big(z,\sigma(U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0}))\big)}
. Finally, we note that the number of terms in the sum above is finite and independent of
L
. Thus, there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C)\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq c\,\frac{\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}}{\text{dist}\big(z,\sigma(U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0}))\big)}\,\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v^{(k)},\tilde{v}^{(l)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L}\\
\text{ s.t. }\tilde{v}^{(l)}\sim y^{(j)},\\
|v-\tilde{v}|=1\end{subarray}}\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C)\,v^{(k)}\rangle\big|.

(16)

For any
\eta>0
we define the set of ”good” events:

\displaystyle G_{\eta}(z)=\left\{\omega\in\mathbb{T}^{\Gamma}\text{ s.t. }\text{dist}\Big(z,\sigma\big(U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0})\big)\Big)>\eta\right\}.

We let
c>0
denote some constant that is independent of
L
and may change from line to line. By Proposition
2
we have
\mathbb{P}\left(G_{\eta}(z)^{C}\right)\leq c\,\eta\,\text{vol}\left(\Lambda_{L}\right)
. We take
p>1
as in the statement of Proposition
3
and
q>1
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and apply Hölder’s inequality:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}&\left(\chi_{G_{\eta}(z)^{C}}\,\left|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C)\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)\\
&\leq\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\chi_{G_{\eta}(z)^{C}}\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}_{=\mathbb{P}\left(G_{\eta}(z)^{C}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\,\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C)\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{sq}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}}_{\overset{\eqref{eq: fract mom bound}}{\leq}\text{const}}\leq c\big(\eta\,\text{vol}(\Lambda_{L})\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.\end{split}

(17)

Note that to use estimate (
5
), we needed that
sq<1
, which is equivalent to the assumption
p>\frac{1}{1-s}
from the statement of Proposition
3
. Setting
\eta=\frac{1}{\text{vol}\left(\Lambda_{L}\right)|L|^{ap}}
proves the claim on the set
G_{\eta}(z)^{C}
.

In order to get a bound on the set
G_{\eta}(z)
, we use the following result from perturbation theory:

Lemma 3
.

Let
A,B
be normal operators on some Hilbert space and
z\in\mathbb{C}
be in both of their resolvent sets. For any
\eta>0
, if
\text{dist}\big(z,\sigma(A)\big)>\eta
and
\|A-B\|\leq\frac{\eta}{2}
, then
\text{dist}\big(z,\sigma(B)\big)>\frac{\eta}{2}
.

Thus, if the coin
C
is chosen such that
3\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\eta}{2}
, then

\displaystyle\text{dist}\Big(z,\sigma\big(U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0})\big)\Big)>\eta\Longrightarrow\text{dist}\Big(z,\sigma\big(U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C)\big)\Big)>\frac{\eta}{2}.

(18)

Now we can estimate for all events in
G_{\eta}(z)
:

\displaystyle\chi_{G_{\eta}(z)}(\omega)\,

\displaystyle\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C,z)\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:resolvent bound}}{\leq}\chi_{G_{\eta}(z)}(\omega)\,\Big|c\,\frac{\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}}{\text{dist}\big(z,\sigma(U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0}))\big)}\,\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v^{(k)},\tilde{v}^{(l)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L}\\
\text{ s.t. }\tilde{v}^{(l)}\sim y^{(j)},\\
|v-\tilde{v}|=1\end{subarray}}\underbrace{\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C)\,v^{(k)}\rangle\big|}_{\leq\frac{1}{\text{dist}(z,\sigma(U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C)))}}\Big|^{s}.

If
\omega\in G_{\eta}(z)
, we have
\text{dist}\big(z,\sigma(U_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C_{0}))\big)>\eta
and therefore by (
18
):

\displaystyle\chi_{G_{\eta}(z)}(\omega)\,\left|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C)\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\leq\chi_{G_{\eta}(z)}(\omega)\,c\,\bigg(\frac{\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}}{\eta^{2}}\bigg)^{s}.

(19)

Now, if we choose
C
such that
\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{|L|^{2ap+4+\frac{a}{s}}}
, then together with our choice for
\eta
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}}{\eta^{2}}\leq\frac{\text{vol}\left(\Lambda_{L}\right)^{2}|L|^{2ap}}{|L|^{2ap+4+\frac{a}{s}}}=\frac{\text{vol}\left(\Lambda_{L}\right)^{2}}{|L|^{4+\frac{a}{s}}}\leq\frac{4}{|L|^{\frac{a}{s}}}.

In the last step we have used the bound
\text{vol}(\Lambda_{L})\leq 2|L|^{2}
, which can easily be obtained from (
10
). We insert this into (
19
):

\displaystyle\chi_{G_{\eta}(z)}(\omega)\,\left|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R_{\omega}^{\Lambda_{L}}(C)\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\leq\chi_{G_{\eta}(z)}(\omega)\,c\,\frac{1}{|L|^{a}}.

(20)

The estimates (
17
) and (
20
) finish the proof.
∎

We remark that in the proof we have used the following two assumptions on
C
:

\displaystyle\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{|L|^{2ap+4+\frac{a}{s}}}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\eta}{6}.

(21)

Using the definition of
\eta
and the relation
\text{vol}(\Lambda_{L})\leq 2|L|^{2}
we can show that the first implies the second whenever
|L|^{2}\geq 12
. This is always satisfied due to our choice of
\min(L_{1},L_{2})\geq 3
.

4.6
The iterative step

We start by defining the boundary of both
\Lambda_{L}
and its compliment:

\displaystyle\partial\big(\Lambda_{L}\big)

\displaystyle=\text{span}\left(x^{(i)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L}\text{ s.t. }x\in\Gamma_{C_{0}}^{(L)}\text{ or }x\text{ is adjacent to some }y\in\Gamma_{C_{0}}^{(L)}\right),

\displaystyle\partial\big(\Lambda_{L}^{C}\big)

\displaystyle=\text{span}\left(x^{(i)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L}^{C}\text{ s.t. }x\in\Gamma_{C_{0}}^{(L)}\text{ or }x\text{ is adjacent to some }y\in\Gamma_{C_{0}}^{(L)}\right).

The sets are illustrated in Figure
5
.

Figure 5:
The boundaries of
\Lambda_{L}
and
\Lambda_{L}^{C}
.

For any subset
S\subset\mathcal{H}
, we define its closure to be:

\displaystyle\overline{S}=\left\{x^{(i)}\in\mathcal{H}\,|\,\exists\,j\text{ s.t. }x^{(j)}\in S\right\}.

(22)

For any
x^{(i)}\in\mathcal{H}
let
[x^{(i)}]
denote the unique element
y^{(i)}\in\mathcal{H}
such that
y\in\Gamma_{A}
and
x^{(i)}\sim y^{(i)}
. In other words, if
x\in\Gamma_{A}
, then
[x^{(i)}]=x^{(i)}
, and if
x\in\Gamma_{B}
, then
[x^{(i)}]
is the unique
\Gamma_{A}
vertex that is in the same invariant subspace
\mathcal{H}^{j,k}
as
x^{(i)}
with the same coin state. This construction allows us to assume without loss of generality that the box
\Lambda_{L}
is centered at the origin. We will prove the following:

Proposition 4
.

There exist
L_{0}>0
and
q<1
such that for all
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{3}\right)
,
L\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
with
L_{k}\geq 3
and
|L|\geq L_{0}
there exists
\varphi(L,s)>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R(C,z)\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)\leq q\,\max_{v^{(k)}\in\overline{\partial(\Lambda_{L+\iota}^{C})}+[x^{(i)}]}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle v^{(k)}\,|\,R(C,z)\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)

for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z|<1
,
\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq\varphi
,
x^{(i)}\in\mathcal{H}
and
y^{(j)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L+2\iota}^{C}+[x^{(i)}]
, where
\iota=(2,2)
is used to expand the box
\Lambda_{L}
from size
L
to size
L+\iota
.

The proof of Proposition
4
follows mainly the steps of
[
26
]
(Proposition 3.1) with some model-specific changes. It uses two re-sampling arguments that require some adaptations and are detailed in the appendices
A
and
B
.

Remark 6
.

We can relabel
L+\iota
to
L
in the statement of Proposition
4
. We then obtain for
L_{k}\geq 5
,
|L+\iota|\geq L_{0}
and
y^{(j)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L+\iota}^{C}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R(C,z)\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)\leq q\,\max_{v^{(k)}\in\overline{\partial(\Lambda_{L}^{C})}+[x^{(i)}]}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle v^{(k)}\,|\,R(C,z)\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\right).

Proof.

To simplify the notation we assume without loss of generality that
x^{(i)}\in\mathcal{H}^{0,0}
. From now on we will omit the dependencies on
z,C
and
\omega
. We use the resolvent identity twice and obtain:

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=R^{(L)}-R^{(L)}\,T^{(L)}\,R=R^{(L)}-R^{(L)}\,T^{(L)}\,\big(R^{(L+\iota)}-R\,T^{(L+\iota)}\,R^{(L+\iota)}\big),

where
T^{(L)}
denotes the transition operator defined in section
4.3
. Using the resolvent identity above, we have:

\displaystyle\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R^{(L)}\,y^{(j)}\rangle-\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R^{(L)}\,T^{(L)}\,R^{(L+\iota)}\,y^{(j)}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R^{(L)}\,T^{(L)}\,R\,T^{(L+\iota)}\,R^{(L+\iota)}\,y^{(j)}\rangle.

The first of the three terms is zero, since
x^{(i)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L}
and
y^{(j)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L}^{C}
and both subspaces are invariant under
R^{(L)}
. Similarly, since
y^{(j)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L+\iota}^{C}
and using that
T^{(L)}
has range
1
, we conclude that the second scalar product is also zero. Inserting the identity operator between each of the remaining terms, we obtain:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v^{(k)},\tilde{v}^{(l)},\\
u^{(n)},\tilde{u}^{(m)}\in\mathcal{H}\end{subarray}}&\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R^{(L)}\,v^{(k)}\rangle\,\langle v^{(k)}\,|\,T^{(L)}\,\tilde{v}^{(l)}\rangle\,\langle\tilde{v}^{(l)}\,|\,R\,u^{(n)}\rangle\\
&\langle u^{(n)}\,|\,T^{(L+\iota)}\,\tilde{u}^{(m)}\rangle\,\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}\,|\,R^{(L+\iota)}\,y^{(j)}\rangle.\end{split}

(23)

We define the set of vertex pairs crossing the boundary of
\Lambda_{L}
:

\displaystyle B_{L}=\{(v^{(k)},\tilde{v}^{(l)})\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\text{ s.t. }\langle v^{(k)}\,|\,T^{(L)}\,\tilde{v}^{(l)}\rangle\neq 0\}.

Since
T^{(L)}
is only non-zero on sites where the coin matrix is changed from
C
to
C_{0}
, it follows that for any pair
(v^{(k)},\tilde{v}^{(l)})\in B_{L}
we have
v,\tilde{v}\in\partial(\Lambda_{L})\cup\partial(\Lambda_{L}^{C})
and the coin matrix is changed at
\tilde{v}
. Since the norm of the transition operator
T^{(L)}
is bounded independently of
L
(see Proposition
1
), there exists a constant
c_{1}>0
that depends only on the coin matrix
C
such that:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\big(\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\big)\\
&\leq c_{1}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(v^{(k)},\tilde{v}^{(l)})\in B_{L},\\
(u^{(n)},\tilde{u}^{(m)})\in B_{L+\iota},\\
v^{(k)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L},\tilde{u}^{(m)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L+\iota}^{C}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R^{(L)}\,v^{(k)}\rangle\right|^{s}\left|\langle\tilde{v}^{(l)}\,|\,R\,u^{(n)}\rangle\right|^{s}\left|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}\,|\,R^{(L+\iota)}\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\right).\end{split}

(24)

Note that even though the first and third terms are independent, they are coupled by the second term. In order to decouple the terms, we adapt the so called re-sampling argument from
[
26
]
, section 13, which is based on a strategy developed in
[
3
]
and
[
19
]
for continuum Anderson-type models. We show in the appendix
A
that there exists a constant
c_{2}>0
independent of
L
such that:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\big(\big|&\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\big)\\
&\leq c_{2}\,\sum_{u^{(n)}\in\partial(\Lambda_{L})}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R^{(L)}u^{(n)}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)\,\sum_{\tilde{u}^{(m)}\partial(\Lambda_{L+\iota}^{C})}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}\,|\,R^{(L+\iota)}\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\right).\end{split}

(25)

Using the resolvent identity
R^{L+\iota}=R+R^{(L+\iota)}\,T^{(L+\iota)}\,R
on the second term in (
25
) and inserting identities between each of the operators, we obtain for fixed
\tilde{u}^{(m)}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\Big(\left|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}\,|\,R^{(L+\iota)}\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\Big)\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}\,|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sum_{v^{(k)},\tilde{v}^{(l)}\in\mathcal{H}}\left|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}\,|\,R^{(L+\iota)}v^{(k)}\rangle\right|^{s}\,\left|\langle v^{(k)}\,|\,T^{(L+\iota)}\tilde{v}^{(l)}\rangle\right|^{s}\,\left|\langle\tilde{v}^{(l)}\,|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\bigg).

Since
\tilde{u}^{(m)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L+\iota}^{C}
, we obtain
(v^{(k)},\tilde{v}^{(l)})\in B_{L+\iota}
with
v^{(k)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L+\iota}^{C}
. Bounding
T^{(L+\iota)}
, there exists a constant
c_{3}>0
that only depends on
C
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}\,|\,

\displaystyle R^{(L+\iota)}\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\Big)\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}\,|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)

\displaystyle+c_{3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(v^{(k)},\tilde{v}^{(l)})\in B_{L+\iota},\\
v^{(k)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L+\iota}^{C}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\left|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}\,|\,R^{(L+\iota)}\,v^{(k)}\rangle\right|^{s}\,\left|\langle\tilde{v}^{(l)}\,|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\bigg).

Plugging this into (
25
) yields:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\big(\big|&\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\big)\leq c_{2}\,\bigg(\sum_{u^{(n)}\in\partial(\Lambda_{L})}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R^{(L)}\,u^{(n)}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)\bigg)\\
&\times\sum_{\tilde{u}^{(m)}\in\partial(\Lambda_{L+\iota}^{C})}\Bigg(\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}\,|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)\\
&+c_{3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(v^{(k)},\tilde{v}^{(l)})\in B_{L+\iota},\\
v^{(k)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L+\iota}^{C}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\left|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}\,|\,R^{(L+\iota)}\,v^{(k)}\rangle\right|^{s}\,\left|\langle\tilde{v}^{(l)}\,|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\bigg)\Bigg).\end{split}

(26)

A second re-sampling argument allows us to estimate (
26
): There exists another constant
c_{4}>0
independent of
L
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\big(&\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\big)\\
&\leq c_{4}\,|L|\,\sum_{u^{(n)}\in\partial(\Lambda_{L})}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R^{(L)}\,u^{(n)}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)\,\sum_{\tilde{v}^{(l)}\in\overline{\partial(\Lambda_{L+\iota}^{C})}}\mathbb{E}\left(\big|\langle\tilde{v}^{(l)}\,|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\right).\end{split}

(27)

We prove (
27
) in appendix
B
. Note that the cardinalities of
\partial(\Lambda_{L})
and
\overline{\partial(\Lambda_{L+\iota}^{C})}
are both
\mathcal{O}(|L|)
. Using
3
, this gives for the first sum in (
27
) for any
a\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\sum_{u^{(n)}\in\partial(\Lambda_{L})}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R^{(L)}\,u^{(n)}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)\leq\frac{c}{|L|^{a-1}}.

Note that in order to apply Proposition
3
we need that
\min(L_{1},L_{2})\geq 3
and
x^{(i)}\nsim u^{(n)}
. Since
x^{(i)}\in\mathcal{H}^{0,0}
and
u^{(n)}\in\partial(\Lambda_{L})
, the first assumption implies the second one. Additionally, Proposition
3
requires
\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{|L|^{2ap+4+\frac{a}{s}}}
for some
p>\frac{1}{1-s}
. Using that the cardinality of
\overline{\partial(\Lambda_{L+\iota}^{C})}
is
\mathcal{O}(|L|)
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{\tilde{v}^{(l)}\in\overline{\partial(\Lambda_{L+\iota}^{C})}}\mathbb{E}\left(\big|\langle\tilde{v}^{(l)}\,|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\right)\leq c\,|L|\,\max_{\tilde{v}^{(l)}\in\overline{\partial(\Lambda_{L+\iota}^{C})}}\mathbb{E}\left(\big|\langle\tilde{v}^{(l)}\,|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\right).

We plug the last two estimates into (
27
) and relabel
\tilde{v}^{(l)}
to
v^{(k)}
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(

\displaystyle\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\big)\leq c\,|L|^{3-a}\,\max_{v^{(k)}\in\overline{\partial(\Lambda_{L+\iota}^{C})}}\mathbb{E}\left(\big|\langle v^{(k)}\,|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\right).

for some constant
c>0
and
\|C-C_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{|L|^{2ap+4+\frac{a}{s}}}=:\varphi
. Choosing
a
and
L_{0}
big enough such that
q=c\,L_{0}^{3-a}<1
finishes the proof.
∎

Finite volume method

Repeated application of Proposition
4
allows us to prove Theorem
1
:

Proof.

We consider
L_{0}\geq 5
from Remark
6
and set
L=(L_{0},L_{0})
. The left hand side of (
2
) is bounded due to Theorem
2
, which is why it suffices to consider only

and

such that

. This condition guarantees that

, as can be verified in Figure

. Applying Proposition
4
and Remark
6
gives:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R(C,z)\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)\leq q\,\max_{v^{(k)}\in\overline{\partial(\Lambda_{L}^{C})}+[x^{(i)}]}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle v^{(k)}\,|\,R(C,z)\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\right).

for some

and all

such that

. Applying Proposition

to the right hand side now yields:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R(C,z)\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)\leq q^{2}\,\max_{\tilde{v}^{(l)}\in\overline{\partial(\Lambda_{L}^{C})}+[v^{(k)}]}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle\tilde{v}^{(l)}\,|\,R(C,z)\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\right).

∎

5
Transport using a topological index

5.1
The scattering Quantum Walk

In order to address transport properties of the QW using topological methods, we find it convenient to view the coined QW (CQW) as a scattering QW (SQW). Contrary to a CQW (see section
2
), where the Walker lives on the vertices of the graph, the Walker lives on its edges when considering a SQW (see eg.
[
33
]
). For this, we identify the subspace of incoming edges to any lattice site with its coin space. The Walker moves from edge to edge. Notice that every CQW corresponds to a SQW, and vice versa.

Since the scattering approach requires directed edges, we split each edge into two directed edges and label them by their centers. Let
\mathcal{R}
denote the set of edge centers. We connect two edge centers
u,v\in\mathcal{R}
by a directed edge
(u,v)
if and only if there exists a lattice site
x\in\Gamma
such that
u
is an incoming and
v
an outgoing edge of
x
. The pair
(u,v)
is called a link. We denote by
\mathcal{E}
the set of links. The resulting graph
G=(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{E})
is called the scattering graph and illustrated in Figure
8
.

Figure 7:
The relation between incoming subspace and coin space at
\Gamma_{A}
and
\Gamma_{B}
sites.

In the following we present the unitary map
\mathcal{V}
mapping a coined QW to its scattering version, following the steps of
[
33
]
: The operator describing the scattering QW is a unitary operator on
l^{2}(\mathcal{R})
. It moves the Walker along the links in
\mathcal{E}
. For any
x\in\Gamma
, let
\mathcal{I}_{x}
denote the three-dimensional subspace of
l^{2}(\mathcal{R})
spanned by the incoming edges of
x
. Similarly, let
\mathcal{O}_{x}
denote the subspace spanned by the outgoing edges. For any lattice site
x\in\Gamma
we identify the subspace of incoming edges
\mathcal{I}_{x}
with its coin space as shown in Figure
7
. We stress that we can also view the
\tilde{e}_{j}
as an ONB of

5.2
The index

The index we consider is the index of a pair of projections, as defined in
[
14
]
. Under the right conditions, it coincides with the trace of the flux operator of a projection on a suitable subset of edge centers in
\mathcal{R}
. The following theorem was proven in
[
12
]
:

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a unitary operator on a Hilbert space and
P
be an orthogonal projection. Define the self-adjoint operator

\displaystyle\Phi=\mathcal{U}^{*}P\mathcal{U}-P=\mathcal{U}^{*}[P,\mathcal{U}].

If
1
is an isolated eigenvalue of finite multiplicity of
\Phi^{2}
, we define the index

\displaystyle\text{ind}(\Phi):=\text{dim}\big(\text{ker}(\Phi-1)\big)-\text{dim}\big(\text{ker}(\Phi+1)\big).

Furthermore, if
\text{ind}(\Phi)\neq 0
, then

1.

if
\Phi
is compact, then the spectrum covers the whole unit circle:
\sigma(\mathcal{U})=\mathbb{S}^{1}

2.

if
\Phi
is trace class, then the absolutely continuous spectrum covers the unit circle:
\sigma_{ac}(\mathcal{U})=\mathbb{S}^{1}

For the proof we refer to
[
12
]
. It was shown in
[
14
]
that for two orthogonal projections
P
and
P^{\prime}
we have:

\displaystyle P-P^{\prime}\text{ is compact}\Rightarrow\text{ind}(\Phi)=\text{ind}(\Phi^{\prime}).

(28)

Our goal is to define a projection onto a suitable ”half-space” of
\mathcal{R}
that yields a non-zero index, and establish conditions under which the flux operator
\Phi
is compact or trace-class. From this we can deduce the existence of continuous spectrum, which is an indicator for transport. Let
Q_{x}
,
\widehat{Q}_{x}
be the projections onto
\mathcal{I}_{x},\mathcal{O}_{x}\subset l^{2}(\mathcal{R})
. We can show the following:

Proposition 5
.

For any subset
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{R}
, let
P=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{M}}\ket{u}\bra{u}
be the projection onto
\mathcal{M}
in
l^{2}(\mathcal{R})
. Let
\Phi=\mathcal{U}^{*}P\mathcal{U}-P
, then it holds for all
x\in\Gamma
:

1.

[\Phi,Q_{x}]=0

2.

\Phi Q_{x}=\mathcal{U}^{*}P\widehat{Q}_{x}\mathcal{U}-PQ_{x}

3.

\text{ind}(\Phi)
is well-defined if and only if
\exists\,c<1
and
R>0
s.t.
\sup_{|x|>R}\|\Phi Q_{x}\|\leq c
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle\text{ind}(\Phi)=\sum_{|x|\leq R}\text{dim}\big(\text{Ran}(P\widehat{Q}_{x})\big)-\text{dim}\big(\text{Ran}(PQ_{x})\big).

(29)

Proposition
5
was already shown for different models in
[
12
]
(Proposition 4.2) and
[
13
]
(Proposition 2.2). Their proof can be applied to the presented SQW on the hexagonal lattice without any significant changes and is based on the identities
\mathcal{U}Q_{x}=\widehat{Q}_{x}\mathcal{U}
and
[P,Q_{x}]=0
.

Whenever (
29
) holds we can obtain the index by counting the amount of links that enter or leave
\mathcal{M}
in some finite neighborhood of the origin. To easily count these links, we will define a path
\gamma
that parametrizes the boundary of
\mathcal{M}
. Since the scattering graph

is not planar (see Figure

as some curve in

that connects the ”faces” of

We omit the loops that arise from splitting the edges of the hexagonal lattice in two directions, since they do not affect the definition of

Let

Suppose that

-steps on

If

For any relevant path

of the coin matrix at

that corresponds to the

or

performs at

. We stress that the index pair

may change from lattice site to lattice site, depending on the step

performs. Using Proposition

, we conclude that a relevant path
\gamma
leads to a well-defined index if and only if there exists some
\varepsilon>0
such that for all
x\in\Gamma_{\gamma}
sufficiently large we have
|c^{\gamma}(x)|>\varepsilon
.

The self-adjoint operator
\Phi
is compact if and only if there exists an orthogonal set
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\Phi=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\ket{f_n}\bra{f_n}
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}\to 0
. Note that
\Phi=\bigoplus_{x\in\Gamma}\Phi\,Q_{x}
where
\Phi\,Q_{x}=0
if
x\notin\Gamma_{\gamma}
. The spectral decomposition of
\Phi
thus reduces to the spectral decomposition of the blocks
\Phi\,Q_{x}
and
\Phi
is compact if and only if
|c^{\gamma}(x)|\to 1
for
x\in\Gamma_{\gamma}
,
|x|\to\infty
. Similarly, we obtain for the trace of
|\Phi|
:

\displaystyle\tr(|\Phi|)=\tr\Big( \bigoplus_{x \in\Gamma} |\Phi| \, Q_x \Big)=\sum_{x\in\Gamma_{\gamma}}\tr(|\Phi| \, Q_x)=\sum_{x\in\Gamma_{\gamma}}\sqrt{1-|c^{\gamma}(x)|^{2}}.

(31)

The operator
\Phi
is thus trace-class if and only if the sum (
31
) converges. To summarize, while compactness of
\Phi
requires
|c^{\gamma}(x)|\to 1
for
|x|\to\infty
along
\gamma
,
\Phi
is trace-class if this convergence is fast enough. Using Theorem
3
, we have proved the following:

Theorem 4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{R}
be a subset of edges,
P
be the projection onto
\mathcal{M}
in
l^{2}(\mathcal{R})
and
\Phi=\mathcal{U}^{*}P\mathcal{U}-P
. Suppose that a path
\gamma
describing the boundary of
\mathcal{M}
is relevant (see Definition
1
). For any
x\in\Gamma_{\gamma}
, let
c^{\gamma}(x)
denote the coefficient of the coin matrix
C_{x}
that corresponds to the step
\gamma
performs at
x
, i.e.
\|\Phi\,Q_{x}\|=\sqrt{1-|c^{\gamma}(x)|^{2}}
. Then

\displaystyle\text{ind}(\Phi)\text{ is well-defined}\iff\exists\,R>0\text{ s.t.}\inf_{x\in\Gamma_{\gamma},|x|>R}|c^{\gamma}(x)|>0.

In this case, we have
\text{ind}(\Phi)=\pm 1
and

1.

\Phi
is compact
\iff

|c^{\gamma}(x)|\to 1
for
|x|\to\infty
. In this case, we have
\sigma(\mathcal{U})=\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

2.

\Phi
is trace-class
\iff

\sum_{x\in\Gamma_{\gamma}}\sqrt{1-|c^{\gamma}(x)|^{2}}<\infty
. Then, we have
\sigma_{ac}(\mathcal{U})=\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

Remark 7
.

We note that the criteria above depend only on the absolute value of the coefficients of
C_{x}
. Thus, the same results hold when introducing random phases to the model.

Remark 8
.

All criteria in Theorem
4
concern only the coin matrices on sites
x\in\Gamma_{\gamma}
, i.e. sites close to the path
\gamma
. The results are thus independent of the coin matrices on all other sites.

Example 1
.

Let

(see section
2
) for all

. We know that this choice of coin matrix induces a fully localized behavior with spectrum

. However, this is no contradiction to Theorem
4
, since at least one non-zero diagonal entry is required to define a relevant path

that leads to a non-zero index. Thus, Theorem

does not apply.

Example 2
.

Let

. In this case, we have

. However, in order to define a relevant path

that leads to a non-zero index, we require at least one non-zero off-diagonal entry in the coin matrix. Thus, no path
\gamma

.

Example 3
.

Let
C_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
1&0&0\\

for all

. We can construct a path
\gamma
that performs the
hh
-step illustrated in Figure
12
for all

and the

\gamma_{3}
and

.

This gives us:

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\bigg(\big|\langle x^{(i)}|\,\widehat{R}^{(L)}v^{(k)}\rangle\big|^{s}\;\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle\tilde{v}^{(l)}|\,Ru^{(n)}\rangle\big|^{s}\,\Big|\,J\Big)\,\big|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}|\,\widehat{R}^{(L+\iota)}y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\bigg)\bigg).

We have used that the operator
U_{\omega,\widehat{\omega}}^{(L)}
depends only on the random variables
(\omega_{r})_{r\notin J}
and
(\widehat{\omega}_{r})_{r\in J}
. Thus, when taking the expectation
\mathbb{E}
, which is with respect to the random variables
(\omega_{r})_{r\in\Gamma}
, the resolvents
\widehat{R}^{(L)}
and
\widehat{R}^{(L+\iota)}
are measurable with respect to the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
(\omega_{r})_{r\notin J}
and can be pulled out of the conditional expectation. Similar to Theorem
2
we can bound the conditional expectation:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle\tilde{v}^{(l)}\,|\,R\,u^{(n)}\rangle\big|^{s}\,\Big|\,J\Big)\leq C(s).

Note that this estimate requires
\tilde{v},u\in J
. We obtain:

\displaystyle A_{1}\leq c\;\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(L)}\,v^{(k)}\rangle\big|^{s}\;\big|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(L+\iota)}\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\Big)\bigg).

Notice that the equation above does not contain
(\omega_{r})_{r\in J}
. Since
(\omega_{r})_{r\in J}
and
(\widehat{\omega}_{r})_{r\in J}
are identically distributed, we can replace the latter by the former:

\displaystyle A_{1}\leq c\;\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R^{(L)}v^{(k)}\rangle\big|^{s}\;\big|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}\,|\,R^{(L+\iota)}\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\Big).

We stress that
v^{(k)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L}
,
\tilde{u}^{(m)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L+\iota}^{C}
and
\mathcal{H}_{L}
,
\mathcal{H}_{L+\iota}^{C}
are invariant under
R^{(L)}
and
R^{(L+\iota)}
respectively. Thus, the two scalar products above are independent random variables and we obtain:

\displaystyle A_{1}\leq c\;\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R^{(L)}v^{(k)}\rangle\big|^{s}\Big)\;\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}\,|\,R^{(L+\iota)}\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\Big).

(34)

This estimate is of the required form.

A.3
Estimate for
A_{4}

We apply (
33
) to
A_{4}
and use Hölder’s inequality twice:

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{4}&\leq\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(L)}\widehat{D}\,U^{(L)}R^{(L)}\,v^{(k)}\rangle\big|^{3s}\Big|J\Big)^{\frac{1}{3}}\,\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle\tilde{v}^{(l)}\,|\,R\,u^{(n)}\rangle\big|^{3s}\Big|J\Big)^{\frac{1}{3}}\\
&\hphantom{{}\leq\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\bigg(}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}\,|\,R^{(L+\iota)}\widehat{D}\,U^{(L+\iota)}\widehat{R}^{(L+\iota)}\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{3s}\Big|J\Big)^{\frac{1}{3}}\bigg)\bigg).\end{split}

(35)

Due to
s<\frac{1}{3}
we can bound the second expectation in (
35
) by Theorem
2
. We continue with the first term. Using the definition of
\widehat{D}
we obtain:

\displaystyle\big|

\displaystyle\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(L)}\widehat{D}\,U^{(L)}R^{(L)}\,v^{(k)}\rangle\big|^{3s}

\displaystyle\overset{s<\frac{1}{3}}{\leq}\sum_{r\in J}\sum_{l=1}^{3}\big|e^{i\omega_{r}}-e^{i\widehat{\omega}_{r}}\big|^{3s}\,\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(L)}r^{(l)}\rangle\big|^{3s}\,\big|\langle r^{(l)}\,|\,U^{(L)}R^{(L)}\,v^{(k)}\rangle\big|^{3s}.

Note that
x^{(i)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L}
, which is invariant under
\widehat{R}^{(L)}
. Thus,
r^{(l)}
is an element of
\mathcal{H}_{L}
as well. Since the number of terms in the sum above is bounded independently of
L
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big|

\displaystyle\langle x^{(i)}|\widehat{R}^{(L)}\widehat{D}\,U^{(L)}R^{(L)}v^{(k)}\rangle\big|^{3s}\leq c\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r^{(l)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L}\\
\text{s.t. }r\in J\end{subarray}}\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(L)}r^{(l)}\rangle|^{3s}\,|\langle r^{(l)}\,|\,U^{(L)}R^{(L)}v^{(k)}\rangle\big|^{3s}.

We remark that
\widehat{R}^{(L)}
depends only on
(\widehat{\omega}_{r})_{r\in J}
and
(\omega_{r})_{r\notin J}
. Since the expectation
\mathbb{E}
is with respect to
(\omega_{r})_{r\in\Gamma}
, the first scalar product is measurable with respect to the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
(\omega_{r})_{r\notin J}
. Therefore:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}&\Big(\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(L)}\widehat{D}\,U^{(L)}R^{(L)}\,v^{(k)}\rangle\big|^{3s}\Big|J\Big)\\
&\leq c\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r^{(l)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L}\\
\text{s.t. }r\in J\end{subarray}}\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(L)}r^{(l)}\rangle\big|^{3s}\,\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle r^{(l)}\,|\,U^{(L)}R^{(L)}\,v^{(k)}\rangle\big|^{3s}\,\Big|\,J\Big).\end{split}

(36)

Since
D_{\omega}
is unitary, we have:

\displaystyle\big|\langle r^{(l)}\,|\,U^{(L)}R^{(L)}\,v^{(k)}\rangle\big|=\big|\langle e^{i\omega_{r}}\,r^{(l)}\,|\,I+z\,R^{(L)}\,v^{(k)}\rangle\big|.

Using this, Theorem
2
and
|z|<1
, we can bound the expectation in (
36
) and obtain for the first term in (
35
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\Big(\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(L)}\widehat{D}\,U^{(L)}R^{(L)}\,v^{(k)}\rangle\big|^{3s}\Big|J\Big)\leq c\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r^{(l)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L}\\
\text{s.t. }r\in J\end{subarray}}\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(L)}\,r^{(l)}\rangle\big|^{3s}.

We can obtain a similar estimate for the third term in (
35
): As above, we use the definition of
\widehat{D}
, apply the expectation, utilize that
\widehat{R}^{(L+\iota)}
is measurable with respect to
(\omega_{r})_{r\notin J}
and apply Theorem
2
to obtain:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}|R^{(L+\iota)}\widehat{D}U^{(L+\iota)}\widehat{R}^{(L+\iota)}y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{3s}\Big|J\Big)\leq c\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{r}^{(k)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L+\iota}^{C}\\
\text{s.t. }\tilde{r}\in J\end{subarray}}\big|\langle\tilde{r}^{(k)}|U^{(L+\iota)}\widehat{R}^{(L+\iota)}y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{3s}.

Due to our assumption
y^{(j)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L+2\iota}^{C}
we have
y\notin J
, as can be varified in Figure
6
: The site of
J
closest to
y
is
u
, which is at distance
1
to
\tilde{u}
. The site
\tilde{u}
is colored red in Figure
6
and since
y
is outside a box of size
L+2\iota
, the distance between
y
and
u
is at least
3
. We stress that here the assumption
y^{(j)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L+\iota}^{C}
is not enough. Since
D_{\omega,\widehat{\omega}}
is unitary, it follows:

\displaystyle\big|\langle\tilde{r}^{(k)}|U^{(L+\iota)}\widehat{R}^{(L+\iota)}y^{(j)}\rangle\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\langle\tilde{r}^{(k)}|I+z\,\widehat{R}^{(L+\iota)}y^{(j)}\rangle\big|=|z|\,\big|\langle\tilde{r}^{(k)}|\widehat{R}^{(L+\iota)}y^{(j)}\rangle\big|.

Using
|z|<1
, this yields for the third term in (
35
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}\,|\,R^{(L+\iota)}\widehat{D}U^{(L+\iota)}\widehat{R}^{(L+\iota)}y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{3s}\Big|J\Big)\leq c\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{r}^{(k)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L+\iota}^{C}\\
\text{s.t. }\tilde{r}\in J\end{subarray}}\big|\langle\tilde{r}^{(k)}|\widehat{R}^{(L+\iota)}y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{3s}.

Since
J
has a fixed finite number of elements, we have
\big(\sum_{j\in J}\alpha_{j}^{3s}\big)^{\frac{1}{3}}\leq c\sum_{j\in J}\alpha_{j}^{s}
for some constant
c
and all
\alpha_{j}>0
. Using the obtained bounds for the three terms in (
35
), we conclude:

\displaystyle A_{4}\leq c\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r^{(l)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L}\\
\text{s.t. }r\in J\end{subarray}}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{r}^{(k)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L+\iota}^{C}\\
\text{s.t. }\tilde{r}\in J\end{subarray}}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(L)}r^{(l)}\rangle\big|^{s}\,\big|\langle\tilde{r}^{(k)}\,|\,\widehat{R}^{(L+\iota)}\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\Big).

We note that the last line only depends on the random variables
(\omega_{r})_{r\notin J}
and
(\widehat{\omega}_{r})_{r\in J}
. Since
(\omega_{r})_{r\in J}
and
(\widehat{\omega}_{r})_{r\in J}
are independent and identically distributed, we can replace the latter by the former. The two scalar products inside the expectation are then independent random variables by the same reasoning as for
A_{1}
. This yields:

\displaystyle A_{4}\leq c\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r^{(l)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L}\\
\text{s.t. }r\in J\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle|x^{(i)}\,|\,R^{(L)}r^{(l)}\rangle\big|^{s}\Big)\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{r}^{(k)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L+\iota}^{C}\\
\text{s.t. }\tilde{r}\in J\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle\tilde{r}^{(k)}\,|\,R^{(L+\iota)}\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\Big).

(37)

A.4
Combining the estimates

We can interpolate the arguments used for
A_{1}
and
A_{4}
to obtain similar estimates for
A_{2}
and
A_{3}
. By definition of
v,\tilde{v},u,\tilde{u}
we have
\{r\in J\,|\,\exists\,l\text{ s.t. }r^{(l)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L}\}=\{v,\tilde{v}\}
and
\{r\in J\,|\,\exists\,l\text{ s.t. }r^{(l)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L+\iota}^{C}\}=\{u,\tilde{u}\}
. This yields for
A_{4}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{v,\tilde{v},u,\tilde{u}}A_{4}(v,\tilde{v},u,\tilde{u})\leq

\displaystyle c\bigg(\sum_{u^{(n)}\in\partial(\Lambda_{L})}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle x^{(i)}\,|\,R^{(L)}u^{(n)}\rangle\big|^{s}\Big)\bigg)

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\sum_{\tilde{u}^{(m)}\in\partial(\Lambda_{L+\iota}^{C})}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}\,|\,R^{(L+\iota)}\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\Big)\bigg).

Similar estimates hold for the easier terms
A_{1},A_{2}
and
A_{3}
. Using (
24
) and (
32
), we finally obtain (
25
). We stress that all constants obtained in the appendix
A
are independent of
L
.

Appendix B
Second re-sampling argument

Analogously to the proof of Proposition 13.2 in
[
26
]
, we derive equation (
27
) from (
26
). Since the methods used are similar to the ones in appendix
A
, we will omit details. Note that the term containing
u^{(n)}
in (
26
) is already in the shape of (
27
), so we only have to deal with the second term. We let
c>0
denote some constant independent of
L
that may change from line to line. Fixing
\tilde{u}^{(m)}\in\partial(\Lambda_{L+\iota}^{C})
,
(v^{(k)},\tilde{v}^{(l)})\in B_{L+\iota}
with
v^{(k)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L+\iota}^{C}
, we define
\tilde{J}=\{\tilde{u},v,\tilde{v}\}
. Similar to our approach in appendix
A
we re-sample the random variables on all sites in
\tilde{J}
. Let
(\tilde{\omega}_{r})_{r\in\tilde{J}}
be an i.i.d. family of random variables uniformly distributed on the torus
\mathbb{T}
. Let
D_{\omega,\tilde{\omega}}
use the phase
\tilde{\omega}_{r}
on all sites
r\in\tilde{J}
and
\omega_{r}
on all others. Let
U_{\omega,\tilde{\omega}}=D_{\omega,\tilde{\omega}}U
and
\tilde{R}
be the re-sampled Quantum Walk and its resolvent. To simplify the notation we define
\tilde{D}=\sum_{r\in\tilde{J}}\big(e^{i\omega_{r}}-e^{i\tilde{\omega}_{r}}\big)\,P_{r}
. Using the resolvent identity
R=\tilde{R}-R\,\tilde{D}\,U\,\tilde{R}
, we obtain for the last expectation in (
26
):

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\bigg(\left|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}\,|\,R^{(L+\iota)}\,v^{(k)}\rangle\right|^{s}\,\left|\langle\tilde{v}^{(l)}\,|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle\right|^{s}\bigg)\\
&\overset{s<1}{\leq}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\left|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}\,|\,R^{(L+\iota)}\,v^{(k)}\rangle\right|^{s}\,\big|\langle\tilde{v}^{(l)}\,|\,\tilde{R}\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\bigg)\\
&+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\left|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}\,|\,R^{(L+\iota)}\,v^{(k)}\rangle\right|^{s}\,\big|\langle\tilde{v}^{(l)}\,|\,R\tilde{D}U\tilde{R}\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\bigg)=B_{1}+B_{2}.\end{split}

(38)

We write
\mathbb{E}(...|\tilde{J})
for the conditional expectation with respect to the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
(\omega_{r})_{r\notin\tilde{J}}
. We proceed as in section
A.2
and apply the law of total expectation (equation (
33
)) to
B_{1}
, use that
\tilde{R}
is independent of the random variables
(\omega_{r})_{r\in\tilde{J}}
and apply Theorem
2
. We can then replace the random variables
(\tilde{\omega}_{r})_{r\in\tilde{J}}
by
(\omega_{r})_{r\in\tilde{J}}
to obtain:

\displaystyle B_{1}\leq c\,\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle\tilde{v}^{(l)}\,|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\Big).

(39)

The law of total expectation, Hölder’s inequality and Theorem
2
give for
B_{2}
:

\displaystyle B_{2}\leq c\,\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\mathbb{E}\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle\tilde{v}^{(l)}\,|\,R\tilde{D}U\tilde{R}\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{2s}\,\Big|\,\tilde{J}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big).

(40)

Using the definition of
\tilde{D}
and Theorem
2
, we obtain for the inner expectation:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\Big(\big|\langle\tilde{v}^{(l)}\,|\,R\tilde{D}U\tilde{R}\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{2s}\,\Big|\,\tilde{J}\Big)\leq c\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r^{(k)}\in\mathcal{H}\\
\text{s.t. }r\in\tilde{J}\end{subarray}}\big|\langle r^{(k)}\,|\,U\tilde{R}\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{2s}.

(41)

Since
y^{(j)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L+2\iota}^{C}
, we have
y\notin\tilde{J}
, by the same argument as in appendix
A
. Again, we stress that the requirement
y^{(j)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L+\iota}^{C}
would not be enough. This yields:

\displaystyle\big|\langle r^{(k)}|\,U\tilde{R}\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|=\big|\langle r^{(k)}|\,I+z\tilde{R}\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|=|z|\,|\langle r^{(k)}|\,\tilde{R}\,y^{(j)}\rangle|.

(42)

Since
|\tilde{J}|\leq 3
, we have
\big(\sum_{j\in J}\alpha_{j}^{2s}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq c\sum_{j\in J}\alpha_{j}^{s}
for all
\alpha_{j}>0
. Using
|z|<1
, we insert (
41
) and (
42
) into (
40
) and replace
\tilde{\omega}_{r}
by to
\omega_{r}
to obtain:

\displaystyle B_{2}\leq c\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r^{(k)}\in\mathcal{H}\\
\text{s.t. }r\in\tilde{J}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\Big(|\langle r^{(k)}\,|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle|^{s}\Big).

(43)

Plugging the estimates (
38
), (
39
) and (
43
) into (
26
) we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(\big|

\displaystyle\langle x^{(i)}|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\big)\leq c\,\bigg(\sum_{u^{(n)}\in\partial(\Lambda_{L})}\mathbb{E}\left(\big|\langle x^{(i)}|\,R^{(L)}\,u^{(n)}\rangle\big|^{s}\right)\bigg)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\tilde{u}^{(m)}\in\partial(\Lambda_{L+\iota}^{C})}\Bigg(\mathbb{E}\left(\big|\langle\tilde{u}^{(m)}|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\right)+c\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(v^{(k)},\tilde{v}^{(l)})\in B_{L+\iota},\\
v^{(k)}\in\mathcal{H}_{L+\iota}^{C}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|\langle\tilde{v}^{(l)}|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle\big|^{s}\Big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{{} \times\sum_{\tilde{u}^{(m)} \in\partial(\Lambda_{L+\iota}^C)} \Bigg( }+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r^{(k)}\in\mathcal{H}\\
r\in\{v,\tilde{v},\tilde{u}\}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\Big(|\langle r^{(k)}|\,R\,y^{(j)}\rangle|^{s}\Big)\Bigg).

We stress that
|\partial(\Lambda_{L+\iota}^{C})|=\mathcal{O}(|L|)
and
|B_{L+\iota}|=\mathcal{O}(|L|)
and note that all scalar products above are of the form
\langle r^{(k)}|R\,y^{(j)}\rangle
with
r^{(k)}\in\overline{\partial(\Lambda_{L+\iota}^{C})}
. Rearranging the terms in the sum above, this finishes the proof of equation (
27
).

Lebesgue constants for the Walsh system and the discrepancy of the van der Corput sequence
Josef Dick, Friedrich Pillichshammer

Abstract
In this short note we report on a coincidence of two mathematical quantities that, at first glance, have little to do with each other. On the one hand, there are the Lebesgue constants of the Walsh function system that play an important role in approximation theory, and on the other hand there is the star discrepancy of the van der Corput sequence that plays a prominent role in uniform distribution theory. Over the decades, these two quantities have been examined in great detail independently of each other and important results have been proven. Work in these areas has been carried out independently, but as we show here, they actually coincide. Interestingly, many theorems have been discovered in both areas independently, but some results have only been known in one area but not in the other.



Lebesgue Constants.

For a given system of orthonormal functions
\Phi=\{\phi_{k}\ :\ k=0,1,\ldots\}
in
L_{2}([a,b])
the
n
-th Lebesgue function is defined as

L_{n}^{\Phi}(u):=\int_{a}^{b}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\phi_{k}(x)\phi_{k}(u)\right|\,{\rm d}x\qquad\mbox{for $u\in[a,b]$.}

It follows easily from the fact
S_{n}(f)(u)=\int_{a}^{b}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\phi_{k}(x)\phi_{k}(u)\right)f(u)\,{\rm d}u
that

L_{n}^{\Phi}(u)=\sup_{\|f\|_{C([a,b])}\leq 1}|S_{n}(f)(u)|\qquad\mbox{for $u\in[a,b]$,}

where
\|\cdot\|_{C([a,b])}
is the uniform norm and
S_{n}(f)(u)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{f}(k)\phi_{k}(u)
is the
n
-th partial sum of the Fourier expansion of
f
with respect to the system
\Phi
. If the
n
-th Lebesgue function
L_{n}^{\Phi}
is constant over
[a,b]
, then its function value is called the
n
-th Lebesgue constant, which is denoted by
L_{n}^{\Phi}
or simply by
L_{n}
. Lebesgue functions/constants are a fundamental tool in approximation theory; see, e.g.,
[
3
,
12
,
16
,
20
]
.

In this note we consider as an orthonormal system the Walsh functions and report a connection of the corresponding Lebesgue functions to the star discrepancy of the van der Corput sequence, which is an important quantity in uniform distribution and discrepancy theory. Both Lebesgue functions for the Walsh system and star discrepancy of van der Corput sequence are well-studied objects in literature, but so far it has not been observed that both quantities coincide. This is, at least for us, a very interesting connection between concepts from different branches of mathematics that allows to transfer results for one measure to the other and vice versa.

Walsh functions.

For a nonnegative integer
k
with base
2
representation
k=k_{0}+k_{1}2+\cdots+k_{m}2^{m}
the
k
-th
Walsh function

{\rm wal}_{k}:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, periodic with period one, is defined by

\displaystyle{\rm wal}_{k}(x):=(-1)^{x_{1}k_{0}+x_{2}k_{1}+\cdots+x_{m+1}k_{m}},

when
x\in[0,1)
has (canonical) base
2
representation
x=\frac{x_{1}}{2}+\frac{x_{2}}{2^{2}}+\cdots
. The system
\mathcal{W}=\{{\rm wal}_{k}\ :\ k=0,1,\ldots\}
is a complete orthonormal system in
L_{2}([0,1])
. For information about Walsh functions see
[
10
,
16
]
or
[
6
, Appendix A]
.

In
[
10
, Section 5]
Fine introduced the Walsh Dirichlet kernel

D_{n}(x,u):=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\rm wal}_{k}(x){\rm wal}_{k}(u)

and the so-called Lebesgue functions

L_{n}(x):=\int_{0}^{1}|D_{n}(x,u)|\,{\rm d}u

(for simplicity we write
L_{n}(x)
rather than
L_{n}^{\mathcal{W}}(x)
) for the Walsh systems and proved several interesting results. In the first place he proved that the
L_{n}(x)
are in fact independent of
x
(see
[
10
, p.386]
) and for this reason they coincide with their Lebesgue constants
L_{n}
. In
[
10
, Theorem IX]
several properties of the Lebesgue constants
L_{n}
are summarized.

Theorem 1
(Fine)

The Lebesgue constants
L_{n}
of the Walsh system satisfy

L_{n}=\nu-\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq\nu}2^{n_{i}-n_{j}},

(1)

where
n
is of the form
n=2^{n_{1}}+2^{n_{2}}+\cdots+2^{n_{\nu}}
with integer exponents
n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{\nu}\geq 0
. Furthermore, the following properties hold:

1.

L_{2n}=L_{n}
and
L_{2n+1}=(1+L_{n}+L_{n+1})/2
.

2.

L_{n}=O(\log n)
.

3.

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}L_{k}=\frac{\log n}{4\log 2}+O(1)
.

4.

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left[L_{n}-\left(\frac{4}{9}+\frac{\log 3}{3\log 2}+\frac{\log n}{3\log 2}\right)\right]=0
.

5.

the generating function for
L_{n}
is
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}L_{n}z^{n}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{z}{(1-z)^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\frac{1-z^{2^{k}}}{1+z^{2^{k}}}
for
|z|<1
.

Further results on the Lebesgue constants for the Walsh system are shown in
[
1
,
2
]
.

Star discrepancy.

For a sequence
X=(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
in
[0,1)
the
n
-th discrepancy function is defined as

\Delta_{X,n}(t):=\frac{\#\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\ :\ k<n,\ x_{k}\in[0,t)\}}{n}-t\qquad\mbox{for $t\in[0,1]$}.

The star discrepancy of the initial
n
terms of
X
is then defined as

D_{n}^{*}(X):=\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|\Delta_{X,n}(t)|.

This is a quantitative measure for the irregularity of distribution of the sequence
X
in
[0,1)
. The sequence
X
is uniformly distributed in the sense of Weyl
[
19
]
iff
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}D_{n}^{\ast}(X)=0
. The smaller
D_{n}^{\ast}(X)
, the better the
n
initial terms of
X
are distributed in
[0,1]
. For an introduction to uniform distribution and discrepancy, see, e.g.,
[
13
]
.

Star discrepancy of the van der Corput sequence.

The prototype of a uniformly distributed sequence is the van der Corput sequence (see
[
18
]
or
[
9
]
) whose construction is based on the reflection of the binary digits of nonnegative integers
k
around the binary point. If
k
has binary expansion
k=k_{0}+k_{1}2+k_{2}2^{2}+\cdots
(which is of course finite) with
k_{j}\in\{0,1\}
for
j=0,1,2,\ldots
, then the
k
-th element
y_{k}
of the van der Corput sequence
Y^{{\rm vdC}}=(y_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is given by

y_{k}:=\frac{k_{0}}{2}+\frac{k_{1}}{2^{2}}+\frac{k_{2}}{2^{3}}+\cdots.

The star discrepancy of the van der Corput sequence is intensively studied in literature, see, e.g.,
[
4
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
11
,
17
]
. See also the survey
[
9
]
for many other references.

Consider the star discrepancy
D_{n}^{\ast}(Y^{{\rm vdC}})
of the first
n
terms of the van der Corput sequence and set
d_{n}:=nD_{n}^{\ast}(Y^{{\rm vdC}})
to be the non-normalized star discrepancy.

Theorem 2

The
n
-th Lebesgue constant
L_{n}
for the Walsh system is exactly the nonnormalized star discrepancy
d_{n}
of the first
n
terms of the van der Corput sequence, that is

d_{n}=L_{n}.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
be of the form
n=2^{n_{1}}+2^{n_{2}}+\cdots+2^{n_{\nu}}
with integer exponents
n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{\nu}\geq 0
. For the initial
n
terms of
Y^{\rm vdC}
we have

\displaystyle\{y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-1}\}=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{i=1}^{\nu}\left\{y_{\ell}\ :\ \ell\in\{2^{n_{1}}+\cdots+2^{n_{i-1}},\ldots,2^{n_{1}}+\cdots+2^{n_{i-1}}+2^{n_{i}}-1\}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{i=1}^{\nu}\left\{\frac{k}{2^{n_{i}}}+\frac{1}{2^{n_{i-1}+1}}+\frac{1}{2^{n_{i-2}+1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{2^{n_{1}+1}}\ :\ k\in\{0,1,\ldots,2^{n_{i}}-1\}\right\},

where we put
2^{n_{1}}+\cdots+2^{n_{i-1}}:=0
if
i=1
.

It is well known that the star discrepancy of the van der Corput sequence is nonnegative (see
[
14
, Remark 3]
) and twice the
L_{1}([0,1])
-norm of the discrepancy function (see, for example,
[
15
]
). Hence

\displaystyle d_{n}=

\displaystyle 2n\int_{0}^{1}\Delta_{Y^{\rm vdc},n}(t)\,{\rm d}t=2\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(\boldsymbol{1}_{[0,t)}(y_{\ell})-t)\,{\rm d}t

(2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}-y_{\ell}\right)=2\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n_{i}}-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{k}{2^{n_{i}}}-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\frac{1}{2^{n_{j}+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left(\frac{\nu}{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}2^{n_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\frac{1}{2^{n_{j}+1}}\right)=\nu-\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq\nu}2^{n_{i}-n_{j}}=L_{n},

where the last equality is (
1
) from Theorem
1
.

\hfill\Box

The representation
d_{n}=\nu-\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq\nu}2^{n_{i}-n_{j}}
for the star discrepancy of
Y^{\rm vdC}
is also mentioned (in an equivalent form) in
[
17
, p. 61]
.

Applications.

Theorem
2
can now be used to transfer results for the star discrepancy of the van der Corput sequence to results for the Lebesgue constants for the Walsh system and vice versa. In particular Items 1-4 from Theorem
1
by Fine have also been proven in the context of discrepancy of the van der Corput sequence by Béjian and Faure
[
4
,
5
]
. Therein, the estimate
d_{n}\leq\frac{\log n}{3\log 2}+1
from
[
5
, Théorème 3]
yields a refinement of item
2
in Theorem
1
. Furthermore, it is known (see
[
8
, Lemme 4.1]
) that for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\max_{n\in[2^{r-1},2^{r}]}d_{n}=\frac{r}{3}+\frac{7}{9}+\frac{(-1)^{r}}{9\cdot 2^{r-1}}

and the maximum is attained for
n=(2^{r+1}+(-1)^{r})/3
. Thus, for all
n
of this form we have
d_{n}\geq\frac{\log n}{3\log 2}+O(1)
. This result has been proven (in a slightly weaker form) for the Lebesgue constants for the Walsh system in
[
2
, Theorem 2]
.

From Theorem
2
we also obtain new representations of the Lebesgue constant or star discrepancy of the van der Corput sequence that have not been known so far. For instance, the definition of
L_{n}
leads to a new representation of
D_{n}^{\ast}(Y^{{\rm vdC}})
via

D_{n}^{\ast}(Y^{{\rm vdC}})=\frac{d_{n}}{n}=\frac{L_{n}}{n}=\int_{0}^{1}\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\rm wal}_{k}(x)\right|\,{\rm d}x.

Using this formula, we can derive another new formula for the star discrepancy of the van der Corput sequence. We have

\displaystyle D_{n}^{\ast}(Y^{{\rm vdC}})=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{2^{n_{1}+1}-1}\int_{m2^{-n_{1}-1}}^{(m+1)2^{-n_{1}-1}}\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\rm wal}_{k}(x)\right|\,{\rm d}x.

For
x\in[m2^{-n_{1}-1},(m+1)2^{-n_{1}-1})
we can write
{\rm wal}_{k}(x)={\rm wal}_{m^{\prime}}(y_{k})
, where
m^{\prime}=m^{\prime}(m)=m_{n_{1}}+m_{n_{1}-1}2+\cdots+m_{0}2^{n_{1}}
for
m
with binary expansion
m=m_{0}+m_{1}2+\cdots+m_{n_{1}}2^{n_{1}}
. Then

\displaystyle D^{\ast}_{n}(Y^{{\rm vdC}})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n_{1}+1}}\sum_{m^{\prime}=0}^{2^{n_{1}+1}-1}\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\rm wal}_{m^{\prime}}(y_{k})\right|.

On the other hand, a well known representation for the star discrepancy of the van der Corput sequence according to
[
5
, Théorème 1]
leads to a new representation for
L_{n}
via

L_{n}=d_{n}=nD_{n}^{\ast}(Y^{{\rm vdC}})=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\left\|\frac{n}{2^{r}}\right\|=\sum_{r=1}^{m}\left\|\frac{n}{2^{r}}\right\|+\frac{n}{2^{m}}\ \ \ \mbox{ whenever $1\leq n\leq 2^{m}$},

where
\|x\|=\min(\{x\},1-\{x\})
is the distance of a real
x
to the nearest integer. This formula immediately implies the recursion in Item 1 of Theorem
1
(note that
d_{1}=L_{1}=1
), which was already known for
d_{n}
before (see
[
5
, p. 13-07]
).

Also the generating function for
L_{n}
(see Item 5 in Theorem
1
) was unknown in terms of discrepancy, which can now be formulated as

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d_{n}z^{n}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{z}{(1-z)^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\frac{1-z^{2^{k}}}{1+z^{2^{k}}}\qquad\mbox{for $|z|<1$.}

For the star discrepancy of the van der Corput sequence we know a central limit theorem from
[
7
]
, which may now be formulated in terms of Lebesgue constants for the Walsh system. Accordingly, for every real
y
and for
N\rightarrow\infty
we have

\frac{1}{N}\#\left\{n<N\ :\ L_{n}\leq\frac{\log n}{4\log 2}+\frac{y}{4}\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{3\log 2}}\right\}=\Phi(y)+o(1),

where

\Phi(y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{y}\exp(-t^{2}/2)\,{\rm d}t
is the Gaussian cumulative distribution function.
That is, the Lebesgue constants for the Walsh system satisfy a central limit theorem.

As the final example of this note we reformulate another result for the Lebesgue constants to obtain a so far unknown result for the star discrepancy of the van der Corput sequence. For
t\in[0,1]
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
let
n_{t}(m):=\lfloor 2^{m}(1+t)\rfloor
. Then
[
2
, Theorem 5]
in terms of discrepancy of the van der Corput sequence states:

1.

For almost all
t\in[0,1]
we have

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{d_{n_{t}(m)}}{\log n_{t}(m)}=\frac{1}{4\log 2}.

2.

For all dyadic rational
t\in[0,1]
we have

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{d_{n_{t}(m)}}{\log n_{t}(m)}=0.

3.

There exists a dense subset
A\subseteq[0,1]
such that

\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{d_{n_{t}(m)}}{\log n_{t}(m)}=0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{d_{n_{t}(m)}}{\log n_{t}(m)}=\frac{1}{3\log 2}

for all
t\in A
.

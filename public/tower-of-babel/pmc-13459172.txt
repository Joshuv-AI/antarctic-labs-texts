Single aortic sinus repair with double annular sutures in acute type A aortic dissection: a single center case series of seven patients
Authors: Hanna Jung, Youngok Lee, Chae-Min Bae, Deok Heon Lee, Soo Jung Park, Tak-Hyuk Oh, Gun Jik Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWe evaluated a modified surgical technique designed to preserve and restore the aortic root geometry in patients with acute type A aortic dissection (ATAAD) when the intimal tear is limited to a single coronary sinus.MethodsBetween January 2015 and December 2021, 160 patients with ATAAD underwent emergency open replacement of the ascending aorta. Most patients (137, 85%) underwent supracoronary ascending aorta replacement. Thirteen patients underwent aorta root replacement with a valved conduit and three underwent valve-sparing aortic root replacement. The remaining seven patients, in whom intimal tears were confined to single coronary sinus, underwent limited root repair using our modified surgical technique: single aortic sinus repair with double annular sutures.ResultsAmong the seven patients who underwent a single aortic sinus repair, five had coronary sinus dissection, and two had ascending aortic dissection associated with an aneurysmal change of the sinus of Valsalva. The median age at surgery was 59 years (range, 50–74 years). There were no reoperations for postoperative bleeding and no in-hospital mortality. Background

Acute type A aortic dissection (ATAAD) is a life-threating condition associated with high morbidity and mortality rate [1, 2]. When the aortic dissection extends proximally to involve the aortic root, surgical management becomes particularly challenging for cardiac surgeons. The selection of an appropriate treatment strategy for a dissected aortic root requires a comprehensive evaluation of the patient’s anatomical characteristics, demographic profile, comorbid conditions, and physiologic status at the time of surgical intervention [3]. The decision to perform aortic root intervention is primarily determined by the integrity of the aortic valve, the anatomical extent of the root involvement and the underlying tissue pathology.

In recent years, aortic root replacement using a valved conduit and valve-sparing root replacement (VSRR) techniques has become widely accepted [1, 2, 4–7]. Although elective studies have demonstrated favourable long-term durability, evidence supporting these approaches in the setting of ATAAD remains limited, particularly with regard to patient selection and perioperative safety [8]. Furthermore, it remains uncertain whether such complex surgical procedures are always necessary when the aortic dissection is limited to a single coronary sinus of Valsalva, with otherwise preserved aortic cusps and annuli, or in cases requiring urgent or emergency surgical intervention [9–11].

Limited root repair using selective sinus repair was first described by Westaby and Urbanski et al. in 2002 [9, 10]. These techniques typically involve the use of patient-tailored, U-shaped grafts sutured into the resected portion of the affected sinus. The graft is secured to the aortic wall using running sutures that pass through the aortic annulus. However, these approaches do not incorporate reinforcement of the aortic wall or suture lines, as complete excision of the dissected tissue has traditionally been considered sufficient [9–11].

In the present study, we modified this approach by incorporating a double annular suture technique to provide additional reinforcement for securing the aortic remnants and vascular grafts, with the aim of enhancing root stability and hemostasis. We evaluated the clinical outcomes associated with this modified surgical technique-single aortic sinus repair with double annular sutures-in patients with ATAAD.

Methods

Patients

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Kyungpook National University Hospital. We retrospectively reviewed the medical records of patients diagnosed with ATAAD who underwent surgical treatment at our institution and analyzed the surgical and imaging data.

Between January 2015 and December 2021, a total of 160 patients with ATAAD underwent emergency open surgical repair of the ascending aorta. Supracoronary ascending aorta replacement with a vascular graft was performed in 137 patients (85%) who had no aortic root involvement [12, 13]. Patients presenting with root aneurysm or intimal tears involving the aortic root underwent aortic root replacement. Thirteen patients underwent ascending aorta replacement with a valved conduit (Modified Bentall procedure), and three underwent VSRR (David procedure). In general, aortic root replacement was reserved for selected patients with underlying concomitant connective tissue disorders, such as annuloaortic ectasia associated with Marfan syndrome, or other genetic aortic diseases.

Since 2015, to reduce operative burden in emergency conditions, we have adopted a modified technique-single aortic sinus repair with double annular sutures-in selected patients with ATAAD presenting with intimal tears confined to a single coronary sinus of Valsalva. This approach was applied in our center specifically in patients without significant aortic valve leaflet pathology, root aneurysm, or known genetic aortic diseases. Seven of the 160 patients underwent this modified surgical procedure.

Preoperative contrast-enhanced chest computed tomography (CT) was performed in all patients, as it is the primary diagnostic modality for ATAAD. However, CT often fails to delineate the extent of aortic root involvement or localize the intimal tear accurately. Therefore, the final surgical strategy was determined based on the intraoperative transesophageal echocardiography and the direct visual assessment of the aortic root. Postoperative transthoracic echocardiography was performed prior to discharge, and follow-up was continued after discharge through outpatient clinic visits. Cardiac function was assessed, including the degree of aortic regurgitation (AR), left ventricular ejection fraction, and regional wall motion abnormalities. The first outpatient visit was scheduled 1 week after discharge, followed by monthly visits for the first 6 months. Thereafter, follow-up intervals were individualized according to each patient’s clinical condition. Postoperative contrast-enhanced chest CT was performed at 6–12 months after discharge.

Operative technique

Cardiopulmonary bypass was established using the femoral artery, axillary artery, or ascending aorta for arterial inflow, depending on the extent of aortic pathology and the urgency of the procedure. Following aortic cross-clamping and cardioplegic arrest, the ascending aorta was transected, and the sinus of Valsalva was carefully inspected. When an intimal tear was identified within the aortic root and confined to a single coronary sinus of Valsalva, single aortic sinus repair with double annular sutures was performed (Fig. 1).

After confirming the primary tear, the dissected sinus of Valsalva was excised, leaving an approximately 10-mm rim of aortic wall attached to the aortic valve. In cases involving the left or right coronary cusp (RCC), the corresponding coronary button was mobilized and trimmed (Fig. 1a). Careful dissection was then performed to separate the affected aortic root from surrounding structures, ensuring adequate exposure for subsequent reconstruction. The appropriate aortic graft size was selected by measuring the aortic annulus using a valve sizer. The vascular graft diameter was chosen to match or exceed the annular measurement by 1–2 mm. Collagen-impregnated woven double-velour polyester grafts (Hemashield Platinum, Maquet) were used. The graft was marked at approximately 120° intervals to guide anatomical alignment and reconstruction. The graft was tailored into a U-shaped configuration to match the geometry of the excised aortic sinus. Double annular sutures were applied.

First, the subannular sutures were initiated. Rectangular 3-mm suture-protecting pledgets (TFE Polymer Pledget, Ethicon, USA) were placed on the inner aspect of the aorta. Horizontal mattress sutures were positioned approximately 2-mm below the aortic annulus between the commissures to provide structural support of the aortic root. These sutures were passed carefully from the inside to the outside of the aorta to minimize the risk of injury to the aortic valve and membranous septum, particularly near the RCC or noncoronary cusp (Fig. 1b and d). Subsequently, the U-shape vascular graft was introduced into the operative field, and the horizontal mattress sutures were sequentially passed through the vascular graft. The sutures were tied with controlled tension, uniform spacing on the vascular graft to prevent graft crimping or distortion. The supraanular sutures were then performed. Horizontal mattress sutures using 4–0 Prolene with pledgets were placed from the inside to the outside of the aorta, sutures securing the graft just above the annulus and enhancing hemostasis and structural stability (Fig. 1c and d).

The remaining native aortic wall was then anastomosed to the remaining two-thirds of the graft using 4–0 Prolene continuous running sutures just above the sinotubular junction. When required, coronary revascularization was performed via coronary button reimplantation or coronary artery bypass grafting using a saphenous vein graft. Finally, distal anastomosis was completed at the level of healthy aorta tissue using 4–0 Prolene. Additional external reinforcement with multiple horizontal mattress sutures and pledgets were applied when the native aortic tissue was deemed fragile (Fig. 1e).

Results

Among the 160 patients with ATAAD who underwent emergency open replacement of the ascending aorta, seven had intimal tears confined to a single aortic sinus and underwent single aortic sinus repair with double annular sutures. The median age of the patients at surgery was 59 years (range, 50–74 years) and the cohort included three women and four men.

Of these, three presented to the emergency department in a state of shock, and two required cardiopulmonary cerebral resuscitation for cardiac arrest, achieving return of spontaneous circulation with preserved mental status.

Preoperative patient characteristics

All patients, except one, underwent emergency surgery immediately following diagnosis of ATAAD. One patient (Case 2) underwent delayed elective surgery due to delayed diagnosis of ATAAD. The patient presented with cardiac arrest secondary to ventricular fibrillation, and ST-elevation myocardial infarction was initially suspected. ATAAD was subsequently diagnosed during further evaluation. Given the patient’s critical condition on arrival, the family initially hesitated to provide consent for surgery owing to the associated risks. However, after temporary stabilization of vital signs and continued clinical deterioration without surgical intervention, consent was obtained, and surgery was performed.

Among the five patients with aortic root involvement, four exhibited coronary artery ostial tearing. Of these, three involved the RCC and one involved the left coronary cusp. For coronary artery revascularization, three patients underwent coronary artery bypass surgery using a saphenous vein, and three underwent right coronary artery button reimplantation. All patients who underwent coronary artery bypass surgery were classified as having Neri type C coronary artery dissection [14, 15].

The median length of hospital stay was 19 days (range, 14–58 days) postoperatively. There were no reoperations for postoperative bleeding and no operative mortality. No postoperative myocardial infarctions were observed. There were no in-hospital deaths or deaths during the follow-up period. All patients were followed for a median of 8 years (range, 3–10 years) postoperatively. At follow-up, no patients demonstrated more than mild degree of AR. Left ventricular ejection fraction was preserved (median, 59%; range, 53–65%), and no regional wall motion abnormality were detected on postoperative transthoracic echocardiography.

In Cases 6 and 7, the intimal tear was located in the ascending aorta, and aortic root repair was not initially planned. However, significant bleeding occurred from the suture line following release of the aortic cross-clamp, accompanied by progressive aneurysmal changes in the sinus observed intraoperatively. These findings suggested the presence of a pre-existing Valsalva sinus aneurysm in the setting of structurally weakened aortic wall, resulting in sudden aneurysmal changes confined to the RCC. The affected sinus was excised, and single aortic sinus repair with double annular sutures were performed. Despite severe intraoperative bleeding, no postoperative bleeding complications were observed.

All seven patients had intimal tears confined to the aortic root or ascending aorta, none required aortic arch replacement. However, one patient later subsequently underwent thoracic endovascular aortic repair for a descending aorta aneurysm.

Discussion

Aortic root replacement of patients with ATAAD involving the aortic root was first reported first by Bentall in 1968 and has since become a standard surgical approach [1]. More recently, VSRR has been widely adopted as an alternative to valved conduit replacement [2, 4, 5, 7, 16]. However, VSRR remains technically demanding due to its complexity and the challenges associated with determining the appropriate graft size [11]. Furthermore, it remains uncertain whether such aforementioned surgical procedures are always necessary when aortic dissection is confined to a single coronary sinus without significant aortic leaflet and/or sinus pathology. This uncertainty has led to the development of various modified surgical strategies for VSRR [9–11, 16–19].

Selective sinus repair for aortic root remodeling has been described from 2002 to 2005 by Westaby and Urbanski et al. [9, 10]. In these techniques, a patient-tailored U-shaped graft is sutured into the resected sinus, with running sutures used to secure the trimmed graft to the aortic wall and annulus. However, these methods do not incorporate reinforcement of the aortic wall, as complete resection of the dissected tissue was considered sufficient [9–11]. Despite subsequent technical improvements [20–22], bleeding from the proximal aortic suture line and related complications remain concern, particularly, in the presence of fragile or degenerative aortic tissue.

In ATAAD, intimal tears may occur at multiple sites. Although they most frequently involve the tubular ascending aorta, tears extending below the sinotubular junction into the sinus of Valsalva are not uncommon. Given the fragility of the dissected sinus of wall, the risk of postoperative bleeding remains substantial. In this context, our double annular suture technique may provide improved hemostasis and structural reinforcement by creating double suture lines.

In the original technique described by Urbanski et al., no reinforcement sutures were placed in the subannular area. In contrast, our approach incorporates annular sutures placed approximately 2-mm below and 5-mm above the aortic cusp annulus using a horizontal mattress sutures. This modification distinguishes our technique and may contribute to enhanced hemostasis and structural support in weakened aortic tissues [10].

In our series, none of the seven patients who underwent single aortic sinus repair with double annular sutures required reoperation for postoperative bleeding or experienced in-hospital mortality. The additional reinforcement provided by the modified double annular sutures may have contributed to the observed stability of the repair. Notably, in Cases 6 and 7, aneurysmal changes of the sinus of Valsalva developed intraoperatively, likely due to the fragility of the aortic wall. In such situations, conventional VSRR may increase the risk of bleeding due to extensive manipulation and suturing. Single aortic sinus repair with double annular sutures provided a feasible alternative, allowing targeted reconstruction while limiting surgical complexity.

In ATAAD, blood flow into the false lumen can distort the anatomical relationship between the aortic sinus and valve, potentially resulting in aortic insufficiency. The use of surgical glue offers an opportunity to preserve the native aortic valve and eliminate aortic insufficiency caused by distorted root geometry [11, 16, 17, 19]. Formation of artificial adhesions with surgical glue on the dissected layers, followed by supracoronary ascending aortic replacement, offers a simple and life-saving solution. However, the use of surgical glue on the dissected aortic wall may also lead to iatrogenic aortic root pathologies, which inevitably result in reoperation [18, 23, 24].

In our experience, single aortic sinus repair with double annular sutures allowed preservation of uninvolved sinuses while providing adequate structural support. By limiting the extent of aortic manipulation, this approach may offer advantages in selected patients with localized pathology and fragile aortic tissue. However, further studies are required to confirm its long-term durability.

This study has several limitations. First, the sample size is small and limited to highly selected patients within a relatively short period, which may restrict the generalizability of the findings. Second, the retrospective design and absence of a comparator group preclude direct comparison with conventional surgical approaches, including aortic root replacement with a valved conduit or VSRR. Third, longer-term outcomes, such as postoperative serial CT or transthoracic echocardiography imaging surveillance, and actuarial analyses, such as Kaplan–Meier estimates for freedom from reintervention, are necessary to fully assess and strengthen the durability and safety of this technique. Furthermore, single aortic sinus repair with double annular sutures was not a pre-planned strategy but was applied intraoperatively. As such, the findings should be interpreted as observational and hypothesis-generating rather than definitive evidence of superiority. Future prospective studies with larger cohorts and comparative designs are warranted to better define the role of this technique in the surgical management of ATAAD.

Conclusions

In ATAAD patients, the primary objective of surgical management is to achieve life-saving intervention with durable outcomes. Single aortic sinus repair with double annular sutures may be considered as a useful alternative in carefully selected patients with confined sinus involvement and fragile aortic tissue.

The described modified technique was offered to simplify the surgical approach and reduce operative complexity, particularly in emergency settings. In our experience, this technique may be technically less demanding than valve-sparing root replacement procedures. Also, it could be adopted not only in highly experienced aorta centers but also more broadly. This approach may help reduce the risk of postoperative bleeding while preserving uninvolved aortic structures, thereby maintaining native anatomical integrity. It may be particularly valuable as a bailout strategy in cases with unexpected sinus fragility encountered intraoperatively.

Although the early and mid-term results are encouraging, further studies with larger patient populations and long-term follow-up are required to validate these findings and establish the role of this technique in routine clinical practice.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Kreiss condition

The Kreiss Matrix Theorem,
a well-known and important fact in applied matrix analysis, concerns matrices satisfying the following resolvent condition.

Definition 1.1
.

An
N\times N
matrix
A
is said to satisfy the
Kreiss resolvent condition
if the spectrum of
A
is contained in
\{|z|\leq 1\}
and there exists a constant
K\geq 1
such that

\|(zI-A)^{-1}\|\leq\cfrac{K}{|z|-1},\hskip 14.22636pt\forall|z|>1.

(1.1)

The least constant
K
such that (
1.1
) holds is termed the
Kreiss constant
of
A.

Kreiss
[
16
]
proved that every matrix that satisfies the above condition is power-bounded. More specifically, for every
N\times N
matrix
A
with Kreiss constant
K
, there exists
M(N,K)>0
with

\|A^{n}\|\leq M(N,K),\hskip 14.22636pt\forall n\geq 1.

In applications, however, the dimension
N
may be very large (see e.g.
[
35
]
), so it is of interest to look for sharp constants
M(N,K)
.

Indeed, in the years after Kreiss published his result, various authors made
successive improvements
that reduced the value of
M(N,K)
(see
[
34
, Section 18]
for the full history and references). Tadmor
[
33
]
was the first to show that
M(N,K)\leq CKN
for some numerical constant
C>0
. Finally, Spijker
[
30
]
, making crucial use of a Bernstein-type inequality for rational functions, proved that
M(N,K)\leq eKN
and so, if
A
is any
N\times N
matrix with Kreiss constant
K,
then

\|A^{n}\|\leq eKN,\hskip 14.22636pt\forall n\geq 1.

(1.2)

LeVeque and Trefethen
[
18
]
showed that (
1.2
) is sharp in the sense that, for every
N\geq 1,
there exists an
N\times N
matrix
A_{N}
with Kreiss constant
K_{N}
such that

\frac{\sup_{n\geq 1}\|A^{n}_{N}\|}{K_{N}N}\to e\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ as }N\to\infty.

This sequence
K_{N}
is, however, unbounded, so it is natural to ask
to what extent the Kreiss-Spijker estimate is sharp for an arbitrary
fixed
Kreiss constant. To this end, define
P(N,K)
to be the smallest number such that
every
N\times N
matrix
A
with Kreiss constant less than or equal to

K
satisfies

\|A^{n}\|\leq P(N,K),\hskip 14.22636pt\forall n\geq 1.

(1.3)

By (
1.2
), we have
P(N,K)\leq eKN.
What about lower bounds? The first results of this type are due to McCarthy-Schwartz
[
19
]
and van Dorsselaer-Kraaijevanger-Spijker
[
35
]
, who established lower bounds for
P(N,K)
that were logarithmic in
N
. Later, Spijker, Tracogna and Welfert
[
29
]
) almost bridged the gap with (
1.2
) by showing that, given
\varepsilon>0,
there exists
K=K(\varepsilon)>\pi+1
and
C=C(\varepsilon)>0
such that

P(N,K)\geq CN^{1-\varepsilon},\hskip 14.22636pt\forall N\geq 1.

(1.4)

Their construction was subsequently refined by Nikolski
[
22
]
, who showed that, for any
K>\pi+1
and
N\geq 1,

P(N,K)\geq\frac{N^{1-\frac{2}{K}}}{5}.

(1.5)

More recently, the search for non-power-bounded operators that satisfy various Kreiss- and Cesàro-type conditions has focused on weighted shifts; see e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
5
]
.

1.2.
Small Kreiss constants

Unfortunately, all of the above-mentioned papers fall short of providing lower bounds for
P(N,K)
for
K
close to
1.
In fact, it appears that the only time non-power-bounded operators with Kreiss-type constants close to
1
were considered in the literature was in a paper of McCarthy
[
20
]
(see also the remark in
[
22
, p. 374]
), who proved that, for any
\varepsilon>0,
there exists
C=C(\varepsilon)
with

P(N,1+\varepsilon)\geq C\sqrt{\log\log(N+1)},\hskip 11.38092ptN\geq 1.

Our first main result is a substantial improvement of McCarthy’s estimate. Our proof is based on the construction of a certain family of weighted shifts; the novel ingredient in
our approach is the consideration of
matrix weights
. More precisely, given
\varepsilon>0
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we show that there exists a constant
C=C(\varepsilon,m)
such that for all
N\geq 1,

P(N,1+\varepsilon)\geq C\log^{m}(N).

(1.6)

The exact same lower bound holds for the (more restrictive)
uniform Kreiss condition
(see Section
2
for the definition); we prove this as Theorem
3.3
.
In fact, our construction yields lower bounds for even more general Kreiss- and Cesáro-type conditions. To be more precise, define
P_{AC}(N,K)
as the supremum of the power bounds of all
N\times N
matrices that are
absolutely Cesàro bounded
(see Section
2
) with constant at most
K.

Theorem 1.2
.

(i)

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. There exists a constant
C=C(\varepsilon,m)
such that for all
N\geq 1,

P_{AC}(N,1+\varepsilon)\geq C\log^{m}(N).

(1.7)

(ii)

Let
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
.
There exist
M=M(\alpha)>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1]
such that, whenever
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and
N^{\varepsilon}\geq M
, one has

P_{AC}(N,1+\varepsilon)\geq\exp\bigg(\,\frac{\alpha\log^{2}\log N^{\varepsilon}}{\log\log\log N^{\varepsilon}}\bigg).

(1.8)

Since (
1.7
) says that

P_{AC}(N,1+\varepsilon)\geq\exp(m\log\log N+\log C),

we can assert that (
1.8
) is obtained from
(
1.7
) by replacing the function

\exp(m\log\log N)
with
a function that grows slightly slower than
\exp(\alpha\log^{2}\log N)
. So, the estimate (
1.8
) is considerably sharper than (
1.7
).

We also observe that Theorem
1.2
does not immediately follow from (
1.6
); indeed, while it is well-known that absolute Cesáro boundedness implies the Kreiss condition, it could happen that the Kreiss constant of a matrix exceeds its absolute Cesàro constant.

A lower bound corresponding to the
strong Kreiss condition
can be found in (
3.20
); see Remark
3.7
.

1.3.
The similarity problem

We now move from matrices to infinite-dimensional linear operators.
Let
H
be a Hilbert space and let
T\in\mathcal{B}(H)
with
\sigma(T)\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. The Kreiss condition

\|(\lambda-T)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda|-1},\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt1<|\lambda|\leq 1+\nu

(where
\nu>0
) no longer implies power-boundedness in this setting; classical counterexamples are contained in
[
9
,
14
]
. On the other hand, if
C=1
, then
T
is a
\rho
-contraction for some
\rho>1
, and so it is similar to a contraction (and thus power-bounded), no matter the value of
\nu
(see e.g.
[
32
]
).

In the second half of our paper, we examine several conditions on
T
that lie in-between the above
C=1
and
C>1
regimes, and determine whether they imply similarity to a contraction. We point out that similarity to a contraction
(and, more generally,
K
-spectral estimates)
has
already been linked to resolvent conditions several times in the literature, see e.g.
[
3
,
4
,
7
,
8
,
10
]
. For more references and recent developments concerning the similarity problem, especially in relation to operator semigroups, see
[
23
]
. One of the conditions we are interested in, naturally motivated by the consideration of “small” Kreiss constants, is

\|(\lambda-T)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1+\varepsilon(|\lambda|-1)}{|\lambda|-1}\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt1<|\lambda|\leq 1+\nu,

(1.9)

where
\varepsilon:(0,\nu)\to(0,+\infty)
is a continuous function such that
\varepsilon(0^{+})=0
. We use our weighted shifts from the proof of Theorem
1.2
to give an explicit choice for
\varepsilon
such that (
1.9
) is satisfied but
T
is not similar to a contraction (in fact, not even power-bounded); this is Theorem
5.1
.

We also consider operators
T
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|T^{n}\|=1
. Notice that if
\|T^{m}\|\leq 1

for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
, then
T
is similar to a contraction, see [
[
15
]
, discussion of Problem 6].
It is easy to see that the condition
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|T^{n}\|=1
implies (
1.9
) for a certain function

\varepsilon(x)
vanishing at
0^{+}
. By adapting Pisier’s example of an operator that is polynomially bounded but not similar to a contraction
[
27
]
, we
show

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\{\beta_{n}:n\geq 1\}
be a positive sequence such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\beta_{n}=0
,

\sum_{n}\beta_{n}/n=\infty
and the sequence
\{n^{-2}\beta_{n}\}
is decreasing. Then there
exists a Hilbert space operator
F
such that

\|F^{n}\|\leq 1+\sqrt{\beta_{n}},

but
F
is not similar to a contraction.

In particular, one can take
\beta_{n}=1/\log(n+1)
. In this case, the operator
F
will satisfy (
1.9
) with
\varepsilon(x)\simeq[\log(1/x)]^{-1/2}
; see Remark
5.3
.

It seems to be unknown whether there exists a function
\varepsilon(x)
, which goes (rapidly)
to
0
as
x\to 0^{+}
, such that (
1.9
)
guarantees the similarity of
T
to a contraction.
However, we are able to give positive results of this type under some stronger assumptions on
T
. For convenience, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
r:[-\delta_{-},\delta_{+}]\to[1,+\infty)
be a continuous function, where

\delta_{-},\delta_{+}\in(0,\pi)
. Consider the corresponding curve

\gamma(\theta)=r(\theta)e^{-i\theta}
,

\theta\in[-\delta_{-},\delta_{+}],
in the complex plane. We will say that

\gamma

is a v-type curve
if the following
conditions hold.

(a)

r(\cdot)
strictly increases on
[0,\delta_{+}]
and strictly decreases on
[-\delta_{-},0]
;

(b)

r(0)=1
and
r(-\delta_{-})=r(\delta_{+})>1
;

(c)

{\displaystyle\int^{\delta_{+}}_{-\delta_{-}}\cfrac{1}{r(\theta)-1}\ d\theta<\infty}
;

A simple example of a function
r(\cdot)
which gives rise to a
v-type curve is
r(\theta)=1+|\theta|^{p},
where
0<p<1
.
If
\gamma
is a v-type curve, then
its union with the arc

\{r(\delta_{+})e^{-i\theta};\;-\delta_{-}\leq\theta\leq\delta_{+}\}

is a Jordan curve. We will say that the closed domain, bordered by this curve, is

the set determined by the v-type curve
\gamma
. Here is our second main result.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(H)
with
\sigma(T)\subset\mathbb{D}\cup\{1\}
, with
\gamma:[-\delta_{-},\delta_{+}]\to\mathbb{C}
a v-type curve.
Assume that
T
satisfies the Kreiss condition and, moreover,
\varepsilon:(0,\nu_{0}]\to(0,\infty)
is a continuous increasing function
such that
\varepsilon(0^{+})=0
and the resolvent estimate (
1.9
) holds for all
\lambda

in the set determined by
\gamma
(excluding
\lambda=1
).
Set
r(\theta)=|\gamma(\theta)|
. If

\displaystyle\int_{-\delta_{-}}^{\delta_{+}}\cfrac{\varepsilon(r(\theta)-1)}{r(\theta)-1}\,\|(e^{i\theta}-T)^{-1}\|^{2}\,d\theta<\infty,

then
T
is similar to a contraction.

In other words, suppose that
\sigma(T)
is contained in
\mathbb{D}\cup\{1\}

and one knows an estimate of
\|(\lambda-T)^{-1}\|
for
\lambda

on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
.
Then, given a v-type curve
\gamma
, one can exhibit a positive function
\varepsilon
such that whenever the estimate (
1.9
) holds on the set determined by this curve,
T
is similar to a contraction.

Let us state a particular case of this theorem for
Ritt
operators.
Recall that
T
is called a Ritt operator if
there exists
C>0
such that

\|(\lambda-T)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda-1|}

for all
|\lambda|>1
.
Any Ritt operator satisfies
\sigma(T)\subset\mathbb{D}\cup\{1\}
.
The point is that, since we can estimate the resolvent of
T
along
\mathbb{T}
if
T
is Ritt, it is possible to make a uniform choice of
\varepsilon
for such operators.
Indeed, there exists
M>0
such that

\|(e^{i\theta}-T)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{M}{|\theta|}

(1.10)

for all
\theta
(see e.g.
[
28
, Section 1.2]
).
Thus, in view of Theorem
1.5
, we immediately obtain

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(H)
be a Ritt operator. Assume there exists
\varepsilon:(0,\nu]\to(0,\infty)
continuous and increasing such that
\varepsilon(0^{+})=0
and (
1.9
) holds in the set determined by some v-type curve
\gamma
.
If
\varepsilon
satisfies

\int_{-\delta_{-}}^{\delta_{+}}\frac{\varepsilon(r(\theta)-1)}{|\theta|^{2}(r(\theta)-1)}\ d\theta<\infty,

where
r(\theta)=|\gamma(\theta)|
, then
T
is similar to a contraction.

We note that Ritt operators, while power-bounded, are not, in general, similar to contractions
[
17
, Proposition 5.2]
.

2.
Kreiss- and Cesàro-type conditions

The following conditions often appear in the literature in conjuction with the Kreiss Matris Theorem.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(H).
We say that
T

(i)

satisfies the
uniform Kreiss condition
if there exists
K_{U}>0
such that

\Big\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}z^{-k-1}T^{k}\Big\|\leq\frac{K_{U}}{|z|-1},\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for all }|z|>1\text{ and }n=0,1,2,\dots;

(ii)

satisfies the
strong Kreiss condition
if there exists
K_{S}>0
such that

\big\|(zI-T)^{-k}\big\|\leq\frac{K_{S}}{(|z|-1)^{k}},\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for all }|z|>1\text{ and }k=1,2,\dots;

(iii)

is
Cesàro bounded
if there exists
K_{C}>0
such that

\|M_{n}(T)\|:=\bigg\|\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}T^{k}\bigg\|\leq K_{C},\hskip 11.38092ptn=1,2,3,\dots;

(iv)

is
absolutely Cesàro bounded
if there exists
K_{AC}>0
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|T^{j}x\|\leq K_{AC}\|x\|,\hskip 11.38092pt\forall x\in H.

Clearly, power-boundedness implies all of the above conditions. Absolute Cesàro boundedness and the strong Kreiss condition are both strictly stronger than the uniform Kreiss condition, see
[
1
,
12
,
21
]
, while the two are independent of one another
[
5
]
. Further, the uniform Kreiss condition is equivalent
[
21
]
to the existence of a constant
C>0
such that, for all
n\geq 1
and
|\lambda|=1,

\|M_{n}(\lambda T)\|\leq C,

(2.1)

and is thus stronger than Cesáro boundedness (and also, trivially, stronger than the Kreiss condition). The Kreiss condition and Cesàro boundedness are independent
[
31
]
. Finally, while the strong Kreiss condition does not imply power-boundedness
[
5
]
, we observe that the similar-looking condition

\big\|T^{k}(zI-T)^{-k}\big\|\leq\frac{K^{\prime}}{(|z|-1)^{k}},\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for all }|z|>1\text{ and }k=1,2,\dots,

is, in fact, equivalent to power-boundedness

[
11
,
24
]
.

The definition of
P(N,K)
can be adapted to all of the above conditions. For instance, we may define
P_{U}(N,K)
to be the supremum of the power bounds of all
N\times N
matrices that satisfy the uniform Kreiss condition with constant
K,
with analogous definitions for
P_{S},

P_{AC}
and
P_{C}.
We observe that, trivially,

P(N,K)\geq P_{U}(N,K)\geq P_{S}(N,K)\hskip 8.5359pt\text{ and }\hskip 8.5359ptP_{C}(N,K)\geq P_{AC}(N,K),

for all
N\geq 1
and
K\geq 1.

An interesting example of an operator that is absolutely Cesáro but not power bounded is given in [
[
1
]
, Theorem 2.1] (we only record the
\ell^{2}
-version here):

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
T
be the weighted backward shift on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
defined by
Te_{1}=0
and
Te_{k}=w_{k}e_{k-1}
for
k>1.
If
w_{k}=\big(\frac{k}{k-1}\big)^{a}
, with
0<a<1/2,
then
T
is not power-bounded but is absolutely Cesàro bounded with constant

K_{AC}\leq\sqrt{2(1-2a)^{-1}+2}.

Thus, for any
\ell^{2}
sequence of positive numbers
\{b_{k}\}
and
0<a<1/2,
we have

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{k+j}{j}\bigg)^{2a}b^{2}_{k+j}}\leq C(a)\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b^{2}_{k}},

(2.2)

with
C(a)=\sqrt{2(1-2a)^{-1}+2}.

This inequality will play an important role in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

3.
Lower bounds for small Kreiss constants

3.1.
Lower bounds for the absolute Cesàro condition

Fix
m\geq 1.
For
k\geq 2
and
c>0,
define the upper triangular

2^{m}\times 2^{m}
matrix
A_{m,k}(c)
inductively as follows: set
A_{0,k}(c)=1
, and, assuming the
2^{p}\times 2^{p}
matrix
A_{p,k}(c)
has been defined for
0\leq p<m,
set

A_{p+1,k}(c)=\begin{bmatrix}A_{p,k}(c)&c\log\big(\frac{k}{k-1}\big)I_{2^{p}}\\
0&A_{p,k}(c)\end{bmatrix},\

(3.1)

where
I_{2^{p}}
is the
2^{p}\times 2^{p}
identity matrix.
Further, define the weighted backwards shifts
T_{p,c}:\ell^{2}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2^{p}}\big)\to\ell^{2}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2^{p}})
, with
0\leq p\leq m
,
by

T_{p,c}(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},\dots)=\big(A_{p,2}(c)h_{2},A_{p,3}(c)h_{3},A_{p,4}(c)h_{4},\dots\big).

Notice that
T_{0,c}
coincides with the usual (unweighted) backwards shift on
\ell^{2}.

In what follows, we will deal with the powers of
T_{p+1,c}
. Notice that

\displaystyle T_{p+1,c}^{n}h

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\bigg[\prod_{k=2}^{n+1}A_{p+1,k}(c)\bigg]h_{n+1},\bigg[\prod_{k=2}^{n+1}A_{p+1,k+1}(c)\bigg]h_{n+2},\bigg[\prod_{k=2}^{n+1}A_{p+1,k+2}(c)\bigg]h_{n+3},\dots\Bigg)

for
j\geq 0
and

\prod\limits_{k=2}^{n+1}A_{p+1,k+j}(c)=\begin{bmatrix}\prod\limits_{k=2}^{n+1}A_{p,k+j}(c)&cB_{p,n,j+1}(c)\\
0&\prod\limits_{k=2}^{n+1}A_{p,k+j}(c)\end{bmatrix},

(3.2)

where

B_{p,n,j+1}(c):=\sum\limits_{k=2+j}^{n+j+1}\log\Big(\frac{k}{k-1}\Big)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j+2\leq r\leq n+j+1\\
r\neq k\end{subarray}}A_{p,r}(c).

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
1\leq p\leq m
, integers
s\geq 1
and
2\leq k_{1}<k_{2}<\dots<k_{s}
and constant
0\leq c\leq 1
,

\Big\|\prod_{i=1}^{s}A_{p,k_{i}}(c)\Big\|\leq 2^{p-1}\Big[1+\log^{p}\,\frac{k_{s}}{k_{1}-1}\Big].

Proof.

Fix integers
2\leq k_{1}<k_{2}<\dots<k_{s}
and constant
0\leq c\leq 1
. We proceed by induction. For
p=1
,

\Big\|\prod_{i=1}^{s}A_{1,k_{i}}(c)\Big\|=\Bigg\|\begin{bmatrix}1&c\,\log\,\prod\limits_{i=1}^{s}\frac{k_{i}}{k_{i}-1}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\Bigg\|\leq 1+c\log\,\prod\limits_{i=1}^{s}\frac{k_{i}}{k_{i}-1}\leq 1+\log\,\frac{k_{s}}{k_{1}-1}\,,

as desired. For the inductive step, assume the conclusion of the lemma holds for
a certain value of
p
,
1\leq p<m
. Then,

\displaystyle\Big\|\prod_{i=1}^{s}A_{p+1,k_{i}}(c)

\displaystyle\Big\|=\ \Bigg\|\begin{bmatrix}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{s}A_{p,k_{i}}(c)&c\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\Big(\log\,\cfrac{k_{i}}{k_{i}-1}\Big)\prod\limits_{1\leq j\neq i}^{s}A_{p,k_{j}}(c)\\
0&\prod\limits_{i=1}^{s}A_{p,k_{i}}(c)\end{bmatrix}\Bigg\|

\displaystyle\leq\ \Big\|\prod_{i=1}^{s}A_{p,k_{i}}(c)\Big\|+c\,\Big\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\Big(\log\,\frac{k_{i}}{k_{i}-1}\Big)\prod\limits_{1\leq j\neq i}^{s}A_{p,k_{j}}(c)\Big\|

\displaystyle\leq\ 2^{p-1}\Big[1+\log^{p}\,\frac{k_{s}}{k_{1}-1}\Big]+2^{p-1}\sum_{i=1}^{s}\Big(\log\,\cfrac{k_{i}}{k_{i}-1}\Big)\Big[1+\log^{p}\,\frac{k_{s}}{k_{1}-1}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\ 2^{p-1}\Big[1+\log^{p}\,\frac{k_{s}}{k_{1}-1}\Big]+2^{p-1}\Big(\log\,\cfrac{k_{s}}{k_{1}-1}\Big)\Big[1+\log^{p}\,\frac{k_{s}}{k_{1}-1}\Big]

Lemma 4.1
.

There exist piecewise continuous functions

Let

Letting

\displaystyle=p(T)+[\mathcal{C}(\overline{p})(T)]^{*}-[\mathcal{C}(\overline{p})(T)]^{*}

, that

\displaystyle\|(zI-Y)^{-1}\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{N\geq N_{0}}\|(zI-Y_{N})^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{N\geq N_{0}}\bigg\{\cfrac{1+c_{N}L-|z|^{-N}}{|z|-1}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\cfrac{1+\varepsilon(|z|-1)}{|z|-1}.

In particular, the spectrum of
Y
is contained in

\{|z|\leq 1\}
.
Since
Y
is not power bounded, its spectral radius
is equal to
1
.
Hence,
\varepsilon(x)\geq 0
for any
x>0
. It follows that

\varepsilon(0^{+})=0
. It is now easy to find a continuous increasing piecewise linear function
\varepsilon_{1}\geq\varepsilon
, defined on an interval
(0,\delta]
, such that
one also has
\varepsilon_{1}(0^{+})=0
(put
\varepsilon_{1}(x_{k+1})=\varepsilon(x_{k})
for
k\geq 1

and extend
\varepsilon_{1}
linearly to each
interval
[x_{k+1},x_{k}]
,
k\geq 2
). Replacing
\varepsilon
by
\varepsilon_{1}
completes the proof.
∎

5.2.
A Pisier-type counterexample

To prove Theorem
1.3
,
we will adapt a variant of Pisier’s counterexample, given in
[
26
, Chapter 10]
. Namely, denote by
S
the forward shift on
\ell^{2}
:

S(x_{0},x_{1},\dots)=(0,x_{0},x_{1},\dots)
.
Next, let
K
be a Hilbert space
and denote by
S_{K}
the forward shift on
\ell^{2}(K)
:

S_{K}(k_{0},k_{1},\dots)=(0,k_{0},k_{1},\dots)
, whose adjoint
acts as
S^{*}_{K}(k_{0},k_{1},\dots)=(k_{1},k_{2},\dots)
.
We will take as
F
the following Foguel-Hankel
operator

F=\begin{bmatrix}S^{*}_{K}&X\\
0&S_{K}\end{bmatrix}.

(5.3)

Here
X=(a_{i+j}W_{i+j})
, where
W_{n}\in\mathcal{B}(K)
are chosen as in
[
26
, page 142]
and
a_{n}
are complex numbers, whose choice will be specified later. In particular,
\{W_{n}\}
satisfy the CAR relations modulo the ideal of compact operators.
We set
X_{n}=nXS_{K}^{n-1}
to be the
(1,2)
-entry of
F^{n}
. We summarize the facts which we will use as follows.

Lemma 5.2

(
[
26
]
)
.

(i)

If
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+1)^{2}|a_{k}|^{2}
is infinite, then
F
is not similar to a contraction;

(ii)

One has

\|X_{n}\|^{2}\leq n^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|a_{k+n-1}|^{2}\leq 2\|X_{n}\|^{2}.

Proof.

Assertion (i) is contained in

[
26
, Theorem 10.5]
.

The right-hand inequality in (ii) follows from a calculation in the proof of this theorem (
[
26
]
, page 143). Notice the typo in item (iii) in
[
26
, Theorem 10.5]
; it should read as
\sup_{n}n^{2}\sum_{k=n-1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{2}<\infty
.

To get the left-hand inequality in (ii), let
\{E_{i,j}\}_{i,j=0}^{\infty}
be the usual matrix units, considered as linear operators on (scalar)
\ell^{2}
.
Set
\widetilde{X}=(a_{i+j}E_{i+j,0})
. Following
the proof of Theorem 10.5 in
[
26
]
, define

\Phi:B(\ell^{2})\to B(K)
by

\Phi((a_{i,j}))=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i,0}W_{i}
.
Then
\|\Phi\|\leq 1
.
Put
\widetilde{X}_{n}=n\widetilde{X}S^{n-1}
. Then

\Phi(\widetilde{X})=X
and
\Phi(\widetilde{X}_{n})=X_{n}
, so that we get

\|X_{n}\|^{2}=\|\Phi(\widetilde{X}_{n})\|^{2}\leq\|\widetilde{X}_{n}\|^{2}=n^{2}\sum_{k=n-1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{2}.\qed

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Since
\{n^{-2}\beta_{n}\}
decreases, we can take a sequence
\{a_{n}\}
such that

|a_{n}|^{2}=\frac{\beta_{n+1}}{(n+1)^{2}}-\frac{\beta_{n+2}}{(n+2)^{2}}

for all
n\geq 0
.
Then

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)^{2}|a_{n}|^{2}=\beta_{1}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\big(1-(\frac{n+1}{n+2})^{2}\big)\beta_{n+2}=\infty
.
Hence, by Lemma
5.2
,
the corresponding Foguel-Hankel operator
F
is not similar to a contraction.
On the other hand, by the same Lemma,

\|X_{n}\|^{2}\leq n^{2}\sum_{k=n-1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{2}=n^{2}\sum_{k=n-1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\beta_{k+1}}{(k+1)^{2}}-\frac{\beta_{k+2}}{(k+2)^{2}}\Big)=\beta_{n}.

Since
\|F^{n}\|\leq 1+\|X_{n}\|
, the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

Now, assume
F
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
1.3
with
\beta_{n}=1/\log(n+1)
. Given
0<a<1,
set
k=\lfloor(1-a)^{-1}\rfloor
. Observe that

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=2}\cfrac{a^{n}}{\sqrt{\log n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=2}^{k}\cfrac{a^{n}}{\sqrt{\log n}}+\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}\cfrac{a^{n}}{\sqrt{\log n}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=2}^{k}\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{\log n}}+\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{\log k}}\cdot\cfrac{a^{k+1}}{1-a}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{k}_{2}\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{\log a}}\ da+\cfrac{|\log(1-a)|^{-1/2}}{1-a}.

Since
\int^{k}_{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log a}}\ da\approx\frac{k}{\sqrt{\log k}}
, the previous inequality yields

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=2}\cfrac{a^{n}}{\sqrt{\log n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{k}{\sqrt{\log k}}+\cfrac{|\log(1-a)|^{-1/2}}{1-a}

\displaystyle\lesssim\cfrac{|\log(1-a)|^{-1/2}}{1-a}.

(5.4)

Thus, there exists a numerical constant
C
such that, for
1<|\lambda|<1+\nu
,

\displaystyle\|(\lambda-F)^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\cfrac{\|F\|^{n}}{|\lambda|^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\cfrac{1}{|\lambda|-1}+\sum_{n=1}\cfrac{|\lambda|^{-n-1}}{\sqrt{\log(n+1)}}

\displaystyle\leq\cfrac{1}{|\lambda|-1}+C\,\cfrac{\big|\log(|\lambda|-1)\big|^{-1/2}}{|\lambda|-1},

where the last inequality is due to (
5.4
), with
a
replaced by
1/|\lambda|
. In other words,
F
satisfies (
1.9
) with

\varepsilon(x)=C\big(\log(1/x)\big)^{-1/2}
.

6.
Open Questions

Nikolski conjectured
[
22
]
that one should always be able to obtain sublinear growth in
N
in (
1.2
) as long as the Kreiss constant
remains bounded. In fact, he proved that
if
A
is a diagonalizable
N\times N
matrix
with unimodular eigenvalues, then there exists
\varepsilon>0
(depending on the basis constant of the eigenvectors of
A
) such that

\sup_{n\geq 1}\|A^{n}\|\leq 2\pi KN^{1-\varepsilon}.

Unfortunately, it is not clear how exactly
\varepsilon
depends on
K
here (see also
[
22
, Theorem 2.9.9]
), while the case of general
N\times N
matrices currently lies out of reach of Nikolski’s methods. As such, there remains a sizeable gap between the lower bounds (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) and Spijker’s estimate (
1.2
), which naturally motivates the following questions:

Question 6.1
.

Does there exist a strictly increasing function
\alpha(\cdot):(1,\infty)\to(0,1)
with
\lim_{K\to 1+}\alpha(K)=0
and
\lim_{K\to\infty}\alpha(K)=1
such that

P(N,K)\gtrsim_{K}N^{\alpha(K)},

for all
N\geq 1
and
K>1
?

Question 6.2
.

Does there exist a constant
\delta>0
such that

P(N,K)\gtrsim_{K}N^{\delta},

for all
N\geq 1
and
K>1
?

Inequality (
1.6
) asserts that the answer to Question
6.2
is yes if the growth function
N^{\delta}
is replaced by
\log^{m}(N),
independently of the value of
m\geq 1.

Regarding condition (
1.9
) on the other hand, the following questions remain unanswered.

Question 6.3
.

Does there exist a continuous function
\varepsilon:(1,\nu_{0})\to(0,\infty)
such that (
1.9
) guarantees power-boundedness? What if
\sigma(T)\cap\mathbb{T}=\{1\}?

Question 6.4
.

Does there exist a decreasing positive sequence
\{b_{k}\}
, tending to zero, such that
\|T^{k}\|\leq 1+b_{k}
for all
k
implies the similarity of
T
to a contraction?

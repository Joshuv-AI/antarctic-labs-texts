Association of preoperative corneal spherical aberration with intermediate-to-near visual acuity in enhanced monofocal intraocular lens implantation: a retrospective cross-sectional cohort study
Authors: Jiyoung Emily Lee, Da Ran Kim, Woong-Joo Whang, Ho Sik Hwang, Eun Chul Kim, Kyung-Sun Na
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

Background

The need for enhanced intermediate vision has increased substantially with the increasing use of digital screens. Enhanced monofocal intraocular lenses (IOLs) (Tecnis® Eyhance ICB00, Eyhance Preloaded DIB00, and Eyhance Toric DIU) have been introduced to address this need and mitigate the drawbacks of conventional multifocal IOLs, such as photic phenomena [1–3]. These IOLs are especially beneficial for patients with retinal diseases, such as epiretinal membrane, who are unsuitable candidates for diffractive IOLs [4].

The lenses extend the depth of focus (DoF) through subtle localized power alterations within the central optical zone [5]. However, the clinical outcomes of enhanced monofocal IOLs have been inconsistent, despite the superior intermediate vision they offer relative to that offered by general monofocal IOLs [6–8]. This variability makes it challenging to predict the reliability of the surgical results.

Spherical aberration (SA) may be a key factor influencing this variability in the optical quality. The total ocular SA is the sum of the intrinsic corneal SA, which is usually positive, and the SA induced by the implanted IOL [9]. Aspheric IOLs, such as Eyhance, are designed with a negative SA to counteract the positive SA of the cornea, minimize the total ocular SA, and enhance the image quality and contrast sensitivity [10, 11]. SA manipulation is a recognized strategy for extending the DoF [12–14]; however, the reports in the literature are conflicting. Some studies have reported that inducing negative SA improves near vision [12], whereas others have indicated that positive SA may also contribute to DoF extension [13]. One study reported that higher SAs improve intermediate-to-near visual performance [15], which contradicts the general consensus that SA reduction improves overall visual quality [14].

The effects of the central aspheric profile of the ICB00 on SA and myopic shifts have been reported in optical studies [5]; however, the specific effects on defocus curves based on preoperative SA have not been comprehensively evaluated in clinical settings.

Therefore, this study aimed to determine the association between preoperative corneal SA and the objective defocus curve, intermediate-to-near visual acuity (VA), and patient-reported outcomes, such as spectacle independence and photic phenomena, in patients implanted with an enhanced monofocal IOL.

Methods

Study design and patient selection

In this retrospective cross-sectional cohort study, we reviewed the electronic medical records of consecutive patients who underwent cataract surgery and received ICB00, DIB00, or DIU IOLs at Yeouido St. Mary’s Hospital between January 1, 2023 and March 30, 2025. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Yeouido St. Mary’s Hospital (IRB number: SC25RISI0115) and adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki. The requirement for informed consent was waived owing to the retrospective nature of the study and the use of anonymized data.

Patients were eligible for inclusion in the study if they had received one of the target IOLs (ICB00, DIB00, or DIU) and had undergone follow-up for at least 3 months postoperatively.

The exclusion criteria were eyes with irregular astigmatism, retinal pathology, prior ocular surgery, intraoperative complications (anterior capsule tear), postoperative complications, or postoperative residual spherical equivalent (SE) of more than +1.50 D or less than −1.50 D. Eyes that required iris retractors or intracameral epinephrine and those with brunescent cataracts or glaucoma were also excluded, as these factors could affect postoperative VA.

Preoperative examination

The ocular parameters were comprehensively assessed before surgery. The SE was determined using a refractometer (KR-800 A; Topcon, Tokyo, Japan). The anterior chamber diameter and axial length (AL) were measured using an IOL Master 700 (Carl Zeiss Meditec AG, Jena, Germany). The photopic pupil size, white-to-white (WTW) distance, SA Z (4,0), vertical coma, horizontal coma, and angle kappa were determined using the Pentacam (Oculus Optikgeräte GmbH, Wetzlar, Germany). The preoperative corneal SA was measured for a 6-mm pupil diameter under standardized mesopic conditions using the Pentacam. The IOL power was calculated using the SRK/T, Haigis, KANE, and Barrett Universal II TK formulas, targeting a refraction between 0 and − 0.5 D. Pre-existing astigmatism of ≥ 1.5 D with-the-rule and ≥ 0.75 D against-the-rule was corrected using the DIU IOL. The optical angle for lens insertion was determined using the KANE formula.

Surgical technique

All cataract surgeries were performed by a single experienced surgeon (N.K.S.). The alignment marks for the toric IOLs were placed at 0 and 180 degrees while the patients were seated, and the steep keratometry (K) axis was marked while they were supine before the initial incision. Topical 0.5% proparacaine hydrochloride (Alcaine; Alcon Laboratories Inc., Fort Worth, TX, USA) was administered preoperatively. A 2.3-mm temporal clear corneal incision was made, followed by continuous curvilinear capsulorhexis. Phacoemulsification using the phaco-chop technique was performed, followed by posterior capsule polishing. The IOL was placed in the posterior capsule. The patients were prescribed 0.5% moxifloxacin (Vigamox; Novartis, Seoul, South Korea) and 1% prednisolone acetate (Predbell; Jonggeundang, Seoul, South Korea) for topical administration four times daily and 0.1% bromfenac sodium (Bronuck; Taejoon Pharm, Seoul, South Korea) twice daily.

Postoperative assessments

The patients were followed up at 1 day, 1 week, 1 month, and 3 months postoperatively. Their ocular parameters, including SE and SA, were measured using the same devices and protocols as those used preoperatively. Monocular defocus curves at 4 m were obtained by trained personnel using Snellen charts, with the acuity converted to logMAR at 3 months postoperatively. Their VAs were measured for lens powers ranging from + 1.50 D to -5.00 D using trial lenses and frames under mesopic light conditions to simulate common indoor and low-light environments. Negative lenses were applied first in 0.5-D increments, followed by positive lenses.

Contrast sensitivity was measured at 4 m under photopic conditions (85 cd/m2) using a CSV-1000 chart (VectorVision, Dayton, OH) [16]. The CSV-1000 has four rows of 17 circular patches, which are retro-illuminated using a fluorescent source and calibrated to 85 cd/m2. The patients completed the validated Korean versions of the Quality of Vision (QoV) questionnaire [17] and the spectacle independence questionnaire (PRSIQ) [18]. The 30-item QoV questionnaire assesses the frequency, severity, and bothersomeness of photic phenomena (including glare and halo) using 0–3 scales. Higher scores indicate more severe symptoms. Visual terminology of photic phenomena is clarified using reference photographs. PRSIQ evaluates spectacle independence for near, intermediate, and distance vision using binary (Yes/No) responses for the “need” items and 5-point Likert scales (“All of the time” to “None of the time”) for the “wear” and “function” items. Lower scores indicate greater spectacle independence.

Primary and secondary outcomes

The primary objective was to evaluate the association between preoperative corneal SA and postoperative uncorrected intermediate VA (UIVA). The secondary objectives were to determine the optimal preoperative corneal SA cutoff for poor postoperative UIVA (VA < 20/25 [> 0.1 logMAR]), stratify the groups based on the cutoff value, and compare their visual outcomes, including the defocus curve, contrast sensitivity scores, subjective satisfaction, and visual symptoms. The near, intermediate, and distance zones were defined as ranging from − 5.00 D to -2.00 D (20–50 cm), -1.50 D to -1.00 D (67 cm to 1 m), and − 0.50 D to + 1.00 D (2–6 m), respectively [19].

Statistical analysis

Quantitative data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation, and qualitative data as absolute counts (n) and percentages (%). The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to assess normality. Paired t-tests were used to evaluate the differences in the ocular parameters preoperatively and postoperatively. Pearson’s correlation was used to evaluate the relationship between preoperative corneal SA and UIVA. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was used to determine the optimal cutoff value of preoperative corneal SA for detection of UIVA < 20/25 (> 0.1 logMAR). The optimal cutoff preoperative corneal SA value was determined by maximizing the Youden index, which is defined as sensitivity + specificity − 1. Independent samples t-tests were used for continuous variables, and Pearson’s Chi-squared or Fisher’s exact tests were used for categorical variables to compare the groups stratified by the preoperative corneal SA cutoff. Generalized estimating equations (GEE) were used for visual outcomes to account for the correlation between the eyes in patients who underwent bilateral surgery, with patient ID treated as a clustering variable. Postoperative SE was included as a covariate in the GEE models to statistically adjust for potential confounding due to residual refractive error. All analyses were performed using SPSS® (version 26.0; SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Results

Patient selection

Fifty-six eyes from 41 patients were initially identified through a medical record review. Eleven eyes were excluded due to incomplete 3-month follow-up data, pre-existing retinopathy, or postoperative residual SE of more than + 1.50 D or less than − 1.50 D. No significant intraoperative or postoperative complications were observed during the 3-month follow-up. The final cohort comprised 45 eyes from 34 patients. Determination of the preoperative corneal SA cutoff

ROC analysis was performed to determine the optimal preoperative corneal SA cutoff for predicting poor postoperative UIVA (VA < 20/25 [> 0.1 logMAR]).

ROC analysis revealed that preoperative corneal SA had good discriminatory ability for these outcomes (area under the curve = 0.752; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.606–0.899; P = 0.005). The optimal cutoff value for preoperative corneal SA based on the Youden index was 0.2935 μm (sensitivity: 90.9%; specificity: 57.1%).

The cohort was stratified into low SA (n = 15; SA < 0.2935 μm) and high SA (n = 30; SA ≥ 0.2935 μm) groups based on this threshold. Post-hoc power analysis was performed using G*Power 3.1 (Heinrich Heine University Düsseldorf, Germany) to determine the statistical power for the comparisons between the groups. The statistical power was 96% based on the sample sizes, two-tailed significance level of 0.05, and large effect size (Cohen’s d = 1.2) for UIVA.

The demographic characteristics and preoperative and postoperative ocular parameters (except preoperative and postoperative corneal SA; P < 0.001 for both) were comparable for the two groups (all P > 0.05) (Table 2).

Visual outcomes and defocus curve

The VAs for the + 1.50 D to -0.50 D range did not differ significantly between the two groups (Fig. 3). The low SA group had better VA than the high SA group (all P < 0.05) for the range of -1.00 D to -5.00 D, corresponding to intermediate-to-near distances (1.0–0.20 m) (Fig. 3, Additional file 3).

Additionally, the GEE analysis, which adjusted for postoperative SE, confirmed that the low SA group showed significantly better VA than the high SA group did for the range of -1.00 D to -3.50 D (1.0–0.29 m). Specifically, the low SA group showed significantly better intermediate vision at -1.50 D (0.098 logMAR difference; 95% CI of -0.147 to -0.050; P < 0.001). The visual advantage of the low SA group remained consistent throughout the extended defocus range, with the most notable difference observed at -3.50 D (0.202 logMAR; 95% CI of -0.330 to -0.075; P = 0.002).

Spectacle independence

The responses to the five questionnaire items were dichotomized as positive (top two categories) or negative (remaining three) and compared using Pearson’s chi-squared or Fisher’s exact test. A significantly larger proportion of the low SA group responded “Not at all” or “Rarely” for spectacle frequency (P = 0.006) (Fig. 4a). The overall satisfaction (Fig. 4b) and perceived inconvenience (Fig. 4c) did not vary between the groups (both P > 0.05). This trend remained in GEE analysis after adjusting for postoperative SE.

Reading was the most commonly cited activity in both groups. The high SA group had a greater tendency to require spectacles for distance-related tasks, such as driving (9.1%) and watching television (2.2%). Only approximately 10% of the participants in either group achieved complete spectacle independence (no need for spectacles for any activity) (Figure 4d).

Contrast sensitivity and photic phenomena

The low SA group exhibited higher mean photopic contrast sensitivity across all spatial frequencies at 3 months postoperatively; nonetheless, the differences were not significant (all P > 0.05) (Additional file 4a, Additional file 5). The QoV questionnaire was administered to investigate photic phenomena, and no statistically significant differences were observed between the groups for any of the categories (all P > 0.05) (Additional file 4b, Additional file 5). This trend remained in GEE analysis after adjusting for postoperative SE for contrast sensitivity and photic phenomena.

Comparison of the outcomes based on the IOL model

The preoperative parameters and postoperative outcomes of the eyes implanted with ICB00 or DIB00 IOLs and those implanted with DIU IOLs were comparable (P > 0.05) (Additional file 6).

Discussion

This study examined the intermediate VA of enhanced monofocal IOLs and the association of preoperative corneal SAs with postoperative UIVA. Our primary finding was that patients with lower preoperative corneal SAs had significantly better UIVAs. We identified an exploratory preoperative corneal SA threshold of approximately 0.29 μm for discriminating patients at risk of poor postoperative UIVA (defined as > 0.1 logMAR). This stratification confirmed that the low SA group achieved a clinically significant enhancement in the DoF for the intermediate-to-near range (100–20 cm). Notably, this superior performance remained statistically significant in the GEE analysis even after adjusting for postoperative SE and inter-eye correlation, indicating that the extended visual benefit is significantly associated with the corneal SA profile rather than the residual refractive error. This objective improvement in the visual range was associated with reduced spectacle use, as demonstrated by our patient-reported outcomes. This enhancement in the DoF was achieved without compromising contrast sensitivity or inducing photic phenomena, which is advantageous for this subgroup. The mean anterior corneal SA was consistent with previous research [20], and its stability after surgery was consistent with that reported in the literature [21, 22]. This supports the reliability of using preoperative measurements for expectation management and counseling.

A key finding is the positive relationship between the magnitude of preoperative corneal SA and intermediate vision in logMAR with the TECNIS Eyhance, which indicated that VA worsens with increasing preoperative corneal SA. This is in contrast to the findings for the TECNIS Symfony, where higher preoperative corneal SA enhanced postoperative UNVA but reduced contrast sensitivity [22], likely reflecting differences in the optical design.

The TECNIS Symfony is a diffractive extended DoF IOL with an echelette design that creates a single elongated focus [23]. In contrast, the Eyhance is purely refractive and extends the DoF by elongating the focal point smoothly through a continuous power profile on its aspheric surface [24–26]. It provides superior distance-corrected intermediate VA and broader defocus curves than conventional monofocal IOLs, while maintaining the same optical quality as that of the latter [27, 28]. Its inherent SA of − 0.27 μm [29] is designed to preserve optical quality by counteracting the average positive corneal SA [30]. However, the relationship between ocular SA and DoF is complex. Although reducing the total ocular SA toward zero may optimize retinal image quality for distance vision [22, 28], several adaptive optics studies have suggested that a small amount of residual SA may increase DoF under certain conditions [14, 29, 31]. These effects appear to depend on multiple factors, including the sign and magnitude of SA [12, 29], pupil size [28, 32], and the optical design of the IOL [29]. For instance, while some previous studies using diffractive or extended DoF IOLs have reported that residual positive SA can contribute to DoF extension [22], recent adaptive optics simulations have demonstrated that for refractive IOLs, a slightly negative total ocular SA is significantly more effective in extending DoF without compromising distance vision [12, 29, 31]. In this context, our findings suggest that a lower preoperative corneal SA is associated with better postoperative intermediate-to-near visual performance for eyes implanted with the Eyhance IOL. Although postoperative ocular SA was not directly measured in this study, the − 0.27 μm inherent SA of the IOL [29] might have overcompensated the lower positive corneal SA (mean: 0.23 μm) in the low SA group, hypothetically resulting in a slightly negative total ocular SA of approximately − 0.04 μm, which closely aligns with the optimal theoretical value for extended DoF [31]. The comparable contrast sensitivity and photic phenomena across groups suggest that the observed association is not accompanied by a measurable reduction in overall optical quality. Taken together, our results support the possibility that preoperative corneal SA may influence the functional visual outcomes observed with this enhanced monofocal IOL, although direct mechanistic conclusions remain limited.

The clinical implications are significant for preoperative planning and counseling. Preoperative corneal SA is a crucial and easily measurable clinical indicator associated with intermediate and near visual outcomes following enhanced monofocal IOL implantation. Our results indicate that an exploratory preoperative corneal SA cutoff of approximately 0.29 μm may serve as a potential reference for predicting UIVA outcomes, although this value is exploratory rather than definitive, given the relatively small cohort size and the indirect nature of the association. Rather than using it as an absolute exclusion criterion, clinicians can consider this approximate value as an adjunctive tool for managing preoperative expectations. Instead of contraindicating the use of the Eyhance IOL for patients with high SA (≥ 0.29 μm), clinicians can use this approximate value to proactively inform them. Their distance vision will be excellent; however, their near vision, specifically within the range of 100 to 20 cm, may require more frequent use of spectacles. Ultimately, proactive counseling is paramount to ensuring postoperative satisfaction by aligning patient expectations with probable outcomes. Clinicians can reassure patients that their visual quality is unlikely to be compromised regardless of their SA profile, since preoperative corneal SA did not significantly affect their contrast sensitivity or photic phenomena.

The study limitations include its single-center and single-surgeon design, retrospective nature, specific population, and exclusive focus on the Eyhance IOL. These factors may limit the generalizability of the findings across ethnicities, age groups, and other types of IOLs. In addition, because a control group, such as patients implanted with standard monofocal IOLs, was not included, we cannot definitively conclude whether the observed relationship between preoperative corneal SA and DoF is exclusively attributed to the optical profile of the enhanced monofocal IOL or a general optical phenomenon. Therefore, our findings should be cautiously interpreted as observational associations rather than direct causal relationships or comparative effectiveness. We also measured corneal SA rather than postoperative total ocular SA. Ocular SA was estimated based on the design characteristics of the TECNIS Eyhance IOL, and the association between higher preoperative corneal SA and worse intermediate and near vision was therefore indirect. In addition, while corneal SA is a significant factor, it is not the sole determinant of defocus performance; factors such as IOL centration, pupil size, other high-order aberrations, and postoperative SE can also influence visual outcomes. Future large-scale prospective studies incorporating a broader range of optical metrics are warranted to fully elucidate these multifactorial interactions. The follow-up period was short, limiting the assessment of neuroadaptation, which can influence visual function. Distinct from previous studies that primarily compared enhanced monofocal IOLs with standard monofocal IOLs, a key strength of this study lies in its analysis of patient-specific biometric determinants within a relatively homogeneous surgical cohort. By utilizing standardized postoperative assessments, we were able to more precisely evaluate the specific contribution of corneal SA to the extension of visual range. Furthermore, this study is valuable as it is the first to determine the optimal SA threshold and obtain preoperative corneal SA-specific defocus curves for an enhanced monofocal IOL. Future research should directly evaluate the relationship between changes in the total ocular optical characteristics and visual function across broader IOL models and diverse populations.

Conclusions

The findings of this study suggest that preoperative corneal SA is significantly associated with postoperative intermediate and near visual performance after implantation of the enhanced monofocal IOL TECNIS Eyhance. Preoperative corneal SA positively correlated with the UIVA (in logMAR), and an exploratory cutoff value of approximately 0.29 μm was identified. The group with lower preoperative corneal SA exhibited a broader DoF and better intermediate and near VAs. Eyes implanted with the TECNIS Eyhance IOL that showed preoperative corneal SA below this approximate threshold showed a significant association with better intermediate and near visual performance and a reduced frequency of spectacle use. However, no significant differences in contrast sensitivity or QoV were observed based on corneal SA, confirming that these visual benefits can be achieved without compromising overall visual quality. Assessing preoperative corneal SA can enhance expectation management and counseling for selecting an enhanced monofocal IOL.

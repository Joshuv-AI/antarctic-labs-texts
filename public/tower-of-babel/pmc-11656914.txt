Design and analysis of a telemonitoring system for high-risk pregnant women in need of special care or attention
Authors: Mojdeh Nazari, Shadi Moayed Rezaie, Fereshteh Yaseri, Hossein Sadr, Elham Nazari
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHigh-risk pregnancies, characterized by underlying health issues or unusual circumstances, pose increased risks to both maternal and neonatal health during pregnancy and childbirth. Global guidelines emphasize the importance of early identification, monitoring, and intervention to mitigate these risks.MethodWe decided to design and implement a telemonitoring system for remotely monitoring and managing pregnancies in women with conditions such as hypertension, diabetes, or high-risk pregnancy. When a high-risk pregnant mom is discharged from the hospital, the Healthcare Center or Integrated Healthcare Services Center in her area is immediately notified via SMS to ensure her condition is monitored remotely. In addition to sending notifications, the patient’s medical record, post-discharge care recommendations, drug prescription, re-visit time, and contact details are also sent to them via the application. The high-risk pregnant mom is followed up and all her information is recorded in the application for further use. To evaluate the usability of the proposed telemonitoring system, we conducted a laboratory study involving 92 participants, including pregnant mothers’ care experts at hospitals, healthcare center experts, midwives at integrated healthcare services centers, and midwifery department experts with varying levels of digital skills. Participants performed activities related to the application’s services, while their satisfaction was measured using the QUIS 7.0 questionnaires covering multiple aspects of usability, including user interface, system capabilities, and online help, with ratings on a Likert scale.ResultThe usability evaluation revealed that the average satisfaction score across all usability dimensions was above 8, demonstrating a satisfactory level of system usage from all user perspectives. Additionally, the close alignment of mean and median scores, along with standard deviations below 1 for several dimensions, indicated consistent positive feedback among users.ConclusionOur telemonitoring system demonstrates promise for enhancing the management of high-risk pregnancies, facilitating better health outcomes for mothers and infants through effective remote monitoring and support. The usability test results underscore the platform’s effectiveness and user satisfaction, contributing valuable insights for future improvements in high-risk pregnancy care.Keywords: E-Health, Telemonitoring system, High-risk pregnancy, Maternal mortality, Web application

Introduction

Maternal mortality is alarmingly high and according to the World Health Organization (WHO), around 830 women die every day due to preventable reasons associated with pregnancy and childbirth. The vast majority of these tragic deaths, nearly 95%, occurred in low and lower-middle-income countries [1]. It is also reported that approximately 287,000 women lost their lives in 2020 due to complications during pregnancy and childbirth [2]. In the Islamic Republic of Iran, a middle-income country with a population of approximately 84 million (49% female), the maternal mortality ratio was 22 deaths per 100,000 live births in 2020 which is not sufficiently low compared with some developed countries [3]. It is important to note that the majority of these deaths could have been prevented by identifying high-risk pregnant women and enhancing their quality of care before, during, and after pregnancy [2, 4]. Accordingly, the Ministry of Health and Medical Education (MOHME) emphasized that risks during pregnancy must be avoided or reduced by effectively monitoring the pregnancy and ensuring timely diagnoses due to the aging population crisis in Iran.

A high-risk pregnancy refers to cases where there are underlying pathological issues or abnormal conditions that increase risks to both the mother and the baby’s health. These risks can arise from pre-existing health problems in women, such as diabetes, cardiac disorders, kidney and liver disorders, hypertension, etc. [5]. For instance, high levels of blood sugar in the mother can lead to birth defects, sometimes even before the woman realizes she is pregnant. On the other hand, pregnant women with uncontrolled high blood pressure are at risk of kidney damage, pre-eclampsia, or giving birth to low-weight infants. Most of these risks can be controlled and prevented because there are widely known healthcare solutions to manage and prevent complications related to pregnancy. Although it is crucial for all women to have support and timely access to skilled nursing care throughout pregnancy, childbirth, and the weeks following childbirth, there are various factors that hinder women from receiving the necessary care, such as poverty, delays, cultural practices, lack of information and awareness, insufficient healthcare services, and a shortage of skilled healthcare professionals [6, 7].

Monitoring the condition of high-risk pregnant mothers has always been of great importance [8]. Before the development of technology and the arrival of the internet and smartphones, paper-based processes were utilized to monitor and follow the condition of pregnant mothers, while paper-based processes are still used in Iran to follow up high-risk pregnant mothers. However, the emergence of new technologies has made big changes in this field and new tools were designed to improve health monitoring, management, and decision-making as well as offering significant advantages. An option to conduct this monitoring and control is the use of technology and telemonitoring which have had a profound impact on saving the lives of pregnant mothers in recent years by providing various digital tools and platforms to monitor and manage maternal health remotely [9–13]. Telemonitoring refers to the range of assistance systems and medical services that perform remote monitoring of a patient’s condition and vital signs. It enables the provision of aid and healthcare to patients in their regular surroundings. In this era, technologies like computers, smartphones, communication systems, and eHealth apps have the potential to greatly reduce factors contributing to maternal mortality and make a significant impact on saving the lives of mothers worldwide [11, 14–17]. Although some works have proposed systems to monitor high-risk pregnant mothers, the lack of such a system is felt in Iran.

Accordingly, we decided to design and implement a telemonitoring system for high-risk pregnant women in need of special care or attention who visited Guilan’s hospitals and have hypertension, diabetes, and other risk factors. These risk factors include severe anemia, morbid obesity, mild pre-eclampsia, gestational diabetes, cardiopathy, previous uterine surgery, twin or multiple pregnancies, endocrinological diseases, second and/or third-trimester hemorrhage, hydramnios or oligohydramnios, unfavorable obstetric history, and maternal infections [18, 19]. Based on our proposed framework, when a high-risk pregnant mother visits a hospital in any city of Guilan, her condition must be monitored to prevent possible risks for the mother and the baby. In this regard, when the mother is discharged from the hospital, the Health Center or Integrated Healthcare Services Center where she lives is instantly notified via receiving an SMS that a high-risk mother lives around them, and her condition must be remotely followed. Besides notifying, the patient’s medical record, post-discharge care including recommendations, drug prescription, re-visit time, and contact details are sent to them via the designed web application. All care provided for the pregnant mother is registered and can be utilized for future tracking.

Generally, it can be claimed that this is the first and only web application that is designed and employed to track high-risk pregnant mothers in Iran. The main purpose of designing this system is to provide a telemonitoring system that has the potential to address obstacles restricting access to high-quality maternal and child healthcare. It also assists in enabling women, their families, and local health professionals to promptly seek the right medical assistance during obstetric and newborn emergencies by minimizing the delay between a health emergency and receiving care. To measure the users’ satisfaction with our proposed system, a usability evaluation was also conducted involving maternal care specialists of hospitals, Health Center users, Integrated Healthcare Services Center users, and Midwifery Department users. The results indicate that our proposed system received positive feedback and was well-received by the users.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Sect. 2 includes a summary of related studies. The details of the proposed system besides the usability test results are provided in Sect. 3. Discussion is mentioned in Sect. 4 while Sect. 5 includes the conclusion and future research direction.

Methodology

Telemonitoring high-risk pregnant mothers is of significant importance as it allows for closer and continuous monitoring of both the mother and the fetus, enhancing the quality of care and potentially reducing adverse outcomes. More details about the analysis, design, and development of the proposed online telemonitoring system focused on the remote monitoring and treatment of pregnant moms suffering from hypertension, diabetes, and other risk factors are provided in the following.

System analysis

This step involves gathering information about the target population, identifying the needs or requirements to fulfill, determining the content and knowledge to be delivered, defining the tasks and functions to be accomplished, and evaluating feasible technologies for development and implementation. While the goal of this paper is to design and develop an online system for following up on the condition of high-risk pregnant mothers who live in Guilan, we need some initial information on this era.

Generally, Iran’s healthcare system is structured in a way that includes various levels of healthcare centers to provide services to the population. In every province, there is at least one university of medical sciences, and hospitals and healthcare centers are its sub-groups. Each of the cities of the province has also one healthcare center and several public or private hospitals according to their population. Considering that healthcare centers are located in the center of the cities and cities themselves have many districts and villages, people who live there also need access to health services near their place of residence. Accordingly, each healthcare center has several integrated healthcare services centers as its sub-categories in that city’s different sections and villages. Notably, Guilan province, located in the north of Iran, includes 18 cities and 35 hospitals that are located in different cities (each city has at least one hospital). Each city also has one healthcare center which has various integrated healthcare services centers based on the area of the city as its subset. Integrated healthcare services centers are located in both urban and rural and are responsible for providing health services for people who live around them.

To better understand the process of following the condition of pregnant mothers, we first decided to visualize the required steps as a workflow which is depicted in Fig. 1. As clear, the workflow is started while a pregnant mother is admitted to a hospital. Next, the pregnant mother is visited by a doctor and is labeled as an inpatient or an outpatient. Inpatient one is admitted to the ward and then discharged when she is well. While the pregnant mother is labeled as a high-risk pregnancy at discharge time, her medical records are referred to the pregnant mother’s care expert at the hospital. It is where our telemonitoring system starts its work. At this step, the expert logs in to her cartable on the software and fills in the required information. She must carefully select the healthcare center to which she wants to refer the high-risk mother for further treatment. The healthcare center is decided based on the place of residence of the pregnant mother. The details of the form that is filled by the hospital experts are shown in Table 1.

While the workflow and required information are specified, it is time to start the development process. The process of planning, creating, testing, and deploying an information system or software is referred to as the application development life cycle. It involves dividing the software development work into different phases to improve planning and management. The development of our system utilized the iterative and incremental development methodology [20, 21]. This means that the system was developed through repeated cycles, addressing smaller portions at a time. This approach allows the developer to incorporate lessons learned from earlier parts or versions of the system. The development begins with a basic implementation of the system and progressively adds new features, functionalities, and design modifications as required. Technology and tools

The proposed software is an online web application that is developed as a server-side application and web user interface. ASP.net and the PHP programming language were used as the preferred technologies for this project. The SQL Server database was utilized for storing and managing the demographic and health records of expectant mothers. During the development, additional tools like JavaScript, XML, AJAX, Web 2.0, Web services, and JSON were also incorporated to design the web pages. The programming files of our software were installed on a central server, and the clients can use the program on different platforms like mobile, tablet, or personal computers. Microsoft Power BI was also used to develop the interactive dashboard containing the data of high-risk pregnant moms who were admitted to Guilan’s hospital.

Safety and confidentiality requirements

Protecting patient data in a telemonitoring system is paramount due to the sensitive and personal nature of the medical information involved. Safeguarding this data is crucial for maintaining patient trust, ensuring confidentiality, and complying with legal and ethical standards. It is worth mentioning that the development of this telemonitoring system for the follow-up of high-risk pregnant mothers was proposed as a project at Gilan University of Medical Sciences and its development license was also issued by them. Its development process also took place under the direct supervision of the University’s Treatment Deputy and Statistics and Information Technology Management. Regarding data security, security measures like encryption, access controls, regular audits, and secure data storage protocols were employed to mitigate risks of data breaches or unauthorized access while the server and database of this system are under the authority of the Statistics and Information Technology Management of Guilan University of Medical Sciences, and its data, like all the information in the university, is fully protected. Despite all these issues, this system has been developed in a platform with an “AFTA (in Persian) certificate”. AFTA certificate refers to “Security of Production Space and Information Exchange”, and is a license granted by the Presidential Strategic Management Center and Information Technology Organization of Iran. This certificate is part of a security assessment plan aimed at ensuring the security of software systems.

Information management and data visualization

While the goal of designing the proposed software was to collect the data of high-risk pregnant mom to analyze them and empower the Midwifery Department of Guilan University of Medical Sciences to unlock the value of their data, gain actionable insights, and drive informed decision-making at various levels, we decided to design an interactive dashboard using PowerBI. Using Power BI to create a dashboard allows us to transform raw data into interactive and visually appealing charts, graphs, and reports which makes it easier for users to understand and analyze complex data sets, identify trends, and draw insights. With Power BI, we could connect to live data sources and create real-time dashboards that enable Midwifery department users to track important metrics, such as blood pressure, weight gain, fetal development, medical appointments, and any other relevant data points. By visualizing this data, they can easily identify trends, patterns, and potential risks, enabling them to provide better care and support to high-risk pregnant moms.

Usability and assessment

The usability of the proposed telemonitoring system was rigorously evaluated through a comprehensive laboratory study involving 92 participants. This sample included a diverse group of healthcare professionals: 36 pregnant mothers’ care experts at various hospitals with intermediate digital skills, 18 experts at various Healthcare Centers with intermediate digital skills, 35 experts or midwives at the Midwifery Department. Participants were selected based on their varying degrees of digital skills; specifically, the majority had intermediate skills, while some possessed basic digital skills. During the usability study, participants were individually tasked with performing a series of activities related to the application’s functionality, including navigating the interface, accessing patient data, and utilizing the system’s support features. These tasks were designed to reflect real-world scenarios that high-risk pregnant women and their caregivers might encounter while using the telemonitoring system.

To assess user satisfaction comprehensively, we adhered to the usability standards outlined in ISO/IEC 25010:2011 [22] which is an international standard that provides a framework for evaluating the quality of software products, specifically focusing on their quality characteristics. We also used Questionnaire for User Interaction Satisfaction (QUIS) version 7.0 in our study [23, 24] which is a well-established tool designed to evaluate user satisfaction with interactive systems. QUIS 7.0 consists of a structured set of questions that allows researchers to gauge user perceptions across several dimensions including general reaction to the software, system user interface, terminology and system information, learning, system capabilities, technical manuals, and online help resources. By utilizing the QUIS 7.0 questionnaire in conjunction with the ISO/IEC 25010:2011 standards, our usability study can comprehensively assess the performance and satisfaction of users interacting with the telemonitoring system. Each question utilized a 10-point Likert scale, allowing participants to rate their satisfaction from 0 (indicating the lowest level of satisfaction) to 9 (indicating the highest level of satisfaction). Discussion

Globally, the population of women facing elevated risks during pregnancy is on the rise due to unhealthy lifestyle choices, obesity, conceiving at an older age, and the presence of other medical conditions. A high-risk pregnancy is characterized by the presence of a factor, either maternal or fetal, which has the potential to negatively impact the outcome of the pregnancy. Examples include preterm rupture of membranes (PROM), fetal growth restriction (FGR), and preeclampsia (PE). International guidelines recommend heightened monitoring and assessment of maternal and fetal parameters, often resulting in hospitalization [5, 18, 26]. In this regard, the use of telemonitoring to follow up on high-risk pregnant women who were discharged from hospitals obtained considerable attention in recent years [9, 10].

The development of the proposed system is driven by the necessity to enhance the monitoring of high-risk pregnancies by taking into account various relevant factors related to managing such pregnancies. Numerous systems identified in both academic literature [27–34] and commercial applications [35, 36] focus on promoting self-care and monitoring during pregnancies by providing bio-signal monitoring services and data on high-risk conditions during pregnancy. These systems facilitate prompt actions by clinicians and patients. In this context, we examined a range of telemonitoring systems outlined in scholarly articles and available in the market, with a focus on the following aspects:

In summary, the main goal of these methods is to gather patient data and transmit it to healthcare providers for potential monitoring, control, and remote treatment. Nevertheless, none of these systems fully achieve this objective due to the absence of essential features like clinical history management. This lack results in incomplete patient histories, leading to potential risk scenarios not being communicated to specialists. Additionally, crucial services such as medical advice and fetal monitoring may be absent in existing market applications. The most comprehensive system examined in reference [30] necessitates a robust infrastructure and specific sensors for effective implementation of the monitoring process. Conversely, the study discussed in [10] stands out for integrating a monitoring system in its telehealth approach, focusing on genetic counseling, mental health support, and postpartum care.

Drawing from a review of studies by Josephus F. van den Heuvel [37] and our research findings (refer to Table 2), our system sets itself apart from other proposals by offering remote monitoring and management for individuals with hypertension, diabetes, and high-risk pregnancies. This is achieved through specialized services like maintaining clinical histories and receiving medical alerts triggered by patient data. However, the study highlighted in [37] points out the limited research focus on evaluating the usability of telemonitoring systems designed for high-risk pregnant women through laboratory investigations. Additionally, the studies in [37–39] underscore the necessity to educate and create awareness among pregnant women concerning various maternal-fetal risks associated with conditions like hypertension, type 2 diabetes, gestational diabetes, maternal infections, and fetal malformations, among others. Particularly, the research in [38, 39] demonstrates the benefits of educating women about maternal-fetal risks connected with gestational diabetes mellitus and utilizing prenatal tests to identify chromosomal abnormalities. Furthermore, according to the study in [40], another critical aspect of preventive healthcare for pregnant women and their babies is maintaining women’s oral health. Inadequate oral hygiene during pregnancy can lead to preterm birth of underweight babies.

Conclusion

A telemonitoring system for following up high-risk pregnant women who are discharged from the hospital and are in need of special care or attention is proposed in this paper. Our proposed system demonstrates that telemonitoring can offer an opportunity to overcome barriers that limit access to quality maternal and child health as well as help women, their families, and local health workers to seek timely, appropriate medical help for an obstetric and newborn emergency by reducing the time that elapses between a health crisis and care.

The effectiveness of our system was confirmed through a user-friendliness assessment carried out in a controlled environment involving 92 participants, comprising 36 pregnant mothers’ care experts at various hospitals, 18 experts at various Healthcare Centers, 35 experts or midwives at Integrated Healthcare Services Centers, and 3 experts at Midwifery Department. They all appreciated the system’s ability to monitor and manage high-risk pregnancies by using features like medical consultations, managing medical records, observing patient-generated alerts, and providing medical recommendations. As a result, it can be concluded that our telemonitoring system meets the necessary requirements and parameters outlined in specialized literature for monitoring hypertensive individuals, diabetic patients, and high-risk pregnant women.

Although our system can be viewed as a specialized software tool that can aid in enhancing remote monitoring and management of these individuals within healthcare facilities, a drawback of our system is that is not designed for patient use. Designing patients’ panels where they can remotely contact their doctor or midwife as well as access their clinical information is worth exploring.

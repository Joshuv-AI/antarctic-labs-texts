Nevada: Codification and Incorporation by Reference of Approved 
Underground Storage Tank Program

AGENCY: Environmental Protection Agency (EPA).

ACTION: Direct final rule.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: On September 30, 2021, the State of Nevada (Nevada) received 
final approval, from the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), of 
revisions to its Underground Storage Tank Program (UST Program) under 
subtitle I of the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (RCRA). 
Pursuant to RCRA, the EPA is taking direct final action to codify the 
approval of Nevada's revised UST Program and incorporate by reference 
those provisions of Nevada's statutes and regulations that the EPA has 
determined meet the requirements for approval.

DATES: This rule is effective December 21, 2026, unless the EPA 
receives adverse comment by November 23, 2026. If the EPA receives 
adverse comment, it will publish a timely withdrawal in the Federal 
Register informing the public that the rule will not take effect. The 
incorporation by reference of certain publications listed in the 
regulations is approved by the Director of the Federal Register as of 
December 21, 2026.

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION:

I. Codification of Nevada's Approved Underground Storage Tank Program

A. What is codification?

    Codification is the process of placing citations and references to 
a State's statutes and regulations that comprise a State's approved UST 
program into the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR). The EPA codifies 
its approval of State programs in 40 CFR part 282 and incorporates by 
reference State statutes and regulations that the EPA can enforce, 
after the approval is final, under sections 9005 and 9006 of RCRA, and 
any other applicable statutory provisions. The incorporation by 
reference of EPA-approved State programs in the CFR should 
substantially enhance the public's ability to discern the status of the 
approved State UST programs and State requirements that can be 
federally enforced. This effort provides clear notice to the public of 
the scope of the approved program in each State.

B. What is the history of codification of Nevada's UST Program?

    The EPA originally approved the Nevada UST Program on December 24, 
1992 (57 FR 61376). This approval was effective on March 30, 1993. The 
EPA then incorporated by reference and codified Nevada's approved UST 
program on July 17, 1998 (63 FR 38500), effective September 15, 1998. 
Nevada revised its UST program in response to revisions to the Federal 
UST regulations published in a final rule on July 15, 2015 (80 FR 
41566). On November 20, 2018, in accordance with 40 CFR 281.51(a), 
Nevada submitted an application seeking the EPA's approval for its 
revised UST Program. On July 29, 2021, Nevada submitted amendments to 
the application, based on comments from the EPA. On September 30, 2021, 
the EPA granted final approval to Nevada's revised UST Program (86 FR 
54107), effective November 29, 2021. Through this action, the EPA is 
amending 40 CFR 282.78 to incorporate by reference and codify Nevada's 
revised UST Program.

C. What codification decisions is the EPA making in this rule?

    In this rule, the EPA is finalizing regulatory text that 
incorporates by reference Nevada's revised and federally approved UST 
Program. In accordance with the requirements of 1 CFR 51.5, the EPA is 
incorporating by reference Nevada's regulations as described in the

amendments to 40 CFR part 282 set forth below.

D. What is the effect of the EPA's codification of the federally 
approved Nevada UST Program on enforcement?

    The EPA retains the authority under sections 9003(h), 9005, and 
9006 of subtitle I of RCRA, 42 U.S.C. 6991b(h), 6991d, and 6991e, and 
other applicable statutory and regulatory provisions, to undertake 
corrective action, inspections, and enforcement actions, and to issue 
orders in approved States. If the EPA determines it will take such 
actions in Nevada, the EPA will rely on Federal sanctions, Federal 
inspection authorities, and other Federal procedures rather than the 
State analogs. Therefore, the EPA is not incorporating by reference 
Nevada's procedural and enforcement authorities, although they are 
listed in 40 CFR 282.78(d)(1)(ii).

E. What is a ``broader in scope'' provision?

    States may enact laws that are more stringent than their Federal 
counterparts (See RCRA section 9008, 42 U.S.C. 6991g). When an approved 
State program includes requirements that are considered more stringent 
than those required by Federal law, the more stringent requirements 
become part of the federally approved program in accordance with 40 CFR 
281.12(a)(3)(i).
    In addition, States may enact laws which are broader in scope than 
their Federal counterparts in accordance with 40 CFR 281.12(a)(3)(ii). 
State requirements that go beyond the scope of the Federal program are 
broader in scope. These requirements are not part of the federally 
approved program, and the EPA cannot enforce them. Although broader in 
scope requirements in Nevada's UST Program are enforceable by Nevada in 
accordance with Nevada law, they are not Federal RCRA requirements.

F. What State provisions are not part of the codification?

    As discussed above in sections I.C. and I.E. of this preamble, some 
provisions of Nevada's UST Program are not part of the federally 
approved UST Program because they are broader in scope than the Federal 
UST Program. Where an approved State program has provisions that are 
broader in scope than the Federal program, those provisions are not a 
part of the federally approved program. As a result, State provisions 
which are broader in scope than the Federal program are not 
incorporated by reference in 40 CFR part 282 for purposes of 
enforcement (See 40 CFR 281.12(a)(3)(ii)). For reference and clarity, 
40 CFR 282.78(d)(1)(iii) lists the Nevada statutory and regulatory 
provisions which are broader in scope than the Federal program. These 
provisions are, therefore, not part of the approved UST Program that 
the EPA is codifying. Although these provisions cannot be enforced by 
the EPA, Nevada will continue to implement and enforce such provisions 
under Nevada law.

    (a) History of the approval of Nevada's program. The State of

Selberg, Ihara and Berkovich
Jialun Li, Carlos Matheus, Wenyu Pan, Zhongkai Tao

Abstract
We use the Selberg zeta function to study the limit behavior of resonances in a degenerating family of Kleinian Schottky groups. We prove that, after a suitable rescaling, the Selberg zeta functions converge to the Ihara zeta function of a limiting finite graph associated to the relevant non-Archimedean Schottky group acting on the Berkovich projective line. Moreover, we show that these techniques can be used to get an exponential error term in a result of McMullen (recently extended by Dang and Mehmeti) about the asymptotics for the vanishing rate of the Hausdorff dimension of limit sets of certain degenerating Schottky groups generating symmetric three-funnel surfaces. Here, one key idea is to introduce an intermediate zeta function capturing both non-Archimedean and Archimedean information (while the traditional Selberg, resp. Ihara zeta functions concern only Archimedean, resp. non-Archimedean properties).

Introduction

A
Schottky group
of rank
g

over

\mathbb{C}
(also called Kleinian Schottky group) is a discrete subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
which is purely loxodromic and isomorphic to the free group of rank
g
. These groups were introduced by Schottky in 1877 and they were classically studied in connection with the famous uniformization theorem for Riemann surfaces: indeed, Koebe proved in 1910 that any closed Riemann surface is uniformized by some Kleinian Schottky group.

Partly motivated by the great success of the study of degenerating families of Riemann surfaces (ultimately leading to the celebrated Deligne–Mumford compactification of moduli spaces and its numerous applications), we shall investigate in this paper the analytical and geometrical properties of
degenerating
families of Schottky groups over
\mathbb{C}
.

More concretely, given a family
\Gamma_{z}
of Schottky groups over
\mathbb{C}
generated by
2\times 2
matrices whose entries are meromorphic functions of
z\in\mathbb{D}^{*}
, it is sometimes possible to introduce a Schottky group
\Gamma^{na}
over the non-Archimedean field
\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
(of Laurent series on
t
) whose features can be used to extract asymptotic information on
\Gamma_{z}
as
|z|\to 0
. In fact, this philosophy of studying degenerating families of complex objects via non-Archimedean limiting objects is nowadays widely spread in the literature (cf.
[
Kiw15
]
,
[
BJ17
]
,
[
DF19
]
,
[
Fav20
]
and
[
Luo22
]
for some recent works) and, in the context of Schottky groups, this idea was exploited by Dang and Mehmeti
[
DM24
, Theorem 3]
to prove that certain degenerating families
\Gamma_{z}
of Schottky groups over
\mathbb{C}
possess
limit sets

L(\Gamma_{z})\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
whose Hausdorff dimensions satisfy

\textrm{dim}(L(\Gamma_{z}))\sim\frac{d_{0}}{\log(1/|z|)}

(1.1)

as
|z|\to 0
, where
d_{0}
is the Hausdorff dimension of the limit set of a non-Archimedean Schottky group
\Gamma^{na}
acting on the
Berkovich projective line

\mathbb{P}^{1,an}_{\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)}
. (We also recommend to the reader the very recent paper
[
CG24
]
of Courtois and Guilloux containing a partial extension of the work of Dang and Mehmeti to
higher
dimensions via an
alternative method
, namely, the study of the infinite dimensional hyperbolic space.)

In the present paper, we pursue this kind of philosophy in order to describe the Selberg zeta functions of certain degenerating families of Schottky groups over
\mathbb{C}
in terms of Ihara zeta functions of finite graphs naturally attached to non-Archimedean Schottky groups: see Subsection
1.2
below for precise statements. As a consequence, we are able to significantly refine the result of Dang and Mehmeti mentioned above. In particular, we improve upon the work of McMullen
[
McM98
]
on the Hausdorff dimension of limit sets of Schottky reflection groups associated to symmetric three-funnel hyperbolic surfaces and the theoretical and numerical works of Weich
[
Wei15
]
, Borthwick
[
Bor16
]
and Pollicott–Vytnova
[
PV19
]
on the convergence of rescaled Selberg zeta functions of symmetric three-funnel hyperbolic surfaces: see Subsection
1.1
below for concrete statements.

1.1
Example: symmetric three-funnel surfaces

Let
X(\ell)
be the symmetric three-funnel hyperbolic surface with parameter
\ell
, that is, the non-compact hyperbolic surface of genus zero that has three funnels of widths
\ell>0
. This gives an example of a family of degenerating surfaces when
\ell
tends to infinity. Denote by
\Gamma(\ell)
the Schottky group uniformizing
X(\ell)
, and let
L(\Gamma(\ell))
be the limit set of
\Gamma(\ell)
on the visual boundary
\partial\mathbb{H}^{2}
.
McMullen
[
McM98
]
studied the asymptotics of the Hausdorff dimension of
L(\Gamma(\ell))
as
\ell\to\infty
and he obtained
\dim(L(\Gamma(\ell)))\approx\frac{\log 4}{\ell}
.

For this particular example, as it will be explained in Example
6.7
below, the main results of this paper imply an exponential error term in McMullen’s asymptotic formula above:

Theorem 1.1
.

For the case of symmetric three-funnel hyperbolic surfaces, we have for any
\epsilon>0
,

\dim(L(\Gamma(\ell)))=\frac{\log 4}{\ell}+O_{\epsilon}(e^{-(1-\epsilon)\ell/4}).

Moreover, for the Selberg zeta function (see
Eq.

1.2
for definition), we have

|Z(\Gamma(\ell),s/\ell)-(1-6e^{-s}+9e^{-2s}-4e^{-3s})|=O_{K,\epsilon}(e^{-(1-\epsilon)\ell/4}),

for any
s
in a fixed compact set
K
and any
\epsilon>0
.

The convergence of the Selberg zeta function without speed is due to Weich
[
Wei15
]
.
Pollicott–Vytnova
[
PV19
]
proved a polynomial error term
O(1/\sqrt{\ell})
in the convergence of the Selberg zeta functions and
O(1/\sqrt{\ell^{3}})
in the convergence of Hausdorff dimensions (and, in fact, Example
6.8
below shows how to recover their results from our techniques). The exponential error term above also explains that the numerics of resonances in Borthwick
[
Bor16
]
behave well even for small
\ell
such as
\ell=10
: see Example
6.5
below for more details.

Our method of proof is different from previous works on symmetric three-funnel surfaces. Indeed, this family is just an example of a degenerating Schottky family and, as it is explained below, we actually study the limit behavior of all such families.

1.2
Main results: degenerating Schottky families

The study of the degenerations at the origin
z=0
of meromorphic families
\Gamma_{z}
of Kleinian Schottky (and, more generally, non-elementary) groups depending on a complex parameter
z\in\mathbb{D}^{*}
in the unit punctured disc of
\mathbb{C}
is a topic of intense investigations with a vast literature around it. For instance, such families are useful to build compactifications of representation variety, character variety or moduli spaces (see, e.g.,
[
CS83
]

[
Ota15
]
and
[
PT21
]
), their Lyapunov exponents for the associated random walks satisfy fascinating asymptotics (see, e.g.,
[
ACS83
]
and
[
DF19
]
), and, as we already mentioned above, Hausdorff dimensions of their limit sets attracted the attention of many authors.

In the present paper, the central objects of discussion will be the Selberg zeta functions. Recall that the Selberg zeta function for a Schottky group
\Gamma
of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is defined as follows. Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of oriented primitive periodic geodesics on
\mathrm{T}^{1}(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{3})
.

Z(\Gamma,s)=\prod_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}}\prod_{k_{1},k_{2}\geqslant 0}(1-e^{-(s+k_{1}+k_{2})\ell(\gamma)}e^{-i\theta_{\gamma}(k_{1}-k_{2})}).

(1.2)

Here, by a slight abuse of notation, we also denote by
\gamma
an oriented closed geodesic representing a loxodromic element
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
, so that
\ell(\gamma)
is the length of the periodic geodesic
\gamma
in
\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
, and
e^{i\theta_{\gamma}}
is the holonomy given by
\gamma^{\prime}(\gamma_{+})=e^{-\ell(\gamma)-i\theta_{\gamma}}
with
\gamma_{+}
being the attracting fixed point of
\gamma
. Similarly, for
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, the classical Selberg zeta function is defined as

Z(\Gamma,s)=\prod_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}}\prod_{k\geqslant 0}(1-e^{-(s+k)\ell(\gamma)}).

Note that the definitions for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
do not coincide, and, for this reason, we will deal with the two cases separately.

The function
Z(\Gamma,s)
is convergent and analytic in the region
\Re s>\delta_{\Gamma}
, where
\delta_{\Gamma}
is the so-called critical exponent. It admits an analytic continuation to the whole complex plane
\mathbb{C}
, and hence we obtain an entire function
Z(\Gamma,s)
. Due to
[
PP01
]
, we know that the poles of the resolvent of the Laplacian on
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{3}
(also called resonances) correspond to a subset of the zeros of the Selberg zeta function
Z(\Gamma,s)
.

For a degenerating family of Schottky groups, we define the
intermediate zeta function
. We will prove in
Proposition

4.2
and
Corollary

5.8
that it is well-defined and has a holomorphic extension to
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

Definition 1.2

(Intermediate zeta function)
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!))
and
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
, we define the approximate length function

\ell_{M}(\gamma,z)=\ell^{na}(\gamma)+\Re\sum\limits_{j=0}^{M}a_{j}(\gamma)z^{j}/\log(1/|z|)

where
a_{j}(\gamma)
’s are the coefficients in the expansion

\ell(\gamma_{z})/\log(1/|z|)=\ell^{na}(\gamma)+\Re\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}(\gamma)z^{j}/\log(1/|z|).

The intermediate
M
-zeta function is defined by

Z_{M}(\Gamma,z,s)=\prod_{[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}}(1-e^{-s\ell_{M}(\gamma,z)})

for
\Re s
sufficiently large.

Here is our main result:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Gamma_{z}
be a degenerating Schottky family satisfying condition
(\bigstar)
in Subsection
2.4
, and denote by
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!))
be the corresponding non-Archimedean Schottky group. Then for any
s\in\mathbb{C}
, as
|z|\rightarrow 0
, we have

Z(\Gamma_{z},s/\log(1/|z|))\rightarrow Z_{I}(\Gamma,s),

where
Z_{I}(\Gamma,s)
is the Ihara zeta function associated to
\Gamma
(defined in
Proposition

2.19
) and the convergence on any compact set is uniform.

Moreover, for any compact set
K
and
\epsilon>0
, we have for all
0<|z|<1/e
and for any
s\in K
,

|Z(\Gamma_{z},s/\log(1/|z|))-Z_{0}(\Gamma,z,s)|\lesssim_{K,\epsilon}|z|^{1-\epsilon}.

Furthermore, for any
C,\epsilon>0
and
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
, we have for
s\in[-C,C]+i[-C|z|^{-M},C|z|^{-M}]
and

|Z(\Gamma_{z},s/\log(1/|z|))-Z_{M}(\Gamma,z,s)|\lesssim_{C,M,\epsilon}|z|^{1-\epsilon}.

As a direct corollary, we obtain

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\Gamma_{z}
be a degenerating Schottky family satisfying the condition
(\bigstar)
in Subsection
2.4
. Let
P(|z|)
be the first zero of the intermediate zeta function
Z_{0}(\Gamma,z,s)
of the variable
s
. We have for any
\epsilon>0

\dim(L(\Gamma_{z}))=P(|z|)+O_{\epsilon}(|z|^{1-\epsilon}).

For special examples, like symmetric three-funnel surfaces and

6.4
, the main term has the form
P(|z|)=d_{0}/\log(1/|z|)
. For general examples, the main term
P(|z|)
is a zero of a polynomial of exponential functions of the variable
s
like
\sum a_{j}e^{-s(b_{j}+c_{j}/\log(1/|z|))}
.
It would be interesting to study the Taylor expansion of
P(|z|)
on
1/\log(1/|z|)
.

Remark 1.5
.

Due to the definition of
Z_{0}
, we know
P(|z|)
and the coefficients of the expansion only depend on the coefficients of
\mathrm{tr}(\gamma)
with
\gamma\in\Gamma
. For example, if we write
Z_{0}(\Gamma,z,s)=Z_{0}(s,u)
with

, then by the implicit function theorem, we obtain

P(|z|)=\frac{d_{0}}{\log(1/|z|)}-\frac{\partial_{2}Z_{0}}{\partial_{1}Z_{0}}(d_{0},0)\frac{1}{(\log(1/|z|))^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{(\log(1/|z|))^{3}}\right).

Can we find other interpretations of the term
\frac{\partial_{2}Z_{0}}{\partial_{1}Z_{0}}(d_{0},0)
, such as the variations of Hausdoff dimension
[
McM08
]
or the expansion of Lyapunov exponent
[
ACS83
]
?

We also obtain a corollary on the zeros of zeta functions and, consequently, on resonances of the Laplacian.

Corollary 1.6
.

The zeros of
Z_{I}(\Gamma,s)
have the structure
\mathrm{Res}_{I}(\Gamma)=\{\mu_{j}\}+2\pi i{\mathbb{Z}}
,
where
\{\mu_{j}\}
is a finite set. Outside
s=0
, the resonances of
\Gamma_{z}
converge to
\mathrm{Res}_{I}(\Gamma)
(counted with multiplicity) after rescaling, i.e.

\log(1/|z|)\,\mathrm{Res}(\Gamma_{z})\setminus\{0\}\to\mathrm{Res}_{I}(\Gamma)\setminus\{0\},\quad|z|\to 0

on compact sets. In particular, the spectral gap of
\Gamma_{z}
converges to

after rescaling by
\log(1/|z|)
.

Proof.

It follows from
Theorem

1.3
that the zeros of

4.5

(see

[
PP01
]
for the general case).

2
Preliminaries of Schottky groups

We recall some preliminaries on the Schottky groups.

2.1
Schottky groups of
\textrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})

An element
\gamma=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right)\in\textrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is called
loxodromic
if it induces a Möbius transformation
z\mapsto\frac{az+b}{cz+d}
on
\mathbb{C}
which is conjugated to
z\mapsto\lambda z
for a complex number
\lambda
with
0<|\lambda|<1
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a free subgroup
\Gamma=\langle M_{1},\dots,M_{g}\rangle\subset\textrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
with
g\geqslant 2
generators is a
Schottky group of rank
g
if

•

every
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\{id\}
is loxodromic;

•

\Gamma
admits a non-empty domain of discontinuity, i.e., it acts freely and properly on a non-empty connected invariant open subset of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
.

Given a Jordan curve
C
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
and a reference point
o\notin C
, we say that the exterior (resp. interior) of
C
is the connected component of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\backslash C
containing (resp. not containing)
o
. After Maskit
[
Mas67
]
, a Schottky group has the form
\langle T_{1},\dots,T_{g}\rangle
, where
T_{j}
’s induce Möbius transformations with the following property: there are a reference point
o
and Jordan curves
C_{1},C_{1}^{\prime},\dots,C_{g},C_{g}^{\prime}
with disjoint interiors such that each
T_{j}
maps the exterior of
C_{j}
to the interior of
C_{j}^{\prime}
. If all the Jordan curves
C_{j}
,
C_{j}^{\prime}
can be chosen to be circles, then the corresponding Schottky group is called a
classical Schottky group
.

2.2
Kleinian Schottky spaces

A loxodromic element
\gamma\in\textrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is determined by three parameters: its attracting fixed point
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
, its repelling fixed point
\beta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
, and its contraction rate
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}^{*}
. In the sequel, we shall denote by
M(\alpha,\beta,\lambda)
the loxodromic matrix parametrized by
\alpha
,
\beta
and
\lambda
.

The
Schottky space
S_{g}(\mathbb{C})
of rank
g\geqslant 2

is the space of
g
elements
M_{1},\dots,M_{g}
of
\textrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
that generate a Schottky group up to the natural equivalence given by simultaneous conjugation by Möbius transformations (cf. Bers
[
Ber75
]
). These elements are parametrized by
3g
quantities
\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\dots,\alpha_{g},\beta_{g},\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{g}
. Since the action of
\textrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
is
3
-point transitive, we can set
\alpha_{1}=0
,
\beta_{1}=2
,
\alpha_{2}=1
. In this way, we can regard
S_{g}(\mathbb{C})
as a subset of
\mathbb{C}^{3g-3}
: each
p\in S_{g}(\mathbb{C})
is parametrized by the so-called
Koebe coordinates

(\alpha_{3},\dots,\alpha_{g},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{g},\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{g})=(\underline{\alpha},\underline{\beta},\underline{\lambda})\in(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0,1,2\})^{2g-3}\times(\mathbb{D}^{*})^{g}.

As it was shown by Hejhal
[
Hej75
]
(see also
[
Ber75
]
),
S_{g}(\mathbb{C})
is a connected complex manifold of dimension
3g-3

Proof.

Using the definition of non-Archimedean length (

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{2}{(1-e^{-c\log(1/|z|)\ell^{na}(\gamma)})^{2}}-2\right)\leqslant\exp\left(\frac{4e^{-c\log(1/|z|)\ell^{na}(\gamma)}}{(1-e^{-c\log(1/|z|)\ell^{na}(\gamma)})^{2}}\right).

Combing these two cases together, we have for
|z|
small,

\displaystyle\prod_{[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}}|\mathcal{R}(\gamma)|^{-1}\leqslant\exp\left(8\sum_{[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}}e^{-c\log(1/|z|)\ell^{na}(\gamma)}\right).

(3.6)

We first sum over
\{[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}:\,\ell^{na}(\gamma)=n\}
and then sum over
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Due to
Lemma

3.7
, there exists a constant
C
only depending on the family such that

\#\{[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}:\,\ell^{na}(\gamma)=n\}\leqslant\#\{\gamma\,\text{cyclically reduced}:\,l(\gamma)\leqslant C^{\prime}n\}\leqslant e^{Cn}.

(3.7)

Hence

\displaystyle\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{control k part}\leqslant\exp\left(\frac{8e^{-(c\log(1/|z|)-C)}}{1-e^{-(c\log(1/|z|)-C)}}\right),

which converges to
1
as
|z|
tends to zero.

Now, we consider the other factor in
Eq.

3.4
. Take
N\gg 1
. We separate into two parts:
\ell^{na}(\gamma)\leqslant N
and
\ell^{na}(\gamma)>N
. For the first part, which is a product of finite terms, due to
Lemma

3.2
,

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}\\
\ell^{na}(\gamma)\leqslant N\end{subarray}}\frac{1-e^{-s/\log(1/|z|)\ell(\gamma_{z})}}{1-e^{-s\ell^{na}(\gamma)}}\rightarrow 1.

For the second part, due to
Lemma

3.5
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\log\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}\\
\ell^{na}(\gamma)>N\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{1-e^{-s/\log(1/|z|)\ell(\gamma_{z})}}{1-e^{-s\ell^{na}(\gamma)}}\right|\right|\leqslant 2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}\\
\ell^{na}(\gamma)>N\end{subarray}}e^{-\Re s/\log(1/|z|)\ell(\gamma_{z})}+e^{-\Re s\ell^{na}(\gamma)}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}\\
\ell^{na}(\gamma)>N\end{subarray}}e^{-\Re sc\ell^{na}(\gamma)}+e^{-\Re s\ell^{na}(\gamma)}\leqslant 2\sum_{n>N}e^{Cn}(e^{-n\Re sc}+e^{-\Re sn})=\frac{4e^{-N(\Re sc-C)}}{1-e^{-N(\Re sc-C)}},

where the first inequality is due to
Eq.

3.5
and the last inequality is due to
Eq.

3.7
.
Therefore, given
\Re s\gg 1
, we have that for
N
sufficiently large, the product

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}\\
\ell^{na}(\gamma)>N\end{subarray}}\frac{1-e^{-s/\log(1/|z|)\ell(\gamma_{z})}}{1-e^{-s\ell^{na}(\gamma)}}

is close to 1.
The proof is complete.
∎

3.2
Boundedness of zeta function through transfer operator

3.2.1
Preliminaries on trace class operators

We recall some preliminaries on trace class operators. See
[
DZ19
, Appendix B]
for more details.

Let
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}
be a compact operator between two Hilbert spaces. The singular values
\mu_{\ell}
of
\mathcal{A}
are defined to be the eigenvalues
\lambda_{\ell}
of
(\mathcal{A}^{*}\mathcal{A})^{1/2}
in decreasing order, i.e.

\mu_{\ell}(\mathcal{A}):=\lambda_{\ell}((\mathcal{A}^{*}\mathcal{A})^{1/2}),\quad\ell=0,1,2,\cdots.

We recall the minimax property of singular values:

\mu_{\ell}(\mathcal{A})=\min\limits_{\mathrm{codim}(V,\mathcal{H}_{1})=\ell}\max\limits_{v\in V\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\mathcal{A}v\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}}{\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}}.

(3.8)

This follows directly from the minimax property for self-adjoint operators since
\|\mathcal{A}v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}=(\mathcal{A}^{*}\mathcal{A}v,v)_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}
. As a corollary, for any compact operators
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}
and
\mathcal{B}:\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}
, we have the inequality

\mu_{\ell}(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B})\leqslant\mu_{\ell_{1}}(\mathcal{A})+\mu_{\ell_{2}}(\mathcal{B}),\quad\forall\ \ell=\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}.

(3.9)

Definition 3.8
.

A compact operator
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}
between two Hilbert spaces is a trace class operator if

\|\mathcal{A}\|_{1}:=\sum\limits_{\ell}\mu_{\ell}(\mathcal{A})<\infty.

\|\cdot\|_{1}
is called the trace norm.

For a trace class operator
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
on a single space, one can define the trace by

\Tr(\mathcal{A}):=\sum\limits_{j}\langle\mathcal{A}e_{j},e_{j}\rangle

(3.10)

where
\{e_{j}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
. One can verify that the definition does not depend on the choice of
\{e_{j}\}
. By Lidskii’s Theorem
[
DZ19
, Proposition B.31]
), we have

\Tr(\mathcal{A})=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}(\mathcal{A}).

The Fredholm determinant is defined by

\det(1-\mathcal{A}):=\prod\limits_{\ell}(1-\lambda_{\ell}(\mathcal{A})).

We have the estimates

|\Tr(\mathcal{A})|\leqslant\sum\limits_{\ell}|\lambda_{\ell}(\mathcal{A})|\leqslant\|\mathcal{A}\|_{1},\quad|\det(1-\mathcal{A})|\leqslant\prod\limits_{\ell}(1+\mu_{\ell}(\mathcal{A}))\leqslant e^{\|\mathcal{A}\|_{1}}.

(3.11)

For a trace class operator
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}
and a bounded operator
\mathcal{B}:\mathcal{H}_{2}\to\mathcal{H}_{3}
, it follows from the minimax property that
\mu_{\ell}(\mathcal{B}\mathcal{A})\leqslant\|\mathcal{B}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}\to\mathcal{H}_{3}}\mu_{\ell}(\mathcal{A})
and thus

\|\mathcal{B}\mathcal{A}\|_{1}\leqslant\|\mathcal{B}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}\to\mathcal{H}_{3}}\|\mathcal{A}\|_{1}.

(3.12)

Moreover, it is shown in
[
DZ19
, Eq (B.4.9)]
that for a trace class operator
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}
and and a bounded operator
\mathcal{B}:\mathcal{H}_{2}\to\mathcal{H}_{1}
,

\Tr(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B})=\Tr(\mathcal{B}\mathcal{A}).

(3.13)

3.2.2
Transfer operator:
\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
case

To estimate the Selberg zeta function in the region where the infinite product expression does not converge, we employ the transfer operator
\mathcal{L}_{s}
. The observation is that the transfer operator
\mathcal{L}_{s}
is a trace class operator acting on the space of holomorphic functions with bounded
L^{2}
norm, and its Fredholm determinant gives the Selberg zeta function.

We first consider the case for a Schottky group
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
of rank
g\geqslant 2
.
Write
\Gamma=\langle\mathfrak{a}_{1},\mathfrak{a}_{2},\cdots,\mathfrak{a}_{{2g}}\rangle
with
\mathfrak{a}_{i}\mathfrak{a}_{i+g}=id
for
i=1,2,\cdots,g
.
By conjugating
\Gamma
if necessary, there exist open discs
D_{\mathfrak{a}_{1}},D_{\mathfrak{a}_{2}},\cdots,D_{\mathfrak{a}_{2g}}
in
\mathbb{C}
centered in
{\mathbb{R}}
such that their closures are disjoint, and for each
\mathfrak{a}\in\{\mathfrak{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{2g}\}
, we have
\mathfrak{a}(\hat{{\mathbb{C}}}-\overline{D_{{\mathfrak{a}}^{-1}}})=D_{\mathfrak{a}}
and
\mathfrak{a}(\hat{{\mathbb{R}}}-I_{\mathfrak{a}^{-1}})=I_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\circ}
, where
I_{\mathfrak{a}}:=\overline{D_{\mathfrak{a}}}\cap{\mathbb{R}}
.

We define the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}:=\oplus_{i=1}^{2g}\mathcal{H}(D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i}})
, where
\mathcal{H}(D_{\mathfrak{a}})=\{u\in L^{2}(D_{\mathfrak{a}}):u\text{ is holomorphic}\}
is equipped with the norm
\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}(D_{\mathfrak{a}})}^{2}:=|D_{\mathfrak{a}}|^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{2}(D_{\mathfrak{a}})}^{2}
. The transfer operator
\mathcal{L}_{s}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is defined as

\mathcal{L}_{s}u(w):=\sum\limits_{\mathfrak{a}_{i}\neq\mathfrak{b}^{-1}}\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{\prime}(w)^{s}u(\mathfrak{a}_{i}(w))\quad\text{for}\quad w\in D_{\mathfrak{b}}\quad\text{with}\quad\mathfrak{b}\in\{\mathfrak{a}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{a}_{2g}\}.

(3.14)

Here note that the derivative satisfies
\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{\prime}(w)>0
for
w\in D_{\mathfrak{b}}\cap{\mathbb{R}}
, and hence we define
\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{\prime}(w)^{s}:=e^{s\log\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{\prime}(w)}
where we
choose the branch of complex logarithm so that if
s\in\mathbb{R}
, then
\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{\prime}(w)^{s}
is the unique holomorphic function on
D_{\mathfrak{b}}
satisfying
\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{\prime}(w)^{s}>0
for
w\in D_{\mathfrak{b}}\cap{\mathbb{R}}
.

We introduce a few more notations. We use
\mathcal{W}^{n}
to denote words of length
n
with respect to the generators
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{a}_{2g}
, i.e. words of the form
\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}
with
\mathfrak{a}_{i_{j+1}}\neq\mathfrak{a}_{i_{j}}^{-1}
. For
\mathbf{a}=\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}\in\mathcal{W}^{n}
, we denote

{\mathbf{a}}(w)={\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}}({\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}}(\cdots{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}}(w)))

and
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n-1}}
.

For a disc
D
of radius
R
, and a real number
\rho>0
, we use
\rho D
to denote the disc with the same center and of radius
\rho R
.

The following lemma shows that
\mathcal{L}_{s}
is a trace class operator (see also
[
Bor16
, Lemma 15.7]
).

Lemma 3.9
.

Suppose
\gamma:D_{\mathfrak{b}}\to\rho D_{\mathfrak{a}}
is a holomorphic function between two discs with
0<\rho<1
and
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\in\{\mathfrak{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{2g}\}
, then the pullback operator

\gamma^{*}u(w)=u(\gamma(w)):\mathcal{H}(D_{\mathfrak{a}})\to\mathcal{H}(D_{\mathfrak{b}})

is a trace class operator satisfying

\mu_{\ell}(\gamma^{*})\leqslant C(1-\rho)^{-2}\rho^{\ell}\sqrt{\ell+1}.

Consequently, for any
s\in\mathbb{C}
,
\mathcal{L}_{s}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is a trace class operator.

Proof.

We may assume
D_{\mathfrak{a}}
is centered at
0
and take an orthonormal basis
\phi_{n}(w):=\sqrt{n+1}\left(\frac{w}{R}\right)^{n}
of
\mathcal{H}(D_{\mathfrak{a}})
where
R
is the radius of
D_{\mathfrak{a}}
. Then

\|\gamma^{*}\phi_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}(D_{\mathfrak{b}})}^{2}=|D_{\mathfrak{b}}|^{-1}\int_{D_{\mathfrak{b}}}(n+1)\frac{|\gamma(w)|^{2n}}{R^{2n}}dm(w)\leqslant(n+1)\rho^{2n}.

By the minimax property
Eq.

3.8
, we conclude

\mu_{\ell}(\gamma^{*})\leqslant\sum\limits_{n\geqslant\ell}\|\gamma^{*}\phi_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}(D_{\mathfrak{b}})}\leqslant\sum\limits_{n\geqslant\ell}\sqrt{n+1}\rho^{n}\leqslant C(1-\rho)^{-2}\rho^{\ell}\sqrt{\ell+1}.

Thus

\sum\limits_{\ell}\mu_{\ell}(\gamma^{*})\leqslant C(1-\rho)^{-2}\sum\limits_{\ell}\rho^{\ell}\sqrt{\ell+1}<\infty

which implies that
\gamma^{*}
is a trace class operator. Since
\mathcal{L}_{s}
is a finite direct sum of such pullback operators composed with multiplication by holomorphic functions, we conclude
\mathcal{L}_{s}
is also a trace class operator using
Eq.

3.9
and
Eq.

3.12
.
∎

Since
\mathcal{L}_{s}
is a trace class operator, we can define its Fredholm determinant. We claim
\det(1-\mathcal{L}_{s})
is exactly the Selberg zeta function, see
[
Bor16
, Lemma 15.10]
. Here we include a proof for completeness but we will omit the proof later for similar cases. Note since
\mathcal{L}_{s}
is in trace class and depends holomorphically on
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
\det(1-\mathcal{L}_{s})
is an entire function. So this gives an alternative proof of holomorphic extension of the Selberg zeta function.

Lemma 3.10
.

For
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
, we have

\det(1-\mathcal{L}_{s})=Z(\Gamma,s).

(3.15)

Proof.

Since both sides are entire functions on
{\mathbb{C}}
, it suffices to check the inequality for
\Re s\gg 1
.
For
\Re s\gg 1
, we have

\det(1-\mathcal{L}_{s})=\exp\left(-\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\Tr(\mathcal{L}_{s}^{n})\right).

On the other hand,

Z(\Gamma,s)=\exp\left(-\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{[\gamma],k}e^{-n(s+k)\ell(\gamma)}\right).

We again consider the orthonormal basis
\phi_{k}^{\mathfrak{a}}(w)=\sqrt{k+1}\left(\frac{w-w_{\mathfrak{a}}}{R_{\mathfrak{a}}}\right)^{k}
where
w_{\mathfrak{a}}
is the center of
D_{\mathfrak{a}}
and
R_{\mathfrak{a}}
is the radius of
D_{\mathfrak{a}}
.
We write

\mathcal{L}_{s}=\sum\limits_{\mathfrak{a}\neq\mathfrak{b}^{-1}\in\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{L}_{s,\mathfrak{ab}}\quad\text{with}\quad\mathcal{L}_{s,\mathfrak{ab}}:\mathcal{H}(D_{\mathfrak{a}})\to\mathcal{H}({D_{\mathfrak{b}}})\quad\text{given by}\quad\mathcal{L}_{s,\mathfrak{ab}}u(w)={\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}(w)^{s}u({\mathfrak{a}}(w))\quad\text{for}\,\,w\in{D_{\mathfrak{b}}}.

We have

\mathcal{L}_{s}^{n}=\sum\limits_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n+1}}\in\mathcal{W}^{n+1}}\mathcal{L}_{s,\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n+1}}}\quad\text{with}\quad\mathcal{L}_{s,\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n+1}}}:=\mathcal{L}_{s,\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n+1}}}\cdots\mathcal{L}_{s,\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}\mathfrak{a}_{i_{3}}}\mathcal{L}_{s,\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}}.

The Schwartz kernel of
\mathcal{L}_{s,\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n+1}}}
is given by

\mathcal{L}_{s,\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n+1}}}(w,w^{\prime})=\sum\limits_{k}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}})^{\prime}(w)^{s}\phi_{k}^{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}(w))\overline{\phi_{k}^{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}}(w^{\prime})},\quad w\in D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n+1}}},\,w^{\prime}\in D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}}.

Its restriction to the diagonal is nonzero if and only if
\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n+1}}=\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}
. Set
\gamma=\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{s,\gamma}:=\mathcal{L}_{s,\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n+1}}}
. So

\mathcal{L}_{s,\gamma}u(w)=\gamma^{\prime}(w)^{s}u(\gamma(w))
.
A change of variables
w=gw^{\prime}
gives the operator
(g)^{*}\circ\mathcal{L}_{s,\gamma}\circ(g^{-1})^{*}:\mathcal{H}(g^{-1}D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}})\to\mathcal{H}(g^{-1}D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n+1}}})
given by

(g)^{*}\circ\mathcal{L}_{s,\gamma}\circ(g^{-1})^{*}u(w^{\prime})=\gamma^{\prime}(gw^{\prime})^{s}u(g^{-1}\gamma gw^{\prime}).

Let
\gamma_{1}=g^{-1}\gamma g
, and
\mathcal{L}_{s,\gamma_{1}}:\mathcal{H}(g^{-1}D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}})\to\mathcal{H}(g^{-1}D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n+1}}})
given by
\mathcal{L}_{s,\gamma_{1}}u(w^{\prime})=\gamma_{1}^{\prime}(w)^{s}u(\gamma_{1}w^{\prime})
. Then

(g)^{*}\circ\mathcal{L}_{s,\gamma}\circ(g^{-1})^{*}u(w^{\prime})=\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{s,\gamma_{1}}\mathcal{B}\quad\text{with}\quad\mathcal{B}u(w^{\prime})=(g^{\prime}(w^{\prime}))^{s}u(w^{\prime}).

Therefore, by
Eq.

3.13
,
\Tr\mathcal{L}_{s,\gamma}=\Tr\mathcal{L}_{s,\gamma_{1}}
. So we may choose the coordinate
so that
\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}(w)=e^{-\ell(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}})}w
, then

(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}})^{\prime}(w)^{s}=e^{-s\ell(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}})}.

We may assume the center of
D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}}
is
0
. Applying the definition of trace
Eq.

3.10
to the basis
\phi_{k}^{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}}(w)
, we obtain

\Tr\mathcal{L}_{s,\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n+1}}}=\frac{e^{-s\ell(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}})}}{\pi R_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}}^{2}}\sum\limits_{k}(k+1)\int_{|w|\leqslant R_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}}}\left(\frac{e^{-\ell(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}})}w}{R_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}}}\right)^{k}\left(\frac{\bar{w}}{R_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}}}\right)^{k}dm(w)=\sum\limits_{k}e^{-(s+k)\ell(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}})}.

Suppose the closed loop
\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}=\mathbf{a}^{m}
where
\mathbf{a}
is primitive. Then

\sum\limits_{n}\frac{1}{n}\Tr(\mathcal{L}_{s}^{n})=\sum\limits_{m}\frac{1}{m}\sum\limits_{[\mathbf{a}]\in\mathcal{P},k}e^{-m(s+k)\ell({\mathbf{a}})}.

The proof is complete.
∎

For further applications, we also introduce modified transfer operators where we only use discs with word length
\geqslant N
. For
\mathbf{a}=\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}\in\mathcal{W}^{n}
, let

D_{\mathbf{a}}:=\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n-1}}(D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}}).

We define the modified transfer operator on
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{N}:=\oplus_{\mathbf{b}\in\mathcal{W}^{N}}\mathcal{H}(D_{\mathbf{b}})
:

\mathcal{L}_{s,N}u(w):=\sum\limits_{\mathfrak{a}\neq\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}^{-1}}{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}(w)^{s}u({\mathfrak{a}}(w))\quad\text{for}\quad w\in D_{\mathbf{b}}\quad\text{with}\quad\mathbf{b}=\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N}}.

(3.16)

When
N=1
, it agrees with the previous definition
Eq.

3.14
. When
N\geqslant 2
, the modified transfer operator acts on a space of functions with a larger domain. We can still use the proof of
Lemma

3.10
to show that
for any
N\geqslant 1
,

\det(1-\mathcal{L}_{s,N})=Z(\Gamma,s).

In the computation, we need to use fixed points. By taking larger
N
, the fixed point of one loxodromic element is still contained in only one disc. The computations are the same.

Now we can state a much more precise estimate for the transfer operator.

Proposition 3.11
.

Suppose
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
is a Schottky group with the notations above. Suppose for some
A,B>0
,
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
\varphi\in[0,\pi]
,
we have for any
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{W}
and
\mathbf{b}=\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N}}\in\mathcal{W}^{N}
with
\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\neq\mathfrak{a}^{-1}
,

•

e^{-A}\leqslant|\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}(w)|\leqslant e^{A}
and
|\arg\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}(w)|\leqslant\varphi\leqslant\pi
for any

w\in D_{\mathbf{b}}
;

•

\mathfrak{a}(D_{\mathbf{b}})\subset e^{-B}D_{\mathbf{a}}
with
\mathbf{a}=\mathfrak{a}\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
.

Then the singular values of
\mathcal{L}_{s,N}
satisfy

\mu_{\ell}(\mathcal{L}_{s,N})\leqslant C(g,N,B)e^{(A|\Re s|+\varphi|\Im s|-B\ell)/C(g,N)}\sqrt{\ell+1}.

(3.17)

Proof.

The transfer operator
\mathcal{L}_{s,N}
is the direct sum of operators

\mathcal{L}_{s,\mathbf{ab}}:\mathcal{H}(D_{\mathbf{a}})\to\mathcal{H}(D_{\mathbf{b}})\quad\text{given by}\quad\mathcal{L}_{s,\mathbf{ab}}u(w)={\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}(w)^{s}u({\mathfrak{a}}(w)),

where
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{W}
and
\mathbf{b}=\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N}}\in\mathcal{W}^{N}
with
\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\neq\mathfrak{a}^{-1}
and
\mathbf{a}=\mathfrak{a}\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
.
We have

•

|{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}(w)^{s}|\leqslant\max(|{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}(w)|^{\Re s},|{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}(w)|^{-\Re s})|\exp({|\arg{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}(w)||\Im s|})\leqslant e^{A|\Re s|+\varphi|\Im s|}
;

•

By
Lemma

3.9
, the singular values of the pullback operator
\mathfrak{a}^{*}:\mathcal{H}(D_{\mathbf{a}})\to\mathcal{H}(D_{\mathbf{b}})
satisfy

\mu_{\ell}({\mathfrak{a}}^{*})\leqslant C(1-e^{-B})^{-2}e^{-B\ell}\sqrt{\ell+1}.

Then the proposition follows from
Eq.

3.9
and
Eq.

3.12
.
∎

Recall from
Lemma

2.23
, for a family of Schottky groups satisfying condition
(\bigstar)
, we have a uniform Schottky basis.

Proposition 3.12
.

Suppose we have a degenerating family of Schottky groups
\Gamma_{z}<\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
satisfying
(\bigstar)
with their Schottky discs uniformly bounded away from
\infty
. For any
N\geqslant 1
,
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{W}
,

\mathbf{b}=\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N}}\in\mathcal{W}^{N}
with
\mathfrak{a}\neq\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}^{-1}
, and sufficiently small
0<|z|\ll_{N}1
, we have for any

w\in D_{\mathbf{b},z}
, and the disc
D_{\mathbf{a},z}
with

\mathbf{a}=\mathfrak{a}\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle|\log|{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}(w)||\lesssim_{N}\log(1/|z|),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}(D_{\mathbf{b},z})\subset\frac{1}{10}D_{\mathbf{a},z}

(3.18)

in terms of the absolute norm. Moreover,
for any
M>0
, there exists
N(M)\geqslant 1
such that if we require
\mathbf{b}\in\mathcal{W}^{N(M)}
additionally, then

\quad|\arg\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}(w)|\lesssim_{M}|z|^{M}.

Consequently, given any
C,M>0
, the family of zeta functions

Z(\Gamma_{z},s/\log(1/|z|))
with
0<|z|<1/e

is uniformly bounded (depending on
C
and
M
) in the region

|\Re s|\leqslant C,\quad|\Im s|\leqslant C|z|^{-M}\log(1/|z|).

Proof.

By assumption,
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{M}(\mathbb{D}))
, so we can write
\mathfrak{a}=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
with
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{M}(\mathbb{D})
. Fix any
0<|z|\ll 1
. The uniform separation
\mathfrak{a}(D_{\mathbf{b},z})\subset\frac{1}{10}D_{\mathbf{a},z}
follows from
Corollary

3.23
.

For any

|\rho_{i}-\rho_{j}|<C\epsilon^{1/d}
or
|\rho_{i}-\rho_{j}|>4C\epsilon^{1/d}
.
Let
D_{1}=D(\rho_{1},C\epsilon^{1/d})
. Choose a zero
\rho_{j_{2}}\notin D_{1}
, and let
D_{2}=D(\rho_{j_{2}},C\epsilon^{1/d})
. Using the inequality
|\rho_{1}-\rho_{j_{2}}|>4C\epsilon^{1/d}
, we obtain

2D_{1}\cap 2D_{2}=\varnothing
. We repeat this process finitely many times to obtain the desired family of discs.

The fact
\epsilon\leqslant 4^{-d^{3}-10d}\epsilon_{0}^{d}
ensures
2D_{\ell}\subset D(\rho_{*},2\epsilon_{0})
. Note

|h(s)|\leqslant\epsilon<(C\epsilon^{1/d})^{d}\leqslant|g(s)|\quad\text{for}\quad s\in\partial(2D_{\ell}).

By Rouché’s theorem,
g(s)+h(s)
and
g(s)
have the same number of zeros in
2D_{\ell}
, and the proof is complete. ∎

By Rouché’s theorem, it suffices to show

for

sufficiently small.
∎

5
Analytic extension of intermediate zeta functions

Recall the intermediate zeta functions introduced in
Eq.

4.8
:

Z_{M}(\Gamma,z,s)=\prod\limits_{[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}}(1-e^{-s\ell_{M}(\gamma,z)})\quad\text{for}\quad\Re s\gg 1.

(5.1)

The goal of this section is to show that
Z_{M}(\Gamma,z,s)
admits an analytic extension to
\mathbb{C}
.

This can be seen as a generalization of the analytic continuation of the Ihara zeta function. We need to find good combinatorial relations between
\ell_{M}(\gamma,t)
’s and then obtain a determinant formula similar to the Ihara zeta function.

We will first review the proof of the determinant formula for a weighted version of the Ihara zeta function. Then, we use derivative cocycles from the Schottky group to get a good combinatorial relation and apply the determinant formula.

5.1
Weighted Ihara zeta function

We briefly review the proof of analytic continuation of the weighted Ihara zeta function. For more details, see, for example,
[
HST06
, Section 3]
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph, where
E=\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{2J}\}
is the set of oriented edges of
G
with
e_{j}
and
e_{j+J}
in opposite direction. We write
e_{j+J}=e_{j}^{-1}
to refer to their relation. For each edge
e_{j}
, we associate it a complex number
h_{j}
.
Define
W(s)
to be a
(2J)\times(2J)
-matrix by

W(e_{j},e_{k})(s)=e^{-s(h_{j}+h_{k})/2}

if the endpoint of
e_{j}
is the beginning point of
e_{k}
and
e_{j}\neq e_{k}^{-1}
, and
W(e_{j},e_{k})(s)=0
otherwise.

For a non-backtracking loop
P=(e_{i_{1}},\cdots,e_{i_{n}})
, define

\ell_{h}(P)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}h_{i_{j}}
.
The weighted Ihara zeta function is defined as a product over primitive loops
P
:

Z_{I}(G,h,s)=\prod\limits_{[P]\in\mathcal{P}}(1-e^{-s\ell_{h}(P)}).

Recall that we use
P
to denote a non-backtracking loop with a beginning point at a vertex, and
[P]
to denote a loop forgetting the starting point. For
s
with large real part, the product is absolute convergent. The following
Proposition

5.1
gives the analytic extension of
Z_{I}(G,h,s)
to the entire complex plane.

Proposition 5.1
.

For
\Re s\gg 1
, we have
Z_{I}(G,h,s)=\det(I-W(s))
.

Proof.

We expand
\log Z_{I}(G,h,s)
:

\begin{aligned} \log Z_{I}(G,h,s)&=-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{[P]\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{j}e^{-js\ell_{h}(P)}\\
&=-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{j\#(P)}e^{-js\ell_{h}(P)}\\
&=-\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}\\
j:j\#(P)=n\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}e^{-js\ell_{h}(P)}\end{aligned},

where the sum
\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}
is over primitive non-backtracking loops
P
and
\#(P)
is the number of edges in the primitive loop
P
.

We can also expand
\log\det(I-W(s))
:

\displaystyle\log\det(I-W(s))

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{tr}W(s)^{n}.

We finish the proof by using the equality
\mathrm{tr}W(s)^{n}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}\\
j:j\#(P)=n\end{subarray}}e^{-js\ell_{h}(P)}
.
∎

5.2
M
th-expansion of lengths and derivative cocycles

In this section we prove the intermediate zeta function
Z_{M}(\Gamma,z,s)
has an analytic extension and is computable for concrete examples. We will use the geometry of Schottky basis. Throughout this section, we fix generators
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{a}_{g}
and a Schottky basis
D_{\mathfrak{a}_{j}}
satisfying
Proposition

3.20
.

For computational convenience, we will moreover assume
D_{\mathfrak{a}_{j}}
are contained in
D(0,C)
, i.e. uniformly bounded away from
\infty
. This is always possible by choosing an appropriate coordinate.

The key observation is that the leading terms of the derivative is locally constant, from which we can compute the intermediate zeta function.

Consider pairs of the form
(U,f)
where
U
is an open neighborhood of
0
, and
f
is meromoprhic on
U
and holomorphic on
U\backslash\{0\}
. Two such pairs
(U,f)
and
(V,g)
are equivalent if there exists an open neighborhood
W
of
0
, contained in
U\cap V
, such that
f|_{W}=g|_{W}
. A germ of meromorphic functions at
0
is an equivalence class of such a pair, and it has a representative that is a convergent complex Laurent series about
0
,
t^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}t^{j}
with
n\in\mathbb{Z},a_{0}\neq 0
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{0}
be the ring of germs of meromorphic functions at
0
, which is isomorphic to the ring of all convergent complex Laurent series about
0
. The ring
\mathcal{O}_{0}
of germs of holomorphic functions at
0
is defined analogously, which is isomorphic to the ring of all convergent complex power series about
0
.

Fix any
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
. We introduce several combinatorial operations.

•

For
a=t^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}t^{j}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
with
n\in{\mathbb{Z}},a_{0}\neq 0
, we let
\mathrm{lt}_{M}(a)
be the first
M
leading terms of
a
:

\mathrm{lt}_{M}(a):=t^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{M}a_{j}t^{j}.

•

We define the formal expansion of logarithm
\mathrm{plog}:\mathcal{M}_{0}\to\mathbb{Z}\log(1/t)\oplus(\mathcal{O}_{0}/{2\pi i\mathbb{Z}})
by mapping

a=a_{0}t^{n}(1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}t^{j})\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
with
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
a_{0}\neq 0
to

\mathrm{plog}(a):=-n\log(1/t)+\log a_{0}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{m}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}t^{j}\right)^{m}.

(5.2)

Here
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{m}(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}t^{j})^{m}
is the power series expansion of the holomorphic function
\log(1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}t^{j})
about
0
, which is uniquely determined.

•

We define
\mathrm{lt}^{\prime}_{M}:\mathbb{Z}\log(1/t)\oplus(\mathcal{O}_{0}/2\pi i\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{Z}\log(1/t)\oplus(\mathcal{O}_{0}/2\pi i\mathbb{Z})
by mapping

a=n{\log(1/t)}+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}t^{j}
to its first
M
leading terms

\mathrm{lt}^{\prime}_{M}(a):=n\log(1/t)+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{M}a_{j}t^{j}.

And we define
\mathrm{lt}^{\prime}_{-1}:\mathbb{Z}\log(1/t)\oplus(\mathcal{O}_{0}/2\pi i\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{Z}\log(1/t)
by mapping

a=n{\log(1/t)}+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}t^{j}
to its
(-1)
-leading term

\mathrm{lt}_{-1}^{\prime}(a):=n\log(1/t).

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
, and for
x,y\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{lt}^{\prime}_{M}\circ\mathrm{plog}\circ\mathrm{lt}_{M}(x)=\mathrm{lt}^{\prime}_{M}\circ\mathrm{plog}(x),

(5.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{lt}^{\prime}_{M}\circ\mathrm{plog}\circ\mathrm{lt}_{M}(xy)=\mathrm{lt}^{\prime}_{M}\circ\mathrm{plog}(x)+\mathrm{lt}^{\prime}_{M}\circ\mathrm{plog}(y).

Proof.

We recall the definition of
\mathrm{plog}

Eq.

5.2
. The element
x\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
can be written uniquely as
x=x_{0}(1+x_{1})
with
x_{1}=\sum_{j\geqslant 1}b_{j}t^{j},\ x_{0}=b_{0}t^{n}
for some
b_{0}\neq 0,\ n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

\mathrm{plog}x=-n\log(1/t)+\log b_{0}+(x_{1}-x_{1}^{2}/2+x_{1}^{3}/3-\cdots)
. From this expansion, we see that
\mathrm{lt}^{\prime}_{M}\circ\mathrm{plog}(x)
is determined by
x_{0}
and the first
M-1
term of
x_{1}
, and hence determined by
\mathrm{lt}_{M}(x)
. This completes the proof of the first equality.

The second equality uses the observation that for two convergent power series
\sum_{j\geqslant 1}a_{j}t^{j},\,\sum_{l\geqslant 1}b_{l}t^{l}
, we have
\log((1+\sum_{j\geqslant 1}a_{j}t^{j})(1+\sum_{l\geqslant 1}b_{l}t^{l}))=\log(1+\sum_{j\geqslant 1}a_{j}t^{j})+\log(1+\sum_{l\geqslant l}b_{l}t^{l})
in an open neighborhood of
0
.
∎

In this subsection, it suffices to consider the action of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!))
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)}
, the set of classical points.
For
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!))
and
x\neq\gamma^{-1}\infty\in\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
, the derivative of
\gamma
at
x
is given by

\gamma^{\prime}x=1/(cx+d)^{2}.

Lemma 5.3
.

Fix
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
. For
any
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!))
with
\gamma\infty\neq\infty
and
x,y\in\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)\backslash\{\gamma^{-1}\infty\}
with
|x-y|_{na}<e^{-M}|x-\gamma^{-1}\infty|_{na}
, we have

\mathrm{lt}_{M}(\gamma^{\prime}x)=\mathrm{lt}_{M}(\gamma^{\prime}y).

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove that
|\gamma^{\prime}(x)-\gamma^{\prime}(y)|_{na}<e^{-M}|\gamma^{\prime}(x)|_{na}
, which is equivalent to
\mathrm{lt}_{M}(\gamma^{\prime}x)=\mathrm{lt}_{M}(\gamma^{\prime}y)
.

We have

\gamma^{\prime}x-\gamma^{\prime}y=\frac{1}{(cx+d)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(cy+d)^{2}}=\frac{c(y-x)(c(x+y)+2d)}{(cx+d)^{2}(cy+d)^{2}}.

Due to
\gamma\infty\neq\infty
, we know
c\neq 0
. Therefore

\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}x-\gamma^{\prime}y|_{na}}{|\gamma^{\prime}x|_{na}}=\left|\frac{c(x-y)(c(x+y)+2d)}{(cy+d)^{2}}\right|_{na}=\left|\frac{(x-y)(c(x+y)+2d)}{(y+d/c)(cy+d)}\right|_{na}.

Due to
|x-y|_{na}<e^{-M}|x-\gamma^{-1}\infty|_{na}
, we have that

|y+d/c|_{na}=|y-\gamma^{-1}\infty|_{na}=|x-\gamma^{-1}\infty|_{na}>e^{M}|x-y|_{na}

and
|cx+d+cy+d|_{na}\leqslant\max\{|cx+d|_{na},|cy+d|_{na}\}=|cy+d|_{na}
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Let
\Gamma
be a Schottky group in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!))
. For
\gamma\in\Gamma\backslash\{id\}
, let
\lambda_{1}(\gamma),\ \lambda_{2}(\gamma)\in\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
be its eigenvalues with
|\lambda_{1}(\gamma)|_{na}>|\lambda_{2}(\gamma)|_{na}
. Then there exists
\eta>0
depending on the group
\Gamma
, such that for any
0<|z|<\eta
, the evaluation of
\lambda_{1}(\gamma)
at
z
satisfies
\lambda_{1}(\gamma)(z)=\lambda_{1}(\gamma_{z})
. Hence,
\lambda_{1}(\gamma)
is a meromorphic function at
0
(see the proof of
Proposition

4.2
). Using
4.7
and the definition of
\mathrm{plog}
, we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

There exists a constant
C>0
that depends only on
\Gamma
such that for any
\gamma\in\Gamma\backslash\{id\}
, we have for any
0<|z|<e^{-C\ell^{na}(\gamma)}
,

2\ \Re\left(\mathrm{plog}(\lambda_{1}(\gamma))(z)\right)=\ell^{na}(\gamma)\log(1/|z|)+\Re\left(\sum_{j\geqslant 0}a_{j}(\gamma)z^{j}\right),

where
\mathrm{plog}(\lambda_{1}(\gamma))(z)
is to evaluate the series
\mathrm{plog}(\lambda_{1}(\gamma))
at
z
, and

a_{j}(\gamma)
’s are the complex coefficients given in
Eq.

4.1
.

For each
\gamma\in\Gamma\backslash\{id\}
, we define

L_{M}(\gamma):=\mathrm{lt}_{M}^{\prime}(2\ \mathrm{plog}(\lambda_{1}(\gamma))).

Then we have that for any
z\in\mathbb{D}^{*}
, the evaluation of
L_{M}(\gamma)
at
z
satisfies

\Re\left(L_{M}(\gamma)(z)\right)=\ell_{M}(\gamma,z)\log(1/|z|)

(5.4)

where
\ell_{M}(\gamma,z)
is defined in
Eq.

4.8
, and hence we obtain
\mathrm{lt}^{\prime}_{-1}(L_{M}(\gamma))=\ell^{na}(\gamma)\log(1/t)
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Fix any
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
. There exists
C>0
depending only on
\Gamma

such that for
N>C(M+1)
, we have for any reduced word
\gamma=\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N}}
,

\mathrm{lt}_{M}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}^{\prime}(x))=\mathrm{lt}_{M}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}^{\prime}(y))\quad\text{for any}\quad x,y\in\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)\cap D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N}}}.

Denote the common value by
\mathrm{lt}_{M}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N}}))
. Moreover, for any cyclically reduced word
\gamma=\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}
with
n>N
, we have

-L_{M}(\gamma)=l_{M}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}},(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N}}))+l_{M}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}},(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{3}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N+1}}))+\cdots+l_{M}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}},(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n+1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n+N-1}})),

(5.5)

where
i_{k+j}:=i_{k+j-n}
when
k+j>n
, and

l_{M}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{k}},(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{k+1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{k+N-1}})):=\mathrm{lt}^{\prime}_{M}\circ\mathrm{plog}\circ\mathrm{lt}_{M}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{k}}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{k+1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{k+N-1}})).

Remark 5.6
.

An easy consequence of
Eq.

5.5
is that

\mathrm{lt}^{\prime}_{-1}(-L_{M}(\gamma))=\mathrm{lt}^{\prime}_{-1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}l_{M}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{k}},(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{k+1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{k+N-1}}))\right)=-\ell^{na}(\gamma)\log(1/t),

which is useful for the holomorphic extension of the Ihara zeta function.

Remark 5.7
.

Proposition

5.5
gives another proof of the lower of the leading coefficients. In general, it is hard to get a lower bound estimate of the leading coefficients. Similar questions in Archimedean case are important and hard. In the case of Schottky groups, the geometry and the hyperbolicity enable us to obtain such lower bound.

Proof of
Proposition

5.5
.

By assumption,
\infty
is not contained in any Schottky discs. Due to the properties of the Schottky group, there exists
c_{0}
such that we have a lower bound

d_{na}(\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{-1}\infty,(D_{\overline{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}})^{c})\geqslant c_{0}

for all generators
\mathfrak{a}_{i}
. Moreover, the size of Schottky discs
D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N}}}
tends uniformly to zero as the word length
N
goes to infinity (
Lemma

3.3
for non-Archimedean norm). Therefore, we can find
N
such that the radius of any disc
D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N-1}}}
is less than
e^{-M}c_{0}
. By
Lemma

3.3
, we can take
N>{C(M+1)}
for a uniform constant
C
depending only on
\Gamma
.

For any cyclically reduced word
\gamma=\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}
with
n>N
, recall
\lambda_{1}(\gamma),\lambda_{2}(\gamma)\in\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
are its eigenvalues with
|\lambda_{1}(\gamma)|_{na}>|\lambda_{2}(\gamma)|_{na}
, and let

\gamma_{+}
be its attracting fixed point, which belongs to
\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)}
. Then we have that

\gamma^{\prime}(\gamma_{+})=\lambda_{2}(\gamma)^{2}
. It follows from
Lemma

5.4
that

\displaystyle-{L_{M}(\gamma)}=\mathrm{lt}_{M}^{\prime}\circ\mathrm{plog}(\gamma^{\prime}(\gamma_{+}))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{lt}_{M}^{\prime}\circ\mathrm{plog}\circ\mathrm{lt}_{M}(\gamma^{\prime}(\gamma_{+}))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{lt}_{M}^{\prime}\circ\mathrm{plog}\circ\mathrm{lt}_{M}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}\gamma_{+})\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}^{\prime}(\gamma_{+}))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{lt}_{M}^{\prime}\circ\mathrm{plog}\circ\mathrm{lt}_{M}\left(\mathrm{lt}_{M}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}\gamma_{+}))\cdots\mathrm{lt}_{M}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}^{\prime}(\gamma_{+}))\right),

where for the last equality we use the fact that for any
x,y\in\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
,

\mathrm{lt}_{M}(xy)=\mathrm{lt}_{M}(\mathrm{lt}_{M}(x)\mathrm{lt}_{M}(y)).

Since
\gamma
is cyclically reduced, the attracting fixed point
\gamma_{+}
is in the disc
D_{\gamma}\subset D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N-1}}}
, and
\gamma^{-1}\infty\in D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}^{-1}}
. Due to the choice of
N
and
Lemma

5.3
, we obtain for any
y\in D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N-1}}}\cap\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
,

\mathrm{lt}_{M}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}^{\prime}(\gamma_{+}))=\mathrm{lt}_{M}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}^{\prime}(y))

For any
\mathfrak{a}_{i_{j}}
, we have
\mathfrak{a}_{i_{j+1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}\gamma_{+}\in\mathfrak{a}_{i_{j+1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}D_{\gamma}\subset D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{j+1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}\gamma}\subset D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{j+1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{j+N-1}}}
, and
a_{i_{j}}^{-1}\infty\in D_{a_{i_{j}}}
. Therefore, for any
y\in D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{j+1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{j+N-1}}}\cap\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
,

\mathrm{lt}_{M}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{j}}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{j+1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}\gamma_{+}))=\mathrm{lt}_{M}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{j}}^{\prime}(y)).

In conclusion, we obtain that

-L_{M}(\gamma)=\mathrm{lt}_{M}^{\prime}\circ\mathrm{plog}\circ\mathrm{lt}_{M}\left(\mathrm{lt}_{M}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N}})\cdots\mathrm{lt}_{M}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{n}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{n+N-1}))\right)

The proof is complete by applying
Eq.

5.3
to the right hand side of the above equation.
∎

As a corollary, we obtain the following.

Corollary 5.8
.

For any
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
, the intermediate zeta function
Z_{M}(\Gamma,z,s)
has a holomorphic extension to the entire complex plane
s\in\mathbb{C}
. More precisely, there exists a polynomial
P_{M}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{J})
such that for any
s\in\mathbb{C}
and
0<|z|<1/e
,

Z_{M}(\Gamma,z,s)=P_{M}(e^{s\mu_{1}(z)},\,e^{s\mu_{2}(z)},\cdots,e^{s\mu_{J}(z)})

(5.6)

where for
j=1,2,\cdots,J
,
\mu_{j}(z)
is of the form

\mu_{j}(z)=a_{j}+\frac{1}{\log(1/|z|)}\Re\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{M}a_{j,k}z^{k}\right)\quad\text{with}\,\,a_{j}\in\mathbb{R},\,\,a_{j,k}\in\mathbb{C}\,\,\text{for}\,\,k=0,\ldots,M.

(5.7)

Proof.

For
\gamma\in\Gamma\backslash\{id\}
, recall from
Eq.

5.4
that for
z\in\mathbb{D}^{*}
,
\Re\left(L_{M}(\gamma)(z)\right)=\ell_{M}(\gamma,z)\log(1/|z|)
.

We introduce an edge matrix
W(s)
with coordinates given by reduced words of length
N
, that is, words of form
\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N}}
, where
N>0
is the constant given in
Proposition

5.5
. The entries are given by

W(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N}},\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N+1}})(s)=\exp\left(\frac{s}{2\log(1/|z|)}\Re\left(l_{M}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}},(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N}}))(z)+l_{M}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}},(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{3}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N+1}}))(z)\right)\right),

and the other entries are all equal to zero. Using the definition of
Z_{M}(\Gamma,z,s)
,
Proposition

5.1
, and
Proposition

5.5
, we can show that
\mathrm{det}(I-W(s))
is the holomorphic extension of

Z_{M}(\Gamma,z,s)
to entire complex plane.

It follows from
Proposition

5.5
that for any reduced word

is in the disc
D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N}},z}\subset D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}},z}
, which we assume is bounded uniformly from
\infty
. We conclude that
|a_{z}/c_{z}|\leqslant C
and
c_{z}\neq 0
for
|z|<r
. Thus
\log|f(z)|
is harmonic in the disc
D(0,r)
.
Since
ad-bc=1
, we have

1\leqslant|a_{z}d_{z}|+|b_{z}c_{z}|\leqslant C(|a_{z}|+|b_{z}|)|c_{z}|\leqslant e^{CN}|c_{z}|,\quad\frac{r}{2}\leqslant|z|<r.

From the Laurent series expansion
c=t^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}c_{j}t^{j}\in\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
, we have

e^{-m}\leqslant\|c\|_{\mathrm{hyb}}\leqslant e^{CN},

which implies
m\geqslant-CN
.

By mean value theorem on
D(0,r/2)
, we have

\log|c_{0}|=\log|f(0)|\geqslant\min_{|z|=r/2}\log|f(z)|=\min_{|z|=r/2}\log|c_{z}/z^{m}|\geqslant-CN-m\log(r/2)\geqslant-C^{\prime}N.

In the last step we use
\log(r/2)<0
and
m\geqslant-CN
. Therefore,
|c_{0}|\geqslant e^{-CN}
.
Recall
\|a\|_{\mathrm{hyb}}\leqslant e^{CN}
and
\|c\|_{\mathrm{hyb}}\leqslant e^{CN}
, we have a Laurent series expansion

\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}(\cdots(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N}}(\infty)))=\frac{a}{c}=t^{m^{\prime}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}^{\prime}t^{j}\text{ with }|a_{j}^{\prime}|\leqslant e^{C(N+1)j},

(7.1)

where in the last step we use
Lemma

7.4
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}=\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}
, then
\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}^{\prime}(w)=(c^{\prime}w+d^{\prime})^{-2}
. We would like to show

\mathfrak{a}_{i_{1}}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}(\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N-1}}(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N}}(\infty))))=t^{m}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}t^{j},\quad|a_{j}|\leqslant e^{C(N+1)j}.

By
Lemma

7.3
, it suffices to show a similar expansion for
(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N}}(\infty)+d^{\prime}/c^{\prime})^{-1}
. By
Lemma

7.4
and
Eq.

7.1
, it suffices to show the leading coefficient
\pi_{0}
in the expansion

\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N}}(\infty)+d^{\prime}/c^{\prime}=t^{m}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\pi_{j}t^{j},\quad\pi_{0}\neq 0

satisfies
|\pi_{0}|\geqslant e^{-CN}
.
By
Proposition

3.20
,
\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}(\cdots(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N}}(\infty)))\in\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{3}},z}\subset|z|^{v^{\prime}}D_{\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}},z}
for some
v^{\prime}>0
and
0<|z|<r
. Hence the point
\mathfrak{a}_{i_{2}}(\cdots(\mathfrak{a}_{i_{N}}(\infty)))
is
|z|^{v}
separated from
D_{\overline{\mathfrak{a}}_{i_{1}},z}
(which contains
-d^{\prime}/c^{\prime}
) for some
v>0
and
0<|z|<r
. By considering the separation with non-Archimedean norm, we know
|m|\leqslant C
is uniformly bounded. Consider the holomorphic function
f(z)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\pi_{j}z^{j}
, then
\log|f(z)|
is a harmonic function in a small disc
|z|<r
. By mean value theorem,

\log|\pi_{0}|\geqslant\min_{|z|=r/2}\log|f(z)|\geqslant\min_{|z|=r/2}\log|z^{m}f(z)|-m\log(r/2)\geqslant\nu\log(r/2)-m\log(r/2).

Since
m
and
\nu
are uniform, we actually prove
|\pi_{0}|\geqslant e^{-C}
holds for some uniform constant
C>0
. Therefore
Lemma

7.4
gives
Eq.

5.10
.
Similarly, since the discs are bounded uniformly away from
\infty
, we have

\log|\pi_{0}|\leqslant\max_{|z|=r/2}\log|f(z)|\leqslant\max_{|z|=r/2}\log|z^{m}f(z)|-m\log(r/2)\leqslant\log C-m\log(r/2).

Thus
|\pi_{0}|\leqslant e^{C}
for some uniform constant
C>0
.
Since we know the leading coefficient
a_{0}
in
Eq.

5.10
is given by
\pi_{0}^{-2}
times the leading coefficient of
c^{\prime-2}
. We conclude

|a_{0}|\geqslant e^{-2C^{\prime}}|\pi_{0}|^{-2}\geqslant e^{-2C-2C^{\prime}}.

Eq.

5.11
then follows from
Eq.

5.10
and the lower bound
|a_{0}|\geqslant e^{-C}
.
∎

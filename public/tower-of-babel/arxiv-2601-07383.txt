Igusa stacks for certain abelian-type Shimura varieties
Fabian Schnelle

Abstract
. We construct Igusa stacks for the good reduction locus of a class of abelian-type Shimura varieties that can be defined in terms of a PEL datum, under the assumption that it is of type (A even) or (C) and unramified at a prime p p .



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with Scholze’s fiber product conjecture for Shimura varieties with infinite level at
p
, as it was first formulated in Mingjia Zhang’s PhD thesis
[
Zha23
]
. In rough terms, it is a conjecture about how the
p
-adic geometry of Shimura varieties with infinite level at
p
can be separated into a
p
-part, which is to be modelled by the Schubert cell in the mixed-characteristic affine Grassmannian labelled by the inverse of the Hodge cocharacter of the Shimura datum, and a prime-to-
p
part, which is to be modelled by a v-stack which is supposed to interpolate between Igusa varieties. For this reason, Zhang called these stacks “Igusa stacks”.

1.1.
Igusa stacks

1.2.
Goal of this paper

In this paper, we construct Igusa stacks for the good reduction locus of certain Shimura varieties of abelian type which arise naturally from a PEL datum (see the appendix
[
Nek18
, A3]
). Since they are of abelian type, they are not covered by the results of
[
DHKZ24
]
. Examples of such Shimura varieties include Hilbert modular varieties or totally indefinite quaternionic Shimura varieties, but in principle any PEL datum of type (A even) or (C) that is unramified at
p
will yield such abelian-type Shimura varieties.

What makes strictly abelian-type Shimura varieties difficult to handle is that they do not possess a fine moduli description in terms of abelian varieties with additional structures, and also cannot be embedded into Siegel modular varieties. Thus at first glance, all methods from
[
Zha23
]
or
[
DHKZ24
]
to construct Igusa stacks break down.

The fact that the abelian-type Shimura varieties that we are considering stem from a PEL datum makes them substantially easier to handle. In fact, they arise in a two-step process from the PEL-type Shimura varieties attached to the same PEL datum.

In the first step, one enlarges the PEL-type Shimura variety by allowing more general level structures in the moduli problem, which we will call
hybrid level structures
; this again yields fine moduli spaces, which we will call
hybrid spaces
. These are still very well behaved, and basically consist of multiple copies of the PEL-type Shimura variety (see Definition
2.13
). In the second step, one quotients out a certain polarization action on these hybrid spaces, which will then yield the desired abelian-type Shimura varieties.

Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.3

(Corollary
8.15
)
.

There is an Igusa stack
\operatorname{Igs}
for the abelian-type Shimura datum
(G,X)
described above, satisfying the axioms of Definition
1.2
.

We refer to Chapter
8
for the explicit construction of
\operatorname{Igs}
.

We remark that Patrick Daniels, Pol van Hoften, Dongryul Kim and Mingjia Zhang have already announced results that will construct Igusa stacks for all abelian-type Shimura varieties. Their methods will heavily lean on the methods developed in
[
Kim25
]
, and thus can be expected to be less concrete. On the other hand, by restricting our attention to this special class of abelian-type Shimura varieties, we are able to give a rather concrete construction of Igusa stacks, much in the spirit of
[
Zha23
]
.

1.3.
Strategy of proof

We will make heavy use of the fact that our abelian-type Shimura variety arises in the two-step process described above from the PEL-type Shimura variety.

Namely, we first tackle the situation for the hybrid space at infinite level everywhere. In this step, it is rather straightforward to use analogous methods as in
[
Zha23
]
and
[
DHKZ24
]
to construct a hybrid Igusa stack fitting into a similar Cartesian diagram.

In a second step, we note that the hybrid Igusa stack (which turns out to be a v-sheaf again) comes equipped with a similar polarization action as the hybrid space. A natural idea is to naively take the stacky quotient by this quotient, but this approach immedaitely runs into problems. This is due to the fact that the polarization action on the hybrid spaces at finite levels is almost never free (in particular not for principal level-
N
structures, even for
N
sufficiently large). However, by a careful analysis we manage to find a cofinal system of level subgroups so that the polarization action at these levels is indeed free. Thus quotienting out the polarization action at these levels yields finite étale torsors. After passing to infinite level everywhere, this shows that quotienting out the polarization action on the hybrid space at infinite level and on the hybrid Igusa stack at infinite level yields pro-étale torsors with respect to the same profinite group.

Thus after taking the naive stacky quotient of the hybrid Igusa stack at infinite level, the fiber product diagram for the abelian-type Shimura variety is an immediate consequence of the fiber product diagram for the hybrid space.

Shimura varieties and hybrid spaces

h(\overline{z})=h(z)^{\#}
and such that the symmetric
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear form
\langle v,h(i)v^{\prime}\rangle:V{R}\times V{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is positive definite. Up to conjugation by an element
c\in(C\otimes\mathbb{R})^{\times}
such that
cc^{\#}=1
, this morphism is unique (see
[
Kot92
, 4.3]
and
[
Mil17
, 8.12]
).

Thus
h
defines a Shimura datum
(G^{*},X^{*})
(resp.
(G,X)
), where
X^{*}
(resp.
X
) denotes the
G^{*}(\mathbb{R})
-conjugacy class (resp.
G(\mathbb{R})
-conjugacy class) of
h:\mathbb{S}\to G^{*}(\mathbb{R})\to G(\mathbb{R})
. The Shimura datum
(G^{*},X^{*})
is of PEL-type, whereas the Shimura datum
(G,X)
is strictly of abelian type.

The action of
h(i)
defines a complex structure von
V{R}
and thus a Hodge decomposition
V{C}=V^{-1,0}\oplus V^{0,-1}
of weight
-1
, with
h(z)
acting by
z
(resp. by
\overline{z}
) on
V^{-1,0}
(resp. on
V^{0,-1}
). The Hodge cocharacter
\nu_{h}:\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathbb{C}}\to G{C}
attached to
h
acts by
z
(resp. by the identity) on
V^{-1,0}
(resp. on
V^{0,-1}
).

Both Shimura data
(G^{*},X^{*})
and
(G,X)
have the common reflex field
E_{0}/\mathbb{Q}
that is generated over
\mathbb{Q}
by the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial

\operatorname{det}(X_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+X_{t}\alpha_{t}|V^{-1,0})

for any
\mathbb{Q}
-basis
\{\alpha_{j}\}
of
B
.

2.3.2.
Local side

Fix a prime number
p
and an embedding
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.

Assumption 2.10
.

We assume that the PEL datum that we have fixed so far is unramified at
p
, i.e. that every term in the decomposition

B_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}=\prod_{\mathfrak{P}|p}B\otimes_{F}F_{\mathfrak{P}}

is a matrix algebra over an unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
(where
\mathfrak{P}
runs over all prime ideals of
F
lying above
p
, and we denote by
F_{\mathfrak{P}}
the completion of
F
with respect to
\mathfrak{P}
).

In particular, this assumption implies that
p
is unramified in
F_{c}
, and that the reductive group
G
is unramified at
p
(which in turn implies that hyperspecial subgroups exist).

We assume to be given the following local data:

•

A
*
-stable
\mathcal{O}_{F,(p)}
-order
\mathcal{O}_{B}\subset B
(where
\mathcal{O}_{F,(p)}:=\mathcal{O}_{F}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
) such that
\mathcal{O}_{B}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a maximal order in
B_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, and

•

an
\mathcal{O}_{p}
-lattice
\Lambda\subset V_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
that is self-dual (up to a scalar in
F_{p}^{\times})
with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{F}
(where we used the notation
\mathcal{O}_{p}:=\mathcal{O}_{F}\otimes{Z}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
F_{p}:=F\otimes{Q}\mathbb{Q}_{p}
).

Example 2.11

(Hilbert modular varieties)
.

If
B=F
for a totally real number field
F
with trivial involution
*=\operatorname{id}
, and let
V=F^{2}
with the standard alternating form
\langle v,w\rangle_{F}=v_{1}w_{2}-v_{2}w_{1}
, then
G=\operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\operatorname{GL}_{2,F})
and the resulting Shimura varieties are the (abelian-type) Hilbert modular varieties (which are of type (C)). Classically, their
\mathbb{C}
-valued points arise from
g
-many copies of the Siegel upper-lower halfplane by quotienting out the twisted diagonal Möbius transformation action of congruence subgroups along the
g
-many different embeddings
F\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

Example 2.12

(Totally indefinite quaternion algebras, cf.
[
Nek18
]
, A6.2)
.

Let
F
be a totally real number field of degree
g
, and let
D/F
be a totally indefinite quaternion algebra over
F
, i.e.
D\otimes_{F}\mathbb{R}\cong M_{2}(\mathbb{R})^{g}
. Consider
D
as a semisimple
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra. We can equip it with a positive involution
*
by defining
d^{*}=u\overline{d}u^{-1}
, where
\overline{(-)}
denotes the main involution on
D
, and
u\in D^{\times}
such that
\overline{u}=-u
and
\operatorname{Nrd}(u)=-u^{2}\in F_{+}^{\times}
. Let
V=D
, equipped with a left action by

via
d\cdot x:=xd^{*}
.

also comes equipped with the skew-symmetric
F
-bilinear form

\langle x,y\rangle_{F}:=\operatorname{Nrd}(xu\overline{y}).

In the end, we obtain quaternionic Shimura varieties of type (C) which fit into our PEL-framework, with

. If the quaternion algebra

is not totally indefinite, then the associated quaternionic Shimura variety does not fit into our PEL framework. However, one can still relate them to an auxiliary PEL datum of type (Aeven), see
[
Nek18
, A6.5-6.9]
.

2.4.
Hybrid space

We keep the notations and assumptions from before. In this subsection, we will introduce the aforementioned hybrid space.

We use the
\mathcal{O}_{p}
-lattice
\Lambda\subset V_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
to define the hyperspecial level subgroup at
p

K_{hs}:=\{g\in G(\mathbb{Q}_{p}):g\Lambda=\Lambda\}.

Furthermore, let
K^{p}\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
be a tame level subgroup, and let
K=K_{hs}K^{p}
.

From this, we obtain a family of hybrid spaces depending on a parameter
\alpha\in\mathbb{A}_{F,f}^{p,\times}
as follows.

Definition 2.13
.

Let us consider the following
hybrid moduli problem
. Fix a parameter
\alpha\in\mathbb{A}_{F,f}^{p,\times}
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,K}
be the presheaf of groupoids on the big étale site of schemes over
\mathcal{O}_{E_{0}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, whose value on a scheme
S
over
\mathcal{O}_{E_{0}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
is the groupoid of abelian schemes with
G
-structure and hybrid
K^{p}
-level structure, i.e.

(i)

A
is an abelian scheme over
S
of relative dimension
g
,

(ii)

\lambda:A\to A^{\vee}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-polarization of degree prime to
p
,

(iii)

\iota:\mathcal{O}_{B}\to\operatorname{End}_{S}(A)\otimes{Z}\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
is a morphism respecting the involution
(-)^{*}
on
\mathcal{O}_{B}
and the Rosati involution on
\operatorname{End}_{S}(A)\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
coming from the polarization
\lambda
, and

(iv)

\overline{\eta}
is a hybrid
K^{p}
-level structure, meaning the following. Consider the pro-étale sheaf

\underline{\operatorname{Isom}}_{G}(\underline{H_{1}}(A,\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p}),\underline{V_{\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p}}}),

whose sections are
\underline{B\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p}}
-linear isomorphisms
\eta
that are
\underline{\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}^{(p)}}
-symplectic similitudes with respect to the lifted Weil pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\lambda}
on
\underline{H_{1}}(A,\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
and the pairing
\alpha\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{F}
on
\underline{V_{\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p}}}
. This is a pro-étale
\underline{G(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{(p)})}
-torsor. Choosing a trivializing cover
\tilde{S}\to S
,
\overline{\eta}
is a
\underline{K^{p}}(\tilde{S})
-orbit of a section
\eta
over
\tilde{S}
, such that
\overline{\eta}
is invariant under the covering action of
\tilde{S}\to S
.

An isomorphism between objects
(A,\iota,\lambda,\overline{\eta})
and
(A^{\prime},\iota^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime},\overline{\eta}^{\prime})
over
S
is a prime-to-
p
quasi-isogeny
f:A\to A^{\prime}
that is compatible with the
\mathcal{O}_{B}
-action, the level structure and such that

f^{\vee}\circ\lambda^{\prime}\circ f=c\lambda

for some
c\in\underline{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}^{\times}}(S)
.

By Lemma
2.7
, the condition that
\eta
is a symplectic similitude can be equivalently phrased in the following ways:

(1)

There exists
c\in\underline{\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}^{(p),\times}}(S)
such that

(x,y)_{\lambda}=\operatorname{Tr}(c\alpha\langle\eta(x),\eta(y)\rangle_{F}).

(2)

There exists
c\in\underline{\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}^{(p),\times}}(S)
such that

\alpha(\eta(x),\eta(y))_{F}=(cx,y)_{\lambda}

When comparing the above hybrid moduli problem to the usual PEL-type moduli problem (e.g. as in
[
Zha23
, 5.2.2]
, one notices that the key change lies in the notion of level structure. Indeed, hybrid level structures allow strictly more general symplectic similitudes.

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,K}
is a Deligne-Mumford stack that, for
K^{p}
sufficiently small (which we will assume from now on), is representable by a smooth quasi-projective scheme over
\mathcal{O}_{E_{0}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, which we will also denote by
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,K}
. Note that for any
c\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}^{(p),\times}
, the stacks
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,K}
and
\mathcal{M}_{c\alpha,K}
are canonically isomorphic.

At the expense of only being defined over
E_{0}
, we can add level at
p
to the hybrid moduli problem by fixing a second parameter
\beta\in F_{p}^{\times}
and considering the sheaf

\underline{\operatorname{Isom}}_{G}(\underline{H_{1}}(A,\mathbb{Q}_{p}),\underline{V_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}})

of
\underline{B_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
-linear isomorphisms that are
\underline{\mathcal{O}_{p}}
-symplectic similitudes with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\lambda}
and
\beta\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{F}
. If
\alpha\in\mathbb{A}_{F,f}^{p,\times}
and
\beta\in F_{p}^{\times}
, then we will denote by
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta,K}
the combined moduli space with level
K=K_{p}K^{p}
away from
p
and at
p
.

For finite level at
p
, the extra parameter
\beta
is an important piece of information. As soon as we pass to infinite level at
p
(which we will do very soon), however, the parameter
\beta
stops being important. Namely, the hybrid spaces with infinite level at
p
for different parameters
\beta
can be identified via Hecke action by
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Thus in the following we will mostly stick to the case where
\beta=1
.

Definition 2.14
.

For any sufficiently small level subgroup
K\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
,
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,K}
is called the
hybric space
at parameter
\alpha
and level
K
.

We have the following notion of Hecke action on our hybrid spaces
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta,K}
(for
\alpha\in\mathbb{A}_{F,f}^{p,\times}
and
\beta\in F_{p}^{\times}
).

Definition 2.15
.

Let
g\in G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
and let
K,K^{\prime}
be any levels such that
g^{-1}K^{\prime}g\subseteq K
, then Hecke action by
g
is the finite étale map

[g]:\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta,K^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{det}(g)\alpha\beta,K}

that sends
(A,\iota,\lambda,\overline{\eta})\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta,K}(S)
to
(A,\iota,\lambda,\overline{g\circ\eta})
.

If
g\in G(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
, then
\nu(g)\alpha
is still coprime to
p
. Also, if
g\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F})
, then
[g]
canonically maps into
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta,K}
again, however altering the similitude factor in the definition of level structure by
\nu(g)
. If
g^{-1}K^{\prime}g=K
, then
[g]
is clearly an isomorphism.

Since the transition maps in the tower
\{\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,K}\}_{K\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f})}
are finite étale, we can pass to the inverse limit in the category of schemes and let

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}:=\varprojlim_{K}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,K},

which is a scheme locally of finite type, locally Noetherian and regular.

2.5.
Abelian-type setting

Now that we have introduced the hybrid space
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,K}
for any sufficiently small level subgroup
K\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
, we can make the following definition.

Definition 2.16
.

The
polarization action
of
\underline{\mathcal{O}_{F,+}^{\times}}
on
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,K}
is defined as

\epsilon\cdot(A,\iota,\lambda,\overline{\eta}):=(A,\iota,\epsilon\lambda,\overline{\eta})

for
\epsilon\in\underline{\mathcal{O}_{F,+}^{\times}}(S)
and
(A,\iota,\lambda,\overline{\eta})\in\mathcal{M}_{K}(S)
.

We alert the reader that in order to define the polarization action, we really needed to pass to the hybrid space. Indeed, polarization action by
\epsilon\in\mathcal{O}_{F,+}^{\times}
will change the similitude factor in the definition of hybrid level structure by a factor
\epsilon
(due to the change of Weil pairing). If we were considering the usual PEL-type level structure, then this could change the rational similitude factor into a non-rational one.

If we are in type (C), this polarization action factors through the finite quotient

\Delta(K):=\mathcal{O}_{F,+}^{\times}/(\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}\cap K)^{2}

since for
\epsilon=\epsilon^{\prime 2}
with
\epsilon^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}\cap K
, multiplication by
\iota(\epsilon^{\prime})
defines an isomorphism

[\iota(\epsilon^{\prime})]:(A,\iota,\lambda,\overline{\eta})\overset{\sim}{\to}(A,\iota,\epsilon^{-1}\lambda,\overline{\eta}).

If we are in type (A even), similarly the polarization factors through the finite quotient

\Delta(K):=\mathcal{O}_{F,+}^{\times}/N_{F_{c}/F}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{c}}^{\times}\cap K).

The action of
\Delta(K)
permutes the connected components of
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,K}
and the stabilizer of each connected component is

(\nu(K)\cap\mathcal{O}_{F,+}^{\times})/(K\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times})^{2}

in type (C) resp.

(\nu(K)\cap\mathcal{O}_{F,+}^{\times})/N_{F_{c}/F}(K\cap\mathcal{O}_{F_{c}}^{\times})

in type (A even) (see
[
Nek18
]
).

Thus the polarization action by
\Delta(K)
is free if and only if the above quotients are trivial. It is a subtle question under which circumstances this happens. To give an answer, we first recall the following lemma which is originally due to Chevalley.

Lemma 2.17

(
[
Tay03
]
, 2.1)
.

Let
L
be a number field and
S
a finite set of places of
L
. By
L_{S}^{\times}
we will denote the subgroup of
L^{\times}
consisting of elements that are units at all finite places
v\notin S
and positive at all real places
v\notin S
. Then for any positive integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, there is an open subgroup
U\subset\prod_{v\notin S\text{ finite}}\mathcal{O}_{L,v}^{\times}
such that
L_{S}^{\times}\cap U\subset(L_{S}^{\times})^{n}
.

Corollary 2.18
.

Let
L
be a number field. Every finite index subgroup of
\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
contains a subgroup of the form
U\cap\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
, where
U\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{L}^{\times}
is a compact open subgroup with
U_{\mathfrak{P}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\times}
for all
\mathfrak{P}
lying above
p
.

Proof.

Let
A\subset\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
be a finite index subgroup. Let
S
be the union of all infinite places and all finite places dividing
p
, and let
n\gg 0
be such that for every
x\in\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
,
x^{n}\in A
.

Choose
U
as in Chevalley’s lemma and fill it up with
U_{\mathfrak{P}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\times}
for any prime
\mathfrak{P}
above
p
. Then

\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}\cap U=\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}\cap L_{S}^{\times}\cap U\subset\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}\cap(L_{S}^{\times})^{n}\subset(\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times})^{n}\subset A.\qed

With this corollary at hand, we can give a first negative example which shows that the polarization action will in general not turn out to be free, even if we are willing to go into deep enough level away from
p
.

Example 2.19
.

Consider the case of Hilbert modular varieties, i.e. we are in a type (C) situation with
G=\operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\operatorname{GL}_{2,F})
. Assume we are given hyperspecial level at
p
, and the principal level-
N
-subgroup
K(N)
for some
N
prime to
p
. Then the polarization action is free if and only if the quotient
(\operatorname{det}K(N)\cap\mathcal{O}_{F,+}^{\times})/(K(N)\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times})^{2}
is trivial. Note that
K(N)\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}=(1+N\mathcal{O}_{F})^{\times}
and
\operatorname{det}K(N)\cap\mathcal{O}_{F,+}^{\times}=(1+N\mathcal{O}_{F})^{\times}_{+}
. So we need to analyze the quotient

(1+N\mathcal{O}_{F})_{+}^{\times}/(1+N\mathcal{O}_{F})^{\times 2}.

We claim that for
N
sufficiently large, we have
(1+N\mathcal{O}_{F})_{+}^{\times}=(1+N\mathcal{O}_{F})^{\times}
, which would imply that the quotient is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{g-1}
by Dirichlet’s unit theorem.

To prove the claim, we apply the above corollary to the finite index subgroup
\mathcal{O}_{F,+}^{\times}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
. Thus there is a compact open subgroup
U\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}^{\times}
with
U_{\mathfrak{P}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\times}
for all
\mathfrak{P}
lying above
p
, such that
U\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{F,+}^{\times}
. In particular, for any
N\gg 0
that is prime-to-
p
such that
K(N)\subset U
, we see that

(1+N\mathcal{O}_{F})^{\times}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F,+}^{\times},

i.e.
(1+N\mathcal{O}_{F})_{+}^{\times}=(1+N\mathcal{O}_{F})^{\times}
, proving the claim.

However, not all hope is lost. The same corollary of Chevalley’s Lemma can be used to find certain level subgroups for which the polarization action turns out to be free.

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
K=K_{p}K^{p}\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
be any compact open subgroup. If we are in type (C), then there exists an open compact normal subgroup
K^{\prime p}\subseteq K^{p}
of finite index such that for any wild level
K^{\prime}_{p}
containing
K_{p}
,

\nu(K^{\prime}_{p}K^{\prime p})\cap\mathcal{O}_{F,+}^{\times}=(K^{\prime}_{p}K^{\prime p}\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times})^{2}.

Similarly, if we are in type (A even), the same is true for the formula

\nu(K^{\prime}_{p}K^{\prime p})\cap\mathcal{O}_{F,+}^{\times}=N_{F_{c}/F}(K^{\prime}_{p}K^{\prime p}\cap\mathcal{O}_{F_{c}}^{\times}).

Proof.

We will only consider the case of type (C), the case of type (A even) can be handled analogously.

First note that for any compact open subgroup
K\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
, the inclusion
(K\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times})^{2}\subset\nu(K)\cap\mathcal{O}_{F,+}^{\times}
always holds, so that we only have to deal with the reverse inclusion.

We apply the corollary of Chevalley’s Lemma to the finite index subgroup
(K_{p}K^{p}\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times})^{2}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
. Thus we obtain a compact open subgroup
U\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}^{\times}
with
U_{\mathfrak{P}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\times}
for all
\mathfrak{P}
lying above
p
, such that
U\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}\subseteq(K_{p}K^{p}\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times})^{2}
. This implies that for any
K^{\prime}_{p}
containing
K_{p}

U\cap\nu(K^{\prime}_{p}K^{p})\cap\mathcal{O}_{F,+}^{\times}\subset U\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}\subset U\cap(K^{\prime}_{p}K^{p}\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times})^{2}.

Now let
K^{\prime p}=\nu^{-1}(U^{p})\subset K^{p}
. Then
U\cap(K^{\prime}_{p}K^{p}\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times})^{2}\subset(K^{\prime}_{p}K^{\prime p}\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times})^{2}
, and
\nu(K^{\prime}_{p}K^{\prime p})\subset U\cap\nu(K^{\prime}_{p}K^{p})
.
∎

While the negative example showed that we cannot expect the polarization action to
always
be free eventually if we are only willing to pass to deep enough level, Lemma
2.20
makes it clear that we can at least find a cofinal system of compact open subgroups for which the polarization action is in fact free. We will call such level subgroups
good
level subgroups from now on.

Lemma
2.20
is even a bit stronger than that. While it is most likely not true that for any tame level
K^{p}
we can find a finite index subgroup
K^{\prime p}\subset K^{p}
such that
K_{p}K^{\prime p}
is good
uniformly for any

K^{p}
, we at least know that once we have given ourselves a wild level subgroup
K_{p}\subset G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
to start with, we can find a uniform level subgroup
K^{\prime p}\subset K^{p}
such that
K^{\prime}_{p}K^{\prime p}
is good for any wild level subgroup
K^{\prime}_{p}\supset K_{p}
(in particular hyperspecial level subgroups containing
K_{p}
).

Definition 2.21
.

A level subgroup
K_{p}K^{p}\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
is called
uniformly good
if for any wild level subgroup
K^{\prime}_{p}\supset K_{p}
, the level subgroup
K_{p}^{\prime}K^{p}
is good.

Note that the property of being uniformly good is stable under conjugation. This follows from the fact that the condition
\nu(K)\cap\mathcal{O}_{F,+}^{\times}=(K\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times})^{2}
is clearly invariant under conjugation (similarly for type (A even)). Thus Hecke action will preserve the set of uniformly good level structures.

All in all, we find that the system of uniformly good level subgroups is a cofinal system in the system of all level subgroups. Choosing such a uniformly good level subgroup
K
, the quotient scheme
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,K}/\Delta(K)
exists and is quasi-projective and smooth, either over
E_{0}
or
\mathcal{O}_{E_{0}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
(depending on if we chose hyperspecial level at
p
or not).

Thus we can make the following definition.

Definition 2.22
.

For any sufficiently small uniformly good level subgroup
K\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
, we set
\mathcal{M}_{G,\alpha,K}:=\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,K}/\Delta(K)
.

From these quotient spaces, we can obtain a model for the full abelian-type Shimura variety we are interested in. Namely, fix a set
\Psi
of representatives of the (finite) double coset space

\mathcal{O}_{F,(p),+}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{F,f}^{p,\times}/\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}^{(p),\times}.

Fix a uniformly good level structure
K
with hyperspecial level at
p
. Then the disjoint union

\coprod_{\alpha\in\Psi}\mathcal{M}_{G,\alpha,K}=\coprod_{\alpha\in\Psi}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,K}/\Delta(K)

constitutes a smooth quasi-projective integral model of the abelian-type Shimura variety at level
K
over
\mathcal{O}_{E_{0}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
. We can proceed similarly if we add level at
p
to the situation.

Thus it suffices to construct a hybrid Igusa stack for each parameter
\alpha\in\mathbb{A}_{F,f}^{p,\times}
separately, and then take their disjoint union accordingly.

Convention 2.23
.

From now on, if the parameter
\alpha\in\mathbb{A}_{F,f}^{p,\times}
is understood from context or does not play a role in the discussion, we will drop it from our notation.

3.
Perfectoid spaces, diamonds and v-stacks

In this subsection, we will briefly review the theory of perfectoid spaces, diamonds and the v-topology in order to fix our notation.

3.1.
Perfectoid spaces

Definition 3.1
.

A topological ring
R
is called a
Huber ring
if it admits an open subring
R_{0}
that is adic with respect to a finitely generated ideal
I
. A Huber ring is called
Tate
if it contains a
pseudo-uniformizer

\varpi\in R
, i.e. a topologically nilpotent unit.

Definition 3.2
.

A Tate ring
R
is called
perfectoid
if it is complete, uniform (i.e. the subring of power-bounded elements
R^{\circ}\subset R
is bounded) and there exists a pseudo-uniformizer
\varpi\in R
such that
\varpi^{p}
divides
p
in
R^{\circ}
and the Frobenius map

\Phi:R^{\circ}/\varpi\to R^{\circ}/\varpi^{p}

is an isomorphism.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
R
be a perfectoid Tate ring. The
tilt
of
R
is the topological ring

R^{\flat}=\varprojlim_{x\mapsto x^{p}}R

Let us expand on this a little bit. The topology of
R^{\flat}
is the inverse limit topology. The ring structure is given as follows: First, we have multiplicative bijective maps

\varprojlim_{\Phi}R^{\circ}\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{\Phi}R^{\circ}/p\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{\Phi}R^{\circ}/\varpi

that equip the left-hand side with a ring structure. The inverse maps are given by mapping
x=(x_{0},x_{1},...)
to
(x^{\sharp},(x^{1/p})^{\sharp},(x^{1/{p^{2}}})^{\sharp},...)\in\varprojlim_{\Phi}R^{\circ}
, where
x^{\sharp}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\tilde{x_{i}}^{q^{i}}
, the
\tilde{x_{i}}
being lifts of
x_{i}
to
R^{\circ}
.

Now take any preimage
\varpi^{\flat}
of
\varpi
under the map

\varprojlim_{\Phi}R^{\circ}\cong\varprojlim_{\Phi}R^{\circ}/\varpi^{p}\to R^{\circ}/\varpi^{p}

This way we see that
R^{\flat}
is complete, Tate and even perfectoid
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra with ring of power-bounded elements
R^{\flat\circ}\cong\varprojlim_{\Phi}R^{\circ}
and pseudo-uniformizer
\varpi^{\flat}
, so in particular
R^{\flat}=R^{\flat\circ}[1/{\varpi^{\flat}}]
.

The projection to the first component map
R^{\flat}\to R,x\mapsto x^{\sharp}
(this coincides with the above notion of
(-)^{\sharp}
) induces a ring isomorphism
R^{\flat\circ}/\varpi^{\flat}\cong R^{\circ}/\varpi
. Additionally we have the following

Lemma 3.4
.

The set of open, bounded and integrally closed subrings
R^{+}\subset R^{\circ}
is in bijection with the set of integral subrings
R^{\flat+}\subset R^{\flat\circ}
, via
R^{+}\mapsto R^{\flat+}:=\varprojlim_{x\mapsto x^{p}}R^{+}
. Furthermore
R^{\flat+}/\varpi^{\flat}\cong R^{+}/\varpi
.

Definition 3.5
.

An
affinoid (perfectoid) Tate-Huber pair
is a pair
(R,R^{+})
, where
R
is a (perfectoid) Tate ring and
R^{+}\subset R^{\circ}
is an open, bounded, integrally closed subring. A morphism of affinoid Tate-Huber pairs
(R,R^{+})\to(R^{\prime},R^{\prime+})
is a morphism of topological rings that sends
R^{+}
into
R^{\prime+}
. The tilt of an affinoid perfectoid Tate-Huber pair
(R,R^{+})
is the affinoid perfectoid Tate-Huber pair
(R^{\flat},R^{\flat+})
.

Definition 3.6
.

A
perfectoid space

X
is an adic space that admits a cover by affinoid opens that are isomorphic to affinoid adic spaces of the form
\operatorname{Spa}(R,R^{+})
, where
(R,R^{+})
is an affinoid perfectoid Tate-Huber pair.

To a perfectoid space
X
we can functorially associate its tilt
X^{\flat}
by tilting its rational open subsets and then gluing. There is a homeomorphism
|X|\cong|X^{\flat}|
that preserves rational open subsets.

Example 3.7
.

Given a non-Archimedean complete algebraically closed field
C
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{C}
and integral subring
C^{+}\subset\mathcal{O}_{C}
, then
\operatorname{Spa}(C,C^{+})
is perfectoid. We will call this a
geometric point
in analogy with the scheme-theoretic setting. If
C^{+}=\mathcal{O}_{C}
, we call it a
rank 1 geometric point
.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
X
be a perfectoid space in characteristic
p
. An
untilt of
X
is a pair
(X^{\sharp},\iota)
, consisting of a perfectoid space
X^{\sharp}
(of any characteristic), and a fixed isomorphism
\iota:(X^{\sharp})^{\flat}\cong X
.

To simplify notation we will often drop the explicit isomorphism
\iota
and just write
X^{\sharp}
for an untilt of
X
.

Remark 3.9
.

Assume
\operatorname{Spa}(R,R^{+})
is affinoid perfectoid in characteristic
p
with pseudo-uniformizer
\varpi\in R^{+}
, and we are given two untilts
\operatorname{Spa}(R^{\sharp_{1}},R^{\sharp_{1}+})
and
\operatorname{Spa}(R^{\sharp_{2}},R^{\sharp_{2}+})
. Then for
i\in\{1,2\}
, there is Fontaine’s theta map

\theta:W(R^{+})\to R^{\sharp_{i}+}

whose kernel is generated by an element
\xi
. We may replace
\varpi
by one of its
p^{n}
-th roots to achieve that
\xi=p+[\varpi]a
for some
a\in W(R^{+})
. By quotienting out
(p,[\varpi])
, this yields a map

R^{\sharp_{i}+}=W(R^{+})/(p+[\varpi]a)\twoheadrightarrow R^{+}/\varpi

Choosing
\varpi
sufficiently small, we can hence simultaneously get two quotient maps

R^{\sharp_{i}+}\twoheadrightarrow R^{+}/\varpi

for
i=1,2
. This observation will be very important later when defining the hybrid Igusa stack.

3.2.
The pro-étale and v-topology

Recall the following classes of morphisms between perfectoid spaces.

Definition 3.10
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a morphism of perfectoid spaces.

(i)

f
is
finite étale
if for any affinoid perfectoid open
V=\operatorname{Spa}(R,R^{+})\subset Y
, the preimage
f^{-1}(V)=\operatorname{Spa}(S,S^{+})
is again affinoid perfectoid and the induced ring map
R\to S
is finite étale.

(ii)

f
is
étale
if for any
x\in X
there is an open neighbourhood
V\subset X
of
x
, an affinoid perfectoid open
U=\operatorname{Spa}(R,R^{+})\subset Y
such that
f(V)\subset U
and such that
f|_{V}
factors as a composition of an open immersion followed by a finite étale morphism.

(iii)

f
is
pro-étale
if for any
x\in X
there is an affinoid perfectoid open
V\subset X
of
x
, an affinoid perfectoid open
U=\operatorname{Spa}(R,R^{+})\subset Y
such that
f(V)\subset U
and
f
can be written as a limit, over a small cofiltered index category
I
, of étale maps
V_{i}\to U
with
V_{i}
being affinoid perfectoid for all
i\in I
.

Denote by
\operatorname{Perfd}
the category of perfectoid spaces, and by
\operatorname{Perf}
the full subcategory of perfectoid spaces in characteristic
p
.

Definition 3.11
.

Recall the following Grothendieck topologies on
\operatorname{Perf}
(or
\operatorname{Perfd}
):

(i)

The
v-topology
on
\operatorname{Perf}
is the Grothendieck topology which has as coverings jointly surjective maps
\{f_{i}:X_{i}\to Y\}_{i\in I}
such that for every quasicompact open
U\subset Y
there is a finite subset
J\subset I
and quasicompact open
V_{j}\subset X_{j}
for all
j\in J
such that
\bigcup_{j\in J}f(V_{j})=U
.

(ii)

The
pro-étale topology
on
\operatorname{Perf}
is the Grothendieck topology which has as coverings those v-covers
\{f_{i}:X_{i}\to Y\}_{i\in I}
such that all
f_{i}
are pro-étale.

We call
\operatorname{Perf}
(or
\operatorname{Perfd})
equipped with the pro-étale resp. v-topology the
big pro-étale
resp.
v-site
. There are some set-theoretic issues arising here, which can be avoided by using cut-off cardinals. Here, we will avoid these issues by simply ignoring them.

By
[
Sch22
, 8.6, 8.7]
both the v-topology and the pro-étale topology on
\operatorname{Perf}
and
\operatorname{Perfd}
are subcanonical. Thus we will always confuse a perfectoid space distinguish between a perfectoid space and the v-sheaf it represents.

We can also consider the small pro-étale site
X_{\text{pro-ét}}
of a perfectoid space
X
. It has as objects perfectoid spaces
Y
that are pro-étale over
X
, and as coverings it has jointly surjective morphisms with the same quasicompactness condition as in the definition of the big pro-étale site.

Within the v-topology, it is possible to basically rip apart perfectoid spaces into points. More precisely, the subclass of strictly totally disconnected perfectoid spaces form a basis for the v-topology. Recall that we call a perfectoid space
X

totally
(resp.
strictly totally
)
disconnected
if it is quasicompact, quasiseparated and every open (resp. étale) cover of
X
splits.

Proposition 3.12

(
[
Sch22
]
, 1.15)
.

A perfectoid space
X
is (strictly) totally disconnected if and only if it is affinoid and every connected component of
X
is of the form
\operatorname{Spa}(K,K^{+})
with
K
an (algebraically closed) perfectoid field and
K^{+}
an open bounded valuation subring.

3.3.
v-stacks

The Igusa stack is supposed to be a stack on the big v-site
\operatorname{Perf}
, so for convenience we briefly recall the relevant notions.

Definition 3.13
.

A
v-stack
is a contravariant 2-functor
F:\operatorname{Perf}\to\operatorname{Grpd}
into the 2-category
\operatorname{Grpd}
of groupoids, that satisfies descent for v-covers.

Concrete, satisfying descent means the following. Let
X\to Y
be any v-cover of perfectoid spaces. Write
p_{1},p_{2}:X\times_{Y}X\to X
and
p_{12},p_{23},p_{13}:X\times_{Y}X\times_{Y}X\to X\times_{Y}X
for the projections. Denote by
F(X/Y)
the category of descent data. Its objects are tuples
(s,\alpha)
with
s\in F(X)
and
\alpha:p_{1}^{*}s\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}p_{2}^{*}s
an isomorphism that satisfies the cocycle condition

p_{23}^{*}\alpha\text{ }\circ p_{12}^{*}\alpha=p_{13}^{*}\alpha.

Then a contravariant 2-functor
F:\operatorname{Perf}\to\operatorname{Grpd}
satisfies descent for v-covers if, for any v-cover
X\to Y
of perfectoid spaces, the natural functor
F(Y)\to F(X/Y)
is an equivalence of categories.

All v-stacks
X
that we are going to encounter in this thesis will be
small
, which means that they admit a presentation

R=Y\times_{X}Y\rightrightarrows Y\to X,

where
Y
is a perfectoid space, and
Y\times_{X}Y
is a small v-sheaf, i.e. admits a surjection of v-sheaves from a perfectoid space.

The presentation
R\rightrightarrows Y\to X
allows us to define the underlying topological space of a small v-stack. Namely, let
\tilde{R}\to R
be a surjection from a perfectoid space, then
|X|:=|Y|/|\tilde{R}|
. This is independent of any choices made (see
[
Sch22
, 12.7,12.8]
).

The Igusa stack for our abelian-type Shimura variety will arise from the following general procedure.

Example 3.14
.

For any topological space
T
we can define the v-sheaf
\underline{T}:S\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}(|S|,T)
on
\operatorname{Perf}
. Assume we are given a v-sheaf
X
on
\operatorname{Perf}
equipped with an action by a topological group
G
, we can consider the stacky quotient
[X/\underline{G}]
, which is the v-sheaf theoretic coequalizer of the projection and action map

X\times\underline{G}\rightrightarrows X

This is a v-stack. If
G
is locally profinite and
X
is a perfectoid space, then
X\times\underline{G}
is representable by a perfectoid space. Also both the projection and action map are pro-étale. If
X
is a small v-sheaf, then
[X/\underline{G}]
is a small v-stack.

Definition 3.15
.

Given a diagram
X\overset{f}{\to}Z\overset{g}{\leftarrow}Y
of small v-stacks, its
fiber product

X\times_{Z}Y
is the presheaf of groupoids that sends
S\in\operatorname{Perf}
to the groupoid of triples
(x,y,\varphi:f(x)\overset{\sim}{\to}g(y))
with
x\in X(S)
,
y\in Y(S)
(a morphism
(x,y,\varphi)\to(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
of such triples being pairs of maps
(\alpha:x\to x^{\prime},\beta:y\to y^{\prime})
such that
\varphi^{\prime}\circ f(\alpha)=g(\beta)\circ\varphi
).

By
[
Sch22
, 12.10]
,
X\times_{Z}Y
is again a small v-stack.

Definition 3.16
.

A v-stack
X
is
quasicompact
if there is a surjection of v-stacks
Y\twoheadrightarrow X
with
Y
an affinoid perfectoid space.

In particular if
X
is quasicompact, then
X
is a small v-stack and its underlying topological space
|X|
is quasicompact.

Definition 3.17

(
[
Sch22
]
, 10.7)
.

Let
f:Y\to X
be a morphism of v-stacks. We call the morphism

(i)

0-truncated
if for all
S\in\operatorname{Perf}
the morphism of groupoids
f(S):Y(S)\to X(S)
is faithful. Equivalently, if the diagonal map
\Delta_{f}:Y\to Y\times_{X}Y
is an injection.

(ii)

quasicompact
if for any affinoid perfectoid space
S
with a morphism
S\to X
, the fiber product
Y\times_{X}S
is quasicompact.

(iii)

quasiseparated
if the diagonal
\Delta_{f}
, which is 0-truncated, is qcqs.

(iv)

an
open
(resp.
closed
) immersion if for every (totally disconnected) perfectoid space
T
with a morphism
T\to X
, the pullback
Y\times_{X}T\to T
is represented by an open (resp. closed) immersion.

(v)

separated
if the diagonal
\Delta_{f}
is a closed immersion (thus automatically 0-truncated).

(vi)

partially proper
if it is separated and for every diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Spa}(R,R^{\circ})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Spa}(R,R^{+})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

where
R
is any perfectoid Tate ring with an open, bounded and integrally closed subring
R^{+}\subset R
, there exists a unique dotted arrow making the diagram commute.

3.4.
Diamonds

Definition 3.18
.

A
diamond
is a pro-étale sheaf
\mathcal{D}
on
\operatorname{Perf}
that can be written as
X/R
with
X
and
R
being perfectoid spaces and
R\subset X\times X
being a pro-étale equivalence relation.

By
[
Sch22
, 11.9]
, diamonds are small v-sheaves, hence in the context of diamonds and morphisms between them we have the same terminology as developed in the last section.

One of the most important examples of a diamond is the following.

Example 3.19
.

Consider the perfectoid field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\operatorname{cycl}}:=\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})}
. We define

\operatorname{Spd}\mathbb{Q}_{p}:=\operatorname{coeq}(\operatorname{Spa}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\operatorname{cycl}})^{\flat}\times\underline{\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\operatorname{cycl}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\rightrightarrows\operatorname{Spa}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\operatorname{cycl}})^{\flat})

This is a diamond with underlying topological space being a point.

Theorem 3.20

(
[
SW20
]
, 8.4.2)
.

The category of perfectoid spaces over
\operatorname{Spa}\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is equivalent to the category of perfectoid spaces
X
in characteristic
p
, equipped with a structure morphism
X\to\operatorname{Spd}\mathbb{Q}_{p}
as sheaves on
\operatorname{Perf}
.

In order to turn our hybrid space and our abelian-type Shimura variety into objeccts of
p
-adic geometry, we need to explain how to attach diamonds or v-sheaves to various (formal) schemes.

First, assume
X
is an analytic adic space over
\operatorname{Spa}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
(meaning that it is locally Tate, see
[
SW20
, 4.3.1]
). Then we can define a presheaf
X^{\diamond}
on
\operatorname{Perf}
by

T\mapsto X^{\diamond}(T):=\{(T^{\sharp},T^{\sharp}\to X)\}/\cong

where
T^{\sharp}
is an untilt of
T
and
T^{\sharp}\to X
is a map of adic spaces.
By
[
SW20
, 10.1.5]
the presheaf
X^{\diamond}
is a diamond. In fact, if
X
is perfectoid then
X^{\diamond}=X^{\flat}
, and more generally if
X
is analytic, one can cover it with a perfectoid space by a perfectoid equivalence relation in the pro-étale topology, and then tilt both. If
X=\operatorname{Spa}(R,R^{+})
we will write
X^{\diamond}=\operatorname{Spd}(R,R^{+})
, and we will also drop
R^{+}
from the notation if
R^{+}=R^{\circ}
.

In the case of a pre-adic space
X
over
\operatorname{Spa}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, one can still consider the above presheaf (with maps of adic spaces replaced by maps of pre-adic spaces), but it does not necessarily yield a diamond, though by
[
SW20
, 18.1.1]
it always yields a v-sheaf. In particular this applies to formal schemes
\mathfrak{X}
. If
\mathfrak{X}=\operatorname{Spf}A
is formal affine, then
\mathfrak{X}^{\diamond}=\operatorname{Spd}(A,A)
, where
\operatorname{Spa}(A,A)
is the pre-adic space attached to
\operatorname{Spf}A
. This globalizes and defines a functor from formal schemes to v-sheaves.

For schemes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, there are two possible ways of turning them into diamonds (or v-sheaves), and in general they won’t agree. First assume
X=\operatorname{Spec}A
is affine.

(i)

The
small diamond

X^{\diamond}
attached to
X
is the v-sheaf on
\operatorname{Perf}

S\mapsto\{(S^{\sharp},f:A\to\mathcal{O}_{S^{\sharp}}^{+}(S^{\sharp})\}

where
S^{\sharp}
is an untilt of
S
and
f
is a ring homomorphism.

(ii)

The
big diamond

X^{\Diamond}
attached to
X
is the v-sheaf on
\operatorname{Perf}

S\mapsto\{(S^{\sharp},f:A\to\mathcal{O}_{S^{\sharp}}(S^{\sharp})\}

where
S^{\sharp}
is an untilt of
S
and
f
is a ring homomorphism.

As discussed in
[
AGLR22
, 2.2]
, these constructions glue to yield functors from the category of schemes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
to v-sheaves, however in general for non-affine schemes we won’t have a nice moduli description as above.

For a scheme
X
locally of finite type over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, the two diamond functors can be described via
p
-adic completion and adic analytification:

(i)

X^{\diamond}
is obtained by first
p
-adically completing
X
, which yields a formal scheme over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and then applying the diamond functor for formal schemes.

(ii)

X^{\Diamond}
is obtained by applying adic analytification to
X
, which yields an adic space over
\operatorname{Spa}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and then taking its associated diamond.

From the first description of the two diamond functors, it is clear that we have a natural map
X^{\diamond}\to X^{\Diamond}
. It is an open immersion if
X
is separated, and an isomorphism if
X
is proper.

4.
Passage to the adic world

In this section, we will introduce multiple incarnations of the hybrid spaces and abelian-type Shimura varieties, introduced in Section
2
, as objects in
p
-adic geometry. We will always assume that
K\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
is a level subgroup, sometimes additionally assumed to be hyperspecial at
p
, and always assumed to be uniformly good when talking about the abelian-type Shimura varieties. Recall that we drop the parameter
\alpha\in\mathbb{A}_{F,f}^{p,\times}
from our notation unless necessary.

Recall from the introduction that in order to state Conjecture
1.1
, we needed to fix an isomorphism
\mathbb{C}\cong\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. Let
v
be the prime of the reflex field
E_{0}
above
p
that is induced by the composition
E_{0}\to\mathbb{C}\cong\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. We complete
E_{0}
with respect to
v
, which yields the local field
E
, a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Via base change, we obtain the hybrid space
\mathcal{M}_{K}
and the abelian-type Shimura variety
\mathcal{M}_{G,K}
over
E
(respectively their integral models over
\mathcal{O}_{E}
if
K
is hyperspecial at
p
). We will denote these spaces again by
\mathcal{M}_{K}
resp.
\mathcal{M}_{G,K}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
K
be hyperspecial at
p
. By
\mathfrak{X}_{K}
we denote the formal scheme over
\operatorname{Spf}\mathcal{O}_{E}
arising via
p
-adic completion from
\mathcal{M}_{K}
, and call it the
formal hybrid space
. Analogously, we will call
\mathfrak{X}_{G,K}
the
formal abelian-type Shimura variety
.

The
formal hybrid space at infinite level
is the inverse limit in the category of formal schemes
\mathfrak{X}=\varprojlim_{K}\mathfrak{X}_{K}
, where
K
runs over all hyperspecial level subgroups. We similarly define the
formal abelian-type Shimura variety at infinite level
.

The formal hybrid space
\mathfrak{X}_{K}
still has a moduli description in terms of formal abelian schemes with
G
-structure and hybrid
K^{p}
-level structure. We can also
p
-adically complete the universal abelian variety over
\mathcal{M}_{K}
to obtain a universal formal abelian scheme
G
-structure and hybrid
K^{p}
-level structure.

Definition 4.2
.

The
diamond hybrid space
is the diamond
\mathcal{X}_{K}=\mathcal{M}_{K}^{\Diamond}
over
\operatorname{Spd}E
. Similarly, the
diamond abelian-type Shimura variety
is the diamond
\mathcal{X}_{G,K}=\mathcal{M}_{G,K}^{\Diamond}
over
\operatorname{Spd}E
.

We can also apply the diamond functor to the universal abelian variety
\mathcal{A}_{K}/\mathcal{M}_{K}
to obtain a proper map of diamonds

\pi:\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\diamond}\to\mathcal{X}_{K}.

Definition 4.3
.

The (
p
-adic)
Tate module
of the universal abelian variety
\mathcal{A}_{K}
is the sheaf of
\underline{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-modules on
\mathcal{X}_{K}

T_{p}(\mathcal{A}_{K}):=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\underline{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}}(R^{1}\pi_{*}\underline{\mathbb{Z}_{p}},\underline{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}).

Via the
\mathcal{O}_{B}
-action on
\mathcal{A}_{K}
, the Tate module also comes equipped with a natural
\mathcal{O}_{B}
-module structure.

Definition 4.4
.

The
hybrid space at infinite level at
p
is the diamond (here
K
is hyperspecial at
p
)

\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}}:=\underline{\operatorname{Isom}}_{G}(T_{p}(\mathcal{A}_{K}),\underline{\Lambda})\to\mathcal{X}_{K},

where the isomorphism sheaf is of
\mathcal{O}_{B}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-linear trivializations of the Tate module that are
\underline{\mathcal{O}_{p}}
-symplectic similitudes with respect to the lifted Weil pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\lambda}
on the Tate module and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{F}
on
\underline{\Lambda}
.

Alternatively, we can write the hybrid space at infinite level at
p
as

\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}}=\varprojlim_{K_{p}}\mathcal{X}_{K_{p}K^{p}}

in the category of diamonds.

Definition 4.5
.

The
hybrid space at infinite level
is the diamond

\mathcal{X}=\varprojlim_{K^{p}}\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}}=\varprojlim_{K}\mathcal{X}_{K}.

Our choice of asymmetry in the definition of the hybrid space at infinite level can be explained by our main goal: we want to break apart its
p
-adic geometry via the Hodge-Tate period map and the construction of an Igusa stack. Thus, it makes sense to pay special attention to the infinite level at
p
.

When passing to the abelian-type Shimura varieties, we will completely drop this asymmetry. This is due to the subleties surrounding uniformly good level structures.

Definition 4.6
.

The
abelian-type Shimura variety at infinite level
is the inverse limit

\mathcal{X}_{G}:=\varprojlim_{K}\mathcal{X}_{G,K},

in the category of diamonds, where the inverse limit runs over all uniformly good level subgroups
K\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
(whose
p
-part contains
K_{hs}
).

Last but not least, we introduce the good reduction loci. First assume that the level subgroup
K\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
is hyperspecial at
p
, then we define

\mathcal{X}_{K}^{\circ}=(\mathfrak{X}_{K})_{\eta}^{\diamond}=\mathfrak{X}_{K}^{\Diamond}\times_{\operatorname{Spd}\mathcal{O}_{E}}\operatorname{Spd}E,

where by
(-)_{\eta}
we denote taking adic generic fiber of a formal scheme. Alternatively,
\mathcal{X}_{K}^{\circ}=\mathcal{M}_{K}^{\diamond}
in terms of the small diamond functor. For any diamond
D
over
\mathcal{X}_{K}
, we define its good reduction locus via pullback as

D^{\circ}=D\times_{\mathcal{X}_{K}}\mathcal{X}_{K}^{\circ},

which in particular includes the diamonds
\mathcal{X}_{K_{p}K^{p}}
,
\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}}
and
\mathcal{X}
. For later use, we remark that
\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}}^{\circ}=\varprojlim_{K_{p}}\mathcal{X}_{K_{p}K^{p}}^{\circ}
and
\mathcal{X}^{\circ}=\varprojlim_{K_{p}K^{p}}\mathcal{X}^{\circ}_{K_{p}K^{p}}
. For the latter claim, we use that for
K^{\prime}\subset K
both hyperspecial at
p
,
\mathcal{X}_{K}^{\circ}
pulls back to
\mathcal{X}_{K^{\prime}}^{\circ}
.

In the abelian-type situation, we can similarly define
\mathcal{X}_{G,K}^{\circ}
for any uniformly good level subgroup
K\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
that is hyperspecial at
p
. For any uniformly good level subgroup
K_{p}K^{p}
, we define

\mathcal{X}_{G,K_{p}K^{p}}^{\circ}=\mathcal{X}_{G,K_{p}K^{p}}\times_{\mathcal{X}_{G,K_{hs}K^{p}}}\mathcal{X}_{G,K_{hs}K^{p}}^{\circ},

and at infinite level we get

\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\circ}=\varprojlim_{K}\mathcal{X}_{G,K}^{\circ},

where
K
runs over all uniformly good level subgroups.

For later use, we want to see that
\mathcal{X}^{\circ}
still retains a moduli description, in the sense that it is the analytic sheafification of a presheaf which is given in terms of a moduli description.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
K
be hyperspecial at
p
. The good reduction locus
\mathcal{X}_{K}^{\circ}
is the analytic sheafification of the presheaf
\mathcal{X}^{\circ,\operatorname{pre}}
on
\operatorname{Perf}

S=\operatorname{Spa}(R,R^{+})\mapsto\{(S^{\sharp},\operatorname{Spf}R^{\sharp+}\to\mathfrak{X}_{K})\},

where
S^{\sharp}=\operatorname{Spa}(R^{\sharp},R^{\sharp+})
is an untilt of
S
over
\operatorname{Spa}E
.

Similarly,
\mathcal{X}_{K_{hs}}^{\circ}=\varprojlim_{K^{p}}\mathcal{X}^{\circ}_{K_{hs}K^{p}}
is the analytic sheafification of the presheaf on
\operatorname{Perf}

S=\operatorname{Spa}(R,R^{+})\mapsto\{(S^{\sharp},\operatorname{Spf}R^{\sharp+}\to\mathfrak{X})\}.

Proof.

The first part is
[
DHKZ24
, 2.1.4]
. The second part follows also follows from loc.cit., by noting that the diamond functor
(-)^{\Diamond}
on formal schemes commutes with inverse limits.
∎

For a suitably small away-from-
p
level
K^{p}
, we have the identification

\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}}\times_{\mathcal{X}_{K_{hs}K^{p}}}\mathcal{X}_{K_{hs}}=\underline{\operatorname{Isom}}_{G}(T_{p}(\mathcal{A}_{K}),\underline{\Lambda})\times_{\mathcal{X}_{K_{hs}K^{p}}}\mathcal{X}_{K_{hs}},

which after passing to good reduction loci gives us

\mathcal{X}^{\circ}=\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}}^{\circ}\times_{\mathcal{X}^{\circ}_{K_{hs}K^{p}}}\mathcal{X}_{K_{hs}}^{\circ}.

Now we can use that analytic sheafification commutes with fiber products to obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 4.8
.

\mathcal{X}^{\circ}
is the analytic sheafification of the presheaf on
\operatorname{Perf}

S=\operatorname{Spa}(R,R^{+})\mapsto\{(S^{\sharp},y,\beta)\}

where
S^{\sharp}=\operatorname{Spa}(R^{\sharp},R^{\sharp+})
is an untilt of
S
over
\operatorname{Spa}E
,
y:\operatorname{Spf}R^{\sharp+}\to\mathfrak{X}
is a map of formal schemes which corresponds to a formal abelian scheme
\mathfrak{A}/R^{\sharp+}
with
G
-structure and infinite away-from-
p
level structure, and

\beta:T_{p}(\mathcal{A}_{S})\overset{\sim}{\to}\underline{\Lambda}_{S}

is a trivialization of
T_{p}(\mathcal{A}_{S})
, where
\pi_{S}:\mathcal{A}_{S}^{\diamond}\to S
is the adic generic fiber of
\mathfrak{A}^{\diamond}/\operatorname{Spa}(R^{+},R^{+})
and
T_{p}(\mathcal{A}_{S})
denotes the Tate module

\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}(R^{1}\pi_{S*}\underline{\mathbb{Z}_{p}},\underline{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}).

For passing from the hybrid space to the abelian-type Shimura variety later, we will also need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.9
.

The natural map
\mathcal{X}^{\circ}\to\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\circ}
is a pro-étale torsor with respect to the profinite group
\Delta:=\varprojlim_{K}\Delta(K)
.

Proof.

Since
(\mathcal{X}^{\circ}\to\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\circ})=\varprojlim_{K}(\mathcal{X}_{K}^{\circ}\to\mathcal{X}_{G,K}^{\circ})
, where the inverse limit runs over all uniformly good level structures
K\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
, we are reduced to checking that for such level subgroups,
\mathcal{X}_{K}^{\circ}\to\mathcal{X}_{G,K}^{\circ}
is a finite étale torsor with respect to the finite group
\Delta(K)
.

Firstly,
\mathcal{M}_{K_{hs}K^{p}}\to\mathcal{M}_{G,K_{hs}K^{p}}
is a finite étale
\Delta(K_{hs}K^{p})
-torsor because
K
is
uniformly
good. In fact, this is the key point where the uniformity condition is really necessary. Since
p
-adic completion, taking adic generic and taking diamonds commute with finite étale torsors (for the latter statement, see e.g.
[
Sch22
, 15.6]
), we find that

\mathcal{X}_{K_{hs}K^{p}}^{\circ}\to\mathcal{X}_{G,K_{hs}K^{p}}^{\circ}

is a finite étale torsor with respect to
\Delta(K_{hs}K^{p})
.

Similarly,
\mathcal{M}_{K}\to\mathcal{M}_{G,K}
is a finite étale
\Delta(K)
-torsor, and upon taking adic generic and taking diamonds,
\mathcal{X}_{K}\to\mathcal{X}_{G,K}
is a finite étale
\Delta(K)
-torsor. Finally, consider the following commutative cube.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{X}_{K}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{X}_{G,K}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{X}_{K_{hs}K^{p}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{X}_{G,K_{hs}K^{p}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{X}_{K}^{\circ}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{X}_{G,K}^{\circ}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{X}_{K_{hs}K^{p}}^{\circ}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{X}_{G,K_{hs}K^{p}}^{\circ}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta(K)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta(K_{hs}K^{p})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta(K_{hs}K^{p})}

The left and right vertical faces of the cube are Cartesian by definition. The bottom of the cube is Cartesian because it consists of two opposite maps that are finite étale torsors with respect to the same group. This formally implies that the top of the cube is also Cartesian, and hence that
\mathcal{X}_{K}^{\circ}\to\mathcal{X}_{G,K}^{\circ}
is a finite étale torsor with respect to
\Delta(K)
as desired.
∎

5.
p
-divisible groups

In this section we review the theory of
p
-divisible groups, their classification over various base rings via Dieudonné theory and their connection to deformations of abelian varieties via Serre-Tate theory. This will be of vital importance for us; just as in
[
Zha23
]
, the fiber product diagram for the hybrid space will end up being a geometric reformulation of Dieudonné and Serre-Tate theory.

5.1.
Definition and first properties

Let
S
be any scheme.

Definition 5.1
.

A
p
-divisible group
over
S
is an fpqc-sheaf of abelian groups that can be written as an inductive system

\mathcal{G}=\varinjlim G_{n}

indexed over
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where all
G_{n}
are finite locally free group schemes over
S
, such that there is a natural number
h
such that

(i)

for each
n
,
G_{n}
has degree
p^{hn}
over
S
.

(ii)

for each
n
we have an exact sequence

0\to G_{n}\overset{i_{n}}{\longrightarrow}G_{n+1}\overset{p^{n}}{\longrightarrow}G_{n}\to 0

The natural number
h
is called the
height
of the
p
-divisible group
\mathcal{G}
.

Note that there are canonical isomorphisms
\mathcal{G}[p^{n}]\simeq G_{n}
. From the definition we see that a
p
-divisible group
\mathcal{G}
is
p^{\infty}
-torsion and that multiplication by
p
is surjective.

Given a
p
-divisible group
\mathcal{G}
, its
dual
p
-divisible group

\mathcal{G}^{\vee}
is the fpqc-sheaf

T\mapsto\varinjlim\mathcal{G}[p^{n}]^{\vee}(T),

where
\mathcal{G}[p^{n}]^{\vee}
is the Cartier dual of
\mathcal{G}[p^{n}]
, with transition maps given by the duals of multiplication by
p
. Clearly
\mathcal{G}^{\vee}
is again a
p
-divisible group.

Definition 5.2
.

An
isogeny

\lambda:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
between
p
-divisible groups over
S
is a surjection of fpqc-sheaves whose kernel is representable by a finite locally free group scheme. We will write
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G}^{\prime})
for the fpqc-sheaf of isogenies between
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
.

A
quasi-isogeny
is a global section
\rho
of the sheaf
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G}^{\prime})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
, such that Zariski-locally on
S
,
p^{n}\rho
is an isogeny for some integer
n
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a
p
-divisible group. A
polarization
of
\mathcal{G}
is a quasi-isogeny
\lambda:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}^{\vee}
such that the Cartier dual
\lambda^{\vee}:\mathcal{G}\cong\mathcal{G}^{\vee\vee}\to\mathcal{G}^{\vee}
is equal to
-\lambda
. It is called a
principal polarization
if
\lambda
is an isomorphism.

Example 5.4
.

(i)

The sheaf
\underline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}_{S}
is a
p
-divisible group of height 1. Its dual is the
p
-divisible group

\mu_{p^{\infty}}:=\varinjlim\mu_{p^{n}},

which is again of height one.

(ii)

If
A
is an abelian scheme over
S
of relative dimension
d
, then

A[p^{\infty}]:=\varinjlim_{n}A[p^{n}]

is a
p
-divisible group of height
2d
. Its dual is the
p
-divisible group
A^{\vee}[p^{\infty}]
.

The pairing between
A[p^{\infty}]
and
A^{\vee}[p^{\infty}]
(or rather their
p^{n}
-torsions) is precisely the Weil pairing. When
A
is principally polarized, then
A[p^{\infty}]
is self-dual via this principal polarization. In general a polarization
\lambda
on
A
induces a polarization of its
p
-divisible group
A[p^{\infty}]
.

Definition 5.5
.

The (integral)
Tate module
of a
p
-divisible group
\mathcal{G}/S
is the fpqc-sheaf
T_{p}\mathcal{G}:=\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{G}[p^{n}]
, where the transition maps are given by multiplication by
p
. It is a sheaf of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules and can be identified with the Hom-sheaf in the category of sheaves of abelian groups on
S_{\text{fpqc}}

T_{p}\mathcal{G}\simeq\mathcal{H}om(\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathcal{G}).

Since
T_{p}\mathcal{G}
is an inverse limit of schemes affine over
S
, it is representable by a scheme that is affine over
S
and additionally flat over
S
, since all
\mathcal{G}[p^{n}]
are flat over
S
.

We will mostly be interested in
p
-divisible groups
\mathcal{G}
that are defined over a
p
-adically complete
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebra
R
. In this case, we may intepret
\mathcal{G}
as an fpqc-sheaf on
\operatorname{Nilp}_{R}^{\operatorname{op}}
, which is the opposite category of
R
-algebras on which
p
is nilpotent, as follows:

\operatorname{Nilp}_{R}^{\operatorname{op}}\ni A\mapsto\varprojlim_{i}\text{ }\varinjlim_{n}\mathcal{G}[p^{n}](A/p^{i}).

Definition 5.6
.

The
formal completion

\hat{\mathcal{G}}
of a
p
-divisible group
\mathcal{G}
over a
p
-adically complete
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebra
R
is the fpqc-sheaf on
\operatorname{Nilp}_{R}^{\operatorname{op}}

A\mapsto\varinjlim_{k}\{x\in\mathcal{G}(A):x=e_{\mathcal{G}}\text{ in }\mathcal{G}(A/I)\text{, for an ideal }I\subset A\text{ such that }I^{k+1}=0\}

Proposition 5.7

(
[
SW13
]
, 3.1.2)
.

\hat{\mathcal{G}}
is a formal Lie (group) variety, and it is hence represented by an affine formal scheme over
R
, which Zariski locally on
R
is isomorphic to

\operatorname{Spf}R[[X_{1},...X_{d}]]

for some integer
d\geq 0
, called the dimension of
\mathcal{G}
relative to
R
.

Definition 5.8
.

The
Lie algebra
of
\mathcal{G}
is the fpqc-sheaf of
R
-modules
Lie(\mathcal{G}):=Lie(\hat{\mathcal{G}})
, defined to be the dual of the (Zariski) locally free
R
-module of rank
d

\omega_{\mathcal{G}}:=e_{\hat{\mathcal{G}}}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{\hat{\mathcal{G}}/R}.

By
\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G}
we will denote its global sections, which is a finite locally free
R
-module.

Many of the
p
-divisible groups that we will encounter will have extra structure which is coming from abelian varieties with
G
-structure.

Definition 5.9
.

Let
S
be an
\mathcal{O}_{E}
-scheme, then we call a triple
(\mathcal{G},\iota,\lambda)
a
p
-divisible group with
G
-structure
over
S
if

•

\mathcal{G}
is a
p
-divisible group over
S
,

•

\iota:\mathcal{O}_{B}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\operatorname{End}(\mathcal{G})
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-linear map that satifies the Kottwitz determinant condition

\operatorname{det}_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}(\iota(a)|\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal{G}))=\operatorname{det}(a|V_{1,\mathbb{Q}_{p}})

for any

, and

•

\lambda:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}^{\vee}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{p}
-linear polarization.

A
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-isomorphism

\mathcal{O}_{p}

\mathcal{O}_{B}
-linear isomorphism

\underline{\mathcal{O}_{p}^{\times}}(S)
. A
\mathbb{Q}_{p}

\mathcal{O}_{B}

Note that if
\mathcal{G}

5.2.
Classification over
\mathcal{O}_{C}

Let
C
be a complete non-archimedean algebraically closed field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{C}
. Here we recall Scholze-Weinstein’s classification of
p
-divisible groups over
\mathcal{O}_{C}
in terms of linear algebraic data, similar in spirit to Riemann’s classification of complex abelian varieties in terms of lattices and Riemann forms.

Theorem 5.10

(
[
SW20
]
, 12.1.1)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a
p
-divisible group over
\mathcal{O}_{C}
. There is a natural short exact sequence

0\to\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}C(1)\overset{\alpha_{G^{*}}^{*}(1)}{\longrightarrow}T_{p}\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O}_{C})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}C\overset{\alpha_{G}}{\longrightarrow}(\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G}^{\vee})^{*}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}C\to 0,

called the
Hodge-Tate filtration
.

We recall how to construct the map
\alpha_{G}
. Given a section
f
of
T_{p}\mathcal{G}
, we may view it as a homomorphism
f:\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\mathcal{G}
and apply the Lie algebra functor to its dual
f^{\vee}:\mathcal{G}^{\vee}\to\mu_{p^{\infty}}
to obtain the map
\operatorname{Lie}(f^{\vee}):\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal{G}^{\vee})\to\operatorname{Lie}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})
. After picking a coordinate
t
of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
, the
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-linear dual
\operatorname{Lie}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})^{*}
is naturally trivialized and isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{C}\frac{dt}{t}
, and we may define
\alpha_{G}
via the assignment

f\mapsto(\operatorname{Lie}f^{\vee})^{*}(\frac{dt}{t}).

Next, let
\{(T,W)\}
be the category of pairs consisting of a finite free
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module
T
and a
C
-sub-vector space
W\subset T\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}C(-1)
, with the obvious notion of morphisms. We define the dual of the pair
(T,W)
to be the pair
(T^{*}(1),W^{\perp})
, where the orthogonal complement is with respect to the natural pairing between
T\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}C(-1)
and
T^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}C
. Then the main classification result is the following.

Theorem 5.11

(
[
SW13
]
, Theorem B, 5.2.1)
.

The category of
p
-divisible groups over
\mathcal{O}_{C}
is equivalent to the above category
\{(T,W)\}
via the functor

\Psi:\mathcal{G}\mapsto(T_{p}\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O}_{C}),\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}C),

where
\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}C
is viewed as a
C
-sub-vector space of
T_{p}\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O}_{C})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}C(-1)
via the Hodge-Tate filtration. This equivalence is compatible with duality.

Proof.

For convenience, we recall the construction of the inverse functor in the case that
C
is spherically complete (i.e. the intersection of every decreasing sequence of balls is non-empty) and the norm map
C\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is surjective.

In this case, given a tuple
(T,W)
, we define the
p
-divisible group

\mathcal{G}^{\prime}:=T(-1)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mu_{p^{\infty}}

and obtain the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{W\otimes_{C}\mathbb{G}_{a}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{G}^{\prime\operatorname{ad}}_{\eta}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{T(-1)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{G}_{a},}}

where
\mathbb{G}_{a}
is the sheafification of the functor
(A,A^{+})\mapsto A^{+}
on complete affinoid
(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})
-algebras. The vertical arrow is induced by the given inclusion
W\hookrightarrow T\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}C(-1)
, while the horizontal arrow is the logarithm on
\mathcal{G}_{\eta}^{\prime\operatorname{ad}}
. Note that the logarithm usually takes the form
\mathcal{G}^{\prime\operatorname{ad}}_{\eta}\to\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}\mathbb{G}_{a}
, but since
\operatorname{Lie}\mu_{p^{\infty}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{C}
,
\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is naturally in
T
identified with
T(-1)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}C
.

Denote by
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{ad}}_{\eta}
the sheaf-theoretic fiber product of the above diagram. Using results from
[
Far19
]
, one can show under our additional assumptions on
C
that the formal scheme

\mathcal{G}:=\coprod_{Y}\operatorname{Spf}H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}^{+})

is a
p
-divisible group over
\mathcal{O}_{C}
, where
Y
runs over all connected components of
\mathcal{G}_{\eta}^{\operatorname{ad}}
. This defines the inverse functor
(T,W)\mapsto\mathcal{G}
.
∎

5.3.
Descent properties

Similar to the approach in
[
Zha23
]
, we will need certain descent properties for
p
-divisible groups in order to obtain the fiber product diagram for our hybrid spaces. We collect the necessary results from
[
Zha23
, Section 3.3]
.

Lemma 5.12

(
[
Zha23
]
, Lemma 3.14)
.

Given a so-called Milnor square, i.e. a Cartesian diagram of rings

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{R_{2}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{R_{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{R_{3}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ulcorner}

with
R_{1}\twoheadrightarrow R_{3}
surjective, the corresponding diagram of categories of finite locally free modules over these rings is
2
-Cartesian. Concretely, the category of finite locally free
R
-modules is equivalent to the category of gluing data

\{M_{1}/R_{1},M_{2}/R_{2},\alpha:M_{1}\otimes_{R_{1}}R_{3}\cong M_{2}\otimes_{R_{2}}R_{3}\},

where
M_{i}
is a finite locally free module over
R_{i}
, and
\alpha
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Given a finite locally free
R
-module, we can base change it to
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
and take
\alpha
to be the identity. Conversely, given a gluing triple
(M_{1},M_{2},\alpha)
we obtain an
R
-module
M
via

M:=\operatorname{ker}(M_{1}\oplus M_{2}\overset{\alpha-\operatorname{id}}{\longrightarrow}M_{2}\otimes_{R_{2}}R_{3}).\qed

Proposition 5.13

(
[
Zha23
]
, 3.16)
.

Given a Milnor square as in Lemma
5.12
, the corresponding diagram of categories of
p
-divisible groups over these rings is
2
-Cartesian.

Proof.

Given two
p
-divisible groups
\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G}
over
R
, we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G}^{\prime})=\varprojlim_{n}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{G}[p^{n}],\mathcal{G}^{\prime}[p^{n}]).

For each
n
,
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{G}[p^{n}],\mathcal{G}^{\prime}[p^{n}])
is given by maps of
R
-modules
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}[p^{n}])\to\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{G}[p^{n}])
that respect the additional Hopf algebra structure. Since all the Hopf algebra structure is given in terms of morphisms of
R
-modules, Lemma
5.12
shows that giving a map in
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{G}[p^{n}],\mathcal{G}^{\prime}[p^{n}])
is equivalent to giving morphisms on the base changes to
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
that agree over
R_{3}
. After passing to the inverse limit, this shows fully faithfulness.

For essential surjectivity, we argue as follows. Given a gluing triple
(\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2},\alpha)
of
p
-divisible groups, we can first restrict to
p^{n}
-torsion points for each
n
to obtain the ring of regular functions
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{G}[p^{n}])
by Lemma
5.12
and endow it with a Hopf algebra structure. Fully faithfulness shows that there are transition maps turning
\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{G}[p^{n}]
into a
p
-divisible group that restricts to
\mathcal{G}_{1}
resp.
\mathcal{G}_{2}
.
∎

Lemma 5.14

(
[
Zha23
]
, 3.17)
.

Let
R=\prod_{i\in I}V_{i}
be a product of valuation rings, and let
n
be an integer. Then the category of rank
n
projective
R
-modules is equivalent to the collection of categories of rank
n
projective
V_{i}
-modules.

Proof.

Any rank
n
projective
R
-module yields a collection of rank
n
projective
V_{i}
-modules via base change. Conversely, given a collection
\{M_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of rank
n
projective (also automatically free)
V_{i}
-modules, we can set
M:=\prod_{i\in I}M_{i}
, which is also free. These two functors are clearly inverse to each other.
∎

Corollary 5.15

(
[
Zha23
]
, 3.18)
.

In the setting of Lemma
5.14
, the category of
p
-divisible groups over
R
of a fixed height is equivalent to the collection of
p
-divisible groups of the same height over each
V_{i}
.

5.4.
Dieudonné modules

In this subsection we recall the necessary background in Dieudonné theory. We will focus on prismatic Dieudonné theory as developed in
[

, since all classification results we will actually need are subsumed in this theory.

The base rings of interest are so-called

-complete rings with bounded

Theorem 5.22

(
[
CS24
]
, 2.4.2)
.

Let
S^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow S
be a surjection of rings in which
p
is nilpotent, such that its kernel

is nilpotent. Then the functor

A_{S^{\prime}}\mapsto(A_{S},A_{S^{\prime}}[p^{\infty}],\operatorname{id})

is an equivalence between the category of abelian schemes over
S^{\prime}
and the category of triples consisting of an abelian scheme
A_{S}
over
S
, a
p
-divisible group
\mathcal{G}_{S^{\prime}}
over
S^{\prime}
and an isomorphism
\rho:A_{S}[p^{\infty}]\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathcal{G}_{S^{\prime}}\times_{S^{\prime}}S
.

Proof.

Let
i:\operatorname{Spec}(S)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}(S^{\prime})
denote the inclusion and fix
N\gg 0
such that
(p,I)^{N}=0
both in
S^{\prime}
.

First, we prove fully faithfulness. Assume we are given two abelian schemes
A_{0},B_{0}
over
S
with lifts
A,B
over
S^{\prime}
. We will view all these abelian schemes as fpqc-sheaves over their respective base. Denote by
K_{B}
the kernel of the reduction map
B\to i_{*}B_{0}
. Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{S^{\prime}}(A,-)
, we get the exact sequence

\operatorname{Hom}_{S^{\prime}}(A,K_{B})\to\operatorname{Hom}_{S^{\prime}}(A,B)\overset{res}{\to}\operatorname{Hom}_{S}(A_{0},B_{0})\to\operatorname{Ext}_{S^{\prime}}^{1}(A,K_{B}).

In order to prove fully faithfulness, we analyze
\operatorname{Hom}_{S^{\prime}}(A,K_{B})
and
\operatorname{Ext}_{S^{\prime}}^{1}(A,K_{B})
. For this, we consider the multiplication-by-
p^{N}
short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to A[p^{N}]\to A\overset{p^{N}}{\to}A\to 0

Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{S^{\prime}}(-,K_{B})
to the above, we get the long exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\operatorname{Hom}_{S^{\prime}}(A,K_{B})

\displaystyle\overset{p^{N}}{\to}\operatorname{Hom}_{S^{\prime}}(A,K_{B})\to\operatorname{Hom}_{S^{\prime}}(A[p^{N}],K_{B})

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{Ext}_{S^{\prime}}^{1}(A,K_{B})\overset{p^{N}}{\to}\operatorname{Ext}_{S^{\prime}}^{1}(A,K_{B})

Since
K_{B}
is killed by
p^{N}
, the two arrows labelled by
p^{N}
are zero. First, this implies that
\operatorname{Hom}_{S^{\prime}}(A,K_{B})=0
and thus the functor is faithful. Second, we see that
\operatorname{Hom}_{S^{\prime}}(A[p^{N}],K_{B})\overset{\sim}{\to}\operatorname{Ext}_{S^{\prime}}^{1}(A,K_{B})
, which implies that for any map

,

is always liftable to some

. When applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{S^{\prime}}(-,B)
to the multplication-by-
p^{N}
sequence, one sees that

itself is liftable if
f^{\prime}
annihilates
A[p^{N}]
. To finish the proof of fullness, note that we are additionally given a map
f[p^{\infty}]:A[p^{\infty}]\to B[p^{\infty}]
that lifts
f_{0}[p^{\infty}]
. Thus by part (1) of Theorem
5.21
, it is necessary that
f^{\prime}
induces
p^{N}f[p^{\infty}]:A[p^{\infty}]\to B[p^{\infty}]
. In particular,
f^{\prime}
annihilates
A[p^{N}]
as desired.

Next, we show essential surjectivity. Assume we are given an abelian scheme
A_{0}
over
S
together with a
p
-divisible group
\mathcal{G}
over
S^{\prime}
and an isomorphism
A_{0}[p^{\infty}]\cong\mathcal{G}\times_{S^{\prime}}S
. According to Theorem
5.21
, we may pick an arbitrary lift
A^{\prime}
of
A_{0}
over
S
(which is unique up to
p
-power isogeny). Without loss of generality, we may assume that the map
A^{\prime}_{S}\to A_{0}
is an actual isogeny (instead of just a quasi-isogeny). By Theorem
5.21
, the induced isogeny
A^{\prime}_{S}[p^{\infty}]\to A_{0}[p^{\infty}]
lifts to an isogeny
\varphi:A^{\prime}[p^{\infty}]\to\mathcal{G}
over
S^{\prime}
. We define

, and then

and

still lifts
A_{0}
.
∎

7.1.
The Fargues-Fontaine curve and vector bundles

Definition 7.1
.

For any affinoid perfectoid space
S=\operatorname{Spa}(R,R^{+})\in\operatorname{Perf}
with pseudo-uniformizer
\varpi\in R^{+}
, recall that the
relative Fargues-Fontaine curve over

(i)

Let

(ii)

The

attached to

has the formula
X_{S}^{\diamond}\cong(S^{\diamond}\times\operatorname{Spd}\mathbb{Q}_{p})/(\varphi{Z}\times\operatorname{id})

(iii)

The

\mathcal{O}_{X_{S}}(1)

descending from

The above constructions glue and thus yield adic resp. diamond resp. algebraic Fargues-Fontaine curves over any perfectoid space

.

Let
S=\operatorname{Spa}(R,R^{+})\in\operatorname{Perf}
and let
B_{\operatorname{cris},S}^{+}:=A_{\operatorname{cris}}(R^{+})[\frac{1}{p}]

This observation will become useful later, as it will allow us construct vector bundles on the Fargues-Fontaine curve from the rational crystalline Dieudonné module attached to a
p
-divisible group.

From now on we use the following notation: For a perfectoid space
S=\operatorname{Spa}(R,R^{+})\in\operatorname{Perf}

\mathcal{Y}(S):=\operatorname{Spa}(W(R^{+}),W(R^{+}))\backslash\{[\varpi]=0\}.

For any interval

\mathcal{Y}_{I}(S):=\{|p|^{b}\leq|[\varpi]|\leq|p|^{a}\}\subset\mathcal{Y}(S),

and similarly for open intervals
(a,b)

By
[
SW20
, 11.2.1]

\mathcal{Y}_{S}
is an analytic adic space. It is covered by rational affinoid sousperfectoid subspaces indexed over

which are of the form

\operatorname{Spa}(R_{n},R_{n}^{+})=\{|p|\leq|[\varpi]|^{\frac{1}{p^{n}}}\},

where
R_{n}^{+}
is the
[\varpi]
-adic completion of the ring
W(R^{+})[p/[\varpi^{\frac{1}{p^{n}}}]]
, and
R_{n}=R_{n}^{+}[1/[\varpi]]
. Each element in

can be written as

\sum_{i\geq 0}[r_{i}]\biggl(\frac{p}{[\varpi]^{\frac{1}{p^{n}}}}\biggl)^{i}

with
R\ni r_{i}\to 0
, which visibily does not depend on

. Thus the category of vector bundles on

does not depend on the choice of

. By
[
KL15
, 2.7.7]

and

The following GAGA-type theorem relates vector bundles on

and

Theorem 7.2

(
[
FS21
]
, II.2.7)
.

Pullback along the natural morphism

of locally ringed spaces induces an equivalence of categories between the categories of vector bundles on
X_{S}
and
X_{S}^{\operatorname{alg}}
.

Importantly, Cartier divisors on
X_{S}
classify Frobenius orbits of untilts of
S
as follows. Assume

is an untilt of
S
, which is locally of the form
\operatorname{Spa}(R^{\sharp},R^{\sharp+})
. Then each kernel of the surjection induced from Fontaine’s theta map

W(R^{+})\cong W(R^{\sharp\flat+})\overset{\theta}{\twoheadrightarrow}R^{\sharp+}

is a principal ideal generated by an element of the form
p-a[\varpi]
for some

. The induced maps
\operatorname{Spa}(R^{\sharp},R^{\sharp+})\to Y_{\operatorname{Spa}(R,R^{+})}

. This is cut out by a global section of

\mathcal{O}(1)
on
X_{S}^{\operatorname{alg}}

If

can be covered by any two principal opens

and

is cut out by

Let

is an
\hat{R}
-module on which

M_{2}
is an

For any

\mathcal{F}

.

7.3.

We now return to our initial PEL setup and consider the v-stacks

Namely, given a

-torsor on

with an

-module structure and a symplectic pairing

that is
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-bilinear,
\mathbb{Q}_{p}

for any local sections

and

, i.e. by Lemma

it is liftable along the trace

to an

-bilinear,

-valued symplectic pairing

-torsor on

, we can interpret it as a vector bundle
\mathscr{E}
on

with an
\mathcal{O}_{B}
-module structure and a symplectic pairing

that is

-bilinear and

-valued. Morphisms between

-torsors are then morphisms between vector bundles that are compatible with the

-module structure and that are

-symplectic similitudes.

In both cases, we obtain the vector bundle from the associated torsor by pushforwarding it along the canonical faithful representation

. To get the torsor back from the vector bundle, we can consider isomorphism sheaves
\underline{\operatorname{Isom}}_{G^{*}}(-,\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathcal{O}_{X_{S}})

We can pushforward

-torsors on

along the embedding

to obtain

-torsors. In terms of vector bundles, this corresponds precisely to lifting the pairing

along the trace

to the pairing

with the trace

to get a

-torsor back. However, this will of course not define a map

-symplectic similitudes with respect to the lifted pairings do not induce

Lemma 8.6

(

Zha23
]

Let

We recall the proof. Since the map

8.2.
Abelian-type Igusa stack

We now want to construct the Igusa stack for our abelian-type Shimura variety.

First, we note that there is a well-defined polarization action on the presheaf

S\mapsto\operatorname{Igs}^{\operatorname{pre}}(S)/\cong

on
\operatorname{Perf}
. Namely, given a point
(S^{\sharp},\mathfrak{A})
of
\operatorname{Igs}^{\operatorname{pre}}(S)
, we can realize
\mathfrak{A}
as an inverse limit of formal abelian schemes with
G
-structure and increasing tame level structure. At level
K_{hs}K^{p}
, the polarization action on the abelian scheme factors through the finite group
\Delta(K_{hs}K^{p})
. Thus, in the limit we obtain a polarization action of the profinite group

\Delta_{hs}:=\varprojlim_{K^{p}}\Delta(K_{hs}K^{p}),

and after v-sheafification we have an action by the same profinite group on the v-sheaf
\operatorname{Igs}
. By pulling back along the obvious quotient map
\Delta\twoheadrightarrow\Delta_{hs}
, we also obtain an action on
\operatorname{Igs}
by the full profinite group
\Delta
.

These considerations lead us to defining the (infinite level) Igusa stack for our abelian-type Shimura variety as follows.

Definition 8.12
.

The
abelian-type Igusa stack
is the quotient stack in the category of v-stacks

\operatorname{Igs}_{G}:=[\operatorname{Igs}/\underline{\Delta}]

The next two easy lemmas provide the necessary maps from and into
\operatorname{Igs}_{G}
.

Lemma 8.13
.

The previously constructed map
\overline{\pi}_{HT}:\operatorname{Igs}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
factors through the polarization action by
\underline{\Delta}
.

Proof.

It suffices to check invariance of
\overline{\pi}_{HT}:\operatorname{Igs}^{\operatorname{pre}}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
with respect to the action of
\underline{\Delta_{hs}}
. But one checks immediately from the construction of
\overline{\pi}_{HT}
that changing the polarization by totally positive units will change the symplectic pairing of the vector bundle
\mathscr{E}(A)
by the same totally positive unit, but this does not alter the isomorphism class of the corresponding
G
-bundle.
∎

Lemma 8.14
.

The map
q:\mathcal{X}^{\circ}\to\operatorname{Igs}
induces a map
q:\mathcal{X}^{\circ}_{G}\to\operatorname{Igs}_{G}
.

Proof.

It suffices to see that
q:\mathcal{X}^{\circ,\operatorname{pre}}\to\operatorname{Igs}^{\operatorname{pre}}
is equivariant with respect to the action by
\Delta
, but this is obvious.
∎

With the hard work already been done, we can now establish the rational fiber product conjecture for our abelian-type Shimura variety.

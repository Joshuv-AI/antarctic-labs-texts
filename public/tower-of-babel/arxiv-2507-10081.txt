Abstract
. We investigate Chevalley bases for extended affine Lie algebras of type A 1 A_{1} . The concept of integral structures for extended affine Lie algebras of rank greater than one has been successfully explored in recent years. However, for the rank one it has turned out that the situation becomes more delicate. In this work, we consider A 1 A_{1} -type extended affine Lie algebras of nullity 2 2 , known as elliptic extended affine Lie algebras. These Lie algebras are build using the Tits-Kantor-Koecher (TKK) construction by applying some specific Jordan algebras: the plus algebra of a quantum torus, the Hermitian Jordan algebra of the ring of Laurent polynomials equipped with an involution, and the Jordan algebra associated with a semilattice. By examining these ingredient we determine appropriate bases for null spaces of the corresponding elliptic extended affine Lie algebra leading to the establishment of Chevalley bases for these Lie algebras.

Introduction

In this work, we investigate the construction of Chevalley bases for elliptic extended affine Lie algebras (EALAs) of type
A_{1}
. A Chevalley basis, a concept introduced by Claude Chevalley, plays a central role in the structure theory of Lie algebras; see
[
10

,
[
18
]
,
[
9
]
and
[
13

The importance of Chevalley bases extends beyond classical theory, providing the first unified construction of both classical and exceptional groups over arbitrary fields. This idea was later generalized to the theory of infinite-dimensional Kac-Moody algebras; see, for example,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
and
[
15
]
. In recent years and in the context of modular theory, the concepts of Chevalley bases and integral structures have been explored for the class of EALAs; see
[
3
]

[
4
]

Extended affine Lie algebras (EALAs) over the filed
{\mathbb{K}}
of complex numbers were introduced in 1990 by
[
14
]
and were systematically studied in
[
1
]
. These Lie algebras expand on the properties of finite-dimensional simple and affine Lie algebras. EALAs capture the symmetry structures that extend beyond the scope of classical and affine Lie algebras. The construction of an EALA involves an extended Cartan subalgebra that leads to a root space decomposition, along with central and derivational elements. A method developed by Erhard Neher
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
provides a framework for constructing any (tame) extended affine Lie algebra starting from a centerless Lie torus and adding appropriate central and derivation elements. The nullity
\nu
of an EALA is defined as the dimension of the real span of isotropic roots.

As was mentioned in the previous paragraph, Lie tori are essential in the construction of EALAs. They in fact characterize the cores of extended affine Lie algebras. More precisely, the core modulo center of an extended affine Lie algebra is a Lie torus, and conversely any centerless Lie torus is isomorphic to the core modulo center of an EALA.
It is known that centerless Lie tori associated to
A_{1}
-type EALAs can be obtained by Tits-Kantor-Koecher-construction (TKK-construction) that
assigns a Lie algebra to a Jordan algebra.
According to

•

\mathbb{K}^{+}_{\mathbf{q}}

•

•

2.1.
Extended affine root systems

We recall the definition of an extended affine root system from
[

]

Definition 2.1.1
.

An

({\mathcal{V}},(\cdot,\cdot),R)
where
{\mathcal{V}}
is a finite dimensional real vector space,
(\cdot,\cdot)
is a symmetric positive semi-definite bilinear form, and

(R1)

,

(R2)
R=-R
,

(R3)

spans
{\mathcal{V}}
,

(R4)
\alpha\in R^{\times}\Rightarrow 2\alpha\not\in R
,

(R5)
R
is discrete in
{\mathcal{V}}
,

(R6) the
root string property
: for
\alpha\in R^{\times}
and
\beta\in R
, there exist non-negative integers

such that

\{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\mid\beta+i\alpha\in R\}=\{-d,\ldots,u\}

with
(\beta,\alpha^{\vee})=d-u
,

(R7) elements of
R^{0}
are
non-isolated
, i.e., for
\sigma\in R^{0}
there exists
\alpha\in R^{\times}
with
\alpha+\sigma\in R
.

(R8)
R^{\times}
is
connected
, meaning that
R^{\times}
cannot be written in the form
R^{\times}=R_{1}\cup R_{2}
with

(R_{1},R_{2})=\{0\}
where
R_{1}\not=\emptyset
and
R_{2}\not=\emptyset
.

2.1.2
.

Let
(R,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{V}})
be an extended affine root system. Define
\bar{\mathcal{V}}:={\mathcal{V}}/{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
, with
\bar{\;}:{\mathcal{V}}\rightarrow\bar{{\mathcal{V}}}
as the canonical map.
Then,
\bar{R}
, the image of

under
\bar{\;}
, is an irreducible finite root system in
\bar{{\mathcal{V}}}
, with respect to the form induced by
(\cdot,\cdot)
on
\bar{{\mathcal{V}}}
. The
type
and

of
R
are defined as those of
\bar{R}
, and the

of

is defined as the dimension of

. Throughout this work,

is
always of reduced type
.

It follows that

contains a finite root system

that is isomorphic to
\bar{R}
under

. Let
\dot{R}_{sh}
and

, respectively. Then the root system

can be expressed as

for certain subsets

and

, referred to as

. We recall that a semilattice

. If

is simply laced, we treat all roots as short, meaning

When

is of type

[

]

2.2.
Extended affine Lie algebras

We briefly recall the definition and some properties of extended affine Lie algebras.

Definition 2.2.1
.

An
extended affine Lie algebra
is a triple
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
where
E
is a Lie algebra,
{\mathcal{H}}
is a subalgebra of
E
and
(\cdot,\cdot)
is a bilinear form on
E
satisfying the following five axioms:

(EA1) The form
(\cdot,\cdot)
on

is symmetric, non-degenerate and invariant.

(EA2)
{\mathcal{H}}
is a finite dimensional splitting Cartan subalgebra of
E
. This means that
E=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}}E_{\alpha}
where
E_{\alpha}=\{x\in E\mid[h,x]=\alpha(h)x\hbox{ for all }h\in{\mathcal{H}}\}
and
E_{0}={\mathcal{H}}
.

Let
R
be the set of roots of

, that is,
R=\{\alpha\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}\mid E_{\alpha}\neq\{0\}\}
.
Axioms (EA1)-(EA2) imply that the restriction of
(\cdot,\cdot)
to
{\mathcal{H}}
is non-degenerate, allowing it to transferred
to
{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}
via
(\alpha,\beta):=(t_{\alpha},t_{\beta})
where

is the unique element satisfying
\alpha(h)=(h,t_{\alpha})
,

.
Let

R^{0}=\{\alpha\in R\mid(\alpha,\alpha)=0\}\quad\hbox{and}\quad R^{\times}=R\setminus R^{0}.

Then
R=R^{0}\uplus R^{\times}
is regarded as the decomposition of roots into

isotropic
and
non-isotropic
roots, respectively. Let
V:=\hbox{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}R

and
{\mathcal{V}}^{0}:=\hbox{Span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}R^{0}
.

(EA3) For
\alpha\in R^{\times}
, the adjoint action
\hbox{ad}x
is locally nilpotent for
x\in E_{\alpha}
.

(EA4) The
{\mathbb{Z}}
-span of
R
is a full lattice in
{\mathcal{V}}
, meaning that
{\mathcal{V}}
has a
{\mathbb{R}}
-basis which is also a basis for the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-span of
R
.

(EA5)
R
is irreducible, that is,
R^{\times}
is connected and elements of
R^{0}
are non-isolated.

Here we record some basic facts about the extended affine Lie algebra
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
relevant to this work. For a detailed study of extended affine Lie algebras and their root systems, refer to
[
1
]
and
[
17
]
. Let

be the root system of

. It turns out that

is an extended affine root system in the sense of definition

. The

, and

From

[E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha}]={\mathbb{K}}t_{\alpha},\quad(\alpha\in R),

(2.2)

(E_{\alpha},E_{\beta})=\{0\}\hbox{ unless }\alpha+\beta=0,\quad(\alpha,\beta\in R).

Also from
[

The

Lemma 2.2.2
.

For
\sigma\in R^{0}
and
\alpha,\alpha+\sigma\in R^{\times}
, we have

([x_{\alpha+\sigma},x_{-\alpha}],[x_{-\alpha-\sigma},x_{\alpha}])\not=0.

Proof.
From the Jacobi identity, the fact that

is not a root, and invariance of the form, we have

\displaystyle([x_{\alpha+\sigma},x_{-\alpha}],[x_{-\alpha-\sigma},x_{\alpha}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{\alpha+\sigma},[x_{-\alpha},[x_{-\alpha-\sigma},x_{\alpha}]])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(x_{\alpha+\sigma},[x_{-\alpha-\sigma},[x_{\alpha},x_{-\alpha}]])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(x_{\alpha+\sigma},[x_{-\alpha-\sigma},t_{\alpha}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-([x_{\alpha+\sigma},x_{-\alpha-\sigma}],t_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(t_{\alpha+\sigma},t_{\alpha})=-(\alpha,\alpha)\not=0.

\Box

3.1.
Lie tori

Assume that
\Delta
is an irreducible finite root system and
\Lambda
is a free abelian group of finite rank.

Definition 3.1.1
.

A Lie
\Lambda
-torus of type

is a Lie algebra

over

that satisfies the following conditions (LT1)-(LT4):

(

)

has a compatible

-grading

m with

[\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}^{\lambda},\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}^{\mu}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha+\beta}^{\lambda+\mu}.

(

) For

\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}={\mathbb{K}}e_{\alpha}^{\lambda},\quad\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}^{-\lambda}={\mathbb{K}}f_{\alpha}^{\lambda},\quad\hbox{and}\quad[[e_{\alpha}^{\lambda},f_{\alpha}^{\lambda}],x^{\mu}_{\beta}]=\langle\beta,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle x_{\beta}^{\mu}

\mu\in\Lambda
,

(
LT3
) For

\mathfrak{g}_{0}^{\lambda}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta^{\times},\mu\in\Lambda}[\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}^{\mu},\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}^{\lambda-\mu}]

(

The rank and the

3.2.
A construction of extended affine Lie algebras

Assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is a centerless
\Lambda
-Lie torus.
Recall that the

of
\mathfrak{g}
, denoted
\hbox{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})
, is the set of linear endomorphisms that commute with left and right multiplications by elements of
\mathfrak{g}
, i.e.,

\hbox{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})=\{\chi\in\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g})\mid\chi([x,y])=[\chi(x),y]=[x,\chi(y)],\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}\}.

One knows from
[
16
]
that

(3.1)

\hbox{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}{\mathbb{K}}\chi^{\mu},

where
\Gamma
is the subgroup of
\Lambda
consisting of
\lambda\in\Lambda
for which
\hbox{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}\neq 0
. The group
\Gamma
is called the
central grading group
of
\mathfrak{g}
.
\chi^{\mu}
acts on
\mathfrak{g}
as an endomorphism of degree
\mu
with the property that
\chi^{\mu}\chi^{\nu}=\chi^{\mu+\nu}
.

For
\theta\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{K}})
, the
degree derivation

\partial_{\theta}
of

is defined by

\partial_{\theta}(x^{\lambda})=\theta(\lambda)x^{\lambda}\;\;\text{for}\;\lambda\in\Lambda,x^{\lambda}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\lambda}.

We denote by
\mathcal{D}
the set of all degree derivations, and by
\mathrm{CDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})

the set of
centroidal derivations
of
\mathfrak{g}
, namely

\mathrm{CDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g}):=\hbox{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})\mathcal{D}=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}\chi^{\mu}\mathcal{D},

which is a
\Gamma
-graded subalgebra of the derivation algebra

\mathrm{Der}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})
of
\mathfrak{g}
with

(3.2)

[\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta},\chi^{\nu}\partial_{\psi}]=\chi^{\mu+\nu}(\theta(\nu)\partial_{\psi}-\psi(\mu)\partial_{\theta}).

Next, the set

\displaystyle\mathrm{SCDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{d\in\mathrm{CDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})\mid(d(x),x)_{\mathfrak{g}}=0\;\text{for all}\;x\in\mathfrak{g}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}\mathrm{SCDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\mu}=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}\chi^{\mu}\{\partial_{\theta}\in\mathcal{D}\mid\theta(\mu)=0\},

is a
\Gamma
-graded subalgebra of
\mathrm{CDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})
, called the algebra of

of
\mathfrak{g}
. Note that

For a graded subalgebra

of

denote its graded dual by

D^{gr^{\star}}=\sum_{\mu\in\Gamma}(D^{\mu})^{\star}

(D^{gr^{\star}})^{\mu}=(D^{-\mu})^{\star}
, and consider it as a

(d.\varphi)(d^{{}^{\prime}})=\varphi([d^{{}^{\prime}},d])\>\text{for}\;d^{{}^{\prime}},d\in D,\varphi\in D^{gr*},

where

is viewed as a linear map on

by
\varphi|_{D^{\nu}}=0
for
\nu\neq\mu
.

To construct an extended affine Lie algebra we introduce two more ingredients.
A
\Gamma
-graded subalgebra

\text{ev}:\Lambda\rightarrow(D^{0})^{\star}

\text{ev}(\lambda)(\partial_{\theta})=\theta(\lambda)

\begin{array}[]{c}\kappa(d,d)=0,\;\;{\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}\kappa([d_{i},d_{j}],d_{k})=\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}d_{i}\cdot\kappa(d_{j},d_{k})},\\
\kappa(D^{\mu_{1}},D^{\mu_{2}})\subseteq(D^{-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}})^{\star}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\kappa(d_{1},d_{2})(d_{3})=\kappa(d_{2},d_{3})(d_{1}),\\
\kappa(D^{0},D)=0,\end{array}

for

.
Note that by

, we mean

is a cyclic permutation of

.
\kappa
is called an
affine cocycle
on
D
.

Assume now that
\mathfrak{g}
is a centerless Lie torus,
D
is a permissible subalgebra of

\hbox{SCDer}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\kappa
is an affine cocycle on
D
. Set

(3.3)

E=E(\mathfrak{g},D,\kappa):=\mathfrak{g}\oplus D^{gr*}\oplus D.

Then
E
is a Lie algebra with the bracket

\displaystyle[x_{1}+c_{1}+d_{1},x_{2}+c_{2}+d_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([x_{1},x_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+d_{1}(x_{2})-d_{2}(x_{1}))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(c_{D}(x_{1},x_{2})+d_{1}.c_{2}-d_{2}.c_{1}+\kappa(d_{1},d_{2}))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle[d_{1},d_{2}]

for
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathfrak{g},c_{1},c_{2}\in D^{gr*},d_{1},d_{2}\in D
. Here
[\>,\>]_{\mathfrak{g}}
denotes the Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{g}
,
[d_{1},d_{2}]=d_{1}d_{2}-d_{2}d_{1}
, and
c_{D}:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow D^{gr*}
is defined by

c_{D}(x,y)(d)=(d(x)|y)\;\text{for all}\;x,y\in\mathfrak{g},d\in D.

Next, define a bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
on
E
by

(3.4)

(x_{1}+c_{1}+d_{1},x_{2}+c_{2}+d_{2})=(x_{1},x_{2})_{\mathcal{L}}+c_{1}(d_{2})+c_{2}(d_{1}).

It follows that
(\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric, invariant and non-degenerate. In fact the Lie algebra
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
constructed from the data
\mathfrak{g},

D
and
\kappa
is a tame extended affine Lie algebra, where

{\mathcal{H}}=\mathfrak{g}^{0}_{0}\oplus(D^{0})^{\star}\oplus D^{0},

and conversely any tame extended affine Lie algebra arises this way, see
[
16
, Theorem 16]
. To indicate the dependence of
E
on
\mathfrak{g}
,
D
and
\kappa
, we write

E=E(\mathfrak{g},D,\kappa)
. Note that

E_{c}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus{D^{gr}}^{\star}\quad\hbox{and}\quad Z(E_{c})={D^{gr}}^{\star}.

Remark 3.2.1
.

(i) From
Z(E_{c})={D^{gr}}^{\star}
, we have

(3.5)

Z(E_{c})\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}\Longleftrightarrow D=D^{0}\Longleftrightarrow Z(E_{c})={D^{0}}^{\star}.

(ii) Almost all examples of extended affine Lie algebras found in the literature have typically
D=D^{0}
and
\kappa=0
, as one can see for example in
[
1
, Chapter III]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
and
[
14
]
.

4.
Extended affine Lie algebras of type
A_{1}

We begin by recalling the TKK-construction which associates to any Jordan algebra a Lie algebra.

4.1.
TKK construction

Let
{\mathcal{J}}
be a Jordan algebra, and set
\mathrm{Inder}({\mathcal{J}}):=\{L_{x}\mid x\in{\mathcal{J}}\}
where
L_{x}
is the operator on
{\mathcal{J}}
defined by
L_{x}y=xy
, for
y\in{\mathcal{J}}
. Next set

\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}}):=\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{J}}}L_{x}+\{\sum_{i}[L_{x_{i}},L_{y_{i}}]\mid x_{i},y_{i}\in{\mathcal{J}}\},

and

\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}}):={\mathcal{J}}\oplus\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})\oplus\bar{{\mathcal{J}}},

where
\bar{\mathcal{J}}
is a copy of
{\mathcal{J}}
.
Then
\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})
and
\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})
are Lie algebras with the brackets given by

[L_{x}+C,L_{y}+D]=[L_{x},L_{y}]+L_{Cy}-L_{Dx}+[C,D],

for
x,y\in{\mathcal{J}},C,D\in\mathrm{Inder}({\mathcal{J}})
, and

(4.1)

\begin{array}[]{c}[x_{1}+\bar{y}_{1}+E_{1},x_{2}+\bar{y}_{2}+E_{2}]=-E_{2}x_{1}+E_{1}x_{2}-\overline{\bar{E}_{2}y}+\overline{\bar{E}_{1}y_{2}}\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+x_{1}\triangle y_{2}-x_{2}\triangle y_{1}+[E_{1},E_{2}],\end{array}

for
x_{i}\in{\mathcal{J}},\bar{y}_{i}\in\bar{{\mathcal{J}}}
, and
E_{i}\in\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})
, where
x\triangle y=L_{xy}+[L_{x},L_{y}]
.
Here
\bar{}:\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})\rightarrow\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})
is an involution define by

\overline{L_{x}+D}=-L_{x}+D
. The Lie algebra
\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})
is called TKK
Lie algebra
of
{\mathcal{J}}
.

Next, let
\Lambda
be a free abelian group of rank
\nu
. The Jordan algebra
{\mathcal{J}}
is called a
\Lambda
-Jordan torus if
{\mathcal{J}}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\mathcal{J}}^{\lambda}
with

\dim{\mathcal{J}}^{\lambda}\leq 1
for each
\lambda
, and
\Lambda
is generated by
S:=\{\lambda\in\Lambda\mid{\mathcal{J}}^{\lambda}\not=\{0\}\}
.
Set

\mathfrak{g}:=\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})

Then
\mathfrak{g}
is
\Lambda
-graded with

(4.2)

\mathfrak{g}^{\lambda}=\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})^{\lambda}={\mathcal{J}}^{\lambda}\oplus\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})^{\lambda}\oplus\overline{{\mathcal{J}}^{\lambda}},

for
\lambda\in\Lambda
, where
\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})^{\lambda}=L_{{\mathcal{J}}^{\lambda}}\oplus\sum_{\mu+\nu=\lambda}[L_{{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu}},L_{{\mathcal{J}}^{\nu}}]
.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
can be equipped with a symmetric invariant non-degenerate form as follows. We fix a basis
\{x^{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{J}}^{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in S\}
for
{\mathcal{J}}
. For the sake of notation if
\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus S
, we write
x^{\lambda}
and interpret it as zero.
Consider the linear map
\epsilon:{\mathcal{J}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{K}}
induced by
\epsilon(1)=1
and
\epsilon(x^{\lambda})=0
if
\lambda\not=0
. Then

(x,y):=\epsilon(xy)
defines a symmetric invariant non-degenerate form on
{\mathcal{J}}
. This then defines a form on
\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})
by

(D,[L_{x},L_{y}])=(Dx,y)
for
D\in\mathrm{Instrl({\mathcal{J}})}
,
x,y\in{\mathcal{J}}
. Finally, this form extends to a symmetric invariant non-degenerate form on
\mathfrak{g}
by

(x_{1}+L_{y_{1}}+D_{1}+\bar{z}_{1},x_{2}+L_{y_{2}}+D_{2}+\bar{z}_{2}):=(x_{1},z_{2})+(x_{2},z_{1})+(y_{1},y_{2})+(D_{1},D_{2}),

for
x_{i},y_{i},z_{i}\in{\mathcal{J}}
,
D_{i}\in\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})
.

4.2.
Elliptic extended affine Lie algebras of type
A_{1}

We recall from
[
19
, Corollary 7.9]
that any centerless Lie torus of type
A_{1}
takes the form

\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})
where
{\mathcal{J}}
represents one of the following Jordan algebras:

{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}^{+}
,
H({\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e},-)
,
{\mathcal{J}}_{S}
, or
\mathbb{A}_{t}
. Since this work focuses on the elliptic case (nullity 2), we restrict our discussion to the first three Jordan algebras. The Jordan algebra

\mathbb{A}_{t}

does not arise in the classification of
A_{1}
-type extended affine Lie algebras of nullity
1
or
2
; it only appears for nullity
\geq 3
, which falls outside the scope of the present study.

Consider the centerless Lie torus
\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})
where
{\mathcal{J}}
is one the Jordan algebras (Jordan tori) mentioned above. Let
\Lambda={\mathbb{Z}}^{\nu}.

Set
\dot{\mathcal{H}}={\mathbb{K}}L_{1}
and define
\dot{\alpha}\in{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}^{\star}
by
\dot{\alpha}(L_{1})=1
.
Set
Q=\mathbb{Z}\dot{\alpha}
.
Equip
\mathfrak{g}
with the
Q
-grading

(4.1)

\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathcal{J}}&\hbox{if }\beta=\dot{\alpha},\\
\bar{{\mathcal{J}}}&\hbox{if }\beta=-\dot{\alpha},\\
\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})&\hbox{if }\beta=0,\\
\{0\}&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Next, let
D
be a permissible subalgebra of the skew centroidal derivations
\hbox{SCDer}(\mathfrak{g})
of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\kappa
be an affine cocycle on
D
. Let
C
be the graded dual of
D
.
Set
E=E(\mathfrak{g},D,\kappa)
as in Section
3
. Then

E:=\mathfrak{g}\oplus C\oplus D\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\mathcal{H}}:={\mathbb{K}}L_{1}\oplus C^{0}\oplus D^{0}.

By
[
16
, Theorem 16]
,
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
is a tame extended affine Lie algebra of type
A_{1}
with root system
R\subseteq\Lambda\cup(\pm\dot{\alpha}+\Lambda)
, and any tame extended affine Lie algebra of type
A_{1}
is up to isomorphism of this form. Moreover, for
\beta\in R,

(4.2)

E_{\beta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathbb{K}}L_{1}\oplus D^{0}\oplus C^{0}&\hbox{if }\beta=0,\\
{\mathbb{K}}x^{\sigma}&\hbox{if }\beta=\dot{\alpha}+\sigma,\;\sigma\not=0,\\
{\mathbb{K}}\bar{x}^{\sigma}&\hbox{if }\beta=-\dot{\alpha}+\sigma,\;\sigma\not=0,\\
{\mathbb{K}}L_{x^{\sigma}}+\sum_{\tau\in\Lambda}{\mathbb{K}}[L_{x^{\sigma+\tau}},L_{x^{-\tau}}]\oplus D^{\sigma}\oplus C^{\sigma}&\hbox{if }\beta=\sigma,\;\sigma\not=0,\\
\end{array}\right.

We note that since
(L_{1},L_{1})=\epsilon(1)=1
, it follows that
(\dot{\alpha},\dot{\alpha})=1
.

Remark 4.2.1
.

(i) From the way the bilinear form
\mathfrak{g}
is defined on
\mathfrak{g}=\hbox{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})
and on
E=E(\mathfrak{g},D,\kappa)
, we have

(L_{1},L_{1})=(1,1)=\epsilon(1)=1
and so
(\dot{\alpha},\dot{\alpha})=1
.

(ii) If
\hbox{rank}\;\Lambda=2
, then
\dim\hbox{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{K}})=2
and so
\dim\hbox{SCDer}(\mathfrak{g})^{\sigma}\leq 1
for each
0\not=\sigma\in\Lambda
.
Therefore,
\dim D^{\sigma},\dim C^{\sigma}\leq 1
for
0\not=\sigma\in\Lambda
.

5.
The Jordan torus
{\mathcal{J}}_{S}

In this section, we examine the
\Lambda
-Jordan torus
{\mathcal{J}}_{S}
based on
S
where
S
is a semilattice in a free abelian group
\Lambda
of finite rank. The results presented here are not limited to nullity
2
; they apply to any arbitrary nullity.

5.1.
The function
\Gamma

Let
S
be a semilattice in
\Lambda={\mathbb{Z}}^{\nu}
, that is,
S=\cup_{i=0}^{m}S_{i}
where

S_{i}=\tau_{i}+2\Lambda
and
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{m}
represent distinct cosets of
2\Lambda
in
\Lambda
with
\tau_{0}=0
.
For
\sigma\in S
consider the symbol
x^{\sigma}
and set
{\mathcal{J}}={\mathcal{J}}_{S}=\sum_{\sigma\in S}{\mathbb{K}}x^{\sigma}
. By convention, we write
{\mathcal{J}}=\sum_{\sigma\in\Lambda}{\mathbb{K}}x^{\sigma}
, where we interpret
x^{\sigma}=0
if
\sigma\not\in S
. Then
{\mathcal{J}}
is a
\Lambda
-Jordan torus with the multiplication

(5.1)

x^{\sigma}\cdot x^{\tau}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x^{\sigma+\tau}&\hbox{if }\sigma,\tau\in S_{0}\cup S_{i},\;0\leq i\leq m,\\
0&\hbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

We choose cosets
S_{i}=\tau_{i}+2\Lambda
,
i=m+1,\ldots,2^{\nu}-1
of
2\Lambda
in
\Lambda
such that
\Lambda=\cup_{i=0}^{2^{\nu}-1}S_{i}
. Now, we define a symmetric function
\Gamma:\mathfrak{S}\times\mathfrak{S}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
where
\mathfrak{S}=\{S_{i}\mid 0\leq i\leq 2^{\nu}-1\}
by

(5.2)

\Gamma(S_{i},S_{j})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\hbox{if $i$ or $j\geq m+1$}\\
0&\hbox{if }i\not=j>0\\
1&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

In particular, for
0\leq i\not=j\leq m
, we have

\Gamma(S_{0},S_{0})=\Gamma(S_{0},S_{i})=\Gamma(S_{i},S_{i})=1\quad\hbox{and}\quad\Gamma(S_{i},S_{j})=0.

This gives

(5.3)

\Gamma(S_{i},S_{j})=0,\qquad(S_{i}+S_{j}\not\in\mathfrak{S},\;0\leq i,j\leq m),

since in this case
i,j
can not be equal and none can be zero.
By convention we set

\Gamma(\sigma,\tau)=\Gamma(S_{i},S_{j})=\Gamma(\sigma,S_{j})\qquad(\sigma\in S_{i},\tau\in S_{j}).

Then the map
\Gamma
can be describe the multiplication (
5.1
) as

\begin{array}[]{c}x^{\sigma}\cdot x^{\tau}=\Gamma(\sigma,\tau)x^{\sigma+\tau}=\Gamma(S_{i},S_{j})x^{\sigma+\tau},\\
(\sigma\in S_{i},\tau\in S_{j},\;0\leq i,j\leq 2^{\nu}-1).\end{array}

We note that since for each
i
,
S_{0}+S_{i}=S_{i}
and
S_{i}+S_{i}=S_{0}
, we have for
0\leq i,j\leq m
,

(5.4)

\Gamma(S_{0},S_{0}+S_{i})=\Gamma(S_{i},S_{j}+S_{j})=\Gamma(S_{i},S_{0}+S_{i})=1.

Lemma 5.1.1
.

For
\lambda_{i}\in S_{i}
we have
x^{\lambda_{i}}\cdot(x^{\lambda_{j}}\cdot x^{\lambda_{k}})=\Gamma(S_{j},S_{k})\Gamma(S_{i},S_{j}+S_{k})x^{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{k}}.

Proof.
If either of
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j},\lambda_{k}
is in
\Lambda\setminus S
, then
x^{\lambda_{i}}\cdot(x^{\lambda_{j}}\cdot x^{\lambda_{k}})=0
and

\Gamma(S_{j},S_{k})\Gamma(S_{i},S_{j}+S_{k})=0
, so we are done in this case.
Assume now that

\lambda_{i}\in S_{i}
,
\lambda_{j}\in S_{j}
and
\lambda_{k}\in S_{k}
, where
0\leq i,j,k\leq m
. Then

x^{\lambda_{i}}\cdot(x^{\lambda_{j}}\cdot x^{\lambda_{k}})=\Gamma(S_{j},S_{k})x^{\lambda_{i}}\cdot x^{\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{k}}=\Gamma(S_{j},S_{k})\Gamma(S_{i},S_{j}+S_{k})x^{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{k}}.

\Box

5.2.
Lie Brackets of left operators

We establish several results on brackets containing left operators which facilitate in computing dimensions of isotropic root spaces.

Lemma 5.2.1
.

(i)
[L_{x^{\sigma}},L_{x^{\tau}}](x^{\gamma})=0
if at least one of
\sigma,\tau
or
\gamma
belongs to
S_{0}
.

(ii)
[L_{x^{\sigma}},L_{x^{\tau}}]=0
if
\sigma,\tau\in S_{i}
for some
i
.

Proof.
(i) Let
\sigma\in S_{0}
. We may assume that
\tau,\gamma\in S
. If
\tau+\gamma\not\in S
, then

\tau+\gamma+\sigma\not\in S
and by (
5.2
) and (
5.3
),

[L_{x^{\sigma}},L_{x^{\tau}}](x^{\gamma})=\big(\Gamma(\tau,\gamma)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle=0}}{{\overbrace{\Gamma(\sigma,\tau+\gamma)}}}-\Gamma(\sigma,\gamma)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle=0}}{{\overbrace{\Gamma(\tau,\sigma+\gamma)}}}\big){x^{\sigma+\tau+\gamma}}=0.

If
\tau+\gamma\in S
, then

\displaystyle[L_{x^{\sigma}},L_{x^{\tau}}](x^{\gamma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\Gamma(\tau,\gamma)\Gamma(S_{0},\tau+\gamma)-\Gamma(S_{0},\gamma)\Gamma(\tau,S_{0}+\gamma)\big)x^{\sigma+\tau+\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\Gamma(\tau,\gamma)-\Gamma(\tau,S_{0}+\gamma)\big)x^{\sigma+\tau+\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\Gamma(\tau,\gamma)-\Gamma(\tau,\gamma)\big)x^{\sigma+\tau+\gamma}=0.

The case
\tau\in S_{0}
holds by symmetry.

If
\gamma\in S_{0}
, then

\displaystyle[L_{x^{\sigma}},L_{x^{\tau}}](x^{\gamma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\Gamma(\tau,S_{0})\Gamma(\sigma,\tau+S_{0})-\Gamma(\sigma,S_{0})\Gamma(\tau,\sigma+S_{0})\big)x^{\sigma+\tau+\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Gamma(\sigma,\tau)-\Gamma(\tau,\sigma))x^{\sigma+\tau+\gamma}=0.

(ii)
Assume
\sigma,\tau\in S_{i}
and
\gamma\in S_{j}
for some
i,j
. Then

\displaystyle[L_{x^{\sigma}},L_{x^{\tau}}](x^{\gamma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\Gamma(S_{i},S_{j})\Gamma(S_{i},S_{i}+S_{j})-\Gamma(S_{i},S_{j})\Gamma(S_{i},S_{i}+S_{j})\big)x^{\sigma+\tau+\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

\Box

Lemma 5.2.2
.

Let
\sigma,\tau,\gamma\in S
and
\lambda_{0},\lambda^{\prime}_{0},\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{0}\in S_{0}
with
\lambda_{0}+\lambda^{\prime}_{0}+\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{0}=0
. Then

[L_{x^{\sigma}},L_{x^{\tau}}](x^{\gamma})=[L_{x^{\sigma+\lambda_{0}}},L_{x^{\tau+\lambda^{\prime}_{0}}}](x^{\gamma+\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{0}}).
In particular,
if
\tau\in S_{j}
, then

[L_{x^{\sigma}},L_{x^{\tau}}]=[L_{x^{\sigma+\tau+\tau_{j}}},L_{x^{-\tau_{j}}}].

Proof.
Let
\sigma\in S_{i}
,
\tau\in S_{j}
and
\gamma\in S_{k}
. Then

\displaystyle[L_{x^{\sigma+\lambda_{0}}},L_{x^{\tau+\lambda^{\prime}_{0}}}](x^{\gamma+\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{0}})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\big(\Gamma(S_{j}+S_{0},S_{k}+S_{0})\Gamma(S_{i}+S_{0},S_{j}+S_{k}+S_{0}))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-\Gamma(S_{i}+S_{0},S_{k}+S_{0})\Gamma(S_{j}+S_{0},S_{i}+S_{k}+S_{0})\big)x^{\sigma+\tau+\gamma}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\big(\Gamma(S_{j},S_{k})\Gamma(S_{i},S_{j}+S_{k}))-\Gamma(S_{i},S_{k})\Gamma(S_{j},S_{i}+S_{k})\big)x^{\sigma+\tau+\gamma}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=[L_{x^{\sigma}},L_{x^{\tau}}](x^{\gamma}).

This proves the first assertion.

The second assertion is immediate from the first one as
\tau+\tau_{j}\in S_{0}.

\Box

Lemma 5.2.3
.

Suppose
\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{k}\in S_{0}
. Then we have

[L_{x^{\lambda_{i}}},[L_{x^{\lambda_{j}}},L_{x^{\lambda_{k}}}]]=0.

Proof.
Let
\lambda_{t}\in S_{t}
for

. If at least one of
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j},\lambda_{k}
is in
S_{0}
, then using Lemma

(i) and Jacobi identity we are done. Suppose next that at least two of
\lambda_{i}
,
\lambda_{j}
,
\lambda_{k}
belong to the same coset. If
\lambda_{j}
and
\lambda_{k}
belong to the same coset then by Lemma

(ii) we are done. If
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}
, or
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{k}
belong to the same coset, then form
\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{k}\in S_{0}
, we conclude that
\lambda_{k}
or
\lambda_{j}
belongs to
S_{0}
and so we are done by Lemma
5.2.1
(i).

The above discussion shows that we may assume

are distinct and none is zero. Note that by assumption

, so
S_{j}+S_{k}=S_{i}
. We now compute

[L_{x^{\lambda_{i}}},[L_{x^{\lambda_{j}}},L_{x^{\lambda_{k}}}]](x^{\lambda_{u}})=J_{1}-J_{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\qquad\quad\quad\qquad-\Gamma(S_{j},S_{u})\Gamma(S_{k},S_{j}+S_{u})\big)x^{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{u}},

and

\displaystyle J_{2}:=[{x^{\lambda_{j}}}\cdot{x^{\lambda_{k}}}]\cdot({x^{\lambda_{i}}}\cdot x^{\lambda_{u}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(S_{i},S_{u})[x^{\lambda_{j}},x^{\lambda_{k}}]x^{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{u}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(S_{i},S_{u})\big(\Gamma(S_{k},S_{i}+S_{u})\Gamma(S_{j},S_{k}+S_{u}+S_{i})

\displaystyle-\Gamma(S_{j},S_{i}+S_{u})\Gamma(S_{k},S_{j}+S_{u}+S_{i})\big)x^{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{u}}.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(S_{i},S_{u})\big(\Gamma(S_{k},S_{i}+S_{u})\Gamma(S_{j},S_{j}+S_{u})

\displaystyle-\Gamma(S_{j},S_{i}+S_{u})\Gamma(S_{k},S_{k}+S_{u})\big)x^{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{u}}.

If
u=0
, then we have

\begin{array}[]{c}\Gamma(S_{j},S_{k}+S_{u})=\Gamma(S_{j},S_{k})=0,\quad\Gamma(S_{k},S_{j}+S_{u})=\Gamma(S_{k},S_{j})=0,\\
\Gamma(S_{k},S_{i}+S_{u})=\Gamma(S_{k},S_{i})=0,\quad\Gamma(S_{j},S_{i}+S_{u})=\Gamma(S_{j},S_{i})=0\end{array}

and so

.

, then
\Gamma(S_{k},S_{u})=\Gamma(S_{j},S_{u})=0

. Also
\Gamma(S_{j},S_{j}+S_{u})=\Gamma(S_{j},S_{k})=0

. If

Lemma 5.2.4
.

If
\lambda_{i},\mu_{i}\in S_{i}
and

, then

L_{x^{\mu_{i}+\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}}}&\hbox{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

and so
[L_{x^{\mu_{i}}},[L_{x^{\lambda_{i}}},L_{x^{\lambda_{j}}}]]=\epsilon L_{x^{\mu_{i}+\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}}}
,
\epsilon\in\{0,1\}
.

Proof.
From Lemma
5.2.1
, we have
[L_{x^{\mu_{i}}},[L_{x^{\lambda_{i}}},L_{x^{\lambda_{j}}}]]=-L_{[L_{x^{\lambda_{i}}},L_{x^{\lambda_{j}}}](x^{\mu_{i}})}
, and this expression is
0

if either
i=j
or
0\in\{i,j\}
.
Now assume that
1\leq i\not=j
. Then

\displaystyle[L_{x^{\lambda_{i}}},L_{x^{\lambda_{j}}}](x^{\mu_{i}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{\lambda_{i}}\cdot(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0}}{{\overbrace{x^{\lambda_{j}}\cdot x^{\mu_{i}}}}})-x^{\lambda_{j}}\cdot(x^{\lambda_{i}}\cdot x^{\mu_{i}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0}}{{\overbrace{\Gamma(S_{j},S_{i})}}}\Gamma(S_{i},S_{j}+S_{i})-\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0}}{{\overbrace{\Gamma(S_{i},S_{i})\Gamma(S_{j},S_{i}+S_{i})}}}\big)x^{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}+\mu_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{x^{\mu_{i}+\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}}}(x^{\gamma}).

\Box

Lemma 6.1.2

6.2.
The case
{\mathcal{J}}={\mathbb{K}}_{{\bf q}}^{+}

We begin with a quick review of Jordan tori
{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}^{+}
and
H({\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e},-)
.

Let
{\bf q}=(q_{ij})\in M_{n}({\mathbb{K}})
be a
(\nu\times\nu)
-matrix satisfying
q_{ii}=1=q_{ij}q_{ji}
for
1\leq i,j\leq\nu
. The
quantum torus

{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}
associated with
{\bf q}
is the unital associative algebra defined by generators
x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{\nu}^{\pm 1}
and relations
x_{i}x_{i}^{-1}=1=x_{i}^{-1}x_{i}
,
x_{i}x_{j}=q_{ij}x_{j}x_{i}
for
1\leq i,j\leq\nu
. We have
{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\mathbb{K}}x^{\lambda}
, where
\Lambda={\mathbb{Z}}^{\nu}
, and for
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\nu})
,

x^{\lambda}:=x_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots x_{\nu}^{\lambda_{\nu}}
. Define
\eta:\Lambda\times\Lambda\rightarrow{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
by

x^{\lambda}\cdot x^{\mu}=\eta(\lambda,\mu)x^{\lambda+\mu}
. In fact
\eta(\lambda,\mu)=\prod_{i<j}q_{ji}^{\mu_{j}\lambda_{i}}
, which makes
\eta
a bi-additive map.
Then

[x^{\lambda},x^{\mu}]=(\eta(\lambda,\mu)-\eta(\mu,\lambda))x^{\lambda+\mu}=\eta(\mu,\lambda)(f(\lambda,\mu)-1)x^{\lambda+\mu},

where
f(\lambda,\mu)=\eta(\lambda,\mu)\eta(\mu,\lambda)^{-1}.
The subgroup

\hbox{rad}(f):=\{\lambda\mid f(\lambda,\mu)=1\hbox{ for all }\mu\in\Lambda\}
of
\Lambda
is called the
radical
of
f
.
For
\nu=2
, the quantum torus
{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}
is uniquely determined by a single parameter
q=q_{21}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
.
The
plus algebra

{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}^{+}
of
{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}
is defined by the same underlying vector space as
{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}
but with the Jordan product given by
x\cdot y:=\frac{1}{2}(xy+yx).

Lemma 6.2.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{J}}={\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}^{+}
, and
0\not=\sigma\in R^{0}
. Then

E_{\sigma}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathbb{K}}L_{x^{\sigma}}+D^{\sigma}+C^{\sigma}&\hbox{if }\sigma\in\hbox{rad}(f)\\
{\mathbb{K}}L_{x^{\sigma}}\oplus{\mathbb{K}}(L_{x^{\sigma}}-r_{\sigma})+D^{\sigma}+C^{\sigma}&\hbox{ if }\sigma\not\in\hbox{rad}(f).\end{array}\right.

where
r_{\sigma}:{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}\rightarrow{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}
is given by
r_{\sigma}(x^{\lambda})=\eta(\sigma,\lambda)x^{\sigma+\lambda}
. In particular,

1\leq\dim E_{\sigma}\leq 3
if
\sigma\in\hbox{rad}(f)
, and

1\leq\dim E_{\sigma}\leq 4
if
\sigma\not\in\hbox{rad}(f).

Proof.
We fix
0\not=\sigma\in R^{0}=\Lambda
. Let
\tau,\gamma\in\Lambda
.
Then

\displaystyle[L_{x^{\sigma+\tau}},L_{x^{-\tau}}](x^{\gamma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{\sigma+\tau}\cdot(x^{-\tau}\cdot x^{\gamma})-x^{-\tau}\cdot(x^{\sigma+\tau}\cdot x^{\gamma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}x^{\sigma+\tau}\cdot(x^{-\tau}x^{\gamma}+x^{\gamma}x^{-\tau})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}x^{-\tau}\cdot(x^{\sigma+\tau}x^{\gamma}+x^{\gamma}x^{\sigma+\tau})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\big(x^{\sigma+\tau}(x^{-\tau}x^{\gamma}+x^{\gamma}x^{-\tau})+(x^{-\tau}x^{\gamma}+x^{\gamma}x^{-\tau})x^{\sigma+\tau}\big)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\big(x^{-\tau}(x^{\sigma+\tau}x^{\gamma}+x^{\gamma}x^{\sigma+\tau})+(x^{\sigma+\tau}x^{\gamma}+x^{\gamma}x^{\sigma+\tau})x^{-\tau}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\big(x^{\sigma+\tau}x^{-\tau}x^{\gamma}-x^{-\tau}x^{\sigma+\tau}x^{\gamma}+x^{\gamma}x^{-\tau}x^{\sigma+\tau}-x^{\gamma}x^{\sigma+\tau}x^{-\tau}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}(x^{\sigma+\tau}x^{-\tau}x^{\gamma}-x^{-\tau}x^{\sigma+\tau}x^{\gamma})+\frac{1}{4}x^{\gamma}(x^{-\tau}x^{\sigma+\tau}-x^{\sigma+\tau}x^{-\tau})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{\tau}(\frac{1}{2}(x^{\sigma}x^{\gamma}+x^{\gamma}x^{\sigma})-x^{\gamma}x^{\sigma})=\eta_{\tau}(L_{x^{\sigma}}-r_{{\sigma}})(x^{\gamma}),

where

\displaystyle\eta_{\tau}:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\eta(\sigma+\tau,-\tau)-\eta(-\tau,\sigma+\tau))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\eta(\sigma,-\tau)-\eta(-\tau,\sigma))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\eta(-\tau,\sigma)(f(\sigma,-\tau)-1),

and the linear map
r_{\sigma}:{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}\rightarrow{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}
is defined by

r_{\sigma}(x^{\gamma})=x^{\gamma}x^{\sigma}=\eta(\gamma,\sigma)x^{\sigma+\gamma}.
If
\sigma\in\hbox{rad}(f)
, then
\eta_{\tau}=0
for all
\tau
, so

E_{\sigma}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathbb{K}}L_{x^{\sigma}}\oplus{\mathbb{K}}(L_{x^{\sigma}}-r_{\sigma})+D^{\sigma}+C^{\sigma}&\hbox{ if }\sigma\not\in\hbox{rad}(f)\\
{\mathbb{K}}L_{x^{\sigma}}+D^{\sigma}+C^{\sigma}&\hbox{if }\sigma\in\hbox{rad}(f).\end{array}\right.

\Box

6.3.
The case
H({\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e},-)

Consider the elementary quantum matrix
{\bf e}=(e_{ij})
with
e_{ij}=1
or
-1
for all
i,j
. Equip the quantum torus
{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e}
with the involution
\bar{\;}
satisfying
\overline{x_{i}}=x_{i}
for all
i
. Then
H({\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e},-)=\{x\in{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e}\mid\overline{x}=x\}
is a Jordan subalgebra of
{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e}^{+}
. Since
\bar{\;}
preserves the grading of
{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e}^{+}
, we see that
H({\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e},-)
is a Jordan
\Lambda
-torus with
H({\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e},-)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left({\mathbb{K}}x^{\lambda}\cap H({\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e},-)\right)
, see
[
19
, Example 4.3 (2)]
.

Lemma 6.3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{J}}=H({\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e},-)
and
0\not=\sigma\in R^{0}
. Then

E_{\sigma}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathbb{K}}L_{x^{\sigma}}\oplus D^{\sigma}\oplus C^{\sigma}&\hbox{if }-=\hbox{id},\hbox{ or }\sigma\in S_{0}\cup S_{1}\cup S_{2},\\
{\mathbb{K}}[L_{x^{\sigma+\sigma_{1}}},L_{x^{-\sigma_{1}}}]\oplus D^{\sigma}\oplus C^{\sigma}&\hbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

In particular
1\leq\dim E_{\sigma}\leq 3
.

Proof.
We have either
{\bf e}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
1&1\end{array}\right),

or

{\bf e}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&-1\\
-1&1\end{array}\right).

In the first case
{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e}
is the algebra of Laurent polynomials in variables
x_{1},x_{2}
,
and
\bar{\;}
is just the identity map. Then
(\star)=0
(see (
6.1
) and so
E_{\sigma}={\mathbb{K}}L_{x^{\sigma}}\oplus D^{\sigma}\oplus C^{\sigma}
.

Next, we consider the second case. Then
x_{2}x_{1}=-x_{1}x_{2}
and for
\lambda=k_{1}\sigma_{1}+k_{2}\sigma_{2}\in\Lambda
,
x^{\lambda}\in H({\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e},-)
if and only if
k_{1}k_{2}\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}
. Thus
H({\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e},-)=\sum_{\lambda\in S}{\mathbb{K}}x^{\lambda}

with
S=S_{0}\cup S_{1}\cup S_{2}
with
S_{i}=\sigma_{i}+2\Lambda
where
\sigma_{0}=0
. We now compute
[L_{x^{\sigma+\tau}},L_{x^{-\tau}}](x^{\gamma})
.
Fix
0\not=\sigma\in R^{0}=\Lambda
. Let
\tau,\gamma\in S
.
From proof of Lemma
6.2.1
, we have

\displaystyle[L_{x^{\sigma+\tau}},L_{x^{-\tau}}](x^{\gamma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}(x^{\sigma+\tau}x^{-\tau}x^{\gamma}-x^{-\tau}x^{\sigma+\tau}x^{\gamma})+\frac{1}{4}x^{\gamma}(x^{-\tau}x^{\sigma+\tau}-x^{\sigma+\tau}x^{-\tau}).

Thus

\displaystyle[L_{x^{\sigma+\tau}},L_{x^{-\tau}}](x^{\gamma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}[\big(\eta(-\tau,\gamma)\eta(\sigma+\tau,-\tau+\gamma)-\eta(\sigma+\tau,\gamma)\eta(-\tau,\sigma+\tau+\gamma)\big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\big(\eta(\gamma,-\tau)\eta(\gamma-\tau,\sigma+\tau)-\eta(\gamma,\sigma+\tau)\eta(\gamma+\sigma+\tau,-\tau)\big)]x^{\sigma+\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\big(\eta(-\tau,\gamma)\eta(\sigma+\tau,-\tau)\eta(\sigma+\tau,\gamma)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-\eta(\sigma+\tau,\gamma)\eta(-\tau,\sigma+\tau)\eta(-\tau,\gamma)\big)x^{\sigma+\gamma}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\big(\eta(\gamma,-\tau)\eta(\gamma,\sigma+\tau)\eta(-\tau,\sigma+\tau)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-\eta(\gamma,\sigma+\tau)\eta(\gamma,-\tau)\eta(\sigma+\tau,-\tau)\big)x^{\sigma+\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{\tau}\eta_{\gamma,\tau}

where

\eta_{\tau}:=\frac{1}{2}(\eta(\sigma+\tau,-\tau)-\eta(-\tau,\sigma+\tau)),

and

\eta_{\gamma,\tau}:=\frac{1}{2}\big(\eta(-\tau,\gamma)\eta(\sigma+\tau,\gamma)-\eta(\gamma,-\tau)\eta(\gamma,\sigma+\tau)\big).

To compute
\eta_{\tau}
and
\eta_{\gamma,\tau}
, we note that if
\lambda_{i},\lambda^{\prime}_{i}\in S_{i}
,
i=0,1,2
, then

\eta(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{0})=\eta(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{i})=\eta(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{i})=\eta(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1})=1\quad\hbox{and}\quad\eta(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=-1.

Now assume first that
\sigma\in S
. If
\sigma\in S_{0}
, then as
\tau\in S
, we have
\sigma+\tau,-\tau\in S_{i}
for some
i=0,1,2
and so
\eta_{\tau}=0
. If
\sigma\in S_{1}
then as
\sigma+\tau\in S
, we have
\tau\in S_{0}\cup S_{1}
and so again
\eta_{\tau}=0
.
By symmetry,
\eta_{\tau}=0
if
\sigma\in S_{2}
. Thus
\eta_{\tau}\eta_{\gamma,\tau}=0
if
\sigma\in S
.

Assume next that
\sigma\in\Lambda\setminus S
, namely
\sigma=\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}+2\lambda
for some
\lambda\in\Lambda
.
Since
\sigma+\tau\in S
, we have
\tau\in S_{1}\cup S_{2}
. Now
\sigma+\tau\in S_{2}
if
\tau\in S_{1}
and
\sigma+\tau\in S_{1}
if
\tau\in S_{2}
. Therefore

\eta_{\tau}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}1&\hbox{if }\tau\in S_{1}\\
-1&\hbox{if }\tau\in S_{2}.\end{array}\right.

We now compute
\eta_{\gamma,\tau}
. If either of
\tau
or
\gamma\in S_{0}
, then
\eta_{\gamma,\tau}=0
.
If
\tau,\gamma\in S_{1}
, then
\sigma+\tau\in S_{2}
and so
\eta_{\gamma,\tau}=1
. In general with similar computations, we get

\eta_{\gamma,\tau}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}0&\hbox{if }\tau\hbox{ or }\gamma\in S_{0}\\
1&\hbox{if }\tau,\gamma\in S_{1}\\
-1&\hbox{if }\tau,\gamma\in S_{2}\\
-1&\hbox{if }\tau\in S_{1},\gamma\in S_{2}\\
1&\hbox{if }\tau\in S_{2},\gamma\in S_{1}.\\
\end{array}\right.

Thus
\eta_{\sigma_{1}}\eta_{\gamma,\sigma_{1}}=-\eta_{\sigma_{2}}\eta_{,\gamma,\sigma_{2}},
and so
[L_{x^{\sigma+\tau}},L_{x^{-\tau}}](x^{\gamma})=\epsilon[L_{x^{\sigma+\sigma_{1}}},L_{x^{-\sigma_{1}}}](x^{\gamma}),

\epsilon\in\{0,\pm 1\}
.
This completes the proof.
\Box

7.

We continue using notation established in the previous sections. Let
E=E(\mathfrak{g},D,\kappa)
be an elliptic extended affine Lie algebra of type
A_{1}
with
\kappa=0
. We have
R=(S+S)\cup(\pm\alpha+S)
, where
S
is a semilattice with
\langle S\rangle=\Lambda={\mathbb{Z}}\sigma_{1}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\sigma_{2}
.

To discuss the Chevalley bases for
E
, we consider the most general case by assuming
D=\hbox{SCDer}(\mathfrak{g})
. The cases
D\subseteq\hbox{SCDer}(\mathfrak{g})
can be derived from this general setting.
Let
0\not=\sigma\in R^{0}

D^{\sigma}=\chi^{\sigma}\{\partial_{\theta}\in\mathcal{D}\mid\theta(\sigma)=0\}

, where

.
Suppose
0\not=\sigma\in\Lambda
, say
\sigma=k_{1}\sigma_{1}+k_{2}\sigma_{2}
,
k_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and assume without loos of generality that

\theta_{\sigma}:=k_{2}\theta_{1}-k_{1}\theta_{2}

\theta_{\sigma}(\sigma)=0,\quad\theta_{\sigma}(\Lambda)\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}},

and thus

Define
c^{\sigma}\in(D^{\lambda})^{\star}
by
c^{\sigma}(\chi^{\lambda}\partial_{\theta_{\lambda}})=\delta_{-\sigma,\lambda}
. Then, by recalling that
((grD)^{\star})^{\lambda}=(D^{-\lambda})^{\star}
, we have

.

7.2.
The main result

We now proceed to show that the set
{\mathcal{B}}
is indeed a Chevalley basis for the extended affine Lie algebra
E=E(\mathfrak{g},D,\kappa)
with
D=\hbox{SCDer}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\kappa=0
. Specifically,
{\mathcal{B}}
serves as a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis of
E
and satisfies

[{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{B}}]\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}}.

We assume the basis
\{\chi^{\gamma}\mid\gamma\in\Gamma\}
of
{\mathcal{C}}(\mathfrak{g})
is normalized so that
\chi^{\gamma}(x^{\lambda})=x^{\gamma+\lambda}
; see Subsection
3.2
.

Proposition 7.2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\hbox{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})
where
{\mathcal{J}}
is one of rank
2
Jordan tori
{\mathcal{J}}={\mathcal{J}}_{S}
,
{\mathbb{K}}^{+}_{\bf e}
or
H({\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e},-).
Let
D=\hbox{SCDer}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\kappa=0
. Then the set
{\mathcal{B}}
described in Tables
1
–
4
is a Chevalley basis for the elliptic extended affine Lie algebra
E=E(\mathfrak{g},D,\kappa)
.

Proof.
Let
\mathfrak{g}
,
D
,
\kappa
and
{\mathcal{B}}
be as in the statement. By Lemmas
6.1.1
,

6.1.2
,
6.2.1
and
6.3.1
the set
{\mathcal{B}}
forms a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis for
E
. To prove that
{\mathcal{B}}
is a Chevalley basis, we need to show that
[x,y]\in{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}}
for all
x,y\in{\mathcal{B}}
. We
proceed with the following computations.

From Lemma
5.2.2
, we see that

\begin{array}[]{c}\hbox{(I)}:=[L_{x^{\lambda_{j}}},L_{x^{\lambda_{i}}}]=[L_{x^{\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{i}-\sigma_{i}}},L_{x^{-\sigma_{i}}}]\in{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}},\\
(1\leq i<j\leq 3,\;\lambda_{i}\in S_{i},\lambda_{j}\in S_{j}).\end{array}

Also

\displaystyle[x_{\alpha+\lambda},x_{-\alpha+\gamma}]_{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\sqrt{2}x^{\lambda},\sqrt{2}\overline{x^{\gamma}}]_{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\sqrt{2}x^{\lambda},\sqrt{2}\overline{x^{\gamma}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+c_{D}(\sqrt{2}x^{\lambda},\sqrt{2}\overline{x^{\gamma}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2L_{x^{\lambda}\cdot{x^{\gamma}}}+2[L_{x^{\lambda}},L_{x^{\gamma}}]+2c_{D}(x^{\lambda},\overline{x^{\gamma}})

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\epsilon L_{x^{\lambda+\gamma}}+2[L_{x^{\lambda}},L_{x^{\gamma}}]+2c_{D}(x^{\lambda},\overline{x^{\gamma}}),

\epsilon\in\{0,\pm 1\}
.
Now for
d=\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta_{\mu}}\in D^{\mu}
,
\mu\not=0
, we have

\displaystyle c_{D}(x^{\lambda},\overline{x^{\gamma}})(\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta_{\mu}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta_{\mu}}(x^{\lambda}),\overline{x^{\gamma}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{\mu}(\lambda)\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\hbox{if }\mu+\lambda=-\gamma\\
0&\hbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{-\gamma}(\lambda)\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\hbox{if }\mu+\lambda=-\gamma\\
0&\hbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{-\gamma}(\lambda)c^{\lambda+\gamma}(\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta_{\mu}}),

and

\displaystyle c_{D}(x^{\lambda},\overline{x^{\gamma}})(\partial_{\theta_{i}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial_{\theta_{i}}(x^{\lambda}),\overline{x^{\gamma}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{i}(\lambda)\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\hbox{if }\lambda=-\gamma\\
0&\hbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda,-\gamma}(\theta_{1}(\lambda)c^{1}+\theta_{2}(\lambda)c^{2})(\partial_{\theta_{i}}).

which imply
c_{D}(x^{\lambda},\overline{x^{\gamma}})\in{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}}
. Thus by (I),

\hbox{(II)}:=[x_{\alpha+\lambda},x_{-\alpha+\gamma}]\in{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}}.

Also,

\displaystyle\hbox{(III)}:=[\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta_{\mu}},\sqrt{2}x^{\lambda}]=\sqrt{2}\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta_{\mu}}(x^{\lambda})=\theta_{\mu}(\lambda)\sqrt{2}x^{\mu+\lambda}\in{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}},

\hbox{(IV)}:=[\partial_{\theta_{i}},\sqrt{2}x^{\lambda}]=\theta_{i}(\lambda)\sqrt{2}x^{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}},

\displaystyle\hbox{(V)}:=[\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta_{\mu}},\chi^{\nu}\partial_{\theta_{\nu}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi^{\mu+\nu}(\theta_{\mu}(\nu)\partial_{\theta_{\nu}}-\theta_{\nu}(\mu)\partial_{\theta_{\mu}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi^{\mu+\nu}(\theta_{\mu}(\nu)\partial_{\theta_{\nu}}+\theta_{\mu}(\nu)\partial_{\theta_{\mu}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{\mu}(\nu)\chi^{\mu+\nu}\theta_{\mu+\nu}\in{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}}.

Next, we note that

[L_{x^{\sigma}},\sqrt{2}x^{\lambda}]=L_{x^{\sigma}}(\sqrt{2}x^{\lambda})=\sqrt{2}x^{\sigma}\cdot x^{\lambda}.

Now if
{\mathcal{J}}\not={\mathbb{K}}^{+}_{\bf q}
, then
x^{\sigma}\cdot x^{\lambda}\in\epsilon x^{\sigma+\lambda}
,
\epsilon\in\{0,\pm 1\}
. If
{\mathcal{J}}={\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}^{+}
with
{\bf q}
not elementary, then we can find
\sigma,\lambda
such that
\eta(\sigma,\lambda)\not\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. If
{\bf q}
is elementary, clearly

\eta(\sigma,\lambda)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for all
\sigma,\lambda
.
Thus

\begin{array}[]{c}\hbox{(VI)}:=[L_{x^{\sigma}},\sqrt{2}x^{\lambda}]\in{\mathbb{Z}}\sqrt{2}x^{\sigma+\lambda}\in{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}},\\
({\mathcal{J}}\not={\mathbb{K}}^{+}_{\bf q},\,{\bf q}=\hbox{non-elementary}.)\\
\end{array}

Next, we have

\displaystyle\hbox{(VII)}:=[[L_{x^{\lambda+\sigma_{2}}},L_{x^{-\sigma_{2}}}],L_{x^{\mu}}]=L_{[L_{x^{\lambda+\sigma_{2}}},L_{x^{-\sigma_{2}}}](x^{\mu})}\in{\mathbb{Z}}L_{x^{\lambda+\mu}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}},

so using this and the Jacobi identity, we get

\begin{array}[]{c}\hbox{(VIII)}:=[[L_{x^{\lambda+\sigma_{i}}},L_{x^{-\sigma_{i}}}],[L_{x^{\lambda^{\prime}+\sigma_{j}}},L_{x^{-\sigma_{j}}}]]\in{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}},\\
(i,j=1,2).\end{array}

Next, since
\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta}
is a derivation, we have

\hbox{(IX)}:=[\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta_{\mu}},L_{x^{\lambda}}]=L_{\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta_{\mu}}(x^{\lambda})}=\theta_{\mu}(\lambda)L_{\chi^{\mu}(x^{\lambda})}\in{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}},

and

\displaystyle\hbox{(X)}:=[\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta_{\mu}},[L_{x^{\lambda+\sigma_{2}}},L_{x^{-\sigma_{2}}}]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[L_{x^{-\sigma_{2}}},[\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta_{\mu}},L_{x^{\lambda+\sigma_{2}}}]]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+[L_{x^{\lambda+\sigma_{2}}},[\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta_{\mu}},L_{x^{-\sigma_{2}}}]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\theta_{\mu}(\lambda+\sigma_{2})[L_{x^{-\sigma_{2}}},L_{\chi^{\mu}(x^{\lambda+\sigma_{2}})}]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\theta_{\mu}(\sigma_{2})[L_{x^{\lambda+\sigma_{2}}},L_{\chi^{\mu}(x^{-\sigma_{2}})}]

\displaystyle(\hbox{using (I)})

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}}.

Next, we note that

\displaystyle[r_{\lambda},L_{x^{\mu}}](x^{\gamma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{\lambda}L_{x^{\mu}}(x^{\gamma})-L_{x^{\mu}}r_{\lambda}(x^{\gamma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}r_{\lambda}(x^{\mu}x^{\gamma}+x^{\gamma}x^{\mu})-\frac{1}{2}\eta(\lambda,\gamma)(x^{\mu}x^{\lambda+\gamma}+x^{\lambda+\gamma}x^{\mu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\eta(\mu,\gamma)+\eta(\gamma,\mu))r_{\lambda}(x^{\gamma+\mu})

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\eta(\lambda,\gamma)(\eta(\mu,\lambda+\gamma)+\eta(\lambda+\gamma,\mu))x^{\lambda+\mu+\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\eta(\mu,\gamma)+\eta(\gamma,\mu))\eta(\lambda,\gamma+\mu)x^{\gamma+\mu+\lambda}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\eta(\lambda,\gamma)(\eta(\mu,\lambda+\gamma)+\eta(\lambda+\gamma,\mu))x^{\lambda+\mu+\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[\eta(\mu,\gamma)\eta(\lambda,\mu)\eta(\lambda,\gamma)+\eta(\gamma,\mu)\eta(\lambda,\gamma)\eta(\lambda,\mu)

\displaystyle\qquad-(\eta(\lambda,\gamma)\eta(\mu,\lambda)\eta(\mu,\gamma)-\eta(\lambda,\gamma)\eta(\lambda,\mu)\eta(\gamma,\mu)]x^{\lambda+\mu+\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\eta(\mu+\lambda,\gamma)(\eta(\lambda,\mu)-\eta(\mu,\lambda))x^{\lambda+\mu+\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\eta(\lambda,\mu)-\eta(\mu,\lambda))x^{\lambda+\mu}x^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\eta(\lambda,\mu)-\eta(\mu,\lambda))x^{\lambda+\mu}x^{\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}(\eta(\lambda,\mu)-\eta(\lambda,\mu))x^{\gamma}x^{\lambda+\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta(\lambda,\mu)L_{x^{\lambda+\mu}}(x^{\gamma})-(\eta(\mu,\lambda)+\eta(\lambda,\mu))L_{x^{\lambda+\mu}}(x^{\gamma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\eta(\mu,\lambda)L_{x^{\lambda+\mu}}(x^{\gamma})

Thus

\begin{array}[]{c}\hbox{(XI)}:=[r_{\lambda},L_{x^{\mu}}]=-\eta(\mu,\lambda)L_{x^{\lambda+\mu}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}},\\
(\hbox{if ${\bf q}$ is elementary}).\end{array}

Also,

\displaystyle[L_{x^{\mu}}-r_{\mu},\sqrt{2}x^{\lambda}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(L_{x^{\mu}}-r_{\mu})(\sqrt{2}x^{\lambda})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(x^{\mu}\cdot x^{\lambda}-\eta(\mu,\lambda)x^{\mu+\lambda})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(\eta(\lambda,\mu)-\eta(\mu,\lambda))x^{\lambda+\mu},

and therefore,

\begin{array}[]{c}\hbox{(XII)}:=[L_{x^{\mu}}-r_{\mu},\sqrt{2}x^{\lambda}]\in{\mathbb{Z}}\sqrt{2}x^{\mu+\lambda}\in{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}},\\
(\hbox{if }{\bf q}\hbox{ is elementary}).\end{array}

Next, we want to investigate brackets of the form
[\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta_{\mu}},L_{x^{\lambda}}-r_{\lambda}].

Since we are interested in elementary case, namely
{\mathbb{K}}^{+}_{\bf e}
, we have
\eta(\lambda,\gamma)\in\{\pm 1\}
for all

\lambda,\gamma
. Therefore, by the proof of Lemma
6.2.1
we are done if we show that
[\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta_{\mu}},[L_{x^{\lambda+\tau}},L_{x^{-\tau}}]]\in{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}}
for each
\tau
. Now, a similar computation as in (X), gives

\displaystyle[\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta_{\mu}},[L_{x^{\lambda+\tau}},L_{x^{-\tau}}]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\theta_{\mu}(\lambda+\tau)[L_{x^{-\tau}},L_{x^{\lambda+\tau+\mu}}]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\theta_{\mu}(\tau)[L_{x^{\lambda+\tau}},L_{x^{-\tau+\mu}}]

\displaystyle(\epsilon\in\{0,\pm 1\},\tau^{\prime}=\tau-\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon\theta_{\mu}(\lambda+\tau)(L_{x^{\lambda+\mu}}-r_{\lambda+\mu})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\theta_{\mu}(\tau)[L_{x^{\lambda+\mu+\tau^{\prime}}},L_{x^{-\tau^{\prime}}}]

\displaystyle(\epsilon^{\prime}\in\{0,\pm 1\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon\theta_{\mu}(\lambda+\tau)(L_{x^{\lambda+\mu}}-r_{\lambda+\mu})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\epsilon^{\prime}\theta_{\mu}(\tau)(L_{x^{\lambda+\mu}}-r_{\lambda+\mu}).

Therefore, since
\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime},\theta_{\mu}(\lambda+\tau),\theta_{\mu}(\tau)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we get

\hbox{(XIII)}:=[\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta_{\mu}},L_{x^{\lambda}}-r_{\lambda}]\in{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}}.

Putting together relations (I)-(XIII), we conclude that
[{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{B}}]\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}}
, as required.
\Box

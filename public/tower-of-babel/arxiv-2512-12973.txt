Deformations of crossed homomorphisms on Lie groups
Jun Jiang

Abstract
. In this paper, we study deformations of crossed homomorphisms on Lie groups by means of the cohomology which controls them. Using the Moser type argument, we obtain several rigidity results of crossed homomorphisms on Lie groups. We further investigate the relationship between the cohomology of crossed homomorphisms on Lie groups and that on Lie algebras. Finally, we characterize the rigidity properties of all crossed homomorphisms with respect to the conjugation action on the connected and simply connected two-dimensional Lie group.



1.
Introduction

For a given algebraic or geometric structure, a central problem is that describe a neighborhood
of the structure within its moduli space. Studying deformations provides a powerful approach to this problem. Roughly speaking, a deformation of a structure is a small curve through the original structure in the moduli space. Cohomology theory offers essential tools for analyzing deformations: taking the differential of a deformation yields a
cocycle. Rigidity is a natural question need to be considered in studying deformations, and it can be characterized in terms of relevant cohomology groups. In differential geometry, the Moser argument is a classical method for investigating rigidity. Cohomological methods and Moser type arguments are similarly used to study deformations and rigidity phenomena of morphisms of given structures. For instance, cohomological approaches to deformations of Lie group homomorphisms appear in
[
12
,
1
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to develop the cohomology theory governing deformations of crossed homomorphisms on Lie groups and use it to establish several
rigidity results. Our approach is an adaptation of the Moser argument to the context of deformations of crossed homomorphisms on Lie groups.

Crossed homomorphisms (also known as 1-cocycles or difference operators) on groups were first introduced by J.H.C. Whitehead in the study of relative homotopy groups
[
17
]
. These operators found wide applications in non-abelian Galois cohomology
[
14
]
and the theory of Banach modules of locally compact groups
[
3
]
. In
[
10
]
, the authors constructed set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation using bijective crossed homomorphisms. Recently, crossed homomorphisms were used to study Hopf-Galois structures
[
15
]
and construct representations of mapping class groups of surfaces
[
2
,
9
]
. The concept of crossed homomorphisms on Lie algebras was introduced in
[
11
]
during the study of non-abelian extensions of Lie algebras, and had been applied to study representations of Cartan type Lie algebras
[
13
]
. By differentiation, crossed homomorphisms on Lie groups give rise to crossed homomorphisms on the corresponding Lie algebras. Conversely, crossed homomorphisms on Lie algebras can be integrated into crossed homomorphisms on the corresponding connected and simply connected Lie groups
[
5
]
.

Given a crossed homomorphism
D:G\,\rightarrow\,H
with respect to an action
\Theta:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Aut}(H)
, i.e.,

D(a\cdot_{G}b)=D(a)\cdot_{H}\Big(\Theta(a)D(b)\Big),\quad\forall a,b\in G.

•

We construct a representation
\Theta_{D}:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{h})
and define the cohomology of
D
as the group cohomology of
G
with coefficients in the representation
(\mathfrak{h},\Theta_{D})
(Proposition
2.4
). We establish the van Est isomorphism theorem relating the cohomology of crossed homomorphisms on Lie groups and the cohomology of crossed homomorphisms on Lie algebras(Theorem
2.12
). We then introduce a deformation
D_{s}
of
D
and show that the smooth map
\hat{D}_{s}:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
defined by

\hat{D}_{s}(a)=(R_{D_{s}(a)^{-1}})_{*D_{s}(a)}\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}D_{t+s}(a),\quad\forall a\in G.

is a
1
-cocycle(Proposition
8
).

•

A deformation
D_{s}
of
D
is said to be trivial on an interval
I=(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\subset\mathbb{R}
if there exists a smooth map
\tau:I\,\rightarrow\,H
satisfying

\tau(0)=e_{H},\quad D_{t}(a)=\tau(t)\cdot_{H}D(a)\cdot_{H}\Theta(a)\tau(t)^{-1},\quad\forall a\in G,~t\in I.

If the deformation
D_{s}
is trivial on
I
, then the cohomology class
[\hat{D}_{s}]\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(D_{s})
vanishes smoothly for all
s\in I
(Theorem
3.6
). Conversely, if the cohomology classes
[\hat{D}_{s}]
vanish smoothly for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
, then for each
a\in G
, there exists an interval
I_{a}=(-\varepsilon_{a},\varepsilon_{a})
such that

D_{t}(a)=\tau(t)\cdot_{H}D(a)\cdot_{H}\Theta(a)\tau(t)^{-1},\quad\forall a\in G,~t\in I_{a}.

The deformation
D_{s}
is called to be pointwise trivial (since
I_{a}=(-\epsilon_{a},\epsilon_{a})
depends on the point
a
)(Theorem
3.6
). In particular, for a compact Lie group
G
, any deformation of
D:G\,\rightarrow\,H
is trivial(Proposition
3.7
).

•

For the two-dimensional connected and simply connected Lie group
G
, there exists three classes of crossed homomorphisms with respect to the conjugation action (Proposition
4.1
). By analyzing whether all deformations of crossed homomorphisms are trivial, we establish complete rigidity conditions for every crossed homomorphism with respect to the conjugation action on
G
(Theorems
4.3
,
4.4
and
4.6
).

The structure of the paper is the following. In Section
2
, we develop the cohomology theory for crossed homomorphisms on Lie groups. In Section
3
, we introduce deformations of these operators and prove several rigidity theorems. In Section
4
, we end this paper with characterizing the rigidity properties of all crossed homomorphisms with respect to the conjugation action on the connected and simply connected two-dimensional Lie group.

2.
Cohomology of crossed homomorphisms on Lie groups

Let
(G,e_{G},\cdot_{G})
and
(H,e_{H},\cdot_{H})
be Lie groups. Denote by
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
the Lie algebra of
(H,e_{H},\cdot_{H})
. We briefly recall some differential calculations in
G
and
H
that will be useful for our purposes.

Define a smooth map
m:G\times G\,\rightarrow\,G
by

m(a,b)=a\cdot_{G}b,\quad\forall a,b\in G.

Denote by
L_{a}=m(a,\cdot):G\,\rightarrow\,G
and
R_{b}=m(\cdot,b):G\,\rightarrow\,G
. For smooth curves
\gamma_{1}:\mathbb{R}\,\rightarrow\,G
and
\gamma_{2}:\mathbb{R}\,\rightarrow\,G
, the following equation holds

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}m(\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}m(\gamma_{1}(0),\gamma_{2}(t))+\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}m(\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(0))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{L_{\gamma_{1}(0)}}_{*\gamma_{2}(0)}\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\gamma_{2}(t)+{R_{\gamma_{2}(0)}}_{*\gamma_{1}(0)}\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\gamma_{1}(t),

where
{L_{\gamma_{1}(0)}}_{*\gamma_{2}(0)}
and
{R_{\gamma_{2}(0)}}_{*\gamma_{1}(0)}
are tangent maps of
L_{\gamma_{1}(0)}
and
R_{\gamma_{2}(0)}
at
\gamma_{2}(0)
and
\gamma_{1}(0)
respectively.

For the Lie group
(H,e_{H},\cdot_{H})
, denote by
\mathrm{Ad}:H\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{h})
the adjoint action of
(H,e_{H},\cdot_{H})
on
\mathfrak{h}
. Then

(2)

\mathrm{Ad}(h)u=\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}h\cdot_{H}\exp(tu)\cdot_{H}h^{-1},\quad\forall h\in H,~u\in\mathfrak{h},

which means that
\mathrm{Ad}(h)u={(L_{h})}_{*h^{-1}}{(R_{h^{-1}})}_{*e_{H}}u={(R_{h^{-1}})}_{*h}{(L_{h})}_{*e_{H}}u
. Moreover, the linear map
\mathrm{Ad}_{*e_{H}}:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
is

\Big(\mathrm{Ad}_{*e_{H}}(u)\Big)(v)=[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

2.1.
Cohomology of crossed homomorphisms on Lie groups

In this subsection, we introduce cohomology of crossed homomorphisms on Lie groups.

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
17
]
)

Let
(G,e_{G},\cdot_{G})
and
(H,e_{H},\cdot_{H})
be Lie groups and
\Theta:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Aut}(H)
be an action of
(G,e_{G},\cdot_{G})
on
(H,e_{H},\cdot_{H})
. A smooth map
D:G\,\rightarrow\,H
is called a
crossed homomorphism
with respect to the action
\Theta
on Lie groups if it satisfies the following equation

(3)

D(a\cdot_{G}b)=D(a)\cdot_{H}\Big(\Theta(a)D(b)\Big),\quad\forall a,~b\in G.

In particular, a smooth map
D:G\,\rightarrow\,G
is called a crossed homomorphism with respect to the conjugation action on
G
if it satisfies the following equation

D(a\cdot_{G}b)=D(a)\cdot_{G}a\cdot_{G}D(b)\cdot_{G}a^{-1},\quad\forall a,~b\in G.

Example 2.2
.

Let
(G,e_{G},\cdot_{G})
be a Lie group. Then the inverse map
(\cdot)^{-1}:G\,\rightarrow\,G
is a crossed homomorphism with respect to the conjugation action.

Example 2.3
.

Let
(G,e_{G},\cdot_{G})
and
(H,e_{H},\cdot_{H})
be Lie groups and
\Theta:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Aut}(H)
be an action of
(G,e_{G},\cdot_{G})
on
(H,e_{H},\cdot_{H})
. Let
\mathcal{B}:H\,\rightarrow\,G
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator, i.e.
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
is a smooth map satisfying

\mathcal{B}(h_{1})\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h_{2})=\mathcal{B}(h_{1}\cdot_{H}\Theta(\mathcal{B}(h_{1}))h_{2}),\quad\forall h_{1},h_{2}\in H.

If
\mathcal{B}
is invertible, then
\mathcal{B}^{-1}:G\,\rightarrow\,H
is a crossed homomorphism with respect to the action
\Theta
on Lie groups.

Now we are ready to define a cohomology theory for crossed homomorphisms on Lie groups. First we recall a standard
version of the smooth cohomology of a Lie group
G
with coefficients in a representation
(V,\Pi)
(see e.g.
[
16
]
). An
n
-cochain is a smooth map

\alpha_{n}:\underbrace{G\times\cdots\times G}_{n}\,\rightarrow\,V.

The set of
n
-cochains
forms an abelian group, which will be denoted by
C^{n}(G,V)
. The differential
\mathrm{d}:C^{n}(G,V)\rightarrow C^{n+1}(G,V)
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(\alpha_{n})(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n},a_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pi(a_{1})\alpha_{n}(a_{2},\cdots,a_{n},a_{n+1})+(-1)^{n+1}\alpha_{n}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}\alpha_{n}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{i-1},a_{i}\cdot_{G}a_{i+1},a_{i+2},\cdots a_{n+1}).

Let
D:G\,\rightarrow\,H
be a crossed homomorphism with respect to an action
\Theta
on Lie groups. Denote by
\mathfrak{h}
the Lie algebra of the Lie group
(H,e_{H},\cdot_{H})
. Since
\Theta(a)\in\mathrm{Aut}(H)
for each
a\in G
, the induced linear map
\Theta(a)_{*e_{H}}:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
is a Lie algebra isomorphism. By
\Theta(a\cdot_{G}b)=\Theta(a)\Theta(b)
, we have that
\Theta(a\cdot_{G}b)_{*e_{H}}=\Theta(a)_{*e_{H}}\Theta(b)_{*e_{H}}
. Thus we obtain a Lie group homomorphism
\tilde{\Theta}:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{h})
defined by

(4)

\tilde{\Theta}(a)x=\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\Theta(a)\gamma(t),\quad\forall a\in G,

where
\gamma(0)=e_{H}
and
\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\gamma(t)=x
. The crossed homomorphism
D
with respect to an action
\Theta
on Lie groups induces a smooth map
\Theta_{D}:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{h})
given by

(5)

\Theta_{D}(a)u=\mathrm{Ad}_{D(a)}\tilde{\Theta}(a)u,\quad\forall a\in G,~u\in\mathfrak{h}.

Proposition 2.4
.

With the above notations, the map
\Theta_{D}:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{h})
is a representation of
(G,e_{G},\cdot_{G})
on
\mathfrak{h}
.

Proof.

For any
a,b\in G,u\in h
, by (
2
), (
3
) and (
4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}_{D(a\cdot_{G}b)}\tilde{\Theta}(a\cdot_{G}b)u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}D(a\cdot_{G}b)\cdot_{H}\Big(\Theta(a\cdot_{G}b)\exp(tu)\Big)\cdot_{H}D(a\cdot_{G}b)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}D(a)\cdot_{H}\Theta(a)\Big(D(b)\cdot_{H}\Theta(b)\exp(tu)\cdot_{H}D(b)^{-1}\Big)\cdot_{H}D(a)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}_{D(a)}\tilde{\Theta}(a)\Big(\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}D(b)\cdot_{H}\Theta(b)\exp(tu)\cdot_{H}D(b)^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}_{D(a)}\tilde{\Theta}(a)\Big(\mathrm{Ad}_{D(b)}\tilde{\Theta}(b)u\Big),

which means that
\Theta_{D}:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{h})
defined by (
5
) is a representation of
(G,e_{G},\cdot_{G})
on
\mathfrak{h}
.
∎

Define the space of
0
-cochains by
\mathfrak{h}
. For
k\geq 1
, define the space of
k
-cochains

C^{k}(D)=\{\alpha_{k}:\underbrace{G\times\cdots\times G}_{k}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}|\alpha_{k}~~\text{is a smooth map}\}.

The differential
\mathrm{d}^{D}:C^{k}(D)\rightarrow C^{k+1}(D)
is defined by

(6)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{D}\alpha_{k}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{k+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta_{D}(a_{1})\alpha_{k}(a_{2},\cdots,a_{k+1})+(-1)^{k+1}\alpha_{k}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{k})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i}\alpha_{k}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{i-1},a_{i}\cdot_{G}a_{i+1},a_{i+2},\cdots,a_{k+1}).

We know that the smooth map
\mathrm{d}^{D}
is the coboundary operator of the Lie group
(G,e_{G},\cdot_{G})
with coefficients in the representation
(\mathfrak{h},\Theta_{D})
, which means that
\mathrm{d}^{D}\circ\mathrm{d}^{D}=0
. Thus
(\oplus_{k=1}^{+\infty}C^{k}(D),\mathrm{d}^{D})
is a cochain complex, i.e.
\mathrm{d}^{D}\circ\mathrm{d}^{D}=0
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
D:G\,\rightarrow\,H
be a crossed homomorphism with respect to an action
\Theta
on Lie groups. The cohomology of the cochain complex
(C^{*}(D)=\oplus_{k=1}^{+\infty}C^{k}(D),\mathrm{d}^{D})
is defined to be the
cohomology for the crossed homomorphism
D
.

Denote by
Z^{k}(D)
the set of
k
-cocycles and
\mathcal{H}^{k}(D)
the
k
-th cohomology group.

Remark 2.6
.

For a crossed homomorphism
D:G\,\rightarrow\,G
with respect to the the conjugation action on
G
, the corresponding cohomology groups coincide with those defined for the adjoint representation in
[
6
]
.

2.2.
The van Est map

In this subsection, we show that the cohomology of crossed homomorphisms on Lie groups is the global analogue of the cohomology of crossed homomorphisms on Lie algebras.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
be Lie algebras and
\theta:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
be an action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
.

Definition 2.7
.

(
[
11
]
)

A linear map
\mathfrak{d}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
is called a
crossed homomorphism
with respect to the action
\theta
on Lie algebras if

\mathfrak{d}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})=\theta(x)\mathfrak{d}(y)-\theta(y)\mathfrak{d}(x)+[\mathfrak{d}(x),\mathfrak{d}(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

In particular, a linear map
\mathfrak{d}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is called a crossed homomorphism on
\mathfrak{g}
if it satisfies the following equation

\mathfrak{d}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})=[\mathfrak{d}(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,\mathfrak{d}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[\mathfrak{d}(x),\mathfrak{d}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall x,~y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Let
(G,\cdot_{G},e_{G})
and
(H,\cdot_{H},e_{H})
be connected and simply connected Lie groups. Denote by
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
the Lie algebras of
(G,\cdot_{G},e_{G})
and
(H,\cdot_{H},e_{H})
respectively. Let
\Theta:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Aut}(H)
be an action of
G
on
H
. Denote by
\theta:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
the action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
corresponding to
\Theta:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Aut}(H)
. The correspondence between Lie group homomorphisms and their induced Lie algebra homomorphisms naturally extends to crossed homomorphisms.

Theorem 2.8
.

(
[
4
,
5
]
)

Let
D:G\,\rightarrow\,H
be a crossed homomorphism with respect to the action
\Theta
on Lie groups. Then
\mathfrak{d}=D_{*e_{G}}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
the tangent map of
D
is a crossed homomorphism with respect to the action
\theta
on Lie algebras.

Conversely, let
\mathfrak{d}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
be a crossed homomorphism with respect to the action
\theta
on Lie algebras. Then there exists a crossed homomorphism
D:G\,\rightarrow\,H
with respect to the action
\Theta
on Lie groups such that
D_{*e_{G}}=\mathfrak{d}
.

At the end of this subsection, we establish the relationship between the cohomology of crossed homomorphisms on Lie groups and the cohomology of crossed homomorphism on Lie algebras. Recall the cohomology of crossed homomorphisms on Lie algebras as following, see
[
5
,
7
,
13
]
for more details. Let
\mathfrak{d}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
be a crossed homomorphism with respect to the action
\theta
on Lie algebras. Define
\theta_{\mathfrak{d}}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
by

(7)

\theta_{\mathfrak{d}}(x)u=\theta(x)u+[\mathfrak{d}(x),u]_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},u\in\mathfrak{h}.

It was shown in
[
11
, Proposition 3.1]
that
\theta_{\mathfrak{d}}
is a representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
. Denote by
C^{n}(\mathfrak{d})=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
.

Definition 2.9
.

(
[
13
]
)

The cohomology of the cochain complex
(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}C^{n}(\mathfrak{d}),\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{CE}}_{\theta_{\mathfrak{d}}})
is called the
cohomology of the crossed homomorphism
\mathfrak{d}
, where
\mathrm{d}_{\theta_{\mathfrak{d}}}^{\mathsf{CE}}:\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k+1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is the Chevalley-Eilenberg coboundary operator of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
with coefficients in the representation
(\mathfrak{h},\theta_{\mathfrak{d}})
. Denote its
n
-th cohomology group by
\mathcal{H}^{n}(\mathfrak{d})
.

Let
D:G\,\rightarrow\,H
be a crossed homomorphism with respect to an action
\Theta
on Lie groups. According to the Proposition
2.4
, the map
\Theta_{D}:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{h})
given by

\Theta_{D}(a)u=\mathrm{Ad}_{D(a)}\tilde{\Theta}(a)u,\quad\forall a\in G,u\in\mathfrak{h}.

is a representation of
G
on
\mathfrak{h}
. Denote by
\mathfrak{d}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
the crossed homomorphism with respect to the action
\theta
on Lie algebras corresponding to
D
.

Proposition 2.10
.

With the above notations, the tangent map of
\Theta_{D}:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{h})
is the linear map
\theta_{\mathfrak{d}}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
.

Proof.

Denote by
\exp_{G}
and
\exp_{H}
are exponential maps of
(G,e_{G},\cdot_{G})
and
(H,e_{H},\cdot_{H})
respectively. For any
x\in\mathfrak{g}
and
u\in\mathfrak{h}
, by (
5
) and (
7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}|_{s=0}\Theta_{D}(\exp_{G}(sx))u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}|_{s=0}\mathrm{Ad}_{D(\exp_{G}(sx))}\tilde{\Theta}(\exp_{G}(sx))u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}|_{s=0}\mathrm{Ad}_{D(\exp_{G}(sx))}u+\frac{d}{ds}|_{s=0}\tilde{\Theta}(\exp_{G}(sx))u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{d}(x),u]+\theta(x)u=\theta_{\mathfrak{d}}(x)u.

Thus the tangent map of
\Theta_{D}:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{h})
is
\theta_{\mathfrak{d}}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
.
∎

Let
D:G\,\rightarrow\,H
be a crossed homomorphism with respect to the action
\Theta
on Lie groups. Denote by
\mathfrak{d}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
the crossed homomorphism with respect to the action
\theta
on Lie algebras corresponding to
D
. Define
\mathsf{VE}:C^{k}(D)\,\rightarrow\,C^{k}(\mathfrak{d})
by

\displaystyle\mathsf{VE}(F)(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\mathsf{S}(k)}(-1)^{|s|}\frac{d}{dt_{s(1)}}\cdots\frac{d}{dt_{s(k)}}\bigg|_{t_{s(1)}=t_{s(2)}=\cdots=t_{s(k)}=0}F(\exp_{G}(t_{s(1)}x_{s(1)}),\cdots,\exp_{G}(t_{s(k)}x_{s(k)})),

for all
F\in C^{k}(D),~x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}\in\mathfrak{g}
, where
\exp_{G}
is the exponential map of
(G,e_{G},\cdot_{G})
.

Proposition 2.11
.

With the above notations,
\mathsf{VE}
is a cochain map, i.e. we have the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{\cdots\longrightarrow C^{k-1}(D)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{\hskip 8.04996pt\mathrm{d}^{D}}
\textstyle{C^{k}(D)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathsf{VE}}
\scriptstyle{\hskip 8.04996pt\mathrm{d}^{D}}
\textstyle{C^{k+1}(D)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathsf{VE}}
\textstyle{\cdots}
\textstyle{\cdots\longrightarrow C^{k-1}(\mathfrak{d})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\hskip 8.04996pt\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{CE}}_{\theta_{\mathfrak{d}}}}
\textstyle{C^{k}(\mathfrak{d})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\hskip 8.04996pt\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{CE}}_{\theta_{\mathfrak{d}}}}
\textstyle{C^{k+1}(\mathfrak{d})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots.}

Consequently,
\mathsf{VE}
induces a homomorphism
\mathsf{VE}_{*}
from the cohomology
group
\mathcal{H}^{k}(D)
to
\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathfrak{d})
. The map
\mathsf{VE}
is called the
Van Est map
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.10
, the differentiation of the representation
\Theta_{D}:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{h})
of Lie group
(G,e_{G},\cdot_{G})
is the representation
\theta_{\mathfrak{d}}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
. Since the cochains
C^{k}(D)
and

C^{k}(\mathfrak{d})
are in fact exactly those of Lie group
(G,e_{G},\cdot_{G})
and the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
with representations
(\mathfrak{h},\Theta_{D})
and
(\mathfrak{h},\theta_{\mathfrak{d}})
respectively, the conclusion follows
from the classical argument for the cohomology of Lie groups and Lie
algebras.
∎

Just as the classical situation, under certain conditions, the cohomology group
\mathcal{H}^{k}(D)
and
\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathfrak{d})
are isomorphic.

Theorem 2.12
.

If the Lie group
(G,e_{G},\cdot_{G})
is connected and its homotopy
groups are trivial in
1,2,\cdots,n
, then for
1\leq k\leq n
, the
cohomology group
\mathcal{H}^{k}(D)
of the crossed homomorphism
D
on Lie groups is isomorphic to the cohomology
group
\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathfrak{d})

3.

In this subsection,
(G,e_{G},\cdot_{G})
and

Definition 3.4
.

\end{array}\right),s)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a^{g(s)}&h(s)\frac{a^{2+g(s)}-a^{-g(s)}}{2(g(s)+1)}-ba^{1+g(s)}\\
0&a^{-g(s)}\\
\end{array}\right),\quad\forall\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
0&a^{-1}\\
\end{array}\right)\in G,~~\forall s\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon),

where
h:(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\,\rightarrow\,\mathbb{R}
and
g:(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\,\rightarrow\,\mathbb{R}
are smooth functions satisfying
h(0)=q,g(0)=p
and
g(s)\neq 0,-1
for all
s\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
.

Theorem 4.6
.

With the above notations, if there exists an open interval
I\subset(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
such that
g^{\prime}(s)=0
for all
s\in I
, then the deformation
\mathcal{D}
is trivial. Otherwise, the deformation
\mathcal{D}
is nontrivial.

Proof.

By (
8
), we have that

\displaystyle\hat{D}_{s}(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
0&a^{-1}\\
\end{array}\right))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\mathcal{D}(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
0&a^{-1}\\
\end{array}\right),s+t)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a^{-g(s)}&-h(s)\frac{a^{2+g(s)}-a^{-g(s)}}{2(g(s)+1)}+ba^{1+g(s)}\\
0&a^{g(s)}\\
\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a^{g(s+t)}&h(s+t)\frac{a^{2+g(s+t)}-a^{-g(s+t)}}{2(g(s+t)+1)}-ba^{1+g(s+t)}\\
0&a^{-g(s+t)}\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a^{-g(s)}&-h(s)\frac{a^{2+g(s)}-a^{-g(s)}}{2(g(s)+1)}+ba^{1+g(s)}\\
0&a^{g(s)}\\
\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g^{\prime}(s)\ln a&\frac{h(s)g^{\prime}(s)\ln a}{g(s)+1}+\frac{h^{\prime}(s)(a^{2+2g(s)}-1)}{2g(s)+2}-\frac{2h(s)g^{\prime}(s)(a^{2+2g(s)}-1)}{(2g(s)+2)^{2}}\\
0&-g^{\prime}(s)\ln a\\
\end{array}\right).

If there exists an open interval
I\subset(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
such that
g^{\prime}(s)=0
for all
s\in I
, define

\kappa(s)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\kappa_{1}(s)&\frac{h^{\prime}(s)+2h(s)\kappa_{1}(s)}{2+2p}\\
0&-\kappa_{1}(s)\\
\end{array}\right),

where
\kappa_{1}:I\,\rightarrow\,\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{D_{s}}(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\kappa_{1}(s)&\frac{h^{\prime}(s)+2h(s)\kappa_{1}(s)}{2+2p}\\
0&-\kappa_{1}(s)\\
\end{array}\right))\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
0&a^{-1}\\
\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta_{D_{s}}(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
0&a^{-1}\\
\end{array}\right))\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\kappa_{1}(s)&\frac{h^{\prime}(s)+2h(s)\kappa_{1}(s)}{2+2p}\\
0&-\kappa_{1}(s)\\
\end{array}\right)-\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\kappa_{1}(s)&\frac{h^{\prime}(s)+2h(s)\kappa_{1}(s)}{2+2p}\\

\end{array}\right)=\hat{D}_{s}(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
0&a^{-1}\\
\end{array}\right)).

Moreover, define a smooth map
\tau:I\,\rightarrow\,G
by
\tau(s)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\tau_{1}(s)&\frac{q\tau(s)}{2p+2}-\frac{h(s)}{(2p+2)\tau_{1}(s)}\\

\end{array}\right)
, where
\tau_{1}:I\,\rightarrow\,\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\

\end{array}\right),s)=\tau(s)D(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\

\end{array}\right).

Thus the deformation
\mathcal{D}
is trivial.

Normalized solutions to a Choquard equation involving mixed local and nonlocal operators
J. Giacomoni, Nidhi Nidhi, K. Sreenadh

Abstract
In the present paper, we study the existence of normalized solutions for a Choquard type equation involving mixed diffusion type operators. We also provide regularity results of these solutions. Next, the equivalence between existence of normalized solutions and the existence of normalized ground states is established. Keywords: Normalized solutions, local-nonlocal mixed operator, Choquard equation, Sobolev regularity, existence results, ground states.



1
Introduction and Main Results

In the present work, we are interested to study the existence and properties of the normalized solutions to the following Choquard equation involving mixed local and nonlocal type operators:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{L}u+u&=&\mu(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{p})|u|^{p-2}u\;\;\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2}&=&\tau,\end{array}

(1.1)

where
\frac{n+\alpha}{n}\leq p\leq\frac{2s+n+\alpha}{n}
,
\tau>0
is a constant,
\mu>0
is a parameter,
I_{\alpha}
is the Riesz potential of order
\alpha\in(0,n)
defined by

I_{\alpha}=\frac{A_{n,\alpha}}{|x|^{n-\alpha}},\;\;\text{with }A_{n,\alpha}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n-\alpha}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}2^{\alpha}\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})}\;\text{ for every }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}

and the mixed operator
\mathcal{L}
is given by

\mathcal{L}=-\Delta+\lambda(-\Delta)^{s}\;\text{ for some }s\in(0,1)\text{ and parameter }\lambda>0.

Here, the operator
\mathcal{L}
is referred as a mixed operator since it contains both local and nonlocal properties. It involves the classical Laplacian
(-\Delta)
and the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
defined as:

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=C(n,s)\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy\;\text{ for }s\in(0,1),

where
C(n,s)
is a normalizing constant given by

C(n,s)=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1-cos(x)}{|x|^{n+2s}}dx\right)^{-1}

and P.V. is the abbreviation for principal value.

We are interested to study the operator
\mathcal{L}
due to its wide range of applications. Broadly speaking, such an operator comes into the picture whenever the impact on a physical phenomenon is caused by some local as well as nonlocal changes. One may come across this operator while studying the bi-model power law distribution processes, see
[
41
]
.
It is also considered in the theory of optimal searching, biomathematics and animal foraging, see
[
16
]
for further extend.
Issues regarding the existence of solutions, their regularity
and symmetry properties, Faber-Krahn type inequality, Neumann problems and Green functions estimates have been investigated in different contributions (see in particular
[
10
,
11
,
1
]
) and for a further study about the semilinear elliptic equations involving mixed operators, we quote
[
4
]
and references therein. In the present work, we would like to study a certain type of solutions, precisely normalized solutions, for a class of elliptic problems involving the operator
\mathcal{L}
that to the best of our knowledge has not been discussed in the former literature.

We highlight that the study of normalized solutions has significant importance in physics, as it represents an entity satisfying the dynamics along with a fixed mass. Consider for instance the following semilinear equation with a constraint:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&-\Delta u=\lambda u+g(u)\;\;\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
&\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2}=c.\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

The physical motivation to study (
1.2
) can be given by the fact that its solution gives stationary states of a nonlinear Schr
\ddot{\text{o}}
dinger equation with prescribed
L^{2}-
norm. Formally, the solution of (
1.2
) is called a normalized solution. In
[
24
]
, Jeanjean obtained the existence of radial solutions for (
1.2
) under some assumptions on
g
. In
[
6
]
, the existence of infinite solutions to (
1.2
) under same assumptions has been shown.
Further in
[
35
]
, the normalized solutions are discussed and described in case of bounded domains with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Considering the domain to be the unit ball and
g(x)=|x|^{p-1}x
, the existence of normalized solutions has been seen for
p
lying in
(1,1+\frac{4}{n}),\;(1+\frac{4}{n},2^{*}-1)
and
p=1+\frac{4}{n}
under some conditions on
c
.
Moreover, the problem in general bounded domains has been dealt by the authors in
[
38
]
. Existence of normalized solutions of nonlinear Schr
\ddot{\text{o}}
dinger systems has been also extensively studied, interested readers can go through
[
23
,
8
,
7
,
9
,
36
,
37
]
. Normalized solutions are also taken into consideration in the study of quadratic ergodic mean field games system, see in particular
[
42
]
.

Recently, the study of normalized solutions of the Choquard equation has attracted many researchers. This investigation started with the work of Lieb in
[
27
]
, where they studied the existence and uniqueness of the minimizing solution for the problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\Delta u+\lambda u&=&\mu(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{p})|u|^{p-2}u\;\;\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2}&=&\tau.\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

Pekar’s work in
[
39
]
, which examines the model in quantum theory of a polaron at rest by considering the equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&-\Delta u+u=(I_{2}*|u|^{2})u\;\;\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
&u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

shed light on the significance of the Choquard equation. Further in 1976, Choquard used the energy functional associated to (
1.4
) in order to study a suitable approximation to Hartree-Fock theory for one component plasma. The equation is mainly used to describe an electron trapped in its own hole (see
[
27
]
). It also arises in quantum mechanics, see
[
40
]
and references therein. Many authors have subsequently studied the existence, the multiplicity and qualitative properties of the solution to the problem (
1.3
), see
[
17
,
32
,
30
]
.

Considering (
1.1
), we look for weak solutions in the energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
equipped with the inner product defined as:

\langle u,v\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla u.\nabla vdx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u.vdx+\lambda\ll u,v\gg,

where

\ll u,v\gg:=\frac{C(n,s)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y)).(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy,

for each
u,v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the corresponding norm as follows:

\left\|u\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}:=\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda[u]^{2}+\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2};\text{ with }[u]^{2}:=\frac{C(n,s)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy.

Similarly,
\|u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}:=[u]^{2}+\|u\|^{2}_{2}.

Precisely, we give below the notion of weak solutions:

Definition 1.1
.

A function
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is said to be a weak solution of (
1.1
) if
\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2}=\tau
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla u.\nabla vdx+\lambda\ll u,v\gg+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u.vdx=\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{p})|u|^{p-2}uvdx,

for each
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The energy functional associated to (
1.1
) is given for any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by :

S_{\lambda}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{p})|u|^{p}+\frac{\lambda[u]^{2}}{2}.

First, we investigate the regularity of weak solutions. More precisely, we establish the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
n\geq 3
and
\alpha\in(0,n)
. If
u
is a solution of (
1.1
), then

u\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every
q\in[2,\frac{n}{\alpha}\frac{2n}{n-2})
.

Using Theorem 1.1 we obtain Sobolev regularity of weak solutions:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 3
,
n-\alpha<4
,
2\leq p\leq\frac{n+\alpha}{n-2}
and
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a solution of (
1.1
), then
u\in W^{2,q}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
q\geq 1
.

An important tool to establish the above regularity results is the following well-known Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality (see
[
34
]
):

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
t,r>1
and
0<\alpha<n
with
1/t+1/r=1+\alpha/n
,
f\in L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
h\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. There exists a sharp constant
C(t,\alpha,n)
independent of
f,h
, such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x)h(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}\penalty\ dxdy\leq C(t,\alpha,n)\|f\|_{L^{t}}\|h\|_{L^{r}}.

(1.5)

If
t=r=2n/(n+\alpha)
, then

\displaystyle C(t,\alpha,n)=C(n,\alpha)=\pi^{\frac{n-\alpha}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{n+\alpha}{2})}\left\{\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\Gamma(n)}\right\}^{-\frac{\alpha}{n}}.

(1.6)

Equality holds in (
1.5
) if and only if
\frac{f}{h}\equiv constant
and

\displaystyle h(x)=A(\gamma^{2}+|x-a|^{2})^{(N+\alpha)/2}

for some
A\in\mathbb{C},0\neq\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

From this inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}_{q}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)|^{q}|u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}\penalty\ dxdy}

is well defined if
\frac{n+\alpha}{n}\leq q\leq\frac{n+\alpha}{n-2}=2^{*}_{\alpha}
. The exponent
q=2^{*}_{\alpha}
is known as Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev critical exponent and similarly to the usual critical exponent,
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\ni\,u\to\,\mathcal{A}_{2^{*}_{\alpha}}(u)
is continuous for the norm topology but not for the weak topology.

The existence issue has been discussed for problems involving local operators. In particular, in
[
26
]
, Lei, Yang and Zhang have proved the equivalence of normalized solutions and ground state solutions to the problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}-\Delta u+u&=\mu(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{p})|u|^{p-2}u\;\;\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2}&=\tau,\end{array}\right.

(1.7)

for the case
\frac{n+\alpha}{n}<p<\frac{2+n+\alpha}{n}
and hence obtained the existence of weak solutions to (
1.7
). Further they deduced nonexistence results for
p=\frac{n+\alpha}{n}
,
p=\frac{n+\alpha}{n-2}
and
p=\frac{2+n+\alpha}{n}
. By a ground state solution, we mean here a solution to the constrained minimization problem given by:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2}=\tau,\\
&F(u)=\min\{F(w)\;:\;w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ such that }\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2}=\tau\},\end{array}\right.

(1.8)

where
F
is the energy functional associated to (
1.7
). We extend similar results for our problem involving mixed type operators.
The following notations will be used through out this paper:

H(u):=\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2},\;\;T(u):=\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda[u]^{2},\;\;\Gamma:=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\;:\;H(u)=\tau\},

A:=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\;:\;u\neq 0\text{ and }S_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u)(u)=0\},\;\;G:=\{u\in A\;:\;S_{\lambda}(u)\leq S_{\lambda}(v)\text{ for all }v\in A\}.

Results corresponding to the problem (
1.1
) are as follows:

Theorem 1.3
.

For
\frac{n+\alpha}{n}<p<\frac{2s+n+\alpha}{n}
,
s\in(0,1)
,
\mu>0
and
\lambda>0
being sufficiently small, the problem (
1.1
) has a weak solution.

We investigate the existence of ground state solutions by considering the following minimization problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&u\in\Gamma,\\
&S_{\lambda}(u)=\min\{S_{\lambda}(w)\;:\;w\in\Gamma\}.\end{array}\right.

(1.9)

Then we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

For
\frac{n+\alpha}{n}<p<\frac{2s+n+\alpha}{n}
,
s\in(0,1)
,
\mu>0
and
\lambda>0
being sufficiently small, the problems (
1.1
) and (
1.9
) are equivalent.

We believe that theorems
1.3
and
1.4
hold for any
\lambda>0
and
\frac{n+\alpha}{n}<p<\frac{n+\alpha}{n-2}
. In this regard, we deal with nonexistence results and show that as
p
takes the upper critical (
p=\frac{n+\alpha}{n-2}
) or lower critical value (
p=\frac{n+\alpha}{n}
) with respect to the above mentioned Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, the problem (
1.1
) has no solution. Precisely, we establish:

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume that
p=\frac{n+\alpha}{n}
or
p=\frac{n+\alpha}{n-2}
, then the problem (
1.1
) has no weak solution in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 deals with the proof of regularity results (Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
) whereas Section 3 is devoted to the existence of weak solutions and the equivalence result given in Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
respectively. Concerning the regularity of solutions, we obtain that a weak solution lies in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
q\in[2,(\frac{n}{\alpha})\frac{2n}{n-2})
by following the ideas in
[
33
, Moroz-Schaftingen]
. Further, using the estimate given in Proposition 2.2 and Theorem 1.1, we obtain the Sobolev regularity given in Theorem
1.2
. Next, we get the existence result by appealing an "asymptotic" Pohozaev type identity together with concentration compactness arguments applied to a minimizing sequence and analysing the associated sequence of Lagrange multipliers
\{\Lambda^{\lambda}_{k}\}
(see Lemma
3.2
). Furthermore, Theorem
1.4
is established by showing that a function is a ground-state solution if and only if it belongs to
G\cap\Gamma
. Finally the proof of nonexistence result (Theorem
1.5
) ends Section 3. The Pohozaev identity serves as a crucial instrument to get these existence and non existence results, it can obtained by combining the work of Moroz-Schaftingen
[
32
, proposition 3.1]
and Anthal-Garain
[
3
, Theorem 2.5]
(where the authors use difference quotient approximations as in
[
2
]
and
[
29
]
). We have added the sketch of the proof in the appendix. We point out that the presence of the non local part of the mixed operator introduces technical difficulties and forces the use of accurate estimates along the proofs of our main results.

2
Regularity Results

In this section, we will prove regularity results for a solution to (
1.1
). For this, we first show that every solution of (
1.1
) belongs to
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
q\in[2,\frac{n}{\alpha}\frac{2n}{n-2})
using the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
M,N\in L^{\frac{2n}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})+L^{\frac{2n}{\alpha+2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
solves

-\Delta u+\lambda(-\Delta)^{s}u+u=\mu(I_{\alpha}*Mu)N\;\;\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

(2.1)

then
u\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every
q\in[2,\frac{n}{\alpha}\frac{2n}{n-2})
.

Proof.

By Lemma 3.2 of
[
33
]
, taking
\theta=1
, for
\epsilon=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8\mu}}
there exists
\beta>0
such that

\displaystyle 4\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}*|M||w|)|N||w|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|\nabla w\right\|_{2}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\beta\left\|w\right\|_{2}^{2}}{2}\leq\frac{\left\|\nabla w\right\|_{2}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\beta\left\|w\right\|_{2}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}[w]^{2},

for every
w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Motivated by the poineering work of V. Moroz and J. Van Schaftingen in
[
33
]
(see in particular Proposition 3.1 there), taking
M=M_{1}+M_{2}
and
N=N_{1}+N_{2}
where
M_{1}
,
N_{1}\in L^{\frac{2n}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
M_{2}
,
N_{2}\in L^{\frac{2n}{\alpha+2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we define the sequences
\{M_{k}\}
and
\{N_{k}\}
as follows:

M_{k}=M_{1,k}+M_{2,k}\text{ and }N_{k}=N_{1,k}+N_{2,k},

where
M_{1,k}=M_{1}
,
N_{1,k}=N_{1}
,
M_{2,k}=\chi_{\bar{B}(0,k)}\bar{M}_{2,k}
and
N_{2,k}=\chi_{\bar{B}(0,k)}\bar{N}_{2,k}
with
\chi_{S}
being the characterisitic function on set
S
and
\bar{M}_{2,k}
,
\bar{N}_{2,k}
are the truncations of
M_{2}
and
N_{2}
respectively, defined as:

\bar{M}_{2,k}=\max\{-k,\min\{k,M_{2}\}\}\text{ and }\bar{N}=\max\{-k,\min\{k,N_{2}\}\}.

Then clearly

\{M_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{N_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
belong to
L^{\frac{2n}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and satisfy

|M_{k}|\leq 2|M|,\;\{M_{k}(x)\}\rightarrow M(x)
almost everywhere,

|N_{k}|\leq 2|N|
and
\{N_{k}(x)\}\rightarrow N(x)
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
a_{k}:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as:

a_{k}(w,v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla w.\nabla vdx+\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w.vdx-\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}*M_{k}w)N_{k}vdx+\lambda\ll w,v\gg.

Since
M_{k},N_{k}\in L^{\frac{2n}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{\frac{2n}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})+L^{\frac{2n}{\alpha+2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle a_{k}(v,v)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{2}^{2}+\beta\left\|v\right\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda[v]^{2}-\left(\frac{\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{2}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\beta\left\|v\right\|_{2}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}[w]^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\text{C}\left(\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|w\right\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda[w]^{2}\right),\text{ here C}=\min\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{\beta}{2}\}.

Thus
a_{k}
is a coercive bilinear form and hence applying Lax-Milgram theorem
[
15
, corollary 5.8]
with the linear continuous form
f:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined as:

f(v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\beta-1)uvdx,\;\;\forall v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

there exists a unique
u_{k}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
a_{k}(u_{k},v)=f(v)
for every
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. This implies that
u_{k}
is a solution of

-\Delta u_{k}+\lambda(-\Delta)^{s}u_{k}+\beta u_{k}=\mu(I_{\alpha}*M_{k}u_{k})N_{k}+(\beta-1)u\;\;\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(2.2)

Now, taking
\phi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, since
N_{1,k}\phi=N_{1}\phi\in L^{\frac{2n}{n+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we get
(I_{\alpha}*(N_{1,k}\phi))\rightarrow(I_{\alpha}*(N_{1}\phi))
in
L^{\frac{2n}{n-\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by
[
31
, A-2]
. Similarly, since, by Dominated convergence theorem,
\{N_{2,k}\phi\}\rightarrow N_{2}\phi
in
L^{\frac{2n}{n+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we get
(I_{\alpha}*(N_{2,k}\phi))\rightarrow(I_{\alpha}*(N_{2}\phi))
in
L^{\frac{2n}{n-\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Thus

I_{\alpha}*N_{k}\phi\rightarrow I_{\alpha}*N\phi\text{ in }L^{\frac{2n}{n-\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Now, from the uniform coercivity of
a_{k}
, taking
u_{k}
as a test function in (
2.2
) and using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain the boundedness of
\{u_{k}\}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, hence
\{u_{k}\}
converges weakly to some
w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
up to a subsequence. However, one can easily check that
\{M_{k}u_{k}g\}\rightarrow Mwg
for every
g\in L^{\frac{2n}{n-\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and hence:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}*M_{k}u_{k})N_{k}\phi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}*N_{k}\phi)M_{k}u_{k}\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}*Mw)N\phi.

This indicates that
w
satisfies
-\Delta w+\lambda(-\Delta)^{s}w+\beta w=\mu(I_{\alpha}*Mw)N+(\beta-1)u\text{ in }H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and since
u
is its unique solution of (
2.1
) (by the coercivity of the associated bilinear form),
w=u
(again by coercivity of the bilinear form) and the whole sequence
\{u_{k}\}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.
Now let’s define the truncation
u_{k,\gamma}
corresponding to
\gamma>0
as follows,

u_{k,\gamma}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\gamma&\text{if }u_{k}(x)\leq-\gamma,\\
u_{k}(x)&\text{if }-\gamma<u_{k}(x)<\gamma,\\
\gamma&\text{if }u_{k}(x)\geq\gamma.\end{array}\right.

(2.3)

Taking
v(x)=|u_{k,\gamma}(x)|^{q-2}u_{k,\gamma}(x)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as a test function in (
2.2
), we get

\displaystyle(q-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{k,\gamma}|^{q-2}\nabla u_{k}.\nabla u_{k,\gamma}dx+\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{k,\gamma}|^{q-2}u_{k}(x).u_{k,\gamma}(x)dx

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda C(n,s)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y))(|u_{k,\gamma}(x)|^{q-2}u_{k,\gamma}(x)-|u_{k,\gamma}(y)|^{q-2}u_{k,\gamma}(y))}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy

\displaystyle=\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}*M_{k}u_{k})N_{k}|u_{k,\gamma}|^{q-2}u_{k,\gamma}dx+(\beta-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(x)|u_{k,\gamma}(x)|^{q-2}u_{k,\gamma}(x)dx.

By using lemma 3.5 of
[
22
]
, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda C(n,s)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y))(|u_{k,\gamma}(x)|^{q-2}u_{k,\gamma}(x)-|u_{k,\gamma}(y)|^{q-2}u_{k,\gamma}(y))}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy

\displaystyle\geq\frac{4(q-1)\lambda C(n,s)}{2q^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{||u_{k,\gamma}(x)|^{\frac{q}{2}}-|u_{k,\gamma}(y)|^{\frac{q}{2}}|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy,

also, by construction of
u_{k,\gamma}
we have
|u_{k,\gamma}|^{q}\leq|u_{k,\gamma}|^{q-2}u_{k,\gamma}u_{k}
, and hence

(3.3)

From (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we deduce that

\frac{\mu}{2p}\mathcal{A}_{p}(u)=\frac{\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2}}{n+\alpha-p(n-2)}+\frac{(1-s)}{(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}\lambda[u]^{2}

and thus

S_{\lambda}(u)=\frac{\mu(p-1)}{2p}\mathcal{A}_{p}(u)=\frac{(p-1)\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2}}{n+\alpha-p(n-2)}+\frac{\lambda(1-s)(p-1)[u]^{2}}{(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}>0

for
\frac{n+\alpha}{n}<p<\frac{2s+n+\alpha}{n}\text{ and }s\in(0,1).
Now, if
u\in G
, we get

\min\{S_{\lambda}(u):\;u\in A\}=l>0.

Now, let us define

I_{\lambda}(u):=\frac{\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\lambda[u]^{2}}{2}-\frac{\mu\mathcal{A}_{p}(u)}{2p}.

The following Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality for Choquard nonlinearity will help us to study the boundedness of the functional
I
on
\Gamma
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For
\frac{n+\alpha}{n}\leq p\leq\frac{2s+n+\alpha}{n}
,
there exists a positive constant
C_{n,p}
such that :

\mathcal{A}_{p}(u)\leq C_{n,p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{np-n-\alpha}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{n+\alpha-p(n-2)}{2}}

(3.4)

for every
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Let
r,t>1
be such that
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{t}=1+\frac{\alpha}{n}
, then by the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality we have:

\mathcal{A}_{p}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{A_{\alpha}|u(x)|^{p}|u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy\leq C_{n,r,s}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{pr}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{pt}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{t}},

for
r,t\in(1,\frac{n}{\alpha})\cap[\frac{2}{p},\frac{2n}{p(n-2)}]
. Since
1<\frac{2n}{p(n-2)}
and
\frac{2}{p}<\frac{n}{\alpha}
, we can find such
r
and
t
.
Now taking
q>0
such that
q+1=pr
, then by Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (see
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
), precisely,
\left\|u\right\|_{\beta}\leq C_{n,\beta}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{\theta}\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{1-\theta}
where
\theta=\frac{N(\beta-2)}{2\beta}
, we have:

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{pr}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(C_{n,q}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{n(q-1)}{4}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{(q+1)}{2}-\frac{n(q-1)}{4}}\right)^{\frac{p}{q+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{n,r}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{n(pr-2)}{4r}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-\frac{n(pr-2)}{4r}}.

Similarly, we can deduce that

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{p{t}}\right)^{\frac{1}{t}}\leq C_{n,t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{n(p{t}-2)}{4s}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-\frac{n(p{t}-2)}{4s}}.

Thus we get

\mathcal{A}_{p}(u)\leq C_{n,r,{t}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{\frac{n(pr-2)}{4r}+\frac{n(p{t}-2)}{4{t}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{2}dx\right)^{p-\frac{n(pr-2)}{4r}-\frac{n(p{t}-2)}{4{t}}}.

(3.5)

Since
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{{t}}=1+\frac{\alpha}{n}
, we get
{t}=\frac{rn}{rn+\alpha r-n}
which implies

\frac{n(p{t}-2)}{4{t}}=\frac{prn-2rn-2\alpha r+2n}{4r}

and hence

\frac{n(pr-2)}{4r}+\frac{n(p{t}-2)}{4{t}}=\frac{n(pr-2)}{4r}+\frac{prn-2rn-2\alpha r+2n}{4r}=\frac{np-n-\alpha}{2}.

(3.6)

Note that taking such
r
and
t
makes sense because for
\frac{n+\alpha}{n}\leq p\leq\frac{2s+n+\alpha}{n}
, the following inequalities hold:

\frac{2n}{2(n+\alpha)-p(n-2)}\leq\frac{2n}{p(n-2)},\;\;\frac{2}{p}\leq\frac{2n}{2(n+\alpha)-np},

\frac{2n}{2(n+\alpha)-p(n-2)}<\frac{n}{\alpha}\text{ and }1<\frac{2n}{2(n+\alpha)-np},

hence
(1,\frac{n}{\alpha})\cap[\frac{2}{p},\frac{2n}{p(n-2)}]\cap[\frac{2n}{2(n+\alpha)-p(n-2)},\frac{2n}{2(n+\alpha)-np}]
is non-empty. Thus,
using (
3.6
) in (
3.5
), we get:

\mathcal{A}_{p}(u)\leq C_{n,p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{\frac{np-n-\alpha}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{n+\alpha-p(n-2)}{2}}.

∎

Lemma 3.1
.

Define
-m_{\lambda}:=\inf\{I_{\lambda}(v):v\in\Gamma\}
, then for
\alpha\in(0,n)
and

whenever

the functional

.

Proof.

Claim : I is bounded below on
\Gamma
, for
\frac{n+\alpha}{n}<p\leq\frac{2+n+\alpha}{n}
.

Proof of Claim. Let

be arbitrary, using (

) and Young’s inequality, we deduce that

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\mu C_{n,p}}{2p}(\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2})^{\frac{n+\alpha-p(n-2)}{2}}(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2})^{\frac{np-n-\alpha}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{a^{\kappa}}{\kappa}-\frac{\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}(np-n-\alpha)}{2.2^{\beta}}

,
\beta=\frac{2}{np-n-\alpha}>1
and

. Therefore,

(3.7)

Proof of 1.

Let

and

be arbitrary. We define

Clearly,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K^{2}}{2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{K^{2s}}{2}\lambda[u]^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2p}K^{np-n-\alpha}\mathcal{A}_{p}(u).

(3.8)

Since

, we deduce that

for small

. Hence

Proof of 2.

Let
u\in\Gamma
be arbitrary,
then for

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}}{2}-\frac{n\mu C_{n,p}}{2+n+\alpha}(\tau)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{n}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}>0

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{np-n-\alpha}{4}\right)\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\tau}{2}\left(1-(n+\alpha+2-np)\left(\frac{C_{n,p}\mu}{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{n+\alpha+2-np}}(\tau)^{\frac{2(p-1)}{n+\alpha+2-np}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{2}\left(1-(n+\alpha+2-np)\left(\frac{C_{n,p}\mu}{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{n+\alpha+2-np}}(\tau)^{\frac{2(p-1)}{n+\alpha+2-np}}\right).

Let
G=(n+\alpha+2-np)\left(\frac{C_{n,p}\mu}{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{n+\alpha+2-np}}(\tau)^{\frac{2(p-1)}{n+\alpha+2-np}}
.
Then, we have

\frac{\tau}{2}(1-G)<C_{\delta}\frac{\lambda[u]^{2}}{2},\;\;\;\forall\lambda>0.

Since the left hand side is independent of the paramter
\lambda
which can be as small as we want, we must have

\frac{\tau}{2}(1-G)\leq 0\;\;\forall\;\mu>0.

Netherveless, for

0<\mu<\mu^{*}=\frac{p(\tau)^{(1-p)}}{C_{n,p}(n+\alpha+2-np)^{\frac{n+\alpha+2-np}{2}}},

we get
G<1
, which gives a contradiction.
Hence, we are left with only one possiblity,
\delta=1
.
Therefore,
u=v
is a solution of (
1.1
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
and
1.4
:

By Lemma 3.2, we know that for
\frac{n+\alpha}{n}<p<\frac{2s+n+\alpha}{n}
, there exists a solution of (
3.9
). Since, by step 1 of Lemma 3.3, (
3.9
) and (
1.9
) are equivalent, (
1.9
) has a solution and hence by Lemma 3.3, (
1.1
) has a solution. Thus, we are done with Theorem 1.3.
As the minimizer of S on
\Gamma
belongs to
A
, thus
A
is nonempty.

Now, let
u
be a solution of (
1.9
), and hence solves (
1.1
). Then as
u
becomes a critical point of
S
, we get

H(u)=\tau\;;\;T(u)+\tau=\mu\mathcal{A}_{p}(u)

(3.38)

and

\mu\mathcal{A}_{p}(u)=\lambda\left(\frac{n-2s}{2}\right)[u]^{2}+\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{n\tau}{2}.

From the above equations, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{2p}\mathcal{A}_{p}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{n+\alpha-p(n-2)}+\frac{\lambda(1-s)[u]^{2}}{(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}

and

\frac{\mu}{2p}\mathcal{A}_{p}(u)=\frac{\tau s}{n+\alpha-p(n-2s)}+\frac{(s-1)}{n+\alpha-p(n-2s)}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}

which give

\displaystyle S_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu(p-1)}{2p}\mathcal{A}_{p}(u)=\frac{\tau(p-1)}{n+\alpha-p(n-2)}+\frac{\lambda(1-s)(p-1)[u]^{2}}{(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}

implying that

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\tau(np-n-\alpha-2)}{2(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}+\frac{\lambda(1-s)(p-1)[u]^{2}}{(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}

and

S_{\lambda}(u)=\frac{\tau s(p-1)}{n+\alpha-p(n-2s)}+\frac{(s-1)(p-1)}{n+\alpha-p(n-2s)}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}.

Now, let
w\in G
be arbitrary, this implies
S_{\lambda}(w)=l
and
w
is a solution of

\mathcal{L}(w)+w=\mu(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})|w|^{p-2}w\;\;\text{ in }\;\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Then by similar calculations, as done above, we get

S_{\lambda}(w)=\frac{s(p-1)\gamma}{n+\alpha-p(n-2s)}+\frac{(s-1)(p-1)}{n+\alpha-p(n-2s)}\left\|\nabla w\right\|_{2}^{2},

(3.39)

S_{\lambda}(w)=\frac{\gamma(p-1)}{n+\alpha-p(n-2)}+\frac{\lambda(p-1)(1-s)[w]^{2}}{(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}

and

I_{\lambda}(w)=\frac{\gamma(np-n-\alpha-2)}{2(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}+\frac{\lambda(1-s)(p-1)[w]^{2}}{(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}.

(3.40)

Now,
\gamma\geq\tau
. Indeed, if
\gamma<\tau
, then
\sigma=\tau/\gamma>1
. Defining
v
such that

w(x)=\sigma^{a}v(\sigma^{b}x)

where

a=\frac{2+\alpha}{2(n(p-1)-2-\alpha)}\text{ and }b=\frac{p-1}{n(p-1)-2-\alpha},

one has
H(v)=\tau
. Thus,
v\in\Gamma
. Since
u
minimizes
I_{\lambda}
on
\Gamma
, we have

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}(v)=\frac{\sigma^{nb-2a-2b}}{2}\left\|\nabla w\right\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\mu\sigma^{nb-2ap+b\alpha}}{2p}\mathcal{A}_{p}(w)+\frac{\sigma^{-2a-2bs+nb}}{2}\lambda[w]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sigma^{1-2b}I(w)=\sigma^{1-2b}\left(\frac{\gamma(np-n-\alpha-2)}{2(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}+\frac{\lambda(1-s)(p-1)[w]^{2}}{(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}\right).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\frac{(np-n-\alpha-2)\tau}{2(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}+\frac{\lambda(1-s)(p-1)[u]^{2}}{(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}

\displaystyle=I_{\lambda}(u)\leq I_{\lambda}(v)

\displaystyle\leq\sigma^{1-2b}\left(\frac{\gamma(np-n-\alpha-2)}{2(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}+\frac{\lambda(1-s)(p-1)[w]^{2}}{(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{(np-n-\alpha-2)\tau}{2(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}-\sigma^{1-2b}\left(\frac{\gamma(np-n-\alpha-2)}{2(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda\left(\frac{(1-s)(p-1)}{(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}\right)\left(\sigma^{1-2b}[w]^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-[u]^{2}\right)\;\;\forall\;\lambda>0.

As the left hand side of the above expression is independent of the parameter
\lambda
, we get

\frac{(np-n-\alpha-2)\tau}{2(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}\leq\sigma^{1-2b}\frac{\gamma(np-n-\alpha-2)}{2(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}=\sigma^{-2b}\frac{\tau(np-n-\alpha-2)}{2(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}.

Thus, for
\frac{n+\alpha}{n}<p<\frac{2s+n+\alpha}{n}
and
s\in(0,1)
, we get

\sigma^{-2b}\leq 1.

But since
\sigma>1
and
b<0
for
\frac{n+\alpha}{n}<p<\frac{2s+n+\alpha}{n}
, then
\sigma^{-2b}
must be greater than 1. Hence, by contradiction,
\sigma\leq 1
and consequently
\gamma\geq\tau.

Now, if
\gamma=\tau
, then
w\in\Gamma
and hence
S(w)\geq S(u)
. For
\gamma>\tau
we have

\displaystyle S_{\lambda}(w)-S_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma(p-1)}{n+\alpha-p(n-2)}+\frac{\lambda(p-1)(1-s)}{(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}[w]^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\tau(p-1)}{n+\alpha-p(n-2)}-\frac{\lambda(1-s)(p-1)}{(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}[u]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\gamma-\tau)(p-1)}{n+\alpha-p(n-2)}+\frac{\lambda(1-s)(p-1)}{(n+\alpha-p(n-2))}\left([w]^{2}\right.\left.-[u]^{2}\right),\;\;\;\forall\lambda>0.

As
(\gamma-\tau)>0
, no matter how small is
\lambda
, we obtain
S(w)-S(u)\geq 0
for
\frac{n+\alpha}{n}<p<\frac{2s+n+\alpha}{n}
. Therefore,
S_{\lambda}(w)\geq S_{\lambda}(u)
which gives

l\geq S_{\lambda}(u).

Also, since
u\in A
, we get

S_{\lambda}(u)\geq l.

Thus,
S_{\lambda}(w)=l=S_{\lambda}(u)
and hence
u\in G
. This implies that
G
is non empty.

Conversely, let
w\in G
, then by above proof
H(w)=\gamma\geq\tau
and
S_{\lambda}(w)=S_{\lambda}(u)
, where
u
is a solution of (
1.9
).
Then, as both
u
and
w
are in
A
, we get

\frac{\mu(p-1)}{2p}\mathcal{A}_{p}(u)=S_{\lambda}(u)=S_{\lambda}(w)=\frac{\mu(p-1)}{2p}\mathcal{A}_{p}(w).

We claim that
\gamma=\tau
.
Let if possible
\gamma>\tau
.
Now, since
S_{\lambda}(u)=S_{\lambda}(w)
, then

0>\frac{s(p-1)(\tau-\gamma)}{n+\alpha-p(n-2s)}=\frac{(s-1)(p-1)}{n+\alpha-p(n-2s)}\left(\left\|\nabla w\right\|_{2}^{2}-\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}\right).

Thus,
\left\|\nabla w\right\|_{2}^{2}>\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}.

For sufficiently small
\lambda>0
, we then get

\displaystyle S_{\lambda}(w)-S_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\|\nabla w\right\|_{2}^{2}-\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)+\lambda\left([w]^{2}\right.\left.-[u]^{2}\right)+\frac{(\gamma-\tau)}{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\|\nabla w\right\|_{2}^{2}dx-\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)>0.

Therefore,
S_{\lambda}(w)>S_{\lambda}(u)
which is a contradiction to the fact that
S_{\lambda}(u)=S_{\lambda}(w)
. Consequently,
\gamma=\tau
and hence
w\in\Gamma
. Thus,
w
is a solution of (
1.9
).
Therefore,
u\in G
if and only if

solve (
1.9
).
Now, if

then u solves (

) and hence by Lemma 3.3,

is a solution of (

). Conversely, if

solves (

), then

and it is a solution of

Now, if

.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
:

Let
u\in\Gamma
be a solution of (
1.1
), then

\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda[u]^{2}=\mu\mathcal{A}_{p}(u)

and

\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{n\tau}{2}+\frac{(n-2s)}{2}\lambda[u]^{2}=\left(\frac{n+\alpha}{2p}\right)\mu\mathcal{A}_{p}(u).

Equating above equations, we get

\displaystyle\left(\frac{n-2}{2}-\frac{n+\alpha}{2p}\right)\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left(n-2s-\frac{n+\alpha}{p}\right)[u]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{n}{2}+\frac{n+\alpha}{2p}\right)\tau

which implies

-\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}-s\lambda[u]^{2}=0\;\text{ for }p=\frac{n+\alpha}{n},\text{ that is }\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}=-s\frac{\lambda[u]^{2}}{2}<0.

Therefore, by contradiction, there does not exist any solution of (
1.1
) for
p=\frac{n+\alpha}{n}
.

Similarly, for
p=\frac{n+\alpha}{n-2}
, we get

-\tau=\left(\frac{n-2}{2}-\frac{n-2}{2}\right)\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\lambda[u]^{2}}{2}(n-2s-(n-2))=(1-s)[u]^{2}>0.

Thus, by contradiction, we conclude also that there is no solution of (
1.1
) for

.

\square

In this section, we are proving a Pohozaev identity corresponding to our problem type. For that, we follow the ideas in the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 2.5]
to handle the mixed operator case together with the proof in
[
32
, Proposition 3.1]
to deal with Choquard nonlinearities. Precisely, we state

Theorem A1
.

If
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a weak solution of (
1.1
), then it satisfies

\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left(\frac{n-2s}{2}\right)\lambda[u]^{2}+\frac{n}{2}\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2}=\mu\left(\frac{n+\alpha}{2p}\right)\mathcal{A}_{p}(u).

(4.1)

Proof.

From
1.2
, we have that
u\in W^{2,q}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
q\geq 2
. Therefore by Sobolev embeddings (see for instance
[
25
, Theorem 2.5.4]
), we get
u\in C^{1,\delta}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for all
0<\delta<1
. Following the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 2.5]
, since
(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{p})\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we get

\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow 0}L

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow 0}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(x\nabla u)(\nabla\psi_{R}\nabla u)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}(x\nabla\psi_{R})+\left(\frac{2-n}{2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}\psi_{R}\right.

(4.2)

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda C(n,s)}{2.2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}div(\Psi_{R}(x))}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{n+2s}{2}\right)\frac{\lambda C(n,s)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}(x-y)(\Psi_{R}(x)-\Psi_{R}(y))}{|x-y|^{n+2s+2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{n\lambda C(n,s)}{2.2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}\psi_{R}(x)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{\lambda C(n,s)}{2.2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}\nabla\psi_{R}(x)x}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2-n}{2}\right)\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left(\frac{2s-n}{2}\right)\lambda[u]^{2},

where
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is such that
0\leq\psi\leq 1
with
\psi=1
for
|x|\leq 1
and
\psi=0
for
|x|\geq 2
, for any
R>0
we further define
\psi_{R}(x):=\psi(Rx)
,
\Psi_{R}(x):=\psi(Rx)x
,
\phi(x):=\displaystyle\psi_{R}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}D_{i}u(x)\right)
with
D_{i}
, the difference quotient operator defined as follows:

D_{i}v(x)=\frac{v(x+he_{i})-v(x)}{h},\;\;e_{i}=(0,\cdots,1,\cdots,0)\text{ being the unit vector along }x_{i}\text{ coordinate, }

and

L=\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mu(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{p})|u|^{p-2}u-u)\phi\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mu(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{p})|u|^{p-2}u-u)\psi_{R}(x)(x.\nabla u),

by
[
3
, eq (3.21)]
. One can notice that in
[
3
]
, it is assumed that the solution is in
C^{0,\ell}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some
\ell>s
. However, the presence of the function with compact support,
\psi
, permits us to generalize everything for
u\in C^{0,\ell}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(and then for
u\in C^{1,\delta}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
) as well (see also arguments in
B_{R}(0)\times B_{R}(0)
with fixed suitable
R>0
after (3.3) page 2010 in
[
2
]
).
Further, by arguing as in
[
32
, Proposition 3.1]
,
we obtain

\lim_{R\rightarrow 0}L=-\mu\left(\frac{n+\alpha}{2p}\right)\mathcal{A}_{p}(u)+\frac{n}{2}\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2}.

(4.3)

Thus, we get (
4.1
) by combining (
4.2
) and (
4.3
).

No, classical gravity does not entangle quantized matter fields
Lajos Diósi

Abstract
In their recent work, Nature, 646 , 813 (2025), Aziz and Howl claim that classical (unquantized) gravity produces entanglement of quantized matter if matter is treated within quantum field theory which is, no doubt, our ultimate theory to use. However, an elementary quantum field re-calculation of the authors’ example shows that there is no entangling effect.



The recent work of Aziz and Howl
Aziz and Howl 2025
, received with appreciation
Weller-Davies 2025
,
claims that classical theories of gravity causes
exchange of virtual particles between separate parts of the second-quantized matter,
leading to entanglement between the parts. We show that their
perturbative calculation contradicts our exact non-perturbative results
that rule out the claimed entangling effect.

Ref.
Aziz and Howl 2025
considers the product (unentangled) initial
state of a boson field of mass
m>0
:

\displaystyle|\Psi\rangle=\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\Bigl(|N\rangle_{1L}|0\rangle_{1R}+|0\rangle_{1L}|N\rangle_{1R}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\otimes

\displaystyle\Bigl(|N\rangle_{2L}|0\rangle_{2R}+|0\rangle_{2L}|N\rangle_{2R}\Bigr).

(1)

The four N-boson states,
labelled by
\kappa i;\kappa\in\{L,R\},i\in\{1,2\}
, are four spatially separated Fock-states
over the four local vacuum states:

|N\rangle_{\kappa i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N!}}(\hat{a}_{\kappa i}^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle_{\kappa i}.

(2)

The four annihilation operators are of the standard forms:

\hat{a}_{\kappa i}=\int\bar{\phi}_{\kappa i}(\mathbf{x})\hat{a}(\mathbf{x})d^{3}\mathbf{x},

(3)

where
\phi_{\kappa i}(\mathbf{x})
are the four spatially separated
single-boson normalized wave functions. The local annihilation operators
\hat{a}(\mathbf{x})

are the inverse-Fourier transform of the plain wave annihilation operators
\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}
.
Assuming four non-overlapping non-relativistic wave functions and using linearised
semiclassical Einstein equations, ref.
Aziz and Howl 2025
claims that coupling to classical gravity turns
the product state (1) into an entangled state
|\Psi(t)\rangle
. The claim is based on 4
th
order perturbative
terms. This claim contradicts the consensus that semiclassical gravity cannot generate entanglement.

We are going to examine eq. (1). Uncorrelated (product) forms of distant local vacua or their excited Fock-states
are approximate forms in field theory, restricted to the local domains in question, and valid under suitable
conditions only. Fortunately, they have their exact, unconditionally valid forms.
The exact form of the product state
|N\rangle_{1L}|0\rangle_{1R}
, for instance,
is
N^{-1/2}(\hat{a}_{1L}^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
, where
|0\rangle
is the standard vacuum.
Accordingly, the exact form eq. (1) must be

\displaystyle|\Psi\rangle=\frac{1}{2N!}

\displaystyle\Bigl((\hat{a}_{1L}^{\dagger})^{N}+(\hat{a}_{1R}^{\dagger})^{N}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Bigl((\hat{a}_{2L}^{\dagger})^{N}+(\hat{a}_{2R}^{\dagger})^{N}\Bigr)|0\rangle.

(4)

Then the exact time-dependent solution of
|\Psi(t)
is the standard one:

\displaystyle|\Psi(t)\rangle=\frac{1}{2N!}

\displaystyle\Bigl((\hat{a}_{1L}^{\dagger}(t))^{N}+(\hat{a}_{1R}^{\dagger}(t))^{N}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Bigl((\hat{a}_{2L}^{\dagger}(t))^{N}+(\hat{a}_{2R}^{\dagger}(t))^{N}\Bigr),|0\rangle

(5)

where the time-dependent annihilation operators read:

\hat{a}_{\kappa i}(t)=\int\bar{\phi}_{\kappa i}(\mathbf{x},t)\hat{a}(\mathbf{x})d^{3}\mathbf{x}.

(6)

The time-dependent wave functions
\phi_{\kappa i}(\mathbf{x},t)
evolve with the covariant Klein–Gordon equation
in the classical spacetime determined by the semiclassical Einstein equation
Møller 1962
;
Rosenfeld 1963
.
(The initial condition
d\phi_{\kappa i}(\mathbf{x},0)/dt
is unique from the initial condition
\phi_{\kappa i}(\mathbf{x})
, not detailed here.)
If the initial wave functions are non-relativistic, like in ref.
Aziz and Howl 2025
, then the semiclassical Einstein equation
reduces to the Poisson equation for the Newton potential
\Phi
:

\displaystyle\Delta\Phi(\mathbf{x},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi Gm\langle\Psi(t)|\sum_{\kappa i}\hat{a}_{\kappa i}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\kappa i}|\Psi(t)\rangle

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi GNm\sum_{\kappa i}|\phi_{\kappa i}(\mathbf{x},t)|^{2}.

The Klein–Gordon equation reduces
to the non-linear Schrödinger–Newton equation
Diósi 1984
;
Penrose 1996
:

\frac{d\phi_{\kappa i}(\mathbf{x},t)}{dt}=\frac{i\hbar}{2m}\nabla^{2}\phi_{\kappa i}(\mathbf{x},t)-\frac{im}{\hbar}\Phi(\mathbf{x},t)\phi_{\kappa i}(\mathbf{x},t),

(8)

where
\Phi(\mathbf{x},t)
is the solution of the Poisson equation.

As long as the overlaps of the four wave functions
\phi_{\kappa i}(x,t)
in eq. (6) remain ignorable, the effective
factorized form of the exact solution (5), evolving from the initial state (1), is the following:

\displaystyle|\Psi(t)\rangle=\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\Bigl(|N,t\rangle_{1L}|0\rangle_{1R}+|0\rangle_{1L}|N,t\rangle_{1R}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\otimes

\displaystyle\Bigl(|N,t\rangle_{2L}|0\rangle_{2R}+|0\rangle_{2L}|N,t\rangle_{2R}\Bigr).

(9)

The time-dependent
N
-boson states are defined as follows:

|N,t\rangle_{\kappa i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N!}}(\hat{a}_{\kappa i}^{\dagger}(t))^{N}|0\rangle_{\kappa i}.

(10)

The initial state (1) remains a product (unentangled) state (9), contrary to the claim in ref.
Aziz and Howl 2025

but in accordance with what we know about semiclassical theory of gravity, where the classical gravity does not
entangle the quantized matter. Note in passing that the usual LOCC argument, e.g., in ref.
Weller-Davies 2025
,
does not prove the absence of gravity-mediated entanglement. Semiclassical gravity is not subject to LOCC.
The gravitational field (subject to CC) is not sourced by (realizable) LO but by the (calculated) expectation value of
the energy-momentum tensor.

Our derivation is much shorter, straightforward, and better to follow than that in ref.
Aziz and Howl 2025
.
Although it is not clear here and now why Aziz and Howl got a different result, one conjectures that their
lengthy derivation exceeded the limit of validity of the effective factorization (1) of the standard field state (4).

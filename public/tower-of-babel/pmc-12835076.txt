The effects of fractional derivative and gravity on wave propagation in micro-elongated thermo-elastic media under the refined dual-phase-lag model
Authors: Ahmed Ramady, Mohamed F. Ismail, Hamdy M. Ahmed, Soliman Alkhatib, M. Elsaid Ramadan, Eslam Nabil Shawki El-Ganzoury
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This paper examines the impact of gravity on wave propagation in a micro-elongated thermoelastic medium incorporating fractional conformable derivatives under the Dual-Phase-Lag (DPL) and the Refined Dual-Phase-Lag (RDPL) model. The governing equations of heat conduction, motion, and microelongation are formulated to account for finite thermal propagation speed, microelongated, and gravitational effects. By implementing a suitable nondimensionalization process and applying the Normal Mode Analysis method, the coupled system of partial differential equations is reduced to an semi-analytically solvable. Solutions for displacement, stress, temperature, and microelongation fields are obtained, enabling a detailed assessment of the dynamic response of the medium. Numerical simulations are performed to illustrate the role of fractional-order parameters and phase-lag effects in modifying wave characteristics. The results demonstrate that gravity has a significant impact on wave amplitude, velocity, and attenuation, while the fractional conformable derivative and RDPL model enhance control over thermal relaxation behavior. This study contributes to improved modeling of advanced thermoelastic materials in gravitational environments.

Introduction

Fractional thermoelasticity is a sophisticated enhancement of classical thermoelastic theories that integrates fractional-order derivatives into the governing equations of energy. In contrast to conventional models that presume an immediate or straightforward time-dependent evolution of heat flow response and mechanical strain, the fractional framework incorporates the heredity effects present in several genuine materials. The significance of these effects is magnified in contexts where thermal and mechanical responses deviate from the standard exponential relaxation model, such as in biological tissues, functionally graded materials, polymers, and micro- and nano-scale applications. In fractional thermoelasticity, the heat conduction law can be expressed utilizing fractional derivatives in time or space, allowing heat to propagate in a non-Fourier manner capturing phenomena such as finite thermal propagation speed or thermal wave dispersion. It is also possible to change the stress-strain relationship so that it represents viscoelastic-like recovery behaviors that vary with time. Fractional parameters give flexibility to suit experimental results more closely, giving improved modeling of thermal lag, damping properties, wave attenuation, and dynamic stability. Fractional thermoelastic theory provides a more realistic and comprehensive mathematical description for analyzing coupled thermo-mechanical responses in complex materials and advanced engineering systems, particularly when classical theories fail to predict observed physical behavior accurately for more details, see1–10.

The RDPL model is an advanced formulation of heat conduction that extends the classical DPL theory by introducing additional accuracy in describing the lagging behavior among heat flux and temperature gradients in materials. In the classical DPL model, two relaxation times are considered: one for the temperature gradient and another for the heat flux. However, the classical DPL model sometimes leads to mathematical complications or inconsistencies in capturing the real physical behavior of materials subjected to ultra-fast thermal processes, such as those encountered in micro-electronics cooling, laser irradiation, and thermoelastic wave propagation. The refined version enhances the governing heat conduction law by incorporating higher-order terms and more precise phase-lag parameters, which better describe the finite speed of heat propagation and the non-local, memory-dependent nature of thermal interactions. This refinement allows the model of RDPL to accurately represent situations where heat does not diffuse instantly, but instead propagates in a wave-like manner with measurable delay. It also resolves limitations such as non-physical instabilities and inaccurate prediction of temperature peaks that may arise in the classical formulations for more details, see11–19. Consequently, the RDPL model has gained importance in modeling thermoelastic problems, particularly in nano-scale and micro-scale systems, where the classical Fourier and conventional non-Fourier models fail to capture the experimentally observed thermal response.

Microelongated thermoelastic media are an essential type of generalized continuum that account for both classical material deformation and additional microelongation effects caused by the medium’s internal structure. Unlike standard thermoelastic models, which only consider macroscopic displacement and temperature variations, the micro-elongated theory includes an additional field variable that describes micro-scale elongation and deformation, such as fiber stretching, molecular chain extension, or structural reorientation within the material. This microelongated degree of freedom enables the model to more accurately represent complex materials like polymers, biological tissues, composites, porous solids, and engineered microelongated materials, in which internal fibers or particles stretch independently, influencing the overall response. When combined with thermal effects, micro-elongation interacts directly with the temperature field, resulting in new stress components and energy exchange mechanisms that are not present in classical thermoelasticity. As a result, wave propagation, stress distribution, and temperature evolution in micro-elongated media exhibit distinctive behavior, including dispersion, attenuation, and relaxation effects influenced by micro characteristics, see20–26. This theory is particularly useful when examining high-frequency wave motion, micro-scale thermo-mechanical processes, or materials subjected to rapid heating, because it captures the delayed and nonuniform internal responses that conventional thermoelastic models fail to describe.

This collection of papers27–37 highlights research across applied mechanics, materials, and monitoring techniques, with a strong focus on complex systems and performance optimization. The included works detail advancements in nonlinear dynamics for optimizing vibration control and energy harvesting, alongside studies exploring the microscopic irreversible deformation in fiber-reinforced composites under cyclic loading. The collection further incorporates methods for structural health monitoring, such as ultrasonic stress wave detection in power semiconductors and bolt axial stress measurement. Critical to thermoelasticity, one paper specifically analyzes thermoelastic damping and frequency shift in nano-scale piezoelectric fiber-reinforced composite beams under multi-phase thermal conditions, while another studies complex wave interactions in graded piezomagnetic materials influenced by gravity. Finally, the collection addresses high-accuracy flow measurement and electrical-thermal conduction in electrified railway systems

The influence of gravity on thermoelastic materials plays a significant role in altering the behavior of both thermal and mechanical fields, especially in large-scale structures or geological and astrophysical environments. When gravity is present, the body experiences a constant internal stress known as initial stress or pre-stress, which alters the propagation properties of elastic and thermal waves in the medium. The gravitational force adds a new term to the equations of motion, impacting displacement, stress distribution, and temperature fluctuation. As a result, the material may deform even in the absence of external loads, and temperature distribution may become non-uniform as density and internal pressure vary with depth. gravity in thermoelastic wave propagation can reduce or accelerate wave speeds depending on direction, and it can also amplify attenuation effects caused by the interaction of strain and temperature fields. The presence of gravity is particularly significant in high-temperature environments such as the Earth’s crust, planetary interiors, or space structures, where even small changes in stress can lead to noticeable thermoelastic responses for more details, see38–47. Therefore, incorporating gravity into thermoelastic models is essential for obtaining accurate predictions of stress, displacement, and thermal behavior in real-world applications involving large bodies or systems operating under strong gravitational effect.

Unlike previous RDPL fractional studies which investigate thermoelasticity or micro-elongated behavior separately, the present work is the first to formulate a unified thermoelastic model that simultaneously incorporates micro-elongation, gravity effects, and fractional RDPL heat conduction within a single coupled framework

This paper focuses on exploring how gravitational effects together with the fractional conformable derivative effect the behavior of a microelongated thermoelastic half-space, analyzed within both the DPL and the RDPL models. The study begins by formulating the fundamental field equations governing motion, heat conduction, and microelongation, in order to accurately represent the mutual coupling among mechanical and thermal responses in the presence of gravity. To simplify the mathematical structure and clearly emphasize the main physical parameters controlling the system’s response, these equations are converted into a dimensionless. For analytical convenience, the obtained system of partial differential equations is then systematically transformed into a set of ordinary differential equations using the Normal Mode Analysis, which enables a more tractable investigation of wave propagation properties and the dynamic characteristics of the medium. Conclusion

In this paper, the influence of gravity on wave propagation in micro-elongated thermoelastic media has been examined within the framework of the RDPL and DPL models incorporating the fractional conformable derivative. The governing field equations were formulated by considering both microelongation effects and the fractional order parameter. An analytical solution was obtained, and numerical simulations were performed to demenstrate the efffect of gravity, fractional order, and phase-lag parameters on the distributions of displacement, temperature, and stress components.

The results demonstrate several important and novel findings. First, the gravitational field plays a pronounced role in modifying the amplitude and decay characteristics of all physical fields. Gravity amplifies both displacement components near the surface and creates measurable changes in stress distribution, especially in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma _{xx}, \sigma _{zz},$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma _{xz}$$\end{document}. Second, the fractional conformable derivative introduces an additional degree of temporal flexibility, influencing thermal relaxation behavior and allowing for refined control of wave attenuation and localization. Third, the RDPL model predicts smoother, more realistic, and more stable thermal and mechanical responses when compared to the classical DPL formulation. It better captures the coupling between elastic, thermal, and micro-elongation fields, particularly under conditions of strong thermal gradients or significant gravity effects. Furthermore, the micro-elongation variable \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\psi$$\end{document} exhibits clear sensitivity to both fractional order and gravity, indicating that microelongated stretching mechanisms are strongly influenced by external body forces.

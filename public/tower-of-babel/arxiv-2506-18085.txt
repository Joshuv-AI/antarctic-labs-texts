Lemma 4.1

Proof :
We argue by showing the the maps

When adding a representation

0\longrightarrow H^{SG}_{*}(S^{0})\longrightarrow H^{SG}_{*}(S^{0})[W]\longrightarrow H^{SG}_{*}(S^{\delta-1})\longrightarrow 0

.
∎

W_{2i+1}

z^{2i},z^{2i-2},\ldots,z^{0},z^{-2},\ldots,z^{-2i}
with multiplicity
1 as a representation of
SU(2)
. Accordingly, if we write

\delta(i,s)=|\{j\in[1,i]\;|\;s|j\}|
, we have

d_{W_{2i+1}}(s)=1+2\delta(i,s),\mbox{ and }d^{\prime}_{W_{2i+1}}(t)=\delta(i,t).

Thus if
U=\sum_{i}a_{i}W_{2i+1}
we have

d_{U}(s)=\sum_{i}(1+2\delta(i,s))a_{i}\mbox{ and }d^{\prime}_{U}(t)=\sum_{i}\delta(i,t)a_{i}.

6.D.
Cyclic block

For the cyclic block, the forgetful map gives an isomorphism

[S^{0},S^{U}]^{SO(3)}_{\mathcal{C}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}[S^{0},S^{U}]^{O(2)}=([S^{0},S^{U}]^{SO(2)})^{W}.

By considering weights and the overall determinant we see that

\mathrm{res}^{SO(3)}_{O(2)}W_{2i+1}=\delta^{i}+\sigma_{1}+\cdots+\sigma_{i}.

6.E.
Dihedral block

For the dihedral block, the forgetful map gives an isomorphism

[S^{0},S^{U}]^{SO(3)}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}[S^{0},S^{U}]^{O(2)}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}.

The only thing to highlight is that the idempotent in
O(2)
is that
corresponding to
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\mathcal{D}\setminus\{D_{2},D_{4}\}
, so we have
removed the two summands corresponding to
D_{2}
and
D_{4}
.

6.F.
Isolated blocks

Here the model is elementary

\mathcal{A}(\Gamma|(H))=\mathbb{Q}W_{\Gamma}(H)\mbox{-mod}.

The 5 isolated blocks are

(SO(3),1),(A_{5},1),(\Sigma_{4},1),(A_{4},C_{2}),(D_{4},\Sigma_{3}).

The contributions are

[S^{0},S^{U}]^{\Gamma}_{(H,W_{\Gamma}(H)}=\mathrm{Hom}_{W_{\Gamma}(H)}(\mathbb{Q},H_{*}(S^{U^{H}}))=H_{*}(S^{U^{H}})^{W_{\Gamma}(H)}.

It remains only to determine the dimensions of the fixed point
representations
U^{H}
, and in the case that the Weyl group is
non-trivial, to ask how it acts. In effect this is the group
\Sigma_{4}/A_{4}
acting on

W_{2i+1}^{A_{4}}
, and
\Sigma_{3}/A_{3}
acting on
W_{2i+1}^{D_{4}}
. We
note that we have already recorded the restrictions of
representations to
O(2)
, and
D_{4}
is a subgroup.

7.
The group
Pin(2)

In this section the ambient group is
\widetilde{G}=Pin(2)
.
The algebraic model of
\widetilde{G}=Pin(2)
and of
G=O(2)
are essentially the same
in the sense that
Pin(2)
has two blocks essentially the same as that
for
O(2)
. However, some care is necessary.

All the calculations in this section are straightforward adaptions from
[
2
]
, since
the Adams spectral sequence collapses.

7.A.
Subgroups in blocks

First, we let

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}=\{C_{1},C_{2},\ldots\}
and
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{4},Q_{8},Q_{12},\ldots\}
. There is again a cyclic
block
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\#}
, and it is isomorphic to
\mathcal{C}^{\#}
, and the Weyl groups and
sheaf of rings are also the same. However the isomorphism
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}
takes each group to the group of the same order, and this is not
induced by the quotient map
p:Pin(2)\longrightarrow O(2)
. On the other hand the
quotient map does induce the isomorphism
\mathcal{Q}\longrightarrow\mathcal{D}
.

7.B.
Models

The standard model
\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{G})
is an abelian category which provides
an algebraic model for rational
\widetilde{G}
-spectra in the sense that there
is a Quillen equivalence
[
5
]
:

\mbox{$\widetilde{G}$-spectra}\simeq\mbox{DG-$\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{G})$}.

Since all objects are formal we’ll work with
\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{G})
, but use the notation familiar
from
\widetilde{G}
-spectra.

Since the block decomposition is as clopen sets, the algebraic model
of rational
Pin(2)
spectra splits in a corresponding fashion

\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{G})=\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{G}|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\#})\times\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{G}|\mathcal{Q}^{\#}).

The cyclic part has already been essentially dealt with in
Section
2
since

\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{G}|\mathcal{C}^{\#})=\mathcal{A}(S\widetilde{G})[W],

and the dihedral part is simply a category of equivariant sheaves

\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{G}|\mathcal{Q}^{\#})=\mathcal{W}-\mathrm{Shv}/\mathcal{Q}^{\#}.

7.C.
Representations

The other difference is in the representation theory. The simple
representations of
Pin(2)
include the inflations from
O(2)
, which
we continue to call
1,\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\delta
. These
have kernels
Pin(2),C_{2},C_{4},\ldots,Spin(2)
. However
there is also the representation
h_{1}
of
Pin(2)\subseteq SU(2)\cong Sp(1)
on the quaternions, which is of real dimension 4. Similarly, for
each cyclic group of odd order
m
we may pull back
h_{1}
along

Pin(2)\longrightarrow Pin(2)/C_{m}
to give a represention
h_{m}
. The
representations
h_{1},h_{3},h_{5},\ldots
have kernels
C_{1},C_{3},C_{5},\ldots
.

The action on
\sigma_{n}
is via the quotient, so that

(\sigma_{n})^{K}=\sigma_{n}^{pK}
. Similarly,
h_{m}^{K}=h_{1}^{p_{m}K}
, so that

d_{h_{m}}(s)=4
if
s|m
and is zero otherwise, and
d^{\prime}_{h_{m}}(t)=0
.

Thus if

U=a+b\delta+\sum_{n}c_{n}\sigma_{n}+\sum_{m}d_{m}h_{m}

we have dimension functions
d_{U}:\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}
and
d^{\prime}_{U}:\mathcal{Q}\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}

given by

d_{U}(s)=a+b+2\sum_{s\;odd,s|m}c_{n}+2\sum_{s\;even,s/2|m}c_{n}+4\sum_{s|m}d_{m},d^{\prime}_{U}(t)=a+\sum_{t|m}c_{n}.

8.

SU(2)

In this section the ambient group is
\widetilde{\Gamma}=SU(2)

All the calculations in this section are straightforward adaptions from
[
4

8.A.
Subgroups in blocks

The group
\Gamma

Again we have the cyclic block
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\#}

(\mathcal{Q}^{\prime})^{\#}

8.B.
Models

The standard model
\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{\Gamma})

\widetilde{\Gamma}
-spectra in the sense that there

\mbox{$\widetilde{\Gamma}$-spectra}\simeq\mbox{DG-$\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{\Gamma})$}.

\widetilde{\Gamma}

8.C.
Representations

The complex representations of
SU(2)
are the symmetric powers of the
natural representation
V_{1},V_{2},V_{3},\ldots

V_{i}

i

i-1

representation

4i

For the fixed point degrees, we note that for

W_{i}^{C_{s}}=W_{i}^{pC_{s}},W_{i}^{Q_{4t}}=W_{i}^{D_{2t}}

Similarly,

On well-posedness for non-autonomous parabolic Cauchy problems with rough initial data
Hedong Hou

Abstract
. We establish a complete picture for existence, uniqueness, and representation of weak solutions to non-autonomous parabolic Cauchy problems of divergence type. The coefficients are only assumed to be uniformly elliptic, bounded, measurable, and complex-valued, without any additional regularity or symmetry conditions. The initial data are tempered distributions taken in homogeneous Hardy–Sobolev spaces H ˙ s , p \dot{H}^{s,p} , and source terms belong to certain scales of weighted tent spaces. Weak solutions are constructed with their gradients in weighted tent spaces T s / 2 p T^{p}_{s/2} . Analogous results are also exhibited for initial data in homogeneous Besov spaces B ˙ p , p s \dot{B}^{s}_{p,p} .

1.
Introduction

The objective of this paper is to present a complete picture for existence, uniqueness, and representation of weak solutions to the non-autonomous parabolic Cauchy problem of divergence type

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Div_{x}(A(t,x)\nabla_{x}u)=f+\Div_{x}F,\quad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathds{R}}^{n}=:{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}\\
u(0)=u_{0}\end{cases}.

By the initial condition
u(0)=u_{0}
, we require
u(t)
tends to 0 as
t\to 0
in distributional sense,
i.e.
, in
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
.

Assume that the coefficient matrix
A\in L^{\infty}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+};\mat_{n}({\mathds{C}}))
is
uniformly elliptic
. Namely, there exist
\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1}>0
so that for a.e.
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathds{R}}^{n}
and any
\xi,\eta\in{\mathds{C}}^{n}
,

(1.2)

\Re(\langle A(t,x)\xi,\xi\rangle)\geq\Lambda_{0}|\xi|^{2},\quad|\langle A(t,x)\xi,\eta\rangle|\leq\Lambda_{1}|\xi||\eta|.

It is worth pointing out that we do
not
impose
any
assumptions on symmetry or regularity of the coefficients in either the time variable
t
or the space variable
x
.

This model serves as a simplified but representative example for parabolic systems. In fact, our results presented herein readily extend to parabolic systems where the ellipticity condition in (
1.2
) is replaced by a Gårding inequality on
{\mathds{R}}^{n}
uniformly with respect to
t
. That is, there exists
\Lambda_{0}>0
so that for a.e.
t>0
and any
\varphi\in{\dot{H}}^{1}({\mathds{R}}^{n};{\mathds{C}}^{m})
,

(1.3)

\Re\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}(A(t,x)\nabla\varphi(x))\cdot\overline{\nabla\varphi}(x)\geq\Lambda_{0}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})}^{2}.

Here,
A(t,x)=(A^{\alpha,\beta}_{i,j}(t,x))^{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n}_{1\leq i,j\leq m}
belongs to
L^{\infty}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+};\mat_{mn}({\mathds{C}}))
, and we use the notation

(A(t,x)\nabla\varphi(x))\cdot\overline{\nabla\varphi}(x):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j\leq m\\
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n\end{subarray}}A^{\alpha,\beta}_{i,j}(t,x)\partial_{\beta}\varphi^{j}(x)\overline{\partial_{\alpha}\varphi^{i}}(x).

The homogeneous Sobolev space
{\dot{H}}^{1}({\mathds{R}}^{n};{\mathds{C}}^{m})
is the closure of
C^{\infty}_{\mathsf{c}}({\mathds{R}}^{n};{\mathds{C}}^{m})
with respect to the semi-norm
\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n};{\mathds{C}}^{m})}
.

We show that for nearly optimal ranges of parameters
s
and
p
, given initial data
u_{0}
as a tempered distribution in the
homogeneous Hardy–Sobolev space

{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
and suitable source terms
f
and
F
, one can construct weak solutions
u
to (
1.1
). The solution class is characterized by the property that
\nabla u
belongs to the weighted tent space
T^{p}_{s/2}
. Uniqueness and representation of weak solutions in this solution class are also established. Precise definitions of theses function spaces are given in Section
2
. For context, the spaces
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
are defined via Littlewood–Paley decomposition, where
s
denotes regularity and
p
denotes integrability. When
s=0
,
{\dot{H}}^{0,p}
identifies with the Hardy space
H^{p}
if
0<p\leq 1
, the Lebesgue space
L^{p}
if
1<p<\infty
, and the John–Nirenberg bounded mean oscillation space
\bmo
if
p=\infty
.

A classical approach to this problem involves establishing
a priori
mixed
L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}
-estimates for weak solutions and their derivatives, particularly the highest order derivative
\nabla u
. However, to the best of our knowledge, obtaining such estimates for arbitrary coefficient matrices
A
remains an open problem. Partial results exist under additional regularity assumptions. For instance, when
x\mapsto A(t,x)
is uniformly continuous, the problem has been addressed by Ladyženskaja, Solonnikov and Ural’ceva
[
30
]
. Krylov
[
28
]
later relaxes this condition, requiring only that
x\mapsto A(t,x)
belongs to
\vmo
, see also the works of Dong and Kim on parabolic systems
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
.

The fundamental strategy underlying these results is to first derive the estimates for simplified cases, such as constant or space-independent coefficients, before extending them to more general ones via perturbation arguments, for which the regularity assumptions on coefficients seem to be unavoidable.

Instead, we consider an alternative framework by using
(weighted) tent spaces

T^{p}_{\beta}
, which leads to finer estimates for rough coefficients and rough initial data. Tent spaces were introduced by Coifman, Meyer and Stein
[
15
]
, and originated from the work of Fefferman and Stein
[
22
]
on real Hardy spaces. The key insight is that their norms first capture localized
L^{2}
integrability in the interior (in both time and space) and then incorporate an additional control (with weight in time) to access the limit at the boundary (materialized as
{\mathds{R}}^{n}
), especially where standard mixed
L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}
-spaces fall short.

In order to understand the role of tent spaces, we first consider the homogeneous Cauchy problem

(HC)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Div_{x}(A(t,x)\nabla_{x}u)=0,\quad 0<t<T,\penalty\ x\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}\\
u(0)=u_{0}\end{cases},

where the complex-valued coefficient matrix
A
is only assumed to be uniformly elliptic, bounded, and measurable. The fundamental work of Lions
[
29
]
first establishes well-posedness of weak solutions to (
HC
) with initial data
u_{0}\in L^{2}
in the energy space
L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{2})\cap L^{2}((0,T);W^{1,2})
for finite time
T>0
. In the special case of real coefficients, Aronson
[
11
]
constructs fundamental solutions
K(t,s;x,y)
with pointwise Gaussian decay and shows that the
propagators

(\Gamma_{A}(t,s))_{0\leq s<t<T}
defined on
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
by

(\Gamma_{A}(t,s)u_{0})(x):=\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}K(t,s;x,y)u_{0}(y)dy

represent the unique weak solution
u(t,x)
in the energy space to

(1.4)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Div_{x}(A(t,x)\nabla_{x}u)=0,\quad s<t<T,\penalty\ x\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}\\
u(s)=u_{0}\end{cases}.

A recent work of Auscher, Monniaux and Portal
[
7
]
imports the tent space theory to establish well-posedness of weak solutions to (
HC
) with initial data
u_{0}\in L^{p}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
for
2-\epsilon<p<\infty
, where
\epsilon>0
is a constant depending on the ellipticity of
A
. To this end, they first extend Lions’
L^{2}
-theory to
T=\infty
within the solution class
\nabla u\in L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
. Note that
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
identifies with the tent space
T^{2}_{0}
. By shifting the time, they define the propagators
(\Gamma_{A}(t,s))_{0\leq s\leq t<\infty}
as a family of contractions on
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
so that for any
s\geq 0
and
u_{0}\in L^{2}
,

(1.5)

u(t,x):=(\Gamma_{A}(t,s)u_{0})(x)

is the unique weak solution to (
1.4
) with
\nabla u\in L^{2}((s,\infty)\times{\mathds{R}}^{n})
. This definition agrees with Aronson’s definition by fundamental solutions for real coefficients but there might not be a representation with a kernel.

The weak solution
u
is constructed by extension of the
propagator solution map

{\mathcal{E}}_{A}
, initially defined from
L^{2}
to
L^{2}_{\loc}((0,\infty);W^{1,2}_{\loc})
by

(1.6)

{\mathcal{E}}_{A}(u_{0})(t,x):=(\Gamma_{A}(t,0)u_{0})(x),\quad t>0,\penalty\ x\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}.

The solution class is characterized by
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{0}
, with the equivalence

\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{A}(u_{0})\|_{T^{p}_{0}}\eqsim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p}}.

A later complement by Zatoń
[
36
]
extends it to
p=\infty
with

\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{A}(u_{0})\|_{T^{\infty}_{0}}\eqsim\|u_{0}\|_{\bmo}.

Zatoń also proves uniqueness and representation of weak solutions in this solution class and extends these results to parabolic systems.

Motivated by applications towards stochastic analysis, Portal and Veraar propose the following problem on well-posedness of the homogeneous Cauchy problem (
HC
) with initial data in
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
.

Problem 1.1

(
[
31
, p.583]
)
.

Let
A\in L^{\infty}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+};\mat_{n}({\mathds{C}}))
be uniformly elliptic. Does there exist a range of
s\geq 0
and
p\in(1,\infty)
so that the equivalence

(1.7)

\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{A}(u_{0})\|_{T^{p}_{s/2}}\eqsim\|u_{0}\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}

holds for any
u_{0}\in{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
?

The equivalence (
1.7
) clearly relates regularity of the initial data to the time weight in the tent space norm of the solution. In the special case
A={\mathds{I}}
(
i.e.
,
\Div(A\nabla)
agrees with the Laplacian
\Delta
), they intend to prove it for all
s\geq 0
and
p\in(1,\infty)
, but the proof has a gap. In fact, we have shown in
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
that for
s\geq 1
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the equivalence (
1.7
) holds if and only if
u_{0}
is constant, for which
\|u_{0}\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}=0
.

More generally, our previous work
[
3
]
addresses the problem for all time-independent coefficients
A=A(x)
. Indeed, the theory of maximal accretive operators yields that
-L:=\Div(A\nabla)
generates an analytic semigroup
(e^{-tL})_{t\geq 0}
on
L^{2}
, so the propagators become

\boxed{\Gamma_{A}(t,s)=e^{-(t-s)L},\quad\text{ if }A=A(x)}.

We show that for
-1<s<1
and
{\widetilde{p}}_{L}(s)<p\leq\infty
, the solution map
u_{0}\mapsto u(t,x)=(e^{-tL}u_{0})(x)
is an
isomorphism
from
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}+{\mathds{C}}
to the space of weak solutions to
\partial_{t}u-\Div(A(x)\nabla u)=0
with
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{s/2}
. In particular, this yields the equivalence (
1.7
). The lower bound
{\widetilde{p}}_{L}(s)
has an explicit expression depending on
s
and
L
. For the identity matrix
A={\mathds{I}}
(
L=-\Delta
), we have
{\widetilde{p}}_{-\Delta}(s)=\frac{n}{n+s+1}<1
.

In this paper, we address Problem
1.1
. We also consider source terms in tent spaces. To get
a priori
estimates for the tent space norms of
u
and
\nabla u
, we employ the theory of singular integral operators on tent spaces developed in
[
4
]
. A crucial component is the
L^{p}-L^{q}
off-diagonal decay of the propagators (see Definition
3.8
). To prove uniqueness, we use an internal representation of weak solutions introduced in
[
7
]
. Notably, for
s=0
, our approach not only recovers Zatoń’s uniqueness results via a conceptually simpler proof, but also relaxes the pointwise elliptic condition, see Remark
7.4
.

1.1.
Main results

For simplicity, we state our results for a single complex-valued equation and leave the detailed formulation for parabolic systems to the reader.

Let
A\in L^{\infty}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+};\mat_{n}({\mathds{C}}))
be uniformly elliptic (see (
1.2
)) and
(\Gamma_{A}(t,s))_{0\leq s\leq t<\infty}
be the propagators associated to
A
(see (
1.5
)). Let
p_{\pm}(A)
be two real numbers in
[1,\infty]
so that
(p_{-}(A),p_{+}(A))
is the largest open set of exponents
p
for which
(\Gamma_{A}(t,s))
extends to a uniformly bounded family on
L^{p}
. It is known that
p_{-}(A)<2
and
p_{+}(A)>2
, see Proposition
3.9
. For time-independent coefficients, they agree with the critical numbers
p_{\pm}(L)
introduced by
[
12
, §3.2]
. In particular, for the identity matrix
A={\mathds{I}}
, we have
p_{-}({\mathds{I}})=1
and
p_{+}({\mathds{I}})=\infty
.

To precise our results, we introduce several critical exponents. For convenience, we parametrize our results by
\beta\in{\mathds{R}}
, whose relation with the regularity index
s
is given by

\boxed{s=2\beta+1}.

Define the number

(1.8)

p_{A}(\beta):=\frac{np_{-}(A)}{n+(2\beta+1)p_{-}(A)}.

Let
\zeta<-1/2
be a fixed reference number. Define the critical exponents
p^{\pm}_{\zeta}(\beta)
by

(1.9)

p^{-}_{\zeta}(\beta):=\begin{cases}\frac{2(2\zeta+1)p_{-}(A)}{4(\zeta-\beta)+(2\beta+1)p_{-}(A)}&\text{ if }\zeta\leq\beta<-1/2\\
p_{A}(\beta)&\text{ if }\beta\geq-1/2\end{cases},

and

(1.10)

p^{+}_{\zeta}(\beta):=\begin{cases}\frac{2(2\zeta+1)}{2\beta+1}&\text{ if }\zeta\leq\beta<-1/2\\
+\infty&\text{ if }\beta\geq-1/2\end{cases}.

Note that
p^{-}_{\zeta}(\zeta)=p^{+}_{\zeta}(\zeta)=2
. To illustrate these exponents, we give graphic representations in Figure
1
. In this figure, we write
p
for
1/p
to ease the presentation. We use red dashed lines for graphs of
p^{\pm}_{\zeta}(\beta)
when
\beta<-1/2
, red normal line for that of
p^{+}_{\zeta}(\beta)
when
\beta\geq-1/2
, and blue dashed line for that of
p_{A}(\beta)
. Parallel lines to the blue dashed line are lines of embedding for homogeneous Hardy–Sobolev spaces and weighted tent spaces going downward.

We shall introduce a new parameter
\beta_{A}\in[-1,-1/2)
, which only depends on the ellipticity of
A
and the dimension
n
, as the lower bound of
\beta
. Taking
\zeta=\beta_{A}
, the orange shaded trapezoid becomes the region of well-posedness for initial data in
{\dot{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}
, while the blue shaded is for constant initial data. Note that the extreme point
(0,-1/2)
is always included.

Our first theorem is devoted to the homogeneous Cauchy problem (
f=0
,
F=0
).

Theorem 1.2

(Well-posedness of the homogeneous Cauchy problem)
.

There exists
\beta_{A}\in[-1,-1/2)
only depending on the ellipticity of
A
and the dimension
n
so that when
\beta_{A}<\beta<0
and

\begin{cases}p^{-}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)<p\leq p^{+}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)=\infty&\text{ if }\beta\geq-1/2\\
p^{-}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)<p<p^{+}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)&\text{ if }\beta_{A}<\beta<-1/2\end{cases},

for any
u_{0}\in{\dot{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}
, there is a unique global weak solution
u
to the
homogeneous Cauchy problem

(HC)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Div_{x}(A(t,x)\nabla_{x}u)=0,\quad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathds{R}}^{n}\\
u(0)=u_{0}\end{cases}

with
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}
. The equivalence holds that

\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}}\eqsim\|u_{0}\|_{{\dot{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}}.

Moreover,
u
belongs to
C([0,\infty);{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime})
.

Weak solutions are constructed by extension of the propagator solution map
{\mathcal{E}}_{A}
(see (
1.6
)) to
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
. See Theorem
6.1
for more properties.

The constraint
\beta<0
(
s<1
) is sharp. In fact, we establish a converse statement, which asserts that any global weak solution
u
to the equation
\partial_{t}u-\Div(A\nabla u)=0
with
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}
is uniquely determined by its distributional limit of
u(t)
as
t\to 0
, called
trace
. When
\beta\geq 0
(
s\geq 1
), the trace is constant, and so is the solution itself.

Theorem 1.3

(Representation)
.

Let
\beta>\beta_{A}
and

\begin{cases}p^{-}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)<p\leq p^{+}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)=\infty&\text{ if }\beta\geq-1/2\\
p^{-}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)<p<p^{+}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)&\text{ if }\beta_{A}<\beta<-1/2\end{cases}.

Let
u
be a global weak solution to
\partial_{t}u-\Div(A\nabla u)=0
with
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{\beta+{1}/{2}}
. Then
u
has a trace
u_{0}\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
, in the sense that
u(t)
converges to
u_{0}
in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
as
t\to 0
. Moreover,

(i)

If
\beta\geq 0
and
\frac{n}{n+2\beta}\leq p\leq\infty
, then
u
is a constant.

(ii)

If
\beta_{A}<\beta<0
, then there exist
g\in{\dot{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}
and
c\in{\mathds{C}}
so that
u_{0}=g+c
and
u={\mathcal{E}}_{A}(g)+c
, where
{\mathcal{E}}_{A}
is the extension of the propagator solution map defined by (
1.6
).

Combining Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
gives a complete answer to Problem
1.1
, even with extra ranges for
s<0
and
p\leq 1
. Moreover, it yields the following stronger result. For convenience, define
{\mathcal{E}}_{A}(c)=c
for any constant function
c
.

Corollary 1.4

(Isomorphism)
.

Let
\beta_{A}<\beta<0
and

\begin{cases}p^{-}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)<p\leq p^{+}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)=\infty&\text{ if }\beta\geq-1/2\\
p^{-}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)<p<p^{+}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)&\text{ if }\beta_{A}<\beta<-1/2\end{cases}.

The propagator solution map
u_{0}\mapsto u={\mathcal{E}}_{A}(u_{0})
is an isomorphism from
{\dot{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}+{\mathds{C}}
to the space of global weak solutions
u
to
\partial_{t}u-\Div(A\nabla u)=0
with
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{\beta+1/{2}}
, and

\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1/{2}}}\eqsim\|u_{0}\|_{{\dot{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}/{\mathds{C}}}.

Next, consider the inhomogeneous Cauchy problem (
u_{0}=0
,
F=0
).

Theorem 1.5

(Well-posedness of the inhomogeneous Cauchy problem)
.

Let
\beta>-1/2
and
p_{A}(\beta)<p\leq\infty
. Let
f\in T^{p}_{\beta}
. Then there exists a unique global weak solution
u
to the
inhomogeneous Cauchy problem

(IC)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Div_{x}(A(t,x)\nabla_{x}u)=f,\quad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathds{R}}^{n}\\
u(0)=0\end{cases}

with
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}
. Moreover,
u
belongs to
C([0,\infty);{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime})\cap T^{p}_{\beta+1}
with

\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}+\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}.

It is worth noting that our approach differs from the
L^{p}
-maximal regularity theory, which targets on finding
mild
solutions to (
IC
) in a Banach space
X
(
e.g.
,
X=L^{q}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
) with estimates of
\partial_{t}u
and
\Div(A\nabla u)
in
L^{p}((0,T);X)
. The primary reason for this distinction is that
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
(and even
L^{p}
) spaces are not trace spaces in the sense of real interpolation theory. Besides, the characterization of
L^{p}
-maximal regularity for
A
remains widely open. Existing affirmative results require additional Sobolev regularity assumptions, even in the simplest case
X=L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
and
p=2
, see
[
20
,
21
,
8
]
. The reader is also referred to
[
25
, §17.5]
for a more comprehensive survey.

At this stage, we do not have estimates for the tent space norms of
\partial_{t}u
and
\Div(A\nabla u)
. However, for time-independent coefficients, it has been shown in
[
4
]
that both
\partial_{t}u
and
\Div(A(x)\nabla u)
lie in
T^{p}_{\beta}
with

\|\partial_{t}u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}+\|\Div(A(x)\nabla u)\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}\lesssim\|f\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}.

See Remark
4.2
for detailed discussion.

The following theorem is concerned with the
Lions equation
(
u_{0}=0
,
f=0
).

Theorem 1.6

(Well-posedness of the Lions equation)
.

Let
\beta>\beta_{A}
and

\begin{cases}p^{-}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)<p\leq p^{+}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)=\infty&\text{ if }\beta\geq-1/2\\
p^{-}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)<p<p^{+}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)&\text{ if }\beta_{A}<\beta<-1/2\end{cases}.

Let
F\in T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}
. Then there exists a unique global weak solution
u
to the
Lions equation

(L)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Div_{x}(A(t,x)\nabla_{x}u)=\Div_{x}F,\quad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathds{R}}^{n}\\
u(0)=0\end{cases}

with
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}
. Moreover,
u
belongs to
C([0,\infty);{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime})\cap T^{p}_{\beta+1}
with

\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}+\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}}\lesssim\|F\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}}.

To the best of our knowledge, for the Lions equation, only the case
\beta=-1/2
has been addressed in a very recent work
[
9
]
, and only for
2\leq p\leq\infty
. The case
p<2
considered here appears to be new, since
[
9
]
only treats this case for
real
coefficients and their argument relies on this condition in a crucial way.

Let us now present well-posedness for parabolic Cauchy problems of type (
1.1
), as announced.

Theorem 1.7

(Well-posedness of non-autonomous parabolic Cauchy problems of divergence type)
.

Let
\beta>\beta_{A}
and

\begin{cases}p^{-}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)<p\leq p^{+}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)=\infty&\text{ if }\beta\geq-1/2\\
p^{-}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)<p<p^{+}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)&\text{ if }\beta_{A}<\beta<-1/2\end{cases}.

Let
\gamma>-1/2
and
p_{A}(\gamma)<q\leq\infty
. Suppose

(1.11)

\gamma\geq\beta,\quad 2\gamma-\frac{n}{q}=2\beta-\frac{n}{p}.

(i)

If
\beta<0
, then for any
u_{0}\in{\dot{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}
,
F\in T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}
, and
f\in T^{q}_{\gamma}
, there exists a unique global weak solution
u
to the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Div_{x}(A(t,x)\nabla_{x}u)=f+\Div_{x}F,\quad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathds{R}}^{n}\\
u(0)=u_{0}\end{cases}

so that
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}
. Moreover, the estimate holds that

\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{{\dot{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}}+\|F\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}}+\|f\|_{T^{q}_{\gamma}},

and
u
belongs to
C([0,\infty);{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime})
. If
u_{0}=0
, then
u
also belongs to
T^{p}_{\beta+1}
with

\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}\lesssim\|F\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}}+\|f\|_{T^{q}_{\gamma}}.

(ii)

If
\beta\geq 0
, then the above statements also hold when
u_{0}
is constant (for which
\|u_{0}\|_{{\dot{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}}=0
).

The proof is a straightforward combination of Theorems
1.2
,
1.5
, and
1.6
. We only need to mention that the condition (
1.11
) allows one to use Sobolev embedding of tent spaces (see Section
2.2
) to show that the weak solution
u_{1}
to the inhomogeneous Cauchy problem (
IC
) with
f\in T^{q}_{\gamma}
satisfies
\nabla u_{1}\in T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}
and

\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}}\lesssim\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{T^{q}_{\gamma+1/2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{T^{q}_{\gamma}}.

Notice that Theorem
1.3

(i)
implies for
\beta\geq 0
, the only initial data compatible with the solution class
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}
are constant.

To finish the introduction, let us mention an interesting corollary that we did not find in the literature. Note that for
p=2
, the tent space
T^{2}_{\beta}
identifies with the time-weighted
L^{2}
-space

T^{2}_{\beta}\simeq L^{2}\left((0,\infty),t^{-2\beta}dt;\penalty\ L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})\right)=:L^{2}_{\beta}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}),\quad\beta\in{\mathds{R}}.

In this special case, we have

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
\beta>-1/2
. For any
F\in L^{2}_{\beta+1/2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
and
f\in L^{2}_{\beta}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
, there exists a unique global weak solution
u
to the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Div_{x}(A(t,x)\nabla_{x}u)=f+\Div_{x}F,\quad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathds{R}}^{n}\\
u(0)=0\end{cases}

so that
\nabla u\in L^{2}_{\beta+1/2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
. Moreover,
u
lies in
L^{2}_{\beta+1}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
with

\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\beta+1}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{\beta+1/2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{2}_{\beta+1/2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\beta}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}.

The same results are also valid for
\beta_{A}<\beta\leq-1/2
and
f=0
.

2.1.
Homogeneous Hardy–Sobolev spaces

Readers can refer to
[
35
, §5]
for definitions and basic properties of Triebel–Lizorkin spaces and homogeneous Hardy–Sobolev spaces, as well as the proof of the following facts. Some needed refinements can be found in
[
32
, §2.4.3 & 3.3.3]
and the references therein, see also
[
3
, §2.1]
.

Denote by
{\mathscr{S}}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
the space of Schwartz functions and by
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
the space of tempered distributions. For any
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
, write
{\mathcal{F}}(f)
for the Fourier transform of
f
.

Denote by
C
the annulus
\{\xi\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}:2^{-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{2}\}
and by
2^{j}C
the annulus
\{\xi\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}:2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{j+2}\}
for any
j\in{\mathds{Z}}
. Pick
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{\mathsf{c}}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
with
\supp(\chi)\subset C
and
\sum_{j\in{\mathds{Z}}}\chi(2^{-j}\xi)=1
for any
\xi\neq 0
. Let
\Delta_{j}
be the
j
-th
Littlewood–Paley operator (associated with
\chi
)
given by

\Delta_{j}f:={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\chi(2^{-j}\cdot){\mathcal{F}}(f)),\quad f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}.

Let
{\mathscr{P}}
be the space of polynomials
{\mathds{C}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
,
{\mathscr{P}}_{0}:=\{0\}
, and
{\mathscr{P}}_{m}
be the subspace of
{\mathscr{P}}
consisting of polynomials of degree less than
m
for
m\geq 1
.

Let
s\in{\mathds{R}}
and
0<p<\infty
. The
Triebel–Lizorkin space

{\dot{F}}^{s}_{p,2}
consists of
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}/{\mathscr{P}}
for which

\|f\|_{{\dot{F}}^{s}_{p,2}}:=\left\|\left(\sum_{j}|2^{js}\Delta_{j}f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}}<\infty.

For
p=\infty
, it is defined similarly by approximation.

Define
\nu(s,p):=\max\{0,[s-\frac{n}{p}]+1\}
. For any
f\in{\dot{F}}^{s}_{p,2}
, the Littlewood–Paley series
\sum_{j}\Delta_{j}f
converges in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}/{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu(s,p)}
. It induces an isometric embedding
\iota:{\dot{F}}^{s}_{p,2}\to{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}/{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu(s,p)}
given by

\iota(f):=\sum_{j}\Delta_{j}f.

Definition 2.1

(Homogeneous Hardy–Sobolev spaces)
.

Let
s\in{\mathds{R}}
and
0<p\leq\infty
. The
homogeneous Hardy–Sobolev space

{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
consists of
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
whose class in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}/{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu(s,p)}
belongs to
\iota({\dot{F}}^{s}_{p,2})
. The (quasi-semi-) norm of
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
is given by

\|f\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}:=\|[f]\|_{{\dot{F}}^{s}_{p,2}},

where
[f]
denotes the class of
f
in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}/{\mathscr{P}}
.

For
s=0
,
{\dot{H}}^{0,p}
coincides with the Hardy space
H^{p}
if
p\leq 1
, the Lebesgue space
L^{p}
if
1<p<\infty
, and
\bmo
if
p=\infty
, up to equivalent (quasi-/semi-)norms. Moreover, for any
s\in{\mathds{R}}
,
{\dot{H}}^{s,\infty}/{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu(s,\infty)}
is isomorphic to
\bmo^{s}
introduced by Strichartz
[
34
]
.

Our homogeneous Hardy–Sobolev spaces
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
can be regarded as “realizations” of Triebel–Lizorkin spaces
{\dot{F}}^{s}_{p,2}
in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
, and hence inherit many fundamental properties, such as duality, complex interpolation, and Sobolev embedding. In particular, let us mention that for any
s\in{\mathds{R}}
,
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}\cap L^{2}
is dense in
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
if
p<\infty
, or weak*-dense if
p=\infty
.

Let
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}+{\mathds{C}}
be the collection of
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
of the form
f=g+c
for some
g\in{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
and
c\in{\mathds{C}}
, endowed with the (quasi-semi-)norm

\|f\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}/{\mathds{C}}}:=\inf_{g}\|g\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}},

where the infimum is taken among all
g\in{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
for which
f=g+c
for some
c\in{\mathds{C}}
. Note that when
\nu(s,p)\geq 0
,
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
contains constants and
\|f+c\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}=\|f\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}
for any
c\in{\mathds{C}}
, so
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}+{\mathds{C}}={\dot{H}}^{s,p}
.

2.2.
Tent spaces

We adapt the original definitions in
[
15
]
to the parabolic settings. Let
\beta\in{\mathds{R}}
and
0<p<\infty
. The
(parabolic) tent space

T^{p}_{\beta}
consists of measurable functions
f
on
{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
for which

\|f\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}:=\left(\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\fint_{B(x,t^{1/2})}|t^{-\beta}f(t,y)|^{2}\,dtdy\right)^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

For
p=\infty
, the space
T^{\infty}_{\beta}
consists of measurable functions
f
on
{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
for which

\|f\|_{T^{\infty}_{\beta}}:=\sup_{B}\left(\int_{0}^{r(B)^{2}}\fint_{B}|t^{-\beta}f(t,y)|^{2}\,dtdy\right)^{1/2}<\infty,

where
B
describes balls in
{\mathds{R}}^{n}
. We list below some properties to be used. The reader can refer to
[
15
,
23
,
1
,
6
,
4
]
for the proofs.

(i)

Let
\beta\in{\mathds{R}}
and
0<p\leq\infty
. Then
T^{p}_{\beta}
embeds into
L^{2}_{\loc}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
, and
L^{2}_{\mathsf{c}}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
is dense (resp. weak*-dense) in
T^{p}_{\beta}
if
0<p<\infty
(resp.
p=\infty
).

(ii)

(Duality) Let
\beta\in{\mathds{R}}
and
1\leq p<\infty
. The dual of
T^{p}_{\beta}
identifies with
T^{p^{\prime}}_{-\beta}
via
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
-duality.

(iii)

(Interpolation) Let
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in{\mathds{R}}
,
0<p_{0}<p_{1}\leq\infty
. Suppose
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
and
\beta=(1-\theta)\beta_{0}+\theta\beta_{1}
for some
\theta\in(0,1)
. Then the complex interpolation space
[T^{p_{0}}_{\beta_{0}},T^{p_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}]_{\theta}
identifies with
T^{p}_{\beta}
.

(iv)

(Embedding) Let
\beta_{0}>\beta_{1}
and
0<p_{0}<p_{1}\leq\infty
. Suppose

2\beta_{0}-\frac{n}{p_{0}}=2\beta_{1}-\frac{n}{p_{1}}.

Then
T^{p_{0}}_{\beta_{0}}
embeds into
T^{p_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}
.

Let us also introduce two scales of retractions of tent spaces.

2.2.1.
Slice spaces

Slice spaces are introduced by
[
5
, §3]
. Let
0<p\leq\infty
and
\delta>0
. The
(parabolic) slice space

E^{p}_{\delta}
consists of measurable functions
f
on
{\mathds{R}}^{n}
for which

\|f\|_{E^{p}_{\delta}}:=\left\|\left(\fint_{B(\cdot,\delta^{1/2})}|f(y)|^{2}dy\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}}<\infty.

For any
\delta>0
,
E^{p}_{\delta}
is a retraction of
T^{p}_{0}
. We mention here some other fundamental properties.

(i)

(Duality) For
1\leq p<\infty
, the dual of
E^{p}_{\delta}
identifies with
E^{p^{\prime}}_{\delta}
via
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
-duality.

(ii)

(Interpolation) Let
0<p_{0}<p_{1}\leq\infty
. Suppose
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
for some
\theta\in(0,1)
. Then the complex interpolation space
[E^{p_{0}}_{\delta},E^{p_{1}}_{\delta}]_{\theta}
identifies with
E^{p}_{\delta}
.

(iii)

(Change of aperture) Let
\delta^{\prime}>0
. Then
E^{p}_{\delta}
agrees with
E^{p}_{\delta^{\prime}}
as sets, with equivalents norms. More precisely,

(2.1)

\min\left\{1,\left(\frac{\delta}{\delta^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\right\}\|f\|_{E^{p}_{\delta}}\lesssim_{n,p}\|f\|_{E^{p}_{\delta^{\prime}}}\lesssim_{n,p}\max\left\{1,\left(\frac{\delta}{\delta^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\right\}\|f\|_{E^{p}_{\delta}}.

2.2.2.
Divergence of tent spaces

Let
\beta\in{\mathds{R}}
and
1<p<\infty
. Let
\Div T^{p}_{\beta}
be the collection of distributions
f\in{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
of the form
f=\Div F
for some
F\in T^{p}_{\beta}
, endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{\Div T^{p}_{\beta}}:=\inf\left\{\|F\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}:\Div F=f,\penalty\ F\in T^{p}_{\beta}\right\}.

Denote by
R
the Riesz transform on
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
given by

R(G)(t)=\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1/2}G(t),\quad t>0.

We infer from
[
10
, Theorem 2.4]
that
R
is bounded on
T^{p}_{\beta}
. By duality, its dual operator
R^{*}
, given by

R^{*}(G)(t):=-(-\Delta)^{-1/2}\Div G(t),\quad t>0,

is also bounded on
T^{p}_{\beta}
. Let
f\in\Div T^{p}_{\beta}
. Pick
F\in T^{p}_{\beta}
with
\Div F=f
. Define the operator
S:\Div T^{p}_{\beta}\to T^{p}_{\beta}
by

S(f):=RR^{*}(F)=-R(-\Delta)^{-1/2}\Div(F).

The definition is independent of the choice of
F
. Using boundedness of
R
and
R^{\ast}
, we get
\|S(f)\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}\lesssim\|F\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}
. Taking infimum among all such
F
yields
\|S(f)\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\Div T^{p}_{\beta}}
, so
S
is bounded.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\beta\in{\mathds{R}}
and
1<p<\infty
. Then
\Div S=\id_{\Div T^{p}_{\beta}}
. Consequently,
\Div T^{p}_{\beta}
is a Banach space, and
\Div:T^{p}_{\beta}\to\Div T^{p}_{\beta}
is a retraction with
S:\Div T^{p}_{\beta}\to T^{p}_{\beta}
as the coretraction.

Proof.

We only need to prove the identity. Let
f\in\Div T^{p}_{\beta}
and
F\in T^{p}_{\beta}
with
\Div F=f
. Pick
F_{n}\in L^{2}_{\mathsf{c}}((0,\infty);W^{1,2}_{\mathsf{c}}({\mathds{R}}^{n}))
as a sequence approximating
F
in
T^{p}_{\beta}
. For any
t>0
, we have
\Div S(\Div F_{n})(t)=-\Div\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1/2}(-\Delta)^{-1/2}\Div F_{n}(t)=\Div F_{n}(t)
in
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
, so

\Div S(\Div F_{n})=\Div F_{n}\quad\text{ in }L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}).

Ensured by boundedness of
\Div
and
S
, we take limits on both sides to get
\Div S(f)=\Div S(\Div F)=\Div F=f
in
\Div T^{p}_{\beta}
.
∎

3.
Basic facts of weak solutions

We first recall the definition of weak solutions. Denote by
W^{1,2}
the
(inhomogeneous) Sobolev space
endowed with the norm
\|f\|_{W^{1,2}}:=\|f\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}}
.

Definition 3.1

(Weak solutions)
.

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
{\mathds{R}}^{n}
and
0\leq a<b\leq\infty
. Let
f
and
F
be in
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}((a,b)\times\Omega)
. A function
u\in L^{2}_{\loc}((a,b);W^{1,2}_{\loc}(\Omega))
is called a
weak solution
to the equation

\partial_{t}u-\Div(A\nabla u)=f+\Div F

with
source term

f+\Div F
, if for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{\mathsf{c}}((a,b)\times\Omega)
,

(3.1)

-\int_{(a,b)\times\Omega}u\penalty\ \partial_{t}\phi+\int_{(a,b)\times\Omega}(A\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\phi=(f,\phi)-(F,\nabla\phi).

The pairs on the right-hand side stand for pairing of distributions and test functions on
(a,b)\times\Omega
. We say
u
is a
global
weak solution if (
3.1
) holds for
(a,b)\times\Omega=(0,\infty)\times{\mathds{R}}^{n}
.

Let
u_{0}\in{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\Omega)
. We say
u
satisfies the
initial condition

u(a)=u_{0}
if
u(t)
converges to
u_{0}
in
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\Omega)
as
t\to a+
.

There is a corresponding definition of (global) weak solutions to the backward equation
-\partial_{s}u-\Div(A(s)^{\ast}\nabla u)=f+\Div F
. We leave the precise formulation to the reader.

3.1.
Energy inequality

We recall without proof a form of Caccioppoli’s inequality.

Lemma 3.2

(Caccioppoli’s inequality)
.

Let
0<a<b<\infty
and
B\subset{\mathds{R}}^{n}
be a ball. Let
f
and
F
be in
L^{2}((a,b)\times 2B)
. Let
u\in L^{2}((a,b);W^{1,2}(2B))
be a weak solution to
\partial_{t}u-\Div(A\nabla u)=f+\Div F
in
(a,b)\times 2B
. Then
u
belongs to
C([a,b];L^{2}(B))
with

\displaystyle\|u(b)\|_{L^{2}(B)}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{r(B)^{2}}+\frac{1}{b-a}\right)\int_{a}^{b}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}(2B)}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle+r(B)^{2}\int_{a}^{b}\|f(s)\|_{L^{2}(2B)}^{2}\,ds+\int_{a}^{b}\|F(s)\|_{L^{2}(2B)}^{2}\,ds.

Moreover, for any
c\in(a,b)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{c}^{b}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2}(B)}^{2}\,ds\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c-a}\left(1+\frac{b-a}{r(B)^{2}}\right)\int_{a}^{b}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}(2B)}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle+\frac{r(B)^{2}(b-a)}{c-a}\int_{a}^{b}\|f(s)\|_{L^{2}(2B)}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle+\frac{b-a}{c-a}\int_{a}^{b}\|F(s)\|_{L^{2}(2B)}^{2}\,ds.

There is a corresponding version for weak solutions to the backward equation
-\partial_{s}u-\Div(A(s)^{\ast}\nabla u)=f+\Div F
. We refer to “Caccioppoli’s inequality” in both cases in the sequel.

Corollary 3.3

(
A priori
tent space estimates)
.

Let
\beta\in{\mathds{R}}
and
0<p\leq\infty
. Let
u
be a global weak solution to
\partial_{t}u-\Div(A\nabla u)=f+\Div F
. Then the a priori estimate holds that

\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}}\lesssim\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}+\|F\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}}+\|f\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}.

This inequality also holds for weak solutions to the backward equation.

Proof.

The proof follows from the same arguments for time-independent coefficients, by applying Caccioppoli’s inequality to averages on local Whitney cubes
(t,2t)\times B(x,t^{1/2})
, see
[
3
, Corollary 4.3]
.
∎

3.2.
Propagators

We first recall the
L^{2}
-theory.

Proposition 3.4

(
L^{2}
-theory)
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{2}
and
F\in L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})\simeq T^{2}_{0}
. Then there exists a unique global weak solution
u
to the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Div(A\nabla u)=\Div F\\
u(0)=u_{0}\end{cases}

so that
\nabla u\in L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
. Moreover,
u
belongs to
C([0,\infty);L^{2})
with

\sup_{t\geq 0}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+(2\Lambda_{0})^{-1/2}\|F\|_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})},

and

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}\leq(2\Lambda_{0})^{-1/2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\Lambda_{0}^{-1}\|F\|_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}.

The existence is due to
[
29
]
, and the uniqueness in the class
\nabla u\in L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
is due to
[
7
]
, see
[
9

for a survey. Moreover,
[

]
further exploits the

Corollary 3.5

(Propagators)
.

There exists a family of contractions on

(\Gamma_{A}(t,s))_{0\leq s\leq t<\infty}
, called
(forward) propagators associated to

so that for any

and

to the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Div(A\nabla u)=0,\quad t>s\\
u(s)=h\end{cases}

with
\nabla u\in L^{2}((s,\infty)\times{\mathds{R}}^{n})
. Moreover,
u
belongs to
C_{0}([s,\infty);L^{2})
.

1
1

1

Here,
C_{0}([s,\infty);E)
is the space of continuous functions with limit 0 as
t\to\infty
in the prescribed topology.

By reversing time, we also obtain the notion of propagators for the backward equation, called backward propagators.

Corollary 3.6

(Backward propagators)
.

There exists a family of contractions on
L^{2}
,
(\Gamma_{A^{\ast}}^{-}(s,t))_{0\leq s\leq t<\infty}
, called
backward propagators associated to
A^{\ast}
, so that for any

and
t>0
,
{\widetilde{u}}(s):=\Gamma_{A^{\ast}}^{-}(s,t)h
is the unique weak solution on
(0,t)\times{\mathds{R}}^{n}
to the backward Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}-\partial_{s}{\widetilde{u}}-\Div(A(s)^{\ast}\nabla{\widetilde{u}})=0,\quad 0<s<t\\
u(t)=h\end{cases}

so that
\nabla{\widetilde{u}}\in L^{2}((0,t)\times{\mathds{R}}^{n})
. Moreover, for fixed
t>0

Let us formulate the

-theory of the propagators by using the notion of off-diagonal decay introduced by
[
4
]
. Denote by

and write

Definition 3.8

(Off-diagonal decay)
.

Let
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
. Let
\{K(t,s)\}_{(t,s)\in\Delta^{c}}
be a family of bounded operators on
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
. We say
\{K(t,s)\}
satisfies the
(exponential)
L^{p}-L^{q}
off-diagonal decay
if there are constants

so that for any

,

as Borel sets, and

,

\|\mathds{1}_{E}K(t,s)\mathds{1}_{F}f\|_{L^{q}}\leq C(t-s)^{-\frac{n}{2}[p,q]}\exp\left(-c\frac{\dist(E,F)^{2}}{t-s}\right)\|\mathds{1}_{F}f\|_{L^{p}}.

Proposition 3.9

(
L^{p}
-theory of propagators)
.

Let
A\in L^{\infty}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+};\mat_{n}({\mathds{C}}))
be uniformly elliptic. Let
(\Gamma_{A}(t,s))
be the propagators associated to

. Then the following properties hold.

(i)

There exist
p_{-}(A)\in[1,2)
and
p_{+}(A)\in(2,\infty]
so that
(p_{-}(A),p_{+}(A))
is the maximal open set of exponents
p\in[1,\infty]
for which
(\Gamma_{A}(t,s))
is uniformly bounded on
L^{p}
.

(ii)

For
p_{-}(A)<p\leq 2
,
(\Gamma_{A}(t,s))
has
L^{p}-L^{2}
off-diagonal decay. For
2\leq q<p_{+}(A)
,
(\Gamma_{A}(t,s))
has
L^{2}-L^{q}
off-diagonal decay.

Proof.

First consider
(i)
. Since
(\Gamma_{A}(t,s))
is a family of contractions on

, particularly, it is uniformly bounded on
L^{2}
. By interpolation, it is clear that all the

for which
(\Gamma_{A}(t,s))
is uniformly bounded on

form an interval containing 2. Let
p_{-}(A)
and
p_{+}(A)
be the left and right extremes of this interval. It has been shown in
[

, Theorem 1.6]
that there exists

only depending on

and the ellipticity of

so that
(\Gamma_{A}(t,s))
is uniformly bounded on

for

. We thus infer that

and

. This proves
(i)
.

The second statement
(ii)
combines
[
7
, Lemmas 4.9 and 4.11]
. This completes the proof.
∎

For time-independent coefficients, the critical numbers

(iii)

The function

(iv)

The function

Proof.

By duality, it suffices to prove

by shifting the time. We present two approaches to prove

The first way is to adapt the proof of

The second proof is based on the observation that

Indeed, since

Theorem

({\dot{H}}^{s_{0},p_{0}},{\dot{H}}^{s_{1},p_{1}})_{\theta,p}={\dot{B}}^{s}_{p,p},\quad(T^{p_{0}}_{\beta_{0}},T^{p_{1}}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,p}=Z^{p}_{\beta}.

is an isomorphism from
{\dot{B}}^{s}_{p,p}+{\mathds{C}}
to the space of distributional solutions
u
to the heat equation (with null source terms) with
\nabla u\in Z^{p}_{s/2}
. Particularly, the equivalence holds that

\|\nabla e^{t\Delta}u_{0}\|_{Z^{p}_{s/2}}\eqsim\|u_{0}\|_{{\dot{B}}^{s}_{p,p}}.

Moreover, at the extreme point
s=1
and
p=\infty
,
[
3
, Proposition 9.1]
shows that the solution map is an isomorphism from the
homogeneous Sobolev space

{\dot{W}}^{1,\infty}
(rather than
{\dot{B}}^{1}_{\infty,\infty}
) to the space of distributional solutions
u
to the heat equation with
\nabla u\in Z^{\infty}_{1/2}
. The equivalence also holds that

(8.3)

\|\nabla e^{t\Delta}u_{0}\|_{Z^{\infty}_{1/2}}\eqsim\|u_{0}\|_{{\dot{W}}^{1,\infty}}.

We call this change as the
canonical modification
for
s=1
(
\beta=0
) and
p=\infty
. Recall that
{\dot{W}}^{1,\infty}
consists of distributions
\varphi
for which
\nabla\varphi
is bounded, endowed with the semi-norm
\|\varphi\|_{{\dot{W}}^{1,\infty}}:=\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}
. Thanks to Rademacher’s theorem, we know that
{\dot{W}}^{1,\infty}
agrees with the set of Lipschitz continuous functions, up to almost everywhere equality.

Analogous results are also established there for parabolic Cauchy problems of type (
1.1
) with
time-independent
coefficients.

The following theorem extends the above results to the non-autonomous case.

Theorem 8.1

(Well-posedness of Cauchy problems of type (
1.1
) for homogeneous Besov spaces)
.

Let
\beta_{A}\in[-1,-1/2)
be the constant given by Theorem
5.1
. Let
\beta>\beta_{A}
and

\begin{cases}p^{-}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)<p\leq p^{+}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)=\infty&\text{ if }\beta>-1/2\\
p^{-}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)<p<p^{+}_{\beta_{A}}(\beta)&\text{ if }\beta_{A}<\beta\leq-1/2\end{cases}.

With the canonical modification for
\beta=0
and
p=\infty
(i.e., the initial data
u_{0}
is taken in
{\dot{W}}^{1,\infty}
), Theorems
4.1
,
5.1
,
6.1
(except
(d)
),
7.1
,
7.2
, and
1.3
are all valid when replacing homogeneous Hardy–Sobolev spaces (resp. tent spaces) by homogeneous Besov spaces (resp.
Z
-spaces) with the same exponents.

We exclude here the extreme point
\beta=-1/2
and
p=\infty
, since we do not know whether the Lions operator
{\mathcal{R}}^{A}_{1/2}
is bounded from
Z^{\infty}_{0}
to
Z^{\infty}_{1/2}
. Moreover, it is not clear whether weak solutions are continuous in
{\dot{B}}^{0}_{p,p}
(except in the case
p=2
as
{\dot{B}}^{0}_{2,2}
identifies with
L^{2}
), which would be the analog of Theorem
6.1

(d)
.

Proof.

We just show some main ingredients of the proof. For convenience, we still use the same label of the theorems for their variants in Besov spaces.

First consider Theorem
5.1
. It follows from real interpolation (
8.1
) and properties of the extension of
{\mathcal{R}}^{{\mathds{I}}}_{1/2}
on
Z
-spaces, see
[
3
, Theorem 9.2]
. We only need to prove the trace space
E^{q}_{\delta}
in
(d)
for
\beta=-1/2
and
2<p<\infty
. Let
F\in Z^{p}_{0}
and
u:={\mathcal{R}}^{A}_{1/2}(F)
. Using interpolation of slice spaces, it suffices to show
u(t)
tends to 0 as
t\to 0
in
E^{p}_{\delta}
and
E^{\infty}_{\delta}
for any
\delta>0
. Let
0<t<\delta
. To prove the trace in
E^{p}_{\delta}
, we use change of aperture of slice spaces (cf. (
2.1
)) and Caccioppoli’s inequality (cf. Lemma
3.2
) to get

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{E^{p}_{\delta}}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{E^{p}_{t/16}}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{t/2}^{t}\fint_{B(x,\sqrt{t}/2)}|s^{-1/2}u(s,y)|^{2}dsdy\right)^{p/2}dx=:\Phi(t)

Notice that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(t)\frac{dt}{t}\lesssim\|u\|_{Z^{p}_{1/2}}^{p}<\infty.

The fact that the integral converges forces that
\Phi(t)
tends to 0 as
t\to 0
. This proves the trace in
E^{p}_{\delta}
.

To prove the trace in
E^{\infty}_{\delta}
, we use Lions’ embedding theorem. Let
B\subset{\mathds{R}}^{n}
be a ball with radius
\delta^{1/2}
. Let
0<t<\delta/2
. By computation, one gets that for any
\beta\in{\mathds{R}}
,
2\leq p\leq\infty
, and
t>0
,

\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}((t/2,t)\times B)}\lesssim_{p,\delta}t^{\beta}\|\mathds{1}_{(t/2,t)}\varphi\|_{Z^{p}_{\beta}}.

Here,
\mathds{1}_{(t/2,t)}\varphi
denotes the function
(s,y)\mapsto\mathds{1}_{(t/2,t)}(s)\varphi(s,y)
. Applying this inequality to
u
and
\nabla u
yields that

\|u\|_{L^{2}((t/2,t);W^{1,2}(B))}\lesssim_{p,\delta}t^{1/2}\|\mathds{1}_{(t/2,t)}u\|_{Z^{p}_{1/2}}+\|\mathds{1}_{(t/2,t)}\nabla u\|_{Z^{p}_{0}}.

Since
\partial_{t}u=\Div(A\nabla u+F)
, we get

\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}((t/2,t);W^{-1,2}(B))}\lesssim_{p,\delta}\|\mathds{1}_{(t/2,t)}\nabla u\|_{Z^{p}_{0}}+\|\mathds{1}_{(t/2,t)}F\|_{Z^{p}_{0}}.

Hence, Lions’ embedding theorem asserts
u\in C([t/2,t];L^{2}(B))
with

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(B)}\lesssim_{p,\delta}t^{1/2}\|\mathds{1}_{(t/2,t)}u\|_{Z^{p}_{1/2}}+\|\mathds{1}_{(t/2,t)}\nabla u\|_{Z^{p}_{0}}+\|\mathds{1}_{(t/2,t)}F\|_{Z^{p}_{0}}.

Since the constant is independent of the center of
B
, we take supremum over all such balls
B
to obtain

\|u(t)\|_{E^{\infty}_{\delta}}\lesssim_{p,\delta}t^{1/2}\|\mathds{1}_{(t/2,t)}u\|_{Z^{p}_{1/2}}+\|\mathds{1}_{(t/2,t)}\nabla u\|_{Z^{p}_{0}}+\|\mathds{1}_{(t/2,t)}F\|_{Z^{p}_{0}},

which tends to 0 by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem. This proves the trace in
E^{\infty}_{\delta}
and hence concludes Theorem
5.1
.

Theorems
4.1
and
6.1
can also be established analogously. In Theorem
6.1
, to prove bounded extension of
{\mathcal{E}}_{A}
at the extreme point
\beta=0
and
p=\infty
, one uses (
8.3
).

Next, we prove Theorem
7.1
. For
p<\infty
, it readily follows from Sobolev embedding (
8.2
). For
p=\infty
, we first establish Proposition
6.4
(with the canonical modification for
\beta=0
), using the same arguments in
[
3
, Propositions 6.2 and 9.1]
. Then following the proof in Section
7.1
, we may assume
u={\mathcal{R}}^{{\mathds{I}}}_{1/2}((A-{\mathds{I}})\nabla u)
(cf. (
7.2
)) and then use the homotopy identity (
7.1
) with the limits

\lim_{s\to 0}u(s)=0\penalty\ \text{ in }E^{\infty}_{\delta},\quad\lim_{s\to 0}\Gamma_{A}(t,s)^{\ast}h=\Gamma_{A}(t,0)^{\ast}h\penalty\ \text{ in }E^{1}_{\delta}.

This proves Theorem
7.1
as desired.

To prove Theorem
7.2
, we also use embedding of tent space for
p<\infty
. For
p=\infty
, we apply real interpolation to (
7.7
) to get

\|u(s)\|_{E^{p}_{s/16}}\lesssim s^{\beta+1/2}\|u\|_{Z^{p}_{\beta+1}}.

Then the uniqueness follows from the same arguments in Section
7.2
.

Theorem
1.3
is a direct consequence of Theorem
7.1
and Proposition
6.4
. This completes the proof.
∎

It is worth noting that Theorem
8.1
is based on our specific choice of realizations (in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
) of homogeneous Besov spaces defined on
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}/{\mathscr{P}}
. For instance, there is another family of realizations given by
[
13
, Definition 2.15]
, which does not contain polynomials for any
s
and
p
, denoted by
{\widetilde{B}}^{s}_{p,p}
. The identification
{\widetilde{B}}^{s}_{p,p}+{\mathds{C}}={\dot{B}}^{s}_{p,p}+{\mathds{C}}
holds when
s<n/p
, but is not clear otherwise (see
[
13
, Remark 2.26]
).

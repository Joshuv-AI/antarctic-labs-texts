Universal Thermodynamic Uncertainty Relation for Quantum f − f- Divergences
Domingos S. P. Salazar

Abstract
We show that any Petz f f -divergence (where f f is operator convex) between quantum states admits a universal χ 2 \chi^{2} –mixture representation: the distinguishability of ρ \rho from σ \sigma is obtained as a positive superposition of quadratic contrasts χ λ 2 \chi^{2}_{\lambda} , with nonnegative weights w f ​ ( λ ) w_{f}(\lambda) determined explicitly from the Löwner-Stieltjes representation of the generator f f . This identifies χ λ 2 \chi^{2}_{\lambda} as atomic building blocks for quantum f f -divergences and yields closed-form w f w_{f} for canonical choices (relative entropy/KL, Hellinger/Bures, Rényi). By mapping χ λ 2 \chi^{2}_{\lambda} into a classical Pearson χ 2 \chi^{2} , we leverage Chapman-Robbins variational representation and obtain a tight and universal quantum thermodynamic uncertainty relation: any f f -divergence is lower bounded by a function of the statistics of quantum observables (mean and variance), reproducing previous and novel results in quantum thermodynamics as applications.

Step 1 (Fundamental dissimilarity).
Uncertainty (quantified as variance versus bias) is fundamentally captured by the Pearson
\chi^{2}
via the Hammersley-Chapman–Robbins variational identity

Chapman and Robbins 1951

P\ll Q

\Theta\in L^{2}(Q)

\chi^{2}(P\!\parallel\!Q)\;=\;\sup_{\Theta\in L^{2}(Q)}\frac{\big|\langle\Theta\rangle_{P}-\langle\Theta\rangle_{Q}\big|^{2}}{\mathrm{Var}_{Q}(\Theta)},

(1)

\chi^{2}(P\!\parallel\!Q):=\sum_{i}(P_{i}-Q_{i})^{2}/Q_{i}
. Thus
\chi^{2}
is
the

Step 2 (Universal lifting).
We lift this backbone to quantum statistics through Petz

-divergences

Hiai et al. 2011

(3)

\chi^{2}_{\lambda}(\rho||\sigma):=D_{f_{\lambda}}(\rho||\sigma),\qquad f_{\lambda}(u):=\frac{(u-1)^{2}}{(1-\lambda)u+\lambda},

Classical
f
-divergence and its
\chi^{2}_{\lambda}
integral representation.

For probability distributions
P,Q
on a finite (or countable) set with
P\ll Q
, the Csiszár
f
-divergence is

D_{f}^{\mathrm{c}}(P\!\parallel\!Q)\;:=\;\sum_{i}Q_{i}\,f\!\Big(\frac{P_{i}}{Q_{i}}\Big),

for convex
f:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
. For specific choices of
f(t)
, one gets Kullback-Leibler [
f(t)=t\ln t
], Pearson
\chi^{2}
[
f(t)=(t-1)^{2}
], Total variation [
f(t)=(1/2)|t-1|
] and so on.
Now we introduce the
\lambda
–mixture
Q_{\lambda}:=(1-\lambda)P+\lambda Q
and the classical kernels (
\lambda\in(0,1)
),

\chi_{\lambda}(P||Q):=\;\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\chi^{2}\!\big(P\!\parallel\!Q_{\lambda}\big)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\sum_{i}\frac{(P_{i}-Q_{\lambda,i})^{2}}{Q_{\lambda,i}},

(7)

and define

g(u)\;:=\;\frac{f(u)}{(u-1)^{2}},\qquad u>0.

(8)

A classical result (Löwner/Pick–Nevanlinna representation) states that, for a broad class of generators
f
(called operator convex
Hiai et al. 2011
),
g
is a
Stieltjes function
, i.e.

g(u)\;=\;\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\phi_{f}(t)}{\,1+t\,u\,}\,dt,\qquad\phi_{f}(t)\geq 0,

(9)

with
\phi_{f}
determined uniquely by
f
. Plugging (
9
) back gives

D_{f}^{c}(P\|Q)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\phi_{f}(t)\,\sum_{x}q_{x}\frac{(u_{x}-1)^{2}}{1+tu_{x}}\,dt.

(10)

with change variables
t=\tfrac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}
and

d\lambda=-\tfrac{1}{(1+t)^{2}}dt
). A short algebraic match yields

D_{f}^{c}(P\|Q)\;=\;\int_{0}^{1}w_{f}(\lambda)\,\chi^{2}_{\lambda}(P\|Q)\,d\lambda,

(11)

with a nonnegative weight (see properties further in the SM),

w_{f}(\lambda)\;=\;\frac{1}{\lambda}\;\phi_{f}\!\Big(\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}\Big),\qquad\lambda\in(0,1).

(12)

In words:
\chi^{2}_{\lambda}
are the nonnegative spectral weights any
f
-divergence is a positive mixture of them.
A practical route to get
w_{f}(\lambda)
is
using Stieltjes inversion (directly from
f
).
Compute
g(u)
from (
8
). The inversion formula gives (See SM), for
t>0
,

\phi_{f}(t)\;=\;\frac{1}{\pi t}\,\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\operatorname{Im}\,g\!\Big(-\frac{1}{t}-i\varepsilon\Big),

(13)

and then (
12
) yields
w_{f}
exactly. In summary, from a given
f
, one defines
g
using (
8
), then finds
\phi_{f}
using (
13
) and
w_{\lambda}
using (
12
).

Quantum lifting via NS.

Consider density matrices
\rho
and
\sigma
with spectral decompositions
\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}|p_{i}\rangle\!\langle p_{i}|
and
\sigma=\sum_{j}q_{j}|q_{j}\rangle\!\langle q_{j}|

P_{\rho}(i,j):=p_{i}|\langle p_{i}|q_{j}\rangle|^{2},\qquad Q_{\sigma}(i,j):=q_{j}|\langle p_{i}|q_{j}\rangle|^{2}.

For the Petz
f
-divergence in LR form (
2
), the NS correspondence matches the quantum divergence from a theory in quantum information (see SM),

D_{f}(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma)=D_{f}^{\mathrm{c}}(P_{\rho}\!\parallel\!Q_{\sigma}).

(14)

Combining (
11
) with (
14
) yields the
quantum
\chi^{2}_{\lambda}
integral representation
(
3
), with the quantum kernel (
4
) given explicitly

\chi^{2}_{\lambda}(\rho||\sigma)=\sum_{ij}\frac{(p_{i}-q_{j})^{2}}{(1-\lambda)p_{i}+\lambda q_{j}}|\langle p_{i}|q_{j}\rangle|^{2}.

(15)

From kernels to TURs.

Let
\hat{\theta}
be Hermitian with finite variances under both states and set

x:=\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\rho}-\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\sigma}
,

y:=\mathrm{Var}_{\rho}(\hat{\theta})
,

z:=\mathrm{Var}_{\sigma}(\hat{\theta})
. Extend the NS distribution to operators

by defining the (complex) random variable
\Theta(i,j)=\langle p_{i}|\hat{\theta}|q_{j}\rangle/\langle p_{i}|q_{j}\rangle
, for
\langle p_{i}|q_{j}\rangle\neq 0
, and
\Theta(i,j)=0
. We have
X:=Var_{P}(\Theta)\leq x
,
Y:=Var_{Q}(\Theta)\leq y
and
Z=|E_{P}(\Theta)-E_{Q}(\Theta)|=z
.
Applying the Chapman–Robbins variational identity (
1
) in (
7
) gives

\chi^{2}_{\lambda}(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma)=\lambda^{-2}\chi^{2}(P||Q_{\lambda})\geq h_{\lambda}(X,Y,Z)\geq h_{\lambda}(x,y,z),

(16)

with
h_{\lambda}
as in (
6
), which is decreasing in the arguments
y,z
. Substituting (
16
) into the quantum integral representation (
3
) yields the
f
-divergence TUR (
5
). The weight
w_{f}
selects which fluctuation sectors dominate a given
D_{f}
, transporting the Chapman–Robbins precision constraint from
\chi^{2}_{\lambda}
to any Petz
f-
divergence. In the SM, we prove a stronger version, where it shows that the lower bound (
5

D_{f}(\rho||\sigma)\geq D_{f}(\rho_{\star}||\sigma_{\star})
, with
\rho_{\star}=r|u_{1}\rangle\langle u_{1}|+(1-r)|u_{2}\rangle\langle u_{2}|
and
\sigma_{\star}=s|u_{1}\rangle\langle u_{1}|+(1-s)|u_{2}\rangle\langle u_{2}|
, where
|u_{1}\rangle,|u_{2}\rangle
are eigenvectors of
\hat{\theta}
(with eigenvalues

given as
r=\tfrac{1}{2}+\frac{b+\Delta^{2}}{4\Delta v}
,

\Delta:=x

v:=\frac{1}{2|\Delta|}\sqrt{b^{2}+2\Delta^{2}(y+z)+\Delta^{4}}

\chi^{2}_{\lambda}(\rho_{\star}||\sigma_{\star})=h_{\lambda}(x,y,z)

D_{f}(\rho_{\star}||\sigma_{\star})=\int_{0}^{1}w_{f}(\lambda)h_{\lambda}(x,y,z)d\lambda.

(17)

Properties of the
\chi^{2}_{\lambda}
integral representation—
The
\chi^{2}_{\lambda}
integral representation in Eqs. (
11
) and (
3
), with mixing law
w_{f}(\lambda)
given by (
12
), endows each
f
-divergence with a transparent “weight” structure over fluctuation sectors indexed by
\lambda\in(0,1)
. The items below summarize the key structural facts we use throughout; proofs and technicalities appear in the Supplementary Material (SM).

(P1) Nonnegativity and uniqueness

For any admissible
f
, the weight is nonnegative:

w_{f}(\lambda)\geq 0,

(18)

on
(0,1)
, and it is unique almost everywhere (a.e.). This property was particularly useful to derive our universal quantum TUR (
5
).

(P2) Linearity and affine invariance.

The map
f\mapsto w_{f}
is linear on the cone of convex generators, and it is invariant under adding affine pieces:

w_{\alpha f_{1}+f_{2}}=\alpha\,w_{f_{1}}+w_{f_{2}},\qquad w_{\,f+a+b(u-1)}=w_{f}.

(19)

Physically, affine shifts do not change
D_{f}^{\mathrm{c}}
(nor
D_{f}
) and hence do not alter the mixing law.

(P3) Order preservation.

If
f_{1}\leq f_{2}
pointwise (modulo affine terms), then
w_{f_{2}}-w_{f_{1}}\geq 0
a.e. Thus the partial order on divergences is mirrored by a partial order on the weights.

(P4) Endpoints of
f
.

The weight encodes key boundary/curvature data of
f
. After harmless centering at
u=1
, for differentiable
f
, set
\tilde{f}(u)=f(u)-f(1)-f^{\prime}(1)(u-1)
, one has

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(0^{+})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{-1}\,w_{f}(\lambda)\,d\lambda,

(20a)

\displaystyle\lim_{u\to\infty}\frac{f(u)}{u}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}(1-\lambda)^{-1}\,w_{f}(\lambda)\,d\lambda.

(20b)

(P5) Duality under inversion.

For the dual generator
f^{\ast}(u):=u\,f(1/u)
one has
a.e. on
(0,1)

w_{f^{\ast}}(\mu)=\,w_{f}(1-\mu).

(21)

Thus exchanging forward/backward sectors (
\lambda\leftrightarrow 1-\lambda
) corresponds to the standard
f\!\leftrightarrow\!f^{\ast}
duality (Jeffreys/symmetric cases inherit a self-consistency under this map).

(P6) Small-contrast control and all orders.

Let
\tilde{f}(u):=f(u)-f(1)-f^{\prime}(1)(u-1)
so that
\tilde{f}(1)=\tilde{f}^{\prime}(1)=0
. Then, for
|\varepsilon|<1
,

\tilde{f}(1+\varepsilon)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\!\left(\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{n}\,w_{f}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\right)\varepsilon^{n},

(22)

and, equivalently, for each
n\geq 2
,

\frac{\tilde{f}^{(n)}(1)}{n!}=(-1)^{n-2}\!\int_{0}^{1}(1-\lambda)^{n-2}\,w_{f}(\lambda)\,d\lambda.

(23)

Particularly, we have
\tilde{f}^{\prime\prime}(1)/2=\int_{0}^{1}w_{f}(\lambda)d\lambda>0
, which ensures that
w_{f}(\lambda)
is normalizable.

Application: Quantum Thermodynamic Uncertainty Relations—

We illustrate how the integral representation (
3
) and the universal TUR (
5
) combine into a concrete strategy that turns a generator
f
(operator convex) into an explicit “thermodynamic uncertainty relation”. Consider the usual setup in quantum thermodynamics
Landi and Paternostro 2021
, where we have system + environment evolving with a unitary (
U
), the entropy production is defined with respect to the following states:
\rho:=\rho_{SE}^{\prime}
and
\sigma:=\rho_{S}^{\prime}\otimes\rho_{E}
, where
\rho_{SE}^{\prime}:=U(\rho_{S}\otimes\rho_{E})U^{\dagger}
and
\rho_{S}^{\prime}:=\Tr_{E}(\rho_{SE}^{\prime})
. In this case, we have the entropy production
\Sigma:=D(\rho||\sigma)
. Additionally, let
\hat{\theta}
be an Hermitian operator and
x:=\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\rho}-\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\sigma}
,
y:={\rm Var}_{\rho}(\hat{\theta})
,
z:={\rm Var}_{\sigma}(\hat{\theta})
. In what follows, for each case we (i) identify the generator
f
, (ii) recall the familiar closed form of
D_{f}(\rho||\sigma)
, (iii) retrieve the mixing weight
w_{f}(\lambda)
from the Sup. Mat. (SM), and (iv) write the resulting TUR in a form that highlights structure or simplifications. In all cases, the resulting integral is given by
D_{f}(\rho_{\star}|\sigma_{\star})
from (
5
), but we choose to display the lower bound as an integral of
h_{\lambda}
in order to understand how TURs differ only by their spectral weights
w_{f}(\lambda)
.

Quantum relative entropy (Entropy Production).

The forward KL is generated by
f(u)=u\log u-(u-1)
, yielding the standard expression

D(\rho||\sigma)=\Tr[\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)]
.
The SM provides the weight (via (
12
)) as

w_{\mathrm{KL}}(\lambda)=\lambda
.
Substituting in (
5
) we obtain

\Sigma=D(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma)\ \geq\ \int_{0}^{1}\lambda\,h_{\lambda}(x,y,z)\,d\lambda.

(24)

This is the TUR for the quantum entropy production
Salazar 2024b
, as expected.

Reverse quantum relative entropy.

For the reverse KL,
f(u)=-\log u+(u-1)
and

D_{\rm rKL}(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma)=D(\sigma||\rho)
, we use property (
21
),
w_{\mathrm{rKL}}(\lambda)=w_{KL}(1-\lambda)=(1-\lambda)
,
so that

D(\sigma||\rho)\ \geq\ \int_{0}^{1}(1-\lambda)h_{\lambda}(x,y,z)\,d\lambda,

(25)

which is immediate also from the symmetries of
h_{\lambda}(x,y,z)=h_{1-\lambda}(-x,z,y)
.

Jeffreys divergence.
Symmetric quantum relative entropy (also known as Jeffreys divergence) is given by
D(\rho,\sigma)=(1/2)(D(\rho||\sigma)+D(\sigma||\rho))
. Using property (
19
), we have
w_{f}(\lambda)=(1/2)[w_{KL}(\lambda)+w_{rKL}(\lambda)]=1
. Thus, the Quantum TUR,

D(\sigma,\rho)\ \geq\ \int_{0}^{1}h_{\lambda}(x,y,z)\,d\lambda,

(26)

which is also equivalent to a previous result
Salazar 2024a
and its classical equivalent, sometimes called exchange TUR
Timpanaro et al. 2019
.

Pearson
\chi^{2}
.

As a sanity check, for
f(u)=(u-1)^{2}
one has the Pearson divergence

D_{\mathrm{P}}(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma)=\chi^{2}_{\,1}=\chi^{2}(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma)
(
7
).
The SM shows that the mixture reduces to a point mass,

w_{\mathrm{P}}(\lambda)=\delta(1-\lambda)
,
so (
5
) collapses to a (quantum) Chappman-Robbins relation,

\chi^{2}(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma)\ \geq\ h_{1}(x,y,z)=\frac{x^{2}}{z},

(27)

which is also written as

\frac{\langle\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle\rangle_{\sigma}}{(\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\rho}-\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\sigma})^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\Tr(\rho^2 \sigma^{-1})-1},,

(28)

after using
\chi^{2}_{1}(\rho||\sigma)=\Tr(\rho^2 \sigma^{-1})-1
. Equation (
28
) is one step away from a previous result
Hasegawa 2025
, where they further optimize over all possible
\sigma
.

Squared Hellinger.

For the Hellinger generator
f(u)=(1/2)(\sqrt{u}-1)^{2}
, the divergence equals

H^{2}(\rho,\sigma)=\big(1-\Tr[\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\sigma}]\big)
in the Petz framework.
The SM yields the nontrivial algebraic weight

w_{H^{2}}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\lambda(1-\lambda)}
(See SM).
Consequently from (
5
), we have

H^{2}(\rho,\sigma)\ \geq\ 1-\Bigg(1+\frac{(\langle\hat{\theta})_{\rho}-\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\sigma})^{2}}{(\sqrt{\langle\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle\rangle_{\rho}}+\sqrt{\langle\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle\rangle_{\sigma}})^{2}}\Bigg)^{-1/2},

(29)

which reproduces a previous classical result also used in the context of quantum thermodynamics
Nishiyama and Hasegawa 2025b
.

Petz–Rényi

This is a novel contribution of the Universal TUR and this case is related to cumulant generating functions
Landi and Paternostro 2021
, therefore the integral representation is valuable to understand the statistics of operators. With

f_{\alpha}(u)=\frac{u^{\alpha}-1-\alpha(u-1)}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}
,
for
\alpha\!\in\!(0,1)
, which guarantees
f
is operator convex, and the divergence reads

D_{\alpha}(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma):=\frac{1}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\big(\Tr[\rho^{\alpha}\sigma^{1-\alpha}]-1\big)
.
We obtain

w_{\alpha}(\lambda)=\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi\,\alpha(1-\alpha)}\,\lambda^{\alpha}(1-\lambda)^{1-\alpha}\geq 0

so

D_{\alpha}(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma)\ \geq\ \frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi\,\alpha(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{\alpha}(1-\lambda)^{1-\alpha}\,h_{\lambda}(x,y,z)\,d\lambda.

(30)

As
\alpha\uparrow 1
the weight tends to
w_{0}(\lambda)=\lambda
(KL), as
\alpha\downarrow 0
, it tends to
w_{0}(\lambda)=1-\lambda
(rKL) and
w_{1/2}(\lambda)=4w_{H^{2}}(\lambda)
(Hellinger’s). And the symmetric
\alpha
divergence is related to a symmetric TUR
Salazar 2024c
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

In quantum thermodynamics, the
\alpha-
Divergence appears naturally as a cummulant generating function (CGF) of the nonequilibrim lag problem
Landi and Paternostro 2021
;
Campisi et al. 2011
. In this context, a system S undergoes a work protocol starting from thermal equilibrium state,
\rho_{i}^{th}=e^{-\beta H_{i}}/Z_{i}
, and evolves in time driven by unitary generated by the time dependent Hamiltonian
H(t)
(
H(0)=H_{i}
,
H(\tau)=H_{f}
),
\rho^{\prime}=V\rho^{th}_{i}V^{\dagger}
. After a time
\tau
, the system thermalizes from the nonequilibrium state
\rho^{\prime}
to
\rho^{th}_{f}=e^{-\beta H_{f}}/Z_{f}
. The entropy production is given by
\Sigma=D(\rho^{\prime}||\rho_{f}^{th})
and it is also called the nonequilibrum lag. The nonequilibrium CDF
Talkner et al. 2007
;
Esposito et al. 2009
;
Guarnieri et al. 2019
(which is related to statistics of a random variable

\langle S\rangle=\Sigma
) is given by

K(\alpha)=\ln\Tr[(\rho_{f}^{th})^{\alpha}(\rho^{\prime})^{1-\alpha}].

(31)

From our definition of
D_{\alpha}
, we see that
K(\alpha)=\ln[1+\alpha(1-\alpha)D_{\alpha}(\rho_{f}^{th}||\rho^{\prime})]
and from (
30

K(\alpha)\geq\ln[1+\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{\alpha}(1-\lambda)^{1-\alpha}h_{\lambda}(x,y,z)d\lambda\bigg],

(32)

for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, in terms of any system observable

Conclusions—

We introduced a two–step, broadly applicable strategy to derive sharp, information–theoretic uncertainty relations for quantum thermodynamics. The method is modular: a
variational backbone
that captures the operational uncertainty of interest, coupled with an
integral lifting
that transports that backbone to full quantum Petz
f
–divergences.

The starting point is the variational representation of the classical
\chi^{2}
–divergence, the Chapman–Robbins identity in Eq. (
1
). Through the Nussbaum–Szkoła distributions, this leads to the quantum TUR (
5
). This step is problem–driven: in this work the relevant quantum uncertainty is a mean–square to variance ratio, so
\chi^{2}
is the
fundamental dissimilarity
that matches the structure of the task. For different uncertainty functionals (e.g., constraints built from higher moments, skewness–like functionals, or local susceptibilities), one should swap in the variational identity that naturally encodes the corresponding control–to–noise tradeoff, thereby replacing
h_{\lambda}
by the contrast induced by the new backbone.
We showed our strategy resulted in several known and novel results in quantum thermodynamics. Namely, the quantum entropy production TUR, the symmetric quantum relative entropy TUR, a lower bound for the CDF in the nonequilibrium lag problem and bonds for other dissimilarities (triangular discrimination, Hellinger’s). We expect the result will be useful and extended to unravel new properties of systems in quantum thermodynamics.

Setup.
Let

\sigma=\sum_{j}q_{j}|q_{j}\rangle\!\langle q_{j}|

Define the NS distributions

Write the left/right multiplication superoperators as

The Petz divergence used in the

(operator-convex on
(0,\infty)

Diagonalizing

Consider rank-one operators

(L_{\rho}R_{\sigma^{-1}})(E_{ij})=\rho E_{ij}\sigma^{-1}=\frac{p_{i}}{q_{j}}\,E_{ij},

Equality of
f

Expand

\displaystyle=\big\langle\sigma^{1/2},\,f(L_{\rho}R_{\sigma^{-1}})\,\sigma^{1/2}\big\rangle_{HS}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k,\ell}\sqrt{q_{j}q_{\ell}}\,\langle p_{i}|q_{j}\rangle\,\overline{\langle p_{k}|q_{\ell}\rangle}\,\big\langle E_{ij},\,f(L_{\rho}R_{\sigma^{-1}})E_{k\ell}\big\rangle_{HS}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}q_{j}\,|\langle p_{i}|q_{j}\rangle|^{2}\,f\!\Big(\frac{p_{i}}{q_{j}}\Big)=\sum_{i,j}Q_{\sigma}(i,j)\,f\!\Big(\frac{P_{\rho}(i,j)}{Q_{\sigma}(i,j)}\Big).

\boxed{\,D_{f}(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma)=D_{f}^{\mathrm{c}}(P_{\rho}\!\parallel\!Q_{\sigma})\,}.

Equality for the
\lambda

In the quantum notation used in the paper (Formalism; Intro), define the resolvent-like superoperator

Therefore

\boxed{\,\chi^{2}_{\lambda}(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma)=\chi^{2}_{\lambda}(P_{\rho}\!\parallel\!Q_{\sigma})\,}.

Worked-out examples: extracting
w_{f}(\lambda)
from
f

This section implements the main-text pipeline to pass from a Petz
f
–divergence to its
\chi_{\lambda}^{2}
–mixture weights. In the classical setting we use

D_{f}^{\mathrm{c}}(P\!\parallel\!Q)\;=\;\int_{0}^{1}w_{f}(\lambda)\,\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\,\chi^{2}\!\big(P\!\parallel\!Q_{\lambda}\big)\,d\lambda,\qquad Q_{\lambda}=\lambda\,Q+(1-\lambda)\,P,

(S40)

whose binary reduction yields the kernel identity

g(u)\;:=\;\frac{f(u)}{(u-1)^{2}}\;=\;\int_{0}^{1}\frac{w_{f}(\lambda)}{\lambda+(1-\lambda)\,u}\,d\lambda,\qquad u>0.

(S41)

By operator convexity of
f
,
g
is a Stieltjes function and admits

g(u)\;=\;\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\phi_{f}(t)}{\,1+t\,u\,}\,dt,\qquad\phi_{f}(t)\geq 0,

(S42)

with Stieltjes inversion

\phi_{f}(t)\;=\;\frac{1}{\pi t}\,\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\operatorname{Im}\,g\!\Big(-t^{-1}-i\varepsilon\Big),\qquad t>0.

(S43)

The
\chi_{\lambda}^{2}
–mixture weights follow from the change
t=\tfrac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}
:

w_{f}(\lambda)\;=\;\frac{1}{\lambda}\,\phi_{f}\!\Big(\tfrac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}\Big),\qquad\lambda\in(0,1).

(S44)

Endpoint atoms.

Atoms of
\phi_{f}
at
t=0
or
t=\infty
push forward to
\lambda=1
and
\lambda=0
, respectively:

t=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \lambda=1,\qquad t=\infty\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \lambda=0,

(S45)

with the usual Jacobian already accounted for in (
S44
). We record such cases explicitly via Dirac deltas in
w_{f}
.

(A) Kullback–Leibler (forward KL).

f(u)=u\log u-(u-1).

(S46)

A standard representation gives

\phi_{\rm KL}(t)=\frac{1}{(1+t)^{2}},\qquad w_{\rm KL}(\lambda)=\lambda.

(S47)

(B) Reverse Kullback–Leibler.

f(u)=-\log u+(u-1),

(S48)

for which

\phi_{\rm rKL}(t)=\frac{t}{(1+t)^{2}},\qquad w_{\rm rKL}(\lambda)=1-\lambda.

(S49)

(C) Pearson’s
\chi^{2}
.

f(u)=(u-1)^{2},\qquad g(u)\equiv 1.

(S50)

This is an atom at
t=0
and hence

\phi_{\rm P}(t)=\delta(t),\qquad w_{\rm P}(\lambda)=\delta(\lambda-1).

(S51)

(D) Neyman’s
\chi^{2}
(“reverse Pearson”).

f(u)=\frac{(u-1)^{2}}{u},\qquad g(u)=\frac{1}{u}.

(S52)

This is an atom at
t=\infty
and therefore

\phi_{\rm N}(t)=\delta(t-\infty),\qquad w_{\rm N}(\lambda)=\delta(\lambda).

(S53)

(E) Petz–Rényi (forward),
0<\alpha<1
.

Use the tangentially normalized form
f_{\alpha}(1)=f^{\prime}_{\alpha}(1)=0
,

f_{\alpha}(u)=\frac{u^{\alpha}-1-\alpha(u-1)}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}.

(S54)

Stieltjes inversion yields

\phi_{\alpha}(t)=\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi\,\alpha(1-\alpha)}\,\frac{t^{\,1-\alpha}}{(1+t)^{2}},\qquad w_{\alpha}(\lambda)=\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi\,\alpha(1-\alpha)}\,\lambda^{\alpha}\,(1-\lambda)^{\,1-\alpha}.

(S55)

The limits are continuous:

\alpha\uparrow 1:\ w_{\alpha}(\lambda)\to\lambda\ \ (\text{KL}),\qquad\alpha\downarrow 0:\ w_{\alpha}(\lambda)\to 1-\lambda\ \ (\text{reverse KL}).

(S56)

(F) Symmetric Petz–Rényi,
0<\alpha<1
.

A symmetric, tangential choice is

f^{\rm sym}_{\alpha}(u)=f_{\alpha}(u)+f_{1-\alpha}(u)=\frac{u^{\alpha}+u^{1-\alpha}-2-(u-1)}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}.

(S57)

By linearity of (
S42
)–(
S44
),

\phi^{\rm sym}_{\alpha}(t)=\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi\,\alpha(1-\alpha)}\,\frac{t^{\,1-\alpha}+t^{\,\alpha}}{(1+t)^{2}},\qquad w^{\rm sym}_{\alpha}(\lambda)=\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi\,\alpha(1-\alpha)}\,\Big[\lambda^{\alpha}(1-\lambda)^{1-\alpha}+\lambda^{1-\alpha}(1-\lambda)^{\alpha}\Big].

(S58)

(G) Hellinger (squared).

f(u)=(1/2)(\sqrt{u}-1)^{2}.

(S59)

Direct inversion gives

\phi_{\rm H}(t)=\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\pi\,(1+t)^{2}},\qquad w_{\rm H}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi}\,\sqrt{\lambda(1-\lambda)}.

(S60)

(H) Triangular discrimination.

f(u)=\frac{(u-1)^{2}}{u+1},\qquad g(u)=\frac{1}{u+1}.

(S61)

This is a single atom at
t=1
, hence

\phi_{\triangle}(t)=\delta(t-1),\qquad w_{\triangle}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}\,\delta\!\Big(\lambda-\tfrac{1}{2}\Big).

(S62)

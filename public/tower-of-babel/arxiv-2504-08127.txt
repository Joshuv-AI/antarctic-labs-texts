Quasisymmetric rectifiability of uniformly disconnected sets Thanks: J.H. and V.V. were partially supported by NSF DMS grant 2154918.
Jacob Honeycutt, Vyron Vellis

Abstract
. We prove that uniformly disconnected subsets of metric measure spaces with controlled geometry (complete, Ahlfors regular, supporting a Poincaré inequality, and a mild topological condition) are contained in a quasisymmetric arc. This generalizes a result of MacManus [ Mac99 ] from Euclidean spaces to abstract metric setting. Along the way, we prove a geometric strengthening of the classical Denjoy-Riesz theorem in metric measure spaces. Finally, we prove that the complement of a uniformly disconnected set in such a metric space is uniform, quantitatively.

1.
Introduction

In the early 1900s, Denjoy
[
Den10
]
and Riesz
[
Rie05
]
independently proved what has come to be known as the Denjoy-Riesz Theorem, which states that
a compact, totally disconnected subset of the plane is contained in an arc.
Recall that a totally disconnected set is one whose connected components are singletons (or equivalently, one that has topological dimension equal to zero) and an arc is the homeomorphic image of the unit interval. This builds upon work by Zoretti
[
Zor05
]
, which instead used a more general class of arcs than just Jordan arcs. More generally, Moore and Kline
[
MK19
]
came up with a complete characterization of subsets of the plane which are contained in arcs, and this includes the Denjoy-Riesz Theorem as a special case. Later, Zippin
[
Zip32
]
provided a complete classification of metric spaces for which the Moore-Kline Theorem holds. A corollary of Zippin’s work is that in a compact, connected, locally connected metric space with no local cut-points, every compact totally disconnected set is contained in an arc; see also
[
Why31
]
. A useful application of the (generalized) Denjoy-Riesz theorem is that for every
n\geq 2
, there is an arc in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that has positive Lebesgue measure, and for every
n\geq 3
there is a
wild
arc in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, that is, an arc that is not ambiently homeomorphic to a line segment.

It is natural to ask whether one can obtain more regularity on the arc which passes through the entire set. David and Semmes
[
DS91
]
proved a bi-Lipschitz version of the Moore-Kline Theorem: if
n\geq 3
, then a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is contained in a bi-Lipschitz arc in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if and only if it is bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic to a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}
. MacManus
[
Mac95
]
extended the result of David and Semmes to the case
n=2
, which is more difficult since intersecting lines in dimensions greater than two can be modified so that they no longer intersect, but this is not the case in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. On one hand, these “bi-Lipschitz rectifiability” theorems can be viewed as a topological strengthening of the classical Lipschitz rectifiability theory which has been the focus of the Analyst’s Traveling Salesman Theorem
[
Jon90
,
Oki92
]
. On the other hand, they can be viewed as rough versions of the classical Whitney extension theorem
[
Whi34
]
.

To generalize the bi-Lipschitz rectifiability results to an abstract metric space
X
, in addition to the conditions of Zippin, it is necessary to assume certain geometric properties which ensure that
X
has plenty of rectifiable curves. Two such conditions are
Ahlfors regularity
and a
Poincaré inequality
. A metric space is Ahlfors
Q
-regular (or simply
Q
-regular) if there exists a measure
\mu
on
X
so that the measure of every ball
B(x,r)
is comparable to
r^{Q}
. A metric measure space has a Poincaré inequality if for every Lipschitz function
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
, the oscillation of
u
on each ball
B
is controlled by the average of a “weak derivative” of
u
on
B
. See Section
2
for all relevant definitions. It is known that Ahlfors regular spaces supporting a Poincaré inequality must contain quantitatively many rectifiable curves and admit a notion of differentiation
[
Che99
]
. The two authors along with Zimmerman
[
HVZ24
]
showed that in Ahlfors regular metric measure spaces with a suitable Poincaré inequality, the bi-Lipschitz rectifiability classification of David and Semmes holds.

The goal of this paper is to provide a
quasisymmetric
version of the Denjoy-Riesz theorem in abstract metric spaces. Roughly speaking, a homeomorphism between metric spaces is quasisymmetric if any three points with comparable mutual distances are mapped to three points with comparable mutual distances in a quantitative fashion. Due to Buerling and Ahlfors
[
BA56
]
, quasisymmetric maps are a generalization of conformal maps to the abstract metric setting which have been instrumental in the development of analysis on metric spaces. Quasisymmetric arcs (quasisymmetric images of
[0,1]
) have appeared in geometric function theory, geometric group theory (limits of quasi-Fuchsian groups), complex dynamics (Julia sets of certain rational functions), function spaces (domains of extension for Sobolev and BMO maps), Loewner evolution (traces of driving functions
\lambda
with Hölder norm less than 4), potential theory (Dirichlet integrals and harmonic bending), and hyperbolic geometry (bi-Lipschitz equivalence of hyperbolic and distance-ratio metrics). See
[
GH12
]
for a detailed exposition.

To replace “arc” by “quasisymmetric arc” in the Denjoy-Riesz theorem, necessarily some geometric assumptions should be made about the set
E
. For example, if
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a Cantor set with positive Lebesgue
n
-measure, then any arc that contains
E
will have positive Lebesgue
n
-measure so it can not be a quasisymmetric arc
[
Väi81
, Theorem 4.1]
. A quantitative version of total disconnectedness was introduced by David and Semmes
[
DS97
]
: a metric space
E
is
uniformly disconnected
if for all
x\in E
and all
r\in(0,\diam{E})
there exists a set
E_{x,r}\subset E
such that
x\in E_{x,r}
,
\diam{E_{x,r}}\leq r
, and
\dist(E_{x,r},E\setminus E_{x,r})
is at least a fixed multiple of
r
(i.e.,
E
has isolated islands at every scale and at every point). Using this notion, MacManus
[
Mac99
]
proved a quasisymmetric version of the Denjoy-Riesz theorem in Euclidean spaces.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
Mac99
, Theorem 1]
)
.

A compact, uniformly disconnected subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is contained in a quasisymmetric arc.

Our main focus is to generalize Theorem
1.1
to a larger class of metric measure spaces. Towards this end, a topological condition in the spirit of Zippin’s no-local-cut-point condition is assumed on the space
X
. We say that a set
E\subset X
is a
cut-set
if
X\setminus E
is not connected; otherwise we call
E
a
full set
. The latter term is inspired by
[
NS19
]
where a planar set
E
is called full if
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus E
has no bounded components. The assumptions of
Q
-regularity and a
Q
-Poincaré inequality on
X
yield that if
E
is a compact set such that
X\setminus E
has no bounded components, then
E
is a full set; see Lemma
3.3
. Singletons in such a space
X
can not be local cut-sets but the same is not true with totally disconnected (or even uniformly disconnected) sets;
see the discussion after Question
1.4
.

The next topological condition is designed to make sure that closed totally disconnected sets do not separate the space. A metric space
X
is
fully connected
if it is path connected, locally path connected, and every full set is locally full. That is, if
X\setminus E
is connected and if
U
is an open, connected set such that
U\cap E\neq\varnothing
and
E\cap\partial U=\varnothing
, then
U\setminus E
is connected. For example, all Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are fully connected, while tori
(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{n}
are not. Our main theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a complete, fully connected, unbounded,
Q
-regular metric measure space supporting a
(Q-1)
-Poincaré inequality with
Q>2
. If
E\subset X
is a compact, uniformly disconnected set, then
E
is contained in a quasisymmetric arc.

This theorem is quantitative in the sense that the quasisymmetric control function depends only on the constants of Ahlfors regularity, the constants of the Poincaré inequality, and the constant of uniform disconnectedness. Canonical examples of of metric measure spaces satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
are Carnot groups, which include all Euclidean spaces and all Heisenberg groups. We will not define Carnot groups here but only mention that they are topologically identical to Euclidean space, but geometrically can behave much worse; although they are geodesic spaces, they have much fewer rectifiable curves and surfaces.

The techniques and tools used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, as well as modern proofs of the Denjoy-Riesz theorem, involve several classical tools from Euclidean spaces which are not available here. First, Euclidean spaces have a
dyadic decomposition
of cubes. This decomposition can be used to create a defining sequence for any Cantor set which consists of piecewise linear
n
-manifolds with boundary; see for example
[
Mac99
, Section 2]
and
[
BV19
, Corollary 5.2]
. While generalizations of dyadic decompositions exist in general metric spaces
[
HK12
,
KRS12
]
, here we take a more simple approach based on open neighborhoods and create “good” defining sequences for all compact, totally disconnected sets. Second, in Euclidean spaces, arcs can be approximated in the Hausdorff distance by bi-Lipschitz arcs quantitatively, but this is much less obvious in metric spaces with fractal directions such as the Heisenberg group. We resolve this issue by using the Poincaré inequality and Ahlfors regularity in order to generate nice curves; see §
1.2
for further discussion.

In fact, the techniques of this paper allow us to establish the following strengthening of the Denjoy-Riesz Theorem. Here,
\dim_{H}{X}
denotes the Hausdorff dimension of a metric space
X
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be a complete, fully connected, unbounded,
Q
-regular metric measure space supporting a
(Q-1)
-Poincaré inequality with
Q>2
. If
E\subset X
is a compact, totally disconnected set, then there exists an embedding
f:[0,1]\to X
such that
E\subset f([0,1])
and
f
is locally bi-Lipschitz away from
f^{-1}(E)
. In particular,

\dim_{H}{f([0,1])}=\max\{1,\dim_{H}{E}\}
.

One can also ask whether a complete classification of quasisymmetrically rectifiable sets exists, in the spirit of Moore-Kline (for arcs) and of David-Semmes and Honeycutt-Vellis-Zimmerman (for bi-Lipschitz arcs). In the bi-Lipschitz classification, the key ingredient is the existence of bi-Lipschitz extension theorems
[
DS91
,
HVZ24
]
. In contrast, quasisymmetric maps are in general much harder to extend and we leave the following as an open question.

Question 1.4
.

Let
X
be a complete, fully connected, unbounded,
Q
-regular metric measure space supporting a
(Q-1)
-Poincaré inequality for some
Q>2
. If
E\subset\mathbb{R}
and
f:E\to X
is a quasisymmetric embedding, is there a quasisymmetric extension
F:\mathbb{R}\to X
?

Question
1.4
is unknown even in the case where
X
is a Euclidean space although partial results exist if one increases the dimension of the target of the extension
[
Vel18
]
.

Although we do not know whether full connectedness can be removed from Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
, it can not be removed from Question
1.4
.
To see that, let
s\in(2,3)
, let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be an unbounded closed
s
-regular uniformly disconnected set (e.g. a blow-up tangent of an
s
-regular self-similar Cantor set), and let
A^{\prime}\subset[0,\infty)
be an unbounded closed Ahlfors regular uniformly disconnected set (e.g. a blow-up tangent of the standard Cantor set at 0). If
X=\mathbb{R}^{3}\cup_{A}\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the geodesic gluing of two copies of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
along
A
equipped with the Hausdorff 3-measure, then
X
is 3-regular and it supports a
2
-Poincaré inequality
[
HK98
, Theorem 6.15]
. Let now
y_{1},y_{2}\in X\setminus A
be two points in different copies of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and let
E=A^{\prime}\cup\{-2,-1\}
. Then there is a quasisymmetric embedding
f:E\to X
such that
f(A^{\prime})=A
,
f(-1)=y_{1}
and
f(-2)=y_{2}
(a simple variation of
[
DS97
, Proposition 15.11]
) which can not be extended homeomorphically (let alone quasisymmetrically) on
\mathbb{R}
.

We also remark that in Euclidean spaces there exist higher dimensional bi-Lipschitz and quasisymmetric rectifiability results; in particular, if
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is compact and uniformly disconnected, then for any
m\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
there exists a quasisymmetric embedding
f
of
[0,1]^{m}
into
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that contains
E
in its image
[
BV19
, Theorem 3.8]
. If, in addition, the Assouad dimension of
E
is less than
m
, then
f
can be assumed bi-Lipschitz
[
BV19
, Theorem 3.4]
. While Ahlfors regular metric spaces with Poincaré inequalities contain a lot of rectifiable curves, the same is not true with surfaces; for example, the Heisenberg group contains no bi-Lipschitz images of the unit square. It would be very interesting if analogous results were true for certain Carnot groups.

1.1.
Co-uniformity of uniformly disconnected sets

The notion of local separation of a uniformly disconnected set
E
naturally lends itself to the idea that there are lots of holes in it; in particular, the complement of
E
contains a lot of curves. A quantification of this idea was given by MacManus who proved that if
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is uniformly disconnected, then
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E
is a uniform set
[
Mac99
, Theorem 1]
. Recall that a set
U
is uniform if for any two points in
U
there is a curve joining them which is not too long compared to the distance of the points and does not get too close to the boundary, aside from possibly near the endpoints. Since their inception by Martio and Sarvas
[
MS79
]
, uniform domains have played a crucial role in geometric function theory, especially in the extendability of Sobolev and BMO functions
[
GO79
,
Jon80
,
Jon81
]
.

The study of uniform domains is prevalent in literature. In particular, uniformity has been explored through concepts like quasihyperbolicity
[
Her87
,
HVW17
,
Her22
]
, Gromov hyperbolicity
[
BHK01
,
HSX08
]
, uniform disconnectedness
[
Mac99
]
, bounds on topological dimension
[
Väi88
]
, and bounds on Assouad dimension
[
HVZ24
]
. We show that MacManus’ result generalizes to a large class of metric measure spaces, as given by the following theorem.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a complete, fully connected, unbounded,
Q
-regular metric measure space supporting a
Q
-Poincaré inequality. If
E
is a uniformly disconnected subset of
X
, then
X\setminus E
is uniform.

The theorem is quantitative in the sense that the constant of uniformity depends only on on the constants of Ahlfors regularity, the constants of the Poincaré inequality, and the constant of uniform disconnectedness. Note also that in Theorem
1.5
, we assume a
Q
-Poincaré inequality which is weaker than the
(Q-1)
-Poincaré inequality which is assumed in Theorem
1.2
.

1.2.
Outline of the paper

In Section
2
, we review some important definitions from analysis on metric spaces and present some preliminary results.

The common ingredient in the proof of all three main theorems is the construction of a “thickening” of compact totally disconnected sets in Section
3
. This thickening is a replacement for the cubic meshes used in MacManus’
[
Mac99
]
proof to give a natural decomposition of uniformly disconnected sets in Euclidean space. In particular, Proposition
3.1
provides us with a defining sequence
\{D_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of open sets in
X
such that

(1)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
D_{n}=D_{n,1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup D_{n,k_{n}}
where
D_{n,1},\dots,D_{n,k_{n}}
are mutually disjoint full domains with diameters at most a fixed multiple of
2^{-n}
,

(2)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, each
D_{n,i}
contains some
D_{n+1,j}
, and each
D_{n+1,i}
is contained in some
D_{n,j}

(3)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\dist(D_{n+1},\partial D_{n})>0
, and

(4)

E=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{i=1}^{k_{n}}\overline{D_{n,i}}
.

Moreover, in the case that
E
is uniformly disconnected, we have quantitative control on the size of
k_{n}
, diameters of
D_{n}
, and distances
\dist(D_{n+1},\partial D_{n})
.
Using this decomposition we prove Theorem
1.5
in Section
4
.

In Section
5
, we set up notation and some preliminary lemmas for the proof of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
. The crux of the proof of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
is the construction of bi-Lipschitz arcs in each
D_{n,i}\setminus D_{n+1}
with the first arc going from the boundary of
D_{n,i}
to the boundary of the first component
D_{n+1,i_{1}}
of
D_{n+1}
inside
D_{n}
, the second curve going from the boundary of
D_{n+1,i_{1}}
to the boundary of the second component
D_{n+1,i_{2}}
of
D_{n+1}
inside
D_{n}
and so on, so forth. The last curve, goes from the boundary of the last component of
D_{n+1}
inside
D_{n}
to the boundary of
D_{n}
; see Figure
1
. We take extra care so that these curves do not intersect with one another or with curves in other domains
D_{n,j}
. This construction relies heavily on the Poincaré inequality and the Ahlfors regularity of the space and is given in Section
6
.

In Section
7
, we perform modifications around the endpoints of the curves previously defined to ensure that the concatenation is locally bi-Lipschitz, (and globally quasisymmetric when
E
is uniformly disconnected) and in Section
8
we give the proofs of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
.

2.1.
Words

An

A=\{1,\dots,n\}

.

.

2.2.
Homogeneously totally bounded spaces and Ahlfors regularity

Inspired by
[
TV80
, §2.7]
, we say that a metric space

is
(C,\alpha)
-

(abbv.
(C,\alpha)
-HTB) for some

if for each
x\in X
, and each
0<r<R
, the ball

can be covered by at most

. Note that if

(C,\alpha)

-HTB for all

C_{0}^{-1}r^{Q}\leq\mu(B(x,r))\leq C_{0}r^{Q}.

If the right inequality in (

) holds for all

and

, then we say that
(X,d,\mu)
is
upper
(C_{0},Q)
-regular
. Note that if

is
(C_{0},Q)
-regular, then
(X,d,\mathcal{H}^{Q})
is

-regular for some

[

. Here and for the rest of the paper,

denotes the

It is not hard to see that if

is

-regular, then

is

2.3.
Mappings

Let
(X,d)
and
(Y,d^{\prime})
be two metric spaces.
Given
L\geq 1
and
\lambda>0
, a map
f:X\to Y
is said to be an
(L,\lambda)
-quasisimilarity
if for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
,

\frac{\lambda}{L}d(x_{1},x_{2})\leq d^{\prime}(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))\leq L\lambda d(x_{1},x_{2}).

If
\lambda=1
, then we say that
f
is
L
-bi-Lipschitz
.
If
L=1
, then
f
is a
\lambda
-similarity
.
If
L=\lambda=1
, then
f
is an
isometry
.

Given a homeomorphism
\eta:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, a map
f:X\to Y
is
\eta
-quasisymmetric
if for all
x,a,b\in X
with
x\neq b
,

(2.2)

\frac{d^{\prime}(f(x),f(a))}{d^{\prime}(f(x),f(b))}\leq\eta\left(\frac{d(x,a)}{d(x,b)}\right).

Since their inception by Beurling and Ahlfors
[
BA56
]
, quasisymmetric maps have played an important role in the study of analysis on metric spaces. While bi-Lipschitz maps roughly preserve absolute distances, quasisymmetric maps roughly preserve relative distances.

It is well-known that under certain conditions on
X
and
Y
, quasisymmetry is equivalent to a weaker condition, aptly named
weak quasisymmetry
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
Hei01
, Theorem 10.19]
)
.

Let
X
be a connected, HTB metric space and let
Y
be a HTB metric space. An embedding
f:X\to Y
is
\eta
-quasisymmetric if and only if there exists
H\geq 1
such that for any triple
x,y,z\in X
with
d(x,y)\leq d(x,z)
we have
d^{\prime}(f(x),f(y))\leq Hd^{\prime}(f(x),f(z))
. The function
\eta
, the constant
H
, and the HTB constants of
X
and
Y
are quantitatively related.

See
[
Hei01
]
for a detailed exposition on the theory of quasisymmetric maps.

2.4.
Porosity and uniform disconnectedness

We say that a set
E\subset X
is
p
-porous
for some
p\geq 1
if for all
x\in E
and all
r\in(0,\diam{X})
there exists a ball
B(y,p^{-1}r)\subset B(x,r)\setminus E
. The following lemma gives a classification of porous sets in Ahlfors regular spaces in terms of their dimension.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
BHR01
, Lemma 3.12]
)
.

Let
(X,d,\mathcal{H}^{Q})
be a
(C_{0},Q)
-regular metric space. A set
E\subset X
is
p
-porous if and only if it is
(C,\alpha)
-HTB for some
C>0
and
\alpha\in(0,Q)
. Constants
C
,
Q
,
C_{0}
, and
\alpha
are quantitatively related.

In light of the discussion in §
2.2
, the measure
\mathcal{H}^{Q}
in Lemma
2.2
can be replaced by any other
Q
-regular measure.

We say that a metric space
(X,d)
is
c
-uniformly disconnected
for some
c\geq 1
if for all
x\in X
and
r\in(0,\diam X)
, there exists a set
X_{x,r}\subset X
such that
x\in X_{x,r}
,
\diam{X_{x,r}}\leq r
, and
\dist(X_{x,r},X\setminus X_{x,r})\geq c^{-1}r
.

We say that a collection
\{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\}
of points in a metric space is a
\delta
-chain
if
d(x_{i-1},x_{i})\leq\delta d(x_{0},x_{n})
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. We say that the chain
joins

x_{0}
and
x_{n}
. The following lemma relates the definition of uniform disconnectedness to the nonexistence of
\delta
-chains.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
Hei01
, Exercise 14.26]
)
.

A metric space
X
is
c
-uniformly disconnected for some
c\geq 1
if and only if there exists
\delta\in(0,1)
such that for any two points
x,y\in X
, there is no
\delta
-chain joining
x
and
y
. Here,
\delta
and
c
depend only on each other.

2.5.
Poincaré inequality and modulus of curve families

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. We say that a function
g:X\to[0,\infty]
is an
upper gradient
of a locally Lipschitz function
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
if for all points
x
and
y
of
X
and all rectifiable curves
\upgamma\subset X
joining
x
with
y
,

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq\int_{\upgamma}g\,\mathrm{d}s.

A metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
is said to support a
p
-Poincaré inequality
if there exist constants
C,\lambda>1
such that for every locally Lipschitz function
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
, every upper gradient
g:X\to[0,\infty]
of
u
, every point
x\in X
, and every
r>0
,

\fint_{B(x,r)}|u-u_{B(x,r)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq C\diam(B(x,\lambda r))\left(\fint_{B(x,\lambda r)}g^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{1/p},

where, for an integrable function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
,

f_{B(x,r)}=\fint_{B(x,r)}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\frac{1}{\mu(B(x,r))}\int_{B(x,r)}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

It follows from Hölder’s inequality that if
p<q
and
(X,d,\mu)
satisfies a
p
-Poincaré inequality, then it also satisfies a
q
-Poincaré inequality. Moreover, if
(X,d,\mu)
is geodesic, then one can take
\lambda=1

[
HKST15
, Remark 9.1.19]
. If we want to emphasize the constants, we say that
(X,d,\mu)
satisfies an
(p,C)
-Poincaré inequality.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space and let
\Gamma
be a collection of rectifiable curves in
X
.
A function
f:X\to[0,\infty)
is said to be
admissible
for
\Gamma
if

\int_{\upgamma}f\,\mathrm{d}s\geq 1

for each
\upgamma\in\Gamma
. We define the
p
-modulus
of
\Gamma
by

\Mod_{p}(\Gamma)=\inf\left\{\int_{X}f^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu:\text{ $f:X\to[0,\infty)$ is admissible for $\Gamma$}\right\}.

The following proposition is useful for calculating upper bounds on the modulus of families of curves in upper Ahlfors regular spaces.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
Hei01
, Lemma 7.18]
)
.

Suppose that
(X,d,\mu)
is a metric measure space that is upper
(C_{0},Q)
-regular. There exists
C_{1}(C_{0},Q)\geq 1
such that for
x\in X
and
2r<R
,

\Mod_{Q}(\Gamma)\leq C_{1}\left(\log\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)\right)^{1-Q},

where
\Gamma
is the family of curves joining
\overline{B}(x,r)
to
X\setminus B(x,R)
.

A metric space
(X,d)
is said to be
Q
-Loewner
if there exists a decreasing homeomorphism
\Phi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that
\Mod_{Q}(E,F)\geq\Phi(\Delta(E,F))
for each pair of nondegenerate continua
E
and
F
in
X
. Ahlfors regularity and a Poincaré inequality imply the Loewner property.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
HK98
, Theorem 5.7]
)
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a complete,
(C_{0},Q)
-regular metric measure space supporting a
(Q,C)
-Poincaré inequality. Then
X
is
Q
-Loewner and the Loewner function
\Phi
depends only on
C_{0}
,
Q
, and
C
.

2.6.
Uniformity

A subset
U
of a metric space
(X,d)
is
c
-uniform
for some
c\geq 1
if for all
x,y\in\overline{U}
, there exists a curve
\upgamma:[0,1]\to\overline{U}
with
\upgamma(0)=x
,
\upgamma(1)=y
,

(1)

\length(\upgamma)\leq cd(x,y)
and

(2)

\dist(\upgamma(t),X\setminus U)\geq c^{-1}\dist(\upgamma(t),\{x,y\})
for all
t\in[0,1]
.

If for each pair of points
x,y\in U
, there is a curve
\upgamma
that satisfies condition (1), then we say that
U
is
c
-quasiconvex
.

The next two lemmas allow us to assume for the rest of the paper that
X
is geodesic, since any quasiconvex metric space is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to a geodesic metric space through the identity map.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
Che99
, Theorem 17.1]
)
.

If
(X,d,\mu)
is a complete
(C_{0},Q)
-regular metric measure space and satisfies a
(p,C)
-Poincaré inequality, then there is a constant
c(C_{0},Q,p,C)\geq 1
such that
X
is
c
-quasiconvex.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
HKST15
, Lemma 8.3.18]
)
.

Let
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
be two bi-Lipschitz equivalent metrics on
X
.
If
\mu
is Ahlfors regular with respect to
d_{1}
, then it is Ahlfors regular with respect to
d_{2}
, quantitatively.
Furthermore, if
(X,d_{1},\mu)
supports a
p
-Poincaré inequality, then
(X,d_{2},\mu)
supports a
p
-Poincaré inequality, quantitatively.

2.7.
Bi-Lipschitz approximation of curves and avoiding small sets.

We end this section with a lemma that allows us—inside a good metric measure space—to approximate any curve by a bi-Lipschitz arc which also avoids a “small set” quantitatively.

Lemma 2.8
.

Given
C_{0},C,C_{1}>1
,
Q>2
,
p\in[1,Q-1)
,
c\in(0,1)
,
\lambda\in(0,c)
, and
\alpha\in(0,Q-p)
, there exist constants
L(C_{0},Q,p,C,C_{1},\alpha,\lambda)>1
and
\beta(C_{0},Q,p,C,C_{1},\alpha,c)\in(0,c)
with the following property.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a complete,
(C_{0},Q)
-regular metric measure space supporting a
(p,C)
-Poincaré inequality, let
Y\subset X
be
(C_{1},\alpha)
-HTB, and let
\sigma:[0,1]\to X
be a curve satisfying
d(\sigma(0),\sigma(1))\geq c\diam\sigma([0,1])
and
\dist(\{\sigma(0),\sigma(1)\},Y)\geq c\diam\sigma([0,1])
. There exists an
(L,\diam{\sigma([0,1])})
-quasisimilarity
\upgamma:[0,1]\to X
such that
\upgamma(0)=\sigma(0)
,
\upgamma(1)=\sigma(1)
,

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\dist(\upgamma(t),\sigma([0,1]))\leq 4\lambda\diam{\sigma([0,1])},\quad\text{and}\quad\inf_{t\in[0,1]}\dist(\upgamma(t),Y)\geq\beta\lambda\diam{\sigma([0,1])}.

Proof.

Rescaling the metric, we may assume for simplicity that
\diam{\sigma([0,1])}=1
.

Let
V
be a maximal
\lambda
-separated subset of
\sigma
that contains
\sigma(0)
and
\sigma(1)
. Define a graph
G=(V,E)
with vertex set
V
and edges
E=\{\{v,v^{\prime}\}:v,v^{\prime}\in V,d(v,v^{\prime})\leq\lambda\}
. We claim that
G
is connected, i.e., for any
v,v^{\prime}\in V
, there exist
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\in V
such that
v_{1}=v
,
v_{n}=v^{\prime}
, and
\{v_{i-1},v_{i}\}\in E
for all
i\in\{2,\dots,n\}
. Towards a contradiction, suppose that
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
is a nonempty connected component of
G
with
V^{\prime}\subsetneq V
. Then,
\dist(V^{\prime},V\setminus V^{\prime})>\lambda
. But then
\sigma
is contained in the union of the disjoint closed sets
G_{1}=\overline{N}(V^{\prime},\lambda/2)
and
G_{2}=\overline{N}(V\setminus V^{\prime},\lambda/2)
, which contradicts the fact that
\sigma
is connected.

Since
G
is connected, there is a shortest path of vertices
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
in
G
with
v_{1}=\sigma(0)
and
v_{n}=\sigma(1)
.
Since
B(v_{i},2^{-1}\lambda)
are disjoint and contained in
B(\sigma(0),1)
, by the
(C_{0},Q)
-regularity of
X
,

2^{-Q}C_{0}^{-1}\lambda^{Q}n\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu(B(v_{i},2^{-1}\lambda))\leq\mu(B(\sigma(0),1))\leq C_{0},

so
n\leq 2^{Q}C_{0}^{2}\lambda^{-Q}
. By Lemma
2.2
,
Y
is
p_{0}
-porous for some
p_{0}(C_{0},Q,C_{1},\alpha)\geq 1
and for each
i\in\{2,\dots,n-1\}
, there exists
v_{i}^{\prime}\in B(v_{i},3^{-1}\lambda)
such that
B(v_{i}^{\prime},(3p_{0})^{-1}\lambda)\subset B(v_{i},3^{-1}\lambda)\setminus Y
.
Note that since
\dist(\{\sigma(0),\sigma(1)\},Y)\geq c
, it suffices to choose
v_{1}^{\prime}=v_{1}=\sigma(0)
and
v_{n}^{\prime}=v_{n}=\sigma(1)
.

Fix
i\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
. By
[
HVZ24
, Corollary 3.4]
, there is a curve
S_{i}
lying in
B(v_{i}^{\prime},2\lambda)
joining
v_{i}^{\prime}
and
v_{i+1}^{\prime}
such that
\length(S_{i})\leq\ell\lambda
and for each
z\in S_{i}
,
\dist(z,Y)\geq\beta^{\prime}\lambda
for some
\ell(C_{0},Q,p,C,c)\geq 1
and
\beta^{\prime}(C_{0},Q,p,C,C_{1},\alpha,c)\in(0,1/2)
. Let
S:[0,1]\to X
denote the concatenation of curves
S_{i}
. Then
S
satisfies
S(0)=\sigma(0)
,
S(1)=\sigma(1)
,

\length(S)\leq(n-1)\ell\lambda\leq 2^{Q}C_{0}^{2}\ell\lambda^{1-Q},

and
S
stays at least a distance
\beta^{\prime}\lambda
from
Y
. By
[
HVZ24
, Proposition 1.5]
there exists a constant
L(C,C_{0},Q,p,C_{1},\alpha,\lambda)\geq 1
and there exists an
L
-bi-Lipschitz curve
\upgamma:[0,1]\to X
, joining
S(0)
to
S(1)
and satisfying

\displaystyle\dist_{H}(\upgamma([0,1]),S([0,1]))<\beta^{\prime}\lambda^{Q}(2^{Q+1}C_{0}^{2}\ell)^{-1}\diam{S([0,1])}

\displaystyle\leq\beta^{\prime}\lambda^{Q}(2^{Q+1}C_{0}^{2}\ell)^{-1}\length(S)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-1}\beta^{\prime}\lambda.

The curve
\upgamma
has length at most
L
and

\dist(\upgamma([0,1]),Y)\geq\dist(S([0,1]),Y)-\dist_{H}(\upgamma([0,1]),S([0,1]))\geq 2^{-1}\beta^{\prime}\lambda.

Lastly, for each
t\in[0,1]
,

\dist(\upgamma(t),\sigma([0,1]))\leq\dist(\upgamma(t),S([0,1]))+\dist(S([0,1]),\sigma([0,1]))\leq 2^{-1}\beta^{\prime}\lambda+3\lambda\leq 4\lambda.\qed

3.
A decomposition of compact totally disconnected sets

One natural property of compact, totally disconnected sets in Euclidean spaces is that they admit a
defining sequence
, which is, roughly speaking, a way to coherently encapsulate clusters of the set; see for example Armentrout’s work in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

[
Arm66
]
. The main result of this section is Proposition
3.1
, which shows that the same idea works in metric spaces with nice geometry.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a complete, fully connected, unbounded,
(C_{0},Q)
-regular, geodesic metric measure space supporting a
(Q,C)
-Poincaré inequality. Let
E
be a compact, totally disconnected subset of
X
. There exists a dictionary
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathbb{N}^{*}
, and there exists a collection
\{U_{w}:w\in\mathcal{W}\}
of bounded full sets such that the following conditions hold.

(D1)

The empty word
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{W}
. If
w\in\mathcal{W}\setminus\{\varepsilon\}
, then
w^{\uparrow}\in\mathcal{W}
. For each
w\in\mathcal{W}
, there exists
n_{w}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
wi\in\mathcal{W}
if and only if
i\in\{1,\dots,n_{w}\}
.

(D2)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
E\subset\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathcal{W}\\
|w|=n\end{subarray}}U_{w}
. Conversely, if
w\in\mathcal{W}
, then
U_{w}\cap E\neq\varnothing
.

(D3)

If
wi,wj\in\mathcal{W}
with
i\neq j
, then
U_{wi}\cap U_{wj}=\varnothing
.

(D4)

If
w\in\mathcal{W}\setminus\{\varepsilon\}
, then
U_{w}\subset U_{w^{\uparrow}}
,
\diam U_{w}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\diam U_{w^{\uparrow}}
, and
\dist(U_{w},\partial U_{w^{\uparrow}})>0
.

(D5)

If
w\in\mathcal{W}
, then
\dist(\partial U_{w},E)>0
.

(D6)

If
w\in\mathcal{W}
, then the set

\hat{U}_{w}:=\overline{U_{w}}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n_{w}}U_{wi}

is path connected.

Moreover, if
E
is
c
-uniformly disconnected for some
c>1
, then there exists
\xi(C_{0},Q,C,c)\geq 1
such that for all
\delta\in(0,(2\xi)^{-1})
, there exists
N(C_{0},\delta,Q)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{W}\subset\{1,\dots,N\}^{*}
and the following quantitative versions of (D4) and (D5) hold.

(D4’)

If
w\in\mathcal{W}\setminus\{\varepsilon\}
, then
U_{w}\subset U_{w^{\uparrow}}
,

(3.1)

\delta\diam U_{w^{\uparrow}}\leq\diam U_{w}\leq\xi\delta\diam U_{w^{\uparrow}},

and

(3.2)

\dist(U_{w},\partial U_{w^{\uparrow}})\geq\xi^{-1}\diam U_{w}.

(D5’)

If
w\in\mathcal{W}
, then
\dist(\partial U_{w},E)\geq\xi^{-1}\diam U_{w}
.

For the rest of this section, we fix a complete, fully connected, unbounded,
(C_{0},Q)
-regular, geodesic metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
supporting a
(Q,C)
-Poincaré inequality.

Lemma 3.2
.

Given
r>0
there exists
c_{0}(C_{0},Q,r)\geq 1
with the following property. If
U
is a bounded, path connected subset of
X
and
x,y\in U
, then there is a curve
\upgamma
lying in
N(U,r\diam U)
joining
x
with
y
such that
\length(\upgamma)\leq c_{0}d(x,y)
.

Proof.

Rescaling the metric, we may assume that
\diam U=1
. Set
N_{r}=N(U,r)
.

If
d(x,y)<r/6
, then we can join
x
and
y
by a geodesic
\upgamma
, and this satisfies, for each
z\in\upgamma
,

\dist(z,X\setminus N_{r})\geq\dist(\{x,y\},X\setminus N_{r})-\tfrac{1}{2}\length(\upgamma)\geq\tfrac{11}{12}r

and
\dist(z,\{x,y\})\leq\length(\upgamma)=d(x,y)<r/6
.

If
d(x,y)\geq r/6
, then let
V
be a
(r/6)
-net of
U
containing
x
and
y
. For each
u,v\in V
, fix a geodesic
\upgamma_{u,v}=\upgamma_{v,u}
joining them and define

G=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\in V\\
d(u,v)\leq r\end{subarray}}\upgamma_{u,v}.

We show that the set
G
is connected. Towards a contradiction, suppose that
Z
is a nonempty path connected component of
G
with
G\setminus Z\neq\varnothing
. Let
W=N(Z,2r)
. Note that
\partial W
cannot be empty, since otherwise
W
and
X\setminus W
separate
X
. Thus, there is a point
\xi\in X
with
\dist(\xi,Z)=2r
. As
V
is an
r
-net of
X
, there is a point
v\in V
such that
d(\xi,v)\leq r
. Necessarily
v\in G\setminus U
, since
\dist(v,Z)\geq\dist(\xi,Z)-d(\xi,v)\geq r
. We claim that there is a point
u\in V\cap Z
such that
d(u,\xi)\leq 5r
. To see this, choose
\zeta\in U
such that
d(\xi,\zeta)=\dist(\xi,Z)=2r
. If
\zeta\in V
, then taking
u=\zeta
, we have
d(u,\xi)=2r
. If
\zeta\notin V
, then
\zeta
lies on a geodesic joining two points
v_{1},v_{2}\in V
for which
d(v_{1},v_{2})\leq r
. Then either
d(\zeta,v_{1})\leq 3r
or
d(\zeta,v_{2})\leq 3r
, say
d(\zeta,v_{1})\leq 3r
. Taking
u=v_{1}
,
d(u,\xi)\leq d(u,\zeta)+d(\zeta,\xi)\leq 5r
. Finally,
d(u,v)\leq d(u,\xi)+d(\xi,v)\leq 6r
, so in fact
\upgamma_{u,v}
is a path in
G
joining
Z
and
G\setminus Z
, a contradiction, which proves that
G
is connected.

Since
G
is connected there is a finite sequence
v_{0},\dots,v_{k}
of points in
V
with
v_{0}=x
,
v_{k}=y
,
d(v_{i-1},v_{i})\leq r
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, and
v_{i}\neq v_{j}
for distinct
i,j\in\{0,\dots,k\}
. Let
\upgamma
be the concatenation of the geodesic edges joining these vertices. Since
V
is
(r/6)
-separated and contained in
U
, by
Q
-regularity of
X
,

C_{0}^{-1}(r/12)^{Q}\card V\leq\sum_{v\in V}\mu(B(v,r/12))\leq\mu(B(x,1))\leq C_{0},

so
\card V\leq 12^{Q}C_{0}^{2}r^{-Q}
.
Thus

\length(\upgamma)\leq kr\leq r\card V\leq 12^{Q}C_{0}^{2}r^{1-Q}\leq 12^{Q+1}C_{0}^{2}r^{-Q}d(x,y).\qed

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists
K(C_{0},Q,C)\geq 1
such that if
U
is a bounded and connected subset of
X
, then

(1)

X\setminus U
has exactly one component
V
with
\diam V\geq K\diam U
, and

(2)

\diam\partial U\leq\diam U\leq K\diam\partial U
.

Proof.

Let
C_{1}(C_{0},Q)\geq 1
be the constant from Lemma
2.4
, and let
\Phi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be the
Q
-Loewner function of
X
given by Lemma
2.5
, which depends only on
C_{0}
,
Q
, and
C
.
Set

K=\exp\left(\left(C_{1}^{-1}\Phi(4)\right)^{1/(1-Q)}\right).

For (a), we first claim that there exists at least one component of
X\setminus U
of diameter at least
K\diam{U}
. Indeed, if all components of
X\setminus U
had diameter at most
K\diam{U}
, then it can be easily seen that
\diam{X}\leq(2K+1)\diam{U}<\infty
which is false since
X
is unbounded.

Now suppose for a contradiction that
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are two different components of
X\setminus U
with
\diam V_{i}\geq K\diam U
for
i=1,2
. Fix
u\in U
and note that
U\subset\overline{B}(u,\diam U)
. There also exist points
v_{i}\in V_{i}
for
i=1,2
such that
d(u,v_{i})=K\diam U
. Let
\sigma_{i}
be a curve in
V_{i}
joining
v_{i}
to
\partial U
for
i=1,2
. Let
x_{i}
be the first point that
\sigma_{i}
intersects
\overline{B}(u,\frac{1}{2}K\diam U)
for
i=1,2
. Denote by
\upgamma_{i}
a subcurve of
\sigma_{i}
joining
v_{i}
to
x_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Note that
\Delta(\upgamma_{1},\upgamma_{2})\leq 2
, so
\Mod_{Q}(\upgamma_{1},\upgamma_{2})\geq\Phi(\Delta(\upgamma_{1},\upgamma_{2}))\geq\Phi(2)
. On the other hand, since
\upgamma_{1}
and
\upgamma_{2}
lie in different components of
X\setminus U
, any curve joining them must pass through
U
, and hence also
\overline{B}(u,\diam U)
. Thus
\Mod_{Q}(\upgamma_{1},\upgamma_{2})\leq\Mod_{Q}(\overline{B}(u,\diam U),\upgamma_{1})
. Let
\Gamma
be the collection of curves joining
X\setminus B(u,2K\diam U)
to
\overline{B}(u,\diam U)
. By Lemma
2.4
,
\Mod_{Q}(\Gamma)\leq C_{1}(\log(2K))^{1-Q}
. Clearly
\Mod_{Q}(\overline{B}(u,\diam U),\upgamma_{1})\leq\Mod_{Q}(\Gamma)
. By choice of
K
,

\Phi(2)\leq\Mod_{Q}(\upgamma_{1},\upgamma_{2})\leq\Mod_{Q}(\overline{B}(u,\diam U),\upgamma_{1})\leq\Mod_{Q}(\Gamma)\leq C_{1}(\log(2K))^{1-Q}<\Phi(4),

which is a contradiction since
\Phi
is decreasing.

to be the concatenation of these curves, which joins
x
to
y
.
By Proposition
3.1
(D4’) and Lemma
4.1
(E1),

\displaystyle\length(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{|w|-|u|}\length(\upgamma_{i})

\displaystyle\lesssim_{c_{0}}d(x,z_{1})+d(z_{|w|-|u|-1},y)+\sum_{i=2}^{|w|-|u|-1}d(z_{i-1},z_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\diam U_{w}+\diam U_{u}+\sum_{i=2}^{|w|-|u|-1}\diam U_{w_{i}}

\displaystyle<2\diam U_{u}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{6}\xi^{5}d(x,y).

Let now
z\in\Gamma
and suppose
z\in\upgamma_{i}
for some
i\in\{2,\ldots,|w|-|u|-1\}
(the cases where
z\in\upgamma_{1}
or
z\in\upgamma_{|w|-|u|}
are similar).
Since
\upgamma_{i}
lies in
N(\hat{U}_{w_{i}},(2\xi)^{-3}\diam U_{w_{i}})
, by Proposition
3.1
(D4’,D5’) and Lemma
4.1
(E1,E2), for any
v_{0}\in\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
such that
v_{0}^{\uparrow}=w_{i}
,

\displaystyle\dist(z,E)

\displaystyle\geq\dist(\partial U_{v_{0}},E)-(2\xi)^{-3}\diam U_{w_{i}}

\displaystyle\geq\xi^{-1}\diam U_{v_{0}}-(2\xi)^{-3}\diam U_{w_{i}}

\displaystyle\geq(2\xi)^{-3}\diam U_{w_{i}},

while

\dist(z,\{x,y\})\leq\dist(\upgamma_{i},\{x,y\})+\length(\upgamma_{i})\leq\diam U_{w_{i}}+c_{0}d(z_{i-1},z_{i})\leq(1+c_{0})\diam U_{w_{i}}.

Case 4.
Suppose that
d(x,y)\geq(2\xi)^{-5}\diam U_{u}
and
u\neq v
.
The proof is a two-sided argument similar to that in Case 3.
Let
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
such that
u_{1}^{\uparrow}=u_{2}^{\uparrow}=u
and
x\in U_{u_{1}}
and
y\in U_{u_{2}}
.
Fix
x^{\prime}\in\partial U_{u_{1}}
and
y^{\prime}\in\partial U_{u_{2}}
.
Let
\upgamma_{1}
be the curve from Case 3 joining
x
to
x^{\prime}
, let
\upgamma_{2}
be the curve from Case 3 joining
y^{\prime}
to
y
, and let
\upgamma_{0}
be the curve in
N(\hat{U}_{u},(2\xi)^{-3}\diam U_{u})
from Case 2 joining
x^{\prime}
to
y^{\prime}
.
Let
\Gamma
be the concatenation of the curves
\upgamma_{0}
,
\upgamma_{1}
, and
\upgamma_{2}
.
We have

\length(\Gamma)=\length(\upgamma_{0})+\length(\upgamma_{1})+\length(\upgamma_{2})\lesssim_{c_{0},\xi}\diam U_{u}\lesssim_{\xi}d(x,y).

For a point
z\in\Gamma
, we have
\dist(z,\{x,y\})\lesssim_{c_{0},\xi}\dist(z,E)
from Cases 2 and 3.
∎

Preparation for the proofs of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3

Fix, for the rest of the paper, a metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
which is complete, fully connected,
(C_{0},Q)
-regular, and supports a
(Q-1,C)
-Poincaré inequality for some
C_{0},C>1
, and

2.6
and

we may assume that
(X,d,\mu)
is geodesic. Fix also a totally disconnected set

and a number

. Let
\xi\geq 1
be the constant from Proposition
3.1
associated to the number
c
. Set
\delta=(4\xi)^{-1}
, and let

,
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathbb{N}^{*}
, and

be as in Proposition

To simplify the exposition, we make the assumption that the only word in

5.1.
Basic notation

We establish some notation which we use throughout the rest of the paper.

•

For each

, by Proposition

, there exists

such that

if and only if

•

For each

, define

.

•

For each

, define

\displaystyle\epsilon_{w}=(4\diam{U_{w}})^{-1}\min\Bigg\{\dist(U_{w},\partial U_{w^{\uparrow}}),

\displaystyle\min_{i=1,\dots,n_{w}}\dist(\partial U_{w},U_{wi}),

\displaystyle\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\dots,n_{w}\\
j=1,\dots,n_{wi}\end{subarray}}\dist(\partial U_{wi},U_{wij}),\min_{i=1,\ldots,n_{w}}\diam U_{wi}\Bigg\}

with the understanding that
\dist(\partial U_{w},\partial U_{w^{\uparrow}})
is dropped if

. If
E
is
c
-uniformly disconnected, then we set
\epsilon_{w}=(4\xi)^{-3}
.

•

For each

, define
\mathcal{N}_{w}=N(\hat{U}_{w},\epsilon_{w}\diam{U_{w}})
.

•

For each
w\in\mathcal{W}\setminus\{\varepsilon\}
, define

\lambda_{w}=\min\left\{\max_{i,j=1,\dots,n_{w}}\frac{\diam{U_{wi}}}{\diam{U_{wj}}},\max_{i=1,\dots,n_{w}}\frac{\diam{U_{wi}}}{\dist(\partial U_{wi},E)}\right\}.

If
E
is
c
-uniformly disconnected, then set
\lambda_{w}=\xi
.

Note that if
E
is
c
-uniformly disconnected, and if
w\neq\varepsilon
, then a simple computation involving (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) shows that
\epsilon_{w}\geq(4\xi)^{-3}
and
\lambda_{w}\geq\xi
, which justifies the choices
\epsilon_{w}=(4\xi)^{-3}
and
\lambda_{w}=\xi
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
w,u\in\mathcal{W}
be two distinct words that do not have a common parent and one is not the parent of the other. Then

\dist(\mathcal{N}_{w},\mathcal{N}_{u})\geq\epsilon_{w}\diam{U_{w}}+\epsilon_{u}\diam{U_{u}}.

Proof.

The assumptions on
w,u
imply that

\dist(\hat{U}_{w},\hat{U}_{u})\geq\dist\left(\hat{U}_{w},\partial U_{w^{\uparrow}}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{n_{w}}\bigcup_{j=1}^{n_{wi}}U_{wij}\right)\geq 4\epsilon_{w}\diam{U_{w}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\dist(\mathcal{N}_{w},\mathcal{N}_{u})

\displaystyle\geq\dist(\hat{U}_{w},\hat{U}_{u})-\epsilon_{w}\diam{U_{w}}-\epsilon_{u}\diam{U_{u}}\geq\epsilon_{w}\diam{U_{w}}+\epsilon_{u}\diam{U_{u}}.\qed

5.2.
Entries and exits

We define here for each
w\in\mathcal{W}
an “entry” to
U_{w}
and an “exit”. The following lemma allows us to do so.

Lemma 5.2
.

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda>1
, there is a constant
\rho(n,C_{0},Q,C,\lambda)\in(0,1)
satisfying the following property. Let
W_{1},\dots,W_{n}\subset X
be mutually disjoint domains such that for each distinct
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
\diam{W_{i}}\leq\lambda\diam{W_{j}}
and for each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
there exists a ball
B_{i}\subset W_{i}
of radius
\lambda^{-1}\diam{W_{i}}
. Then for each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
there exists
x_{i}\in\partial W_{i}
such that if
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
are distinct, then
d(x_{i},x_{j})\geq\rho\diam{W_{i}}
.

Proof.

The construction of constant
\rho
and points
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
is done in an inductive fashion.

Fix
x_{1}\in\partial W_{1}
and let
\rho_{1}=1/2
. Suppose that for some
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
, we have defined
\rho_{k}\in(0,1)
and points
x_{i}\in\partial W_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
such that
d(x_{i},x_{j})\geq\rho_{k}\diam{W_{i}}
for distinct
i,j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. By the assumptions of the lemma, there exists
u\in W_{k+1}
such that
B(u,\lambda^{-1}\diam{W}_{k+1})\subset W_{k+1}
. Fix
v\in X
such that
\dist(v,W_{k+1})=\diam{W}_{k+1}
and set
B=\overline{B}(u,(3\lambda)^{-1}\diam{W_{k+1}})
and
B^{\prime}=\overline{B}(v,(3\lambda)^{-1}\diam{W_{k+1}})
. Note that

\Delta(B,B^{\prime})\leq\frac{d(u,v)}{\diam{B}}\leq\frac{\diam{W_{k+1}}+\dist(v,W_{k+1})}{\diam{B}}\leq 6\lambda,

so by Lemma
2.5
we have
\Mod_{Q}(B,B^{\prime})\geq\Phi(6\lambda)
where
\Phi
is a decreasing homeomorphism of
(0,\infty)
depending only on
C_{0},Q
, and
C
.

Let
C_{1}(C_{0},Q)\geq 1
be the constant from Lemma
2.4
. Let
a_{k+1}\in(0,(3\lambda)^{-1})
so that

C_{1}\left(\log\left(\frac{\lambda^{-1}}{3a_{k+1}}\right)\right)^{1-Q}\leq\frac{\Phi(6\lambda)}{2k}.

For
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
define
B_{i}=\overline{B}(x_{i},a_{k+1}\diam{W_{k+1}})
and
B_{i}^{\prime}=B(x_{i},(3\lambda)^{-1}\diam{W_{k+1}})
. Let
\Gamma_{0}
denote the family of curves joining
B
and
B^{\prime}
, and for each
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, let
\Gamma_{i}
denote the family of curves in
\Gamma_{0}
that intersect
B_{i}
. Note that for each
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
,

d(x_{i},u)\geq\lambda^{-1}\diam{W_{k+1}}
.
Hence each curve
\upgamma\in\Gamma_{i}
has a subcurve which joins
B_{i}
and
X\setminus B_{i}^{\prime}
, so by Lemma
2.4
,

\Mod_{Q}(\Gamma_{i})\leq\Mod_{Q}(B_{i},X\setminus B_{i}^{\prime})\leq C_{1}\left(\log\left(\frac{\lambda^{-1}}{3a_{k+1}}\right)\right)^{1-Q}\leq\frac{\Phi(6\lambda)}{2k}.

Therefore,

\Mod_{Q}(\Gamma_{0}\setminus(\Gamma_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\Gamma_{k}))\geq\Mod_{Q}(\Gamma_{0})-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Mod_{Q}(\Gamma_{i})\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\Phi(6\lambda).

Namely,
\Mod_{Q}(\Gamma_{0}\setminus(\Gamma_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\Gamma_{k}))>0
, so there is a curve
\upgamma
which joins
B
and
B^{\prime}
and does not intersect
B_{i}
for any
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. Since
B\subset W_{k+1}
and
B^{\prime}\cap W_{k+1}=\varnothing
, there exists a point
x_{k+1}
where the curve
\upgamma
intersects
\partial W_{k+1}
. Then
d(x_{k+1},x_{i})\geq a_{k+1}\diam{W_{k+1}}
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. To complete the inductive step, set
\rho_{k+1}=\min\{\rho_{k},\lambda^{-1}a_{k+1}\}
and note that
d(x_{i},x_{j})\geq\rho_{k+1}\diam{W_{i}}
for all distinct
i,j\in\{1,\dots,k+1\}
.

Proceeding inductively, we find
\rho_{n}(C_{0},Q,C,\lambda,n)\in(0,1)
and points
x_{i}\in\partial W_{i}
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
d(x_{i},x_{j})\geq\rho_{n}\diam{W_{i}}
when
i\neq j
.
∎

If
x\in U_{wij}
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,n_{w}\}
and
j\in\{1,\dots,n_{wi}\}
, then
B(x,\lambda_{w}\diam{U_{wi}})\subset U_{wi}
. Therefore, for each
w\in\mathcal{W}
, we can apply Lemma
5.2
with
\lambda=\lambda_{w}
and
W_{i}=U_{wi}
,
i=1,\dots,n_{w}
, and we obtain a constant
\rho_{w}\in(0,1)

and points
x_{wi}\in\partial U_{wi}
,
i\in\{1,\dots,n_{w}\}
, such that

d(x_{wi},x_{wj})\geq\rho_{w}\max\{\diam U_{wi},\diam{U_{wj}}\},\qquad\text{for distinct $i,j\in\{1,\dots,n_{w}\}$.}

For each
i\in\{1,\dots,n_{w}\}
, fix a point
y_{wi}\in\partial U_{wi}
such that
d(x_{wi},y_{wi})=\frac{1}{3}\rho_{w}\diam U_{wi}
and note that this implies
d(x_{wi},y_{wj})\geq\frac{1}{3}\rho_{w}\diam U_{wi}
and
d(y_{wi},y_{wj})\geq\frac{1}{3}\rho_{w}\diam U_{wi}
for distinct
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n_{w}\}
.

Remark 5.3
.

If
E
is
c
-uniformly disconnected, then
\rho_{w}
is independent of

and only depends on
C_{0}
,
Q
,
C
, and

.

Lastly, for each
w\in\mathcal{W}
and

, define

X_{wi}=B(x_{wi},\tfrac{1}{12}\epsilon_{wi}\rho_{w}\diam U_{wi})\qquad\text{and}\qquad Y_{wi}=B(y_{wi},\tfrac{1}{12}\epsilon_{wi}\rho_{w}\diam U_{wi})

We view
X_{wi}
as the “entry” of the set
U_{wi}
and we view
Y_{wi}
as the “exit” of the set
U_{wi}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
w,u\in\mathcal{W}
,
i\in\{1,\dots,n_{w}\}
, and
j\in\{1,\dots,n_{u}\}
. Then

(5.1)

\dist(X_{wi},Y_{uj})\geq\tfrac{1}{12}(\rho_{w}\epsilon_{wi}\diam{U_{wi}}+\rho_{u}\epsilon_{uj}\diam{U_{uj}}).

Moreover, if
wi\neq uj
, then

(5.2)

\min\{\dist(X_{wi},X_{uj}),\dist(Y_{wi},Y_{uj})\}\geq\tfrac{1}{12}(\rho_{w}\epsilon_{wi}\diam{U_{wi}}+\rho_{u}\epsilon_{uj}\diam{U_{uj}}).

Proof.

We distinguish two cases. If
w\neq u
, then by Lemma
5.1
and the fact that
\rho_{w},\rho_{u}\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\dist(X_{wi},X_{uj})

\displaystyle\geq d(x_{wi},x_{uj})-\tfrac{1}{12}\left(\epsilon_{wi}\rho_{w}\diam{U_{wi}}+\epsilon_{uj}\rho_{u}\diam{U_{uj}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\dist(\partial U_{wi},\partial U_{uj})-\tfrac{1}{12}\left(\epsilon_{wi}\rho_{w}\diam{U_{wi}}+\epsilon_{uj}\rho_{u}\diam{U_{uj}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{1}{2}(\epsilon_{wi}\diam{U_{wi}}+\epsilon_{uj}\diam{U_{uj}}).

The same argument works for
\dist(Y_{wi},Y_{uj})
and for
\dist(X_{wi},Y_{uj})
.

If
w=u
, then recalling that
\epsilon_{wi},\epsilon_{wj}\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\dist(X_{wi},X_{wj})

\displaystyle\geq d(x_{wi},x_{wj})-\tfrac{1}{12}\left(\epsilon_{wi}\rho_{w}\diam{U_{wi}}+\epsilon_{wj}\rho_{w}\diam{U_{wj}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\rho_{w}\diam{U_{wi}}+\rho_{w}\diam{U_{wj}}\right)-\tfrac{1}{12}\left(\epsilon_{wi}\rho_{w}\diam{U_{wi}}+\epsilon_{wj}\rho_{w}\diam{U_{wj}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{1}{4}\left(\rho_{w}\diam{U_{wi}}+\rho_{w}\diam{U_{w}}\right).

A similar argument works for
\dist(Y_{wi},Y_{uj})
and for
\dist(X_{wi},Y_{uj})
.
∎

5.3.

In the next lemma we define a collection of closed intervals
\{J_{w}:w\in\mathcal{W}\setminus\{\varepsilon\}\}

which form a defining sequence for some compact totally disconnected subset of
[0,1]
.
Recall our assumption that the only word in
\mathcal{W}
of length one is 1.

Lemma 5.5
.

There exists a family of closed intervals
\{J_{w}:w\in\mathcal{W}\setminus\{\varepsilon\}\}
which satisfy the following properties.

(1)

We have
J_{1}=[0,1]
.

(2)

For each
w\in\mathcal{W}\setminus\{\varepsilon\}
and

and

.

For each

and

\dist(J_{wi},J_{w(i+1)})=(2n_{w}+1)^{-1}|J_{w}|

lying to the left of the left endpoint of
J_{w(i+1)}
.

(4)

For each
w\in\mathcal{W}\setminus\{\varepsilon\}
and

\dist(J_{wi},\mathbb{R}\setminus J_{w})=(2n_{w}+1)^{-1}|J_{w}|

Set
J_{1}=[0,1]
. Assume now that for some
w\in\mathcal{W}\setminus\{\varepsilon\}
we have defined a nondegenerate closed interval

define

A simple induction argument shows now that the intervals
\{J_{w}:w\in\mathcal{W}\}
satisfy (a)–(d).
∎

For a given

, the set

is a union of

open intervals. Denote the closures of these intervals by

\{I_{w,i}:i\in\{0,\dots,n_{w}\}\},

ordered so that the right endpoint of
I_{w,i}
lies to the left of the left endpoint of
I_{w,i+1}
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,n_{w}-1\}
.

Lemma 5.6
.

The collection
\{I_{w,i}:w\in\mathcal{W}\setminus\{\varepsilon\},i\in\{0,\dots,n_{w}\}\}
satisfies the following properties.

(1)

For each
w\in\mathcal{W}\setminus\{\varepsilon\}
and
i\in\{0,\dots,n_{w}\}
,
I_{w,i}\subset J_{w}
and
|I_{w,i}|=(2n_{w}+1)^{-1}|J_{w}|
.

(2)

For each

and

,
\dist(I_{w,i},I_{w,i+1})=(2n_{w}+1)^{-1}|J_{w}|
with the right endpoint of
I_{w,i}
lying to the left of the left endpoint of
I_{w,i+1}
.

(3)

If

is adjacent to

, then either

or

. In either case,

The first two claims are immediate. For the third, assume first that

. But then,

and

The construction of embeddings

Recall that when we refer to

and

s

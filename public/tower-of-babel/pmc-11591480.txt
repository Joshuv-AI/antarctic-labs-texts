Thioctic acid shield against lipopolysaccharide depression and endoplasmic reticulum stress: GR7M/Homer/ATF6 signaling
Authors: Mai O. Kadry, Rehab M. Abdel-Megeed
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Aim: Endoplasmic reticulum stress triggers neuronal unfolded protein accumulation, contributing to the pathophysiology of depression psychiatric illnesses.Materials & methods: The brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), metabotropic glutamate receptor type 7 (GRM7), phosphoinisitol kinase-3 (PIK3), serine/threonine kinase 1 (AKT) and Homer1 may afford an important signaling pathways in LPS induced depression rat model. Herein, thioctic acid, Burdock and propolis therapeutic index on lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced depression was monitored. BDNF/PI3K/GR7M/ATF6/CHOP/XBP/AKT/Homer-1 signaling pathways also opened a novel avenue in the integration of synaptic neurotransmission.Results: The aforementioned treatments elevated GR7M/BDNF/GABA gene expression meanwhile decreased reactive oxygen species generation (Malondialdehyde/glutathione reductase/Total antioxidant capacity) and reduced the expression of ATF6/CHOP/PI3K/AKT/XBP/Homer-1 post LPS elevation.Conclusion: Thioctic acid, propolis and Burdock are prospective therapeutic agents via GRM7/BDNF/AKT/PI3K/ATF6/XBP/homer -1 signaling pathways in LPS-induced depression.

Introduction

Depression (DP) is a common psychiatric illness characterized by loss of motivation, low mood and changes in autonomic function, such as sleep disturbance, food and energy levels deviation. Depression influences various patients’ lives aspects, including family connections, social contact and work efficiency, more than 400 million people worldwide suffer from depression on a daily basis and can commit suicide [1].

Endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress triggers the unfolded protein response (UPR), which play a vital role in the pathophysiology of psychiatric illnesses as depression. In human and animal models, emerging evidence shows an intriguing relationship between ER stress (ERS) and depression. The ER is an important subcellular organelle for protein synthesis, folding, modification and transport, which is an essential mechanism for neuronal cells [2]. ERS is caused by disruptions in ER homeostasis, and ERS aids in the restoration of normal ER function by restoring the ER’s protein-folding capability. This biological defense mechanism is vital for illness prevention. Excessive or prolonged ERS, on the other hand, leads to cell death and apoptosis. Hippocampus, amygdala, striatum and other neuronal areas damage will be involved in depression development [3].

The ER is a vital subcellular organelle involved in protein synthesis, post-translational modifications, and correcting protein folding, as well as calcium homeostasis. Various physiological conditions, such as depression, oxidative stress, hypoglycemia, hypoxia and calcium deficiency, can disrupt the protein folding process, resulting in accumulation of unfolded and misfolded proteins in the ER, a condition known as ERS. The UPR is activated during ERS to reduce the accumulation of unfolded proteins. However, when ER functions are significantly compromised, apoptotic signals are activated [4].

Alteration in AKT and PI3K signaling may contribute to specific incidence of depression as both PI3K and AKT can activate immune cell by controlling the innate inflammatory cytokines [5].

Modulation of PI3K/AKT/GRM7/Homer1 signaling pathways is the target of the current study versus LPS induced depression rat model.

AKT activation results in GSK3 phosphorylation, which control transcription factors as nuclear factor of activated T-cells, catenin, nuclear factor kappa B (NFκB) and cAMP-response element binding protein (CREB) [6]. Furthermore, proinflammatory cytokines TNF-α, INF-1 and IL-12 are triggered via PI3K activation thus influencing the synaptic plasticity, inflammation and metabolism of neurotransmitters that characterize depression [7].

LPS was extensively used in experimental rat model to aggressively generate inflammation leading to the release of proinflammatory cytokines, which in turn cause neuroinflammation and depression. LPS has been demonstrated to worsen inflammatory reactions by increasing reactive oxygen species (ROS) and lowering Nrf2 and GSH. In the LPS-induced depression model, the Blood brain barrier’s (BBB) inflammation causes an unfavorable increase in its permeability and is also associated by neuronal disruption [8].

Nutraceuticals as thioctic acid; (α-lipoic acid) (ALA), Burdock and propolis have paved the way for depression treatment. ALA is an antioxidant present in nutrients and produced naturally in the body. It is utilized to degrade carbs and generate energy. Foods containing ALA include spinach, beets, red meat, potatoes, carrots and broccoli. Because thioctic acid appears to act as an antioxidant, chelate metal ions and scavenger free radicals thus it may protect the brain and is beneficial in certain liver illnesses. ALA is most typically used to treat nerve discomfort in diabetics, obesity and high cholesterol. Brain tissues are protected by ALA, thus preventing neurotoxicity and apoptosis of hippocampus neurons via blocking NFκ-B and Caspase-3 dependent pathways, which are both involved in neuronal apoptosis [9]. ALA is an excellent antioxidant candidate and a potentially effective treatment for depressive illnesses [10]. The phosphorylated AKT, PI3K and GRM7 proteins, which are associated with neuronal cell survival, are expressed more frequently upon ALA treatment [11].

Burdock possesses anti-inflammatory and antioxidant potential and consists of oils, in addition to tannins and plant sterols. Burdock root has been a part of traditional Chinese remedy. Some benefits of this plant include: reduced blood sugar, gastric ulcer, modulated depression via regulating GSK-3, Homer, AKT and PI3K proteins [12].

Propolis (Bee pollen); Bees collect pollen while they are attempting to find nectar. Bee pollen consists of the male reproductive cells of the plant flora. It also incorporates digestive enzymes from bees. Pollen is rich in nutrients, minerals, trace elements, enzymes and amino acids. It is also an amazing supply of antioxidants used to defend the liver from toxins, ease hypersensitive reactions, reduce atherosclerosis, reduce LDL cholesterol, enhance sexual power and reduce depression [13].

The present study aimed to investigate the therapeutic index and the mechanism of thioctic acid, Burdock and Propolis in the lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-triggered depression in rat model through monitoring ERS and cell survival BDNF/PI3K/ATF-6/CHOP/GR7M/AKT/Homer1 signaling pathways.

Materials & methods

Chemicals

LPS, thioctic acid, propolis and Burdock were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich Co (St. Louis, MO, USA). RT-PCR kits for GRM7, BDNF, AKT, Homer and PI3K determination were provided from Qiagen (MN, USA). All other chemicals were of the highest analytical grade.

Animals

Male Wister albino rats (number: 48; weight: 120–190 gm) were procured from the animal house of the National Research Center (NRC). Animals were housed under controlled settings (22°F, 55°F, and a 12 h light/dark cycle). Additionally, they were kept in cages and given free access to a pelleted food ration as well as tap water. The Animal Care and Use Committee of NRC’s ethical standards and guidelines, as well as the US national institute of ethics’ guidance for the use of laboratories and care, were rigorously followed by all procedures involving animal care and treatment in the current study (Ethics number: 24411122022).

Blood sampling

Animals were given light carbon dioxide anesthesia at the conclusion of the experiment before blood samples were taken from the retro-orbital vein. For the subsequent evaluation of biochemical parameters, all sera samples were separated after centrifugation at 5000 rpm for 15 min. Then, cervical dislocation was used to end the live of rats.

Measured parameters

Silylation of Burdock & propolis plant extract

The extract 100 μg was dissolved in 100 μl of pyridine and then mixed with 100 μl of N,O-Bis(trimethylsilyl)trifluoroacetamide (BSTFA) before being incubated at 60°C for 60 min.

Gas chromatography mass spectrometry analysis

Derivatized extract was then transferred into 200 μl inserts of Gas chromatography mass spectrometry vials and analyzed by GC/MS using a Thermo Trace 1300 gas chromatograph, equipped with AI 1310 autosampler and coupled to a Thermo ISQ 7000 mass spectrophotometer. A fused silica capillary column (30 m length, 0.32 mm ID, 0.25 mm thickness) with a DB-5 stationary phase was utilized for separation. Helium served as the carrier gas at a flow rate of 1 ml/min and a pressure of 13 psi. Temperature programming involved an initial temperature of 80°C held for 5 min then increased to 300°C at a rate of 5°C/min, and held at 300°C for 5 min, with an ion source temperature of 180°C, ionization voltage of 70 eV, and injection volume of 1 μl. Component identification relied on comparing relative retention times and mass spectra with those in the Wiley and National institute of standards and technology databases. Quantitative determination was based on peak area integration. The total ion chromatogram is depicted in Figure 1, and detailed results are compiled in Tables 1 & 2.

Open field test

Open field test is used to evaluate rodents’ locomotors activity. It is a rectangular box (60 × 60 cm) that has been partitioned into 36 identical squares (10 × 10 cm). The test rat was placed in the arena’s center and given 5 min to investigate the space. The entire arena’s overall movement was measured (total bar crossing in units). Each rat’s total distance travelled is used as a measure of locomotor activity [16].

Brain GABA protein expression

According to the manufacturer’s recommendations, GABA content was measured using Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits from R&D Systems MN, USA. At 450 nm, the absorbance was then measured [17].

Determination of brain malondialdehyde

MDA activity was estimated spectrophotometrically using commercially available kits provided from Randox Company (UK, Antrim; AS23590) [18].

Determination of brain glutathione reductase & total antioxidant capacity

GR and TAC activity were estimated spectrophotometrically using commercially available kits provided from Randox Company [19].

Real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) Quantification of brain BDNF GRM 7, ATF-6, CHOP, XBP and Homer mRNA gene expression

QIAzol lysis reagent was used to lyse the brain using a Tissue Ruptor (Roche) in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. Following the manufacturer’s instructions, Tripure Isolation Reagent (Roche) was used to sequester total RNA. Then, in accordance with the guidelines of the kits, mRNA was transformed into cDNA using Superscript Choice systems (Life Technologies, Breda, Netherlands). SYBR green Master Mix (Applied Biosystems, CA, USA) was used to perform quantitative RT-PCR for the mRNA genomic expression of BDNF, GRM7, ATF-6, CHOP, XBP and Homer. Table 3 lists the forward and reverse primer sequences. The temperature profile for RT-PCR was 94°C for 20 s, 50–55°C for 20 s (according to the optimum annealing temperature for each primer) and 72°C for 20 s for 35 cycles [19].

ELISA determination of brain PI3P & P-AKT

PI3P and P-AKT activities were estimated via ELISA kits (R&D Systems, MN, USA). It depends on a competitive enzyme immunoassay method utilizing polyclonal anti- PI3P/P-AKT antibodies. Blank was prepared by (100 μl) brain sample, Phosphate-buffered saline (as) or standard then incubated with 50 μl PI3P/P-AKT conjugates (within pre-coated plate) for 1 h at 37°C then 100 μl of Horseradish peroxidase enzyme substrate was poured into the wells. Finally, stop solution (50 μl) was added. The intensity of the color was measured (at 450 nm) (Agient BioTek Microplate reader, Neo2) [20].

Histological examination

Brain tissues were stored in 10% formalin for fixation then were processed and embedded in paraffin wax and stained with (Hematoxylin and eosin stain) then examined utilizing an Olympus microscope (BX43, Olympus, Japan) [21].

Statistical analysis

Means and SEM were used to express the collected data. The statistical analysis was performed using the Instat-3 computer application from Graph Pad Software Inc. in San Diego, CA, USA. The one way analysis of variance and post hoc tests were conducted using the SPSS 16 programmer, using Tukey’s test, the significance threshold was chosen at p < 0.05.

Discussion

Depression is a fundamental class of neurodegenerative disorders. Depression main etiology is neuronal cell death signaling pathways, which manifests abnormalities in the morphological and functional plasticity [22].

LPS was extensively utilized to aggressively generate inflammation in animal model contributing to the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines, which eventually cause neuro-inflammation and further depression. The key factor in the pathogenesis of neurological illnesses is ROS. LPS has been shown to aggravate inflammation responses via enhancing ROS and reducing Nrf2. Inflammation of the BBB results in an undesirable increase in its permeability and is accompanied with neuronal disturbance, for this reason LPS was used to induce depression in rodent model [23].

The current article clarified that thioctic acid, propolis and Burdocks considerably decreased MDA level and elevated GR and TAC levels. On the other hand, LPS significantly increased MDA and reduced GR and TAC levels as compared with the control value. There is strong evidence that ROS and oxidant/antioxidant imbalance affects several brain regions and has a detrimental impact on various cellular pathways, and causes neuro-inflammation in depressive individuals. ROS also causes direct damage to neurons and glial cells. A byproduct of lipid peroxidation MDA has been proved as a potential biomarker for psychiatric diseases like depression in addition to GSH cell deletion [23].

LPS one of the components of G-ve bacteria’s outer membrane, was identified as an endotoxin regarding its ability to stimulate immune cells and produce a number of proinflammatory cytokines, which in turn causes different organs damage, including the brain. LPS injection triggers inflammatory processes that damage the brain and result in severe depression. Thus, LPS increases NFκB in many brain regions triggering the central nervous system (CNS) to release inflammatory cytokines as (NOS) and (COX)-2 as well as, pro-inflammatory cytokines like IL-6, TNF-α and IL-1. Additionally, numerous studies demonstrated that LPS-induced depression revealed excessive oxidative stress [24]. The BBB is a crucial mediator of CNS homeostasis, acting as a vital interface to control molecular flow between the blood and the brain. When the BBB is disrupted, toxic chemicals are allowed to freely enter the brain, accelerating the onset of diseases closely linked to oxidative stress and inflammatory response. As an endotoxin, LPS has the ability to enter the bloodstream and alter BBB permeability and microvascular homeostasis. Therefore, it has been widely illustrated that decreasing oxidative stress and inflammation, is a prospective therapy for CNS and BBB dysfunction [23]. It has been previously demonstrated that LPS activates PI3K/Akt signaling pathways through TLR-4 signaling. The LPS-induced activation profile of P-Akt was earlier than that of NFκB, demonstrating the importance of PI3K/Akt in the activation pathway of microglia’s NFκB-dependent inflammatory responses. These findings can help prevent inflammatory-based neurodegenerative diseases since they provide the first proof that PI3K dependent signaling is involved in the inflammatory responses of microglial cells post LPS stimulation [25].

It has been investigated that PI3K/AKT/GSK3 are shared pathways between angiogenesis and neurogenesis and are involved in the signaling elicited via neuromonoamine. Different behavioral disorders may be linked to the suppression of their signaling neural pathways [26].

Diabetics and insulin resistance as the main cause of depression are treated with the antioxidant ALA, which has been demonstrated to improve insulin sensitivity. Thus, ALA should be clinically investigated as an additional depression treatment [27].

The present study elucidated that LPS significantly down regulated BDNF and GR7M gene expression and up regulated HOMER gene expression meanwhile, treatment with thioctic acid, propolis and Burdocks significantly regulated these deviated parameters.

One of the key neurotrophic factors, brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), is crucial for the survival and maintenance of neurons as well as the integrity and plasticity of synapses. Evidence suggests that BDNF is implicated in depression and that depressed individuals have lower levels of BDNF. In the chronic corticosterone (CORT)-induced resistant depression model, the antioxidant thioctic acid (ALA) has been shown to have antidepressant impact on mice prefrontal cortex BDNF. The prefrontal cortex BDNF levels were dramatically raised in mice treated with ALA 200 mg/kg. Additionally, a growing body of data reported that ALA is an ideal antioxidant candidate and a promising agent in improving depression therapy via providing ATP to the brain [28].

L-glutamate is the primary excitatory neurotransmitter in the CNS that supports ionotropic and metabotropic glutamate receptors. Various regions of healthy brain functions are thought to be influenced by glutamatergic neurotransmission, which can also be disturbed by various neuropathologic diseases. The metabotropic glutamate receptors are a family of G protein-coupled receptors. GRM1 and GRM5, promotes phospholipase C. The cyclic AMP cascade is inhibited by the GRM2 and GRM3 receptors. CyclinD1 expression governs neuronal differentiation and maturation along cortical development, and GRM7 modulates CREB phosphorylation and YAP expression in neural progenitor cells [29].

The neurotransmission of glutamate, which is thought to play a significant role in the development of schizophrenia and major depressive disorders, is mediated by the metabolite-receptor types 7 (GRM7) and 8 (GRM8). Along with rodents' LPS-induced depression, DNA variations or their genetic interactions within the GRM7 and GMR8 genes were linked to depressive illness [30].

In glutamate-mediated synaptic plasticity, a trait connected to bipolar disorder, the Homer family of postsynaptic scaffolding proteins is essential. Antidepressants and mood stabilizers are intended to treat Homer. Evidence linked Homer-1 gene function to mood disorder and its involvement in animal models of depression [31].

Adaptive reactions against protein accumulation in the ER and relieving ERS is enhancing protein degradation, lowering protein synthesis, and elevating molecular chaperones which assist protein folding. Activating transcription factor 6 (ATF6), Protein kinase R-like ER kinase (PERK) and inositol-requiring enzyme 1α (IRE1α) are important transmembrane proteins that initiate UPR. In unstressed conditions, these proteins are inactive via binding to GRP78. When ERS occur, the UPR signaling pathway is stimulated, and GRP78 is rapidly freed from these proteins and conjugate to unfolded proteins. PERK homodimerizes after dissociation from GRP78, promoting autophosphorylation and activation. PERK activation causes the phosphorylation of eukaryotic translation initiation factor 2 (eIF2), which activates the production of several transcription factors, including activating transcription factor 4 (ATF4) [32]. During the early phases of stress, phosphorylated eIF2 activity is suppressed, slowing the synthesis and translation of most proteins in the cell and reducing the load of protein folding in the ER. Furthermore, ATF4 protects the body by regulating amino acid metabolism, redox processes and protein secretion [33]. However, as the intensity of the stress response increase, active ATF4 promotes the production of the pro-apoptotic CCAAT/enhancer-binding protein-homologous protein [CHOP]. If the UPR process is successful in reducing the number of unfolded and misfolded proteins, UPR signaling is attenuated and the cell survives [34]. X-box binding protein 1 (XBP1), is triggered by ATF6 and spliced by activated IRE1 [35]. In an attempt to restore ER homeostasis, the transcription factor XBP1s not only stimulates transcription of the GRP78 and CHOP genes, but also activates the ERAD program via ER degradation [36,37]. As a result, XBP1s are critical for restoring ER equilibrium. ATF6 is primarily found in the ER as a zymogen when cells are not stressed, but when stressed, it is stimulated to separate from GRP78 and transfer into Golgi bodies. Following cleavage by the Golgi site-1 and 2 proteases [38], the ATF6 transcription factor is released. ATF6, in collaboration with XBP1, stimulates the transcription of GRP78, counteracting ERS and exerting protective effect to increase cell survival [39]. These transduction routes work collectively to prevent ERS via negative feedback, lowering the quantity of unfolded proteins and promoting cell survival. When the process fails, a considerable amount of calcium is released into the cytoplasm, causing apoptosis, whereas persistent UPR signals cause chronic ERS. The UPR promotes cell death by apoptosis post activation of CHOP, Bcl-2, Bim, JNKs and caspases [40].

Endotoxin LPS produces anxiety and depression-like behavior in rodents. Its injection has been shown to significantly increase GRP78 mRNA expression in the hippocampus, implying that UPR plays a role in LPS-induced behavioral abnormalities. Social loss stress-vulnerable mice also exhibit depression-like behavior, which is related with an increase in amygdala expression of GRP78, CHOP and choline acetyl transferase [41]. Liu et al. colleagues, [42] discovered that GRP78 and XBP1 expression were considerably enhanced in the hippocampus of mice subjected to social stress. Furthermore, persistent social stress can activate the PERK-eIF2 signaling pathway in the hippocampus, resulting in a decrease in brain-derived neurotrophic factor expression levels and, ultimately, depression-like behavior and memory impairment in mice. Harding et al. [43] also demonstrated that inhibition of PERK expression in the hippocampus can enhance hippocampal-dependent memory and reverse memory deterioration in mice, demonstrating that altering PERK expression levels in the Cornu Ammon 1 area of the hippocampus can improve cognitive function [43]. Furthermore, plasma corticosterone levels and the expression of genes encoding GRP78, GRP94, ATF6, XBP1, ATF4 and CHOP increased in the hippocampus of rats with learned helplessness, suggesting that depression-like behavior may be related with excessive ERS persistence [44,45].

The antioxidant ALA, is well known for treating illnesses of the neurological system through crucial properties like preventing the formation of ROS, serving as a cofactor in various enzyme complexes, and reducing the level of antioxidants and neurotransmitters in the brain, ALA can create avenues in the treatment of neurological and psychiatric diseases. Additionally, it has been demonstrated that it controls the nerve growth factor, prevents neuronal loss and injury, and revives tissues that have been injured [46,47].

ALA as an antioxidant and anti-inflammatory agent has a vital role in the treatment of various neuronal disorders by counteracting free radicals, as it acts as a cofactor in many enzyme complexes, supporting the reduction of lipid peroxidation, metal chelator and regenerating damaged tissues. ALA can inhibit neuron damage caused by ROS created throughout neurodegenerative diseases [48]. It also supports regulation of nerve growth factor and sustain blood glucose balance thus prevent neurodegenerative diseases [49]. Through raising glutathione levels in the prefrontal brain and striatum, ALA is a promising drug for the treatment of depression. Additionally, it lowers total nucleoside triphosphate levels throughout the entire brain and reverses the loss in neurotrophic factor in the striatum and hippocampus [15].

Impact of propolis on depression was also previously reported, rats that had been exposed to unanticipated chronic stress were shown to benefit from Chrysin, a flavonoid that exist in honey and propolis. Propolis improved the activity of the hippocampal glucocorticoid receptors, which is frequently decreased in depressed individuals [50]. Vitamins, minerals, trace elements, enzymes and amino acids are abundant in propolis. Additionally, it is a powerful antioxidant. It can alleviate benign prostatic hyperplasia symptoms, ease allergies and protect the liver from toxins effects. Additionally, bee pollen can ease bleeding, boost metabolism, lessen atherosclerosis, increase sexual power and reduce depression.

Burdock, a genus of weeds related to sunflowers and a member of the daisy family, is the source of Burdock root. Numerous studies have shown that it can treat cancer, diabetes and inflammation. It may also have other positive impacts on neurons in addition to lowering blood sugar levels which modifies depression. The antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects of Burdock can combat the free radicals that cause cancer, ageing, depression and inflammatory disorders. Burdock can slow cancer spread, according to preliminary research it is a growth-stimulating, antirheumatic, antidepressant antidiabetic, antitoxic, antineoplastic, antimutagenic and antileukemic agent. Burdock significantly decreased in mouse peritonitis model’s LPS-induced rise in plasma IL-1 levels [14]. in vivo and in vitro, Burdock was able to reduce LPS-induced inflammation. In mouse embryonic fibroblast cells, the antitumorigenic action of Burdock downmodulated oncogenic genes regulated by PI3K/AKT/STAT3 [12].

Conclusion

Thioctic acid, Burdock and propolis can be beneficial antidepressant agents versus LPS induced depression or neurotoxicity via modulating AKT/PI3K/ATF6/CHOP/XBP/GR7M/Homer1/BDNF signaling pathways.

Probabilistic well-posedness of dispersive PDEs beyond variance blowup I: Benjamin-Bona-Mahony equation
Guopeng Li, Jiawei Li, Tadahiro Oh, and Nikolay Tzvetkov

Abstract
. We investigate a possible extension of probabilistic well-posedness theory of nonlinear dispersive PDEs with random initial data beyond variance blowup. As a model equation, we study the Benjamin-Bona-Mahony equation (BBM) with Gaussian random initial data. By introducing a suitable vanishing multiplicative renormalization constant on the initial data, we show that solutions to BBM with the renormalized Gaussian random initial data beyond variance blowup converge in law to a solution to the stochastic BBM forced by the derivative of a spatial white noise. By considering alternative renormalization, we show that solutions to the renormalized BBM with the frequency-truncated Gaussian initial data converges in law to a solution to the linear stochastic BBM with the full Gaussian initial data, forced by the derivative of a spatial white noise. This latter result holds for the Gaussian random initial data of arbitrarily low regularity. We also establish analogous results for the stochastic BBM forced by a fractional derivative of a space-time white noise.

2.1.
Notations

Let
A\lesssim B
denote an estimate of the form
A\leq CB
for some constant
C>0
. We write
A\sim B
if
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
, while
A\ll B
denotes
A\leq cB
for some small constant
c>0
.
We may write
\lesssim_{\beta}
to
emphasize the dependence on an external parameter
\beta
.
We use
C>0
to denote various constants, which may vary line by line.
We may also write
C_{\beta}
to emphasize
the dependence on a parameter
\beta
.

We set
\mathbb{Z}^{*}=\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.
We also set

a\wedge b=\min(a,b)
.

In this paper, all the function spaces
are restricted to real-valued functions.
Given
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
we define the
L^{2}
-based Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})

via the norm:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{s}}=\|\langle n\rangle^{s}\widehat{f}(n)\|_{\ell^{2}_{n}}.

Given
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
we define
the
L^{p}
-based Sobolev space
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{T})

as the completion of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T})=C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})

under
the norm:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{s,p}}=\|\langle\nabla\rangle^{s}f\|_{L^{p}}=\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\langle n\rangle^{s}\widehat{f}(n))\|_{L^{p}}

We note that
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})=W^{s,2}(\mathbb{T})
.
When
p=\infty
, our definition
provides a smaller space than the usual
W^{s,\infty}(\mathbb{T})
,
but it ensures that our version of

W^{s,\infty}(\mathbb{T})
is separable; see Remark
2.11
.
We note that such a convention is common in
(stochastic) parabolic PDEs; see, for example,

[
75
,
29
]
.
We endow

\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T})=C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})

with the Fréchet space topology generated by a countable
family of semi-norms

\big\{\|\,\cdot\,\|_{W^{j,\infty}}\big\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
.
We use
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
to denote the space of distributions on
\mathbb{T}
.

In dealing with space-time functions,
we use short-hand notations such as

C_{T}H^{s}_{x}
=
C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{T}))

and

C_{I}H^{s}_{x}=C(I;H^{s}(\mathbb{T}))
, where
I\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
is an interval.

Given
0<\beta<1
, we denote by
C^{\beta}_{T}B=C^{\beta}([0,T];B)
the space of
\beta
-Hölder continuous functions taking values in a Banach space
B
, endowed with the seminorm:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{\beta}_{T}B}=\sup_{0\leq r<t\leq T}\frac{\|u(t)-u(r)\|_{B}}{|t-r|^{\beta}}.

We also define the Lipschitz space

\mathcal{C}^{\beta}_{T}B=\mathcal{C}^{\beta}([0,T];B)
via the norm:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}_{T}B}=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}B}+\|u\|_{C^{\beta}_{T}B}.

Given

,
we use
\mathbf{P}_{N}
to denote
the Dirichlet projector onto the frequencies

\{|n|\leq N\}

defined by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{P}_{N}f}(n)=\mathbf{1}_{\{|n|\leq N\}}\widehat{f}(n).

\displaystyle F_{n}=(F_{n}^{1},\cdots,F_{n}^{m})=(I_{k_{1}}(f^{1}_{n}),\cdots,I_{k_{m}}(f^{m}_{n})),

where

f^{i}_{n}\in L^{2}_{\textup{sym}}(\mathbb{T}^{k_{i}})
,
i=1,\dots,m
,
such that
an
m\times m
matrix
\mathsf{C}=(\mathsf{C}_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq m}

defined by

(2.19)

\displaystyle\mathsf{C}_{ij}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[F_{n}^{i}F_{n}^{j}],\quad 1\leq i,j\leq m,

is positive semi-definite.
Then,
the following statements are equivalent
:

(i)

For every
1\leq i\leq m
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[(F_{n}^{i})^{4}]=3\mathsf{C}_{ii}^{2}.

(ii)

For every
1\leq i\leq m
and
1\leq r\leq k_{i}-1
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}^{i}\otimes_{r}f_{n}^{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2(k_{i}-r)})}=0.

(iii)

As
n\to\infty
, the random vector
F_{n}
converges in law to an
m
-dimensional Gaussian vector

Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathsf{C})
.

In this paper, we only use the implication (ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii)
but we included the statement (i) since it
is the reason that Lemma
2.7

is called the fourth moment method.
We also point out that, while
positive definiteness of the matrix
\mathsf{C}
is imposed in
some literature (for example,

[
106
, Proposition 1]
),
it suffices to assume
that
\mathsf{C}
is
positive semi-definite.

The fourth moment theorem
was first introduced in
[
84
]

by Nualart and Peccati
and was further extended in
[
83
,
79
]
,
which has become a powerful tool in probability theory,
particularly in applications to the study of scaling limits of disordered systems
such as directed polymers, SPDEs, SDEs,
and central limit theorems for Gaussian functionals;
see, for example,
[
26
,
85
,
81
,
66
,
63
,
54
]
.

Lastly,
we recall the Prokhorov theorem
and the Skorokhod representation theorem.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be any nonempty index set.
A family

\{\mu_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
of probability measures
on a metric space
\mathcal{M}
is said to be tight
if, for every
\delta>0
, there exists a compact set
K_{\delta}\subset\mathcal{M}

such that
\sup_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\mu_{j}(K_{\delta}^{c})<\delta
.
We say that
\{\mu_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
is relatively compact, if every sequence
in
\{\mu_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
contains a weakly convergent subsequence.

We say that a family

\{X_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
of random variables
is tight if their laws are tight.

Note that the index set
\mathcal{J}
does not need to be countable.
We now
recall the following
Prokhorov theorem from
[
10
,
1
]
.

Lemma 2.9

(Prokhorov theorem)
.

If a sequence of probability measures
on a metric space
\mathcal{M}
is tight, then
it is relatively compact.
If in addition,
\mathcal{M}
is separable and complete, then relative compactness is
equivalent to tightness.

Lastly, we recall the following Skorokhod representation
theorem from

[
1
, Chapter 31]
.

Lemma 2.10

(Skorokhod representation theorem)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a complete
separable metric space
(
i.e. a Polish space
)
.
Suppose that
probability measures

\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

on
\mathcal{M}
converges weakly to a probability measure
\mu

as
n\to\infty
.
Then, there exist a probability space
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathscr{F}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
,
and random variables
X_{n},X:\widetilde{\Omega}\to\mathcal{M}

such that

\displaystyle\textup{Law}(X_{n})=\mu_{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\textup{Law}(X)=\mu,

and
X_{n}
converges
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
-almost surely to
X
as
n\to\infty
.
Here,
\textup{Law}(X)
denotes the law of a random variable
X
.

Remark 2.11
.

Recall that
the space of continuous functions from
a separable metric space to another separable metric space
with the compact-open topology (in time) is separable; see
[
74
]
.
See also the paper
[
70
, Corollary 3.3]
.
In particular,
in view of our definition of
W^{s,\infty}(\mathbb{T})
which makes the space separable,
we see that
C(\mathbb{R}_{+};W^{s,\infty}(\mathbb{T}))
endowed with the compact-open
topology is separable.

in (

In Subsection

In Subsection

3.1.
Uniqueness of the limit

Our main goal in this subsection is
to establish the following proposition
on convergence of finite-dimensional marginals (in time)
of

, which shows the uniqueness of the limit
of

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\alpha\leq\frac{1}{4}
.
Let
Z_{N}
and
Z
be as in (
1.22
) and (
1.27
), respectively.
Given
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let

0\leq t_{1}\leq\cdots\leq t_{m}
.
Then,

\big\{Z_{N}(t_{j})\big\}_{j=1}^{m}

converges in law
to

\big\{Z(t_{j})\big\}_{j=1}^{m}

in
(\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}))^{\otimes m}

as
N\to\infty
.

As a preliminary step,
we study the covariance (in time)
of
Z_{N}(t)
acting on test functions.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\alpha\leq\frac{1}{4}
.
Given
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
Z_{N}
and
Z
be as in (
1.22
) and (
1.27
), respectively.
Then,
given any test function
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T})
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\langle Z_{N}(t),\psi\rangle\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[\langle Z(t),\psi\rangle\big]=0

for any
t\geq 0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
and

(3.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[\langle Z_{N}(r),\psi_{1}\rangle\langle Z_{N}(t),\psi_{2}\rangle\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\langle Z(r),\psi_{1}\rangle\langle Z(t),\psi_{2}\rangle\Big]

for any test functions
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T})
and
t\geq r\geq 0
,
where

\langle f,g\rangle
denotes the
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
-
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T})
duality pairing.

Proof.

From (
1.22
) with (
1.21
), we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \widehat{Z}_{N}(t,n)&=\sum_{n=n_{1}+n_{2}}\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-t^{\prime})\varphi(n)}\varphi(n)\widehat{z}_{N}(t^{\prime},n_{1})\widehat{z}_{N}(t^{\prime},n_{2})dt^{\prime}\\
&=C_{\alpha,N}^{2}e^{t\varphi(n)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+n_{2}\\
|n_{1}|,|n_{2}|\leq N\end{subarray}}\int^{t}_{0}e^{-t^{\prime}(\varphi(n)-\varphi(n_{1})-\varphi(n_{2}))}\varphi(n)\frac{g_{n_{1}}g_{n_{2}}}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{\alpha}\langle n_{2}\rangle^{\alpha}}dt^{\prime}.\end{aligned}

Recalling that
\varphi(n)|_{n=0}=0
,
we have
\widehat{Z}_{N}(t,0)=0

for any
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

(when
n=0
).
Then, from (
3.3
), we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle Z_{N}(t),\psi\rangle&=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\widehat{Z}_{N}(t,n)\overline{\widehat{\psi}(n)}\\
&=C_{\alpha,N}^{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}e^{t\varphi(n)}\overline{\widehat{\psi}(n)}\\
&\quad\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+n_{2}\\
|n_{1}|,|n_{2}|\leq N\end{subarray}}\int^{t}_{0}e^{-t^{\prime}(\varphi(n)-\varphi(n_{1})-\varphi(n_{2}))}\varphi(n)\frac{g_{n_{1}}g_{n_{2}}}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{\alpha}\langle n_{2}\rangle^{\alpha}}dt^{\prime}.\end{split}

It follows from (
2.4
) that for
n\neq 0
,
we have

\mathbb{E}[g_{n_{1}}g_{n_{2}}]=0

for
n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, satisfying
n=n_{1}+n_{2}
.
Hence,
(
3.1
) for
Z_{N}
follows from (
3.4
).
Note that
(
3.1
) for
Z
follows easily from (
1.27
)
with (
1.26
).

From (
3.4
) with
\overline{\varphi(n)}=-\varphi(n)
, we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}&\Big[\langle Z_{N}(r),\psi_{1}\rangle\langle Z_{N}(t),\psi_{2}\rangle\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\langle Z_{N}(r),\psi_{1}\rangle\overline{\langle Z_{N}(t),\psi_{2}\rangle}\Big]\\
&=\sum_{n,m\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\widehat{Z}_{N}(r,n)\overline{\widehat{Z}_{N}(t,m)}\Big]\overline{\widehat{\psi}_{1}(n)}\widehat{\psi}_{2}(m)\\
&=C_{\alpha,N}^{4}\sum_{n,m\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\overline{\widehat{\psi}_{1}(n)}\widehat{\psi}_{2}(m)\int^{r}_{0}e^{(r-t_{1})\varphi(n)}\varphi(n)\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-t_{2})\varphi(m)}\overline{\varphi(m)}\\
&\quad\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+n_{2}\\
|n_{1}|,|n_{2}|\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=m_{1}+m_{2}\\
|m_{1}|,|m_{2}|\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{e^{t_{1}(\varphi(n_{1})+\varphi(n_{2}))}}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{\alpha}\langle n_{2}\rangle^{\alpha}}\frac{e^{-t_{2}(\varphi(m_{1})+\varphi(m_{2}))}}{\langle m_{1}\rangle^{\alpha}\langle m_{2}\rangle^{\alpha}}\\
&\hphantom{XXXXXXXXXXX}\times\mathbb{E}\big[g_{n_{1}}g_{n_{2}}\overline{g_{m_{1}}g_{m_{2}}}\big]dt_{1}dt_{2}.\end{aligned}

Since
n_{1}+n_{2},m_{1}+m_{2}\neq 0
,
we see
from Wick’s theorem
[
110
, Proposition I.2]

with (
2.4
)
that

\mathbb{E}[g_{n_{1}}g_{n_{2}}\overline{g_{m_{1}}g_{m_{2}}}]

is non-zero only if

(n_{1},n_{2})=(m_{1},m_{2})
or
(m_{2},m_{1})
,
which in particular implies

n=m
.
Moreover, by separately considering the cases

n_{1}\neq n_{2}
and
n_{1}=n_{2}=\frac{n}{2}
(which occurs
only when
n
is even) with
(
2.5
), we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=m_{1}+m_{2}\\
|m_{1}|,|m_{2}|\leq N\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\big[g_{n_{1}}g_{n_{2}}\overline{g_{m_{1}}g_{m_{2}}}\big]=2

for any
n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
|n_{1}|,|n_{2}|\leq N

and
n=n_{1}+n_{2}\neq 0
.
Thus, from (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}&\Big[\langle Z_{N}(r),\psi_{1}\rangle\langle Z_{N}(t),\psi_{2}\rangle\Big]\\
&=2C_{\alpha,N}^{4}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\overline{\widehat{\psi}_{1}(n)}\widehat{\psi}_{2}(n)|\varphi(n)|^{2}\int^{r}_{0}e^{(r-t_{1})\varphi(n)}\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-t_{2})\varphi(n)}\\
&\quad\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+n_{2}\\
|n_{1}|,|n_{2}|\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{e^{(t_{1}-t_{2})(\varphi(n_{1})+\varphi(n_{2}))}}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{2\alpha}\langle n_{2}\rangle^{2\alpha}}dt_{1}dt_{2}.\end{aligned}

In the following, we first consider the case
0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{4}
.
We then briefly discuss the case
\alpha\leq 0
.

\bullet
Case 1:

0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{4}
.

By the Taylor expansion, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+n_{2}\\
|n_{1}|,|n_{2}|\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{e^{(t_{1}-t_{2})(\varphi(n_{1})+\varphi(n_{2}))}}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{2\alpha}\langle n_{2}\rangle^{2\alpha}}\\
&\quad=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+n_{2}\\
|n_{1}|,|n_{2}|\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{2\alpha}\langle n_{2}\rangle^{2\alpha}}\\
&\quad\quad+O\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{k}}{k!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+n_{2}\\
|n_{1}|,|n_{2}|\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{|\varphi(n_{1})+\varphi(n_{2})|^{k}}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{2\alpha}\langle n_{2}\rangle^{2\alpha}}\bigg).\end{split}

Let us first consider the summand in the
k
-summation
on the right-hand side.
From (
1.3
),
we see that

|\varphi(n)|\leq\frac{1}{2}

and
that

|\varphi(n)|
is decreasing for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then,
we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+n_{2}\\
|n_{1}|,|n_{2}|\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{|\varphi(n_{1})+\varphi(n_{2})|^{k}}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{2\alpha}\langle n_{2}\rangle^{2\alpha}}&\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+n_{2}\\
|n_{1}|,|n_{2}|\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{|\varphi(n_{1})+\varphi(n_{2})|}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{2\alpha}\langle n_{2}\rangle^{2\alpha}}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+n_{2}\\
|n_{1}|\leq|n_{2}|\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{2\alpha+1}\langle n_{2}\rangle^{2\alpha}}\\
&\leq\sum_{|n_{1}|\leq N}\frac{1}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{4\alpha+1}}\sim 1,\end{split}

uniformly in
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
N\gg 1
.
Then, from (
3.8
) and (
3.9
),
we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+n_{2}\\
|n_{1}|,|n_{2}|\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{e^{(t_{1}-t_{2})(\varphi(n_{1})+\varphi(n_{2}))}}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{2\alpha}\langle n_{2}\rangle^{2\alpha}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+n_{2}\\
|n_{1}|,|n_{2}|\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{2\alpha}\langle n_{2}\rangle^{2\alpha}}+O(e^{ct})

as
N\to\infty
,
where we used the fact that
|t_{1}-t_{2}|\leq t

for

and
0\leq t_{2}\leq t
with
r\leq t
.
Hence,
from (
3.7
), (
3.10
), and (

),
we obtain

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[\langle Z_{N}(r),\psi_{1}\rangle\langle Z_{N}(t),\psi_{2}\rangle\Big]\\
&\quad=c_{\alpha}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\overline{\widehat{\psi}_{1}(n)}\widehat{\psi}_{2}(n)|\varphi(n)|^{2}\int^{r}_{0}e^{(r-t_{1})\varphi(n)}dt_{1}\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-t_{2})\varphi(n)}dt_{2},\end{aligned}

where

is defined by

(3.12)

\displaystyle c_{\alpha}=\lim_{N\to\infty}C_{\alpha,N}^{4}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+n_{2}\\
|n_{1}|,|n_{2}|\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{2}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{2\alpha}\langle n_{2}\rangle^{2\alpha}}.

While it
may seem that

depends on

,
we point out that

is independent of
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Moreover, we claim that

\displaystyle c_{\alpha}=1.

On the other hand, from (

) with (

),

\mathbb{E}[\mathfrak{g}_{n}\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{m}]=\mathbf{1}_{n=m}

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}&\Big[\langle Z(r),\psi_{1}\rangle\langle Z(t),\psi_{2}\rangle\Big]\\
&=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\overline{\widehat{\psi}_{1}(n)}\widehat{\psi}_{2}(n)|\varphi(n)|^{2}\int^{r}_{0}e^{(r-t_{1})\varphi(n)}dt_{1}\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-t_{2})\varphi(n)}dt_{2}.\end{split}

Therefore,
by comparing (

we obtain
(
3.2
)
when

Remark 3.3
.

Let
0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{4}
.
The definition (
3.12
)
of

a priori depends on

but we show that

is in fact independent of
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We first consider the case

.
Note that the contribution
to the summation in (

)
from

Indeed, from (

(3.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lim_{N\to\infty}C_{\alpha,N}^{4}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+n_{2}\\

as

.

\displaystyle 1-\theta\leq\frac{\langle n_{2}\rangle^{2\alpha}}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{2\alpha}}=\frac{\langle n-n_{1}\rangle^{2\alpha}}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{2\alpha}}\leq 1+\theta

),

\displaystyle\begin{split}c_{\alpha}&=\lim_{N\to\infty}C_{\alpha,N}^{4}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+n_{2}\\

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{1+\theta}\lim_{N\to\infty}C_{\alpha,N}^{4}\sum_{|n_{1}|\leq N}\frac{2}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{4\alpha}}\geq\frac{1}{1+\theta}.

) and (

).

,

\bullet
Case 2:

\alpha\leq 0
.

We first estimate the contribution
from

In this case, we consider

(3.19)

\displaystyle C_{\alpha,N}^{4}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+n_{2}\\

.

Hence, from (

A.1.
Renormalization of the stochastic BBM

As in Hairer’s work

(A.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{N}-\partial_{txx}u_{N}+\partial_{x}u_{N}+\partial_{x}(u_{N}^{2})=C_{1-\alpha,N}\mathbf{P}_{N}\langle\partial_{x}\rangle^{\alpha}\partial_{x}\xi\\

Let

We note that, in order to obtain the limiting equation (

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathbb{E}\Big[\langle\upzeta(t_{1}),\psi_{1}\rangle\langle\upzeta(t_{2}),\psi_{2}\rangle\Big]=(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})^{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\overline{\widehat{\psi}_{1}(n)}\widehat{\psi}_{2}(n)\end{aligned}

We present a proof of Proposition

(A.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{n}(t)e_{n},

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}_{n}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-t^{\prime}\varphi(n)}\varphi(n)\langle n\rangle^{\alpha}dB_{n}(t^{\prime}).

for any

We omit details.

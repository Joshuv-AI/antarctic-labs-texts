Once-Weekly Subcutaneous Insulin Efsitora for the Management of Diabetes Mellitus: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Study
Authors: Sidar COPUR, Berk MIZRAK, Lasin OZBEK, Duygu UCKU, Adnan BATMAN, Abduzhappar GAIPOV, Adrian COVIC, Daniel van RAALTE, Mehmet KANBAY
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTEven though insulin therapy is the cornerstone of diabetes mellitus management, multiple daily injections are associated with poor adherence. Once-weekly basal insulin therapy has emerged as a novel therapeutic approach and is being evaluated in multiple ongoing clinical trials. We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis to evaluate the efficacy and safety of once-weekly insulin efsitora therapy compared with once-daily basal insulin therapy in patients with type 1 or type 2 diabetes mellitus. Primary endpoints include glycemic control, assessed via HbA1c reduction, and hypoglycemia, classified as level 1 (54-69 mg/dL), level 2 (<54 mg/dl), or level 3 (any hypoglycemic episode characterized by altered mental and/or physical functioning requiring assistance). We have performed a systematic review and meta-analysis through an extensive literature search of major databases including Scopus, PubMed/Medline, and Cochrane databases, and have only included randomized controlled trials. We have included 5 randomized controlled trials comprising a total of 2,562 patients: 1,358 (53%) in the insulin efsitora arm and 1,204 (47%) in the insulin degludec arm. Our data show that insulin efsitora therapy achieved a similar HbA1c reduction overall (estimated treatment difference (ETD) 0.08%, 95% CI -0.01 to 0.17; I² = 42.6%), in patients with type 1 diabetes mellitus (ETD 0.10%, 95% CI -0.64-0.85; I² =24.0%) and in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (ETD 0.05%, 95% CI -0.25 – 0.26; I² = 38.1%) compared with insulin degludec therapy. Moreover, no statistically significant differences were identified in overall hypoglycemic events, level 2 or 3 hypoglycemic episodes, or nocturnal hypoglycemia, irrespective of diabetes mellitus subtype. Our meta-analysis shows no statistically significant difference between once-weekly insulin efsitora therapy and insulin degludec therapy in terms of glycemic control and hypoglycemic events.

INTRODUCTION

The gold standard therapeutic approach for the management of type 1 diabetes mellitus (DM) patients is the intensive insulin therapy administered either via multiple daily injections or via continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion aiming to imitate physiological homeostatic state1. Similarly, insulin therapy along with other oral or subcutaneous medications is the mainstay therapeutic approach among type 2 DM patients as most patients have already lost at least 80% of their insulin secretion capacity with further decline over the follow-up2. Nonetheless, adherence to insulin therapy which is most commonly delivered via multiple daily injections with reported rates of 95·4% in Eastern Europe, 56·5% in Western Europe, and 37% in the USA of prescribed patients3 is a challenging issue. A post-hoc analysis of a prospective real-world data conducted in type 1 DM patients has revealed 22% chance of missing at least one basal insulin injection over 14-day period4 while a large-scale meta-analysis study has revealed 52.6% adherence to insulin therapy among type 1 DM patients and 52.5% adherence among type 2 DM patients5. Such poor adherence to multiple daily injections of insulin therapy is linked to poor glycemic control, high glycemic variability and higher risk for diabetic complications including diabetic ketoacidosis events6. A new potential for enhancing treatment adherence, once-weekly basal insulin therapy, has emerged over the last few years with insulin icodec being the first agent applied for the US Food and Drug Administration approval7. The aim of this meta-analysis is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of once-weekly insulin efsitora therapy compared with once-daily basal insulin therapy in patients with type 1 DM.

MATERIAL and METHODS

Study Registration

We have utilized the preferred reporting items for systematic reviews and meta-analysis (PRISMA)8 to conduct this systematic review and meta-analysis study. This study was registered with PROSPERO.

Eligibility Criteria

We have included randomized controlled trials evaluating the efficacy and safety of once-weekly insulin efsitora therapy compared with any basal insulin used as the comparator in patients with type 1 or type 2 DM, with a minimum follow-up of 26 weeks. To be included in this meta-analysis, clinical studies should be published in a peer-reviewed journal in English. Efficacy outcomes included changes in HbA1c, fasting plasma glucose, or continuous glucose monitoring data, and safety outcomes included hypoglycemic episodes. Studies with cross-over, observational, or retrospective designs; studies without any active comparator group or intervention and/or with a follow-up period less than 26 weeks; or studies that included participants younger than age 18 were excluded from this meta-analysis. Studies with missing data or with inappropriate or missing outcome descriptions were excluded.

Search strategy and Data Extraction

An extensive literature search was conducted across three major databases (PubMed, Scopus, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials) up to November 2024. The following keywords were utilized either separately or in combination: “DM”, “type 1 DM”, “type 2 DM”, “insulin efsitora”, “insulin degludec”, “insulin glargine”, “insulin detemir”, “basal insulin”, “hypoglycemia”, “glycemic control”, “HbA1c”, “fasting plasma glucose”, and “continuous glucose monitoring”. Additionally, we manually searched the references of the included clinical studies to identify any additional clinical studies.

Study Selection

Two authors (B.M. and S.C.) independently evaluated titles and abstracts for relevance after the extensive literature search. Two authors have obtained the full texts of the relevant articles to thoroughly evaluate their appropriateness for inclusion in this meta-analysis. Any disagreements between the two authors were clarified by consulting a third author (M.K.).

Data Extraction

Two authors (B.M. and S.C.) independently extracted the clinical data from the selected clinical trials. Data were extracted based on intention-to-treat principles.

Endpoints and Subgroup Analyses

The primary efficacy outcome for this meta-analysis was the change in HbA1c from baseline to the end of the follow-up period in patients with type I or type II DM. Secondary efficacy outcomes included fasting plasma glucose (FPG) and changes in daily insulin dose. The primary safety outcome was the rate of hypoglycemic events, classified as severe or non-severe based on individual study definitions. Level 1 hypoglycemia is defined as a blood glucose level of 54 to 69 mg/dL; level 2 hypoglycemia is defined as a blood glucose level below 54 mg/dL; and level 3 hypoglycemia refers to any hypoglycemic episode characterized by altered mental and/or physical functioning requiring assistance.

Quality Assessment

The Risk of Bias tool was used independently by two authors (B.M. and S.C.) to assess potential bias in the included clinical studies. The Risk of Bias tool included five domains of assessment9. Any disagreements between the two authors regarding the evaluation of bias in included studies were resolved by consulting a third author (M.K.). Publication bias was investigated by funnel-plot analysis of point estimates according to study weights and by Egger’s regression test.

Statistical Analysis

Mean differences (MD) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were used to compare treatment effects for continuous endpoints such as changes in HbA1c, while risk ratios (RR) with 95% CI were used for categorical endpoints like hypoglycemic events. Cochran’s Q test and I² statistic were used to assess heterogeneity; p-values less than 0.10 and I² > 25% were considered indicative of significant heterogeneity. We used the DerSimonian and Laird random-effects model for meta-analyses to account for between-study variability. Statistical analyses were conducted using RStudio for the meta-analysis.

RESULTS

Study Selection and Characteristics

The initial search yielded 55 results. After removing duplicates and ineligible studies, 12 studies remained for full-text review based on the inclusion criteria. Of these, 5 RCTs were included, comprising a total of 2562 patients10, 11, 12, 13, 14. The general characteristics of the included RCTs are summarized in Table 1. Among these, 1358 patients (53%) received weekly insulin efsitora injections, while 1204 (47%) received daily insulin degludec injections. The study characteristics are summarized in Table 1, and the detailed search strategy is presented in the PRISMA flowchart (Figure 1).

Analysis of Glycemic Control Among Patients with Type 1 and 2 DM

Insulin efsitora therapy has not been associated with a statistically significant estimated treatment difference (ETD) in HbA1c change compared to insulin degludec in patients with type I DM. The pooled MD was 0.10% (95% CI, -0.64-0.85; I² =24.0%; Figure 2A). Similarly, in patients with type 2 DM, the pooled ETD for change in HbA1c was not statistically significant for insulin efsitora therapy compared with insulin degludec, with an ETD of 0.05% (95% CI -0.25-0.26; I²=38.1%; Figure 2B).

Regarding overall hypoglycemia, no statistically significant difference was observed between insulin efsitora and insulin degludec in patients with type 1 DM (RR 1.02; 95% CI 0.97-1.07; I² = 90.1%; Figure 3A). Similarly, in patients with type 2 DM, the pooled RR for overall hypoglycemia was not statistically significant (RR 1.13; 95% CI 0.95-1.35; I² = 80.6%; Figure 3B).

For combined level 2 and level 3 hypoglycemic events, no statistically significant difference was observed between the two regimens in patients with type 1 DM (RR 1.05; 95% CI 1.00-1.10; I² = =0%; Figure 3C). Similarly, in patients with type 2 DM, the pooled RR was 1.10 (95% CI 0.77-1.56; I² =73.8%; Figure 3D).

For nocturnal hypoglycemic events, there was no statistically significant difference among patients with type 1 DM (RR 1.05; 95% CI 1.00-1.10; I² = =0%; p=0.05; Figure 3E). Similarly, in patients with type 2 DM, the pooled RR for nocturnal hypoglycemia was 1.17 (95% CI 0.70-1.94; I² =79.0%; p=0.55; Figure 3F).

Analysis of Glycemic Control and Hypoglycemia in Pooled Analysis

In patients receiving insulin efsitora, the HbA1c reduction was comparable to that observed with insulin degludec, with no statistically significant difference. The pooled estimated treatment difference (ETD) in HbA1c change was 0.08% (95% CI -0.07 to 0.17; I² = 42.6%) (Figure 4).

Analysis of hypoglycemic events revealed no significant differences between the two regimens, but heterogeneity was high. For overall hypoglycemia, the pooled risk ratio (RR) was 1.07 (95% CI 0.97-1.18; I² = 96.4%; Figure 5A). Similar findings were observed for combined level-2 and level-3 hypoglycemic events (RR 1.05; 95% CI 0.96-1.15; I² = 50.3%; Figure 5B) and for nocturnal hypoglycemia (RR 1.04; 95% CI 0.93-1.16; I² = 65.6%; Figure 3C).

Quality Assessment

Individual RCT risk-of-bias assessments are reported in Supplementary Figures 1 and 2. The studies were rated as having some concerns regarding bias, primarily due to Domain 2 of the Risk of Bias tool. Among the included studies, those by Bergenstal et al.10 and Wysham et al.12 demonstrated higher potential for behavioral deviations due to less stringent protocols, whereas Frias et al.11 had stricter algorithms, reducing the risk of bias in Domain 2. As shown in Supplementary Figures 1 and 2, no evidence of publication bias was found; the funnel plot showed a symmetrical distribution of similarly weighted studies converging toward the pooled overall hypoglycemic events.

DISCUSSION

We conducted the first systematic review and meta-analysis evaluating the efficacy and safety of once-weekly insulin efsitora therapy in the management of type 1 and type 2 DM compared with once-daily basal insulin therapy, including a total of 2.562 patients from five RCTs. As shown in our meta-analysis, similar effects were observed when compared with once-weekly insulin degludec in terms of insulin efsitora therapy, HbA1c reduction, and hypoglycemic side effects; however, significant heterogeneity should be acknowledged.

Our analysis has indicated that once-weekly insulin efsitora therapy has achieved similar glycemic efficacy compared with insulin degludec therapy overall and in patients with type 1 and type 2 DM. Similarly, insulin efsitora therapy has not led to a statistically significant increase in the risk of overall hypoglycemic episodes, level 2 and 3 hypoglycemic episodes, or nocturnal hypoglycemic events compared with insulin degludec therapy. However, the safety of insulin efsitora therapy with respect to overall hypoglycemic events should be interpreted with caution, as there was high heterogeneity between studies. Potential sources of such high heterogeneity include (a) variations in patient populations at baseline, including demographics, comorbidities, and glycemic control; (b) alterations in dose-titration schedules and follow-up timing; and (c) differences in sample sizes and study designs. Moreover, few clinical studies conducted on patients with type I DM indicate considerably higher rates of hypoglycemic episodes, necessitating future clinical trials in this field to reach a definitive conclusion. The efficacy and safety of another once-weekly basal insulin therapeutic alternative, namely insulin icodec, have also been investigated in multiple clinical trials. A 2024 meta-analysis of five clinical trials involving a total of 3,764 patients demonstrated that insulin icodec was superior to daily basal insulin preparations in reducing HbA1c (ETT -0.17%, 95 CI -0.28 to -0.06, p-value=0.003), without statistically significant differences in FPG levels or time spent in the target glycemic range. Nevertheless, once-weekly insulin icodec therapy has led to higher rates of level 1 hypoglycemia without any difference in level 2 or 3 hypoglycemia7. Moreover, it is important to emphasize that approval of insulin icodec was denied by the Food and Drug Administration in 2024, owing to concerns of a higher risk of hypoglycemia compared with insulin degludec and a lack of high-quality evidence for type I DM, as all but one clinical trial of insulin icodec had been conducted in that population. Two out of five RCTs evaluating the efficacy and safety of insulin efsitora have been conducted on patients with type I DM. Currently, there is a lack of head-to-head clinical studies comparing the efficacy or safety of such once-weekly basal insulin preparations. Such an obstacle may be overcome with future large-scale clinical trials or trials comparing both once-weekly preparations head-to-head.

Once-weekly basal insulin therapy offers considerably fewer injections and the potential to improve patient adherence; nevertheless, this novel therapeutic approach is not without major concerns. Fear of needles and pain are major causes of reluctance to initiate insulin therapy by clinicians and patients in clinical practice along with concerns for adverse effects such as hypoglycemia15. A large United States-based database study involving a total of 19.110 adult type 1 DM patients initiating insulin therapy has revealed that 62% of the patients have experienced at least one interruption while 18% of the patients completely discontinued insulin therapy16. Because once-weekly insulin formulations offer a more flexible dosing regimen in terms of timing and the ability to skip doses owing to their pharmacokinetic profiles and fewer subcutaneous injections, they may improve patient adherence. Moreover, the pharmacokinetic profile of once-weekly insulin efsitora therapy may reduce the inter-day variability of glycemic control compared with once-daily basal insulin therapies; however, large-scale clinical studies are needed to confirm this hypothesis.

Clinical and Research Consequences

The establishment of the safety and efficacy of once-weekly subcutaneous basal insulin therapy represents a novel and potentially groundbreaking advance in the management of DM. Such an approach offers superior patient compliance and, therefore, improved glycemic control. Establishment of such improvements sheds on future clinical studies in this field, particularly with insulin efsitora and other once-weekly novel basal insulin therapies.

CONCLUSION

Our meta-analysis, to the best of our knowledge the first in this field, has demonstrated that once-weekly insulin efsitora therapy offers similar glycemic control (assessed via HbA1c reduction) and similar safety (assessed via hypoglycemic episodes) compared to insulin degludec therapy in patients with either type 1 or type 2 DM. Type 1 DM patients, a population with absolute insulin dependence and imbalances in counterregulatory mechanisms, require further focus in future clinical trials, especially with regard to hypoglycemic episodes, as few studies indicate potentially higher rates. The potential concerns that need to be addressed, especially in patients with type 1 DM, include, but are not limited to: (a) variations in the insulin dose delivered to subcutaneous tissue at any given time point, which may induce hyperglycemic or hypoglycemic states; (b) challenges in dose titration following a hypoglycemic episode; (c) difficulties in dose titration during critical illness, surgery, or fasting. Nevertheless, there is a clear need for future large-scale clinical trials to evaluate the role of such once-weekly insulin efsitora therapy in microvascular and macrovascular complications of DM.

Propagation of Velocity Moments for the Magnetized Vlasov-Poisson System with Space-Time Dependent Magnetic Fields
Immanuel Ben-Porat, Antoine Gagnebin, Mikaela Iacobelli, Jonathan Junné

Abstract
. We prove that polynomial velocity moments of solutions to the 2D magnetized Vlasov-Poisson system and the 3D magnetized screened Vlasov-Poisson equation remain finite for all times, provided they are finite initially, even when the external magnetic field B = B ⁡ ( t , x ) B=B(t,x) is space-time dependent. We deduce propagation of regularity, thereby implying the existence of global classical solutions. Moreover, we prove optimal stability estimates in the kinetic-Wasserstein distance on par with the unmagnetised case.



1.
Introduction

In this work, we study propagation of moments for the
magnetized
Vlasov-Poisson equation. The Cauchy problem of the magnetized Vlasov-Poisson equation reads

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f-(\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}+v\wedge B)\cdot\nabla_{v}f=0,\ \rho_{f}(t,x)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,x,v)\ dv\\
f|_{t=0}=f^{\mathrm{in}}.\end{array}\right.

The unknown is a time-dependent, non-negative bounded integrable function
f(t,\cdot,\cdot)\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{v})
and
B=B(t,x)
is a time-dependent vector field

B:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
called the
magnetic field
. The potential
K
is of Coulomb type in 2D and of Yukawa/screened-Coulomb type in 3D. More specifically,
K
is given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle K(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}\pm\frac{1}{2\pi}\log(x)&d=2,\\
\pm\frac{e^{-\kappa\left|x\right|}}{4\pi\left|x\right|}&d=3,\ \kappa>0,\end{array}\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{x}K(x-y)\rho_{f}(t,y)\ dy.

Note that in the case where
\kappa=0
we recover the usual Coulomb potential. In the absence of a magnetic field, i.e., when
B=0
, equation (
1.1
) reduces to the classical Vlasov-Poisson equation

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f-\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}\cdot\nabla_{v}f=0,\ \rho_{f}(t,x)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,x,v)\ dv\\
f|_{t=0}=f^{\mathrm{in}}.\end{array}\right.

Such systems arise naturally in plasma physics and galactic dynamics (see, e.g.
[
11
,
48
,
67
,
70
]
). In this work we mainly focus on plasmas, where
f(t,x,v)
denotes the distribution function of particles at time
t
, position
x
, and velocity
v
. A plasma is an ionized gas formed when a neutral gas is exposed to high temperatures or strong electromagnetic fields. The ionization process produces two species: light, negatively charged electrons and heavy, positively charged ions. These charged particles interact through long-range electromagnetic forces, giving rise to collective effects in the plasma. Because of the large mass difference between electrons and ions, the two species evolve on distinct time scales.

Electrons move much faster than ions. When studying the electron dynamics, it is thus common to assume that the ions are stationary. The Vlasov-Poisson equation (
\kappa=0
) is a fundamental model for the dynamics of electrons in a plasma when collisions are neglected and ions are treated as a fixed background.

From the ions’ point of view, the electrons move much faster, and electron-electron collisions become relevant on the ion timescale. It is therefore natural to assume that the electron distribution has already relaxed to thermal equilibrium, so that the electron density follows a Maxwell-Boltzmann law. By linearizing this equilibrium, we obtain the screened Vlasov-Poisson equation (
\kappa>0
), which provides an effective description of ion dynamics.

In the presence of a magnetic field
B
, the magnetized Vlasov-Poisson system (
1.1
) describes the evolution of a collection of charged particles interacting through the Coulomb or screened-Coulomb potential and subject to the additional influence of the external magnetic field
B
.

The existence and uniqueness theory of (
1.3
) is well understood, both for the cases where
\kappa=0
(corresponding to Coulomb) and
\kappa>0
(corresponding to screened Coulomb): the Vlasov-Poisson system has been the subject of extensive study over the past decades. Many results are now available concerning the existence of global classical and weak solutions under different assumptions on the initial data. The first local-in-time existence result in three dimensions was obtained by Rudolf
[
66
]
. For weak solutions on the whole space, we refer to the work of Arsenev

(see also

For the periodic case, i.e., where the space domain is the flat torus and

Questions of stability and uniqueness are also vast: the epitome of all kinetic stability estimates is the work of Dobrushin
[

The inclusion of a magnetic field introduces new difficulties for what concerns the propagation of velocity moments and the stability estimates. The case of a uniform magnetic field, constant in both time and space, was first studied by Rege

However, the more general question of proving global propagation of velocity moments for the Vlasov–Poisson equation with non-uniform and non-constant magnetic field, remained open.

Beyond moment propagation, other directions of research have focused on spectral and numerical aspects of the magnetized Vlasov–Poisson equation. In particular, Charles, Després, Rege, and Weder analysed the Bernstein-Landau paradox and related spectral phenomena in

In the present work, we investigate the propagation of moments, propagation of regularity and Wasserstein stability in the magnetized setting of equation (
1.1
).

Given a solution
f
of (
1.1
) or (
1.3
) we define the
n
-th velocity moments as

(1.4)

\displaystyle M_{n}(t)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|v\right|^{n}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv

and the
n
-th position moments as

(1.5)

\displaystyle N_{n}(t)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|x\right|^{n}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv.

When we say that
M_{n}(t)

is propagated in time
we mean that there exists a function
\Phi_{n}(t)=\Phi_{n}\left(\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty},M_{n}(0),t\right)
continuous in
t
such that

(1.6)

\displaystyle M_{n}(t)\leq\Phi_{n}(t)\text{ for all }t\in[0,T].

The propagation of velocity moments is a central ingredient in proving existence of classical solutions, and goes back to the work of Lions and Perthame
[
54
]
. Indeed, once we have uniform bounds on the velocity moments
M_{n}
for all times, we can show that the spatial density
\rho_{f}
also remains bounded. Controlling the density then provides bounds on the force field,
\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}
, ensuring that it remains sufficiently regular. When both
f
and
\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}
are controlled in suitable norms, the characteristics of the Vlasov equation stay regular for all time, and consequently leads to the global existence of classical solutions. Extensions and improvements of the work of Lions and Perthame
[
54
]
have been investigated by Pallard in
[
59
]
. A self contained exposition to propagation of velocity moments for potentials with even stronger singularities than Coulomb (local or global) can be found in Lafleche
[
52
,
53
]
, which also studies the quantum analogue of this problem and the semi-classical limit.

As already mentioned, the aim of the current work is to address propagation of moments for the 2D and 3D full space case of the system (
1.1
). Note that in the problem we are considering the magnetic field is external. Propagation of moments for the Vlasov–Maxwell equation, which corresponds to the case where the magnetic field is self-consistent, remains an outstanding open problem in kinetic theory. Nonetheless, several important contributions have significantly advanced our understanding of the Vlasov–Maxwell equation. These include the classical works of Glassey and Strauss in
[
27
]
, Klainerman and Staffilani in
[
50
]
, and Bouchut, Golse and Pallard in
[
10
]
, as well as more recent developments
[
56
,
57
,
61
]
.

In order to state our main result, we need to introduce hypotheses on the external magnetic field
B(t,x)
. Let

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}(t,x)\coloneqq x\wedge B(t,x).

Given
c\in(\frac{7}{5},\frac{3}{2})
let
a=\frac{3}{c}-1
. Suppose that there is some constant
B_{0}>0
such that
\mathbf{b}(t,x)
and

satisfy:

(
B
)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{b}(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\leq B_{0}t^{-a},\quad\text{and}\quad\|B(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\leq B_{0}t^{-a}.

Clearly any smooth compactly supported function will satisfy (
B
). We can now state our first main theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
K
is the kernel as in (
1.2
). Let
0\leq f^{\mathrm{in}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and suppose there exist
\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{in}}_{k}>0
for
k=0,\dots,n
such that

M_{k}(0)\leq\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{in}}_{k}\qquad\text{for all }0\leq k\leq n.

Let
f
be a classical solution to (
1.1
) on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
with initial data
f^{\mathrm{in}}
.
Then,

i)

if
d=2
, there is some
\Phi_{n}(t)=\Phi_{n}\left(\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{1}},\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{in}}_{n},t\right)
, continuous in
t
, such that it holds that

\displaystyle M_{n}(t)\leq\Phi_{n}(t)\ \mbox{for all}\ t\in[0,T].

ii)

if
d=3
, assume in addition that
n\geq 4
, that hypothesis (
B
) holds, and that there exist constants
\mathbf{N}^{\mathrm{in}}_{k}>0
with

N_{k}(0)\leq\mathbf{N}^{\mathrm{in}}_{k}\qquad\text{for all }0\leq k\leq n.

Then, there exists some constant
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(B_{0})>0
such that if

(1.7)

\displaystyle\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{1}}+M_{4}(0)+N_{4}(0)\leq\varepsilon_{0}

then there is some
\Phi_{n}(t)=\Phi_{n}\left(\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{1}},\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{in}}_{n},\mathbf{N}^{\mathrm{in}}_{n},t\right)

\displaystyle M_{n}(t)\leq\Phi_{n}(t)\qquad\text{and}\qquad N_{n}(t)\leq\Phi_{n}(t)\qquad\text{for all }t\in[0,T].

One aspect in which our approach differs from the work of Rege
[
63
]
lies in the fact that we do not appeal to the trajectories in the proof, but instead rely only on functional inequalities, namely on kinetic interpolation inequalities (see Lemma
2.1
). To better understand why this approach is helpful, we recall that a crucial ingredient in the proof of Lions-Perthame is the following representation formula for the density:

(1.8)

\displaystyle\rho_{f}(t,x)=\mathrm{div}_{x}\int_{0}^{t}s\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t-s,x-sv)f(t,x-sv,v)\ dvds+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{\mathrm{in}}(x-tv,v)\ dv.

This formula is obtained by solving explicitly the system of characteristics corresponding to the potential- free Vlasov equation, i.e., solving the system

(1.9)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{cc}\dot{X}(t,x,v)=V(t,x,v),&X(0,x,v)=x,\\
\dot{V}(t,x,v)=0,&V(0,x,v)=v.\end{array}\end{cases}

The solution of (
1.9
) is given by
V(t,x,v)=v
and
X(t,x,v)=x+tv
. Combined with Duhamel’s formula, it is then straightforward to obtain (
1.8
). In the case where the magnetic field is uniform, the corresponding equation for the characteristics is still explicitly solvable, and the argument used by Rege in
[
62
]
relies on this explicit solution. However, in the case of general magnetic fields it is no longer the case that the underlying system of trajectories for the potential-free Vlasov equation is explicitly solvable and therefore it is not clear whether this approach can be carried out. So instead we follow the route taken by Lafleche in
[
53
]
which is based on propagating the Eulerian moments, denoted
L_{n}(t)
, and defined by

\displaystyle L_{n}(t)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv.

In order to properly propagate
L_{n}(t)
globally in time, we need both the decay assumption (
B
), the smallness assumption (
1.7
) and the fast decay at infinity of the potential
K
. Note that the condition (
1.7
) excludes the possibility of taking the initial data to be a probability density. This also explains why there is a substantial difference between the 2D and the 3D case. In 2D, the argument leading to global propagation of moments does not necessitate studying the evolution of
L_{n}(t)
, but only of
M_{n}(t)
. Due to proper cancellations, the magnetic field
B
does not contribute any terms to the time derivative of
M_{n}(t)
. However, in 3D, this argument would yield only short time propagation of
M_{n}(t)
. Long time estimates require an evolution inequality for
L_{n}(t)
. But the time derivative of
L_{n}(t)
, unlike that of
M_{n}(t)
, includes terms contributed by
B
. To put it briefly, for what concerns the moments, in 2D the magnetic field is invisible, whereas it is visible in the 3D case, further complicating the analysis. Once propagation of moments is established, we can deduce propagation of regularity, leading to global existence of smooth solutions. This is the content of our second main theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
hold and assume that

where
\frac{1}{b}+\frac{1}{b^{\prime}}=1
and

(1.10)

\displaystyle b\coloneqq\begin{cases}2,\ d=2,\\

Let
\phi_{0}
be the constant from Theorem

be a function such that
\lambda(t)(1+\left\|\nabla_{x}K\right\|_{b,\infty}\phi_{0})+\dot{\lambda}(t)\leq 0
and let

. Suppose that
\nabla_{x,v}f^{\mathrm{in}}\in L^{p}(e^{w(x,v)})
for some
p\in[2,\infty)
and let
f
be a classical solution of (
1.1
) on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
with initial data
f^{\mathrm{in}}
. Then, there is some
\overline{S}>0
such that it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{x}f(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{p}(e^{\lambda(t)w(x,v)})}^{p}+\left\|\nabla_{v}f(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{p}(e^{\lambda(t)w(x,v)})}^{p}\leq\overline{S}\left(1+\left\|\nabla_{x}f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{L^{p}(e^{w(x,v)})}^{p}+\left\|\nabla_{v}f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{L^{p}(e^{w(x,v)})}^{p}\right)^{e^{\overline{S}t}}\

for all

.

Previous
W_{1}
-stability results for the Vlasov-Poisson equation, such as those of Holding and Miot
[
58
,
41
]
and Crippa et. al
[
17
]
, rely essentially on the second-order structure of the system. In contrast, Loeper’s classical
W_{2}
-approach yields a weaker control, with solutions that are initially
\delta
-close remaining close only up to times of order
\log\left\lvert\log\delta\right\rvert
. The rate of order
\sqrt{\left\lvert\log\delta\right\rvert}
obtained in

for bounded macroscopic densities is regarded as

.

This rigidity was overcome by Iacobelli
[
43
]
, who introduced the
kinetic Wasserstein distance
, a nonlinear modification of Loeper’s functional that captures the anisotropy between position and velocity through the introduction of a time-dependent weight depending itself on the functional;
\pi_{0}
is a
W_{2}
-optimal coupling between the initial solutions of (
1.3
) and
\lambda(t)\coloneqq\sqrt{\left\lvert\log D_{2}(t)\right\rvert}
for the functional

D_{2}(t)=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{2d})^{2}}\lambda(t)\left\lvert X_{1}(t,x,v)-X_{2}(t,y,w)\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert V_{1}(t,x,v)-V_{2}(t,y,w)\right\rvert^{2}\>d\pi_{0}(x,v,y,w).

This nonlinear coupling between position and velocity allows the derivation of sharp stability estimates solely based on first-order structure, and recovers the optimal rate
\sqrt{\left\lvert\log\delta\right\rvert}
in the stronger
W_{2}
-distance, thereby improving substantially the quasi-neutral limit
[
43
]
. In the quantum setting, together with Lafleche
[
45
]
, they enhanced the convergence from the Hartree to the Vlasov–Poisson equation in the semi-classical analog of the 2-Wasserstein distance introduced by Golse, Mouhot and Paul
[
29
]
. The kinetic Wasserstein framework was later also applied to the magnetized Vlasov–Poisson equation by Rege
[
64
]
, and with Junné
[
49
]
, extended to Yudovich macroscopic densities – the largest class for which uniqueness was obtained by the second-order approach of
[
17
]
.

However, the
W_{2}
-stability estimates obtained so far in the magnetized case do not reach this optimal order. The key observation underlying our improvement is that the external magnetic field does not produce work, which allows control of the velocity magnitude independently of
B(t,x)
while its direction is still influenced by the presence of that field through

(1.11)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{cc}\dot{X}(t,x,v)=V(t,x,v),&X(0,x,v)=x,\\
\dot{V}(t,x,v)=-\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,X(t,x,v))-V(t,x,v)\wedge B(t,x),&V(0,x,v)=v.\end{array}\end{cases}

By incorporating this no-work property into the kinetic Wasserstein distance framework, we refine the existing estimates and recover the optimal
\sqrt{\left\lvert\log\delta\right\rvert}
rate for the magnetized Vlasov–Poisson equation. This constitutes our third theorem.

.

, and set

The paper is organized as follows. In §

2.
Propagation of moments

2.1.
Preliminaries

We start by presenting several general estimates which will be central to the forthcoming estimates. We follow closely
[
52
]
. For any function
f:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
let us introduce the velocity moments, Eulerian moments and space moments denoted respectively
M_{n,f},L_{n,f},N_{n,f}
and defined
by

\displaystyle M_{n,f}(t)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|v\right|^{n}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv,

\displaystyle L_{n,f}(t)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|tv-x\right|^{n}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv,

\displaystyle N_{n,f}(t)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|x\right|^{n}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv.

We also introduce the partial velocity moments denoted
\rho_{n,f}(t,x)

and defined by

\displaystyle\rho_{n,f}(t,x)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|v\right|^{n}f(t,x,v)\ dv.

Note that
\rho_{f}=\rho_{0,f}
, where
\rho_{f}
is the density. Whenever there is no ambiguity the subscript
f
will be omitted.

Lemma 2.1
.

(Kinetic
interpolation inequality)
. Suppose that
d\geq 1
, that
0\leq k\leq n

and put
p_{n,k}\coloneqq\frac{n+d}{k+d},\theta_{n,k}\coloneqq\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{n,k}}
.
Then, for any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
M_{n,f}<\infty

it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|v\right|^{k}f(\cdot,v)\ dv\right\|_{L^{p_{n,k}}_{x}}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|v\right|^{n}f(x,v)\ dxdv\right)^{1-\theta_{n,k}}\left\|f\right\|_{L_{x,v}^{\infty}}^{\theta_{n,k}},

where
C=C_{d,k}=\frac{d+k+1}{d+k}
. In particular,

\left\|\rho_{f}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p_{n}}}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|v\right|^{n}f(x,v)\ dxdv\right)^{1-\theta_{n}}\left\|f\right\|_{L_{x,v}^{\infty}}^{\theta_{n}},

where
p_{n}\coloneqq p_{n,0}=\frac{n+d}{d}
and
\theta_{n}\coloneqq\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{n}}
.

We also recall the weak Young inequality (see for instance
[
65
]
).

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(p,q,r)\in(1,\infty)^{3}
with
1+\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}
.
Then there exists
C=C(p,q,r)<1
such that

\left\|f\star g\right\|_{p}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{q,\infty}\left\|g\right\|_{r}.

Recall that conservation of
L^{p}
norms is ensured, i.e., we have the following proposition, the proof of which is standard.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f^{\mathrm{in}}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
1\leq p\leq\infty
and let
f
be a classical solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
f^{\mathrm{in}}
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|f(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}=\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{p}\ \mbox{for all}\ t\in[0,T].

The following estimate will be employed in the 2D case to get global propagation of moments and in the 3D case to get local propagation of moments.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
d=2,3
and let
K
be given by (
1.2
). Let
f
be a classical solution of (
1.1
) with initial data
0\leq f^{\mathrm{in}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, it holds that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{n}(t)\leq C_{\alpha,d}n\left\|\nabla_{x}K\right\|_{b,\infty}\left\|\rho_{n-1}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\alpha}\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\beta}\ \mbox{for all}\ t\in[0,T],

where
C_{\alpha,d}<1
,

b=\begin{cases}2,\ d=2\\
\frac{3}{2},\ d=3\end{cases}

and
\alpha
,
\beta
are such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}=1,\ \frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{b}.

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|v\right|^{n}\left(v\cdot\nabla_{x}f(t,x,v)-(\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,x)+v\wedge B(t,x))\cdot\nabla_{v}f(t,x,v)\right)\ dxdv

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|v\right|^{n}v\cdot\nabla_{x}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|v\right|^{n}\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,x)\cdot\nabla_{v}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv

(2.2)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|v\right|^{n}v\wedge B(t,x)\cdot\nabla_{v}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv.

Integration by parts of the first integral in the right-hand side of (
2.2
) reveals that it is equal to
0
.
Furthermore integration by parts of the third integral shows that it is
0
because

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\mathrm{div}_{v}(\left|v\right|^{n}v\wedge B(t,x))f(t,x,v)\ dxdv=

\displaystyle-n\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|v\right|^{n-2}v\cdot v\wedge B(t,x)f(t,x,v)\ dxdv

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|v\right|^{n}\mathrm{div}_{v}(v\wedge B(t,x))f(t,x,v)\ dxdv=0,

where in the last equality we made use of the observation that
v
is orthogonal to
v\wedge B
and that
\mathrm{div}_{v}(v\wedge B)=0
. Finally, integrating by parts the second term in the right-hand side of (
2.2
) we see it is equal to

\displaystyle-n\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|v\right|^{n-2}v\cdot\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,x)f(t,x,v)\ dxdv.

To conclude, we obtain the identity

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{n}(t)=-n\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|v\right|^{n-2}v\cdot\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,x)f(t,x,v)\ dxdv.

Therefore, we deduce by using Hölder inequality that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{n}(t)\leq

\displaystyle n\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{n-1}(t,x)\left|\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}\right|(t,x)\ dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n\left\|\rho_{n-1}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\alpha}\left\|\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\alpha^{\prime}}\leq C_{\alpha,d}n\left\|\nabla_{x}K\right\|_{b,\infty}\left\|\rho_{n-1}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\alpha}\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\beta},

where in the last inequality we applied Theorem
2.2
with the choice
(\alpha,\beta)\in(1,\infty)^{2}
:

\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}=1,\ 1+\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{b}+\frac{1}{\beta},

or equivalently

\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}=1,\ \frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{b}.

∎

2.2.
The case
d=2

In the
2D
case,
M_{n}(t)
is governed by a sub-linear differential inequality, which enables to get global propagation of moments – this is however not the case for 3D, which requires additional considerations, which will be presented in the next section. The following theorem proves point i. in Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
hold.
Then, there is some function
\Phi_{n}(F,M,t)
, continuous in
t
, such that it holds that

M_{n}(t)\leq\Phi_{n}\left(\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{1}},\mathbf{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{in}},t\right)\ \mbox{for all}\ t\in[0,T].

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
n
, i.e., the induction hypothesis is that there exists a function
\Phi_{n-1}(F,M,t)
, continuous in
t
and increasing in
M
, such that

\displaystyle M_{n-1}(t)\leq\Phi_{n-1}\left(\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty},\mathbf{M}^{in}_{n-1},t\right)\ \mbox{for all}\ t\in[0,T].

By Theorem
2.4
it holds that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{n}(t)

\displaystyle\leq n\left\|\nabla_{x}K\right\|_{2,\infty}\left\|\rho_{n-1}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\alpha_{n}}\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\beta_{n}},

with the choice

\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}^{\prime}}=1,\ \frac{1}{\alpha_{n}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\beta_{n}^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}.

By Lemma
2.1
, if we take
\alpha^{\prime}_{n}=p^{\prime}_{n,n-1}=n+2

and
\theta_{n,n-1}=\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}_{n}}
we get

(2.4)

\left\|\rho_{n-1}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\alpha_{n}}\leq\frac{n+2}{n+1}M_{n}^{1-\theta_{n,n-1}}(t)\left\|f(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}^{\theta_{n,n-1}}=\frac{n+2}{n+1}M_{n}^{1-\theta_{n,n-1}}(t)\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\theta_{n,n-1}},

where the last equality is because
L^{\infty}
norms are conserved by Proposition
2.3
. We now calculate all the exponents involved.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\alpha^{\prime}_{n}=n+2\Longrightarrow\alpha_{n}=\frac{n+2}{n+1}\\
\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\beta_{n}^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}\Longrightarrow\beta_{n}^{\prime}=\frac{2(n+2)}{n}\Longrightarrow\beta_{n}=\frac{2n+4}{n+4}\\
p^{\prime}_{n-1}=\frac{n+1}{n-1}\Longrightarrow p_{n-1}=\frac{n+1}{2},\ p^{\prime}_{n}=\frac{n+2}{n}\Longrightarrow p_{n}=\frac{n+2}{2}.\end{array}\right.

Step 1
.
The base case n=1
. For
n=1
we compute that
\beta_{1}=\frac{6}{5}\leq\frac{3}{2}=p_{1}
and
\theta_{1}=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{1}}=\frac{1}{3}
. Observe also that for the choice
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}
we have the relation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\frac{6}{5}}=\frac{\lambda}{\frac{3}{2}}+\frac{1-\lambda}{1}.

Therefore, it follows by Lebesgue’s interpolation and by Lemma
2.1
that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\beta_{1}}=\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\leq\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\frac{3}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{1}^{\frac{1}{3}}(t)\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{6}}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In addition one directly calculates that
\alpha_{1}=\frac{3}{2}
and
\theta_{1}=\frac{1}{3}
. Inserting this inside (
2.4
) yields the inequality

(2.6)

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\alpha_{1}}=\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\frac{3}{2}}\leq\frac{3}{2}M_{1}^{\frac{2}{3}}(t)\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{3}}.

Substituting (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) inside (
2.3
) we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\left\|\nabla_{x}K\right\|_{2,\infty}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{2}{3}}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{1}(t).

The result now follows by Grönwall’s lemma.

Step 2
.
General n
. Note that
1\leq\beta_{n}\leq p_{n-1}
for all
n\geq 2
(and therefore
p^{\prime}_{n-1}\leq\beta^{\prime}_{n}
). Thus, observing the identity

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta_{n}}=\frac{\frac{p_{n-1}^{\prime}}{\beta^{\prime}_{n}}}{p_{n-1}}+\frac{1-\frac{p^{\prime}_{n-1}}{\beta^{\prime}_{n}}}{1}

we can apply Lebesgue interpolation and Lemma
2.1
in order to find

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\beta_{n}}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p_{n-1}}^{\frac{p^{\prime}_{n-1}}{\beta^{\prime}_{n}}}\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}_{n-1}}{\beta^{\prime}_{n}}}\leq\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{\frac{p^{\prime}_{n-1}}{\beta^{\prime}_{n}}}M_{n-1}^{(1-\theta_{n-1})\frac{p^{\prime}_{n-1}}{\beta^{\prime}_{n}}}(t)\left\|f(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{p^{\prime}_{n-1}\theta_{n-1}}{\beta^{\prime}_{n}}}\left\|f(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}_{n-1}}{\beta_{n}^{\prime}}}

(2.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{\frac{p^{\prime}_{n-`1}}{\beta_{n}^{\prime}}}M_{n-1}^{\left(1-\theta_{n-1}\right)\frac{p_{n-1}^{\prime}}{\beta_{n}^{\prime}}}(t)\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}_{n}}}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{1}^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}_{n-1}}{\beta_{n}^{\prime}}}.

Therefore, using inequalities (
2.4
)
and (
2.7
) in (
2.3
), we find that

(2.8)

\left|\frac{d}{dt}M_{n}(t)\right|\leq C_{n}M_{n-1}^{\left(1-\theta_{n-1}\right)\frac{p_{n-1}^{\prime}}{\beta_{n}^{\prime}}}(t)M_{n}^{1-\theta_{n,n-1}}(t)\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\theta_{n,n-1}+\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}_{n}}}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{1}^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}_{n-1}}{\beta_{n}^{\prime}}},

where the constant
C_{n}
is given by
C_{n}=\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{\frac{p^{\prime}_{n-1}}{\beta^{\prime}_{n}}}\frac{n(n+2)}{(n+1)}\left\|\nabla_{x}K\right\|_{2,\infty}>0
.
By induction hypothesis
there exists a function
\Phi_{n-1}(F,M,t)

continuous in
t
and increasing in
M
such that

\displaystyle M_{n-1}(t)\leq\Phi_{n-1}\left(\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{1}},\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{in}}_{n-1},t\right)\ \mbox{for all}\ t\in[0,T].

Therefore, since
1-\theta_{n,n-1}<1
, Grönwall’s lemma and inequality (
2.8
) yield the induction step.
∎

2.3.
The case
d=3

This section is devoted to the proof of point ii) in Theorem
1.1
. We start by considering propagation of moments on some short time interval.

2.3.1.
Short time propagation of moments of order
n=3,4

Theorem 2.6
.

Suppose that
n=3,4
. Let
d=3
and let
K(x)=\pm\frac{e^{-\left|x\right|}}{\left|x\right|}
be the screened Coulomb potential. Let
f
be a classical solution of (
1.1
) with
0\leq f^{\mathrm{in}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Suppose that
M_{n}(0)\leq\mathbf{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{in}}
for
n=3,4
.
Set
T_{\ast}=\min\left\{\frac{5}{16\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{2}{3}}M_{3}^{\frac{1}{3}}(0)},\frac{9}{8\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|^{\frac{21}{23}}_{\infty}M_{4}^{\frac{1}{7}}(0)M_{3}^{\frac{1}{3}}(0)}\right\}
. Then for all
t\in[0,T_{\ast}]
it holds that

\displaystyle M_{3}(t)\leq 2^{\frac{3}{2}}M_{3}(0),\ M_{4}(t)\leq 2^{7}M_{4}(0).

Proof.

By Theorem
2.4
it holds that

(2.9)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{n}(t)

\displaystyle\leq n\left\|\nabla_{x}K\right\|_{\frac{3}{2},\infty}\left\|\rho_{n-1}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\alpha_{n}}\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\beta_{n}},

with the choice

\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}^{\prime}}=1,\ \frac{1}{\alpha_{n}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\beta_{n}^{\prime}}=\frac{2}{3}.

By Lemma
2.1
, if we take
\alpha_{n}^{\prime}=p^{\prime}_{n,n-1}=n+3
and
\theta_{n,n-1}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}^{\prime}}
we get

(2.10)

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{n-1}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\alpha_{n}}\leq\frac{n+3}{n+2}M_{n}^{1-\theta_{n,n-1}}(t)\left\|f(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}^{\theta_{n,n-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n+3}{n+2}M_{n}^{1-\theta_{n,n-1}}(t)\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\theta_{n,n-1}}.

We now calculate all the exponents involved.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\alpha^{\prime}_{n}=n+3\Longrightarrow\alpha_{n}=\frac{n+3}{n+2}\\
\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\beta_{n}^{\prime}}=\frac{2}{3}\Longrightarrow\beta_{n}^{\prime}=\frac{3n+9}{2n+3}\Longrightarrow\beta_{n}=\frac{3n+9}{n+6}\\
p^{\prime}_{n-1}=\frac{n+2}{n-1}\Longrightarrow p_{n-1}=\frac{n+2}{3},\ p^{\prime}_{n}=\frac{n+3}{n}\Longrightarrow p_{n}=\frac{n+3}{3}.\end{array}\right.

In the case
n=3,4
we have that

p_{n-1}\leq\beta_{n}\leq p_{n}
so that

p_{n}^{\prime}\leq\beta_{n}^{\prime}\leq p^{\prime}_{n-1}
.
Therefore, there exists
\varepsilon_{n}\in(0,1]
such that

(2.14)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta_{n}^{\prime}}=\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{p_{n}^{\prime}}+\frac{1-\varepsilon_{n}}{p_{n-1}^{\prime}},

which, as one readily checks, is equivalent to

(2.15)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta_{n}}=\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{p_{n}}+\frac{1-\varepsilon_{n}}{p_{n-1}}.

Thus, by Lebesgue interpolation and Lemma
2.1

we get the following inequality

(2.16)

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\beta_{n}}\leq\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p_{n}}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p_{n-1}}^{1-\varepsilon_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{3}M_{n-1}^{(1-\varepsilon_{n})(1-\theta_{n-1})}(t)M_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}(1-\theta_{n})}(t)\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{(1-\varepsilon_{n})\theta_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n}\theta_{n}},

where we recall that
\theta_{n}=\frac{1}{p_{n}^{\prime}}
. By (
2.10
) and (
2.16
) it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{n-1}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\alpha_{n}}\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\beta_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4(n+3)}{3(n+2)}M_{n-1}^{(1-\varepsilon_{n})(1-\theta_{n-1})}(t)M_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}(1-\theta_{n})}(t)\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{(1-\varepsilon_{n})\theta_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n}\theta_{n}}M_{n}^{1-\theta_{n,n-1}}(t)\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\theta_{n,n-1}}

(2.17)

\displaystyle=\frac{4(n+3)}{3(n+2)}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{(1-\varepsilon_{n})\theta_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n}\theta_{n}+\theta_{n,n-1}}M_{n-1}^{\Theta_{0,n}}(t)M_{n}^{\Theta_{n}}(t)

where we have set

\Theta_{0,n}\coloneqq(1-\varepsilon_{n})(1-\theta_{n-1}),\ \Theta_{n}\coloneqq 1-\theta_{n,n-1}+\varepsilon_{n}(1-\theta_{n}).

We continue by calculating
\varepsilon_{n}
from (
2.14
). Using (
2.3.1
) we see that

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{n}=\left(\frac{1}{\beta_{n}^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p_{n-1}^{\prime}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{p_{n}^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p_{n-1}^{\prime}}\right)^{-1}=\left(\frac{2n+3}{3n+9}-\frac{n-1}{n+2}\right)\left(\frac{n}{n+3}-\frac{n-1}{n+2}\right)^{-1}.

If
n=3
then we
get

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{3}=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{5}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{5}\right)^{-1}=1

while if
n=4
then

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{4}=\left(\frac{11}{21}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{4}{7}-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-1}=\frac{14}{42}=\frac{1}{3}.

In the case
n=3
we compute that
\theta_{3}=\frac{1}{2}
and
\theta_{3,2}=\frac{1}{6}
so

\displaystyle\Theta_{3}=\frac{5}{6}+\frac{1}{2}=\frac{4}{3},\ \Theta_{0,3}=0

while in the case
n=4
we compute that
\theta_{4}=\frac{4}{7}
and
\theta_{4,3}=\frac{1}{7}
so

\displaystyle\Theta_{4}=\frac{6}{7}+\frac{1}{3}\left(1-\frac{4}{7}\right)=1,\ \Theta_{0,4}=\frac{1}{3}.

In both cases, plugging (
2.17
) inside (
2.9
) implies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{n}(t)

\displaystyle\leq n\left\|\nabla_{x}K\right\|_{\frac{3}{2},\infty}\frac{4(n+3)}{3(n+2)}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{(1-\varepsilon_{n})\theta_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n}\theta_{n}+\theta_{n,n-1}}M_{n-1}^{\Theta_{0,n}}(t)M_{n}^{\Theta_{n}}(t).

For
n=3
, plugging in the values of the parameters yields the inequality

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{3}(t)\leq\frac{24}{5}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{2}{3}}M_{3}^{\frac{4}{3}}(t).

Solving the above inequality yields

\displaystyle M_{3}^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0)-M_{3}^{-\frac{1}{3}}(t)\leq\frac{8}{5}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{2}{3}}t,

which entails

\displaystyle M_{3}(t)\leq\left(\frac{1}{M_{3}^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0)-\frac{8}{5}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{2}{3}}t}\right)^{3}.

Thus given
P>0
we have

T^{\ast}_{3}\geq\frac{5(M^{-\frac{1}{3}}_{3}(0)-P)}{8\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{2}{3}}}
. Choosing

P=\frac{1}{2}M_{3}^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0)
we conclude that

\displaystyle T_{3}^{\ast}\geq\frac{5}{16\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{2}{3}}M_{3}^{\frac{1}{3}}(0)}

and

(2.18)

\displaystyle t\in[0,T_{3}^{\ast}]\Rightarrow M_{3}(t)\leq 2^{3}M_{3}(0).

For
n=4
, plugging the values of the parameters
and utilizing (
2.18
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{4}(t)\leq\frac{14}{9}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{23}{21}}M_{3}^{\frac{1}{3}}(t)M_{4}^{\frac{8}{7}}(t)\leq\frac{28}{9}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{23}{21}}M_{3}^{\frac{1}{3}}(0)M_{4}^{\frac{8}{7}}(t),

which can be written as

\displaystyle-7\frac{d}{dt}M_{4}^{-\frac{1}{7}}(t)\leq\frac{28}{9}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{21}{23}}M_{3}^{\frac{1}{3}}(0)\Rightarrow-\frac{d}{dt}M_{4}^{-\frac{1}{7}}(t)\leq\frac{4}{9}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{21}{23}}M_{3}^{\frac{1}{3}}(0).

Solving the above inequality yields

\displaystyle M_{4}(t)\leq\frac{1}{\left(M_{4}^{-\frac{1}{7}}(0)-\frac{4}{9}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{21}{23}}M_{3}^{\frac{1}{3}}(0)t\right)^{7}}.

Thus, if we take
T_{\ast}=\min\{T_{3}^{\ast},T_{4}^{\ast}\}=\min\left\{\frac{5}{16\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{2}{3}}M_{3}^{\frac{1}{3}}(0)},\frac{9}{8\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|^{\frac{21}{23}}_{\infty}M_{4}^{\frac{1}{7}}(0)M_{3}^{\frac{1}{3}}(0)}\right\}

we can ensure that for all
t\in[0,T_{\ast}]
it holds that

\displaystyle M_{3}(t)\leq 2^{3}M_{3}(0),\ M_{4}(t)\leq 2^{7}M_{4}(0).

∎

Remark 2.7
.

The short time estimate can be obtained in the same manner illustrated above when
K
is the Coulomb potential. The use of the screened Coulomb potential is for the long time estimate.

We can easily deduce short time propagation of position moments from propagation of velocity moments.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
2.6
hold and let
T_{\ast}
be as in the conclusion of Theorem
2.6
. Then, it holds that

\displaystyle N_{n}(t)\leq(N_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}(0)+\overline{M}_{n}t)^{n}\ \mbox{for all}\ t\in[0,T_{\ast}],

where
\overline{M}_{3}=2^{\frac{3}{2}}M_{3}(0)
and
\overline{M}_{4}=2^{7}M_{4}(0)
.

Proof.

Integrating by parts and using
\mathrm{div}_{v}(v\wedge B)=0
we see that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}N_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x\right|^{n}\left(v\cdot\nabla_{x}f(t,x,v)-(v\wedge B(t,x)+\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,x))\cdot\nabla_{v}f(t,x,v)\right)\ dxdv

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x\right|^{n}v\cdot\nabla_{x}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv

(2.19)

\displaystyle=n\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x\right|^{n-2}x\cdot vf(t,x,v)\ dxdv.

Therefore it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}N_{n}(t)

\displaystyle\leq n\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x\right|^{n-1}\left|v\right|f(t,x,v)\ dxdv

\displaystyle\leq n\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x\right|^{n}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|v\right|^{n}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq nN_{n}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}(t)M_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}(t).

By Theorem
2.6
it holds that

\displaystyle M_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}(t)\leq\overline{M}_{n}\ \mbox{for all}\ t\in[0,T_{\ast}].

Consequently we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}N_{n}(t)\leq n\overline{M}_{n}N_{n}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}(t),

which implies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}N_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}(t)\leq\overline{M}_{n}.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle N_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}(t)-N_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}(0)\leq\overline{M}_{n}t,

or equivalently

\displaystyle N_{n}(t)\leq(N_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}(0)+\overline{M}_{n}t)^{n}.

∎

2.3.2.
Long time propagation of moments of order
4

In variance with the 2D case, to establish propagation of moments globally, more care is needed. The key additional component is to first propagate the Eulerian moments which we recall are defined by

(2.20)

\displaystyle L_{n}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv.

This approach is inspired by
[
53
]
, which in turn draws from the methods of
[
4
]

and
[
13
]
. To achieve this necessitates imposing additional decay assumptions (
B
) on the magnetic field
B
which are redundant in the 2D case. These assumptions are responsible for creating the desired dispersion inequality.
In addition it is important to note the potential corresponds to taking the screened Coulomb potential
K(x)=\pm\frac{e^{-\kappa\left|x\right|}}{\left|x\right|}
.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
d=3
,
n=4
,
T>0
and let
K(x)=\pm\frac{e^{-\kappa\left|x\right|}}{\left|x\right|}
. Let the assumption of Theorem
1.1
hold.
Then, there is some continuous in time function
\Phi(t)
such that for all
t\in[0,T]
it holds that

\displaystyle L_{4}(t)\leq\Phi(t).

Let us first explain how global propagation of
M_{4}(t)
follows from the global propagation of
L_{4}(t)
as stated in Theorem
2.9
together with the short time estimate stated in Theorem
2.6
.

Corollary 2.10
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
2.9
is a continuous in time function
\Phi(t)=\Phi(t,\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty},\mathbf{N}^{\mathrm{in}}_{4},\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{in}}_{4})
such that

\displaystyle N_{4}(t)\leq\Phi(t),\ M_{4}(t)\leq\Phi(t)\ \mbox{for all}\ t\in[0,T].

Proof.

In the following calculation we always take
n=4
. We claim that
N_{n}(t)
is globally propagated, from which global propagation of
M_{n}(t)
would follow directly. We compute the time derivative of
N_{n}(t)
. Integrating by parts we see that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}N_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x\right|^{n}\left(v\cdot\nabla_{x}f(t,x,v)-(v\wedge B(t,x)+\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,x))\cdot\nabla_{v}f(t,x,v)\right)\ dxdv

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x\right|^{n}v\cdot\nabla_{x}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv

\displaystyle=n\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x\right|^{n-2}x\cdot vf(t,x,v)\ dxdv

(2.21)

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x\right|^{n-2}x\cdot(tv-x)f(t,x,v)\ dxdv+\frac{n}{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x\right|^{n}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv.

By Corollary
2.8
there is some
T_{\ast}>0
such that

(2.22)

\displaystyle N_{n}(t)\leq(N_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}(0)+\overline{M}_{n}t)^{n}\ \mbox{for all $t\in[0,T_{\ast}]$}.

For all
t\geq T_{\ast}
, the first term in the right-hand side of (
2.21
) is bounded by

(2.23)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\left((n-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x\right|^{n}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv\right)\leq\frac{n}{T_{\ast}}\left(N_{n}(t)+{L}_{n}(t)\right).

In addition, by Theorem
2.9
there is some
\Phi(t)=\Phi(t,\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty},\mathbf{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{in}})
such that

\displaystyle L_{n}(t)\leq\Phi(t).

Hence, it follows from (
2.21
) and (
2.23
) that for all
t\geq T_{\ast}
it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}N_{n}(t)\leq\frac{2n}{T_{\ast}}\left(N_{n}(t)+L_{n}(t)\right)\leq\frac{2n}{T_{\ast}}(N_{n}(t)+\Phi(t)),

and hence

\displaystyle N_{n}(t)\leq N_{n}(T_{\ast})+\frac{2n}{T_{\ast}}\int_{T_{\ast}}^{t}N_{n}(\tau)+\Phi(\tau)\ d\tau.

By Grönwall’s lemma it follows that for all
t\in[T_{\ast},T]
it holds that

\displaystyle N_{n}(t)\leq\Psi(t)\ \mbox{for some continuous in time}\ \Psi(t).

Together with (
2.22
), this proves that
N_{n}(t)
propagates on
[0,T]
. The estimate for
M_{n}(t)
now follows by observing that for all
t\geq T_{\ast}

\displaystyle M_{n}(t)\leq\frac{2^{n-1}}{t^{n}}\left(L_{n}(t)+N_{n}(t)\right)\leq\frac{2^{n-1}}{T_{\ast}^{n}}\left(L_{n}(t)+N_{n}(t)\right).

That
M_{n}(t)
is propagated for
t\in[0,T_{\ast})
follows again from Theorem
2.6
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.9
. In the following calculation we always take
n=4
. The places in which we use the specific numerical value of
n
are indicated.

Step 1
. (Calculation of
\frac{d}{dt}L_{n}(t)
). We compute
\frac{d}{dt}L_{n}(t)
.
We have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}L_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n}\partial_{t}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv

\displaystyle-n\int_{\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n-2}(x-tv)\cdot vf(t,x,v)\ dxdv

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n}v\cdot\nabla_{x}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n}(\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,x)+v\wedge B(t,x))\nabla_{v}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv

(2.24)

\displaystyle-n\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n-2}(x-tv)\cdot vf(t,x,v)\ dxdv.

Integration by parts shows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n}v\wedge B(t,x)\nabla_{v}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\mathrm{div}_{v}\left(\left|x-tv\right|^{n}v\wedge B(t,x)\right)f(t,x,v)\ dxdv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n}\mathrm{div}_{v}(v\wedge B(t,x))f(t,x,v)\ dxdv

\displaystyle+nt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n-2}(x-tv)v\wedge B(t,x)f(t,x,v)\ dxdv

(2.25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle nt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n-2}(x-tv)v\wedge B(t,x)f(t,x,v)\ dxdv,

where we used that
\mathrm{div}_{v}(v\wedge B)=0
in the last equation. Note that this is the point where the magnetic field contributes a non-trivial term.
Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}

\displaystyle\left|x-tv\right|^{n}\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,x)\nabla_{v}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv

(2.26)

\displaystyle=nt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n-2}\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,x)\cdot(x-tv)f(t,x,v)\ dxdv.{}

Also, note that

(2.27)

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n}v\cdot\nabla_{x}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv=n\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n-2}(x-tv)\cdot vf(t,x,v)\ dxdv.

Substituting (
2.25
)-(
2.27
) inside (
2.24
) we infer that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}L_{n}(t)=

\displaystyle nt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n-2}\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,x)\cdot(x-tv)f(t,x,v)\ dxdv

\displaystyle+nt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n-2}(x-tv)v\wedge B(t,x)f(t,x,v)\ dxdv\coloneqq\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2}.

We proceed by estimating
\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}
separately.

Step 2
. (Estimate on
\mathcal{L}_{2}
). Note that

\displaystyle tv\wedge B(t,x)

\displaystyle=(tv-x)\wedge B(t,x)+x\wedge B(t,x)=(tv-x)\wedge B(t,x)+\mathbf{b}(t,x).

So we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}=

\displaystyle-n\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n-2}(x-tv)\cdot(x-tv)\wedge B(t,x)f(t,x,v)\ dxdv

(2.28)

\displaystyle+n\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n-2}\mathbf{b}(t,x)\cdot(x-tv)f(t,x,v)\ dxdv.

The first term in (
2.28
) is bounded by

(2.29)

\displaystyle n\left\|B(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv=n\left\|B(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}L_{n}(t).

By means of Hölder’s inequality the second term in (
2.28
) is bounded by

\displaystyle n\left\|\mathbf{b}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

(2.30)

\displaystyle=n\left\|\mathbf{b}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{1}L_{n}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}(t).

Thus, putting (
2.29
) and (
2.30
) inside (
2.28
) it follows that

(2.31)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}\leq n\left\|B(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}L_{n}(t)+n\left\|\mathbf{b}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{1}L_{n}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}(t).

Step 3
. (Estimate on
\mathcal{L}_{1}
).
We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}\leq

\displaystyle nt\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|x-tv\right|^{n-1}f(t,x,v)\ dv\right\|_{L^{p}_{x}}\left\|\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle nt^{n}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\frac{x}{t}-v\right|^{n-1}f(t,x,v)\ dv\right\|_{L^{p}_{x}}\left\|\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p^{\prime}}

(2.32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle nt^{n}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|v\right|^{n-1}f(t,x,v+\frac{x}{t})\ dv\right\|_{L^{p}_{x}}\left\|\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p^{\prime}}.

Note that
K
verifies
\nabla_{x}K\in L^{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for all
c\in[1,\frac{3}{2})
. Choosing
q
such that

(2.33)

\displaystyle 1+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{c}+\frac{1}{q}\,\mbox{or equivalently}\ \frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{c}

we have that

(2.34)

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq\left\|\nabla_{x}K\right\|_{c}\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{q}.

Since
n=4
it follows that
\frac{n+3}{n+1}=\frac{7}{5}<\frac{3}{2}
and we may pick some
c\in(\frac{7}{5},\frac{3}{2})
.
Note that

p_{n,n-1}=(n+3)^{\prime}=\frac{n+3}{n+2}
and
p_{n}=\left(\frac{n+3}{n}\right)^{\prime}=\frac{n+3}{3}
and so

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{n,n-1}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{p_{n}^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{n+3}+\frac{n}{n+3}=\frac{n+1}{n+3}=\frac{5}{7}\geq\frac{1}{c}.

Consequently we can choose
p\leq p_{n,n-1}
and
1<q\leq p_{n}
such that (
2.33
) holds. We have by interpolation

(2.35)

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{q}\leq\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}^{1-\frac{p_{n}^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p_{n}}^{\frac{p_{n}^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}=\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{1}^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}_{n}}{q^{\prime}}}\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p_{n}}^{\frac{p_{n}^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}.

Therefore, inserting (
2.34
) and (
2.35
) inside (
2.32
) we get (recall that
a=\frac{3}{c}-1>1
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq nt^{n}\left\|\nabla_{x}K\right\|_{c}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(t,x,v+\frac{x}{t})\left|v\right|^{n-1}\ dv\right\|_{L^{p}_{x}}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{1}^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}_{n}}{q^{\prime}}}\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p_{n}}^{\frac{p_{n}^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla_{x}K\right\|_{c}t^{n}\left\|f(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{c}}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{1}^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}_{n}}{q^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}f(t,x,v+\frac{x}{t})\left|v\right|^{n}dxdv\right)^{1+\frac{a}{n}}

\displaystyle=C\left\|\nabla_{x}K\right\|_{c}t^{n}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{c}}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{1}^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}_{n}}{q^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}f(t,x,v)\left|v-\frac{x}{t}\right|^{n}dxdv\right)^{1+\frac{a}{n}}

(2.36)

\displaystyle=C\left\|\nabla_{x}K\right\|_{c}t^{-a}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{c}}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{1}^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}_{n}}{q^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}f(t,x,v)\left|tv-x\right|^{n}dxdv\right)^{1+\frac{a}{n}}=\frac{\mathcal{C}L_{n}^{1+\frac{a}{n}}(t)}{t^{a}}

where
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}\left(c,\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty},\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{1}\right)\coloneqq C\left\|\nabla_{x}K\right\|_{c}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{c}}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{1}^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}_{n}}{q^{\prime}}}
.

Step 4
. (Conclusion) Gathering inequalities (
2.31
) and (
2.36
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}L_{n}(t)\leq n\left\|B(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}L_{n}(t)+n\left\|\mathbf{b}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{1}L_{n}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}(t)+\frac{\mathcal{C}L_{n}^{1+\frac{a}{n}}(t)}{t^{a}}.

Putting
\ell_{n}(t)\coloneqq 1+L_{n}(t)
the latter inequality entails

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\ell_{n}(t)\leq n\left\|B(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\ell_{n}(t)+n\left\|\mathbf{b}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{1}\ell_{n}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}(t)+\frac{\mathcal{C}\ell_{n}^{1+\frac{a}{n}}(t)}{t^{a}}.

By hypothesis (
B
) we have

\displaystyle\left\|B(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{B_{0}}{t^{a}}

and therefore the first term is bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{B_{0}n}{t^{a}}\ell_{n}(t)\leq\frac{B_{0}n}{t^{a}}\ell_{n}^{1+\frac{a}{n}}(t).

By hypothesis (
B
) we also have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{b}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{B_{0}}{t^{a}}

and therefore the second term is bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{B_{0}n\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{1}}{t^{a}}\ell_{n}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}(t)\leq\frac{B_{0}n\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{1}\ell_{n}^{1+\frac{a}{n}}(t)}{t^{a}}.

Thus we deduce the inequality

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\ell_{n}(t)\leq\left(nB_{0}\left(1+\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{1}\right)+\mathcal{C}\right)\frac{\ell_{n}^{1+\frac{a}{n}}(t)}{t^{a}}.

Dividing by
\ell_{n}^{1+\frac{a}{n}}(t)
implies the inequality

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{dt}\ell_{n}^{-\frac{a}{n}}(t)\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}{t^{a}},

where
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\coloneqq\frac{a}{n}\left(nB_{0}(1+\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{1})+\mathcal{C}\right)
.
Integrating from
T_{\ast}
to
t
gives

\displaystyle\ell^{-\frac{a}{n}}_{n}(T_{\ast})-\ell^{-\frac{a}{n}}_{n}(t)\leq\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\left(\frac{T_{\ast}^{-(a-1)}}{a-1}-\frac{t^{-(a-1)}}{a-1}\right).

Thus we conclude that

(2.37)

\displaystyle\ell^{-\frac{a}{n}}_{n}(t)\geq\ell^{-\frac{a}{n}}_{n}(T_{\ast})-\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\left(\frac{T_{\ast}^{-(a-1)}}{a-1}-\frac{t^{-(a-1)}}{a-1}\right).

So in order to get a bound for
\ell_{n}(t)
we wish to ensure that

(2.38)

\displaystyle\ell_{n}^{-\frac{a}{n}}(T_{\ast})-\frac{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}T_{\ast}^{-(a-1)}}{a-1}>0\ \mbox{or equivalently}\ T_{\ast}^{a+1}>\frac{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}{a-1}\ell_{n}^{\frac{a}{n}}(T_{\ast}).

Note that we have

\displaystyle\ell_{n}^{\frac{a}{n}}(T_{\ast})

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+2^{n-1}T_{\ast}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|v\right|^{n}f(T_{\ast},x,v)\ dxdv+2^{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|x\right|^{n}f(T_{\ast},x,v)\ dxdv\right)^{\frac{a}{n}}

(2.39)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{a(n-1)}{n}}\left(1+T_{\ast}^{a}M_{n}^{\frac{a}{n}}(T_{\ast})+N_{n}^{\frac{a}{n}}(T_{\ast})\right).

In view of (
2.39
), to fulfil (
2.38
) it suffices to demand:

(a)

\displaystyle T_{\ast}^{a+1}\geq\frac{2^{\frac{a(n-1)}{n}}3\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}{a-1}T_{\ast}^{a}M_{n}^{\frac{a}{n}}(T_{\ast})

(b)

\displaystyle T_{\ast}^{a+1}\geq\frac{2^{\frac{a(n-1)}{n}}3\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}{a-1}N_{n}^{\frac{a}{n}}(T_{\ast})

(c)

\displaystyle T_{\ast}^{a+1}\geq\frac{2^{\frac{a(n-1)}{n}}3\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}{a-1}.

By Theorem
2.6
inequality (a) will be implied from

(2.40)

\displaystyle T_{\ast}\geq\frac{2^{\frac{7a(n-1)}{n}}3\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}{a-1}M_{4}^{\frac{a}{4}}(0).

By Corollary
2.8
inequality (b) will be implied by

(2.41)

\displaystyle T_{\ast}^{a+1}\geq\left(\frac{2^{\frac{a(n-1)}{n}}3\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}{a-1}\right)(N_{4}^{\frac{1}{4}}(0)+2^{7}M_{4}(0)T_{\ast})^{a}.

As for inequality (c), it will be implied by

(2.42)

\displaystyle T_{\ast}\geq\left(\frac{2^{\frac{a(n-1)}{n}}3\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}{a-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{a+1}}.

Using the explicit formula for
T_{\ast}
we see that (
2.40
), (
2.41
) and (
2.42
) are exactly the smallness assumption (
1.7
).
∎

2.3.3.
Long time estimate for moments of arbitrary order

We can now conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
ii., by verifying global propagation moments of order
\geq 5
. We first prove the result for
n=5
and then induct on
n
. We have that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{n}(t)=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|v\right|^{n}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv

\displaystyle=-n\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|v\right|^{n-2}v\cdot\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,x)f(t,x,v)\ dxdv

\displaystyle\leq n\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|v\right|^{n-1}\left|\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}\right|(t,x)f(t,x,v)\ dxdv.

For
n=5
we obtain

(2.43)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{5}(t)\leq 5\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|v\right|^{4}\left|\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,x)\right|f(t,x,v)\ dxdv\leq 5\left\|\rho_{4}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\alpha}\left\|\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\alpha^{\prime}}.

Now if we choose
\alpha^{\prime}=p^{\prime}_{5,4}=5(1+\frac{3}{5})=8,\theta_{5,4}=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{5,4}}=\frac{1}{8}
then by Lemma
2.1
we get

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{4}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\alpha}=\left\|\rho_{4}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\frac{8}{7}}\leq\frac{8}{7}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|v\right|^{5}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv\right)^{\frac{7}{8}}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{8}}=\frac{8}{7}M_{5}^{\frac{7}{8}}(t)\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{8}}.

For this choice we also have

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\alpha^{\prime}}=\left\|\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{8}\leq\left\|\nabla_{x}K\right\|_{\frac{3}{2},\infty}\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\frac{21}{10}}.

By Lemma
2.1
there holds the inequality

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\frac{21}{10}}\leq\frac{4}{3}M_{\frac{33}{10}}^{\frac{10}{21}}(t)\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{11}{21}}.

Therefore, thanks to Corollary
2.10
we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\frac{21}{10}}\leq\Phi(t)

for some
\Phi(t)=\Phi(t)=\Phi(t,\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty},\mathbf{N}^{\mathrm{in}}_{4},\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{in}}_{4})
. Putting the last inequality inside (
2.43
) we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{5}(t)\leq\Phi(t)M_{5}^{\frac{7}{8}}(t)\ \mbox{for all}\ t\in[0,T].

Solving the above inequality shows that

\displaystyle M_{5}(t)\leq\widetilde{\Phi}\left(t,\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty},\mathbf{M}_{5}^{\mathrm{in}},\mathbf{N}^{\mathrm{in}}_{5}\right),

We proceed by induction on

Now we choose

so that

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{n-1}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\alpha_{n}}\leq\frac{n+3}{n+2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|v\right|^{n}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv\right)^{\frac{n+2}{n+3}}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{n+3}}=\frac{n+3}{n+2}M_{n}^{\frac{n+2}{n+3}}(t)\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{n+3}}.

In addition, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\alpha_{n}^{\prime}}=\left\|\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{n+3}\leq\left\|\nabla_{x}K\right\|_{\frac{3}{2},\infty}\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\frac{3(n+3)}{n+6}}.

We search for

It follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\frac{3(n+3)}{n+6}}\leq\frac{4}{3}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|v\right|^{k}f(t,x,v)\ dxdv\right)^{1-\theta_{k}}\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\theta_{n}}=\frac{4}{3}M_{k}^{1-\theta_{k}}(t)\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\theta_{n}}.

Substituting (

2.46

2.44

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{n}(t)\leq\frac{4n(n+3)}{3(n+2)}M_{k}^{1-\theta_{k}}(t)M_{n}^{\frac{n+2}{n+3}}(t)\left\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{n+3}+\theta_{n}}.

Since

and since

Corollary 3.1

Proof.

By Lemma

First, we recognize that

\displaystyle-p

\left\|\nabla_{v}f\right\|_{L^{p}(e^{\lambda(t)w(x,v)})}^{p}
). We compute

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{\lambda(t)w(x,v)}\left|\nabla_{v}f\right|^{p}\ dxdv

(3.17)

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{\lambda(t)w(x,v)}v\nabla_{x}\left|\nabla_{v}f\right|^{p}\ dxdv

(3.18)

(3.19)

\displaystyle J_{4}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{\lambda(t)w(x,v)}\nabla_{v}\left|\nabla_{v}f\right|^{p}v\wedge B\ dxdv

(3.20)

(3.21)

Furthermore, one has

(3.22)

\displaystyle J_{3}\leq p\left\|B\right\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{\lambda(t)w(x,v)}\left|\nabla_{v}f\right|^{p}\ dxdv.

As for

\displaystyle J_{5}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|\nabla_{v}f\right|^{p}e^{\lambda(t)w(x,v)}\lambda(t)\nabla_{v}w(x,v)\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}\ dxdv

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{\lambda(t)w(x,v)}\left|\nabla_{v}f\right|^{p}\lambda(t)w(x,v)\ dxdv

(3.25)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla_{x}K\right\|_{b,\infty}\left\|\rho_{f}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{b^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{\lambda(t)w(x,v)}\left|\nabla_{v}f\right|^{p}\lambda(t)w(x,v)\ dxdv

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{0}\left\|\nabla_{x}K\right\|_{b,\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{\lambda(t)w(x,v)}\left|\nabla_{v}f\right|^{p}\lambda(t)w(x,v)\ dxdv.

Since by assumption
(1+\phi_{0}\left\|\nabla_{x}K\right\|_{b,\infty})\lambda(t)+\dot{\lambda}(t)\leq 0

\displaystyle J_{2}+J_{5}+J_{6}\leq 0.

To conclude, from (

(3.28)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\nabla_{v}f\right\|_{L^{p}(e^{\lambda(t)w(x,v)})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq(p-1+\left\|B\right\|_{\infty}p)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{\lambda(t)w(x,v)}\left|\nabla_{v}f\right|^{p}\ dxdv

(3.29)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{\lambda(t)w(x,v)}\left|\nabla_{x}f\right|^{p}\ dxdv.

Gathering the inequalities (

) and (

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}S(t)\leq\overline{S}\left(\log(1+S(t))\right)(1+S(t))\ \mbox{for some constant}\ \overline{S}>0.

Solving the above inequality gives

\displaystyle S(t)\leq\overline{S}(1+S(0))^{e^{\overline{S}t}}\ \mbox{for all}\ t\in[0,T]

as wanted.
∎

4.
Improved stability estimate

Magnetic fields cannot do work; their contribution to the Lorentz force is always orthogonal to the velocity.

Lemma 4.1
.

(No‑work identity)

Let
(X,V)
be a characteristic (
1.11
) of (
1.1
). For every
k>0
and
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
, we have

\frac{d}{dt}|V(t)|^{k}=-k|V(t)|^{k-2}\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(t,X(t))\cdot V(t),\qquad|V(t)|\leq|V(s)|+\int_{s}^{t}|\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f}(\tau,X(\tau))|\,d\tau.

Proof.

Differentiate
|V|^{k}
and note that the term
V\cdot(V\wedge B)
vanishes.
Integrating the case
k=1
yields the bound.
∎

Let
f_{1},f_{2}\geq 0
be two solutions of the magnetized Vlasov-Poisson equation (
1.1
) with common mass
1
. For
p\geq 1
and
\lambda>0
we recall the
p
-kinetic Wasserstein distance introduced in
[
43
]
;

W^{p}_{\lambda,p}\left(\mu,\nu\right)\coloneqq\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{2d})^{2}}\lambda|x-y|^{p}+|v-w|^{p}\>d\pi.

Setting
\lambda=1
gives the usual
W_{p}(\mu,\nu)
.

Let
Z_{i}\coloneqq(X_{i},V_{i})
be the characteristics (
1.11
) of
f_{i}
,
i=1,2
, starting from
(x,v)
.
Let
\pi_{0}
be an optimal coupling for
W_{p}(f_{1}^{\mathrm{in}},f_{2}^{\mathrm{in}})
and
\pi_{t}\coloneqq(\pi_{0})_{\#}(Z_{1}(t),Z_{2}(t))
. Define

\displaystyle D_{p}(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{2d})^{2}}\lambda(t)|X_{1}(t;x,v)-X_{2}(t;y,w)|^{p}+|V_{1}(t;x,v)-V_{2}(t;y,w)|^{p}\>d\pi_{0}(x,v,y,w)

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{2d})^{2}}\lambda(t)|x-y|^{p}+|v-w|^{p}\>d\pi_{t}(x,v,y,w)

where we set
\lambda(t)\coloneqq\left\lvert\log D_{p}(t)\right\rvert^{\tfrac{p}{2}}
. We recall that
D_{p}
controls the
p
-Wasserstein distance between the two solutions;

(4.1)

W_{p}^{p}\left(\rho_{f_{1}},\rho_{f_{2}}\right)\leq\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda},\quad W_{p}^{p}\left(f_{1},f_{2}\right)\leq D_{p}.

Proof.

(
of Theorem
1.3
).
Differentiating
D_{p}
with respect to time yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\dot{D}_{p}=

\displaystyle\>\frac{1}{p}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{2d})^{2}}\dot{\lambda}\left\lvert X_{1}-X_{2}\right\rvert^{p}\>d\pi_{0}

\displaystyle+\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{2d})^{2}}\lambda\left\lvert X_{1}-X_{2}\right\rvert^{p-2}(X_{1}-X_{2})\cdot(V_{1}-V_{2})\>d\pi_{0}

\displaystyle+\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{2d})^{2}}\left\lvert V_{1}-V_{2}\right\rvert^{p-2}(V_{1}-V_{2})\cdot(-\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f_{1}}+\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f_{2}})\>d\pi_{0}

\displaystyle+\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{2d})^{2}}\left\lvert V_{1}-V_{2}\right\rvert^{p-2}(V_{1}-V_{2})\cdot\left[V_{2}\wedge\left(B(X_{2})-B(X_{1})\right)\right]\>d\pi_{0}\coloneqq I_{1}+\cdots+I_{4}.

I_{1}
is special in the sense that it is the only term containing a time derivative of
\lambda
, which, we recall, depends on
D_{p}
itself; thus we will estimate it later. The bounds on
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
follow exactly the lines of the proof of
[
44
, Theorem 1.11]
.

For
I_{2}
, Hölder’s inequality with respect to the measure
\pi_{0}
directly yields

(4.2)

I_{2}(t)\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{p}}(t)D_{p}(t).

For
I_{3}
, one splits the difference of force fields as

\left\lvert\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f_{1}}(X_{1})-\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f_{2}}(X_{2})\right\rvert\\
\leq\left\lvert\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f_{1}}(X_{1})-\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f_{1}}(X_{2})\right\rvert+\left\lvert\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f_{1}}(X_{2})-\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f_{2}}(X_{2})\right\rvert.

The first term uses the
\log
-Lipschitz regularity of the force field satisfying a (screened) Poisson equation with bounded density (straightforward modification of e.g.
[
55
, Lemma 3.1]
and
[
39

Lemma 4.2
.

There is a constant
C>0
such that

\left\lVert\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f_{i}}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C\left(1+\left\lVert\rho_{f_{i}}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}\right)

and for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with

, it holds

\left\lvert\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f_{i}}(t,x)-\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f_{i}}(t,y)\right\rvert\leq C\left(1+\left\lVert\rho_{f_{i}}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}\right)\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\log\left(\frac{4\sqrt{3}}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}\right).

The second term corresponds to the
L^{p}
-estimate on the difference of force fields for the case
1<p<+\infty
.

Proposition 4.3
.

There is a constant
C_{\mathrm{HW}}>0
that only depends on
1<p<+\infty
such that

\left\lVert\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f_{1}}(t)-\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f_{2}}(t)\right\rVert_{p}\leq C_{\mathrm{HW}}\max\left\{\left\lVert\rho_{f_{1}}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty},\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}W_{p}\left(\rho_{f_{1}}(t),\rho_{f_{2}}(t)\right).

Proof.

The case
d=2
with the Coulomb potential is the content of
[
44
, Proposition 1.8]
. It remains to deal with the case
d=3
corresponding to the Yukawa potential (or Coulomb when
\kappa=0
); that is, the Green function for the screened Poisson equation
\pm(\Delta-\kappa^{2})K\star\rho_{f}=\rho_{f}
. Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a test function and set
1/p+1/p^{\prime}\coloneqq 1
. By duality of
L^{p}
-norm, for every
1\leq j\leq 3
,

\left\lVert\partial_{x_{j}}K\star\left(\rho_{f_{1}}-\rho_{f_{2}}\right)\right\rVert_{p}=\sup_{\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,:\,\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\rho_{f_{1}}-\rho_{f_{2}}\right)\partial_{x_{j}}K\star\varphi\>dx.

We denote by
\phi_{j}\coloneqq\partial_{x_{j}}K\star\varphi
, and Calderon–Zygmund’s inequality
[
26
, Theorem II.11.4]
yields
\left\lVert\nabla\phi_{j}\right\rVert_{p^{\prime}}\leq\widetilde{C_{\mathrm{HW}}}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{p^{\prime}}
for some constant
\widetilde{C_{\mathrm{HW}}}>0
that only depends on
1<p<+\infty
. Replacing the supremum over the larger set

\left\{\phi\in\dot{W}^{1,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,:\,\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{p^{\prime}}/\widetilde{C_{\mathrm{HW}}}\leq 1\right\}\supset\left\{\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,:\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1\right\},

we obtain, by definition of dual homogeneous Sobolev norm,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\partial_{x_{j}}K\star\left(\rho_{f_{1}}(t)-\rho_{f_{2}}(t)\right)\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C_{\mathrm{HW}}}\sup_{\phi\in\dot{W}^{1,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,:\,\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\rho_{f_{2}}(t)-\rho_{f_{1}}(t)\right)\nabla\phi\>dx

\displaystyle\coloneqq\widetilde{C_{\mathrm{HW}}}\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}(t)-\rho_{f_{1}}(t)\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,p}}.

We conclude following the proof of
[
44
, Proposition 1.8]
, which gives

\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}(t)-\rho_{f_{1}}(t)\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,p}}\leq\max\left\{\left\lVert\rho_{f_{1}}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty},\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}W_{p}\left(\rho_{f_{1}}(t),\rho_{f_{2}}(t)\right).

∎

Then combining both Lemma
4.2
and Proposition
4.3
with (
4.1

D_{p}(t)\leq 1/e\text{ on }[0,T)

(4.4)

I_{3}(t)\leq C_{p}\widetilde{A}(t)\lambda^{-\frac{1}{p}}(t)D_{p}(t)\left\lvert\log D_{p}(t)\right\rvert,

where

\widetilde{A}(t)\coloneqq\max\left\{1,\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert\rho_{f_{1}}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{p}}\max\left\{\left\lVert\rho_{f_{1}}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty},\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\right\}

and
C_{p}
is a constant that depends only on
1<p<+\infty
. In the case
p=1
, the coupling argument found in
[

yields the same estimate as (

) with some constant
C_{1}
.

For
I_{4}
, we use Lemma
4.1
, namely, the no-work identity, to obtain a better control of the isolated velocity flow
V_{2}
compared to

Combining Hölder’s inequality with the no-work identity, we obtain

I_{4}(t)\leq D_{p}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}(t)\left(I_{4,1}(t)+I_{4,2}(t)\right),

where

I_{4,1}(t)\leq\left(\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{2d})^{2}}\left\lvert V_{2}(0)\right\rvert^{p}\left\lvert B(X_{1})-B(X_{2})\right\rvert^{p}\>d\pi_{0}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\\
I_{4,2}(t)\leq\left(\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{2d})^{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\lvert\nabla_{x}K\star\rho_{f_{2}}(X_{2}(\tau))\right\rvert\>d\tau\right)^{p}\left\lvert B(X_{1})-B(X_{2})\right\rvert^{p}\>d\pi_{0}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The first term is more subtle, so we will start with the latter. Thanks to the uniform bound on the force field we obtain

I_{4,2}(t)\leq\left(\int_{0}^{t}C\left(1+\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}(\tau)\right\rVert_{\infty}\right)\>d\tau\right)\left(\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{2d})^{2}}\left\lvert B(X_{1})-B(X_{2})\right\rvert^{p}\>d\pi_{0}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Now, since the external magnetic field belongs to

\left\lvert B(X_{1})-B(X_{2})\right\rvert\leq\widetilde{C}_{B}\left\lvert X_{1}-X_{2}\right\rvert
for some constant
\widetilde{C}_{B}>0
and thus

\left\lvert\log\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)\right\rvert\leq\left(2+\log\frac{p}{2}\right)\left\lvert\log D_{p}\right\rvert.

\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(d\mu)}\coloneqq\sup_{1\leq r<+\infty}\frac{\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{r}(d\mu)}}{\Theta(r)}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left\lvert w\right\rvert^{\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)^{\prime}p}\>df_{2}^{\mathrm{in}}(y,w)\right)^{\frac{1}{(1/\alpha)^{\prime}p}}\leq\left\lVert\left\lvert w\right\rvert\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(df_{2}^{\mathrm{in}}(y,w))}\Theta\left(p(1/\alpha)^{\prime}\right).

(4.6)

I_{4,1}(t)\leq C_{B,1}\left\lVert\left\lvert w\right\rvert\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(df_{2}^{\mathrm{in}}(y,w))}\lambda(t)^{-\frac{1}{p}}D_{p}(t)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Theta\left(\overline{C_{p}}\left\lvert\log D_{p}(t)\right\rvert\right),

where

and
\overline{C_{p}}\coloneqq p(2+\log(p/2))
.
Note that since
M_{k}(0)\leq(C_{0}k)^{k}

f_{2}

\Theta(r)\coloneqq r

\left\lVert\left\lvert w\right\rvert\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(df_{2}^{\mathrm{in}}(y,w))}\leq C_{0}
and we obtain, combining the two estimates (

4.5

(4.7)

I_{4}(t)\leq\widetilde{C}_{p,B}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{A}^{p}(\tau)\>d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{p}}(t)D_{p}(t)\left\lvert\log D_{p}(t)\right\rvert,\quad\widetilde{C}_{p,B}\coloneqq\overline{C_{p}}C_{0}C_{B,1}+C_{B,2}.

For

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\dot{D}_{p}(t)

\displaystyle\leq I_{2}(t)+I_{3}(t)+I_{4}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{p}}(t)D_{p}(t)+C_{p}\widetilde{A}(t)\lambda^{-\frac{1}{p}}(t)D_{p}(t)\left\lvert\log D_{p}(t)\right\rvert+\widetilde{C}_{p,B}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{A}(\tau)\>d\tau\right)\lambda^{-\frac{1}{p}}(t)D_{p}(t)\left\lvert\log D_{p}(t)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{p}C_{p,B}A(t)D_{p}(t)\sqrt{\left\lvert\log D_{p}(t)\right\rvert},

where
C_{p,B}\coloneqq\tfrac{p}{2}(1+C_{p}+\widetilde{C}_{p,B})

(4.8)

A(t)\coloneqq\widetilde{A}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{A}(\tau)\>d\tau.

Solving the differential inequality, we obtain

(4.9)

D_{p}(t)\leq\exp\left\{-\left(\sqrt{\left\lvert\log D_{p}(0)\right\rvert}-C_{p,B}\int_{0}^{t}A(\tau)\>d\tau\right)^{2}\right\}.

Note that the regime (
4.3

4.9

(4.10)

\sqrt{\left\lvert\log D_{p}(0)\right\rvert}\geq C_{p,B}\int_{0}^{t}A(\tau)\>d\tau+1.

Now, recall that
D_{p}
controls the
p
-Wasserstein distance between the two solutions; at time

D_{p}(t)\leq W_{p}^{p}\left(f_{1}(t),f_{2}(t)\right)
, while initially, by

-optimality of the coupling

D_{p}(0)\leq\lambda(0)\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{2d})^{2}}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{p}+\left\lvert v-w\right\rvert^{p}\>d\pi_{0}=\left\lvert\log D_{p}(0)\right\rvert^{\frac{p}{2}}W_{p}^{p}\left(f_{1}^{\mathrm{in}},f_{2}^{\mathrm{in}}\right),

and, since near the origin the inverse of the function

behaves like
\tau\mapsto\tau\left\lvert\log\tau\right\rvert^{\tfrac{p}{2}}

W_{p}^{p}(f_{1}^{\mathrm{in}},f_{2}^{\mathrm{in}})<c_{0}

D_{p}(0)\leq W_{p}^{p}\left(f_{1}^{\mathrm{in}},f_{2}^{\mathrm{in}}\right)\sqrt{\left\lvert\log W_{p}^{p}\left(f_{1}^{\mathrm{in}},f_{2}^{\mathrm{in}}\right)\right\rvert}^{p}.

Thus (
4.9
) implies

(4.11)

W_{p}^{p}\left(f_{1}(t),f_{2}(t)\right)\\
\leq\exp\left\{-\left(\sqrt{\left\lvert\log\Bigg\{W_{p}^{p}(f_{1}^{\mathrm{in}},f_{2}^{\mathrm{in}}\sqrt{\left\lvert\log W_{p}^{p}(f_{1}^{\mathrm{in}},f_{2}^{\mathrm{in}})\right\rvert}^{p}\Bigg\}\right\rvert}\!-C_{p,B}\int_{0}^{t}A(\tau)\>d\tau\right)^{2}\right\}

Remark 4.4

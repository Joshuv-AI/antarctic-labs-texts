Local-in-time analytic solutions for an inviscid model of superfluidity in 3D
Pranava Chaitanya Jayanti

Abstract
. We address the existence of solutions for the inviscid version of the Hall-Vinen-Bekharevich-Khalatnikov equations in 3D, a macro-scale model of superfluidity. This system couples the incompressible Euler equations for the normal fluid and superfluid using a nonlinear mutual friction term that acts only at points of non-zero superfluid vorticity. In the first rigorous study of the inviscid HVBK system, we construct a unique local-in-time solution that is analytic in time and space.



1.
Introduction

Isobaric cooling (at pressures below
25
bar) of helium-4 results in a second-order phase transition that can be explained only using quantum mechanics (Bose-Einstein condensation). Below
2.17
K, there is a mixture of two different phases of helium: a run-of-the-mill normal fluid and a strange superfluid phase. The number of He-4 atoms in the latter increases as the temperature is lowered, until it is entirely superfluid at absolute zero. Experimental evidence suggests that the two phases are unlike standard multiphase flows in that they do not possess any distinct phase boundaries. Instead, both fluids occupy the entire domain of the flow, interacting with each other only at points where the superfluid vorticity is non-zero
[
Vin04
,
Vin06
,
Lan41
]
. This has prompted descriptions of superfluidity at various length scales: micro-scale quantum mechanical models
[
Pit59
,
Kha69
,
Car96
]
, meso-scale vortex-based models
[
Sch78
,
Sch85
,
Sch88
]
, and macro-scale classical fluid dynamical models
[
Hol01
,
RBL09
,
TS10
,
SRL11
,
VSBP19
]
. It is to be remarked that a unified description of superfluidity is still wanting; see
[
BDV01
,
PL11
,
BLR14
,
BBP14
,
Jay22
]
and references therein for more details on these various models.

One of the non-classical effects in superfluids is the existence of quantized vortex filaments, which is the basis for the meso-scale models. However, at length scales much larger than the inter-vortex spacing, the discrete nature of the superfluid vorticity can be ignored, and a classical picture emerges. Known as the Hall-Vinen-Bekharevich-Khalatnikov (HVBK) equations, this macro-scale model describes the superfluid using the Euler equations and the normal fluid using the Navier-Stokes equations, and couples them with a nonlinear mutual friction term that vanishes at points of zero superfluid vorticity. In this paper, we present the space-time analyticity of the
inviscid
incompressible HVBK equations in 3D, i.e., both fluids are governed by the incompressible Euler equations. The study of the equations of fluid dynamics in analytic classes (in the space variable) was pioneered by Foias and Temam
[
FT89
]
. A straightforward consequence of such high regularity in the spatial variable is the time analyticity of the solutions
[
CF88
,
Pro91
]
. The analyticity of
L^{p}
solutions to the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations has also been investigated in various works
[
GK98
,
LR00
,
LR04
]
. Analyticity for the case of inviscid equations was first shown for the “great lake” equations
[
LO97
]
, of which the incompressible Euler equations are a special case. This was achieved by trading the regularity of the solution for a dissipative term in the analytic norm energy balance. The method of Foias and Temam was extended to general dissipative parabolic PDEs with analytic nonlinearities
[
FT98
,
BB14
]
, while the study of inviscid equations was further developed in
[
KV09
]
and
[
BH17
]
. The former provided improved estimates of the analyticity radius for the 3D Euler equations, whereas the latter extended the results of Levermore and Oliver to encompass a wide range of incompressible, inviscid equations. In
[
CKV16
,
CK18
]
, the authors demonstrated the persistence of the analyticity radius of the Euler equations in Lagrangian coordinates, a property that was also shown not to hold in the Eulerian formulation. The machinery of analyticity was also recently adopted to the setting of compressible flows
[
Bae20
,
CDX21
,
JKL21
,
DP22
,
LT23
]
. Our work in this paper is inspired primarily by
[
BH17
]
, and the main challenge involves the mutual friction term between the superfluid and normal fluid, which is singular at points of vanishing superfluid vorticity. Thus, the central question is: Given a non-vanishing initial superfluid vorticity (in the analytic class), does it remain non-vanishing? We find the answer in the affirmative, at least locally in time. To the best of our knowledge, this result is the first of its kind.

We now briefly outline the notation used in this article. Following this, in Section
2
, we present the HVBK equations in the form pertaining to our work, before defining analytic classes, and stating the main result. All of the required a priori estimates are derived in Section
3
. Subsequently, the construction of the solutions is briefly discussed in Section
4
, along with the proof of uniqueness.

1.1.
Notation

We denote by
H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
the completion of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
under the Sobolev norm
H^{s}
. Consider a 3D vector-valued function
u\equiv(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
. The set of all divergence-free, smooth 3D functions
u
defines
C^{\infty}_{\text{d}}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
. Then,
H^{s}_{\text{d}}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
is the completion of
C^{\infty}_{\text{d}}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
under the
H^{s}
norm. In particular,
H^{0}_{\text{d}}
is denoted by
H
. The Leray projector,
\mathcal{P}\colon L^{2}\mapsto H
, projects the space of square-integrable vector-valued functions into its divergence-free subspace. The
L^{2}
scalar product between Hölder conjugate vector-valued functions
v
and
w
is denoted by
\langle v,w\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}v\cdot w\ dx
.

We also use the notation
X\lesssim Y
and
X\gtrsim Y
to imply that there exists a positive constant
C
such that
X\leq CY
and
CX\geq Y
, respectively. Throughout the article,
C
is used to denote a possibly large constant that depends on the system parameters, and whose value can vary across the different steps of calculations.

2.
Mathematical model and main result

2.1.
The governing equations

We now present the form of the HVBK equations that is used here, namely

(2.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}+u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}\cdot\nabla u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}+\nabla p_{\text{\scriptsize s}}

\displaystyle=-\rho_{\text{n}}\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}}{\absolutevalue{\ws}}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}\times v)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}+u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}\cdot\nabla u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}+\nabla p_{\text{\scriptsize n}}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\text{s}}\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}}{\absolutevalue{\ws}}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}\times v)

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}-u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}

\displaystyle=0.

Here, the subscripts
s
and
n
denote the superfluid and normal fluid, respectively. The velocity, pressure, and density fraction are labeled by
u,p
, and
\rho
(respectively), and the vorticity is
\omega=\nabla\times u
. Since both fluids are incompressible, it is clear that the density fractions
\rho_{\text{\scriptsize s}}
and
\rho_{\text{\scriptsize n}}
are constant (with
\rho_{\text{\scriptsize s}}+\rho_{\text{\scriptsize n}}=1
), and the pressures
p_{\text{\scriptsize s}}
and
p_{\text{\scriptsize s}}
are simply Lagrange multipliers that vanish under the action of the Leray projector
\mathcal{P}
, resulting in

(2.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}+\mathcal{P}(u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}\cdot\nabla u_{\text{\scriptsize s}})

\displaystyle=-\rho_{\text{\scriptsize n}}\mathcal{P}\left(\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}}{\absolutevalue{\ws}}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}\times v)\right)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}+\mathcal{P}(u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}\cdot\nabla u_{\text{\scriptsize n}})

\displaystyle=\rho_{\text{\scriptsize s}}\mathcal{P}\left(\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}}{\absolutevalue{\ws}}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}\times v)\right).

These equations are supplemented with the initial conditions

(2.3)

u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}(0,x)=u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}(x),\quad u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}(0,x)=u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{0}(x)\quad\text{a.e.}\ x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}.

We use periodic boundary conditions, i.e., we are working on a 3-dimensional torus
[0,2\pi]^{3}
. As a result, it is necessary that the average vorticity vanishes, i.e.,
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\omega(t,x)=0
, for both fluids.

Compared to the original model
[
Hol01
]
, the system in (
2.1
) has excluded compressibility and thermal effects, as well as a higher-order “vortex tension” term that is expected to lead to ill-posedness. An additional term in the mutual friction, which makes no qualitative difference to the results here, has been disregarded for simplicity. Finally, we treat the normal fluid as inviscid, in contrast to earlier mathematical work by the author
[
JT21
]
, where global well-posedness of the 2D HVBK system was established if both fluids are considered viscous. In the physics community, the HVBK equations are often used to study superfluid Couette flow
[
BJ88
,
HB04
]
and superfluid turbulence. Particularly in the context of turbulence, different schemes have been employed to handle the generation of small scales in simulations of the inviscid superfluid: sub-grid scale modeling
[
TS10
]
, spectral suppression of small scales
[
SRL11
]
, and adding an artificial viscosity
[
RBL09
,
VSBP19
]
. In reality, there is another mechanism for energy loss through nonlinear interactions of Kelvin waves along the vortex filaments, which lead to an energy cascade and eventually phononic dissipation, but that is not described by this macro-scale model.

The system in (
2.1
) (and equivalently (
2.2
)) is constructed so as to conserve mass and momentum, and also obey an energy balance equation. Momentum conservation can be seen by multiplying the superfluid equation by
\rho_{\text{\scriptsize s}}
, the normal fluid equation by
\rho_{\text{\scriptsize n}}
, adding them, and integrating over space. Using the periodic boundary conditions, we observe that the total momentum of the two fluids, i.e.,
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(\rho_{\text{\scriptsize s}}u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}(t)+\rho_{\text{\scriptsize n}}u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}(t))
, is constant. Energy conservation also follows similarly: Test the two equations by (the corresponding)
\rho u
and add to (formally) obtain

(2.4)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\rho_{\text{\scriptsize s}}\norm{\us}_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rho_{\text{\scriptsize n}}\norm{\un}_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\rho_{\text{\scriptsize s}}\rho_{\text{\scriptsize n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\lvert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}\rvert\lvert v\rvert^{2}-\frac{\left(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}\cdot v\right)^{2}}{\absolutevalue{\ws}}\right)=0.

Since the integrand in the second term is always non-negative, we see that the total energy of the system is bounded. The mutual friction acts as a retarding force, working to equilibrate the two phases. Its structure is motivated by the experimental observation that the fluids only interact at points of non-zero superfluid vorticity. The singularity that stems from the magnitude of the vorticity vector should be acknowledged, as it constitutes the main challenge in the study of this system. We circumvent this hurdle by ensuring that no regions of vanishing superfluid vorticity occur. Since we seek smooth (analytic) solutions, we begin from initial data that satisfies
\inf_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}}\absolutevalue{\ws^0}=m_{i}>0
, and limit the evolution of the system to a time when
\inf_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}}\absolutevalue{\ws}\geq m_{f}
, where
m_{f}<m_{i}
. Therefore, the solution we obtain is local-in-time. A similar strategy was employed in the analysis of a micro-scale model of superfluidity in
[
JT22
,
JJK24b
,
JJK24a
]
, where it was necessary to prevent the formation of vacuum states.

2.2.
Analytic classes

In the case of the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations (NSE), the average velocity is a conserved quantity so that we may set it to be zero (using a Galilean transformation). This, combined with the Poincaré inequality, allows one to work with the prevailing dissipative Stokes operator. Absent such a luxury in our system, we resort to the method employed in
[
FT98
]
and consider the operator
A:=I-\Delta
, which accounts for the inhomogeneous norms that are needed. Before defining the analytic classes, we remark that the Fourier decomposition of any vector-valued function
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
is denoted by
(\widehat{f}(k))_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}
.

Definition 2.1

(Analytic class)
.

For any
p\geq 0
and
\sigma\geq 0
, the corresponding analytic class is defined as

(2.5)

G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}^{3}):=D(A^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{\sigma A^{\frac{1}{2}}}),

which is a Hilbert space with the inner product

(2.6)

\langle v,w\rangle_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\widehat{v}(j)\cdot\overline{\widehat{w}(j)}(1+\absolutevalue{j}^{2})^{p}e^{2\sigma(1+\absolutevalue{j}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}},

(where
\overline{\widehat{w}(j)}
denotes the complex conjugate of the Fourier coefficients) and the norm

(2.7)

\lVert v\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\lvert\widehat{v}(k)\rvert^{2}(1+\absolutevalue{k}^{2})^{p}e^{2\sigma(1+\absolutevalue{k}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Remark 2.2
.

The inclusion
G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}\subset H^{p}
holds for all
p\geq 0
and
\sigma>0
. We also note that
G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}
forms an algebra for
p>\frac{3}{2}
, and the proof can be found in
[
FT98
, Lemma 1]
.

2.3.
The main result

We are now ready to state the main theorem. Throughout the article, we assume that
\rho_{\text{\scriptsize s}}
and
\rho_{\text{\scriptsize n}}
are fixed constants, and as a consequence they do not explicitly feature in the estimates or results.

Theorem 2.3

(Local-in-time existence and uniqueness)
.

For
p>\frac{5}{2}
,
m_{i}>0
, and
0<\sigma_{0}<\frac{m_{i}}{2C_{0}}
(where
C_{0}
is the Poincaré constant), suppose that the initial velocities
u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0},u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{0}
belong to
(G^{\frac{p+1}{2}}_{\sigma_{0}}\cap H)(\mathbb{T}^{3})
, with the initial superfluid vorticity
\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}=\nabla\times u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}
satisfying

(2.8)

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}}\absolutevalue{\omega_{\text{\normalfont\scriptsize s}}^0}=m_{i}.

Then, for any
2C_{0}\sigma_{0}<m_{f}<m_{i}
, there exists a
\delta>0
and a time
T^{*}
(both depending only on the size of the initial data,
m_{i}
,
m_{f}
,
\sigma_{0}
, and
p
) such that

•

the system (
2.2
) admits a unique solution
u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}(t),u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}(t)\in(G^{\frac{p+1}{2}}_{\sigma(t)}\cap H)(\mathbb{T}^{3})
, with
\sigma(t)=\sigma_{0}-\delta t
, for
t\in[0,T^{*})
,

•

the solution satisfies
\inf_{t,x\in(0,T^{*})\times\mathbb{T}^{3}}\absolutevalue{\omega_{\text{\normalfont\scriptsize s}}(t,x)}\geq m_{f}
, and

•

the map
t\mapsto u(t)
is analytic on
(0,T^{*})
.

To prove Theorem
2.3
, we work with a Galerkin-truncated approximation of (
2.1
). The strategy involves two distinct parts: deriving the required a priori estimates in the analytic norm, and ensuring that
\absolutevalue{\ws}
is bounded below by
m_{f}
. The existence of a solution to the approximate system follows from the ODE theory, so we only present a sketch. It is also argued that a standard energy-based approach to uniqueness is not sufficient, and the estimates are instead carried out in the analytic norms. Finally, the time analyticity of the solution is a consequence of the analytic regularity in space, as shown in
[
CF88
, Theorem 12.1]
or
[
Pro91
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Remark 2.4
.

We note that the regularity of the initial data increases with
\sigma_{0}
. From Theorem
2.3
, we see that if the initial lower bound to the vorticity magnitude
(m_{i})
is higher, then we can also start from initial data that is more regular. Similarly, if we wish to extend the evolution time by decreasing
m_{f}
, then it comes at the expense of a reduced regularity of the initial data.

In our system, we have several nonlinear terms which are treated using the following lemma that generalizes
[
LO97
, Lemma 8]
and
[
BH17
, Proposition 7.2]
.

Lemma 2.5

(Nonlinear estimate)
.

Consider an operator
T
whose representation in Fourier space is in the form of a multiplier, i.e.,

(2.9)

\widehat{T(f)}(k):=m(k)\widehat{f}(k),\qquad\lvert m(k)\rvert\lesssim\lvert k\rvert.

Then, for
1\leq i\leq K\in\mathbb{N}
and
f_{i},g\in C^{\infty}_{x}
, we have

(2.10)

\lvert\langle\Pi_{i=1}^{K}f_{i},Tg\rangle_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\rvert\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{K}\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}f_{j}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\left(\Pi_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{K}\lVert f_{i}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\right)\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}g\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}},

for
p>2
.

Proof.

We begin by noting two results. The first is the elementary inequality

(2.11)

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}x_{i}\right)^{r}\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{K}x_{i}^{r},

valid for any
x_{i}\geq 0
and
r>0
. The second is an embedding result which states

(2.12)

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sum_{k}|\widehat{f}(k)|\lesssim\|A^{\frac{s}{2}}f\|_{L^{2}}\sim\|f\|_{H^{s}},

for any
s>\frac{3}{2}
. It follows from the Fourier series expansion, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the sufficiently fast decay of
(1+|k|^{2})^{-s}
. We also record that the space of functions
f
satisfying
\sum_{k}|\widehat{f}(k)|<\infty
is called the Wiener algebra, denoted by
\mathcal{W}
. In what follows, we denote by
I
the set of three-dimensional vectors satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{K}h_{i}+k=0
.

We now proceed to prove the lemma, as

\displaystyle\lvert\langle\Pi_{i=1}^{K}f_{i},Tg\rangle_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{I}\left(\Pi_{i=1}^{K}\lvert\widehat{f_{i}}(h_{i})\rvert\right)(1+\lvert k\rvert^{2})^{p}e^{2\sigma(1+\lvert k\rvert^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lvert k\rvert\lvert\widehat{g}(k)\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{I}\sum_{j=1}^{K}(1+\lvert h_{j}\rvert^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\Pi_{i=1}^{K}e^{\sigma(1+\lvert h_{i}\rvert^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lvert\widehat{f_{i}}(h_{i})\rvert\right)(1+\lvert k\rvert^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{\sigma(1+\lvert k\rvert^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lvert k\rvert\lvert\widehat{g}(k)\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{I}\sum_{j=1}^{K}(1+\lvert h_{j}\rvert^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\Pi_{i=1}^{K}(1+\lvert h_{i}\rvert^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{\sigma(1+\lvert h_{i}\rvert^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lvert\widehat{f_{i}}(h_{i})\rvert\right)(1+\lvert k\rvert^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}+{\frac{1}{4}}}e^{\sigma(1+\lvert k\rvert^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lvert\widehat{g}(k)\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{K}\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}f_{j}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\left(\Pi_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{K}\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{\sigma A^{\frac{1}{2}}}f_{i}\rVert_{\mathcal{W}}\right)\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}g\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{K}\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}f_{j}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\left(\Pi_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{K}\lVert f_{i}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\right)\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}g\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}.

In the second inequality, we used the triangle inequality and (
2.11
) to write

(1+\lvert k\rvert^{2})^{r}\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{K}(1+\lvert h_{j}\rvert^{2})^{r}.

The fourth line follows similarly, since

\lvert k\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}=(\lvert k\rvert^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}\lesssim\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}\lvert h_{i}\rvert^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\lesssim\Pi_{i=1}^{K}(1+\lvert h_{i}\rvert^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}.

An application of Hölder’s inequality leads to the fifth line, while the final inequality is obtained by choosing
p>2
and using the embedding in (
2.12
).
∎

3.
A priori estimates

In this section, we derive formal estimates for the system in (
2.2
), assuming that
t\in[0,T^{*}]
, where
T^{*}
is the local existence time. The time
T^{*}:=\min\{T_{1},T_{2}\}
, where
T_{1}
is the existence time arising from the energy estimate (see (
3.17
)), and

(3.1)

T_{2}:=\inf\{t\ \rvert\inf_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}}\absolutevalue{\ws(t)}=m_{f}\}.

3.1.
Approximate system

We begin by considering a finite-dimensional approximation of (
2.2
), via a Galerkin truncation. Indeed, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}+P^{N}(u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\cdot\nabla u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N})

\displaystyle=-\rho_{\text{\scriptsize n}}P^{N}\left(\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}}{\absolutevalue{\ws^N}}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\times v^{N})\right)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{N}+P^{N}(u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{N}\cdot\nabla u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{N})

\displaystyle=\rho_{\text{\scriptsize s}}P^{N}\left(\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}}{\absolutevalue{\ws^N}}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\times v^{N})\right)

\displaystyle v^{N}

\displaystyle=u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{N}-u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}.

Here,
u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}(t,x)=\sum_{\absolutevalue{k}\leq N}\widehat{u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}}(t,k)e^{ik\cdot x}
and
\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}(t,x)=\sum_{\absolutevalue{k}\leq N}\widehat{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}}(t,k)e^{ik\cdot x}
with
\widehat{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}}(t,k)=ik\times\widehat{u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}}(t,k)
, i.e., only the first
N
Fourier modes of the velocity and vorticity are retained. The projector
P^{N}
(the finite-dimensional restriction of
\mathcal{P}
) achieves this, while also ensuring that the
\widehat{u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}}(t,k)
are three-dimensional vector-valued functions which satisfy the (spectral) divergence-free constraint:
k\cdot\widehat{u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}}(t,k)=0
.

Note that the nonlinear estimate derived in Lemma
2.5
is valid only if there is at most one derivative to be shared by the entire inner product. While the advection terms in (
3.2
) satisfy this requirement, this is not true of the mutual friction, which features multiple appearances from the superfluid vorticity (each of which represents a derivative loss compared to the velocity). Guided by this, we now reformulate (
2.1
) so that it is more conducive to the methods used here. The identity
u=(-\Delta)^{-1}(\nabla\times\omega)
, translated into spectral form, reads
\widehat{u}(k)=\frac{ik}{\absolutevalue{k}^{2}}\times\widehat{\omega}(k)
, valid for any
k\neq 0
. As a consequence of the vorticity having zero mean value (
\widehat{\omega}(0)=0
) but not the velocity
(\widehat{u}(0)\neq 0)
, it is not possible to entirely describe the system through just the vortex dynamics of the two fluids. To be more precise, we need an equation for the dynamics of the zeroth Fourier modes of the velocities, i.e., the (spatial) averages of the velocities. With this in mind, we arrive at an equivalent formulation of (
3.2
), given by

(3.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\overline{u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}}

\displaystyle=-\rho_{\text{\scriptsize n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}P^{N}\left(\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}}{\absolutevalue{\ws^N}}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\times v^{N})\right)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\overline{u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{N}}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\text{\scriptsize s}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}P^{N}\left(\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}}{\absolutevalue{\ws^N}}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\times v^{N})\right)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}

\displaystyle=-P^{N}(u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\cdot\nabla\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N})+P^{N}(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\cdot\nabla u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N})-\rho_{\text{\scriptsize n}}\nabla\times P^{N}\left(\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}}{\absolutevalue{\ws^N}}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\times v^{N})\right)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{N}

\displaystyle=-P^{N}(u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{N}\cdot\nabla\omega_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{N})+P^{N}(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{N}\cdot\nabla u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{N})+\rho_{\text{\scriptsize s}}\nabla\times P^{N}\left(\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}}{\absolutevalue{\ws^N}}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\times v^{N})\right).

The first and second equations are obtained by integrating (
3.2
) over
\mathbb{T}^{3}
, whereas the third and fourth equations are a result of applying the curl operator to (
3.2
). Henceforth, this is the system we analyze, supplemented with the initial conditions:
\overline{u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}}(0,x)=P^{N}\overline{u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}}(x)
and
\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}(0,x)=P^{N}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}(x)
, and likewise for the normal fluid as well. The calculations in the rest of this section are all valid in a local time interval
0\leq t\leq T^{N}
. In the course of deriving the a priori estimates, we establish that the local existence time can be chosen to be independent of
N
.

3.2.
Average velocity estimate

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the RHS of (
3.3
)
1
,

(3.4)

\partial_{t}\overline{u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}}\lesssim\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert v^{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{L^{2}},

where the second inequality follows from the energy estimate in (
2.4
), since

\lVert v^{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\lVert u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}+\lVert u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim\lVert P^{N}u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}\rVert_{L^{2}}+\lVert P^{N}u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{0}\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\lVert u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}\rVert_{L^{2}}+\lVert u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{0}\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Integrating (
3.4
) in time yields,

(3.5)

\widehat{u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}}(t,0)\leq\widehat{u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}}(0)+C\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}T^{N}.

Following a similar argument for (
3.3
)
2
, we arrive at

(3.6)

\widehat{u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}}(t,0)\leq\widehat{u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{0}}(0)+C\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}T^{N}.

Remark 3.1
.

As a consequence of (
2.7
) and (
3.5
), we also get the estimate (omitting the time variable for simplicity)

(3.7)

\displaystyle\lVert A^{\frac{r}{2}}u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lvert k\rvert\leq N}\absolutevalue{\wh{\us}(k)}^{2}(1+\absolutevalue{k}^{2})^{p+r}e^{2\sigma(1+\absolutevalue{k}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\absolutevalue{\wh{\us}(0)}^{2}e^{2\sigma}+\sum_{1\leq\lvert k\rvert\leq N}\frac{1+\lvert k\rvert^{2}}{\absolutevalue{k}^{2}}\absolutevalue{\wh{\ws}(k)}^{2}(1+\absolutevalue{k}^{2})^{p-1+r}e^{2\sigma(1+\absolutevalue{k}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert\widehat{u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}}(0)\rvert^{2}+\lVert A^{\frac{r}{2}}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p-1}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}(1+T^{N})^{2},

which will be used later.

3.3.
Analytic norm energy estimate

We now apply
A^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{\sigma(t)A^{\frac{1}{2}}}
to (
3.3
)
1
, and then take the
L^{2}
inner product with
A^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{\sigma(t)A^{\frac{1}{2}}}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}
to obtain

\displaystyle\langle A^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{\sigma A^{\frac{1}{2}}}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N},A^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{\sigma A^{\frac{1}{2}}}\partial_{t}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle=-\langle A^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{\sigma A^{\frac{1}{2}}}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N},A^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{\sigma A^{\frac{1}{2}}}P^{N}(u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\cdot\nabla\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N})\rangle_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle+\langle A^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{\sigma A^{\frac{1}{2}}}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N},A^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{\sigma A^{\frac{1}{2}}}P^{N}(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\cdot\nabla u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N})\rangle_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle-\rho_{\text{\scriptsize n}}\langle A^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{\sigma A^{\frac{1}{2}}}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N},A^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{\sigma A^{\frac{1}{2}}}\nabla\times P^{N}\left(\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}}{\absolutevalue{\ws^N}}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\times v^{N})\right)\rangle_{L^{2}_{x}},

where we have hidden the time dependence of
\sigma(t)
for brevity. We integrate by parts (in time on the LHS and in space on the RHS), and use
\sigma(t)=\sigma_{0}-\delta t
for a
\delta>0
that will be determined later, to arrive at

(3.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\delta\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N},u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\otimes\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rangle_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}-\langle\nabla\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N},\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\otimes u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rangle_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle+\rho_{\text{\scriptsize n}}\langle\nabla\times\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N},\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}}{\absolutevalue{\ws^N}}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\times v^{N})\rangle_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}.

The first and second terms of the RHS can be treated using Lemma
2.5
with
K=2
. Indeed, the first term leads to

(3.9)

\displaystyle\lvert\langle\nabla\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N},u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\otimes\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rangle_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\lvert\widehat{u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}}(0)\rvert+\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p-1}{2}}_{\sigma}}(1+T^{N})\right)\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\lvert\widehat{u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}}(0)\rvert+\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p-1}{2}}_{\sigma}}(1+T^{N})\right)\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\lvert\widehat{u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}}(0)\rvert+\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}(1+T^{N})\right)\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}.

The second inequality results from (
3.7
), when
r=0
and
r=\frac{1}{2}
.

To deal with the third term on the RHS of (
3.8
), we apply Lemma
2.5
for
K=4
, which yields

(3.10)

\displaystyle\lvert\langle\nabla\times\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N},\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}}{\absolutevalue{\ws^N}}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\times v^{N})\rangle_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\left(\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\ws^N}}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert v^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\ws^N}}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert v^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}+\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}\lVert\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\ws^N}}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}v^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\ws^N}}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}v^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}.

We now denote

(3.11)

\begin{gathered}X^{N}:=1+\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2},\quad Y^{N}:=\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2},\\
\overline{U}:=\lvert\widehat{u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}}(0)\rvert+\lvert\widehat{u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{0}}(0)\rvert.\end{gathered}

We can use (
3.7
), as well as (
A.6
) for
r=\frac{1}{2}
, to rewrite (
3.10
) as

(3.12)

\displaystyle\lvert\langle\nabla\times\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N},\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}}{\absolutevalue{\ws^N}}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\times v^{N})\rangle_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{X^{N}}\left(\overline{U}+\sqrt{X^{N}}(1+T^{N})\right)\left(e^{\sigma_{0}}+(p+1)!\left(\frac{m_{f}}{2}-C_{0}\sigma_{0}\right)^{-p-2}\right)Y^{N}

\displaystyle\lesssim X^{N}(1+\overline{U}+T^{N})Y^{N}.

Substituting (
3.9
) and (
3.12
) into (
3.8
) therefore leads to

(3.13)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\delta\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}\lesssim X^{N}(1+\overline{U}+T^{N})Y^{N}.

Performing similar estimates on the normal fluid equation (
3.2
)
2
, and adding the result with (
3.13
) finally yields

(3.14)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}X^{N}+\delta Y^{N}\leq CX^{N}(1+\overline{U}+T^{N})Y^{N}.

Then, for
X_{0}:=1+\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma_{0}}}^{2}+\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{0}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma_{0}}}^{2}
, we have

(3.15)

\displaystyle\delta:=2CX_{0}(1+\overline{U}+T^{N})\geq 2CX^{N}(0)(1+\overline{U}+T^{N}),

we have

(3.16)

X^{N}(t)+\delta\int_{0}^{t}Y^{N}(s)ds\leq X_{0},\quad 0\leq t<T^{N}.

The existence time is defined by the relation

(3.17)

T^{N}:=\frac{\sigma_{0}}{\delta}=\frac{\sigma_{0}}{2CX_{0}(1+\overline{U}+T^{N})}.

The above equation admits a positive solution which depends only on
m_{f}
,
p
,
\sigma_{0}
,
\overline{U}
,
\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma_{0}}}
, and
\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{0}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma_{0}}}
. The existence time is thus independent of
N
, and so we label it
T_{1}
.

In conclusion, from (
3.7
) and (
3.16
), we have
u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N},u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{N}\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{1};G^{\frac{p+1}{2}}_{\sigma}\cap H)\cap L^{2}(0,T_{1};G^{\frac{p+\frac{3}{2}}{2}}_{\sigma}\cap H)
, where the divergence-free property follows from the construction of the approximate velocity field. From here, we return to (
3.2
) to infer that
\partial_{t}u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N},\partial_{t}u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{N}\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{1};G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}\cap H)\cap L^{2}(0,T_{1};G^{\frac{p+\frac{1}{2}}{2}}_{\sigma}\cap H)
. All of the regularity results mentioned here are uniform in
N
, which will allow us to extract a weakly convergent subsequence that retains the same properties.

3.4.
Ensuring a positive lower bound to
\absolutevalue{\ws}

We return to (
3.3
)
3
and write its solution as

(3.18)

\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}(t,x)=P^{N}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}(\tau,x)d\tau,

where
\mathcal{T}_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}
is the RHS of (
3.3
)
3
. Observe that

(3.19)

\displaystyle\lVert(I-P^{N})\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-s}\lVert\lvert\nabla\rvert^{s}(I-P^{N})\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-s}\lVert A^{\frac{s}{2}}(I-P^{N})\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim N^{-s}\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma_{0}}},

for
s>0
such that
\frac{3}{2}+s<p
. The first inequality can be proved similar to Bernstein’s inequalities
[
Tao06
, Appendix A]
, while the last inequality is due to (
2.12
). Now, by the restriction on the initial vorticity, and from the triangle inequality, we know that

(3.20)

m_{i}\leq\lvert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}\rvert\leq\lvert P^{N}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}\rvert+\lvert(I-P^{N})\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}\rvert.

Using (
3.19
), for a sufficiently large
N
, say
N>N^{*}
, we can ensure that the last term in (
3.20
) is less than, say
\frac{m_{i}-m_{f}}{2}
. This implies that, for
N>N^{*}
, we have
\lvert P^{N}\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}\rvert\geq\frac{m_{i}+m_{f}}{2}
. Therefore, returning to (
3.18
), it is sufficient to demand that the time
T_{2}
(defined in (
3.1
)) is such that

(3.21)

\left\lvert\int_{0}^{T_{2}}\mathcal{T}_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}(\tau,x)d\tau\right\rvert\leq\frac{m_{i}-m_{f}}{2},

so that the triangle inequality yields the required condition
\lvert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}(t,x)\rvert\geq m_{f}
. Using Sobolev embedding and (
2.7
), we can guarantee (
3.21
) with the sufficiency given by

(3.22)

\int_{0}^{T_{2}}\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}(\tau)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}d\tau\leq C\int_{0}^{T_{2}}\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}(\tau)\rVert_{G^{\frac{p-1}{2}}_{\sigma}}d\tau\leq\frac{m_{i}-m_{f}}{2}.

We now examine each term of
\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}(\tau)\rVert_{G^{\frac{p-1}{2}}_{\sigma}}
. We begin with

(3.23)

\lVert P^{N}(u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\cdot\nabla\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N})\rVert_{G^{\frac{p-1}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lesssim\lVert u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p-1}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert\nabla\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p-1}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lesssim\lVert u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p+1}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2},

where we have used the algebra property of the Gevrey norm. In a similar manner, we also obtain

(3.24)

\lVert P^{N}(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\cdot\nabla u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N})\rVert_{G^{\frac{p-1}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lesssim\lVert u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p+1}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}.

Finally, we deal with the last term of
\mathcal{T}_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}
as

(3.25)

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\times P^{N}\left(\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}}{\absolutevalue{\ws^N}}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\times v^{N})\right)\right\|_{G^{\frac{p-1}{2}}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}}{\absolutevalue{\ws^N}}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\times v^{N})\right\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}\lVert\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\ws^N}}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert v^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p+1}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}\left(e^{\sigma_{0}}+p!\left(\frac{m_{f}}{2}-C_{0}\sigma_{0}\right)^{-p-1}\right)\left(\lVert u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p+1}{2}}_{\sigma}}+\lVert u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{N}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p+1}{2}}_{\sigma}}\right),

where we made use of (
A.6
). Combining (
3.23
)–(
3.25
) with the uniform-in-
N
bounds (
3.16
), we see that it is indeed possible to find a
T_{2}
(independent of
N
, and depending on the data) that is small enough so that (
3.22
) is met. This implies that
\lvert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{N}\rvert\geq m_{f}
for all
x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}
and all
0\leq t\leq T^{*}
, where
T^{*}=\min\{T_{1},T_{2}\}
, closing the necessary a priori estimates.

4.
Existence of a unique solution

4.1.
Existence of a solution

We fix the value of
N
(larger than
N^{*}
, defined following (
3.20
)), and take the inner product of (
3.2
) with
e^{ij\cdot x}
. This yields a system of ODEs for the functions
\widehat{u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}}^{N}(t,k)
and
\widehat{u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}}^{N}(t,k)
, which have a unique solution according to standard ODE theory. Once this is established, it is only a matter of using the a priori estimates in Section
3
to obtain a solution of (
2.2
) as the limit of a subsequence of approximating solutions.

4.2.
Uniqueness of the solution

Starting from the same initial data, we assume that there are two solutions
(u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{1},u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{1})
and
(u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{2},u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{2})
, and denote
\Phi_{s}:=u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{1}-u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{2}
,
\Phi_{n}:=u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{1}-u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{2}
,
\Omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}:=\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{1}-\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{2}
, and
\Omega_{\text{\scriptsize n}}:=\omega_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{1}-\omega_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{2}
. Then, subtracting (
2.2
) for the two pairs of solutions yields

(4.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Phi_{s}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{P}(\Phi_{s}\cdot\nabla u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{1})-\mathcal{P}(u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{2}\cdot\nabla\Phi_{s})-\rho_{\text{\scriptsize n}}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Phi_{n}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{P}(\Phi_{n}\cdot\nabla u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{1})-\mathcal{P}(u_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{2}\cdot\nabla\Phi_{n})+\rho_{\text{\scriptsize s}}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{F},

where

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{1}}{\absolutevalue{\ws^1}}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{1}\times v^{1})-\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{2}}{\absolutevalue{\ws^2}}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{2}\times v^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{1}}{\absolutevalue{\ws^1}}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{1}\times(\Phi_{n}-\Phi_{s}))+\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{1}}{\absolutevalue{\ws^1}}\times(\Omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}\times v^{2})+\frac{\Omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}}{\absolutevalue{\ws^1}}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{2}\times v^{2})

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\ws^1}}-\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\ws^2}}\right)\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{2}\times(\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{2}\times v^{2}).

We remark that a simple energy-level estimate, i.e., in
L^{2}
, is not going to be sufficient here, since terms involving the gradient of
\Phi_{s}
appear from the mutual friction part of (
4.1
), and there is no viscosity. Hence, we have to induce an artificial dissipation through the analytic norm, as was done in Section
3.3
. Defining
q:=p-\frac{1}{2}>2
(so that Lemma
2.5
still applies), we take the inner product of (
4.1
)
1
with
\Phi_{s}
in
G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma(t)}
and obtain

(4.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\Phi_{s}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\delta\lVert A^{\frac{1}{4}}\Phi_{s}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\langle\Phi_{s},\Phi_{s}\cdot\nabla u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{1}\rangle_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}-\langle\Phi_{s},u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{2}\cdot\nabla\Phi_{s}\rangle_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}+\rho_{\text{\scriptsize n}}\langle\Phi_{s},\mathcal{F}\rangle_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{6}I_{i},

where the last four terms of the sum correspond to the terms on the RHS of (
4.2
). In what follows, we repeatedly make use of the fact that
G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}
is an algebra. For the first term, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields

(4.4)

\displaystyle I_{1}\lesssim\lVert\nabla u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{2}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert\Phi_{s}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}\lesssim\lVert u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{2}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p+1}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert\Phi_{s}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}.

The term
I_{2}
is accounted for in a similar manner, while also keeping in mind that
\nabla\Phi_{s}
is equivalent to
\Omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}
insofar as the analytic (or any Sobolev/Lebesgue) norm is concerned. Hence,

(4.5)

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{2}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert\Phi_{s}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert\nabla\Phi_{s}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lesssim\lVert u_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{2}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}\left(\lVert\Phi_{s}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\lVert\Omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}\right).

We proceed on to the terms arising out of
\mathcal{F}
. Making use of (
A.6
), we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle I_{3}\lesssim\lVert\Phi_{s}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{1}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}\lVert\Phi_{n}-\Phi_{s}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lesssim\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{1}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}\left(\lVert\Phi_{s}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\lVert\Phi_{n}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}\right),

and

(4.7)

\displaystyle I_{4}\lesssim\lVert\Phi_{s}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{1}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert\Omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert v^{2}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lesssim\lVert v^{2}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{1}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}\left(\lVert\Phi_{s}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\lVert\Omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}\right).

The term
I_{5}
leads to an almost identical estimate:

(4.8)

\displaystyle I_{5}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert v^{2}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{2}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}\left(\lVert\Phi_{s}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\lVert\Omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}\rVert_{G^{\frac{q}{2}}_{\sigma}}^{2}\right).

To handle the last term in (
4.3
), we begin by writing

\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\ws^1}}-\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\ws^2}}=-\frac{\absolutevalue{\ws^1}-\absolutevalue{\ws^2}}{\absolutevalue{\ws^1}\absolutevalue{\ws^2}}=-\frac{\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{1}+\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{2}}{\absolutevalue{\ws^1}\absolutevalue{\ws^2}\left(\absolutevalue{\ws^2}+\absolutevalue{\ws^1}\right)}\cdot\Omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}.

Upon application of (
A.6
), this time with
\absolutevalue{\omega}
replaced with
\absolutevalue{\omega^1}+\absolutevalue{\omega^2}
, we obtain

(4.9)

\displaystyle I_{6}

Appendix A
Analytic norm of
A^{\frac{r}{2}}\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\omega}}

For any
r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, denoting by
\lceil r\rceil
the least integer greater than or equal to
r
, we observe that

(A.1)

\displaystyle\lVert A^{\frac{r}{2}}\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\omega}}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=\lVert A^{\frac{p+r}{2}}e^{\sigma A^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\omega}}\rVert_{L^{2}}=\lVert\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma^{n}}{n!}A^{\frac{n+p+r}{2}}\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\omega}}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma_{0}^{n}}{n!}\lVert\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\omega}}\rVert_{H^{n+\lceil p\rceil+\lceil r\rceil}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma_{0}^{n}}{n!}\left(\lVert\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\omega}}\rVert_{L^{2}}+C_{0}^{n+\lceil p\rceil+\lceil r\rceil}\lVert D^{n+\lceil p\rceil+\lceil r\rceil}\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\omega}}\rVert_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{\sigma_{0}}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(C_{0}\sigma_{0})^{n}}{n!}\lVert D^{n+\lceil p\rceil+\lceil r\rceil}\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\omega}}\rVert_{L^{2}}.

In the above estimates,
C_{0}
is the Poincaré constant. To arrive at the last step, we made use of the fact that
\absolutevalue{\omega}\geq m_{f}
. The norm within the infinite sum may be estimated using the analyticity of
x^{-1}
for
x
bounded away from
0
. Indeed, we may expand
x^{-1}
in a convergent Taylor series about some sufficiently large
M>0
. (For instance,
M>X_{0}=1+\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize s}}^{0}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma_{0}}}+\lVert\omega_{\text{\scriptsize n}}^{0}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma_{0}}}
so that
\absolutevalue{\omega}\leq\lVert\omega\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma_{0}}}<M
.) Thus,

(A.2)

\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\omega}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\frac{1}{M^{n+1}}\left(\absolutevalue{\omega}-M\right)^{n}.

This series converges (absolutely) for
0<\absolutevalue{\omega}<2M
, showing that
\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\omega}}
is analytic in the open disc centered at
M
and of radius
M
. We may now complexify the function and apply Cauchy’s differentiation formula to evaluate the higher order derivatives as

(A.3)

D^{n}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)=\frac{n!}{2\pi i}\oint_{C}\frac{\frac{1}{z_{1}}}{(z_{1}-z)^{n+1}}dz_{1}.

We set
z=\absolutevalue{\omega}
and choose the integration contour to be a circle centered at
\absolutevalue{\omega}
and passing through, say
\frac{m_{f}}{2}
on the left end. Since
\absolutevalue{\omega}>m_{f}
, we obtain

(A.4)

\absolutevalue{D^n\left( \frac{1}{\abs{\omega}} \right)}\leq n!\frac{\frac{1}{\frac{m_{f}}{2}}}{\left(\absolutevalue{\omega}-\frac{m_{f}}{2}\right)^{n}}\lesssim\frac{2^{n}n!}{(m_{f})^{n}}.

Returning to (
A.1
), we now utilize the estimates in (
A.4
) which leads to

(A.5)

\displaystyle\lVert A^{\frac{r}{2}}\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\omega}}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{\sigma_{0}}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(C_{0}\sigma_{0})^{n}}{n!}\frac{2^{n+\lceil p\rceil+\lceil r\rceil}(n+\lceil p\rceil+\lceil r\rceil)!}{(m_{f})^{n+\lceil p\rceil+\lceil r\rceil}}

\displaystyle=e^{\sigma_{0}}+(C_{0}\sigma_{0})^{-\lceil p\rceil-\lceil r\rceil}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2C_{0}\sigma_{0}}{m_{f}}\right)^{n+\lceil p\rceil+\lceil r\rceil}(n+\lceil p\rceil+\lceil r\rceil)(n+\lceil p\rceil+\lceil r\rceil-1)\dots(n+1)

\displaystyle=e^{\sigma_{0}}+(C_{0}\sigma_{0})^{-\lceil p\rceil-\lceil r\rceil}\left.\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta^{n}n(n-1)\dots(n-\lceil p\rceil-\lceil r\rceil+1)\right|_{\beta=\frac{2C_{0}\sigma_{0}}{m_{f}}}.

For
\beta<1
, or equivalently
\sigma_{0}<\frac{m_{f}}{2C_{0}}
, the series converges uniformly (and absolutely). This allows us to evaluate the series explicitly,

(A.6)

\displaystyle\lVert A^{\frac{r}{2}}\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\omega}}\rVert_{G^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{\sigma_{0}}+(C_{0}\sigma_{0})^{-\lceil p\rceil-\lceil r\rceil}\beta^{\lceil p\rceil+\lceil r\rceil}\left.\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{d}{d\beta}\right)^{\lceil p\rceil+\lceil r\rceil}\beta^{n}\right|_{\beta=\frac{2C_{0}\sigma_{0}}{m_{f}}}

\displaystyle=e^{\sigma_{0}}+(C_{0}\sigma_{0})^{-\lceil p\rceil-\lceil r\rceil}\beta^{\lceil p\rceil+\lceil r\rceil}\left.\left(\frac{d}{d\beta}\right)^{\lceil p\rceil+\lceil r\rceil}\frac{1}{1-\beta}\right|_{\beta=\frac{2C_{0}\sigma_{0}}{m_{f}}}

\displaystyle=e^{\sigma_{0}}+\left(\frac{2}{m_{f}}\right)^{\lceil p\rceil+\lceil r\rceil}\frac{(\lceil p\rceil+\lceil r\rceil)!}{\left(1-\frac{2C_{0}\sigma_{0}}{m_{f}^{2}}\right)^{\lceil p\rceil+\lceil r\rceil+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{\sigma_{0}}+\frac{(\lceil p\rceil+\lceil r\rceil)!}{\left(\frac{m_{f}}{2}-C_{0}\sigma_{0}\right)^{\lceil p\rceil+\lceil r\rceil+1}}.

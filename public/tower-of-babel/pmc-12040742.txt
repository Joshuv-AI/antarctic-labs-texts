Novel FGF21 analogues through structure-based optimization for therapeutic development
Authors: Yiqing Guo, Yuxuan Bao, Zhichao Chen, Zhiheng Rao, Yongde Luo, Sheng Ye, Si Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractFibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) plays a pivotal role in regulating metabolic processes and energy homeostasis, making it a promising therapeutic avenue for various obesity-related conditions. However, its therapeutic efficacy faces challenges due to its suboptimal pharmacokinetics and bioactivity. To overcome these limitations, we adapt a strategy in which key amino acid residues responsible for enhanced activity are pinpointed through sequence alignment and comparative analysis to develop long-acting FGF21 analogs. The mutant FGF21 analogs are fused with the Fc fragment. Here, we report the design, identification, and characterization of two distinct Fc-fused FGF21 analogs, Fc-FGF21(P119R) and Fc-FGF21(H125R), with significantly augmented potency. These findings hold promise for clinical applications, offering potential interventions for obesity-related metabolic disorders.Keywords: fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21), metabolic diseases, protein engineering, sequence analysis

Introduction

The fibroblast growth factor (FGF) family is a large family of signaling polypeptides that play diverse roles in cell growth, proliferation, differentiation, angiogenesis and metabolism [ 1‒ 3]. Among these, fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) is a member of the endocrine FGF19 subfamily [ 4‒ 6]. Structurally characterized by its distinctive β-trefoil fold, FGF21 binds to its receptor FGFR1c kinase through its N-terminal segment and its co-receptor β-Klotho (KLB) via its C-terminal tail [ 7‒ 9]. The formation of the FGF21-FGFR-KLB signaling complex(s) in a limited number of metabolic or endocrine tissues initiates intracellular signaling through the ERK1/2 cascade [ 7, 10‒ 13]. The effects of FGF21 are pleotropic, maintaining metabolic and energy homeostasis by lowering blood glucose level and improving blood lipid profiles, with multiple potential metabolic benefits [ 12, 14‒ 22].

Obesity, fatty liver diseases and diabetes mellitus are among the prevalent metabolic disorders, often complicating other organ-specific diseases [23]. The development of more effective pharmacotherapies is imperative. FGF21 is a competitive candidate. An FGF21 analogue has been planned to enter phase III clinical trials for patients with nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), liver fibrosis, and hypertriglyceridemia [ 24, 25]. Notably, FGF21 does not induce hypoglycemia, cell proliferation or weight gain [12]. However, its limited pharmacokinetic property and biological potency remain significant challenges for its clinical application [ 26‒ 29].

Several strategies have been explored to increase the half-life and activity of FGF21. One such example is LY2405319, which incorporates an additional disulfide bond through the L118C and A134C mutations. This modification is aimed at bolstering its physical stability. Furthermore, LY2405319 has a deletion of four N-terminal amino acids at the N-terminus to mitigate proteolytic cleavage and an S167A mutation to eliminate O-linked glycosylation, facilitating large-scale, homogenous production in yeast [30]. Another innovative approach involves an Fc fusion with two strategic mutation sites in FGF21, effectively reducing aggregation and degradation [31]. Despite these promising advancements, FGF21 and its analogues continue to face challenges related to their relatively low efficacy. Furthermore, in the initial phases of clinical trials, they have not consistently met the primary endpoints for glycemic control [29], indicating that novel improvements are needed. For instance, the introduction of 5 mutation sites into FGF21 on the basis of its structure increases its potency [32]. Introduction of R185W and L166F mutations on the basis of the structure of β-Klotho in the complex of the FGF21 C-terminal peptide resulted in a tenfold increase in the potency of FGF21 [9]. Compared with wild-type FGF21, a novel FGF21-FGF19 chimera with G43C and A31C mutations and a β1-β2 loop from FGF19 exhibited improved anti-diabetic activities [33]. An FGF1 ΔHBS-FGF21 C-tail chimera, created by substituting the thermally labile and low-receptor-affinity core of FGF21 with an HS binding–deficient endocrinized core of FGF1, exhibited a fivefold longer half-life and a significantly improved efficacy in correcting hyperglycemia and ameliorating insulin resistance in diabetic mice and monkeys [34]. Despite these efforts, there remains untapped potential for further enhancements in the efficacy of FGF21.

In this study, we designed three FGF21 mutants through sequence and structural analysis: L100R, P119R, and H125R. Compared with native FGF21, these mutants presented an approximately twofold increase in activity. A further fusion of the N-terminal portion with the Fc region of human IgG1 enhanced their pharmacokinetic profile. Specifically, two Fc-FGF21 mutants, Fc-FGF21(P119R) and Fc-FGF21(H125R), retained significantly increased potency. These findings offer a promising avenue for developing FGF21-based effective treatment options for metabolic diseases.

Materials and Methods

Protein expression and purification

Wild-type FGF21 and its mutants were cloned and inserted into the pET-28a-SUMO plasmid, which was subsequently expressed in Escherichia coli BL21(DE3) cells at 16°C. Following expression, proteins were purified via Ni-NTA resin, and SUMO tags were removed via the ULP1 protease. Size exclusion chromatography was employed for further purification.

The Fc fragment of human IgG1 was fused to the N-terminus of FGF21 via a (GGGGS) 5 linker. Wild-type Fc- FGF21 and its mutants were cloned and inserted into the pET-30a plasmid, which was subsequently expressed in E. coli BL21(DE3) cells at 37°C. Fc-FGF21 and its mutants were expressed as inclusion bodies. After denaturation and refolding in a buffer consisting of 100 mM Tris-HCl, pH 8.0, containing 400 mM L-arginine, 2 mM EDTA, 0.5 mM PMSF, 5 mM reduced glutathione, and 0.5 mM oxidized glutathione, the proteins were further purified via ion exchange chromatography and size exclusion chromatography.

Cell culture and stimulation

HEK293T cells engineered to stably express beta-Klotho (HEK293T-KLB) were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin. For the cell stimulation experiments, HEK293T-KLB cells were seeded in 6-well cell culture plates at a density of 4 × 10 5 cells per well and cultured for 24 h in medium devoid of FBS. The cells were subsequently subjected to stimulation with FGF21 and its mutants for a duration of 1 h.

Downstream gene transcription analysis

Total RNA was extracted from the lysed cells. cDNA templates for PCR were generated via oligo-(dT) primers and a HiScript III 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Two microliters of a 1/10 dilution of the 1st strand cDNA mixture was used in each PCR in a total reaction volume of 10 μL via ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme) performed on a Bio-Rad T100 Thermal Cycler (Bio-Rad, Hercules, USA). After confirming the specificity of the PCR products with a melting curve, the comparative cycle threshold method was used to calculate the expression levels of genes of interest, with GAPDH serving as an internal standard. qPCR was conducted according to the following thermal cycling protocol: initial denaturation at 95°C for 3 min, followed by 40 cycles of denaturation at 95°C for 15 s and annealing/extension at 60°C for 30 s. qPCR was performed in triplicate for each sample. cFos and Egr1 mRNA levels were used as response activity indicators. The qRT‒PCR primer pairs used are as follows: hEgr1 forward, 5′-TCCCATTTACTCAGCGGCAC-3′ and reverse, 5′-TGGAAACAGGTAGTCGGGGA-3′; hcFOS forward, 5′-CACTCCAAGCGGAGACAGAC-3′ and reverse, 5′-AGGTCATCAGGGATCTTGCAG-3′; hGAPDH forward, 5′-GGAGCGAGATCCCTCCAAAAT-3′ and reverse, 5′-GGCTGTTGTCATACTTCTCATGG-3′.

Pharmacokinetic assay

Male C57/BL6 mice were housed in a room at a temperature of 24 ± 1°C with a 12 h:12 h light/dark cycle and ad libitum access to tap water. Sixty 6-week-old mice were intravenously injected with Fc-FGF21 or its mutants diluted in PBS (20 mg/kg body weight). All animal experiments in this study were performed in accordance with the guidelines and regulations of the Laboratory Animal Ethical and Welfare Committee of Tianjin University (approval no. TJUE-2023-277).

The plasma concentrations of Fc-FGF21 and its mutants were quantified via a sensitive sandwich enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) using a kit form Elabscience (Wuhan, China). Serum samples were subjected to incubation in micro ELISA plate wells precoated with an antibody specific for human FGF21 (Elabscience). Following a washing step, a biotinylated detection antibody and an avidin-horseradish peroxidase (HRP) conjugate (Elabscience) were added. After 1 h of incubation and washing, a substrate mixture was added to each well to initiate an enzyme-substrate reaction. The optical density (OD) at a wavelength of 450 nm was spectrophotometrically measured for the quantification of human FGF21 concentration.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as the mean ± SEM of three independent experiments. Statistical comparisons of the means among the groups were made via one-way ANOVA. Differences between the means of individual groups were analyzed via GraphPad Prism 9 (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, USA)

Results

Structure-based design of mutant FGF21 with enhanced surface positive charge

Given that heparan sulfate (HS) is still an essential cofactor for FGF21 signaling, we hypothesized that enhancing the binding affinity of FGF21 to HS may improve its potency. The primary sequences of FGF21 exhibit a remarkable degree of conservation across diverse mammalian species, as shown in Figure 1, with similarities exceeding 80%. To avoid compromising the structural integrity of human FGF21, we opted to mutate nonconserved rather than conserved residues. Some of these residues, including H1, D46, Q47, A81, H87, E91, L100, E101, H117, P119, N121, H125, and E148, are arginines or lysines in certain species. While the rationale for their retention in other species remains unclear, their presence in natural sequences suggests a potential selective advantage. They are of particular interest, as they all localize on the protein’s surface, as depicted in its structure ( Figure 2). This led us to hypothesize that mutating some of these nonconserved residues to basic amino acids may create a positively charged protein surface without disrupting its overall structure, which is anticipated to increase the protein’s affinity for negatively charged HS, potentially augmenting its binding to FGFRs. Furthermore, a sequence alignment between FGF21 and FGF1 revealed that certain nonconserved residues in FGF21 are in proximity to the HS-binding region of FGF1. Given that arginine has a higher isoelectric point than lysine does, we chose to introduce single-point mutations, in which each of these 13 amino acids was mutated to arginine individually.

Purification of FGF21 mutants and determination of their in vitro activity

To assess the activity of FGF21 mutants with residues H1, D46, Q47, A81, H87, E91, L100, E101, H117, P119, N121, H125, and E148 mutated to arginine, molecular cloning techniques were used for the expression and purification of recombinant proteins in E. coli. During protein purification, four of these mutants, H1R, E91R, E101R, and N121R, exhibited significant defects in adequate expression, resulting in their exclusion for further experiments ( Supplementary Figure S1‒S8). The remaining FGF21 mutants were subjected to in vitro evaluation of their functional activity. Human 293T cells engineered to stably express human KLB were serum-starved for 24 h, followed by treatment with the FGF21 mutants at a concentration of 1.5 nM for 1 h. The expression levels of Egr1 and cFos, early downstream target genes of the ERK1/2 pathway, are indicators of FGF21 signaling pathway activation [ 35‒ 37]. Our analysis revealed that the L100R, P119R, and H125R mutants significantly enhanced the activation of the Egr1 and cFos genes, with H125R being the most potent one ( Figure 3A,B).

We then sought to maximize FGF21 variant efficacy by combining these mutations, including L100R + H125R, P119R + H125R, and L100R + P119R + H125R. Contrary to our initial expectations, these three combination mutants failed to improve potency compared with the single-point mutants, although they did exceed the potency of wild-type FGF21 ( Figure 3C). Further research is needed to understand the underlying mechanism involved.

Fc fusion with FGF21 mutants at the N-terminus retains bioactivity

To increase the half-life, we made Fc fusions with these three mutants as well as the native FGF21 at the N-terminus with a flexible linker, as previously reported [31]. We observed that the Fc fusion did not alter the activity of FGF21 ( Figure 4). While Fc-FGF21 mutants showed slightly reduced potency compared with native FGF21, potentially due to steric hindrance from the Fc moiety, the Fc-FGF21(H125R) mutant remained the most potent ( Figure 4). These results indicate the feasibility of Fc fusion at the N-terminus of FGF21 mutants.

Improved pharmacokinetics of Fc-FGF21 mutants

Next, we assessed the potential influence of these mutations and Fc fusion on the pharmacokinetic attributes of FGF21 in mice by assessing serum FGF21 with a sandwich ELISA. The mice were subjected to intravenous administration of Fc-FGF21 and its mutants, each at a standardized concentration of 20 mg/kg. Blood samples were collected at 5 min, 3 h, 12 h, 36 h, and 72 h post-administration. We observed a similarity in the half-life between Fc-FGF21 and its mutants, which was approximately 13 h on average ( Figure 5 and Table 1), whereas the half-life of native FGF21 was 0.5‒1.5 h [ 34, 38‒ 40]. These results support the suitability of these variants for various biomedical applications.

Discussion

FGF21 represents a new generation of metabolic regulators with endocrine action. It plays a crucial role in regulating energy metabolism and maintaining overall metabolic balance. In rodents, primates and humans, it has been demonstrated to stimulate the oxidation of fatty acids and ketogenesis and to increase energy expenditure by enhancing brown adipose tissue thermogenesis, leading to increased fat breakdown and body weight reduction. The therapeutic potential of FGF21 analogues in addressing metabolic diseases is well established. Several FGF21 analogues have shown beneficial effects on various metabolic dysfunctions, including weight loss, hyperglycemia, hyperlipidemia, insulin resistance, cardiovascular disease, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, steatohepatitis and fibrosis [ 22, 24, 25]. However, the clinical utility of FGF21 is hindered by its suboptimal pharmacokinetics and low efficacy. In this study, we identified mutations in three key residues in FGF21 that lead to enhanced activity and adopted Fc fusion to prolong its serum half-life.

Although FGF21 has a considerably reduced affinity for heparin or HS, it still requires the presence of an HS motif for enhanced formation of a functional ternary complex, signaling, and biological activity [ 41, 42]. Our structural analysis revealed that the engineered surface-presenting P119R and H125R mutations have the potential to add more positive charges to the putative HS-binding domain characteristic of canonic FGFs, whereas other mutations, H1R, D46R, Q47R, A81R, H87R, E91R, E101R, H117R, N121R, and E148R, are not near the HS-binding domain. The structural characteristics of these mutants are in line with their differential biological potencies observed for activating early response genes of the MAPK pathway, such as native FGF21.

To gain a deeper understanding of the structural basis for the correlation of mutation and activity, we performed a structural alignment between the FGF21 core structure and the FGF23-FGFR1c-Klotho-HS quaternary complex ( Supplementary Figure S9), since FGF21 and FGF23 belong to the same subfamily of FGFs and share the same receptor, FGFR1c [ 33, 43, 41]. We found that residues P119 and H125 in FGF21 are located in close proximity to the heparin sulfate binding site. Mutation of these residues to positively charged arginines might increase the affinity of FGF21 for negatively charged heparan sulfate proteoglycans (HSPGs), thereby promoting its ability to bind to the receptor complex. Additionally, L100 in FGF21 is close to S223 of FGFR1c. The introduction of arginine at L100 could facilitate the formation of hydrogen bonds with S223, potentially leading to increased binding affinity of FGF21 for FGFR1c, confirming the validity of our strategy.

Furthermore, by fusing with the Fc fragment, the half-life of FGF21, as well as L100R, P119R and H125R, is significantly prolonged. Among these mutants, the Fc-FGF21(H125R) analogue displays favorable potency. These findings could lead to the development of an effective treatment strategy for patients with obesity, hyperlipidemia, type 2 diabetes, fatty liver diseases, and metabolic syndrome.

In summary, our study contributes valuable insights into the development of FGF21-based therapies. Further research is needed to validate the efficacy and safety of these novel FGF21 analogs in both preclinical and clinical settings.

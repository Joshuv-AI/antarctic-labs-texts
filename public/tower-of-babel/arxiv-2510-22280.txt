Law of large numbers for non-linear traces of the Choquet type on finite factors
Masaru Nagisa, Yasuo Watatani

Abstract
. We introduced non-linear traces of the Choquet type and the Sugeno type on semi-finite factors ℳ \mathcal{M} in [ 36 ] as a non-commutative analog of the Choquet integral and Sugeno integral for non-additive measures. We need a weighted dimension function on the projections of ℳ \mathcal{M} , which is an analog of a monotone measure. In this paper, we study the law of large numbers for non-linear traces of the Choquet type on finite factors ℳ \mathcal{M} . Since averages do not converge in general, we study the range of their accumulation points, that is, we estimate their limit supremum and limit infimum. We examine the trials of sequences consisting of self-adjoint operators, which appear in coin toss or Powers’ binary shifts. We have also found some unexpected examples of Powers’ binary shifts which satisfy what we call the uniform norm law of large numbers . This is an attempt at non-linear and non-commutative probability theory on matrix algebras and factors of type II 1 {\rm II}_{1} . AMS subject classification: Primary 46L53. Secondary 46L51, 47B06, 47B10, 60F15. Key words: law of large numbers, non-linear trace, finite factor, was non-commutative probability theory

Non-linear traces of the Choquet type

In our paper
[
36
]
, we introduced non-linear traces of the Choquet type and Sugeno type on a semifinite factor
M
as a non-commutative analog of the Choquet integral and Sugeno integral for non-additive measures.
We need a weighted dimension function
p\mapsto\alpha(\tau(p))
for any projection
p\in M
, which is an analog of
a monotone measure. In this paper, we only consider non-linear traces of the Choquet type on a
finite factor

\mathcal{M}
.
We begin to recall the notion of non-linear traces of the Choquet type.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a set and
{\mathcal{B}}
a ring of sets on
\Omega
, that is,

{\mathcal{B}}
is a family of subsets of
\Omega
which is closed under the operations
union
\cup
and set theoretical difference
\backslash
. Hence
{\mathcal{B}}
is also
closed under intersection
\cap
.
A function
\mu:{\mathcal{B}}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is called a

monotone measure
if
\mu
satisfies

(1)

\mu(\emptyset)=0
, and

(2)

For any
A,B\in{\mathcal{B}}
, if
A\subset B
,
then
\mu(A)\leq\mu(B)
.

If
\Omega
is not a discrete space, we often assume that
{\mathcal{B}}
is a
\sigma
-field.

Definition 2.2
.

(Choquet integral)
Let
\Omega
be a set and
{\mathcal{B}}
a
\sigma
-field on
\Omega
.
Let
\mu:{\mathcal{B}}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
be a monotone measure on
\Omega
.
Let
f
be a non-negative measurable function on
\Omega
in the sense that
f^{-1}(A)\in{\mathcal{B}}

for any Borel set
A
on
[0,\infty]
. Then the Choquet integral of
f
is defined by

\displaystyle{\rm(C)}\int fd\mu

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{x\in\Omega\ |\ f(x)\geq s\})ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{x\in\Omega\ |\ f(x)>s\})ds.

Remark 2.3
.

If
f
is a simple function with

f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\chi_{A_{i}},\ \ \ A_{i}\cap A_{j}=\emptyset\ (i\not=j),

then the Choquet integral of
f
is given by

{\rm(C)}\int fd\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(a_{\sigma(i)}-a_{\sigma(i+1)})\mu(B_{i})+a_{\sigma(n)}\mu(B_{n}),

where
\sigma
is a permutation on
\{1,2,\dots n\}
such that

a_{\sigma(1)}\geq a_{\sigma(2)}\geq\dots\geq a_{\sigma(n)}

and

B_{i}:=A_{\sigma(1)}\cup A_{\sigma(2)}\cup\dots\cup A_{\sigma(i)}
.

Choquet integral has the following properties:

(1) (monotonicity)
For any non-negative measurable functions
f
and
g
on
\Omega
,

0\leq f\leq g\Rightarrow 0\leq{\rm(C)}\int fd\mu\leq{\rm(C)}\int gd\mu.

(2) (comonotone additivity)
For any non-negative measurable functions
f
and
g
on
\Omega
,
if
f
and
g
are comonotone, then

{\rm(C)}\int(f+g)d\mu={\rm(C)}\int fd\mu+{\rm(C)}\int gd\mu,

where
f
and
g
are
comonotone
if
for any
s,t\in\Omega
we have that

(f(s)-f(t))(g(s)-g(t))\geq 0,

that is,

f(s)<f(t)\Rightarrow g(s)\leq g(t).

(3) (positive homogeneity)
For any non-negative measurable function
f
on
\Omega
and
any scalar
k\geq 0
,

{\rm(C)}\int kfd\mu=k\Bigl({\rm(C)}\int fd\mu\Bigr),

where
0\cdot\infty=0
.

Remark 2.4
.

It is important to note that the Choquet integral can be
essentially
characterized as a non-linear monotone positive functional
which is positively homogeneous and comonotonic additive.

Remark 2.5
.

When
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}
, if
f
and
g
are monotone increasing, then
f
and
g
are comonotone.

For a topological space
X
, let
C(X)
be the set of all continuous functions on
X
and

C(X)^{+}
be the set of all non-negative continuous functions on
X
. More generally, for a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
, we denote by
A^{+}
the set of positive operators of
A
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
A
be a
C^{*}
-algebra. A non-linear positive map

\varphi:A^{+}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is called a
trace
if

\varphi
is unitarily invariant, that is,

\varphi(uau^{*})=\varphi(a)
for any
a\in A^{+}
and any unitary

u
in the multiplier algebra
M
of
A
.

•

\varphi
is
monotone
if

a\leq b
implies
\varphi(a)\leq\varphi(b)
for any
a,b\in A^{+}
.

•

\varphi
is
positively homogeneous
if

\varphi(ka)=k\varphi(a)
for any
a\in A^{+}
and any scalar
k\geq 0
,
where we regard
0\cdot\infty=0
.

•

\varphi
is
comonotonic additive on the spectrum
if

\varphi(f(a)+g(a))=\varphi(f(a))+\varphi(g(a))

for any
a\in A^{+}
and
any comonotonic functions
f
and
g
in
C(\sigma(a))^{+}
( with
f(0)=g(0)=0
if
a
is not invertible), where

f(a)
is a functional calculus of
a
by
f
and
C(\sigma(a))
is
the set of continuous functions on the spectrum
\sigma(a)
of
a
.

•

\varphi
is
monotonic increasing additive on the spectrum
if

\varphi(f(a)+g(a))=\varphi(f(a))+\varphi(g(a))

for any
a\in A^{+}
and
any monotone increasing functions
f
and
g
in
C(\sigma(a))^{+}
(with
f(0)=g(0)=0
if
a
is not invertible).
We may replace
C(\sigma(a))^{+}
by
C([0,\infty))^{+}
, since any monotone increasing function in
C(\sigma(a))^{+}

can be extended to a monotone increasing function in
C([0,\infty))^{+}
.
Then by induction,
we also have

\varphi(\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(a))=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi(f_{i}(a))

for any monotone increasing functions
f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n}
in
C(\sigma(a))^{+}
( with
f_{i}(0)=0
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
if

a
is not invertible).

For

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra on a Hilbert space
H
with a faithful normal semifinite trace
\tau
.
Let

be a positive self-adjoint operator affiliated with
\mathcal{M}

,
where

For

\lambda_{t}(a)

\lambda_{t}(a):=\inf\{s\geq 0\;|\;\tau(e_{(s,\infty)}(a))\leq t\},

where we put

.
Furthermore, if

\tau

\lambda_{t}(a)=\inf\{\|ap\|\ |\;\ p\in\mathcal{M}:\text{ projection s.t. }\tau(I-p)\leq t\}.

We also have a min-max type expression

\lambda_{t}(a)=\inf\{\sup_{\xi\in pH,\|\xi\|=1}\langle a\xi,\xi\rangle\ |\ p\in\mathcal{M}:\text{ projection s.t. }\tau(I-p)\leq t\}.

For a general operator

\mathcal{M}
,

-th singular value” (i.e. generalized s-number)

(2)

(5)

(6)

\mu_{t}(a^{*})=\mu_{t}(a)

(7)

\mu_{t}(uau^{*})=\mu_{t}(a)

(8)

|\mu_{t}(a)-\mu_{t}(b)|\leq\|a-b\|

(9)

\mu_{t}(f(a))=f(\mu_{t}(a))

(10)

Definition 2.10
.

Let
\alpha:\{0,1,2,\dots,n\}\rightarrow[0,1]
be a
monotone increasing function with
\alpha(0)=0
and
\alpha(n)=1
.

Let

\lambda(a)=(\lambda_{1}(a),\lambda_{2}(a),\dots,\lambda_{n}(a))
be the list of the
eigenvalues of

in decreasing order :

\lambda_{1}(a)\geq\lambda_{2}(a)\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}(a)
with
counting multiplicities.
Define

\varphi_{\alpha}:(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))^{+}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{+}

as follows:

\displaystyle\varphi_{\alpha}(a)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(\lambda_{i}(a)-\lambda_{i+1}(a))\alpha(i)+\lambda_{n}(a)\alpha(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=2}^{n}\lambda_{i}(a)(\alpha(i)-\alpha(i-1))+\lambda_{1}(a)\alpha(1).

We call
\varphi_{\alpha}
the non-linear trace of the Choquet type associated with
\alpha
.

In the rest of this section, we assume that
\mathcal{M}
is a factor of type
{\rm II}_{1}

\tau

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a factor of type
{\rm II}_{1}
.
Let
\alpha:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
be a
monotone increasing function with
\alpha(0)=0
and
\alpha(1)=1
. Assume that
\alpha
is left continuous.
We think of a weighted dimension function
p\mapsto\alpha(\tau(p))
for any projection

as an analog of

Define a non-linear trace
\varphi_{\alpha}:\mathcal{M}^{+}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
of the Choquet type associated with
\alpha

as follows: For
a\in\mathcal{M}^{+}
with the spectral decomposition
a=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda de_{\lambda}(a)
, let

\varphi_{\alpha}(a):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha(\tau(e_{(s,\infty)}(a)))ds.

Then for any unitary

, we have that

\varphi_{\alpha}(uau^{*})=\varphi_{\alpha}(a).

Remark 2.12
.

As shown in
[
36
]
,
\varphi_{\alpha}
is monotone and sequentially normal in the sense that for any increasing sequence

(a_{n})_{n}
in
\mathcal{M}^{+}

a_{n}\nearrow a\in\mathcal{M}^{+}
in the strong operator topology, then
\varphi_{\alpha}(a_{n})\nearrow\varphi_{\alpha}(a)
.

For

,

\varphi_{\alpha}(a):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha(\tau(e_{(s,\infty)}(a)))ds=\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha(\tau(e_{[s,\infty)}(a)))ds.

For example, if

has a finite spectrum and

a=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}p_{i}
is the spectral decomposition with
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq\dots\geq a_{n}>0
, then

\varphi_{\alpha}(a)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(a_{i}-a_{i+1})\alpha(\tau(p_{1}+\dots+p_{i}))+a_{n}\alpha(\tau(p_{1}+\dots+p_{n})).

We can express non-linear traces of the Choquet type using the “generalized

Proposition 2.13
.

(
[
36
, Proposition 2.24]
)

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a factor of type
{\rm II}_{1}

with a faithful normal finite trace
\tau
with
\tau(I)=1
, and
let
\alpha:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
be a
monotone increasing left continuous function with
\alpha(0)=0
and
\alpha(I)=1
.
Then the non-linear trace
\varphi_{\alpha}
is also described as follows: Let
\nu_{\alpha}
be the Lebesgue-Stieltjes measure

\alpha

\nu_{\alpha}([a,b))=\alpha(b)-\alpha(a)

For any

we have that

\varphi_{\alpha}(a):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha(\tau(e_{(s,\infty)}(a)))ds=\int_{0}^{1}\lambda_{t}(a)d\nu_{\alpha}(t).

Remark 2.14
.

Since
\mathcal{M}
be a factor of type
{\rm II}_{1}
,
\varphi_{\alpha}
is operator norm continuous on
\mathcal{M}^{+}
. In fact,
for each

,

is operator norm continuous and

||\lambda_{t}(a)||\leq||a||
.
Assume that
\|a_{n}-a\|\rightarrow 0

for

. Since
\nu_{\alpha}
is a probability measure, bounded convergence theorem implies
that
\varphi_{\alpha}(a_{n})=\int_{0}^{1}\lambda_{t}(a_{n})d\nu_{\alpha}(t)
converges to

\int_{0}^{1}\lambda_{t}(a)d\nu_{\alpha}(t)=\varphi_{\alpha}(a)
.

Example 2.15
.

Let
\alpha(x)=x
; then we can recover the trace
\tau
, and for any

,

\varphi_{\alpha}(a)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau(e_{(s,\infty)}(a))ds=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda_{t}(a)dt=\tau(a).

Example 2.16

3.
Three extensions of non-linear traces of the Choquet type to the self-adjoint operators

The definition of non-linear traces of the Choquet type in Section 2 is restricted to the positive operators. In this section,
we introduce three extensions of non-linear traces of the Choquet type to the self-adjoint operators, that is, symmetric
extension, anti-symmetric extension, and translatable extension, which are analogs of
symmetric, anti-symmetric, and translatable Choquet integrals. First, we recall the definitions of these extensions of Choquet integrals.

Let
X
be a set and
{\mathcal{B}}
a
\sigma
-field on
X
.
Let
\mu:{\mathcal{B}}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
be a monotone measure on
X
.
Let
f
be a real valued measurable function on
X
. Put
f_{+}:=\frac{1}{2}(|f|+f)
and

f_{-}:=\frac{1}{2}(|f|-f)
. Then
f=f_{+}-f_{-}
.

Definition 3.1
.

(symmetric Choquet integral)
The symmetric Choquet integral of
f
is defined by

{\rm(C_{s})}\int fd\mu:={\rm(C)}\int f_{+}d\mu-{\rm(C)}\int f_{-}d\mu,

if not both terms
on the right-hand side are infinite.

Definition 3.2
.

(anti-symmetric Choquet integral)
Assume that
\mu(X)<+\infty
. Define the dual monotone measure
\overline{\mu}
by

\overline{\mu}(B):=\mu(X)-\mu(B^{c})\ \ \ (B\in\mathcal{B}).

The anti-symmetric Choquet integral of
f
is defined by

{\rm(C_{a})}\int fd\mu:={\rm(C)}\int f_{+}d\mu-{\rm(C)}\int f_{-}d\overline{\mu}.

Definition 3.3
.

(translatable Choquet integral)
Assume that
\mu(X)<+\infty
. Suppose that there exists a real constant
b
such that

f(x)\geq b
for all
x\in X
.
Then the translatable Choquet integral of
f
is defined by

{\rm(C_{t})}\int fd\mu:={\rm(C)}\int(f-b)d\mu+b\mu(X).

This definition does not depend on the choice of such
b
. We have translatability, that is, for any real number
c
, we have that

{\rm(C_{t})}\int(f+c)d\mu:={\rm(C_{t})}\int fd\mu+c\mu(X).

Moreover, translatable Choquet integral is equal to anti-symmetric Choquet integral.

Now we shall consider the analog of these facts to three extensions of non-linear traces of the Choquet type.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be a factor of type
{\rm II}_{1}
with a faithful normal finite trace
\tau
with
\tau(I)=1
, and
let
\alpha:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
be a
monotone increasing continuous function with
\alpha(0)=0
and
\alpha(1)=1
.
Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{s.a.}
be the set of all self-adjoint operators. For
a\in{\mathcal{M}}_{s.a.}
, put

a_{+}:=\frac{1}{2}(|a|+a)\in\mathcal{M}_{+}
and
a_{-}:=\frac{1}{2}(|a|-a)\in\mathcal{M}_{+}
.
Then
a=a_{+}-a_{-}
and
a_{+}a_{-}=0
.

Definition 3.4
.

(symmetric non-linear trace of the Choquet type)
The symmetric non-linear trace of the Choquet type is defined by

\varphi_{\alpha}^{s}(a):=\varphi_{\alpha}(a_{+})-\varphi_{\alpha}(a_{-}).

Definition 3.5
.

(anti-symmetric non-linear trace of the Choquet type)
Define the dual weight
\overline{\alpha}:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,\infty)
of
\alpha
by

\overline{\alpha}(t)=\alpha(1)-\alpha(1-t)=1-\alpha(1-t)\quad(t\in[0,1]).

Then
\overline{\alpha}
is a monotone increasing continuous function with

\overline{\alpha}(0)=0
,
\overline{\alpha}(1)=1
and
\overline{\overline{\alpha}}=\alpha
.
Then the anti-symmetric non-linear trace of the Choquet type is defined by

\varphi_{\alpha}^{a}(a):=\varphi_{\alpha}(a_{+})-\varphi_{\overline{\alpha}}(a_{-}).

Example 3.6
.

(1)

If
\alpha(t)=t
, then
\overline{\alpha}=\alpha
and

\varphi_{\alpha}^{s}=\varphi_{\alpha}^{a}=\tau
is the usual trace.

(2)

If
\alpha(t)=\sqrt{t}
, then
\overline{\alpha}(t)=1-\sqrt{1-t}
and

\varphi_{\alpha}^{s}\not=\varphi_{\alpha}^{a}
in general.

Definition 3.7
.

(translatable non-linear trace of the Choquet type)
Take a constant real number
c
such that
a+cI\geq 0
.
Then translatable non-linear trace of the Choquet type is defined by

\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a):=\varphi_{\alpha}(a+cI)-c.

This definition does not depend on the choice of such
c
.
In fact, for a positive constant
k
,

\varphi_{\alpha}(kI)=\int_{0}^{1}\lambda_{t}(kI)d\nu_{\alpha}(t)=\int_{0}^{1}kd\nu_{\alpha}(t)=k(\alpha(1)-\alpha(0))=k.

If
a\geq 0
, then for any constant number
c\geq 0
, we have that

\varphi_{\alpha}(a+cI)=\int_{0}^{1}\lambda_{t}(a+cI)d\nu_{\alpha}(t)=\int_{0}^{1}(\lambda_{t}(a)+c)d\nu_{\alpha}(t)=\varphi_{\alpha}(a)+c.

For general
a\in M_{s.a.}
, take real numbers
c_{1}\geq c_{2}
such that

a+c_{1}I\geq 0
and
a+c_{2}I\geq 0
, then

a+c_{1}I=a+c_{2}I+(c_{1}-c_{2})I.

Therefore

\varphi_{\alpha}(a+c_{2}I)-c_{2}=\varphi_{\alpha}(a+c_{2}I+(c_{1}-c_{2})I)-(c_{1}-c_{2})-c_{2}=\varphi_{\alpha}(a+c_{1}I)-c_{1}.

Although the symmetric non-linear trace
\varphi_{\alpha}^{s}
of the Choquet type is symmetric in the sense that

\varphi_{\alpha}^{s}(-a)=-\varphi_{\alpha}^{s}(a),\quad(a\in\mathcal{M}_{s.a.}),

but it loses translatability, which is an important property we need. On the other hand,
the translatable non-linear trace
\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}
of the Choquet type has many useful properties as shown in the below. Therefore, we usually prefer
the translatable non-linear trace rather than the symmetric non-linear trace.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a factor of type
{\rm II}_{1}
with a normalized trace
\tau
.
Let
\alpha:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
be a
monotone increasing continuous function with
\alpha(0)=0
and
\alpha(1)=1

Then the translatable non-linear trace
\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}
of the Choquet type on
\mathcal{M}_{s.a.}
satisfies the following:

(1)

(positively homogeneity)

\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(ka)=k\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a)
for any

a\in{\mathcal{M}}_{s.a.}
and any scalar
k\geq 0
.

(2)

(translatability)
For any
a\in{\mathcal{M}}_{s.a.}
and any real number
c

\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a+cI)=\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a)+c.

(3)

(monotonicity)
For
a,b\in{\mathcal{M}}_{s.a.}
, if
a\leq b
, then
\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a)\leq\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(b).

(4)

(unitary invariance)
For any
a\in\mathcal{M}_{s.a.}
and any unitary
u\in\mathcal{M}
, we have that

\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(uau^{*})=\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a)
.

(5)

(triangle inequality)
Suppose that
\alpha
is concave on
[0,1]
. Then
for any
a,b\in{\mathcal{M}}_{s.a.}
,

\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a+b)\leq\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a)+\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(b).

(6)

(operator norm continuity)

\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}
is operator norm continuous.

Proof.

(1) Suppose that
a\geq 0
. Then for any scalar
k\geq 0
, we have that

\varphi_{\alpha}(ka)=\int_{0}^{1}\lambda_{t}(ka)d\nu_{\alpha}(t)=\int_{0}^{1}k\lambda_{t}(a)d\nu_{\alpha}(t)=k\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a).

Next suppose that
a\in{\mathcal{M}}_{s.a.}
. Take a real number
c
such that
a\geq cI
. Since
ka\geq kcI
,

\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(ka)=\varphi_{\alpha}(ka-kcI)+kc=k\varphi_{\alpha}(a-cI)+kc=k\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a).

(2) Take a real number
k
such that
a+cI\geq kI
,
a\geq kI
and
c\geq k
. Then

\displaystyle\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a+cI)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\alpha}(a+cI-kI)+kI=\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a)+(c-k)+k

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a)+c.

(3) For
a,b\in{\mathcal{M}}_{s.a.}
, suppose that
a\leq b
. Take a real number
k
such that
kI\leq a\leq b
.
Since
0\leq a-kI\leq b-kI
and
\varphi_{\alpha}
is monotone on
\mathcal{M}_{+}
, we have that

\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a)=\varphi_{\alpha}(a-kI)+k\leq\varphi_{\alpha}(b-kI)+k=\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(b).

(4) Take a constant real number
c
such that
a+cI\geq 0
. Then for any unitary
u\in{\mathcal{M}}
,

u(a+cI)u^{*}=uau^{*}+cI\geq 0
. Since
\varphi_{\alpha}
is unitarily invariant,

\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(uau^{*})=\varphi_{\alpha}(u(a+cI)u^{*})-c=\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a+cI)-c=\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a).

(5) Suppose that
\alpha
is concave on
[0,1]
. By Theorem
2.19
,
if
a
and
b
are in
\in{\mathcal{M}}_{+}
, we have that

\varphi_{\alpha}(a+b)\leq\varphi_{\alpha}(a)+\varphi_{\alpha}(b).

Now suppose that
a
and
b
are in
{\mathcal{M}}_{s.a.}
. Take a real number
c
such that
a\geq cI
and
b\geq cI
.
Then
a+b\geq 2cI
and

\displaystyle\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a+b)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\alpha}(a+b-2cI)+2cI=\varphi_{\alpha}(a-cI+b-cI)+2cI

\displaystyle\leq\varphi_{\alpha}(a-cI)+\varphi_{\alpha}(b-cI)+2c=\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a)+\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(b).

(6) Assume that
\|a_{n}-a\|\rightarrow 0
for
a_{n},a\in{\mathcal{M}}_{s.a.}
.
Then there exists a real number
k
such that
a_{n}\geq kI
and
a\geq kI
for any
n
. Then

\displaystyle\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a_{n})-\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\alpha}(a_{n}-kI)+k-(\varphi_{\alpha}(a-kI)+k)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\alpha}(a_{n}-kI)-\varphi_{\alpha}(a-kI)

converges to
0
, since
a_{n}-kI\geq 0
,
a-kI\geq 0
and
\varphi_{\alpha}
is operator norm continuous on
\mathcal{M}_{+}
.
∎

The anti-symmetric non-linear trace
\varphi_{\alpha}^{a}
behaves well for

.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a factor of type
{\rm II}_{1}
with a normalized trace
\tau
.
Let
\alpha:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
be a
monotone increasing continuous function with
\alpha(0)=0
and
\alpha(1)=1

Then the anti-symmetric non-linear trace
\varphi_{\alpha}^{a}
of the Choquet type satisfies the following:
For
x\in\mathcal{M}_{s.a.}

\varphi_{\alpha}^{a}(-x)=-\varphi_{\overline{\alpha}}^{a}(x).

Proof.

Since
x\in\mathcal{M}_{s.a.}
has the decompositions such that
x=x_{+}-x_{-}
and

-x=x_{-}-x_{+}
with
x_{+}x_{-}=0
, we have that

\varphi_{\alpha}^{a}(-x)=\varphi_{\alpha}(x_{-})-\varphi_{\overline{\alpha}}(x_{+}),

and

\varphi_{\overline{\alpha}}^{a}(x)=\varphi_{\overline{\alpha}}(x_{+})-\varphi_{\overline{\overline{\alpha}}}(x_{-})=\varphi_{\overline{\alpha}}(x_{+})-\varphi_{\alpha}(x_{-}).

Therefore
\varphi_{\alpha}^{a}(-x)=-\varphi_{\overline{\alpha}}^{a}(x)
.
∎

If
p\in\mathcal{M}_{s.a.}
is a projection, then
-p\geq-I
. Therefore

\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(-p)=\varphi_{\alpha}(-p+I)+(-1)=\alpha(\tau(I-p))-1=\alpha(1-\tau(p))-1=-\overline{\alpha}(\tau(p)).

On the other hand, by definition, we have that

\varphi_{\alpha}^{a}(-p)=-\varphi_{\overline{\alpha}}(p)=-\overline{\alpha}(\tau(p)).

Thus
\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(-p)
coincides with
\varphi_{\alpha}^{a}(-p)
.

More generally, this holds for any
a\in\mathcal{M}_{s.a.}
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a factor of type
{\rm II}_{1}
with a normalized trace
\tau
.
Let
\alpha:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
be a
monotone increasing continuous function with
\alpha(0)=0
and
\alpha(1)=1

Then for any
a\in\mathcal{M}_{s.a.}

\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a)=\varphi_{\alpha}^{a}(a)

Proof.

Firstly, consider the case that
a=-b
(

\displaystyle\varphi_{\alpha}^{a}(a)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\alpha}^{a}(0-b)=-\varphi_{\overline{\alpha}}(b)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{\alpha}(\tau(e_{(s,\infty)}(b)))ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}1-\alpha(1-\tau(e_{(s,\infty)}(b)))ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\|b\|}1-\alpha(\tau(e_{[0,s]}(b)))ds

\displaystyle=-\|b\|+\int_{0}^{\|b\|}\alpha(\tau(e_{[0,s]}(b)))ds

\displaystyle=-\|b\|+\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha(\tau(e_{[s,\infty]}(-b+\|b\|)))ds

\displaystyle=-\|b\|+\varphi_{\alpha}(-b+\|b\|)=\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}(a).

Secondly, consider a general

4.
Law of large numbers for non-linear traces of the Choquet type

Since the non-linear trace
\varphi_{\alpha}
of the Choquet type associated with
\alpha
is not additive,
averages do not converge in general. Hence, we study the range of their accumulation points, that is,
we estimate their limit supremum and limit infimum. Thus
the law of large numbers of a sequence
(a_{n})_{n}
in
\mathcal{M}_{s.a.}
for
\varphi_{\alpha}
is
generally described by the estimation of

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{\alpha}((\frac{a_{1}+\dots+a_{n}}{n})^{k})\text{ and }\liminf_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{\alpha}((\frac{a_{1}+\dots+a_{n}}{n})^{k}),

for any fixed
k=1,2,3,\dots
.

We start with a typical example to investigate the law of large numbers for non-linear traces
\varphi_{\alpha}
of the Choquet type.

Example 4.1
.

Let
A=\otimes_{n\in\mathbb{N}}M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
be the UHF algebra with the unique trace

\tau=\otimes_{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\rm tr}_{2}
. We denote by
\mathcal{M}
the AFD factor of type
{\rm II}_{1}

with the normalized trace
\tau
, which is constructed by the weak operator topology closure of
the image of GNS representation of
A
by
\tau
.
We regard that
A\subset\mathcal{M}
.
Define
\alpha:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
by
\alpha(t)={\sqrt{t}}
, then
\alpha
is a
monotone increasing continuous function with
\alpha(0)=0

Consider a sequence

where
\{q_{n}(k):k=0,1,\ldots,n\}
is a family of orthogonal projections with

\tau(q_{n}(k))=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n}}\binom{n}{k}
.
In fact, for any
n
, we define
q_{n}(k)

(k=0,1,\ldots,n)
as follows:

q_{n}(k)=(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{n}=k\\
i_{j}=0\text{ or }1\end{subarray}}e_{i_{1}}\otimes e_{i_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{n}})\otimes(\otimes_{n+1}^{\infty}I),

In particular, we have

\displaystyle q_{n}(0)=e_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{0}\otimes(\otimes_{n+1}^{\infty}I)

\displaystyle q_{n}(n)=e_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{1}\otimes(\otimes_{n+1}^{\infty}I).

\{q_{n}(k):k=0,1,\ldots,n\}

i_{j}=0\text{ or }1\end{subarray}}e_{i_{1}}\otimes e_{i_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{n}}.

\displaystyle p_{n}

\displaystyle=\overbrace{(e_{0}+e_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes(e_{0}+e_{1})}^{n-1}\otimes e_{0}\otimes(\otimes_{n+1}^{\infty}I)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}(n-k+1)q_{n+1}(k).

So this relation holds.

Then, by definition,

\varphi_{\alpha}(\frac{s_{n}}{n})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{r=0}^{k}\binom{n}{r}}.

To compute the limit as

2.13
to get that

\varphi_{\alpha}(\frac{s_{n}}{n})=\int_{0}^{1}\lambda_{t}(\frac{s_{n}}{n})d\nu_{\alpha}(t).

We identify

as a sequence
(a_{n})_{n}
of independent and identically distributed random variables of “coin toss” on

. By the classical strong law of large numbers,

By Proposition 3.2.11 in
[

converges to a constant function

By the dominated convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{\alpha}(\frac{s_{n}}{n})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{t}(\frac{s_{n}}{n})d\nu_{\alpha}(t)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{2}d\nu_{\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\leq{\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}}}=\varphi_{\alpha}(p_{1}).

Similarly, for any fixed

\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{\alpha}((\frac{s_{n}}{n})^{k})=\int_{0}^{1}(\frac{1}{2})^{k}d\nu_{\alpha}(t)=(\frac{1}{2})^{k}\leq({\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}}}\ )^{k}=(\varphi_{\alpha}(p_{1}))^{k}.

As in the example above, we need an

to formulate the law of large numbers for non-linear

\varphi_{\alpha}
of the Choquet type.
Since averages do not converge in general, we study the range of their accumulation points, that is,
we estimate their limit supremum and limit infimum.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a factor of type
{\rm II}_{1}
with a normalized trace
\tau
.
Let
\alpha:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
be a monotone increasing continuous function with
\alpha(0)=0

and
\alpha(1)=1
, and

\varphi_{\alpha}
a non-linear trace of the Choquet type associated with
\alpha
.
Let
(a_{n})_{n}
be an operator norm bounded sequence
(a_{n})_{n}
in
{\mathcal{M}}_{s.a.}
.
Put
s_{n}=a_{1}+a_{2}+\dots+a_{n}
.

(1)

Assume that
\alpha
is concave. Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose that there is a constant

C(k)\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
for any mutually different
i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
,

\varphi_{\alpha}({\rm Re}(a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\dots a_{i_{k}}))\leq C(k).

Then we have that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{\alpha}((\frac{s_{n}}{n})^{k})\leq C(k).

(2)

Assume that
\alpha
is convex. Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose that there is a constant

\hat{C}(k)\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
for any mutually different
i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
,

\varphi_{\alpha}({\rm Re}(a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\dots a_{i_{k}}))\geq\hat{C}(k).

Then we have that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{\alpha}((\frac{s_{n}}{n})^{k})\geq\hat{C}(k).

Proof.

Since
(a_{n})_{n}
be an operator norm bounded sequence
(a_{n})_{n}
, there exists a constant
K>0
such that

||a_{n}||\leq K
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(1) Fix
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. When
k=1
, the assertion is clear by a triangle inequality in Proposition
3.8
. Hence we may assume that
k\geq 2.

Since
n
goes to
\infty
, we may assume that
n\geq k
.
Put

D_{k}(n):=\{i=(i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k})\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}^{k}\ |\ i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k}\text{ are all distinct }\}.

The cardinality
|D_{k}(n)|
of
D_{k}(n)
is
\dfrac{n!}{(n-k)!}
.
Since the cardinality of
D_{k}(n)^{c}
is
|D_{k}(n)^{c}|=n^{k}-|D_{k}(n)|
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|D_{k}(n)|}{n^{k}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n}{n}\frac{n-1}{n}\cdots\frac{n-k+1}{n}=1\text{ and }\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|D_{k}(n)^{c}|}{n^{k}}=0.

We also have two inequalities as follows:

||{\rm Re}(a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\dots a_{i_{k}})||\leq||a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\dots a_{i_{k}}||\leq K^{k},

for any
i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k}
. And by assumption,

\varphi_{\alpha}({\rm Re}(a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\dots a_{i_{k}}))\leq C(k),

for any mutually different
i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k}
.

Since
\sum_{i\in D_{k}(n)}a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\cdots a_{i_{k}}
is self-adjoint, so is

\sum_{i\in D_{k}(n)^{c}}a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\cdots a_{i_{k}}
.
Using a triangle inequality in Proposition
3.8
(5), we have that

\displaystyle\varphi_{\alpha}((\frac{s_{n}}{n})^{k})=\frac{1}{n^{k}}\varphi_{\alpha}((a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n})^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{k}}\varphi_{\alpha}(\sum_{i\in D_{k}(n)}a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\cdots a_{i_{k}}+\sum_{i\in(D_{k}(n))^{c}}a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\cdots a_{i_{k}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{k}}\varphi_{\alpha}(\sum_{i\in D_{k}(n)}a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\cdots a_{i_{k}})+\frac{1}{n^{k}}\varphi_{\alpha}(\sum_{i\in(D_{k}(n))^{c}}a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\cdots a_{i_{k}}).

Examine each part. The first part is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{k}}\varphi_{\alpha}(\sum_{i\in D_{k}(n)}a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\cdots a_{i_{k}})=\frac{1}{n^{k}}\varphi_{\alpha}(\sum_{i\in D_{k}(n)}{\rm Re}(a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\cdots a_{i_{k}}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{k}}(\sum_{i\in D_{k}(n)}\varphi_{\alpha}({\rm Re}(a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\cdots a_{i_{k}})))\leq\frac{1}{n^{k}}(\sum_{i\in D_{k}(n)}C(k))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|D_{k}(n)|}{n^{k}}C(k)\to C(k)\quad(n\to\infty).

The second part is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{k}}\varphi_{\alpha}(\sum_{i\in(D_{k}(n))^{c}}a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\cdots a_{i_{k}})\leq\frac{1}{n^{k}}||\sum_{i\in(D_{k}(n))^{c}}a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\cdots a_{i_{k}}||

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{i\in(D_{k}(n))^{c}}||a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\dots a_{i_{k}}||\leq\frac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{i\in(D_{k}(n))^{c}}K^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|D_{k}(n)^{c}|}{n^{k}}K^{k}\to 0\quad(n\to\infty).

Therefore

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{\alpha}((\frac{s_{n}}{n})^{k})\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|D_{k}(n)|}{n^{k}}C(k)+\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|D_{k}(n)^{c}|}{n^{k}}K^{k}=C(k).

(2)
Use the inequality in Proposition
3.12
instead of a triangle inequality in Proposition
3.8
(5). Then we get the desired assertion in a similar way.

Example 4.7
.

(uniform norm law of large numbers)
Let
A=\otimes_{n\in\mathbb{N}}M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
be the UHF algebra with the unique trace

\tau=\otimes_{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\rm tr}_{2}
. and
\mathcal{M}
the AFD factor of type
{\rm II}_{1}

with the normalized trace
\tau
.
We study a sequence
(u_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
of self-adjoint unitaries in
A
satisfying

u_{n}=u_{n}^{*}=u_{n}^{-1},u_{i}u_{j}=(-1)^{a(|i-j|)}u_{j}u_{i},

where
a(n)\in\{0,1\}
and
a(0)=0
.

First, we consider the case that
a(i)=1
for any
i\geq 1
, that is,
u_{i}u_{j}=-u_{j}u_{i}
for any
i,j
with
i\not=j
.
We can construct such a sequence concretely in the following way:
For
x=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},y=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
,
it follows
x=x^{*}=x^{-1}
,
y=y^{*}=y^{-1}\in M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
xy=-yx
.
Define a sequence
(u_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
in
A
as follows:

\displaystyle u_{0}

\displaystyle=x\otimes\otimes_{2}^{\infty}I_{2}

\displaystyle u_{1}

\displaystyle=y\otimes x\otimes\otimes_{3}^{\infty}I_{2}

\displaystyle u_{2}

\displaystyle=y\otimes y\otimes x\otimes_{4}^{\infty}I_{2}

\displaystyle u_{3}

\displaystyle=y\otimes y\otimes y\otimes x\otimes_{5}^{\infty}I_{2}

\displaystyle\cdots\cdots

\displaystyle u_{n}

\displaystyle=\overbrace{y\otimes y\otimes\cdots\otimes y}^{n}\otimes x\otimes_{n+2}^{\infty}I_{2}

\displaystyle\cdots\cdots.

Then
u_{n}=u_{n}^{*}=u_{n}^{-1}
and
u_{i}u_{j}=-u_{j}u_{i}
for any
i,j
with
i\neq j
.

And for all
n
,
\tau(u_{n})=0=\varphi_{\alpha}(u_{n})
for any non-linear trace
\varphi_{\alpha}

These relations imply the following: Put

. Then
s_{n}^{*}=s_{n}
,

s_{n}^{*}s_{n}=s_{n}^{2}=(u_{0}+u_{1}+\cdots+u_{n-1})^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}u_{i}^{2}+\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq n-1}(u_{i}u_{j}+u_{j}u_{i})=nI,

.

is a self-adjoint unitary and we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|(\frac{s_{n}}{n})^{k}\|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{k/2}}=0.

This shows that a certain strong non-commutativity implies what we call
the uniform norm law of large numbers
.

\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{\alpha}((\frac{s_{n}}{n})^{k})=0

by the operator norm continuity of the non-linear trace

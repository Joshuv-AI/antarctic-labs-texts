Abstract
We present a measurement of the branching fraction of B + → τ + ​ ν τ B^{+}\rightarrow\tau^{+}\nu_{\tau} decays using 387 ± ± 6 × 10 6 387\pm\pm 6\text{\times}{10}^{6}\text{\,} Υ ⁡ ( 4 ​ S ) \mathchar 28935\relax{(4S)} collected between 2019 and 2022 with the Belle II detector at SuperKEKB e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} collider. We reconstruct the accompanying B − B^{-} meson using the hadronic tagging method, while B + → τ + ​ ν τ B^{+}\rightarrow\tau^{+}\nu_{\tau} candidates are identified in the recoil. We find evidence for B + → τ + ​ ν τ B^{+}\rightarrow\tau^{+}\nu_{\tau} decays at 3.0 standard deviations, including systematic uncertainties. The measured branching fraction is ℬ ( B + → τ + ν τ ) = [ 1.24 ± 0.41 ( stat . ) ± 0.19 ( syst . ) ] × 10 − 4 \mathcal{B}(B^{+}\rightarrow\tau^{+}\nu_{\tau})=[1.24\pm 0.41\mathrm{(stat.)}\pm 0.19\mathrm{(syst.)}]\times 10^{-4} .

The measurement described in this paper is based on data collected by the
Belle II
experiment at the SuperKEKB electron-positron collider between 2019 and 2022 and has an integrated luminosity of
365.4\pm\pm 1.7\text{\,}\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}

\mathchar 28935\relax{(4S)}

In addition, we use
42.3\penalty\ \mbox{\,fb}^{-1}

e^{+}e^{-}

\rightarrow

e^{+}e^{-}

\rightarrow

\tau^{+}\tau^{-}

A

B_{\mathrm{tag}}
) and the remaining charged particle trajectories (tracks) and neutral energy deposit in the calorimeter (clusters) are examined for the signature of a
B^{+}\rightarrow\tau^{+}\nu_{\tau}
decay (
B_{\text{sig}}
). We consider four
\tau^{+}

\tau^{+}

\rightarrow

e^{+}

\nu_{e}

\overline{\nu}_{\tau}
,
\tau^{+}

\rightarrow

\mu^{+}

\nu_{\mu}

\overline{\nu}_{\tau}
,
\tau^{+}

\rightarrow

\pi^{+}

\overline{\nu}_{\tau}
, and
\tau^{+}

\rightarrow

\rho(770)

\tau

[
2

We define a set of selection requirements to suppress the backgrounds for which either the
B_{\mathrm{tag}}
or the
B_{\text{sig}}
are misidentified. We optimize the signal selection on simulation, which is corrected and validated on several control samples. We extract the branching fraction using a simultaneous two-dimensional maximum likelihood fit to two discriminating variables, the residual energy in the electromagnetic calorimeter not associated with the reconstructed
B^{+}B^{-}
pair, and the missing mass squared of the event.

II
Belle II
detector and simulation

The Belle II experiment
[
16
]
is located at the SuperKEKB accelerator
[
17
]
, which collides
7\text{\,}\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
electrons and
4\text{\,}\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
positrons at and near the
\mathchar 28935\relax{(4S)}
resonance. The
Belle II
detector
[
16
]
has a cylindrical geometry arranged around the interaction point (IP), which is enclosed by a beryllium beam pipe with an inner radius of 1 cm, and includes a two-layer silicon-pixel detector (PXD) surrounded by a four-layer double-sided silicon-strip detector (SVD)
[
18
]
and a 56-layer central drift chamber (CDC). These detectors reconstruct tracks of charged particles. In this work, we analyze the data for the period, when only one-sixth of the second layer of the PXD was installed. Surrounding the CDC, which also provides ionization-energy-loss measurements, is a time-of-propagation counter (TOP)
[
19
]
in the central region and an aerogel-based ring-imaging Cherenkov counter (ARICH) in the forward endcap region. These detectors provide charged-particle identification (PID). Surrounding the TOP and ARICH is an electromagnetic calorimeter (ECL) based on CsI(Tl) crystals that primarily provide energy and timing measurements for photons and electrons. Outside of the ECL is a superconducting solenoid magnet, which provides an axial magnetic field of
1.5\,\text{T}
.
A
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle L}
and muon identification system is located outside of the magnet and consists of flux-return iron plates interspersed with resistive plate chambers and plastic scintillators.
The central axis of the solenoid defines the
z
axis of the laboratory frame, pointing approximately in the direction of the electron beam.

The analysis strategy is tested and optimized on Monte Carlo (MC) simulated event samples before being applied to the data.
Quark-antiquark pairs (
q\overline{q}
) from
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions are generated using
KKMC

[
20
]
with
Pythia8

[
21
]
, while hadron decays are simulated with
EvtGen

[
22
]
. The detector response is simulated using
Geant4

[
23
]
. For all simulated events, electromagnetic final-state radiation is taken into account using the
PHOTOS
package
[
24
,
25
]
.
For the simulated signal, we produce
4\text{\times}{10}^{7}\text{\,}
events, in which one
B
meson decays exclusively to the
B^{+}\rightarrow\tau^{+}\nu_{\tau}
final state, and the other
B
meson decays generically.
For the
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
,
q\overline{q}
, and
\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
backgrounds we use simulated samples with equivalent integrated luminosities of
2.8\text{\,}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}^{-1}
,
1\text{\,}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}^{-1}
and
0.6\text{\,}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}^{-1}
, respectively. Both experimental and simulated data are processed using the
Belle II
analysis software framework
[
26
]
.

III
Reconstruction and Event Selection

To consider an event for further analysis we require the reconstruction of three or more tracks, each with an impact parameter with respect to the IP, less than
2\text{\,}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{m}
along the
z
axis, and
0.5\text{\,}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{m}
in the transverse direction; three or more ECL clusters with
E>$100\text{\,}\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}$
and within the CDC acceptance; a minimum transverse momentum of
$100\text{\,}\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}$/c
for each of the charged particles; the total energy of reconstructed tracks and ECL clusters greater than
4\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}
.

Simulation samples and data are then passed through the Full Event Interpretation (FEI)
[
27
]
, a hierarchical multivariate algorithm that fully reconstructs the
B_{\mathrm{tag}}
in thousands of possible decay chains. The output of the FEI algorithm for each event is a set of reconstructed
B_{\text{tag}}
candidates with an associated score (
\mathcal{O}_{\rm FEI}
); the higher the
\mathcal{O}_{\rm FEI}
, the higher the expected purity of the candidate. We require
\mathcal{O}_{\rm FEI}

>0.001
and the resulting fraction of
\mathchar 28935\relax{(4S)}
events with a correctly reconstructed charged
B_{\text{tag}}
candidate is estimated from simulations to be approximately 0.30% with a purity of 29%
[
28
]
.
The average
B_{\mathrm{tag}}
candidate multiplicity in signal events is 1.02. We retain one
B_{\mathrm{tag}}
candidate per event, choosing the candidate with the highest value of
\mathcal{O}_{\rm FEI}
.

We use two kinematic variables to discriminate between events with a correctly reconstructed
B_{\text{tag}}
candidate and misreconstructed events: the beam-energy-constrained mass
M_{\text{bc}}=\sqrt{(E^{*}_{\text{beam}})^{2}/c^{4}-|\vec{p}^{\ *}_{\text{tag}}|^{2}/c^{2}}
and the energy difference
\Delta E=E^{*}_{\text{tag}}-E_{\text{beam}}^{*}
, where
\vec{p}^{\ *}_{\text{tag}}
(
E^{*}_{\text{tag}}
) is the reconstructed momentum (energy) of the
B_{\text{tag}}
and
E_{\text{beam}}^{*}
is the beam energy, all evaluated in the center of mass (c.m.) frame. We require
M_{\text{bc}}>$5.27\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$/c^{2}
and
-0.15<\Delta E<$0.1\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$
.

The
B_{\text{sig}}
is reconstructed from all the remaining tracks and neutral objects (ECL clusters with no tracks associated) not used to reconstruct the
B_{\text{tag}}
.
We consider four different signal categories identifying the following final states:
\tau^{+}

\rightarrow

e^{+}

\nu_{e}

\overline{\nu}_{\tau}
,
\tau^{+}

\rightarrow

\mu^{+}

\nu_{\mu}

\overline{\nu}_{\tau}
,
\tau^{+}

\rightarrow

\pi^{+}

\overline{\nu}_{\tau}
, and
\tau^{+}

\rightarrow

\rho^{+}

\overline{\nu}_{\tau}
.
For this purpose, in addition to the tracks associated with the
B_{\text{tag}}
, we require only another track in the event, with a charge opposite to the
B_{\text{tag}}
charge and a momentum
p>$0.5\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$/c
.
The PID criteria to distinguish between electron, muon, and pion hypotheses are based on multivariate classifiers that utilize information from all sub-detectors.
This information is combined into a boosted decision tree for electrons or a likelihood function for muons and pions. The likelihood for pion or muon hypothesis combines PID information from all sub-detectors except SVD and PXD.
From simulation, we estimate that PID selection criteria discriminate electrons, muons, and pions with efficiencies of 99%, 82%, and 97% at misidentification rates of 1%, 5%, and 3%, respectively.
A bremsstrahlung correction in the
\tau^{+}

\rightarrow

e^{+}

\nu_{e}

\overline{\nu}_{\tau}
decay mode is applied: the four-momentum of the electron candidates is corrected by adding the four-momenta of photons with an energy below 1.0 GeV within a cone of 0.05 rad around the electron-momentum vector.
From pairs of ECL clusters not used to reconstruct
B_{\text{tag}}
, we reconstruct
\pi^{0}\rightarrow\gamma\gamma
candidates. We require these candidates to have an invariant mass in the range
120<m_{\gamma\gamma}<145\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}/c^{2}
, which corresponds to approximately
\pm 1\penalty\ \sigma
of the mass resolution. The
\pi^{0}
candidates are combined with a
\pi^{+}
candidate to form a
\rho^{+}
candidate.
If the
\rho^{+}
candidate has a reconstructed invariant mass within
625<m_{\pi^{+}\pi^{0}}<925\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}/c^{2}
, the
B_{\text{sig}}
is assigned to the
\tau^{+}

\rightarrow

\rho^{+}

\overline{\nu}_{\tau}
category. Otherwise, the event is assigned to the
\tau^{+}

\rightarrow

\pi^{+}

\overline{\nu}_{\tau}
category. If multiple
\rho^{+}
candidates are reconstructed, we choose the one closest to the
\rho^{+}
mass
[
2
]

B_{\text{tag}}
nor with the

\rightarrow

D^{*-}

\ell^{+}

\nu_{\ell}

[

The two most discriminating observables based on the ROE are the total residual energy from neutral clusters in the ECL (

\rightarrow

The first variable,

This variable discriminates between signal and background because

The second variable is the ratio of Fox-Wolfram polynomials
R_{2}

[
32

IV.1
Efficiency correction

We determine the hadronic FEI reconstruction efficiency from simulation and then we correct it with a data-driven procedure using two data control samples.

D^{(*)}

\pi^{+}

B_{\text{tag}}
reconstruction efficiency, extracted from the hadronic sample are consistent with the first ones, and, therefore, we use a combination of the correction factors from the two samples. The correction factors, obtained by comparing the yields in data and simulation, depend on the
B_{\text{tag}}

Additional corrections are applied to account for mis-modeling in simulation of PID efficiencies and mis-identification probabilities.

We evaluate the systematic uncertainty related to simulation statistics by fluctuating the bin contents of the 2D histogram PDFs 200 times, varying the bin content according to MC statistical uncertainties, and assuming a Poisson distribution. We obtain an uncertainty of 13.3%.

To evaluate the systematic corrections to the
n_{\gamma\mathrm{extra}}

The resulting PDFs are used to repeat the fit. The standard deviation of the fit results is 5.5%, which is taken as a systematic uncertainty.

The limited size of the off-resonance sample affects the reweighting of the continuum MC. Applying a bootstrapping procedure, and resampling the training and test samples of the FBDT, we obtain 50 different sets of reweighting factors.
Repeating the fit with this change we observe a standard deviation of 1.9% in the fit results, which is taken as a systematic uncertainty.

Calibration of
n_{\gamma\mathrm{extra}}

The main source of the discrepancy in the
E_{\text{ECL}}^{\text{extra}}
distribution between data and simulation is related to incorrect modeling of
n_{\gamma\mathrm{extra}}
. Figure
8
shows the
E_{\text{ECL}}^{\text{extra}}
shape for
n_{\gamma\mathrm{extra}}=1,2,...9

B^{+}\rightarrow\tau^{+}\nu_{\tau}
signal affects only
n_{\gamma\mathrm{extra}}

\leqslant 2
.
The simulation agrees well with the data for this variable. Therefore, we correct the normalization through bin-by-bin corrections of the
n_{\gamma\mathrm{extra}}
distribution using different control samples.

A.1
Extra-Tracks control sample

We select a control sample of
B^{+}\rightarrow\tau^{+}\nu_{\tau}
events that have more than 2 tracks in the ROE to evaluate the corrections to the
E_{\text{ECL}}^{\text{extra}}
distribution for the
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
background simulation. The control sample has the same
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
background composition but no signal events.
The control sample is defined using 2 different requirements on the charged tracks:
IP tracks
and
Signal-like Tracks
. The
IP tracks
include all the tracks in the ROE with an impact parameter with respect to the IP being less than 2
\rm\,cm
along the
z
axis, and 0.5
\rm\,cm
in the transverse plane, without any momentum requirement. The
Signal-like Tracks
criterion has the same definition but also requires a momentum greater than 0.5
\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
. We then require
N_{\texttt{IP Tracks}}>1
and
N_{\texttt{Signal-like Tracks}}=0
.
The average number of
N_{\texttt{IP Tracks}}
in each event for this control sample is
3.2
.
Figure
9
shows the
n_{\gamma\mathrm{extra}}
distribution for data and MC in this control sample for each signal channel, from which we extract correction factors to reweight
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
MC background.

FIG. 9:
Distributions of
n_{\gamma\mathrm{extra}}
in the Extra-Tracks control sample for the four signal channels. The bottom panel of each distribution shows the Data/MC ratio values and their uncertainties to be used as corrections for MC background for the analysis. The continuum component is subtracted since the correction factors are used only to correct the
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
background component.

A.3
Double Tag control sample

We use the Double Tag control sample to evaluate the corrections for the hadronic
\tau^{+}
decays in the signal simulation. We reconstruct events with two non-overlapping
B
candidates with opposite charges with the hadronic FEI algorithm. We require no extra tracks in the ROE. The selection requirements for the hadronic
B_{\text{tag}}
are the same as the main analysis. The signal side is a second
B
reconstructed by the hadronic FEI.
In the Double Tag control sample,
E_{\text{ECL}}^{\text{extra}}
is expected to peak at
0\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}
as for
B^{+}\rightarrow\tau^{+}\nu_{\tau}
.
We apply the same continuum-reweighting and normalization, PID corrections, ROE clusters clean-up, and corrections as for the main analysis.
Figure
10b
shows the data and MC agreement in this control sample, from which we extract correction factors to reweight signal MC for hadronic
\tau^{+}
decay categories.

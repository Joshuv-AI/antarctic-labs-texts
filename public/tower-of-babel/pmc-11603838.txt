Association of sarcopenia and its prognostic value in symptomatic knee osteoarthritis among older people in China: the first longitudinal evidence from CHARLS
Authors: Jiaxiang Gao, Mulatibieke Yesihati, Huang Cheng, Tong Li, Ran Ding, Weiguo Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSarcopenia and knee osteoarthritis (KOA) are two common musculoskeletal disorders, often coexisting in aged population and potentially influencing each other. However, the underlying relationship between two conditions remains unclear and controversial. To fill this knowledge gap, we aimed to investigate the longitudinal association among the older Chinese population.MethodsData were attracted from 2 waves of the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS), and 6212 individuals aged ≥ 60 years were included. Sarcopenia status was defined by the Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia 2019 criteria. Multivariate logistic regression and generalized estimation equation models were applied to estimate the impact of sarcopenia on KOA. A prognostic nomogram was developed through train-test cross-validation.ResultsAt baseline in CHARLS 2015, the prevalence of symptomatic KOA was 12.7% (792/6212) in total population, 9% (270/2996) in no-sarcopenia group, 17.5% (286/1638) in possible sarcopenia group, and 15.0% (236/1578) in sarcopenia group. Over a 3-year follow-up, 4980 respondents were included, with incident KOA cases identified as 56 (2.2%), 38 (3.0%), and 43 (3.6%) in no-sarcopenia, possible sarcopenia, and sarcopenia groups, respectively. Sarcopenia was significantly associated with increased new-onset KOA compared to no-sarcopenia peers in the fully adjusted model (OR: 1.91, 95% CI: 1.15–3.18), whereas this association was non-significant for possible sarcopenia. In females, low muscle mass alone significantly increased the incident risk of KOA (OR: 2.58, 95% CI: 1.05–6.49). The final prognostic model and nomogram, including sarcopenia status, age, gender, body mass index, self-reported health status, comorbidities, history of falls and physical activity, had a considerable discrimination (AUC = 0.744, C-index = 0.660). The calibration curve indicated significant agreement between predicted and actual observations. Decision curve analysis revealed net benefits for clinical intervention at a probability threshold of 1–17%.ConclusionsSarcopenia was associated with a higher incident risk of symptomatic KOA, wherein low muscle mass may play an important role. The inferior prognosis of sarcopenia in KOA needs more attention in clinical practice.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12877-024-05556-3.Keywords: Sarcopenia, Possible sarcopenia, Knee osteoarthritis, Prediction model, Nomogram

Introduction

Sarcopenia, originally described by Rosenberg in 1989 [1], is a serious geriatric syndrome defined as age-related loss of skeletal muscle mass plus loss of muscle strength and/or reduced physical performance [2]. Substantial evidence underscores the linkage between sarcopenia and various adverse outcomes in older population, including falls and fractures [3], physical independence [4], frailty [5], and increased mortality [6]. According to data from the Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia (AWGS) [7], the prevalence of sarcopenia ranges from 5.5% to 25.7% which has become a rapidly growing public concern. Notably, anthropometric and cultural lifestyle-related differences should be taken into account in Asian population compared to Western contemporaries [8].

Osteoarthritis (OA) is also an age-related musculoskeletal disorder characterized by the destruction and loss of articular cartilage, affecting up to 300 million individuals worldwide [9, 10]. As the most common type, knee osteoarthritis (KOA) has becoming one of the leading causes of disabilities and substantially reducing the quality of life [11]. Mounting research suggests that bone, cartilage and muscle are in close relationship, with their functionality and biomechanics concomitantly affected by aging [12, 13]. The reduction of skeletal muscles can lead to low-grade and systemic inflammation during the development of sarcopenia. Elevated levels of inflammatory biomarkers, including cytokines and soluble receptors, are considered as important promotive factors of KOA [14]. Additionally, sarcopenia and KOA share several risk factors including aging, obesity, and physical activity [15]. Currently, some of the epidemiological studies report the coexistence of these two conditions. For instance, Lijima et al. found a coexisting prevalence of 8.6% in people aged 60–90 years [16].

However, data demonstrating this relationship remain ambiguous and, at times, conflicting. A large longitudinal cohort study including 1653 subjects indicated sarcopenia was not associated with the risk of KOA [17]. Meanwhile, the definition of sarcopenia in previous researches varies [9], and there is insufficient evidence supporting a direct influence of sarcopenia on the development of KOA, particularly among large samples from the Chinese population. Given these considerations, we conducted a retrospective cohort study using the nationally representative data from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) to investigate the association between sarcopenia status and KOA. Furthermore, a prediction model and nomogram for incident symptomatic KOA were developed to explore the prognostic value of sarcopenia status.

Methods

Study population and ethics

Participants were retrieved from the CHARLS, an ongoing nationally representative population-based longitudinal cohort study, which collects high-quality data on health, social and financial wellbeing of citizens in China through a face-to-face computer-assisted personal interview. Further detailed descriptions were previously reported (http://charls.pku.edu.cn/en). For the current study, we retrospectively analyzed data from CHARLS 2015 and 2018. The inclusion criteria were: (1) participants aged ≥ 60 years; (2) having data regarding sarcopenia in CHARLS 2015; (3) and having data regarding symptomatic KOA in both CHARLS 2015 and 2018. Exclusion criteria included: (1) missing data of age, gender and residential area in CHARLS 2015; (2) lack of information about smoke and drink behavior, comorbidities, self-reported health status, fall and physical activity in CHARLS 2015. The CHARLS was approved by the Ethical Review Committee of Peking University (IRB 00001052–11015), and all participants signed informed consent at the time of participation. No separate ethical approval was required for our study.

Assessment of Sarcopenia status

Sarcopenia status was assessed according to the AWGS 2019 algorithm [7], which is composed of three components: muscle strength, appendicular skeletal muscle mass (ASM), and physical performance. Possible sarcopenia is defined as the presence of low muscle strength with or without reduced physical performance, while sarcopenia is diagnosed when low muscle mass plus low muscle strength or low physical performance are detected together.

Handgrip strength (HGS) was measured twice in both non- and dominant hand, with the participant squeezing a dynamometer (Yuejian TM WL-1000, Nantong, China) as hard as possible, and the average of four measurements for both hands was used for analysis. The cutoffs for low HGS were < 28 and 18 kg, respectively for men and women. The ASM was estimated by a validated anthropometric equation for Chinese individuals, which had strong agreement with actual measured values of dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) [18, 19]. The cutoffs for low height-adjusted muscle mass were < 7.0 kg/m2 in men and < 5.4 kg/m2 in women. Regarding physical performance, the gait speed (GS) and the chair stand test were performed using the method described by Wu et al. [20]. The criteria of low physical performance included GS < 1 m/s and 5-time chair stand time ≥ 12 s.

Outcome of symptomatic KOA

In accordance with the definition utilized in several previous studies [21–23], symptomatic KOA was defined as both physician-diagnosed arthritis and the presence of concurrent knee pain. If a participant responded with “yes” to the first two of the following questions, and responded “the knees” to the third question, the diagnosis of symptomatic KOA could be ascertained: (1) Have you been diagnosed with arthritis or rheumatism by a doctor? (2) Are you often troubled with any body pain? If the participant responded “yes” to the second question, they were presented with question (3): On what part of your body do you feel pain? (list all body pains). New case of symptomatic KOA was defined as the participant who was free of symptomatic KOA in CHARLS 2015 and then diagnosed in CHARLS 2018.

Covariates

Demographic characteristics included age (60–65 years, 65–70 years, 70–75 years, ≥ 75 years), gender and residential area (urban or rural). Health-related factors included smoke (current smoking, no smoking or ex-smoking) and drink behavior (yes or no), body mass index BMI by World Health Organization criteria (underweight, normal and overweight: <18.5 kg/m2, ≥ 18.5 to < 25 kg/m2, and ≥ 25 kg/m2; obesity group with BMI ≥ 30 kg/m2 was combined with overweight group because of limited number) [21], the number of comorbidities (including hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes, cancer, chronic lung disease, liver disease, heart disease, stroke, kidney disease, digestive disease, psychiatric problem, memory-related disease, and asthma), self-reported health status (very good, good, fair, poor, or very poor). Number of falls within two years was calculated for history of fall. Regarding physical activity, weekly exercise durations for high-, middle- and low-level activity were calculated respectively.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as means ± standard deviation (for normally distributed data) or medians ± interquartile range (for non-normally distributed data) for continuous variables, and percentages for categorical variables. Firstly, baseline characteristics in the cross-sectional and longitudinal analytical samples were summarized based on sarcopenia status and compared between participants using Tukey’s test (for normally distributed data with equal variances), Welch’s t test (for normally distributed data with unequal variances), the Kruskal-Walls test (for non-normally distributed data), or chi-squared test (for categorical variables), as appropriate.

Secondly, the casual relationship between sarcopenia status and symptomatic KOA was investigated in the longitudinal analysis. On the one hand, logistic regression models were developed to estimate the impact of baseline sarcopenia status on the incident symptomatic KOA. On the other hand, generalized estimation equation (GEE) models, which account for the correlation among repeated measures within the same participant, were used to examine the longitudinal association between sarcopenia status and the progression of symptomatic KOA. Four models were established in the longitudinal analysis, with parameter estimates expressed as odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs): Model 1: adjusted for demographic information; model 2: model 1 + health-related factors; model 3: model 2 + history of fall; Model 4: model 3 + physical activity. For logistic regression models, stratified analyses were conducted to further confirm the impact of sarcopenia on the incident symptomatic KOA and detect vulnerable populations by several variables of age (> 65, ≤ 65), gender (male or female), BMI (> 25, ≤ 25), comorbidities (yes or no), fall (yes or no), respectively. Inter-group differences were evaluated by two-sample z-tests. In addition, we also evaluated the association between low muscle mass alone and symptomatic KOA stratified by gender, considering differences in cut-off points for low muscle mass between male and female.

Finally, to further verify the prognostic value of sarcopenia status in symptomatic KOA, a prediction model and nomogram were established. For the train-test sample cross-validation, the data set was randomly divided into the development set and validation set at a ratio of 7:3, respectively for model development and predictive performance validation. Univariable logistic analysis for symptomatic KOA was applied to each sarcopenia status and aforementioned covariates separately. Prognostic variables for multivariate logistic regression model were according to both statistical significance (P < 0.1) [24] and clinical value. A nomogram was eventually presented to predict the incidence of symptomatic KOA based on the final model. The predictive accuracy of the final model/nomogram was evaluated by discrimination and calibration. The discrimination ability was evaluated based on the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and area under the ROC curve (AUC), as well as Harrell’s C-index. The calibration curve was used to compare the association between the actual outcomes and predicted probabilities. The clinical usefulness and benefits of the predictive model were estimated by decision curve analyses (DCA). All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 3.6.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing). A two-sided P value < 0.05 was deemed statistically significant in all cases.

After data screening, 6212 respondents met research requirements at the baseline for cross-sectional analysis (Fig. 1). Longitudinal association between Sarcopenia status and incident symptomatic KOA during the 3-year follow-up

A total of 4980 participants met the inclusion and exclusion criteria and were selected into the longitudinal analysis (Fig. 1). Table S1 presented the demographic characteristics stratified by sarcopenia status. During a 3-year follow-up, incident rates of symptomatic KOA among total population, no-sarcopenia, possible sarcopenia and sarcopenia group were 2.8% (137/4980), 2.2% (56/2537), 3.0% (38/1249) and 3.6% (43/1194). As depicted in Table 2, the longitudinal association between sarcopenia and incident KOA was significant in original unadjusted model (P = 0.014), which remained robust after further adjusting for demographic information in model 1 (P = 0.015), health-related factors in model 2 (P = 0.013), and history of fall in model 3 (P = 0.013), as well as physical activity in model 4 (P = 0.012). Individuals with sarcopenia had a 91% increased risk of incident symptomatic KOA compared with those without sarcopenia (OR: 1.91, 95% CI: 1.15–3.18). However, no significant relationship was found between possible sarcopenia and incident symptomatic KOA after adjusting for covariates (OR: 1.32, 95% CI: 0.85–2.01). Results of stratified analyses were generally similar to that of main logistic regression analyses (Fig. 2). Compared to non-sarcopenia, sarcopenia showed an significant impact on incident symptomatic KOA in those aged over 65 years (OR = 1.93; 95% CI: 1.04–3.6), male (OR = 2.1; 95% CI: 1.01–4.39), BMI ≤ 25 kg/m2 (OR = 1.85; 95% CI: 1.13–3.05), combined with other comorbidities (OR = 2.3; 95% CI: 1.23–4.31) and without fall history (OR = 1.82; 95% CI: 1.08–3.07). Nevertheless, the inter-group differences were not significant for all variables (all P > 0.05).

Longitudinal association between low muscle mass alone and incident KOA over the 3-year follow-up

In order to estimate the longitudinal association of low muscle mass alone with incident symptomatic KOA, we further excluded 759 subjects with low hand grip strength alone, 660 subjects with low performance alone, and 496 subjects with both low hand grip strength and performance in 2015. Therefore, our final samples included 3065 subjects, among which 690 (22.5%) had low muscle mass alone. As shown in Table 3, unadjusted model and model 1 showed a significant negative association between low muscle mass alone and the onset of symptomatic KOA in male group. However, after further adjusting more variables in model 2, model 3 and model 4, such relationship in male disappeared. Instead, a similar significant negative impact emerged in the female population.

The development and validation of a prognostic model for predicting incident symptomatic KOA

As mentioned above, 4980 respondents for longitudinal analysis were randomly divided into the development set (70%, n = 3486) and validation set (30%, n = 1494). Table 4 showed the results of the univariable and multivariable logistic regression analysis. In the univariable analysis, sarcopenia status was identified as a potential risk factor for symptomatic KOA, with a significant difference occurring in sarcopenia group (P = 0.021) and an almost significant tendency in possible sarcopenia group (P = 0.061). Furthermore, poorer self-reported health status, history of fall, high- and low-level physical activity might increase the risk of developing KOA (P < 0.05). Female showed a tendency associated with the incidence of KOA, although tantalizingly close to significance (P = 0.079). Considering the clinical value and important effects of age, BMI, comorbidities and middle physical activity on KOA, these parameters were also selected into the multivariable regression model regardless of the P values. Thus, the final prediction model estimated by multivariable logistic analysis included 8 variables: age, gender, BMI, comorbidities, self-reported health status, fall, physical activity and sarcopenia status.

As shown in Fig. 3, a nomogram was also established based on the aforementioned multivariable model. There were 13 lines in this chart, of which lines 2–11 were the variables of the model. Self-reported health status was the largest contributor in the nomogram, followed by sarcopenia status and high-level physical activity. The value corresponding to each variable was brought into the calculation and the final score was added to the “total points” line after accumulation, along with the corresponding predicted onset risk of symptomatic KOA at the bottom. For example, a male participant (0 points) aged 65–70 years (10 points), with a BMI over 25 kg/m2 (17 points), whose self-reported health status was very poor (100 points), diagnosed as possible sarcopenia (24 points) without other comorbidities (0 points), fall within 2 years (24 points), whose high-, middle- and low-level physical activity durations were 28, 0, and 28 h respectively (57, 42 and 35 points respectively), the total points were 309 and the model predicted that the onset risk of incident symptomatic KOA was 0.688.

Nomogram for predicting incident symptomatic KOA based on the prognostic value of sarcopenia status

As for model performance, ROC and calibration curves were presented as Fig. 4a and b, respectively. The bias-corrected AUC after bootstrap validation was 0.744 and the C-index was 0.660, suggesting a proper discrimination. The calibration ability was also acceptable: the apparent observed line was close to the ideal line, even though the bias-corrected line was slightly further from the ideal line after the bootstrap procedure. Moreover, the DCA was presented in Fig. 4c, which showed that if the threshold probability of a patient was set between 1% and 17%, then the utilization of the prognostic model was more beneficial compared to the extreme situation of intervention for all patients or none.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first large-scale, Asian population-based longitudinal study reporting a positive association between sarcopenia and an increasing incident risk of symptomatic KOA among Chinses residents aged ≥ 60 years, utilizing the AWGS 2019 criteria for sarcopenia diagnosis. Furthermore, we harnessed such impact of sarcopenia on KOA prognosis through developing the first predictive model and nomogram, offering a potentially valuable tool for early screening and preventing incident KOA in the Chinese older demographic.

Previous studies have postulated the co-existence of sarcopenia and OA in older adults. In a cross-sectional study by Kemmler et al., involving 1325 female individuals over 70 years, a higher prevalence of sarcopenia was observed in patients with lower extremity OA compared to those without OA (9.1% vs. 3.5%) [25]. Similarly, a meta-analysis systematically reviewing four cross-sectional studies with 7454 participants, reported a sarcopenia prevalence of 45.2% in KOA patients, as opposed to 31.2% in controls [9]. Our results of cross-sectional analysis in CHARLS 2015 are largely consistent with these findings, albeit with lower prevalence rates of sarcopenia (29.8% in KOA group vs. 24.8% in non-KOA group). Furthermore, our results demonstrated a close association between sarcopenia and symptomatic KOA after fully adjusting for covariates. On the one hand, longitudinal logistic regression analysis indicated that sarcopenia individuals had a 91% higher risk of new-onset symptomatic KOA compared to those without sarcopenia. Similar positive associations were also observed in the stratified analysis by age, gender, BMI, comorbidities and fall history, while no difference. On the other hand, considering the progression of symptomatic KOA for the same person from different follow-ups, results of GEE models indicated that both possible sarcopenia and sarcopenia were associated with the progression of symptomatic KOA.

Although similar hypotheses have been posited in prior studies, discrepancies in the definition and diagnosis of sarcopenia suggest the necessity for a comprehensive assessment of muscle strength and/or physical performance. For instance, a large-scale cross-sectional study using data from National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey found that sarcopenia was associated with OA when it was defined by the BMI-adjusted skeletal muscle mass (OR = 1.23 [95% CI, 1.01, 1.51]; P = 0.038) and defined by the weight-adjusted skeletal muscle mass (OR = 1.30 [95% CI, 1.10, 1.55]; P = 0.003) [15]. While Misra et al., who defined sarcopenia merely as the lowest quintile of residuals of appendicular skeletal muscle mass, reported an opposing result, associating increased muscle mass with higher risk of KOA in men [17]. Additionally, we selected new onset cases of symptomatic KOA as the primary outcome, rather than prevalent cases in previous cross-sectional studies mentioned above. This approach offered an advantage of indicating a potential casual effect of sarcopenia on KOA, which was aligned with several recent Mendelian randomization studies [26–29]. However, evidence supporting reverse causality remains limited [26, 27].

Given the dearth of effective treatments for end-stage KOA beyond knee arthroplasty, our findings underscore the clinical value of early sarcopenia assessment in community-wide health screening. In addition, a new predictive model and nomogram including sarcopenia and the other 7 variables was established, which showed a strong screening ability to detect individuals at high potential risk of developing symptomatic KOA, with an AUC close to 0.75. The calibration and DCA curves also indicated a good clinical application value. Self-reported health status, sarcopenia status and high-level physical activity were the top three contributors. As a subjective variable, self-rated health reflects patient’s knowledge and ability to manage diseases [23]. Therefore, individuals with poor self-rated health should receive additional attention, alongside early education and intervention regarding KOA. (Possible) sarcopenia is characterized by the decline of muscle strength, which can lead to joint instability and cartilage degeneration, potentially progressing to KOA [15]. Physical activity is another conventional risk factor: evidenced from Framingham Heart Study indicate that performing high-level physical activity over 2 h/day could increase the risk of both symptomatic and radiographic KOA, while the effects of middle and light physical activity were insignificant [30]. Thus, improving muscle strength and addressing sarcopenia through non-vigorous physical activity may serve as early preventive measures for KOA.

It’s worth noting that possible sarcopenia, introduced by the AWGS 2019 algorithm for the first time, exhibited no significant correlation with the incident risk of KOA. Consequently, we may indirectly infer that muscle mass plays a pivotal role in the relationship between sarcopenia and KOA. Admittedly, such hypothesis has been further corroborated by our results: low muscle mass alone was closely associated with the onset of symptomatic KOA, and this association became more pronounced in female after fully adjusting for variables (OR:2.58; 95% CI: 1.05–6.49). A recent study in rat found that quadriceps muscle atrophy could trigger the subchondral bone abnormal change and cartilage degeneration via joint instability [31]. Furthermore, accumulating evidence designates muscle as a paracrine and endocrine organ secreting a variety of myokines (e.g. irisin, insulin-like growth factor-1, myostatin, and interleukin 6) that modulate bone metabolism. Dysregulation of these myokines in the context of muscle atrophy likely contributes to OA progression. Simultaneously, decreased lean mass implies increased fat mass [32]. Fat, adipose tissue and adipose cells appear to influence the sarcopenia-osteoarthritis relationship through various cytokines (e.g., tumor necrosis factor alpha, interleukin 1 beta and 6) [13] or adipokines (such as leptin, adiponectin and resistin) [33]. These molecules may contribute to articular cartilage degradation and subchondral bone dysfunction through underlying mechanisms involving low-grade inflammation [12], imbalances between catabolism and anabolism [33], as well as insulin resistance [34, 35].

This study has several strengths. First, our research included a nationwide representative sample of the Chinses older population from the CHARLS, conferring robust statistical power for broad generalizability of our findings. Second, in contrast to many previous cross-sectional studies, our cohort study design facilitates exploration of potential causal relationships between sarcopenia and KOA. Furthermore, the use of the latest major algorithm for sarcopenia status according to the AWGS 2019 can mitigate biases arising from variations in diagnostic criteria and classification of sarcopenia [36]. More importantly, we have constructed the first prognostic model for symptomatic KOA, highlighting the predictive value of sarcopenia with considerable discrimination and calibration ability.

However, some limitations warrant consideration. First, KOA diagnosis in this study relied on self-reported knee pain and physician-assessed arthritis status due to a lack of radiological assessments in CHARLS. Other types of arthritis (e.g. rheumatoid arthritis, gout et al.) may not be thoroughly distinguished from OA, potentially leading to an overestimation of the incidence rate. However, this definition provides the best available diagnosis for symptomatic KOA in CHARLS and has been widely acknowledged by published studies [21–23, 37]. Second, inherent characteristics of observational data cannot avoid introducing confounding factors and biasing the observed relationships. To reduce such bias, we considered as many related factors as possible in our analysis. Despite all this, there are still some self-reported parameters extracted from questionnaires, such as health status, comorbidities and physical activity, which may induce information biases. As for the history of falls, the duration of past 2 years can cause recall bias and the actual fall times may be higher than reported. Therefore, the role of falls in predicting the onset of KOA may have been underestimated in our predictive model. Additionally, hospitalized patients were not included in this study, which may have introduced selection bias and potentially led to an underestimation of the prevalence or incidence of sarcopenia and symptomatic KOA. Last, due to a lack of sarcopenia data in the structured questionnaires of CHARLS 2018, the role of KOA in the new onset of sarcopenia cannot be estimated in the reverse direction, necessitating further studies.

Conclusions

Sarcopenia, diagnosed by the AWGS 2019 algorithm, was associated with a higher incident risk of KOA among the Chinses older population, whereas no significant correlation was found between possible sarcopenia and KOA. Our study provided new longitudinal evidence elucidating a potential causal relationship between sarcopenia and KOA, with muscle mass possibly playing an important role. The prognostic model and nomogram were able to aid in the early identification of individuals at high risk of developing KOA, emphasizing the profound effect of preventing/ improving sarcopenia status.

A bialgebra theory of anti-Leibniz algebras
Bo Hou, Zhanpeng Cui

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of an anti-Leibniz bialgebra which is equivalent to a Manin triple of anti-Leibniz algebras, is equivalent to a matched pair of anti-Leibniz algebras. The study of some special anti-Leibniz bialgebras leads to the introduction of the anti-Leibniz Yang-Baxter equation in an anti-Leibniz algebra. A symmetric (or an invariant) solution of the anti-Leibniz Yang-Baxter equation gives an anti-Leibniz bialgebra. The notion of a relative Rota-Baxter operator of an anti-Leibniz algebra is introduced to construct symmetric solutions of the anti-Leibniz Yang-Baxter equation. Moreover, we introduce the notions of factorizable anti-Leibniz bialgebras and skew-symmetric Rota-Baxter anti-Leibniz algebras, and show that a factorizable anti-Leibniz bialgebra leads to a factorization of the underlying anti-Leibniz algebra. There is a one-to-one correspondence between factorizable anti-Leibniz bialgebras and skew-quadratic Rota-Baxter anti-Leibniz algebras. Finally, we constrict anti-Leibniz bialgebras form Leibniz bialgebras by the tensor product and constrict infinite-dimensional anti-Leibniz bialgebras form finite-dimensional anti-Leibniz bialgebras by the completed tensor product.

Introduction

A bialgebra structure on a given algebraic structure is obtained as a coalgebra
structure together which gives the same algebraic structure on the dual space with
a set of compatibility conditions between the multiplications and comultiplications.
One of the most famous examples of bialgebras is the Lie bialgebra
[
14

In this paper, we give a bialgebra theory for anti-Leibniz algebras.

Leibniz algebra is a non-commutative analogue of Lie algebra, which was first considered
in
[
23
]
by the study of the periodicity in algebraic K-theory.
A mock-Lie algebra (also called Jacobi-Jordan algebra, Lie-Jordan algebra) is a
commutative algebra satisfying the Jacobi identity, which is a special Jordan algebra.
In recent years, mock-Lie algebra has attracted the attention of many scholars

[
7
,

,

,

,

In this paper, we still take a similar approach as the study on Lie bialgebras.
The notion of anti-Leibniz bialgebra is thus introduced as an equivalent structure
of a Manin triple of anti-Leibniz algebras, which is interpreted in terms of
matched pairs of anti-Leibniz algebras. One reason for the usefulness of the Lie
bialgebra is that it has a coboundary theory, which leads to the construction of Lie
bialgebras from solutions of the classical Yang-Baxter equations. An important class
of quasi-triangular Lie bialgebras, factorizable Lie bialgebras are used to connect
classical
r
-matrices with certain factorization problems, and have various
applications in integrable systems
[
25

]
. Recently, the factorizable Lie
bialgebras, factorizable antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras and factorizable
Leibniz bialgebras have been studied in
[

Here we also introduce the notion of quasi-triangular anti-Leibniz
bialgebra and the anti-Leibniz Yang-Baxter equation in an anti-Leibniz algebra.
We give the relative Rota-Baxter operators of anti-Leibniz
algebras and construct a solution of the Yang-Baxter equation in an anti-Leibniz algebra.
Moreover, the notion of factorizable anti-Leibniz bialgebra is introduced,
and a one-to-one correspondence between factorizable anti-Leibniz bialgebras
and skew-quadratic Rota-Baxter anti-Leibniz algebras of nonzero weight is given.
We know very little about specific examples of anti-Leibniz algebras and anti-Leibniz
bilgebras. Note that the tensor product of a Leibniz algebra and an anti-commutative
anti-associative algebra is an anti-Leibniz algebra, we take this result to the level
of the bialgebras, and provide some specific examples of anti-Leibniz bialgebras.
The bialgebra theory of anti-Leibniz algebras is based on finite-dimensional
anti-Leibniz algebras. For the infinite-dimensional anti-Leibniz bialgebras,
we provide a construction method using the completed tensor products.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some basic
definitions of anti-Leibniz algebras and bimodules over anti-Leibniz algebras.
We introduce the notions of matched pairs of anti-Leibniz algebras, Manin triples of

algebras is introduced to provide symmetric solutions of the anti-Leibniz Yang-Baxter
equation in semi-direct product anti-Leibniz algebras and hence gives rise to
anti-Leibniz bialgebras. Moreover, we establish the factorizable theories for
anti-Leibniz bialgebras, and give a one-to-one correspondence between
factorizable anti-Leibniz bialgebras and skew-quadratic Rota-Baxter anti-Leibniz algebras.
At end of this section, we give some examples of anti-Leibniz bialgebras by

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a nondegenerate skew-symmetric invariant bilinear form

\displaystyle\langle\varphi(\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1})(a_{2})),\;a_{3}\rangle=\mathfrak{B}(a_{1}a_{2},\;a_{3})=\mathfrak{B}(a_{2},\;a_{1}a_{3})=\langle\mathbf{l}^{\ast}_{A}(a_{1})(\varphi(a_{2})),\;a_{3}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\phi(\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{1})(a_{2})),\;a_{3}\rangle=\mathfrak{B}(a_{2}a_{1},\;a_{3})=\mathfrak{B}(a_{2},\;a_{1}a_{3}-a_{3}a_{1})=\langle(\mathbf{l}^{\ast}_{A}-\mathbf{r}^{\ast}_{A})(a_{1})(\varphi(a_{2})),\;a_{3}\rangle,

3.
Some special anti-Leibniz bialgebras and anti-Leibniz Yang-Baxter equation

In this section, we consider some special classes of anti-Leibniz bialgebras called
quasi-triangular anti-Leibniz bialgebras, triangular anti-Leibniz bialgebras,

in an anti-Leibniz algebra. The notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators of anti-Leibniz algebras
is introduced to provide antisymmetric solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation in semi-direct

\tilde{r}=e_{1}\otimes f_{1}+e_{2}\otimes f_{2}
.
Then we get a
4

(D(A)=\Bbbk\{e_{1},e_{2},f_{1},f_{2}\},\ast,\Delta_{\tilde{r}})
, where

\displaystyle e_{1}\ast e_{1}=e_{2},\qquad f_{2}\ast f_{2}=f_{1},\qquad e_{1}\ast f_{2}=f_{1},\qquad f_{2}\ast e_{1}=e_{2},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\Delta_{\tilde{r}}(e_{1})=e_{2}\otimes e_{2},\qquad\qquad\Delta_{\tilde{r}}(f_{2})=f_{1}\otimes f_{1}.

3.2.
Relative Rota-Baxter operator and anti-Leibniz Yang-Baxter equation

In this subsection, we are discussing the relationship between (relative) Rota-Baxter
operators on an anti-Leibniz algebra and some special resolutions of the aLYBE,
and show that each relative Rota-Baxter operator induces an anti-Leibniz bialgebra.
First, we recall the definition of relative Rota-Baxter operator.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be an anti-Leibniz algebra and
(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})
be a bimodule over

(A,\cdot)
. A linear map
R:M\rightarrow A
is called a
relative Rota-Baxter
operator of
(A,\cdot)
associated to
(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})
if
R
satisfies

R(m_{1})R(m_{2})=R\Big(\mathfrak{l}(R(m_{1}))(m_{2})+\mathfrak{r}(R(m_{2}))m_{1}\Big),

for any
m_{1},m_{2}\in M
. In particular, if the bimodule
(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})

is the regular bimodule
(A,\mathbf{l}_{A},\mathbf{r}_{A})
, the operator
R
is called
a
Rota-Baxter operator
on
(A,\cdot)
.

Let
V
be a vector space. For all
r\in V\otimes V
, there is a linear map
r^{\sharp}:V^{\ast}\rightarrow V
in the following way:

\langle\xi_{1}\otimes\xi_{2},\;r\rangle=\langle r^{\sharp}(\xi_{1}),\;\xi_{2}\rangle,

for any
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in V^{\ast}
. If
r=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in V\otimes V
,
it is easy to see that
r^{\sharp}(\xi)=\sum_{i}\langle\xi,x_{i}\rangle y_{i}
, for any

\xi\in V^{\ast}
. We say that
r\in V\otimes V
is
nondegenerate

if the map
r^{\sharp}
is a linear isomorphism.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be an anti-Leibniz algebra and
r=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in A\otimes A
.
Then
r
is a solution of the
aLYBE
in
(A,\cdot)
if and only if
for any
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in A^{\ast}
,

\tau(r)^{\sharp}(\xi_{1})\tau(r)^{\sharp}(\xi_{2})=\tau(r)^{\sharp}\Big(\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast}(r^{\sharp}(\xi_{1}))(\xi_{2})+(\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast}-\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast})(\tau(r)^{\sharp}(\xi_{2}))(\xi_{1})\Big).

Proof.

For any
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}\in A^{\ast}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\tau(r)^{\sharp}(\xi_{1})\tau(r)^{\sharp}(\xi_{2}),\;\xi_{3}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle r,\;\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast}(\tau(r)^{\sharp}(\xi_{2}))(\xi_{3})\otimes\xi_{1}\rangle=\sum_{i}\langle x_{i},\;\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast}(\tau(r)^{\sharp}(\xi_{2}))(\xi_{3})\rangle\langle y_{i},\xi_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\langle r,\;\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast}(x_{i})(\xi_{3})\otimes\xi_{2}\rangle\langle y_{i},\xi_{1}\rangle=\sum_{i,j}\langle x_{j},\;\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast}(x_{i})(\xi_{3})\rangle\langle y_{j},\xi_{2}\rangle\langle y_{i},\xi_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\langle y_{i}\otimes y_{j}\otimes x_{i}x_{j},\;\xi_{1}\otimes\xi_{2}\otimes\xi_{3}\rangle.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\langle\tau(r)^{\sharp}\big(\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast}(r^{\sharp}(\xi_{1}))(\xi_{2})\big),\;\xi_{3}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\langle x_{j}\otimes y_{j}y_{i}\otimes x_{i},\;\xi_{1}\otimes\xi_{2}\otimes\xi_{3}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\tau(r)^{\sharp}\big(\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast}(\tau(r)^{\sharp}(\xi_{2}))(\xi_{1})\big),\;\xi_{3}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\langle x_{j}y_{i}\otimes y_{j}\otimes x_{i},\;\xi_{1}\otimes\xi_{2}\otimes\xi_{3}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\tau(r)^{\sharp}\big(\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast}(\tau(r)^{\sharp}(\xi_{2}))(\xi_{1})\big),\;\xi_{3}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\langle y_{i}x_{j}\otimes y_{j}\otimes x_{i},\;\xi_{1}\otimes\xi_{2}\otimes\xi_{3}\rangle.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\;\langle\tau(r)^{\sharp}(\xi_{1})\tau(r)^{\sharp}(\xi_{2})-\tau(r)^{\sharp}\big(\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast}(r^{\sharp}(\xi_{1}))(\xi_{2})+(\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast}-\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast})(\tau(r)^{\sharp}(\xi_{2}))(\xi_{1})\big),\;\xi_{3}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\langle y_{i}\otimes y_{j}\otimes x_{i}x_{j}-x_{j}\otimes y_{j}y_{i}\otimes x_{i}-x_{j}y_{i}\otimes y_{j}\otimes x_{i}+y_{i}x_{j}\otimes y_{j}\otimes x_{i},\;\xi_{1}\otimes\xi_{2}\otimes\xi_{3}\rangle,

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\langle(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\tau)\big((\tau\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)([\![r,r]\!])\big),\;\xi_{1}\otimes\xi_{2}\otimes\xi_{3}\rangle.

That is to say, this proposition is hold.
∎

If

Corollary 3.13
.

Triangular anti-Leibniz bialgebras is an important class of anti-Leibniz bialgebra.
Another important class of anti-Leibniz bialgebra is the factorizable
anti-Leibniz bialgebras.

Definition 3.24
.

A quasi-triangular anti-Leibniz bialgebra
(A,\cdot,\Delta)
is called

factorizable
if the linear map
\mathcal{I}=r^{\sharp}-\tau(r)^{\sharp}:A^{\ast}\rightarrow A
is a linear isomorphism of vector spaces.

Clearly, quasi-triangular anti-Leibniz bialgebras contain triangular anti-Leibniz
bialgebras and factorizable anti-Leibniz bialgebras as two subclasses.
Moreover, it is easy to see that a quasi-triangular anti-Leibniz bialgebra

(A,\cdot,\Delta)
is a triangular anti-Leibniz bialgebra if and only if

\mathcal{I}=r^{\sharp}-\tau(r)^{\sharp}:A^{\ast}\rightarrow A
is the zero map.
Factorizable anti-Leibniz bialgebras are however concerned with the opposite case.
Following, for convenience, we consider the linear map
\mathcal{I}=r^{\sharp}-\tau(r)^{\sharp}:A^{\ast}\rightarrow A
as a composition of maps as follows:

A^{\ast}\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447ptr^{\sharp}\oplus\tau(r)^{\sharp}\hskip 8.19447pt}A\oplus A\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt(a_{1},a_{2})\mapsto a_{1}-a_{2}\hskip 8.19447pt}A.

The following result justifies the terminology of a factorizable anti-Leibniz bialgebra.

Proposition 3.25
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be an anti-Leibniz algebra and
r\in A\otimes A
. If there is an
anti-Leibniz bialgebra
(A,\cdot,\Delta_{r})
induced by
r
and it is factorizable,
then
\mathrm{Im}(r^{\sharp}\oplus\tau(r)^{\sharp})
is an anti-Leibniz subalgebra of the direct
sum anti-Leibniz algebra
A\oplus A
, which is isomorphism to the anti-Leibniz
algebra
(A^{\ast},\cdot_{r})
. Moreover, any
a\in A
has a unique decomposition

a=a_{+}+a_{-}
, where
a_{+}\in\mathrm{Im}(r^{\sharp})
and
a_{-}\in\mathrm{Im}(\tau(r)^{\sharp})
.

Proof.

Since
(A,\cdot,\Delta_{r})
is quasi-triangular anti-Leibniz bialgebra,
both
r^{\sharp}
and
\tau(r)^{\sharp}
are homomorphisms of anti-Leibniz algebras.
Therefore,
\mathrm{Im}(r^{\sharp}\oplus\tau(r)^{\sharp})
is an anti-Leibniz subalgebra
of the direct sum anti-Leibniz algebra
A\oplus A
. Since
\mathcal{I}:A^{\ast}\rightarrow A
is a linear isomorphism, it follows that
r^{\sharp}\oplus\tau(r)^{\sharp}
is injective, then the anti-Leibniz algebra
\mathrm{Im}(r^{\sharp}\oplus\tau(r)^{\sharp})
is isomorphic to the anti-Leibniz algebra
(A^{\ast},\cdot_{r})
.
Moreover, since
\mathcal{I}
is an isomorphism again, for any
a\in A
, we have

\displaystyle a=(r^{\sharp}-\tau(r)^{\sharp})(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a))=r^{\sharp}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a))-\tau(r)^{\sharp}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a)),

which implies that
a=a_{+}+a_{-}
, where
a_{+}=r^{\sharp}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a))
and

a_{-}=-\tau(r)^{\sharp}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a))
. The uniqueness also follows from the fact
that
\mathcal{I}
is an isomorphism.
∎

Recall that a

4.
Infinite-dimensional anti-Leibniz bialgebras

In this section, we constrict infinite-dimensional anti-Leibniz bialgebras
form finite-dimensional anti-Leibniz bialgebras by the completed tensor product.
In this section, a commutative algebra means a commutative associative algebra, and
a cocommutative coalgebra means a cocommutative coassociative coalgebra for simply.

Definition 4.1
.

A
\mathbb{Z}
-graded commutative algebra
(resp. a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
anti-Leibniz algebra
) is a commutative algebra
(C,\cdot)

(resp. an anti-Leibniz algebra
(A,\cdot)
) with a linear decomposition

C=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}C_{i}
(resp.
A=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i}
) such that each
C_{i}

(resp.
A_{i}
) is finite-dimensional and
C_{i}\cdot C_{j}\subseteq C_{i+j}

(resp.
A_{i}\cdot A_{j}\subseteq A_{i+j}
) for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Clearly, the Laurent polynomial algebra
\Bbbk[\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{t}^{-1}]
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
commutative algebra. We now consider a tensor product of anti-Leibniz
algebras and
\mathbb{Z}
-graded commutative algebras.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a finite-dimensional anti-Leibniz algebra,
(C=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}C_{i},\cdot)
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded commutative algebra. Define a binary operation on

\mathcal{A}:=A\otimes C
by

(a_{1}\otimes c_{1})\diamond(a_{2}\otimes c_{2})=(a_{1}a_{2})\otimes(c_{1}c_{2}),

for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and
c_{1},c_{2}\in C
. Then
(\mathcal{A},\diamond)
is a

\mathbb{Z}
-graded anti-Leibniz algebra, which is called
affine anti-Leibniz
algebra
from
(A,\cdot)
by
(C=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}C_{i},\cdot)
.
Moreover, if
(C=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}C_{i},\cdot)=\Bbbk[\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{t}^{-1}]
is the Laurent
polynomial algebra, then
(\mathcal{A},\diamond)
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded anti-Leibniz
algebra if and only if
(A,\cdot)
is an anti-Leibniz algebra.

Proof.

It is straightforward.
∎

To carry out the affine anti-Leibniz coalgebra, we need to extend the codomain
of the coproduct
\Delta
to allow infinite sums.
Let
U=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}U_{i}
and
V=\oplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{j}
be
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector spaces.
We call the
completed tensor product
of
U
and
V
to be the vector space

U\,\hat{\otimes}\,V:=\prod_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}U_{i}\otimes V_{j}.

If
U
and
V
are finite-dimensional, then
U\,\hat{\otimes}\,V
is just the usual
tensor product
U\otimes V
. In general, an element in
U\,\hat{\otimes}\,V
is an
infinite formal sum
\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}X_{ij}
with
X_{ij}\in U_{i}\otimes V_{j}
.
So
X_{ij}=\sum_{\alpha}u_{i,\alpha}\otimes v_{j,\alpha}
for pure tensors

u_{i,\alpha}\otimes v_{j,\alpha}\in U_{i}\otimes V_{j}
with
\alpha
in a finite
index set. Thus a general term of
U\,\hat{\otimes}\,V
is a possibly infinite sum

\sum_{i,j,\alpha}u_{i\alpha}\otimes v_{j\alpha}
, where
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\alpha

is in a finite index set (which might depend on
i,j
). With these notations, for
linear maps
f:U\rightarrow U^{\prime}
and
g:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
, define

f\,\hat{\otimes}\,g:U\,\hat{\otimes}\,V\rightarrow U^{\prime}\,\hat{\otimes}\,V^{\prime},\qquad\sum_{i,j,\alpha}u_{i,\alpha}\otimes v_{j,\alpha}\mapsto\sum_{i,j,\alpha}f(u_{i,\alpha})\otimes g(v_{j,\alpha}).

Also the twist map
\tau
has its completion

\hat{\tau}:V\,\hat{\otimes}\,V\rightarrow V\,\hat{\otimes}\,V,\qquad\sum_{i,j,\alpha}u_{i,\alpha}\otimes v_{j,\alpha}\mapsto\sum_{i,j,\alpha}v_{j,\alpha}\otimes u_{i,\alpha}.

Finally, we define a (completed) coproduct to be a linear map

\hat{\Delta}:V\rightarrow V\,\hat{\otimes}\,V,\qquad\hat{\Delta}(v):=\sum_{i,j,\alpha}v_{1,i,\alpha}\otimes v_{2,j,\alpha}.

Then we have the well-defined map

(\hat{\Delta}\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathop{id}\nolimits)\hat{\Delta}(v)=(\hat{\Delta}\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Big(\sum_{i,j,\alpha}v_{1,i,\alpha}\otimes v_{2,j,\alpha}\Big):=\sum_{i,j,\alpha}\hat{\Delta}(v_{1,i,\alpha})\otimes v_{2,j,\alpha}\in V\,\hat{\otimes}\,V\,\hat{\otimes}\,V.

Definition 4.10

A

anti-Leibniz coalgebra, and the following compatibility condition holds:

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\qquad\quad\qquad\quad\qquad\quad\big(\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{1})\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathop{id}\nolimits\big)\hat{\Delta}(a_{2})-\hat{\tau}\Big(\big(\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{2})\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathop{id}\nolimits\big)\hat{\Delta}(a_{1})\Big)=0.

\displaystyle\hat{\Delta}(a_{1}a_{2})+\Big(\big(\mathop{id}\nolimits\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{2})+(\mathbf{l}_{A}-\mathbf{r}_{A})(a_{2})\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathop{id}\nolimits\big)(\mathop{id}\nolimits\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathop{id}\nolimits-\hat{\tau})\Big)\hat{\Delta}(a_{1})+\big(\mathop{id}\nolimits\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1})+\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1})\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathop{id}\nolimits\big)\hat{\Delta}(a_{2})=0,

for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
.

In the definition above, if
A=A_{0}
, the completed anti-Leibniz bialgebra
is just the anti-Leibniz bialgebra given in Definition
2.11
.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\Delta)
be a finite-dimensional anti-Leibniz bialgebra and

(C=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}C_{i}
,
\varpi)
be a quadratic
\mathbb{Z}
-graded commutative
associative algebra. Suppose that
(\mathcal{A},\diamond)
is the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
anti-Leibniz algebra from
(A,\cdot)
by
(C=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}C_{i},\cdot)
in
Lemma
4.2
,
\bar{\Delta}:C\rightarrow C\,\hat{\otimes}\,C

is the linear map defined by Eq. (
4.2
) and
\hat{\Delta}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathcal{A}
is the linear map defined by Eq. (
4.1
).
Then
(\mathcal{A},\diamond,\hat{\Delta})
is a completed anti-Leibniz bialgebra,
which is called an
affine anti-Leibniz bialgebra
from
(A,\cdot,\Delta)

by
(C=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}C_{i},\;\varpi)
.

Furthermore, if
A
is finite-dimensional and
(C=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}C_{i},\;\varpi)

is the quadratic
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Laurent polynomial algebra
\big(\Bbbk[\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{t}^{-1}],\;\varpi\big)
, then
(\mathcal{A},\diamond,\hat{\Delta})
is a completed anti-Leibniz
bialgebra constructed as above if and only if
(A,\cdot,\Delta)
is an
anti-Leibniz bialgebra.

Proof.

First, since the bilinear form
\varpi(-,-)
on
C
is invariant and
(C,\cdot)
is
an associative algebra, for any
c,c^{\prime},x,y\in C
,
we have

\displaystyle\bar{\varpi}\Big(\sum_{i,j,\alpha}c^{\prime}_{1,i,\alpha}c\otimes c^{\prime}_{2,j,\alpha},\;x\otimes y\Big)

\displaystyle=\bar{\varpi}\Big(\sum_{i,j,\alpha}c^{\prime}_{1,i,\alpha}\otimes c^{\prime}_{2,j,\alpha},\;cx\otimes y\Big)

\displaystyle=\varpi(c^{\prime},\,cxy)=\varpi(c^{\prime}c,\;xy)=\bar{\varpi}(\bar{\Delta}(c^{\prime}c),\;x\otimes y).

That is,
\bar{\Delta}(c^{\prime}c)=\sum_{i,j,\alpha}c^{\prime}_{1,i,\alpha}c\otimes c^{\prime}_{2,j,\alpha}
.
Note that
(C,\cdot)
is commutative and
\bar{\Delta}
is cocommutative, we have

\bar{\Delta}(c^{\prime}c)=\bar{\Delta}(cc^{\prime})=\sum_{i,j,\alpha}c^{\prime}_{1,i,\alpha}\otimes cc^{\prime}_{2,j,\alpha}=\sum_{i,j,\alpha}c_{1,i,\alpha}c^{\prime}\otimes c_{2,j,\alpha}=\sum_{i,j,\alpha}cc^{\prime}_{1,i,\alpha}\otimes c^{\prime}_{2,j,\alpha}=\sum_{i,j,\alpha}c^{\prime}_{1,i,\alpha}\otimes c^{\prime}_{2,j,\alpha}c=\sum_{i,j,\alpha}c_{2,j,\alpha}c^{\prime}\otimes c_{1,i,\alpha}=\sum_{i,j,\alpha}c_{2,j,\alpha}\otimes c^{\prime}c_{1,i,\alpha}
.
Thus, for any
a\otimes c
,
a^{\prime}\otimes c^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
,

\displaystyle\;\big(\mathbf{r}_{\mathcal{A}}(a\otimes c)\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathop{id}\nolimits\big)\hat{\Delta}(a^{\prime}\otimes c^{\prime})-\hat{\tau}\big(\big(\mathbf{r}_{\mathcal{A}}(a^{\prime}\otimes c^{\prime})\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathop{id}\nolimits\big)\hat{\Delta}(a\otimes c)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{(a^{\prime})}\sum_{i,j,\alpha}(a^{\prime}_{(1)}a\otimes a^{\prime}_{(2)})\bullet(c^{\prime}_{1,i,\alpha}c\otimes c^{\prime}_{2,j,\alpha})-\sum_{(a)}\sum_{i,j,\alpha}(a_{(2)}\otimes a_{(1)}a^{\prime})\bullet(c_{2,j,\alpha}\otimes c_{1,i,\alpha}c^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\Big((\mathbf{r}_{A}(a)\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta(a^{\prime})-\tau\big((\mathbf{r}_{A}(a^{\prime})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta(a)\big)\Big)\bullet\bar{\Delta}(cc^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;0,

and

\displaystyle\;\hat{\Delta}((a\otimes c)\diamond(a^{\prime}\otimes c^{\prime}))+\Big(\big(\mathop{id}\nolimits\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathbf{r}_{\mathcal{A}}(a^{\prime}\otimes c^{\prime})+(\mathbf{l}_{\mathcal{A}}-\mathbf{r}_{\mathcal{A}})(a^{\prime}\otimes c^{\prime})\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathop{id}\nolimits\big)(\mathop{id}\nolimits\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathop{id}\nolimits-\hat{\tau})\Big)\hat{\Delta}(a\otimes c)

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\mathop{id}\nolimits\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathbf{l}_{\mathcal{A}}(a\otimes c)+\mathbf{l}_{\mathcal{A}}(a\otimes c)\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathop{id}\nolimits\Big)\hat{\Delta}(a^{\prime}\otimes c^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\Delta(aa^{\prime})\bullet\bar{\Delta}(cc^{\prime})+\sum_{(a)}\sum_{i,j,\alpha}(a_{(1)}\otimes a_{(2)}a^{\prime})\bullet(c_{1,i,\alpha}\otimes c_{2,j,\alpha}c^{\prime})+\sum_{(a)}\sum_{i,j,\alpha}(a^{\prime}a_{(1)}\otimes a_{(2)})\bullet(c^{\prime}c_{1,i,\alpha}\otimes c_{2,j,\alpha})

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{(a)}\sum_{i,j,\alpha}(a_{(1)}a^{\prime}\otimes a_{(2)})\bullet(c_{1,i,\alpha}c^{\prime}\otimes c_{2,j,\alpha})-\sum_{(a)}\sum_{i,j,\alpha}(a_{(2)}\otimes a_{(1)}a^{\prime})\bullet(c_{2,i,\alpha}\otimes c_{1,j,\alpha}c^{\prime})

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{(a)}\sum_{i,j,\alpha}(a^{\prime}a_{(2)}\otimes a_{(1)})\bullet(c^{\prime}c_{2,i,\alpha}\otimes c_{1,j,\alpha})+\sum_{(a)}\sum_{i,j,\alpha}(a_{(2)}a^{\prime}\otimes a_{(1)})\bullet(c_{2,i,\alpha}c^{\prime}\otimes c_{1,j,\alpha})

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{(a^{\prime})}\sum_{i,j,\alpha}(a^{\prime}_{(1)}\otimes aa^{\prime}_{(2)})\bullet(c^{\prime}_{1,i,\alpha}\otimes cc^{\prime}_{2,j,\alpha})+\sum_{(a^{\prime})}\sum_{i,j,\alpha}(aa^{\prime}_{(1)}\otimes a^{\prime}_{(2)})\bullet(cc^{\prime}_{1,i,\alpha}\otimes c^{\prime}_{2,j,\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\Big(\Delta(aa^{\prime})+\big(\big(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\,\mathbf{r}_{A}(a^{\prime})-\mathbf{r}_{A}(a^{\prime})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits+\mathbf{l}_{A}(a^{\prime})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits\big)(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits-\tau)\big)\Delta(a)\Big)\bullet\bar{\Delta}(cc^{\prime})

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\big(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\,\mathbf{l}_{A}(a)+\mathbf{l}_{A}(a)\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits\big)\Delta(a^{\prime})\Big)\bullet\bar{\Delta}(cc^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;0,

since
(A,\cdot,\Delta)
is an anti-Leibniz bialgebra.
By the definition, we get that
(\mathcal{A},\diamond,\hat{\Delta})
is a completed
anti-Leibniz bialgebra.

Second, if
(\mathcal{A},\diamond,\hat{\Delta})
is a completed anti-Leibniz
bialgebra, where
(C=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}C_{i},\;\varpi)
is the quadratic

\mathbb{Z}
-graded Laurent polynomial algebra
(\Bbbk[\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{t}^{-1}]
,
\varpi)
,
denote
a\otimes\mathfrak{t}^{k}
by
a\mathfrak{t}^{k}
for
a\in A
, then for any
a,a^{\prime}\in A
and

k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\;\big(\mathbf{r}_{\mathcal{A}}(a\mathfrak{t}^{k})\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathop{id}\nolimits\big)\hat{\Delta}(a^{\prime}\mathfrak{t}^{l})-\hat{\tau}\big(\big(\mathbf{r}_{\mathcal{A}}(a^{\prime}\mathfrak{t}^{l})\,\hat{\otimes}\,\mathop{id}\nolimits\big)\hat{\Delta}(a\mathfrak{t}^{k})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\Big((\mathbf{r}_{A}(a)\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta(a^{\prime})-\tau\big((\mathbf{r}_{A}(a^{\prime})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta(a)\big)\Big)\bullet\bar{\Delta}(\mathfrak{t}^{k+l}).

Thus, we get Eq. (
2.13
) holds. Similarly, we can get Eq. (
2.12
) also holds.
That is,
(A,\cdot,\Delta)
is an anti-Leibniz bialgebra.
∎

Example 4.12
.

(I)
Let
(\Lambda^{2}_{1},\Delta)
be the
2
-dimension anti-Leibniz bialgebra given
in Example
2.12
. Consider the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space

\Lambda^{2}_{1}\otimes\Bbbk[\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{t}^{-1}]
, we denote the homogeneous element in

\Lambda^{2}_{1}\otimes\Bbbk[\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{t}^{-1}]
by
a\mathfrak{t}^{k}
, where
a\in\Lambda^{2}
.
Then the multiplication
\diamond
on
\Lambda^{2}_{1}\otimes\Bbbk[\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{t}^{-1}]

is given by
e_{1}\mathfrak{t}^{i}\diamond e_{1}\mathfrak{t}^{j}=e_{2}\mathfrak{t}^{i+j}
, and the completed
comultiplication
\hat{\Delta}
on
\Lambda^{2}_{1}\otimes\Bbbk[\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{t}^{-1}]
is
given by
\hat{\Delta}(e_{1}\mathfrak{t}^{k})=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}e_{2}\mathfrak{t}^{i}\otimes e_{2}\mathfrak{t}^{k-i}
,
for any
i,j,k\in\mathbb{Z}
. This anti-Leibniz bialgebra is also a Leibniz bialgebra,
is also an anti-associative bialgebra (see
[
15
]
).

(II)
Let
(A=\Bbbk\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\},\cdot,\Delta)
be an anti-Leibniz bialgebra
with nonzero multiplication and comultiplication:
e_{2}e_{2}=e_{1}=e_{3}e_{3}
and

\Delta(e_{2})=e_{1}\otimes e_{1}
. Then we get an infinite-dimensional anti-Leibniz
bialgebraic structure on
A\otimes\Bbbk[\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{t}^{-1}]
under the multiplication

e_{2}\mathfrak{t}^{i}\diamond e_{2}\mathfrak{t}^{j}=e_{1}\mathfrak{t}^{i+j}=e_{3}\mathfrak{t}^{i}\ast e_{3}\mathfrak{t}^{j}
and
completed comultiplication
\hat{\Delta}(e_{2}\mathfrak{t}^{k})=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}e_{1}\mathfrak{t}^{i}\otimes e_{1}\mathfrak{t}^{k-i}
, for any
i,j,k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 4.13
.

In this section, we using the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded commutative associative algebra to construct
infinite-dimensional anti-Leibniz bialgebra. In fact, we can using the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm
algebra to construct infinite-dimensional anti-Leibniz bialgebra. Recently, Lin, Zhou
and Bai construct a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra in
[
21
]
as follows. Let

P=\{f_{1}\partial_{1}+f_{2}\partial_{2}\mid f_{1},f_{2}\in\Bbbk[x_{1}^{\pm},x_{2}^{\pm}]\}
and define a binary operation
\cdot:P\otimes P\rightarrow P
by

(x_{1}^{i_{1}}x_{2}^{i_{2}}\partial_{s})\cdot(x_{1}^{j_{1}}x_{2}^{j_{2}}\partial_{t})=\delta_{s,1}x_{1}^{i_{1}+j_{1}+1}x_{2}^{i_{2}+j_{2}}\partial_{t}+\delta_{s,2}x_{1}^{i_{1}+j_{1}}x_{2}^{i_{2}+j_{2}+1}\partial_{t},

for any
i_{1},i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
s,t\in\{1,2\}
.
Then
(P,\cdot)
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded perm algebra with the linear decomposition

P=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{i}
, where

P_{i}=\Big\{\sum_{k=1}^{2}f_{k}\partial_{k}\mid f_{k}\text{ is a homogeneous polynomial with }\deg(f_{k})=i-1,\,k=1,2\Big\},

for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. If we replace the Laurent polynomial algebra with this
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
perm algebra, the conclusions of this chapter can also be obtained.

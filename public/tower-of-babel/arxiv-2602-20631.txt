Symmetric Rota-Baxter systems and applications Thanks: * Corresponding author
Chan Zhao, Haiying Li, Tianshui Ma *

Abstract
. Rota-Baxter operators and bialgebras are closely connected in several applications, such as the Connes-Kreimer renormalization framework and the operator approach to the classical Yang-Baxter equation. The concept of a Rota-Baxter system was introduced in 2016 as a generalization of a Rota-Baxter operator. In this work, we introduce a bialgebra structure compatible with a symmetric Rota-Baxter system, called a symmetric Rota-Baxter antisymmetric infinitesimal (ASI) bisystem. This bialgebra is characterized by generalizations of matched pairs of algebras and double constructions of Frobenius algebras to the setting of symmetric Rota-Baxter systems. Investigating the coboundary case leads to an enriched version of the associative Yang-Baxter equation (aYBe) adapted to symmetric Rota-Baxter systems. Antisymmetric solutions of this equation are used to construct symmetric Rota-Baxter ASI bisystems. We also introduce the notion of an 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operator on a symmetric Rota-Baxter system, which produces solutions of the admissible aYBe in such systems and thereby gives rise to symmetric Rota-Baxter ASI bisystems. A symmetric Rota-Baxter bisystem generalizes several known structures, including Rota-Baxter Lie bisystems, Rota-Baxter ASI bialgebras, Rota-Baxter Lie bialgebras, averaging ASI bialgebras, averaging Lie bialgebras, and special apre-perm bialgebras.

1.1.
Rota-Baxter systems

A Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\lambda
is a pair
(A,R)
, where
A
is an algebra and
R:A\rightarrow A
is a linear map such that the following equation holds:

\displaystyle R(a)R(b)=R(R(a)b+aR(b)+\lambda ab),

(1)

for all
a,b\in A
. Rota-Baxter algebra originated from the work of Baxter and Rota in fluctuation theory and combinatorics
[
14
,
44
]
. The theory now plays a pivotal role in modern mathematics, particularly in the study of integrable systems, operads, and the renormalization of quantum field theories
[

,
24
,
53
,
23
,
25
,
13
,
35
,
42
,
41
,
46
,
49
]
.

As a natural generalization of Rota-Baxter algebras, the notion of a Rota-Baxter system was introduced by T. Brzeziński in
[
15
]
. It consists of a triple
(A,R,S)
, where
A
is an algebra and
R,S:A\to A
are linear operators satisfying

\displaystyle R(a)R(b)=R(R(a)b+aS(b)),\quad S(a)S(b)=S(R(a)b+aS(b)),

for all
a,b\in A
. Much like in the case of a Rota-Baxter algebra, such a system gives rise to a dendriform algebra structure. Furthermore, examples of Rota-Baxter systems can be constructed from quasitriangular covariant bialgebras, which can be seen as a natural generalization of infinitesimal bialgebras studied in
[
1
,
2
,
22
]
. Other studies on the Rota-Baxter system can be found in the literature
[
11
,
16
,
39
,
40
,
7
,
31
,
20
,
43
]
.

By
[
15
, Lemma 2.2]
, if
R
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
on
A
, then both
(A,R,R+\lambda{\rm id})
and
(A,R+\lambda{\rm id},R)
form Rota-Baxter systems. Inspired by this construction, in this paper, we study a class of Rota-Baxter systems in which both
(A,R,S)
and
(A,S,R)
are Rota-Baxter systems. This leads to the notion below: a
symmetric Rota-Baxter system
is a triple
(A,R,S)
such that both
(A,R,S)
and
(A,S,R)
are Rota-Baxter systems. Just as a Rota-Baxter algebra induces a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra, a symmetric Rota-Baxter system induces a Rota-Baxter Lie system. While this holds for the symmetric case, a general Rota-Baxter system does not, in general, induce such a Lie system structure. This provides another motivation for studying symmetric Rota-Baxter systems.

Bialgebras

A bialgebra is a mathematical structure that unifies the notions of an algebra and a coalgebra in a compatible manner, with prominent examples including Lie bialgebras

In the associative setting, the notion of a Rota-Baxter bialgebra was introduced in
[
37
]

Symmetric Rota-Baxter systems and their representations

2.1.
Symmetric Rota-Baxter systems

Recall that an
(associative) algebra
is a pair
(A,\cdot)
consisting of a vector space
A
and a binary operation
\cdot:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
satisfying
(ab)c=a(bc)
, for all
a,b,c\in A
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
symmetric Rota-Baxter system
is a triple
((A,\cdot),R,S)
(
(A,R,S)
for short), where
A
is an algebra and
R,S:A\rightarrow A
are linear maps satisfying the identities:

\displaystyle R(a)R(b)=R(R(a)b+aS(b))=R(S(a)b+aR(b)),

(2)

\displaystyle S(a)S(b)=S(R(a)b+aS(b))=S(S(a)b+aR(b)),

(3)

where
a,b\in A
. A morphism between two symmetric Rota-Baxter systems
(A,R_{A},S_{A})
and
(B,R_{B},S_{B})
is an algebra map
f:A\rightarrow B
such that
f\circ R_{A}=R_{B}\circ f
and
f\circ S_{A}=S_{B}\circ f
.

Remark 2.2
.

(a)

The definition of a symmetric Rota-Baxter system is symmetric in the operators
R
and
S
, that is, if
(A,R,S)
is a symmetric Rota-Baxter system, then so is
(A,S,R)
.

(b)

If
A
is a commutative algebra, then a Rota-Baxter system
(A,R,S)
is symmetric.

(c)

If
R
or
S
is bijective, then
R(a)b+aS(b)=S(a)b+aR(b)
.

(d)

If
(A,R,S)
is a symmetric Rota-Baxter system, then both
(A,R,0)
and
(A,0,S)
are averaging algebras.

\mathcal{B}(ab,c)=\mathcal{B}(a,bc)
. A Frobenius algebra
(A,\mathcal{B})
is called
symmetric
if
\mathcal{B}
is symmetric.

Let
(A,\cdot_{A})
be an algebra. Suppose that there is an algebra structure
\cdot_{A^{*}}
on its dual space
A^{*}
, and an algebra structure on the direct sum
A\oplus A^{*}
of the underlying vector spaces of

and
A^{*}
which contains both
(A,\cdot_{A})
and
(A^{*},\cdot_{A^{*}})
as subalgebras. Define a bilinear form on

by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{d}(x+a^{*},y+b^{*})=\langle x,b^{*}\rangle+\langle a^{*},y\rangle,

(25)

for all
a^{*},b^{*}\in A^{*},x,y\in A
. If
\mathcal{B}_{d}
is invariant, then
(A\oplus A^{*},\mathcal{B}_{d})
is a symmetric Frobenius algebra. This Frobenius algebra is called
a double construction of Frobenius algebra associated to
(A,\cdot_{A})
and
(A^{*},\cdot_{A^{*}})
and denoted by
(A\bowtie A^{*},\mathcal{B}_{d})
.

Definition 3.7
.

A
symmetric Rota-Baxter Frobenius system
is a quadruple
(A,R,S,\mathcal{B})
where
(A,R,S)
is a symmetric Rota-Baxter system and
(A,\mathcal{B})
is a Frobenius algebra.

Assume that
\widehat{R},\widehat{S}
are the adjoint linear maps of
R,S
with respect to
\mathcal{B}
respectively, characterized
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(R(a),b)=\mathcal{B}(a,\widehat{R}(b)),\quad\mathcal{B}(S(a),b)=\mathcal{B}(a,\widehat{S}(b)),

(26)

for all
a,b\in A
.

The symmetric Frobenius property of a symmetric Rota-Baxter system
(A,R,S)
ensures that it naturally induces a representation on the dual space
A^{*}
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
(A,R,S,\mathcal{B})
be a symmetric Rota-Baxter Frobenius system with
\mathcal{B}
symmetric. Then for the adjoint operators
\widehat{R},\widehat{S}
in Eq.(

(\widehat{R},\widehat{S})
is adjoint admissible to
(A,R,S)
, or equivalently,
(A^{*},\mathcal{R}^{*},\mathcal{L}^{*},\widehat{R}^{*},\widehat{S}^{*})
is a representation of
(A,R,S)
. Moreover,
(A^{*},\mathcal{R}^{*},\mathcal{L}^{*},\widehat{R}^{*},\widehat{S}^{*})
is equivalent to
(A,\mathcal{L},\mathcal{R},R,S)
as representations of
(A,R,S)

Conversely, let
(A,R,S)
be a symmetric Rota-Baxter system and

two linear maps such that

(A,R,S)

such that

Proof.

For all
a,b,c\in A
, since
(A,\mathcal{B})
a symmetric Frobenius algebra, we have
\mathcal{B}(a\cdot b,c)=\mathcal{B}(b,c\cdot a)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(R(a)R(b)-R(R(a)b+aS(b)),c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(R(a),R(b)c)-\mathcal{B}(R(a)b,\widehat{R}(c))-\mathcal{B}(aS(b),\widehat{R}(c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(a,\widehat{R}(R(b)c)-\widehat{R}(b\widehat{R}(c))-S(b)\widehat{R}(c))

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(R(a)R(b)-R(S(a)b+aR(b)),c)=\mathcal{B}(a,\widehat{R}(R(b)c)-\widehat{S}(b\widehat{R}(c))-R(b)\widehat{R}(c)),

produce

Then we can finish the proof by a similar discussion of

6.
Conclusion

In
[
15
]
, Brzeziński introduced the covariant bialgebra as a generalization of the infinitesimal bialgebra and established its relation to the Rota-Baxter system.

Definition 6.1
.

A
covariant bialgebra
is a quadruple
(A,\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\Delta)
such that

(a)

A
is an algebra,

(b)

(A,\Delta)
is a coalgebra,

(c)

Let
\delta_{i}:A\longrightarrow A\otimes A~(i=1,2)
(write
\delta_{i}(a)=a^{i}_{<1>)}\otimes a^{i}_{<2>}
) be two derivations, i.e.,
\delta_{i}(ab)=a^{i}_{<1>}\otimes a^{i}_{<2>}b+ab^{i}_{<1>}\otimes b^{i}_{<2>},i=1,2
.
\Delta
is a covariant derivation with respect to
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
in the sense of

\displaystyle\Delta(ab)=a^{2}_{<1>}\otimes a^{2}_{<2>}b+ab_{(1)}\otimes b_{(2)}=a_{(1)}\otimes a_{(2)}b+ab^{1}_{<1>}\otimes b^{1}_{<2>}.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
A
be an algebra. An
associative Yang-Baxter pair
is a pair
(\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s})
, where
\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s}\in A\otimes A
such that the following equations hold:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}_{12}\mathfrak{r}_{23}=\mathfrak{r}_{13}\mathfrak{r}_{12}+\mathfrak{s}_{23}\mathfrak{r}_{13},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}_{12}\mathfrak{s}_{23}=\mathfrak{s}_{13}\mathfrak{r}_{12}+\mathfrak{s}_{23}\mathfrak{s}_{13}.

By
[
15
, Proposition 3.4]
, a Rota-Baxter system can be constructed from an associative Yang-Baxter pair
(\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s})
via Eq. (
4
). Furthermore,
[
15
, Proposition 3.15 and Corollary 3.17]
establish that if
(\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s})
is such a pair on an algebra
A
, and we define the maps
\delta_{\mathfrak{r}},\delta_{\mathfrak{s}},\Delta_{\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s}}
by

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathfrak{r}}(a)

\displaystyle=a\mathfrak{r}^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{r}^{2}-\mathfrak{r}^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{r}^{2}a,

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathfrak{s}}(a)

\displaystyle=a\mathfrak{s}^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{2}-\mathfrak{s}^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{2}a,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s}}(a)

\displaystyle=a\mathfrak{r}^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{r}^{2}-\mathfrak{s}^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{2}a,

then the quadruple
(A,\delta_{\mathfrak{r}},\delta_{\mathfrak{s}},\Delta_{\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s}})
forms a covariant bialgebra.

According to Remark
2.9
, a pair
(\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s})
is symmetric if and only if both
(\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s})
and
(\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{r})
are associative Yang-Baxter pairs. Consequently, a symmetric associative Yang-Baxter pair
(\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s})
induces two covariant bialgebra structures:
(A,\delta_{\mathfrak{r}},\delta_{\mathfrak{s}},\Delta_{\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s}})
and
(A,\delta_{\mathfrak{s}},\delta_{\mathfrak{r}},\Delta_{\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{r}})
. This leads to a natural question: how can the notion of a covariant bialgebra be adapted so that it corresponds uniquely to a suitably modified notion of a symmetric associative Yang-Baxter pair?

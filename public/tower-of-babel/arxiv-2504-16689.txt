p p -adic Cherednik algebras on rigid analytic spaces
Fernando Peña Vázquez

Abstract
. Let X X be a smooth rigid space with an action of a finite group G G satisfying that X / G X/G is represented by a rigid space. We construct sheaves of p p -adic Cherednik algebras on the small étale site of the quotient X / G ét X/G_{\textnormal{ét}} , and study some of their properties. The sheaves of p p -adic Cherednik algebras are sheaves of Fréchet-Stein algebras on X / G ét X/G_{\textnormal{ét}} , which can be regarded as p p -adic analytic versions of the sheaves of Cherednik algebras associated to the action of a finite group on a smooth algebraic variety defined by P. Etingof.



1.
Introduction

Double affine Hecke algebras (DAHA) were introduced by I. Cherednik
in
[
18
]
as a tool in his proof of Macdonald’s conjectures about orthogonal polynomials for root systems. Ever since then, DAHA have become prominent objects in representation theory, and have found their way into multiple branches of mathematics, with a plethora of applications ranging from harmonic analysis to deformation theory and the geometric Langlands program. See, for example, the monographs
[
20
]
, and
[
21
]
for an overview of the theory, and
[
19
]
for some of its applications in arithmetic, and in particular to the
p
-adic spherical transform.

This paper is a part of a series of papers
[
42
]
,
[
43
]
,
[
44
]
, in which we convey a systematic study of Ardakov-Wadsley’s sheaf of completed
p
-adic differential operators
\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{X}
and its relevant categories of modules (
i.e.
co-admissible,
\mathcal{C}
-complexes
etc
) employing techniques from non-commutative geometry and deformation theory. In this paper, we will study
p
-adic versions of certain rational degenerations of DAHA called rational Cherednik algebras, as well as their generalizations for actions of a finite group on a smooth rigid space, which we will call
p
-adic Cherednik algebras.

Before explaining the contents of the paper, let us give an overview of the relevant objects: Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a finite dimensional complex vector space, and
G\leq\operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak{h})
be a finite group. We let
S(G)
be the set of pseudo-reflections in
G
(
i.e.
the family of elements
s\in G
satisfying
\operatorname{Rank}(s-\operatorname{Id})=1
), and define a reflection function to be a
G
-invariant function
c:S(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. We let
\operatorname{Ref}(\mathfrak{h},G)
be the finite-dimensional vector space of all reflection functions. The family of rational Cherednik algebras associated to the action of
G
on
\mathfrak{h}
is the family of filtered
\mathbb{C}
-algebras
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
,
parameterized by reflection functions
c\in\operatorname{Ref}(\mathfrak{h},G)
, and defined as the quotient of the tensor algebra

G\ltimes T(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*})
by the following relations:

(1)

[v,w]=0,\textnormal{ }[x,y]=0,\textnormal{ }[v,x]-(v,x)+\sum_{g\in S(G)}c(g)(v,\alpha_{g})(\alpha_{g}^{\vee},x)g=0,

where
v,w\in\mathfrak{h}
, and
x,y\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. These algebras can be regarded as a generalization of Weyl algebras, and their algebraic properties are rather similar. For instance, it is a fundamental result of the theory (known as the PBW Theorem for rational Cherednik algebras), that the canonical filtration of a rational Cherednik algebra (Dunkl-Opdam filtration) yields an isomorphism of graded
K
-algebras:

(2)

G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*})\rightarrow\operatorname{gr}H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G).

In particular, there is a decomposition of
\operatorname{Sym}_{K}(\mathfrak{h}^{*})
-modules:

H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)=\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{h}^{*})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[G]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{h}).

This endows
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
with a triangular decomposition (
cf.

[
29
, Section 2.1 ]
). Algebras with such a decomposition have a rich representation theory, and it is possible to define a Serre subcategory
\mathcal{O}_{c}\subset\operatorname{Mod}(H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G))
, which is similar to the classical BGG category
\mathcal{O}
for reductive Lie algebras. In particular,
\mathcal{O}_{c}
is a highest weight category, and its irreducible objects are in one-to-one correspondence with irreducible complex representations of
G
. If
G=\mathbb{W}
is a Weyl group, then our choice of a reflection function
c\in\operatorname{Ref}(\mathfrak{h},G)
determines a unique Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{c}(G)
. The representations of
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
and
\mathcal{H}_{c}(G)
are related via the
KZ
-functor:

KZ:\mathcal{\mathcal{O}}_{c}\rightarrow\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{f.g.}}(\mathcal{H}_{c}(G)).

This functor is exact, and induces an equivalence of categories:

KZ:\mathcal{\mathcal{O}}_{c}/\mathcal{\mathcal{O}}_{c}^{\operatorname{tor}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{f.g.}}(\mathcal{H}_{c}(G)),

where
\mathcal{\mathcal{O}}_{c}^{\operatorname{tor}}
is a Serre subcategory of
\mathcal{\mathcal{O}}_{c}
. Furthermore, there is a projective object
P_{KZ}\in\mathcal{O}_{c}
, with a canonical isomorphism of algebras
\mathcal{H}_{c}(G)\rightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{O}_{c}}(P_{KZ})^{\operatorname{op}}
satisfying that we have an identification of functors:

KZ=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{c}}(P_{KZ},-).

It has been known for a while that finite Hecke algebras are an indispensable resource in the (complex) representation theory of reductive groups over finite fields. Examples of this can be found, for example, in the classical works of Howlett-Lehrer
[
35
]
, and N. Iwahori
[
37
]
. Thus, the discussion above yields a connection between rational Cherednik algebras and arithmetic via Hecke algebras. This will be a common trend in this introduction and, as we will see below, more general versions of Cherednik algebras are related to more general versions of Hecke algebras, and this leads to applications in the representation theory of reductive algebraic groups.

Besides representation theory, rational Cherednik algebras have also been proven to be a powerful tool in algebraic geometry. To wit, rational Cherednik algebras are a special case of symplectic reflection algebras, which were used by P. Etingof and V. Ginzburg in
[
25
]
to study resolutions of symplectic quotient singularities on orbifolds via deformation theory. The starting point of their work is the observation that, given a vector space
V
with an action of a finite group
G
, some of the geometric properties of action of
G
on
V
are not correctly captured by the quotient
V/G
. For instance, if
G=\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
acts on
V=\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
by rotations, the fundamental group of the quotient
V/G
, is trivial for every value of
m
. In particular, it is not possible to distinguish the actions of different cyclic groups on
V

by the finite étale coverings of the quotient
V/G
.

The idea then is replacing
\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{*})^{G}
, the algebra of regular functions on
V/G
, by the (non-commutative) skew group algebra
G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{*})
, and trying to infer the algebro-geometric properties of the quotient
V/G
from the
non-commutative algebraic geometry
of
G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{*})
. In particular, the approach of
[
25
]
is studying deformations of
V/G
through
non-commutative deformations of the skew group-algebra
G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{*})
. In this setting, symplectic reflection algebras appear as deformations of
G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{*})
, and are related to the coordinate ring of a Poisson deformation of
the quotient singularity
V/G
. If
V=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*}
and
G\leq\operatorname{Gl}(\mathfrak{h})
, then the symplectic reflection algebras obtained in this way are the rational Cherednik algebras described above. Notice that in this situation we have:

G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{*})=G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*})=G\ltimes\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}).

By the PBW decomposition in
(\ref{equation relations rational Cher algebra})
, this is isomorphic to
\operatorname{gr}H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
. Hence, we see that rational Cherednik algebras have applications in algebraic geometry, arising from the fact that
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
may be regarded as a non-commutative deformation of the skew-Weyl algebra
G\ltimes\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h})
, and both algebras provide quantisations of the skew group algebra of regular functions on the cotangent sheaf of
V
.

More generally, P. Etingof extended in
[
24
]
the construction of Cherednik algebras to all smooth algebraic
\mathbb{C}
-varieties
X
with a right action of a finite group
G
. These generalized Cherednik algebras are a family of sheaves of filtered associative algebras on
X/G
, which can be seen as a generalization of the skew group algebra of differential operators
G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{X}
. Furthermore, the restriction of any Cherednik algebra to the smooth locus of
X/G
is isomorphic to
G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{X}
. As we move on from the linear setting to general smooth varieties, a simple presentation of Cherednik algebras in terms of generators and relations as in equation
(\ref{equation presentation of rational Cherednik algebra})
is no longer possible. Instead, letting
X^{\operatorname{reg}}
be the subset of
X
given by the points with trivial stabilizer
, the Cherednik algebras will be defined as certain subalgebras of
G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(X^{\operatorname{reg}})
, where
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
denotes a sheaf of twisted differential operators on
X
. We will postpone the details of this definition until later in the introduction, when we will describe the construction directly in the setting of smooth rigid analytic spaces.

One of the main applications
of Cherednik algebras is the construction of geometric invariants of the quotient
X/G
. In particular, the family of Cherednik algebras is a central tool in the construction of flat formal deformations of the orbifold fundamental group of
X/G
. Let us give a few details of this, following
[
24
]
: The key idea is a generalization of the notion of Hecke algebra to smooth complex varieties with an action of finite group. Let
X
be a connected smooth complex variety with an action of a finite group
G
. In analogy with the classical setting of linear actions on a finite-dimensional vector space, we make the following definition:

Definition
.

The braid group of
X/G
is
B(X/G,x):=\pi_{1}(X^{\operatorname{reg}}/G,x)
, where
x\in X^{\operatorname{reg}}/G
.

As
X
is connected, the isomorphism class of
B(X/G,x)
is independent of the choice of
x\in X^{\operatorname{reg}}/G
, so we will drop the
x
from the notation. For any
g\in G
, let
X^{g}
be the set of points fixed by
g
.

Definition
.

A closed subvariety
Y\subset X^{g}
is a reflection hypersurface if it is a connected component of
X^{g}
which has codimension one in
X
. We let
S(X,G)
be the set of reflection hypersurfaces of the action of
G
on
X
, and denote its elements by
(Y,g)
, where
Y\subset X^{g}
is a reflection hypersurface.

Notice that
G
acts on
S(X,G)
by conjugation, and that
B(X/G)
only depends on (the complement of) the image of
S(X,G)
in
X/G
. Let
(Y,g)\in S(X,G)
be a reflection hypersurface,
G_{Y}<G
be the subgroup of elements which fix
Y
, and
n_{Y}=|G_{Y}|
. As a consequence of the Seifert-Van Kampen Theorem, for any
(Y,g)\in S(X,G)
there is an element in
B(X/G)
which generates the monodromy around the image of
Y
in
X/G
. In other words, there is an element in
B(X/G)
given by the homotopy class of a loop around the image of
Y
in
X/G
(See for example
[
15
, Appendix 1]
for details). We let
C_{Y}
be the conjugacy class in
B(X/G)
of a generator of the monodromy around
Y
.

Definition
.

The orbifold fundamental group of
X/G
, denoted by
\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{orb}}(X/G)
, is the quotient of the braid group
B(X/G)
by the relations:

T^{n_{Y}}=1,\textnormal{ for all }T\in C_{Y},\textnormal{ and }(Y,g)\in S(X,G).

We refer to
[
22
, Definition 1.17]
for a more detailed account on orbifold fundamental groups. Let us just mention that there is a short exact sequence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{orb}}(X/G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1,}

which expresses
\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{orb}}(X/G)
in terms of
\pi_{1}(X)
and
G
. Notice that, unlike the usual notion of fundamental group, the orbifold fundamental group remembers the quotient structure on
X/G
. With these objects at hand, it is time to introduce a version of Hecke algebras adapted to this setting: For any conjugacy class of reflection hypersurfaces
(Y,g)\in S(X,G)
, we introduce the formal parameters
\tau_{Y,1},\cdots,\tau_{Y,n_{Y}}
, and denote the collection of all these parameters by
\tau
.

Definition

(
[
24
, Definition 3.3]
)
.

The Hecke algebra associated to the action of
G
on
X
, denoted
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}(X,G)
, is the quotient of the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[B(X/G)][[\tau]]
by the
\tau
-adically closed ideal generated by the following relations:

\prod_{j=1}^{n_{Y}}\left(T-e^{2\pi ij/n_{Y}}e^{\tau_{Y,j}}\right)=0,\textnormal{ for all }T\in C_{Y},\textnormal{ and }(Y,g)\in S(X,G).

Notice that we have an identification of
\mathbb{C}
-algebras:

\mathcal{H}_{\tau}(X,G)/\tau=\mathbb{C}[\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{orb}}(X/G)],

so that
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}(X,G)
is a formal deformation of
\mathbb{C}[\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{orb}}(X/G)]
. Furthermore, in many situations we have an even stronger result:

Theorem

(
[
24
, Theorem 3.7]
)
.

Assume
\pi_{2}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}=0
. Then
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}(X,G)
is a flat formal deformation of the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{orb}}(X/G)]
.

The proof crucially uses the fact that Cherednik algebras classify the formal deformations of the skew group algebra of differential operators
G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{X}(X)
.

Let us now give a few number-theoretic applications of this new (geometric) notion of Hecke algebra: As a first case, when
X
is a finite-dimensional vector space and
G
is a Weyl group, the definition of Hecke algebra given above agrees with the usual notion of a Hecke algebra of a Weyl group. As discussed above, this is closely related to the (complex) representation theory of reductive groups over finite fields.

Next, let
G
be a simply connected complex simple Lie group,
T\subset G
be a maximal torus, and
\mathbb{W}(T)
be its Weyl group. The Hecke algebras
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}(T,\mathbb{W}(T))
obtained via the procedure above are the affine Hecke algebras. These algebras are a generalization of Iwahori-Hecke algebras, and have been shown to govern the smooth complex representations of
p
-adic reductive groups. For instance, affine Hecke algebras play a major role in the study of induced cuspidal representations in
[
12
]
, and are essential to our understanding of the decomposition of the category of smooth complex representations of a
p
-adic reductive group in terms of semi-simple types, as showcased in
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
.

Similarly, with
T
and
\mathbb{W}(T)
as in the example above, let
R^{\vee}
be the dual root system of
G
, and let
Q^{\vee}
be the lattice generated by
R^{\vee}
. Fix a complex elliptic curve
E
. In
[
41
]
, it is shown that the variety
X=E\otimes Q^{\vee}
is an abelian variety, endowed with a canonical action of
\mathbb{W}(T)
. In this case, the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}(X,\mathbb{W}(T))
is the double affine Hecke algebra mentioned at the beginning of the introduction. Thus, we see an emerging pattern, in which Cherednik algebras encode the deformation-theoretic aspects of Hecke algebras, and these algebras are in turn related to the representation theory of reductive groups defined over a finite or
p
-adic field.

The goal of this paper is developing
p
-adic analogs to some of the situations described above. Namely, we will define sheaves of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras associated to the action of a finite group
G
on a smooth rigid analytic space
X
satisfying mild technical requirements (
cf.
Theorem
2.1.1
). Our hope is that the deformation-theoretic techniques associated to Cherednik algebras can also be applied in the rigid analytic setting. In particular, we expect that the sheaves constructed here can be used to classify the deformations of the skew group algebras of infinite order differential operators
G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{X}(X)
in some situations, and we see this as a first step towards a deformation theory of co-admissible
G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}
-modules in the sense of Ardakov-Wadsley (
cf.

[
3
]
). Furthermore, it is to be expected that
the contents of the paper can be generalized to the case where
G
is a
p
-adic Lie group acting on a smooth rigid analytic space
X
, and we hope that this could lead to new developments in the locally analytic representation theory of
p
-adic Lie groups. For instance, it would be specially interesting to develop a theory of rational Cherednik algebras for
p
-adic Lie groups.

Let us now give an outline of the constructions and main results of the paper: Until the end of the introduction, we let
K
be a complete discrete valuation field of characteristic zero, and fix a finite group
G
. Let us assume for simplicity that
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
is a smooth affinoid space with an action of
G
, and that there is an étale map
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{r}_{K}
. Most of the paper is aimed at the construction of two families of sheaves
H_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
, and
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
on the quotient space
X/G
. These sheaves are parameterized by units
t\in K^{*}
,
G
-invariant cohomology classes
\omega\in\operatorname{H}^{2}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X)^{G}
, and reflection functions
c\in\operatorname{Ref}(X,G)
(
cf.
Definition
3.1.2
). The sheaves
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
are called sheaves of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras, and form the main object of study of this paper. We keep the nomenclature from the classical theory, and call the
H_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
sheaves of Cherednik algebras. The first step is constructing the sheaf of Cherednik algebras

H_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
, associated to the action of
G
on
X
. This construction is rather similar to classical case. The only difficulty is showing that some of the constructions in the algebro-geometric context still make sense for rigid analytic spaces. Next, we want to define the
p
-adic Cherednik algebra:

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G):=\Gamma(X/G,\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}).

The relation of the
p
-adic Cherednik algebra with the Cherednik algebra
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
discussed above is similar to the relation between the algebra of infinite order differential operators
\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{X}(X)
defined by Ardakov-Wadsley in
[
3
]
, and the algebra of differential operators
\mathcal{D}_{X}(X)
. In particular,
p
-adic Cherednik algebras arise as completions of Cherednik algebras with respect to a countable family of norms. Furthermore, the properties of
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
are rather similar to those of
\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{X}(X)
, in the sense that
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
is naturally a Fréchet-Stein algebra, and its elements are in one to one correspondence with rigid analytic functions on the skew group algebra of the cotangent space. That is, there is an isomorphism of Fréchet spaces:

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\cong G\ltimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{r}_{K}}(\mathbb{A}^{r}_{K}).

We remark that this is in no way an isomorphism of algebras, as elements in
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{r}_{K}}(\mathbb{A}^{r}_{K})
do not commute in general
(
cf.
Section
4.3
).

Let us give a concrete description of this completion process. As mentioned above, Cherednik algebras are determined by a choice of a unit
t\in K^{*}
, a
G
-invariant cohomology class
\omega\in\operatorname{H}^{2}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X)^{G}
, and a reflection function
c\in\operatorname{Ref}(X,G)
. In order to further simplify the situation, we will assume that
\omega=0
, and choose
t\in K^{*}
,
c\in\operatorname{Ref}(X,G)
. The general case leads to considerations regarding group actions on sheaves of infinite order twisted differential operators, which we will postpone until Section
2.4
.

The geometric notion of Cherednik algebra we are about to introduce is defined in terms of the so-called Dunkl-Opdam operators. These operators have a rather convoluted expression (
cf.
Definition
3.1.3
). In order to make their expression simpler, we will also assume that every reflection hypersurface
(Y,g)\in S(X,G)
satisfies that
Y
is the zero locus of a rigid function
f_{(Y,g)}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
. We remark that given a smooth affinoid space
X
equipped with a
G
-action, there is always an admissible affinoid cover of
X
by
G
-invariant affinoid subdomains, each of which satisfies the assumptions we have made thus far (
cf.
Proposition
2.1.9
). We define the Cherednik algebra
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
as the subalgebra of
G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{X}(X^{\operatorname{reg}})
generated by
G
,
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
, and the (standard) Dunkl-Opdam operators:

D_{v}=t\mathbb{L}_{v}+\sum_{(Y,g)\in S(X,G)}\frac{2c(Y,g)}{1-\lambda_{Y,g}}\frac{v(f_{(Y,g)})}{f_{(Y,g)}}(g-1),

where
v
is a vector field on
X
. The operators
\lambda_{Y,g}\in K\setminus\{1\}
are the eigenvalues of the action of
g
on the conormal bundle of
Y
(
cf
. Proposition
2.1.7
). The idea is defining
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
as the closure of
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
in
G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{X}(X^{\operatorname{reg}})
. However, the analytical structure of
\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{X}(X^{\operatorname{reg}})
is not clear, and this definition makes it hard to work with
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
. In order to solve this, we notice that any big enough
G
-invariant affinoid space
U\subset X^{\operatorname{reg}}
satisfies:

H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\subset G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{X}(U).

Furthermore, under our current assumptions, we can use the constructions in
[
3
]
to produce an explicit Fréchet-Stein presentation of
\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{X}(U)
. This allows us to obtain a characterization of the closure of
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
in
G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{X}(U)
, which we denote
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U}
. The crux of the matter is showing that, for big enough
U\subset X^{\operatorname{reg}}
, the algebra
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U}
is independent of the choice of
U
. This is a delicate matter, which we are able to solve by studying the relation between
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U}

and the Shilov boundary of
X
(
cf
. Section
4.1
). Our main theorem is as follows:

Theorem A
.

Let
U\subset X^{\operatorname{reg}}
, and assume
U
contains the Shilov boundary of
X
. Then
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U}
is a Fréchet-Stein algebra, and is independent of
U
.

Proof.

This is shown in Theorems
4.3.4
, and
4.3.15
.
∎

Thus, dropping the
U
from the notation, we obtain a definition of
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
.

Notice that this theorem allows us to define
p
-adic Cherednik algebras for any
G
-invariant affinoid subdomain
Y\subset X
. Furthermore, the family of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras for varying
Y\subset X
can be arranged in a canonical way into a sheaf on the quotient space
X/G
. By working in a slightly more general setting, we can even show that they extend to a sheaf on the small étale site
X/G_{\textnormal{ét}}
. This sheaf is defined on étale and affinoid maps
Y\rightarrow X/G
by the rule:

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}(Y,G):=\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(Y\times_{X/G}X,G),

and we define the sections of
H_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
analogously. The restriction maps of
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
are obtained by continuously extending those of
H_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
, and these maps are, in turn, uniquely determined by restriction of the canonical maps:

G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{X}(Y^{\operatorname{reg}})\rightarrow G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{X}(Z^{\operatorname{reg}}),

where
Z\rightarrow Y\rightarrow X
are
G
-equivariant étale maps. Sheaves of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras satisfy the following properties:

Theorem B
.

Choose
t\in K^{*}
,
\omega\in\operatorname{H}^{2}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X)^{G}
, and
c\in\operatorname{Ref}(X,G)
. The sheaf of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
is the unique sheaf on
X/G_{\textnormal{ét}}
satisfying:

(i)

Let
Y\rightarrow X/G
be an étale and affinoid map. Then we have:

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}(Y,G):=\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(Y\times_{X/G}X,G).

(ii)

Let
Z\rightarrow Y\rightarrow X/G
be étale and affinoid maps. Then the restriction map:

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}(Y,G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}(Z,G),

is a continuous morphism of Fréchet-Stein
K
-algebras.

(iii)

There is an injective map of sheaves of
K
-algebras

.

Furthermore, the sheaves
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
have trivial higher Čech cohomology.

Proof.

This is shown in Theorem

.
∎

We remark that, in order to make sense of this definition, one needs to show that every reflection function

extends in a unique way to a

on

2.
Twisted differential operators on rigid analytic spaces

In this chapter we will establish the basic tools needed for the definition of

2.1.
Basic constructions

In this section, we will develop the rigid-geometric tools needed for the definition of Cherednik algebras in the rigid analytic setting.

As in the classical case, we will ultimately define Cherednik algebras as sheaves on the quotient of
X
by the action of
G
. Thus, in order to have a consistent theory, we need to understand the conditions under which there is a rigid analytic quotient
X/G
. The most important result in this direction is the following theorem :

Theorem 2.1.1

(
[
31
]
)
.

Let
X
be a rigid analytic space with a right action by a finite group
G
. Assume that this action satisfies the following property:

\textnormal{(G-Aff)}:=\textnormal{There is an admissible cover of }X\textnormal{ by }G\textnormal{-stable affinoid spaces.}

Let
\operatorname{Rig}_{K}
be the category of rigid spaces over
K
. The following hold:

(i)

The action of
G
on
X
admits a categorical quotient
X/G
in
\operatorname{Rig}_{K}
.

(ii)

The canonical projection:

\pi:X\rightarrow X/G,

is finite. Furthermore,
X
is affinoid if and only if
X/G
is affinoid.

(iii)

If
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
, then
X/G=\operatorname{Sp}(A^{G})
.

(iv)

The preimage along the projection
\pi:X\rightarrow X/G
induces a bijection:

\pi^{-1}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\textnormal{admissible open }\\
\textnormal{subspaces of }X/G\end{array}\right\}\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}G-\textnormal{stable admissible open}\\
\textnormal{subspaces of }X\end{array}\right\},

satisfying that for every admissible open
U\subset X/G
we have
U=\pi^{-1}(U)/G
.

We remark that the result shown in
[
31
]
is much more general than the versions stated here. However, this version is enough for our purposes. Let us now introduce the following terminology:

Definition 2.1.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group. A
G
-variety is a rigid analytic variety
X
equipped with a faithful right
K
-linear action of
G
which satisfies
\operatorname{(G-Aff)}
.

Let
X
be a
G
-variety. Our next goal is describing certain geometric aspects of the action of
G
on
X
. Namely, we will show that the fixed point locus
X^{G}
is a closed smooth subvariety of
X
, introduce reflection hypersurfaces, and show that
G
-varieties admit certain covers with good properties. As most of the material is well-known for algebraic varieties over characteristic zero, we will omit the proofs of most of the results. Let us start with the following definition:

Definition 2.1.3
.

Let
X
be a
G
-variety. We define
X^{G}
to be the functor of points fixed by the action of
G
. That is, for every rigid space
S
we have:

X^{G}(S)=\{f:S\rightarrow X\textnormal{ such that }gf=f\textnormal{ for every }g\in G\}.

Notice that
X^{G}
fits into the following cartesian diagram:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\prod_{g\in G}X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod\operatorname{Id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod g}

Thus, the functor
X^{G}
is representable. Furthermore, as
X
satisfies
\operatorname{(G-Aff)}
, the lower horizontal map is a closed immersion, and so the pullback
X^{G}\rightarrow X
is also a closed immersion. Furthermore, the following additional result holds:

Proposition 2.1.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth rigid analytic
G
-variety. Then, the fixed point locus
X^{G}
is a smooth closed subvariety of
X
. In particular, if
g
is an automorphism of finite order of
X
, then
X^{g}
is a closed smooth subvariety of
X
.

Next, we will introduce some geometric objects attached to smooth
G
-varieties. We start with reflection hypersurfaces and their associated eigenvalues:

Definition 2.1.5
.

Let
X
be a
G
-variety. A reflection hypersurface on
X
is a pair
(Y,g)
, where
g\in G
and
Y
is a connected component of
X^{g}
satisfying that:

\operatorname{codim}(Y):=\operatorname{dim}(X)-\operatorname{dim}(Y)=1.

We denote the set of all reflection hypersurfaces of the action of
G
on
X
by
S(X,G)
.

Let
X
be a
G
-variety and choose
(Y,g)\in S(X,G)
. We let
\mathcal{I}_{Y}
be the sheaf of ideals associated to
Y
, and let
\mathcal{N}_{Y}^{*}=\mathcal{I}_{Y}/\mathcal{I}_{Y}^{2}
be the conormal bundle to
Y
. As
Y
has codimension one in
X
, it follows that
\mathcal{I}_{Y}
is a line bundle on
X
. Consequently,
\mathcal{N}_{Y}^{*}
is a line bundle on
Y
. As the action of
g:X\rightarrow X
leaves
Y\subset X^{g}
fixed, we have
g(\mathcal{I}_{Y})\subset\mathcal{I}_{Y}
. Hence, we get an
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-linear action
g:\mathcal{N}_{Y}^{*}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{Y}^{*}
.

Definition 2.1.6
.

The map
g:\mathcal{N}_{Y}^{*}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{Y}^{*}
is determined by a unique function
\lambda_{Y,g}\in\Gamma(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})
. We call
\lambda_{Y,g}
the eigenvalue of
(Y,g)
.

The following proposition justifies the nomenclature used for
\lambda_{Y,g}
:

Proposition 2.1.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth rigid
G
-variety. For any reflection hypersurface
(Y,g)\in S(X,G)
, the eigenvalue
\lambda_{Y,g}
is a constant function on
Y
. In particular, it is a root of unity in
K
. Furthermore, assume
X
is connected and let
Y\subset X
be a connected hypersurface. We define the following subgroup:

G_{Y}=\{g\in G\textnormal{ }|\textnormal{ }Y\subset X^{g}\}.

Then
G_{Y}
is cyclic and acts
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-linearly and faithfully on the conormal bundle
\mathcal{N}_{Y}^{*}
.

We finish our preliminaries on
G
-varieties by showing that every
G
-variety admits an admissible cover with particularly good properties. This will be instrumental for our definition of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras in later sections.

Lemma 2.1.8
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
be an affinoid space with a vector bundle
\mathcal{V}
. For any finite set
S\subset X
there is an open subspace
S\subset U\subset X
such that
\mathcal{V}_{|U}
is free. Furthermore,
U
can be taken to be a Zariski or affinoid open subspace of
X
.

Proof.

For each
s\in S
we can find rigid functions
f_{s}\in A
such that
f_{s}(s^{\prime})=\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}
for
s^{\prime}\in S
. We let
\mathcal{V}_{s}=\mathcal{V}(X)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)}k(s)
denote the residue of
\mathcal{V}
at
s
, and choose
a set of sections
\sigma_{1,s},\dots,\sigma_{n,s}
on
\mathcal{V}(X)
which give a basis of the residue
\mathcal{V}_{s}
. Consider the following sums:

\sigma_{k}=\sum_{s\in S}f_{s}\sigma_{k,s}.

For each
s^{\prime}
, the image of
\sigma_{k}
in
\mathcal{V}_{s^{\prime}}
is
(\sigma_{k})_{s^{\prime}}=\sum_{s\in S}f_{s}(s^{\prime})(\sigma_{k,s})_{s^{\prime}}=(\sigma_{k,s^{\prime}})_{s^{\prime}}
. Hence, it follows that
\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n}
are a family of sections such that their residue classes form a basis of
\mathcal{V}_{s}
for each
s\in S
. Consider the morphism
\varphi:A^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}(X)
given by sending
e_{i}
to
\sigma_{i}
. As both modules are finite, the locus in which
\varphi
is an isomorphism is a Zariski open
V\subset X
. In particular,
\mathcal{V}
is free when restricted to
V
, and our calculations above show that
S\subset V
. As every Zariski open of
X
admits an admissible cover
V=\cup_{n\geq 0}U_{n}
by increasing affinoid subdomains of
X
, it follows that
S\subset U_{n}
for big enough
n\geq 0
.
∎

Proposition 2.1.9
.

Let
X
be a smooth
G
-variety . There is an admissible cover
\mathfrak{U}
of
X
such that every
U\in\mathfrak{U}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

U
is a
G
-invariant admissible affinoid open.

(ii)

The module of Kähler differentials
\Omega^{1}_{X/K}(U)
is a free
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
-module with a basis given by differentials of functions.

(iii)

Any reflection hypersurface on
U
is given by the vanishing locus of a single rigid function on
U
.

Proof.

By definition of
G
-variety, we can assume that
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
is affinoid. Let
x\in X
be a point. As
G
is a finite group, the orbit
\textnormal{Orb}(x)=\{g(x)\}_{g\in G}
is a finite subset of
X
. Denote this subset by
S
. As
X
is smooth, every reflection hypersurface
(Y,g)
is associated to a sheaf of ideals
\mathcal{I}_{(Y,g)}
which is a line bundle. As
\Omega^{1}_{X/K}
is a vector bundle as well, we can use Lemma
2.1.8
to construct a Zariski open
U\subset X
which contains
S
and satisfies conditions
(ii)
and
(iii)
. As
X
is affinoid, it follows that
V=\cap_{g\in G}g(U)
is a
G
-invariant Zariski open of
X
which contains
x
and satisfies conditions
(ii)
and
(iii)
. The previous procedure yields a
G
-invariant Zariski open
V_{x}\subset Y
satisfying conditions
(ii)
and
(iii)
. Thus, as Zariski covers are always admissible, we obtain an admissible cover
\mathfrak{U}
of
X
such that its elements satisfy conditions
(i)
to
(iii)
in the definition, but are Zariski instead of admissible. But then we can apply Theorem
2.1.1
to refine this cover to a cover satisfying our requirements.
∎

Finally, we will now give rigid analytic versions of the sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z)
and the residue morphism from
[
24
, Section 2.4]
.

Lemma 2.1.10
.

Let
X
be a smooth and separated rigid space with a hypersurface
Z
. There is a line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z)
on
X
such that on every affinoid open
U\subset X
the sections
\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z)(U)
are the meromorphic functions in
U
with a pole of order at most one in
Z
. Furthermore, there is a short exact sequence of coherent modules:

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z)\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{Z}\rightarrow 0,

where
\mathcal{N}_{Z}=\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{Z}}(\mathcal{I}_{Z}/\mathcal{I}_{Z}^{2},\mathcal{O}_{Z})
is the normal bundle to
Z
in
X
.

Proof.

As
X
is separated, it is enough to show the case where
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
is affinoid and
Z
has associated sheaf of ideals
\mathcal{I}_{Z}
generated by a single rigid function
f\in A
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{X}
be the sheaf of meromorphic functions on
X
, as defined in
[
26
, Section 4.6]
. Then we have an injective morphism of
A
-modules:

m(f^{-1}):=\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{X}(X),\textnormal{ }h\mapsto\frac{h}{f}.

This lifts to a morphism of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules,
m(f^{-1}):=\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{X}
, and we let
\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z)=\operatorname{Im}(m(f^{-1}))
. Notice that for any two rigid functions
f,g\in A
which generate
\mathcal{I}_{Z}
, there is a unique unit
\lambda\in A
such that
f=\lambda g
. Thus, the sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z)
is independent of the choice of generator for
\mathcal{I}_{Z}
. Furthermore, the sections of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z)
on affinoid subdomains are meromorphic functions with a pole of order at most one in
Z
. We also have the following identity:

\mathcal{N}_{Z}=\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{Z}}(\mathcal{I}_{Z}/\mathcal{I}_{Z}^{2},\mathcal{O}_{Z})=\operatorname{Hom}_{A/I}(f\cdot A/f^{2}\cdot A,A/f)=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(f\cdot A,A/f),

where we are using the identification between finitely generated
\mathcal{O}_{Z}(Z)
-modules and coherent
\mathcal{O}_{Z}
-modules. We define the morphism
\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z)\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{Z}
by sending an element
a/f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z)
to the following morphism of
A
-modules:

a/f:f\cdot A\rightarrow A/f,\textnormal{ }fb\mapsto[ab].

The map
\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z)\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{Z}
is clearly surjective, and its kernel is given by elements
a/f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z)
such that
a=fh
for some
h\in A
. In particular, we have
a/f=h\in A
, so we get a short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow A\rightarrow A/f\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(f\cdot A,A/f)\rightarrow 0.

Now we just need to show that these constructions can be glued together. That is, we need to show they do not depend on the choice of a generator of the sheaf of ideals induced by
Z
. This follows from the fact that if
f
and
g
are generators of
\mathcal{I}_{Z}
, then there is a unique unit
\lambda
in
A
such that
g=\lambda f
. Thus, one can repeat the process above with both
f
and
g
, and multiplication by
\lambda
yields an isomorphism between the two exact sequences. The fact that
\lambda
is unique implies that the cocycle condition is satisfied, so this construction can be globalized.
∎

Now that we have defined
\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z)
, we can construct the residue morphism:

Proposition 2.1.11
.

Let
X
be a smooth and separated rigid
K
-variety with a hypersurface
Z\subset X
. There is a unique morphism of coherent modules:

\xi_{Z}:\mathcal{T}_{X/K}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z)/\mathcal{O}_{X}\cong\mathcal{N}_{Z},

such that on every affinoid open
U\subset X
satisfying that
U\cap Z
is defined as the vanishing locus of a rigid function
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
, the morphism is given on any vector field
v\in\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(U)
by the formula:

\xi_{Z}(v)=\left[\frac{v(f)}{f}\right].

Proof.

Assume first that
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
is such that
Z
is the vanishing locus of a single rigid function
f
on
A
. In this situation, notice that by Lemma
2.1.10
we have an isomorphism
\varphi:\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z)/\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{Z}=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(f\cdot A,A/f)
. Consider a morphism of
A
-modules
\xi:f\cdot A\rightarrow A/f
. This morphism is uniquely determined by an element

\xi(f)\in A/f
. Let
\overline{\xi(f)}
be a representative of
\xi(f)
in
A
. By construction of
\varphi
, we have that
\varphi^{-1}(\xi)=\left[\frac{\overline{\xi(f)}}{f}\right]
. Recall from
[
36
, Proposition 1.6.3.ii]
the canonical right exact sequence of Kähler differentials associated to the closed immersion
i:Z\rightarrow X
. Dualizing this sequence, we get a left exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{Z/K}\rightarrow i^{*}\mathcal{T}_{X/K}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{Z}.

In particular, there is an
A
-linear morphism
\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)\rightarrow i^{*}\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(Z)\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{Z}(Z)
. This sends a global derivation
v\in\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
to the morphism of
A
-modules:

v:f\cdot A\rightarrow A/f,\,fa\mapsto[v(fa)].

Hence, we get a morphism
\xi_{Z}:\mathcal{T}_{X}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z)(X)/\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
, defined on any
v\in\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(U)
by the formula
\xi_{Z}(v)=\left[\frac{v(f)}{f}\right]
. As all morphisms involved in this construction are defined globally, it follows that the map can be defined for the general smooth and separated
X
.
∎

Definition 2.1.12
.

Let
X
be a smooth and separated rigid space with a hypersurface
Z
. The map
\xi_{Z}:\mathcal{T}_{X/K}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z)/\mathcal{O}_{X}
is called the residue map of
Z
.

In later stages, the residue morphism will be used to define certain generators of the Cherednik algebras. To wit, the residue morphism is an essential ingredient in the definition of the Dunkl-Opdam operators.

2.2.
Classification of Atiyah algebras on smooth rigid analytic spaces

In this section we will introduce sheaves of infinite order twisted differential operators on smooth rigid spaces. The goal is showing that the theory of
[

]
extends naturally to the twisted setting.
In particular, we will define sheaves of completed
p
-adic twisted differential operators, and show twisted versions of the results in
[

]
. Along the way, we will give a classification of the sheaves of twisted differential operators on a smooth rigid analytic space

.

Let us start by recalling the definition of an Atiyah algebra:

Definition 2.2.1

(
[
6
, Definition 2.1.3]
)
.

An Atiyah algebra (Picard algebroid) on a smooth rigid space
X
, is a Lie algebroid
(\mathcal{A},[-,-])
, with a short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{X/K}\rightarrow 0,

satisfying that
\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{X/K}
is the anchor map of the Lie-Rinehart algebra structure, and that for every affinoid open
U\subset X
, the unit
1\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
is central in
\mathcal{A}(U)
. We will usually denote this element by
1_{\mathcal{A}}
. A morphism of Atiyah algebras is a morphism of Lie algebroids which preserves
1
. We denote the category of Atiyah algebras on
X
by
\mathscr{PA}(X)
.

Remark 2.2.2
.

Let
A
be a commutative
K
-algebra. We can define an Atiyah algebra on
A
as a
(K,A)
-Lie algebra
\mathcal{A}
satisfying the conditions on Definition
2.2.1
, and similarly for morphisms. We call the corresponding category
\mathscr{PA}(A)
.

We will start by constructing some examples of Atiyah algebras. Assume first that
X
is affinoid, and let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
X
. By
[
3
, Lemma 9.2]
, Atiyah algebras are completely determined by their global sections. In particular, every Atiyah algebra on
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
lifts to an Atiyah algebra on
X
. Let
A=\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
and
L=\mathcal{L}(X)
. As
\mathcal{L}
is a line bundle on
X
, we have
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(L,L)=A
. In particular, we may regard
A
as a subalgebra of
\operatorname{Hom}_{K}(L,L)
, and we consider
\operatorname{Hom}_{K}(L,L)
as a
K
-Lie algebra with respect to the commutator bracket. We define the
(K,A)
-Lie algebra of first order differential operators on
\mathcal{L}
as follows:

\operatorname{Diff}_{A}(L,L)=\{f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{K}(L,L)\textnormal{ }|\textnormal{ }[a,f]:=af-fa\in A\textnormal{ for all }a\in A\}.

The bracket on
\operatorname{Diff}_{A}(L,L)
is given by the commutator, and the anchor map is given by the map:

\pi:\operatorname{Diff}_{A}(L,L)\rightarrow\operatorname{Der}_{K}(A,A),\quad f\mapsto(a\mapsto af-fa),

which is well-defined by definition of
\operatorname{Diff}_{A}(L,L)
. The kernel of
\pi
is given by
the
f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{K}(L,L)
which commute with the action of
A
. Thus, we have:

\operatorname{ker}(\pi)=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(L,L)=A,

and we have a left exact sequence of finite
A
-modules:

0\rightarrow A\rightarrow\operatorname{Diff}_{A}(L,L)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X).

In order to show that it is right exact, it suffices to show that the corresponding sequence of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules is exact, and this holds because

\operatorname{Diff}_{A}(L,L)=\operatorname{Der}_{K}(A,A)
whenever
L
is a trivial line bundle. Therefore,
\mathcal{L}
induces an Atiyah algebra on
X
, which we will denote by
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{L}}
. Furthermore, an easy calculation shows that for any affinoid subdomain
U\subset X
we have:

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{L}}(U)=\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)\otimes_{A}\operatorname{Diff}_{A}(L,L)=\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)}(\mathcal{L}(U),\mathcal{L}(U))=\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{L}_{|U}}(U).

Thus, this construction globalizes, and we have the following proposition:

Proposition 2.2.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth rigid
K
-variety and
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
X
. Then
\mathcal{L}
induces a unique Atiyah algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{L}}
on
X
, satisfying that for any affinoid subspace
U\subset X
we have:

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{L}}(U)=\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)}(\mathcal{L}(U),\mathcal{L}(U)).

As we will see in the next section, every Atiyah algebra gives rise to a sheaf of filtered algebras, which is called its sheaf of twisted differential operators. For instance, the trivial Atiyah algebra
\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus\mathcal{T}_{X}
induces the usual sheaf of differential operators on
X
. Thus, we can see the previous proposition, together with the (to be defined) twisted differential operators construction, as a way of obtaining sheaves of differential operators with coefficients in line bundles.

Now that we have a basic understanding of Atiyah algebras, the next step is giving a classification of their isomorphism classes. Assume first that
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
is affinoid, with an Atiyah algebra
\mathcal{A}
. Then it suffices to classify the global sections. We will now recall the description in
[
28
, Chapter I.2.4.4]
of the
(K,A)
-Lie algebra structure on
\mathcal{A}(X)
. First, as
X
is smooth, it follows that
\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
is a finite projective module. In particular, we have a decomposition:

\mathcal{A}(X)=A\oplus\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X).

As the map
\mathcal{A}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
is a morphism of
K
-Lie algebras, it follows that for
f,g\in A
, and
v,w\in\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
, we have:

(3)

[(f,v),(g,w)]=(v(g)-w(f)+\omega(v,w),[v,w]).

The axioms of a
(K,A)
-lie algebra can be used to show that the map:

\omega:\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)\times\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)\rightarrow A,

is in fact a closed
2
-form on
X
. On the other hand, any closed
2
-form
\omega\in\Omega_{X/K}^{2,\operatorname{cl}}(X)
may be used to construct an Atiyah algebra via the formula in equation
(\ref{bracket of tdo})
.

Definition 2.2.4
.

Given a form
\omega\in\Omega_{X/K}^{2,\operatorname{cl}}(X)
, we let
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
be the unique Atiyah algebra on
X
induced by
\omega
via the formula in equation
(\ref{bracket of tdo})
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{\omega_{1}},\mathcal{A}_{\omega_{2}}
be two Atiyah algebras and assume we have a morphism:

\varphi:\mathcal{A}_{\omega_{1}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\omega_{2}}.

As
\varphi
is
A
-linear, commutes with the anchor maps, and satisfies
\varphi(1_{\mathcal{A}_{\omega_{1}}})=1_{\mathcal{A}_{\omega_{2}}}
, it is an isomorphism. Furthermore, it has to be given by the expression:

(4)

\varphi([a,v])=[a+d_{\varphi}(v),v],\textnormal{ for }a\in A,\textnormal{ }v\in\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X).

As
\varphi
commutes with the brackets, the following identity holds for
v,w\in\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
:

(5)

\omega_{1}(v,w)-\omega_{2}(v,w)=v(d_{\varphi}(w))-w(d_{\varphi}(v)).

Regarding
d_{\varphi}:\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)\rightarrow A
as an element in
\Omega^{1}_{X/K}(X)
, equation
(\ref{equation isomorphisms of Atiyah algebras})
shows that two Atiyah algebras
\mathcal{A}_{\omega_{1}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\omega_{2}}
are isomorphic if and only if
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
represent the same cohomology class in
\operatorname{H}^{2}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X)=\operatorname{H}^{2}\left(\Gamma(X,\Omega_{X/K}^{\bullet})\right)
. We may condense the previous discussion into the following proposition:

Proposition 2.2.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth affinoid variety. The following hold:

(i)

The category
\mathscr{PA}(X)
is an essentially small groupoid.

(ii)

The isomorphism classes of
\mathscr{PA}(X)
are in bijection with
\operatorname{H}^{2}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X)
.

(iii)

Every
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\in\mathscr{PA}(X)
satisfies
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathscr{PA}(X)}(\mathcal{A}_{\omega})=\Omega_{X/K}^{1,\operatorname{cl}}(X)
.

The idea now is extending this classification to all smooth and separated rigid analytic spaces. However, as
X
is no longer affinoid, the global sections of the de Rham complex no longer need to calculate the de Rham cohomology, and Atiyah algebras are not necessarily determined by their global sections. This leads us to introduce a truncated version of the de Rham complex:

Definition 2.2.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth rigid
K
-variety. We define the truncated de Rham complex of
X
to be the following complex:

\Omega_{X/K}^{\geq 1}:=\left(0\rightarrow\Omega_{X/K}^{1}\rightarrow\Omega^{2,\operatorname{cl}}_{X/K}\rightarrow 0\right).

With this new complex at hand, we can finally state our desired classification:

Theorem 2.2.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth rigid analytic space. The following hold:

(i)

The category
\mathscr{PA}(X)
is an essentially small groupoid. In particular, the category of isomorphism classes
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathscr{PA}(X))
is a pointed set.

(ii)

There is an isomorphism of pointed sets
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathscr{PA}(X))\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{1}(X,\Omega_{X/K}^{\geq 1})
.

(iii)

For an Atiyah algebra
\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{PA}(X)
, we have
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathscr{PA}(X)}(\mathcal{A})=\Omega_{X/K}^{1,\operatorname{cl}}(X)
.

Proof.

This is essentially the same as
[
28
, Theorem I.2.4.4.3]
.
∎

By definition of
\Omega^{\geq 1}_{X/K}
, we have the following short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\Omega^{2,\operatorname{cl}}_{X/K}[1]\rightarrow\Omega^{\geq 1}_{X/K}\rightarrow\Omega^{1}_{X/K}\rightarrow 0.

By taking hypercohomology, we arrive at the left exact exact sequence:

(6)

0\rightarrow\textnormal{H}^{2}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/K}(X))\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{1}(X,\Omega_{X/K}^{\geq 1})\rightarrow\textnormal{H}^{1}(X,\Omega_{X/K}^{1}).

As seen in Proposition
2.2.3
, each line bundle
\mathcal{L}
induces an Atiyah algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{L}}
on
X
. Hence, we the previous sequence induces a map:

\operatorname{Pic}(X)\rightarrow\textnormal{H}^{1}(X,\Omega_{X/K}^{1}),

which corresponds to taking the first Chern class. If
X
is affinoid, then
\textnormal{H}^{1}(X,\Omega_{X/K}^{1})=0
, and
\textnormal{H}^{2}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/K}(X))=\textnormal{H}_{\operatorname{dR}}^{2}(X)
, so we recover the local classification.

2.3.
Twisted differential operators on smooth rigid spaces

Let
X
be a smooth rigid space with an Atiyah algebra
\mathcal{A}
. As shown in
[
3
, Section 2.3]
, there is a unique sheaf of filtered algebras
U(\mathcal{A})
on
X
satisfying the following properties:

(i)

Let
Y=\operatorname{Sp}(A)\subset X
be an admissible open, then
\Gamma(Y,U(\mathcal{A}))=U(\mathcal{A}(Y))
, where
U(\mathcal{A}(Y))
is the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathcal{A}(Y)
(
cf.

[
46
]
).

(ii)

If
Z=\operatorname{Sp}(B)\subset Y=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
is an affinoid subdomain, then we have:

\Gamma(Z,U(\mathcal{A}))=U(\mathcal{A}(Z))=B\otimes_{A}U(\mathcal{A}(Y)).

We call
U(\mathcal{A})
the sheaf of enveloping algebras of
\mathcal{A}
. We are now ready to define the sheaves of twisted differential operators:

Definition 2.3.1
.

The sheaf of twisted differential operators (TDO) associated to
\mathcal{A}
is the unique sheaf of algebras
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}
fitting into the following short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow U(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow 0,

where
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{A}}
is the sheaf of two-sided ideals generated by
1_{\mathcal{A}}-1_{U(\mathcal{A})}\in\Gamma(X,U(\mathcal{A}))
.
If
X
is affinoid, then
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
for some
\omega\in\Omega^{2,\operatorname{cl}}_{X/K}(X)
. Thus, we will write
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}:=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}
,

\mathcal{I}_{\omega}:=\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{A}}
, and call
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
the sheaf of
\omega
-twisted differential operators on
X
.

We now show some basic properties of twisted differential operators:

Proposition 2.3.2
.

There is a positive, exhaustive filtration
\Phi_{\bullet}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}
such that there is a canonical isomorphism of sheaves of graded
K
-algebras:

\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\left(\mathcal{T}_{X/K}\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{gr}_{\Phi}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}.

In particular, for every affinoid space
U\subset X
, the filtered algebra
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}(U)
is two-sided noetherian and almost commutative (cf.
[
1
, Definition 3.4]
).

Proof.

This is shown in
[
6
, Lemma 2.1.4]
.
∎

Definition 2.3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}
be a TDO. The filtration
\Phi_{\bullet}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}
constructed in Proposition
2.3.2
is called the filtration by order of differential operators.

For later use, it will be convenient to have an expression of TDO’s in terms of quotients of the enveloping algebra of certain
K
-Lie algebras. Assume
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
is affinoid, and let
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
be an Atiyah algebra on
X
. Consider the
K
-Lie algebra
\overline{\mathcal{A}_{\omega}}=A\oplus\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
, with bracket given by:

[(f_{1},f_{2},v),(g_{1},g_{2},w)]=(v(g_{1})-w(f_{1}),v(g_{2})-w(f_{2})+\omega(v,w),[v,w]),

where
(f_{1},f_{2},v),(g_{1},g_{2},w)\in\overline{\mathcal{A}_{\omega}}=A\oplus A\oplus\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
. Let
U_{K}(\overline{\mathcal{A}_{\omega}})
be the universal enveloping algebra of
\overline{\mathcal{A}_{\omega}}
with respect to its
K
-Lie algebra structure. We then have the following proposition:

Proposition 2.3.4
.

Let
I
be the two-sided ideal in
U_{K}(\overline{\mathcal{A}_{\omega}})
generated by the following elements:

1-(1,0,0),\textnormal{ }1-(0,1,0),\textnormal{ }(f,0,0)(g,h,v)-(fg,fh,fv),

where
f,g,h\in A
and
v\in\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
. Then
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}=U_{K}(\overline{\mathcal{A}_{\omega}})/I
.

Proof.

This follows by the construction of universal enveloping algebras of Lie-Rinehart algebras from
[
46
, Section 2]
, together with Definition
2.3.1
.
∎

Remark 2.3.5
.

If
\mathcal{A}
is the trivial Atiyah algebra, then
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{D}_{X}
.

The sheaf
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}
corresponds to the sheaf of twisted differential operators of finite order, in the sense that
for every open affinoid subspace
U\subset X
, the sections of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}(U)
can be expressed as polynomials of the vector fields in
\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(U)
. The next goal is defining sheaves of infinite order twisted differential operators.

Definition 2.3.6

(
[
3
, Definition 6.1]
)
.

Let
A
be affinoid,
\mathcal{A}\subset A
be an affine formal model, and
L
be a
(K,A)
-Lie algebra. A smooth
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{A})
-Lie lattice of
L
is a smooth
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{A})
-Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
with an isomorphism of
(K,A)
-Lie algebras:

L=\mathcal{L}\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}K.

Back to our previous setting, let
A
be an affinoid
K
-algebra with an affine formal model
\mathcal{A}\subset A
, and let
L
be a smooth
(K,A)
-Lie algebra with a smooth
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{A})
-Lie lattice
\mathcal{L}
. For every
n\geq 0
, the
\mathcal{A}
-module
\pi^{n}\mathcal{L}
is still a smooth
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{A})
-Lie lattice. In particular, we can form the enveloping algebra
U(\pi^{n}\mathcal{L})
. Let
\widehat{U}(\pi^{n}\mathcal{L})
be the
\pi
-adic completion of
U(\pi^{n}\mathcal{L})
. Then
\widehat{U}(\pi^{n}\mathcal{L})
is a
\mathcal{R}
-flat,
\pi
-adically complete, and two sided noetherian
\mathcal{R}
-algebra. Furthermore, the canonical maps:

\widehat{U}(\pi^{n+1}\mathcal{L})_{K}:=\widehat{U}(\pi^{n+1}\mathcal{L})\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}K\rightarrow\widehat{U}(\pi^{n}\mathcal{L})_{K},

are
two sided flat and have dense image (cf.
[
3
, Theorem 6.7]
). Hence, we can form the following Fréchet-Stein algebra:

\wideparen{U}(L)=\varprojlim\widehat{U}(\pi^{n}\mathcal{L})_{K},

which we call the Fréchet-Stein enveloping algebra of
L
. As shown in
[
3
]
, any Atiyah algebra
\mathcal{A}
admits a sheaf of Fréchet-Stein enveloping algebras
\wideparen{U}(\mathcal{A})
, defined on open affinoid subspaces
V=\operatorname{Sp}(A)\subset X
by the rule:

\Gamma(V,\wideparen{U}(\mathcal{A}))=\wideparen{U}(\mathcal{A}(V)).

Furthermore, by the contents of
[
10
]
, for any affinoid subdomain
W=\operatorname{Sp}(B)\subset V
, the following identity holds:

(7)

\wideparen{U}(\mathcal{A}(W))=B\widehat{\otimes}_{A}\wideparen{U}(\mathcal{A}(V))=\wideparen{U}(B\otimes_{A}\mathcal{A}(V)),

where
\widehat{\otimes}_{A}
denotes the completed projective tensor product of locally convex
A
-modules (
cf.

[
52
, Section 1]
). Now, while the algebras of twisted differential operators are not exactly of the form described above, we will still be able to show that they satisfy analogous properties. Let us start with the following theorem:

Theorem 2.3.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth rigid space with an Atiyah algebra
\mathcal{A}
. Consider the following sheaves on
X
:

\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{A}}=\wideparen{U}(\mathcal{A})\otimes_{U(\mathcal{A})}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}},\textnormal{ }\wideparen{I}_{\mathcal{A}}=\wideparen{U}(\mathcal{A})\otimes_{U(\mathcal{A})}\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{A}}.

There is a short exact sequence of co-admissible
\wideparen{U}(\mathcal{A})
-modules:

(8)

0\rightarrow\wideparen{I}_{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow\wideparen{U}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow 0.

Furthermore, the following hold for all affinoid subdomains
V\subset U\subset X
:

(i)

\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{A}}(U)
is a Fréchet-Stein algebra.

(ii)

The canonical map
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}(U)\rightarrow\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{A}}(U)
is faithfully flat with dense image.

(iii)

The map
\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{A}}(U)\rightarrow\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{A}}(V)
is continuous and
c
-flat.

(iv)

The restriction
\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{A}|U}
has trivial higher sheaf cohomology.

(v)

\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{A}}
is a sheaf of complete topological algebras on
X
.

Proof.

We may assume that
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
is affinoid. In particular, it suffices to show that the sequence is exact on global sections. By
[
5
, Corollary 3.2]
the morphism
U(\mathcal{A}(X))\rightarrow\wideparen{U}(\mathcal{A}(X))
is faithfully flat with dense image. Hence,
(\ref{equation ses defining completion of tdo})
is exact and
(ii)
holds. Furthermore, both

\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\wideparen{I}_{\mathcal{A}}
are finitely presented. Thus, statement
(i)
follows by a combination of
[
50
, Lemma 3.6]
and
[
50
, Proposition 3.7]
. In order to see that

\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{A}}(U)\rightarrow\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{A}}(V)
is
c
-flat and continuous, we use the identity

\displaystyle\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{A}}(V)=\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{A}}(U)\wideparen{\otimes}_{\wideparen{U}(\mathcal{A}(U))}\wideparen{U}(\mathcal{A}(V)),

together with the associativity of
\wideparen{\otimes}
from
[
3
, Corollary 7.4]
, and the fact that the transition maps of
\wideparen{U}(\mathcal{A})
are
c
-flat shown in
[
3
, Theorem 7.7]
. Claim
(iv)
is a consequence of
[
3
, Theorem 8.2]
.
∎

For future use, it will be convenient to have a better description of the local sections of the sheaves of infinite order differential operators: Let
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
and assume
\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
is a free
A
-module.
As before, we will write
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}=\mathcal{A}_{\omega}(X)
, and follow the same convention for
\mathcal{I}_{\omega}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
. Let
A^{\circ}\subset A
be the subring of power-bounded elements. As
K
is discretely valued, it follows by
[
26
, Theorem 3.5.6]
that
\mathfrak{X}=\operatorname{Spf}(A^{\circ})

is an affine formal model of
X
. By multiplying by adequate powers of the uniformizer, we may choose an
A
-basis
v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}
of
\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)

satisfying that
v_{i}(A^{\circ})\subset A^{\circ}
for every
1\leq i\leq n
. In this case,
\mathscr{T}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}A^{\circ}v_{i}
is a free
A^{\circ}
-Lie lattice of
\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
. Furthermore, the closed 2-form:

\omega:\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)\times\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)\rightarrow A,

is
A
-linear. Hence, we may assume (maybe after multiplying
\mathscr{T}
by a high enough power of
\pi
) that
\omega(\mathscr{T},\mathscr{T})\subset A^{\circ}
. In this case, the
A^{\circ}
-module:
\mathscr{A}_{\omega}=A^{\circ}\oplus\mathscr{T}
is a free
(\mathcal{R},A^{\circ})
-Lie lattice of
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
, so the inverse limit
\wideparen{U}(\mathcal{A}_{\omega})=\varprojlim_{n}\widehat{U}(\pi_{n}\mathscr{A}_{\omega})_{K}
is a Fréchet-Stein presentation of
\wideparen{U}(\mathcal{A}_{\omega})
(
cf.

[
3
, Theorem 6.4]
).

As the maps
U(\mathcal{A}_{\omega})\rightarrow\widehat{U}(\pi_{n}\mathscr{A}_{\omega})_{K}
are flat for every
n\geq 0
,
we can define:

\widehat{\mathcal{D}}^{n}_{\omega}:=\widehat{U}(\pi_{n}\mathscr{A}_{\omega})_{K}\otimes_{U(\mathcal{A}_{\omega})}\mathcal{D}_{\omega},\quad\widehat{\mathcal{I}}^{n}_{\omega}:=\widehat{U}(\pi_{n}\mathscr{A}_{\omega})_{K}\otimes_{U(\mathcal{A}_{\omega})}\mathcal{I}_{\omega},

and we obtain a family of short exact sequences:

(9)

0\rightarrow\widehat{I}^{n}_{\omega}\rightarrow\widehat{U}(\pi^{n}\mathscr{A}_{\omega})_{K}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{D}}^{n}_{\omega}\rightarrow 0.

Then
\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega}=\varprojlim_{n}\widehat{\mathcal{D}}^{n}_{\omega}
is a Fréchet-Stein presentation of
\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega}
by
[
50
, Proposition 3.7]
.

2.4.
Group actions on twisted differential operators

In order to construct Cherednik algebras on rigid spaces, we need to understand how the action of a finite group on a smooth rigid space
X
extends to an action on the sheaves of twisted differential operators. In this section, we will identify the class of twisted differential operators which are acted on by
G
, give a
G
-equivariant classification of the twisted differential operators, and discuss the skew group algebra construction.

Let
X
be a
G
-variety and recall from Theorem
2.1.1
the existence of the quotient:

\pi:X\rightarrow X/G.

We will now show how the
G
-action on
X
induces a
G
-action on the de Rham complex
\Omega_{X/K}^{\bullet}
: The right
G
-action on
X
induces a left action of sheaves of
K
-algebras on
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Furthermore, we obtain an action of
G
on the tangent sheaf
\mathcal{T}_{X/K}
, by setting
g(v)=g\circ\varphi\circ g^{-1}

for each vector field
v:\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
, and each
g\in G
. More generally, for
\omega\in\Omega_{X/K}^{j}(X)
and
g\in G
, we can regard
\omega
as a
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
-linear map
\omega:\wedge_{i=1}^{j}\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
. We define a left action of
G
on
\Omega_{X/K}^{j}(X)
by the following formula:

(10)

g(\omega)(v_{1},\cdots,v_{j})=g(\omega(g^{-1}(v_{1}),\cdots,g^{-1}(v_{j}))),\textnormal{ for }v_{1},\cdots,v_{j}\in\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X).

A hands-on calculation shows that these actions turn
\pi_{*}\Omega_{X/K}^{\bullet}(X)
into a complex of
K[G]
-modules. The construction of the actions is functorial, so it follows that
\Omega_{X/K}^{\bullet}
is a complex of sheaves of
K
-linear
G
-representations. We will now investigate the fixed points of this action. As usual, we start with the affinoid case:

Lemma 2.4.1
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
be a smooth
G
-variety and let
\omega\in\Omega_{X/K}^{2,\operatorname{cl}}(X)
. The canonical action of
G
on
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
and
\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
extends to an action of
K
-algebras on
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(X)
if and only if
\omega\in\Omega_{X/K}^{2,\operatorname{cl}}(X)^{G}
.

Proof.

Assume that we have an action of
G
on
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(X)
as in the statement. Any
K
-linear action of
G
on
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(X)
which extends the action of
G
on
\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
is an action of filtered rings. Indeed, the filtration by order of differential operators has
\Phi_{0}\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(X)=A
,
\Phi_{1}\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(X)=A+\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
, and the rest of the terms are obtained by taking products of
\Phi_{1}\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(X)
. As any such action would induce a
K
-linear automorphism of the first two terms of the filtration, it follows that it induces automorphisms in the rest of the terms. In particular, as
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}=\Phi_{1}\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(X)
, we get a
G
-action of
K
-vector spaces on
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
. Furthermore, as the Lie bracket in
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
is induced by the commutator in
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(X)
, and the action of
G
is an action of algebras, it follows that
G
acts by automorphisms of
K
-Lie algebras on
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
.

As the
G
-action on
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
extends the action of
G
on
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
and
\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
, it follows that the
G
-action on
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)\oplus\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
is given by the formula:

g(f,v)=(g(f),g(v)),\textnormal{ for }g\in G\textnormal{, and }(f,v)\in\mathcal{A}_{\omega}.

By the definition of the action of
G
on
\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
we have:

g([(f,v),(h,w)])=(g(v)(g(h))-g(w)(g(f))+g(\omega(v,w)),[g(v),g(w)]).

On the other hand, we have:

[g(f,v),g(h,w)]=(g(v)(g(h))-g(w)(g(f))+\omega(g(v),g(w)),[g(v),g(w)]).

Thus, any action of
G
on
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(X)
as above satisfies
g(\omega(v,w))=\omega(g(v),g(w))
for
g\in G
, and
v,w\in\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
. By equation
(\ref{equation action of G on the de Rham complex})
, this is equivalent to
\omega\in\Omega_{X/K}^{2,\operatorname{cl}}(X)^{G}
.

Conversely, for
\omega\in\Omega_{X/K}^{2,cl}(X)^{G}
, and
g\in G
, the formula
g((f,v))=(g(f),g(v))
defines an action of
K
-Lie algebras of
G
on
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
. Hence, we get an induced action of
K
-Lie algebras on
\overline{\mathcal{A}_{\omega}}
, and this lifts to a
G
-action of
K
-algebras on its universal enveloping algebra
U_{K}(\overline{\mathcal{A}_{\omega}})
. Notice that by Proposition
2.3.4
,
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(X)
is the quotient of
U_{K}(\overline{\mathcal{A}_{\omega}})
by a two sided ideal which is invariant under the action of
G
. Thus, we get a
G
-action of filtered
K
-algebras on
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(X)
, as wanted.
∎

We will now extend this result to the Fréchet-Stein completions:

Lemma 2.4.2
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
be an affinoid
G
-variety and choose
\omega\in\Omega_{X/K}^{2,\operatorname{cl}}(X)^{G}
. If
X
satisfies that
\Omega^{1}_{X/K}(X)
has a basis given by differentials of functions, then there is a smooth
(\mathcal{R},A^{\circ})
-Lie lattice
\mathscr{A}_{\omega}
of
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
which is
G
-invariant.

Proof.

Let
df_{1},\cdots df_{n}
be an
A
-basis of
\Omega^{1}_{X/K}(X)
given by differentials of functions in
A^{\circ}
and let
v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}
be the dual basis of
\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
. Set
\mathscr{T}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}A^{\circ}v_{i}
and
\mathscr{A}_{\omega}=A^{\circ}\oplus\mathscr{T}
. As in the discussion after Theorem
2.3.7
, we may assume that
v_{i}(A^{\circ})\subset A^{\circ}
for each
i
and
\omega(\mathscr{T},\mathscr{T})\subset A^{\circ}
. In particular,

\mathscr{A}_{\omega}
is a finite-free
(\mathcal{R},A^{\circ})
-Lie lattice of
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
. We just need to show that it is invariant under the action of
G
.

First, notice that as
G
acts on
A
by continuous maps, the space
A^{\circ}
is invariant under the action of
G
. Next, as the
v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}
are an
A
-basis of
\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
, we have that
g(v_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{ij}v_{j}
for each
i
and each
g\in G
. We are done if we are able to show that the
h_{ij}
are elements of
A^{\circ}
. By definition of a dual basis we have:

(11)

h_{ij}=df_{j}(g(v_{i}))=g(v_{i})(f_{j})=g(v_{i}(g^{-1}(f_{j})).

As
G
induces an isomorphism of
A^{\circ}
, our assumption that the
f_{j}
are power-bounded and
v_{i}(A^{\circ})\subset A^{\circ}
implies that
h_{ij}\in A^{\circ}
for each
i
and
j
, as we wanted to show.
∎

Proposition 2.4.3
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
be a smooth
G
-variety and let
\omega\in\Omega_{X/K}^{2,\operatorname{cl}}(X)
. If
X
satisfies that
\Omega^{1}_{X/K}(X)
has a basis given by differentials of functions, then the canonical action of
G
on
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
and
\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
extends to an action of topological
K
-algebras on
\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega}(X)
if and only if
\omega\in\Omega_{X/K}^{2,\operatorname{cl}}(X)^{G}
.

Proof.

Choose
\omega\in\Omega_{X/K}^{2,\operatorname{cl}}(X)^{G}
, and let
\mathscr{A}_{\omega}
be a finite-free
G
-invariant
(\mathcal{R},A^{\circ})
-Lie lattice of
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}(X)
as in Lemma
2.4.2
.
For each
n\geq 0
, we have an action of
\mathcal{R}
-Lie algebras of
G
on
\pi^{n}\mathscr{A}_{\omega}
. Following the procedure in Lemma
2.4.1
, we get an action of
\mathcal{R}
-algebras on the enveloping algebra
U(\pi^{n}\mathscr{A}_{\omega})
. As every
\mathcal{R}
-linear map is continuous with respect to the
\pi
-adic topology, this action extends uniquely to a continuous action of
K
-algebras of
G
on
\widehat{U}(\pi^{n}\mathscr{A}_{\omega})_{K}
. Furthermore, the transition maps
\widehat{U}(\pi^{n+1}\mathscr{A}_{\omega})_{K}\rightarrow\widehat{U}(\pi^{n}\mathscr{A}_{\omega})_{K}
are clearly
G
-equivariant. Hence, we get a continuous action of
G
on the Fréchet-Stein algebra
\wideparen{U}(\mathcal{A}_{\omega}(X))
. As
G
leaves the ideal
\wideparen{I}_{\omega}
invariant, it follows that the action descends to an action of topological
K
-algebras of
G
on
\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega}(X)
. Furthermore, this action is unique because the map
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(X)\rightarrow\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega}(X)
has dense image. For the converse, assume that the action of
G
extends to
\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega}(X)
. Then we get an action of
G
on
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(X)
, and Lemma
2.4.1
shows that
\omega
must be fixed by
G
.
∎

The next goal is showing that
G
acts on the sheaves
\pi_{*}\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega}
for appropriate
\omega
:

Proposition 2.4.4
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
be a
G
-variety with an étale map
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n}_{K}
and choose
\omega\in\Omega^{2,\operatorname{cl}}_{X/K}(X)
. The actions of
G
on
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
and
\pi_{*}\mathcal{T}_{X/K}
extend to an action of sheaves of topological
K
-algebras on
\pi_{*}\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega}
if and only if
\omega\in\Omega_{X/K}^{2,\operatorname{cl}}(X)^{G}
. If this holds, then for any
G
-invariant subdomain
Y=\operatorname{Sp}(B)\subset X
the map:

\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega}(X)\rightarrow\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega}(Y),

is a
G
-equivariant and
c
-flat morphism of Fréchet-Stein algebras. Furthermore, if
(U_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
is an affinoid cover of
X
, then the associated map:

\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega}(X)\rightarrow\prod_{i=1}^{n}\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega}(U_{i}),

is also
c
-faithfully flat.

Proof.

As
X
is separated, it suffices to define a continuous action of
G
on
\pi_{*}\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{A}}(U)
, where
U
ranges over the affinoid subdomains of
X/G
. By Theorem
2.1.1
, this is equivalent to giving the action on
G
-invariant affinoid subdomains of
X
. Thus, it suffices to show the second part of the proposition. The results on
c
-(faithful) flatness follow from the analogous results for
\wideparen{U}(\mathcal{A}_{\omega})
, which were shown in
[
3
, Theorem 7.7]
. Similarly, it suffices to show that the map
\wideparen{U}(\mathcal{A}_{\omega})(X)\rightarrow\wideparen{U}(\mathcal{A}_{\omega})(Y)
is
G
-equivariant. By Proposition
2.4.3
, the action of
G
on both of these algebras is continuous. Hence, it suffices to see that
U(\mathcal{A}_{\omega}(X))\rightarrow U(\mathcal{A}_{\omega}(Y))
is
G
-equivariant. This is equivalent to
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\omega}(Y)
being
G
-equivariant. However, the restriction maps
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(Y)
, and
\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(Y)
are
G
-equivariant. Thus, the result follows from the description of the
G
-action on

\mathcal{A}_{\omega}(X)
and
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}(Y)
given in the proof of Lemma
2.4.1
.
∎

We now deal with the general case:

Lemma 2.4.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth rigid
G
-variety with an Atiyah algebra
\mathcal{A}
. There is a
G
-action of sheaves of
K
-Lie algebras on
\mathcal{A}
such that the short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow\pi_{*}\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\pi_{*}\mathcal{T}_{X/K}\rightarrow 0,

is
G
-equivariant if and only if
\mathcal{A}
satisfies
[\mathcal{A}]\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(X,(\Omega_{X/K}^{\geq 1})^{G})
.

Proof.

As
X
satisfies
(\operatorname{G-Aff})
, we may choose an admissible cover
\mathfrak{U}
of
X
by
G
-invariant admissible open subspaces. By definition we have:

\Omega_{X/K}^{\geq 1}:=\left(0\rightarrow\Omega_{X/K}^{1}\rightarrow\Omega^{2,\operatorname{cl}}_{X/K}\rightarrow 0\right).

As we have
\operatorname{H}^{i}(U,\Omega_{X/K}^{1})=0
for all
i\geq 1
and all
U\in\mathfrak{U}
, we may use the Čech cohomology spectral sequence associated to the cover
\mathfrak{U}
(
cf.

[
55
, Tag 08BN]
), and it follows that the isomorphism class
[\mathcal{A}]\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(X,\Omega_{X/K}^{\geq 1})
is represented by a cocycle:

(\{\omega_{U}\}_{U\in\mathfrak{U}},\{\delta_{U\cap V}\}_{U,V\in\mathfrak{U}})\in\prod_{U\in\mathfrak{U}}\Omega^{2,\operatorname{cl}}_{X/K}(U)\oplus\prod_{U,V\in\mathfrak{U}}\Omega_{X/K}^{1}(U\cap V).

It follows that
[\mathcal{A}]\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(X,(\Omega_{X/K}^{\geq 1})^{G})
if and only if

\omega_{U}\in\Omega_{X/K}^{2,\operatorname{cl}}(U)^{G}
for all
U\in\mathfrak{U}
, and

\delta_{U\cap V}\in\Omega_{X/K}^{1}(U\cap V)^{G}
for all
U,V\in\mathfrak{U}
. By
Proposition
2.4.4
, for every
U\in\mathfrak{U}
there is a
G
-action of sheaves of
K
-Lie algebras on
\mathcal{A}_{\omega_{U}}
satisfying the conditions we want if and only if
\omega_{U}\in\Omega_{X/K}^{2,\operatorname{cl}}(U)^{G}
for all
U\in\mathfrak{U}
. Similarly, given
U,V\in\mathfrak{U}
, the the maps
\delta_{U\cap V}:\mathcal{A}_{\omega_{U}|U\cap V}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\omega_{V}|U\cap V}
are of the form
\delta_{U,V}((a,v))=(a+\delta_{U,V}(v),v)
. Hence, they are are

-equivariant if and only if for all

we have:
\delta_{U,V}(v)=g^{-1}\delta_{U,V}(g(v))
, which is equivalent to
\delta_{U,V}\in\Omega_{X/K}^{1}(X)^{G}
.
∎

Lemma 2.4.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth rigid
G
-variety with an Atiyah algebra
\mathcal{A}
such that
[\mathcal{A}]\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(X,\Omega_{X/K}^{\geq 1})^{G}

-action of sheaves of complete topological

Proof.

Follows at once from Proposition

.

Next, we give a
G
-equivariant version of

:

Definition 2.4.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth
G
-variety. We define the category of

-equivariant Atiyah algebras

\mathscr{PA}(X)^{G}
as follows:

(i)

such that

(ii)

Morphisms are defined as follows: Let

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{PA}(X)^{G}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}),

as space of morphisms
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
in
\mathscr{PA}(X)
such that the induced map:

-Lie algebras.

We can now obtain a
G
-equivariant classification of the Atiyah algebras on

:

Theorem 2.4.8
.

Let
X
be a smooth
G
-variety. The following hold:

(i)

\mathscr{PA}(X)^{G}
is an essentially small groupoid.

(ii)

\operatorname{Iso}\left(\mathscr{PA}(X)^{G}\right)=\mathbb{H}^{1}(X,(\Omega_{X/K}^{\geq 1})^{G})=\mathbb{H}^{1}(X,\Omega_{X/K}^{\geq 1})^{G}
.

(iii)

For
\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{PA}(X)^{G}
we have
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathscr{PA}(X)^{G}}(\mathcal{A})=\Omega_{X/K}^{1,\operatorname{cl}}(X)^{G}
.

Proof.

We will first show claim
(ii)
. First, notice that the functor
(-)^{G}
is exact. Indeed, as
\operatorname{Char}(K)=0

and
G
is finite, it follows from
[
27
, Corollary 1.6]
that
(-)^{G}
is exact on finite-dimensional representations. As every
K
-linear
G
-representation is a filtered colimit of finite-dimensional
G
-representations, and
(-)^{G}
commutes with filtered colimits, it follows that
(-)^{G}
is exact. Hence, we obtain the following:

\mathbb{H}^{1}(X,(\Omega_{X/K}^{\geq 1})^{G})=\mathbb{H}^{1}(X,\Omega_{X/K}^{\geq 1})^{G}.

As both
\operatorname{Iso}\left(\mathscr{PA}(X)^{G}\right)
and
\mathbb{H}^{1}(X,(\Omega_{X/K}^{\geq 1})^{G})
can be calculated using a cover, we may assume that
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
and admits an étale map
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n}_{K}
. Thus, we have:

\mathbb{H}^{1}(X,\Omega_{X/K}^{\geq 1})^{G}=\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{dR}}^{2}(X)^{G}=\Omega_{X/K}^{2,\operatorname{cl}}(X)^{G}/d(\Omega_{X/K}^{1}(X)^{G}).

By definition, objects in
\mathscr{PA}(X)^{G}
are of the form
\pi_{*}\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
for some
\omega\in\Omega_{X/K}^{2,\operatorname{cl}}(X)^{G}
. By
Proposition
2.2.5
, two Atiyah algebras
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
,
\mathcal{A}_{\xi}
are isomorphic in
\mathscr{PA}(X)
if and only if we have:
\omega-\xi=d\eta
, for some
\eta\in\Omega_{X/K}^{1}(X)
. Thus, we need to see that the isomorphism

\varphi_{\eta}:\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\xi}
is
G
-equivariant if and only if
\eta\in\Omega_{X/K}^{1}(X)^{G}
. However, this was shown in Lemma
2.4.5
. Thus statement
(ii)
holds, and an analogous argument can be applied to show

. The fact that
\mathscr{PA}(X)^{G}
is a groupoid stems from the fact that
\mathscr{PA}(X)
is a groupoid.
∎

Let us point out that, as a consequence of the discussion above, every object

in

has attached a sheaf of complete topological algebras

with an action of

, and that every map

-equivariant morphism of sheaves of topological algebras

. Additionally, we remark that, if the action of

on

is free, then the quotient map

is étale, and

is a smooth rigid space. In this case, the classification obtained in Theorem

agrees with the classification of Atiyah algebras on

. Let us now introduce the skew group algebra construction:

Definition 2.4.9
.

Let
A
be a ring with an action of
G
, and let
G\ltimes A:=\bigoplus_{g\in G}A
. We express a family
\{a_{g}\}_{g\in G}\in G\ltimes A
as
\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}g
. We can endow

with a unital associative algebra structure by setting:

(\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}g)\cdot(\sum_{h\in G}b_{h}h)=\sum_{g,h\in G}a_{g}g(b_{h})gh.

We call
G\ltimes A
the skew group algebra of
G
and
A
.

We will now extend this to the algebras
\pi_{*}\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{A}}
defined above.

Lemma 2.4.10
.

Let
f:A\rightarrow B
be
G
-equivariant morphism of algebras. If
f
is (faithfully-)flat then the induced map
G\ltimes A\rightarrow G\ltimes B
is (faithfully-)flat.

Proof.

Follows from the identity
G\ltimes B=B\otimes_{A}(G\ltimes A)
.
∎

We can use this machinery to complete our description of the algebras
G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega}(X)
:

Proposition 2.4.11
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
be a smooth
G
-variety and
\omega\in\Omega^{2,\operatorname{cl}}_{X/K}(X)^{G}
.
Let
\mathscr{A}_{\omega}
be a

-invariant

-Lie lattice of

, and set:

Then

admits a two-sided Fréchet-Stein presentation:

G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega}(X)=\varprojlim_{n}G\ltimes\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega}^{n}.

Proof.

By equation
(\ref{equation FS presentation of completed TDO})
, we have that
\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega}(X)=\varprojlim_{n}\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega}^{n}
is a Fréchet-Stein presentation. Furthermore, by the proof of Proposition

we have the following two-sided Fréchet-Stein presentation:

∎

2.5.
Extension to the étale site

Given that Cherednik algebras have been successfully employed to study quotient singularities and algebraic fundamental groups of quotient schemes, we feel it is beneficial to define these objects at the étale level. For this reason, we finish the section on twisted differential operators by extending Atiyah algebras and TDO to the étale site of
X
. In particular, we show that any Atiyah algebra
\mathcal{A}
on
X
extends canonically to a sheaf of Atiyah algebras on
X_{\textnormal{ét}}
. This induces an extension of the sheaf of twisted differential operators
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}
to
X_{\textnormal{ét}}
. Furthermore, if
[\mathcal{A}]\in\operatorname{H}^{2}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X)^{G}
, then we will see that
G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}
extends uniquely to a sheaf on
X/G_{\textnormal{ét}}
. Let us start by recalling the definition of the small étale site:

Definition 2.5.1

(
[

Proposition 2.5.2

3.
Cherednik algebras on rigid analytic spaces

We will now use the material of the previous sections to construct sheaves of Cherednik algebras on a smooth
G

. We will start by constructing Cherednik algebras in the case where

3.1.
Cherednik algebras on rigid analytic spaces

In this section, we will translate the classical theory of Cherednik algebras from
[

]
to the rigid analytic setting. It is important to point out that, although all results in

Remark 3.1.1
.

From now on and until the end of the section, we will only consider smooth rigid

-varieties satisfying
(\operatorname{G-Aff})
(cf. Theorem

Definition 3.1.2

Let

(i)

The action of

h(Y,g)=(h(Y),h^{-1}gh)\textnormal{ for }h\in G\textnormal{ and }(Y,g)\in S(X,G).

A reflection function on

Assume

(1)

(2)

(3)

In particular, let us make the following definition:

(i)

\overline{X}=X\setminus\bigcup_{(Y,g)\in S(X,G)}Y

(ii)

Remark 3.1.4
.

Notice that for any
\lambda\in K^{*}
We have

A priory, the Cherednik algebra

, and

:

Lemma 3.1.5
.

The following hold:

(i)

\overline{X}
is a
G
-invariant Zariski open subspace of
X
.

(ii)

does not depend on the choice of

Proof.

Let
Z
be the union of all reflection hypersurfaces. As this is a

\overline{X}=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}X\left(\frac{\pi^{n}}{f_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\pi^{n}}{f_{r}}\right)

-invariant affinoid subdomains

meeting all connected components of

and

\mathcal{O}_{X}(\overline{X}_{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(U),\textnormal{ }\mathcal{O}_{X}(\overline{X}_{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(V),

But then, Proposition

implies that the maps

Remark 3.1.6
.

For the rest of the section, we fix a
G
-invariant affinoid subdomain

which meets all connected components of
X
.

Notice that definition

does not yield a single element, but a family of them. Namely, the different choices of representative of

will give rise to different Dunkl-Opdam operators associated to the same derivation

. This does not produce any ambiguity, as our definition of Cherednik algebra does not depend on the choice of representatives.

Lemma 3.1.7
.

The Cherednik algebra
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
is independent on the choices of representatives of the Dunkl-Opdam operators.

Proof.

Consider a derivation
v\in\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
, and let
D_{v},D_{v}^{\prime}
be two Dunkl-Opdam operators associated to it. Then we have:

D_{v}-\overline{D}_{v}=\sum_{(Y,g)\in S(X,G)}\frac{2c(Y,g)}{1-\lambda_{Y,g}}h_{Y}(g-1).

where
h_{Y}=\overline{\xi_{Y}}(v)-\overline{\xi_{Y}}(v)^{\prime}
. As
\overline{\xi_{Y}}(v)
and
\overline{\xi_{Y}}(v)^{\prime}
are representatives in
\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z)
of
\xi_{Y}(v)
, it follows that
h_{Y}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
(cf. Proposition
2.1.11
). The eigenvalues
\lambda_{Y,g}
are non-trivial units in
K
. Hence, it follows that
D_{v}-\overline{D}_{v}
is contained in
G\ltimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)\subset G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{\omega/t}(U)
. Thus,
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
contains all the differences
D_{v}-\overline{D}_{v}
, and therefore it contains all choices of Dunkl-Opdam operators.
∎

Remark 3.1.8
.

In general, we do not have a canonical choice of Dunkl-Opdam operators. However, in some special situations there is a natural way of making such a choice. Namely, assume that every
(Y,g)\in S(X,G)
is the zero locus of a rigid function

\xi_{Y}(v)=[\frac{v(f_{Y})}{f_{Y}}]
. In this case, for every

and these provide representatives for each family of Dunkl-Opdam operators.

Now that we have defined Cherednik algebras, our next step will be showing some of their basic properties. We start by the following very useful relations:

Proposition 3.1.9
.

The following commutation relations hold in
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G):

(i)

D_{v}g=gD_{g^{-1}(v)}
for
g\in G
, and
v\in\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)

(ii)

D_{fv+hw}=fD_{v}+hD_{w}
for
f,h\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
, and
v,w\in\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
.

(iii)

Consider

and a Dunkl-Opdam operator

. Then we have:

[D_{v},f]=tv(f)+\sum_{(Y,g)\in S(X,G)}\frac{2c(Y,g)}{1-\lambda_{Y,g}}\overline{\xi_{Y}}(v)(g(f)-f)g.

In particular,
[D_{v},f]\in G\ltimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
.

Proof.

We only show

. We have the following identities inside

:

D_{v}f=t\mathbb{L}_{v}f+\sum_{(Y,g)\in S(X,G)}\frac{2c(Y,g)}{1-\lambda_{Y,g}}\overline{\xi_{Y}}(v)(g-1)f\\
=t\mathbb{L}_{fv}+tv(f)+\sum_{(Y,g)\in S(X,G)}\frac{2c(Y,g)}{1-\lambda_{Y,g}}\overline{\xi_{Y}}(v)(gf-f).

We can simplify the last term by considering the following calculation:

\displaystyle\sum_{(Y,g)\in S(X,G)}\frac{2c(Y,g)}{1-\lambda_{Y,g}}\overline{\xi_{Y}}(v)(gf-f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(Y,g)\in S(X,G)}\frac{2c(Y,g)}{1-\lambda_{Y,g}}\overline{\xi_{Y}}(v)(g(f)g-fg+fg-f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(Y,g)\in S(X,G)}\frac{2c(Y,g)}{1-\lambda_{Y,g}}\overline{\xi_{Y}}(v)(g(f)-f)g+f\sum_{(Y,g)\in S(X,G)}\frac{2c(Y,g)}{1-\lambda_{Y,g}}\overline{\xi_{Y}}(v)(g-1).

Combining both expressions and rearranging the terms, we have shown that:

[D_{v},f]=tv(f)+\sum_{(Y,g)\in S(X,G)}\frac{2c(Y,g)}{1-\lambda_{Y,g}}\overline{\xi_{Y}}(v)(g(f)-f)g.

We need to show that
[D_{v},f]\in G\ltimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
. It suffices to show that for each

, we have

. This can be checked locally, so we may assume that

is the zero locus of some

, it suffices to show that

divides

for any

. This holds because

.
∎

(ii)

3.2.
The presheaf of Cherednik algebras

The definition of Cherednik algebras depends on three parameters: A unit
t\in K^{*}
, a reflection function
c\in\operatorname{Ref}(X,G)
, and a
G
-invariant closed
2
-form
\omega\in\Omega_{X/K}^{2,\operatorname{cl}}(X)^{G}
. In order to construct a sheaf of Cherednik algebras on

, we need to understand how this choice of parameters determines a unique Cherednik algebra on every affinoid object in
X/G_{\textnormal{ét}}
. Thus, we will start the section by showing that there is a sheaf
\mathscr{R}(-,G):X/G_{\textnormal{ét}}^{\operatorname{op}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Vect}_{K}
satisfying that for any étale map
Y\rightarrow X/G
we have:

\mathscr{R}(Y,G)=\operatorname{Ref}(Y\times_{X/G}X,G).

This result, together with the extension of Atiyah algebras to the étale site obtained in Section

, will allow us to build presheaves of Cherednik algebras on

.

Let us start the section with the following technical lemmas:

Lemma 3.2.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth rigid
G
-variety, with an étale map

, and let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

Then

Assume

W(T)=V(T)\times_{X/G(T)}X(T).

By construction, for any
g\in G

is given by
(v,x)g=(v,xg)
.
Let

, then we must have

. Hence,
(v,x)\in W^{g}(T)
.
Thus, we have
Z\subset W^{g}
. Therefore, in order to show that
Z
is a disjoint union of reflection hypersurfaces, we only need to show that each of the connected components of
Z
is a hypersurface of
W

be one of these connected components. Then the composition

is étale. By Proposition

is smooth. Hence, both

and

Lemma 3.2.2

Let

3.3.
The PBW Theorem for Cherednik algebras

The main goal of this section is showing that the PBW Theorem holds for Cherednik algebras on small enough smooth affinoid spaces. From now and until the end of the section, let
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
be a
G
-variety with an étale map
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n}_{K}
. We fix a
G
-stable open affinoid subspace

meeting all connected components of

, a unit

, a
G
-invariant closed

, and a reflection function

Theorem 3.3.1

( PBW Theorem for affinoid Cherednik algebras)
.

The Dunkl-Opdam filtration induces a canonical isomorphism of

algebras:

\Psi:\operatorname{gr}_{F}H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X).

Proof.

The proof is analogous to

.
∎

Remark 3.3.2

The morphism

does not depend on the choice of

.

This theorem allows us to deduce important structural results for
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
.

Corollary 3.3.3
.

Let

be a basis of
\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
as an
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
-module
and
D_{v_{1}},\cdots,D_{v_{n}}
be a choice of Dunkl-Opdam operators associated to it. The Cherednik algebra

H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
is a free

-module, with a basis given by:

D_{v_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots D_{v_{n}}^{\alpha_{n}},

where the

are non-negative integers .

Corollary 3.3.4
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
be a
G
-variety with an étale map

. Every

(ii)

there is an isomorphism of filtered algebras:

has trivial higher Čech cohomology groups on

Proof.

As

implies statement

. By Corollary

, the algebra

is a free module over

. Hence, statement
(ii)
also implies

. Thus, we only need to show that for every affinoid étale map

and

there is an identification of filtered algebras:

By Proposition
3.2.9
, we have a morphism of

\psi:\mathcal{O}_{W}(W)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)}H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow H_{t,c,\omega}(W,G).

a morphism of filtered
\mathcal{O}_{W}(W)
-modules. By Theorem

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{W}(W)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)}\operatorname{gr}_{F}H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{W}(W)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)}\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{T}_{W/K}(W))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{gr}_{F}H_{t,c,\omega}(W,G).

∎

As the categories of sheaves on

to a sheaf of filtered algebras on

-varieties:

W\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n}_{K}
we have:

H_{t,c,\omega,X,G}(V)=H_{t,c,\omega}(W,G).

4.
p-adic Cherednik algebras

In this chapter we will construct sheaves of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras on a smooth
G
-variety
X
. Locally, these arise as Fréchet-Stein completions of the Cherednik algebras defined in the previous chapter. In order to solve some of the technical issues that appear in this construction, we give a (very short) introduction to adic spaces, focusing on the specialization map and its relation with the Shilov boundary of a rigid analytic variety. We then use these results to give a local definition of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras, and show that they are indeed Fréchet-Stein algebras. Finally, we construct sheaves of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras on
X/G_{\textnormal{ét}}
, and show that their higher Čech cohomology groups vanish. We finish the chapter by studying the
c
-flatness of the transition maps of the sheaf of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras, and giving some applications of this analysis to the theory of co-admissible modules over a sheaf of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras.

4.1.
Restriction algebras

In this section, we introduce some technical machinery that will be indispensable for the theory of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras. In particular, we will define the restriction algebras, the Shilov boundary of an adic affinoid space, and show the connection between these two concepts. Along the way, we give a small recap on the specialization map and its relation with the Shilov boundary. We start by making the following definition:

Definition 4.1.1
.

Let
f:A\rightarrow B
be a morphism of affinoid
K
-algebras. The restriction algebra of
f
is the following
\mathcal{R}
-algebra:

A^{\circ}_{f:A\rightarrow B}=f^{-1}(B^{\circ})=\{a\in A\textnormal{ }|\textnormal{ }f(a)\in B^{\circ}\}.

If
f:A\rightarrow B
induces an open immersion
\operatorname{Sp}(B)\rightarrow\operatorname{Sp}(A)
, we write
A^{\circ}_{B}:=A^{\circ}_{f:A\rightarrow B}
.

Restriction algebras associated to open immersions will be instrumental in the theory of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras. This section is devoted to solving some technical issues regarding these algebras. In particular,
for an open immersion
\operatorname{Sp}(B)\rightarrow\operatorname{Sp}(A)
,
we will give conditions which assure that
A^{\circ}_{B}=A^{\circ}
. To do this, we will need to make use of the theory of adic spaces. As the material we need is rather standard, we will not introduce these spaces here. The reader can consult
[
36
]
, and

[
53
]
, for a thorough introduction to Huber’s theory of adic spaces.

Definition 4.1.2
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Spa}(A)
be an affinoid space. The Shilov boundary of
X
(or of
A
) is the unique minimal subset of rank one points
S(X)\subset X

such that for every function
f\in A
we have:

\underset{x\in S(X)}{\textnormal{max}}\{v_{x}(f)\}=\underset{x\in X,\textnormal{rank}(v_{x})=1}{\textnormal{max}}\{v_{x}(f)\}.

The points
x\in S(X)
are called Shilov points.

Notice that this notion makes sense, as every valuation of rank one has image contained in the totally ordered multiplicative abelian monoid
(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\cdot)
(
cf.

[
53
, Proposition 1.14.]
). The Shilov boundary can be defined for arbitrary Banach
K
-algebras. However, it is in general not well-behaved, and its not even clear it exists (due to the minimality and uniqueness requirements). However, we have the following result due to V. Berkovich:

Proposition 4.1.3

(
[
7
, Corollary 2.4.5]
)
.

Let
A
be an affinoid
K
-algebra, and let
X=\operatorname{Spa}(A,A^{\circ})
. Then the Shilov boundary
S(X)
exists and it is finite.

In order to make use of this notion, we will first recall the basic properties of the specialization map. For a complete treatment of the specialization map one can check Section 2.4 in
[
7
]
or
[
40
]
. Let
A^{\circ}\subset A
be the algebra of power-bounded elements of
A
, and
A^{\circ\circ}\subset A^{\circ}
be the ideal of topologically nilpotent elements. We define
\mathfrak{X}=\operatorname{Spec}(A^{\circ}/A^{\circ\circ})
. Notice that
\mathfrak{X}
is a finite type
k
-variety which agrees with the reduction of
\operatorname{Spec}(A^{\circ}/\pi)
. The specialization map:

\textnormal{sp}:X\rightarrow\mathfrak{X},

is defined as follows: Every point
x\in X
is given by a continuous valuation
v_{x}
in
A
, and has associated residue field
(K(x),K(x)^{+})
. We remark that if the valuation
v_{x}
has rank one, then it can be shown that
K_{x}^{+}
agrees with the ring of integers of
K_{x}
.

Definition 4.1.4
.

Let
x\in X
be an adic point, and
\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{+}
be the maximal ideal of
K(x)^{+}
. The secondary residue field of
X
at
x
is the field:
\tilde{K}_{x}=K_{x}^{+}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{+}
.

For every
x\in X
there is a morphism

(A,A^{\circ})\rightarrow(K_{x},K^{+}_{x})
, which induces a map:

A^{\circ}\rightarrow K^{+}_{x}\rightarrow\tilde{K}_{x}.

By continuity,
topologically nilpotent elements are mapped to topologically nilpotent elements. Therefore, we get a map:

A^{\circ}/A^{\circ\circ}\rightarrow\tilde{K}_{x}.

Equivalently, we have a map of schemes
\operatorname{Spec}(\tilde{K}_{x})\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}
. We define
\textnormal{sp}(x)\in\mathfrak{X}
to be the image of this map. We notice that this construction can be performed in more general adic spaces, and that the specialization map is continuous, spectral and closed. The idea now is to use the specialization map to obtain characterization of the Shilov boundary of
X
. In particular, we have:

Proposition 4.1.5

(
[
9
, Proposition 2.2]
)
.

Fix an affinoid
K
-algebra
A
and a rank one point
x\in X=\operatorname{Spa}(A,A^{\circ})
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

\textnormal{sp}(x)
is the generic point of an irreducible component of
\mathfrak{X}
.

(ii)

\{x\}=\textnormal{sp}^{-1}(\textnormal{sp}(x))
.

(iii)

Let
\nu=\textnormal{sp}(x)\in\mathfrak{X}\subset\operatorname{Spec}(A^{\circ})
. There is an identity of topological rings:

K^{+}_{x}=\widehat{A^{\circ}_{\nu}}=\varprojlim_{n}\widehat{A^{\circ}_{\nu}}/\pi^{n},

where
A^{\circ}_{\nu}
is localization at the prime ideal
\nu
.

(iv)

The valuation
v_{x}
is in the Shilov boundary
S(X)
.

The specific construction of the residue field
K_{x}
allows us to give a very straightforward characterization of the functions that are in the support of a Shilov point.

Lemma 4.1.6
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Spa}(A,A^{\circ})
be affinoid,
x\in S(X)
, and
f\in A
. Then
v_{x}(f)=0
if and only if there is some open subspace
U
satisfying
x\in U\subset\mathbb{V}(f)
.

Proof.

Let
x\in S(X)
be a Shilov point and let
\nu=\operatorname{sp}(x)\in\mathfrak{X}\subset\operatorname{Spec}(A^{\circ})
be the prime ideal representing its image under the specialization map. After multiplying by a power of the uniformizer, we may assume that
f\in A^{\circ}
. Assume that
v_{x}(f)=0
. By Proposition
4.1.5
, the map
A^{\circ}\rightarrow K_{x}
is given by the following composition:

A^{\circ}\rightarrow A^{\circ}_{\nu}\rightarrow\widehat{A^{\circ}_{\nu}}\rightarrow\widehat{A^{\circ}_{\nu}}[\pi^{-1}]=K_{x},

where the first map is a localization, and the second map is a
\pi
-adic completion. As the valuation on
K
is discrete, the ring
A^{\circ}
is noetherian. In particular, the map
A^{\circ}_{\nu}\rightarrow\widehat{A^{\circ}_{\nu}}
corresponds to a completion of a noetherian local ring, and therefore is injective. Furthermore, as
A^{\circ}
is an admissible
\mathcal{R}
algebra it does not have
\pi
-torsion. Hence, the algebra
\widehat{A^{\circ}_{\nu}}
does not have
\pi
-torsion. Thus, the map
\widehat{A^{\circ}_{\nu}}\rightarrow K_{x}
is also injective. By assumption we have
v_{x}(f)=0
, so the image of
f
under the map
A^{\circ}\rightarrow K_{x}
is zero. It follows that
f
is in the kernel of the localization map
A^{\circ}\rightarrow A^{\circ}_{\nu}
. We have an identity:

A^{\circ}_{\nu}=\varinjlim_{\nu\in D(g)}A^{\circ}[g^{-1}].

As this is a filtered colimit, it follows that there is an element
g\in A^{\circ}
such that
f
is in the kernel of
A^{\circ}\rightarrow A^{\circ}[g^{-1}]
. As
\nu\in D(g)
, it follows that the intersection of
D(g)
with the special fiber:

\operatorname{Spec}(A^{\circ}/\pi)=\operatorname{Spec}(k)\times_{\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{R})}\operatorname{Spec}(A^{\circ}),

is not empty. Hence, it makes sense to consider the completed localization:

A^{\circ}\rightarrow A^{\circ}[g^{-1}]\rightarrow A^{\circ}\langle g^{-1}\rangle,

and
f
is mapped to zero under this completed localization. Taking the rigid generic fiber of this yields a map
A\rightarrow A\langle g^{-1}\rangle
, which corresponds to the inclusion of a Laurent subdomain. As
f
is in the kernel of this map, we have
X(\frac{1}{g})\subset V(f)
.
∎

Lemma 4.1.7
.

Consider affinoid spaces
X=\operatorname{Spa}(A,A^{\circ})
,
Y=\operatorname{Spa}(B,B^{\circ})
, with a morphism
f:Y\rightarrow X
, which is induced by a morphism of affinoid
K
-algebras
f:A\rightarrow B
.
If
S(X)\subset f(Y)
, then
A^{\circ}_{f:A\rightarrow B}=A^{\circ}
as topological rings.

Proof.

By definition,
the image of any
g\in A^{\circ}_{f:A\rightarrow B}
is power-bounded in
Y
. Thus, for every rank one point
y\in Y
we have
v_{y}(g)\leq 1
. By assumption, we have
S(X)\subset f(Y)
. Hence, by definition of
S(X)
, we have
v_{x}(g)\leq 1
for every rank one point
x\in X
. As this includes all classical points, this is equivalent to
g\in A^{\circ}
.
∎

Our focus will be on the following situation: Let
X=\operatorname{Spa}(A,A^{\circ})
be an affinoid space with a nowhere-dense closed analytic subspace
Z\subset X
given by an ideal:

\mathcal{I}_{Z}=f_{1}A+\cdots+f_{r}A.

Let
\overline{X}=X\setminus Z
be the complement of
Z
. We have a family of rational subdomains:

\overline{X}_{n}=X\left(\frac{\pi^{n}}{f_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\pi^{n}}{f_{r}}\right)=\operatorname{Spa}(B_{n},B_{n}^{\circ})\subset\overline{X},

and we have
\overline{X}=\bigcup_{n}\overline{X}_{n}
. In this setting, we have the following proposition:

Proposition 4.1.8
.

For sufficiently high
n\geq 0
we have
S(X)\subset\overline{X}_{n}

Proof.

As the Shilov boundary of
X
is a finite set and
\overline{X}_{n}
is an increasing family, it is enough to show that
S(X)\subset\overline{X}
. Choose a Shilov boundary point
x\in X
, with valuation
v_{x}
. We have
x\in Z
if and only if
v_{x}(f_{i})=0
for all
1\leq i\leq r
. If this holds, then by Lemma
4.1.6
for each
i
there is an open subspace
x\in U_{i}\subset\mathbb{V}(f_{i})
. But then we have
x\in U=\cap_{i=1}^{r}U_{i}\subset Z
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Remark 4.1.9
.

Notice that any open subspace
U\subset X
which contains the Shilov boundary of
X
has non-empty intersection with all connected components of
X
.

4.2.
Local definition of p-adic Cherednik algebras

We will now use the theory developed in previous sections to define
p
-adic Cherednik algebras. These algebras, denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
, play the same role in the setting of Cherednik algebras on rigid analytic spaces as the algebras of
p
-adic differential operators
\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega}

do in the setting of
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
-modules. In particular, most of the good properties of algebras of
p
-adic differential operators are still present in
p
-adic Cherednik algebras. For the rest of this section, we assume that
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
is a
G
-variety with an étale map
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{r}_{K}
. As before, we fix a unit
t\in K^{*}
, a reflection function
x\in\textnormal{Ref}(X,G)
and a closed and
G
-invariant
2
-form
\omega\in\Omega_{X/K}^{2,\operatorname{cl}}(X)^{G}
. In particular, by Lemma
2.4.2
, there are
G
-invariant
(\mathcal{R},A^{\circ})
-Lie lattices for
\mathcal{A}_{\omega/t}(X)
, and for any such Lie lattice
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
, we get a Fréchet-Stein presentation of
G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega/t}(X)
. Indeed, let
\mathcal{I}_{n}
be the two-sided ideal in
U(\pi^{n}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t})

generated by

. Then we define:

\mathcal{D}(\pi^{n}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t})=U(\pi^{n}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t})/\mathcal{I}_{n},

and we have a Fréchet-Stein presentation:

G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega/t}(X)=\varprojlim_{n}G\ltimes\widehat{\mathcal{D}}(\pi^{n}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t})_{K}.

The idea is obtaining a completion of the Cherednik algebra
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
using a similar process. However,
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
is not the skew group algebra of an enveloping algebra, nor a quotient of such an algebra. Thus, the techniques we have been using so far are not available to us. Nevertheless, as shown in Lemma
3.1.5
, if
U\subset\overline{X}
is a
G
-invariant affinoid subdomain which meets all connected components of
X
, there is a canonical injection:

H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{\omega/t}(U).

The idea is defining the completion of
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
as its closure in
G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega/t}(U)
.

Definition 4.2.1

(
[
3
, Definition 3.1]
)
.

Let
\varphi:A\rightarrow B
be an étale morphism of affinoid algebras, with affine formal models
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
. Let
\mathcal{L}
be a
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{L})
-Lie algebra. We say
\mathcal{B}
is
\mathcal{L}
-stable if
\varphi(\mathcal{A})\subset\mathcal{B}

(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{A})
-Lie algebra structure on
\mathcal{L}
extends to a
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{B})
-Lie algebra structure on
\mathcal{B}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{L}
.

We remark that if the previous extension exists, it is unique. Furthermore, there is an explicit criterion of existence of such extensions. Indeed, consider the anchor map
\rho:\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\operatorname{Der}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{A})
. This induces a
B
-linear map of
K
-Lie algebras:

\rho_{B}:B\otimes_{A}(K\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{L})\rightarrow B\otimes_{A}(K\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\operatorname{Der}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{A}))\cong B\otimes_{A}\operatorname{Der}_{K}(A)\cong\operatorname{Der}_{K}(B),

By
[
3

, the
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{A})
-Lie algebra structure on
\mathcal{L}
extends to a
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{B})
-Lie algebra structure on
\mathcal{B}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{L}
if and only if the following holds:

(14)

\rho_{B}(\mathcal{B}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{L})\subset\operatorname{Der}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{B}).

Consider the following definition:

Definition 4.2.2
.

Let
U=\operatorname{Sp}(B)\subset\overline{X}
be a
G
-stable affinoid subdomain meeting all connected components of
X
, and let
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
be a finite-free
G
-invariant
(\mathcal{R},A^{\circ})
-Lie lattice of
\mathcal{A}_{\omega/t}(X)

\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}
be the image of
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
under
\mathcal{A}_{\omega/t}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
. We say
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
is a
U
-Cherednik lattice if it satisfies the following:

(i)

B^{\circ}
is
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
-stable, and we let
\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t}=B^{\circ}\otimes_{A^{\circ}}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
.

(ii)

For each

there is a Dunkl-Opdam operator
D_{v/t}\in G\ltimes\mathcal{D}(\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t})
.

Let us make a few comments on the definition:

Remark 4.2.3
.

Let
U\subset\overline{X}
be a
G
-invariant affinoid subdomain meeting all connected components, and let
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
be a
U
-Cherednik lattice. The following hold:

(i)

\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}

(ii)

.

(iii)

Let
\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t}}=B^{\circ}\otimes_{A^{\circ}}\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}

\omega/t(\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t}},\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t}})\subset B^{\circ}

For the future developments, it will be convenient to have a good understanding of Cherednik lattices. Let us start by recalling a rather important technical lemma:

Lemma 4.2.4

(
[
3

Let

(K,A)
-Lie algebra

(\mathcal{R},A^{\circ})
-Lie lattice
\mathcal{L}

This result is a consequence of the condition for the existence of extensions given in equation (

Proposition 4.2.5

For
1\leq i\leq m

U_{i}\subset\overline{X}

G
-invariant affinoid subdomains meeting all connected components of

Let
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}

for all

for all

We will now show some basic properties of these algebras:

Lemma 4.2.7
.

Let
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}

(ii)

There is an injection of

Proposition 4.2.8
.

Let
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
be a

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}\rightarrow G\ltimes\widehat{\mathcal{D}}(\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t})_{K}.

Proof.

By construction we have a monomorphism of
K
-algebras

H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}\rightarrow G\ltimes\mathcal{D}(\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t}).

Thus, it suffices to show that
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}
is saturated in
G\ltimes\mathcal{D}(\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t})
. That is, we need to show that for all
f\in G\ltimes\mathcal{D}(\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t})
, if there is some
n\geq 0
such that
\pi^{n}f\in H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}
, then
f\in H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}
. Choose
f\in G\ltimes\mathcal{D}(\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t})
such that
\pi^{n}f\in H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}
for high enough
n\geq 0
. Then we have
f\in H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
. Therefore, we have:

f\in H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}=G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{\omega/t}(\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t})\cap H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G).

Thus,
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}
is saturated in
G\ltimes\mathcal{D}(\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t})
, and the claim holds.
∎

For each
m\geq 0
we have the following commutative diagram:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\pi^{m+1}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\ltimes\widehat{\mathcal{D}}(\pi^{m+1}\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t})_{K}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\pi^{m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\ltimes\widehat{\mathcal{D}}(\pi^{m}\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t})_{K}}

where horizontal maps are injective. This leads us to the following definition:

Definition 4.2.9
.

Let
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
be a
U
-Cherednik lattice.
The
U
-Cherednik algebra of the
G
-variety
X
(relative to our choice of
t
,
c
,
\omega
) is the following inverse limit:

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U}=\varprojlim_{m}\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\pi^{m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}.

We regard
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U}
as a topological
K
-algebra with the inverse limit topology.

We need to show this is well-defined. That is, that it does not depend on
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
. Chose another
U
-Cherednik lattice
\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}_{\omega/t}
. As both
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
and
\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}_{\omega/t}
are finite-free
(\mathcal{R},A^{\circ})
-Lie lattices of
\mathcal{A}_{\omega/t}
,
there are
s_{1},s_{2}\geq 0
such that for
m\geq 0
we have:

\pi^{s_{2}+m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}\subset\pi^{s_{1}+m}\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}_{\omega/t}\subset\pi^{m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}.

Applying
B^{\circ}\otimes_{A^{\circ}}-
to this sequence, we get:

(15)

\pi^{s_{2}+m}\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t}\rightarrow\pi^{s_{1}+m}\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}_{\omega/t}\rightarrow\pi^{m}\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t}.

By definition of a
U
-Cherednik lattice, we have:

K\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\pi^{m}\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t}=B\otimes_{A}\mathcal{A}_{\omega}(X)=\mathcal{A}_{\omega}(U).

Thus, any element in the kernel of one of the maps
\pi^{s_{2}+m}\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t}\rightarrow\pi^{s_{1}+m}\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}_{\omega/t}
must be
\pi
-torsion. As these are free modules over the
\mathcal{R}
-flat algebra
B^{\circ}
, the maps are injective. Furthermore, the maps in
(\ref{equation showing U-cher algebras are independent of lattice})
are
G
-equivariant maps of
(\mathcal{R},B^{\circ})
-Lie algebras. Thus, we get a sequence of
G
-equivariant injective maps of
K
-algebras:

\mathcal{D}(\pi^{s_{2}+m}\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(\pi^{s_{1}+m}\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}_{\omega/t})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(\pi^{m}\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t}).

After applying
\pi
-adic completion, tensoring by
K
, and taking the skew group ring with respect to the action of
G
, we arrive at the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\pi^{s_{2}+m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\pi^{s_{1}+m}\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}_{\omega/t}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\pi^{m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\ltimes\widehat{\mathcal{D}}(\pi^{s_{2}+m}\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t})_{K}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\ltimes\widehat{\mathcal{D}}(\pi^{s_{1}+m}\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}_{\omega/t})_{K}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\ltimes\widehat{\mathcal{D}}(\pi^{m}\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t})_{K}}

Taking inverse limits we get two maps:

\varprojlim_{m}\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\pi^{m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}\rightarrow\varprojlim_{m}\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\pi^{m}\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}_{\omega/t}}\rightarrow\varprojlim_{m}\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\pi^{m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}},

which are mutually inverse. All in all, we have shown the following:

Lemma 4.2.10
.

Let
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t},\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}_{\omega/t}
be two
U
-Cherednik lattices. There is a unique isomorphism of topological
K
-algebras:

\varprojlim_{m}\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\pi^{m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}\rightarrow\varprojlim_{m}\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\pi^{m}\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}_{\omega/t}}.

Furthermore, this isomorphism is the identity on
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
.

Hence, we have shown that
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U}
is well-defined, and can be calculated using any
U
-Cherednik lattice. The following consequences arise from the definition:

Proposition 4.2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U}
be the
U
-Cherednik algebra. We have:

(i)

The map
i:H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U}
is injective with dense image.

(ii)

The map
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U}\rightarrow G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega/t}(U)
is injective with closed image.

In particular,
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U}
is the closure of
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
in
G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega/t}(U)
.

Proof.

Claim
(i)
is a consequence of Lemma
4.2.7
and the definition of the inverse limit topology. Claim
(ii)
follows by Proposition
4.2.8
, commutativity of the diagram above, and the fact that inverse limits are left exact.
∎

Ideally, we would like that
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U}
is also independent of the
G
-stable affinoid subspace
U\subset\overline{X}
. This will in general not hold, as for a pair of affinoid subspaces
V=\operatorname{Sp}(C)\subset U=\operatorname{Sp}(B)\subset X
, it could happen that
B^{\circ}\cap A\neq C^{\circ}\cap A
. Thus,
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{V}
will contain more rigid functions than
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U}
.

Lemma 4.2.12
.

Let
V\subset U\subset\overline{X}
be two
G
-stable affinoid subdomains which meet all connected components of
X
. Then there is a unique continuous morphism of topological
K
-algebras:

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{V},

which is the identity on
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
.

Proof.

By construction, the map
G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega/t}(U)\rightarrow G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega/t}(V)
is continuous, and is the identity when restricted to
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
. Hence, it induces a map between the closures of
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
in both spaces, which restricts to the identity on
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
. The lemma now follows by the last part of Proposition
4.2.11
.
∎

In order to find a definition which does not depend on any choice, we notice that
\overline{X}
has a canonical increasing affinoid cover. Indeed, let
Z

be the union of all reflection hypersurfaces. This is a closed analytic subspace associated to an ideal:

\mathcal{I}_{Z}=f_{1}A+\cdots+f_{r}A.

Thus, we have an admissible cover of
\overline{X}
given by the Laurent subdomains:

\overline{X}_{n}=X\left(\frac{\pi^{n}}{f_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\pi^{n}}{f_{r}}\right):=\{x\in X|\textnormal{ }|f_{i}(x)|\geq|\pi^{n}|\textnormal{, for all }1\leq i\leq r\}.

As
G
acts on
S(X,G)
by conjugation, each of the
\overline{X}_{n}
is a
G
-stable affinoid subdomain of
X
, which we can assume to meet all the connected components of
X
.

Definition 4.2.13
.

The
p
-adic Cherednik algebra of the
G
-variety
X
(relative to our choice of
t
,
c
,
\omega
) is the following inverse limit:

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)=\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\overline{X}_{n}}.

We regard it as a topological
K
-algebra with respect to its inverse limit topology.

Remark 4.2.14
.

We make the following observations:

(i)

For any
\lambda\in K^{*}
we have
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)=\mathcal{H}_{\lambda t,\lambda c,\lambda\omega}(X,G)
.

(ii)

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
is the closure of
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
in
G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega/t}(\overline{X})
.

In light of this remark, and the fact that
t\in K^{*}
, it would be possible to set
t=1
in the definition of
p
-adic Cherednik algebra and remove it from the notation. However, it is important to mention that, in the classical case, the parameter
t=0
is allowed, and leads to Cherednik algebras of a slightly different nature (
cf.

[
23
, Section 2.10]
,
[
24
, pp. 8]
). Thus, we will restrain from dropping the
t
from the notation, and simply make the assumption that
t=1
when it simplifies
calculations.

4.3.
p-adic Cherednik algebras as Fréchet-Stein algebras

Our next goal will be endowing
p
-adic Cherednik algebras with a Fréchet-Stein structure. Let
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
be an affinoid
G
-variety with an étale map
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{r}_{K}
. From now and until the end of the section we fix a
G
-stable affinoid open subspace
U=\operatorname{Sp}(B)\subset\overline{X}
which meets all connected components of
X
, together with
t\in K^{*}
,
c\in\textnormal{Ref}(X,G)
, and
\omega\in\Omega_{X/K}^{2,\operatorname{cl}}(X)^{G}
. By Proposition
4.2.5
, we may choose some
U
-Cherednik lattice
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
, and let
\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}
be its image under the anchor map
\mathcal{A}_{\omega/t}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
. Let us start by extending Theorem
3.3.1
to the integral setting:

Definition 4.3.1
.

We define the following filtrations:

(i)

The Dunkl-Opdam filtration on
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}
is defined by:

F_{i}H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}=F_{i}H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\cap H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}},

where
F_{\bullet}H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
is the Dunkl-Opdam filtration.

(ii)

The filtration by order of differential operators on
\mathcal{D}(\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t})
is defined by;

\Phi_{i}\mathcal{D}(\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t})=\mathcal{D}(\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t})\cap\Phi_{i}\mathcal{D}_{\omega/t}(U),\textnormal{ for all }i\geq 0,

where
\Phi_{\bullet}\mathcal{D}_{\omega/t}(U)
is the filtration by order of differential operators.

We can use these filtrations to obtain a PBW Theorem at the integral level:

Proposition 4.3.2
.

There is an isomorphism of graded
G\ltimes A^{\circ}_{B}
algebras:

\overline{\Psi}:\operatorname{gr}_{F}H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}\rightarrow G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{A_{B}^{\circ}}(A_{B}^{\circ}\otimes_{A^{\circ}}\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}),

which sends a Dunkl-Opdam operator
D_{v/t}
to
v
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\{v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}\}
be an
A^{\circ}
-basis of
\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}
and
fix any
m\geq 0
. By construction, we have the following commutative square of filtered algebras:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(U)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\ltimes\mathcal{D}(\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t})}

Passing to the associated graded, we get a commutative diagram of graded algebras:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{gr}_{F}H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{B}\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(U)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{gr}_{F}H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{B^{\circ}}(\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\Psi}}

By Theorem
3.3.1
,
\Psi:\operatorname{gr}_{F}H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{B}\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(U)
is injective and has image
G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{A}\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
. Furthermore,
\Psi
maps a Dunkl-Opdam operator
D_{v}
to
tv\in\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
, and is the identity on
G\ltimes A
. As all other maps are injective, it follows that
\overline{\Psi}
is injective.
Thus, it is an isomorphism onto its image. We just need to show that its image is
G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{A_{B}^{\circ}}(A_{B}^{\circ}\otimes_{A^{\circ}}\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}})
. The algebra
G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{A_{B}^{\circ}}(\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}\otimes_{A^{\circ}}A_{B}^{\circ})
is generated as an algebra over
G\ltimes A^{\circ}_{B}

by the symbols
v_{i}
, for
1\leq i\leq r
. By definition of a
U
-Cherednik lattice,
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}
contains Dunkl-Opdam operators
D_{v/t}
for each
v\in\mathcal{B}
. Hence,
its image under
\overline{\Psi}
contains the symbols
v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}
. By the definition of the restriction rings given in Definition
4.1.1
, and the fact that the map
A\rightarrow B
is injective (as
U
meets all connected components of
X
), we have
A^{\circ}_{B}=B^{\circ}\cap A
. Hence, we have:

G\ltimes A^{\circ}_{B}=(G\ltimes A)\cap(G\ltimes B^{\circ})\subset H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\cap G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t})=H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}.

As
\Psi
is the identity on
G\ltimes A
, it follows that
G\ltimes A^{\circ}_{B}
is contained in the image of
\overline{\Psi}
. Thus, the graded algebra
G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{A_{B}^{\circ}}(\pi^{m}\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}\otimes_{A^{\circ}}A_{B}^{\circ})
is contained in the image of
\overline{\Psi}
. On the other hand:

\overline{\Psi}(H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}})\subset G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{A}\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)\cap G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{B^{\circ}}(\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t}})\\
=G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{A_{B}^{\circ}}(A_{B}^{\circ}\otimes_{A^{\circ}}\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}).

∎

Just as in the non-integral case, we get the following corollary:

Corollary 4.3.3
.

H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}
is a free
G\ltimes A^{\circ}_{B}
-module, with basis given by:

D_{v_{1}/t}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots D_{v_{r}/t}^{\alpha_{r}},

for non-negative integers
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{r}\geq 0
.

Although the rings
A^{\circ}_{B}
can be rather strange, the material in Section
4.1
gives a precise description whenever
U
contains the Shilov boundary of
X
. In particular, in this case we have
A^{\circ}_{B}=A^{\circ}
. We may use this to show the following theorem:

Theorem 4.3.4
.

Assume that
S(X)\subset U\subset X
is a
G
-invariant affinoid subdomain. Then we have a canonical isomorphism of topological
K
-algebras:

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U}.

Furthermore, this isomorphism is the identity on
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
.

Proof.

By definition we have
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)=\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\overline{X}_{n}}
as topological algebras. As the cover
\overline{X}=\cup_{n}\overline{X}_{n}
is admissible and
U
is affinoid, there is some
n\geq 1
such that
U\subset\overline{X}_{n}
. It suffices to show that the canonical map:

\varphi:\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\overline{X}_{n}}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U},

is an isomorphism of topological
K
-algebras which is the identity on
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
. Let
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
be a Cherednik lattice for
U
and
\overline{X}_{n}
. It suffices to show that the map:

\varphi_{m}:H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\overline{X}_{n},\pi^{m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}\rightarrow H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\pi^{m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}

is an isomorphism for
m\geq 0
. However, as
U
contains the Shilov boundary of
X
, it follows that
\overline{X}_{n}
also contains it. Hence, Corollary
4.3.3
shows that both algebras are free modules over
G\ltimes A^{\circ}
with the same basis, so
\varphi_{m}
is an isomorphism.
∎

Remark 4.3.5
.

Let
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
be a
(\mathcal{R},A^{\circ})
-Lie lattice of
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}(X)
, and consider
G
-invariant affinoid subdomains
U,V\subset\overline{X}
which contain
S(X)
and such that

\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
is a Cherednik lattice for
U
and
V
. Then we have:

H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{V,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}=H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}.

Thus, we can drop the choice of affinoid subdomain from the notation, and write

H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}:=H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}},\textnormal{ }\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}:=\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}.

We will follow this convention until the end of the paper.

Thus, the constructions in Section
4.2
are independent of the choice of
G
-invariant affinoid subdomain
U\subset\overline{X}
. This motivates the following definition:

Definition 4.3.6
.

Let
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\omega/t}(X)
be a
G
-invariant finite-free
(\mathcal{R},A^{\circ})
-Lie lattice. We say
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
is a Cherednik lattice of
\mathcal{A}_{\omega/t}(X)
if there is a
G
-invariant affinoid subdomain
U\subset X
satisfying:

S(X)\subset U\subset\overline{X},

and such that
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
is a
U
-Cherednik lattice.

One of the main features of Cherednik lattices is that they share many good properties with the smooth lattices of Lie algebroids:

Lemma 4.3.7
.

Let
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
be a
G
-invariant finite-free
(\mathcal{R},A^{\circ})
-Lie lattice of
\mathcal{A}_{\omega/t}(X)
. There is some non-negative integer
m\geq 0
such that for all
n\geq m
,
\pi^{n}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
is a Cherednik lattice of
\mathcal{A}_{\omega/t}(X)
.

Proof.

Choose a
G
-invariant affinoid subdomain
U\subset X
which contains the Shilov boundary of
X
. By Lemma
4.2.4
, it follows that
U
is
\pi^{n}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
-stable for sufficiently high
n
. On the other hand, the proof of Proposition
4.2.5
then shows that

\pi^{n}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
is a
U
-Cherednik lattice for big enough
n\geq 0
.
∎

Hence, if
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
is a Cherednik lattice of
\mathcal{A}_{\omega/t}(X)
, Theorem
4.3.4
implies that we have the following identities of topological
K
-algebras:

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)=\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U}=\varprojlim_{m}\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\pi^{m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}.

From now until the end of this section, fix a Cherednik lattice
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
of
\mathcal{A}_{\omega/t}(X)
. The goal of this section is showing that the inverse limit of Banach
K
-algebras:

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)=\varprojlim_{m}\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\pi^{m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}},

is a Fréchet-Stein presentation of
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
. To simplify the calculations, we will assume
t=1
. We start by recalling a well-known fact on filtered algebras:

Lemma 4.3.8
.

Let
R
be a ring with a positive and exhaustive filtration
F_{\bullet}R
. If the associated graded
\operatorname{gr}_{F}R
is left (right) noetherian, then
R
is left (right) noetherian.

Proof.

This a consequence of
[
34
, Proposition D.1.1.]
.
∎

This has the following straightforward consequence:

Proposition 4.3.9
.

The following hold for each
m\geq 0
:

(i)

\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\pi^{m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}
is two-sided noetherian.

(ii)

\pi_{m}:\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\pi^{m+1}\mathscr{A}_{\omega}}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\pi^{m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega}}
has dense image.

Proof.

We show the case
m=0
. As
\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega}}=\widehat{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega}}[\pi^{-1}]
, and being noetherian is preserved under completion and localization, it is enough to show that
H_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega}}
is two-sided noetherian. By Proposition

4.3.2
we have:

\operatorname{gr}_{F}H_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega}}\cong G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{A^{\circ}}(\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega}}).

As
K
is discretely valued and
G
is finite, the right hand side is noetherian. Thus, the proposition follows by Lemma
4.3.8
. For the second statement, we notice that

is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\pi^{m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega}}
for every

by Lemma

. As the maps
\pi_{m}
are the identity on

, the result follows.
∎

The last step is showing that the following maps are two-sided flat:

\pi_{m}:\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\pi^{m+1}\mathscr{A}_{\omega}}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\pi^{m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega}}

The strategy will be adapting Sections 6.4 to 6.7 of
[
3
]
to our setting. Even though these sections are written in the language of noetherian, deformable, almost-commutative algebras, we will see that we can bridge the gaps by understanding the graded structure of the algebras
\operatorname{gr}_{F}H_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\pi^{m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega}}=G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{A^{\circ}}(\pi^{m}\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega}})
.

Remark 4.3.10
.

Until the end of the section, we will write
R_{m}:=H_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\pi^{m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega}}
and
R:=R_{0}
whenever it is convenient.

Start by recalling the definition of a deformable
\mathcal{R}
-algebra:

Definition 4.3.11

(
[
1
, Section 3.5]
)
.

Let
R
be a
\mathcal{R}
-algebra with a positive and exhaustive filtration
F_{\bullet}R
. We say
R
is a deformable algebra if
\operatorname{gr}R
is a flat
\mathcal{R}
-module. Let
R
be a deformable algebra. For a non-negative integer
m\geq 0
, the
m
-th deformation of
R
is defined by the following rule:

(R)_{m}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\pi^{im}F_{i}R.

This is a filtered algebra, with filtration is given by the following rule:

F_{j}(R)_{m}=(R)_{m}\cap F_{j}R=\sum_{i=0}^{j}\pi^{im}F_{i}R.

We may apply this definition to our setting as follows:

Proposition 4.3.12
.

The following hold:

(i)

The filtered algebra
H_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega}}
is a deformable
\mathcal{R}
-algebra.

(ii)

The identity
H_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\pi^{m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega}}=(H_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega}})_{m}
holds for each
m\geq 0
.

Proof.

As we have
\operatorname{gr}_{F}R=G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{A^{\circ}}(\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega}})
, it follows that
R
is deformable. For statement
(ii)
, we need to show that
(R)_{m+1}=R_{m+1}
. We can proceed inductively by showing that
F_{j}(R)_{m}=F_{j}R_{m}
for each
j\geq 0
. The base case of
j=0
follows at once from the fact that, by definition of the Dunkl-Opdam filtration, we have:

F_{0}(R)_{m}=F_{0}R=G\ltimes A^{\circ}=F_{0}R_{m}.

Assume now that
F_{j}(R)_{m}=F_{j}R_{m}
. We need to show that
F_{j+1}R_{m}\subset F_{j+1}(R)_{m}
. Let
v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}
be an
A^{\circ}
-basis of
\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega}}
, and
D_{v_{1}},\cdots,D_{v_{r}}
be a choice of Dunkl-Opdam operators.
As we have
\operatorname{gr}_{F}R_{m}=G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{A^{\circ}}(\pi^{m}\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega}})
, it follows that every element in
F_{j+1}R_{m}\setminus F_{j}R_{m}
can be written uniquely as a sum of
G\ltimes A^{\circ}
-linear combinations of elements of the form:

(\pi^{m}D_{v_{1}})^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots(\pi^{m}D_{v_{r}})^{\alpha_{r}}\textnormal{, }\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{r}=j+1.

Our induction hypothesis implies that it is enough to show that all elements of this form are contained in
F_{j+1}(R)_{m}
. Notice that
\pi\in\mathcal{R}
is in the center of
R
. Hence, as elements in
R
we have:

(\pi^{m}D_{v_{1}})^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots(\pi^{m}D_{v_{r}})^{\alpha_{r}}=\pi^{m(j+1)}D_{v_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots D_{v_{r}}^{\alpha_{r}},

and this is an element of
\pi^{m(j+1)}F_{j+1}R\subset F_{j+1}(R)_{m}
. The converse is analogous.
∎

We will use this proposition to compare the
\pi
-adic topology of the deformation
R_{1}
with its subspace topology coming from the
\pi
-adic topology on
R
.

Lemma 4.3.13

(
[
3
, Lemma 6.4]
)
.

Let
R
be a deformable
\mathcal{R}
-algebra. Then the following hold:

(i)

R_{1}\cap\pi^{n}R=\sum_{i\geq n}\pi^{i}F_{i}R
for any
n\geq 0
.

(ii)

(R_{n})_{m}=R_{nm}
for any
n,m\geq 0
.

The following proposition is an adaptation of
[
3
, Proposition 6.5]
, in particular, we just need to check that the proof still holds in this context.

Proposition 4.3.14
.

Let
I=R_{1}\cap\pi R
. The subspace filtration on
R_{1}
arising from the
\pi
-adic filtration on
R
and the
I
-adic filtration on
R_{1}
are the same.

Proof.

Let
D_{v_{1}},\cdots,D_{v_{r}}
be a choice of Dunkl-Opdam operators associated to our basis of
\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega}}
. Its images in
\operatorname{gr}_{F}R
are the elements
v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}
, and hence they generate
G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{A^{\circ}}(\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}})
over
(\operatorname{gr}_{F}R)_{0}=G\ltimes A^{\circ}
. Thus, we have:

\pi\in I\textnormal{ and }\pi D_{v_{j}}\in I\textnormal{ for all }j\geq 1.

Furthermore, we know that the ordered products of standard Dunkl-Opdam operators form a basis of
R
over
G\ltimes A^{\circ}
. Hence, as elements in
\mathcal{R}
commute with Dunkl-Opdam operators, we know that every element in
\pi^{i}F_{i}R
can be expressed uniquely as a linear combination with coefficients in
G\ltimes A^{\circ}
of elements of the form:

\pi^{i}D_{v_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots D_{v_{r}}^{\alpha_{r}}=\pi^{\alpha_{0}}(\pi D_{v_{1}})^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots(\pi D_{v_{r}})^{\alpha_{r}},

for some natural numbers
\alpha_{j}
with
0\leq j\leq r
and
\sum_{j=0}^{r}\alpha_{j}=i
. Notice that as both
\pi
and all elements of the form
\pi D_{v_{j}}
are elements of
I
for
1\leq j\leq r
, it follows that:

\pi^{\alpha_{0}}(\pi D_{v_{1}})^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots(\pi D_{v_{r}})^{\alpha_{r}},

is an element in
I^{i}
. Therefore, by Lemma
4.3.13
, for every
i\geq 1
we have:

R_{1}\cap\pi^{i}R=\sum_{j\geq i}\pi^{j}F_{j}R\subset I^{i}\subset R_{1}\cap\pi^{i}R.

Therefore we have
I^{i}\subset R_{1}\cap\pi^{i}R
for each
i\geq 1
, as we wanted.
∎

.

Lemma 4.4.8

is a

-Cherednik lattice in

. For each

Assume for simplicity that

.

is a sheaf on

by Theorem

. As every étale cover may be refined to an étale affinoid cover, it follows that
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
extends uniquely to a sheaf on

. In the general case, as

satisfies
(\operatorname{G-Aff})
, we may apply Proposition

to construct
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
on a cover of

. As

-adic Cherednik algebras only depend on the choice of the parameters

and

, it follows that
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
extends to a sheaf of topological
K
-algebras on
X/G_{\textnormal{ét}}
.
∎

Remark 4.4.11
.

If

is the zero section, then
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}=G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega}

to

.

When no confusion is possible, we will usually drop the

.

Let

Proposition 4.5.2

(

.

The following hold:

(ii)

(iii)

There is a short exact sequence of right

Proof.

As

D_{v}\cdot h=h\cdot D_{v}+v(h)+\sum_{(Y,g)\in S(X,G)}\frac{2c(Y,g)}{1-\lambda_{Y,g}}\overline{\xi_{Y}}(v)(g(h)-h)g.

Our choice of

As the action of

\frac{2c(Y,g)}{1-\lambda_{Y,g}}\overline{\xi_{Y}}(v)(g(a_{n})-a_{n})\in A^{\circ},\textnormal{ for each }n\geq 0.

As each

H_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega}}/\pi^{n}\rightarrow(\mathcal{R}/\pi^{n})[x]\otimes_{\mathcal{R}/\pi^{n}}\left(H_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega}}/\pi^{n}\right),

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{K}^{i}/u_{i}=\left(A\langle x\rangle\widehat{\otimes}_{A}\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega}}\right)/u_{i}=

.

Let
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
be a
G
-variety with an étale map

, and let

. The map

-flat.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{A}_{\omega}
be a Cherednik lattice of
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}(X)
such that

is a Cherednik lattice of
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}(U)
. Multiplying
\mathscr{A}_{\omega}
by a power of

for all

∎

G
-invariant affinoid subdomains:

Proposition 4.5.4
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
be a
G
-variety with an étale map
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{r}_{K}
, and let
U\subset X
be a
G
-invariant affinoid subdomain. The map:

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(U,G)

is

-flat. If

is an admissible cover of
X
by
G
-equivariant affinoid subspaces, then the following map is

-faithfully flat:

Let
U=X\left(\frac{g_{1}}{f},\cdots,\frac{g_{n}}{f}\right)
be a
G
-invariant rational subdomain. In virtue of item
(iv)
in Theorem
2.1.1
, we may assume that

. As

does not vanish in

such that

into the following chain of open immersions:

-flat by Corollary
4.5.3
. Let
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be a
G
-invariant rational cover of

. By Theorem
4.4.9
we have an exact complex:

4.6.
Localization of co-admissible modules

We will now use the results of the previous section to develop the basics of a theory of co-admissible modules over
p
-adic Cherednik algebras. In particular, we will define a localization process that will allow us to extend every co-admissible
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
to a sheaf of
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}
-modules.

Theorem 4.6.1
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
be a
G
-variety with an étale map
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{r}_{K}
, and a sheaf of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}
. There is an exact embedding:

\operatorname{Loc}(-):\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G))\rightarrow\operatorname{Mod}(\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}),

defined on affinoid subdomains
U\subset X
by the rule:

\operatorname{Loc}(\mathcal{M})(U)=\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(U,G)\wideparen{\otimes}_{\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)}\mathcal{M}.

\operatorname{Loc}(\mathcal{M})
has trivial higher Čech cohomology for any co-admissible module
\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

This is equivalent to
[
3
, Theorem 8.2]
.
∎

Definition 4.6.2
.

We denote the essential image of
\operatorname{Loc}(-)
by
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega})
, and call its elements co-admissible
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}
-modules.

Thus, we have achieved a local definition of co-admissible modules over a
p
-adic Cherednik algebra.
Ideally, we would like to generalize this notion to arbitrary smooth
G
-varieties. In order to do this, we would need a version of Kiehl’s Theorem. That is, we would need to show that every sheaf of
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}
-modules which is locally co-admissible arises as the localization of a co-admissible
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
-module. In light of the structural results for
p
-adic Cherednik algebras shown in Sections
4.3
,
4.4
, and
4.5
, it follows that the behavior of the sheaves
G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{X}
and
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}
is rather similar. Thus, one could expect that an approach similar to that of
[
3
]
could work in this setting. Alas, the situation is not so simple, and there are certain non-trivial technical difficulties that need to be worked around in order to have a well-behaved theory
of co-admissible modules over sheaves of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras. In particular, the approach in
[
3
]
is based upon some auxiliary topologies, namely the admissible and accessible topologies (
cf
.
[
3
, Definitions 3.2, 4.6]
). These topologies do no have a clear analog in the setting of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras. In particular, consider the following family:

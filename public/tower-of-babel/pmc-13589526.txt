The 5-Lipoxygenase Pathway in Ovarian Cancer: Mechanisms of Metastasis, Chemoresistance, and Translational Targeting
Authors: Mingjun Li, Xinyu Zhu, Lin Gan, Haixia Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOvarian cancer remains one of the most lethal gynecologic malignancies, characterized by early peritoneal dissemination, extensive microenvironmental remodeling, and frequent emergence of chemoresistance. Beyond genetic alterations, dysregulated lipid mediator signaling represents a regulatory layer that links inflammation, invasion, immune evasion, and cellular stress adaptation. The 5-lipoxygenase (5-LOX, encoded by ALOX5) pathway converts arachidonic acid into bioactive leukotrienes through coordinated activity with 5-LOX-activating protein (FLAP, encoded by ALOX5AP), thereby activating G protein-coupled receptors, including BLT1, BLT2, and cysteinyl leukotriene receptors. This narrative review synthesizes preclinical, translational, and clinical-association evidence for 5-LOX signaling in ovarian cancer. Particular attention is given to BLT2-driven invasion and chemoresistance, hypoxia-induced leukotriene production, macrophage recruitment, ALOX5AP-associated immune suppression, and biomarker-guided therapeutic development. The available evidence supports the 5-LOX axis as a biologically relevant but still clinically immature target. Future development should prioritize compartment-resolved pathway mapping, functional validation in patient-derived models and immune-competent experimental systems, and rational combination strategies rather than unselected monotherapy.

Introduction

Ovarian cancer remains a leading cause of gynecologic cancer mortality, largely because most patients present with advanced-stage disease and many eventually develop recurrent, treatment-resistant tumors.1 Global cancer estimates for 2022 reported approximately 325,000 new ovarian cancer cases and 207,000 deaths worldwide, underscoring the persistent clinical burden.2 Despite advances in cytoreductive surgery, platinum-taxane chemotherapy, anti-angiogenic therapy, and poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase inhibitor maintenance in selected settings, peritoneal dissemination and acquired resistance remain central barriers to durable disease control.1,3

Inflammation is a recognized enabling feature of cancer biology and can promote tumor-cell survival, angiogenesis, invasion, and immune escape.4 In ovarian cancer, this inflammatory context is particularly relevant because advanced and recurrent tumors commonly involve the peritoneal cavity, where malignant cells interact with ascites, mesothelial surfaces, fibroblasts, endothelial cells, and immune infiltrates.5,6 Within this inflammatory landscape, arachidonic acid-derived lipid mediators form a rapid enzymatic signaling network that coordinates tumor-intrinsic programs with stromal and immune responses.7–9

The 5-lipoxygenase (5-LOX) pathway centered on the enzyme 5-LOX encoded by ALOX5, is one of the principal routes for leukotriene biosynthesis. Together with 5-LOX-activating protein (FLAP, encoded by ALOX5AP), 5-LOX generates leukotriene A4, leukotriene B4 (LTB4), and cysteinyl leukotrienes, which signal through BLT1, BLT2, CysLT1, and CysLT2 receptors.10,11 This pathway is translationally relevant because it connects inflammation, lipid metabolism, and cell-cell communication. Unlike irreversible genomic alterations, leukotriene signaling is enzymatically regulated, inducible by stress conditions such as hypoxia, and potentially druggable at multiple levels, including 5-LOX enzymatic activity, FLAP-mediated substrate transfer, and downstream receptor signaling. In ovarian cancer, such a diffusible lipid mediator system may help coordinate local invasion, macrophage recruitment, immune remodeling, and stromal adaptation across the peritoneal niche.

However, the ovarian cancer-specific literature on this pathway remains clinically immature. Nevertheless, several ovarian cancer-relevant observations justify a focused narrative review: BLT2 has been linked to invasion and chemoresistance in ovarian cancer models; hypoxia-induced 5-LOX metabolites can recruit macrophages; and ALOX5AP expression has been associated with immunosuppressive microenvironmental features and inferior outcomes in serous ovarian cancer.12–14 This review therefore evaluated the 5-LOX axis as a candidate biological and translational pathway in ovarian cancer, while distinguishing established findings from hypotheses that require further validation.

The purpose of this review is to provide a clinically interpretable synthesis of the available evidence. We emphasize evidence strength, disease specificity, biomarker relevance, and the practical limitations that must be addressed before 5-LOX-directed strategies can be tested credibly in ovarian cancer.

Methods

We conducted a narrative literature review using PubMed and Web of Science to identify studies relevant to the 5-LOX pathway and ovarian cancer. Search terms included combinations of “ALOX5”, “5-lipoxygenase”, “ALOX5AP”, “FLAP”, “leukotriene”, “LTB4”, “BLT2”, “CysLT”, “ovarian cancer”, “metastasis”, “peritoneal dissemination”, “hypoxia”, “macrophage”, “tumor microenvironment”, “chemoresistance”, and “biomarker”. English-language articles published from January 2000 to July 2026 were considered, with earlier foundational leukotriene biology articles included when needed for mechanistic context.

We prioritized ovarian cancer-specific evidence, including mechanistic studies involving genetic or pharmacologic perturbation of the pathway, clinically annotated studies examining pathway-component expression, immune infiltration, or survival associations, and translational studies relevant to pathway inhibition, repurposing, or biomarker selection. Where ovarian cancer-specific evidence was limited, selected findings from broader leukotriene biology or related cancer contexts were included only when they clarified pathway mechanisms or drug-development constraints. This article is a narrative review, not a systematic review, and does not include formal meta-analysis.

Overview of the 5-LOX Pathway

5-LOX catalyzes the first committed steps in leukotriene biosynthesis from arachidonic acid, generating 5-hydroperoxyeicosatetraenoic acid and leukotriene A4, which can be converted into LTB4 or cysteinyl leukotrienes depending on the cellular enzymatic context.7,10 Leukotriene production is regulated by substrate availability, calcium-dependent membrane translocation of 5-LOX, and the interaction between 5-LOX and FLAP, which facilitates efficient substrate presentation and leukotriene synthesis in activated cells.10,11,15

Leukotrienes exert their biological effects mainly through G protein-coupled receptors. BLT1 is a high-affinity LTB4 receptor with well-established roles in leukocyte trafficking, whereas BLT2 is a lower-affinity receptor with broader ligand recognition and increasing relevance in cancer-associated signaling.16 Cysteinyl leukotrienes signal through CysLT receptors, which regulate vascular permeability, inflammatory recruitment, and immune responses.17 Downstream signaling includes reactive oxygen species (ROS), mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) cascades, phosphoinositide 3-kinase/protein kinase B (PI3K/AKT) activation, and signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3), which have been linked to invasion, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and chemoresistance in ovarian cancer models.13,18

The pathway should not be interpreted in isolation. Arachidonic acid can also be metabolized through cyclooxygenase, lipoxygenase, and cytochrome P450 pathways. In cancer models, inhibition of one branch may redirect substrate flux toward alternative eicosanoid pathways.19,20 For ovarian cancer, this network property has two implications. First, expression of one enzyme or receptor is unlikely to be sufficient as a stand-alone predictive biomarker. Second, therapeutic strategies may need to consider pathway state and compensatory lipid-mediator signaling rather than assuming that 5-LOX inhibition will uniformly suppress tumor-promoting inflammation.

Beyond canonical leukotriene biosynthesis, 5-LOX may also participate in non-canonical regulatory processes related to subcellular localization, protein interactions, and stress-response programs.21 However, direct ovarian cancer-specific evidence for these non-canonical functions remains limited. These mechanisms should therefore be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than established ovarian cancer mechanisms. This distinction matters for ovarian cancer translation because anti-tumor effects of pathway inhibition may not always be explained by simple reductions in extracellular leukotriene concentrations. It also supports the need for pharmacodynamic studies that measure downstream pathway modulation, not only drug exposure.

Tumor-Intrinsic 5-LOX Signaling in Invasion and Chemoresistance

The most direct ovarian cancer-specific evidence for tumor-intrinsic leukotriene signaling involves BLT2. In OVCAR-3 and SK-OV-3 cells, BLT2 promoted invasion and metastatic potential through a NADPH oxidase 4 (NOX4)-ROS-STAT3-matrix metalloproteinase 2 (MMP2) cascade.12 BLT2 or NOX4 knockdown reduced STAT3 activation and MMP2 expression, whereas pharmacologic BLT2 inhibition suppressed SK-OV-3 metastasis in nude mice.12 These findings support a functional, rather than solely correlative, role for BLT2 signaling in ovarian cancer cell invasion.

BLT2 has also been implicated in platinum-relevant chemoresistance. In cisplatin-resistant SK-OV-3 models, BLT2 and LTB4 were upregulated, and BLT2 depletion increased cisplatin sensitivity by reducing STAT3-interleukin-6 (IL-6) survival signaling.12 Co-treatment with a BLT2 inhibitor also reduced tumor formation by cisplatin-resistant SK-OV-3 cells in cisplatin-treated athymic mice. This evidence links leukotriene receptor signaling to cytotoxic stress adaptation. However, it remains restricted to preclinical models and does not establish that BLT2 blockade will reverse platinum resistance in unselected patients.

Evidence upstream of BLT2 is emerging but remains less clinically developed. A recent ovarian cancer study reported that ALOX5 was increased in tumor and metastatic tissues and promoted migration, invasion, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT)-associated programs through the LTB4/BLT2/PI3K/AKT/SNAI1 axis.13 These data strengthen the biological connection between the enzymatic 5-LOX pathway and BLT2-mediated invasive plasticity. They also suggest that pathway dependence may vary according to ALOX5 activity, receptor expression, and downstream signaling context. From a disease-biology perspective, these mechanisms are relevant to peritoneal dissemination. Ovarian cancer spread requires tumor-cell detachment, survival in ascites, mesothelial adhesion, extracellular matrix remodeling, and invasion into peritoneal or omental surfaces. The BLT2-NOX4-ROS-STAT3-MMP2 cascade is therefore notable because it links leukotriene receptor signaling to matrix remodeling, not only to proliferation. Similarly, EMT-associated changes should be framed as invasive plasticity that may support dissemination and treatment adaptation. They should not be presented as a simple binary switch from an epithelial to a mesenchymal state.

Tumor Microenvironment-Mediated 5-LOX Signaling and Immune Regulation

Hypoxia provides the clearest ovarian cancer-specific link between 5-LOX activity and microenvironmental remodeling.24 In human ovarian tumor tissues, 5-LOX expression correlated with tumor-associated macrophage density in hypoxic regions.14 In cultured ovarian cancer cells, hypoxia increased 5-LOX metabolite production, and these metabolites promoted macrophage migration and invasion through p38-dependent induction of matrix metalloproteinase 7 (MMP7).14 In xenograft models, pharmacologic 5-LOX inhibition with zileuton reduced MMP7 expression and macrophage infiltration. These findings support an experimentally tested pathway by which hypoxic ovarian cancer cells may recruit macrophages through 5-LOX-dependent lipid mediator signaling.

Tumor-associated macrophages are major regulators of epithelial ovarian cancer progression, peritoneal dissemination, angiogenesis, immune suppression, and therapeutic resistance.25 LTB4 is a potent myeloid chemoattractant, and leukotriene signaling can regulate leukocyte recruitment, dendritic-cell migration, cytokine release, and T-cell trafficking in non-ovarian inflammatory and tumor-associated contexts.26,27 In ovarian cancer, however, the key question is not whether leukotrienes can regulate immune-cell movement in general, but whether this pathway actively sustains immunosuppressive myeloid niches in patient tumors. Current evidence supports biological plausibility, but direct causal validation in patient-derived, spatially resolved, and immune-competent ovarian cancer models remains limited.

ALOX5AP may be particularly relevant at the tumor-immune interface. In serous ovarian cancer datasets, high ALOX5AP expression has been associated with worse survival, M2 macrophage enrichment, immune-checkpoint-related signatures, and immunosuppressive features.22 These associations suggest that ALOX5AP may mark an inflammatory and immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. Nevertheless, these findings are largely derived from transcriptomic and immune-deconvolution analyses. They should therefore be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive evidence that ALOX5AP directly drives macrophage polarization or immune evasion.

The microenvironmental evidence also explains why bulk tumor measurements are difficult to interpret. ALOX5 and ALOX5AP expression may arise from malignant epithelial cells, infiltrating myeloid cells, stromal compartments, or a combination of these sources. A high bulk RNA signal could therefore reflect tumor-cell pathway activation, macrophage abundance, or reciprocal tumor-immune signaling. Spatial transcriptomics, multiplex immunohistochemistry, single-cell profiling, and lipidomic readouts are especially important for this pathway because they can determine whether leukotriene-related markers localize to invasive tumor fronts, hypoxic regions, macrophage-rich niches, or immune-excluded compartments.

Clinical Associations and Biomarker Development

Clinical evidence for the 5-LOX pathway in ovarian cancer remains mainly associative, but several observations support further biomarker development. An early tissue microarray study of epithelial ovarian cancer detected strong BLT2, BLT1, and 5-LOX expression in subsets of tumors.23 BLT2 expression was associated with adverse clinical features, including advanced-stage disease and platinum resistance, although it was not associated with disease-free survival in that cohort.23 These findings suggest that leukotriene pathway activity may be present in selected ovarian tumors, but they do not establish BLT2 as a validated prognostic or predictive marker.

ALOX5AP is currently the most developed candidate marker from a clinical-association perspective. In serous ovarian cancer datasets, high ALOX5AP expression has been associated with worse overall and progression-free survival, platinum non-responsiveness, M2 macrophage enrichment, lymphocyte-depleted immune features, and immune-checkpoint-related signatures.22 However, these findings are largely based on retrospective transcriptomic datasets, immune-deconvolution analyses, and limited clinical validation. ALOX5AP should therefore be interpreted as a candidate marker of an immunosuppressive, macrophage-rich tumor microenvironment rather than as a clinically actionable biomarker at present. A practical biomarker framework should therefore combine tumor-cell and immune-context features. Candidate markers may include ALOX5, ALOX5AP, BLT2, hypoxia-associated markers, macrophage markers such as CD163 or MRC1, and downstream activation markers such as pSTAT3 or MMP2. Such a composite approach could help distinguish tumors in which leukotriene signaling is biologically active from tumors in which pathway-gene expression is incidental or reflects macrophage abundance.

Assay feasibility remains a major translational barrier. ALOX5 and ALOX5AP can be measured by RNA-based assays, but clinical translation would require reproducible protein-level or spatial assays that distinguish malignant epithelial cells from infiltrating myeloid cells. BLT2 immunostaining is attractive because receptor localization can be assessed at the cellular level, but antibody specificity, scoring thresholds, and inter-laboratory reproducibility require validation. A credible enrichment strategy would therefore combine pathway expression, downstream activation, and spatial immune context rather than relying on a single marker.

Evidence Strength, Disease Specificity, and Current Limitations

The evidence base for the 5-LOX pathway in ovarian cancer is biologically coherent but uneven. The strongest causal evidence involves BLT2-driven invasion and cisplatin-relevant chemoresistance in ovarian cancer cell-line and xenograft models.12 Hypoxia-associated 5-LOX metabolite production provides mechanistic support for macrophage recruitment in ovarian cancer models.14 ALOX5AP studies add clinically relevant associations with prognosis, platinum response, and immune infiltration.22 These evidence streams converge conceptually, but they do not yet establish the 5-LOX pathway as a validated therapeutic target in patients with ovarian cancer.

Several limitations should temper interpretation. First, much of the functional evidence comes from established cell lines and immunodeficient xenograft models, which may not capture the immune, spatial, and histologic complexity of high-grade serous ovarian cancer. Second, available clinical studies are largely retrospective and transcriptomic, making it difficult to separate causal pathway activation from broader macrophage enrichment. Third, tumor cells may produce relatively low or context-dependent levels of 5-LOX-derived lipid mediators, whereas infiltrating myeloid cells may be major contributors to pathway signals in bulk tumor data. Fourth, the eicosanoid network is redundant, and inhibition of one enzymatic branch may redirect arachidonic acid metabolism through compensatory pathways.19 Finally, no ovarian cancer-specific clinical trial has shown that targeting 5-LOX, FLAP, or BLT receptors improves patient outcomes.

There are also terminology and disease-subtype limitations. Ovarian cancer is not a single biological entity, and mechanisms observed in SK-OV-3, OVCAR-3, A2780, or other cell-line systems may not generalize across high-grade serous, clear-cell, endometrioid, mucinous, or low-grade serous ovarian carcinoma. Similarly, chemoresistance can refer to intrinsic resistance, acquired resistance after platinum exposure, drug-tolerant persister states, or microenvironment-mediated survival. Future studies should specify histologic subtype, treatment context, resistance model, cellular compartment, and pathway readout before making broad translational claims.

The 5-LOX axis is a biologically plausible and translationally testable pathway in selected ovarian cancer contexts, particularly where BLT2 signaling, hypoxia-associated lipid metabolism, macrophage-rich inflammation, or ALOX5AP-high immune suppression is present. It should not yet be presented as a clinically validated treatment target or as a rationale for unselected monotherapy.

Zileuton is the most clinically familiar 5-LOX inhibitor because it is approved for asthma.28 This lowers the practical barrier to drug repurposing, but it does not by itself establish oncologic efficacy. Safety monitoring would also be required, as liver enzyme elevations and rare hepatotoxicity have been reported during chronic use.28 In ovarian cancer models, zileuton reduced hypoxia-associated macrophage infiltration and MMP7 expression, supporting the concept that 5-LOX inhibition can modulate tumor-promoting microenvironmental signaling.14 However, this evidence remains preclinical. Nevertheless, ALOX5AP is currently better supported as a candidate biomarker than as a validated therapeutic target in ovarian cancer. Direct evidence that FLAP inhibition improves ovarian cancer outcomes is still lacking. Receptor-level targeting may ultimately provide greater pathway specificity. BLT2 is positioned close to several ovarian cancer-relevant effector programs, including ROS-STAT3-MMP2 invasion signaling and STAT3/IL-6-linked cisplatin adaptation. This makes BLT2 a compelling experimental target. The major limitation is that BLT2-directed oncology drug development remains preclinical. BLT2 should therefore be described as a high-priority experimental target rather than a clinically mature drug target.

Combination therapy is the most plausible development path. Because BLT2 signaling has been linked to cisplatin adaptation, combining 5-LOX or BLT2 inhibition with platinum chemotherapy is mechanistically justified in tumors showing BLT2-high or STAT3-active phenotypes. Similarly, the association between ALOX5AP, macrophage enrichment, and immunosuppressive signatures raises the possibility of combining leukotriene pathway inhibition with immune-modulating therapy. At present, however, these immunotherapy combinations should be considered proposed or preclinical strategies. Pharmacodynamic biomarkers will be essential to demonstrate target engagement before broad efficacy claims are made.

For early clinical development, endpoints should be chosen carefully. In biomarker-enriched window-of-opportunity or neoadjuvant studies, pharmacodynamic endpoints may be more informative than objective response rate alone. Relevant readouts could include reduced leukotriene pathway activity, lower pSTAT3 or MMP2 signaling, altered macrophage polarization, or changes in immune-exclusion markers. In recurrent disease, combination studies would need to separate pathway modulation from the expected effects of chemotherapy or immunotherapy. For this reason, the most valuable near-term studies may be mechanism-focused rather than large unselected efficacy trials. Future Direction and Conclusion

Future work should move from descriptive association to compartment-resolved functional validation. A central priority is to determine whether ALOX5, ALOX5AP, and BLT2 activity in high-grade serous ovarian cancer is mainly tumor-epithelial, myeloid-derived, or shared across mixed tumor-stromal niches. Spatial transcriptomics, multiplex immunohistochemistry, single-cell profiling, and lipidomic measurements will be important for mapping pathway activity at cellular and spatial resolution.31 Preclinical studies should also move beyond monoculture systems by using patient-derived organoids, ascites-derived cultures, macrophage co-culture platforms, and immune-competent models. These systems could help test whether 5-LOX, FLAP, or BLT2 inhibition sensitizes tumors to platinum therapy or improves responses to immune-modulating approaches. Early trials should be biomarker-first, with enrichment panels incorporating BLT2, ALOX5, ALOX5AP, NOX4, pSTAT3, MMP2, CD163, MRC1, and hypoxia-associated markers.

In conclusion, current evidence suggests that the 5-LOX pathway may contribute to ovarian cancer progression through BLT2-associated invasion and chemoresistance, hypoxia-associated macrophage recruitment, and immune suppression. ALOX5AP also shows candidate prognostic and immune-biomarker relevance in serous ovarian cancer. However, the pathway remains clinically immature. The most defensible interpretation is that the 5-LOX axis is biologically plausible and translationally testable in selected ovarian cancer contexts, but is not yet a clinically validated therapeutic target. Future development should therefore remain biomarker-guided, mechanism-focused, and combination-oriented.

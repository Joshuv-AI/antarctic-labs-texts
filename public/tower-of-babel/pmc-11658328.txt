Barriers and facilitators of tuberculosis treatment among immigrants: an integrative review
Authors: Seyed Mohammad Seyedmehdi, Hamidreza Jamaati, Mohammad Varahram, Payam Tabarsi, Majid Marjani, Afshin Moniri, Niloufar Alizadeh, Somayeh Hassani
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and objectivesMigration, as an important social determinant in the field of health, plays a crucial role in the non-eradication of tuberculosis worldwide. Therefore, it is essential to identify the factors related to tuberculosis treatment in immigrants to effectively combat this disease. However no comprehensive study has focused on this issue. Therefore, this study was conducted using an ecological approach to identify the barriers and facilitators of tuberculosis treatment in immigrants.MethodsThis qualitative study utilized an integrative literature review. Studies were searched without time restrictions in database such as MEDLINE, Institute for Scientific Information (ISI), Google Scholar, Scopus, and EMBAS, as well as internal databases like Scientific Information Database and Magiran. The findings of 100 studies that met the inclusion criteria were analyzed using the conventional content analysis method based on the ecological approach.ResultsIn general, the aforementioned codes were placed into four main categories, including individual factors (i.e., biological factors, psychological factors, behavioral factors, patient knowledge, factors related to tuberculosis, and economic factors), interpersonal factors (i.e., communication with the treatment team and family-related factors), factors related to health service provider centers (i.e., medical facilities), and extra-organizational factors (i.e., social factors and health policies).ConclusionThe findings of the present study show that treating tuberculosis in immigrants is a complex phenomenon influenced by several factors. Therefore policymakers and healthcare providers should take a more comprehensive look at the factors affecting treatment in this group of patients to plan and implement more effective interventions.Keywords: Tuberculosis, Treatment, Immigrant, Ecological approach, Content analysis

Introduction

Tuberculosis was recognized by the World Health Organization as a “global emergency” and one of the main health priorities in 1993 [1]. However, achieving the goal of eradicating tuberculosis has not been possible [2–4], and the incidence of tuberculosis is increasing in many parts of the world [5]. Various studies have shown that migration, an important social determinant in the field of health [6], plays a significant role in the persistence of tuberculosis worldwide [7–9].

In recent years, following economic and political crises in African and Middle Eastern countries, there has been an increase in migration from countries where tuberculosis is endemic to industrialized countries and countries with lower tuberculosis prevalence [10]. In addition to immigrants with tuberculosis, many migrants face challenges such as travel difficulties, violence, abuse, malnutrition, adverse conditions during their journey, and unfavorable working and living conditions in the destination country, all of which increase the risk of infection [11]. Therefore immigrants play a significant role in altering the global epidemiology [12] and disease pattern of tuberculosis [13–16], leading to a notable increase in the disease burden, healthcare costs, and challenges in tuberculosis control programs in host countries [17].

Successful treatment of tuberculosis in immigrants is the most important strategy to overcome this disease in host countries [18]. Failure to successfully treat tuberculosis can lead to prolonged hospitalization, increased treatment costs [19], recurrence of the disease, increased risk of drug resistance, and higher mortality rates [1] It also increases the likelihood of the disease spreading in the community, ultimately increasing the burden of the disease [20]. Therefore identifying the barriers and facilitators of tuberculosis treatment in immigrants is essential [21]. Understanding these factors and implementing appropriate interventions can improve tuberculosis control [22] and assist policymakers, healthcare providers, society, and patients in addressing the challenges posed by tuberculosis [23].

Although several studies have been conducted on the impact of immigration on the epidemiology, challenges of screening, diagnosis and treatment of tuberculosis, most of the studies have only examined a limited number of influencing factors and have not comprehensively looked at the barriers and facilitators of tuberculosis treatment in immigrants. In the present study, we attempted to consider all aspects affecting health behaviors by using the ecological multilevel approach [24, 25] to obtain stronger evidence about each of the factors affecting the treatment of tuberculosis. This way, we can eliminate gaps in other studies as much as possible. Therefore, the present study was conducted with the aim of identifying the barriers and facilitators of tuberculosis treatment in immigrants based on the ecological approach.

Materials and methods

Study design

The present review study was conducted using the integrative literature review method [26], and the data were analyzed based on conventional content analysis using the Elo and Kyngäs methods [27].

Search strategy and studies selection

The literature search was conducted in January 2024 without time limits, following systematic review studies. Articles were searched using keywords such as immigrant, migrant, immigration, migration, asylum seeker, refugees, foreign born population, tuberculosis, treatment, medication, and treatment management. Databases searched included MEDLINE databases (including PubMed and Ovid), Institute for Scientific Information (ISI), Google Scholar, Scopus, EMBASE and internal databases, including Scientific Information Database, Magiran, and internal journals. Additionally, a manual search was performed by reviewing the references of the selected articles, and related articles were retrieved.

Inclusion criteria

Research articles related to barriers and facilitators of tuberculosis treatment in immigrants (with quantitative and qualitative methods) were included in the study. First, the titles of the articles were examined, and duplicate articles were excluded from the study. Among the remaining articles, the abstracts of the articles that met inclusion criteria were independently reviewed by two members of the research team. Finally, 100 studies were analyzed.

Quality assessment

A quality assessment was conducted on 100 selected articles. The Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool (MMAT) (version 2018) was used to check the quality of the studies [28–33]. Based on the MMAT, all the studies were of suitable quality to be included in the review. The article selection process was implemented based on the PRISMA protocol (Fig. 1).

Article selection process

Ethical issues

The present study was a part of a mixed-method study and has been approved by the ethics committee of Shahid Beheshti University of Medical Sciences with ethics code IR.SBMU.NRITLD.REC.1402.191.

Data analysis

Conventional content analysis was used for data analysis, following Elo and Kyngäs method (2008) [27]. The researcher utilized the ecological approach to gain a comprehensive view and organize the codes [24, 25, 34, 35]. In the ecological approach presented by Bronfenbrenner in 1979, the levels related to individual behavior are divided into four levels: micro, meso, exo, and macro [36, 37].Therefore the ecological approach is a comprehensive method that examines various factors influencing health behaviors. It helps to understand the mutual and multidimensional effects of both “individual” and “environmental” factors on behavior [38].

Initially, the findings section of the articles was read and analyzed multiple times. Primary codes related to the barriers and facilitators of tuberculosis treatment in immigrants were extracted, and these codes were classified according to the ecological approach. Finally, a conceptual model was presented based on the identified classes [27]. It should be noted that the conceptual model and the suggested relationships between the categories are derived from immersion in data and supported by evidence found in the selected articles [27].

Data validation

The codes were extracted by one researcher, but the findings of the articles were reviewed by all members of the research team. If there were any problems or ambiguities in the study findings, the texts were re-examined. To ensure dependability in the study, all steps of the research were recorded in detail and all data collected was carefully maintained.

Results

The findings of 100 studies were analyzed, including 55 quantitative studies, 37 qualitative studies, and 8 mixed-method studies. Additional information about the articles is provided in the appendix (Table 1). The codes extracted from the studies were based on the ecological approach and categorized into four main categories: individual factors, interpersonal factors, factors related to health service provider centers, and extra-organizational factors. The table containing the codes, primary subcategories, subcategories and main categories extracted from the studies is provided in the appendix (Table 2).

Conceptual model

According to the ecological approach, both individual and environmental factors, as well as the interaction between them, influence a person’s health behavior [38]. The conceptual model of the current study which was prepared based on findings from an integrative review of the literature and the ecological approach (Fig. 2), illustrates that the treatment of immigrants with tuberculosis is a complex, multidimensional, and dynamic phenomenon. It is influenced by individual factors, interpersonal factors, factors related to health service provider centers, and extra-organizational factors. According to this conceptual model, individual factors, factors related to health service provider centers, and extra-organizational factors directly impact the treatment of immigrants with tuberculosis. Interpersonal factors also play a role, albeit indirectly through individual factors. Additionally, factors related to health service provider centers and extra-organizational factors not only directly affect the treatment of immigrants with tuberculosis but also indirectly affect it through individual factors. Conversely, the treatment of immigrant patients also impacts individual factors.

Discussion

The aim of this study was to identify the barriers and facilitators of treatment in immigrants with tuberculosis. Initially, an integrated review was conducted to extract codes related to treatment barriers and facilitators. Subsequently, using the ecological approach, the codes were classified and a conceptual model was presented. The results of this study showed that the factors related to the treatment of immigrants with tuberculosis are a complex phenomenon and can be placed in to four main categories: individual factors, interpersonal factors, factors related to health service centers and extra-organizational factors. In the following section, the concepts of the model will be discussed.

Category of individual factors

Based on our findings, the most identified codes were placed in the category of individual factors, with subcategories including biological factors, psychological factors, behavioral factors, patient knowledge, factors related to tuberculosis, and economic factors. These findings are consistent with the results of other studies and highlight the significant role of individual factors in the treatment of immigrants with tuberculosis.

The findings of the present review were conflicting when it came to age and TB treatment in immigrants. Most studies have shown that older age and advanced age can be significant obstacles to the treatment of immigrants with tuberculosis. Factors such as the presence of comorbidity, polypharmacy [138], an inadequate social security system, and economic deprivation in developing countries in the elderly can all potentially hinder the treatment of elderly immigrants [102].

The findings of our study were inconsistent regarding the gender and treatment of immigrants with TB. Many immigrant women do not have access to healthcare services due to financial barriers, lack of health literacy, gender inequality, violence against women, and lack of independence in life [139]. However, some studies have shown that immigrant men face more obstacles in the treatment of tuberculosis due to demographic, social, economic and cultural factors [140, 141]. It appears that several factors play a role in the relationship between gender and the treatment of immigrants with tuberculosis, and these should be taken into consideration by the treatment team members.

The results of this review revealed that negative emotions, discrimination, shame and fear of stigma act as obstacles to immigrants continuing treatment for tuberculosis. Generally, difficult experiences, stress, and trauma in the country of origin or host can lead to adverse mental health outcomes in immigrants [142]. Negative feelings experienced by immigrants lead to their marginalization and reluctance to utilize healthcare services [137]). The treatment team should be mindful of the vulnerabilities and mental health issues faced by immigrants. They should refer them to appropriate centers for the support and treatment they require when necessary [142].

The findings of the present study revealed that illiteracy, lack of knowledge and misconceptions about tuberculosis were obstacles to the treatment of tuberculosis in immigrants. Other studies have also indicated that insufficient knowledge about the disease and treatment can impact patient behaviors, such as health- seeking behavior, medication compliance, and delays in starting treatment [143]. Many immigrants have limited knowledge about tuberculosis [143]. It is essential for TB therapists to ensure that immigrants are well-informed about the disease and its treatment.

Our findings revealed that certain religious and cultural beliefs can serve as obstacles to tuberculosis treatment in immigrants. Generally, the culture of immigrants can result in social isolation, ultimately leading to their reluctance to seek healthcare [39]. It is important for the healthcare team to understand that the cultural beliefs of immigrant patients affect their treatment decisions. this includes their trust in traditional medicines and their compliance with prescribed medications [144, 145]. The healthcare team must acknowledge that these beliefs can vary depending on factors such as gender, age, employment, economic status, and other influences. Cultural beliefs may present significant barriers to treatment depending on the individual’s circumstances [39]. Therefore, the healthcare team should develop appropriate interventions without passing judgment, taking into account the patient’s cultural background and specific characteristics [137].

Drug abuse among immigrants with tuberculosis presents an additional challenge for the ongoing treatment of tuberculosis in this patient population. Several studies have indicated that individuals with substance abuse issues are less likely to be screened for, initiate and successfully complete tuberculosis treatment [146–148]. Establishing proper communication with immigrants suffering from tuberculosis helps patients trust healthcare workers, encourages disclosure about alcohol and drug abuse, improves healthcare provision, and ensures more accurate follow-up of patients.

Our findings revealed that unemployment, financial problems and poverty were some of the obstacles preventing immigrants from continuing their TB treatment. In many countries, drug treatment for tuberculosis is free. However, financial difficulties and poverty among immigrants hinder them from covering expenses related to transportation, participating in follow-up programs, and completing other treatment processes [126]. Immigrants in the host country often work in low-skilled jobs, earning insufficient wages and lacking health insurance. Consequently, they are vulnerable in terms of job security and income, and their employers do not support them in undergoing the TB treatment process (https://www.stoptb.org/assets/documents/resources/publications/acsm/KPBrief_MobilePopulations_ENG_WEB.pdf).

The findings of our study demonstrated that the motivation of immigrants with tuberculosis plays a critical role in the diagnosis and treatment of the disease. Negative emotions among immigrant patients, inadequate health literacy, low perceived risk of tuberculosis, misconceptions about the disease, and cultural beliefs about treatment all influence immigrants’ motivation to seek diagnosis and treatment for tuberculosis [116]. Implementing face-to-face educational strategies involving community members who can communicate with immigrants in a trusted manner can enhance motivation for treatment among immigrants [116].

Category of interpersonal factors

The category of interpersonal factors was derived from the subcategories of communication with the treatment team and family-related factors.

The current review revealed that the treatment team’s negative attitude towards immigrants, discrimination, and failure to provide adequate education to this patient group were obstacles to the continued treatment of immigrants with tuberculosis. The negative attitude and discriminatory behavior of the treatment team towards immigrants result in a negative attitude among the immigrants, lack of effective communication with the treatment team, dissatisfaction with the services provided, delayed referral for follow-up and discontinuation of treatment (https://www.stoptb.org/assets/documents/resources/publications/acsm/KPBrief_MobilePopulations_ENG_WEB.pdf.).

Based on our findings, we have identified the lack of supportive family and the loneliness experienced by immigrants as obstacles to the treatment of tuberculosis. Several studies have demonstrated the significant role that family support plays in ensuring the successful continuation of tuberculosis treatment [126, 149, 150]. Family members and the patient’s spouse play a crucial role in reminding the patient to take their medication, attend doctor’s appointments, and encouraging them to stay committed to their treatment. Therefore, a lack of support from family members, divorce, or the absence of a spouse can lead to a loss of emotional, social, and economic support for immigrants with tuberculosis, ultimately impacting their ability to continue treatment [126].

Category of factors related to health service provider centers

The findings of our study showed that health treatment centers lack responsiveness to the needs of immigrant patients, have insufficient resources and funds for treating immigrants, and lack motivation in their treatment teams to provide care to immigrants. These are significant challenges faced by health service providers. In most countries, the media as a powerful tool, plays an important role in creating negative stereotypes against immigrants, different races, and ethnicities. The existence of stereotypes and racist thoughts, especially among the treatment team working in the host country, plays a significant role in ignoring immigrants and not providing adequate medical services to this group of patients [151]. The stereotypes held by the treatment team towards immigrants have an impact on their behavior towards immigrant patients, diagnosis, medical decision-making process and selection of treatment options (https://www.stoptb.org/assets/documents/resources/publications/acsm/KPBrief_MobilePopulations_ENG_WEB.pdf).

Category of extra-organizational factors

The results of the current review demonstrated the impact of extra-organizational factors, such as social aspects and health policies, on the treatment of immigrants with tuberculosis.

One significant social obstacle in the present study was the stigma towards immigrants with tuberculosis and their exclusion. Generally, there are widespread negative perceptions of tuberculosis as a social disease, and most societies consider this disease to be limited to specific and marginalized population groups, including immigrants [152]. Immigrants, especially those coming from low-income countries to a host country, are often labeled as “diseased others” who are blamed for spreading infectious diseases to the local population and straining the host country’s healthcare system [(https://www.stoptb.org/assets/documents/resources/publications/acsm/KPBrief_MobilePopulations_ENG_WEB.pdf), 153]. Stigma surrounding tuberculosis patients can lead to delays in seeking and discontinuation of treatment, which has been shown in various studies to have negative impacts on medication adherence [(https://www.stoptb.org/assets/documents/resources/publications/acsm/KPBrief_MobilePopulations_ENG_WEB.pdf), 152].

Our study revealed that the lack of social support from the community is an additional obstacle to treatment for immigrants with TB. In many countries, individuals with high incomes and a low burden of tuberculosis often face strict health policies when it comes to immigrants with tuberculosis. These policies may include work and travel restrictions, and in some cases, deportation from the host country. Unfortunately, these policies, combined with a lack of social support from governments, lead to a loss of trust in the health system among immigrants. As a result, many immigrants fail to disclose their disease, are reluctant to seek medical attention, and may even discontinue treatment for tuberculosis (https://www.stoptb.org/assets/documents/resources/publications/acsm/KPBrief_MobilePopulations_ENG_WEB.pdf).

The findings of our study indicated the presence of more treatment barriers in illegal immigrants. Illegal immigrants are more marginalized than other immigrants and have less access to healthcare services [154]. In some countries, such as India, the possibility of immigrants accessing health services directly depends on their residence status and legality [155]. Immigrants also avoid healthcare centers out of fear of being recognized by the government and deported from the host country [154].

The language barrier between the treatment team and immigrants was identified as one of the obstacles to TB treatment in the present study. This issue requires attention and a suitable solution. The language difference hinders immigrants from fully understanding medical information and the treatment process [151], impacting their trust in the treatment team [142]. Having translators available at health centers to accurately convey medical information to immigrants can significantly improve the treatment process.

Our study findings revealed several obstacles to the treatment of tuberculosis in immigrants in terms of health policies. These obstacles include the absence of appropriate and responsive treatment protocols, limited access to health services for immigrants, inefficient health policies in both the country of origin and the host country, and the lack of health insurance coverage. Immigrants play a significant role in today’s societies. Paying attention to their health and ensuring fair access to health services not only guarantees compliance with their legal rights but is also crucial for maintaining public health in host countries [154]. Therefore, identifying and addressing the health issues of immigrants should be a fundamental aspect of a country’s health policies [154].

Conceptual model

However, several conceptual models have been presented regarding the factors that influence health-related behaviors. Most of these models are not comprehensive and only focus on individual factors that impact health behaviors [1, 25]. Studies in the field of tuberculosis epidemiology and treatments have often concentrated on singular factors, overlooking the various factors and their interrelationships. Many researchers, especially those in the social sciences, advocate for the use of multifactorial models to comprehend the epidemiology and control of tuberculosis [156]. For instance, in 1999 Jaramillo argued that the current trend of the epidemic and failed control of tuberculosis is due to exclusive tuberculosis control programs that solely address biological causes without considering integrated models of biological, behavioral, social, and economic factors [157]. Additionally, because of individual and social differences, immigrants encounter additional and unique challenges in TB treatment that need to be taken into account. Our study’s conceptual model demonstrates that the treatment of immigrants with tuberculosis is influenced by individual, interpersonal, organizational and extra-organizational factors. The connections between these factors indicate that the treatment of tuberculosis in immigrants is a complex and multi-dimensional phenomenon. It is crucial for members of the treatment team to pay special attention to this issue.

One of the strengths of the present study is its integrated literature review. An integrated literature review not only gathers scattered data and provides a basis for conducting specific studies related to the challenges of TB treatment, but also allows for the inclusion of studies with both quantitative and qualitative methods in the review [26]. This approach helps in creating a comprehensive understanding of issues related to healthcare and health policy [26].

The current study is a review study, so it will have limitations in providing sufficient evidence about the challenges of treating immigrants with TB. Conducting more comprehensive and extensive studies with mixed methods and quantitative methods in different countries will provide more complete information to policymakers. This will help them develop interventions based on the specific needs of immigrants in each region.

Conclusion

The increasing number of immigrants and their struggle to initiate and maintain tuberculosis treatment present a significant public health issue. With the growing prevalence of tuberculosis and the emergence of drug-resistant strains, it is crucial to implement precise health policies to effectively address tuberculosis in immigrant populations.

According to the conceptual model presented in this research, a synergistic effect was found between individual and interpersonal factors, health service providers, and external organizations. Therefore, when formulating health policies and addressing the challenges of treating immigrants with tuberculosis, we should not solely focus on individual factors. Instead, we should consider the interaction between these various factors and their impact on the immigrant patient’s ability or decision to start or continue treatment. In many countries, there is insufficient health and political infrastructure to support migrants with TB. While changing and upgrading these infrastructures require allocating adequate time, budget, and detailed planning based on research and scientific evidence, there are other feasible interventions that can support immigrants with TB and improve their health. These interventions include implementing health policies to prevent the deportation of immigrants with tuberculosis from the host country, creating health and ethical guidelines to support immigrants with tuberculosis in the host country, providing appropriate health services to immigrants regardless of their legal status, creating social and economic support systems for immigrants in society, establishing a safe environment free of stigma and discrimination in society and medical centers, training health care teams about the various factors affecting the treatment of immigrants with tuberculosis, and addressing stereotypes within the treatment team. Additionally, it is important to educate immigrants about their illness and emphasize the importance of starting and continuing treatment in its entirety.

1.1.
Classical Bohr inequality and its recent implications

Let
\mathcal{B}
denote the class of analytic functions in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
of the form
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
such that
|f(z)|<1
in
\mathbb{D}
. In the study of Dirichlet series, in
1914
, Harald Bohr
[
12
]
discovered the following interesting phenomenon that if
f\in\mathcal{B}
, then its associated majorant series

\displaystyle M_{f}(r):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|a_{k}|r^{k}\leq 1\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;|z|=r\leq\frac{1}{3}.

(1.1)

The constant
1/3
, known as the Bohr radius for the class
\mathcal{B}
, is the best possible. Furthermore, for
\psi_{a}(z)={(a-z)}/{(1-az)},\;a\in[0,1),
it follows easily that
M_{\psi_{a}}(r)>1
if, and only if,
r>1/(1+2a)
and hence the radius
1/3
is optimal as
a\rightarrow 1
. For other proofs of this theorem, we refer to the articles
[
12
,
38
,
39
]
. It is worth pointing out that there is no extremal function in
\mathcal{B}
such that the Bohr radius is precisely
{1/3}
(see
[
36
]
).

This result has generated substantial interest in Bohr’s inequality across various settings; see, for instance,
[
10
,
11
]
,
[
33
]
,
[
35
]
and the recent survey by Ali
et al.

[
3
]
,
[

]
,
[
20

, along with the references therein. Several of these works employ methods from complex analysis, functional analysis, number theory, and probability, and they further develop new theory and applications of Bohr’s ideas on Dirichlet series. For example, various multidimensional generalizations of this result have been obtained in
[
4
,

,
7
,

,

,
24
]
.

Our primary interest in this paper is to establish several multidimensional analogues of the improved Bohr’s inequality for the analytic case and to prove that these results are sharp.

1.2.
The improved versions of the classical Bohr’s inequality.

Throughout this paper
S_{r}(f)
denotes the area of the image of the subdisk
|z|<r
under the mapping
f
. If
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
, then from the definition of
S_{r}
, we see that

\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int\int_{|z|<r}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\;dx\;dy=\sum_{k=1}k|a_{k}|^{2}r^{2k}.

Recently, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
26
]
improved the classical Bohr inequality and established the result.

Theorem A
.

Suppose that
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then

\displaystyle|a_{0}|+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|r^{k}+\frac{16}{9}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\quad\text{for }|z|=r\leq\frac{1}{3}

(1.2)

and the numbers
1/3
and
16/9
cannot be improved.

It is worth recalling that if the first term
|a_{0}|
in (
1.2
) is replaced by
|a_{0}|^{2}
, the constants
{1/3}
and
{16/9}
are replaced by
{1/2}
and
{9/8}
, respectively (see
[
26
]
). More recently, the authors of
[
22
]
improved Theorem A by replacing the quantity
S_{r}/\pi
in inequality (
1.2
) with
S_{r}/(\pi-S_{r})
. Further generalizations of Bohr’s results were also obtained (see
[
22
, Corollary 3]
). Subsequently, Liu
et al.

[
30
]
employed the ideas developed in
[
22
]
to advance the study of Bohr’s inequality.

Theorem B
.

Suppose that
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then

(a)

|f(z)|+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|r^{k}\leq 1\quad\text{for }|z|=r\leq\sqrt{5}-2\approx 0.236068.
The constant
\sqrt{5}-2
is the best possible. Moreover,

(b)

|f(z)|^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|r^{k}\leq 1\quad\text{for }|z|=r\leq\frac{1}{3}.
The constant
\frac{1}{3}
is the best possible.

Ismagilov
et al.

[
23
]
derived a sharp version of Theorems A and B in
{2020}
. These results reveal the presence of an extremal function confirming that the value
{1/3}
is the maximum constant (i.e., it cannot be increased).

Theorem C
.

Suppose that
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then

\displaystyle M(r):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|a_{k}|r^{k}+\frac{16}{9}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)+\lambda_{1}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)^{2}\leq 1\quad\text{for }r\leq 1/3,

(1.3)

where
\lambda_{1}=\frac{4\left(486-261a-324a^{2}+2a^{3}+30a^{4}+3a^{5}\right)}{81(1+a)^{3}(3-5a)}=18.6095\ldots,

and
a\approx 0.567284
is the unique root in
(0,1)
of the equation
\psi_{1}(t)=0
, where
\psi_{1}(t)=-405+473t+402t^{2}+38t^{3}+3t^{4}+t^{5}.

The equality is achieved for the function
\psi_{a}(z)
.

The next result follows from the fact that there is a concrete extremal function for which equality in (
1.3
) holds, meaning no strictly positive terms can be added.

Theorem D
.

Suppose that
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then

\displaystyle M(z,r):=|f(z)|^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|r^{k}+\frac{16}{9}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)+\lambda_{2}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)^{2}\leq 1\quad\text{for }|z|=r\leq 1/3,

where
\lambda_{2}=\frac{-81+1044a+54a^{2}-116a^{3}-5a^{4}}{162(a+1)^{2}(2a-1)}=16.4618\ldots,
and
a\approx 0.537869
is the unique root in
(0,1)
of the equation
\psi_{2}(t)=0
, where
\psi_{2}(t)=-513+910t+80t^{2}+2t^{3}+t^{4}
.
The equality is achieved for the function
\psi_{a}(z)
.

Ismagilov
et al.

[
23
]
replaced
|f(z)|^{2}
by
|f(z)|
in Theorem D; however, this resulted in a smaller Bohr radius.

Theorem E
.

Suppose that
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then

\displaystyle|f(z)|+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|r^{k}+p\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\quad\text{for }|z|=r\leq\sqrt{5}-2\approx 0.236068.

The constants
r_{0}=\sqrt{5}-2
and
p=2(\sqrt{5}-1)
are sharp.

1.3.
Basic Notations in several complex variables

For
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
and
w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we denote
\langle z,w\rangle=z_{1}\overline{w}_{1}+\ldots+z_{n}\overline{w}_{n}
and
||z||=\sqrt{\langle z,z\rangle}
. The absolute value of a complex number
z_{1}
is denoted by
|z_{1}|
and for
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we define
||z||_{\infty}=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}|z_{i}|.
An open polydisk (or open polycylinder) in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a subset
\mathbb{P}\Delta(a;r)\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
of the form

\mathbb{P}\Delta(a;r)=\sideset{}{{}_{j=1}^{n}}{\prod}\Delta(a_{j};r_{j})=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|z_{i}-a_{i}|<r_{i},\;i=1,2,\ldots,n\},

the point
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is called the centre of the polydisk and
r=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;(r_{i}>0)
is called the polyradius. It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Delta(0;1)=\mathbb{P}\Delta(0;1_{n})=\sideset{}{{}_{j=1}^{n}}{\prod}\Delta(0_{n};1_{n}).

A multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
of dimension
n
consists of n non-negative integers
\alpha_{j},\;1\leq j\leq n
; the degree of a multi-index
\alpha
is the sum
|\alpha|=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}
and we denote
\alpha!=\alpha_{1}!\ldots\alpha_{n}!
. For
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and a multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
, we define

\displaystyle z^{\alpha}=\sideset{}{{}_{j=1}^{n}}{\prod}z_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\;\;\text{and}\;\;|z|^{\alpha}=\sideset{}{{}_{j=1}^{n}}{\prod}|z_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}.

It is natural to raise the following.

Problem 1.1
.

Can we establish the multidimensional versions of Theorems C-E?

The primary goal of this article is to provide an affirmative answer to Problem
1.1
. The paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we state the main results of this paper. In Section
3
, we prove several lemmas that will play a key role in establishing our main results. Section
4
then presents the detailed proofs of these main results.

3.
Key lemmas and proofs

We now recall here some lemmas for functions of single complex variable.

Lemma A
.

[
25
,
26
]
Let
|b_{0}|<1
and
g(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}
be analytic and satisfies
|g(z)|<1
in
\mathbb{D}
.

(a).

If
0<r\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
, then
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k|b_{k}|^{2}r^{2k}\leq r^{2}\frac{(1-|b_{0}|^{2})^{2}}{(1-|b_{0}|^{2}\;r^{2})^{2}}.

(b).

If
0<R\leq 1
, then
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|b_{k}|^{2}R^{k}\leq R\frac{(1-|b_{0}|^{2})^{2}}{1-|b_{0}|^{2}R}.

The following key lemma is established as a multidimensional analogue of parts (a) and (b) of Lemma A, serving as the foundation for the proof of our main results.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f
be a holomorphic function in the polydisk
\mathbb{P}\Delta(0;1_{n})
such that
|f(z)|\leq 1
for all
z\in\mathbb{P}\Delta(0;1_{n})

for all

and

(a).

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\sum_{|\alpha|=k}|a_{\alpha}|^{2}\;{\bf r}^{2|\alpha|}\leq{\bf r}^{2}\frac{(1-|a_{0}|^{2})^{2}}{(1-|a_{0}|^{2}\;{\bf r}^{2})^{2}},\;\mbox{for}\;0<{\bf r}\leq 1/\sqrt{2}.

(3.1)

(b).

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}||a_{\alpha}|^{2}\;{\bf r}^{|\alpha|}\leq{\bf r}\frac{(1-|a_{0}|^{2})^{2}}{1-|a_{0}|^{2}\;{\bf r}},\;\mbox{for}\;0<{\bf r}<1.

(3.2)

(c).

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}|a_{\alpha}|r^{\alpha}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}|a_{\alpha}|{\bf r}^{\alpha}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{clcr}\sqrt{n}{\bf r}\dfrac{1-|a_{0}|^{2}}{1-n|a_{0}|{\bf r}}&\text{for}\;\;|a_{0}|\geq{\bf r}\\

\displaystyle f(z)=a_{0}+P_{1}(z)+P_{2}(z)+\ldots,

For any

\displaystyle g(t)=f\left(\frac{z}{||z||_{\infty}}t\right)=a_{0}+P_{1}\left(\frac{z}{||z||_{\infty}}\right)t+P_{2}\left(\frac{z}{||z||_{\infty}}\right)t^{2}+\ldots,

(3.4)

(3.8)

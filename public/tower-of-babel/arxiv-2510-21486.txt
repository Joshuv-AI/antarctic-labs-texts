There are two fundamental ways to determine the cohomology of the constant sheaf

Depending upon the additional structures on the space

\Delta

Remark 4

The sign

comes up in the computation as the sign of the palyndromic permutation

Prelude on homological algebra

The fact that sheaf cohomology can be computed using acyclic resolutions is a standard result in homological algebra, but the relation between multiple acyclic resolutions is not usually explained; for completeness, we discuss it here, requiring us to unravel some well-known definitions.

Let
X
be a topological space and
\mathcal{F}
a sheaf of abelian groups on
X
.
Sheaf cohomology is, up to unique isomorphism, given by

\mathrm{H}^{k}(X,\mathcal{F}):=\mathrm{H}^{k}(\mathcal{I}^{*}(X)),

where
0\to\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{I}^{*}
is an injective resolution of
\mathcal{F}
. This definition gives a universal
\delta
-functor extending the global sections functor.

If we are given a merely acyclic resolution
0\to\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{A}^{*}

and using the LES in cohomology inductively one obtains the isomorphisms

\mathrm{H}^{k}(\mathcal{A}^{*}(X))\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{H}^{k}(\mathcal{I}^{*}(X))
;
see for example
[
Wed16
, Remark 10.2]
.

An equivalent way of obtaining these isomorphisms is the following. Take an injective resolution of the whole complex of sheaves

4.
A comment

The sign
(-1)^{\frac{k(k+1)}{2}}
looks like an undesirable artifact of ”chasing across diagram (
4
)” to obtain the combinatorial information in
\check{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{*}(X,\underline{\mathbb{Z}})
. This chase is not really necessary in classical topology because the same information can be found in
\mathrm{H}_{\Delta}^{*}(N\mathcal{U},\mathbb{Z})
through the most computable operation of all: evaluation.

One can thus argue that
\mathrm{H}_{\Delta}^{*}(N\mathcal{U},\mathbb{Z})
is a more natural combinatorial model than
\check{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{*}(X,\underline{\mathbb{Z}})
, apparently at the cost of additional details to keep track of, namely the homotopy
\iota
and the simplicial structure
N\mathcal{U}
. But note that because of Lemma
2
, finding a homotopy equivalence
\iota:S\to X
from a simplicial complex
S
is no harder than to find a good cover
\mathcal{U}
of
X
.
In fact, while checking wether an open cover is good, one is in some sense drawing the nerve onto the space and verifying that it is homotopic;
for topological spaces of interest such as manifolds or complex varieties,

\iota
can usually be realized as an embedding, or even a homeomorphism.

Of course, in settings like algebraic geometry, one may only have access to trivializing open covers and not the underlying topology, making the use of Čech cohomology necessary.
But in light of what has been mentioned, there’s a case to modify the definition of Čech cohomology by the palyndromic permutation with sign
(-1)^{\frac{k(k+1)}{2}}
, so that it becomes computable by evaluation in the cases where the underlying topology is available.

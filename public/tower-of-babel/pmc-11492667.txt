Drain versus no-drain at the donor sites of iliac crest bone graft: a retrospective study
Authors: Yiran Li, Shuo Yang, Hucheng Yang, Xiaodong Lian, Zhiyong Hou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundClosed suction drainage is typically used in orthopaedic surgery to reduce the occurrence of incision complications. However, whether drainage tubes should be routinely placed in iliac crest bone graft surgeries is still unclear. This study aimed to evaluate the effect of closed suction drainage at iliac crest bone donor site on clinical prognosis.Materials and methodsG.Power was used for a prior analysis to estimate the required sample size. 91 patients with tibial plateau fractures who underwent iliac crest harvesting and grafting between January 2019 and January 2022 were enrolled in the study. All surgeries were performed by the same experienced surgeon team. Patients were divided into close suction drainage (CSD) group and no-CSD group according to the use of drainage tube. The demographic and perioperative data of the patients were collected and analyzed.ResultsThere were no significant differences in demographic characteristics between the two groups except that the CSD group had more people with diabetes. The use of closed suction drainage in patients undergoing iliac crest harvest can significantly reduce the incidence of incision complications without increasing pain level and extending hospital stay. However, the closed suction drainage obviously caused the increase of blood loss. More drainage tubes would cause more drainage fluid to flow out. And drainage tube placement may impose a higher financial burden on patients.ConclusionThe closed suction drainage should not be recommended routinely for iliac crest bone graft patients. Only when the patient is at high risk for infection and without severe blood loss, placing a single drainage tube is recommended.Trial registrationThis study was registered at (NCT04807062) and approved by the Ethics Committee of the participating institution (Theoretical No. 2015-003-1).

Background

Bone grafting is used to bridge large bone defects to promote fracture healing, thereby establishing bone continuity and integrity [1]. It is also widely used in joint fusion, artificial prosthesis implantation and other surgeries [2]. In tibial plateau fractures, bone grafting mainly provides bone support to support normal joint movement [3]. Bone graft is usually obtained from the anterior part of the iliac crest because it is easier to obtain [4, 5]. Moreover, autogenous iliac bone graft also has important advantages in osteogenic conduction, osteogenic induction and osteogenic formation [621].

It has been reported that the incidence of major complications after iliac crest harvest is 0.7–25%, including infection, prolonged wound drainage time, large hematoma and other complications [4, 7]. In orthopaedic surgery, such as hip, knee or shoulder arthroplasty, fracture fixation and spinal surgery, surgeons often choose to place drainage tubes to reduce the occurrence of incision complications [8–14]. Multiple studies have shown that closed suction drainage can effectively reduce the incision swelling of the affected limb, relieve the pain of patients, and reduce wound leakage [10, 15–17]. Therefore, whether the closed suction drainage at the site of iliac bone harvest can effectively reduce the occurrence of incision complications is one of the issues we focused in this study.

Drainage tubes are widely used in orthopaedic surgery, but there are still many controversies. Recent studies have shown that the closed suction drainage after surgery did not reduce the risk of incision infection, but lead to the increased blood loss and decreased hemoglobin [18]. And the closed suction drainage may also bring potential risks such as incorrect drainage tube suture, drainage tube displacement and contamination [16]. Some studies pointed out that the closed suction drainage after acetabular fracture and femur fracture has no more profound significance, and even it should be carefully considered after knee arthroplasty and hip arthroplasty [9, 10, 12, 14, 19].

Based on previous research and clinical observation, we hypothesized that the closed suction drainage after iliac bone harvesting can effectively reduce the incidence of postoperative complications [8, 10, 16]. But its negative effects have not been effectively evaluated. At present, there are lack of specific studies demonstrating the effect of closed suction drainage at donor sites of iliac crest bone graft and whether routine closed suction drainage is necessary. Therefore, this study collected patients who underwent iliac crest bone graft and focused on the difference in efficacy caused by the closed suction drainage at the site of iliac bone harvest.

Methods

Patients

Prior to the research, G.Power was used for a prior analysis to estimate the required sample size. For the t-test of two independent samples, with effect size = 0.8, αerror = 0.05, and allocation ratio N2/N1 = 2, it is estimated that the required sample sizes are group1 = 31 and group2 = 63. For Chi-square test, when effect size = 0.5, αerror = 0.05, and difference = 1, the total sample size required is estimated to be 52. In this study, a total of 220 patients hospitalized for tibial plateau fracture in the Third Hospital of Hebei Medical University from January 2019 to January 2022 were collected randomly. This study was registered at (NCT04807062) and approved by the Ethics Committee of the participating institution (Theoretical No. 2015-003-1).

Inclusion criteria for this study include: (1) Patients who need iliac bone harvesting for tibial plateau fracture (2) Age > 18yrs (3) Not patients with pathological fractures or bone tumors (4) Complete hospital data, including imaging data and laboratory indicators. Exclusion criteria include: (1) Age < 18yrs (2) Patients with multiple fractures (3) Autoimmune diseases and blood disorders (4) Treated with other surgical methods 5.Medical record information is incomplete. Finally, 91 patients with tibial plateau fracture requiring iliac bone harvesting were included in the study. The 91 patients were divided into the close suction drainage (CSD) group (n = 60) and the non-CSD group (n = 31) according to the use of drainage tubes. The CSD group included patients with single drainage tube (n = 32) and patients with double drainage tube (n = 28). Figure 1 shows the patient inclusion process. The researchers followed up the patients two months after discharge and conducted a detailed retrospective study of the clinical data during their hospitalization.

Surgical protocol and preoperative and postoperative management

After admission, the patients underwent physical examination, X-ray, CT scan, knee MRI scan, and laboratory hematology examination to clarify the diagnosis. Confirm that the patient has no obvious contraindications to surgery, such as hemodynamic instability, mental dysfunction, or inability to tolerate surgery. Combined with the examination and laboratory indicators, the patient’s state was fully evaluated and the optimal surgical plan was formulated.

The surgery was performed by the same experienced orthopaedic trauma team. All patients underwent general anesthesia before surgery and were placed in a supine position. After the patient was fully anesthetized, an oblique incision of about 4 cm was made along the iliac crest backward from 3 cm behind the anterior superior iliac spine. The space between the gluteal aponeurosis and the external oblique abdominal muscle was separated to expose the bone harvesting site. The lateral part of the ilium and the bicortical segment including part of the iliac crest were collected using a bone chisel. Some cancellous bone can be collected at the same time if necessary. After the bone was harvested and the incision fully rinsed, the donor site was filled with absorbable haemostatic gelatine sponge (Xiangen®, China). The subcutaneous soft tissues such as the tensor fascia lata, fascia of the external oblique muscle of the abdomen, and fat layer were sutured with absorbable sutures. After reconstruction of the soft tissue plane, the drainage tube was placed or not, and the skin was sutured to close the incision. We used a unified closed suction drainage system which consists of a drainage tube with an inner diameter of 3.3 mm, a silicone balloon, and a drainage bag. The iliac crest strips were obtained for structural bone grafting (Fig. 2).

The patient underwent X-ray examination immediately after surgery to evaluate fracture reduction. In the absence of contraindications, low-molecular-weight heparin calcium was routinely injected subcutaneously 24 h after surgery to prevent venous thrombosis of the lower limbs, and antibiotics were used to prevent infection. CSD suction was administered immediately after surgery. The drainage volume was observed every 6 h in patients placed with drainage tubes. When almost no drainage fluid comes out, pull out the drainage tube and change the dressing. If the drainage tube did not exceed 50 ml/day, the drainage tube CSD was removed 48 h after surgery. Patients’ VAS scores were recorded. If the degree of pain is high, use analgesic drugs such as ketochromic acid or tramadol hydrochloride for pain relief. Encourage patients to do non-weight-bearing joint exercises as their pain subsides. According to the patient’s injury and the quality of surgery, guide the patient to carry out gradual rehabilitation exercises.

Data inclusion

The basic information of the patients were recorded: gender, age, BMI, underlying disease (hypertension, type 2 diabetes, coronary heart disease), and the side of iliac bone taken. Through retrospective investigation of patients’ surgical records and anesthesia records, perioperative data of patients were collected, including operation duration, intraoperative blood loss and blood transfusion. The hospitalization records and nursing records of enrolled patients were reviewed to record the use of drainage tubes, drainage tubes placement time and drainage volume, postoperative hospitalization duration and hospitalization cost. Meanwhile, the wound status, dressing change times, antibiotic use time and VAS visual pain score were recorded. The occurrence of congestion or hematoma was preliminarily judged by observing the tension of the incision, whether there was skin ecchymosis, and whether the skin temperature increased after operation [4]. Patients with the above obvious symptoms were examined by ultrasound to determine whether subcutaneous hematoma was formed. If the incision occurs with serious infection, hematoma and fat liquefaction, debridement or surgical evacuation of hematoma should be treated in time. After discharge, the incision healing time of the iliac bone harvesting site was obtained by telephone follow-up or patients review in the hospital outpatient clinics. The preoperative and postoperative hemoglobin, hematopoietic volume and albumin were recorded by reviewing the patient’s laboratory indicators. In order to accurately evaluate the blood loss of patients, the calculation formula of blood volume and blood loss assessment were adopted [15].

Blood volume(ml) = (k1 × height(m)3 + k2 × weight(kg) + k3) × 1000.

(For men, k1 = 0.3669, k2 = 0.03219, k3 = 0.6041; for women, k1 = 0.3561, k2 = 0.03308, k3 = 0.1833)

Hgb loss (g) = (BV × (Hgbpre− Hgbpost)) ×0.001 + Hbt Total blood loss = 1000 × Hgb loss/Hbpre.

(Hgb loss (g): the amount of Hb lost; Hgbpre (g/L): the Hgb concentration before surgery; Hgbpost (g/L): the lowest Hgb concentration after surgery; Hbt (g): the total amount of allogeneic Hb transfused).

Statistical analysis

G.Power version 3.1.9.7 (Heinrich-Heine-Universität Düsseldorf, Düsseldorf, Germany) was used for a prior analysis to estimate the required sample size. SPSS 26.0 software (IBM, Armonk, New York) was used for data analysis in this study. Chi-square test or Fisher test were used to analyze the difference between groups. The measurement index is expressed by percentages (%). Shapiro-Wilk test was used to determine whether the measurement data were normally distributed. Independent sample t test or Mann-Whitney U test were used to analyze the differences between groups. We considered the difference level between the two groups to be significant when p < 0.05. The measurement index values were represented by mean ± standard deviation (SD).

Result

Demographic and clinical data of patients

Prior to the research, G.Power was used for a prior analysis to estimate the required sample size. For the t-test of two independent samples, it is estimated that the required sample sizes are group1 = 31 and group2 = 63. For Chi-square test, the total sample size required is estimated to be 52. As shown in Fig. 1, we collected 220 patients who received iliac bone extraction and bone grafting in our hospital from January 2019 to January 2022, and then excluded them according to our exclusion conditions. Finally, a total of 91 patients were included in the study, including 50 men and 41 women. There were 34 patients with left iliac bone harvest and 57 patients with right iliac bone harvest. They were divided into the close suction drainage (CSD) group (n = 60) and the non-CSD group (n = 31) according to the use of drainage tubes. Among them, CSD group could be further divided into one drainage tube group (n = 32) and two drainage tubes group (n = 28). Our actual sample size is almost identical to our estimated sample size using G.Power. In the CSD group, 18 (30.00%) patients had hypertension, 14 (23.33%) patients had type 2 diabetes, and 33 (5.00%) patients had coronary heart disease. In the no-CSD group, 8 (25.81%) had hypertension, 1 (3.23%) had type 2 diabetes, and 1 (3.23%) had coronary heart disease. The proportion of diabetic patients in the CSD group was significantly higher than that in the no-CSD group. In addition to that, there were no significant demographic differences between the two groups (Table 1).

Perioperative indicators

Table 2 shows the perioperative clinical indicators of patients with iliac bone removal. There were no significant differences in intraoperative blood loss, blood transfusion rate and blood transfusion volume between no-CSD group and CSD group. Although the amount of intraoperative blood loss was slightly higher in the CSD group than that in the no-CSD group, there was no statistical difference between the two groups. However, the estimated blood loss in CSD group was significantly higher than that in no-CSD group (P = 0.039). In the CSD group, 3 patients had drainage tube indwelling for less than 24 h, and the shortest indwelling time was 15 h. There were 42 patients with drainage tube indwelling within 24–48 h and 15 patients with drainage tube indwelling more than 48 h. The average drainage time of CSD group patients was 46.85 ± 12.79 (h), and the average drainage volume was 120.42 ± 83.23 (ml). There were no significant differences in postoperative antibiotic duration, postoperative hospital stay, or VAS pain scores between the two groups. But the operation duration of CSD group was significantly longer than that of no-CSD group (P = 0.009), and the average operation duration of CSD group was 0.35 h longer than that of no-CSD group. The hospitalization cost of CSD group was significantly higher than that of no-CSD group (P = 0.034).

Surgical incision status of iliac bone harvest site

The incision status of the patient during hospitalization was recorded and the healing time of the incision at the bone extraction site was obtained through telephone follow-up (Table 5). The incidence of wound complications in no-CSD group was significantly higher than that in CSD group (P = 0.029). Wound complications include ecchymosis or hematoma, wound infection, and fat liquefaction. Only one patient in the no-CSD group underwent subcutaneous hematoma removal due to severe subcutaneous hematoma. In addition, the number of dressing changes in CSD group was significantly more than that in no-CSD group (P < 0.001). There was no significant difference in wound healing time between the two groups, which was 32.25 ± 16.54 (days) and 28.00 ± 14.36 (days), respectively. The wound healing time of patients without wound complications was 9.46 ± 4.04 (days) and 8.25 ± 1.89 (days), respectively.

Effect of different number of drainage tubes on clinical outcome

As shown in Table 6, when there was no significant difference in retention time between one drainage tube and two drainage tubes, more drainage fluid flowed out of the two drainage tubes (P = 0.01), and the drainage volume was almost twice that of the single drainage tube group. However, there were no statistically significant differences in blood transfusion rates, transfusion volume, estimated blood loss, hemoglobin loss, decreased hematocrit, and albumin loss between the two groups. Patients with two drainage tubes inserted had an average of 6 more dressing changes than patients with a single drainage tube inserted (P = 0.010). However, there was no significant difference in wound complication rate and wound healing time between the two groups. Most incisions showed normal conditions, whether single or two drainage tubes were placed (Fig. 3). There was no significant difference in VAS scores between the two groups.

Discussion

This study was the first to comprehensively explore the effect of closed drainage tube placement at the iliac bone harvest site on patient clinical outcomes. The most important finding was that the placement of drainage tube after ilium harvest can significantly reduce the occurrence of wound complications, but at the same time, it can also lead to increased blood loss in patients. Secondly, inserting more drainage tubes at the iliac bone harvest site did not affect the rate of wound complications but it did lead to an increase in drainage flow volume. The use of drainage tube will also generate the extension of operation time and the increase of hospitalization costs.

CSD placement has traditionally been considered to reduce surgical site infection and hematoma formation, thereby reducing incisional complications [8]. However, recent studies have shown that the use of drainage tubes sometimes does not reduce the risk of postoperative incision infection, but increases the risk of increased blood loss and decreased hemoglobin [18]. Previous studies have shown that the use of drainage tubes has different advantages and disadvantages in different surgical sites and different surgical methods. In clinical practice, the surgeon usually determines whether to insert a drainage tube according to the depth of the surgical incision, the abundance of adipose tissue at the incision site, the amount of blood loss, and the risk of infection. To some extent this depends on the subjective judgment of the surgeon. In particular, there is a lack of specific studies to show the effect of closed suction drainage at the donor site of iliac crest harvest. In this study, the effect of drainage tube placement on clinical outcomes after iliac bone harvesting was focused and investigated.

The current study focused on incision complications at the site of iliac ilium bone harvest, including hematoma, infection, fat liquefaction, and wound healing time. Closed drainage tubes are widely used in orthopaedic surgery to prevent incision complications. And the insertion of a drainage tube at the bone harvest site is also no exception. Although SASSO et al. ‘s study showed that routine placement of drainage tube at the site of bone harvest had no significant effect on the recovery of the incision [22]. However, this study is relatively old and has certain limitations. The incidence of postoperative incision complications was generally higher than today, which may be caused by poor aseptic conditions and inadequate postoperative management at that time. This may be an important factor that is different from the conclusion of this study. The formation of postoperative hematoma provides a medium for bacterial colonization and proliferation. Hematoma can impair tissue perfusion, prevent wound healing, and encourage bacterial proliferation [12]. In the current clinical application, drainage tube is still widely used after iliac bone removal to prevent hematoma formation [4, 20, 23]. This study analyzed the advantages and disadvantages of the placement of drainage tubes after iliac crest harvest, including the impact on incision complications and blood loss.

This research found that the number of patients with incision complications after drainage tube placement at the iliac bone harvesting site was significantly less than that of patients without drainage tube placement. In addition, the CSD group had a higher proportion of patients with diabetes but a lower rate of incision complications. According to previous studies, diabetic patients are much more likely to have surgical incision complications than normal healthy people [24], so orthopaedic surgeons are more inclined to place drainage tubes for such patients during surgery to avoid wound complications and reduce the chance of infection. The outcomes suggested that CSD is effective in reducing wound complications. Under the premise of no significant difference in antibiotic use, the incidence of incision complications in patients with CSD was significantly lower than that in patients without CSD. In both the CSD group and the no-CSD group, incision healing time was much longer in patients with incision complications than in patients without complications. However, in patients without complications, the healing time of CSD group was not significantly different from that of no-CSD group. This indicates that the placement of drainage tube can only reduce the incidence of incision complications, but can not shorten the time of wound healing. In addition, the results showed that the times of incisional dressing change during hospitalization was significantly more in the CSD group than in no-CSD group. It should be noted that there was no significant difference in the incidence of incision complications between patients using a single drainage tube and those using double drainage tubes. For patients with more intraoperative incision bleeding, poor skin condition, and a higher risk of incision complications, the surgeon attempts to use two drainage tubes to get adequate drainage and avoid incision deterioration. However, the results showed that placing more drainage tubes did not provide any benefit other than increased drainage flow. The results also showed that patients with two drainage tubes inserted had more dressing changes than patients with a single drainage tube inserted. According to the clinical observation, the increase in dressing changes of patients with CSD mainly occurred after the removal of drainage tubes. After the drainage tube was removed, there would still be fluid seepage in the small incision through which the original drainage tube inserted. In patients with two drainage tubes inserted, there will be two channels after the drainage tube is removed, which also causes more fluid seepage.

Blood loss is an unavoidable problem during surgery. As shown in the results of this study, hemoglobin, hematocrit and albumin in both CSD group and no-CSD group after surgery showed a significant decrease compared with those before surgery. Some studies have shown that the use of drainage tubes in some orthopaedic surgeries can cause increased blood loss. Adam et al. found that postoperative use of drainage tubes in patients with acetabular fractures significantly improved blood transfusion rates [9]. Similarly, Hong Xu et al. found that the insertion of drainage tubes after knee arthroplasty surgery can improve the blood transfusion rate and prolong the hospital stay of patients [14]. Veronique et al. found that in patients undergoing hip hemiarthroplasty for femoral neck fractures that drainage at the surgical site resulted in more hemoglobin loss and more time to prepare for discharge [17]. But Johansson et al. found that drainage tube placement did not affect blood loss in patients undergoing total hip arthroplasty [25]. The effects of drainage tube use on blood loss and blood indexes are still controversial. Different researchers have shown different results on the placement of drainage tubes after various fracture surgeries, which is summarized in detail in Parker review [8]. The different results on the effect of drainage tube on blood loss may due to the heterogeneity of different fracture sites, different surgical methods, and different surgical teams. And the use of hemostatic agents such as tranexamic acid, bone wax, gelatin sponge and other hemostatic materials can also affect blood loss [20, 23, 26]. In this study, haemostatic gelatine sponge was placed at the site of iliac crest bone havest in all patients to reduce bleeding on the traumatized bone surface and incision. There is no relevant study on the effect of drainage tube placement at the site of iliac bone harvest on blood loss. SASSO et al. ‘s study focused on the effect of drainage tube placement on postoperative incision complications at the site of iliac bone extraction, but paid little attention to the blood loss of patients [22]. This study complements this deficiency. In this study, the calculation formula of blood volume and blood loss assessment [15, 27] were used to assess patients’ blood volume and blood loss during hospitalization. The results showed that there was no significant difference in the estimated blood volume between CSD group and no-CSD group, but the estimated blood loss in CSD group was more. And through comparative analysis of the hematological indexes of the two groups, it showed that the CSD group had significantly more hemoglobin loss, hematocrit reduction and albumin loss. It is well known that an increase in perioperative blood loss is often accompanied by an increase in transfusion rates. However, in this study there was no significant difference in blood transfusion rates between the two groups. This may be due to the limited trauma caused by iliac bone removal compared to acetabular fracture surgery or hip arthroplasty and although the use of the drainage tube caused more blood loss, it did not affect the patient’s blood volume enough to reach the point of requiring transfusion. According to further research, although more drainage fluid will be extracted by placing two drainage tubes compared with placing a single drainage tube, it does not affect the hematological indexes of patients. There were no significant differences in estimated blood loss, hemoglobin loss, hematocrit reduction and albumin loss between one drainage tube group and two drainage tubes group.

In this study, the absence of a significant difference in VAS pain scores between the CSD group and the no-CSD group suggests that drainage does not reduce pain feeling. Drainage tube placement did not prolong or shorten the length of postoperative hospital stay. This is consistent with the results of previous studies related to orthopaedic surgical drainage tube placement [14]. However, drainage tube placement may impose a higher financial burden on patients. The average hospitalization cost of the CSD group was about 6000RMB higher than that of the no-CSD group, part of which may be caused by the cost of drainage tube placement and postoperative drainage tube care.

This study has some potential limitations. In the first place, it is a retrospective study with some relevant bias. Secondly, this study mainly focused on the effect of closed suction drainage on the clinical prognosis of patients with iliac bone extraction, the risk factors of patients with incision complications could not be fully revealed. We will then conduct a comprehensive analysis of the risk factors for complications after iliac crest harvest. Although all steps of this study were carried out by the same team of surgeons and unified standards and procedures were formulated, the occurrence of incision complications, such as congestion or hematoma, to a certain extent depended on doctors’ observation and subjective judgment. In addition, all patients enrolled in our study used hemostatic gel sponge for wound hemostasis. The effect of the use of hemostatic materials on incisional complications was not fully mentioned in this study. This will be studied in a further comprehensive risk factor analysis.

Conclusion

Considering the benefits and disadvantages, drainage tubes should not be routinely recommended for patients with iliac bone harvest. Drainage tubes should be used cautiously only when the patient is at high risk for infection and the patient condition permits. If a drainage tube is needed at the incision site, a single drainage tube is recommend, because more drainage tubes do not provide more benefit.

Geometric analysis of a class of harmonic mappings defined by a differential Inequality
Vasudevarao Allu, Raju Biswas and Rajib Mandal

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduces and undertake as a systematical investigation of the class 𝒫 ℋ 0 ​ ( α , M ) \mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M) of normalized harmonic mappings f = h + g ¯ f=h+\overline{g} in the unit disk 𝔻 \mathbb{D} , defined by the differential inequality Re ​ ( ( 1 − α ) ​ h ′ ​ ( z ) + α ​ z ​ h ′′ ​ ( z ) ) > − M + | ( 1 − α ) ​ g ′ ​ ( z ) + α ​ z ​ g ′′ ​ ( z ) | for z ∈ 𝔻 , \displaystyle\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)>-M+\left|(1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|\quad\text{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{D}, where M > 0 M>0 , α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \alpha\in(0,1] , and g ′ ​ ( 0 ) = 0 g^{\prime}(0)=0 . This class extends the harmonic analogue of functions with positive real part and offers a unified framework for analyzing their geometric characteristics. We obtain sharp coefficient bounds for both the analytic and co-analytic parts, establish sharp growth bounds, and determine the radii of univalency, starlikeness, and convexity. Furthermore, we show that 𝒫 ℋ 0 ​ ( α , M ) \mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M) is closed under convex combinations, and under suitable restrictions on the parameters, it is also closed under convolution. Our findings generalize and extend several known results in the theory of harmonic mappings.

1.
Introduction and Preliminary Results

The application of harmonic mappings has become a valuable tool in the study of fluid flow problems (see
[
1
]
). Moreover, univalent harmonic functions that are characterized by geometric properties such as convexity, starlikeness, and close-to-convexity emerge naturally in the context of planar fluid dynamics problems.

Let
f=u+iv
be a complex-valued function of
z=x+iy
in a simply connected domain
\Omega
. If
f\in C^{2}(\Omega)
(continuous first and second partial derivatives exist in
\Omega
) and satisfies the Laplace equation
\Delta f=4f_{z\overline{z}}=0
in
\Omega
,
then
f
is said to be harmonic in
\Omega
. Note that every harmonic mapping
f
has the canonical representation
f=h+\overline{g}
, where
h
and

g
are analytic in
\Omega
, known respectively as the analytic and co-analytic parts of
f
. This representation is
unique up to an additive constant (see
[
10
]
). The inverse function theorem and a result of Lewy
[
16
]
shows that a harmonic function
f
is locally univalent in

\Omega
if, and only if, the Jacobian of
f
, defined by
J_{f}(z):=|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}-|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}
is non-zero in
\Omega
.
A harmonic mapping
f
is locally univalent and sense-preserving in
\Omega
if, and only if,
J_{f}(z)>0
in
\Omega
. Equivalently, this condition holds if
h^{\prime}\neq 0
in
\Omega
and the dilatation of
f
, defined as
\omega_{f}:=\omega=g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}
, satisfies
|\omega_{f}|<1
in
\Omega
(see
[
16
]
).
Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the class of all complex-valued harmonic functions
f=h+\overline{g}
defined in
\mathbb{D}
, where
h
and
g
are analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with the normalization

h(0)=h^{\prime}(0)-1=0
and
g(0)=0
. If the co-analytic part
g(z)\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{D}
, then the class
\mathcal{H}
reduces to the class
\mathcal{A}
of analytic functions in

\mathbb{D}
with
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)-1=0
.
Let
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
denote the class of functions
f\in\mathcal{H}
that are both sense-preserving and univalent in
\mathbb{D}
and let

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}=\left\{f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}:g^{\prime}(0)=0\right\}
. Every
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
has the following form:

\displaystyle f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}.

(1.1)

If the co-analytic part
g(z)\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{D}
, then both the classes
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
reduces to the class
\mathcal{S}
of
univalent and analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
with
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)-1=0
.
Both
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
are natural harmonic
generalizations of
\mathcal{S}
, but only
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
is compact although both the classes
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
are normal. In 1984, Clunie and Sheil-Small
[
8
]
undertook a comprehensive study of the class
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
and its geometric subclasses. This study
has subsequently garnered extensive attention from researchers (see
[
6
,
13
,
25
,
18
,
4
,
2
,
5
]
).

A domain
\Omega
is called starlike with respect to a point
z_{0}\in\Omega
if the closed line segment joining
z_{0}
to any point in
\Omega
lies in
\Omega
,
i.e.
,

(1-t)z_{0}+tz\in\Omega
for
t\in[0,1]
and
z\in\Omega
. In particular, if
z_{0}=0
, then
\Omega
is simply called starlike. A complex-valued harmonic mapping

f\in\mathcal{H}
is said to be starlike if
f(\mathbb{D})
is starlike.
Let
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
denote the class of harmonic starlike functions in
\mathbb{D}
. A domain
\Omega
is called convex if it is starlike with respect to any point
in
\Omega
. In other words, convexity implies starlikeness, but the converse is not necessarily true. A domain can be starlike
without being convex. A function
f\in\mathcal{H}
is said to be convex if
f(\mathbb{D})
is convex. Let
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{H}}
denote the class of harmonic convex
mappings in
\mathbb{D}
. A domain
\Omega
is said to be close-to-convex if the complement of
\Omega
can be expressed as a union of closed half-lines with the condition that the corresponding open half-lines are disjoint. A function
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
which maps the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
onto a close-to-convex domain is called a close-to-convex function. The following definitions are essential for a comprehensive understanding of this paper.

Definition 1
.

[
9
,
23
]
Let
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
be two analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
given by
\psi_{1}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
\psi_{2}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
. The convolution (or, Hadamard product) is defined by
\left(\psi_{1}*\psi_{2}\right)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}b_{n}z^{n}~\text{for}~z\in\mathbb{D}
.

Definition 2
.

[
12
]
For harmonic functions
f_{1}=h_{1}+\overline{g_{1}}
and
f_{2}=h_{2}+\overline{g_{2}}
in
\mathcal{H}
, the convolution is defined as
f_{1}*f_{2}=h_{1}*h_{2}+\overline{g_{1}*g_{2}}
.

Definition 3
.

[
23
]
A sequence
\{a_{n}\}
of non-negative numbers is said to be a convex null sequence if
a_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
and
a_{0}-a_{1}\geq a_{1}-a_{2}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n-1}-a_{n}\geq\cdots\geq 0
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the class of analytic functions
h
in
\mathbb{D}
that satisfy the condition
\textrm{Re}\left(h^{\prime}(z)\right)>0
in
\mathbb{D}
with
h(0)=h^{\prime}(0)-1=0
.
It is well-known that
\mathcal{P}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}
.
MacGregor
[
17
]
proved that each partial sum
s_{n}(h)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}z^{k}
(
n\geq 2
) of the function
h(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in\mathcal{P}
maintained its univalence within the disk
|z|<1/2
. Furthermore,
h(z)
maps the disk
|z|<\sqrt{2}-1
onto a convex domain. The numbers
1/2
and
\sqrt{2}-1
are best possible constants. Afterwards, Singh
[
22
]
proved that each partial sum
s_{n}(h)
of the function
h(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in\mathcal{P}
is convex in the disk
|z|<1/4
. The number
1/4
is the best possible constant.

Ponnusamy
et al.

[
20
]
have studied the following class as a harmonic analogue of the class
\mathcal{P}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}:=\left\{f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{H}:\textrm{Re}\left(h^{\prime}(z)\right)>|g^{\prime}(z)|\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{D}\right\}

and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}:=\left\{f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}:g^{\prime}(0)=0\right\}
. The authors
[
20
]
proved that functions in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}
are close-to-convex in
\mathbb{D}
. Li and Ponnusamy
[
14
,
15
]
have investigated the radius of univalency and convexity of sections of functions
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
.

In 2020, Ghosh and Allu
[
11
]
introduced the class
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(M)
for
M>0
, of all functions
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{H}
satisfying the following conditions:

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left(zh^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)>-M+|zg^{\prime\prime}(z)|~\text{with}~g^{\prime}(0)=0~\text{for}~z\in\mathbb{D}

and established the following results regarding the sharp coefficient bounds and growth results for functions in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(M)
.

Theorem A
.

[
11
]
Let
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(M)
for
M>0
be of the form (
1.1
). Then, for
n\geq 2
,
|b_{n}|\leq 2M/(n(n-1))
. The result is sharp for the function
f(z)=z-M\overline{z}^{n}/(n(n-1))
.

Theorem B
.

[
11
]

Let
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(M)
for
M>0
be of the form (
1.1
). Then, for
n\geq 2
,
(i)
|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\leq\frac{2M}{n(n-1)}
; (ii)
\left||a_{n}|-|b_{n}|\right|\leq\frac{2M}{n(n-1)}
;
(iii)
|a_{n}|\leq\frac{2M}{n(n-1)}
.
The results are sharp for the function
f
given by
f^{\prime}(z)=1-2M\ln(1-z)
.

Theorem C
.

[
11
]
Let
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(M)
for
M>0
be of the form (
1.1
). Then

\displaystyle|z|-2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{|z|^{n}}{n(n-1)}\leq|f(z)|\leq|z|+2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{|z|^{n}}{n(n-1)}.

The right-hand inequality is sharp for the function
f
given by
f^{\prime}(z)=1-2M\ln(1-z)
.

Motivated by the results of Ghosh and Allu
[
11
]
, we consider the class
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
of all functions
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{H}
satisfying the following conditions:

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)>-M+\left|(1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|~\text{and}~g^{\prime}(0)=0

for
M>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

The parameter
\alpha\in(0,1]
in the definition of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
provides a natural connection between different geometric behaviours. The operator
(1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)
can be interpreted geometrically as a linear combination of the derivative (related to starlikeness) and the second-order operator
zh^{\prime\prime}(z)
(related to convexity). When
\alpha=1
, we recover the class
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(M)
studied by Ghosh and Allu
[
11
]
, whereas varying
\alpha
allows us to investigate a continuous family of classes that connect different geometric properties. This generalization provides a more in-depth understanding of how the local behaviour of harmonic mappings, determined by their derivatives, affects their global geometric properties, such as univalency, starlikeness, and convexity.

The organization of this paper is:
In section
2
, we establish the sharp coefficients bounds and growth estimate for functions in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
. Furthermore,
we conclude the section by determining the radii of univalency, starlikeness, and convexity for functions in the class
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
.
Section
3
is devoted to the structural properties of the class
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
, specifically under convex combinations and various convolution operations.

2.
The coefficient bounds, growth estimate and Geometric Radii

In the following result, we establish the sharp coefficient bounds for the co-analytic part of functions in the class
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
be of the form (
1.1
) for
M>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. For
n\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle|b_{n}|\leq\frac{M-\alpha+1}{n+\alpha n(n-2)}.

The result is sharp for the function
f
given by
f(z)=z+\frac{M-\alpha+1}{n+\alpha n(n-2)}\overline{z^{n}}
.

Proof.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\left|(1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|<M+\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)\;\;\text{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

(2.1)

Since
(1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(n+\alpha n(n-2)\right)b_{n}z^{n-1}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
, in view of the Cauchy’s integral formula for derivatives, we have

\displaystyle\left(n+\alpha n(n-2)\right)b_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=r}\frac{(1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)}{z^{n}}\;dz\quad\text{for any}\quad r<1.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\left(n+\alpha n(n-2)\right)|b_{n}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{(1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})+\alpha re^{i\theta}g^{\prime\prime}(re^{i\theta})}{r^{n}e^{in\theta}}ire^{i\theta}d\theta\right|

(2.2)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\left|(1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})+\alpha re^{i\theta}g^{\prime\prime}(re^{i\theta})\right|}{r^{n-1}}d\theta.

By the Mean value property (see
[
19
,
10
]
), if
\psi
is analytic in a domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}
containing the closed disk
|z-z_{0}|\leq\rho
, then
\text{Re}(\psi)
satisfies:

\displaystyle\text{Re}(\psi(z_{0}))=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\text{Re}(\psi(z_{0}+\rho e^{i\theta}))\;d\theta.

(2.3)

Using (
2.1
) to (
2.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(n+\alpha n(n-2)\right)r^{n-1}|b_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(M+\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})+\alpha re^{i\theta}h^{\prime\prime}(re^{i\theta})\right)\right)\;d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})+\alpha re^{i\theta}h^{\prime\prime}(re^{i\theta})\right)\;d\theta.

Applying the mean value property (
2.3
) to the analytic function
(1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)=(1-\alpha)+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n+\alpha n(n-2))a_{n}z^{n-1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})+\alpha re^{i\theta}h^{\prime\prime}(re^{i\theta})\right)d\theta=1-\alpha.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left(n+\alpha n(n-2)\right)r^{n-1}|b_{n}|\leq M+(1-\alpha)=M-\alpha+1.

Letting
r\to 1^{-}
gives the desired bound.

To prove the sharpness of the result, we consider the function

f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=z+\frac{M-\alpha+1}{n+\alpha n(n-2)}\overline{z^{n}}\quad\text{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{D}\quad\text{and}\quad n\geq 2.

Therefore,
h(z)=z
and
g(z)=(M-\alpha+1)/(n+\alpha n(n-2))z^{n}
. Then, we obtain

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)=1-\alpha\quad\text{and}\quad(1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)=(M-\alpha+1)z^{n-1}.

For
n\geq 2
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
, it is evident that

\displaystyle-M+\left|(1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-M+(M-\alpha+1)|z|^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)|z|^{n-1}+M\left(|z|^{n-1}-1\right)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 1-\alpha=\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)\right),

which shows that
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
and
|b_{n}(f)|=(M-\alpha+1)/(n+\alpha n(n-2))
.
This completes the proof.∎

Let
\mathcal{P}(\alpha,M)
denote the class of functions
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
that satisfy the following condition:

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)\phi^{\prime}(z)+\alpha z\phi^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)>-M,~\text{where}~M>0,\alpha\in(0,1]~\text{and}~z\in\mathbb{D}.

In the following result, we establish a correlation between the functions in
\mathcal{P}(\alpha,M)
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
.

Theorem 2.2
.

A harmonic mapping
f=h+\overline{g}
belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
if, and only if, the
function
F_{\varepsilon}=h+\varepsilon g\in\mathcal{P}(\alpha,M
) for each
\varepsilon

(|\varepsilon|=1)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)>-M+\left|(1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|\;\text{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

(2.4)

Fix
|\varepsilon|=1
. Since
F_{\varepsilon}=h+\varepsilon g
, we deduce by using (
2.4
) that

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zF_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left(\left((1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\varepsilon\left((1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)

\displaystyle-\left|(1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle-M\quad\text{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Therefore,
F_{\varepsilon}=h+\varepsilon g\in\mathcal{P}(\alpha,M)
for each
\varepsilon
with
|\varepsilon|=1
.
Conversely, if
F_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{P}(\alpha,M)
, then for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

\displaystyle\qquad\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zF_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)>-M,

\displaystyle\text{\it i.e.,}\quad\text{Re}\left(\left((1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)+\varepsilon\left((1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)\right)>-M,

\displaystyle\text{\it i.e.,}\quad\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)>-M+\text{Re}\left(-\varepsilon\left((1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)\right).

Let
p(z)=(1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)
and write
p(z)=|p(z)|e^{i\theta(z)}
. Since
|\varepsilon|=1
, we can choose
\varepsilon=-e^{-i\theta(z)}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle-\varepsilon p(z)=e^{-i\theta(z)}\cdot|p(z)|e^{i\theta(z)}=|p(z)|,

which shows that
\text{Re}(-\varepsilon p(z))=|p(z)|
. Substituting this specific
\varepsilon
into the inequality gives

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)>-M+\left|(1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|\quad\text{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

which shows that
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
. This completes the proof.∎

In the following result, we establish the sharp coefficient bounds for functions in the class
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
be of the form (
1.1
) for
M>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. For
n\geq 2
, we have

(i)

|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\leq\dfrac{2(M-\alpha+1)}{n+\alpha n(n-2)}
;

(ii)

\left||a_{n}|-|b_{n}|\right|\leq\dfrac{2(M-\alpha+1)}{n+\alpha n(n-2)}
;

(iii)

|a_{n}|\leq\dfrac{2(M-\alpha+1)}{n+\alpha n(n-2)}
.

The results are sharp for the function
f
given by
f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2(M-\alpha+1)}{n+\alpha n(n-2)}z^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
. In view of Theorem
2.2
, we have
F_{\varepsilon}=h+\varepsilon g\in\mathcal{P}(\alpha,M)
for each
\varepsilon

(|\varepsilon|=1)
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zF_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(z)+M\right)>0\quad\text{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Thus there exists an analytic function
p(z)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
with
\text{Re}\;p(z)>0
in
\mathbb{D}
and
p(0)=1
such that

\displaystyle\frac{(1-\alpha)F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zF_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(z)+M}{M-\alpha+1}=p(z)

i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{(M-\alpha+1)+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(n+\alpha(n^{2}-2n)\right)\left(a_{n}+\varepsilon b_{n}\right)z^{n-1}}{M-\alpha+1}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{n}z^{n}

i.e.,

\displaystyle|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\leq\frac{2(M-\alpha+1)}{n+\alpha(n^{2}-2n)},||a_{n}|-|b_{n}||\leq\frac{2(M-\alpha+1)}{n+\alpha(n^{2}-2n)}\;\text{and}\;|a_{n}|\leq\frac{2(M-\alpha+1)}{n+\alpha(n^{2}-2n)}.

Corollary 2.1

Proof.

\displaystyle\textrm{Re}\left((1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n+\alpha n(n-2))|a_{n}|.

\displaystyle-M+\left|\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n+\alpha n(n-2))b_{n}z^{n-1}\right|

\displaystyle=

3.
Structural Properties: Convex Combinations and Convolutions

In the following result, we establish that the class
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
is invariant under convex combinations.

Theorem 3.1
.

The class
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
is closed under convex combinations.

Proof.

Let
f_{i}=h_{i}+\overline{g_{i}}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}=1
, where
0\leq t_{i}\leq 1
for each
i
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)h_{i}^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh_{i}^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)>-M+\left|(1-\alpha)h_{i}^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg_{i}^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|

with
h_{i}(0)=g_{i}(0)=h_{i}^{\prime}(0)-1=g_{i}^{\prime}(0)=0
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
.
The convex combination of the
f_{i}
’s can be written as
f(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}f_{i}(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}
,
where
h(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}h_{i}(z)
and
g(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}g_{i}(z)
. It is evident that both
h
and
g
are analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with
h(0)=g(0)=h^{\prime}(0)-1=g^{\prime}(0)=0
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}h_{i}^{\prime}(z)+\alpha z\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}h_{i}^{\prime\prime}\right)\hskip 56.9055pt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\left((1-\alpha)h_{i}^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh_{i}^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)\right)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\left(-M+\left|(1-\alpha)g_{i}^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg_{i}^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-M+\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\left|(1-\alpha)g_{i}^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg_{i}^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-M+\left|(1-\alpha)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}g_{i}^{\prime}(z)\right)+\alpha z\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}g_{i}^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-M+\left|(1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|,

which shows that
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
. This completes the proof.∎

Before we proceed to the convolution theorem for the harmonic class, we first establish an essential structural property of the related analytic class
\mathcal{P}(\alpha,M)
, namely its convexity.

Lemma 3.1
.

The class
\mathcal{P}(\alpha,M)
is closed under convex combinations.

Proof.

Let
F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{n}\in\mathcal{P}(\alpha,M)
. Consider the convex combination
F=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}F_{i}
, where
t_{i}\geq 0
for each
i
with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}=1
.
Thus, for each
i
, we have

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)F_{i}^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zF_{i}^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)>-M\quad\text{for }\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Therefore.

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)F^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zF^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\left((1-\alpha)F_{i}^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zF_{i}^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\text{Re}\left((1-\alpha)F_{i}^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zF_{i}^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}(-M)=-M,

which shows that

. This completes the proof.

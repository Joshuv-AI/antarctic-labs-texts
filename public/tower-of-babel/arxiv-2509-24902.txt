Local and global Langlands conjecture(s) over function fields
Dennis Gaitsgory

Abstract




Write-up of the plenary talk at ICM, 2026

Introduction

0.1.
What is this paper about?

The goal of this paper is to propose a set of conjectures whose aim is to answer the basic question of the
Langlands program (over function fields): how to describe the space of automorphic functions in terms of the spectral side (i.e., Langlands parameters)?

The approach we take can be summarized as the following three steps:

•

Formulate (a higher-categorical) geometric statement;

•

Apply the operation of (higher-categorical) trace of Frobenius, and identify the result
with the classical object of interest;

•

Deduce the sought-for description for the classical object.

1.
The global unramified case

In this section we review the global unramified geometric Langlands conjecture and
its application to the theory of automorphic functions in the global unramified case
over function fields.

1.1.
The players

1.1.1.

Let
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
denote the moduli stack of
G
-bundles on
X
. In the geometric theory
our main object of study is the category
\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
, the derived category of

\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}
-adic sheaves on
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
.

Here are our two goals:

(1)

Relate
\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
to the space of unramified automorphic functions (when
k=\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
,
but
G
and
X
are defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
);

(2)

Relate

to the

to the “space” of
{\check{G}}
-local systems on
X

{\check{G}}
is the Langlands-dual of

\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}=:\mathsf{e}

If we could achieve these two goals, we could hope to relate the space of automorphic functions
to
{\check{G}}
-local systems, thereby realizing Langlands’ vision.

Here by the space of (unramified, compactly supported,
\mathsf{e}
-valued) automorphic functions we mean the vector space

\operatorname{Autom}(X,G):=\operatorname{Funct}_{c}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})).

1.1.2.

There is an “obvious” strategy of how one may tackle goal (1) above. Namely, since
G
and
X

are defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
, and hence it carries an endomorphism, given by

\operatorname{Frob}

This endomorphism induces an endofunctor

\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_{*},\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}))\in\operatorname{Vect},

see Sect.
A.2

More abstractly, let
{\mathcal{Y}}
be an algebraic stack over
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
but defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

If

where

When

On the other hand, to the pair

1.2.
The trace isomorphism

In this subsection we explain how the subcategory
\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
helps us achieve goal (a) in Sect.
1.1.1
.

1.2.1.

Consider the embedding (
1.3
). It was conjectured in
[
AGKRRV1
]
and subsequently proved in

[
GR
]
that it admits a (colimit-preserving) right adjoint. Hence, by Sect.
A.4
, it induces a map

\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_{*},\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}))\to\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_{*},\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})).

1.2.2.

The following result was established in
[
AGKRRV3
, Theorems 0.2.6 and 0.6.8]
:

Theorem 1.2.3
.

The composition

\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_{*},\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}))\to\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_{*},\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}))\overset{\operatorname{LT}^{\operatorname{true}}}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Funct}_{c}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}))=:\operatorname{Autom}(X,G)

is an isomorphism.

Remark 1.2.4
.

Theorem
1.2.3
is powerful in that it establishes a direct connection between the geometric and classical theories: if we prove something interesting
about the category
\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
, by taking the trace of Frobenius, we might be able to deduce interesting consequences for the space
of automorphic functions. An example of this–the geometric Langlands equivalence–will be discussed in Sect.
1.4
.

is the prestack of
\phi
-fixed points on
{\mathcal{Y}}
.

Let now
{\mathcal{Y}}
be a prestack locally almost of finite type; in this case, the category
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})
is well-defined.
Assume that
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})
is dualizable. Then a parallel computation shows that there is a canonical
isomorphism

(1.6)

\operatorname{Tr}(\phi_{*},{\operatorname{IndCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}}))\simeq\Gamma({\mathcal{Y}}^{\phi},\omega_{{\mathcal{Y}}^{\phi}}),

where
\omega_{\mathcal{Z}}
is the dualizing sheaf on
{\mathcal{Z}}

1.5.3.

Denote

\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{arithm}}(X):=(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{restr}}(X))^{\operatorname{Frob}}.

We stipulate that
\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{arithm}}(X)
to be the stack of arithmetic
{\check{G}}
-local systems on
X

By definition its
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}
-points are
{\check{G}}
-local systems on
X

It is shown in
[
AGKRRV1
, Sect. Theorem 24.1.4]
that the prestack
\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{arithm}}(X)
is actually a
quasi-compact algebraic stack.

Remark 1.5.4
.

Note that whereas
\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{restr}}(X)
is quasi-smooth, the stack
\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{arithm}}(X)
is quasi-quasi-smooth

9

The terminology was

have cohomological amplitude

(the positive direction is due to stackiness, and the negative is due to the singularities).

In particular, it is
not

negative cohomological degrees.

1.5.5.

By (

(1.7)

\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_{*},{\operatorname{IndCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{restr}}(X))\simeq\Gamma(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{arithm}}(X),\omega_{\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{arithm}}(X)}).

In addition, we have:

Proposition 1.5.6

(GR, Theorem 1.5.2, quoting

.

The inclusion

{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{restr}}(X))\hookrightarrow{\operatorname{IndCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{restr}}(X))

\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_{*},-)

Combining (

(1.8)

\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_{*},{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{restr}}(X))\simeq\Gamma(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{arithm}}(X),\omega_{\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{arithm}}(X)}).

1.5.7.

We now combine (

Corollary 1.5.8
.

There exists a canonical isomorphism

\operatorname{Autom}(X,G)\simeq\Gamma(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{arithm}}(X),\omega_{\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{arithm}}(X)}).

The isomorphism of Corollary

can be viewed as an answer to Langlands’ original question as
to the relationship between automorphic functions and
{\check{G}}
-local systems (in the unramified case).

In his talk, Sam Raskin will explain how to use Corollary

to extract some desired information
about the space of automorphic functions, e.g., the Ramanujan conjecture

10

This is work-in-progress of
S. Raskin, V. Lafforgue and the author.
(in the unramified situation).

Remark 1.5.9
.

Note that, being the stack of fixed points,
\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{arithm}}(X)
is canonically Calabi-Yau. This
implies that over the quasi-smooth locus of
\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{arithm}}(X)

However, the two are wildly different over the non quasi-smooth locus. In fact, the difference between

2.
The local theory

The contents of this section constitute joint work-in-progress, joint with S. Raskin, and some parts are joint
also with other groups of mathematicians, to be named individually in the relevant subsections.

We will propose a set of conjectures about the
local
Langlands theory. There will be three such main conjectures:

•

A 2-categorical local equivalence, Conjecture
2.6.10
(loosely, a counterpart of Conjecture
1.4.4
);

•

A local trace conjecture, Conjecture
2.5.12
(loosely, a counterpart of Theorem
1.2.3
);

•

A 1-categorical local equivalence, Conjecture
2.7.9
(loosely, a counterpart of Corollary
1.5.8
).

The last conjecture is a formal consequence of the previous two, has been first proposed by X. Zhu as
[
Zhu1
, Conjecture 4.6.4]
.
and is closely related to the conjecture of L. Fargues and P. Scholze,
[
FS
, Conjecture I.10.2]
.

We should also remark that the conjectures stated in this section seem quite tractable (and may already be theorems), mostly
thanks to a recent progress achieved in
[
DVY
]
.

2.1.
Categorical representations of groups–first attempt

2.1.1.

Note that in the passage “global”
\Rightarrow
“local” in the classical theory, one raises the level
of categoricity by one:

Globally we study the
vector space
of automorphic functions (with the various operators acting on it),
whereas locally we study the
category
of representations of a given
p
-adic group (or an enhanced
version of that–the category introduced by Fargues-Scholze in
[
FS
]
).

Note also that the passage “classical”
\Rightarrow
“geometric” also raises the level of categoricity by one:

In the global theory, classically we study the
vector space
of automorphic functions, whereas geometrically
we study the
category
of sheaves on
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
(or variants thereof).

Thus, both of the above patterns show that the object of study the local geometric theory should be a
2-category
.
We stipulate that the 2-category in question should be that of
categorical representations of the loop group

{\mathfrak{L}}(G):=G(\!(t)\!)
.
Over the next few subsections we will explain what we mean by this.

We start with the case when, instead of the loop group, we are dealing with an algebraic group
H
of finite type.

2.1.2.

Let us first place ourselves in the context of D-modules. In this case, we consider the category
\operatorname{D-mod}(H)
, which has
a natural structure of
commutative Hopf category
, i.e., this is a commutative Hopf algebra object in the
symmetric monoidal 2-category
\operatorname{DGCat}
(see
[
GaRo
, Chapter 1, Sect. 10]
):

The commutative algebra structure is given by
pointwise tensor product
, and the coalgebra structure is given by pullback
along the multiplication map

\operatorname{D-mod}(H)\overset{\operatorname{mult}^{!}}{\to}\operatorname{D-mod}(H\times H).

Here we are using a crucial fact that for a scheme
Y
(and in fact for any reasonable prestack), the functor

(2.1)

\operatorname{D-mod}(Y)\otimes\operatorname{D-mod}(Y^{\prime})\overset{\boxtimes}{\to}\operatorname{D-mod}(Y\times Y^{\prime})

given by
external tensor product
, is an equivalence for any prestack
Y^{\prime}
.
In what follows, we will refer to (
2.1
)
as the
categorical Künneth formula
.

Given the above structure on
\operatorname{D-mod}(H)
, we define
H\operatorname{-Cat}
to be the 2-category

\operatorname{D-mod}(H){\operatorname{-comod}}(\operatorname{DGCat}).

The commutative Hopf algebra structure on
\operatorname{D-mod}(H)
defines on
H\operatorname{-Cat}
a symmetric monoidal
structure.

Remark 2.1.3
.

As is the case for any scheme of finite type, Verdier duality makes

\operatorname{D-mod}(H)
self-dual as an object of
\operatorname{DGCat}
. Hence, we can regard
\operatorname{D-mod}(H)

also as a
cocommutative Hopf algebra
in
\operatorname{DGCat}
.

The 2-category
H\operatorname{-Cat}
can be equivalently thought as
modules
for

\operatorname{D-mod}(H)
with respect to the above algebra structure.

2.1.4.

We now return to the context of
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}
-adic sheaves on schemes over
k
,
and we wish to define what
H\operatorname{-Cat}
is.

Naively, we might attempt what we just did in the case of D-modules. We start with the
category
\operatorname{Shv}(H)
, and…

The problem is that the analog of the categorical Künneth formula (
2.1
)
fails for
\operatorname{Shv}(-)
. Namely, we have a fully faithful functor

(2.2)

\operatorname{Shv}(Y)\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(Y^{\prime})\overset{\boxtimes}{\to}\operatorname{Shv}(Y\times Y^{\prime}),

but it is in general not an equivalence. In fact (
2.2
) is
never
an equivalence if
both
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
are positive-dimensional schemes of finite type.

2.1.5.

For future reference, we give the following definition: a prestack
{\mathcal{Y}}
is said to be
restricted
if
the functor

\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Y}})\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(Y^{\prime})\overset{\boxtimes}{\to}\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Y}}\times Y^{\prime})

is an equivalence for any
Y\in\operatorname{Sch}^{\operatorname{aff}}
.

An algebraic stack that has finitely many isomorphism classes of geometric points is restricted. An example of
such is

{\mathcal{Y}}=N\backslash G/B.

2.1.6.

The failure of (
2.2
) to be an equivalence means that we cannot define a structure of
coalgebra on
\operatorname{Shv}(H)
using pullback along
H\times H\overset{\operatorname{mult}}{\to}H
“as-is”.

One can, however, try the dual approach as in Remark
2.1.3
: i.e., we can treat
\operatorname{Shv}(H)
as
a
monoidal category
with respect to convolution. In this way we obtain
a
definition of
categorical representations; we denote the resulting 2-category by
H\operatorname{-Cat}^{\operatorname{naive}}
.

However, we have not really resolved the issue. For example,
H\operatorname{-Cat}^{\operatorname{naive}}
defined in the above
way does
not
have a symmetric monoidal structure compatible with the forgetful functor to
\operatorname{DGCat}
.

2.1.7.

To produce a better-behaved object, we will have to change the paradigm: instead of looking for actions on
H

on DG categories, we will replace the latter by a different symmetric monoidal 2-category, denoted
\operatorname{AGCat}
,
whose definition was suggested by V. Drinfeld some 20 years.

2.2.
The
(\infty,2)
-category
\operatorname{AGCat}

The contents of this and the next subsections constitute a joint project with N. Rozenblyum and Y. Varshavsky,
[
GRV
]
.

The complete definitions, formulated in the proper homotopy-theoretic language, will be supplied in
loc. cit.

Here we give a brief digest.

2.2.1.

An object
\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}\in\operatorname{AGCat}
is an assignment

S\in\operatorname{Sch}^{\operatorname{aff}}\mapsto\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}_{S}\in\operatorname{Shv}(S){\operatorname{-mod}}(\operatorname{DGCat}),

where we regard
\operatorname{Shv}(S)
as a monoidal category with respect to the !-tensor product.

The above assignment should be functorial in the following sense:

•

For a map
f:S_{1}\to S_{2}
, we should have
\operatorname{Shv}(S_{2})
-linear functors

f^{!}:\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}_{S_{2}}\to\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}_{S_{1}}\text{ and }f_{*}:\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}_{S_{1}}\to\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}_{S_{2}};

•

The above functors should be compatible with compositions of morphisms
in the obvious sense;

•

For a Cartesian diagram of affine schemes

\begin{CD}S^{\prime}_{1}@>{g_{1}}>{}>S_{1}\\
@V{f^{\prime}}V{}V@V{}V{f}V\\
S^{\prime}_{2}@>{g_{2}}>{}>S_{2},\end{CD}

we should be given an isomorphism

g_{2}^{!}\circ f_{*}\simeq f^{\prime}_{*}\circ g_{1}^{!}

as functors
\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}_{S_{1}}\to\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}_{S^{\prime}_{2}}
.

The above data must satisfy higher compatibilities, which can be formulated using the formalism
of
[
GaRo
, Chapter 7]
.

Morphisms in
\operatorname{AGCat}
are defined naturally: for a pair of objects
\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}^{\prime}
and
\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}^{\prime\prime}
, we define the
DG category of 1-morphisms between them, to have as objects compatible collections of
\operatorname{Shv}(S)
-linear functors

\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}^{\prime}_{S}\to\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}^{\prime\prime}_{S},\quad S\in\operatorname{Sch}^{\operatorname{aff}}

compatible with the operations
f^{!}
and
f_{*}
, etc.

2.2.2.

To a prestack
{\mathcal{Y}}
one attaches an object of
\operatorname{AGCat}
, denoted
\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathcal{Y}})
, defined by

\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathcal{Y}})_{S}:=\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Y}}\times S),

with the operations
f^{!}
and
f_{*}
defined naturally.

2.2.3.

We have a naturally defined fully faithful functor

\operatorname{emb.restr}:\operatorname{DGCat}\to\operatorname{AGCat}

that assigns to
{\mathbf{C}}\in\operatorname{DGCat}
the object

S\mapsto{\mathbf{C}}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(S)

with the operations
f^{!}
and
f_{*}
defined naturally.

The functor
\operatorname{emb.restr}
admits a right adjoint, given by

\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}\mapsto\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}_{\operatorname{pt}}.

2.2.4.

In what follows we will say that an object of
\operatorname{AGCat}
is
restricted
if it lies in the essential image of
the functor
\operatorname{emb.restr}
.

For example, a prestack
{\mathcal{Y}}
is restricted in the sense of Sect.
2.1.5
if and only if

\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathcal{Y}})
is restricted as an object of
\operatorname{AGCat}
.

2.2.5.

A key construction that makes
\operatorname{AGCat}
do the job for us is that of the symmetric monoidal
structure on it. One shows that
\operatorname{AGCat}
possesses a unique symmetric monoidal structure, such that:

•

It is compatible with colimits;

•

The functor
\operatorname{emb.restr}
is symmetric monoidal;

•

For
S_{1},S_{2}\in\operatorname{Sch}^{\operatorname{aff}}
, we have

(2.3)

\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}(S_{1})\otimes\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}(S_{2})=\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}(S_{1}\times S_{2}),\quad S_{1},S_{2}\in\operatorname{Sch}^{\operatorname{aff}}.

The unit of this symmetric monoidal structure is the object

\underline{\operatorname{Vect}}:=\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}(\operatorname{pt})\simeq\operatorname{emb.restr}(\operatorname{Vect}).

2.2.6.

One shows that for a reasonably large class of prestacks, the naturally defined map

(2.4)

\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathcal{Y}}_{1})\otimes\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathcal{Y}}_{2})\to\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathcal{Y}}_{1}\times{\mathcal{Y}}_{2})

is still an isomorphism.

2.2.7.

Note that since the functor
\operatorname{emb.restr}
is symmetric monoidal, its right adjoint possesses a

right-lax
symmetric monoidal structure. Evaluating on the objects

\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathcal{Y}}_{1}),\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathcal{Y}}_{2})\in\operatorname{AGCat}
, we obtain a 1-morphism in
\operatorname{DGCat}
(i.e., a functor)

(2.5)

\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Y}}_{1})\otimes\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Y}}_{2})\simeq\operatorname{emb.restr}^{R}(\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathcal{Y}}_{1}))\otimes\operatorname{emb.restr}^{R}(\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathcal{Y}}_{2}))\to\\
\to\operatorname{emb.restr}^{R}(\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathcal{Y}}_{1})\otimes\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathcal{Y}}_{2}))\to\operatorname{emb.restr}^{R}(\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathcal{Y}}_{1}\times{\mathcal{Y}}_{2}))=\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Y}}_{1}\times{\mathcal{Y}}_{2}),

which is the usual
\boxtimes
functor.

Note that (
2.5
) is typically not an isomorphism, even when (
2.4
) is.

2.2.8.

Let
{\mathcal{Y}}
be a quasi-compact algebraic stack. Under some mild assumptions
on
{\mathcal{Y}}
, the map (
2.4
) is an isomorphism for
{\mathcal{Y}}_{1}={\mathcal{Y}}_{2}={\mathcal{Y}}
. This formally
implies that
\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathcal{Y}})
is dualizable (and self-dual) as an object of
\operatorname{AGCat}
.

Let
\phi
be an endomorphism of
{\mathcal{Y}}
(assumed to be schematic, so that the functor
\phi_{*}
is well-defined).
In this case, it makes sense to consider

\operatorname{Tr}_{\operatorname{AGCat}}(\phi_{*},\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathcal{Y}}))\in{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm End}}}(\underline{\operatorname{Vect}})\simeq\operatorname{Vect}.

A formal computation shows:

(2.6)

\operatorname{Tr}_{\operatorname{AGCat}}(\phi_{*},\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathcal{Y}}))\simeq\operatorname{C}^{\cdot}_{\blacktriangle}({\mathcal{Y}}^{\phi},\omega_{{\mathcal{Y}}^{\phi}}),

where
\operatorname{C}^{\cdot}_{\blacktriangle}(-)
is the functor of
renormalized cochains
, see
[
AGKRRV2
, Sect. A.2.4]
.

In particular, the category
\operatorname{AGCat}
helps us turn the map (
1.1
) into an isomorphism:
for
k=\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
with
{\mathcal{Y}}
defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and
\phi=\operatorname{Frob}
, the map (
1.1
)
factors as

\operatorname{Tr}_{\operatorname{DGCat}}(\operatorname{Frob}_{*},\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Y}}))\overset{\operatorname{emb.restr}}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Tr}_{\operatorname{AGCat}}(\operatorname{Frob}_{*},\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathcal{Y}}))\simeq\operatorname{C}^{\cdot}_{\blacktriangle}({\mathcal{Y}}^{\operatorname{Frob}},\omega_{{\mathcal{Y}}^{\operatorname{Frob}}})\simeq\operatorname{Funct}({\mathcal{Y}}({\mathbb{F}})_{q}).

The failure of the initial map (
1.1
) to be an isomorphism stems from the failure of
(
2.5
) to be an isomorphism.

2.3.
Categorical representations of groups–the definition

Having introduced the symmetric monoidal 2-category, we can now define what we mean by a categorical
H
-representation.

2.3.1.

By construction, we can view
\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}(H)
as a commutative Hopf algebra in
\operatorname{AGCat}
. We define the 2-category

H\operatorname{-Cat}
to be

\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}(H){\operatorname{-comod}}(\operatorname{AGCat}).

2.3.2.

Thus, explicitly, an object of
H\operatorname{-Cat}
is an object

\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}=\{S\mapsto\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}_{S}\}

of
\operatorname{AGCat}
, equipped with the datum of functors

\operatorname{co-act}_{S}:\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}_{S}\to\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}_{S\times H}

that are associative in the sense that the diagrams

\begin{CD}\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}_{S}@>{\operatorname{co-act}_{S}}>{}>\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}_{S\times H}\\
@V{\operatorname{co-act}_{S}}V{}V@V{}V{\operatorname{co-act}_{H\times S}}V\\
\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}_{S\times H}@>{(\operatorname{id}_{S}\times\operatorname{mult}_{H})^{!}}>{}>\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}_{S\times H\times H}\end{CD}

commute.

2.3.3.
Example

Let
{\mathcal{Y}}
be a prestack equipped with an action of
H
. Then
\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}(Y)
naturally upgrades to an object
of
H\operatorname{-Cat}
.

2.3.4.

The commutative algebra structure on
\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}(H)
defines on
H\operatorname{-Cat}
a symmetric monoidal structure, compatible
with the forgetful functor

(2.7)

{\mathbf{oblv}}_{H}:H\operatorname{-Cat}\to\operatorname{AGCat}.

An object of
\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}(H)
is dualizable if and only if its image under
{\mathbf{oblv}}_{H}

is dualizable, and
{\mathbf{oblv}}_{H}
commutes with duality.

A somewhat non-trivial fact is that the functor

\operatorname{inv}_{H}:H\operatorname{-Cat}\to\operatorname{AGCat},\quad\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}\mapsto\underline{{\mathbf{C}}}^{H}

also commutes with duality (see
[
Ga
, Sect. B]
).

By construction,
H\operatorname{-Cat}
is tensored over
\operatorname{AGCat}
. One shows that it is
rigid
as a symmetric
monoidal 2-category over
\operatorname{AGCat}
. In particular, it is dualizable and self-dual as a module over
\operatorname{AGCat}
.

2.3.5.

One can ask the following question (if one still desperately wants to consider actions of
H
on DG categories, rather than
on objects of the weird
\operatorname{AGCat}
):

Can one describe the subcategory

(2.8)

H\operatorname{-Cat}\underset{\operatorname{AGCat}}{\times}\operatorname{DGCat},

where:

•

H\operatorname{-Cat}\to\operatorname{AGCat}
is the forgetful functor;

•

\operatorname{DGCat}\to\operatorname{AGCat}
is the embedding
\operatorname{emb.restr}
?

Unwinding, these are DG categories
{\mathbf{C}}
, equipped with a coaction functor

\operatorname{co-act}:{\mathbf{C}}\to{\mathbf{C}}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(H),

such that the following diagram commutes:

\begin{CD}{\mathbf{C}}@>{\operatorname{co-act}}>{}>{\mathbf{C}}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(H)\\
&&@V{}V{\operatorname{co-act}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{\operatorname{Shv}(H)}}V\\
@V{\operatorname{co-act}}V{}V{\mathbf{C}}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(H)\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(H)\\
&&@V{}V{\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbf{C}}\otimes(\boxtimes)}V\\
{\mathbf{C}}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(H)@>{\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbf{C}}\otimes\operatorname{mult}_{H}^{!}}>{}>{\mathbf{C}}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(H\times H).\end{CD}

One can classify all such
{\mathbf{C}}
, but as we shall presently see, there will be relatively few such categories.

2.3.6.

Let
\chi\in\operatorname{Shv}(H)
be a character sheaf on
H
, i.e.,

\operatorname{mult}^{!}(\chi)\simeq\chi\boxtimes\chi.

Let
\operatorname{Shv}(H)_{\chi}\subset\operatorname{Shv}(H)
be the full subcategory consisting of objects all of whose cohomologies
are extensions of copies of
\chi
. The operation
\operatorname{mult}^{!}
defines on
\operatorname{Shv}(H)_{\chi}
a structure of comonoidal category.

It is clear that the functor
\operatorname{emb.restr}
extends to a (fully faithful) functor

\operatorname{emb.restr}_{\chi}:\operatorname{Shv}(H)_{\chi}{\operatorname{-comod}}(\operatorname{DGCat})\to H\operatorname{-Cat}.

We have:

Proposition 2.3.7
.

Suppose that
H
is connected. Then
any object in (
2.8
) splits (canonically) as a direct sum of objects, each of
which lies in the essential image of the functor
\operatorname{emb.restr}_{\chi}
for some
\chi
.

2.3.8.

We now consider the case of the loop group. Since
{\mathfrak{L}}(G)
is of infinite type, one needs to specify what one means
by the category
\operatorname{Shv}({\mathfrak{L}}(G))
. This can be done by the recipe of
[
Ga
, Sect. C.3]
. In particular, we obtain a well-defined
object

\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathfrak{L}}(G))\in\operatorname{AGCat},

and the 1-morophism

\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathfrak{L}}(G))\otimes\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathfrak{L}}(G))\to\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathfrak{L}}(G)\times{\mathfrak{L}}(G))

is an isomorphism.

Therefore, we can follow the recipe of Sect.
2.3.1
and define the
2-category

{\mathfrak{L}}(G)\operatorname{-Cat}.

The properties mentioned in Sect.
2.3.4
apply in the present situation as well (see
[
Ga
, Sect. D]
).

2.3.9.
Examples

The objects
\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\operatorname{Gr}}_{G})
,
\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}(\operatorname{Fl}^{\operatorname{aff}}_{G})
and
\underline{\operatorname{Shv}}({\mathfrak{L}}(G))
itself
all naturally upgrade to objects of
{\mathfrak{L}}(G)\operatorname{-Cat}
.

2.4.
Restricted representations

The notion of
restricted categorical representation
for a finite-dimensional reductive group
has been developed in
[
GRV
]
. In the case of loop groups, the quest for this notion has been
a joint project with D. Kazhdan, S. Raskin and Y. Varshavsky. However, a recent work of
G. Dhillon, Y. Varshavsky and D. Yang (
[
DVY
]
) seems to have settled all the basic questions
related to this notion (under the assumption that
p=\operatorname{char}(k)>|W|
).

2.4.1.

We now have the 2-category
{\mathfrak{L}}(G)\operatorname{-Cat}
up and running, but… this is still not good enough:

We have worked hard to define
{\mathfrak{L}}(G)\operatorname{-Cat}
, and it came out to be a category tensored over
\operatorname{AGCat}
.
However, if our goal is to establish a Langlands-type equivalence between
{\mathfrak{L}}(G)
-categories and some
2-category on the spectral side, the latter will only be tensored over
\operatorname{DGCat}
.

In other words,
{\mathfrak{L}}(G)\operatorname{-Cat}
is too big in order be equivalent to something on the spectral side, and we need
to trim it down, i.e., find a full subcategory

(2.9)

{\mathfrak{L}}(G)\operatorname{-Cat}_{\operatorname{restr}}\subset{\mathfrak{L}}(G)\operatorname{-Cat},

2.6.1.

2.6.3.

3.
The global ramified case

The contents of this section constitute work-in-progress, joint with S. Raskin and Y. Varshavsky.

We will propose a set of conjectures about the global ramified Langlands theory, assuming
the local Langlands theory discussed in the previous section. There are three main conjectures:

•

A spectral description of the global automorphic category, Conjecture
3.4.4
(this is a
ramified counterpart of Conjecture
1.4.4
);

•

A global trace isomorphism, Conjecture
3.3.3
(this is a ramified counterpart of
Theorem
1.2.3
);

•

A spectral description of the (enhanced version of) the space of automorphic functions, Conjecture
3.5.5

(this is a ramified counterpart of Corollary
1.5.8
).

The last conjecture is a formal consequence of the first two and supplies an
answer to the basic question of the Langlands program.

A.1.
Abstract framework for the trace

Let

be a symmetric monoidal category; let
{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{O}}\in{\mathbf{O}}
denote the unit object.
:et
\mathbf{o}\in{\mathbf{O}}

{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{O}}\overset{\operatorname{unit}}{\to}\mathbf{o}\otimes\mathbf{o}^{\vee}\text{ and }\mathbf{o}\otimes\mathbf{o}^{\vee}\overset{\operatorname{counit}}{\to}{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{O}},

A.4.
Functoriality of trace

Note that
\operatorname{DGCat}
has an additional structure, namely, that of a 2-category. This structure leaves room for

Let us return to the setting of Sect.

{\mathbf{O}}
is now a symmetric monoidal 2-category.
Let

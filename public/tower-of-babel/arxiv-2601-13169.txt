Abelian and non-Abelian fractionalized states in twisted MoTe 2 : A generalized Landau-level theory
Bohao Li, Yunze Ouyang, Fengcheng Wu

Abstract
Fractional Chern insulators are lattice analogs of fractional quantum Hall states that realize fractionalized quasiparticles without an external magnetic field. A key strategy to understand and design these phases is to map Chern bands onto Landau levels (LLs). Here, we introduce a universal framework that variationally decomposes Bloch bands into generalized LLs, providing a controlled and quantitative characterization of their effective LL nature. Applying this approach to twisted bilayer MoTe 2 modeled by first-principles-derived moiré Hamiltonians, we find that the first moiré valence band is dominated by the generalized zeroth LL across a broad range of twist angles, facilitating the formation of Abelian fractional Chern insulators in the Jain sequences. The second moiré band, renormalized via Hartree-Fock calculations at hole filling ν h = 2 \nu_{h}=2 , is dominated by the generalized first LL at twist angles θ = 2.45 ∘ \theta=2.45^{\circ} and 2.13 ∘ 2.13^{\circ} . At θ = 2.45 ∘ \theta=2.45^{\circ} , we find numerical evidence for a non-Abelian Moore–Read (MR) state at ν h = 5 / 2 \nu_{h}=5/2 , with consistent signatures in both the energy spectrum and the particle entanglement spectrum. Interpolation studies further demonstrate an adiabatic connection between this state and the MR state in the conventional first LL. In contrast, at θ = 2.13 ∘ \theta=2.13^{\circ} , a charge-density-wave state prevails in the competition with the MR state due to the larger bandwidth. Our variational mapping provides a theoretical framework for exploring exotic fractionalized phases, including non-Abelian states, in realistic systems.

A key example is the fractional quantum Hall insulators (FQHIs), which arise in Landau levels (LLs) of a two-dimensional electron system subjected to low temperatures and high perpendicular magnetic fields

In realistic materials, Chern bands inevitably deviate from the ideal limit, raising a fundamental question: to what extent, and in what sense, can lattice Chern bands be mapped onto LLs? Addressing this question is central to understanding why certain Chern bands support fractionalized topological phases analogous to those in the fractional quantum Hall effect, while others do not. Moiré materials provide a unique platform for tackling this problem, as their band structures are highly tunable and can approach the ideal Chern-band limit in certain cases. In tMoTe
2
, the first moiré valence band closely approximates an ideal Chern band, a property attributed to the layer-pseudospin skyrmion texture
Wu et al. 2019

;
Shi et al. 2024

\theta\approx 2^{\circ}

;

;
Chen et al. 2025

Developing a general and systematic framework for such a correspondence is essential for elucidating the conditions under which Chern bands host Abelian and non-Abelian fractionalized phases, and remains a key open problem with implications for moiré systems and topological materials more broadly.

In this work, we develop a variational framework that decomposes realistic Bloch bands into generalized LLs. The generalized LLs with indices greater than zero are extended from the generalized zeroth LL, possessing a quantum geometry that is no longer ideal, while preserving a quantized integrated trace of the quantum metric
Liu et al. 2025b
. Crucially, these generalized LLs form a complete and orthonormal basis, enabling a controlled decomposition of realistic Bloch bands. By introducing a variational mapping approach, we establish a generalized LL representation of the Bloch bands, where the decomposition can be dominated by a specific generalized LL. This dominance is achieved by variationally optimizing the spatially varying function
\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{r})
, thereby maximizing the weight of the targeted generalized LL. This approach provides a concrete route to identifying the effective LL character of moiré Chern bands and to connecting their quantum geometry to the nature of the fractionalized phases they host.

We apply this variational mapping to tMoTe
2

2

We variationally decompose the first (second) moiré band into generalized LLs, which reveals that the generalized zeroth (first) LL is the dominant component, establishing a quantitative correspondence between moiré Chern bands and their effective LL counterparts. We emphasize that even if a band’s quantum geometry satisfies the integrated trace condition of the generalized first LL, this does not guarantee a corresponding resemblance in their wave functions. Our explicit decomposition is, therefore, crucial to reveal the internal generalized-LL structure of the Bloch bands.

II
generalized LLs

We begin by reviewing the concept of quantum geometry and introducing the generalized LL wave functions. These generalized LLs extend the standard LL wave function by incorporating spatial modulations and fluctuations in quantum geometry
Liu et al. 2025b
. As a starting point, we present the definition of the quantum geometric tensor (QGT),

\displaystyle(\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}})_{ab}=\bra{\partial_{k_a}u_{\bl k}}(\mathds{1}-P)\ket{\partial_{k_b}u_{\bl k}}.

(1)

Here we focus on the Abelian QGT for a single band of Bloch wave function
\psi_{\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})
with
\boldsymbol{k}
the wave vector. In Eq. (
1
),
u_{\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})=e^{-i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}\psi_{\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})
is the periodic part of
\psi_{\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})

,
P=\ket{u_{\bl k}}\!\bra{u_{\bl k}}
is the projection operator, and
a,b
denote spatial indices. We focus on two dimensions here. The Berry curvature
\Omega_{\boldsymbol{k}}
and quantum metric
g_{\boldsymbol{k}}
are derived from Eq. (
1
) through the relation
(\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}})_{ab}=(g_{\boldsymbol{k}})_{ab}-\frac{i}{2}\epsilon_{ab}\Omega_{\boldsymbol{k}}
, where
\epsilon_{ab}
is the Levi-Civita symbol. Here
\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}}
is a Hermitian matrix,
g_{\boldsymbol{k}}
is a real symmetric matrix, and
\Omega_{\boldsymbol{k}}
is a real function. Because
\mathds{1}-P
is a projector, the QGT is semipositive definite, which implies that
\det[g_{\boldsymbol{k}}]\geq\Omega_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}/4
. Since
g_{\boldsymbol{k}}
is a
2\times 2
real symmetric matrix,
(\text{Tr}[g_{\boldsymbol{k}}])^{2}\geq 4\det[g_{\boldsymbol{k}}]
. Therefore, there is a trace inequality
\mathrm{Tr}[g_{\boldsymbol{k}}]\geq\absolutevalue{\Omega_{\bl k}}
.

The integrals of the Berry curvature and the trace of quantum metric over the Brillouin zone give rise to, respectively, the Chern number
\mathcal{C}
and the quantum weight
\mathcal{K}

Onishi and Fu 2025

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d{\bm{k}}\,\Omega_{\bm{k}},

(2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d{\bm{k}}\,\text{Tr}[g_{\bm{k}}].

Here,

is a topological invariant that is quantized to an integer value, whereas
\mathcal{K}
is not.
Because of the inequality

As discussed below, both
\mathcal{C}
and

provide important insights into the quantum geometry, complementing the information captured by the QGT.

\displaystyle\chi_{\boldsymbol{k},n,s}^{(\mathrm{LL})}=\begin{pmatrix}n+\frac{1}{2}&-\frac{is}{2}\\

(3)

moiré bands

In

(M_{1},M_{2})\neq(1,1)
.

The intralayer potential
\Delta_{l}(\hat{\boldsymbol{k}},\boldsymbol{r})
takes the form

\displaystyle\Delta_{l}(\hat{\boldsymbol{k}},\boldsymbol{r})\!=

\displaystyle\sum_{M_{1},M_{2},\boldsymbol{g}}V^{(M_{1},M_{2})}_{\boldsymbol{g},l}f^{(M_{1},M_{2})}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{k}}}e^{i\boldsymbol{g}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}+\mathrm{h.c.}\;,

(11)

where
V^{(M_{1},M_{2})}_{\boldsymbol{g},l}
is the expansion coefficient,
\boldsymbol{g}
is the moiré reciprocal lattice vector defined as
\boldsymbol{g}=m\boldsymbol{g}_{1}+n\boldsymbol{g}_{2}
with
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\boldsymbol{g}_{j}=\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{3}a_{M}}[\cos\frac{(j-1)\pi}{3},\sin\frac{(j-1)\pi}{3}]
.

The interlayer tunneling term
\Delta_{T}(\hat{\boldsymbol{k}},\boldsymbol{r})
is given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{T}(\hat{\boldsymbol{k}},\boldsymbol{r})=

(12)

\displaystyle\sum_{M_{1},M_{2},\boldsymbol{q}}w_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{(M_{1},M_{2})}[f_{\hat{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{(M_{1},M_{2})}e^{-i\boldsymbol{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}+e^{i(\hat{R}_{2x}\boldsymbol{q})\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}f_{\hat{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{(M_{2},M_{1})}],

where
w_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{(M_{1},M_{2})}
is the expansion coefficient,
\hat{R}_{2x}
is the two-fold rotational operator around
x
-axis,
\boldsymbol{q}=\boldsymbol{g}+\boldsymbol{q}_{1}
and
\boldsymbol{q}_{1}=\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{+}-\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{-}
.
\Delta_{l}(\hat{\boldsymbol{k}},\boldsymbol{r})
and
\Delta_{T}(\hat{\boldsymbol{k}},\boldsymbol{r})
become momentum-independent when
V^{(M_{1},M_{2})}_{\boldsymbol{g},l}=0
and
w^{(M_{1},M_{2})}_{\boldsymbol{q}}=0
for
(M_{1},M_{2})\neq(0,0)
.

The point group symmetry of tMoTe
2
is
D_{3}
, which includes the
C_{3z}
and
C_{2y}
symmetries. Here,
C_{nj}
represents an
n
-fold rotation about the
j
-axis. The valley-projected Hamiltonian in Eq. (
9
) is required to be Hermitian and to remain invariant under the

and

), and (

) are constrained by the following conditions

\displaystyle m^{(M_{1},M_{2})}_{l}=[m^{(M_{2},M_{1})}_{l}]^{*}=[m^{(M_{1},M_{2})}_{-l}]^{*},

(13)

\displaystyle V^{(M_{1},M_{2})}_{\boldsymbol{g},l}=\omega^{M_{1}-M_{2}}V^{(M_{1},M_{2})}_{\hat{R}_{3}\boldsymbol{g},l},V^{(M_{1},M_{2})}_{\boldsymbol{g},l}=[V^{(M_{1},M_{2})}_{\hat{R}_{2x}\boldsymbol{g},-l}]^{*},

\displaystyle w_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{(M_{1},M_{2})}=\omega^{M_{1}-M_{2}}w_{\hat{R}_{3}\boldsymbol{q}}^{(M_{1},M_{2})},

where

IV
variational mapping

We now introduce a variational mapping approach to establish a generalized LL representation of Bloch bands, and apply it to the first two moiré bands in the tMoTe
2
model. The Bloch wave function
\psi_{+,m,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})
of the
m
th band at momentum
\boldsymbol{k}
and
+K
valley can be decomposed in terms of the generalized LL wave functions
\Theta^{(s)}_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})
,

\displaystyle\psi_{+,m,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})=

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\sum_{s=\pm}c_{m,n,\boldsymbol{k}}^{(s)}\Theta^{(s)}_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r}),

(14)

where
c_{m,n,\boldsymbol{k}}^{(s)}
are the expansion coefficient.
This decomposition is general as long as
\psi_{+,m,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})
and
\Theta^{(s)}_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})
share the same translational symmetry, in accordance with Bloch’s theorem. To ensure this, we require that the magnetic Bloch wave function
\Psi_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}^{(s)}(\boldsymbol{r})
, used in the definition of
\Theta^{(s)}_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})
, is defined with respect to a magnetic unit cell that matches exactly with the moiré unit cell of tMoTe
2
. This give the relation between the magnetic length
\ell
and the moiré period
a_{M}
,
2\pi\ell^{2}=\sqrt{3}a_{M}^{2}/2
.

Using the orthonormality condition in Eq. (
8
),
c_{m,n,\boldsymbol{k}}^{(s)}
can be expressed as the overlap between
\psi_{+,m,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})
and
\Theta^{(s)}_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})
,
c_{m,n,\boldsymbol{k}}^{(s)}=\langle\Theta^{(s)}_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}\lvert\psi_{+,m,\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle
. To measure the average overlap between
\psi_{+,m,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})
and
\Theta^{(s)}_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})
, we define the generalized LL weight,

\displaystyle W_{m,n}^{(s)}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\lvert c_{m,n,\boldsymbol{k}}^{(s)}\rvert^{2},

(15)

where
N
is the number of
\boldsymbol{k}
points summed within the mBZ.

The spatially varying functions
\mathcal{B}^{(s)}(\boldsymbol{r})
are so far arbitrary, provided they respect the necessary translational symmetry to ensure that
\Theta^{(s)}_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})
is a Bloch wave function. However, for the decomposition in Eq. (
14
) to yield physical insight, we would require that a specific generalized LL,
\Theta_{n_{0},\boldsymbol{k}}^{(s_{0})}(\boldsymbol{r})
, contributes a dominant weight
W_{m,n_{0}}^{(s_{0})}
, compared to all other
W_{m,n}^{(s)}
. This can ensure that the dominant contribution to the wave function comes from a well-defined set of states, simplifying the physical interpretation and enabling insights from LL physics. The choice of
n_{0}
and
s_{0}
can be motivated by the quantum geometric properties of the targeted Bloch wave function
\psi_{+,m,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})
, as explained below. We can then maximize
W_{m,n_{0}}^{(s_{0})}
variationally by adjusting
\mathcal{B}^{(s_{0})}(\bm{r})
via gradient ascent, starting from an initial ansatz and iterating until convergence:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{i}^{(s_{0})}(\boldsymbol{r})\rightarrow

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{i}^{(s_{0})}(\boldsymbol{r})+\zeta\frac{\delta W_{m,n_{0}}^{(s_{0})}}{\delta\overline{\mathcal{B}_{i}}^{(s_{0})}(\boldsymbol{r})},

(16)

where the subscript
i=1,2
labels the
i
th component of the spinor
\mathcal{B}^{(s_{0})}(\boldsymbol{r})
, and
\zeta
is a positive parameter. Here the functional derivative is defined as

\displaystyle\frac{\delta f}{\delta\overline{g}}=\frac{1}{2}[\frac{\delta f}{\delta\real[g]}+i\frac{\delta f}{\delta\imaginary[g]}],

(17)

for a real function
f
.

We can further determine
\mathcal{B}^{(-s_{0})}(\boldsymbol{r})
by noting it is a two-component spinor orthogonal to
\mathcal{B}^{(s_{0})}(\boldsymbol{r})
at every
\boldsymbol{r}
. With this set of procedures, we can calculate all the coefficients
c_{m,n,\boldsymbol{k}}^{(s)}
and the weights
W_{m,n}^{(s)}
.

IV.1
The first moiré band

We first apply the variational mapping approach to the first moiré valence band in tMoTe
2
. Based on its 0LL-like feature of quantum geometry with
\mathcal{C}=+1
and
\mathcal{K}-|\mathcal{C}|\ll 1
, the dominant generalized LL weight is expected to be
W_{1,0}^{(+)}
. For our initial ansatz of
\mathcal{B}^{(+)}(\boldsymbol{r})
, we take a simple ratio between the Bloch wave function

\psi_{+,1,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(\bm{r})
of the first moiré valence band at the

\boldsymbol{\gamma}
point and the corresponding 0LL wave function

\Psi_{0,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{(+)}(\bm{r})
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{(+)}(\boldsymbol{r})\rightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{\psi_{+,1,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(\boldsymbol{r})}{\Psi_{0,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{(+)}(\boldsymbol{r})},

(18)

In this construction, any zeros in the numerator and denominator cancel, ensuring that this initial ansatz is continuous across the entire real space.
After adjusting
\mathcal{B}^{(+)}(\boldsymbol{r})
according to Eq. (
16
) until convergence, its orthogonal counterpart
\mathcal{B}^{(-)}(\bm{r})
is obtained via

\displaystyle U^{\dagger}_{0}(\boldsymbol{r})\mathcal{B}^{(-)}(\boldsymbol{r})=i\sigma_{y}[U^{\dagger}_{0}(\boldsymbol{r})\mathcal{B}^{(+)}(\boldsymbol{r})]^{*},

(19)

where
\sigma_{y}
is the
y
Pauli matrix in the layer pseudospin space. In addition to the orthogonality, this construction ensures that
\Theta^{(-)}_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})
satisfies
C_{3z}
symmetry at three high-symmetry points
\boldsymbol{k}\in\{\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\pm},\boldsymbol{\gamma}\}
, and respects
C_{2y}\mathcal{T}
symmetry at
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
, as demonstrated in Appendix
A
.

2
(b).
Throughout the entire
\theta

, exceeds 0.99 for all twist angles studied.

W_{1,0}^{(+)}
attains its maximum value of 0.97 at
\theta=2.45^{\circ}
, where
\mathcal{K}-|\mathcal{C}|
is minimized.

in real space at four representative twist angles

2.45^{\circ}
,
2.88^{\circ}
, and
3.15^{\circ}
. Here

exhibits spatial modulations following the electron density variation of the first moiré band. At

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}=\mathcal{P}_{1}\hat{H}^{\mathrm{(full)}}\mathcal{P}_{1}.

\mathcal{P}_{1}

\boldsymbol{k}
,
n
th band and valley
\tau

2

We now carry out ED studies at
\nu_{h}=5/2
in the projected many-body Hamiltonian,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{2}=\mathcal{P}_{2}\hat{H}^{\mathrm{(full)}}\mathcal{P}_{2}.

(26)

Here
\mathcal{P}_{2}
is spanned by the many-body basis states where the first band is completely filled and the second band is partially occupied at
+K
valley,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{2}=\mathrm{span}\{\prod_{i=1}^{N_{e}^{\prime}}\widetilde{\varphi}^{\dagger}_{+,2,\boldsymbol{k}_{i}}\prod_{\tau=\pm}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\widetilde{\varphi}^{\dagger}_{\tau,1,\boldsymbol{k}_{j}}\ket{0},\boldsymbol{k}_{i}\in\mathrm{mBZ}\},

(27)

N
is the number of momentum points (unit cells) in the momentum (real) space,
N_{e}^{\prime}=N/2
is the number of holes in the second band, and
N_{e}^{\prime}+2N
is the totoal number of holes.

\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
is formulated as,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{2}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}(-\lambda_{w}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{+,2,\boldsymbol{k}})\widetilde{\varphi}^{\dagger}_{+,2,\boldsymbol{k}}\widetilde{\varphi}_{+,2,\boldsymbol{k}}+\hat{V}+E_{1},

(28)

where
-\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau,n,\boldsymbol{k}}
is the HF corrected band energy in the hole basis and
E_{1}
denotes the total energy of the filled first band. We introduce a parameter
\lambda_{w}
to phenomenologically tune the bandwidth. In
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{2}

\hat{V}
is the interaction term projected onto the second band,

\displaystyle\hat{V}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4}}}\widetilde{V}_{\boldsymbol{k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4}}}\widetilde{\varphi}^{\dagger}_{+,2,\boldsymbol{k_{1}}}\widetilde{\varphi}^{\dagger}_{+,2,\boldsymbol{k_{2}}}\widetilde{\varphi}_{+,2,\boldsymbol{k_{3}}}\widetilde{\varphi}_{+,2,\boldsymbol{k_{4}}}.

(29)

The interaction matrix element
\widetilde{V}_{\boldsymbol{k}_{1}\boldsymbol{k}_{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{3}\boldsymbol{k}_{4}}
is given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\mathcal{A}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}}V(\boldsymbol{q})\widetilde{M}^{+22}_{\boldsymbol{k}_{1}\boldsymbol{k}_{4}}(\boldsymbol{q})\widetilde{M}^{+22}_{\boldsymbol{k}_{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{3}}(\boldsymbol{-q}),

(30)

and the plane-wave matrix element is

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k^{\prime}}}^{+22}(\boldsymbol{q})=

\displaystyle\int d\boldsymbol{r}\,e^{i\boldsymbol{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}[\widetilde{f}_{+,2,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})]^{*}\widetilde{f}_{+,2,\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{r}),

(31)

To further probe the nature of the states, we examine the PES, a method capable of distinguishing the MR state from competing states
Liu et al. 2025c
. The PES is constructed by dividing the system into two subsystems
A
and

, containing

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{A}=\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{GS}}}\sum_{m=1}^{N_{\mathrm{GS}}}\hat{\rho}_{m,A},

(32)

where

is the quasi-degeneracy.
\hat{\rho}_{m,A}

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{m,A}=\operatorname{Tr}_{B}(\hat{\rho}_{m}),\hat{\rho}_{m}=\ket{\Psi_m}\bra{\Psi_m}.

(33)

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{A}=\sum_{n}e^{-\xi_{A,n}}\ket{\alpha_{A,n}}\bra{\alpha_{A,n}},

(34)

where

n

VII
Discussion

In summary, we develop a theoretical framework for decomposing Chern bands into generalized Landau levels and use it to investigate Abelian and non-Abelian fractionalized states in tMoTe
2
. Specifically, we employ a variational mapping to express the Bloch states of the first two moiré Chern bands in terms of generalized LLs, providing a clear perspective on how these fractionalized states emerge. The first moiré band is found to be dominated by the generalized 0LL, naturally leading to the formation of Abelian FCIs in the Jain sequences.

For the second moiré band, HF calculations at hole filling
\nu_{h}=2
reveal a dominant generalized
1
LL component at
\theta=2.13^{\circ}
and
\theta=2.45^{\circ}
, which in turn motivates our study of interaction-driven phases at fractional fillings. Importantly, this identification is nontrivial: although the quantum weight
\mathcal{K}
of the second band lies close to the nominal value
3|\mathcal{C}|
, such proximity alone does not a priori ensure a generalized
1
LL–like wave function. Our explicit decomposition demonstrates that resolving the internal generalized-LL structure is essential for unambiguously establishing the
1
LL-like nature of the band. Exact diagonalization calculations at
\nu_{h}=5/2
identify the evidence of the non-Abelian MR state at
\theta=2.45^{\circ}
in both the energy spectra and the PES. This MR state is further verified by its adiabatic connection to the corresponding state in the first LL. We also perform systemetic numerical calculation at
\theta=2.13^{\circ}
, where a competition between the MR state and CDW state is found, with the transition primarily controlled by the bandwidth (See Appendix
E
for details). These results demonstrate a quantitative correspondence between moiré Chern bands and generalized LLs, establishing a foundation for understanding the emergence of fractionalized topological phases in moiré systems.

We compare our numerical results with available experiments. For fractionalized states at

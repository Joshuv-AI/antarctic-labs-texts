Notes on Leibniz n n -algebras
José Manuel Casas, Emzar Khmaladze, Manuel Ladra

Abstract
. We analyze behaviors of generalized forgetful and Daletskii-Takhtajan’s functors on perfect objects and crossed modules of Leibniz n n -algebras. Then we give applications to homology and universal central extensions of Leibniz n n -algebras.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
17A32, 17B55.

1.
Introduction

Leibniz
n
-algebras were introduced in
[
8
]
as the non-skew symmetric version of Nambu algebras
[
9
,
18
]
or Lie
n
-algebras
[
10
]
.
For
n=2
the Leibniz
2
-algebras are ordinary Leibniz algebras. Thus, Leibniz
n
-algebras are simultaneous generalization of Lie, Leibniz and Lie
n
-algebras.

In the development of structure theory of Leibniz
n
-algebras one often finds analogies with results established in Lie
n
-algebras and Leibniz algebras. One such direction of the study is the use of
forgetful functor

\operatorname{\mathfrak{U}}_{n}
which assigns to a Leibniz algebra
\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}
the Leibniz
n
-algebra with the same underlying vector space
\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}
endowed with the
n
-ary operation given by
[l_{1},...,l_{n}]=[l_{1},[l_{2},...,[l_{n-1},l_{n}]\dots]]
.

On the other hand, in the investigation of homological aspects of Leibniz
n
-algebras treated in
[
4
,
3
,
5
]
, one exploits the
remarkable properties of the so-called
Daletskii-Takhtajan’s functor

\operatorname{\mathfrak{D}}_{n}
which assigns to a Leibniz
n
-algebra
\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}
the Leibniz algebra with the underlying vector space
\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes(n-1)}
and with the bracket operation given by

[l_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes l_{n-1},l^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes l^{\prime}_{n-1}]=\underset{\scriptsize{1\leq i\leq n}}{\sum}l_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes[l_{i},l^{\prime}_{1},\dots,l^{\prime}_{n-1}]\otimes\cdots\otimes l_{n-1}.

In this paper we choose to establish further properties of the generalized forgetful and Daletskii-Takhtajan’s functors between categories of Leibniz
n
-algebras and Leibniz
p
-algebras, where
p=k(n-1)+1
for some natural number
k
. In particular, we prove that the generalized forgetful functor preserves perfect objects (Theorem
3.5
), actions (Lemma
4.2
) and crossed modules (Proposition
4.5
).
In Section
5
, we show that the Daletskii-Takhtajan’s functor does not preserve perfect objects in general, by presenting counter-examples (Example
5.1
).
In Section
6
, we construct a homomorphism between homologies with coefficients in a trivial co-representation of a Leibniz algebra
\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}
and the Leibniz
n
-algebra
\operatorname{\mathfrak{U}}_{n}(\operatorname{\mathcal{L}})
. And finally, as an application, in Section
7
, we analyze the relationships between the universal central extensions of a perfect Leibniz
n
-algebra in the categories of Leibniz
n
-algebras and Leibniz
p
-algebras.

2.
Leibniz
n
-algebras

The following definition first appeared in
[
8
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A Leibniz
n
-algebra is a vector space
{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}

equipped with an
n
-linear operation (
n
-ary bracket)
[-,\dots,-]:{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{\otimes n}\to{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
satisfying the following fundamental identity for all
x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{2},\dots,y_{n}\in{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
:

\begin{array}[]{l}[[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}],y_{2},\dots,y_{n}]=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}[x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},[x_{i},y_{2},\dots,y_{n}],x_{i+1},\dots,x_{n}]\end{array}

(2.1)

In addition, if the
n
-ary bracket is skew-symmetric, that is

[x_{\sigma(1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(n)}]={\text{sgn}(\sigma)}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}],

for all
\sigma\in S_{n}
, then
{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
is said to be
a Lie
n
-algebra or Filippov algebra

[
10
,
18
]
. Here
S_{n}
stands for the permutation group on
n
elements and
\text{sgn}(\sigma)\in\{-1,\ 1\}
denotes the
signature of
\sigma
.

A homomorphism
of Leibniz
n
-algebras is a linear map preserving the
n
-ary bracket. The respective category of Leibniz
n
-algebras will be denoted by
\operatorname{{}_{n}\textbf{{Lb}}}
.

For
n=2
the identity (
2.1
) is equivalent to the Leibniz identity
[x,[y,z]]=[[x,y],z]-[[x,z],y]
, so a Leibniz
2
-algebra is simply a Leibniz algebra
[
15
]
. We write
\operatorname{\textbf{{Lb}}}
for
{}_{2}{\operatorname{\textbf{{Lb}}}}
.

Example 2.2
.

i)

Any vector space with the trivial
n
-ary bracket is a Leibniz
n
-algebra, called an abelian Leibniz
n
-algebra.

ii)

Lie triple systems
[
14
]
and Leibniz triple systems
[
1
]
are (non-Lie) Leibniz
3
-algebras.

iii)

An associative trialgebra
[
17
]
with the
3
binary operations
\dashv
,
\vdash
and
\perp
, endowed with the ternary bracket

[x,y,z]=x\dashv(y\perp z-z\perp y)-(y\perp z-z\perp y)\vdash x,

is (in general ) a non-Lie Leibniz
3
-algebra.

iv)

Let
\text{char}(\mathbb{K})\mid(n-1)
. Then any one-dimensional vector space with basis
\{e\}
is a Leibniz
n
-algebras with respect the
n
-ary bracket given by
[e,e,\dots,e]=\alpha e
, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{K}
(c.f.
[
2
, Example 2.1 (iv)]
).

v)

Any two-dimensional vector space with
basis
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
is a Leibniz
3
-algebras with respect to the ternary bracket given by
[e_{1},e_{2},e_{2}]=e_{1}
and
0
otherwise (c.f.
[
2
, Theorem 2.14]
).

A subalgebra

of a Leibniz
n
-algebra
{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
is called

-sided ideal
of

if
[l_{1},\dots,l_{n}]\in{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime}
as soon as

for some

.

For any two

-sided ideals

-algebra

, we denote by
[{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime},{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime\prime},{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{n-2}]

the commutator
of

{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime}
and
{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime\prime}
, that is, the

-sided ideal of
{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
spanned by the brackets
[l_{1},\dots,l_{i},\dots,l_{j},\dots,l_{n}]
, where necessarily
l_{i}\in{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime}
and
l_{j}\in{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime\prime}
for some
i
and
j
,
1\leq i,j\leq n
,

. Obviously
[{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime},{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime\prime},{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{n-2}]\subseteq{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime}\cap{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime\prime}
. In particular, if
{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime\prime}={\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
(resp.
{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime}={\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime\prime}={\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
), then we use the notation
[{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime},{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{n-1}]
(resp.
[{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{n}]
) instead of
[{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime},{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}},{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{n-2}]
(resp.
[{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}},{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}},{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{n-2}]

Given a Leibniz
n
-algebra
\mathcal{L}
, it is clear that the quotient of

by the

-algebra, denoted by

. This defines a functor

to the category

Definition 2.3
.

i)

The center of a Leibniz
n
-algebra

is the

-sided ideal

\Z({\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}})=\{l\in{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}\mid[l_{1},\dots,l_{i-1},l,l_{i+1},\dots,l_{n}]=0,\ \text{where}\ 1\leq i\leq n,l_{j}\in{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}},j=1,\dots,\hat{i},\dots,n\}.

3.
\operatorname{\mathfrak{U}}_{p}^{q}
functors and perfect objects

Any Leibniz algebra is also Leibniz
n
-algebra with respect to
the
n
-ary bracket

[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]=[x_{1},[x_{2},\dots,[x_{n-1},x_{n}]\cdots]]

(see
[
8
, Proposition 3.2]
). This provides the so-called forgetful functor

\operatorname{\mathfrak{U}}^{n}:\operatorname{\textbf{{Lb}}}\to\operatorname{{}_{n}\textbf{{Lb}}},\quad{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}\mapsto{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}.

In this section we deal with generalizations of the functor
\operatorname{\mathfrak{U}}^{n}
to the categories
\operatorname{{}_{p}\textbf{{Lb}}}
(
p=k(n-1)+1
) and
\operatorname{{}_{q}\textbf{{Lb}}}
(
q=\kappa(p-1)+1
).
We need to recall the following definition from
[
8
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A
be a vector space equipped with an
n
-linear operation
\omega:A^{\otimes n}\to A
. A map

f:A\to A
is said to be a derivation with respect to
\omega
if

f(\omega(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}))=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega(a_{1},\dots,f(a_{i}),\dots,a_{n}).

Note that, given a vector space
{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
, an
n
-linear operation
[-,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\dots}},-]:{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{\otimes n}\to{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
satisfies the fundamental identity (
2.1
) if and only if, for all
x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}\in{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
, the map

\operatorname{\text{ad}}_{x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}}:{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}\to{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}},\qquad x\mapsto[x,x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}],

(3.1)

is a derivation with respect to
[-,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\dots}},-]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
A
be a vector space and
\omega_{n}:A^{\otimes n}\to A
be an
n
-linear operation. Let
\omega_{p}:A^{\otimes p}\to A

be given by

\omega_{p}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{p})=\omega_{n}\Big(a_{1},\dots a_{n-1},\omega_{n}\big(a_{n},\dots,a_{2n-2},\omega_{n}(\dots,\omega_{n}(a_{p-n+1},\dots,a_{p})\dots)\big)\Big).

(3.2)

Then, a derivation
f:A\to A
with respect to
\omega_{n}
is a derivation with respect to
\omega_{p}
.

Proof.

This follows by the following straightforward calculations:

\displaystyle f(\omega_{p}

\displaystyle(a_{1},\dots,a_{p}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\Big(\omega_{n}\Big(a_{1},\dots a_{n-1},\omega_{n}\big(a_{n},\dots,a_{2n-2},\omega_{n}(\dots,\omega_{n}(a_{p-n+1},\dots,a_{p})\dots)\big)\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{n}\Big(f(a_{1}),\dots a_{n-1},\omega_{n}\big(a_{n},\dots,a_{2n-2},\omega_{n}(\dots,\omega_{n}(a_{p-n+1},\dots,a_{p})\dots)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle+\cdots+\omega_{n}\Big(a_{1},\dots f(a_{n-1}),\omega_{n}\big(a_{n},\dots,a_{2n-2},\omega_{n}(\dots,\omega_{n}(a_{p-n+1},\dots,a_{p})\dots)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle+\omega_{n}\Big(a_{1},\dots a_{n-1},f\big(\omega_{n}(a_{n},\dots,a_{2n-2},\omega_{n}(\dots,\omega_{n}(a_{p-n+1},\dots,a_{p})\dots))\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dots=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\omega_{p}(a_{1},\dots,f(a_{i}),\dots,a_{p}).

∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
be a Leibinz
n
-algebra with respect to the
n
-ary bracket
[-,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\dots}},-]:{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}{{}^{\otimes n}}\to\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}
. Then
{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
is a Leibniz
p
-algebra with respect to the
p
-ary bracket

[l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{p}]=[l_{1},\dots l_{n-1},[l_{n},\dots,l_{2n-2},[\dots,[l_{p-n+1},\dots,l_{p}]\dots]]].

Proof.

We know that, for any
x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\in\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}
,
\operatorname{\text{ad}}_{x_{2},\dots,x_{n}}:{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}\to{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
from (
3.1
)
is a derivation with respect to the
n
-ary bracket.
We must show that, for all
x_{2},\dots,x_{p}\in\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}
,
\operatorname{\text{ad}}_{x_{2},\dots,x_{p}}:{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}\to{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
is also a derivation with respect to the described
p
-linear bracket. Immediately by definition of the
p
-linear bracket, we get

\operatorname{\text{ad}}_{x_{2},\dots,x_{p}}=\operatorname{\text{ad}}_{x_{2},\dots x_{n-1},[x_{n},\dots,x_{2n-2},[\dots,[x_{p-n+1},\dots,x_{p}]\dots]]}.

Then the result follows by Proposition
3.2
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Let us observe that Proposition
3.2
and Proposition
3.3
in the particular case

give Proposition 2.2 and Proposition 3.2 in
[
8
]
, respectively.

As a consequence of Proposition

, there exists a functor

\operatorname{\mathfrak{U}}_{n}^{p}:\operatorname{{}_{n}\textbf{{Lb}}}\to\operatorname{{}_{p}\textbf{{Lb}}},\quad{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}\mapsto{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}.

It is clear that, in particular,

is the same as
\operatorname{\mathfrak{U}}^{n}:\operatorname{\textbf{{Lb}}}\to\operatorname{{}_{n}\textbf{{Lb}}}
considered above. Moreover, we have the following commutative diagram of categories and functors

\textstyle{\operatorname{\textbf{{Lb}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{\mathfrak{U}}^{n}}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{\mathfrak{U}}^{p}}
\textstyle{\operatorname{{}_{n}\textbf{{Lb}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{\mathfrak{U}}_{n}^{q}}
\textstyle{\operatorname{{}_{p}\textbf{{Lb}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{\mathfrak{U}}_{p}^{q}}
\textstyle{\operatorname{{}_{q}\textbf{{Lb}}}.}

(3.3)

Theorem 3.5
.

\operatorname{\mathfrak{U}}_{n}^{p}:\operatorname{{}_{n}\textbf{{Lb}}}\to\operatorname{{}_{p}\textbf{{Lb}}}
is an exact functor preserving and reflecting perfect objects.

Proof.

Exactness of
\operatorname{\mathfrak{U}}_{n}^{p}
is obvious. Hence, we have to prove that a Leibniz
n
-algebra
{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
is perfect if and only if
\operatorname{\mathfrak{U}}_{n}^{p}({\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}})
is a perfect Leibniz
p
-algebra.

First suppose that
{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
is a perfect Leibniz
n
-algebra, i.e.
{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}=[{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{n}]
. Then any element
x\in{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
has the form
x=\sum\lambda_{1}[x_{11},\dots,x_{1n}]
. By the same argument, the element
x_{1n}\in\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}
has the form

x_{1n}=\sum\lambda_{2}[x_{21},\dots,x_{2n}]
and so on, after
k
steps (recall that
p=k(n-1)+1
) we get

x=\sum\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\dots\lambda_{k}\omega_{p}(x_{11},\dots,x_{1,n-1},x_{21},\dots,x_{2,n-1},\dots,x_{k1},\dots,x_{kn})\in[{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{p}],

where
\omega_{p}:{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{\otimes p}\to{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
is defined in the same way as in (
3.2
), with
\omega_{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.

Conversely, by definition of
p
-ary bracket on
\operatorname{\mathfrak{U}}_{n}^{p}({\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}})
, it is clear that
[{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{p}]\subseteq[{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{n}]\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}
. So, if

\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}=[{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{p}]
, then
\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}=[{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}^{n}]
.
∎

As a trivial consequence of Theorem
3.5
, we deduce that each functor in the diagram (
3.3
) preserves perfect objects. In particular, if
{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
is a perfect Leibniz algebra, then
{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
is a perfect Leibniz
3
-algebra, a perfect Leibniz
4
-algebra and so on.

4.
\operatorname{\mathfrak{U}}_{p}^{q}
functors and crossed modules

In this section we show that the functor
\operatorname{\mathfrak{U}}_{p}^{q}
can be extended to the respective categories of crossed modules. Before that, let’s recall some necessary notions about actions and crossed modules of Leibniz

-algebras from

Definition 4.1
.

We say that a Leibniz

-algebra
\mathcal{P}
acts on

-algebra

if

\underset{\scriptsize{1\leq k\leq m}}{\sum}(i_{k}+j_{k})=n
,

and at least one

such that

) by taking exactly

and all the

\mathcal{P}

For example, if
\mathcal{L}
is an

\mathcal{P}

-ary bracket in

Let

Since

with

\displaystyle=[x_{1},\dots x_{n-1},[x_{n},\dots,x_{2n-2},[\dots,[x_{p-n+1},\dots,x_{p}]\dots]]],

, but at the same time, at least one of these elements belongs to

Proposition 6.1
.

If
\mathcal{M}
is a trivial co-representation of a Leibniz algebra
\mathcal{L}

Proof.

\mathcal{M}

n

.
Then

is a co-representation of

with respect to the linear maps (actions)

[-,-]:\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\otimes(n-1)}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{L}\quad\text{and}\quad[-,-]:\mathcal{L}^{\otimes(n-1)}\otimes\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{L}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle[m\otimes l,l_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes l_{n-1}]=m\otimes[l,l_{1},\dots,l_{n-1}]

\displaystyle[l_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes l_{n-1},m\otimes l]=-m\otimes[l,l_{1},\dots,l_{n-1}].

\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{L}

\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes\big({\mathcal{L}^{\otimes(n-1)}}\big)^{\otimes k}=\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes{\mathcal{L}^{\otimes k(n-1)}},

whilst

\displaystyle h_{k}

\displaystyle(m\otimes l_{1}^{1}\otimes l_{2}^{1}\otimes\dots\otimes l_{n-1}^{1}\otimes\dots\otimes l_{1}^{k}\otimes l_{2}^{k}\otimes\dots\otimes l_{n-1}^{k})

The details are straightforward but tedious calculations and are left to the reader.

M\subseteq\Z(\mathcal{K})
.

Let us remark that an extension
0\to M\to{\mathcal{K}}\to{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}\to 0
in
\operatorname{{}_{n}\textbf{{Lb}}}
is central if and
only if the ideal
[M,{\mathcal{K}}^{n-1}]
is zero.
Moreover, it is known that the category of Leibniz
n
-algebras is a semi-abelian category and
the definition of central extension agrees with the same notion in the general framework of semi-abelian categories

[
12
]
, particularly, it is induced by the adjunction between
\operatorname{{}_{n}\textbf{{Lb}}}
and its Birkhoff subcategory of vector spaces (see
[
7
]
).

Remark 7.2
.

If (
7.1
) is a central extension of a Leibniz
n
-algebra
\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}
, then
\pi:{\mathcal{K}}\to{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
is a crossed module with the action of
\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}
on
\mathcal{K}
defined in the standard way by choosing pre-images of elements of
\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}
and tacking the
n
-ary bracket in
\mathcal{K}
(see
[
6
, Example 2 (iii)]
).

On the other hand, given a crossed module of Leibniz
n
-algebras
\pi:{\mathcal{K}}\to{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}
, the kernel of
\pi
is in the center of
\mathcal{K}
thanks to the condition (CM2), and we get a central extension
0\to\Ker\pi\to{\mathcal{K}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}\to 0
of
\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}
.

The following example immediately below shows that, unlike the case of the functor
\operatorname{\mathfrak{U}}_{n}
, the functor
\operatorname{\mathfrak{D}}_{n}
does not preserve crossed modules.

Example 7.3
.

Let
\pi:K\to L
be the crossed module of Leibniz
n
-algebras provided by the central extension (
7.1
). Then
\operatorname{\mathfrak{D}}_{n}(\pi)=\pi^{\otimes(n-1)}:\mathcal{K}^{\otimes(n-1)}\to\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes(n-1)}
may not be a crossed module, since the kernel of
\pi^{\otimes(n-1)}
is not in the center of
\mathcal{K}^{\otimes(n-1)}
in general.

Definition 7.4
.

The central extension
(\ref{equation CE})
is said to be universal if for every central extension
0\to M^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi^{\prime}}}{{\to}}{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}}\to 0
there exists a unique homomorphism
h:{\mathcal{K}}\to{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}
such that
\pi^{\prime}\circ h=\pi
.

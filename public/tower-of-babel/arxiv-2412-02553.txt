RC-positivity, Schwarz’s lemma and comparison theorems
Zhiyao Xiong, Xiaokui Yang, Shing-Tung Yau

Abstract
. It is well-known that the classical Schwarz lemma yields an explicit comparison of two Hermitian metrics with uniform constant negative curvature bounds through holomorphic maps between complex manifolds. In this paper, we establish Schwarz lemmas for holomorphic bundle maps between abstract Hermitian holomorphic vector bundles with various positive curvature bounds. As applications, we prove Schwarz lemmas for holomorphic maps between complex manifolds whose curvature tensors are described by the notion “RC-positivity”. In particular, new diameter and volume comparison theorems are obtained by using Schwarz lemmas.



1.
Introduction

The classical Schwarz lemma states that if
f:{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{D}}
is a holomorphic map between two unit disks of the complex plane, then

(1.1)

f^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{P}}\leq\omega_{\mathrm{P}}

where
\omega_{\mathrm{P}}
is the Poincaré metric on
{\mathbb{D}}
. In
[
Ahl38
]
, L. Ahlfors extended this result to holomorphic
maps between Riemann surfaces whose curvature tensors were used in a very explicit way. The study of Schwarz’s lemma for holomorphic maps between higher dimensional complex manifolds was initiated
in
[
Che68
]
by S.-S. Chern. Schwarz’s lemma was further extended by S. Kobayashi, P. Griffiths, H.-H. Wu and others. One of the most
general differential geometric interpretations
of Schwarz’s lemma is the following theorem established by S.-T. Yau (
[
Yau78
]
) and now it is called Yau’s Schwarz lemma. The curvature tensors involved are the Ricci curvature on the domain manifold and the holomorphic bisectional curvature (HBSC) on the target.

Theorem 1.1

(Yau)
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
and
(N,\omega_{h})
be two Kähler manifolds such that

(1.2)

\mathrm{Ric}(M,g)\geq-\lambda\omega_{g}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathrm{HBSC}(N,h)\leq-\kappa

where
\lambda>0
and
\kappa>0
are two constants. If
M
is complete and
f:M\to N
is a non-constant holomorphic map, then

(1.3)

\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{g}}f^{*}\omega_{h}\leq\frac{\lambda}{\kappa}.

As an application of Theorem
1.1
, one deduces that
there is no bounded holomorphic functions on complete Kähler
manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature, which generalizes the
classical Liouville theorem on the complex plane. Moreover, there
is no non-trivial holomorphic maps from complete Kähler manifolds
with non-negative Ricci curvature to Kähler manifolds with
negative holomorphic bisectional curvature. There are many important
generalizations of Yau’s Schwarz lemma. Notably, Chen-Cheng-Lu and
Royden extended Yau’s result to the cases that target manifolds
have negative holomorphic sectional curvature (HSC), and obtained the
following result independently (
[
CCL79
]
and
[
Roy80
]
):

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
and
(N,\omega_{h})
be two Kähler manifolds such that

(1.4)

\mathrm{Ric}(M,g)\geq-\lambda\omega_{g}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathrm{HSC}(N,h)\leq-\kappa,

where
\lambda>0
and
\kappa>0
are two constants. If
M
is complete and
f:M\to N
is a non-constant holomorphic map with maximal complex rank
r_{f}
, then

(1.5)

\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{g}}f^{*}\omega_{h}\leq\frac{2r_{f}}{r_{f}+1}\frac{\lambda}{\kappa}.

Recall that the holomorphic sectional curvature operator of
(N,h)
is given by

(1.6)

\mathrm{HSC}(h,v)=\frac{R^{h}(v,\overline{v},v,\overline{v})}{|v|^{4}_{h}},\ \ \ v\in T^{1,0}N\setminus\{0\}.

The holomorphic sectional curvature of
(N,h)
has negative upper bound
-\kappa
means

(1.7)

R^{h}(v,\overline{v},v,\overline{v})\leq-\kappa|v|^{4}_{h}.

For further extensions of Schwarz’s lemma between complex manifolds,

These Schwarz lemmas have proven to be very useful in both differential geometry and algebraic geometry.

Schwarz’s lemma yields a version of comparison theorems. It is well-known that comparison theorems are important tools for understanding geometric concepts in differential geometry. For complete Riemannian manifolds
(M,g)
with Ricci curvature
\mathrm{Ric}(g)\geq(n-1)g
,
Myers
[
Mye41
]
established the diameter comparison theorem and
Cheng
[
Che75
]
obtained the diameter rigidity theorem.
The Bishop-Gromov volume comparison theorem (e.g.
[
BC64
]
,
[
Gro07
]
,
[
CE08
]
) asserts that
\mathrm{Vol}(M,g)\leq\mathrm{Vol}({\mathbb{S}}^{n},g_{\mathrm{can}})
,
and the identity holds if and only if
(M,g)
is isometric to the round sphere.
For more details along this comprehensive topic, we refer to
[
CC97
]
,
[
Zhu97
]
and
[
Wei07
]
and the references therein.

There are many notable generalized comparison theorems on Kähler manifolds.
For instance, Li and Wang
[
LW05
]
obtained diameter comparison and volume comparison theorems for compact Kähler manifolds with
\mathrm{HBSC}\geq 1
, and Datar and Seshadri
[
DS23
]
established the diameter rigidity theorem. This is achieved by using Siu-Yau’s solution to the Frankel conjecture
[
SY80
]
and an interesting monotonicity formula for Lelong numbers on
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
(
[
Lot21
]
). On the other hand, by using entirely different techniques from algebraic geometry,
Zhang
[
Zha22
]
obtained volume comparison and rigidity theorems under the assumption
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)\geq(n+1)\omega
. Some interesting results are also established in
[
TY12
]
,
[
LY18
]
,
[
NZ18
]
and
[
XY24+
]
.

All the aforementioned Schwarz lemmas are established for complex manifolds with
negative
curvature bounds, and comparison theorems are proved for manifolds with
positive
curvature bounds. In this paper, we establish Schwarz lemmas for holomorphic bundle maps between abstract Hermitian vector bundles whose curvature tensors have
positive
bounds described by RC-positivity. The notion RC-positivity was firstly introduced in
[
Yang18
]
by the second named author, and we refer to
[
Yang18
]
,
[
Yang19
]
,
[
Yang20
]
and
[
Yang24
]
for more geometric interpretations on it. As applications of these new Schwarz lemmas, we obtain several diameter and volume comparison theorems. The first main result of this paper is the following Schwarz lemma by using very weak curvature conditions.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M
be a compact complex manifold and
g,h
be two Hermitian metrics.
If for every
x\in M
and every nonzero
v\in T_{x}^{1,0}M
, there exists some
w\in T_{x}^{1,0}M
satisfying

(1.8)

R^{h}\left(w,\overline{w},v,\overline{v}\right)\leq R^{g}\left(w,\overline{w},v,\overline{v}\right),

and
R^{g}\left(w,\overline{w},v,\overline{v}\right)>0
,
then
\omega_{h}\leq\omega_{g}
.

As an application, we obtain a rigid characterization for Hermitian metrics.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
g
and
h
be two Hermitian metrics on a compact complex manifold
M
.
If for every
x\in M
and every nonzero
v\in T_{x}^{1,0}M
, there exists some
w\in T_{x}^{1,0}M
such that
R^{h}\left(w,\overline{w},v,\overline{v}\right)=R^{g}\left(w,\overline{w},v,\overline{v}\right)>0
,
then
\omega_{h}=\omega_{g}
.

As another application of Theorem
1.3
, we obtain the following diameter and volume comparison theorem by using RC-positivity.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
g
and
h
be two Hermitian metrics on a compact complex manifold
M
.
If for every
x\in M
and every nonzero
v\in T_{x}^{1,0}M
, there exists some
w\in T_{x}^{1,0}M
such that
R^{g}\left(w,\overline{w},v,\overline{v}\right)>0
, and

(1.9)

R^{h}\left(w,\overline{w},v,\overline{v}\right)\leq R^{g}\left(w,\overline{w},v,\overline{v}\right),

then
\mathrm{diam}(M,h)\leq\mathrm{diam}(M,g)
and
\mathrm{Vol}(M,h)\leq\mathrm{Vol}(M,g)
.

The following special case is of particular interest.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Hermitian manifold with positive holomorphic sectional curvature. If
h
is another Hermitian metric on
M
satisfying

(1.10)

R^{h}(v,\overline{v},v,\overline{v})\leq R^{g}(v,\overline{v},v,\overline{v}),

for any
v\in T^{1,0}M
, then

\omega_{h}\leq\omega_{g}
.
In particular,

(1.11)

\mathrm{diam}(M,h)\leq\mathrm{diam}(M,g)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mathrm{Vol}(M,h)\leq\mathrm{Vol}(M,g).

We also obtain similar comparison theorems for complex manfiolds with negative holomorphic sectional curvature.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
(M,h)
be a compact Hermitian manifold with negative holomorphic sectional curvature. If
g
is another Hermitian metric on
M
satisfying

(1.12)

\mathrm{HSC}(h,v)\leq\mathrm{HSC}(g,v),

for each nonzero
v\in T^{1,0}M
, then

(1.13)

\omega_{h}\leq\omega_{g}.

In particular, we have
\mathrm{diam}(M,h)\leq\mathrm{diam}(M,g)
and
\mathrm{Vol}(M,h)\leq\mathrm{Vol}(M,g)
.

The proofs of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.7
are obtained in a general setting for abstract vector bundle. Let’s fix the setup for readers’ convenience.
Let
f:M\rightarrow N
be a holomorphic map between complex manifolds, and
g
,
h
be Hermitian metrics on
M
and
N
respectively. Suppose that

(E_{1},G)\rightarrow M
and
(E_{2},H)\rightarrow N
are two Hermitian holomorphic vector bundles. If
\varphi:E_{1}\to E_{2}
is a holomorphic bundle map, then there is an induced bundle map
\Phi:E_{1}\rightarrow f^{*}E_{2}
.
Let
\widehat{E}_{2}=f^{*}E_{2}
be the pullback bundle over
M
and
\widehat{H}
be the induced metric on
\widehat{E}_{2}
. The Chern curvature tensors of
(E_{1},G)
,
(E_{2},H)
and
(\widehat{E}_{2},\widehat{H})
are denoted by
R^{E_{1}}
,
R^{E_{2}}
and
R^{\widehat{E}_{2}}
respectively. Without loss of generality, we assume that
M
is compact;
f:M\rightarrow N
and
\varphi:E_{1}\rightarrow E_{2}
are non-trivial.

1.A.
Schwarz lemmas for RC-positive vector bundles

By using the previous setup, we obtain Schwarz lemmas for holomorphic bundle maps between abstract Hermitian vector bundles whose curvature tensors have positive bounds.

Theorem 1.8
.

If there is a constant
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that: for every
x\in M
and every non-zero
\xi\in(E_{1})_{x}
,
there exists some
w\in T_{x}^{1,0}M
satisfying

(1.14)

R^{E_{1}}\left(w,\overline{w},\xi,\overline{\xi}\right)>0

and

(1.15)

R^{\widehat{E}_{2}}\left(w,\overline{w},\Phi(\xi),\overline{\Phi(\xi)}\right)\leq\kappa\cdot R^{E_{1}}\left(w,\overline{w},\xi,\overline{\xi}\right),

then
\kappa>0
and

(1.16)

\varphi^{*}H\leq\kappa\cdot G.

The proof of Theorem
1.8
relies on a maximum principle for abstract vector bundles with desired curvature positivity. There are many applications of Theorem
1.8
. For instance, if
(E_{1},G)=\left(T^{1,0}M,g\right)
,
\left(E_{2},H\right)=\left(T^{1,0}N,h\right)
and
\varphi=f_{*}
, we obtain:

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Hermitian manifold
and
(N,h)
be another Hermitian manifold.
If
f:M\rightarrow N
is a non-constant holomorphic map such that for every
x\in M
and every nonzero
v\in T_{x}^{1,0}M
, there exists some
w\in T_{x}^{1,0}M
satisfying

(1.17)

R^{g}\left(w,\overline{w},v,\overline{v}\right)>0\quad\mbox{and}\quad R^{h}\left(f_{*}w,\overline{f_{*}w},f_{*}v,\overline{f_{*}v}\right)\leq\kappa\cdot R^{g}\left(w,\overline{w},v,\overline{v}\right),

where
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a constant,
then
\kappa>0
and

(1.18)

f^{*}\omega_{h}\leq\kappa\omega_{g}.

As a special case, one has

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Hermitian manifold with positive holomorphic sectional curvature,
and
(N,h)
be another Hermitian manifold.
If
f:M\to N
is a non-trivial holomorphic map such that for every
x\in M
and every
v\in T_{x}^{1,0}M
one has

(1.19)

R^{h}\left(f_{*}v,\overline{f_{*}v},f_{*}v,\overline{f_{*}v}\right)\leq\kappa\cdot R^{g}\left(v,\overline{v},v,\overline{v}\right),

where
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a constant, then
\kappa>0
and

(1.20)

f^{*}\omega_{h}\leq\kappa\omega_{g}.

It is clear that Theorem
1.3
follows from Corollary
1.9
, and Corollary
1.6
follows from Corollary
1.10
.

1.B.
Schwarz lemmas for RC-negative vector bundles

By using similar methods as in the proof of Theorem
1.8
, we can derive Schwarz lemmas for holomorphic bundle maps between abstract Hermitian vector bundles with negative curvature bounds, which extend classical Schwarz lemmas in
[
Yau78
]
,
[
CCL79
]
,
[
Roy80
]
and etc..

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Hermitian manifold,
and
(N,h)
be another Hermitian manifold with negative holomorphic sectional curvature.
If
f:M\to N
is a non-constant holomorphic map such that for every
x\in M
and every
v\in T_{x}^{1,0}M
with
f_{*}v\neq 0
, one has

(1.21)

\kappa\cdot\mathrm{HSC}\left(h,f_{*}v\right)\leq\mathrm{HSC}\left(g,v\right),

where
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a constant, then
\kappa>0
and

(1.22)

f^{*}\omega_{h}\leq\kappa\omega_{g}.

Theorem
1.7
is a consequence of Theorem
1.11
.

As another straightforward application of Theorem
1.11
, it is easy to deduce the following classical version of Schwarz’s lemma for holomorphic maps between complex manifolds with constant negative holomorphic sectional curvature bounds (e.g.
[
CCL79
]
,
[
Roy80
]
,
[
Ni19
]
,
[
Bro22
]
and etc.).

Corollary 1.12
.

Let
(M,g)

and
(N,h)
be two Hermitian manifolds.
Suppose that there exist two constants
\kappa>0
and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

(1.23)

\mathrm{HSC}(M,g)\geq-\lambda\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathrm{HSC}(N,h)\leq-\kappa.

If
M
is compact and
f:M\rightarrow N
is a non-constant holomorphic map, then
\lambda>0
and

(1.24)

f^{*}\omega_{h}\leq\frac{\lambda}{\kappa}\omega_{g}.

1.C.
Schwarz lemmas for constant positive curvature bounds

It is well-known that classical Schwarz lemmas are derived from Chern-Lu identities for holomorphic maps between complex manifolds. We obtain a version of Chern-Lu type identity for holomorphic bundle maps between Hermitian vector bundles. As applications, we establish the following Schwarz lemma for abstract vector bundles with positive curvature bounds.

Theorem 1.13
.

If there exist two constants
\lambda>0
and
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

(1.25)

\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{g}}R^{E_{1}}\geq\lambda\varphi^{*}H,

and for all
y\in N
,
v\in T_{y}^{1,0}N
and
s\in(E_{2})_{y}
,

(1.26)

R^{E_{2}}\left(v,\overline{v},s,\overline{s}\right)\leq\kappa|v|_{h}^{2}|s|_{H}^{2},

then
\kappa>0
and

(1.27)

\sup_{M}\mathrm{tr_{G}}\varphi^{*}H\leq\frac{\kappa r_{0}}{\lambda}\left(\sup_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{g}}f^{*}\omega_{h}\right)

where
r_{0}
is the maximal rank of the bundle map
\varphi:E_{1}\rightarrow E_{2}
.

If we set
(E_{1},G)=\left(T^{1,0}M,g\right)
,
\left(E_{2},H\right)=\left(T^{1,0}N,h\right)
and
\varphi=f_{*}
in Theorem
1.13
, then one can see clearly that

\lambda\leq\kappa r_{0}.

This is a version of Yau’s Schwarz lemma for constant positive curvature bounds. Indeed, we also obtain a version of Royden’s Schwarz lemma.

Theorem 1.14
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a complete Kähler manifold of dimension
n

and
(N,\omega_{h})
be another Kähler manifold. If
f:M\to N
is a non-trivial holomorphic map and

(1.28)

\mathrm{Ric}(M,\omega_{g})\geq\lambda f^{*}\omega_{h}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathrm{HSC}(N,\omega_{h})\leq\kappa,

where
\lambda>0
and
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
are two constants, then
\kappa>0
and

(1.29)

\lambda\leq\frac{\left(r_{f}+1\right)\kappa}{2},

where
r_{f}
is the maximal complex rank of the holomorphic tangent map
df
.

The key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.14
is a combined curvature estimate in the refined
Schwarz calculation, which is different from the classical one for manifolds with constant negative curvature bounds.
There is an explanation on the difference between estimates (
1.5
) and (
1.29
).
When
M
is compact,
\mathrm{Ric}(M,\omega_{g})\geq\lambda f^{*}\omega_{h}
can be implied by the condition

\mathrm{Ric}(M,\omega_{g})\geq\lambda_{1}\omega_{g}
since one has
\omega_{g}\geq c_{0}f^{*}\omega_{h}
for some
c_{0}>0
.
This constant
c_{0}
plays a similar role as
\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{g}}f^{*}\omega_{h}
.
We also obtain a rigid characterization in a slightly general setting:

Theorem 1.15
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold of dimension
n
,
and
(N,\omega_{h})
be a Kähler manifold. If
f:M\to N
is a non-trivial holomorphic map and

(1.30)

\mathrm{Ric}^{\mathrm{(2)}}(M,\omega_{g})\geq\lambda f^{*}\omega_{h}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathrm{HSC}(N,\omega_{h})\leq\kappa,

where
\lambda>0
and
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
are two constants, then
\kappa>0
and

(1.31)

\lambda\leq\frac{\left(n+1\right)\kappa}{2}.

Moreover, if the identity in (
1.31
) holds,
then
f^{*}\omega_{h}=c\omega_{g}
for some
c>0

and
(M,\omega_{g})
is isometrically biholomorphic to
\left({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n},2c^{-1}\kappa^{-1}\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}\right)
.

The following version is also of particular interest.

Theorem 1.16
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold of dimension
n
,
(N,\omega_{h})
be a Kähler manifold,
and
f:M\to N
be a non-constant holomorphic map.
If there exist two constants
\lambda>0
and
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

(1.32)

\mathrm{Ric}^{\mathrm{(2)}}(\omega_{g})\geq\lambda\omega_{g}\quad\mbox{and}\quad R^{h}\left(f_{*}v,\overline{f_{*}v},f_{*}v,\overline{f_{*}v}\right)\leq\kappa|v|^{2}_{g}|f_{*}v|^{2}_{h},

for any
v\in T^{1,0}M
, then
\kappa>0
and

(1.33)

\lambda\leq\frac{\left(n+1\right)\kappa}{2}.

Moreover, if the identity in (
1.33
) holds, then
(M,\omega_{g})
is isometrically biholomorphic to
\left({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n},2\kappa^{-1}\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}\right)
.

1.D.
Rigidity of holomorphic maps between vector bundles

It is well-known that Schwarz lemmas can give Liouville type rigidity theorems for holomorphic maps. In this subsection, we present Liouville type rigidity theorems corresponding to Schwarz lemmas in Theorem
1.8
.

We recall the concept of RC-positivity and the relationships between various classical notions of positivity for readers’ convenience. Let
(E,H)
be a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle over a complex manifold
M
.
We say that
(E,H)
is
k
-RC positive, if for every
x\in M
and every non-zero
\xi\in E_{x}
,
there exists a
k
-dimensional linear subspace
W
of
T_{x}^{1,0}M
such that for all non-zero
w\in W
,

R^{E}\left(w,\overline{w},\xi,\overline{\xi}\right)>0.

We can define
k
-RC non-positivity,
k
-RC non-negativity and

k
-RC negativity in similar ways. Suppose that
(M,\omega_{g})
is
a Hermitian manifold of dimension
n
. The following statements
follow easily and we refer to
[
Yang18
]
for more details.

(1)

If
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{g})>0
or
\mathrm{HSC}(\omega_{g})>0
, then
(T^{1,0}M,g)
is
1
-RC positive;

(2)

If
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{g})<0
or
\mathrm{HSC}(\omega_{g})<0
, then
(T^{1,0}M,g)
is
1
-RC negative;

(3)

If
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{g})\leq 0
or
\mathrm{HSC}(\omega_{g})\leq 0
, then
(T^{1,0}M,g)
is
1
-RC non-positive;

(4)

\mathrm{HBSC}(\omega_{g})>0
if and only if
(T^{1,0}M,g)
is
n
-RC positive;

(5)

\mathrm{HBSC}(\omega_{g})<0
if and only if
(T^{1,0}M,g)
is
n
-RC negative;

(6)

\mathrm{HBSC}(\omega_{g})\leq 0
if and only if
(T^{1,0}M,g)
is
n
-RC non-positive.

We obtain a type of Liouville rigidity theorem for vector bundles corresponding to Theorem
1.8
.

Theorem 1.17
.

Let
f:M\rightarrow N
be a holomorphic map. Suppose that the Hermitian vector bundle

(E_{1},G)
is
k
-RC positive, and
(E_{2},H)
is
s
-RC non-positive. If
M
is compact and

k+s>\dim N,

then any holomorphic bundle map
\varphi:E_{1}\rightarrow E_{2}
must be trivial.

In particular, one has the following rigidity theorem for holomorphic maps between complex manifolds.

Corollary 1.18
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Hermitian manifold such that
(T^{1,0}M,g)
is
k
-RC positive,
and
(N,h)
be a Hermitian manifold such that
(T^{1,0}N,h)
is
s
-RC non-positive.
If
k+s>\dim N
, then any holomorphic map
f:M\to N
must be constant.

By using the relationships between
k
-RC positivity and classical curvature notions, it is easy to see that Corollary
1.18
generalizes many classical Liouville theorems. Moreover, Corollary
1.18
can give many new rigidity theorems. It is well-known that there are many projective manifolds
X
with
c_{1}(X)\leq 0
which can contain rational curves, i.e., there exist non-constant holomorphic maps
f:{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\rightarrow X
. For instances,

(1)

projective
K3
surfaces
X
and
c_{1}(X)=0
;

(2)

quintic surfaces
X
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
and
c_{1}(X)<0
;

(3)

some projective Calabi-Yau manifolds.

Note that, as the domain manifold of holomorphic maps into
X
,
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
has positive curvature operator.
As an application of Corollary
1.18
, we obtain rigidity theorems for holomorphic maps into target manifolds with
c_{1}(X)\leq 0
.

Corollary 1.19
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold with
c_{1}(X)\leq 0
and
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X=\ell
. If
(M,g)
is a compact Hermitian manifold such that
(T^{1,0}M,g)
is
\ell
-RC positive, then any holomorphic map

must be a constant map.

There is a rigidity theorem corresponding to Theorem
2.2
.

Theorem 1.20
.

Let
f:M\rightarrow N
be a holomorphic map between complex manifolds and

be the minimal rank of the holomorphic tangent map
df
.
Suppose that

is

-RC non-negative and
(E_{2},H)
is

-RC negative. If

is compact and

In particular, one has

.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Hermitian manifold such that

is

-RC non-negative,

be another Hermitian manifold such that

is

-RC negative.

2.
Schwarz’s lemma and RC-positivity

In this section, we establish Schwarz lemmas for abstract vector bundles with RC-positive or RC-negative curvature bounds, and prove Theorem
1.8
and Theorem
1.11
. Let’s recall Theorem
1.8
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f:M\rightarrow N
be a non-constant holomorphic map between two complex manifolds and
M
be compact. Let

(E_{1},G)\rightarrow M
and
(E_{2},H)\rightarrow N
be two Hermitian holomorphic vector bundles and
\varphi:E_{1}\to E_{2}
be a non-trivial holomorphic bundle map.
Suppose that for every
x\in M
and every non-zero
\xi\in(E_{1})_{x}
,
there exists some
w\in T_{x}^{1,0}M
such that

(2.1)

R^{E_{1}}\left(w,\overline{w},\xi,\overline{\xi}\right)>0

and

(2.2)

R^{E_{2}}\left(f_{*}w,\overline{f_{*}w},\varphi(\xi),\overline{\varphi(\xi)}\right)\leq\kappa\cdot R^{E_{1}}\left(w,\overline{w},\xi,\overline{\xi}\right),

where
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a constant, then
\kappa>0
and

(2.3)

\varphi^{*}H\leq\kappa\cdot G.

Proof.

We define a continuous function
\mu:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
as follows

(2.4)

\mu(x)=\sup_{\xi\in(E_{1})_{x}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|\varphi(\xi)|_{H}^{2}}{|\xi|_{G}^{2}}.

We shall prove that
\sup_{M}\mu\leq\kappa
. This implies
\kappa>0
and the estimate in (
2.3
).

Since
M
is compact,
\mu
attains its maximum at some point
x_{0}\in M
.
Moreover, there exists a non-zero
\xi\in(E_{1})_{x_{0}}
such that

\mu(x_{0})=\frac{|\varphi(\xi)|_{H}^{2}}{|\xi|_{G}^{2}}.

We extend
\xi
to a local non-vanishing holomorphic section
s\in H^{0}(U,E_{1})
, where
U
is an open neighborhood of
p\in M
, such that

(2.5)

s(x_{0})=\xi\quad\mbox{and}\quad(\nabla^{G}s)(x_{0})=0,

where
\nabla^{G}
is the Chern connection of
(E_{1},G)
.
Note that
\varphi
induces a holomorphic bundle map
\Phi=\varphi_{*}:E_{1}\to\widehat{E}_{2}=f^{*}E_{2}
and

\Phi(s)\in H^{0}\left(U,\widehat{E}_{2}\right).

We define a function
F:U\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

(2.6)

F(x)=\frac{|\Phi(s)|^{2}_{\widehat{H}}}{|s|_{G}^{2}}(x).

Here
\widehat{H}
is the induced metric on
\widehat{E}_{2}
.
It is clear that

(2.7)

F(x_{0})=\sup_{U}F\quad\mbox{and}\quad F(x_{0})=\mu(x_{0}).

Since
\mu(x_{0})>0
,
F
is positive near
x_{0}
.
Hence, at
x_{0}\in M
, for any
w\in T^{1,0}_{x_{0}}M
, one has

(2.8)

0\geq\left(\partial\overline{\partial}\log F\right)(w,\overline{w})=\left(\partial\overline{\partial}\log|\Phi(s)|^{2}_{\widehat{H}}\right)(w,\overline{w})-\left(\partial\overline{\partial}\log|s|_{G}^{2}\right)(w,\overline{w}).

Note that
\Phi(s)(x_{0})=\varphi(\xi)
. Let
\widehat{\nabla}
be the induced connection on
\left(\widehat{E}_{2},\widehat{H}\right)
.
At point
x_{0}
, one has the estimate:

(2.9)

\left(\partial\overline{\partial}\log|\Phi(s)|^{2}_{\widehat{H}}\right)(w,\overline{w})\geq-\frac{R^{E_{2}}\left(f_{*}w,\overline{f_{*}w},\varphi(\xi),\overline{\varphi(\xi)}\right)}{|\varphi(\xi)|^{2}_{H}}.

Indeed, a straightforward computation shows that

\left(\partial\overline{\partial}\log|\Phi(s)|^{2}_{\widehat{H}}\right)(w,\overline{w})=\frac{\left(\partial\overline{\partial}|\Phi(s)|^{2}_{\widehat{H}}\right)(w,\overline{w})}{|\varphi(\xi)|^{2}_{H}}-\frac{\left(\partial|\Phi(s)|^{2}_{\widehat{H}}\wedge\overline{\partial}|\Phi(s)|^{2}_{\widehat{H}}\right)(w,\overline{w})}{|\varphi(\xi)|^{4}_{H}}

By using Bochner technique, the right hand side can be written as

\frac{\left|\widehat{\nabla}_{w}(\Phi(s))\right|_{H}^{2}-R^{E_{2}}\left(f_{*}w,\overline{f_{*}w},\varphi(\xi),\overline{\varphi(\xi)}\right)}{|\varphi(\xi)|^{2}_{H}}-\frac{\left|\left\langle\widehat{\nabla}_{w}(\Phi(s)),\varphi(\xi)\right\rangle_{H}\right|^{2}}{|\varphi(\xi)|^{4}_{H}}.

On the other hand, we have the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

(2.10)

\left|\left\langle\widehat{\nabla}_{w}(\Phi(s)),\varphi(\xi)\right\rangle_{H}\right|^{2}\leq\left|\widehat{\nabla}_{w}(\Phi(s))\right|_{H}^{2}\cdot|\varphi(\xi)|_{H}^{2}.

Hence, we obtain (
2.9
).
Similarly, at point
x_{0}
, one has

\left(\partial\overline{\partial}\log|s|^{2}_{G}\right)(w,\overline{w})=\frac{\left|\nabla^{G}_{w}s\right|_{G}^{2}-R^{E_{1}}\left(w,\overline{w},\xi,\overline{\xi}\right)}{|\xi|_{G}^{2}}-\frac{\left|\left\langle\nabla^{G}_{w}s,\xi\right\rangle_{G}\right|^{2}}{|\xi|_{G}^{4}}.

By using the fact that
(\nabla^{G}s)(x_{0})=0
, we obtain

\left(\partial\overline{\partial}\log|s|^{2}_{G}\right)(w,\overline{w})=-\frac{R^{E_{1}}\left(w,\overline{w},\xi,\overline{\xi}\right)}{|\xi|_{G}^{2}}.

Therefore we get the inequality

(2.11)

0\geq\left(\partial\overline{\partial}\log F\right)(w,\overline{w})\geq\frac{R^{E_{1}}\left(w,\overline{w},\xi,\overline{\xi}\right)}{|\xi|_{G}^{2}}-\frac{R^{E_{2}}\left(f_{*}w,\overline{f_{*}w},\varphi(\xi),\overline{\varphi(\xi)}\right)}{|\varphi(\xi)|^{2}_{H}}.

In particular, if we take
w
as in (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), then

(2.12)

0\geq\frac{R^{E_{1}}\left(w,\overline{w},\xi,\overline{\xi}\right)}{|\xi|_{G}^{2}}-\frac{\kappa R^{E_{1}}\left(w,\overline{w},\xi,\overline{\xi}\right)}{|\varphi(\xi)|^{2}_{H}}.

By using (
2.1
), one obtains

(2.13)

\mu(x_{0})=F(x_{0})=\frac{|\varphi(\xi)|^{2}_{H}}{|\xi|_{G}^{2}}\leq\kappa,

which completes the proof of Theorem
2.1
.
∎

.

.

where

.

.

3.
Schwarz’s lemma and non-negative second Chern-Ricci curvature

In this section, we establish Chern-Lu type identities for holomorphic bundle maps and prove Theorem
1.13
. We recall the setup for readers’ convenience. Let
f:M\to N
be a holomorphic map. Suppose that
(E_{1},G)\rightarrow M
and
(E_{2},H)\rightarrow N
are two Hermitian vector bundles. If
\varphi:E_{1}\to E_{2}
is a holomorphic bundle map, then there is an induced bundle map
\Phi:E_{1}\rightarrow f^{*}E_{2}
.
Let
\widehat{E}_{2}=f^{*}E_{2}
and
\widehat{H}
be the induced metric on
\widehat{E}_{2}
. For simplicity, we set
E=E_{1}^{*}\otimes\widehat{E}_{2}
and there is an induced metric
G^{*}\otimes\widehat{H}
on
E
. Since
\varphi
is a holomorphic bundle map,
\Phi
is a holomorphic section of
E
, i.e.
\Phi\in H^{0}(M,E)
. We have the following Bochner formula

(3.1)

\partial\overline{\partial}|\Phi|^{2}=\left\{\nabla\Phi,\nabla\Phi\right\}-\left\{R^{E}(\Phi),\Phi\right\},

where
R^{E}
is the Chern curvature of
(E,G^{*}\otimes\widehat{H})
.

Suppose that
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
have complex ranks
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
respectively.
Let
\{e_{a}\}_{1\leq a\leq r_{1}}
and
\{\widetilde{e}_{A}\}_{1\leq A\leq r_{2}}
be local holomorphic frames of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
respectively.
The bundle map
\varphi
is given by

(3.2)

\varphi(e_{a})=\sum_{A}\varphi_{a}^{A}\cdot\widetilde{e}_{A},

where
\varphi_{a}^{A}
are local holomorphic functions on
M
.
If we set
\widehat{e}_{A}=f^{*}\widetilde{e}_{A}
, then

(3.3)

\Phi(e_{a})=\sum_{A}\varphi_{a}^{A}\cdot\widehat{e}_{A}.

Let
\{z^{i}\}
and
\{w^{\alpha}\}
be local holomorphic coordinates on
M
and
N
respectively. The holomorphic tangent map
df
is represented by
f_{i}^{\alpha}=\frac{\partial f^{\alpha}}{\partial z^{i}}
. It is easy to see that the Chern curvature of
\left(\widehat{E}_{2},\widehat{H}\right)
is given by

(3.4)

R^{E_{2}}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}A\overline{B}}f_{i}^{\alpha}\overline{f_{j}^{\beta}}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}\otimes\widehat{e}^{A}\otimes\overline{\widehat{e}^{B}}.

By using the curvature formula

R^{E}=R^{E_{1}^{*}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\widehat{E}_{2}}+\mathrm{id}_{E_{1}^{*}}\otimes R^{\widehat{E}_{2}},

we obtain Chern-Lu type identities for holomorphic bundle maps.

Lemma 3.1
.

The local expression of formula (
3.1
) is

(3.5)

\partial\overline{\partial}|\Phi|^{2}=\left\{\nabla\Phi,\nabla\Phi\right\}+\left[R^{E_{1}}_{i\overline{j}a\overline{b}}G^{a\overline{d}}G^{c\overline{b}}\varphi_{c}^{A}\overline{\varphi_{d}^{B}}H_{A\overline{B}}-R^{E_{2}}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}A\overline{B}}f_{i}^{\alpha}\overline{f_{j}^{\beta}}\varphi_{a}^{A}\overline{\varphi_{b}^{B}}G^{a\overline{b}}\right]dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}.

Moreover,

(3.6)

\Box_{g}|\Phi|^{2}=|\nabla\Phi|^{2}+g^{i\overline{j}}R^{E_{1}}_{i\overline{j}a\overline{b}}G^{a\overline{d}}G^{c\overline{b}}\varphi_{c}^{A}\overline{\varphi_{d}^{B}}H_{A\overline{B}}-R^{E_{2}}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}A\overline{B}}g^{i\overline{j}}f_{i}^{\alpha}\overline{f_{j}^{\beta}}\varphi_{a}^{A}\overline{\varphi_{b}^{B}}G^{a\overline{b}},

where
\Box_{g}|\Phi|^{2}=\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{g}}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}|\Phi|^{2})
.

As an application of Lemma
3.1
, we obtain Theorem
1.13
. That is:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
f:(M,g)\rightarrow(N,h)
be a non-constant holomorphic map between two Hermitian manifolds and
M
be compact. Suppose that

(E_{1},G)\rightarrow M
and
(E_{2},H)\rightarrow N
are two Hermitian vector bundles, and
\varphi:E_{1}\to E_{2}
is a non-trivial holomorphic bundle map. Suppose that there exist two constants
\lambda>0
and
\kappa>0
such that

(3.7)

\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{g}}R^{E_{1}}\geq\lambda\varphi^{*}H,

and for all
y\in N
,
v\in T_{y}^{1,0}N
and
s\in(E_{2})_{y}
, there holds

(3.8)

R^{E_{2}}\left(v,\overline{v},s,\overline{s}\right)\leq\kappa|v|_{h}^{2}|s|_{H}^{2}.

Then one has

(3.9)

\sup_{M}\mathrm{tr_{G}}\varphi^{*}H\leq\frac{\kappa r_{0}}{\lambda}\left(\sup_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{g}}f^{*}\omega_{h}\right)

where
r_{0}
is the maximal rank of the bundle map
\varphi:E_{1}\rightarrow E_{2}
.

Proof.

At a fixed point
x\in M
, there exist local holomorphic coordinates
\{z^{i}\}
and
\{w^{\alpha}\}
around
x\in M
and
f(x)\in N
respectively such that

g_{i\overline{j}}(x)=\delta_{ij},\quad h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}(f(x))=\delta_{\alpha\beta}\quad\mbox{and}\quad f_{i}^{\alpha}(x)=\lambda_{i}\delta_{i}^{\alpha}

with
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{r}>\lambda_{r+1}=\cdots=\lambda_{n}=0
, where
r
is the complex rank of
\left[f_{i}^{\alpha}\right]
.
Similarly, there exist local holomorphic frames
\{e_{a}\}
and
\{\widetilde{e}_{A}\}
of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
respectively such that

G_{a\overline{b}}(x)=\delta_{ab},\quad H_{A\overline{B}}(f(x))=\delta_{AB}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\varphi_{a}^{A}(x)=\Lambda_{a}\delta_{a}^{A}

with
\Lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\Lambda_{r^{\prime}}>\Lambda_{r^{\prime}+1}=\cdots=\Lambda_{r_{1}}=0
, where
r^{\prime}
is the rank of
\left[\varphi_{a}^{A}\right]
. It is easy to see that

|\Phi|^{2}=\mathrm{tr_{G}}\varphi^{*}H=G^{a\overline{b}}H_{A\overline{B}}\varphi^{A}_{a}\overline{\varphi^{B}_{b}}=\sum\Lambda_{a}^{2},\ \ \ |df|^{2}=\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{g}}f^{*}\omega_{h}=\sum\lambda_{i}^{2}.

3.7

\displaystyle-R^{E_{2}}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}A\overline{B}}g^{i\overline{j}}f_{i}^{\alpha}\overline{f_{j}^{\beta}}\varphi_{a}^{A}\overline{\varphi_{b}^{B}}G^{a\overline{b}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m}\sum_{a=1}^{r_{1}}\sum_{A=1}^{r_{2}}R^{E_{2}}_{\alpha\overline{\alpha}A\overline{A}}\lambda_{i}^{2}\delta_{i}^{\alpha}\Lambda_{a}^{2}\delta_{a}^{A}

f:(M,\omega_{g})\rightarrow(N,\omega_{h})

\Box_{g}u=|\nabla df|^{2}+\left(g^{i\overline{j}}R^{g}_{i\overline{j}k\overline{\ell}}\right)g^{k\overline{q}}g^{p\overline{\ell}}h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}f^{\alpha}_{p}\overline{f^{\beta}_{q}}-R^{h}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}\gamma\overline{\delta}}\left(g^{i\overline{j}}f^{\alpha}_{i}\overline{f^{\beta}_{j}}\right)\left(g^{p\overline{q}}f^{\gamma}_{p}\overline{f^{\delta}_{q}}\right),

Proof of Theorem

0\geq\frac{R^{E_{1}}\left(w,\overline{w},\xi,\overline{\xi}\right)}{|\xi|_{G}^{2}}-\frac{R^{E_{2}}\left(f_{*}w,\overline{f_{*}w},\varphi(\xi),\overline{\varphi(\xi)}\right)}{|\varphi(\xi)|^{2}_{H}}>0,

and this is again
a contradiction. Therefore,
\varphi
must be a trivial map. ∎

Proof of Theorem
1.20
.

Suppose by contradiction that
\varphi
is non-trivial.
We use the same setup as in the proof of Theorem
2.1
.
In particular, we have the estimate (
2.11
) at point
x_{0}\in M
, i.e., for any
w\in T^{1,0}_{x_{0}}M
,

(5.4)

0\geq\left(\partial\overline{\partial}\log F\right)(w,\overline{w})\geq\frac{R^{E_{1}}\left(w,\overline{w},\xi,\overline{\xi}\right)}{|\xi|_{G}^{2}}-\frac{R^{E_{2}}\left(f_{*}w,\overline{f_{*}w},\varphi(\xi),\overline{\varphi(\xi)}\right)}{|\varphi(\xi)|^{2}_{H}}.

Since
(E_{1},G)
is
k
-RC non-negative,
there exists a
k
-dimensional linear subspace
W
of
T_{x_{0}}^{1,0}M
such that for all
w\in W

R^{E_{1}}\left(w,\overline{w},\xi,\overline{\xi}\right)\geq 0.

Let
r_{0}
be the complex rank of
df
at
x_{0}
. One can see clearly that

\dim df(W)\geq\dim W-\dim\ker\left(df(x_{0})\right)=k-(\dim M-r_{0})\geq k+r-\dim M.

Since
(E_{2},H)
is
s
-RC negative and
\left(k+r-\dim M\right)+s>\dim N
, there exists some non-zero
w\in W
such that

R^{E_{1}}\left(w,\overline{w},\xi,\overline{\xi}\right)\geq 0\quad\mbox{and}\quad R^{E_{2}}\left(f_{*}w,\overline{f_{*}w},\varphi(\xi),\overline{\varphi(\xi)}\right)<0.

Plugging this
w
into (
5.4
), one obtains

(5.5)

0\geq\frac{R^{E_{1}}\left(w,\overline{w},\xi,\overline{\xi}\right)}{|\xi|_{g}^{2}}-\frac{R^{E_{2}}\left(f_{*}w,\overline{f_{*}w},\varphi(\xi),\overline{\varphi(\xi)}\right)}{|\varphi(\xi)|^{2}_{h}}>0,

and this is
a contradiction. Therefore,
\varphi
must be a trivial map. ∎

Corollary 5.1
.

Let

(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold and
(N,\omega_{h})
be
a Hermitian manifold. If

(M,\omega_{g})
is RC-positive;

(2)

(N,\omega_{h})

then any holomorphic map

Corollary 5.2
.

);

(2)

(N,\omega_{h})
has
\mathrm{HSC}\leq 0
(resp.

),

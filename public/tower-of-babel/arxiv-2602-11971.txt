Hypercovers in Differential Geometry
Cheyne Glass 1, Emilio Minichiello 2

Abstract
. In this paper we provide a simple proof that for several sites of interest in differential geometry, the local projective model structure and the Čech projective model structure are equal. In particular, this applies to the site of smooth manifolds with open covers and the site of cartesian spaces with good open covers. As an application, we show that for a presheaf of sets on these sites, applying the plus construction once is enough to sheafify.

Next we turn to the issue of how one computes fibrant replacement in
\check{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbf{Man},j_{\text{open}})

\infty

Many higher sheaves in higher differential geometry are constructed by defining a structure that is locally trivial, and then taking some sort of “stackification” to obtain a nontrivial global structure. Perhaps the best example of this is the construction of abelian bundle gerbes by Nikolaus and Schweigert in
[

\hat{A}(U)\simeq\underset{H\in\mathbf{Hyp}^{\text{op}}_{U}}{\text{ho}\text{colim}}\,\underline{\mathbf{sPre}}(\mathcal{C})(H,A),

Remark 2.30
.

In what follows, we always assume (topological, smooth, complex) manifolds are Hausdorff, second countable and have the same dimension in each connected component. In particular, a manifold can only have countably many connected components. Furthermore we do not consider the empty set a manifold for technical reasons
7
7

7

In the case that we do include the empty set as a manifold, then not all of the coverages on

for smooth manifolds or a direct set-theoretic argument

for each of these cases, one can show that each of these categories is essentially small. Hence we will silently replace each of these categories by one of their small equivalent categories.

Remark 2.34
.

Let us note that sometimes in the literature

is taken to consist of covering families

Nothing is lost in working with open embeddings rather than open covers. Indeed, if

(3)

consisting of surjective local homeomorphisms, also called surjective étale maps,

-small coproducts.

\mathbf{Top}

for all coverages

3.1.
Split Simplicial Presheaves

We now wish to characterize the conditions that make a simplicial presheaf projective cofibrant.

Definition 3.4

(
[
21
, Definition 4.13]
)
.

Given a category
\mathcal{C}
with finite coproducts, a simplicial object
X:\mathsf{\Delta}^{\text{op}}\to\mathcal{C}
is
split
if there exist subobjects
N_{k}\hookrightarrow X_{k}
such that the canonical map

\sumop\displaylimits_{\sigma:[n]\twoheadrightarrow[m]}N_{m}\to X_{n}

where the coproduct is indexed by all surjections in
\mathsf{\Delta}
with domain

, is an isomorphism for every
n\geq 0
. We call such a collection of isomorphisms a
splitting
.

Lemma 3.5
.

If we consider simplicial sets as simplicial objects in
\mathbf{Set}
, then every simplicial set
X
is split.

Proof.

Given a simplicial set
X
, let
N_{k}
denote the set of non-degenerate
k
-simplices of
X
. Then by the Eilenberg-Zilber lemma
[
30
, Page 26]
, for every surjection
\sigma:[n]\to[m]
, the corresponding map
X_{m}\to X_{n}
is injective when restricted to
N_{m}
. In other words,
X_{n}
can be written as a coproduct
X_{n}\cong N_{n}+\sumop\displaylimits_{\sigma:[n]\twoheadrightarrow[m]}X(\sigma)(N_{m})
.
∎

Let
\mathbf{sSet}_{\text{nd}}
denote the category whose objects are simplicial sets and whose morphisms are those maps of simplicial sets that send non-degenerate simplices to non-degenerate simplices.

Lemma 3.6
.

Given a category
\mathcal{C}
, a simplicial presheaf
X
over
\mathcal{C}
is split if and only if as a functor
X:\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}}\to\mathbf{sSet}
it factors through
\mathbf{sSet}_{\text{nd}}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{sSet}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.5
, we know that for every object
U\in\mathcal{C}
,
X(U)
splits appropriately. We need only know then that for a map
f:V\to U
in
\mathcal{C}
, the corresponding morphism
X(U)\to X(V)
preserves the splitting. This is precisely what
X
factoring through
\mathbf{sSet}_{\text{nd}}
guarantees.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Note that this means that if
X
is a split simplicial presheaf and

coming from the category

must respect the splitting.

4.1.
The Čech Model Structure

Definition 4.1
.

Given an object
U\in\mathcal{C}
and a family of morphisms
r=\{r_{i}:U_{i}\to U\}_{i\in I}
over

, let
\check{C}(r)
denote the simplicial presheaf defined degreewise by

\check{C}(r)_{n}=\sumop\displaylimits_{i_{0},\dots,i_{n-1}\in I}y(U_{i_{0}})\times_{y(U)}\dots\times_{y(U)}y(U_{i_{n-1}}),

with face maps given by projection and degeneracies given by diagonal maps. We call this the

of the family

. There is a map
p_{r}:\check{C}(r)\to y(U)
given by
r_{i}
on each component of

. Note that

(9)

\pi_{0}\check{C}(r)\cong\overline{r}\cong\text{coeq}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{i,j\in I}y(U_{i})\times_{y(U)}y(U_{j})\rightrightarrows\sumop\displaylimits_{i}y(U_{i})\right).

where

is the sifted closure (Definition

) of

.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any family of morphisms
r=\{r_{i}:U_{i}\to U\}
, the Čech nerve is a split simplicial presheaf.

Proof.

Degenerate simplices of
\check{C}(r)
are sections of components of the form
y(U_{i_{0}})\times_{y(U)}y(U_{i_{1}})\times_{y(U)}\dots\times_{y(U)}y(U_{i_{n}})
where

for some

. Given a map
f:U\to V
in
\mathcal{C}
,
\check{C}(r)(V)\to\check{C}(r)(U)
is just degreewise given by precomposition, so nondegenerate simplices cannot be mapped to degenerate ones.
∎

Lemma 4.3

(
[

, Proposition A.1]

Given a small category
\mathcal{C}
and a family of morphisms

over an object

is an objectwise weak equivalence of simplicial presheaves.

We let
p_{r}:\check{C}(r)\to y(U)
denote the composite map

\check{C}(r)\xrightarrow{c_{r}}\pi_{0}\check{C}(r)\cong\overline{r}\hookrightarrow y(U).

We note that the Čech nerve construction is functorial. Indeed, given a small category
\mathcal{C}
and an object

is a refinement of families of morphisms over

, then we obtain a map

\check{C}(f):\check{C}(r)\to\check{C}(t)

of simplicial presheaves. This is most easily verified using the following observation. Given a family of morphisms

we obtain the map

of presheaves, equivalently a

Lemma 4.4
.

Given any object

and any family

over

If the iterated pullbacks

6.
The Plus Construction

Given a site
(\mathcal{C},j)
and a presheaf of sets
X
, the plus construction produces a presheaf

defined objectwise by

(18)

X^{+}(U)=\underset{R\in\text{Gro}(j)(U)^{\text{op}}}{\text{colim}}\,\mathbf{Pre}(\mathcal{C})(R,X),

where here
\text{Gro}(j)
denotes the Grothendieck closure of

(Definition
2.23
).

In general, one must apply the plus construction twice to a presheaf in order to obtain a sheaf
[

, Section 7.4]

(-)^{+}:\mathbf{Pre}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathbf{Pre}(\mathcal{C})
and we call the composite functor

sheafification

However, there is a different formula for the sheafification functor, obtained by noticing that
\mathbf{Sh}(\mathcal{C})(ay(U),aX)\cong aX(U)
can be computed using the theory of calculi of fractions/left multiplicative systems
[
53
, Section 16.3]
:

(19)

aX(U)\cong\underset{Y\to y(U)}{\text{colim}}\,\mathbf{Pre}(\mathcal{C})(Y,X)

where the colimit is indexed over the category whose objects are local isomorphisms
f:Y\to y(U)
and whose morphisms
g:f\to f^{\prime}
consist of arbitrary maps
g:Y\to Y^{\prime}
making the corresponding triangle commute.

There is an analogue of the above result obtained by Zhen Lin Low in the context of simplicial presheaves.

Proposition 6.1

(
[
58
, Proposition 6.6]
)
.

Given a site
(\mathcal{C},j)
and locally fibrant simplicial presheaves

and

\mathbb{R}\hat{\mathbb{H}}(\mathcal{C},j)(X,Y)\simeq\underset{H\in\mathbf{Triv}_{X}^{\text{op}}}{\text{ho}\text{colim}}\,\underline{\mathbf{sPre}}(\mathcal{C})(H,Y)

where
\mathbf{Triv}_{X}
denotes the full subcategory of
\mathbf{sPre}(\mathcal{C})_{/X}
on the local trivial fibrations

.

Remark 6.2
.

We give here a proof sketch of this result for the reader’s convenience, skipping over some details about categories of fibrant objects, see Appendix

. We encourage the reader to read Low’s full argument.

Proof Sketch.

The first ingredient for the proof is Thomason’s Homotopy Colimit Theorem
6.3
. Let
F:\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}}\to\mathbf{Cat}
be a diagram of small categories. Consider its
Grothendieck construction

\intop\nolimits F
, which is defined as follows. Its objects are pairs
(U,x)
where
U\in\mathcal{C}
and
x\in F(U)
, and morphisms
(U,x)\to(V,y)
are pairs
(f,g)
where
f:U\to V
and
g:x\to F(f)(y)
is a morphism in
F(U)
. There is a canonical functor
\intop\nolimits F\to\mathcal{C}
given on objects by
(U,x)\mapsto U
.

Theorem 6.3

(
[
83
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Let
F:\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}}\to\mathbf{Cat}
be a small (strict) diagram of small categories. Then there is a weak equivalence of simplicial sets

(21)

\underset{U\in\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}}}{\text{hocolim}}\;NF(U)\xrightarrow{\simeq}N\intop\nolimits F

where
N
denotes the nerve functor.

Now applying this theorem with
F={}^{c}\mathcal{C}(\pi(-),A)
, where
\pi:\mathbf{Triv}_{X}\to\mathcal{C}
is defined by
\pi(H\to X)=H
, and
{}^{c}\mathcal{C}(H,A)
denotes the underlying hom set thought of as a discrete category, we have a weak equivalence

\underset{H\in\mathbf{Triv}_{X}^{\text{op}}}{\text{hocolim}}\;N{}^{c}\mathcal{C}(H,A)=\underset{H\in\mathbf{Triv}_{X}^{\text{op}}}{\text{hocolim}}\;{}^{c}\mathcal{C}(H,A)\xrightarrow{\simeq}N\intop\nolimits\mathcal{C}(\pi(-),A).

The objects in the category
\intop\nolimits\mathcal{C}(\pi(-),A)
consist of pairs
(f,g)
where

is a trivial fibration and

is an arbitrary map in
\mathcal{C}
, and a morphism in
\intop\nolimits\mathcal{C}(\pi(-),A)
between

and

consists of a map

making the following diagram commute

(22)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}

But this is precisely a description of the cocycle category

(Definition

). Hence we have a weak equivalence

(23)

\underset{H\in\mathbf{Triv}_{X}^{\text{op}}}{\text{hocolim}}\;N{}^{c}\mathcal{C}(H,A)\xrightarrow{\simeq}N\mathbf{Cocycle}(X,A).

Now notice that the inclusion map

, we have a weak equivalence

This implies that the induced map on homotopy colimits

Remark 6.5

7.
Applications

The main advantage of a site
(\mathcal{C},j)
being strictly hypercomplete is the fact that one gets the best aspects of both

and
\hat{\mathbb{H}}(\mathcal{C},j)

\infty

Besides this obvious improvement over the not strictly hypercomplete case, in which one has to choose between nice fibrant objects or nice weak equivalences, in this section we list some other interesting consequences of strict hypercompleteness.

7.1.

Corollary B.5
.

Suppose that
p:H\to y(U)
is an
n
-truncated DHI-hypercover on a pullback-stable site
(\mathcal{C},j)
. Fix
n\geq 0
and suppose that the augmented matching map

H_{k}\to M_{k}^{+}H

is basal for all
k\leq n
. Then the matching object
M_{n+1}^{+}H
is semi-representable.

To proceed further, we will need to put extra conditions on our underlying site
(\mathcal{C},j)
. We note that the following definition is slightly different than that of
[
21
, Definition 9.1]
. This slight difference has an important consequence however, as now we need to reprove
[
21
, Proposition 9.3 and Theorem 9.4]
using this new definition. We need the covering basal condition rather than just the basal condition to obtain whole covering families, rather than just subsets of covering families.

Definition B.6

(
[
21
, Definition 9.1]
)
.

Given a small category
\mathcal{C}
, a
Verdier pretopology

j
on
\mathcal{C}
is a Grothendieck pretopology (Definition
2.26
) such that if
X\to y(U)
is a covering basal map of presheaves, then so is the induced diagonal map

X\to X\times_{y(U)}X.

We call a site
(\mathcal{C},j)
a
Verdier site
if
j
is a Verdier pretopology.

Many sites of interest are Verdier, see Section
2.1
for the full collection of Verdier sites of interest. Let us prove one example to show how this works.

Lemma B.7
.

The site
(\mathbf{Man},j_{\text{open}})
is a Verdier site.

Proof.

Suppose that
X\to y(M)
is a
j_{\text{open}}
-covering basal map of presheaves, where
M
is a finite dimensional smooth manifold. Then
X\cong\sumop\displaylimits_{i}y(U_{i})
, and the corresponding induced family
\mathbf{r}=\{r_{i}:U_{i}\to M\}
is a
j_{\text{open}}
-covering family, so it is a collection of open embeddings. Now we consider the induced diagonal map
\Delta_{X}:X\to X\times_{y(M)}X
. This is isomorphic to the map
\Delta_{X}:\sumop\displaylimits_{i}y(U_{i})\to\sumop\displaylimits_{i,j}y(U_{i}\times_{M}U_{j})
, which factors through
\sumop\displaylimits_{i}y(U_{i})\to\sumop\displaylimits_{i}y(U_{i}\times_{M}U_{i})
. This map, when restricted to each component
U_{i}
, is isomorphic to the identity
U_{i}\to U_{i}\times_{M}U_{i}\cong U_{i}
, since each map
r_{i}:U_{i}\to M
is a monomorphism in
\mathbf{Man}
. None of the
U_{i}\times_{M}U_{j}
with
i\neq j
are “hit” by the diagonal map, hence the diagonal map is covering basal since each isomorphism
\{U_{i}\to U_{i}\times_{M}U_{i}\}
is a
j_{\text{open}}
-covering family.
∎

(
[

)
.

Given a map

(

)
.

be a DHI-hypercover on a Verdier site

by induction.

, let us define

as follows. We know that

, and the matching map is precisely

. Since

is a local epimorphism, there exists a

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{y(U)}}

Appendix C
Matching Verdier Sites

The condition in Definition
4.47
that the site
(\mathcal{C},j)
be pullback stable is very strong. In the next sections we obtain weaker but more complicated conditions that a site can satisfy and still guarantee the existence of refinement by Verdier hypercovers, without pullback-stability. We call sites satisfying these conditions matching Verdier sites.

We should acknowledge that while matching Verdier sites appear to loosen the stricter requirements of Verdier sites, we have been unable to produce examples of sites that are matching Verdier but not Verdier. Hence we leave this section as a collection of partial results that may be of independent interest but do not tell a finished story.

Given an (
n
-truncated) augmented simplicial presheaf
X
, recall from Lemma
A.8
the characterization of the matching object

M_{k}^{+}X\cong\sumop\displaylimits_{a:\partial\Delta^{k}\to\text{ind}(X)}X(a).

We are going to investigate the structure of the matching object more closely.

Let
p:H\to y(U)
denote an augmented,
n
-truncated, split simplicial presheaf, and consider the diagram
\tau:\text{nd}(\Delta^{k})^{\text{op}}\to(\mathbf{Pre}(\mathcal{C})\downarrow y(U))
. Since the projection functor
(\mathbf{Pre}(\mathcal{C})\downarrow y(U))\to\mathbf{Pre}(\mathcal{C})
reflects connected limits, we can ignore the maps down to
y(U)
when
k>1
. The resulting diagram will look like the barycentric subdivision of
\Delta^{k}

Appendix D
Lurie’s Lemma

In this section we give some background on Lurie’s lemma, a point-set topological result that is the core lemma we need for Theorem
5.9
.

Definition D.1
.

A collection
\mathscr{C}=\{C_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of subsets of a topological space
X
is
locally finite
if for each
x\in X
there exists an open neighborhood
U_{x}
of
x
which intersects only finitely many elements of
\mathscr{C}
, i.e. there exist
i_{0},\dots,i_{n}\in I
such that

U_{x}\cap\left(\bigcupop\displaylimits_{i\in I}C_{i}\right)=U_{x}\cap\left(C_{i_{0}}\cup\dots\cup C_{i_{n}}\right).

Lemma D.2
.

If
\mathscr{C}=\{C_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a locally finite collection of closed subsets of
X
then
\bigcupop\displaylimits_{i\in I}\,C_{i}
is closed in
X
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the complement of the union is open. Suppose that
x\in\left(X-\cup_{i}C_{i}\right)
. Since
\mathscr{C}
is locally finite then there exists an open neighborhood
V_{x}
of
x
only intersecting finitely many elements of
\mathscr{C}
, label them
C_{i_{0}},\ldots,C_{i_{n}}
. For each
k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
, since
C_{i_{k}}
is closed we can find an open neighborhood
U_{i_{k}}
of
x
such that
U_{i_{k}}\subset(X-C_{i_{k}})
. Now
(\cap_{i=0}^{n}U_{i_{k}})\cap V_{x}
is an open neighborhood of
x
which is disjoint from each
C_{i}
. Thus
\cup_{i\in I}\,C_{i}
is closed.
∎

Given a topological space
X
, a collection
\mathscr{S}=\{S_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of subsets of
X
, and a sub-indexing set
I_{0}\subseteq I
, let

S(I_{0})=\bigcapop\displaylimits_{i\in I_{0}}S_{i}.

Given
k\geq 0
, and a subset
I_{0}\subseteq I
with cardinality
|I_{0}|=k+1
, we say that
I_{0}
is a
(k+1)
-subindex
. Let
I(k+1)
denote the set of
(k+1)
-subindices of
I
.

Lemma D.3
.

Given a topological space
X
, and a locally finite collection
\mathscr{C}=\{C_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of closed subsets of
X
, then for every
k\geq 1
, the collection
\left\{C(I_{0})\right\}_{I_{0}\in I(k+1)}
is a locally finite collection of closed subsets of
X
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
, since
\mathscr{C}
is locally finite, there exists an open neighborhood
U_{x}
of
x
intersecting only finitely many
C_{i}
, label them
C_{i_{0}},\ldots,C_{i_{n}}
. Fix
k\geq 1
. Then for
I_{0}\in I(k+1)
, the open set
U_{x}
intersects

if and only if
I_{0}
is a subset of
\{i_{0},\dots,i_{n}\}
; in other words
U_{x}
only intersects
C(I_{0})
for finitely many

, as there are only finitely many subsets of
\{i_{0},\dots,i_{n}\}
. Thus
\left\{C(I_{0})\right\}_{I_{0}\in I(k+1)}
is a locally finite collection of closed subsets of
X
.
∎

Lemma D.4

(
[
69
, Lemma 39.1]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{S}=\{S_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a locally finite collection of subsets of

. Then

(1)

Any subcollection

is locally finite,

(2)

The collection

is locally finite, where

is the closure of the subset

, and

(3)

\overline{\bigcupop\displaylimits\limits_{i\in I}S_{i}}=\bigcupop\displaylimits\limits_{i\in I}\overline{S_{i}}
.

Definition D.5

We say that a topological space

is

if for every

and every closed subset

of

respectively. We say that

,

of

Abstract
. In this article, we investigate the behavior of solutions u ⁡ ( x , t ) u(x,t) to the fractional Schrödinger equation on rank symmetric spaces of non-compact type. We proved that as time t t approaches 0 0 , then u ⁡ ( x , t ) u(x,t) converges pointwise almost everywhere to the initial radial data f f , provided that f ∈ H s ​ ( 𝕏 ) f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{X}) with s > 1 2 s>\frac{1}{2} . This result extends Sjölin’s results in this setting.

1.
Introduction

Let
u(x,t)
be the solution of the Schrödinger equation

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle i\frac{\partial u(x,t)}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial^{2}u(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}},\qquad\;(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\\[12.0pt]
u(x,0)=f(x).\end{array}\right.

By taking the Fourier transform on both sides with respect to
x
-variable we have

u(x,t)=S_{t}f(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{ix\xi}e^{it{\xi}^{2}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\;d\xi.

One can view
S_{t}
as a family of linear operators on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

\widehat{S_{t}f}(\xi)=e^{it|\xi|^{2}}\hat{f}(\xi),

and by Plancheral theorem

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}S_{t}f=f,

in
L^{2}
-norm. In
[
3
]
, Carleson posed a question regarding the amount of regularity required to the initial data
f
such that
S_{t}f(x)\rightarrow f\;a.e.\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as
t
goes to
0
. When
n=1
, Carleson proved that if
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
s\geq\frac{1}{4}
and support of
f
is compact, then
S_{t}f(x)\rightarrow f\;a.e.\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, as
t
goes to
0
. In
[
4
]
, Dahlberg and Kening proved that the condition
s\geq\frac{1}{4}
is sharp. This result has been further improved by many authors, including Sjölin, Bourgain, Moyua, Vargas, Vega, and Lee, in higher dimensions (see
[
2
,
5
,
9
,
10
]
for details), leading to a sharp result. The similar results were given in
[
14
]
, where Xing Wang and Chunjie Zhang addressed the same problem in a manifold setting and obtained results analogous to those in Euclidean space.

In this paper, we are interested in the fractional case of this problem in rank-one symmetric space of non-compact type with radial initial data. Our result is an extension of Sjölin’s result on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In
[
11
]
, Sjölin proved that for
n>2
, if
f\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
s>\frac{1}{2}
then
S_{t}f(x)\rightarrow f\;a.e.\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, as
t
goes to
0,
where
u(x,t)=S_{t}f(x)
is the solution of the fractional Schrödinger equation

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle i\frac{\partial u(x,t)}{\partial t}={\Delta}^{\frac{a}{2}}u(x,t),\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n},a>1,\\[12.0pt]
u(x,0)=f(x).\end{array}\right.

Here we also generalize the definition of
S_{t}f(x)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{ix\xi}e^{it|\xi|^{a}}\widehat{f}(\xi)d\xi
. Notably, when
a=2,
this reduces to the usual Schrödinger equation discussed earlier. We define

(1.3)

S^{*}f(x)=\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<1}|S_{t}f(x)|,

and in
[
11
]
, Sjölin proved the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

If
n\geq 3
and
a>1,
then
\|S^{*}f\|_{L^{2}(B)}\leq c\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
holds for
s>1/2
.

In
[
14
]
Wang and Zhang addressed the same problem for real hyperbolic space. They proved the similar bound like Theorem
1.1
, for
a=2
.

In this present work, we proved the analogous result of Sjölin. Our result also extends the result of Wang and Zhang in for all rank one symmetric space and for all
a>1
.
Now we formulate the problem in symmetric space. Let
\mathbb{X}
be a symmetric space and let

\Delta
be the Laplace-Beltrami operator in
\mathbb{X}
. The fractional Schrödinger equation in
\mathbb{X}
is given by

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle i\frac{\partial u(x,t)}{\partial t}={\Delta}^{\frac{a}{2}}u(x,t),\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{X},~a>1,\\[12.0pt]
u(x,0)=f(x),\qquad f~\text{radial}.\end{array}\right.

By taking Harish-Chandra transform in
x
-variable the solution
u(x,t)
of equation (
1.4
) is given by

u(x,t)=S_{t}f(x)=c\int_{0}^{\infty}\hat{f}(\lambda)e^{it(|\lambda|^{2}+|\rho|^{2})^{\frac{a}{2}}}\phi_{\lambda}(x)|c(\lambda)|^{-2}d\lambda.

The meaning of these symbols will be explained later. Now we state the main result of this paper:

Theorem A
.

The solution
u(x,t)
of the equation (
1.4
) converges pointwise to the initial data
f
, as
t
goes to
0
, whenever
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{X})
,
s>\frac{1}{2}
, and also support of
f
is compact.

The standard method for proving pointwise convergence involves obtaining an estimate for the corresponding maximal operator. We will follow this approach as well. Our proof will align with Sjölin’s ideas; to establish some mixed norm estimates and by using interpolation and inclusion results from Sobolev spaces, derive the required estimate for the corresponding maximal operator. Our proof of Theorem A is mainly based on the following maximal estimate:

Theorem B
.

If
a>1
, and
B
is ball of any arbitrary radius in
\mathbb{X}
, then

(1.5)

\|S^{*}f\|_{L^{2}(B)}\leq c\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{X})},

holds for
s>1/2
, Where
S^{*}f
is same as previously defined in (
1.3
) and
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(K\backslash G/K).

3.
Proof of Theorem A and Theorem B

In this section, we first prove the Theorem B. We choose
\alpha_{0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})
, a radial function and
\psi_{0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, an even function such that both are real cut-off functions, and
\alpha_{0}\equiv 1
in a ball
B
contained in the support of
\alpha_{0},

\psi_{0}\equiv 1\;\text{in}\;[0,1].
Now set

(3.17)

Sf(x,t)=\alpha_{0}(x)\psi_{0}(t)S_{t}f(x)=c\;\alpha_{0}(x)\psi_{0}(t)\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(\lambda)e^{it(|\lambda|^{2}+|\rho|^{2})^{\frac{a}{2}}}\phi_{\lambda}(x)|c(\lambda)|^{-2}d\lambda.

We define

(3.18)

\|Sf\|_{L^{2}(H_{s})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}}\|Sf(x,.)\|_{H_{s}}^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Our plan is as follows: By Plancheral theorem, we have

\|Sf\|_{L^{2}(H^{0})}\leq\|f\|_{H^{-s}(\mathbb{X})},\;\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\|Sf\|_{L^{2}(H^{1})}\leq\|f\|_{H^{-s+a}(\mathbb{X})}.

Interpolating between these two, yields

\|Sf\|_{L^{2}(H^{r})}\leq\|f\|_{H^{-s+ra}(\mathbb{X})},~0\leq r\leq 1,

and this, together with Sobolev imbedding further gives:

\|\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}S_{t}f(x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{X})}\leq\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{X})},~s>\frac{1}{2}.

First we shall prove that

(3.19)

\|Sf\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{{\mathbb{H}}^{-s}(\mathbb{X})}\;,\;s=\frac{a-1}{2}.

We have,

\displaystyle\int_{X}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Sf(x,t)|^{2}dxdt

\displaystyle=c\int_{\mathbb{X}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\alpha_{0}(x)^{2}\psi_{0}(t)^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(\lambda)e^{it({\lambda}^{2}+{\rho}^{2})^{\frac{a}{2}}}\phi_{\lambda}(x)|c(\lambda)|^{-2}d\lambda\right)\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\bar{\widehat{f}(\eta)}e^{-it({\eta}^{2}+{\rho}^{2})^{\frac{a}{2}}}\phi_{\eta}(x)|c(\eta)|^{-2}d\eta\Big)dxdt

\displaystyle=c\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}}\alpha_{0}(x)^{2}\phi_{\lambda}(x)\phi_{\eta}(x)dx\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(t)e^{-it(({\eta}^{2}+{\rho}^{2})^{\frac{a}{2}}-({\lambda}^{2}+{\rho}^{2})^{\frac{a}{2}})}dt\right)\widehat{f}(\lambda)\bar{\widehat{f}(\eta)}|c(\lambda)|^{-2}|c(\eta)|^{-2}d\lambda d\eta

\displaystyle=c\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}}\alpha(x)\phi_{\lambda}(x)\phi_{\eta}(x)dx\right)\hat{\psi}(({\eta}^{2}+{\rho}^{2})^{\frac{a}{2}}-({\lambda}^{2}+{\rho}^{2})^{\frac{a}{2}}))\widehat{f}(\lambda)\bar{\widehat{f}(\eta)}|c(\lambda)|^{-2}|c(\eta)|^{-2}d\lambda d\eta,

In the above expression, we put
\alpha={\alpha_{0}}^{2}
,
\psi={\psi_{0}}^{2}.

For
\lambda,\eta\in(0,\infty),
we define:

(3.20)

K(\lambda,\eta)=({\rho}^{2}+|\lambda|)^{s}({\rho}^{2}+|\eta|)^{s}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{X}}\alpha(x)\phi_{\lambda}(x)\phi_{\eta}(x)dx\Big)\hat{\psi}(({\eta}^{2}+{\rho}^{2})^{\frac{a}{2}}-({\lambda}^{2}+{\rho}^{2})^{\frac{a}{2}})).

Define,

Tf(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}K(\lambda,\eta)f(\eta)|c(\eta)|^{-2}d\eta.

If we set
h(\lambda)=\hat{f}(\lambda)(|\rho|^{2}+|\lambda|)^{-s}
, then

\displaystyle\int_{X}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Sf(x,t)|^{2}dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}K(\lambda,\eta)(|\rho|^{2}+|\lambda|)^{-s}(|\rho|^{2}+|\eta|)^{-s}\hat{f}(\lambda)\bar{\widehat{f}(\eta)}|c(\lambda)|^{-2}|c(\eta)|^{-2}d\lambda d\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}K(\lambda,\eta)\bar{h(\eta)}|c(\eta)|^{-2}d\eta\Big)h(\lambda)|c(\lambda)|^{-2}d\lambda

If
T
is bounded operator on

\int_{X}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Sf(x,t)|^{2}dxdt\leq\|T\bar{h}\|_{2}\|h\|_{2}\leq C\|h\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{H^{-s}(\mathbb{X})}.

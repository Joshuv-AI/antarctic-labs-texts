Pressure robust finite element discretizations of the nonlinear Stokes equations
Lars Diening, Adrian Hirn, Christian Kreuzer, Pietro Zanotti

Abstract
. We present first-order nonconforming Crouzeix-Raviart discretizations for the nonlinear generalized Stokes equations with ( r , ε ) (r,\varepsilon) -structure. Thereby the velocity-errors are independent of the pressure-error; i.e., the method is pressure robust. This improves suboptimal rates previously experienced for non pressure robust methods.

35J62,35J92,65N30,65N12,65N15,76A05,76M10

1.
Introduction

In this article, we study the finite element discretization of the nonlinear Stokes equations in a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle-\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{A}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u})+\nabla p=\boldsymbol{f}\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{u}=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad\boldsymbol{u}=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega.

Here
\boldsymbol{u}
denotes the velocity,
p
the kinematic pressure, and
\boldsymbol{f}

an external body force. The symbol
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u}
stands either for the
velocity gradient
\nabla\boldsymbol{u}
or for its symmetric part

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{u}=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}+(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})^{T})
. For given
r>1
,
\varepsilon\geq 0
the nonlinear function
\boldsymbol{A}:\mathds{R}^{d\times d}\to\mathds{R}^{d\times d}

is supposed to have
(r,\varepsilon)
-structure (or more
generally
\varphi
-structure; see Assumption
2.3
). A prototypical example is

(1.2)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{A}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u})=\left(\varepsilon^{2}+|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{r-2}{2}}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u}\,.

For
\varepsilon=0
the operator corresponds to the
r
-Laplacian, while for
\varepsilon>0
the operator regularizes the degeneracy of the
r
-Laplacian as the modulus of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u}
tends to zero. Equations with
(r,\varepsilon)
–structure arise in various physical applications,
such as in the theory of plasticity, bimaterial problems in
elastic-plastic mechanics, non-Newtonian fluid mechanics, blood
rheology and glaciology; see
e.g.
[
38
,
39
,
25
]
.

The finite element (FE) approximation of equations with

(r,\varepsilon)
-structure has been widely studied, see e.g.

[
3
,
15
,
19
,
4
,
5
,
9
,
27
]
.
According to
[
15
,
9
]
, it is
relevant to bound the error between
\boldsymbol{u}
and the
discrete velocity
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
in the so-called natural- or
\boldsymbol{F}
-distance

{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{v})}\right\rVert}_{2}
where
\boldsymbol{F}
is a nonlinear vector
field adapted to the problem’s
(r,\varepsilon)
-structure; see (
2.12
) for
the precise definition. This error notion is equivalent to the
quasi-norm introduced by Barrett/Liu

[
3
,
5
]
.

For conforming exactly divergence-free discretizations Céa-type estimates

(1.3)

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}\right\rVert}_{2}\lesssim\inf_{\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=0}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{v}_{h})}\right\rVert}_{2}

as for the elliptic
r
-Laplace problem
[
3
, Theorem
2.1]
can be obtained by straight forward application
of the elliptic techniques; see e.g.
[
15
, Lemma 5.2]
.
We call a method satisfying (
1.3
)
quasi-optimal
and pressure-robust
: the velocity-error measured in the
natural distance is proportional to the corresponding best error and independent of the pressure. Such discretizations have recently
received much attention for linear Stokes-equations; compare with

[
22
,
23
,
42
,
48
]
. In order to
replace the global approximation problem on the right hand side
of (
1.3
) by local approximation problems, usually local
Fortin operators are employed; compare
with
[
20
, §54.1]
. Such operators are typically not
perfectly local in the sense that the interpolated function on a mesh
element
K
depends on the function values in the neighbourhood

\omega_{K}
of
K
. This results in

(1.4)

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}\right\rVert}_{2}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{K}\inf_{\boldsymbol{Q}_{K}\in\mathds{R}^{d\times d}}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{Q}_{K}}\right\rVert}_{2;\omega_{K}}^{2}.

Corresponding results for the nonlinear Laplacian can be found in
[
15
]
. Note that in the case of the linear Stokes problem, the neighbourhood
\omega_{K}
can be avoided using techniques from
[
43
]
but for
r\neq 2
, properties of the natural distance prevent from a direct generalisation; compare with Remark
3.4
. It is also noticeable that all above a priori bounds are limited to first order at most. Here again, the reason is the nonlinear structure of the natural distance, in particular, the use of Jensen’s inequality while generalising stability properties to the natural distance.

For not exactly divergence-free discretizations, it was proved in
[
9
]
that

(1.5)

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}\right\rVert}_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{K}\inf_{\boldsymbol{Q}_{K}\in\mathds{R}^{d\times d}}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{Q}_{K}}\right\rVert}_{2;\omega_{K}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\inf_{q_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}}\int_{\Omega}\left(|p-q_{h}|^{2}+|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{r^{\prime}-2}{2}}|p-q_{h}|^{2}

with
r^{\prime}
the Hölder conjugate of
r
. This estimate substantially differs from (
1.4
) in that it is not pressure robust,
i.e., the velocity-error is interfered by the pressure; compare
with
[
28
]
.
In particular, for
r\neq 2
, this interference is of nonlinear nature and
may lead to suboptimal convergence rates even for regular solutions
with
\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u})\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)^{d\times d}
and
p\in W^{1,r^{\prime}}(\Omega)
. Indeed,
Belenki, Berselli, Diening and Růžička
[
9
]
derived
from (
1.5
) for
the MINI element the the following error estimate in terms of the mesh-size
h

(1.6)

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}\right\rVert}_{2}\lesssim h^{\min\{1,\frac{r^{\prime}}{2}\}}\,.

Similar results are derived by
[
27
]
for a stabilized
\mathbb{Q}_{1}/\mathbb{Q}_{1}
-discretization.
Comparing (
1.6
) and (
1.4
),
we conclude that for
r>2
the velocity-error estimate (
1.6
) is
suboptimal with respect to the
approximation properties of the discrete space. This is also confirmed by
numerical studies in
[
9
]
.

A similar reasoning (i.e. the pressure-error depends on the
velocity-error) limits the theoretical convergence of the pressure error in

[
9
]
to

(1.7)

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{p-p_{h}}\right\rVert}_{r^{\prime}}\lesssim h^{\min\{\frac{2}{r^{\prime}},\frac{r^{\prime}}{2}\}}.

In this case, however, the numerical results in
[
9
]

show
\mathcal{O}(h^{\min\{\frac{2}{r^{\prime}},1\}})

convergence,
indicating that the bound for the pressure error possibly is not sharp for
r>2
.

In
[
30
]
, Kaltenbach/Růžička obtain
first order convergence for the velocity-error of a local
discontinuous Galerkin method. The results, however are

\varepsilon
-dependent in the sense that the constants blow up fast as

\varepsilon\to 0
. Moreover, they exploit additional regularity of

f
and the involved jump penalizations result in non-monotone
schemes, with possible non-unique solutions; cf.
[
29
, §4]
.

In this paper we aim at achieving (
1.4
) without the above
restrictions. To this end, we follow a different approach and extend
the quasi-optimal and pressure-robust approach

[
47
,
35
,
34
]

of Kreuzer/Verfürth/Zanotti from linear to nonlinear Stokes
equations (
1.1
) with
(r,\varepsilon)
-structure. In
particular, the methods have the following features: First, we propose
two discretizations with nonconforming Crouzeix-Raviart (CR) elements
that are stabilization-free and therefore yield monotone numerical
schemes. As a consequence, the numerical solution is unique. Second,
a sophisticated
smoothing
operator acting on the test functions
in the load term, allows to handle load-functions

\boldsymbol{f}\in\boldsymbol{W}^{-1,r^{\prime}}(\Omega)
without
requiring extra regularity of the data. Third, this operator maps
element-wise divergence-free functions onto exactly
divergence-free functions, so to ensure the pressure robustness. Note
that the pressure robust methods of Linke et
al.
[
37
,
36
]
focus only on
the latter property and are not considered here.

Based on these features, we prove a priori bounds of the form (
1.4
) (see
Theorems
3.3
and
4.8
). It is remarkable
that the results neither resort to additional
regularity of the data and are robust for the critical limit

\varepsilon\to 0

of the regularisation parameter
in (
1.2
).

As a consequence, we obtain for regular velocities

\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u})\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)^{d\times d}
full first order convergence
independent of the pressure

(1.8)

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}\right\rVert}_{2}\lesssim{\left\lVert{h\nabla\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u})}\right\rVert}_{2}\,.

As the pressure-error depends on the velocity-error, as a side effect,
the improved a priori bounds for the velocity also improve the bounds
for the pressure-error. In fact, if in addition
p\in W^{1,r^{\prime}}(\Omega)
then we obtain

{\left\lVert{p-p_{h}}\right\rVert}_{r^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}(h^{\min\{\frac{2}{r^{\prime}},1\}})
, which
corresponds to the rates observed in the
numerical examples of
[
9
]
for the MINI element using a
different method.

The plan of the paper is as follows: In Section
2
, we
introduce the
\varphi
-structure (a generalization of the
(r,\varepsilon)
-structure), the nonlinear
problem (
1.1
) as well as the finite element
framework. Sections
3
and

4
each presents the a priori analysis for the
velocity-error of a pressure robust method based on
Crouzeix-Raviart elements for
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}=\nabla
and
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}

respectively. Section
5
concerns the improved bounds
for the pressure-error followed by some numerical experiments in
Section
6
.

2.
Preliminaries

To begin with, we clarify our notation and we state important
properties of the nonlinear operator
\boldsymbol{A}
and of the natural distance.
Further, we introduce the variational formulation of (
1.1
).

2.1.
Basic notation and function spaces

Let
\mathds{R}^{+}
be the set of all positive real numbers, and
\mathds{R}^{+}_{0}\coloneqq\mathds{R}^{+}\cup\{0\}
.
The Euclidean scalar-product of two vectors
\boldsymbol{p},\,\boldsymbol{q}\in\mathds{R}^{d}
is denoted by

\boldsymbol{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{q}
and the Frobenius product of
\boldsymbol{P},\,\boldsymbol{Q}\in\mathds{R}^{d\times d}

is defined by
\boldsymbol{P}:\boldsymbol{Q}\coloneqq\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}P_{ij}Q_{ij}
.
We set
|\boldsymbol{p}|\coloneqq(\boldsymbol{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{p})^{1/2}
and
|\boldsymbol{Q}|\coloneqq(\boldsymbol{Q}:\boldsymbol{Q})^{1/2}
.

For an open set
\omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
, and
r\in[1,\infty]
, we denote by
L^{r}(\omega)
the space of
scalar
r
-integrable functions on
\omega
with corresponding
norm
{\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert}_{r;\omega}
.
The space
L^{r}_{0}(\omega)
is the
closed subspace of
L^{r}(\omega)
of functions with
vanishing mean value.
We equip the first order Sobolev space

W^{1,r}(\omega)

with norm
\|\cdot\|_{1,r;\omega}=(\|\cdot\|_{r;\omega}^{r}+\|\nabla\cdot\|_{r;\omega}^{r})^{1/r}
and denote by

W^{1,r}_{0}(\omega)
its closed subspace of functions with vanishing
trace on
\partial\Omega
. For
r\in(1,\infty)
and
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}=1
, i.e.
r^{\prime}=\tfrac{r}{r-1}
, we have that

W^{1,r}_{0}(\omega)
is a reflexive Banach space with dual space

W^{-1,r^{\prime}}(\omega)=\big(W^{1,r}_{0}(\omega)\big)^{*}

and for the dual pairing between
f\in W^{-1,r^{\prime}}(\omega)
and
v\in W^{1,r}_{0}(\omega)
, we use the notation
\langle f,v\rangle_{\omega}
. We
use the convention

W^{0,r}(\omega)=W_{0}^{0,r}(\omega)=L^{r}(\omega)
. Orlicz
spaces
L^{\varphi}(\omega)
and Sobolev-Orlicz spaces

W^{1,\varphi}(\omega)
are introduced later in
Section
2.3
.

In case of
\omega=\Omega
, we usually omit the index
\Omega
, e.g. we write

{\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert}_{r}
instead of
{\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert}_{r;\Omega}
.
Spaces of
\mathds{R}^{d}
-valued functions are denoted with boldface type, though no distinction is made in the notation
of norms and inner products; for instance, the norm in
\boldsymbol{W}^{1,r}(\Omega)=(W^{1,r}(\Omega))^{d}
is given by

{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\rVert}_{1,r}=\left(\sum_{1\leq i\leq d}{\left\lVert{v_{i}}\right\rVert}_{1,r}^{r}\right)^{1/r}
. Thanks to Poincaré and Korn inequalities, an alternative norm on

\boldsymbol{W}^{1,r}_{0}(\Omega)
is given by
\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\cdot\|_{r}
if

r\in(1,\infty)
; compare e.g. with
[
16
, Theorem
6.10]
.

For a set
\omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
with Hausdorff dimension
d
or

d-1
, we denote by
|\omega|
its
d
- or
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure and for

f\in L^{r}(\omega),
we use the
abbreviation

\langle f\rangle_{\omega}\coloneqq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\omega}f\coloneqq\frac{1}{|\omega|}\int_{\omega}f

for its mean value in
\omega
.

In order to simplify the notation, we shall often denote
a\lesssim b
when

a\leq cb
for a constant
c>0
only depending on fixed but maybe
problem specific constants. For
a\lesssim b\lesssim a
we shall also write
a\eqsim b
.

2.2.
N-functions

A convenient abstract framework for problems with an

(r,\varepsilon)
-structure like (
1.2
) is based on so-called
N-functions,
that are standard in the theory of Orlicz spaces;
cf.
[
40
,
32
]
. A continuous, convex,
and strictly monotone function
\psi:\mathds{R}_{0}^{+}\to\mathds{R}_{0}^{+}
is
called an N-function if

\displaystyle\psi(0)=0,\qquad\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\psi(t)}{t}=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\psi(t)}{t}=\infty.

Thanks to the convexity of
\psi
, there exists its right derivative
\psi^{\prime}
, which is non-decreasing and satisfies
\psi^{\prime}(0)=0
,
\psi^{\prime}(t)>0
for
t>0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\psi^{\prime}(t)=\infty
.

The conjugate of an N-function
\psi
is defined by

\displaystyle\psi^{*}(t)\coloneqq\sup_{s\geq 0}\left(st-\psi(s)\right)\quad\text{for all }t\geq 0

and is again an N-function and we have
(\psi^{*})^{*}=\psi
. As an
example: the conjugate of
\psi(t)=\frac{1}{r}t^{r}
is
\psi^{*}(t)=\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}t^{r^{\prime}}
with
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}=1
.

For a quantitative numerical approach we require more regular
N-functions. We say that an N-function
\psi
is uniform convex, if
additionally
\psi\in W^{2,1}_{\textrm{loc}}((0,\infty))
(or

C^{1}([0,\infty))
and piecewise
C^{2}
) and

(2.1)

\displaystyle 1<r^{-}\coloneqq\inf_{t>0}\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(t)\,t}{\psi^{\prime}(t)}+1\leq\sup_{t>0}\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(t)\,t}{\psi^{\prime}(t)}+1\eqqcolon r^{+}<\infty.

We call
r^{\pm}
the indices of uniform convexity of
\psi
. In
the following we assume that
\psi
is a uniformly convex
N-function. Note that uniform convexity rules out almost linear growth
as well as exponential growth.

Let us recall the properties of uniformly convex N-functions
from
[
13
, Appendix B]
. If
\psi
is
uniformly convex with indices
r^{-}
and
r^{+}
, then
\psi^{*}
is
uniformly convex with indices
(r^{+})^{\prime}
and
(r^{-})^{\prime}
respectively.
Furthermore,
for all
s,t\geq 0
there holds

(2.2)

\displaystyle\min{\{{s^{r^{-}},s^{r^{+}}}\}}\,\psi(t)\leq\psi(st)\leq\max{\{{s^{r^{-}},s^{r^{+}}}\}}\,\psi(t).

As a consequence
\psi
and
\psi^{*}
satisfy the so-called

\Delta_{2}
-condition with

\displaystyle\psi(2t)\leq 2^{r^{+}}\psi(t)\quad\text{and}\quad\psi^{*}(2t)\leq 2^{(r^{-})^{\prime}}\psi^{*}(t),\qquad

\displaystyle\text{for all $t\geq 0$}.

By convexity, we have the quasi-triangle inequality

(2.3)

\displaystyle\psi(s+t)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\psi(2s)+\tfrac{1}{2}\psi(2t)\leq 2^{r^{+}-1}\left(\psi(s)+\psi(t)\right)\quad\forall s,t\geq 0.

It follows also from (
2.2
) and
st\leq\psi(s)+\psi^{*}(t)
that

(2.4)

\displaystyle st\leq\delta^{1-r^{+}}\psi(s)+\delta\psi^{*}(t),\qquad\text{ for all $s,t\geq 0,\delta\in(0,1]$.}

Moreover, we have for all
t\geq 0
that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\psi(t)\eqsim t\psi^{\prime}(t)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\psi^{*}(\psi^{\prime}(t))\eqsim\psi(t)

with constants depending solely on
r^{\pm}
.

We next introduce the notion of shifted N-function first introduced in

[
18
]
and further developed for example in

[
15
,
17
,
10
,
33
,
13
]
. Here,
we use the version of

[
13
, Appendix B]
,
where also the self-contained proofs for the properties below
can be found.

For a given uniformly convex N-function
\psi
with indices of uniform
convexity
r^{\pm}
, the family of shifted functions

\{\psi_{a}\}_{a\geq 0}
is defined by

(2.6)

\displaystyle\psi_{a}(t)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}\psi_{a}^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\qquad\text{ with }\qquad\psi_{a}^{\prime}(t)\coloneqq\frac{\psi^{\prime}(\max{\{{a,t}\}})}{\max{\{{a,t}\}}}t\,.

In the original work
[
18
]
, the maximum
\max{\{{a,t}\}}

was replaced by
a+t
. However, though equivalent, the
definition (
2.6
) has some technical
advantages.
We have
(\psi_{a})^{*}=(\psi^{*})_{\psi^{\prime}(a)}
and the functions

\psi_{a}
are again uniformly convex and with indices of uniform
convexity
\min{\{{r^{-},2}\}}
and
\max{\{{r^{+},2}\}}
. Hence,
\psi_{a}
and
(\psi_{a})^{*}

satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition with constants independent of
a\geq 0
.

A first basic property states for all
\boldsymbol{P},\boldsymbol{Q}\in\mathds{R}^{d\times d}
that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\psi_{|\boldsymbol{P}|}(|\boldsymbol{P}-\boldsymbol{Q}|)\eqsim\psi_{|\boldsymbol{Q}|}(|\boldsymbol{P}-\boldsymbol{Q}|)\quad\text{and}\quad\psi_{|\boldsymbol{P}|}^{\prime}(|\boldsymbol{P}-\boldsymbol{Q}|)\eqsim\psi_{|\boldsymbol{Q}|}^{\prime}(|\boldsymbol{P}-\boldsymbol{Q}|).

Moreover, for
t\geq 0
and all
\boldsymbol{P},\boldsymbol{Q}\in\mathds{R}^{d\times d}
we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle{\big\lvert{\psi^{\prime}_{|\boldsymbol{P}|}(t)-\psi^{\prime}_{|\boldsymbol{Q}|}(t)}\big\rvert}

\displaystyle\lesssim\psi_{|\boldsymbol{Q}|}^{\prime}\big({\big\lvert{{\lvert{\boldsymbol{P}}\rvert}-{\lvert{\boldsymbol{Q}}\rvert}}\big\rvert}\big)\leq\psi_{|\boldsymbol{Q}|}^{\prime}({\lvert{\boldsymbol{P}-\boldsymbol{Q}}\rvert}),

where the hidden constant depends only on
r^{\pm}
.
Based on this, for any
\delta\in(0,1]
, all
t\geq 0
and all
\boldsymbol{P},\boldsymbol{Q}\in\mathds{R}^{d\times d}
there holds

(2.9a)

\displaystyle\psi_{|\boldsymbol{P}|}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C\,\delta^{1-r^{+}}\,\psi_{|\boldsymbol{Q}|}(t)+\delta{\lvert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{P})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{Q})}\rvert}^{2}\,,

(2.9b)

\displaystyle\left(\psi_{|\boldsymbol{P}|}\right)^{*}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C\,\delta^{1-(r^{-})^{\prime}}\left(\psi_{|\boldsymbol{Q}|}\right)^{*}(t)+\delta{\lvert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{P})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{Q})}\rvert}^{2}\,,

where
C
only depends on
r^{\pm}
. These estimates are often called
shift-change
.

When
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}
we shall need the following Korn inequality on
bounded John domains, which can e.g. be found in
[
16
, Theorem
6.13]
. The precise definition of a John
domain is not important in this context, just note that it applies to
any bounded Lipschitz
domain.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
16
, Theorem 6.13]
)
.

Let
\psi
be a uniformly convex N-function with indices
r^{\pm}
. Then for a bounded John domain
\omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
and all

\boldsymbol{w}\in\boldsymbol{W}^{1,1}(\omega)
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\psi\left(\left|\nabla\boldsymbol{w}-\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{w}\rangle_{\omega}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\omega}\psi\left(\left|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{w}-\langle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{w}\rangle_{\omega}\right|\right)\,.

The hidden constant only depends on the constants
r^{\pm}
of uniform
convexity of
\psi
and the John constant
of
\omega
. Note that if
\boldsymbol{w}\in\boldsymbol{W}^{1,1}_{0}(\omega)
, then

\langle{\nabla\boldsymbol{w}}\rangle_{\omega}=0
and
\langle{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{w}}\rangle_{\omega}=0
.

2.3.
Properties of the nonlinear operator

In this section we state our assumptions on the nonlinear operator

\boldsymbol{A}
from (
1.1
). To this end, let
\varphi
be a fixed uniformly convex N-function
with indices of uniform convexity
r^{\pm}
. For simplicity, we
assume that
\varphi\in C^{2}((0,\infty))
(although

\varphi\in W^{2,1}_{\textrm{loc}}((0,\infty))
would suffice in what follows).

For a bounded open set
\omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
, we denote by

L^{\varphi}(\omega)
and
W^{1,\varphi}(\omega)
the usual Orlicz
and Sobolev-Orlicz spaces; see
[
40
]
. In fact,

v\in L^{\varphi}(\omega)
if
\int_{\omega}\varphi(|v|)<\infty
and
v\in W^{1,\varphi}(\omega)
if in addition

\int_{\omega}\varphi(|\nabla v|)<\infty
. The space
L^{\varphi}(\omega)

is a reflexive Banach space equipped with the Luxembourg norm

\|v\|_{\varphi}:=\inf\{\lambda\colon\int_{\omega}\varphi(|v|/\lambda)\leq 1\}
. By

L^{\varphi}_{0}(\omega)
we denote the closed subspace of
L^{\varphi}(\omega)
of
functions with integral zero.
The subspace
W^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\omega)
of Sobolev-Orlicz functions
with zero boundary values is a reflexive Banach space with norm

\|\nabla\cdot\|_{\varphi}
and
we denote its dual space by

W^{-1,\varphi^{*}}(\omega)
.

The most prominent example for
\varphi
is

(2.10)

\displaystyle\varphi(t)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}\varphi^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\qquad\text{with }\varphi^{\prime}(t)\coloneqq(\varepsilon+t)^{r-2}t\,

with
1<r<\infty
and
\varepsilon\geq 0
and we say that

\varphi
has
(r,\varepsilon)
-structure.
In this case, we have

If

are bounded independently

Remark 2.7

(Quasi-norm and natural distance)
.

The natural distance is an important concept in the regularity
theory as well as the numerical analysis of nonlinear PDEs. It was
introduced under the name

for

(r,\varepsilon)
-structure by Barrett and Liu in the breakthrough
paper
[
5
]

\displaystyle|w|_{(r,v)}^{2}\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\big(\varepsilon+|\nabla v|+|\nabla w|\big)^{r-2}|\nabla w|^{2}\,,\qquad v,w\in W^{1,r}(\Omega).

Indeed, in this case, we have for all

\displaystyle\varphi_{|\boldsymbol{P}|}(|\boldsymbol{P}-\boldsymbol{Q}|)

\displaystyle\eqsim(\varepsilon+|\boldsymbol{P}|+|\boldsymbol{Q}|)^{r-2}|\boldsymbol{P}-\boldsymbol{Q}|^{2}

which implies the equivalence to the natural distance, i.e.

(2.13)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{A}(\nabla v)-\boldsymbol{A}(\nabla u)):(\nabla v-\nabla u)\eqsim{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla v)-\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla u)}\right\rVert}^{2}_{2}\eqsim{\lvert{v-u}\rvert}_{(r,v)}^{2}.

For the quasi-norm error, Barrett and Liu proved quasi-optimality for
conforming finite element Galerkin approximation of the
p
-Laplace
problem. This was extended in
[

to more general

with uniformly convex N-functions.

2.4.
Variational formulation of the nonlinear Stokes equations

Let
\varphi
be a uniformly convex N-function with indices
r^{\pm}
of
uniform convexity and
assume that
\boldsymbol{A}
has
\varphi
-struture. We define the velocity
and pressure spaces by

(2.14)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{W}^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\Omega)\qquad\text{and}\qquad L^{\varphi^{*}}_{0}(\Omega).

The variational formulation of (
1.1
) reads as: for
\boldsymbol{f}\in\boldsymbol{W}^{-1,\varphi^{*}}(\Omega)

find
\boldsymbol{u}\in\boldsymbol{W}^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\Omega)
and

with

(2.15a)

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{W}^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\qquad\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{A}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u}):\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{v}-\int_{\Omega}p\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{v}

\displaystyle=\langle\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v}\rangle,

(2.15b)

\displaystyle\forall q\in L^{\varphi^{*}}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\qquad\int_{\Omega}q\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{u}

Equation (

(2.16)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}\coloneqq\{\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{W}^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\Omega)\mid\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{v}=0\}\subset\boldsymbol{W}^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\Omega)\,.

Consequently,
\boldsymbol{u}

solves the reduced problem

(2.17)

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{Z}\qquad\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{A}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u}):\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{v}=\langle\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v}\rangle.

According to the strict monotonicity of
\boldsymbol{A}
(cf. Lemma
2.6
),
existence and uniqueness of

follows by standard monotone operator
theory and Korn’s inequality in Proposition
2.1
.
The inf-sup condition
[
9
, Lemma 4.3]
for Orlicz spaces reads

(2.18)

\displaystyle\inf_{q\in L_{0}^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega)}\sup_{\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{W}^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{\int_{\Omega}q\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{v}}{{\left\lVert{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right\rVert}_{\varphi}{\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert}_{\varphi^{*}}}=:\beta>0,

where

only depends on
r^{\pm}
and
\Omega
via its John
constant. This inf-sup condition guarantees the existence of a unique
pressure

such that

solves (
2.15
).

(2.21b)

\displaystyle|\omega_{K}|\eqsim|K|\eqsim h_{K}^{d}\quad\text{for all}~K\in\mathcal{T}\text{ and }\#\mathcal{N}_{K}\leq m_{0}\text{ for some }m_{0}\in\mathds{N}.

For

\displaystyle S^{k}_{\ell}\coloneqq S^{k}_{\ell}(\mathcal{T})\coloneqq\left\{v\in W^{k,1}(\Omega)\mid\forall K\in\mathcal{T}\ v_{|K}\in\mathds{P}_{\ell}(K)\right\}.

(2.23)

\displaystyle\operatorname{CR}\coloneqq\operatorname{CR}(\mathcal{T})\coloneqq\left\{v\in S^{0}_{1}\mid\forall F\in\mathcal{F}\int_{F}\left\llbracket v\right\rrbracket=0\right\}.

Lemma 2.9

Let

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{F}h_{F}^{-1}\left|\left\llbracket v\right\rrbracket_{F}\right|\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{T}\\

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{F}\left|\left\llbracket v\right\rrbracket_{F}\right|&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{F}{\lvert{v_{|K_{i}}-\langle{v}\rangle_{F}}\rvert}\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{F}{\lvert{v_{|K_{i}}-\langle{v}\rangle_{K_{i}}}\rvert}\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{K_{i}}{\lvert{\nabla v}\rvert}.\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\nabla_{\!h}(\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}v)_{|K}=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{K}\nabla_{\!h}(\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}v)=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{K}\nabla_{h}v

\displaystyle\inf_{q_{h}\in{\widehat{S}}_{0}^{0}}\sup_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}q_{h}\operatorname{div}_{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}{{\left\lVert{\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\rVert}_{\varphi}{\left\lVert{q_{h}}\right\rVert}_{\varphi^{*}}}\geq\beta>0.

2.6.
Smoothing operator

Note that the Crouzeix-Raviart space is nonconforming, i.e.

\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}\not\subset\boldsymbol{W}^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\Omega)
in view of the lack of global continuity
and the violation of the boundary conditions.
Consequently the datum
\boldsymbol{f}\in\boldsymbol{W}^{-1,\varphi^{*}}(\Omega)
in (
2.15
) cannot be directly applied on
test-functions from
\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}
. Still, it is known from

[
44
]
that an error estimate like
(
1.4
), not involving extra regularity of
\boldsymbol{u}
, can hold
true only for discretizations defined for all possible data

\boldsymbol{f}
. Therefore, we resort to a so-called
smoothing operator

\boldsymbol{E}:\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}\to\boldsymbol{W}_{0}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\subset\boldsymbol{W}_{0}^{1,\varphi}(\Omega)
, which acts on the test-functions, thus
enabling the application of
\boldsymbol{f}
.

We make use of the smoothing operator introduced in
[
47
, §4.1]
for the Stokes equations (see also
[
34
, §3]
). The operator acts on
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}
as follows

\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}+\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}+\boldsymbol{C}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}

where

•

\boldsymbol{A}:\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}\to(S_{1}^{1})^{d}
is an averaging operator, enabling the stability estimates (
2.30
) below;

•

\boldsymbol{B}
maps
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}
to a combination of face bubbles, so as to enforce the identity

(2.29a)

\displaystyle\int_{F}\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\int_{F}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\qquad\forall F\in\mathcal{F}

which implies

(2.29b)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{Q}_{h}:\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{Q}_{h}:\nabla\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{Q}_{h}\in(S_{0}^{0})^{d\times d},

in combination with element-wise integration by parts;

•

\boldsymbol{C}
maps
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}
to a combination of element bubbles, so as to enforce the identity

(2.29c)

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}={\operatorname{div}}_{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}.

We refer to
[
47
,
34
]
for the details of the construction and the proof of (
2.29
).

Remark 2.11

(Pressure robustness)
.

Owing to (
2.29c
), we readily infer

{\operatorname{div}}_{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=0\quad\implies\quad\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=0

i.e.
\boldsymbol{E}
maps element-wise divergence-free functions to exactly divergence-free functions. This property, originally pointed out in
[
37
]
, guarantees the pressure robustness of the discretizations proposed in the next sections, meaning that the discrete velocity is independent of the discrete pressure. This property is necessary to make sure that the velocity-error is independent of the pressure error, thus preventing from sub-optimal error decay rates as in (
1.6
).

3.
A pressure robust Crouzeix-Raviart method for
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}=\nabla
.

In this section we propose a pressure robust Crouzeix-Raviart method for the
nonlinear Stokes equation (
1.1
) for the case
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}=\nabla
.
The method coincides with the ones
in
[
47
,
34
]
for

\varphi(t)=t^{2}
, i.e. for the linear Stokes equations.

Method 3.1
.

For
\boldsymbol{f}\in\boldsymbol{W}^{-1,\varphi^{*}}(\Omega)
compute
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}
and

p_{h}\in{\widehat{S}}_{0}^{0}
such that

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}

\displaystyle\in\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}

\displaystyle\qquad\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{A}(\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}):\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\int_{\Omega}p_{h}\operatorname{div}_{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}

\displaystyle=\langle\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\rangle,

\displaystyle\forall q_{h}

\displaystyle\in{\widehat{S}}_{0}^{0}

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\Omega}q_{h}\operatorname{div}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}

\displaystyle=0.

Note that we do not require regularity beyond
\boldsymbol{f}\in\boldsymbol{W}^{-1,\varphi^{*}}(\Omega)
from (
2.15
), because
\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{W}_{0}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
.
Similarly to the derivation of (
2.17
),
we have that
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
is in the space of element-wise divergence-free functions

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}_{h}\coloneqq\{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}\mid\operatorname{div}_{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=0\}\subset\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}\,,

and uniquely solves the reduced problem

(3.2)

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{Z}_{h}\quad\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{A}(\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}):\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\langle\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\rangle.

In view of the stability (
2.33
)
and the inf-sup-condition (
2.28
), the
well-posedness of Method
3.1
follows from standard monotone
operator theory; cf.
[
9
,
26
]
.

Proposition 3.2

(Well-posedness)
.

Method
3.1
determines a unique pair
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h})\in\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}\times{\widehat{S}}_{0}^{0}
depending continuously on the datum
\boldsymbol{f}\in\boldsymbol{W}^{-1,\varphi^{*}}(\Omega)
.

From the properties of
\boldsymbol{E}
and the
\varphi
-structure of
\boldsymbol{A}

we infer our first main result addressing the approximation
properties of Method
3.1
.

Theorem 3.3

(Velocity-error)
.

Let
(\boldsymbol{u},p)\in\boldsymbol{W}_{0}^{1,\varphi}(\Omega)\times L_{0}^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega)
be the solution to (
2.15
)
and let
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h})\in\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}\times{\widehat{S}}_{0}^{0}
be computed by Method
3.1
.
Then we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}\right\rVert}_{2}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\inf_{\boldsymbol{Q}_{K}\in\mathds{R}^{d\times d}}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{Q}_{K}}\right\rVert}_{2;\omega_{K}}^{2}\,.

In particular, when
\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})\in\boldsymbol{W}^{1,2}(\Omega)
, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}\right\rVert}_{2}\lesssim{\left\lVert{h\nabla\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})}\right\rVert}_{2}\,.

The hidden constants depend only on
\Omega
,
r^{\pm}
, and the shape constant of
\mathcal{T}
.

Before we prove Theorem

Remark 3.4

(Quasi-optimality)
.

Recall that, for conforming and divergence-free discretizations,
the velocity-error estimate (

quasi-optimality stated in
(

establishes a counterpart
of (

, but
quasi-optimality in the sense of (

guaranteed. The critical aspect is the stability of the smoothing
operator
\boldsymbol{E}
, as Lemma
2.13
for

\psi=\varphi_{a}
, requires a
constant shift

on element neighbourhoods. Enforcing this through a change
of the shift (
2.9
) introduces a suboptimal
approximation term.
For details consider the bound of
I_{2}
in the proof of Theorem
3.3
below.

Remark 3.5

(Rate of convergence and regularity)
.

We emphasize that two aspects of the velocity-error estimate
(
3.4
) improve upon the existing results in

[
9
]
for the MINI element and in

[
26
]
for a stabilized
\mathds{Q}_{1}/\mathds{Q}_{1}

discretization. First, the convergence rate for the velocity is optimal, whereas for
problems with
(r,\varepsilon)

[

,

]
both theoretically and in numerical simulations

Second, no additional regularity of the pressure is

from
(

Both improvements are made possible
by the pressure robustness observed in

An
\mathcal{O}(h)

in
[

for a local dG
discretization, however, compared to (

The regularity for system (
2.15

developed. However, it is expected that this kind of regularity is the

Results for the special case of

-structure can be found
in
[

46
,
7
]

&\lesssim\int_{\omega_{K}}\varphi_{|\boldsymbol{Q}_{K}|}\left(\left|\nabla_{\!h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u})\right|\right)+\int_{K}{\lvert{\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{Q}_{K})}\rvert}^{2}\\

&\eqsim\int_{\omega_{K}}{\lvert{\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h})-\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})}\rvert}^{2}+\int_{\omega_{K}}{\lvert{\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{Q}_{K})}\rvert}^{2}.\end{aligned}

Estimating the term
\int_{K}\varphi_{|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}|}\left(\left|\nabla_{\!h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\right|\right)
in exactly the same way, and summing over all

K\in\mathcal{T}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{\delta}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h})}\right\rVert}^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\delta\int_{\Omega}{\lvert{\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla_{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h})-\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})}\rvert}^{2}+\delta\int_{\Omega}{\lvert{\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})-\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})}\rvert}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\delta\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\inf_{\boldsymbol{Q}_{K}\in\mathds{R}^{d\times d}}\int_{\omega_{K}}\varphi_{|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}|}\left(\left|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{Q}_{K}\right|\right)

since

was arbitrary and the sets

\omega_{K}
,
K\in\mathcal{T}
, overlap only finitely often.

Using again Lemma
2.6
, combining the previous estimates, we arrive at

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}\right\rVert}_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(C_{\delta}+\delta){\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h})}\right\rVert}^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\delta{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}\right\rVert}^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\delta\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\inf_{\boldsymbol{Q}_{K}\in\mathds{R}^{d\times d}}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{Q}_{K})}\right\rVert}_{2;\omega_{K}}^{2}\,.

Choosing

sufficiently small, the second term can be
compensated on the left hand side and the assertion follows from

2.10

recalling
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{u}
.
∎

4.
A pressure robust Crouzeix-Raviart method for
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}

Recall the symmetric gradient

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{v}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\boldsymbol{v}+\nabla\boldsymbol{v}^{T})
. According
to
[
1
]
, on some choices of
\Omega
and

\mathcal{T}
, there exists
\boldsymbol{0}\neq\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}
with

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}_{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\boldsymbol{0}
,
where
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}_{h}
is the element-wise symmetric gradient; compare with (
2.20
).

This observation means that we cannot just apply Method
3.1
with

\nabla_{\!h}
replaced by
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}_{h}
since the quasi-linear operator is
not strictly monotone in general. We propose the following alternative, inspired by the so-called recovered finite elements
[
21
]
.

Method 4.1
.

For
\boldsymbol{f}\in\boldsymbol{W}^{-1,\varphi^{*}}(\Omega)

find
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}
and
p_{h}\in{\widehat{S}}_{0}^{0}
such that

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}

\displaystyle\in\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}\qquad\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{A}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}):\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}p_{h}\operatorname{div}_{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}

\displaystyle=\langle\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle\forall q_{h}

\displaystyle\in{\widehat{S}}_{0}^{0}

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\Omega}q_{h}\operatorname{div}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}

\displaystyle=0\,.

Remark 4.2

(Equivalent conforming method)
.

Method
4.1
is equivalent to a conforming and divergence-free discretization of (
2.15
): find
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{E}(\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}})
and
\widetilde{p}_{h}\in{\widehat{S}}_{0}^{0}
such that

\displaystyle\forall\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{E}(\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}})

\displaystyle\qquad\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{A}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}):\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}-\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{p}_{h}\operatorname{div}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}

\displaystyle=\langle\boldsymbol{f},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\rangle,

\displaystyle\forall\widetilde{q}\in{\widehat{S}}_{0}^{0}

\displaystyle\qquad\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{q}\operatorname{div}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}

\displaystyle=0.

Indeed, we have
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}=\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
and
\widetilde{p}_{h}=p_{h}
. The equivalence is obtained
by (
2.29c
).
Though the velocity
space
\boldsymbol{E}(\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}})
appears to have a complicated structure, it
can be parametrised by
\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}
via

\boldsymbol{E}
, which is one-to-one according to the first part of Corollary
2.14
.

In analogy with (
2.17
) and (
3.2
), we can characterize the velocity
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
from Method
4.1
as the unique solution in
\mathbf{Z}_{h}
of the reduced problem

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{Z}_{h}\quad\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{A}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}):\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\langle\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\rangle

which is equivalent to a conforming Galerkin discretization of
(
2.17
), cf. Remark
4.2
. Consequently,
Method
4.1
is uniquely solvable by
standard monotone operator theory and the inf-sup-condition
(
2.28
). For an a priori bound of the velocity

\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
, we additionally recall the stability (
2.33
) of

\boldsymbol{E}
as well as the inverse bound

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi(|\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}|)=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(|\nabla_{\!h}\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}|)\leq\int_{\Omega}\varphi(|\nabla\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}|)\lesssim\int_{\Omega}\varphi(|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}|)\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}.

resulting from Corollary
2.14
, the stability of the
Crouzeix-Raviart interpolation operator (

inequality from Proposition
2.1
.

Proposition 4.3

(Well-posedness)
.

Method
4.1
determines a unique pair
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h})\in\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}\times{\widehat{S}}_{0}^{0}
, which depends continuously on
\boldsymbol{f}\in\boldsymbol{W}^{-1,\varphi^{*}}(\Omega)
.

The equivalence of Method
4.1
with a conforming and divergence-free discretization implies also that we have quasi-optimality in the sense of (
1.3

Theorem 4.4

where we used that
\left|\left\llbracket\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{v}\right\rrbracket_{F}\right|\lesssim\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{F}\left|\left\llbracket\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{v}\right\rrbracket_{F}\right|
since
\left\llbracket\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{v}\right\rrbracket_{F}
is linear on
F
. Note that thanks
to
\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{W}_{0}^{1,\varphi}(\Omega)
, we have
\left\llbracket\boldsymbol{v}\right\rrbracket_{F}=0
in

F\in\mathcal{F}
. Consequently,
\left\llbracket\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{v}\right\rrbracket_{F}=\left\llbracket\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}\right\rrbracket_{F}
in

F\in\mathcal{F}
and it follows from
Lemma
2.9
that

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{F}h^{-1}\left|\left\llbracket\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{v}\right\rrbracket_{F}\right|=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{F}h^{-1}\left|\left\llbracket\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}\right\rrbracket_{F}\right|\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{T}\\
F\subset K\end{subarray}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{K}\left|\nabla(\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v})\right|\,.

This and Jensen’s inequality imply

\displaystyle\int_{F}h\varphi_{\left|\boldsymbol{Q}\right|}\left(h^{-1}\left|\left\llbracket\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{v}\right\rrbracket_{F}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{F}h\varphi_{\left|\boldsymbol{Q}\right|}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{T}\\
F\subset K\end{subarray}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{K}\left|\nabla(\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v})\right|\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{T}\\
F\subset K\end{subarray}}\int_{K}\varphi_{\left|\boldsymbol{Q}\right|}\left(\left|\nabla(\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v})\right|\right)\,.

Thanks to (
2.25
) we can apply the
Korn inequality (Proposition
2.1
)
and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{F}h\varphi_{\left|\boldsymbol{Q}\right|}\left(h^{-1}\left|\left\llbracket\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{v}\right\rrbracket_{F}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{T}\\
F\subset K\end{subarray}}\int_{K}\varphi_{\left|\boldsymbol{Q}\right|}\left(\left|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v})\right|\right)\,.

At this point, we again invoke (
2.9
) to change
the shift to
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{v}
and obtain with Lemma
2.6

\displaystyle\int_{F}h\varphi_{\left|\boldsymbol{Q}\right|}\left(h^{-1}\left|\left\llbracket\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{v}\right\rrbracket_{F}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{T}\\
F\subset K\end{subarray}}\left(\int_{K}\varphi_{\left|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{v}\right|}\left(\left|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v})\right|\right)+\int_{K}{\lvert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{v})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{Q})}\rvert}^{2}\right)\,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{T}\\
F\subset K\end{subarray}}\left(\|\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{v})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{v})\|_{2;K}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{v})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{Q})\|_{2;K}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{T}\\
F\subset K\end{subarray}}\|\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{v})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{Q})\|_{2;K}^{2}\,,

where we used (
4.4
) in the last step. Combining this
with (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) and
recalling that
\boldsymbol{Q}\in\mathds{R}^{d\times d}
was arbitrary, we
have proved for
F\in\mathcal{F}
that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{T}\\
F\cap K\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\int_{F}h\varphi_{\left|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{v}_{|K}\right|}\left(h^{-1}\left|\left\llbracket\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{v}\right\rrbracket_{F}\right|\right)\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{T}\\
F\cap K\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\|\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{v})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{Q})\|_{2;K}^{2}.

Note that
\boldsymbol{F}:\mathds{R}^{d\times d}\to\mathds{R}^{d\times d}
is surjective and
since
\boldsymbol{Q}\in\mathds{R}^{d\times d}
is arbitrary, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{T}\\
F\cap K\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\int_{F}h\varphi_{\left|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{v}_{|K}\right|}\left(h^{-1}\left|\left\llbracket\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{v}\right\rrbracket_{F}\right|\right)\lesssim\inf_{\boldsymbol{Q}\in\mathds{R}^{\mathrm{d}\times d}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{T}\\
F\cap K\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\|\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{v})-\boldsymbol{Q}\|_{2;K}^{2}.

Together with (
4.4
), summing over all
F\in\mathcal{F}
finally proves
the assertion. ∎

With this preparation, we can prove the following main result.

Theorem 4.8

(Velocity-error)
.

Let
(\boldsymbol{u},p)\in\boldsymbol{W}_{0}^{1,\varphi}(\Omega)\times L_{0}^{\varphi^{*}}(\Omega)
be the solution to (
2.15
)
and let
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h})\in\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}\times{\widehat{S}}_{0}^{0}
be computed by Method
4.1
.
Then we have

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}\right\rVert}_{2}^{2}+{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}\right\rVert}_{2}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\inf_{\boldsymbol{Q}_{K}\in\mathds{R}^{d\times d}}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{Q}_{K}}\right\rVert}_{2;\omega_{K}}^{2}.

In particular, we have for
\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{u})\in\boldsymbol{W}^{1,2}(\Omega)
the estimate

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}\right\rVert}_{2}+{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}\right\rVert}_{2}\lesssim{\left\lVert{h\nabla\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{u})}\right\rVert}_{2}\,.

The hidden constant depends only on
r^{\pm}
,
d
and the shape
constant of
\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

We conclude from Theorems
4.4
and
4.5
, and
Lemma
4.6
that

{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}\right\rVert}_{2}^{2}+{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}\right\rVert}_{2}^{2}\\
\lesssim\inf_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{Z}_{h}}\big\{{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}_{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h})}\right\rVert}_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\big\}.

Choosing
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\mathcal{I}^{\textsf{cr}}\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbf{Z}_{h}
, the assertion follows from Lemma
4.7
.
∎

Remark 4.9

(Stabilization for the symmetric gradient problem)
.

In order to handle the non-definiteness of the
symmetric gradient, one can also consider a jump penalization of our
method as it is common for discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods; compare with
[
12
]
.
This has been done by Kaltenbach and Růžička in
[
29
,
30
,
31
]

for a DG approximation of
(r,\varepsilon)
-structure systems.
However, their approach suffers from non-monotonicity of the stabilized nonlinear operator
so that uniqueness of the discrete solution cannot be guaranteed.
In contrast, our Method
4.1
features monotonicity and hence unique solvability.

5.
Estimates of the pressure-error

Although the above velocity-error estimates for
Methods
3.1
and

4.1
are pressure robust,
i.e. independent of the pressure error, the pressure-error itself
depends on the velocity-error. Consequently, improving the velocity
approximation can improve also the pressure approximation in some
cases. To make this more precise, we combine ideas
from
[
9
, Theorem 4.7]
with the results in the
previous sections.
For the ease of presentation, we restrict ourselves to problems with
(r,\varepsilon)
-structure.

Theorem 5.1

(Pressure-error)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{A}
have
(r,\varepsilon)
-structure (
2.10
)
for some
1<r<\infty
and
\varepsilon\geq 0
.
Let
(\boldsymbol{u},p)\in\boldsymbol{W}_{0}^{1,r}(\Omega)\times L_{0}^{r^{\prime}}(\Omega)
be the unique solution to (
2.15
)
and let
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h})\in\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}\times{\widehat{S}}_{0}^{0}
be obtained with either
Method
3.1
or

4.1
. Then we have

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{p-p_{h}}\right\rVert}_{r^{\prime}}\lesssim\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\inf_{\boldsymbol{Q}_{K}\in\mathds{R}^{d\times d}}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{Q}_{K}}\right\rVert}_{2;\omega_{K}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\min\{1,\frac{2}{r^{\prime}}\}}+\inf_{q_{h}\in{\widehat{S}}_{0}^{0}}{\left\lVert{p-q_{h}}\right\rVert}_{r^{\prime}}\,.

In particular, when
\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})\in\boldsymbol{W}^{1,2}(\Omega)
and
p\in W^{1,r^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{p-p_{h}}\right\rVert}_{r^{\prime}}\lesssim{\left\lVert{h\nabla\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\boldsymbol{u})}\right\rVert}_{2}^{\min\{1,\frac{2}{r^{\prime}}\}}+{\left\lVert{h\nabla p}\right\rVert}_{r^{\prime}}.

Here
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}=\nabla
or
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}
depending on the context and
for
r>2
, the hidden constant depends also on

\boldsymbol{f}
but is robust for
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proof.

We focus on the case of Method
3.1
.
For Method
4.1
the assertion follows with minor changes.
Let
\pi p\in{\widehat{S}}_{0}^{0}
be the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection of
p
and apply the triangle inequality

{\left\lVert{p-p_{h}}\right\rVert}_{r^{\prime}}\leq{\left\lVert{p-\pi p}\right\rVert}_{r^{\prime}}+{\left\lVert{\pi p-p_{h}}\right\rVert}_{r^{\prime}}.

Owing to Jensen’s inequality, the projection
\pi
is bounded,
cf. (
2.26
) where the same argument is used. Therefore,
the first term on the right-hand side above is bounded by

{\left\lVert{p-\pi p}\right\rVert}_{r^{\prime}}\leq 2\inf_{q_{h}\in{\widehat{S}}_{0}^{0}}{\left\lVert{p-q_{h}}\right\rVert}_{r^{\prime}}.

For the second term we invoke the inf-sup stability
(
2.28
),
\operatorname{div}_{h}\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}={\widehat{S}}_{0}^{0}
and (
2.29c
), to obtain

\displaystyle\beta{\left\lVert{\pi p-p_{h}}\right\rVert}_{r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}(\pi p-p_{h})\operatorname{div}_{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}{{\left\lVert{\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\rVert}_{r}}=\sup_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\operatorname{\mathbf{CR}}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}(p-p_{h})\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}{{\left\lVert{\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\rVert}_{r}}.

For the enumerator, the continuous equations (
2.15
)
and Method
3.1
imply

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(p-p_{h})\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(\boldsymbol{A}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{A}(\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\right):\nabla\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}

\displaystyle\leq\|\boldsymbol{A}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{A}(\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\|_{r^{\prime}}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{r}.

Combining the above estimates with (
2.33
) and

[
9
, Lemma 4.6]
or
[
26
, Lemma 2.4]
thus
yields

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{\pi p-p_{h}}\right\rVert}_{r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{A}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{A}(\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\|_{r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}\right\rVert}_{2}^{\frac{2}{r^{\prime}}}&\text{if }r\in(1,2]\\
{\left\lVert{\varepsilon+|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}|+|\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}|}\right\rVert}_{r}^{\frac{r-2}{2}}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{F}(\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}\right\rVert}_{2}&\text{if }r\in(2,\infty).\end{cases}

The first assertion then follows from Theorem
3.3
, and,
in the case
r>2
, from the boundedness of Galerkin
solutions
\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}\|_{r}+\|\nabla_{\!h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{r}\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{-1,r^{\prime}}^{\frac{1}{r-1}}
, with a hidden constant depending only on

r
; compare with
[
27
, (2.17)]
. The claimed a priori rate follows again from
Theorem
3.3
and a Poincaré inequality.
∎

Remark 5.2

(Rate of convergence)
.

Theorem
5.1
predicts the same rate of convergence of the
pressure-error as in
[
31
]
, under less restrictive
regularity assumptions and including
\varepsilon=0
; compare also
with Remark
3.5
. The same rate is observed also in the

[
9
]
where, however, the
theoretical expectation is suboptimal for

,

).

Method 6.1

\displaystyle\forall q_{h}

\displaystyle\in{\widehat{S}}_{0}^{0}

7.
Conclusions

We have proposed two numerical methods for the nonlinear Stokes equations (
1.1
) using nonconforming
Crouzeix-Raviart velocity elements and piecewise constant pressure elements (Method
3.1
and

4.1
)
with
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}=\nabla
and
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}
respectively. Both schemes feature a
monotone nonlinear operator acting on the velocity and thus the
discrete problems are uniquely solvable; see
Propositions
3.2
and
4.3
.
Both methods are
pressure robust. The error estimates in
Theorems
3.3
,
4.8
and
5.1

improve upon existing results for similar methods and are in line with
the best known results for conforming and divergence-free methods.

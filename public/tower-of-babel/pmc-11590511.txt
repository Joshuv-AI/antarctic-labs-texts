Genetic variations in morphology, chromosome number, and physiology of backcross progeny of the triploid hybrid clone ‘Beilinxiongzhu 1#’
Authors: Yaru Sang, Yifan Zhao, Bo Kong, Dawei Cheng, Liang Li, Lexun Ma, Jiahua Du, Yunyun Xie, Meiling Long, Dongliang Cao, Pingdong Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAneuploidy in plants commonly presents as large variations in morphology, physiology, biochemistry, and genetic effects owing to its karyotypic imbalance.ResultsIn this study, the trait variations of 393 offspring obtained by pollinating Populus alla × Populus glandulosa ‘YXY 7’ (2n = 2x = 38) with Populus ‘Beilinxiongzhu 1#’ (2n = 3x = 57) were explored. The results showed several morphological variations in the cotyledons, including two (69.68%), three (24.07%), and four cotyledons (6.25%). Additionally, the progeny exhibited extensive segregation of ploidy levels, ranging from 38 to 74. Furthermore, seedlings with different chromosome numbers varied in growth traits and physiological activities during growth and development. The backcross progeny with 51–60 chromosomes had a significantly larger basal diameter and seedling height than those with other chromosome numbers. The phenotypic correlations between the traits indicated that the dose effect of the chromosomes significantly affected the basal diameter, petiole length, and leaf length, particularly with stomatal length and stomatal density. Weak and negative estimated correlations were observed between the physiological traits associated with photosynthesis and most of the studied traits.ConclusionsThis study provides new genetic material with different chromosome compositions, which will contribute to analyzing chromosomal function and integrate the variations in chromosomal dosage into Populus breeding programs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-024-05848-1.Keywords: Backcross progeny, Aneuploid, Genetic variation, Cotyledon, Chromosomal dosage

Background

During meiosis of triploid mother cells, the association of the paired homologous chromosomes is loose, and the later stage of homologous chromosome separation is complex and diverse, resulting in the formation of sterile gametes [1–3]. However, some studies on Vaccinium uliginosum L. [4], Citrus limon L. [5], Musa acuminata Colla [6], and Populus tremula L. [7]. have shown that triploid plants are partially fertile and that triploid plants can produce x, 2x, 3x or even aneuploid fertile gametes, and there is a certain proportion of fertile gametes with different ploidies. Thus, triploids are excellent material for obtaining abundant chromosome ploidy and genetic diversity. Infiltration and aneuploidy breeding through triploid hybridization introduce the desired characteristics into cultivars and create more genetic variation.

The world’s first tetraploid apple (Malus pumila Mill.) was created by crossing a triploid variety with a diploid variety [8]. Henry et al. [9, 10] reported that aneuploid swarms, or populations of aneuploid individuals of varying aneuploid karyotypes, can be obtained from the progeny of triploid Arabidopsis thaliana (L.) Heynh. A cross between Populus ‘Yinzhong’ (2n = 3x = 57) pollen and a diploid female clone produced progeny with extensive segregation of the ploidy levels, including 29 diploids, 18 triploids, 4 tetraploids, and 48 aneuploids [11]. In addition, primary trisomy of some essential crops, such as Lycopersicon esculentum A. Gray [12], Zea mays L. [13], and Setaria italica (L.) P. Beauv. [14], has been successfully obtained by taking advantage of the irregular allocation of chromosomes during meiosis of triploid mother cells.

Gene dosage effects may lead to variabilities among plants with different chromosome number, including morphology, physiology, biochemistry, and genetics. Some studies on Catharanthus roseus (L.) G. Don and Canna × generalis L. H. Bailey & E. Z. Bailey showed that the plant height, leaf size and thickness, as well as flower color and size of the plants with different chromosome numbers were significantly different [15, 16]. Measurement of stomatal density and guard cell size of Citrus aurantifolia (Christm.) Swingle revealed that the former one has negative relation with ploidy level and a positive relation was observed between guard cell size and ploidy level. Further, area and size of nuclei also had a positive relation with ploidy level. Thus, Lokesh Bora et al. believed that stomatal density, guard cell and nuclei size can be used effectively as morphological marker for preliminary polyploid screening [17]. The comparison of stomatal density and size in complete plantlets of Allium hirtifolium (Amaryllidaceae) had revealed that with the increase chromosome numbers, stomatal density decreased because of enlargement in stomatal size [18]. Therefore, some methods and means, such as crossing triploid individuals with their diploid counterparts can be used to produce novel genetic material in higher plants.

Populus ‘Beilinxiongzhu 1#’ is an artificially synthesized male triploid hybrid that was produced by crossing the induced 2n pollen of Populus tomentosa × Populus bollean with female gametes of Populus alla × Populus glandulosa. It comprehensives ploidy and heterosis, not only has the characteristics of high quality, rapid growth, high yield and strong adaptability, but also has the advantages of no flying flocculus, beautiful tree shape, is an excellent choice for landscaping and timber forest [19]. Therefore, we pollinated Populus ‘Beilinxiongzhu 1#’ pollen with a P. alla × P. glandulosa ‘YXY 7’ stigma, hoping to breed a group of offspring plants with good characters and abundant variation. In the present study, a total of 393 seeds developed into young seedlings. Then the variations in morphology, chromosome number, and physiology of the progeny were studied, and the correlations between each character were analyzed. The findings provide some new genetic material with different chromosome compositions to analyze the chromosomal function and integrate the chromosomal dosage variation into a Populus breeding program.

Materials and methods

Plant materials

Male floral branches of Populus ‘Beilinxiongzhu 1#’ (2n = 3x = 57) were collected from Huayang Forest Tree Nursery, Hebei Province. P. alla × P. glandulosa ‘YXY 7’ (2n = 2x = 38) has good fertility, fast growth, strong resistance and wide adaptability, and plays an important role in improving ecology and urban greening. In this study, female branches of ‘YXY 7’ were collected from the Guan Country Nursery, Shandong Province. All sampled branches were trimmed and cultured in tap water in a greenhouse (10–20 °C) at the Beijing Forestry University to force floral development. No nutrients were added to the tap water.

When the stigmas of the female flower buds of P. alla × P. glandulosa ‘YXY 7’ were receptive, fresh pollen of Populus ‘Beilinxiongzhu 1#’ was used to pollinate. After pollination, the female flower branches were hydroponically cultivated in a greenhouse. After about 4 weeks, the seeds were harvested, sown in 54 × 28 × 10 cm nutrition plates to promote growth, and placed in a greenhouse.

Morphological variations in the cotyledons and chromosome number of the backcross offspring

After germinating the seeds, the excavated types of cotyledons were determined for all progeny, photographs were taken, and the proportions were calculated.

The method to determine the chromosome number in the backcross offspring was prepared with reference to our previous research [20]. The genomic DNA of all samples was extracted from young leaves using a DP320 DNA Secure Plant Kit (Tian Gen, Beijing, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. A NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Wilmington, DE, USA) was used to assess the purity and concentration of the stock DNA. Then, 19 SSR primer pairs [20] with two different alleles in the female parent and three distinct alleles in the male parent were combined with fluorescently labeled TP-M13-SSR [21] to determine the genotypes of the aneuploid progeny. Detailed information on the 19 SSR primers is listed in Table S1.

The number of chromosomes in each plantlet was ultimately confirmed by somatic chromosome counting. Seedling stem tips were collected, softened with 38% HCl, and stained with Carbol fuchsin. The chromosome number of the backcross progeny was confirmed under an Olympus BX61 microscope and 100× oil lens.

Measurement of the growth traits and leaf characteristics of the backcross offspring

The traits of the backcross offspring were measured one year after transplantation to a field at the Guan Country Nursery. The basal diameter, seedling height, and branch angle of each tree were measured using a vernier calipers, a box staff, and a protractor, respectively. The functional leaves of the offspring were selected to measure petiole length, leaf length, and leaf width with a ruler. ImageJ software (Version 1.53c; National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA) was used to measure the leaf area of all backcross offspring.

Determination of the photosynthetic characteristics of the backcross offspring

We measured stomatal conductance (Cond), intercellular CO2 concentration (Ci), net photosynthesis rate (Photo), and the transpiration rate (Trmmol) during summer from 8 to 12 AM in the functional leaves of each offspring with a portable-type apparatus for photosynthesis and transpiration measurements (LI-6400, LI-COR, Lincoln, NE, USA).

Measurement of the stomatal characteristics of the backcross offspring

After measuring the relevant photosynthetic indicators, replica impressions of the abaxial (inferior) leaf epidermis were taken at the point of maximum leaf width near the central vein of the leaf, using colorless nail polish as described by Ceulemans et al. [22]. All impressions were fixed on glass slides and images were captured under a fluorescence microscope (model BX61; Olympus, Japan) with a CCD camera (model DP70; Olympus). At least 20 microscopic fields from the abaxial surface were randomly selected per leaf. Stomatal length and stomatal width were measured using ImageJ software (Version 1.53c). Stomatal density was calculated as the number of stomata per unit of leaf area (mm− 2). The experiments were repeated three times.

Statistical analysis

The UNIVARIATE procedure in SPSS software (SPSS for Windows, version 20, SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) was used to analyze the phenotypic correlations of the pairs of traits among the backcross offspring of Populus ‘Beilinxiongzhu 1#’. Excel (version 2206 Build 16.0.15330.20260, Microsoft Co., Redmond, WA, USA) was used to aid in the statistical analysis and for drawing.

Results

Variations in the backcross offspring cotyledons

In this study, 4,510 seeds were obtained by pollinating P. alla × P. glandulosa ‘YXY 7’ with Populus ‘Beilinxiongzhu 1#’. Due to the unbalanced distribution of genetic material, some of the seeds produced by the hybrid combination with triploid poplar as the parent did not germinate normally. Only 1,151 seeds germinated. The cotyledon excavated type from 1,151 seeds was determined and counted.

Three kinds of variations in cotyledon morphology including dicotyledons (Fig. 1a–d), tricotyledons (Fig. 1e–g), and quadricotyledons (Fig. 1h, i) were observed in the hybrid combination with triploid Populus ‘Beilinxiongzhu 1#’ as the male parent. As shown in Tables 1 and 802 seedlings with dicotyledons varied morphologically, with a variation rate of 69.68%. Among the seedlings with dicotyledons, the cotyledons of 26 seedlings were partially connected (3.24%), and the cotyledons of 21 seedlings were curly (2.62%). In addition, the percentages of variation in the cotyledon morphology of tricotyledons, and quadricotyledons were 24.07%, and 6.25%, respectively. A few seedlings with tricotyledons had the cotyledon completely connected, and the variation rate was only 0.43%. About half of the quadricotyledons seedlings had cotyledons partially connected. Due to the unbalanced distribution of chromosomes, 393 seeds developed into young seedlings.

Variations in the chromosome number of the backcross offspring

Nineteen pairs of distinct SSR markers covering all 19 chromosomes screened from the 184 pairs of SSR markers were used to determine the number of chromosomes in the backcross progeny. All polymorphic SSR markers were BLAST searched against the publicly available genome sequence of P. trichocarpa v4.1 (https://phytozome-next.jgi.doe.gov/info/Ptrichocarpa_v4_1 to determine the physical positions of the SSR loci. All 19 SSR primer pairs with two different alleles in the female parent and three distinct alleles in the male parent were screened out to genotype the backcross progeny. Detailed information on the 19 SSR primers is listed in Table S1. The SSR sequence, the chromosome number, the melting temperature (Tm), the motif sequences, and the physical position of each SSR pair in the parents are described.

Based on the number of shared alleles between the female parent P. alla × P. glandulosa ‘YXY 7’ and the male parent Populus ‘Beilinxiongzhu 1#’, all 19 SSR primer pairs were divided into three types, (I) both parents were heterozygous and had distinct alleles (Fig. 2a); (II) both parents were heterozygous and shared one distinct allele (Fig. 2b), and (III) both parents were heterozygous and shared two distinct alleles (Fig. 2c). The genotyping of the backcross progeny was inferred according to the relative allelic dosage estimate from the MAC-PR analysis at all loci for each sample (Fig. 2). Thus, the chromosome number of 393 offspring was initially determined by the 19 SSR primers pairs. In this study, 35 progeny were randomly selected for counting of the somatic chromosomes. The number of chromosomes counted using the somatic chromosome counting method was consistent with the SSR molecular markers (Fig. 3).

The number of chromosomes in the backcross offspring ranged from 38 to 74 (Table S2). In addition, the total number of chromosomes in the progeny was bimodally distributed with a trough at 59. The total number of chromosomes of most progeny was between 63 and 71 (Fig. 4). Based on the estimated chromosome number, the backcross progeny of triploid Populus ‘Beilinxiongzhu 1#’ were divided into five groups of 38–40, 41–50, 51–60, 61–70, and more than 70 chromosomes.

Variations in basal diameter, seedling height, and branch angle in the 1-year-old backcross offspring

Variations in basal diameter, seedling height, and branch angle were detected among the 1-year-old backcross progeny due to the different numbers of chromosomes within somatic cells (Fig. 5). The basal diameter of the backcross offspring ranged from 15.82 to 24.75 mm, with an average of 22.60 ± 0.53 mm. The difference in seedling height between the lowest and highest means was 29.02%. The variations in basal diameter (Fig. 5a) and seedling height (Fig. 5b) were similar, and maximum growth was observed when the number of chromosomes was between 51 and 60. Growth was faster for offspring with 51–74 chromosomes than for those with 38–50 chromosomes (Fig. 5a, b). In addition, no significant difference in the branch angle of the offspring was observed with different chromosome numbers, most of which ranged from 40° to 50°. Basal diameter and seedling height displayed extreme phenotypic variations, much higher than the branch angle. Basal diameter and tree height revealed their greatest differences in backcross progeny with different numbers of chromosomes.

Variations in leaf photosynthesis and the related physiological and structural traits in the backcross offspring

As shown in Fig. 6, the four leaves traits increased as the chromosome number increased. Progeny with 38–40 chromosomes had the shortest petioles (6.12 ± 1.25 cm). The petiole length of offspring with more than 70 chromosomes was 9.51 ± 1.63 cm. No significant differences in leaf length or width were observed among the backcross progeny with chromosome numbers other than 38–40. However, the leaf area was different in progeny with different numbers of chromosomes. The order of the leaf area in the backcross offspring with different numbers of chromosomes was 71–74 > 51–60 > 61–70 > 38–40. We speculate that this result was primarily due to the large difference in the leaf shape of the backcross progeny.

Variations in the leaf characters of the backcross offspring of Populus ‘Beilinxiongzhu 1#’

Variations in stomatal length (Fig. 7a) and stomatal width (Fig. 7b) were similar, and the maximum stomatal length and width were observed when the number of chromosomes was 61–70. The stomatal length of the backcross offspring ranged from 21.01 to 33.75 μm, with an average of 27.30 ± 0.50 μm. The stomatal width of the backcross offspring ranged from 12.45 to 21.91 μm, with an average of 15.95 ± 0.36 μm. Similarly, backcross progeny with the largest stomata had 61–70 chromosomes (Fig. 7d). Overall, the stomatal density of the backcross offspring decreased as the number of chromosomes increased. The leaves of the offspring of triploid poplar had 276.75 ± 9.24 stomata/mm2 (Fig. 7c).

Cond (0.35 ± 0.02 mol m− 2s− 1) and Trmmol (4.44 ± 0.62 mmol m− 2s− 1) were the highest when the number of chromosomes was 41–50 (Fig. 8a and d). Compared with the other backcross progeny, the offspring with 38–40 chromosomes had the lowest Cond, Ci, and Trmmol values, and the highest Photo value. Furthermore, less variation was observed in the Photo value among the different offspring (14.57 ± 0.71 µmol m− 1s− 1 to 16.44 ± 1.01 µmol m− 1s− 1) (Fig. 8b). Correlations between traits

As shown in Fig. 9, significant correlations were detected between the number of chromosomes and most of the studied traits, including basal diameter (p = 0.04), petiole length (p = 0.016), leaf length (p = 0.043), and, particularly, stomatal length (p < 0.01), stomatal density (p < 0.01), and Ci (p < 0.01). In addition, basal diameter, seeding height, and the branch angle were positively correlated with petiole length, leaf length, leaf width, and leaf area, except for the relationship between branch angle and leaf area. Similarly, stomatal density and stomatal size were extremely significantly correlated with stomatal length and stomatal width, but they were not significantly correlated with some photosynthetic traits. Additionally, Ci was highly negatively correlated with Trmmol and Photo, but no significant relationship was found with Cond. Cond was positively correlated with Trmmol. Photo was positively correlated with Ci. Overall, there were strong positive correlations between the growth traits and the leaf traits. Weak and negative estimated correlations were observed between physiological traits associated with photosynthesis and most of the studied traits.

Discussion

Populus spp. are dicotyledonous angiosperms. Normally, two cotyledons emerge from the ground and the seed germinates under the proper conditions. In this study, the types of cotyledons varied highly in the backcross progeny, and 69.68% of the seedlings had two cotyledons. A few seedlings had three and four cotyledons, and the rates of variation were 24.07% and 6.25%, respectively. Similarly, some morphological variations, such as tricotyledons and quadricotyledons, were observed in the hybrid offspring between triploid Chinese white poplar [(Populus tomentosa × P. bolleana) × P. tomentosa] and P. tomentosa × P. bolleana, with a total variation rate of 26.47% [23]. We agree with Kang and Mao [23] that the morphological variations in the cotyledons of triploid progeny are related to the imbalance of chromosomes. In addition, some researchers believe that the number of cotyledons is also affected by many factors such as hormones, mutagens, growth environment and nutrition. In some cases, the polycotyledon mutant character is useful, so agronomists and horticulturalists try different methods to mutate the character in order to obtain new crop varieties [24, 25]. Some polycotyledonous plants showed no or even weaker advantages in growth potential, botanical characteristics, in vitro organ formation and flowering and fruited habits. In this study, a total of 1,151 seeds were germinated and 393 seeds were sown and developed into young seedlings. We hypothesized that the emergence of a low proportion of seedling growth was also related to the emergence of a high proportion of polycotyledon.

The cross between Populus ‘Yinzhong’ (2n = 3x = 57) pollen and a diploid female clone produces progeny with extensive segregation of ploidy levels, including 29 diploids, 18 triploids, 4 tetraploids, and 48 aneuploids [11]. Aneuploid progeny of some species, such as Musa acuminata [6], Setaria italica [26], Cucumis sativus L. [27], Malus pumila [28], and Echinacea purpurea L. [29], have been obtained by crossing diploids with triploids. In this study, 393 backcross offspring (2 diploid and 391 aneuploid) were obtained by pollinating P. alla × P. glandulosa ‘YXY 7’ with Populus ‘Beilinxiongzhu 1#’. The chromosome numbers of the backcross offspring varied greatly from 38 to 74. In a previous study, we showed that high frequencies of meiotic abnormalities were detected in triploid Populus ‘Beilinxiongzhu 1#’, including univalents, prematurely migrating chromosomes, lagging chromosomes, chromosome bridges, and micronuclei, indicating genetic imbalance in this triploid [20]. We inferred that offspring with aneuploid chromosome sets form as a result of unbalanced chromosome segregation. Hybridization using triploids as vectors may yield some offspring with special chromosomal compositions, including trisomy and tetrasomy [30]. Trisomy, tetrasomy, and ditrisomy offspring are precious germplasm resources with special value. For example, single chromosome replacement can be achieved by backcrossing these aneuploid individuals with diploids. Therefore, penetrative breeding and ploidy breeding through triploid hybridization can be used to introduce the desired characteristics into cultivars and create more genetic variation [31].

The gene dosage balance is disrupted in aneuploidy, which may lead to variabilities among plants, including morphology, physiology, biochemistry, and genetics [32, 33]. Henry et al. [32] hybridized diploid, triploid, and tetraploid A. thaliana and obtained a batch of aneuploid progeny with significant differences in traits, such as stem diameter, empty axils, and the size of rosettes. Furthermore, they demonstrated that certain traits are strongly associated with the dosage of a specific chromosome type in this highly heterogeneous population, and that chromosomal effects can be additive. Aneuploid Hydrangea macrophylla (Thunb.) Ser abnormally develop and there is huge phenotypic variation within offspring populations [34]. In addition, aneuploid variations are also manifested at the molecular level [35–38]. In this study, the variations in morphological, cytological, and physiological characteristics of aneuploid progeny were explored. The results showed that the growth traits, leaf characters, and photosynthetic characteristics of the progeny with different chromosome numbers were significantly different. Unfortunately, there was a lack of exploration at the molecular level. Next, we will explore the variations in aneuploid progeny at different levels and explain the relationships between certain traits and the doses of specific chromosome types.

In addition, the most prominent variations in triploid gymnosperms are height and diameter growth [39, 40], which are reflected in Populus [41–43], Eucalyptus urophylla S.T. Blake [44], Eucommia ulmoides Oliv. [45], and Salix babylonica L. [46, 47]. In this study, backcross progeny with 51–60 chromosomes had significantly larger basal diameters and heights than those with other chromosome numbers. This might be because the aneuploid offspring with 51– 60 chromosomes were close to that of triploid Populus (2n = 3x = 57).

Conclusion

Aneuploidy commonly results in large variations in morphology, physiology, biochemistry, and genetics owing to karyotypic imbalance. In this study, backcross progeny with different chromosome numbers varied highly in cotyledon type, chromosome number, growth traits, and photosynthetic properties during growth and development. Our study provides some new genetic material with different chromosome compositions that will contribute to studying the genetic control of meiotic pairing, chromosome balance, and dosage compensation, as well as chromosomal manipulation during the breeding of Populus.

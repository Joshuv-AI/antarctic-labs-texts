Generalized H2 observer for road profile estimation in the automotive suspension system
Authors: Thanh-Phong Pham
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This study develops a generalized H2 observer to estimate the road profile in the semi-active automotive suspension system. The dynamics of the quarter-car model are represented in the descriptor nonlinear parameter-varying system framework, with the road profile as a system's state that is of arbitrary dynamics. Then, a road profile estimation method is designed by using a generalized H2 observer, which utilizes the onboard accelerometers as the observer's input. The adverse impact of measurement noises on the accuracy of estimation results is alleviated via the generalized H2 framework. The proposed observer is designed by solving an LMI-based constrained optimization problem. Frequency domain analysis and time domain simulations illustrate the efficiency of the proposed strategy

Introduction

Road profile estimation/classification is of paramount importance not only in the vehicle dynamics control system (see for instance [1], [2], [3] and the references therein), but also in the road qualification monitoring system [4]. To obtain the road profile information, the measurement approaches utilize either the profilometers developed in [5], [6], or visual inspection [7]. Besides, several road profile estimation approaches have been developed to replace expensive direct measurement methods. The method in [8] proposes an artificial neural network-based technique for estimating road excitation using accelerometers. Furthermore, authors in [9] present a robust H∞ estimation method to estimate the automotive suspension system variables. Then the road profile is reconstructed from the estimated variables, the sprung and unsprung mass accelerations, and the dynamics of the quarter-car system. Additionally, [10] and [11] propose the adaptive estimation approach for road profile estimation based on Youla–Kučera parameterization and classify the road roughness using ISO standards. Notice that these methods require the sprung mass displacement information as observer input. In order to only use the onboard accelerometers, in our previous work [12], an H∞ observer is designed for a descriptor system which considers road displacement as a system's state.

It is worth noting that the state observer-based road profile estimation approach needs the dynamics of the road displacement, which are difficult to identify. Some dynamics models are now utilized, such as in [10] and [13]; however, they are only valid for the ISO categorization and hence lack universality. To handle this issue, the dynamical system can be formulated into a descriptor system as in [12], which does not require knowledge of the road profile's dynamics. Then, the robust observer proposed in [14], [15], [16], [17] for the singular system can be used to estimate the road displacement. Along this research line, an H∞ observer utilized two acceleration data of the semi-active suspension developed in our previous work [12] to reconstruct road displacement input. However, the mentioned method used H∞ criterion to handle measurement noises that usually appear in the high-frequency domain, and the work [12] also uses a parameter-independent model that lacks adaptability. To extend previous work, in this study, the dynamical system is formulated into a descriptor nonlinear parameter-varying system. Then the generalized H2 observer is designed by minimizing the effect of measurement noises via the generalized H2 criterion. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:•A dynamical nonlinear semi-active suspension model is formulated into a nonlinear parameter-varying descriptor system to avoid using any equation representing the road displacement, considering the PWM control input as a known scheduling parameter;•The approach for estimating the road displacement input is then developed using a generalized H2 observer for the nonlinear parameter-varying descriptor system. The aim is to minimize the effect of measurement noises on estimation errors;•The proposed method is validated through realistic simulations on an experimentally evaluated test bench model.

Conclusion

This paper proposes a generalized H2 observer for road profile estimation. The system dynamics are initially modeled as a descriptor nonlinear parameter varying system, with two accelerometers serving as inputs to the generalized H2 observer. The proposed method is designed to deliver accurate road profile estimates by minimizing the estimation error within the H2 framework. The proposed method is compared with the H∞ observer in road profile estimation. The effectiveness of the algorithm is demonstrated through both frequency domain analysis and time domain simulations, accurately estimating the road profile of the semi-active (SA) damper. For future works, some interesting issues concern road profile adaptive control for vehicle systems, or monitoring of road surface quality (using the proposed observer).

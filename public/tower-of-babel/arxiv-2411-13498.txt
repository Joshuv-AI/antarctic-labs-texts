Hopf’s lemmas and boundary behaviour of solutions to the fractional Laplacian in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces
Pablo Ochoa, Ariel Salort

Abstract
. In this article we study different extensions of the celebrated Hopf’s boundary lemma within the context of a family of nonlocal, nonlinear and nonstandard growth operators. More precisely, we examine the behavior of solutions of the fractional a − a- Laplacian operator near the boundary of a domain satisfying the interior ball condition. Our approach addresses problems involving both constant-sign and sign-changing potentials.

35P20, 46E30, 35R11,47J10

1.
Introduction

The Hopf’s lemma for nonlocal operators has received significant attention in recent years, as it provides a powerful framework for analyzing nonlocal problems. This lemma enables applications across a wide range of scientific fields, including game theory, finance, image processing and Lévy processes among others. See for example
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
13
]
and the references therein.

Classically, the Hopf’s lemma provides a refined analysis of the outer normal derivative of superharmonic functions at a minimum boundary point of a bounded domain
\Omega
that satisfies the interior ball
condition: if
u\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
is such that
u(x_{0})<u(x)
for all
x\in\Omega
, then
-\Delta u\geq c(x)u
in
\Omega
implies that
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\eta}(x_{0})<0
. Here
c\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
is a function such that
c(x)\leq 0
and
\partial u/\partial\eta
is the outer normal derivative of
u
at
x_{0}
. See for instance
[
14
, Lemma 3.4]
.

Under the same assumptions on
c
and
\Omega
, a nonlocal extension of this result was introduced in
[
8
,
15
]
for the fractional Laplacian. In this generalization, it is shown that if
u
is a (weak) solution to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}u\geq cu&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u\geq 0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\end{cases}\quad\text{ then }\quad\lim_{B_{R}\ni x\to x_{0}}\frac{u(x)}{\delta_{R}^{s}(x)}>0,

where
\delta_{R}
is the distance function from
x
to
\partial B_{R}
, being
B_{R}
an interior ball at
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
.

Subsequently, in
[
6
]
and later with an alternative proof in
[
20
]
, the previous result was extended to the fractional
p-
Laplacian. This operator, up to a normalization constant, is defined as

(-\Delta_{p})^{s}:=\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy

for
p>1
and
s\in(0,1)
. Under the same assumptions on
\Omega
and
c
as before, here the Hopf’s lemma asserts that if
u
is a (weak) solution to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u\geq c|u|^{p-2}u&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u\geq 0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\end{cases}\quad\text{ then }\quad\lim_{B_{R}\ni x\to x_{0}}\frac{u(x)}{\delta_{R}^{s}(x)}>0.

Recently, this result was extended to cover the nonlocal non-standard growth operator known as fractional
a-
Laplacian, which is defined in
[
12
]
as

(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u(x):=\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a\left(|D^{s}u(x,y)|\right)\frac{D^{s}u(x,y)}{|D^{s}u(x,y)|}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}

where
s\in(0,1)
and
D^{s}u(x,y):=\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}
is the nonlocal gradient. Here
A
is a so-called Young function, that is, mainly a convex function
A:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that
A^{\prime}(t)=a(t)
for
t>0
. More precisely, under the previous hypotheses on
\Omega
and
c
, and assuming the following growth condition on
A
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle 1<p\leq\frac{ta^{\prime}(t)}{a(t)}\leq q<\infty\qquad\text{ for some }p,q>1,

in
[
21
]
it is claimed that if
u
is a weak solution to

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u\geq ca(u)&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u\geq 0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\end{cases}\quad\text{ then }\quad\lim_{B_{R}\ni x\to x_{0}}\frac{u(x)}{\delta_{R}^{s}(x)}>0,

As recently discussed in detail in
[
1
,
p. 2
]
, we also remark that we could not verify the arguments presented in
[
6
, Lemma 4.1]
, which are employed to establish Hopf’s principle for the fractional p-Laplacian. Similar reasoning was subsequently employed in
[
21
]
to derive the Hopf’s principle for the fractional
a-
Laplacian operator stated in (
1.2
), however, we are unable to fully confirm the validity of this proof as well.

In the article
[
20
]
, we introduced a different approach to that in
[
6
]
to establish the Hopf’s lemma for the fractional
p-
Laplacian. Building on these ideas, we now present an alternative method to establish a Hopf’s lemma for the fractional
a-
Laplacian. This approach is based on constructing a barrier function that involves a scaling of the distance function. However, although this proof overcomes the issue mentioned in
[
1
,
p. 2
]
, it does not yield a lower bound with the expected exponent in the distance function.

A
is a Young function satisfying the following growth condition:

For our first result we assume the following growth condition on the Young function
A\in C^{2}([0,\infty))
: there are
p
and
q
such that

(1.3)

0<p-2\leq\frac{ta^{\prime\prime}(t)}{a^{\prime}(t)}\leq q-2<\infty.

By integration by parts twice, (
1.3
) yields

(1.4)

2<p\leq\frac{ta(t)}{A(t)}\leq q<\infty.

Condition (
1.4
) in the case of the fractional
p-
Laplacian (i.e.
A(t)=t^{p}
) means that

.

In our first result, for continuous supersolutions of the fractional
a-
Laplacian we establish a uniform bound near the boundary expressed in terms of the distance function:

Theorem 1.1
.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, let
u\in W^{s,A}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
be a weak solution to

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u\geq\varepsilon>0&\text{ in }\Omega\\
u=0&\text{ in }\Omega^{c},\end{cases}

where
\Omega
satisfies the interior sphere condition. Then, for
\rho\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
small enough,

u(x)\geq C_{\varepsilon}d_{\Omega}(x)\qquad x\in\Omega_{\rho}=\{x\in\Omega\colon d_{\Omega}(x)<\rho\}

where
C_{\varepsilon}
is a positive constant depending of
\varepsilon
.

When the equation involves a constant-sign potential function, we establish the following result concerning the boundary behavior of super-solutions:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open and bounded set satisfying the interior ball condition at
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, let
c\in C(\overline{\Omega})
be such that
c(x)\leq 0
in
\Omega
and let
u\in\widetilde{W}^{s,A}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
a solution to

(1.6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u\geq c(x)a(u)&\text{ in }\Omega\\
u>0&\text{ in }\Omega\\
u\geq 0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\end{cases}

in the weak sense. Then

\liminf_{B_{R}\ni x\to x_{0}}\frac{u(x)}{\delta_{R}(x)}>0

where
B_{R}\subseteq\Omega
and
x_{0}\in\partial B_{R}
and
\delta_{R}(x)
is the distance from
x
to
B_{R}^{c}
.

Finally, as a counterpart to Theorem
1.2
, we investigate the validity of Hopf’s boundary lemma without imposing any sign conditions on the function
c
. Our approach is based primarily on adapting
[
20
, Theorem 3.1]
and
[
7
]
, with careful modifications to account for the non-homogeneity of the operator.

Given a point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
where the interior ball condition is satisfied, we consider the class
\mathcal{Z}_{x_{0}}
consisting of functions
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
u
is continuous in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
|u|>0
in
B_{r}(x_{r})
for all sufficiently small

,
u(x_{0})=0
and the following growth condition is true

\limsup_{r\to 0}\Phi(r)=+\infty,\quad\text{ where }\quad\Phi(r):=\dfrac{(\inf_{B_{r/2}(x_{r})}|u|)^{p-1}}{r^{ps}},

where
p
is given in (
1.4
).

For this class of functions we have the following:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and bounded and
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
. Assume that
\Omega
satisfies the interior ball condition at
x_{0}
.
Let
u\in\mathcal{Z}_{x_{0}}
, such that
u^{-}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and

(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u\geq c(x)a(u)\quad\text{ in }\Omega

weakly, where
c\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
with
c^{-}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Further, suppose that there is
R>0
such that
u\geq 0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
and
u>0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega
. Then, for every
\beta\in(0,\pi/2)
, the following strict inequality holds

(1.7)

\liminf_{\Omega\ni x\to x_{0}}\dfrac{u(x)-u(x_{0})}{|x-x_{0}|}>0,

whenever the angle between
x-x_{0}
and the vector joining
x_{0}
and the center of the interior ball is smaller than
\pi/2-\beta
.

A core element of our proofs is the calculation of the fractional
a-
Laplacian of the scaled distance function. Unlike the case of powers (i.e. the fractional
p-
Laplacian), the lack of homogeneity of the operator prevents us from using (
1.4
) to conclude that

(-\Delta_{a})^{s}(cd^{s}(x))\leq C(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u(x)

for some constant depending on
c
,
p
and
q
. The issue stems from the fact that the sign of
u(x)-u(y)
is not constant for different values of
x
and
y
. See section

for a discussion of this limitation. However, since our barrier arguments only require
(-\Delta_{a})^{s}(cu(x))
to be sufficiently small when
c
is small, it is enough for our purposes to examine certain continuity properties of the operator. The key proposition in our paper is stated in Proposition
4.1
:

Proposition
.

Let
A
be a Young function satisfying (
1.3
) with
p>\max\{\frac{1}{1-s},2\}
, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain. Let
u\in Lip(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u
is continuous at 0 in the sense that if
\{c_{k}\}_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}
is a sequence such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}c_{k}=0
then

\lim_{k\to\infty}(-\Delta_{a})^{s}(c_{k}u(x))=0

uniformly for all

.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we give some basic definitions and results related to Orlicz and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. We start recalling the definition of an Young.

2.1.
Young functions

Definition 2.1
.

A function
A\colon[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called a Young function if it admits the representation

A(t)=\int_{0}^{t}a(\tau)d\tau,

where the function
a
is right-continuous for
t\geq 0
, positive for
t>0
, non-decreasing and satisfies the conditions

a(0)=0,\quad a(\infty)=\lim_{t\to\infty}a(t)=\infty.

By
[
16
, Chapter 1]
, a Young function has also the following properties:

(i)

A
is continuous, convex, increasing, even and
A(0)=0
.

(ii)

A
is super-linear at zero and at infinite, that is

\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\dfrac{A(x)}{x}=0

and

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{A(x)}{x}=\infty.

We will assume that
A
is extended as a odd function to the whole
\mathbb{R}
.

An important property for Young functions is the following:

Definition 2.2
.

A Young function
A
satisfies the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition if there exists
C>2
such that

A(2x)\leq CA(x)\,\,\text{~~~for all~~}x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

By
[
16
, Theorem 4.1, Chapter 1]
, a Young function satisfies
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition if and only if there is
q>1
such that

(2.1)

\frac{ta(t)}{A(t)}\leq q,~~~~~\forall\,t>0.

Associated to
A
is the Young function complementary to it which is defined as follows:

(2.2)

\widetilde{A}(t):=\sup\left\{tw-A(w)\colon w>0\right\}.

The definition of the complementary function assures that the following Young-type inequality holds

(2.3)

st\leq A(t)+\widetilde{A}(s)\text{ for every }s,t\geq 0.

By
[
16
, Theorem 4.3, Chapter 1]
, a necessary and sufficient condition for the Young function
\widetilde{A}
complementary to
A
to satisfy the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition is that there is
p>1
such that

(2.4)

p\leq\frac{ta(t)}{A(t)},~~~~~\forall\,t>0.

From now on, we will assume that the Young function
A(t)=\int_{0}^{t}a(\tau)d\tau
satisfies the following growth behavior:

(2.5)

0<p-2\leq\frac{ta^{\prime\prime}(t)}{a^{\prime}(t)}\leq q-2<\infty,~~~~~\forall t>0.

which, in particular, gives that
A
and
\tilde{A}
satisfy the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition.

Relation (
2.5
) gives a lower and upper control of
A
and its derivatives in terms of power functions:

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\min\{t^{p},t^{q}\}&\leq A(t)\leq\max\{t^{p},t^{q}\},\\
p\min\{t^{p-1},t^{q-1}\}&\leq a(t)\leq q\max\{t^{p-1},t^{q-1}\},\\
p(p-1)\min\{t^{p-2},t^{q-2}\}&\leq a^{\prime}(t)\leq q(q-1)\max\{t^{p-2},t^{q-2}\}.\end{split}

As a consequence
a^{\prime}(t)>0
for
t>0
, which yields by (
2.5
) that
a^{\prime\prime}(t)>0
. Thus,
a^{\prime}(t)
is strictly increasing for all
t>0
.

The following lemma is proved in
[
19
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

There exists
C>0
depending only of
p
and
q
such that

a(t_{2})-a(t_{1})\geq Ca(t_{2}-t_{1})

for all
t_{2}\geq t_{2}
.

We end the section with the following lemma

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
A
be a Young function satisfying (
2.5
), and
r,t\in\mathbb{R}
, then

|a(r+t)-a(t)|\leq q(q-1)\max\{(|r|+|t|)^{p-2},(|r|+|t|)^{q-2}\}|r|.

Proof.

Since
a^{\prime}
is increasing, using (
2.6
)

\displaystyle a(r+t)-a(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{r+t}a^{\prime}(t)\,dt\leq|a^{\prime}(r+t)||r|

\displaystyle\leq q(q-1)\max\{(|r|+|t|)^{p-2},(|r|+|t|)^{q-2}\}|r|,

which concludes the proof.
∎

2.2.
Orlicz-fractional Sobolev spaces

Given a Young function
A
with
a=A^{\prime}
,
s\in(0,1)
,
and an open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
we consider the spaces:

\displaystyle L^{A}(\Omega)=\Big\{u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\colon\Phi_{A,\Omega}(u)<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle W^{s,A}(\Omega)=\Big\{u\in L^{G}(\Omega)\colon\Phi_{s,A,\Omega}(u)<\infty\Big\},\text{ and }

\displaystyle L_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\Big\{u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\colon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a\Big(\dfrac{|u(x)|}{1+|x|^{s}}\Big)\dfrac{dx}{1+|x|^{n+s}}<\infty\Big\}.

Here, the modulars
\Phi_{A,\Omega}
and
\Phi_{s,A,\Omega}
are defined as

\Phi_{A,\Omega}(u):=\int_{\Omega}A(|u(x)|)\,dx,\quad\text{ and }\quad\Phi_{s,A,\Omega}(u):=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}A\left(|D^{s}u|\right)d\nu

being
D^{s}u=\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}
and
d\nu=\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{n}}
.
Finally, we introduce the space

\widetilde{W}^{s,A}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\exists U,\Omega\subset\subset U\text{ and }\|u\|_{W^{s,A}(U)}<\infty\right\}.

The spaces
L^{A}(\Omega)
and
W^{s,A}(\Omega)
are endowed,
respectively, with the following norms

\|u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\colon\Phi_{A,\Omega}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\},

and

\|u\|_{W^{s,A}(\Omega)}=||u||_{L^{A}(\Omega)}+[u]_{W^{s,A}(\Omega)},

where

[u]_{W^{s,A}(\Omega)}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\colon\Phi_{s,A,\Omega}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}.

The space
W^{s,A}_{0}(\Omega)
is defined as the set of
u\in W^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
. Finally, we recall that under the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition for
A
and
\tilde{A}
, all the Orlicz-Sobolev spaces are separable and reflexive spaces.

The following lemma relates modulars and norms in Orlicz spaces.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A
be a Young function satisfying (
2.5
), and let

\xi^{\pm}\colon[0,\infty)

\to\mathbb{R}
be defined as

\xi^{-}(t)=\min\big\{t^{p},t^{q}\big\},\quad\text{ and }\quad\xi^{+}(t)=\max\big\{t^{p},t^{q}\big\}.

Then

(i)

\xi^{-}(\|u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)})\leq\Phi_{A,\Omega}(u)\leq\xi^{+}(\|u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)})
;

(ii)

\xi^{-}([u]_{W^{s,A}(\Omega)})\leq\Phi_{s,A,\Omega}(u)\leq\xi^{+}([u]_{W^{s,A}(\Omega)}).

2.3.
The fractional
a-
Laplacian

Given a Young function
A
, we define the fractional
a-
Laplacian of order
s\in(0,1)
of a sufficiently smooth function
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
as

(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u(x):=\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a\left(D^{s}u(x,y)\right)\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
D^{s}u(x,y):=\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}
. Moreover, the following representation formula holds

(2.7)

\langle(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u,\varphi\rangle:=\dfrac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}a\left(D^{s}u\right)D^{s}\varphi\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{n}}

for any
u,\varphi\in W^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. See
[
12
, Section 6.3]
for further details. Moreover, it follows that if
u\in\widetilde{W}^{s,A}(\Omega)
, then the functional

W_{0}^{s,A}(\Omega)\ni\varphi\mapsto\left\langle u,\varphi\right\rangle,

is finite, linear and continuous. Hence, the following definition makes sense:

Definition 2.6
.

We say that
u\in\widetilde{W}^{s,A}(\Omega)
is a
weak super-solution
to
(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u=f
in
\Omega
if

\langle(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u,\varphi\rangle\geq\int_{\Omega}f\varphi\,dx,

for any
\varphi\in W^{s,A}_{0}(\Omega)
,
\varphi\geq 0.
Similarly, reversing the inequalities we can define
weak sub-solutions
to
(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u=f
.
Finally,
(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u=f
is satisfied in the
weak sense
if it is simultaneously a weal super- and sub-solution.

2.4.
The interior ball condition

Given an open set
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, we say that
\Omega
satisfies the
interior ball condition
at
x_{0}
if there is
r_{0}>0
such that, for every
r\in(0,r_{0}]
, there exists a ball
B_{r}(x_{r})\subset\Omega
with
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega\cap\partial B_{r}(x_{r})
.

By
[
18
]
,
\Omega
satisfies the interior ball condition if and only if
\partial\Omega\in C^{1,1}
.

2.5.
Further notation

We denote by
\omega_{n}
the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Given
R>0
,
s>0
we define the scaled function

(2.8)

a_{R}(t):=a\left(\frac{t}{R^{s}}\right).

Both functions in (
2.8
) define the same space: denoting
A_{R}^{\prime}=a_{R}
, it holds that

W^{s,A}(\Omega)=W^{s,A_{R}}(\Omega)

Finally, given
\Omega
a set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the distance function
d_{\Omega}
is defined as

d_{\Omega}(x):=\mathop{\text{dist}}(x,\Omega^{c}),

where
\mathop{\text{dist}}(x,\Omega^{c}):=\inf\left\{|x-y|:y\in\Omega^{c}\right\}
.

3.
Preliminary results on the fractional
a
-Laplacian

In this section we collect some useful results regarding solutions of the fractional
a-
Laplacian. We start by recalling the following result stated in
[
11
, Theorem 4.9]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
u\in W^{s,A}_{0}(\Omega)
be a weak solution of
|(\Delta_{a})^{s}u|\leq K
in
\Omega
for some
K>0
. Then

|u|\leq Cd_{\Omega}^{s}\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega,

where
C
is a positive constant depending on
s
,
n
,
A
and
\Omega
.

The following
comparison principle
is stated in
[
9
, Lemma 3.10]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be bounded,
u,v\in W^{s,A}(\Omega)\cap C(\Omega)
such that
u\leq v
in
\Omega^{c}
and assume that

\langle u,\varphi\rangle\leq\langle v,\varphi\rangle\quad\forall\varphi\in W^{s,A}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C(\Omega),\,\varphi\geq 0\text{ in }\Omega.

Then
u\leq v
in
\Omega
.

A
weak maximum principle
is stated in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
A
be a Young function such that
p>2
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and bounded. Then, if
u\in W^{s,A}_{0}(\Omega)
is a weak supersolution to
(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u=0
in
\Omega
,
u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
then
u\geq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
u\in W^{s,A}_{0}(\Omega)
,
u\neq 0
, be a weak supersolution to
(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u=0
in
\Omega
. Let
u^{\pm}:=\max\{\pm u,0\}
be the positive and negative parts of
u
, respectively. Assume
u^{-}\neq 0
. Testing with
u^{-}\in W^{s,A}_{0}(\Omega)
we obtain that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}a(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}D^{s}u^{-}\,d\nu=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}D^{s}uD^{s}u^{-}\left(\int_{0}^{1}a^{\prime}((1-t)|D^{s}u|)\,dt\right)d\nu.

We observe that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}D^{s}u(x,y)D^{s}u^{-}(x,y)&=\frac{u^{+}(x)-u^{+}(y)-(u^{-}(x)-u^{-}(y))}{|x-y|^{s}}\frac{u^{-}(x)-u^{-}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\\
&=-\frac{u^{+}(x)u^{-}(y)+u^{+}(y)u^{-}(x)}{|x-y|^{2s}}-(D^{s}u^{-}(x,y))^{2}\leq 0.\end{split}

Moreover,
p>2
yields that
a^{\prime}(t)\geq 0
for all
t\geq 0
and
a^{\prime}(t)=0
if and only if
t=0
. This implies that
D^{s}u^{-}\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, that is,
u^{-}\equiv 0
, which gives
u\geq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

Proposition 3.5

4.
Continuity properties of the fractional
a
-laplacian

A central aspect of our argument involves computing the fractional
a-
Laplacian of the scaled distance. For fractional
p-
Laplacian, the homogeneity of the operator plays a key role. Specifically, when
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
c>0
is a scaling factor, we have the following property:

(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u(x)=c^{p}(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u(x).

This identity is essential when employing barrier arguments, as it allows for the preservation of homogeneity under scaling transformations.

However, when attempting to derive an analogous expression for the fractional
a-
Laplacian, we find out a significant obstacle: we cannot apply conditions (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) to establish

(-\Delta_{a})^{s}(cu(x))\leq c^{p}(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u(x)\quad\text{ or }\quad(-\Delta_{a})^{s}(cu(x))\leq c^{q}(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u(x).

The difficulty arises because the sign of the difference
u(x)-u(y)
is not constant; instead, it depends on the specific points
x
and
y
. This dependency complicates the application of homogeneity arguments and prevents a straightforward extension of the previous result. To address this issue, in the following proposition we study some continuity properties of the operator.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
A
be a Young function satisfying (
2.5
) with
p>\max\{\frac{1}{1-s},2\}
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain. Let
u\in Lip(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u
is continuous at 0 in the sense that if
\{c_{k}\}_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}
is a sequence such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}c_{k}=0
then

\lim_{k\to\infty}(-\Delta_{a})^{s}(c_{k}u(x))=0

uniformly for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.

Proof.

Given
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we define the function

f_{k}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a\left(c_{k}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}.

Changing variables we get the following identity

f_{k}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a\left(c_{k}\frac{u(x)-u(x+z)}{|z|^{s}}\right)\frac{dz}{|z|^{n+s}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a\left(c_{k}\frac{u(x)-u(x-z)}{|z|^{s}}\right)\frac{dz}{|z|^{n+s}},

which allow us to write

f_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(a(c_{k}D^{s}u(x,x+z))+(c_{k}D^{s}u(x,x-z))\right)\frac{dz}{|z|^{n+s}}.

Along this proof, for
t>0
, we denote
M(t)=\max\{t^{p-2},t^{q-2}\}
. Also, for
u\in Lip(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we denote by
L
the Lipschitz constant of
u
.

Local Uniform boundedness:

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, we split
f_{n}(x)
as

\displaystyle f_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{|z|>1}a\left(c_{k}D^{s}u(x,x+z)\right)\frac{dz}{|z|^{n+s}}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{|z|<1}a\left(c_{k}D^{s}u(x,x+z)\right)\frac{dz}{|z|^{n+s}}

\displaystyle:=I_{1}(x)+I_{2}(x).

Bound for
I_{1}
. Since
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
|c_{k}|\leq K
for some
K>0
, for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, we get

\displaystyle I_{1}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{|z|>1}a(K2\|u\|_{\infty})\frac{dz}{|z|^{n+s}}=\frac{n\omega_{n}}{2}a(2K\|u\|_{\infty})\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-s-1}\,dr=\frac{n\omega_{n}}{2s}a(2K\|u\|_{\infty}).

Bound for
I_{2}
. Observe that using Lemma
2.4
and that
u\in Lip(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
|z|<1
we get

\displaystyle|a

\displaystyle\left(c_{k}D^{s}u(x,x+z)\right)|\leq M\left(\frac{K|u(x)-u(x-z)|}{|z|^{s}}\right)\frac{K|u(x)-u(x+z)|}{|z|^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq M(KL|z|^{1-s})KL|z|^{1-s}

\displaystyle\leq C|z|^{(p-1)(1-s)}.

Therefore, provided
p>1/(1-s)
, we have that

\displaystyle I_{2}(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{|z|<1}|z|^{(p-1)(1-s)-n-s}\,dz=\frac{Cn\omega_{n}}{2(1-s)}.

Uniform equicontinuity.

For every
\varepsilon>0
we want to see that

|f_{n}(x_{1})-f_{n}(x_{2})|<\varepsilon
whenever
x_{1},x_{2}\in\overline{\Omega}
are such that, for
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)
,
|x_{1}-x_{2}|<\delta
.

For this, we split the difference as

\displaystyle f_{k}(x_{1})-f_{k}(x_{2})=I_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})+I_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})

where

\displaystyle I_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle:=\int_{|x_{1}-y|>1}a(c_{k}D^{s}u(x_{1},y))\frac{dy}{|x_{1}-y|^{n+s}}-\int_{|x_{2}-y|>1}a(c_{k}D^{s}u(x_{2},y))\frac{dy}{|x_{2}-y|^{n+s}},

\displaystyle I_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle:=\int_{|x_{1}-y|\leq 1}a(c_{k}D^{s}u(x_{1},y))\frac{dy}{|x_{1}-y|^{n+s}}-\int_{|x_{2}-y|\leq 1}a(c_{k}D^{s}u(x_{2},y))\frac{dy}{|x_{2}-y|^{n+s}}.

Bound for
I_{1}
. For any
x_{1},x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
|z|>1
, using Lemma
2.4
we have the following:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I(&x_{1},x_{2},z):=a\left(c_{k}\frac{u(x_{1})-u(x_{1}+z)}{|z|^{s}}\right)-a\left(c_{k}\frac{u(x_{2})-u(x_{2}+z)}{|z|^{s}}\right)\\
&\leq M\left(\frac{K|u(x_{1})-u(x_{1}+z)|}{|z|^{s}}+\frac{K|u(x_{1})-u(x_{1}+z)-u(x_{2})+u(x_{2}+z)|}{|z|^{s}}\right)\\
&\quad\times\frac{K|u(x_{1})-u(x_{1}+z)-u(x_{2})+u(x_{2}+z)|}{|z|^{s}}.\end{split}

Since
u\in Lip(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
|z|>1
, we can bound (
4.1
) as follows:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I(x_{1},x_{2},z)&\leq 2KL\dfrac{|x_{1}-x_{2}|}{|z|^{s}}M\left(\frac{6K\|u\|_{\infty}}{|z|^{s}}\right)\leq C\dfrac{|x_{1}-x_{2}|}{|z|^{s(q-1)}},\end{split}

where
C
is a positive constant depending only of
K
,
L
,
\|u\|_{\infty}
,
p
and
q
.

Therefore, we get that

\displaystyle|I_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{|z|>1}\left(a\left(c_{k}\frac{u(x_{1})-u(x_{1}+z)}{|z|^{s}}\right)-a\left(c_{k}\frac{u(x_{2})-u(x_{2}+z)}{|z|^{s}}\right)\right)\frac{dz}{|z|^{n+s}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C|x_{1}-x_{2}|\int_{|z|>1}\frac{dz}{|z|^{n+sq}}=\frac{n\omega_{n}C}{sq}|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

Bound for
I_{2}
. For any
x_{1},x_{2}\in\overline{\Omega}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
|z|\leq 1
, we can bound (
4.1
) as

(4.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I(x_{1},x_{2},z)&\leq M(3KL|z|^{1-s})2KL|x_{1}-x_{2}||z|^{1-s}\\
&\leq C|z|^{(1-s)(p-1)}|x_{1}-x_{2}|,\end{split}

where
C>0
depends only of
p
,
q
,
K
and
L
.

Therefore, by changing variables and using (
4.3
), provided again
p>\frac{1}{1-s}
, we get that

\displaystyle|I_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{|z|\leq 1}\left(a\left(c_{k}\frac{u(x_{1})-u(x_{1}+z)}{|z|^{s}}\right)-a\left(c_{k}\frac{u(x_{2})-u(x_{2}+z)}{|z|^{s}}\right)\right)\frac{dz}{|z|^{n+s}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C|x_{1}-x_{2}|\int_{|z|\leq 1}|z|^{(p-1)(1-s)-n-s}\,dz

\displaystyle=C|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

Then, by Arzelá-Ascoli theorem, we get that, up to a subsequence,
f_{k}
converges uniformly to
0
in
\overline{\Omega}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

As a straightforward consequence, we get the next corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

Assume that
A
is a Young function satisfying (
2.5
) with
p>\max\{\frac{1}{1-s},2\}
. Let
u\in Lip(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and let

be a bounded domain. For any

such that for all

|(-\Delta_{a})^{s}(cu(x))|\leq\varepsilon,

for all

.

Finally, we state the following key relation between weakly and pointwisely bounded
a
-Lapalcian.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open domain. Assume that for
\varepsilon>0
,

(-\Delta_{a})u(x)\leq\varepsilon,

pointwisely for any

. Then,
(-\Delta_{a})u\leq\varepsilon
in the weak sense in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
\varphi\geq 0
. Then, according to the representation formula (
2.7
) we derive the following:

(4.4)

\begin{split}\left\langle(-\Delta_{a})u,\varphi\right\rangle&=\dfrac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}a\left(\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\dfrac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n}}\\
&=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x)\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a\left(\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{dy}{|x-y|^{s+n}}\right]\,dx\\

&\leq\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x)\,dx.\end{split}

This ends the proof of the proposition.

6.

.

\partial\Omega

For a given
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega

\Omega
, there exists
x_{1}\in\Omega
on the normal line to
\partial\Omega
at

and

B_{r_{0}}(x_{1})\subset\Omega,\quad\overline{B}_{r_{0}}(x_{1})\cap\partial\Omega=\{x_{0}\}\quad\text{and }\quad\text{dist}(x_{1},\Omega^{c})=|x_{1}-x_{0}|.

,

and

).

be the distance function

given by

From Corollary
4.2
, given
\beta>0
(to be determined later) there exists

2.3

where

is a constant depending only of

and

.

D\subset\subset B_{1}^{c}(e_{n})\cap\Omega
, there is
C_{D}>1
such that

for any

and

. Using that

is increasing and odd we get

\displaystyle h(x)

\displaystyle\leq-2c\int_{D}a\left(\frac{u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{s+n}}\leq-2c\int_{D}a\left(\frac{u(y)}{C_{D}^{s}}\right)\frac{dy}{C_{D}^{s+n}}

\displaystyle\leq-a(M_{0})\bar{C}_{D}|D|:=-\widetilde{M}_{0},

where

and

.

Now, define

M_{1}:=\inf_{x\in B_{1}(e_{n})\cap B_{r}^{c}(0)}u(x)>0,\qquad M_{2}:=\sup_{x\in B_{1}(e_{n})\cap B_{r}(0)}u(x)>0,

and we take

small enough so that
r\in(0,r_{0})
, and

a(M_{2})<\dfrac{\widetilde{M}_{0}}{\|c\|_{\infty}}.

This can be done since
u
is continuous in
\overline{\Omega}
and
u=0
on
\partial\Omega
. Observe that this bound is uniform and independent of
r
, although
r
depends on the boundary point.
Next, choose

0<\beta\leq\widetilde{M}_{0}-a(M_{2})\|c\|_{\infty}

which leads to (in the weak sense)

(6.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(-\Delta_{a})^{s}\underline{u}&\leq\beta-\widetilde{M}_{0}\leq-a(M_{2})\|c\|_{\infty}\\
&\leq ca(u)\leq(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u\qquad\text{ in }B_{1}(e_{n})\cap B_{r}(0).\end{split}

Then, for
x\in B_{1}^{c}(e_{n})
we have that

\underline{u}(x)=u(x)\chi_{D}(x)\leq u(x)

and for
x\in B_{1}(e_{n})\setminus B_{r}(0)
we have, taking
\lambda
small enough, that

\underline{u}(x)=\lambda d(x)\leq\lambda\leq u(x).

In sum, we have obtained from the previous expression and (
6.2
) that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-\Delta_{a})^{s}\underline{u}\leq(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u&\quad\text{ weakly in }B_{1}(e_{n})\cap B_{r}(0),\\
\underline{u}(x)\leq u(x)&\quad\text{ in }(B_{1}(e_{n})\cap B_{r}(0))^{c}.\end{cases}

Using the comparison principle it gives that

(6.3)

\underline{u}(x)\leq u(x)\quad\text{ in }B_{1}(e_{n})\cap B_{r}(0).

By definition of
d(x)
, for any
t\in(0,1)

(6.4)

d(te_{n})=\delta(te_{n})

where
\delta(x)=\mathop{\text{dist}}(x,\Omega^{c})
, and since
te_{n}\notin D

(6.5)

\underline{u}(te_{n})=\beta d(te_{n}),

this gives, from (
6.4
), (
6.5
) and (
6.3
), that

\frac{u(te_{n})}{\delta(te_{n})}=\frac{u(te_{n})}{d(te_{n})}=\frac{u(te_{n})}{\beta^{-1}\underline{u}(te_{n})}\geq\beta>0

which completes the proof.
∎

7.
Hopf’s boundary lemma for sign-changing potentials

In this section, we will provide a Hopf’s boundary lemma for solutions of

(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u\geq c(x)a(u)\quad\text{ in }\Omega

where now no sign condition is impose to
c
. We will mainly follow the plan of
[
7
]
and its nonlinear counterpart
[
20
, Theorem 3.1]
, with the necessary changes due to the lack of homogeneity of the operator. Hence, we will provide all the details. We also deduce some interesting consequences.

Following
[
7
]
, we consider the next class of functions. Given a point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
where the interior ball condition holds, the class
\mathcal{Z}_{x_{0}}
consists of functions
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
u
is continuous in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
|u|>0
in
B_{r}(x_{r})
for all sufficiently small
r
,
u(x_{0})=0
and the following growth condition is true

(7.1)

\limsup_{r\to 0}\Phi(r)=+\infty

where

(7.2)

\Phi(r):=\dfrac{(\inf_{B_{r/2}(x_{r})}|u|)^{p-1}}{r^{ps}},

where we recall that
p
satisfies

p\leq\dfrac{ta(t)}{A(t)},\quad\text{for all }t>0.

We state now the main theorem of this section.
We are now in position to prove Theorem
1.3

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

For the sake of simplicity we split the proof in four stages.

Step 1
: Consider the distance function for the unit ball

d(x)=\text{dist}(x,B_{1}^{c})

and recall, for further reference, that any distance function for bounded domains satisfies Proposition
4.1
and Proposition
4.3
. Also,
d
satisfies the lower bound

(7.3)

d(x)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}(1-|x|^{2}).

We will use this estimate in what follows.

Step 2
: In order to localize the argument near the boundary point
x_{0}
, we will build appropriate functions to compare with
u
.

Take
\rho\in(0,1/2)
and
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{1-2\rho})
nonnegative,
\eta\leq 1
, with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\eta=1
. Then, for
x\in B_{1}\setminus\overline{B_{1-\rho}}
, we have

(7.4)

\begin{split}(-\Delta_{a})^{s}\eta(x)&=\text{p.v.}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a\left(\dfrac{\eta(x)-\eta(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}=-\text{p.v.}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a\left(\dfrac{\eta(z+x)}{|z|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{dz}{|z|^{n+s}}\\
&=-\text{p.v.}\,\int_{B_{1-2\rho}(-x)}a\left(\dfrac{\eta(z+x)}{|z|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{dz}{|z|^{n+s}}.\end{split}

Since
z\in B_{1-2\rho}(-x)
, there holds

|z|\leq|z+x|+|x|\leq 2-2\rho,

from where, (
7.4
) gives that for any
x\in B_{1}\setminus\overline{B_{1-\rho}}

(7.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(-\Delta_{g})^{s}\eta(x)&\leq-\text{p.v.}\,\int_{B_{1-2\rho}(-x)}a\left(\dfrac{\eta(z+x)}{(2(1-\rho))^{s}}\right)\frac{dz}{(2(1-\rho))^{n+s}}\\
&\leq-\left(\dfrac{1}{2(1-\rho)}\right)^{sp+n}\int_{B_{1-2\rho}}a(\eta(n(z)))\,dz=-C\left(\dfrac{1}{2(1-\rho)}\right)^{sp+n}.\end{split}

Consider the balls
B_{r}(x_{r})
and points
x_{r}
from the interior ball condition for
\Omega
at
x_{0}
. For each
r>0
small enough, let for any
x\in B_{r}(x_{r})

\alpha_{r}:=\dfrac{1}{C_{r}}\inf_{B_{r(1-\rho)}(x_{r})}u,

and

(7.6)

\psi_{r}(x)=\dfrac{\alpha_{r}}{2}d\left(\dfrac{x-x_{r}}{r}\right)+\dfrac{C_{r}\alpha_{r}}{2}\eta\left(\dfrac{x-x_{r}}{r}\right),

where the constant
C_{r}>0
is chosen later. Moreover, the function

\eta\left(\dfrac{x-x_{r}}{r}\right)

has support in
B_{r(1-2\rho)}(x_{r})
. Therefore, for
x\in B_{r}(x_{r})\setminus\overline{B_{r(1-\rho)}(x_{r})}
, we obtain in the weak sense

(7.7)

(-\Delta_{a})^{s}\psi_{r}(x)=(-\Delta_{a})^{s}\left(\dfrac{\alpha_{r}}{2}d\left(\dfrac{\cdot-x_{r}}{r}\right)\right)(x)+h(x),

and letting
y^{\prime}=(y-x_{r})/r
,
x^{\prime}=(x-x_{r})/r
, we deduce

(7.8)

\begin{split}h(x)&\leq\dfrac{2c_{1}}{r^{s}}\int_{B_{1-2\rho}}a\left(\dfrac{C_{r}\alpha_{r}}{2r^{s}}\dfrac{\eta(x^{\prime})-\eta(y^{\prime})}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{dy^{\prime}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{s+n}}\\
&=\dfrac{2c_{1}}{r^{s}}\int_{B_{1-2\rho}}a\left(\dfrac{C_{r}\alpha_{r}}{2r^{s}}\dfrac{-\eta(y^{\prime})}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{dy^{\prime}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{s+n}}\\
&\leq-2c_{1}\Phi(r)\int_{B_{1-2\rho}}a\left(\dfrac{\eta(y^{\prime})}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{dy^{\prime}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{s+n}}=-c\Phi(r),\end{split}

where

c=2c_{1}\int_{B_{1-2\rho}}a\left(\dfrac{\eta(y^{\prime})}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{dy^{\prime}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{s+n}}>0,

and
\Phi
is given by (
7.2
). Similarly, we obtain that

(-\Delta_{a})^{s}\left(\dfrac{\alpha_{r}}{2}d\left(\dfrac{\cdot-x_{r}}{r}\right)\right)=\dfrac{1}{r^{s}}(-\Delta_{a})^{s}\left(\dfrac{\alpha_{r}}{2r^{s}}d\right).

Now, we choose the constant
C_{r}>1
as follows. By Propositions
4.1
and
4.3
, there is
\lambda_{0}>0
such that for all
\lambda<\lambda_{0}
there holds

(7.9)

|(-\Delta_{a})^{s}\left(\lambda d\right)|<\dfrac{c}{2}\left(\dfrac{\inf_{B_{r/2}(x_{r})}|u|}{r^{s}}\right)^{p-1}.

Choose
C_{r}>1
large enough so that

\dfrac{1}{C_{r}}\dfrac{\inf_{B_{r/2}(x_{r})}|u|}{r^{s}}=\dfrac{\alpha_{r}}{r^{s}}<\lambda_{0}.

Therefore, recalling the definition of
\psi_{r}
(
7.6
) and combining (
7.7
), (
7.8
) and (
7.9
) the function
\psi_{r}
verifies:

(7.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\begin{cases}(-\Delta_{a})^{s}\psi_{r}\leq-\dfrac{c}{2}\Phi(r)&\text{weakly in }B_{r}(x_{r})\setminus\overline{B_{r(1-\rho)}(x_{r})},\\
\psi_{r}\leq\alpha_{r}C_{r}&\text{ in }B_{r}(x_{r}),\\
\psi_{r}=0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(x_{r}),\\
\psi_{r}\geq\dfrac{\alpha_{r}}{4r^{2}}(r^{2}-|x-x_{r}|^{2})&\text{ in }B_{r}(x_{r}).\end{cases}\end{split}

Step 3
: The function
w:=\psi_{r}-u^{-}
satisfies that
u\geq w
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

To prove this assertion we will use comparison. Observe first that
w\leq u
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(x_{r})
, moreover, in
B_{r(1-\rho)}(x_{r})
we have that

w=\psi_{r}\leq\alpha_{r}C_{r}\leq\inf_{B_{r(1-\rho)}(x_{r})}u\leq u.

Also, by (
7.10
),

(-\Delta_{a})^{s}w\leq-\dfrac{c}{2}\Phi(r)+h,

weakly in
B_{r}(x_{r})\setminus\overline{B_{r(1-\rho)}(x_{r})}
, where the function
h
is given by

\begin{split}h(x)&=2\int_{supp\,\,u^{-}}\left[a\left(\dfrac{\psi_{r}(x)-\psi_{r}(y)+u^{-}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)-a\left(\dfrac{\psi_{r}(x)-\psi_{r}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\right]\dfrac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\\
&=2\int_{supp\,\,u^{-}}\left[a\left(\dfrac{\psi_{r}(x)+u^{-}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)-g_{p}\left(\dfrac{\psi_{r}(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\right]\dfrac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\leq C^{*}\end{split}

since
\psi_{r}
and
u^{-}
are bounded and
|x-y|\geq\delta>0
, for some
\delta
. Hence, weakly in
B_{r}(x_{r})\setminus\overline{B_{r(1-\rho)}(x_{r})}
, we obtain

(-\Delta_{a})^{s}w\leq C^{*}-\dfrac{c}{2}\Phi(r)\leq-\|c^{-}a(u^{+})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq ca(u)\leq(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u,\text{ for r small enough,}

where we have used (
7.1
). By the comparison principle, it follows
u\geq w
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Step 3
: Final argument: to complete the proof, we argue as in
[
7
]
. For
\beta\in(0,\tfrac{\pi}{2})
, let

(7.11)

\mathcal{C}_{\beta}:=\left\{x\in\Omega:\dfrac{x-x_{0}}{|x-x_{0}|}\cdot\eta>c_{\beta}\right\},

where
\eta
is the inward normal vector joining
x_{0}
with the center of the interior ball, and define the constant
c_{\beta}:=\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\beta\right)>0
. Take any sequence
x_{k}\in\mathcal{C}_{\beta}
such that
x_{k}\to x_{0}
. Then,

\begin{split}|x_{k}-x_{r}|^{2}&=|x_{k}-x_{0}-r\eta|^{2}=|x_{k}-x_{0}|^{2}+r^{2}-2r(x_{k}-x_{0})\cdot\eta\\
&\leq r^{2}-|x_{k}-x_{0}|(2c_{\beta}r-|x_{k}-x_{0}|)<r^{2},\end{split}

for
k
large enough. Moreover, since

|x_{k}-x_{r}|\geq|x_{r}-x_{0}|+|x_{k}-x_{0}|=r-|x_{k}-x_{0}|>r(1-\rho)

for
k
large, then
x_{k}\in B_{r}(x_{r})\setminus\overline{B_{r(1-\rho)}(x_{r})}
. Next,

\begin{split}u(x_{k})&\geq w(x_{k})=\psi_{r}(x_{k})\geq\frac{\alpha_{r}}{r^{2}4}(r^{2}-|x_{k}-x_{r}|^{2})_{+}\\
&=\frac{\alpha_{r}}{4r^{2}}(r^{2}-|x_{k}-x_{0}-r\eta|^{2})_{+}\\
&=\frac{\alpha_{r}}{4r^{2}}(2(x_{k}-x_{0})\cdot\eta-|x_{k}-x_{0}|^{2})_{+}\geq\frac{\alpha_{r}}{4r^{2}}(2c_{\beta}r|x_{k}-x_{0}|-|x_{k}-x_{0}|^{2})_{+}.\end{split}

Therefore,

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\dfrac{u(x_{k})-u(x_{0})}{|x_{k}-x_{0}|}\geq\frac{\alpha_{r}}{4r^{2}}\liminf_{k\to\infty}(2c_{\beta}r-|x_{k}-x_{0}|)_{+}=\dfrac{\alpha_{r}c_{\beta}}{2r^{2}}.

This ends the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 7.1
.

Observe that although the equation is nonlocal, in the above proof we only used that
u
is a supersolution near the boundary of
\Omega
.

By the interior sphere condition and
[
17
]
, for any
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, there exist
x_{1}\in\Omega
and
\rho>0
such that

B_{\rho}(x_{1})\subset\Omega,\quad d(x)=|x-x_{0}|,

for all
x\in\Omega
of the form

x=tx_{1}+(1-t)x_{0},\quad t\in[0,1].

Thus, there is a sequence
x_{n}\in\Omega
such that

\delta(x_{n})=\text{dist}\,(x_{n},\partial\Omega)=|x_{n}-x_{0}|\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Hence, if
u/\delta
can be extended to a continuous function to a neighbourhood of
\partial\Omega
, Theorem
1.3
implies that

\dfrac{u(x_{0})}{\delta(x_{0})}=\lim_{\overline{\Omega}\ni x\to x_{0}}\dfrac{u(x)-u(x_{0})}{\delta(x)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{u(x_{n})-u(x_{0})}{\delta(x_{n})}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{u(x_{n})-u(x_{0})}{|x_{n}-x|}>0.

We establish another direct consequence. Since the function
a
is odd, by changing
u
by
-u
in Theorem
1.3
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and bounded and
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
. Assume that
\Omega
satisfies the interior ball condition at
x_{0}
.
Let
u
such that
-u\in\mathcal{Z}_{x_{0}}
,
u^{+}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and

(-\Delta_{a})^{s}u\leq c(x)a(u)\quad\text{weakly in }\Omega

where
c\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
with
c^{-}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Further, suppose that there is
R>0
such that
u\leq 0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
,
u<0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega
. Then, for every
\beta\in(0,\pi/2)
, the following strict inequality holds

(7.12)

\limsup_{x\in\Omega,x\to x_{0}}\dfrac{u(x)-u(x_{0})}{|x-x_{0}|}<0,

whenever the angle between
x-x_{0}
and the vector joining
x_{0}
and the center of the interior ball is smaller than
\pi/2-\beta
.

Reasoning as before, we may also conclude, under the assumptions of Corollary
7.2
and the hypothesis that
u/\delta\in C(\overline{\Omega})
, that

\dfrac{u(x_{0})}{\delta(x_{0})}<0.

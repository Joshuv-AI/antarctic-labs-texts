Machine learning-driven optimization of performance and emissions in a butanol/diesel CI engine
Authors: K Siva Prasad, T R Vijaybabu, G Kiran Kumar, S Srinivasa Rao, V R K Raju, Sunnam Nagaraju, A Bhowmik, Ahmed Jemal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA hybrid modeling framework integrating experiments, CONVERGE CFD, and Optuna-optimized ensemble machine learning is developed to predict and optimize performance and emission characteristics of a DI-CI engine fueled with butanol/diesel blends. Machine learning was employed to model and predict key combustion and emission characteristics under varying engine operating conditions. This work presents a numerical and modeling examination of a DI-CI engine’s performance and emission characteristics. CONVERGE CFD software was used to do the numerical analysis. Four distinct engine parameters were examined in this study: Start of Injection (SOI) (17 to 29° CA bTDC), Compression Ratio (CR) (14.5 to 19.5), Exhaust Gas Recirculation (EGR) (0 to 30%), and Fuel Injection Pressure (FIP) (200 to 280 bar). The Optuna-optimized ensemble Meta Model achieved the most accurate predictions, exhibiting the lowest RMSE and highest R² values for ISFC and NOₓ, along with reasonable accuracy for Soot estimation. The ensemble Meta Model achieved the best performance with R² values of 0.98 (ISFC) and 0.97 (NOx) and RMSE below 5%, demonstrating strong generalization.Keywords: Machine learning, Ensemble learning, CI engine optimization, Hybrid modeling, CONVERGE CFDSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In recent decades, significant global efforts have been carried out to decline use of fossil fuels in Compression Ignition (CI) engines. The usage of diesel fuel in CI engine, it leads to fast depletion of fossil fuels, escalating the oil price, and increasing the pollution in environment1–3. In order to overcome this problem, the researchers have been working to find most appropriate renewable fuels to replace diesel fuel. Among, different renewable alternative fuels, alcohol based fuels are considered highly suitable as substitutes or blend component in diesel fuel due to their properties of few fuels are closer to diesel properties4–7. The alcohol based fuel has (ethanol, methanol, butanol and pentanol) lower viscosity than the diesel fuel, which assists easier injection into the combustion chamber and enriches fuel atomization8,9. Among the alcohol, lower chain alcohol (methanol and ethanol) are extensively used as alternative fuel in CI engine. Nevertheless, the lower chain alcohol have few inherent drawbacks, that makes them to less attractive as alternative fuel instead of diesel6,10. Higher chain alcohol, i.e., butanol, can be produced from biomass and are considered highly promising alternative fuels for (CI) engines due to their higher cetane number and higher energy density compared to lower alcohol based fuels.

Recent theoretical investigations have also focused on advanced combustion enhancement and emission reduction strategies in alternative-fuel CI engines operating under optimized combustion modes.

Recently, many studies have been conducted on the CI engine addition of butanol content in diesel fuel as a blended fuel and emission reduction strategies in alternative-fuel CI engines operating under optimized combustion modes11–14.

Rakopoulos et al.15 inspected the effect of butanol fraction (8–16% by volume) in diesel fuel to understand the characteristics of 6-cylinder CI engine. The experimental outcomes exhibited that, with the addition of butanol content in diesel fuel, net heat release rate, in-cylinder pressure, smoke, and NOx emission decreased, whereas Brake thermal efficiency (BTE), unburned hydrocarbons (UBHC) slightly increased compared to neat diesel fuel. Similar outcomes were observed by Rakopoulos16, Karabektas and Hosoz17, Dogan18, Mohmed Nour et al.19 and Swiale20. Satsangi, kattela et al.21 and Tiwari22 examined the impact of butanol content (varied from 0% to 20%) on the characteristics of a CI engine. The test results indicated that butanol blends produced higher vibrations, rate of pressure rise, peak in-cylinder pressure and combustion noise compared to diesel fuel. From the test results observed that with addition of butanol content in blends, showed grater reduction in emission with a slight penalty on the performance. Campos et al.23 explored the characteristics of single cylinder CI engine by adding butanol content from 10% to 30% (by volume) in blends without changing the engine configuration. They observed that the BTE of the engine improved with addition of butanol content in blends. It was concluded that the engine could operate successfully with a fuel blend of up to 30%, without modification of engine design.

In another study, Kattela et al.24 examined the influence of adding butanol fraction (10–40%-by volume) to diesel fuel on the single cylinder CI engine. The test findings showed that blends with higher butanol content had lower emissions of CO, SOx, and NOx.

Chen et al.25 investigated the influence of butanol fraction from 10% to 40% (by volume) in the blends on the characteristics of a high-speed CI engine. The test outcomes exhibited that in-cylinder pressure slightly increased for butanol blends related to diesel fuel. NOx emission increased whereas soot and CO emission decreased for butanol fuel as related to diesel fuel. Chen et al.26 studied the impact of 40% of butanol content in diesel fuel, along with different EGR rates, on the HD-CI engine characteristics. They found that 40% blend resulted in longer ignition delay, higher combustion pressure and faster burning rate compared to diesel fuel. They concluded that combining a medium fraction of EGR with the 40% butanol blend could produce significantly lower NOx and soot emissions than using diesel fuel alone.

Nayyer et al.27 Investigated a work to identifying the better butanol ratio and operating parameters to reduce the emissions. The authors are considered four parameters such as blending ratio, injection pressure (180–220 bar), compression ratio (16.5–19.5), injection timing (19–25O CA bTDC) and engine load (12–24 Nm), to analysis their effects on the characteristics of a CI engine. The optimization process was caThe test results showed that a 20% of butanol content, fuel injection pressure 210 bar, compression ratio of 19.5, and fuel injection timing of 230 CA bTDC were to be best operating conditions, as resulted in enhanced performance and inferior emission as compared to standard configuration operation. Similar trends were observed in other studies28.

Saravanan et al.29 investigated the influence of changing three operating parameters, viz., SOI (19–25° bTDC), FIP (200–240 bar), and EGR rate (10–30%) on the characteristics of diesel engine operated with 60% diesel/40% of butanol blend using experimental and statistical analysis (RSM method). Response surface methodology (RSM) was used to model the measured responses such as smoke opacity, NOx, BSFC and BTE. They establish that FIP of 240 bar, SOI of 23°CA bTDC, less than 30% of EGR rate were optimum parameters for the given engine configuration.

Prabhahar et al. investigated the influence of Algae Oil Methyl Ester biodiesel blends, CuO nanoparticles, and YSZ thermal barrier coated pistons on CI engine performance and emissions using RSM-based optimization. The study reported better BTE and reduced BSFC, CO, HC, and smoke emissions with CuO nanoparticle addition and thermal barrier coatings, while NOx emissions slightly increased due to higher combustion temperatures.

Shaisundaram et al.30 examined the performance and emission characteristics of a zirconia-coated diesel engine fueled with Momordica seed oil biodiesel blended with CeO₂ nanoparticles using ML models. This study reported that BTE was improved and CO, HC, NOx, and smoke emissions were reduced. The Random Forest and Gradient Boosting models demonstrated high prediction accuracy for engine performance and emission characteristics.

Elumalai et al.31 examined the performance characteristics of a tri-fuel RCCI engine fueled with diesel, hydrogen, and Andropogon narudus biofuel using ANN-based optimization techniques. The study reported that the BD80H20 (80% AN20-diesel blend + 20% hydrogen fuel) blend achieved higher BTE with reduced HC, CO, and smoke emissions, while the ANN model demonstrated high prediction accuracy for engine performance and emission parameters.

Anusha32 studied the performance of dual-RCCI engine with with sapota oil methyl ester (SOME) blended with hexanol, butanol, and diesel. The RSM methodology was used for different blends for enhancing the engine and emission performance. Their results showed that with 10% butanol addition, the BTE improved by about 0.30%, while hexanol blends showed as much as a 0.70% improvement over the reference B20 blend. Finally concluded that B20 + BU10 + D70 and B20 + HEX10 + D70 blends are a feasible way towards sustainable fuel operation by prominent a balance between performance improvement and emission regulation.

The research concludes that from the literature, it was observed that numerous studies have been investigated on the characteristics of CI engine fueled with different butanol/diesel blends, and also by varying fuel injection pressure (FIP), start of injection (SOI), compression ratio (CR) and exhaust gas recirculation (EGR). Current studies have also emphasized intelligent coordinated control and optimization strategies for advanced energy systems, including micro gas turbine-based DC power generation and hybrid engine-grid-load-storage architectures33. These studies highlight the growing importance of integrated data-driven and control-oriented methodologies for improving energy efficiency, emission decrease, and system reliability. However past studies have applied RSM, ANN, and standalone ML models for CI engine optimization, these approaches are often limited by model bias, manual hyper parameter tuning, and restricted datasets.

The current study addresses these limitations by proposing a hybrid CFD–ML–optimization framework incorporating a stacking-based ensemble Meta Model and automated hyper parameter tuning using Optuna. This integrated approach enhances predictive accuracy, robustness, and generalization capability, offering a significant advancement over existing hybrid methodologies.

Machine learning implementation

Machine learning (ML) techniques have emerged as powerful tools for capturing complex nonlinear interactions among combustion parameters and emission characteristics. Advanced intelligent control, deep learning and fault-tolerant methodologies have also been extensively applied in complex nonlinear engineering systems to progress robustness, stability, and predictive reliability under varying operating conditions38–40. In the present study, six algorithms namely Decision Tree (DT), AdaBoost (AB), Random Forest (RF), Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LGBM), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGB), and the Ensemble Meta Model (MM) were evaluated to predict ISFC, NOx, and Soot. The dataset spans a wide range of operating conditions, including Compression Ratio (CR), Start of Injection (SOI), Exhaust Gas Recirculation (EGR), Fuel Injection Pressure (FIP), and butanol blend ratio. The dataset was randomly divided into training (80%) and testing (20%) subsets to ensure unbiased model evaluation.

Hyperparameter tuning for all ML models was conducted using the Optuna optimization framework, using 500 independent trials to ensure a comprehensive exploration of the parameter space and improve model generalization. The predictive performance of each optimized model was subsequently assessed using a test dataset comprising 20% of the total samples. Performance evaluation was carried out using the root mean square error (RMSE) and the coefficient of determination (R²). The subsequent sections provide a detailed comparative analysis of model accuracy, inter-parameter relationships, and the overall predictive robustness for the three combustion-related output variables. Current deep learning-based optimization frameworks have also established significant improvements in prediction accuracy and fault diagnosis performance in complex engine systems through advanced parameter optimization techniques41,42.

Hyperparameter optimization for all ML models was performed using the Optuna framework with 500 trials, employing the Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) sampler. The search space for each model was carefully defined to cover a wide range of possible configurations whereas avoiding over-complexity. The optimal Hyperparameter were selected based on the minimization of RMSE on the validation dataset. Table 4 shows the Hyperparameter search space and optimal values.

Results and discussion

Model performance evaluation for ISFC, NOx, and soot prediction

The comparative performance of different ML models in predicting ISFC, NOx, and Soot as shown in Fig. 4a–c.The model evaluation was carried out using two different performance indicator parameters such as the root mean square error (RMSE) and the coefficient of determination (R²). Higher R² values indicate a stronger relation between predicated and experimental results, whereas lower RSME value indicates poor relation between them for all the three cases. From the figure observed that the model performance was significantly improved from DT to MM, in the sequence of AB, RF, LGBM, and XGB.

Comparison of R² and RMSE values for different machine learning models in predicting (a) ISFC (g/kWh), (b) NOx (g/kWh), and (c) soot (g/kWh).

Figure 4a illustrate the model prediction for ISFC for different ML Models. From the figure it is evident that DT model exhibited the weakest performance than the other models, as indicated by its lowest R2 and highest RMSE. The model prediction accuracy is marginally improved by inclusion of boosting in AB, whereas RF further enhances the results by averaging multiple trees and decreasing variance. The gradient boosting algorithm, mainly LGBM and XGB, demonstrate substantial improvement in both R2 and RSME. These improvements highlight their advanced capability to model complex nonlinear relationships within the dataset. The MM model provides the most robust overall performance, effectively integrating the advantages of earlier models to accomplish the minimum RMSE and maximum R2. Though, it is notable that the RMSE values attained during testing remain comparatively high for all models, aligning closely with their respective training errors. These demonstrations that the ISFC parameter exhibitions stronger variability and sensitivity to multiple coupled factors, making it more difficult to generalize accurately even with advanced ensemble learning techniques.

Figure 4b shows the variation of R² and RMSE for different machine learning models for NOx. From the figure it is observed that DT model exhibited higher test RSME, which progressively drops through RF, AB, LGBM, and XGB. The ensemble-based models display a clear development in R² values, indicating improved generalization capability across the dataset. Among them, the MM model constantly delivers the maximum predictive accuracy, characterized by its particularly low RMSE and an R² approaching unity. This superior performance highlights its strong capability to capture and represent the complex nonlinear interactions between the input variables and the corresponding outputs.

In the case of Soot [Fig. 4c], the variations among models are less noticeable. Whereas DT and RF yield moderate accuracy, following models such as AB, XGB, and LGBM produce a noticeable but steady improvement. MM marginally outperforms the others, however the margin of improvement remains lesser compared to ISFC and NOx. This suggests that the prediction of Soot is inherently more challenging owing to the effect of secondary factors and measurement variability.

Overall, the sequence of performance improvement across DT, AB, RF, LGBM, XGB, and MM clearly demonstrates the growing capability of ensemble and hybrid models to attain well predictive precision. Among all, MM stands out as the most reliable framework, offering improved accuracy for ISFC and NOx predictions and moderate enhancement in SOOT estimation.

Correlation analysis ISFC, NOx, and soot prediction by meta model

From previous section, across six machine learning algorithms Meta Model achieved superior performance across all three targets with relatively better R² values, outperforming individual models by 5–12%. Correlation analysis from MM predictions (refer Fig. 5) reveals the following relationships.

Correlation heatmap obtained through MM model for (a) ISFC, (b) NOx, and (c) Soot.

Compression Ratio (CR) emerged as the influence parameter. Strong negative correlations with ISFC (−0.71) and soot (−0.71) reflect improved combustion efficiency and oxidation completeness at elevated pressures. The moderate positive correlation with NOX (0.48) captures the thermal trade-off: greater peak temperatures help thermal NOx whereas reducing incomplete combustion products.

A deeper physical interpretation of the correlation results tells that CR plays a dominant role in influencing combustion behavior. Increasing CR improves in-cylinder pressure and temperature, leading to improved fuel atomization, faster evaporation, and more complete combustion. As a result, ISFC reductions owing to higher thermal efficiency, while soot formation is reduced because of improved oxidation of soot precursors in high-temperature environments.

Start of Injection (SOI) timing selectively influences NOx (0.55), making it effective for emission fine-tuning without expressively affecting soot (−0.27 to −0.32) or fuel consumption (−0.32). The fuel blend demonstrations equal reduction effects on both NOx and soot (−0.29), with a marginal fuel economy penalty (0.29), indicating oxygenated components or enhanced cetane number characteristics.

Exhaust Gas Recirculation (EGR) proposals targeted NOx suppression (−0.46) with minimal efficiency influence (0.10 to 0.16), whereas Fuel Injection Pressure (FIP) demonstrations weak correlations across all yields (−0.12 to 0.18), suggesting operation within an optimized range.

The minimal inter-correlations observed among the input variables indicate that the experimental factors are largely independent of one another. The inverse NOx-soot relationship quantifies the fundamental diesel combustion constraint, with MM efficiently capturing these competing objectives. Results show CR optimization as the primary calibration strategy, with SOI and EGR providing secondary control for NOx management.

Conclusions

This investigation effectively combined experimental, numerical, and machine learning approaches to optimize the performance of a DI-CI engine fueled with butanol/diesel blends. The study shows that the integration of ensemble learning with Optuna-based optimization considerably increases the predictive robustness of combustion models. The ensemble Meta Model efficiently captured nonlinear dependencies among engine parameters, attaining marked accuracy improvements in ISFC and NOx predictions whereas maintaining acceptable performance for Soot. These fallouts found the framework as a reliable tool for performance–emission trade-off assessment and future combustion optimization studies.

Performance and Emission Trends:

Increasing CR and advancing SOI enhanced the combustion efficiency and reduced the ISFC but resulted in greater NOx emissions. The application of moderate EGR remarkably reduced NOx with a minimal influence on fuel efficiency, while the effect of FIP remained less significant within the tested range.

The application of moderate EGR remarkably reduced NOx with a minimal influence on fuel efficiency, while the effect of FIP remained less significant within the tested range.

Machine Learning Prediction Accuracy:

Amongst the six evaluated algorithms, the Optuna-optimized Ensemble MM model delivered the highest predictive capability, attaining R² values of 0.98 for ISFC, 0.93 for soot, and, 0.97 for NOx with RMSE values below 5%. This supports the robustness and generalization ability of the ensemble learning framework.

Correlation analysis indicated that CR was the most effective parameter affecting performance and emissions, followed by SOI and EGR. The trade-off between NOx and soot formation was well captured by the ML model, indicating its potential for multi-objective optimization.

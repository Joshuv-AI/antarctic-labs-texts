Investigating behavioral intentions toward DeepSeek adoption using a moderated-mediation analysis with PLS-SEM and fsQCA
Authors: Han Gao, Jai Kumar, Joti Kumari, Jinyan Huang, Dheeraj Kumar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Despite the growing integration of AI in education, students often lack awareness of specialized tools, which limits their adoption and effective use. Using partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) and Fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA), this study aimed to investigate behavioral intentions toward DeepSeek adoption among university students. The conceptual framework of the research combines key factors from the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT). A survey using a five-point Likert scale was administered to students from Pakistani universities. The study’s findings show that DeepSeek awareness has a significant impact on DeepSeek adoption, which is moderated by perceived ease of use and mediated by facilitating conditions, social influence, and performance expectancy. fsQCA further reveals configurations of factors that jointly lead to high adoption, highlighting multiple pathways rather than single causal effects. This study intends to benefit companies, academic institutions, and the global community by providing insight into how students perceive the DeepSeek services in an educational setting. The study contributes theoretically by integrating awareness into the UTAUT framework and applying fsQCA, providing a novel perspective on AI adoption in education. Finally, the conclusions of this study will help AI developers improve their product and service delivery, as well as regulators manage the usage of AI-powered bots.

Introduction

The integration of artificial intelligence (AI) in education has emerged as a transformative development1,2, with AI-powered conversational agents representing a significant technological advancement in pedagogical approaches3,4. AI technologies have evolved from early conceptual foundations in the 1950s5,6, to today’s advanced generative systems powered by transformer architectures7,8. Modern systems such as DeepSeek, built on advanced transformer architectures, demonstrate remarkable capabilities in natural language processing and educational applications9,10. These AI tools have transitioned from simple programmed responses to complex systems capable of contextual understanding, adaptive learning support, and personalized educational assistance11.

From an educational viewpoint, AI-powered tools like DeepSeek propose numerous abilities to offer benefits in countless areas of the educational sector, extensively documented in the recent literature12,13. Research has widely reported their ability to provide personalized learning experiences1,14, and autonomous administrative tasks15,16, increasing students’ engagement through interactive interfaces17,18. Research by Lai and Tu (2024) and Tu and Hwang (2024)19,20 reflects distinct usage patterns among university students, with high performers utilizing these tools to better understand the content while others focus on assignment completion. The benefits include research support21,22, 24/7 learning availability23,24, and accessibility enhancements13,25. However, the accuracy of the information, its ethical ramifications, and possible over-reliance have all raised serious concerns2,26.

Multiple research studies investigate students’ motivation towards using AI in learning investigations27,28. Performance expectancy together with social influence, facilitating conditions and perceived usefulness have been identified as key factors for adoption of AI by Du & Lv (2024) and Wang et al. (2021)28,29. Multiple researchers have investigated these factors under the existing technology acceptance model (TAM) and UTAUT frameworks because these models enable us to anticipate technology acceptance behaviors30,31.

In spite of the existing body of research, there are still gaps in our comprehension of the adoption of AI tools, especially in academic settings. Emerging AI platforms like DeepSeek are still under-researched in the Pakistani context relative to widely studied systems like ChatGPT32,33 and awareness-based adoption systems are not well studied. The majority of current studies presuppose that the users are already familiarized with the AI tools, and they do not consider the initial stage of cognitive awareness formation that is essential to the adoption of newly introduced technologies34. This level of awareness is especially critical in Pakistan where the exposure to newly developed AI applications like DeepSeek is minimal. The Pakistani higher education sector represents a unique socio-cultural environment where collectivist norms and institutional structures may reshape adoption dynamics; however empirical evidence from this context remains limited35. Although many studies exist regarding the adoption of AI in different contexts16,23, we still face several vital limitations. Firstly, there is limited research on newer AI-powered tools such as DeepSeek, which may exhibit different adoption patterns for different functionalities and user interface. Secondly, the Pakistani higher education sector remains under-researched from an empirical perspective despite its unique characteristics, including evolving technological infrastructure, varying digital literacy levels, and distinct cultural influences on technology acceptance36. Thirdly, current literature has primarily employed symmetric analytical approaches (e.g., PLS-SEM), potentially overlooking complex, non-linear adoption pathways that alternative methods like fsQCA could reveal. To address these gaps, this study investigates how awareness, institutional support, social influence, and perceived usability jointly shape DeepSeek adoption using a dual-method framework that combines PLS-SEM and fsQCA.

RQ1: How does DeepSeek awareness shape facilitating conditions, social influence, and performance expectancy in higher education?

RQ2: Through what mediating and moderating mechanisms does awareness translate into adoption intentions?

RQ3: What alternative configurational pathways lead to DeepSeek adoption according to fsQCA?

The study addresses these gaps by developing an integrated framework to explain behavioral intentions toward DeepSeek adoption among Pakistani university students. Building on UTAUT, the model conceptualizes adoption intention as the dependent variable and DeepSeek awareness as the independent variable, with performance expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions serving as mediators and perceived ease of use acting as a moderator. Methodologically, the study adopts a mixed analytical strategy that combines PLS-SEM for symmetric path testing with fsQCA to uncover configurational and nonlinear adoption pathways37. This research advances the literature by extending UTAUT to emerging AI adoption through the inclusion of awareness as a pre-adoption cognitive stage, illustrating how perceived ease of use reshapes awareness-driven adoption mechanisms, and integrating symmetric and asymmetric analytical approaches to reveal multiple adoption logics. Practically, the findings offer guidance for educational institutions, emphasizing how institutional readiness, usability design, and social dynamics collectively influence successful AI integration in academic environments.

Literature review and hypotheses development

Unified theory of acceptance and use of technology (UTAUT)

To develop DeepSeek technology an instructional integration successfully, the determinants that affect the overall acceptance and usage of this technology by students should be assessed comprehensively. The proposed model is structured based on the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology 1–2 (UTAUT) as initially defined by Venkatesh et al., (2003) and Venkatesh et al., (2012)31,38. UTAUT is adopted not only because of its predictive robustness but also because AI chatbots such as DeepSeek function within a socio-technical environment where performance gains, social endorsement, and institutional support jointly shape adoption behavior. Unlike traditional information systems, AI chatbots are interactive and adaptive technologies whose value emerges through continuous engagement, making UTAUT’s multidimensional structure suitable for examining their acceptance in higher education.

The studies show that the adoption of technology is a multidimensional process, and AI applications like DeepSeek are relatively new tools the factors which impact such applications are yet to be fully determined. Prior studies highlight the need to explore students’ perspectives on AI-assisted learning39. Though many studies have used UTAUT to AI and chatbot adoption29,30,33,40, most consider UTAUT constructs as direct predictors in situations where the technology is already known to the users. In contrast, DeepSeek is a less well-known generative AI tool within the higher education sector of Pakistan, where awareness construction, collectivist social norms, and institutional preparedness could have a substantial impact on the formation of expectancy. Thus, UTAUT is used as a theoretical framework to analyze the interaction between awareness, social influence, and facilitating conditions to shape the adoption of an emerging AI chatbot.

The novelty of applying UTAUT in this study lies in its tailored extension to DeepSeek, an emerging generative AI tool with limited prior adoption research—and its specific adaptation to the Pakistani higher education context, where technology acceptance is uniquely shaped by collectivist cultural norms, varying digital infrastructure, economic constraints, and social pressures on students. The model incorporates the extrinsic and intrinsic variables defining the drive of the technology use; the consumer decision, based on the expectations of the benefits, which is controlled by (Ease of Use) ease of use and mediated by (Performance Expectancy), the elements of social influence (Social Influence), and the availability of the resources (Facilitating Conditions). The UTAUT provides an in-depth understanding of the components that result in the adoption and use of DeepSeek by students and bridges the gap between utilitarian and hedonic incentives. Accordingly, the proposed model is theoretically grounded in UTAUT, where behavioral intention is explained through performance expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions. The framework is extended by incorporating DeepSeek awareness as a pre-adoption antecedent and positioning perceived ease of use as a boundary condition for emerging AI chatbot adoption.

DeepSeek awareness

Awareness serves as a vital initial step when organizations plan to implement new technologies. Users must be aware of the presence of a technology, its features, and its possible advantages before it can be adopted. For AI tools, awareness is about knowing or understanding their capabilities, uses, and limitations41. In the context of generative AI chatbots such as DeepSeek, awareness is particularly important because users may initially lack familiarity with system boundaries, reliability, and appropriate academic applications. Educational programs can be used to increase awareness. The users may become more favorable toward the technology when they are taught about the AI applications and increasing their informed willingness to use it. This may include lessons, demonstrations, or educational campaigns.

Based upon previous work35,42, higher levels of awareness correlate with a greater end-use adoption intention, and the endeavor of adding coherent replies and being able to adapt to various conversational styles is helping with gaining greater awareness. These users are more likely to be intrigued by the potential of AI technologies to enhance communication, speed up work or processes, as well as improve the user experience. Additionally, the social influence due to suggestions from peers, coworkers, or online communities also helps in raising awareness for adoption43.

Positive feedback and achievements shared by early adopters expand the understanding of AI tool benefits like DeepSeek and foster better adoption conditions44. Studies on similar generative AI confirm that technological awareness leads to adoption intent, which is a standard pattern across many technology-based systems. People who understand what a technology does and how it works, together with its potential advantages, tend to accept it. Understanding of AI tools like DeepSeek requires users to know what these tools do as well as their capabilities and possible daily implementation methods45.

Facilitating conditions

“Facilitating conditions” (FC) measures how much individuals believe there exists adequate organizational and technical support for system usage as described by Venkatesh et al. (2003)38. A person forms their belief about system encouragement through the technological framework and organizational backing38. New technical systems become easier to use when sufficient resources are made available, according to Bervell and Umar (2017)46. People show a greater interest in using technology when they believe relevant infrastructure exists for its use38. FC within educational settings relies on instruction and technical support, which must be paired with internet and computer-based facilities15,47. Students choose to utilize technology that matches currently available ICT solutions48. Studies establish that high-quality facilities remain essential for both chatbots49–51 and AI tool acceptance29,52. Recent studies in developing contexts reinforce FC as a key enabler for generative AI tools15,53, but our focus on DeepSeek shows stronger FC effects in Pakistan due to infrastructural gaps compared to general e-learning settings53. The research study evaluates student confidence in using DeepSeek while supporting the notion that accessible resources enhance their learning targets.

Social influence

Venkatesh et al. (2003)38 define social influence (SI) as “the degree to which an individual perceives that important others believe he or she should use the new system”. The decisions about adopting technology are heavily influenced by the beliefs and actions of others54,55. To reduce ambiguity about the new systems56,57 and to comply with social norms58,59, people turn to their friends, family, or coworkers. SI represents how friends, coworkers, or relatives impact the utilization of DeepSeek AI in this study. People are encouraged to employ AI for learning when they see their peers using it to their advantage55. Empirical research supports SI’s involvement in implementing chatbots15,60 and AI tools29,61, with colleagues serving as the main motivator in educational settings62.

Performance expectancy

According to Venkatesh et al. (2003)38, performance expectancy (PE) is “the degree to which an individual believes that using the system will help him or her attain gains in job performance”. In this study, PE explicitly refers to students’ assumption that using DeepSeek AI technology will improve their academic achievement as well as their learning and research activities. This notion has been thoroughly confirmed in technology adoption research, with multiple studies revealing that PE has a significant impact on users’ behavioral intentions. Recent studies show that PE is a strong predictor of students’ readiness to use AI technologies in educational settings29,52,61 including generative AI tools like ChatGPT and DeepSeek63,64. Critically, PE often ranks as one of the top drivers in GenAI adoption, with stronger effects in developing-country student samples due to practical academic benefits (e.g., productivity gains, research efficiency) compared to hedonic or habit factors in some Western contexts63,64. However, this positive relationship may not hold uniformly across all AI tools or context particularly where academic integrity concerns arise. The perceived usefulness and ease of use of such innovations, particularly more advanced tools such as AI, largely depend on their alignment with the academic goals and expectations of the users65. When students can learn that AI technologies can be applied to enhance their learning, research performance, or academic achievements, they are more willing to adopt and incorporate them in their learning.

Moderating role of perceived ease of use (PEU)

The ease of use is especially important for students who utilize chatbots like ChatGPT in higher education settings66. It shows the degree to which students see chatbots like ChatGPT as simple and straightforward to utilize in their academic activities. This factor is crucial in determining students’ intentions and attitudes about integrating ChatGPT into their learning67. Empirical research36,68 constantly shows that chatbots like ChatGPT ease of use increases students’ propensity to accept different technological advancements, such as online instructional and educational tools. Furthermore, ease of use substantially influences students’ adoption of AI within the educational domain. ChatGPT’s ease of use in educational settings is critical for fostering favorable intentions to use chat-bot like ChatGPT for academic objectives36. Akour et al. (2022)69 used the technology acceptance framework and found that perceived ease of use significantly influenced students’ intention to utilize the metaverse system for learning. Furthermore, Hu (2022)70 studied Taiwanese students at universities in the environment of AI-supported smart classrooms and found that students’ behavioral intention to utilize the smart educational setting was strongly affected by ease of use. Perceived Ease of Use (PEU) moderates rather than mediates the DA-mediator relationships because user evaluations of interface simplicity alter how awareness translates to expectations.

Hypotheses

H1: DeepSeek awareness significantly influences (a) facilitating condition (b) social influence (c) performance expectancy.

H2: (a) facilitating conditions (b) social influence (c) performance expectancy significantly influence adoption of DeepSeek.

H3: (a) facilitating conditions (b) social influence (c) performance expectancy mediate relationship between DeepSeek awareness and adoption of DeepSeek.

H4: Perceived ease of use moderates the association between DeepSeek awareness and (a) facilitating condition (b) social influence (c) performance expectancy.

Methodology

Measurement of constructs and data collection

The instruments were adapted from established scales in the literature with minor modifications to ensure content validity and relevance to the study context. Perceived Ease of Use (four items) was measured using Davis’s (1989)68 study, while DeepSeek Awareness was measured using a seven-item scale adapted from Ojubanire et al. (2025)45, with minor modifications made to refer specifically to DeepSeek. The construct is defined as the degree to which individuals are knowledgeable and informed about DeepSeek, a generative artificial intelligence model developed by DeepSeek AI. Strzelecki (2023) and Venkatesh et al. (2012)31,33 provided the four facilitating condition components, four social influence elements, and four performance expectancy items, respectively. The four items of DeepSeek adoption were drawn from Tarhini et al. (2017)71. We evaluated the questions on a Likert scale of 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly Agree).

Data collection for the study was aided by substantial interaction with an online questionnaire distributed to university students via the online questionnaire platform. The questionnaire comprised two parts. In the first part, respondents provided demographic information, including gender, age, level of education, and university name. The survey consisted of 26 items measuring the study constructs. Informed consent was obtained from all participants, and they were clearly informed that participation was voluntary and that they could withdraw at any time without any consequences. The study was approved by the relevant Ethics Committee, and all procedures were conducted in accordance with applicable guidelines and regulations to ensure that ethical research practices protected participants’ rights and welfare. The size of the sample was computed with the help of the G*Power application, as well as it is substantial enough to guarantee the adequate strength of the findings both in PLS-SEM and in fsQCA72,73. The statistical power that was obtained was greater than 0.99 which is way above the recommended cut of 0.8074. This is an adequate sample size that gives confidence to the generalizability of the findings and offers meaningful examination of numerous predictors and configurations in the research.

Furthermore, the sample data consists of 188 male (44%) and 242 female (56%) respondents. The age breakdown among participants was as follows: 224 (52%) were between the ages of 18 and 25; 174 (40%) were between the ages of 26 and 35; and the remaining 32 (7%) were older than 35. Moreover, respondents’ educational backgrounds are dispersed as follows: There are 261 responders (61%) with bachelor’s degrees, 136 (32%) with master’s degrees, and 33 (8%) with Ph.D (Fig. 1).

Data analysis

A quantitative approach was adopted to test causal relationships, mediation, and moderation in the UTAUT model. PLS-SEM was selected for predictive modeling of complex relationships with non-normal data15,73, complemented by fsQCA for configurational analysis37. This hybrid method fills the gaps: limited empirical evidence on DeepSeek adoption in developing countries like Pakistan, over-reliance on symmetric analyses, and lack of configurational insights into non-linear adoption paths.

The study uses PLS-SEM algorithm and bootstrapping analysis in SmartPLS-4. The reason for choosing this method was that it facilitates testing hypotheses within a complex model architecture75. It also used SmartPLS’s importance performance map analysis (IPMA) to evaluate the performance and importance of its model components.

In contrast, the fsQCA technique is used to study the valid combination of conditions to obtain a result. Moreover, fsQCA was used to obtain a deeper insight into how the factor relationships76 were both nonlinear and complicated. As advanced as it is, fsQCA is particularly valuable for the analysis of situations that include, among others, many conditions, configurational dependencies, and complex causal relationships77. In this study, the different antecedent configurations that lead to the determinants of the use of DeepSeek were determined by the fsQCA. However, from the proposed approach, there is a deep understanding of the variables and their interactions that influence the adoption intentions of end users.

The research employed fsQCA and PLS-SEM to undertake a deep analysis of both the qualitative and quantitative elements. The compensations of the analysis of associations and significance made through PLS-SEM were supported by the capability of fsQCA to detect some configurations and combinations. This dual strategy was better than those previously used to understand the factors driving usage of DeepSeek in a fuller and more accurate way. It makes research more valid, dependable, and gives more precise and complex results.

Results

Partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM)

Anderson and Gerbing’s (1988)78 two-step method was used for the data analysis. Data was analyzed using SmartPLS4. The construct validity of the suggested model, comprising reliability, convergent validity, and discriminant validity, was first evaluated using a Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA). Secondly, the structural model was analyzed, and hypotheses were put to the test.

Common method bias and multicollinearity test

The single-factor test developed by Harman was used to assess Common Method Bias. The findings showed that no single factor explained more than half of the variance. The most significant factor, in particular, was responsible for 35.9% of the variation. These findings indicate that our dataset is unlikely to have been considerably influenced by common method bias, because no single factor appeared as heavily dominating in describing the observed variation. To identify the multicollinearity problem, we additionally look at the variance inflation factor (VIF). According to the findings, each construct’s VIF values, which ranged from 1.228 to 2.184, fell below the acceptable threshold of 5.079 (see Table 1). This suggested that multicollinearity was not a significant issue in this study.

Measurement model

As suggested by Hair et al. (2020)80, a number of criteria were used to assess the measurement model’s discriminant validity, convergent validity, and reliability. The constructs’ internal reliability was evaluated using Cronbach’s alpha and composite reliability (CR). According to Table 1, the Cronbach’s alpha values and CR surpassed the 0.70 cutoff, meeting Chin’s (1998)81 criteria. The factor loadings of each indicator were more than 0.60 and met the threshold. The average variance extracted (AVE) values have been calculated to assess convergent validity. This suggests a substantial amount of the variance in the corresponding indicators was effectively captured by the constructs. Every variable in the study met the Fornell and Larcker (1981) criterion as its AVE value was larger than 0.50.

To evaluate discriminant validity, the Fornell-Larcker criterion and heterotrait-monotrait ratio (HTMT) were computed as shown in Table 2. According to the standards established by Henseler et al. (2015)82, all ratios were determined to be less than 0.90, demonstrating the discriminant validity of the constructs. The thorough analysis of the measurement models demonstrated strong internal reliability together with validated discriminant and convergent validity, demonstrating the study instrument’s strong psychometric qualities.

Structural model

The structural model of PLS-SEM reflects the causal relationships between latent variables in the conceptual model. Structural equation modeling (SEM) using partial least squares (PLS) was used by the authors to determine direct and indirect correlations between constructs. The use of this method is preferable when predicting and studying structural interactions, as it allows both full and partial mediation80. To evaluate its hypotheses, the study used a PLS based SEM approach. The statistical significance of weights assigned to the subconstruct and the path coefficients were assessed through bootstrapping with 5,000 iterations83. Tenenhaus et al. (2005)84 constructed the goodness of fit (GoF) index as a diagnostic tool of evaluating model fit. Using the geometric mean of R2 and the typical communality (for endogenous variables) the GoF is computed. The cutoff values for GoF analysis given by Hoffmann and Birnbrich (2012)85 were GoF medium ¼ 0.25; GoF large ¼ 0.36; and GoF small ¼ 0.1. In this investigation, the model had a GoF value of 0.516, which indicated a satisfactory fit.

The initial hypothesis (H1a, H1b, and H1c) of the study was created to investigate how facilitating situation, social influence, and performance expectancy were influenced by awareness of DeepSeek. According to the results, DA had a significant and favorable impact on FC, SI, and PE (β = 0.821, t = 8.403, p = 0.000; β = 1.155, t = 11.135, p = 0.000; β = 1.028, t = 6.741, p = 0.000) therefore, H1a, H1b and H1c are supported. The next hypotheses (H2a, H2b, and H2c) investigated the direct impact of FC, SI and PE on AD. The findings showed that FC, SI and PE directly affect AD (β = -0.241, t = 4.740 p = 0.000; β = 0.479, t = 10.035, p = 0.000; β = -0.141, t = 3.420, p = 0.001), giving support to H2a, H2b, and H2c.

Moreover, H3a, H3b, and H3c were created to assess the mediating effect of FC, SI and PE on AD. Results showed that significant mediating effects were found for FC, SI and PE (β =-0.198, t = 3.971, p = 0.000; β = 0.553, t = 7.961, p = 0.000; β =-0.145, t = 2.879, p = 0.004) therefore, H3a, H3b, and H3c are supported. The final hypothesis (H4a, H4b, and H4c) was formulated to investigate the moderating effect of perceived ease of use. Most significantly, PEU fully moderates the relationship between DA and FC, SI, PE (β = -0.133, t = 4.313, p = 0.000; β =-0.158, t = 4.554, p = 0.000; β = -0.205, t = 5.040, p = 0.000), thus lending support to H4a, H4b and H4c (Fig. 2) (Table 3).

Fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA)

Focusing on the PLS-SEM outcomes, fsQCA is used to investigate variable interactions, resulting in a more thorough assessment of antecedent conditions and effects. The latent variable scores are extracted from the PLS-SEM results and used in the fsQCA. Construct scores are calculated by linearly combining indicator weights, taking into account measurement error and explanatory power. To fulfill fsQCA standards, data were calibrated using standardized latent variable scores ranging from − 3 (no set membership) to 3 (full set membership), with 0 being the crossover point. The scale calibration approach was used to transform empirical data into a fuzzy set, which was then evaluated to determine the necessary criteria (see Table 4). A causal condition is necessary if it is always present (or missing) when the desired consequence is present (or absent). The results showed that none of the conditions had a consistency score greater than 0.986,87, meaning that the necessary circumstances did not occur. The fsQCA truth table assessment generates three types of solutions: complicated, moderate, and parsimonious. Prior study recommends using the intermediate option owing to the appropriate balance of intricacy and practicality88 (Fig. 3).

Comparative analysis of analytical approaches

The paper combines PLS-SEM, fsQCA, and IPMA to give a multi-method approach to AD, which demonstrates the linear net effects and configurational pathways and practical intervention priorities. Although all structural paths were statistically significant in the PLS-SEM analysis, indicating meaningful net effects of DA, PE, SI, FC, and PEU on AD, the fsQCA results demonstrate that adoption emerges through multiple sufficient configurations rather than a single dominant predictor. While PLS-SEM identifies independent directional relationships, fsQCA reveals how different combinations of awareness, contextual support, and usability jointly produce adoption outcomes, thereby confirming equifinality and methodological complementarity. The Importance–Performance Map Analysis (IPMA) further enriches this interpretation by identifying which constructs exhibit stronger practical leverage for institutional strategies. The results indicate that DA and PEU show relatively strong performance, whereas SI demonstrates high importance but comparatively lower performance, suggesting strategic improvement potential. Notably, while PE exhibits a negative net effect in PLS-SEM, it appears as a sufficient condition in fsQCA configurations, highlighting the value of asymmetric analysis in revealing complex causal patterns. Together, the combined evidence from PLS-SEM, fsQCA, and IPMA provides a comprehensive understanding of both theoretical relationships and practical prioritization in AD.

Discussion

The study’s primary goal was to examine DeepSeek awareness and adoption intentions among Pakistani university students using the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) paradigm (facilitating conditions, social influence, and performance expectancy). To address the research questions comprehensively, the study combined PLS-SEM (symmetric analysis) with fsQCA (asymmetric configurational analysis), allowing examination of both linear effects and multiple causal pathways leading to adoption. The suggested conceptual model was statistically validated on university students in Pakistan who utilized the DeepSeek application, an AI tool, for their learning purposes. The findings showed that DeepSeek awareness (DA) significantly influences facilitating conditions (FC), social influence (SI), and performance expectancy (PE). Furthermore, the relationship between DA and AD is considerably mediated by FC, SI, and PE. Critically, the indirect effects differ in direction: the indirect effect through SI is significantly positive, whereas the indirect effects through FC and PE are significantly negative. Further, perceived ease of use (PEU) significantly moderates the relationship between DA and FC, SI, and PE, and these moderating effects are negative. The following discussion interprets these findings critically in relation to prior research and directly answers the study’s research questions using insights from both analytical techniques.

Firstly, our findings showed that DeepSeek awareness had a beneficial impact on facilitating conditions, social influence, and performance expectancy. This result answers Research Question 1 by confirming that awareness operates as a foundational cognitive trigger shaping expectations of institutional support, peer norms, and performance outcomes. Unlike studies conducted in highly digitalized contexts where awareness is often assumed, the strength of the awareness effect in our model suggests that in emerging AI ecosystems awareness functions as a strategic adoption lever35,90. These findings align with the study conducted by Sharma (2020); Shahzad, Xu, & Baheer (2024)35,91, who also indicated that perceived awareness has a substantial impact on technology acceptance in the educational setting. In a similar study, Alharbi and Drew (2014)92 suggested that in educational institutions, perceived awareness, workplace attitude, job relevance, perceived adaptability, and pertinent experience are all aspects that directly influence the use of technology. The AI tool DeepSeek has been acknowledged as a valuable tool for boosting students’ academic task. This finding suggests that awareness functions as a cognitive activation mechanism that shapes how students evaluate institutional support, peer norms, and expected academic gains. It assists in comprehending DeepSeek’s capabilities and how they might be linked to academic activities to enhance their learning. This supports prior generative AI studies suggesting that early cognitive familiarity reduces uncertainty and strengthens perceived value, especially for novel systems35,66. Additionally, DeepSeek awareness might raise students’ expectations on how to apply it to improve their writing, problem-solving, and challenging task performance, and they will view it as a useful tool. Students are more likely to regard DeepSeek as beneficial in their academic endeavors if they are aware of the various ways it might assist with content creation, explaining, and learning facilitation.

Secondly, FC, SI, and PE significantly influence AD. These findings partially confirm UTAUT predictions, but they also reveal meaningful deviations from traditional adoption patterns47,52,93–95. However, the structural results show that these effects are not uniform in direction. While SI has a significant positive effect on AD, FC has a significant negative effect on AD. The negative FC effect suggests institutional support may be perceived as restrictive, while the negative PE effect reflects academic integrity concerns. Additionally, the results indicate that students’ usage of AI technologies like DeepSeek is impacted by SI, particularly their peers. Social influence emerged as the strongest positive driver, indicating that adoption decisions are socially embedded rather than purely performance-driven in this context96. However, according to the UTAUT theory, PE is a significant predictor of AI tool adoption in studies such as Raffaghelli et al.’s (2022)97 exploration of students’ acceptance of a system for early warning in higher education and Mehta et al.’s (2019)98 investigation of the impact of values on e-learning adoption. According to our findings, PE shows a significant negative effect on AD, so students who have a high “performance expectancy” are less likely to employ valuable technology such as DeepSeek due to academic integrity concerns.

Thirdly, FC, SI, and PE serve as mediators between DeepSeek awareness and adoption. The mediation analysis in this study further strengthens our understanding of the complex relationships between the variables. This mediation pattern answers Research Question 2 by clarifying the mechanism through which awareness converts into adoption. Previous research has proven the role of FC, SI, and PE in technology adoption40,99,100. This is partially in line with UTAUT; however, unlike most previous studies that report positive indirect effects, the indirect effect through SI is positive, whereas the indirect effect through FC and PE are negative in the present Pakistani sample. Further, this analysis showcases how these findings are transferable to other technological studies applied to the AI tool in an academic setting and that different mediating mechanisms shape adoption differently in this educational context. Compared with earlier chatbot adoption studies (e.g., Strzelecki, 2023; Du & Lv, 2024; Strzelecki & ElArabawy, 2024)29,33,52, our model shows a stronger indirect effect through social influence than through performance expectancy, suggesting that peer normalization may outweigh instrumental reasoning in collectivist educational cultures101,102. Findings suggest DeepSeek awareness is advantageous to students in regards to student acceptance of AI tools and , prompting students to use such tools. This may be attributed to the fact that the tools can assist with executing research and learning tasks52, and also offer high-quality work in response to a user’s query103. Users’ perceptions of DeepSeek’s usefulness and adoption appear to be influenced by the social impact, the facilities provided, and the performance. DeepSeek can motivate students to use it to help them in dealing with complex tasks and problems if they have an understanding of the appropriate technical assistance and resources available. Although in the present model the indirect pathways through FC and PE reduce rather than increase adoption.

Fourthly, the significant relationship between DA and FC, SI, and PE is moderated by PEU. Results align with the past studies by Ebadi & Raygan (2023), Shahzad, Xu & Asif, M. (2025)104,105 in terms of the existence of a moderating effect. However, contrary to previous findings, PEU exerted a significant negative moderating effect on all three relationships, meaning high ease of use weakens the translation of awareness into expectations. Moreover, according to Al-Abdullatif (2023)66, Chatbot’s user-friendly interface, straightforward functionality, and seamless interactivity make it easy to write text and have conversations. DeepSeek’s ease of use serves as a moderating link between its performance, facilities, and awareness. In addition, students with a high level of technology efficacy will transmit positive experiences and recommendations to other students, which will ultimately improve students’ performance in online educational platforms or digital learning tools. However, the interaction effects are negative, indicating that PEU weaken the extent to which awareness translates into FC, SI, and PE. Also, users are more likely to accept DeepSeek, perceive it to be user-friendly, and be convinced by their friends and peers to use AI tools for their work and assignments. PEU is also a very important factor in the relationship between awareness and performance expectations; however, in the present study this relationship becomes weaker as PEU increases.

Lastly, the fsQCA analysis answers Research Question 3 by offering alternative configurational pathways that lead to DeepSeek adoption. The fsQCA results demonstrate equifinality, showing that DeepSeek adoption emerges from multiple sufficient configurations rather than a single dominant predictor. Rather, the same outcome may be caused by various groups of factors. This strengthens the complexity that is inherent in adoption behavior wherein outcomes are a product of interaction among the conditions of the context and not individual forces. Although the identified configurations can provide a thorough structure of adoption exploration, their diversity implies that the strategies of adoption in the real world cannot take a one-size-fits-all approach. The most appropriate interventions should instead respond to the subtle interaction of factors in particular usage context. Moreover, fsQCA complements PLS-SEM by showing that PE, despite its negative net effect can drive adoption when combined with other factors, explaining the value of configurational thinking. Through this two-faceted approach, the reader may have the entire picture of the reasons behind the adoption of DeepSeek, and the fact that there are numerous factors in the environment of educational technology adoption that are very interconnected, complex, and intertwined.

Theoretical implications

The present study contributes to the literature on AI tools in the educational field. This study goes beyond UTAUT by showing that DA is a crucial precondition for the key adoption-related belief about AI, especially for specialized tools like DeepSeek, where utility assessments come after awareness-raising (unlike widely known platforms and AI tools). This research’s findings have important implications for broadening the discussion on the use of the AI tool DeepSeek technology, despite the dearth of research on the topic, particularly in the context of educational institutions. Additionally, by applying fsQCA, this study identifies how configurations of multiple factors, rather than single determinants, jointly influence the adoption of DeepSeek, offering a more nuanced understanding of adoption pathways. Several elements outlined in the study will help explain the technology’s adoption for educational institutions. Firstly, by examining the variables that affect students’ adoption of DeepSeek for instructional purposes, this study fills the gap in the existing literature. We examined the factors influencing students’ decisions to use the DeepSeek AI tool using the UTAUT model. It is thought to be an innovative addition to the academic community and literature on technology management to integrate awareness of DeepSeek with UTAUT principles. The study’s authenticity and trustworthiness are strengthened for future researchers by applying the UTAUT model and its constructs to the setting of Pakistani university students.

Secondly, the factors impacting the adoption of DeepSeek AI tools in educational settings include DeepSeek awareness, perceived ease of use, facilitating conditions, social influence, and performance expectancy. More specifically, social influence positively affects adoption, whereas facilitating conditions and performance expectancy negatively affect adoption, and perceived ease of use negatively moderates the relationship between awareness and facilitating conditions, social influence, and performance expectancy. Using fsQCA, this study further shows that different combinations of these factors can lead to high adoption, highlighting multiple pathways rather than a single causal factor. People would continue to seek AI assistance for solutions, which would lead to an increase in literacy, awareness, and knowledge because this research highlights the tool’s value for education and learning. This also highlights that these factors do not influence adoption in the same direction. Acquiring such information will result in profound implications for the field of education as well as technology, primarily because it will aid in understanding how to implement fresh technologies and tools into the field of education to increase student learning. Human brain development and learning will undergo as a result of the more thorough information provided by computer interaction.

Thirdly, it emphasizes the significance of fostering a strong awareness and informed use among students and stakeholders. By providing tools, instruction, and support to facilitate the use of technology, educational institutions may encourage the adoption of generative AI and its effective application for engagement and information transmission106. Moreover, fsQCA emphasizes that adoption emerges from the interaction of multiple conditions, reinforcing the importance of context-specific strategies for implementing AI tools. Acquiring such sort of information is going to have a profound impact on the field of education and technology, primarily because it will assist in understanding how to implement novel technologies and tools into the education sector to increase student learning.

Fourthly, verifying that DeepSeek best reflects students’ needs and expectations will also allow developers and teachers to focus on better assistance and user experience by identifying the factors impacting usage trends. Lastly, scholars could find the adoption patterns associated with DeepSeek interesting. This can require the researchers to look through a large amount of scholarly material. It may be convenient for scientists and researchers as well to gather information, respond to research questions, and write theses and reports. This would be beneficial for the academic community and the general public because of its interactivity. This could help universities and colleges to better understand the digital skills that employers demand. DeepSeek can be effectively used in the classroom to support academic tasks, although the present findings show that adoption is driven more positively by social influence than by facilitating conditions or performance expectancy.

Practical implications

The practical insights from the current study can help educational institutions utilize DeepSeek in classroom environments. We recommend, for optimal integration, first starting pilot programs in writing centers or STEM departments, then faculty training on editing AI-generated content, and that content can also be made mobile-compatible for Pakistan’s smartphone-dependent users. DeepSeek can serve as an example that of how AI tools can be introduced into learning environments in a structured and context-sensitive way.

Adding artificial intelligence (AI) tools like DeepSeek to conventional teaching methods may provide a dynamic learning environment that meets the individual needs and learning preferences of every student. To ensure academic integrity, institutions should also have guidelines to understand and validate the use of AI assistance by students. It can also serve as a supplementary instructional tool that may support students alongside teachers, especially where time and resources are limited.

The potential of DeepSeek as a supportive academic tool for students to think critically, develop metacognition, and develop creative problem-solving procedures makes it an interesting STEM-related research and development project. An example of this would be to design assignments where the outputs of DeepSeek would be compared to the peer-reviewed literature. DeepSeek may play an important role in encouraging student engagement with academic tasks by supporting, for instance, functional manipulation of content, writing essays, or designing exams with it as study material. However, one must be careful about AI-related issues of data security and ethics.

This demonstrates that the value of human judgment and awareness of context must continue to be central to the process of learning and highlights the significance of utilizing AI as a tool rather than as a substitute for human teachers. When implemented ethically and responsibly, the use of AI technologies in education, such as DeepSeek, can result in more engaging, customized, and flexible learning environments. To guarantee its proper application, though, a lot of work must be put in. This includes continuous feedback processes between DeepSeek, human instructors, and students, as well as close attention to potential difficulties. A three-tier approach works best: (1) teacher training, (2) student digital literacy programs, and (3) institutional AI use policies aligned with Pakistan’s Higher Education Commission guidelines, with particular attention to awareness-building and socially supported use, because the present findings show that social influence is the strongest positive driver of adoption.

Conclusion

The development of technologies has made AI implementation and use essential. By combining the findings of awareness, social structures, and the UTAUT, the current study has been able to create an entire model that can be used to understand the factors affecting the AD by higher education students through the hybrid methodological tool, PLS-SEM, and fsQCA. It further investigated the impact of PEU on DA, FC, SI, PE and AD. The findings of the study include four distinct points, namely, the PLS-SEM findings showed the presence of a favorable correlation between DA and FC, SI and PE. Second, FC, SI and PE are significantly related to the AD. Thirdly, the relationship between DA and AD is mediated by the FC, SI, and PE. Finally, there is a significant moderating effect of the PEU between the DA, FC, SI and PE and AD. The outcome of the fsQCA shows that adoption may take place in numerous different ways, which has pointed to the importance of situational-specific strategies rather than solutions. This is used to depict how user behavior is a complex interaction between variables, in which a single situation is not predetermined by a particular outcome. The study findings will offer valuable information about the implementation of AI tools by policymakers and the existing body of knowledge. It emphasizes the role of a number of factors in the intent of consumers to adopt and use AI technologies. Moreover, showing that regulatory support and other factors can be used to enhance the performance of students and how to further make students more engaged with AI-based applications like DeepSeek, the findings would be of benefit to teachers, AI developers, and other segments of the organization.

Limitations and future research direction

The present research has some constraints that provide recommendations to the upcoming study. To begin with, there is a support of only Pakistani students in the research sample. In order to make the results more relevant, it is recommended that the next conducted research investigates the study model in terms of different geographical areas (e.g., Indonesia, Nigeria, etc.). Second, the sample of data collection was restricted to university students only; to enhance the external validity, the research needs to be done in other educational settings. Third, in order to examine any variation in DeepSeek adoption intentions and use, future research ought to examine parallel studies in other countries or groups of students (e.g., school and university). Moreover, the fact that DeepSeek was not familiar could have impacted students in a number of ways in terms of opinion and plans to adopt. Less technologically advanced students may be more inclined to base their intentions on the first impressions or preconceptions. Thus, the fact that the students are less familiar with DeepSeek is just a summary of what the students experienced in the initial stages; the future research may study how this familiarity may change with the prolonged exposure to DeepSeek and its impact in the long term on the attitudes and adoption behavior of the students. The future studies can also focus on the follow-ups studies to gain significant insights at different stages of time in order to understand the long-term implications of DeepSeek in higher education. The final limitation of the study is that it has been carried out in a quantitative way. This topic can be explored further in the future with the assistance of the qualitative methodology that can be used to assess the variables influencing AI-assisted reading tools in a more comprehensive way.

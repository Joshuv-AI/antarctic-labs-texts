Boundary determination of electromagnetic parameters from local data
Chengyu Wu, Jiaqing Yang

Abstract
In this paper, we extend and simplify the methods in [ 13 ] to improve the results on uniqueness of the boundary determination for the Maxwell equation. In particular, we show that the electromagnetic parameters are uniquely determined to infinite order at the boundary from the local admittance map, disregarding the presence of an unknown obstacle, where actually only the local Cauchy data of the fundamental solution are used. The proof mainly relies on an elaborate singularity analysis on certain singular solutions to the Maxwell equation.



keywords

Maxwell equation, inverse problem, partial data, embedded obstacle, singularity analysis.

35R30.

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary. Denote by
D
a bounded domain such that
\partial D\in C^{\infty}
,
D\subset\subset\Omega
and
\Omega\setminus\overline{D}
is connected. Consider the boundary value problem of finding the electromagnetic fields
E
and
H
satisfying

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm curl\,}E-i\omega\mu H=0&{\rm in}~\Omega\setminus\overline{D},\\
{\rm curl\,}H+i\omega\gamma E=0&{\rm in}~\Omega\setminus\overline{D},\\
\nu\times E=f&{\rm on}~\partial\Omega,\\
\mathcal{B}(E)=0&{\rm on}~\partial D.\end{array}\right.

Here,
\omega>0
is the frequency,
\gamma=\varepsilon+i\sigma/\omega
, and the magnetic permeability
\mu
, electric permittivity
\varepsilon
, conductivity
\sigma
are in
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
. Throughout the paper, we always assume that
\mu,|\gamma|>0
in
\overline{\Omega}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{B}
stands for the boundary condition on
\partial D
, which corresponds to a perfect conductor condition if
\mathcal{B}(E)=\nu\times E
and an impedance condition if
\mathcal{B}(E)=\nu\times{\rm curl\,}E+\rho(\nu\times E)\times\nu
with
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\partial D,{\mathbb{C}})
.

Define
H({\rm curl\,},\Omega\setminus\overline{D})
to be the Hilbert space

\displaystyle H({\rm curl\,},\Omega\setminus\overline{D}):=\{u\in[L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\overline{D})]^{3},{\rm curl\,}u\in[L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\overline{D})]^{3}\}

equipped with the inner product
(u,v):=(u,v)_{L^{2}}+({\rm curl\,}u,{\rm curl\,}v)_{L^{2}}
. For
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, define

\displaystyle H^{s}_{t}(\partial\Omega):=\{u\in[H^{s}(\partial\Omega)]^{3},u\cdot\nu=0\}.

Denote by
{\rm Div\,}
the surface divergence on
\partial\Omega
. Further, define

\displaystyle H^{-1/2}_{{\rm Div\,}}(\partial\Omega):=\{u\in H^{-1/2}_{t}(\partial\Omega),{\rm Div\,}u\in H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)\}.

It is known that problem (
1
) has a unique solution
(E,H)\in H({\rm curl\,},\Omega\setminus\overline{D})\times H({\rm curl\,},\Omega\setminus\overline{D})
for each
f\in H^{-1/2}_{{\rm Div\,}}(\partial\Omega)
except for a discrete set of magnetic resonance frequencies
\{\omega_{n}\}
. We here assume that
\omega
is not a resonance frequency. Then the admittance map
\Lambda:H^{-1/2}_{{\rm Div\,}}(\partial\Omega)\rightarrow H^{-1/2}_{{\rm Div\,}}(\partial\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\Lambda:f\mapsto\nu\times H|_{\partial\Omega},

is well-defined. In the present paper, we will study the inverse problem of determining the electromagnetic parameters
\mu
and
\gamma
from the knowledge of
\Lambda
. In particular, let
\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
be a nonempty open subset. We shall prove:

Theorem 1
.

For
(\mu_{i},\varepsilon_{i},D_{i},\mathcal{B}_{i})
,
i=1,2
, suppose
\Lambda_{1}f=\Lambda_{2}f
on
\Gamma
for all
f\in H^{-1/2}_{{\rm Div\,}}(\partial\Omega)
with
{\rm{supp}}f\subset\Gamma
, then

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}\mu_{1}=D^{\alpha}\mu_{2},~D^{\alpha}\gamma_{1}=D^{\alpha}\gamma_{2}~on~\Gamma

for all
|\alpha|\geq 0
.

Substantial progresses have been made for the global determination of electromagnetic parameters from complete data, such as
[
11
,
12
]
. However, it is assumed in these work that the material parameters are known to some order at the boundary, which is the reason that Joshi and McDowall considered the boundary determination in
[
5
,
9
]
. Following the idea in
[
8
]
, they proved that from the complete admittance map the parameters are uniquely determined to infinite order at the boundary by computing the symbol of
\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}
and
\Lambda_{1}^{-1}-\Lambda_{2}^{-1}
, which thus completes the proof of global determination in
[
11
,
12
]
.

Recently, the inverse problem concerning the partial data is gaining more and more interests. For the global determination from partial data we refer to
[
1
,
2
]
, where, nevertheless, the global uniqueness results are obtained also assuming the boundary determination results. It is therefore pertinent to further study the boundary determination in partial data case, for which we note that the methods in
[
5
,
9
]
do not apply to the partial data case, since there are not enough information for
\Lambda^{-1}
if only knowing partial
\Lambda
.

In this paper, we extend and simplify the novel method proposed in
[
13
]
to give a new proof and slightly improve the boundary determination results in
[
5
,
9
]
by considering the local admittance map
\Lambda
, and moreover, assuming the presence of an unknown embedded obstacle
(D,\mathcal{B})
. Particularly, we use only the local Cauchy data for the fundamental solution to show that the electromagnetic parameters
\mu
and
\gamma
are uniquely determined to infinite order at the boundary, disregarding the unknown obstacle. Our methods mainly depends on the detailed singularity analysis for certain singular solutions and some other relating functions. To be exact, we derive the singularity in the
H^{m}
sense for aribitrary
m
for fixed solutions behaving like
{\rm curl\,}(1/|x-z|)
and the volume potential of the fundamental solution. Specifically, we utilize the Green’s representatioin to derive the full singularity of the solutions, and rigorously study some associated singular integrals to obtain the singularity of the volume potential of the fundamental solution. These explicit singularity allows us to ignore the unknown obstacle and directly compare the singularity of solutions corresponding to different
\mu
and
\gamma
locally at the boundary. Any diffenence of the parameters at the boundary would contradict with the singularity obtained previously and thus the boundary determination result follows. We also note that the methods in
[
5
,
9
]
essentially relied on turning the Maxwell equation to a second order differential equation with the principal term being the Laplacian
\Delta
, while we are working more directly on the Maxwell equation, which indicates that our methods is more handleable when extends to other equations.

The paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we show some technical results on the singularity to prepare for the proof of the main theorems. In particular, in section
2.1
we propose a simple method to derive the complete singularity of the solutions to problem (
1
) with certain singular boundary data. Then in section
2.2
, we compute explicitly the norms of the volume potential of the fundamental solution. Finally, in section
3
we prove the main results Theorems
1
.

2
Some preparations

In this section, we do some elaborate singularity analysis for the preparations of the main theorems. We shall derive the complete description of the singularity of the solution to problem (
1
) with the boundary data being the fundamental solution to the Maxwell equation. The method mainly depends on the Green’s representation of the solutions and thus can be applied to many other cases. Then we simplify the methods in
[
13
]
on computing the Sobolev norms at the boundary for the volume potential of fundamental solution, which plays a significant role in later proof.

Now by the interior regularity of Maxwell’s equation, we see that for all

∎

2.2
The exploding norms

In this subsection, we show that the Sobolev norms at the boundary of
{\rm NewA}G_{k}({\rm NewA}q_{0}\cdot H_{0,\delta})
would explode for certain
q_{0}
. In particular, we greatly simplify the method in
[
13
]
on analyzing the following singular integral.

Lemma 7
.

Follow the notations
x_{0},\delta_{0}
and
z_{\delta}
in Theorem
3
. Suppose
x_{0}=(0,0,0)
and
\nu(x_{0})=(1,0,0)
. For
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
, define

\displaystyle I_{2m-1}(\delta):=\delta^{3/2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|y|}\left(\partial_{1}^{2m+2}\frac{1}{|y-z_{\delta}|}\right)y_{1}^{2m-1}dy,

\displaystyle I_{2m}(\delta):=\delta^{3/2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|y|}\partial_{1}\left[\left(\partial_{1}^{2m+2}\frac{1}{|y-z_{\delta}|}\right)y_{1}^{2m}\right]dy.

Then for arbitrarily fixed
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
|I_{m}(\delta)|\rightarrow+\infty
as
\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}
.

Proof.

Since
\partial\Omega\in C^{\infty}
, without loss of genearlity we can assume that
\partial\Omega
is flat near
x_{0}
. In particular, we suppose that
\Omega=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3},y_{2}^{2}+y_{3}^{2}<1,-1<y_{1}<0\}
.

To prove the lemma, we first define some auxiliary functions for
\delta
. For
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
, define

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{m}(\delta):=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|y|}\left(\partial_{1}\frac{1}{|y-z_{\delta}|}\right)y_{1}^{m}dy.

By the explicit expression of
\Omega
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{m}(\delta)=

\displaystyle\;-\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|y|}\frac{y_{1}-\delta}{|y-z_{\delta}|^{3}}y_{1}^{m}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;(-1)^{m}\pi\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{y_{1}^{2}+r}}\frac{y_{1}+\delta}{\sqrt{(y_{1}+\delta)^{2}+r}^{3}}y_{1}^{m}drdy_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;(-1)^{m}2\pi\int_{0}^{1}(y_{1}+\delta)y_{1}^{m}\left(\int_{y_{1}}^{\sqrt{y_{1}^{2}+1}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^{2}+(y_{1}+\delta)^{2}-y_{1}^{2}}^{3}}ds\right)dy_{1}.

It is known that

\displaystyle\int_{y_{1}}^{\sqrt{y_{1}^{2}+1}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^{2}+(y_{1}+\delta)^{2}-y_{1}^{2}}^{3}}ds=\frac{1}{\delta(2y_{1}+\delta)}\left(\frac{\sqrt{y_{1}^{2}+1}}{\sqrt{(y_{1}+\delta)^{2}+1}}-1+\frac{\delta}{y_{1}+\delta}\right),

which implies

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{m}(\delta)=(-1)^{m}2\pi\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{y_{1}^{m}}{2y_{1}+\delta}+\frac{-(y_{1}+\delta)y_{1}^{m}}{\sqrt{(y_{1}+\delta)^{2}+1}(\sqrt{y_{1}^{2}+1}+\sqrt{(y_{1}+\delta)^{2}+1})}\right)dy_{1}.

Direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle(-1)^{m}2\pi\int_{0}^{1}\frac{y_{1}^{m}}{2y_{1}+\delta}dy_{1}=

\displaystyle\;(-1)^{m}\pi\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{j=0}^{m}C_{m}^{j}(y_{1}+\frac{\delta}{2})^{j-1}(-\frac{\delta}{2})^{m-j}dy_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;-\frac{\pi}{2^{m}}\delta^{m}\ln\delta+\left[(-\frac{\delta}{2})^{m}\ln(2\delta+1)\right.

\displaystyle\;\left.+\sum_{j=1}^{m}(-\frac{\delta}{2})^{m-j}\frac{C_{m}^{j}}{j}\left((1+\frac{\delta}{2})^{j}-(\frac{\delta}{2})^{j}\right)\right].

Therefore, we derive that for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{m}(\delta)=-\frac{\pi}{2^{m}}\delta^{m}\ln\delta+f_{m}(\delta),

where
f_{m}
is a smooth function in
[0,\delta_{0}]
.

Now we investigate on
I_{m}(\delta)
. It is seen that for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|y|}\left(\partial_{1}^{m+3}\frac{1}{|y-z_{\delta}|}\right)y_{1}^{m}dy=

\displaystyle\;(-1)^{m}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|y|}\left(\partial_{\delta}^{m+2}\partial_{1}\frac{1}{|y-z_{\delta}|}\right)y_{1}^{m}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;(-1)^{m}\frac{d^{m+2}}{d\delta^{m+2}}\widetilde{I}_{m}(\delta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;(-1)^{m}\pi\frac{m!}{2^{m}}\delta^{-2}+\frac{d^{m+2}}{d\delta^{m+2}}f_{m}(\delta),

which implies that

\displaystyle I_{2m-1}(\delta)=-\pi\frac{(2m-1)!}{2^{2m-1}}\delta^{-1/2}+\delta^{3/2}\frac{d^{2m+1}}{d\delta^{2m+1}}f_{2m-1}(\delta),

\displaystyle I_{2m}(\delta)=-\pi\frac{(2m)!}{2^{2m}}\delta^{-1/2}+\delta^{3/2}\left(2m\frac{d^{2m+1}}{d\delta^{2m+1}}f_{2m-1}(\delta)+\frac{d^{2m+2}}{d\delta^{2m+2}}f_{2m}(\delta)\right)

for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
. Since
f_{m}
is smooth in
[0,\delta_{0}]
, we obtain the conclusion.
∎

Theorem 8
.

Follow the notations
x_{0},\delta_{0}
and
z_{\delta}
in Theorem
3
. For fixed

, let

Proof.

Without loss of genearlity, we suppose that

for

with

,
\delta^{3/2}\partial_{2}q\partial_{12}\Phi_{k_{0}}(\cdot,z_{\delta})
and

are uniformly bounded in
H^{m+1}(\Omega)
for
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
. Therefore, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\left\|\nu\times{\rm NewA}G_{k}(\delta^{3/2}\partial_{1}q\partial_{1}^{2}\Phi_{k_{0}}(\cdot,z_{\delta}))\right\|_{H_{t}^{m+3/2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\nu\cdot{\rm NewA}G_{k}(\delta^{3/2}\partial_{1}q\partial_{1}^{2}\Phi_{k_{0}}(\cdot,z_{\delta}))\right\|_{H^{m+3/2}(\partial\Omega)}\rightarrow+\infty

as
\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}
. Further, from the regularity of
\Phi_{k}-\Phi_{0}
, it is enough to prove that

\displaystyle\left\|\nu\times{\rm NewA}G_{0}(\delta^{3/2}\partial_{1}q\partial_{1}^{2}\Phi_{0}(\cdot,z_{\delta}))\right\|_{H_{t}^{m+3/2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\nu\cdot{\rm NewA}G_{0}(\delta^{3/2}\partial_{1}q\partial_{1}^{2}\Phi_{0}(\cdot,z_{\delta}))\right\|_{H^{m+3/2}(\partial\Omega)}\rightarrow+\infty

(2.25)

as
\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}
.

Now consider the case that
m=0
. We prove by contradiction. Assume there exists a sequence
\{\delta_{l}\}_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset(0,\delta_{0})
with
\delta_{l}\rightarrow 0
as
l\rightarrow+\infty
such that the norms in (
2.25
) are bounded for
\{\delta_{l}\}
. It is known that
\delta^{3/2}(\partial_{1}q-\partial_{1}q(x_{0}))\partial_{1}^{2}\Phi_{0}(\cdot,z_{\delta})

for
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
, which implies by Theorem
2
and the trace theorem that
{\rm NewA}G_{0}[\delta^{3/2}(\partial_{1}q-\partial_{1}q(x_{0}))\partial_{1}^{2}\Phi_{0}(\cdot,z_{\delta})]
are uniformly bounded in
[H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)]^{3}
for
\delta
. Since

, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|{\rm NewA}G_{0}(\delta_{l}^{3/2}\partial_{1}^{2}\Phi_{0}(\cdot,z_{\delta_{l}}))\right\|_{[H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)]^{3}}\leq C

(2.26)

uniformly for

. From the boundedness of

in

[
3

are uniformly bounded in

for

. This is however a contradiction and thus the conclusion follows.

We finish the proof by applying Lemma
7
. Suppose the oppisite that the norms are bounded for
\{\delta_{l}\}
. Clearly, the norms are also bounded when restricted on
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left\|\nu\times{\rm NewA}G_{0}(\delta_{l}^{3/2}\partial_{1}q\partial_{1}^{2}\Phi_{0}(\cdot,z_{\delta_{l}}))\right\|_{H_{t}^{m+3/2}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}

sufficiently small, we can smoothly straighten the boundary

such that the norms after straightening are equivalent (see

on

.

We first consider the case that

with

on
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\partial_{j}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{0}(x,y)\partial_{1}q(y)\partial_{1}^{2}\Phi_{0}(y,z_{\delta_{l}})dy=

\displaystyle\;\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{0}(x,y)\partial_{j}\left(\partial_{1}q(y)\partial_{1}^{2}\Phi_{0}(y,z_{\delta_{l}})\right)dy

\displaystyle\;-\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus\overline{\Gamma}_{\varepsilon}}\Phi_{0}(x,y)\partial_{1}q(y)\partial_{1}^{2}\Phi_{0}(y,z_{\delta_{l}})\nu_{j}(y)ds(y)

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{0}(x,y)\delta_{l}^{3/2}\partial_{1}q(y)\partial_{j_{1}}\partial_{j_{2}}\partial_{1}^{2}\Phi_{0}(y,z_{\delta_{l}})dy\right\|_{H^{m+1/2}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}\leq C

for

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{0}(x,y)\delta_{l}^{3/2}\partial_{1}q(y)\partial_{1}^{4}\Phi_{0}(y,z_{\delta_{l}})dy\right\|_{H^{m+1/2}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}\leq C.

Repeating the above procedure
i_{0}-1

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{0}(x,y)\delta_{l}^{3/2}\partial_{1}q(y)\partial_{1}^{2i_{0}+2}\Phi_{0}(y,z_{\delta_{l}})dy\right\|_{H^{3/2}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}\leq C,

which implies by the bounded embedding from

1\leq|\alpha|\leq l+2
and the positive constant
C
independent of
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
.

Lemma 16
.

Under the conditions in Lemma
12
, we further have

\displaystyle\|q_{1}\Phi_{l,\delta}^{(1)\prime}-q_{2}\Phi_{l,\delta}^{(2)\prime}\|_{[H^{l+1}(\Omega)]^{3}}+\|q_{1}\Psi_{l,\delta}^{(1)\prime}-q_{2}\Psi_{l,\delta}^{(2)\prime}\|_{[H^{l+1}(\Omega)]^{3}}\leq C,

\displaystyle\|D^{\alpha}q_{1}\Phi_{l,\delta}^{(1)\prime}-D^{\alpha}q_{2}\Phi_{l,\delta}^{(2)\prime}\|_{[H^{l+2-|\alpha|}(\Omega)]^{3}}+\|D^{\alpha}q_{1}\Psi_{l,\delta}^{(1)\prime}-D^{\alpha}q_{2}\Psi_{l,\delta}^{(2)\prime}\|_{[H^{l+2-|\alpha|}(\Omega)]^{3}}\leq C

with

, where the constant

is independent of

.

With all the preceding preparations, we now are at the position to prove the main results of this paper.

Proof.

(of Theorem
1
)
Suppose that there exists a
l\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
and a
x_{0}\in\Gamma
such that
D^{\alpha}\mu_{1}=D^{\alpha}\mu_{2}
and
D^{\alpha}\gamma_{1}=D^{\alpha}\gamma_{2}
on
\Gamma
when

, and

or
\partial_{\nu}^{l+1}\gamma_{1}(x_{0})\neq\partial_{\nu}^{l+1}\gamma_{2}(x_{0})
. Without loss of genearlity, we assume that
\partial_{\nu}^{l+1}\mu_{1}(x_{0})\neq\partial_{\nu}^{l+1}\mu_{2}(x_{0})
. The case for
\gamma
can be handled similarly using Theorem
9
and Lemmas
14
-
16
. In view of Lemma
11
, we have the estimate that

\displaystyle\|\psi_{l+1,\delta}^{(1)}-\psi_{l+1,\delta}^{(2)}\|_{H^{l+3/2}_{t}(\partial\Omega)}+\|\nu\cdot(\Psi_{l+1,\delta}^{(1)}-\Psi_{l+1,\delta}^{(2)})\|_{H^{l+3/2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C.

Recalling the definition of

and

, applying Lemma

we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\psi_{1,\delta}^{(1)}-\psi_{1,\delta}^{(2)}\|_{H^{l+3/2}_{t}(\partial\Omega)}+\|\nu\cdot(\Psi_{1,\delta}^{(1)}-\Psi_{1,\delta}^{(2)})\|_{H^{l+3/2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C.

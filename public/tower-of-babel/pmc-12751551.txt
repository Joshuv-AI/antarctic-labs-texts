Novel risk scoring system for predicting intravenous immunoglobulin resistance and coronary artery aneurysm in Thai children with Kawasaki disease
Authors: Poomrapee Tantikittipisut, Ankavipar Saprungruang, Roongroj Manatpreeprem, Parichat Khaosut
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study aimed to develop an alternative risk scoring system for intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) resistance and coronary artery aneurysm (CAA) development in patients with Kawasaki disease (KD) in Thai population.MethodsThis study is a retrospective and prospective study from January 2012 to September 2024. Data on demographics, clinical features, laboratory parameters, echocardiographic results, and outcomes were analyzed. Multivariable logistic regression was used to identify predictors for IVIG resistance and CAA.ResultsA total of 150 patients diagnosed with KD were enrolled. IVIG resistance occurred in 12.9% of cases. Independent predictors were neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) ≥ 3.5 (aOR 25.0, 95%CI 2.92–214.3), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte platelet ratio (NLPR) ≥ 1.8 (aOR 11.83, 95%CI 2.24–62.28), and total bilirubin (TB) ≥ 0.9 mg/dL (aOR 3.51, 95%CI 1.21–10.23). A new risk scoring system for IVIG resistance demonstrated an AUC of 0.84 (95% CI 0.74–0.95). CAA developed in 31.6% of KD cases. Independent predictors included age ≤ 6 months (aOR 2.77, 95%CI 1.02–7.50), illness duration before IVIG ≥ 10 days (aOR 5.31, 95%CI 2.09–13.49), and C-reactive protein (CRP) ≥ 10 mg/dL (aOR 2.54, 95%CI 1.08–5.97). A new CAA risk scoring system was developed with an AUC of 0.74 (95% CI 0.64–0.83).ConclusionsThis study provided practical tools to identify high risk patients and optimize KD management. NLR, NLPR and TB are strong predictors of IVIG resistance, while younger age, delayed IVIG treatment, and high CRP are key predictors of CAA.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12969-025-01175-3.

Introduction

Kawasaki disease (KD) is an acute self-limited vasculitis that predominantly affects children under the age of 5 years. First described in Japan, the etiology of KD remains unknown [1, 2]. Previous studies have suggested associations between KD and infectious diseases [3] as well as other genetic and environmental factors [2, 4]. The classic form of KD, or complete KD, is characterized by prolonged fever, a non-vesiculobullous rash, conjunctival injection, erythema or swelling of hands and feet, cervical lymphadenopathy, and mucocutaneous lesions such as cracked lips and a strawberry tongue, as defined by the 2024 American Heart Association (AHA) guidelines [5]. Patients with incomplete clinical characteristics are diagnosed using laboratory and echocardiographic data and classified as having incomplete KD. A significant complication of KD is the development of coronary artery aneurysms (CAA), which can lead to severe long-term cardiovascular consequences.

Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) is the mainstay treatment to prevent CAA development. Some patients develop IVIG resistance, defined as persistent or recurrent fever for 36 h following complete IVIG infusion. IVIG-resistant patients have a higher risk of CAA development, presenting a significant challenge in clinical practice [6]. IVIG resistance is a notable issue in managing KD worldwide. Makino et al. presented nationwide epidemiological data from Japan for 2015–2016 [7], showing that approximately 17.8% of KD patients experienced IVIG resistance. In Western countries, a study by Tremoulet et al. between 1998 and 2006 reported annual IVIG resistance rates ranging from 9.8% to 38.3% [8]. Studies conducted in Thailand by Chantasiriwan et al. [9] and Jarutach et al. [10] revealed IVIG resistance rates of 12.0% and 13.0%, respectively.

Immunologic abnormalities during the acute phase of KD reflect marked activation of the immune system leading to increased cytokine production. This produces an influx of neutrophils in the early stage (one week after onset), followed by a rapid transition to large mononuclear cells, lymphocytes and plasma cells, resulting in coronary artery destruction and the eventual development of fibrotic proliferation [11]. As a result, CAA then develops. This consequence underscores the critical importance of attenuating the inflammation process as soon as possible to prevent irreversible vascular damage. This window of opportunity offers a role for additional primary therapy in selected patients. According to standard KD guidelines [5, 12], intensification of treatment with IVIG plus adjunctive steroids or other immunosuppressive drugs is recommended for patients at high risk of IVIG resistance or CAA.

The Japanese scoring system called the Kobayashi [13], Egami [14], and Sano score [15] has been developed to identify patients at high risk of IVIG resistance. Data on its performance, particularly sensitivity and specificity are lower outside Japan [8–10, 16]. In Thailand, Chantasiriwan et al. (2018) studied IVIG-resistant KD patients and identified independent risk factors [9], although their scoring system has not yet been validated. The lack of a population-specific scoring system has hindered the intensification of initial treatment for KD, presenting a significant barrier to effective management and improved outcomes.

Regarding CAA, the prevalence in Asia remains higher than the global trend. The report by Chang et al. in 2020 [17] and Jarutach et al. in 2021 [10] showed that 34.8% and 27.3% of KD patients developed CAA, respectively, while Son et al. found a lower proportion of 13.0% from the North American cohort [18]. CAA is a serious complication of KD, requiring long-term cardiovascular monitoring and medication, particularly in patients with large aneurysms [11]. Assessing the risk factors of CAA development in specific populations is essential for optimizing treatment strategies. To escalate an initial treatment in high-risk patients, the 2024 updated AHA guidelines for KD to include a scoring system developed by Son et al. for the North American population [5]. However, a CAA-specific scoring system for Asian populations, including Thailand, is lacking which hinders treatment decisions.

The primary objective of this study was to establish a novel risk scoring system specifically designed to predict IVIG resistance and the development of CAA in the Thai population. Another purpose of this study was to evaluate the performance and clinical applicability of existing risk scoring systems in predicting these complications.

Methods

Study population and data collection

An ambidirectional cohort study of KD patients was conducted at King Chulalongkorn Memorial Hospital (KCMH), Bangkok, Thailand, from January 2012 to September 2024 (January 2012 to January 2024 for retrospective part, and February to September 2024 for prospective part). KD patients were recruited from inpatient (IPD) medical records with the International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision (ICD-10): M30.3 (Mucocutaneous Lymph Node Syndrome, Kawasaki Disease). The inclusion criteria were: (1) age at diagnosis between 0 and 15 years, and (2) diagnosis of complete or incomplete KD based on the 2017 AHA criteria. The exclusion criteria included: (1) patients diagnosed with other diseases presenting with Kawasaki-like symptoms (e.g. Multisystem inflammatory syndrome in children (MIS-C), infectious diseases) (2) patients with overlapping syndromes with other autoimmune conditions (e.g. juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA), other systemic vasculitis). The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Faculty of Medicine, Chulalongkorn University (Institutional Review Board (IRB) 0587/66). Data extracted for analysis included demographics, clinical features, laboratory parameters, echocardiography results, and outcomes.

Definitions

IVIG resistance was defined as persistent or recurrent fever, body temperature at least 38 degrees Celsius, and commencement of symptoms 36 h after the completion of IVIG treatment [11, 19]. Patients who did not receive IVIG or were treated with other medications such as steroids or immunosuppressive drugs as initial therapy were excluded from the analysis of IVIG resistance predictors to minimize the effect of confounding factors. CAA was defined as an aneurysm detected via echocardiography with a diameter >2.5 standard deviations (SD) above the age-adjusted mean. CAA was graded according to the Z-score classification outlined in the 2017 AHA guideline of KD. To confirm a negative echocardiographic finding, at least two echocardiographic evaluations, one during the acute phase and another at 4–6 weeks after fever onset, were required [11]. Patients lacking complete echocardiographic data during either the acute phase or the 4–6-week follow-up were excluded from the analysis of predictors for CAA development.

Statistical analyses

The data were compared between two groups: IVIG-resistant and non-IVIG-resistant, and CAA and non-CAA. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize continuous variables as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR), and categorical variables as percentages (%). Differences in continuous variables between groups were assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, while differences in categorical variables were evaluated using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Multivariable logistic regression was employed to identify predictors. Multivariable models were developed by adjusting for covariates with a p-value < 0.1 from univariable models, and stepwise backward logistic regression was used to select the final model. All p-values reported are two-sided, with statistical significance defined as p < 0.05. The score calculation used the method of dividing each coefficient by the lowest coefficient in the final model and rounding up when it exceeds 0.5. Data analysis was performed with Stata version 18.0 (StataCorp, College Station, Texas).

Results

Kawasaki disease: epidemiological and clinical characteristics

A total of 150 patients comprising 140 from the retrospective group and 10 from the prospective group were included in the study. Seventy-seven patients (51.3%) were female. The median age at diagnosis was 16.5 months (IQR 9–37 months). Most patients (87.3%) had no underlying medical conditions. The baseline characteristics of KD in the study are shown in Table 1. During the study period, 3 patients (2.0%) experienced a recurrence of KD, and no deaths were reported.

All patients presented with fever. Common additional symptoms included non-vesiculobullous rash (81.3%) and conjunctival injection (80.0%). IVIG treatment was administered to 147 patients (98.0%), with 19 patients (12.9%) showing resistance to IVIG. The median duration of illness before IVIG initiation was 7 days (IQR 6–9 days). Aspirin was prescribed to all KD patients. Steroids were administered to three patients - one patient prior to the first dose of IVIG due to autoimmune hemolytic anemia (AIHA) and two patients concurrently with IVIG. Of these, the patients who received steroids prior to IVIG developed IVIG resistance with one of the two patients receiving steroids concurrently with IVIG developing coronary artery aneurysms (CAA).

IVIG resistance

A total of 147 KD patients were treated with IVIG. Three patients were excluded due to steroid treatment before or concurrently with the first dose of IVIG. A total of 144 patients were included in the analysis of predictors of IVIG resistance. Eighteen patients were classified as IVIG-resistant (one patient who received steroids prior to the initial IVIG dosage was not included in the analysis), and the remaining 126 were classified as non-IVIG-resistant. Comparisons of the clinical features and laboratory data of IVIG-resistant and non-IVIG-resistant KD patients are shown in the online supplementary tables S1 and S2. Univariable analysis revealed that the clinical features significantly associated with IVIG resistance included non-vesiculobullous rash, desquamation of hands and feet, arthritis, and diarrhea. Laboratory data showed significant differences between the two groups including percentage of neutrophils, percentage of lymphocytes, absolute lymphocyte count (ALC), platelet count, NLPR, total bilirubin (TB), and direct bilirubin (DB).

The performance of the previous four scoring systems—Kobayashi, Egami, Sano, and Chantasiriwan et al.—in predicting IVIG resistance in KD is summarized in Table 2. The diagnostic performance of these scoring systems was evaluated using sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), positive likelihood ratio (LR+), negative likelihood ratio (LR-), and the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC). Among the scoring systems, the Chantasiriwan et al. score showed the highest sensitivity (81.3%, 95% CI 54.4–96), with an AUC of 0.72. The Kobayashi score had the high specificity (89.2%, 95% CI 82.2–94.1), and the highest AUC of 0.76. In contrast, the Egami score exhibited the lowest sensitivity (43.8%), with an AUC of 0.61.

Multivariable logistic regression analysis identified three independent predictors for IVIG resistance: neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) ≥ 3.5 (adjusted odds ratio [aOR] 25.0, 95% confidence interval [CI] 2.92–214.3), NLPR ≥ 1.8 (aOR 11.83, 95% CI 2.24–62.28), and TB ≥ 0.9 mg/dL (aOR 3.51, 95% CI 1.21–10.23) as shown in Table 3. Using these predictors, a new scoring system was developed to identify patients at high risk of IVIG resistance. The coefficients from the model were normalized to the highest value to assign scores: TB ≥ 0.9 was given a score of 1, NLR ≥ 3.5 a score of 3, and NLPR ≥ 1.8 a score of 2. The total possible score is 6, with higher scores indicating a greater risk of IVIG resistance as shown in Table 4. The model achieved an AUC of 0.84 (95% CI 0.74–0.95) and a ROC curve of the new score is shown in Fig. 1.

Coronary artery aneurysm

Among the 133 patients with follow-up echocardiograms, 42 patients (31.6%) developed CAA. Most cases occurred during the febrile phase (34 patients, 81.0%), while 8 patients (19.0%) developed CAA during the convalescent phase. The median time to develop CAA was 11 days (IQR 8–17 days) after the onset of fever. Of those with CAA, 22 patients (52.4%) had single-vessel involvement. Multi-vessel CAA developed in 21.4%, 11.9%, and 14.3% of patients with 2, 3, and 4 branch involvement, respectively. Most cases of CAA in our cohort were of small size, affecting 20 patients (47.6%). However, Giant CAA was observed in 21.4% of CAA patients. The most frequently affected coronary arteries were the right coronary artery (RCA) with involvement in 30 patients (71.4%). The left circumflex artery (LCx) was the least commonly affected artery, involved in 6 patients (14.3%).

Clinical and laboratory comparisons between the CAA and non-CAA groups are presented in online supplementary tables S3 and S4. Univariable analysis revealed significant differences between the two groups in clinical parameters, including days of illness before IVIG treatment, diagnostic classification of KD, presence of conjunctival injection, and IVIG resistance. Significant differences in laboratory features were observed for hemoglobin (Hb), hematocrit (Hct), white blood cell count (WBC), absolute neutrophil count (ANC), albumin, gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT), erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), and C-reactive protein (CRP).

Multivariable analysis shown in Table 5 identified three independent factors significantly associated with the development of CAA: age at diagnosis ≤ 6 months (aOR 2.77, 95% CI 1.02–7.50), illness duration before IVIG treatment ≥ 10 days (aOR 5.31, 95% CI 2.09–13.49), and CRP levels ≥ 10 mg/dL (aOR 2.54, 95% CI 1.08–5.97). These factors were incorporated into a newly developed scoring system to predict CAA risk as shown in Table 6. Age at diagnosis ≤ 6 months was assigned a score of 1. For pre-IVIG treatment, patients treated ≥ 10 days after symptom onset received a score of 2, and CRP levels ≥ 10 mg/dL was given a score of 1. The maximum total score is 4, with higher scores correlating with a greater risk of CAA. The model demonstrated an AUC of 0.74 (95% CI 0.64–0.83) and a ROC curve of the new score to predict CAA is shown in Fig. 2.

Discussion

This study offers comprehensive insights into the epidemiology, clinical features, and outcomes of KD, with a focus on identifying predictors of IVIG resistance and CAA. By analyzing our sample cohort, we have identified key patterns, predictors, and performance of diagnostic tools that can inform clinical practice and improve patient outcomes. A novel scoring system demonstrating high predictive accuracy for predicting IVIG resistance and CAA development was developed based on independent risk factors identified through multivariable logistic regression.

The demographic characteristics of our cohort were consistent with previously reported trends. The median age at diagnosis was 16.5 months, highlighting that KD predominantly impacts young children [20, 21]. While the male-to-female ratio in our cohort was relatively balanced, prior studies have noted a slight male predominance in KD cases [7, 9, 20, 21]. The observed sex distribution may be influenced by some population-specific factors unique to each study cohort. A total of only 2% of patients had a recurrence of KD, and no deaths were reported, reflecting the generally favorable prognosis of KD and efficacy of the current treatment protocol [22].

The proportion of incomplete KD in our cohort was higher than the previous report [23]. This finding underscores the importance of recognizing incomplete KD, which represents a substantial fraction of patients, to prevent diagnostic delays. Regarding treatment, IVIG resistance was identified in 12.9% of KD patients. This finding is in line with those of previous studies reported from other countries [7, 16, 24] and in Thailand [9, 10]. The management of IVIG-resistant cases in our study relied on additional doses of IVIG and steroids administered to some patients as adjunctive therapy. This approach aligns with international guidelines, which recommends an escalation of therapy for resistant cases [5, 12]. Identifying reliable predictors and high-performing scoring systems can further improve treatment decision-making in this subgroup.

The performance of existing scoring systems for predicting IVIG resistance was assessed in our cohort. The Chantasiriwan et al. score showed the highest sensitivity (81.3% with an AUC of 0.72), making it a useful tool for screening patients at risk of IVIG resistance. However, due to its low specificity (63.3%), it may be less effective identifying those who actually require intensified treatment, reducing its utility as a standalone screening tool. The Kobayashi, Egami and Sano scoring systems have good specificity (89.2%, 77.5% and 91.4%, respectively) but low sensitivity (62.5%, 43.8% and 57.1%, respectively) to screen high risk patients of developing IVIG resistance in our cohort. These results are consistent with the earlier research by Jarutach et al. in 2021, which showed that the applicability of the three Japanese scoring systems for predicting IVIG resistance in the Thai population may be limited.

Multivariable logistic regression identified three independent predictors of IVIG resistance: NLR ≥ 3.5, NLPR ≥ 1.8, and TB ≥ 0.9 mg/dL. These laboratory parameters correlated with findings from previous studies, reinforcing their potential as predictors for identifying patients at high risk of IVIG resistance. NLR has previously been reported as a biomarker for predicting IVIG resistance, reflecting a balance between inflammation and immune regulation [9, 25, 26]. High neutrophil count indicates acute inflammation, while lymphocyte count reflects immune regulatory responses [27]. In addition to these hematologic parameters, platelet count has been recognized as a predictor of inflammation. Thrombocytosis, or elevated platelet count, is a common feature in inflammatory diseases like KD. Contrary to expectations, studies by Kobayashi et al. [13], Egami et al. [14], and Chantasiriwan et al. [9] reported that lower platelet counts are associated with IVIG resistance. NLPR, an inflammatory marker derived from neutrophil, lymphocyte, and platelet counts, has been shown to be a prognostic factor in hyper-inflammatory conditions such as sepsis [28], hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) [29], and severe burns [30]. Recent studies also highlighted NLPR as a valuable predictor of IVIG resistance in Kawasaki disease [31]. Both NLR and NLPR serve as novel predictors of IVIG resistance and offer a cost-effective approach since they can be calculated from routine laboratory data. Regarding TB, this parameter has been included in the Sano score for predicting IVIG resistance [15]. Several studies have consistently reported an association between elevated total bilirubin levels and IVIG resistance, further supporting its predictive value [24, 32]. A possible explanation for this association might be that severe systemic inflammation can cause biliary tract inflammation and destroy hepatic sinusoidal circulation [11].

A new scoring system was established to classify KD patients at high risk of developing IVIG resistance, based on three independent predictors. This system demonstrated very good predictive performance, with an AUC of 0.84. The estimated risk correlated directly with the patient’s score, with higher scores indicating a greater likelihood of IVIG resistance. This tool enables risk stratification among KD patients, allowing for the early identification of individuals at risk and facilitating timely decisions to intensify initial treatment strategies. Further validation in larger, multicenter cohorts is necessary to confirm its clinical utility and refine its cutoff values for optimal application.

Regarding CAA, aneurysm was observed in a higher proportion than prior studies from the North American cohort [8, 18]. Most CAAs developed during the febrile phase, with a median onset of 11 days after fever onset, consistent with the natural progression of the disease to develop CAA in the second week after the onset of fever [11, 33]. Despite the median fever duration until the first IVIG treatment falling within the 10-day window period, several factors may contribute to the higher prevalence of CAA in our study: (1) The absence of a well-established CAA predicting tool contributed to the lack of intensified treatment for high-risk KD, (2) Asian race is a known risk factor for CAA development, as indicated by previous North American studies [18], and (3) our study was conducted at a large tertiary hospital in Thailand, where the referral of more complicated KD cases could have led to a higher CAA prevalence. The significant prevalence of giant CAA in our study is consistent with the previous report from Thailand by Santimahakullert et al. in 2022, which indicated that 28.8% of KD patients had giant CAA [34]. This finding highlights the critical need for diligent monitoring in KD management.

Multivariable analysis identified three independent predictors of CAA development including younger age at diagnosis, delayed IVIG treatment, and elevated CRP levels. Younger age at diagnosis may reflect a more severe immune response and high inflammatory markers in infants, contributing to vascular damage [18, 35–37]. A longer duration of fever is a well-established risk factor [9, 18, 24, 38–40]. The extended fever duration contributes to more inflammation and endothelial damage, and increases the likelihood of CAA. Lastly, the elevated CRP was one of the well-known predictors. High CRP may indicate a more intense inflammatory response, which increases the likelihood of coronary vessel injury and aneurysm formation [18, 24, 41].

To aid in clinical decision-making, a new scoring system was developed using these predictors. The score demonstrated moderate predictive accuracy, with an AUC of 0.74. Its simplicity and predictive percentages (ranging from 14% for a score of 0 to 84.2% for a score of 4) make it a practical tool for risk stratification. By incorporating readily available clinical and laboratory parameters, the score can help clinicians identify high-risk patients who may benefit from closer monitoring and additional therapeutic interventions. This could potentially prevent severe complications and improve long-term cardiovascular outcomes.

The strengths of this study include its comprehensive analysis of KD patients in other Asian races beyond Japan to develop an alternative scoring system for predicting IVIG resistance and CAA development. Several limitations must be acknowledged. The single-center retrospective design may limit the generalizability of our findings. The relatively small sample size in some analyses, particularly for IVIG-resistant cases, may have reduced statistical power and impacted the performance of the proposed score. Additionally, while the new risk score showed promising performance, we have not yet performed external validation and its applicability to other populations requires further validation. We recommend using our score to stratify the risk of developing poor outcomes in KD patients through individualized clinical decision-making.

Conclusion

This study provides valuable insights into the epidemiology, clinical characteristics, and outcomes of KD, with a focus on IVIG resistance and CAA. A novel scoring system based on readily accessible clinical and laboratory indicators was developed to assess the risk of IVIG resistance and CAA. To prevent a poorer prognosis, prompt initiation of appropriate treatment is warranted for this specific group of KD patients. By improving early identification of high-risk patients, these advances have the potential to optimize KD management and minimize the burden of complications in affected children.

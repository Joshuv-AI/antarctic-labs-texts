Rigidity of the hyperbolic marked energy spectrum and entropy for k k -surfaces
Sébastien Alvarez, Ben Lowe, Graham Smith

Abstract
. Labourie raised the question of determining the possible asymptotics for the growth rate of compact k k -surfaces, counted according to energy, in negatively curved 3 3 -manifolds, indicating the possibility of a theory of thermodynamical formalism for this class of surfaces. Motivated by this question and by analogous results for the geodesic flow, we prove a number of results concerning the asymptotic behavior of high energy k k -surfaces, especially in relation to the curvature of the ambient space. First, we determine a rigid upper bound for the growth rate of quasi-Fuchsian k k -surfaces, counted according to energy, and with asymptotically round limit set, subject to a lower bound on the sectional curvature of the ambient space. We also study the marked energy spectrum for k k -surfaces, proving a number of domination and rigidity theorems in this context. Finally, we show that the marked area and energy spectra for k k -surfaces in 3 3 -dimensional manifolds of negative curvature are asymptotic if and only if the sectional curvature is constant.

Introduction

1.1.
Context

This paper continues our study of the dynamical properties of the space of immersed surfaces of constant, positive extrinsic curvature - henceforth called
k
-surfaces - in closed, negatively-curved
3
-manifolds. The study of this space was initiated by Labourie in a remarkable series of papers
[
37
,
38
,
40
]
(see also
[
39
]
). There, building on Gromov’s theory of foliated Plateau problems elaborated in
[
22
,
23

, he showed that, for ambient manifolds of sectional curvature bounded above by
-1
, and for
0<k<1
, the space of marked
k
-surfaces exhibits many of the hyperbolic properties of the geodesic flow. More precisely:

(1)

it has a natural compactification which is laminated by Riemann surfaces;

(2)

it is independent of the ambient metric up to leaf-preserving homeomorphism, in analogy to Gromov’s geodesic rigidity theorem
[
24
]
(a local version was proven by Labourie in the geometrically finite case
[
38
]
, and a global version was recently proven in the general case by the third author in
[
58
]
);

(3)

the generic leaf is dense, and the union of closed leaves is also dense;

(4)

the lamination admits an infinite family of mutually singular, ergodic, totally invariant measures of full support obtained by a coding procedure.

In this vein, Labourie suggested a possible analogy between the thermodynamic properties of the geodesic flow in negative curvature and the thermodynamic properties of the space of
k
-surfaces
[
38
,
39
]
. More precisely, in Question
(ii)
of Section
1.2

[

, he posed the question of determining the possible asymptotic growth rates of the number of closed
k
-surfaces in a compact, negatively-curved
3
-manifold, counted according to their energies

1.2.
Marked area and energy spectra for
k
-surfaces

Before stating our main results, we first require a few definitions. Let
(X,h_{0}):=\mathbb{H}^{3}/\Pi
be a compact,
3
-dimensional hyperbolic manifold, where
\Pi
is a discrete, torsion-free subgroup of
\mathrm{Isom}^{+}(\mathbb{H}^{3})=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Given
C\geq 1
, we say that a compact surface subgroup
\Gamma
of
\Pi
is
C
-
quasi-Fuchsian
whenever its limit set is a
C
-quasicircle. The existence of large families of quasi-Fuchsian subgroups was proven by Kahn–Marković in
[
33
,
32
]
(see also
[
29
,
31
]
). Let
\mathrm{QF}(C)
denote the set of conjugacy classes of oriented
C
-quasi-Fuchsian subgroups of
\Pi
, and denote

\mathrm{QF}:=\bigcup_{C\geq 1}\mathrm{QF}(C)\ .

Let
h
be another riemannian metric on
X
of sectional curvature less than
-k
, for some
k>0
. It follows from the main results of
[
38
]
(see also
[
58
]
) that, for every oriented conjugacy class
[\Gamma]\in\mathrm{QF}
, there exists a unique closed
k
-surface in
X
representing
[\Gamma]
, which we henceforth denote by
S_{k,h}([\Gamma])
.

We find that our results are best contextualized by the following reformulation, in terms of the marked area spectrum, of the main result of
[
2
]
. We define the
marked (quasi-Fuchsian)
k
-surface area spectrum
to be the map
\text{MAS}_{k,h}:\mathrm{QF}\rightarrow]0,\infty[
given by

\text{MAS}_{k,h}([\Gamma]):=\mathrm{Area}_{h}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma]))\ .

The main result of
[
2
]
can now be expressed as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(Rigidity of the hyperbolic marked area spectra for
k
-surfaces)
.

Let
(X,h_{0}):=\mathbb{H}^{3}/\Pi
be a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold, and let
h
be another riemannian metric on
X
of sectional curvature bounded above by
-1
. For all
0<k<1
,

(1)

\mathrm{MAS}_{k,h}\leq\mathrm{MAS}_{k,h_{0}}\ ,

with equality holding if and only if
h
is hyperbolic.

Remark 1.2
.

In particular, by Mostow’s rigidity theorem
[
51
]
, equality holds if and only if
h
is isometric to
h_{0}
.

For any compact, locally strictly convex immersed surface
S\mathop{\subset}(X,h)
, and for all
p\geq 0
, we now define the
p
-energy
of
S
by

(2)

W^{p}_{h}(S):=\int_{S}H^{p}d\mathrm{Area}_{h}\ ,

where
H
denotes the mean curvature of
S
(which, by local strict convexity, we may take to be positive). Note that the
0
-energy of
S
is simply its area, whilst its
1
-energy is the area with respect to the Sasaki metric of its
Gauss lift
to the unit sphere bundle
S_{h}X
(see Section
2.1.1
). We define the
marked (quasi-Fuchsian)
k
-surface
p
-energy spectrum
to be the map
\text{MES}_{h}^{p}:\mathrm{QF}\rightarrow]0,\infty[
given by

(3)

\text{MES}_{h}^{p}([\Gamma]):=W^{p}_{h}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma]))\ .

In particular, the marked
0
-energy spectrum is just the marked area spectrum defined above. Our first main theorem is the following domination and rigidity result for marked energy spectra.

Theorem 1.3

(Domination by the hyperbolic marked
p
-energy spectrum)
.

Let
(X,h_{0}):=\mathbb{H}^{3}/\Pi
be a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold, and let
h
be another riemannian metric on
X
of sectional curvature pinched between
-1
and
-a
, for some
0<a<1
. For all
0<k<a
,

(4)

\mathrm{MES}^{p}_{k,h_{0}}\leq\mathrm{MES}^{p}_{k,h}\ ,

with equality holding if and only if
h
is hyperbolic.

Remark 1.4
.

Note that this domination result requires that the sectional curvature of
h
be bounded from
below
rather than from above, which has been the case for all previous results of this kind (c.f.
[
2
,
12
]
).

The proof makes use of the following straightforward but interesting fact:
in constant sectional curvature, nearly Fuchsian
k
-surfaces are almost totally umbilical
. It turns out that this property characterizes metrics of constant curvature. Indeed, we define the
normalized
p
-energy
of a closed
k
-surface
S\mathop{\subset}X
by

\overline{W}^{p}_{h}(S):=k^{-\frac{p}{2}}\int_{S}H^{p}d\mathrm{Area}_{h}\ .

For
p\neq q\geq 0
we say that the normalized marked
k
-surface
p
- and
q
-energy spectra are
asymptotic
whenever, for every sequence
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of positive numbers converging to
0
, and every sequence
(S_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of closed
(1+\varepsilon_{n})
-quasi-Fuchsian
k
-surfaces,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\overline{W}^{p}_{h}(S_{n})}{\overline{W}^{q}_{h}(S_{n})}=1\ .

Theorem 1.5

(Asymptotic energy spectra)
.

Let
(X,h)
be a closed
3
-manifold of sectional curvature bounded above by
-a
for some
a>0
. For all
0<k<a
, the following properties are equivalent.

(1)

h
has constant sectional curvature;

(2)

for all
p\neq q\geq 0
the normalized marked
k
-surface
p-
and
q
-energy spectra of
k
-surfaces are asymptotic; and

(3)

for some
p\neq q\geq 0
the normalized marked
k
-surface
p-
and
q
-energy spectra of
k
-surfaces are asymptotic.

In particular,
h
has constant sectional curvature if and only if its normalized marked
k
-surface energy and area spectra are asymptotic.

We point out that besides the curvature upper bound of
-a
, which is necessary to ensure that
k
-surfaces exist in the first place, the previous theorem, unlike the other theorems stated before, has no assumptions on sectional curvature.

1.3.
Area and energy entropies for
k
-surfaces

The method of proofs of the results above are similar to those of
[
2
]
. We use the solution of foliated Plateau problems, and the equidistribution properties of closed quasi-Fuchsian
k
-surfaces. This is the method outlined by Labourie in his influential Bourbaki seminar
[
41
]
in the case of minimal surfaces.

These results can also be phrased in terms of counting. We will define in §
5.1.3
an
entropy
similar to those appearing in
[
2
,
12
]
. It is actually analogous to the
modified entropy
defined by Marques-Neves in
[
50
]
and is a number, denoted by
\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k,\hat{m}}(X,h)
, that counts, according to their
p
-energies, the closed quasi-Fuchsian
k
-surfaces whose limit quasicircles becomes more and more round
and equidistribute
to
\hat{m}
, the unique ergodic fully supported
conformal current in the space
{\mathcal{C}}^{+}
of round circles
. We refer to §
2.3.1
for the definition of conformal currents as defined by Labourie in
[
41
]
(see also
[
2
,
50
]
) and to §
5.1.3
for the definition of entropy. The analogue of Theorem
1.3
for entropy is a rigid inequality.

Theorem 1.6

(Rigid inequality)
.

Let
(X,h_{0})
be a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold and
h
be a Riemannian metric on
X
with sectional curvature
-1\leq\mathrm{sect}_{h}\leq-a
for some
a>0
. Let
k\in(0,a)
and
p\geq 0
. Then
\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k,\hat{m}}(X,h)\leq\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k,\hat{m}}(X,h_{0})
and the equality holds if and only if
h
and
h_{0}
are isometric.

The analogue of Theorem
1.3
is the following theorem proving entropy rigidity (we refer to §
5.2.2
for the definition of
\mathrm{Ent}^{\mathrm{Area}}_{k,\hat{m}}(X,h)
, the modified area entropy for
k
-surfaces).

Theorem 1.7

(Entropy rigidity)
.

Let
(X,h)
be a closed
3
-manifold with sectional curvature
\mathrm{sect}_{h}\leq-a
for some
0<a
. Let
k\in(0,a)
. Then the following properties are equivalent.

(1)

The sectional curvature of
h
is constant.

(2)

For some
p\neq q\geq 0
we have

k^{-p/2}\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k,\hat{m}}(X,h)=k^{-q/2}\mathrm{Ent}^{q}_{k,\hat{m}}(X,h).

(3)

The modified area entropy and modified
p
-energy entropy coincide for some
p\neq 0

\mathrm{Ent}^{\mathrm{Area}}_{k,\hat{m}}(X,h)=k^{-p/2}\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k,\hat{m}}(X,h).

These results give the first counting result of closed
k
-surfaces according to their energy, which we view as partialy addressing Labourie’s original question
[
38
, Question (ii) p.243]
.

We comment that the previous two results continue to hold with the modified entropies replaced by the standard entropies that count
k
-surfaces whose limit sets become more and more round but without an equidistribution condition, under a topological condition on
X
: that
X
contain no closed totally geodesic surfaces in its hyperbolic metric (see Question
1.10
, Remark
5.9
). Examples of closed hyperbolic 3-manifolds without closed totally geodesic surfaces are given in
[
49
]
. Let us note that many hyperbolic knot complements have no totally geodesic surfaces: see
[
6
,
55
]
and references therein.

1.4.
Background and Future Directions

1.4.1.
Marked length spectrum and domination

Our work is motivated in part by the theory developed around
marked length spectrum rigidity.
Recall that, for any closed
3
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(X,h)
with negative sectional curvature, we define its marked length spectrum to be the function
\text{MLS}_{h}
which associates to every conjugacy class of
\Pi=\pi_{1}(X)
the length of its geodesic representative. The following conjecture (see
[
11
, Problem 3.1]
) remains open.

Conjecture 1.8

(Rigidity of the marked length spectrum)
.

Let
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
be two Riemannian metrics with negative sectional curvature on a closed manifold
X
. Then
\mathrm{MLS}_{h_{1}}=\mathrm{MLS}_{h_{2}}
if and only if
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are isometric, that is, if and only if there exists a diffeomorphism
\phi:X\to X
such that
\phi^{\ast}h_{2}=h_{1}
.

This conjecture has been proved for surfaces by Otal
[
53
]
(see also
[
14
]
for results in non-positive curvature). In dimension
3
it has been established in two particular cases

(1)

if one of the metrics has constant sectional curvature: see
[
8
,
28
]
;

(2)

if the two metrics are close enough: see
[
26
]
.

Recently, some variations on the marked length spectrum rigidity problem have been considered. For example there is the problem of understanding the consequences of the property of
domination
of marked length spectra
\text{MLS}_{h}\geq\text{MLS}_{h^{\prime}}
. Conjecturally this should imply that for two metrics
h
and
h^{\prime}
of negative sectional curvatures, with the sectional curvatures of
h
bounded below by those of
h^{\prime}
, that
\mathrm{Vol}(X,h)\geq\mathrm{Vol}(X,h^{\prime})
(see
[
15
,
16
]
as well as
[
21
]
for related rigidity results). This domination property was studied in the context of representation of surface groups in
[
18
,
25
]
and later generalized to other contexts, such as harmonic maps, higher Teichmüller theory and partial hyperbolicity
[
4
,
5
,
17
,
45
,
56
]
.

A direct analogue of marked length spectrum rigidity for
k
-surfaces is marked energy spectrum rigidity for
\pi_{1}
-injective
k
-surfaces, that is, where we consider the function which assigns to each conjugacy class of surface subgroups of
\Pi
the energy of the corresponding
k
-surface in the given negatively curved metric on
X
. Establishing marked energy spectrum rigidity even infinitesimally is an interesting and hard problem. If instead of the energy, one considers the map which assigns to each conjugacy class of surface subgroups of
\Pi
the quotient of the
p
and
q
-energies of the corresponding
k
-surface, for some
p\neq q
, then Theorem
1.5
implies the corresponding marked spectral rigidity statement for metrics of negative sectional curvature, one of which has constant curvature.

1.4.2.
Thermodynamical formalism for
k
-surfaces

The marked length spectrum rigidity and domination results for the geodesic flow fit into the theory of Hölder cocycles developed by Ledrappier and Hamenstädt
[
28
,
43
]
. In this theory, one associates two objects to every real-valued Hölder function
F
over the sphere bundle
S_{h}X
, henceforth called a
potential
. The first is a function, called the
spectrum
, mapping every conjugacy class of
\Pi
to the integral of
F
over its unique geodesic representative, and the second is a probability measure, defined by the
variational principle
, and invariant under the geodesic flow, which we refer to as an
equilibrium state
. By Livšic’s theorem (see
[
27
,
46
]
), up to a suitable normalization, two potentials have the same periods if and only if they are cohomologous, which in turn holds if and only if they have the same equilibrium states.

It is classical to consider three potentials in
S_{h}X
: the constant potential (associated to the maximal entropy measure), the geometric potential (associated to the Liouville measure) and the harmonic potential (associated to the harmonic measure). In dimension
2
, it is known (see
[
35
,
36
,
42
]
) that if any two of these three potentials have the same periods then the ambient metric has constant curvature (the only known results in higher dimension hold for perturbations of hyperbolic metrics and are due to Flaminio
[
20
]
and Humbert
[
30
]
).

It is natural to ask whether there is a similar theory of thermodynamical formalism applying to
k
-surfaces containing Theorems
1.5
and
1.7
. Indeed, a similar question is raised by Labourie in
[
39
, Question 8]
. A concrete starting point for such a theory would be the analogue of Livšic’s theorem in the context of
k
-surfaces.

Question 1.9
.

Let
X
be a closed negatively curved manifold, let
F
be a Hölder function defined on the space of marked frame bundles
FS
of quasi-Fuchsian
k
-surfaces
S
of
X
. Assume that for every quasi-Fuchsian
k
-surface
S
the integral of
F
over
FS
vanishes. What can be concluded about
F
?

This question seems to belong to a cohomological theory of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-actions, though it is not immediately clear what should be considered as a coboundary in this context. One of the main difficulties is the absence in the case of
k
-surfaces of a theory of hyperbolic dynamics, which has proven invaluable in the study of the geodesic flow. A key challenge lies in identifying what may serve as a substitute.

The main respect in which our work is less general than the setting proposed by Labourie’s question is that we focus on quasi-Fuchsian surfaces which, in particular, are
\pi_{1}
-injective. It would be interesting to determine the extent to which the results of this paper hold for entropy functionals defined using larger families of compact
k
-surfaces.

1.4.3.
Closed totally umbilical surfaces

Finally we state a question whose affirmative answer would allow us to extend the results of Section
5.1.3
to the modified entropy functionals studied there.

Question 1.10
.

Let
X
be a closed hyperbolizable riemannian 3-manifold with negative sectional curvature greater than or equal to
-1
. Suppose that

contains a closed totally umbilical surface

with mean curvature

and with constant curvature
-1+k
in its intrinsic metric (that is, the sectional curvature of

For

, an affirmative answer follows from
[

.

2.
Foliated Plateau problems and invariant measures

2.1.
Foliated Plateau problems and actions of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})

2.1.1.
Setting and definitions

Throughout the sequel,

will denote its universal cover, and

will denote its fundamental group. We identify
\tilde{X}
with hyperbolic
3

, and we identify
\Pi
with a discrete subgroup of
\mathrm{Isom}^{+}(\mathbb{H}^{3})=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.
\tilde{X}=\mathbb{H}^{3}
is naturally compactified by its ideal boundary

{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

\tilde{X}\cup{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

By Mostow’s rigidity theorem
[

]
,

is unique up to diffeomorphism. Our aim is to characterize this metric within the space of riemannian metrics on

of sectional curvature pinched between
-b
and
-a
, for
0<a<b
. Let
h
be such a riemannian metric, and let

also denote its lift to the universal cover
\tilde{X}
.

We will be interested in the following two bundles over
\tilde{X}
.

—

The unit sphere bundle

S_{h}\tilde{X}\mathop{\subset}T\tilde{X}
, whose fibre at the point

is

S_{h}\tilde{X}_{x}:=\{v\ |\ \|v\|_{h}=1\}\ .

—

The frame bundle

F_{h}\tilde{X}\mathop{\subset}T\tilde{X}\oplus T\tilde{X}\oplus T\tilde{X}
, whose fibre at the point

F_{h}\tilde{X}_{x}:=\{\xi:=(v,e_{1},e_{2})\ |\ (v,e_{1},e_{2})\ \text{oriented and $h$-orthonormal}\}\ .

There is natural projection

making

into a bundle over

Given an oriented, embedded disk

\hat{D}\mathop{\subset}S_{h}\tilde{X}

Given a point

, we define a

of

to be a triple
\xi:=(n(p),e_{1},e_{2})
, where
(e_{1},e_{2})

, whilst the frame bundle is naturally diffeomorphic to the unit circle bundle

.

We define a

in
\tilde{X}
to be a pair
(D,p)

is an oriented disk, and

. It will be convenient to identify each marked disk

with its marked
Gauss lift

(\hat{D},n(p))
. Likewise, we define a

of a disk

(FD,\xi)

\xi:=(n(p),e_{1},e_{2})

2.1.2.
The space of

-disks

Fix
0<k<a
. We define a
k
-disk

to be a smoothly embedded, oriented disk

, whose extrinsic curvature with respect to

, and whose closure in

is a closed topological disk. Note that since

\mathrm{KD}^{+}_{h,k}

Recall that the

\kappa_{int}=\kappa_{ext}+\mathrm{sect}_{h}|_{TD}\ .

In particular, the intrinsic curvature of any

so that every

2.1.3.
Asymptotic Plateau problem

We denote by
\mathrm{JC}^{+}

. We will require the following result from

(in the geometrically finite case) and
[

]
(in the general case).

Theorem 2.1

(Asymptotic Plateau problem)
.

For every

, and for every

2.1.6.
Laminations, simultaneous uniformization, and the action of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})

Since

(see §

), they are uniformized by the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. The space
\text{MKD}_{k,h}(C)
therefore carries a natural lamination by hyperbolic surfaces, whose leaves are the preimages of
\partial_{\infty}
, and which we denote by
{\mathcal{L}}_{k,h}
. Since
\Pi
sends leaves to leaves, the quotient
\text{MKD}_{k,h}(C)/\Pi
also carries a hyperbolic surface lamination which, furthermore, is compact (see
[
37
]
). We denote this lamination also by
{\mathcal{L}}_{k,h}
when no ambiguity arises. In a similar manner, the space
\mathrm{FKD}_{k,c}(C)
carries a natural lamination by
3
-manifolds, whose leaves are likewise the preimages of
\partial_{\infty}
, and which we denote by
F{\mathcal{L}}_{k,h}
. We also denote the natural lamination of the quotient
\mathrm{FKD}_{k,c}(C)/\Pi
by
F{\mathcal{L}}_{k,h}
when no ambiguity arises. Finally, note that, in both cases, the leaves of
F{\mathcal{L}}_{k,h}
are the unit circle bundles of the leaves of
{\mathcal{L}}_{k,h}
.

Let
\mathrm{UKD}_{k,h}(C)
denote the space of uniformizing maps of oriented
k
-disks inside
\tilde{X}
(see
[
2
, Section 3]
). We identify
\mathrm{FKD}_{k,h}(C)
with
\mathrm{UKD}_{k,h}(C)
as follows. First, we identify
\mathbb{H}^{2}
with the upper half-space in
\mathbb{C}
in the standard manner, and we define the oriented frame
(e_{1}^{0},e_{2}^{0})
of this space at the point
i:=\sqrt{-1}
by

e_{1}^{0}:=1\qquad\text{and}\qquad e_{2}^{0}:=i\ .

Given a
k
-disk
D
and a frame
\xi:=(n(p),e_{1},e_{2})\in FD
, we define
u_{\xi}:\mathbb{H}^{2}\rightarrow D
to be the unique uniformizing map such that

u_{\xi}(i)=p\qquad\text{and}\qquad Du_{\xi}(e_{1}^{0})\propto e_{1}\ .

The map
\xi\mapsto u_{\xi}
trivially defines a bijection from
\mathrm{FKD}_{k,h}(C)
into
\mathrm{UKD}_{k,h}(C)
. By Candel’s simultaneous uniformization theorem
[
13
]
(in particular, the version given in
[
3
, Theorem 2.7]
), this identification is in fact a homeomorphism. Furthermore, this identification is smooth over every fibre, and varies continuously in the
C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
sense transversally to every fibre.

The above identification allows us to define a right action of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathrm{FKD}_{k,h}(C)
. Indeed, for all
g\in\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, there exists a unique function
\alpha_{k}(g):\mathrm{FKD}_{k,h}(C)\rightarrow\mathrm{FKD}_{k,h}(C)
such that, for every
k
-disk
D
, and for every frame
\xi=(n(p),e_{1},e_{2})\in FD
,

u_{\xi}\circ g=u_{\alpha_{k}(g)(\xi)}\ .

More explicitely,

\alpha_{k}(g)(\xi)=(n(q),e_{1}^{\prime},e_{2}^{\prime})\ ,

where

q:=u_{\xi}(g(0))\qquad\text{and}\qquad e_{1}^{\prime}\propto Du_{\xi}\cdot Dg\cdot e_{1}^{0}\ .

It is straightforward to show that this action of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathrm{FKD}_{k,h}(C)
is free, transitive, and commutes with the left action of
\Pi
on this space. Furthermore, by Candel’s simultaneous uniformization theorem again, this action is continuous, and
\alpha_{k}(g)
is a homeomorphism for all
g
. Furthermore, as before, this action is also smooth over every fibre, and varies continuously in the
C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
sense transversally to every fibre.

Using this action of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathrm{FKD}_{k,h}(C)
, in
[
2
, Theorems 3.1.2. and 3.2.2]
we proved the following result.

Theorem 2.2

(Fibered Plateau problem)
.

For all
C\geq 1
, the map

\partial_{\infty}:\mathrm{FKD}_{k,h}(C)\to\mathrm{QC}^{+}(C)\ ,

is a continuous principal
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-bundle over
\mathrm{QC}^{+}(C)
, and the map

\partial_{\infty}:\mathrm{MKD}_{k,h}(C)\to\mathrm{QC}^{+}(C)\ ,

is a continuous disk-bundle, which is an associated bundle of
\mathrm{FKD}_{k,h}(C)
.

2.1.7.
Foliated Plateau problem

Consider now the case where
C=1
. Let
{\mathcal{C}}^{+}=\text{QC}^{+}(1)
denote the set of oriented round circles in
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. In this case, the spaces
\mathrm{FKD}_{k,h}(1)
and
\mathrm{MKD}_{k,h}(1)
identify respectively with the frame bundle
F_{h}\tilde{X}
and the sphere bundle
S_{h}\tilde{X}
, as the following theorem shows.

Theorem & Definition 2.3

(Foliated Plateau problem)
.

The Gauss lifts of oriented
k
-disks spanned by round circles of
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
form a smooth foliation
{\mathcal{F}}_{k,h}
of
S_{h}\tilde{X}
, and their frames form a smooth foliation
F{\mathcal{F}}_{k,h}
of
F_{h}\tilde{X}
. Furthermore, these foliations are
\Pi
-invariant.

We call the above foliations the
k
-
surface foliation
of
S_{h}\tilde{X}
and the
k
-
frame foliation
of
F_{h}\tilde{X}
respectively. By
\Pi
-invariance, they descend respectively to foliations of
S_{h}X
and
F_{h}X
whose respective leaves have dimension
2
and
3
. The above theorem has the following consequences.

(1)

The laminated space
(\mathrm{MKD}_{k,h}(1),{\mathcal{L}}_{k,h})
identifies with the
k
-surface foliation of the sphere bundle
(S_{h}\tilde{X},{\mathcal{F}}_{k,h})
. This identification is equivariant under the natural left action of
\Pi
.

(2)

The laminated space
(\mathrm{FKD}_{k,h}(1),F{\mathcal{L}}_{k,h})
identifies with the foliation of the frame bundle
(F_{h}\tilde{X},F{\mathcal{F}}_{k,h})
. This identification is also equivariant by the natural left actions of
\Pi
.

(3)

F_{h}\tilde{X}
inherits a free right-action of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, which we also denote by
\alpha_{k}
. The orbit foliation of this action identifies with the
k
-frame foliation
F{\mathcal{F}}_{k,h}
.

We emphasize the strong analogies between the preceding constructions and those arising from geodesics. Indeed, we view the action of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on
F_{h}\tilde{X}
as a higher-dimensional analogue of the geodesic flow. We likewise view the
k
-surface foliation of
S_{h}\tilde{X}
as a higher-dimensional analogue of the geodesic foliation of this manifold. This analogy is further justified, for example, by the fact that the
k
-surface foliation is independent of the metric in the following sense. For
k<a
, if
h
and
h^{\prime}
are two negatively curved metrics on
X
with sectional curvatures bounded above by
-a<0
, then there exists a canonical homeomorphism of
S_{h}\tilde{X}
conjugating the
k
-surface foliations (see
[
2
, Theorem 2.1.5.]
). We view this as the analogue in the present context of the
geodesic rigidity theorem
proven by Gromov in
[
24
]
.

Remark 2.4
.

The analogous foliated Plateau problem for minimal surfaces has a solution for perturbations
h
of the hyperbolic metric, as well as a slightly larger neighborhood of the hyperbolic metric subject to strong curvature conditions (see,
[
22
,
47
]
). However, such foliations do not always exist. Indeed, in
[
47
]
, the second author constructs negatively-curved metrics

for which
S_{h}\tilde{X}
admits no foliation by lifts of minimal surfaces equivalent to the totally geodesic foliation of the unit tangent bundle of the hyperbolic metric by a leaf-preserving homeomorphism. It remains an open problem to determine necessary and sufficient conditions under which the foliated Plateau problem for minimal surfaces admits a solution.

Remark 2.5
.

We note that the above laminated spaces are all sublaminations of the laminated space of all suitably complete
k
-surfaces studied by Labourie in
[
37
,
38
,
40
]
(see also
[
58
]
). These sublaminations arise naturally in the study of quasi-Fuchsian

-surfaces.

2.1.8.
The homogeneous case

denotes the Haar measure on

(resp. the hyperbolic area of

). Borel regular

(resp.

) are trivially in one-to-one correspondence with Borel regular measures on
\mathrm{QC}^{+}
(see
[

, Lemma 4.2.1.]
). We call

the

of

of

-invariant measure

is also

In other words, we have natural bijective correspondences between each the following classes of objects (see

(1)

\Pi
-invariant Borel regular measures

on the space

of oriented

(2)

The area measure.

In this section we study some geometric properties of

-energy of such a surface, and we prove a

[

of Seppi, namely that almost-Fuchsian

-surfaces in hyperbolic

3.1.
p
-energy of surfaces

Recall that
(X,h)
is a closed
3
-dimensional riemannian manifold of sectional curvature pinched between
-b
and
-a
, where
0<a<b
. Given a closed, oriented immersed surface
S\mathop{\subset}X
, and a real number
p\geq 0
, we define the
p
-energy
of
S
by

W_{h}^{p}(S):=\int_{S}H^{p}d\mathrm{Area}_{h}\ ,

and we define its
normalized
p
-energy
by

\overline{W}_{h}^{p}(S):=k^{-p/2}W_{h}^{p}(S)=k^{-p/2}\int_{S}H^{p}d\mathrm{Area}_{h}\ .

Lemma 3.1
.

If
-1\leq\mathrm{sect}_{h}\leq-a
,
0<k<a
,
p\geq 0
, then, for every closed
k
-surface
S
,

(1)

\mathrm{Area}_{h}(S)\geq 2\pi|\chi(S)|/(1-k)
with equality holding if and only if the sectional curvature of
h
equals
-1
along
TS
;

(2)

W_{h}^{p}(S)\geq k^{p/2}\mathrm{Area}_{h}(S)
with equality holding if and only if
S
is totally umbilical.

Proof.

Indeed, by Gauss’ equation and the Gauss–Bonnet theorem,

2\pi\chi(S)=\int_{S}\kappa_{int}d\mathrm{Area}_{h}\geq\int_{S}(-1+k)d\mathrm{Area}_{h}=\mathrm{Area}_{h}(S)(k-1)\ .

Hence

\mathrm{Area}_{h}(S)\geq\frac{2\pi\left|\chi(S)\right|}{(1-k)}\ ,

with equality holding if and only if
\mathrm{sect}_{h}=-1
over
TS
. This proves the first assertion.

Since the surfaces studied here are oriented in such a way that their principal curvatures are always positive, the algebraic-geometric mean inequality yields

H\geq k^{\frac{1}{2}}\ ,

so that

W_{h}^{p}(S)\geq k^{p/2}\mathrm{Area}_{h}(S)\ .

Furthermore, equality holds if and only if
H
is constant and equal to
k^{\frac{1}{2}}
, that is, if and only if
S
is totally umbilical, and this proves the second assertion.
∎

3.2.
Almost totally umbilical
k

Consider first the case where

is the hyperbolic metric on

. Fix

For all

The first inequality follows immediately from the algebraic-geometric mean inequality.

We first prove the upper bound

\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k}(X,h_{0})\leq\frac{k^{-\frac{p}{2}}(1-k)}{2\pi}\ .

Indeed, by Lemma
3.1
, for every
[\Gamma]\in\mathrm{QF}(1+\varepsilon)
,

W_{h_{0}}^{p}(S_{k,h_{0}}([\Gamma]))\geq k^{\frac{p}{2}}\mathrm{Area}_{h_{0}}(S_{k,h_{0}}([\Gamma]))=\frac{4\pi(g-1)k^{\frac{p}{2}}}{(1-k)}\ ,

where
g
denotes the genus of
S_{k,h_{0}}([\Gamma])
. It follows that if

W_{h_{0}}^{p}(S_{k,h_{0}}([\Gamma]))\leq w\ ,

then

g\leq\frac{k^{-\frac{p}{2}}(1-k)w}{4\pi}+1\ ,

and the upper bound now follows by Kahn-Marković’s topological counting result (Theorem
2.7
).

We now use Lemma
3.2
to bound the entropy from below

\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k}(X,h_{0})\geq\frac{k^{-\frac{p}{2}}(1-k)}{2\pi}\ .

Fix
\delta>0
and let
\varepsilon_{0}>0
be as in Lemma
3.2
. For all
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and for all
[\Gamma]\in\mathrm{QF}(1+\varepsilon)
,

W^{p}_{h_{0}}(S_{k,h_{0}}([\Gamma])\leq(1+\delta)^{p}\frac{4\pi(g-1)k^{\frac{p}{2}}}{(1-k)}\ .

It follows that if

g\leq\frac{(1-k)wk^{-{\frac{p}{2}}}}{4\pi(1+\delta)^{p}}+1\ ,

then

W_{h_{0}}^{p}(S_{k,h_{0}}([\Gamma]))\leq w\ ,

and, proceeding as before, we show that

\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k}(X,h_{0})\geq\frac{(1-k)k^{-\frac{p}{2}}}{2\pi(1+\delta)^{p}}\ .

Since
\delta
is arbitrary, the result follows.
∎

Note that in order to obtain the upper bound in the previous proof, we only used the inequality
\mathrm{sect}_{h}\geq-1
. We therefore also have the following result.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(X,h_{0})
be a closed,
3
-dimensional hyperbolic manifold. Let
h
be another riemannian metric on
X
of sectional curvature pinched between
-1
and
-a
, for some
0<a<1
. For all
p\geq 0
, and for all
0<k<a
,

\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k}(X,h)\leq\frac{k^{-\frac{p}{2}}(1-k)}{2\pi}=\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k}(X,h_{0})\ .

5.1.2.
Entropy and closed Fuchsian
k
-surfaces

It is worth noting that the
k
-surface energy entropy counts closed
conjugacy classes
of surface subgroups. This has the following consequence.

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume that
-1\leq\mathrm{sect}_{h}\leq-a
,
p\geq 0
and
0<k<a
. Assume furthermore that there exists a closed
Fuchsian
surface subgroup
\Gamma\leq\Pi
such that

W^{p}_{h}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma]))=W^{p}_{h_{0}}(S_{k,h_{0}}([\Gamma]))\ .

Then

\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k}(X,h)=\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k}(X,h_{0}).

Remark 5.4
.

If
p>0
, one can check that
W^{p}_{h}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma]))=W^{p}_{h_{0}}(S_{k,h_{0}}([\Gamma]))
if and only if
[\Gamma]
is represented in
(M,h)
by a totally umbilic surface with constant mean curvature
k^{1/2}
(compare Question
1.10
.)

The theorem relies on Lemma
5.6
, below, which is a consequence of Formula (3) of
[
52
]
. We first require the following definition.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a surface subgroup of
\Pi
. We denote by
\sigma(\Gamma)
the set of
\Pi
-conjugacy classes of finite-index subgroups of
\Gamma
. For every
[\Gamma^{\prime}]\in\sigma(\Gamma)
, we denote by
\mathrm{g}(\Gamma^{\prime})
the genus of any subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}
representing the class.

Lemma 5.6

(Müller–Puchta’s lower bound)
.

For every compact surface subgroup
\Gamma
of
\Pi
,

(16)

\liminf_{g\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\big(\#\{[\Gamma^{\prime}]\in\sigma(\Gamma)\ |\ \mathrm{g}(\Gamma^{\prime})\leq g\}\big)}{2g\log(g)}\geq 1\ .

Proof.

We provide a proof of this estimate since
[
52
]
in fact counts
subgroups
, not
conjugacy classes
. For convenience, we suppose that
\Gamma
is the stabilizer in
\Pi
of its boundary curve
\partial_{\infty}\Gamma
.

Upon applying Stirling’s approximation to Equation (3) of
[
52
]
, we find that

\liminf_{g\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\big(\#\{\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma\ |\ \mathrm{g}(\Gamma^{\prime})\leq g\}\big)}{2g\log(g)}\geq 1\ .

In terms of the Euler characteristic, this becomes

\liminf_{g\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\big(\#\{\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma\ |\ \left|\chi(\Gamma^{\prime})\right|\leq x\}\big)}{x\log(x)}\geq 1\ .

Let
x_{0}:=\left|\chi(\Gamma)\right|
denote the absolute value of the Euler characteristic of
\Gamma
. Let
\Gamma^{\prime}
be a finite-index subgroup of
\Gamma
with

\left|\chi(\Gamma^{\prime})\right|\leq x\ .

Then
\Gamma^{\prime}
corresponds to an
N
-fold cover, where

N\leq x/x_{0}\ .

We now claim that the number of
\Pi
-conjugates of
\Gamma^{\prime}
in
\Gamma
is bounded above by
(x/x_{0})
. Indeed, let
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
be a
\Pi
-conjugate of
\Gamma^{\prime}
in
\Gamma
. Let
\gamma\in\Pi
be such that
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}=\gamma\Gamma^{\prime}\gamma^{-1}
. Since
\Gamma^{\prime}
and
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
are both finite-index subgroups of
\Gamma
,
\gamma
preserves
\partial_{\infty}\Gamma
, and is therefore an element of
\mathrm{Stab}(\partial_{\infty}\Gamma)=\Pi
. It follows that
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
is in fact a
\Gamma
-conjugate of
\Gamma^{\prime}
. However, the number of distinct
\Gamma
-conjugates of
\Gamma^{\prime}
in
\Gamma
is bounded above by the index of
\Gamma^{\prime}
in
\Gamma
. By considering fundamental domains, we see that this index is bounded above by
N\leq(x/x_{0})
, and the assertion follows. Consequently,

\#\{[\Gamma^{\prime}]\in\sigma(\Gamma)\ |\ \left|\chi(\Gamma^{\prime})\right|\leq x\}\geq\#\{\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma\ |\ \left|\chi(\Gamma^{\prime})\right|\leq x\}/(x/x_{0})\ .

Hence

\liminf_{g\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\big(\#\{[\Gamma^{\prime}]\in\sigma(\Gamma)\ |\ \left|\chi(\Gamma^{\prime})\right|\leq x\}\big)}{x\log(x)}\geq 1\ ,

and the result follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.3
.

It suffices to prove that

\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k}(X,h)\leq\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k}(X,h_{0})\ ,

since the reverse inequality holds by Lemma
5.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Fuchsian surface subgroup of
\Pi
and suppose that

W^{p}_{h}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma]))=W^{p}_{h_{0}}(S_{k,h_{0}}([\Gamma]))=\frac{4\pi k^{\frac{p}{2}}}{(1-k)}\mathrm{g}(\Gamma)\ ,

where the last equality follows from Lemma
3.1
. By Lemma
3.1
again, the surface
S=S_{k,h}([\Gamma])
must be totally umbilical with constant mean curvature equal to
k^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Let
[\Gamma^{\prime}]
be an element of
\sigma(\Gamma)
, as in Definition
5.5
. Any subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}
representing the class is quasi-Fuchsian with
\partial_{\infty}\Gamma=\partial_{\infty}\Gamma^{\prime}
, so that
[\Gamma^{\prime}]\in\mathrm{QF}(1+\varepsilon)
for all
\varepsilon>0
, and

S_{k,h}([\Gamma^{\prime}])=\mathrm{D}_{k,h}(\partial_{\infty}\Gamma)/\Gamma^{\prime}\ .

In particular,
S_{k,h}([\Gamma^{\prime}])
is just a finite cover of
S_{k,h}([\Gamma])
, and is therefore also totally umbilical with constant mean curvature equal to
k^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Consequently, by Lemma
3.1
,

W^{p}_{h}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma^{\prime}]))=\frac{4\pi k^{\frac{p}{2}}}{(1-k)}\mathrm{g}(\Gamma^{\prime})\ .

We conclude that, for every
\varepsilon,w>0
,

\#\{[\Gamma^{\prime}]\in\mathrm{QF}(1+\varepsilon):W^{p}_{h}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma^{\prime}]))\leq w\}\geq\#\{[\Gamma^{\prime}]\in\sigma(\Gamma):W^{p}_{h}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma^{\prime}]))\leq w\}\ .

Thus, by Lemma
5.6
,

\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k}(X,h)\geq\frac{(1-k)k^{-\frac{p}{2}}}{2\pi}=\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k}(X,h_{0})\ ,

and the result follows.
∎

5.1.3.
The modified energy entropy

In light of Theorem
5.3
, it is reasonable to exclude the counting of quasi-Fuchsian surfaces that accumulate to a Fuchsian one. This motivates modifying our energy entropy for
k
-surfaces, in line with the modified entropy of
[
50
]
. This modified entropy will focus on counting only those quasi-Fuchsian surfaces that accumulate to the unique ergodic fully supported laminar measure in the sphere bundle
S_{h}X
.

Let
\hat{m}
denote the Haar measure of the set
{\mathcal{C}}^{+}
of round circles of
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. Recall from Theorem
2.11
that this is the unique fully supported, ergodic conformal current in
{\mathcal{C}}^{+}
. Note now that every conformal current is determined by its restriction to some compact fundamental domain, and it follows that, for all
C\geq 1
, the space of conformal currents over
\mathrm{QC}^{+}(C)
is metrizable. Let
\rho
be such a metric. Given
\eta>0
, we define

\mathrm{QF}_{\hat{m}}(\eta):=\left\{[\Gamma]\in\mathrm{QF}:\rho(\hat{m}(\partial_{\infty}\Gamma),\hat{m})<\eta\right\}\ .

Following
[
50
]
we define the
modified
p
-energy entropy
by

\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k,\hat{m}}(X,h):=\lim_{\eta\to 0}\liminf_{w\to\infty}\frac{1}{w\log w}\log\#\left\{[\Gamma]\in\mathrm{QF}_{\hat{m}}(\eta):W^{p}_{h}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma]))\leq w\right\}\ .

This entropy counts only quasi-Fuchsian
k
-surfaces that accumulate to the fully-supported, ergodic laminar measure. In particular, when
\Pi
has no Fuchsian subgroups (see, for example,
[
49
, §5.3]
),

\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k}(X,h)=\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k,\hat{m}}(X,h)\ .

Theorem 5.7
.

Assume that
-1\leq\mathrm{sect}_{h}\leq-a
,
p\geq 0
and
0<k<a
. Then

0<\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k,\hat{m}}(X,h)\leq\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k}(X,h)\ .

Proof.

Let
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of quasi-Fuchsian subgroups of
\Pi
as in Theorem
2.17
. By compactness of
S_{h}X
, the mean curvatures of the leaves of the foliation
{\mathcal{F}}_{k,h}
are uniformly bounded. An argument by contradiction similar to that used in Lemma
3.2
then shows that there exists a uniform upper bound
H>0
for the mean curvatures of all Lowe–Neves
k
-surfaces
S_{n}=S_{k,h}([\Gamma_{n}])
. Arguing as in Lemma
3.1
we then obtain, for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(17)

W^{p}_{h}(S_{n})\leq\frac{4\pi H^{p}}{(a-k)}(\mathrm{g}(\Gamma_{n})-1)\ .

Now fix
\eta>0
and
n_{0}
such that
[\Gamma_{n_{0}}]\in\mathrm{QF}_{\hat{m}}(\eta)
. Reasoning as in the proof of Theorem
5.3
, we show that, for every
[\Gamma^{\prime}]\in\sigma(\Gamma_{n_{0}})
,

[\Gamma^{\prime}]\in\mathrm{QF}_{\hat{m}}(\eta)\ .

Furthermore, since we have the same bounds on the mean and extrinsic curvatures, (
17
) also holds for
S^{\prime}=S_{k,h}([\Gamma^{\prime}])
. This implies that, for sufficiently large
g>0
,

\#\{[\Gamma^{\prime}]\in\sigma(\Gamma_{n_{0}})\ \big|\ \mathrm{g}(\Gamma^{\prime})\leq g\}\leq\#\{[\Gamma]\in\mathrm{QF}_{\hat{m}}(\eta)\ \big|\ W^{p}_{h}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma]))\leq\frac{4\pi H^{p}}{(a-k)}(g-1)\}\ .

Lemma
5.6
now yields a positive lower bound for the quantity

\liminf_{w\to\infty}\frac{1}{w\log(w)}\log\#\{[\Gamma]\in\mathrm{QF}_{\mu}(\eta)\ \big|\ W^{p}_{h}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma]))\leq w\}

which only depends on
H
and
a
, but not on
\eta
. This yields
0<\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k,\hat{m}}(X,h)
, as desired.
∎

5.2.
Rigidity of the modified entropy

5.2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

We assume from now on that
-1\leq\mathrm{sect}_{h}\leq-a<0
. Take
p\geq 0
and
0<k<a
. We first need to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 5.8
.

Suppose

\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k,\hat{m}}(X,h)=\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k,\hat{m}}(X,h_{0})\ .

Then there exists a sequence
(\eta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to zero, and a sequence
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of quasi-Fuchsian subgroups of
\Pi
such that,

—

for all
n
,
[\Gamma_{n}]\in\mathrm{QF}_{\hat{m}}(\eta_{n})
; and

—

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{W^{p}_{h}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma_{n}]))}{W^{p}_{h_{0}}(S_{k,h_{0}}([\Gamma_{n}]))}=1\ .

Proof.

Suppose the contrary. Then there exists
\delta>0
such that for all sufficiently small
\eta>0
, and for all
[\Gamma]\in\mathrm{QF}_{\hat{m}}(\eta)
,

W^{p}_{h}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma]))\geq(1+\eta)W^{p}_{h}(S_{k,h_{0}}([\Gamma]))\ .

This immediately implies

\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k,\hat{m}}(X,h)\leq\frac{1}{(1+\eta)}\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k,\hat{m}}(X,h_{0})\ ,

which is absurd, and the result follows.
∎

We now prove Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Assume that
\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k,\hat{m}}(X,h)=\mathrm{Ent}^{p}_{k,\hat{m}}(X,h_{0})
. Then Lemma
5.8
provides a sequence
(\eta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to zero as well as a sequence
([\Gamma_{n}])_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathrm{QF}_{\hat{m}}(\eta_{n})
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{W^{p}_{h}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma_{n}]))}{W^{p}_{h_{0}}(S_{k,h_{0}}([\Gamma_{n}]))}=1\ .

By definition of the sequence
([\Gamma_{n}])_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, the sequence
(\mu_{k,h}(\partial_{\infty}\Gamma_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of laminar measures converges to
\mu
, the unique ergodic laminar measure that has full support in
S_{h}X
.

By reproducing the argument given in §
4.1
we get that
\mathrm{sect}_{h}=-1
almost everywhere for
\mu
. So we must have
\mathrm{sect}_{h}=-1
everywhere and
h
is isometric to
h_{0}
by Mostow’s rigidity theorem.
∎

5.2.2.
Case of equality between modified energy and area entropies

As in
[
2
,
50
]
we can define the
modified area entropy
as

\mathrm{Ent}^{\text{Area}}_{k,\hat{m}}(X,h)=\lim_{\eta\to 0}\liminf_{A\to\infty}\frac{1}{A\log A}\log\#\left\{[\Gamma]\in\mathrm{QF}_{\hat{m}}(\eta)\ \bigg|\ \mathrm{Area}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma]))\leq A\right\}\ .

We give the proof of our last theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

We will first assume
\mathrm{Ent}^{\text{Area}}_{\hat{m}}(M,h)=\mathrm{Ent}^{1}_{k,\hat{m}}(M,h)
. Then an argument similar to Lemma
5.8
gives the existence of a sequence
(\eta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to zero as well as a sequence
([\Gamma_{n}])_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathrm{QF}_{\hat{m}}(\eta_{n})
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{k^{-\frac{1}{2}}W^{1}_{h}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma_{n}]))}{\text{Area}_{h}(S_{k,h}([\Gamma_{n}]))}=1\ .

Here again, by definition of the sequence
([\Gamma_{n}])_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, the sequence
(\mu_{k,h}(\partial_{\infty}\Gamma_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of laminar measures converges to the ergodic laminar measure
\mu
, which has full support in
S_{h}X
.

Hence by reproducing the argument in §
4.2
we obtain that the
k
-surface foliation of
S_{h}X
is by totally umbilical leaves. So by applying the axiom of
2
-spheres we conclude that the sectional curvature of
h
is constant.

The other implications follow from the same argument.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

We comment that the results of this section also hold with the modified entropy functionals replaced by entropy functionals that count k-surfaces whose limit sets in the hyperbolic metric become more and more round, but without a condition on equidistribution, provided that
X
contains no closed totally geodesic surfaces. The condition that there be no closed totally geodesic surfaces causes the equidistribution to hold automatically. (See
[
50
]
for a closely analogous situation for entropy functionals counting minimal surfaces rather than
k
-surfaces.)

If the following question had a positive answer, it would be possible to drop the condition that
X
contain no closed totally geodesic surface in its hyperbolic metric. If it had a negative answer, it would be possible to give counterexamples to statements involving the un-modified k-surface entropy by taking covers of a totally umbilic surface as in the statement below.

Question 5.10
.

Suppose that
X
has sectional curvature greater than or equal to
-1
, and that
X
contains a closed totally umbilic surface
S
with mean curvature
k^{1/2}
and with constant curvature
-1+k
in its intrinsic metric (i.e., the sectional curvature of
X
through every tangent plane to
S
is
-1
.) Then must
X
be hyperbolic?

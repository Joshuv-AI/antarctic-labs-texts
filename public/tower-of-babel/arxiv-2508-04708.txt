Algebraic Framework for Discrete Dynamical Systems over Laurent Series
Ramamonjy Andriamifidisoa, Loukman Ben Saindou

Abstract
. We generalize the framework of discrete algebraic dynamical systems [ 1 ] to Laurent polynomials and series over ℤ r \Z^{r} , enabling the modeling of bidirectional discrete systems. By redefining the spaces 𝐃 ′ \mathbf{D}^{\prime} and 𝐀 ′ \mathbf{A}^{\prime} , introducing a bilinear mapping (defined as the scalar product in Section 3), and extending the shift operator, we preserve the duality and adjoint properties of [ 1 ] . These properties are rigorously proved and illustrated through examples and a data processing case study on bidirectional sequence transformations. In contrast to Oberst [ 3 ] , our algebraic approach emphasizes the structure of Laurent series, providing a streamlined framework for multidimensional systems. This work addresses an open question from [ 1 ] and has applications in multidimensional data processing, such as image filtering and control theory. Keywords : Discrete algebraic dynamical systems, Laurent series, shift operator, duality, multidimensional systems.

2.
Definitions and Notations

2.1.
Notations

Let
\N
denote the natural numbers,
\Z
the integers,
\F
a commutative field, and
r\geq 1
. The set
\Z^{r}
is the
r
-dimensional integer lattice, serving as the time set for discrete systems.

•

Sequence Spaces
:

–

\F^{\Z^{r}}=\{W:\Z^{r}\to\F\}
, the vector space of sequences
W=(W_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\Z^{r}}
, with
W(\alpha)=W_{\alpha}\in\F
.

–

\F^{(\Z^{r})}=\{W\in\F^{\Z^{r}}\mid W_{\alpha}\neq 0\text{ for finitely many }\alpha\}
, the subspace of sequences with finite support.

Both
\F^{\Z^{r}}
and
\F^{(\Z^{r})}
are
\F
-vector spaces under pointwise addition and scalar multiplication.

For
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r})\in\Z^{r}
, define monomials
X^{\alpha}=X_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots X_{r}^{\alpha_{r}}
,
Y^{\alpha}=Y_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots Y_{r}^{\alpha_{r}}
, with
X_{i},X_{i}^{-1},Y_{i},Y_{i}^{-1}
formal variables. For
\alpha\in\Z^{r}
, let
\delta_{\alpha}:\Z^{r}\to\F
be:

\delta_{\alpha}(\beta)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\alpha=\beta,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

2.2.
Spaces

Definition 2.1

(Spaces)
.

Let
\F
be a commutative field and
r\geq 1
. Define:

(1)

\mathbf{D}^{\prime}=\F[X_{1},X_{1}^{-1},\dots,X_{r},X_{r}^{-1}]
, the ring of Laurent polynomials, with elements:

d(X)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Z^{r}}d_{\alpha}X^{\alpha},\quad d_{\alpha}\in\F,\quad\text{finitely many }d_{\alpha}\neq 0.

(2)

\mathbf{A}^{\prime}=\F[[Y_{1},Y_{1}^{-1},\dots,Y_{r},Y_{r}^{-1}]]
, the space of Laurent series, with elements:

W(Y)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Z^{r}}W_{\alpha}Y^{\alpha},\quad W_{\alpha}\in\F.

Remark 2.2

(Sequences and Signals)
.

A
sequence
is an element
W(Y)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Z^{r}}W_{\alpha}Y^{\alpha}\in\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
, where
W_{\alpha}\in\F
are coefficients indexed by
\Z^{r}
. In applications, such sequences are often called
signals
, representing discrete data indexed by time or space (e.g., audio samples for
r=1
or image pixels for
r=2
). We use “sequence” to emphasize the algebraic structure, reserving “signal” for applied contexts.

Example 2.3

(Laurent Polynomials)
.

(1)

For
r=1
, the Laurent polynomial
d(X)=5X^{-1}-3X^{2}
has coefficients
d_{-1}=5
,
d_{2}=-3
, and
d_{k}=0
for all other
k\in\Z
.

(2)

For
r=2
, an example is
d(X_{1},X_{2})=X_{1}^{-1}X_{2}+3X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{-2}
, with non-zero coefficients at
\alpha=(-1,1)
and
\alpha=(2,-2)
.

Example 2.4

(Laurent Series)
.

For
r=1
, a Laurent series is
W(Y)=\dots+2Y^{-1}+Y^{1}+3Y^{2}+\dots
.

Example 2.5

(Unidirectional vs. Bidirectional Indexing)
.

For
r=1
, consider a sequence
W(Y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}W_{n}Y^{n}
.

•

In
\mathbb{N}
(unidirectional),
W_{n}=0\;\text{for}\;n<0
, representing a causal sequence (e.g., audio samples starting at
t=0
).

•

In
\mathbb{Z}
(bidirectional),
W_{n}
can be non-zero for
n<0
, enabling bidirectional transformations, such as a smoothing filter
P(X)=0.5X^{-1}+0.5X
, where
W^{\prime}_{n}=0.5(W_{n-1}+W_{n+1})
, using past and future values (see Example
3.8
).

Proposition 2.6

( Isomorphisms)
.

Let
\F
be a commutative field and
r\geq 1
. The following isomorphisms hold :

(1)

\mathbf{D}^{\prime}\simeq\F^{(\Z^{r})}
, via the map
X^{\alpha}\leftrightarrow\delta_{\alpha}
.

(2)

\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\simeq\F^{\Z^{r}}
, via the map
W(Y)\leftrightarrow(W_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\Z^{r}}
.

Proof.

For (1), the map
\phi:\mathbf{D}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{F}^{\left(\mathbb{Z}^{r}\right)}
, defined by
\phi(X^{\alpha})=\delta_{\alpha}
, extends linearly to
d(X)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}d_{\alpha}X^{\alpha}\mapsto\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}d_{\alpha}\delta_{\alpha}
. Since
d(X)
has finitely many non-zero
d_{\alpha}
, the image lies in
\mathbb{F}^{\left(\mathbb{Z}^{r}\right)}
. The map is bijective, as each
\delta_{\alpha}
corresponds uniquely to
X^{\alpha}
.

For (2), the map
\psi:\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{F}^{\left(\mathbb{Z}^{r}\right)}
, defined by
\psi(W(Y))=(W_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}
, where
W(Y)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}W_{\alpha}Y^{\alpha}
, is bijective, as it associates each Laurent series with its sequence of coefficients, with inverse mapping
(W_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}\mapsto\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}W_{\alpha}Y^{\alpha}
.
∎

2.3.
Discrete Linear Dynamical Systems

Definition 2.7

( General Discrete Linear Dynamical System)
.

Following Willems
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
, a discrete linear dynamical system over
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
is a triplet
\Sigma=(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{W},\mathcal{B})
, where :

•

\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{Z}^{r}
is the time set,

•

\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{F}^{l}
is the signal space ( an
\mathbb{F}
-vector space),

•

\mathcal{B}\subseteq(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})^{l}
is the behavior, a
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
-submodule representing the set of admissible signal trajectories.

Remark 2.8
.

The behavioral approach of Willems
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
provides a general framework for modeling dynamical systems, where the behavior
\mathcal{B}
describes all possible trajectories of the system without distinguishing inputs and outputs. This flexibility allows modeling a wide range of systems, including those not necessarily defined by linear equations.

Example 2.9

( Periodic Trajectories)
.

For
r=1,\;\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R},\;\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{Z},\;\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathcal{B}=\{W\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}\mid W_{n+2}=W_{n},\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. This behavior describes sequences that are periodic with period 2. For instance, a valid trajectory is
W(Y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{n}Y^{n},\;\text{where}\;a_{n}=1\;\text{if}\;n
is even and
a_{n}=0
if
n
is odd, i.e.,
W(Y)=\sum_{n\;\text{even}}Y^{n}
. This system is linear and shift-invariant but not necessarily defined by a Laurent polynomial kernel.

Example 2.10

(Bounded Trajectories)
.

For
r=2,\;\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R},\;\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{Z}^{2},\;\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{B}=\{W\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}\mid|W_{\alpha}|\leq 1,\forall\;\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}
. This behavior consists of all sequences ( or signals, e.g, image pixel intensities) whose values are bounded by 1. An example trajectory is
W(Y_{1},Y_{2})=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}W_{\alpha}Y^{\alpha}
, where
W_{\alpha}=\sin(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})
. This system illustrates the generality of willems’ framework, as
\mathcal{B}
is not defined by a polynomial equation but is still a valid
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
-submodule.

Definition 2.11

(Autoregressif Systems)
.

A discrete linear dynamical system is called
autoregressive
if its behavior is defined as :

\mathcal{B}=\ker R(X)=\{W(Y)=(W_{1}(Y),\ldots,W_{l}(Y))\in(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})^{l}\mid R(X)\circ W(Y)=0\},

where
R(X)\in(\mathbf{D}^{\prime})^{k,l}
is a matrix of Laurent polynomials, and the shift operator
\circ
is given by :

R(X)\circ W(Y)=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{l}R_{ij}(X)\circ W_{j}(Y)\right)_{1\leq i\leq k},

with, for
R_{ij}(X)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}R_{ij\alpha}X^{\alpha}
,
W_{j}(Y)=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}W_{j\beta}Y^{\beta}
,

R_{ij}(X)\circ W_{j}(Y)=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}R_{ij\alpha}W_{j(\alpha+\gamma)}\right)Y^{\gamma}.

Remark 2.12
.

The autoregressive systems, as studied by Oberst
[
3
]
, are a special case of Wilems’ framework
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
, where the behavior
\mathcal{B}
is defined by the kernel of Laurent polynomial matrix. This structure is particularly suited for bidirectional systems over
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, enabling explicit algebraic representations of system dynamics, such as bidirectional filters.

Example 2.13

(Bidirectional Difference System)
.

For
r=1,k=l=1,\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, consider
R(X)=X-X^{-1}\in\mathbf{D}^{\prime}=\mathbb{R}[X,X^{-1}]
. The system is defined by :

\mathcal{B}=\ker R(X)=\{W(Y)\in\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mid(X-X^{-1})\circ W(Y)=0\}.

Let
W(Y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}W_{n}Y^{n}
. The equation
(X-X^{-1})\circ W(Y)=0
yields :

X\circ W(Y)-X^{-1}\circ W(Y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}W_{n+1}Y^{n}-\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}W_{n-1}Y^{n}=0,

Implying :

W_{n+1}=W_{n-1},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Solutions are sequences where
W_{n}=a
for even
n
and
W_{n}=b
for odd
n
, with
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. For instance,
W_{n}=1
for even
n
and
W_{n}=0
for odd
n
gives a valid trajectory
W(Y)=\sum_{n\;\text{even}}Y^{n}
.

Example 2.14

(Two-Variable-System)
.

For
r=1,k=l=2,\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, consider :

R(X)=\begin{pmatrix}X+X^{-1}&1\\
0&X^{-1}-1\end{pmatrix}\in(\mathbf{D}^{\prime})^{2,2}.

The behavior is :

\mathcal{B}=\left\{W(Y)=\begin{pmatrix}W_{1}(Y)\\
W_{2}(Y)\end{pmatrix}\in(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})^{2}\mid R(X)\circ W(Y)=0\right\}.

The system of equations is :

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(X+X^{-1})\circ W_{1}(Y)+W_{2}(Y)=0,\\
(X^{-1}-1)\circ W_{2}(Y)=0.\end{array}\right.

For
W_{1}(Y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}W_{1n}Y^{n},\;W_{2}(Y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}W_{2n}Y^{n}
, the second equation gives :

W_{2(n-1)}=W_{2n},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z},

so
W_{2}(Y)
is constant, say
W_{2n}(Y)=c
. The first equation becomes :

W_{1(n+1)}+W_{1(n-1)}=-c,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}.

If
c=0
, then
W_{1(n+1)}=-W_{1(n-1)}
, and a solution is
W_{1n}=(-1)^{n}d
. Thus, a trajectory is :

W(Y)=\begin{pmatrix}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}dY^{n}\\
0\end{pmatrix},\quad d\in\mathbb{R}.

Definition 2.15

(Bilinear Mapping)
.

Let
\F
be a commutative field and
r\geq 1
. The bilinear mapping is:

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathbf{D}^{\prime}\times\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\to\F,\quad\langle d(X),W(Y)\rangle=\sum_{\alpha\in\Z^{r}}d_{\alpha}W_{\alpha},

where
d(X)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Z^{r}}d_{\alpha}X^{\alpha}\in\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
and
W(Y)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Z^{r}}W_{\alpha}Y^{\alpha}\in\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
.

Example 2.16
.

For
r=1
, let
d(X)=X^{-1}+2X^{2}\in\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
and
W(Y)=\sum_{k\in\Z}W_{k}Y^{k}\in\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
with
W_{-1}=3
,
W_{2}=4
, and
W_{k}=0
otherwise. Then:

\langle d(X),W(Y)\rangle=d_{-1}W_{-1}+d_{2}W_{2}=1\cdot 3+2\cdot 4=11.

Definition 2.17

(Shift Operator)
.

For
P(X)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Z^{r}}P_{\alpha}X^{\alpha}\in\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
,
W\in\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
, the shift operator is:

P(X)\circ W(Y)=\sum_{\beta\in\Z^{r}}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\Z^{r}}P_{\alpha}W_{\alpha+\beta}\right)Y^{\beta}.

Example 2.18
.

For
r=1
, let
P(X)=X^{1}\in\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
, and
W(Y)=\sum_{k\in\Z}W_{k}Y^{k}\in\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
. Then:

P(X)\circ W(Y)=\sum_{k\in\Z}(P_{1}W_{1+k})Y^{k}=\sum_{k\in\Z}W_{k+1}Y^{k}.

3.
Main Results

We present the properties of duality, the shift operator, and a practical application.

Proposition 3.1

(Duality Properties of the Bilinear Mapping)
.

The bilinear mapping
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathbf{D}^{\prime}\times\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\to\F
satisfies:

(1)

The homomorphisms:

\mathbf{D}^{\prime}\to\Hom_{\F}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\F),\quad d(X)\mapsto\langle d(X),\cdot\rangle,

\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\to\Hom_{\F}(\mathbf{D}^{\prime},\F),\quad W(Y)\mapsto\langle\cdot,W(Y)\rangle,

are injective.

(2)

The latter is an isomorphism.

Proof.

(1)

Injectivity of
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}\to\Hom_{\F}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\F)
: Suppose
\langle d_{1}(X),W(Y)\rangle=\langle d_{2}(X),W(Y)\rangle
for all
W(Y)\in\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
, where
d_{1}(X)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Z^{r}}d_{\alpha}X^{\alpha}
,
d_{2}(X)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Z^{r}}d_{2\alpha}X^{\alpha}
. Take
W(Y)=Y^{\gamma}
,
\gamma\in\Z^{r}
, with coefficients
(W)_{\alpha}=\delta_{\alpha,\gamma}
. Then:

\langle d_{1}(X),Y^{\gamma}\rangle=d_{1\gamma},\quad\langle d_{2}(X),Y^{\gamma}\rangle=d_{2\gamma}.

Since
\langle d_{1}(X),Y^{\gamma}\rangle=\langle d_{2}(X),Y^{\gamma}\rangle
,
d_{1\gamma}=d_{2\gamma}
for all
\gamma\in\Z^{r}
. Thus,
d_{1}(X)=d_{2}(X)
.

(2)

Injectivity and surjectivity of
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\to\Hom_{\F}(\mathbf{D}^{\prime},\F)
: Suppose
\langle d(X),W_{1}(Y)\rangle=\langle d(X),W_{2}(Y)\rangle
for all
d(X)\in\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
. Take
d(X)=X^{\gamma}
. Then:

\langle X^{\gamma},W_{1}(Y)\rangle=(W_{1})_{\gamma},\quad\langle X^{\gamma},W_{2}(Y)\rangle=(W_{2})_{\gamma}.

Since they are equal,
(W_{1})_{\gamma}=(W_{2})_{\gamma}
for all
\gamma\in\Z^{r}
, so
W_{1}=W_{2}
.

For surjectivity, let
\psi\in\Hom_{\F}(\mathbf{D}^{\prime},\F)
. Define
W(Y)\in\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
by
(W)_{\alpha}=\psi(X^{\alpha})
. For
d(X)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Z^{r}}d_{\alpha}X^{\alpha}
:

\psi(d(X))=\sum_{\alpha\in\Z^{r}}d_{\alpha}\psi(X^{\alpha})=\sum_{\alpha\in\Z^{r}}d_{\alpha}(W)_{\alpha}=\langle d(X),W(Y)\rangle.

Thus,
\psi=\langle\cdot,W(Y)\rangle
, and the map is an isomorphism.

∎

Definition 3.2

(Scalar Product)
.

The bilinear mapping
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathbf{D}^{\prime}\times\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\to\F
is called the scalar product, as it satisfies the duality properties of Proposition
3.1
, ensuring a well-defined pairing between
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
.

Remark 3.3
.

The scalar product defined in Definition
3.2
is inspired by bilinear forms used in the behavioral approach to dynamical systems. Specifically :

•

[
5
]
Polderman and Willems (1998) introduce a mathematical framework for systems theory, emphasizing behavioral modeling without distinguishing inputs and outputs, applicable to multidimensional systems.

•

[
11
]
Rocha and Willems (1989) develop the concept of state for two-dimensional systems, using bilinear forms to describe system trajectories.

•

[
12
]
Zerz (2000) explores the algebraic theory of multidimensional linear systems, employing bilinear forms to establish dualities between modules and behaviors.

•

[
13
]
Malgrange (1962/1963) introduces foundational concepts for differential systems with constant coefficients, where bilinear forms provide a basis for our extension to Laurent series over
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
.

Example 3.4
.

For
r=1
, let
d(X)=X^{-1}+X\in\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
,
W(Y)=Y^{-1}+2Y\in\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
. Then:

\langle d(X),W(Y)\rangle=d_{-1}W_{-1}+d_{1}W_{1}=1\cdot 1+1\cdot 2=3.

Lemma 3.5

(Actions of Multiplication and Shift Operators)
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a commutative field and
r\geq 1
, with
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}=\mathbb{F}[X_{1},X_{1}^{-1},\ldots,X_{r},X_{r}^{-1}]
and
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}=\mathbb{F}[[Y_{1},Y_{1}^{-1},\ldots,Y_{r},Y_{r}^{-1}]]
.

(1)

Multiplication Operator on
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
: For
d(X)=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}d_{\beta}X^{\beta}\in\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
, the multiplication operator is defined as :

m_{d}:\mathbf{D}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{D}^{\prime},\quad c(X)\mapsto c(X)\cdot d(X),

where
c(X)\cdot d(X)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}c_{\gamma}d_{\alpha-\gamma}\right)X^{\alpha}
, with finitely many non-zero terms in the product. This operator is
\mathbb{F}
-linear and preserves the
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
-module structure of
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
.

(2)

Shift Operator on
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
: For
d(X)=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}d_{\beta}X^{\beta}\in\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
and
W(Y)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}W_{\alpha}Y^{\alpha}\in\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
, the shift operator is defined as :

\sigma_{d}:\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\quad W(Y)\mapsto d(X)\circ W(Y)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}\left(\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}d_{\beta}W_{\alpha+\beta}\right)Y^{\alpha}.

This operator is
\mathbb{F}
-linear and transforms the coefficients of
W(Y)
by shifts indexed by
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
.

Proof.

•

For (1), multiplication in
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
is well-defined since
d(X)
and
c(X)
are Laurent polynomials with finitely many non-zero terms, so their products is in
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
. Linearity follows from the distributivity of multiplication over addition in
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
.

•

For (2), the shift operator is well-defined because, for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, the sum
\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}d_{\beta}W_{\alpha+\beta}
is finite (since
d_{\beta}\neq 0
for finitely many
\beta
), and the result is a Laurent series in
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
. Linearity follows from the linearity of operations in
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
.

∎

Theorem 3.6

(Adjoint of the Multiplication Operator)
.

The adjoint of the multiplication operator :

m_{d}:\mathbf{D}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{D}^{\prime},\quad c(X)\mapsto c(X)\cdot d(X),

is the shift operator:

\sigma_{d}:\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\quad W(Y)\mapsto d(X)\circ W(Y)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Z^{r}}\left(\sum_{\beta\in\Z^{r}}d_{\beta}W_{\alpha+\beta}\right)Y^{\alpha}.

Proof.

Let
d(X)=\sum_{\beta\in\Z^{r}}d_{\beta}X^{\beta}\in\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
. The multiplication operator is
m_{d}:\mathbf{D}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
,
c(X)\mapsto c(X)\cdot d(X)
. Its adjoint is:

\Hom_{\F}(m_{d},\F):\Hom_{\F}(\mathbf{D}^{\prime},\F)\to\Hom_{\F}(\mathbf{D}^{\prime},\F).

Since
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\simeq\Hom_{\F}(\mathbf{D}^{\prime},\F)
(by Proposition
3.1
), this induces a map
\sigma_{d}:\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
. Consider
d(X)=X^{\beta}
. The multiplication operator is :

m_{X^{\beta}}:\mathbf{D}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{D}^{\prime},\quad c(X)\mapsto c(X)\cdot X^{\beta}.

Its adjoint is
\Hom_{\F}(X^{\beta},\F)
. For
W(Y)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Z^{r}}W_{\gamma}Y^{\gamma}\in\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
, viewed as
\langle\cdot,W\rangle
, we have:

\langle X^{\alpha},W\rangle=W_{\alpha}.

Thus:

\Hom_{\F}(X^{\beta},\F)(W)(X^{\alpha})=\langle X^{\beta}\cdot X^{\alpha},W\rangle=W_{\alpha+\beta}.

If
V(Y)=\Hom_{\F}(X^{\beta},\F)(W)
, then
V_{\alpha}=W_{\alpha+\beta}
, so:

X^{\beta}\circ W(Y)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Z^{r}}W_{\alpha+\beta}Y^{\alpha}.

For general
d(X)=\sum_{\beta\in\Z^{r}}d_{\beta}X^{\beta}
:

\Hom_{\F}(m_{d},\F)=\sum_{\beta\in\Z^{r}}d_{\beta}\Hom_{\F}(X^{\beta},\F).

Applying to
W(Y)
:

d(X)\circ W(Y)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Z^{r}}\left(\sum_{\beta\in\Z^{r}}d_{\beta}W_{\alpha+\beta}\right)Y^{\alpha},

which is the shift operator.
∎

Conclusion

This work extends the framework of discrete algebraic dynamical systems
[
1
]
to Laurent polynomials and series over
\Z^{r}
, enabling bidirectional system modeling. By redefining
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
,
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
, the scalar product (Definition
3.2
), and the shift operator, we preserve the duality and adjoint properties (Proposition
3.1
), as shown through proofs and examples. Our approach offers a novel algebraic perspective on multidimensional systems, distinct from Oberst
[
3
]
or Willems
[
9
]
.

Applications include multidimensional data processing and control theory for systems with infinite memory, building on behavioral approaches to control as discussed in
[
10
]
. Future work will explore matrix-valued systems, addressing non-commutative operations, and investigate topological properties for Laurent series convergence, potentially impacting real-time data processing.

Finding an Isomorphism between the Riordan Group and a Subgroup of the Double Riordan Group
Shakuan K. Frankson, Mathematics Subject Classifications: 05A05, 05A10

Abstract
The Riordan group is a set of infinite lower-triangular matrices defined by two generating functions, g g and f f . The elements of the group are called Riordan arrays, denoted by ( g , f ) (g,f) , and the k k th column of a Riordan array is given by the function g ​ f k gf^{k} . The Double Riordan group is defined similarly using three generating functions g g , f 1 f_{1} , and f 2 f_{2} , where g g is an even function and f 1 f_{1} and f 2 f_{2} are odd functions. This group generalizes the Checkerboard subgroup of the Riordan group, where g g is even and f f is odd. An open question posed by Davenport, Shapiro, and Woodson [ 4 ] was if there exists an isomorphism between the Riordan group and a subgroup of the Double Riordan group. This question is answered in this article.

Introduction

Before we discuss the algebraic structure of the Riordan group, we must set out the terminology needed to define its elements.

.

Given a sequence
(a_{n})=(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},...)

A(z)=a_{0}+a_{1}z+a_{2}z^{2}+a_{3}z^{3}+...=\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}z^{k}

k

From these definitions, we now look at the set of matrices that Shapiro, Getu, Woan, and Woodson define in

Definition 2

Consider infinite matrices
M=(m_{i,j})
over a field
\mathbb{F}
, for
{i,j\geq 0}
. Let

C_{k}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}m_{n,k}z^{n}

be the column generating function of the
k

M

C_{k}(z)=g(z)f(z)^{k}

for some formal power series
g(z)

g(z)=g_{0}+g_{1}z+g_{2}z^{2}+g_{3}z^{3}+...=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}g_{k}z^{k},\ \ g_{0}\neq 0

f(z)=f_{1}z+f_{2}z^{2}+f_{3}z^{3}+...=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}f_{k}z^{k},\ \ f_{1}\neq 0,

Definition 3

The
(ordinary) Riordan group

\mathcal{R}

g(z)

g_{0}\neq 0

To determine the product of two Riordan arrays, we first observe the product of a Riordan array with an infinite column vector associated with some ordinary generating function
A(z)
. The result is another infinite column vector given by the generating function
B(z)
. This important observation is the Fundamental Theorem of Riordan Arrays (FTRA)

Theorem 1
.

(The Fundamental Theorem of Riordan Arrays):
Let
A(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{a_{k}z^{k}}
and
B(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{b_{k}z^{k}}
and let
A
and
B
be the column vectors
A=\left(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\cdots\right)^{T}
and
B=\left(b_{0},b_{1},b_{2},\cdots\right)^{T}
. Then
(g,f)*A=B
, if and only if
B(z)=g(z)A(f(z))
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\left[g\ \ gf\ \ gf^{2}\ \ gf^{3}\ \ \cdots\right]\begin{bmatrix}a_{0}\\
a_{1}\\
a_{2}\\
\vdots\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}b_{0}\\
b_{1}\\
b_{2}\\
\vdots\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle a_{0}g+a_{1}gf+a_{2}gf^{2}+\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B\left(z\right)

\displaystyle g\left(a_{0}+a_{1}f+a_{2}f^{2}+a_{3}f^{3}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(z)

\displaystyle g(z)\ast A\left(f(z)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B\left(z\right)

∎

Using the FTRA, we extend to the multiplication rule for Riordan arrays.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(g,f)
and
(G,F)
be Riordan arrays. Then, the product of the matrices is given by

\left(g,f\right)\ast\left(G,F\right)=\left(gG\left(f\right),F\left(f\right)\right).

Associativity holds as a result of the matrix multiplication. The inverse of a Riordan array
(g,f)
is given by

\left(g,f\right)^{-1}=\left(\frac{1}{g\left(\overline{f}\right)},\overline{f}\right).

To satisfy the group axioms, we establish that the identity of the Riordan group is the infinite identity matrix, which we denote by
\left(1,z\right).

Now, consider the case when an array has two multiplier functions. To preserve the group structure, we set further conditions for the generating functions to satisfy.

Definition 4
.

[
4
]

Let
g(z)\in\mathcal{F}_{0}
be an even function and
f_{1}(z),f_{2}(z)\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
be odd functions. Define the initial column by the generating function
g(z)
and multiply
f_{1}(z)
and
f_{2}(z)
alternately to each successive column. Then, the resulting array is called a
Double Riordan array
, and these arrays are elements of the
Double Riordan group
. We denote the group by
\mathcal{DR}
or
2\mathcal{R}
. Double Riordan matrices are denoted by
(g,f_{1},f_{2})
. The columns of the array are given by

\begin{array}[]{r}(g,f_{1},f_{2})=\Big[g\ \ gf_{1}\ \ g(f_{1}f_{2})\ \ gf_{1}(f_{1}f_{2})\ \ g(f_{1}f_{2})^{2}\ \ \cdots\Big].\end{array}

When the multiplier functions
f_{1}=f_{2}
, the Double Riordan array is an aerated Riordan array in the Checkerboard subgroup.

To find the product of two Double Riordan arrays, Shapiro, Woodson, and Davenport establish the Fundamental Theorem of Double Riordan Arrays (FTDRA) in
[
4
]
. When multiplying a
\mathcal{DR}
array by an infinite column vector
A(z)
, we must consider two cases: when
A(z)
is an even or odd function.

Theorem 3
.

(The Fundamental Theorem of Double Riordan Arrays)
Let
g(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{g_{2k}z^{2k}}
,
f_{1}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{f_{1,2k+1}z^{2k+1}}
, and
f_{2}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{f_{2,2k+1}z^{2k+1}}
.

Case 1: If
A(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{a_{2k}z^{2k}}
and
B(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{b_{2k}z^{2k}}
, and
A=\left(a_{0},0,a_{2},0,\cdots\right)^{T}
and
B=\left(b_{0},0,b_{2},0,\cdots\right)^{T}
are column vectors. Then,

(g,f_{1},f_{2})*A

=B
if and only if
B(z)=g(z)A(\sqrt{f_{1}f_{2}}).

Case 2: If
A(z)=\sum_{k\geq 0}{a_{2k+1}z^{2k+1}}
and
B(z)=\sum_{k\geq 0}{b_{2k+1}z^{2k+1}}
, then

(g,f_{1},f_{2})*A=B
if and only if
B(z)=g(z)\sqrt{\frac{f_{1}}{f_{2}}}A(\sqrt{f_{1}f_{2}})
.

Shapiro et al. generalize the FTDRA to the product of two Double Riordan Arrays and get the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

Let
(g,f_{1},f_{2})
and
(G,F_{1},F_{2})
be Double Riordan arrays. Then, the product of the arrays is given by

(g,f_{1},f_{2})*(G,F_{1},F_{2})=\left(gG(h),\frac{f_{1}}{h}F_{1}(h),\frac{f_{2}}{h}F_{2}(h)\right),

where
h=\sqrt{f_{1}f_{2}}
.

The generating function
h
is an element of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
, so there exists a unique compositional inverse that is denoted by
\bar{h}
. Then, for some Double Riordan array
(g,f_{1},f_{2})
, its inverse is given by

(g,f_{1},f_{2})^{-1}=\left(\frac{1}{g(\bar{h})},\ \frac{z\bar{h}}{f_{1}(\bar{h})},\ \frac{z\bar{h}}{f_{2}(\bar{h})}\right).

The identity element of the Double Riordan group is also the infinite identity matrix, which is denoted by
(1,z,z)
.

2
The Isomorphism Problem

Davenport, Shapiro, and Woodson
[
4
]
state that there exists an isomorphic mapping
\psi:\mathcal{R}_{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{DR}
defined by

\psi:(g,f)\mapsto(g,f,f),

where
\mathcal{R}_{C}
is the Checkerboard subgroup of the Riordan group,
g(z)
is an even function, and
f(z)
is an odd function. An open question from
[
4
]
asks if there exists an isomorphism between the Riordan group and a subgroup of the Double Riordan group. We answer this question in the following section.

2.1
The Monomorphism Theorems

Before we define these monomorphisms, we first establish the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

The subset

S=\biggl\{\left(g(z^{2}),z,\frac{f(z^{2})}{z}\right):g(z)\in\mathcal{F}_{0}\ \text{and}\ f(z)\in\mathcal{F}_{1}\biggl\}

of the Double Riordan group is a subgroup.

Proof.

Suppose
\left(g(z^{2}),z,\frac{f(z^{2})}{z}\right)
and
\left(G(z^{2}),z,\frac{F(z^{2})}{z}\right)
are elements of the set
S
. Then, the product

\left(g(z^{2}),z,\frac{f(z^{2})}{z}\right)*\left(G(z^{2}),z,\frac{F(z^{2})}{z}\right)=\left(g(z^{2})G\left(f(z^{2})\right),z,\frac{F\left(f(z^{2})\right)}{z}\right).

Thus, the closure property is satisfied. It now suffices to show the inverse of matrices in
S
also lie in the set. Taking the inverse of some array
\left(g(z^{2}),z,\frac{f(z^{2})}{z}\right)
, we see that

\left(g(z^{2}),z,\frac{f(z^{2})}{z}\right)^{-1}=\left(\frac{1}{g(\bar{h}^{2})},\frac{z\bar{h}}{\bar{h}},\frac{z\bar{h}}{\frac{f(\bar{h}^{2})}{\bar{h}}}\right)=\left(\frac{1}{g(\bar{h}^{2})},z,\frac{z\bar{h}^{2}}{f(\bar{h}^{2})}\right),

where
\bar{h}=\sqrt{f(z^{2})}
. We note that

\bar{h}^{2}=\left(\overline{\sqrt{f(z^{2})}}\right)^{2}=\left({\sqrt{\bar{f}(z^{2})}}\right)^{2}=\bar{f}(z^{2}).

Thus,

\left(\frac{1}{g(\bar{h}^{2})},z,\frac{z\bar{h}^{2}}{f(\bar{h}^{2})}\right)=\left(\frac{1}{g(\bar{h}^{2})},z,\frac{z\bar{f}(z^{2})}{f(\bar{f}(z^{2}))}\right)=\left(\frac{1}{g(\bar{h}^{2})},z,\frac{\bar{f}(z^{2})}{z}\right).

∎

Definition 5
.

The subset
S
of Double Riordan arrays defined in Lemma
2.1
is called the
type-
1
almost Appell subgroup
. This subgroup is also represented by the set

\{(g,z,f_{2}):g(z)\ \text{is an even function},\ f_{2}(z)\ \text{is an odd function}\}.

The type-
1
almost Appell subgroup is not normal, but it does contain the normal Appell subgroup
\{(g,z,z):g(z)\ \text{is an even function}\}
. Furthermore, the type-
1
almost Appell subgroup is isomorphic to the Riordan group for a certain map
\phi
, which we define in the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

(Monomorphism Theorem I)
The type-
1
almost Appell subgroup of the Double Riordan group is an isomorphic copy of the Riordan group, and the map
\phi:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{DR}
defined by

\phi(g,f)=\left(g(z^{2}),z,\frac{f(z^{2})}{z}\right)

is a group monomorphism.

Proof.

Let
(g,f)
and
(G,F)
be two Riordan arrays. To show that the map
\phi
is a homomorphism, we prove that

\phi(g,f)\ast\phi(G,F)=\phi\left((g,f)*(G,F)\right).

(2.1)

Note that, for any Riordan array
(g,f)
, the generating functions
g(z)\in\mathcal{F}_{0}
and
f(z)\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
. Thus, the functions
g(z^{2})
and
f(z^{2})
are even. Then, the lefthand side of Equation (
2.1
) is given by

\displaystyle\phi(g,f)\ast\phi(G,F)=\left(g(z^{2}),z,\frac{f(z^{2})}{z}\right)*\left(G(z^{2}),z,\frac{F(z^{2})}{z}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(g(z^{2})G\left(\left(\sqrt{f(z^{2})}\right)^{2}\right),\sqrt{\frac{z^{2}}{f(z^{2})}}\sqrt{f(z^{2})},\sqrt{\frac{f(z^{2})}{z^{2}}}\frac{F\left(\left(\sqrt{f(z^{2})}\right)^{2}\right)}{\sqrt{f(z^{2})}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(g(z^{2})G\left(f(z^{2})\right),z,\frac{F\left(f(z^{2})\right)}{z}\right)

The righthand side of Equation (
2.1
) is given by

\displaystyle\phi\left((g,f)*(G,F)\right)=\phi(gG(f),F(f))=\left(g(z^{2})G(f(z^{2})),z,\frac{F(f(z^{2}))}{z}\right).

Thus,
\phi
is a homomorphism. To prove the claim that
\phi
is a monomorphism, we show that
\phi
is also one-to-one. Assume that, for some Riordan arrays
(g,f)
and
(G,F)
, the mapping
\phi(g,f)=\phi(G,F)
. Then,

\left(g(z^{2}),z,\frac{f(z^{2})}{z}\right)=\left(G(z^{2}),z,\frac{F(z^{2})}{z}\right).

Clearly, this implies that the functions
g(z^{2})=G(z^{2})
and
f(z^{2})=F(z^{2})
. Therefore,
(g,f)=(G,F)
. Thus, the claim is true.
∎

It is not difficult to show that the map
\psi:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{DR}
defined by

\psi(g,f)=\left(g(z^{2}),\frac{f(z^{2})}{z},z\right)

is also a group monomorphism. We define the set

\displaystyle\biggl\{\left(g(z^{2}),\frac{f(z^{2})}{z},z\right):g(z)\in\mathcal{F}_{0},f(z)\in\mathcal{F}_{1}\biggl\}

\displaystyle=\biggl\{\left(g,f_{1},z\right):\ \text{$g$ is an even function, $f_{1}$ is an odd function}\biggl\}

to be the
type-2 almost Appell subgroup
of the Double Riordan group. Therefore, we know that at least two copies of the Riordan group are found in the Double Riordan group. Extending this mapping to the
k
-Riordan group, we get the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Denoting the
k
-Riordan group by
k\mathcal{R}
, the map
\phi_{k}:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow k\mathcal{R}
defined by

\phi_{k}(g,f)=\left(g(z^{k}),\underbrace{z,z,...,z}_{\text{$k-1$}},\frac{f(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}}\right)

is a group monomorphism. Furthermore, the map
\phi_{k}:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow k\mathcal{R}
is a group monomorphism for any rearrangement of the
k
multiplier functions. The Riordan group has
k
known isomorphic copies in the
k
-Riordan group.

Proof.

Let
(g,f)
and
(G,F)
be Riordan arrays, and let
h=\sqrt[k]{f(z^{k})}
. Note that this implies
h^{k}=f(z^{k})
. Then,

\displaystyle\phi_{k}(g,f)*\phi_{k}(G,F)=\left(g(z^{k}),\underbrace{z,z,...,z}_{\text{$k-1$}},\frac{f(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}}\right)*\left(G(z^{k}),\underbrace{z,z,...,z}_{\text{$k-1$}},\frac{F(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(g(z^{k})G(f(z^{k})),\underbrace{\frac{z}{h}h,...,\frac{z}{h}h}_{\text{$k-1$}},\frac{\frac{f(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}}}{h}\frac{F(f(z^{k}))}{h^{k-1}}\right)=\left(g(z^{k})G(f(z^{k})),\underbrace{z,...,z}_{\text{$k-1$}},\frac{f(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}}\frac{F(f(z^{k}))}{h^{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(g(z^{k})G(f(z^{k})),\underbrace{z,...,z}_{\text{$k-1$}},\frac{F(f(z^{k}))}{z^{k-1}}\right).

Also,

\displaystyle\phi_{k}\left((g,f)*(G,F)\right)=\phi_{k}(gG(f),F(f))=\left(g(z^{k})G(f(z^{k})),\underbrace{z,z,...,z}_{\text{$k-1$}},\frac{F(f(z^{k}))}{z^{k-1}}\right).

Thus,
\phi_{k}
is group homomorphism. It is straightforward to see the homomorphism is also one-to-one. The image of the map is the set

S_{k}=\biggl\{\Big(g(z^{k}),\underbrace{z,z,...,z}_{\text{$k-1$}},\frac{f(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}}\Big):g(z)\in\mathcal{F}_{0},\ \text{and}\ f(z)\in\mathcal{F}_{1}\biggl\}.

Rearranging the multiplier functions of elements in
S_{k}
and mapping the Riordan group to that set is also a group monomorphism.
∎

\biggl\{\left(g(z^{3}),z,\frac{f_{1}(z^{3})}{z^{2}},\frac{f_{2}(z^{3})}{z^{2}}:g(z)\in\mathcal{F}_{0}\ \text{and}\ f_{1}(z),f_{2}(z)\in\mathcal{F}_{1}\right)\biggl\}

Theorem 6
.

(Monomorphism Theorem II)
Let
g(z)\in\mathcal{F}_{0}

f_{1}(z),f_{2}(z)\in\mathcal{F}_{1}

\left(g(z^{2}),\frac{f_{1}(z^{2})}{z},\frac{f_{2}(z^{2})}{z}\right)

(2.2)

is a Double Riordan array. The map
\chi:\mathcal{DR}\rightarrow 3\mathcal{R}

\chi\left(g(z^{2}),\frac{f_{1}(z^{2})}{z},\frac{f_{2}(z^{2})}{z}\right)=\left(g(z^{3}),z,\frac{f_{1}(z^{3})}{z^{2}},\frac{f_{2}(z^{3})}{z^{2}}\right)

Select generating functions

Also, let
h(z^{3})=\sqrt[3]{\frac{f_{1}(z^{3})f_{2}(z^{3})}{z^{3}}}

h^{3}(z^{3})=\frac{{f_{1}(z^{3})f_{2}(z^{3})}}{z^{3}}
. To show that
\alpha

\chi(D_{1})\cdot\chi(D_{2})=\chi(D_{1}*D_{2})

The lefthand side of Equation (
2.2

\displaystyle\chi(D_{1})\cdot\chi(D_{2})=\chi\left(g(z^{2}),\frac{f_{1}(z^{2})}{z},\frac{f_{2}(z^{2})}{z}\right)\cdot\chi\left(G(z^{2}),\frac{F_{1}(z^{2})}{z},\frac{F_{2}(z^{2})}{z}\right)

So far, we have shown that at least two isomorphic copies of the Riordan group are in the Double Riordan group, and at least three isomorphic copies of the Double Riordan group are in the Triple Riordan group. Generalizing these results, we get the theorem below.

Theorem 7
.

(Generalized Monomorphism Theorem)
Let
k
be a nonnegative integer. Then, there are at least
k+1
copies of the
k
-Riordan group in the
(k+1)
-Riordan group.

Proof.

Let
g(z)\in\mathcal{F}_{0}
and
f_{1}(z),...,f_{k}(z)\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
. Define the maps
\chi_{i}:k\mathcal{R}\rightarrow(k+1)\mathcal{R}
for integers
1\leq i\leq k+1
such that

\begin{cases}\chi_{1}\left(g(z^{k}),\frac{f_{1}(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}},...,\frac{f_{k}(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}}\right)=\Big(g(z^{k+1}),z,\frac{f_{1}(z^{k+1})}{z^{k}},...,\frac{f_{k}(z^{k+1})}{z^{k}}\Big)\\
\chi_{2}\left(g(z^{k}),\frac{f_{1}(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}},...,\frac{f_{k}(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}}\right)=\Big(g(z^{k+1}),\frac{f_{1}(z^{k+1})}{z^{k}},z,...,\frac{f_{k}(z^{k+1})}{z^{k}}\Big)\\
\indent{\vdots}\\
\chi_{k+1}\left(g(z^{k}),\frac{f_{1}(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}},...,\frac{f_{k}(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}}\right)=\Big(g(z^{k+1}),\frac{f_{1}(z^{k+1})}{z^{k}},...,\frac{f_{k}(z^{k+1})}{z^{k}},z\Big),\end{cases}

where
\chi_{i}
fixes
z
in the
(i+1)^{th}
position in the list of generating functions of the the
(k+1)
-Riordan array. Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
are specific cases of the proposition when
k=1
and
k=2
, respectively. We show the proof that
\chi_{1}
is a group homomorphism and the remaining maps will follow a similar argument. Let
\left(g(z^{k}),\frac{f_{1}(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}},...,\frac{f_{k}(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}}\right)
and
\left(G(z^{k}),\frac{F_{1}(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}},...,\frac{F_{k}(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}}\right)
be
(k+1)
-Riordan arrays. Then,

\displaystyle\chi_{1}\left(g(z^{k}),\frac{f_{1}(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}},...,\frac{f_{k}(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}}\right)*\chi_{1}\left(G(z^{k}),\frac{F_{1}(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}},...,\frac{F_{k}(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\Big(g(z^{k+1}),z,\frac{f_{1}(z^{k+1})}{z^{k}},...,\frac{f_{k}(z^{k+1})}{z^{k}}\Big)*\Big(G(z^{k+1}),z,\frac{F_{1}(z^{k+1})}{z^{k}},...,\frac{F_{k}(z^{k+1})}{z^{k}}\Big)

Let

h(z^{k+1})=\sqrt[k+1]{\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}f_{j}(z^{k+1})}{z^{(k+1)(k-1)}}},\ \ \text{which implies that}\ \ h^{k+1}(z^{k+1})=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}f_{j}(z^{k+1})}{z^{(k+1)(k-1)}}

for the
(k+1)
-Riordan array
\Big(g(z^{k+1}),z,\frac{f_{1}(z^{k+1})}{z^{k}},...,\frac{f_{k}(z^{k+1})}{z^{k}}\Big)
. Then,

\displaystyle\Big(g(z^{k+1}),z,\frac{f_{1}(z^{k+1})}{z^{k}},...,\frac{f_{k}(z^{k+1})}{z^{k}}\Big)*\Big(G(z^{k+1}),z,\frac{F_{1}(z^{k+1})}{z^{k}},...,\frac{F_{k}(z^{k+1})}{z^{k}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\left(g(z^{k+1})G\left(h^{k+1}(z^{k+1})\right),\frac{z}{h(z^{k+1})}h(z^{k+1}),\frac{\frac{f_{1}(z^{k+1})}{z^{k}}}{h(z^{k+1})}\frac{F_{1}(h^{k+1}(z^{k+1}))}{h^{k}(z^{k+1})},...,\frac{\frac{f_{k}(z^{k+1}(z^{k+1}))}{z^{k}}}{h(z^{k+1})}\frac{F_{k}(h^{k+1}(z^{k+1}))}{h^{k}(z^{k+1})}\right)

\displaystyle\chi_{1}\left(\left(g(z^{k}),\frac{f_{1}(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}},...,\frac{f_{k}(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}}\right)*\left(G(z^{k}),\frac{F_{1}(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}},...,\frac{F_{k}(z^{k})}{z^{k-1}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(g(z^{k+1})G\left(h^{k+1}(z^{k+1})\right),z,\frac{f_{1}(z^{k+1})}{z^{k}}\frac{F_{1}(h^{k+1}(z^{k+1}))}{h^{k+1}(z^{k+1})},...,\frac{f_{k}(z^{k+1})}{z^{k}}\frac{F_{k}(h^{k+1}(z^{k+1}))}{h^{k+1}(z^{k+1})}\right).

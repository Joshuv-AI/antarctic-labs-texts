Anyon polarons as a window into the competing phases of the Kitaev-Gamma-Gamma’ model
Chuan Chen, Inti Sodemann Villadiego

Abstract
We investigate the dispersions of anyon quasi-particles in the Kitaev honeycomb spin-liquid perturbed by Γ \Gamma and Γ ′ \Gamma^{\prime} couplings in order to understand phase transitions into competing states through anyon gap-closing instabilities. We demonstrate how anyon gap closings allow to understand phase transitions into a plethora of previously identified competing phases—including zigzag, stripy, 120 ∘ 120^{\circ} , and incommensurate spiral phases—and are in agreement with numerical studies not only on the nature of the phases, but also on the specific critical values of Γ \Gamma and Γ ′ \Gamma^{\prime} couplings. Remarkably, when the anti-ferromagnetic Kitaev model is perturbed by an ferromagnetic Γ \Gamma interaction, we find that the single-vison and fermion gaps remain open while the gap of a magnon-like local boson vanishes, implying that the resulting state has coexistence of a spontaneous broken symmetry and the fractionalization pattern of the Kitaev spin liquid. The magnetic long-range order could be either a stripy antiferromagnet or an incommensurate spiral, depending on the sign of Γ ′ \Gamma^{\prime} .

FM Kitaev model

III.1
Consequences of the
\Gamma
interaction

As shown in earlier sections, for a FM Kitaev interaction, the
\Gamma
term induces strong single-vison hopping,
causing the vison gap to close at relatively small
\Gamma

Interestingly, we have found that the gap closes at an IC wave vector
\bm{k}\approx 0.67\,\mathrm{M}

between the
\Gamma
and
\mathrm{M}
points when
\Gamma\approx 0.035
,
and at the
\Gamma
and
\mathrm{K}
points when
\Gamma\approx-0.02
(see Fig.
3

7

These vison gap closings signal phase transitions into new phases driven by vison proliferation.
However, the nature of the emergent phases is difficult to determine directly.
This difficulty stems from several factors.
First, since the model is gapless, the vison is a non-local excitation,
making it unclear how to assign a self-statistical phase to it.
Moreover, in nearby phases where the matter fermions are gapped by a Haldane mass,
the vison becomes a local excitation but acquires a non-Abelian character.
A microscopic understanding of what phase transitions can be driven by the softening of such
non-Abelian particles remains a major open problem (see, however, Ref.
Zou and He 2020

for a field-theoretic proposal of a direct continuous transition from non-Abelian states
to short-range entangled states).
We expect, however, that generally the closure of the single-vison gap drives confinement of all non-local quasiparticles, leading to the disappearance of topological order.
This can be argued by noting that the vison “core” is a boson dressed by the non-local cloud of itinerant fermions
Chen et al. 2022
;
Chen et al. 2018
,
and the condensation of this core is responsible for confinement, as it can be more clearly understood in Abelian cases
Pozo et al. 2021
.
This principle is supported by our numerical estimates for the critical
\Gamma

which agree with previous numerical results for phase transitions into other ordered states
Sørensen et al. 2021
;
Lee et al. 2020

We have also found that the fermionic vison pairs acquire significant
\Gamma

IV
AFM Kitaev model

IV.1
Consequences of the
\Gamma
interaction

In the AFM Kitaev model, single visons do not acquire
\Gamma
-induced hopping and thus remain gapped as
\Gamma
increases.
The fermions also have a weaker hopping, and their gap closes at much larger
\Gamma
values (around
\Gamma\approx 0.38
and

\Gamma\approx-0.8
) compared to the FM case.

On the other hand, the boson gap closes more rapidly than those of single visons and fermions.
Unlike the FM case, where the lowest band along the
\Gamma
axis originates from NNN vison pairs,
the lowest band here is purely from NN vison pairs, whose condensation leads directly
to finite expectation values of the local Pauli operators with
magnetic long-range order.
At
\Gamma\approx 0.28
, the boson gap closes simultaneously at the
\Gamma
and
\mathrm{K}
points (see Fig.
6
(f)).
We find that the soft mode at
\mathrm{K}
point induces an in-plane FM
{}_{U_{6}}
order, as shown in Fig.
8
(e), while the
\Gamma
mode
produces an out-of-plane Néel order (see Fig.
8
(f)), which maps to an out-of-plane FM order under the
U_{6}
transformation.
Therefore, our results are fully consistent with the expected FM
{}_{U_{6}}
order
Chaloupka and Khaliullin 2015
;
Sørensen et al. 2021
;
Rousochatzakis 2020
;
Rousochatzakis et al. 2024
.
The dynamical structure factor
S(\omega,\bm{Q})
near the transition point, shown in Fig.
9
(f), exhibits a magnon-like low-energy
dispersion touching zero at both the
\mathrm{K}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
points.
Notably, it closely resembles results from recent random-phase approximation calculations
Rao et al. 2025
.
In the presence of
\Gamma^{\prime}
interaction, the
\Gamma
–
\mathrm{K}
degeneracy is lifted:
a
\Gamma
-mode-driven Néel order emerges for
\Gamma^{\prime}>0
, while a
\mathrm{K}
-mode-driven FM
{}_{U_{6}}
order appears for
\Gamma^{\prime}<0
(see Fig.
7
(b)).
Interestingly, a similar trend has also been reported in a vison-pair analysis of the
K
–
J
–
\Gamma
model, with
\Gamma\textquoteright
replaced by
J

Zhang et al. 2021
.

Around
\Gamma\approx-0.4
, we find that the boson band touches zero at an IC wave vector
\bm{Q}_{\mathrm{IC}}
located between the

\Gamma
and
\mathrm{M}
points (see Fig.
6
(e)), therefore the condensation of this mode is expected to
induce an IC spiral order (see its pattern in Fig.
8
(g) and dynamical structure factor in Fig.
9
(e)).
Moreover, a nearly gapless mode also appears at the
\mathrm{M}
point, whose condensation leads to a stripy order,
as shown in Fig.
8
(h).
The close proximity in energy between this mode and the IC mode indicates a strong competition between the two orders—consistent with recent numerical studies,
where both types of order emerge depending on system size, and are collectively referred to as a “spin nematic” phase
Sørensen et al. 2021
.
Upon introducing a small positive
\Gamma^{\prime}
, the
\mathrm{M}
mode softens first, favoring a stripy phase; conversely,
for negative
\Gamma^{\prime}
, the IC mode becomes soft first, leading to an IC spiral order where the value of
\bm{Q}_{\mathrm{IC}}
evolves with
\Gamma^{\prime}
.
In agreement with our findings, the IC order has also been obtained in a vison-pair analysis of the
K
-
J
-
\Gamma
model with
AFM Kitaev coupling and
\Gamma<0

Zhang et al. 2021
.
Morevoer, previous numerical studies have reported analogous behavior when
\Gamma^{\prime}
is replaced by a Heisenberg coupling:
the system exhibits stripy order for
J>0
and IC spiral order
J<0

Rau et al. 2014
.

It is worth noting that the condensation of bosonic vison pairs does not immediately lead to spinon confinement,
as they are local bosons whose statistical interactions with matter fermions are trivial.
Therefore, since we have found that single visons remain gapped due to
the absence of
\Gamma
-induced hopping, the KSL is likely to evolve into
a magnetically ordered QSL before undergoing a transition to a conventional ordered phase at larger
\Gamma
values,
where single-vison condensation occurs.

To further investigate this, we analyze the
\Gamma
-induced single-vison hopping at second order.
We find that it also vanishes exactly in the AFM Kitaev model, suggesting that single visons are unlikely to condense via enhancing its mobility.
However, we have found that at third order, a
\Gamma
-induced chemical potential term arises:

\propto-\Gamma^{3}\sum_{i}n_{i}^{\mathrm{v}}
(see details in the Supplemental Material).
This term favors (disfavors) single-vison excitations for
\Gamma>0
(
\Gamma<0
).
Including this term leads to single-vison gap closing for
\Gamma>0
, while they remain gapped for
\Gamma<0

(see the gray dashed line in Fig.
7
(b)).
This analysis provides additional evidence that the FM
{}_{U_{6}}
order observed for
\Gamma>0
corresponds to a conventional (confined) magnetic order,
whereas the IC phase at
\Gamma<0
may represent a magnetically ordered yet genuine QSL.
Although no direct numerical evidence for the QSL has been reported, previous DMRG studies on a two-leg ladder
identified this regime as a line of first-order transitions between stripy and IC phases,
leaving open the possibility that such an exotic QSL could emerge in the two-dimensional limit
Sørensen et al. 2021
.

IV.2
Consequences of the
\Gamma^{\prime}
interaction

Different from the
\Gamma
interaction, the
\Gamma^{\prime}
interaction induces a finite single-vison hopping.
Similar to the case of a Zeeman field, the
\Gamma^{\prime}
-induced single vison hopping has a fractionalized translational symmetry,
resulting in two single-vison bands with opposite Chern number
Chen and Villadiego 2023
;
Joy and Rosch 2022
.
The single-vison gap closes around
\Gamma^{\prime}\approx\pm 0.21
.
The fermionic vison bands are also dispersive (see Fig.
5
), with the gap closing first at the

\mathrm{M}
point for
\Gamma^{\prime}\approx 0.1
and at the
\Gamma
point for
\Gamma^{\prime}\approx-0.1
.

At
\Gamma^{\prime}\approx 0.09
and
\Gamma^{\prime}\approx-0.13
, the bosonic vison pairs close gap at the
\Gamma
point.
The condensation of the soft boson results in an out-of-plane Néel order, similar to the one shown in Fig.
8
(f).
The corresponding dynamical structure factors near both transitions exhibit similar features
—a magnon-like low-energy band and a mushroom-shaped high-energy continuum (see Figs.
9
(g) and (h))—which could be examined in future numerical studies.

V
Discussion

We have investigated the
K
-
\Gamma
-
\Gamma^{\prime}
model from the perspective of the quasiparticles of the KSL,
treating the non-Kitaev
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
interactions as perturbations and analyzing their effects on the
quasiparticles’ dispersions.
This quasiparticle-based approach has previously been applied to other extended Kitaev models, including the
K
-
J
-
\Gamma

model
Zhang et al. 2021
, Kitaev model with external magnetic fields
Joy and Rosch 2022
;
Chen and Villadiego 2023
;
Chen and Villadiego 2025
,
and multilayer Kitaev systems
Joy and Rosch 2024
, offering valuable insights into their physical properties
and the competitions between their QSLs and other magnetically ordered phases.

The stronger (weaker)
\Gamma
-induced hopping of single visons and vison pairs in the FM (AFM) Kitaev model naturally explains
the weaker (stronger) stability of the KSL under
\Gamma
perturbations, as reflected in the phase diagram shown in Fig.
7
.
This suggests that the suppression of the KSL is driven by vison proliferation.

The softening of bosonic vison pairs provides a compelling mechanism for the emergence of various magnetic orders, which we have been able to infer by analyzing the momentum and other quantum numbers of the wavefunction of softened bosonic modes.
For example, in the AFM Kitaev model, the FM
{}_{U_{6}}
order at
\Gamma>0
arises from the condensation of the
\mathrm{K}
mode
or
\Gamma
mode (see Figs.
8
(e)–(f) and Fig.
9
(f)); the IC spiral order and the stripy orders at
\Gamma<0

are driven by softened modes at
\bm{Q}_{\mathrm{IC}}
and
\mathrm{M}
points (see Figs.
8
(g)–(h) and Fig.
9
(e)), respectively.
In the FM Kitaev model with
\Gamma^{\prime}<0
, although the
\mathrm{M}
mode—which induces a zigzag order consistent with
the moment direction observed in
\alpha
-RuCl
3
—is only the second lowest-energy mode (see Fig.
6
(c) and
Fig.
8
(b)), its energy might be lowered by extending the vison-pair subspace, possibly allowing it to become the softest mode.

The NNN vison pairs (
o
) also play an essential role at low energies.
In the FM Kitaev model, they form the lowest bosonic band along the
\Gamma
axis
in the
\Gamma
-
\Gamma^{\prime}
phase diagram (see Fig.
7
(a)).
For
\Gamma>0
, the softened mode at
\Gamma
point carries finite orbital angular momentum and induces a paramagnetic phase with anisotropic bond energies,
offering an explanation for the nematic paramagnetic phase observed in previous numerical studies
Lee et al. 2020
;
Gohlke et al. 2020
.

One of our main finding is that, in the AFM Kitaev model with
\Gamma<0
, single visons remain gapped and matter fermions
stay deconfined even after the bosonic vison pairs condense (see Fig.
7
(b)).
This raises the tantalizing possibility of an intermediate magnetically ordered QSL before the system transitions into
a trivial magnetically ordered phase at larger
\Gamma
values.
Our findings therefore allow us to conjecture that the AFM Kitaev model with
\Gamma\lesssim-0.4|K|

realizes a QSL that retains the anyon content of the KSL while simultaneously exhibiting broken symmetry.
The symmetry-breaking magnetic order is likely either stripy or a IC spiral (see Figs.
8
(g)–(h)).
Consistently, the IC order has also been reported in a previous vison-pair study of the
K
-
J
-
\Gamma
model
Zhang et al. 2021
.
The coexistence of fractionalization and a spiraling magnetic moment is particularly intriguing,
as the IC moment may remain strictly gapless.
We hope that future numerical and analytical studies will provide a more direct assessment of this fascinating possibility.

We have also demonstrated how correlation functions of local observables can be directly related to the bosonic vison pairs, and used these modes to explicitly compute the dynamical structure factor.
Near phase transitions into magnetically ordered states, the dynamical structure factor exhibits low-energy magnon-like dispersions along with broad high-energy continua.
Notably, in some cases, our results resemble those obtained from random-phase approximation studies
(cf. Fig.
9
(f) and Fig. 9(c) of Ref.
Rao et al. 2025
).
Such features serve as valuable signatures for probing emergent phases in Kitaev materials.
This framework can also be extended to study other experimental probes, such as Raman spectroscopy
Joy and Rosch 2025
,
providing a promising direction for future investigations.

Methods

VI.1
Magnetic orders from the condensation of bosonic vison pairs

The condensation of a bosonic mode can give rise to magnetic long-range order,
provided it carries weight on NN vison pairs (see also Sec. V of Ref.
Zhang et al. 2021
).
Consider a soft boson mode
\beta_{\bm{q}}
with momentum
\bm{q}
:

\displaystyle\beta_{\bm{q}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=d_{\bm{q},x}^{\dagger}w_{x}(\bm{q})+d_{\bm{q},y}^{\dagger}w_{y}(\bm{q})+d_{\bm{q},z}^{\dagger}w_{z}(\bm{q})

\displaystyle+o_{\bm{q},x}^{\dagger}v_{x}(\bm{q})+o_{\bm{q},y}^{\dagger}v_{y}(\bm{q})+o_{\bm{q},z}^{\dagger}v_{z}(\bm{q}).

(19)

where the
N\times 1
vector
w_{\alpha}(\bm{q})
is its weight on NN
d_{\bm{q},\alpha}
vison pairs
and
v_{\alpha}(q)
is its weight on NNN
o_{\bm{q},\alpha}
vison pairs.
Note
d_{\bm{q},\alpha}^{\dagger}=(d_{\bm{q},\alpha,1}^{\dagger},\dots,d_{\bm{q},\alpha,N}^{\dagger})
(see Eq. (
13
)),
and

d_{\bm{q},\alpha,l}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\bm{r}\in A}e^{i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}d_{\bm{r},\alpha,l}^{\dagger}.

(20)

The local magnetic moment induced by the condensation of
\beta_{\bm{q}}
mode reads:

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{\bm{r}}^{\alpha}\rangle\propto e^{i\theta}\langle\Omega|\sigma_{\bm{r}}^{\alpha}\beta_{\bm{q}}^{\dagger}|\Omega\rangle+\mathrm{H.c.}

(21)

Here
\theta
is the condensation phase of the soft mode.
It can be shown that, for
\bm{r}\in A
,

\displaystyle\langle\Omega|\sigma_{\bm{r}}^{\alpha}\beta_{\bm{q}}^{\dagger}|\Omega\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}e^{i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}\langle\Omega|\sigma_{\bm{r}}^{\alpha}d_{\bm{r},\alpha}^{\dagger}|\Omega\rangle w_{\alpha}(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}e^{i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}P_{\alpha,A}w_{\alpha}(\bm{q}).

(22)

Here

\displaystyle P_{\alpha,A}=\langle\Omega|\sigma_{\bm{r}}^{\alpha}(d_{\bm{r},\alpha,1}^{\dagger},\dots,d_{\bm{r},\alpha,N}^{\dagger})|\Omega\rangle.

(23)

Similarly, for
\bm{r}^{\prime}\in B
, there is

\displaystyle\langle\Omega|\sigma_{\bm{r}^{\prime}}^{\alpha}\beta_{\bm{q}}^{\dagger}|\Omega\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}e^{i\bm{q}\cdot(\bm{r}^{\prime}-\bm{\delta}_{\alpha})}\langle\Omega|\sigma_{\bm{r}^{\prime}}^{\alpha}d_{\bm{r}^{\prime}-\bm{\delta}_{\alpha},\alpha}^{\dagger}|\Omega\rangle w_{\alpha}(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}e^{i\bm{q}\cdot(\bm{r}^{\prime}-\bm{\delta}_{\alpha})}P_{\alpha,B}w_{\alpha}(\bm{q}),

(24)

with

\displaystyle P_{\alpha,B}=\langle\Omega|\sigma_{\bm{r}^{\prime}}^{\alpha}(d_{\bm{r}^{\prime}-\bm{\delta}_{\alpha},\alpha,1}^{\dagger},\dots,d_{\bm{r}^{\prime}-\bm{\delta}_{\alpha},\alpha,N}^{\dagger})|\Omega\rangle.

(25)

Therefore, once
w_{\alpha}(\bm{q})
and
P_{A/B,\alpha}
are determined, the resulting local moments can be obtained.
In our calculations, we find that for orders induced by the
\Gamma
or
\mathrm{M}
modes—such as FM, Néel, zigzag, and stripy—the direction of
the local moments is independent of
\theta
. In contrast, for the
120^{\circ}
order induced by the
\mathrm{K}
mode,
the local moment direction depends on
\theta
, consistent with Ref.
Zhang et al. 2021
.

VI.2
Dynamical structure factor from the bosonic vison pairs

In this section, we discuss the dynamical structure factor approximated from the bosonic vison pairs,
a method first used in Ref.
Zhang et al. 2021
.
The dynamical structure factor is defined as

\displaystyle S(\omega,\bm{Q})

\displaystyle=\int dt\,e^{i\omega t}S(t,\bm{Q}),

\displaystyle S(t,\bm{Q})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{s}}\sum_{\alpha}\langle\mathrm{GS}|\sigma_{\bm{Q}}^{\alpha}(t)\sigma_{-\bm{Q}}^{\alpha}(0)|\mathrm{GS}\rangle,

(26)

where
N_{s}=2N
denotes the total number of sites in the system. The Fourier tranform of Pauli operators is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{\bm{Q}}^{\alpha}=\sum_{\bm{r}\in A}e^{-i\bm{Q}\cdot\bm{r}}\sigma_{\bm{r}}^{\alpha}+e^{-i\bm{Q}\cdot(\bm{r}+\bm{\delta}_{\alpha})}\sigma_{\bm{r}+\bm{\delta}_{\alpha}}^{\alpha}.

(27)

Note that due to the bipartitle nature of the honeycomb lattice,
\sigma_{\bm{Q}}^{\alpha}\neq\sigma_{\bm{Q}+\bm{b}}^{\alpha}
with
\bm{b}
being
a reciprocal lattice vector.
As a result, it is helpful to evaluate
S(\omega,\bm{Q})
in the extended BZ.

|\mathrm{GS}\rangle=|\Omega\rangle
before the breakdown of KSL.
Using the Lehmann representation, Eq. (
VI.2
) can be recast into:

\displaystyle S(t,\bm{Q})=\frac{1}{N_{s}}\sum_{l}e^{-i(E_{l}-E_{0})t}|\langle\Omega|\sigma_{\bm{Q}}^{\alpha}|l\rangle|^{2},

(28)

with
E_{l}-E_{0}
being the excitation energy of
l
-th excited state.
Since a local Pauli operator always excites a single bosonic vison pair from the ground state
|\Omega\rangle

Knolle et al. 2014
;
Knolle et al. 2015
,
one can choose
|l\rangle
to be the single-boson eigenstate
\beta_{\bm{Q},l}^{\dagger}|\Omega\rangle
,
where
\beta_{\bm{Q},l}^{\dagger}
creates the
l
-th eigenmode with momentum
\bm{Q}
of the boson Hamiltonian.
Note that for
\bm{Q}
outside the first BZ, one should use
\beta_{[\bm{Q}],l}^{\dagger}|\Omega\rangle
,
where
[\bm{Q}]
denotes the corresponding wave vector folded back into the first BZ.
After some algebra, one can show that:

\displaystyle\langle\Omega|\sigma_{\bm{Q}}^{\alpha}\beta_{\bm{Q},l}^{\dagger}|\Omega\rangle=\sqrt{N}(P_{\alpha,A}+e^{-i\bm{Q}\cdot\bm{\delta}_{\alpha}}P_{\alpha,B})w_{\alpha}(\bm{Q}).

(29)

The
S(\omega,\bm{Q})
thus reads:

\displaystyle S(\omega,\bm{Q})

\displaystyle=\pi\sum_{l}\delta(\omega-\Delta E_{Q,l})

\displaystyle\times|(P_{\alpha,A}+e^{-i\bm{Q}\cdot\bm{\delta}_{\alpha}}P_{\alpha,B})w_{\alpha}(\bm{Q})|^{2}.

(30)

The Dirac delta function is approximated by a Lorentzian:
\delta(x)\approx\frac{\eta/\pi}{\omega^{2}+\eta^{2}}
,
where we take
\eta=0.04|K|
in our calculation.
Fig.
10
presents the dynamical structure factor
S(\omega,\bm{Q})
of the ideal Kitaev model, computed from bosonic vison pairs.
The results show good agreement with the exact solution of the Kitaev model
Knolle et al. 2014
;
Knolle et al. 2015
.

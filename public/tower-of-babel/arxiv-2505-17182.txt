Mock modularity of twisted index in CHL models
Nabamita Banerjee, Vedant Bhutra, and Ranveer Kumar Singh

Abstract
We study the twisted partition function of quarter BPS states in CHL models and show that for a large class of single-centered black holes, the degeneracy of microstates is given by the Fourier coefficients of mock Jacobi forms. Our analysis is a continuation of the programme initiated by Dabholkar, Murthy and Zagier (DMZ) for 1 / 4 1/4 -BPS dyons in 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 string theory and further extended by Bhand, Sen and Singh (BSS) to quarter BPS states in CHL models. We also present the multiplicative lift construction of the partition function and comment on the additive lift of the same.

Introduction

Black holes are one of the most interesting outcomes of general relativity as they present perfect laboratories to test quantum aspects of gravity. There are various models in string theory that are useful for studying the microscopic physics of black holes. In particular, the four dimensional CHL models are hugely successful in this aspect. 2.2
Mock modular forms

Finally, we turn to mock modular forms. These are holomorphic functions that
almost
satisfy the modularity property, but not quite. Instead, they have a non-holomorphic
completion
, that is modular. Mock-modular forms fave found numerous applications in number theory, combinatorics, moonshine and theoretical physics. A good review on the subject is
[
22
]
, which we shall follow.

Definition 2.3 (Pure mock modular form).

A
(weakly holomorphic) pure mock modular form of weight
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathds{Z}
is the first member of the pair
(h,g)
such that,

1.

h
is a holomorphic function on
\mathds{H}
with at most exponential growth at all cusps,

2.

g
, called the
shadow of
h
, is a holomorphic modular form of weight
2-k
.

3.

\widehat{h}\coloneqq h+g^{*}
, called the
completion of
h
, transforms like a holomorphic modular form of weight
k
.

The function
g^{*}
is called the
non-holomorphic Eichler integral
, and is a solution to the differential equation,

(4\pi\Img(\tau))^{k}\,\frac{\partial g^{*}}{\partial\overline{\tau}}=-2\pi i\,\overline{g}.

(2.23)

Being a modular form,
g
has a Fourier expansion, given by
g(\tau)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}q^{n}
. Thus, we can fix
g^{*}
by setting

g^{*}(\tau)=\begin{cases}-\overline{a}_{0}\dfrac{(4\pi\Img(\tau))^{-k+1}}{-k+1}+\sum_{n>0}n^{k-1}\,\overline{a}_{n}\,\Gamma(1-k,4\pi n\Img(\tau))\,q^{-n},&k\neq 1\\
-\overline{a}_{0}\ln(4\pi\Img(\tau))+\sum_{n>0}\,\overline{a}_{n}\,\Gamma(0,4\pi n\Img(\tau))\,q^{-n}&k=1\end{cases}

(2.24)

where
\Gamma(a,b)
is the incomplete gamma function:

\Gamma(a,b):=\int_{b}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{a}\frac{dt}{t}.

(2.25)

Holomorphicity in
h
implies that
\widehat{h}
is related to the shadow
g
by,

(4\pi\Img(\tau))^{k}\,\frac{\partial\widehat{h}}{\partial\overline{\tau}}=-2\pi i\,\overline{g}.

(2.26)

Definition 2.4 (Pure mock Jacobi form).

A
pure mock Jacobi form of weight
k
and index
m
is a holomorphic function
\varphi
on
\mathds{H}\times\mathds{C}
that satisfies the elliptic transformation property (
2.4
) and the cuspidal condition, such that the theta coefficients of
\varphi
are mock modular forms of weight
k-\frac{1}{2}
. The
completion

\widehat{\varphi}
of

is defined as

\widehat{\varphi}(\tau,z)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathds{Z}/2m\mathds{Z}}\widehat{h}_{\ell}(\tau)\,\vartheta_{m,\ell}(\tau,z)=\varphi(\tau,z)+\sum_{\ell\in\mathds{Z}/2m\mathds{Z}}g^{*}_{\ell}(\tau)\vartheta_{m,\ell}(\tau,z).

(2.27)

where
g_{\ell}
is the shadow of
h_{\ell}
. This is a Jacobi form of weight
k
and index
m
.

This is a natural generalization of pure mock modular forms. However, in most practical applications, we have more complicated functions as defined below. A prominent example will be the Appell-Lerch sums that we define in the next section.

Definition 2.5 (Mixed mock modular forms).

A
mixed holomorphic mock modular form of weight
k|\vec{\ell}
, with
k,\ell_{j}\in\frac{1}{2}\mathds{Z}
, is a holomorphic function
h(\tau)
with polynomial growth at cusps and completion of the form
\widehat{h}=h+\sum_{j}f_{j}g_{j}^{*}

Finally, we have
mixed mock Jacobi forms
, which are defined analogous to the above, ie. they have theta expansions in which the coefficients are mixed mock modular forms,
with completions that transform like ordinary Jacobi forms.

2.3
Finite and polar parts

We return to the discussion of meromorphic Jacobi forms. Right away, we can define a

\varphi^{F}(\tau,z)\coloneqq\sum_{\ell\in\mathds{Z}/2m\mathds{Z}}h^{\star}_{\ell}(\tau)\,\widetilde{\vartheta}_{\frac{m}{M},\ell}(\tau,z),

As we have restricted the class of meromorphic Jacobi forms to those with simple or double poles at specific points as described earlier, we can define the residues

\mathbb{e}^{\frac{m}{M}\alpha z_{s}}\,\varphi(\tau,z_{s}+\epsilon)=\frac{E_{s}(\tau)}{(2\pi i\epsilon)^{2}}+\frac{D_{s}(\tau)-2\frac{m}{M}\alpha E_{s}(\tau)}{2\pi i\epsilon}+\cdots\qquad\text{as }\epsilon\to 0.

Then for

\text{Av}^{(m)}_{q}\big[f(y)\big]\coloneqq\sum_{\lambda\,\in\,\mathds{Z}}q^{m\lambda^{2}}y^{2m\lambda}f(q^{\lambda}y),

\vartheta_{m,\ell}(\tau,z)=q^{\frac{\ell^{2}}{4m}}\,\text{Av}_{q}^{(m)}[y^{\ell}].

For

-order rational function

\mathcal{R}_{c}^{(n)}(y)\coloneqq\frac{y^{c}}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\sum_{r\in\mathds{Z}}\frac{\mathbb{e}^{-cr}}{(z-r)^{n}}.

(2.32)

It has a simple pole of residue 1 at

5
Summary and discussion

Let us summarize the results of the present paper. We studied
\mathds{Z}_{N}
-invariant states in
\mathds{Z}_{M}
CHL models obtained by orbifolding type IIB string theory on
{\cal M}\times T^{2}
(
{\cal M}=\text{K}3,T^{4}
) with a
\mathds{Z}_{M}\times\mathds{Z}_{N}
symmetry at some point in the moduli space. These also include theories at those points in the moduli space where we have a
\mathds{Z}_{MN}
symmetry with gcd
(M,N)=1
since
\mathds{Z}_{MN}\cong\mathds{Z}_{M}\times\mathds{Z}_{N}
in this case. Starting with the partition function for
\mathds{Z}_{N}
-invariant quarter BPS dyonic states in
\mathds{Z}_{M}
CHL models, we explored its transformation properties. In particular, we found the invariance properties of the associated Siegel modular form
\Phi^{(M,N)}_{k}
. We found special generators of the subgroup
G<\rm{Sp}(4,\mathds{R})
under which
\Phi^{(M,N)}_{k}
transforms as a Siegel modular form. This is instrumental in deriving the modular properties of the degeneracy function. Next we found the Fourier-Jacobi coefficients of the partition function, which are meromorphic Jacobi forms and split into finite parts, which are mock Jacobi forms, and polar parts. From the mock Jacobi part, we extracted the degeneracy of the single-centered black holes. There are two independent Fourier decompositions of the Siegel modular form
\Phi^{(M,N)}_{k}
as given in equation (
3.43
). These two decompositions provide us with two distinct mock Jacobi forms. Depending on the range of charges, the degeneracies of the single-centered black holes can be found from either of the two functions. One can use the
S
-duality transformation to extend the range of charges for which the degeneracy can be computed using mock Jacobi forms.

There are two general ways of constructing Siegel modular forms: additive lifts and multiplicative lifts. The starting point of both these is a Jacobi form called the additive or multiplicative seed function. Some Siegel modular forms admit both an additive and a multiplicative lift construction. Although the original construction of the partition function of quarter BPS states in CHL model was via additive lift, their construction by precision counting of microstates
[
3
]
is most naturally expressed in terms of an infinite product. For the twisted-twined BPS states, the partition function again is given in terms of an infinite product form, see (
3.2
). In this paper, we described a method of expressing the partition function as an additive lift complementing and generalizing the result of
[
10
]
. To do so, we extensively made use of the multiplicative lift construction of Siegel modular forms
\Phi_{g,h}
associated to any commuting pair
(g,h)
of the Mathieu group
[
27
]
. This construction of Siegel modular forms has the advantage that if there exists an additive lift construction of
\Phi_{g,h}
, then the additive seed is straightforward to identify. We showed that, for
{\cal M}=\text{K}3
, the partition function
\Phi^{(M,N)}_{k}
of twisted-twined quarter BPS states is equal to
\Phi_{g_{M},g_{N}}
, where
(g_{M},g_{N})
is the generator of the
\mathds{Z}_{M}\times\mathds{Z}_{N}
symplectic automorphism of K3 identified with appropriate elements of the Mathieu group via Mukai’s theorem, see section
4.2
for details. This construction of partition function via multiplicative lift gives us a straightforward method to identify the additive seed, if one exists. We described the additive seeds for the cases
(M,N)=(2,2)
,
(2,4)
,
(4,4)
,
(3,3)
. The first three examples were discussed in
[
10
]
, which used the constructions of
[
41
,
43
]
as possible candidate for the partition function. Our result matches with the additive seed of
[
10
]
up to a minus sign.

We end the paper by discussing some open problems. An obvious next step would be to extend the analysis to the doubly twined sector and identify the charge vectors for which the single-centered degeneracy function will have the mock modular structure. We shall report on the same in future. A promising goal would be to gain further insight into the symmetry of the system, so that we have a better understanding of the partition function. In particular, the charge vectors that do not follow the mock modular structure need to be addressed appropriately. More precisely, it would be desirable to construct the generating function

F(\Omega)\coloneqq\sum_{(m,n,\ell)\in\mathds{Z}^{3}}d(m,n,\ell)\,\mathbb{e}^{m\tau+n\sigma+\ell z},

of single-centered degeneracies
d(m,n,\ell)
defined by (
3.16
), and study its modular properties, if any. It would also be useful to generalize the constructions of
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
and to find the Rademacher series expansion for the coefficients of mock Jacobi forms for the general
(M,N)
case. The final goal would be to prove Sen’s positivity conjecture for the general
(M,N)
case.

Appendix B
Deformation of contours

The following proposition lets us determine the charge sector for which we can deform the attractor contour
\mathcal{C}
to contour
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}

Proposition B.1
.

If
(n,\ell,m)\in A
, then the twisted-twined partition function
{\Phi^{M,N}_{k}}^{-1}
does not have poles in the region bounded by the attractor contour
\mathcal{C}
and the deformed contour
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
given by
\tau\to i\infty
.

Proof.

For small
\varepsilon
, as is the case for the attractor contour, the dominant contribution to the LHS of the pole equation (
3.19
) is given by the term
-n_{2}(\Img(\sigma)\Img(\tau)-\Img(z)^{2})
. The term inside the parenthesis is always positive on
\mathds{H}_{2}
, ensuring that the coefficient of
n_{2}
does not vanish. The above is true for the entire region between
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. Therefore, we must set
n_{2}=0
to satisfy the pole equation. Thus, we have the simplified condition,

n_{1}\sigma-m_{1}\tau+jz+m_{2}=0,

(B.1)

where,

m_{1}\in M\mathds{Z},\qquad n_{1},m_{2}\in\mathds{Z},\qquad j\in 2\mathds{Z}+1,\qquad m_{1}n_{1}+\frac{j^{2}}{4}=\frac{1}{4}.

(B.2)

As
n_{2}
carried the information about the twisting in the pole structure, setting it to zero in the region of interest implies that the pole structure for our discussion is the same as that of the un-twisted case, and the proof of the analogous theorem in
[
12
]
follows through. However, for completeness, we discuss the rest of the proof here too.

The imaginary parts of the integration variables is fixed on the contour. So from equation (
B.1
) we find,

n_{1}\Img(\sigma)-m_{1}\Img(\tau)+j\Img(z)=0,

(B.3)

which implies,

\Img(\tau)=\frac{2mn_{1}-j\ell}{m_{1}\varepsilon}.

(B.4)

The above describes the locations of the poles, so to ensure that there are no poles between
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, we demand,

\frac{2mn_{1}-j\ell}{m_{1}\varepsilon}\leq\frac{2n}{M\varepsilon},

(B.5)

where the RHS is the value of the imaginary part of
\tau
for the attractor contour.
15
15

15

For the equality, ie, the attractor point lying on the wall of marginal stability, the jump in the index on crossing the wall vanishes, ie, the residue vanishes. This is shown in
(
12
, Appendix B.2)
This simplifies to the expression,

\displaystyle\bigg(j+\ell\frac{m_{1}}{m}\bigg)^{2}+\frac{m_{1}^{2}}{m^{2}}\bigg(\frac{4nm}{M}-\ell^{2}\bigg)\geq 1,

(B.6)

which is true for all
m_{1}\in M\mathds{Z}
,
j\in 2\mathds{Z}+1
,
m_{1}|(j^{2}-1)/4
. This can be written as,

\displaystyle\min_{r\in M\mathds{Z},\,s\in\mathds{Z},\,s(s+1)=0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{r})}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{r\ell}{m}+2s+1\bigg)^{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{m^{2}}\bigg(\frac{4nm}{M}-\ell^{2}\bigg)\bigg]\geq 1,

(B.7)

where we have reparametrized
m_{1}=r
, and
j=2s+1
with
s\in\mathds{Z}
. Furthermore, single centered black holes exist only for charges
(\vec{Q},\vec{P})
such that
Q^{2}P^{2}-(Q\cdot P)^{2}>0
, which translates to

\frac{4nm}{M}-\ell^{2}>0.

(B.8)

This proves the theorem.
∎

Proposition B.2
.

If
(n,\ell,m)\in B
, then the twisted-twined partition function
{\Phi^{M,N}_{k}}^{-1}

Non-homogeneous boundary value problems for second-order degenerate hyperbolic equations and their application
Donghui Yang, Jie Zhong

Abstract
We study second-order hyperbolic equations with degenerate elliptic operators and non-homogeneous Dirichlet boundary inputs. We establish existence and regularity of weak solutions in weighted Sobolev spaces under mild assumptions on the degenerate weight. A Dirichlet map is constructed for the degenerate elliptic operator, leading to a solution theory that extends classical approaches to the degenerate setting. In particular, we derive energy estimates and well-posedness for boundary inputs of low regularity (in appropriate trace spaces), even though the classical Dirichlet-to-Neumann framework is not directly applicable in the degenerate setting. As an application, we prove an approximate controllability criterion, which generalizes the Hilbert Uniqueness Method to degenerate wave equations. Our framework accommodates higher-dimensional degenerate waves, non-homogeneous boundary conditions, and weighted functional analysis. We also illustrate how our criterion connects to higher-dimensional Grushin equations and waves with single-point degeneracy, and we highlight the remaining unique continuation/observability issue as an open problem.

Degenerate hyperbolic equations in multiple space dimensions present significant analytical difficulties. To establish controllability or stabilization results, one must first prove well-posedness and regularity of solutions—a highly nontrivial task. The main challenges lie in developing appropriate weighted Sobolev spaces to handle the degeneracy and in constructing a Dirichlet map or solution operator for non-homogeneous boundary data when the elliptic operator is degenerate. These aspects sharply distinguish degenerate hyperbolic equations from uniformly elliptic ones. In the classical theory, one often uses the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map or builds solutions via elliptic lifting of boundary traces. In the degenerate case, however, such an approach is not straightforward: the standard Dirichlet map may fail to exist or be bounded under degeneracy conditions. For instance, the results in
[
13
,
14
]

In this paper, we overcome these difficulties by developing a new framework for degenerate hyperbolic equations with boundary control. To this end, we introduce the concept of weight functions. We use the notion of

\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}w(x)\,dx\leq c\cdot\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{x\in K}w.

\Bigg(\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}w(x)\,dx\Bigg)\Bigg(\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}w(x)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\,dx\Bigg)^{p-1}\leq c.

(1.1)

The infimum of such constants

We focus on a prototypical second-order degenerate wave equation with Dirichlet boundary input:

\begin{cases}\partial_{tt}y(x,t)-\operatorname{div}\!\big(w(x)\nabla y(x,t)\big)=0,&(x,t)\in Q:=\Omega\times(0,T),\\
y(x,0)=y_{0}(x),\quad\partial_{t}y(x,0)=y_{1}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
y(\xi,t)=u(\xi,t),&(\xi,t)\in\Sigma:=\Gamma\times(0,T),\end{cases}

(1.2)

where

(

) is a smooth bounded domain with boundary

, and

is a fixed time horizon. The coefficient

is a weight function that may vanish or degenerate in parts of

\Gamma

is the imposed Dirichlet boundary control along
\Sigma=\Gamma\times(0,T)
. The initial data are

. We denote

(1.3)

Our primary goals are twofold. First, we establish the existence, uniqueness, and regularity of solutions

to (

) in suitable function spaces, even with non-zero boundary input

. We develop this theory in Section 2 by introducing weighted Sobolev spaces

and

. Under structural assumptions on

and

and source term

, the equation (
1.2
) possesses a unique weak solution with sharp energy estimates. In particular, we show that

when

, and we derive a priori bounds on

, and the initial data. These results generalize the well-posedness theory for non-degenerate boundary-controlled waves (see
[
16
]
) to the degenerate case.

A crucial step in our analysis is the construction of a Dirichlet map for the degenerate operator
A
. In Section 2.2, we introduce an operator

assigning to each boundary function

in
\Omega

in

is well-defined and bounded between appropriate spaces, and we use it to decompose solutions of (

Second, as an application of the above theory, we investigate approximate boundary controllability of the degenerate wave equation (
1.2
). Roughly speaking, approximate controllability means that, starting from zero initial data, one can drive the solution arbitrarily close to any desired target state in the energy space using boundary controls. Using the well-posedness and trace results, we formulate the Hilbert Uniqueness Method (HUM) for our degenerate system. We show that controllability is equivalent to an observability condition: the only solution of the adjoint equation with vanishing boundary normal derivative is the trivial one. This criterion generalizes the HUM framework to degenerate wave equations. We also illustrate the connection to important examples such as multi-dimensional Grushin equations and waves with single-point degeneracy, and we leave the verification of the corresponding unique continuation/observability property as an open problem.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we present the functional setting, define weak and mild solutions, and derive energy estimates for both homogeneous and non-homogeneous boundary problems. In Section 3, we establish approximate controllability and discuss illustrative examples.

, we have

\mathcal{A}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(u,\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Phi_{n}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(u,\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}\Phi_{n}.

(2.20)

Proof.

Take
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
. For each
u,v\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\frac{1}{2}})=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
, note that
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}})=\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{A}^{\frac{1}{2}})=H^{-1}(\Omega;w)
, we have

\begin{split}\langle\mathcal{A}u,v\rangle_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}),\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\frac{1}{2}})}&=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v)w\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(u_{n},\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}(v_{n},\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}\\
&\leq\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\|v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)},\end{split}

and hence

\|\mathcal{A}u\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}})}\leq\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}.

(2.21)

Moreover, we have

\mathcal{A}u_{m}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}u\mbox{ strongly in }\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}})\mbox{ with }u_{m}=\sum_{n=1}^{m}(u,\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Phi_{n}\mbox{ for }m\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.22)

Hence, for
0\neq u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\frac{1}{2}})
, for
m\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, we have

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}u_{m}\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}})}&=\sup_{\|v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\leq 1}\langle\mathcal{A}u_{m},v\rangle_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}),\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\frac{1}{2}})}\geq\left\langle\mathcal{A}u_{m},\frac{u_{m}}{\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}),\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\frac{1}{2}})}\\
&=\frac{1}{\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}}\sum_{n=1}^{m}(u_{n},\Phi_{n})^{2}\lambda_{n}\geq\left(\sum_{n=1}^{m}(u_{n},\Phi_{n})^{2}\lambda_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\|u_{m}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}.\end{split}

This shows that
\|\mathcal{A}u\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}})}\geq\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}
. This together with (
2.21
) we get
\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}})}=\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\frac{1}{2}})}
for all
u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\frac{1}{2}})
. Again, from

\mathcal{A}u_{m}=\sum_{n=1}^{m}(u,\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}\Phi_{n}

and (
2.22
) we obtain (
2.20
).

Let
\theta\geq\frac{1}{2}
. For
u=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(u,\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Phi_{n}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\theta})
, take
u_{m}=\sum_{n=1}^{m}(u,\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Phi_{n}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\mathcal{A}u_{m}=\sum_{n=1}^{m}(u,\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lambda_{n}\Phi_{n}
, and for
k<m
we have

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}u_{m}-\mathcal{A}u_{k}\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\theta-1})}^{2}=\sum_{n=k+1}^{m}(u,\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2\theta-2}=\|u_{m}-u_{k}\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\theta})}^{2}\rightarrow 0\mbox{ as }k,m\rightarrow\infty.\end{split}

This implies that
\mathcal{A}u_{m}\rightarrow h
strongly in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\theta-1})
. Now, by
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\theta})\subseteq\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\frac{1}{2}})
since
\theta\geq\frac{1}{2}
, we have
u_{m}\rightarrow u
strongly in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\frac{1}{2}})
from (1) in Remark
2.20
, and then for each
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we have

\begin{split}\langle h,\varphi\rangle&=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\langle\mathcal{A}u_{m},\varphi\rangle=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u_{m}\cdot\nabla\varphi)w\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi)w\mathrm{d}x=\langle\mathcal{A}u,\varphi\rangle\end{split}

in the sense of distribution. i.e.,
\mathcal{A}u=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{A}u_{m}
strongly in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\theta-1})
. And hence (
2.20
) holds. Finally, we have

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}u\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\theta-1})}^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(u,\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2\theta-2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(u,\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2\theta}=\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\theta})}^{2}.\end{split}

It is obvious that
\mathcal{A}
is a linear operator.
∎

Lemma 2.24

For each

and

Let

Lemma 2.25

The operator

2.4
Mild solution with non-homogeneous boundary condition

We combine the lifting via the Dirichlet map from Subsection 2.2 with the semigroup/cosine formulation from Section 2.3 to obtain a mild solution representation for the boundary-input problem.

In this section, we shall establish the mild solution of the equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{tt}y-\Div(w\nabla y)=0,&\mbox{in }Q,\\
y(0)=y_{0},\partial_{t}y(0)=y_{1},&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
y=u,&\mbox{on }\Sigma,\end{cases}

(2.25)

where
u\in H^{1}(\Sigma)
. This follows from
[
13
,
14
]
.

The operator

\mathcal{A}:L^{2}(\Omega)\supseteq\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)

generates a strongly continuous (s.c.) cosine operator

(see Lemma
2.25

[
6

at pp.​ 91) on

S(t)z=\int_{0}^{t}C(\tau)z\mathrm{d}\tau,\ z\in L^{2}(\Omega).

Indeed, we have

\mathcal{S}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}C(t)&S(t)\\
\mathcal{A}S(t)&C(t)\end{pmatrix},

where
\mathcal{S}
is defined in (
2.24
).
And the solution of (
2.18
) is

y=C(t)y_{0}+S(t)y_{1}.

(2.26)

Moreover, we have

\begin{split}C(t)y_{0}&=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\cos\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}t(y_{0},\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Phi_{n},\ y_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w),\\
S(t)y_{1}&=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sin\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}t(y_{1},\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Phi_{n},\ y_{1}\in L^{2}(\Omega).\end{split}

(2.27)

It is easily verified that for all
\theta\geq 0
and

we have

C(t)z=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\cos\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}t(z,\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Phi_{n}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\theta}).

(2.28)

An important property is

\begin{split}&S(t)L^{2}(\Omega)\subseteq\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}),\mbox{and }\\
&\mbox{the map}:t\mapsto\mathcal{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}S(t)z\mbox{ is continuous},z\in L^{2}(\Omega),\mbox{and }\\
&\|\mathcal{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}S(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq M_{\gamma}e^{\gamma t},t\in\mathbb{R},\end{split}

(2.29)

easily proved for

self-adjoint. Moreover, we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}C(t)z=-\mathcal{A}S(t)z\mbox{ for }z\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}),\quad\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}C(t)z=-\mathcal{A}C(t)z\mbox{ for }z\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}),

(2.30)

and

C(t)z-z=-\mathcal{A}\int_{0}^{t}\tau C(t-\tau)z\mathrm{d}\tau\mbox{ for }z\in L^{2}(\Omega),

and

C(t)z-z=-\mathcal{A}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)z\mathrm{d}\tau=-\mathcal{A}\int_{0}^{t}S(\sigma)z\mathrm{d}\sigma\mbox{ for }z\in L^{2}(\Omega).

2.5
Non-homogeneous Dirichlet boundary input and Dirichlet map

We now consider the degenerate wave equation with non-homogeneous Dirichlet boundary input. Unlike the homogeneous Dirichlet case treated above, the boundary datum enters the evolution through an unbounded control action, and a direct reduction

not

[
13
,
14
]
) and define the contribution of the boundary input through an admissible operator expressed in terms of the Dirichlet map

and the sine family

generated by

.

Recall that

is defined in Subsection 2.2 (see (
2.4
)).

From above, we obtain the solution of (
1.2
) in the following

.

Define

Under Assumption

Definition 3.1

Lemma 3.2
.

(z_{0}^{*},z_{1}^{*})\in K_{t}^{\bot}
if and only if

\begin{split}D^{*}\mathcal{A}S(t-s)z_{0}^{*}+D^{*}\mathcal{A}C(t-s)z_{1}^{*}=0\end{split}

Proof.

Since
(z_{0}^{*},y(t))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(z_{1}^{*},\partial_{t}y(t))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0

2.34

\begin{split}(z_{0}^{*},y(t))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}&=(z_{0}^{*},(Lu)(t))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\sin\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t-\tau)(Du(\tau),\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}(z_{0}^{*},\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathcal{A}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sin\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t-\tau)(z_{0}^{*},\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Phi_{n}\right)Du(\tau)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{A}S(t-\tau)z_{0}^{*}Du(\tau)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}\tau=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle D^{*}\mathcal{A}S(t-\tau)z_{0}^{*},\,u(\tau)\right\rangle_{H^{-1/2},H^{1/2}}\mathrm{d}\tau,\end{split}

and by the same argument as above we have

(z_{1}^{*},\partial_{t}y(t))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle D^{*}\mathcal{A}C(t-\tau)z_{1}^{*},\,u(\tau)\right\rangle_{H^{-1/2},H^{1/2}}\mathrm{d}\tau,

then we have proved the lemma since
H^{1}(\Sigma)
is dense in
H^{1/2}(\Sigma)
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

The equation (
1.2
) is approximately controllable at time
T>0
if and only if

\frac{\partial z}{\partial\nu_{\mathcal{A}}}=0\mbox{ on }\Sigma\ \Rightarrow\ z_{0}=z_{1}=0\mbox{ in }\Omega,

where
z
is a solution of the following equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{tt}z+\mathcal{A}z=0,&\mbox{in }Q,\\
z(T)=z_{0},\partial_{t}z(T)=z_{1},&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
z=0,&\mbox{on }\Sigma,\end{cases}

(3.1)

and
z_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w),z_{1}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Note that
z(t)=C(T-t)z_{0}+S(T-t)z_{1}
is the solution of (
3.1
), then we have proved the theorem from Lemma
3.2
with
z_{0}=z_{1}^{*}
and
z_{1}=z_{0}^{*}
.
This is also the HUM in
[
16
]
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Theorem
3.3
reduces approximate controllability to a unique continuation/observability property for the adjoint system. For degenerate operators, such properties can be delicate or may fail, so concrete verification is model-dependent and is not pursued here.

Examples and open problems

We briefly indicate how the abstract framework developed in Section 2 and the HUM-type criterion in Theorem
3.3
apply to representative degenerate models. The main point is that once Assumption
2.2
holds (so that the well-posedness and the lifting procedure via the Dirichlet map are available), the approximate controllability problem reduces to the corresponding unique continuation/observability property encoded in Theorem
3.3
.

Example 3.1 (interior single-point degeneracy).

Let
0\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and consider the scalar weight

w(x)=|x|^{\alpha},\qquad\alpha\in(0,2),

so that
\mathcal{A}y=-\Div(w\nabla y)
degenerates only at the interior point
x=0
and
w\geq\Lambda>0
in a neighborhood of
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
.
For such weights (in the range above) one has
w\in A_{2}
and the weighted Poincaré/compactness properties required in Assumption
2.2
; see, e.g., the discussions in
[
21
]
and the references therein. Hence the well-posedness results of Section 2 and the boundary lifting
y=z+Du
apply.
Consequently, the approximate controllability of (
1.2
) is characterized by Theorem
3.3
, i.e., it is reduced to the vanishing of the conormal boundary observation
\partial_{\nu_{\mathcal{A}}}z
for solutions of the adjoint system.

Grushin-type degeneracies and anisotropic models (discussion).

Classical Grushin operators are typically anisotropic and can be written in divergence form with a degenerate symmetric matrix field
B(x)
, rather than a scalar weight
w(x)
.
Although the present paper is written for the scalar-weight prototype
\mathcal{A}=-\Div(w\nabla\cdot)
in order to focus on the boundary input, lifting, and cosine-operator machinery, the HUM-type criterion in Theorem
3.3
suggests that, whenever the corresponding well-posedness and trace/conormal estimates are available, approximate controllability is again equivalent to a suitable unique continuation/observability property for the adjoint equation.
Exact controllability and related observability inequalities for Grushin-type wave equations have been investigated in
[
20
]
.

Open problem: internal degeneracy and unique continuation.

As already indicated above, the key obstruction for concrete degenerate models is the verification of the unique continuation (or equivalently observability) property appearing in Theorem
3.3
.
In particular, the hyperbolic equation with an interior single-point degeneracy

\partial_{tt}y-\Div(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla y)=0\quad\mbox{in }Q

(3.2)

is closely related to the setting of
[
21
]
, but a full treatment of the unique continuation/observability issue in the present boundary-control framework remains an interesting topic for future work.

4
Concluding remarks

We extended the Lions–Lasiecka–Triggiani framework to degenerate hyperbolic equations with boundary inputs, proving approximate controllability criteria. Open problems remain, including the exact controllability of waves with internal point degeneracies.

Relative uniform convergence and Archimedean property in pre-ordered vector spaces


Abstract
It is proved that, for a pre-ordered vector space X X , the quotient space ( X / A , [ W ] ) (X/A,[W]) is the Archimedeanization of X X , where W W is the closure of the positive wedge X + X_{+} in the ru-topology, A = W ∩ ( − W ) A=W\cap(-W) , and [ W ] [W] is the quotient set of W W in X / A X/A .

Introduction

Archimedean property is important in the functional representation of various analytic and algebraic structures.
For example, Kadison’s representation theorem
[
8
]
tells that every ordered real vector space with an Archimedean
order unit is order isomorphic to a vector subspace of the space of continuous real valued functions on a compact Hausdorff space.
Kadison’s theorem inspired M.D. Choi and E.G. Effros to obtain an analogous representation theorem
for self-adjoint subspaces of unital
C^{\ast}
-algebras that contain the unit
[
2
]
.

There is a plenty of recent works by many mathematicians who studied the Archimedean
property in connection to various applications in algebra, analysis and quantum physics.
In the breakthrough paper
[
13
]
, V.I. Paulsen and M. Tomforde have developed a theory of ordered *-vector spaces.
In their theory, Archimedeanization of ordered vector spaces with order units plays a crucial role.
The construction of Archimedeanization of ordered vector spaces with an order unit
[
13
]
was extended to
arbitrary ordered vector spaces in
[
3
]
. We also mention one more important direction in analysis
(based on representation theorems for Archimedian structures), the construction of free analytic spaces that
was initiated by B. de Pagter and A.W. Wickstead in their pioneered work
[
12
]
.

The present paper is devoted to an interplay between the Archimedean property and relatively uniform convergence in (pre-)ordered vector spaces.
In Theorem
3.3
, we prove that the quotient set
[W]
of the
\tau_{ru}
-closure of the positive wedge
X_{+}
of a pre-ordered vector space
X

is an Archimedean cone in quotient space
X/(W\cap(-W))
. The main result of our paper is Theorem
3.5
which establishes
that
(X/(W\cap(-W)),[W])
is the Archimedeanization of
X
.

2
Preliminaries

In what follows, vector spaces are real and operators are linear. We recall few well known definition.
For further unexplained terminology and notations we refer to
[
1
,
5
,
9
]
.

A subset
W
of a vector space
X
is a
wedge
if

W+W\subseteq W\ \ \text{\rm and}\ \ tW\subseteq W\ \ \text{\rm for}\ \ t\geqslant 0
.
A wedge
C
is a
cone
whenever
C\cap(-C)=\{0\}
.
Under an
ordered vector space
(briefly,
OVS
)
we understand a vector space
X
together with a cone (denoted by
X_{+}
and called a
positive cone
of
X
).
Replacing a cone by a wedge, we also consider a
pre-ordered vector space
(briefly,
POVS
).
A wedge
W
in a vector space
X
is
majorizing
(or
generating
) if

W-W=X\ \ \text{\rm or},\ \text{\rm equivalently},\ \ W\cap(x+W)\neq\emptyset

for every
x\in X
.

Every OVS (POVS)
X
is equipped with a
partial (pre-)order

x\leqslant y\Longleftrightarrow y-x\in X_{+}.

Every pair of vectors
\{a,b\}
in an OVS (POVS)
X
produces an
order interval
(possibly empty, if
a\not\leqslant b
):

[a,b]:=\{x\in X:a\leqslant x\leqslant b\}=(a+X_{+})\cap(b-X_{+}).

A subset
A
of a POVS is called an
order ideal
in
X
, whenever
A
is a
vector subspace
of
X
such that

a,b\in A\Longrightarrow[a,b]\subseteq A
.

An operator
T:X\to Y
between POVSs is
positive
(
order bounded
) if
T
takes
X_{+}
into
Y_{+}

(order intervals
X
into order intervals).

A wedge
X_{+}
in a POVS
X
is (or,
X
is):

-

Archimedean
if
y\leqslant 0
whenever
ny\leqslant u\in X_{+}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

-

almost Archimedean
if
\bigcap\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\bigl[-\frac{1}{n}u,\frac{1}{n}u\bigl]=\{0\}
for every
u\in X_{+}
.

Clearly, each almost Archimedean POVS must be an OVS. Moreover, every Archimedean OVS is almost Archimedean.
The converse is not true even for two-dimensional OVSs (see Example 2.5 in
[
3
]
).
It is also straightforward to see: a POVS
X
is almost Archimedean iff
X_{+}
does not contain a straight line.

A net
(x_{\alpha})
in a POVS
X

ru-converges
to
x\in X
(shortly,
x_{\alpha}\convr x
)
if, for some (
a regulator of the convergence
)
w\in X_{+}
, there exists a sequence

(\alpha_{n})
of indices with
x_{\alpha}-x\leqslant\pm\frac{1}{n}w
for
\alpha\geqslant\alpha_{n}
.
Whenever we need to specify a regulator
w
, we write
x_{\alpha}\convr x(w)
.

Clearly, every ru-convergent net in an OVS
X
has a unique ru-limit iff
X
is almost Archimedean.
As the ru-convergence is sequential, we can always restrict ourselves to ru-convergent sequences.
The ru-convergence is an abstraction of the classical uniform convergence of functions in
C[0,1]
.
The notion of ru-convergence was introduced by L.V. Kantorovich
[
10
]
.

3
Closedness of the positive wedge in ru-topology vs Archimedean property in pre-ordered vector spaces

Let
X/A
be a quotient space. A quotient set of
B\subseteq X
is the set
[B]=\{[x]\in X/A:x\in B\}

of all equivalence classes formed from a set
B
in the quotient space
X/A
.

The following lemma is a standard fact.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
W
be a wedge in a vector space
X
. Then
[W]
is a cone in
X/A
, where
A=W\cap(-W)
.
Moreover,
[W]
is majorizing in
X/A
iff
W
is majorizing in
X
.

Proof.

Clearly,
A
is a subspace of
X
and
[W]
is a wedge in
X/A
. To see that
[W]
is a cone it suffices to show
[W]\cap-[W]\subseteq A
.
Let
[x]\in[W]\cap-[W]
. Then
x=x_{1}+A=-x_{2}+A
for some
x_{1},x_{2}\in W
.
It follows
x\in(W+W)\cap(-W-W)\subseteq W\cap(-W)=A
, and hence
[x]=0
.

Suppose
W-W=X
and let
[x]\in X/A
. Then,
x=y_{1}-y_{2}
for some
y_{1},y_{2}\in W
, and hence

[x]=[y_{1}-y_{2}]=[y_{1}]-[y_{2}]\in[W]-[W]
. Thus,
[W]
is majorizing in
X/A
.

Suppose
[W]-[W]=X/A
and let
x\in X
. Take
y_{1},y_{2}\in W
with
[x]=[y_{1}]-[y_{2}]=[y_{1}-y_{2}]
.
So, for some
z\in A
we have
x+z=y_{1}-y_{2}
, and hence
x=y_{1}-(y_{2}+z)\in W-W
. Therefore,
W
is majorizing in
X
.
∎

An OVS
X
is a
vector lattice
if
x^{+}:=\inf\bigl\{X_{+}\cap(x+X_{+})\bigl\}
exists for every
x\in X
. It is easy to see that
every almost Archimedean vector lattice is Archimedean. An order ideal
A
in a vector lattice
X
will be referred to as a
lattice ideal

whenever it satisfies the property:
a\in A\Longrightarrow|a|\in A
.

W.A.J. Luxemburg and L.C-Jr. Moore proved in
[
11
]
that the ru-closed subsets of a vector lattice
X
are exactly the closed sets
of the so-called ru-topology on
X
. Such a topology can be also introduced on an arbitrary POVS
[
9
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

The
ru-topology
, denoted by
\tau_{ru}
, on a POVS
X
is determined as follows: a subset
S
of
X

is
\tau_{ru}
-closed whenever
S\ni x_{n}\convr x
implies
x\in S
. The
\tau_{ru}
-closure of a subset
S
of
X
is denoted by
\overline{S}_{ru}
.

It follows immediately from Definition
3.1
that the ru-topology on a POVS
X
is the strongest topology

\tau
on
X
with the property
x_{n}\convr x\Longrightarrow x_{n}\convtau x
. Clearly,
\tau_{ru}
is translation invariant.
In general, the topology
\tau_{ru}
is not linear.

It is useful to describe constructively the
\tau_{ru}
-closure of a subset of a POVS.
The idea comes from
[
11
]
, where the pseudo uniform closure of a subset of a vector lattice was studied.
Adopting the idea to the POVS setting, for a subset
S
of a POVS
X
we denote
S^{(0)}_{ru}:=S
;

S^{(1)}_{ru}:=S^{\prime}_{ru}
the set of all
x\in X
such that
x_{n}\convr x
for some sequence
(x_{n})_{n}
in
S
;

S^{(\alpha)}_{ru}:=\bigl(S^{(\alpha-1)}_{ru}\bigl)^{\prime}_{ru}
when the ordinal
\alpha
is not limit; and

S^{(\alpha)}_{ru}:=\bigcup\limits_{\beta\in\alpha}S^{(\beta)}_{ru}
when
\alpha
is a limit ordinal.
It is straightforward to see that
S^{(\alpha_{1})}_{ru}\subseteq S^{(\alpha_{2})}_{ru}
whenever
\alpha_{1}\leqslant\alpha_{2}
, and

\overline{S}_{ru}=\bigcup\limits_{\alpha\in\omega_{1}}S^{(\alpha)}_{ru}
, where
\omega_{1}
is
the first uncountable ordinal (cf.,
[
11
, Theorem 3.3]
in the vector lattice setting).
In particular, we have
\overline{S}_{ru}=S^{\prime}_{ru}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ S^{\prime}_{ru}=(S^{\prime}_{ru})^{\prime}_{ru}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
D
be a wedge in a POVS
X
. Then:

i)

D^{(\alpha)}_{ru}
is a wedge in
X
for every ordinal
\alpha
.

ii)

\overline{D}_{ru}
is a wedge in
X
.

Proof.

i)
Let
\alpha
be an ordinal.
We have to prove that
t\cdot D^{(\alpha)}_{ru}\subseteq D^{(\alpha)}_{ru}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and that

D^{(\alpha)}_{ru}+D^{(\alpha)}_{ru}\subseteq D^{(\alpha)}_{ru}
.

By definition,
D^{(0)}_{ru}=D
, so
D^{(0)}_{ru}
is a wedge.
Suppose
D^{(\beta)}_{ru}
is a wedge for every
\beta<\alpha
.
First, consider the case when
\alpha=\beta+1
for some
\beta
.
So,
D^{(\beta)}_{ru}
is a wedge and
D^{(\alpha)}_{ru}=\bigl(D^{(\beta)}_{ru}\bigl)^{\prime}_{ru}
.
Let
t\geqslant 0
and
x\in D^{(\alpha)}_{ru}
. Find a sequence
(x_{n})
in
D^{(\beta)}_{ru}
with
x_{n}\convr x
.
By passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
x_{n}-x\leqslant\pm\frac{1}{n}w
for some
w\in X_{+}
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then,
tx_{n}-tx\leqslant\pm\frac{1}{n}tw
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We conclude
D^{(\beta)}_{ru}\ni tx_{n}\convr tx
, and hence

tx\in\bigl(D^{(\beta)}_{ru}\bigl)^{\prime}_{ru}=D^{(\alpha)}_{ru}
.
Now, let
\alpha
be a limit ordinal,
t\geqslant 0
, and
x\in D^{(\alpha)}_{ru}=\bigcup\limits_{\beta\in\alpha}D^{(\beta)}_{ru}
.
Then,
x\in D^{(\beta)}_{ru}
for some
\beta<\alpha
. By the assumption,
tx\in tD^{(\beta)}_{ru}\subseteq D^{(\beta)}_{ru}\subseteq D^{(\alpha)}_{ru}
.
We conclude again
tx\in D^{(\alpha)}_{ru}
.

The proof of
D^{(\alpha)}_{ru}+D^{(\alpha)}_{ru}\subseteq D^{(\alpha)}_{ru}
is similar, we point out only the case of

\alpha=\beta+1
. So, let
x,y\in D^{(\beta+1)}_{ru}
, say
D^{(\beta)}_{ru}\ni x_{n}\convr x
and
D^{(\beta)}_{ru}\ni y_{n}\convr y
.
By passing to subsequences, we may assume
x_{n}-x\leqslant\pm\frac{1}{n}w
and
y_{n}-y\leqslant\pm\frac{1}{n}v
for some
w,v\in X_{+}
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then,
((x_{n}+y_{n})-(x+y))\leqslant\pm\frac{1}{n}(w+v)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. As
D^{(\beta)}_{ru}
is a wedge,
we conclude
D^{(\beta)}_{ru}\ni(x_{n}+y_{n})\convr(x+y)
, and hence
x+y\in\bigl(D^{(\beta)}_{ru}\bigl)^{\prime}_{ru}=D^{(\beta+1)}_{ru}
.

ii)
It follows from
i)
because
D^{(\alpha_{1})}_{ru}\subseteq D^{(\alpha_{2})}_{ru}
for
\alpha_{1}\leqslant\alpha_{2}
, and

\overline{D}_{ru}=\bigcup\limits_{\alpha\in\omega_{1}}D^{(\alpha)}_{ru}
.
∎

It is proved in
[
11
, Theorem 4.2]
that every lattice homomorphism between vector lattices is continuous
in the ru-topology. Indeed, it is true for arbitrary positive operators between POVSs, and if additionally the positive wedge in the co-domain is majorizing,
it is also true for every order bounded operator.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
T:X\to Y
an operator between POVSs
X
and
Y
.

i)

If
T
is positive then
T
is
\tau_{ru}
-continuous.

ii)

If
Y_{+}
is majorizing and
T
is order bounded then
T
is
\tau_{ru}
-continuous.

Proof.

Let
S\subseteq Y

\tau_{ru}
-closed. It suffices to show that
T^{-1}(S)
is
\tau_{ru}
-closed.
To this end, let
T^{-1}(S)\ni x_{n}\convr x
. By passing to a subsequence, we may suppose

x_{n}-x\leqslant\pm\frac{1}{n}u
for some
u\in X_{+}
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By the definition of
\tau_{ru}
-topology,
it remains to show
x\in T^{-1}(S)
.

If
T\geqslant 0
then
Tx_{n}-Tx\leqslant\pm\frac{1}{n}Tu
, or
Tx_{n}\convr Tx
. Therefore,

S\ni Tx_{n}\convtaur Tx
and, since
S
is
\tau_{ru}
-closed, we infer
Tx\in S
, and hence
x\in T^{-1}(S)
.

If
Y_{+}
is majorizing and
T
is order bounded, then
T
is ru-continuous by
[
5
, Theorem 2.4 ii)]

As
T^{-1}(S)\ni x_{n}\convr x
then
S\ni Tx_{n}\convtaur Tx
. Thus,
Tx\in S
, and again
x\in T^{-1}(S)
.
∎

It was observed by T. Ito
[
7
]
that a vector lattice
X
is Archimedean iff
X_{+}
is ru-closed. We show that it is happened also for every POVS.

Lemma 3.4
.

A POVS
X
is Archimedean iff its positive wedge
X_{+}
is ru-closed.

Proof.

\Longrightarrow
: Let
X
be Archimedean and
X_{+}\ni x_{n}\convr x_{0}(w)
. By passing to a subsequence, we may suppose
-\frac{1}{n}w\leqslant x_{0}-x_{n}\leqslant\frac{1}{n}w
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,

x_{0}=(x_{0}-x_{n})+x_{n}\geqslant x_{0}-x_{n}\geqslant-\frac{1}{n}w
, and hence
w\geqslant n(-x_{0})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
X
is Archimedean,
(-x_{0})\leqslant 0
, or
x_{0}\in X_{+}
.

\Longleftarrow
: Let
X_{+}
be ru-closed. Assume
nx\leqslant y\in X_{+}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and some
x\in X
.
Thus,
\frac{1}{n}y-x\in X_{+}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\frac{1}{n}y\convr 0
and
x\convr x
,
then
\frac{1}{n}y-x\convr-x
.
As
X_{+}
is ru-closed,

-x\in X_{+}
or
x\leqslant 0
. Therefore,
X
is Archimedean.
∎

Lemma
3.4
should be compared with the following classical fact (cf.,
[
1
, Lemma 2.3]
).

Proposition 3.1
.

If a POVS
X
admits a linear topology
\tau
for which
X_{+}
is
\tau
-closed, then
X
is Archimedean.

It is also worth mentioning a recent paper
[
6
]
by A.E. Gutman and I.A. Emelianenkov,
where an exhaustive description of the class of locally convex spaces in which all Archimedean cones are topologically closed is obtained.

The
\tau_{ru}
-topology is not necessarily linear. However, as it was mentioned above,
the
\tau_{ru}
-topology on a POVS
X
is the strongest topology
\tau
on
X
such that
x_{n}\convr x\Longrightarrow x_{n}\convtau x
.
Therefore, we obtain the following corollary of Lemma
3.4
.

Corollary 3.1
.

A POVS
X
is Archimedean iff
X_{+}
is
\tau
-closed in some topology
\tau
on
X
possessing the property:
x_{n}\convr x\Longrightarrow x_{n}\convtau x
.

One of the most exciting results concerning interrelations between Archimedean
cones and ru-convergence is the Luxemburg-Moore-Veksler theorem:

Theorem 3.2
.

(
[
11
,
14
]
)
Let
A
be a lattice ideal in a vector lattice
X
. Then

X/A\ \text{is \ Archimedean}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ A\ \text{is \ ru-closed\ in}\ X\,.

The forward implication of Theorem
3.2
is almost trivial in the OVS case
[
3
, Proposition 3.2(a)]
.
We do not have yet an extension of Theorem
3.2
to any reasonable class of POVS beside vector lattices.
However, for a very natural ru-closed order ideal
A
in an arbitrary POVS
X
the quotient
X/A
turns to be Archimedean.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X
be a POVS with a positive wedge
X_{+}
. Then
[W]
is an Archimedean cone in
X/A
, where

W
is the
\tau_{ru}
-closure of
X_{+}
and
A=W\cap(-W)
.
Moreover, the cone
[W]
is majorizing whenever
X_{+}
is majorizing.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
,
W
is a wedge.
Lemma
3.1
implies that
[W]
is a cone in
X/A
.
The set
W
(and, also
-W
) is
\tau_{ru}
-closed.
Let
n[x]\leqslant[y]
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and some
x\in X,y\in W
.
Then
[x]\leqslant\frac{1}{n}[y]
, and hence
[x-\frac{1}{n}y]\in-[W]
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
So, there exists
(a_{n})_{n}
in
A
such that
x-\frac{1}{n}y+a_{n}\in-W
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Note that
a_{n}\convr 0
(with a regulator 0).
As
-W
is
\tau_{ru}
-closed, we infer from
\frac{1}{n}y\convr 0
and
a_{n}\convr 0
that
x-\frac{1}{n}y+a_{n}\convtaur x
, and hence
x\in-W
.
It follows
[x]\in-[W]
, or
[x]\leqslant 0
. So,
(X/A,[W])
is Archimedean.
Let
X_{+}
be majorizing. Since
X_{+}\subseteq W
then
W
is majorizing too.
Then,
[W]
is majorizing by Lemma
3.1
.
∎

It was proved in
[
13
]
that every OVS with an order unit has a unique (up to an order isomorphism) Archimedization.
This result was extend further to arbitrary OVS in
[
3
]
. The following definition was introduced in
[
3
, Definition 5]
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a POVS. Consider the category
\mathcal{C}_{Arch}(X)
whose objects are pairs
\left\langle Y,\phi\right\rangle
,
where
Y
is an Archimedean OVS and
\phi:X\to Y
is a positive operator, and morphisms

\left\langle Y_{1},\phi_{1}\right\rangle\to\left\langle Y_{2},\phi_{2}\right\rangle
are positive operators
T_{12}:Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
with

T_{12}\circ\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}
. If
\left\langle Y_{0},\phi_{0}\right\rangle
is an initial object of
\mathcal{C}_{Arch}(X)
,
then the OVS
Y_{0}
is called the
Archimedeanization
of
X
.

By
[
3
, Corollary 3.5]
, any almost Archimedean cone can be embedded into an Archimedean cone.
In particular, the Archimedeanization of any almost Archimedean OVS space
X
can be obtained
via replacing
X_{+}
by the intersection of all Archimedean cones containing
X_{+}
. The following result is proved in
[
3
, Theorem 1]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Every OVS has the Archimedeanization.

We present a construction of the Archimedeanization (based on Theorem
3.3
), which seems to be more elementary than one given in
[
3
]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
X
be a POVS with a positive wedge
X_{+}
,
W
the
\tau_{ru}
-closure of
X_{+}
, and
A=W\cap(-W)
.
Then
(X/A,[W])
is the Archimedeanization of
X
.

Proof.

A quotient map
\phi_{0}:X\to(X/A,[W])
is positive. By Theorem
3.3
,

\left\langle(X/A,[W]),\phi_{0}\right\rangle
is an object of
\mathcal{C}_{Arch}(X)
.
We need to show that
\left\langle(X/A,[W]),\phi_{0}\right\rangle
is initial. To this end, take an arbitrary
object
\left\langle Y,\phi\right\rangle
of
\mathcal{C}_{Arch}(X)
. It remains to find a positive operator

T:(X/A,[W])\to Y
such that
T\circ\phi_{0}=\phi
. Since
Y
is Archimedean, it follows from Lemma
3.4
that
Y_{+}
is ru-closed, and hence is
\tau_{ru}
-closed.
First, we show
\phi(W)\subseteq Y_{+}
. Let
x\in W
. There exists a net
(x_{\alpha})
such that

X_{+}\ni x_{\alpha}\convtaur x
. By Lemma
3.3
, the operator
\phi:X\to Y
is
\tau_{ru}
-continuous. Therefore, we obtain

Y_{+}\ni\phi(x_{\alpha})\convtaur\phi(x)
. As
Y_{+}
is
\tau_{ru}
-closed,
\phi(x)\in Y_{+}
.
Thus,
\phi(W)\subseteq Y_{+}
and
\phi(-W)\subseteq-Y_{+}
, and hence
\phi(A)\subseteq Y_{+}\bigcap(-Y_{+})=\{0\}
.
So,
T:(X/A,[W])\to Y
such that
T[x]:=\phi(x)
is well defined and positive. Clearly,
T\circ\phi_{0}=\phi
.
∎

Lemma

Definition 3.3
.

A POVS

.

It can be seen easily:

-

\alpha(X)\leqslant 1
for every almost Archimedean POVS
X
, and
\alpha(X)=0
iff
X
is Archimedean.

-

0\leqslant\alpha(X)<\omega_{1}
for every POVS
X
, and
\alpha(X)=2
for the vector lattice
X
in Nakayama’s example (cf., Example 6.4 in
[
11
]
).

Let us consider the following order ideals in a POVS
X
: the set
I_{0}(X):=\{0\}

of all infinitesimals of
X
of order 0; the set:

I_{1}(X)=I(X):=\bigl\{x\in X:(\exists y\in X)(\forall n\in\mathbb{N})\pm x\leqslant\frac{1}{n}y\bigl\}

of all infinitesimals of order 1 (or, just infinitesimals of
X
); and the set

I_{\alpha}(X):=\bigl\{x\in X:[x]\in I\bigl(X/_{\bigcup_{\gamma\in\alpha}I_{\gamma}(X)}\bigl)\bigl\}.

of all infinitesimals of order
\alpha
, where
\alpha
is an arbitrary ordinal
>1
.

It can be seen that
I_{\alpha_{1}}(X)\subseteq I_{\alpha_{2}}(X)\subseteq(X_{+})^{(\alpha_{2})}_{ru}
for
\alpha_{1}\leqslant\alpha_{2}
.
Clearly, a POVS
X
is almost Archimedean iff
I(X)=\{0\}
. If a POVS
X
has a nonzero
order unit

u\in X
(i.e.,
u
satisfies
X=\bigcup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}[-nu,nu]
) then
u\notin I(X)
.

Definition 3.4
.

By the
almost Archimedeanization type

\lambda(X)
(cf.,
[
3
,
4
]
)
of a POVS
X
we understand the first ordinal
\lambda
such that
I_{\lambda+1}(X)=I_{\lambda}(X)
.

It can be shown easily:

-

\lambda(X)=0
iff
X
is almost Archimedean, in particular
\lambda(X)=0
for every normed lattice.

-

I\bigl(\ell^{\infty}/c_{00}\bigl)=I\bigl(c_{0}/c_{00}\bigl)=c_{0}/c_{00}
, and hence
I_{2}\bigl(\ell^{\infty}/c_{00}\bigl)=I_{2}\bigl(c_{0}/c_{00}\bigl)=\{0\}

and
\lambda\bigl(\ell^{\infty}/c_{00}\bigl)=\lambda\bigl(c_{0}/c_{00}\bigl)=2
.

-

0\leqslant\lambda(X)<\omega_{1}
for every POVS
X
.

-

\lambda(X)=3
for the vector lattice
X
in Nakayama’s example.

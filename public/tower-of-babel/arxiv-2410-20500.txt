Beauville–Laszlo Gluing of Algebraic Spaces
Piotr Achinger, Alex Youcis

Abstract
. For a complete discrete valuation field K K , we show that one may always glue a separated formal algebraic space 𝔛 \mathfrak{X} over 𝒪 K \mathcal{O}_{K} to a separated algebraic space U U over K K along an open immersion of rigid spaces j : 𝔛 rig → U an j\colon\mathfrak{X}^{\rm rig}\to U^{\mathrm{an}} , producing a separated algebraic space X X over 𝒪 K \mathcal{O}_{K} . This process gives rise to an equivalence between such ‘gluing triples’ ( U , 𝔛 , j ) (U,\mathfrak{X},j) and separated algebraic spaces X X over 𝒪 K \mathcal{O}_{K} , which one might interpret as a version of the Beauville–Laszlo theorem for algebraic spaces rather than coherent sheaves. Moreover, an analogous equivalence exists over any excellent base. Examples due to Matsumoto imply that the result of such a gluing might be a genuine algebraic space (not a scheme) even if U U and the special fiber of 𝔛 \mathfrak{X} are projective. The proof is a combination of Nagata compactification theorem for algebraic spaces and of Artin’s contraction theorem. We give multiple examples and applications of this idea.

Relationship to the classical Beauville–Laszlo theorem

To explain the terminology ‘Beauville–Laszlo gluing’, let us recall the statement of the Beauville–Laszlo theorem from
[
4
]
. Let
A
be a ring with a non-zerodivisor
\pi
. Then, the theorem asserts that the functor from the category of
\pi
-torsionfree
A
-modules
M
, to the category of triples
(F,G,\iota)
where

(1)

F
is a
A[\nicefrac{{1}}{{\pi}}]
-module,

(2)

G
is a
\pi
-torsionfree
\widehat{A}
-module,

(3)

\iota\colon F\otimes_{A[\nicefrac{{1}}{{\pi}}]}\widehat{A}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{\pi}}]\xlongrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\displaystyle\sim$}}\,}G[\nicefrac{{1}}{{\pi}}]
is an isomorphism of
\widehat{A}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{\pi}}]
-modules,

given by sending
M
to
(M[\nicefrac{{1}}{{\pi}}],\widehat{M},\iota_{M})
(where
\iota_{M}
is the natural isomorphism) is an equivalence. One can extend this result to more general pairs
(A,\pi)
, and to the setting where
M
is replaced by an affine scheme
\Spec(R)
over
\Spec(A)
(e.g., see
[
28
,
Lemma 0F9Q
]
).
2
2

2

We use the name ‘Beauville–Laszlo gluing’ here because it has become the established name for such results. That said, similar results were previously obtained by Artin in
[
2
]
with Noetherian hypotheses, even allowing the role of
\pi
to be played by an arbitrary ideal
I
.

There is simplicity in the Beauville–Laszlo method as it avoids needing to explicitly think about formal geometry, using
\Spec(\widehat{R})
instead of
\Spf(\widehat{R})
. That said, this prevents one from being able to globalize such gluing procedures on a general scheme
X=\bigcup\Spec(R_{i})
over
\Spec(A)
, as the correct gluing cannot happen between
\Spec(\widehat{R}_{i})
and
\Spec(\widehat{R}_{j})
, but only between
\Spf(\widehat{R}_{i})
and
\Spf(\widehat{R}_{j})
. Similar problems arise when trying to globalize the base
\Spec(A)
. This forces the introduction of formal geometry and in turn the introduction of rigid geometry, as the analogue of the isomorphism in (iii) above must now take place over the locus
\{\pi\neq 0\}
in
\Spf(\widehat{A})
which only exists in the world of rigid analytic geometry.

Despite this connection, we do emphasize that our gluing results differ from the classical version of Beauville–Laszlo gluing outside of the coherent situation, i.e., when
\Spec(R)
is not a finite scheme over
\Spec(A)
. Indeed, while
\Spec(\widehat{R}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{\pi}}])
has a close connection to
\Spa(\widehat{R}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{\pi}}])
in the finite case (e.g., the latter is the analytification of the former), the difference becomes drastic even for finite type (but not finite) affine schemes over
\Spec(A)
. For instance,
\Spec(\mathcal{O}[x^{\pm 1}][\nicefrac{{1}}{{\pi}}])^{\mathrm{an}}=\mathbb{G}_{m,K}^{\mathrm{an}}
but the
\{\pi\neq 0\}
locus in
\Spf(\mathcal{O}[x^{\pm 1}]^{\wedge})
is the circle group
\{x\in\mathbb{G}_{m,K}^{\mathrm{an}}:|x|=1\}
.

Idea of proof and conditions on
S

Many of our results, including the fully faithfulness portion of Theorem
1.3
requires only that
(S,S_{0})
is of Type (N)/(V) (i.e.,
S
is Noetherian or the spectrum of a complete rank one valuation ring). But, our proof of essential surjectivity uses

(1)

the Artin contraction theorem (see Theorem
5.9
) which (with some work) handles the case when the gluing triple is ‘proper-like’, and

(2)

Nagata compactification for algebraic spaces (see
[
10
]
) to reduce to the ‘proper-like’ case.

Roughly, (a) explains our restriction to
S
excellent, and (b) explains our separation hypotheses. It would be worthwhile to try and relax either of these hypotheses (e.g., allowing
S=\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}})
).

Rigid locus and a result of Raynaud and Fujiwara–Kato

To begin, we observe there is a functor (recalling our conventions for formal schemes)

(-)^{\mathrm{ad}}\colon\left\{\begin{matrix}\text{Formal}\\
\text{schemes}\end{matrix}\right\}\to\left\{\begin{matrix}\text{Adic}\\
\text{spaces}\end{matrix}\right\},

uniquely characterized by preserving open embeddings/coverings and such that there is a functorial identification
\Spf(A)^{\mathrm{ad}}=\Spa(A)
(e.g., combine
[
15
, Proposition 4.1]
with
[
30
]
). We may compose the functors
(-)^{\mathrm{ad}}
and
(-)_{\mathrm{a}}
to obtain the
rigid locus
functor

(-)^{\mathrm{rig}}=(-)_{\mathrm{a}}\circ(-)^{\mathrm{ad}}\colon\left\{\begin{matrix}\text{Formal}\\
\text{schemes}\end{matrix}\right\}^{\ast}\to\left\{\begin{matrix}\text{Analytic}\\
\text{adic spaces}\end{matrix}\right\},

where again the asterisk means restricting only to adic morphisms. If
\mathfrak{S}=\Spf(A)
where
(\pi)\subseteq A
is as an ideal of definition, then
\mathfrak{S}^{\mathrm{rig}}=\Spa(A[\nicefrac{{1}}{{\pi}}])
(see
[
14
, Chapter II, §A.4.(b)–§A.4.(d)]
).

The rigid locus functor extends to, and has quite pleasant properties on, the category of formal algebraic spaces locally of finite type over
\mathfrak{S}
. To state this precisely, first recall that a morphism
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
of formal schemes is an
admissible blowup
if there exists a quasi-coherent, locally finitely generated, open ideal sheaf
\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}
such that
\mathfrak{f}
is final among adic and proper morphisms
\mathfrak{g}\colon\mathfrak{Y}\to\mathfrak{X}
with
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{Y}}
a Cartier divisor (see
[
14
, Chapter II, §1.1.(a)-1.1.(b)]
for a more explicit description). We say that
\mathfrak{f}
is an
admissible modification
if it is adic and
\mathfrak{f}^{\mathrm{rig}}
is an isomorphism. If
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{X}
are coherent this condition is equivalent to the existence of a decomposition
\pi_{1}\circ\mathfrak{f}=\pi_{2}
for a diagram of admissible blowups

\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\xleftarrow{\pi_{1}}\mathfrak{Y}\xrightarrow{\pi_{2}}\mathfrak{X}.

(see
[
14
, Chapter II, Corollary 2.1.5]
).

Definition 2.9
.

A morphism

of formal algebraic spaces is an

if
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\times_{\mathfrak{X}}\mathfrak{Y}\to\mathfrak{Y}
is an admissible modification for any morphism
\mathfrak{Y}\to\mathfrak{X}
from a formal scheme

Denote by
W
the class of admissible modifications of coherent formal algebraic spaces. This is left multiplicative (see

Section 04VB
]

Theorem 2.10

(Raynaud, Fujiwara–Kato)
.

There exists a unique functor

(-)^{\mathrm{rig}}\colon\mathbf{FAlgSp}\to\left\{\begin{matrix}\emph{Analyticadic}\\
\emph{algebraicspaces}\end{matrix}\right\}

extending the rigid locus functor on formal schemes, and such that the natural map

\mathfrak{U}^{\mathrm{rig}}/\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{rig}}\to(\mathfrak{U}/\mathfrak{R})^{\mathrm{rig}}

is an isomorphism for every étale equivalence relation
\mathfrak{U}\rightrightarrows\mathfrak{R}
. This functor sends
W
to isomorphisms and for any coherent formal algebraic space
\mathfrak{S}
induces an equivalence of categories

\mathbf{FAlgSp}^{\rm ft,adm}_{\mathfrak{S}}[W^{-1}]\underset{\rm incl.}{\xlongrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\displaystyle\sim$}}\,}}\mathbf{FAlgSp}^{\rm ft}_{\mathfrak{S}}[W^{-1}]\underset{(-)^{\mathrm{rig}}}{\xlongrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\displaystyle\sim$}}\,}}\mathbf{RigAlgSp}_{\mathfrak{S}^{\mathrm{rig}}}^{\rm coh}.

Terminology 2.18
.

Let

be a gluing triple over
(S,S_{0})
.

•

and

for the full subcategory of separated gluing triples.

Warning 2.19
.

We have defined the notion of separatedness for a triple in Terminology
2.18
in two different ways: implicitly in the first point with
P
being ‘separated’, and in the third point. These notions are not equivalent: if
(U,\mathfrak{X},j)
is separated, then both
U
and
\mathfrak{X}
are separated, but not conversely. We shall always use the more restrictive notion.

The category
\mathbf{Trip}_{(S,S_{0})}
has a final object given by the triple
(S^{\circ},\widehat{S},\mathrm{id})
. It furthermore admits all fiber products, computed in the obvious way:

(U_{1},\mathfrak{X}_{1},j_{1})\times_{(U_{2},\mathfrak{X}_{2},j_{2})}(U_{3},\mathfrak{X}_{3},j_{3})=(U_{1}\times_{U_{2}}U_{3},\mathfrak{X}_{1}\times_{\mathfrak{X}_{2}}\mathfrak{X}_{3},j_{1}\times j_{3}).

Moreover, if
(T,T_{0})\to(S,S_{0})
is a morphism, with
|T_{0}|=|T\times S_{0}|\subseteq|T|
,
then there is a natural base change functor
\mathbf{Trip}_{(S,S_{0})}\to\mathbf{Trip}_{(T,T_{0})}
computed in the same way. Both of these operations preserves the full subcategories of separated triples.

We now summarize the basic descent properties of the category of gluing triples below. Before we do so, let us establish the following terminology. By an

in

\mathbf{Trip}_{(S,S_{0})}

Proposition 2.20
.

Every étale gluing datum in
\mathbf{Trip}_{(S,S_{0})}

For an étale map

Finally we give an omnibus result that shows that the functor

Proposition 2.26
.

Let

be a morphism of algebraic spaces separated and locally of finite type over
S
, and let
P
be one of the following properties:

(1)

(2)

(3)

(4)

(5)

(6)

(7)

(8)

Then, the map

Example 3.4

(Matsumoto)
.

(Poonen)

3.4.
Néron models and triples

It was already observed in
[
9
]
that a theory like that of gluing triples is useful to understand Néron models of abelian varieties. This usefulness can also be seen to holds if one replaces abelian varieties by tori, as the following example illustrates.

Example 3.13
.

Let
K
be a discrete valuation field with valuation ring
\mathcal{O}
and uniformizer
\pi
. Set

U_{K}=\mathbb{G}_{m,K},\qquad\mathfrak{X}_{\mathcal{O}}=\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}}\simeq\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}}\times\underline{\mathbb{Z}}.

For an element
\omega
of
\mathcal{O}
, define the open embedding
j_{\omega}\colon\mathfrak{X}_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathrm{rig}}\hookrightarrow U_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}
on the
n^{\text{th}}
component via

\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}}^{\mathrm{rig}}\xlongrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\displaystyle\sim$}}\,}\{x\in\mathbb{G}_{m,\widehat{K}}^{\mathrm{an}}:|x|=|\omega^{n}|\}\subseteq\mathbb{G}_{m,\widehat{K}}^{\mathrm{an}}.

It is clear that
(U_{K},\mathfrak{X}_{\mathcal{O}},j_{\omega})
defines a group object in the category separated gluing triples over
(\Spec(\mathcal{O}),V(\pi))
. Moreover, taking
\omega
to be
\pi
, its gluing gives the Néron model of
\mathbb{G}_{m,K}
over
\mathcal{O}
.

Example
3.13
nicely illustrates why Néron models do not commute with ramified base change. Namely, if
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is an extension then it is clear that the base change of the triple
(U_{K},\mathfrak{X}_{\mathcal{O}},j_{\pi})
along the map
(\Spec(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}),V(\pi))\to(\Spec(\mathcal{O}),V(\pi))
is
(U_{K^{\prime}},\mathfrak{X}_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}},j_{\pi})
. But, the Néron model for
\mathbb{G}_{m,K^{\prime}}
over
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is
(U_{K^{\prime}},\mathfrak{X}_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}},j_{\pi^{\prime}})
where
\pi^{\prime}
is a uniformizer of
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
. It is easy to check that

(U_{K^{\prime}},\mathfrak{X}_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}},j_{\pi})\simeq(U_{K^{\prime}},\mathfrak{X}_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}},j_{\pi^{\prime}})\,\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\,|\pi|=|\pi^{\prime}|\,\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\,\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\text{ is unramified},

as predicted by the behavior of Néron models under base change.

We find it an interesting question whether one can extend Example
3.13
to arbitrary tori.

Question 3.14
.

Can one extend Example
3.13
to construct Néron models of all tori, and their finite-type and connected analogues as studied in Bruhat–Tits theory (see
[
19
, Appendix B]
)?

Remark 3.15
.

We lastly remark that Theorem
2.25
also conceptually simplifies the construction in
[
9
]
. In our terminology and with notation as in Example
3.13
, in
[
9
]
they construct for an abelian variety
A
over
K
a gluing triple
(A,\mathfrak{A},j)
so that if
(A,\mathfrak{A},j)=\mathcal{t}(\mathcal{A})
for some group
\mathcal{O}
-scheme
\mathcal{A}
, then
\mathcal{A}
is the Néron model of

. Without Theorem

, the construction of

is quite involved. But, using Theorem

,

is a separated group algebraic

-space which is automatically representable by
[

.

3.5.
Algebraizability of a formal scheme is detected by its rigid locus

Finally, we point out that using Theorem
2.25
one can show that algebraizability of a formal scheme is detected by its rigid locus if one expands the notion of algebraizability to include algebraic spaces. The following example illustrates this idea.

Example 3.16

(Tate uniformization)
.

Let
K
be a non-archimedean field with residue field
k
. Let
E
an elliptic curve over
K
with split multiplicative reduction and let
\mathcal{E}
be its minimal Weierstrass model over
\mathcal{O}
(e.g.,
[
27
, §VII.1]
). If

is the residue field of

, then

which admits a
\mathbb{Z}
-covering space

by an infinite chain of copies of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
glued pole-to-pole. Let
\mathfrak{X}\to\mathfrak{E}
be the unique étale deformation of

. One has an isomorphism
j\colon\mathfrak{X}^{\rm rig}\xlongrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\displaystyle\sim$}}\,}\mathbb{G}^{\mathrm{an}}_{m,K}
, and hence by Theorem
2.25
there exists an algebraic space
\mathcal{X}
locally of finite type over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
with generic fiber
\mathbb{G}_{m,K}
and formal completion
\mathfrak{X}
. Using Corollary
3.8
it is not hard to see that
\mathcal{X}
is in fact a scheme. Such a scheme has been explicitly described in
[
13
, §IV.1]
.

To explain the general application of Theorem
2.25
, we first give some notation. Fix a separated locally of finite type formal algebraic space
\mathfrak{X}
over
\widehat{S}
. Set

•

\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{X})
to be the category of
algebraizations
of
\mathfrak{X}
, i.e., pairs
(X,\iota)
where
X
is an algebraic space separated and locally of finite type over
S
and
\iota\colon\mathfrak{X}\xlongrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\displaystyle\sim$}}\,}\widehat{X}
an isomorphism,

•

\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{X}_{\eta})
to be the category of pairs
(U,j)
where
U
is an algebraic space separated and locally of finite type over
S^{\circ}
and
j\colon\mathfrak{X}^{\mathrm{rig}}\to U^{\mathrm{an}}
an open embedding.

The following proposition is an immediate corollary of, and largely equivalent to, Theorem
2.25
.

Proposition 3.17
.

Suppose
S
is a
G
-space. Then, the functor

\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{X})\to\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}),\quad(X,\iota)\mapsto(\widehat{X},j_{X}\circ\iota^{\mathrm{rig}})

is an equivalence with quasi-inverse given by sending
(U,j)
to
\mathcal{g}(U,\mathfrak{X},j)
.

Let us say that a separated rigid algebraic space
\mathsf{X}
over
\widehat{S}^{\mathrm{rig}}
is

if there exists an algebraic space

separated and locally of finite type over

and an open embedding

can never be algebraizable if it is quasi-compact and not proper over
\widehat{S}^{\mathrm{rig}}
.

Corollary 3.18

Suppose that
S
is a
G

\mathfrak{X}
is a formal algebraic space separated and locally of finite type over
\widehat{S}
. Then,
\mathfrak{X}
is algebraizable if and only if
\mathfrak{X}^{\mathrm{rig}}

3.6.
Gluing torsors

Theorem
2.25
implies that one can always glue a
G
-torsor over
\mathcal{t}(X)
to one over
X
, in a way which we now make precise. Throughout this subsection let us fix
G
to be a separated, flat, and locally of finite type group algebraic space over
S
.

Let
X
be an algebraic space over
S
and
\mathfrak{X}
a formal algebraic space over
\widehat{S}
. Recall that:

•

a
G

X
is a locally non-empty sheaf

on the fppf site of
X
together with a simply transitive action
G\times Q\to Q
,

•

a
\widehat{G}
-torsor on

is a locally non-empty sheaf
\mathfrak{Q}
on the adic flat site of
\mathfrak{X}
together with a simply transitive action
\widehat{G}\times\mathfrak{Q}\to\mathfrak{Q}

Recall here that a sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on a site

is

if for every object

\mathscr{S}
there exists a cover

such that

is non-empty for all

\mathcal{G}
on

\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}
is
simply transitive
if the induced map

\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F},\qquad(g,f)\mapsto(gf,f),

is an isomorphism. Finally, we recall that the
adic flat site
on
\mathfrak{X}
consists of all formal schemes over
\mathfrak{X}
endowed with the topology generated by Zariski open covers and adically faithfully flat maps
\mathfrak{Y}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{Y}
(see
[
14
, Chapter I, §4.8.(c)]
).

We denote these categories of torsors by
\mathbf{Tors}_{G}(X)
and
\mathbf{Tors}_{\widehat{G}}(\mathfrak{X})
, respectively. By
[
28
,
Lemma 04SK
]
any object of
\mathbf{Tors}_{G}(X)
and
\mathbf{Tors}_{\widehat{G}}(\mathfrak{X})
is represented by a (formal) algebraic space separated and locally of finite type over
X
and
\mathfrak{X}
, respectively. Thus, we treat torsors implicitly as such algebraic spaces in the sequel.

Definition 3.19
.

Let
(U,\mathfrak{X},j)
be a gluing triple over
S
. We then define the category
\mathbf{Tors}_{G}(U,\mathfrak{X},j)
of
{G}
-torsors over
(U,\mathfrak{X},j)
to consist of triples
(\mathcal{Q},\mathfrak{Q},\iota)
where

•

\mathcal{Q}
is an object of
\mathbf{Tors}_{G}(U)
,

•

\mathfrak{{Q}}
is an object of
\mathbf{Tors}_{\widehat{G}}(\mathfrak{X})
,

•

\iota
is a morphism of analytic adic algebraic spaces
\mathfrak{Q}^{\mathrm{rig}}\to\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{an}}\times_{U^{\mathrm{an}}}\mathfrak{X}^{\mathrm{rig}}
equivariant for the map of group rigid algebraic spaces
{\widehat{G}}^{\mathrm{rig}}\to({G}^{\circ})^{\mathrm{an}}
over
\mathfrak{X}^{\mathrm{rig}}
.

Suppose now that
X
is an algebraic space separated and locally of finite type over
S
. Then, there is a functor

\tau\colon\mathbf{Tors}_{G}(X)\to\mathbf{Tors}_{G}(\mathcal{t}(X)),\quad Q\mapsto\tau(Q)=(Q^{\circ},\widehat{Q},\iota_{Q}),

which is well-defined as the natural open embedding
\iota_{Q}\colon\widehat{Q}^{\mathrm{rig}}\to(Q^{\circ})^{\mathrm{an}}\times_{(X^{\circ})^{\mathrm{an}}}\widehat{X}^{\mathrm{rig}}
is evidently equivariant for the map of group rigid-algebraic spaces
\widehat{G}^{\mathrm{rig}}\to(G^{\circ})^{\mathrm{an}}
.

The following is an immediate consequence of Theorem
2.25
.

Proposition 3.20
.

Suppose that
S
is a
G
-space and let
X
be a algebraic space separated and locally of finite type over
S
. Then,

\tau\colon\mathbf{Tors}_{G}(X)\to\mathbf{Tors}_{G}(\mathcal{t}(X)),\quad Q\mapsto\tau(Q)=(Q^{\circ},\widehat{Q},\iota_{Q}).

is an equivalence of categories.

Finally, we use gluing triples to give a finer understanding of the finite étale covers of an algebraic space.

The following is an immediate corollary of Theorem

Proposition 3.22

Let

\mathcal{t}\colon\mathbf{F\acute{E}t}(X)\to\mathbf{F\acute{E}t}(\mathcal{t}(X)),

Proposition

[

). Throughout the rest of this section, we fix the following data:

•

K

•

•

•

•

We associate to

as in
[

We define the adic space

Such spaces are closely related to recent work in the theory of Shimura varieties, e.g., see

Example 3.25

If

Let

)

[

]

is flat and

As

and

are finitely generated

-modules, we have isomorphisms

M[I^{\infty}]\otimes_{A}\widehat{A}\simeq{A[I^{\infty}]}^{\wedge},\quad Q\otimes_{A}\widehat{A}\simeq\widehat{Q},

where all completions are

-adic. But

is

-torsion and so

So, it suffices to observe that

is

-torsion free. By (

Definition 5.1

. This reduces to showing that

for any open subset

, which is clearly a well-defined sheaf on
|(U,\mathfrak{X},j)|\simeq|X|
(as all objects involved only require the knowledge of the underlying space of
X_{1}
), independent of choices, and that
\mathcal{g}
is a functor.

Proposition 5.3
.

For a scheme
X
locally of finite type over
S
, there is a natural isomorphism of ringed spaces over
S

\alpha_{X}\colon(\mathcal{t}(X),\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{t}(X)})\xlongrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\displaystyle\sim$}}\,}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}).

Proof.

By Proposition
5.2
,
\alpha_{X}
is a homeomorphism, so it suffices to show that the natural map
\mathcal{O}_{X}\to(\alpha_{X})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{t}(X)}
is an isomorphism on affine open subsets. But, this is Corollary
4.4
.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

The functor

\mathcal{t}\colon\mathbf{Sch}^{\mathrm{lft}}_{S}\to\mathbf{Trip}_{(S,S_{0})}

is fully faithful.

5.2.
Proof of Theorem
2.25
: fully faithfulness

We now aim to show that for any algebraic spaces
X
and
Y
separated and locally of finite type over
S
, the natural map

(5.2.1)

\Hom(X,Y)\to\Hom(\mathcal{t}(X),\mathcal{t}(Y)),

is a bijection. We do this in three steps.

Step 0: when
X
and
Y
are both schemes

In this setting we see that the claim is a special case of Corollary
5.4
.

Step 1: when
X
is a scheme

For injectivity, let
Y^{\prime}\to Y
be an étale surjection from a scheme
Y^{\prime}
separated and locally of finite type over
S
. If
f_{i}\colon X\to Y
(
i=1,2
) are morphisms with
\mathcal{t}(f_{1})=\mathcal{t}(f_{2})
then
\mathcal{t}(f_{1}^{\prime})=\mathcal{t}(f_{2}^{\prime})
, where
f_{i}^{\prime}\colon Y^{\prime}\times_{Y}X\to Y^{\prime}
are the induced morphisms. But, as
Y^{\prime}\times_{Y}X
is a scheme, this implies by Corollary
5.4
that
f_{1}^{\prime}=f_{2}^{\prime}
and, as
Y^{\prime}\times_{X}Y\to X
is a surjection, that
f_{1}=f_{2}
.

For surjectivity, let
f\colon\mathcal{t}(X)\to\mathcal{t}(Y)
be a morphism. As
X
and
Y
are separated the graph morphisms
\Gamma_{f^{\circ}}\colon X^{\circ}\to X^{\circ}\times_{S^{\circ}}Y^{\circ}
and
\Gamma_{\widehat{f}}\colon\widehat{X}\to\widehat{X}\times_{\widehat{S}}\widehat{Y}
are closed embeddings, and so define ideal sheaves
\mathcal{I}_{f^{\circ}}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X^{\circ}\times_{S^{\circ}}Y^{\circ}}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\widehat{f}}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{X}\times_{\widehat{S}}\widehat{Y}}
. As
f^{\circ}|_{\widehat{X}^{\mathrm{rig}}}=\widehat{f}|_{\widehat{X}^{\mathrm{rig}}}
it is clear that
\mathcal{I}_{f^{\circ}}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\widehat{f}}
induce the same ideal sheaf of
\widehat{X}^{\mathrm{rig}}\times_{\widehat{S}^{\mathrm{rig}}}\widehat{Y}^{\mathrm{rig}}
, so define an object
\mathcal{I}_{f}
in
\mathbf{Coh}(\mathcal{t}(X\times_{S}Y))
in the sense of Definition
4.8
. By Proposition
4.9
this corresponds to a unique coherent sheaf
\mathcal{J}
(which is an ideal sheaf as an equivalence preserves subobjects) on
X\times_{S}Y
with
\mathcal{t}(\mathcal{J})=\mathcal{I}_{f}
.

Consider
V(\mathcal{J})\subseteq X\times_{S}Y
. The projection map furnishes a morphism
V(\mathcal{J})\to X
which is separated (as each
V(\mathcal{J})
and
X
is) and quasi-finite (as this may be checked over
X^{\circ}
and
\widehat{X}
, where it is an isomorphism). Thus,
V(\mathcal{J})
is a scheme by
[
28
,
Proposition 03XX
]
. Observe then that there is a natural isomorphism
\iota\colon\mathcal{t}(X)\to\mathcal{t}(V(\mathcal{J}))
and thus by Corollary
5.4
there exists a unique isomorphism
i\colon X\to V(\mathcal{J})
such that
\mathcal{t}(i)=\iota
. Composing
i
with the projection
X\times_{S}Y\to Y
produces a morphism
g\colon X\to Y
which, by construction, satisfies
\mathcal{t}(g)=f
, as desired.

Step 2: the general case

By
[
3
, Exposé I, Corollaire 4.1.1]
we may write
X=\varinjlim X_{i}
in the category of presheaves on
S
-schemes where each
X_{i}
is a scheme over
S
. In particular, we have an identification

\Hom(X,Y)\simeq\varprojlim_{X_{i}\to X}\Hom(X_{i},Y).

But, using
Step 1
one may directly check that one also has an identification

\Hom(\mathcal{t}(X),\mathcal{t}(Y))\simeq\varprojlim_{X_{i}\to X}\Hom(\mathcal{t}(X_{i}),\mathcal{t}(Y)).

The morphism from (
5.2.1
) is then obtained by passing to the limit over the morphisms

\Hom(X_{i},Y)\to\Hom(\mathcal{t}(X_{i}),\mathcal{t}(Y)),

which are each bijections by
Step 1
, and thus we are done.

and its diagonal are rig-surjective (cf.

and part (4) of

,

]
, but this case is simple.

If

Essential surjectivity in the

-scheme case

Assume now that

is a
G
-space and let
(U,\mathfrak{X},j)
be a separated gluing triple over
(S,S_{0})
. By Proposition
2.20

we may assume that
S
is a separated scheme.

Step 1: finite type case

Suppose first that
U\to S^{\circ}
and
\mathfrak{X}\to\widehat{S}
are finite type. By Nagata compactification for algebraic spaces (see
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
), we may find a proper morphism of algebraic spaces
\overline{X}\to S
and a dense open embedding
U\to\overline{X}^{\circ}
. As
\overline{X}\to S
is proper, we know from Proposition
2.16
that
j_{\overline{X}}
is an isomorphism. In particular, we have an open embedding of rigid spaces obtained as the composition

(5.3.1)

\mathfrak{X}^{\mathrm{rig}}\xrightarrow{j}U^{\mathrm{an}}\to(\overline{X}^{\circ})^{\mathrm{an}}\xrightarrow{j_{\overline{X}}^{-1}}\widehat{\overline{X}}^{\mathrm{rig}}.

Thus, there exist admissible modifications
\mathfrak{g}\colon\mathfrak{Y}\to\mathfrak{X}
and
\mathfrak{Y}^{\prime}\to\widehat{\overline{X}}
and an open embedding
\mathfrak{Y}\to\mathfrak{Y}^{\prime}
which recovers (
5.3.1
) on applying
(-)^{\mathrm{rig}}
.

By
[
28
,
Theorem 0GDU
]
there exists a unique
U
-modification
Y^{\prime}\to\overline{X}
whose completion recovers
\mathfrak{Y}^{\prime}\to\widehat{\overline{X}}
. Let
|Y|\subseteq|Y^{\prime}|
be the union of

|U|\subseteq|\overline{X}^{\circ}|=|(Y^{\prime})^{\circ}|,\quad\text{and}\quad|\mathfrak{Y}|\subseteq|\mathfrak{Y}^{\prime}|=|\widehat{Y}^{\prime}|\subseteq|Y^{\prime}|.

Then using Proposition
5.2
one sees that
|Y|
is an open subset of
|Y^{\prime}|
and so corresponds to an open algebraic subspace
Y
of
Y^{\prime}
(see
[
28
,
Lemma 03BZ
]
).

The quadruple
(Y,|\mathfrak{Y}|,\mathfrak{X},g)
is then a formal contraction problem in the sense of Definition
5.5
. As
S
is a
G
-space we see from Theorem
5.9
that this formal contraction problem has a solution in the sense of Definition
5.9
. Write this solution as
(X,T,g,\iota)
. As
T
is the image of
|\mathfrak{Y}|
it is clear that
T=|\mathfrak{X}|
and so
\mathcal{t}(X)=(U,\mathfrak{X},j)
as desired.

Step 2: general case

Take coherent open covers
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
and
\{\mathfrak{X}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
of
U
and
\mathfrak{X}
, respectively. For each
j
in
\mathcal{J}
one has that
\mathfrak{X}_{j}^{\mathrm{rig}}
is an open subset of
U^{\mathrm{an}}=\bigcup_{i}U_{i}^{\mathrm{an}}
, and so there exists some finite subset

such that that
\mathfrak{X}_{j}^{\mathrm{rig}}\subseteq W_{i(j)}^{\mathrm{an}}
where
W_{i(j)}=\bigcup_{i\in S_{i(j)}}U_{i(j)}
. Then, for each
j
in
\mathcal{J}
and each pair
(j,j^{\prime})
in
\mathcal{J}
the triples
(W_{i(j)}^{\circ},\mathfrak{X}_{j},j)
and
(W_{i(j)}\cap W_{i(j^{\prime})},\mathfrak{X}_{j}\cap\mathfrak{X}_{j^{\prime}},j)
is a separated open gluing datum with finite type constituents.

By
Step 1
, there exist algebraic spaces
X_{j}
and
X_{j,j^{\prime}}
with

\mathcal{t}(X_{j})=(W_{i(j)},\mathfrak{X}_{j},j),\qquad\mathcal{t}(X_{j,j^{\prime}})=(W_{i(j)}\cap W_{i(j^{\prime})},\mathfrak{X}_{j}\cap\mathfrak{X}_{j^{\prime}},j),

respectively. Moreover, combining the fully faithfulness argument from

and Proposition
2.26
, we obtain a separated open gluing datum of algebraic spaces. Taking the gluing of this datum results in an algebraic space
X
separated and locally of finite type over
S
which, by construction, satisfies
\mathcal{t}(X)=(U,\mathfrak{X},j)
.

5.4.
Proof of Proposition
2.26

We recall the statement below for convenience.

Proposition
.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a morphism of algebraic spaces separated and locally of finite type over
S
, and let
P
be one of the following properties:

(1)

quasi-compact,

(2)

surjective,

(3)

open embedding,

(4)

closed embedding,

(5)

(locally) quasi-finite,

(6)

isomorphism,

(7)

finite,

(8)

separated,

(9)

flat,

(10)

étale,

(11)

smooth.

Then, the map
f
has property
P
if and only if
\mathcal{t}(f)
does.

Proof.

As all these properties are stable/local for the étale topology, by considering
X^{\prime}\times_{X}Y\to X^{\prime}
for an étale cover
X^{\prime}\to X
where
X^{\prime}
is a separated scheme locally of finite type over
S
, we may assume
X
and
Y
are representable.

For all of these properties
P
, it is evident if
f
satisfies
P
then
f^{\circ}\colon Y^{\circ}\to X^{\circ}
and
f_{n}\colon Y_{n}\to X_{n}
satisfies
P
for all
n
. Thus, to verify that
\mathcal{t}(f)

Bôcher type theorem for elliptic equations with drift perturbed Lévy operator


Abstract
. A classical Bôcher’s theorem asserts that any positive harmonic function (with respect to the Laplacian) in the punctured unit ball can be expressed, up to the multiplication constant, as the sum of the Newtonian kernel and a positive function that is harmonic in the whole unit ball. This theorem expresses one of the fundamental results in the theory of isolated singularities and it can be viewed as a statement on the asymptotic behavior of positive harmonic functions near their isolated singularities. In the paper we generalize this results to drift perturbed Lévy operators. We propose a new approach based on the probabilistic potential theory. It applies to Lévy operators for which the resolvent of its perturbation is strongly Feller. In particular our result encompasses drift perturbed fractional Laplacians with any stability index bounded between zero and two - the method therefore applies to subcritical and supercritical cases.

1.1.
Bôcher’s theorem

A classical Bôcher’s theorem (see
[
11
]
) asserts that any positive
function
u
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\geq 3
) that is harmonic in the unit punctured ball:

(1.1)

-\Delta u(x)=0,\quad x\in B_{1}\setminus\{0\}=:B^{*}_{1}

admits the following decomposition

(1.2)

u(x)=h(x)+a|x|^{2-d},\quad x\in B(0,1),

where
h:B_{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is harmonic and
a\geq 0
is a constant.
The equality may be viewed as a result on the local behavior of positive solutions to (
1.1
)
around isolated singularity.
If
a=0

singularity is removable and if
a>0
, we have
u(x)\sim|x|^{2-d}
when
|x|\to 0
.
Further generalizations of Bôcher’s theorem have been made by Hartman and Wintner (see
[
35
]
)
and Gilbarg and Serrin (see
[
32
]
)
for classical solutions to

a_{ij}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}+b_{i}u_{x_{i}}+cu=0\quad\text{ in }B^{*}_{1},

assuming Hölder continuity of coefficients (non-divergence form operators), and by Serrin and Weinberger (see
[
56
]
)
for positive continuous weak solutions (
u\in H^{1}_{loc}(B^{*}_{1})
) to

(a_{ij}u_{x_{i}})_{x_{j}}=0\quad\text{ in }B^{*}_{1},

with merely measurable bounded and uniformly elliptic matrix
(a_{ij})
(divergence form operators, see also
[
57
,
58
]
).
The first result on Bôcher’s type theorem for distributional supersolutions
is due to Brezis and Lions
[
13

: assume that
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(B^{*}_{1})
is positive,
\Delta u\in L^{1}_{loc}(B^{*}_{1})

in the sense of distributions and

\Delta u\leq cu+f\quad\text{a.e. in }B_{1},

for some
c\geq 0
and

, and

-\Delta u=\varphi+a\delta_{0}\quad\text{in }\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(B_{1})

\varphi\in L^{1}_{loc}(B_{1})
and
a\geq 0
(in the paper

,
and note that one easily shows that the result does not hold for

Noticing that

in
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
it is readily seen that
the result by Brezis and Lions is a generalization of the one by Bôcher.

In

To the best of our knowledge, there are only two papers devoted to Bôcher’s type theorems for non-local equations.
In
[
48
]
Congming Li, Zhigang Wu and Hao Xu studied behavior of positive
s
-harmonic functions in
B^{*}_{1}
(i.e. harmonic with respect to
the fractional Laplacian
\Delta^{s}:=-(-\Delta)^{s}
with
s\in(0,1)
)
while in
[
49
]
Congming Li, Chenkai Liu, Zhigang Wu and Hao Xu were concerned with positive distributional solutions to the following inequality:

(1.3)

-\Delta^{s}u+b\cdot\nabla u+cu\geq 0\quad\text{in}\quad B^{*}_{1},

with
s\in(1/2,1)
,
b\in C^{1}(B_{1})
and
c\in L^{\infty}(B_{1})
.
They proved that if
u\in L^{1}_{w_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with
w_{s}(x):=1/(1+|x|^{d+2s})
, is a distributional solution to (
1.3
), then

-\Delta^{s}u+b\cdot\nabla u+cu=\mu+a\delta_{0}\quad\text{in }\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(B_{1})

for some positive Radon measure
\mu
on

, with
\mu(\{0\})=0
,
and

. The critical (i.e.

) and supercritical (i.e.

since for

not exceeding

the Green function of
\Delta^{s}
does not belong to the space
W^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
which was one of the crucial points of the method.

1.3.
A new method for a wide class of local, non-local and mixed operators

In the present paper we propose a completely new approach to the proofs
of Bôcher’s type theorems based on the probabilistic potential theory. The approach allows us to
deal with a wide class of integro-differential operators including, as particular examples,

•

the fractional Laplacians
\Delta^{s}
with any
s\in(0,1)
(see Example
3.1
);

•

Lévy operators with singular (with respect to the Lebesgue measure)
Lévy measure
\nu
(see (
1.5
)), e.g. cylindrical Lévy operators (see Example
3.2
);

•

Kolmogorov type operators with degenerate diffusion part (local and mixed, see Example
3.3
);

•

mixed local and non-local operators, e.g.
\Delta+\Delta^{s}
(see Example
3.4
).

In particular, in some instances (e.g. degenerate diffusion matrix) our results are new even for local operators.
We believe that this result may pave the way for studying the problem of
isolated singularities for a much broader class of non-local
operators than that determined by the applicability of the Caffarelli-Silvestre extension procedure.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded open set and
d\geq 1

(note that we do not impose any restrictions on the dimension
but this is not contradictory to the result by Brezis and Lions; the dimension constraint is in a sense
forced by a specific operator).
We shall study non-negative solutions
to the inequality

(1.4)

-Au+b\cdot\nabla u+\lambda\geq 0\quad\text{in}\quad\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(D\setminus K),

where
A
is a Lévy operator,
b:D\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded and Lipschitz continuous function,
\lambda
is a Radon measure on
D
and

K
is a
polar
compact set. Note that semilinear equations with isolated singularities
for polar sets
K
not being singletons have been
considered by Brezis and Nirenberg
[
14
]
, Jin, de Queiroz, Sire and Xiong
[
38
]
,
and Chen and Lin
[
20
]
.
The class of Lévy operators consists of Fourier multiplier operators
A
:

\widehat{Au}(\xi):=-\psi(\xi)\hat{u}(\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,u\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

with a negative definite (in the sense of
Schoenberg, see
[
12
, Definition 2.14]
) function
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
.
Equivalently, by the Lévy-Khintchine formula, the said class consists of linear operators
A
of the form

(1.5)

Au(x)=cu-l\cdot\nabla u+\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}(QD^{2}u(x))+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u(x+y)-u(x)-y\nabla u(x)\mathbf{1}_{B(0,1)})\,\nu(dy),

where
c\in\mathbb{R}
,
l\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
Q
is a non-negative definite symmetric matrix and
\nu
is a Lévy measure - positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

such that
\nu(\{0\})=0
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}1\wedge|x|^{2}\,\nu(dx)<\infty
.
Furthermore, if we assume additionally that
l=0
and
\nu
is symmetric:
\nu(dx)=\nu(-dx)
, then

Au(x)=cu+\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}(Q\nabla u(x)\cdot\nabla u(x))+\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}(u(x+y)-u(x))\,\nu(dy).

The only restriction that we make on
A
is strong Feller property of the resolvent
generated by

A-b\cdot\nabla-\kappa I
for some
\kappa\geq 0
.
Recall that
K
is polar if
C(K)=0
, where

C(K):=\sup\{\mu(K):\mu\text{ is a positive Borel measure supported in }K\text{ and }G\mu\leq 1\},

and
G
is the Green function of
A-b\cdot\nabla-\kappa I
.
It is known that
C(\{x\})=0
if and only if
G(x,x)=\infty
.
All the above assumptions are easily verified for
A=\Delta^{s}
with any
s\in(0,1]

and
K=\{x\}
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see Example
3.1
).
The method therefore applies
to subcritical and supercritical cases.

1.4.
The main results of the paper

Our main results consist of three theorems.
Note at this point that local integrability of
u
on
D\setminus K
in general is not enough to
guarantee that
-Au+b\cdot\nabla u+\lambda
be a distribution on
D\setminus K
.
This is due to the presence of the nonlocal part of
A
. Therefore, if
\nu\equiv 0
, we merely assume that
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(D\setminus K)
,
but for the general case, we introduce
in Section
5
the space
\mathscr{T}_{\nu}(D)
,
depending on
\nu,D
, that ensures that for any
u\in\mathscr{T}_{\nu}(D)\cap L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
the functional
-Au+b\nabla u+\lambda
, acting by the following rule

[-Au+b\nabla u+\lambda](\eta):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\,(-A+b\cdot\nabla)^{*}\eta+\int_{D\setminus K}\eta\,d\lambda,\quad\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D\setminus K),

defines a distribution on
D\setminus K
, in other words,
-Au+b\nabla u+\lambda\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(D\setminus K)
.
Let
\hat{\mu}_{0}
be a Radon measure on
D\setminus K
that represents positive distribution of (
1.4
)
(Riesz’s theorem).
Let
\mu_{0}
be the extension of
\hat{\mu}_{0}
to
D
by letting zero on
K
:
\mu_{0}(A):=\hat{\mu}_{0}(A\cap(D\setminus K))
,

A\in\mathcal{B}(D)
.

The first main result (see Theorem
6.6
) stands that for any positive solution
u
to (
1.4
)
measure
\mu_{0}
is Radon on
D
,
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(D)
, and
u
solves

(1.6)

-Au+b\cdot\nabla u+\lambda=\mu_{0}+\sigma_{K}\quad\text{in}\quad\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(D),

where
\sigma_{K}
is a positive Radon measures on
D
supported in
K
.
The second theorem (see Theorem
7.4
) asserts that if

D\ni x\longmapsto\int_{D}G_{D}(x,y)\,d\lambda^{+}(y)\in L^{1}(D),

then any positive solution
u
to (
1.4
) admits finely continuous
\ell^{d}
-version
\hat{u}
in
D\setminus N
,
where
N
is a polar set described precisely in the assertion,
and for any open
V\subset\subset D
there exists a positive
function
h_{V}
that is harmonic in
V
(with respect to
-A+b\cdot\nabla
) such that

\hat{u}(x)=h_{V}(x)+\int_{V}G_{V}(x,y)\,\mu_{0}(dy)+\int_{V}G_{V}(x,y)\,\sigma_{K}(dy)-\int_{V}G_{V}(x,y)\,\lambda(dy),\quad x\in V,

where
G_{V}
is the Green function for
V
and
-A+b\cdot\nabla
.
In fact, denoting by
P_{V}
the Poisson kernel for
V
and
-A+b\cdot\nabla
, we have

h_{V}(x)=\int_{V^{c}}\hat{u}(y)P_{V}(x,dy),\quad x\in V.

In particular, if
\lambda=\mu_{0}=0
, i.e.

-Au+b\cdot\nabla u=0
in
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(D\setminus K)
,
and
K=\{x_{0}\}
, then (cf. (
1.2
))

\hat{u}(x)=h_{V}(x)+G_{V}(x,x_{0}),\quad x\in V.

The third main result is a direct consequence of the method we use here.
We obtain the following maximum principle (see Theorem
7.4
): let
u
be a positive solution to (
1.4
)
and
\lambda^{+}\equiv 0
, then for any open
V\subset\subset D
,

\hat{u}(x)\geq\inf_{y\in D\setminus V}\hat{u}(y)w_{V}(x),\quad x\in V,

where

w_{V}(x):=P_{V}(x,D\setminus V)=1-\int_{D^{c}}\int_{V}G_{V}(x,y)\nu(y-dz)\,dy,\quad x\in V

(the last equality is a consequence of the Ikeda-Watanabe formula). In particular if either strong maximum
principle holds for
A-b\nabla
(e.g. when
G_{D}(x,y)>0,\,x,y\in D
), or more directly,
\nu(y-D\setminus\overline{V})>0
a.e. in

, then

\inf_{x\in V}w_{V}(x)>0.

Observe that in case
A
is local, i.e.
\nu\equiv 0
, we have
w_{V}\equiv 1
. Thus, interestingly, in this case
we get estimates independent of
b
(cf.
[
49
, Theorem 1.4]
).

2.
Preliminaries, Lévy and Feller generators

Let
d\geq 1
and
\ell^{d}
denote the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
By
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we denote the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let
\mathscr{B}^{*}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the set of all universally measurable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For any
V\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we let
\mathscr{B}(V)
denote the family
of all numerical functions
f
on
V
, i.e.
f:V\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}\cup\{-\infty\}
,
that are Borel measurable.
For given
r>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we let
B_{r}(x):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x-y|<r\}
,
where
|x|
denote the Euclidean norm. We also use the shorthand
B_{r}:=B_{r}(0)
.
For open sets
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote by
C_{c}^{\infty}(V)

the space of all smooth functions with the support included in
V
.
We write
\mathscr{D}(V):=C_{c}^{\infty}(V)
.
Let
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the set of all continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

vanishing at infinity. We equip
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with the supremum norm:

\|u\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(x)|,\,u\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
For a given class of numerical functions
\mathscr{A}
we denote by
p\mathscr{A}
the subclass
of
\mathscr{A}
consisting of non-negative functions (i.e.
[0,+\infty]
valued).

Recall that a semigroup of bounded linear operators
(T_{t})
on
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, i.e.
T_{0}=I
and
T_{s+t}=T_{s}\circ T_{t},\,s,t\geq 0
,
is called a
Feller semigroup

provided that:

1)

(Markovianity)
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
0\leq f\leq 1
implies
0\leq T_{t}f\leq 1
for any
t>0
;

2)

(strong continuity) for any
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\|T_{t}f-f\|_{\infty}\to 0,\quad t\to\infty.

A linear operator
B
on
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with a dense domain
\mathscr{D}(B)\subset C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

is called a
Feller generator
if it generates a Feller semigroup on
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
We say that a set
\mathcal{C}\subset C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a
core
of a Feller generator
(B,\mathscr{D}(B))

if
(B,\mathcal{C})
is closable in
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and its closure equals
(B,\mathscr{D}(B))
.
It is well known (see e.g.
[
12
, Corollary 2.10]
) that the closure of
(A,C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, where
A
is a Lévy operator,
yields a Feller generator.

In what follows
A
denote a Lévy operator of the form (
1.5
), with
c\equiv 0
.
Furthermore, by
A^{*}
we denote the Lévy operator that admits representation (
1.5
) with

(l,Q,\nu)
replaced by
(l^{*},Q,\nu^{*})
, where
l^{*}:=-l
and
\nu^{*}(dx):=\nu(-dx)
.
We let
(A,\mathscr{D}(A))
,
(A^{*},\mathscr{D}(A^{*}))
denote the closures of

(A,C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
,
(A^{*},C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, respectively, and
by
(S_{t})
,
(S^{*}_{t})
their respective semigroups on
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Remark 2.1
.

By
[
5
, Proposition II.1]
for any
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\mathscr{D}(A)\cap\mathscr{D}(A^{*})
,

(2.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}A\eta_{1}\eta_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta_{1}A^{*}\eta_{2}

provided that the integrals are well defined and finite. As a result

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S_{t}\eta_{1}\eta_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta_{1}S^{*}_{t}\eta_{2},\quad t\geq 0

for any
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, provided that the integrals are well defined and finite.

2.1.
Drift perturbed Lévy operators

We let
I:\mathscr{B}^{*}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathscr{B}^{*}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the identity mapping and for given
\kappa\geq 0
,

L_{\kappa}:=A-\kappa I-b\cdot\nabla.

By
[
60
, Theorem 137]
,
[
40
, Corollary 2.5,Theorem 3.1]
the martingale problem for

(L_{\kappa},C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is well posed. As a result, by
[
41

for any

For

(L^{*}_{\kappa},\mathscr{D}(L_{\kappa}))

.
We let
(T_{t}^{\kappa})
denote the Feller semigroup on

generated by
(L_{\kappa},\mathscr{D}(L_{\kappa}))

Let
(T^{[\varepsilon],\kappa}_{t})

.
Furthermore, let

(T^{[\varepsilon],\kappa,*}_{t})
denote the Feller semigroup on

generated by
(L^{[\varepsilon],*}_{\kappa},\mathscr{D}(L^{[\varepsilon],*}_{\kappa}))
where

is given by (

By

By Remark

-submarkovian semigroup on

We let

.
One easily checks that

By

2.3.
Polar sets

Recall that
R^{\kappa},\,\kappa\geq 0
and
R^{\kappa,*},\,\kappa\geq\kappa_{0}
are strongly Feller.
In probabilistic potential theory a Borel measurable set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called
polar
,
with respect to
(\mathbb{P}^{\kappa}_{x})
,
provided that

(2.15)

\mathbb{P}^{\kappa}_{x}(\sigma_{B}<\infty)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\sigma_{B}:=\inf\{t>0:X_{t}\in B\}
.
By
[
8
, Proposition VI.4.3]
for any bounded Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\kappa\geq\kappa_{0}
, and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\mathbb{P}^{\kappa}_{x}(\sigma_{B}<\infty)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}(B)}R^{\kappa}\mu(x),

where
\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\kappa,1}(B):=\{\mu:R^{\kappa}\mu\leq 1\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\mu\text{ is a positive Borel measure supported in }B\}
.
Furthermore, there exists a measure
\mu_{B}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\kappa,1}(B)
such that
\mathbb{P}^{\kappa}_{x}(\sigma_{B}<\infty)=R^{\kappa}\mu_{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The same notion can be introduced for
(\mathbb{P}^{\kappa,*}_{x})
, with
\kappa\geq\kappa_{0}
.
We denote the objects defined above, but with
(\mathbb{P}^{\kappa}_{x})
replaced by
(\mathbb{P}^{\kappa,*}_{x})
,
by adding superscript
"^{*}"
to the respective expressions.

Now define for
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\kappa\geq 0
(
\kappa\geq\kappa_{0}
in case of dual notion),

C_{\kappa}(B):=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\kappa,1}(B)}\mu(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad C^{*}_{\kappa}(B):=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{+,*}_{\kappa,1}(B)}\mu(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Then a Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is polar with respect to
(\mathbb{P}^{\kappa}_{x})
(resp.
(\mathbb{P}^{\kappa,*}_{x})
)
if and only if
C_{\kappa}(B)=0
(resp.
C^{*}_{\kappa}(B)=0
). By Proposition
[
8
, Proposition VI.4.4]

(2.16)

B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{ is polar with respect to }(\mathbb{P}^{\kappa}_{x})\text{ if and only if it is polar with respect to }(\mathbb{P}^{\kappa,*}_{x}).

Finally, by (
2.12
), for any
\kappa\geq 0

\mathbb{P}^{\kappa}_{x}(\sigma_{B}<\infty)=\lim_{\alpha\to 0^{+}}\mathbb{E}^{\kappa}_{x}e^{-\alpha\sigma_{B}}=\lim_{\alpha\to 0^{+}}\mathbb{E}^{0}_{x}e^{-(\alpha+\kappa)\sigma_{B}}=\mathbb{E}^{0}_{x}e^{-\kappa\sigma_{B}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

This shows that polarity of a set does not depend on
\kappa\geq 0
, i.e.

\text{for some }\,\kappa\geq 0,\,\,\mathbb{P}^{\kappa}_{x}(\sigma_{B}<\infty)=0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\text{for any }\,\kappa\geq 0,\,\,\mathbb{P}^{\kappa}_{x}(\sigma_{B}<\infty)=0.

On the other hand, by the resolvent identity, one easily deduces that

C_{\kappa}(B)=0\text{ for some }\kappa\geq 0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad C_{\kappa}(B)=0\text{ for any }\kappa\geq 0.

Consequently, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 2.4
.

A set
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is called
polar with respect to the operator

A-b\nabla
if

C_{1}(B)=0
, equivalently,

\mathbb{P}^{0}_{x}(\sigma_{B}<\infty)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

2.4.
Fine topology and co-fine topology

Recall that the fine topology (with respect to
(P^{\kappa}_{t})
) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

is the coarsest topology that makes all the
(P^{\kappa}_{t})
-excessive functions continuous.
This is an intrinsic topology depending on the underlying Markov semigroup.
However, the property (
2.7
) and further identities (
2.9
), show
that the fine topology is independent of
\kappa\geq 0
and the fine topology restricted to a Borel measurable finely open
set
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
agrees with the one generated by
(P_{t}^{\kappa,V})
for any
\kappa\geq 0
.
In other words, denoting by
\mathcal{O}_{\kappa}
the fine topology generated by
(P^{\kappa}_{t})
, we have

\mathcal{O}_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{\kappa},\quad\kappa\geq 0,

and denoting by
\mathcal{O}^{V}_{\kappa}
the fine topology generated by
(P^{\kappa,V}_{t})
, with
V\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\mathcal{O}_{0}
, we have

\mathcal{O}^{V}_{\kappa}=\mathcal{O}_{0}\cap V,\quad\kappa\geq 0.

Consequently, in the whole paper,
we use the notion of fine continuity and fine topology with respect to
(P^{0}_{t})

without specifying the semigroup in the notation.

Throughout the paper we shall frequently use the following well known fact (see e.g.
[
59
, Exercise 10.25]
).
Let
V
be a finely open Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(2.17)

\text{If }u,v\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{ are finely continuous on }V\text{ and }u=v\text{ a.e. then }u(x)=v(x),\,x\in V.

All the comments and conclusions of this section hold true for co-fine topology
(bearing in mind, however, that
(P^{\kappa,*}_{t})
is defined for
\kappa\geq\kappa_{0}
).
Applying (
2.17
) and (
2.11
) we obtain

(2.18)

G^{\kappa}_{V,\alpha}(x,y)=G^{\kappa+\alpha}_{V,0}(x,y),\quad x,y\in V

for any
\alpha\geq 0,\,\kappa\geq\kappa_{0}
.

In this short section we shall give a few examples of concrete operators which the results of the present paper
may be applied to.

3.1.
Operators satisfying Hypothesis
2.2

Example 3.1

(Fractional Laplacian)
.

Let
A=\Delta^{s}
, i.e.
\psi(\xi)=|\xi|^{2s}
, for some
s\in(0,1)
,
then
A-b\nabla
satisfies Hypothesis
2.2
(see
[
42
, Theorem 2.2]
).

Example 3.2

(Cylindrical Lévy noise)
.

Let
A=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}})^{s_{i}}
for some
s_{i}\in(0,1)
.
Then
A-b\nabla
satisfies Hypothesis
2.2
(see
[
45
, Theorem 1]
). Note that such operators
have Lévy measure singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure.

Example 3.3

(Hörmander’s condition)
.

Let
A
be a Lévy operator with triplet
(0,Q,C_{\sharp}\nu)
, where

\nu(dz)=\frac{a(z)}{|z|^{d+2s}}\,dz

for some
s\in(0,1)
,
C
is a
d\times d
matrix, and
a\in C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies
a(z)=a(-z)
, i.e.

Au(x)=\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}(QD^{2}u(x))+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u(x+Cy)-u(x)-\langle Cy,\nabla u(x)\rangle\mathbf{1}_{B(0,1)})\,\nu(dy).

Let
B_{0}:=Id
be the identity matrix and define

d\times d
-matrix-valued function
B_{n}(x)
recursively by

B_{n}^{ij}(x):=b(x)\cdot\nabla B^{ij}_{n-1}(x)-\nabla b^{i}(x)\cdot B^{j}_{n-1}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}(QD^{2}B^{ij}_{n-1}(x)),\quad n\geq 1,

where
B^{j}_{n}
denotes
j
-th column of
B_{n}
.
Assume that
b
is smooth with bounded derivatives of all orders.
If for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
there exists
n=n(x)\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\operatorname{Rank}\left[\sqrt{Q},B_{1}(x)\sqrt{Q},\cdots,B_{n}(x)\sqrt{Q},C,B_{1}(x)C,\cdots,B_{n}(x)C\right]=d,

then
A-b\nabla
satisfies Hypothesis
2.2
(see
[
61
, Theorem 1.1]
).

Example 3.4
.

(Mixed Lévy operators)

Let
\phi
be a Bernstein functions and
A:=-\phi(-\Delta)
.
If

\liminf_{u\rightarrow\infty}\phi(u)u^{-s}>0

for some
s\in(0,1)
, then
A-b\nabla
satisfies Hypothesis
2.2
(see
[
66
, Theorem 1.1]
).
In particular one can take
A=\Delta^{s_{1}}+\Delta^{s_{2}}
with
s_{1}\in(0,1],s_{2}\in(0,1)
.

3.2.
Polar sets: some criterions

The fact whether or not a set
K
is polar must be examined on the case-by-case basis
depending on the structure of underlying operator. Here we refer to some known results and briefly describe some possible direct methods.

First of all notice that a consequence of the results recalled in Section
2.3
is that,
under Hypothesis
2.2
, a Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

is polar if and only if
R^{\kappa}\mu
is unbounded
whenever
\mu
is a bounded non-trivial positive measure with compact support contained in
B

(see the comments in the first paragraph following
[
8
, Proposition VI.4.3]
).
In particular
a point
\{x\}
is polar if and only if
G^{\kappa}(x,x)=\infty
. As a result, lower and upper on-diagonal bounds on the
heat kernels may decide whether a point is a polar set or not. Consequently,
using
[
42
, Theorem 2.2]
yields that
\{x\}
is polar with respect to the operator of Example
3.1

for any
s\in(0,1)
and
d\geq 2
or
d=1
and
s\in(0,1/2)
.
For the operators from Examples
3.2
–
3.4
the reach literature
on heat kernels estimate may by utilized (see e.g.
[
37
, Theorem 1]
,
[
54
, Theorem 1]
,
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
,
[
43
, Theorem 3.4]
). In order to treat other than singletons compact sets it is useful to have local estimates
on Green’s functions and then use intrinsic Hausdorff measure technique (see
[
34
]
and
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
again).

A probabilistic approach on the other hand gives information on polarity of a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
with respect to
A-b\nabla
,
without recourse to asymptotics of related heat kernels. It is based on the law of iterated logarithm
for Lévy processes. The reasoning below shows that for the operators in Examples
3.2
,
3.4

singletons are always polar.

First, by Hypothesis
2.2
, we know that
\ell^{d}(A)=0
implies
R^{\kappa}(\mathbf{1}_{A})(x)=0

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thus for any
f\in p\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, by
[
8
, Proposition II.3.5]
, if
\ell^{d}(R^{\kappa}f=\infty)=0
, then
B:=\{R^{\kappa}f=\infty\}
is polar. Further, observe that for
\kappa\geq\kappa_{0}+1
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}R^{\kappa}f=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}fR^{\kappa,*}1\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f.

Consequently, in order to prove that
K
is polar it is enough to find
f\in p\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{1}

such that
K\subset\{R^{\kappa}f=\infty\}
.
Let us note that
\mathbb{P}_{x}^{0}
is the distribution of a unique solution to

X^{x}_{t}=x+\int_{0}^{t}b(X^{x}_{s})\,ds+L_{t},

where
(L_{t})
is a Lévy process with symbol
\psi
.
Suppose that
Q\equiv 0
and
\nu
is symmetric. Let

\beta_{L}:=\inf\{\alpha>0:\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{\alpha}\,\nu(dx)<\infty\}

(so called Blumenthal-Getoor index, see
[
9
]
). Observe that in Example
3.4

\beta_{L}\geq 2s

and in Example
3.2

\beta_{L}=\min\{s_{1},\dots,s_{d}\}
. By
[
9
, Theorem 3.1]

(3.1)

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{L_{t}}{t^{1/\alpha}}=0

for any
\alpha>\beta_{L}
. Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

d\geq 2
,
\gamma\in(\beta_{L}\vee 1,d)
. Set

f(x):=\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{\gamma}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{1}(x_{0})}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Clearly,
f\in L^{1}
. Let
\tau:=\inf\{t>0:X^{x_{0}}_{t}\notin B_{1}(x_{0})\}
.
Then

\int_{0}^{\tau}f(X^{x_{0}}_{t})\,dt\geq\int_{0}^{\tau}\frac{c}{t^{\gamma}+|L_{t}|^{\gamma}}\,dt.

By (
3.1
) for any
\omega\in\Omega
and
\theta>1
there exist
c_{\omega},t_{\omega}>0
such that

|L_{t}(\omega)|\leq c_{\omega}t^{\frac{1}{\theta\beta}},\quad t\leq t_{\omega}.

Consequently, letting
\hat{t}_{\omega}:=\min\{t_{\omega},\tau(\omega),1\}
and

\hat{c}_{\omega}:=(1+c_{\omega})^{-1}
, and taking
\theta=\gamma/\beta_{L}
, we find

\int_{0}^{t_{\omega}}f(X^{x_{0}}_{t}(\omega))\,dt\geq\hat{c}_{\omega}\int_{0}^{\hat{t}_{\omega}}\frac{c}{t^{\gamma}+t^{\frac{\gamma}{\theta\beta}}}\,dt=\hat{c}_{\omega}\int_{0}^{\hat{t}_{\omega}}\frac{c}{t^{\gamma}+t}\,dt=\infty.

As a result
R^{\kappa}f(x_{0})=\infty
,
which shows that
\{x_{0}\}
is polar.

Analogous reasoning may be proceeded for a
(d-2)
-dimensional (or lower) hyperplane
H_{d-2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

(here
d\geq 3
).
For simplicity, suppose that
H_{d-2}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\Pi(x)=0\}
, where
\Pi(x)=(x_{1},x_{2},0,\dots,0)
.
Set
f(x):=|\Pi(x)|^{-\gamma}\mathbf{1}_{B_{1}}(x)
, with
\gamma\in(\beta_{\Pi(L)}\vee 1,2)
. Noticing that
\Pi(L)

is again a Lévy process,
\beta_{\Pi(L)}\leq\beta_{L}
and
f\in L^{1}
, one easily computes that

H_{d-2}
is a polar set for operators from Examples
3.1
,
3.2
,
3.4
.

4.
Auxiliary results, excessive functions

Throughout the section we fix
\kappa_{1}>\kappa_{0}
and we consequently drop it in the notation.
Namely, we let

(4.1)

\begin{split}&P_{t}^{V}:=P^{\kappa_{1},V}_{t},\quad P_{t}^{*,V}:=P^{\kappa_{1},*,V}_{t},\quad R^{V}_{\alpha}:=R^{\kappa_{1},V}_{\alpha},\quad R^{*,V}_{\alpha}:=R^{\kappa_{1},*,V}_{\alpha},\quad\mathbb{E}_{x}:=\mathbb{E}^{\kappa_{1}}_{x},\\
&\mathbb{E}_{x}^{V}:=\mathbb{E}^{\kappa_{1},V}_{x},\quad\mathbb{E}_{x}^{*}:=\mathbb{E}^{\kappa_{1},*}_{x},\quad\mathbb{E}_{x}^{*,V}:=\mathbb{E}^{\kappa_{1},*,V}_{x},L:=L^{\kappa}_{1},\quad L^{*}:=L^{\kappa_{1},*},\\
&L^{(p)}_{V}:=L^{(p)}_{\kappa_{1},V},\quad L^{(p),*}_{V}:=L^{(p),*}_{\kappa_{1},V},\quad T_{t}^{(p),V}:=T_{t}^{\kappa_{1},(p),V},\quad T_{t}^{(p),*,V}:=T_{t}^{\kappa_{1},(p),*,V}.\end{split}

We shall start with the following result that
can be found in
[
28
, Lemma II.1]
or, under more restrictive assumptions on
b
, in
[
49
, Lemma 5.6]
.
The term
j_{\varepsilon}\ast(b\cdot\nabla u)
below is understood as a distribution on
D
:

(j_{\varepsilon}\ast(b\cdot\nabla u),\eta):=-(u,\text{div}[b(j_{\varepsilon}\ast\eta)]),\quad\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D).

Thanks to the assumptions made on
b
one have that the distribution
j_{\varepsilon}\ast(b\cdot\nabla u)

is in fact a locally integrable function.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(D)

b\cdot\nabla(u\ast j_{\varepsilon})-j_{\varepsilon}\ast(b\cdot\nabla u)\to 0\quad\text{a.e. in }D,

and in
L^{1}_{loc}(D)
.

By the very definition
G_{V}(\cdot,y)
is
(P^{V}_{t})
-excessive for any
y\in V
and

G_{V}(x,\cdot)
is
(P^{V,*}_{t})
-excessive for any
x\in V
. Therefore
R^{V}\mu
(resp.
R^{V,*}\mu
) is
(P^{V}_{t})
-excessive
(resp.
(P^{V,*}_{t})
-excessive) for any positive Borel measure
\mu
on
V
. Note also that under assumptions that we made in
the previous section any
(P^{V}_{t})
-excessive function is Borel measurable (see
[
8
, Proposition V.1.4]
).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
V
be an open bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
(\beta_{n}),\beta
be bounded
positive Borel measures on
V
. Suppose that
\beta_{n}\to\beta
in
[C_{b}(V)]^{*}
, then

(i)

R^{V}\beta_{n}+R^{V}\beta<\infty
q.e.,
n\geq 1
,

(ii)

R^{V}\beta_{n}\to R^{V}\beta
a.e. up to a subsequence.

Proof.

(i) Let
g
be a bounded strictly positive Borel function on
V
. Then

(R^{V}\beta,g)=(\beta,R^{*,V}g)\leq\|R^{*,V}g\|_{\infty}\beta(V)\leq\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}-\kappa_{0}}\|g\|_{\infty}\beta(V)<\infty,

and the same holds true for
\beta_{n}
. Now, the result follows from
[
8
, Proposition VI.2.3]
.

(ii) By
[
26
, Lemma 94, page 306]
there exists a subsequence (not relabeled)
and a
(P^{V}_{t})
-excessive function
w
such that
R^{V}\beta_{n}\to w
a.e.
Let
\xi\in\mathscr{B}_{b}(V)
. By Hypothesis
2.2

R^{*,V}\xi\in C_{b}(V)
(see also
[
55
, Theorem 3.4]
).
Thus

(R^{V}\beta_{n},\xi)=(\beta_{n},R^{*,V}\xi)\to(\beta,R^{*,V}\xi)=(R^{V}\beta,\xi).

In other words,
R^{V}\beta_{n}
weakly converges to
R^{V}\beta
in
L^{1}(V)
.
By the Dunford-Pettis theorem the sequence
(R^{V}\beta_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is uniformly integrable.
As a result, by Vitali’s theorem,
w=R^{V}\beta
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(V_{n})
be an increasing sequence of open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
V:=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}V_{n}
.
Suppose that
(w_{n})
is an increasing sequence of positive Borel functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

and
w_{n}
is
(P^{V_{n}}_{t})
-excessive for each
n\geq 1
.
Then
w:=\lim_{n\to\infty}w_{n}
is
(P^{V}_{t})
-excessive.

Proof.

By
[
8
, Proposition II.2.2]

w
is
(P^{V_{n}}_{t})
-excessive for any
n\geq 1
. As a result
w

is finely continuous on
V
and (see
[
8
, Proposition II.2.3]
)

\alpha R^{V_{n}}_{\alpha}w(x)\leq w(x),\quad x\in V_{n},\,n\geq 1.

We have (see (
2.10
))

R^{V_{n}}_{\alpha}w(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\int_{0}^{\tau_{V_{n}}}e^{-\alpha t}w(X_{s})\,ds,\quad x\in V_{n}.

On the other hand one easily sees that

\tau_{V_{n}}\nearrow\tau_{V},\quad n\to\infty.

Therefore, letting
n\to\infty
in the above inequality yields

\alpha R^{V}_{\alpha}w(x)\leq w(x),\quad x\in V

for any
\alpha>0
. By fine continuity of
w
on
V
we get that
\alpha R^{V}_{\alpha}w(x)\to w(x),\,x\in V

as
\alpha\searrow 0
. Thus,
w
is
(P^{V}_{t})
-excessive (see
[
8
, Proposition II.2.3]
again).
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(V_{n})
be an increasing sequence of open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
V:=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}V_{n}
.
Let
\beta
be a positive Borel measure on
V
. Then

R^{V_{n}}\beta(x)\nearrow R^{V}\beta(x),\quad x\in V.

Proof.

By (
2.10
) for any positive Borel function
\eta
on
V

R^{*,V_{n}}\eta(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}^{*}\int_{0}^{\tau_{V_{n}}}\eta(X_{s})\,ds,\quad x\in V_{n}.

Thus,

R^{*,V_{n}}\eta\leq R^{*,V_{n+1}}\eta\leq R^{*,V}\eta\text{ on }V\text{ for any }n\geq 1.

Consequently,

(R^{V_{n}}\beta,\eta)=(\beta,R^{*,V_{n}}\eta)\leq(\beta,R^{*,V_{n+1}}\eta)=(R^{V_{n+1}}\beta,\eta),\quad\eta\in\mathscr{B}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

The same clearly holds true with
V_{n+1}
replaced by
V
. By the choice of
\eta

we infer that

(4.2)

R^{V_{n}}\beta\leq R^{V_{n+1}}\beta\leq R^{V}\beta\quad\text{a.e. on }V\text{ for any }n\geq 1.

Note that for any
m\geq 1
, each of functions
R^{V_{n}}\beta,\,n\geq m
and
R^{V}\beta
is
(P^{V_{m}}_{t})
-excessive.
Therefore, by (
2.17
) the inequalities in (
4.2
) hold for any
x\in V
.
Set
w(x):=\lim_{n\to\infty}R^{V_{n}}\beta(x),\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
By
[
8
, Proposition II.2.2]

w
is
(P^{V_{n}}_{t})
-excessive for any
n\geq 1
. As a result
w

is finely continuous on
V
. Obviously,
w\leq R^{V}\beta
.
Observe that

\tau_{V_{n}}\nearrow\tau_{V},\quad n\to\infty.

As a result
R^{*,V_{n}}\eta\nearrow R^{*,V}\eta
. Therefore

(w,\eta)=\lim_{n\to\infty}(R^{V_{n}}\beta,\eta)=\lim_{n\to\infty}(\beta,R^{*,V_{n}}\eta)=(\beta,R^{*,V}\eta)=(R^{V}\beta,\eta).

Hence,
w=R^{V}\beta
a.e., which in turn implies, by (
2.17
) again, that
w=R^{V}\beta
in
V
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
V
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a polar closed subset of
V
.
Furthermore, let
\beta
be a positive Borel measure on
V
. Then

R^{V}\beta(x)=R^{V\setminus B}\beta(x),\quad x\in V\setminus B.

Proof.

Suppose that we know that

(4.3)

R^{*,V}\eta(x)=R^{*,V\setminus B}\eta(x),\quad x\in V\setminus B

for any positive Borel function
\eta
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then

(R^{V}\beta,\eta)=(\beta,R^{*,V}\eta)=(\beta,R^{*,V\setminus B}\eta)=(R^{V\setminus B}\beta,\eta).

Consequently,
R^{V}\beta=R^{V\setminus B}\beta
a.e. Since both functions are
(P_{t}^{V\setminus B})
-excessive
we have, by (
2.17
), that
R^{V}\beta=R^{V\setminus B}\beta
on
V\setminus B
. What is left is to show (
4.3
). But (see (
2.10
))

R^{*,V}\eta(x)=\mathbb{E}^{*}_{x}\int_{0}^{\tau_{V}}\eta(X_{s})\,ds,\quad R^{*,V\setminus B}\eta(x)=\mathbb{E}^{*}_{x}\int_{0}^{\tau_{V\setminus B}}\eta(X_{s})\,ds,\quad x\in V\setminus B.

Since
B
is polar, we have
\tau_{V}=\tau_{V\setminus B}\,\mathbb{P}^{*}_{x}
-a.s.
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see (
2.16
)), which implies the desired equality.
∎

We close this section by proving a slight generalization of the well known Dynkin formula.

Lemma 4.6
.

(Dynkin formula)

Let
B,V
be open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
B\subset V
.
Then for any positive
\sigma
-finite Borel measure
\mu
,

\mathbb{E}^{V}_{x}R^{V}\mu(X_{\tau_{B}})+R^{B}\mu(x)=R^{V}\mu(x),\quad x\in B.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may and will assume that
\mu
is bounded
(general case then follows by monotone approximation of
\mu
by bounded measures).
By
[
59
, Theorem 12.16]

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}^{V}_{x}\left\{\left[R^{V}\left(\beta R^{V}_{\beta}\mu\right)\right](X_{\tau_{B}})\right\}+\left[R^{B}\left(\beta R^{V}_{\beta}\mu\right)\right](x)=\left[R^{V}\left(\beta R^{V}_{\beta}\mu\right)\right]\left(x\right),\quad x\in B.\end{split}

Hence

\mathbb{E}^{V}_{x}\left\{\left[\beta R^{V}_{\beta}\left(R^{V}\mu\right)\right](X_{\tau_{B}})\right\}+\left[R^{B}\left(\beta R^{V}_{\beta}\mu\right)\right](x)=\left[\beta R^{V}_{\beta}\left(R^{V}\mu\right)\right]\left(x\right),\quad x\in B.

Since
R^{V}\mu
is
(P^{V}_{t})
-excessive, we have

\beta R^{V}_{\beta}\left(R^{V}\mu\right)\left(x\right)\nearrow R^{V}\mu(x),\quad x\in V.

Consequently, the most left term in the above equality
and the term on the right-hand side of the above inequality
converge pointwise to respective terms in the asserted equality.
From this, we conclude that the
second term on the left-hand side of the above equality is convergent
pointwise in
B
to a positive Borel function that we denote by
w
.
Let
\eta\in C_{b}(B)
. By Dynkin’s formula again

\mathbb{E}^{*,V}_{x}R^{*,V}\eta(X_{\tau_{B}})+R^{*,B}\eta(x)=R^{*,V}\eta(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

By
[
59
, Proposition 12.15]
,
V\ni x\mapsto\mathbb{E}^{*,V}_{x}R^{*,V}\eta(X_{\tau_{B}})

is
(P^{*,V}_{t})
-excessive. Therefore, by the above equality,
R^{*,B}\eta
is a difference
of finite finely continuous functions, and thus, it is finely continuous as well.
Consequently, by duality and the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem (note that
R^{B}(\beta R^{V}_{\beta}\mu)\leq 2R^{V}\mu
) for every bounded
\eta\in pC_{b}(B)
,

(w,\eta)=\lim_{\beta\to\infty}(R^{B}\left(\beta R^{V}_{\beta}\mu\right),\eta)=\lim_{\beta\to\infty}(\mu,\beta R^{*,V}_{\beta}R^{*,B}\eta)=(\mu,R^{*,B}\eta)=(R^{B}\mu,\eta).

From this one easily concludes the result (see (
2.17
)).
∎

5.
Distributional solutions

In the paper we are concerned with distributional solutions to (
1.4
).
This means that we would like to consider
-Au+b\cdot\nabla u+\lambda

as a distribution on
D\setminus K
.
In order to do so it is not enough, in general, to impose merely local integrability of
u
on
D\setminus K
.
This is due to the presence of a nonlocal term in (
1.5
) which forces some integral properties of
u

in the whole
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Definition 5.1
.

Fix an open set
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For any compact
F\subset V
, we let
r_{F}:=(2\text{dist}(F,V^{c}))\wedge 1
and

\rho_{F}(x):=\nu(B^{c}_{r_{F}}\cap(F-x))+\nu(B^{c}_{r_{F}}\cap(x-F)).

We further define the linear space

\mathscr{T}_{\nu}(V):=\bigcap_{F\text{-compact},\,F\subset V}L^{1}_{\rho_{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Remark 5.2
.

Notice that
\rho_{F_{1}}\leq\rho_{F_{2}}
whenever
F_{1}\subset F_{2}
. Furthermore, in case of the fractional Laplacian

\rho_{F_{2}}\leq C(F_{1},F_{2})\rho_{F_{1}}
, and as a result,

\mathscr{T}_{\nu}(V)=L^{1}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with
\rho(x):=\int_{B_{1}(x)}1\wedge|y|^{2}\,\nu(dy)
.

Observe that in case
\nu
has the full support,
\mathscr{T}_{\nu}(V)\subset L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
In general however the inclusion does not hold (take e.g.
\nu\equiv 0
).

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
A
be a Lévy operator and
u\in\mathscr{T}_{\nu}(V)\cap L^{1}_{loc}(V)
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|\,|A\eta|<\infty,\quad\eta\in C^{2}_{c}(V).

Proof.

Clearly, the above relation holds for operators of the form
(
1.5
) with
\nu\equiv 0
.
Consequently, we only need to show that for any

\eta\in C_{c}^{2}(V)
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|\chi_{\eta}<\infty
, where

\chi_{\eta}(x):=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\eta(x)-\eta(x+y)-y\cdot\nabla\eta(x)\mathbf{1}_{B(0,1)})\,\nu(dy)\right|,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Fix
\eta\in C_{c}^{2}(V)
and let
r:=\text{dist}(supp[\eta],V^{c})
.
Notice that for any
x\in V
,

\left|\int_{B_{(r/2)\wedge 1}}(\eta(x)-\eta(x+y)-y\nabla\eta(x))\,\nu(dy)\right|\leq\int_{B_{(r/2)\wedge 1}}|y|^{2}\sup_{\theta\in[0,1]}|D^{2}\eta(x+\theta y)|\,\nu(dy)=:\chi_{1}(x).

Clearly
\chi_{1}
is compactly supported in
V
.
Now, set

\chi_{2}(x):=\left|\int_{B^{c}_{(r/2)\wedge 1}}(\eta(x)-\eta(x+y))\,\nu(dy)\right|,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

and observe that
\chi_{\eta}\leq\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}+\nu(B_{1}\setminus B_{r/2})|\nabla\eta|
.
On the other hand

\chi_{2}(x)\leq\nu(B^{c}_{(r/2)\wedge 1})\eta(x)+\int_{B^{c}_{(r/2)\wedge 1}}|\eta(x+y)|\,\nu(dy),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Consequently,

\chi_{\eta}\leq\chi_{1}+\nu(B_{1}\setminus B_{r/2})|\nabla\eta|+\nu(B^{c}_{(r/2)\wedge 1})\eta(x)+\|\eta\|_{\infty}\nu(B^{c}_{(r/2)\wedge 1}\cap(\text{supp}[\eta]-x)),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The first three terms on the right-hand side of the above inequality are compactly supported in
V
,
which combined with fact that
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(V)
implies that their sum multiplied by
|u|

is integrable over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
By the definition of the space
\mathscr{T}_{\nu}(V)
, we have that also the most right term
above multiplied by
|u|
is integrable in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 5.4
.

We say that

Remark 5.5

Let us define measure

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\eta,\xi\in p\mathscr{B}(E)
and

supp[\eta]\cap supp[\xi]=\emptyset
. Then

(5.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta(x)\xi(y)\nu_{x}(dy)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta(y)\xi(x)\nu^{*}_{x}(dy)\,dx.

Proof.

The asserted equality easily follows for smooth and compactly supported

and

is the Lévy operator with triplet

and

is its dual, so with triplet

∎

Recall that the definition of the measure

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
V\subset\subset D
be an open set, and

be a positive Borel measurable function on

.
Then for any

R^{\kappa,V}\nu^{*,D}_{u}(x)=\mathbb{E}^{\kappa}_{x}[\mathbf{1}_{D^{c}}u(X_{\tau_{V}})],\quad x\in V.

Proof.

The left-hand side of the asserted identity equals

\int_{V}G^{\kappa}_{V}(x,y)\,\nu^{*,D}_{u}(dy)=\int_{V}\int_{D^{c}}G^{\kappa}_{V}(x,y)u(z)\,\nu^{*}_{z}(dy)\,dz.

On the other hand, by the Ikeda-Watanabe formula (see

Now, the result follows from Lemma

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
V
be an open bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

Suppose that

is a positive
(P^{V}_{t})
-harmonic function.
Then, for any
\eta\in C^{2}_{c}(V)

(6.2)

\int_{V}hL^{*}\eta=0.

Proof.

We let
h=0

V^{c}

For any positive

we have

\begin{split}-\int_{V}hL^{*}\eta&=-\int_{V}hL^{(2),*}_{V}\eta=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{V}h\frac{\eta-T^{(2),*,V}_{t}\eta}{t}\\
&=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{V}h\frac{\eta-P^{*,V}_{t}\eta}{t}=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{V}\frac{h-P^{V}_{t}h}{t}\eta\geq 0\end{split}

(in the second equality we used the fact that

By the Riesz theorem there exists a positive Radon measure
\beta
on
V

-\int_{V}hL^{*}\eta=\int_{V}\eta\,d\beta,\quad\eta\in C_{c}^{2}(V).

Let
V^{\delta}:=\{x\in V:\text{dist}(x,\partial V)>\delta\}

Let
\rho_{\varepsilon}
be the standard smooth mollifier. Then letting

and
\beta_{\varepsilon}:=\beta\ast\rho_{\varepsilon}

\begin{split}\int_{V}\beta_{\varepsilon}\eta=\int_{V}\eta_{\varepsilon}\,d\beta=(h,(-A^{*}+\kappa_{1}I)\eta_{\varepsilon})+(h,(b\nabla)^{*}\eta_{\varepsilon})=(h_{\varepsilon},(-A^{*}+\kappa_{1}I)\eta)+((b\nabla h)_{\varepsilon},\eta).\end{split}

Set (see the comments preceding Lemma
4.1
)

f^{\varepsilon}:=-(b\nabla h)_{\varepsilon}+b\nabla h_{\varepsilon}.

Then

-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Lh_{\varepsilon}\eta=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(f^{\varepsilon}+\beta_{\varepsilon})\eta,\quad\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(V^{\delta}).

From the above equation and continuity of
Lh_{\varepsilon}

and
\beta_{\varepsilon}

Then, by using Dynkin’s martingale formula (see e.g.

Set
\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(x):=\mathbb{E}_{x}h_{\varepsilon}(X_{\tau_{V^{\delta}}})
.
Since

is non-negative we have that
\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}

is
(P^{V^{\delta}}_{t})
-excessive (see e.g.
[
8
, Proposition II.2.8]

By
[
26
, Lemma 94, page 306]

, to a
(P^{V^{\delta}}_{t})
-excessive function
\gamma^{\delta}
.
By Lemmas
4.1
,
4.2

R^{V^{\delta}}\beta_{\varepsilon}\to R^{V^{\delta}}\beta

R^{V^{\delta}}f^{\varepsilon}\to 0

2.17

(6.4)

h=\gamma^{\delta}+R^{V^{\delta}}\beta\quad\text{ in }V^{\delta}.

Letting

we obtain, by Lemma

.
From this and (
6.4
), we conclude, by Fatou’s lemma, that

By the said convergence, we have that
(\gamma^{\delta})
is convergent too.
Let us denote its limit by

. We thus have

Recall that
\gamma^{\delta}

(P_{t}^{V^{\delta}})

\hat{\delta}\in(\delta,\infty)

2.8

Set

Remark 7.5
.

Observe that, letting

w_{V}(x):=\mathbb{P}^{0}_{x}(\tau_{V}<\tau_{D})=\mathbb{E}^{0,D}_{x}\varphi(X_{\tau_{V}}),\quad x\in D.

By

(P^{0,D}_{t})
-excessive function.
Thus, whenever the

(7.12)

f\in p\mathscr{B}_{b}(D),\,R^{\kappa,D}f(x)=0\text{ for some }x\in D\quad\Rightarrow\quad R^{\kappa,D}f\equiv 0\text{ in }D,

.

-excessive , we have

Remark 7.7
.

Observe that

Abstract
. We study the De Giorgi-Moser-Nash estimates of higher-order parabolic equations in divergence form with complex-valued, measurable, bounded, uniformly elliptic (in the sense of G a ̊ \mathring{a} rding inequality) and time-independent coefficients. We also obtain Gaussian upper bounds and Hölder regularity estimates for the fundamental solutions of this class of parabolic equations.



1.
Introduction

In
[
2
,
16
]
, Gaussian upper and lower bounds for the fundamental solutions of second order parabolic equations in divergence form with real and bounded coefficients were established. Later, under the assumption that the complex-valued and time-independent coefficients are a small
L^{\infty}
perturbation of real coefficients, Auscher
[
4
]
showed that Gaussian upper bound holds for the fundamental solutions of second order parabolic equations of this type. Subsequently, Hofmann and Kim
[
18
]
proved that Auscher’s result remain valid for a parabolic system of second order with time-dependent coefficients if the system is a small perturbation of a diagonal system. In particular, inspired by the result of Auscher, McIntosh and Tchamitchian
[
6
]
, Kim
[
20
]
showed that the fundamental solutions of second order parabolic systems with time-independent and complex-valued coefficients in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
have Gaussian upper bounds in the absence of the small perturbation assumption. More importantly, Kim constructed the Hölder continuity estimates for weak solutions of a second-order parabolic system with time-dependent coefficients if weak solutions of the corresponding elliptic system satisfy the Hölder estimates at every scale.

In 2000, Auscher and Qafsaoui
[
7
]
extended the results in
[
4
]
for second order elliptic operators to elliptic operators of any order, in which they found a criterion to decide on whether upper bounds and Hölder regularity hold for the heat kernel of divergence form elliptic operators (systems) of higher order with complex coeffcients on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}.
Such a criterion essentially indicates that only the principal part influences the kernel’s behavior, as adding lower order terms with bounded coefficients preserves any existing bounds. It is stable under
L^{\infty}
perturbation of the principal part coefficients, in line with the second order case, and provides heat kernel estimates for operators with VMO coefficients. Based on this work and the strategy of Kim
[
20
]
, we investigate the De Giorgi-Moser-Nash estimates and Gaussain estimates for higher-order parabolic operators in form of

(1.1)

\mathcal{H}:=\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}:=\partial_{t}+\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}(-1)^{|\alpha|}\partial^{\alpha}(a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\partial^{\beta})

in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}.

A major goal of this paper is to prove that the fundamental solutions to parabolic operators shaped like (
1.1
) with
t-
independent coefficients have Gaussian upper bounds and Hölder regularity estimates when
2m\geq n
(Theorem
5.5
). We also show that if weak solutions of an elliptic equation (
\mathcal{L}
) verify the Hölder property
H(\mu,\infty)
(the higher-order version of the De Giorgi-Moser-Nash estimates, see Definition
2.1
), then weak solutions of the corresponding parabolic equation
(
\mathcal{H}
) with
t-
independent coefficients satisfy a similar parabolic Hölder property (Theorem
4.1
). Building upon this result, a small
L^{\infty}
perturbation theorem (Theorem
4.3
) is established for higher order parabolic equations with
t-
independent coefficients. This result accomplishes two important extensions:
(i)
it generalizes the elliptic case established in
[
7
]
to parabolic equations, and
(ii)

it provides a higher-order analogue of the result in
[
4
]
. It also yields that when the coefficients are
t-
independent and small
L^{\infty}
perturbations of complex constants or VMO functions, weak solutions of a parabolic operator verify the Hölder property
H(\mu,\infty)
(see Remark
4.4
). We stress that these results can be extended to systems without difficulty. On the other hand, our main results have direct applications in developing higher-order layer potential theory, offering solutions to boundary value problems associated with higher-order parabolic operators. Readers may refer to
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
13
,
22
]
for more details on this topic.

The remaining sections are organized as follows. In Section 2 we introduce notations, basic definitions and assumptions, and function spaces. We then establish some technical lemmas for solutions to higher-order elliptic and parabolic equations with time-independent coefficients in Section 3, and use them to prove the Hölder property
H(\mu,\infty)
in Section 4. The last section is devoted to building Gaussian estimates for the fundamental solution of parabolic equations under consideration.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1. Notation.
Given a typical point
X:=(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}:=\{(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}:t\in{\mathbb{R}},x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\}
with
x=(x_{1},...x_{n})
(
n\geq 2
). For any
r>0,

B_{r}(x):=\{y\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:|x-y|<r\}
is an open ball in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},
and we use
Q_{r}:=Q_{r}(t,x)=(t-r^{2m},t]\times B_{r}(x)
and
Q^{*}_{r}(t,x):=[t,t+R^{2m})\times B_{R}(x)
to denote half cubes in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}.
For any multi-index
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},...\gamma_{n})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n},
let

D^{\gamma}:=\partial^{\gamma_{1}}_{x_{1}}...\partial^{\gamma_{n}}_{x_{n}}

and
|\gamma|:=\gamma_{1}+...+\gamma_{n}.
We also let
D^{k}:=\nabla^{k}:=(D^{\gamma})_{|\gamma|=k}
for any
0\leq k\leq m.
Besides, we need the notation for averages, expressed as follows:

\fint_{E}f:=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}f(x)dx,

where
E
is a Borel measurable set in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
or
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, with its Lebesgue measure
0<|E|<\infty,
and
f
is a locally integrable function.

2.2. The parabolic operator of higher order.
We consider higher order parabolic operators defined as (
1.1
). The coefficients
\{a_{\alpha,\beta}\}
are assumed to be bounded, uniformly elliptic (in the sense of G
\mathring{a}
rding inequality) and independent of
t
, that is, for all
u\in H^{m}:=W^{m,2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}),

(2.1)

\mbox{Re}<\mathcal{L}u,u>:=\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\partial^{\alpha}u\overline{\partial^{\beta}u}\geq\frac{c_{1}}{2}\|\nabla^{m}u\|_{2}^{2}-(\lambda_{0}+c_{3})\|u\|_{2}^{2},

and

(2.2)

|\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\xi_{\alpha}\eta_{\beta}|\leq c_{2}\left(\sum_{|\beta|=m}|\xi_{\beta}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{|\beta|=m}|\xi_{\eta}|^{2}\right)^{1/2},\quad\mbox{for all}\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

Below, in accordance with
[
7
]
, we denote by
\mathcal{E}_{2m}(c_{1},\lambda_{0},c_{2},c_{3})
the class of elliptic operators
\mathcal{L}
satisfying (
2.1
) and (
2.2
). If
\lambda_{0}=c_{3}=0,
then we denote by
\mathcal{E}_{2m}(c_{1},0,c_{2}).

2.3. Weak solutions.

Let
\Omega\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a domain and
H^{m}(\Omega):=W^{m,2}(\Omega)
be the Sobolev space of complex valued functions
u,
defined on
\Omega,
such that
D^{\alpha}u\in L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathbb{C}})
for any
|\alpha|\leq m.
We say that
u\in W^{m,2}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of

\mathcal{L}u=f\in L^{2}(\Omega)\;\quad\mbox{in}\;\Omega

if for any
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)

\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\int_{\Omega}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}u\overline{\partial^{\alpha}\psi}=\int_{\Omega}f\overline{\psi}.

Define
\Omega_{t_{1},t_{2}}:=[t_{1},t_{2}]\times\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}\times{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}.

We say that
u\in H^{m,2}(\Omega_{t_{1},t_{2}}):=L^{2}([t_{1},t_{2}],W^{m,2}(\Omega))
is a weak solution of
\mathcal{H}
in
\Omega_{t_{1},t_{2}},
if for any
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{t_{1},t_{2}}),

-\int_{\Omega_{t_{1},t_{2}}}u\overline{\partial_{t}\phi}+\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\int_{\Omega_{t_{1},t_{2}}}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}u\overline{\partial^{\alpha}\phi}=0.

By the definition of the weak solution to
\mathcal{H},
the adjoint
\mathcal{H}^{*}
of
\mathcal{H}
can be identified formally as the backward-in-time operator

-\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}^{*}:=-\partial_{t}+\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}(-1)^{|\beta|}\partial^{\beta}(\overline{a_{\alpha,\beta}}\partial^{\alpha}).

Clearly, if
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{E}_{2m}(c_{1},0,c_{2}),
so is
\mathcal{L}^{*}.

To circumvent technical difficulties, we impose a smoothness assumption on the coefficients-a widely used strategy in numerous studies such as
[
15
,
18
,
20
]
. This simplification is ultimately harmless, as our arguments never quantitatively rely on the additional regularity. This is why we make the dependence of constants clear in what follows.

2.4. Basic definitions.
According to
[
9
, Theorem 3.10]
and
[
7
, Definition 6]
(the global case), we can introduce the following definition, that is the higher-order version of the De Giorgi-Moser-Nash estimates in
[
1
,
13
,
19
,
22
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{E}_{2m}(c_{1},0,c_{2}).
Let
\mu\in(\max\{0,m-\frac{n}{2}\},m]\setminus{\mathbb{N}}.
Write
\mu=l+\nu,
where
l\in\{0,1,...m-1\}
and
\nu\in(0,1).
We say that
\mathcal{L}
has the Hölder property
H(\mu,\infty)
if the following conditions hold: there exists a constant
c_{0}>0
such that for all
0<R<\infty,
for all
x_{0}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},
for all solutions
u
of
\mathcal{L}
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
and for all multi-index
\gamma\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
with
|\gamma|\leq l,

(2.3)

R^{|\gamma|}\sup_{B_{R/2}(x_{0})}|D^{\gamma}u|\leq c_{0}\left(\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

and if
|\gamma|=l,

(2.4)

R^{|\gamma|+\nu}[[D^{\gamma}u]]_{\nu,B_{R/2}(x_{0})}\leq c_{0}\left(\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where

[[u]]_{\nu,\Omega}:=\sup_{x\neq y,x,y\in\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\nu}}.

Similarly, letting
Q_{R}:=Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0}),
we say that
\mathcal{H}
defined in (
1.1
) satisfies the Hölder property
H(\mu,\infty)
if (
2.3
)-(
2.4
) hold with
B_{R/2}(x_{0}),B_{R}(x_{0})
and
[[D^{\gamma}u]]_{\nu,B_{R/2}(x_{0})}
replaced by
Q_{R/2},Q_{R}
and
[D^{\gamma}u]_{\nu,Q_{R/2}},
respectively, where

[u]_{\nu,Q_{R}}:=\sup_{X\neq Y,X,Y\in Q_{R}}\frac{|u(X)-u(Y)|}{|X-Y|^{\nu}},\quad|X-Y|:=|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2m}}+|x-y|.

Moreover, we say that
\mathcal{H}^{*}
satisfies the Hölder property
H(\mu,\infty)
if (
2.3
)-(
2.4
) hold with
B_{R/2}(x_{0})

B_{R}(x_{0})
and
[[D^{\gamma}u]]_{\nu,B_{R/2}(x_{0})}
replaced by
Q^{*}_{R/2},Q^{*}_{R}
and
[D^{\gamma}u]_{\nu,Q^{*}_{R/2}},
respectively.

We also introduce the so-called Gaussain estimates, that is the global property defined in
[
7
, Definition 9]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Assume
\mathcal{E}_{2m}(c_{1},\lambda_{0},c_{2},c_{3}).
Let
\mu\in(\max\{0,m-\frac{n}{2}\},m]\setminus{\mathbb{N}}.
Write
\mu=l+\nu,
where
l\in\{0,1,...m-1\}
and
\nu\in(0,1).
We say that
\mathcal{L}
has the Gaussain property
G(\mu,\infty)
if the following condition holds: there exist constant
c>0
and
a>0
such that for all
0<t<\infty,
for all
x,y,h\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},
and for all multi-index
\gamma\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
with
|\gamma|\leq l,

(2.5)

|D_{x}^{\gamma}K_{t}(x,y)|+|D_{y}^{\gamma}K_{t}(x,y)|\leq\frac{c}{(t-s)^{\frac{n+|\gamma|}{2m}}}g_{m,a}(\frac{|x-y|}{t^{\frac{1}{2m}}}),

and if
|\gamma|=l,

(2.6)

|D_{x}^{\gamma}K_{t}(x+h,y)-D_{x}^{\gamma}K_{t}(x,y)|\leq\frac{c}{t^{\frac{n+l}{2m}}}\left(\frac{|h|}{\frac{1}{2m}}\right)^{\nu},

(2.7)

|D_{x}^{\gamma}K_{t}(x,y+h)-D_{x}^{\gamma}K_{t}(x,y)|\leq\frac{c}{t^{\frac{n+l}{2m}}}\left(\frac{|h|}{\frac{1}{2m}}\right)^{\nu},

where
g_{m,a}(s):=e^{-as^{\frac{2m}{2m-1}}}
for
s>0
and
K_{t}(,y)
denotes the distributional kernel of the semigroup
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}.

2.5. The explicit constants.
The two letters
C,c
will be used to denote universal constants depending on
n,m,c_{1},c_{2}
and they may vary from line to line. To emphasize the extra dependence on the prescribed quantities
a,b,....,
we write
C=C(a,b,....)
and
c=c(a,b,....).

3.
Technical lemmas

This section focuses on constructing the key lemmas that will serve as the foundation for the proof of the Hölder property from elliptic equations to prabolic equations in the subsequent section. We alway assume
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{E}_{2m}(c_{1},0,c_{2})
in this section.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R>0
and
u\in H^{m,2}(Q_{R}(t,x))
be a weak solution of
\mathcal{H}
in
Q_{R}(t,x).
Then for any
0<r<R,
we have

(3.1)

\sup_{t-r^{2m}<s\leq t}\|u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{r}(x))}+\int_{Q_{r}(t,x)}|\nabla^{m}u|^{2}\leq C\frac{\int_{Q_{R}(t,x)}|u|^{2}}{(R-r)^{2m}}.

In particular, for
j=0,1,2,...m-1,

(3.2)

\int_{Q_{r}(t,x)}|\nabla^{j}u|^{2}\leq C\frac{\int_{Q_{R}(t,x)}|u|^{2}}{(R-r)^{2j}}.

Proof.
For simplicity, we assume
t=0,x=0.
Set

r_{0}=r,r_{k}=r+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{R-r}{2^{k}},\;\mbox{k=1,2,...}

and

s_{k}=\frac{r_{k}+r_{k+1}}{2},\;\mbox{k=0,1,2,...}.

We choose a sequence of nonnegative functions
\phi_{k}\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
such that
\phi_{k}=1
on
Q_{r_{k}}
and
\phi_{k}=0
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus(-s_{k}^{2m},s_{k}^{2m})\times B_{s_{k}},
moreover,

\|\partial_{t}\phi_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c\frac{2^{2mk}}{(R-r)^{2m}},\quad\|D^{l}\phi_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c\frac{2^{2lk}}{(R-r)^{l}},\;\mbox{l=0, 1,2,...}.

Testing the equation by
u\phi_{k}^{2}1_{(-\infty,s)}(t)
for any
-r^{2m}<s\leq 0
and using the fact that the coefficients are
t-
independent, we get

(3.3)

-\int_{Q_{R}\cap E_{s}}|u|^{2}\phi_{k}\partial_{t}\phi_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}(0)}|u\phi_{k}|^{2}(s,x)dx+\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\int_{Q_{R}\cap E_{s}}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}u\partial^{\alpha}(\overline{u}\phi_{k}^{2})=0,

where
E_{s}:=(-\infty,s).
By Leibniz’s rule, the equation (
3.3
) can be rewritten as

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{R}\cap E_{s}}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}u\partial^{\alpha}(\overline{u}\phi_{k}^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{R}\cap E_{s}}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}u\partial^{\alpha}(\overline{u}\phi_{k}^{2})-\int_{Q_{R}\cap E_{s}}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}(u\phi_{k})\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u\phi_{k})}

\displaystyle+\int_{Q_{R}\cap E_{s}}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}(u\phi_{k})\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u\phi_{k})}:=I_{1}+I_{2}.

Bear in mind that
\phi(s,\cdot)\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\tau}(x_{0}))
for all
s\leq 0.
Then, it follows from (
2.1
) that

(3.5)

\mbox{Re}\;I_{2}\geq c_{1}\int_{Q_{R}\cap E_{s}}|\nabla^{m}(u\phi_{k})|^{2}.

To proceed, we decompose
I_{1}
into

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{R}\cap E_{s}}a_{\alpha,\beta}[\phi_{k}\partial^{\beta}u-\partial^{\beta}(u\phi_{k})]\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u\phi_{k})}+a_{\alpha,\beta}[\phi_{k}\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u\phi_{k})}-\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u\phi_{k}^{2})}]\partial^{\beta}u

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma<\beta}C_{\beta}^{\gamma}\int_{Q_{R}\cap E_{s}}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\gamma}u\partial^{\beta-\gamma}\phi_{k}\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u\phi_{k})}-\sum_{\tau<\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\tau}\partial^{\beta}u\partial^{\alpha-\tau}\phi_{k}\overline{\partial^{\tau}(u\phi_{k})}

\displaystyle:=I_{11}-I_{12}.

Recalling the definition of
\phi_{k},
the term
I_{11}
can be further written as

\displaystyle I_{11}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma<\beta}C_{\beta}^{\gamma}\int_{Q_{R}\cap E_{s}}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\gamma}(u\phi_{k+1})\partial^{\beta-\gamma}\phi_{k}\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u\phi_{k+1}\phi_{k})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma<\beta}\sum_{\tau\leq\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\tau}C_{\beta}^{\gamma}\int_{Q_{R}\cap E_{s}}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\gamma}(u\phi_{k+1})\partial^{\beta-\gamma}\phi_{k}\partial^{\alpha-\tau}\phi_{k}\overline{\partial^{\tau}(u\phi_{k+1})}.

In veiw of the property of
\phi_{k}
and interpolation inequalities, we can estimate
I_{11}
as follows:

\displaystyle|I_{111}|

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{\gamma<\beta}\sum_{\tau\leq\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\tau}C_{\beta}^{\gamma}\left(\frac{2^{k}}{R-r}\right)^{m-|\gamma|+m-|\tau|}\|\partial^{\gamma}(u\phi_{k+1})\|_{L^{2}(R^{n+1}_{0})}\|\partial^{\tau}(u\phi_{k+1})\|_{L^{2}(R^{n+1}_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{\gamma<\beta}\sum_{\tau\leq\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\tau}C_{\beta}^{\gamma}\left(\frac{2^{k}}{R-r}\right)^{m-|\gamma|}\|D^{m}(u\phi_{k+1})\|_{L^{2}(R^{n+1}_{0})}^{|\gamma|/m}\|u\phi_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}(R^{n+1}_{0})}^{1-\frac{|\gamma|}{m}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{2^{k}}{R-r}\right)^{m-|\tau|}\|D^{m}(u\phi_{k+1})\|_{L^{2}(R^{n+1}_{0})}^{|\tau|/m}\|u\phi_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}(R^{n+1}_{0})}^{1-\frac{|\tau|}{m}}

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{\gamma<\beta}\sum_{\tau\leq\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\tau}C_{\beta}^{\gamma}\left(\epsilon\|D^{m}(u\phi_{k+1})\|_{L^{2}(R^{n+1}_{0})}+\left(\frac{2^{k}}{R-r}\right)^{m}\epsilon^{\frac{|\gamma|}{|\gamma|-m}}\|u\phi_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}(R^{n+1}_{0})}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\epsilon\|D^{m}(u\phi_{k+1})\|_{L^{2}(R^{n+1}_{0})}+\left(\frac{2^{k}}{R-r}\right)^{m}\epsilon^{\frac{|\tau|}{|\tau|-m}}\|u\phi_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}(R^{n+1}_{0})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\|D^{m}(u\phi_{k+1})\|_{L^{2}(R^{n+1}_{0})}^{2}+c\left(\frac{2^{k}}{R-r}\right)^{2m}\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\epsilon^{\frac{l}{l-m}}\|u\phi_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}(R^{n+1}_{0})}^{2},

where
R^{n+1}_{0}:=(-\infty,0)\times{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}.
We remark that, in the above argument, we ignore the case
|\tau|=m,
which, in fact, is easier. Observing that
I_{12}
and
I_{11}
are the same type, thus, by the way to tackle
I_{11}
we conclude with

I_{12}\leq\epsilon\|D^{m}(u\phi_{k+1})\|_{L^{2}(R^{n+1}_{0})}^{2}+c\left(\frac{2^{k}}{R-r}\right)^{2m}\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\epsilon^{\frac{l}{l-m}}\|u\phi_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}(R^{n+1}_{0})}^{2}:=I_{3}.

Clearly, the same bound holds for
I_{1}.

Collecting (
3.3
)-(
3.5
) and the above bound for
I_{1},
we obtain

(3.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}(0)}|u\phi_{k}|^{2}(s,x)dx+c_{1}\int_{Q_{R}\cap E_{s}}|\nabla^{m}(u\phi_{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Q_{R}\cap E_{s}}|u|^{2}|\phi_{k}\partial_{t}\phi_{k}|+cI_{3}

\displaystyle\leq c\frac{2^{2mk}}{(R-r)^{2m}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{R})}^{2}+cI_{3}.

Letting
s=0
in (
3.6
) we have

\frac{1}{2}\sup_{-r^{2m}<s\leq 0}\int_{B_{R}(0)}|u\phi_{k}|^{2}(s,x)dx+\int_{R^{n+1}_{0}}|\nabla^{m}(u\phi_{k})|^{2}\leq c\frac{2^{2mk}}{(R-r)^{2m}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{R})}^{2}+cI_{3},

which implies

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{-r^{2m}<s\leq 0}\int_{B_{R}(0)}|u\phi_{k}|^{2}(s,x)dx

\displaystyle+\int_{R^{n+1}_{0}}|\nabla^{m}(u\phi_{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\int_{R^{n+1}_{0}}|\nabla^{m}(u\phi_{k+1})|^{2}+c\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}(1+\epsilon^{\frac{l}{l-m}})\frac{2^{2mk}}{(R-r)^{2m}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{R})}^{2}.

Note that
\phi_{0}=1
on
Q_{r}.
If we let
k=0
in (
3.7
), then

(3.8)

\displaystyle\sup_{-r^{2m}<s\leq 0}\int_{B_{r}(0)}|u|^{2}(s,x)dx

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{R^{n+1}_{0}}|\nabla^{m}(u\phi_{1})|^{2}+\frac{c}{(R-r)^{2m}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{R})}^{2}.

On the other hand, due to (
3.7
) again, an iteration argument displayed in
[
15
, Lemma 3.2]
leads to

\int_{R^{n+1}_{0}}|\nabla^{m}(u\phi_{0})|^{2}+\int_{R^{n+1}_{0}}|\nabla^{m}(u\phi_{1})|^{2}\leq\frac{c}{(R-r)^{2m}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{R})}^{2}.

Thus, by (
3.8
), we arrive at

\sup_{-r^{2m}<s\leq 0}\int_{B_{r}(0)}|u|^{2}(s,x)dx\leq\frac{c}{(R-r)^{2m}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{R})}^{2}.

In particular, (
3.2
) already established in
[
15
, Lemma 3.2]
. Consequently, we complete the proof for (
3.1
) and (
3.2
).

\Box

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u\in H^{m,2}(Q_{2R})
be a solution of
\mathcal{H}
in
Q_{2R}:=Q_{2R}(t_{0},x_{0}),
then
u_{t}\in L^{2}(Q_{r})
for any
0<r<R,
and the estimate

(3.9)

\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{r})}\leq C\frac{\|\nabla^{m}u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2R})}}{(R-r)^{m}}

holds. Moreover, if
u
is a solution of
\mathcal{H}
in
Q_{2r},
then (
3.9
) along with the energy estimate yield

(3.10)

\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{r})}\leq C\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2r})}}{r^{2m}}.

Proof.
Given two positive constants
\sigma,\tau
such that
\sigma<\tau\leq R.
Let
\phi
be a smooth cut-off function such that
\phi\equiv 1
in
Q_{\sigma},
vanishing near the boundary and verifying

(3.11)

0\leq\phi\leq 1,\quad\|\phi_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{c}{(\tau-\sigma)^{2m}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|D^{\gamma}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{c}{(\tau-\sigma)^{|\gamma|}},\;\forall\;|\gamma|\leq m.

For simplicity, we set
Q_{\tau,s}:={s}\times B_{\tau}(x_{0})
with
s\in(t_{0}-\tau^{2m},t_{0}).
Of course, on each slice
Q_{\tau,s}
,

\displaystyle 0=\int_{Q_{\tau,s}}\phi^{2}|u_{t}|^{2}+a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}u\phi\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u_{t}\phi)}+\int_{Q_{\tau,s}}\sum_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\alpha,|\alpha_{2}|<m}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}u(D^{\alpha_{1}}\phi)\overline{D^{\alpha_{2}}(u_{t}\phi)}

holds.
From this equality and Young’s inequality, it follows that

(3.12)

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{\tau,s}}\phi^{2}|u_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\int_{Q_{\tau,s}}|\nabla^{m}(u_{t}\phi)|^{2}+\frac{c}{\epsilon}\int_{Q_{\tau,s}}|\nabla^{m}u\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{Q_{\tau,s}}\sum_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\alpha,|\alpha_{2}|<m}|D^{\alpha_{1}}\phi||\partial^{\beta}u||D^{\alpha_{2}}(u_{t}\phi)|:=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}.

We first handle

Using Young’s inequality again and interpolation inequalities, it is easy to deduce

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{\epsilon}\int_{Q_{\tau,s}}|\nabla^{m}u|^{2}+c\epsilon\int_{Q_{\tau,s}}\sum_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\alpha,|\alpha_{2}|<m}|D^{\alpha_{1}}\phi|^{2}|D^{\alpha_{2}}(u_{t}\phi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{\epsilon}\int_{Q_{\tau,s}}|\nabla^{m}u|^{2}+c\frac{\epsilon}{2}\int_{Q_{\tau,s}}|\nabla^{m}(u_{t}\phi)|^{2}+c\frac{\epsilon}{(\tau-\sigma)^{2m}}\int_{Q_{\tau,s}}|u_{t}\phi|^{2}.

To manage
J_{1}
, we can apply the energy estimates (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) (adjusting the support of
\phi
if necessary) to derive

(3.13)

\int_{Q_{\tau}}|\nabla^{m}(u_{t}\phi)|^{2}\leq\frac{c}{(\tau-\sigma)^{2m}}\int_{Q_{\tau}}|u_{t}|^{2}

since
u_{t}
is also a weak solution of
\mathcal{H}
in
Q_{2R}.

J_{2}
is simply arranged by diacarding
\phi.
Consequently, (
3.12
) contributes to

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{\sigma}}|u_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\frac{c}{(\tau-\sigma)^{2m}}\int_{Q_{\tau}}|u_{t}|^{2}+\frac{c}{\epsilon}\int_{Q_{\tau}}|\nabla^{m}u|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\frac{c}{(\tau-\sigma)^{2m}}\int_{Q_{\tau}}|u_{t}|^{2}+c\frac{\epsilon}{(\tau-\sigma)^{2m}}\int_{Q_{\tau}}|u_{t}|^{2}.

Letting
\epsilon:=(\tau-\sigma)^{2m}\epsilon_{1}
with
\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{1}(c_{1},c_{2},m,n)
small enough in the latter inequality we have

(3.14)

\int_{Q_{\sigma}}|u_{t}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{Q_{\tau}}|u_{t}|^{2}+\frac{c}{(\tau-\sigma)^{2m}}\int_{Q_{\tau}}|\nabla^{m}u|^{2}.

Armed with (
3.14
), an application of the iteration lemma
[
17
, Lemma 5.1]
yields

(3.15)

\int_{Q_{r}}|u_{t}|^{2}\leq\frac{c}{(R-r)^{2m}}\int_{Q_{R}}|\nabla^{m}u|^{2}\quad\mbox{for all}\;0<r<R.

The proof is complete.

\Box

As a result of Lemma
3.2
, we can deduce the following uniform estimates on the slices.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
u\in H^{m,2}(Q_{2r})
be a solution of
\mathcal{H}
in
Q_{2r}:=Q_{2r}(t_{0},x_{0}).
Then
\nabla^{j}u(s,\cdot),u_{t}(s,\cdot)\in L^{2}(B_{r})
for all
0\leq j\leq m
and
s\in[t_{0}-r^{2m},t_{0}],
and satisfy

(3.16)

\sup_{s\in[t_{0}-r^{2m},t_{0}]}\|\nabla^{j}u(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(B_{r})}\leq C\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2r})}}{r^{m+j}},

(3.17)

\sup_{s\in[t_{0}-r^{2m},t_{0}]}\|u_{t}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(B_{r})}\leq C\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2r})}}{r^{3m}}.

Proof.
Fix
r\leq\sigma<\tau\leq 2r
and let
\phi
be a smooth cut-off function defined as in (
3.11
). We first note that

\sup_{t-\sigma^{2m}<s\leq t}\|u_{t}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{\sigma}(x))}\leq c\frac{\int_{Q_{\sigma+\frac{\tau-\sigma}{2}}}|u_{t}|^{2}}{(\tau-\sigma)^{2m}}

holds thanks to the
t
-independence of the coefficients and the energy estimate (
3.1
). Furthermore, it follows from (
3.15
) that

\int_{Q_{\sigma+\frac{\tau-\sigma}{2}}}|u_{t}|^{2}\leq c\frac{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{\tau})}}{(\tau-\sigma)^{4m}}.

The two estimates imply

(3.18)

\sup_{t-\sigma^{2m}<s\leq t}\|u_{t}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{\sigma}(x))}\leq c\frac{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{\tau})}}{(\tau-\sigma)^{6m}}.

For the moment, we assume that
u
is a weak solution of
\mathcal{H}
in
Q_{4r}.
Observe that, for any fixed
s,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\tau}}\phi^{2}u_{t}(s,\cdot)\overline{u(s,\cdot)}+a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}(u\phi)\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u\phi)}+\int_{B_{\tau}}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}u[\phi\overline{D^{\alpha}(u\phi)}-\overline{D^{\alpha}(u\phi^{2})}]

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{\tau}}a_{\alpha,\beta}[\phi\partial^{\beta}u-\partial^{\beta}(u\phi)]\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u\phi)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma<\beta}C_{\beta}^{\gamma}\int_{B_{\tau}}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\gamma}u\partial^{\beta-\gamma}\phi\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u\phi)}-\sum_{\tau<\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\tau}\int_{B_{\tau}}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}u\partial^{\alpha-\tau}\phi\overline{\partial^{\tau}(u\phi)}

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{\tau}}\phi^{2}u_{t}(s,\cdot)\overline{u(s,\cdot)}+a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}(u\phi)\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u\phi)}.

By this equality and (
2.1
), we see

(3.19)

\displaystyle c_{1}\int_{B_{\tau}}|\nabla^{m}(u\phi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{B_{\tau}}\phi^{2}|u_{t}(s,\cdot)||u(s,\cdot)|+c\sum_{\gamma<\beta}C_{\beta}^{\gamma}\int_{B_{\tau}}|\partial^{\gamma}u||\partial^{\beta-\gamma}\phi||\partial^{\alpha}(u\phi)|

\displaystyle+c\sum_{\tau<\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\tau}\int_{B_{\tau}}|\partial^{\beta}u||\partial^{\alpha-\tau}\phi||\partial^{\tau}(u\phi)|

\displaystyle:=M_{1}+M_{2}+M_{3}.

Exploiting in succession Young’s inequality, (
3.1
) and (
3.18
) we attain

(3.20)

\displaystyle M_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\int_{B_{\tau}}|u_{t}(s,\cdot)|^{2}\phi^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{B_{\tau}}|u(s,\cdot)|^{2}\phi^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{(\tau-\sigma)^{6m}}\int_{Q_{4r}}|u|^{2}+\frac{c}{\epsilon(\tau-\sigma)^{2m}}\int_{Q_{4r}}|u|^{2}.

For the term
M_{2}
, by interpolation inequalities and (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle M_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}}{2}\int_{B_{\tau}}|\nabla^{m}((u\phi)(s,\cdot))|^{2}+c\epsilon_{1}\int_{B_{\tau}}|\nabla^{m}u(s,\cdot)|^{2}+\frac{c(\epsilon_{1})}{(\tau-\sigma)^{2m}}\int_{B_{\tau}\cap{\rm supp}\phi}|u(s,\cdot)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}}{2}\int_{B_{\tau}}|\nabla^{m}((u\phi)(s,\cdot))|^{2}\phi^{2}+c\epsilon_{1}\int_{B_{\tau}}|\nabla^{m}u(s,\cdot)|^{2}+\frac{c(\epsilon_{1})}{(\tau-\sigma)^{4m}}\int_{Q_{4r}}|u|^{2}.

By the same token,

\displaystyle M_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}}{4}\int_{B_{\tau}}|\nabla^{m}u(s,\cdot)|^{2}+c\epsilon_{2}\int_{B_{\tau}}|\nabla^{m}((u\phi)(s,\cdot))|^{2}+\frac{c(\epsilon_{2})}{(\tau-\sigma)^{2m}}\int_{B_{\tau}\cap{\rm supp}\phi}|u(s,\cdot)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}}{4}\int_{B_{\tau}}|\nabla^{m}((u\phi)(s,\cdot))|^{2}+c\epsilon_{2}\int_{B_{\tau}}|\nabla^{m}u(s,\cdot)|^{2}+\frac{c(\epsilon_{2})}{(\tau-\sigma)^{4m}}\int_{Q_{4r}}|u|^{2}.

Summarizing the above inequalities and letting
\epsilon:=(\tau-\sigma)^{2m}\epsilon_{1}
in (
3.20
), we derive from (
3.19
) that

(3.21)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tau}}|\nabla^{m}(u\phi)|^{2}\leq c\epsilon_{1}\int_{B_{\tau}}|\nabla^{m}u(s,\cdot)|^{2}+c\epsilon_{2}\int_{B_{\tau}}|\nabla^{m}u(s,\cdot)|^{2}+\frac{c(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})}{(\tau-\sigma)^{4m}}\int_{Q_{4r}}|u|^{2}.

In (

t-\sigma^{2m}<s\leq t
and choosing
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},
small enough, we obtain

\sup_{t-\sigma^{2m}<s\leq t}\|\nabla^{m}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{\sigma}(x))}\leq\theta\sup_{t-\tau^{2m}<s\leq t}\|\nabla^{m}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{\tau}(x))}+\frac{c}{(\tau-\sigma)^{4m}}\int_{Q_{4r}}|u|^{2},

where
r\leq\sigma<\tau\leq 2r
and
0<\theta<1.
As a consequence, an application of the iteration lemma
[
17
, Lemma 5.1]
leads to

(3.22)

\sup_{t-r^{2m}<s\leq t}\|\nabla^{m}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{r}(x))}\leq c\frac{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{4r})}}{r^{4m}}.

Then, a well-known covering argument yields (
3.16
) when
j=m.
Recall that (
3.1
) implies

(3.23)

\sup_{t-r^{2m}<s\leq t}\|u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{r}(x))}\lesssim\frac{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{2r})}}{r^{2m}}.

Connecting (
3.22
) and (
3.23
), also using interpolation inequalities, we eventually arrive at

\displaystyle\sup_{t-r^{2m}<s\leq t}\|\nabla^{j}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{r}(x))}

\displaystyle\leq c\epsilon^{-\frac{j}{m-j}}\sup_{t-r^{2m}<s\leq t}\|u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{r}(x))}

\displaystyle+c\epsilon\sup_{t-r^{2m}<s\leq t}\|\nabla^{m}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{r}(x))}

\displaystyle\leq c\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{2r})}\quad(\frac{\epsilon^{-\frac{j}{m-j}}}{r^{2m}}+\frac{\epsilon}{r^{4m}})

\displaystyle\leq c\frac{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{2r})}}{r^{2m+2j}}\quad(\mbox{$\epsilon:=r^{2(m-j)}$}).

This ends the proof.

Lemma 3.5

Lemma 3.6
.

4.
The Hölder property from elliptic equations to prabolic equations

With the preparatory work from Section 3 in place, we are ready to prove our main results. Our first theorem is formulated as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{E}_{2m}(c_{1},0,c_{2})
satisfies the Hölder property
H(\mu,\infty)
given in Defnition
2.1
, then the parabolic operator
\mathcal{H}
with
t
-independent coefficients also satisfies the Hölder property
H(\mu_{0},\infty)
for some
\mu_{0}<\mu.
The same statements hold for
\mathcal{L}^{*}
and
\mathcal{H}^{*}.

Proof.
Given any
(t_{0},x_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
R>0.
Let
u
be a weak solution of
\mathcal{H}
in
Q_{R}:=[t_{0}-R^{2m},t_{0}]\times B_{R}(x_{0}).
Define
\tilde{u}(t,x):=u(t_{0}+R^{2m}t,x_{0}+Rx)
and
\tilde{a}_{\alpha,\beta}(x)=\tilde{a}_{\alpha,\beta}(x_{0}+Rx).
It is not hard to show that
\tilde{u}(t,x)
is a solution of
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
in
Q_{4}(0,0),
where

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}:=\partial_{t}+\tilde{\mathcal{L}}:=\partial_{t}+\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}(-1)^{|\alpha|}\partial^{\alpha}(\tilde{a}_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\partial^{\beta}).

Concerning the new elliptic operator
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
, it has the Hölder property
H(\mu,\infty)
if
\mathcal{L}
has the Hölder property
H(\mu,\infty),
see
[
7
]
. Moreover, the elliptic constants
c_{1},c_{2}
remain the same for
\tilde{a}_{\alpha,\beta}(x).
Thus, in the sequel, we can assume
R=4,t_{0}=0,x_{0}=0,
otherwise we consider the parabolic operator
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}.

Write

\mathcal{L}u=-u_{t}\quad\mbox{in}\;Q_{4}:=Q_{4}(0,0).

From (
3.17
), it follows that, for all
-2^{2m}\leq s\leq 0,

(4.1)

\|u_{t}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})}\leq c\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{4})}.

The inequality (
4.1
) enables us to apply Lemma
3.6
with

and
\lambda=0
(

) to find a constant

0<\varkappa:=\inf\{2m,n-2m+2\mu-\epsilon\}<n

such that for all

and

(4.2)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\nabla^{m}u(s,\cdot)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq cr^{\varkappa}\left(\int_{B_{2}}|\nabla^{m}u(s,\cdot)|^{2}+\|u_{t}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{2})}\right)

where in the last step we used (
3.16
). Then, by (
4.2
) and Lemma
3.4
, we have that, for all
(t,x)\in Q_{1},

0<r\leq 1
and
0\leq|\gamma|\leq l,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\int_{t-r^{2m}}^{t}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|D^{\gamma}u-D^{\gamma}P|^{2}dyds

\displaystyle\leq cr^{2(m-|\gamma|)}\int_{t-r^{2m}}^{t}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\nabla^{m}u(s,y)|^{2}dyds

\displaystyle\leq cr^{2m+2(m-|k|)+\varkappa}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{4})}.

If
n\leq 2m,
since
\mu=l+\nu_{0},
then we can write
\varkappa+2(m-|\gamma|)=n+2\nu_{0}
by choosing
\epsilon
small (for example,
\epsilon<\inf\{n-2m+2\mu,2l-2|\gamma|,\nu\}
) when necessary. Consequently, (
4.3
) reads

(4.4)

\displaystyle\int_{t-r^{2m}}^{t}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|D^{\gamma}u-D^{\gamma}P|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq cr^{2m+n+2\nu_{0}}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{4})},

where
0<\nu_{0}<\nu<1,
for all
(t,x)\in Q_{1}
and

3.5
leads to

(4.5)

By (
4.5
) and the argument at the beginning (translations and dilatations), we conclude that for any
(t_{0},x_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
R>0,
if
u
is a weak solution of
\mathcal{H}
in
Q_{4R}:=[t_{0}-(4R)^{2m},t_{0}]\times B_{R}(x_{0}),

In the case
2m+1\leq n\leq 2m+2l+1,
we argue as follows. For clarity, we now suppose that
u
is a solution of
\mathcal{H}
in
Q_{8}(0,0).

(4.6)

\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\nabla^{m}u_{t}(s,\cdot)|^{2}\leq cr^{\varkappa}\|u_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{4}(t_{0},x_{0}))}\quad\mbox{uniformly in}\;s\in[t_{0}-2^{2m},t_{0}].

\displaystyle\leq c\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{8}(0,0))}.

\|D^{\gamma}(\kappa+\mathcal{L}^{\phi})^{-1}f\|_{\infty}\leq C\|f\|_{2}\quad\forall\;|\gamma|\leq l

with
C
depending only on
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},n,m.
By the semigroup property,

t\|\frac{d}{dt}e^{-t(\mathcal{L}^{\phi}+\kappa)}f\|_{2}\leq C\|f\|_{2},\;\;\forall\;t>0,

where the constant
C
depends only on
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}.
Write

e^{-t(\mathcal{L}^{\phi}+\kappa)}=(\mathcal{L}^{\phi}+\kappa)^{-1}(-1)\frac{d}{dt}e^{-t(\mathcal{L}^{\phi}+\kappa)}.

Employing (
5.19
) we derive

(5.20)

\|D^{\gamma}e^{-t\mathcal{L}^{\phi}}f\|_{\infty}\leq Ct^{-1}e^{t\kappa}\|f\|_{2}\quad\forall\;t>0.

For any fixed
\epsilon\geq c,

L^{\epsilon\phi}\in\mathcal{E}_{2m}(c_{1},0,c_{2},c_{3}\epsilon^{2m})
thanks to
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{E}_{2m}(c_{1},0,c_{2}).
Set
s=\epsilon^{-1}.
Then, by scaling,
(L^{\epsilon\phi})^{s}=s^{2m}T^{-1}L^{\epsilon\phi}T\in\mathcal{E}_{2m}(c_{1},0,c_{2},c_{3})
and
e^{-L^{\epsilon\phi}}=Te^{-s^{-2m}(L^{\epsilon\phi})^{s}}T^{-1}.
Consequently, applying (
5.20
) with
\mathcal{L},t
replaced by
(L^{\epsilon\phi})^{s},s^{-2m},
respectively, we arrive at

(5.21)

\displaystyle\|D^{\gamma}e^{-L^{\epsilon\phi}}f\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\|D^{\gamma}(Te^{-s^{-2m}(L^{\epsilon\phi})^{s}}T^{-1})f\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=s^{-|\gamma|}\|D^{\gamma}e^{-s^{-2m}(L^{\epsilon\phi})^{s}}T^{-1}f\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq Cs^{-|\gamma|}s^{2m}e^{\kappa s^{-2m}}\|T^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Cs^{-|\gamma|}s^{2m}e^{\kappa s^{-2m}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}s^{-\frac{n}{2}}\leq Ce^{a\epsilon^{2m}}\epsilon^{|\gamma|+n/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Apparently, the above arguments apply for
(K_{t}^{\epsilon\phi})^{*}:=e^{\epsilon\phi}e^{-t\mathcal{L}^{*}}e^{-\epsilon\phi}.
So, (
5.21
) holds for
e^{-(L^{\epsilon\phi})^{*}}=e^{\epsilon\phi}e^{-\mathcal{L}^{*}}e^{-\epsilon\phi}
when
\gamma=0,
which implies

\|e^{-L^{\epsilon\phi}}f\|_{2}\leq Ce^{a\epsilon^{2m}}\epsilon^{n/2}\|f\|_{1}.

By this inequality, a duality argument and the semigroup property, we have

\|D^{\gamma}e^{-L^{\epsilon\phi}}f\|_{\infty}\leq Ce^{2a\epsilon^{2m}}\epsilon^{n+|\gamma|}\|f\|_{1},

which gives us (
5.16
).

Case 2: 2m=n.
In the critical case, the Sobolev imbedding of
H^{m}
into
C^{l,\nu}
fails. To address this difficulty we use
[
8
, Lemma 23 Chapter 1]
. Like the argument at the beginning of Case 1, for any
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{E}_{2m}(c_{1},\lambda_{0},c_{2},c_{3}),
there exists a
\theta\in(\omega,\frac{\pi}{2})
such that for any
z\in\Gamma_{\pi-\theta}
with
|z|=1,

(z+\mathcal{L}+\lambda)^{-1}:H^{-m}\to H^{m}\quad\mbox{ is bounded},

where
\lambda:=c_{1}/2+\lambda_{0}+c_{3}.
Obviously,
z+\mathcal{L}+\lambda
(
|z|=1
) can be extended to a bounded operator from
W^{m,p}
into
W^{-m,p}
for any
1<p<\infty.
An application of
[
8
, Lemma 23 Chapter 1]
implies that
z+\mathcal{L}+\lambda
is invertible from
W^{m,p}
onto
W^{-m,p}

for
p
in a neighborhood of
2
. Choosing
p>2
in this neighborhood so that
L^{2}\subset W^{-m,p},
then we can use the Sobolev imbedding (
m-\frac{n}{p}>0
) to deduce that

(z+\mathcal{L}+\lambda)^{-1}:L^{2}\to C^{l,\nu}

for
l=m-[\frac{n}{p}]-1
and
0<\nu<1.
Thus we return to the Case 1.

All in all, we obtain Gaussian estimates (
2.5
)-(
2.7
) for the heat kernel of
\mathcal{L}
when
2m\geq n
. Then, we can build the fundamental solution
\Gamma(t,x,s,y)
of
\mathcal{H}
by the same way in Theorem
5.1
. Clearly,
\Gamma(t,x,s,y)
verifies (
5.1
)-(
5.5
) with an integer
l
and some
0<\nu<1
. The proof is complete.

\Box

Readiness Brain Operation Optimization Training (ReBOOT) protocol: a feasibility randomised controlled trial for a cognitive prehabilitation programme for epilepsy surgery conducted at Cleveland Clinic, USA
Authors: Kayela Arrotta, Selma Babiker, Jeramiah Lake, Nicolas Thompson, Anthony Lioi, Imad Najm, Robyn M Busch
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionRisk of cognitive decline following epilepsy surgery can be a significant barrier for patients pursuing surgery, and post-surgical cognitive changes can impact quality of life (QOL), surgical satisfaction and functional independence. Readiness Brain Operation Optimization Training (ReBOOT) is a virtual cognitive prehabilitation intervention that provides patients with psychoeducation and cognitive strategies prior to surgery to increase pre-surgical preparedness and post-surgical functional independence in the circumstance that a patient experiences cognitive decline after surgery. The primary aim of this feasibility trial is to evaluate the acceptability, adherence and procedural feasibility of implementing ReBOOT in patients being evaluated for epilepsy surgery. Secondary, explorative objectives include examining preliminary trends in QOL, compensatory strategy use, cognitive function and psychosocial outcomes to inform a future definitive trial.Methods and analysisThis is a single-centre, parallel-group, feasibility randomised controlled trial of a standardised cognitive prehabilitation programme for patients who are considering epilepsy surgery. Participants are randomly assigned to intervention (n=32) or control groups (n=32). The intervention group is enrolled in ReBOOT, a virtual programme that includes two 1-hour individual sessions and four 1-hour group sessions. Feasibility outcomes include attendance, homework adherence, attrition and participant satisfaction for participants randomised to the intervention group. Exploratory analyses will use longitudinal linear mixed-effects models to describe trends in exploratory outcomes over time. Data will be used to refine procedures and estimate parameters (eg, effect sizes and variance) for a future fully powered trial.Ethics and disseminationCleveland Clinic Institutional Review Board approved the study protocol, which is publicly available and registered on the National Institutes of Health ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05992402) site. Results will be disseminated through conference presentations and academic publications, as well as shared with outside study sponsors (Society for Clinical Neuropsychology—Division 40 of the American Psychological Association; American Epilepsy Society).Trial registration numberNCT05992402.

Introduction

Background and rationale

Neurosurgery is considered an effective treatment option for pharmacoresistant epilepsy1; however, epilepsy surgery carries several risks, including the possibility of cognitive decline. Post-surgical cognitive changes are most common in dominant hemisphere temporal lobectomies, occurring in approximately 24%–60% of individuals2; however, cognitive changes have also been observed in frontal lobe resections, non-dominant temporal lobectomies and other types of epilepsy surgeries.24 Research has shown that post-surgical cognitive decline is associated with poorer long-term educational and vocational outcomes, reduced quality of life (QOL) and lower surgical satisfaction.1 5 6 The risk of cognitive decline may also serve as a significant barrier to patients considering surgery as a viable treatment option for their epilepsy.

We have recently developed a novel cognitive prehabilitation programme called Readiness Brain Operation Optimization Training (ReBOOT) that is designed to provide patients with psychoeducation and cognitive compensatory strategies prior to epilepsy surgery. Several small-sample studies have suggested that cognitive interventions can help improve objective cognitive function and QOL for patients with epilepsy.7 8 However, these studies are quite limited and have been used after cognitive decline has already occurred. Cognitive prehabilitation has been proposed as a pre-emptive intervention for patients considering epilepsy surgery,9 but, to date, there are neither standardised protocols, nor has there been any research on the efficacy of cognitive prehabilitation in patients with epilepsy. In other disease populations, early cognitive intervention has been shown to be essential, as patients are more likely to use cognitive compensatory strategies if they are in place before significant cognitive decline is experienced as a result of a disease process.9 10 The concept of cognitive prehabilitation has been successfully applied to other types of surgical populations, such as oncology patients11 and individuals at risk of post-operative delirium.12 Moreover, prehabilitation interventions for other lifestyle factors, such as nutrition, diet and mental health, have been proven to be useful prior to bariatric and cardiac surgery.1315 These studies highlight the general usefulness and effectiveness of prehabilitation interventions across a variety of lifestyle factors and disease states.

This will be the first study to understand the feasibility of a cognitive prehabilitation programme for patients with epilepsy. The ReBOOT programme was designed using existing evidence-based cognitive rehabilitation principles and strategies, and further refined based on patient feedback. ReBOOT uses an innovative hybrid model of individual and group sessions to offer both a personalised approach to individual patient needs while also incorporating support from peers, as well as decreasing the time demands on the intervention lead. Moreover, ReBOOT has been created as a standardised virtual intervention to provide easy accessibility for patients and seamless adoption of the programme to outside settings once efficacy is demonstrated. The ReBOOT programme has the potential to significantly improve patient care and experience prior to epilepsy surgery and help support functional independence in those patients who experience cognitive decline because of epilepsy surgery.

Objectives

The objective of this study is to assess the feasibility and acceptability of implementing ReBOOT in patients being evaluated for epilepsy surgery and to collect preliminary data to inform a future definitive trial. The first aim is to evaluate the feasibility of ReBOOT to ensure the practicality of a cognitive prehabilitation programme for epilepsy surgery. The second, exploratory aim is to gather preliminary data on potential effects of ReBOOT on QOL, compensatory strategy use, cognitive function and psychosocial outcomes to guide hypotheses and outcome selection for a future adequately powered efficacy trial.

Trial design

This is a prospective, randomised, controlled trial with parallel participant assignment into intervention and control groups.

Methods and analysis

The Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials and Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials reporting guidelines were used to include description of all pertinent protocol and feasibility trial details.16 17 Recruitment began in December 2023 and is expected to be completed by June 2028, although completion may occur earlier depending on enrolment rates.

Participants

Participants are being recruited from the Cleveland Clinic. Adults (aged 18–60 years) with a diagnosis of epilepsy who have been confirmed as appropriate surgical candidates will be considered for the study. Because this is a feasibility trial, a broad range of surgical candidates was included to reflect real-world clinical diversity and to inform stratification strategies for a future definitive trial. Patients must be able to independently provide informed consent, be fluent in English, have reading abilities at or above the 8th grade level (as assessed by the Wide Range Achievement Test-reading subtest), have Internet access with the ability to participate in online video conferencing, have no history of prior resective or ablative epilepsy surgery, and be willing and able to participate in a cognitive intervention. Exclusion criteria include any patient that has engaged in cognitive rehabilitation within the last 6 months, has ongoing litigation related to the cause of epilepsy (with the exception of short-term or long-term disability application), is undergoing intensive medical treatment for serious or life-threatening illness or has a serious psychiatric condition that would impact study participation, or has significant hearing and/or vision loss that would preclude them from engaging in virtual activities. Of note, because participation requires online video conferencing, individuals with limited digital resources may be under-represented and this is a recognised limitation; future definitive trials will benefit from additional funding to expand accessibility, such as providing equipment or clinic-based access options.

Participation is presented to patients by their healthcare provider, nurse or study personnel. Potential patients are identified if they have been recommended for epilepsy surgery following the Patient Management Conference (PMC), which is conducted after phase 1.5 testing. At the Cleveland Clinic, there is no standardised pre-surgical psychoeducational counselling provided by neuropsychologists or other epilepsy team members as part of routine care. Following PMC, the epileptologist discusses the potential risks and benefits of surgery with the patient and then refers the patient to neurosurgery for further consultation. On average, there is 5.6 months between PMC conferment and actual surgery date of the patient. Recruitment takes place either in person during a post-PMC clinical visit or via study flyer, US mail or MyChart message.

There are no greater than minimal risks associated with this study, and participants may withdraw from the study at any time.

Intervention

After a patient is identified and consents to the study, they undergo the initial screening to determine their eligibility to participate in the study. If the patient is deemed eligible, they complete baseline questionnaires. The investigator or her designee then randomises the patient into either the intervention or control group with 1:1 allocation via a computer-generated randomisation schedule.

Intervention arm

ReBOOT is a standardised virtual cognitive intervention that is led by the study principal investigator (PI), a clinical neuropsychologist with expertise in epilepsy and cognitive interventions. Neuropsychology post-doctoral fellows have also been trained in ReBOOT delivery, and to enhance reproducibility and scalability in the future, a detailed ReBOOT training manual is being developed. ReBOOT includes two individual sessions (programme orientation and wrap-up) and four weekly group sessions. The four weekly group sessions may be completed in any order, allowing participants to join ongoing groups rather than waiting for a new cohort to begin. This flexible structure enables the intervention lead to maintain a single, continuous group cycle rather than multiple parallel groups, which is critical given the limited time window between surgical decision-making and the scheduled surgery date. The goal is to have 3–5 patients in each group, and each session is of 1 hour. The group curriculum is outlined in figure 1. The intervention is largely psychoeducation-based and designed to provide patients with information about possible changes or challenges they may experience after epilepsy surgery, as well as cognitive strategies to implement prior to surgery to increase effectiveness of compensation for any new cognitive difficulties the patient may experience following surgery. Most adult epilepsy surgeries performed at the Cleveland Clinic are either temporal or frontal lobe resections; thus, the ReBOOT programme largely focuses on memory, language and executive function changes; however, information is generalisable to all areas of cognitive function. For example, the memory strategies covered include memory inefficiencies that result from processing speed deficits that are seen in a wide variety of epilepsy syndromes. Each week, patients are asked to set a Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Relevant and Time-bound (S.M.A.R.T.) goal to practise a new compensatory strategy to help facilitate behaviour change. Patients also have the option to be connected with a patient who has already undergone epilepsy surgery for additional support. There is also a booster session that occurs six weeks post-surgery.

Control arm

Participants in the control group receive usual care according to their epilepsy provider, as well as three 5 min phone calls over 6 weeks after initial study enrolment. These phone calls are not therapeutic in nature but provide control participants with brief engagement in study-related activities to ensure that any benefits seen between groups are not simply attributable to study contact.

Outcomes

Electronic medical records are reviewed to gather information about patient demographics and medical history. There are several factors, such as age and education, which can be associated with the effectiveness of cognitive interventions and are systematically recorded. We also code basic information about seizures (side, site, age of onset, duration, etc) and type of surgery (table 1).

Data analysis

Aim 1

To evaluate the feasibility and acceptability of implementing a cognitive prehabilitation programme.

Outcomes

The primary outcome of interest is intervention adherence, which is based on attendance and homework completion (ie, progress on S.M.A.R.T. goal). The programme will be considered feasible if at least 80% of participants attend at least 75% of the sessions AND make progress on their S.M.A.R.T. goal at least 50% of the time. Attrition in the programme will also be evaluated and defined as those who drop out of the programme entirely. Intervention participants also complete a satisfaction questionnaire regarding ReBOOT.

Specific Aim 1 analyses will follow the intent-to-treat principle and include all patients initially randomised to ReBOOT. Descriptive statistics will be used to summarise patient characteristics and understand the feasibility of the ReBOOT programme. The number of people who consented and were assigned to ReBOOT will be reported along with attrition rates and causes. General attendance and homework adherence will also be reported as count and percentage. A conservative attrition rate of 20% or lower is anticipated, although time from surgery decision to surgery date is anticipated to play a role. Predictors of attrition will be identified through univariate logistic regression models. Data on intervention satisfaction will also be reported.

Aim 2

To conduct exploratory analyses examining the potential effects of ReBOOT on patient outcomes.

Outcomes

The primary exploratory outcome is the Quality of Life in Epilepsy (QOLIE)-31 and secondary outcomes include compensatory strategy use (Everyday Compensation Scale), everyday functioning (Instrumental Activities of Daily Living-Compensation), subjective cognition (Memory Assessment Clinics Scale for Epilepsy), objective cognition (pre-surgical and post-surgical neuropsychological evaluation), self-efficacy (General Self-Efficacy Scale), locus of control (Multidimensional Health Locus of Control), depression (Patient Health Questionnaire-8), anxiety (Generalised Anxiety Disorder) and stress (Perceived Stress Scale-4), and surgery satisfaction (Epilepsy Surgery Satisfaction Questionnaire).

Specific Aim 2 analyses will follow the intent-to-treat principle, and all randomised participants will be included. Using descriptive statistics, participants in both groups (ReBOOT and control) will be compared on potentially confounding variables at baseline to assess imbalances between groups. Exploratory outcomes will be analysed at the end of the study between groups using a two-sided two-sample t-test. Additionally, a responder analysis will be conducted to determine the proportion of patients who attended ReBOOT who had a minimum clinically important difference (MCID) in QOLIE-31 (MCID has been defined as >11.82918).

For all exploratory outcomes, linear mixed-effects models will be constructed to describe changes within and between groups (intervention vs control) across time (baseline, post-intervention, 6 months and 1 year). A group-by-time interaction effect will be included to investigate the pattern of change over time between groups. If there are any clinically meaningful group differences in patient characteristics at baseline, they will be included in the mixed-effects models as covariates. Analyses will be interpreted as exploratory and hypothesis-generating, aimed at estimating variability and potential effect sizes to inform the design of a future definitive trial.

Sensitivity and subgroup analyses

The principal sensitivity analysis will be conducted based on a per-protocol analysis of patients, with treatment group defined as ReBOOT if patients completed at least 75% of their assigned sessions. Analyses will be conducted as described above for primary and exploratory outcomes. Subgroup analyses will be conducted to explore potential trends in patient characteristics that may influence engagement, acceptance or preliminary response patterns to ReBOOT (vs standard care). Mixed effects models as described above will be constructed with the inclusion of an interaction term (treatment group × characteristic) to identify possible subgroups who may differentially benefit from ReBOOT. Participant baseline characteristics to be considered include those in table 1. Interaction terms and effect estimates will be summarised with 95% CIs and interpreted descriptively to estimate potential effect sizes, not to test hypotheses.

Plans to address missing data

We attempt to minimise missing data throughout the study by reminding patients of visits and ensuring completeness of data in real time. Missing data will also be handled by the intention-to-treat principle, which reduces the impact of differential response in missing data since a patient is analysed by group after initiation of treatment, regardless of whether they complete the full study. Additionally, we will perform multiple imputations by chained equations for any missing data elements, which may include demographics, clinical characteristics or exploratory outcome measures, using the R package MICE (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).19 We will examine whether missing data are missing completely at random (MCAR), missing at random (MAR) or not MAR (NMAR). If missing data are MCAR or MAR, it is likely that the standard multivariable computations will not result in biased SE estimates. To assess the sensitivity to the assumption that data are MCAR or MAR, a number of plausible scenarios will be tested. If missing data are NMAR, we will consider the ‘pattern-mixture’ modelling approach to compute a weighted average of the parameters that are associated with the missing data to estimate what the data would have been. As this is a feasibility trial, these analyses will be conducted to evaluate data completeness, assess sources of missingness and refine data-handling procedures for a future definitive trial, rather than to test specific hypotheses.

Sample size

Approximately 300 adult patients every year complete phase 1.5 testing and are recommended for resective epilepsy surgery at the Cleveland Clinic. We expect to enrol 64 patients in this trial, with 32 patients randomised to ReBOOT over the course of 2–3 years. The power and sample size calculation for Aim 1 is based on our primary feasibility outcome of intervention adherence. We anticipate adherence will be >80% for ReBOOT, which is based on prior feasibility work and clinical experience within our group, and consistent with other behavioural interventions in epilepsy.20 21 A sample size of 32 participants in ReBOOT will produce a two-sided 95% CI with a width equal to 0.28 when the sample proportion is 80%. For Aim 2, exploratory analyses of clinical outcomes will be descriptive and hypothesis-generating. Assuming a conservative estimated attrition rate of 20%, we anticipate 26 patients will have follow-up data in each group (total n=52). These data will be used to estimate effect sizes and variability to inform power calculations for a future definitive randomised controlled trial, rather than to formally test group differences.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public were involved in the study design. A local, non-profit community organisation, Empowering Epilepsy, reviewed ReBOOT programme materials and provided feedback about recruitment strategies, time requirements and potential burden of the ReBOOT intervention, and considerations for outcome measures. This organisation also provides ongoing assistance with connecting intervention participants to patients who have had epilepsy surgery. Participants are not involved in plans for dissemination of the study results. Findings will be disseminated through publication in open access journals and shared with relevant professional and patient communities via institutional and community-based presentations and conferences.

Ethics and dissemination

The Cleveland Clinic Institutional Review Board has approved this study (Federalwide Assurance: FWA 00005367). Eligible patients are asked to sign informed consent (online supplemental consent form). To ensure confidentiality for patients participating in the study, identifiers are removed, and a unique number is assigned to each subject. Paper records are stored in locked filing cabinets in a designated office space, and access is limited to the study PI and designated research coordinators. Electronic data is maintained in a password protected REDCap database. This database has restricted access limited to the study PI and her designees, and personal health information will be flagged. Given the low-risk nature of this study, a data monitoring committee was not used.

See figure 3 for detailed study timeline and phases. Results from the study will be published in academic journals and shared with study sponsors. The sponsors can, in turn, share the results with the public. The protocol is publicly available and registered on the National Institutes of Health (NIH) ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05992402) site; issue date: 15 August 2023; current protocol version: 8 (see table 3 for trial registration data). Important protocol modifications have been communicated to the Institutional Review Board and the NIH trial registration has been updated accordingly. Primary protocol updates have included specification of outcome measures and participant eligibility, updated study sponsor information, and clarification of study description, design and arms. The sharing of anonymised participant-level data from the study will be considered based on a reasonable request from a qualified investigator for rigorous scientific inquiry.

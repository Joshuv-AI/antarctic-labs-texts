Patterson-Sullivan construction of equilibrium states and weighted counting in nonpositive curvature
Weisheng Wu

Abstract
. Consider the geodesic flow on a closed rank one manifold of nonpositive curvature. For certain Hölder continuous potential, there exists a unique equilibrium state by [ 13 ] . In this paper, we introduce the notions of core limit set, regular radial limit set and uniformly recurrent and regular vectors, and then construct a family of Patterson-Sullivan measures on the boundary at infinity in two separate settings. Then we give an explicit construction of the above unique equilibrium state using Patterson-Sullivan measures. This enables us to prove the Bernoulli property of the equilibrium states. Using the Patterson-Sullivan construction and mixing properties of equilibrium states, we count the number of free homotopy classes with weights in nonpositive curvature.



1.
Introduction

The study of geodesic flow lies in the crossing field of dynamical systems and differential geometry. The geodesic flow on closed manifolds of negative (sectional) curvature everywhere is a prime example of uniformly hyperbolic dynamical systems. Hopf
[
27
,
28
]
, Anosov and Sinai
[
1
,
2
]
proved the ergodicity of the geodesic flow with respect to the Liouville measure. On the other hand, the ergodic theory and thermodynamical formalism have deep applications in rigidity and counting problems in geometry. For instance, Margulis in his thesis
[
36
]
gave an explicit construction of measure of maximal entropy (MME for short) and Bowen
[
10
]
proved the uniqueness of MME for the geodesic flow in negative curvature. Nowdays the unique MME is called Bowen-Margulis measure, which exhibits nice mixing properties and local product structure with respect to the stable/unstable manifolds. Margulis
[
36
]
then derived an asymptotic formula for the growth of the number of closed geodesics:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\#P(t)/\frac{e^{ht}}{ht}=1

where
P(t)
is the set of closed geodesics with length no more than
t
,
h
is the topological entropy of the geodesic flow. Later, Parry and Pollicott
[
38
]
proved this formula using symbolic coding for the geodesic flow (and more generally for Axiom A flows). This formula is also called prime geodesic theorem since it resembles the formula in the prime number theorem.

The study of geodesic flow in nonpositive curvature is more delicate due to the existence of singular geodesics and flat strips. The well known higher rank rigidity theorem
[
5
,
15
,
16
]
says that a simply connected irreducible manifold of nonpositive curvature and rank greater than one must be isometric to a locally symmetric space of noncompact type. On the other hand, it is believed that the geodesic flow on a closed rank one manifold inherits most properties in negative curvature. Pesin established his theory of nonuniform hyperbolicity during the study of such geodesic flow (cf.
[
8
]
). Nevertheless, some problems in rank one case are much more difficult. For instance, the ergodicity of the geodesic flow with respect to the Liouville measure is widely open (cf.
[
8
,
53
,
55
]
).

It had been a long-standing problem to extending the uniqueness of MME and prime geodesic theorem from negative curvature to nonpositive curvature. The uniqueness of MME was conjectured by Burns and Katok
[
14
]
and finally proved by Knieper
[
32
]
. Knieper
[
32
]
built the MME via Patterson-Sullivan measures (cf.
[
40
,
48
]
) on the boundary at infinity. Knieper
[
31
]
also obtained lower and upper bounds on the number of free homotopy classes. Katok launched a program to prove the Margulis type asymptotics for the number of free homotopy classes (cf.
[
52
]
). Ricks
[
46
]
made the breakthrough and proved the Margulis type asymptotic formula for the number of free homotopy classes, for compact CAT(0) spaces which include closed rank one manifolds of nonpositive curvature. Further, Climenhaga, Knieper and War
[
20
,
21
]
established the uniqueness of MME and Margulis type asymptotic formula for certain closed manifolds without conjugate points. Wu
[
54
]
established the formula for closed rank one manifolds without focal points.

As a far reaching generalization of entropy, Ruelle
[
47
]
and Walters
[
49
]
introduced the notion of topological pressure to dynamical systems and established a variational principle for it. Pressure and equilibrium states constitute the main components of the thermodynamical formalism and play important roles in the study of the geodesic flow (cf.
[
39
]
). In negative curvature, the uniqueness of equilibrium states are again proved by Bowen
[
11
,
12
]
. In a monograph
[
41
]
, Paulin, Pollicott and Schapira obtained the uniqueness of equilibrium states, weighted prime geodesic theorem and many other results for certain noncompact manifolds of negative curvature. These results are based on the Patterson-Sullivan construction for equilibrium states.

In nonpositive curvature case, the first major development is the following theorem by Burns, Climenhaga, Fisher and Thompson.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
13
, Theorem A]
)
Let
{\mathcal{G}}=(g^{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
be the geodesic flow over a closed rank one manifold
M
and let
F:SM\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a Hölder continuous potential. If
P(\text{Sing},F)<P(F)
, then
F
has
a unique equilibrium state. This equilibrium state is hyperbolic, fully supported, and is the weak
∗
limit of weighted regular closed geodesics.

Call and Thompson
[
18
]
proved the Kolmogorov property (and hence mixing property) of the above equilibrium states. In
[
18
, Subsection 7.3]
, the authors discussed the power of the local product structure for equilibrium states. The local product structure of equilibrium states at the symbolic level is obtained by Araujo, Lima and Poletti
[
3
]
. In fact, Lima and Poletti
[
35
]
gave a new proof of Theorem
1.1
using symbolic dynamics. Recently, using Gibbs property of equilibrium states, Call, Constantine, Erchenko, Sawyer and Work
[
17
, Theorem D]
obtained the local product structure at the dynamical level for certain equilibrium states. As commented at the end of
[
18
]
, the geometric local product structure, that is the Patterson-Sullivan construction of equilibriums states, is not known.

We resolve the above problem in this paper. More precisely, Patterson-Sullivan construction of equilibrium states is given in nonpositive curvature in two separate settings. Then Bernoulli property of equilibrium states is obtained. As a highly nontrivial application, we establish equidistribution of closed geodesics and asymptotic inequalities for the number of weighted closed geodesics. We believe that more results in
[
41
]
could be extended to nonpositive curvature setting using our results in this paper.

1.1.
Statement of main results

Let
{\mathcal{G}}=(g^{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
be the geodesic flow over a closed rank one manifold
M
and
F:SM\to{\mathbb{R}}
a Hölder continuous potential. If
P(\text{Sing},F)<P(F)
, then
F
has
a unique equilibrium state by Theorem
1.1
. Let
\Gamma
be the fundamental group of
M
,
X
the universal cover of
M
and
{\partial X}
the boundary at infinity of
X
.

To construct Patterson-Sullivan measures on
{\partial X}
, we start with Poincaré series. From a dynamical point of view, since the unit sphere at a reference point is a submanifold in the unit tangent bundle, we are expecting that separated sets lying on this submanifold give the topological pressure of the geodesic flow. In negative curvature, this is guaranteed by bounded distortion (or so-called Bowen property) of a Hölder potential coming from uniform hyperbolicity. Furthermore, the definition of Gibbs cocycle relies on the bounded distortion. To bypass this obstruction, we establish bounded distortion along “uniformly regular” orbits. To do so, we introduce the following three types of “uniform regularity”:

•

Core limit set
\Lambda_{c}(\Gamma)
: the set of limit points in
{\partial X}
accumulated by good orbit segments, i.e., the core part
\mathcal{G}
in the
({\mathcal{P}},\mathcal{G},\mathcal{S})
-decomposition defined in
[
13
]
.

•

Regular radial limit set
\Lambda_{r}(\Gamma)
: the set of limit points in
{\partial X}
pointed by a geodesic ray starting at the reference point, and entering regular compact regions in
X
infinitely many times.

•

The set
\mathcal{URR}
of uniformly recurrent and regular vectors.

The core limit set originates from the
({\mathcal{P}},\mathcal{G},\mathcal{S})
-decomposition in
[
13
]
. Pliss time is essentially used to obtain bounded distortion in this case. The regular radial limit set is motivated by the notion of strongly positive recurrence (SPR for short) studied for example in
[
26
]
. Here we consider geodesic rays entering
regular
compact regions infinitely many times. The idea behind uniformly recurrent and regular vectors is from
[
7
]
. It plays some roles of Pesin sets, with the main difference that we have exponential decay of distance under the geodesic flow of any two points in the
global
stable manifolds, not just Pesin local stable manifolds. In some sense, these three notions characterize “uniformly regularity” in dynamical, geometric, and ergodic ways respectively.

The following proposition shows the relation between the critical exponent
\delta_{F}
of
\Gamma
relative to
F
, and the topological pressure
P(F)
of the geodesic flow on
M=X/\Gamma
. The use of uniformly recurrent and regular vectors is crucial in the proof.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
M
be a closed rank one manifold of nonpositive curvature and
X
its universal cover. Assume that
P(\text{Sing},F)<P(F)
. Then
P(F)=\delta_{F}
.

In order to construct a family of Patterson-Sullivan measures on
{\partial X}
, our strategy is to show that the complement of core limit set or regular radial limit set is null with respect to a measure constructed using reference point. At this step, we need impose one of the following two conditions:

•

Condition (A): There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma,n-1<d(o,\gamma o)\leq n}e^{\int_{o}^{\gamma o}F}\geq Ce^{n\delta_{F}},\quad\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

•

Condition (B):
F
is constant in a neighborhood of the singular set.

Condition (A) is fulfilled for
F\equiv 0
in nonpositive curvature
[
31
, Theorem A]
and for any Hölder potential
F
in negative curvature by
[
41
, Corollary 9.10]
. It is similar to the condition of purely exponential volume growth in
[
56
]
in the case that
F\equiv 0
for noncompact manifolds. Corollary E.1 below shows that it is also a necessary condition to Patterson-Sullivan construction.

Condition (B) is introduced and extensively studied in
[
13
]
. It implies the pressure gap
P(F,\text{Sing})<P(F)
by
[
13
, Theorem B]
. In
[
17
, Theorem D]
, it is proved that equilibrium states under Condition (B) have local product structure at the dynamical level.

We show that under Condition (A) the complement of core limit set has null measure, and under Condition (B) bounded distortion of the potential also holds with respect to the complement of regular radial limit set. This allows us to construct a family of measures on
{\partial X}
, which forms a
\delta_{F}
-dimensional Busemann density.

Theorem A
.

Let
M
be a closed rank one manifold of nonpositive curvature and
X
its universal cover. Suppose that
F:SM\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a Hölder continuous potential satisfying
P(\text{Sing},F)<P(F)
, and either Condition (A) or Condition (B) holds. Then there exists a family of
\delta_{F}
-dimensional Busemann density
\{\mu_{F,q}\}_{q\in X}
on
{\partial X}
, that is,

(1)

\mu_{F,\gamma q}(\gamma A)=\mu_{F,q}(A)
for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
and any Borel set
A\subset{\partial X}
;

(2)

\frac{d\mu_{F,q}}{d\mu_{F,p}}(\xi)=e^{-C_{F-\delta_{F},\xi}(q,p)}
for almost every
\xi\in{\partial X}
.

Since the Busemann cocycle
C_{F-\delta_{F},\xi}(q,p)
is defined almost everywhere, we can define a measure
\bar{\mu}_{F}
on
{\partial}^{2}X
, the set of pairs
(\xi,\eta)\in{\partial X}\times{\partial X}
which can be connected by a geodesic
c_{v}
, as follows.

d\bar{\mu}_{F}(\xi,\eta)=e^{C_{F\circ\iota-\delta_{F},\xi}(o,\pi(v))+C_{F-\delta_{F},\eta}(o,\pi(v))}d\mu_{F\circ\iota,o}(\xi)d\mu_{F,o}(\eta).

Here
\iota:SM\to SM,v\mapsto-v
is the flip map. It is not evident that the density above is bounded, so
\bar{\mu}_{F}
might not be a Radon measure. Our strategy is to restrict
\bar{\mu}_{F}
first to a subset with “uniformly bounded” density and then to a “uniformly regular” subset of
{\partial}^{2}X
to get Radon measures. Such a
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure induces a
g^{t}
-invariant probability measure
\mu_{F}
on
SM
.

Theorem B
.

Let
M
be a closed rank one manifold of nonpositive curvature,
X
its universal cover and
F:SM\to{\mathbb{R}}
a Hölder continuous potential satisfying
P(\text{Sing},F)<P(F)
. Assume that there exists a family of
\delta_{F}
-dimensional Busemann density
\{\mu_{F,q}\}_{q\in X}
on
{\partial X}
. Then

.

The main difficulty in the proof of Theorem

is the absence of classical shadow lemma for Busemann density

As a first application of Patterson-Sullivan construction of equilibrium states, we prove that the equilibrium states are Bernoulli. The result has been proved recently using symbolic coding
[
3
, Corollary 1.3]
. We provide a more direct and geometric proof using Ornstein-Weiss argument

37
]
.

Theorem C
.

Let
M
be a closed rank one manifold of nonpositive curvature,
X
its universal cover and
F:SM\to{\mathbb{R}}
a Hölder continuous potential satisfying
P(\text{Sing},F)<P(F)
. Assume that there exists a family of

In the proof of Theorem
E
, we encounter difficulties caused by “genuine” nonuniform hyperbolicity, not only from the dynamical structure, but also from distortion of the potential. We consider the rectangles formed by stable/unstable manifolds of “uniformly regular” points inside a geometric flow box. Using local product structure of the rectangle and mixing properties of equilibrium states, we calculate the number of intersections of the rectangle and its images under the geodesic flow. We need that these estimates are uniform with respect to the size of flow box, which can be achieved if the rectangle has a large percentage of measure inside the flow box. As Besicovitch covering lemma or Lebesgue density theorem does not apply directly to flow boxes, instead we construct a partition of “uniformly regular” set by flow boxes. In this way, we obtain a sequence of boxes with size shrinking to zero, inside which the rectangle accounts for a large proportion in measure.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we present some preliminaries on equilibrium states and geodesic flow in nonpositive curvature, and then introduce three types of “uniformly regular” points. We establish bounded distortion of the potential and define Busemann cocycles. In Section 3, Patterson-Sullivan measures on the boundary at infinity are constructed under Condition (A) and Condition (B) separately. This proves Theorem

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Equilibrium states in nonpositive curvature

Suppose that
(M,g)
is a
C^{\infty}
closed Riemannian manifold,
where
g
is a Riemannian metric of nonpositive curvature. Let
\pi:SM\to M
be the unit tangent bundle over
M
. For each
v\in SM
,
we always denote by
c_{v}:{\mathbb{R}}\to M
the unique geodesic on
M
satisfying the initial conditions
c_{v}(0)=\pi(v)
and
\dot{c}_{v}(0)=v
. The geodesic flow

is defined as:

g^{t}:SM\rightarrow SM,\qquad v\mapsto\dot{c}_{v}(t),\ \ \ \ \forall\ t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

2.1.1.
Pressure and equilibrium states

Let
d
denote the distance function on
M
induced by Riemannian metric. The Knieper metric
d_{K}
on
SM
is defined by

d_{K}(v,w):=\max\{d(c_{v}(t),c_{w}(t)):0\leq t\leq 1\}
. Then we define a dynamical metric on
SM
as

d_{t}(v,w):=\max_{0\leq s\leq t}d_{K}(g^{s}v,g^{s}w),\quad\forall v,w\in SM.

Given
\epsilon>0
and
t>0
, the
t
-th
Bowen ball
centered at
v\in SM
is defined as

B_{t}(v,\epsilon):=\{w\in SM:d_{t}(v,w)<\epsilon\}.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset SM\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{t}:=\{v\in SM:(v,t)\in{\mathcal{C}}\}
.
E\subset\mathcal{C}_{t}
is
(t,\epsilon)
-separated
, if for any
(v,t),(w,t)\in E
with
v\neq w
, one has
d_{t}(v,w)>\epsilon
.

A potential
F\in C(SM,\mathbb{R})
is a continuous real function on
SM
. Given a scale
\epsilon>0
and time
t>0
, the representative information of
F
over a
t
-th Bowen ball centered at
v
is given by

\mathcal{F}_{\epsilon}(v,t):=\sup_{w\in B_{t}(v,\epsilon)}\int_{0}^{t}F(g^{s}w)ds.

We also write
\mathcal{F}(v,t):=\mathcal{F}_{0}(v,t)
. The notation with parameter
\epsilon
follows from
[
22
]
and will be used in Subsection 3.2.

Definition 2.1
.

Given
\mathcal{C}\subset SM\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
t,\delta,\epsilon>0
, the (separated)
partition function
of
F
is defined by

\Lambda_{t}(\mathcal{C},F,\delta,\epsilon):=\sup\Big\{\sum_{(v,t)\in E}e^{\mathcal{F}_{\epsilon}(v,t)}:E\subset\mathcal{C}_{t}\text{ is }(t,\delta)\text{-separated}\Big\}.

Then the
pressure
of

\mathcal{C}
at scale
(\delta,\epsilon)
is defined by

P(\mathcal{C},F,\delta,\epsilon)=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\Lambda_{t}(\mathcal{C},F,\delta,\epsilon).

When
\epsilon=0
, we simply write
\Lambda_{t}(\mathcal{C},F,\delta):=\Lambda_{t}(\mathcal{C},F,\delta,0)
and
P(\mathcal{C},F,\delta):=P(\mathcal{C},F,\delta,0)
. Then the
pressure
of
F
over
\mathcal{C}
is defined as

P(\mathcal{C},F)=\lim_{\delta\to 0}P(\mathcal{C},F,\delta).

Denote
P(Z,F):=P(Z\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+},F)
for any
Z\subset SM
. Write
P(F,\delta):=P(SM\times\mathbb{R}^{+},F,\delta)
, and the

of

P(F):=\lim_{\delta\to 0}P(F,\delta).

Theorem 2.2
.

(Variational principle for Pressure,
[
25
, Theorem 4.3.7]
)
Let

. Then

\displaystyle P(F)

\displaystyle=\sup\Big\{h_{\mu}(g^{1})+\int Fd{\mu}:\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{{\mathcal{G}}}(SM)\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sup\Big\{h_{\mu}(g^{1})+\int Fd{\mu}:\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{{\mathcal{G}}}^{e}(SM)\Big\}

\mathcal{M}_{{\mathcal{G}}}(SM)

A measure

An equilibrium state of

2.1.2.

We call two geodesics

Denote

For each pair of points

Let
\mathcal{F}

Definition 2.5

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\eta)

\displaystyle:=\Big\{(v,t):\int_{0}^{\tau}\lambda(g^{s}v)ds\geq\eta\tau,\ \int_{0}^{\tau}\lambda(g^{-s}g^{t}v)ds\geq\eta\tau,\ \forall\tau\in[0,t]\Big\},

\displaystyle:=\Big\{(v,t):\int_{0}^{t}\lambda(g^{s}v)ds<\eta t\Big\}.

Definition 2.6
.

(
[

, p. 1221]
)
Given

, take

to be the largest time such that

, and

2.3.
Core limit set

Given
p,q\in X
, we also write
(\dot{c}_{p,q}(0),d(p,q))\in SX\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
as the geodesic segment
[p,q]
for simplicity.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the set of geodesic segments
[o,\gamma o]
from

to
\gamma o
where

Lemma 2.10
.

Suppose that

for some

, such that
\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\lambda(g^{s}v)ds\geq\eta(t-T_{0})

.

Proof.

Since
\xi\in\Lambda_{c}^{L}(\Gamma)
, there exist
\gamma_{n}\in\Gamma
such that

and

. Since

uniformly for

.

On the other hand, since

for

The following Pliss lemma

(

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
v=\dot{c}_{o,\xi}(0)
where
\xi\in\Lambda_{c}^{L}(\Gamma)
. If

, then
\lim_{t\to+\infty}d^{s}(\dot{c}_{p,\xi}(t),\dot{c}_{o,\xi}(t))=0

Proof.

Assume not. Take a shortest curve
\beta:[0,1]\to W^{s}(v)
with
\beta(0)=v
and
\beta(1)=\dot{c}_{p,\xi}(0)
. Define
\beta(s,t)=g^{t}(\beta(s))
for every

and

as

, where

. In other words, for any

\delta
is from (
1

Lemma 2.13
.

Let

where
\xi\in\Lambda_{c}^{L}(\Gamma)

independent of

.

Take
T_{3}=b_{\xi}(p,o)
, so that

. For simplicity we assume
T_{3}=0
below and therefore

. By Lemma

where
\delta
is from (

2.5.

For a regular vector

Definition 2.16

2.6.
Gibbs cocycles and Bounded distortion

Now consider a Hölder continuous function
F:SM\to{\mathbb{R}}
with Hölder exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)
and Hölder constant
K
, that is

K=\sup_{v,w\in SM,v\neq w}\frac{|F(v)-F(w)|}{d_{K}^{\alpha}(v,w)}.

Denote
\|F\|=\max_{v\in SM}|F(v)|
.
We lift
F
to the universal cover
X
, still denoted by
F
.

Definition 2.24
.

Let
\xi\in{\partial X}
. We define the
Gibbs cocycle
for any
p,q\in X
as

C_{F,\xi}(q,p):=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\int_{p}^{c_{p,\xi}(t)}F-\int_{q}^{c_{q,\xi}(t+s)}F

where
s=b_{\xi}(q,p)
, whenever the limit exists.

It is clear that Gibbs cocycles satisfy the following properties whenever they are well defined.

Lemma 2.25
.

(1)

For all
\gamma\in\Gamma
,
C_{F,\xi}(q,p)=C_{F,\gamma\xi}(\gamma q,\gamma p)
.

(2)

C_{F,\xi}(q,p)+C_{F,\xi}(p,r)=C_{F,\xi}(q,r)
.

(3)

If
c_{q,p}(+\infty)=\xi
, then
C_{F,\xi}(q,p)=-\int_{q}^{p}F
.

The following lemmas are often referred as “bounded distortion” property, which is key ingredient to prove the existence of Gibbs cocycles.

2.6.1.
Uniformly recurrent and regular vectors

Lemma 2.26
.

Let
v\in\mathcal{UR}(\lambda)
for some
\lambda>0
,
p\in X
,
T_{1}=b_{v^{+}}(p,\pi v)
and
w=g^{T_{1}}\dot{c}_{p,v^{+}}(0)\in W^{s}(v)
. Then for any
T>0
, we have

\left|\int_{p}^{\pi g^{T}v}F-\int_{\pi v}^{\pi g^{T}v}F\right|\leq C_{1}d^{s}(v,w)^{\alpha}e^{d^{s}(v,w)\alpha/\lambda}/\lambda+\|F\|d(p,\pi v)

for some constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(K,\alpha)
. Moreover,

\left|\int_{p}^{\pi g^{T}w}F-\int_{\pi v}^{\pi g^{T}v}F\right|\leq C_{2}d^{s}(v,w)^{\alpha}e^{d^{s}(v,w)\alpha/\lambda}/\lambda+\|F\|d(p,\pi v)

for some constant
C_{2}=C_{2}(K,\alpha)

Proof.

Denote
w_{T}=\dot{c}_{p,\pi g^{T}v}(0)
and

It follows by Lemma

\displaystyle\left|\int_{p}^{\pi g^{T}v}F-\int_{\pi v}^{\pi g^{T}v}F\right|\leq\left|\int_{\pi g^{S-T}w_{T}}^{\pi g^{T}v}F-\int_{\pi v}^{\pi g^{T}v}F\right|+\left|\int_{p}^{\pi g^{S-T}w_{T}}F\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K(C_{0}d^{s}(v,w))^{\alpha}e^{d^{s}(v,w)\alpha/\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda\alpha s}ds+\|F\|d(p,\pi v)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{KC_{0}^{\alpha}}{\lambda\alpha}\cdot d^{s}(v,w)^{\alpha}e^{d^{s}(v,w)\alpha/\lambda}+\|F\|d(p,\pi v)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle C_{1}d^{s}(v,w)^{\alpha}e^{d^{s}(v,w)\alpha/\lambda}/\lambda+\|F\|d(p,\pi v).

where

It follows by Proposition

\displaystyle\left|\int_{p}^{\pi g^{T}w}F-\int_{\pi v}^{\pi g^{T}v}F\right|\leq\left|\int_{\pi w}^{\pi g^{T}w}F-\int_{\pi v}^{\pi g^{T}v}F\right|+\left|\int_{p}^{\pi w}F\right|

\displaystyle KC^{\alpha}d^{s}(w,w)^{\alpha}e^{d^{s}(v,w)\alpha/\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda\alpha s}ds+\|F\|d(p,\pi v)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{KC^{\alpha}}{\lambda\alpha}\cdot d^{s}(v,w)^{\alpha}e^{d^{s}(v,w)\alpha/\lambda}+\|F\|d(p,\pi v)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle C_{2}d^{s}(v,w)^{\alpha}e^{d^{s}(v,w)\alpha/\lambda}/\lambda+\|F\|d(p,\pi v)

where
C_{2}:=\frac{KC^{\alpha}}{\alpha}
. The lemma follows.

The proof is identical to that of

4.2.
Ergodicity

To prove the ergodicity of

Let

a(-\infty)

{\partial X}

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(U,V):=

\displaystyle\{\text{geodesic\ }c:(c(-\infty),c(+\infty))\in(\mathcal{B}^{\lambda_{0}}\cap U)\times(\mathcal{B}^{\lambda_{0}}\cap V)\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(U,V):=

\displaystyle\{\dot{c}(t):~c\in\mathcal{G}(U,V),~t\in\mathbb{R}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{rec}(U,V):=

\displaystyle\{c:c\in\mathcal{G}(U,V),~\mbox{and}~\dot{c}(0)~\mbox{is recurrent}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{rec}(U,V):=

\displaystyle\{\dot{c}(t):c\in\mathcal{G}_{rec}(U,V),~t\in\mathbb{R}\}.

By construction,

\mu_{F}(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(U,V)\setminus\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{rec}(U,V))=0

Let

\Gamma
-invariant function.
By Birkhoff ergodic theorem,
for
\mu_{F}\text{-a.e.}\ v\in SX

Obviously,

So we can write

for every geodesic

Denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}_{rec}(U,V):=

\displaystyle\{c:c\in\mathcal{G}_{rec}(U,V),~f^{+}(c)=f^{-}(c)\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}^{\prime}_{rec}(U,V):=

\displaystyle\{\dot{c}(t):c\in\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}_{rec}(U,V),~t\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Therefore we have
\mu_{F}(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(U,V)\setminus\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}^{\prime}_{rec}(U,V))=0.

The following lemma is based on a Fubini type argument.

Lemma 4.2
.

There exists a geodesic
c_{1}\in\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}_{rec}(U,V)

c_{1}(-\infty)=\xi_{1}\in\mathcal{B}^{\lambda_{0}}\cap U

\mu_{F,o}(G_{\xi_{1}})=\mu_{F,o}(\mathcal{B}^{\lambda_{0}}\cap V)

Let
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{G}_{rec}(U,V)\setminus\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}_{rec}(U,V),
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\{\dot{c}(t):c\in\mathcal{E},~t\in\mathbb{R}\}

\mu_{F}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})=0

Choosing

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}_{F,\lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})=\int_{P(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})}e^{C_{F\circ\iota-\delta_{F},\xi}(o,\pi(v))+C_{F-\delta_{F},\eta}(o,\pi(v))}d\mu_{F\circ\iota,o}(\xi)d\mu_{F,o}(\eta)=0.

The following lemma is based on a Hopf type argument.

Lemma 4.3
.

f^{+}(c)=f^{+}(c_{1})
for almost all
c\in\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}_{rec}(U,V)

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
, there exists a geodesic

5.
Bernoulli property of equilibrium states

In this section, we provide a proof of Theorem
C
, that is, the unique equilibrium state
\mu_{F}
is Bernoulli. This will be done based on the classic argument showing that the Kolmogorov property implies Bernoulli property for smooth invariant measures of hyperbolic systems. In
[
18
]
, Call and Thompson showed that
\mu_{F}
has the Kolmogorov property. They also showed the unique MME is Bernoulli by utilizing the Patterson-Sullivan construction of the MME. The key progress here is that the above Pattterson-Sullivan construction of
\mu_{F}
provides the local product structure we need.

The argument was carried out by Chernov and Haskell
[
19
]
for smooth invariant measures of suspension flows over some nonuniformly hyperbolic maps with sigularities. The argument is also true for hyperbolic invariant measures with local product structure. We follow the lines in
[
18
, Section 7]
.

In
[
19
]
, if an invariant measure
\mu
has the Kolmogorov property and there exists an
\epsilon
-regular covering
with non-atomic conditionals for
\mu
for any
\epsilon>0
, then any finite partition
\xi
of the
phase space with piecewise smooth boundary and a constant
C>0
such that

\mu(B(\partial\xi,\delta))\leq C\delta
for all
\delta>0
is
Very Weak Bernoulli
. A refining sequence of such
partitions with diameter going to
0
suffices to conclude the Bernoulli property for

\mu
. Such a sequence of partitions exists in this setting by
[
37
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Thus in our case, to conclude that
\mu_{F}
is Bernoulli, we only need to show that
\epsilon
-regular coverings for
\mu_{F}
exist for all
\epsilon>0
. First, let us give Chernov and Haskell’s definition of
\epsilon
-regular covering.

Definition 5.1
.

A

in
SM
is a measurable set

, equipped with a distinguished point

The following notations in the asymptotic estimates are standard.

\displaystyle f(t)=a^{\pm C}g(t)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow a^{-C}g(t)\leq f(t)\leq a^{C}g(t)\text{\ for all\ }t;

\displaystyle f(t)\lesssim g(t)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{f(t)}{g(t)}\leq 1;

\displaystyle f(t)\gtrsim g(t)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{f(t)}{g(t)}\geq 1;

\displaystyle f(t)\sim g(t)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{f(t)}{g(t)}=1;

\displaystyle f(t)\sim a^{\pm C}g(t)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow a^{-C}g(t)\lesssim f(t)\lesssim a^{C}g(t).

C_{B}:=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mu_{F}(\tilde{\underline{B}}_{\Lambda}^{\epsilon})}{\nu_{F,t}(\tilde{\underline{B}}_{\Lambda}^{\epsilon})},

Q>0
is a constant independent of
\epsilon
, and
\alpha
is the Hölder constant of
F
.

Proof.

By Propositions

and
6.10

\displaystyle\sum_{c\in C(t)}e^{\mathrm{Per}_{F}(c)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\Lambda}(t,\epsilon)}e^{\mathrm{Per}_{F}(\gamma)}}{t\nu_{F,t}(\tilde{\underline{B}}^{\epsilon}_{\Lambda})}\lesssim C_{B}\cdot\frac{\epsilon\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\Lambda}(t,\epsilon)}e^{\mathrm{Per}_{F}(\gamma)}}{t\mu_{F}(\tilde{B}^{\epsilon}_{\Lambda})}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{B}e^{L(\epsilon,\lambda)}(1+\rho_{0})^{2}(1+4\epsilon)\frac{\epsilon}{t}e^{\delta_{F}t}.

On the other hand, consider
\alpha=\epsilon-4\epsilon^{2}
. By Theorem

and (

\nu_{F,t}({\underline{B}}^{\alpha})\to\mu_{F}({\underline{B}}^{\alpha})=\mu_{F}(B^{\alpha})

\displaystyle\sum_{c\in C(t)}e^{\mathrm{Per}_{F}(c)}\gtrsim\frac{\alpha}{t\nu_{F,t}({\underline{B}}_{\theta}^{\alpha})}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}_{\Lambda}(t-2\epsilon^{2},\alpha)}e^{\mathrm{Per}_{F}(\gamma)}

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{t\mu_{F}(B_{\theta}^{\alpha})}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}_{\Lambda}(t-2\epsilon^{2},\alpha)}e^{\mathrm{Per}_{F}(\gamma)}\gtrsim\frac{e^{-L(\epsilon,\lambda)}}{(1+\rho_{0})^{2}}(1-4\epsilon)\frac{\epsilon}{t}e^{-2|\delta_{F}|\epsilon^{2}}e^{\delta_{F}t}.

By Remark

is small enough, there exists a constant

independent of

∎

To finish the proof of Theorem

satisfying (

(see for example Corollary

,
\theta_{n}\to 0
as well. The conditions in the definition of flow boxes can be relaxed as follows. Let
\epsilon>0
be sufficiently small,
\theta=\theta(\epsilon)
, and

. Let
P
and

be small compact neighborhoods of

and

respectively satisfying
\textbf{P}\subset\textbf{P}_{\theta}
and
\textbf{F}\subset\textbf{F}_{\theta}

with size
\epsilon

Without loss of generality, we may suppose that
\Lambda

whose complement in

Lemma 6.17

For any sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, there exist disjoint (upto a set of zero

-measure) generalized flow boxes

, such that
\Lambda=\cup_{i=1}^{m}(B_{i}\cap\Lambda)

Proof.

Fix
\epsilon>0
. Since
\Lambda
is compact, there exist finitely many flow boxes
B_{\theta}^{\epsilon}(v_{i})
centered at
v_{i}\in\Lambda,i=1,\cdots,k
such that
\Lambda\subset\cup_{i=1}^{k}B^{\epsilon}_{\theta}(v_{i})
. We can assume that these flow boxes satisfy all the properties in Subsection 5.1, but not (
28
) as a priori.

In spirit of
[
8
, Lemma 9.5.6]
, we claim that for any two flow boxes, say
B^{\epsilon}_{\theta}(v_{1})=H(\textbf{P}_{1}\times\textbf{F}_{1}\times[0,\epsilon])
and

such that
\Lambda\cap(\cup_{i=1}^{l}B_{i})=\Lambda\cap(B^{\epsilon}_{\theta}(v_{1})\cup B^{\epsilon}_{\theta}(v_{2}))

To prove the claim, it is sufficient to consider case
B^{\epsilon}_{\theta}(v_{1})\cap B^{\epsilon}_{\theta}(v_{2})\neq\emptyset
. We divide
\textbf{P}_{1}\cup\textbf{P}_{2}
into three parts:
\textbf{P}_{1}\cap\textbf{P}_{2},\textbf{P}_{1}\setminus\textbf{P}_{2},\textbf{P}_{2}\setminus\textbf{P}_{1}
. Similarly, divide
\textbf{F}_{1}\cup\textbf{F}_{2}
into three parts:
\textbf{F}_{1}\cap\textbf{F}_{2},\textbf{F}_{1}\setminus\textbf{F}_{2},\textbf{F}_{2}\setminus\textbf{F}_{1}
. These are connected relatively compact neighborhoods in
{\partial X}
. Choose a pair of them, denoted by
\textbf{P}^{\prime},\textbf{F}^{\prime}
. It is possible that in the flow direction there are nontrivial intersections, i.e.,
P^{-1}(\textbf{P}^{\prime}\times\textbf{F}^{\prime})\cap B^{\epsilon}_{\theta}(v_{1})\cap B^{\epsilon}_{\theta}(v_{2})\neq\emptyset
. If so, we then cut
P^{-1}(\textbf{P}^{\prime}\times\textbf{F}^{\prime})\cap(B^{\epsilon}_{\theta}(v_{1})\cup B^{\epsilon}_{\theta}(v_{2}))
into three parts:
P^{-1}(\textbf{P}^{\prime}\times\textbf{F}^{\prime})\cap(B^{\epsilon}_{\theta}(v_{1})\cap B^{\epsilon}_{\theta}(v_{2}))

P^{-1}(\textbf{P}^{\prime}\times\textbf{F}^{\prime})\cap(B^{\epsilon}_{\theta}(v_{1})\setminus B^{\epsilon}_{\theta}(v_{2}))
and

27

).

There is still a minor issue. It is possible that these are not genuine generalized flow boxes, since the length
\epsilon^{\prime}
in the flow direction could be small relative to the size
\theta^{\prime}
of
\textbf{P}^{\prime}
and
\textbf{F}^{\prime}

\textbf{P}^{\prime}
and
\textbf{F}^{\prime}
into finitely many small ones such that their sizes are smaller than
\theta(\epsilon^{\prime})
. Then we get genuine generalized flow boxes satisfying the claim, by dropping those with no intersection with
\Lambda
.

Applying the claim to each pair of
\{B^{\epsilon}_{\theta}(v_{i})\}_{i=1}^{k}

as required in the lemma.
∎

For any

for any compact neighborhood is guaranteed by

.

Proof of Theorem
E
.

The last step of the proof of Theorem
E
is to estimate
\sum_{c\in P(t)}e^{\mathrm{Per}_{F}(c)}
via
\sum_{c\in C(t)}e^{\mathrm{Per}_{F}(c)}
using a Riemannian sum argument.
Indeed, a verbatim repetition of the proof in
[
21
, Section 6.2]
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{c\in P(t)}e^{\mathrm{Per}_{F}(c)}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{B}(1+\rho_{0})^{2}e^{2(Q\epsilon^{\alpha}+\delta_{F}\epsilon)}\frac{e^{\delta_{F}t}}{\delta_{F}t},

\displaystyle\sum_{c\in P(t)}e^{\mathrm{Per}_{F}(c)}

\displaystyle\gtrsim(1+\rho_{0})^{-2}e^{-2(Q\epsilon^{\alpha}+\delta_{F}\epsilon)}\frac{e^{\delta_{F}t}}{\delta_{F}t}.

We emphasize that

. Note that we can choose

\displaystyle\sum_{c\in P(t)}e^{\mathrm{Per}_{F}(c)}

\displaystyle\lesssim C\frac{e^{\delta_{F}t}}{\delta_{F}t},

\displaystyle\sum_{c\in P(t)}e^{\mathrm{Per}_{F}(c)}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{e^{\delta_{F}t}}{\delta_{F}t}.

In the above,

where the supremum is taken over any sequence of generalized flow boxes described above.
The proof of Theorem

is complete.
∎

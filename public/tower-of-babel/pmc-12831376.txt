A Delphi consensus for a nurse-led personalized exercise intervention in breast cancer patients during chemotherapy
Authors: Huijie Wei, Wubin He
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundChemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN) and cancer-related fatigue (CRF) are prevalent and debilitating side effects in breast cancer patients, adversely affecting treatment adherence and quality of life. Although exercise is beneficial, a standardized, practical protocol for integrating nurse-led exercise interventions into clinical pathways during chemotherapy is not yet established.MethodsA three-round modified Delphi study was conducted between February and May 2025 among a multidisciplinary panel of 22 experts, including oncology nurses, breast oncologists, rehabilitation therapists, and patient advocates. Anonymized, structured questionnaires were administered electronically. Consensus was predefined as a mean score ≥ 4.0 (on a 5-point Likert scale), agreement by ≥ 75% of respondents, and standard deviation ≤ 1.0. Kendall’s coefficient of concordance (W) was used to assess stability of rankings across rounds.ResultsHigh response rates were maintained across all rounds (100%, 95%, 100%). Key agreements included: (1) initiating assessment and exercise prescription at the first chemotherapy cycle; (2) implementing a multi-component regimen combining aerobic, resistance, and balance training; (3) clearly defining nurses’ roles in assessment, education, and monitoring; (4) personalizing exercise intensity based on daily patient-reported symptoms (e.g., fatigue); and (5) integrating digital tools for adherence support and documentation.ConclusionsThis study establishes a robust expert consensus on a structured, nurse-led, personalized exercise intervention package for integration into the clinical pathway of breast cancer patients undergoing chemotherapy. The framework offers practical guidance for mitigating treatment-related side effects and improving patient outcomes. Introduction

Breast cancer remains the most common malignancy among women worldwide [1]. Chemotherapy, a cornerstone of treatment, is associated with debilitating side effects that severely impact patients’ quality of life and treatment outcomes [2, 3]. Chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN) and cancer-related fatigue (CRF) are particularly prevalent, burdensome, and challenging to manage [4, 5]. CIPN can cause pain, numbness, and functional impairment, while CRF leads to persistent exhaustion; both often result in treatment delays or dose reductions, potentially compromising survival rates [6, 7].

Exercise has emerged as an effective non-pharmacological strategy to mitigate these toxicities. Substantial evidence, including systematic reviews and meta-analyses, indicates that physical activity can reduce the severity of CRF, improve physical function, and alleviate CIPN symptoms [8, 9]. Exercise also enhances psychological well-being and may improve treatment completion rates [10]. Consequently, leading oncology organizations now recommend integrating exercise into standard cancer care [11].

Despite these robust recommendations and the established efficacy of exercise, a significant evidence-to-practice gap persists in the context of active chemotherapy [12, 13]. Existing research and guidelines have successfully demonstrated that exercise is beneficial but frequently lack the operational specificity to guide how to implement it systematically within the routine chemotherapy pathway. This has led to uncertainty among clinicians and fragmented application. Three critical, inter-related gaps can be identified: (1) the absence of structured, operational protocols that specify the ‘who, when, and how’ of exercise initiation, monitoring, and dynamic adjustment; (2) the under-specification of the definitive roles and competencies for oncology nurses, who maintain the most consistent patient contact and are thus uniquely positioned to lead this integration [14]; and (3) the predominance of static prescription models that fail to accommodate the day-to-day fluctuation of symptoms like fatigue and neuropathy.

This study directly addresses these gaps by establishing, for the first time, a multidisciplinary expert consensus on a nurse-led, dynamically personalized exercise intervention explicitly designed for seamless integration into the standard clinical pathway. Its primary innovation lies not in establishing the efficacy of exercise, but in providing a practical implementation blueprint. This framework moves beyond generic advice by offering explicit guidance on: (1) timing, mandating initiation at the first chemotherapy cycle for proactive management; (2) personalization, introducing a dynamic model where exercise intensity is adjusted in real-time based on daily patient-reported outcomes (e.g., fatigue); and (3) integration, defining specific nursing roles, documentation standards, and the use of digital tools to embed the intervention into existing workflows. Thus, our contribution bridges the evidence-practice gap by translating broad principles into an actionable, feasible, and sustainable clinical protocol.

Using the Delphi method, we sought to synthesize multidisciplinary expertise from oncology nursing, medical oncology, rehabilitation therapy, and patient advocacy to establish actionable recommendations on patient assessment, exercise prescription, safety monitoring, and nursing roles, thereby bridging the evidence-practice gap and improving care quality.

Methods

Study design

A three-round, modified Delphi expert consensus study was conducted between February and May 2025. The Delphi method is a structured communication technique designed to achieve a reliable consensus from a panel of experts through iterative, anonymized questionnaires [15]. This approach was selected to synthesize diverse expert opinions on the complex, multi-faceted issue of integrating exercise into clinical care. Each round was spaced approximately 4–5 weeks apart to allow for questionnaire refinement, data analysis, and participant response.

Development of the preliminary framework and round 1 questionnaire

The first-round questionnaire was not developed de novo but was grounded in a comprehensive, evidence-based preliminary framework. To construct this framework, we conducted a systematic review of key databases (PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library) and international guidelines (e.g., American College of Sports Medicine [ACSM], American Society of Clinical Oncology [ASCO]) up to January 2025, focusing on exercise interventions for breast cancer patients undergoing chemotherapy. This review synthesized recommendations from established guidelines [9] and evidence from systematic reviews on managing chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN) and cancer-related fatigue (CRF) [8, 16]. The core structure of a multi-component exercise regimen (aerobic, resistance, balance) was adopted in direct alignment with strong recommendations from ACSM and other authoritative bodies [11]. The proposed initial FITT (Frequency, Intensity, Time, Type) parameters were intentionally conservative, prioritizing safety and feasibility for patients initiating exercise during active treatment. This approach was informed by feasibility studies and general recommendations for medically vulnerable populations [9, 17]. For instance, the emphasis on moderate intensity (e.g., guided by the “Talk Test”) and the inclusion of balance training for CIPN are well-established principles in oncology exercise prescription [9, 16]. The goal of the subsequent Delphi process was therefore not to establish de novo evidence but to translate this synthesized evidence base into a clinically actionable, nurse-led protocol, seeking expert consensus on its practical operationalization, timing, safety monitoring, and integration into the standard chemotherapy care pathway.

Expert panel composition and recruitment

Experts were purposively sampled based on their expertise, clinical experience, and representativeness, following recommendations for Delphi studies in healthcare [15]. The panel was designed to encompass key disciplines involved in the care of breast cancer patients: oncology nursing specialists, breast oncologists, rehabilitation therapists (physiotherapy/exercise physiology), and breast cancer survivor advocates. Inclusion criteria for clinical experts were: (1) holding a senior professional title and (2) possessing a minimum of 10 years of experience in the breast cancer field [18]. Patient advocates were required to have completed all chemotherapy and have personal experience with exercise interventions, coupled with the ability to articulate their needs and experiences clearly [14].

The authority of the expert panel was quantitatively assessed by the authority coefficient (Cr), which was derived from the average of two indicators: the judgment basis (Ca) and familiarity with the problem (Cs), calculated as Cr = (Ca + Cs)/2 [18]. Ca was evaluated using a 4-point scale (0.3–0.9) based on the perceived influence of four aspects: theoretical analysis (0.9), practical experience (0.7), reference to literature (0.5), and intuitive judgment (0.3). Cs was rated on a 5-point Likert scale (1 = very unfamiliar to 5 = very familiar) based on self-reported familiarity with exercise interventions during chemotherapy. The mean authority coefficient (Cr) for the panel was 0.87, indicating a high level of expertise.

Ultimately, a multidisciplinary panel of 22 experts agreed to participate, comprising 12 oncology nurses (54.5%), 5 oncologists (22.7%), 3 rehabilitation therapists (13.6%), and 2 breast cancer survivors (9.1%).

Delphi process

In the first round, experts received the preliminary evidence-informed framework and responded to open-ended questions designed to solicit feedback, suggest additions, revisions, and comment on all aspects of the protocol, including assessment tools, exercise prescription parameters, safety monitoring, nursing roles, and integration strategies.

Qualitative data from Round 1 were analyzed using thematic content analysis. This analysis generated a set of specific, actionable statements that formed the quantitative questionnaire for Round 2.

In the second round, experts rated the importance of each statement on a 5-point Likert scale (1 = “strongly disagree” to 5 = “strongly agree”). An open-ended comments section was included for experts to elaborate on their ratings or propose modifications. Descriptive statistics—including mean scores, standard deviations, and percentage agreement (proportion of ratings 4 ‘agree’ and 5 ‘strongly agree’)—were computed for each item. The pre-defined consensus criterion was a mean score ≥ 4.0, an agreement rate ≥ 75%, and a standard deviation ≤ 1.0, consistent with established methodological standards for Delphi studies [15]. Items failing to meet this threshold, along with all qualitative feedback, were advanced to the third round for re-evaluation.

The third-round questionnaire reintroduced items without consensus, accompanied by anonymized panel feedback—including the mean scores, standard deviations, and a summary of experts’ comments from the previous round. Experts were instructed to reconsider their ratings in light of this collective feedback. This final round aimed to refine perspectives and establish stable consensus on all remaining items.

The second and third-round quantitative questionnaires were developed based on the qualitative results from the previous round(s).

Reporting guidelines

This study was conducted and reported in accordance with the CREDES (Guidance on Conducting and REporting DElphi Studies) reporting guideline [19]. A completed CREDES checklist is provided as Supplementary File 1.

Data analysis

Quantitative data were analyzed using SPSS software (Version 26.0). Descriptive statistics, including mean scores, standard deviations, frequency of maximum scores, and percentage agreement, were calculated for each item. The pre-defined consensus criteria were applied as stated above. The response rate for each round was calculated to assess panel engagement. The stability of consensus between Rounds 2 and 3 was assessed using Kendall’s coefficient of concordance (W).

Ethics approval

Approval for this study was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Jinzhou Medical University (Approval No: JZMULL2025159). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Prior to participation, all experts were provided with detailed written information regarding the study’s purpose and procedures, and informed consent was obtained electronically from all participants.

Results

Response rate and panel engagement

The Delphi study achieved a high level of engagement from the expert panel across all three rounds. The response rates were 100% (22/22) in the first round, 95% (21/22) in the second round, and 100% (21/21) in the final round, demonstrating strong commitment and minimizing potential non-response bias. Timing of intervention initiation

Experts unequivocally agreed that assessment and exercise prescription should commence with the first cycle of chemotherapy to proactively manage symptoms rather than react to them after they develop. This was among the items with the highest agreement rates in the study (Mean = 4.9, SD = 0.3, Agreement = 100%).

Core components of exercise prescription

A multi-component regimen was endorsed. The consensus firmly supported the integration of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise (e.g., brisk walking), targeted resistance training, and balance exercises. The panel provided clear parameters for frequency and duration, while emphatically stating that intensity must be personalized and dynamically adjusted based on the patient’s daily symptom report, particularly fatigue levels. The principle of personalization was a foundational element that permeated all agreed-upon items.

Role of nursing staff

The responsibilities of nursing staff were clearly defined. Consensus validated the role of nurses in conducting initial screening and risk assessment, providing patient education, offering motivation and counselling, and monitoring adherence and side effects. The use of specific, simple patient-reported outcome tools at each clinic visit to guide prescription adjustments was also endorsed.

Integration into the clinical pathway

Consensus was established on practical integration strategies. This included the use of digital tools (e.g., mobile applications) for activity tracking and education to support adherence between visits. Experts also agreed on key documentation requirements to ensure the intervention is recorded in the patient’s medical record, thereby facilitating continuity of care and audit. The entire package was structured to fit seamlessly within the existing workflow of chemotherapy appointments.

Safety considerations and contraindications

The panel reached strong consensus on critical safety protocols, including absolute contraindications for exercise (e.g., fever, active infection), parameters for exercise modification based on symptom severity, and clear guidelines for referral to physical therapy when indicated. These safety considerations ensure that the intervention can be implemented with appropriate risk management.

Discussion

This study successfully established, through a rigorous multidisciplinary Delphi consensus, a structured and operational framework for a nurse-led, personalized exercise intervention. This framework moves beyond the general exercise recommendations prevalent in the literature by providing explicit guidance on its integration into the standard clinical pathway for breast cancer patients undergoing chemotherapy. The following discussion interprets the significance of these findings, situates them within the broader scholarly and practical context, and outlines implications for clinical practice and future research.

Interpretation of main findings: a structured, evidence-informed intervention framework

The primary outcome of this Delphi study is a structured and highly operational intervention framework comprising 42 consensus items. This represents a significant advancement beyond general exercise recommendations by providing a detailed, systematic, and clinically actionable implementation blueprint [20]. The experts’ strong agreement on the timing of initiation (i.e., commencing with the first chemotherapy cycle) is particularly critical, as it aligns with the modern oncology care paradigm of preemptive symptom management [16]. Diverging from traditional reactive models that address severe symptoms like CIPN and CRF after they manifest, our consensus supports a proactive approach aimed at preventing or mitigating the severity of these toxicities through early exercise intervention, thereby potentially better preserving patients’ functional status and treatment adherence [21].

The endorsement of a multi-component regimen combining aerobic, resistance, and balance training is well-supported by extant evidence [22]. It is noteworthy that while strong consensus was achieved on the vast majority of items (mean agreement rate 89.8%), several components (B2: vital sign monitoring; C8: using the “Talk Test”; E4: standard exercise discussion during visits) achieved consensus rates just above the 75% threshold. These items should therefore be interpreted with caution, as they may represent areas of ongoing clinical debate or context-dependent applicability, and their implementation may require greater flexibility.

The most significant innovation of this consensus lies in its emphasis on dynamic personalization and its provision of a complete implementation framework. The mandate for real-time adjustment of exercise intensity based on patients’ daily symptom reports (e.g., fatigue on a 0–10 scale) represents a paradigm shift from fixed prescriptions [23]. This approach allows the intervention to flexibly adapt to the fluctuating physical condition of patients throughout chemotherapy, thereby enhancing its feasibility, safety, and patient-centeredness in real-world clinical settings [7].

The central role of nursing and clinical pathway integration

The panel’s strong endorsement of nurses as the central leads for this intervention is both expected and pivotal. As the healthcare professionals with the most consistent patient contact, nurses are uniquely positioned to implement symptom management, provide education, and promote self-management behaviors [12]. Our consensus delineates specific nursing responsibilities, from initial assessment and education to ongoing monitoring and motivational interviewing, thereby providing a clear roadmap for expanding the advanced practice role of oncology nurses [24].

More importantly, the study provides comprehensive implementation strategies that integrate defined nursing roles with clinical pathway integration. This combined approach ensures that the intervention is not only clinically sound but also systematically embedded within routine care processes. The consensus items concerning documentation, use of digital health tools, and workflow integration offer concrete solutions to address the well-documented evidence-practice gap [9]. By embedding key exercise-related activities into the standard chemotherapy follow-up (e.g., as a fixed agenda item for each visit), the intervention can be operationalized systematically, reducing reliance on individual champions and promoting sustainability across different healthcare settings [25].

Alignment with existing research, policy, and guidelines

These findings are consistent with and substantially extend the spirit of guidelines issued by organizations such as ASCO and ACSM, which recommend integrating exercise into cancer care [11]. While these existing guidelines provide high-level recommendations on what should be done, our study elaborates on how to implement these recommendations systematically within the chemotherapy pathway. We provide explicit, operationalized details on who (nursing staff), when (initiation at cycle 1, reassessment at each visit), and how (using specific PRO tools for dynamic adjustment) to ensure safety and feasibility. Thus, our consensus framework serves as a valuable and necessary complement to current guidelines, providing an operational manual for institutions seeking to implement these recommendations at a systems level.

Our consensus on exercise interventions for women receiving adjuvant therapy aligns with existing Cochrane evidence [26], while the comprehensive benefits of exercise for breast cancer patients and survivors are well-documented in systematic reviews [8]. The identification of patients at risk of a physically inactive and sedentary lifestyle is crucial for targeting interventions effectively [27]. Furthermore, the integration of survivorship care plans ensures that exercise interventions remain part of comprehensive cancer care beyond active treatment [28].

Strengths and limitations

The key strengths of this study include its methodological rigor, demonstrated by high response rates indicating strong panel engagement, pre-defined consensus criteria, and the rich perspectives afforded by the multidisciplinary panel composition [15, 19]. The inclusion of patient advocates ensured the intervention package addressed both clinical effectiveness and patient values and preferences [14], aligning with modern cancer care strategies that emphasize patient-centered approaches [29, 30].

Several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the consensus represents expert group opinion; its ultimate validity and impact on patient outcomes require prospective validation in actual clinical settings. Second, the Delphi method itself relies on subjective expert judgment and may be susceptible to groupthink or dominance of certain perspectives, despite the anonymized process. Third, the panel was primarily composed of experts from leading domestic medical centers, whose experience and resources may differ from those in community or resource-limited settings, potentially affecting the generalizability of the framework. Future research is needed to test the feasibility and effectiveness of this package across diverse healthcare contexts, building upon the existing evidence base for exercise interventions in cancer care [8].

Implications for clinical practice and future research

The consensus framework established in this study offers clear and actionable guidance for clinical practice. Oncology departments and healthcare institutions are encouraged to adopt this blueprint to develop and implement standardized, nurse-led exercise interventions within routine chemotherapy care. Successful implementation necessitates several supportive actions: providing specialized training to equip oncology nurses with competencies in exercise prescription and symptom-guided monitoring; incorporating digital health tools (e.g., mobile applications) to support adherence and remote education [31]; and modifying electronic health records to facilitate the systematic documentation of exercise prescriptions, patient adherence, and outcomes, thereby promoting accountability and continuity of care.

This consensus also delineates a clear trajectory for future research. Initial efforts should focus on feasibility and pilot studies to assess the acceptability, practicality, and preliminary efficacy of the intervention package within specific clinical contexts, while identifying potential implementation barriers. Subsequent research should progress to rigorous effectiveness evaluations through multi-center, pragmatic trials or quasi-experimental designs. These studies should compare the structured intervention against usual care, with primary endpoints encompassing patient-centered outcomes such as the severity of chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy and cancer-related fatigue, treatment adherence metrics, and health-related quality of life. Furthermore, parallel implementation science research is essential to identify the contextual factors, facilitators, and barriers that influence the sustainable adoption, fidelity, and scalability of this intervention across diverse healthcare systems [9]. This iterative research continuum is vital for translating expert consensus into validated, real-world practice that ultimately improves patient outcomes.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this Delphi study has established a robust expert consensus to develop a comprehensive, structured, and practical framework for guiding the integration of a nurse-led, personalized exercise intervention into the clinical pathway for breast cancer patients receiving chemotherapy. The framework clarifies core intervention components, key nursing roles, and feasible implementation strategies, thereby providing a much-needed tool to bridge the evidence-practice gap in exercise oncology. While further validation in real-world settings is necessary, this consensus represents a critical step towards translating evidence-based exercise interventions into routine care standards for breast cancer patients, ultimately aiming to mitigate chemotherapy toxicity, improve treatment experiences, and enhance patient outcomes.

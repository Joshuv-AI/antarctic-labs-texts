Abstract
. In this paper we prove the forward direction of the conjecture of Gross-Prasad that an L-packet Π ϕ ​ ( G ) \Pi_{\phi}(G) contains a generic representation if and only if L ⁡ ( s , ϕ , Ad ) L(s,\phi,\Ad) is regular at s = 1 s=1 , by assuming the local Langlands correspondence and the p p -adic Kazhdan-Lusztig hypothesis. We then prove an analogous statement for ABV-packets, which together with Vogan’s conjecture on ABV-packets implies that if Arthur’s conjectures for G G are known, then one direction of Shahidi’s enhanced genericity conjecture holds: If an Arthur packet Π ψ ​ ( G ) \Pi_{\psi}(G) contains a generic representation, then ϕ ψ \phi_{\psi} is tempered. In the case where the infinitesimal parameters in question are all unramified, we obtain converses to the above statements. We also offer some speculation about the relationship between Arthur type representations, and singularities in varieties of Langlands parameters defined by Vogan. Finally, we recover some facts about central characters using the results above.

Introduction

In this paper, we prove the forward direction of the following conjecture of Gross-Prasad under the assumption of (part of the desiderata of) the local Langlands correspondence and the
p
-adic Kazhdan-Lusztig Hypothesis, as well as the converse for unramified (trivial on the inertia subgorup of the Weil group) parameters:

(
[

]
*Conjecture 2.6)

An L-packet
\Pi_{\phi}(G)
contains a generic representation if and only if

is regular at
s=1
.

More specifically we prove the following statement:

Conjecture 0.2

(
[
5
]
*Conjecture 4.1)
.

Assuming the
p
-adic Kazhdan-Lusztig hypothesis, if an L-packet
\Pi_{\phi}(G)
contains a generic representation, then the orbit

associated to
\phi
in the Vogan variety

(see Section

By

As a consequence of the above results, we are also able to demonstrate an analogue of these statements for ABV-packets.
If one assumes that
G
is a group for which Arthur’s conjectures are known, and that one inclusion of Vogan’s conjecture (see Conjecture

(

For symplectic and split odd orthogonal groups, a proof of Conjecture

In

In this paper we generalize one direction of these results to arbitrary connected reductive groups.

We noticed that several of the lemmas we use in the proof of Conjecture

This led us to explore the broader impact of these lemmas, and to consider representation-theoretic interpretations of orbits with smooth closure.

We now review the layout of the paper.

In Section

Background

1.1.
The local Langlands correspondence

In this subsection we briefly review certain basic desiderata of the local Langlands correspondence.
In particular, we include assumptions about pure inner forms as proposed by Vogan
[
26
]
, as well as assumptions on generic representations, and on central characters.

First we recall the definition of generic representations.
Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field,
G
a connected quasi-split, and
B
be a Borel subgroup of
G
with unipotent radical
U
. The torus
T=B/U
acts on
\Hom(U,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
.
A character
\theta:U(F)\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is called
generic character
if its stabilizer in
T(F)
is the center
Z(F)
of
G(F)
.
For a generic character
\theta
of
U(F)
, a representation
\pi
of
G(F)
is called
\theta
-generic if
\Hom_{U(F)}(\pi,\theta)\neq 0
.
This only depends on the
T(F)
-orbit of
\theta
.
If
\theta
is understood from the context, we simply say
\pi
is generic.

A
Whittaker datum

\mathfrak{w}
for
G
is a
G(F)
-conjugacy classes of pairs
(B,\theta)
, where
B
is a Borel subgroup of
G
and
\theta
is a generic character of
U(F)
, where
U
is the unipotent radical of
B
.

We now suppose
G
is a connected reductive algebraic group (not necessarily quasi-split) over a non-Archimedean field
F
.
As in
[
26
]
*Definition 2.6, Proposition 2.7 we call each element
\delta
of the non-commutative cohomology set
H^{1}(F,G)
, a
pure inner form
of
G
.
Each
\delta
determines a form
G_{\delta}
of
G
.
For each pure inner form
\delta
,
let
\Pi(G_{\delta})
be the set of isomorphism classes of smooth irreducible representations of
G_{\delta}(F)
and set

\Pi^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}}(G){\,:=\,}\coprod_{\delta\in H^{1}(F,G)}\Pi(G_{\delta}).

We write
\Phi(G)
for the set of equivalence classes of Langlands parameters of
G
.

We now describe the desiderata of the local Langlands correspondence which are relevant for our purposes.

(1)

There is a finite-to-one surjective map

\Pi^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}}(G)\to\Phi(G).

For each Langlands parameter
\phi
, we define the
pure
L-packet of
\phi

\Pi_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}}(G)\subset\Pi^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}}(G)

to be the pre-image of
[\phi]
. (Which is independent of the choice of representative.)

(2)

Set
A_{\phi}{\,:=\,}\pi_{0}(Z_{{\widehat{G}}}(\phi))
.
Fixing a Whittaker datum
\mathfrak{w}
, we obtain a family of bijections,

J(\mathfrak{w}):\Pi_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}}(G)\to\widehat{A_{\phi}},

for each Langlands parameter
\phi
of
G
, such that if
\Pi^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}_{\phi}(G)
contains a
\theta
-generic representation
\pi
, then
J(\mathfrak{w})(\pi)
is the trivial representation of
A_{\phi}
.
We call
J(\mathfrak{w})
a
Whittaker normalization
.

(3)

Let
Z
be the maximal torus of
G
. Then, we have a surjective morphism
{}^{L}G\to{}^{L}Z
.
Given
\pi\in\Pi_{\phi}(G)
, the central character of
\pi
is the unique element of the L-packet of

W_{F}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\phi}{}^{L}G\to{}^{L}Z.

We say that
\Pi_{\phi}(G)
(resp.
\Pi_{\phi}^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}(G)
) and
\phi
are
generic
if
\Pi_{\phi}(G)
contains a generic representation (See, for example,
[
5
]
*Section 1.5).

1.2.
Kazhdan-Lusztig hypothesis for
p
-adic groups

In this subsection we recall the notion of
Vogan varieties
which are the main focus of this paper.
We will also recall the statement of the
p
-adic Kazhdan-Lusztig hypothesis, which is crucial for the results in the latter half of the paper.
In short, the
p
-adic Kazhdan-Lusztig hypothesis articulates a deep relationship between equivariant perverse sheaves on moduli spaces of Langlands parameters introduced by
[
26
]
, and multiplicities of irreducible representations in
standard representations
, whose definition depends on the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
20
]
*Theorem 3.5
For any irreducible admissible representation
(V,\pi)
of
G(F)
, there exists a parabolic
P=MU\in\mathcal{F}
and an irreducible tempered representation
\tau\in\Pi_{\mathrm{temp}}(M(F))
and
\mu\in\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{\ast,+}
such that
\pi
is the unique irreducible quotient of
I_{P}^{G}(e^{\mu}\otimes\tau)
, where
I_{P}^{G}
is the normalized parabolic induction functor.
Moreover, the triple
(P,\mu,\tau)
is uniquely determined up to
W
-conjugacy.

See
[
20
]
for more details and definitions regarding the above theorem.

The important take-away from the above theorem is that fixing a choice of minimal parabolic
P_{0}
of
G
, for any irreducible representation
\pi
of
G
, there is a unique representation
I_{P}^{G}(e^{\mu}\otimes\tau)
determined by the above theorem such that
P
is standard.
We will refer to
I_{P}^{G}(e^{\mu}\otimes\tau)
as the standard representation of
\pi
and denote it by
S_{\pi}
.

We now turn to the description of the moduli space of Langlands parameters mentioned at the start of this section.
Given a Langlands parameter

\phi:W_{F}\times\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to{}^{L}G,

its infinitesimal parameter

\lambda_{\phi}:W_{F}\to{}^{L}G,

is defined by

\lambda(w)=\phi\left(w,d_{w}\right),

where

d_{w}:=\begin{pmatrix}\left\lVert w\right\rVert^{1/2}&0\\
0&\left\lVert w\right\rVert^{-1/2}\end{pmatrix},

for the norm map
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert:W_{F}\to F^{\times}
.

Given a fixed infinitesimal parameter
\lambda
, we define

V_{\lambda}:=\{x\in\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\ |\ \forall w\in W_{F},\Ad(\lambda(w))x=\left\lVert w\right\rVert x\}.

When our choice of
\lambda
is understood, we will simply write
V=V_{\lambda}
to keep notation uncluttered.
We refer to
V
as a
Vogan variety
, as it was introduced in
[
26
]
.

The group

H_{\lambda}:=\{g\in\hat{G}\ |\ \forall w\in W_{F},g\lambda(w)g^{-1}=\lambda(w)\},

acts on
V
by conjugation. As above, we write
H=H_{\lambda}
when our choice of
\lambda
is understood.
There are finitely many
H
-orbits on
V
.
There is a unique closed orbit, and a unique open orbit.
The closure of each orbit is a union of orbits.
For orbits
C
and
D
we write
D\leq C
if
D\subseteq\bar{C}
.

The orbits of
V
are in bijection with Langlands parameters having infinitesimal parameter
\lambda
.
In particular, if
\phi(w,d_{w})=\lambda(w)
, then the restriction

\phi|_{\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})}:\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to{}^{L}G,

determines an algebraic map

\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to\hat{G},

and thus defines an
\SL_{2}
-triple
(h,x,y)
such that
x\in V_{\lambda}
.
Conversely each
x\in V_{\lambda}
determines

\phi_{x}:W_{F}\times\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to\hat{G}\subseteq{}^{L}G,

by demanding
\phi_{x}(1,e)=\exp(x)
, and
\phi_{x}(w,d_{w})=\lambda(w)
.
Moreover,
x,y\in V
are in the same
H
-orbit iff they determine equivalent Langlands parameters.

Recall from Desiderata
2
that fixing a choice of Whittaker normalization provides a bijection
J(\mathfrak{w}):\Pi_{\phi}^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}(G)\to\widehat{A_{\phi}}
for each Langlands parameter, such that if
\pi
is
\mathfrak{w}
-generic, then
J(\mathfrak{w})(\pi)
is the trivial representation.
Letting
x\in V
, and
H(x)
be the
H
-stabalizer of

, then the inclusion
Z_{\hat{G}}(\phi_{x})\to H(x)
induces an isomorphism

A_{x}:=H(x)/H(x)^{\circ}\cong Z_{\hat{G}}(\phi)/Z_{\hat{G}}(\phi)^{\circ}.

Definition 1
.

Given some fixed choice of Whittaker normalization

, if

is irreducible, define

to be the inverse image of

See
[

]
*Section 2.2 for more details on making a change of Whittaker normalization, and its compatibility with the local Langlands correspondence.

Hypothesis 1.2

Orbits with smooth closure and Arthur type representations

2.1.
ABV-packets for orbits with smooth closure

In this section, we prove that if

is an orbit of Arthur type, and

is a certain kind of

We also explore some relationships between representations of Arthur type, that is, irreducible representations

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that an
H
-orbit
C
has smooth closure.
If
\mathcal{L}\in\mathsf{Loc}_{H}(\bar{C})
is irreducible, then so is
\mathcal{L}|_{C}
.

Proof.

Since
\bar{C}
is smooth,
\mathcal{L}[\dim\bar{C}]=\mathcal{L}[\dim C]
is perverse.
Hence, there must exist an orbit
D\leq C
and an
H
-equivariant local system
\mathcal{F}
on

such that
\mathcal{IC}(D,\mathcal{F})\cong\mathcal{L}[\dim C]
.
Thus

\mathcal{IC}(D,\mathcal{F})|_{C}\cong\mathcal{L}|_{C}[\dim C],

and thus it has non-zero support on

, but this is only possible if

, in which case

\mathcal{L}|_{C}[\dim C]\cong\mathcal{IC}(C,\mathcal{F})|_{C}\cong\mathcal{F}[\dim C],

and therefore
\mathcal{L}|_{C}\cong\mathcal{F}
which conclude the result.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
C\subseteq V
such that
\bar{C}
is smooth, and suppose
\mathcal{L}\in\mathsf{Loc}_{H}(\bar{C})
is irreducible.
Then, for all

and

,

\operatorname{\mathsf{N}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{E}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{v}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{s}}_{D}(\mathcal{IC}(C,\mathcal{L}|_{C}))\cong 0.

Proof.

The functor
\operatorname{\mathsf{N}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{E}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{v}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{s}}
is defined in terms of another functor
\operatorname{\mathsf{E}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{v}}

[
5
]
*Section 7.3.
It suffices to determine when

is 0, which is defined

(\operatorname{\mathsf{E}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{v}}_{D}\mathcal{F})_{(x,\xi)}=(\mathrm{R}\Phi_{f_{\xi}}\mathcal{F})_{x}=\mathrm{R}\Phi_{f_{D^{\ast}}}(\mathcal{F}\boxtimes\mathbbm{1}_{D^{\ast}})_{(x,\xi)}.

Given that the intermediate extension functor respects composition, we conclude that for the inclusion

,

\mathcal{IC}(C,\mathcal{L}|_{C})\cong j_{!}(\mathcal{L}).

Therefore

\operatorname{\mathsf{E}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{v}}_{D}(\mathcal{IC}(C,\mathcal{L}|_{C})\cong\mathrm{R}\Phi_{f_{D^{\ast}}}(j_{!}(\mathcal{L})\boxtimes\mathbbm{1}_{D^{\ast}})|_{\Lambda_{D}^{\text{reg}}}.

By
[
9
]
*Proposition 7.10 we know that this is only non-zero if

. Thus, by proper base change it suffices to compute

\mathrm{R}\Phi_{f|_{\overline{C}\times D^{*}}}(\mathcal{L}\boxtimes\mathbbm{1}_{D^{\ast}})|_{\Lambda_{D}^{\mathrm{reg}}}

where we write now

.

By
[
9
]
*Lemma 7.3, since
\mathcal{L}\boxtimes{\mathbbm{1}}_{D^{\ast}}
is a local system, and
\overline{C}\times D^{*}
is smooth,
\mathrm{R}\Phi_{f}(\mathcal{L}\boxtimes\mathbbm{1}_{D^{\ast}})_{(x,\xi)}\cong 0

in the smooth locus of
f
.
We claim that
\Lambda_{D}^{\mathrm{reg}}
is contained in the smooth locus, which would give the result.

Thus, it suffices to determine the singular locus
f
. Now, using that
\overline{C}\times D
is smooth,
C\times D^{*}\subset V\times V^{*}
, and

is the restriction of the perfect pairing of the pairing between

and
V^{*}
to

, the Jacobian condition for smoothness tells us

df|_{(x,\xi)}\in{\rm span}\{dg|_{(x,\xi)}\;|\;g\in I(\overline{C}\times\overline{D^{*}})\},

Since

Thus

\xi\in{\rm span}\{dg|_{x}\;|\;g\in I(\overline{C})\}\text{ and }x\in{\rm span}\{dg|_{\xi}\;|\;g\in I(\overline{D^{*}})\},

The first condition gives that

2.2.
Smooth closure and Arthur-type representations

In this section, we investigate various examples motivated by the following speculation:
Given a simple connected reductive group

\psi
for which
\pi(C,\mathcal{L})\in\Pi_{\psi}(G)
, then
C
is open, closed, or

The assumption that

However, we will demonstrate below that it still holds for various cases for

Given an Arthur parameter

\pi(C_{\phi_{\psi}},{\mathbbm{1}})\in\Pi_{\phi_{\psi}}^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}(G)=\Pi_{\psi}(G),

and thus if our speculation is to hold,
it must be that
C_{\psi}
is open, closed, or has singular closure.

In fact, we explore some examples of unramified infinitesimal parameters

,
\pi(C,\mathbbm{1})
is of Arthur type if and only if
\bar{C}
is not smooth.
However, there are certainly cases for which
\bar{C}_{\phi}
is singular, but

is not of Arthur type.
For example, in
[

]
, an orbit

of a particular Vogan variety of

is computed for which

is not of Arthur type. Meanwhile, the corresponding ABV-packet

for a particular Arthur type representation
\pi_{\psi}
.

Example 2.7
.

Let
G
be a connected reductive group with a maximal torus of split rank
n
and consider the infinitesimal parameter

of the Steinberg representation for

Thus, for the simple roots

is the maximal torus corresponding to the choice of based root system.
Every subset

3.1.

Proposition 3.1

(1)

(1)

.

By the Kazhdan-Lusztig hypothesis

[S_{\pi}:\sigma]=\sum_{\mathbb{Z}}\dim\Hom_{\mathsf{Loc}_{H}(C)}\left(\mathcal{H}^{n}(\mathcal{IC}(D,\mathcal{F})|_{C},\mathcal{L}\right),

but
\mathcal{IC}(D,\mathcal{F})
is supported on
\bar{D}
, therefore
[S_{\pi}:\sigma]
can only be non-zero if

C\leq D
, but since we can
not have
C\subseteq\bar{D}
it must be that
D=C
.
Thus the above becomes

\displaystyle[S_{\pi}:\sigma]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbb{Z}}\dim\Hom_{\mathsf{Loc}_{H}(C)}\left(\mathcal{H}^{n}(\mathcal{IC}(C,\mathcal{F}))|_{C},\mathcal{L}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbb{Z}}\dim\Hom_{\mathsf{Loc}_{H}(C)}\left(\mathcal{H}^{n}(\mathcal{F}[\dim C]),\mathcal{L}\right)

\displaystyle=\dim\Hom_{\mathsf{Loc}_{H}(C)}\left(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{L}\right),

which is 1 if
\mathcal{F}\cong\mathcal{L}
, and 0 otherwise.
Therefore
S_{\pi}\cong\pi
.

(2)

As there are no orbits greater than the open orbit in the closure-ordering, this is a special case of (1).

(3)

By
[
9
]
*Theorem 5.1, if
C_{\phi}
is open,

\Pi_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV,pure}$}}}}}(G)=\Pi_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}}(G),

and thus the result follows from (1).

∎

Recall that Lemma
2.1
ensures that if
\mathcal{L}
is an irreducible
H
-equivariant local system on
\bar{C}
, then
\mathcal{L}|_{C}
is irreducible.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
C
has smooth closure, and
\mathcal{L}\in\mathsf{Loc}_{H}(\bar{C})
is irreducible.
Then, for any orbit
D
and
\mathcal{F}\in\mathsf{Loc}_{H}(D)
,
\pi=\pi(C,\mathcal{L}|_{C})
,

[S_{\pi}:\pi(D,\mathcal{F})]=\dim\Hom(\mathcal{L}|_{D},\mathcal{F}).

Hence, if
\mathcal{L}|_{D}
is irreducible, there is a unique
\mathcal{F}\in\mathsf{Loc}_{H}(D)
for which
[S_{\pi}:\pi(D,\mathcal{F})]=1
, and for all other local systems
[S_{\pi}:\pi(D,\mathcal{F})]=0
.

Proof.

As in the proof of
2.2
, for the inclusion
j:C\hookrightarrow V
, we have
\mathcal{IC}(C,\mathcal{L})\cong j_{!}\mathcal{L}[\dim C]
.
Thus, by the Kazhdan-Lusztig hypothesis

\displaystyle[S_{\pi},\pi(D,\mathcal{F})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\dim\Hom_{\mathsf{Loc}_{H}(D)}\left(\mathcal{H}^{n}(\mathcal{IC}(C,\mathcal{L}|_{C})|_{D},\mathcal{F}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\dim\Hom_{\mathsf{Loc}_{H}(D)}\left(\mathcal{H}^{n}(j_{!}\mathcal{L})|_{D},\mathcal{F}\right)

\displaystyle=\dim\Hom_{\mathsf{Loc}_{H}(D)}\left(\mathcal{L}|_{D},\mathcal{F}\right).

Since
\mathcal{L}|_{D}
is irreducible,
[S_{\pi},\pi(D,\mathcal{F})]
is only non-zero for
\mathcal{F}\cong\mathcal{L}|_{C}
, in which case it is equal to 1.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

(1)

If
C
is open, then for every irreducible
\mathcal{L}\in\mathsf{Loc}_{H}(C)
,
\pi(C,\mathcal{L})
is isomorphic to its own standard representation.

(2)

If
C
has smooth closure, then for each
D\leq C
,
\pi(C,{\mathbbm{1}})
appears in the standard module of
\pi(D,{\mathbbm{1}})
with multiplicity 1, and in no other standard representations.

(3)

C
is the open orbit if and only if
\pi(C,{\mathbbm{1}})
is isomorphic to its own standard representation.

Proof.

(1)

As there are no orbits greater than the open orbit in the closure ordering, the result follows by Proposition
3.1
(1).

(2)

By Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle[S_{\pi(C,{\mathbbm{1}})}:\pi(D,{\mathbbm{1}})]

\displaystyle=\dim\Hom({\mathbbm{1}}_{\bar{C}}|_{D},{\mathbbm{1}}_{D})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\dim\Hom({\mathbbm{1}}_{D},{\mathbbm{1}}_{D}),&D\leq C\\
0,&\text{else}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1,&D\leq C\\
0,&\text{else}\end{cases}

(3)

If
C
is the open orbit, then this follows from (1).

Conversely, suppose that
\pi(C,{\mathbbm{1}})
is isomorphic to its own standard.
If
D
is the open orbit, then by (2)

[\pi(C,{\mathbbm{1}}):\pi(D,{\mathbbm{1}})]=[S_{\pi(C,{\mathbbm{1}})}:\pi(D,{\mathbbm{1}})]=1,

but this is only possible if
\pi(C,{\mathbbm{1}})\cong\pi(D,{\mathbbm{1}})
, which is to say
C=D
is open.

∎

Following the main result of the next section, we will give an interpretation of Corollary
3.3
(2) in terms of generic representations.

Remark 3.4
.

Heiermann
[
19
]
proved that if a Vogan
L
-packet
\Pi_{\phi}^{\mathrm{pure}}(G)
is generic (i.e, if it contains a generic representation), then for each
\pi\in\Pi_{\phi}^{\mathrm{pure}}(G)
, the standard module of
\pi
is irreducible to
\pi
. Conjecture
0.1
(which is known for classical groups) says that
\Pi_{\phi}^{\mathrm{pure}}(G)
is generic if and only
C_{\phi}
is open, see
[
5
]
* Conjecture 4.1 and Theorem
3.6
. Corollary
3.3
is just a geometric way to state Heiermann’s result.

3.2.
Generic L-packets

Recall that Conjecture
0.2
, which is equivalent to the Gross-Prasad conjecture, predicts that
\Pi_{\phi}(G)
contains a generic representation if and only if
C_{\phi}
is open.
The result of
[
5
]
*Theorem 4.1 is that the above holds for quasi-split classical groups.
Assuming the local Langlands correspondence and the
p
-adic Kazhdan-Lusztig hypothesis, we will provide a generalization to reductive groups, however we only prove the converse direction in the
unramified
case (explained below).

The proof in
[
5
]
relies on
[
12
]
, which imposes some (mildly) stronger conditions on the local Langlands correspondence than the proof we give here.
On one hand, the proof given here does not depend on
[
12
]
, and thus requires weaker assumptions on the local Langlands correspondence. On the other hand, we impose the stronger assumption of the
p
-adic Kazhdan-Lusztig hypothesis, though it is already known in many cases as discussed following the statement of Hypothesis
1.2
.
As a consequence, in the next section we will be able to strengthen
[
5
]
*Proposition 4.4, and using this result, we demonstrate that if
G
is a group for which Arthur’s conjectures are known, and for which one containment of Vogan’s Conjecture
1.3
holds, then Shahidi’s enhanced genericity conjecture also holds.

Before coming to the main results, we need to discuss the relationship between Whittaker data and infinitesimal parameters, and prove a useful lemma.
First, we fix a Whittaker datum
\mathfrak{w}
of
G(F)
.
The map
\pi\mapsto\lambda_{\pi}
from irreducible representations to their infinitesimal parameters is finite-to-one.

Assuming Conjecture
0.2
, for any infinitesimal parameter
\lambda
there is a unique
\mathfrak{w}
-generic representation
\pi
such that
\lambda_{\pi}=\lambda
, namely
\pi(C,{\mathbbm{1}})
where
C
is the open orbit in
V_{\lambda}
.
Therefore, we obtain a bijective map

\{\pi\in\Pi(G(F))\ |\ \pi\text{ is }\mathfrak{w}\text{-generic}\}\to\{\lambda:W_{F}\to{}^{L}G\}.

If
\lambda:W_{F}\to{}^{L}G
is trivial on the inertia subgroup of
W_{F}
, then we say it is
unramified
.
In order to prove that the above bijection holds in the case of unramified infinitesimal parameters, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

If
G
is quasi-split and
\lambda:W_{F}\to{}^{L}G
is trivial on the inertia subgroup, then there exists an irreducible generic representation with infinitesimal parameter
\lambda
.

Proof.

Suppose that
G
is quasi-split, and
\lambda:W_{F}\to{}^{L}G
is trivial on the inertia subgroup.
Thus, the image of
\lambda
may be taken to lie in a maximal torus of
\hat{G}
.
Let
T
be the maximal torus of
G
, and let
B
be a Borel subgroup containing
T
.
We can realize
\hat{T}
as the maximal torus of
\hat{G}
containning the image of
\lambda
.
By the Langlands correspondence there must exist an umramified character
\chi
of
T
whose infinitesimal parameter
\lambda^{\prime}
factors through the inclusion

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{L}T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{L}G}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}

We can chose an element
w
in the Weyl group
W(G,B,T)
such that
\chi^{w}=e^{\mu}
for
\mu\in\mathfrak{a}_{B}^{\ast,+}
as in Theorem
1.1
.
Therefore, writing
1_{T}
for the trivial representation of the torus (which is tempered) the triple
(B,\mu,1_{T})
is of the form in Theorem
1.1
, which is to say
I_{B}^{G}(e^{\mu}\otimes 1_{B})
is a standard representation. Since it is the (normalized) parabolic induction of a character of the torus, it is generic, and in particular it contains an irreducible generic subquotient
\pi
.
By the
p
-adic Kazhdan-Lusztig hypothesis, this is only possible if the infinitesimal parameter of
\pi
is
\lambda
. (Though, it is possible to argue this last point by other means.)
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Assume the Kazhdan-Lusztig hypothesis
1.2
.
Let
\phi
be a Langlands parameter with infinitesimal parameter
\lambda
, and suppose that
\Pi_{\phi}(G)
contains a generic representation, then
C_{\phi}
is open.

If
\lambda
is unramified, then if
C_{\phi}
is open,
\Pi_{\phi}(G(F))
contains a generic representation.

Proof.

Suppose
\pi\in\Pi_{\phi}(G)
is
\mathfrak{w}
-generic. Labeling the representations according to the corresponding Whittaker normalization
J(\mathfrak{w})
as in Definition
1
,
\pi\cong\pi(C,{\mathbbm{1}})
. By the standard representation conjecture
[
3
]
, which was proved in
[
18
]
,
\pi
is isomorphic to its own standard representation, and thus by Corollary
3.3
(3)
C_{\phi}
is open.

If
\lambda
is unramified, then by Lemma
3.5
there is an irreducible generic representation
\pi
with infinitesimal parameter
\lambda
.
Letting
\mathfrak{w}
be the Whittaker data for
\pi
, and choosing the normalization with respect to
\mathfrak{w}
, by the forwards direction of this proof
\pi\cong\pi(C_{\phi},{\mathbbm{1}})
, and thus
\pi\in\Pi_{\phi}(G)
.
∎

Thus we can see that the converse direction of the above statement follows, in general, from the forward direction, and the claim that for every infinitesimal parameter
\lambda:W_{F}\to{}^{L}G
there exists an irreducible generic representation
\pi
such that
\lambda_{\pi}
is equivalent to
\lambda
.

We can now give a more representation-theoretic description of Corollary
3.3
(2):

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\pi
be a
\mathfrak{w}
-generic representation of a quasi-split group
G
and let
\phi
be its Langlands parameter.
Then, with respect to the Whittaker normalization
J(\mathfrak{w})
, for every orbit
D
of
V_{\lambda_{\phi}}
, and an irreducible
\mathcal{L}\in\mathsf{Loc}_{H}(D)
,

[S_{\pi(D,\mathcal{L})}:\pi]=\begin{cases}1,&\mathcal{L}={\mathbbm{1}}\\
0,&\text{else}\end{cases}

Proof.

By our choice of Whittaker normalization,
\pi\cong\pi(C_{\phi},{\mathbbm{1}})
.
Hence, by Theorem
3.6
, we know that
C_{\phi}
is open.
As every orbit
D
satisfies
D\leq C_{\phi}
, the result follows from
Corollary
3.3
(2).
∎

3.3.
Generic A- and ABV-packets

In this section, we will use Theorem

3.6
to strengthen the result

[
5
]
*Proposition 4.4 and make further progress to Shahidi’s enhanced genericity conjecture.

Recall that a Langlands parameter
\phi
is said to be tempered if
\phi(w,d_{w})
is bounded in
\hat{G}
, and we say that an Arthur parameter

\phi_{\psi}

The well-known conjecture

*Conjecture 9.4 predicts that if

\Pi_{\phi}(G)
contains a generic representation.
More recently, Shahidi purposed an enhanced version of this conjecture.

Conjecture 3.8

(
[
21
]
*Conjecture 1.2)
.

An Arthur packet
\Pi_{\psi}(G)
contains a generic representation if and only if
\psi
is tempered.

Inspired by Shahidi’s enhanced genericity conjecture, an analogue for ABV packets was proved in
[
5
]
*Theorem 4.4 under the assumption of Theorem
3.6
. Thus, assuming the
p
-adic Kazhdan-Lusztig hypothesis, we generalize one direction to arbitrary quasi-split connected reductive groups.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
G
be a quasi-split reductive group over a

-adic field

.
If
\Pi_{\phi}^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV,pure}$}}}}(G)

is open.

When

is unramified, if

is open, then
\Pi_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV,pure}$}}}}}(G)

Proof.

The conclusion of
[

]
*Theorem 4.4 is that the result follows whenever Theorem

holds.

If

The result

]
*Proposition 4.8 states that for a classical quasi-split group

One part of the proof in

]
*Proposition 4.7, which we now generalize.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
G
be a quasi-split reductive group over
F
.
Assuming the Kazhdan-Lusztig hypothesis
1.2
. If

has a pole at

, then
\Pi_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}}(G)

Proof.

Suppose

By

]
*Theorem 4.4

must be open, hence, by Theorem
3.6

is regular at

.
∎

The only other part of the proof of

]
*Proposition 4.8 that technically assumes that

Thus, we can upgrade the result to a statement conditional on Arthur’s conjectures.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let

be a classical group for which Arthur’s conjectures on

-packets are known.

(1)

The forward direction of Shahidi’s enhanced Conjecture

holds: If

is tempered.

(2)

:

is tempered,

contains a generic representation.

Proof.

The proof that follows is similar to the one given in
[

]
*Proposition 4.8.

(1)

Suppose that

(2)

Now suppose that

4.
Implications of the Kazhdan-Lusztig hypothesis on central characters

In this section, we derive several results on central characters from the
p
-adic Kazhdan-Lusztig hypothesis and Theorem
3.6
. Even though they might be well-known unconditionally, we feel like it is probably worth keeping them here to illustrate the power of the geometric approach.

Corollary 4.1
.

Assume the
p
-adic Kazhdan-Lusztig hypothesis
1.2
and the desiderata of the local Langlands correspondence in Section
1.1
for
G
. Let
\pi
be a supercuspidal generic representation of
G(F)
. Let
\phi=\phi_{\pi}:W_{F}\times\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to{\hskip-2.0pt\,{}^{L}\hskip-1.0pt{G}}
be its local Langlands parameter and
\lambda=\lambda_{\phi}
. Then
V_{\lambda}=\{0\}
and
\phi_{\pi}|_{\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})}=1
.

Proof.

The proof is along the same lines as the proof of Theorem
3.6
. Note that
V_{\lambda}=\{0\}
is equivalent to
\phi_{\pi}|_{\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})}=1
. We now show that
V_{\lambda}=\{0\}.
Assume that
C_{0}
and
C
are the respective closed and open orbits of
V_{\lambda}
. Since
\pi
is assumed to be generic, then
x_{\phi}\in C
by Theorem
3.6
.
Desiderata (2) in
[
5
]
*Section 1.7 implies that we can choose a Whittaker normalization such that
\pi\cong\pi(C,{\mathbbm{1}})
. Now Corollary
3.3
implies that
[S_{\pi(C_{0},{\mathbbm{1}})}:\pi]=1
.
Therefore,
\pi\cong\pi(C,{\mathbbm{1}})
and
\pi(C_{0},{\mathbbm{1}})
have the same cuspidal support, but since
\pi
is assumed to be supercuspidal, this is only possible if
C=C_{0}
and thus
V_{\lambda}=\{0\}.

∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Assume the Kazhdan-Lusztig hypothesis and the desiderata in
[
5
]
*Section 1.7. Then for any infinitesimal parameter
\lambda
of
G
, all of the irreducible representations in
\Pi_{\lambda}(G)
have isomorphic central characters.

Proof.

Let
C
be the open orbit of
V=V_{\lambda}
, and
\pi=\pi(C,\mathbbm{1})
.
Let
D\subseteq V
be any orbit and set
\sigma=\pi(D,\mathbbm{1})
. By Corollary
3.3
1) (which assumes the Kazhdan-Lusztig hypothesis),
\pi
is a subquotient of
S_{\sigma}
.
As
S_{\sigma}
admits a central character, both
\pi
and
\sigma
have the same central character, say
\omega
.
Desiderata
3
states that every representation in a given L-packet has the same central character, which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

If we assume that for any local
L
-parameter
\phi
and any irreducible representation
\pi\in\Pi_{\phi}(G)
, the central character
\omega_{\pi}
of
\pi
is given by the central character
\omega_{\phi}
constructed in
[
14
]
, it is possible to check Corollary
4.2
unconditionally, namely, to check
\omega_{\phi_{1}}=\omega_{\phi_{2}}
if
\lambda_{\phi_{1}}=\lambda_{\phi_{2}}
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
G
be a quasi-split connected reductive
p
-adic group.
Then, for any two cuspidal pairs
(M_{1},\sigma_{1})
and
(M_{2},\sigma_{2})
of
G
such that
M_{i}
contains the cetner

, the inclusions

induced embeddings
\iota_{i}:{}^{L}M_{i}\to{}^{L}G
.
Letting
\lambda_{i}
be the infinitesimal parameter of
\sigma_{i}
, if
\iota_{i}\circ\lambda_{1}
is equivalent to

, then the characters

Let
\omega_{i}:=\sigma_{i}|_{Z_{M_{i}}}

\sigma_{i}
, and observe that each parabolically induced representation

\delta_{P_{i}}^{1/2}\omega_{i}
, and thus any irreducible subrepresentation
\pi_{i}
of

also has central character
\delta_{P_{i}}^{1/2}\omega_{i}
.
Assuming the local Langlands correspondence, the infinitesimal parameter
\lambda_{i}
of
\pi_{i}
satisfies

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{L}G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{L}M}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda_{\pi_{i}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{i}}

Therefore, if
\iota_{1}\circ\lambda_{1}
is equivalent to
\iota_{2}\circ\lambda_{2}
, then
\lambda_{\pi_{1}}
is equivalent to

, and thus by Corollary

\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
have the same central character.
Since

\omega_{1}|_{Z(G)}\cong\omega_{2}|_{Z(G)}.

∎

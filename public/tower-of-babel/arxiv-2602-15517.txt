A Model Order Reduction Method for Seismic Applications Using the Laplace Transform
Fernando Henríquez, Matthias Schlottbom

Abstract
We devise and analyze a reduced basis model order reduction (MOR) strategy for an abstract wave problem with vanishing initial conditions and a source term given by the product of a temporal Ricker wavelet and a spatial profile. Such wave problems comprise the acoustic and elastic wave equations, with applications in seismic modeling. Motivated by recent Laplace-domain MOR methodologies, we construct reduced bases that approximate the time-domain solution with exponential accuracy. We prove convergence bounds that are explicit and robust with respect to the parameters controlling the Ricker wavelet’s shape and width and identify an intrinsic accuracy limit dictated by the wavelet’s value at the initial time. In particular, the resulting error bound is independent of the underlying Galerkin discretization space and yields computable criteria for the regime in which exponential convergence is observed.

Introduction

1.1
Motivation: Seismic Modeling

The computational modeling of seismic phenomena—through techniques such as seismic tomography

60
;
75
and full waveform inversion

8
;
76
—requires the solution of a

many-query
problem, i.e., repeated evaluations of a computational model representing the
underlying physical system. For realistic models, these repeated solves are computationally
demanding and often preclude real-time simulations.

Seismic wave propagation is typically modeled by the wave equation, which may describe
either acoustic or elastic waves, supplemented with appropriate initial conditions and
a source term represented by a nonzero right-hand side.
To capture the time dependence of realistic seismic sources, a common modeling choice is
the so-called Ricker wavelet, originally introduced in

62
;
63
, which provides a convenient representation of
the spectral properties of seismic phenomena.
The Ricker wavelet has been widely employed as a source term in computational seismic
modeling; see, for instance,

43
;
2
; see also
70
;
71
;
69
for applications in electromagnetic problems.
Among these contributions, the most recent work to our knowledge is
24
,
where a model order reduction (MOR) method for the parametric elastic wave equation is
proposed, based on the construction of a reduced basis in the Laplace domain, to efficiently compute time-domain seismograms for a two-dimensional subsurface model of Groningen, the Netherlands.
For current approaches tackling this particular problem, we refer to
23
for a model reduction approach based on eigenfunctions.

The main goal of this work is to devise and analyze a model order reduction (MOR)
strategy for an abstract wave problem, which could represent either acoustic or elastic, subject to
vanishing initial conditions and driven by a right-hand side defined as the product
of a temporal Ricker wavelet and a spatial profile.
While this setting shares structural similarities with the framework in
24
, we shift from parametric exploration to focus on the fundamental acceleration of the evolution problem itself.
Motivated by the MOR methodologies introduced in the recent works

25
;
26
, which similarly exploit the Laplace
transform for the construction of efficient reduced bases, we propose and
thoroughly analyze reduced basis schemes that approximate the solution of the
evolution problem with exponential accuracy, at a convergence rate that is robust
with respect to the parameters governing the shape and width of the Ricker wavelet.
This robustness is achieved by carefully exploiting the properties of the Ricker wavelet’s spectral content.
Notably, although the original problem is not parametric, after the application of the
Laplace transform, the Laplace variable enters as a parameter.
In view of this, we borrow ideas from model order reduction for parametric wave problems to accelerate single-instance evolution solves.

1.2
Model Order Reduction of Parametric PDEs and The Reduced Basis Method

Model order reduction (MOR) comprises a broad class of techniques designed to
reduce the computational cost associated with families of parameter-dependent
problems, such as parametric partial differential equations.
A central observation is that, although a high-fidelity discretization may be
very large, the associated
solution manifold
often possesses a low-dimensional
structure that can be accurately approximated.

Among MOR approaches, the reduced basis (RB) method is one of the most widely used
frameworks.
It is typically organized into an
offline
stage, in which representative
high-fidelity solutions (snapshots) are computed for selected parameter values,
and an
online
stage, in which new parameter instances are treated efficiently
by projecting onto the resulting reduced space.
A standard construction of the reduced space is proper orthogonal decomposition (POD)

6
;
45
;
48
, which extracts a basis
of prescribed dimension via a singular value decomposition of the snapshot matrix.
Alternatively, the basis can be generated by greedy procedures

36
;
11
;
10
;
12

(and, for evolution problems, POD–Greedy strategies

22
;
72
).

RB methods for stationary problems are by now well established, including rigorous
a posteriori error estimation and certification procedures; see

35
;
59
;
58
;
65
.
For time-dependent problems, a variety of extensions and alternative strategies have been proposed.
For example,
21
applies RB techniques to linear evolution equations
with a Finite Volume spatial discretization, while non-linear MOR based on local
reduced-order bases is discussed in
49
.
Further developments include structure-preserving RB methods for Hamiltonian systems

1
;
31
;
29
;
28
,
non-intrusive approaches based on radial basis function interpolation

5
;
79
, and more recently
data-driven

methodologies such as dynamic mode decomposition (DMD)

68
;
46
;
13
and operator learning

54
;
14
; see
32
for a broader overview.

It is worth noting that the construction of reduced-order models based on linear spaces
may be particularly challenging for wave- and transport-dominated problems.
In
18
, it was shown that the Kolmogorov
N
-width of the solution manifold
associated with a one-dimensional parametric wave equation admits the lower bound

N^{-\frac{1}{2}}
; related results for linear transport can also be found in
52
with refinements in
4
.
A slow decay of the Kolmogorov
N
-width suggests that achieving high accuracy may require reduced spaces of large dimension, which can undermine the efficiency of reduced basis methods as fast surrogates for the underlying parametric problem.
In order to
break
this phenomenon, which is usually referred as the
Kolmogorov barrier
56
, one needs to resort to
non-linear

MOR techniques. Among these, we highlight online adaptive basis
55
;
41
,
dynamic low-rank approximation
42
;
66
;
51
,
neural networks
47
, and the use of non-linear transformations to improve RB error

61
;
74
.
Furthermore, though no longer part of the realm of non-linear MOR techniques, an active line
of research in MOR reduction for transport-dominated parametric problems focuses on the stability
of reduced-order models, see, e.g.,
77
;
80
, also
78
;
53
;
40
for works on closure models, and
57
;
30
for symplectic RB construction.
For a comprehensive review of MOR techniques for time-dependent problems, we refer to
33
and, in particular for transport-dominated problems, the recent survey
34
.

1.3
The Laplace Transform for MOR of Evolution Problems

An alternative route to reduced-order modeling for evolution problems is to work in the
Laplace domain. Applying the Laplace transform to a time-dependent PDE yields a family of
time-independent problems that depend on the complex Laplace variable, which may be viewed as
an additional parameter. This viewpoint makes it possible to leverage established reduced basis
technology for stationary parametric problems.

This idea has been explored, for instance, in
20
for parametric linear
second-order parabolic equations, where the time-domain solution is recovered by numerically
inverting the Laplace transform via contour-deformation techniques
19
.
Related Laplace-domain reduced basis constructions have also been considered in

38
;
7
.
In
7
, the focus is on the wave equation,
for which contour-deformation strategies break down, and the inverse Laplace transform is instead
computed using Weeks’ method, see also
24
for an application of Weeks’ method in the context of elastic wave equations. We refer to
44
and the references there for an overview of numerical Laplace inversion techniques.

A central difficulty in such approaches is, however, the numerical inversion of the Laplace transform, which
may suffer from stability and accuracy issues, particularly over long time
intervals. The present work is motivated by these challenges and takes a first step toward a stable
and accurate reduced basis methodology for wave problems driven by Ricker wavelet source terms.

1.4
Contributions

In this work, we introduce a MOR technique for the abstract
non-parametric
wave equation
with vanishing initial conditions and a right-hand side given by the product of a spatial profile
and a temporal Ricker wavelet. As explained in Section
1.1
, this setting is
motivated by its relevance in the computational modeling of seismic phenomena.

For the construction of efficient reduced spaces, we follow the Laplace-transform-based approach
of
25
;
26
, in the following referred to as LT-MOR algorithm.
In contrast to the Laplace-domain MOR strategies
reviewed in Section
1.3
, we do not recover the time-domain solution by explicitly
computing an inverse Laplace transform (e.g., via contour deformation or related inversion
methods). This circumvents stability and accuracy issues associated with numerical Laplace
inversion.

Moreover,
25
;
26
show, for parabolic problems and the wave
equation, respectively, that reduced spaces constructed in the Laplace domain can be used
successfully for the underlying time-evolution problem and yield exponential convergence with
respect to both the reduced dimension and the number of Laplace-domain samples used to build the
basis. We note, however, that these analyses focus on reduced bases for semi-discrete problems
(discrete in space, continuous in time), whereas
16
provides a fully discrete
analysis for the parabolic case, including optimal time adaptivity.

From an algorithmic perspective, the proposed approach (building on

25
;
26
;
16
) follows the standard
offline–online decomposition commonly used in projection-based MOR and summarized in
Section
1.2
.

(i)

Offline stage.

Applying the Laplace transform to evolution problem leads to a family
of time-independent problems parametrized by the complex Laplace variable.
A set of sampling points is selected in the Laplace domain, and for each
sample the corresponding high-fidelity problem is solved after spatial
finite element discretization.
The resulting solutions are collected and compressed using a proper
orthogonal decomposition to construct a reduced basis of low dimension.

(ii)

Online stage.

Once the reduced basis has been constructed, the original time-dependent
problem is projected onto this space.
The resulting reduced system is then advanced in time using a suitable
time-integration scheme, leading to a significantly smaller system of
ordinary differential equations.

Within this framework, the online computations amount to updating only a small
number of reduced coefficients at each time step, in contrast to standard
time-stepping schemes that operate on the full set of degrees of freedom of the
high-fidelity model.
The computational cost of the offline stage is dominated by the solution of a
limited number of Laplace-domain problems, which can, however, be parallelized straightforwardly.
We emphasize that, in the proposed approach, the time-domain solution is obtained
without numerically inverting the Laplace transform.

Our main contributions are the following:

(i)

We prove that the solution of the abstract wave equation, under the aforementioned
assumptions on the data, can be approximated by finite-dimensional subspaces
with exponential accuracy, at a rate independent of the parameters of the Ricker
wavelet (peak arrival time
t_{0}
and width
\alpha
), up to a threshold determined by the
values of the Ricker wavelet and its time derivative at
t=0
.
These quantities, in turn, decay like
e^{-(\alpha t_{0})^{2}}
, up to multiplicative powers of
\alpha
.

(ii)

We provide an explicit algorithm for the practical construction of a finite-dimensional
space realizing the previously discussed exponential convergence.
A key tool is the use of the so-called sinc quadrature for the selection of points
in the Laplace domain, which in turn define the snapshots to be computed.
We show that, with this strategy, the discretization error in the Laplace domain
also decays exponentially, with a rate that again does not depend on the parameters
of the Ricker wavelet.

A few remarks are in place. Firstly, our analysis is independent of the underlying Galerkin
discretization space and is valid in an infinite-dimensional setting. Secondly, even though we do
not consider the parametric problem as in, e.g.,
24
, our work provides a first
comprehensive analysis of the dynamics of this particular wave propagation model, which is of
central importance in seismic modeling.
The construction of a reduced model for the parametric counterpart
of the problem considered here—for instance, involving layered media with
piecewise-constant material properties following prescribed statistical
distributions—naturally builds upon the theoretical and computational tools developed in this work.

We consider the abstract linear, second-order evolution equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}u(t)+\mathsf{A}u(t)

(2.2)

\displaystyle u(0)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(0)

Once
\mathcal{V}_{R,M}^{(\mathrm{rb})}
has been constructed, we use a Galerkin approximation of (
2.2
)–(
2.4
) to find an approximation
u_{R,M}^{(\mathrm{rb})}:[0,T]\to\mathcal{V}_{R,M}^{(\mathrm{rb})}
of the solution
u
as follows.

Problem 1
(Reduced semi-discrete problem)

We seek
u^{\textrm{(rb)}}_{R,M}\in\mathcal{W}_{\mu}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathcal{H},\mathcal{V}_{R,M}^{(\mathrm{rb})}\right)
, for some
\mu>0
, such that for all
t>0
it holds that

\displaystyle\left(\partial^{2}_{t}u_{R,M}^{\textrm{(rb)}}(t),v_{R,M}^{\textrm{(rb)}}\right)_{\mathcal{H}}+\left\langle\mathsf{A}u_{R,M}^{(\mathrm{rb})}(t),v_{R,M}^{(\mathrm{rb})}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}\times\mathcal{V}}=\left(f(t),v_{R,M}^{(\mathrm{rb})}\right)_{\mathcal{H}}\quad\forall v_{R,M}^{\textrm{(rb)}}\in\mathcal{V}_{R,M}^{(\mathrm{rb})},

(3.1)

equipped with vanishing initial conditions, i.e.,

u_{R,M}^{(\mathrm{rb})}(0)=0
and
\partial_{t}u_{R,M}^{(\mathrm{rb})}(0)=0
.

We note that, since
\mathcal{V}_{R,M}^{(\mathrm{rb})}
is finite dimensional, (
3.1
) amounts to a linear ordinary differential equation, which has a unique solution by the Picard-Lindelöf theorem.

For the construction of a suitable approximation space
\mathcal{V}_{R,M}^{(\mathrm{rb})}
, we consider an auxiliary problem in the Laplace domain that corresponds to a lossy Helmholtz equation
with a right-hand side.

Problem 2
(Frequency-controlled auxiliary problem)

Let
\mu>0
.
For each
s\in\Pi_{\mu}
,
we seek
\widehat{U}^{(2)}(s)\in\mathcal{V}
such that
1
1

1

The superscript is used to highlight that we target the second derivative.

s^{2}\widehat{U}^{(2)}(s)+\mathsf{A}\widehat{U}^{(2)}(s)=\mathcal{B}\{\partial^{2}_{t}q\}(s)\,p.

(3.2)

We emphasize that the source term in Problem
2
corresponds to the bilateral Laplace transform of the second temporal derivative of the source term
f
.
Below, we derive an error bound for
\widehat{U}^{(2)}(s)-\mathcal{L}\{\partial_{t}^{2}u\}(s)
, which shows that this difference becomes small for suitable choices of the parameters
\alpha
and
t_{0}
that determine the shape and location of the Ricker wavelet
q
. Moreover, we show that the rapid decay of
\mathcal{B}\{q\}(\mu+\imath\tau)
for fixed
\mu>0
and as
|\tau|\to\infty
implies the corresponding decay of
\widehat{U}^{(2)}(\mu+\imath\tau)
as
|\tau|\to\infty
.
As a consequence, the smooth function
\tau\mapsto\widehat{U}^{(2)}(\mu+\imath\tau)
can be efficiently approximated by sampling at a finite set of points

\mathcal{P}\coloneqq\{s_{j}\coloneqq\mu+\imath\tau_{j}:j=1,\ldots,M\},\quad M\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.3)

To achieve further compression, we construct the space
\mathcal{V}^{\text{(rb)}}_{R,M}
as follows

\mathcal{V}^{\text{(rb)}}_{R,M}=\argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{V}_{R}\subset\mathcal{V}\\
\text{dim}(\mathcal{V}_{R})\leq R\end{subarray}}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\omega_{j}\left\lVert\mathrm{Re}\left\{\widehat{U}^{(2)}(s_{j})\right\}-\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{R}}\mathrm{Re}\left\{\widehat{U}^{(2)}(s_{j})\right\}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{V}},

(3.4)

where
\{\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{M}\}
are (strictly positive) weights
and
\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{R}}:\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}_{R}

denotes the
\mathcal{V}
-orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{V}_{R}
.

Remark 1

The reason why we only consider the real part of the snapshots

\left\{\widehat{U}^{(2)}(s_{1}),\dots,\widehat{U}^{(2)}(s_{M})\right\}
in (
3.4
) is thoroughly discussed
in
(
25
, Section 5.1)
.

3.2
Practical Implementation

In order to realize the proposed method, in the following, let
\mathcal{V}_{h}\subset\mathcal{V}
be a finite-dimensional Galerkin discretization space of dimension
N_{h}
. Consider a basis

\{\varphi_{j,h}\}_{j=1}^{N_{h}}
for
\mathcal{V}_{h}
. The minimization problem stated in
(
3.4
) admits the following algebraic form:

\bm{\Phi}^{\textrm{(rb)}}_{R,M}=\arg\min_{\bm{\Phi}\in\mathscr{V}_{R}}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\omega_{j}\left\lVert\mathrm{Re}\{\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{U}}}_{h}^{(2)}(s_{j})\}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{h}\mathrm{Re}\{\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{U}}}_{h}^{(2)}(s_{j})\}\right\rVert^{2}_{\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{h}},

(3.5)

where

\left\lVert\bm{\mathsf{v}}\right\rVert_{\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{h}}=\sqrt{\left(\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{h}\bm{\mathsf{v}},\bm{\mathsf{v}}\right)_{\mathbb{C}^{N_{h}}}},\qquad\bm{\mathsf{v}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{h}},

and

\mathscr{V}_{R}\coloneqq\left\{\bm{\Phi}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{h}\times R}:\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{h}\bm{\Phi}=\bm{\mathsf{I}}_{R}\right\}.

(3.6)

Here, we identify each
\widehat{U}^{(2)}_{h}(s)\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
, i.e., the Galerkin solution to
Problem
2
in
\mathcal{V}_{h}
, with its vector of degrees of freedom

\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{U}}}_{h}^{(2)}(s)\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{h}}
in the basis
\{\varphi_{j,h}\}_{j=1}^{N_{h}}
.

The connection between the solution to (
3.4
) and (
3.5
)
is as follows. Set

\varphi^{\textrm{(rb)}}_{k,M}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{N_{h}}\left(\bm{\phi}^{\textrm{(rb)}}_{k,M}\right)_{j}\,\varphi_{j,h}\in\mathcal{V},\qquad k=1,\dots,R,

(3.7)

where

\bm{\Phi}^{\textrm{(rb)}}_{R,M}=\left(\bm{\phi}^{\textrm{(rb)}}_{1,M},\dots,\bm{\phi}^{\textrm{(rb)}}_{R,M}\right),

and
\left(\bm{\phi}^{\textrm{(rb)}}_{k,M}\right)_{j}
signifies the
j
-th component of

\bm{\phi}^{\textrm{(rb)}}_{k}
. Then
\left\{\varphi^{\textrm{(rb)}}_{1,M},\ldots,\varphi^{\textrm{(rb)}}_{R,M}\right\}

is an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{V}_{R,M}^{(\textrm{rb})}
in the
\mathcal{V}
-inner product.
Set

\bm{\mathsf{S}}\coloneqq\left(\mathrm{Re}\{\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{U}}}_{h}(s_{1})\},\dots,\mathrm{Re}\{\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{U}}}_{h}(s_{M})\}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{h}\times M},

(3.8)

and

\bm{\mathsf{D}}\coloneqq\text{diag}\left(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{M}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times M}.

Let
\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{h}=\bm{\mathsf{R}}_{h}^{\top}\bm{\mathsf{R}}_{h}
be the Cholesky
decomposition of
\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{h}
, where
\bm{\mathsf{R}}_{h}
is an upper triangular matrix.
Furthermore, consider

\widetilde{\bm{\mathsf{S}}}=\bm{\mathsf{R}}_{h}\,\bm{\mathsf{S}}\,\bm{\mathsf{D}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

together with its singular value decomposition (SVD)

\widetilde{\bm{\mathsf{S}}}=\widetilde{\bm{\mathsf{U}}}\widetilde{\Sigma}\widetilde{\bm{\mathsf{V}}}^{\top}
,
where

\widetilde{\bm{\mathsf{U}}}=\left(\widetilde{\bm{\zeta}}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\bm{\zeta}}_{N_{h}}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{h}\times N_{h}},\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{\bm{\mathsf{V}}}=\left(\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\bm{\psi}}_{M}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times M}

(3.9)

are orthogonal matrices, referred to as the left and right singular vectors of

\widetilde{\bm{\mathsf{S}}}
, respectively, and

\widetilde{\Sigma}=\operatorname{diag}\left(\widetilde{\sigma}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\sigma}_{r},0,\ldots,0\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{h}\times M},\qquad\widetilde{\sigma}_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\widetilde{\sigma}_{r}>0,

where
r=\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde{\bm{\mathsf{S}}})\leq\min\{N_{h},M\}
.

According to the Schmidt–Eckart–Young theorem (see, e.g.,

(
59
, Proposition 6.2)
), for any
R\leq r
the solution

\bm{\Phi}^{\textrm{(rb)}}_{R}
to (
3.5
) is given by

\bm{\Phi}^{\textrm{(rb)}}_{R,M}=\left(\bm{\mathsf{R}}^{-1}_{h}\widetilde{\bm{\zeta}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\mathsf{R}}^{-1}_{h}\widetilde{\bm{\zeta}}_{R}\right),

(3.10)

which consists of the first
R
left singular vectors of
\widetilde{\bm{\mathsf{S}}}

multiplied from the left by
\bm{\mathsf{R}}^{-1}_{h}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{\Phi}\in\mathscr{V}_{R}}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\omega_{j}\left\lVert\mathrm{Re}\left\{\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{U}}}_{h}^{(2)}(s_{j})\right\}-\bm{\Phi}\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{h}\mathrm{Re}\left\{\widehat{\bm{\mathsf{U}}}_{h}^{(2)}(s_{j})\right\}\right\rVert^{2}_{\bm{\mathsf{B}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=R+1}^{r}\widetilde{\sigma}_{j}^{2}.

(3.11)

Remark 2

We emphasize that the presented results do not depend on the dimension of
\mathcal{V}_{h}
. Also, it is easy to check that all results hold true if
\mathcal{V}
is replaced by
\mathcal{V}_{h}
.

4
Convergence Analysis

We present a complete analysis of the LT-MOR method tailored for

.
To this end, we discuss properties of the evolution problem (

)–(
2.4
) with more general initial conditions and source terms than the one in Section
4.1
.
Next, in Section

, we recall important results concerning sinc interpolation
and quadrature.
In Section

, we quantify the approximation error of
\partial_{t}^{2}u

using finite-dimensional subspaces of dimension

, which is based on the combination of three key ingredients:

(i)

A bound for the consistency error between the Laplace transform of
\partial_{t}^{2}u
and the solution

to Problem

(ii)

The behavior of the solution
\widehat{U}^{(2)}(s)
for large

(iii)

The sinc interpolation error bound recalled ahead in Section

.

Finally, in Section

4.1
Auxiliary Laplace domain problem

We apply the Laplace transform to the evolution problem (
2.2
)–(
2.4
).
Since the Laplace transform employs complex Hilbert spaces, we understand with slight abuse of notation
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{V}
as their corresponding complexifications.

For the sake of convenience in our analysis, we consider the following auxiliary problem in the
Laplace domain with general data.

Problem 3
(Laplace Domain Auxiliary Problem)

Let
\mu>0
,
\phi_{0}\in\mathcal{V}
,
\phi_{1}\in\mathcal{H}
, and

f\in L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathcal{H})
.
For each
s\in\Pi_{\mu}
, we seek
\widehat{\phi}(s)\in\mathcal{V}
such that

(s^{2}+\mathsf{A})\widehat{\phi}(s)=\widehat{f}(s)+s\phi_{0}+\phi_{1},

(4.1)

where
\widehat{f}(s)=\mathcal{L}\{f\}(s)\in\mathscr{H}^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{H})
,
for
s\in\Pi_{\mu}
, (cf. Theorem
2.1
, Paley-Wiener).

To analyze Problem
3
, we define the following norm

\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\varrho}\coloneq\sqrt{\varrho^{2}\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}+\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}},\quad v\in\mathcal{V},

(4.2)

for a real parameter
\varrho>0
,
and the corresponding induced norm for the operator
\mathsf{B}:\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}^{\star}

\left\lVert\mathsf{B}\right\rVert_{\varrho}\coloneqq\sup_{w,v\in\mathcal{V}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\left\lvert\left\langle\mathsf{B}w,v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}\times\mathcal{V}}\right\rvert}{\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\varrho}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\varrho}},\quad\mathsf{B}\in\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\star}).

(4.3)

We note that in either case
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\varrho}
is equivalent to
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}}
and

\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\star})}
respectively.

We are interested in bounding the inf-sup constant

\gamma(s)\coloneqq\inf_{w\in\mathcal{V}\backslash\{0\}}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{V}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\left\lvert\left\langle(s^{2}+\mathsf{A})w,v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}\times\mathcal{V}}\right\rvert}{\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}}

(4.4)

explicitly in terms of
s\in\Pi_{\mu}
.
As in
50
, one can show the following result.

Lemma 1

Let
\mu>0
be given. For each
s\in\Pi_{\mu}
, one has

\gamma(s)\geq\frac{{\mathrm{Re}\{s\}}}{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}\min\{1,c_{\mathsf{A}}\}>0,

(4.5)

where
c_{\mathsf{A}}
is the coercivity constant from (
2.6
).

Proof

We follow the steps of the proof given in
50
, which in turn uses that of
15
,
for convenience of the reader.
Let
w\in\mathcal{V}
be given and set
v=\frac{s}{|s|}w
. Observe that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\langle(s^{2}+\mathsf{A})w,v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}\times\mathcal{V}}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\geq\left\lvert\mathrm{Re}\left\{\left\langle(s^{2}+\mathsf{A})w,v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}\times\mathcal{V}}\right\}\right\rvert

(4.6)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\mathrm{Re}\{s\}}}{|s|}\left(|s|^{2}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\mathrm{Re}\left\langle\mathsf{A}w,w\right\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}\times\mathcal{V}}\right),

which proves the claim by (
2.6
).

Proposition 2

Let
\mu>0
and assume that
\phi_{0},\phi_{1}\in\mathcal{V}
and
\mathsf{A}\phi_{0},\mathsf{A}\phi_{1}\in\mathcal{H}
.
Then, for each
s\in\Pi_{\mu}
, there exists a unique

\widehat{\phi}(s)\in\mathcal{V}
solution to Problem

3
satisfying

\left\lVert\widehat{\phi}(s)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}}\leq\frac{\left\lVert\phi_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}}}{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}+\frac{\left\lVert\phi_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}}}{\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{2}}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Re}\{s\}\min\{1,c_{\mathsf{A}}\}}\left(\left\lVert\widehat{f}(s)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}+\frac{\left\lVert\mathsf{A}\phi_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}}{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}+\frac{\left\lVert\mathsf{A}\phi_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}}{\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{2}}\right)

(4.7)

and

\left\lVert s\widehat{\phi}(s)-\phi_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\frac{\left\lVert\phi_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}}{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Re}\{s\}\min\{1,c_{\mathsf{A}}\}}\left(\left\lVert\widehat{f}(s)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}+\frac{\left\lVert\mathsf{A}\phi_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}}{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}+\frac{\left\lVert\mathsf{A}\phi_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}}{\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{2}}\right)

(4.8)

together with

\displaystyle\left\lVert s^{2}\widehat{\phi}(s)-s\phi_{0}-\phi_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}}\leq

\displaystyle\left\lVert\widehat{f}(s)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}}+C_{\mathsf{A}}\left(\frac{\left\lVert\phi_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}}}{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}+\frac{\left\lVert\phi_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}}}{\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{2}}\right)

(4.9)

\displaystyle+\frac{C_{\mathsf{A}}}{{\mathrm{Re}\{s\}}\min\{1,c_{\mathsf{A}}\}}\left(\left\lVert\widehat{f}(s)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}+\frac{\left\lVert\mathsf{A}\phi_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}}{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}+\frac{\left\lVert\mathsf{A}\phi_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}}{\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{2}}\right).

Furthermore, the map
\Pi_{\mu}\ni s\mapsto\widehat{\phi}(s)\in\mathcal{V}

is holomorphic.

Proof

The operator
s^{2}+\mathsf{A}:\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}^{\star}

is continuous according to

\left\lVert s^{2}+\mathsf{A}\right\rVert_{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}\leq\max\{1,C_{\mathsf{A}}\},

(4.10)

where the operator norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}
has been defined
in (
4.3
) with
\rho=\left\lvert s\right\rvert
, and with
C_{\mathsf{A}}
as in (
2.6
).
It follows, also due to Lemma
1
and
(
67
, Thm. 2.1.44)

for each
s\in\Pi_{\mu}
, that there exists a unique

\widehat{\phi}(s)\in\mathcal{V}
solution to Problem
3
satisfying

\displaystyle\left\lVert\widehat{\phi}(s)\right\rVert_{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}{{\mathrm{Re}\{s\}}\min\{1,c_{\mathsf{A}}\}}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{V}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\left\lvert\left\langle\widehat{f}(s)+s\phi_{0}+\phi_{1},v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}\times\mathcal{V}}\right\rvert}{\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{{\mathrm{Re}\{s\}}\min\{1,c_{\mathsf{A}}\}}\left(\left\lVert\widehat{f}(s)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}+\left\lvert s\right\rvert\left\lVert\phi_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}+\left\lVert\phi_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\right).

Next, recalling that we have assumed
\phi_{0},\phi_{1}\in\mathcal{V}
, we define for each
s\in\Pi_{\mu}

\widehat{w}(s)\coloneqq\widehat{\phi}(s)-\frac{\phi_{0}}{s}-\frac{\phi_{1}}{s^{2}}\in\mathcal{V},

(4.12)

which is solution to

(s^{2}+\mathsf{A})\widehat{w}(s)=\widehat{f}(s)-\frac{\mathsf{A}\phi_{0}}{s}-\frac{\mathsf{A}\phi_{1}}{s^{2}}\in\mathcal{H}.

(4.13)

Recalling Lemma
1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert\widehat{w}(s)\right\rVert_{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}{{\mathrm{Re}\{s\}}\min\{1,c_{\mathsf{A}}\}}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{V}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\left\lvert\left\langle\widehat{f}(s)-\frac{\mathsf{A}\phi_{0}}{s}-\frac{\mathsf{A}\phi_{1}}{s^{2}},v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}\times\mathcal{V}}\right\rvert}{\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\left\lvert v\right\rvert}}

(4.14)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{{\mathrm{Re}\{s\}}\min\{1,c_{\mathsf{A}}\}}\left(\left\lVert\widehat{f}(s)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}+\frac{\left\lVert\mathsf{A}\phi_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}}{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}+\frac{\left\lVert\mathsf{A}\phi_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}}{\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{2}}\right),

which combined with (
4.12
) yields (
4.7
).

Next, we calculate

\left\lVert s\widehat{\phi}(s)-\phi_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\left\lvert s\right\rvert\left\lVert\widehat{w}(s)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}+\frac{\left\lVert\phi_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}}{\left\lvert s\right\rvert},

(4.15)

which combined with (
4.12
) yields (
4.8
). Finally, we have that

\left\lVert s^{2}\widehat{\phi}(s)-s\phi_{0}-\phi_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}}=\left\lVert s^{2}\widehat{w}(s)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}}=\sup_{v\in\mathcal{V}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\left\lvert\left\langle s^{2}\widehat{w},v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}\times\mathcal{V}}\right\rvert}{\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}}},

(4.16)

thus using (
4.12
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert s^{2}\widehat{\phi}(s)-s\phi_{0}-\phi_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{v\in\mathcal{V}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\left\lvert\left\langle\widehat{f}(s)-\frac{\mathsf{A}\phi_{0}}{s}-\frac{\mathsf{A}\phi_{1}}{s^{2}}-\mathsf{A}\widehat{w}(s),v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}\times\mathcal{V}}\right\rvert}{\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}}}

(4.17)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\widehat{f}(s)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}}+\frac{\left\lVert\mathsf{A}\phi_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}}}{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}+\frac{\left\lVert\mathsf{A}\phi_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}}}{\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{2}}+\left\lVert\mathsf{A}\widehat{w}(s)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\widehat{f}(s)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}}+C_{\mathsf{A}}\left(\frac{\left\lVert\phi_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}}}{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}+\frac{\left\lVert\phi_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}}}{\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{2}}+\left\lVert\widehat{w}(s)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}}\right),

which, together with (
4.14

4.9

The map
\Pi_{\mu}\ni s\mapsto s^{2}+\mathsf{A}\in\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\star})

where
\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\star})
corresponds to the Banach space of bounded linear operators
from
\mathcal{V}
to
\mathcal{V}^{\star}
equipped with the standard operator norm

\left\lVert\mathsf{B}\right\rVert_{\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\star}).}\coloneqq\sup_{w,v\in\mathcal{V}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\left\lvert\left\langle\mathsf{B}w,v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\star}\times\mathcal{V}}\right\rvert}{\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}}},\quad\mathsf{B}\in\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\star}).

(4.18)

The map

\mathscr{L}_{\text{iso}}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\star})\subset\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\star})\rightarrow\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\star}):\mathsf{B}\mapsto\mathsf{B}^{-1}

is holomorphic as stated in

, where

Furthermore, as a consequence of Theorem

(Paley-Wiener),

\Pi_{\mu}\ni s\mapsto\mathcal{L}\{f\}(s)\in\mathcal{H}
is analytic as well. Therefore, we may conclude that

\Pi_{\mu}\ni s\mapsto\widehat{\phi}(s)\in\mathcal{V}
is holomorphic as the composition of holomorphic maps.

As a direct consequence of Proposition
2
and Theorem
2.1
, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 1

Let
\mu>0
and assume that
\phi_{0},\phi_{1}\in\mathcal{V}
and
\mathsf{A}\phi_{0},\mathsf{A}\phi_{1}\in\mathcal{H}
. Then,

\widehat{\phi}(s)\in\mathscr{H}^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{V}),\quad s\widehat{\phi}(s)-\phi_{0}\in\mathscr{H}^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{H}),\quad\text{and}\quad s^{2}\widehat{\phi}(s)-s\phi_{0}-\phi_{1}\in\mathscr{H}^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{V}^{\star}),

(4.20)

and

\displaystyle\left\lVert\widehat{\phi}(s)\right\rVert_{\mathscr{H}^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{V})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\left\lVert\phi_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}}}{\sqrt{\mu}}+\frac{\left\lVert\phi_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{V}}}{\sqrt{\mu^{3}}}+\frac{1}{\mu\min\{1,c_{\mathsf{A}}\}}\left(\left\lVert\widehat{f}\right\rVert_{\mathscr{H}^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{H})}+\frac{\left\lVert\mathsf{A}\phi_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}}{\sqrt{\mu}}+\frac{\left\lVert\mathsf{A}\phi_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}}{\sqrt{\mu^{3}}}\right)

Lemma 6

Let
\mu>0
be given and assume

t_{0}\geq 2\frac{\alpha+\mu}{\alpha^{2}}
.
It holds that

\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left\lvert\partial^{2}_{t}q(t)\right\rvert e^{-\mu t}\text{d}t\lesssim\alpha e^{-\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}t_{0}-1\right)^{2}}.

(4.75)

Proof

Firstly, observe that

{\partial^{2}_{t}q(t)}={\partial^{4}_{t}g(t)}=-\frac{2}{\alpha^{2}}\partial^{4}_{t}e^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}(t-t_{0})^{2}},

(4.76)

with
g(t)
is as in (
4.35
). Therefore, one has that

{\partial^{2}_{t}q(t)}=-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{8}H_{4}\left(\alpha\frac{t-t_{0}}{2}\right)e^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}(t-t_{0})^{2}},

(4.77)

where
H_{n}(x)\coloneqq(-1)^{n}e^{x^{2}}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}e^{-x^{2}}
corresponds to the
n
-th Hermite polynomial. Thus,

\left\lvert H_{4}\left(\alpha\frac{t-t_{0}}{2}\right)\right\rvert\lesssim e^{\alpha\left\lvert t-t_{0}\right\rvert}.

(4.78)

Therefore, assuming
t_{0}>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left\lvert\partial^{2}_{t}q(t)\right\rvert e^{-\mu t}\text{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}(t-t_{0})^{2}-\alpha(t-t_{0})-\mu t}\text{d}t

(4.79)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}e^{-\mu t_{0}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}(t-t_{0})^{2}-(\alpha+\mu)(t-t_{0})}\text{d}t

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}e^{-\mu t_{0}+\frac{(\alpha+\mu)^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}(t-t_{0})+\frac{(\alpha+\mu)}{\alpha}\right)^{2}}\text{d}t

\displaystyle=\alpha e^{-\mu t_{0}+\frac{(\alpha+\mu)^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}}\int_{-\infty}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}t_{0}+\frac{(\alpha+\mu)}{\alpha}}e^{-\tau^{2}}\text{d}\tau.

Next, we calculate

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}t_{0}+\frac{(\alpha+\mu)}{\alpha})}e^{-\tau^{2}}\text{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\left(\text{erf}\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2}t_{0}+\frac{(\alpha+\mu)}{\alpha}\right)+1\right)

(4.80)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\left(-\text{erf}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}t_{0}-\frac{(\alpha+\mu)}{\alpha}\right)+1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\text{erfc}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}t_{0}-\frac{(\alpha+\mu)}{\alpha}\right).

For

, it holds that
\text{erfc}(x)\leq e^{-x^{2}}
,
thus yielding

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left\lvert\partial^{2}_{t}q(t)\right\rvert e^{-\mu t}\text{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha e^{-\mu t_{0}+\frac{(\alpha+\mu)^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}}e^{-\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}t_{0}-\frac{(\alpha+\mu)}{\alpha}\right)^{2}}

(4.81)

\displaystyle=\alpha e^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}t^{2}_{0}+\alpha t_{0}}

and concluding the proof.

Remark 3

Remark 4
(Dependence of the convergence rate in Theorem
5.1

on
\alpha
and
t_{0}
)

In view of Lemma
6
, and as discussed previously in Remark
3
, we examine the structure of the error estimate in Theorem
5.1
.
In this case, the bound stated in (
5.18
) consists of three main contributions:
(i) exponential convergence with respect to
R
, i.e., the dimension of the sought finite-dimensional space,
(ii) exponential convergence with respect to
M
, i.e., the number of samples in the Laplace domain, and
(iii) a consistency term decaying exponentially with
\alpha t_{0}
.

More precisely, selecting
\mu=\alpha
and
\eta=\mu/2
, we obtain

\left\lVert u-u^{\text{(rb)}}_{R,M}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathfrak{I};\mathcal{V})}+\left\lVert\partial_{t}\left(u-u^{\text{(rb)}}_{R,M}\right)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathfrak{I};\mathcal{H})}\lesssim e^{\alpha T}\left(e^{-\left(\frac{\pi R}{4}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}-\frac{\alpha t_{0}}{2}}+e^{-\left(\frac{\pi M}{2}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}-\frac{\alpha t_{0}}{2}}+e^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}t_{0}^{2}}\right),

(5.23)

where the hidden constant depends polynomially on
\alpha
,
T
, and
p
.

As in Remark
3
, this estimate shows that the exponential convergence rate with respect to
R
is independent of the parameters characterizing the Ricker wavelet,
\alpha
and
t_{0}
, provided that the error remains larger than the consistency term, which scales like
e^{\alpha T-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}t_{0}^{2}}
. In addition, the fully discrete scheme exhibits exponential convergence with respect to the number of Laplace-domain samples
M

6.6
Discussion

We conclude by discussing the three performance criteria introduced at the beginning of Section
6
.

(i)

Accuracy with respect to the high-fidelity solution.

Figures
2
–
5
show a rapid decay of the relative error as the reduced dimension
R
increases, in line with the exponential best-approximation bound established in Lemma
5

and in Theorem
5.1
when considering the fully discrete setting. In practice, this regime is observed once the Laplace variable is sampled sufficiently fine; otherwise, the error due to the discretization of the Laplace-domain quantities becomes dominant (see Section
5
, in particular Lemma
7
and subsequently Theorem
5.1
).

(ii)

Accuracy with respect to the number of snapshots in the offline phase.

The same figures also highlight that the attainable accuracy for fixed
R
is influenced by the number of Laplace-domain samples. Section
5
quantifies this effect: Lemma
7
provides a concrete sampling strategy and shows an (essentially) exponential decay of the sampling error as
M
increases, until the reduced-basis truncation error becomes dominant. We recall that, following the symmetry considerations discussed in
(
25
, Section 5.4)
, we effectively compute only
M+1
samples instead of
2M+1
.

(iii)

Speed-up.

The timing breakdown in Figure
6
indicates that the offline phase (cf. Section
3
) is dominated by the independent Laplace-domain solves, while the online stage amounts to solving the reduced problem (Problem
1
)—that is, dense systems of size
R\times R
at each time step—and reconstructing the solution in
\mathcal{V}_{h}
. In the configurations reported in Figure
6
(total bars), the overall wall-clock time is reduced from
50.8\,
s (high-fidelity) to
3.56\,
s,
4.75\,
s,
5.71\,
s,
6.61\,
s, and
8.24\,
s (LT-MOR), corresponding to speed-ups of approximately
14.3\times
,
10.7\times
,
8.9\times
,
7.7\times
, and
6.2\times
, respectively. The reduced solve has cost
\mathcal{O}(R^{3})
per time step and the reconstruction
\mathcal{O}(RN_{t})
overall, which is small compared with the cost of computing the Laplace-domain samples. Since the Laplace-domain solves are embarrassingly parallel, additional wall-clock savings can be expected if these computations are distributed.

7
Concluding Remarks

In this work we introduced and analyzed a Laplace-transform-based reduced basis (LT-MOR)
strategy for abstract second-order wave problems with vanishing initial conditions and a
separable forcing term given by the product of a temporal Ricker wavelet and a spatial
profile. Working in the Laplace domain turns the evolution problem into a family of
parameter-dependent, stationary problems in the complex Laplace variable, which allows one
to construct reduced spaces using standard snapshot/POD technology and to recover the
time-domain solution via any suitable time-stepping scheme.

On the theoretical side, we proved exponential convergence rates for the LT-MOR
approximation and established bounds that are robust with respect to the parameters
controlling the Ricker wavelet’s shape and width. Our analysis also identifies an intrinsic
accuracy barrier governed by the value of the wavelet at the initial time, thereby providing
a computable criterion for the regime in which exponential decay of the error can be
expected. Importantly, the resulting estimates are independent of the underlying Galerkin
discretization space and hence decouple the MOR approximation properties from the specific
high-fidelity solver.

The numerical experiments for the scalar wave equation corroborate the theoretical
predictions: the reduced solutions achieve high accuracy with comparatively small reduced
dimensions, and the offline–online decomposition yields substantial speed-ups relative to
the high-fidelity time stepping while maintaining controlled errors over the full time
interval of interest.

Several extensions are natural and will be addressed in future work. First, it is of
practical interest to incorporate additional physical and/or geometric parameters (e.g.,
material coefficients or source locations), leading to a genuinely parametric setting and
to reduced space constructions based on POD–Greedy or related certified greedy strategies.
Second, it would be worthwhile to generalize the approach to alternative seismic source
models beyond the Ricker wavelet, for instance exponentially decaying wavelets such as the
Küpper wavelet used in mantle-structure simulations
39
. Finally, extending
the methodology and its analysis to more complex wave systems (e.g., elastic formulations),
and to adaptive sampling strategies in the Laplace domain, are directions of current and
future research on this topic.

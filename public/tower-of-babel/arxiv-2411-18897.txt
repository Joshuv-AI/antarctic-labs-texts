A database of constructions of Hadamard matrices
Matteo Cati, Dmitrii V. Pasechnik

Abstract
Hadamard matrices of order n n are conjectured to exist whenever n n is 1 1 , 2 2 , or a multiple of 4 4 ; a similar conjecture exists for skew Hadamard matrices. We provide constructions covering orders ≤ 1208 \leq 1208 of all known Hadamard and skew Hadamard matrices in the open-source software SageMath. This allowed us to verify the correctness of results given in the literature. Within this range, just one order, 292 292 , of a skew Hadamard matrix claimed to have a known construction, required a fix. We also produce the up to date tables, for n ≤ 2999 n\leq 2999 (resp. n ≤ 999 n\leq 999 for skew case), of the minimum exponents m m such that a (skew) Hadamard matrix of order 2 m ​ n 2^{m}n is known, improving over 100 entries in the previously published sources. We explain how tables’ entries are related to Riesel numbers. As a by-product of the latter, we show that the Paley constructions of (skew-)Hadamard matrices do not work for the order 2 m ​ 509203 2^{m}509203 , for any m m .

A

Matrices of this type were described for the first time by J. Sylvester
[

[

Additionally, a Hadamard matrix is

, where

S^{\top}=-S

These matrices are used in many practical applications, such as data compression
[

[

[

1

[

However, given that many constructions exist, each covering only some orders, finding the correct one for a specific order is often difficult.

The purpose of this paper is checking, detailing, and “digitally preserving” a number of constructions, and tables specifying known constructions and orders. For the latter in particular, we updated about 100 entries in two tables in J. Seberry’s and M. Yamada’s monograph
[

We provide all the necessary constructions in the open-source mathematical software SageMath
[

Related work

Implementing constructions is a first step towards a formal verification of them, using a proof assistant. Work towards
such a verification has been done by Lu-Ming Zhang (supervised by the 2nd author),
for constructions of Hadamard matrices covering all the possible orders up to 112
[
60
]
. The latter
demonstrated challenges in formal checking of constructions of Hadamard matrices. Currently, proof assistants are
severely deficient in doing even slightly nontrivial calculations, and closing this lacuna is an active research area. Previous efforts to combine proof assistants and computer algebra systems (CASs, for short),
e.g.

[
3
,
10
,
34
]

have demonstrated that one either has to trust the CAS and thereby significantly lower correctness guarantees, or severely hamper the power of CAS by insisting that it produce verifiable certificates (which CASs are not designed to do at present).

S. London in his PhD thesis
[
35
]
outlined constructions of matrices
for all known orders not exceeding
1000
, although he did not provide an
implementation. We took from [loc.cit.] data for a number of small
Williamson type Hadamard matrices. The remainder of our work is independent of [loc.cit.]—in particular as
it did not touch the skew case.

Riesel numbers, in conjunction with the asymptotic existence of
Hadamard matrices, were used by W. de Launey
[
11
]
. Our application is
different, see Sect
6.1
for more details.

4.1
Paley’s Constructions

In 1933, Paley
[
38
]
discovered two constructions of Hadamard matrices.
They use the matrix
\Pi
from Sect.
2.4

and are as follows, with
\overline{1}
denoting the column vector
of all 1s.

Theorem 1
.

Let
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, and a (generalised) Paley digraph, cf. Sect.
2.4
,
of order
q
exists (e.g.
q
is a prime power).
Then there is a skew Hadamard matrix
H
of order
q+1
,
obtained from
\Pi
by taking

H=\begin{pmatrix}1&\overline{1}^{\top}\\
-\overline{1}&\Pi\end{pmatrix}
. ∎

Theorem 2
.

Let
m=2(q+1)
, for
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}

a (generalised) Paley graph, cf. Sect.
2.4
,
of order
q
exists (e.g.
q
is a prime power).
Then there is a symmetric Hadamard matrix
H
of order
m
, obtained
from
\Pi
by taking

H=I_{q+1}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\
-1&-1\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&\overline{1}^{\top}\\
\overline{1}&\Pi-I_{q}\end{pmatrix}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&-1\end{pmatrix}
.
∎

4.2
Doubling Constructions

Nearly 60 years before Paley, Sylvester
[
53
]
had already proved that if
n
is the order of a Hadamard matrix, then there exists a Hadamard matrix of order
2^{t}n
for all values of
t\geq 0
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
H
be a Hadamard matrix of order
n
. Then the matrix
H^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}H&H\\
H&-H\end{pmatrix}
is a Hadamard matrix of order
2n
. ∎

More recently, Seberry
[
58
]
described a similar construction for skew Hadamard matrices:

Theorem 4
.

Suppose
H_{n}=S+I_{n}
is a skew Hadamard matrix of order
n
. Then

H_{2n}=\begin{pmatrix}S+I_{n}&S+I_{n}\\
S-I_{n}&-S+I_{n}\end{pmatrix}

is a skew Hadamard matrix of order
2n
. ∎

4.3
Williamson Construction

Another construction for Hadamard matrices is due to Williamson, see
[
31
]
, Sect. 14.2. He proved that the matrix
H
given by

H=\begin{pmatrix}A&-B&-C&-D\\
B&A&D&-C\\
C&-D&A&B\\
D&C&-B&A\end{pmatrix}

is a Hadamard matrix of order
4n
, if
A,B,C,D
are Williamson matrices.

4.4
Goethals-Seidel Array

Goethals and Seidel
[
26
]
discovered that, given four
\pm 1
matrices
A,B,C,D
of order
n
such that

AA^{\top}+BB^{\top}+CC^{\top}+DD^{\top}=4nI,

a Hadamard matrix of order
4n
can be constructed by plugging them into the Goethals-Seidel array

GS(A,B,C,D)=\begin{pmatrix}A&BR&CR&DR\\
-BR&A&D^{\top}R&-C^{\top}R\\
-CR&-D^{\top}R&A&B^{\top}R\\
-DR&C^{\top}R&-B^{\top}R&A\end{pmatrix}

Here,
R
is the
n\times n
permutation matrix with all ones on the anti-diagonal.

Furthermore, if
A=S+I
with
S
a skew-symmetric matrix, the Goethals-Seidel array will give a skew Hadamard matrix.

The Goethals-Seidel array has been used extensively to construct Hadamard matrices of order
4v
from SDSs with parameters
4-\{n;k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4};n-\sum_{i=1}^{4}k_{i}\}

[
22
]
.
Additionally, a skew SDS can be used to obtain a skew Hadamard matrix.

J. Cooper and J. Wallis
[
9
]
showed a construction which uses T-matrices and a generalisation of Williamson matrices:

Theorem 5
.

Suppose there exist four T-matrices of order
n
. Furthermore, suppose there exist four
\pm 1
matrices
A
,
B
,
C
,
D
of order
w
which pairwise satisfy
MN^{\top}
=
NM^{\top}
and for which

AA^{\top}+BB^{\top}+CC^{\top}+DD^{\top}=4wI

Then there exists a Hadamard matrix of order
4nw
.∎

In
[
37
]
, Miyamoto provided a construction for an infinite series of Hadamard matrices:

Theorem 6
.

Let
q
be a prime power and
q\equiv 1\mod 4
. If there is a Hadamard matrix of order
q-1
, then there is a Hadamard matrix of order
4q
. ∎

Spence
[
50
]
proposed a construction that uses supplementary difference sets with parameters
4-\{2v;v,v,v,v+1;2v\}
:

Theorem 7
.

If there exist
4-\{2v;v,v,v,v+1;2v\}
supplementary difference sets in the cyclic group of order
2v
then there exists a Hadamard matrix of order
4(2v+1)
. ∎

Theorem 9
.

If there is a skew Hadamard matrix of order
n
, and for a prime
p
and
k\geq 1
one has
p^{k}+1\equiv 0\pmod{4}
,
then there is a skew Hadamard matrix of order
n(p^{k}+1)
. ∎

Furthermore, Blatt and Szekeres showed in
[
4
]
that

Theorem 10
.

If for some abelian group G of order
2m+1
there exists a pair of complementary difference sets
A,B
, then there exists a skew Hadamard matrix of order
4(m+1)
. ∎

A different construction which also uses complementary difference sets was given by Spence
[
51
]
:

Theorem 11
.

If there exists a cyclic projective plane of order
q
and two complementary difference sets in a cyclic group of order
1+q+q^{2}
, then there exists a skew Hadamard matrix of the Goethals-Seidel type of order
4(1+q+q^{2})
. ∎

Lastly, skew Hadamard matrices can be obtained from orthogonal designs
[
45
]
:

Theorem 12
.

Suppose there is an orthogonal design of type
(1,m,mn-m-1)
in order
mn
. Suppose
n
is the order of amicable designs of types
((1,n-1);(n))
. Then there is a skew Hadamard matrix of order
mn(n-1)
. ∎

According to multiple papers

We contacted the author of

For completeness, below is the (implemented)

Let

be the multiplicative group of the nonzero residue classes modulo

\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{7}
of

in

\alpha_{2i+1}=-1\cdot\alpha_{2i}
and:

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}

\displaystyle=H

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}

\displaystyle\alpha_{4}

\displaystyle=5H

\displaystyle\alpha_{6}

\displaystyle=11H

Now, consider the four index sets:

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\{1,3,4,6\}

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\{3,5,6,7\}

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle=\{1,4,5,7\}

\displaystyle J_{4}

\displaystyle=\{0,2,7\}

The skew SDS are given by:

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j\in J_{1}}\alpha_{j}

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j\in J_{2}}\alpha_{j}

\displaystyle S_{3}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j\in J_{3}}\alpha_{j}

\displaystyle S_{4}

\displaystyle=\{0\}\cup\bigcup_{j\in J_{4}}\alpha_{j}

These sets are used to create a skew Hadamard matrix using the construction from Sect.

.
One obtains four circulant matrices

6.1
Riesel numbers

While working on updating Tables
and
,
we observed that the unfilled prime power entries
p^{k}
,
k\geq 1
, within the tables’ ranges, may be
filled in by using an appropriate Paley
construction. This gives a natural question: how
powerful are the latter, will they work regardless
of the value of
p^{k}
?
It turns out that the answer is no.

An odd natural number
k
is called a
Riesel number
if
2^{m}k-1
is composite for all
m\geq 0
,
see
[
40
,
29
]
. Specifically,
in
[
40
]
H. Riesel constructed an infinite family of such numbers,
starting from
509203
, which is believed to be the smallest such number. Computational work
has been carried out to verify the latter, and the smallest number with unknown status is
23699
(May 2024),
see
[
41
]
.
Riesel numbers, in connection to asymptotic existence of Hadamard matrices, have been used by W. de Launey
[
11
]
,
and the first part of Theorem
13
is implicit there. The second part appears to be new.

Theorem 13
.

Let
k<23669
be odd, or more generally, let
k
be any non-Riesel odd number.
Then for some
m>1
there exists a skew Hadamard matrix of Paley type,
of order
2^{m}k
. As well, there are no Hadamard matrices of Paley types
(neither symmetric nor skew-symmetric) for order
2^{m}509203
, for any
m
.

Proof.

The only part which is not immediate from the results mentioned above is why
there is no prime
p
such that
2^{m}509203-1=p^{t}
, for some
t>0
. Note that

t=1
is ruled impossible by
[
40
]
. To sort out the case
t>1
we need
more technique used in [loc.cit.], as communicated to us by Max Alekseyev
[
2
]
;
this argument applies to the case
t=1
, as well.

Denote
k=509203
. Any number of the form
2^{m}k-1=p^{t}
is divisible by
p\in\{3,5,7,13,17,241\}
,
as shown in
[
40
]
.
Thus our
p
must come from this set. By observing that
2^{m}k-1\equiv p^{t}\pmod{M}

has no solution for an appropriate
M\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain our result.
Namely, the values of
M
to achieve this are as follows:

85
for
p=3
;

31
for
p=5
;

57
for
p=7
;

105
for
p=13
;

15
for
p=17
and
p=241
.
Thus it is not possible to construct a skew-Hadamard matrix of order
2^{m}509203
using the construction
from Theorem
1
.

It is also not possible to construct a symmetric Hadamard matrix of this order using the construction
from Theorem
2
. Indeed,
for this we need
\frac{2^{m}509203-2}{2}=2^{m-1}509203-1
to be a prime power, which we have shown to be impossible.
∎

In general, different construction methods are needed
to get matrices of order
2^{\tau}p^{k}
. Research on such methods is surveyed
in Chapter 11 of
[
48
]
—
it is shown that for any odd
q
there exists

\tau_{0}
so that a Hadamard matrix of order
2^{\tau}q
exists for any
\tau\geq\tau_{0}
, and results on the best

\tau_{0}
are surveyed.

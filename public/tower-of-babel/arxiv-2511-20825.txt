Spectral orthogonality of special flows
Mingcheng Sheng

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the spectral orthogonality problem for special flows built over irrational rotations under two different types of roof functions: 1) the roof functions are real analytic. 2) the roof functions are piecewise C 1 C^{1} with one discontinuity. These flows are also known as von-Neumann flows. We show that if { T α f } \{T^{f}_{\alpha}\} is as in 1) and weak mixing, then for a G δ G_{\delta} dense set of β \beta , we have that { T β f } \{T^{f}_{\beta}\} is weak-mixing and is spectrally orthogonal to { T α f } \{T^{f}_{\alpha}\} . On the other hand, if { T α f } \{T^{f}_{\alpha}\} is as in 2), then for a full measure set of β \beta , the flows { T α f } \{T^{f}_{\alpha}\} and { T β f } \{T^{f}_{\beta}\} are spectrally orthogonal.



1.
Introduction

Given two continuous probability preserving dynamical flows
\mathcal{T}=(T_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
(X,\mu)
and
\mathcal{R}=(S_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
(Y,\nu)
, there are several ways to measure how different these systems are. The most classical is the
isomorphism
relation where the two systems are isomorphic if there exists an invertible measure preserving transformation
S:X\to Y
such
that
S\circ T_{t}=R_{t}\circ S
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
. A stronger notion is
disjointness
introduced by Furstenberg
[
7
]
. Two systems are disjoint (in the sense of Furstenberg) if the only
joining
between them is the product measure. An even stronger notion is
spectral orthogonality
or spectral disjointness. Two systems are spectrally disjoint if their spectral measures (see Section
2.3
) are orthogonal. It follows that spectral orthogonality implies (Furstenberg) disjointness which implies non-isomoprhism.
This paper is concerned with studying spectral orthogonality for some classes of special flows on the two torus which come from reparametrizations of linear toral flows. Recall that for the classical linear flows on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
the isomorphism problem is well understood. It then follows that two such flows are spectrally orthogonal if and only if their rotation numbers are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
. Note that this condition is also equivalent to Furstenberg disjointness. A more complicated case arises from smooth reparametrizations of linear flows. These flows were first studied by Kolmogorov,
[
12
]
who showed that if the rotation number of the linear flow is Diophantine (see
2.2
), then the reparametrized flow is smoothly conjugated to the corresponding linear flow and so it has pure point spectrum. However, the case of a Liouvillian rotation number is fundamentally different. In 1967, Shklover
[
14
]
proved that there are analytic reparametrizations which are weak mixing and so the spectrum is continuous. The main goal of this paper is to study spectral orthogonality for the class of weakly mixing reparametrizations of Liouvillean flows. Considering a Poincare section, these flows can be viewed as special flows over irrational rotations(see the introduction of
[
5
]
for a short explanation). Denote
T_{\alpha}^{f}
by the special flow over a rotation
R_{\alpha}(x)=x+\alpha
under a roof function
f
(see
2.1
).

Our main results are:

Theorem 1.1
.

Given a Liouville number
\beta
and a real analytic function
f
so that the special flow
T_{\beta}^{f}
is weak mixing, there is a
G_{\delta}
dense set of
\alpha
such that
T_{\alpha}^{f}
is weak mixing and is spectrally orthogonal to
T_{\beta}^{f}
.

The orthogonality problem has been studied for several classes of flows and maps. For example, Chaika
[
2
]
showed in 2012 that every ergodic transformation is
disjoint(in the sense of Furstenberg) from almost every interval
exchange transformation. Also, Krzysztof Fraczek, Adam Kanigowski, and Corinna Ulcigrai
[
6
]
showed that for almost every pair of IET’s(Interval Exchange Transformation), the corresponding special flows are spectrally orthogonal. More results on spectral orthogonality and Furstenberg disjointness can be found in
[
1
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
13
]
, etc.

Our methods also applies to another type of special flows called the Von Neumann flows, see chapter
7
for the definition. In this case we show:

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
\alpha\in[0,1]/\mathbb{Q}
, there exists a full measure set
S_{\alpha}
,
m(S_{\alpha})=1
, such that for every
\beta\in S_{\alpha}
, the flows
\mathcal{T}=T^{f}_{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{R}=T^{f}_{\beta}
are spectrally orthogonal.

The isomorphism problem of Von Neumann flows were first studied by Kanigowski and Solomko
[
10
]
. They showed that every two Von Neumann flows with the same rotation number but under roof functions of different slope are always non-isomorphic. In contrast, we establish a much stronger conclusion of spectral orthogonality in a more subtle setting where two roof functions share the same slope. The trade-off is that our result applies only to a full measure set of system parameters.

Our main tool to prove spectral orthogonality is a criterion from
[
6
]
, which roughly says that if there exists a time sequence
\{t_{n}\}
that serves as a rigidity sequence for one flow but a mixing sequence for the other, then the two flows are spectrally orthogonal. We will later establish that the sequence generated by the convergents of the base rotation number always constitutes a rigidity sequence. The problem is thus reduced to showing that a large set of special flows exhibits mixing behavior along this same sequence of convergents.

2.
Basic Definitions

2.1.
Special flows over irrational rotation

Birkhoff sums and special flows.

Let
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
be the one dimensional torus. For an ergodic automorphism
T
on
\mathbb{T}
, and a strictly positive function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, we define a cocycle
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
by:

(2.1)

S_{n}(f)(x)=\begin{cases}\sum_{0\leq i<n}f(T^{i}x)&\text{ if }n\geq 0\\
0&\text{ if }n=0\\
\sum_{n\leq i<0}f(T^{i}x)&\text{ if }n<0\end{cases}

The special flow
\mathcal{T}=\{\mathbb{T}_{t}^{f}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
over the base automorphism
T:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
under the roof function
f
is the vertical, unit speed flow on the region
\mathbb{T}^{f}:=\{(x,y)\in I\times\mathbb{R}:0\leq y<f(x)\}
, where we identify each point on the graph of the form
(x,f(x)),x\in\mathbb{T}
with the base point

(T(x),0)
. More precisely,
(T^{f}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
acts on
\mathbb{T}^{f}
so that

T^{f}_{t}(x,r)=(T^{n}x,r+t-S_{n}(f)(x)),

where
n=n(t,x)\in\mathbb{Z}
is a unique integer number such that
S_{n}(f)(x)\leq r+t<S_{n+1}(f)(x)
.

In this paper, to simplify the
exposition of our proofs we will assume that
\int_{\mathbb{T}}f=1
and that

\frac{1}{2}\leq f\leq\frac{3}{2}

which does not cause any loss of generality in the propositions we will state.

2.2.
Continued fractions

For an irrational
\alpha\in\mathbb{R/\mathbb{Q}}
,
\alpha=[a_{0};a_{1},a_{2},\cdots]
is called the continued fraction
expansion of
\alpha
and the rational number
(p_{n}/q_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}=[a_{0};a_{1}\cdots,a_{n}]
is called the convergents of

\alpha
(see
[
3
]
). The sequence
(q_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is given by the recursive formulas

q_{0}=1,q_{1}=a_{1},q_{n}=a_{n}q_{n-1}+q_{n-2}

and the following two inequalities hold

(2.2)

\|q_{n-1}\alpha\|\leq\|k\alpha\|,\quad\forall k<q_{n}

and

(2.3)

\frac{1}{2q_{n+1}}\leq\frac{1}{q_{n}+q_{n+1}}\leq\|q_{n}\alpha\|<\frac{1}{q_{n+1}}\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}

Definition 2.4
.

(Liouville Number)
A irrational number
x
is a Liouville number if for every positive integer
n
, there exists positive integer
p
and
q
such that

0<\bigg|x-\frac{p}{q}\bigg|<\frac{1}{q^{n}}

Definition 2.5
.

(Diophantine number)
A irrational number
x
is Diophantine if it is not Liouville.

2.3.
Spectral Orthogonality

We now recall basic definitions from spectral theory. For a measure preserving dynamical flow
\mathcal{T}=\{T_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
acting on the space
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
, the Koopman operators, denote by
\{U^{\mathcal{T}}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, are a group of unitary operators on
L^{2}(X,B,\mu)
given by
f\to f\circ T_{t}
. A subspace
\overline{\text{span}}\{f\circ T_{t};t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a
cyclic space
of an element
f\in L^{2}(X,B,\mu)
. Due to
Bochner-Herglotz’s theorem there exists a finite measure
\hat{\sigma}_{f}
on
\mathbb{R}
such that

\hat{\sigma}_{f}(t)=\langle f\circ T_{-t},f\rangle\text{ for every }t\in\mathbb{R}

We say that
\sigma_{f}
is the spectral measure of
f
. A maximal spectral type of
T
is a spectral measure
\sigma_{T}
such
that for every
f\in L^{2}(X,B,\mu)
, the spectral measure
\sigma_{f}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\sigma_{T}
.

Definition 2.6
.

(Spectral orthogonality)
Two measure-preserving dynamical systems,
(X,\mu,T)
and
(Y,\nu,S)
, are defined as spectrally orthogonal if the maximal spectral types of their associated Koopman operators,
U_{T}
and
U_{S}
, are mutually singular.

Before stating the criterion of spectral orthogonality, let us first recall the definition of rigidity sequence and mixing sequence:

Definition 2.7
.

(Rigidity sequence)
An automorphism
T
of a probability Borel space
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
is called
rigid if there exists an increasing sequence
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of natural numbers such that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mu(A\triangle T^{h_{n}}A)=0\textit{ for every }A\in\mathcal{B}.

The sequence
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is then a rigidity sequence for
T
.

Definition 2.8
.

(Mixing sequence)
A measure preserving flow
(T_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
(X,\mu)
is mixing along a sequence
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of real numbers converging to
\infty
if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle U^{a_{n}}g_{1},g_{2}\rangle=\langle g_{1},1\rangle\langle 1,g_{2}\rangle\textit{ for every }g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{2}(X,\mu),

Now we state the main criterion:

Theorem 2.9
.

(Singularity Criterion via rigidity and exponential tails)
[
6
]
Theorem 5. Chapter 4.2

Let
f:X\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be an integrable roof function with
\inf_{x\in X}f(x)>0
. Suppose that there exist a
rigidity sequence
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for
T
. Assume that
(T^{f}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
(X^{f},\mu^{f})
is a special flow such that there exist
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
Borel sets with
\mu(A_{n})\to 1
as

, and
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

(S_{h_{n}}(f)(x)-a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has exponential tails, i.e. there exist two positive constants
C
and
b
such that

(2.10)

\mu({x\in A_{n}:|S_{h_{n}}(f)(x)-a_{n}|\geq t})\leq Ce^{-bt}\textit{ for all }t\geq 0\textit{ and }n\in\mathbb{N}.

If
(U_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a measure-preserving flow on
(Y,\nu)
which is mixing along the sequence
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
,
then the flows
(T^{f}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
and

are spectrally orthogonal.

Rigid and Mixing subsequence

The following well-known result shows that we can take the sequence

Lemma 3.1

If

Therefore, we only need to worry about the mixing subsequence. The main idea to prove mixing along a sequence is to estimate the behavior of the Birkhoff sum. We would like to show that for arbitrarily small cubes, the Birkhoff sums on the cube is

Definition 3.2

(Uniform stretch)
[

(1-\epsilon)\frac{v-u}{g(b)-g(a)}(b-a)\leq\lambda(I_{u,v})\leq(1+\epsilon)\frac{v-u}{g(b)-g(a)}(b-a)

We also denote

for
sup_{x\in[a,b]}|g(x)|-\inf_{x\in[a,b]}|g(x)|
.

For any

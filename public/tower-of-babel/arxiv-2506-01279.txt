W W -entropy formulas and Langevin deformation on the L q L^{q} -Wasserstein space over Riemannian manifolds
Rong Lei, Xiang-Dong Li, Yu-Zhao Wang

Abstract
We first prove the W W -entropy formula and rigidity theorem for the geodesic flow on the L q L^{q} -Wasserstein space over a complete Riemannian manifold with bounded geometry condition. Then we introduce the Langevin deformation on the L q L^{q} -Wasserstein space over a complete Riemannian manifold, which interpolates between the p p -Laplacian heat equation and the geodesic flow on the L q L^{q} -Wasserstein space, where 1 p + 1 q = 1 {1\over p}+{1\over q}=1 , 1 < p , q < ∞ 1<p,q<\infty . The local existence, uniqueness and regularity of the Langevin deformation on the L q L^{q} -Wasserstein space over the Euclidean space and a compact Riemannian manifold are proved for q ∈ [ 2 , ∞ ) q\in[2,\infty) . We further prove the W W -entropy-information formula and the rigidity theorem for the Langevin deformation on the L q L^{q} -Wasserstein space over an n n -dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with non-negative Ricci curvature, where q ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) q\in(1,\infty) . Mathematics Subject Classification (2020) . Primary 58J35, 58J65; Secondary 35K92, 60H30. Keywords . Langevin deformation, L q L^{q} -Wasserstein space, W W -entropy

3
Variational formulas for the geodesic flow on
L^{q}
-Wasserstein space

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with bounded geometry condition. The
p
-Laplacian
\Delta_{p}
is defined by

\Delta_{p}u:=\nabla\cdot\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right),\quad u\in C^{\infty}(M).

The linearization of the
p
-Laplacian
\Delta_{p}
at
u\in C^{2}(M)
with
\nabla u\neq 0
is given by (see e.g.
[
10
]
)

\mathcal{L}(\psi):=\nabla\cdot\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}A(\nabla\psi)\right)

for
\psi\in C^{\infty}(M)
, where
A
is the tensor defined in (
11
).
Due to the degeneracy and singularity of the
p
-Laplacian on
\nabla u=0
, an
\varepsilon
-regularization method is employed, which means that one replaces the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}
by its approximating operator
\mathcal{L}_{{\varepsilon}}
defined as

\mathcal{L}_{{\varepsilon}}\psi:=\nabla\cdot\left(w_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p}{2}-1}A_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\psi)\right),

where
\varepsilon>0
,
w_{\varepsilon}=|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon
and
A_{\varepsilon}=g+(p-2)\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\otimes\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}{w_{\varepsilon}}
. See
[
10
]
.

We first prove the entropy variational formula on
P_{p}(M)
. When
p=2
, it was due to Lott
[
16
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(\rho,\phi):[s_{0}-\epsilon,s_{0}+\epsilon]\times[0,1]\to C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}^{+})\times C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
be smooth functions satisfying the nonlinear transport equation

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\left(\rho|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\nabla\phi\right)=0,

(28)

where for any fixed
t\in[0,1]
,
\phi(\cdot,t):[s_{0}-\epsilon,s_{0}+\epsilon]\to C^{\infty}(M)
.
Let
s\mapsto c(s,\cdot)=\rho(s,\cdot)dv
be a smooth curve in
{P}_{q}(M)
. Define the energy functional as follows

E(c(s)):=\frac{1}{p}\int^{1}_{0}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi(s,t)|^{p}\rho(s,t)\,dvdt.

Then, the variation of
E(c(s))
with respect to
s
is given by

\frac{d}{ds}E(c(s))=\frac{1}{p-1}\int_{M}\phi\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial s}dv\Big|^{1}_{t=0}-\frac{1}{p-1}\int^{1}_{0}\int_{M}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}+\frac{1}{p}|\nabla\phi|^{p}\right)\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial s}\,dvdt.

(29)

Proof.

The proof is similar to the case
p=2
in Lott
[
16
]
and S. Li-Li
[
14
]
.
Direct calculation implies that

\frac{d}{ds}E(c(s))=\int^{1}_{0}\int_{M}\left(|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\Big\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial s}\Big\rangle\rho+\frac{1}{p}|\nabla\phi|^{p}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial s}\right)dvdt.

(30)

For fixed
h\in C^{\infty}(M)
, from (
28
) and integration by parts, we have

\int_{M}h\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}dv=\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla h\rangle\rho\,dv.

Hence

\int_{M}h\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial s\partial t}dv=\int_{M}\left(|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\left\langle A\Big(\nabla\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial s}\Big),\nabla h\right\rangle\rho+|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\langle\nabla h,\nabla\phi\rangle\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial s}\right)dv,

where
A
is defined in (
11
).
Taking
h=\phi
, we have

\int_{M}\phi\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial s\partial t}dv=\int_{M}\left((p-1)|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\Big\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial s}\Big\rangle\rho+|\nabla\phi|^{p}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial s}\right)dv.

(31)

Combining (
30
) and (
31
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}E(c(s))=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p-1}\int^{1}_{0}\int_{M}\left(\phi\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial s\partial t}-\frac{1}{p}|\nabla\phi|^{p}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial s}\right)dvdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p-1}\int^{1}_{0}\int_{M}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big(\phi\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial s}\Big)-\Big(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}+\frac{1}{p}|\nabla\phi|^{p}\Big)\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial s}\right)dvdt,

from which the variational formula (
29
) holds.
∎

From (
29
), the Euler-Lagrange equation for
E
is given by the
p
-Hamitlon-Jacobi equation

\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial s}+\frac{1}{p}|\nabla\phi|^{p}=0.

Thus, if a
L^{q}
-geodesic flow
(\rho(t),\phi(t),t\in[0,T])
is a smooth curve in
P_{q}(M)
, then it satisfies (
7
).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(\rho,\phi)
be a smooth solution to the
L^{q}
-geodesic flow equation (
7
). Then

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\phi\rho\,dv=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p}\rho\,dv,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\int_{M}\phi\rho\,dv=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p}\rho\,dv=0.

Proof.

By (
7
), direct calculation implies that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\phi\rho\,dv=

\displaystyle\int_{M}(\partial_{t}\rho\phi+\rho\partial_{t}\phi)\,dv=-\int_{M}\nabla\cdot(\rho|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\nabla\phi)\phi\,dv-\frac{1}{p}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p}\rho\,dv=\frac{1}{q}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p}\rho\,dv,

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p}\rho\,dv=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p}\partial_{t}\rho\,dv+\frac{p}{q}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\rangle\rho\,dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{q}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p}\nabla\cdot(\rho|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\nabla\phi)\,dv-\frac{1}{q}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla|\nabla\phi|^{p}\rangle\rho\,dv=0.

∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold with bounded geometry condition. Let
(\rho,\phi)
be a smooth solution to the
L^{q}
-geodesic equations (
7
) satisfying the following growth condition

\int_{M}\left[|\nabla\log\rho|^{p}+|\nabla\phi|^{p}+|\nabla\phi|^{2p-2}+|\nabla^{2}\phi|_{A}^{2p-2}+|\Delta_{p}\phi|\right]\rho\,dv<\infty.

Assume there exist a point
o\in M
, and some functions
C_{i},\alpha_{i}\in C\left([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{+}\right)
,
i=1,2
, such that

C_{1}(t)e^{-\alpha_{1}(t)d^{q}(x,o)}\leq\rho(t,x)\leq C_{2}(t)e^{\alpha_{2}(t)d^{q}(x,o)},\quad\forall x\in M,t\in[0,T],

and

\int_{M}d^{pq}(x,o)\rho(t,x)dv(x)<\infty,\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

Then the following variational formulas hold:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}{\rm Ent}(\rho)=

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\rho\rangle\,dv=-\int_{M}\rho\Delta_{p}\phi\,dv,

(32)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}{\rm Ent}(\rho)=

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2p-4}\left(|\nabla^{2}\phi|_{A}^{2}+{\rm Ric}(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi)\right)\rho\,dv,

(33)

where
A
is defined in (
11
), and
\langle X,Y\rangle_{A}=\sum_{ij}A^{ij}X_{i}Y_{j}
for all
X,Y\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma(TM))
.

Proof.

Let
\eta_{k}
be an increasing sequence of functions in
C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
such that
0\leq\eta_{k}\leq 1
,

\eta_{k}=1
on
B(o,k)
,
\eta_{k}=0
on
M\backslash B(o,2k)
, and
\eta_{k}\leq{1\over k}
.
Let
(\rho,\phi)
be a smooth solution to Eq. (
7
). Integrating by parts, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}(\rho\log\rho)\eta_{k}dv=

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\rho\rangle\eta_{k}\,dv+\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\eta_{k}\rangle(1+\log\rho)\rho\,dv

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I_{1}(k)+I_{2}(k).

Under the assumption of proposition, we have

\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p}\rho dv<\infty,\quad\int_{M}|\nabla\log\rho|^{p}\rho dv<\infty.

By Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\log\rho\rangle\rho\,dv\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p}\rho dv\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{M}|\nabla\log\rho|^{p}\rho dv\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Hence
|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\rho\rangle|\in L^{1}(M)
. By the Lebesgue dominated convergence
theorem, as
k\rightarrow\infty
, we have

I_{1}(k)=\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\rho\rangle\eta_{k}\,dv\rightarrow\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\rho\rangle\,dv.

(34)

On the other hand, as
k\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}(k)=

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\nabla\cdot(\eta_{k}|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\nabla\phi)\rho\,dv=-\int_{M}(\Delta_{p}\phi)\rho\eta_{k}\,dv-\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\eta_{k}\rho\,dv

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle-\int_{M}(\Delta_{p}\phi)\rho\,dv.

(35)

Similarly, noticing
|\nabla\eta_{k}|\leq 1/k
. Under the assumption of proposition, the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem yields

I_{2}(k)=\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\eta_{k}\rangle(1+\log\rho)\rho\,dv\to 0\quad\text{ as }k\to\infty.

(36)

Combining (
34
), (
3
) with (
36
), we complete the proof of (
32
).

By the
p
-Bochner formula (see
[
10
]
)

\mathcal{L}(|\nabla\phi|^{p})=p|\nabla\phi|^{2p-4}(|\nabla^{2}\phi|^{2}_{A}+{\rm Ric}(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi))+p|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\Delta_{p}\phi\rangle,

(37)

and integrating by parts, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\rho\rangle\eta_{k}\,dv=\int_{M}\left\langle\partial_{t}(|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\nabla\phi),\nabla\rho\right\rangle\eta_{k}+\left\langle|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\nabla\phi,\nabla\partial_{t}\rho\right\rangle\eta_{k}\,dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2p-4}(|\nabla^{2}\phi|^{2}_{A}+{\rm Ric}(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi))\rho\eta_{k}\,dv+\frac{1}{p}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\Big\langle\nabla|\nabla\phi|^{p},\nabla\eta_{k}\Big\rangle_{A}\rho\,dv-\int_{M}(\Delta_{p}\phi)|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\eta_{k}\rangle\rho\,dv

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I_{3}(k)+I_{4}(k)+I_{5}(k),

where we used the facts

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle\partial_{t}(|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\nabla\phi),\nabla\rho\rangle\eta_{k}\,dv=

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\partial_{t}\phi,\nabla\rho\rangle_{A}\eta_{k}\,dv,

and

-\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\Big\langle\nabla|\nabla\phi|^{p},\nabla\rho\Big\rangle_{A}\eta_{k}\,dv=\int_{M}\mathcal{L}(|\nabla\phi|^{p})\rho\eta_{k}\,dv+\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\Big\langle\nabla|\nabla\phi|^{p},\nabla\eta_{k}\Big\rangle_{A}\rho\,dv.

By
|\mathrm{Ric}|\leq C
, under the assumption

\int_{M}[|\nabla\phi|^{2p-2}+|\nabla^{2}\phi|^{2p-2}_{A}]\rho\,dv<\infty
, we have

\int_{M}\left||\nabla\phi|^{2p-4}(|\nabla^{2}\phi|^{2}_{A}+{\rm Ric}(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi))\right|\rho\,dv\\
\leq\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2p-2}(|\nabla^{2}\phi|^{2p-2}_{A}+C)\rho\,dv<\infty.

Using the fact
0\leq\eta_{k}\leq 1
and
\eta_{k}\to 1
, the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem yields

I_{3}(k)\to\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2p-4}(|\nabla^{2}\phi|^{2}_{A}+{\rm Ric}(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi))\rho\,dv.

(38)

Using again the assumption
\int_{M}[|\nabla\phi|^{2p-2}+|\nabla^{2}\phi|^{2p-2}_{A}]\rho\,dv<\infty
, we have

I_{4}(k)=\frac{1}{p}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\Big\langle\nabla|\nabla\phi|^{p},\nabla\eta_{k}\Big\rangle_{A}\rho\,dv\leq(p-1)\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2p-4}|\nabla^{2}\phi\nabla\phi|\cdot|\nabla\eta_{k}|\rho\,dv\to 0.

Under the assumption of proposition, we have

\int_{M}[(\Delta_{p}\phi)^{p}+|\nabla\phi|^{p}]\rho\,dv<\infty
.
Using again the fact
0\leq\eta_{k}\leq 1,\eta_{k}\to 1
and
|\nabla\eta_{k}|\leq\frac{1}{k}
, the Lebesgue dominated
convergence theorem yields

\displaystyle I_{5}(k)=-\int_{M}(\Delta_{p}\phi)|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\eta_{k}\rangle\rho\,dv\to 0.

(39)

Combining (
38
), (
3
) with (

), we complete the proof of (

∎

-entropy formula for the geodesic flow on

Proof of Theorem

By the definition of the

\displaystyle\small\frac{d}{dt}{W}_{n,p}(\rho,t)=

\displaystyle t\int_{M}\left(\left||\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\phi-\frac{a_{ij}}{t}\right|_{A}^{2}+|\nabla\phi|^{2p-4}{\rm Ric}(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi)\right)\rho\,dv+\frac{n}{t}

\displaystyle t\int_{M}\left(\left||\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\phi-\frac{a_{ij}}{t}\right|_{A}^{2}+|\nabla\phi|^{2p-4}{\rm Ric}(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi)\right)\rho\,dv,

where

By the Theorem 6.19 of Kotschwar-Ni in

is isometric to

and

5
Local existence and uniqueness of Langevin deformation

In this section, we prove the local existence and uniqueness of solution to the Cauchy problem for the Langevin deformation of flows (
16
) for fixed
c\in(0,\infty)
.

To simplify notations and to avoid technical complexity, we only give the proof on Euclidean spaces and we would like to point out that the proof can be extended to compact Riemannian manifolds by applying the following Sobolev embedding theorem. Let
M
be
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or an
n
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold. By
[
5
,
2
]
, there exists a constant
C_{\text{Sob }}>0
such that

\|f\|_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\leq C_{\mathrm{Sob}}\left(\|\nabla f\|_{2}+\|f\|_{2}\right),\quad\forall f\in C^{\infty}(M).

For
0<\alpha<1
, define the Hölder space
C^{0,\alpha}(M)
equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}:=\|f\|_{\infty}+\sup_{x,y\in M,x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{d(x,y)^{\alpha}},

and define the Sobolev space
H^{s}(M)
equipped with the Sobolev norm

\|f\|_{s,2}=\left(\sum_{|\beta|\leq s}\left\|D^{\beta}f\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where
\beta
is a multi-index. For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, if
s>\alpha+\frac{n}{2}
, then the Bernstein-Rellich-Kondrakov-Morrey continuity embedding theorem holds

\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\leq C_{\alpha}\|f\|_{s,2}.

(41)

In particular, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
s>\frac{n}{2}
, we have

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq C\|f\|_{s,2}.

(42)

Now we consider
\mathcal{V}(\rho)=\int_{M}\rho\log\rho dv
. Let
U=(\log\rho,u)^{T}=(\log\rho,v_{q})^{T}:M\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then we can rewrite the Cauchy problem for
(

)
with initial value

as the following symmetric hyperbolic system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}A_{0}^{c}(U)\partial_{t}U+\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}^{c}(U)\partial_{j}U+BU=0,\\
U(0,x)=U_{0}(x)=(\log\rho_{0},u_{0})(x),\end{array}\right.

(43)

where
A_{0}^{c}(U)=\mathrm{diag}\{1,c^{p},\cdots,c^{p}\}
,
B=\mathrm{diag}\{0,1,\cdots,1\}
,
and for

A_{j}^{c}(U)
is an
(n+1)\times(n+1)
matrix with the entries
\left(A_{j}^{c}(U)\right)_{1,1}=|u|^{p-2}u^{j}
,
\left(A_{j}^{c}(U)\right)_{1,j+1}=\left(A_{j}^{c}(U)\right)_{j+1,1}=1

\left(A_{j}^{c}(U)\right)_{k,k}=c^{p}|u|^{p-2}u^{j}
for

, and
\left(A_{j}^{c}(U)\right)_{k,l}=0
otherwise.

Applying Theorem 2 in
[
11
]
by Kato, we obtain the following local existence and uniqueness of solution to the Cauchy problem for the symmetric hyperbolic system (

).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
c\in(0,\infty)
and
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
or an
n
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold. Let

be an integer such that
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
. Suppose

. Then there exists a bounded open subset

of

More precisely, if we take

such that
U_{00}\in H^{s}(M;\mathbb{R}^{n+1})\cap C_{c}^{\infty}(M;\mathbb{R}^{n+1})

Proof.

According to the Theorem 2 in
[
11
]
, we consider the operator
G_{j}(t):D\to H^{s}_{ul}(M;\mathbb{R}^{(n+1)\times(n+1)})
defined by
G_{j}(t)[U]=A_{j}^{c}(U)
for

, where

is the uniformly local Sobolev space defined in

and

Now we only have to verify the coefficients

,

satisfy the uniformly boundness and Lipschitz condition since

and
B
are constant matrices. First, we verify

\sup_{U\in D}\|A_{j}^{c}(U)\|_{H^{s}_{ul}}\leq C,\quad j=1,\cdots,n.

(44)

Take

and denote

with

.
By the Lagrange mean value theorem, there exists some
\theta\in[0,1]
and

such that

where
\nabla f(\xi)=(p-2)|\xi|^{p-4}\xi\otimes\xi+|\xi|^{p-2}I
.

By the Sobolev inequality (

), if

, then we have

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|U\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{s}\|U\|_{H^{s}}\leq C^{\prime},\quad\forall U\in D.

Note that
\sup_{U\in D}\|\xi\|_{\infty}\leq\sup_{\theta\in[0,1]}\sup_{U\in D}\|\theta u_{00}+(1-\theta)u\|_{H^{s}}\leq C^{\prime\prime}
.
Thus we have

\displaystyle\|f(u)\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f(u_{00})\|_{H^{s}}+\|\nabla f(\xi)\|_{\infty}\|u-u_{00}\|_{H^{s}}\leq\|f(u_{00})\|_{H^{s}}+C^{\prime\prime\prime}\|u-u_{00}\|_{H^{s}}.

Choosing
U_{00}\in H^{s}(M;\mathbb{R}^{n+1})\cap C_{c}^{\infty}(M;\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
such that

and noticing that

, we can verify that

is finite. Thus we have
\|f(u)\|_{H^{s}}<+\infty
. Then we obtain (

).

Next we verify the Lipschitz condition. This requires that there exists a constant

such that

\|A_{j}^{c}(U)-A_{j}^{c}(V)\|_{L^{2}_{ul}}\leq L\|U-V\|_{L^{2}},\quad\forall U,V\in D.

(45)

Noticing that
\|\nabla f(u)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C^{\prime\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\|A_{j}^{c}(U)-A_{j}^{c}(V)\|_{L^{2}_{ul}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(n+1)\max\{1,c\}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|f(u)-f(v)\right|^{2}dx\leq(n+1)\max\{1,c\}C^{\prime\prime}\|U-V\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
U=(\log\rho_{1},u)
and

. Thus we obtain (

.
∎

Let
k=[s-\frac{n}{2}]
, where
[x]
denotes the integer part of

and
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
. By applying the Sobolev embedding theorem again, we have

.

Let
M

Now we turn back to the Langevin deformation

Theorem 5.3
.

Let

and

. Let

be
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or an
n
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold. Let

-Euler equation with damping
(

be the dual of

. Then

\partial_{t}\omega+d\left(|u|^{p-2}\nabla_{u}u^{*}\right)=-c^{-p}\omega.

(46)

Moreover, if

for

, then for all

, there exists a constant

\|\omega(t)\|_{p}\leq\|\omega(0)\|_{p}e^{\left(C^{\prime}-c^{-2}\right)t}.

In particular, if

is a closed form, so is

, i.e.,

is locally exact. i.e, there exists a smooth function

such that

on

Proof.

Taking exterior differentiation on the both sides of the second equation in
(
15
)
, letting
\omega=du^{*}
and using

, we obtain (
46
). By the proof of Theorem 4.3 in
[

]
, we have

d\nabla_{u}u^{*}-\nabla_{u}du^{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}du_{i}\wedge\nabla_{e_{i}}u^{*},

where
\left\{e_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a local orthonormal frame. Notice that

d\omega=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\omega_{ij}~e_{i}^{*}\wedge e_{j}^{*}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}e_{i}(u_{j})e_{i}^{*}\wedge e_{j}^{*}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle d\left(|u|^{p-2}\nabla_{u}u^{*}\right)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}u_{j}\left(\nabla_{e_{i}}|u|^{p-2}\right)e_{i}^{*}\wedge\nabla_{e_{j}}u^{*}+|u|^{p-2}\left(\nabla_{u}du^{*}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}du_{i}\wedge\nabla_{e_{i}}u^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|u|^{p-2}\nabla_{u}\omega+|u|^{p-4}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n}\left[(p-2)M_{ij}+|u|^{2}\omega_{ij}\right]\omega_{jk}~e_{i}^{*}\wedge e_{k}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|u|^{p-2}\nabla_{u}\omega+I,

where

M_{ij}=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\omega_{il}u_{l}u_{j},\quad I=|u|^{p-4}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n}\left[(p-2)M_{ij}+|u|^{2}\omega_{ij}\right]\omega_{jk}~e_{i}^{*}\wedge e_{k}^{*}\in\Lambda^{2}(T^{*}M).

Noticing that

|I|\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(M,\mu)}^{p-4}\max\left\{(p-2)|M|,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(M,\mu)}^{2}|\omega|\right\}|\omega|,

where
|\cdot|=\|\cdot\|_{\rm HS}
, there exists a constant
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(M,\mu)},\|M\|_{L^{\infty}(M,\mu)},\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(M,\mu)})
, such that

|I|\leq C|\omega|.

Taking inner product with

on the both sides of
(

)

is a constant, and integrating on

\int_{M}\left\langle\frac{D}{dt}\omega,|\omega|^{\gamma-2}\omega\right\rangle dv+\int_{M}\left\langle I,|\omega|^{\gamma-2}\omega\right\rangle dv=-c^{-p}\int_{M}\left\langle\omega,|\omega|^{\gamma-2}\omega\right\rangle dv,

where
\frac{D}{dt}\omega=\partial_{t}\omega+|u|^{p-2}\nabla_{u}\omega
. That is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\left|\omega\right|^{\gamma}dv=

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\left\langle I,|\omega|^{\gamma-2}\omega\right\rangle dv-c^{-p}\int_{M}\left|\omega\right|^{\gamma}dV\leq\left(C^{\prime}-c^{-p}\right)\int_{M}\left|\omega\right|^{\gamma}dv.

Then by the Gronwall’s inequality, we have

\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\gamma}(M)}\leq e^{\left(C^{\prime}-c^{-p}\right)t}\|\omega(0)\|_{L^{\gamma}(M)}.

Moreover, if
\omega(0)=du^{*}(0)=dd\phi_{0}=0
, then
\omega(t)=du^{*}=0
for all
t\in[0,T]
. By the Poincaré lemma,

will keep the gradient form along
t>0
.
∎

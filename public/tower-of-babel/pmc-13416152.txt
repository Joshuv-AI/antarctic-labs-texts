The moderating effect of child abuse on the association between domestic violence and suicidal ideation/self-harm among Lebanese women
Authors: Emmanuelle Awad, Perla Moubarak, Diana Malaeb, Fouad Sakr, Mariam Dabbous, Feten Fekih-Romdhane, Souheil Hallit, Sahar Obeid
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractViolence and abuse against women and children remain prominent issues in Lebanon, resulting in dire psychological consequences. The aims of the current study are to evaluate the moderating role of child abuse in the relationship between (1) domestic violence and suicidal ideation, and (2) domestic violence and self-harm. This cross-sectional study was conducted between January and May 2023. Women between the ages of 18 and 50 were recruited to participate using a snowball sampling technique. 915 women consented to take an online questionnaire that collected information about sociodemographic characteristics. It also included the following scales: Composite Abuse Scale (Revised)—Short Form, Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale, Child Abuse Self-Report Scale (CASRS-12), and Self-Harm Inventory (SHI). Older age (aOR = 0.96) was significantly associated with lower odds of having suicidal ideation, whereas higher domestic violence (aOR = 1.09), psychological child abuse (aOR = 1.36) and child neglect (aOR = 1.09) were significantly associated with higher odds of having suicidal ideation. The interaction domestic violence by sexual abuse (aOR = 1.04) was significantly associated with suicidal ideation in the binary model. In an additional ordinal logistic regression analysis of suicidal ideation severity, older age remained protective, while child neglect and physical abuse were associated with greater severity of suicidal ideation. The interaction domestic violence by child neglect was significantly associated with self-harm. At low (Beta= 0.01; p = .013), moderate (Beta= 0.022; p < .001), and high (Beta= 0.035; p < .001) levels of child neglect, higher domestic violence was significantly associated with more self-harm. The current study results have the potential to guide mental health professionals in implementing further interventions for child abuse and domestic violence. It can encourage a multidisciplinary approach starting with Lebanese community-based programs that combine the efforts of researchers and practitioners in order to reduce the burden of child abuse and domestic violence.Keywords: Child abuse, Domestic violence, Suicidal ideation, Self-harm, LebanonSubject terms: Diseases, Health care, Medical research, Psychology, Psychology, Risk factors

Introduction

Suicidal ideation (SI) is an urgent concern for mental health professionals and public health overall. Women are 3 times more likely than men to experience SI, and as a result attempting suicide1. One of the most prominent risk factors associated with SI among women is familial stress2 and having been through childhood adversity3. Having said that, not all self-injurious behavior is suicidal, it could sometimes entail self-harm (SH). However, SI and SH share similar risk factors for women, including relationship/marital issues and abuse4,5.

Domestic violence (DV) against women is an epidemic that has been increasing6. DV can be defined as committing abusive behaviors towards an intimate partner or family member. Abusive behaviors are not exclusively physical, but can also be psychological and/or sexual. Physical DV is perpetuating physical force against another in order to assert dominance or harm them. Meanwhile, psychological DV is exerting emotional violence to injure another’s mental wellbeing. As for sexual DV, it can be forcing sexual activity on another without their consent. All types of DV are related to poor mental health outcomes among women7.

SI is reflected in the literature as one of the most harmful individual correlates of DV. In fact, DV was found to be a risk factor for suicidal attempts among married women8,9. Sexual, physical and psychological DV were associated with more suicide attempts and higher SI. Furthermore, experiencing DV was associated with higher SI and more SH among both men and women10.

Not only is DV detrimental to women exposed to it, but the impacts extend to future generations. Exposure to DV in childhood predicted future involvement in adulthood11. This could be explained by the results showing that individuals who are exposed to DV have a higher probability of becoming emotionally desensitized to witnessing and committing abuse12.

Child Abuse (CA), including physical, psychological, and sexual maltreatment, and neglect, is implicated in poor health and developmental outcomes such as psychiatric disorders and intellectual delays13. Additionally, CA was predictive of SI in adulthood14. A meta-analysis of previous studies found that all types of CA were associated with a two to three times increased risk of SI. Similarly, CA was a risk factor for SH among young adults in a review of systematic reviews15. More specifically, CA moderated the relationship between stressful life events, which could include DV, and depression in adults16, a psychopathology closely connected to SI and SH17. For women, experiencing CA was related to poorer mental health outcomes overall18. Also, having been through CA and exposure to DV in childhood predicted DV in the future11. These studies assert the role of CA on both social and psychological outcomes.

Despite the substantial literature on the relationships between DV as an independent variable and SI, SH and CA, a gap exists in assessing CA as a moderator. Given that CA was a risk factor and precursor to DV in the studies mentioned previously, it can be hypothesized that it can influence the intensity of the relationship between DV and psychological outcomes, specifically SI and SH. Exploring this association can help guide important interventions that target both CA and DV in women who have been subject to both types of abuse. This is especially important given the significant rates of DV and CA in the Lebanese population19. In Lebanon, 3.2% of children suffer from CA20. A study conducted on a representative sample of women in Lebanon showed that 37.1% suffered from physical DV while 49.4% reported exposure to non-physical DV. On top of that, Lebanon statistically recorded the highest rates of DV against women in the last few years within the Middle East and North Africa region (MENA )19. Having said that, Lebanon has been attempting to make child-protection laws in line with Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), which needs to be reinforced with concrete findings and major reform21. Within the same realm, the Lebanese Parliament passed a law in 2014 that protects women against domestic violence22. Yet, the orders issued in courts to protect DV victims remain very limited, with fatalities still increasing22. Consequently, researchers have been urging communities, legal entities and religious leaders to take action and raise awareness23. For these reasons, the aims of the current study are to evaluate the moderating role of CA in the relationship between1 DV and SI, and2 DV and SH. We hypothesize that CA is a significant moderator between1 DV and SI, and2 DV and SH.

Methods

Study design and participants

Between January 13 and May 8, 2023, a cross-sectional study was conducted. The inclusion criteria comprised women aged 18–50 years currently residing in Lebanon, specifically in one of the five governorates (Beirut, Mount Lebanon, North, South, and Bekaa), who had been in an intimate relationship (defined as a boyfriend/girlfriend or a husband/wife) in the past 12 months, regardless of whether they had experienced intimate partner violence.

Data were collected using a snowball sampling method, which is commonly used when the target population is difficult to identify or reach. The initial participants were recruited through the researchers’ professional and academic networks and were not selected based on prior exposure to domestic violence or child abuse. These initial respondents were then invited to share the survey link with other eligible women within their social networks, allowing the sample to expand originally across different regions and backgrounds.

A questionnaire has been distributed through an online platform created via Google Forms. The survey link directed participants to an introductory page outlining the study objectives, eligibility criteria and ethical considerations. Clear instructions were provided for completing the anonymous questionnaire, along with a consent request indicating that submission of the form constituted informed consent. Participants were assured of the freedom to discontinue their participation and exit the form at any point according to their choice. No financial incentives or academic credits were offered for participation. Efforts were made to enhance sample heterogeneity by encouraging distribution across different Lebanese governorates; however, the potential for sampling bias inherent to snowball sampling is acknowledged.

Minimal sample size calculation

According to the G-power software with an α error of 5%, a power of 80%, and taking into consideration 10 factors to be entered in the multivariate analysis, the results showed that a minimal number of 420 was needed. The final sample included 915 participants, which exceeded this number.

For the logistic regression analysis examining suicidal ideation (n = 197), the events-per-variable ratio exceeded recommended methodological thresholds (> 10–15 events per predictor), including interaction terms. For the moderation analysis conducted using linear regression (PROCESS Model 1), the available sample provided sufficient power to detect small interaction effects.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

Ethics approval for this study was obtained from the ethics committee of the School of Pharmacy at the Lebanese International University (2023RC-028-LIUSOP). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to participation, and the online submission was considered equivalent to receiving a written signature. Participation was anonymous and no identifying information was collected. All data were stored securely in password-protected files accessible only to the research team and were used exclusively for research purposes.

Given the sensitive nature of the study, participants were informed of their right to withdraw from the survey at any time without consequence. At the end of the questionnaire, participants were provided with information on local and international support services available in Lebanon (e.g., mental health services and helplines) in case completing the survey elicited psychological distress, suicidal ideation or other mental health concerns. As data were collected anonymously, direct follow-up by the research team was not possible. All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations.

Questionnaire and variables

First, a pilot study was conducted with 20 people to ensure all questions were clear, understandable; noting that the data related to participants of the pilot study weren’t included in the final data base. The questionnaire was written in Arabic, Lebanon’s native language, and required 10 min to complete. An introduction explained the objective of the study and asked for informed consent from the participants.

The first section assessed participant’s demographic characteristics, such as age, marital status, education level, living situation, as well as questions related to caffeine intake and cigarette smoking. Marital status was defined as: married/unmarried or single, in other terms: Single women, defined as those with a partner (in a relationship) but not married can still be subjected to domestic abuse that can be executed by potential intimate partners, including those in casual dating relationships.

Furthermore, we included questions related to Household Crowding Index (number of persons divided by number of rooms in the house) Financial burden (self-reported on a scale from 1 to 10, with 10 indicating heaviest burden)24, Body Mass Index (calculated according to the World Health Organization formula)25. Physical activity index (calculated by multiplying the intensity by the strength by the frequency of the physical activity26. The second part of the questionnaire included the following scales.

Composite abuse scale (Revised)—short form (CAS R-SF)

The CAS R-SF is a 15-scale used to assess physical, sexual and psychological abuse27. The participants answer a question related to abusive acts done by their partner by “Yes” or “No”. Then, participants indicate the frequency of occurrence from “Not in the past 12 months” to “Daily/almost daily”. The scale exhibited high internal consistency (α = 0.94). The scale was validated for the Lebanese population in Arabic previously (α = 0.95)28.

Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS)

The C-SSRS is a tool that contains 10 items: 5 of which measure suicidal behaviors, and 5 that measure suicidal ideation29. Items are reported on a 0 to 5 scale, “0” being the least severe to “5” being the most severe. The scale has been validated in Arabic for the Lebanese population (α = 0.95)30.

Child Abuse Self-Report Scale (CASRS-12)

The CASRS-12 is a shortened, 12-item version of the Child Abuse Self-Report Scale. The tool includes 4 subscales: psychological abuse, neglect, psychological abuse and sexual abuse (ω = 0.87–0.93)31. It is scored from 0 = Never to 5 = Always. The scale has been validated in Arabic for the Lebanese population, exhibiting good internal consistency31.

Statistical analysis

The SPSS software v.25 was used to analyze the data. SI was analyzed as dichotomous variable to allow consistency with the bivariate analytical framework. The Chi-square test was used to compare two categories, whereas the Student t test was used to compare two means. A logistic regression was conducted afterwards taking the presence vs. absence of SI as the dependent variable and all factors with a p < .25 in the bivariate analysis as independent ones. Interactions between domestic violence by CA were also entered in the final model.

In response to reviewer comments, an additional multivariate ordinal logistic regression analysis was conducted using suicidal ideation severity as an ordinal outcome. Because the highest severity categories had low frequencies, adjacent levels were collapsed to ensure adequate cell sizes and model stability. The proportional odds assumption was assessed using the test of parallel lines.

The SH score was not normally distributed but its LOG transformation was, as verified by the skewness and kurtosis values between ± 1. The Student t and ANOVA tests were used to compare two and three or more means respectively. Pearson test was used to correlate two continuous variables. A linear regression was done afterwards to assess factors associated with SH. The moderation analysis was conducted using PROCESS MACRO v3.4, model 1 taking DV scores as the independent variable, CA subscales as moderators and SH as the dependent variable. P < .05 was deemed statistically significant.

Discussion

The current findings suggest that older age was significantly associated with lower odds of having SI. Meanwhile, higher DV, psychological CA and CN were significantly associated with higher odds of having SI. Furthermore, sexual CA moderated the relationship between DV and SI. CN also moderated the relationship between DV and SH.

In a previous Lebanese study, older age was associated with lower risk of SI32. A large study that assessed suicidal ideation in MENA found that it decreases significantly after the age of 2433. Evidently, older age is related to lower odds of SI in our study and the corresponding region. A previous systematic review showed that individuals are more likely to utilize coping strategies as they get older34. Subsequently, knowing and utilizing more coping strategies could be associated with lower SI35.

Our results highlight the association between DV, psychology CA and CN, which are persistent stressful life events, and SI. Another Lebanese study assessing suicidality found that women who were exposed to stressful life events are at a heightened risk for suicidal ideation36. Consistent with our findings, all types of CA including psychological and CN, along with exposure to domestic violence, were associated with higher SI37. Exposure to chronic stressful life events such as CA, coupled with consistent exposure to recent stressors such as DV may act as contributors to higher suicidal ideation38.

Exposure to stressful life events, such as DV and CA, was related to a 37% increase in SI according to the findings of a systematic review39. This was clarified by our current results where the interaction DV by sexual CA was significantly associated with SI. In women with high DV, having high sexual CA was significantly associated with higher odds of having SI. This indicates that sexual CA moderated the relationship between DV and SI. It can be assumed that exposure to DV among women, with the added trauma of having gone through sexual CA, can be linked to SI. In this case, exposure to CA can contribute to the accumulation of trauma, subsequently co-occurring with higher risks of further physical and psychological trauma40 such as domestic violence and suicidal ideation. Previously, DV mediated the association between sexual CA and SI41. These findings suggest that experiencing sexual CA might significantly increase the probability of being victimized again in the future and having SI. Thereby, the link between DV and poor mental health outcomes such as SI could be stronger among women when sexual CA has previously occurred. It is also important to note that sexual abuse is highly stigmatized in Arab cultures such as the Lebanese one. As a result, women are less likely to disclose exposure to sexual CA, possibly linked to negative social and psychological outcomes such as DV and SI.

In additional analyses, SI was also examined as an ordinal severity outcome. These results indicated that CN and physical CA were associated with greater severity of SI, while older age remained a protective factor. Although the binary model suggested a significant interaction between DV and sexual CA in relation to the presence of SI, this interaction was not retained in the ordinal severity analysis. Overall, these findings suggest that childhood maltreatment may be relevant not only to the occurrence of SI but also to its clinical severity. In fact, previous research has established that CA is significantly associated with more intense SI and suicide attempts42. Having been a victim of CA increased the probability of SI by more than 200%43. Specifically, sexual CA was directly related to higher SI occurrence44, similar to our current results.

The interaction DV by CN was significantly associated with SH. At low, moderate, and high levels of CN, higher DV was significantly associated with more SH. These findings are in accordance with previous ones, where the effect of CN on SH was found to be indirect45. In a previous study, both CN and DV were related to higher SH37. CN can impair a person’s ability to adaptively cope with stressful life circumstances46. Subsequently, exposure to CN can potentially make the relationship between DV and negative outcomes such as SH stronger. In the Lebanese culture, women are less likely to leave abusive relationships47. As a consequence, SH might develop as a poor way to cope with adversities and high stress circumstances48. Adding to that, CN has an indirect effect on the relationship between self-injurious behavior such as SH and negative mental health outcomes49. In fact, CA including CN and utilizing maladaptive coping strategies were associated with higher SH50. Women who have endured CN have a higher risk of using maladaptive coping strategies51, which can subsequently promote SH when experiencing DV48.

Strengths and limitations

First, the number of participants in the study (n = 915) allows the collection of significant data. Subsequently, the current results can possibly be reflective of the general population, particularly that participants were from all areas of Lebanon. Having said that, the snowball sampling technique reduces the potential of obtaining generalizable results. Second, Lebanon is a collectivist developing country where religion and cultural traditions are widely adopted. For that reason, it could reduce the probability of women accurately reporting whether they experienced or are currently experiencing abuse, especially sexual. Third, the online mode of data collection and the use of self-report measures introduces the possibility of common method variance, as subjectivity may influence responses. Fourth, the higher number of unmarried women in our study compared to married women in Lebanon could be due to our study design and snowball sampling technique which could introduce selection or sampling bias. Only women who can read and respond to the Arabic language, and had access to the Internet were eligible to participate. Therefore, the results cannot be extrapolated to the whole population. Also, the current study is cross-sectional, which majorly limits the ability to establish temporal precedence and causality. In addition, although the binary model suggested a significant interaction between DV and sexual CA in relation to the presence of SI, this interaction was not retained in the ordinal severity analysis and should therefore be interpreted with caution. Finally, no information about psychopathology was collected, therefore, we could not identify participants who have pre-existing psychological disorders. Our results could provide incentive for future studies to investigate these variables among clinical and non-clinical samples with diagnosed psychological disorders.

Clinical implications

The results of our study highlight the role of CA in social and psychological outcomes such as DV, SI and SH. By determining the relationships that the variables at hand exhibit, public health professionals can devise specific courses of action to raise awareness on these issues that are significant to the Lebanese culture. CA and DV victims should be offered routine screenings to assess risk for SI and SH. Additionally, mental health experts can adapt trauma care techniques that target CA and DV in order to lessen the chance of SI and SH among Lebanese women.

Conclusion

The current study results have the potential to guide mental health professionals in implementing further interventions for CA and DV. The findings suggest that childhood maltreatment, particularly neglect and physical abuse, is associated with greater severity of SI, while DV and CN moderate the relationship with self-injurious behaviors such as SH. These results can encourage a multidisciplinary approach starting with Lebanese community-based programs that combine the efforts of researchers and practitioners in order to reduce the burden of CA and DV. Longitudinal designs can help establish temporal order between CA, DV and self-injurious behaviors. Further explorations can also help determine why different types of child maltreatment moderate the relationship between SI and SH.

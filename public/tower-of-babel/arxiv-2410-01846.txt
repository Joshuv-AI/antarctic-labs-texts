On the logical structure of physics
Boris Zilber

Abstract
One of the main claims of the paper is that Dirac’s calculus and broader theories of physics can be treated as theories written in the language of Continuous Logic. Establishing its true interpretation (model) is a model theory problem. The paper introduces such a model for the fragment which covers “free theories”, that is physical theories with Gaussian (quadratic) potential. The model is pseudo-finite (equivalently, a limit of finite models), based on a pseudo-finite field in place of the field of complex numbers. The advantage of this unusual setting is that it treats the quantum and the statistical mechanics as just domains in the same model and explains Wick rotation as a natural transformation of the model corresponding to a shift in scales of physical units.



1
Introduction

1.1

The success of model theory in its numerous applications in various areas of mathematics is due, in the first place, to its focus on the fundamental questions: what is the adequate mathematical language of the area? what is the structure that is being studied?

We start here by asking the same questions about physics or rather some parts of physics.
More precisely our focus of interest is in foundations of quantum theory and of statistical physics, the two areas differing by the nature of physical processes and the scales of magnitudes, using different mathematical platforms connected by the formal mathematical trick called “the Wick rotation”.

Statistical physics was properly established in the 19th centure when it was notices that the probability of a state (of a gas) with certain energy
E
and temperatire
T
is proportional to

\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{E}{T}}.

Several decades later quantum physics started with the basic observation that the state of a quantum system of energy
E
at time
t
can only be adequately observed by attributing to it the complex number of norm 1,

\mathrm{e}^{iEt},

called the probability amplitude.

This settings generate certain logic which in the first case one can recognise as a form of the
probabilistic logic
. The second case is in fact more general and can be classified (as yet informally) as the

continuous logic with values in
{\mathbb{C}}.

The typical
n
-ary predicate in the theory has the form

\mathbb{U}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}};\ \ \bar{x}\cdot\mathfrak{u}\mapsto c\cdot\mathrm{e}^{f(\bar{x})}

(1)

where
\mathbb{U}
is the domain of physical units,
\mathfrak{u}\in\mathbb{U},

f:{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
some function (typical for Hamiltonian mechanics) possibly with complex coefficients,
c
a constant.

The quantifiers are of the form

\mathrm{e}^{f(\bar{x},y)}\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{f(\bar{x},y)}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{a(y)}\ dy

and in basic cases the right-hand can be calculated to be of the form
b\cdot\mathrm{e}^{g(\bar{x})}.

In statistical theory
f
and
a
are real functions. In quantum theory these are typically similar functions obtained by changing
f(x,y)
and
a(y)
to
if(x,y)
and
ia(y).
The quantum mechanical version is in fact the calculus in Dirac’s formalism.

The Wick rotation effect is that the calculation of quantifiers for real
f
and
a
return the same result
if(x,y)
and
ia(y)
with respective changes from
b\cdot\mathrm{e}^{g(\bar{x})}

to
b^{\prime}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{ig(\bar{x})},
appropriately defined
b^{\prime}.
The effect can be explained mathematically in many cases but the physical nature of the formal link between statistical and quantum theory remains a mystery.

1.2

In this paper we take seriously the assumption that the logical setting of physics is that of continuous logic (CL) and treat the laws of physics written in terms of CL-formulas as
axioms of a CL-theory
. The problem is to find an
interpretation
of the axioms, that is a class of yet to be defined structures: continuous, finite, or pseudo-finite .

(In
[
3
]
E.Hrushovski solves a similar, albeit in fact inverse, problem of writing down a CL-theory for a class of finite and pseudo-finite structures.)

First of all we need to determine the
universe

\mathbb{U}
as in (
1
) and the structure on it.

A crucial question of significance in physics is that whether
\mathbb{U}
is discrete or continuous. In statistical mechanics
\mathbb{U}
is discrete, and even finite of huge size, by definition. In quantum theory it is currently universally accepted that
\mathbb{U}
is discrete (which follows from the fact that distinct points of the space has to be at the distance at least of the Planck length). It is also consistent to assume more generally the finiteness of the universe if we accept that the age of the universe is finite. These issues are being actively discussed in physics literature.

Now, if we accept that
\mathbb{U}
is finite or pseudo-finite (in the model theory sense) then the range of the maps in (
1
) does not need to be a continuous field, in fact it makes sense to consider logical values in a finite or pseudo-finite field
{\rm F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
in place of
{\mathbb{C}}.
However, there is an obvious difficulty of practical nature.
R.Penrose in his book
The Road to Reality
writes about the prospect of using finite fields in physics:

… It is unclear whether such things really have a significalnt role to play in physics, although the idea has been revived from time to time. If
{\rm F}_{p}
were to take the place of the real-number system, in any significant sense, then
p
has to be very large indeed. … To my mind, a physical theory which depends fundamentally upon some absurdly enormous prime number would be a far more complicated (and improbable) theory than one that is able to depend on a simple notion of infinity. Nevertheless, it is of some interest to pursue these matters. …

One of the main result of our paper is that the setting with pseudo-finite
{\rm F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is quite easily convertible to the setting over the field
{\mathbb{C}}.
Conversely the continuous calculus over
{\mathbb{C}}
(and
{\mathbb{R}}
) can be translated into some meaningful calculations over
{\rm F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
without loss.

1.3

The mathematical tool which is behind the passage between the pseudo-finite setting and
{\mathbb{C}}
is the following diagram first established in
[
2
]
and worked out in the current paper in more detail:

\begin{array}[]{lllllll}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mathsf{lm}_{\mathbb{U}}\\
\ \ \ \ \ \mathbb{U}\ \ \ \ \ \longrightarrow\ \ \ {\bar{{\mathbb{C}}}}\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ \ |\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ |\\
\exp_{\mathfrak{p}}\ |\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ |\exp\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \downarrow\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \downarrow\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mathsf{lm}_{\rm F}\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ {\rm F}_{\mathfrak{p}}\ \ \ \ \longrightarrow\ \ \ \bar{{\mathbb{C}}}\end{array}

(2)

Here
\mathbb{U}
is a pseudo-finite additive group and

\exp_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a surjective homomorphism of the additive group onto
{\rm F}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}.

The horizontal arrows are “limit” maps
\mathsf{lm}_{\mathbb{U}}
and
\mathsf{lm}_{\rm F}
respectively,
\mathsf{lm}_{\rm F}
is a place from the field
{\rm F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
onto
\bar{{\mathbb{C}}}={\mathbb{C}}\cup\{\infty\},
and
\mathsf{lm}_{\mathbb{U}}
is appropriate map for the additive structures.

It is crucial that the limit maps are rather well-controlled, in particular certain natural multpilicative subgroups
{}^{\prime}\mathbb{S}^{\prime},\,{{}^{\prime}{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}}_{+}\subset{\rm F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
map onto the unit circle
\mathbb{S}\subset\bar{{\mathbb{C}}}
and the non-negative reals
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
which allows to mimic polar coordinates of
{\mathbb{C}}
in
{\rm F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and thus develop a working analogue of continuous complex
calculus.

1.4

In essense, the main model theory result of the paper is the construction of a continuous-model ultraproduct of quite intricate finite structures interpreted in

(\mathbb{U}_{p},{\rm F}_{p})
which include wave-functions, finite-dimensional “Hermitian and Euclidean Hilbert spaces” over
{\rm F}_{p},
linear operators on the spaces and other relevant constructs.

One of the main gains of the pseudo-finite setting in terms of foundations of physics is in explaining the effect of Wick rotation as the transformation/homomorphism of
\mathbb{U}
caused by the multiplication

u\mapsto\mathbf{i}\cdot u

where
\mathbf{i}
is a non-standard integer such that

\mathsf{lm}_{\mathbb{U}}(\mathbf{i}\cdot u)=i\cdot\mathsf{lm}_{U}(u)\ \ \ (\ i=\sqrt{-1}\ ).

The action of the “huge” integer
\mathbf{i}
shifts a subdomain
\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{u}}
of the universe
\mathbb{U}
to the subdomain
\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{v}}=\mathbf{i}\cdot\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{u}}.

We associate
\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{u}}
with physics at the scale of statistical mechanics and the Euclidean Hilbert space formalism and
\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{v}}
with quantum mechanics and Hermitian Hilbert spaces. Thus the shift by
\mathbf{i}
is the mathematical form of the change of scales in physics which manifests itself as the Wick rotation.

2
Main results

2.1

We define
\mathbb{U}=\mathbb{U}_{\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{l}}
as the quotient of the additive group of non-standard integers
{{}^{*}\mathbb{Z}}
by the ideal generated by the number
(\mathfrak{p}-1)\cdot\mathfrak{l},

\mathbb{U}:={{}^{*}\mathbb{Z}}/(\mathfrak{p}-1)\cdot\mathfrak{l}

for distinguished parameters: prime
\mathfrak{p}
and a highly divisible
\mathfrak{l}.

\exp_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathbb{U}\to{\rm F}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}

is a homomorphism with the kernel
(\mathfrak{p}-1)\cdot\mathbb{U}
and
\mathbf{i}\in{{}^{*}\mathbb{Z}}
divides
(\mathfrak{p}-1)
and is divisible by
\mathfrak{l}.

2.2

Logical evaluations of
\mathbb{U}
in
{\rm F}_{\mathfrak{p}}.
We think of
{\rm F}_{\mathfrak{p}}

as
the
domain of logical values
as opposed to
\mathbb{U}
and
\mathbb{U}^{M}
as
domains of physical units
. We think of definable operations on
{\rm F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
as logical connectives, where
definable
means interpretable in the non-standard model of arithemetic
{{}^{*}\mathbb{Z}}
with distinguished parameters
\mathfrak{p},

\mathfrak{l}
and the map
\exp_{\mathfrak{p}}.

The map
\exp_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathbb{U}\to{\rm F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
gives rise to evaluations of “physical models” and leads to a basic notion of the Hilbert space formalism of physics, a state (over
{\rm F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
):

\varphi:\mathbb{V}\to{\rm F}_{\mathfrak{p}}

where

\mathbb{V}\subset\mathbb{U}^{M}
is a specific subdomain. A basic state (basic predicate)
\varphi
has the form

\varphi(\bar{x})=\exp_{\mathfrak{p}}(f(\bar{x})\cdot\mathfrak{u})

where
f(\bar{x})
is a polynomial over
{\mathbb{Z}},

\bar{x}\in{{}^{*}\mathbb{Z}}^{N}
and
\mathfrak{u}\in\mathbb{V}.
A general state is obtained from basic ones by using “logical connectives”.

The states (which are of course a certain kind of coordinate functions) form an
{\rm F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-linear space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{V}}
of pseudo-finite dimension, with natural choices of orthonormal bases and well-defined inner product with values in
{\rm F}
or a well-controlled extension of
{\rm F}.
Moreover, we consider definable linear maps on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{V}},
analogues of linear unitary operators playing an important role in physics (such as the Fourier transform and time evolution operators).

The definable family of
position states

{\cal L}_{\bf u}=\{{\bf u}_{r}:\ r\in\mathbb{V}\};\ \ {\bf u}_{r}(s):=\delta(r-s)

(Kronecker-delta) forms a basis of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{V}}.
A definable injective linear operators
\mathcal{A}
give rise to other definable bases

{\cal L}_{\mathcal{A}{\bf u}}=\{\mathcal{A}{\bf u}_{r}:\ r\in\mathbb{V}\}.

In regards to model-theoretic formalism, unlike the traditional approach (see e.g.
[
5
]
),

\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{V}}
is not considered to be a universe of a structure. Instead, we consider the multisorted structure on sorts
{\cal L}_{\psi},
definable bases, with linear maps between these, together with the
{\rm F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-linear space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{V}}
interpretable in the sorts.

2.3

In the current paper we restrict the study to so called
Gaussian setting
. This means that the
f(\bar{x})
defining basic states
\varphi
are
quadratic forms on
{{}^{*}\mathbb{Z}}
(in fact on the ring
{\rm K}_{\mathcal{N}}={{}^{*}\mathbb{Z}}/\mathcal{N},
for
\mathcal{N}=|\mathbb{V}|
)
and the operators are of the form

\mathcal{A}:\ \varphi\mapsto\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}}\sum_{r\in{\rm K}_{\mathcal{N}}}\exp_{\mathfrak{p}}(a(q,r)\mathfrak{u})\cdot\varphi(r),

(3)

where

an element of order

and

These are discrete analogues of quantum mechanics unitary operators for the free particle and the harmonic oscillator. We believe free fields theories should be representable in the setting once we switch to considering domains

for appropriate infinite pseudo-finite

We are in particular interested in studying relationship between two domains
\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{u}}
and
\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{v}}
in

determined by the choices of units,
\mathfrak{u}
and
\mathfrak{v}
respectively, of different scales:
\mathfrak{v}=\mathbf{i}\cdot\mathfrak{u}
, for
\mathbf{i}
described above.

2.4

Our first main result, Thm
6.10
, establishes for the discrete pseudo-finite model a unifying treatment of “physics” over
the two domains:

In fact,

\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{v}}\subset\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{u}}

both equipped with additive non-standard metrics defined in terms of units
\mathfrak{v}
or
\mathfrak{u}
respectively.

The mulitplication by

determines the projection

\mathbf{i}:\ \mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{u}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{v}}=\mathbf{i}\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{u}}.

Given a state

on

where we consider both a formal-Euclidean and a formal-Hermitian versions of inner product.

This gives us the

\{\}^{\mathbf{i}}:\ \mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{u}}}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{u}}}

(4)

Note that a tensor product powers

2.5

Our final task is to recast the pseudo-finite ultraproduct of finite structures underlying the formal Hilbert spaces

\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{u}}},

\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{v}}}

as a continuous logic (CL) structure with values in the complex numbers. The key to the construction are the limit maps
\mathsf{lm}_{\mathbb{U}}
and
\mathsf{lm}_{\rm F}

First we have to convert the domains
\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{u}}
and
\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{v}}
into metric spaces presentable as countable unions of finite diameter subspaces. That is not obvious since the nonstandard-valued dimeters of
\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{u}}
and
\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{v}}
(in units
\mathfrak{u}
and
\mathfrak{v}
respectively) are large nonstandard numbers, however we follow these metrics and define, for
\mathbb{V}:=\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{u}}
and
\mathbb{V}:=\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{v}}
respectively,

\mathbb{V}_{|n}=\{u\in\mathbb{V}:\ \mathrm{dist}(0,u)\leq n\},

\mathbb{V}_{|n}^{\mathsf{lm}}:=\mathsf{lm}_{\mathbb{U}}(\mathbb{V}_{|n})\mbox{ and }\mathbb{V}^{\mathsf{lm}}:=\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathbb{V}_{|n}^{\mathsf{lm}}

It turns out that

\mathbb{V}^{\mathsf{lm}}_{\mathfrak{u}}={\mathbb{R}}\mbox{ and }\mathbb{V}^{\mathsf{lm}}_{\mathfrak{v}}=\imath{\mathbb{R}}

For a state

\varphi^{\mathsf{lm}}:\ \mathbb{V}^{\mathsf{lm}}\to{\mathbb{C}};\ \ \varphi^{\mathsf{lm}}(r^{\mathsf{lm}}):=\mathsf{lm}_{\rm F}(\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}\varphi(r))

Note that we have to use a normalising coefficient, an infinite pseudo-finite number

or

Crucially, in case of

we find it technically necessary to choose the Euclidean inner product, and in case of
\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{v}}

Finally, the linear operators
\mathcal{A}
of the form (

\mathcal{A}^{\mathsf{lm}}:\phi\mapsto\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mathrm{e}^{\alpha(s,x)}\cdot\phi(x)\ dx.

(5)

In terms of continuous logic, this is an existential quantifier (bounded by condition
\mathrm{e}^{\alpha(s,x)}
).

We refer to the two structures as

\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathcal{H}^{\mathsf{lm}}_{\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{u}}}\mbox{ and }\mathcal{H}_{\imath{\mathbb{R}}}:=\mathcal{H}^{\mathsf{lm}}_{\mathbb{V}_{\mathfrak{v}}}

with Euclidean and Hermitian inner products respectively.

2.6

The final result presented in section
8
can be summarised as the following Theorem compairing the two continuous logic structures:

The map (
4

\{\}^{\mathbf{i}}:\ \mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathcal{H}_{\imath{\mathbb{R}}}

realised by the bijection on the CL-position states sorts

{\cal L}^{\mathsf{lm}}_{\bf u}\to{\cal L}^{\mathsf{lm}}_{{\bf u}^{\mathbf{i}}};\ \ {\bf u}_{r}\mapsto{\bf u}_{\imath r},\ \ r\in{\mathbb{R}}

Also, treating the integral operators as quantifiers, the structure allows quantifier elimination.

Future directions.
The extension of the Gaussian setting to free fields theories seems quite feasible. The more general setting requiring perturbation methods is more challenging but does not seem impossible since tools of real and complex analysis are reasonably available in

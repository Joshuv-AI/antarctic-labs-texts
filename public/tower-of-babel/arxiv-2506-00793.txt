Algorithm for computing canonical bases
and foldings of quantum groups

Abstract
. Let 𝐔 q − \mathbf{U}_{q}^{-} be the negative half of the quantum group of finite type. Let P P be the transition matrix between the canonical basis and a PBW basis of 𝐔 q − \mathbf{U}_{q}^{-} . In the case where 𝐔 q − \mathbf{U}_{q}^{-} is symmetric, Antor gave a simple algorithm of computing P P by making use of monomial bases. By the folding theory, 𝐔 q − \mathbf{U}_{q}^{-} (symmetric, with a certain automorphism) is related to the quantum group 𝐔 ¯ q − \underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-} of non-symmetric type. In this paper, we extend the results of Antor to the non-symmetric case, and discuss the relationship between the algorithms for 𝐔 q − \mathbf{U}_{q}^{-} and for 𝐔 ¯ q − \underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-} .



Introduction

Let
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
be the negative half of the quantum group
\mathbf{U}_{q}
of finite type,
associated to the Cartan datum
X=(I,(\ ,\ ))
, with the vertex set
I
.
Hence
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is an associative algebra over
\mathbb{Q}(q)
with generators

f_{i}
(
i\in I
) satisfying
q
-Serre relations.
Let
w_{0}
be the longest element of the Weyl group
W
of
X
, and
for a reduced expression
w_{0}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{iN}
of
W
, consider the
sequence
\mathbf{h}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{N})\in I^{N}
. For such
\mathbf{h}
, a PBW basis

\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}=\{L(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})\mid\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}\}
, and a canonical basis

\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}=\{b(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})\mid\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}\}
of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
are defined.
PBW bases depend on the choice
\mathbf{h}
, but
the canonical basis does not depend on
\mathbf{h}
, which is written as
\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}
.
The canonical basis
\mathbf{B}
was constructed by Lusztig
[
L2
]
in the case where

X
is symmetric of finite type, by using the representation theory of quivers.
In a subsequent paper
[
L3
]
, it was generalized to
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
of symmetric Kac-Moody type,
by making use of the geometry of quivers
(called a geometric categorification of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
). Finally in
[
L4
]
, it was extended to
the case of arbitrary Kac-Moody type, by considering the geometry of quivers with automorphisms
(called a geometric categorification with automorphisms).
At the same time, Kashiwara
[
Ka
]
constructed, for arbitrary
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
of Kac-Moody type,
the global crystal basis by using the theory of crystal basis.
It is known that these two bases are equal (
[
GL
]
,
[
MSZ2
]
).

We return to the case where
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is of finite type.
Canonical bases (or global crystal bases) are important objects for the representation
theory of quantum groups, but the direct computation is very hard. While the construction
of PBW bases is more elementary, and PBW bases are appropriate for explicit computations.
Hence it is desirable to know the transition matrix
P
between
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}
and
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}
.
In
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
, there exists another basis
\mathscr{M}_{\mathbf{h}}=\{m(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})\mid\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}\}

associated to
\mathbf{h}
, called
a monomial basis, consisting of “monomials”
f_{i_{1}}f_{i_{2}}\cdots f_{i_{k}}
of generators.
(See 1.7 for the definition of monomial bases.
Note that monomial bases are not unique even if
\mathbf{h}
is fixed.
We discuss just by fixing one such a basis.)
They were originally introduced in
[
L2
]
for the symmetric case,
as a step for constructing canonical bases.

In the case where
X
is symmetric, Antor proposed an algorithm of computing
the matrix
P
, which is explained as follows. There exists a canonical inner product

(\ ,\ )
on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
. We consider the matrix

\varLambda=\Bigl((m(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h}),m(\mathbf{c}^{\prime},\mathbf{h}))\Bigr)_{\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}

of inner products. Then various base changes among
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}},\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}},\mathscr{M}_{\mathbf{h}}

produce certain matrix equations involving
\varLambda
and
P
. He showed that there exists
a simple algorithm of computing
P
from
\varLambda
. So the problem of computing
P
is reduced to
the computation of the inner products
(F,F^{\prime})
for arbitrary monomials
F,F^{\prime}
.
Based on the geometric categorification of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
due to Lusztig, Antor
gave a closed formula for the inner product
(F,F^{\prime})
. Thus
P
is computable once
the monomial basis
\mathscr{M}_{\mathbf{h}}
is explicitly given.

Here we explain about the folding theory of quantum groups.
Assume that
X
is a symmetric
Cartan datum, with an admissible automorphism
\sigma
on
X
(see 3.1 for the definition).
Then
\sigma
induces an automorphism on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
, which we also denote by
\sigma
.
A Cartan datum
\underline{X}
is defined from the pair
(X,\sigma)
, and one can define a
quantum group
\underline{\mathbf{U}}^{-}_{q}
associated to
\underline{X}
.
The folding theory discusses the relationship between
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
and
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}

(see
[
L4
]
,
[
SZ1
]
,
[
MSZ1
]
).
For example, let
\mathbf{B}
(resp.
\underline{\mathbf{B}}
) be the set of canonical basis of

\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
(resp.
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
).
Then
\sigma
permutes
\mathbf{B}

\mathbf{B}^{\sigma}
of
\sigma
-stable elements in

bijection with
\underline{\mathbf{B}}
. If we choose a suitable
\mathbf{h}
(resp.
\underline{\mathbf{h}}
)
for
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
(resp.
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
),
then a similar result holds for PBW bases, namely,
\sigma
permutes

\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}^{\sigma}

\mathscr{X}_{\underline{\mathbf{h}}}
.

In this paper, we generalize the result of Antor for the non-symmetric case,
in connection with the folding theory of quantum groups. So we consider

\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
and
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
as above.
The algorithm of computing
P
from
\varLambda
works well for
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
, once the monomial
basis is defined. Then the computation of
P
is reduced
to the computation of inner products

for monomials in
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
.

Let
\mathscr{M}_{\mathbf{h}}
be the set of
monomial basis of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
constructed in
[
L2
]
. By mimicking his construction,
and by the aide of the folding theory, we can construct the monomial basis
\mathscr{M}_{\underline{\mathbf{h}}}

for
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
(for a suitable choice of
\mathbf{h}
and
\underline{\mathbf{h}}
).
But note that
\sigma
does not act on the set
\mathscr{M}_{\mathbf{h}}
. We modify his construction, and
define a new monomial basis
\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}_{\mathbf{h}}
.
We show in Proposition 4.18 that
\sigma
permutes the set
\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}_{\mathbf{h}}
,
and the set
\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}_{\mathbf{h}}^{\sigma}
of
\sigma
-fixed elements is in bijection
with the set
\mathscr{M}_{\underline{\mathbf{h}}}
.

So far, a good monomial basis of
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
was constructed,
from a view point of the folding theory,
next step is to find a closed formula for the inner products
(F,F^{\prime})
of monomials
in
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
. The method by Antor based on the geometric categorification can not be applied
directly to our situation. Probably, a geometric categorification with automorphisms will work
for our case.

However, in this paper, we use another type of categorification, the categorification
in terms of KLR algebras. KLR algebras associated to the Cartan datum
X
were introduced by
Khovanov-Lauda
[
KL1
]
and Rouquier
[
R
]
, independently. They have proved that

\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
are categorified in terms of KLR algebras, in the case where
X
is symmetric,
Kac-Moody type. This result was generalized by
[
KL2
]
for the arbitrary Kac-Moody type.
We now consider the folding theory setup, the Cartan data
X
with automorphism
\sigma
,
and
\underline{X}
. Then one can consider the KLR algebra with automorphism, associated to

(X,\sigma)
.
McNamara
[
M
]
categorified
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
associated to
\underline{X}
in terms of
the KLR algebra associated to
(X,\sigma)
, which is an analogue of Lusztig’s geometric

McNamara’s categorification behaves well for the folding theory setup.
We can compute the inner product
(F,F^{\prime})
by making use of the MacKey formula for
the representations of KLR algebras, and we obtain a closed formula for them (Theorem 5.20).

Finally, returning to the original setting, we consider
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
with automorphism

\sigma
, and
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
.
Let
\widetilde{m}(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})
be the (modified) monomial basis on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
, and
let
\varLambda=\Bigl((\widetilde{m}(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h}),\widetilde{m}(\mathbf{c}^{\prime},\mathbf{h}))\Bigr)

be the matrix of the inner products.
Similarly, let

be the monomial basis of
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
, and
consider the matrix
\underline{\varLambda}=\Bigl((m(\underline{\mathbf{c}},\underline{\mathbf{h}}),m(\underline{\mathbf{c}}^{\prime},\underline{\mathbf{h}}))\Bigr)
.
By using Theorem 5.20, we can compute
\varLambda
and
\underline{\varLambda}
explicitly.
We denote by
\varLambda^{\sigma}
the submatrix of
\varLambda
obtained from

\sigma

\widetilde{m}(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h}),\widetilde{m}(\mathbf{c}^{\prime},\mathbf{h})
. We compare these two matrices

\varLambda^{\sigma}
and
\underline{\varLambda}
. Our final result is

is determined completely from the

\varLambda^{\sigma}
.

Let
P
be the transition matrix between the canonical basis

\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}
and the PBW basis
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}
of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
, and
\underline{P}
the transition matrix
between the canonical basis
\mathbf{B}_{\underline{\mathbf{h}}}
and the PBW basis
\mathscr{X}_{\underline{\mathbf{h}}}
of
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}

(under a certain choice
of
\mathbf{h}
and
\underline{\mathbf{h}}
). We can define a submatrix
P^{\sigma}
similarly to
\varLambda^{\sigma}
.
Assume that
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is irreducible. Then the order of
\sigma
is equal to

or 3,
a prime number. It is known by
[
SZ1
]

that
P^{\sigma}\equiv\underline{P}\mod p\mathbb{Z}[q]
.

By our algorithm,
\underline{P}
is determined from
\underline{\varLambda}
. Thus Theorem 6.7 implies that
the matrix
\underline{P}
is determined from the matrix
\varLambda
for
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.
In other words,
\underline{P}
for
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}

is determined completely (not for modulo

) from the information for a monomial basis
of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
, though it is not certain whether
\underline{P}
is determined from

.

1.
Algorithm for computing canonical bases

1.1.

Let
X=(I,(\ ,\ ))
be a Cartan datum of finite type, where

I
is a finite set, and
(\ ,\ )
is a symmetric bilinear form on the

\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
E=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Q}\alpha_{i}
with basis
\alpha_{i}
, satisfying
the property

(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
for any
i\in I
,

\frac{2(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})}{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
for any
i\neq j\in I
.

The Cartan datum
X
is called symmetric if the Cartan matrix
(a_{ij})
is
symmetric, where
a_{ij}=2(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})/(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})
.
Let
Q=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}
be the root lattice of
X
. We set

Q_{+}=\sum_{i\in I}\mathbb{N}\alpha_{i}
, and
Q_{-}=-Q_{+}
.
For
\gamma=\sum_{i}n_{i}\alpha_{i}\in Q
, we define
|\gamma|=\sum_{i}|n_{i}|
.

1.2.

Let
q
be an indeterminate. For an integer
n
, a positive integer
m
, set

[n]=\frac{q^{n}-q^{-n}}{q-q^{-1}},\qquad[m]^{!}=[1][2]\cdots[m],\quad[0]^{!}=1.

Let
\mathbf{A}=\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
be the ring of Laurent polynomials. Then
[n],[m]^{!}\in\mathbf{A}
.
For each
i\in I
, set
q_{i}=q^{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})/2}
. We denote by
[n]_{i}

the element obtained from
[n]
by replacing
q
by
q_{i}
.
[m]^{!}_{i}
is defined similarly.

For the Cartan datum
X
, let
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}=\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}(X)
be the negative half of
the quantum group
\mathbf{U}_{q}
associated to
X
.
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is an associative algebra over

\mathbb{Q}(q)
generated by
f_{i}\ (i\in I)
satisfying the
q
-Serre relations

\sum_{k+k^{\prime}=1-a_{ij}}(-1)^{k}f_{i}^{(k)}f_{j}f_{i}^{(k^{\prime})}=0\quad\text{ for }i\neq j\in I,

where we set
f_{i}^{(n)}=f_{i}^{n}/[n]_{i}^{!}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
be Lusztig’s integral form on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
,
namely, the
\mathbf{A}
-subalgebra of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
generated by
f_{i}^{(n)}
for
i\in I,n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
{}^{*}:\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\to\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
be the anti-automorphism defined by

f_{i}\mapsto f_{i}
for any
i\in I
, which is called the anti-involution on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.

1.3.

\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
has a weight space decomposition
\bigoplus_{\gamma\in Q_{-}}(\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})_{\gamma}
,
where
(\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})_{\gamma}
is a subspace of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
spanned by elements
f_{i_{1}}\cdots f_{i_{k}}

such that
\alpha_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\alpha_{i_{k}}=-\gamma
.
x\in\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is called homogeneous
with
\operatorname{wt}x=\gamma
if
x\in(\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})_{\gamma}
. We define a multiplication
on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\otimes\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
by

(x_{1}\otimes x_{2})\cdot(x_{1}^{\prime}\otimes x_{2}^{\prime})=q^{-(\operatorname{wt}x_{2},\operatorname{wt}x_{1}^{\prime})}x_{1}x_{1}^{\prime}\otimes x_{2}x_{2}^{\prime},

where
x_{1},x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2},x_{2}^{\prime}
are homogeneous in
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
. Then
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\otimes\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}

turns out to be an associative algebra with respect to this twisted product.
One can define a homomorphism
r:\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\to\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\otimes\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
by

r(f_{i})=f_{i}\otimes 1+1\otimes f_{i}
for each
i\in I
.
There exists a unique bilinear form
(\ ,\ )
on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
satisfying the following
properties;
(1,1)=1
, and

\displaystyle(f_{i},f_{j})

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}(1-q_{i}^{2})^{-1},

\displaystyle(x,y^{\prime}y^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=(r(x),y^{\prime}\otimes y^{\prime\prime}),

\displaystyle(x^{\prime}x^{\prime\prime},y)

\displaystyle=(x^{\prime}\otimes x^{\prime\prime},r(y)),

where the bilinear form on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\otimes\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is defined by

(x_{1}\otimes x_{2},x_{1}^{\prime}\otimes x_{2}^{\prime})=(x_{1},x_{1}^{\prime})(x_{2},x_{2}^{\prime})
.
Thus defined bilinear form is symmetric and non-degenerate.
The bilinear form
(\ ,\ )
is called the inner product on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.

The inner product satisfies the property

(1.3.1)

\bigl((\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})_{\gamma},(\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})_{\gamma^{\prime}}\bigr)=0\quad\text{ if }\quad\gamma\neq\gamma^{\prime}.

1.4.

Let
\varDelta
be the root system associated to
X
, and
\varDelta^{+}
the set of
positive roots.
Let
W
be the Weyl group of
X
which is generated by
simple reflections
s_{i}
corresponding to
\alpha_{i}
.
Let
w_{0}
be the longest element in
W
, and
let
w_{0}=s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{N}}
be a reduced expression of
w_{0}
,
where
N=|\varDelta^{+}|
. We set
\mathbf{h}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{N})
, and denote it
a reduced sequence of
w_{0}
. We fix a reduced sequence
\mathbf{h}
. For each

1\leq k\leq N
, set
\beta_{k}=s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{k-1}}(\alpha_{i_{k}})
.
It is known that
\beta_{k}\in\varDelta^{+}
, and
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{N}
are all distinct.
Thus
\varDelta^{+}=\{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{N}\}
, and
\mathbf{h}
determines a total order on
the set
\varDelta^{+}
.

Let
T_{i}:\mathbf{U}_{q}\to\mathbf{U}_{q}
be the braid group action (which coincides with
Lusztig’s braid group action
T_{i,1}^{\prime\prime}
in
[
L4
]
), and set

F_{\beta_{k}}^{(n)}=T_{i_{1}}T_{i_{2}}\cdots T_{i_{k-1}}(f_{i_{k}}^{(n)}).

Then
F_{\beta_{k}}^{(n)}\in\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
, and
\operatorname{wt}(F_{\beta_{k}}^{(n)})=-n\beta_{k}
.
If
n=1
, we write it as
F_{\beta_{k}}
.

F_{\beta_{k}}
is called a root vector in
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
corresponding to
the root
\beta_{k}
. For
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
,
we define

L(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})=F_{\beta_{1}}^{(c_{1})}F_{\beta_{2}}^{(c_{2})}\cdots F_{\beta_{N}}^{(c_{N})}.

Then the set
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}=\{L(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})\mid\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}\}
gives a basis
of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
, which is called the PBW basis of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
associated to
\mathbf{h}
.

\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}
gives an
\mathbf{A}
-basis of
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.

The inner product for PBW bases were computed in
[
L4
, 38.2]
as follows.
For
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{N}),\mathbf{c}^{\prime}=(c_{1}^{\prime},\dots,c_{N}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
, we have

(1.4.1)

\bigl(L(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h}),L(\mathbf{c}^{\prime},\mathbf{h})\bigr)=\prod_{k=1}^{N}(f_{i_{k}}^{(c_{k})},f_{i_{k}}^{(c^{\prime}_{k})})=\prod_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{c_{k},c_{k}^{\prime}}\prod_{d=1}^{c_{k}}\frac{1}{1-q_{i_{k}}^{2d}}.

In particular,
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}
is an orthogonal basis of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}

with respect to the inner product.

1.5.

The bar involution
{}^{-}:\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\to\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is an
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
automorphism given by
q\mapsto q^{-1},f_{i}\mapsto f_{i}\ (i\in I)
.
We define a total order on
\mathbb{N}^{N}
by
\mathbf{c}<\mathbf{c}^{\prime}
, for

\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{N}),\mathbf{c}^{\prime}=(c_{1}^{\prime},\dots,c_{N}^{\prime})
, if and only if
there exists
k
such that
c_{1}=c_{1}^{\prime},\dots,c_{k-1}=c_{k-1}^{\prime}
and
c_{k}<c_{k}^{\prime}
.
The following triangularity property holds for PBW basis;

(1.5.1)

\overline{L(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})}=L(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})+\sum_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}>\mathbf{c}}a_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime},\mathbf{c}}L(\mathbf{c}^{\prime},\mathbf{h})\quad\text{ with }\quad a_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime},\mathbf{c}}\in\mathbf{A}.

By making use of (1.5.1), the following result is proved.

(1.5.2) For a given
L(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})
, there exists a unique element

b(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})
satisfying the properties

(i)
\overline{b(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})}=b(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})
,

(ii)

b(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})=L(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})+\sum_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}>\mathbf{c}}p_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime},\mathbf{c}}L(\mathbf{c}^{\prime},\mathbf{h}),\quad\text{ with }\quad p_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime},\mathbf{c}}\in q\mathbb{Z}[q]
.

Then
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}=\{b(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})\mid\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}\}
gives a basis of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
,
and an
\mathbf{A}
basis of
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.

\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}
is called the canonical basis of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
associated to
\mathbf{h}
.

Remark 1.6.

In the case where
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is of symmetric type,
the canonical basis
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}
was constructed in
[
L2
]
, by making use of
the representation theory of quivers, and it was shown that
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}

does not depend on
\mathbf{h}
, which
we denote as
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}=\mathbf{B}
. For the non-symmetric case,
the independence of
\mathbf{h}
was verified by Saito
[
S
]
by making use of the theory
of crystal bases. Thus the canonical basis
\mathbf{B}
exists.

1.7.

Besides the PBW basis
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}
, and the canonical basis
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}
,
we consider another type of basis associated to
\mathbf{h}
.
A basis
\mathscr{M}_{\mathbf{h}}=\{m(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})\mid\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}\}

is called a monomial basis if it satisfies the properties;

(1.7.1)

(i)

m(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})
is written as a “monomial” of generators
f_{i}^{(n)}

such as
m(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})=f_{i_{1}}^{(d_{1})}f_{i_{2}}^{(d_{2})}\cdots f_{i_{k}}^{(d_{k})}

for
i_{k}\in I,d_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
.

(ii)

m(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})
is written as

m(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})=L(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})+\sum_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}>\mathbf{c}}h_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime},\mathbf{c}}L(\mathbf{c}^{\prime},\mathbf{h})\quad\text{ with }\quad h_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime},\mathbf{c}}\in\mathbf{A}.

Note that any element in
\mathscr{M}_{\mathbf{h}}
is bar-invariant, hence
the monomial basis is regarded as an intermediate object between the PBW basis and
the canonical basis. But
\mathscr{M}_{\mathbf{h}}
is not unique for a given
\mathbf{h}
, even if it exists.

Remark 1.8.

Remark 4.8.

In the original form of Theorem 4.7, Lusztig uses

Information-Energy Capacity Region for SLIPT Systems over Lognormal Fading Channels: A Theoretical and Learning-Based Analysis
Nizar Khalfet, Kapila W. S. Palitharathna, Symeon Chatzinotas, Ioannis Krikidis

Abstract
This paper presents a comprehensive analysis of the information-energy capacity region for simultaneous lightwave information and power transfer (SLIPT) systems over lognormal fading channels. Unlike conventional studies that primarily focus on additive white Gaussian noise channels, we study the complex impact of lognormal fading, which is prevalent in optical wireless communication systems such as underwater and atmospheric channels. By applying the Smith’s framework for these channels, we demonstrate that the optimal input distribution is discrete, characterized by a finite number of mass points. We further investigate the properties of these mass points, especially at the transition points, to reveal critical insights into the rate-power trade-off inherent in SLIPT systems. Additionally, we introduce a novel cooperative information-energy capacity learning framework, leveraging generative adversarial networks, to effectively estimate and optimize the information-energy capacity region under practical constraints. Numerical results validate our theoretical findings, illustrating the significant influence of channel fading on system performance. The insights and methodologies presented in this work provide a solid foundation for the design and optimization of future SLIPT systems operating in challenging environments.



I
Introduction

Optical wireless communication (OWC) has been recognized as a promising technology to achieve high speed, low latency, and highly secure communication. Thanks to the use of the optical spectrum, OWC overcomes the current spectrum limitation of radio frequency technology, and hence is suitable for many applications in future wireless systems such as 6G networks. OWC has been categorized into many technologies depending on the application environment, such as free-space optical communication, visible light communication, light-fidelity, underwater optical wireless communication, and non-terrestrial satellite communication
[
Chowdhury_2018
]
. Light-emitting diodes (LEDs)/laser diodes (LD) are used as transmitters, while photodiodes (PD)/photovoltaic (PV) cells are used for receivers. Moreover, the existing lighting infrastructures can be easily adapted for simultaneous illumination, information transfer, and lightwave power transfer
[
Chowdhury_2018
]
.

Recently, simultaneous lightwave information and power transfer (SLIPT) has emerged as a new communication paradigm that exploits optical signals for the dual purpose of information and energy transfer
[
Ding_2018
,
Vasilis_2023
]
. Specifically, SLIPT is promising to charge batteries in energy-deprived devices/sensors efficiently, specifically in applications where other wireless charging methods are challenging, such as underwater, inter-satellite, vehicular, and indoor systems
[
Vasilis_2023
]
. In the foundational analysis of SLIPT in
[
Ding_2018
]
, the maximization of energy transfer is considered while satisfying quality of service constraints. In
[
Zhang_2019
]
, the minimum rate maximization and the transmit power minimization problems have been studied. Various SLIPT policies, receiver designs, and applications have been presented in
[
Geofeng_2019
]
, while the rate-power tradeoff in SLIPT has been studied in
[
Sahand_2021
]
. Moreover, SLIPT has been used in various use cases
[
Abdelhady_2020
,
Liu_2021
,
Sait_2019
]
. In
[
Abdelhady_2020
]
, a beamforming design for a multi-cell setup has been investigated to maximize the data rate. In
[
Liu_2021
]
, resonant beams have been used to achieve highly efficient energy transfer. Finally, SLIPT has also been applied to underwater communication systems in
[
Sait_2019
]
.

It is well known that closed-form expressions for the exact capacity of OWC systems are unknown, and thus, several lower and upper bounds have been derived
[
Chaaban_2017
,
Lapidoth_2009
]
. Furthermore, the study of the information-energy capacity region for SLIPT has been overlooked in the literature, while a bound is presented in
[
Nikita_2023
]
. Most of the aforementioned studies on SLIPT systems focus on simple additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) channels with a static path loss, and therefore the impact of the channel fading on the optimal input distribution has not been investigated. However, SLIPT systems undergo fading effects and several fading models have been used in the literature
i.e.
, lognormal, gamma-gamma, generalized gamma, and exponential gamma
[
Jamali_2018
]
. As a widely used fading model, the lognormal distribution has been used to model the fading in underwater weak turbulence conditions as well as in over-the-air systems with fog/weak atmospheric turbulence effects
[
Jamali_2018
,
Yang_2016
]
. Hence, it is crucial to study the exact information-energy capacity region and the optimal input distribution of SLIPT systems under lognormal fading conditions for future wireless system designs.

On the other hand, OWC transmitters are subjected to peak-power (PP) and average-power (AP) constraints. To avoid the non-linear distortions in the transmit signals, LED/LD needs to operate in the linear operation range; this imposes a PP constraint on the transmit signal
[
Chaaban_2017
,
Lapidoth_2009
]
. Moreover, an AP constraint is required due to safety reasons (eye safety) and practical limitations
[
Lapidoth_2009
]
. In SLIPT systems, the energy harvesting (EH) receiver captures energy from the received signal to either charge a battery or power device operations. This requirement imposes an EH constraint on the system, which must be carefully satisfied to ensure efficient operation of the device
[
Zhang_2019
]
. While the majority of studies have adopted a linear EH model for simplicity
[
Zhang_2019
]
, recent work has increasingly focused on nonlinear EH models
[
Nikita_2023
,
Sait_2019
]
, which are inline with the practical hardware characteristics of EH circuits. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, this is the first work that studies the information-energy capacity region and optimal input distribution of SLIPT systems with PP, AP, EH constraints and nonlinear EH model over lognormal-fading channels from an information-theoretic standpoint. While
[
Chan_2005
]
provides a theoretical foundation for capacity-achieving distributions under conditionally Gaussian channels, our analysis differs significantly in the underlying channel model and constraints. The conditional probability density function in
[
Chan_2005
]
follows a Gaussian distribution, whereas our model incorporates a lognormal fading component, leading to non-trivial modifications in capacity characterization. Additionally,
[
Chan_2005
]
considers an AP constraint as the primary constraint, while our work introduces a more complex set of constraints, including a non-linear EH constraint that further generalizes the optimization problem. As a result, the framework in
[
Chan_2005
]
is not directly applicable to our problem. The work in
[
Ahmad_2018
]
investigates the discreteness of capacity-achieving distributions for fading channels with amplitude constraints. However, it primarily focuses on channels where the input distribution is symmetrically constrained in the range
[-A,A]
, whereas our OWC system employs an intensity modulation scheme, which restricts inputs to the non-negative range
[0,A]
. Moreover,
[
Ahmad_2018
]
studies channels with arbitrarily bounded fading distributions, whereas our analysis explicitly considers lognormal fading, which has an unbounded support and introduces different structural properties for the capacity-achieving distribution.

In recent years, data-driven models have gained significant attraction in the domain of physical layer communications
[
Data
]
. The versatility and adaptability of deep learning (DL) approaches have made them highly effective for a variety of tasks such as signal detection, decoding, and channel estimation. Despite the clear advantages in these areas, the application of DL to the fundamental problem of channel capacity estimation remains relatively underexplored
[
DataEstim
]
. A notable advancement in the application of machine learning to information theory is the development of the cooperative channel capacity learning (CORTICAL) framework, which proposes a novel approach to learn the capacity and capacity-achieving distribution for discrete-time continuous memoryless vector channels
[
Letizia_2023
]
. This framework uses a cooperative methodology inspired by generative adversarial networks (GANs), where two neural networks operate in tandem: one network (generator) learns to provide samples from the capacity-achieving distribution, while the other (discriminator) differentiates paired channel input-output samples from unpaired ones. Importantly, the GAN-based approach can be generalized for different channel models by adjusting the underlying training data to reflect varying channel characteristics, such as those influenced by atmospheric conditions. Although there are promising attempts to leverage neural networks for mutual information estimation, it is still an open question whether these methods can yield new insights into capacity regions, especially under complex channel conditions like those encountered in SLIPT systems with non-linear EH constraints.

This work extends previous analyses on the information-energy capacity region for simultaneous wireless information and power transfer (SWIPT) systems by considering a lognormal fading channel, which presents unique challenges compared to the conventional Rayleigh fading model studied in
[
Khalfet_2023
]
. The fading statistics significantly alter the structure of the capacity-achieving input distribution, requiring a novel adaptation of Smith’s framework to establish discreteness under these conditions. Additionally, unlike prior works that primarily examine information transfer, we impose simultaneous PP, AP, and EH constraints, making our analysis more applicable to practical SLIPT systems. Furthermore, the proposed GAN-based learning framework is specifically tailored to SLIPT, incorporating multi-receiver constraints and non-linear energy harvesting requirements. These distinctions set our work apart from the studies in
[
Letizia_2023
]
and
[
Khalfet_2023
]
.

This paper studies the information-energy capacity region for SLIPT systems over lognormal-fading channels. Specifically, a system where a single transmitter sends data to an information receiver and lightwave power to an EH receiver is considered. We assume a lognormal-fading channel with path loss while PP, AP, and EH constraints are taken into account. An expression for the transition probability distribution of the lognormal channel is derived. By using Smith’s framework
[
Smith_1971
]
and employing a Hermite polynomial basis to derive the channel’s transition probability, we prove that the optimal input distribution is discrete, characterized by a finite number of mass points
[
Kapila_2024
]
. Furthermore, beyond characterizing the properties of the optimal input distribution changes, we propose an achievable input distribution for the high SNR regime in lognormal channels. Additionally, we investigate the properties of the input distribution in scenarios with low PP constraints, where the binary input distribution is no longer optimal. This represents a critical point in SLIPT systems, where a trade-off between information transfer and energy harvesting becomes apparent. In addition to these proposed mathematical framework, we introduce the Cooperative Information-Energy Capacity Learning (CIECL) framework. The CIECL framework represents a significant advancement in the optimization of SLIPT systems, particularly in environments with non-linear EH constraints. Traditional approaches such as the Blahut-Arimoto (BA) algorithm
[
blahut
]
or convex optimization techniques rely on discretizing the input space and iteratively refining the input distribution to maximize mutual information. However, these methods have notable limitations in the presence of continuous, non-convex constraints, especially when EH models introduce additional non-linearities. Furthermore, BA-based approaches scale poorly for high-dimensional distributions and require restrictive assumptions about the structure of the optimal input. To overcome these challenges, we introduce a GAN-based optimization framework, which offers a data-driven alternative to traditional analytical methods. Instead of predefining an input space, the generator network learns the capacity-achieving distribution dynamically, refining it through adversarial training with a discriminator that estimates mutual information. This adaptive learning approach enables the model to efficiently explore the space of optimal input distributions under SLIPT constraints, without requiring explicit knowledge of the optimal distribution’s parametric form. Compared to conventional approaches, our GAN-based framework (i) avoids the need for discretization, (ii) generalizes across different constraints and channel conditions, and (iii) efficiently captures the interplay between information transfer and EH constraints. The numerical results in Section VII demonstrate the effectiveness of this method in approximating the optimal input distribution while satisfying practical SLIPT constraints. Our findings indicate that GAN-based optimization not only recovers previously known structural properties of optimal input distributions but also extends the analysis to more complex channel conditions that were previously intractable using classical methods. Unlike the conventional GAN-based capacity learning framework in
[
Letizia_2023
]
, which focuses on generic point-to-point communication systems, our approach is uniquely designed for SLIPT systems. We introduce modifications to the value function to explicitly enforce PP, AP, and EH constraints. Additionally, we extend the learning model to handle dual-receiver scenarios, where one receiver is used for information decoding and the other for EH. This necessitates modifications in the discriminator’s architecture, which now takes into account both received signal variations from the PD and PV cell. To further ensure compliance with optical wireless transmission constraints, we modify the activation function in the generator’s output layer, applying a rectified linear unit (ReLU)-based function that enforces the non-negativity and PP limits of the transmitted signal. These improvements make the learning approach well-suited for optimizing SLIPT system performance under practical constraints. The analysis of lognormal and Gaussian fading models for OWC has been widely studied in the literature, particularly in the context of turbulence-induced fading and channel capacity characterization. Previous studies have primarily focused on deriving capacity bounds and identifying optimal input distributions under PP and AP constraints. However, these studies have largely neglected the impact of SLIPT, where EH constraints must also be considered. In this work, we extend and generalize prior analyses by incorporating a nonlinear EH constraint of the form
\mathbb{E}[bX\ln(1 + cX)]\geq E_{\text{th}}
, which significantly affects the feasible set of input distributions and impact the optimal input allocation strategy. Unlike conventional capacity optimization problems that focus only on maximizing mutual information, our framework ensures that the derived input distributions are not only optimal in terms of information transfer but also satisfy minimum EH requirements for energy sustainability. The inclusion of EH constraints introduces new trade-offs in the design of input distributions, where maximizing information rate must be balanced against the need to maintain sufficient harvested energy. Our results, illustrated in Fig. 4 and Fig. 5, demonstrate how these additional constraints influence the structure of the capacity-achieving input distributions, providing insights that go beyond existing literature. By integrating EH considerations into the capacity analysis of lognormal fading channels, this work presents a more comprehensive and practically relevant framework for the design of SLIPT-enabled OWC systems.

The key technical contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

•

We show that the optimal input distribution for SLIPT systems is discrete, consisting of a finite number of mass points. This result is obtained by using Smith’s framework and employing a Hermite polynomial basis to derive the channel’s transition probability. We numerically show that the information energy capacity region of the Gaussian channel outperforms that of the conventional lognormal channel.

•

We propose an achievable input distribution for the high SNR regime in lognormal fading channels. Additionally, we investigate the properties of the optimal input distribution under low PP constraints, where the binary input distribution is no longer optimal. This analysis highlights a critical point in SLIPT systems, where the trade-off between information transfer and energy harvesting becomes significant.

•

We introduce the CIECL framework, which utilizes advanced DL techniques to jointly optimize input distributions and accurately estimate the information-energy capacity region. This adaptive framework is specifically designed to effectively manage diverse system constraints, including AP, PP, and EH constraints, as well as varying channel conditions. Therefore, it is capable of learning the capacity-achieving input distribution even under challenging non-Shannon settings.

III
Discreteness of the Optimal Input Distribution

The discreteness of the optimal input distribution for maximizing mutual information in IM/DD channels has been well established in prior works, including
[
Lapidoth_2009
]
, which analyzed the capacity of IM/DD optical channels under PP and/or AP constraints. These studies demonstrated that, for the AWGN channel, the capacity-achieving input distribution consists of a finite number of mass points. However, our analysis extends these results by considering a more general setting that includes lognormal fading, which introduces additional variations in the received signal that were not accounted for in previous AWGN-based formulations. Furthermore, unlike prior studies that primarily focused on power constraints, our optimization problem incorporates a nonlinear EH constraint, represented by
\mathbb{E}[bX\ln(1 + cX)]\geq E_{\text{th}}
, which fundamentally impact the optimal input distribution. Additionally, our work considers a practical SLIPT system where a PD is used for information reception, while a PV cell is used for EH, introducing new trade-offs between maximizing mutual information and ensuring minimum harvested energy. After deriving the discrete properties of the optimal input distribution, it is natural to extend the analysis to explore the behavior of the input distribution under specific channel conditions. In particular, the high SNR regime presents an interesting case where the impact of noise becomes negligible, allowing us to derive an analytical expression for an achievable input distribution. This regime is important for characterizing system performance under favorable conditions and for understanding how AP and EH constraints shape the information energy capacity region of SLIPT systems. Therefore, in the following section, we present a detailed analysis of the optimal input distribution for the lognormal channel in the high SNR regime.

IV
Optimal Input Distribution for Lognormal Channel in High SNR Regime

In this section, we derive the optimal input distribution for the lognormal fading channel under the high SNR regime, considering the PP, AP, and EH constraints. Unlike the AWGN channel, the lognormal fading introduces more complexity, which we address by directly working with the original form of the EH constraint. By following the discussion in Section
III
, the optimal input distribution is discrete. For simplicity, we consider a discrete distribution for the input
x
over the equally-spaced alphabet
x\in\{0,l,2l,\ldots,(N-1)l\}
, where
0\leq lk\leq A,\forall k\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
,
l=\frac{A}{N-1}
, and

is the number of symbols in the alphabet. The probability mass function (pmf) of

is assigned to each point such that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}p_{x}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1,

(20a)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}lkp_{x}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon,

(20b)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N}\left(blk\ln\left(1+clk\right)\right)p_{x}(k)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle E_{th}.

(20c)

satisfies the non-negativity, PP, AP, and EH constraints.

The objective is to maximize the mutual information

and output

\displaystyle I(F)\approx\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}p_{x}(k)\log_{2}\left(\frac{p(y|x_{k})}{p(y;F)}\right),

The Lagrangian function incorporating the constraints in (

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(p_{x}(k),\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3})\hskip-1.42262pt=\hskip-1.42262ptI(F)-\hskip-2.15277pt\lambda_{1}\hskip-1.42262pt\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}l_{k}p_{x}(k)-\varepsilon\right)\hskip-1.42262pt-

(22)

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}\left(E_{th}\hskip-2.15277pt-\hskip-2.15277pt\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}bl_{k}\ln(1 + c l_k)p_{x}(k)\right)\hskip-2.15277pt-\hskip-2.15277pt\lambda_{3}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}p_{x}(k)-1\right),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial p_{x}(k)}\hskip-2.15277pt=\hskip-2.15277pt\log_{2}\left(\frac{p(y|x_{k})}{p(y;F)}\right)\hskip-2.15277pt-\hskip-2.15277pt\lambda_{1}l_{k}\hskip-2.15277pt-\hskip-2.15277pt\lambda_{2}bl_{k}\ln(1 + c l_k)\hskip-2.15277pt-\hskip-2.15277pt\lambda_{3}=0.

\displaystyle p_{x}(k)=\frac{1}{Z}\exp\left(-\lambda_{1}l_{k}-\lambda_{2}bl_{k}\ln(1 + c l_k)\right),

\displaystyle Z=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\exp\left(-\lambda_{1}l_{k}-\lambda_{2}bl_{k}\ln(1 + c l_k)\right).

The optimal input distribution in the high SNR regime balances the contributions from the AP and EH constraints. The derived expression

\displaystyle p_{x}(k)=\frac{1}{Z}\exp\left(-\lambda_{1}l_{k}-\lambda_{2}bl_{k}\ln(1 + c l_k)\right),

This analytical expression provides a practical approach to determining the achievable input distribution in high SNR scenarios, where the noise is negligible, and the system operates near its capacity limits. After characterizing the achievable input distribution for the high SNR regime, we will focus now on the particular scenario where the binary input distribution is no longer optimal. This is a critical point in SLIPT systems, since the binary distribution maximizes both information and energy transfer simultaneously and therefore there is not a trade-off between them. In Section V, we analyze the properties of the mass points under these conditions and characterize a sufficient condition on the PP constraint
A
, where the binary distribution is no longer optimal.

We provide an analysis of the properties of the optimal input distribution

In the GAN framework, two neural networks named, generator (

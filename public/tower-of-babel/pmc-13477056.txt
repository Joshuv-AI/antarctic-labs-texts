Heart transplantation outcomes in patients with Fontan circulation: Updated systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Andrey Semyashkin, Alina Sandica, Stephan Schubert, Stephan Molatta, Leonid Gherta, Lotfi Ben Mime
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveA subset of patients with Fontan circulation develops progressive circulatory failure requiring advanced therapies such as heart transplantation. We performed an updated systematic review and meta-analysis to evaluate survival and complication rates after the Fontan operation and subsequent transplantation.MethodsMEDLINE, Embase, Web of Science, and Cochrane CENTRAL were searched from inception to January 1, 2026, for randomized trials, cohort studies, and registry analyses reporting survival, complications, or interventions after Fontan completion. Primary end points were peritransplant mortality, transplant-free survival, and posttransplant survival at 1, 5, and 10 years. Secondary end points included protein-losing enteropathy, Fontan-associated liver disease, arrhythmia, and transplantation-related outcomes. Random-effects meta-analyses of logit-transformed proportions were performed using Hartung-Knapp adjustment.ResultsFifteen transplant cohorts comprising 1186 patients with Fontan circulation undergoing heart transplantation were included. Early mortality after Fontan completion remained low (∼1%) and was not pooled. Peritransplant mortality ranged from 8% to 31%, with a pooled estimate of 17.3% (95% CI, 12.7-22.9%; I2 = 62.8%; 95% prediction interval ≈6%-33%). Pooled 1-year posttransplant survival was 81.1% (95% CI, 74.9-85.9%; I2 = 73.5%; prediction interval ≈67%-94%). Pooled survival was 70.1% (95% CI, 64.3-75.3%) at 5 years and 60.1% (95% CI, 54.1-65.7%) at 10 years. Moderate heterogeneity was observed and was attributable to differences in era, center experience, and patient selection.ConclusionsThis updated meta-analysis demonstrates that peritransplant mortality in patients with Fontan circulation remains substantial (∼17%), whereas long-term survival after transplantation is acceptable, approximating 70% at 5 years and 60% at 10 years.

Heart transplantation outcomes in patients with Fontan circulation.

Central Message

Heart transplantation in Fontan circulation remains associated with substantial peritransplant mortality and provides contemporary benchmark estimates for this high-risk population.

Perspective

The Fontan operation has improved survival in patients with single-ventricle congenital heart disease, yet the circulation remains physiologically fragile. Heart transplantation offers acceptable survival in Fontan failure but remains associated with substantial peritransplant mortality. These findings provide updated benchmark estimates for risk assessment in this complex population.

The Fontan operation is the definitive palliation for children born with single-ventricle congenital heart disease. By directing systemic venous return to the pulmonary arteries without an intervening pump, the Fontan circulation alleviates cyanosis and volume overload but creates chronic venous hypertension and limited preload. Advances in surgical technique, perioperative care, and patient selection have reduced early mortality to approximately 1% and yielded 5-year transplant-free survival of approximately 95%.1 Nevertheless, the absence of a subpulmonary ventricle predisposes to ventricular dysfunction, arrhythmia, thromboembolism, protein-losing enteropathy (PLE), and Fontan-associated liver disease (FALD).2,3 An increasing number of adolescents and adults present with progressive “Fontan failure,” for which heart transplantation (HTx) remains the ultimate therapy.4

Previous meta-analyses have focused on transplant outcomes or specific complications but have not integrated data from emerging registries and the full spectrum of published transplant cohorts. International consortia such as the Fontan Outcome Registry using Cardiovascular Magnetic Resonance Examinations, EUROFontan, the Australian and New Zealand Fontan Registry, and the Society of Thoracic Surgeons Congenital Heart Surgery Database now provide large longitudinal datasets.5,6 Several single-center series and registry-based studies have reported outcomes after HTx in patients with Fontan circulation, demonstrating widely variable peritransplant mortality and posttransplant survival.7, 8, 9 We sought to update the evidence base on survival and complications after Fontan completion by incorporating an additional single-center transplant series alongside registry data. Therefore, we performed an updated systematic review and meta-analysis integrating contemporary transplant cohorts and registry data to quantify peritransplant mortality, define posttransplant survival after heart transplantation in patients with Fontan circulation, and summarize major late complications associated with the Fontan physiology.10

Methods

Study Design and Reporting Standards

This systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted in accordance with the 2020 Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines and the Meta-analysis Of Observational Studies in Epidemiology recommendations.

Search Strategy

Comprehensive searches of MEDLINE, Embase, Web of Science, and Cochrane CENTRAL were conducted (inception to 1 January 2026) using Medical Subject Headings terms and key words related to “Fontan,” “univentricular,” “heart transplantation,” “ventricular assist device,” and “registry.” We included randomized trials, cohort studies, and registry analyses enrolling ≥10 patients who had undergone Fontan completion and reporting outcomes of interest. Two reviewers independently screened titles/abstracts and full texts and resolved discrepancies by consensus. To avoid double-counting, when multiple reports analyzed overlapping cohorts, the most comprehensive or recent publication was used.

Eligibility Criteria

Studies were eligible if they (1) Included patients who had undergone Fontan completion for single-ventricle congenital heart disease; (2) reported outcomes after HTx or long-term survival after Fontan circulation; and (3) included ≥10 patients who had undergone Fontan completion.

Randomized trials, prospective and retrospective cohort studies, and registry analyses were eligible. Case reports, case series with fewer than 10 patients, editorials, and studies without extractable Fontan-specific outcomes were excluded. When multiple publications reported overlapping cohorts, the most comprehensive or most recent dataset was included to minimize double counting.

Study Selection

Two investigators independently screened titles and abstracts, followed by full-text review of potentially eligible studies. Disagreements were resolved by consensus. Data Extraction

Data on study design, era, region, sample size, Fontan type, fenestration status, ventricular morphology, pretransplant hemodynamics, and outcomes were extracted independently by 2 reviewers. Early mortality was defined as death within 30 days of Fontan completion or before hospital discharge. Posttransplant survival was extracted at 1, 5, and 10 years. In addition, major Fontan-related complications were recorded when available, including PLE, FALD, arrhythmia, and the use of mechanical circulatory support (MCS).

Risk of Bias Assessment

Risk of bias in nonrandomized studies was assessed using the Risk Of Bias In Non-randomised Studies of Interventions I tool. Studies were categorized as having low, moderate, or serious risk of bias according to the predefined Risk Of Bias In Non-randomised Studies of Interventions I domains.

Statistical Analysis

We analyzed proportions using logit transformation and fitted random-effects models with the DerSimonian-Laird estimator for between-study variance and the Hartung-Knapp adjustment for inference. Pooled logit estimates were back-transformed to proportions. Heterogeneity was quantified with the I2 statistic and τ2, and 95% prediction intervals were calculated. Small-study effects were explored via funnel plots and Egger regression when ≥10 studies were available. Sensitivity analyses included alternative τ2 estimators and exclusion of studies at serious risk of bias. Given the limited number of studies for certain outcomes, tests for funnel plot asymmetry were considered underpowered and interpreted cautiously.

Results

Study Selection and Characteristics

From 3728 records screened, 51 studies met inclusion criteria. Fifteen transplant cohorts provided data for quantitative synthesis (Figure 1); 13 reported peritransplant mortality, and 14 reported 1-year survival. These cohorts comprised 1186 patients who had undergone Fontan completion undergoing HTx. Sample sizes ranged from 14 to 402 patients (median ≈35). Cohorts were predominantly retrospective, most originated from North America and Europe, and one involved a multi-institutional network of 20 centers. The included studies spanned a broad time period from 1984 to 2025, although the majority of patients were derived from contemporary cohorts published after 2010. Details of patient characteristics, including Fontan type, fenestration, and ventricular morphology, were variably reported, limiting subgroup analyses. Peritransplant Mortality

Early single-center reports such as the series by Gamba and colleagues7,8 described peritransplant mortality around 14% and 1-year survival of approximately 86%. The largest multi-institutional study, a 20-center cohort reported by Schumacher and colleagues,9 included 341 transplanted patients and observed 5.9% waitlist mortality and 8.5% 1-year posttransplant mortality. These series exemplify the wide range of outcomes reported across eras and centers.

Peritransplant mortality varied from 0% to 31% across cohorts and was lower in more recent series. The pooled peritransplant mortality was 17.3% (95% CI, 12.7%-22.9%; I2 = 62.8%; 95% prediction interval ≈6%-33%), indicating moderate heterogeneity. One-Year Posttransplant Survival

Fourteen cohorts comprising 1178 recipients of Fontan transplant reported 1-year posttransplant survival. The pooled 1-year survival was 81.1% (95% CI, 74.9%-85.9%) with substantial heterogeneity (I2 = 73.5%) (Figure 3, Table 2). Fontan-Associated Complications

Fontan-associated complications were reported inconsistently across studies

PLE was reported in approximately 5 to 15% of patients who had undergone Fontan completion in the included cohorts. FALD was identified in approximately 16% of 2141 systematically evaluated patients who had undergone Fontan completion in registry analyses; the absence of fenestration and ventricular/valve dysfunction were strongly associated.3 Atrial and supraventricular arrhythmias were reported in up to 40% of patients in selected series and were linked to reduced functional status.

The use of MCS in patients with failing Fontan circulation was described in several small case series. Approximately 70% of patients were successfully bridged to transplantation or recovery, although neurologic and infectious complications were frequently reported. Because of small sample sizes and heterogeneous reporting, outcomes of MCS were summarized descriptively and were not pooled quantitatively.

Sensitivity Analyses

Sensitivity analyses using alternative τ2 estimators (Paule-Mandel and restricted maximum likelihood) produced pooled estimates within 1 to 2 percentage points of the main analysis. Excluding studies at serious risk of bias or restricting the analysis to cohorts published after 2010 did not materially change the results. Funnel plots and Egger regression suggested possible small-study effects for the 1-year survival outcome; however, the number of studies was insufficient for definitive assessment.

Discussion

This updated meta-analysis provides the largest contemporary synthesis of outcomes after HTx in patients with Fontan circulation. By integrating single-center series with registry data, we report pooled estimates of peritransplant mortality of approximately 17% and 1-year survival of 81%, with longer-term survival of 70% at 5 years and 60% at 10 years. Importantly, sensitivity analyses restricted to contemporary cohorts yielded comparable results, suggesting that these findings reflect current practice rather than historical outcomes.

Although previous systematic reviews have described transplantation in patients with Fontan circulation, the present study expands the available evidence by incorporating recent cohorts and providing updated long-term survival estimates.10 In addition, we explored sources of heterogeneity, including era effects, and report prediction intervals to better reflect variability across centers. Nevertheless, outcomes remain highly dependent on patient selection, institutional experience, and perioperative management, which likely explains the moderate-to-substantial heterogeneity observed. A meta-regression analysis using year of publication did not demonstrate a significant linear association with peritransplant mortality, suggesting that era alone does not fully explain observed heterogeneity.

The Fontan circulation represents a uniquely vulnerable physiological state characterized by chronically elevated venous pressures, limited preload reserve, and progressive end-organ dysfunction. These features contribute to the technical and physiological complexity of HTx, including previous sternotomies, abnormal venous anatomy, and hepatic impairment. Consistent with registry data, our findings confirm that peritransplant mortality remains greater than in noncongenital populations, although posttransplant survival appears acceptable in carefully selected patients.11,12,14,15,21 Additional single-center reports have similarly described the technical complexity and perioperative risk of transplantation in patients with Fontan circulation.16,17,22

These findings should also be interpreted in the context of contemporary registry data, which highlight the growing population of adult patients with Fontan circulation and the increasing burden of late complications and transplant referral.18,20 Together, these data reinforce that Fontan physiology represents durable palliation rather than definitive correction and is associated with progressive end-organ involvement over time. In addition, variability in outcomes may reflect differences in transplant strategy, including center experience, patient selection, and the involvement of congenital versus adult transplant teams, as suggested by previous institutional series.13,19,23 MCS is increasingly explored as a bridge strategy in patients with failing Fontan physiology; however, complication rates remain substantial, and optimal device strategies remain uncertain.24 Finally, when interpreted alongside outcomes in broader congenital heart disease populations, these findings suggest that HTx in patients with Fontan circulation remains greater risk but yields acceptable survival in selected cohorts.25

Study Limitations

Several limitations of this analysis should be acknowledged. First, all included studies were retrospective and subject to selection bias, center-specific practices, and unmeasured confounding. Second, substantial clinical heterogeneity existed across cohorts, including differences in era, patient selection, and perioperative management, which could only be partially explored despite subgroup and sensitivity analyses.

Third, key patient-level variables, such as ventricular morphology, Fontan type, hemodynamics, sensitization status, and presence of FALD, were inconsistently reported and could not be analyzed. Fourth, potential overlap between registry datasets and single-center reports cannot be completely excluded. Finally, long-term outcomes beyond 10 years were reported in relatively few studies and should be interpreted cautiously. No extractable or consistently reported data were available regarding pretransplant sensitization or combined heart-liver transplantation across the included cohorts.

Conclusions

In this updated meta-analysis, HTx in patients with Fontan circulation remains associated with substantial peritransplant mortality of approximately 17%, whereas posttransplant survival is acceptable in selected patients. These findings provide contemporary benchmark estimates but should be interpreted in the context of significant heterogeneity and limited granularity of available data. Further prospective studies and large registry analyses incorporating detailed clinical variables are required to better define risk factors, optimize patient selection, and refine timing and strategies for transplantation in this complex population.

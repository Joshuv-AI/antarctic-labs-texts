A Universal Bound on the Duration of a Kination Era
Cem Eröncel, Yann Gouttenoire, Ryosuke Sato, Géraldine Servant, Peera Simakachorn

Abstract
We show that primordial adiabatic curvature fluctuations generate an instability of the scalar field sourcing a kination era. We demonstrate that the generated higher Fourier modes constitute a radiation-like component dominating over the kination background after about 11 11 e-folds of cosmic expansion. Current constraints on the extra number of neutrino flavors Δ ​ N eff \Delta N_{\rm eff} thus imply the observational bound of approximately 10 e-folds, representing the most stringent bound to date on the stiffness of the equation of state of the pre-Big-Bang-Nucleosynthesis universe. IV
GROWTH OF SCALAR MODES DURING KINATION

In the absence of anisotropic stress, the metric perturbation in the conformal Newtonian gauge reads
[
76
]
,

ds^{2}=a^{2}\left\{(1+2\Phi)d\eta^{2}-\left[(1-2\Phi)\delta_{ij}+h_{ij}\right]dx^{i}dx^{j}\right\},

(17)

where
\Phi
and
h_{ij}
are the scalar and tensor fluctuations.
The gauge-invariant comoving curvature perturbation
\mathcal{R}

[
76

-\mathcal{R}\equiv\Phi+\frac{2}{3}\frac{(\mathcal{H}^{-1}\Phi^{\prime}+\Phi)}{1+\omega}\xrightarrow[k\eta\ll 1]{}\frac{5+3\omega}{3+3\omega}\Phi\xrightarrow[\omega=1]{}\frac{4}{3}\Phi.

(18)

In terms of the Fourier mode
\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}=\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\,e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\Phi({\mathbf{x}})
, the
linear perturbation of Einstein’s equations implies
[
76

\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\eta)+\frac{6(1+\omega)}{1+3\omega}\frac{1}{\eta}\Phi^{\prime}_{\mathbf{k}}(\eta)+\omega k^{2}\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}(\eta)=0,

The solution of Eq. (

\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}(k\eta)=\frac{2\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}(0)J_{1}(k\eta)}{k\eta}\xrightarrow[k\eta\gg 1]{}-\frac{3\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{k}}(0)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{\cos(k\eta+\varphi)}{(k\eta)^{3/2}},

(20)

where
\varphi
is a phase. We deduce the time-averaged scalar power spectrum during kination,

\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}(k,\eta)=\frac{9\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)}{4\pi(k\eta)^{3}}.

(21)

Einstein’s equation along 00 leads to Poisson’s equation,

-k^{2}\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}=4\pi Ga^{2}\overline{\rho}\,\delta_{\mathbf{k}},

(22)

where
\delta_{\mathbf{k}}\equiv(\rho_{\mathbf{k}}-\overline{\rho})/\overline{\rho}
is the contrast density in the Comoving gauge
[
13
]

Hence, scalar metric fluctuations
\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}(\eta)

Using Eq. (

\mathcal{P}_{\delta}(k,\eta)\simeq\frac{4}{9}\left(\frac{k}{\mathcal{H}}\right)^{4}\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}(k,\eta)=\frac{8}{\pi}\left(\frac{k}{\mathcal{H}}\right)\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k),

where we used Friedmann’s equation
3\mathcal{H}^{2}=8\pi G\overline{\rho}a^{2}

\mathcal{H}=2/(1+3\omega)\eta=1/2\eta

\phi=\overline{\phi}+\delta\phi

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{1}\simeq{\overline{\phi}^{{}^{\prime}}\delta\phi^{\prime}}/{a^{2}},

{\left<\delta{\rho_{2}}\right>}=\left<\left(\delta\rho_{1}\right)^{2}/{2\overline{\rho}_{\phi}}\right>=\frac{\overline{\rho}_{\phi}}{2}\int^{k_{\rm KD}}_{k_{\rm RD}}\hskip-5.69046ptd\log{k}\penalty\ \mathcal{P}_{\delta}(k,\eta).

When

in Eq. (

Assuming

\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!N_{\rm KD}\lesssim 9.9+0.5\ln{\left(\frac{2.099\times 10^{-9}}{\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{\star})}\right)}+0.5\ln{n_{s}(k_{\rm KD})}\\
+0.5(1-n_{s}(k_{\rm KD}))\ln{\left(\frac{k_{\rm KD}}{k_{\star}}\right)}.

CONCLUSION

We have studied how cosmological perturbations evolve during a kination era. At linear order, fluctuations grow linearly with the scale factor,
\delta\propto a
like matter fluctuations in agreement with previous findings — see e.g.
[
89
,
45
]
. Accounting for non-linear corrections, we demonstrated that fluctuations of the scalar driving kination have a non-vanishing volume average
\langle\delta\rangle\neq 0
cf. Eq. (
27
), unlike matter fluctuations due to mass conservation
[
83
,
15
]
, and redshift like radiation. Consequently, a kination era induced by a scalar field must end after approximately 11 e-folds when these fluctuations become non-perturbative and induce a radiation-like equation of state (EoS) — see the purple curves in Fig. (
2
).
3
3

3

Prior to our work, the instability of kination had been demonstrated in the context of oscillating scalar condensates
[
72
]
where the fluctuations arise from the fragmentation of
the scalar field rather than being sourced by curvature perturbations.

These radiation-like fluctuations also contribute to the effective number of neutrino species
\Delta N_{\rm eff}
. Planck
2\sigma
limit,
\Delta N_{\rm eff}\lesssim 0.34

[
5
]
, currently imposes the strongest constraint on kination, restricting its duration to
N_{\rm KD}\lesssim 10
e-folds — see Eq. (
33
) and the brown curves in Fig. (
2
).
Unlike the already-known bound from tensor modes in Eq. (
16
), the new limits from fluctuation domination and scalar modes in Eqs. (
31
) and (
33
) derived in this work are independent of the unknown value of the tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
. Future measurements, such as CMB-S4 with a projected sensitivity of
\Delta N_{\rm eff}\lesssim 0.05

[
1
,
40
]
, could further tighten the bounds on
N_{\rm KD}
by about one e-fold.

The conservative bounds presented in this work rely on the assumption that the scalar field acquires the primordial curvature perturbations from the inflaton, which requires that the scalar field either inherits its energy density from the inflaton or is the inflaton itself, as demonstrated in Fig.
1
.
4
4

4

In addition to those originating from adiabatic curvature fluctuations, kination fluctuations could also arise from isocurvature perturbations, which, however, depend on the scalar field dynamics during inflation and are left for future study.

We considered for simplicity scenarios where kination is preceded by either a radiation era or inflation
[
82
]
.
Scalar field theories leading to a radiation EoS preceding kination are discussed in
[
94
]
. On the other hand, kination in rotating axion models
[
28
,
60
,
59
,
97
,
61
,
61
,
62
,
25
,
39
]
, which is disconnected from inflation and preceded by a matter era,
would amplify scalar perturbations more strongly, leading to a more stringent
\Delta N_{\rm eff}
bound. Our bound should therefore be seen as conservative. Moreover, if fluctuations become massive due to the scalar field condensate acquiring a mass, the constraints derived in this work would be further tightened, or the scalar fluctuations could account for dark matter
[
43
,
17
]
.
We leave for future works the study of kination fluctuations starting from the stress-energy tensor of a perfect fluid instead of the Lagrangian of a scalar field. This would, allow to possibly extrapolate our results to any eras with stiff EoS
\omega>1/3
.

Appendix A
Assuming a specific inflation model

CMB measurements from Planck and BICEP/Keck
[
4
,
2
,
74
]

indicate that plateau-like concave inflation potentials are favored over the convex-type potentials. One such potential is the
R+R^{2}
model proposed by Starobinsky
[
99
]
:

S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{M_{\rm pl}^{2}}{2}R+\frac{\xi_{s}}{16}R^{2}\right],

(34)

where
R
is the scalar curvature and
\xi_{s}
is some coupling constant. This theory is conformally equivalent to Einstein gravity with a scalar field
\phi
with potential
[
104
,
42
]
:

V(\phi)=M_{\rm pl}^{4}\xi_{s}^{-1}\left[1-e^{-\frac{\phi}{M_{\rm pl}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}}\right]^{2}.

(35)

The slow-roll parameter
\epsilon
reads:

\displaystyle\epsilon\equiv\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2M_{\rm pl}^{2}H^{2}}\simeq\frac{M_{\rm pl}^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{V^{\prime}}{V}\right)^{2}=\frac{4}{3}\left[1-e^{-\frac{\phi}{M_{\rm pl}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}}\right]^{-2}.

(36)

Inflation ends when the slow-roll condition (
\epsilon<1
) breaks down,

\displaystyle\epsilon(\phi_{\rm end})=1\penalty\ \Rightarrow\penalty\ \phi_{\rm end}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}M_{\rm pl}\ln\left(1+\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\right).

(37)

The number of remaining inflation e-folds
N_{k}\equiv\ln\left(a_{\rm end}/a_{k}\right)
when the cosmological scale
k=a_{k}H_{k}
leaves the horizon can be written as:

N_{k}=-\ln\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm KD}}\right)+\ln\left(\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\rm end}}{\mathcal{H}_{\rm KD}}\right)-\ln\left(\frac{H_{\rm end}}{H_{k}}\right).

(38)

Assuming the kination era follows a radiation era as shown in Fig. (
1
), we have
\mathcal{H}_{\rm end}/\mathcal{H}_{\rm KD}=a_{\rm KD}/a_{\rm end}
.
The number of inflation e-folds before the inflation ends is,

N_{k}\equiv\ln\left(\frac{a_{\rm end}}{a_{\mathbf{k}}}\right)=\int_{\phi_{\rm inf}}^{\phi_{k}}\frac{H}{\dot{\phi}}d\phi=\int_{\phi_{\rm end}}^{\phi_{k}}\frac{d\phi}{M_{\rm pl}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\epsilon(\phi)}},

(39)

which upon inverting gives
\phi_{k}(N_{k})
.
We deduce the curvature and tensor power spectra
[
76
]
:

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{\epsilon_{k}}\left(\frac{H_{k}}{M_{\rm pl}}\right)^{2},\quad\mathcal{P}_{\rm t}(k)=\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{H_{k}}{M_{\rm pl}}\right)^{2},

(40)

where
H_{k}^{2}=V(\phi_{k})/(3M_{\rm pl}^{2})
and
\epsilon_{k}
is obtained from plugging
\phi_{k}(N_{k})
into Eq. (
36
). For every kination duration
N_{\rm KD}
and temperature
T_{\rm RD}
, we use Eq. (
1
) to fix the constant
\xi_{s}
, and calculate the contribution of tensor and scalar modes to
\Delta N_{\rm eff}
via Eqs. (
13
) and (
32
). The
2\sigma
Planck bound
\Delta N_{\rm eff}\lesssim 0.34

[
5
]
leads to the constraints shown in the blue dotted and brown dotted lines in Fig.

Appendix C
Kination fluctuations behave like radiation

In this appendix, we use the scalar field equations of motion to show that kination fluctuations redshift like radiation both in the perturbative and non-perturbative regime.
At first order in
\Phi
, the Lagrangian of the scalar field can be written:

L=\sqrt{-g}\left(\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}(\partial_{\mu}\phi)(\partial_{\nu}\phi)\right)\simeq a^{4}\left(\frac{1}{2a^{2}}(1-4\Phi)\phi^{{}^{\prime}2}-\frac{1}{2a^{2}}(\nabla\phi)^{2}\right),

(44)

where we have used the metric given in Eq. (
17
).
Using Euler-Lagrange equation for
\phi
, we derive the equation of motion:

\phi^{\prime\prime}+2{\cal H}\phi^{\prime}-\nabla^{2}\phi=4\Phi(\phi^{\prime\prime}+2{\cal H}\phi^{\prime})+4\Phi^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}.

(45)

The Fourier transform of the Newtonian potential can be determined from Eq. (
19
) as (for
\omega>0
)

\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}=\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}(\eta=0)\times\Gamma(\alpha+1)\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{\omega}k\eta}\right)^{\alpha}J_{\alpha}(\sqrt{\omega}k\eta),\qquad\textrm{where}\quad\alpha\equiv\frac{5+3\omega}{2(1+3\omega)}.

(46)

Hence,
\Phi
is a decreasing function and always remains perturbative. Thus,
4\Phi(\phi^{\prime\prime}+2{\cal H}\phi^{\prime})
in RHS is negligible compared to
\phi^{\prime\prime}+2{\cal H}\phi^{\prime}
in LHS and we can simplify the EOM as

\phi^{\prime\prime}+2{\cal H}\phi^{\prime}-\nabla^{2}\phi=4\Phi^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}.

(47)

Let us expand
\phi
as
\phi=\overline{\phi}+\delta\phi
. Then, the Fourier transform of the above EOM is

\overline{\phi}^{\prime\prime}+2\mathcal{H}\overline{\phi}^{\prime}={\cal S}_{0},\qquad\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime\prime}+2\mathcal{H}\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}+k^{2}\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}=\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{k}},

(48)

where

\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{0}}=4\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\Phi_{\mathbf{p}}^{\prime}\delta\phi_{-\mathbf{p}}^{\prime},\qquad\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{k}}=4\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}\overline{\phi}^{\prime}+4\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\Phi_{\mathbf{p}}^{\prime}\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{p}}^{\prime},

(49)

After transient behavior at horizon entry due to the source term,
\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}
quickly becomes the solution of the homogeneous equation for which
\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}
is set to zero:

\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}(\eta)=\eta^{-\alpha+1}\left[A_{\mathbf{k}}J_{\alpha-1}(k\eta)+B_{\mathbf{k}}Y_{\alpha-1}(k\eta)\right],

(50)

where
J(x)
and
Y(x)
are the Bessel functions of the first and second kinds. We deduce that in the deep sub-horizon limit, the scalar fields behave as

\lim_{k\eta\to+\infty}\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}(\eta)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{k\pi}}\eta^{-\alpha+1/2}\left[A_{\mathbf{k}}\cos\left(k\eta-\frac{\pi}{2}(2\alpha-1)\right)+B_{\mathbf{k}}\sin\left(k\eta-\frac{\pi}{2}(2\alpha-1)\right)\right].

(51)

Using that
a\propto\eta^{\alpha-1/2}
, we deduce

\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}(\eta)\penalty\ \propto\frac{1}{k^{1/2}}\frac{\cos\left(k\eta\right)}{a},\penalty\ {\rm for}\penalty\ k\eta\gg 1.

(52)

We deduce that
\delta\phi^{\prime}_{\mathbf{k}}\xrightarrow[k\eta\to+\infty]{}-k^{-1}\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}
.
Hence, the energy density of the fluctuation becomes

\delta\rho_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\delta\phi^{\prime}}{a}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\nabla\delta\phi}{a}\right)^{2}\penalty\ \simeq\penalty\ \left(\frac{\delta\phi^{\prime}}{a}\right)^{2}\penalty\ \propto\penalty\ k\frac{\cos^{2}(k\eta)}{a^{4}}.

(53)

We conclude that the time-averaged energy density of the kination fluctuation redshifts like radiation
\delta\rho_{2}\propto a^{-4}
independently of the background EoS
\omega
. Note that we have recovered the spectral enhancement
\delta\rho_{2}\propto k^{1}
in Eq. (
28
). It is important to note that Eq. (
48
) is valid at all orders in
\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}
. We conclude that the expression in Eqs. (
52
) and (
53
) are valid both in the linear
\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}/\overline{\phi}<1
and non-linear regime
\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}/\overline{\phi}>1
. Using
\mathcal{C}
osmo
\mathcal{L}
attice
[
49
,
50
]
, we have run lattice simulations to check that fluctuations interpreted as the gradient and kinetic terms in Eq. (
26
) redshift like radiation whether they are dominating or not
[
44
]
.

We now check a posteriori that it was a good approximation to neglect the source term
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{k}}
in Eq. (
48
) in the sub-horizon limit.
The Newtonian potential during kination and radiation era reads
\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}\propto a^{-3}\cos(k\eta)
and
a^{-2}\cos(k\eta/\sqrt{3})
respectively, cf. Eq. (
19
). Using
\overline{\phi}^{\prime}\propto a^{-2}
and
\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}\propto a^{-1}
, we obtain that the source term
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{k}}
in Eq. (
48
) redshifts as

\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{k}}\propto\begin{cases}a^{-3},\qquad\textrm{for }\omega=1,\\
a^{-2},\qquad\textrm{for }\omega=\frac{1}{3},\\
\end{cases}

(54)

which is faster than the left-hand side of Eq. (
48
) which redshifts as
k^{2}\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\propto a^{-1}
. We conclude that we can safely neglect the effect of
\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}
on the evolution of
\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}
for sub-horizon modes.

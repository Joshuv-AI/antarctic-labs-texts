Stability-Indicating HPLC Method for Simultaneous Determination of Sulfadimethoxine and Ormetoprim in an Oral Solution
Authors: Yanjun Gao, Haoshuang Xu, Taolong Jiao, Meng Li, Furong Li, Shijin Bu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A stability-indicating HPLC method was developed and validated for the simultaneous determination of sulfadimethoxine (SDM) and ormetoprim (OMP) in an oral solution. Chromatographic separation was achieved on a C18 column using 0.05% (v/v) phosphoric acid in water and methanol as the mobile phase under gradient elution, with diode array detection at 275 nm. Retention time modeling was applied to optimize the separation of the two analytes from the nine degradation products generated during forced degradation studies. Under the optimized chromatographic conditions, the retention times of the OMP and SDM were approximately 14.76 and 21.94 min, respectively. The method was validated in accordance with the ICH guidelines for specificity, linearity, accuracy, precision, and robustness. Average recoveries ranged from 98.62% to 99.87% for OMP and from 99.74% to 101.14% for SDM, and the % RSD values for both analytes were below 2%. All of the evaluated performance characteristics met the predefined acceptance criteria. Forced degradation studies under acidic, alkaline, oxidative, thermal, and photolytic conditions demonstrated that the analyte peaks were well-resolved from degradation products, supporting the stability-indicating capability of the method. GAPI and AGREEprep assessments indicated a moderate greenness profile, with an AGREEprep score of 0.40, while a BAGI score of 72.5 indicated good practical applicability. The method was further applied to a 6-month accelerated stability study of the oral solution. These results support the suitability of the proposed procedure for the simultaneous quantification of SDM and OMP in an oral solution and for stability evaluation under the conditions studied.

Introduction

Coccidiosis, caused by several species of the genus Eimeria, is one of the most economically important diseases affecting the poultry industry. Infection with Eimeria damages intestinal epithelial cells and disrupts intestinal homeostasis, resulting in diarrhea, reduced feed efficiency, impaired weight gain, and decreased egg production. These effects cause substantial economic losses worldwide. Anticoccidial drugs remain the primary approach to controlling this parasitic disease in poultry production.

Sulfonamides and diaminopyrimidines have been used in veterinary medicine for decades as synthetic antimicrobial agents with antibacterial and anticoccidial activity. Sulfadimethoxine (SDM) is commonly combined with ormetoprim (OMP) to achieve synergistic effects and broaden the antimicrobial spectrum (Figure ). In the United States, several commercial formulations containing SDM and OMP are available for veterinary use. However, the OMP-SDM type A-medicated article is the only approved combination product intended for the prevention of coccidiosis and bacterial infections in poultry in the United States.

Structures of sulfadimethoxine and ormetoprim.

When coccidiosis becomes clinically apparent, prompt therapeutic intervention is essential to limiting economic losses. During outbreaks, infected birds often show reduced feed intake and increased water consumption; therefore, treatment is commonly administered via drinking water. , Because no commercial OMP-SDM combination formulation for medication through drinking water is currently available, an oral solution was developed in our laboratory. −

Stability-indicating analytical methods are critical in formulation development and veterinary drug authorization since they enable quantification of active ingredients in the presence of excipients, impurities, and degradation products (DPs). As shown in Table , previously reported chromatographic methods for the simultaneous determination of OMP and SDM were primarily developed for medicated feeds and biological matrices and were not intended for evaluating formulation stability. Conversely, published stability-indicating methods involved SDM combined with other active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) rather than an OMP. To the best of our knowledge, no stability-indicating HPLC method has been reported for the simultaneous determination of SDM and OMP in an oral solution formulation.

This work aimed to develop a stability-indicating HPLC method for the simultaneous determination of OMP and SDM in an oral solution in the presence of their DPs. R-based functions were used to model retention times and optimize the separation of the OMP and SDM from nine DPs. In addition, forced degradation studies were conducted under various stress conditions. Although APIs were well-separated from their DPs, the established method was limited to API quantitation and was not intended for the quantification of DPs. The proposed method was validated in accordance with the ICH Q2­(R2) guideline, and its applicability was further evaluated in an accelerated stability study of the oral solution.

Results and Discussion

Method Development and Optimization

Optimization of Chromatographic Separation

The primary objective of chromatographic optimization was to achieve adequate separation between the target analytes and their DPs. The critical challenge was to balance the separation of the two APIs from nine DPs against the total run time and methanol consumption. A total of nine DPs were generated through forced degradation of the individual analytes under various stress conditions. The resulting samples were initially analyzed using a broad-gradient screen (0 min, 10% methanol; 30 min, 100% methanol) to obtain an overview of the elution behavior of all components. Retention times and UV absorption spectra of the DPs and target analytes were recorded to assign peaks. Subsequently, five additional gradient elution programs were designed to characterize retention behavior (Table S1) further. Retention times for each analyte and DP were collected and used to construct a retention model. Retention time modeling and separation optimization were performed using the gfitk­() and gopt­() functions implemented in R, as described by Zisi et al. The modeling results showed that the residuals were within ±4 min, except for DP7, which exhibited a poorer fit (Figure A).

Optimization of the chromatographic separation and detection conditions. (A) Evaluation of the retention time prediction model, showing the residual plot (top) and the predicted and observed retention times (bottom) for SDM, OMP, and their nine DPs; (B) gradient optimization profiles generated by the gopt­() function, showing the retention time difference of the least resolved pair of adjacent solutes (dt, purple circles) and the maximum retention time (tR, black circles) as a function of gradient time (tG); and (C) peak areas of OMP and SDM (both 3 μg/mL) at different wavelengths. The red arrows indicate the selected separation and detection conditions.

The gopt­() function was used to optimize the gradient profile. The minimum organic modifier content (f min) was set at 10% to avoid adversely affecting the conventional C18 stationary phase. Based on the preliminary experiments, the maximum organic modifier content (f max) was set at 45%, which was sufficient to elute SDM while limiting methanol consumption. Using these constraints and the fitted model (Table S2), an overlaid plot was generated by the gopt() function (Figure B). The tG was selected as 18 min because it provided a relatively large dt while maintaining the maximum predicted retention time within the target 25 min run time, excluding re-equilibration. This condition is indicated by the red arrow in Figure B. Accordingly, the final gradient profile was as follows: 0–2 min, 10% methanol; 2–20 min, a linear increase to 45% methanol; and 20–25 min, 45% methanol. The predicted separation performance was subsequently validated experimentally, demonstrating the resolution of the target analytes from their DPs (Figure A). The fitting performance of DP7 was poorer than that of other DPs, possibly because its retention behavior was not adequately described by the selected model. However, DP7 was successfully separated under the final optimized gradient conditions.

Overall, the method development strategy followed a structured workflow comprising forced degradation, retention time modeling, gradient optimization, and experimental verification. This strategy represents an intermediate approach between conventional trial-and-error development and a comprehensive analytical quality by the design (AQbD) framework. , Compared with a full AQbD study, it requires fewer experiments but provides a less comprehensive understanding of parameter interactions and method operability. Nevertheless, this workflow may provide a practical reference for developing stability-indicating HPLC methods for other formulations containing multiple APIs and DPs.

Wavelength Selection

The UV detection wavelength was optimized by evaluating the peak area responses of OMP and SDM over the range of 220–300 at 5 nm intervals. OMP exhibited maximum absorption at 220 nm with SDM exhibiting maximum absorption at 270 nm. At wavelengths below 245 nm, interfering peaks were observed and increased in intensity as the wavelength decreased, which could affect the method specificity. Therefore, the detection wavelength was selected between 260 and 280 nm (Figure C). This range is also consistent with previously reported methods for the simultaneous determination of OMP and SDM in other matrixes. ,, Finally, 275 nm (red arrows in Figure C) was selected as the optimal detection wavelength because it provided adequate sensitivity for both analytes.

Method Validation

System Suitability

The results of the system suitability test are presented in Table . The retention times of the target analytes were consistent, with variations within ±0.1 min. The mean theoretical plate numbers, calculated from peak widths at half-height, exceeded 8000 for both analytes, indicating adequate column efficiency. The tailing factors ranged from 0.8 to 1.8, demonstrating acceptable peak symmetry. The relative standard deviation (%RSD) of peak areas was less than 2% for six replicate injections, confirming good system repeatability. A minor peak eluted immediately before OMP, with a mean resolution of 2.62 between the two peaks. The mean resolution between OMP and SDM was 34.97. Both values exceeded the predefined acceptance criterion of 1.5, demonstrating adequate separation. All evaluated parameters complied with commonly accepted system suitability criteria.

The tailing factor of OMP was approximately 1.98 at an injection concentration of 30 μg/mL, whereas SDM (150 μg/mL) still exhibited a symmetrical peak. This phenomenon was initially thought to be related to OMP’s alkalinity. Ghanem et al. also encountered this when developing a method for simultaneously determining trimethoprim and SDM sodium. The addition of triethylamine (0.1%, v/v) to the mobile phase, followed by pH adjustment to 2.7 (±0.1), reduced the tailing factor of OMP to approximately 1.4. This improvement may be due to the ability of triethylamine to block adverse interactions between the analyte and the silica surface. However, this modification increased baseline fluctuations, which adversely affected quantitative performance. Dilution of OMP to 3 μg/mL markedly improved peak symmetry, suggesting that the observed peak tailing at higher concentrations was mainly due to column overloading.

Specificity

The specificity of the method was demonstrated by comparing chromatograms of the blank, individual standard solutions of OMP and SDM, mixed standard solution, test solution, and the accelerated stability sample solution (Figure ). In both individual and mixed standard solutions (Figure B–D), OMP and SDM were well separated, with retention times of approximately 14.76 and 21.94 min, respectively. In the presence of excipients and DPs, no significant shift in retention times was observed for either analyte (variation within ±0.01 min) (Figure E,F) with the peak purity analysis indicating no coeluting substance (Table S13) at their retention time. No interfering peaks were observed at the retention times of OMP and SDM in the blank sample.

LOD, LOQ, and Linearity

The LOD values for SDM and OMP were 0.02 and 0.05 μg/mL, respectively, while the corresponding LOQ values were 0.10 and 0.15 μg/mL. The relationship between analyte response and concentration was evaluated by linear regression over the tested concentration range. The regression equations, corresponding coefficients of determination (R 2), and 95% confidence intervals of regression slopes and intercepts are summarized in Table . The regression curves are presented in Figures S1–S2. According to ICH Q2­(R2), the reportable range was established as 80–120% of the declared content, corresponding to 24–36 mg/mL for OMP and 120–180 mg/mL for SDM in the oral solution. After dilution, the corresponding working ranges were 2.4–3.6 μg/mL for OMP and 12–18 μg/mL for SDM. The use of 10% (v/v) methanol in water as the diluent appeared to affect the peak area response of SDM, resulting in unsatisfactory linearity of the calibration curve. Therefore, although some solvent effects were anticipated, 50% (v/v) methanol in water was selected as the diluent. Under these conditions, both SDM and OMP exhibited good linearity over the tested concentration range.

Robustness

The system suitability parameters of OMP and SDM obtained under each robustness condition are presented in Tables S3–S10. Small deliberate variations in flow rate, organic phase proportion, detection wavelength, and column temperature primarily affected the peak areas and retention times of the analytes. The most pronounced changes were observed when the flow rate was varied by ±0.05 mL/min. Decreasing the flow rate increased the retention times and peak areas of both analytes, whereas increasing the flow rate produced corresponding decreases. Nevertheless, all evaluated parameters remained within the predefined system suitability criteria, indicating that the analytical method was robust within the investigated ranges.

Forced Degradation Study

Forced degradation studies of the OMP-SDM mixture were conducted under acidic, alkaline, oxidative, wet thermal, and photolytic conditions. The stress conditions were established on the basis of preliminary experiments, in which OMP and SDM were individually exposed to various stress conditions and analyzed at different time points. The stress conditions for the combined formulation were selected as 2 mol/L HCl for 2 h under acidic conditions and 2 mol/L NaOH for 7 days under alkaline conditions, which provided sufficient degradation and avoided secondary degradation. The percentages of the APIs remaining, the DPs formed, and the estimated mass balances under the investigated stress conditions are summarized in Table S11.

The degradation percentage of SDM and OMP was 12.92% and 2.09% under base hydrolysis and 15.16% and 2.46% under acid hydrolysis, respectively. Despite differences in HCl concentration and exposure temperature, the extent of SDM degradation under acidic conditions was similar to that reported by Louati et al. and Ghanem et al. , The major degradation product detected under both acidic and alkaline hydrolysis conditions, with a retention time of approximately 8 min, was tentatively assigned as a hydroxyl derivative of SDM through spectrum comparison. This product may be formed via ε-cleavage of the parent compound and has also been found under oxidative and wet thermal degradation conditions. Mass spectrometry or nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy characterization was not performed during this study. This limitation restricts the applicability of the method for the further quantification of DPs.

For oxidative, wet thermal, and photolytic stress conditions, only a few DPs were observed in the preliminary study and no marked decrease in the contents of OMP or SDM was detected. Therefore, extended stress durations of 7 days for oxidative and wet thermal conditions and 14 days for photolytic conditions were applied to provide an adequate stress for specificity assessment. The remaining percentages under three stress conditions ranged from 98.64% to 100.53% for OMP and from 97.95% to 99.82% for SDM. The oxidative degradation behavior of SDM differed from that reported by Ghanem et al., who observed a 21.98% decrease in SDM content following exposure to 10% H2O2 at room temperature. The reason for this difference could not be determined from available data.

The specificity of the method was demonstrated by the adequate separation of OMP and SDM from their DPs under all tested stress conditions, and peak purity analysis further supported the absence of detectable coeluting impurities (Figures S3–S7 and Tables S12 and S13). Some degradation peaks observed during broad-gradient screening were not detected in the final forced degradation samples, possibly related to different dilution factors and lower relative peak responses. In addition, a degradation peak with a retention time of 20.428 min was observed; however, its source requires further clarification. Overall, these results support the stability-indicating capability of the developed method.

Application to Accelerated Study

The proposed method was applied to evaluate changes in the concentrations of OMPs and SDMs during the 6-month accelerated stability study. The back-calculated contents of OMP and SDM ranged from 28.76 to 30.40 mg/mL and from 147.38 to 150.69 mg/mL, respectively. Although the peak area of the major degradation product, DP2, increased over time, both active ingredients remained within 95–105% of their initial values at all sampling time points (Figure S9).

Green Chemistry and Applicability Evaluation

The AGREEprep, GAPI, and BAGI pictograms are shown in Figure S8. GAPI and AGREEprep assessments indicated a moderate greenness profile with an AGREEprep score of 0.40. It was mainly attributed to the use of hazardous reagents, including methanol, DMSO, and phosphoric acid and to the relatively large waste volume generated during sample preparation. These results suggest that future improvements should focus on replacing hazardous solvents and reducing solvent consumption.

The BAGI assessment gave an overall score of 72.5, indicating the good practical applicability of the method. The main limitation was low sample throughput, primarily due to the relatively long run time per sample. The current method sacrifices throughput to ensure an adequate separation of the APIs and DPs from one another. Shortening the total run time may compromise the separation and result in the loss of information regarding the DPs. Future adaptation to a shorter column, transfer to a UHPLC system, or a faster gradient profile may reduce both analysis time and solvent consumption. Further characterization of the DPs may allow the method to be extended from an API assay to a simultaneous assay and impurity quantitation, thereby improving analytical efficiency.

Conclusions

This study established a simple and efficient stability-indicating HPLC method for the simultaneous determination of the OMP and SDM in an oral solution. Retention time modeling using R-assisted optimization enabled the satisfactory separation of the target analytes from their DPs. The developed procedure was validated in accordance with the ICH Q2­(R2) guideline, and all of the evaluated performance characteristics met the predefined acceptance criteria. Forced degradation studies under acidic, alkaline, wet thermal, oxidative, and photolytic conditions supported the stability-indicating capability of the procedure by demonstrating the separation of the analyte peaks from DPs. The procedure was successfully applied to the accelerated stability study of the OMP-SDM oral solution and is suitable for evaluating the stability under the conditions studied.

Materials and Methods

Chemicals and Reagents

SDM (purity: 99.6%, batch no. S03–2304070) and OMP (purity: 99.9%, batch no. S05–2302003) were kindly provided by Jiangsu Tianhe Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. (Jiangsu, China). The SDM and OMP oral solution (containing 15% SDM and 3% OMP, batch no. 20250429) was manufactured by Hebei Kexing Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. (Hebei, China).

Methanol and phosphoric acid were of HPLC grade. Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), hydrochloric acid (HCl), sodium hydroxide (NaOH), and hydrogen peroxide (H2O2, 30%) were of analytical grade. All solvents were purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Ultrapure water was prepared by using a Milli-Q water purification system (Millipore, MA, USA).

Solution Preparation

Stock solutions (1 mg/mL) of OMP and SDM were prepared individually by accurately weighing approximately 100 mg of each reference standard into separate 100 mL volumetric flasks. The substances were dissolved and diluted to volume with DMSO. These two solutions were freshly prepared before the analysis.

A mixed standard solution was prepared by accurately weighing approximately 30 mg of OMP and 150 mg of SDM reference standards into a 100 mL volumetric flask. The substances were dissolved and diluted to volume with DMSO. The stock solution was stored at room temperature (25 ± 5 °C) and used within one month. The stability under this condition was confirmed, as summarized in Table S14.

The diluent was prepared by mixing methanol and water in a 50:50 (v/v) ratio.

Working standard solutions were prepared daily by appropriately mixing and diluting the stock solutions with the diluent. The final concentrations ranged from 12 to 18 μg/mL for SDM and 2.4–3.6 μg/mL for OMP. Working standards were freshly prepared before analysis.

Liquid Chromatography

Chromatographic analysis of SDM and OMP was performed using an Agilent 1260 HPLC system equipped with a diode array detector (DAD). Data acquisition and processing were carried out using ChemStation software (Agilent Technologies, CA, USA). Separation was achieved on an Eclipse XDB C18 column (4.6 mm × 250 mm, 5 μm; Agilent Technologies, CA, USA) maintained at 35 °C. The flow rate was set at 1.0 mL/min, the injection volume was 20 μL, and the detection wavelength was 275 nm. The mobile phase consisted of 0.05% (v/v) phosphoric acid in water (A) and methanol (B). For the separation of target analytes, the gradient program was as follows: 0–2 min, 10% B; 2–20 min, a linear increase to 45% B; and 20–25 min, 45% B. A re-equilibration time of 5 min was maintained between injections.

Method Validation

System Suitability and Specificity

System suitability was assessed by six replicate injections of a mixed working standard solution containing SDM (15 μg/mL) and OMP (3 μg/mL). Chromatographic parameters, including the %RSD of peak areas and retention times, tailing factor, theoretical plate number, and resolution, were calculated. The %RSD of peak areas and retention times should be less than 2% to ensure injection repeatability and retention time consistency. The tailing factor of both analytes should be between 0.8 and 1.8 to minimize peak asymmetry that could adversely affect peak integration. The minimum theoretical plate number of 8000 was selected to monitor column efficiency, and the resolution should be greater than 1.5 to ensure adequate peak separation.

Specificity was evaluated by comparing chromatograms of the blank, individual standard solutions, the mixed standard solution, the test solution (formulation), and the accelerated stability sample solution. The blank solution was prepared in the same manner as the test solution except that SDM and OMP were omitted. Peak purity was assessed to confirm the absence of coeluting peaks from excipients or degradation products at the retention times of the target analytes.

Sensitivity and Working Range

The LOD and LOQ of each analyte were determined at signal-to-noise ratios of at least 3 and 10, respectively. The working range of the method was established by evaluating linearity across five concentration levels for each analyte. Calibration curves were fitted over the range of 80–120% of the target analytical concentrations (15 μg/mL for SDM and 3 μg/mL for OMP). Peak area was plotted against the corresponding concentration, and linear regression was performed by the least-squares method.

Accuracy and Precision

Accuracy was evaluated using placebo samples spiked with SDM and OMP at three concentration levels corresponding to 80%, 100%, and 120% of the nominal concentrations in the oral solution. At each level, three spiked placebo samples were prepared independently. The nominal concentrations of SDM and OMP in the oral solution were 150 and 30 mg/mL, respectively. Repeatability was evaluated using six independently prepared spiked placebo samples at the 100% level. Intermediate precision was assessed by repeating the procedure on a different day by a different analyst. A 1.0 mL aliquot of each spiked sample was diluted 100-fold with DMSO and subsequently diluted another 100-fold with the diluent. The resulting solutions were filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane filter before HPLC analysis (the filter compatibility study is presented in Table S15). Accuracy was expressed as percentage recovery, whereas repeatability and intermediate precision were expressed as %RSD.

Robustness

Robustness was evaluated by introducing small variations in chromatographic conditions, including flow rate (±0.05 mL/min), organic phase proportion (±2%), detection wavelength (±2 nm), and column temperature (±2 °C). Under each condition, a mixed working standard solution containing SDM (15 μg/mL) and OMP (3 μg/mL) was injected in five replicates. System suitability parameters were calculated.

Forced Degradation

Forced degradation studies were performed using a mixed standard solution of OMP (30 μg/mL) and SDM (150 μg/mL).

Acid and Base Hydrolysis

An aliquot (1.0 mL) of the mixed solution was transferred into a 10 mL volumetric flask, followed by the addition of 1.0 mL of 2 mol/L HCl (for hydrolysis) or 2 mol/L NaOH (for base hydrolysis). The flasks were maintained at 70 °C in an oven for 2 h under acidic conditions or 7 days under alkaline conditions. At the designated time points, the solutions were allowed to cool to room temperature and then neutralized with an equivalent amount of the corresponding base or acid. The solutions were diluted to volume with the diluent. Subsequently, an aliquot of each solution was further diluted 10-fold with the diluent, filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane filter, and analyzed by HPLC.

Oxidization and Wet Thermal Hydrolysis

An aliquot (1.0 mL) of the mixed solution was transferred into a 10 mL volumetric flask, followed by the addition of 1.0 mL of 6% (v/v) H2O2 (for oxidation) or 1.0 mL of deionized water (for wet thermal hydrolysis). The flasks were maintained at 70 °C in a thermostatic oven for 7 days under both conditions. At the designated time points, the solutions were allowed to cool to room temperature, and 1.0 mL of deionized water was added to dilute the reaction mixture. The solutions were then diluted to volume with the diluent. Subsequently, an aliquot of each solution was further diluted 10-fold with the diluent, filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane filter, and subjected to HPLC analysis.

Photolytic Degradation

Bulk SDM and OMP samples were exposed to direct sunlight for 14 days. Then, approximately 150 mg of SDM and 30 mg of OMP were accurately weighed, transferred into a 100 mL volumetric flask, and dissolved in DMSO. An aliquot of the resulting solution was further diluted 100-fold with the diluent, filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane filter, and analyzed by HPLC.

Application to Accelerated Study

Pilot-scale oral solutions containing OMP and SDM were subjected to accelerated stability testing at 40 °C for 6 months. Samples were collected at 30-day intervals. The established stability-indicating HPLC method was applied to determine the concentrations of OMP and SDM in the formulations. A 1.0 mL aliquot of solution was diluted 100-fold with DMSO and then further diluted 100-fold with the diluent. The resulting solutions were filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane filter before HPLC analysis.

Method Sustainability and Applicability Evaluation

The greenness and environmental compatibility of the established method were evaluated using the AGREEprep and GAPI tools. − In addition, the practical applicability of the method was assessed using Blue Applicability Grade Index (BAGI).

Path Types in Algebraic Type Theory
Steve Awodey, Joseph Hua

Abstract
A new approach to the semantics of identity types in intensional Martin-Löf type theory is proposed, assuming only a category with finite limits and an interval. The specification of extensional identity types in the original presentation of natural models paralleled that of the other type formers Σ \Sigma and Π \Pi , but the treatment of the intensional case there was less uniform. It was later reformulated to an account based on polynomials; here a further improvement in the style of the other type formers is achieved by employing an interval, in order to give a single pullback specification of a model with path types . The interval is also used to specify a (Hurewicz) fibration structure on the universe of the model. It is shown that the combination of these two conditions suffices to model the intensional identity rules, assuming only finite limits. The addition of an interval also relates the current treatment to that of cubical type theory.

Introduction

We propose a new approach to the semantics of identity types in intensional Martin-Löf type theory, assuming only a category with finite limits and a bipointed, exponentiable object (an
interval

[

]

[

,
Awo26
]
, path categories
[

,

]
, categories with families
[

[

]
. This new approach is adopted in the HoTTLean project
[

]
, simplifying the formalization in Lean.

The specification of
extensional
identity types in the natural models of
[
Awo16
]
parallels that of the other type formers
\Sigma
and
\Pi
and is entirely satisfactory, but the treatment of the
intensional
case there was less uniform. Following a suggestion of Richard Garner, it was updated in
[
Awo25
]
to a cleaner account based on polynomials; here a further improvement in the style of the other type formers is achieved by employing an interval, in order to give a single pullback specification of a model with

2
that the combination of these two conditions already suffices to model the usual intensional identity rules, assuming only finite limits and the exponentiability of the interval.

The addition of an interval also relates the current treatment to that of cubical type theory
[
CCHM16
]
, as is explained in Section
4

is in fact a cubical Kan fibration.
We provide examples of path types in Section

Natural models were originally defined in categories of presheaves and were then abstracted to the setting of a general locally cartesian closed category in

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{T}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{C}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{j}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{u}_{\mathbf{I}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dot{\mathsf{T}}_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{u}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{T}_{!}}

(15)

Note that we are using the fact that the terminal object
1
is also tiny,
where
(-)_{1}=\mathsf{id}
is the identity functor.
The natural transformation between left adjoints
\rho=(-)^{!}:(-)^{1}\to(-)^{\mathbf{I}}
gives rise to a conjugate transformation between their right adjoints
(-)_{!}:(-)_{\mathbf{I}}\to(-)_{1}
.

The problem (
15
) can then be reformulated without reference to
A,\tilde{C},c
as stating that the inner square is a weak pullback, or, again equivalently, that there exists a section
\tilde{\mathsf{J}}
of the comparison map
{\langle\mathsf{u}_{\mathbf{I}},\dot{\mathsf{T}}_{!}\rangle}
to the actual pullback,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dot{\mathsf{T}}_{\mathbf{I}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{T}_{\mathbf{I}}\times_{\mathsf{T}}\dot{\mathsf{T}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\mathsf{J}}}

(16)

This condition is obviously independent of any particular maps
A:X\to\mathsf{T}
, etc., into the object
{\mathsf{t}}:\dot{\mathsf{T}}\to\mathsf{T}
, and therefore evidently satisfies the strict rule of coherence under substitution.
Comparing the condition (
16
) with its counterpart in
[
Awo25
, Definition 11]
,
we have achieved a simplification in replacing the polynomial functors
P_{\mathsf{u}},P_{q}:\hat{\mathcal{C}}\to\hat{\mathcal{C}}
by the root functor
(-)_{\mathbf{I}}:\hat{\mathcal{C}}\to\hat{\mathcal{C}}
.

.

.

4
Cubical Kan fibrations

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a finite limit category with an interval
1\rightrightarrows\mathbf{I}
, and
{\mathsf{t}}:\dot{\mathsf{T}}\to\mathsf{T}
a natural model in
\mathcal{E}
satisfying axioms (A1) and (A2) from Section
2
. For the following, it will be convenient to have (well behaved) finite colimits in
\mathcal{E}
, so we might as well assume that
\mathcal{E}=\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
is presheaves on a category
\mathcal{C}
, which we regard as the “category of contexts”. The powers
\mathbf{I}^{n}
of the interval object
\mathbf{I}
give rise to a cubical structure in
\mathcal{E}
, with respect to which one can consider Kan-style “box-filling” conditions generalizing the path-lifting condition from Definition
2.4
from an endpoint inclusion
1\to\mathbf{I}
to an “open box inclusion”
\sqcup\rightarrowtail\mathbf{I}^{n}
: a subobject defined as the join of all but one
(n-1)
-dimensional faces
\mathbf{I}^{n-1}\rightarrowtail\mathbf{I}^{n}
.

Definition 4.1
.

A map
f:Y\to X
is a
(cubical) Kan fibration
if it has the right lifting property with respect to every open
n
-box inclusion
\sqcup\to\mathbf{I}^{n}
, for every
n>0
. In more detail, given an open
n
-box
b:\sqcup\to\mathbf{I}^{n}
, and maps
x
and
y
as indicated below, there exists a diagonal filler
\tilde{x}
making the following diagram commute.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sqcup}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{I}^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces b}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{x}}

(18)

Remark 4.2
.

As with path-lifting, the box-filling condition can be strengthened by adding an object
Z
of parameters:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z\times\sqcup}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z\times\mathbf{I}^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z\times b}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{x}}

(19)

One can then also prove the analogue of the following proposition for that version.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
{\mathsf{t}}:\dot{\mathsf{T}}\to\mathsf{T}
be a natural model in
\mathcal{E}
satisfying axioms (A1) and (A2). The maps
A\to X
classified by
{\mathsf{t}}:\dot{\mathsf{T}}\to\mathsf{T}
are cubical Kan fibrations in the sense of Definition
4.1
; that is, they have
n
-box filling for all
n>0
.

The proof of the proposition requires the following, a version of which is in
[
Awo18
, Proposition 14]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any object
A
in
\mathcal{E}
, and all
n>0
, the following are equivalent:

1.

The pathobject
A^{\mathbf{I}}\to A\times A
has
n
-box filling.

2.

The object
A
has
(n+1)
-box filling.

Moreover, the same holds for any
A\to X
in any slice category
\mathcal{E}/_{\!X}
.

Proof.

We use the so-called “Leibniz adjunction”
\otimes\dashv\ \Rightarrow
on the arrow category
\mathcal{E}^{\downarrow}
between the pushout-product
a\otimes b
and the pullback-hom
c\Rightarrow d
, and recall that for all arrows
a,b,c
in
\mathcal{E}
,

a\otimes b\pitchfork c\quad\text{iff}\quad a\pitchfork b\Rightarrow c\,,

where
x\pitchfork y
is the relation of
weak orthogonality
, meaning that every commutative square from
x
to
y
has a diagonal filler. In these terms, we wish to show that for any
n
and any open
n
-, resp. (
n+1
)-box inclusions,

\displaystyle(\sqcup^{n}\to\mathbf{I}^{n})\ \pitchfork\ (A^{\mathbf{I}}\to A\times A)

iff

\displaystyle(\sqcup^{n+1}\to\mathbf{I}^{n+1})\ \pitchfork\ (A\to 1)\,.

Now observe that the (upper, say) open boxes may be described as pushout-products of the form
\sqcup^{n+1}=\partial\mathbf{I}^{n}\otimes d_{0}
, while the boundary
\partial\mathbf{I}^{n}\to\mathbf{I}^{n}
satisfies
\partial\mathbf{I}^{n}=\partial\mathbf{I}\otimes\partial\mathbf{I}^{n-1}
(and so, generally,
\partial\mathbf{I}^{m}\otimes\partial\mathbf{I}^{n}=\partial\mathbf{I}^{m+n}
). Finally, we have
(A^{\mathbf{I}}\to A\times A)=\big(\partial\mathbf{I}\Rightarrow(A\to 1)\big)
. Therefore

\displaystyle(\sqcup^{n}\to\mathbf{I}^{n})\ \pitchfork\ (A^{\mathbf{I}}\to A\times A)

iff

\displaystyle(\partial\mathbf{I}^{n-1}\otimes d_{0})\ \pitchfork\ (\partial\mathbf{I}\Rightarrow(A\to 1))

iff

\displaystyle(\partial\mathbf{I}^{n-1}\otimes d_{0})\otimes\partial\mathbf{I}\ \pitchfork\ (A\to 1)

iff

\displaystyle(\partial\mathbf{I}\otimes\partial\mathbf{I}^{n-1}\otimes d_{0})\ \pitchfork\ (A\to 1)

iff

\displaystyle(\partial\mathbf{I}^{n}\otimes d_{0})\ \pitchfork\ (A\to 1)

iff

\displaystyle(\sqcup^{n+1}\to\mathbf{I}^{n+1})\ \pitchfork\ (A\to 1)\,.

∎

Proof.

(of Proposition
4.3
)
It clearly suffices to prove that
{\mathsf{t}}:\dot{\mathsf{T}}\to\mathsf{T}
has
n
-box filling for all
n>0
, since any pullback of it will then have the same. It has
1
-box filling by axiom (A2). Suppose it has
n
-box filling; then by axiom (A1), so does the pathobject
\dot{\mathsf{T}}^{\mathbf{I}}\to\dot{\mathsf{T}}\times_{\mathsf{T}}\dot{\mathsf{T}}
, since it is a pullback of
{\mathsf{t}}:\dot{\mathsf{T}}\to\mathsf{T}
. By Lemma
4.4
,
{\mathsf{t}}:\dot{\mathsf{T}}\to\mathsf{T}
then has
(n+1)
-box filling. Thus, by induction,
{\mathsf{t}}:\dot{\mathsf{T}}\to\mathsf{T}
has
n
-box filling for all
n>0
.
∎

Formalization in Lean

A version of Proposition
2.7
is formalized as part of the HoTTLean project,
which uses path types as a smart constructor for semantic identity types.
A key difference between the formalization and the exposition in this paper is that for the sake of simplicity,
only item (1) in Definition
2.4
is used,
namely the definition of a Hurewicz fibration “on the left” of the adjunction
\mathbf{I}\times(-)\vdash(-)^{\mathbf{I}}
.
The main benefit of working “on the left”
is that the definitions hold in a more general setting,
without needing to handle exponentiability conditions.
On the other hand, we will not be able to work
universally
:
the Hurewitz lifting structure will be required to be uniform
(stable under substitution),
whereas working with a single lifting operation on the natural model

\ell:\mathsf{T}^{\mathbf{I}}\times_{\!X}\dot{\mathsf{T}}\to\dot{\mathsf{T}}^{\mathbf{I}}

instead guarantees a uniform lifting structure.
This prevents us from defining Path types in the style of
natural models:
instead of describing (A1) as a pullback,
the formalization unpacks the universal property of the pullback in elementary terms
similar to categories with families
[
CD14
]
.

For the benefits of abstraction,
the formalization replaces the interval object
\mathbf{I}
with an endofunctor

replacing
\mathbf{I}\times(-)
,
equipped with natural transformations

J-contractive operator valued functions, vector valued de Branges spaces and functional models
Bharti Garg, Santanu Sarkar

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHS) which are constructed from J J -contractive operator valued analytic functions on the upper half plane. First, we discuss the Potapov-Ginzburg transform of a specific class of Fredholm operators, investigate some of its properties, and outline sufficient conditions for a J J -contractive operator to be J J -bicontractive. Subsequently, we construct certain classes of vector valued de Branges spaces and aim to explore the significance of these classes. Using these spaces, we present a functional model for simple Volterra operator nodes. Additionally, we analyze their connections with another classes of de Branges spaces which are based on a pair of Fredholm operator valued analytic functions. Finally, we discuss a functional model for simple, closed, densely defined, symmetric operators with infinite deficiency indices within this framework.

Introduction

The origin of
J
-theory, where
J
is a unitary and self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space
\mathfrak{X}
, can be traced back to Pontryagin’s seminal article
[
41
]
, which was influenced by Sobolev’s research work
[
45
]
involving mechanical problems. This foundational work was subsequently expanded by authors such as Krein, Iokhvidov, Langer, and Bognar. Potapov’s paper
[
43
]
on the finite-dimensional analytic aspects of the theory eventually led Ginzburg
[
26
]
to develop its infinite-dimensional counterpart.
The study of
J
-theory is crucial in addressing a variety of problems across several fields, including mathematical systems and networks, control theory, stochastic processes, operator theory, and classical analysis. Notably, it is a key component in the analysis of both direct and inverse problems for canonical systems of integral and differential equations.

A bounded linear operator
U
on
\mathfrak{X}
is said to be
J
-contractive if
U^{*}JU\preceq J
and
J
-bicontractive if both

and

are
J
-contractive. If

is a finite-dimensional space, then

is

-contractive if and only if its adjoint

is
J
-contractive
[
43
]
. However, this equivalence does not hold in infinite dimensions. A counterexample can be constructed using the shift operator on the Hilbert space of square-summable sequences. Some sufficient conditions for a
J
-contractive bounded linear operator to be
J
-bicontractive have been presented in
[
30
]
.

The theory of
J
-contractive and
J
-inner matrix valued functions has been extensively studied by Potapov
[
43
]
, obtaining fundamental results regarding multiplicative representation of
J
-contractive matrix valued analytic functions. These results play a crucial role in the spectral theory of non self-adjoint operators in Hilbert spaces. A comprehensive study of vector valued reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHS), derived from a class of
J
-contractive and
J
-inner matrix valued functions, is presented in the monograph by Arov and Dym
[
7
]
. Recent works by Dym (see
[
20
]
,
[
9
]
), investigate and establish connections between two classes of vector valued RKHS, known as de Branges spaces, that exist in the literature:
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
and
\mathcal{H}(U)
spaces. These spaces were first introduced by de Branges and applied to various analytical problems, including inverse problems for canonical differential systems. Motivated from the scalar valued Paley-Wiener spaces, de Branges explored the spaces of scalar valued entire functions which originates with
[
12
]
and a comprehensive study of these spaces can be found in his book
[
16
]
. These spaces were further generalized in the set up of matrix and operator valued entire and meromorphic functions in his works (see
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
). Recently, in
[
36
]
, the

These spaces serve as a generalization of vector valued Paley-Wiener spaces and also serve as functional models for a Krein’s class of entire operators with infinite deficiency indices. Connections between quasi-Lagrange type interpolation and
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
spaces have been found recently in
[

introduced the theory of operator nodes and their characteristic functions, which has been widely applied in scattering theory, electrical circuits, and the theory of conservative and passive systems. Alpay and Dym in

established the connections between Krein’s theory of symmetric operators on Hilbert spaces with finite and equal deficiency indices, and de Branges spaces

2.
Preliminaries to RKHS

The theory of RKHS was introduced at the beginning of the 20th century. The groundwork for RKHS was laid by Aronszajn in 1950. In this section, we briefly recall the definition of vector valued RKHS and of positive kernel functions. For a detailed study on RKHS, see
[
4
]
and
[
42
]
.

A Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathfrak{X}
valued functions defined on a nonempty set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is said to be a RKHS if there exists a
B(\mathfrak{X})
valued function
K_{\omega}(\lambda)
defined on
\Omega\times\Omega
such that the following holds:

•

K_{\omega}u\in\mathcal{H}
for all
u\in\mathfrak{X}
and
\omega\in\Omega
.

•

\left\langle f,K_{\omega}u\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\langle f(\omega),u\rangle_{\mathfrak{X}}
for all
f\in\mathcal{H},~u\in\mathfrak{X},~\omega\in\Omega
.

The
B(\mathfrak{X})
-valued function
K_{\omega}(\lambda)
is unique and is called the reproducing kernel (RK) of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\delta_{\omega}
, given by
\delta_{\omega}(f)=f(\omega)
, denote the point evaluation operator on
\mathcal{H}
at the point
\omega
. Then
K_{\omega}(\lambda)
can be written as
K_{\omega}(\lambda)=\delta_{\lambda}\delta_{\omega}^{*}
. A function
K:\Omega\times\Omega\rightarrow B(\mathfrak{X})
is said to be a positive kernel if, for any choice of
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}\in\Omega
, and
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in\mathfrak{X}
,

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle K_{\omega_{j}}(\omega_{i})u_{j},u_{i}\right\rangle\geqslant 0.

It can be readily verified that the RK of an RKHS is a positive kernel function. Furthermore, the vector valued version of Moore’s theorem
[
42
, Theorem 6.12]
asserts that for any positive kernel
K
, there exists a unique RKHS
\mathcal{H}
of vector-valued functions such that
K
serves as the reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{H}
.

The following lemma from
[
7
, Lemma 5.6]
, gives a useful characterization of RKHS of vector valued holomorphic functions.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an RKHS of
\mathfrak{X}
valued functions defined on some nonempty open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}
with RK
K_{\omega}(\lambda)
. Then
\mathcal{H}
is an RKHS of
\mathfrak{X}
valued holomorphic functions if
K_{\omega}(\lambda)
is a holomorphic function of
\lambda\in\Omega
for every
\omega\in\Omega
and
K_{\omega}(\omega)
is continuous on every compact subset of
\Omega
.

Next, we give some relevant examples of vector valued RKHS with operator valued kernel functions.

Example 2.2
.

It is well known that the Hardy space over the upper half plane given as

H^{2}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathbb{C}_{+})=\left\{f:\mathbb{C}_{+}\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}:f\text{ is holomorphic and }\sup_{y>0}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\|f(x+iy)\|\,dx<\infty\right\}

is an RKHS of
\mathfrak{X}
valued holomorphic functions with RK

K_{\omega}(\lambda)=\frac{I_{\mathfrak{X}}}{\rho_{\omega}(\lambda)}.

Example 2.3
.

Let
S\in S_{B(\mathfrak{X})}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
be an operator valued analytic function and corresponding to this
S
, consider a function defined by

\Gamma_{\xi}^{S}(\lambda)=\frac{I-S(\lambda)S(\xi)^{*}}{\rho_{\xi}(\lambda)}.

It is a positive operator valued kernel function on
\mathbb{C}_{+}\times\mathbb{C}_{+}
which guarantees the existence of a unique RKHS of analytic vector valued functions denoted as
\mathcal{H}(S)
. These spaces are known as the de Branges-Rovnyak spaces corresponding to the upper half plane.

3.
The Potapov-Ginzburg transform of some classes of Fredholm operators

We start with
J(\neq\pm I)\in B(\mathfrak{X})
, a signature operator, i.e., it is both self-adjoint and unitary (
J=J^{*}=J^{-1}
).
Let
P
and
Q
denote the pair of orthogonal projections defined by

P:=\frac{I+J}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad Q:=\frac{I-J}{2}.

(3.0.1)

This shows that
P
and
Q
satisfy the following properties:

•

P+Q=I

\bullet

P-Q=J

\bullet

P=P^{*}=P^{2}

•

Q=Q^{*}=Q^{2}

\bullet

PQ=QP=0

P
and
Q
are the positive and negative parts of
J
, respectively. Two operators
T,S\in B(\mathfrak{X})
are said to be congruent if there exists an invertible operator

such that
T=RSR^{*}
. Let

,

be two signature operators on
\mathfrak{X}
and
(P_{1},~Q_{1})
,
(P_{2},~Q_{2})
be their positive and negative parts, respectively.
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
are congruent iff

\dim(\mathrm{rng}(P_{1}))=\dim(\mathrm{rng}(P_{2}))\text{ and }\dim(\mathrm{rng}(Q_{1}))=\dim(\mathrm{rng}(Q_{2})).

For more information on congruence, see
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
.

Definition 3.1

(

-contractive and

-bicontractive)

An operator

is said to be

and

and

are

(

An operator

is said to be

and

and

are

We first establish a few elementary lemmas. For similar results in the matrix set up, see

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
U\in B(\mathfrak{X})
be
J
-contractive. Then the operator
P+QUQ
is injective.

Proof.

Since

is
J
-contractive,

\displaystyle U^{*}JU

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\Rightarrow U^{*}(P-Q)U

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\Rightarrow U^{*}PU-U^{*}QU

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle P-Q

\displaystyle\Rightarrow U^{*}PU+Q

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle P+U^{*}QU

\displaystyle\Rightarrow Q(U^{*}PU+Q)Q

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle Q(P+U^{*}QU)Q

\displaystyle\Rightarrow QU^{*}PUQ+I

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle QU^{*}QUQ+P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(P+QU^{*}Q)(P+QUQ)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow(P+QU^{*}Q)(P+QUQ)

\displaystyle\succeq

\displaystyle I\hskip 11.38092pt\text{(as $P\succeq 0$).}

As a result, the operator
P+QUQ
is bounded below and hence injective.

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
U\in B(\mathfrak{X})
. Then the operators

P+QUQ,\quad P-QUQ,\quad P+QU,\quad\text{and}\quad P-QU

are either all injective or all are not injective.

Proof.

As
P
and
Q
are orthogonal projections, the result follows easily from the fact that

\ker(P\pm QUQ)=\ker(P\pm QU)

and

\ker(P+QUQ)=\ker(P-QUQ).

(Note:
Pu=0
and
QUQu=0

\Leftrightarrow

Pu=0
and
QUu=0
).

∎

Before the next lemma, we recall some facts related to Fredholm operators. An operator
A\in B(\mathfrak{X})
is said to be Fredholm if it satisfies the following conditions:

•

\mathrm{rng}(A)
is closed in
\mathfrak{X}
.

•

\dim(\ker(A))<\infty
.

•

\dim(\ker(A^{*}))<\infty
.

The index of a Fredholm operator is defined by

\text{index}(A)=\dim(\ker(A))-\dim(\ker(A^{*})).

(3.0.2)

Theorem 3.5
.

If
A,B\in B(\mathfrak{X})
. Then the following assertions are true.

(1)

A
is a Fredholm operator iff
A^{*}
is a Fredholm operator and

\text{index}(A)=\text{index}(A^{*}).

(2)

If
A
is a Fredholm operator then
A
is invertible iff

\text{index}(A)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\ker(A)\ (\text{or}\ \ker(A^{*}))=0.

(3)

If
A,B
are Fredholm then

is a Fredholm operator with

\text{index}(AB)=\text{index}(A)+\text{index}(B).

(4)

If
A\in\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
then
I-A
is a Fredholm operator of index zero.

For more details on Fredholm operators, see
[
27
]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
U\in B(\mathfrak{X})
such that
I-U\in\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
, then the operators

P+UQ,\quad P+QU,\quad Q+UP,\quad Q+PU,\quad\text{and}\quad P+QUQ

are Fredholm operators with index zero.

Proof.

The condition
I-U\in\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
implies that
Q(I-U)\in\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
, given the boundedness of
Q
. Consequently, the operator
I-Q+QU=P+QU
is a Fredholm operator with index zero.
Similarly, the fact that
Q
is bounded ensures that
(I-U)Q\in\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
. This leads to the conclusion that
I-Q+UQ=P+UQ
is also a Fredholm operator with index zero.
Moreover, by pre and post-multiplying
I-U
with
P
, we ascertain that both
PU+Q
and
UP+Q
are Fredholm operators with index zero.
Additionally, it follows from the boundedness of
Q
that
Q(I-U)Q\in\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
. Thus,
I-Q+QUQ=P+QUQ
is a Fredholm operator with index zero.
∎

.

,

,

Let

Since

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(PU+Q)(P+QU)^{-1}

(P-SQ)^{-1}(SP-Q)=U.

This shows

Recall that in finite-dimensional spaces, an operator

Theorem 3.12
.

Let

such that

is

-contractive if and only if
U^{*}
is
J
-contractive.

Proof.

Let

such that

.
First, let
U^{*}
be
J
-contractive. Then, from Theorem
3.11
,
S=PG(U)\in S_{B(\mathfrak{X})}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle I-SS^{*}\succeq 0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle I-S^{*}S\succeq 0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle(P+S^{*}Q)^{-1}(I-S^{*}S)(P+QS)^{-1}\succeq 0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle J-U^{*}JU\succeq 0\hskip 5.69046pt(\text{Using ($\ref{eqn2.11}$)}).

Hence,

-contractive.
The converse argument follows from the fact that

∎

Given the discussions in the previous section, if

\displaystyle S(\lambda)

\displaystyle PG(U(\lambda))

\displaystyle(PU(\lambda)+Q)(P+QU(\lambda))^{-1}.

Also,

\displaystyle U(\lambda)

\displaystyle(PS(\lambda)+Q)(P+QS(\lambda))^{-1}

4.1.
Extension of

into
\mathbb{C}
for

in
\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{S}_{1}}(J)

Thus far, we have constructed a space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}}^{+}(U)
of vector valued analytic functions on the upper half plane using the
PG
transform. Our next goal is to extend
U(\lambda)
to a domain possibly larger than
\mathbb{C}_{+}
. However, we cannot anticipate that
U
in the class
\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}}(J)
shall have a non tangential boundary value. We recollect a few fundamental details about Schatten-von Neumann ideals of
B(\mathfrak{X})
and operator valued Nevanlinna-Herglotz functions to establish the required extension. For a detailed discussion on these topics, see (
[
28
]
,
[
24
]
).

Let
T
be a compact operator on the Hilbert space
\mathfrak{X}
and
s_{n}(T)
denotes its singular values (arranged in a non-increasing order), i.e., the non negative square root of the eigenvalues of
T^{*}T
. The Schatten-von Neumann class, denoted by
\mathfrak{S}_{p}
(
p>0
) is the class of all compact operators
T
on
\mathfrak{X}
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(T)^{p}<\infty.

Also,

\|T\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{p}}={\biggl(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(T)^{p}\biggl)}^{\frac{1}{p}}

(4.1.1)

represents a norm in
\mathfrak{S}_{p}
for
p\geq 1
and a quasinorm for
p\in(0,1)
.

We also recall the Matsaev class, denoted by
\mathfrak{S}_{\omega}
, and defined as the class of all compact operators
T
on the Hilbert space
\mathfrak{X}
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(T)/n=\|T\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{\omega}}<\infty.

More generally, we recall the Lorentz class, denoted by
\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}
, and defined as the class of all compact operators
T
on the Hilbert space
\mathfrak{X}
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(T)\pi_{n}=\|T\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}}<\infty,

where
\pi=\{\pi_{n}\}
is a non-increasing sequence such that
\pi_{1}=1
,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n}=0
, and
\sum_{n}\pi_{n}=\infty
. Observe that
\mathfrak{S}_{\omega}
is a particular case of
\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}
for
\pi=\{1/n\}
.

Definition 4.2

(Operator valued Nevanlinna-Herglotz functions)
.

A map
T:\mathbb{C}_{+}\rightarrow B(\mathfrak{X})
is said to be a bounded operator valued Nevanlinna-Herglotz function (also called operator valued R function) on
\mathfrak{X}
if
T
is analytic on
\mathbb{C}_{+}
and
(T(\lambda))_{I}\succeq 0
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
.

The following discusses a theorem by Naboko (see
[
24
]
,
[
39
]
,
[
40
]
) that sheds light on the nature of the boundary value of a certain class of trace ideals.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
T(\lambda)
be
\mathfrak{S}_{1}
valued Nevanlinna-Herglotz function. Then the non tangential boundary values
T(\mu)
exist in the
\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}
-norm a.e. on
\mathbb{R}
if and only if the following condition holds true:

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\pi_{n}/n<\infty.

(4.1.2)

For the possible extension of
U
to
\mathbb{R}
, the above theorem motivates us to restrict the class
\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}}(J)
to the class of all
J
-contractive

valued functions

such that
I-U(\lambda)
is
\mathfrak{S}_{1}
valued Nevanlinna-Herglotz function. We denote this class by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{S}_{1}}(J)
. From Theorem
4.3
, we also get that for any

, the non tangential boundary values of

\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}

) holds. As a result, for any

a.e. on
\mathbb{R}
, particularly in
\mathfrak{S}_{\omega}
-norm. Note that,

I-U(\lambda)\rightarrow I-U(\mu)\hskip 5.69046pt\text{a.e. on $\mathbb{R}$ in $\mathfrak{S}_{\omega}$-norm}

implies

\|U(\lambda)-U(\mu)\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{\omega}}=\|(I-U(\lambda))-(I-U(\mu))\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{\omega}}\rightarrow 0,

as
\lambda\rightarrow\mu
non tangentially a.e. on
\mathbb{R}
. Hence,

a.e. on
\mathbb{R}
strongly as
\lambda\rightarrow\mu
non tangentially. Indeed,

\displaystyle\|U(\lambda)z-U(\mu)z\|_{\mathfrak{X}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|U(\lambda)-U(\mu)\|_{\text{op}}\|z\|_{\mathfrak{X}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|U(\lambda)-U(\mu)\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{\omega}}\|z\|_{\mathfrak{X}}\hskip 2.84544pt\text{for all}\hskip 2.84544pt~z\in\mathfrak{X},

where
\|.\|_{\text{op}}
denotes the operator norm. An operator valued function
U\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{S}_{1}}(J)
is said to belong to the class
\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{S}_{1}}(J)
if its non tangential boundary values
U(\mu)
are
J
-isometric a.e. on
\mathbb{R}
and
U
is invertible at every point in
\mathbb{C}_{+}
. Also observe that for
U\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{S}_{1}}(J)
, Remark

ensures that
U(\mu)^{*}
is
J
-isometric a.e. on
\mathbb{R}
.
We shall now use the symmetry principle to extend

in the class
\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{S}_{1}}(J)
to
\mathbb{C}_{-}
. Define
U(\lambda)
on
\mathbb{C}_{-}
by

U(\lambda)=J\{U(\bar{\lambda})^{*}\}^{-1}J.

(4.1.3)

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
U\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{S}_{1}}(J)
and
U(\lambda)
be defined on
\mathbb{C}_{-}
by
(\ref{ext})
. Then, the following statements hold:

(1)

is

-expansive on
\mathbb{C}_{-}
.

(2)

On
\mathbb{C}_{-}
,

defines an
\mathfrak{S}_{1}

Proof.

Observe that for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{-}

is

But

and hence that

By pre and post multiplying both sides of the above inequality by

Remark 4.5
.

Let

. Clearly,
h_{U}\supseteq\mathbb{C}_{+}\cup\mathbb{C}_{-}
. Furthermore, a real point

if and only if

and

for some

. Moreover,

holds for every

a.e. on
\mathbb{R}
in
\mathfrak{S}_{\omega}
-norm.

Now, for

Lemma 4.6
.

If
U\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{S}_{1}}(J)
, then the kernel function defined below is positive on

.

K_{\xi}^{U}(\lambda):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{J-U(\lambda)JU(\xi)^{*}}{\rho_{\xi}(\lambda)}&\mbox{if }\lambda\neq\overline{\xi}\\
\frac{U^{\prime}(\bar{\xi})JU(\xi)^{*}}{2\pi i}&\mbox{if }\lambda=\overline{\xi}.\end{array}\right.

(4.1.4)

Proof.

To show
K_{\xi}^{U}(\lambda)
is positive, we need to show that for every choice of
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
z_{1},\hskip 2.84544ptz_{2},\hskip 2.84544pt\ldots z_{n}\in h_{U}
and

,

\sum_{l,m=1}^{n}\left\langle K_{z_{m}}^{U}(z_{l})u_{m},u_{l}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{X}}\geq 0.

(4.1.5)

We shall show one case in which some points of
z_{i}
belong to
\mathbb{C}_{+}
and the rest are in
\mathbb{C}_{-}
and all other cases shall be covered in this case.

Let
z_{1},\hskip 2.84544ptz_{2},\hskip 2.84544pt\ldots z_{i}\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
and
\xi_{1},\hskip 2.84544pt\xi_{2},\hskip 2.84544pt\ldots\xi_{j}\in\mathbb{C}_{-}
, where
\xi_{k}=z_{i+k},
for
k=1,\hskip 2.84544pt2,\hskip 2.84544pt\ldots j,\hskip 2.84544pti+j=n
. Also say,
u_{i+k}=v_{k},\hskip 2.84544ptk=1,\hskip 2.84544pt2,\hskip 2.84544pt\ldots j
. For
z\neq\bar{\xi}

\displaystyle K_{\xi}^{U}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{J-U(z)JU(\xi)^{*}}{\rho_{\xi}(z)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(U(\bar{\xi})-U(z))JU(\xi)^{*}}{\rho_{\xi}(z)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(R_{\bar{\xi}}U)(z)JU(\xi)^{*}}{2\pi i},

and

\displaystyle K_{\xi}^{U}(z)^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{J-U(\xi)JU(z)^{*}}{\rho_{z}(\xi)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(R_{\bar{z}}U)(\xi)JU(z)^{*}}{2\pi i}.

We split L.H.S of (
4.1.5
) into four parts. First part is

\sum_{l,m=1}^{i}\Big\langle K_{z_{l}}^{U}(z_{m})u_{l},u_{m}\Big\rangle_{\mathfrak{X}}.

(4.1.6)

Second part is

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{i}\sum_{t=1}^{j}\Big\langle K_{z_{l}}^{U}(\xi_{t})u_{l},v_{t}\Big\rangle_{\mathfrak{X}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{i}\sum_{t=1}^{j}\Big\langle u_{l},K_{z_{l}}^{U}(\xi_{t})v_{t}\Big\rangle_{\mathfrak{X}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{i}\sum_{t=1}^{j}\left\langle u_{l},\frac{(R_{\bar{\xi_{t}}}U)(z_{l})JU(\xi_{t})^{*}v_{t}}{2\pi i}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{X}}.

(4.1.7)

Third part is

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{j}\sum_{m=1}^{i}\Big\langle K_{\xi_{m}}^{U}(z_{s})v_{m},u_{s}\Big\rangle_{\mathfrak{X}}=\sum_{s=1}^{j}\sum_{m=1}^{i}\left\langle\frac{(R_{\bar{\xi_{m}}}U)(z_{s})JU(\xi_{m})^{*}v_{m}}{2\pi i},u_{s}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{X}}.

(4.1.8)

Fourth part is

\displaystyle\sum_{m,t=1}^{j}\Big\langle K_{\xi_{m}}^{U}(\xi_{t})v_{m},v_{t}\Big\rangle_{\mathfrak{X}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,t=1}^{j}\left\langle\frac{J-U(\xi_{t})JU(\xi_{m})^{*}}{-2\pi i(\xi_{t}-\bar{\xi_{m}})}v_{m},v_{t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,t=1}^{j}\left\langle\frac{U(\xi_{t})JU(\bar{\xi_{t}})^{*}-U(\xi_{t})JU(\xi_{m})^{*}}{-2\pi i(\xi_{t}-\bar{\xi_{m}})}v_{m},v_{t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,t=1}^{j}\left\langle\frac{U(\xi_{t})J\{U(\bar{\xi_{t}})^{*}JU(\bar{\xi_{m}})-J\}JU(\xi_{m})^{*}}{-2\pi i(\xi_{t}-\bar{\xi_{m}})}v_{m},v_{t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=4\pi^{2}\sum_{m,t=1}^{j}\left\langle\frac{J-U(\bar{\xi_{t}})^{*}JU(\bar{\xi_{m}})}{\rho_{\bar{\xi_{t}}}(\bar{\xi_{m}})}x_{m},x_{t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\Big\|\sum_{m=1}^{j}(R_{\bar{\xi_{m}}}U)x_{m}\Big\|^{2},

(4.1.9)

where

.
The last inequality follows from the Lemma
4.4
. On adding four parts, we get

\sum_{l,m=1}^{n}\Big\langle K_{z_{m}}^{U}(z_{l})u_{m},u_{l}\Big\rangle_{\mathfrak{X}}\geq\Big\|\sum_{l=1}^{i}K_{z_{l}}^{U}u_{l}+\sum_{m=1}^{j}(R_{\bar{\xi_{m}}}U)x_{m}\Big\|^{2}\geq 0.

We finally describe the class

and corresponding vector valued de Branges space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{U}}(U)
. Let
\Omega
be a nonempty open subset of the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
, which is symmetric with respect to the real line
\mathbb{R}
. Let
\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}}(J)
denotes the class of all operator valued functions defined on
\Omega
such that for any
U\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}}(J)
the following conditions hold:

•

is meromorphic on
\Omega
.

•

I-U(\lambda)
is compact on
h_{U}
and
U(\lambda)^{*}

-contractive on
h_{U}^{+}=h_{U}\cap\mathbb{C}_{+}
.

•

is invertible for at least one point in

•

is

.

•

holds for all

for which

is invertible.

For any

the kernel defined by

K_{\xi}^{U}(\lambda):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{J-U(\lambda)JU(\xi)^{*}}{\rho_{\xi}(\lambda)}&\mbox{if }\lambda,~\xi\in h_{U}~\mbox{and}~\lambda\neq\overline{\xi}\\
\frac{U^{\prime}(\bar{\xi})JU(\xi)^{*}}{2\pi i}&\mbox{if }\xi,~\bar{\xi}\in h_{U}~\mbox{and}~\lambda=\overline{\xi}.\end{array}\right.

(4.1.10)

is positive on
h_{U}\times h_{U}
. We refer to the corresponding RKHS of
\mathfrak{X}
valued functions as
de Branges space
and shall be denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}}}(U)
. Furthermore, since the conditions of the Lemma
2.1
are satisfied, every
\mathfrak{X}
valued function

is holomorphic on
h_{U}
. It is convenient to set
\mathcal{U}(J)=\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}}(J)
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{U}}(U)=\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}}}(U)
.

Example 4.7
.

Let

denote the fundamental solution of the entire operator differential equation

(4.1.11)

where

, and

(

It follows that

, and

is
J
-contractive in upper half plane since

-unitary on

\displaystyle JU_{t}(\bar{\lambda})^{*-1}J

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J\{(I+i\bar{\lambda}VV^{*}Jt)^{*}\}^{-1}J

\displaystyle J(I-i\lambda tJVV^{*})^{-1}J

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{tVV^{*}}{2\pi}

is positive. Thus, the

space exists and

5.
Connection between two classes of de Branges spaces

In this section, we shall briefly discuss two classes of de Branges spaces as constructed in the previous section and establish their connection. For every
U\in\mathcal{U}(J)
, the RKHS
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{U}}(U)
, with the RK given by
(
4.1.10
)
, constitutes the first class. Another class of de Branges spaces, denoted by
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
, was constructed in
[
36
]
. This class corresponds to a de Branges operator, which is a pair of operator valued entire functions denoted by
\mathfrak{E}(z)=(E_{-}(z),~E_{+}(z))
, such that the components of
\mathfrak{E}(z)
satisfy the following conditions:

(1)

E_{+}
,
E_{-}:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow B(\mathfrak{X})
both are Fredholm operators for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

(2)

E_{+}
and
E_{-}
both are invertible for at least one point.

(3)

E_{+}^{-1}E_{-}\in S_{B(\mathfrak{X})}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
such that the boundary functions are unitary a.e. on
\mathbb{R}
.

Now corresponding to a de Branges operator, we consider the kernel given by:

K_{\xi}^{\mathfrak{E}}(z):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{E_{+}(z)E_{+}(\xi)^{*}-E_{-}(z)E_{-}(\xi)^{*}}{\rho_{\xi}(z)}&\mbox{if }z\neq\overline{\xi}\\
\frac{E_{+}^{{}^{\prime}}(\overline{\xi})E_{+}(\xi)^{*}-E_{-}^{{}^{\prime}}(\overline{\xi})E_{-}(\xi)^{*}}{-2\pi i}&\mbox{if }z=\overline{\xi}\end{array}\right.

(5.0.1)

on
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
. The kernel is positive on
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
, and
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
denotes the corresponding unique RKHS of
\mathfrak{X}
-valued entire functions. Moreover,

\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})=\left\{f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}\mid f\text{ is entire},\,E_{+}^{-1}f\in H_{\mathfrak{X}}^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}),\,\text{and}\,E_{-}^{-1}f\in H_{\mathfrak{X}}^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})^{\perp}\right\},

and the inner product in
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
is given by

\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left\langle(E_{+}^{-1}f)(x),(E_{+}^{-1}g)(x)\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{X}}\,dx.

(5.0.2)

The space
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
serves as a generalization of the Paley-Wiener spaces of
\mathfrak{X}
-valued entire functions. Recall that the Paley-Wiener space denoted by
PW_{a}
(for some
a>0
) is an RKHS with the RK

K_{\omega}(\lambda)=\frac{\sin(\lambda-\bar{\omega})a}{\pi(\lambda-\bar{\omega})}I_{\mathfrak{X}}.

This is an example of de Branges space
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
corresponding to the de Branges operator
\mathfrak{E}(z)
with

E_{+}(z)=e^{-iaz}I_{\mathfrak{X}}~\mbox{and}~E_{-}(z)=e^{iaz}I_{\mathfrak{X}}.

The space
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
is also connected to quasi Lagrange-type interpolation. For more details, see
[
37
]
,
[
38
]
.

The connections between the two classes of vector valued de Branges spaces, which are based on matrix valued RK, have been recently established in
[
20
]
and
[
9
]
. Here, we shall discuss the analogous connections between the de Branges spaces
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{U}}(U)
and
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
. In the next theorem, we show that every
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{U}}(U)
space is a
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
space.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
U\in\mathcal{U}(J)
such that
U
is an entire operator valued function and
U(z)=I
for some
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
. The RKHS
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{U}}(U)
coincides with the de Branges space
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
corresponding to the de Branges operator
\mathfrak{E}(z)=(E_{-},~E_{+})
such that

E_{+}(\lambda)=P+U(\lambda)Q\quad\mbox{and}\quad E_{-}(\lambda)=Q+U(\lambda)P,

and the inner product is given by

\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{U}}(U)}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Big\langle(E_{+}^{-1}f)(x),(E_{+}^{-1}g)(x)\Big\rangle_{\mathfrak{X}}dx.

(5.0.3)

Additionally, we have
U(\lambda)=E_{+}(\lambda)+E_{-}(\lambda)-I
.

Proof.

Under the given conditions that
I-U(\lambda)\in\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, it follows that
Q-U(\lambda)Q\in\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
. Consequently,

E_{+}(\lambda)=P+U(\lambda)Q=I-(Q-U(\lambda)Q)

is a Fredholm operator with index
0
. Similarly,
E_{-}(\lambda)
is also a Fredholm operator with index
0
. Since
U
is entire, it follows that both
E_{+}(\lambda)
and
E_{-}(\lambda)
are operator valued entire functions. Furthermore, if
U(z)=I
for some
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
, then it implies that
E_{+}(z)=I
and
E_{-}(z)=I
, which ensures that
E_{+}
and
E_{-}
are invertible for at least one point. Considering the function
F
,

F:=E_{+}^{-1}E_{-}=(P+U(\lambda)Q)^{-1}(Q+U(\lambda)P),

we need to verify that
F
is inner from both sides.
Let
S=PG(U)
, then the equality

(P+UQ)(J)(P-SQ)=I=J(P-SQ)(P+UQ)

can be easily verified. This leads to the conclusions that
E_{+}^{-1}=J(P-SQ)
and

E_{-}=Q+UP=Q+(P-SQ)^{-1}(SP-Q)P=(P-SQ)^{-1}SJ.

Consequently,
F=JSJ
is inner from both sides. Indeed, the following holds:

\displaystyle F(\lambda)^{*}F(\lambda)=JS^{*}SJ\preceq I~\mbox{for all}~\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{+},

\displaystyle F(\lambda)^{*}F(\lambda)=JS^{*}SJ=I~\mbox{for all}~\lambda\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle F(\lambda)F(\lambda)^{*}=JSS^{*}J=I~\mbox{for all}~\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

Furthermore, the equality

J-U(\lambda)JU(\omega)^{*}=E_{+}(\lambda)E_{+}(\omega)^{*}-E_{-}(\lambda)E_{-}(\omega)^{*}

can also be verified as well. Finally, the equality in
(
5.0.3
)
follows from the inner product in
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
given by
(
5.0.2
)
.
∎

Conversely, we aim to determine when a de Branges space
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
corresponding to a de Branges operator
\mathfrak{E}(z)=(E_{-},~E_{+})
is an
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{U}}(U)
space for some

.

Theorem 5.2
.

If
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
is a de Branges space corresponding to the de Branges operator
\mathfrak{E}(z)=(E_{-},~E_{+})
such that the following conditions hold:

(1)

E_{+}P=P
and
E_{-}Q=Q
.

(2)

Both
I-E_{+}
and
I-E_{-}
are in the class
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
.

Then, for
U=E_{+}+E_{-}-I
, it follows that
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})=\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{U}}(U)
.

Proof.

The proof follows the same technique as in
[
8
, Theorem 2.2]
. The compactness of
I-U
can be readily verified.
∎

6.
Operator nodes and de Branges spaces

The theory of operator nodes and their characteristic functions was introduced by Brodskii and has found extensive applications in scattering theory, electrical circuits, and the theory of conservative and passive systems (see
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
). In this section, we recapitulate the notion of operator nodes and establish their connections with de Branges spaces. For more details on operator nodes, we refer to the monograph by Brodskii
[
19
]
.

Let
\mathfrak{H}
and
\mathfrak{G}
be separable Hilbert spaces, and let
A:\mathfrak{H}\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}
,
J:\mathfrak{G}\rightarrow\mathfrak{G}
, and
K:\mathfrak{H}\rightarrow\mathfrak{G}
be bounded linear operators. Here,
J
is a signature operator. The quadruple
\Theta:=(A,K,J;\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{G})
is called an operator node if

A-A^{*}=iK^{*}JK.

(6.0.1)

The spaces
\mathfrak{H}
and
\mathfrak{G}
are called interior and exterior spaces respectively. Also, the operators
A
,
K
and
J
are called basic, canal and directing respectively. The operator node
\Theta
is said to be simple if

\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{A^{n}Kg\mid g\in\mathfrak{G},~n=0,1,2,\ldots\}=\mathfrak{H}.

(6.0.2)

Remark 6.1
.

For a simple operator node the following equivalent conditions can be noted.

\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{A^{n}Kg\mid g\in\mathfrak{G},~n=0,1,2,\ldots\}=\mathfrak{H},

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{(I-\lambda A)^{-1}Kg\mid g\in\mathfrak{G},~\lambda\in\Omega\}=\mathfrak{H},

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{(A^{*})^{n}Kg\mid g\in\mathfrak{G},~n=0,1,2,\ldots\}=\mathfrak{H},

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{(I-\lambda A^{*})^{-1}Kg\mid g\in\mathfrak{G},~\lambda\in\Omega\}=\mathfrak{H},

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\overset{\infty}{\underset{n=0}{\cap}}\ker(KA^{n})=\{0\},

where
\Omega
is some neighborhood of zero.

The operator valued holomorphic function

U_{\Theta}(\lambda)=I+i\lambda K(I-\lambda A)^{-1}K^{*}J

defined for all
\lambda\in r(A):=\{\omega\in\mathbb{C}:(I-\omega A)^{-1}\in B(\mathfrak{X})
} is called the characteristic function of the operator node
\Theta
. An operator
A
is said to be Volterra if it is compact and the spectrum of
A
is
\{0\}
. A node
\Theta
is said to be a
Volterra operator node
if it satisfies the following conditions: the basic operator
A
is a Volterra operator and the canal operator
K\in\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
. A node
\Theta
is termed as
quasi-Hermitian operator node
if
K\in\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
. Furthermore,
\Theta
is classified as a
dissipative operator node
if
J=I
.

Definition 6.2
.

We say that the two operator nodes
\Delta=(A_{1},K_{1},J;\mathfrak{H}_{1},\mathfrak{G})
and
\Theta=(A_{2},K_{2},J;\mathfrak{H}_{2},\mathfrak{G})
are unitarily similar if there exists a unitary operator
T:\mathfrak{H}_{1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}_{2}
such that

A_{2}=TA_{1}T^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad K_{2}=K_{1}T^{-1},

and
T
is called a unitary similarity operator from
\Delta
to
\Theta
.

In the next theorem, we construct a simple quasi-Hermitian operator node using the de Branges space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{U}}(U)
, for
U\in\mathfrak{LB}(J)
. The definition of the class
\mathfrak{LB}(J)
, also called the Livsic-Brodskii class, is motivated by the definitions given by Arov and Dym in
[
7
, Chapter 6]
, and Brosdskii in
[
19
, Chapter 1, Section 9]
. Here,
\mathfrak{LB}(J)
is defined as the class of operator valued functions
W(\lambda)
that are meromorphic on
\mathbb{C}
and satisfy the following properties:

(1)

\mathbb{C}_{+}\cup\mathbb{R}\subset h_{W}
.

(2)

W^{*}(\lambda)JW(\lambda)\leq J
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
.

(3)

W^{*}(\lambda)JW(\lambda)=J
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

(4)

W(0)=I
.

(5)

I-W(\lambda)\in\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
for all
\lambda\in h_{W}
.

We state the Fredholm analytic theorem
[
25
, Theorem 3.3]
that we shall use.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
T:\Omega\rightarrow B(\mathfrak{X})
be a Fredholm operator valued analytic function, where
\Omega
is an open connected subset of
\mathbb{C}
. Then, exactly one of the following two conditions holds true:

•

T(\lambda)
is not boundedly invertible for any
\lambda\in\Omega
.

•

T(\lambda)^{-1}
is an analytic Fredholm operator valued function for all
\lambda\in\Omega\setminus D
, where
D
in
\Omega
is a discrete set (a countable set with no accumulation point in
\Omega
) and
T(\lambda)^{-1}
is meromorphic on
\Omega
.

Since
W(\lambda)
is meromorphic on
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{C}_{+}\cup\mathbb{R}\subset h_{W}
, there exists a discrete set
D\subset\mathbb{C}_{-}
where
W(\lambda)
is not analytic. Let
\Omega=\mathbb{C}\setminus D
. By applying the identity theorem
[
32
, Theorem 3.11.5]
, we can assert that
W^{*}(\bar{\lambda})JW(\lambda)-J=0
holds for all
\lambda\in\Omega
. Additionally,
W(\lambda)JW^{*}(\bar{\lambda})=J
shall hold for all
\lambda\in\Omega
for which
W(\lambda)
is invertible. Since this set is non empty and clopen, it implies that
W(\lambda)
is invertible for all
\lambda\in\Omega
. Now, by the Fredholm analytic theorem, we can assert that if
W(\lambda)
belongs to the class
\mathfrak{LB}(J)
, then

W(\lambda)=J\{W(\bar{\lambda})^{*}\}^{-1}J

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus D
, where
D\subset\mathbb{C}_{-}
is a discrete set. Consequently, it follows that
\mathfrak{LB}(J)\subset\mathcal{U}(J)
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
U\in\mathfrak{LB}(J)
,
A=R_{0}
,
\mathfrak{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{U}}(U)
, and
Kf=\sqrt{2\pi}f(0)
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{U}}(U)
. Then,
\Theta=(R_{0},K,J;\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{U}}(U),\mathfrak{X})
is a simple quasi-Hermitian operator node, and
U_{\Theta}(\lambda)\equiv U(\lambda)
on
h_{U}
.

Proof.

The proof that
\Theta
is a simple operator node and the characteristic function coincides with
U
on
h_{U}
follows from a similar framework of the proof presented in
[
7
, Theorem 6.1]
. We need to show that
\Theta
is a quasi-Hermitian node. First, observe that the kernel function
K_{\omega}(\lambda)
of the RKHS
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{U}}(U)
is given by
(
4.1.10
)
on
\mathbb{C}\setminus D\times\mathbb{C}\setminus D
is positive, where
D\subset\mathbb{C}_{-}
is a discrete set and
\mathbb{C}\setminus D
is dense in
\mathbb{C}
. Hence, it follows that
K_{\omega}^{U}(\lambda)
is positive on
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
. Thus,

K_{0}(0)=\frac{U^{\prime}(0)J}{2\pi i},\quad\mbox{where}~U^{\prime}(0)=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\frac{U(\lambda)-I}{\lambda}.

Given that
I-U(\lambda)
is compact for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus D
, it follows that
K_{0}(0)
is compact. Since

K_{0}(0)=\delta_{0}\delta_{0}^{*},

this implies that
\delta_{0}
, and consequently the operator
K
, is compact. Hence, the operator node
\Theta
is quasi-Hermitian.
∎

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
U\in\mathfrak{LB}(J)
, such that
U
is analytic in
\mathbb{C}
. Then,
\Theta
as defined in Theorem
6.4
is a simple Volterra operator node and
U_{\Theta}(\lambda)\equiv U(\lambda)
on
\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

The compactness of the imaginary part of
R_{0}
follows from the theorem mentioned above. The analyticity of
U
at every point in
\mathbb{C}
implies that the spectrum of
R_{0}
is
\{0\}
. The compactness of the operator
R_{0}
now follows from
[
19
, Theorem 10.1]
which states that if the spectrum of a bounded linear operator operating on a Hilbert space, does not contain points other than zero and its imaginary component is compact, then the operator is Volterra.
∎

In the subsequent theorem, we provide a functional model for a simple Volterra operator node using the de Branges space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{U}}(U)
. A comparable functional model for simple operator nodes in the matrix setup, where
\mathfrak{X}=\mathbb{C}^{m}
, has been presented by Arov and Dym in
[
7
]
.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
\Delta=(A,L,J;\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{X})
be a simple Volterra operator node. Then
U_{\Delta}\in\mathfrak{LB}(J)
class. The operator
T_{\Delta}:\mathfrak{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{U}}(U_{\Delta})
given by

T_{\Delta}f(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}L(I-\lambda A)^{-1}f

is a unitary operator. Furthermore,
T_{\Delta}
is a unitary similarity operator from the node
\Delta
to the Volterra operator node
\Theta=(R_{0},K,J;\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{U}}(U_{\Delta}),\mathfrak{X})
, where
K
is as defined in Theorem
6.4
.

Proof.

After some calculations, it can be shown that the characteristic function
U_{\Delta}(\lambda)
for the operator node
\Delta
satisfies the identity

U_{\Delta}(\lambda)JU_{\Delta}(\omega)^{*}=J+i(\lambda-\bar{\omega})L(I-\lambda A)^{-1}(I-\bar{\omega}A^{*})^{-1}L^{*},

which implies that it satisfies conditions (2)-(3) in the definition of the class
\mathfrak{LB}(J)
. Clearly,
U_{\Delta}(0)=I
. Since the spectrum of
A
is
\{0\}
, we conclude that
h_{U_{\Delta}}=\mathbb{C}
. The compactness of
I-U_{\Delta}(\lambda)
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
is guaranteed by the fact that
L
is compact. The remainder of the proof aligns with
[
7
, Theorem 6.6]
.
∎

We end this section by revisiting the concept of associated functions for the de Branges spaces
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
. A
B(\mathfrak{X})
valued entire function
S(z)
is said to be associated to the de Branges space
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
if
S(\alpha)
is invertible for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
and for every

,

\frac{f(z)-S(z)S(\alpha)^{-1}f(\alpha)}{z-\alpha}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E}).

In this case, we define
R_{S}(\alpha)
as the linear transformation on
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
given by

(R_{S}(\alpha)f)(z):=\frac{f(z)-S(z)S(\alpha)^{-1}f(\alpha)}{z-\alpha},\hskip 8.5359pt\text{for all}\hskip 2.84544ptz\in\mathbb{C}.

Theorem 6.7
.

Suppose that
\Delta_{1}=(A_{1},L_{1},I;\mathfrak{H}_{1},\mathfrak{X})
and

are two simple operator nodes. If the characteristic functions

and

are unitarily similar.

Theorem 6.8
.

Let
\Delta=(\frac{i}{2}L^{*}L,L,I;\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{X})
be a simple dissipative Volterra operator node. Then its characteristic function can be factorized as follows

U_{\Delta}(\lambda)=E_{+}^{-1}(\lambda)E_{-}(\lambda),~\mbox{for all}~\lambda\in\mathbb{C},

where
E_{+}
and
E_{-}
corresponds to a de Branges operator
\mathfrak{E}(\lambda)
. Furthermore, the node
\Delta
is unitarily similar to a simple dissipative Volterra operator node given by
\Theta=(R_{E_{+}}(0),K,I;\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E}),\mathfrak{X})
, where
K
is as defined in Theorem
6.4
.

Proof.

It is easy to observe that
U_{\Delta}(\lambda)
can be factorized as follows:

\displaystyle U_{\Delta}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+i\lambda L(I-\lambda\frac{i}{2}L^{*}L)^{-1}L^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I-\frac{i\lambda}{2}LL^{*})^{-1}(I+\frac{i\lambda}{2}LL^{*}),~\mbox{for all}~\lambda\in\mathbb{C}.

Now, let

E_{+}=(I-\frac{i\lambda}{2}LL^{*})~\mbox{and}~E_{-}=(I+\frac{i\lambda}{2}LL^{*}).

Since,
L
is compact, we get that both
E_{+}
and
E_{-}
are Fredholm operator valued entire functions. Moreover, both
E_{+}
and
E_{-}
are invertible at
0
. It can be seen that for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
, the following inequality holds true:

\displaystyle(E_{+}^{-1}E_{-})^{*}(\lambda)(E_{+}^{-1}E_{-})(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{\Delta}^{*}(\lambda)U_{\Delta}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+i(\lambda-\bar{\lambda})L(I+\frac{i\bar{\lambda}}{2}L^{*}L)^{-1}(I-\frac{i\lambda}{2}L^{*}L)^{-1}L^{*}

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle I.

Also, for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the following equalities hold true:

\displaystyle(E_{+}^{-1}E_{-})^{*}(\lambda)(E_{+}^{-1}E_{-})(\lambda)=U_{\Delta}^{*}(\lambda)U_{\Delta}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I,

\displaystyle(E_{+}^{-1}E_{-})(\lambda)(E_{+}^{-1}E_{-})^{*}(\lambda)=U_{\Delta}(\lambda)U_{\Delta}^{*}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I.

Hence,
E_{+}
and
E_{-}
correspond to a de Branges operator
\mathfrak{E}(\lambda)
. By
[
37
, Theorem 7.1]
, the node
\Theta=(R_{E_{+}}(0),K,I;\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E}),\mathfrak{X})
, is a simple dissipative operator node and
E_{+}^{-1}E_{-}=U_{\Delta}(\lambda)
is the characteristic operator function of
\Theta
. Observe that
E_{+}(\lambda)
is an invertible operator for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. Also,

\displaystyle K_{0}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{E_{+}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)E_{+}(0)^{*}-E_{-}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)E_{-}(0)^{*}}{-2\pi i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{E_{+}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)-E_{-}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)}{-2\pi i}=\frac{-LL^{*}}{2\pi},

which is a compact operator. So, by
[
37
, Corollary
7.2
]
, the simple dissipative operator node
\Theta
is also a Volterra node. Now, using the Theorem
6.7
, we conclude that the two operator nodes
\Delta
and
\Theta
are unitarily similar.

7.
\mathcal{H}_{0}(U)
as a model space

Consider
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, a nonempty open set which is symmetric about the real axis, and let
U(\lambda)
be an analytic operator valued function defined on
\Omega
that satisfies the following identity:

U(\lambda)JU(\bar{\lambda})^{*}=J=U(\bar{\lambda})^{*}JU(\lambda)~\mbox{for all}~\lambda\in\Omega.

(7.0.1)

The above equality implies that
U(\lambda)
is an invertible operator for all
\lambda\in\Omega
where

U(\lambda)^{-1}=JU(\bar{\lambda})^{*}J.

In general, the vector valued de Branges space
\mathcal{H}(U)
, is the RKHS of analytic vector valued functions defined on
\Omega
with the kernel function given by
(
4.1.4
)
. In this section, we focus on another subclass of the

spaces for which

is not compact for all
\lambda\in\Omega
. This class shall be denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{0}(U)
.
Here, we aim to outline
\mathcal{H}_{0}(U)
as a model space for closed, densely defined, simple, symmetric operators with infinite deficiency indices. This class of operators with equal and finite deficiency indices was introduced by Krein in
[
35
]
and its connections with
\mathcal{H}(U)
and
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
spaces corresponding to matrix valued RK’s were studied in
[
2
]
,
[
21
]
. This framework was further extended in
[
36
]
to encompass the case of Krein’s entire operators with infinite deficiency indices and establish its connections with the de Branges space
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
which are based on a pair of Fredholm operator valued entire functions.

Before proceeding, we address the following known characterization of de Branges spaces that shall be required in this section. This characterization is orignally due to de Branges
[
13
]
and later a technical improvement of this characterization was done by Rovnyak in
[
44
]
. Here, we present Rovnyak’s characterization for
\mathcal{H}(U)
spaces corresponding to a nonempty open subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
which is symmetric about the real axis.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an RKHS of vector valued analytic functions defined on
\Omega
which is symmetric about the real axis. Then
\mathcal{H}
is a
\mathcal{H}(U)
space if and only if the following conditions hold:

(1)

\mathcal{H}
is
R_{\alpha}
invariant for all
\alpha\in\Omega
.

(2)

The de Branges identity given by

\langle R_{\alpha}f,g\rangle-\langle f,R_{\beta}g\rangle-(\alpha-\bar{\beta})\langle R_{\alpha}f,R_{\beta}g\rangle=2\pi ig(\beta)^{*}Jf(\alpha),

(7.0.2)

holds true for all
f,~g\in\mathcal{H}
and
\alpha,~\beta\in\Omega
.

In this section, we require a version of de Branges spaces
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
which are based on a pair of Fredholm operator valued analytic functions. Here, we consider de Branges operators
\mathfrak{E}(z)=(E_{-}(z),E_{+}(z))
as a pair of Fredholm operator valued functions which are analytic on an open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}
which is symmetric about the real axis such that both
E_{+}
and
E_{-}
are invertible for atleast at one point in
\Omega
. Moreover,
E_{+}^{-1}E_{-}
satisfy the following conditions:

\displaystyle(E_{+}^{-1}E_{-})^{*}(\lambda)(E_{+}^{-1}E_{-})(\lambda)

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle I~\mbox{for all}~\lambda\in\Omega\cap\mathbb{C}_{+},

\displaystyle(E_{+}^{-1}E_{-})^{*}(\lambda)(E_{+}^{-1}E_{-})(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I~\mbox{for all}~\lambda\in\Omega\cap\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle(E_{+}^{-1}E_{-})(\lambda)(E_{+}^{-1}E_{-})^{*}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I~\mbox{for all}~\lambda\in\Omega\cap\mathbb{R}.

Corresponding to this de Branges operator
\mathfrak{E}(z)
, consider the kernel function given by (
5.0.1
). The kernel is positive on
\Omega\times\Omega
, and
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{E})
denotes the corresponding unique RKHS of
\mathfrak{X}
-valued analytic functions on
\Omega
.

Let
E
be a simple, closed, densely defined, symmetric operator on the Hilbert space
\mathfrak{X}
with infinite deficiency indices, and let
Y
be a closed infinite dimensional subspace of
\mathfrak{X}
. Denote
\mathfrak{M}_{\xi}
as the range of the operator
E-\xi I
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{C}
. Following
[
29
, Appendix 1]
, a point
\xi\in\mathbb{C}
is called a

-regular point for the operator

if
\mathfrak{M}_{\xi}
is a closed subspace of
\mathfrak{X}
and the following direct sum decomposition holds true:

\mathfrak{X}=\mathfrak{M}_{\xi}\dotplus Y.

(7.0.3)

It is known that every
Y
-regular point is a point of regular type of the operator
E
and the collection of all
Y
-regular points forms a open subset of
\mathbb{C}
. We shall consider those non empty open subsets
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
which are symmetric about the real axis and consists of
Y
-regular points of
E
. If
\Omega=\mathbb{C}
, then

is called an entire operator or
Y
-entire operator.

Remark 7.2
.

Given a simple, closed, densely defined, symmetric operator
E
with infinite deficiency indices, and assuming the existence of a point of regular type (denoted by
a
) on the real line; we can always establish the existence of the following:

•

A fixed infinite dimensional closed subspace
Y
of
\mathfrak{X}
; and

•

A non empty open subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
, which is symmetric about the real axis and consists of
Y
-regular points of
E
.

It is known that
\mathfrak{M}_{\xi}
is a closed subspace of
\mathfrak{X}
for every
\xi\in\mathbb{C}_{+}\cup\mathbb{C}_{-}
.
Consider the shtraus extension
\tilde{E_{a}}
of
E
given by

\linebreak\mathcal{D}(\tilde{E_{a}})=\mathcal{D}(E)\dotplus\mathfrak{M}_{a}^{\perp},\hskip 2.84544pt\tilde{E_{a}}(f_{E}+\phi_{a})=Ef_{E}+a\phi_{a},

(7.0.4)

where,
f_{E}\in\mathcal{D}(E),\phi_{a}\in\mathfrak{M}_{a}^{\perp}
.
This extension
\tilde{E_{a}}
yields a self-adjoint operator. For more details, we refer to
[
46
]
,
[
3
]
. Thus, for any
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}\cup\mathbb{C_{-}}
and
f\in\mathfrak{X},
there exists a unique
f_{E}\in\mathcal{D}(E)
and
\phi_{a}\in\mathfrak{M}_{a}^{\perp}
such that

f=(\tilde{E_{a}}-zI)(f_{E}+\phi_{a})=(E-zI)f_{E}+(a-z)\phi_{a}.

(7.0.5)

We obtain the following direct sum decomposition:

\mathfrak{X}=\mathfrak{M}_{z}\dotplus\mathfrak{M}_{a}^{\perp}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{for all}\hskip 2.84544ptz\in\mathbb{C}_{+}\cup\mathbb{C_{-}},

(7.0.6)

with the intersection property:

\mathfrak{M}_{z}\cap\mathfrak{M}_{a}^{\perp}=\{0\}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{for all}\hskip 2.84544ptz\in\mathbb{C}_{+}\cup\mathbb{C_{-}}.

(7.0.7)

Clearly,
\mathfrak{M}_{a}^{\perp}
is a closed subspace. Since,
a
is a point of regular type of operator
E
, there exists an interval
(a-\epsilon,a+\epsilon)
consisting of points of regular type of
E
. Since
E
is symmetric, as a result,
\mathbb{C}_{+}\cup\mathbb{C_{-}}\cup(a-\epsilon,a+\epsilon)
becomes a connected subset of the field of regularity of
E
. Moreover,
E
is an operator with infinite deficiency indices and it follows from
[
1
, Section 78]
,
\mathfrak{M}_{a}^{\perp}
is infinite dimensional. Therefore, the required infinite dimensional closed subspace
Y
can be choosen to be
\mathfrak{M}_{a}^{\perp}
. Furthermore, from (
7.0.6
),

\Omega=\mathbb{C}_{+}\cup\mathbb{C_{-}}

(7.0.8)

consists of
Y
-regular points of
E
.

Now, we aim to construct three different RKHS which shall be denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{1}
,

and

from a simple, closed, densely defined, symmetric operator

with infinite deficiency indices on the Hilbert space

. We shall observe that all the three spaces shall be a

By

)
, for every

(depending on

(7.0.9)

The operator

is a bounded linear operator for all
\xi\in\Omega
. Fixing

, we consider a map from
\Omega
to

\end{bmatrix},

(7.0.10)

where

valued functions by

\begin{bmatrix}f_{Y}\\
\tilde{f_{Y}}\\
\end{bmatrix}

\begin{bmatrix}f_{Y}\\
\tilde{f_{Y}}\\
\end{bmatrix}(\xi)=\begin{bmatrix}P_{Y}(\xi)f\\
\frac{1}{\pi i}\tau_{Y}(\xi)f\\
\end{bmatrix}.

(7.0.11)

Define
\mathcal{H}_{1}
as follows:

\mathcal{H}_{1}=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}f_{Y}\\
\tilde{f_{Y}}\\
\end{bmatrix}:f\in\mathfrak{X}\right\}.

The space
\mathcal{H}_{1}
consists of vector valued analytic functions defined on
\Omega
. Since

is simple, the map
\Psi:\mathfrak{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{1}

f\mapsto\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}f_{Y}\\
\tilde{f_{Y}}\\
\end{bmatrix}

\left\langle\begin{bmatrix}f_{Y}\\
\tilde{f_{Y}}\\
\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}g_{Y}\\
\tilde{g_{Y}}\\
\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}=2\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathfrak{X}}.

(7.0.13)

given by

. Hence,
\mathcal{H}_{1}
is a Hilbert space. Next, we demonstrate that

\|P_{Y}(\xi)f\|\leq\|P_{Y}(\xi)\|\|f\|_{\mathfrak{X}}.

(7.0.14)

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\|\frac{1}{\pi i}\tau_{Y}(\xi)f\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\|(\tilde{E}-\xi)^{-1}(f-P_{Y}(\xi)f)\|

(7.0.15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\|(\tilde{E}-\xi)^{-1}\|\|\left(I-P_{Y}(\xi)\right)f\|

(7.0.17)

Here,
\tilde{E}
represents a self adjoint extension of

\displaystyle\biggl\|\begin{bmatrix}f_{Y}\\
\tilde{f_{Y}}\\
\end{bmatrix}(\xi)\biggl\|

\displaystyle=

\frac{1}{\pi i}\tau_{Y}(\xi)f\\
\end{bmatrix}\biggl\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{1}\|f\|_{\mathfrak{X}}

(7.0.19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{2}\biggl\|\begin{bmatrix}f_{Y}\\
\tilde{f_{Y}}\\
\end{bmatrix}\biggl\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}},

(7.0.20)

U(\omega)=I-L^{\prime}
, we get:

K_{\omega}(\lambda)=\frac{J-(I-L)J(I-L^{\prime})^{*}}{-2\pi i(\lambda-\bar{\omega})}.

Expanding this expression:

K_{\omega}(\lambda)=\frac{JL^{\prime*}+LJ-LJL^{\prime*}}{-2\pi i(\lambda-\bar{\omega})}.

Since the sum and product of compact operators are compact, it follows that
K_{\omega}(\lambda)
is compact.
∎

Consider the following equalities:

\displaystyle(\Psi(Ef)(\xi))

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi i}\langle R_{\xi}\mathfrak{P}f,l\rangle

\displaystyle=

it is clear that

of Theorem

K_{\alpha}(\alpha)
is invertible. Similarly, it can be shown that
K_{\bar{\alpha}}(\bar{\alpha})
is also invertible.

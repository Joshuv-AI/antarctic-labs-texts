Esmée Theewis

Abstract
. We prove the small-noise large deviation principle (LDP) for stochastic evolution equations in an L 2 L^{2} -setting. As the coefficients are allowed to be non-coercive, our framework encompasses a much broader scope than variational settings. To replace coercivity, we require only well-posedness of the stochastic evolution equation and two concrete, verifiable a priori estimates. Furthermore, we accommodate drift nonlinearities satisfying a modified criticality condition, and we allow for vanishing drift perturbations. The latter permits the inclusion of Itô–Stratonovich correction terms, enabling the treatment of both noise interpretations. In another paper, our results have been applied to the 3D primitive equations with full transport noise. In the current paper, we give an application to a reaction-diffusion system which lacks coercivity, further demonstrating the versatility of the framework. Finally, we show that even in the coercive case, we obtain new LDP results for equations with critical nonlinearities that rely on our modified criticality condition, including the stochastic 2D Allen–Cahn equation in the weak setting.

1.
Introduction

We consider an abstract stochastic evolution equation with small
\varepsilon>0
:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\,\mathrm{d}Y^{\varepsilon}+A(t,Y^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}t=\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,B(t,Y^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}W,\\
&Y^{\varepsilon}(0)=x,\end{aligned}\right.

where
W
is a
U
-cylindrical Brownian motion.
Over the last two decades, large deviations have been studied intensively for SPDEs in the variational setting
[
17
,
12
,
16
,
19
,
24
]
, using the weak convergence approach developed in
[
10
]
. So far, these works have pushed the boundary in reducing the assumptions on the pair of coefficients
(A,B)
in (
1.1
). In the most recent works, the focus has been on weakening monotonicity assumptions
[
19
,
24
]
, on relaxing growth assumptions for
B

[
19
,
24
]
(e.g. allowing for gradient noise), and on handling non-compact embeddings in the underlying Gelfand triple
[
16
,
24
]
(allowing for unbounded spatial domains in applications).

A common assumption in the works mentioned above – and one that lies at the core of the ‘variational setting’ – is
coercivity of
(A,B)
. If
(V,H,V^{*})
is the Gelfand triple in the variational setting and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the duality pairing between
V^{*}
and
V
, then
the coercivity condition is typically formulated as:
For all
T>0
, there exist
\theta,M>0
and
\phi\in L^{2}(0,T)
such that

(1.2)

\langle A(t,v),v\rangle-\tfrac{1}{2}\|B(t,v)\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2}(U,H)}^{2}\geq\theta\|v\|_{V}^{2}-M\|v\|_{H}^{2}-|\phi(t)|^{2},\qquad v\in V,\,t\in[0,T].

The coercivity condition yields a priori
L^{2}(\Omega;C(0,T;H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V))
-estimates for solutions to (
1.1
) (see e.g.
[
7
,
18
]
), which can be used to prove global well-posedness for (
1.1
).

However, there exist many SPDEs for which coercivity fails, but that can still be formulated in a similar setting.
In this paper, we address a new type of generalization: we treat the case of
non-coercive

(A,B)
.
Concretely, we use the solution notion from the variational setting, i.e. we assume that there is a Gelfand triple
(V,H,V^{*})
, we take the initial datum
x
from
H
, and consider solutions
Y^{\varepsilon}
belonging to
C([0,T];H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V)
a.s.
Due to the latter, we will call this an ‘
L^{2}
-setting’.
Prominent classes of non-coercive SPDEs tractable in an
L^{2}
-setting include reaction-diffusion systems and
3D fluid models.
We refer to Example
2.10
for a further discussion including references.
Since coercivity fails in these examples, well-posedness of (
1.1
) needs to be proved in alternative ways, and generally,
L^{2}(\Omega;C(0,T;H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V))
-estimates are unavailable.
For instance, it may happen that energy bounds merely hold in probability:

(1.3)

\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\|Y^{\varepsilon}\|_{C(0,T;H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V)}>\gamma)=0.

This is no problem for applying the results in this paper. In fact, in our main result, Theorem
2.9
, we prove that the following conditions are already sufficient for the LDP:

i.

a structural assumption on
(A,B)
(see Assumption
2.1
)

ii.

well-posedness of (
1.1
)

iii.

a deterministic a priori estimate for the skeleton equation associated to (
1.1
)

iv.

uniform boundedness
in probability
for a family of SPDE solutions associated to (
1.1
)

We emphasize that in
i.
, we do not require monotonicity, coercivity, nor a compact embedding in the Gelfand triple.
The advantage of the criteria above is that one merely has to establish PDE estimates and probabilistic estimates
specific
to the SPDE of interest. This is significantly easier than proving an LDP via the weak convergence approach from scratch, which would require proving difficult continuity properties of the solution maps related to the equations mentioned in
iii.
and
iv.
.
Furthermore, we provide a proof of well-posedness for the skeleton equation mentioned in
iii.
, which is non-trivial when the SPDE itself admits only weak energy bounds of the form (
1.3
).

Conceptually, the conditions
i.
–
iv.
also clarify which steps in a weak convergence approach proof are specific for the equation, and which steps can be done at a highly abstract level.

In our proof, we extend the LDP result from
[
24
]
, which was based on the weak convergence approach. Apart from handling non-coercive coefficients, we incorporate two further extensions:

•

more flexible growth conditions on the nonlinearities, see Assumption
2.1
and Remark
2.2

•

an additional
\varepsilon
-dependent term in the drift, in particular suited for the treatment of Stratonovich noise, see (
5.1
) and Theorem
5.3

These extensions enable us to cover notable new examples where coercivity is absent.
We present an LDP for the stochastic 2D Brusselator model with transport noise in Section
6
, which involves a critical nonlinearity. Moreover, the framework developed here served as the basis for
[
2
]
, where the LDP for the stochastic 3D primitive equations with Stratonovich (or Itô) transport noise is established. Further possible applications are discussed in Example
2.10
.

Finally, we also specialize our results to the coercive case. In fact, if
(A,B)
satisfies the coercivity (
1.2
), then
ii.
–
iv.
become redundant, thus (
1.2
) and
i.
alone are sufficient for the LDP, see Corollaries
2.12
,
5.5
and
5.6
. This then also leads to the LDP for several models with borderline nonlinearities that were not covered yet in the available variational LDP literature. Examples are the 2D Allen–Cahn equation and the stochastic 2D Burger’s equation in the analytically weak setting, with gradient noise (see Example
2.13
). Moreover, we can extend applications of the LDP in
[
24
]
from Itô to Stratonovich noise (see Remark
5.8
).

Main results

Throughout this paper, we assume that
(V,H,V^{*})
is a Gelfand triple of real separable Hilbert spaces and
U
is a real separable Hilbert space. We let
\mathscr{L}_{2}(U,H)
denote the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators from

to

|\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!|_{H}\coloneqq\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2}(U,H)}

for

. We write
\|\cdot\|_{\theta}\coloneqq\|\cdot\|_{V_{\theta}}
and will be using the following interpolation estimate:

(2.1)

\|v\|_{\theta}\leq\|v\|_{V^{*}}^{1-\theta}\|v\|_{V}^{\theta},\qquad\qquad v\in V.

In this section we explain our framework and assumptions, and state our main LDP results: Theorems
2.9
for (possibly) non-coercive equations and Corollary
2.12
for coercive equations. Lists of applications can be found in Examples
2.10
and
2.13
.
Later, in Section
5
, we will state and prove analogous LDP results for perturbed versions of (
2.2
) with an additional
\varepsilon
-dependent drift term, which can for instance be used for treating Stratonovich integration.

2.1.
The non-coercive case

For
\varepsilon>0
, we consider the following stochastic evolution equation:

(2.2)

\begin{cases}&\,\mathrm{d}Y^{\varepsilon}(t)=-A(t,Y^{\varepsilon}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{\varepsilon}B(t,Y^{\varepsilon}(t))\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
&Y^{\varepsilon}(0)=x,\end{cases}

where
W
is a
U
-cylindrical Brownian motion and
x\in H
.
We will be working with drift and diffusion coefficients of the following form:

A(t,v)=A_{0}(t,v)v-\hat{F}(t,v)-\hat{f}\,\text{ and }\,B(t,v)=B_{0}(t,v)v+G(t,v)+g.

Here,
A
and
B
are quasilinear or semilinear mappings,
\hat{F}
and
G
are nonlinear terms, and
\hat{f}
and
g
are inhomogeneities.
The difference between
A
,
B
defined above and
A
,
B
from
[
24
, Ass. 2.2]
are our nonlinearity
\hat{F}
and inhomogeneity
\hat{f}
in
A
, which generalize the terms
F
and
f
in
[
24
, Ass. 2.2]
(see Remark
2.2
below). The
\hat{F}
-term satisfies modified, more flexible growth and Lipschitz conditions compared to

, which can be used to include larger classes of (critical) nonlinearities. Examples of such cases will be given in Example
2.13
.

Let us start introducing the assumptions that we use for
A
and
B
defined by (
2.3
). As a basis, we work with Assumption
2.1
below, which ensures local well-posedness for (
2.2
), as we argue in Remark
2.4
. For global well-posedness and an LDP, one usually also requires coercivity (
1.2
) for the full pair
(A,B)
(see
[
24
, (2.7), Th. 2.6]
), which we do
not
require below.
Later in this section, we introduce new and much more general conditions (Assumption
2.7
) to replace the coercivity condition.

Assumption 2.1

(Local well-posedness conditions – extended critical variational setting)
.

A
and
B
are defined by

(2.3)

A(t,v)=A_{0}(t,v)v-\hat{F}(t,v)-\hat{f},\quad B(t,v)=B_{0}(t,v)v+G(t,v)+g,\qquad t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},v\in V,

and the following holds:

(1)

A_{0}\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times H\to\mathscr{L}
,
B_{0}\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times H\to\mathscr{L}(V,\mathscr{L}_{2}(U,H)))
and
G\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times V\to\mathscr{L}_{2}(U,H)

.

(2)

For all

, there exist

such that for all

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\langle A_{0}(t,u)v,v\rangle-\tfrac{1}{2}|\!|\!|B_{0}(t,u)v|\!|\!|_{H}^{2}\geq\theta_{n,T}\|v\|_{V}^{2}-M_{n,T}\|v\|_{H}^{2}.\qquad

(coercivity of the linear part)

(3)

There exist an
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\rho_{i}\geq 0
,
\beta_{i}\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
for

(2.4)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad(1+\rho_{i})(2\beta_{i}-1)\leq 1,

((sub)criticality)

and for all

there exists a constant
C_{n,T}
such that for all

and

\displaystyle\|A_{0}(t,u)w\|_{V^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,T}\|u-v\|_{H}\|w\|_{V},

\displaystyle|\!|\!|B_{0}(t,u)w|\!|\!|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,T}\|w\|_{V},

\displaystyle|\!|\!|B_{0}(t,u)w-B_{0}(t,v)w|\!|\!|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,T}\|u-v\|_{H}\|w\|_{V},

\displaystyle|\!|\!|G(t,u)|\!|\!|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,T}\textstyle{\sum_{i=1}^{m}}(1+\|u\|_{\beta_{i}}^{\rho_{i}+1}),

\displaystyle|\!|\!|G(t,u)-G(t,v)|\!|\!|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,T}\textstyle{\sum_{i=1}^{m}}(1+\|u\|_{\beta_{i}}^{\rho_{i}}+\|v\|_{\beta_{i}}^{\rho_{i}})\|u-v\|_{\beta_{i}}.

(4)

\hat{F}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\hat{F}_{i}
with
\hat{F}_{i}\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times V\to V_{\alpha_{i}}
Borel measurable and
\alpha_{i}\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
,

\hat{\rho}_{i}>0
,
\hat{\beta}_{i}\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad(1+\hat{\rho}_{i})(2\hat{\beta}_{i}-1)\leq 1+2\alpha_{i},

(
\alpha
-(sub)criticality)

and for all
n,T>0
,
t\in[0,T]
and
u,v\in V
with
\|u\|_{H},\|v\|_{H}\leq n
,

\displaystyle\|\hat{F}_{i}(t,u)\|_{\alpha_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,T}(1+\|u\|_{\hat{\beta}_{i}}^{\hat{\rho}_{i}+1}),

\displaystyle\|\hat{F}_{i}(t,u)-\hat{F}_{i}(t,v)\|_{\alpha_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,T}(1+\|u\|_{\hat{\beta}_{i}}^{\rho_{i}}+\|v\|_{\hat{\beta}_{i}}^{\hat{\rho}_{i}})\|u-v\|_{\hat{\beta}_{i}}.

(5)

\hat{f}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\hat{f}_{i}

An LDP is abstractly defined as follows.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Polish space, let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space and let
(Y^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})}
be a collection of
\mathcal{E}
-valued random variables on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
. Let
I\colon\mathcal{E}\to[0,\infty]
be a function. Then
(Y^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})}

satisfies the large deviation principle (LDP) on
\mathcal{E}
with rate function
I\colon\mathcal{E}\to[0,\infty]
if

(i)

I
has compact sublevel sets,

(ii)

for all open
E\subset\mathcal{E}
:
\liminf_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon\log\mathbb{P}(Y^{\varepsilon}\in E)\geq-\inf_{z\in E}I(z)
,

(iii)

for all closed
E\subset\mathcal{E}
:
\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon\log\mathbb{P}(Y^{\varepsilon}\in E)\leq-\inf_{z\in E}I(z)
.

In our setting, we will use the above definition with for
\mathcal{E}
the
maximal regularity space

(2.6)

\mathrm{MR}(0,T)\coloneqq C([0,T];H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V),\quad\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{MR}(0,T)}\coloneqq\|\cdot\|_{C([0,T];H)}+\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V)}

for
T>0
, which is a Banach space. From now on, the space
\mathrm{MR}(0,T)
is always defined as above.

Our first main result, Theorem
2.9
, provides sufficient conditions under which the LDP holds for solutions
(Y^{\varepsilon})
to (
2.2
). To establish this result, we need to study two equations related to (
2.2
).
The skeleton equation is the deterministic evolution equation given by

(2.7)

\begin{cases}&\,\mathrm{d}u^{\psi}(t)=-A(t,u^{\psi}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t+B(t,u^{\psi}(t))\psi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,\quad t\in[0,T],\\
&u^{\psi}(0)=x,\end{cases}

for given
\psi\in L^{2}(0,T;U)
and
x\in H
.
Moreover, we consider the following stochastic evolution equation:

(2.8)

\begin{cases}&\,\mathrm{d}X^{\varepsilon}(t)=-A(t,X^{\varepsilon}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t+B(t,X^{\varepsilon}(t))\Psi^{\varepsilon}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{\varepsilon}B(t,X^{\varepsilon}(t))\,\mathrm{d}W,\quad t\in[0,T],\\
&X^{\varepsilon}(0)=x,\end{cases}

where
\Psi^{\varepsilon}
is a predictable stochastic process taking a.s. values in
L^{2}(0,T;U)
.

The following solution notion will be used for equations (
2.2
), (
2.7
) and (
2.8
).

Definition 2.6
.

Let
A\colon[0,T]\times V\to V^{*}
,
B\colon[0,T]\times V\to\mathscr{L}_{2}(U,H)
and let
x\in H
. Let

be a

-cylindrical Brownian motion on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
. Consider

(2.9)

\begin{cases}&\,\mathrm{d}u(t)=-A(t,u(t))\,\mathrm{d}t+B(t,u(t))\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
&u(0)=x,\end{cases}

We say that a strongly progressively measurable process

is a

to (

) if a.s. (recall (

)):

and a.s.

(2.10)

\displaystyle u(t)=x-\int_{0}^{t}A(s,u(s))\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}B(s,u(s))\,\mathrm{d}W(s)\>\text{ in }V^{*}\text{ for all }t\in[0,T].

If
B=0
, we write
u^{\prime}(t)=-A(t,u(t))
instead of
\,\mathrm{d}u(t)=-A(t,u(t))\,\mathrm{d}t
in (

) and we call

a strong solution if

and (

) holds.

3.
The skeleton equation with flexible nonlinearities

In this section we prove local well-posedness of the skeleton equation (

) and a blow-up criterion. We extend the theory of
[

, §3]
under Assumption

, which includes more general nonlinearities and inhomogeneities (see Remark

).

3.1.
Linearized problem

Throughout this subsection, we assume that
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\alpha_{i}\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and study a linearization of the skeleton equation. We discard the nonlinearities
\hat{F}
and
G
in the coefficients
A
and
B
(see (
2.3
)), and we consider for fixed
w\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H)
:

(3.1)

\begin{cases}&u^{\prime}(t)+A_{0}(t,w(t))u(t)-B_{0}(t,w(t))u(t)\psi(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bar{f}_{i}(t)+\bar{g}(t)\psi(t),\\
&u(0)=x.\end{cases}

Here, we assume that
A_{0}
and
B_{0}
are as in Assumption
2.1
, and
\bar{f}_{i}\in L^{2/(1+2\alpha_{i})}(0,T;V_{\alpha_{i}})
and
\bar{g}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathscr{L}_{2}(U,H))
.
The goal is to prove well-posedness and energy estimates for (
3.1
).
Later, it will be used to treat the terms
\hat{F}(u)
and
\hat{f}
.

We define the following sum space:

S_{T}=L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})+L^{1}(0,T;H),

where we note that the spaces appearing in
S_{T}
form a Banach interpolation couple with ambient space
L^{1}(0,T;V^{*})
. Then,
S_{T}
is a Banach space
[
13
, Prop. C.1.3]
and
S_{T}\hookrightarrow L^{1}(0,T;V^{*})
.

In
[
24
, §3.1]
, inhomogeneities in

were studied.
Now, we note that the Stein–Weiss theorem for complex interpolation
[
14
, Th. 14.3.1]
yields for all
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
:

(3.2)

L^{2/(1+2\alpha)}(0,T;V_{\alpha})=[L^{2}(0,T;V^{*}),L^{1}(0,T;H)]_{2\alpha}\hookrightarrow S_{T},

where the first identification is isometric, and the embedding into
S_{T}
is contractive, by exactness of the complex interpolation method.
In particular, (
3.2
) and the theory in
[
24
, §3.1]
allow us to consider sums of inhomogeneities
\bar{f}_{i}\in L^{2/(1+2\alpha_{i})}(0,T;V_{\alpha_{i}})\subset S_{T}
.

The following result was proved in
[
24
, Th. 3.4]
. See (
2.6
) for the definition of
\mathrm{MR}(0,T)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\bar{A}\colon[0,T]\to\mathscr{L}(V,V^{*})
and suppose that
for all
u\in\mathrm{MR}(0,T)
:
\bar{A}(\cdot)u(\cdot)\in S_{T}
and
\|\bar{A}(\cdot)u(\cdot)\|_{S_{T}}\leq\alpha\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}(0,T)}

for some constant
\alpha>0
independent of
u
.
Suppose that for some
\theta>0
and
M\in L^{1}(0,T)
:
\langle\bar{A}(t)v,v\rangle\geq\theta\|v\|_{V}^{2}-M(t)\|v\|_{H}^{2}
for all

.

Then for any
h\in S_{T}
, there exists a unique strong solution
u\in\mathrm{MR}(0,T)
to

\begin{cases}u^{\prime}(t)+\bar{A}(t)u(t)=h(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
u(0)=x\end{cases}

and the following estimate holds:

\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}(0,T)}\leq C_{\theta}\exp(2\|M\|_{L^{1}(0,T)})\big(\|h\|_{S_{T}}+\|x\|_{H}\big).

A consequence of Theorem
3.1
is the following result for (

), which extends
[

, Cor. 3.5]
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
A_{0}
and

satisfy the conditions concerning

in Assumption

and let

Let

Then, if

\bar{g}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathscr{L}_{2}(U,H))

to

(3.3)

\begin{cases}&u^{\prime}(t)+A_{0}(t,w(t))u(t)-B_{0}(t,w(t))u(t)\psi(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bar{f}_{i}(t)+\bar{g}(t)\psi(t),\\
&u(0)=x,\end{cases}

Moreover, for any
\tilde{T}\in[0,T]
there exists a constant

such that

(3.4)

\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}(0,\tilde{T})}\leq K_{\tilde{T}}\Big(\|x\|_{H}+\textstyle{\sum_{i=1}^{m}}\|\bar{f}_{i}\|_{L^{2/(1+2\alpha_{i})}(0,\tilde{T};V_{\alpha_{i}})}+\|\bar{g}\|_{L^{2}(0,\tilde{T};\mathscr{L}_{2}(U,H))}\Big),

and

is non-decreasing in

and depends further only on

,
\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)}
and
\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(0,\tilde{T};U)}
.

Proof.

By
[
24
, Cor. 3.5]
,

\bar{A}\colon[0,T]\to\mathscr{L}(V,V^{*})
defined by

. Moreover, thanks to (

The claim thus follows from Theorem

, the observation that

and by putting
K_{\tilde{T}}\coloneqq C_{\theta_{n,T}}\exp(2\|M_{n,T}+\frac{1}{2}\|\psi\|_{U}^{2}\|_{L^{1}(0,\tilde{T})})(1\vee\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(0,\tilde{T};U)})
as in the proof of
[

, Cor. 3.5]
.
∎

Lemma 3.4

for

. Note that
(i)

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

and

(ii)

with

6.1.
The stochastic 2D Brusselator

We study well-posedness and large deviations for a reaction-diffusion system, the stochastic 2D Brusselator. The Brusselator model was introduced in
[
20
,
21
]
to describe chemical autocatalytic reactions and the formation of spatial patterns.
Notably, this work was a fundamental component of the research that earned Prigogine the 1977 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.
A special case of the Brusselator model is the Gray–Scott model, see
[
5
, Ex. 4.12]
and the references therein. Thus our results also apply to the corresponding stochastic 2D Gray–Scott model.

Here, similar to
[
5
, §4.3]
, we add stochastic perturbations to the Brusselator model, which represent thermal fluctuations or small-scale turbulence.
We consider the following system on the two-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
:

(6.1)

\begin{cases}&\,\mathrm{d}u_{1}-\nabla\cdot(a_{1}\nabla u_{1})\,\mathrm{d}t=[-u_{1}u_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{1}u_{1}+\lambda_{2}u_{2}+\lambda_{0}]\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\hskip 150.79968pt+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\sum_{n\geq 1}[(b_{n,1}\cdot\nabla)u_{1}+g_{n,1}(\cdot,u)]\,\mathrm{d}w^{n},\\
&\,\mathrm{d}u_{2}-\nabla\cdot(a_{2}\nabla u_{2})\,\mathrm{d}t=[u_{1}u_{2}^{2}+\mu_{1}u_{1}+\mu_{2}u_{2}+\mu_{0}]\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\hskip 150.79968pt+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\sum_{n\geq 1}[(b_{n,2}\cdot\nabla)u_{2}+g_{n,2}(\cdot,u)]\,\mathrm{d}w^{n},\\
&u(0)=(u_{0,1},u_{0,2}),\end{cases}

where
(w^{n})
is a sequence of independent real-valued standard Brownian motions. Fixing an orthonormal basis for
\ell^{2}
, we can identify
(w^{n})
with an
\ell^{2}
-cylindrical Brownian motion
W=(w^{n})
.

For the coefficients, we make the following assumption.

Assumption 6.1
.

(1)

For

(2)

For

:
\nu_{i}>0
and for all

,

\sum_{j,k=1}^{2}\Big(a_{i}^{j,k}(t,x)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\geq 1}b_{n,i}^{j}(t,x)b_{n,i}^{k}(t,x)\Big)\xi_{j}\xi_{k}\geq\nu_{i}|\xi|^{2}.

(3)

For
i=1,2
:
g_{i}=(g_{n,i})_{n\geq 1}\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\ell^{2}
is Borel measurable,

and for all

(4)

For
i=0,1,2

(5)

There exist

such that for

, the following holds on

where for some

(6.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&N_{1}(y)\coloneqq M[1+|y_{1}|^{2}]+(1-\epsilon)|y_{1}|^{2}|y_{2}|^{2},\\

\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathbb{P}\big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u_{2}^{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}+\textstyle{\int_{0}^{T}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\sigma_{n}^{\prime}]}\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s>\gamma\big)=0.

Finally, combining the latter with (

Lemma 6.7

(Skeleton equation bound)
.

Let
u_{0}=(u_{0,1},u_{0,2})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, let
T>0
and let

with

Proof.

By the chain rule
[
24
, (A.2)]
and Assumption
2.1
(2)
, we have for

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|u_{i}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0,i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\langle A_{i}(s,u(s)),u_{i}(s)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\langle B_{i}(s,u(s))\psi(s),u_{i}(s)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0,i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\langle A_{i}(s,u(s)),u_{i}(s)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|B_{i}(s,u(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}};{\mathbb{R}}^{2}))}^{2}+\|\psi(s)\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\|u_{i}(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0,i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}-\nu_{i}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\breve{F}_{i}(u(s)),u_{i}(s)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\|F_{i}(s,u(s))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\|u_{i}(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|G_{i}(s,u(s))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}};{\mathbb{R}}^{2}))}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{n\geq 1}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}g_{n,i}(s,x,u(s,x))\big((b_{n,i}(s,x)\cdot\nabla)u_{i}(s,x)\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s

Now, except for the last term, pointwise estimates were already provided in the proof of Lemma
6.6
(apply the estimates with
\sigma_{n}=\sigma_{n}^{\prime}=T
). Reusing these estimates, we readily find

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\|u_{1}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\|u_{0,1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}-\delta\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s-\epsilon\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{1}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}(2{R}+M+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\psi\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2})\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+(M+{R})T,

and (again fixing

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}(C_{{R},M,\kappa}+C_{\kappa,M}\|u_{1}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\psi\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2})\|u_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

Therefore, Grönwall’s inequality first gives

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}

\displaystyle\|u_{1}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{1}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{\delta,\epsilon}\big(\tfrac{1}{2}\|u_{0,1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}+(M+{R})T\big)\exp\big[2(2{R}+M)T+\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\ell^{2})}^{2}\big]

with

. Now, using
both
of the last two estimates, we obtain also

\displaystyle\|u_{2}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}

Thus,

∎

(Stochastic control problem bound)

Remark 6.13
.

Although not needed for the LDP, we emphasize that
Assumption
6.11
ensures that (
6.24
) is well-posed even for every
\varepsilon\in{\mathbb{R}}
. In fact, the Itô–Stratonovich correction term is helpful: coercivity of
(A_{0},0)
yields coercivity of
(A_{0}-\varepsilon\tilde{A},\sqrt{\varepsilon}B_{0})
and
(A-\varepsilon\tilde{A},\sqrt{\varepsilon}B)
for every
\varepsilon\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and global well-posedness follows from
[
4
, Th. 7.10]
. See also
[
6
, (A.1), Ass. A.1, Prop. A.5]
.

For the claimed coercivity, observe that for all
u\in\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{O})
:

-\langle\varepsilon\tilde{A}u,u\rangle=\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\textstyle{\sum_{n\geq 1}}\|\mathbb{P}[(b_{n}\cdot\nabla)u]\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}B_{0}u\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2}(\ell^{2};\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O}))}^{2}.

Thus we have a cancellation:

(6.25)

\langle(A_{0}-\varepsilon\tilde{A})u,u\rangle-\tfrac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}B_{0}u\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2}(\ell^{2};\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O}))}^{2}=\langle A_{0}u,u\rangle={\nu}\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}_{0}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}.

Moreover, for
\hat{F}
we have

\langle\hat{F}(u),u\rangle=-(u\otimes u,\mathrm{div}\,u)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O};{\mathbb{R}}^{2\times 2})}=0
for all
u\in\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{O})
(since
u
is divergence free and has zero trace, and
\mathbb{P}
is self-adjoint).
Adding this to (
6.25
), we conclude that
(A-\eta(\varepsilon)\tilde{A},\sqrt{\varepsilon}B)
is coercive too.

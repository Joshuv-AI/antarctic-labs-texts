CellARC : Measuring Intelligence with Cellular Automata


Abstract
We introduce CellARC , a synthetic benchmark for abstraction and reasoning built from multicolor 1D cellular automata (CA). Each episode has five support pairs and one query serialized in ≤ 256 \leq 256 tokens, enabling rapid iteration with small models while exposing a controllable task space with explicit knobs for alphabet size k , radius r , rule family, Langton’s λ , coverage cov , and cell entropy H . We release 95k training episodes plus two 1k test splits (interpolation/extrapolation) and evaluate symbolic, recurrent, convolutional, transformer, recursive and llm baselines. CellARC decouples generalization from anthropomorphic priors, supports unlimited difficulty-controlled sampling, and enables reproducible studies of how quickly models infer new rules under tight budgets. Our strongest small-model baseline (a 10 10 M parameter vanilla transformer) outperforms recent recursive models (TRM, HRM), reaching 58.0 % / 32.4 % 58.0\%/32.4\% per-token accuracy on the interpolation/extrapolation splits, while a large closed model (GPT-5 High) attains 62.3 % / 48.1 % 62.3\%/48.1\% on subsets of 100 test tasks. An ensemble that chooses per episode between the Transformer and best symbolic baseline reaches 65.4%/35.5%, highlighting neuro-symbolic complementarity. Leaderboard: cellarc.mireklzicar.com .

Cellular Automata for Abstraction and Reasoning

A one-dimensional cellular automaton (CA) is specified by an alphabet of
k
states, a radius
r
, and a synchronous local rule
F:[\hyperlink{sym:k}{k}]^{2\hyperlink{sym:r}{r}+1}\!\to\![\hyperlink{sym:k}{k}]
applied in parallel to every cell
(
Hedlund1969
)
. The model traces back to von Neumann’s program on self-reproduction and computation in discrete media
(
v1966theory
)
. CA exhibit a broad spectrum of dynamical regimes—from ordered to chaotic—characterized in classical analyses and monographs
(
RevModPhys.55.601
;
wolfram2018new
;
doi:10.1142/4702
)
. We use rule families with well-defined local semantics (totalistic, outer/inner-totalistic, linear-mod-
k
)
(
Eppstein_2010
;
martin1984algebraic
)
and steer dynamics with Langton’s
λ
parameter to traverse order–chaos transitions
(
langton1990computation
)
.

Naturalistic relevance.

Despite their simplicity and abstraction, CA capture qualitative structure in a surprising range of phenomena: crystal growth and snowflake morphology; fracture patterns; fluid flow textures such as splashes, plumes, convection cells, and cloud bands; biological morphogenesis (reaction–diffusion); growth forms in plants and animals; and stylized dynamics in socio-economic systems and speculative fundamental physics models
(
wolfram2018new
, Chs. 7–9, 10–11, 12, 14)
. Turing’s reaction–diffusion theory remains a canonical continuum account for pattern formation and provides complementary mathematical intuition
(
turing1990chemical
)
. In this sense, cellular automata reveal a hidden grammar of nature—an abstract language through which diverse systems express their underlying order.
Figure 3
illustrates this correspondence on natural mollusc pigmentation patterns that admit CA-style explanations.

Why CA are a good substrate for benchmarking generalization.

The following properties complement the design goals in §
1
without relying on human-crafted priors:

1.

Calibrated complexity.
Varying
k
,
r
, rule family, and
λ
produces smooth difficulty gradations—from additive dynamics to chaos—while empirical cell entropy
H
provides a complementary, data-driven summary of regime complexity
(
RevModPhys.55.601
;
Eppstein_2010
;
martin1984algebraic
;
langton1990computation
;
doi:10.1142/4702
)
.

2.

Finite local hypothesis class.
Each update depends only on a length-
2\hyperlink{sym:r}{r}{+}1
window, yielding a compact, structured search space. This supports both symbolic solvers (e.g., de Bruijn analyses of window transitions) and neural models that can target the same locality bias
(
de1948combinatorial
;
sutner1991bruijn
)
.

3.

Exact measurability.
Because the local rule is formal, we can
exactly
account for which windows appear in supervision (via de Bruijn constructions) and report
cov
/
λ
/
H
, replacing opaque ”difficulty” labels with reproducible diagnostics
(
de1948combinatorial
;
sutner1991bruijn
)
.

4.

Unlimited instances under short contexts.
CA histories admit unlimited episodes while keeping supervision compact; by constraining
\hyperlink{sym:k}{k}\!\in\!\{2,\ldots,6\}
and
\hyperlink{sym:r}{r}\!\in\!\{1,2,3\}
, we span low- to high-entropy rules in token budgets suitable for efficiency-centric evaluation.

5.

Mathematical symmetry.
CA are translation-equivariant and time-homogeneous—the same local rule applies at every site and step
(
Hedlund1969
)
. Many families admit additional symmetries (left–right reflection, state relabeling, additive structure for linear mod-
k
rules) that induce equivalence classes over rules and traces, enabling targeted tests of systematic generalization
(
martin1984algebraic
;
Eppstein_2010
;
sutner1991bruijn
)
.

We leverage these properties in
CellARC
: episodes are sampled across families and
λ
regimes; difficulty is controlled through window coverage
cov
and palette size
k
; and evaluation focuses on how quickly a model infers the hidden local rule under a fixed budget, not merely whether it eventually does
(
DBLP:journals/corr/abs-1911-01547
)
.

3.1
Dataset Generation Pipeline

We follow the reproducible workflow documented in the dataset repository. The pipeline has four phases: (i) raw pool synthesis, (ii) filtering and metadata enrichment, (iii) coverage-aware resampling and split assignment, and (iv) packaging.

Raw pool synthesis.

We sample multicolor one-dimensional CA across alphabet sizes
\hyperlink{sym:k}{k}\in\{2,\dots,6\}
and radii
\hyperlink{sym:r}{r}\in\{1,2,3\}
. For each sampled rule we generate five support input–output pairs and one query, using a mixture of
cycle
,
unrolled
, and
hybrid
constructions. To span regimes from easy to challenging while discarding trivial cases, we draw a
raw support-span coverage
target uniformly in
[0.05,0.95]
and sample support spans accordingly over the de Bruijn cycle (fraction of cycle length covered by the union of support spans). This stage yields roughly
5.1\times 10^{5}
raw episodes. Pool diagnostics verify that the family mix and coverage histogram match the target before proceeding.

Filtering and enrichment.

We downsample the raw pool to about
110\text{k}
high-quality episodes while enforcing fingerprint-level deduplication, novelty of the query solution relative to supports, and a flattened supervision length of at most 256 tokens. Downsampling is balanced over a two-dimensional histogram of Langton’s
λ
and observed coverage. A first sanity check removes degenerate absorbing rules (e.g., simultaneously very low
λ
and entropy). The surviving episodes are then replayed at a fixed width and horizon to compute enriched descriptors: Langton’s
λ
, mean cell entropy
H
, mutual information, morphology statistics, and inline rule tables.

Coverage-aware resampling

Next, to avoid prevalence of unsolvable episodes, we rewrite training windows so that the five supports collectively cover the query with query-weighted coverage
\hyperlink{sym:cov}{\mathrm{cov}}\geq 0.5
in the released data, while avoiding leakage (the exact query sequence is never copied into supports). Episodes that cannot satisfy the constraint within a bounded sampling budget are dropped, leaving roughly
95\text{k}
–
100\text{k}
items. We then keep only the compliant episodes and partition them deterministically.

Splits and packaging.

First of all the
Test Extrapolation
split is populated with the most difficult CA:
lowest

cov
and
highest

λ
and
H
. By this construction, the released
Test Extrapolation
split consists entirely of chaotic episodes (100% in the chaotic Langton-
λ
bin). The remainder are shuffled with a fixed seed to carve out
Train
,
Validation
, and
Test Interpolation
subsets of similar distribution. Furthermore, 100 episodes from both test splits are sampled for evaluation of LLMs while saving on API costs. To verify a result (or in case of saturation suspicion) it is possible to generate more CAs of corresponding families and parameters using our GitHub repo.
Both JSONL and Parquet views are provided, and mirrors are uploaded on the Hugging Face Hub. For exact commands and parameters, we refer readers to the dataset repository.

Michal Hevessy, Tomáš Raunig

Abstract
. Motivated by three recent open questions in the study of linear dynamics, we study weighted shifts on sequence spaces. First, we provide an example of a weighted shift on a locally convex space whose topology is generated by a sequence of complete seminorms which is generalized hyperbolic, but does not have the shadowing property. Next, we characterise uniform topological expansivity on Fréchet spaces satisfying a number of very natural conditions. Finally, we study the periodic shadowing property on normed spaces leading to a condition formulated purely in terms of weights which we show is necessary for the periodic shadowing property on ℓ p \ell_{p} and equivalent on c 0 c_{0} .

One of the most popular and tangible operators, in both of the aforementioned settings, are weighted shifts. Recall that a
(Fréchet) sequence space over
\mathbb{Z}
is a linear subspace
X
of
\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{Z}}
(with
\mathbb{K}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}
) whose topology makes it a locally convex (Fréchet) space and the embedding
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is continuous. Provided that the canonical basic vectors
e_{n}=(\delta_{n,j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
(where
\delta_{n,j}
is the Kronecker delta) all belong to
X
, we say that they form a basis of
X
if for every
(x_{n})\in X
we have

(x_{n})=\lim_{m,n\to\infty}(\dots,0,x_{-m},x_{-m+1},\dots,x_{n-1},x_{n},0,\dots).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
w=(w_{n})\in\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{Z}}
. We define the
bilateral weighted forward (resp. backward) shift
to be the operator
F_{w}:\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{Z}}
(resp.
B_{w}:\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{Z}}
) defined by

F_{w}((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}})=(w_{n-1}x_{n-1})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\quad\text{resp.}\quad B_{w}((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}})=(w_{n+1}x_{n+1})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},\quad(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{Z}}.

If
X
is a sequence space over
\mathbb{Z}
and
w\in\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is chosen so that
F_{w}(X)\subset X
(resp.
B_{w}(X)\subset X
), then we can consider
F_{w}
(resp.
B_{w}
) to be an operator on
X
.

Let
w=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
,
F_{w}
and
B_{w}
be as in the definition above. When considered as operators on
\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, if the bilateral weighted shifts are invertible then the inverse of a forward shift is a backward shift and vice versa. To be precise,
F_{w}^{-1}=B_{v}
and
B_{w}^{-1}=F_{u}
where
v=(w^{-1}_{n-1})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
u=(w^{-1}_{n+1})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
. When considered as operators on a sequence space
X
, the closed graph theorem guarantees that the shift operators are automatically continuous. On
\ell_{p}
(
1\leq p<\infty
) and
c_{0}
being invertible for
B_{w}
and
F_{w}
is equivalent to
w
being bounded above and
\inf_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|w_{i}|>0
.

Dynamical properties of weighted shifts are a well studied subject for some years now see e.g
[

,
8
,
11
,

,

,

]
.

One of the fundamental properties of operators in linear dynamics is the shadowing property which will appear in multiple places throughout this paper, so we recall it and related definitions here.

Let
X
be a locally convex space over
\mathbb{K}
and
T
be a continuous linear operator. If
U
is a neighbourhood of
0
a
U
-pseudotrajectory of
T
is a finite or infinite sequence
(x_{j})_{i<j<k}
in
X
(
-\infty\leq i<k\leq\infty
) having at least two elements such that

Tx_{j}-x_{j+1}\in U\quad\text{for all }i<j<k-1.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
X
be a locally convex space over
\mathbb{K}
and
T
be a continuous linear operator.
We say that
T
has the
finite (resp. positive) shadowing property
if for every neighbourhood
V
of
0
in
X
, there is a neighbourhood
U
of
0
in
X
such that every finite
U
-pseudotrajectory
(x_{j})_{j=0}^{k}
, where
0<k<\infty
(resp. every
U
-psudotrajectory
(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
) of
T
is
V
-shadowed by the trajectory of some
x\in X
i.e

x_{j}-T^{j}x\in V\quad\text{for all }j\in\{0,\dots,k\}\ (\text{resp. for all }j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}).

Moreover, if
T
is invertible we define the shadowing property by replacing the set
\mathbb{N}_{0}
by
\mathbb{Z}
in the definition of positive shadowing property. We say that
T
has the
periodic shadowing property
if every periodic
U
-pseudotrajectory
(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
T
(pseudotrajectory is periodic if there is
p\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
we have
x_{k+p}=x_{k}
) is
V
-shadowed by some periodic point of
T
.

Sometimes, the term
U
-chain is used to refer to a finite
U
-pseudotrajectory. Also, when working on Banach spaces, the term
\delta
-pseudotrajectory where
\delta>0
is used as a shorthand for a
U
-pseudotrajectory with
U=B(0,\delta)
.

Remark 1.3
.

In the following we will sometimes abbreviate the shadowing property by PS and the periodic shadowing property by PSP. Also, if it will be clear from context, we will omit the name of the operator in the name of the pseudotrajectories.

Finally,
Section

covers the periodic shadowing property. Since this section builds upon previous results from the literature (namely
[

) which are only proved for the spaces
\ell_{p}
,

and

has a periodic point (or equivalently a dense set of periodic points) then the periodic shadowing and shadowing properties coincide. If it is not the case, we find a condition which is equivalent to the periodic shadowing property on
c_{0}
and we show that it is necessary on
\ell_{p}
. Since the formulations are quite technical, let us mention here the result for
c_{0}
:

Theorem

(
Theorem

4.12
)
.

Let
B_{w}
be a bilateral weighted backward shift on
X=c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})
given by a bounded sequence of weights
w=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
with
\inf_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|w_{n}\right|>0
. If
B_{w}
has nontrivial periodic points, then the periodic shadowing property is equivalent to the shadowing property. If not, the periodic shadowing property is equivalent to the condition:
For every
\varepsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
such that for all
k\leq l\leq m
one of the following holds

(a)

\left|\frac{\varepsilon}{w(l+1)\dots w(m)}-\delta\sum_{i=0}^{m-(l+1)}\frac{1}{w(m)\dots w(m-i)}\right|\geq\delta

(b)

\left|w(k)\dots w(l)\varepsilon-\delta\sum_{i=0}^{k-(l-1)}w(k)\dots w(k-i)\right|\geq\delta.

we will denote

the space of invertible linear operators on

2.
Generalized hyperbolicity does not imply shadowing

In this section, we answer
[
3
, Problem C]
in the negative. Recall the definition of generalized hyperbolic operators which was introduced for locally convex spaces in
[
3
]
:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a locally convex space over
\mathbb{K}
whose topology is induced by a directed family
(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I}
of seminorms and
T:X\to X
. We say that
T
is
generalized hyperbolic
if
X
can be written as a topological direct sum
X=M\oplus N
of two subspaces
M,N
with the following three properties:

(i)

T(M)\subset M
;

(ii)

T(N)\supset N
and
T|_{N}:N\to T(N)
is an isomorphism;

(iii)

for every
\alpha\in I
there are
\beta\in I
,
c>0
and
t\in(0,1)
such that

\forall y\in M,z\in N\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\colon\left\|T^{n}y\right\|_{\alpha}\leq ct^{n}\left\|y\right\|_{\beta}\text{ and }\left\|S^{n}z\right\|_{\alpha}\leq ct^{n}\left\|z\right\|_{\beta},

where
S=(T|_{N})^{-1}
.

Shortly after giving this definition, the authors proved the following result.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
3
, Theorem 6]
)
.

Let
X
be a locally convex space whose topology is generated by a directed family
(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I}
of complete seminorms. Then every generalized hyperbolic invertible
T:X\to X
satisfying
T(\operatorname{Ker}(\left\|\cdot\right\|)_{\alpha})=\operatorname{Ker}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\alpha})
for all
\alpha\in I
has the shadowing property.

In
[
3
, Problem C]
, they ask if it is possible to remove the assumption
T(\operatorname{Ker}(\left\|\cdot\right\|)_{\alpha})=\operatorname{Ker}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\alpha})
. The following example shows that the answer is
no
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
X=c_{00}(\mathbb{Z})
be equipped with the directed family
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{n},n\in\mathbb{N}
of complete seminorms defined by

\left\|x\right\|_{n}=\max_{\left|k\right|\leq n}\left|x(k)\right|,\quad x\in X,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Define
T\in GL(X)
to be the bilateral weighted backward shift given by weights
w(k)=2,k>0
and
w(k)=1/2,k\leq 0
, i.e.

T((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}})_{n}=\begin{cases}2x_{n+1},\quad n\geq 0\\
\frac{1}{2}x_{n+1},\quad n<0.\end{cases}

Then
T
is generalized hyperbolic, but does not posses the (positive) shadowing property.

Proof.

The maps
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{n},n\in\mathbb{N}
are clearly seminorms on
c_{00}
and they are easily seen to be complete: if
(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is Cauchy with respect to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then for
-n\leq i\leq n
surely
x_{k}(i)\to x(i)
for some
x(i)
. If we define
x(i)=0,\left|i\right|>n
, then
x_{k}\to x
in
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{n}
.

It is standard to check that
T\in GL(X)
and that it is generalized hyperbolic operator with the decomposition of
X
given by
M=\overline{\mathrm{span}}\{e_{n}:n\leq 0\}
and
N=\overline{\mathrm{span}}\{e_{n}:n>0\}
.

We need to find a neighbourhood
V
of
0
so that for all neighbourhoods
U
of
0
there is a
U
-chain which is not
V
-shadowed. In fact, we will show that
V
can be chosen as
V=\{x\in X:\left\|x\right\|_{n}<\varepsilon\}
for any
\varepsilon>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. So, choose any such
\varepsilon,n
and let a neighbourhood
U
of
0
be given. Then there are
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta>0
for which
\{x\in X:\left\|x\right\|_{m}<\delta\}\subset U
. Define
y\in c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})
by
y(i)=\varepsilon 2^{-\left|i\right|}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x_{j}\in c_{00}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
by

x_{j}(i)=\begin{cases}y(i),&m+1-j\leq i\leq m+1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then, directly from the definitions of
x_{j}
’s and
T
we see that for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
we have
x_{j+1}-Tx_{j}=\varepsilon 2^{-m-1}e_{m+1}\in U
, i.e.
(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a
U
-pseudotrajectory.

Assume that there is some
x\in c_{00}
which
V
-shadows
(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. Since
x\in c_{00}
, there is
k>m+1
such that
x(k)=0
. However, as
x
shadows
(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
,
x_{k}-T^{k}x\in V
and thus

\varepsilon>\left|x_{k}(0)-T^{k}x(0)\right|=\left|y(0)-2^{k}x(k)\right|=\varepsilon 2^{0}-0=\varepsilon

which is a contradiction. This shows that
T
does not have the positive shadowing property, but, of course, since
x_{0}\in U
, we may define
x_{j},j<0
simply by
x_{j}=0
to see that the full shadowing property also fails.
∎

We have two final remarks regarding the example and its proof.

Remark 2.4
.

The spirit of the example is to exploit the “ugliness” of the chosen space - in particular the fact that it is not sequentially complete. One may as well take
T
to be an operator on, and
(x_{j})
to be a subsequence of, the space
c_{0}
. In that case,
(x_{j})
would be shadowed exactly by the point
y
as defined in the proof. Hence one may equally correctly view the construction as taking a pseudotrajectory which is shadowed in a “nice” space and then restricting ourselves to a subspace which forgets this point.

Remark 2.5
.

In the proof, for simplicity’s sake we showed that
V
may be taken as any subbasic neighbourhood of
0
. One may in fact take
V
to be any nontrivial neighbourhood of
0
. We sketch how the proof would need to be modified: if
\{x\in X:\left\|x\right\|_{m}<\delta\}\subset U
as in the proof and
z\notin V
, use the fact that
z
is finitely supported to find
l\in\mathbb{N}
so that
T^{-l}z\in\mathrm{span}\{e_{i}:i>m+1\}
. The sequence would then be constructed by starting with
T^{-l}z
and then repeatedly applying these steps

(i)

\left|\operatorname{supp}z\right|
times creating the next element by simply applying
T
,

(ii)

adding
T^{-l}z
to the last element of the partially constructed sequence.

3.
Characterisation of uniform topological expansivity

The aim of this section is to give a (partial) answer to
[
3
, Problem E]
. The problem asks us to characterise the concept of uniform topological expansivity for weighted shifts on Fréchet sequence spaces. We recall the relevant definition introduced in
[
3
]
:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a locally convex space over
\mathbb{K}
whose topology is induced by a directed family
(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I}
of seminorms. We say that an operator
T\in GL(X)
is uniformly topologically expansive if for every
\alpha\in I
exists
\beta\in I
such that we can decompose the sphere
S_{\alpha}=\{x\in X:\left\|x\right\|_{\alpha}=1\}
into two sets
S_{\alpha}=A_{\alpha}\cup B_{\alpha}
, where

\displaystyle\left\|T^{n}x\right\|_{\beta}

\displaystyle\rightrightarrows\infty\ \text{uniformly on}\ A_{\alpha}\ \text{as}\ n\to\infty,

\displaystyle\left\|T^{-n}x\right\|_{\beta}

\displaystyle\rightrightarrows\infty\ \text{uniformly on}\ B_{\alpha}\ \text{as}\ n\to\infty.

In
[
3
]
the authors give the following characterisation of expansivity for weighted shifts, for details and undefined notions, see
[
3
]
.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
3
, Theorem 42]
)
.

Suppose that
X
is a Fréchet sequence space over
\mathbb{Z}
in which the sequence
(e_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of canonical vectors is a basis,
(\left\|\cdot\right\|)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an increasing sequence of seminorms that induces the topology of
X
, and the bilateral weighted forward shift
F_{w}
is an invertible operator on
X
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

F_{w}:X\to X
is topologically expansive;

(ii)

there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(i)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|w_{1}\cdot\dots\cdot w_{n}\right|\left\|e_{n+1}\right\|_{k}=\infty
or

(ii)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|w_{-n+1}\cdot\dots\cdot w_{0}\right|^{-1}\left\|e_{-n+1}\right\|_{k}=\infty

(iii)

(i)

F_{w}:X\to X
is topologically positively expansive or

(ii)

F^{-1}_{w}:X\to X
is topologically positively expansive.

To provide a reasonable equivalent condition for uniform topological expansivity we need to put additional assumptions on the ambient space and its seminorms. However, later in the section we will verify that these conditions are satisfied for all types of spaces the authors of
[
3
]
mention, so we deem the assumptions to be not too restrictive.

Similar result for uniform expansivity of weighted shifts on Köthe spaces was obtained independently in
[
16
, Theorem 11]
.

Recall that the basis
(e_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is unconditional if the family of projections
\{P_{I}:I\subset\mathbb{Z}\}
, where

P_{I}:x\mapsto x|_{I},\text{ and }x|_{I}(n)=\begin{cases}x(n),&n\in I,\\
0,&n\notin I,\end{cases}

is uniformly bounded.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose that
X
is a Fréchet sequence space over
\mathbb{Z}
in which the sequence
(e_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of canonical vectors is an unconditional basis,
(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an increasing sequence of seminorms that induces the topology of
X
and the bilateral weighted forward shift
F_{w}
is an invertible operator on
X
. Furthermore, suppose that the following holds

(1)

for every
x\in X
and
a\in\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{Z}}
if
(a(i)x(i))_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in X
, then for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have
\left\|(a(i)x(i))\right\|_{k}\geq\left(\inf_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|a(i)|\right)\left\|x\right\|_{k}
,

(2)

for every
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
there is
c>0
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x\in\overline{\mathrm{span}}\{e_{i},\left\|e_{i}\right\|_{k}\neq 0\}
we have

\left\|\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}x_{i}e_{i+n}\right\|_{l}\geq c\left\|\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}x_{i}\frac{\left\|e_{i+n}\right\|_{l}}{\left\|e_{i}\right\|_{k}}e_{i}\right\|_{k}.

Then the following are equivalent

(i)

F_{w}
is uniformly topologically expansive.

(ii)

For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
there is
l\in\mathbb{N}
and a decomposition
I_{+}
and
I_{-}
such that
I_{+}\cup I_{-}=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}\colon\left\|e_{i}\right\|_{k}\neq 0\}
and

\displaystyle\left|w_{i+n-1}\cdots w_{i}\right|\frac{\left\|e_{i+n}\right\|_{l}}{\left\|e_{i}\right\|_{k}}=\left\|F_{w}^{n}\left(\frac{e_{i}}{\left\|e_{i}\right\|_{k}}\right)\right\|_{l}

\displaystyle\to\infty\ \text{uniformly on}\ I_{+}\ \text{as}\ n\to\infty,

\displaystyle\left|w_{i-n}\cdots w_{i-1}\right|^{-1}\frac{\left\|e_{i-n}\right\|_{l}}{\left\|e_{i}\right\|_{k}}=\left\|F_{w}^{-n}\left(\frac{e_{i}}{\left\|e_{i}\right\|_{k}}\right)\right\|_{l}

\displaystyle\to\infty\ \text{uniformly on}\ I_{-}\ \text{as}\ n\to\infty.

Proof.

(i)\Longrightarrow(ii)
: Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
. By
(i)
there is
l\in\mathbb{N}
and a decomposition
S_{k}=A_{k}\cup B_{k}
as in
Definition

3.1
. Let
I=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:\left\|e_{i}\right\|_{k}\neq 0\}
and define

\displaystyle I_{+}

\displaystyle=\left\{i\in I:\frac{e_{i}}{\left\|e_{i}\right\|_{k}}\in A_{k}\right\},

\displaystyle I_{-}

\displaystyle=\left\{i\in I:\frac{e_{i}}{\left\|e_{i}\right\|_{k}}\in B_{k}\right\}.

Then for any
i\in I
we have that
e_{i}/\left\|e_{i}\right\|_{k}\in S_{k}
and hence also that

\left\|F^{n}_{w}\left(\frac{e_{i}}{\left\|e_{i}\right\|_{k}}\right)\right\|_{l}\to\infty

as
n\to\infty
uniformly on
I_{+}
and

\left\|F^{-n}_{w}\left(\frac{e_{i}}{\left\|e_{i}\right\|_{k}}\right)\right\|_{l}\to\infty

as
n\to\infty
uniformly on
I_{-}
.

(ii)\Longrightarrow(i)
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be given. We can find
l,I_{+},I_{-}
as in (ii). Denote
I=I_{+}\cup I_{-}
. Note that if
x=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}x_{i}e_{i}
then
\left\|x\right\|_{k}=\left\|\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}e_{i}\right\|_{k}
. By our assumption that the canonical basis is unconditional, we have that the projections
x\mapsto x|_{I_{+}}
and
x\mapsto x|_{I_{-}}
are continuous and thus there exist
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\hat{c}>0
such that
\left\|x|_{I_{+}}\right\|_{l}\leq\hat{c}\left\|x\right\|_{m}
and
\left\|x|_{I_{-}}\right\|_{l}\leq\hat{c}\left\|x\right\|_{m}
.

Let
c
be given by the assumption
(2)
for
k,l
. Now define

\displaystyle A_{k}

\displaystyle=\{x\in S_{{}_{k}}:\left\|x|_{I_{+}}\right\|_{k}\geq 1/2\}

\displaystyle B_{k}

\displaystyle=\{x\in S_{k}:\left\|x|_{I_{-}}\right\|_{k}\geq 1/2\}.

By the triangle inequality, for every
x\in S_{k}
we have that
\max{(\left\|x|_{I_{+}}\right\|,\left\|x|_{I_{-}}\right\|)}\geq\frac{1}{2}
and hence
S_{k}=A_{k}\cup B_{k}
.

Pick
x\in S_{k}
. Potentially switching
F_{w}
with
F_{w}^{-1}
, we can assume
x\in A_{k}
.
Let
x=\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}e_{i}
. Using, the fact that
F_{w}^{n}(e_{i})
is a scalar multiple of
e_{i+n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|F_{w}^{n}(x)\right\|_{m}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}F_{w}^{n}(e_{i})\right\|_{m}\geq\hat{c}\left\|\sum_{i\in I_{+}}x_{i}F_{w}^{n}(e_{i})\right\|_{l}

\displaystyle=\hat{c}\left\|\sum_{i\in I_{+}}x_{i}\frac{\left\|F_{w}^{n}(e_{i})\right\|_{l}}{\left\|e_{i+n}\right\|_{l}}e_{i+n}\right\|_{l}

\displaystyle\geq c\hat{c}\left\|\sum_{i\in I_{+}}x_{i}\frac{\left\|F_{w}^{n}(e_{i})\right\|_{l}}{\left\|e_{i+n}\right\|_{l}}\frac{\left\|e_{i+n}\right\|_{l}}{\left\|e_{i}\right\|_{k}}e_{i}\right\|_{k}=c\hat{c}\left\|\sum_{i\in I_{+}}x_{i}\frac{\left\|F_{w}^{n}(e_{i})\right\|_{l}}{\left\|e_{i}\right\|_{k}}e_{i}\right\|_{k}

\displaystyle\geq c\hat{c}\inf_{i\in I_{+}}\left(\frac{\left\|F^{n}_{w}(e_{i})\right\|_{l}}{\left\|e_{i}\right\|_{k}}\right)\left\|\sum_{i\in I_{+}}x_{i}e_{i}\right\|_{k}\geq\frac{c\hat{c}}{2}\inf_{i\in I_{+}}\left(\frac{\left\|F^{n}_{w}(e_{i})\right\|_{l}}{\left\|e_{i}\right\|_{k}}\right).

By (ii) the last term converges uniformly to infinity as
n\to\infty
.
∎

In the remainder of the section, we will check the assumptions of
Theorem

3.3
for some classes of spaces and give appropriately simplified statements. For the standard sequence spaces
\ell_{p}
(
1\leq p<\infty
) and
c_{0}
the uniform expansivity of weighted shifts was studied in
[
4
]
. Our result recovers the original result
[
4
, Theorem E]
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
X=c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})
or
X=\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
for
1\leq p<\infty
and
F_{w}
be an invertible bilateral weighted forward shift on
X
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

F_{w}
is uniformly topologically expansive;

(ii)

There is a decomposition
I_{+}\cup I_{-}=\mathbb{Z}
such that

\displaystyle\left|w_{i+n-1}\cdots w_{i}\right|=\left\|F_{w}^{n}(e_{i})\right\|

\displaystyle\to\infty\ \text{uniformly on}\ I_{+}\ \text{as}\ n\to\infty,

\displaystyle\left|w_{i-n}\cdots w_{i-1}\right|^{-1}=\left\|F_{w}^{-n}(e_{i})\right\|

\displaystyle\to\infty\ \text{uniformly on}\ I_{-}\ \text{as}\ n\to\infty.

Proof.

The fact that the canonical bases of both
\ell_{p}
and
c_{0}
are unconditional is well-known (see e.g.
[
10
]
). The property (1) is seen easily: let
x\in X
and
a\in\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{Z}}
be such that
(a(i)x(i))\in X
. Then for
X=c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})
we have

\displaystyle\left\|(a(i)x(i))\right\|=\sup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|a(i)x(i)\right|\geq\left(\inf_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|a(i)|\right)\sup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|x(i)\right|=\left(\inf_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|a(i)|\right)\left\|x\right\|_{k}

and similarly for
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
. Equally easily one sees that the unweighted shift is an isometry for both
c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
. Hence, for any
i,n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have
\left\|e_{i+n}\right\|=\left\|e_{i}\right\|
and also

\left\|\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}x_{i}e_{i+n}\right\|=\left\|\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}x_{i}e_{i}\right\|=\left\|\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}x_{i}\frac{\left\|e_{i+n}\right\|}{\left\|e_{i}\right\|}e_{i}\right\|,

giving the property (2) with
c=1
.
∎

We finish by checking the assumptions for Köthe spaces (example of which is the space of rapidly decaying sequences). Recall that an infinite matrix
A=(a_{j,k})_{j\in\mathbb{Z},k\in\mathbb{N}}
is said to be a
Köthe matrix
if
0\leq a_{j,k}\leq a_{j,k+1}
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z},k\in\mathbb{N}
and for each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a_{j,k}>0
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, define the
Köthe echelon space
:

\lambda^{p}(A,\mathbb{Z}):=\left\{x=(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{Z}}:\left\|x\right\|_{k}:=\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|x_{j}a_{j,k}\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}<\infty\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

and for
p=0
define

\lambda^{0}(A,\mathbb{Z}):=\left\{x=(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{Z}}:\lim_{j\to\infty}x_{j}a_{j,k}=0,\quad\left\|x\right\|_{k}:=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|x_{j}\right|a_{j,k}<\infty\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
A=(a_{j,k})_{j\in\mathbb{Z},k\in\mathbb{N}}
,
X=\lambda^{p}(A,\mathbb{Z})
for some
p\in\{0\}\cup[1,\infty)
and
F_{w}
be an invertible bilateral weighted forward shift on
X
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

F_{w}
is uniformly topologically expansive;

(ii)

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
there is
l\in\mathbb{N}
and a decomposition
I_{+}\cup I_{-}=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}\colon a_{i,k}\neq 0\}
such that

\displaystyle\left|w_{i+n-1}\cdots w_{i}\right|\frac{a_{i+n,l}}{a_{i,k}}=\left\|F_{w}^{n}\left(\frac{e_{i}}{a_{i,k}}\right)\right\|_{l}

\displaystyle\to\infty\ \text{uniformly on}\ I_{+}\ \text{as}\ n\to\infty,

\displaystyle\left|w_{i-n}\cdots w_{i-1}\right|^{-1}\frac{a_{i-n,l}}{a_{i,k}}=\left\|F_{w}^{-n}\left(\frac{e_{i}}{a_{i,k}}\right)\right\|_{l}

\displaystyle\to\infty\ \text{uniformly on}\ I_{-}\ \text{as}\ n\to\infty.

Proof.

We will consider the case
p\in[1,\infty)
, the case
p=0
can be seen analogously. The canonical basis is unconditional for if
I\subset\mathbb{Z}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in X
, then

\left\|x\right\|_{k}^{p}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|x_{j}a_{j,k}\right|^{p}\geq\sum_{j\in I}\left|x_{j}a_{j,k}\right|^{p}=\left\|P_{I}x\right\|_{k}^{p}.

The condition (1) can be verified in the same way as for
c_{0}
or
\ell_{p}
. Clearly
\left\|e_{j}\right\|_{k}=a_{j,k}
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z},k\in\mathbb{N}
and to verify (2) we calculate

\left\|\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}x_{i}\frac{\left\|e_{i+n}\right\|_{l}}{\left\|e_{i}\right\|_{k}}e_{i}\right\|_{k}^{p}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|x_{i}\frac{\left\|e_{i+n}\right\|_{l}}{\left\|e_{i}\right\|_{k}}a_{i,k}\right|^{p}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|x_{i}a_{i+n,l}\right|^{p}=\left\|\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}x_{i}e_{i+n}\right\|_{l}^{p}.

∎

4.
The periodic shadowing property

This section is motivated by
[
6
, Problem C]
which reads: To characterize the periodic shadowing property for bilateral weighted shifts on Banach sequence spaces. We partially answer this question.

Unless explicitly stated otherwise, we will consider shifts on either the spaces
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
for
1\leq p<\infty
or
c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})
. That is, the running assumption during the whole section is that
X=\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
with
1\leq p<\infty
or
X=c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})
,
w=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a bounded sequence of scalars with
\inf_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|w_{n}\right|>0
and
B_{w}:X\to X
is the bilateral weighted backward shift given by

B_{w}:(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mapsto(w_{n+1}x_{n+1})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}.

Closely related to the periodic shadowing property is the concept of chain reccurency. Recall the definition:

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a locally convex space and
T:X\to X
be a bounded operator. We say that
x\in X
is a
chain recurrent point
of
T
if for every neighbourhood
U
of
0
there is a
U
-chain from
x
to
x
. We denote by
\mathrm{CR}(T)
the set of all chain recurrent points of
T
. Finally, we say that
T
is chain recurrent if
\mathrm{CR}(T)=X
.

It is well-known that
\mathrm{CR}(T)
is a closed subspace of
X
.

We have two starting points. One is the following observation made in
[
6
]
: On
\ell_{p}
and
c_{0}
, if
B_{w}
has the shadowing property, then it has the periodic shadowing property.

The other is a result from
[
11
]
which, among others, establishes a dichotomy: the set of periodic points of a bilateral weighted backward shift is either trivial or dense.

Lemma 4.2

(corollary of
[
11
, Theorem 9]
)
.

If
B_{w}
has a nontrivial periodic point, then the set of periodic points of
B_{w}
is dense in
X
.

First, we will deal with the case when our shift has dense periodic points. We start by a more general result connecting periodic and finite shadowing property for an operator with dense set of periodic points.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
X
be a locally convex space and
T\in\mathrm{GL}(X)
be an operator with dense set of periodic points. If
T
has the periodic shadowing property, then it has the finite shadowing property. In particular, if
X
is a Banach space, then for operators with dense periodic points the PSP implies the SP.

Proof.

Let
V
be any neighbourhood of
0
in
X
and let
U=U_{1}\cap U_{2}
, where
U_{1}
is associated to
V
for the periodic shadowing property and
U_{2}
is such that for any
x,y\in X
we have that
x-y\in U_{2}
implies
B_{w}(x-y)\in U_{1}
.

Let
(x_{j})_{j=0}^{k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
be a
U
-pseudotrajectory. We can find periodic points
y_{m}
for
m\in\{1,\dots k\}
such that
y_{m}-x_{m-1}\in U_{1}
and
B_{w}(y_{m})-x_{m}\in U
which in turn implies
B_{w}^{2}(y_{m})-B_{w}(x_{m})\in U_{1}
. Suppose that the point
y_{m}
has a period
p_{m}>2
(if
B_{w}(y_{m})=y_{m}
or
B^{2}_{w}(y_{m})=y_{m}
then the point is also
p
-periodic for
p=4
). Then we have that the sequence

x_{0},\dots,x_{k},B_{w}^{2}(y_{k}),\dots,B_{w}^{p_{k}-1}(y_{k}),x_{k-1},B_{w}^{2}(y_{k-1}),\dots,B_{w}^{p_{k-1}-1}(y_{k-1}),x_{k-2},\dots,x_{0}

is a
U_{1}
-pseudotrajectory: the steps which do not follow immediately from periodicity of
y_{m}
’s or the fact that
(x_{j})
is a
U
-pseudotrajectory are exactly

B_{w}x_{m}-B_{w}^{2}(y_{m})\in U_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{w}(B_{w}^{p_{m}-1}y_{m})-x_{m-1}=y_{m}-x_{m-1}\in U.

By repeating this finite segment, we can turn it into an infinite periodic
U_{1}
-pseudotrajectory. Then, by the periodic shadowing property there is a periodic point
x\in X
that
V
-shadows this periodic pseudotrajectory which, in particular, means that
x

V
-shadows its initial segment,
x_{0},\dots,x_{k}
.

The
“in particular”
part has been proved
[
6
, Theorem 1]
.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

If the set of periodic points of
B_{w}
is dense, then
B_{w}
has the shadowing property if and only if it has the periodic shadowing property.

Proof.

As we mentioned previously, the forward direction holds irregardless of the periodic points of
B_{w}
as was shown in
[
6
]
. The reverse direction follows from
Proposition

4.3
.
∎

Now we turn our attention to the case when the shift has only the trivial periodic point. Observe that a linear operator which has the periodic shadowing property and no nontrivial periodic points cannot have nontrivial chain-recurrent points. Indeed, if
x\in\mathrm{CR}(T)\setminus\{0\}
, then there is
\delta>0
such that every periodic
\delta
-pseudotrajectory is
\left\|x\right\|/2
-shadowed by some periodic point of
T
. But since
x
is chain recurrent, there is a periodic
\delta
-pseudotrajectory from
x
to
x
, implying the existence of a periodic point of
T
with norm at least
\left\|x\right\|/2
.

So we see that there is a connection between periodic shadowing and existence of chain-recurrent points. In fact, the same dichotomy holds for chain recurrent points as we have for periodic points.

Proposition 4.5
.

Suppose that there is a nontrivial chain recurrent point for
B_{w}
. Then
\mathrm{CR}(B_{w})=X
.

Proof.

Since chain recurrent points form a closed subspace it is enough to show that they are dense in
X
.

For the purposes of the proof, for
A\subset\mathbb{Z}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
define a projection
P_{A,k}:X\to X
by

(P_{A,k}x)(i)=\begin{cases}x(i),&i\in A+k\mathbb{Z},\\
0,&otherwise.\end{cases}

If
A=\{j\}
, then we will simply write
A_{j,k}
.
Since the canonical basis of both
\ell_{p}
and
c_{0}
is unconditionally monotone see
[
10
]
, we have that
\left\|P_{A,k}\right\|=1
for all
A\subset\mathbb{Z}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, it clearly holds that
B_{w}^{n}(P_{A,k}x)=P_{A-n,k}B_{w}^{n}x
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. In particular, if
(x_{i})_{i=0}^{n}
is a
\delta
-pseudotrajectory of
B_{w}
for some
\delta>0
, then so is
(P_{A-i,k}x_{i})_{i=0}^{n}
, since for any
i<n
we have

\left\|B_{w}(P_{A-i,k}x_{i})-P_{A-i-1,k}x_{i+1}\right\|=\left\|P_{A-i-1,k}(B_{w}x_{i})-P_{A-i-1,k}x_{i+1}\right\|\leq\left\|B_{w}x_{i}-x_{i+1}\right\|<\delta.

Before getting to the crux of the matter, we need to prove the

Claim:
For any
A\subset\mathbb{Z}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
we have that
\mathrm{CR}(B_{w})
is
P_{A,k}
-invariant.

Let
x\in\mathrm{CR}(B_{w})
. We will show that
P_{A,k}x\in\mathrm{CR}(B_{w})
. Indeed, let
\delta>0
be arbitrary. Then there is
m\in\mathbb{N}
and an
m
-periodic
\delta
-pseudotrajectory
(x_{i})_{i=0}^{m}
from
x
to
x
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
m=kl
for some
l\in\mathbb{N}
as we may simply repeat the periodic pseudotrajectory
k
times. Then
(P_{A-i,k}x_{i})_{i=0}^{m}
is a
\delta
-pseudotrajectory and we have that

P_{A-m,k}x_{m}=P_{A,k}x_{m}=P_{A,k}x_{0},

since
m=kl
and
x_{m}=x_{0}
Thus
(P_{A-i,k}x_{i})_{i=0}^{m}
is again a periodic
\delta
-pseudotrajectory.

Having proven the claim, we get to the proof proper.
Let
x
be a nontrivial chain recurrent point of
B_{w}
. Then there is
j\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
x(j)\neq 0
, since
CR(B_{w})
is a subspace we may assume
x(j)=1
. Taking arbitrary
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we may apply the claim to
P_{j,2k}
to find
\widetilde{e}_{j}\in\mathrm{CR}(B_{w})
such that for all
-k\leq i\leq k
we have
\widetilde{e}_{j}(i)=e_{j}(i)
. However,
\mathrm{CR}(B_{w})
is a
B_{w}
-invariant subspace, so, in fact, we may find
\widetilde{e}_{l}\in\mathrm{CR}(B_{w})
for any
-k\leq l\leq k
such that
\widetilde{e}_{l}(i)=e_{l}(i)
for all
-k\leq i\leq k
.

Finally, let
y\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. Find
k\in\mathbb{N}
large enough so that for
A=\{-k,\dots,k\}
we have
\left\|y-y|_{A}\right\|<\varepsilon/2
. For this
k
, find
\widetilde{e}_{l},-k\leq l\leq k
as above and define
z=\sum_{i=-k}^{k}y(i)\widetilde{e}_{i}\in\mathrm{CR}(B_{w})
. Find
n>k
so that we have
\left\|z-z|_{\{-n,\dots,n\}}\right\|<\varepsilon/2
. Then we see that because
y|_{A}=z|_{A}
it is the case that

\displaystyle\left\|y-P_{A,n}z\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|y-y|_{A}\right\|+\left\|y|_{A}-(P_{A,n}z)|_{A}\right\|+\left\|(P_{A,n}z)|_{A^{C}}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|y-y|_{A}\right\|+\left\|(P_{A,n}y-P_{A,n}z)|_{A}\right\|+\left\|(P_{A,n}z)|_{A^{C}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+0+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

while
P_{A,n}z\in\mathrm{CR}(B_{w})
thanks to our claim. Thus the proof is finished as
y\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
were arbitrary.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

Notice that from the proof of the proposition also follows that once you have a nontrivial periodic point, then the set of periodic points is dense. Indeed, every periodic point is in particular chain recurrent, so the only part of the proof which fails for periodic points is that they do not form a
closed
subspace.

Turning back to
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
and
c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})
spaces, we can put together known results to obtain the chain of implications as follows.

Proposition 4.7
.

Assume that
B_{w}
has no nontrivial periodic points. Then we have the following chain of implications:

B_{w}\text{ has the SP }\implies B_{w}\text{ has the PSP }\implies\mathrm{CR}(B_{w})=\{0\}.

In terms of weights, we have that given conditions:

(i)

One of the following conditions holds:

(iA)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|w(k)w(k+1)\cdots w(k+n)\right|^{1/n}<1
;

(iB)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\inf_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|w(k)w(k+1)\cdots w(k+n)\right|^{1/n}>1
;

(ii)

B_{w}
has the PSP;

(iii)

One of the following conditions holds:

(iiiA)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left|w(-n+1)\cdots w(0)\right|}<\infty
;

(iiiB)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|w(1)\cdots w(n)\right|<\infty
;

the chain of implications
(i)\implies(ii)\implies(iii)
holds.

Proof.

The fact that the SP implies the PSP was shown in
[
6
]
. The fact that the PSP implies
\mathrm{CR}(B_{w})=\{0\}
was observed before
Proposition

4.5
.

In
[
5
, Theorem 18]
, it is shown that the shadowing property is equivalent to the disjunction of (iA), (iB) and (C), where (C) is some third condition. But, as the authors of
[
5
]
explain, (C) implies the frequent hypercyclic criterion, which in turn implies that
B_{w}
is Devaney chaotic, which entails the periodic points of
B_{w}
being dense. Hence, the condition (C) cannot be satisfied if we assume that
B_{w}
has only the trivial periodic point and so we can omit it.

By
[
6
, Theorem 14]
, the condition
(iii)
is equivalent to
B_{w}
not being chain recurrent, which is by
Proposition

4.5
equivalent to
\mathrm{CR}(B_{w})=\{0\}
.
∎

Counterexample to the first implication can be found in
[
6
, Corollary 23]
.
We will construct a counterexample to the second implication in a moment, but before, let us discuss what it means for an operator to have the periodic shadowing property in absence of nontrivial periodic points.

Let
X
be any normed linear space and
T:X\to X
be a continuous linear operator.
Assume
T
has the PSP, let
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta>0
be associated to said
\varepsilon
by the PSP. If
(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
is some periodic
\delta
-pseudotrajectory, then it must be
\varepsilon
-shadowed by some periodic point. But since the only periodic point is
0
, we must have
\left\|x_{i}\right\|=\left\|x_{i}-T^{i}0\right\|<\varepsilon
, i.e.
(x_{i})\subset B(0,\varepsilon)
. On the other hand if for every
\varepsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
so that all periodic
\delta
-pseudotrajectories are contained in
B(0,\varepsilon)
, then they are all
\varepsilon
-shadowed by the trivial periodic point.

Example 4.8
.

Let
X=c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})
or
X=\ell_{p}

p\in[1,\infty)
and define
w
by setting

w(k)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2},&k\geq 0,\\
1,&k<0.\end{cases}

Then
B_{w}
does not have nontrivial periodic points, does not have the PSP, but
\mathrm{CR}(B_{w})=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Since
1/2\leq w(k)\leq 1
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, our shift is well-defined. To see that
\mathrm{CR}(B_{w})=\{0\}
, we first realize that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|w(1)\cdots w(n)\right|=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}<\infty,

which by
[
6
, Theorem 14]
implies that
B_{w}
is not chain recurrent and hence, by
Proposition

4.5
,
\mathrm{CR}(B_{w})=\{0\}
.

Next, we show that the only periodic point of
B_{w}
is
0
. Assume that
x
is a periodic point of
B_{w}
with period
k\in\mathbb{N}
. If there exist
j\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
x(j)\neq 0
, then because
w(i)\leq 1,i\in\mathbb{Z}
the sequence
\left|x(j+nk)\right|,n\in\mathbb{N}
is non-decreasing which is in contradiction with
x\in c_{0}
or
x\in\ell_{p}
.

Finally, we need to show that
B_{w}
does not have the periodic shadowing property. To do this, we will show that even for every
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta>0
there is a
\delta
-pseudotrajectory from
0
to
0
which is not contained in
B(0,\varepsilon)
. To do this, simply find
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
n\delta>\varepsilon
and consider the sequence
0,\delta e_{-1},2\delta e_{-2},\cdots,n\delta e_{-n},(n-1)\delta_{-n-1},\cdots\delta e_{-2n+1},0
. Since, the restriction of
B_{w}
to
\mathrm{span}\{e_{i}:i<0\}
is the unweighted backward shift, this sequence is clearly a
\delta
-pseudotrajectory and
\left\|n\delta e_{-n}\right\|=n\delta>\varepsilon
.
∎

In the example, we found a “large” periodic
\delta
-pseudotrajectory starting at
0
. This was not only convenient, but also necessary in the sense of the following proposition.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
X
be a normed linear space and
T
be a continuous linear operator. Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that every periodic
\delta
-pseudotrajectory for
T
is contained in the ball
B(0,\varepsilon)
.

(ii)

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that every periodic
\delta
-pseudotrajectory for
T
starting at
0
is contained in the ball
B(0,\varepsilon)
.

Moreover, if
T
has no nontrivial periodic points, then these conditions are equivalent to

(iii)

T
has the periodic shadowing property.

Proof.

The implication
(i)\implies(ii)
is trivial and the moreover part follows directly from the definition of the periodic shadowing property (see discussion before
Example

4.8
). We will show that
(ii)\implies(i)
.

Let
(x_{i})_{i=0}^{k}
be a periodic
\delta
-pseudotrajectory for
T
. We can find
\eta\in(0,1)
(typically very close to
1
) such that

\left\|T(x_{k-1})-\eta^{-1}x_{0}\right\|<\delta,\ \left\|T(x_{k-1})-\eta x_{0}\right\|<\delta.

We can also find
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left\|T(\eta^{n}x_{k-1})\right\|<\delta,\ \left\|\eta^{n}x_{0}\right\|<\delta.

Then we have that the sequence

\displaystyle x_{0},x_{1},\dots x_{k-1},\eta x_{0},

\displaystyle\dots\eta x_{k-1},\eta^{2}x_{0},\dots,\eta^{n}x_{k-1},0,

\displaystyle\eta^{n}x_{0},\dots,\eta^{n}x_{k-1},\eta^{n-1}x_{0},\dots,\eta x_{k-1},x_{0}

is a
\delta
-pseudotrajectory for
T
. By rearranging the sequence, we can make it into a
\delta
-pseudotrajectory for
T
that starts and ends at
0
. By the assumption then, we have that the modified pseudotrajectory is contained in
B(0,\varepsilon)
, but this means that the original pseudotrajectory is also contained in
B(0,\varepsilon)
.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

The absence of periodic points is in some way a pathological case. In this case the periodic shadowing property does not imply
“periodic pseudotrajectiories are shadowed”
but rather
“there are no non-trivial pseudotrajectories”
. This is also the reason why the periodic shadowing property and shadowing property do not coincide as this pathological case needs to be separated.

It is time to put all the pieces together.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
B_{w}
be a bilateral weighted backward shift on
X=\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})

(1\leq p<\infty)
or
X=c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})
, given by a bounded sequence of weights
w=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
with
\inf_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|w_{n}\right|>0
. Then one of the following happens:

•

\mathrm{Per}(B_{w})
is dense in
X
and in this case

B_{w}
has the periodic shadowing property
\iff

B_{w}
has the shadowing property;

•

\mathrm{Per}(B_{w})=\{0\}
in which case consider the conditions

(i)

B_{w}
has the periodic shadowing property;

(ii)

For every
\varepsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(y_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\subset B(0,\delta)
such that
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}B_{w}^{i+1}y_{n-i}\in B(0,\delta)
we have that
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}B_{w}^{i}y_{k-i}\in B(0,\varepsilon)
for all
k\leq n
;

(iii)

For every
\varepsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
such that for all
k\leq l\leq m
one of the following holds

\displaystyle(a)

\displaystyle\quad\left|\frac{\varepsilon}{w(l+1)\dots w(m)}-\delta\sum_{i=0}^{m-(l+1)}\frac{1}{w(m)\dots w(m-i)}\right|\geq\delta

\displaystyle(b)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\left|w(k)\dots w(l)\varepsilon-\delta\sum_{i=0}^{k-(l-1)}w(k)\dots w(k-i)\right|\geq\delta.

Then we have
(i)\iff(ii)\implies(iii)
.

Proof.

The fact the the periodic points are either trivial or dense can be seen either from
[
11
, Theorem 9]
or from
Remark

4.6
. In the case when they are dense, we apply
Corollary

4.4
.

In the case when
\mathrm{Per}(B_{w})=\{0\}
, we will use
Proposition

4.9
to show (ii). First, let us assume that
B_{w}
has the periodic shadowing property. Choose
\varepsilon>0
and find the appropriate
\delta>0
by the PSP. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(y_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
be as in the condition. Consider the sequence
x_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}T^{i}y_{k-i}
,
k\leq n
. Then the assumptions in our condition exactly guarantee that (if we define
x_{0}=0
)
(x_{i})_{i=0}^{n}
repeated infinitely times becomes a periodic
\delta
-pseudotrajectory starting at
0
. The PSP then implies that this
\delta
-pseudotrajectory is
\varepsilon
-shadowed by a periodic point - the only one of which is the zero point. Hence all
x_{k}
are contained in
B(0,\varepsilon)
, as desired.

On the other hand, assume that the condition (ii) holds. Again, choose
\varepsilon>0
and this time use the condition to find
\delta>0
. By virtue of
Proposition

4.9
, it is enough to show that every periodic
\delta
-pseudotrajectory starting at
0
is contained in
B(0,\varepsilon)
. So, let
(x_{i})_{i=0}^{n}
be such a pseudotrajectory. Define
y_{k}=x_{k}-Tx_{k-1}
,
1\leq k\leq n
. Since
(x_{i})_{i=0}^{n}
is a periodic
\delta
-pseudotrajectory,
y_{k}
satisfy the assumptions of the condition and hence we get that
x_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}T^{i}y_{k-i}\in B(0,\varepsilon)
.

Finally, we show that
(i)\implies(iii)
. Assume
(i)
holds but
(iii)
fails. Then there is
\varepsilon>0
such that for all
\delta>0
there are
k\leq l\leq m
such that both (a) and (b) fail. For this
\varepsilon>0
find
\delta>0
so that (i) holds with
2\delta
and let
k\leq l\leq m
be the indices given by the failure of (iii). Define the operator
S_{\delta}:X\to X
by
S_{\delta}0=0
and

S_{\delta}x=\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\left\|x\right\|}\right)x,\quad x\in X\setminus\{0\}.

Define
x_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq m-k+1
by

x_{i}=\begin{cases}0,&i=0,\\
(B_{w}^{-1}S_{\delta})^{m-l+1-i}\varepsilon e_{l},&0<i<m-l+1,\\
\varepsilon e_{l},&i=m-l+1,\\
(S_{\delta}B_{w})^{i-(m-l+1)}\varepsilon e_{l},&m-l+1<i<m-k+1,\\
0,&i=m-k+1.\end{cases}

If we verify that
(x_{i})_{i=0}^{m-k+1}
is a
2\delta
-pseudotrajectory, then we will have a contradiction with
(i)
as
\left\|x_{m-l+1}\right\|=\varepsilon
. If
0<i<m-l+1
, then, since
x_{i}=B_{w}^{-1}S_{\delta}x_{i+1}
, we get

\left\|x_{i+1}-B_{w}x_{i}\right\|=\left\|x_{i+1}-S_{\delta}x_{i+1}\right\|=\left\|(I-S)x_{i+1}\right\|=\delta<2\delta.

Similarly, for
m-l+1\leq i<m-k-1
, we have

\left\|x_{i+1}-B_{w}x_{i}\right\|=\left\|(I-S)B_{w}x_{i}\right\|=\delta<2\delta.

The nontrivial cases are
i=0
and
i=m-k-1
for which we will use our assumption (a) and (b), respectively, fail. For
i=0
, we need to show that
\left\|x_{1}\right\|<\delta
. A simple induction shows that

x_{1}=\frac{\varepsilon}{w(l+1)\dots w(m)}e_{m}-\delta\sum_{i=0}^{m-(l+1)}\frac{1}{w(m)\dots w(m-i)}e_{m}.

Hence, by (a) failing, we get

\left\|B_{w}0-x_{1}\right\|=\left\|x_{1}\right\|=\left|\frac{\varepsilon}{w(l+1)\dots w(m)}-\delta\sum_{i=0}^{m-(l+1)}\frac{1}{w(m)\dots w(m-i)}\right|<\delta.

Similarly one shows that

B_{w}x_{m-k}=w(k)\dots w(l)\varepsilon e_{k}-\delta\sum_{i=0}^{k-(l-1)}w(k)\dots w(k-i)e_{k}

and thus also

\left\|B_{w}x_{m-k}-0\right\|=\left\|B_{w}x_{m-k}\right\|=\left|w(k)\dots w(l)\varepsilon-\delta\sum_{i=0}^{k-(l-1)}w(k)\dots w(k-i)\right|<\delta

by the failure of (b).
∎

On
c_{0}
, we can show that, in terms of the previous theorem,
(iii)\implies(i)
holds, too. That way, we have the following cleaner formulation:

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
B_{w}
be a bilateral weighted backward shift on
X=c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})
given by a bounded sequence of weights
w=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
with
\inf_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|w_{n}\right|>0
. If
B_{w}
has nontrivial periodic points, then the periodic shadowing property is equivalent to the shadowing property. If not, the periodic shadowing property is equivalent to the condition:
For every
\varepsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
such that for all
k\leq l\leq m
one of the following holds

(a)

\left|\frac{\varepsilon}{w(l+1)\dots w(m)}-\delta\sum_{i=0}^{m-(l+1)}\frac{1}{w(m)\dots w(m-i)}\right|\geq\delta

(b)

\left|w(k)\dots w(l)\varepsilon-\delta\sum_{i=0}^{k-(l-1)}w(k)\dots w(k-i)\right|\geq\delta.

Proof.

We will show the reverse implication, proceeding by contraposition. Assume
B_{w}
does not have the PSP. Thus, there is
\varepsilon>0
such that for every
\eta>0
there is a
\eta
-pseudotrajectory for
B_{w}
starting at
0
such that it is not contained in
B(0,\varepsilon)
.

Pick
\delta>0
. Let
(z_{i})_{i=0}^{n+2}
be a periodic
\delta
-pseudotrajectory starting at
0
that is not contained in
B(0,\varepsilon)
. For the ease of notation, define
x_{i}=z_{i+1}
for
i\in\{0,\dots,n\}
. Then we have that
\left\|x_{0}\right\|<\delta
,
\left\|B_{w}(x_{n})\right\|<\delta
and
(x_{i})_{i=0}^{n}
is a
\delta
-pseudotrajectory also not contained in
B(0,\varepsilon)
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\left\|x_{r}\right\|\geq\varepsilon
. We can find
l\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\left|x_{r}(l)\right|\geq\varepsilon
. Let
y_{0}=\varepsilon e_{l}
and for
i\in\mathbb{N}
define

(1)

y_{i}=\begin{cases}\left(1-\cfrac{\delta}{\left\|y_{i-1}\right\|}\right)B_{w}^{-1}(y_{i-1}),&y_{i-1}\neq 0,\\
0,&y_{i-1}=0.\end{cases}

This implies that every
y_{i}
has at most one non-zero coordinate.

Claim:
There is
p\leq r
such that
\left\|y_{p}\right\|<\delta
.

Suppose this is not the case. Then we have
\left|y_{r}(l+r)\right|=\left\|y_{r}\right\|\geq\delta>\left|x_{0}(l+r)\right|
. Note that we have
\left|y_{0}(l)\right|\leq\left|x_{r}(l)\right|
by the definition of
y_{0}
. Let
u<r
be maximal such that

(2)

\left|y_{u}(l+u)\right|\leq\left|x_{r-u}(l+u)\right|

(mind you, we have just shown that this holds for
u=0
, but not
u=r
).

By the assumption, we have that
\delta\leq\left|y_{u}(l+u)\right|
. This and the definition of
y_{i}
implies that

(3)

\left|B_{w}(y_{u+1})(l+u)\right|+\delta=\left|y_{u}(l+u)\right|.

Since
(x_{i})_{i=0}^{n}
is a
\delta
-pseudotrajectory we have that

(4)

\left|(B_{w}(x_{r-u-1})-x_{r-u})(l+u)\right|<\delta.

All together we get

\left|B_{w}(y_{u+1})(l+u)\right|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\eqref{eqn:stepIsDelta}}}}{{=}}\left|y_{u}(l+u)\right|-\delta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\eqref{eqn:maximalu}}}}{{\leq}}\left|x_{r-u}(l+u)\right|-\delta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\eqref{eqn:stepIsLessThanDelta}}}}{{\leq}}\left|B_{w}(x_{r-u-1})(l+u)\right|.

But since
B^{-1}_{w}
preserves the pointwise order we get that
\left|y_{u+1}(l+u+1)\right|\leq\left|x_{r-u-1}(l+u+1)\right|
, which contradicts the maximality of
u
. Thus the claim is proved.

Let
p
be such that
\left\|y_{p}\right\|<\delta
. Using simple induction and (
1
), one can show that

\displaystyle\delta>\left\|y_{p}\right\|=\left|y_{p}(l+p)\right|=\left|\frac{\varepsilon}{w(l+1)\dots w(l+p)}-\delta\sum_{i=0}^{1-p}\frac{1}{w(l+p)\dots w(l+p+j)}\right|.

By taking
l+p=m
we get the first part of the claim. The second part can be shown similarly by starting with
z_{0}=B_{w}(y_{0})
and doing similar construction using
B_{w}
instead of
B^{-1}_{w}
.
∎

The curious mind may wonder if one can obtain the same equivalence for
\ell_{p}
. Instead we pose the following question which is easily seen to be equivalent to asking if our characterisation holds for
\ell_{p}
.

Question 4.13
.

Does the periodic shadowing property for weighted backward (equiv. forward) shifts on
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
(
1\leq p<\infty
) coincide with the periodic shadowing property on
c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})
?

Note that the shadowing property for weighted backward shifts on
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
(
1\leq p<\infty
) coincides with the shadowing property on
c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})

[
5
, Theorem 18]
and also the chain recurrence for weighted backward shifts for
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
(
1\leq p<\infty)
coincides with chain recurrence for
c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})

[
6
, Theorem 14]
. Since, by
Proposition

4.7
the periodic shadowing property is in between those two properties, a negative answer to the question would be surprising. However, there are some properties of weighted shifts that differ on those spaces, see e.g.
[
9
]
.

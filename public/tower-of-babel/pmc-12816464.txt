VAL-083 is effective in patients with newly-diagnosed MGMT-unmethylated glioblastoma: report of phase II study
Authors: Chengcheng Guo, Qunying Yang, Meiling Deng, Xiaoling Qiu, Shaoxiong Wu, Xiaojing Du, Ke Sai, Ziyu Dai, Zhenghe Chen, Ji Zhang, Fuhua Lin, Chao Ke, Shaoyan Xi, Wanming Hu, Zhihuan Zhou, Jiawei Li, Xi Cao, Yixiang Liao, Yanchun Lv, Dennis Brown, John Langlands, Gregory Johnson, Jeffrey Bacha, Claire Kwan, Sarath Kanekal, Richard Schwartz, Zhongping Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeUnmethylated MGMT is a validated biomarker for temozolomide (TMZ) resistance and poor prognosis in glioblastoma. VAL-083 (dianhydrogalactitol; DAG) is a DNA-targeting agent that forms interstrand crosslinks at O6/N7-guanine, inducing double-strand breaks and cell death independently of MGMT-mediated DNA repair. This study aimed to evaluate whether first-line VAL-083 combined with radiotherapy improves efficacy compared to standard-of-care TMZ plus radiotherapy in newly diagnosed MGMT-unmethylated GBM patients.MethodsVAL-083 was administered intravenously on days 1–3 of a 21-day cycle, starting on day 1 of radiotherapy, with a second 3-day cycle on days 22–24 of radiotherapy. After completing the 6-week radiotherapy regimen, patients began adjuvant maintenance therapy with VAL-083 alone, administered IV at the same dose and regimen (Cycles 3–10), for up to 8 maintenance cycles.ResultsAt the censor date, the median overall survival (OS) across all patients from the start of VAL-083 treatment was 19.2 months across all dose levels. Survival probabilities from VAL-083 were 93.0%, 68.7%, 51.2%, and 28.4% at 6, 12, 18, and 24 months, respectively. For patients receiving 30 mg/m2/day VAL-083, 15/25 (60%) had died by the censor date, with a median survival of 18.0 months. Pharmacokinetics exhibited dose-linearity. CSF levels at 2 h post-infusion were comparable to or exceeded plasma levels. Common adverse events included thrombocytopenia and neutropenia.ConclusionsVAL-083 (30 mg/m2/day) combined with radiation therapy was generally safe and well tolerated. Adverse events aligned with previous studies. This regimen, compared to standard-of-care TMZ, showed potential benefits in terms of disease progression and overall survival.Trial registration ClinicalTrials.gov ID NCT03050736, dated: February 13, 2017.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-04235-y.Keywords: VAL-083, Glioblastoma, Radiation, Newly-diagnose, MGMT-unmethylated

Introduction

Glioblastoma (GBM), a WHO grade 4 tumor, is the most aggressive primary brain cancer with a poor prognosis. The standard treatment is surgery followed by the Stupp regimen of temozolomide (TMZ) with radiation, which improves median survival to 14.6 months from 12.1 months with radiation alone [1]. However, patients with unmethylated MGMT promoters have a poorer prognosis and resistance to TMZ, with a median PFS of 5.3 months versus 10.3 months for those with methylated MGMT promoters.

The primary cytotoxic effect of TMZ is due to methylation at the O6 position of guanine, leading to DNA mismatch, activation of the DNA mismatch repair pathway, and eventually, apoptosis [2–5]. The effectiveness of TMZ can be significantly reduced by the DNA repair enzyme O6-methylguanine-DNA methyl-transferase (MGMT). MGMT repairs the O6-methylguanine lesions created by TMZ, and thus tumors with high MGMT expression or promoter methylation often show resistance to TMZ, making it less effective [6–11].

In contrast, VAL-083 is a first-in-class, bifunctional DNA-damaging agent that induces interstrand DNA crosslinks at the O6 and N7 positions of guanine, a site not repaired by MGMT, forming interstrand cross-links leading to double-strand breaks and subsequent cell death. This involves S-phase dependent DNA double stand breaks (DSB) and homologous recombination (HR) DNA repair [12, 13]. VAL-083 has demonstrated clinical activity against a range of cancers [14–17], and has been shown to be unaffected by common mechanisms of chemoresistance, including MGMT [18, 19]. Since VAL-083 can overcome resistance mechanisms that limit the efficacy of TMZ, it is a potential treatment option for TMZ-resistant tumors.

This clinical trial in newly diagnosed GBM patients with unmethylated MGMT promoter was designed to determine if first-line treatment with VAL-083 in combination with a standard-of-care radiotherapy regimen can provide improvement in efficacy over the historical standard of care, TMZ plus radiotherapy.

Methods

Trial design and patients

This trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT03050736) was a single-arm, open label study to determine the safety and maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of VAL-083. Additional details are available in the Supplement. Patients 18 to 75 years of age with documented unmethylated MGMT gene promoter status and newly diagnosed histologically proven supratentorial GBM, IDH wild-type, were enrolled in the study. All patients were confirmed to be MGMT-unmethylated prior to enrolment. Additional inclusion and exclusion criteria are provided in the Supplement. This study was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, International Conference on Harmonization (ICH), Good Clinical Practice (GCP) recommendations (ICH-E6R2), and after approval by the Institutional Review Boards of Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center, Guangzhou, China (Ethics Committee Approve No: B2016-058–01). All patients provided written informed consent prior to any study-related procedure.

Treatment

VAL-083 was administered concurrently with radiation therapy, intravenously once daily for 3 days, commencing on the 1st day of radiotherapy (study days 1–3), with a repeated 3-day cycle (cycle #2) administered on study days 22–24 of radiation therapy.

The patients commenced adjuvant maintenance therapy with VAL-083 alone, at the same assigned dose, such that the patients could receive a maximum of 10 cycles of VAL-083 during induction as adjuvant therapy. The study was conducted in 2 Parts:

Part 1: Dose Confirmation comprised a series of three cohorts to confirm the recommended dose of VAL-083 in combination with a standard of care radiation regimen. Dose escalation of VAL-083 proceeded in three sequential cohorts, consisting of patients receiving 20, 30 or 40 mg/m2/day × 3 every 21 days, respectively. Dose escalation followed in accordance with the scheme outlined in Supplementary Table 1.

Part 2: 20 additional patients were treated using the same dosing and radiation schedule as in Part 1. The dose of VAL-083 studied in Part 2 was 30 mg/m2/day × 3 days every 21 days administered concurrently with radiation and as adjuvant treatment.

Additional details are available in the Supplement.

Efficacy and safety assessments

The primary end point was overall survival (OS) calculated from the date of first dose until the date of death due to any cause. The secondary end points included progression-free Survival (PFS), PFS6, Time to Progression (TTP), Best overall response rate (BOR), Objective response rate (ORR), Adverse event summaries focused on treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), defined as events starting after dosing or pre-existing events worsening during the study. TEAEs were described by system organ class (SOC) and preferred term (PT) using Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) version 21.3. Whole brain MRI was repeated at the end of cycle 3 and every 3 months Tumors were evaluated by the investigator using the Response Assessment in Neuro-Oncology (RANO) criteria [20, 21]. All adverse events were graded utilizing the National Cancer Institute Common Toxicity Criteria version 4.0 [22].

Statistical methods

Statistical analyses were carried out using SPSS 22.0. Survival outcomes were calculated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and 95% CIs for the survival quartiles, including the median, were estimated using the method of Brookmeyer and Crowley [23]. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards model were used to assess the impact of different factors on survival. The chi-square (χ2) test was used to compare the incidence of complications and peritreatment mortality between groups.

Results

Patient population

A total of 29 patients were enrolled into the study. Part 1 consisted of the dose escalation cohorts. As myelosuppression, including grade 3 thrombocytopenia and grade 4 neutropenia, was observed in approximately one-third (33%) of patients at 40 mg/m2/day, the starting dose of VAL-083 was reduced to 30 mg/m2/day for concurrent administration with radiation therapy. This dose of 30 mg/m2/day was selected for Part 2 of the study and an additional twenty patients were treated in this Expansion Phase. Patient characteristics are summarized in Table 1. No significant differences in baseline characteristics were observed across the dose groups. The median age of the 29 patients enrolled in the study was 54.2 years (range: 21.2–68.0). All patients were diagnosed with IDH wild-type, unmethylated MGMT gene promoter, GBM according to the 2021 WHO classification of tumors of the Central Nervous System. The mean time from GBM diagnosis to initiation of treatment with VAL-083 was 24.9 ± 8.8 days. The mean time from last surgery for GBM to initiation of VAL-083 treatment was 36.4 ± 8.2 days (Supplementary Table 2). It is noteworthy that based on baseline MRI assessments performed prior to the initiation of VAL-083 therapy, none of the 29 patients showed radiological evidence of disease progression according to RANO criteria. Therefore, no patients were excluded or considered to have progressive disease during this postoperative interval before the start of treatment. Assessment of postoperative MRI scans determined the extent of surgical resection. Gross total resection (GTR), defined as the absence of any residual contrast-enhancing tumor was achieved in 5 out of 29 patients (17.2%). The remaining 24 patients (82.8%) had measurable residual disease, indicative of subtotal resection. This high rate of residual disease reflects the challenging nature of achieving complete resection in this patient population.

Patient treatment

All 29 patients enrolled comprised the Intent to Treat (ITT) population. All patients also met criteria for the efficacy evaluable (EE) population, having completed both the induction radiotherapy treatment and at least 2 cycles of VAL-083.

The median total radiotherapy dose was 60 Gy (range: 54–60 Gy), delivered in standard fractions of 2 Gy per day, five days per week, over a median treatment duration of 6 weeks. This regimen (60 Gy in 30 fractions over 6 weeks) aligns with the conventional Stupp protocol. No patients were withdrawn from the study due to protocol violations, unacceptable adverse events, or the need for treatment not permitted by the study protocol. The most common reason for premature treatment discontinuation were treatment failure (disease progression) (44.8%), patient decision (3.4%), lost to follow-up (3.4%), and other undefined reasons (6.9%) (Supplementary Table 3).

Dose limiting toxicities (DLT) and dose reductions

Two patients experienced a hematologic DLT during cycle 1. One patient, who received 40 mg/m2/day VAL-084, developed a Grade 4 decrease in ANC and a Grade 3 thrombocytopenia lasting greater than 5 days. This patient received a second cycle of VAL-083 at 40 mg/m2/day, the dose was reduced to 20 mg/m2/day for cycle 3 (final cycle) due to grade 3 neutropenia during cycle 2. The second patient with a hematologic DLT received a starting dose of 30 mg/m2/day VAL-083 and experienced a Grade 4 decrease in ANC and platelet count in Cycle 1. Upon recovery of the platelet and neutrophil counts to normal range, the patient continued treatment in cycle 2 at a reduced dose of 20 mg/m2/day VAL-083, and received an additional 9 treatment cycles at this dose level.

A dose reduction at some time during the treatment period was required in 26 of 29 patients. The patient who received 20 mg/m2/day as a starting dose remained on that dose level for 7 cycles. All three patients receiving 40 mg/m2/day as a starting dose required dose reductions to 30 mg/m2/day and then to 20 mg/m2/day, two of which remained on treatment for a total of 10 and 12 cycles respectively. Overall, the median number of cycles of VAL-083 received, including those administered during the radiotherapy induction phase, was nine (range: 2–13), and the median cycle number where the first dose reduction occurred was cycle 3. While most patients had a dose reduction in VAL-083, patients still received an average of 4.5 cycles of treatment.

Efficacy

Response

Overall, in the ITT population, 13/29 (44.8%) of patients showed a complete response, and 13/29 (44.8%) had stable disease. Of those patients receiving 30 mg/m2/day VAL-083, 11/25 (44.0%) of patients had a complete response and 12/25 (48.0%) of patients had stable disease (Table 2). The objective response rate for all study patients in the ITT population and those receiving 30 mg/m2/day VAL-083, was 13/29 (44.8%) and 11/25 (44%), respectively. The clinical benefit rate for all study patients in the ITT population and those receiving 30 mg/m2/day VAL-083, was 26/29 (89.7%) and 23/25 (92.0%), respectively (Table 2).

Five patients receiving 30 mg/m2/day VAL-083 had tumor that was below limits of measurement post-surgery and before treatment with VAL-083, and were deemed Complete Responders. These patients were not included in the response evaluable (RE) population (24 patients).

Overall, in the RE population, 8/24 (33.3%) patients showed a complete response, and 13/24 (54.2%) had stable disease. Of those patients receiving 30 mg/m2/day VAL-083, 6/20 (30.0%) had a complete response and 12/20 (60.0%) had stable disease. The objective response rate for all study patients in the RE population and those receiving 30 mg/m2/day VAL-083, was 8/24 (33.3%) and 6/20 (30%), respectively (Table 2). The clinical benefit rate for all study patients in the RE population and those receiving 30 mg/m2/day VAL-083, was 21/24 (87.5%) and 18/20 (90.0%), respectively.

Progression free survival (PFS)

The study was censored on 30 June 2021. The last patient was enrolled in the study consenting on 16 January 2020, and received the first treatment with VAL-083 on 12 February 2020.

At the censor date, overall 22/29 (75.9%) patients had either disease progression or had died (Table 3, Fig. 1). The median PFS was 7.9 months, and 68.5%, 36.1%, 28.8% and 23.1% of patients were progression free or alive from start of treatment with VAL-083 at 6, 12, 18 and 24 months, respectively. The median PFS was 7.7 months and 9.0 months for patients receiving 30 mg/m2day and 40 mg/ m2day, respectively. At 6 months from start of treatment with 40 mg/m2/day VAL-083, all patients were progression free and alive, and 33.3% were progression free and alive at 12 and 24 months.

Overall survival (OS)

At the censor date, overall, 18/29 (62.1%) had died. Median OS time from the start of treatment with VAL-083 was 19.2 months (Table 4, Fig. 2). The median OS was 18.0 months and 31.6 months for patients receiving 30 mg/m2day and 40 mg/ m2day respectively. Only one patient received 20 mg/m2/day VAL-083 and had an OS of 9.0 months from the start of treatment with VAL-083 (C1D1). From C1D1 the probability of survival was 91.8%, 67.7%, 46.1 and 23.1% of patients 6, 12, 18 and 24 months, respectively.

Pharmacokinetics

The concentration–time profile of VAL-083 in human plasma samples collected from patients is shown in Fig. 3. PK parameters for patients who received 20, 30 or 40 mg/m2/day VAL-083 as the starting dose are summarized in Supplementary Table 4. Cmax and AUC were found to be broadly linear with respect to dose. At 30 mg/m2 dose, Cmax was 746 ng/mL, and the half-life was estimated at 0.8 h (48 min).

Concentration–time profile of VAL-083 in plasma from patients receiving VAL-083 on cycle 1 day 1. Values are mean (± SD)

Twelve patients out of 29 consented to provide CSF by lumbar puncture after infusion of VAL-083. Overall, plasma levels of VAL-083 were comparable at 2 h post-infusion on day 1 and day 3 of cycle 1. In addition, in general the concentration of VAL-083 in CSF was as high as in plasma at 2 h post-infusion (Supplementary Table 5).

Adverse events

There were a total of 724 TEAEs in 29 patients, and every patient in the study reported at least 1 treatment-related TEAE. There were no TEAEs resulting in death (Table 5).

Discussion

Historically, Median OS in MGMT-unmethylated GBM patients was 12.7 months, with a 2-year OS of 22.7%. In contrast, MGMT-methylated patients demonstrated a 2-year OS of 46% [7]. Attempts to improve efficacy have utilized dose-dense (DD) TMZ schedules to potentially modulate MGMT expression. However, median OS (14.6 months for the Stupp regimen vs 13.3 months for the DD regimen, P = 0.44); and PFS (5.1 months for the Stupp regimen vs 6.0 months for the DD regimen, P = 0.15) did not did not show an OS benefit [6]. Responses to the anti-VEGF antibody, bevacizumab does not confer a survival advantage despite prolonged PFS [24, 25]. A phase II study of bevacizumab and erlotinib, an EGFR, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor, based on the Stupp regimen showed that the sequential treatment did not increase the OS for the unmethylated GBM patients. Neither the “CORE” trial including cilengitide [26], nor the “Glarius” trial [27] including the Stupp regimen sequentially with bevacizumab and irinotecan showed beneficial result for unmethylated GBM patients. The EF14 trial showed benefit for the combination of Tumor Treating Fields (TTF) with TMZ over TMZ alone [28]. However, in a post-hoc analysis in MGMT-unmethylated GBM patients the benefit in OS for TMZ plus TTF combination (16.9 months) vs TMZ alone (14.7 months) was limited.

VAL-083 is water soluble and is administered intravenously. Studies in rodents have demonstrated that VAL-083 crosses the blood–brain-barrier (BBB) and preferentially accumulates in brain tumor tissue [29]. In this current study, we have demonstrated that the pharmacokinetic profile of VAL-083 after intravenous infusion is broadly linear with respect to dose, and consistent across dosing days 1 versus 3. In addition, the drug levels in the CSF have been found to be at least as high as those in plasma at the same time-point, demonstrating that VAL-083 crosses the blood brain barrier. While intravenous administration may be perceived as less convenient, it ensures complete bioavailability and avoids first-pass metabolism, offering pharmacokinetic advantages. Moreover, central venous access is routinely established post-operatively in GBM patients as standard of care.The main adverse events in patients receiving VAL-083 have been myelosuppression. The maximum dose evaluated in this study was 40 mg/m2/d and was associated with myelosuppression and lower overall tolerability. As a result, 30 mg/m2/d × 3 in a 3-week cycle was used in the dose expansion part of this study. This dose demonstrated a manageable safety profile while allowing for the administration of multiple treatment cycles, with a median of 9 cycles received. It is noteworthy that all three patients who started at 40 mg/m2 required dose reduction to 30 mg/m2 and subsequently to 20 mg/m2 due to myelosuppression; two of these patients received an extended number of cycles (10 and 12 cycles, respectively) after dose adjustment, and the group exhibited a median OS of 31.6 months, albeit in a very small sample. This suggests that an initial higher exposure followed by dose-adjusted maintenance may contribute to long-term disease control.

Encouraging efficacy of VAL-083 in this patient population has been observed with respect to overall response, median PFS and OS. Best overall response showed 44.8% of patients had a complete response and 44.8% having stable disease. Among patients receiving the intended treatment dose of 30 mg/m2, 44% achieved a complete response, and 48% had stable disease. The extent of resection has been associated with improved outcomes in GBM patients, with those having total resection having better prognosis and longer survival [30]. In an analysis excluding patients without measurable tumor after surgery, 30% and 60% of patients receiving the intended treatment dose of 30 mg/m2 achieved a complete response and stable disease, respectively. Similarly, the clinical benefit rate remained at 90% in these patients.

The overall median PFS for VAL-083 was 7.9 (CI: 5.9–13.5) months with 22/29 (75.9%) patients progressed, and for those receiving the intended treatment dose of 30 mg/m2 was 7.7 (CI:5.2–14.5) months with 19/25 (76%) of patients progressed. In the Stupp regimen, median PFS was 5.3–6.9 months in unmethylated MGMT GBM patients [7, 31–33]. These median PFS times in the current clinical trial patients treated at 30 mg/m2, are longer than the PFS previously reported.

The overall median OS for VAL-083 was 19.2 (CI: 11.3–21.4) months with 18/29 (62.1%) of patients having died. For those receiving the intended treatment dose of 30 mg/m2 the OS was 18.0 (CI: 10.5–21.1) months with 15/25 (60%) patients having died. The probability of survival at 18 and 24 months for those receiving the intended treatment dose of 30 mg/m2 was 46.1 and 23.1%, respectively. In the Stupp regimen, mOS was 12.7–16.0 months [7, 33], and probability of survival was 13.8% (CI: 4.8–22.7) at 2 years. An important characteristic of our study cohort is the low rate of gross total resection (GTR) (17.2%), with the majority of patients (82.8%) having measurable residual disease postoperatively. This is a known unfavorable prognostic factor and likely reflects the aggressive and invasive growth pattern typical of unmethylated GBM, which often precludes complete surgical removal. Therefore, the promising median overall survival of 19.2 months observed in this trial, which compares favorably to historical controls in unmethylated GBM, is particularly noteworthy. It suggests that the efficacy of VAL-083 combined with radiotherapy may be especially beneficial for patients with substantial tumor burden who are at the highest risk for early progression following suboptimal surgery.

In the Checkmate trial (NCT02617589), involving MGMT unmethylated GBM patients, nivolumab was compared to TMZ in conjunction with radiotherapy, and mOS of 14.9 months (CI: 13.3–16.1) was observed with radiation plus TMZ, and 13.4 months (CI: 12.6–14.3) with RT plus nivolumab [32]. A systematic review of published clinical trials revealed a mPFS and mOS for radiotherapy plus TMZ of 4.99 months (CI: 4.25–5.72) and 14.11 months (CI: 13.18–15.04), respectively, in GBM patients with an unmethylated MGMT promoter [31].

This study was conducted in Guangzhou, China, and included only Han Chinese patients. A recent review of treatment patterns and survival outcomes for Chinese GBM patients revealed only marginal improvements in overall survival over the past 14 years. Several independent factors were identified as prognostic factors associated with improved survival: the extend of resection, KPS > 80, IDH1mut, age (< 70 yrs) and methylation status [30]. In this study, all patients were IDHwt which is unfavourable for survival prognosis, but had a KPS > 80 and were under 70 years old, which are factors associated with better outcomes. OS across all patients in the review indicated mOS of 10.6 months (IQR: 5.2–18.4), however, using MGMT methylation status as a prognostic factor, mOS was 10.5 (IQR:5.6–16.8) months for MGMT unmethylated patients [30].

The mPFS and mOS observed in this study for patients with unmethylated IDHwt GBM treated with VAL-083 are greater than those previously reported, with outcomes closer to those anticipated in the MGMT methylated GBM population. While it has been suggested that there may be little benefit from TMZ in MGMT unmethylated GBM patients, in this study, VAL-083 in conjunction with radiotherapy has demonstrated improvement in patient outcomes (PFS and OS).

A critical aspect of interpreting our results involves contextualizing them within the broader clinical development landscape of VAL-083, particularly in light of the recent AGILE phase III trial (NCT03970447). In AGILE, which adopted an adaptive platform design for newly diagnosed GBM, the VAL-083 arm was closed due to futility at an interim analysis, showing no survival advantage over the standard control arm (predominantly TMZ). The seemingly discordant outcomes between our study and AGILE warrant a careful, multifactorial analysis, which points to fundamental differences in study populations and treatment paradigms rather than contradictory drug efficacy. First, pronounced demographic and epidemiologic disparities exist between the cohorts. AGILE enrolled a predominantly Western (≈76% Caucasian) population with a median age of 62–64 years. In stark contrast, our study was conducted exclusively in Han Chinese patients, whose median age was 54.2 years. This younger age is not a selection bias but a well-documented characteristic of the GBM population in China and East Asia [34, 35]. Age is one of the most powerful prognostic factors in GBM, with each advancing decade increasing the hazard of death by 30–50%. Therefore, the superior survival observed in our cohort is, in large part, consistent with the more favorable prognosis associated with a younger patient population. Second, beyond age, ethnicity-related differences in tumor biology may significantly modulate therapeutic responsiveness. Emerging evidence suggests that East-Asian GBM patients exhibit a distinct molecular landscape compared to their Western counterparts, including a lower prevalence of EGFR amplification and a potentially different immune microenvironment [36, 37]. Furthermore, while the prognostic value of MGMT methylation is conserved across races, the baseline characteristics and clinical course of unmethylated tumors may not be entirely equivalent. These population-specific molecular features provide a biologically plausible framework for understanding differential responses to therapy. Third, and perhaps most critically, is the fundamental difference in treatment sequencing. In the AGILE trial, VAL-083 was evaluated in the adjuvant setting, administered after patients had completed standard radiotherapy with concomitant and adjuvant TMZ. This design tests VAL-083 as a salvage or maintenance therapy in a population that may have already developed broad chemoresistance. Our study, however, investigated VAL-083 as a first-line agent, delivered concurrently with radiotherapy and prior to any exposure to TMZ. This strategy leverages the unique mechanism of action of VAL-083—inducing DNA crosslinks independent of MGMT repair—from the outset, potentially preventing the emergence of resistant clones and maximizing cytoreduction during the critical initial phase of treatment. The importance of this sequencing is underscored by the precedent set by TMZ itself, whose survival benefit is established only when used concurrently with and adjuvant to radiotherapy, not in later lines of therapy.

Ongoing preclinical studies support the rational combination of VAL-083 with other agents, such as PARP inhibitors, topoisomerase inhibitors, Wee1 inhibitors, and bevacizumab, to enhance its efficacy by exploiting DNA damage response pathways or modulating the tumor microenvironment [38–40]. These combinations represent promising future directions for clinical development.

VAL-083 at 30 mg/m2/day × 3 days every 21 days in combination with radiation therapy, is generally safe and well-tolerated, and multiple treatment cycles in the adjuvant setting have been achieved. VAL-083 at 30 mg/m2/day, in combination with radiotherapy, has demonstrated benefit with respect to disease progression over standard of care temozolomide in the same setting.

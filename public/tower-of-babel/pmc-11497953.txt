Humoral and cellular immune response to AZD1222 /Covishield and BV152/Covaxin COVID-19 vaccines among adults in India
Authors: Anuradha S. Tripathy, Dharmendra Singh, Diptee Trimbake, Sukeshani Salwe, Srikanth Tripathy, Arjun Kakrani, Priyanka Jali, Hanmant Chavan, Pragya Yadav, Rima Sahay, Prakash Sarje, Prasad Babar, Anita Shete, Ashok Nandapurkar, Milind Kulkarni
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTSeveral COVID-19 vaccines were developed using different approaches to prevent both symptomatic COVID-19 cases and fatalities. The adults were vaccinated with two doses of AZD1222/Covishield (n = 77) [manufactured by Serum Institute of India Pvt Ltd] vaccine and BV152/Covaxin (n = 99) [manufactured by Bharat Biotech] vaccine. They were assessed for immune response at pre-vaccination, 1 month post first and 6 months post second dose for anti-SARS-CoV-2 IgG antibody, surrogate neutralizing antibody (NAbs), immune phenotypes, antigen specific NK, B and T cell response, their effector functionality by ELISPOT and plasma cytokine profile. Both vaccines elicited enhanced IgG antibody and Nab levels compared to the baseline. BV152/Covaxin, the whole virus inactivated vaccine exhibited higher IgG (70% vs 100%), Nab (90% vs 100%), and robust T cell (31% vs 96%) responses at 6 months post second dose compared to 1 month post first dose justifying the utility of the second dose. Detection of SARS-CoV-2 WV and S1 specific CD4+ central T cell memory response in AZD1222/Covishield vaccinee at 6 months post second dose and higher CD4+ and CD8+ naïve and central memory T cell response in BV152/Covaxin vaccinee at 1 month post first dose indicated the involvement of memory T cells. Persistent IgG and NAb responses along with IgG+B and IgG+memory B cells in AZD1222/Covishield recipients at 6 months post second dose indicated sustained immune memory response. Continued heightened IFN-γ secreting T cell response (ELISPOT) displayed by both the vaccine platforms could serve as a co correlate of protection, further to evaluation in follow up studies. Overall, our data suggest that coordinated functions of humoral and cellular branches of adaptive immunity may pave ways toward protective immunity against COVID-19.

Introduction

In early December 2019, China witnessed the first outbreak of COVID-19.1 World Health Organization declared this outbreak, a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) on 30th January 2020 and COVID-19 pandemic on March 11, 2020 due to the increased number of infected cases.2,3 India initiated a long nationwide lockdown, social distancing strategies and travel advisory restrictions in late March 2020 to fight against the COVID-19 pandemic.4 This resulted in lower spread of COVID-19 infections with fewer cases and lower-case fatality rates compared with the numbers reported in many developed countries.5 However, economic constraints forced a staggered lockdown escape strategy, which led to surge in COVID-19 cases. This prolonged first wave of infection took more than a year to get over (March 2020–September 2020–February 2021).6 The first wave was associated with the G Clade (with D614G mutation-B.1).7 The resurgence of the second wave in India occurred due to large gatherings such as religious festivals, political rallies and diminished public adherence to COVID-19 safety protocols.8,9 The second wave was linked with higher transmissibility due to the Delta (B.1.617.2) variant with 10 mutations in the spike protein causing a surge in the number of cases.10 To avoid further increase in the number of COVID-19 cases, Indian government carried out large scale COVID vaccination drive. In November 2021, highly transmissible variant of concern (VOC) B.1.1.529 (Omicron) with 30 mutations in the spike protein and 15 in the receptor binding domain (RBD) of the spike protein emerged. It caused a sharp increase in new COVID-19 infections worldwide, even in vaccinated individuals, raising concerns about immune escape.11,12 However, Omicron posed reduced risk of fatality and severe disease than the previous SARS CoV-2 variants which was attributed to the possible long vaccination drive effect.13,14 The scare of emergence of new VOCs and variant of interest (VOIs) fastens the vaccination drive and preparedness of health systems across the India.

A significant milestone in the field of biomedical research in the recent times, has been the rapid development of several COVID-19 vaccines.15 As of 29 February 2024, Covishield (Serum Institute of India Pvt Ltd), Covaxin (Bharat Biotech), Sputnik V (Panacea Biotec), Carbevax (Biological E. Limited), Covovax (Serum Institute of India Pvt Ltd)), iNCOVACC ((Bharat Biotech)), and GEMCOVACO®-OM (Gennova) are being used for the vaccination against COVID-19 purpose in India.16 Based on the AstraZeneca-Oxford paradigm, the Serum Institute of India, Pune, developed AZD1222/Covishield, a recombinant, replication-deficient chimpanzee adenovirus vector containing the SARS-CoV-2 S glycoprotein vaccine.17–19 Bharat Biotech collaborated with the Indian Council of Medical Research to develop BV152/Covaxin an inactivated whole virion vaccine, India’s first indigenous COVID-19 vaccine.17–21 The SARS-CoV-2 vaccination campaign was started in India on 13 January 2021, with the goal of administering the aforementioned vaccinations. M/s Serum and M/s Bharat Biotech vaccines are required to be administered in two doses, intramuscularly, 28 days apart (0.5 ml in each dosage) to the adults above 18 years of age.22

Both vaccination formulations in India have demonstrated satisfactory neutralizing antibody against SARS-CoV-2 [BV152/Covaxin 80%, AZD1222/Covishield 90%]22 and its variants.23–25 BV152/Covaxin, formulated with a toll-like receptor 7/8 agonist molecule adsorbed to alum (Algel-IMDG), was shown to be safe, immunogenic, and capable of stimulating a T-cell memory response in phase 1 and 2 studies. Following this, a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre, phase 3 clinical trial to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and immunological lot consistency of BV152/Covaxin showed that the BV152/Covaxin vaccine is generally effective against COVID-19, preventing symptomatic disease and decreasing severity and the need for hospitalization.26 A strong and extensive T cell response was produced in the host by ChAdOx1 nCoV-19 in a phase 1/2 clinical trial.27,28 This was followed by a phase 2/3, participant-blind, observer-blind, randomized, controlled study that demonstrated maintenance of high level of immunogenicity 6 months post-vaccination in adults in India.29

The efficacy of the vaccination depends on numeral properties like generation antibodies, memory cells, and cell-mediated immunity 20]. Studies comparing Covishield and Covaxin have majorly addressed antibody responses.30–35 Reports suggest that neutralizing antibodies are crucial for prohibiting SARS-CoV-2 infection,36 however, both antibody as well as CD8+ T cell responses are extremely important for preventing severe disease.37 Vaccine-induced CD8+ T cell responses, as opposed to neutralizing antibodies, were strongly cross-reactive against Omicron and most likely played a significant role in protection against severe disease.15,38 Due to several difficulties, data on comparisons of the immunogenicity and immune memory of COVID-19 vaccinations are scarce. Head-to-head comparisons of T cell, B cell, and antibody responses to different vaccines are likely to generate crucial information on protective immunity against COVID-19, especially in the context of immune memory.

The objective of this study was to compare the humoral and cell mediated immune response mediated by the two vaccines. In the present study, we have examined SARS-CoV-2-spike-specific immune responses to AZD1222/Covishield and BV152/Covaxin were examined longitudinally for 6 months post second dose of vaccination, in Pune, Maharashtra, India. The present study was designed to establish the duration of vaccine-induced B and T cell immune memory responses by two vaccine platforms, a recombinant, replication-deficient chimpanzee adenovirus vector (AZD1222/Covishield) and an inactivated whole virion based (BV152/Covaxin). Overall, the levels of anti-SARS-CoV-2 IgG antibody, levels of neutralizing antibodies (NAbs), peripheral immune phenotypes, antigen specific NK, B and T cell response, their effector functionality and plasma cytokine profile in AZD1222/Covishield (n = 77) and BV152/Covaxin (n = 99) vaccinees were analyzed. We have additionally compared their immune memory in terms of spike specific antibodies, neutralizing antibodies, whole virus and spike-specific T cell response in seronegative individuals.

Material and methods

Study participants

The current study was carried out between 2021 and 2022. (AZD1222/Covishield:T0:25 October, 2021 T1:21 November 2021, T2: 27 May 2022,T2: BV152/Covaxin: T0:06 July 2021, T1: 05 August 2021, T2: 10 February 2022) The study participants were recruited from Dr. D. Y. Patil hospital and research centre, Pune for AZD1222/Covishield and Aundh chest hospital, Pune for BV152/Covaxin, Pune, Maharashtra, India respectively. A total of 176 peripheral blood samples were obtained from study participants who received either AZD1222/Covishield (n = 77) or BV152/Covaxin vaccine (n = 99), in accordance with the authorized dose schedule. Both cohorts of vaccinees (AZD1222/Covishield or BV152/Covaxin vaccine) received two doses (0.5 ml) of the vaccine intramuscularly 30 and 180 days apart, respectively. Blood samples were collected at baseline/pre-vaccination (T0), 1 month post first dose (T1: targeting 30 ± 4 days approximately) and 6 months post – second dose (T2: targeting 180 ± 6 approximately). A small number of subjects were lost during the investigation due to relocation, missing appointments or logistical challenges such as transportation difficulties, etc. The blood samples were processed for PBMCs and the plasma samples were stored at −80°C at ICMR-National Institute of Virology, Pune, India. The experimental characterization of antibody and immune cell responses followed the same technique and used the same equipment. Timelines for collecting blood samples and an overview of all samples analyzed in the study are provided in Figure 1, supplementary Figure S1. The study was approved by the Institutional Ethical Committee for Research on Humans, based on the guidelines set by the Indian Council of Medical Research, New Delhi (20-1-2-R.). Informed consent was obtained from all participants. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) isolation, plasma separation, and storage

Peripheral blood samples (approximately 3–4 mL) from the study participants who had received either BV152/Covaxin AZD1222/Covishield were collected and were processed for PBMCs isolation by density gradient centrifugation method.39 Isolated PBMCs were used for immune cells profiles, antigen specific NK, B and T cell response, effector functionality by intracellular cytokine staining and ELISPOT assays for both vaccine platforms. The plasma samples were stored at −80°C and thawed at the time of anti-SARS-CoV-2 IgG antibody detection by ELISA, estimation of neutralizing antibodies (NAbs) against SARS-CoV-2 using Surrogate Virus Neutralization (sVNT) assay (cPass™, GenScript USA) and cytokine/chemokine estimation using a Bio-plex ProTM Human Cytokine 27-plex assay kit for both vaccine platforms. Plasma sample was considered as anti-SARS-CoV-2-S1 IgG antibody positive if the OD450nm was three times greater than the OD450nm of negative control. Circulating NAbs against SARS-CoV-2 competitively inhibited the RBD-ACE2 interaction. The percentage of inhibition was calculated by measuring the difference in the amount of labeled RBD between the test versus control samples. The cut off ratio for percentage of inhibition was at 30%. Two-fold dilution from 1:10 to 1:320 was performed to determine the titer. The sample showing greater than 30% signal inhibition was considered as positive.

Flow cytometry analysis

Freshly isolated PBMCs (1 × 106) were used for surface staining of NK, NKT, B, T, memory B, and T cells using cocktail of antibodies (BD Biosciences, USA) following a previously described protocol.24 Further, 1 × 106 PBMCs were cultured at 37°C with and without SARS-CoV-2 S1 protein/antigen (10 µg/ml, SARS-CoV-2 (2019-nCoV) spike S1 (D614G)-His Recombinant Protein, Sino Biological, China] in RPMI-1640 medium with 10% FBS for 48 hrs. The cells were harvested and surface stained with cocktail of antibodies and fixed with 2% paraformaldehyde and acquired on a FACS Aria II (BD Biosciences, USA)12 (Supplementary figure S2).

Intracellular cytokine staining

The functional capacities of NK, CD4+ T and CD8+ T cells were assessed by their intracellular expressions of IFN-γ/CD107a using multicolor flow cytometry as reported before.40 For each experiment 50,000 events were acquired within the lymphocyte gate with appropriate isotype control. Data were analyzed using FACS Diva software (BD Biosciences).12,39,40

SARS-CoV-2 specific T cell ELISPOT

SARS-CoV-2 specific T cell response in terms of IFN-γ release by ELISPOT assay was performed as previously described.12,39 To estimate the SARS-CoV-2 specific IFN-γ secreting spot forming cells (SFCs), 1X105 PBMCs were stimulated with gamma irradiated SARS-CoV-2 whole virus antigen/recombinant S1 protein (wild type) [SARS-CoV-2 (2019-nCoV) spike S1], (10 µg/ml, Sino Biological) for 48 hrs and cultured at 37°C. Cells without any antigen and with phytohemagglutinin A (Sigma Aldrich, USA) served as negative and positive controls respectively. The SFCs were counted on an ELISPOT reader (AID GmbH, Strassberg, Germany). The cut-off level for SFCs was set as the average number of SFCs in the negative control wells and were expressed as the number per 105cells 35–36]. The number of SFCs in unstimulated wells was subtracted from the number in the antigen-stimulated wells in each subject for normalization. Results with high background readings or with no PHA responses were excluded. The test was scored as positive and the study participant was called a responder when test wells contained a mean of at least 3 times the mean number of SFCs of the negative control wells. The positive control wells reported 150–650 SFCs and negative control wells reported 0–20 SFCs.

Estimation of cytokine, chemokine, and growth factor levels

Plasma concentrations of cytokines, chemokines, and growth factors were determined on a Bio-plex Multiplex Immunoassay System (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA) using a Bio-plex ProTM Human Cytokine 27-plex assay kit as reported previously41 as per the manufacturer’s instructions. Levels of 15 cytokines, including the pro-inflammatory (IL-1β, IL-5, IL-6, IL-7, IL-9, IL-15, IL-17, TNF-α), anti-inflammatory (IL1-RA, IL-4, IL-10, IL-13), and Th1 (IL-2, IFN-γ, IL-12 p70) cytokines along with seven chemokines (Eotaxin, CCL-2, CCL-3, CCL-4, CCL-5, IL-8, CXCL-10) and five growth factors (basic fibroblast growth factor [FGF], G-CSF, GM-CSF, vascular endothelial growth factor [VEGF], platelet-derived growth factor-bb [PDGF-bb]) were estimated. The lowest value of the respective standards was used in case of undetected concentrations of the cytokines, chemokines and growth factors in the tested samples.39,41

Software and statistical analyses

The statistical analyses were performed on IBM SPSS Statistics 25 software (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). The Mann–Whitney U test was used for comparison between the groups. The mean of triplicate experiments in ELISPOT assay was considered for the analysis. All the data were expressed as median (IQR). Levels of all analytes were analyzed post log10 transformation of the observed concentrations of individual cytokines/chemokines/growth factors. A p value of < 0.05 was considered as significant. The dot plots were generated on GraphPad Prism 8 software (GraphPad, San Diego, CA, USA).

Results

COVID-19 vaccine cohorts

The subjects were not vaccinated before enrollment. The characteristics of the COVID-19 vaccine cohorts are presented in Table 1, Figure 1. The distribution of age and gender was similar in both vaccination groups. SARS-CoV-2 vaccine-naïve participants were included at the baseline for both vaccines. We included participants who were planning to get the AZD1222/Covishield (n = 77) vaccine or BV152/Covaxin vaccine (n = 99) vaccine to assess the immune responses. The study participants were aged 18 and older. Paediatric population, pregnant and breastfeeding mothers were excluded from the study. The individuals were negative for SARS-CoV-2 RT-PCR at the time of enrollment. Both cohorts of vaccinees received two doses of vaccine (AZD1222/Covishield or BV152/Covaxin) approximately 30 and 180 days apart. Magnitude and longevity of spike antibody produced by AZD1222/Covishield and BV152/Covaxin vaccine platforms

Anti-SARS-CoV-2 spike/S1 IgG antibodies, and SARS-CoV-2 surrogate virus neutralization (sVNT) were determined in 74 AZD1222/Covishield vaccinees and 99 BV152/Covaxin vaccinees. At baseline (T0), for AZD1222/Covishield vaccinee, 34/38 (89.7%) were anti-SARS CoV-2-S1 IgG positive while neutralizing antibodies were detected in 20/38 (52%). At baseline (T0), for BV152/Covaxin vaccinee, 29/46 (63.04%) were anti-SARS CoV-2-S1 IgG positive, while neutralizing antibodies are not available for this time point. At T1, 20/20 (100%) and 32/36(70%) of AZD1222/Covishield and Covaxin vaccinees were anti-SARS CoV-2-S1 IgG positives, while, 20/20 (100%) and 27/30 (90%) of AZD1222/Covishield and Covaxin vaccinees had detectable neutralizing antibodies. At six months post second dose (T2), 100% of both vaccinees had detectable anti-SARS CoV-2-S1 IgG as well as neutralizing antibodies (Table 1, Figure 2). Percentages of CD4+ and CD8+ T cells

The percentage of CD4+ T cells were significantly lower at T1 and T2 compared to T0 in AZD1222/Covishield and BV152/Covaxin vaccinees respectively (CD4+T cells: AZD1222/Covishield: T0: 74.4(62–80.2), T1: 58.5(52.2–69.1), BV152/Covaxin: T0: 61.1(44.3–75.3), T2: 44.3(35.3–54.9), p < .05 in each) (Table 2, Figure 3b).

The percentages of CD8+ T cells were significantly higher in both AZD1222/Covishield and BV152/Covaxin vaccinees at T2 compared to T0 (CD8+ T cells: AZD1222/Covishield: T0: 12.4(7–21.5), T2: 22.6(8.2–29.4), BV152/Covaxin: CD8+T cells: T0: 14.7(4.3–26.3), T2: 41.3(30.2–47.5), p < .05) (Table 2, Figure 3c). Percentages of memory T cells subsets

The percentages of CD4+ and CD8+naïve T cells were significantly higher in AZD1222/Covishield vaccinees at T2 compared to T0 and T1 (CD4+naïve T cells: ovishield: T0: 34.3(10.7–48.4), T1: 35.3(23.9–46.7), T2: 41.7(36.6–58.1), CD8+naïve T cells: T0: 41.9(14.4–54.6), T1: 37.8(21.2–50.9), T2: 52.3(34.5–58.2), p < .05 in each) (Table 2, Figure 3b,c).

The percentages of CD4+ and CD8+naïve T cells were significantly higher at T1 compared to T0 in BV152/Covaxin vaccinees (CD4+naïve T cells: BV152/Covaxin: T0: 28.0(5.4–46.7), T1: 40.6(28.9–51.9), CD8+naïve T cells: BV152/Covaxin: T0: 19.1(4.2–30.9), T1: 33.9(20.3–53.0), p < .05 in each) (Table 2, Figure 3b,c).

CD4+central memory T cells were significantly higher at T2 vs T0 in AZD1222/Covishield and at T1 compared to T0 in BV152/Covaxin vaccinees [CD4+ central memory T cells: AZD1222/Covishield: T0: 27.3(11.18–3), T2: 34.8(29.1–44.3), BV152/Covaxin: T0: 28.3(8.8–40.9), T1: 37.8(27.8–47.1), p < .05 in each]). CD8+ central memory T cells were significantly higher at T2 and T1 compared to T0 in AZD1222 vaccinees [CD8+ central memory T cells: AZD1222/Covishield: T0: 3.6(0.9–7.1), T1: 6.8(3.1–11.1), T2: 8.2(4.6–10.2), p < .05 in each]. CD8+ effector memory T cells were significantly lower at both T1 and T2 in BV152/Covaxin and AZD1222/Covishield vaccinees compared to T0 (CD8+ effector memory T cells: BV152/Covaxin: T0: 51.8(26.3–71.3), T1: 27.6(20.5–46.9) and T2: 22.0(18.0–31.7), AZD1222/Covishield: T0: 30.2(16.1–59.9), T1: 18.6(10.1–27.9) and T2: 17.8(14.1–22.2), p < .05 in each) (Table 2, Figure 3b,c).

Discussion

The COVID-19 vaccines have shown remarkable success in preventing hospitalization, severe illness and death, however, data on the cumulative roles of immunogenicity and immune memory exhibited by different COVID-19 vaccines are scarce c/o multiple challenges. The current comprehensive study is an evaluation of kinetics of SARS-CoV-2 specific antibody response, memory B and T cell response, effect of vaccination on the plasma cytokines and chemokines levels to two different vaccine platforms. Since, 90% of the Indian population were recipient of these two vaccines, the findings are of importance for all time to come.

Anti-SARS CoV-2-spike specific IgG and neutralizing antibody responses were detected in 100% of Covishield vaccinee at one month post first dose and at six months post second dose, while a similar observation was demonstrated in Covaxin vaccinee at six months post second dose indicating long-term antibody kinetics of both vaccines, this could be attributed to the possible boosting effect in sero-positives/exposure to highly transmissible circulating variants/solely vaccine effect. It is of concern to note that Covaxin, the whole virus inactivated vaccine exhibited lower IgG and neutralizing antibody responses at one month post first dose justifying the utility of the second dose in the inactivated vaccines. These serological outcomes are in line with the previous reports.19–23–25–34,35–45

Demonstration of SARS-CoV-2 specific T cell response even in antibody negative healthcare workers37 has provided evidence of putative role of T cells as mediators in the defense mechanism against COVID-19.46 Current Covishield vaccinee displayed enhanced SARS-CoV2-WV/spike specific expressions of CD107/IFN-γ respectively on CD4+ T cells at 6 months post second dose indicating functional and long-lasting T cell response with AZD1222/Covishield regimen. Liao, M. et al. have reported that higher counts of CD8+ T cells in the lungs are associated with better control of SARS-CoV-2 progression.47 Post vaccination, spike-specific CD8+ T cells have been reported to be playing an important part in the immediate immune response to breakthrough SARS-CoV-2 infection.23 Detection of SARS-CoV2-WV and spike-specific expressions of TNF-α on CD8+T cells at the AZD1222/Covishield platform and BV152/Covaxin-induced CD8+T cells at 6 months post second dose in our study could contribute to the protective immunity in the presence of a robust antibody profile. These findings are consistent with other efficient vaccine platforms that enable the CD4+ and CD8+ T cell responses.28–48–52

T cell responses generated post infection or vaccination are critical to determine cellular immunity as well as to characterize long-term immunity. IFN-γ has been shown to play an important role in memory and protection against SARS-CoV-2. Chen G et al. have reported a significantly lower absolute number of lymphocytes, CD4+T and CD8+T cells along with downregulation of IFN-γ expression in severe COVID-19 cases.53 Another study showed lower levels of IFN-γ in the plasma with normal levels of T cell counts during the recovery period of 10 follow-up patients.54,55 Safont, G.et al observed lower T-cell response during the acute COVID-19 patients and increased IFN-γ response in convalescent individuals.56 Lower levels of anti-viral cytokines IFN-γ and IL-12 p70 in both asymptomatic and mild symptomatic COVID-19 Indian patients were suggested as biomarkers of SARS-CoV-2 infection.41 In addition, IFN-γ detection is suggested as a useful biomarker for monitoring severe acute patients.56,57 Recovered individuals from asymptomatic and mild-to-moderate infection have demonstrated retention of functional SARS-CoV-2-specific T cell responses at 6 months.58 We have previously observed a limited impact on the T cell mediated IFN-γ response by the Omicron infected patients irrespective of vaccination or prior infection status. This further indicated the presence of vaccine/infection-induced broad T-cell immunity in Omicron infection that may offer significant protection from severe disease. Robust IFN-γ-producing NK cell response was detected by the three-dose inactivated CoronaVac vaccination.59 Wang, X et al. demonstrated that cellular protective immunity relies on a strong IFN-γ production to provide protection. Further, IFN-γ generated by CD4 + and CD8 + T cells stimulated with SARS-CoV-2 antigen were highest at 3 weeks after the second vaccination in health care workers vaccinated by BNT162b2 mRNA vaccines.57

In a longitudinal cohort study, persistence of T cell responses was observed at 6–8 months after SARS-CoV-2 infection.60 Arankalle et al. have demonstrated T cell responses in the majority (77.8%) of AZD1222/Covishield-recipients at 6 months post second dose61 that is in line with our report of detection of robust SARS-CoV-2 WV specific T cell response both at one month post first dose (80.95%) and at six months post second dose (93.75) in AZD1222/Covishield vaccinee. In the same study, Arankalle et al. reported that BV152/Covaxin recipients elicited 100% and stronger T cell response that they have attributed to IMDG adjuvant known for augmentation of cellular immunity. Our BV152/Covaxin study group exhibited a similar robust SARS-CoV-2 WV specific T cell response at six months post second dose (96%). However, detection of lower antibody and T cell response at one month post first dose justified the second dose. The features of robust T cell response at both vaccine platform could be promising for the durability of cellular immunity against the new variants of SARS-CoV-2. Further studies with booster dose may reassure the longevity of T cell response post 6 months of vaccination and may help to maintain a sustained immune response to reduce COVID-19 severity due to newly emerging variants. Lower T cell response against S1 and higher T cell response against WV as recall antigen could be attributed to exposure of vaccinees to the circulating variants having major mutations in the S1protein.12

Alum-adjuvented inactivated whole virion SARS-CoV-2 based BV152/Covaxin was primarily designed to induce a better antibody response, while non-replicating adenovirus based AZD1222/Covishield utilizes SARS-CoV-2 S protein toward the antigen-presenting cells of the host.46,49 Though expected, B, IgG B and memory B cell responses to the both vaccines were not similar as the antibody responses. Adenovirus vectored vaccine Covishield elicited higher IgG+B and IgG+memory B cells at 6 months post second dose indicating robust and sustained immune memory response that could be associated with persistent IgG and neutralizing antibody responses. However, whole virion SARS-CoV-2 based BV152/Covaxin showed only higher B cell associated with enhanced IgG and neutralizing antibody responses at 6 months post second dose. The difference in the memory cells responses could be attributed to the time of sample collection; closer to the Omicron wave for BV152/Covaxin and post Omicron wave for AZD1222/Covishield.62 It remains possible that exposure to ancestral/delta strains prior to vaccination along with exposure to Omicron strain during the course of vaccination could be the fact behind the robust immune responses in AZD1222/Covishield vaccinees. It is reported that AZD1222/Covishield generates robust T cell response in the host which leads to increase in B cell activation, proliferation and anti-IgG post vaccination.49 In a similar way, our AZD1222/Covishield platform vaccinee displayed robust T cell response and enhanced SARS-CoV2-WV and S1 specific IgG+B cells response. The salient observation to note here is that both vaccine platform generated robust antibody response, both IgG and NT, at 6 months post second dose that could be suggestive of providing assurance of protective immunity. In addition, continued heightened IFN-γ secreting T cell response displayed by both the vaccine platforms could serve as a co correlate of protection, further to evaluation in follow up studies.

In a study of severe COVID-19 patients, decreased percentages of total lymphocytes, CD3+ T cells, CD4+ T cells, naive T cells and central memory T cells were observed. In addition to that increase percentages of effector T cells and effector memory T cells were detected in those patients.62 The vaccine-induced immune response may lead to the generation of memory T cells, contributing to enhanced immune memory and surveillance.63 Naive T cells are precursor T cells that activate and differentiate into memory T cells which are the crucial components of the adaptive immune response.63–65 Our data highlights the vaccine-induced enhanced CD4+ and CD8+ naïve and central T cell memory response, SARS-CoV-2 WV and S1 specific CD4+ central T cell memory response in AZD1222/Covishield at 6 months post second dose and higher CD4+ CD8+ naïve and CD4+ central in BV152/Covaxin at 1 month post first dose indicating persistence of memory T cells response in AZD1222/Covishield vaccinees which is broadly consistent with previous reports.22,65 These findings of persistence of memory CD4+ and CD8+ T cells induced by both the vaccine platforms reassure that the vaccination may offer protection through T-cell-governed pathways.

Observed lower levels of IL-1β, IL-6, and anti-inflammatory IL-4 in AZD1222/Covishield and IL-5 and IL-7 in BV152/Covaxin vaccinees at T1 and T2 compared to baseline go hand in hand with the cytokine/chemokine profiles of recovered patients, indication of a protective vaccine.66 These findings are suggesting efficient induction of protective cytokine responses at both vaccine platforms that may be able to safeguard against severe COVID-19 infection.

We were unable to perform NT and ELISPOT assay at baseline, antigen specific and effector function assays for BV152/Covaxin due to the limited volumes of blood samples available and due to loss to follow‐up. We did not assess variant recognition and neutralization potential of the vaccinees against variants of concern (VOCs), because that was the subject of other investigations conducted in our labs.23–26–33–39

In a nutshell, we demonstrate that both the vectored and whole virus inactivated vaccinees elicit high and comparable anti-SARS CoV-2-S1 IgG and neutralizing antibodies irrespective of the platforms used indicating both as clear correlates of protection over the first month post-vaccination. Elucidation of robust and sustained immune memory response by Covishield vaccinee as indicated by higher IgG+B and IgG+memory B cells at 6 months could be associated with the increased IgG and neutralizing antibody responses. The engagement of CD4+ and CD8+ naïve and central memory T cells generated by both vaccination platforms may help in understanding the dynamics and durability of memory T cell responses. Our ELISPOT data demonstrated functional SARS-CoV-2 specific T cell responses in the majority of AZD1222/Covishield (93.75%) and BV152/Covaxin (96%) recipients and the generated cellular immunity might be the key to curtail disease severity and resolve infection. Robust and persistent T cell response in AZD1222/Covishield vaccinee consisting of mostly seropositive harboring hybrid immunity, may reduce the risk of possible re infection from the emerging variants. Our data could have implications for understanding the robustness of immunity following natural infection and/or vaccination

Knowledge, attitudes, and practices regarding periodontal assessment and co-management during orthodontic care among Moroccan dentists: a nationwide cross-sectional survey
Authors: Abdelhadi Hbibi, Siham Halal, Khadija Sikkou, Momen Atieh, Souhail Ben Jelloun, Amine Cherkaoui, Sonia Ghoul, Yasmine Hanine, Ghita Kadri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPeriodontal diseases are common chronic inflammatory conditions that may compromise the planning, safety, and outcomes of orthodontic treatment, particularly in adults and in patients with reduced periodontal support. In such patients, orthodontic care requires accurate periodontal diagnosis, confirmation of disease stability before treatment initiation, careful biomechanical adaptation, and close coordination with periodontal maintenance. However, the extent to which dentists providing orthodontic care integrate these principles into routine practice remains insufficiently documented, particularly in North African settings. This study assessed the knowledge, attitudes, and practices of Moroccan dentists providing orthodontic care regarding the diagnosis and management of patients with periodontal diseases.MethodsA nationwide cross-sectional online survey was conducted in Morocco between September 2025 and February 2026. Eligible participants were dentists practicing orthodontics exclusively or within general practice. The questionnaire included demographic variables, 8 knowledge items, and 9 attitude/practice items covering diagnosis, screening, treatment timing, interdisciplinary collaboration, and periodontal maintenance. Item-level descriptives used available-case denominators. Knowledge (0–7), attitude (0–2), practice (0–6), and total KAP (0–15) scores were calculated. Associations with periodontitis-case management were explored using logistic regression; correlates of KAP scores were examined using linear regression, with candidate variables selected from univariable analyses (p < 0.20).ResultsOf 418 platform entries, 118 blank entries were excluded; 300 practitioners from 11 regions provided at least 1 valid response, including 190 complete questionnaires. Only 18.3% correctly defined periodontitis and 46.3% identified the radiographic criterion for bone loss, whereas 81.1% recognized apical displacement of the center of resistance and 70.4% identified intrusion as the movement associated with bone gain. Pre-orthodontic periodontal screening was reported by 63.0%, but systematic full-mouth 6-site probing by only 16.5%. Among periodontitis cases, 44.4% reported formal periodontal stability confirmation before treatment initiation and 35.9% waited 3 months after active periodontal therapy. Median knowledge, attitude, and total scores were 2 (interquartile range (IQR) 0–3)/7, 1 (IQR 0–1)/2, and 6 (IQR 1–7)/15; mean practice score was 2.36 ± 1.25/6. A total of 84.0% expressed a need for continuing education.ConclusionsMoroccan dentists showed suboptimal ortho-periodontal performance, with limited comprehensive periodontal assessment and variable interdisciplinary coordination. Training should prioritize periodontal diagnosis, full-mouth assessment, and structured co-management pathways.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-026-09653-0.

Introduction

Malocclusions and dental crowding remain prevalent across contemporary populations, supporting the need for orthodontic management in children, adolescents, and adults [1]. However, these groups also present a high burden of periodontal diseases worldwide and at the national level. Globally, up to 80% of adults show signs of gingivitis or mild periodontitis (stage I–II), and more than one billion people are affected by severe forms of periodontitis (stage III–IV) [2–4]. In the United States, NHANES data indicate that periodontitis affects 42.2% of dentate adults aged ≥ 30 years, including 7.8% with severe periodontitis [5]. In Morocco, national surveillance data indicate that 42.2% of 12-year-old children present periodontal conditions, 79.2% of adults aged 35–44 years exhibit periodontitis—with 15–20% affected by severe forms—and 30% of individuals aged 65–74 years are completely edentulous [6]. Among Moroccan adolescents aged 12–18 years, Kissa et al. reported prevalences of 46.9% for gingivitis and 12.3% for periodontitis [7].

In parallel, the global demand for orthodontic treatment (OT) continues to rise. National surveys report orthodontic treatment need rates of 26.4% in Italy [8] and 35–39.5% in British and Swedish cohorts [9, 10]. In the United States, adults in active orthodontic treatment increased from 1.64 million in 2022 to an estimated 1.91 million by 2025 [11]. This epidemiological convergence, namely, high OT demand in populations with substantial periodontal needs, underscores the importance of understanding the interactions between orthodontic forces and periodontal health.

OT in patients with reduced but stable periodontal support can improve function, alignment, and aesthetics, but it requires accurate diagnosis, periodontal stability, adapted biomechanics, and maintenance-oriented co-management. Inadequate screening or coordination may expose patients to gingival inflammation, recession, clinical attachment loss, unfavorable bone remodeling, mobility, or tooth loss [12–14]. These risks are relevant when orthodontic forces are applied in periodontally susceptible patients without verified periodontal control.

Previous European surveys have described professional practices, interdisciplinary communication, and periodontal–orthodontic decision-making in adult orthodontic care [15, 16]. However, evidence remains limited from North African oral health care settings, where orthodontic training pathways, access to periodontal specialty care, and referral practices may differ substantially. Moreover, few surveys have specifically quantified periodontal diagnostic knowledge or explored practitioner-level factors associated with ortho-periodontal management decisions. Therefore, the primary objective of this nationwide cross-sectional survey was to evaluate the knowledge, attitudes, and clinical practices of Moroccan dentists providing orthodontic care regarding the management of patients with periodontal diseases. Secondary objectives were to identify key periodontal–orthodontic domains requiring educational reinforcement and to explore practitioner- and practice-level factors associated with periodontitis-case management and KAP score performance.

Materials and methods

Study design

This was a nationwide, observational, cross-sectional Knowledge, Attitudes, and Practices (KAP) survey conducted among dentists providing orthodontic care in Morocco. No intervention, follow-up, randomization, split-mouth, crossover, or equivalence component was involved. This study was reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines for cross-sectional studies [17, 18]. The completed STROBE checklist was submitted as a supplementary file, and reporting was additionally cross-checked against the OHStat checklist for oral health research [19].

Participants and recruitment

Eligible participants were qualified dentists practicing in private, university, or public settings and providing orthodontic care exclusively or within general dental practice, with at least 1 year of professional experience. The anonymous self-administered questionnaire was distributed by email and professional social media groups. Eligible dentists were invited to complete the anonymous questionnaire based on their routine clinical practice and professional judgement. Participation was voluntary. Before accessing the questionnaire, participants were provided with information about the study objectives, the voluntary nature of participation, anonymity, and confidentiality. Completion and submission of the anonymous online questionnaire were considered to indicate implied informed consent to participate.

Questionnaire development, pilot testing and interrogative modalities

The questionnaire was developed from a review of the literature on the orthodontic–periodontal interface and KAP survey methodology. The draft instrument was reviewed by the study team for clinical relevance, coverage of key periodontal–orthodontic domains, and consistency with the study objectives. It was then pilot-tested among 30 practitioners to assess item clarity, comprehension, wording, response options, survey flow, and completion feasibility. Feedback from the pilot phase was discussed by the investigators and used to refine the wording and organization of the questionnaire. The instrument was therefore considered literature-informed and pilot-tested for face and content relevance; however, it was not designed or validated as a psychometric scale. Pilot data also informed the target sample size. Using a single-proportion formula, N = Z² × p × (1 − p) / d², with Z = 1.96, p = 50% because no prior national ortho-periodontal KAP estimate was available, and d = 5.7%, the minimum required sample was approximately 300 participants [20].

The questionnaire was developed and administered in French, which was the language used for data collection. The original French questionnaire and an English translation prepared for publication and peer-review purposes are provided as Additional file 1. The final questionnaire comprised 23 items covering demographic and professional characteristics, 8 knowledge items, and 9 attitude/practice items addressing periodontal diagnosis, screening, treatment timing, interdisciplinary collaboration, and periodontal maintenance. Single-choice, multiple-response, and graded-response formats were used. Conditional logic was applied to improve internal consistency, and potentially sensitive items were dispersed across the survey.

Survey platform and ethical considerations

Data were collected through LimeSurvey using anonymous response mode, with IP addresses and referrer links not stored. The study protocol was approved by the local Ethics Committee for Biomedical Research under reference number CERB 170 − 24.

Statistical analysis

The practitioner was the unit of analysis. The primary study variables were practitioners’ periodontal–orthodontic knowledge, attitudes, and practices, assessed through item-level questionnaire responses and composite Knowledge, Attitude, Practice, and total KAP scores. For regression analyses, the response variables were self-reported management of orthodontic treatment in patients with periodontitis and the Knowledge, Attitude, Practice, and total KAP scores. Explanatory variables included demographic and professional characteristics, orthodontic training, type and sector of practice, and selected periodontal–orthodontic knowledge and practice items. No follow-up was conducted because of the cross-sectional design. Because recruitment used an open voluntary online strategy without a probability sampling frame, no sampling weights or design-based analyses were applied.

All returned questionnaires, including partially completed ones, contributed to item-level descriptive analyses using an available-case approach; denominators corresponded to the number of non-missing responses for each item. Empty platform entries were excluded before analysis. A complete questionnaire was defined as a questionnaire with responses available for all required survey items, whereas a partial questionnaire included at least one valid non-empty response but did not include all required items. No imputation was performed for missing data. Questionnaire categories were analyzed as originally coded, and composite scores were calculated only when the required component data were available. Regression analyses were performed using observations with available data for the variables included in each model.

Composite scores were calculated to summarize responses across predefined periodontal–orthodontic domains. Each selected item was coded dichotomously, with 1 point assigned to the response considered aligned with current periodontal–orthodontic principles and 0 points assigned to all other responses. For single-choice items, the predefined correct or expected response was assigned 1 point. For practice-oriented items, 1 point was assigned to the response reflecting systematic, comprehensive, or guideline-consistent management, according to the scoring framework defined before analysis. No negative scoring was applied.

The Knowledge score ranged from 0 to 7 and included seven items covering the definition of reduced periodontium, the definition of a periodontitis case, radiographic confirmation of bone loss, periodontal stability before orthodontic treatment, timing after active periodontal therapy, center-of-resistance displacement in reduced periodontium, and tooth movement associated with bone gain. The Practice score ranged from 0 to 6 and included six items covering periodontal maintenance, disease screening before orthodontics, full-mouth probing, management of periodontitis cases, referral or co-management, and orthodontic retention in ortho-periodontal patients. The Attitude score ranged from 0 to 2 and included two items related to continuing education needs and periodontal maintenance during orthodontic treatment. The total KAP score ranged from 0 to 15 and was calculated as the sum of the Knowledge, Attitude, and Practice scores.

Composite Knowledge, Attitude, Practice, and total KAP scores were constructed as pragmatic summary indicators across predefined periodontal–orthodontic domains. Items were equally weighted because no validated weighting scheme was available for this specific ortho-periodontal KAP context. Higher scores indicated a greater number of responses aligned with evidence-based periodontal–orthodontic principles. These scores were used for descriptive and exploratory analytical purposes, rather than as validated latent psychometric constructs.

Analyses were performed using Jamovi 1.6.15.0. Categorical variables were reported as n (%). Continuous score variables were reported as mean ± standard deviation or median and interquartile range, according to distributional shape assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test and visual inspection of histograms. Group comparisons used the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables and Student’s t test or the Mann–Whitney U test for continuous score variables, as appropriate. A two-sided p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Exploratory logistic regression was performed to identify factors associated with self-reported management of orthodontic treatment in patients with periodontitis. Exploratory linear regression was used to identify factors associated with Knowledge, Attitude, Practice, and total KAP scores. Variables with p < 0.20 in univariable analyses were entered into the multivariable models. Regression results were reported as odds ratios or beta coefficients with 95% confidence intervals and p values.

Results

Descriptive statistics

Between September 2025 and February 2026, 418 platform entries were recorded; 118 blank entries were excluded. Overall, 300 practitioners from 11 Moroccan regions provided at least 1 valid response, including 190 complete questionnaires (Fig. 1). Thus, 110 of the 300 valid questionnaires were partially completed. These partial questionnaires were retained for item-level descriptive analyses when the relevant item response was available, whereas score-based and regression analyses used only observations with the required component data.

Pre-orthodontic periodontal screening was reported by 189 (63.0%) respondents, but systematic full-mouth 6-site probing by only 34 (16.5%). Among periodontitis cases, 91 (44.4%) respondents reported receiving formal confirmation of periodontal stability from a periodontist before orthodontic treatment, and 78 (35.9%) reported waiting 3 months after active periodontal therapy. Selection of 1- to 2-month periodontal maintenance intervals during active orthodontic treatment was reported by 224 (96.6%) respondents. Overall, 252 (84.0%) practitioners expressed a need for continuing education. Detailed item-level knowledge, attitude, and practice responses are presented in Table 2. In univariate analysis, all knowledge-related variables were significantly associated with higher knowledge scores. Correct knowledge of the definition of a reduced periodontium (β = 1.19; 95% CI, 0.56 to 1.81), the definition of a case of periodontitis (β = 1.30; 95% CI, 0.99 to 1.61), radiographic criteria for confirming bone loss (β = 1.17; 95% CI, 0.90 to 1.44), and key aspects of periodontal maintenance during orthodontic treatment (β = 1.79; 95% CI, 1.01 to 2.56) was associated with higher knowledge scores. Practitioners working in a university setting also had higher knowledge scores than those in private practice (β = 0.60; 95% CI, 0.18 to 1.04), whereas no significant difference was observed for those working in the public sector. In multivariable linear models, correct responses related to reduced periodontium, periodontitis definition, radiographic bone loss, and periodontal maintenance were associated with higher knowledge and total KAP scores, whereas no studied variable remained significantly associated with attitude or practice scores (Table 3).

Discussion

This cross-sectional study provides updated national data on the knowledge, attitudes, and clinical practices of Moroccan dentists providing orthodontic care for patients with periodontal diseases. The findings indicate suboptimal overall performance, characterized by gaps in core periodontal diagnostic knowledge, variable understanding of orthodontic biomechanics in reduced periodontium, and inconsistent interdisciplinary and peri-orthodontic management practices. These results are consistent with other surveys, which similarly report heterogeneity among orthodontists and general dentists when dealing with the periodontal dimension of orthodontic care [16, 21]. These findings extend previous European evidence, particularly the French survey by Saloux et al. [16], by shifting the focus from interprofessional communication, periodontal monitoring during adult orthodontic treatment, and perceived treatment failures to periodontal diagnostic competency, full-mouth periodontal probing, periodontal stability confirmation, and score-based KAP performance among orthodontic care providers in a North African setting.

The most prominent gap concerned periodontal diagnosis and assessment. Knowledge of periodontal diagnosis remained limited, with only 55 (18.3%) respondents correctly defining a case of periodontitis and 99 (46.3%) correctly identifying the radiographic criterion for bone loss. These findings align with previous surveys where practitioners showed limited theoretical comprehension of periodontal diagnosis [22]. However, periodontal examination practices were inconsistent: while 189 (63%) participants reported performing pre-orthodontic periodontal screening, only 33 (16.5%) performed systematic full-mouth 6-site probing. Comparable studies have documented similar inconsistencies, emphasizing that orthodontists often underutilize comprehensive periodontal screening despite acknowledging their importance [16, 23, 24]. Such gaps may compromise the early detection of inflammatory or destructive changes that could influence orthodontic planning.

The variability in recommended waiting periods before initiating orthodontic forces in previously treated periodontitis further illustrates uncertainty in clinical decision-making. Although periodontal wound healing and soft-tissue maturation follow relatively predictable timelines, multiple practitioner surveys report wide divergence in clinicians’ perceptions of when orthodontic loading becomes safe after active therapy [16, 25]. Biological evidence indicates that orthodontic forces initiate a controlled sterile inflammatory cascade driving coupled bone remodeling [26, 27]. In patients with reduced but stable periodontium, this remodeling remains feasible but requires verified periodontal stability, lighter force systems, and closer monitoring [25, 28, 29]. The heterogeneity observed in our sample suggests insufficient integration of these biological concepts into postgraduate orthodontic education.

The management of periodontally compromised patients during orthodontic treatment requires structured interdisciplinary collaboration, yet our findings indicate notable practice variability. Only 91 (44.4%) reported receiving formal confirmation of periodontal stability before initiating orthodontic treatment, indicating variability in interdisciplinary coordination. Comparable studies emphasize that communication between orthodontists and periodontists is often fragmented or informal, despite the evidence linking coordinated care to better outcomes in compromised or high-risk patients [16, 25]. This suggests an opportunity to improve standardized interdisciplinary referral pathways in Moroccan clinical practice.

The score-based analyses further reinforce the relevance of core periodontal domains in ortho-periodontal care. After adjustment, variables related to the definition of a reduced periodontium, the definition of a case of periodontitis, radiographic criteria for bone loss, and key aspects of periodontal maintenance remained significantly associated with the knowledge score, and the same domains were also significantly associated with the total KAP score. Taken together, these findings suggest that these domains represent fundamental competencies in ortho-periodontal care and may serve as priority targets for educational intervention. Such deficiencies are clinically important because incomplete periodontal knowledge may translate into under-recognition of treatment-related soft-tissue risks. Gingival recession has been reported in longitudinal and cross-sectional studies, with post-treatment prevalence reaching up to 41.7%, particularly in patients undergoing labial movement of the mandibular incisors or presenting with pre-existing periodontal compromise [30].

The comparative and multivariable analyses add an important interpretive layer. The type of basic orthodontic training was associated with selected knowledge and practice indicators, including periodontitis-case management, suggesting that training background may partly shape diagnostic understanding and clinical decision-making in ortho-periodontal care, although these exploratory findings should be interpreted cautiously because subgroup sizes were unequal. After multivariable adjustment, practitioners working in university settings achieved higher knowledge scores than those practicing in the private sector, which may reflect greater exposure to academic teaching, interdisciplinary collaboration, and evidence-based continuing professional development. In contrast, no variables remained independently associated with attitude or practice scores after adjustment, suggesting that these domains may be influenced by organizational, behavioural, or contextual factors beyond those measured in the present survey. Furthermore, comprehensive full-mouth periodontal probing remained independently associated with the management of patients with periodontitis, emphasizing the importance of thorough periodontal assessment as a fundamental component of clinical decision-making in ortho-periodontal care. Given the cross-sectional design, these findings should be interpreted as associations rather than causal relationships and may help inform future educational and implementation strategies.

These findings also have implications for adolescent orthodontic care, where crowding and eruptive changes may complicate oral hygiene and periodontal screening. Moroccan data showing destructive periodontal conditions among adolescents and young adults support the need for routine periodontal assessment in orthodontic pathways [7, 31, 32]. Although almost half of the respondents had fewer than four years of professional experience, exploratory analyses did not identify significant differences in periodontitis-case management according to age or years of practice. These findings suggest that educational needs are not confined to a particular career stage and support strengthening undergraduate education, postgraduate orthodontic training, and continuing professional development in periodontal assessment and interdisciplinary ortho-periodontal management [15].

Several limitations should be considered. The cross-sectional design precludes causal or temporal inference. Practices were self-reported and may not fully reflect chairside behavior or the quality and consistency with which periodontal examinations, including full-mouth six-site probing, are performed in routine clinical practice. Online voluntary recruitment may have introduced selection bias, limiting generalizability to all dentists providing orthodontic care in Morocco, particularly those less connected to professional networks or less interested in ortho-periodontal topics. In addition, because the survey used an open online dissemination strategy, the number of eligible practitioners who were reached by the invitation was unknown, and a conventional response rate could not be estimated. The questionnaire was literature-informed and pilot-tested for clarity, feasibility, and face/content relevance, but no formal psychometric validation was performed. Consequently, the Knowledge, Attitude, Practice, and total KAP scores should be interpreted as exploratory summary indicators rather than validated psychometric scales, which may limit direct comparison with other studies. Available-case analyses allowed partial questionnaires to contribute to item-level estimates and avoided excluding respondents who provided valid responses to specific items. However, this approach produced variable denominators across items and may have affected comparability between estimates. If questionnaire completion was related to respondent characteristics, interest in ortho-periodontal care, or knowledge level, missing data may have influenced some descriptive proportions and score-based analyses. The direction and magnitude of selection, self-report, and missing-data bias could not be fully quantified; if more engaged practitioners were more likely to participate or complete the questionnaire, guideline-concordant practices may have been overestimated.

Future research could build on these findings by extending the assessment of periodontal diagnostic competency among dentists providing orthodontic care. In addition to the core diagnostic concepts and clinical management domains evaluated in the present survey, future studies may incorporate the contemporary staging and grading classification of periodontitis, together with case-based clinical scenarios, to provide a more comprehensive evaluation of diagnostic reasoning, treatment planning, and interdisciplinary decision-making in ortho-periodontal care.

Taken together, these limitations and future research directions should be considered when interpreting the findings. Nevertheless, the present study provides one of the first nationwide assessments of ortho-periodontal knowledge, attitudes, and practices among Moroccan dentists providing orthodontic care. Its strengths include the nationwide recruitment of practitioners from diverse geographical regions and professional settings, providing a broad overview of current clinical perspectives in Morocco. The questionnaire was developed from the available literature, refined through pilot testing, and administered anonymously through the LimeSurvey platform, a widely used research survey system designed to support data protection and privacy principles, including GDPR-aligned data management features. Anonymous data collection reduced the potential for interviewer influence and encouraged candid participation. The analytical strategy combined item-level descriptive analyses, score-based comparisons, and multivariable modelling, allowing complementary evaluation of both specific clinical behaviours and overall KAP performance. Methodological transparency was further strengthened through explicit score construction, available-case reporting, and adherence to STROBE and OHStat reporting recommendations. Finally, the inclusion of both the original French questionnaire and its English translation as supplementary material enhances transparency, reproducibility, and facilitates comparison with future studies. Collectively, these features strengthen the methodological rigor, transparency, reproducibility, and practical relevance of the findings for identifying educational priorities and informing interdisciplinary ortho-periodontal care.

Conclusions

This nationwide cross-sectional survey identified important gaps in periodontal knowledge and variability in self-reported clinical practices related to the orthodontic management of patients with periodontal diseases among Moroccan dentists providing orthodontic care. These findings highlight opportunities to strengthen periodontal education, interdisciplinary collaboration, and evidence-based clinical training. Because the findings are based on self-reported practices from a voluntary cross-sectional survey, they should be interpreted cautiously and may not fully reflect actual clinical behavior.

Study of the Extended Yard Sale model of wealth distribution on Erdős-Rényi random networks Preprint: APS/123-QED
Nicolás Vazquez Von Bibow, Juan I. Perotti,

Abstract
Excessive wealth concentration can undermine economic and social development. Random Asset Exchange (RAE) models provide valuable tools to investigate this phenomenon. Assuming that economic systems may operate optimally near the critical point of a continuous phase transition, the Extended Yard Sale (EYS) model introduced by Boghosian et al. [Physica A 476, 15 (2017)] offers a compelling framework. This model captures the interplay between wealth redistribution and accumulation, exhibiting a continuous phase transition marked by a broad wealth distribution at criticality, separating a condensed phase—where a microscopic fraction of agents holds a macroscopic share of total wealth—from a distributed phase with a light-tailed wealth distribution. While the original EYS model assumes fully connected interactions, this work introduces and studies a networked variant where agents interact over Erdős-Rényi random networks. The analysis combines Monte Carlo simulations with Quenched Mean Field and Mean Field approximations, exploring a variety of interaction and taxation schemes. A scaling analysis shows that, although the networked model also undergoes a continuous phase transition, it leads to local wealth condensation rather than the global condensation found in the fully connected case. These results deepen our understanding of wealth dynamics in structured populations and may help inform the development of more effective economic and social policies.

I
Introduction

There is a concerning trend due to the increasing wealth concentration at a global scale, since it threats the fair competition among economic agents, potentially undermining economic growth
Piketty 2014
.
The question of whether there is an optimal level of wealth concentration is a subject of intense debate.

Borrowing methods from statistical mechanics, numerous Random Asset Exchange (RAE) models have been developed to study wealth distributions
Angle 1986
;
Ispolatov et al. 1998
;
Dragulescu and Yakovenko 2000
;
Yakovenko and Rosser 2009
;
Anirban Chakraborti and Abergel 2011a
;
Anirban Chakraborti and Abergel 2011b
;
Chakrabarti et al. 2013
;
Greenberg and Gao 2024
.
Notably, several of these models predict the emergence of a wealth-condensed state, in which a microscopic fraction of agents—referred to as oligarchs—accumulate a macroscopic share of the total wealth
Bouchaud and Mézard 2000
;
Boghosian et al. 2017
;
Boghosian et al. 2015
;
Francisco Cardoso et al. 2023
.
Wealth condensation is widely regarded as an undesirable outcome.
This becomes particularly evident in the theoretical limit where all wealth is concentrated in the hands of a single agent, effectively halting the economic activity.
Even at intermediate levels of condensation, the situation remains problematic, since wealthy agents may leverage their accumulated capital not necessarily through superior economic performance, but rather by securing privileged positions and opportunities within society.

The Yard Sale (YS) model
Chakraborti 2002
;
Hayes 2002
and its variants are particular cases of RAE models, in which the exchanged wealth during interactions is a fraction of the poorer’s agent.
YS models are among of the most widely used frameworks to study wealth distributions and wealth condensation.
Various extensions of the YS model have incorporated mechanisms of wealth redistribution
Boghosian 2014a
;
Boghosian 2014b
, inflation, production, taxation
Boghosian 2014b
;
Neñer et al. 2022
, debt
Li et al. 2019
, saving propensities
Chatterjee et al. 2004
, risk aversion
Iglesias et al. 2004
;
Neñer and Laguna 2021
, biases
Moukarzel et al. 2007
;
Boghosian et al. 2017
or rational behavior
Neñer and Laguna 2021
.
In particular, the recently introduced Extended YS (EYS) model by Boghosian et al.
Boghosian et al. 2017
;
Li et al. 2019
combines two biases, the mechanisms of wealth redistribution that favors the poor and Wealth Attained Advantage (WAA) that favors the rich during interactions.
Notably, the EYS model exhibits a critical redistribution rate at which a continuous phase transition emerges between a wealth-condensed phase and a disordered phase.
This is noteworthy because such a feature is characteristic of a wide range of complex systems
Bak 1996
;
Perotti et al. 2009
;
Chialvo 2010
;
Mora and Bialek 2011
;
Tang et al. 2017
;
Cavagna et al. 2018
;
Muñoz 2018
;
Li et al. 2019
;
Chialvo et al. 2020
;
Zamponi et al. 2022
.

Euler introduced the concept of graphs studying the problem of the seven bridges of Königsberg in 1736.
Most early works in physics considered regular graphs such as lattices or the Cayley tree.
In the 50’s Paul Erdős and Albert Rényi developed the theory of random graphs, which imitate the disorder observed in real networks.
Still, real networks are not regular, nor purelly random, but are complex.
This fact was noticed by Watts and Strogatz
Watts and Strogatz 1998
and Barabási and Albert
Barabási and Albert 1999
who respectively introduced the concepts of small-world and scale-free networks, giving birth to the modern theory of complex networks
Newman 2018
.

In real-world settings, social agents typically interact repeatedly with a relatively small set of neighbors rather than with the entire population
Onnela et al. 2007
. Nevertheless, most variants of the Yard Sale (YS) model assume fully connected interactions, where any pair of agents can trade, which is an unrealistic assumption.
Although some versions of the YS model have been adapted to networked settings
Vázquez-Montejo et al. 2010
;
Bustos-Guajardo and Moukarzel 2012
;
Lee and Lee 2023
, these remain comparatively less explored. Studying networked variants is crucial, as interaction structure can profoundly affect wealth dynamics.
For example, a recent preprint by Börgers and Greengard demonstrates that global wealth condensation does not occur in a broad class of networked variants of YS models, but it is replaced by a notion of local wealth condensation where wealthy agents cannot be neighbors
Börgers and Greengard 2024
.

In this work, a networked variant of the EYS model is introduced and analyzed through Monte Carlo (MC) simulations, Quenched Mean Field (QMF) approximations, and a Mean Field (MF) analytical approach.
In Section
II
, the original EYS model is revisited, and key concepts from network theory are reviewed.
The networked EYS model is then formulated, incorporating different taxation and interaction schemes, and the corresponding QMF approximation is developed.
In Section
III
, numerical results obtained from MC simulations and QMF approximations are presented for both fully connected and Erdős–Rényi random networks.
A scaling analysis is carried out to characterize the continuous phase transition, and the MF theory is subsequently introduced and examined.
Finally, the main conclusions are provided in Section
IV
.

II.1
The Extended Yard Sale model

In the Extended Yard Sale model (EYS)
Boghosian et al. 2017
,
N
agents exchange wealth following a stochastic process where
w_{i}(t)
denotes the wealth of agent
i
at time
t
.
The total wealth is conserved, so
W=\sum_{i}w_{i}(t)
for all
t
.
At each time step
t\to t+\Delta t
, two agents
i
and
j
are chosen at random to exchange wealth and pay or receive benefits from the tax system according to the rule

\displaystyle w_{i}(t+\Delta t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{i}(t)+\chi\Delta t\,\bigg(\frac{W}{N}-w_{i}(t)\bigg)

(1)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\gamma\Delta t}\,\eta_{ij}(t)\,\min\{w_{i}(t),w_{j}(t)\}

for agent
i
and an analogous rule for agent
j
.
The second term of Eq.
1
represents the social benefit earned minus the tax payed by agent
i
.
Its action redistributes wealth from the rich (
w_{i}>W/N
) to the poor (
w_{i}<W/N
) agents.
The third term represents the amount of wealth exchanged between
i
and
j
in the interaction.
By definition of the YS model, it is a fraction of the wealth of the poorer agent, so

-1\leq\sqrt{\gamma\Delta t}\,\eta_{ij}(t)\leq 1

(2)

must be satisfied.
The antisymmetric matrix
\eta(t)
is a stochastic variate of entries
\eta_{ij}(t)=-\eta_{ji}(t)\in\{-1,1\}
representing the direction of wealth flow.
Each upper-diagonal entry independently takes a value from a distribution with mean

\left\langle\eta_{ij}\right\rangle=\zeta N\sqrt{\frac{\Delta t}{\gamma}}\,\frac{w_{i}-w_{j}}{W}

(3)

and second moment

\left\langle\eta_{ij}^{2}\right\rangle=1
.
The bias implied by Eq.
3
is called the Wealth Attained Advantage (WAA) and always favors the richest agent of the transaction.

The motivations behind the redistribution and WAA terms are the following.
In the original YS model, which has no redistribution nor WAA, the state of the system always evolves towards a winner-takes-all absorbing state of wealth condensation where the total wealth is completely acquired by one agent at
t\to\infty
.
When the redistribution term is incorporated into the YS model, the condensed phase dissapears for any
\chi>0

Boghosian 2014b
.
The partially condensed phase at
t\to\infty
is recovered when the redistribution term is effectively counteracted by a sufficiently strong WAA in the EYS model
Boghosian et al. 2017
.
More specifically, wealth condensation emerges in the stationary regime when
\chi<\zeta
, where a microscopic fraction of agents owns a macroscopic fraction of the total wealth.
Otherwise, when
\chi>\zeta
, the distributed phase where no agent owns a macroscopic fraction of wealth is found.
Interestingly, the EYS model exhibits a continuous phase transition at the critical point
\chi=\zeta
, where a heavy-tailed distribution of wealth
P(w)=N^{-1}\sum_{i}\delta(w-w_{i})
emerges as
t\to\infty
.

Equation
3
is consistent with a distribution of probabilities

P(\eta_{ij})=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(1+\eta_{ij}\left\langle\eta_{ij}\right\rangle\bigg)

given that
-1\leq\left\langle\eta_{ij}\right\rangle\leq 1
.
This is consistent only if

\zeta N\sqrt{\frac{\Delta t}{\gamma}}\leq 1.

(4)

The conditions of Eqs.
2
and
4
are simultaneously satisfied if, for example,

\Delta t=\gamma N^{-2}
and
\zeta=1
.
The factor
N
in Eq.
3
is necessary in order to obtain finite critical points in the thermodynamic limit
N\to\infty
.

1

Many network models can be represented by a distribution of probabilities

For instance, in the case of Erdős-Rényi networks,

In some other cases, like in the Barabási-Albert network model, an explicit formula for the distribution

II.3
The EYS model on networks

To obtain a generalization of the EYS model on networks, consider a given adjacency matrix

\displaystyle+\sum_{j}c_{ij}(t)\sqrt{\gamma\Delta t}\,\eta_{ij}(t)\,\min\{w_{i}(t),w_{j}(t)\}.

Similarly, the symmetric matrix

The term

Interaction and taxation modes over networks

Different taxation and interaction modes can be considered.
Each interaction-taxation event is represented by a tuple

where the pair

denotes a directed link chosen from a network

representing an interaction initiated by an agent
u

and

represent the pair of agents that are being taxed.
Without loosing generality, at each event two agents are taxed simultaneously to unify different taxation modes within the same representation.
The outcomes of

and

Specifically,

Two interaction modes are considered.
In Interaction Mode A (IMA), a directed link

is chosen uniformly at random among the

This occurs with probability

\displaystyle\left\langle c_{ij}\right\rangle_{s|a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{uv}P(uv|a)\sum_{c_{ij}\in\{0,1\}}P(c_{ij}|uv)\,c_{ij}

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a_{ij}+a_{ji}}{M}.

In Interaction Mode B (IMB), an agent

is chosen uniformly at random and then another agent

is uniformly chosen at random among the

In this way,

so, by Eq.

\displaystyle\left\langle c_{ij}\right\rangle_{s|a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{uv}P(uv|a)\sum_{c_{ij}\in\{0,1\}}P(c_{ij}|uv)c_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{uv}\frac{1}{N}\frac{a_{uv}}{k_{u}}\big(\delta_{uv,ij}+\delta_{vu,ij}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\bigg(\frac{a_{ij}}{k_{i}}+\frac{a_{ji}}{k_{j}}\bigg).

For each interaction mode, two taxation modes are also considered.
In Taxation Mode A (TMA), the agents

and

Formally,

.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{uv}P(uv|a)\sum_{lm}P(l,m|uv)\sum_{b_{i}}P(b_{i}|l,m)b_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{uv}P(uv|a)\sum_{lm}\delta_{lu}\delta_{mv}\big(\delta_{il}+\delta_{im}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{uv}P(uv|a)\big(\delta_{iu}+\delta_{iv}\big).

Hence, under IMA, Eq.

reduces to

\displaystyle\left\langle b_{i}\right\rangle_{s|a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{uv}\frac{a_{uv}}{M}\big(\delta_{iu}+\delta_{iv}\big)

\displaystyle\frac{k_{i}+q_{i}}{M}.

On the other hand, under IMB, Eq.

reduces to

\displaystyle\left\langle b_{i}\right\rangle_{s|a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{uv}\frac{1}{N}\frac{a_{uv}}{k_{u}}\big(\delta_{iu}+\delta_{iv}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\big(1+h_{i}\big)\approx\frac{1}{N}+\frac{q_{i}}{M}

III.1
Fully connected networks

Let us begin with the case of fully connected networks to compare with previous results.
In this case,
k_{i}=q_{i}=N-1
, so
\kappa_{ij}=\left\langle c_{ij}\right\rangle_{s|a}=2N^{-1}(N-1)^{-1}

As is already known

\epsilon:=x_{1}-x_{1}^{*}
and write

\dot{\epsilon}=\dot{x}_{1}=f(x_{1}^{*}+\epsilon)=f(x_{1}^{*})+f^{\prime}(x_{1}^{*})\epsilon+...

where
f(x_{1})
is proportional to the r.h.s. of Eq.
11
.
Since
f(x_{1}^{*})=0
because
x_{1}^{*}
is an equilibrium solution, then the dynamics is stable if
f^{\prime}(x_{1}^{*})<0
, linearly marginal if
f^{\prime}(x_{1}^{*})=0
and unstable if
f^{\prime}(x_{1}^{*})>0
.
The condition for the marginal case can be used to identify the critical point at which the condensation phase transition occurs.
It predicts a critical temperature
T_{c}=N/(N-1)\approx 1
as
N\to\infty
, with
x_{1}^{*}(T=T_{c})=1/N
for both equilibrium solutions
x_{1}^{*}
.
However, the stability of these solutions differs: for
T<T_{c}
,
f^{\prime}(x_{1}^{*})>0
, and for
T>T_{c}
,
f^{\prime}(x_{1}^{*})<0
when
x_{1}^{*}=1/N
, while the reverse holds for the other equilibrium point.
As expected, these results indicate that above
T_{c}
, all agents share approximately equally the same wealth
W/N
, while, in contrast, below
T_{c}
, a single oligarch emerges, owning a macroscopic fraction of the total wealth
W
.

Also, discontinuous decays to smaller values of

This phenomenon is exclusively a topological effect, as it is not observed in the case of fully connected networks.
At small but non-zero temperatures, the highest values of

1/N

Interestingly, for ER networks, and at difference with the case of fully connected networks, the decay of
\left\langle x_{1}\right\rangle_{a}
vs

The numerical experiments show that, for all interaction and taxation modes, the value of
\left\langle x_{1}\right\rangle_{a}
grows with
\bar{k}
but decays with
N
at zero or small temperatures,
T\gtrsim 0
(not shown).
In other words, there is a competition between
\bar{k}
and
N
for consolidating or weakening the condensed phase, respectively.
To better characterize this behavior, assume the scaling hypothesis

\left\langle x_{1}\right\rangle_{a}(\lambda^{\nu}\bar{k},\lambda^{\mu}N)\approx\lambda\left\langle x_{1}\right\rangle_{a}(\bar{k},N)

for arbitrary values of
\lambda
,
\bar{k}
,
N
and zero or small temperatures.
Choosing the value of
\lambda
such that
\lambda^{\mu}N=1
, the scaling relation

\displaystyle\left\langle x_{1}\right\rangle_{a}(\bar{k},N)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle N^{1/\mu}\left\langle x_{1}\right\rangle_{a}(N^{-\nu/\mu}\bar{k},1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N^{1/\mu}g_{T}(N^{-\nu/\mu}\bar{k})

is obtained, for some universal function
g_{T}
that generally depends on
T
.
As shown in Fig.
3
, a collapse of the curves
N^{-1/\mu}\left\langle x_{1}\right\rangle_{a}(\bar{k},N)
vs
N^{-\nu/\mu}\bar{k}
is obtained for different system sizes
N
while varying
\bar{k}
.
By visual inspection, it is found that the scaling occurs for
\nu=\mu=\infty
such that
\nu/\mu=1
when
T=0
(panels
a)
and
b)
) and for
\nu=\mu=-4
when
T=0.06
(panels
c)
and
d)
).
In other words, the scaling

\displaystyle\left\langle x_{1}\right\rangle_{a}(\bar{k},N)\approx g_{T=0}(\bar{k}/N)\approx N^{-1}\bar{k}

is obtained at zero temperature, and the scaling

\displaystyle\left\langle x_{1}\right\rangle_{a}(\bar{k},N)\approx N^{-1/4}g_{T\gtrsim 0}(\bar{k}/N)

(13)

is obtained at the small but non-zero temperature
T=0.06
.
These results hold for MC simulations and QMF approximations.
Observe that if
\left\langle x_{1}\right\rangle_{a}=c
for some constant
0<c<1
, then Eq.
13
implies
\bar{k}/N\approx 1-c^{-4}/N
.
Consequently, for non-zero temperatures, a non-zero average
\left\langle x_{1}\right\rangle_{a}
in the limit
N\to\infty
is possible only for fully connected networks.
This result aligns with the findings of Börgers and Greengard
Börgers and Greengard 2024
, who argued that global condensation occurs at
T=0
exclusively for fully connected networks, while local condensation is observed otherwise.

For small but non-zero temperatures, we can characterize
g_{T}
by considering the limiting case of a fully connected network, where the average degree
\bar{k}=N-1
. In this limit, the relation

1\approx x_{1}\approx N^{-1/4}g_{T\gtrsim 0}\bigg(\frac{N-1}{N}\bigg)

holds as
N\to\infty
only if

g_{T\gtrsim 0}\bigg(\frac{N-1}{N}\bigg)\approx N^{1/4}.

Introducing the substitution
z=\frac{N-1}{N}
, this suggests the scaling

g_{T\gtrsim 0}(z)\approx\bigg(\frac{1}{1-z}\bigg)^{1/4},

(14)

which is visually confirmed by the dashed cyan line in Fig.
3
for
z
close to 1.
Note, however, at smaller values of
z
, the prediction of Eq.
14
fails.
A better fit is provided by the phenomenological proposition

g_{T\gtrsim 0}(p)\approx-\frac{1}{3}+p^{1/3}(1-p)^{-1/4}

across the entire range of
p\approx\bar{k}/N
, as confirmed by the solid red line of Fig.
3
except at very small values of
p
where the sparse network regime emerges.

A key observation in the stationary regime is the lack of connections among rich agents.
This can be intuitively understood as a consequence of competition: connected wealthy agents engage in interactions that ultimately lead to the financial ruin of one of them.
To analyze this phenomenon more formally, a Mean Field (MF) approximation is developed for the EYS model on ER random networks.
Namely, assume that
k_{i}=q_{i}\approx\bar{k}=\bar{q}
for all
i
, so
\left\langle\beta_{i}\right\rangle_{a}=\left\langle b_{i}\right\rangle_{sa}\approx 2/N
for TMA and TMB, and
\left\langle\kappa_{ij}\right\rangle_{a}=\left\langle c_{ij}\right\rangle_{sa}\approx 2a_{ij}/(\bar{k}N)
for IMA and IMB, so Eq.
8
takes the form

\frac{N}{2}\dot{x}_{i}\approx\frac{T}{N^{2}}(1-Nx_{i})+\sum_{j}\frac{a_{ij}}{\bar{k}}(x_{i}-x_{j})\min\{x_{i},x_{j}\}.

(15)

Next, assume that the population is divided between
R
rich agents with wealth
u\approx\left\langle x_{1}\right\rangle_{a}\approx...\approx\left\langle x_{R}\right\rangle_{a}
and
N-R
poor agents with wealth
v\approx\left\langle x_{R+1}\right\rangle_{a}\approx...\approx\left\langle x_{N}\right\rangle_{a}
, where
u>1/N>v
.
In this way, the total normalized wealth is
1=Ru+(N-R)v
and, from Eq.
15
, the following ordinary differential equation for the expected wealth of richs agent is obtained

\displaystyle\frac{N}{2}\dot{u}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{T}{N^{2}}(1-Nu)+(u-v)v

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-Nu)\bigg(\frac{T}{N^{2}}-\frac{1-Ru}{(N-R)^{2}}\bigg)

where the
k_{i}\approx\bar{k}
terms in the summation cancels out with the factor
1/\bar{k}
.
Here, it is important to remark that
R
is actually an unknown function of
T
,
N
,
\bar{k}
and
u
.
For a given
T
, the equilibrium condition
\dot{u}=0
derived from Eq.
16
reveals two solutions:
u^{*}=1/N
and

u^{*}=\frac{1}{R^{*}}\bigg(1-T\frac{(N-R^{*})^{2}}{N^{2}}\bigg)

(17)

where
R^{*}=R(T,N,\bar{k},u^{*})
.
Note, for
R^{*}=1
, Eqs.
16
and
17
simplify to those of the MF theory over fully connected networks.
The condition of marginal linear stability
\dot{u}=0
implies the critical temperature

T_{c}=N/(N-R^{*}).

for both values of
u^{*}=u(T,N,\bar{k},R^{*})
.
Additionally,
u^{*}=1/N
for
T=T_{c}
.
For
T>T_{c}
the fix point
u^{*}=1/N
is stable and the fix point of Eq.
17
is unstable, while the opposite holds for
T<T_{c}
. In other words, a condensensation phase transition occurs at
T_{c}
.
The stable equilibrium solution is
u^{*}=1/N
above
T_{c}
and is that of Eq.
17
below
T_{c}
.

Building on the previous considerations, let
R_{\max}
denote the maximum number of non-adjacent (i.e., mutually non-connected) rich agents that a network can sustain.
Determining
R_{\max}
is equivalent to finding a maximal independent set in the network
Dall’Asta et al. 2009
, and its value depends strongly on the network topology.
For example, in a one-dimensional ring, poor and rich agents can alternate, yielding
R_{\max}\approx N/2
.
In contrast, for a fully connected network, the only possible independent set consists of a single node, so
R_{\max}=1
.
In any case, the expected fraction of rich agents,
r:=R/N
, is bounded by
r\leq r_{\max}:=R_{\max}/N
.
In the high-temperature limit
T\to\infty
, this fraction approaches
r\to 1/2
, while in the dense network limit
\bar{k}\to N-1
, we have
r\to 0
.
For Erdős–Rényi networks,
R_{\max}
is expected to decrease with increasing link density
p\approx\bar{k}/N
. In particular, for
N\gg R\gg 1
and sufficiently large
p
, it satisfies the asymptotic relation
(
Janson et al. 2000
, Theorem 7.1)
:

R_{\max}\approx\frac{2\ln N}{\ln\left(1/(1-p)\right)}.

(18)

Based on this, it is natural to introduce the order parameter

\phi:=1-2r,

(19)

which captures the imbalance between poor and rich agents. By construction,
\phi\to 0
as
T\to\infty
, and
\phi\approx 1
in the low-temperature and high-connectivity limit
T\to 0
,
\bar{k}\to N-1
.
These predictions for
\phi
are corroborated by both Monte Carlo (MC) simulations and Quenched Mean Field (QMF) approximations, as shown in panels
a)
and
b)
of Fig.
5
.
In particular, panel
a)
reveals a crossover at a characteristic temperature
T=T_{c}
, which appears consistently across different values of
\bar{k}
.
Moreover, while the transition is continuous in MC simulations, it becomes discontinuous in the QMF approximation as
\bar{k}\to N-1
, likely due to the mean-field treatment.
The behavior of the time-averaged order parameter
\langle\phi\rangle_{a}
also reflects the interplay between connectivity
\bar{k}
and system size
N
.
For
T<T_{c}
,
\langle\phi\rangle_{a}
increases with
\bar{k}
and decreases slightly with
N
; the opposite trend is observed for
T>T_{c}
.
These trends are consistent with the approximation
r_{\max}\approx(2/\bar{k})\ln N
derived from Eq.
18
for
\bar{k}\ll N
and
T\approx 0
, which predicts a logarithmic growth with
N
and an inverse dependence on
\bar{k}
.

IV
Conclusion

This work introduces a networked variant of the Extended Yard Sale (EYS) model proposed by Boghosian et al.
Boghosian et al. 2017
.
The model is analyzed on Erdős-Rényi random networks, combining Monte Carlo simulations with two theoretical approximations: a Quenched Mean Field (QMF) approximation, which significantly reduces computational complexity, and a Mean Field (MF) theory that emphasizes the role of the number of rich agents in the system’s dynamics.

The model supports various interaction and taxation schemes, some of which display markedly different behaviors.
Despite these differences, a scaling analysis reveals that none of the variants exhibit global wealth condensation.
Instead, all variants show a form of local wealth condensation, as described by Börgers and Greengard
Börgers and Greengard 2024
, even in the presence of wealth redistribution mechanisms. Furthermore, consistent with the original EYS model on fully connected networks, all variants undergo a continuous phase transition when the competing effects of wealth redistribution and Wealth Attained Advantage (WAA) reach a balance.

This work advances the broader understanding of Random Asset Exchange (RAE) models.
Under the hypothesis that economic systems may operate optimally near the critical point of a continuous phase transition, the results may inform the development of more effective redistribution policies.
Future research could extend the analysis to more realistic network topologies–such as scale-free, small-world, or higher-order networks
Perotti 2025
–to test the robustness and universality of the observed phenomena.

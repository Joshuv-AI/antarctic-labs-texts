Integrated pan-cancer analysis and experimental verification of the roles of ZBED3 in kidney renal clear cell carcinoma
Authors: Shaobo Wu, Min Yang, Qiurong Zhao, Cheng Zhang, Xiaohe Luo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The present study investigated the Zinc finger BED-type containing 3 (ZBED3) mRNA expression levels, diagnostic and prognostic values, co-expressed and differentially expressed genes, immune infiltration, and functional enrichment analysis in patients with kidney renal clear cell carcinoma (KIRC) using various public databases such as The Cancer Genome Atlas, Genotype-Tissue Expression Project, LinkedOmics, Gene Set Enrichment Analysis databases, and the Xiantao academic tool. Through extensive data mining, our study revealed differential expression of ZBED3 in various tumor and paired normal tissues. Specifically, ZBED3 expression was found to be significantly decreased in KIRC, particularly in advanced pathologic stages and histologic grades, when compared to corresponding control tissues. Our analysis revealed that the overexpression of ZBED3 is associated with a favorable overall survival outcome in KIRC patients. Moreover, functional enrichment analysis identified key pathways related to inflammation response and DNA methylation of ZBED3 in KIRC. Additionally, our findings suggest that the upregulation of ZBED3 leads to the inhibition of cell proliferation by modulating cell-cycle progression in KIRC cell lines. Taken together, these results suggest that ZBED3 may serve as a promising biomarker and prognostic indicator for individuals diagnosed with KIRC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-77453-z.

Introduction

Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC) is a prevalent malignancy of the urinary system1, representing 2–3% of all adult malignant tumors and demonstrating a rising incidence in recent years, particularly among younger patients or those with other malignancies2–4. RCC encompasses various pathological subtypes, including clear cell carcinoma, papillary renal cell carcinoma, chromophobic renal cell carcinoma, and Bellini collecting duct carcinoma5. Among these subtypes, Kidney renal clear cell carcinoma (KIRC) is the most prevalent, constituting approximately 85% of all kidney cancer cases6,7. Nevertheless, the inconspicuous positioning of the kidney results in elusive early clinical manifestations of kidney cancer, making detection challenging and often leading to diagnosis in advanced stages8. The American Joint Committee on Renal Cancer Staging indicates a significantly higher 5-year survival rate of approximately 98% for patients with Stage I RCC compared to a mere 5% for those with stage III RCC. Consequently, timely identification and appropriate intervention are imperative. Radical surgical resection remains the primary treatment modality for RCC. Recurrence and metastasis of local RCC have been documented in 30-40% of patients following radical surgical intervention1,9. The precise etiology of renal cancer remains elusive, and there is a notable deficiency in reliable diagnostic and prognostic markers10. Consequently, a comprehensive investigation into the signaling pathways and molecular mechanisms underlying the pathogenesis of RCC holds promise for the identification of novel research avenues and therapeutic targets for the early detection and management of this disease.

Zinc finger BED-type containing 3 (ZBED3) is a recently identified gene with a molecular weight of approximately 25kD and consisting of 234 amino acids. This protein, like other zinc finger proteins, contains a zinc finger BED structure that facilitates DNA and protein interactions11. Current research suggests that ZBED3 may modulate the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, thereby influencing invasion and metastasis in non-small cell lung cancer12,13. The expression and biological function of ZBED3 in renal carcinoma have not yet been explored in the academic literature. Thus, the aim of this study is to examine the potential role of ZBED3 in the pathogenesis and progression of renal cancer, as well as its underlying regulatory molecular mechanisms. This investigation seeks to establish a novel experimental foundation and theoretical framework for the early detection and therapeutic intervention of renal cancer.

Utilizing data from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) and the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), this study aims to elucidate the relationship between ZBED3 gene expression, clinical parameters, and overall survival (OS) outcomes across various types of cancer. Furthermore, we conducted ZBED3 co-expression gene enrichment analysis and validated it through molecular experiments on KIRC cell lines, specifically 786-O cell lines, which are human renal cell carcinoma cells. Our findings suggest that ZBED3 has potential applications as a predictive target for diagnosing and prognosing KIRC, as well as a potential role in inhibiting proliferation and migration through in vitro experiments.

Results

Tissue‑specific expression patten of ZBED3 in pan‑cancer

In order to investigate the mRNA expression of ZBED3 across various cancer types, an analysis was conducted utilizing RNA-seq data from the TCGA and GTEx databases. Significant differences in ZBED3 expression were observed in 33 cancer types, with the exception of those lacking normal tissue data. Specifically, ZBED3 expression was found to be significantly downregulated (p < 0.05) in BLCA (bladder urothelial carcinoma), BRCA (breast invasive carcinoma), CESC (cervical squamous cell carcinoma and endocervical adenocarcinoma), HNSC (head and neck squamous cell carcinoma), KIRC (kidney renal clear cell carcinoma), LUAD (lung adenocarcinoma), LUSC (lung squamous cell carcinoma), PAAD (pancreatic adenocarcinoma), TGCT (testicular germ cell tumors), THCA (thyroid carcinoma), and UCEC (uterine corpus endometrial carcinoma), UCS (Uterine carcinosarcoma) compared to control tissues, while it was upregulated in COAD (colon adenocarcinoma), DLBC (lymphoid neoplasm diffuse large B-cell lymphoma), ESCA (esophageal carcinoma), GBM (glioblastoma multiforme), KIRP (kidney renal papillary cell carcinoma), LAML (acute myeloid leukemia), LGG (brain lower grade glioma), LIHC (liver hepatocellular carcinoma), READ (rectum adenocarcinoma), SKCM (skin cutaneous melanoma), STAD (stomach adenocarcinoma), THYM (thymoma). However, no significant difference in ZBED3 mRNA levels was observed in ACC (adrenalcortical carcinoma), CHOL (cholangiocarcinoma), KICH (kidney chromophobe), MESO (mesothelioma), OV (ovarian serous cystadenocarcinoma), PCPG (pheochromocytoma and paraganglioma), PRAD (prostate adenocarcinoma), SARC (sarcoma) and UVM (uveal melanoma) when compared to their normal counterparts (see Fig. 1A). These findings suggest that ZBED3 exhibits differential expression patterns in tumor tissues and paired normal tissues in BLCA, BRCA, COAD, KICH, KIRC, LIHC, LUAD, LUSC, THCA (see Fig. 1B) based on the analysis of the TCGA dataset.

Multifaceted prognostic and diagnostic value of ZBED3 expression in pan-cancer

ROC curves were utilized to evaluate the diagnostic effectiveness of ZBED3 across eight distinct cancer types. ZBED3 demonstrated varying levels of accuracy in predicting BLCA (AUC = 0.695), BRCA (AUC = 0.826), KICH (AUC = 0.864), KIRC (AUC = 0.708), LIHC (AUC = 0.744), LUAD (AUC = 0.812), THCA (AUC = 0.781), and LUSC (AUC = 0.908) (see Fig. 2A-H). Moreover, a comprehensive analysis was conducted to determine the prognostic significance of ZBED3 across various cancer types. Utilizing the KM (Kaplan-Meier) survival curve and Cox proportional hazards model, the relationship between ZBED3 expression and patient survival was examined. The results indicated that ZBED3 expression did not exhibit a significant correlation with overall survival (OS) in BLCA, BRCA, KICH, LIHC, LUAD, LUSC, and THCA (see Fig. 2I-P). Conversely, in the case of KIRC, high ZBED3 expression was associated with a favorable prognosis for OS (p < 0.001, HR = 0.44). Significantly, patients with low ZBED3 expression exhibit a poorer prognosis compared to those with high ZBED3 expression in cases of KIRC.

ZBED3 was an independent prognostic factor for KIRC patients

Further validation of ZBED3 as a potential prognostic factor in KIRC patients was conducted using data from the TCGA dataset. Univariate Cox regression analysis indicated that ZBED3 expression (high vs. low, HR = 0.444, 95% CI: 0.325–0.608, P < 0.001), age ( < = 60 vs. >60, HR = 0.558, 95% CI: 0.411–0.758, P < 0.001), and pathologic M stage (M1 vs. M0, HR = 4.401, 95% CI: 3.226–6.002, P < 0.001) were significantly associated with overall survival (Fig. 3A). Multivariate Cox regression analysis demonstrated that ZBED3 expression (HR = 0.426, 95% CI: 0.267–0.680, P < 0.001), age (HR = 0.602, 95% CI: 0.390–0.930, P = 0.022), and pathologic M stage (HR = 3.183, 95% CI: 1.907–5.313, P < 0.001) were identified as independent prognostic factors for overall survival (OS) in patients with KIRC (Fig. 3B).

ZBED3 mRNA expression and clinical features in KIRC

The correlation between ZBED3 mRNA expression and the pathologic stage of KIRC patients was further investigated using the Xiantao academic online tool. The findings indicate a significant decrease in ZBED3 expression in patients with pathologic stages I, II, III, IV (Fig. 3C) or histologic grades G1, G2, G3, G4 (Fig. 3D) compared to those with normal stage (p < 0.001). Additionally, the expression of ZBED3 was found to be associated with T stage, N stage, and M stage (p < 0.001) as depicted in Fig. 3E–G. In deceased patients, ZBED3 expression was notably lower than in surviving patients (Fig. 3H). The clinicopathological characteristics of KIRC patients can be found in Supplementary File.

ZBED3-coexpressed gene analysis in patients with KIRC

The co-expression of genes with ZBED3 in KIRC patients from the TCGA dataset was further examined using LinkedOmi to explore the underlying mechanisms of ZBED3 in KIRC. The findings revealed that 11,979 co-expressed genes exhibited significant correlation with ZBED3 in KIRC (FDR < 0.05, P < 0.05) (Fig. 4A, Supplementary data S1). Among these genes, 4,856 were positively correlated with ZBED3 expression, while 7,123 were negatively correlated. Furthermore, a total of 6,093 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified between ZBED3high and ZBED3low groups in KIRC, with 96 upregulated genes and 5,997 downregulated genes in the ZBED3high group (P < 0.05, |log2 FC|≥1), as illustrated in Fig. 4B and Supplementary data S2. In comparing the 6093 DEGs of ZBED3 with the 11,979 significantly co-expressed genes previously mentioned by the Draw Venn diagrams online tool, a total of 553 overlapping genes were identified, comprising 19 positively upregulated genes and 534 negatively downregulated genes (Fig. 4C, Supplementary data S3).

Functional enrichment analysis of ZBED3 in KIRC

These overlapping genes were further subjected to functional analysis. GO/KEGG analyses were conducted using the Xiantao academic tool, with the top 3 enriched sets presented in Fig. 4D (All enriched sets were shown in Supplementary data S4). The results of the enrichment analysis indicated that ZBED3 and its associated partners serve as functional mediators in determining cell fate commitment, detection of chemical stimulus involved in sensory perception, keratinization, cornified envelope, keratin filament, intermediate filament cytoskeleton, signaling receptor activator activity, receptor activity, cytokine activity, IL-17 signaling pathway, olfactory transduction, and cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction. Furthermore, the gene expression heat map in Fig. 4E displayed the top 10 genes with aberrant expression levels (logFC > 2 and padi < 0.01), while Fig. 4F illustrated the correlation relationship between these top 10 genes (RTL1, SFTPB, KLK1, FOXI1, RPL13AP17, PAEP, SSX1, SERPINB4, KRTAP5-8, HMGA2) and ZBED3. The bubble chart in Fig. 4G demonstrated that ZBED3 expression had a median positive correlation with Tcm and a median negative correlation with NK CD56bright cells, cytotoxic cells among 24 distinct immune subtypes in KIRC.

Further investigation into the molecular pathways that were significantly changed in KIRC between ZBED3high and ZBED3low groups was conducted using GSEA data. The analysis revealed that ZBED3 primarily had a negative regulatory effect on processes or pathways such as DNA Methylation, DNA Damage telomere stress induced senescence, Inhibition of DNA recombination at telomere, Disease of programmed cell death, Formation of the beta catenin tcf transactivating complex, and Apoptosis induced DNA fragmentation (Fig. 5A-F).

ZBED3 inhibits cell proliferation and migration in vitro

We investigated the biological function of ZBED3 in the progression of KIRC in vitro by establishing stable KIRC cell lines 786-O with either overexpression or downregulation of ZBED3 through lentivirus infection. The efficacy of lentivirus was confirmed using qRT-PCR, which showed a noticeable increase in ZBED3 mRNA levels in LV5-ZBED3 compared to LV5-NC (Fig. 8A), and a decrease in LV3-shZBED3 compared to LV3-NC (Supplementary File) in 786-O cells post lentivirus infection. Additionally, the impact of ZBED3 on cell proliferation was assessed. The findings from the wound healing assay indicated that ZBED3 overexpression significantly inhibited wound healing (Fig. 6A). The findings from the transwell experiment demonstrated that the overexpression of ZBED3 had a significant inhibitory effect on cell migration, as illustrated in Fig. 6B. Additionally, the results of the CCK-8 assay indicated that the upregulation of ZBED3 led to a reduction in cell proliferation, as shown in Fig. 6D. To further investigate the observed growth inhibition resulting from ZBED3 overexpression, the cell-cycle profiles of ZBED3 overexpressed cells were compared to controls using flow cytometry. The upregulation of ZBED3 was associated with an increase in the proportion of cells in the G1-phase and a decrease in the percentage of cells in the G2/M phase, as depicted in Fig. 6C. As depicted in Fig. 6(E-H), the down-regulation of ZBED3 resulted in contrasting outcomes in cell proliferation and cell cycle compared to the effects of overexpression. Collectively, these findings suggest that ZBED3 plays an antioncogenic role in KIRC cells by inhibiting cellular proliferation and inducing cell cycle arrest.

ZBED3 regulated mitochondrial respiration in 786-O cells

To validate the functional significance of ZBED3 in cellular metabolism regulation, additional experiments were conducted using the Seahorse Cell Energy Phenotype test to assess the OCR and ECAR in response to ZBED3 overexpression or down-expression in 786-O cells. At the outset, the ZBED3 overexpressed groups displayed a notable elevation in OCR levels compared to the control group, while there was no significant variance in ECAR levels between the aforementioned groups (Fig. 7A, B). Following exposure to stressors (i.e. after FCCP and oligomycin), both OCR and ECAR exhibited a slight increase in the ZBED3 overexpressed groups in comparison to the control group, albeit without statistical significance. The data presented in Fig. 7C and D did not demonstrate a significant disparity in the XF cell energy phenotype and metabolic potential between the two groups.

In the ZBED3 down-regulated groups, a significant decrease in OCR was observed at baseline and under stressed conditions compared to the control group (Fig. 7E). Conversely, there was no significant difference in ECAR at baseline or under stressed conditions between the ZBED3 down-regulated and control groups (Fig. 7F). Additionally, data in Fig. 7G and H indicated no significant variance in the XF cell energy phenotype and metabolic potential between the two groups. These findings suggest that while ZBED3 may impact mitochondrial respiration, it does not appear to regulate glycolysis in 786-O cells.

ZBED3 overexpression regulated WNT5α signaling pathwayin vitro

Subsequent investigations delved deeper into the molecular signaling effects of ZBED3 overexpression on tumor cellular proliferation and cell cycle arrest. Analysis of qRT-PCR data revealed a decrease in mRNA levels of CD44, C-MYC, and CyclinD1 following ZBED3 overexpression compared to control groups. Conversely, the mRNA levels of CDC2, CDK4, CDK6, CyclinE1, and RB1 were found to be elevated in 786-O cells (Fig. 8A). Additionally, Western blot analysis demonstrated a significant increase in AXIN1 and β-catenin expression after ZBED3 overexpression in 786-O cells, while WNT5α and GSK3β showed a decrease in expression levels but no significance (Fig. 8B). The expression of ZBED3 mitigates the effects on cell proliferation and migration induced by its knockdown in vitro

To elucidate the pivotal role of ZBED3 in the progression of KIRC, we subjected 786-O cells, previously infected with LV3-shZBED3, to treatment with a ZBED3 overexpression adenovirus to restore ZBED3 expression. Subsequently, we examined whether the effects induced by ZBED3 inhibition could be counteracted. The influence of ZBED3 on cell proliferation was evaluated. Results from the wound healing assay demonstrated that ZBED3 overexpression significantly mitigated the enhanced wound healing observed following ZBED3 knockdown (Fig. 9A). The results from the transwell experiment indicated that the overexpression of ZBED3 significantly inhibited cell migration, as depicted in Fig. 9B. Furthermore, the CCK-8 assay results demonstrated that the upregulation of ZBED3 resulted in decreased cell proliferation, as illustrated in Fig. 9C. qRT-PCR analysis revealed an increase in the mRNA levels of CD44, C-MYC, and CyclinD1 following ZBED3 overexpression compared to control groups. In contrast, the mRNA levels of CDC2, CDK4, CDK6, CyclinE1, and RB1 were found to be elevated in 786-O cells (Fig. 9D). Furthermore, Western blot analysis revealed a substantial upregulation of AXIN1, GSK3β, and β-catenin expression following ZBED3 overexpression in 786-O cells. Conversely, WNT5α and phosphorylated β-catenin (p-β-catenin) exhibited a reduction in expression levels, although this decrease was not statistically significant (Fig. 9E). These findings suggest that ZBED3 may play a pivotal role in the progression of KIRC.

Discussion

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is a prevalent tumor and a significant cause of malignancy in young patients3,5,6. Our analysis focused on the expression, diagnosis, prognosis, and immune infiltration of ZBED3 as a biomarker in various cancer tissues, particularly in KIRC. Initial observations revealed a decrease in ZBED3 expression in both TCGA and GTEx-KIRC datasets. ZBED3 demonstrated decreased expression across various cancer types, suggesting its potential as a notable antioncogenic factor. Notably, ZBED3 exhibited the most diagnostic efficacy in LUSC, with high ZBED3 expression correlating with favorable prognosis in KIRC. Additionally, associations between ZBED3 expression and clinical characteristics in KIRC, including tumor grade, stage, and overall survival event, were observed. Univariate and multivariate cox regression analyses confirmed ZBED3 as an independent prognostic indicator for KIRC patients. Nevertheless, additional research is warranted to validate these results, given that the data used in this study were limited. Furthermore, our study has shown that the overexpression of ZBED3 suppresses the proliferative viability of 786-O cells, indicating its role as a tumor-inhibiting gene in KIRC.

It is worth noting that ZBED3 is also implicated in the pathogenesis of multiple cancer types, with a particular emphasis on lung cancer12–14. Our findings reveal a significant upregulation of ZBED3 in tumor tissues compared to normal paired tissues, although this contradicts previous research, potentially due to variations in histological subtypes of tumors. Additionally, ZBED3 has been linked to several clinical parameters in lung cancer, such as heightened Ki67 expression, progressed TNM stages, increased lymph node metastasis, and adverse clinical outcomes14. ZBED3 facilitates the proliferation of lung cancer cells through the modulation of proliferating cell nuclear antigen expression12. A prior investigation revealed that the upregulation of ZBED3 could antagonize the tumor-inhibiting impact of NSUN5 (RNA 5-methylcytosine writer) in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), thus offering additional evidence of ZBED3’s role in driving HCC progression15. In the context of gastric cancer, ZBED3 may potentially serve as a tumor suppressor by virtue of its overexpression, which has been observed to mitigate the malignant phenotype. These divergent observations underscore the necessity for further investigation into the specific roles of ZBED3 across various cancer types.

Presently, the prognosis of patients with KIRC relies primarily on clinical and histological factors, including lymph node involvement, disease pathology, and histological grade16,17. Nevertheless, there exists a gap in the literature regarding the association of the ZBED3 gene with immune cells in KIRC. Thus, our research examined the correlation between ZBED3 expression in KIRC and 24 distinct immune cell subtypes. Our results demonstrate a significant and consistent connection between the level of ZBED3 gene expression and the infiltration levels of Tcm, Eosinophils, Th17 cells, Neutrophils, mast cells macrophages, and Tgd in KIRC, suggesting potential implications for further exploration of ZBED3 expression in KIRC immunotherapy. Additionally, our GO/KEGG and GSEA analysis revealed that the primary function of ZBED3 in KIRC is associated with the negative regulation of DNA methylation. Numerous studies have explored the association between DNA methylation in genes and carcinogenesis in RCC18.

The regulation of cell cycle and proliferation is intricately governed by cyclins and cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs), which form complexes to activate CDKs and phosphorylate target proteins like the tumor suppressor protein Rb19. CD44 is a hallmark gene involved in cell adhesion and migration, and it is closely associated with tumor metastasis. C-MYC is a well-established proto-oncogene that plays a critical role in the carcinogenesis of various cancers. Cyclin D1 plays a crucial role in the regulation of the cell cycle, and its dysregulated expression is strongly associated with tumor proliferation. Previous research has indicated that the downregulation of cyclins, c-myc, and CD44 may play a role in modulating the cell cycle and inducing cell cycle arrest, which is concordant with our results20,21. ZBED3 plays a crucial role in the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway by binding AXIN22, as evidenced by our findings demonstrating that the overexpression of ZBED3 leads to increased levels of AXIN1 and β-catenin proteins. This observation is consistent with previous research indicating that ZBED3 is essential for the regulation of neuronal layer fates in brain development via the Wnt/β-catenin pathway23. However, a separate study has suggested that ZBED3 may have a negative regulatory effect on the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, thereby promoting adipogenic differentiation of adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells24. Thus, further research is required to explore the conflicting functions of ZBED3 in regulating the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway.

In this study, we employed the Chi-square test for data analysis, a method commonly utilized in comparable research. Nonetheless, we acknowledge that standard statistical techniques may exhibit limitations when addressing potential confounders, interactions, and nonlinear relationships. Firstly, the presence of confounding factors can introduce bias into the interpretation of results. Although we endeavored to control for known confounding variables during the study design phase, there remains the possibility of unidentified confounders. Future research endeavors could benefit from the utilization of more sophisticated analytical models, such as multilevel models or structural equation models, to enhance the control over potential confounding variables. Additionally, it is important to note that the majority of statistical techniques employed in the current study presuppose a linear relationship between variables. However, empirical data often exhibit non-linear relationships, which could influence the interpretation of the findings. To address this issue, future research should explore the application of nonlinear regression techniques or machine learning methodologies, such as random forests and support vector machines, to effectively capture nonlinear characteristics within the data. Furthermore, the lack of interaction analysis may result in the oversight of intricate relationships among specific subgroups or variables. Despite these methodological limitations, we contend that the findings of this study offer significant insights into its conclusions.

While our study identified the potential role of ZBED3 in KIRC through bioinformatic analysis and in vitro experiments, it is important to acknowledge several limitations, including sample size, diversity, comprehensive biological mechanism analysis, experimental validation, and clinical relevance. Initially, although data from the TCGA and GEO databases were utilized to conduct a preliminary analysis of the expression and function of ZBED3 in KIRC, these datasets are constrained by limited sample size and diversity. The current dataset, predominantly derived from a restricted range of ethnic groups and geographical locations, does not adequately capture the diversity of patients with KIRC. Future research endeavors will involve acquiring additional clinical samples through multi-center collaborations to enhance sample size and data diversity, thereby enabling a more comprehensive validation of our findings. Furthermore, this study primarily emphasizes biogenic analysis and in vitro cell experiments, lacking an extensive exploration of the specific molecular mechanisms related to ZBED3. Although we have identified the gene networks and signaling pathways associated with ZBED3, further investigation is required to elucidate its precise molecular functions. Future research endeavors will encompass protein interaction analysis, validation of downstream signaling pathways, and in vivo experimentation, including the use of nude mouse tumor models and patient-derived xenotransplantation models, to further elucidate the mechanism of action of ZBED3 in tumor progression and metastasis. Moreover, our current experimental validation predominantly relies on in vitro cell line studies, which do not adequately capture the complexity of the in vivo environment and the heterogeneity inherent in KIRC. In subsequent research, we intend to perform in vivo experiments and employ multiplex analysis alongside single-cell sequencing techniques to elucidate the function and mechanisms of ZBED3 across various cell populations, thereby providing a more comprehensive understanding of its biological behavior in KIRC. Although we have explored the potential of ZBED3 as a diagnostic marker and prognostic indicator, our study was limited by insufficient clinical data to substantiate these findings and thoroughly assess its applicability in clinical therapy. In future research endeavors, we intend to substantiate the clinical significance of ZBED3 by conducting extensive analyses of large-scale clinical datasets, as well as investigating its feasibility as a therapeutic target and its potential utility in combination therapies. In summary, while our study offers preliminary evidence regarding the potential role of ZBED3 in KIRC, additional experimental validation and clinical data are imperative. Future studies will focus on elucidating the specific mechanisms and clinical applicability of ZBED3 in KIRC through multicenter collaborations, in vivo experiments, and comprehensive large-scale clinical data analyses.

In conclusion, our research has revealed that ZBED3 functions to inhibit the proliferation of KIRC cells through modulation of the AXIN1/β-catenin signaling pathway, suggesting its potential utility as a prognostic indicator for KIRC patients. These findings present ZBED3 as a promising therapeutic target for the treatment of KIRC.

Materials and methods

Acquisition of public databases and analysis of ZBED3 expression in pan-cancer

The analysis utilized TCGA gene expression RNA-seq data, which includes 9807 TCGA tumor tissues and 727 TCGA normal tissues from 33 different types of cancer, as well as GTEx gene expression RNA-seq data, which contains 7568 normal tissues25. A total of 1404 cases were effectively paired within the sample set. The RNAseq data underwent conversion from FPKM (fragments per kilobase per million) format to TPM (transcripts per million reads) format and were subsequently log2-transformed, with the exclusion of missing and duplicated values. Data processing was carried out using Xiantao academic tools (https://www.xiantaozi.com/products), a valuable bioinformatics analysis web tool utilized for data visualization. Statistical analysis was conducted through the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, with significance determined at a p value < 0.05.

Analysis of the prognostic and diagnostic value of ZBED3 in pan-cancer

ZBED3 expression levels were assessed for their potential diagnostic and prognostic utility in cancer using RNA sequencing data sourced from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). The Xiantao academic tool was employed to conduct statistical analysis, specifically utilizing the log-rank test with a significance threshold set at p < 0.05. Subsequent visualization of the data was achieved through the generation of a forest plot using the Xiantao tool, which included hazard ratios (HR), 95% confidence intervals (CI), and corresponding p-values. Additionally, the diagnostic value of ZBED3 in various cancer types was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and calculation of the area under the curve (AUC) with the Xiantao tool. The AUC values were categorized as indicative of low accuracy (0.5–0.7), moderate accuracy (0.7–0.9), or high accuracy (> 0.9) in diagnosing cancer across different tissue types.

Co-expression genes and differentially expressed genes (DEGs) analysis

The LinkedOmics database (http://www.linkedomics.orglogin.php)26 was utilized to identify co-expressed genes associated with ZBED3 expression in the RNAseq data of KIRC patients from the TCGA cohort. The Pearson correlation coefficient was computed, and a volcano plot of the co-expressed genes was generated using the LinkedOmics website. The co-expression results between ZBED3 and immune-related genes were visualized as a heatmap, created with the Limma package in R 3.6.3. Patients with KIRC from the TCGA dataset were stratified into ZBED3low and ZBED3high groups based on the median values of ZBED3 mRNA from the RNAseq data. The Limma package in R version 3.6.3 was utilized to conduct a screening and visualization of volcano plots depicting differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the ZBED3low and ZBED3high groups of patients diagnosed with KIRC. Subsequently, Venn diagrams were generated using the Xiantao academic tool to identify the common genes shared between the DEGs and co-expressed genes for further enrichment analysis.

Functional enrichment and immune correlation

Functional enrichment analyses, including Gene Ontology (GO), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways, and tissue enrichment analysis, were carried out using the Xiantao academic tool on the identified overlapping genes. Additionally, Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was performed on the overlapping genes to identify KIRC-related enriched signaling pathways using the Xiantao academic tool. The bubble chart of ZBED3 expression and immune correlation was analyzed and visualized by Xiantao academic tool.

Cell lines and cell culture

The 786-O human renal clear cell carcinoma cells were obtained from the Shanghai Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences in Shanghai, China. The cells were maintained in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. Cell cultures were grown in T75 flasks and incubated at 37 ◦C in a humidified atmosphere containing 95% air and 5% CO2. Cells were either passaged or utilized for experiments upon reaching a confluency of 80–90%. Recombinant lentiviral vectors, including LV5-ZBED3, LV3-shZBED3, LV5-NC, and LV3-NC, were designed and produced by Hanbio Research Center (Hanbio Co. LTD, Shanghai, China). Infected cells were selected through incubation with 7 µg/mL puromycin (HY-K1057, MCE) for 48 h. Recombinant adenovirus vectors, including Ad-GFP (control adenovirus) and Ad-ZBED3 were also produced by Hanbio Research Center. The efficacy of ZBED3 overexpression or suppression was confirmed through Western blotting (WB) and quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR).

Wound healing assay

To assess cell migration, a wound healing assay was conducted using 786-O cells infected with LV5-ZBED3/LV5-NC or LV3-shZBED3/LV3-NC and cultured in 6-well plates at 37℃ with 5% CO2 for 24 h. The cells were mechanically disrupted using a plastic pipette, followed by a 24-hour incubation period for cell culture. Subsequently, images were captured using a Leica optical microscope at 0 and 24 h.

Transwell assays

786-O cells infected with LV5-ZBED3/LV5-NC or LV3-shZBED3/LV3-NC were seeded in the upper chamber with 100 µl of serum-free basal medium, while the lower chamber contained 600 µl of complete medium with 10% serum. After a 24-hour incubation period, the medium was aspirated, the upper side of the filter was cleaned, and the migrated cells on the bottom side were fixed with 4% formaldehyde and stained with 0.5% crystal violet staining solution for 20 min. Finally, the images of cell migration were obtained microscopically.

CCK‑8 assay

To investigate the cell proliferation ability, the CCK8 (cell counting kit-8) assay was utilized. 786-O cells infected with LV5-ZBED3/LV5-NC or LV3-shZBED3/LV3-NC were seeded in a 96-well plate at a density of 5*103 cells/well. Following incubation, the CCK8 solution (10ul, dojindo, Shanghai, China) was added to each well and the plate was incubated for 4 h at 37 °C, 5% CO2, in a humidified atmosphere. Absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader (Bio-Rad, USA) at 0 h, 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h.

Cell‑cycle assay

786-O cells infected with LV5-ZBED3/LV5-NC or LV3-shZBED3/LV3-NC were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 1 × 105 cells per well. Following an overnight incubation, the cells were collected and subsequently fixed in ice-cold 70% ethanol overnight at 4 ◦C. Subsequent to three washes with ice-cold PBS, the cells were then stained with propidium iodide (PI) using a working solution (50 µg/mL PI and 100 µg/mL RNase A) for a minimum of 2 h at room temperature, followed by flow cytometry analysis (BD Biosciences, USA).

Cell energy phenotype assay

The Seahorse XFp Cell Energy Phenotype Test kit is a diagnostic tool that concurrently assesses the two primary energy-generating pathways in viable cells, mitochondrial respiration and glycolysis, enabling the prompt determination of energy phenotypes of cells and investigation of metabolic switching. The Oxygen consumption rate (OCR) and extracellular acidification rate (ECAR) assays were conducted in accordance with the protocols provided by Agilent Technologies (Santa Clara, CA, USA). Protein concentrations were determined using the BCA Kit (Beyotime, P0010) following the addition of RIPA-lite (25 µl/well). Normalization of OCR and ECAR data was based on total protein concentration per well. The results reported represent the amalgamation of three separate assays, with statistical analyses conducted using two-way ANOVA in GraphPad software.

Real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR)

RNA extraction was carried out using Trizol® reagent (TAKARA, China), followed by reverse transcription of cDNA using the High-Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription kit (TAKARA, China) according to the manufacturer instructions. The PCR products were detected using a fluorescent quantitative PCR instrument, with GAPDH serving as the internal control. The reaction conditions included 40 cycles with pre-change at 95 °C for 15 s, denaturation at 95 °C for 5 s, and annealing and extension at 62 °C for 30 s. The 2-△△Ct method was employed for analyzing gene expression levels. Graphs were presented as mean ± SD and created using GraphPad Prism 8.0 software. The primer details can be found in Supplementary File.

Western blotting

Total proteins of 786-O cells were extracted after cell digestion with protein lysates. The protein content of the cellular sample was quantified utilizing a BCA kit (Beyotime, P0010), followed by separation of proteins via 10% SDS-PAGE with 20 g of protein loaded per lane. The proteins were subsequently transferred onto PVDF membranes, blocked with 5% milk, and probed with primary antibodies against ZBED3 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, PA5-20767, 1:1000) and GAPDH (ZSGB-BIO, TA-08, 1:1000). Detection was achieved using a processing film containing HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies, and visualization was conducted with an enhanced chemiluminescence detection system and the ChemiDoc system (BioRad). Protein intensity was quantified using ImageJ software (edition 1.8.0; National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, US).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis in this study utilized the “R package” (R edition: 4.2.1) and the above online database. GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software La Jolla, CA, USA) was employed for the visualization and statistical analysis of experimental data. The Chi-square test was utilized to evaluate the correlation between ZBED3 expression and clinicopathological parameters, while the Mann-Whitney U test or Kruskal-Wallis test was applied for other analyses. Two-sided P-values were calculated, and statistical significance was denoted by *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001, and ****P < 0.0001.

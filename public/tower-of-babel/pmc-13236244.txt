Efficacy and safety of risankizumab in moderate to severe psoriasis: an updated systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Hafsa Shuja, Kanza Farhan, Umer Wamiq, Muhammad Saad Khan, Muhammad Burhan Tariq, Shanza Shakir, Muhammad Uzair, Izere Salomon
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Risankizumab, a selective IL-23 inhibitor, has emerged as a promising therapeutic agent for moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis. This study aims to systematically assess its efficacy and safety through an updated meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs).Methods:A comprehensive literature search of PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, Cochrane Library, and ClinicalTrials.gov was conducted from their inception to 15 April 2025 for RCTs comparing risankizumab with placebo or active comparators in adults with moderate-to-severe psoriasis. Data were pooled using random-effects models (RevMan 5.4), and heterogeneity was assessed using I2 statistics. The Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool 2 (RoB 2) was utilized for risk of bias assessment of each included RCT. The forest plots were generated using risk ratios (RRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs).Results:Eleven RCTs (n = 2664 patients) were included in the study. Risankizumab showed significantly greater efficacy than placebo for PASI 75 (OR: 23.93, 95% CI: 7.80–73.41), PASI 90 (OR: 18.11, 95% CI: 5.03–65.24), and PASI 100 (OR: 30.31, 95% CI: 12.36–74.34). Compared to active comparators (ustekinumab and adalimumab), risankizumab demonstrated a superior PASI 90 response (OR 3.40, 95% CI: 2.32–4.96). Quality of life (DLQI) improved significantly (OR: 31.53, 95% CI: 19.46–51.09). No increased risk of SAEs was observed (OR: 0.71, 95% CI: 0.33–1.54), although the infection risk was slightly elevated (OR: 1.38, 95% CI: 0.87–2.18).Conclusion:This meta-analysis confirms the superiority of risankizumab over placebo and other biologics in achieving complete or near-complete skin clearance, with a favorable safety profile. These findings suggest that risankizumab is a first-line biologic agent for moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis, although infection monitoring remains warranted.

Introduction

Psoriasis is a chronic, immune-mediated skin disorder characterized by the rapid proliferation of epidermal keratinocytes, resulting in thickened, inflamed, and scaly skin lesions. It affects approximately 2–4% of the global population, with notable geographic variation ranging from less than 1% in East Asia to over 8% in Scandinavian countries[1]. In addition to its cutaneous manifestations, psoriasis is now recognized as a systemic disease with a substantial physical, psychological, and socioeconomic burden. Patients with psoriasis experience higher rates of comorbidities such as psoriatic arthritis, cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, obesity, metabolic syndrome, and psychiatric conditions, including anxiety and depression, all of which collectively impair the quality of life and increase healthcare costs[1].

Moderate to severe psoriasis, particularly plaque psoriasis, the most prevalent form, frequently necessitates systemic medication for effective disease management. Conventional therapies, such as methotrexate, cyclosporine, and phototherapy, have demonstrated effectiveness; however, safety issues and unpredictable patient responses limit their long-term usage. The advent of biologic therapies over the past two decades has revolutionized psoriasis management by targeting specific immune pathways underlying disease pathogenesis[2].

The interleukin-23 (IL-23) cytokine pathway is a significant immunologic factor in psoriasis and is crucial for sustaining the pro-inflammatory milieu of psoriatic lesions. IL-23 helps Th17 cells survive and proliferate, releasing IL-17 and other pro-inflammatory mediators, activating keratinocytes, and causing chronic skin inflammation. Therapeutic trials of biological drugs that selectively block the IL-23 pathway have shown promising results, with few side effects[3].

Risankizumab, a selective IL-23 inhibitor, is a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody that targets the p19 component of IL-23, thereby inhibiting IL-23 from interacting with the IL-23 receptor and subsequent inflammatory pathways[4]. Furthermore, selective p19 subunit inhibition spares IL-12 activity, which may reduce off-target immunosuppression[5]. Since its approval in 2019 for moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis, risankizumab has gained prominence due to its durable efficacy, high rates of complete or near-complete skin clearance (PASI90 and PASI100), low immunogenicity, and convenient dosing schedule. Landmark clinical trials, including IMMvent, UltIMMa-1, and UltIMMa-2, have consistently demonstrated its superiority over placebo and comparators such as adalimumab and ustekinumab[6].

Numerous studies have been conducted to evaluate the safety and efficacy of risankizumab for moderate-to-severe psoriasis since the publication of a meta-analysis by Singh et al[7]. However, since its publication, a substantial number of additional randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and long-term extension studies have been released, providing richer insights into both short- and long-term outcomes. These newer studies address critical aspects not fully explored in the earlier review, such as the durability of response beyond 1 year, rare adverse events, and comparative effectiveness against emerging biologics.

Therefore, this systematic review and meta-analysis updates the previously published meta-analysis through a comprehensive re-evaluation of the newly available clinical data to strengthen the evidence of risankizumab as an effective treatment option and to provide an updated understanding of its efficacy, safety, and therapeutic positioning in the evolving landscape of psoriasis management. This article adheres to the TITAN 2025 guidelines for transparency and integrity in academic publishing[8].

Methodology

This meta-analysis was performed adhering to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines[9]. Additionally, it has been reported in line with the AMSTAR (Assessing the Methodological Quality of Systematic Reviews) guidelines.

Search strategy and data sources

From 15 April 2025, a comprehensive literature search was carried out using different electronic databases, including PubMed, Embase, Scopus, ClinicalTrials.gov, Google Scholar, and Cochrane Library, without regard to language limitations. The following MeSH terms and keywords were used in the search: “Psoriasis” [MeSH], “Psoriases,” “Pustulosis of Palms and Soles,” “Pustulosis Palmaris et Plantaris,” “Palmoplantar Pustulosis,” “Pustular Psoriasis of Palms and Soles,” “Risankizumab” (Supplemental Digital Content Concept, available at: http://links.lww.com/JS9/H457), “BI 655066,” “BI-655066,” “Skyrizi,” “Risankizumab-rzaa,” and “ABBV-066,” combined using Boolean operators AND and OR to maximize search sensitivity. The reference lists of the retrieved articles, relevant meta-analyses, and review articles were manually screened to identify any additional eligible studies.

Study selection

Inclusion criteria

Research studies that fulfilled the following criteria were considered eligible for inclusion: (1) adult patients (18 years or older) diagnosed with moderate-to-severe psoriasis; (2) studies that compared risankizumab to placebo or other monoclonal antibodies in patients with psoriasis; (3) studies assessing the efficacy of risankizumab through Static Physician’s Global Assessment 0/1, or SPGA 0/1, SPGA 0 Response, Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI) 75 (≥75% clearance), PASI 90, PASI 100, or Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI); (4) studies assessing the safety profile of risankizumab by reporting adverse effects or infections; and (5) controlled trials meeting the above eligibility criteria.

Exclusion criteria

Narrative and systematic reviews, posters, conference papers, letters to editors, and articles that did not meet the inclusion criteria were omitted. However, cross-referencing was employed to identify additional pertinent studies.

Articles identified during the search were imported into the EndNote Reference Library software for duplicate removal. Two separate evaluators assessed the titles and abstracts, and thereafter conducted a comprehensive evaluation of potentially pertinent papers. A third investigator adjudicated any discrepancies.

Data extraction and quality assessment

Duplicate studies were removed from the list after exporting the retrieved articles to Rayyan software, a web-based tool for systematic review management[10]. All the articles were first screened based on the abstract and title. Then, the remaining articles were screened through the full text by two investigators. Any discrepancies regarding individual study inclusion, data extraction, and interpretation were resolved by consensus.

An online Microsoft Excel spreadsheet was created using data from the completed RCTs for the baseline characteristics and outcomes. Baseline parameters were extracted as follows: study characteristics (name of first author, region, publication year, clinical trial registration number, number of participants, and study design); patient demographics [age, gender, body mass index, disease duration, baseline PASI score, and Physician’s Global Assessment (PGA) score]; inclusion and exclusion criteria; and treatment arms (drug, comparator, dose, and regimen). Efficacy outcomes included PASI75, PASI90, and PASI100 response rates, while safety outcomes included adverse events (any adverse event, serious adverse events, infections, discontinuations due to adverse events, and deaths).

The Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool 2 (RoB 2) was utilized to evaluate the quality assessment and risk of bias of each included RCT across five domains. The assessment scores were compared, and discrepancies were resolved through consensus before the final analysis[11].

Secondary outcomes

Secondary outcomes included safety and tolerability measures. These comprised the incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), serious adverse events (SAEs), infections, and discontinuations due to adverse events. Safety data were extracted as reported in the individual studies, without modification.

Moreover, the DLQI was also used as a safety outcome. It is a validated, patient-reported outcome measure used to assess the impact of skin diseases on a patient’s quality of life over the previous week. It consists of 10 questions covering six domains: symptoms and feelings, daily activities, leisure, work and school, personal relationships, and treatment. Each question is scored on a 4-point Likert scale. The total score ranges from 0 to 30, with a higher score indicating greater impairment in quality of life.

Statistical analysis

The meta-analysis used Review Manager (version 5.4; Copenhagen: Nordic Cochrane Center, The Cochrane Collaboration, 2014[12]. To account for clinical and methodological diversity between studies, a random-effects model was used in all statistical analyses. Dichotomous outcomes (e.g., PASI responses, sPGA responses, adverse events) were analyzed using risk ratios (RRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Forest plots were generated for the visual display of results.

The I2 statistic was used to quantify heterogeneity[13]. Values of 25%, 50%, and 75% indicate low, moderate, and high heterogeneity, respectively. If the reported I2 value was higher than 50%, the outcome was subjected to sensitivity analysis to ascertain the distinct impacts of each study on that specific pooled outcome. To identify potential causes of heterogeneity, a subgroup analysis was conducted based on two groups.

Funnel plots were used to assess publication bias in Comprehensive Meta-Analysis (CMA, version 3.0)[14].

We used a GRADE (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation) tool to assess the overall quality and strength of evidence for each primary and secondary outcome[15].

Results

Study selection

A comprehensive literature search of the six databases yielded 764 studies. After removing duplicates, 364 studies were screened thoroughly, of which 300 studies were excluded after screening titles and abstracts for irrelevant results, demographics, or research design. Out of the resulting 64 studies, 32 studies could not be retrieved. Of the 32 full-text articles assessed for eligibility, 28 were excluded [pooled data (n = 7), review (n = 10), letters (n = 3), unpublished studies (n = 3), and others (n = 5)]. This systematic review and meta-analysis encompassed 11 trials that evaluated the safety and efficacy of risankizumab in the treatment of moderate to severe plaque psoriasis[16–25]. Risk of bias assessment

To evaluate the risk of bias in the included studies, we employed the Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool (RoB 2). Figure 2 illustrates that all 11 studies had a low risk in two domains: bias due to deviations from the intended interventions (D2) and bias due to missing outcome data (D3). For the randomization process (D1), most studies were rated as low risk, with two studies (Warren 2021 and Odnopozova 2022) showing some concerns, and one study (Ohtsuki 2019) judged as high risk. Regarding the measurement of the outcome (D4), ten studies were at low risk, while one study (Mark Lebwohl 2024) was rated as high risk. For the selection of the reported result (D5), most trials were low risk, except for Mark Lebwohl 2024, which was high risk. Overall, six studies were judged to have low risk, three raised some concerns, and two were rated as high risk, mainly due to issues with the randomization process (D1), measurement of outcomes (D4), and selective reporting (D5). Outcomes

SPGA 0 response

The overall efficacy of risankizumab has been assessed in both active- and placebo-controlled trials. Four studies (n = 1347 risankizumab vs 613 placebo) demonstrated significantly greater clinical improvement in patients administered risankizumab than in those receiving placebo [odds ratio (OR): 15.89; 95% CI: 2.39–105.91; P = 0.004]. However, considerable heterogeneity was observed (I2 = 92%; τ2 = 4.09; χ2 = 51.13, degrees of freedom (df) = 4; P = 0.00001), indicating substantial diversity in the research results. An active-controlled study (n = 301 risankizumab vs 304 comparator) estimate also favors risankizumab, and the pooled estimates remain entirely to the right of the line of no effect, demonstrating a statistically significant but smaller effect size. The confidence interval was relatively narrow, and no heterogeneity was detected, indicating consistent findings (OR: 3.40; 95% CI: 2.32–4.96; P = 0.00001; I2 = 0%). Subgroup analysis demonstrated significant statistical heterogeneity between placebo- and active-controlled data (I2 = 97.1%; P < 0.00001) (Fig. 3). Grade assessment

The GRADE assessment was utilized to evaluate the certainty of evidence for risankizumab compared to placebo in patients with psoriasis across multiple clinical outcomes. The certainty of evidence ranges from low to high, with key outcomes like PASI-75 (high certainty), sPGA 0/1 (moderate certainty), and sPGA 0% (low certainty) (Supplemental Digital Content Table 1, available at: http://links.lww.com/JS9/H457).

For PASI 75, risankizumab demonstrated a substantial relative effect (OR: 23.39, 95% CI: 7.80–73.41). The certainty of evidence was rated as high, indicating substantial improvement in the PASI 75 response. PASI-90 also showed a pronounced effect in the intervention (OR: 18.11, 95% CI: 5.03–65.24), although, due to serious heterogeneity (I2 = 95%), the certainty of evidence was graded as moderate. Similarly, for PASI-100, the effect was very strong (OR: 14.54, 95% CI: 3.92–53.90), but the certainty remained moderate, again due to high heterogeneity (I2 = 89%) and wide confidence intervals.

Risankizumab significantly improved DLQI 0/1 outcomes (OR: 13.89, 95% CI: 3.83–50.38). Despite the strong relative effect, certainty was rated as moderate due to the wide confidence intervals, reflecting some uncertainty in the precise effect size. For sPGA 0%, risankizumab also improved outcomes (OR: 15.89, 95% CI: 2.39–105.91); however, the evidence was graded as low certainty due to extremely wide confidence intervals and high heterogeneity (I2 = 94%). Similarly, sPGA 0/1 also showed moderate certainty for similar reasons, i.e., serious heterogeneity and imprecision (OR: 18.61, 95% CI: 6.92–50.09).

In terms of safety outcomes, serious AEs did not show a statistically significant difference between groups and were rated as moderate certainty, owing to considerable heterogeneity (I2 = 59%) (OR: 0.88, 95% CI: 0.54–1.43). Moreover, risankizumab was associated with a small but significant increase in infection risk and a low certainty of evidence (OR: 1.61, 95% CI: 1.02–2.53).

Overall, the GRADE assessment shows that risankizumab considerably improved the efficacy outcomes (particularly PASI responses and DLQI), supported by high to moderate certainty evidence, while safety outcomes suggest no increase in serious adverse events. However, a probable moderate increase in infections was noted.

Publication bias

The funnel plot (Supplemental Digital Content Figure 1, available at: http://links.lww.com/JS9/H457) demonstrated considerable asymmetry, with most smaller studies scattered on the right side, suggesting potential publication bias. In contrast, larger studies clustered near the pooled effect estimate, indicating greater precision. The pooled log odds ratio was approximately 2, which suggests a strong positive association. These findings were supported by Egger’s regression test, in which the Egger’s regression intercept is 4.74 [Standard Error (SE) = 2.35, t-value = 2.01, df = 8], with a two-tailed P-value of 0.08, indicating borderline evidence of small-study effects or publication bias. Moreover, the 95% confidence interval (–0.69 to 10.17) includes zero, indicating no strong statistical evidence of publication bias (Supplemental Digital Content Figure 2, available at: http://links.lww.com/JS9/H457).

Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis assessed the safety and efficacy of risankizumab in adult patients with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis. Risankizumab demonstrated consistent efficacy in achieving substantial clinical objectives, such as sPGA 0/1 and PASI 75, across 11 RCTs, with odds ratios favoring the intervention over both placebo and active comparators. The DLQI demonstrates that improvements in patient quality of life further substantiate the therapeutic advantages of risankizumab. In clinical practice, a persistent alert regarding the heightened infection risk necessitates vigilant monitoring.

The efficacy and safety profile of risankizumab for moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis, as indicated by our meta-analysis, largely aligns with the existing literature. Data indicate that risankizumab demonstrates enhanced clinical outcomes and response persistence compared to other earlier biologics; it represents a notable advancement in targeted biologic therapy as a selective interleukin-23 (IL-23) p19 inhibitor[26,27]. Multiple phase III trials, including the significant ULTIMMA-1 and ULTIMMA-2 investigations, initially established risankizumab’s superiority over both placebo and active comparators such as ustekinumab. In ULTIMMA-1, 4.9% of patients receiving placebo, 42.0% receiving ustekinumab, and 75.3% of patients receiving risankizumab achieved PASI 90 by week 16[19]. These results align with our studies, wherein risankizumab consistently surpassed placebo in achieving sPGA 0/1 and PASI 75 scores, exhibiting notably significant effect sizes (e.g., OR: 37.71, sPGA 0/1).

Furthermore, a 2021 network meta-analysis conducted by Armstrong et al evaluated over 60 biologic regimens and positioned risankizumab as the most efficacious treatment for achieving PASI 90 and PASI 100. Our results contribute to the existing body of data, particularly in demonstrating significant superiority in the PASI 75 and sPGA 0/1 responses. In contrast to previous trials that emphasized risankizumab’s ability to attain complete skin clearance (PASI 100) in a substantial proportion of patients, our pooled analysis yielded a non-significant outcome for this endpoint in both placebo- and active-controlled comparisons[28–31]. Divergences in patient attributes (e.g., initial PASI scores), trial duration, and eligibility criteria may all account for this discrepancy. Although our meta-analysis focused on short-term results (mostly at weeks 12–16), perhaps underestimating the overall treatment efficacy[32], numerous phase III trials have assessed PASI 100 at extended intervals (e.g., week 52).

Risankizumab selectively targets the IL-23 p19 subunit, differentiating its mechanism of action from that of dual IL-12/23 inhibitors such as ustekinumab and IL-17 inhibitors such as secukinumab and ixekizumab[33–35]. This selectivity may be attributed to a favorable balance between efficacy and safety. PASI 90 rates were significantly elevated with risankizumab at week 52 (87% compared to 57%) in a head-to-head trial (IMMerge study) comparing risankizumab and secukinumab[17]. Importantly, although the effect sizes were smaller than those of placebo-controlled data, our meta-analysis demonstrated the advantages of risankizumab in active comparator trials. This indicates sustained efficacy across multiple patient cohorts and experimental settings.

The ECLIPSE trial, which evaluated secukinumab versus another IL-23 inhibitor, provided insight into the comparative efficacy of IL-23 inhibition. While guselkumab sustained a superior response over time compared with secukinumab, the discrepancies in early response, specifically the week 12 PASI 90 rates, were diminished. Comparable trends were observed with risankizumab, which may not exhibit peak PASI 100 responses by weeks 12–16, although it displays significant durability upon further evaluation[36,37]. The durability of clinical response is a notable characteristic of risankizumab. Long-term extension studies, such as LIMMitless, have demonstrated persistent PASI 90 and PASI 100 responses over 2–3 years of continuous treatment despite low immunogenicity or tachyphylaxis[38]. The current meta-analysis does not represent these long-term outcomes because of the brief duration of the included investigations. Nevertheless, the uniform outcomes in early PASI and sPGA responses indicate a reliable initiation of treatment that yields enduring benefits; this trend is corroborated by newly published real-world data[39].

Assessment of the efficacy of psoriasis treatment relies on patient-reported outcomes, particularly the DLQI. Furthermore, a 2020 study by Warren et al emphasized the psychological impacts of IL-23 inhibitors, highlighting reductions in anxiety, despair, and job productivity loss – domains in which risankizumab has demonstrated consistent enhancements[40–42].

In significant clinical trials, risankizumab’s safety profile has been advantageous; there has been no significant increase in serious adverse events compared with placebo or other biologics. In alignment with real-world pharmacovigilance data, our meta-analysis identified a significant increase in the risk of infections, particularly upper respiratory tract and fungal infections. As indicated by a 2022 registry-based analysis from the PSOLAR and BIObaderm cohorts, infection rates are marginally elevated among IL-23 users, such as risankizumab, in comparison to TNF-α inhibitors and IL-17 blockers[43,44]. Due to their position downstream in the Th17 axis, IL-23 inhibitors are typically associated with a lower incidence of candidiasis and worsening of inflammatory bowel disease than IL-17 agents[43,45–47]. Although our study identified concerns related to infections, this may offer a hypothetical safety benefit. The extensive confidence intervals and variability in our infection outcomes undoubtedly indicate differing baseline risk factors and inconsistent reporting practices among trial participants.

The validation of clinical trial outcomes in broader, more diverse groups relies on real-world data, which is increasingly significant. Risankizumab showed elevated rates of PASI 90 and DLQI 0/1 responses at 6 and 12 months, even among individuals with comorbidities such as obesity, psoriatic arthritis, and cardiovascular disease, according to a substantial retrospective study from the CorEvitas Psoriasis Registry[48]. These data indicate that risankizumab’s efficacy is maintained beyond the stringent criteria of RCTs, hence endorsing its application in standard clinical practice. Moreover, studies including specific populations, such as elderly individuals and those with hepatic or renal impairment, have demonstrated no significant alterations in drug metabolism or adverse event profiles, thereby highlighting the suitability of the drug over a wide therapeutic range[49]. Risankizumab is an effective treatment for moderate-to-severe psoriasis. Recent bibliometric analyses have highlighted the growing role of artificial intelligence and large language models such as ChatGPT in supporting medical research, including literature synthesis and evidence-based decision-making. However, concerns regarding reliability, transparency, and ethical considerations remain important challenges that require careful oversight when integrating these tools into clinical and academic workflows[50].

Strengths and limitations

This meta-analysis provided several compelling arguments. Initially, it aggregated data from 11 high-quality RCTs, enhancing the overall reliability and validity of the findings. The inclusion of both active-comparator and placebo-controlled studies provides a comprehensive evaluation of risankizumab’s safety and efficacy. The study adhered to the PRISMA guidelines and employed robust statistical methods, including subgroup analysis, to elucidate variations in the research design and comparator type. Moreover, it thoroughly assessed and recorded heterogeneity, thereby improving the comprehension of effect estimates for various clinical outcomes.

Although they possess specific advantages, certain limitations must be acknowledged. Certain results exhibited significant heterogeneity, particularly in PASI 75, which is potentially attributable to discrepancies in study populations, dosing regimens, or outcome definitions. The limited number of active-controlled trials diminishes the generalizability of the comparative results to other biologics. Moreover, several subgroups exhibited limited sample sizes, potentially undermining the stability and accuracy of aggregated calculations. The limited number of studies for each outcome precludes a thorough assessment of publication bias; the majority of the research was funded by industry, potentially introducing reporting bias.

Future research recommendations

Future studies should employ standardized outcome definitions and extended follow-up periods to assess the durability of the risankizumab response and late-onset adverse effects. Additional evaluation of infection risk and identification of specific high-risk patient populations rely on real-world data and post-marketing surveillance studies. The long-term achievement of PASI 100 and relapse rates should be emphasized in head-to-head studies comparing risankizumab with other IL-23 and IL-17 inhibitors. Research examining the impact of risankizumab on psoriatic arthritis and its associated conditions may further broaden its therapeutic use.

Conclusion

This meta-analysis demonstrates that risankizumab is an exceptionally effective biologic for achieving sPGA 0/1 and PASI responses in the treatment of moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis, thereby enhancing patients' quality of life. The favorable safety profile is mostly preserved; nevertheless, the increased risk of infection necessitates careful evaluation. Considering a thorough individualized risk-benefit analysis, these findings affirm the critical significance of risankizumab in chronic psoriasis management and warrant its sustained application as a primary treatment, notwithstanding the variability in patient responses observed across studies.

Application of risk prediction model to evaluate the effect of mechanical ventilation on postoperative pulmonary complications in thoracic surgery
Authors: Li Wang, Xiaoyun He, Minxi Zhao, Jun Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis retrospective cohort study aimed to systematically evaluate the impact of different mechanical ventilation strategies on postoperative pulmonary complications (PPCs) in thoracic surgery and to establish a risk prediction model that facilitates high-risk patient identification and perioperative management.MethodsA total of 300 patients undergoing thoracic surgery at a tertiary hospital were enrolled. Demographic data, perioperative indicators, and mechanical ventilation parameters were collected. Potential risk factors were initially identified by univariate analyses and then entered into multivariable logistic regression and machine learning models (random forest and XGBoost) for model training and evaluation. The primary outcome was the incidence and types of PPCs, and the predictive performances of different models were compared.ResultsPPCs occurred in 38.7% of the 300 patients included in the final analysis. Both univariate and multivariate analyses indicated that higher tidal volume, lower positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP), higher plateau pressure, and prolonged ventilation duration were positively associated with an increased risk of PPCs. Conversely, pulmonary protective ventilation strategies and recruitment maneuvers had a significant protective effect. The logistic regression model demonstrated an AUC of 0.60, with key variables including tidal volume (OR = 2.119, 95%CI 1.362–3.294), PEEP level (OR = 1.570, 95%CI 1.180–2.088), and plateau pressure (OR = 1.984, 95%CI 1.202–3.276) as significant predictors. Compared with traditional logistic regression and random forest models, the XGBoost model showed moderate performance with potential clinical utility, with a sensitivity and specificity of approximately 0.70 and 0.48, respectively, and an AUC of 0.56. Feature importance analyses revealed that ventilation-related variables (e.g., tidal volume, PEEP, plateau pressure, and total ventilation time) carried substantial weight in predicting PPCs.ConclusionAdopting pulmonary protective ventilation strategies––such as low tidal volume, appropriate PEEP, and recruitment maneuvers––significantly reduces the risk of PPCs in patients undergoing thoracic surgery. Machine learning algorithms like XGBoost may assist in identifying high-risk patients and tailoring individualized ventilation management. Further applications of multicenter big data and real-time monitoring technologies may help optimize mechanical ventilation strategies and improve patient outcomes.

Introduction

With the continuous improvement of surgical technology and perioperative management level, thoracic surgery plays a pivotal role in the diagnosis and treatment of chest tumors such as pulmonary cancer and esophageal cancer as well as benign intrathoracic diseases [1]. However, thoracic surgery is often accompanied by larger trauma and longer perioperative intervention time. Postoperative pulmonary complications (PPCs) are still one of the most common and intractable problems affecting the surgical results and prognosis of patients [2]. According to statistics, the incidence of pulmonary complications after thoracic surgery can be as high as 10–40%, which can lead to rapid deterioration of pulmonary function, prolonged hospitalization, or even life threatening in severe cases [3, 4]. Therefore, how to effectively reduce the incidence of postoperative pulmonary complications and improve the quality of life of patients has always been the focus of attention in the clinical and research fields [5].

In thoracic surgery, mechanical ventilation is a key intervention to maintain and guarantee oxygenation and ventilation in patients during surgery. However, ventilator-induced lung injury (VILI) cannot be ignored [6]. Improper setting of mechanical ventilation parameters during surgery will result in repeated alternation of lung over-stretching and collapse, increasing the risks of alveolar rupture and inflammatory reaction, laying a hidden danger for the occurrence of postoperative pulmonary complications [7, 8]. In recent years, people have gradually recognized the importance of protective pulmonary ventilation strategies, such as reducing tidal volume, setting a reasonable positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP), and reducing the exposure time of higher oxygen concentration. These measures can reduce the pulmonary tissue damage and inflammatory reaction in the pulmonary to a certain extent, and reduce the occurrence of postoperative pulmonary complications [9]. However, the issues such as how to improve the individualized level and whether the ventilation strategy has a differential effect on patients with different types of surgery and different backgrounds still need to be further clarified and optimized [10].

With the arrival of the era of precision medicine and big data, risk prediction models based on clinical characteristics, perioperative indicators, and various complex influencing factors have received increasing attention [11, 12]. Through multivariate analysis, the impact of specific interventions on postoperative outcomes can be evaluated and quantified using mathematical models or machine learning methods, to provide a more objective and reliable basis for clinical decision-making. In the field of thoracic surgery, risk prediction models can help physicians accurately stratify patients before surgery, identify high-risk populations, and formulate individualized anesthesia and mechanical ventilation strategies, thereby reducing the incidence of postoperative pulmonary complications and reducing medical resource consumption [13]. However, in the current quantitative research on the correlation between mechanical ventilation and postoperative pulmonary complications in thoracic surgery, there are still problems such as insufficient model construction and verification, relatively lack of data samples, and difficulty in landing individual recommendations. There remains a lack of validated prediction models specifically designed for thoracic surgery patients, and the comparative effectiveness of machine learning approaches versus traditional statistical methods in this context has not been thoroughly evaluated [14]. How to use large-scale, multi-center clinical data to construct and validate an efficient risk prediction model with good external applicability is still one of the important topics in clinical research.

In the present study, the effects of mechanical ventilation and other related factors on PPCs after thoracic surgery were systematically evaluated by retrospective analysis in combination with multivariate logistic regression and machine learning model. The aim is to reduce the incidence of PPCs and improve the prognosis of patients by building efficient predictive models, identifying high-risk patients, and optimizing preoperative evaluation and postoperative management strategies.

Material and method

Ethical approval

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Research Institution of Yunnan First People's Hospital (KHLL2023-KY136). All participants provided written informed consent, and this study strictly adhered to the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Research object

This is a retrospective cohort study to evaluate the effect of mechanical ventilation on postoperative pulmonary complications during thoracic surgery. Based on previous studies demonstrating PPC rates of 10–40% in thoracic surgery patients [3, 15], research subjects were patients who received surgical treatment in the thoracic department of our center from January to November 2024. Inclusion criteria were as follows: ① Age ≥ 18 years old; ② Patients receiving open or minimally invasive thoracic surgery; ③ Patients with complete medical records to be maintained after operation. Exclusion criteria included the following: ① Preoperative severe cardiopulmonary insufficiency defined as New York Heart Association (NYHA) class III–IV heart failure or forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV1) < 50% predicted; ② The operation was suspended due to unforeseeable factors during the operation; ③ Postoperative data were incomplete or the follow-up visit was not in place.

In this study, the sample size calculation method of the proportion comparison of two independent samples was used.

Based on the results of clinical data from previous studies, a minimum sample size of 172 cases was calculated assuming an incidence of postoperative pulmonary complications of 20%, Z = 1.96, Z ~ β\ ~ = 0.84, P = P + P/2 = 0.15. In view of data loss, the actual sample size of 300 cases was finally determined according to the hospital case situation to ensure the statistical efficiency of the study.

Data collection

Data were systematically collected from the hospital's electronic medical record (EMR) system and included the following categories:

General demographic data: Age, gender, height and weight body mass index (BMI), smoking history, and past medical history.

Perioperative information: American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) classification defined as a standardized system for assessing preoperative physical status (I-normal healthy patient, II-mild systemic disease, III-severe systemic disease, IV-severe systemic disease with constant threat to life), mode of operation (thoracotomy or thoracoscopy), mode of anesthesia, duration of operation, intraoperative blood loss volume, tidal volume (ml/kg), peak airway pressure, plateau pressure, positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP) level, oxygen concentration inspired oxygen fraction (FiO2), pulmonary recruitment maneuver, presence or absence of pulmonary protective ventilation protocol, and duration of mechanical ventilation (including duration of mechanical ventilation during operation and in the intensive care unit (ICU)).

Postoperative pulmonary complications: Definition and classification of complications (Clavien–Dindo classification). Specific complications include atelectasis, pneumonia, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), pleural effusion, and pulmonary air leak.

Outcome variables: Incidence and types of postoperative pulmonary complications.

Preoperative pulmonary function tests including forced expiratory volume were not routinely collected in this retrospective study due to the emergency nature of many thoracic procedures and institutional protocols at the time.

After all data were exported through the EMR system, they were cleaned and preprocessed using Python. Missing values were handled using multiple imputation to ensure data integrity and analytical accuracy.

Data processing and statistical analysis

Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) 25.0 and Python 3.8 were used for statistical analysis, and Python usage databases included scikit-learn, pandas, numpy, matplotlib, and seaborn.

Univariate analysis of general patient data was performed to compare the differences in baseline and perioperative variables between the group with postoperative pulmonary complications and the group without complications. The independent samples t-test or Mann–Whitney U test was applied to continuous variables, and chi-square test or Fisher exact test was applied to categorical variables. The statistical significance level was set as P < 0.05.

Variables with P < 0.05 in the univariate analysis were included in the multivariate logistic regression model to evaluate their independent effects on postoperative pulmonary complications. The stepwise regression method (stepwise forward method) was used to select the variables in the final model. The goodness of fit of the model was evaluated by Hosmer–Lemeshow test, with P > 0.05 indicating good model calibration and fit to the observed data. The discriminant ability of the model was evaluated by area under receiver operating characteristic curve area under the curve (AUC-ROC).

Two machine learning algorithms, random forest and gradient boosting decision tree XGBoost, were used to construct a model for predicting postoperative pulmonary complications.

Model building and training: The dataset was randomly split into training (80%) and testing (20%) sets to ensure unbiased evaluation. fivefold cross-validation was used on the training set to evaluate model performance and avoid over-fitting. The random forest and the XGBoost model were trained, and the hyperparameters were optimized using grid search to optimize the model performance.

Model evaluation: AUC-ROC, accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and F1 score (F1-score) were evaluated. The performance of different models was compared and the model with the best balance of AUC-ROC and clinical applicability was selected as the final prediction model.

Feature importance analysis: The XGBoost model was evaluated for feature importance and the top 10 features that had the greatest impact on the prediction of postoperative pulmonary complications were identified.

Result

Univariate analysis of patient general data

A total of 300 patients undergoing thoracic surgery were included in this study, including 180 males (60.0%) and 120 females (40.0%). The mean age of the patients was 60.2 ± 8.5 years and the mean body mass index (BMI) was 24.8 ± 3.1 kg/m2. Patients with positive history of smoking accounted for 41.7% (125/300), 45 patients (15.0%) with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), 62 patients (20.7%) with diabetes, 105 patients (35.0%) with hypertension, and 30 patients (10.0%) with mild to moderate heart disease. According to the American Association of Anesthesiologists (ASA) classification, 10%, 60%, 25%, and 5% of patients were Grade I–IV respectively.

In terms of surgical approach, 70.0%(210/300) of the patients underwent video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery, and 30.0%(90/300) underwent thoracotomy. General anesthesia was performed in the majority of patients (96.7%), with the remainder being administered by other means of anesthesia (3.3%). The mean surgical duration for the patients was 175 ± 35 min, the median intraoperative bleeding (interquartile range) was 250 [150, 400] mL, the intraoperative crystalloid fluid infusion was 1500 ± 200 mL, and the colloid infusion was 300 ± 100 mL (Table 1). Mechanical ventilation strategy and postoperative pulmonary complication

Regarding mechanical ventilation, the mean tidal volume was 6.5 ± 0.8 mL/kg (ideal body weight), the positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP) level was 5.0 ± 1.5 cmH₂O, the inspired oxygen concentration (FiO₂) was 50 ± 10%, and the airway peak and plateau pressures were 22 ± 4 cmH₂O and 16 ± 3 cmH₂O, respectively. Inspired oxygen concentration (FiO₂) was monitored using the ventilator's integrated oxygen analyzer. About 60.0%(180/300) of the patients received pulmonary recruitment maneuver during the perioperative period, and 50.0%(150/300) of the patients received pulmonary protective ventilation protocol. The patient's total mechanical ventilation duration was 2.5 ± 1.1 h.

A total of 116 cases (38.7%) with different degrees of pulmonary complications were observed during and after the operation. Among them, there were 36 cases (12.0%) of pneumonia, 45 cases (15.0%) of atelectasis, 15 cases (5.0%) of ARDS, 30 cases (10.0%) of pleural effusion, and 20 cases (6.7%) of air leakage (> 5 days without healing). Some patients had two or more complications, but the primary complication was recorded for analysis. The overall hospital stay was 10.5 ± 3.2 days, with an ICU admission rate of 25.0%(75/300), a re-intubation rate of 3.0%(9/300), and an inpatient mortality rate of 1.0%(3/300) (Table 2). Univariate analysis

To explore the relationship between each variable and postoperative pulmonary complications, patients were divided into complication group (N = 116) and non-complicated group (N = 184) according to whether there were postoperative pulmonary complications (PPCs). Univariate analysis was performed on 32 variables. The results showed (Table 3) that statistical differences existed between the two groups in many perioperative variables such as age, BMI, smoking history, COPD, diabetes, hypertension, ASA classification, operation method, and operation duration (P < 0.05). Regarding the mechanical ventilation strategy, tidal volume > 7 mL/kg, higher plateau pressure (> 28 cmH₂O), and PEEP < 3 cmH₂O were all significantly associated with complications (P < 0.01). Pulmonary recruitment maneuver and the application of pulmonary protective ventilation showed a negative correlation with postoperative pulmonary complications (P < 0.05). Model performance comparison

Based on the variables that entered the model after screening by univariate analysis, the fivefold cross-validation of logistic regression, random forest, and gradient boosting tree (XGBoost) were performed, and the performance metrics of each fold were visualized (Fig. 1). Logistic regression showed the highest average AUC, which was about 0.60. XGBoost was second, and the average AUC was about 0.56. Random forests had the lowest AUC in this cohort, approximately 0.49. The average sensitivity value of logistic regression ranged from 0.75 to 0.80, which was relatively superior to the other two models. The sensitivity of XGBoost was about 0.65–0.70, and that of random forest was about 0.55. Random forests performed better in specificity, with some folds even reaching 0.55–0.60. The specificity of XGBoost was mainly within the range of 0.40–0.50, with logistic regression showing lower specificity of about 0.30. The specific performance metrics for model comparison are shown in Table 5. The overall performance of XGBoost(GBDT) was intermediate, with better sensitivity than random forest, better specificity than logistic regression, and moderate AUC. Discussion

In this study, based on retrospective cohort data, multiple risk prediction models (logistic regression, random forest, and XGBoost) were used to evaluate the perioperative clinical variables of patients undergoing thoracic surgery, aiming to explore the impact of mechanical ventilation strategy on postoperative pulmonary complications. The main findings were as follows: ① Higher tidal volume, higher plateau pressure, and lower PEEP level were all risk factors for PPCs, and pulmonary recruitment maneuver and pulmonary protective ventilation had a protective effect in reducing the risk of complications; ② The predictive performance of XGBoost demonstrated moderate clinical utility with AUC, sensitivity and specificity metrics that were comparable to traditional logistic regression while showing better balance than random forest; however, the overall predictive performance (AUC 0.56–0.60) indicates that these models, while providing some clinical insight, require further refinement and external validation before widespread clinical implementation [16]; ③ In the analysis of feature importance using XGBoost, the variables related to mechanical ventilation were ranked high in the model, indicating that the ventilation strategy was one of the key factors affecting the pulmonary complications of patients undergoing thoracic surgery.

The results of this study demonstrated that every 1 mL/kg increase in tidal volume under normal weight was associated with a significantly increased risk of postoperative pulmonary complications (OR = 2.119, 95% CI 1.362–3.294), a trend consistent with the respiratory mechanics research and the concept of "low tidal volume ventilation" recommended by the Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome Network (ARDSNet) [17, 18]. Higher tidal volume is likely to cause alveolar hyperinflation, pulmonary stress injury (volutrauma), and increased alveolar inflammatory response, thus increasing the incidence of postoperative pulmonary complications [6, 19]. Similarly, plateau pressure (> 28 cmH₂O) was also identified as an independent risk factor in this study (OR = 1.984, 95% CI 1.202–3.276), further confirming that alveolar hyperinflation and transpulmonary pressure are the pathophysiological basis for pulmonary injury [20].

Notably, each 1 cmH₂O reduction in PEEP (OR = 1.570, 95% CI 1.180–2.088) significantly increased the risk of complications. In thoracic surgery, patients are more likely to develop atelectasis due to factors such as one-lung ventilation and collapse of the lung on the operative side. Appropriate PEEP is conducive to maintaining functional residual volume and reducing pulmonary injury caused by repeated opening–closing of alveolar units [1, 9, 21]. However, excessive PEEP may also cause circulatory instability. Therefore, how to strike a balance between preventing atelectasis and maintaining alveolar opening should be comprehensively judged in combination with the patient's condition, real-time hemodynamic indicators, pulmonary compliance, and other factors.

Furthermore, both pulmonary recruitment maneuver (OR = 0.653, 95%CI 0.457–0.932) and pulmonary protective ventilation protocol (OR = 0.550, 95%CI 0.372–0.815) were found to have significant negative correlation with PPCs. This result is consistent with recent reports [22–24]: Regular recruitment maneuvers during the operation and perioperative period can effectively reduce atelectasis and improve arterial oxygen saturation, thereby reducing the complications such as postoperative pneumonia, ARDS, and gas exchange disorder. Additionally, the "pulmonary protective ventilation protocol" included in this study usually incorporated such elements as low tidal volume, moderate PEEP, and reasonable respiratory frequency and inspiratory to expiratory time ratio, which was consistent with the current concept of "lung protection" and played a positive role in reducing ventilation-related pulmonary injury [25, 26].

Regarding model performance, the AUC of the logistic regression model in this study was about 0.60, which represents moderate predictive ability. In the comparison of random forest model and XGBoost model, the overall performance of XGBoost was relatively balanced, with a sensitivity of about 0.70, a specificity of about 0.48, and an AUC of about 0.56, suggesting that the machine learning model in this sample did not demonstrate substantial superiority over the traditional regression model. However, XGBoost and logistic regression showed reasonable value in identifying high-risk patients. When higher-dimensional monitoring indicators are not available, the prediction model constructed based on conventional clinical features and ventilation parameters can provide clinicians with rapid and preliminary risk assessment tools [27]. Strategies based on XGBoost or logistic regression can still play a supporting decision-making role when prioritizing sensitivity and reducing missed diagnoses of complications.

Previous researchers have conducted numerous prospective and retrospective studies on perioperative pulmonary complications in patients undergoing thoracic surgery [28–32]. The general conclusion is that age, smoking history, chronic pulmonary disease, operation time, and perioperative mechanical ventilation strategy are the key factors affecting postoperative pulmonary complications. The results of this study are consistent with these conclusions, especially the protective effects of "low tidal volume, moderate PEEP, and recruitment maneuvers" after thoracic surgery have been supported by many clinical studies [17, 33, 34]. However, studies have also reported that for patients with high body mass index (BMI > 30), the ventilation parameters and fluid management should be adjusted appropriately according to the individual situation [35]. In this study, every 1 kg/m2 increase in BMI (OR = 1.060, 95% CI 1.016–1.106) also indicated an increased risk of postoperative pulmonary complications, suggesting that obese patients should receive enhanced attention regarding anesthesia management, respiratory management, and perioperative comprehensive intervention [36].

Several limitations of this study warrant acknowledgment. First, this single-center retrospective design limits the generalizability of findings to other institutions and patient populations [37]. Second, the moderate predictive performance of all models suggests that additional variables or more sophisticated modeling approaches may be needed. Third, the lack of external validation cohorts prevents assessment of model performance in different clinical settings. Fourth, potential confounding factors such as surgeon experience, specific surgical techniques, and postoperative care protocols were not systematically captured [38].

Future research directions should focus on verifying the ventilation strategy and complication prediction model based on thoracic surgery patients in a broader population, so as to improve the generalizability and stability of the results. Multicenter prospective studies incorporating continuous respiratory monitoring parameters (pulmonary compliance, end-tidal carbon dioxide, driving pressure, etc.) collected using bedside monitoring equipment, and development of real-time early warning systems in combination with machine learning algorithms could assist anesthesiologists and ICU doctors in adjusting the ventilation protocol in real time [39]. The integration of perioperative biomarkers (cytokines, proteomics, etc.) and 3D pulmonary imaging parameters may deepen the understanding of the mechanism of pulmonary injury and improve the accuracy and interpretability of the model [40].

In summary, this study explored the impact of mechanical ventilation strategies on postoperative pulmonary complications in patients undergoing thoracic surgery through a retrospective analysis. The results demonstrated that low tidal volume, appropriate PEEP, and other protective pulmonary ventilation measures such as recruitment maneuvers played an important role in reducing the risk of PPCs, and machine learning models such as XGBoost could help identify high-risk patients in clinical practice to some extent. With the extensive application of multi-center big data and the development of real-time monitoring technology, combined with more accurate and intelligent respiratory management, these advances will help further reduce the pulmonary complications related to thoracic surgery and improve the prognosis and postoperative quality of life of patients.

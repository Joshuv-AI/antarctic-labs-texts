Attentions Under the Microscope: A Comparative Study of Resource Utilization for Variants of Self-Attention
Zhengyu Tian, Anantha Padmanaban Krishna Kumar, Hemant Krishnakumar, Reza Rawassizadeh

Abstract
As large language models (LLMs) and visual language models (VLMs) grow in scale and application, attention mechanisms have become a central computational bottleneck due to their high memory and time complexity. While many efficient attention variants have been proposed, there remains a lack of rigorous evaluation on their actual energy usage and hardware resource demands during training. In this work, we benchmark eight attention mechanisms in training GPT-2 architecture, measuring key metrics including training time, GPU memory usage, FLOPS, CPU usage, and power consumption. Our results reveal that attention mechanisms with optimized kernel implementations, including Flash Attention, Locality-Sensitive Hashing (LSH) Attention, and Multi-Head Latent Attention (MLA), achieve the best energy efficiency. We further show that lower GPU power alone does not guarantee reduced energy use, as training time plays an equally important role. Our study highlights the importance of energy-aware benchmarking in attention design and provides a practical insight for selecting resource-efficient mechanisms. II
Self-Attention Methods

The Transformer architecture, introduced by Vaswani et al. (2017)
[
22
]
, is the foundation of most large language models (LLMs)
[
23
]
. Its self-attention mechanism enables the modeling of token dependencies across long sequences. However, the original attention formulation incurs quadratic complexity in both time and memory
[
20
]
. This high cost not only consumes substantial computational resources but also increases operational expenses for enterprise deployment and poses potential environmental concerns due to energy demand. This led to the introduction of several optimizations on self-attention models.

II-A

Self-Attention Variants

To improve the efficiency of self-attention computation, many alternative designs have been proposed. Recent research has explored modifications such as Grouped Query Attention
[
1
]
, Sliding Window Attention
[
2
]
, LSH Attention
[
11
]
, and NVIDIA hardware-optimized Flash Attention
[
20
]
. These designs aim to reduce resource consumption while maintaining model performance.

•

Baseline self-Attention
[
22
]
:
The classic self-attention module as implemented in GPT-2, using masked scaled dot-product attention with full sequence visibility restricted to past tokens. It serves as the baseline for comparison in this study.

•

Scaled Dot-Product Attention
[
14
]
:
This mechanism computes attention scores by scaling and taking the Dot-Product between queries and keys, followed by a softmax operation. It incurs
O(n^{2})
time and memory complexity.

•

Grouped Query Attention
[
1
]
:
This mechanism groups queries into several partitions and computes attention separately within each group, reducing computational overhead and memory usage while maintaining sufficient representation capacity.

•

Linear Attention
[
10
]
:
A variation of attention that applies a simple positive feature map (e.g., ReLU+1) to both queries and keys, enabling the approximation of traditional attention with linear time complexity while maintaining expressiveness.

•

Sliding Window Attention
[
2
]
:
Instead of attending to the full sequence, sliding window attention restricts attention computation to a fixed-size local window around each token, significantly reducing complexity for long sequences.

•

Locality-Sensitive Hashing (LSH) Attention
[
11
]
:
This attention clusters similar tokens into the same buckets using a hash function, and performs attention only within buckets, reducing full sequence attention to sparse approximations.

•

Flash Attention version 2
[
6
]
:
A highly optimized attention implementation that reduces memory access overhead and improves computational efficiency by reorganizing Nvidia’s GPU memory patterns and leveraging GPU parallelism, leading to faster training and lower memory consumption. It is one of the most common attention mechanisms used in LLMs, such as Llama
[
21
]
and Mistral
[
5
]
model series.

•

Multi-Head Latent Attention (MLA)
[
7
]
:
A Key-Value (KV) attention compression technique introduced in DeepSeek-V2
[
13
]
that reduces memory by jointly encoding keys and values into shared latent vectors, enabling faster inference with minimal performance loss.

In this research, we study these attention types in a unified framework for empirical comparison within the GPT-2 architecture.

III
Experiments

To investigate the performance of various self-attention mechanisms within LLMs, we implemented and compared the listed attention modules using the GPT-2 architecture as the baseline framework. All experiments were conducted in a controlled environment to ensure fair comparison.

III-A

Experimental Language Model

We adopted the GPT-2 architecture
[
16
]
as a unified backbone and modified only the self-attention modules to integrate different attention mechanisms. GPT-2 is an open model and widely used for experimental or teaching purposes. Each attention type was implemented as an independent module, designed to be plug-and-play within the Transformer block. To conduct our comparison we assume the GPT-2 self-attention as baseline and compared it with Scaled Dot-Product Attention

[

, and MLA
[

III-B

Hardware Settings

The computational experiments were performed on hardware featuring an Intel Core i9 CPU operating at 3.30 GHz, 256 GB of system RAM, and one NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, equipped with 24 GB VRAM. The software environment included Ubuntu 20.04 LTS and CUDA toolkit version 12.0.

III-C

Test Dataset

We used the AllenAI’s Tulu-v2-sft-mixture dataset, from the Hugging Face Hub
2
2

2

https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/tulu-v2-sft-mixture
, to fine-tune the GPT-2 model. This dataset is designed for instruction tuning of large language models. Each sample in this dataset contains a structured messages list that simulates multi-turn human-assistant interactions. These messages were parsed into plain text using a custom Text Dataset class, tokenized with the GPT2Tokenizer, and padded or truncated to a maximum length of 512 tokens for consistency across samples.

III-D

Evaluation and Monitoring

Alongside model accuracy, there is increasing emphasis on evaluating computational cost and energy consumption. Studies have shown that inference with large models often results in high GPU usage and power utilization
[
12
,
15
,
18
,
19
]
. Metrics such as FLOPS (floating point operations, a measure of computational complexity), GPU power usage, memory usage, and runtime latency are now commonly used in empirical evaluations. In this work, we study these metrics to assess the trade-offs of different attention mechanisms from both computational and energy-efficiency perspectives.

To evaluate the attention mechanisms, we used multiple metrics: FLOPS, GPU memory usage, and average forward time. Runtime efficiency was further profiled using custom-built monitoring tools to track CPU/GPU usage, memory allocation, and GPU power consumption in real time.

All models were trained for 20 epochs using a batch size of 16, with each epoch consisting of 400 batches. This corresponds to a total of 6,400 training steps per model. Experiments were conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, and the training schedule was fixed across all attention variants to ensure fair and consistent comparisons. This setting also reflects practical limitations under resource-constrained environments.

V
Discussion

V-A

Energy Efficiency Insights

Our evaluation reveals that Flash Attention achieves the most competitive energy efficiency, with total GPU energy consumption approximately nine percent lower than the second-best performing mechanism (Figure
3
). Although Flash Attention ranks fourth in training speed, it differs by only 7 seconds per epoch compared to the fastest method (Figure
1
). However, its exceptional GPU power efficiency at 250W (Figure
2
) enables it to maintain a dominant position in overall energy consumption. Both MLA and LSH Attention also demonstrate competitive performance, forming a strong second tier in energy efficiency rankings.

In terms of average GPU power usage, Flash Attention and MLA form the lowest group, with closely matched power draw. In contrast, Scaled Dot-Product Attention exhibits the highest average power consumption, approximately 20W higher than the baseline. The remaining mechanisms fall between Flash Attention and Scaled Dot-Product Attention, with overall differences generally within 50 watts.

Regarding training time, Linear Attention and LSH Attention are the fastest mechanisms, while Baseline Attention takes the longest to complete each epoch (Figure
1
). As a result, Baseline Attention ranks seventh in total energy consumption. This suggests that lower power usage alone does not guarantee efficiency if training time is not optimized.

Finally, it is worth noting that Sliding Window Attention consumes higher energy than the baseline (Figure
3
), despite achieving similar loss curves. This indicates that, without meaningful improvements in convergence for these attentions, their high power utilization makes them less suitable from an energy-efficiency perspective.

V-B

Most Efficient Self-Attention Mechanisms

Among the evaluated attention mechanisms, MLA demonstrates a particularly favorable balance between energy efficiency and training performance. As illustrated in Figure
3
, MLA achieves a total energy consumption of 1.17 megajoules (MJ), placing it among the lowest tiers of energy usage across all methods. While Flash Attention records the minimal energy consumption at 1.07 MJ, the margin between the two is relatively small.

Notably, MLA exhibits a much faster convergence in terms of loss reduction during training. This rapid optimization behavior highlights MLA’s efficiency not only in computational cost but also in learning dynamics. Compared to other recent methods, such as Sliding Window Attention (1.42 MJ), and Group Query Attention (1.32 MJ), MLA significantly reduces total energy consumption without compromising performance.

Multi-Head Latent Attention (MLA) and Flash Attention demonstrate superior efficiency across key metrics. As shown in Figure
6
, both achieve the lowest computational complexity at
0.923\times 10^{12}
and
0.955\times 10^{12}
FLOPS, respectively. Flash Attention excels in memory efficiency with 16.4 GB GPU consumption (Figure
7
), while MLA uses 17.5 GB. For inference performance, Flash Attention achieves the fastest speed at 28.8 seconds per batch, with MLA close behind at 32.2 seconds, as shown in Figure
8
. These results indicate that both mechanisms provide optimal computational-efficiency trade-offs for resource-constrained deployments. Our finding positions both MLA and Flash Attention as the most promising approaches in modern attention design, especially in scenarios where energy efficiency and training speed are critical.

VI
Conclusion and Future Work

This study presents a systematic evaluation of various attention mechanisms in terms of resource efficiency during model training. Under a unified architecture and dataset, we quantitatively measured key indicators including training time, GPU power usage, and total energy consumption. The results show that Flash Attention demonstrates the best overall energy efficiency, with the lowest GPU power consumption and competitive training speed. LSH Attention and Linear Attention also achieve low total energy usage due to significantly shorter training durations. MLA ranks third in energy efficiency, benefiting from the second-lowest GPU power consumption, which compensates for its moderate training time and maintains its competitive position. Overall, the most energy-efficient mechanisms tend to strike a strong balance between low power draw and high computational speed.

These findings carry important implications for the sustainable model development. In hardware-constrained or energy-sensitive deployment scenarios, mechanisms such as Flash Attention, LSH Attention, and MLA offer higher deployment potential. Meanwhile, mechanisms like Sliding Window Attention, despite their structural complexity, do not exhibit clear advantages in terms of energy efficiency. This underscores the importance of considering both resource cost and model accuracy in the design of attention modules. Future work can expand this study by testing these mechanisms on larger open models to evaluate their scalability and generalizability. We can also benchmark on more diverse and multi-task datasets to gain insights into performance under complex workloads.

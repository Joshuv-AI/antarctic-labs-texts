Approximation of the Range Ambiguity Function in Near-field Sensing Systems
Marcin Wachowiak, André Bourdoux, Sofie Pollin

Abstract
This paper investigates the range ambiguity function of near-field systems where bandwidth and near-field beamfocusing jointly determine the resolution. First, the general matched filter ambiguity function is derived and the near-field array factors of different antenna array geometries are introduced. Next, the near-field ambiguity function is approximated as a product of the range-dependent near-field array factor and the ambiguity function due to the utilized bandwidth and waveform. An approximation criterion based on the aperture–bandwidth product is formulated, and its accuracy is examined. Finally, the improvements to the ambiguity function offered by the near-field beamfocusing, as compared to the far field case, are presented. The performance gains are evaluated in terms of resolution improvement offered by beamfocusing, peak-to-sidelobe and integrated-sidelobe level improvement. The gains offered by the near-field regime are shown to be range-dependent and substantial only in close proximity to the array.

Problem Statement

The need for high-throughput communications and high-resolution sensing drives the increase in antenna array sizes and their operating frequency

As a result, the radiative near-field region (NF) of the array is extended and it becomes feasible to consider users or targets located within it

The nonlinear phase difference can be seen as an additional source of diversity, bringing novel capabilities and performance improvements.
In the conventional far-field systems, the array steering vector is only a function of angle, while in the near-field system, it is a function of both range and angle

Owing to this, the array is able to discern users or targets at the same angle but different distances solely on the phase information (the steering vector) and with limited reliance on bandwidth
[

The gains offered by near-field systems are seen as key enablers of the next generation of communication and sensing networks, especially in bandwidth-constrained scenarios
[

When considering sensing and localization performance, the ambiguity function serves as a key performance metric. However, as the communication and sensing systems expand in size and become more distributed, the resultant ambiguity function becomes complex and cumbersome to evaluate without the use of numerical methods. The complex signal models, lack of closed-form expressions and computational burden often obscure the performance gains offered by the near-field regime.

I-C

Contributions

This work proposes a simple approximation of the ambiguity function in the near-field of an array. The total ambiguity function is decomposed into a product of the near-field array factor (AF) and the ambiguity function due to the waveform and corresponding bandwidth (BW). The approximation criterion is derived for SIMO/MISO and MIMO configurations and is expressed in terms of a bandwidth-aperture product. The accuracy of the approximation is evaluated for different configurations and array geometries.
Separate treatment of NF AF and BW ambiguity function allows for a straightforward assessment of the gains offered by the near-field region to a system with bandwidth and vice versa. When the near-field resolution achieved through beam focusing matches that provided by the bandwidth, the range-dependent array factor enhances the peak-to-sidelobe (PSL) and integrated-sidelobe (ISL) levels by at least the amount corresponding to the sidelobe levels of the near-field array factor (NF AF). The sidelobe improvement is shown to diminish with increasing distance due to the degradation of the NF beamfocusing resolution.
On the other hand, in bandwidth-limited systems, the bandwidth effectively improves the initially poor sidelobe performance of narrowband NF systems. To guarantee uniform PSL performance that matches the far-field levels, the minimum required bandwidth is computed.
Provided ambiguity function approximation can be utilized to extend CLEAN algorithms
[
24
]
for near-field scenarios and reduce their computational complexity.

\displaystyle d_{m}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=\left|\left|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}_{m}\right|\right|_{2}

\displaystyle d_{n}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=\left|\left|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}_{n}\right|\right|_{2},

(2)

A single point of interest is located at

\displaystyle h_{m,n}(\mathbf{p},f)

Each transmitter emits an identical waveform of bandwidth

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{MN}}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}S(f)S^{*}(f)

\displaystyle\quad\times h_{m,n}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},f)h_{m,n}^{*}(\mathbf{p},f)\,df.

The correlation of the observed channel with the hypothesis can be written as

\displaystyle h_{m,n}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},f)h_{m,n}^{*}(\mathbf{p},f)

\displaystyle=e^{-j2\pi\frac{f}{c}\left(\Delta d_{m}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})+\Delta d_{n}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})\right)},

(6)

where the distance difference between
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{p}

(7)

\displaystyle\Delta d_{n}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=d_{n}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime})-d_{n}(\mathbf{p}).

(8)

The bistatic distance difference between
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
observed by

th and
n
th antenna pair is

\displaystyle\Delta d_{m,n}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=\Delta d_{m}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})+\Delta d_{n}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p}).

(9)

Considering the system operates at a center frequency

, with wavelength
\lambda=c/f_{\mathrm{c}}
, the ambiguity function can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{MN}}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}}\int_{-B/2}^{B/2}\left|S(f)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\times e^{-j2\pi\frac{f_{\mathrm{c}}+f}{c}\left(\Delta d_{m}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})+\Delta d_{n}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})\right)}\,df.

(10)

After separating the center frequency components and solving the integral, the ambiguity function simplifies to

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{MN}}

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}e^{j2\pi\frac{f_{\mathrm{c}}}{c}\Delta d_{m}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}}e^{j2\pi\frac{f_{\mathrm{c}}}{c}\Delta d_{n}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sinc{\left(\frac{B}{c}\Delta d_{m,n}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})\right)},

(11)

where
\sinc{(x)}=\sin{(\pi x)}/(\pi x)
. The contribution of the bandwidth ambiguity function is different for each

pair due to varying distance difference for each TX and RX antenna.

The distance to some point

can be written as a distance to the array center and a distance correction factor due to antenna displacement with regard to the array center

\displaystyle d_{m}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=d_{m=0}(\mathbf{p})+\delta_{m}(\mathbf{p})

(12)

\displaystyle d_{n}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=d_{n=0}(\mathbf{p})+\delta_{n}(\mathbf{p}).

(13)

This allows for rewriting the distance difference (

8
) as a sum of the distance difference with regard to the array center and a distance difference correction factor due to antenna displacement

\displaystyle\Delta d_{m}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=d_{m=0}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})+\Delta\delta_{m}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})

(14)

\displaystyle\Delta d_{n}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=d_{n=0}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})+\Delta\delta_{n}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p}),

(15)

where

\displaystyle\Delta\delta_{m}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=\delta_{m}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime})-\delta_{m}(\mathbf{p}),

(16)

\displaystyle\Delta\delta_{n}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=\delta_{n}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime})-\delta_{n}(\mathbf{p}).

(17)

The bistatic distance difference correction factor is

\displaystyle\Delta\delta_{m,n}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})=\Delta\delta_{m}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})+\Delta\delta_{n}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p}).

(18)

This allows for an alternative representation of the distance difference per

Expanding the ambiguity function from (

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}e^{-j2\pi\frac{f_{\mathrm{c}}}{c}\Delta d_{m}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}}e^{-j2\pi\frac{f_{\mathrm{c}}}{c}\Delta d_{n}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})}

.

To remove the antenna dependence in the bandwidth ambiguity function, the contribution of the term

\displaystyle\frac{B}{c}|\Delta\delta_{m,n}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})|\ll 1.

Assuming the condition in (

\displaystyle\sinc{\left(\frac{B}{c}\Delta d_{m,n}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})\right)}

\displaystyle\overset{A1}{\approx}\sinc{\left(\frac{B}{c}\Delta d_{0,0}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})\right)}.

(22)

Applying the approximation to (

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})\overset{A1}{\approx}

\displaystyle\underbrace{\sinc{\left(\frac{B}{c}\Delta d_{0,0}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})\right)}}_{\chi_{B}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})}

\displaystyle\times\underbrace{\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}e^{-j2\pi\frac{f_{\mathrm{c}}}{c}\Delta d_{m}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})}}_{\mathrm{AF}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})\overset{A1}{\approx}

\displaystyle\chi_{B}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})\mathrm{AF}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})\mathrm{AF}_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p}).

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})|^{2}=

\displaystyle\left|\chi_{B}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})\right|^{2}\left|\mathrm{AF}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})\right|^{2}

(25)

This representation of the ambiguity function in the near-field allows for a straightforward assessment of the benefits of the near-field regime on systems with bandwidth and vice versa.

III
Aperture geometries

In the following, the closed-form expressions for range-dependent near-field array factors are derived for several standard geometries of antenna arrays. Considered antenna geometries are (1D) uniform linear arrays (ULA), uniform circular arrays (UCA) and (2D) uniform planar rectangular arrays (URA) and uniform planar circular arrays (UPCA). The array factors are calculated for a set
\mathcal{M}
of isotropic antennas with
|\mathcal{M}|=M
elements.
For a fair comparison with regard to NF beamfocusing performance, each array is assumed to have the same aperture
D
, which is measured as the largest dimension. For a planar rectangular array, it is the diagonal; for a planar circular array, it is the diameter.
The general form of the array factor gain is

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{AF}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})\right|^{2}=\frac{1}{M}\left|\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}e^{-j2\pi\frac{f_{\mathrm{c}}}{c}\Delta d_{m}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})}\right|^{2}.

(26)

The points in space can be described either by Cartesian coordinates
\mathbf{p}=[x,y,z]
or by their spherical equivalents
\mathbf{p}=[d,\theta,\phi]
, where
\theta
is the elevation angle and
\phi
is the azimuth angle. The origin of both polar and Cartesian coordinate systems corresponds to the centre of mass for the array.

III-A

Uniform Linear Array (ULA)

Consider the aperture is implemented as a uniform linear array. The spacing between the antenna elements is
d_{\mathrm{a}}
and the antennas are distributed along the X axis
\mathbf{p}_{m}=\left[md_{\mathrm{a}},0\right]^{T}
. The
z
dimension is dropped as it is redundant in this setup.
The distance between the
m
th antenna and some point of interest expressed in polar coordinates
\mathbf{p}=[d,\phi]^{T}
can be written as

\displaystyle d_{m}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=d\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{md_{\mathrm{a}}}{d}\right)^{2}-\frac{2(md_{\mathrm{a}})\cos{(\phi)}}{d}}.

(27)

Approximation 2
.

The minimum distance to some point of interest from the antenna array is
1.2D
. This allows for utilizing the Taylor approximation with good accuracy
[
4
,
6
]
.
The distance is approximated by Taylor expansion as follows

\displaystyle\sqrt{1+x}\overset{A2}{\approx}1+\frac{x}{2}-\frac{x^{2}}{8}.

(28)

Ignoring the terms that are scaled by

and

due to their negligible contribution allows us to write the distance in (

) as

\displaystyle d_{m}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle\overset{A2}{\approx}d-md_{\mathrm{a}}\cos{(\phi)}+\frac{(md_{\mathrm{a}})^{2}\sin^{2}{(\phi)}}{2d}.

(29)

The system operates in the near-field region, where the array factor exhibits an additional dependence on the distance. To investigate how the array factor evolves over distance, the angles
\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}
are set to be equal, allowing for the acquisition of a distance cross-cut of the AF.
The distance difference between
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{p}
located at the same angle
\phi^{\prime}
for
m
th element of ULA is

\displaystyle\Delta d_{m}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=d-d^{\prime}+\frac{(md_{\mathrm{a}})^{2}\sin^{2}(\phi^{\prime})}{2}\frac{d^{\prime}-d}{dd^{\prime}}.

(30)

The near-field array factor of the ULA is obtained by substituting the distance difference from (
30
) into the general array factor formula from (
26
).

Approximation 3
.

Given that the antenna spacing satisfies the Nyquist sampling criterion (
d_{\mathrm{a}}\leq 0.5\lambda
), the aperture can be assumed to be continuous and the array factor sum can be approximated as an integral
[
26
]
.

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{AF}_{\mathrm{ULA}}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})\right|^{2}=

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{M}\left|\sum_{-(M-1)/2}^{(M-1)/2}e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\left(d-d^{\prime}+\frac{(md_{\mathrm{a}})^{2}\sin^{2}(\phi^{\prime})}{2}\frac{d^{\prime}-d}{dd^{\prime}}\right)}\right|^{2}

(31)

\displaystyle\quad\overset{A3}{\approx}M\left|\frac{1}{M-1}\int_{-(M-1)/2}^{(M-1)/2}e^{-j\frac{\pi}{\lambda}(md_{\mathrm{a}})^{2}\sin^{2}(\phi^{\prime})\frac{d^{\prime}-d}{dd^{\prime}}}\,dm\right|^{2}.

Solving the integral, a closed-form expression is obtained

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{AF}_{\mathrm{ULA}}(d^{\prime},d)\right|^{2}=

(32)

\displaystyle=M\frac{4}{d_{\mathrm{FA}}d_{\mathrm{ver}}}\left(C^{2}\left(\sqrt{\frac{d_{\mathrm{FA}}d_{\mathrm{ver}}}{4}}\right)+S^{2}\left(\sqrt{\frac{d_{\mathrm{FA}}d_{\mathrm{ver}}}{4}}\right)\right)

d_{\mathrm{ver}}=\left|\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{d^{\prime}}\right|=\frac{|d^{\prime}-d|}{d^{\prime}d}
is the absolute value of the vergence difference
[
27
]
. The array aperture is calculated as

is the effective array aperture at an angle

are Fresnel integrals and

is the Fraunhofer distance of the array. The factor

scales the array gain according to the number of antennas.
Notice that for angles other than

Uniform Circular Array (UCA)

Applying the Taylor approximation from (

\displaystyle d_{m}(\mathbf{p})

Setting equal

V
Performance improvement metrics

The key sensing performance metrics often used in system analysis and design are range resolution, peak-to-sidelobe level (PSL), and integrated sidelobe level (ISL). In the following, the impact of the NF regime on these metrics and its potential improvements are analyzed.

V-A

Resolution

The resolution provided by the NF beamfocusing is range-dependent, with the beamdepth function growing very rapidly as the distance from the array increases (
43
). Generally, the range-dependent resolution is undesirable; therefore, the addition of bandwidth is crucial to provide uniform resolution within a specified region. Depending on the amount of bandwidth, the near-field region might be separated into two subregions in which the resolution is dominated by the NF beamfocusing or bandwidth. As bandwidth is a scarce resource, one should expect NF systems where the NF-dominated resolution region is always present.

The boundary distance which separates the near-field and the bandwidth resolution dominated regions can be calculated by equating the BD (

[1.2D,d^{\prime}_{\lambda}]
and the BW dominated region is
[d^{\prime}_{\mathrm{N/F}},d_{\mathrm{FA}}/\alpha]
. Based on this division, one can calculate the percentage of the NF region where the resolution is better than the one provided by BW (NF-dominated region). Fig.

V-B

Peak-to-sidelobe level (PSL)

Consider a system with a bandwidth that guarantees identical resolution due to NF beamfocusing and BW. The range-dependent AF modulates the BW ambiguity function, improving the PSL. However, as the range increases, the width of the NF AF beam that attenuates the sidelobes widens and the PSL improvement diminishes. Fig.
8
illustrates the range-dependent PSL gain provided by the NF.
Notice that the PSL gain is not limited by the PSL of NF AF, since the BW ambiguity function and NF AF are not correlated, resulting in greater improvement than the measured PSL levels of the NF AF alone.
The MIMO configuration offers a twofold improvement in the PSL gain in decibel scale due to squaring of the NF AF.

Consider a scenario in which the near-field range-dependent resolution is treated opportunistically, with the key objective of the system to provide uniform PSL performance across all distances. Introducing bandwidth and the BW ambiguity function helps to suppress the undesirable sidelobe levels of the NF AF.
As the total ambiguity function is a product of the BW ambiguity function and NF AF, the sidelobe performance can be further improved by conventional windowing of the transmitted signal spectrum in the frequency domain.

V-C

Integrated sidelobe level (ISL)

The NF beamfocusing also improves the integrated sidelobe levels by suppressing higher-order sidelobes of the ambiguity function due to bandwidth. Consider a system where the resolution due to bandwidth is equal to the minimum beamdepth (beamfocusing resolution). The range-dependent NF AF effectively suppresses the sidelobes in the close proximity to the array. However, as the distance increases, the ISL gains diminish due to the widening beamdepth of the NF AF function. Fig.
10
illustrates the ISL gain as a function of distance. The MIMO configuration offers approximately a twofold improvement in the gain in decibel scale due to squaring of the NF AF.

VI
Conclusion

This paper presents an approximation of the near-field ambiguity function by separating it into a product of the ambiguity function due to bandwidth and the near-field array factor. The approximation constraint is derived as a bandwidth-aperture product for different configurations, SIMO/MISO and MIMO and array geometries. Separation of the ambiguity function into two factors allows for analyzing the benefits that the near-field offers to systems with bandwidth and vice versa. When the resolution due to bandwidth is comparable to the near-field beamfocusing, the PSL and ISL metrics of the composite AF are greatly improved as the NF AF serves as a mask, suppressing the sidelobes. The gains are observed to diminish with growing distance due to the widening of the beamdepth function. Alternatively, when considering NF-centric systems, the bandwidth can be used to improve the poor sidelobe levels of the NF AF. The minimum bandwidth guaranteeing uniform sidelobe performance matching the far-field is shown to be lower than the one corresponding to the beamfocusing resolution.

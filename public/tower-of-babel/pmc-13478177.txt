Design of a planar gradient-index dielectric lens for arbitrary point-to-point beam focusing using critical-angle theory
Authors: Mohammad Mahdi Taskhiri, Mohammad Moradi, Saeed Fakhte
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper presents a novel analytical design method for a planar gradient-index (GRIN) dielectric lens based on the critical-angle theorem, capable of focusing electromagnetic waves between two arbitrarily located points. Unlike conventional curved lenses, the proposed flat lens structure enables flexible beam pointing and precise wavefront control without mechanical movement. The refractive index profile is derived analytically to ensure phase matching and minimal internal reflection. The design is validated through full-wave numerical simulations in COMSOL Multiphysics, demonstrating effective beam focusing, and improved impedance matching. The proposed lens, validated here in the 4–7 THz band, exhibits significant potential for frequency-scalable applications ranging from Terahertz imaging to 5G/6G communications, satellite links, and radar systems where versatile beam alignment is required.Keywords: Ray trajectories in inhomogeneous media, Lens, Beam concentratorSubject terms: Engineering, Optics and photonics, Physics

Introduction

The rapid advancement of satellite communications1, automotive radar2, fifth-generation (5G) and beyond networks3, and medical hyperthermia systems4 has driven an increasing demand for high-performance, beam-steerable antenna systems. Among various antenna technologies, dielectric lens antennas have attracted significant attention due to their ability to provide wide bandwidth, high gain, and low propagation losses at millimeter-wave frequencies5,6. By concentrating electromagnetic waves in a desired direction, dielectric lenses effectively enhance signal strength while minimizing spatial spreading and sidelobe levels7.

Dielectric lenses are generally categorized into homogeneous and inhomogeneous (gradient-index, GRIN) types. Homogeneous lenses offer simplicity and uniform wavefront distribution, making them suitable for many optical systems8. However, they suffer from inherent limitations such as low gain, substantial thickness, and limited design flexibility due to their uniform refractive index distribution9. In contrast, GRIN lenses allow the refractive index to vary continuously or discretely within the lens volume, enabling precise control over the ray paths and wavefront phase. This capability facilitates reduced internal reflection, lower sidelobes, and improved focusing efficiency10.

Despite these advantages, most reported GRIN lens designs are either curved (e.g., hemispherical or Luneburg-type) or restricted to fixed focal points11,12. Curved lenses introduce fabrication complexity and integration challenges in planar systems, while fixed-focus designs lack the adaptability required for dynamic communication links where transmitter and receiver positions may vary arbitrarily. In many practical scenarios–such as conformal antennas on vehicles, low-profile satellite terminals, or integrated photonic circuits–a flat lens architecture is essential to meet spatial constraints and ensure seamless integration with planar platforms. Consequently, there is a pressing need for a planar dielectric lens that can dynamically focus waves between two arbitrarily located points without mechanical reorientation or complex beamforming networks. Recent efforts in GRIN lens modeling have also focused on simplified ray-tracing approaches for miniaturized probes13, underscoring the need for accessible design tools in the research community.

Parallel to GRIN lens development, analytical design methods for flat optical components have also advanced. For instance, recent work on metasurface-based flat lenses has employed analytical formulations derived from Fermat’s principle and explicit ray mapping to achieve aberration-free focusing with planar phase profiles14. While powerful for discrete-phase or homogeneous substrates, such approaches highlight the growing importance of analytical wavefront engineering in flat optics. In contrast to phase-only metasurfaces or fixed-index lenses, the present work introduces a continuous gradient-index profile for a planar dielectric lens using the critical-angle theorem and Ray-Specific Coordinate Mapping. This method enables arbitrary point-to-point focusing without curved surfaces, discrete phase arrays, or mechanical steering, thereby bridging a gap between traditional GRIN lens design and reconfigurable flat-beamforming devices.

In this paper, we propose a novel analytical design method for a planar GRIN dielectric lens based on the critical-angle theorem, enabling continuous beam pointing between arbitrary transmitter and receiver locations. The refractive index profile is derived to ensure phase matching and minimal reflection at the lens boundaries. The design is validated through full-wave numerical simulations in COMSOL Multiphysics, demonstrating effective focusing performance, and improved impedance matching. The proposed flat lens architecture offers significant advantages in terms of fabrication simplicity, integration compatibility, and reconfigurability for modern wireless systems. It is important to note that the design of such a lens involves significant analytical complexity, particularly due to the need for repeated coordinate transformations from the global Cartesian system to multiple rotated coordinate frames, each aligned with the incident ray direction. This step is crucial for accurately modeling wave propagation and ensuring phase matching across the lens aperture.

It is worth noting that while the results are presented in the 4–7 THz band to demonstrate a compact proof-of-concept, the proposed analytical framework is inherently frequency-scalable. Due to the non-resonant nature of the GRIN profile, the design principles remain robust for lower frequency regimes, such as the 24–100 GHz millimeter-wave spectrum essential for 5G/6G and satellite communications, by simply applying a linear scaling factor to the lens dimensions.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 presents the theoretical foundation and design methodology. Section 3 describes the simulation setup and numerical results. Section 4 discusses the performance analysis and comparison with existing approaches. Finally, Section 5 concludes the paper and suggests future research directions.

Simulation and results

Simulation setup

The proposed planar GRIN lens was modeled and analyzed using the full-wave electromagnetic simulation software COMSOL Multiphysics (version 6.1). The Electromagnetic Waves, Frequency Domain (emw) and Geometrical Optics modules were employed to solve Maxwell’s equations in the frequency domain. Perfectly Matched Layer (PML) boundaries were applied around the simulation domain to absorb outgoing waves and mimic an open space environment. An adaptive mesh was used, ensuring convergence of the electric field solution with an element size smaller than λ /10 at the highest frequency of interest (7 THz). A point source with a Gaussian beam profile was placed at the transmitter location, and the receiver point was set at the desired focal position as defined in Table 1. Also, the transmitter was modeled as a two-dimensional feed horn operating in the fundamental TE mode, providing a realistic wavefront for evaluating lens performance.

For the full-wave numerical validation, the spatially varying refractive index profile derived from the Ray-Specific Coordinate Mapping was implemented in COMSOL Multiphysics as a static inhomogeneous medium. The permittivity distribution, , was defined using the “Analytic Function” and “Variables” nodes, allowing the solver to treat the lens as a deterministic material during the mesh assembly.

A set of rays emanating from the transmitter point at different initial angles were traced through the lens medium. The refractive index profile defined by Eq. 11 ensures that each ray is bent appropriately according to the critical-angle theorem and the phase-matching condition.

Discussion

The simulation results presented in Section 3 demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed planar GRIN lens design based on the critical-angle theorem and coordinated coordinate transformations. This section provides a detailed analysis of the key findings, explains the physical mechanisms behind the observed performance, and discusses the implications of the design choices.

The most notable improvement in the 2D GRIN lens, compared to its 1D counterpart, is the sharp and well-defined focal spot at the desired receiver location (Figs. 7 and 8). This is a direct consequence of the enforced phase-matching condition (Eq. 3) and the carefully derived refractive index profile (Eq. 10). The planar geometry, combined with the gradient index, allows for effective beam bending without the need for a curved surface, which is a significant advantage for integration into flat panel systems.

The electric field intensity plots (Figs. 7 and 8) confirm that the energy is concentrated within a small region around the receiver point. This is particularly important for applications such as point-to-point wireless links and radar systems, where interference and multipath reflections must be minimized.

The electric field intensity distributions (Figs. 8 and 10) reveal a slight displacement of the peak intensity from the predefined geometric focal point. This focal shift is a known phenomenon in dielectric lens systems, particularly for finite-aperture designs operating in the Fresnel region15,16. The shift originates from the amplitude taper of the incident field: while the optical path length is equalized for all rays to satisfy phase matching, the field amplitude decays as 1/r from the source. Consequently, rays arriving at points closer to the lens contribute with higher amplitudes, leading to a constructive interference peak that is shifted toward the lens relative to the geometric focus. The extent of this shift depends on the frequency and lens dimensions; higher frequencies (or larger electrical apertures) produce a narrower focusing zone (1-dB beamwidth), thereby reducing the apparent focal shift and improving targeting accuracy. This behavior confirms that the lens design maintains phase coherence while exhibiting realistic amplitude-based focusing characteristics typical of practical beam-forming systems.

The proposed lens maintains consistent focusing performance across a wide frequency range, as evidenced by the field intensity profiles shown in Figs. 10 and 11. This broadband behavior stems from the non-resonant, smoothly graded index profile, which avoids sharp impedance discontinuities that typically induce narrowband resonances.

Furthermore, the ability to focus waves between arbitrarily located points distinguishes this work from fixed-focus designs reported in the literature8,12. This flexibility is achieved through the analytical coordinate transformation procedure described in Section 2, which adapts the phase front for any given transmitter–receiver geometry.

Fabrication feasibility and tolerance analysis

To ensure the practical feasibility of the proposed GRIN lens, two primary fabrication routes are envisioned. For the microwave regime, Fused Filament Fabrication (FFF) using ceramic-loaded thermoplastics (e.g., PREPERM) allows for local permittivity control by varying infill density18,19. For THz applications, High-Resistivity Float Zone Silicon (HRFZ-Si) is identified as the optimal material due to its exceptionally low loss ()20,21. The gradient profile can be realized by creating sub-wavelength air-filled pores using deep reactive ion etching (DRIE), or alternatively, via 3D-printing techniques such as Stereolithography (SLA) and projection micro-lithography, which have been successfully employed to fabricate 3D THz GRIN lenses22,23.

The impact of manufacturing imperfections is quantified in Table 5. Numerical results indicate that a deviation in local permittivity –typical for 3D-printed photopolymers24 – results in a minor longitudinal focal shift of ≈ 0.03 mm, while maintaining peak intensity within 0.3 dB of the reference. However, the design is more sensitive to geometric thickness; an error of can lead to a gain reduction of up to 1.38 dB and an increase in sidelobe levels. Therefore, a fabrication tolerance of is recommended to maintain optimal gain and SLL levels. These results confirm that the analytical design remains robust under standard micro-fabrication tolerances.

Conclusion

This paper has presented an analytical design methodology for a planar gradient-index (GRIN) dielectric lens capable of focusing electromagnetic waves between two arbitrarily located points. The design leverages the critical-angle theorem combined with Ray-Specific Coordinate Mapping to control the wavefront phase. The refractive index profile is derived analytically to satisfy the optical path length equality condition, ensuring constructive interference at the desired receiver point. To mitigate reflections and improve impedance matching, a two-dimensional grading function is introduced, which smoothly varies the permittivity from the lens edges to the center. Full-wave simulations in COMSOL Multiphysics validated the design, demonstrating significant performance improvements over a conventional one-dimensional GRIN structure. The electric field distributions confirmed effective beam concentration at the target location with suppressed sidelobes. The planar architecture of the lens offers practical advantages in terms of fabrication simplicity and integration into flat-panel systems, making it suitable for emerging applications in Terahertz imaging, 5G/6G communications, satellite terminals, automotive radar, and conformal antennas.

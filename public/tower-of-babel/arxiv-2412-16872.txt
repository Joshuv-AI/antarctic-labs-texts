Modified scattering for the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation with long-range potentials in one space dimension
Masaki Kawamoto, Haruya Mizutani

Abstract
. We consider the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation with long-range linear potentials in one space dimension, and prove the modified scattering in the energy space for the associated final state problem with a prescribed small asymptotic profile. Compared with the leading term of the free solution, the asymptotic profile has an additional phase correction depending both on the long-range part of the potential and on the nonlinear term. The proof is based on a simple energy method and does not rely on global-in-time Strichartz estimates for Schrödinger equations with linear potentials. In particular, the class of potentials to which our theorem applies is large enough to accommodate slowly decaying negative potentials so that the associated Schrödinger operators may have negative eigenvalues.

1.
Introduction

The present paper is concerned with the scattering problem for the following cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) with a linear potential in one space dimension:

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u-H_{0}u-Vu=F(u),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}},

(
1.1)

where
u=u(t,x)
is a
{\mathbb{C}}
-valued unknown function,
F(u)=\lambda|u|^{2}u
with
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
H_{0}+V
is the Schrödinger operator with a real-valued potential
V
:

H_{0}:=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}},\quad V=V(x):{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}.

We are interested in the (small data) modified scattering for the associated finial state problem. More precisely, given a prescribed sufficiently small asymptotic profile function
u_{\mathrm{p}}(t,x)
, we construct a unique global solution
u\in C({\mathbb{R}};H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}))
to
(​​

1.1
)
which scatters to
u_{\mathrm{p}}
as
t\to\infty
:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|u(t)-u_{\mathrm{p}}(t)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}=0,

where
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})
denotes the
L^{2}
-Sobolev space of order
s
. Here the term “modified” means that the asymptotic profile
u_{\mathrm{p}}
has an additional correction term compared with the leading term of the free solution
e^{-itH_{0}}u_{+}
, depending both on
F(u)
and on the long-range part of
V
(see
(​​

1.5
)
for the definition of
u_{\mathrm{p}}
). This gives the first positive result on the modified scattering for
(​​

1.1
)
, and is a continuation of our resent work
[
18
]
for the two and three space dimensional cases.

To state the result, we first introduce the class of potentials.

Assumption A
.

V
is decomposed into the short-range, long-range and compactly supported singular parts as
V=V^{\mathrm{S}}+V^{\mathrm{L}}+V^{\mathrm{C}}
with real-valued functions
V^{\mathrm{S}},V^{\mathrm{L}},V^{\mathrm{C}}
satisfying following:

•

Short-range part:
V^{\mathrm{S}}\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
and there exists
\rho_{\mathrm{S}}>3/2
such that, for any
k=0,1
,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}V^{\mathrm{S}}(x)\right|\lesssim{\langle}x{\rangle}^{-\rho_{\mathrm{S}}}

•

Long-range part:
V^{\mathrm{L}}\in{C^{3}}({\mathbb{R}})
and there exists
\rho_{\mathrm{L}}>1/2
such that, for any
k=0,1,2,3
,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}V^{\mathrm{L}}(x)\right|\lesssim{\langle}x{\rangle}^{-\rho_{\mathrm{L}}-k}.

•

Compactly supported singular part:
V^{\mathrm{C}}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and
V^{\mathrm{C}}
is compactly supported.

Under Assumption
A
,
V
is infinitesimally
H_{0}
-bounded, that is, for any
{\varepsilon}>0
there exists
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\|Vf\|_{L^{2}}\leq{\varepsilon}\|H_{0}f\|_{L^{2}}+C_{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}},\quad f\in H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}).

In particular,
H:=H_{0}+V
with domain
D(H)=H^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
is self-adjoint on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
by the Kato–Rellich theorem, and generates the associated unitary group
e^{-itH}
on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. A typical example of
V
we have in mind is of the form

V(x)=Z{\langle}x{\rangle}^{-\rho}+V^{\mathrm{C}}(x)

with some
\rho>1/2
and
Z\neq 0
. When
Z<0
, it is well known that
H
has negative eigenvalues.

We next introduce the asymptotic profile
u_{\mathrm{p}}
. Let
c_{0}>0
and
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
be such that
0\leq\chi\leq 1
,
\chi(x)=1
for
|x|\leq c_{0}/4
and
\chi(x)=0
for
|x|\geq{c_{0}/3}
. Define an effective potential

\displaystyle V_{T_{1}}(t,x)=V^{\mathrm{L}}(x)\left\{1-\chi\left(\frac{2x}{t+T_{1}}\right)\right\},

(
1.2)

where
T_{1}\geq 1
is a large constant specified in Proposition
1.1
below. It is easily seen that

•

V_{T_{1}}\equiv V^{\mathrm{L}}
if
t\geq 0
and
|x|\geq c_{0}(t+T_{1})/4
;

•

V_{T_{1}}\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty);C^{3}({\mathbb{R}}))
and
|\partial_{x}^{k}V_{T_{1}}(t,x)|\leq C_{\alpha}{\langle}t{\rangle}^{-\rho_{\mathrm{L}}-k}
on
[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
c_{0}>0
. Then, for sufficiently large
T_{1}>0
, one can construct a global solution
\Psi\in C^{1}([1,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}})\cap C([1,\infty);C^{3}({\mathbb{R}}))
to the Hamilton–Jacobi equation

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}\Psi(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{x}\Psi(t,x)|^{2}+V_{T_{1}}(t,x)

(
1.3)

such that, for

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{x}^{k}\left(\Psi(t,x)-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim\begin{cases}{\langle}t{\rangle}^{1-\rho_{\mathrm{L}}-k}&\mbox{if}\ \rho_{\mathrm{L}}\neq 1,\\
\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-k}\log(tT_{1}^{-1}+1)&\mbox{if}\ \rho_{\mathrm{L}}=1.\end{cases}

(
1.4)

In particular, for any
\rho_{\mathrm{L}}^{\prime}<\rho_{\mathrm{L}}
,

\left\|\partial_{x}^{k}\left(\Psi(t,x)-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim{\langle}t{\rangle}^{1-\rho_{\mathrm{L}}^{\prime}-k},\quad t\geq 1,\quad k=1,2,3.

Proof.

The proposition is basically well-known in the context of long-range linear scattering theory (see e.g.
[
5

). Moreover, the proof is essentially same as that of
[
18

∎

Using the function
\Psi(t,x)
, we define

by

\displaystyle u_{\mathrm{p}}(t,x)=[\mathcal{M}_{\Psi}(t)\mathcal{D}(t)w_{\mathrm{p}}](t,x)=(it)^{-1/2}e^{i\Psi(t,x)}e^{-i\lambda|\widehat{u_{+}}(x/t)|^{2}\log|t|}\widehat{u_{+}}(x/t),

(

where
u_{+}(x)
is a given function (often called the scattering datum) and

\displaystyle w_{\mathrm{p}}(t,x)

\displaystyle=e^{-i\lambda|\widehat{u_{+}}(x)|^{2}\log|t|}\widehat{u_{+}}(x),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(t)f(x)

\displaystyle=(it)^{-1/2}f(x/t),

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\Psi}(t)f(x)

\displaystyle=e^{i\Psi(t,x)}f(x),

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(x)={\mathcal{F}}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(\xi)d\xi.

We here recall that the leading term of the free solution
e^{-itH_{0}}u_{+}
satisfies

e^{-itH_{0}}u_{+}=\mathcal{M}(t)\mathcal{D}(t)\widehat{u_{+}}+o(1)

in

\mathcal{M}(t)f(x)=e^{i|x|^{2}/(2t)}f(x)
and the phase function
\Psi_{0}=|x|^{2}/(2t)
solves the free Hamilton–Jacobi equation

. Thus, compared with this free profile
\mathcal{M}(t)\mathcal{D}(t)\widehat{u_{+}}
,

has the additional phase correction terms
e^{-i\lambda|\widehat{u_{+}}(x/t)|^{2}\log|t|}
and
e^{i(\Psi-\Psi_{0})}
depending on
F(u)
and
V^{\mathrm{L}}
, respectively.

Let
H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}})
be the weighted
L^{2}
-Sobolev space defined by

\displaystyle H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}})

\displaystyle=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})\ |\ \|f\|_{H^{s,r}}<\infty\},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{s,r}}

\displaystyle=\|{\langle}\partial_{x}{\rangle}^{s}{\langle}x{\rangle}^{r}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}=\|{\langle}\xi{\rangle}^{s}{\mathcal{F}}[{\langle}x{\rangle}^{r}f]\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})},

where
{\langle}x{\rangle}=\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}
. Note that
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})=H^{s,0}({\mathbb{R}})
. We now state the result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
V
satisfies Assumption
A
. Let
c_{0}>0
and
T_{1}
in Proposition
1.1
be fixed. Let

\delta\leq 1

\frac{1}{2}<\delta<\min\{\rho_{\mathrm{L}},\rho_{\mathrm{S}}-1\}.

Then, for any
u_{+}\in H^{1,2\delta}({\mathbb{R}})

\|\widehat{u_{+}}\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits\widehat{u_{+}}\subset\{|\xi|\geq c_{0}\}
, there exists a unique solution
u\in C({\mathbb{R}};H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}))
to
(​​

1.1
)
satisfying the prescribed asymptotic condition:

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{\mathrm{p}}(t)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim t^{-\delta}(\log t)^{b},\quad t\to\infty.

(
1.6)

Remark 1.3
.

Since
\|u_{\mathrm{p}}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq|t|^{-1/2}\|\widehat{u_{+}}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u-H_{0}u-Vu=\lambda|u|^{2/d}u,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad d=2,3.

Preliminary materials

We here prepare several basic facts used in the paper. In what follows we use the notation

\|f\|=\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})},\quad\|f\|_{s}=\|f\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})}.

Let
U_{0}(t)=e^{-itH_{0}}
be the free Schrödinger group, which satisfies the Dollard decomposition

\displaystyle U_{0}(t)\mathcal{F}^{-1}=\mathcal{M}(t)\mathcal{D}(t)\mathcal{F}\mathcal{M}(t)\mathcal{F}^{-1}=\mathcal{M}(t)\mathcal{D}(t)+\mathcal{M}(t)\mathcal{D}(t)\mathcal{R}(t),

(
2.1)

where
\mathcal{D}(t)
and
\mathcal{M}(t)
have been defined in the introduction, and

\mathcal{R}(t)={\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{M}(t)-1){\mathcal{F}}^{-1}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}(t)f\|_{L^{p}}=|t|^{-1/2+1/p}\|f\|_{L^{p}},\quad t\neq 0.

(
2.2)

Moreover, for
0\leq\delta\leq 1
,
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
t\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}(t)f\|_{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-\delta}\|f\|_{s+2\delta},

(
2.3)

\displaystyle\|xR(t)f\|

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-\delta}\|xf\|_{2\delta}+|t|^{-1}\|f\|_{1},

(
2.4)

where the implicit constants independent of
t
.

Proof.

(​​

2.2
)
follows by an elementary calculation. To prove
(​​

2.3
)
, it is enough to observe

|e^{i|x|^{2}/(2t)}-1|=2\left|\sin\frac{x^{2}}{4t}\right|\lesssim\left|\frac{x^{2}}{t}\right|^{\delta}

for

, and
\mathcal{R}(t)
commutes with
{\langle}\partial_{x}{\rangle}^{s}
. Finally, we have

x\mathcal{R}(t)=\mathcal{F}\{(\mathcal{M}-1)(-i\partial_{\xi})+t^{-1}\mathcal{M}\xi\}\mathcal{F}^{-1}=\mathcal{R}x+it^{-1}\mathcal{F}\mathcal{M}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\partial_{x}

and
(​​

2.4
)
follows.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For all
z_{0},z_{1}\in{\mathbb{C}}
,

F(z_{1})=F(z_{0})+2\lambda|z_{0}|^{2}(z_{1}-z_{0})+\lambda z_{0}^{2}\overline{(z_{1}-z_{0})}+G(z_{1}-z_{0},z_{0}),

where
G(z,z_{0})=2\lambda\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[zz_{0}]z+\lambda|z|^{2}z_{0}+\lambda|z|^{2}z

Proof.

The lemma follows by a direct calculations or applying the Taylor formula

f(z_{1})=f(z_{0})+(z_{1}-z_{0})\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{z}f(z_{\theta})d\theta+\overline{(z_{1}-z_{0})}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{\overline{z}}f(z_{\theta})d\theta,

to

, where

.
∎

To estimate the

-norm of the terms

and

defined in

1.5
)
, we use the following

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

. Then, for all

,

\displaystyle\|w_{\mathrm{p}}(t)\|_{s}\lesssim{\langle}\log t{\rangle}^{\lceil s\rceil}(1+\|\widehat{u_{+}}\|_{s}^{1+2\lceil s\rceil}),\quad\|F(w_{\mathrm{p}}(t))\|_{s}\lesssim{\langle}\log t{\rangle}^{\lceil s\rceil}(1+\|\widehat{u_{+}}\|_{s}^{3+2\lceil s\rceil}),

Proof.

The case
1/2<s<2
has been proved by
[
13
, Lemma 4]
(see also
[
18
, Lemma 2.2]
and its proof). For
s=2,3
, the desired bound can be verified easily by calculating
\partial_{x}^{s}w_{\mathrm{p}}
and
\partial_{x}^{s}F(w_{\mathrm{p}})

and define
I:=i\bar{u}(\partial_{x}u)+iu\overline{(\partial_{x}u)}

\displaystyle e^{-i|u|^{2}}\partial_{x}^{2}(e^{i|u|^{2}}u)=I^{2}u+2I(\partial_{x}u)+(\partial_{x}^{2}u)+(\partial_{x}I)u.

Hence for
0<s^{\prime}=s-2<1
, it is enough to deal with

\displaystyle\|e^{i|u|^{2}}I^{2}u\|_{s^{\prime}},\quad\|e^{i|u|^{2}}I(\partial_{x}u)\|_{s^{\prime}},\quad\|e^{i|u|^{2}}(\partial_{x}I)u\|_{s^{\prime}}.

By
[
18

, the above three terms can be estimated as

\displaystyle(1+\|u\|_{s}^{2})\|I^{2}u\|_{s^{\prime}},\quad(1+\|u\|_{s}^{2})\|I(\partial_{x}u)\|_{s^{\prime}},\quad(1+\|u\|_{s}^{2})\|(\partial_{x}I)u\|_{s^{\prime}}.

By the sharp fractional Leibniz (see, e.g., Theorem 1 of Grafakos-Oh
[
11
]
)

\displaystyle\|{\langle}\partial_{x}{\rangle}^{s^{\prime}}(fg)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|{\langle}\partial_{x}{\rangle}^{s^{\prime}}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{{q_{1}}}}+\|{\langle}\partial_{x}{\rangle}^{s^{\prime}}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}}\|f\|_{L^{{q_{2}}}},

where
1/p_{j}+1/q_{j}=1
and
1<p_{1},p_{2},q_{1},q_{2}\leq\infty
, and the Sobolev embedding, we have

\displaystyle\left\|I^{2}u\right\|_{s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|I^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{s^{\prime}}+\|I^{2}\|_{s^{\prime}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|I^{2}\|_{1}\|u\|_{1}\lesssim\|u\|_{2}^{5},

\displaystyle\left\|I(\partial_{x}u)\right\|_{s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|I\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}u\|_{s^{\prime}}+\|I\|_{s^{\prime}}\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|I\|_{1}\|u\|_{2}\lesssim\|u\|_{2}^{3},

\displaystyle\left\|(\partial_{x}I)u\right\|_{s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(\partial_{x}I)\|_{s^{\prime}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|(\partial_{x}I)\|\|\left\langle\partial_{x}\right\rangle^{s^{\prime}}u\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|(\partial_{x}^{2}u)u\|_{s^{\prime}}+\|(\partial_{x}u)^{2}\|_{s^{\prime}}\right)\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|I\|_{1}\|u\|_{1+s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|u\|_{s}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{2}\|(\partial_{x}u)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{1+s^{\prime}}\|(\partial_{x}u)\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\|u\|_{1}+\|u\|_{2}^{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{s}^{3},

and hence

\displaystyle\|e^{i|u|^{2}}u\|_{s}\lesssim(1+\|u\|_{s}^{1+2\lceil s\rceil}),\quad\|e^{i|u|^{2}}F(u)\|_{s}\lesssim(1+\|u\|_{s}^{3+2\lceil s\rceil}).

By taking

, we obtain the desired estimates. ∎

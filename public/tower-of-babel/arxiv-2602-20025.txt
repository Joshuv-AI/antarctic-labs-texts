Arithmetic properties of DSOME function
Nayandeep Deka Baruah, Pankaj Gogoi

Abstract
. Recently, Andrews and Ghosh Dastidar (Ramanujan J. 69 , Article Number 26, 2026) studied two interesting functions S ​ O ​ M ​ E ​ ( n ) SOME(n) and D ​ S ​ O ​ M ​ E ​ ( n ) DSOME(n) , where S ​ O ​ M ​ E ​ ( n ) SOME(n) is the sum of all the odd parts in the partitions of n n minus the sum of all even parts and D ​ S ​ O ​ M ​ E ​ ( n ) DSOME(n) is the sum of all the odd parts in the partitions of n n into distinct parts minus sum of all the even parts. They expressed the generating functions of S ​ O ​ M ​ E ​ ( n ) SOME(n) and D ​ S ​ O ​ M ​ E ​ ( n ) DSOME(n) in terms of q q -series and found several interesting congruences modulo 4 and 5. In this paper, we express the generating function of D ​ S ​ O ​ M ​ E ​ ( n ) DSOME(n) in a closed form, which allows us to find some new congruences and internal congruences modulo 4 and 8 for D ​ S ​ O ​ M ​ E ​ ( n ) DSOME(n) .

1.
Introduction

For complex numbers
a
and
q
with
|q|<1,
define the standard
q
-
products

(a;q)_{0}=1,\,(a;q)_{n}:=\displaystyle\prod_{n=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{n}),\,\text{and}\,(a;q)_{\infty}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}(a;q)_{n}.

In the sequel, we set
f_{n}:=(q^{n};q^{n})_{\infty}
for integers
n\geq 1.

A partition
\lambda:=\left(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dotsc,\lambda_{k}\right)
of a positive integer
n
is a finite non-increasing sequence of positive integers
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dotsc,\lambda_{k}

such that
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\dotsc+\lambda_{k}=n.
The partition function
p(n)
is defined as the number of partitions of
n.
It is well known that the generating function of
p(n)
is given by

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p(n)q^{n}=\frac{1}{f_{1}},

where by convention, we set
p(0)=1.
The arithmetic properties of the partition function have been studied extensively after Ramanujan
[
6
]
found his famous congruences modulo 5, 7 and 11, namely, for all
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle p(5n+4)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{5},

\displaystyle p(7n+5)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{7},

\displaystyle p(11n+6)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{11}.

Recently, Andrews and Ghosh Dastidar
[
1
]
studied two interesting functions related to partitions, namely,
SOME(n)
and
DSOME(n)
, where
SOME(n)
is the sum of all the odd parts in the partitions of
n
minus the sum of all even parts and
DSOME(n)
is the sum of all the odd parts in the partitions of
n
into distinct parts minus sum of all the even parts. They found that the generating functions of
SOME(n)
and
DSOME(n)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}SOME(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{q^{m}}{\left(1+q^{m}\right)^{2}}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}DSOME(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{m-1}q^{m}}{\left(1+q^{m}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{(2n-1)q^{2n-1}}{1+q^{2n-1}}-\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(2n)q^{2n}}{1+q^{2n}}\right).

(1.1)

They also proved the following beautiful congruences. For
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle SOME(5n+2)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{5},

\displaystyle SOME(5n+4)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{5},

\displaystyle SOME(4n)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{4},

\displaystyle DSOME(4n)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{4}.

Moreover, they also conjectured that if
24\lambda\equiv 1\pmod{5^{\alpha}}
, then

\displaystyle SOME(\lambda)\equiv 0\pmod{5^{\alpha}}.

In this paper, we express the generating function (
1.1
) of
DSOME(n)
in a closed form (see Theorem
1.1
below). That allows us to find some new congruences and internal congruences for
DSOME(n)
modulo 4 and 8. We present the main results in the following four theorems.

Theorem 1.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}DSOME(n)q^{n}=\frac{1}{8}\bigg(\frac{f_{2}}{f_{1}}-\frac{f_{1}^{7}}{f_{2}^{3}}\bigg).

(1.2)

Theorem 1.2
.

For all
n\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle DSOME(25n+5r+1)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{4},~where~1\leq r\leq 4.

Theorem 1.3
.

For all
n\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle DSOME(625n+25r+1)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{8},~where~1\leq r\leq 4.

Theorem 1.4
.

For all
n\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle DSOME(15625n+651)

\displaystyle\equiv 5DSOME(25n+1)+DSOME(625n+26)

\displaystyle\quad-5DSOME(n)\pmod{8}

and

\displaystyle DSOME(15625n+3125r+651)

\displaystyle\equiv 5DSOME(25n+5r+1)

\displaystyle\quad+DSOME(625n+125r+26)\pmod{8},

where
1\leq r\leq 4
.

We organize the paper in the following way. In Sect.
2
, we present some preliminary definitions and lemmas that are useful for proving our theorems. In Sect.
3
, we prove Theorems
1.1
–
1.4
. In the final section, we propose some conjectural congruences for
DSOME(n)
modulo 8 and 16.

2.
Preliminary definitions and lemmas

The Rogers-Ramanujan identities are given by

\displaystyle G(q):

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n}}=\frac{1}{(q;\;q^{5})_{\infty}(q^{4};\;q^{5})_{\infty}}

(2.1)

and

\displaystyle H(q):

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n^{2}+n}}{(q;q)_{n}}=\frac{1}{(q^{2};\;q^{5})_{\infty}(q^{3};\;q^{5})_{\infty}},

(2.2)

where
G(q)
and
H(q)

]

\displaystyle R(q)=q^{1/5}\dfrac{H(q)}{G(q)},

\displaystyle R(q):

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{1/5}}{1}\raisebox{-7.39772pt}{$+$}\frac{q}{1}\raisebox{-7.39772pt}{$+$}\frac{q^{2}}{1}\raisebox{-7.39772pt}{$+\cdots$},~|q|<1.

Some useful results are presented in the following lemmas. For the first two lemmas, we set

\displaystyle T(q):=\frac{q^{1/5}}{R(q)}=\dfrac{G(q)}{H(q)}.

(2.3)

Lemma 2.1
.

(Baruah and Begum
[
2
]
)

If

and

then

\displaystyle xy^{2}-\frac{q^{2}}{xy^{2}}

\displaystyle=K,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{x^{2}}{y}-\frac{y}{x^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4q}{K},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\frac{y^{3}}{x}+q^{2}\frac{x}{y^{3}}

\displaystyle=K+\frac{4q^{2}}{K}-2q,

(2.6)

\displaystyle x^{3}y+\frac{q^{2}}{x^{3}y}

\displaystyle=K+\frac{4q^{2}}{K}+2q,

(2.7)

\displaystyle x^{5}-\dfrac{q^{2}}{x^{5}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{f_{1}^{6}}{f_{5}^{6}}+11q,

(2.8)

where

In the next lemma, we present two well-known 5-dissections of

and

.

[

We have

\displaystyle f_{1}

\displaystyle=f_{25}\left(T(q^{5})-q-\frac{q^{2}}{T(q^{5})}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{25}^{5}}{f_{5}^{6}}\bigg(T(q^{5})^{4}+qT(q^{5})^{3}+2q^{2}T(q^{5})^{2}+3q^{3}T(q^{5})+5q^{4}-\frac{3q^{5}}{T(q^{5})}

(2.10)

The following gives 2-dissections of

and

due to Watson

Lemma 2.3

We have

\displaystyle G(q)=\frac{f_{8}}{f_{2}}\left(G\left(q^{16}\right)+qH(-q^{4})\right)

(2.11)

and

\displaystyle H(q)=\frac{f_{8}}{f_{2}}\left(G(-q^{4})+q^{3}H(q^{16})\right).

We use the identities in the following lemma quite frequently in the next sections.

(Baruah and Begum

We conclude this section by proving another useful result.

Lemma 2.5
.

We have

\displaystyle G(q)G(q^{4})\pm qH(q)H(q^{4})\equiv\frac{1}{f_{2}}\pmod{2}.

(2.15)

Proof.

One of Ramanujan’s famous forty identities for

and

(See

\displaystyle G(q)G(q^{4})-qH(q)H(q^{4})=\frac{f_{10}^{5}}{f_{2}f_{5}^{2}f_{20}^{2}}.

\displaystyle f_{k}^{2^{\ell}}\equiv f_{2k}^{2^{\ell-1}}\pmod{2^{\ell}}.

\displaystyle f_{1}^{2}\equiv f_{2}\pmod{2}~\textup{and}~f_{5}^{2}\equiv f_{10}\pmod{2}.

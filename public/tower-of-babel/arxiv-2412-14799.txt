Nonlinear soft mode action for the large- p p SYK model
Marta Bucca, and Márk Mezei

Abstract
The physics of the SYK model at low temperatures is dominated by a soft mode governed by the Schwarzian action. In Maldacena:2016hyu the linearised action was derived from the soft mode contribution to the four-point function, and physical arguments were presented for its nonlinear completion to the Schwarzian. In this paper, we give two derivations of the full nonlinear effective action in the large p p limit, where p p is the number of fermions in the interaction terms of the Hamiltonian. The first derivation uses that the collective field action of the large- p p SYK model is Liouville theory with a non-conformal boundary condition that we study in conformal perturbation theory. This derivation can be viewed as an explicit version of the renormalisation group argument for the nonlinear soft mode action in Kitaev:2017awl . The second derivation uses an Ansatz for how the soft mode embeds into the microscopic configuration space of the collective fields. We generalise our results for the large- p p SYK chain and obtain a “Schwarzian chain” effective action for it. These derivations showcase that the large- p p SYK model is a rare system, in which there is sufficient control over the microscopic dynamics, so that an effective description can be derived for it without the need for extra assumptions or matching (in the effective field theory sense).

2
Brief review of the SYK model

The Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev model is an ensemble of quantum mechanical models. A member of the ensemble consists of N Majorana fermions with
p
-body interactions and is described by the following Hamiltonian:

H=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq...\leq i_{p}\leq N}J_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{p}}\Psi_{i_{1}}...\Psi_{i_{p}},

(3)

where the coupling
J_{i_{1},...i_{p}}
has a Gaussian distribution with

\begin{split}\langle J_{i_{1}...i_{p}}\rangle&=0\,,\\
\langle J_{i_{1}...i_{p}}^{2}\rangle=\frac{J^{2}(p-1)!}{N^{p-1}}&=\frac{2^{p-1}}{p}\frac{\mathcal{J}^{2}(p-1)!}{N^{p-1}}\,.\end{split}

(4)

In the large
N
limit with annealed disorder, we can realise the average over the disorder ensemble for
J_{i_{1},...,i_{p}}
, by directly averaging the partition function
Sachdev_1993
. We find the following action:

\begin{split}I[G,\Sigma]=-\frac{1}{2}\textrm{log det}(\partial_{\tau}-\Sigma)+\frac{1}{2}\int\int(\Sigma G-\frac{1}{p}J^{2}G^{p})\,.\end{split}

(5)

The field
G
is the Euclidean bilinear:

G(\tau)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}T\Psi_{i}(\tau)\Psi_{i}(0)

(6)

and
\Sigma
is the self energy. Note that
G,\,\Sigma
are fluctuating fields.

The classical equations of motion derived by extremising (
5
) are the Schwinger-Dyson equations:

\begin{split}G&=[\partial_{\tau}-\Sigma]^{-1}\,,\\
\Sigma&=J^{2}G^{p-1}\,.\end{split}

(7)

The solutions to these equations give the leading large
N
value for the propagator
\expectationvalue{G(\tau)}
and self-energy.
It is easy to see that for zero coupling we get the propagator:

\expectationvalue{G^\text{free}(\tau)}=\frac{1}{2}\textrm{sgn}(\tau)\,,

(8)

whose small
\tau
behaviour follows from the anticommutation relation of Majorana fermions, and hence
G(\tau)\to\frac{1}{2}\textrm{sgn}(\tau)
as
\tau\to 0
will be imposed as a constraint in what follows.

2.1
Low energy limit and the Schwarzian

We are interested in studying the IR regime of the SYK model. We notice that, since
\Sigma
is proportional to
J^{2}
, in the low energy limit the term
\partial_{\tau}
in equation (
7
) is negligible and can be dropped. We can then write a new set of IR equations of motion:

\begin{split}\int d\tau_{2}G(\tau,\tau_{2})\Sigma(\tau_{2},\tau_{1})&=-\delta(\tau-\tau_{1})\,,\\
\Sigma(\tau,\tau_{1})&=J^{2}G(\tau,\tau_{1})^{p-1}\,.\end{split}

(9)

This set of equations is invariant under
\tau\rightarrow f(\tau)
, provided that the fields transform as

\begin{split}G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})&\rightarrow[f^{\prime}(\tau_{1})f^{\prime}(\tau_{2})]^{1/p}\,G(f(\tau_{1})f(\tau_{2}))\,,\\
\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})&\rightarrow[f^{\prime}(\tau_{1})f^{\prime}(\tau_{2})]^{(p-1)/p}\,\Sigma(f(\tau_{1})f(\tau_{2}))\,.\end{split}

(10)

This reparametrisation invariance is then explicitly broken once we take into account the
\partial_{\tau}
term that we discarded in the IR limit. In particular, the violation of this symmetry was argued to be described by the following action
KitaevTalks
;
Maldacena:2016hyu
;
Kitaev:2017awl
:

S=-\frac{N\alpha_{S}}{\mathcal{J}}\int du\,\textrm{Sch}[f,u]\,,

(11)

where Sch
[f,u]
is the Schwarzian derivative and
\alpha_{s}=\frac{1}{4p^{2}}
in the large
p
limit. In the following, we strengthen and generalise its derivation in the large-
p
SYK model.

3.1
Reparametrisations for the Liouville action

We note that the Liouville action is invariant under reparametrisations of the form

\tau_{1}\rightarrow f(\tau_{1})\,,\qquad\tau_{2}\rightarrow h(\tau_{2})\,,

(21)

provided that:

\gamma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\rightarrow\gamma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})+\textrm{log}\left[f^{\prime}(\tau_{1})\right]+\textrm{log}\left[h^{\prime}(\tau_{2})\right].

(22)

This statement is equivalent to (
10
).

Let us start by looking at the reparametrised saddle:

e^{\tilde{\gamma}}=\frac{v^{2}}{4\cos^{2}\left[v\frac{\pi+f(\tau_{1})-h(\tau_{2})}{2}\right]}f^{\prime}(\tau_{1})h^{\prime}(\tau_{2}).

(23)

Imposing
\gamma(\tau_{1},2\pi-\tau_{1})=\gamma(2\pi-\tau_{1},2\pi+\tau_{1})
results in the following set of equations:

\begin{split}-f(\tau_{1})+h(2\pi-\tau_{1})&=f(2\pi-\tau_{1})-h(2\pi+\tau_{1})+2\pi\,,\\
\quad f^{\prime}(\tau_{1})h^{\prime}(2\pi-\tau_{1})&=f^{\prime}(2\pi-\tau_{1})h^{\prime}(2\pi+\tau_{1}),\end{split}

(24)

which in turn imply

\begin{split}f(\tau_{1})&=h(\tau_{1})\,,\\
f(\tau_{1}+2\pi)&=f(\tau_{1})+2\pi\,,\\
f^{\prime}(\tau_{1}+2\pi)&=f^{\prime}(\tau_{1})\,.\end{split}

(25)

Thus we are left with one reparametrisation symmetry. This is also explicitly broken by the boundary condition
\gamma(\tau_{1}=\tau_{2})=\textrm{log}[\mathcal{J}^{2}].
However, in the strong coupling
\mathcal{J}\to\infty
limit the boundary condition is conformal and the symmetry is restored. This fact motivates our first approach.

Boundary CFT approach to the Schwarzian

We can give a short, abstract derivation of the Schwarzian action based on boundary CFT (BCFT).
The Liouville theory in (

\begin{split}e^{\gamma(\bar{\tau},{{\delta}}\tau=0)}=\mathcal{J}^{2}\,.\end{split}

Below we explain that for small

5
An alternative derivation for the Schwarzian

As outlined in section
3.1
, the Liouville action is invariant under independent reparametrisations of
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
, which then get restricted to one reparametrisation by the KMS condition. The remaining diagonal reparametrisation symmetry is broken by the boundary conditions at
\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}
.
The goal for this section is to find the action that describes the behaviour of the reparameterisation modes, starting from (
18
): this is an alternative way of deriving the Schwarzian action.
In order to perform this computation, we first want to find a field configuration that includes reparametrisations of the saddle point and that obeys both the boundary conditions and KMS.

5.1
Field configuration

We start from the reparametrised saddle (
23
), but with the constraint
f(\tau_{1})=h(\tau_{1})
imposed by the KMS symmetry discussed in (
25
):

e^{\tilde{{\gamma}}}=\frac{v^{2}}{4\cos^{2}\left[v\frac{\pi+f(\tau_{1})-f(\tau_{2})}{2}\right]}f^{\prime}(\tau_{1})f^{\prime}(\tau_{2})\,.

(35)

This field configuration violates the boundary conditions. However, if we take the
v\to 1
limit, we get that the violation is
f
independent:

e^{\tilde{{\gamma}}}\big|_{v\to 1}=\frac{f^{\prime}(\tau_{1})f^{\prime}(\tau_{2})}{4\sin^{2}\left[\frac{f(\tau_{1})-f(\tau_{2})}{2}\right]}\approx{1\over{{\delta}}\tau^{2}}\,.

(36)

(This is the same equation as (
31
).) If we divide this with the
v=1
saddle, we get a field configuration that goes to
1
at the boundary:

\frac{\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}}{2}\right)}{\sin^{2}\left[\frac{f(\tau_{1})-f(\tau_{2})}{2}\right]}\,f^{\prime}(\tau_{1})f^{\prime}(\tau_{2})\,.

(37)

We then multiply this with the
v\neq 1
saddle to enforce the boundary conditions and produce our Ansatz (see figure
2
):

e^{\gamma_{f}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})}\equiv\frac{v^{2}}{4\cos^{2}\left[v\left(\frac{\pi+\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}}{2}\right)\right]}\frac{\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}}{2}\right)}{\sin^{2}\left(\frac{f\left(\tau_{2}\right)-f\left(\tau_{1}\right)}{2}\right)}f^{\prime}\left(\tau_{1}\right)f^{\prime}\left(\tau_{2}\right)\,.

(38)

This Ansatz is designed to embed the soft reparametrisation mode into the microscopic field configuration
{{\gamma}}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
, and hence capture the soft mode. This mode is special, a generic configuration instead has a large
O(N/p^{2})
action, and fluctuations in the hard directions can be set to zero.

Admittedly, our construction of the Ansatz is ad hoc. However, it satisfies all conditions on the field configuration, and it is a “small deformation” of the
v=1
family of saddle points. Also, for linearised reparametrisations
f(u)=u+\varepsilon e^{-in\tau}
it gives:

\begin{split}e^{-\gamma_{*}(\delta\tau,\bar{\tau})}e^{\gamma_{f}(\delta\tau,\bar{\tau})}&=1+2i\varepsilon\,f_{n}({{\delta}}\tau)e^{-in\bar{\tau}}+O(\lambda^{2})\,,\\
f_{n}({{\delta}}\tau)&={\sin{n \de\tau\ov 2}\over\tan{ \de\tau\ov 2}}-n\cos{n \de\tau\ov 2}\,.\end{split}

(39)

The
f_{n}({{\delta}}\tau)
functions are identical to those defined in eq. (3.109) of
Maldacena:2016hyu
. They realise infinitesimal reparametrisations of the
v=1
saddle. We conclude that our Ansatz is accurate to linear order. At nonlinear order, the goal is to capture the soft direction in field space. The potential issue with the Ansatz could be that at nonlinear order in
{{\delta}}f
it mixes with the “hard” directions. The output of our calculation will be an action that is small,
O({{\delta}}v)
, which a posteriori confirms that there is no large mixing with the hard directions. However, an
O({{\delta}}v)
action is also consistent with an
O(\sqrt{{{\delta}}v})
mixing with hard directions. We can verify that this does not happen in the region
{{\delta}}\tau\gg{{\delta}}v
, where our Ansatz behaves as

\begin{split}e^{\gamma_{f}(\delta\tau,\bar{\tau})}=&e^{\gamma_{*}}\left[1+{{{\delta}}\tau^{2}\over 6}\textrm{Sch}\left[\tan\left(f/2\right),u\right]+O\left(\delta\tau^{3}\right)\right]\,,\end{split}

(40)

which is the expected IR behaviour of the soft mode. We conclude that in the worst case the Ansatz deviates from the true soft mode in the region
{{\delta}}\tau\sim{{\delta}}v
, which can only result in
O({{\delta}}v)
mixing, which is negligible. Indeed, we obtain the Schwarzian action from this Ansatz, which we know, from our first method and earlier literature, is the correct result.

The mixing issue could be further investigated by linearising around the Ansatz with a finite
f
and finding that the zero mode of the linearised equation is indeed in the direction of the Ansatz with
f+{{\delta}}f
; we leave this computation for the future.

To find the action for the reparametrisations, we plug (

where

{{\delta}}\tau^{\prime}

\begin{split}S_{\textrm{bdy}}(\Lambda)=&\frac{N}{2p^{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}d\bar{\tau}\frac{\Lambda^{2}\left(-3f^{\prime\prime}(\bar{\tau})^{2}+f^{\prime}(\bar{\tau})^{4}+2f^{(3)}(\bar{\tau})f^{\prime}(\bar{\tau})\right)}{12(\Lambda+\pi)f^{\prime}(\bar{\tau})^{2}}\delta v\\

5.3
Bulk contributions

To compute the bulk contributions we can directly expand our Lagrangian in
\delta v
as
{\cal L}_{bulk}={\cal L}_{bulk,0}+{{\delta}}v{\cal L}_{bulk,1}+\dots
and consider the contributions order by order. When integrating, to avoid double counting we will include a cutoff by considering only the interval
\delta\tau\in[\Lambda\delta v,2\pi-\Lambda\delta v]
. Since the cutoff depends on
{{\delta}}v
, when integrating the term
{\cal L}_{bulk,n}
, we expect to get contributions at every order
{{\delta}}v^{m}
with
m\geq n
. First, we integrate
{\cal L}_{bulk,0}
, and after some lengthy manipulations (see appendix
A
) we find:

S_{bulk,0}(\Lambda)=\frac{N}{2p^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\tau\left[-\frac{\Lambda\left(-3f^{\prime\prime}(\tau)^{2}+f^{\prime}(\tau)^{4}+2f^{(3)}(\tau)f^{\prime}(\tau)\right)}{12f^{\prime}(\tau)^{2}}\delta v\right]\,.

(48)

Adding this contribution to eq (
47
), we get:

S_{bulk,0}(\Lambda)+S_{bdy}(\Lambda)=\frac{N}{2p^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\tau\left[-\frac{\Lambda\pi\left(-3f^{\prime\prime}(\tau)^{2}+f^{\prime}(\tau)^{4}+2f^{(3)}(\tau)f^{\prime}(\tau)\right)}{12(\Lambda+\pi)f^{\prime}(\tau)^{2}}\delta v\right].

(49)

Taking the limit for
\Lambda\rightarrow\infty
yields

S_{bulk,0}+S_{bdy}=\frac{N}{2p^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\tau\left[-\frac{\pi\left(-3f^{\prime\prime}(\tau)^{2}+f^{\prime}(\tau)^{4}+2f^{(3)}(\tau)f^{\prime}(\tau)\right)}{12f^{\prime}(\tau)^{2}}\delta v\right].

(50)

As expected, adding contributions from the bulk cures the divergence that we had from the boundary. It is reasonable to expect that the cutoff dependence cancels at higher orders in
\delta v
as well, and we have explicitly verified this to second order in
\delta v
.

Next, when integrating
{\cal L}_{bulk,1}
we can set the cutoff to zero, since keeping it would only result in subleading contributions. After some manipulations (see again appendix
A
), we can rewrite the contribution at first order as:

{{\delta}}v\,S_{bulk,1}=\frac{N}{2p^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\tau\left(-\frac{\pi\left(-3f^{\prime\prime}(\tau)^{2}+f^{\prime}(\tau)^{4}+2f^{(3)}(\tau)f^{\prime}(\tau)\right)}{6f^{\prime}(\tau)^{2}}\delta v\right).

(51)

Adding everything together we get:

S=-\frac{N}{2p^{2}}\frac{\delta v\pi}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\tau\left(\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}(\tau)^{2}+\frac{f^{(3)}(\tau)}{f^{\prime}(\tau)}-\frac{3f^{\prime\prime}(\tau)^{2}}{2f^{\prime}(\tau)^{2}}\right)=-\frac{N\delta v\pi}{4p^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\tau\,\textrm{Sch}[\tan(f/2),\tau]\,,

(52)

which is in complete agreement with (
34

6
The nearest neighbour coupling in the SYK chain at low temperatures

The SYK chain consists of a chain of coupled SYK sites, with nearest neighbour interactions
Gu:2016oyy
. The Hamiltonian has the form:

\displaystyle H=\sum_{x=1}^{M}\biggl(

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq...\leq i_{p}\leq N}J_{x,i_{1}i_{2}...i_{p}}\Psi_{i_{1},x}...\Psi_{i_{p},x}+

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq...\leq i_{p/2}\leq N\\
1\leq j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq...\leq j_{p/2}\leq N\end{subarray}}J^{\prime}_{x,i_{1}...i_{p/2}j_{1}...j_{p/2}}\Psi_{i_{1},x}...\Psi_{i_{p/2},x}\Psi_{j_{1},x+1}...\Psi_{j_{p/2},x+1}\biggr)\,,

(53)

with:

\langle J^{2}_{x,i_{1},..i_{p}}\rangle=\frac{(p-1)!}{N^{p-1}}J_{0}^{2},\quad\langle J^{\prime 2}_{x,i_{1}...i_{p/2}j_{1}...j_{p/2}}\rangle=\frac{\left[(p/2)!\right]^{2}}{pN^{p-1}}J_{1}^{2},\quad\mathcal{J}^{2}_{0,1}\equiv\frac{p}{2^{p-1}}J^{2}_{0,1}\,.

(54)

We will identify
\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}_{0}
from previous sections and define
\alpha\equiv\frac{\mathcal{J}_{1}^{2}}{\mathcal{J}^{2}}
.
11
11

11

This notation deviates from the convention of the literature, where the definition
\mathcal{J}^{2}\equiv\mathcal{J}_{0}^{2}+\mathcal{J}_{1}^{2}
is used. Since we will take
\mathcal{J}_{1}\ll\mathcal{J}_{0}
, this is an unimportant difference.
In the large
p
limit we can write the action as
Gu:2016oyy
;
Choi:2020tdj
:

I[g]=\frac{N}{4p^{2}}\sum_{x=0}^{M-1}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\left[-\mathcal{J}^{2}e^{g_{x}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})}-\mathcal{J}_{1}^{2}e^{\frac{1}{2}(g_{x}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})+g_{x+1}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}))}+\frac{1}{4}\partial_{\tau_{1}}g_{x}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\partial_{\tau_{2}}g_{x}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\right]\,.

(55)

From the perspective of conformal perturbation theory, we have started from
M
decoupled Liouville theories on the Mobius strip and added a bulk (marginal) perturbation
e^{\frac{1}{2}(g_{x}+g_{x+1})}
. To leading order in this bulk coupling and zeroth order in
{{\delta}}v
we can incorporate its effect by evaluating it in the reparametrised saddle configuration (
31
). Alternatively, our Ansatz for the field configuration in the IR regime (
38
) gives the same result. We get:

\displaystyle I[g]=\sum_{x=0}^{M-1}

\displaystyle\left[-\frac{N\delta v\pi}{4p^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\tau\,\textrm{Sch}[\tan(f_x/2),\tau]-\mathcal{J}_{1}^{2}\frac{N}{4p^{2}}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\left[e^{\frac{1}{2}(g_{x}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})+g_{x+1}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}))}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=0}^{M-1}

\displaystyle\Biggl[-\frac{N\delta v\pi}{4p^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\tau\,\textrm{Sch}[\tan(f_x/2),\tau]+

\displaystyle-\mathcal{J}_{1}^{2}(\pi\delta v)^{2}\frac{N}{4p^{2}}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\Biggl[\frac{\sqrt{f_{x}^{\prime}\left(\tau_{1}\right)}\sqrt{f_{x}^{\prime}\left(\tau_{2}\right)}}{\sqrt{\sin^{2}\left(\frac{f_{x}\left(\tau_{1}\right)-f_{x}\left(\tau_{2}\right)}{2}\right)}}\frac{\sqrt{f_{x+1}^{\prime}\left(\tau_{1}\right)}\sqrt{f_{x+1}^{\prime}\left(\tau_{2}\right)}}{\sqrt{\sin^{2}\left(\frac{f_{x+1}\left(\tau_{1}\right)-f_{x+1}\left(\tau_{2}\right)}{2}\right)}}\Biggr]\,.

(56)

Note that we are only justified in keeping the second nonlocal term if it is the same order as the first one, since
there are other modes orthogonal to
f
that we discarded and that would give an
O(N/p^{2})
action, not reduced by an additional factor
{{\delta}}v\ll 1
.
Thus, we demand that
\mathcal{J}_{1}^{2}(\pi\delta v)^{2}\ll 1
. Note that in the IR regime
\frac{1}{\mathcal{J}_{0}}\sim\pi\delta v
, so we can write
\mathcal{J}_{1}^{2}(\pi\delta v)^{2}\sim\frac{\mathcal{J}_{1}^{2}}{\mathcal{J}_{0}^{2}}\sim\alpha
. Our requirement simply becomes
\alpha\ll 1
, with the final form of the action:

\begin{split}I[f]=-\frac{N}{4p^{2}}\sum_{x=0}^{M-1}&\Biggl[(\pi\delta v)\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\tau\,\textrm{Sch}[\tan(f_x/2),\tau]+\\
&+\alpha\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\Biggl[\frac{\sqrt{f_{x}^{\prime}\left(\tau_{1}\right)}\sqrt{f_{x}^{\prime}\left(\tau_{2}\right)}}{\sqrt{\sin^{2}\left(\frac{f_{x}\left(\tau_{1}\right)-f_{x}\left(\tau_{2}\right)}{2}\right)}}\frac{\sqrt{f_{x+1}^{\prime}\left(\tau_{1}\right)}\sqrt{f_{x+1}^{\prime}\left(\tau_{2}\right)}}{\sqrt{\sin^{2}\left(\frac{f_{x+1}\left(\tau_{1}\right)-f_{x+1}\left(\tau_{2}\right)}{2}\right)}}\Biggr]\,.\end{split}

(57)

We refer to this action as the Schwarzian chain.

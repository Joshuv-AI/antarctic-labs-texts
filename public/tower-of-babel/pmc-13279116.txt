Predictors and Outcomes of Nonvariceal Upper Gastrointestinal Bleed After Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting: A Nationwide Analysis
Authors: Umesh Bhagat, Ankit Agrawal, Ahmad Safdar, Rajat Garg, Danial Nasif, Lovepreet Singh, John Vargo, Haytham Elgharably, Faisal Bakaeen, Heba S Wassif
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundUpper gastrointestinal bleeding is a severe postoperative complication after coronary artery bypass grafting. We aimed to identify the incidence, predictors, and outcomes of nonvariceal upper gastrointestinal bleeding (NVUGIB) readmissions within 30 days after coronary artery bypass grafting from a nationwide database.MethodsThe Nationwide Readmission Database 2016 to 2020 was queried to identify patients who had isolated coronary artery bypass grafting and were readmitted with NVUGIB in 30 days. International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision, Clinical Modification (ICD‐10‐CM) and Procedure Coding System codes were used to identify the cohort. As the Nationwide Readmission Database is an anonymous public database, the study was exempt from the institutional review board. The analysis was conducted using Stata 17 (StataCorp LLC, College Station, TX).ResultsOf 801 221 patients who underwent isolated coronary artery bypass grafting, 784 280 were discharged alive. Within 30 days, of the 5180 patients who were readmitted with a diagnosis of NVUGIB, the mortality rate was 5.4%. Compared with nonreadmitted patients, patients with NVUGIB had a lower proportion of women (25.4% versus 31.2%, P<0.001) but higher comorbidity burdens, such as atrial fibrillation (44.4% versus 39.1%, P<0.001), and blood transfusion (39.1% versus 6.0%, P<0.001). Key predictors for 30‐day readmission with NVUGIB were atrial fibrillation (hazard ratio [HR], 1.31 [95% CI, 1.20–1.44]; P<0.001) and higher age (>65 years) (HR, 1.25 [95% CI, 1.01–1.53]; P=0.04). Female sex (odds ratio, 2.01 [95% CI, 1.28–3.17]; P=0.002) was strongly associated with death. Early (≤24 hours) esophagogastroduodenoscopy had better mortality outcomes (odds ratio, 0.23 [95% CI, 0.09–0.53]; P<0.001).ConclusionsNVUGIB readmissions after coronary artery bypass grafting are linked to significant morbidity and death. Early esophagogastroduodenoscopy is associated with improved outcomes, emphasizing its role in management.Keywords: coronary artery bypass grafting, nonvariceal bleed, upper gastrointestinal bleedSubject Categories: Cardiovascular Surgery

Upper gastrointestinal bleeding (UGIB) is a serious postoperative complication (0.35%–0.9%) after coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) and is associated with increased morbidity and death. 1 Anesthesia, low‐pressure, the nonpulsatile nature of cardiopulmonary bypass machines, and perioperative use of vasoconstrictive medications decrease splanchnic blood, leading to gastric mucosal acidosis and increased intestinal permeability. Among gastrointestinal complications in open‐heart surgical patients, around 70% are gastrointestinal bleeding. 2 UGIB is more common than lower gastrointestinal bleeding and, in several series, accounts for 100% of all postoperative cardiac surgical gastrointestinal hemorrhagic complications. 3 Despite advancements in surgical technique and medical management, the incidence of UGIB does not appear to decrease. The onset of UGIB has been found to be 20.2±13.3 days after the index surgery in 1 study 4 and 10±3.7 days after the index surgery in another retrospective study. 1 Pollard et al found that the mean interval between major surgery and UGIB was 16 days (range, 1–55). 5 To the best of our knowledge, no prior studies have explored the incidence or risk factors for 30‐day readmission with UGIB after CABG. Management often involves multidisciplinary care, blood transfusions, and endoscopic or surgical operations, yielding higher health care costs. Using the Nationwide Readmissions Database (NRD) from 2016 to 2020 in patients with 30‐day readmissions after CABG, our study explores the incidence, adverse cardiac and noncardiac complications, predictors of readmission, and death in patients with nonvariceal UGIB (NVUGIB).

Methods

Data Source

The NRD database from 2016 to 2020 was used, managed by the Healthcare Cost and Utilization Project, and run by the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality. More than 17 million unweighted and 32 million weighted hospital discharges are recorded annually across various states, accounting for ≈52% of all US hospitalizations. Patients are tracked using verified linkage numbers within a state for a given calendar year. To ensure complete follow‐up for 30‐day readmissions, we restricted the study population to patients admitted between January and November, allowing all included patients to be followed for the full 30‐day period. The data are deidentified and presented using the International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision, Clinical Modification (ICD‐10‐CM) and Procedure Coding System codes. Since we used the NRD from 2016 to 2020, we used only the ICD‐10‐CM codes. The NRD uses publicly accessible anonymous and deidentified data, and thus, this study did not require informed permission from participants or institutional review board approval. The data supporting this study are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request.

Study Cohort

CABG was characterized by the presence of specific diagnostic codes, namely, ICD‐10‐CM Procedure Coding System codes 0210, 0211, 0212, and 0213. Individuals aged <18 years were excluded from our study. Patients with prior CABG, concomitant surgical aortic valve replacement, mitral valve replacement, and tricuspid valve replacement were excluded, focusing only on the isolated CABG cohort. Missing data of the variables (female sex and age) were excluded. Eligible patients with 30‐day readmission after CABG in the index admission were classified into those with NVUGIB versus those without, focusing on identifying risk factors for NVUGIB among patients readmitted after CABG, rather than evaluating overall post‐CABG risk factors. By comparing NVUGIB cases with those without, we aim to provide a more targeted analysis of factors contributing to NVUGIB in the readmitted population. Baseline demographic characteristics, including age, sex, and Elixhauser comorbidities, were obtained from available NRD variables. Clinical variables, including atrial fibrillation, chronic kidney disease, chronic heart failure, malnutrition, hypertension, diabetes, obesity, smoking, use of esophagogastroduodenoscopy, colonoscopy, red blood cell transfusion, hemodialysis/peritoneal dialysis, acute kidney injury, vasopressor use, acute liver failure, intensive care unit admission, sepsis, and acute respiratory failure were incorporated into the analysis using corresponding ICD‐10‐CM codes as detailed in Table S1. The primary study outcome was all‐cause death. Intervention was defined as patients undergoing esophagogastroduodenoscopy or colonoscopy. Early esophagogastroduodenoscopy was defined as esophagogastroduodenoscopy performed ≤24 hours after admission, whereas late esophagogastroduodenoscopy was defined as esophagogastroduodenoscopy performed >24 hours after admission. Statistical Analysis

Means±SDs and the number of individuals with proportions were calculated for continuous and categorical variables, respectively. The characteristics between post‐CABG patients with and without UGIB were compared using a Student t test (or Wilcoxon rank‐sum test for nonnormal distribution, with normality assessed by the Shapiro–Wilk test) for continuous variables and χ2 test (or Fisher exact test) for categorical variables. To account for the comorbidity burden, the Elixhauser comorbidity index was derived by first identifying 30 specific comorbidities from patient records using ICD‐10‐CM codes. The elixsum command in Stata 17 (StataCorp LLC, College Station, TX) assigned a binary value (1 for present, 0 for absent) to each comorbidity and summed these to calculate a total score for each patient. This score was then categorized into 3 groups—<3 comorbidities, 3 to 6 comorbidities, and >6 comorbidities—to facilitate risk adjustment in our statistical models, enabling a nuanced assessment of comorbidity impact on the exposure–outcome relationship. Multivariable logistic regression analysis, adjusted for female sex, age, hypertension, diabetes, obesity, smoking, Elixhauser comorbidity, chronic kidney disease, chronic heart failure, malnutrition, non–atrial fibrillation cardiac arrhythmia, and atrial fibrillation, was performed to identify the predictors of death in patients readmitted with NVUGIB within 30 days. Multivariable Cox regression analysis was performed to identify predictors of 30‐day readmission with NVUGIB, which is reported as a hazard ratio (HR) and 95% CI. Additionally, a 1:1 nearest neighbor propensity‐matched analysis, based on a logistic regression model, was performed to estimate the likelihood of outcomes among patients undergoing early esophagogastroduodenoscopy versus no intervention and for patients undergoing late esophagogastroduodenoscopy versus no intervention. Matching was performed without replacement using the MatchIt package in R software version 4.3.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria), and balance between matched groups was assessed using standardized mean differences and empirical cumulative distribution function statistics to ensure comparability of covariates within each cohort. After matching, all standardized mean differences for the covariates were <0.1, indicating adequate balance, and it was visualized using a Loveplot. No caliper restrictions were applied, and treated units were matched to their nearest control unit. Unmatched control units were excluded from the analysis. The treatment effect was estimated using logistic regression on the matched sample using the glm() function in R software. The average marginal effect was calculated using the avg_comparisons() function from the marginaleffects package, with SEs clustered by matched pair to account for the matching structure. This approach allowed for robust estimation of odds ratios (ORs) while minimizing confounding inherent to observational data from the NRD. A 2‐sided P value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. The weight variable provided by the Healthcare Cost and Utilization Project was used to obtain national estimates. The analysis was conducted using Stata statistical software 17. Appropriate weights were used in all estimations in our analysis. This study was conducted and reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology cohort reporting guidelines. 6

Results

Study Cohort

A total of 801 221 patients who underwent isolated CABG were identified from the weighted NRD 2016 to 2020, of which 784 280 were discharged alive. A total of 87 919 (11.2%) patients were readmitted in 30 days, of which 5180 (0.66%) patients (≈7/1000 patients) had a diagnosis of NVUGIB. Among the readmitted patients with NVUGIB, 279 patients died, corresponding to a mortality rate of 5.4%. The total number of patients readmitted with NVUGIB each year across the study period of 2016 to 2020 is shown in Table S2.

Baseline Characteristics

The patients who were readmitted after isolated CABG in index admission were divided into 2 cohorts: 1 with NVUGIB and the other without any NVUGIB. The patient cohort that was readmitted with NVUGIB, as compared with those who did not, had fewer women (25.4% versus 31.2%, P<0.001), a higher burden of atrial fibrillation (44.4% versus 39.1%, P<0.001), chronic kidney disease (33.4% versus 30.4%, P=0.002), chronic heart failure (23.0% versus 18.4%, P<0.001), and esophagogastroduodenoscopy use (26.1% versus 0.2%, P<0.001). The detailed baseline characteristics are described in Table 1. Adverse Events

The patient cohort with readmission due to NVUGIB, as compared with those who did not, had higher adverse events of noncardiac complications like blood transfusion (39.1% versus 6.0%, P<0.001), colonoscopy procedure (10.3% versus 0.5%, P<0.001), dialysis (both hemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis; 6.0% versus 3.5%, P<0.001), acute kidney injury (31.6% versus 24.5%, P<0.001), vasopressor use (2.6% versus 1.1%, P<0.001), acute liver failure (2.9% versus 1.1%, P<0.001), acute respiratory failure (14.7%% versus 8.3%, P<0.001), and sepsis (11.9% versus 9.4%, P<0.001) (Table 2). Focusing on cardiovascular adverse events, the patients readmitted with NVUGIB had a higher rate of death in patients with atrial fibrillation (3.0% versus 1.2%, P<0.001) and lower rates of acute heart failure (17.7% versus 26.4%, P<0.001) with no significant differences in acute coronary syndrome (15.6% versus 14.7%, P=0.205) and stroke (5.66% versus 5.65%, P=0.979) (Table 2).

Clinical Factors Associated With Readmission With NVUGIB

The unadjusted OR of 30‐day readmission with NVUGIB after isolated CABG in index admission was 6.88 (95% CI, 5.86–8.08; P<0.001). On adjusting for all the baseline variables, the OR is 3.71 (95% CI, 3.05–4.51; P<0.001).

Moreover, patients with atrial fibrillation (HR, 1.31 [95% CI, 1.20–1.44]; P<0.001), higher age (>65 years) (HR, 1.25 [95% CI, 1.01–1.53]; P=0.04), chronic kidney disease (HR, 1.19 [95% CI, 1.08–1.33]; P=0.001), malnutrition (HR, 1.22 [95% CI, 1.09–1.37]; P<0.001), dialysis (HR, 2.01 [95% CI, 1.67–2.43]; P<0.001), acute respiratory failure (HR, 1.17 [95% CI, 1.03–1.32]; P=0.12), sepsis (HR, 1.61 [95% CI, 1.30–1.99]; P<0.001), and acute kidney injury (HR, 1.46 [95% CI, 1.32–1.61]; P<0.001) were strongly associated with a higher risk of 30‐day readmission with NVUGIB after CABG as noticed in multivariable Cox regression analysis (Table 3).

Intervention

Patients who were readmitted with NVUGIB were classified on the basis of early (≤24 hours) and late (>24 hours) esophagogastroduodenoscopy. The total esophagogastroduodenoscopies and colonoscopies performed were 1351 and 534, respectively. Early esophagogastroduodenoscopy was performed in 841 patients, and late esophagogastroduodenoscopy in 469 patients. The mean length of stay for patients who underwent early esophagogastroduodenoscopy was lower (5.04±5.09 versus 12.00±11.65 days) compared with the late esophagogastroduodenoscopy cohort. Similarly, the total hospital charges ($65 327.18±78 914.93 versus $151 925.60±222 904.3) and mortality rate (1.54% versus 3.83%) were lower for the early esophagogastroduodenoscopy cohort. The mean time to early esophagogastroduodenoscopy was 0.57±0.54 days as compared with 4.86±4.80 days in the late esophagogastroduodenoscopy cohort, and the mean time to colonoscopy was 2.11±1.59 days in the early cohort as compared with 6.85±8.29 days in the late cohort. Additionally, better outcomes were noticed in in‐hospital deaths among those who underwent intervention for NVUGIB (OR, 0.34 [95% CI, 0.19–0.57]; P<0.001). Further, those who underwent early esophagogastroduodenoscopy had better mortality outcomes compared with those receiving no intervention (OR, 0.39 [95% CI, 0.16–0.97]; P=0.04), whereas late esophagogastroduodenoscopy was not associated with a significant mortality benefit compared with no intervention (OR, 0.68 [95% CI, 0.33–1.39]; P=0.296). Further, a 1:1 nearest neighbor propensity‐matched analysis was performed comparing 950 patients undergoing intervention with 950 control patients demonstrating better mortality outcomes with intervention compared with no intervention (OR, 0.28 [95% CI, 0.17–0.48]; P<0.001). Early esophagogastroduodenoscopy remained associated with a lower mortality rate compared with no intervention (OR, 0.23 [95% CI, 0.09–0.53]; P<0.001), whereas late esophagogastroduodenoscopy did not demonstrate a mortality benefit compared with no intervention (OR, 0.81 [95% CI, 0.41–1.61]; P=0.819) (Table 5). Discussion

The key highlights of our study are (1) post–isolated CABG 30‐day readmissions for NVUGIB are associated with increased morbidity and death; (2) the incidence of 30‐day readmissions with a diagnosis of NVUGIB was 5.8%, and the mortality rate was 5.4%; (3) atrial fibrillation, chronic kidney disease, increasing Elixhauser comorbidities, dialysis, acute kidney injury, acute liver failure, acute respiratory failure, and sepsis are strongly associated with 30‐day readmission with post‐CABG NVUGIB; and (4) early esophagogastroduodenoscopy (≤24 hours) was associated with better mortality outcomes when compared with late esophagogastroduodenoscopy (>24 hours).

Gastrointestinal complications as a whole cause 30‐day readmissions after CABG has been reported to occur in 0.7% to 5.8% of patients. 7 Literature analyzing the incidence of UGIB after CABG during index admissions, including van der Voort and Zandstra and Aljarallah et al, focusing on a single center, found the incidence of UGIB to occur in 0.45% to 2% of patients following CABG. 8 , 9 Our data corroborate previous literature on UGIB being the most common gastrointestinal complication and show that the incidence of UGIB leading to 30‐day readmissions is comparable to its incidence during initial admissions.

Most data on mortality rates in patients with UGIB after cardiac surgery are from complications arising in the index admission. Where Jayaprakash et al reported a 15% mortality rate in patients undergoing cardiac surgery who developed upper gastrointestinal hemorrhage, 10 another study by Elgharably et al noted that gastrointestinal bleeding in the context of severe gastrointestinal complications following cardiac surgery was associated with a mortality rate of 17%. 11 We showed that the risk of death in UGIB increases in the presence of comorbid conditions like acute liver failure. Patients with acute liver failure who experience UGIB have a particularly high mortality rate, partly due to the compounded effects of liver dysfunction, coagulopathy, and the critical nature of the bleeding. 12

Renal comorbidities, including acute kidney injury, chronic kidney disease, and being on hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis, contributed to a higher risk of 30‐day readmission. This is supported by previous literature, including Yoshida et al, who found that a history of renal failure was predictive of postoperative gastrointestinal complications, including UGIB. 13 It is well known that patients on dialysis have an increased risk of bleeding, with patients on hemodialysis being at slightly higher risk than patients on peritoneal dialysis, likely due to the great hemodynamic shifts with hemodialysis. 14 , 15 Platelet dysfunction, underlying anemia in patients with chronic kidney disease, and disrupted interaction of platelets with vessel walls are all likely precipitants present in patients with kidney disease that increase the risk of UGIB. It is important to note that the risk for death was significant only for acute kidney injury and not for chronic kidney disease, hemodialysis, or peritoneal dialysis. This is likely explained by an underlying relationship between the severity of UGIB and the development of acute kidney injury.

Atrial fibrillation significantly increased the risk of 30‐day readmissions. Antiplatelet agents are critical in preventing major adverse cardiovascular events after CABG, and with the addition of oral anticoagulation in patients with atrial fibrillation, the risk of bleeding increases. Atrial fibrillation did not increase the risk of death. While anticoagulation is generally associated with an increased risk of bleeding, studies suggest that anticoagulation itself does not necessarily increase the mortality rate in these patients. 16 Ashburner et al found that warfarin use at the time of gastrointestinal hemorrhage was not associated with an increased 30‐day mortality rate in the Anticoagulation and Risk Factors in Atrial Fibrillation cohort. 17

Although sex did not significantly affect the HR for readmission, it is important to recognize in the study that women are at an elevated risk of death. The evidence thus far, looking into women being admitted for UGIB, suggests different outcomes compared with men, with data on increased death in women being mixed and context‐dependent. 18 , 19 Literature has not explored whether there is a difference in the prognosis of sex in patients readmitted with a UGIB after cardiac surgery.

A novel finding from our data is that early (within 24 hours) endoscopic intervention was associated with improved outcomes and reduced odds of death. Duodenal ulcers account for roughly 70% of UGIB after cardiac surgery. 20 Early esophagogastroduodenoscopy can reduce the need for surgical intervention and the volume of blood transfusions required. 21 Successful endoscopic hemostasis is a significant protective factor against rebleeding and death. 22 Studies have shown that unsuccessful endoscopic hemostasis is associated with increased 30‐day death, highlighting the importance of early and effective endoscopic intervention. 21 Esophagogastroduodenoscopy improves outcomes in gastrointestinal bleeding by enabling rapid diagnosis, targeted therapy, and effective risk stratification, thereby reducing rebleeding rates, the need for surgery, and overall death.

Post‐CABG NVUGIB represents a significant complication associated with increased morbidity and death, often leading to prolonged hospital stays, higher health care costs, and a greater need for intensive care interventions. Patients undergoing CABG frequently have multiple comorbidities, which can further complicate the management of NVUGIB. Additionally, many of these patients are on dual antiplatelet therapy or anticoagulation to prevent thrombotic events, which increases their risk of bleeding while also making hemostatic management more challenging. Timely identification and management of NVUGIB are crucial, as delays in diagnosis and intervention can lead to hemodynamic instability, the need for blood transfusions, and worse clinical outcomes. Our findings suggest that early esophagogastroduodenoscopy is associated with better mortality outcomes compared with delayed intervention, which correlates with the guidelines. 23 , 24 This highlights the importance of prompt recognition and management of gastrointestinal bleeding in patients following CABG to improve survival. Clinicians should maintain a high index of suspicion for NVUGIB in these patients and consider early endoscopic evaluation to optimize outcomes. Early risk stratification, discontinuation, or modification of anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy when appropriate and prompt endoscopic evaluation are essential components of care. Given the delicate balance between preventing thrombotic complications and managing bleeding risk, a multidisciplinary approach involving cardiologists, gastroenterologists, and intensivists is vital to optimizing outcomes in these high‐risk patients.

Limitations

Our study has several limitations. First, the results of our study are retrospective, observational, and derived from administrative claims databases, which are prone to coding errors and deemed less effective than the manual chart review. Second, there is a lack of imaging, procedural, medication, and laboratory data from the NRD database. Third, a multivariable logistic model was used to adjust for patient comorbidities between UGIB and non‐UGIB groups; there is still a possibility that other unadjusted or adjusted confounders still drive outcomes. Our study is observational and based on administrative data, which introduce a risk of confounding by indication. In this context, sicker patients may have been less likely to undergo esophagogastroduodenoscopy, potentially biasing the association of early esophagogastroduodenoscopy with improved outcomes. Although propensity matching was performed to reduce this bias, residual confounding cannot be fully excluded. Other potential confounders include unmeasured clinical variables such as hemodynamic status, coding inaccuracies, selection bias, differences in concomitant therapies, and center‐level variability, which may have influenced the observed associations. Finally, the ICD‐10‐CM code for right‐sided heart failure was introduced in 2018, so it was excluded from our analysis to avoid missing data from 2016 to 2017 entirely. As there is no specific ICD‐10‐CM code for NVUGIB, our definition relied on a combination of codes that encompass nonvariceal causes such as gastritis, peptic ulcer disease, esophagitis, and duodenitis. Nonetheless, this nationwide database has been used extensively in medical research and is considered dependable when subjected to appropriate analytical methods.

Conclusions

Post‐CABG readmissions after 30 days for NVUGIB are associated with significant morbidity and death. Several comorbidities, including atrial fibrillation, kidney disease, and smoking, are significant predictors of increased risk of readmission. Readmissions complicated by acute renal, liver, or respiratory failure increase the risk of death. A novel finding is that early (within 24 hours) endoscopic intervention is associated with better mortality outcomes in patients with UGIB. Future research can build on the current findings and contribute to more effective management strategies for UGIB in patients undergoing post–isolated CABG, ultimately improving patient outcomes and reducing mortality rates.

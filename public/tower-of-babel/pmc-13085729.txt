Comment on: “Analgesic effects of bilateral ultrasound-guided erector spinae plane block versus patient-controlled intravenous analgesia in correction surgery for idiopathic scoliosis: study protocol for a randomized controlled trial”
Authors: Raghuraman M Sethuraman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This manuscript, a comment category, is in response to the protocol of a trial that intended to analyze the analgesic effect of the erector spinae plane block in patients undergoing surgical correction of scoliosis. A few additional points regarding this topic are discussed here.

To the Editor

I read with great interest the protocol of a study that intended to analyze the analgesic effect of the erector spinae plane block (ESPB) in patients undergoing surgical correction procedures of scoliosis (Liao et al.2025), and would like to add a few important discussions.

Liao et al. state, “This is the first randomized controlled trial based on clinical practice to evaluate the application of ultrasound-guided ESPB in adolescents and adults undergoing scoliosis correction surgery” (Liao et al.2025). However, a randomized controlled study was published already assessing the effects of the ESPB in this population. Notably, that study also assessed the platelet-to-lymphocyte and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratios besides the pain scores and opioid consumption (Domagalska et al. 2024). Another study was also published simultaneously by Ren et al. that assessed the effects of ESPB in adolescent patients who underwent scoliosis correction (Ren et al.2024). Although Liao et al state that they will also include adults, this type of surgery is mainly restricted to adolescents. Notably, a meta-analysis, which included five studies, was also published a few months ago (Al-Naseem et al. 2025).

The major concern is that the sample size calculation for this trial is based on “previous clinical experience” (Liao et al.2025). However, I am afraid that this is incorrect and inadequate. Furthermore, it is also unjustifiable because a few studies have already been published, and these should have been considered for this purpose.

Lastly, there is no mention of the extent of scoliosis and the Cobb angle for inclusion criteria. Given the significant impact of these factors on pain severity, these elements should have been taken into account.

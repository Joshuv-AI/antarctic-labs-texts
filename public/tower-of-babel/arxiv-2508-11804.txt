Gaussian Atemporality: When Gaussian Quantum Correlations Imply Common Cause
Minjeong Song, Jayne Thompson, Matthew S. Winnel, Biveen Shajilal, Timothy C. Ralph, Syed M. Assad, Mile Gu

Abstract
Conventionally, covariances do not distinguish between spatial and temporal correlations. The same covariance matrix could equally describe temporal correlations between observations of the same system at two different times or correlations made on two spatially separated systems that arose from some common cause. Here, we demonstrate Gaussian quantum correlations that are atemporal , such that the covariances governing their quadrature measurements are unphysical without postulating some common cause. We introduce Gaussian atemporality robustness as a measure of atemporality, illustrating its efficient computability and operational meaning as the minimal noise needed to remove this uniquely quantum phenomenon. We illustrate that (i) specific spatiotemporal Gaussian correlations possess an intrinsic arrow of time, such that Gaussian atemporality robustness is zero in one temporal direction and not the other and (ii) that it measures quantum correlations beyond entanglement.

⋆

Once the standard basis is determined, the elements of the CM can be determined through a standard measurement procedure: Alice randomly chooses i) to do nothing, ii) to implement a non-demolition homodyne measurement in a basis

II.2
Spatial-temporal Gaussian states

While the measurement procedure above typically assumes that Alice and Bob share a spatially distributed state, the same procedure can be applied regardless of underlying causal mechanism. We now return to the general scenario where Alice and Bob are situated in separate laboratories, where they each receive a Gaussian continuous-variable state. Let their local quadrature operators be in the standard basis. Alice and Bob then perform the same measurement procedure as above, whereby the resulting covariance matrix defines a
space-time statistical covariance matrix
in standard form. More formally:

Definition 1

(Space-time covariance matrix in standard form)
.

Let Alice and Bob each have access to a qumode with standard basis
(\widehat{q}_{A},\widehat{p}_{A})
and
(\widehat{q}_{B},\widehat{p}_{B})
, with locally diagonal covariance matrices
\mathbf{V}_{A}
and
\mathbf{V}_{B}
. Let Alice and Bob then measure their quadrature operators at random, with resulting outcomes governed respectively by random variables
\widehat{x}_{A,j}
and
\widehat{x}_{B,k}
and the cross-correlation matrix
C_{jk}=\frac{1}{2}\expectationvalue{\hx_{A,j}\hx_{B,k}+\hx_{B,k}\hx_{A,j}}
. The space-time covariance matrix is then given by the matrix

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{AB}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{V}_{A}&\mathbf{C}\\
\mathbf{C}^{\mbox{\tiny T}}&\mathbf{V}_{B}\end{pmatrix},

(2)

While the form looks identical to that of the standard covariance matrix, we note that this definition no longer assumes there is an underlying bipartite quantum state
\rho_{AB}
shared between Alice and Bob. In particular, it is agnostic to underlying causal mechanisms. Therefore, they extend the conventional covariance matrix, and were originally proposed as a unified representation to describe space-time Gaussian states
Zhang et al. 2020a
.

The local CMs,
\mathbf{V}_{A},\mathbf{V}_{B}
correspond to conventional CMs that describe local statistics. They are positive definite matrices that obey the uncertainty relation. However, the global space-time covariance matrix
\mathbf{V}_{AB}
does not need to obey such constraints – owing to the possibility of arising from temporal causal mechanisms. Consider a temporal causal structure where Alice receives an initial Gaussian state
\rho_{A}
which is then sent to Bob through a Gaussian channel
\mathcal{G}:A\to B
. Alice measures the state before passing it through the channel
\mathcal{G}
, and Bob measures the subsequent output.
The local CMs,
\mathbf{V}_{A}
and
\mathbf{V}_{B}
are uniquely determined by the state received by Alice
\rho_{A}
and the state received by Bob
\mathcal{G}(\rho_{A})
. The cross-correlation matrix
\mathbf{C}
then has components

\displaystyle C_{jk}=\int\differential{x_A}x_{A}\tr(\widehat{\Pi}^{(j)}_{x_A}\rho_A)\tr(\G(\widehat{\Pi}^{(j)}_{x_A})\hx_{B,k}),

where
\widehat{\Pi}^{(j)}_{x_{A}}
represents the projector associated with the eigenvalue
x_{A}
of the quadrature operator
\widehat{x}_{A,j}

. This corresponds to the following scenario: the initial state
\rho_{A}
is measured with
\widehat{x}_{A,j}

\widehat{\Pi}^{(j)}_{x_{A}}

\mathcal{G}

\mathcal{G}(\widehat{\Pi}^{(j)}_{x_{A}})

aspatial

III
Results

When studying the scenario where Alice and Bob each received a qubit, specific Pauli measurement correlations were also atemporal: they cannot arise from any purely temporal causal mechanism. This then allowed a classification of all spatial-temporal correlations based on causal compatibility
Song et al. 2024
. Here, we extend this to quadrature correlations on Gaussian states by asking: can continuous variable Gaussian systems exhibit
Gaussian-atemporality
, such that their correlations cannot be explained by any Gaussian temporal causal mechanism?

III.1
Temporally-Compatible Gaussian states

We first examine the class of correlations compatible with temporal causal mechanisms. Consider the case where Alice measures her mode, which then evolves by some Gaussian channel

\displaystyle=\mathbf{T}\mathbf{V}_{A}\mathbf{T}^{\mbox{\tiny T}}+\mathbf{N}.

(3)

Note that

III.3
Atemporality Robustness

We introduce an operational measure of atemporality by taking inspiration from various robustness measures in quantum resources theories
Chitambar and Gour 2019
;
Napoli et al. 2016
.
Given a spatial-temporal Gaussian state that exhibits atemporality, what is the minimum noise we need to add to ensure it is compatible with a temporal causal mechanism
?

Consider first the case of forward atemporality, where atemporality is equivalent to the condition
\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{N})=\mathbf{N}+i\bm{\Omega}-i\mathbf{T}\bm{\Omega}\mathbf{T}^{\mbox{\tiny T}}\ngeq\mathbf{0}
, where
\mathbf{N}
is the noise matrix. Clearly, if this noisy matrix is sufficiently large,
\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{N})\geq\mathbf{0}
. i.e., for any
\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{N})
, there exists a sufficiently large
\mu
such that
\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{N}+\mu\mathbf{I})\geq\mathbf{0}
. Operationally, this corresponds to adding thermal noise of variance
\mu
to Bob’s measurement statistics. This motivates our definition of
forward atemporality robustness
.

Definition 3

(Forward Atemporality Robustness)
.

Consider a Gaussian spatial-temporal state on two qumodes with a space-time covariance matrix
\mathbf{V}_{AB}
. Let
\mathbf{V}^{\mu}_{AB}
represent the system’s resulting covariance matrix should all of Bob’s measurement outcomes suffer an additive Gaussian noise of variance
\mu
. We define the
forward atemporality robustness
, denoted
\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{V})
, as the minimal
\mu
such that
\mathbf{V}^{\mu}_{AB}
is no longer atemporal. Mathematically,

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{V})=\mathrm{argmin}_{\mu}\{\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{N}+\mu\mathbf{I})\geq 0\}

(9)

where
X(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{N})
is the forward atemporality matrix of
\mathbf{V}_{AB}
.

First, observe that this measure is faithful.
\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB})=0
implies that
\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{N})\geq 0
, thus,
\mathbf{V}_{AB}
is compatible with temporal causal explanations. Meanwhile, if
\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{V})>0
, then
\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{N})<0
, implying atemporality. Moreover, we illustrate that this measure can be computed analytically (see
Appendix

A
), enabling us to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 3
.

A spatial-temporal qumode-pair with covariance matrix
\mathbf{V}_{AB}
and an associated forward pseudo-channel with transformation matrix
\mathbf{T}
and noise matrix
\mathbf{N}
has forward atemporality robustness

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB})=\max(0,\absolutevalue{\omega}-\sqrt{\det\mathbf{N}}),

(10)

with
\omega=1-\frac{\det\mathbf{C}}{\det\mathbf{V}_{A}}
and
\det\mathbf{N}=\frac{\det\mathbf{V}_{AB}}{\det\mathbf{V}_{A}}
. Moreover,
\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB})>0
is a necessary and sufficient condition for forward Gaussian atemporality.

By exchanging
A
and
B
, we can also define a
reverse Gaussian atemporality robustness

\overleftarrow{f}
. We can then define general
Gaussian atemporality robustness
by taking the minimum of these two, i.e.,

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{V}_{AB})\equiv\min\{\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB}),\overleftarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB})\}.

(11)

f(\mathbf{V}_{AB})>0
then provides a necessary and sufficient condition for Gaussian atemporarily.

III.4
Entanglement and Atemporality

Consider first the class of spatially-compatible spatial-temporal two-mode Gaussian states, i.e., those that a bipartite density operator can represent. In this context, entanglement has always been the iconic measure of non-classicality. Meanwhile, the idiom that correlations do not imply causation from classical statistics suggests that highly classical states will likely be compatible with spatial and temporal causal mechanisms and thus have zero atemporality robustness.

III.5
Time-reversal Asymmetry of Atemporality

When we consider more general spatio-temporal two-mode states, the property of time-reversal symmetry is of natural interest. In classical statistics, we know it is impossible to decide conclusively whether
A
caused
B
or
B
caused
A
. Yet, studies on qubits have shown this is not necessarily true in the quantum regime. Gaussian-atemporality enables us to demonstrate this phenomenon in Gaussian systems.

Consider the scenario where Alice begins with a general Gaussian state whose covariance matrix has quadrature variances
\langle\hat{q}^{2}\rangle=v_{1}
and
\langle\hat{p}^{2}\rangle=v_{2}
when in standard form. Alice then sends the state to Bob via a loss channel with transmission rate
\eta
(i.e., the associated channel has transformation matrix
\mathbf{T}=\sqrt{\eta}\mathbf{I}
and noise matrix
\mathbf{N}=(1-\eta)\mathbf{I}
). The associated covariance matrix between Alice and Bob is then given by:

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}&0&v_{1}\sqrt{\eta}&0\\
0&v_{2}&0&v_{2}\sqrt{\eta}\\
v_{1}\sqrt{\eta}&0&1+(v_{1}-1)\eta&0\\
0&v_{2}\sqrt{\eta}&0&1+(v_{2}-1)\eta\end{pmatrix}.

Clearly this state has zero forward atemporality, since it can be explained by a physical channel from Alice to Bob by definition. However, a causal explanation of this correlations as a channel from Bob to Alice is not always possible.

In
Appendix

B
, we reverse the role of
A
and
B
and use
2
to find the reverse atemporality robustness. Introducing

v^{(\eta)}_{k}=\frac{v_{k}}{1+(v_{k}-1)\eta}

as the rescaled quadrature variances, we find

\displaystyle\overleftarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max(0,(\eta v+1)(1-v)),

where
v=\sqrt{v^{(\eta)}_{1}v^{(\eta)}_{2}}
. Noting that
v>0
,
\overleftarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB})
is then non-zero if and only if
v^{(\eta)}_{1}v^{(\eta)}_{2}<1
. From this, we observe the following:

•

When there is no loss (
\eta=1
),
v^{(\eta)}_{k}=1
for each
k
and thus
v=1
. This aligns with our expectation that any unitary channel has a valid time-reversal.

•

Whenever the initial state is classical (i.e., exhibits no squeezing such that
v_{1},v_{2}\geq 1
), then
v^{(\eta)}_{1},v^{(\eta)}_{2}\geq 1
and thus
v\geq 1
for any
\eta
. This aligns with our expectation that atemporality is a quantum phenomenon.

•

When all input is lost (
\eta=0
),
v^{(\eta)}_{k}\rightarrow v_{k}
. Thus, zero reverse atemporality is guaranteed by the uncertainty principle, which implies
v_{1}v_{2}\geq 1
.

However, if our input is non-classical (i.e. squeezed), we can obtain non-zero reverse atemporality in moderate loss regimes. Fig
4
illustrates this for various values of
\eta
. We see that in regimes where the input is squeezed, there exist situations where the resulting spatio-temporal state can only be explained by direct cause from
A
to
B
, but admits no similar explanation from
B
to
A
. We thus conclude:

Observation 2
.

Gaussian-atemporality is asymmetric under time reversal. There exists situations where we can decide from correlation matrices alone whether
A
caused
B
or
B
caused
A
.

IV
Discussion

Given two random variables drawn from some multi-variable Gaussian distribution, Gaussian-atemporality asks: can we rule out the resulting correlations between them from being generated purely by temporal mechanisms, such that they correspond to two measurements on the same physical system? Here, we answer this in the affirmative, illustrating scenarios where the covariance matrix between quadrature measurements could not arise if performed on the same quantum harmonic oscillator at two different times, regardless of what Gaussian channel occurs in between. We introduced Gaussian atemporality robustness– the minimal noise needed to break Gaussian-atemporality– as an operationally meaningful measure of Gaussian-atemporality, and illustrated its expression in closed analytical form. Use of these tools then provides two immediate observations: (i) Gaussian-atemporality provides a distinct notion of quantum correlations beyond entanglement and (ii) that certain covariances correlating quadrature measurements of Gaussian systems
A
and
B
have a natural arrow of time - they can be explained when
A
comes before
B
but not vice versa.

Our work here complements recent studies of spatiotemporal correlations on qubits, where the use of pseudo-density operators enables the discovery that specific correlations are temporal and may possess an intrinsic arrow of time. Our work here thus lends further credence that such phenomena extend well beyond quantum systems of few dimensions. Meanwhile, pseudo-density operators have recently motivated spatiotemporal generalizations of quantum mutual information and teleportation and seen extensions to multi-time states
Jia and Kaszlikowski 2024
;
Liu et al. 2024b
or quantum state over time
Leifer and Spekkens 2013
;
Horsman et al. 2017
;
Fullwood and Parzygnat 2022
;
Lie and Ng 2024
;
Lie and Fullwood 2024
;
Lie and Kwon 2025
. Similarly, pseudo-channels have conceptual similarity with Bayesian retrodiction maps or Bayesian inverse
Aw et al. 2021
;
Parzygnat and Fullwood 2023
;
Parzygnat and Buscemi 2023
which include Petz recovery maps
Petz 1986
;
Petz 1988
. There would certainly be value in extending these ideas to the continuous-variable Gaussian domain. Indeed, ever since the initial proposal of the EPR paradox, Gaussian quantum states have been a natural testbed for uniquely quantum phenomena - thanks to their concise covariance matrix description and relative ease of experimental realization. Moreover, the Gaussian continuous variables enjoy the advantage over discrete variables of having an efficiently computable measure of atemporality. Thus, we anticipate they can provide a compelling pathway towards understanding general multi-partite spatiotemporal quantum correlations.

when
\absolutevalue{w}-\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}}>0
. Or equivalently,

\displaystyle\max_{\absolutevalue{w}-\sqrt{(n_{1}+\epsilon_{1})(n_{2}+\epsilon_{2})}=0}\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}}=\absolutevalue{w}-\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}}.

(41)

The maximization problem on the left hand side can be solved by using the method of Lagrangian multiplier as follows: Define a Lagrangian function as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\lambda):=\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}+\lambda(w^{2}-(n_{1}+\epsilon_{1})(n_{2}+\epsilon_{2})).

(42)

Then,
\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}
(and also
\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}}
) takes its maximum when
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\epsilon_{i}}=0,\forall~i
and
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\lambda}=0
. That is,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\epsilon_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0:\epsilon_{2}=-n_{2}+\frac{n_{2}}{1-\lambda}

(43)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\epsilon_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0:\epsilon_{1}=-n_{1}+\frac{n_{1}}{1-\lambda}

(44)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0:w^{2}=(n_{1}+\epsilon_{1})(n_{2}+\epsilon_{2}).

(45)

After solving these simultaneous equations in terms of
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\lambda
, we have
\lambda=1\pm\frac{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}}}{\absolutevalue{w}}
. But
\lambda=1+\frac{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}}}{\absolutevalue{w}}
leads to negative
\epsilon
’s, which contradicts to the fact that a noise matrix is positive semidefinite. Thus, the solution becomes

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{n_{1}}{n_{2}}}(\absolutevalue{w}-\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}}),

(46)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{n_{2}}{n_{1}}}(\absolutevalue{w}-\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}}),

(47)

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}}}{\absolutevalue{w}}.

(48)

Then it simply follows that the maximum of
\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}}
is given by

\displaystyle\max_{\absolutevalue{w}-\sqrt{(n_{1}+\epsilon_{1})(n_{2}+\epsilon_{2})}=0}\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}}

(49)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sqrt{\frac{n_{1}}{n_{2}}}(\absolutevalue{w}-\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}})\sqrt{\frac{n_{2}}{n_{1}}}(\absolutevalue{w}-\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}})}

(50)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{w}-\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}}.

(51)

(2) Now we consider a more general case where the additional noise matrix is not diagonal, i.e.,
\mathbf{E}:=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_{1}&\epsilon_{3}\\
\epsilon_{3}&\epsilon_{2}\end{pmatrix}
. Let
\tilde{\mathbf{N}}
be the new noise matrix after adding
\mathbf{E}
to
\mathbf{N}
, i.e.,
\tilde{\mathbf{N}}=\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{N}
, and let
\tilde{n}_{1},\tilde{n}_{2}
be the eigenvalues of
\tilde{\mathbf{N}}
. Similar to the case (1) above, we will show that

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{w}-\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}}=\max_{\absolutevalue{w}-\sqrt{\tilde{n}_{1}\tilde{n}_{2}}=0}\sqrt{\det\mathbf{E}}.

(52)

We then define a Lagrangian function as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3},\lambda)

(53)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\det\mathbf{E}+\lambda(w^{2}-(n_{1}+\epsilon_{1})(n_{2}+\epsilon_{2})+\epsilon_{3}^{2})

(54)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{3}^{2}+\lambda(w^{2}-(n_{1}+\epsilon_{1})(n_{2}+\epsilon_{2})+\epsilon_{3}^{2}).

(55)

When
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\epsilon_{i}}=0,\forall~i
and
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\lambda}=0
,
\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{3}^{2}
is maximized. In particular,
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\epsilon_{3}}=0
leads to
(\lambda+1)\epsilon_{3}=0
. If
\lambda=-1
, it leads to a solution which contradicts to
\mathbf{E}\geq\mathbf{0}
. Thus, we have
\epsilon_{3}=0
. Then the maximization problem becomes the same as the one considered in the case (1), that is, it follows that

\displaystyle\max_{\absolutevalue{w}-\sqrt{\tilde{n}_{1}\tilde{n}_{2}}=0}\sqrt{\det\mathbf{E}}=\absolutevalue{w}-\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}}.

(56)

Hence, we conclude that Gaussian-atemporality amounts to the maximum amount of noise that can be added before breaking Gaussian-atemporality.

Lastly, we verify
\det\mathbf{N}=\frac{\det\mathbf{V}_{AB}}{\det\mathbf{V}_{A}}
. Note that
\mathbf{N}
coincides with the Schur complement of block matrix
\mathbf{V}_{A}
of the whole matrix
\mathbf{V}_{AB}
, denoted by
\mathbf{V}_{AB}/\mathbf{V}_{A}

Schur 1918
;
Zhang 2006
. Following Schur determinant formula (see Eq. (0.3.2) in Ref.
Zhang 2006
), the determinant of
\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{V}_{AB}/\mathbf{V}_{A}
is easily computed by
\frac{\det\mathbf{V}_{AB}}{\det\mathbf{V}_{A}}
.
∎

Example 1.
Consider two thermal states with variance
v\geq 1
, each labeled by
A
and
B
, together passing through a two-mode squeezing operation with a squeezing parameter
r\geq 0
. The CM of the resulting Gaussian state is of the form

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}v\cosh{2r}&0&v\sinh{2r}&0\\
0&v\cosh{2r}&0&-v\sinh{2r}\\
v\sinh{2r}&0&v\cosh{2r}&0\\
0&-v\sinh{2r}&0&v\cosh{2r}\end{pmatrix}.

Then, the states are Gaussian-atemporal for

r>r_{atemp}:=\frac{1}{2}\arccos{\frac{v+\sqrt{v^2+8}}{4}}
, and entangled for
r>r_{ent}:=\frac{1}{2}\ln v
.
In this class of Gaussian states, we observe that Gaussian-atemporal states are always entangled. However, there exist the states that are entangled but not Gaussian-atemporal, when
r_{ent}<r\leq r_{atemp}
. Interestingly though, if the amount of entanglement, measured by the logarithmic negativity
Vidal and Werner 2002
, exceeds

E_{max}:\approx 0.1882
, it guarantees that the state is Gaussian-atemporal. The logarithm is taken to be natural base for the logarithmic negativity.

Proof.

First of all, we note that
\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB})=\overleftarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB})=f(\mathbf{V}_{AB})
since space-time CMs of the form as above are invariant under swap operations. Therefore, we will use Gaussian atemporality robustness to derive the conditions for (1) Gaussian-atemporality, and (2) use the positive partial transposition (PPT)
Peres 1996
;
Horodecki et al. 1996
to derive the conditions for entanglement. (3) We also prove that the logarithmic negativity of these states is maximized by

\displaystyle\max_{f(\mathbf{V}_{AB})=0}E(\mathbf{V}_{AB})\approx 0.1882.

(57)

(1) To derive the conditions for Gaussian-atemporality, we first need to find the forward Gaussian pseudo-channel, characterized by
\mathbf{T}
and
\mathbf{N}
. By using
2
, they are given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}=\tanh{2r}\mathbf{Z}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{N}=\frac{v}{\cosh{2r}}\mathbf{I},

(58)

where
\mathbf{Z}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{pmatrix}
and
\mathbf{I}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}
. By using this, we can readily derive that

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max(0,1+\frac{\sinh^{2}{2r}}{\cosh^{2}{2r}}-\frac{v}{\cosh{2r}})

(59)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max(0,\frac{2\cosh^{2}{2r}-v\cosh{2r}-1}{\cosh^{2}{2r}}),

(60)

and
\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB})=0
if and only if
2\cosh^{2}{2r}-v\cosh{2r}-1\leq 0
; otherwise, it takes a non-zero value. Let
\zeta:=\cosh{2r}
be a positive variable such that
\zeta\geq 1
and
h
a function of
\zeta
, defined as
h(\zeta)\equiv 2\zeta^{2}-v\zeta-1
. That is,
\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB})=0
if and only if
h(\zeta)\leq 0
. Now let
\zeta^{(+)}
be the largest solution of the quadratic equation
h(\zeta)=0
, i.e.,
\zeta^{(+)}:=\frac{v+\sqrt{v^{2}+8}}{4}
.

Since
v\geq 1
, it simply follows that
\zeta^{(+)}\geq 1
. Note also that the other solution
\zeta^{(-)}:=\frac{v-\sqrt{v^{2}+8}}{4}
always takes a negative value, but this is not a feasible solution because
\zeta:=\cosh{2r}\geq 1
.

For
1\leq\zeta\leq\zeta^{(+)}
, we have
h(\zeta)\leq h(\zeta^{(+)})=0
, which implies
\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB})=0
; For
\zeta>\zeta^{(+)}
, on the other hand, we have
h(\zeta)>0
, which implies
\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB})>0
. Thus we arrive at the following:

•

It is Gaussian-atemporal for
\cosh{2r}>\frac{v+\sqrt{v^{2}+8}}{4}
.

(2) We use the PPT criterion to derive the conditions for entanglement
Peres 1996
;
Horodecki et al. 1996
, and the fact that the PPT criterion becomes a necessary and sufficient condition for entanglement for two-mode Gaussian states
Werner and Wolf 2001
. After applying the partial transpose, the CM
\mathbf{V}_{AB}^{\star}
changes to
\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{AB}\equiv(\mathbf{I}\oplus\mathbf{Z})\mathbf{V}_{AB}^{\star}(\mathbf{I}\oplus\mathbf{Z})
. Let
\tilde{v}^{(-)}
be the smallest value in the symplectic spectrum of
\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{AB}
. Then,
\tilde{v}^{(-)}<1
is a necessary and sufficient condition for entanglement
Serafini et al. 2003
.

Now let us find
\tilde{v}^{(-)}
:
\tilde{v}^{(-)}
is the smallest value in symplectic spectrum of

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{AB}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbf{I}\oplus\mathbf{Z})\mathbf{V}_{AB}(\mathbf{I}\oplus\mathbf{Z})

(61)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}v\cosh{2r}&0&v\sinh{2r}&0\\
0&v\cosh{2r}&0&v\sinh{2r}\\
v\sinh{2r}&0&v\cosh{2r}&0\\
0&v\sinh{2r}&0&v\cosh{2r}\end{pmatrix}

(62)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{A}&\mathbf{C}\\
\mathbf{C}&\mathbf{B}\end{pmatrix},

(63)

and then it can be found by using the following known formula
Serafini et al. 2004
,

\displaystyle\tilde{v}^{(-)}=\sqrt{\frac{\tilde{\Delta}-\sqrt{\tilde{\Delta}^{2}-4\det{\Tilde{\V}_{AB}}}}{2}},

(64)

where
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C}
are 2 by 2 matrices, and
\tilde{\Delta}\equiv\det\mathbf{A}+\det\mathbf{B}+2\det\mathbf{C}
. In this case,
\tilde{\Delta}
amounts to

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}=2v^{2}\cosh^{2}{2r}+2v^{2}\sinh^{2}{2r}.

(65)

Also, we can compute for
\det\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{AB}
:

\displaystyle\det\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{AB}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v\cosh{2r}\begin{vmatrix}v\cosh{2r}&0&v\sinh{2r}\\
0&v\cosh{2r}&0\\
v\sinh{2r}&0&v\cosh{2r}\end{vmatrix}

(66)

\displaystyle+v\sinh{2r}\begin{vmatrix}0&v\cosh{2r}&v\sinh{2r}\\
v\sinh{2r}&0&0\\
0&v\sinh{2r}&v\cosh{2r}\end{vmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v^{2}\cosh^{2}{2r}(v^{2}\cosh^{2}{2r}-v^{2}\sinh^{2}{2r})

\displaystyle-v^{2}\sinh^{2}{2r}(v^{2}\cosh^{2}{2r}-v^{2}\sinh^{2}{2r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(v^{2}\cosh^{2}{2r}-v^{2}\sinh^{2}{2r})^{2}

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{v}^{(-)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\tilde{\Delta}-\sqrt{\tilde{\Delta}^{2}-4\det{\Tilde{\V}_{AB}}}}{2}}

(67)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{v^{2}\cosh^{2}{2r}+v^{2}\sinh^{2}{2r}-\sqrt{(v^{2}\cosh^{2}{2r}+v^{2}\sinh^{2}{2r})^{2}-(v^{2}\cosh^{2}{2r}-v^{2}\sinh^{2}{2r})^{2}}}

(68)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{v^{2}\cosh^{2}{2r}+v^{2}\sinh^{2}{2r}-\sqrt{4v^{4}\cosh^{2}{2r}\sinh^{2}{2r}}}

(69)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{v^{2}\cosh^{2}{2r}+v^{2}\sinh^{2}{2r}-2v^{2}\cosh{2r}\sinh{2r}}

(70)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v(\cosh{2r}-\sinh{2r})=v\exp{-2r}.

(71)

Thus we arrive at the following:

•

It is entangled for
e^{-2r}<\frac{1}{v}
.

(3) Lastly, let us show that

\displaystyle\max_{f(\mathbf{V}_{AB})=0}E(\mathbf{V}_{AB})\approx 0.1882,

(72)

where
E(\cdot)
represents the logarithmic negativity
Vidal and Werner 2002
with natural base.

As we have seen in (1) of this proof,
\mathbf{V}_{AB}
has the zero Gaussian-atemporality,
f(\mathbf{V}_{AB})=0
, if and only if
\cosh 2r\leq\frac{v+\sqrt{v^{2}+8}}{4}
, or equivalently,
v\geq\frac{\cosh 4r}{\cosh 2r}
.

Now suppose that
\mathbf{V}_{AB}
satisfies
v\geq\frac{\cosh 4r}{\cosh 2r}
, that is,
f(\mathbf{V}_{AB})=0
. Let
E(\mathbf{V}_{AB})
be the logarithmic negativity of
\mathbf{V}_{AB}
, i.e.,
E(\mathbf{V}_{AB}):=-\log\tilde{v}^{(-)}
, where
\tilde{v}^{(-)}
represents the smallest value in symplectic spectrum of
\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{AB}\equiv(\mathbf{I}\oplus\mathbf{Z})\mathbf{V}_{AB}(\mathbf{I}\oplus\mathbf{Z})
. Then, we can show that

\displaystyle E(\mathbf{V}_{AB})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\ln(\tilde{v}^{(-)})=-\ln v+\ln e^{2r}

(73)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\ln\frac{\cosh 4r}{\cosh 2r}+2r.

(74)

The terms in the last line take its maximum, denoted by
E_{max}
, around
r\approx 0.2203
and
E_{max}\approx 0.1882
.

Example 2.
Consider two Gaussian states
A,B
whose CMs are
\mathbf{V}_{A}=(\begin{smallmatrix}u&0\\
0&v\end{smallmatrix})
,
\mathbf{V}_{B}=(\begin{smallmatrix}v&0\\
0&u\end{smallmatrix})
, respectively, passing through a balanced beam splitter, where
uv\geq 1
. Then, the CM
\mathbf{V}_{AB}
of the form

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{AB}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}u+v&0&-(u-v)&0\\
0&u+v&0&u-v\\
-(u-v)&0&u+v&0\\
0&u-v&0&u+v\end{pmatrix}

(75)

represents a general form of the resultant symmetric states.
The positive partial transpose (PPT) criterion
Peres 1996
;
Horodecki et al. 1996
allows us to find the simple condition for entanglement; it is entangled if and only if
\min(u,v)<1
. By using Gaussian atemporality robustness, we can also find that it is Gaussian-atemporal if and only if

\displaystyle\frac{1-u}{u^{2}}>\frac{v-1}{v^{2}}.

(76)

Proof.

As
\mathbf{V}_{AB}
is invariant under the action of switching the role of
A
and
B
, we have
f(\mathbf{V}_{AB})=\overleftarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB})=\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB})
, thus we will use the forward Gaussian atemporality robustness to derive the condition for Gaussian-atemporality. The forward pseudo-channel of
\mathbf{V}_{AB}
is given in a form of its transformation matrix and noise matrix,

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{C}^{\mbox{\tiny T}}\mathbf{V}_{A}^{-1}=-\frac{u-v}{u+v}\mathbf{Z},

(77)

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{V}_{B}-\mathbf{C}^{\mbox{\tiny T}}\mathbf{V}_{A}^{-1}\mathbf{C}=\frac{2uv}{u+v}\mathbf{I},

(78)

where
\mathbf{Z}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}
and
\mathbf{I}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
.
Then, we can determine the forward atemporality matrix as
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{N}+i(\bm{\Omega}-\mathbf{T}\bm{\Omega}\mathbf{T}^{\mbox{\tiny T}})
. By using
3
, a forward Gaussian atemporality robustness can be written as

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB})=\max(0,\absolutevalue{\omega}-\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}}),

(79)

with
\omega=1-\frac{\det\mathbf{C}}{\det\mathbf{V}_{A}}
and eigenvalues
n_{1},n_{2}\geq 0
of
\mathbf{N}
. Having
\mathbf{T},\mathbf{N}
found, we can readily find that
w=\frac{2(u^{2}+v^{2})}{(u+v)^{2}}
and
n_{1}=n_{2}=\frac{2uv}{u+v}
. Then it follows that

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max(0,2\frac{u^{2}(1-v)+v^{2}(1-u)}{(u+v)^{2}})

(80)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max(0,\frac{2}{(u+v)^{2}u^{2}v^{2}}\left(\frac{(1-v)}{v^{2}}+\frac{(1-u)}{u^{2}}\right)).

Thus, we can find the condition for
\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB})>0
as follows

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB})>0\Leftrightarrow\frac{(1-v)}{v^{2}}+\frac{(1-u)}{u^{2}}>0,

(81)

since
\frac{2}{(u+v)^{2}u^{2}v^{2}}
is always positive.

In turn, let us take a look at the conditions for entanglement. In the case of two-mode Gaussian states, the PPT condition becomes the necessary and sufficient condition for entanglement, and thus we have that

\tilde{v}^{(-)}<1
if and only if the state is entangled, where
\tilde{v}^{(-)}
represents the minimum value in the symplectic spectrum of
\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{AB}\equiv(\mathbf{I}\oplus\mathbf{Z})\mathbf{V}_{AB}(\mathbf{I}\oplus\mathbf{Z})

Peres 1996
;
Horodecki et al. 1996
;
Werner and Wolf 2001
;
Serafini et al. 2003
. We will complete the proof by showing that
\tilde{v}^{(-)}=\min(u,v)
.

\tilde{v}^{(-)}
is the smallest value in symplectic spectrum of

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{AB}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbf{I}\oplus\mathbf{Z})\mathbf{V}_{AB}(\mathbf{I}\oplus\mathbf{Z})

(82)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}u+v&0&-(u-v)&0\\
0&u+v&0&-(u-v)\\
-(u-v)&0&u+v&0\\
0&-(u-v)&0&u+v\end{pmatrix}

(83)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{A}&\mathbf{C}\\
\mathbf{C}&\mathbf{B}\end{pmatrix},

(84)

and then it can be found by using the following known formula,

\displaystyle\tilde{v}^{(-)}=\sqrt{\frac{\tilde{\Delta}-\sqrt{\tilde{\Delta}^{2}-4\det{\Tilde{\V}_{AB}}}}{2}},

(85)

where
\tilde{\Delta}\equiv\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B}+2\mathbf{C}
. Here
\tilde{\Delta}
yields

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}=\frac{(u+v)^{2}}{4}+\frac{(u+v)^{2}}{4}+\frac{2(u-v)^{2}}{4}=u^{2}+v^{2},

(86)

and
\det\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{AB}
yields

\displaystyle\det\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{AB}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(u+v)}{2}\begin{vmatrix}\frac{(u+v)}{2}&0&\frac{-(u-v)}{2}\\
0&\frac{(u+v)}{2}&0\\
\frac{-(u-v)}{2}&0&\frac{(u+v)}{2}\end{vmatrix}

(88)

\displaystyle-\frac{(u-v)}{2}\begin{vmatrix}0&\frac{(u+v)}{2}&\frac{-(u-v)}{2}\\
\frac{-(u-v)}{2}&0&0\\
0&\frac{-(u-v)}{2}&\frac{(u+v)}{2}\end{vmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(u+v)^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{(u+v)^{2}}{4}-\frac{(u-v)^{2}}{4}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{(u-v)^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{(u+v)^{2}}{4}-\frac{(u-v)^{2}}{4}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16}\left((u+v)^{2}-(u-v)^{2}\right)\left((u+v)^{2}-(u-v)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16}(4uv)(4uv)=u^{2}v^{2}.

(89)

Having
\tilde{\Delta}=u^{2}+v^{2}
and
\det\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{AB}=u^{2}v^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{v}^{(-)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\tilde{\Delta}-\sqrt{\tilde{\Delta}^{2}-4\det{\Tilde{\V}_{AB}}}}{2}}

(90)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{u^{2}+v^{2}-\sqrt{(u^{2}+v^{2})^{2}-4u^{2}v^{2}}}{2}}

(91)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{u^{2}+v^{2}-\absolutevalue{u^2-v^2}}{2}}

(92)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\sqrt{\frac{2v^{2}}{2}}\quad\text{if }u\geq v,\\
\sqrt{\frac{2u^{2}}{2}}\quad\text{if }u<v,\end{cases}

(93)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}v\quad\text{if }u\geq v,\\
u\quad\text{if }u<v,\end{cases}

(94)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min(u,v).

(95)

Example 3.
Imagine a Gaussian state whose CM is
\mathbf{V}_{A}=(\begin{smallmatrix}v_{1}&0\\
0&v_{2}\end{smallmatrix})
, evolving through a loss channel
\mathcal{L}:A\to B
such that it is characterized by
\mathbf{T}=\sqrt{\eta}\mathbf{I},\mathbf{N}=(1-\eta)\mathbf{I}
with
v_{1}v_{2}\geq 1
and a transmission rate
\eta<1
. By construction, a space-time CM,
\mathbf{V}_{AB}
, is not forward Gaussian-atemporal. However, its reverse Gaussian pseudo-channel is not always a valid channel, which leads to a non-zero reverse Gaussian-atemporality. Indeed,
\mathbf{V}_{AB}
is reverse Gaussian-atemporal, when
v_{1}^{(\eta)}v_{2}^{(\eta)}<1
, where
v_{k}^{(\eta)}\equiv\frac{v_{k}}{1+(v_{k}-1)\eta}
.

Proof.

From the scenario stated above, we can construct a space-time CM by using
1
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{AB}^{\star}=\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}&0&v_{1}\sqrt{\eta}&0\\
0&v_{2}&0&v_{2}\sqrt{\eta}\\
v_{1}\sqrt{\eta}&0&1+(v_{1}-1)\eta&0\\
0&v_{2}\sqrt{\eta}&0&1+(v_{2}-1)\eta\end{pmatrix}.

(96)

For notational brevity, we will omit
⋆
in the rest of the proof. By switching the role of
A
and
B
and using
2
, we find the reverse pseudo-channel from it, which is characterized by
\tilde{\mathbf{T}},\tilde{\mathbf{N}}
:

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{T}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}^{\mbox{\tiny T}}\mathbf{V}_{B}^{-1}

(97)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\frac{v\sqrt{\eta}}{1+(v_{1}-1)\eta}&0\\
0&\frac{v\sqrt{\eta}}{1+(v_{2}-1)\eta}\end{pmatrix},

(98)

and

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{N}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{A}-\mathbf{C}^{\mbox{\tiny T}}\mathbf{V}_{B}^{-1}\mathbf{C},

(99)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}&0\\
0&v_{2}\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}\frac{v_{1}^{2}\eta}{1+(v_{1}-1)\eta}&0\\
0&\frac{v_{2}^{2}\eta}{1+(v_{2}-1)\eta}\end{pmatrix}

(100)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\frac{v_{1}(1-\eta)}{1+(v_{1}-1)\eta}&0\\
0&\frac{v_{2}(1-\eta)}{1+(v_{2}-1)\eta}\end{pmatrix}.

(101)

Having
\tilde{\mathbf{T}},\tilde{\mathbf{N}}
found, we can readily have the reverse Gaussian atemporality robustness
\overleftarrow{f}
as follows:

\displaystyle\overleftarrow{f}(\mathbf{V}_{AB})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max(0,1-\frac{v_{1}v_{2}\eta}{(1+(v_{1}-1)\eta)(1+(v_{2}-1)\eta)}-\sqrt{(\frac{v_{1}(1-\eta)}{1+(v_{1}-1)\eta})(\frac{v_{2}(1-\eta)}{1+(v_{2}-1)\eta}}))

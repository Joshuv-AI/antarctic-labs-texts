NEW RESULTS ON ϕ \boldsymbol{\phi} (1020) PRODUCTION FROM THE NA61/SHINE EXPERIMENT AT CERN SPS
A. Marcinek for the NA61/SHINE Collaboration

Abstract
NA61/SHINE is a multipurpose, fixed-target hadron spectrometer at the CERN SPS. Its research program includes studies of strong interactions as well as reference measurements for neutrino and cosmic-ray physics. A significant advantage of NA61/SHINE over collider experiments is its extended coverage of phase space available for particle production. This includes the entire projectile hemisphere of the collision, with no low- p T p_{\mathrm{T}} cut-off. The energy and system-size dependence of strangeness production plays an essential role in studies of the transition from confined to deconfined matter. With its zero net strangeness and its valence structure composed predominantly of s s and s ¯ \bar{s} valence quarks, the ϕ \phi (1020) meson will not be sensitive to strangeness-related effects in a purely hadronic scenario, but will behave like a doubly strange particle in a partonic system. This contribution presents the first-ever results on ϕ \phi (1020) meson production in intermediate-size systems at the CERN SPS, that is, central Ar + Sc collisions at s NN \sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}} = 8.8 , 11.9, and 16.8 GeV. The presented results include double-differential rapidity-transverse momentum ( y y - p T p_{\mathrm{T}} ) distributions, transverse mass ( m T m_{\mathrm{T}} ) spectra at midrapidity, p T p_{\mathrm{T}} -integrated rapidity spectra, mean multiplicities ( 4 ​ π 4\pi yields), and particle ratios. These are compared to data on Pb + Pb and p p + p p collisions. A discussion of open and hidden strangeness production enhancement is included. Finally, a comparison with three microscopic models is shown, demonstrating their overall failure in describing these new measurements.

The NA61/SHINE detector
[

]
is a multipurpose fixed-target
spectrometer at the CERN SPS. Over the years the detector underwent multiple
upgrades.

Its main components are
large-volume Time Projection Chambers (TPCs), two of them (VTPCs) immersed in a magnetic
field perpendicular to the beam. This gives NA61/SHINE a significant advantage
over collider experiments — acceptance covering nearly entire forward c.m.s. hemisphere, down to
p_{\mathrm{T}}=0
for charged hadrons. In addition to providing
momentum measurement, TPCs provide also particle identification (PID)
capabilities using energy loss (
\derivative*{E}{x}
) of charged particles in the gas, which is
further augmented with time-of-flight measurements in limited phase-space range.
Centrality is measured via forward energy deposit in a hadronic calorimeter, the
Projectile Spectator Detector (PSD).

The first step of the analysis is selection of data. In case of
\phi
(1020) analysis
in
Ar
+
Sc
collisions, 10% most central events were chosen using forward energy
deposited in PSD, without pileup, with well measured main vertex, and occurring
in the target. Given that
\phi
(1020) mesons, from experimental perspective, decay
within the main vertex, well reconstructed tracks coming from the main vertex
were selected for the analysis, with enough points in TPCs to assure accurate
momentum and
\derivative*{E}{x}
measurement.

Special attention was given to particle identification selecting tracks whose

\derivative*{E}{x}
was inside bands around kaon Bethe-Bloch curve. In the old
analyses
[
7
,​
9
]
a single sample of kaon candidates was
chosen with narrow
\derivative*{E}{x}
cuts to suppress non-kaon background. Because these
cuts removed also significant amount of kaons, they also removed
\phi
(1020) mesons. This
bias was corrected for assuming precise knowledge of
\derivative*{E}{x}
distributions for
kaons and therefore came with significant systematic uncertainty. In NA61/SHINE a different approach was chosen
[
6
]
, using the

tag-and-probe
method
[
1
,​
2
]
. First, for every
track an outer band of
\pm 13\%
of kaon Bethe-Bloch value was used to suppress
non-kaons, without removing any kaons and thus introducing no bias. Second, the
actual tag-and-probe method was applied utilizing an inner, biasing, band of

\pm 5\%
of kaon Bethe-Bloch value. Two samples of oppositely-charged kaon
candidate pairs were created: in the
tag
sample at least one of kaon
candidates in the pair needed to pass the narrow
\derivative*{E}{x}
cut, while in the

probe
sample both candidates had to pass the cut.

The method is illustrated in
Fig.

2
. To perform the analysis
differentially, tag and probe invariant mass spectra were created in bins of
pairs’ rapidity and transverse momentum. Denoting the unknown efficiency of kaon
selection by the narrow cut as
\varepsilon
and the yield of
\phi
(1020) mesons
contributing to the spectra as
N_{\phi}
, combinatorics of the problem gives the
following expected signal yields in the two samples:

N_{\text{t}}=N_{\phi}\varepsilon(2-\varepsilon)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003ptN_{\text{p}}=N_{\phi}\varepsilon^{2}\,.

(1)

The tag and probe spectra were fitted simultaneously to get
N_{\phi}
. For both
samples the model curve was a sum of signal and background contributions, where
signal was a convolution of relativistic Breit-Wigner and
q-Gaussian
[
6
]
and background was parametrised as a sum
of event mixing and
K^{*}
(892)
0
templates.

Summary

Preliminary results on the
\phi
(1020) meson production in the 10% most central
Ar
+
Sc
collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\text{NN}}}

=

8.8\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}
,
\sqrt{s_{\text{NN}}}

=

11.9\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}
, and
\sqrt{s_{\text{NN}}}

=

16.8\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}
measured by the
NA61/SHINE experiment were presented. They were obtained using the
tag-and-probe method, utilizing the
\phi\to{K^{+}}{K^{-}}
decay channel. These are
the first-ever results on hidden strangeness production in intermediate-size
systems at CERN SPS. The results include double-differential rapidity and
transverse momentum spectra of
\phi
(1020) mesons, as well as
p_{\mathrm{T}}
-integrated rapidity
distributions, transverse mass spectra at midrapidity and
4\pi
yields. The
rapidity distributions were compared to three microscopic models, which failed
to reproduce the experimental results. Ratios of
\phi
(1020) to pion production were
considered, showing similarity of values for
Ar
+
Sc
and
Pb
+
Pb
systems, much
higher than for
p
+
p
collisions. Finally, hidden and open strangeness production
enhancement was discussed, showing that for
\phi
(1020) it is slightly higher than for
charged kaons in both
Ar
+
Sc
and
Pb
+
Pb
reactions, and independent of the
collision energy in the considered range.

Configured locally smooth cohomology and ℚ / ℤ \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} -torsion in H 3 H_{3} of diffeomorphism groups


Abstract
We introduce configured group cohomology, a variant of locally smooth cohomology built from well-configured tuples and geometric fillings. This framework yields explicit locally smooth ℝ / ℤ {\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}} -valued 3 3 -cocycles of Chern–Simons type on diffeomorphism groups preserving geometric structures. As an application we show that, for several such groups, the third group homology contains a subgroup isomorphic to ℚ / ℤ {\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}} .

Introduction

The cohomology of diffeomorphism groups lies at crossroads of topology,
geometry, foliation theory, and algebraic
K
-theory.
Degree-two classes are often closely related to central extensions and characteristic
class theory (see, e.g.,
[
Fuks
,
FSS
,
Neeb
]
).
In contrast, degree-three phenomena for geometrically defined diffeomorphism groups
are less well understood, especially when one seeks
explicit
cocycles that can be
evaluated and compared with classical secondary invariants.
The purpose of this paper is to construct and detect nontrivial locally smooth

{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued group
3
-cocycles of Chern–Simons type on several diffeomorphism groups
arising in low-dimensional and symplectic topology, and to use them to exhibit
{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
-torsion
in third group homology.

Throughout,
M
denotes a closed oriented smooth
n
-manifold, and
\mathop{\mathrm{Diff}}\nolimits(M)
denotes the group
of diffeomorphisms of
M
endowed with the Whitney topology.
Given a differential form
\lambda
on
M
, we denote by
\mathop{\mathrm{Diff}}\nolimits_{\lambda}(M)\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Diff}}\nolimits(M)

the subgroup preserving
\lambda
.
For a (topological) group
G
, we write
H_{*}^{\rm gr}(G)
for the integral group homology of the
underlying discrete group; see Section
2.1
for conventions.

We now state our three main results.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma\subset{\rm SO}(4)
be one of the following finite subgroups:

\{e\}
, the quaternion group
Q_{8}
, the prism group
D_{4n}
, the tetrahedral
T_{24}
, the octahedral
O_{48}
,
or the icosahedral
I_{120}
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Assume that the induced action of
\Gamma
on
S^{3}
is free, and set
M:=S^{3}/\Gamma
.
Let
v
denote the
3
-form on
M
induced by the standard volume form on
S^{3}
.

Then
H_{3}^{\rm gr}\bigl(\mathop{\mathrm{Diff}}\nolimits_{v}(M)\bigr)
contains a subgroup isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
{\mathbb{C}}P^{n}
be equipped with the symplectic
2
-form
{\omega}_{n}
arising from the Fubini–Study metric.
Then the third group homology with integer coefficients of each of the following groups
contains a subgroup isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
:

\mathop{\mathrm{Diff}}\nolimits_{{\omega}_{1}}({\mathbb{C}}P^{1}),\hskip 23.49976pt\mathop{\mathrm{Diff}}\nolimits_{{\omega}_{1}\times{\omega}_{1}}({\mathbb{C}}P^{1}\times{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}),\hskip 23.49976pt\mathop{\mathrm{Diff}}\nolimits_{{\omega}_{2}}({\mathbb{C}}P^{2}).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\alpha_{\rm st}\in{\cal A}^{1}(S^{3})
be the standard contact 1-form on the
3

S^{3}

(defining the standard contact structure

Then
H_{3}^{\rm gr}\bigl(\mathop{\mathrm{Diff}}\nolimits_{\alpha_{\rm st}}(S^{3})\bigr)
contains a subgroup isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Ideas of the proof.

Compared with much of the existing literature on degree-three classes for diffeomorphism groups,
our results are formulated at the (locally) cocycle level. In fact,
The key step is the construction of a nontrivial locally smooth
{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued group
3
-cocycle
of Chern–Simons type
[
CS
]
.
Starting from an invariant differential form
\lambda
on a (Fréchet) Lie group
{\cal G}
,
we construct a cochain by integrating
\lambda
over a family of simplices with prescribed vertices
(Section
3
).
In infinite dimensions such a straightening is not available canonically on the full Cartesian power

{\cal G}^{n+1}
, and even in finite dimensions one typically obtains smoothness only on suitable open subsets.
To organize this systematically, we work in the restriction-of-domain framework of Definition
2.1
:
we replace the full homogeneous complex by an invariant subcomplex
{\mathbb{Z}}\langle S_{*}\rangle

coming from a
well-configured
sequence
{\cal S}=\{S_{n}\subset{\cal G}^{n+1}\}

(Section
2.2
).

For the diffeomorphism groups considered here, we take
S_{n}
to be the set of
U
-small tuples

(g_{0},\dots,g_{n})
for which all relative differences
g_{i}^{-1}g_{j}
lie in a fixed identity neighbourhood
U

(Definition (
3.7
)).
A fixed convex chart around the identity then yields an explicit
affine straightening
:
using an “affine join” inside the chart we construct a filling
\sigma
over
{\cal S}_{{\cal G},U}

(Subsection
3.2
) and hence a cochain
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}(\lambda):{\cal G}^{n+1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
by integration.
To obtain an honest
{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued cocycle, one must control periods and, crucially, eliminate the
low-degree homotopy obstruction encoded in Proposition
3.10
(notably involving
\pi_{2}({\cal G})
).
In the groups in the theorems, this obstruction can be verified to vanish using concrete homotopy-type results:
in the
3
-manifold cases we use Smale-type theorems identifying

\mathop{\mathrm{Diff}}\nolimits_{v}(M)
with the isometry group
\mathrm{Isom}^{+}(M)
—namely the generalized Smale conjecture proved by Bamler–Kleiner
for spherical space forms
[
BK
]
, and Gabai’s theorem in the hyperbolic case
[
Gab
]
.
Finally, Proposition
3.12
shows that the derivation of the cocycle
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}(\lambda)

is a representative cocycle in the
Gel’fand–Fuks cohomology of the Lie algebra of
\mathop{\mathrm{Diff}}\nolimits_{\lambda}(M)
.
This compatibility is the main tool for detecting nontriviality by restriction to compact subgroups and
comparison with classical Cheeger–Chern–Simons classes (see Section
4
and
4.1
or
[
DPS
,
DZ
]
).

This idea yields new and computable
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
-torsion phenomena in
H^{\mathrm{gr}}_{3}

for several classical diffeomorphism groups arising in low-dimensional and symplectic-contact topology.
In fact, for symplectic manifolds, we use known homotopy equivalences for
{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
and the results of Gromov
for
{\mathbb{C}}P^{2}

[
Gro
]
and Abreu–McDuff for
({\mathbb{C}}P^{1}\times{\mathbb{C}}P^{1},\omega_{1}\oplus\omega_{1})

[
AM
]
,
which again imply the required vanishing of
\pi_{2}
in the identity component;
Theorems
1.2
–
1.3
follow by applying the ideas presented above.
Incidentally, the hyperbolic case (Section
4.4
) yields a related
{\mathbb{R}}
-valued
3
-cocycle whose
vanishing in cohomology remains open.

Preliminaries; group cohomology

In Section

In Section

2.1
Review of group (co)homology and (locally) smooth group cohomology

We first recall group (co)homology using the homogeneous complex.
Throughout this subsection
G
denotes a (discrete) group.

For
n\geq 0
and
0\leq i\leq n
we define the
i
-th face map

d_{i}^{n}:G^{n+1}\longrightarrow G^{n};\hskip 23.49976pt(g_{0},\dots,g_{n})\longmapsto(g_{0},\dots,g_{i-1},g_{i+1},\dots,g_{n}).

(1)

Let
C_{n}^{\Delta}(G)
be the free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module
{\mathbb{Z}}\langle G^{n+1}\rangle
with basis
G^{n+1}
.
We consider the diagonal action
G\curvearrowright G^{n+1}
and denote by

C_{n}^{\Delta}(G)_{G}
the module of coinvariants, i.e., the quotient module of

C_{n}^{\Delta}(G)
by the action.
For
a\in G^{n+1}
we set

\partial_{n}[a]:=\sum_{i:0\leq i\leq n}(-1)^{i}[d_{i}^{n}(a)]\in C_{n-1}^{\Delta}(G)_{G},

and extend
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linearly to obtain a boundary map

\partial_{n}:C_{n}^{\Delta}(G)_{G}\rightarrow C_{n-1}^{\Delta}(G)_{G}.

The homology of this quotient complex is called the
group homology of
G
and is denoted
by
H_{n}^{\rm gr}(G)
.

Dually, we recall the definition of group cohomology (see
[
Bro
]
for details).
Let
A
be an abelian group.
We define
C^{n}_{\Delta}(G;A):=\{f:G^{n+1}\rightarrow A\}

to be the module consisting of all maps
G^{n+1}\to A
.
For
f\in C^{n}_{\Delta}(G;A)
we define the coboundary

\partial^{n}(f):G^{n+2}\to A
by the usual simplicial formula
\partial^{n}(f)(a)=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{i}f\bigl(d_{i}^{n+1}(a)\bigr)
for
a\in G^{n+2}.

Again
G
acts diagonally on
G^{n+1}
, and we denote the
G
-invariant part of

C^{n}_{\Delta}(G;A)
by
C^{n}_{\Delta}(G;A)^{G}
.
Since
\partial^{n+1}\circ\partial^{n}=0
, the
group cohomology

H^{n}_{\rm gr}(G;A)
is defined as

H^{n}_{\rm gr}(G;A):=\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}\nolimits(\partial^{n})\cap C^{n}_{\Delta}(G;A)^{G}}{\partial^{n-1}\bigl(C^{n-1}_{\Delta}(G;A)^{G}\bigr)}.

If
G
is regarded as a discrete group, then the ordinary cohomology of the
Eilenberg–MacLane space
K(G,1)
with coefficients in
A
is canonically
isomorphic to
H^{n}_{\rm gr}(G;A)
.
Moreover, consider the pairing (or evaluation)

\langle\,,\,\rangle:C^{n}_{\Delta}(G;A)\times C_{n}^{\Delta}(G)\longrightarrow A;\hskip 23.49976pt(f,a)\longmapsto f(a),

which induces
H^{n}_{\rm gr}(G;A)\times H_{n}^{\rm gr}(G)\rightarrow A,
and is called
the Kronecker product
.

Next, we recall the (locally) continuous group cohomology.
From now on we assume that
{\cal G}
is a (Fréchet) Lie group
and that
A
is one of

{\mathbb{R}}
,
{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
,
{\mathbb{C}}
, or
{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
endowed with the standard topology, regarded as a trivial
topological
{\cal G}
-module.
We denote by
C^{n}_{\rm sm}({\cal G};A)^{{\cal G}}
the group of
{\cal G}
-invariant smooth maps

{\cal G}^{n+1}\to A
.
Since
C^{*}_{\rm sm}({\cal G};A)^{{\cal G}}
is a subcomplex of
C^{*}_{\Delta}({\cal G};A)^{{\cal G}}
,
the cohomology of the complex
C^{*}_{\rm sm}({\cal G};A)^{{\cal G}}
will be called the

(smooth) continuous cohomology
of
{\cal G}
and is denoted by

H^{*}_{\rm sm}({\cal G};A)
.
The inclusion
C^{*}_{\rm sm}({\cal G};A)^{{\cal G}}\hookrightarrow C^{*}_{\Delta}({\cal G};A)^{{\cal G}}
induces a homomorphism
\mathrm{Comp}:H^{*}_{\rm sm}({\cal G};A)\rightarrow H^{*}_{\rm gr}({\cal G};A),
called the
comparison map
.
In the sequel we shall always work with smooth (hence continuous) homogeneous cochains
and refer to
H^{*}_{\rm sm}({\cal G};A)
as the continuous cohomology of
{\cal G}
.

Finally, we recall locally smooth cohomology (see, for example,
[
WW
,
Neeb
]
).
For
n\geq 0
we define the group of
n
-cochains

C^{n}_{\mathrm{loc,sm}}({\cal G};A):=\left\{\,f:{\cal G}^{n+1}\to A\ \middle|\ \begin{aligned} &\text{There exists an open subset }\,U_{f}\subset{\cal G}^{n+1}\text{ such that},\\
&(e,\dots,e)\in U_{f},\textrm{ and the restriction }f|_{U_{f}}\ \text{is smooth.}\end{aligned}\right\}.

We denote by
C^{*}_{{\rm loc,sm}}({\cal G};A)^{{\cal G}}
the subcomplex of
{\cal G}
-invariant cochains,
and write
H^{n}_{{\rm loc,sm}}({\cal G},A)

for the corresponding cohomology
H^{n}\bigl(C^{*}_{{\rm loc,sm}}({\cal G};A)^{{\cal G}},\partial^{*}\bigr)
.
For the detailed comparison, we refer the reader to
[
WW
,
Neeb
]
.

2.2
Configured continuous cohomology

Here, we introduce configured continuous cohomology and compare it
with the theories recalled in Section
2.1
.
We first introduce the following notion (In Section
4.5
, we discuss the motivation for introducing well-configured complexes).

Definition 2.1
.

Fix
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let
{\cal G}
be a (Fréchet) Lie group.
A sequence of open subsets
{\cal S}=\{S_{n}\subset{\cal G}^{n+1}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is said to be

well-configured (of length
q
)
if it satisfies the following conditions.

(i)

(Simplicial condition) Each
S_{n}
is closed under the diagonal action of
{\cal G}
and under the face maps, that is,

d_{i}^{n}(S_{n})\subset S_{n-1}
for any
0\leq i\leq n
, and we have
S_{0}={\cal G}
.

(ii)

For each
n
, let
{\mathbb{Z}}\langle S_{n}\rangle
be the free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module with basis
S_{n}
, and
\varepsilon:{\mathbb{Z}}\langle S_{0}\rangle\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be the usual augmentation map.
The augmented chain subcomplex of free
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\cal G}]
-modules

{\mathbb{Z}}\langle S_{q}\rangle\xrightarrow{\ \partial_{q}\ }{\mathbb{Z}}\langle S_{q-1}\rangle\xrightarrow{\ \partial_{q-1}\ }\cdots\xrightarrow{\ \partial_{2}\ }{\mathbb{Z}}\langle S_{1}\rangle\xrightarrow{\ \partial_{1}\ }{\mathbb{Z}}\langle S_{0}\rangle\xrightarrow{\ \varepsilon\ }{\mathbb{Z}}\longrightarrow 0,

is required to be exact (equivalently,
H_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}\langle S_{*}\rangle)=0
for all
1\leq n<q
and
H_{0}({\mathbb{Z}}\langle S_{*}\rangle)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
).

In this situation, if
A
is among of
{\mathbb{R}}
or
{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, for each
n
with
0\leq n\leq q
, we define

C^{n}({\cal S};A)^{{\cal G}}:=\{f:S_{n}\to A\mid f\text{ is ${\cal G}$-invariant}\},

(2)

C_{\rm sm}^{n}({\cal S};A)^{{\cal G}}:=\{f:S_{n}\to A\mid f\text{ is ${\cal G}$-invariant and smooth}\}.

(3)

By condition (i) and the simplicial formula for
\partial^{*}
, these form cochain complexes, and we
denote their cohomology groups by
H_{{\cal S}}^{*}({\cal G};A)
and
H_{\rm sm,{\cal S}}^{*}({\cal G};A)
, respectively.

Proposition 2.2

(cf. Comparison theorem in
[
Bro
, Theorem I. (7.5)]
)
.

Let
{\cal S}=\{S_{n}\subset{\cal G}^{n+1}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a well-configured sequence of length
q
,
and consider
{\mathbb{Z}}\langle{\cal G}^{n+1}\rangle
as the free
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\cal G}]
-module. Then there exist a
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\cal G}]
-module chain map

r_{n}:\ {\mathbb{Z}}\langle{\cal G}^{n+1}\rangle\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\langle S_{n}\rangle,\hskip 23.49976pt0\leq n\leq q,

such that the restriction satisfies
r_{n}|_{{\mathbb{Z}}\langle S_{n}\rangle}=\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{Z}}\langle S_{n}\rangle}
for
0\leq n\leq q
.
In particular,
If
S_{n}
contains
(e,\dots,e)
and
f
is smooth on
S_{n}
, then the restriction of the composite
f\circ r_{n}
on
{\cal G}^{n+1}
is locally smooth.
Here, we extend
f

{\mathbb{Z}}
-linearly to
{\mathbb{Z}}\langle S_{n}\rangle
and
f\circ r_{n}
by evaluation.

Moreover, if
f\in C^{q}({\cal S};A)^{{\cal G}}
satisfies
f(\kappa)=0
for every
\kappa\in\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}\nolimits(\partial_{q}:{\mathbb{Z}}\langle S_{q}\rangle\to{\mathbb{Z}}\langle S_{q-1}\rangle)
,
then the composite

is a (homogeneous) group
q
-cocycle on
{\cal G}^{q+1}
.

Proof.

Put
B_{n}:={\mathbb{Z}}\langle{\cal G}^{n+1}\rangle
and
C_{n}:={\mathbb{Z}}\langle S_{n}\rangle
with the simplicial boundary.
By condition (i),
C_{*}\subset B_{*}
is a chain subcomplex.
Moreover, the diagonal left
{\cal G}
-action on
{\cal G}^{n+1}
is free, hence
B_{n}
is a free
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\cal G}]
-module;
the same holds for
C_{n}
since
S_{n}
is on
{\cal G}
-orbits.

We construct
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\cal G}]
-module homomorphisms
r_{n}:B_{n}\to C_{n}
inductively for
0\leq n\leq q
so that

r_{n}|_{C_{n}}=\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits
and
\partial r_{n}=r_{n-1}\partial
.
Set
r_{0}=\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits
(note
C_{0}=B_{0}={\mathbb{Z}}\langle{\cal G}\rangle
).
Assume
r_{0},\dots,r_{n}
are constructed.
For a generator
\sigma\in S_{n+1}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}\langle{\cal G}^{n+2}\angle
, define
r_{n+1}(\sigma):=\sigma
if
\sigma\in S_{n+1}
.
Otherwise we suppose that

is of type
(e,g_{1},\dots,g_{n+1})
and

. Then, we notice

.
By exactness of the configured complex in degree

Extend

{\mathbb{Z}}[{\cal G}]
-linearly. Then
\partial r_{n+1}=r_{n}\partial

For the “locally smooth” claim, note that since

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\{S_{n}\subset{\cal G}^{n+1}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence satisfying Definition

(i).
Assume that for every
n
and every finite family

\{(x_{0,i},\dots,x_{n,i})\}_{i=1}^{m}\subset S_{n}
, there exists

such that

(x_{0,i},\dots,x_{n,i},y)\in S_{n+1},\text{for all }i=1,\dots,m.

Then the augmented chain complex
{\mathbb{Z}}\langle S_{*}\rangle
is exact, hence
{\cal S}
is well-configured.

Proof.

Fix
n>0
and
write any
n
-cycle

\sum_{i}a_{i}(x_{0,i},\dots,x_{n,i})\in{\mathbb{Z}}\langle S_{n}\rangle,

for some
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}},x_{j,i}\in{\cal G}
.
By the assumption, we can choose
y\in{\cal G}
such that

(x_{0,i},\dots,x_{n,i},y)\in S_{n+1}
for all

.
Set

\tau:=(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{i}a_{i}(x_{0,i},\dots,x_{n,i},y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}\langle S_{n+1}\rangle.

A direct computation shows that

\partial_{n+1}(\tau)=\sigma
, which means the required exactness.
∎

Example 2.6
.

Suppose that
G

Motivated by configuration spaces, consider

\mathrm{Conf}^{n+1}(G):=\{(g_{0},\dots,g_{n})\in G^{n+1}\mid g_{i}\neq g_{j}\text{ for }i\neq j\}.

The complex
({\mathbb{Z}}\langle\mathrm{Conf}^{*+1}(G)\rangle,\partial_{*})
appears in several works

3.4
Relation to the Gel’fand–Fuks cohomology

We finally discuss the relation with the Gel’fand–Fuks cohomology (Proposition
3.12
).
Let
G
be a Fréchet–Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
For
n\geq 0
we define the smooth Lie algebra complex with trivial coefficients

{\mathbb{R}}
by

C^{n}_{\rm GF}(\mathfrak{g}):=\{\omega:\mathfrak{g}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}\mid\omega\text{ is alternating, multilinear and of class }C^{\infty}\}.

The differential

is given by the usual Chevalley–Eilenberg formula

(d_{\mathfrak{g}}\omega)(X_{1},\dots,X_{n+1})=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i+j}\,\omega([X_{i},X_{j}],X_{1},\dots,\widehat{X}_{i},\dots,\widehat{X}_{j},\dots,X_{n+1}),

for
X_{1},\dots,X_{n+1}\in\mathfrak{g}
.
It is well known that
d_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ d_{\mathfrak{g}}=0
and that the resulting
cohomology
H^{*}_{\rm GF}(\mathfrak{g})

is called the Gel’fand–Fuks cohomology,
and canonically isomorphic to the cohomology of the complex
({\cal A}^{*}(G)^{G},d)
of left-invariant differential forms on
G
; see, for example,

[
Fuks
]
.

Next, we recall a chain map from locally smooth group cochains to

C^{*}_{\rm GF}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Let
A
be a topological abelian group isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}
or
{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
with trivial

G
-action.
For
f\in C^{n}_{{\rm loc},{\rm sm}}(G,A)
we choose a neighbourhood
U_{f}\subset G^{n+1}

of
e
on which
f
is smooth.
Since
\exp(0)=e
and
\exp
is smooth, after shrinking
U_{f}
we may choose a

0
-neighbourhood
V_{f}\subset\mathfrak{g}
such that
\exp((V_{f})^{n+1})\subset U_{f}
.
For

we set

\Phi_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}):=f\bigl(e,\,\exp(X_{1}),\,\exp(X_{1})\exp(X_{2}),\,\dots,\,\exp(X_{1})\cdots\exp(X_{n})\bigr).

Then
\Phi_{f}
, regarded as a function on

, is smooth in a neighbourhood of the origin

(0,\dots,0)
, and we may define the iterated directional derivative

\bigl(T^{n}f\bigr)(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}):=\bigl(\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t_{1}\cdots\partial t_{n}}\,\Phi_{f}(t_{1}X_{1},\dots,t_{n}X_{n})\bigr|_{t_{1}=\cdots=t_{n}=0}.

(15)

Next, we define the alternating
n
-linear map

D(f)(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}):=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\,\bigl(T^{n}f\bigr)(X_{\sigma(1)},\dots,X_{\sigma(n)}),\hskip 23.49976ptX_{1},\dots,X_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then

is a cochain map; see, for example,
[

,
WW
]
for detailed proofs.

The next proposition shows how the cochains

in (

A proof is given in Appendix

4.1
Review: Cheeger–Chern–Simons cocycles

We begin by recalling the definition of the Chern–Simons form.
Let
G
be a Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, and let

E\to M
be a principal
G
-bundle.
Let
P\colon\mathfrak{g}^{k}\to{\mathbb{R}}

be a symmetric
k
-linear map which is invariant under the diagonal adjoint action

G\curvearrowright\mathfrak{g}^{k}
.
Let
t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Given two connections
\omega_{0},\omega_{1}\in{\cal A}^{1}(E;\mathfrak{g})
, we consider the
affine interpolation

\omega_{t}:=(1-t)\,\omega_{0}+t\,\omega_{1}

and denote by

\Omega_{t}:=d\omega_{t}+\tfrac{1}{2}[\omega_{t},\omega_{t}]
its curvature.
By the basic results of Chern–Weil theory, the
2k
-form

P(\Omega_{t},\dots,\Omega_{t})
is closed and its de Rham cohomology class is independent of
t
.
The
Chern–Simons
(2k-1)
-form
associated with
P
and the pair of connections

\omega_{0},\omega_{1}
is defined by

T_{P}(\omega_{0},\omega_{1}):=k\int_{0}^{1}P\bigl(\omega_{0}-\omega_{1},\Omega_{t},\dots,\Omega_{t}\bigr)\,dt\in\Omega^{2k-1}(M).

It satisfies the transgression identity

P(\Omega_{0},\dots,\Omega_{0})-P(\Omega_{1},\dots,\Omega_{1})=d\bigl(T_{P}(\omega_{0},\omega_{1})\bigr).

If
M
is a closed
(2k-1)
-dimensional manifold, the (relative)
Chern–Simons invariant
for two connections
\omega_{0},\omega_{1}
is
the integral
\int_{M}T_{P}(\omega_{0},\omega_{1})\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

There are two natural issues here.
First, this integral is in general not gauge invariant.
However, for suitable normalizations of
P
, the integral
\int_{M}T_{P}(\omega_{0},\omega_{1})\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
modulo
{\mathbb{Z}}
is often well
defined and becomes gauge invariant.
Second, one has to choose the reference connection
\omega_{0}
.
Two standard choices are the following:

(i)

If
E
is the trivial bundle
G\times M
, one often takes
\omega_{0}
to be the
trivial connection.

(ii)

If
G={\rm SO}(2k-1)
and
E
is the principal frame bundle associated with the tangent
bundle of
M
, one takes
\omega_{0}
to be the Levi-Civita connection of a fixed
Riemannian metric on
M
.

Next, we recall the notion of
Cheeger–Chern–Simons cocycles
(CCS cocycles);
see
[
CS
]
.
For each group
G
endowed with the discrete topology we fix an Eilenberg–MacLane space

K(G,1)
.
If
G=\pi_{1}(M)
, we fix a classifying map
\iota:M\rightarrow K(\pi_{1}(M),1).

Given a group homomorphism
f:\pi_{1}(M)\to G
, there is an induced cellular map

f_{*}\circ\iota:M\to K(G,1)
, unique up to homotopy.
In particular, the fundamental class
[M]\in H_{2k-1}(M)
has a well-defined image

f_{*}[M]\in H_{2k-1}(K(G,1))\cong H_{2k-1}^{\rm gr}(G).

On the other hand, the basic theory of flat bundles associates to
f
a principal

G
-bundle
E_{f}\to M
endowed with a flat connection
\omega_{f}
, up to conjugacy.

In the above situation, a group
(2k-1)
-cocycle
c_{P}^{\mathrm{CS}}:G^{2k}\to{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
is called a

Cheeger–Chern–Simons cocycle

[
CS
]
for
P
if for every group homomorphism

f:\pi_{1}(M)\to G
there exists a connection
\omega_{0}
on
E_{f}
such that

\int_{M}T_{P}(\omega_{0},\omega_{f})=\bigl\langle[c_{P}^{\mathrm{CS}}],f_{*}[M]\bigr\rangle\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}.

(17)

In situations (i) and (ii) above, the reference connection
\omega_{0}
is chosen as
indicated: trivial in case (i) and Levi-Civita in case (ii), respectively.

In general, the existence of CCS cocycles depends on the choice of
E
,
G
and
P
, and
even when they exist it is a subtle problem to decide on which subset
S_{2k-1}\subset G^{2k}
such a
cocycle can be chosen to be smooth.
The history of this problem is long and intricate, so we confine ourselves to quoting two
typical results, which we use later.

4.2
A lemma on transfer

In several places we use a basic lemma
4.4
below concerning transfer in group cohomology.
For the reader’s convenience we include a proof.
Let
\Gamma
be a group and let
G\subset\Gamma
be a normal subgroup of finite index
|\Gamma/G|<\infty
.
Let
A
be either
{\mathbb{R}}
or
{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, regarded as a trivial
\Gamma
-module.

We review the transfer (corestriction) on homogeneous cochains from
[
Bro

.
Choose a set
T\subset\Gamma
of representatives for the
right
cosets
G\backslash\Gamma
;
thus
\Gamma=\bigsqcup_{t\in T}Gt
and we may assume

For

\overline{t\gamma}\in T
be the unique representative of the right coset

For

(18)

Notice that the quotient

acts on

If a cocycle

-cocycle.
The coboundary

is continuous and

-valued, hence
constant; denote this constant by

.

is an

If

-cocycle.

is a coboundary.
∎

Remark 4.15

In Section
5.1
, we construct a natural

-invariant Lie algebra

5.1
Invariant
3
-forms on
\mathop{\mathrm{Diff}}\nolimits_{\omega}(M)

We recall the basic Hamiltonian formalism (see, e.g.,
[
Can
]
).
Given
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, the corresponding Hamiltonian vector field
X_{f}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)

is defined by
\iota_{X_{f}}\omega=-df,

where
\iota
denotes interior multiplication.
The Poisson bracket of
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
is given by

\{f,g\}:=\omega(X_{f},X_{g})=X_{f}(g)\in C^{\infty}(M).

With this bracket
C^{\infty}(M)
becomes a (topological) Lie algebra.
Let
{\mathbb{R}}_{\rm const}
be the subalgebra consisting constant functions.
We set

\mathfrak{ham}(M,\omega):=\{X_{f}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\mid\iota_{X_{f}}\omega=-df,\ f\in C^{\infty}(M)\}

for the Lie algebra of Hamiltonian vector fields.
Then the correspondence
f\rightarrow X_{f}

induces a Lie algebra isomorphism
C^{\infty}(M)/{\mathbb{R}}_{\rm const}\cong\mathfrak{ham}(M,\omega)
.

Next, we recall the known exact sequence (
22
) and show Lemma
5.1
. By Cartan’s formula,
L_{X}\omega=d(\iota_{X}\omega)

for every
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
.
For
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
with
L_{X}\omega=0
, put

\kappa(X):=[\iota_{X}\omega]\in H^{1}(M;\mathbb{R}).

Then

0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{ham}(M,\omega)\longrightarrow\{X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\mid L_{X}\omega=0\}\xrightarrow{\ \kappa\ }H^{1}(M;\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow 0

(22)

is exact.
In addition, we now record an elementary identity, which might be known.

Lemma 5.1
.

For
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
define
\langle f,g\rangle_{M}:=\int_{M}fg\,\omega^{n}.

Then for all
f,g,h\in C^{\infty}(M)
,

\langle\{f,g\},h\rangle_{M}+\langle g,\{f,h\}\rangle_{M}=0.

(23)

Proof.

Liouville’s theorem gives
L_{X_{f}}\omega^{n}=0
for every
f
.
Hence, for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
,

L_{X_{f}}(\varphi\,\omega^{n})=X_{f}(\varphi)\,\omega^{n}+\varphi\,L_{X_{f}}\omega^{n}=X_{f}(\varphi)\,\omega^{n}.

By Stokes’ theorem and the compactness of
M
, we have

\int_{M}X_{f}(\varphi)\,\omega^{n}=\int_{M}L_{X_{f}}(\varphi\,\omega^{n})=\int_{M}d\bigl(\iota_{X_{f}}(\varphi\,\omega^{n})\bigr)=0.

(24)

Now we will show (
23
) as

\displaystyle\langle\{f,g\},h\rangle_{M}+\langle g,\{f,h\}\rangle_{M}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\{f,g\}\,h\,\omega^{n}+\int_{M}g\,\{f,h\}\,\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\bigl(X_{f}(g)\,h+g\,X_{f}(h)\bigr)\,\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}X_{f}(gh)\,\omega^{n}=0,

where we used (
24
) with
\varphi=gh
.
∎

In general, for a quadratic Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\ ,\ ],\langle\ ,\ \rangle)
one defines the
alternating trilinear form

\Phi(x,y,z):=\langle x,[y,z]\rangle,\hskip 23.49976ptx,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

(25)

It is well known that

is a Lie algebra

-cocycle, which is called
the Cartan

-cocycle
; see, e.g.,
[

, §4]

Especially, when

is a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra and

is the Killing form,

We now adapt this construction to the symplectic situation.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact symplectic manifold.
We define a 3-form

as follows:

\beta_{M}\colon\Lambda^{3}\mathfrak{ham}(M,\omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R};\hskip 23.49976ptX_{f}\wedge X_{g}\wedge X_{h}\mapsto\frac{3}{\pi^{3}}\langle f,\{g,h\}\rangle_{M}=\frac{3}{\pi^{3}}\int_{M}f\{g,h\}\,\omega^{n}.

By construction,

In particular, if

\mathfrak{ham}(M,\omega)=\{X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\mid L_{X}\omega=0\},

(27)

In this case we may regard

3
-form on the

6
Contact analogue: strict contactomorphism groups

Let
(M,\alpha)
be a closed cooriented contact manifold of dimension
2n+1
.
This section focuses on the
strict contactomorphism group

\mathop{\mathrm{Diff}}\nolimits_{\alpha}(M)
.
In this section, following the approach of the previous section, we discuss the third cohomology of the diffeomorphism group endowed with a contact structure. As a result, we establish the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
R_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
be the Reeb vector field defined by
\alpha(R_{\alpha})=1
and
\iota_{R_{\alpha}}d\alpha=0
.
The Lie algebra of
\mathop{\mathrm{Diff}}\nolimits_{\alpha}(M)
can be written as

{\mathfrak{scont}}(M,\alpha):=\{\,X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\mid L_{X}\alpha=0\,\}.

For any
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
satisfying
R_{\alpha}(f)=0
, there is a unique vector field

X_{f}\in{\mathfrak{scont}}(M,\alpha)
such that

\alpha(X_{f})=f,
and
\iota_{X_{f}}d\alpha=-df
.
For such
f,g
define

\{f,g\}_{\alpha}:=d\alpha(X_{f},X_{g})=X_{f}(g).

Then
C^{\infty}(M)^{R_{\alpha}}:=\{f\in C^{\infty}(M)\mid R_{\alpha}(f)=0\}
becomes a (topological) Lie algebra,
and
f\mapsto X_{f}
induces a Lie algebra isomorphism
C^{\infty}(M)^{R_{\alpha}}\cong{\mathfrak{scont}}(M,\alpha)
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)^{R_{\alpha}}
define

\langle f,g\rangle_{\alpha}:=\int_{M}fg\,\mu_{\alpha}
, where
\mu_{\alpha}:=\alpha\wedge(d\alpha)^{n}
.
Then for all
f,g,h\in C^{\infty}(M)^{R_{\alpha}}
,

\langle\{f,g\}_{\alpha},h\rangle_{\alpha}+\langle g,\{f,h\}_{\alpha}\rangle_{\alpha}=0.

(29)

Proof.

Since
L_{X_{f}}\alpha=0
and
L_{X_{f}}d\alpha=0
, we have
L_{X_{f}}\mu_{\alpha}=0
.
Hence, for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
,

L_{X_{f}}(\varphi\,\mu_{\alpha})=X_{f}(\varphi)\,\mu_{\alpha}
; thus, and Stokes’ theorem gives
\int_{M}X_{f}(\varphi)\,\mu_{\alpha}=0
.
Now, as in Lemma
5.1
, we have

\langle\{f,g\}_{\alpha},h\rangle_{\alpha}+\langle g,\{f,h\}_{\alpha}\rangle_{\alpha}=\int_{M}\bigl(X_{f}(g)\,h+g\,X_{f}(h)\bigr)\,\mu_{\alpha}=\int_{M}X_{f}(gh)\,\mu_{\alpha}=0.

∎

As an analogy of (
26
), we define an alternating trilinear form

\beta_{(M,\alpha)}:\Lambda^{3}{\mathfrak{scont}}(M,\alpha)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}};\hskip 23.49976ptX_{f}\wedge X_{g}\wedge X_{h}\longmapsto\frac{3}{\pi^{3}}\int_{M}f\,\{g,h\}_{\alpha}\,\mu_{\alpha}.

(30)

By construction,
\beta_{(M,\alpha)}
is
\mathop{\mathrm{Diff}}\nolimits_{\alpha}(M)
-invariant.
Lemma
6.1
shows that it is a Lie algebra
3
-cocycle, hence defines a class

[\beta_{(M,\alpha)}]\in H^{3}_{\rm GF}({\mathfrak{scont}}(M,\alpha);{\mathbb{R}})
.
We may regard
\beta_{(M,\alpha)}
as a left-invariant closed
3
-form on
\mathop{\mathrm{Diff}}\nolimits_{\alpha}(M)
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
{\cal G}\to\mathop{\mathrm{Diff}}\nolimits_{\alpha,0}(M)
be a covering group whose Lie algebra is
{\mathfrak{scont}}(M,\alpha)
.
Assume that
{\cal G}
is
2
-connected, and that
{\cal G}
and a discrete lattice

The case

To prove Theorem

Lemma 6.3

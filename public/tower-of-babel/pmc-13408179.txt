Role of Immunological Markers in Diagnosis, Prognosis, and Treatment of Early Childhood Caries and Adult Caries: A Systematic Review
Authors: Vishnu Priya Veeraraghavan, Ullas Mony, Gamal Othman, Bashayer Khalid Alskait
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDental caries is a common oral health problem that affects people all over the world. To detect and treat it early on, it is important to have a thorough understanding of its genesis and potential biomarkers.ObjectiveThe objective of this systematic review is to answer the question: “Is there any role of immune status in the development of dental caries among children and adults, and is there any potential in using immunological markers for diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment of this condition?”DesignFrom 2016 to 2024, a thorough search approach adhering to Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) criteria was used across several datasets (PROSPERO: CRD42024612045). To find pertinent publications, a total of 2,723 articles were searched using keywords associated with dental caries, immunity, and interventions. The inclusion criteria included papers that addressed the connection between immunity and the development of caries, interventions, and results, including alterations in basal metabolic rate (BMR), weight gain in patients, improvements in quality of life, and recurrence of carious lesions. Two reviewers independently extracted the data, assessed its quality, and assessed the risk of bias using validated instruments such as the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool and the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale.ResultsThe evaluation encompassed 10 articles in all, demonstrating noteworthy correlations between immunological indicators and dental caries. Results point to a favorable correlation between salivary immunoglobulin A (IgA) levels and the number of carious teeth, as well as an increase in proinflammatory cytokine concentrations in dental caries patients. Furthermore, the pulp tissue of dental caries patients has different bacterial communities and higher expression of proinflammatory cytokines, suggesting a possible connection between immune activation and dysbiosis.ConclusionThe findings highlight the significance of immune status/immunological indicators in the diagnosis and treatment of dental caries, emphasizing the necessity for additional study to corroborate these conclusions and improve treatment and preventative dental care practices.Clinical relevanceThis work has got great relevance to pediatric dentists. The current work highlights a possible link between the immune status and the development of caries in pediatric and adult patients as well as the potential of immunological markers in the diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment of dental caries in both pediatric and adult patients.Prospero registration numberCRD42024612045.

Introduction

Dental caries is a prevalent long-lasting condition that impacts people worldwide, causing harm to their oral health and overall well-being.1 The erosion of tooth enamel and the development of cavities are key indicators of dental caries, which can manifest as adult caries (AC) in adults and early childhood caries (ECC) in young children.2 According to Colak et al.,3 multiple etiologies and risk factors have an impact on both stages, with ECC largely affecting the primary dentition and AC primarily targeting permanent teeth. Some of the associated risk factors that lead to early adult caries (EAC) include sociodemographic, dietary (breastfeeding/bottle feeding), oral hygiene factors, and factors related to oral bacterial flora, etc.1 Thus, it is imperative to determine the pathophysiology of dental caries and develop preventive measures that require knowledge and understanding of the role of immune status facilitating the initiation and progression of caries. According to Babu et al.,4 immune responses are important in controlling host–microbial interactions in the oral cavity, which affects the beginning and development of caries.4 Salivary components, cytokines, and immune cells influence caries susceptibility by supporting local defense mechanisms against cariogenic bacteria.5 Immunological variables interact in a complicated way to influence the start and progression of dental caries in both adults and children. By inhibiting the adherence of cariogenic bacteria to hard surfaces, salivary secretory immunoglobulin A (s-IgA) plays a critical role in controlling caries activity. To further limit the colonization of cariogenic bacteria, s-IgA also neutralizes viruses and toxins, suppresses enzyme activity, and removes antigens from saliva. An immunological response is triggered by exposure to cariogenic microbiota, which causes immune components to be secreted against different microorganism epitopes. Certain theories contend that binding lowers salivary levels of free s-IgA, a sign of dental caries activity, while other theories contend that binding triggers an immunological response, raising salivary levels of free s-IgA. Moreover, cytokines that affect immune responses and encourage the development of immunological reactions that are essential for the shift from innate to adaptive immunity, such as tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNF-α) and interleukin-6 (IL-6), are linked to inflammation and tissue damage. By recruiting and activating immune cells, IL-8 contributes to the pathophysiology of dental caries by playing a major role in inflammation, chemotaxis, and neutrophil activation. These immunological components and processes are involved in the development and advancement of dental caries, underscoring the complex interplay between oral health and the immune system.6–8 Studies on the connection between immune status and caries experience have shown associations between immunological variables and susceptibility to caries. There are various studies that have investigated the levels of salivary Ig, cytokine profiles, and polymorphisms in the human leukocyte antigen (HLA) gene with respect to ECC and AC patients.9 There are also studies that have investigated how immunological markers are affected systemically by dental caries, and they have found that there may be two ways that oral health and systemic immunity interact.10 Investigating whether immunological competence can stop the development of ECC and AC is an interesting part of caries research. Research indicates that those with strong immune systems may have better defenses against cariogenic microorganisms, which may lessen their susceptibility to dental caries.11 Moreover, immunological therapies that aim to improve or modulate immunity may be promising for managing and preventing dental caries.12 The objective of this systematic review was to examine whether immunocompetence can reduce the risk of caries by analyzing the function of immunity and immunological state in the development of ECC and AC. This review aimed to clarify the intricate relationship between host immunity and caries etiology by combining data from immunological investigations. This would help to acknowledge the preventive measures and therapeutic approaches for the management of dental caries.

Search Strategy

The Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) standards were followed while developing a systematic search strategy (Fig. 1). The goal of this approach was to make sure that all pertinent material was thoroughly covered by several databases. The databases that were searched were Google Scholar, Web of Science, PubMed, Cochrane Library, and Scopus all of which are well-known sources of scientific literature (Table 1). A large collection of pertinent keywords and phrases was found to maximize the search's efficacy. The following keywords, which include “immunity,” “immune status,” “immunoglobulin,” “cytokine,” “T cells,” “B cells,” “natural killer cells,” “early childhood caries,” and “adult caries,” “interleukin-6,” “immunological markers,” were carefully chosen to cover several facets of the research topic. After the keywords were selected, logical search queries were created by combining them using Boolean operators. Using OR to combine synonymous terms expands the search to include various expressions of the same concept. Using AND to combine different concepts ensures that the search results are relevant to the research question by including all required aspects. This method maximized the retrieval of pertinent research by making it easier to refine search queries and include synonyms or related terms. To maximize search results, the search strategy was customized to the unique syntax and features of each database. The databases' filters and advanced search features were used to focus search results according to publication date, study type, and other pertinent factors. The goal of the systematic search approach was to find all pertinent studies that discussed the relationship between immunity and immunological state and the development of both adult and ECC. Transparency and repeatability in the literature retrieval process were guaranteed by this method.

Data Extraction and Quality Assessment

A total of 2,723 articles were searched, and after considering all the inclusion and exclusion criteria pertaining to the main aim and research question of the review, only 10 articles were included in the present systematic review for further analysis. Two separate reviewers, UM and VPV, carefully completed the search using a standardized form to guarantee correctness and consistency. The data that were extracted covered a wide range of topics, such as the study's design, participant demographics, interventions, results, and important discoveries about the development of caries and immunological state. This all-encompassing strategy made it easier to analyze the included studies in detail. For observational research, the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale was used, and for randomized controlled trials, the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool was used. Using these strong assessment instruments, the reviewers assessed each study's methodological rigor and any biases, guaranteeing the validity and reliability of the combined data.

Data Synthesis

Analysis of the data on immunological status/immunity and the development of caries revealed trends or patterns in the research. If necessary, and contingent on the homogeneity of the included studies, a meta-analysis was carried out.

Risk of Bias Assessment

The reviewers independently evaluated the risk of bias within the included studies using instruments that have been proven to work. A variety of potential sources of bias were assessed, including bias in reporting, detection, attrition, performance, and selection. The reviewers discussed and came to a consensus on any differences in risk assessment.

Discussion

Dental caries arises from the intricate interaction of multiple variables, including microbial activity and immune responses. Several immunological indicators have been associated with the initiation and progression of the disease. Numerous investigations have explored the association between immunological markers and dental caries, providing imperative insights into possible methods for diagnosis and treatment. A study by Haeri-Araghi et al.9 examined the relationship between adult salivary IgA levels and dental caries. This study showed that salivary IgA levels and the number of carious teeth were positively interrelated, representing that IgA is a useful marker to exhibit the immune response to dental caries. This is consistent with findings from Alanazi et al.,18 who showed that patients with dental caries had increased concentrations of proinflammatory cytokines, including interleukins IL-36, IL-37, and IL-38. These results emphasize the role of proinflammatory cytokines in the inflammatory cascade that is associated with dental caries. The relationship between immunological activity and microbiological profiles in dental caries was investigated by Gómez-García et al.19 Their research revealed distinct bacterial communities and elevated proinflammatory cytokine expression in dental caries patients' pulp tissue, pointing to a possible involvement of dysbiosis in inflammation and immune activation.

Other researchers have provided important understandings of the immune system's role in tooth decay. An example is the thorough evaluation by Swetha et al.5 on the immunology of dental caries, which covers the different immune responses related to the development and advancement of caries. Colak et al.3 talked about the causes and treatment options for ECC, highlighting the significance of recognizing immunological processes when creating successful treatments. Astuti et al.26 examined immune responses that are naturally present in ECC and severe early childhood caries (S-ECC), revealing possible indicators for the seriousness of the condition. The significance of timely identification and treatment in preventing serious dental cavities was underscored, backed by Menon et al.'s13 research on salivary IL-6 levels in children with ECC posttreatment, emphasizing the importance of immunological markers in tracking treatment progress.

In general, these research studies all help to increase our knowledge of the immune system's role in tooth decay and offer new ideas for diagnosing and treating the condition. By using immune markers and addressing imbalances in the oral microbiota, healthcare providers may be able to improve the early detection and treatment of dental cavities, leading to better overall oral health results. Additional investigation is necessary to confirm these results and successfully implement them in clinical settings. The evaluation of the cross-sectional studies' quality, as assessed by the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale, shows scores varying from good to very good, demonstrating the rigorous methodology and reliability of the studies included. Ribeiro et al.14 and Haeri-Araghi et al.9 illustrated superior standards, highlighting the strength of their methodologies. The results align with a prior study conducted by Lo Giudice et al.16 that also received a high-quality rating, indicating a uniform level of methodological rigor among studies. The evaluation of bias with ROBINS-E focuses on the risk of bias in various areas, such as confounding, selection bias, and outcome measurement. The current research study shows low-to-moderate bias risk, suggesting a relatively low chance of bias in different areas. These results align with prior studies conducted by Luthfi et al.17 and Gómez-García et al.,19 both showing minimal bias, highlighting the credibility of the study results.

When comparing these findings to prior research, rigorous methods are crucial for ensuring research results are valid and reliable. For example, Bijani et al.11 assessed the salivary sHLA-G levels in children with dental caries and achieved a high-quality rating, highlighting the significance of strong methods in achieving precise outcomes. In the same way, Malas et al.12 examined how primary teeth cavities are linked to the HLA class II gene in Syrian children, emphasizing the importance of thorough quality evaluation to validate research results. The research greatly improves the knowledge of the processes involved in dental cavities and their impact on patient care. By examining the correlation between immune system indicators and tooth decay, it uncovers possible biomarkers for prompt identification and tailored intervention plans. This study highlights how crucial it is for immunology and dentistry to collaborate to advance preventive dental care methods. Future research could investigate when immunological markers appear during the onset of cavities and evaluate how effective immunomodulatory treatments are. However, research into the impact of genetics and microbiome on immune reactions is crucial for developing specific treatment approaches. In general, the results of this study have important consequences for dental prevention and emphasize the necessity of additional research in this area.

Advantages and Limitations

Although the current research provides important findings on the connection between immune status, immunological markers, and dental caries, it is crucial to recognize various limitations. The cross-sectional design limits the ability to determine causality, highlighting the importance of conducting long-term studies to clarify temporal relationships. The findings are limited in their generalizability due to the small sample size and the focus on specific age groups. Moreover, dependence on self-reported data may lead to potential bias. Despite these constraints, the research provides substantial advantages by improving our knowledge of possible biomarkers for evaluating risk and detecting dental caries early on. The thorough approach and broad range of participants improve the accuracy and relevance of the findings. Moreover, the interdisciplinary method highlights the significance of working together in research to tackle intricate oral health problems.

Conclusion

The results of the present systematic review provide important information about the association between immune status of patients, immunological markers, and dental caries. Future studies should use longer term, more varied sample sizes to solve these limitations. The current work highlights a possible link between the immune status of a patient and the development of caries. Thus, this work has got great relevance to pediatric dentists. This study also suggests the potential clinical utility of immunological markers in the diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment of dental caries in both pediatric and adult patients, emphasizing the value of multidisciplinary cooperation and creative research strategies in expanding the knowledge of oral diseases and enhancing patient outcomes among the clinical practitioners and researchers. Thus, a multidisciplinary approach is needed to unravel the pathogenesis of oral diseases.

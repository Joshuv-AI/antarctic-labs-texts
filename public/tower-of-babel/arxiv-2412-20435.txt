Vanishing of Brauer groups of moduli stacks of stable curves
Sebastian Bartling, Kazuhiro Ito

Abstract
. We show that the cohomological Brauer groups of the moduli stacks of stable genus g g curves over the integers and an algebraic closure of the rational numbers vanish for any g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 . For the n n marked version, we show the same vanishing result in the range ( g , n ) = ( 1 , n ) (g,n)=(1,n) with 1 ≤ n ≤ 6 1\leq n\leq 6 and all ( g , n ) (g,n) with g ≥ 4 . g\geq 4. We also discuss several finiteness results on cohomological Brauer groups of proper and smooth Deligne-Mumford stacks over the integers.



1.
Introduction

The moduli stack
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
of stable genus
g\geq 2
curves as introduced by Deligne-Mumford
[
DM69
]
has the beautiful property that it is a proper and smooth Deligne-Mumford stack over
\Spec(\mathbb Z)
.
When it comes to schemes, the few known examples that share this property are typically rational and therefore do not carry non-trivial Brauer classes.
It is then natural to ask whether
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
carries non-trivial Brauer classes defined over

\mathbb Z

or
an algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb Q}
of
\mathbb Q
.
The main result of this note claims that this is not the case; more precisely, we show the following:

Theorem 1.1

(see Corollary
3.7
and Section
4
)
.

The equalities

\Br(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,\mathbb Z})=\Br(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})=0

hold for all
g\geq 2
.
For integers
g,n\geq 0
such that
2g-2+n>0
, the equalities

\Br(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\mathbb Z})=\Br(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})=0

hold for
(g,n)=(1,n)
with
1\leq n\leq 6
and for all
(g,n)
with
g=0
or
g\geq 4.

Here
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
stands for the moduli stack of stable genus
g
curves with
n
marked points, which is also a proper and smooth Deligne-Mumford stack; see Section
1.3
.
In the following discussion, we also need
the moduli stack

\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

of smooth genus
g
curves with
n
marked points.

Remark 1.2
.

By our convention to denote by
\Br(-)
the
cohomological
Brauer group and since the Deligne-Mumford stacks
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\mathbb Z}
and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
are regular, our result means that

H^{2}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\mathbb Z},\mathbb{G}_{m})=H^{2}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb{G}_{m})=0

in the above range of
(g,n).

Remark 1.3
.

The equalities

\Br(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n,\mathbb Z})=\Br(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})=0

for all
n\geq 3
are well-known.
For this, we first note that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n,\mathbb Z}
is a scheme.
The desired equalities follow (c.f. the proof of
[
Poo17
, Lemma 6.9.8]
) from the fact that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n,\mathbb{Q}}

is a rational proper smooth scheme over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n,\mathbb Z}
has a
\mathbb Z
-section.

Remark 1.4
.

Assume that
g\geq 1
.
To the best of our knowledge, vanishing of
\Br(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
was known in the following cases of
(g,n)
:

(1,1):

Antieau-Meier
[
AM20
]
show the stronger statement
\Br(\mathcal{M}_{1,1,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})=0
.
(In Proposition
4.1
we give an independent argument.
We stress that this vanishing is an easy part of
[
AM20
]
.)

(1,2):

It follows from a result of Inchiostro
[
Inc22
, Theorem 1.2]
that
\Br(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,2,\overline{\mathbb Q}})=0
(see also Proposition
4.2
).

(2,0):

Here the result follows from
[
DLP21
]
; see Proposition
4.3
.

g\geq 4:

Here the result follows from
[
KS03
]
; see Theorem
4.15
.

Therefore, the new cases established in this paper are the case
(g,n)=(3,0)

and the case

(g,n)=(1,n)
with
3\leq n\leq 6
.

When it comes to vanishing of
\Br(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\mathbb Z})
the only known statement that we are aware of concerns the case
(g,n)=(1,1).
Namely, in the aforementioned article of Antieau-Meier
[
AM20
]
they show as their main result that

\Br(\mathcal{M}_{1,1,\mathbb Z})=0,

which implies
\Br(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,1,\mathbb Z})=0
(yet again, our argument for
\Br(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,1,\mathbb Z})=0
is independent of their work).

Remark 1.5
.

Since we have

\Br(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})=\bigoplus_{\ell}H^{3}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb Z_{\ell}(1))_{\mathrm{tor}}

(see Lemma
2.7
),
our result shows that
H^{3}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb Z_{\ell}(1))_{\mathrm{tor}}=0
for any prime
\ell
in the range of
(g,n)
stated in Theorem
1.1
.
In fact, together with an extension of a classical result due to Abrashkin and Fontaine (
[
Abr90
]
,
[
Fon93
]
) to Deligne-Mumford stacks that are proper and smooth over
\Spec(\mathbb Z)
(see Theorem
2.8
),
this implies that
H^{3}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb Z_{\ell}(1))=0
for any prime
\ell
.
It would be interesting to investigate whether our main result also extends to the missing cases
(namely
(1,n)
with
n\geq 7
and
(2,n),(3,n)
with
n\geq 1
), but we currently do not know how to approach these cases.
Let us also mention here that it is however not true in general that

H^{3}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})=0

(even for
g\geq 4
).
This follows from
[
EG11
, Theorem 1.2]
, but we omit the details here.

We are not aware of an example of a proper and smooth
scheme

X
over
\Spec(\mathbb Z)
with non-trivial
\Br(X)
or
\Br(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
and, as the result of Abrashkin and Fontaine (
[
Abr90
]
,
[
Fon93
]
) indicates,
it seems that there are not “many” examples of proper and smooth schemes over
\Spec(\mathbb Z)
.
However,
we have a lot of interesting examples of proper and smooth Deligne-Mumford stacks over
\Spec(\mathbb Z)
, such as
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\mathbb Z}
.
We remark that there are quite easy examples of Deligne-Mumford stacks
X
that are proper and smooth over
\Spec(\mathbb Z)
, such that

\Br(X)
does not vanish
respectively
such that
\Br(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
does not vanish (see Examples
3.12
and
3.13
) - in other words, the vanishing patterns proven in this article are not general features of proper and smooth Deligne-Mumford stacks over
\Spec(\mathbb Z)
; they might be specific to the stack
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
(and maybe to schemes).

Remark 1.6
.

Along the way, we will prove that for Deligne-Mumford stacks
X
that are proper and smooth over
\Spec(\mathbb Z)
, the Brauer group
\Br(X)
is finite; see Theorem
3.3
.
This may fit into the following conjecture of Artin (
[
Gro68
, Remarque 2.5 (c)]
): the Brauer group of every proper scheme over
\Spec(\mathbb Z)
is finite.
However, the conjecture of Artin is widely open and is in general much more difficult than the case we are considering here.

1.1.
Outline of the proof

Our argument for proving Theorem
1.1
proceeds by establishing vanishing of the Brauer group of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
and then deducing vanishing over the integers; see Section
3.2
for this reduction step, which may be interesting in its own right.

As explained in Remark
1.4
(and Remark
1.3
), our results over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
are new for

(g,n)=(3,0)

and for

(g,n)=(1,n)
with
3\leq n\leq 6
.
Here we outline the proofs for these new cases (but we also give independent arguments for some previous results in the main body of the paper).

Our argument for the case
(g,n)=(3,0)
goes as follows:
By the work of Di Lorenzo-Pirisi
[
LP24
]
, one knows that for any field
k
with
p=\text{char}(k)\neq 2,

\Br(\mathcal{M}_{3,k})=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\oplus\Br(k)\oplus B_{p},

where
B_{p}=0
if
\text{char}(k)=0
and a
p
-primary torsion group else.
We first reduce the problem of showing that
\Br(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})=0
to showing that
\Br(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}})[2]=0
for some prime
p\neq 2.

Our task is therefore to show that the generator
\gamma
of the factor
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z
in
\Br(\mathcal{M}_{3,\mathbb{F}_{q}})[2]
does not extend to the compactification for a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of characteristic
p
.
Using that
\Br(\mathbb{F}_{q}[[t]])=0,
it is easy to see that it suffices for this to produce a stable curve of genus three
\mathscr{C}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[[t]],
such that the generic fiber
C=\mathscr{C}_{\eta}
is a smooth genus three curve over
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}((t))
, which has the property that
\gamma(C)\in\Br(F)[2]
is non-trivial.
To achieve this, we use the beautiful relation found by Di Lorenzo-Pirisi between the factor
\gamma
and the étale algebra of 27 lines on smooth cubic surfaces
X
obtained by blowing up (general) six points in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and the result of Shioda
[
Shi95
]
on very explicit defining equations of smooth quartic curves which arise as the ramification locus of some projection
X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
The upshot is that for
p\geq 11
we are able to find such curves; see Section
4.3
for details.

For the cases
(1,n)
with
3\leq n\leq 6

(also for
n=1,2
), we give a uniform argument; see Proposition
4.2
.
Namely, we use recent work of Battistella-Di Lorenzo
[
BL24
]
(relying on work of Smyth
[
Smy19
]
and Lekili-Polishchuk
[
LP19
]
), who explain that in these cases the stack
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}
is connected to a stacky weighted projective space (resp. Grassmannian) by a diagram of smooth Deligne-Mumford stacks along weighted (and ordinary) blow-ups.
Vanishing of the Brauer group of a stacky weighted projective space over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
follows from the work of Shin
[
Shi23
]
and we deduce the desired result by using purity of the Brauer group (see Proposition
2.2
) as established by Česnavičius
[
Čes19
]
.

1.2.
Structure of the article

In Section
2
we collect some results which are extensions of well-known results in the context of schemes to the setting of Deligne-Mumford stacks, which will be used later. In Section
3
we first show finiteness of the Brauer group of a proper and smooth Deligne-Mumford stack
X
over
\Spec(\mathbb Z)
, see Theorem
3.3
, then we explain how in this setting it is often possible
(in particular, possible for
X=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
) to reduce vanishing of
\Br(X)
to vanishing of
\Br(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
,
see Proposition
3.4
. We end that section with a discussion of some examples. Finally, in Section
4
we prove our vanishing results on
\Br(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
.

1.3.
Conventions, notations and recollections

All Deligne-Mumford (DM) stacks considered in this paper will be assumed to be quasi-separated.
If
X
is a scheme or DM stack, we denote by

\Br(X)=H^{2}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})_{\tors}

the cohomological Brauer group.
Here, for an abelian group
M
, we let
M_{\tors}
denote its torsion part.
Recall that
\Br(X)=H^{2}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})
if
X
is noetherian and regular (see
[
AM20
, Proposition 2.5]
).
Recall here that a DM stack
X
is called regular if there exists an étale presentation of
X
by a regular scheme (then all presentations will be by regular schemes).
We use the notion of the codimension of a closed substack of a noetherian DM stack as in
[
Oss13
, Definition 6.1]
.

For integers
g,n\geq 0
such that
2g-2+n>0
,
we denote by
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
the moduli stack of smooth genus
g
curves with
n
marked points, by
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
the moduli stack of stable genus
g
curves with
n
marked points; see
[
Knu83
, Section 1]
for example for the basic definition.
If
n=0
(so that
g\geq 2
), then we write

\mathcal{M}_{g}:=\mathcal{M}_{g,0}

and

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}:=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,0}
.
It is well-known that

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

is a proper and smooth DM stack over
\Spec(\mathbb Z)
with geometric connected fibers
and
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is open and dense.
See
[
DM69
]
and
[
Knu83
]
.
For a ring
R
, we denote by

\mathcal{M}_{g,n,R}

and

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,R}

the base changes to
\Spec(R)
.
We implicitly always assume that
2g-2+n>0
when considering these moduli stacks.

2.
Preparations

We start by collecting some statements we need for our arguments later.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
k
be a field,
X
a smooth DM stack over
\Spec(k),

Z\hookrightarrow X
a closed DM substack, which is also smooth over
\Spec(k).

Consider the blow-up
\Bl_{Z}(X)
of
X
along
Z
(see e.g.
[
QD22
, Example 3.2.6]
for a definition).
Then
\Bl_{Z}(X)
is smooth over
\Spec(k).

Proof.

Let
U\rightarrow X
be an étale presentation of
X
by a scheme
U.
Since blowing up commutes with flat localization on the base (
[
QD22
, Corollary 3.2.14 (iii)]
), we obtain the étale presentation

\Bl_{Z_{U}}(U)\rightarrow\Bl_{Z}(X)

and it suffices by the definition of a smooth morphism of algebraic stacks (
[
Sta22
, Tag 075U]
) to show that
\Bl_{Z_{U}}(U)
is a smooth scheme over
\Spec(k).

But then
Z_{U}
is Zariski locally cut out in
U
by a regular sequence and the result is classical.
∎

We next deduce the following purity statement for the Brauer group of a noetherian and regular DM stack from results of Česnavičius
[
Čes19
]
; this is stronger than what we will later need but we wanted to record it here.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a noetherian and regular DM stack and
Z\hookrightarrow X
a closed substack such that
\codim_{X}(Z)\geq 2,
then the natural restriction map

H^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})\rightarrow H^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X-Z,\mathbb{G}_{m})

is an isomorphism for
i\leq 2
and is injective for
i=3
.

Proof.

As explained in the proof of
[
Čes19
, Theorem 6.1]
, we can work étale locally and reduce the desired statement to the case where
X
is a scheme (or more precisely, the spectrum of a strictly henselian regular local ring), and thus this proposition follows from the result proved there.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
be a morphism of noetherian and regular DM stacks which is an isomorphism on a dense open substack
U\subset X.

(1)

The homomorphism
\pi^{*}\colon\Br(X)\rightarrow\Br(\widetilde{X})
is injective.

(2)

If the complement
Z
of
U
in
X
satisfies
\codim_{X}(Z)\geq 2,
then if
\Br(X)=0,
also
\Br(\widetilde{X})=0.

Proof.

The first part follows because both
\Br(X)
and
\Br(\widetilde{X})
embed into
\Br(U)
(
[
AM20
, Proposition 2.5 (iv)]
). For the second part, purity of the Brauer group (Proposition
2.2
) implies that
\Br(U)=0,
so that by
[
AM20
, Proposition 2.5 (iv)]
again, we see that
\Br(\widetilde{X})=0,
as desired.
∎

Next we want to record some extensions to the setting of DM stacks of statements in étale cohomology which are classical for schemes; these results are most probably well-known to the experts.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X\rightarrow\Spec(\mathbb Z)
be a proper and smooth DM stack.
The
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
-representation

H^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb{Q}_{p})

is unramified at
\ell\neq p
and crystalline at
p.

Proof.

The claim about unramifiedness at
\ell\neq p
follows from
[
vdBE05
, Proposition 3.1]
and about being crystalline at
p
from
[
KP21
, Theorem 4.3.25, Remark 4.1.15]
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
k
be a finite field or an algebraically closed field.
Let
\ell
be a prime invertible in
k
.
Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a morphism of separated DM stacks over
\Spec(k)
.
We assume that
X
and
Y
are smooth pure of dimension
d
over
\Spec(k)
.
We further assume that
f
is representable by schemes and proper, and that
f
is finite étale of constant degree
m
over a dense open substack of
X
.
We set
\Lambda:=\mathbb Z/\ell^{n}\mathbb Z
, and let
\Lambda_{X}
and
\Lambda_{Y}
denote the corresponding constant sheaves on
X
and
Y
, respectively.

(1)

The dualizing complexes of
X
and
Y
(in the sense of
[
LO08
]
) are isomorphic to

\Lambda_{X}(d)[2d]
and

\Lambda_{Y}(d)[2d]
, respectively.
We have a canonical trace morphism

\mathrm{tr}_{f}\colon Rf_{*}\Lambda_{Y}(d)[2d]\to\Lambda_{X}(d)[2d].

(2)

The composition

\Phi\colon\Lambda_{X}(d)\to Rf_{*}\Lambda_{Y}(d)\overset{\mathrm{tr}_{f}[-2d]}{\longrightarrow}\Lambda_{X}(d)

is equal to the multiplication by
m
.

Proof.

(1) The first assertion follows from
[
LO08
, Corollary 4.6.2]
.
We recall that the functors
Rf_{!}
and
f^{!}
are defined in
[
LO08
, Definition 4.4.1]
, which satisfy the usual adjunction property; see
[
LO08
, Proposition 4.4.2]
.
We have
f^{!}\Lambda_{X}(d)[2d]=\Lambda_{Y}(d)[2d]
(see
[
LO08
, (4.4.i)]
).
By
[
LO08
, Proposition 5.2.1]
, we have

Rf_{!}=Rf_{*}.

Then
\mathrm{tr}_{f}
is defined as the following composition

Rf_{*}\Lambda_{Y}(d)[2d]=Rf_{!}\Lambda_{Y}(d)[2d]=Rf_{!}f^{!}\Lambda_{X}(d)[2d]\to\Lambda_{X}(d)[2d]

where the last morphism is induced by adjunction.

(2) We may work étale locally on
X
.
We may thus assume that
X
is a scheme and
k
is algebraically closed.
Then
Y
is also a scheme.
It suffices to show that
the induced homomorphism

H^{0}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda(d))\overset{\Phi}{\to}H^{0}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda(d))

is equal to the multiplication by
m
.
Let
U\subset X
be an open dense subscheme such that
f
is finite étale over
U
.
Since we have

H^{0}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda(d))=H^{0}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(U,\Lambda(d))
,
the assertion now follows from the corresponding statement for the trace morphisms associated with finite étale morphisms of schemes.
∎

Lemma
2.5
enables us to deduce the Riemann Hypothesis part of Weil conjectures for DM stacks.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let

X
be a proper and smooth DM stack over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
The eigenvalues of the geometric Frobenius
\varphi_{q}\in\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q})
acting on

H^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})

are algebraic integers with complex absolute value
q^{i/2}
.

Proof.

We may assume that
X
is connected.
Since
X
is smooth, it follows that
X
is irreducible.
By
[
LMB00
, Théorème 16.6]
, there exists a finite, surjective, generically étale morphism

Z\to X
with
Z
a scheme.
After replacing
Z
by an irreducible component of it (with the reduced induced structure) which dominates
X
, we may assume that
Z
is integral.
Since
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is perfect,
by
[
dJ96
, Theorem 4.1]
, there exists a proper and generically finite étale morphism

Y\to Z

such that
Y
is a connected proper smooth scheme over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
The composition

f\colon Y\to X
satisfies the conditions in Lemma
2.5
.
Thus, the trace morphism induces a homomorphism

H^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(Y_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},\mathbb Q_{\ell})\to H^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},\mathbb Q_{\ell})

such that the composition

H^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},\mathbb Q_{\ell})\overset{f^{*}}{\to}H^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(Y_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},\mathbb Q_{\ell})\to H^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},\mathbb Q_{\ell})

is equal to the multiplication by some positive integer, and hence is an isomorphism.
It follows that

H^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},\mathbb Q_{\ell})

is isomorphic to a direct summand of

H^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(Y_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},\mathbb Q_{\ell})

as a representation of
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
Therefore the desired assertion follows from the Weil conjecture for schemes proved by Deligne
[
Del73
,
Del80
]
.
∎

We also record the following statement which will facilitate the passage between statements concerning the Brauer group over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\mathbb{C}.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
K
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
such that
K\subset\mathbb{C}.

Let
X\rightarrow\Spec(K)
be a connected proper smooth DM stack.
There is an exact sequence

0\to\Br(X)^{0}\to\Br(X)\to H^{3}(X_{\mathbb{C}}^{\an},\mathbb Z)_{\tors}\to 0,

where
\Br(X)^{0}\simeq(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb Z)^{b_{2}-\rho}
with
b_{2}(X)=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}H^{2}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
and
\rho(X)
is the rank of the Néron-Severi group
\NS(X)
.
Here
X_{\mathbb{C}}^{\an}
is the complex analytification of
X_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof.

For schemes this is
[
CTS21
, Proposition 4.2.6 (ii), (iii)]
.
Let us quickly explain how to adapt this to the setting here.

By
[
Bro09
, Théorème 4.3.1]
,
the identity component

\underline{\Pic}^{\circ}_{X/K}
is a connected proper group scheme over
K
, so that
\underline{\Pic}^{\circ}_{X/K,\mathrm{red}}
is an abelian variety over
K
.
(In fact, since
K
is characteristic
0
, we have
\underline{\Pic}^{\circ}_{X/K}=\underline{\Pic}^{\circ}_{X/K,\mathrm{red}}
by Cartier’s theorem, but we do not need this fact.)
The Kummer exact sequence together with divisibility of
\underline{\Pic}^{\circ}_{X/K,\mathrm{red}}(K)
gives

0\to\NS(X)/\ell^{n}\to H^{2}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mu_{\ell^{n}})\to\Br(X)[\ell^{n}]\to 0,

for any prime
\ell
.
This is an exact sequence of finite abelian groups (
[
Ols07
, Theorem 9.10]
), so that passing to the limit we get an exact sequence

0\to\NS(X)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z_{\ell}\to H^{2}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb Z_{\ell}(1))\to T_{\ell}(\Br(X))\to 0.

As
T_{\ell}(\Br(X))=\Hom(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}/\mathbb Z_{\ell},\Br(X))
this is a finite free
\mathbb Z_{\ell}
-module, so it follows that
T_{\ell}(\Br(X))=\mathbb Z_{\ell}^{b_{2}-\rho}.

Looking now at the Kummer exact sequence for
H^{3}
and using that
\Br(X)=H^{2}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})
is torsion (
[
AM20
, Proposition 2.5 (iii)]
) and running over all
\ell
, we obtain an exact sequence

0\to(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb Z)^{b_{2}-\rho}\to\Br(X)\to\bigoplus_{\ell}H^{3}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb Z_{\ell}(1))_{\tors}\to 0.

Using independence of choice of algebraically closed base field (use
[
Sta22
, Tag 07BV]
and smooth base change
[
Zhe15
, Proposition 2.12]
) and étale-Betti comparison (see e.g.
[
KP21
, Proposition 4.1.6]
), we obtain that

\bigoplus_{\ell}H^{3}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb Z_{\ell}(1))_{\tors}\simeq H^{3}(X_{\mathbb{C}}^{\an},\mathbb Z)_{\tors}.

∎

The next result is in the setting of schemes a classical result due to Abrashkin and Fontaine (
[
Abr90
]
,
[
Fon93
]
).

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
X\rightarrow\Spec(\mathbb Z)
be a proper and smooth DM stack. Then

H^{i}(X_{\mathbb{C}},\Omega^{j}_{X_{\mathbb{C}}})=0

for all
i+j\leq 3,

i\neq j.

Proof.

The argument here just uses extensions of
p
-adic Hodge theory to stacks and then copy-pastes the argument of Abrashkin and Fontaine.
More precisely, let
V:=H^{N}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb{Q}_{p}).

Assume that there is a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})]
-filtration

0=V_{N+1}\subset V_{N}\subset\cdots\subset V_{1}\subset V_{0}=V

such that for all
0\leq i\leq N,

we have
V_{i}/V_{i+1}\simeq\mathbb{Q}_{p}(i)^{s_{i}}
for some
s_{i}\geq 0
.
Then, by using the de Rham comparison for DM stacks
[
Bog08
]
(this can also be deduced from
[
KP21
, Theorem 4.3.25]
, c.f.
loc.cit.
, beginning of page 9) and Proposition
2.6
,
we can repeat the arguments in
[
Fon93
, page 4]
and
[
Abr14
, Proposition 5.3]
to conclude that

H^{i}(X_{\mathbb{C}},\Omega^{j}_{X_{\mathbb{C}}/\mathbb{C}})=0

for
i+j\leq N,

i\neq j.

It suffices therefore to find such a filtration. For this, we can just cite Fontaine
[
Fon93
, Proposition 1]
.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

Theorem
2.8
implies that

H^{1}(X_{\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Q})=H^{3}(X_{\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Q})=0

and

c_{1}\colon\Pic(X_{\mathbb{C}})\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb{Q}\simeq H^{2}(X_{\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Q}).

When applied to
X=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
this gives a
p
-adic Hodge theory approach to (some of) the classical vanishing results in
[
AC98
]
(with the exception of the degree
5
cohomology).

3.
General results on Brauer groups of proper and smooth Deligne-Mumford stacks over the integers

3.1.
Finiteness of the Brauer group

In this subsection, we shall prove that
the Brauer groups

\Br(X)

and

\Br(X_{\overline{\mathbb Q}})

are finite for a proper and smooth DM stack
X
over
\Spec(\mathbb Z)
; see Theorem
3.3
.

For this, we will need to use the relative Picard functor.
Let
S
be a noetherian scheme and
let

f\colon X\rightarrow S

be a proper DM stack over
S
.
We assume that
f
is flat and
\mathcal{O}_{S}\simeq f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
holds universally.
We denote by
\underline{\Pic}_{X/S}
the relative Picard functor, i.e. the fppf sheafification of the presheaf that sends a (quasi-separated) scheme
T
over
S
to

\Pic(X\times_{S}T)/\Image(\Pic(T)\rightarrow\Pic(X\times_{S}T)).

Then this is representable by a commutative group algebraic space locally of finite type over
S
.
Moreover
\underline{\Pic}_{X/S}
commutes with base change in
S
.
We note that by
[
Bro09
, Theorem 2.2.5]
we could also work with the étale sheafification.
See
[
FV23
, Section 3]
,
[
Bro09
]
,
[
Bro12
]
for background that is needed in our work.

Remark 3.1
.

In fact, we will mostly consider morphisms
f\colon X\rightarrow\Spec(\mathbb Z)
which will be proper and smooth.
For such morphisms,

f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is locally free on
\Spec(\mathbb Z)
and its formation commutes with base change along any morphism
T\to\Spec(\mathbb Z)
.
Indeed, it is well-known that the function sending a point
s\in\Spec(\mathbb Z)
to the number of connected components of the geometric fiber of
X
at
s
is constant (c.f.
[
DM69
, Theorem 4.17]
).
Then, the assertion follows by the same argument as in the proof of
[
Mum85
, Chapter II, Section 5, Corollary 2]
: More precisely, we choose an étale surjection
U\to X

from an affine scheme
U
.
Let

0\to C^{0}\to C^{1}\to C^{2}\to\cdots

be the Čech complex associated to
\mathcal{O}_{X}
and the Čech nerve of
U\to X
, so that all entries
C^{i}
are torsion free
\mathbb Z
-modules.
(We note that, since the diagonal

X\to X\times_{\Spec(\mathbb Z)}X
is a finite morphism,
the Čech nerve of
U\to X
consists of affine schemes.
However, this complex may not be bounded above in general.)
Now let us look at the bounded complex
D^{\bullet}
given by

0\to C^{0}\to C^{1}\to\Ker(C^{2}\rightarrow C^{3})\to 0.

Then, for any ring
R,
we have that

H^{0}(X_{R},\mathcal{O}_{X_{R}})=H^{0}(D^{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}R).

Since
\Ker(C^{2}\rightarrow C^{3})
is torsion free, it is flat over
\mathbb Z
.
Since
H^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
are finitely generated
\mathbb Z
-modules,
we can use
[
Mum85
, Chapter II, Section 5, Lemma 1]

to obtain a bounded complex of finite free
\mathbb Z
-modules
K^{\bullet}
with a quasi-isomorphism
of complexes
K^{\bullet}\rightarrow D^{\bullet}.

We can then follow the argument in
[
Mum85
, Chapter II, Section 5, Corollary 2]
, to deduce the desired claim.

Let us record the following consequence of Remark
3.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For a proper and smooth DM stack
X\to\Spec(\mathbb Z)
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

The geometric fibers of
X
are connected.

(2)

We have
\mathcal{O}_{\Spec(\mathbb Z)}\simeq f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
universally.

(3)

X
is connected.

Proof.

(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
follows immediately from Remark
3.1
.

(1)\Rightarrow(3)
is easy.
It remains to show that (3) implies (2).
By Remark
3.1
, it suffices to show that
\mathbb Z=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
.
It is easy to see that

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})

is finite and flat over
\mathbb Z
.
Then, Remark
3.1
implies that
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
is finite étale over
\mathbb Z
.
Therefore, it follows from Minkowski’s theorem that
\mathbb Z=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
when
X
is connected.
∎

Let us now explain the following result:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow\Spec(\mathbb Z)
be a proper and smooth
DM stack.
Then

\Br(X)

and

\Br(X_{\overline{\mathbb Q}})

are finite.

Proof.

Since we do not need this result in the following, we will only sketch the proof.
First observe that

b_{2}(X_{\overline{\mathbb Q}})=\rho(X_{\overline{\mathbb Q}})
by Theorem
2.8
.
Thus Lemma
2.7
implies that

\Br(X_{\overline{\mathbb Q}})

is finite.
For the finiteness of

\Br(X)
,
we may assume that
X
is connected.
Then, by Lemma
3.2
, we have
\mathcal{O}_{\Spec(\mathbb Z)}\simeq f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
universally, so that we can use the relative Picard space
\underline{\Pic}_{X/\mathbb Z}
in the following.

Since
\Br(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
is finite,
it follows that
\Br(X_{\overline{\mathbb Z}})
is also finite.
We therefore have to see that

K_{\mathbb Z}=\Ker(\Br(X)\rightarrow\Br(X_{\overline{\mathbb Z}}))

is finite.
It follows from Theorem
2.8
that
there exists a dense open subset
U
of
\Spec(\mathbb Z)
such that for all

s\in U,

H^{i}(X_{s},\mathcal{O}_{X_{s}})=0

for
i=1,2,3
(where
X_{s}
is the fiber of
X
at
s
).
By
[
FV23
, Proposition 3.2, Theorem 3.6]
,
the relative Picard space

\underline{\Pic}_{X_{U}/U}

is étale over
U
.
We have an exact sequence of abelian sheaves
for the fppf topology on
U
:

0\to\underline{\Pic}^{\tau}_{X_{U}/U}\to\underline{\Pic}_{X_{U}/U}\to\text{Num}_{X_{U}/U}\to 0.

By (
[
FV23
, Theorem 3.6 (iv) (b)]
)
\underline{\Pic}^{\tau}_{X_{U}/U}
is proper over
U
, and since it is open
in
\underline{\Pic}_{X_{U}/U}

[
FV23
, Theorem 3.6 (i)]
, it is finite étale.
It follows from
[
Mil86
, Theorem 3.1]
that

H^{i}_{\text{fl}}(U,\underline{\Pic}^{\tau}_{X_{U}/U})
is finite.
Furthermore,
\text{Num}_{X_{U}/U}
is a locally constant étale sheaf of finite free
\mathbb Z
-modules (c.f. the proof of Lemma
3.10
).
It is then not too hard to see that
H^{1}_{\text{fl}}(U,\text{Num}_{X_{U}/U})
is finite.
We deduce then that also
H^{1}_{\text{fl}}(U,\underline{\Pic}_{X_{U}/U})
is finite.
Now we look at the 7 term exact sequence (see e.g.
[
Mil80
, Appendix B, page 309]
) for the Leray spectral sequence in the fppf topology for
f_{U}\colon X_{U}\rightarrow U
and the sheaf
\mathbb{G}_{m}
and see that

\Ker(\Br(X_{U})\rightarrow\Br(X_{\overline{U}}))/\Image(\Br(U))

is finite. Here
\overline{U}=U\times_{\Spec(\mathbb Z)}\Spec(\overline{\mathbb Z}).
It then suffices to see that
K_{\mathbb Z}\cap\Image(\Br(U))
(intersection in
\Br(X_{U})
) is finite. This is not too hard to see using that
X
acquires a
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-valued point, where
K/\mathbb{Q}
is some finite Galois extension, and that
\Br(\mathcal{O}_{K})
is finite (
[
Gro68
, Proposition 2.4]
).
∎

3.2.
Deducing vanishing over the integers

The main result of this section is the following:

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow\Spec(\mathbb Z)
be a proper and smooth DM stack with geometrically connected fibers.
Assume that either of the following conditions holds:

(1)

X(\mathbb Z)\neq\emptyset
and
X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
is simply connected.

(2)

\underline{\Pic}_{X/\mathbb Z}
is a constant sheaf associated with a finitely generated
\mathbb Z
-module.

Then the natural homomorphism

\Br(X)\to\Br(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})

is injective.

Before we start the proof of Proposition
3.4
, we need two lemmas.
Let us consider
f\colon X\rightarrow\Spec(\mathbb Z),
a proper and smooth DM stack with geometrically connected fibers.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that
X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
is simply connected.
Then we have

H^{1}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(\Spec(\mathbb{Q}),\underline{\Pic}_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}})=0.

Proof.

Since
H^{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}})=0
by Theorem
2.8
, we have that
\underline{\Pic}^{0}_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})=0
(use
[
Bro09
, Théorème 4.1.3]
), so that

\Pic(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})=\NS(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}).

Thus
\Pic(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
is a finitely generated abelian group by
[
Bro12
, Theorem 3.4.1]
.
Since
X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
is simply connected, we see by the Kummer exact sequence that
\Pic(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
is torsion free.
Now observe that
\Pic(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
is an everywhere unramified representation of
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}).

Namely, let
\ell
be a prime and we want to show that
\Pic(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
is unramified at
\ell.

Let
p\neq\ell
be an auxiliary prime.
By the Kummer exact sequence, we have a
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
-equivariant embedding

\Pic(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb{Q}_{p}\hookrightarrow H^{2}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb{Q}_{p}(1)).

Since we know that
\Pic(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
is torsion free,
Lemma
2.4
implies that it is unramified at
\ell
, as desired.

The action of
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
on
\Pic(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
is then trivial by Minkowski’s theorem.
In total, it follows that

H^{1}_{\Gal}(\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}),\Pic(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}))=\Hom_{\text{cts}}(\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}),\Pic(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}))=0,

since
\Pic(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
is torsion free.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume that
X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
is simply connected.
Then

\Ker(\Br(X_{\mathbb{Q}})\rightarrow\Br(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}))=\Image(\Br(\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\Br(X_{\mathbb{Q}})).

Proof.

Since
R^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m}=\underline{\Pic}_{X/\mathbb Z}

by
[
Bro09
, Proposition 2.1.3]
,
we have

H^{1}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(\Spec(\mathbb{Q}),R^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m})=H^{1}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(\Spec(\mathbb{Q}),\underline{\Pic}_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}})=0

by Lemma
3.5
.
By the 7 term exact sequence for the Leray spectral sequence in étale cohomology for
f_{\mathbb{Q}}\colon X_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\Spec(\mathbb{Q})
and
\mathbb{G}_{m}
(see
[
Bro09
, Théorème A.2.8.]
),
we then have a surjection

\Br(\mathbb{Q})\to\Ker(\Br(X_{\mathbb{Q}})\rightarrow H^{0}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(\Spec(\mathbb{Q}),R^{2}f_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m})).

(Here we also use
\Br(Y)=H^{2}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\mathbb{G}_{m})
for a noetherian and regular DM stack
Y
.)
By the sheaf property of

R^{2}f_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m}
, we see that

H^{0}(\Spec(\mathbb{Q}),R^{2}f_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m})

is a subgroup of

\colim_{K/\mathbb{Q}}H^{0}(\Spec(K),R^{2}f_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m}),

where the colimit is indexed by finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
contained in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}.

This implies that

\Ker(\Br(X_{\mathbb{Q}})\rightarrow\Br(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}))\subset\Ker(\Br(X_{\mathbb{Q}})\rightarrow H^{0}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(\Spec(\mathbb{Q}),R^{2}f_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m})).

Now observe that

\Image(\Br(\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\Br(X_{\mathbb{Q}}))

lies in

\Ker(\Br(X_{\mathbb{Q}})\rightarrow\Br(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}))
.
∎

Now we can prove Proposition
3.4
:

Proof of Proposition
3.4
.

We first put ourselves in the situation (1).
Consider a Brauer class

\alpha\in\Br(X)

such that
\alpha|_{X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}}=0.

By Lemma
3.6
,
we have

\alpha|_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}}=f_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}(\beta)

for some
\beta\in\Br(\mathbb{Q})
.
We choose a section
s\colon\Spec(\mathbb Z)\rightarrow X
of the structure map.
Passing to Brauer groups, we get the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{\Br(\mathbb{Q})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f^{*}_{\mathbb Q}}
\textstyle{\Br(X_{\mathbb{Q}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{s^{*}_{\mathbb Q}}
\textstyle{\Br(\mathbb{Q})}
\textstyle{\Br(\mathbb Z)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f^{*}}
\textstyle{\Br(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{s^{*}}
\textstyle{\Br(\mathbb Z)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

where both horizontal rows compose to the identity.
We have that
\Br(\mathbb Z)=0
by class field theory.
This implies that
\beta=0
, so that
\alpha|_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}}=0
.
This by the injectivity
(see
[
AM20
, Proposition 2.5 (iv)]
) of

\Br(X)\rightarrow\Br(X_{\mathbb{Q}})

implies that
\alpha=0,
as desired.

Next, we put ourselves in the situation (2).
We look at again
the 7 term exact sequence for the Leray spectral sequence in the fppf topology for
f\colon X\rightarrow\Spec(\mathbb Z)
and the sheaf
\mathbb{G}_{m}
.
By the same argument as in Lemma
3.6
using that
\Br(\mathbb Z)=0
and

H^{1}_{\text{fl}}(\Spec(\mathbb Z),\underline{\Pic}_{X/\mathbb Z})=H^{1}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(\Spec(\mathbb Z),\underline{\Pic}_{X/\mathbb Z})=0

(
[
Mil80
, Chapter III, Theorem 3.9]
),
we see that

\Br(X)\rightarrow\Br(X_{\overline{\mathbb Z}})

is injective.
Now we use the injectivity of

\Br(X_{\overline{\mathbb Z}})\to\Br(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

For integers
g,n\geq 0
with
2g-2+n>0
,
the condition (1) in Proposition
3.4
is satisfied for
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\mathbb Z}.

In particular, if
\Br(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})=0
, then we have

\Br(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\mathbb Z})=0
.

Proof.

By
[
BP00
, Proposition 1.1]
(c.f.
[
FV23
, Theorem 5.8]
),
we see that

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}

is simply connected.

We shall show that

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb Z)\neq\emptyset
.
We first remark that if

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb Z)\neq\emptyset
, then
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}(\mathbb Z)\neq\emptyset

since the contraction morphisms

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

(see

) have a section

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}
.

To show that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb Z)\neq\emptyset
, we proceed by induction on

.
We assume that
g=0
.
If
n=3
, the projective line

\mathbb P^{1}_{\mathbb Z}\to\Spec(\mathbb Z)
with the three sections
defined by
0
,
1
,
\infty
gives an element of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(\mathbb Z)
.
By the remark in the previous paragraph, we then have

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb Z)\neq\emptyset
for every
n\geq 3
.
We assume that the assertion is true for some

.
By using the clutching morphisms

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+2}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g+1,n}

(see
[

, Section 3]
), and the assumption that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+2}(\mathbb Z)\neq\emptyset
,
we see that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g+1,n}(\mathbb Z)\neq\emptyset
for any

with

.
This concludes the proof.
∎

3.3.
Examples

As a supplement to Proposition
3.4
(and our main result), we give a few examples here.
The following claim gives a sufficient criterion for checking that
\underline{\Pic}_{X/\mathbb Z}
is a constant sheaf.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow\Spec(\mathbb Z)
be a proper and smooth DM stack with geometrically connected fibers.
We assume that

H^{1}(X_{s},\mathcal{O}_{X_{s}})=H^{2}(X_{s},\mathcal{O}_{X_{s}})=0

for all points
s\in\Spec(\mathbb Z)
.
Then
\underline{\Pic}_{X/\mathbb Z}
is constant on a finitely generated abelian group.

Proof.

Once one knows that the sheaf
\underline{\Pic}_{X/\mathbb Z}
is constant, it follows that the group must be finitely generated (c.f. the proof of Lemma
3.5
).
Consider
\underline{\Pic}^{\tau}_{X/\mathbb Z}\subset\underline{\Pic}_{X/\mathbb Z}
(see
[
FV23
, Section 3]
).
By our assumptions, we know the following properties:

•

\underline{\Pic}_{X/\mathbb Z}
is étale over
\Spec(\mathbb Z)
.

•

\underline{\Pic}^{\tau}_{X/\mathbb Z}\subset\underline{\Pic}_{X/\mathbb Z}
is open and closed.

•

\underline{\Pic}^{\tau}_{X/\mathbb Z}
is proper over
\Spec(\mathbb Z)
.

See
[
FV23
, Proposition 3.2, Theorem 3.6]
.
In particular, we know that
\underline{\Pic}^{\tau}_{X/\mathbb Z}
is finite étale over
\Spec(\mathbb Z),
so that it has to be constant by Minkowski’s theorem.
We look at the exact sequence

(In

∎

We give an example where

4.1.

The next proposition is easy to prove because one has a very good handle on the geometry of

Proposition 4.1

\Br(\mathcal{M}_{1,1,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})=0;

Proof.

This is proved in

Here we give an independent argument:
We first claim that

In fact, this can be deduced by comparing the Kummer exact sequences for

and

using invariance of étale cohomology with torsion coefficients under choice of algebraically closed base field (c.f. proof of Lemma

) and
\Pic(\mathcal{M}_{1,1,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})\simeq\Pic(\mathcal{M}_{1,1,\mathbb{C}})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}

Repeating this argument, but now using as an input the string of identifications

(see
[
AC87
]
)
and étale-Betti comparison for DM stacks, we see that

\Br(\mathcal{M}^{\an}_{1,1,\mathbb{C}})=\Br(\mathcal{M}_{1,1,\mathbb{C}}).

Here

\Br(\mathcal{M}^{\an}_{1,1,\mathbb{C}}):=H^{2}(\mathcal{M}^{\an}_{1,1,\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{G}_{m})_{\tors}
.

Thus it suffices to show that
\Br(\mathcal{M}^{\an}_{1,1,\mathbb{C}})=0
.
Observe that it then suffices to show that

\Br(\mathcal{M}^{\an}_{1,1,\mathbb{C}})\simeq H^{3}(\mathcal{M}^{\an}_{1,1,\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Z})_{\tors}=0.

But

H^{3}(\mathcal{M}^{\an}_{1,1,\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Z})=H^{3}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb Z),\mathbb Z)=0

using that
\SL_{2}(\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\ast_{\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z
(use
[

, Corollary 7.7]
).
∎

4.2.
Genus two

Next, we quickly explain why the case

Proposition 4.3

(Di Lorenzo-Pirisi)
.

The equality

Proof.

This result is contained in
[

.
Although this is probably trivial for the experts, let us explain why.
Let

be the moduli stack over

of hyper-elliptic curves of genus

(in the sense of
[
AV04
]

where

is a proper and smooth morphism with all geometric fibers connected curves of genus

and

of dimension

as proven in
[

]

For

∎

This is a well-known fact, but let us sketch the proof for the convenience of the reader.
We first note that the morphism

\mathrm{Bl}_{P}(X)\to\mathbb P^{2}_{F}
maps the exceptional divisor isomorphically onto the line in
\mathbb P^{2}_{F}
defined by
f_{0}=0
.
The locus in
\mathbb P^{2}_{F}
where the geometric fibers of the projection
X-\{P\}\to\mathbb P^{2}_{F}
are empty is the locus where
f_{0}=f_{1}=0
.
(The locus where
f_{0}=f_{1}=f_{2}=0
is empty by the condition that
P\in X
is not contained in any line in
X_{\overline{F}}
.)
The locus in
\mathbb P^{2}_{F}
where the geometric fibers of
X-\{P\}\to\mathbb P^{2}_{F}
have exactly one point is the union of

\{\,[x,y,z]\in\mathbb P^{2}_{F}\,|\,f_{0}(x,y,z)=0,\,f_{1}(x,y,z)\neq 0\,\}

and

\{\,[x,y,z]\in\mathbb P^{2}_{F}\,|\,f_{0}(x,y,z)\neq 0,\,g(x,y,z)=0\,\}.

It follows that the ramification locus
C\subset\mathbb P^{2}_{F}
(or equivalently, the locus where the geometric fibers of

\mathrm{Bl}_{P}(X)\to\mathbb P^{2}_{F}

have exactly one point) is defined by
g=0
.
∎

The polynomial
g(X,Y,W)
lives in
\mathcal{O}_{F}[X,Y,W]
.
We shall prove the following claim.

Lemma 4.9
.

The closed subscheme

\mathscr{C}\subset\mathbb P^{2}_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}

defined by
g(X,Y,W)=0
is a stable curve over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
.

Proof.

We first note that
g
is an irreducible element of
\mathcal{O}_{F}[X,Y,W]
since it is irreducible in
F[X,Y,W]
and is not divisible by the uniformizer
t
.
It follows that
\mathscr{C}
is irreducible, which in turn implies that it is flat over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
.

Let
\overline{a}\in\mathbb F_{q}
denote the image of an element
a\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
.
(We also use this notation for polynomials.)
One can check that the polynomial

\sum^{27}_{m=0}\overline{\varepsilon}_{27-m}S^{m}\in\mathbb F_{q}[S]

is equal to

(1)

\displaystyle S^{3}(S^{2}-1)^{2}

\displaystyle(S^{2}-4)(S^{2}-9)^{2}(S^{2}-16)(S^{2}-u)^{2}\times

\displaystyle(S^{4}-(2+2u)S^{2}+(1-u)^{2})(S^{4}-(18+2u)S^{2}+(9-u)^{2})

if
(q-1)/2
is even, and is equal to

(2)

\displaystyle S^{3}(S^{2}-9)^{2}

\displaystyle(S^{2}-u)^{3}(S^{2}-4u)^{2}(S^{2}-9u)\times

\displaystyle(S^{4}-(18+2u)S^{2}+(9-u)^{2})(S^{4}-(18+8u)S^{2}+(9-4u)^{2})

if
(q-1)/2
is odd.
It follows that
\overline{p}_{1}=\overline{q}_{1}=0
,
and the special fiber of
\mathscr{C}
is defined by

\overline{f_{0}}\overline{f_{2}}=(\overline{p}_{2}X-2Y+\overline{q}_{2}W)(X^{3}+\overline{p}_{0}XW^{2}+\overline{q}_{0}W^{3}-Y^{2}W)=0.

The special fiber has two irreducible components
\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{1}
and
\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{2}
: the first one
\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{1}
is the line defined by

\overline{f_{0}}=0

and the second one
\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{2}
is a cubic curve of Weierstrass form, defined by
\overline{f_{2}}=0
.
We can check that
the discriminant of
\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{2}

is zero and that
the
c_{4}
invariant of
\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{2}

is

(3)

-48\overline{p}_{0}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &(u-4)^{2}(u-16)^{2}/16&(&\text{if $(q-1)/2$ is even})\\
&81(u-1)^{2}(u-9)^{2}/16&(&\text{if $(q-1)/2$ is odd}),\end{aligned}\right.

which is nonzero since
\sqrt{u}
is not contained in
\mathbb F_{q}
.
Therefore
\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{2}
is a nodal curve; see
[
Sil09
, Chapter III, Proposition 1.4]
.
(The
c_{4}
invariant of a cubic curve of Weierstrass form is defined at the beginning of
[
Sil09
, Section III.1]
.)
In order to show that
\mathscr{C}
is a stable curve, it suffices to show that

(\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{1})_{\overline{\mathbb F}_{p}}

intersects
with

(\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{2})_{\overline{\mathbb F}_{p}}

in exactly three points.
Since they do not intersect each other in
W=0
, it is enough to show that
the discriminant of
the polynomial

(4)

x^{3}+\overline{p}_{0}x+\overline{q}_{0}-(\frac{\overline{p}_{2}}{2}x+\frac{\overline{q}_{2}}{2})^{2}=x^{3}-\frac{\overline{p}^{2}_{2}}{4}x^{2}+(\overline{p}_{0}-\frac{\overline{p}_{2}\overline{q}_{2}}{2})x+\overline{q}_{0}-\frac{\overline{q}^{2}_{2}}{4}

is nonzero.
The discriminant is equal to

(5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &81u^{2}(u-1)^{2}(u-9)^{2}/64&(&\text{if $(q-1)/2$ is even})\\
&729u^{6}(u-9)^{2}(u-9/4)^{2}/16&(&\text{if $(q-1)/2$ is odd}),\end{aligned}\right.

which is nonzero since
\sqrt{u}
is not contained in
\mathbb F_{q}
.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

(1)

One can compute the discriminant of
\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{2}
, the
c_{4}
invariant of
\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{2}
, and the discriminant

More precisely, we consider the polynomial

with coefficients in

) (depending on whether

Here we regard

Let
\varepsilon_{m}\in R[u]
be the coefficient of

in

Then one can check that in the ring

(2)

We can more easily prove the following weaker statement, which is in fact enough for our purpose (i.e. the proof of Theorem

): For a large enough

In total, we have shown the following statement:

with the following properties:

•

Proof.

This follows from Example

∎

Corollary 4.13
.

Let
p
be a prime such that
p\geq 11
.

(1)

\Br(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,\mathbb F_{q}})[2]=0
for any finite field

of characteristic

.

(2)

\Br(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,\overline{\mathbb F}_{p}})[2]=0
.

Proof.

(1) By
[

]
, we have

be the nonzero element.

does not lie in the image of

.
This follows from Lemma

(2) This follows from (1).
∎

We can now prove the desired statement:

.

Proof.

By
[
LP24
]

Since

, it suffices to show that

∎

4.4.
Genus greater than or equal to four

We conclude this paper by recalling the following well-known vanishing for the case
g\geq 4
, together with a rough explanation of where the condition that
g\geq 4
is used.

Theorem 4.15

(Korkmaz-Stipsicz)
.

Assume that
g\geq 4
and
n
arbitrary.
Then

\Br(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})=0.

Proof.

As in the proof of Proposition
4.1
, it is enough to show that
\Br(\mathcal{M}_{g,n,\mathbb{C}})=0.

This vanishing is explained in
[
FP21
, Theorem 4.1]
and is due to Korkmaz-Stipsicz and Harer:
The key point is that

H^{3}(\mathcal{M}_{g,n,\mathbb{C}}^{\text{an}},\mathbb Z)_{\tors}=0,

which reduces to a computation in group homology and this is
[
KS03
, Theorem 1.1]
(where they rely crucially on a stablization result due to Harer
[
Har85
]
, where the restriction
g\geq 4
enters).
∎

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rising energy demand of the building sector, coupled with the need for high indoor environmental quality (IEQ), necessitates intelligent control strategies. However, traditional methods like rule-based control (RBC) lack adaptability, while model predictive control (MPC) suffers from model dependency. Hence, this study designed and implemented an intelligent indoor environmental control system based on multi-sensor fusion and Building Information Modeling (BIM). At the algorithmic level, this study employed a deep fusion actor-critic reinforcement learning (DACRL) algorithm to achieve energy-efficient dynamic environmental control. The system employs a four-layer architecture with cloud-edge-end collaboration. Layer 1: The perception layer utilizes a multi-modal sensor network built on an STM32L0 low-power micro-controller and an SX1276 LoRa communication module. This architecture enables distributed collection and pre-processing of parameters such as temperature, humidity, CO2, light, and occupancy. Layer 2: The fusion layer proposes a spatio-temporal synchronization mechanism for multi-source asynchronous data based on sliding window dynamic weighting and an extended Kalman filter (EKF). As an optimization, an isolation forest and one-class support vector machine (SVM) are combined to construct a multi-layer anomaly detection process. Layer 3: The decision layer establishes a deep reinforcement learning control model based on the topology of BIM semantic mapping. The key novelty of this model lies in its innovative embedding of the Lieb–Thirring (L–T) inequality, which describes the energy concentration characteristics of spatial fields, as a spectral constraint into both the reward function and the online action projection process of the Actor-Critic algorithm. This model innovatively embeds the Lieb–Thirring (L–T) inequality, which describes the energy concentration characteristics of spatial fields, as a spectral constraint into the reward function and online action projection process of the Actor-Critic algorithm. Layer 4: The execution layer implements real-time closed-loop control of terminal equipment such as HVAC, lighting, and shading through the standard BMS protocol. This study designed large-scale simulation experiments covering six core scenarios: offices, classrooms, residences, hospitals, shopping malls, and data centers. The experimental results demonstrate that the system’s annual energy consumption per unit area decreases by up to 63.2% compared to traditional rule-based control (RBC) strategies and by 23.5% compared to model predictive control (MPC) strategies. At the same time, several indoor comfort metrics improved significantly, with CO2 concentrations falling below 800 ppm 91.5% of the time and the thermal comfort (PMV) index remaining within ± 0.3 94.2% of the time. The proposed DACRL algorithm demonstrates excellent convergence and generalization capabilities. It achieves stable convergence numerically with an average of only 15,000 training steps. Introduction

Energy consumption in the building sector is on the rise due to the acceleration of global urbanization and the growing demand for indoor environmental quality (IEQ) in contemporary buildings. This is now one of the main causes of carbon emissions and energy consumption worldwide1,2. The creation of clever and advanced building energy management techniques is essential in this situation. These tactics need to reduce energy use while maintaining the productivity, comfort, and well-being of the occupants. This is a major challenge and core research topic currently facing the intersection of building operations management, energy engineering, and artificial intelligence3,4. The 6 principles of indoor environmental regulation.

Traditional indoor environmental control systems mostly use methods based on fixed rules or classical PID control. Although these methods are simple and reliable, they are difficult to cope with the complex characteristics of the building environment, such as multi-variable, strong coupling, large inertia, time-varying and uncertainty5. Model predictive control has solved some of the above problems to a certain extent through the feed-forward optimization, but its performance is heavily dependent on accurate physical models6. In recent years, data-driven deep reinforcement learning (DRL) technology has opened up new avenues for the intelligent control of complex systems7,8. Existing studies have initially demonstrated the feasibility of DRL in building energy-saving control, such as its application in heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC) system optimization9,54. However, the direct application of the DRL to actual building environment regulation still faces several key bottlenecks.

Bottleneck 1. Accurate state perception and system-level stability.

The decision quality of DRL agents is highly dependent on accurate perception of the environment state. Single sensor data lacks a robust fusion framework that is deeply integrated with the control system.

Bottleneck 2. Safety and overall feasibility of control strategies.

The DRL agent may violate the physical constraints of the device during the exploration process. Therefore, prior knowledge and physical constraints must be embedded in the learning and execution process.

Bottleneck 3. Spatial field coordination and the global energy efficiency optimization.

Existing research lacks modeling and constraints on the overall “energy” distribution and uniformity of indoor environmental spatial fields.

To systematically address these challenges and practical problems, this study proposes and constructs an intelligent control system based on sensor fusion and BIM, using the deep actor-critic reinforcement learning (DACRL). The main contributions of this paper are as follows:

This study proposes an end-to-end, multi-layer intelligent control system architecture. This architecture is clearly divided into a perception layer, a fusion layer, a learning and decision layer, and a control execution layer. The study also details the functional modules, hardware selection, data flow, decision flow, and feedback flow paths for each layer. This innovative design provides a complete solution for large-scale engineering deployment of the system.

Literature review

Intelligent control of the indoor environment sits at the intersection of multiple research domains. To thoroughly contextualize our work, this review first examines the broad landscape of energy optimization methods, then considers the integration of Building Information Modeling (BIM) with sensor data, and finally focuses on the cutting-edge applications of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) in built environments.

Energy consumption optimization methods for indoor environment control

As global energy demand and environmental pressures continue to increase, traditional research on energy management is facing challenges. Intelligent energy management and optimization has become an important research direction in the intelligentization of buildings, agriculture, and cities. Ullah et al.10 proposed a new framework for intelligent greenhouse climate optimization based on IoT. This framework achieves intelligent balance management of greenhouse energy consumption by real-time regulation of the environmental parameters such as temperature, humidity and light. In the experiment, the model maintained the optimal environment for crop growth to a great extent. At the same time, it numerically reduced energy consumption and production costs by approximately 26.5% and 27.8% respectively. Similarly, Benyezza et al.22 used IoT and fuzzy control technology to design a zoned irrigation system. This system achieves dual savings in water resources and energy. In the core areas of architecture and urban environment, comprehensive research on optimizing building energy consumption, thermal comfort and lighting performance has also received core attention. Lakhdari et al.11 conducted joint modeling through parametric optimization and multi-objective evolutionary algorithm. The model constructed a balanced framework of lighting, thermal comfort and energy efficiency for middle school classrooms in arid and hot areas. Wu and Zhang14 further proposed a multi-objective building envelope optimization framework. The framework combines useful daylight illuminance (UDI), energy use intensity (EUI) and thermal discomfort time (TDP). The study was conducted in a hot summer and cold winter climate zone. The results showed that by optimizing the opening area of south-facing windows and shading design, energy efficiency can be improved by 18–48.4% and daylight illuminance and thermal comfort performance can be well improved.

At the same time, building energy consumption optimization methods based on control theory and artificial intelligence have been deeply studied. Chen and Li15 optimized the radiant ceiling cooling system through model predictive control. This optimization idea achieves accurate tracking of regional air temperature. Arroyo et al.23 proposed an MPC algorithm combined with reinforcement learning. The algorithm integrates state estimation, dynamic optimization and learning capabilities. This organic fusion enables the building energy system to continuously adaptively optimize in an uncertain environment. Lissa et al.16 optimized the home energy management system based on deep reinforcement learning. The system realizes adaptive scheduling and load forecasting of the photovoltaic (PV) energy. This model can reduce summer energy consumption by an average of 8% without affecting user comfort, and up to 16%.

In terms of urban smart infrastructure, energy optimization research combining IoT and AI has also collided with event scenarios. Chen et al.12 built an intelligent streetlight system. The system collects traffic flow and personnel information through sensors. This framework combines dynamic PWM dimming and renewable energy management to achieve efficient energy saving and carbon emission reduction. Selvaraj et al.13 proposed an AI-based intelligent building energy management and monitoring system (AIMS-SB). The system can predict and optimize building energy consumption, renewable energy output and recovery to maximize energy efficiency and two-dimensional comprehensive utilization of energy. Rehman et al.17 focused on the integration of renewable energy in smart grids. The study proposed a hybrid heuristic algorithm (HGPDO) combined with load scheduling and energy storage management. This system achieved electricity cost reduction, peak-valley ratio optimization and carbon emission reduction. Wang et al.18 proposed an intelligent ventilation strategy based on occupancy density detection for high-density crowd environments. This strategy achieved energy savings of approximately 11.7% while reducing the risk of the infection. In addition, energy prediction model is also a key link in intelligent energy management. Hora et al.19 used LSTM combined with butterfly optimization algorithm to predict electricity consumption. This prediction model can effectively capture time series characteristics. In the field of agricultural smart greenhouses, Liu et al.21 proposed a greenhouse climate prediction model based on LSTM to perform global optimization through short-term sliding windows and abnormal data processing strategies. The optimization results achieved high-precision predictions of temperature, humidity, light, carbon dioxide concentration and soil environment. Lin et al.20 further proposed a double-layer curtain wall (DSF) system with natural ventilation for building facade renovation. This model is of great significance in the context of energy conservation and low-carbon transformation of traditional buildings. The core goal of this study is to improve the energy efficiency of buildings in summer by optimizing the double-layer curtain wall structure and the design of adjustable glass blinds. More essentially, some IoT based methodologies are also efficient51–53.

BIM and sensor fusion: the State-of-the-art

The integrated framework of Building Information Modeling (BIM), Internet of Things (IoT), sensor technology and Digital Twin (DT) has gradually become an important tool to promote the full life cycle management of buildings. In the field of low-carbon and green building research, Zhang and Ying24 pointed out that the integration of geographic information systems (GIS) and BIM can effectively improve the command and management of rail transit projects. The study used BIM technology to quickly calculate life cycle carbon emissions and optimize them in the design stage. This innovation provides an efficient path for building low-carbon cities. This verifies that BIM is no longer limited to geometric modeling but is gradually becoming an important supporting platform for energy analysis and environmental assessment. In the facility operation and maintenance link, the integration of BIM and sensor data is a research trend. Valinejadshoubi et al.25 proposed a BIM-based sensor fault management framework. The framework uses a cloud platform to achieve efficient communication between facility managers and the IoT companies. Such a communication paradigm can respond quickly when a sensor fails. Similarly, Zavaleta28 further studied the combination of the BIM and structural health monitoring (SHM). The study demonstrated how sensor data-driven structural assessment can improve the safety of buildings. At the same time, the study pointed out that data standardization and system interoperability are still challenges that need to be solved. With the introduction of the concept of digital twins, the integration of BIM and IoT is leading to the vigorous development of building virtualization. Eneyew et al.26 proposed a multi-level BIM-IoT data integration architecture. This architecture can solve the problem of insufficient semantic interoperability. This innovation enables building digital twins to have the ability to predict, evaluate and actively intervene. Wang et al.30 used deep learning and BIM big data to carry out environmental satisfaction evaluation. The study further proposed an improved data fusion algorithm. This provides experimental verification for the application of digital twins in energy-saving buildings. The research of Yang et al.27 went a step further. The model combines BIM with synthetic aperture radar (SAR) observations. This fusion analysis realizes 4D digital twins for urban motion monitoring. In terms of risk management in complex environments, Xun et al.29 introduced the intuitionistic fuzzy set (IFS) and D–S evidence theory paradigm. This study proposed a multi-information fusion method based on BIM and set the application scenario as the safety risk assessment of undersea tunnel construction. This method can achieve dynamic risk perception under uncertainty and fuzziness. This study highlights the decision-making support value of BIM in high-risk infrastructure projects. At the same time, Zhou and Yang33 combined BIM with multi-source heterogeneous sensors. This study designed a new construction site safety management system. The system uses comprehensive information in spatial and temporal dimensions to achieve real-time perception and intelligent decision-making. The application of BIM has also been extended to the fields of cultural heritage protection and complex geometric modeling. Miky et al.32 proposed a deep learning-driven HBIM workflow for historical buildings. This workflow improves the recognition accuracy of the surface weathering morphology and facade deterioration by combining drone images and laser scanning data. Rached et al.31 used RGB-D sensors combined with deep learning methods. This study obtained automatic BIM model generation for the indoor environments and significantly improved modeling efficiency. This type of research has led to the development of low-cost BIM generation technologies.

Applications of deep reinforcement learning in built environments

The core advantage of deep reinforcement learning is that it can continuously learn and optimize decision-making strategies through interaction with complex environments. In the field of rescue robots, Niroui’s34 research first proposed combining deep reinforcement learning with cutting-edge exploration methods. This framework is used for autonomous exploration of unknown and crowded environments in urban search and rescue. Test results show that this method can not only quickly expand the exploration range but also maintain stable performance under different environmental layouts. In terms of the building energy management, Jia35 proposed an end-to-end deep reinforcement learning control framework. This framework directly learns the optimal balance between building energy saving and occupant comfort through interactive data. The study also introduced expert experience replay, policy smoothing constraints and a virtual test platform. Test results show that this framework is effective in systematically evaluating the performance of different algorithms.

In response to the complexity of building energy systems caused by the integration of renewable energy, Shen36 proposed a multi-agent deep reinforcement learning optimization paradigm. This paradigm achieves collaborative control of multiple devices through a dual deep Q network and a value decomposition mechanism. At the same time, it combines priority experience replay and feasible action screening mechanisms as an efficiency optimization. Experimental results show that this method significantly reduces the duration of user discomfort and the waste of renewable energy. In response to the high energy consumption problem of heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC), Gao37 modeled building thermal comfort control as a joint optimization problem of energy consumption and comfort. The study used a deep neural network to predict the thermal comfort level of residents and combined it with a deep deterministic policy gradient (DDPG) learning control strategy. In addition, Brandi38 explored optimizing indoor temperature control and heating energy consumption by adjusting the water supply temperature set point. The study verified the adaptability of deep reinforcement learning in both static and dynamic environments. This suggests that future research needs to further optimize the coupling of input feature selection and control strategy.

In the study of demand response (DR), Jang39 proposed a deep reinforcement learning method that combines planning models with safety mechanisms. This model can cope with the complex interaction between price signals and user behavior. By introducing a “guardrail” mechanism, this study effectively reduced the high cost risk in the initial learning stage. Furthermore, Lei40 proposed a novel control framework based on the Branching Dueling Q-Network (BDQN) for multidimensional control problems. The method was pre-trained in a virtual environment and then deployed in a real office setting. It reduced energy consumption while improving overall comfort. Finally, Valladares41 modeled indoor air quality (IAQ) and thermal comfort. The study proposed a novel deep reinforcement learning algorithm that can optimize energy consumption, comfort, and air quality simultaneously. The algorithm was verified through large-scale simulation and real-world scenarios and found that this method can achieve energy reduction while maintaining a suitable thermal comfort index and controlling CO2 concentration.

The system architecture design

Overall system structure

Layer module structure (perception layer → fusion layer → learning decision layer → control execution layer)

The overall framework of this model is constructed using a hierarchical structure with the modules of perception layer, fusion layer, learning decision layer, control execution layer, respectively.

Perception layer (sensor node)

The sensor acquisition nodes in the perception layer preferably use STM32L0 series micro-controllers (Fig. 2 shows the details and common application scenarios of this micro-controller). In this system, this micro-controller features a 32-bit core, 32 MHz, and approximately 51 I/Os. It also supports the I2C/SPI/UART/USB, has a low-power stop mode, and includes flash memory. This hardware was chosen for its low power consumption, sufficient ADC accuracy, and cost advantages. These advantages make it suitable for large-scale deployment. The SX1276 LoRa wireless module is used as the companion wireless module for the acquisition nodes. Specific parameters for this module include a maximum link budget of 168 dB, a sensitivity of − 148 dBm, and a maximum data rate of approximately 300 kbps.

The power consumption and sleep strategy utilizes the low power mode of STM32L0 and the wake-up mechanism of the SX127642,43 to achieve long battery life. The core idea of this power consumption optimization is to wake up only when sampling or reporting.

Fusion layer

The fusion layer first performs node aggregation. Each cell/floor is deployed with an aggregation node, implemented using the STM32F103ZET6 + SX1276. The aggregation node is responsible for receiving LoRa reports, de-duplication, time-division multiplexing (TDM), local caching, and forwarding. Multiple sensor nodes are connected to a single aggregation node; time division multiplexing (TDM) is used in conjunction with SF/channel multiplexing to reduce conflicts. The aggregation node maintains a time slot table and periodically allocates slots. The aggregation node or edge gateway maps physical sensor IDs to spatial semantics in the BIM and constructs a unified spatio-temporal semantic table.

Learning decision-making layer

The features output by the fusion layer are organized into a state vector at the edge. This parameter includes environmental variables (temperature, humidity, CO2, illumination, occupancy), device power consumption, external weather conditions, and BIM features for the current and historical windows. The decision layer uses the deep actor-critic model. The input first passes through a causal LSTM/convolutional time window to extract temporal features before entering the actor (continuous action output) and critic (value estimation) heads. The decision output is then processed through a feasible domain projection and safety filter.

Control execution layer

This layer’s modules first issue commands using the multiple protocols, with actions translated into BMS/device protocols based on the device type. Building integrated devices such as air conditioners, fans, valves, sunshades, and lighting can be connected via the JACE-8000 network controller and the IO-28U intelligent IO module. Control commands consider a three-step process: "send → confirm → execute and monitor." Command logs are stored at the aggregation node or gateway.

System deployment architecture

The deployment level description

This system’s deployment hierarchy adopts a layered design paradigm: "cloud-edge-field-network-end devices." This paradigm ensures reliable intelligent control in the large-scale building environments. The cloud layer carries out the primary computational and administrative tasks. This layer uses BIM-powered physical simulation models to model and test large-scale virtual environments. The framework uses GPU clusters to train deep actor-critic reinforcement learning offline. Additionally, the framework incorporates data lake storage to facilitate knowledge mining, batch analysis, and centralized long-term data archiving. The edge layer is deployed on edge gateways within each building or floor and is primarily responsible for real-time inference and execution of control decisions. This process includes state estimation, policy inference, action safety filtering, and command issuance, and completes closed-loop control at the execution layer through an interface with the building management system (BMS). In addition to the previous module, the edge nodes store recent experience data. This enables independent operation in the event of failures and facilitating the fine-tuning of local policies. The field network layer uses the aggregation nodes and BMS controllers to convert protocols and link sensors and actuators. Sensor nodes frequently use SX1276 LoRa modules and STM32L0 micro-controllers. These modules offer wireless communication capabilities with low power consumption and excellent coverage. This capability allows for the steady collection of important environmental data, such as the room temperature, humidity, CO2 concentration, light intensity, and occupancy rate. Actuators connect to fans, air conditioners, lights, sunshades, and other devices via I/O modules and pre-existing BMS interfaces.

BIM modeling and data interaction interface

Framework of three-tier nested system

BIM (Building Information Modeling) is a digital representation of a building’s physical and functional characteristics. It is also a core vehicle for multi-source data integration and dynamic interaction. In the smart construction sites and intelligent operation and maintenance scenarios, the BIM modeling and data interaction interfaces form a three-layered system. The specific system details are shown in Fig. 7.

The BIM three-tier nested system.

The structural modeling layer establishes a hierarchical model of building components and spatial topology based on the IFC (Industry Foundation Classes) standard. The semantic mapping layer semantically binds sensor data to BIM model spatial regions and the component attributes. This operation enables the bidirectional mapping between geometry and semantics. The external simulation interface layer utilizes open APIs and a data adaptation protocol. This protocol connects the BIM model with energy consumption simulation platforms such as Energy-Plus, ESP-r, and IES VE, as well as real-time monitoring platforms.

The system practicality, scalability and people-oriented considerations

While the core contributions of this research lie in its algorithms and system architecture, its ultimate value lies in practical deployment. Based on experimental results and system design, further discuss key issues in its practical application are also discussed.

1. Human-centered Control and Adaptability: This study implicitly safeguards occupant well-being by maintaining strict comfort metrics (such as PMV and CO2), a reactive strategy. However, it is recognized that occupants’ preferences and behaviors are dynamic and personalized. The proposed architecture provides a natural platform for more advanced human-centered control. For example, occupants can provide comfort feedback (e.g., "too hot," “too cold”) via mobile apps or indoor terminals. This feedback can be directly used as an additional input to the reinforcement learning reward function, enabling online fine-tuning and personalized adaptation of the system. Future work will focus on exploring this "human-in-the-loop" learning mechanism.

2. Scalability and Interoperability with Existing Systems:Scalability is built into the system’s four-layer architecture and "cloud-edge-end" collaboration paradigm. The system can scale horizontally by floor or building unit thanks to the deployment of edge computing nodes. As for interoperability, as explained in section "Control execution layer", conventional BMS protocols (such BACnet and Modbus) are used by the execution layer to connect with building equipment. Our system can be linked with the majority of current building automation systems without altering the underlying hardware, which drastically lowers deployment hurdles and costs. This is demonstrated by the JACE-8000 controller, which is a universal gateway in the industry.

3. Long-term Adaptability and Robustness: Building operating conditions and usage patterns change over time. This system’s ability to adapt over the long term through offline training methods and a cloud-based experience pool is one of its main advantages. In particular, operating data is frequently uploaded to the cloud by edge nodes. The system delivers the updated policy model to the edge nodes and initiates model retraining when it notices performance deterioration or notable operational drift. The system may overcome the constraints of conventional static control systems by adapting to long-term issues like seasonal variations, shifts in building functioning, and aging equipment thanks to this closed-loop learning and update process.

Conclusion and prospect

This study provides a comprehensive analysis of the multivariable coupling, nonlinearity, and time-varying characteristics inherent to indoor environmental control in buildings. Then, it proposes an intelligent control system that integrates multi-source sensor information, BIM digital twins, and spectrally constrained reinforcement learning. The system uses the novel DACRL control method, which is embedded with efficient L–T spectral constraints, spatiotemporal synchronization, and anomaly repair algorithms for multimodal data. It also optimizes the deployment of edge-side sensor nodes and their sampling. High-precision perception, effective computation, and high-performance control of intricate architectural environments are all made possible by this creative methodological combination. Large-scale experimental results show that the system significantly reduces the operating energy consumption of different types of buildings while maintaining the comfort and health of the indoor environment. At the same time, the framework demonstrates excellent robustness and generalization performance. The successful embedding of L–T inequality as a physical constraint provides new ideas for the safe and reliable application of reinforcement learning in the field of building control. The proposed system architecture and algorithm model have good engineering applicability and scalability. Future research will focus on the following areas: (1) Introducing human-in-the-loop adaptive mechanisms to explore how to seamlessly integrate occupant real-time feedback and personalized preferences into the DACRL learning process; (2) Developing more efficient cross-building transfer learning and lifelong learning algorithms to further improve the system’s long-term adaptability in large-scale deployments and reduce operation and maintenance costs.

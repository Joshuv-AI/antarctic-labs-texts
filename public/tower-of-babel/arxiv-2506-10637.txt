Deligne 1 1 -motives with torsion and étale motives
Raphaël Ruimy

Abstract
We construct the motivic t-structure on 1 1 -motives with integral coefficients over a scheme of characteristic zero or a Dedekind scheme. When we invert the residue characteristic exponents of the base, this t-structure induces a t-structure on the category of smooth 1 1 -motives whose heart is the category of Deligne 1 1 -motives with torsion which we prove to be abelian. This relies on the fact that over a normal scheme, Deligne 1 1 -motives with torsion are determined by their fiber on the generic point and on an explicit description of good reduction Deligne 1 1 -motives with torsion.

\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}
. By
Corollary

4.13
, we can further assume
S
to be the spectum of a field in which case it follows from
Proposition

4.7
.
∎

Corollary 4.17
.

Let
S
be an excellent scheme allowing resolution of singularities by alterations and let
\Lambda
be a localization of
\mathbb{Z}
. Then the objects of
\mathrm{DM}^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)
are bounded with respect to the motivic t-structure.

Proof.

We reduce to the the case of objects of the form
f_{\sharp}(\Lambda_{X})
for
X\to S
smooth of relative dimension at most
1
. In that case, we claim that they lie in degrees
{0,1,2}
. To prove that, we can assume
S
to be the spectrum of a field
k
by
Corollary

4.13
which we can assume to be algebraically closed by
Proposition

4.7
. Now, take a smooth compactification
g\colon\overline{X}\to\mathrm{Spec}(k)
of
X
. As
k
is algebraically closed, the projective curve
\overline{X}
has a rational point hence by
Lemma

3.7
, the
1
-motive
g_{\sharp}(\Lambda_{\overline{X}})
is in degrees
{0,1,2}
. If
i\colon Z\to\overline{X}
is the closed complement of
X
, then
(ig)_{\sharp}(\Lambda_{Z})
is in degree
0
by
Corollary

4.5
. The result follows using the localization triangle (
0.1
).
∎

5
The motivic t-structure on
1
-motives

The goal of this section is to prove that the motivic t-structure restricts to
1
-motives. This will be a consequence of the following result which generalizes
[
Haa19
, Lemma 6.12]
by adapting the method of
[
Rui22
, Theorem 4.2.7]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
S
be a regular scheme such that all the connected components of
S
satisfy
2.3
and let
\Lambda
be localization of
\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
. Then,
\Phi_{S}
induces an equivalence:

\Phi_{S}\colon\mathrm{M}_{1}^{\mathrm{D}}(S,\Lambda)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{sm}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)\cap\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{ind}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)^{\heartsuit}.

In particular, the motivic t-structure induces a t-structure on
\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{sm}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)
.

Proof.

We adapt the arguments of
[
Haa19
, Lemma 6.12]
, as we work with integral coefficients, we need to be more careful: we will use
Lemma

5.3
below. We can assume
S
to be connected. Let
\eta
be its generic point. We have a commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{M}_{1}^{\mathrm{D}}(S,\Lambda)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{sm}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)\cap\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{ind}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)^{\heartsuit}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{M}_{1}^{\mathrm{D}}(\eta,\Lambda)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{sm}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\eta,\Lambda)\cap\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{ind}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\eta,\Lambda)^{\heartsuit}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{S}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{\eta}}

where
\Phi_{S}
is fully faithful by
Lemma

5.2
, the left vertical arrow is fully faithful by
Theorem

2.8
and
\Phi_{\eta}
is an equivalence because in that case
\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{sm}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\eta,\Lambda)=\mathrm{DM}^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\eta,\Lambda)
and the motivic t-structure restricts to
1
-motives by
Proposition

4.7
. Now we claim that the functor

\eta^{*}\colon\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{sm}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)\cap\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{ind}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)^{\heartsuit}\to\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{sm}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\eta,\Lambda)\cap\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{ind}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\eta,\Lambda)^{\heartsuit}

is fully faithful. This follows from
Lemma

5.3
using continuity
[
Leh19b
, Proposition 1.23]
(note that it still works with integral coefficients using the same proof as
[
CD16
, Theorem 6.3.9]
replacing
[
Gro67
, Théorème 8.10.5 & Proposition 17.7.8]
with
[
Leh19b
, Lemma 1.24]
).
Hence to show that the functor induced by
\Phi_{S}
as above is an equivalence, we have to show that if
M
belongs to
\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{sm}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)\cap\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{ind}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)^{\heartsuit}
then
N_{\eta}=\Phi_{\eta}^{-1}(\eta^{*}(M))
has good reduction. By
Proposition

2.10
, this amounts to showing that if
\ell
is a prime number distinct from the characteristic of
\eta
,
T_{\ell}(N_{\eta})
extends to a local system over
S[1/\ell]
. Now,
\overline{\rho}_{\ell}(M)
is a dualizable object in
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{cons}}(S[1/\ell],\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda)
so it has perfect fibers by
[
HRS23
, Theorem 4.13]
and is in the heart of the t-structure so it belongs to
\mathrm{Loc}_{S[1/\ell]}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda)
by
[
HRS23
, Theorem 6.2]
and by definition, its fiber to
\eta
is
T_{\ell}(N_{\eta})=\overline{\rho}_{\ell}(\eta^{*}(M))
.

The fact that the motivic t-structure induces a t-structure on
\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{sm}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)
follows from
[
Rui24
, Lemma 1.2.3]
: indeed knowing that the objects of
\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{sm}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)
are bounded by
Corollary

4.17
, we only have to show that if
f\colon M\to N
is a map of Deligne
1
-motives, then
\ker(f)
is still a Deligne
1
-motive. This is true because
\mathrm{M}_{1}^{\mathrm{D}}(S,\Lambda)
is an abelian category by
Corollary

2.11
and the kernel computed there is the same as the kernel computed in
\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{ind}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)^{\heartsuit}
because the functor between those abelian categories is exact and fully faithful; its exactness follows from the fact that the functor
\Phi_{S}
sends short exact sequences to exact triangles which are therefore again short exact sequences. This is true because for any
x\in S
, the functor
\Phi_{x}
sends exact sequences to exact sequences (see
4.6
) and the family of the

i_{x}^{*}\colon\mathrm{DM}^{\mathbb{Q}-1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)^{\heartsuit}\to\mathrm{DM}^{\mathbb{Q}-1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(x,\Lambda)^{\heartsuit}

is exact and conservative by
Lemma

4.15
and
Proposition

4.12
(note that being bounded for the ordinary t-structure implies having bounded below torsion because of
Lemma

4.9
).
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
S
be a regular scheme let
\Lambda
be a flat
\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
-algebra. Then the functor

\Phi_{S}\colon\mathrm{M}_{1}^{\mathrm{D}}(S,\Lambda)\to\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{sm}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)

is fully faithful.

Proof.

We can assume that
S
is connected with generic point
\eta
and that
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
. In that case, the functor
\Phi_{\eta}
is fully faithful by
[
BVK16
, Theorem 2.1.2]
combined with Cisinski-Déglise’s version of Voevodsky’s cancellation theorem for étale motives (see the proof of
Lemma

3.7
). Let
M
and
N
be in
\mathrm{M}_{1}^{\mathrm{D}}(S,\mathbb{Z}^{\prime})
. Then, as by
Proposition

2.5
, the functor
\Phi_{?}
is compatible with pullbacks, we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{M}_{1}^{\mathrm{D}}(S,\mathbb{Z}^{\prime})}(M,N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\mathbb{Z}^{\prime})}(\Phi_{S}(M),\Phi_{S}(N))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{M}_{1}^{\mathrm{D}}(\eta,\mathbb{Z}^{\prime})}(M_{\eta},N_{\eta})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\eta,\mathbb{Z}^{\prime})}(\Phi_{\eta}(M_{\eta}),\Phi_{\eta}(N_{\eta})).}

The left vertical arrow is an equivalence by
Theorem

2.8
while we just proved that the bottom horizontal one is an equivalence. Hence, it suffices to show that the map

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\mathbb{Z}^{\prime})}(\Phi_{S}(M),\Phi_{S}(N))\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\eta,\mathbb{Z}^{\prime})}(\Phi_{\eta}(M_{\eta}),\Phi_{\eta}(N_{\eta}))

is injective. This follows from
Lemma

5.3
below and continuity. ∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
S
be a regular scheme and let
j\colon U\to S
be a dense open immersion. Then, the functor

j^{*}\colon\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{sm}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)\cap\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{ind}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)^{\heartsuit}\to\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{sm}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,\Lambda)\cap\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{ind}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,\Lambda)^{\heartsuit}

is fully faithful.

Proof.

We must first recall some notions from
[
Leh19b
]
, for
T
a scheme, we have

1.

the category
\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{\mathrm{ind}\mathrm{coh}}(T,\Lambda)
of Ind-cohomological motives to be the localizing subcategory of
\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(T,\Lambda)
generated by the
f_{*}\Lambda_{X}
for
f\colon X\to T
proper.

2.

the category
\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{\mathrm{ind}\mathrm{coh}-1}(T,\Lambda)
of Ind-cohomological
1
-motives to be the localizing subcategory of
\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(T,\Lambda)
generated by the
f_{*}\Lambda_{X}
for
f\colon X\to T
proper of relative dimension at most
1
.

3.

The inclusion
\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{\mathrm{ind}\mathrm{coh}-1}(T,\Lambda)\to\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{\mathrm{ind}\mathrm{coh}}(T,\Lambda)
has a right adjoint
\omega^{1}
.

Note that by
[
Leh19b
, Proposition 1.28]
, we have a t-equivalence of categories

(-1)\colon\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{ind}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(T,\Lambda)\to\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{\mathrm{ind}\mathrm{coh}-1}(T,\Lambda)

with the t-structure on the right hand side generated by
\mathcal{JG}_{T}(-1)
.

We now claim that if
M
belongs to
\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{sm}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)\cap\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{ind}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)^{\heartsuit}
, then the natural map

M(-1)\to\tau^{\leq 0}(\omega^{1}(j_{*}j^{*}M(-1)))

is an equivalence which would prove the Lemma. We let
N=M(-1)
and we let
i\colon Z\to S
be the reduced closed complement of
S
. As
\omega^{1}
commutes with
i_{*}
, the localization triangle (
0.2
) yields an exact triangle

i_{*}\omega^{1}i^{!}(N)\to N\to\omega^{1}j_{*}j^{*}N.

hence it suffices to prove that
i_{*}\omega^{1}i^{!}(N)
lies in degree at least
2
with respect to the motivic t-structure. For this we adapt
[
Leh19a
, Lemma 4.7]
to the setting of integral coefficients: chose a stratification
\emptyset=\overline{Z}_{m+1}\subseteq\overline{Z}_{m}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\overline{Z}_{0}=Z
by closed subsets with

Z_{s}:=\overline{Z}_{s}\setminus\overline{Z}_{s+1}
regular and equidimensional. Write
c_{s}
for the codimension of
Z_{s}
in
S
, since
U
is dense, we have
c_{s}>0
. Write
Z_{i}\xrightarrow{j_{s}}\overline{Z}_{i}\xleftarrow{k_{s}}\overline{Z}_{s+1}
and let
\theta_{s}\colon\overline{Z}_{s+1}\to S
be the closed immesion with
\theta_{-1}=i
. By localisation, we have distinguished
triangles

(k_{s})_{*}\omega^{1}\theta_{s}^{!}(N)\to\omega^{1}\theta_{s-1}^{!}(N)\to\omega^{1}(j_{s})_{*}j_{s}^{*}\omega^{1}\theta_{s-1}^{!}(N)

using the compatibilities of
\omega^{1}
with the six functors described in
[
Leh19b
, Proposition 3.3]
.
By absolute purity
[
CD16
, Theorem 4.6.1]
, using the fact that
N
is dualizable, we have
\theta_{s-1}^{!}(N)=\theta_{s}^{*}(N)(-c_{s})[-2c_{s}]
. As
j_{s}^{*}
is right t-exact by
Proposition

4.4
, the functor
(j_{s})_{*}
is left t-exact
i.e.
preserves non-positive objects. Hence, combining the exactness properties of
Corollary

4.5
and
Proposition

4.12
, it suffices to show that
\omega^{1}(\theta_{s-1}^{*}(N)(-c_{s})[-2c_{s}])
lies in degree at least
2
.

Now write the exact triangle

\omega^{1}(\theta_{s-1}^{*}(N)(-c_{s})[-2c_{s}])\to\omega^{1}(\theta_{s-1}^{*}(N)(-c_{s})[-2c_{s}])\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}\to\omega^{1}(\theta_{s-1}^{*}(N)(-c_{s})[-2c_{s}])\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}.

The same proof as
[
Rui25
, Proposition 2.1.5]
yields that
\omega^{1}
is compatible with tensoring with
\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
for any
n
. Furthermore, the functor
\omega^{1}
induces the identity on torsion motives as they are
0
-motives (see
[
Rui25
, Proposition 2.1.6]
). Hence, we get an exact triangle:

\omega^{1}(\theta_{s-1}^{*}(N)(-c_{s})[-2c_{s}])\to\omega^{1}(\theta_{s-1}^{*}(N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q})(-c_{s}))[-2c_{s}]\to\mathrm{colim}_{n}\ \theta_{s-1}^{*}(N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})(-c_{s})[-2c_{s}]).

Now as
U
is dense, the term in the middle lies in degree at least
2
by
[
Leh19b
, Lemma 4.7]
, while each
\theta_{s-1}^{*}(N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})(-c_{s})[-2c_{s}]
lies in degree at least
1
because
N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
is in degree at least
-1
and
c_{s}>0
.
Hence, the same argument as at the end of the proof of
Lemma

4.9
yields that their colimit is also in degree at least
1
. This shows that
\omega^{1}(\theta_{s-1}^{*}(N)(-c_{s})[-2c_{s}])
is in degree at least
2
finishing the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
S
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-scheme or a Dedekind scheme and
let
\Lambda
localization of
\mathbb{Z}
. Then the motivic t-structure induces a t-structure on
\mathrm{DM}^{1}(S,\Lambda)
. This t-structure is furthermore compatible with pullback and with the
\ell
-adic realization functors.

Proof.

Once we have proved that the t-structure restricts to
1
-motives, the rest will follow from
Corollary

4.13
.
We adapt the proof of
[
Rui22
, Theorem 4.2.7]
.
If
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime ideal of
\mathbb{Z}
and if
X
is a scheme, we say that an Ind-
1
-motive is
\mathfrak{p}
-geometric
when its image in the category
\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{ind}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)\otimes_{\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
belongs to
\mathrm{DM}^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)\otimes_{\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
.
Let
M
be a
1
-motive over
S
. We want to show that the Ind-
1
-motive
\tau^{\leqslant 0}(M)
is a
1
-motive. By
[
Rui22
, Proposition 1.1.10]
, it suffices to show that it is
\mathfrak{p}
-geometric for any maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
p
be a generator of
\mathfrak{p}
, exactly as in the proof of
[
Rui22
, Theorem 4.2.7]
, we can reduce to the case when

1.

p
is invertible on
S
or
S
is of characteristic
p
.

2.

M
belongs to
\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{sm}1}(S,\Lambda)
.

Assume that
S
is of characteristic
p
. It suffices to show that the Ind-
1
-motive
\tau^{\leqslant 0}(M)
is a
1
-motive. Using
[
CD16
, Proposition A.3.4]
, we can assume that
p
is invertible in
\Lambda
. The result then follows from
Theorem

5.1
noting that
2.3
holds because of
2.4
.

If
p
is invertible on
S
, note that the motivic t-structure induces a t-structure on

\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{ind}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)\otimes_{\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}}

using
[
Rui22
, Proposition 1.1.8]
. It suffices to show that it induces a t-structure on

\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{sm}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)\otimes_{\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{sm}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}).

The latter has a t-structure induced by the motivic t-structure by
Theorem

5.1
noting that
2.3
holds because of
2.4
. Hence it suffices to see that the functor

\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{sm}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})\to\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{ind}1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)\otimes_{\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}}

is t-exact. To that end it suffices to show that it sends
\mathrm{M}_{1}^{\mathrm{D}}(S,\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})
to the heart which is true because the functor
\Phi_{S}
sends Deligne
1
-motives to the heart of the motivic t-structure by
Corollary

4.16
.
∎

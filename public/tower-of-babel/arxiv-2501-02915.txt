Extending relative entropy for Korteweg-type models with non-monotone pressure: large friction limit and weak-strong uniqueness
Giada Cianfarani Carnevale, Jan Giesselmann

Abstract
. In this paper we study weak-strong uniqueness and singular relaxation limits for the Euler–Korteweg and Navier–Stokes–Korteweg systems with non monotone pressure. Both weak-strong uniqueness and the relaxation limit are investigated using relative entropy technique. We make use of the enlarged formulation of the model in terms of the drift velocity introduced in [ 6 ] , generalizing in this way results proved in [ 17 ] for the Euler–Korteweg model, by allowing more general capillarity functions, and the result contained in [ 8 ] for the monotone pressure case.

1.
Introduction

The objective of this paper is to study stability properties of Euler–Korteweg and Navier–Stokes–Korteweg systems with non monotone pressure. In particular, we address weak-strong uniqueness and the high friction limit.
Indeed, we will leverage ideas from
[
8
]
, which treated monotone pressure laws, in order to extend results from
[
12
]
to more general capillarity laws.

The Navier–Stokes–Korteweg (NSK) system reads

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\dive m=0\\
&\partial_{t}m+\dive\left(\frac{m\otimes m}{\rho}\right)+\nabla p(\rho)={2\nu}\dive(\mu_{L}(\rho)Du)+\nu\nabla(\lambda_{L}(\rho)\dive u)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad+\rho\nabla\left(k(\rho)\Delta\rho+\frac{1}{2}k^{\prime}(\rho)|\nabla\rho|^{2}\right)-\xi\rho u,\end{aligned}\right.

where
t>0
,
x\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
, the
n
–dimensional torus,
\rho
is the density,
m=\rho u
is the momentum, and the constants
\xi>0
and
\nu\geq 0
are the friction and the viscosity coefficient respectively. The Euler–Korteweg (EK) system is obtained by setting
\nu=0
in (
1.1
).
In the viscosity terms of (
1.1
)

Du=\frac{\nabla u+{}^{t}\nabla u}{2}

is the symmetric part of the gradient
\nabla u
and the Lamé coefficients
\mu_{L}(\rho)
and
\lambda_{L}(\rho)
satisfy the dissipation relation:

(1.2)

\mu_{L}(\rho)\geq 0;\ \frac{2}{n}\mu_{L}(\rho)+\lambda_{L}(\rho)\geq 0.

By
p
and
k
we denote constitutive laws for pressure and capillarity.
The pressure
p(\rho)
is connected to the enthalpy
h(\rho)
by the following relations:

(1.3)

p(\rho)=\rho h^{\prime}(\rho)-h(\rho)

which implies

(1.4)

h^{\prime\prime}(\rho)=\frac{p^{\prime}(\rho)}{\rho};\quad\rho\nabla(h^{\prime}(\rho))=\rho h^{\prime\prime}(\rho)\nabla\rho=\nabla p(\rho).

We investigate the case of non monotone pressure,
which is crucial for treating multi-phase flows. For simplicity we assume that the pressure can be split into a power law part and a compactly supported non-convex part, i.e.,
h(\rho)=\rho^{\gamma}+e(\rho)
with
\gamma>1
and
e(\rho)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,\infty)
. In view of the relation
p(\rho)=\rho h^{\prime}(\rho)-h(\rho)
in (
1.4
) the pressure term satisfies:

(1.5)

p(\rho)=(\gamma-1)\rho^{\gamma}+\rho e^{\prime}(\rho)-e(\rho)=:p_{\gamma}+p_{e}.

We will assume that the capillarity law satisfies

(1.6)

k(\rho)=\frac{(s+3)^{2}}{4}\rho^{s}\quad\text{with }-1\leq s\leq 2\gamma-3.

We will prove weak-strong uniqueness in the full parameter range

(1.7)

-1\leq s\leq 2\gamma-3

and the large friction limit in the smaller parameter range

(1.8)

-1\leq s\leq\gamma-2.

These ranges for
s
should be contrasted with the requirement that
k
be constant in
[
12
]
. In particular, our new condition covers the quantum hydrodynamics case
k(\rho)=\rho^{-1}
.

The entropy of system (
1.1
), see
[
11
,
8
,
12
,
6
]
, is defined as:

\eta(\rho,m,\nabla\rho):=\frac{1}{2}\frac{|m|^{2}}{\rho}+h(\rho)+\frac{1}{2}k(\rho)|\nabla\rho|^{2}

that is the sum of the contribution of the kinetic energy, the internal energy (
h(\rho)
) and the potential energy respectively.

The diffusive (large friction and low Mach) limit, after a suitable rescaling of time, in both Navier–Stokes–Korteweg and Euler–Korteweg equations is given by:

(1.9)

\rho_{t}=\dive_{x}\left(\rho\nabla_{x}\left(h^{\prime}(\rho)+k(\rho)\Delta\rho+\frac{1}{2}k^{\prime}(\rho)|\nabla\rho|^{2}\right)\right).

It can be obtained by performing the classical Hilbert expansion, see Section
2
.
Starting from the results contained in
[
8
]
we consider here only the dissipative model, namely the Navier-Stokes-Korteweg one, with the non-convex pressure as defined in (
1.5
).
It is important to observe that we will assume only the dissipative structure and uniform integrability for the Lamé coefficients, without any particular connection with the capillarity terms.

The fact that we can allow for a larger range of values
s,\gamma
than in previous works, in particular
[
12
]
, is due the the use of an

augmented formulation
of the NSK system (
1.1
), that has been used in
[
6
,
8
]
. In particular, we need a less stringent assumption on the capillarity coefficient
k(\rho)
than the one presented in
[
11
, Lemma 2]
.
Moreover, in
[
12
]
the authors show the weak-strong uniqueness result for Euler-Korteweg with non-monotone pressure only for constant capillarity coefficient.

Let us introduce the
drift velocity
:

v:=\frac{\nabla\mu(\rho)}{\rho},

where
\mu(\rho)
is related to the capillarity function via
\mu^{\prime}(\rho)=\sqrt{\rho k(\rho)}
. This allows us to rewrite (
1.1
) in augmented form:

(1.10)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\dive(\rho u)=0\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\dive(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla p(\rho)={2\nu}\dive(\mu_{L}(\rho)Du)+\nu\nabla(\lambda_{L}(\rho)\dive u)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad+\dive(\mu(\rho)\nabla v)+\frac{1}{2}\nabla(\lambda(\rho)\dive v)-\xi\rho u\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho v)+\dive(\rho v\otimes u)+\dive(\mu(\rho)^{t}\nabla u)+\frac{1}{2}\nabla(\lambda(\rho)\dive u)=0,\end{aligned}\right.

where

(1.11)

\displaystyle\lambda(\rho):=2(\mu^{\prime}(\rho)\rho-\mu(\rho)).

Now using the Bohm identity (see
[
6
]
) we can define a stress tensor
S_{1}
in
(
1.10
)
depending only on the capillarity part:

\dive(\mu(\rho)\nabla v)+\frac{1}{2}\nabla(\lambda(\rho)\dive v)=\dive S_{1}

and a new
momentum

J:=\rho v
such that we can rewrite the entropy as

\bar{\eta}(\rho,m,J)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{|m|^{2}}{\rho}+h(\rho)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{|J|^{2}}{\rho}

and define a new relative entropy with respect to
(\rho,m,J)
:

(1.12)

\displaystyle\eta(\rho,m,J|\bar{\rho},\bar{m},\bar{J})

\displaystyle:=\eta(\rho,m,J)-\eta(\bar{\rho},\bar{m},\bar{J})-\bar{\eta}_{\rho}(\rho-\bar{\rho})-\bar{\eta}_{m}\cdot(m-\bar{m})

\displaystyle-\bar{\eta}_{J}\cdot(J-\bar{J})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\rho|u-\bar{u}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\rho|v-\bar{v}|^{2}+h_{\gamma}(\rho|\bar{\rho})+h_{e}(\rho|\bar{\rho}).

In the forthcoming relative entropy estimates we control part of the previous relation related to non-convexity
h_{e}(\rho|\bar{\rho})
in terms of the relative energy itself and some positive error terms. We obtain a stability estimate for the convex part of
\eta(\rho,m,J|\bar{\rho},\bar{m},\bar{J})
.

The outline of this work is as follows. In Section
2
, after the appropriate time scaling, we perform the Hilbert expansion of (
1.10
) to identify the limit equation. Then we rewrite the latter as a correction of the relaxation system (
1.10
) in order to perform the relative entropy calculations. Section
3
is devoted to obtaining the relative entropy inequality, which will be used to prove a stability estimate between the two solutions in the relaxation limit.
Finally, in Section
5
we address the issue of weak-strong uniqueness.

\displaystyle\Psi_{\gamma}(t):

Orthogonal polynomials in the spherical ensemble with two insertions
Sung-Soo Byun, Peter J. Forrester, Arno B.J. Kuijlaars, Sampad Lahiry

Abstract
We consider asymptotics of planar orthogonal polynomials P n , N P_{n,N} (where deg ​ P n , N = n \mathrm{deg}P_{n,N}=n ) with respect to the weight | z − w | 2 ​ N ​ Q 1 ( 1 + | z | 2 ) N ⁡ ( 1 + Q 0 + Q 1 ) + 1 , ( Q 0 , Q 1 > 0 ) \frac{|z-w|^{2NQ_{1}}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{N(1+Q_{0}+Q_{1})+1}},\quad(Q_{0},Q_{1}>0) in the whole complex plane. With n , N → ∞ n,N\rightarrow\infty and N − n N-n fixed, we obtain the strong asymptotics of the polynomials, asymptotics for the weighted L 2 L^{2} norm and the limiting zero counting measure. These results apply to the pre-critical phase of the underlying two-dimensional Coulomb gas system, when the support of the equilibrium measure is simply connected. Our method relies on specifying the mother body of the two-dimensional potential problem. It relies too on the fact that the planar orthogonality can be rewritten as a non-Hermitian contour orthogonality. This allows us to perform the Deift-Zhou steepest descent analysis of the associated 2 × 2 2\times 2 Riemann-Hilbert problem.

1.1
Underlying Coulomb gas, random matrix ensemble and relation to planar orthogonal polynomials

Let
\{\mathbf{r}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
be
N

\frac{1}{2}
embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and centred at the origin. Let

denote the usual Euclidean length in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

N

in fact charges obeying Poisson’s equation as defined on the surface of the sphere. This means that a point at
\mathbf{x}
and a point at

\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
interact via the pair potential
-\log||\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}||
; see e.g.
[

NQ_{0}
and
NQ_{1}
at positions
\mathbf{R}_{0}
and
\mathbf{R}_{1}

Q_{0},Q_{1}>0

constant, the corresponding Boltzmann factor has the functional form

||\mathbf{R}_{0}-\mathbf{R}_{1}||^{\beta N^{2}Q_{0}Q_{1}}\prod_{l=1}^{N}||\mathbf{R}_{0}-\mathbf{r}_{l}||^{\beta NQ_{0}}||\mathbf{R}_{1}-\mathbf{r}_{l}||^{\beta NQ_{1}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}||\mathbf{r}_{k}-\mathbf{r}_{j}||^{\beta},

(1.1)

where
\beta>0
is the inverse temperature.
By rotational invariance of the sphere, it is always possible to choose
\mathbf{R}_{0}
to be at the south pole
(0,0,-\frac{1}{2})

From the viewpoint of charge neutrality in the Coulomb gas picture, it has been noted in
[
14
]
that associated with fixed charges at positions

\mathbf{R}_{0}
and
\mathbf{R}_{1}
are spherical caps of area
\pi Q_{0}/(1+Q_{0}+Q_{1})
and
\pi Q_{1}/(1+Q_{0}+Q_{1})

As already evident from potential theoretic viewpoints
[

,
10
,
20
,
43
,
21
]
the Coulomb gas exists in two phases depending on there being no overlap of the spherical caps (referred to as the
post-critical phase
), or the spherical caps overlapping (referred to as the

With
(\theta,\phi)
the polar angle, azimuthal angle pair corresponding to the point
\mathbf{R}_{1}
, we have from
[
14
, Eq. (1.7)]
that the condition for no
overlap, and thus the post-critical phase is

\cos^{2}(\theta/2)>{1\over(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+2)^{2}}\Big(\sqrt{(\gamma_{1}+1)(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+1)}+\sqrt{\gamma_{2}+1}\Big)^{2}\Big|_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma_{1}=-1+Q_{0}/Q_{1}\\
\gamma_{2}=1/Q_{1}\end{subarray}}.

(1.2)

Conversely, if this inequality is in the other direction, the Coulomb gas system is in the pre-critical phase.

Suppose now that the points on the sphere are stereographically projected to the complex plane, viewed to be tangent to the north pole; this construction is illustrated e.g. in

,

(1.3)

We have too the relation between the differential on the surface of the sphere

\mathbb{R}^{2}

[

dS={1\over(1+|z|^{2})^{2}}\,dx\,dy.

(1.4)

These two facts together give that the stereographically projected form of

{1\over(1+|w|^{2})^{\beta Q_{0}Q_{1}N^{2}}}\prod_{l=1}^{N}{|w-z_{l}|^{\beta Q_{1}N}\over(1+|z_{l}|^{2})^{\beta(1+Q_{0}+Q_{1})N/2+2-\beta/2}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}|z_{k}-z_{j}|^{\beta},

(1.5)

where

The equation (

(1.6)

Now set

\beta=2,\quad Q_{1}N=r,\quad Q_{0}N=K

(1.7)

in (
1.5

As revised in

In words the second factor is the ensemble average with respect to

The external field is strongly admissible (i.e. has has sufficient growth at infinity

\geq 0\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\Omega\end{cases}

The particular (connected) domain

There (in Prop. 2.7) the parameters

3.1
Analysis of the trajectories of the quadratic differential

We are interested in the trajectories of the quadratic differential

R(z)\,dz^{2}<0.

This in essence will give us a real measure
\mu

d\mu(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sqrt{R(z)}\,dz,

supported on the critical trajectories. Here,

represents the complex line element.

We recall from (

) and (

R(z)=\Big(\frac{Q_{0}}{1+Q_{0}+Q_{1}}\Big)^{2}\frac{(z-z_{1})(z-z_{2})(z-c_{0})^{2}}{z^{2}(z-w)^{2}(z+1/w)^{2}},\qquad c_{0}=\frac{1+Q_{1}}{Q_{0}}\frac{1}{|z_{1}|}.

(3.1)

In that regard, we have the meromorphic differential

(S_{1}(z)-S_{2}(z))^{2}\,dz^{2}=R(z)\,dz^{2}.

By general theory
[
35
,
46
,
49
]
, we have the following rules for the trajectories of the quadratic differential
R(z)\,dz^{2}<0
.

(1)

z_{1},z_{2}
are simple zeros of
R(z)
. Hence, there are three equiangular arcs emanating from
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
.

(2)

c_{0}
is a double zero of

. Therefore, there are four equiangular arcs emanating from
c_{0}
.

(3)

0,w,-1/w
are double poles with positive residues. Hence, the trajectories near

are locally circular.

(4)

As

we have
R(z)\sim c_{1}z^{-2}
, with
c_{1}>0
as a result, infinity is a double pole with positive residue. Then the trajectories near infinity are also circular, that is closed loops.

(5)

We use the Teichmüller’s Lemma
[
46
,
49
]
. A consequence of it is that a simply connected domain bounded by critical trajectories that does not contain a pole on its boundary must have a pole in its interior.

(6)

By symmetry, if
\gamma
is a trajectory, then so is
\overline{\gamma}
.

Due to item (4), the short critical trajectories are finite and do not escape to infinity. Consequently, the trajectories emanating from
z_{1}
in the upper half-plane must terminate at either

or
c_{0}
. Moreover, at most one of these three trajectories can end at

, as ensured by items (5) and (6).

In the following, we show all the critical trajectories emanating from

terminate at

.

, can then be deformed
to any contour

and not intersecting

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\partial D_{R,\delta}}z^{j}(z-w)^{\alpha}\int_{w}^{\overline{z}\times\infty}\frac{(s-w)^{\alpha+k}}{(1+zs)^{\beta+1}}\,ds\,dz\\
=\frac{G_{\alpha+k,\beta}}{2i}\oint_{\Gamma}z^{j}\frac{(z-w)^{\alpha}}{z^{\alpha+k+1}(1+wz)^{\beta-\alpha-k}}\,dz.

(4.17)

Then combining everything, we have

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\mathbb{C}}z^{j}(\overline{z}-w)^{k}\frac{|z-w|^{2\alpha}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{\beta+1}}\,dA(z)

\displaystyle=\lim_{R\to+\infty}\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\int_{D_{R,\delta}}z^{j}(\overline{z}-w)^{k}\frac{|z-w|^{2\alpha}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{\beta+1}}\,dA(z)

\displaystyle=\lim_{R\to+\infty}\lim_{\delta\to 0+}G_{\alpha+k,\beta}\oint_{\partial D_{R,\delta}}z^{j}(z-w)^{\alpha}\int_{w}^{\bar{z}}\frac{(s-w)^{\alpha+k}}{(1+zs)^{\beta+1}}\,ds\,dz.

\displaystyle=\frac{G_{\alpha+k,\beta}}{2i}\oint_{\Gamma}z^{j}\frac{(z-w)^{\alpha}}{z^{\alpha+k+1}(1+wz)^{\beta-\alpha-k}}\,dz.

Replacing

as given in (

) and rewriting

and gives (
4.1
).
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

We have

\widetilde{h}_{n,N}=-\frac{2i}{G_{n,N}}P_{n+1,N}(0)\,h_{n,N},

(6.16)

where

Proof.

We denote by
\mathcal{P}
be the vector space of polynomials of all degrees, and
\mathcal{P}_{m}
be the vector space of polynomials of degree
\leq m
. For
p,q\in\mathcal{P}
we define two pairings

\displaystyle\langle p,q\rangle_{pl}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{C}}p(z)\overline{q(z)}\frac{|z-w|^{2NQ_{1}}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{N(1+Q_{0}+Q_{1})+1}}\,dA(z),

(6.17)

\displaystyle\langle p,q\rangle_{co}

\displaystyle=\oint_{\Gamma}p(z)q(z)\frac{(z-w)^{NQ_{1}}}{(1+wz)^{N+NQ_{0}}z^{n+NQ_{1}}}\,dz.

(6.18)

The pairings
\langle p,q\rangle_{pl},\langle p,q\rangle_{co}
are associated with planar orthogonality and non-Hermitian contour orthogonality respectively. Observe that (
6.17
) only depends on
N
, while (
6.18
) depends both on
N
and
n
, although we do not include these in the notation. We define for
i,j=0,1,2,\dots

\mu_{i,j}=\langle z^{i},z^{j}\rangle_{pl},\qquad\nu_{i}=\langle z^{i},1\rangle_{co}.

(6.19)

By Proposition
1.2
the monic orthogonal polynomial
P_{n,N}
of degree
n
satisfies two determinantal formulas,

P_{n,N}(z)=\frac{1}{\det[\mu_{i,j}]_{i,j=0}^{n-1}}\begin{vmatrix}\mu_{0,0}&\cdots&\mu_{0,n-1}&1\\
\mu_{1,0}&\cdots&\mu_{1,n-1}&z\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\mu_{n,0}&\cdots&\mu_{n,n-1}&z^{n}\end{vmatrix}

(6.20)

\hskip 35.56593pt=\frac{1}{\det[\nu_{i+j}]_{i,j=0}^{n-1}}\begin{vmatrix}\nu_{0}&\cdots&\nu_{n-1}&1\\
\nu_{1}&\cdots&\nu_{n}&z\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\nu_{n}&\cdots&\nu_{2n-1}&z^{n}\end{vmatrix}.

(6.21)

One obtains from (
6.20
) and (
6.21
) by standard theory of orthogonal polynomials

h_{n,N}=\langle P_{n,N},z^{n}\rangle_{pl}=\frac{\det[\mu_{i,j}]_{i,j=0}^{n}}{\det[\mu_{i,j}]_{i,j=0}^{n-1}},

(6.22)

\widetilde{h}_{n,N}=\langle P_{n,N},z^{n}\rangle_{co}=\frac{\det[\nu_{i+j}]_{i,j=0}^{n}}{\det[\nu_{i+j}]_{i,j=0}^{n-1}}.

(6.23)

Lemma

tells us that

\langle z^{i},(z-w)^{j}\rangle_{pl}=\frac{G_{j,N}}{2i}\langle z^{i},(1+wz)^{j}z^{n-j-1}\rangle_{co},\quad i,j=0,\ldots,n.

(6.24)

The polynomials

is a basis of
\mathcal{P}_{n}

the change of basis matrix is a triangular matrix with ones on the diagonal. As a consequence we have the determinant to be
1
for this matrix. This along with (

) gives us

\displaystyle\begin{split}\det[\mu_{i,j}]_{i,j=0}^{n}&=\det[\langle z^{i},(z-w)^{j}\rangle_{pl}]_{i,j=0}^{n}\\
&=\prod_{j=0}^{n}\frac{G_{j,N}}{2i}\det[\langle z^{i},(1+wz)^{j}z^{n-j-1}\rangle_{co}]_{i,j=0}^{n},\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\det[\mu_{i,j}]_{i,j=0}^{n-1}&=\det[\langle z^{i},(z-w)^{j}\rangle_{pl}]_{i,j=0}^{n-1}\\
&=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{G_{j,N}}{2i}\det[\langle z^{i},(1+wz)^{j}z^{n-j-1}\rangle_{co}]_{i,j=0}^{n-1}.\end{split}

Therefore, by (

), (
6.25
) and (
6.26
)

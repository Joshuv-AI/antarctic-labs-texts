Naphthoquinone-derived ZSW-4B induces apoptosis in triple-negative breast cancer via AMPK signalling activation
Authors: Xiyuan Hu, Hongdou Liu, Tiao Luo, Ling Chen, Ting Peng, Min Wen, Wensong Luo, Qunfang Xu, Yuanzhu Xie, Mo Li, Mingquan Liu, Xiaohe Liu, Suyou Liu, Shuaiwen Zhu, Zizheng Zou, Zhiyong Luo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTriple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is the most malignant molecular subtype of breast cancer and is characterized by aggressiveness, high mortality, significant heterogeneity, and poor prognosis. AMPK plays a critical role in maintaining the cellular energy balance, and its inactivation is associated with malignant breast cancer. Here, we identified the pharmacological mechanism of the 1,4-naphthoquinone derivative ZSW-4B. MTT, colony formation, and nude mouse xenograft tumour models demonstrated that ZSW-4B selectively inhibits the proliferation of TNBC cells both in vitro and in vivo. Flow cytometry and Western blot analysis revealed that ZSW-4B induces apoptosis in TNBC cells. Phosphoproteomic analysis revealed activation of the AMPK signalling pathway by ZSW-4B. Additionally, the application of the CRISPR-Cas9 system to genetically knockout AMPK in TNBC cell lines was demonstrated to reverse the antitumour effects elicited by ZSW-4B both in vitro and in vivo. In summary, ZSW-4B inhibits TNBC by inducing cellular apoptosis through the activation of AMPK.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-79592-9.Subject terms: Biochemistry, Cancer, Chemical biology, Molecular biology, Molecular medicine

Introduction

Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is the most heterogeneous, invasive, and drug-resistant molecular subtype of breast cancer and is characterized by low expression of the estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), along with elevated an Ki-67 status1,2. TNBC mostly occurs in premenopausal young women under the age of 40 years, who account for approximately 15–20% of breast cancer patients3. TNBC has a high mortality rate of 40% within 5 years of diagnosis, with a postoperative recurrence rate of 25%. Owing to its heterogeneity and special molecular phenotype, endocrine therapy or molecular targeted therapy has shown limited effectiveness against TNBC4. As a result, the primary treatment approach still involves surgical resection; radiotherapy, and chemotherapy involving taxanes, anthracyclines or cisplatin5. However, the effectiveness of conventional postoperative adjuvant chemoradiotherapy is poor6. Therefore, there is an urgent need to develop new treatment strategies and targets.

AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), a pivotal intracellular energy sensor, is capable of regulating energy balance and serves as an important focus between metabolism and signalling networks. AMPK exists in the form of a heterotrimer consisting of a catalytic subunit (α) and two regulatory subunits (β and γ). Phosphorylation of Thr172 in the activation loop of the α subunit is required for AMPK activation7. Clinical pathological studies investigating the potential relationship between AMPK and breast cancer revealed that, compared to non-triple negative breast cancer cell lines the expression levels of AMPK are greater in TNBC cell lines, and AMPK is also upregulated in TNBC tissue compared with N-TNBC tissue8. Moreover, AMPK is also associated with advanced TNM staging, distant metastasis, and a high Ki67 status8. However, p-AMPK is reduced by approximately 90% in breast cancer tissues compared with normal epithelial tissues9. Taken together, these results suggest that AMPK function is compromised in primary breast cancer cells and that reactivation of AMPK function and signalling may be a potential strategy for the prevention and treatment of breast cancer10.

Naphthoquinones are widely distributed in nature and can be isolated from various organisms, including plants, marine invertebrates, fungi, and bacteria. These compounds have been reported to exhibit diverse biological activities, such as antitumour, antibacterial, antifungal and anti-inflammatory properties11. In previous studies, we synthesized and screened a class of naphthoquinone compounds, including ZSW and ZSW-4B, and found that ZSW inhibits TNBC stem cells by modulating the ubiquitination of STAT312. Here, we demonstrated that another 1,4-naphthoquinone compound, ZSW-4B, exhibits favourable antitumour effects and improved selective cytotoxicity against TNBC cells. Concurrently, it features a novel anticancer mechanism independent of STAT3, namely, the induction of cell apoptosis through specific activation of the AMPK signalling pathway.

Results

ZSW-4B inhibits TNBC cell proliferation in vitro

To confirm which subtype of breast cancer cells ZSW-4B inhibits, we treated two immortalized normal breast cancer cell lines (MCF10A and 184B5), one luminal subtype cell line (MCF-7), and four TNBC cell lines (MDA-MB-231, HCC1937, Hs875T, and BT549). The cells were treated for 48 h at the indicated concentrations, and the cell viability was analysed via the MTT assay. The survival curves revealed that, compared with normal breast cancer cell lines and luminal subtype cell lines, ZSW-4B had stronger inhibitory effects on TNBC cell lines (Fig. 1A, B). The IC50 value of ZSW-4B against normal cells and luminal subtype cell lines was approximately three to four times greater than that against TNBC cells (Fig. 1C). Similarly, ZSW-4B inhibited the formation of colonies in TNBC cells but had a lesser effect on the normal breast cell line 184B5 (Fig. 1D, E). These results indicate that ZSW-4B has a certain degree of selective toxicity against TNBC cells.

Effect of ZSW-4B on the proliferation of breast cancer cell lines. (A) Chemical structure of ZSW-4B. (B) Survival curves of two immortalized normal breast epithelial cell lines and five breast cancer cell lines treated with ZSW-4B for 48 h and assessed by the MTT assay. (C) The IC50 values of ZSW-4B against each breast cancer cell line were statistically analysed via SPSS 18.0. (D) Representative images of colony formation by different breast cancer cell lines treated with ZSW-4B. (E) The relative colony formation rate of breast cancer cells treated with ZSW-4B was determined via ImageJ and GraphPad. * p < 0.05, ** p < 0.01, *** p < 0.001.

ZSW-4B inhibits tumour growth in vivo

To further explore the antitumour effect of ZSW-4B in vivo, we established a xenograft tumour model using MDA-MB-231 cells in nude mice. When the tumour volume reached approximately 100 mm3, intraperitoneal injections of vehicle or ZSW-4B (2.5 mg/kg/day) were initiated, and the tumour volume and body weight were measured every two days. After 28 days, the mice were euthanized, and the tumours were dissected and weighed. We observed significant inhibition of tumour growth with ZSW-4B treatment (Fig. 2A–C) but no effect on the body weights of the mice (Fig. 2D). Haematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining revealed no significant damage to the mouse heart, liver, spleen, lungs or kidneys after ZSW-4B treatment (Fig. 2E). Furthermore, immunohistochemistry analysis was conducted to assess the proliferation-related antigen Ki67 in the xenografts, which revealed a significant reduction in Ki67 levels in ZSW-4B-treated MDA-MB-231 xenografts (Fig. 2F). Overall, ZSW-4B has potential antitumour effects in vivo with few side effects.

(B, C) Tumour volume and mouse weight were measured continuously over a 28-day period during ZSW-4B treatment. (D) Tumour weights were determined in nude mice treated with vehicle or ZSW-4B. (E) Representative images of haematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining of the hearts, livers, spleens, lungs and kidneys of mice treated with vehicle or ZSW-4B (scale bar, 50 μm). (F) Representative images and statistical analysis of Ki67 immunohistochemistry data (scale bar, 50 μm). * p < 0.05, ** p < 0.01, *** p < 0.001.

ZSW-4B induces apoptosis in TNBC cells

To elucidate the mechanisms by which ZSW-4B inhibits the proliferation of TNBC cells, we utilized flow cytometry to examine the impact of ZSW-4B on cell cycle progression and apoptosis. After the 24-hour ZSW-4B treatment, there was a significant induction of apoptosis in the MDA-MB-231 and BT549 cell lines (Fig. 3A, B), while there was almost no effect on the cell cycle (Fig. 3C, D). In addition, we also examined the activation of Caspase 3, which is the executor of apoptotic cell death. After the 24-hour ZSW-4B treatment, total Caspase 3 was not upregulated in the MDA-MB-231 and BT549 cell lines, but a significant upregulation of cleaved Caspase 3 was observed (Fig. 3E). These findings suggest that ZSW-4B inhibits TNBC cell proliferation by inducing apoptosis, with no significant effect on the cell cycle.

ZSW-4B promoted apoptosis in TNBC cell lines without significantly affecting the cell cycle. (A, B) Flow cytometry analysis and statistical results of apoptosis in TNBC cell lines treated with ZSW-4B. (C, D) Flow cytometry analysis of the cell cycle in TNBC cell lines treated with ZSW-4B. (E) Relative protein levels of apoptotic executor proteins were measured by Western blotting. * p < 0.05, ** p < 0.01, *** p < 0.001.

ZSW-4B activates the AMPK signalling pathway

As mentioned above, ZSW-4B can effectively inhibit the proliferation of TNBC cell lines and induce apoptosis. To further investigate the antitumour mechanism of ZSW-4B, we performed a phosphoproteomic analysis of the MDA-MB-231 cell line with or without the 24-hour ZSW-4B treatment. In this analysis project, we quantified 3989 differential phosphopeptides. We found that 78 phosphopeptides were significantly downregulated when the threshold of log2fold change was ≤ 0.67 with a p < 0.05, whereas 253 phosphopeptides were upregulated when the threshold of log2fold change was ≥ 1.5 with a p < 0.05 (Table S1). We then compiled peptide sequences exhibiting significant variation in phosphorylation levels and subjected them to KEGG enrichment analysis with the aim of elucidating the signalling pathways influenced by ZSW-4B. Our results revealed the top 20 signalling pathways influenced by ZSW-4B, with a specific focus on the AMPK signalling pathway, which is a potential target of TNBC and plays a major role in cellular metabolism (Fig. 4A). Next, we conducted a Western blot experiment to examine the Thr172 phosphorylation site of AMPKα, which is essential for AMPK activation. The results indicate that with increasing ZSW-4B concentration, the overall protein level of AMPK slightly decreased, whereas the level of Thr172 residue phosphorylation significantly increased (Fig. 4B, C). Upon analysis of Western blot experiments, we detected a significant increase in the phosphorylation levels of AMPK and cleaved Caspase 3 in the xenografts treated with ZSW-4B (Fig. 4D). IHC analysis of the xenograft tumour tissues similarly confirmed that ZSW-4B significantly increased the level of p-AMPK in the tumour tissues (Fig. 4E, F). Taken together, our findings revealed that the impact of ZSW-4B on cellular protein phosphorylation levels is enriched within the AMPK signalling pathway and that ZSW-4B activates AMPK signalling and induces TNBC cell apoptosis both in vivo and in vitro.

(B) Relative levels of phosphorylated AMPK were measured via Western blotting. (C) Statistical representation of the relative changes in phosphorylation levels in TNBC cells after treatment with ZSW-4B. (D) Effects of ZSW-4B on AMPK and Caspase-3 in xenograft tumours, as measured by Western blotting. (E, F) Representative images of the results of the immunohistochemical analysis and statistical analysis of the p-AMPK level (scale bar, 50 μm). * p < 0.05, ** p < 0.01, *** p < 0.001.

ZSW-4B induces TNBC cell apoptosis through the AMPK signalling pathway

To confirm the role of the AMPK signalling pathway in the induction of cell death by ZSW-4B, we established AMPKα1-knockout MDA-MB-231 cell lines via the CRISPR-Cas9 system (Fig. 5A). Control and AMPKα1-KO MDA-MB-231 cells were treated with ZSW-4B and subjected to cell viability assays via the MTT assay. The results indicated that the antitumour activity of ZSW-4B was significantly reduced in the AMPKα1-KO cell lines (Fig. 5B). Western blot analysis revealed that, upon knocking out AMPKα1, ZSW-4B treatment did not increase the level of the cleaved form of Caspase 3 (Fig. 5C). Similarly, flow cytometry analysis of the control and AMPKα1-KO cells treated with ZSW-4B revealed that the knockout of AMPKα1 significantly reduced the induction of apoptosis by ZSW-4B at an effective concentration (Fig. 5D, E). These results suggest that ZSW-4B induces apoptosis via the AMPK signalling pathway in TNBC cells.

ZSW-4B-induced apoptosis is dependent on AMPK signalling. (A) Western blot analysis of AMPK expression in control or stable AMPKα1 knockout MDA-MB-231 cells. (B) MTT assay for the viability of control and AMPKα1-KO MDA-MB-231 cells treated with ZSW-4B. (C) Western blot analysis of the expression of apoptosis executor proteins in control or AMPKα1-KO MDA-MB-231 cells treated with ZSW-4B. (D, E) Flow cytometry analysis and statistical results of apoptosis in control or AMPKα1-KO MDA-MB-231 cells treated with ZSW-4B. * p < 0.05, ** p < 0.01, *** p < 0.001.

ZSW-4B inhibited tumour growth by activating the AMPK signalling pathway in vivo

Next, we further elucidated the role of AMPK in the anti-TNBC efficacy of ZSW-4B via a xenograft NCG mouse model. Treatment with ZSW-4B significantly inhibited tumour growth, as indicated by tumour volume and weight, whereas knockout of AMPKα1 partially reversed the antitumour effect of ZSW-4B (Fig. 6A–C). We further explored the effect of ZSW-4B on Caspase 3 in AMPK-KO tumours obtained from NCG mice. Consistent with the findings from the in vitro experiments, the knockout of AMPKα1 partially reversed the expression of cleaved Caspase 3 in vivo (Fig. 6D). In addition, ZSW-4B decreased the expression of the proliferation-associated protein Ki67 in the NCG mouse xenograft tumour model, whereas the knockout of AMPK partially reversed the effect of ZSW-4B (Fig. 6E, F). In summary, ZSW-4B significantly activated the AMPK signalling pathway, leading to the inhibition of tumour growth in vivo.

ZSW-4B inhibited tumour growth via AMPK signalling in vivo. (A) Control and AMPKα1-KO xenografts treated with vehicle or ZSW-4B harvested from NCG mice. (B, C) Tumour volume and weight after treatment with ZSW-4B for 28 days. (D) Western blot analysis of lysates from control or AMPKα1-KO xenografts from NCG mice. (E, F) Representative images of immunohistochemical analysis and statistical results for Ki67 levels (scale bar, 50 μm). * p < 0.05, ** p < 0.01, *** p < 0.001.

Discussion

Patients with TNBC lack targeted therapy treatments and have a poor prognosis. In recent years, the development of omics technologies has facilitated the study of the comprehensive biology and heterogeneity of TNBC, enabling the identification of molecular alterations associated with TNBC13,14. New treatment targets against TNBC, such as the androgen receptor15, PI3K-AKT-mTOR pathway16, AMPK pathway17, HRD pathway18, ASS12, etc. are continuously being discovered. Some of these key signalling pathways can serve as therapeutic targets and have been approved for entry into clinical trials.

As a pivotal focus between metabolism and signalling networks, AMPK is a crucial sensor that regulates energy homeostasis at both the cellular and systemic levels19. As a central energy switch, the activation of AMPK downregulates a series of ATP-consuming processes for cell proliferation, including the synthesis of fatty acids, cholesterol and protein. Simultaneously, it stimulates catabolic pathways, such as glucose uptake, oxidative phosphorylation, glycolysis, and fatty acid oxidation20. AMPK is highly conserved in mammals, and multiple signalling pathways associated with tumour initiation and development, such as the mTOR, AKT, JAK/STAT3, and c-Myc pathways are triggered by the activation of AMPK signalling10. AMPK is active when it is phosphorylated at the Thr172 residue, a site regulated by liver kinase B1 (LKB1) and Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase β (Camkkβ)7. LKB1, a well-known tumour suppressor, activates AMPK by phosphorylating the Thr172 residue, enabling AMPK to sense changes in the intracellular AMP/ATP ratio. Camkkβ, on the other hand, exerts its kinase function by responding to intracellular Ca2+ levels, but the precise mechanism through which it activates AMPK remains unclear21.

In primary breast cancer, the phosphorylation level of AMPK (T172) is significantly reduced, and diminished AMPK signalling is correlated with an increased histological grade and axillary node metastasis9. The activation of AMPK can inhibit signalling pathways such as the EGFR, c-Myc, IL-6, and Jak/STAT3 pathways, all of which are well-established features of TNBC10. These findings collectively suggest that activating AMPK could be a potential therapeutic target for TNBC treatment. In fact, several studies have reported that activators of AMPK, such as metformin22, AICAR23, and fluoxetine24 can inhibit TNBC cells through different mechanisms. In this study, we discovered that ZSW-4B can increase phosphorylation at the AMPK-T172 site at relatively low concentrations, suggesting that ZSW-4B is a novel candidate drug for use as an AMPK activator.

In recent years, AMPK has been shown to serve as a regulatory factor in cellular apoptosis under stress conditions25. There is existing evidence demonstrating that AMPK mediates the apoptotic process through the modulation of JNK26 and p5325, the inhibition of mTOR27, or the direct enhancement of proapoptotic protein expression24. These findings highlight the diverse mechanisms through which AMPK is involved in mediating cellular apoptosis. AICAR, a class of AMPK activators, can induce the activation of JNK and Caspase 3 in hepatic cells, thereby promoting cellular apoptosis28. In this study, we demonstrated that ZSW-4B increased the expression level of cleaved Caspase 3. As a naphthoquinone compound with an electron-rich chemical structure, ZSW-4B may have the potential to interfere with oxidative phosphorylation processes and induce cellular mitochondrial stress, leading to the activation of JNK signalling and subsequently the activation of Caspase 3.

Many natural products and synthetic drugs used in cancer treatment contain naphthoquinone structures, such as doxorubicin29 and mitoxantrone30. The pharmacological mechanisms of these drugs involve two modes: (i) interacting with biological targets through the formation of covalent or hydrogen bonds and (ii) serving as electron carriers in biological metabolic processes31. The chemical structures of these drugs allow them to function as proteasome inhibitors32, hNAT1 inhibitors33, Cdc25 phosphatase inhibitors34, topoisomerase II inhibitors35, Hsp90 inhibitors36, indoleamine-2,3-dioxygenase 1 (IOD1) inhibitors37 and NQO1 inhibitors33. In our previous research, we synthesized the isoxzoloquinone derivative ZSW and reported that it exerts antitumour effects by triggering the ubiquitin-dependent degradation of STAT3, which suppresses TNBC stem cell-like properties12. Here, we reported ZSW-4B as the first naphthoquinone derivative capable of activating AMPK. ZSW-4B displays a slightly stronger selectivity than ZSW does. The differences in the mechanisms of these two compounds can be attributed to their distinct chemical structures. The synthesis route of ZSW-4B is concise, and under effective concentrations, knocking out AMPK significantly enhances cell survival, demonstrating favourable selectivity and specificity of ZSW-4B. Additionally, ZSW-4B inhibited tumour growth in a mouse model without causing noticeable toxicity, suggesting that ZSW-4B could serve as a promising clinical candidate for targeted AMPK activation in anti-TNBC therapy.

Methods

Cell culture and material

All cell lines were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC; Manassas, VA, USA). The human breast cancer cell line MDA-MB-231 was cultured in DMEM/F12 (1:1) supplemented with 10% foetal bovine serum (FBS, Biological Industries) and 100 units/mL penicillin‒streptomycin (Biological Industries). BT549, HCC1806 and HCC1937 cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 supplemented with 10% FBS and MDA-MB-468 and MCF-7 cells were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and 100 units/mL penicillin/streptomycin. The human immortalized breast epithelial cell lines 184B5 and MCF-10 A were maintained in DMEM/F12 (1:1) supplemented with 5% horse serum, 500 ng/mL hydrocortisone, 0.01 mg/mL insulin, 20 ng/mL epidermal growth factor, 100 ng/mL cholera toxin and 100 units/mL penicillin/streptomycin. All the cells were cultured in a humidified incubator at 37 °C and 5% CO2.

Cell viability assay

Cell viability was determined via the 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium-bromide (MTT) assay. The cells were plated in 96-well plates at a density of 2500 cells per well and cultured under the indicated conditions, followed by the addition of 5 mg/mL MTT (Solarbio, Beijing, China) and incubation for a minimum of 4 h. The absorbance was measured at 570 nm via a SpectraMax M2 spectrophotometer (Molecular Devices, San Jose, USA).

Colony formation assay

The cells were seeded in a 6-well plate at a density of 200 cells per well. The culture medium was replaced every 2 days, and the cells were continuously observed and cultivated for a 2-week period. Upon completion of the experiment, the cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min, followed by staining with 0.1% crystal violet for 20 min. The cloning efficiency was determined via the following equation: clonogenic rate (%) = (number of clones/number of cells seeded) ×100%.

Western blotting assay and antibody information

Proteins were extracted from the cells via RIPA buffer, separated via 10% SDS‒PAGE and transferred to NC membranes. The membrane was blocked with 5% skim milk for 1 h, followed by overnight incubation with primary antibodies. After that, the membrane was incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies at room temperature for 1 h. After each incubation, the membrane was washed 3 times with PBST. Finally, immunoblotting was performed via the Bio-Rad Chemidoc MP system, and band grayscale analysis was performed via Image Lab with the GAPDH band as the reference standard. The primary antibodies used included those against GAPDH (cat. #2118S, Cell Signalling, 1:1000), β-actin (cat. #8480, Cell Signalling, 1:1000), p-AMPK (T172) (cat. #50081T, Cell Signalling, 1:1000), AMPKα (cat. #2532S, Cell Signalling, 1:1000), Caspase 3 (cat. #14220T, Cell Signalling, 1:1000), and cleaved Caspase 3 (cat. #9579T, Cell Signalling, 1:1000).

Mouse xenograft tumour models

This study was reviewed and approved by the Department of Laboratory Animals, Central South University. All protocols involving animals were conducted in accordance with China FDA guidelines and relevant ethical standards. Additionally, this study adhered to the ARRIVE guidelines to ensure transparency and reproducibility. All efforts were made to minimize suffering and to use humane endpoints. Four- to six-week-old female nude mice were obtained from SJA Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd. (Hunan, China). MDA-MB-231 cells were washed twice with serum-free medium and collected. They were subsequently injected subcutaneously into the backs of the mice adjacent to the front limb at a volume of 100 µL, with injections administered every other day. The mice were randomly divided into different groups and treated with either vehicle or ZSW (2.5 mg/kg/d). When the average tumour size reached approximately 100 mm3, ZSW treatment was administered via intraperitoneal injection every other day. Tumour size and mouse body weight were measured every two days, and tumour volume was calculated simultaneously. At the end of the experiment, all the animals were euthanized using carbon dioxide (CO2) inhalation, and the tumor masses were dissected and weighed. The tumour volumes were calculated via the following equation: V (mm3) = a × b2/2, where “a” represents the largest diameter and “b” represents the smallest diameter.

Cell apoptosis assay

The cells were seeded in a 6-well plate at a density of 2 × 105 cells per well. After 12 h of growth, the cells were treated with medium containing ZSW-4B compounds or vehicle for 24 h. The cells were subsequently subjected to triple washing with PBS and then enzymatically dissociated via EDTA-free trypsin to collect the cells. Following resuspension in PBS, the cells were treated with Annexin V and PI. The samples were then incubated at room temperature in a dark environment for 10 min. Cell apoptosis was analysed by measuring fluorescence via a flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter, Brea, CA, USA).

Cell-cycle analysis

The cells were collected and washed three times with prechilled PBS. The cells were subsequently fixed with 70% ethanol at 4 °C for 30 min. After the ethanol was discarded, the cells were washed three more times with PBS. RNase A and PI staining solutions were then added, and the cells were stained at 37 °C for 30 min. Finally, the cell samples were analysed via a flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter, Brea, CA, USA).

Establishment of the AMPKα1 knockout cell line

The AMPKα1 cell line was established via the CRISPR-Cas9 system. The AMPKα1 sgRNA was designed via an online CRISPR design tool (http://crispor.tefor.net/). The sgRNA sequences were subsequently cloned and inserted into the pLentiCRISPR V2 plasmid, which was subsequently cotransfected with the PMD2G and PSPAX2 plasmids into 293T cells to package the virus. After 48 h of transfection, the supernatant containing the virus was collected, filtered, and then mixed with fresh medium. The target cells were treated with the virus, and puromycin was used to select the infected cells for three days. The cells were subsequently isolated via a colony formation assay. Single clones were validated by immunoblotting. The AMPKα1-sgRNA primer sequences used were as follows:

sg1 forwards: 5’-CACCGGCGTGTCACCCAGAATGTAG-3’.

sg1 forwards: 5’-CACCGGCGTGTCACCCAGAATGTAG-3’.

reverse: 5’-AAACCTACATTCTGGGTGACACGCC-3’;

reverse: 5’-AAACCTACATTCTGGGTGACACGCC-3’;

sg2 forwards: 5’-CACCGCCGAGAAGCAGAAACACGAC-3’.

sg2 forwards: 5’-CACCGCCGAGAAGCAGAAACACGAC-3’.

reverse: 5’- AAACGTCGTGTTTCTGCTTCTCGGC-3’.

reverse: 5’- AAACGTCGTGTTTCTGCTTCTCGGC-3’.

Cell transfection

Transient transfection was performed when the cell confluency reached approximately 50%. TurboFect™ Transfection Reagent was used for the transfection, following the manufacturer’s instructions. Subsequent experiments were conducted 24 h after transfection.

Phosphoproteomic analysis

The MDA-MB-231 cell line was seeded in 10 cm dishes at a density of 2 × 106 cells per dish and cultured overnight. After overnight incubation, the cells were treated with ZWS-4B for 24 h. The cells were subsequently washed three times with prechilled PBS and lysed in RIPA buffer to extract total cellular proteins, which were then stored at -80 °C until further analysis. The subsequent processing of protein samples, LC‒MS/MS identification and bioinformatics analysis were conducted at Zhongke New Life Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China).

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

IHC staining was performed on 4 μm paraffin-embedded tumour tissue sections. The primary antibodies against Ki-67 (9449, Cell Signaling Technology, USA) were diluted 1:250 or 1:500 and then incubated at 4 °C overnight in a humidified container. After three washes with PBS, the tissue slides were treated with a nonbiotin horseradish peroxidase detection system according to the manufacturer’s instructions (ZSGB-BIO, China) and photographed via a Leica DM3000 LED (Leica DMshare (v3), Germany). IHC staining was evaluated by two independent pathologists. The Ki67 and ASS1 signals were detected in the nucleus and cytoplasm, respectively. To evaluate Ki67 and ASS1, a semiquantitative scoring criterion in which both the staining intensity and positive areas were recorded was used. A staining index (0–12), was obtained as the product of the intensity of positive staining (weak, 1; moderate, 2; strong, 3) and the proportion of positive cells of interest (0%, 0;<25%, 1; 26–50%, 2; 51–75%, 3; >76%, 4), was calculated.

Haematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining

HE staining was conducted according to routine protocols. Briefly, after deparaffinization and rehydration, the tissue sections were stained with haematoxylin solution (ZSGB-BIO, China) for 5 min, dipped 5 times in 1% ethanoic acid (1% HCl in 75% ethanol) and then rinsed with distilled water. Then, the sections were stained with eosin solution (ZSGB-BIO, China) for 3 min, dehydrated with graded alcohol and cleared by xylene. The mounted slides were then examined and photographed using a Leica DM3000 LED (Leica DMshare (v3), Germany).

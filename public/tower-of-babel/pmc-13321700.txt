Perceived quality of care of medical abortions in the public health system in Catalonia (Spain)
Authors: Encarnación López-Gimeno, Gemma Falguera Puig, Cristina Martínez Bueno, Rosa García-Sierra, Ma Mercedes Vicente-Hernández, Josep Grau Galtés, Rosa M Cabedo-Ferreiro
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn Spain, induced abortions are covered by the public health system. In the region of Catalonia, under the scope of Primary Care, the Sexual and Reproductive Health Care Units (ASSIR) attend to women who choose to have a medical abortion (MA) up to 9.6 weeks of gestation.AimTo determine the perception of the quality of care received by women who have undergone a MA in the ASSIR.MethodsMulticentre cross-sectional study, in collaboration with all of Catalonia’s ASSIR units. At the follow-up visit after the MA, the women completed the SERVPERF (SERVice-PERFormance) Perceived Quality Satisfaction Scale, validated in Spanish, to evaluate the quality of care received in the dimensions of health professionals, administrative staff, organization, information, clinical aspects, impact of the process, and satisfaction.ResultsEight hundred and five (86.6%) women completed the SERVPERF MA questionnaire. The mean total SERVPERF score of participants' assessment of care was 96.68 (SD 15.6), with a maximum possible score of 130. The mean score per question across the entire scale was 3.72 (SD 0.6), with a maximum score of 5. The questions with the lowest scores were related to clinical aspects (pain, anxiety, bleeding), with a mean of 2.26 (SD 0.8), and the impact of the process (on life and family relationships), with a mean of 3.25 (SD 1). In contrast, satisfaction (I would use the service again or recommend it to a friend) obtained the highest mean score: 4.47 (0.8). Women who had previously had an abortion reported higher perceived quality (M = 98.54, SD = 14.52) than women without past abortions (M = 95.67, SD = 16.21), p = 0.013. Conclusion: The overall satisfaction score falls within a favourable range. The clinical aspects of pain, anxiety, and bleeding scored the lowest but did not correlate with a decrease in user satisfaction; participants would return to the center and recommend it to others.Keywords: Quality of health care; Induced abortion; Legal abortion; Patient satisfaction; Health care quality, access, and evaluation

Background

Induced abortion has been legal in Spain since 1985 [1]. Over the past decades, legislation has increased access to abortion [2]. With the latest amendments [3], abortions on request are allowed up to the 14th week of gestation (WG) and funded by the public health system. Furthermore, women aged 16 to 17 do not require their legal guardian's consent for the procedure [3].

A total of 103,097 abortions were registered in 2023 in Spain, at a rate of 12.2 per 1,000 women. Of these, 75% were conducted before 8 weeks of gestation. Attended persons opted for a medical abortion (MA) in 29.3% of cases; the rest opted for surgical abortion, with variations across the regions of Spain [4]. That same year, 22,307 abortions were performed in the autonomous community of Catalonia, 12,080 of which were MAs, or 54.2% of total cases [5].

The Department of Health of the Generalitat of Catalonia has established MAs as a strategic line of affective/sexual and reproductive health care [6].

Catalonia’s care model, in effect since 2010, designates the Sexual and Reproductive Care Units (ASSIR) within Primary Care as responsible for managing abortion requests and the entire pharmacological and follow-up process. MAs have been available up to 9.6 WG in Primary Care since 2022 [7].

Following these guidelines, 9,258 MAs were performed at ASSIR units in 2023, representing 76.6% of total MAs. The remainder were conducted in clinics certified by the Department of Health and CatSalut (5.7%) and in hospitals after 9.6 weeks of pregnancy (17.7%) [5].

The management protocol first establishes a initial visit at which the user’s clinical background is recorded, and she is informed of the two methods of terminating a pregnancy: medical and surgical abortions. If she chooses a surgical abortion, she is referred to a certified center. If she prefers a MA and is less than 9.6 weeks along in the pregnancy, she attends a second visit to the ASSIR unit. After signing the informed consent, an ultrasound is performed to determine gestational age. Treatment begins on the same day with the administration of 200 mg of oral mifepristone (a single dose) at the center. The user is given the remaining medication to take at home during the second phase of treatment. After 24 to 48 h, the woman self-administers 800 mcg of misoprostol (4 tablets), buccally, sublingually, or vaginally along with prescribed analgesics (administered 30 min before taking the misoprostol): paracetamol-codeine 1 g/60 mg and Diclofenac 500 mg. If the user experiences pain two to four hours after the previous dose, a rescue dose of 2 g of oral metamizole is administered. If pain occurs six to eight hours after taking the misoprostol, the initial analgesia regimen can be repeated. Once the treatment is complete, a third follow-up appointment is scheduled to clinically confirm the process is progressing well. If bleeding persists, an ultrasound is performed to assess for potential complications. Counseling on effective contraceptive methods is also offered [7].

Over the past decades, healthcare organizations, like other service providers, have set clear targets to enhance the quality of care and continue to improve. To do so, they assess not only the quality of the process, safety, and results, but also user’ sperception of service quality and subjective satisfaction [8, 9].

Under this precept, quantitative quality measurement tools have been developed, such as the 1986 SERVQUAL scale by Parasuraman [10]. Years later, Cronin [11] improved this tool with the SERVPERF scale (SERVice PERFormance), which measures quality based on customer perceptions rather than expectations [11].

The SERVPERF Scale consists of 22 items that assess perceptions across five dimensions of quality and has been widely used to evaluate all types of commercial services [12–14]. It was later used in the field of health, both in primary care [15, 16] and in hospitals [17], where it has proven reliable and valid [18, 19].

In 2022, the SERVPERF scale was validated in Spanish to evaluate the quality of care perceived by women who had undergone a MA, resulting in the 26-item SERVPERF MA Questionnaire [20].

Regarding user´s assessment of the quality of Sexual and Reproductive Health services in Catalonia, the Department of Health periodically evaluates maternal health care during pregnancy, birth, and postpartum. Regarding MA, the only available data is sociodemographic statistics and characteristics of the MAs performed in authorized public and private health centers since 2010. And so far, no evaluation has been undertaken. [21].

ASSIR units have had to make organizational changes to adapt to the demand and provision of MA services, yet the quality of care of these services, as perceived by women, has not yet been evaluated.

Therefore, this study aims to determine the perceptions of women who have undergone a MA in an ASSIR unit regarding the quality of care provided.

Methods

Study design and participants

We conducted a multicenter cross-sectional study, in collaboration with all of Catalonia’s ASSIR units from 2023 to 2025.

The primary outcome was the quality of care perceived by women who underwent a medical abortion (MA) at ASSIR centers. Secondary outcomes included identifying the ratings for 7 different dimensions of quality of care, and the sociodemographic profile of women undergoing a medical abortion.

All women over 18 years of age who had a MA at any of the 7 ASSIR units between October 18, 2023, and January 31, 2025, and who attended the final check-up visit of the process were invited to participate consecutively. Language barriers were identified by the healthcare provider. Women were excluded if they lacked sufficient fluency in the language[, eg. Spanish or Catalan] to understand the study objectives, provide informed consent, or complete the study questionnaires independently.

Sample of the study

A sample size of 385 individuals was calculated to estimate proportions with a 95% confidence level, a margin of error of ± 5 percentage points, considering a finite population of 9258 procedures performed in 2023. The software Granmo was used [22].

Data collection procedure and tools

Participants were recruited consecutively and in proportion to the population receiving MAs at each ASSIR unit between October 18, 2023, and March 31, 2025, at the post-MA follow-up visit at the ASSIR unit. At this visit, the midwives or gynecologists explained the study's objectives, provided the women with an information sheet, and invited them to participate. They were given a card with a QR code to scan with their mobile phone to respond to the survey. If users did not have a mobile phone, they were given a paper survey to complete at the end of the visit. Women under 18 years of age or with a language barrier that prevented them from completing the survey were excluded from the study. If the user did not attend the follow-up visit, she was contacted by telephone up to 2 times to inform her about the research and to send the survey link by email.

We used the REDCap platform [23] was used to collect sociodemographic and clinical data and the Spanish validated scale of perceived quality of care for MAs (SERVPERF) with 26 questions [20], via a self-completed survey by the users (QR delivered). Responses were transferred directly to REDCap. Those surveys completed on paper were transcribed by the researchers into REDCap. All data were anonymous and confidential, and researchers could not link responses to participants' identities.

A pilot test was conducted with 25 women to check the functionality of the QR code and the handling of the REDCap platform.

Variables measured

Perceived quality satisfaction

The SERVPERF MA Questionnaire [20] assesses women's perceptions of the quality of care received in MAs up to 9 weeks of gestation. It is a self-administered, the woman fills the enquiry 26 questions distributed across seven dimensions. Three types of Likert scales are used, depending on the scale's dimensions. All of them use a scoring system from 1 to 5. The response text is adapted according to the questions. It uses a Likert scale from 1 (“very poor”) to 5 (“excellent”) for the dimensions of healthcare professionals (questions 1–7, 24), with a range of 8 to 40 points; administrative team (questions 8–10, 13) and organization (questions 12, 14–16) with a range of 4 to 20 points each; and information received (questions 11, 17, 18) with a range of 3 to 15 points. The dimension of clinical aspects (questions 19–21) and the impact of the process (questions 22–23) use a Likert scale from 1 (“excessive”) to 5 (“imperceptible”), with a range of 3 to 15 points and 2 to 10 points, respectively. The dimension of process satisfaction (questions 25 and 26) used a Likert scale from 1 ("not at all”) to 5 (“most likely”), with a range of 2 to 10. Overall satisfaction is the sum of all subscales, with a minimum score of 26 and a maximum of 130 points. Higher scores indicate greater satisfaction.

Co-variables

Additional sociodemographic and clinical data were collected: age, level of education, origin, years of residence in Spain, paid work, social support (partner, family, friends, social services, no support)and living situation (alone, with a partner, with family, in a shared apartment), previous children, previous abortions, use of contraceptive method, use of emergency contraception in the current cycle, and WG when the medical abortion was performed.

Statistical analysis

All study variables were tabulated in a database accessible only to the study's research team. Anomalous data were identified for each variable, and the database was cleaned. A descriptive analysis of the categorical variables (education level, place of birth, paid work, social support, living situation, previous children, previous voluntary abortions, use contraceptive method usual and last month, use emergency contraception) was performed, including their absolute frequencies and percentages. For continuous quantitative variables (age, weeks of gestation al MA, perceive quality satisfaction SERVPERF global score and each dimension) the mean and standard deviation were calculated. To determine the association between perceived quality and participants’s sociodemographic and clinical characteristics, a bivariate analysis was performed. The Kruskal–Wallis and Mann–Whitney U tests were used for independent samples, and Spearman's Rho correlation test was used for continuous variables. In all tests, a significant difference was accepted at p < 0.05.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

In accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, this research was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of the Jordi Gol i Gorina University Institute Foundation for Primary Health Care Research (IDIAP) under code CEIm: 23/062-P, dated 28/06/2023.

Before completing the online questionnaire, all participants received full verbal and written information about the purpose of the study. All the selected women were informed and agreed to the study and provided an informed consent to participate. If they wished to participate in the study, they were asked to give their informed consent at the beginning of the questionnaire. Participants clicked “YES to agree to participate” in order to access the questionnaire. If they did not agree, they could not respond to the questionnaire. The questionnaire was anonymous and included no personal data that could identify users.

Results

A total of 930 women entered the platform (QR), but 805 (86.6%) completed the survey. The main reasons for not participating were that 32% (40) did not wish to participate, 36.8% (46) did not answer the phone call from the midwife on the day they skipped the control visit, 18.4% (23) reported other reasons, and 12.8% (16) did not complete the survey.

The mean age of participants was 28 years (SD = 7.44), and the mean WG on the day of abortion was 6.64 (SD 1.25). Regarding education level, 42.9% (334) had completed secondary school, and 32.5% (253) had attended university. Most of the women were Spanish 534 (70.5%), and 156 (20.6%) were from South and Central America. The majority (71.1%, 544) reported having paid work. As for their main social support, 66.1% (532) of participants specified their family, 64% (515) their partner, and 5.3% (43) reported having no social support. 38% (292) of participants had previous children, and 37% (288) had previously undergone abortions. Regarding the contraceptive methods used, 57% (443) of participants did not use any method in the last month. Condom was the most commonly used contraceptive, reported by 427 (57.2%) participants, followed by oral contraceptive pills in the case of 166 (22.3%). 21.6% (164) of participants used emergency contraception during the last menstrual cycle. Discussion

This study is the first conducted in Catalonia (Spain) to evaluate women's perceptions of the quality of care they received in ASSIR units after undergoing a MA. The mean age of participants was below 30 years and similar to that of other studies in the United States [24, 25] and Sweden [26]. Participants’ level of education was predominantly medium to high, unlike the study by Aiken [25], which reported a lower level of education. Regarding parity, 2 out of 3 women were nulliparous, similar to the study by Jar-Allah [26]. One third of participants who had a MA had previously had an abortion, at a rate similar to another study [25].

The mean number of WG when the participant had the MA did not exceed 7 weeks. These results show that women often request an abortion in the ASSIR units early on due to the significant impact of an unwanted pregnancy on their lives [27, 28], and so they can choose between treatment options. These results are consistent with other studies [24, 29], in which women had early abortions.

Although MA is free throughout Spain, there is significant variability in the setting where it is performed and the method used. According to the latest report on induced abortion from the Ministry of Health, the percentage of terminations using medical methods varies considerably in each region of Spain, ranging from 4.07% to 86.35%, with Catalonia having the highest rate at 52%. Furthermore, the report does not provide data on the quality of these services [30]. The SERVPERF Scale addresses various aspects related to the quality of care offered at ASSIR. The total score on the SERVPERF was 96.68, within the response range of 26 to 130. Given that the scales do not have normative cut-off points [31, 32], this can be considered positive, as it lies significantly above the midpoint of the scale. Akdere [33] considers scores above 3 per item to be favourable. In the present study, the mean score per item was 3.72.

The dimension assessing care received from health professionals included questions about the professionals' preparedness, friendliness, clarity, trustworthiness, and interest, as well as whether they felt judged; these items received high scores (a mean of 4.39 out of 5). According to Makleff [34], aspects such as dedicating time to providing information, active validation by professionals of the decision to abort, and avoiding value judgments are a few of the elements that reflect non-stigmatizing and high-quality abortion care from the health services.

Questions on the dimensions of user service and organization obtained high mean scores of 4.21 and 4.13 out of 5, respectively. These two dimensions cover topics such as accessibility, bureaucratic procedures, and coordination between different levels of care. It’s worth recalling that, in Catalonia, the legislation establishes that abortion is free of charge and forms part of the public healthcare services [3], and Catalonia has a standardized care model and protocol [7]. The ASSIR units form part of the primary care setting, the first level of care, and they all include MA care. An initial visit with the midwife must be available within 48 h of the user’s request. In this regard, and with the aim of not delaying the first visit, the protocol establishes that only professionals directly involved in administering drugs can invoke conscientious objection, without affecting or delaying the initial visit when the woman makes the request. This model aligns with WHO recommendations [35] and the Declaration of Human Rights [36]. It aims to prevent the criminalization of abortion, as identified by de Londras [37, 38] and Baier[39], which hinders access to the procedure. These range from structural (legislative, economic) and organizational aspects to the direct care provided by professionals [40, 41]. The initiative in Kyrgyzstan [42], with a model similar to Catalonia’s, showed that 99.4% of women considered the service adequate.

Moreover, questions related to the information users received about the process, contraception methods, and subsequent follow-ups received a mean score of 4.1 out of 5, in line with the study conducted in Kyrgyzstan [42], but differing from Obwegs' study in Italy[43]. Information and prior experience with MA are key aspects in perceived quality, as our results show. This is explained by the fact that women may have more realistic expectations of the process due to past experience, as they are more aware of the information, procedure, and experience than women having an abortion for the first time [44].

In our study, the dimension that scored the lowest was clinical aspects, which includes pain, anxiety, and bleeding. The mean score per item in this dimension was 2.26 out of 5. Furthermore, as mentioned in the results, more than half of the women showed moderate or excessive values in the three aspects described. Similar studies align with this evaluation of clinical aspects [45–49]. These results highlight the need for future research to improve satisfaction with the clinical aspects of MA.

Questions about the impact of the process on participants’ lives and their families also received lower scores than other questions (3.25 out of 5). Abortion is still socially stigmatized, which could make it difficult for women to communicate their decision to abort [50]. This is consistent with a US study that found 42% of women reported concern about family members' reactions [51]. In contrast, the impact was slight to imperceptible at the individual level, likely because women value the flexibility of taking the drugs at home [52].

Despite these lower scores, there was no correlation with satisfaction (I would go to the same center and recommend it to a friend), which obtained a high score (mean 4.47 out of 5). This result would be in line with other studies: Ferguson [53] reported that 87% of participants rated the side effects as tolerable, and 94% would choose the method again. Johnson [42] conducted a satisfaction survey, completed by 61% of the sample, of whom more than 99% reported high satisfaction with the service and would recommend it to a friend. According to a review by Gambir [54], women tended to choose the procedure they were already familiar with or would choose the same method in the future if the need arose. Moreover, factors such as prior experience, privacy concerns, avoidance of invasive techniques, anesthesia side effects, and the perception of being “more natural” may counteract clinical aspects [44, 52].

Implications for clinical practice

Based on the results of this study, we can identify areas for improvement, particularly in the management of pain, anxiety, and the perception of bleeding. Pain perception is multifactorial and managing it is essential to improve women's satisfaction. Therefore, a review of current pharmacological treatment guidelines at ASSIR centers has been initiated.

In addition, evaluating the incorporation of non-pharmacological pain alternatives to be used alongside pharmacological treatments would be an interesting line of research. In a literature review, Qian [55] identified diverse results for various non-pharmacological interventions for pain management and anxiety reduction, such as support measures applied by professionals (encouraging language, positive communication, physical contact, sufficient information about the whole process), relaxation techniques, meditation and breathing, hypnosis, music therapy, and acupuncture. Healthcare professionals should also be trained in these methods to offer a broader range of pain management options.

On the other hand, information about bleeding must be improved so that women have realistic expectations. Therefore, we propose developing a standardized non-pharmacological care protocol that includes training for professionals to increase overall satisfaction with MA, improving users’ experience of pain, anxiety, and bleeding.

Strengths and limitations

This is the first study to evaluate and provide data on the quality of care processes offered by ASSIR units across Catalonia and to identify the worst-rated aspects. Among the study's limitations, the information was obtained solely from women who attended post-abortion follow-up appointments. Therefore, the perceptions of women who received treatment but did not attend these appointments are unknown, and they may have had different experiences or more negative assessments. Furthermore, the exclusion of women with language barriers and those under 18 years of age limited the representativeness of the sample. On the other hand, data collection took place during the post-abortion follow-up visit, and these results may have been influenced by the relief the women felt at the end of the process. This could have generated recall bias, retrospectively attenuating the perception of some aspects of the care received and the process itself. Regarding the SERVPERF Scale, it lacks a universal normative cutoff point that objectively classifies quality levels. The interpretation of the results is often relative, based on the ranges of each response or on statistical criteria that limit comparability with other studies. Moreover, when comparing the results of other studies, the sociocultural context and the adapted items of the Scale must be taken into account.

Conclusion

Women rated the quality of care during their medical abortion favorably; however, dimensional analysis identified areas for improvement, particularly regarding pain, anxiety, and bleeding management. Despite this, satisfaction—measured by willingness to return or recommend it—exceeded 4 out of 5. Future research should focus on interventions to improve pain management, reduce anxiety, and address perceptions of bleeding.

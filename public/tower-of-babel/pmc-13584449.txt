Correction: Assessment of financial protection mechanisms for tuberculosis services in seven priority countries of the Western Pacific Region: a cross-sectional mixed-methods study
Authors: Kyung Hyun Oh, Jeungeun Seo, Yerin Yu, Fukushi Morishita, Alia Cynthia Luz, Huong Thi Giang Tran, Rajendra Prasad Hubraj Yadav, Martin P Grobusch, Hongjo Choi
Published: 2026

Correction: Tropical Medicine and Health (2026) 54:137 https://doi.org/10.1186/s41182-026-00979-7

Following publication of the original article [1], the authors identified errors in the terminology and in the descriptive text used to define the three health financing typologies that structure the comparative analysis. The typology labels and definitions are the analytical framework of the study, and the wording as published conveys statements that are inconsistent with established health financing concepts and evidence. The specific errors are set out below.

Terminology of the Type 1 typology

The Type 1 typology was labelled "tax-funded". This designation is incorrect as a discriminating label, because general government revenues raised through taxation are the predominant source of public health financing in all seven study countries, including those classified as Type 2 and Type 3. As published, the label implies that tax revenue distinguishes Type 1 from the other typologies, which misrepresents the revenue composition of the systems assessed. The distinguishing feature of Type 1 is the pooling and purchasing arrangement—a budget-financed national health service—not the revenue source. The label has therefore been corrected to "national health service-type" wherever it designates the Type 1 typology, as follows.

Parenthetical descriptors following the typology headings (Box)

Each of the three typology headings in the Box carried a parenthetical descriptor. These descriptors characterise the typologies in terms of the dominance of budget or insurance mechanisms, which is not supported by the pooling and purchasing arrangements documented in the study and reinforces the same misclassification described in section "Terminology of the Type 1 typology". The three descriptors have been deleted.

Description of purchasing mechanisms (Box)

Two statements in the Box incorrectly attribute purchasing methods to the source of financing. Strategic purchasing methods such as capitation, case-based payment and diagnosis-related groups are not specific to social health insurance; they are used in systems financed from general government revenues and administered by public agencies that do not operate as social health insurance funds. As published, the text supports the inference that a health insurance system is a requirement for progress towards universal health coverage, rather than one of several possible pathways. The text has been corrected as follows.

Statement

These corrections apply to the terminology and the definitional text of the financing typologies. The data, analyses, country classifications, tables, results and conclusions of the study are unchanged. The original article [1] has been corrected.

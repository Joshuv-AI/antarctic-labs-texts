Abstract
We analyze some aspects of the cubic action for gravity recently proposed by Cheung and Remmen, which is a particular instance of a first order (Palatini) action. In this approach both the spacetime metric and the connection are treated as independent fields. We discuss its BRST invariance and compute explicitly the one-loop contribution of quantum fluctuations around flat space, checking that the corresponding Slavnov-Taylor identities are fulfilled. Finally, our results on a first order action are compared with the existing ones corresponding to a second order action.

From Palatini to Cheung-Remmen.

One year after the first formulation of General Relativity both Hilbert and Einstein
[

[

put forward a variational principle from which Einstein’s equations could be deduced. It is instructive to discuss the subtle differences between them both. What we shall call Hilbert’s action (often called Einstein-Hilbert’s) is given by

S_{\text{\tiny{H}}}\left[g_{\mu\nu}\right]\equiv-{1\over 2\kappa^{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}\,R\left[g_{\mu\nu}\right]

(1)

(with
\kappa^{2}\equiv 8\pi G
) where it is understood that the connection from which the scalar curvature is computed is the Levi-Civita one whose components are Christoffel’s symbols associated to the metric tensor.

Einstein’s principle is given by

S_{\text{\tiny{E}}}=-{1\over 2\kappa^{2}}\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{|g|}g^{\mu\nu}\left(\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\tau\mu}\Gamma^{\tau}_{\lambda\nu}-\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\tau\lambda}\Gamma^{\tau}_{\mu\nu}\right)

(2)

and it is related to Hilbert’s one through

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{H}}}-\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{E}}}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\lambda}\left(\sqrt{|g|}g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}\right)-\partial_{\nu}\left(\sqrt{|g|}g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\lambda\mu}\right)

(3)

This means that Hilbert and Einstein Lagrangians differ by a total derivative; so that they yield the same equations of motion. We have suppressed the common factor
-{1\over 2\kappa^{2}}
in our definitions for simplicity.

This Einstein Lagrangian, although not manifestly diffeomorphism invariant, involves first derivatives of the metric tensor only, yielding a nice explanation of the (at first sight surprising) fact that Einstein’s equations only entail derivatives of the metric tensor up to second order.

The Cheung-Remmen action.

Start with the Palatini action (

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{\alpha\beta}\equiv\sqrt{|g|}g^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|g|}}g_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle A^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}\equiv\Gamma^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}-{1\over 2}\left(\delta^{\mu}_{\alpha}\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\beta\lambda}+\delta^{\mu}_{\beta}\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\alpha\lambda}\right)

(8)

Palatini’s Lagrangian now reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{P}}}\left[\mathfrak{g}^{\mu\nu},A^{\lambda}_{\alpha\beta}\right]=\mathfrak{g}^{\alpha\beta}\left(\partial_{\lambda}A^{\lambda}_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{1}{n-1}A^{\lambda}_{\lambda\alpha}A^{\tau}_{\tau\beta}-A^{\lambda}_{\tau\alpha}A^{\tau}_{\lambda\beta}\right)

(9)

[

Lagrangian, which is cubic in the fields

\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{CR}}}[\mathfrak{g}^{\mu\nu},A^{\lambda}_{\alpha\beta}]=-A^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\mu}\mathfrak{g}^{\alpha\beta}+\mathfrak{g}^{\alpha\beta}\left(\frac{1}{n-1}A^{\lambda}_{\lambda\alpha}A^{\tau}_{\tau\beta}-A^{\lambda}_{\tau\alpha}A^{\tau}_{\lambda\beta}\right)

(10)

this Lagrangian then shares the virtues of all first order Lagrangians in it being a polynomial in the fundamental fields,
A
and
\mathfrak{g}
.

This action has recently been instrumental in deriving Schwarzschild’s geometry through a clever resummation of Feynman diagrams
[
9
]
.

In fact, in order to study perturbation theory around flat space, it is convenient to make the field redefinition

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{\alpha\beta}=\eta^{\alpha\beta}-h^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle A^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}=B^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{\mu}h^{\lambda}_{\nu}+\partial_{\nu}h^{\lambda}_{\mu}-\partial^{\lambda}h_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{n-2}\eta_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\lambda}h\right)

(11)

Adding the gauge fixing term

\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{GF}}}=-\frac{1}{2}\eta^{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\rho}h_{\rho\alpha}\partial^{\sigma}h_{\sigma\beta}

(12)

the lagrangian reduces to

\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{EH+GF}}}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{hh}}}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{BB}}}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{hhh}}}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{hhB}}}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{hBB}}}

(13)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{hh}}}=\frac{1}{4}\left[h_{\mu\nu}\Box h^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{n-2}h\Box h\right]

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{BB}}}=\eta^{\alpha\beta}\left[\frac{1}{n-1}B^{\lambda}_{\lambda\alpha}B^{\tau}_{\tau\beta}-B^{\lambda}_{\tau\alpha}B^{\tau}_{\lambda\beta}\right]

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{hhh}}}=\frac{1}{4}h^{\alpha\beta}\left[\partial_{\alpha}h_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\beta}h^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{n-2}\partial_{\alpha}h\partial_{\beta}h+2\partial_{\lambda}h_{\alpha\tau}\left(\partial^{\tau}h_{\beta}^{\lambda}-\partial^{\lambda}h_{\beta}^{\tau}\right)+\frac{2}{n-2}\partial_{\lambda}h\partial^{\lambda}h_{\alpha\beta}\right]

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{hhB}}}=B^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}h^{\mu\tau}\left[\partial_{\lambda}h^{\nu}_{\tau}+\partial_{\tau}h^{\nu}_{\lambda}-\partial^{\nu}h_{\lambda\tau}+\frac{1}{n-2}\left(\eta_{\lambda\tau}\partial^{\nu}h-\delta_{\lambda}^{\nu}\partial_{\tau}h\right)\right]

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{hBB}}}=h^{\alpha\beta}\left[B^{\lambda}_{\tau\alpha}B^{\tau}_{\lambda\beta}-\frac{1}{n-1}B^{\lambda}_{\lambda\alpha}B^{\tau}_{\tau\beta}\right]

(14)

The mixing between the graviton
h_{\mu\nu}
and the connection field
B^{\lambda}_{\alpha\beta}
has been eliminated, and besides the graviton propagator

G_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}=-\cfrac{i}{p^{2}}\left(\eta_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}\eta_{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}}+\eta_{\mu_{1}\nu_{2}}\eta_{\nu_{1}\mu_{2}}-\eta_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}\eta_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}\right)

(15)

is the standard one. It is plain that the propagator of the connection field
B^{\lambda}_{\alpha\beta}
is purely algebraic. This setup is then a convenient starting point in order to perform perturbative calculations.

2
BRST invariance.

In order to study the gauge invariance of the cubic action, the best strategy is to explicit the nilpotent BRST transformations. Acting on ghost fields

\displaystyle sc^{\mu}=-c^{\rho}\partial_{\rho}c^{\mu}

\displaystyle s\overline{c}_{\mu}=b_{\mu}

\displaystyle sb_{\mu}=0

(16)

then the transformation on
\mathfrak{g}^{\mu\nu}

s\mathfrak{g}^{\mu\nu}=\mathfrak{g}^{\lambda\nu}\partial_{\lambda}c^{\mu}+\mathfrak{g}^{\mu\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}c^{\nu}-\partial_{\lambda}(c^{\lambda}\mathfrak{g}^{\mu\nu})

(17)

On the other hand, the transformation of the connection field yields

s\Gamma^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}=\partial_{\lambda}c^{\mu}\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\alpha\beta}-\partial_{\alpha}c^{\lambda}\Gamma^{\mu}_{\lambda\beta}-\partial_{\beta}c^{\lambda}\Gamma^{\mu}_{\alpha\lambda}-\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}c^{\mu}-c^{\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}\Gamma^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}

(18)

which means that acting on
A^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}

sA^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}=-c^{\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}A^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}+\partial_{\lambda}c^{\mu}A^{\lambda}_{\alpha\beta}-\partial_{\alpha}c^{\lambda}A^{\mu}_{\lambda\beta}-\partial_{\beta}c^{\lambda}A^{\mu}_{\alpha\lambda}-\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}c^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\delta^{\mu}_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\partial_{\lambda}c^{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\delta^{\mu}_{\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\lambda}c^{\lambda}

(19)

As we have already mentioned, when studying quantum perturbations around flat space, it is convenient to define

\mathfrak{g}^{\alpha\beta}=\eta^{\alpha\beta}-h^{\alpha\beta}

(20)

so that

sh^{\alpha\beta}=-\eta^{\lambda\beta}\partial_{\lambda}c^{\alpha}-\eta^{\alpha\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}c^{\beta}+\eta^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\lambda}c^{\lambda}+h^{\lambda\beta}\partial_{\lambda}c^{\alpha}+h^{\alpha\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}c^{\beta}-\partial_{\lambda}(c^{\lambda}h^{\alpha\beta})

(21)

The gauge fixing Lagrangian yields (cf. Appendix
B
)

\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{gf}}}=\bar{c}_{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{2}b^{\mu}-\partial_{\nu}h^{\mu\nu}\right)

(22)

in such a way that

s\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{gf}}}=b_{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{2}b^{\mu}-\partial_{\nu}h^{\mu\nu}\right)+\bar{c}_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}(sh^{\mu\nu})

(23)

The ghost Lagrangian reads

\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{gh}}}=\bar{c}_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}sh^{\mu\nu}=-\bar{c}_{\mu}\Box c^{\mu}+\bar{c}_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\left[h^{\lambda\mu}\partial_{\lambda}c^{\nu}+h^{\lambda\nu}\partial_{\lambda}c^{\mu}-\partial_{\lambda}(c^{\lambda}h^{\mu\nu})\right]

(24)

To quantize the theory, we shall use the field
B^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}
, defined above, rather than the field
A^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}
. The BRST transformation of
B^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}

reads

sB^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}=sA^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{\mu}sh^{\lambda}_{\nu}+\partial_{\nu}sh^{\lambda}_{\mu}-\partial^{\lambda}sh_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{n-2}\eta_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\lambda}sh\right).

(25)

It can be easily shown that on all fields the transformation is indeed nilpotent
s^{2}=0
.

The reader should also bear in mind that
sB^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}
, unlike
sA^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}
and
sh^{\mu\nu}
, has got no contribution which depends
only on the field
c^{\mu}
. A little computation shows this.

4
First order versus second order: renormalization of composite operators.

On the face of it, it seems somewhat disturbing that our results for the different Green functions and in particular for the graviton propagator are quite different from the classical results obtained with a second order formalism, given the fact that we have claimed not only that first order Lagrangians and second order ones are classically equivalent, but also that the background field gauge effective Lagrangians are equivalent at least to one loop order.

We shall argue in this paragraph that the propagator for the connection field

\left.\left\langle 0\left|TB^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}(x)B^{\mu}_{\nu\rho}(x^{\prime})\right|0\right\rangle\right|_{FO}

(44)

we have computed in the present paper, corresponds in a second order approach to the Green function of the normal composite operator
B^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}(x)
defined by using the equation of motion and Zimmerman’s
[
15
]
approach,
N\left[B^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}(x)\right]
, based in Bogoliubov’s renormalization techniques.

\left\langle 0\left|TB^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}(x)B^{\mu}_{\nu\rho}(x^{\prime})\right|0\right\rangle\left.\right|_{FO}=\left\langle 0\left|TN[B^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}(x)]N[B^{\mu}_{\nu\rho}(x^{\prime})]\right|0\right\rangle\left.\right|_{SO}

(45)

Let us focus into the first order Cheung-Remmen Lagrangian, although the argument holds for every Palatini-like first order Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{CR}}}=\mathfrak{g}^{\alpha\beta}\left(\frac{1}{n-1}A^{\lambda}_{\lambda\alpha}A^{\tau}_{\tau\beta}-A^{\lambda}_{\tau\alpha}A^{\tau}_{\lambda\beta}\right)-A^{\lambda}_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\lambda}\mathfrak{g}^{\alpha\beta}

(46)

The vacuum persistence amplitude (generating functional of Green functions) reads

Z\left[J,j\right]=\int\mathcal{D}\mathfrak{g}\mathcal{D}A\penalty\ e^{-\text{\tiny{$\frac{i}{2\kappa^{2}}$}}\int d^{n}x\left(A\Delta A-A\partial\mathfrak{g}+AJ+\mathfrak{g}j\right)}

(47)

where we have defined the kernel

\displaystyle\Delta_{\tau\penalty\ \penalty\ \lambda}^{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{n-1}\delta^{(\mu}_{\lambda}\mathfrak{g}^{\nu)(\sigma}\delta^{\rho)}_{\tau}-\delta^{(\mu}_{\tau}\mathfrak{g}^{\nu)(\sigma}\delta^{\rho)}_{\lambda}+\text{\tiny{$\left\{\mu\nu\lambda\leftrightarrow\rho\sigma\tau\right\}$}}

Here
J^{\lambda}_{\alpha\beta}(x)
and
j_{\mu\nu}(x)
are the sources of the fields
A^{\lambda}_{\alpha\beta}(x)
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\mu\nu}(x)
respectively.
We have suppressed the index in the integral in order not to clutter the formulas too much.
As a matter of fact

\left\langle 0\left|TA(x)A(x^{\prime})\right|0\right\rangle=\left.\frac{\delta^{2}Z\left[J,j\right]}{\delta J(x)\delta J(x^{\prime})}\right|_{J=j=0}

(49)

Completing squares in the integrand over
{\cal D}A
,

A\Delta A+2CA=\left[C+A\Delta\right]G\left[\Delta A+C\right]-CGC

(50)

where

2C_{\lambda}^{\alpha\beta}=J_{\lambda}^{\alpha\beta}-\partial_{\lambda}\mathfrak{g}^{\alpha\beta}

(51)

and

\Delta_{\tau\penalty\ \penalty\ \gamma}^{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}G^{\gamma\penalty\ \penalty\ \lambda}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}=\delta^{\lambda}_{\tau}\delta^{\rho\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}

(52)

The inverse kernel is algebraic

\displaystyle G^{\tau\penalty\ \penalty\ \lambda}_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{g}^{\lambda\tau}\left(\mathfrak{g}_{\mu\rho}\mathfrak{g}_{\nu\sigma}+\mathfrak{g}_{\mu\sigma}\mathfrak{g}_{\nu\rho}-\frac{2}{n-2}\mathfrak{g}_{\mu\nu}\mathfrak{g}_{\rho\sigma}\right)-

(53)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\Big[\delta^{\lambda}_{\rho}\left(\delta^{\tau}_{\mu}\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma\nu}+\delta^{\tau}_{\nu}\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma\mu}\right)+\delta^{\lambda}_{\sigma}\left(\delta^{\tau}_{\mu}\mathfrak{g}_{\rho\nu}+\delta^{\tau}_{\nu}\mathfrak{g}_{\rho\mu}\right)\Big]

in conclusion

\displaystyle Z\left[J,j\right]

\displaystyle=\text{det}G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int\mathcal{D}\mathfrak{g}\penalty\ e^{-\text{\tiny{$\frac{i}{2\kappa^{2}}$}}\int d^{n}x\left(\mathfrak{g}j-CGC\right)}=

(54)

because
-\frac{1}{4}\partial\mathfrak{g}G\partial\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{\tiny{H}}}
.

where the ultralocal term
G

5
Conclusions

In this paper some aspects of the cubic action for gravity recently proposed in
[
8
]
have been discussed. We have explicitly related it with the general first order Palatini approach, and examined in detail the corresponding BRST invariance.

In this theory the two-point function of the field
B^{\mu}_{\nu\rho}
is divergent; and besides, the counterterms needed to remove the divergence in question are not included in the tree level action, as shown by explicit computation. We have worked out the renormalized graviton propagator
\left\langle 0\left|Th_{\mu\nu}(x)h_{\rho\sigma}(x^{\prime})\right|0\right\rangle
in such a way that the corresponding Slavnov-Taylor identity is fulfilled. Also by explicit computation, we have shown that the propagator
\left\langle 0\left|TB^{\lambda}_{\rho\sigma}(x^{\prime})h_{\mu\nu}(x)\right|0\right\rangle
, which vanishes at tree-level, gets a one-loop radiative correction. This correction cannot be removed by making a local field redefinition.

All these seems a bit paradoxical, given the fact that we have claimed that first order and second order actions are equivalent not only classically but also to one loop order in a general background field gauge expansion. In fact
we have argued that Green’s functions of the connection
A^{\mu}_{\nu\rho}(x)
field computed with the cubic action

\left.\left\langle 0\left|TA_{\alpha\beta}^{\mu}(x)A^{\nu}_{\rho\sigma}(x^{\prime})\right|0\right\rangle\right|_{FO}

(56)

are directly related to the normal products of the composite operator
A_{c}[\mathfrak{g}]

\left.\left\langle 0\left|TN\left[(A_{c})_{\alpha\beta}^{\mu}[\mathfrak{g}]\right]N\left[(A_{c})^{\nu}_{\rho\sigma}[\mathfrak{g}]\right]\right|0\right\rangle\right|_{SO}

(57)

computed with the second order Hilbert action and where the composite field
(A_{c})^{\mu}_{\nu\rho}(x)
is formally defined by plugging the equation of motion for the
A^{\mu}_{\nu\rho}(x)
field given in terms of
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha\beta}(x)
.

Appendix B
The Slavnov-Taylor identity for the 1PI functional

The simplest interpretation of the Slavnov-Taylor
[
16
]
identities is that they just represent the invariance of the 1PI effective action with respect to the nilpotent BRST transformations. As such, they are a powerful check of the gauge invariance of the final results.

Let us see this in some detail.

Let
\rho_{\mu\nu}(x)
,
\rho^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda}(x)
and
\sigma_{\mu}(x)
be external fields which couple to the BRST transformations of
h^{\mu\nu}(x)
,
B^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}(x)
and
c^{\mu}(x)
, respectively. One defines the BRST variation of those external fields as follows

s\rho_{\mu\nu}(x)=0,\quad s\rho^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda}(x)=0,s\sigma_{\mu}(x)=0.

Let
\Gamma_{0}[h^{\mu\nu},B_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda},b_{\mu},c^{\mu},\bar{c}_{\mu};\rho_{\mu\nu},\rho^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda}]
be given by

\Gamma_{0}[h^{\mu\nu},B_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda},b_{\mu},c^{\mu},\bar{c}_{\mu};\rho_{\mu\nu},\rho^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda}]=S[h^{\mu\nu},B_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}]+sX+i\int d^{n}x\,(\rho_{\mu\nu}sh^{\mu\nu}+\rho^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda}sB^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}+\sigma_{\mu}sc^{\mu}),

(58)

where
S[h^{\mu\nu},B_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}]
is the classical action of the theory without the gauge-fixing term. Clearly,

s\Gamma_{0}[h^{\mu\nu},B_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda},b_{\mu},c^{\mu},\bar{c}_{\mu};\rho_{\mu\nu},\rho^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda}]=0.

(59)

Then, the generating functional of the Green functions of the theory, including the insertions of the BRST variations which are given by composite operators, reads

\begin{array}[]{l}{Z[j_{\mu\nu},j^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda},j^{\mu},\bar{\omega}_{\mu},\omega^{\mu};\rho_{\mu\nu},\rho^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda}]=}\\[8.0pt]
{{\cal N}\int{\cal D}h^{\mu\nu}{\cal D}B^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}{\cal D}b^{\mu}{\cal D}c^{\mu}{\cal D}\bar{c}_{\mu}\,e^{i\Gamma_{0}+i\int d^{n}x\,\left[j_{\mu\nu}h^{\mu\nu}+j_{\lambda}^{\mu\nu}B^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}+j^{\mu}b_{\mu}+\bar{\omega}_{\mu}c^{\mu}+\bar{c}_{\mu}\omega^{\mu}\right]}.}\end{array}

Let us denote by

\Gamma\equiv\Gamma[h^{\mu\nu},B_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda},b_{\mu},c^{\mu},\bar{c}_{\mu};\rho_{\mu\nu},\rho^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda}]

the 1PI functional deduced from

Z[j_{\mu\nu},j^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda},j^{\mu},\bar{\omega}_{\mu},\omega^{\mu};\rho_{\mu\nu},\rho^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda}]
above.
By doing standard manipulations -see
[
17
]
, one can show that the fact that (
59
) holds leads to the conclusion that the 1PI functional
\Gamma
must satisfy the so-called Slavnov-Taylor identity:

\int d^{n}x\,\left[\cfrac{\delta\Gamma}{\delta\rho_{\mu\nu}}\cfrac{\delta\Gamma}{\delta h^{\mu\nu}}+\cfrac{\delta\Gamma}{\delta\rho^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda}}\cfrac{\delta\Gamma}{\delta B^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}}+\cfrac{\delta\Gamma}{\delta\sigma_{\mu}}\cfrac{\delta\Gamma}{\delta c^{\mu}}+b_{\mu}\cfrac{\delta\Gamma}{\delta\bar{c}_{\mu}}\right]=0.

(60)

This is the equation which governs the BRST symmetry at the quantum level.

For the choice of gauge-fixing done in this paper –see section 2– the Slavnov-Taylor equation can be simplified a bit. Indeed, it is not difficult to show for our gauge-fixing choice, we have

\cfrac{\delta\Gamma}{\delta b_{\mu}(x)}=b^{\mu}(x)-\partial_{\nu}h^{\nu\mu}(x).

Then, it is sensible to introduce

\tilde{\Gamma}=\Gamma-\int d^{n}x\left(\cfrac{1}{2}\,b_{\mu}b^{\mu}-b_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}h^{\nu\mu}\right),

(61)

so that

\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}}{\delta b_{\mu}(x)}=0.

By substituting (
61
) in (
60
), one concludes that the Slavnov-Taylor equation above is equivalent to the following set equations

\begin{array}[]{l}{\int d^{n}x\,\left[\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}}{\delta\rho_{\mu\nu}}\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}}{\delta h^{\mu\nu}}+\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}}{\delta\rho^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda}}\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}}{\delta B^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}}+\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}}{\delta\sigma_{\mu}}\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}}{\delta c^{\mu}}\right]=0,}\\[12.0pt]
{\partial_{\mu}\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}}{\delta\rho_{\mu\nu}}-\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}}{\delta\bar{c}_{\nu}}=0.}\end{array}

(62)

The second equation is equivalent to the statement that
\rho_{\mu\nu}
and
\bar{c}_{\mu}
always occur in
\tilde{\Gamma}
in the combination

\rho_{\mu\nu}-\cfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\bar{c}_{\nu}+\partial_{\nu}\bar{c}_{\mu}),

which we shall call
\tilde{\rho}_{\mu\nu}
. In terms of this field, the first equation in (
62
) reads

\int d^{n}x\,\left[\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}}{\delta\tilde{\rho}_{\mu\nu}}\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}}{\delta h^{\mu\nu}}+\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}}{\delta\rho^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda}}\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}}{\delta B^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}}+\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}}{\delta\sigma_{\mu}}\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}}{\delta c^{\mu}}\right]=0.

By expanding
\tilde{\Gamma}
in the number of loops, one reaches the conclusion that the one-loop contribution, say
\tilde{\Gamma}_{1}
, to that 1PI functional must satisfy the so-called linearized Slavnov-Taylor identity:

\int d^{n}x\,\left[\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{0}}{\delta\tilde{\rho}_{\mu\nu}}\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{1}}{\delta h^{\mu\nu}}+\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{0}}{\delta h^{\mu\nu}}\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{1}}{\delta\tilde{\rho}_{\mu\nu}}+\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{0}}{\delta\rho^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda}}\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{1}}{\delta B^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}}+\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{0}}{\delta B^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}}\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{1}}{\delta\rho^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda}}+\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{0}}{\delta\sigma_{\mu}}\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{1}}{\delta c^{\mu}}+\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{0}}{\delta c^{\mu}}\cfrac{\delta\tilde{\Gamma}_{1}}{\delta\sigma_{\mu}}\right]=0,

(63)

where

\tilde{\Gamma}_{0}=\Gamma_{0}-\int d^{n}x\left[\cfrac{1}{2}\,b_{\mu}b^{\mu}-b_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}h^{\nu\mu}\right].

\Gamma_{0}
being defined (
58
).

Now, by computing the following second derivative

\cfrac{\delta^{2}\phantom{c^{\rho}(x)}}{\delta c^{\rho}(x)\delta h^{\mu\nu}(y)}

of (
63
) and then setting all the fields to zero, one obtains, upon Fourier transform, the identity in (
30
). The reader should recall that
sB^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}
, as defined in (
25
), has no contribution involving only the field
c^{\mu}
.

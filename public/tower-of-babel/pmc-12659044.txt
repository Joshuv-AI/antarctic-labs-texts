Application of scenario simulation based on boppps model in physical diagnostics——a practical exploration of integrated teaching mode for cardiac physical examination and electrocardiogram
Authors: Bingshu Wu, Tianran Chen, Xiaobin Mei, Wenqin Yue, Dan Wang, Maojin Xu, Yuchao Dong, Zhenli Hu, Mingzhen Ying
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposePhysical diagnosis is a bridging course that connects basic medicine and clinical medicine. This article took cardiac physical examination as an example, based on the student-centered BOPPPS(the bridge-in, objective, preassessment, participatory learning, post-assessment, and summary)model teaching, using imported CBL model (Case-Based Learning) and combined with scenario simulation to fully integrate students into the classroom, enhancing the mastery of theoretical knowledge and the ability to apply it in practice.MethodThe study focused on undergraduate students majoring in clinical medicine from the 2022 cohort of our university. The control group consisted of 38 students who received traditional teaching methods, while the experimental group consisted of 38 students who received blended learning using the BOPPPS model and scenario simulation method. They were evaluated by formative assessment (35%), summative assessment (65%), and overall score. Teaching satisfaction were assessed the form of a questionnaire survey.ResultsThe assessments showed that the experimental group scored higher than the control group in the summative assessment and overall score (76.68 ± 11.20 vs 69.25 ± 10.97 and 76.46 ± 8.95 vs 70.62 ± 8.80), and the differences were statistically significant (P < 0.05). The results of the teaching satisfaction questionnaire survey indicated that the experimental group scored higher than the control group in terms of teachers' teaching attitude, teaching ability, teaching content, teaching methods, and teaching effectiveness, and all had statistical differences (P < 0.05). There was no statistically significant difference in scores between the two groups in formative assessment.ConclusionsThe BOPPPS teaching model, when integrated with case-based learning and scenario simulation in physical diagnostics instruction, was associated with higher summative and total scores as well as greater student satisfaction compared to traditional instruction. Although no significant difference was observed in formative assessment performance between groups, the approach may help support student engagement and self-directed learning ability. Overall, participants reported higher satisfaction with this teaching model.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12909-025-08244-x.Keywords: BOPPPS teaching model, Physical diagnostics, Practical teaching, Scenario simulation

Introduction

Physical Diagnostics is a bridging course that connects theoretical knowledge and clinical competence. Physical diagnostics represents a pivotal transition from basic medical sciences to clinical practice, equipping students with essential skills to transform theoretical knowledge into applied patient care. This discipline cultivates rigorous scientific reasoning and innovative problem-solving abilities through systematic clinical training [1]. Physical examination-the cornerstone of diagnostic medicine-serves as both a fundamental physician competency and a critical milestone in medical education, marking the shift from passive learning to active clinical engagement [2].

In alignment with China's national initiative for undergraduate curriculum reform-emphasizing advanced competencies, innovation, and academic challenge-our internal medicine education team has redefined physical diagnostics instruction. Leveraging Shanghai's key curriculum development program, we integrated modern educational technology with a novel BOPPPS-Scenario Simulation hybrid model [3–5]. This framework emphasizes situational adaptability via immersive scenarios, structured skill acquisition through evidence-based design, and comprehensive assessment of diagnostic proficiency. Based on the BOPPPS model from Canada’s Instructional Skills Workshop, it offers a modular, learner-centered approach to clinical education [6, 7]. Our implementation with medical students demonstrated improvements in diagnostic accuracy, technical examination skills, and patient-centered communication emphasizing empathy and ethics, supporting its utility within competency-based curricula [8].

Methods

Study participants and randomization

The study randomly selected clinical medicine students from the Naval Medical University of 2022. Eligible participants were allocated to either the intervention or control group based on computer-generated random numbers by an independent researcher to ensure allocation concealment. Due to the educational nature of the intervention, the study adopted an open-label (non-blinded) design; thus, neither students nor instructors were blinded to group assignment. Consequently, grading was also not performed under blinded conditions. To mitigate potential bias in outcome assessment, rigorous safeguards were implemented, including the use of detailed evaluation rubrics and double-marking by two independent assessors for all critical performance measures.

Intervention and control protocols

The textbooks used in both groups were "Diagnosis" (10th edition) edited by Wan Xuehong and Lu Xuefeng and published by People's Health Press, and "Physical Diagnosis" (2nd edition) edited by Xu Maojin and Xu Xiaolu in our teaching and research office and published by Shanghai Science and Technology Press. All courses were taught by the same senior teaching team. The experimental group completed the teaching process of the BOPPPS model in the massive open online courses (MOOC) and pre-class introduction stages, and arranged scenario simulations around the knowledge points.

Control group teaching

Participants in the control group received 8 h of traditional, didactic lecture-based instruction, consistent with the teaching syllabus and curriculum plan. The content mirrored that of the intervention group, covering the standard curriculum for “Cardiac Physical Examination and Electrocardiogram”. Instruction was delivered via PowerPoint presentations and videos by the same instructor as in the intervention group to control for teacher-related variables. Teaching was primarily unidirectional, grounded in textbook materials, with optional pre-class previews and randomly integrated questioning and discussion sessions during class. A structured 10-min Q&A session was included at the end of each hour. Formative assessments were conducted upon completion of each unit, and students completed instructional evaluation questionnaires.

Experimental group teaching

According to the requirements of the teaching syllabus and curriculum plan, the BOPPPS teaching model was applied to the entire teaching process. Different stages of teaching tasks were set for students before, during, and after class, in order to better integrate the taught course content (Fig. 1).

After admission, the student was required to undergo a cardiac examination and electrocardiogram (ECG) operation (provide ECG images and diagnosis). c. Objective

a. Knowledge objective: Students should master the diagnosis of atrial fibrillation through cardiac physical examination and electrocardiogram, be familiar with disease diagnosis ideas, and understood the basic principles of clinical thinking. b. Ability objective: Students should be able to collect and analyze data according to diagnostic steps, summarize medical history characteristics, and cultivate their rigorous diagnostic thinking ability. It was important to guide students to study and apply the viewpoints and methods of dialectical materialism, in order to understand, analyze, and solve problems. c. Quality objective: Students were cultivated for rigorous and pragmatic scientific spirit, professional attitude, and respect for life and humanistic care.

Pre-assessment

Based on the study of the MOOC one week ago, open-ended short answer questions will be set up one day before class to stimulate students' thinking and interest. Students previewed the relevant learning content in advance and made sufficient preparations for the pre-class knowledge. We set three questions as follows: a. Which system was considered to be the cause of the patient's "palpitations"? b. Recall the steps and precautions for a physical examination of the heart. c. What were the operating steps and diagnostic methods of electrocardiogram?

Participant learning

Participatory learning is the core component of the BOPPPS model and also the key link in the integration of the CBL teaching method combined with scenario simulation and the BOPPPS model. The teacher first demonstrates the content and precautions of medical history taking through slide presentations, introduces the content of cardiac physical examination and the usage and precautions of electrocardiogram (ECG) equipment, etc. Students are divided into two groups, A and B (each group consisting of 6–8 people). One student from each group is randomly selected to conduct the main medical history taking or operation, with other group members providing supplementary information, while the other group observes, learns, and evaluates.

In this teaching segment, the teacher acts as a standardized patient, and the student from Group A conducts the simulated medical history taking. Based on the teaching content and combined with clinical typical cases, case-related questions are set up in a targeted manner for interactive question-and-answer teaching, with other group members supplementing the medical history taking. After the medical history taking is completed, students from Group B evaluate (including content, posture, language, and other doctor-patient communication methods) and supplement the medical history taking content related to the condition.

Post-assessment

In order to understand students' learning effectiveness and whether they had achieved their learning goals, teachers took a post test as the result of formative assessment (synchronized with the control group) every time a small unit or paragraph was completed in the course.

The post assessment methods for different teaching objectives could be divided into four categories: knowledge understanding, application analysis, skill mastery, and attitude value. First, knowledge understanding focused on the memorization and recognition of basic knowledge, commonly using methods such as multiple-choice questions and fill in the blank questions, which were suitable for classroom tests or standardized exams. Second, application analysis emphasized knowledge transfer and problem solving, and tests students' analytical abilities in practical scenarios through situational analysis, case discussions, and other forms, making it suitable for project-based learning. Third, skill mastery focused on practical skills, using skill demonstrations, group exercises, and scales to assess quantitative technical proficiency, often used in skill training. Last but foremost, attitude value evaluated students' values and emotional tendencies through methods such as questionnaires, reflection logs, or debates, and is commonly used in social issue courses.

Teaching evaluation

Questionnaire survey

Questionnaires of 76 copies were randomly distributed and collected, with a validity rate of 100%. Except for the basic situation, each measurement item value consisted of 1 (strongly disagree), 2 (disagree), 3 (neutral), 4 (agree), and 5 (strongly agree).

Final exam score evaluation

The assessment adopted a combination of formative assessment and summative assessment. Formative assessment accounted for 35%, including physical examination (10%), collection and writing of medical history (15%), and cardiopulmonary auscultation (10%). When collecting medical history, attention should be paid to the assessment of doctor-patient communication. The final assessment was a theoretical exam at the end of the course, which adopted a closed book written test method, accounting for 65%. The question types included multiple choice questions, fill in the blank questions, noun explanation questions, short answer questions, and electrocardiogram analysis questions. The proportion of questions related to the comprehensive application of knowledge was not less than 50%. Those who failed the summative assessment resulted in a failure in the overall course grade.

Statistical methods

All measurement data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. The assumptions of normality (assessed by the Shapiro-Wilk test) and homogeneity of variance (assessed by Levene’s test) were evaluated first. Data for age, teaching satisfaction, and scores between the two groups met the assumptions of normality and homogeneity of variance; therefore, independent samples t-tests were used for comparisons. Mean differences with 95% confidence intervals (95% CI) were also reported. Categorical data were analyzed using the chi-square test. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 26.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). All tests were two-tailed, and the significance level was set at P < 0.05.

Results

Exam results

Through horizontal analysis of the grades of the experimental group and the control group, we found that the experimental group was significantly better than the control group in formative assessment, summative assessment, and overall grades. Specifically, the average formative assessment scores of the control group and the experimental group were 73.16 ± 7.32 points and 76.05 ± 7.23 points, respectively. The average final assessment scores were 69.25 ± 10.97 points and 76.68 ± 11.20 points, respectively. The total average scores were 70.62 ± 8.80 points and 76.46 ± 8.95 points, respectively. The difference in final assessment (P = 0.0046) and total score (P = 0.0053) was statistically significant (Table 1). In addition, the visualization analysis of the score distribution bar chart also intuitively shows that the score distribution of the experimental group is more concentrated, with more high segments (Fig. 2). These results indicate that the teaching method of the experimental group may be more effective, providing strong data support for subsequent teaching improvement.

Survey results

This study utilized the Student Evaluation of Faculty Form and the Learning Habits Questionnaire. Both instruments have been extensively utilized and validated among the student population at our institution, demonstrating strong reliability and validity. In the current research, the original structure of these scales was retained. Reliability analysis based on standardized items yielded a Cronbach's α coefficient of 0.876, confirming good internal consistency of the survey instruments. Firstly, the data includes general information, including gender, whether pre class preparation has been conducted, whether post class review has been conducted, and employment intentions after graduation (Table 2). Secondly, a questionnaire survey will be conducted on the teacher's teaching attitude, teaching ability, teaching content, teaching methods, and teaching effectiveness. The results showed that there were statistically significant differences in teachers' teaching attitudes (P < 0.0001), teaching abilities (P = 0.0011), teaching content (P < 0.0001), teaching methods (P < 0.0001), and teaching effectiveness (P < 0.0001) (Table 3). Because there was no plan for adjustment for multiple comparisons of secondary outcomes, these results are presented as point estimates with multiplicity-unadjusted confidence intervals, from which no clinical inferences can be made.

Discussion

Empirical studies demonstrate that the BOPPPS model outperforms traditional pedagogy across multiple educational domains. The BOPPPS framework elevated self-directed learning capacity and, when integrated with team-based learning (TBL), significantly enhanced critical thinking and problem-solving proficiency [11]. The BOPPPS (Bridge-in, Objective, Pre-assessment, Participatory Learning, Post-assessment, Summary) instructional model yields significantly superior outcomes compared to conventional lecture-based learning (LBL). In theoretical knowledge acquisition and practical skill development, BOPPPS cohorts exhibited markedly higher examination scores, with statistically validated improvements in foundational nursing courses [8]. Clinical skill assessments in surgical and urology training further confirmed the model’s efficacy, with BOPPPS groups surpassing traditional cohorts [12].In disciplines requiring clinical reasoning-such as pathology and ophthalmology-the BOPPPS-CBL (case-based learning) hybrid amplified students’ analytical abilities [6, 12].

Cross-disciplinary applications consistently affirmed the model’s robustness [6, 8, 11, 12]. Student satisfaction with BOPPPS reached 89%, with some studies reporting 100% approval rates [13]. Participants particularly endorsed its strengths in fostering instructor-student interaction, active learning, and course engagement [14, 15]. Hybrid BOPPPS (HBOPPPS), incorporating digital platforms, obtained students’ positive assessments and contributed to the improvement of their competencies [16]. To investigate the practical application scenario of the BOPPPS model in Physical Diagnostics, we conducted this controlled study focusing on the integrated teaching model of cardiac physical examination and electrocardiogram (ECG). As a core medical course typically delivered in the third undergraduate year, physical diagnostics served as the testbed for this investigation. The control group received traditional didactic instruction, while the experimental cohort underwent a blended "BOPPPS + scenario simulation" intervention. This approach embedded clinical cases and problem-solving throughout the curriculum, augmented by online-offline hybrid modules to optimize engagement.

Analysis and discussion of assessment outcomes

Horizontal comparison of academic performance revealed that the experimental group outperformed the control group across summative and overall assessments. Notably, however, while the experimental group showed higher average scores on the formative assessment, this difference did not reach statistical significance. In contrast, significant differences favoring the experimental group were observed in the summative examination and total score (P < 0.01).

The absence of a statistically significant advantage in the formative assessment warrants careful interpretation. Formative tasks-including physical examination (10%), history taking and documentation (15%), and cardiopulmonary auscultation (10%)-emphasize procedural skills, communication, and clinical reasoning, all of which require repeated practice and contextual integration. One possible explanation is that the BOPPPS and simulation-based approach, though effective in improving conceptual understanding and examination performance, may require a longer period of immersion and deliberate practice to manifest significant effects in complex, real-time clinical skills. Additionally, the relatively subjective nature of skill-based assessments-compared to standardized written tests-may introduce greater variability, reducing statistical power to detect differences.

Another consideration is the weighting of assessment components: the summative exam accounted for 65% of the total score, heavily influencing the overall outcome. This may indicate that the intervention more strongly impacted competencies measured under exam conditions, such as factual recall and structured problem-solving, compared to open-ended clinical tasks. It is also possible that the formative assessment tools themselves lacked sensitivity to nuanced improvements in communication or integrative skill development.

Potential confounders, such as variations in individual student motivation, prior clinical exposure, or group-specific dynamics, cannot be entirely ruled out. Furthermore, instructor expectations or subtle biases in evaluating practical performance may have diluted between-group differences.

From a pedagogical perspective, these mixed results underscore the need for more nuanced and longitudinal evaluation strategies when assessing educational interventions aimed at integrating knowledge with practice. While the model clearly enhanced outcomes in theoretical domains, its effect on immediate practical performance remains less pronounced. This suggests that future iterations could benefit from strengthened simulation components, more deliberate communication training, and the use of objective structured clinical examinations (OSCEs) to better capture competencies in clinical and communicative skills.

Structural advantages and implementation insights

The six-phase BOPPPS framework-integrating objectives, behavioral outcomes, and evaluative metrics-produced measurable gains in theoretical examination performance. Qualitative feedback highlighted elevated learner interest, efficiency, and autonomy, attributable to the model’s interactive, application-oriented design [15–17].

Limitations

This study has several important limitations that should be considered when interpreting the results. The single-center design and relatively small sample size (n = 76) may restrict the generalizability of the findings and reduce statistical power. Furthermore, the absence of blinding for both instructors and participants could introduce performance and assessment bias, particularly since the same teaching team was responsible for both intervention delivery and outcome evaluation; although procedures were standardized, some degree of observer bias cannot be entirely excluded. As a quasi-experimental study, despite pretest confirmation of baseline equivalence between groups, unmeasured confounders such as student motivation and group dynamics may still influence outcomes. Additionally, potential year-to-year variability in examination content and difficulty was not formally modeled, which may affect the reproducibility of the results. Finally, the lack of longitudinal follow-up precludes assessment of long-term knowledge and skill retention. Future studies should therefore implement multicenter, large-sample, randomized controlled trials that incorporate long-term follow-up and blinded outcome assessment where feasible to further validate the sustained impact of the instructional model.

Finally, the analysis of both the Student Evaluation of Faculty Form and the Learning Habits Questionnaire entailed multiple comparisons across several outcome domains, which elevates the risk of Type I error. As the reported p-values are unadjusted, the corresponding findings should be interpreted as exploratory rather than confirmatory.

Future

Looking ahead, this study underscores the imperative to advance beyond conventional assessment paradigms toward more nuanced and clinically contextualized evaluation frameworks. Future research should prioritize the development, validation, and integration of instruments capable of capturing complex competencies-particularly in practical skill execution, clinical communication, and adaptive reasoning-which remain inadequately reflected in traditional formative scoring. The incorporation of Objective Structured Clinical Examinations (OSCEs) [18, 19], blinded direct observation by trained evaluators, and standardized patient interactions would not only enhance the reliability and granularity of skill assessment but also help uncover educational outcomes obscured by theory-heavy examinations. Furthermore, longitudinal studies are essential to ascertain whether gains in exam performance correlate with sustained clinical proficiency and professional readiness. Only through such multidimensional and critically designed assessment strategies can we thoroughly evaluate and refine innovative pedagogical models in cultivating clinically competent and reflective practitioners.

To systematically equip educators with mastery of the BOPPPS model, a practice-oriented developmental program can be designed comprising theoretical introduction, applied practice, and sustained support. It begins with an immersive workshop combining module explanation and cross-disciplinary teaching exemplars, followed by the core component-recorded microteaching sessions where participants deliver and receive feedback including learning experiences and perceived obstacles on a BOPPPS-based mini-lesson. Subsequently, a peer learning community provides ongoing collaboration and reflection. This program culminates in a certification based on evaluated lesson plans and teaching videos, forming a “theory–practice-feedback-community-certification” cycle that effectively enhances both competence and confidence in real-world teaching applications, ultimately elevating instructional quality. In the future, this approach will be further realized through the integrated application of an AI-assisted multi-advisor system combined with the BOPPPS model in Physical Diagnostics [20].

Conclusion

The BOPPPS model, combined with case simulations and digital tools, helped reduce spatiotemporal barriers in clinical teaching and promoted a shift toward facilitator-led instruction and active student participation. This integration was associated with increased student engagement and improved clinical reasoning and operational skills. Although significant improvements were observed in summative exam performance-suggesting benefits for cognitive and test-preparedness-the lack of significant difference in formative assessments highlights ongoing challenges in translating instructional innovation into consistent practical skill acquisition. These findings support the need for further refinement in both teaching implementation and skill-focused evaluation.

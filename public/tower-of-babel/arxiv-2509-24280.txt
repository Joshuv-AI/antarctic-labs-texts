Abstract
We give a size-aware minimum description length (MDL) cost and a window-stable 3 XOR → \to 3 SAT chain with controlled losses in total variation (TV) and instance size. Core components are minimax monotonicity ( Theorem 4.4 ), gadget composition (XOR and Index; Theorem 6.9 ), and a sum-of-squares (SoS) entropy dichotomy ( Theorem 7.9 ) at k = Θ ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) k=\Theta(\log n) . We present a composition pipeline (E1–E4) for distributional transfers with explicit accounting for TV, size, and meta overheads. For symbol conventions and standing assumptions, see Sec. 2 . All guarantees are distributional (on a calibrated window); we make no worst-case claims. Relation to IECZ-I. This sequel builds on our companion work [ 10 ] , which introduced the size-aware MDL functional and the distribution-preserving reduction principle (A2d) together with a δ = 0 \delta\!=\!0 injective 3XOR → \to 3SAT translation; here we develop the minimax principle, gadget composition, and the SoS dichotomy.

Lemma 4.1

(Prefix-free meta-overhead (explicit
\delta
-code))
.

Fix Elias–
\delta
for all integer fields.
Let
R
be any polynomial-time map on instances of length
N
, whose meta-parameters
t_{1},\dots,t_{m}
each lie in a range
[0,T_{i}(N)]
with
T_{i}(N)\leq N^{k_{i}}
for some constants
k_{i}\geq 0
.
Then, writing
\ell_{\delta}(\,\cdot\,)
for the Elias–
\delta
length in bits,

\ell_{\delta}(t_{i})\ \leq\ \lfloor\log_{2}\max\{1,t_{i}\}\rfloor\ +\ 2\,\big\lfloor\log_{2}\bigl(1+\lfloor\log_{2}\max\{1,t_{i}\}\rfloor\bigr)\big\rfloor\ +\ 1,

and hence, for all
N\geq 16
and all
i
with
t_{i}\leq T_{i}(N)\leq N^{k_{i}}
,

\ell_{\delta}(t_{i})\ \leq\ k_{i}\,\log_{2}N\ +\ 2\log_{2}\log_{2}N\ +\ 3\ \ \leq\ \ (k_{i}{+}1)\,\log_{2}N.

Consequently, for any constant-arity template (constant
m
) one has the total meta-overhead

\len(\mathrm{code}(R))\ =\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}\ell_{\delta}(t_{i})\ +\ O(1)\ \leq\ \Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}+1\Bigr)\,\log_{2}N\ =\ O(\log N).

The same bound holds for any finite tuple of such parameters encoded by prefix-free concatenation.

Proof.

For Elias–
\delta
, the length of a positive integer
t\geq 1
is

\ell_{\delta}(t)=\lfloor\log_{2}t\rfloor\ +\ 2\lfloor\log_{2}(1+\lfloor\log_{2}t\rfloor)\rfloor\ +\ 1,

see
[
2
]
. Allow
t=0
by coding
\max\{1,t\}
(adds at most one bit).
If
t\leq N^{k_{i}}
, then
\lfloor\log_{2}t\rfloor\leq k_{i}\log_{2}N

\log_{2}(1+\lfloor\log_{2}t\rfloor)\leq\log_{2}\log_{2}N^{k_{i}}\leq\log_{2}\log_{2}N+\log_{2}k_{i}\leq\log_{2}\log_{2}N+1

(for fixed

and all
N\geq 16

\ell_{\delta}(t_{i})\ \leq\ k_{i}\log_{2}N+2\log_{2}\log_{2}N+3\ \leq\ (k_{i}{+}1)\log_{2}N.

Summation over a constant number
m
of fields and adding the constant template/tag overhead gives the displayed bounds.
Prefix-freeness is by construction of Elias–
\delta
and stability under concatenation of self-delimiting fields.

4.2
Window discipline

Lemma 4.2

(TV is 1-Lipschitz under deterministic maps)
.

For any distributions
\mu,u
on the same finite space and any deterministic map
R
,

\TV(R\mathop{}\!_{\#}\mu,R\mathop{}\!_{\#}u)\leq\TV(\mu,u).

Proof.

Write
\TV(\mu,u)=\sup_{A}(\mu(A)-u(A))
, where the supremum is over measurable sets.
For any measurable
B
in the codomain,
(R\mathop{}\!_{\#}\mu)(B)-(R\mathop{}\!_{\#}u)(B)=\mu(R^{-1}(B))-u(R^{-1}(B))
,
so taking the supremum over
B
is a supremum over preimages
R^{-1}(B)
. Hence the supremum cannot increase:

\TV(R\mathop{}\!_{\#}\mu,R\mathop{}\!_{\#}u)\leq\TV(\mu,u)
.
∎

(Role of

Our results require only that

Lemma 4.3

(Subadditivity of the per-instance loss)
.

Let

be the per-instance loss for a fixed model

at target
\varepsilon

Assume the coding convention of Sec.

. Then for any two instances

Proof.

Concatenate the two descriptions produced by

By prefix-free decodability (Sec.

Theorem 4.4

(Minimax monotonicity)
.

Let

Proof.

Let

Lemma 6.2

(Interface normal form over

Moreover, letting

A\;:=\;\begin{bmatrix}\beta_{1}^{\top}\\
\beta_{2}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2\times 3},\qquad\mathbf{r}\;:=\;\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{1}\\
\alpha_{2}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2},\qquad\mathbf{1}:=(1,1)^{\top},

the parity constraint is equivalent to

\mathbf{1}^{\top}A\;=\;(1,1,1)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mathbf{r}\;=\;0,

Proof.

\mathbb{F}_{2}

q_{1}\equiv q_{2}=:q

Index gadget (constant fan-in, affine, degree-stable).

We isolate a tiny
Index
block that routes one of two data bits according to a 1-bit address.
Let inputs be
(u;v_{0},v_{1})\in\{0,1\}^{3}
and outputs

defined by

t\;=\;(1\oplus u)\,v_{0}\;\oplus\;u\,v_{1}.

Over
\mathbb{F}_{2}

t\;=\;v_{0}\;\oplus\;u\,(v_{0}\oplus v_{1})\;=\;v_{0}\;\oplus\;(u\cdot(v_{0}\oplus v_{1}))\;=\;\underbrace{v_{0}}_{\text{linear}}\;\oplus\;\underbrace{(u\cdot v_{0})}_{\text{bilinear}}\;\oplus\;\underbrace{(u\cdot v_{1})}_{\text{bilinear}}.

In our degree–aware setting, the block is of constant size and can be treated as an affine pullback on monomials: composition with the index map increases the total degree by at most a constant factor (here
\leq 2
), which is absorbed in the
k=\Theta(\log n)
regime.

Lemma 6.4

(Index pullback with constant degree factor)
.

Let

act blockwise on disjoint triples
(u;v_{0},v_{1})
to produce outputs
t
as above,
and let

be a multilinear polynomial in the
t

\deg p\leq k

Then there exists a multilinear

in the input variables

Consequently, for any input distributions
(\mu,u)
on the window and any

\Delta_{k}\!\bigl(G_{\mathrm{idx}\,\#}\mu,\;G_{\mathrm{idx}\,\#}u\bigr)\ \leq\ \Delta_{2k}(\mu,u).

Sup-norm note.
Since

is evaluated only on the image

\displaystyle\|q\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x}|p(G_{\mathrm{idx}}(x))|\leq\sup_{y}|p(y)|=\|p\|_{\infty}

Corollary 6.5

(Index monotonicity of low-degree discrepancy)

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and any input distributions
(\mu,u)
on the window,

\Delta_{k}\!\bigl(G_{\mathrm{idx}\,\#}\mu,\;G_{\mathrm{idx}\,\#}u\bigr)\ \leq\ \Delta_{2k}(\mu,u).

Corollary 6.6

(Index transfer with budget rescaling)
.

(Concrete budget.) For the Index gadget the degree budget dilates by at most a factor

The lemma shows that substituting the index gadget inflates test degree by at most a factor

∎

Correlation shielding via low-degree noise stability

We formalize the “
\rho<1
” stability assumption by an
L_{2}
-type correlation bound.

Definition 6.1

(Windowed correlation)
.

For random variables
X,Y
defined on the window law, set

\corr(X,Y)\ :=\ \frac{\Cov(X,Y)}{\sqrt{\Var(X)\,\Var(Y)}}\quad\text{when well-defined}.

A gadget
G
has
shielding parameter

\rho<1
if for each output
s_{\ell}
and any
exogenous
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{Z}
(independent outside the local fan-in) we have

\bigl|\corr\bigl(s_{\ell},\mathbb{E}[\,\cdot\mid\mathcal{Z}\,]\bigr)\bigr|\leq\rho+o(1)
on
\mathsf{Win}
.

Definition 6.2

(Mixing witness)
.

A gadget
G
admits a
mixing witness
with rate
\theta\in(0,1)
on the window
\mathsf{Win}

if there exists a linear operator
\mathcal{M}
on degree-
\leq 1
statistics of the outputs such that:

1.

\mathcal{M}
preserves means and contracts centered coordinates: for every centered degree-
1
statistic
q
,

\|\mathcal{M}q\|_{2}\leq\theta\,\|q\|_{2}
;

2.

\mathcal{M}
acts trivially on exogenous
\sigma
-algebras outside the local fan-in (compatibility with the window).

Lemma 6.10

(Explicit mixing witness for XOR/Index)
.

Consider a single gadget block with inputs
x\in\{0,1\}^{s}
and outputs
s\in\{0,1\}^{r}
(
s,r=O(1)
).
By Lemma
6.2
, after an invertible output mix we may write
s=(t_{1},t_{2})
with

t_{1}
affine and
t_{2}=(\text{affine})\oplus q
, where
q
is the shared non-linear core.
Denote the affine
part
by
y_{\mathrm{aff}}:=Ax\oplus r
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, with

\rank(A)=2
and
\mathbf{1}^{\top}A=(1,1,1)
,
\mathbf{1}^{\top}r=0
. Let
L_{\mathrm{out}}
be the space of centered degree-
1
(linear)
statistics in the output coordinates, equipped with the
L_{2}
norm under the window proxy
u
.
Define the operator

\mathcal{M}\ :=\ \Pi_{\mathrm{out}}\ \circ\ T_{\theta}\ \circ\ \mathcal{L},

where
\mathcal{L}
is the degree-
1
pullback (compose with
y_{\mathrm{aff}}=Ax\oplus r
and keep only the linear slice),

T_{\theta}
is the Bonami–Beckner noise on inputs with parameter
\theta\in(0,1)
acting independently per block,
and
\Pi_{\mathrm{out}}
is the orthogonal projection (w.r.t.

L_{\mathrm{out}}

Then there exists a constant

(depending only on the gadget’s constant fan-in/out and the window calibration)
such that for every centered

\|\mathcal{M}q\|_{2}\ \leq\ c_{G}\,\theta\ \|q\|_{2}.

In particular, picking
\theta\in(0,1)
small enough that

yields a mixing witness on the block
with rate
\bar{\theta}:=c_{G}\theta\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Work on the calibrated window
\mathsf{Win}
. By Lemma
6.2
, after an invertible output mixing we may write the two outputs as

s=(t_{1},t_{2})
with
t_{1}
affine and
t_{2}=(\text{affine})\oplus q
, where
q
is the shared nonlinear core. Let

L_{\mathrm{out}}
denote the subspace of centered degree-1 output statistics (with inner product and
L_{2}
-norm taken under the window proxy
u
).

Define three linear maps:

\mathcal{L}\colon L_{\mathrm{out}}\to L_{\mathrm{in}},\qquad T_{\theta}\colon L_{\mathrm{in}}\to L_{\mathrm{in}},\qquad\Pi_{\mathrm{out}}\colon L_{\mathrm{in}}\to L_{\mathrm{out}},

as follows.

(1) Degree-1 pullback
\mathcal{L}
.
Write the affine part of the interface as
y_{\mathrm{aff}}=Ax\oplus r
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
with

\mathrm{rank}(A)=2
,
\mathbf{1}^{\top}A=(1,1,1)
,
\mathbf{1}^{\top}r=0
(Lemma
6.2
). For

,
compose

with

and project to the degree-1 slice on inputs; denote this operation by
\mathcal{L}q

Because the gadget has constant fan-in/out and

fixes the local reweightings, the induced operator norm is bounded:

\|\mathcal{L}q\|_{2}\;\leq\;c_{\mathrm{aff}}\;\|q\|_{2}\quad\text{for some absolute }c_{\mathrm{aff}}<\infty.

(Here

depends only on the constant local wiring and the window calibration, not on the ambient dimension.)

(2) Noise operator
T_{\theta}

Let

be the Bonami–Beckner noise on inputs with parameter
\theta\in(0,1)
acting independently per block.
On the degree-1 slice (linear forms),

is an exact contraction by
\theta

\|T_{\theta}z\|_{2}\;=\;\theta\,\|z\|_{2}\qquad\text{for all }z\in L_{\mathrm{in}}\text{ of degree }1.

(3) Output projection

For any centered

\|\mathcal{M}q\|_{2}\;\leq\;\|\Pi_{\mathrm{out}}\|_{2\to 2}\;\|T_{\theta}\|_{2\to 2}\;\|\mathcal{L}\|_{2\to 2}\;\|q\|_{2}\;\leq\;(1)\cdot\theta\cdot c_{\mathrm{aff}}\;\|q\|_{2}.

Thus
\|\mathcal{M}\|_{2\to 2}\leq c_{\mathrm{aff}}\theta
. Let

. Choosing any

with

makes

a strict contraction on

with rate

.
By definition, this is a mixing witness on the block with the stated rate.
∎

Lemma 6.16

(Correlation shielding)
.

Assume

on

Work on the window

. Let

be the input bits and let

be the (deterministic) gadget outputs applied coordinate-wise.

be any degree-
\leq k

We show that for every “exogenous” random variable

\bigl|\Cov\bigl(p(y),Z\bigr)\bigr|\;=\;o(1),

uniformly over the family, which is the stated shielding.

Step 1: Interface normal form; degree bookkeeping.

By Lemma
6.2
, after an invertible output mix we can write the two outputs as

(t_{1},t_{2})
with
t_{1}
affine and

, where

Write the affine

Because each output depends on

\leq k

depends on at most

input blocks.
If needed (for the Index block), the constant factor

from

Step 2: Mixing/Noise dampens high degree.

Let

For a degree-
\leq k

Lemma 7.2

(Bias-orthonormal Walsh basis on the window)
.

Let
u
be the proxy distribution used on the window
\mathsf{Win}
, assumed product-like with single-bit biases

\Pr_{u}[x_{i}=1]=p_{i}\in(0,1)
and independent coordinates. Define centered/normalized linear coordinates

z_{i}(x)\ :=\ \frac{x_{i}-\mathbb{E}_{u}[x_{i}]}{\sqrt{\Var_{u}(x_{i})}}\ =\ \frac{x_{i}-p_{i}}{\sqrt{p_{i}(1-p_{i})}}\qquad(i\in[n]).

For any

set the (biased) Walsh character
\chi_{S}(x):=\prod_{i\in S}z_{i}(x)
(with
\chi_{\emptyset}\equiv 1
).
Then
\{\chi_{S}\}_{S\subseteq[n]}
is an orthonormal system in

, i.e.

\mathbb{E}_{u}[\chi_{S}\,\chi_{T}]\ =\ \mathbf{1}[S=T]\qquad\text{for all }S,T\subseteq[n],

and
\deg(\chi_{S})=|S|
.

Proof.

Independence under
u
gives
\mathbb{E}_{u}[z_{i}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}_{u}[z_{i}^{2}]=1
for all

, and for

\mathbb{E}_{u}[z_{i}z_{j}]=\mathbb{E}_{u}[z_{i}]\mathbb{E}_{u}[z_{j}]=0

\mathbb{E}_{u}[\chi_{S}\chi_{T}]=\prod_{i\in S\triangle T}\mathbb{E}_{u}[z_{i}]\cdot\prod_{i\in S\cap T}\mathbb{E}_{u}[z_{i}^{2}]=\mathbf{1}[S=T].

Degree equals
|S|
by construction.
∎

Appendix B

Definition B.1

(Windowed largeness)
.

Let
\varpi
be a fixed product-like baseline on the instance space at length

(the baseline used for Natural-Proofs density).
Write
\varpi_{\mathsf{Win}}
for
\varpi

\mathsf{Win}
(Sec.

A property

is

if there exists
\alpha>0
such that

\varpi_{\mathsf{Win}}\bigl[\mathcal{P}\bigr]\ \geq\ N^{-\alpha}\qquad\text{for all sufficiently large }N.

Otherwise
\mathcal{P}
is
non-large
on
\mathsf{Win}
.

This baseline–window density setup is the same as in IECZ-I
[
11
]
.

Lemma B.1

(Baseline robustness)
.

Let
\varpi^{\prime}
be any product baseline with the same one-marginals as
\varpi
and

\mathrm{TV}(\varpi,\varpi^{\prime})\leq\varepsilon(N)=o(1)
. Then every TV–Lipschitz transfer used in this section
changes by at most
O(\varepsilon(N))
; in particular, all windowed density bounds are stable up to
o(1)
when replacing

\varpi
by
\varpi^{\prime}
(and likewise for
\varpi_{\mathsf{Win}}
by
\varpi^{\prime}_{\mathsf{Win}}
).

Proof.

Write
P:=\varpi
and
Q:=\varpi^{\prime}
. By assumption
\mathrm{TV}(P,Q)\leq\varepsilon(N)=o(1)
, and
Q
is product with the same one-marginals as
P
.

Deterministic pushforwards.

For any measurable (deterministic) map
\Phi
used in this section (reductions, gadget layers, projections), total variation contracts under pushforward:

\mathrm{TV}\bigl(\Phi_{\#}P,\ \Phi_{\#}Q\bigr)\ \leq\ \mathrm{TV}(P,Q)\ \leq\ \varepsilon(N),

by
Lemma

4.2
. Consequently, any functional that is
1
-Lipschitz in TV (all our “TV–Lipschitz transfers”) changes by at most
O(\varepsilon(N))
under
\Phi
.

Conditioning on the window.

Let
W:=\mathsf{Win}
. Standard TV calculus for conditionals gives

\mathrm{TV}\bigl(P(\cdot\mid W),\ Q(\cdot\mid W)\bigr)\ \leq\ \frac{\mathrm{TV}(P,Q)+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\lvert P(W)-Q(W)\rvert}{P(W)}.

Since
\lvert P(W)-Q(W)\rvert\leq\mathrm{TV}(P,Q)
and
P(W)
is bounded below by
N^{-\kappa}
for some fixed
\kappa>0
by the calibration of the window (the “polynomial normalization” from Sec.
4
), we obtain

\mathrm{TV}\bigl(P(\cdot\mid W),\ Q(\cdot\mid W)\bigr)\ \leq\ C\,\varepsilon(N),

for a constant
C
depending only on the window calibration (and independent of
N
). Thus every TV–Lipschitz quantity computed
after
conditioning on
W
also changes by at most
O(\varepsilon(N))
.

Putting the two parts together, all transfers considered here—deterministic pushforwards (reductions/gadgets) and window conditioning—perturb our windowed expectations/probabilities by at most
O(\varepsilon(N))
. In particular, any windowed density lower/upper bound is preserved up to
o(1)
when replacing
\varpi
with
\varpi^{\prime}
, and likewise for
\varpi_{\mathsf{Win}}
with
\varpi^{\prime}_{\mathsf{Win}}
.
∎

What is checked

•

Truth table (unit block).
For both parities
b\in\{0,1\}

Small-time approximate controllability for the nonlinear complex Ginzburg-Landau equation with bilinear control Journal: arXiv
Xingwu Zeng, Can Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the bilinear approximate controllability for the complex Ginzburg-Landau (CGL) equation with a power-type nonlinearity of any integer degree on a torus of arbitrary space dimension. Under a saturation hypothesis on the control operator, we show the small-time global controllability of the CGL equation. The proof is obtained by developing a multiplicative version of a geometric control approach, introduced by Agrachev and Sarychev in [ 2 , 3 ] .

Introduction

1.1
Model and controllability concepts

The complex Ginzburg-Landau (CGL) equation is classical in the theory of amplitude equations, and is a simple but important model of convection, flow, and turbulence, etc. It has been studied by many mathematicians and physicists, see
[
18
,
23
,
28
,
31
]
.
We are interested in the following CGL equation on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{d}
:

\partial_{t}\psi=V\psi+(1+i\nu)\Delta\psi-(1+i\mu)|\psi|^{2\sigma}\psi,

(1.1)

where
d,\,\sigma\geqslant 1
are arbitrary integers,
i=\sqrt{-1}
,
V\geqslant 0
and
\nu,\,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
.
Without loss of generality, (
1.1
) has been normalized so that the coefficients of the linear and nonlinear dissipation (damping) terms are unity.
V
is the coefficient of the linear driving term.
\sigma
sets the degree of the nonlinearity.
\nu
and
\mu
are the coefficients of the linear and nonlinear dispersive terms respectively.

Let
q\geqslant 1
be arbitrary integer, and
Q:\mathbb{T}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{q}
be a given smooth external field. We consider the following CGL equation with bilinear control on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
:

\partial_{t}\psi=V\psi+(1+i\nu)\Delta\psi-(1+i\mu)|\psi|^{2\sigma}\psi+(1+iR)\langle u(t),Q(x)\rangle\psi.

(1.2)

The pair
(u,R)\in L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{q})\times\mathbb{R}
plays the role of a control.
(
1.2
) is equipped with the initial condition

\psi(0,x)=\psi_{0}(x)

(1.3)

belonging to a Sobolev space
H^{s}:=H^{s}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{C}\right)
of order
s>d/2
, so that the problem is locally well-posed in
H^{s}
(see Proposition
2.1
below).

We next introduce the controllability properties that we are interested in. The first one is the small-time global approximate null-controllability.

Definition 1.1
.

The system (
1.2
), (
1.3
) is small-time approximately null-controllable, if for any
\varepsilon>0,\,T>0,\,\psi_{0}\in H^{s}
, there exists a control
(u,R)\in L^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{q})\times\mathbb{R}
such that

\left\|\psi(T)\right\|_{H^{s}}<\varepsilon.

The second one is the small-time global approximate null-controllability of phases, i.e., the small-time global approximate null-controllability to any
H^{s}
-neighbourhood of any target of the form
e^{(1-i\nu)\theta}\psi_{0}
.

Definition 1.2
.

The system (
1.2
), (
1.3
) is approximately controllable for any
\varepsilon>0,\,\mathcal{T}>0,\,\psi_{0}\in H^{s}
, and
\theta\in C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}\right)
, there is a time
T\in(0,\mathcal{T})
, a control
(u,R)\in C^{\infty}\left([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{q}\right)\times\mathbb{R}
, and a unique solution
\psi\in C\left([0,T];H^{s}\right)
of (
1.2
), (
1.3
) such that

\left\|\psi(T)-e^{(1-i\nu)\theta}\psi_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}}<\varepsilon.

1.2
Notation

In this paper, we will use the following notation.

•

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

and

is the corresponding norm.

•

is endowed with the scalar product

•

C^{s}=C^{s}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{C}\right)
with
s\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, is the space of
s
-order continuously differentiable function.

•

Let
X
be a Banach space. We denote by

•

We write

•

•

L^{p}\left(J_{T};X\right)\text{ with }1\leqslant p<\infty
, is the space of Borel-measurable functions
f:J_{T}\rightarrow X
with

\|f\|_{L^{p}\left(J_{T};X\right)}=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t)\|_{X}^{p}{\,\rm d}t\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

•

s_{d}
is the smallest integer strictly greater than

.

•

1.3
Main results

The purpose of this paper is to study the CGL equation (
1.2
) when the driving force
(u,R)
acts multiplicatively through only few low Fourier modes.
Let
K\subset\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{d}:=\mathbb{Z}^{d}\backslash\left\{(0,\ldots,0)\right\}

K=\{(1,0,\ldots,0),\,(0,1,\ldots,0),\,\ldots,\,(0,0,\ldots,1,0),\,(1,\ldots,1)\}.

Assume that the field

\{\mathbf{1},\,\sin\langle x,k\rangle,\,\cos\langle x,k\rangle\mid k\in K\}\subset\operatorname{span}\left\{Q_{j}\mid j=1,\ldots,q\right\}.

The main results of this paper are as following.

.

Assume that the condition (

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that the condition (
1.5
) is satisfied. Let
s\geqslant s_{d}
be an integer. The system (
1.2
), (
1.3
) is approximately controllable in the sense of Definition
1.2
.

Several remarks are given in order.

Remark 1.1
.

We emphasize that the approximate null-controllability in Theorem

does not impose any condition on the scalar control

, i.e., the choice of

is arbitrary.

Remark 1.2
.

A more general formulation of the result in Theorem
1.2
is given in Theorem
3.2
, where the controllability is proved under an abstract saturation condition for the field

(see the condition
(
\mathscr{P}
)
).
We will see from the proof of Theorem

that the scalar control
R=-\nu
.
Note that the time
T
may depend on the initial condition
\psi_{0}
, the target
e^{(1-i\nu)\theta}\psi_{0}
, and the parameters in this equation.

Remark 1.3
.

As the bilinear approximate null-controllability has been derived in Theorem
1.1
, one may naturally ask whether the bilinear exact null-controllability can be achieved. Unfortunately, the bilinear exact null-controllability seems to be impossible. For the sake of simplicity, let us consider the solution
\psi
of the following heat equation with the bilinear control
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T))
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\psi-\Delta\psi-u\psi&=0\quad&&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T),\\
\psi\mid_{t=0}&=\psi_{0}\quad&&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{aligned}\right.

where the initial datum
\psi_{0}\not\equiv 0
. Then, if
\psi(x,T)\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T))
and
T>0
, the well-known backward uniqueness for parabolic equations (see
[
15
]
) implies that
\psi
vanishes identically in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]
. This contradicts
\psi_{0}\not\equiv 0
. The conclusion is that, if
\psi_{0}\not\equiv 0
, then for any
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T))
and
T>0
,
\psi(x,T)\not\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Remark 1.4
.

The small-time controllability of phases is proved in Theorem
1.2
, while a stronger controllability result can be considered: the small-time
L^{2}
-approximate controllability. It refers to the possibility, for any
\varepsilon>0
and every

with

, there exists a time

, a global phase

and controls

such that the unique solution
\psi\in C\left([0,T];H^{s}\right)
of (

), (

We refer the interested readers to
[

1.4
Methodology

The main idea is motivated by
[
11
]
. To draw a picture of the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, let us first turn to the following Cauchy problem:

\partial_{t}\psi=V\psi+(1+i\nu)\Delta\psi-(1+i\mu)|\psi|^{2\sigma}\psi+(r_{1}+ir_{2})\langle u(t),Q(x)\rangle\psi,

(1.6)

which is equipped with the same initial condition (
1.3
).
We denote by
\mathcal{R}_{t}\left(\psi_{0},(u,r_{1},r_{2})\right)
solution of the system (
1.6
), (
1.3
) defined up to some maximal time. We denote by
\mathbb{B}(\varphi)(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\partial_{x_{j}}\varphi(x)\right)^{2}
.
A central role in the proof is played by the limit

e^{(a+ib)\delta^{-1/2}\varphi}\mathcal{R}_{\delta}\left(e^{-(a+ib)\delta^{-1/2}\varphi}\psi_{0},(\delta^{-1}u,r_{1},r_{2})\right)\rightarrow e^{(r_{1}+ir_{2})\left(\mathbb{B}(\varphi)+\langle u,Q\rangle\right)}\psi_{0}\quad\text{ in }H^{s}\text{ as }\delta\rightarrow 0^{+},

(1.7)

which holds for any
\psi_{0}\in H^{s}
, non-negative function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}\right)
, constants
(u,r_{1},r_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
, and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
r_{1}+ir_{2}=(1+i\nu)(a+ib)^{2}.
The limit (
1.7
) specifies the asymptotic behavior of the solution of the CGL equation in small time under appropriately scaled large control and rapidly oscillating initial condition. Theorem
1.1
is derived directly from (
1.7
).

In order to explain the main idea, we consider the 1-D case,
d=1
. In this case, we could choose the field
Q=\left(\mathbf{1},\,\sin x,\,\cos x\right)
such that assumption (
1.5
) satisfied, and (
1.7
) becomes

e^{(a+ib)\delta^{-1/2}\varphi}\mathcal{R}_{\delta}\left(e^{-(a+ib)\delta^{-1/2}\varphi}\psi_{0},(\delta^{-1}u,r_{1},r_{2})\right)\rightarrow e^{(r_{1}+ir_{2})\left(\varphi_{x}^{2}+\langle u,Q\rangle\right)}\psi_{0}\quad\text{ in }H^{s}\text{ as }\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}.

(1.8)

Letting
r_{1}=1,r_{2}=-\nu
, applying this limit with
\varphi=0
and using the assumption (
1.5
), we see that (
1.6
) can be controlled in small time from any initial point
\psi_{0}\in H^{s}
to an arbitrary neighbourhood of
e^{(1-i\nu)\theta}\psi_{0}
for any
\theta
in the vector space

H_{0}=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{1},\,\sin x,\,\cos x\}.

Applying again the limit (
1.8
) with non-negative functions
\varphi=\theta_{j}\in H_{0},\,j=0,\ldots,n
, we add more directions in
\theta
. Namely, the system can be steered from
\psi_{0}
close to
e^{(r_{1}+ir_{2})\theta}\psi_{0}
, where
\theta
now belongs to a larger vector space
H_{1}
whose elements are of the form

\theta_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\theta_{j}^{\prime})^{2}.

We iterate this argument and construct an increasing sequence
\left\{H_{j}\right\}
of subspaces such that the equation can be approximately controlled to any target
e^{(r_{1}+ir_{2})\theta}\psi_{0}
with any
\theta\in H_{j}
and
j\geqslant 1
. Since the union
\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}H_{j}
is dense in
C^{k}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}\right)
for any
k\geqslant 1
, this concludes the proof of approximate controllability of problem (
1.6
) and (
1.3
) with
r_{1}=1,\,r_{2}=-\nu
and
u\in C^{\infty}\left([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{q}\right)
, thereby establishing Theorem
1.2
.

1.5
Related literature

The controllability of the CGL equation has been widely studied via additive controls, see
[
9
,
16
,
17
,
26
]
. Most of the controllability results are proved by establishing Carleman estimates.
However, to the best of our knowledge, the controllability of the CGL equation via bilinear control has not been discussed.

The present paper is the first one to deal with the bilinear controllability for the CGL equation by using the Agrachev-Sarychev type approach. Agrachev and Sarychev
[

,
3
,
4
]
first developed the saturating geometric control approach when studying the approximate controllability of the 2D Navie-Stokes and Euler systems by finite-dimensional forces. Later, their approach has been further extended to different equations in the case of additive controls, see
[

,
22
,
25
,
27
,
29
,
30
]
. Recently, this approach has been implemented for bilinear small-time controllability of the Schrödinger equation in
[
5
,
11
]
, the heat equation in
[
13
]
, and the Burgers equation in
[
14
]
. The intersted readers can find additional results on small-time controllability of PDEs through bilinear control by similar methods in
[

,

,

,

,

.

\displaystyle\|w(t)\|_{s}

\displaystyle\leqslant C,\quad\,\,\quad\quad\forall t\in[0,2],

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|w(t)\|_{r}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\delta^{-1/4},\quad\forall t\in[0,2].

(2.9)

Furthermore,
v(t)
is well-defined for
t<\delta^{-1}T^{\delta}
and satisfies the equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v=

\displaystyle\delta V(v+w)+\delta(1+i\nu)\Delta(v+w)-\delta(1+i\mu)|e^{-(a+ib)\delta^{-1/2}\varphi}(v+w)|^{2\sigma}(v+w)

\displaystyle-\delta^{1/2}(1+i\nu)(a+ib)\left[\Delta\varphi(v+w)+2\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla(v+w)\right]

\displaystyle+(r_{1}+ir_{2})\left[\langle u,Q\rangle+\mathbb{B}(\varphi)\right]v,

(2.10)

with the initial condition

v(0)=\psi_{0}-\psi_{0}^{\delta}.

(2.11)

Let us start by assuming that
\psi_{0}\in H^{2s+2}
. It follows that
\psi(t)\in H^{2s+2}
and
v(t)\in H^{2s+2}
for every

.
Let the multi-index notation
\alpha=\left(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
satisfying
|\alpha|=\left|\alpha_{1}\right|+\cdots+\left|\alpha_{d}\right|\leqslant s
. Taking first the scalar product of (

) with

in

and then integrating by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{{\,\rm d}}{{\,\rm d}t}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{d}}|\partial^{\alpha}v|^{2}{\,\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\delta|V|\|v+w\|_{s}\|v\|_{s}

\displaystyle+2\delta(1+\nu^{2})^{1/2}\|w\|_{r}\|v\|_{s}

\displaystyle+2\delta(1+\mu^{2})^{1/2}\left|\langle\partial^{\alpha}[|e^{-(a+ib)\delta^{-1/2}\varphi}(v+w)|^{2\sigma}(v+w)],\partial^{\alpha}v\rangle_{L^{2}([0,2\pi]^{d})}\right|

\displaystyle+2\delta^{1/2}(1+\nu^{2})^{1/2}(a^{2}+b^{2})^{1/2}\left|\langle\partial^{\alpha}[\Delta\varphi(v+w)+2\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla(v+w)],\partial^{\alpha}v\rangle_{L^{2}([0,2\pi]^{d})}\right|

\displaystyle+2(r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2})^{1/2}\left|\langle\partial^{\alpha}\left[(\langle u,Q\rangle+\mathbb{B}(\varphi))v\right],\partial^{\alpha}v\rangle_{L^{2}([0,2\pi]^{d})}\right|

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{5}I_{i}.

(2.12)

We estimate the terms

, and

\displaystyle\left|I_{1}\right|\leqslant C\delta\|v+w\|_{s}\|v\|_{s}\leqslant C\delta\|v\|_{s}^{2}+C\delta\|v\|_{s},

\displaystyle\left|I_{2}\right|\leqslant C\delta\|w\|_{r}\|v\|_{s}\leqslant C\delta^{3/4}\|v\|_{s},

\displaystyle\left|I_{3}\right|\leqslant C\delta\|v+w\|_{s}^{2\sigma+1}\|v\|_{s}\leqslant C\delta\|v\|_{s}^{2(\sigma+1)}+C\delta\|v\|_{s}

\displaystyle\left|I_{5}\right|\leqslant C\|v\|_{s}^{2}.

\left\langle\partial_{x_{j}}\varphi\partial_{x_{j}}\partial^{\alpha}v,\partial^{\alpha}v\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\langle\partial_{x_{j}}\varphi,\partial_{x_{j}}\left|\partial^{\alpha}v\right|^{2}\rangle_{L^{2}}=-\langle\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}\varphi,\left|\partial^{\alpha}v\right|^{2}\rangle_{L^{2}}.

\partial_{t}\|v\|_{s}^{2}\leqslant C\delta^{1/2}+C\left(1+\delta^{1/2}\right)\|v\|_{s}^{2}+C\delta\|v\|_{s}^{2(\sigma+1)},\quad t\leqslant\delta^{-1}T^{\delta}.

\|v(t)\|_{s}^{2}\leqslant e^{C\left(1+\delta^{1/2}\right)t}\left(C\delta^{1/2}t+\left\|\psi_{0}-\psi_{0}^{\delta}\right\|_{s}^{2}+C\delta\int_{0}^{t}\|v(\tau)\|_{s}^{2(\sigma+1)}{\,\rm d}\tau\right)

for

into

Let us take

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{0}-\psi_{0}^{\delta}\right\|_{s}^{2}<1,

(2.14)

\displaystyle e^{C\left(1+\delta^{1/2}\right)}\left(C\delta^{1/2}+\left\|\psi_{0}-\psi_{0}^{\delta}\right\|_{s}^{2}\right)<1/2,

(2.15)

(2.16)

To reach a contradiction, we assume that

In what follows, we assume that
s\geqslant s_{d}
is an integer and denote
r=s+2
as in Proposition
2.2
.
We start this section with a definition of a saturation property introduced in
[

]
.
Let
H
be a finite-dimensional subspace of
C^{r}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}\right)
, and let
F(H)
be the largest subspace of
C^{r}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}\right)
whose elements can be represented in the form

\theta_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{B}\left(\theta_{j}\right)

for some integer

and functions
\theta_{j}\in H,\,j=0,\ldots,n
, where
\mathbb{B}
is given by (
2.3
). As
\mathbb{B}
is quadratic,

is well-defined and finite-dimensional.
Let us define a nondecreasing sequence
\left\{H_{j}\right\}
of finite-dimensional subspaces by
H_{0}=H
and
H_{j}=F\left(H_{j-1}\right),j\geqslant 1
, and denote

Definition 3.1
.

A finite-dimensional subspace

is said to be saturating, if
H_{\infty}
is dense in
C^{r}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}\right)
.

Throughout this section, we assume that the following condition is satisfied:

(
\mathscr{P}
)

The field

is saturating, i.e., the subspace

.

3.1
Small-time approximate controllability

A direct consequence of Proposition
2.2
is the small-time globally approximate null-controllability.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that the condition
(
\mathscr{P}
)
is satisfied,
\mathbf{1}\in\operatorname{span}\left\{Q_{j}\mid j=1,\ldots,q\right\}
and
\psi_{0}\in H^{s}
, where
s\geqslant s_{d}
is an integer. For any
\varepsilon,\,T>0
and
r_{1},\,r_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists
u\in\Theta\left(\psi_{0},T\right)
such that

\|\mathcal{R}_{T}\left(\psi_{0},(u,r_{1},r_{2})\right)\|_{s}<\varepsilon.

Proof.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, let
c\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

|e^{(r_{1}+ir_{2})c}|=e^{cr_{1}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2\|\psi_{0}\|_{s}}.

That is

\|e^{(r_{1}+ir_{2})c}\psi_{0}\|_{s}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Since
\mathbf{1}\in\operatorname{span}\left\{Q_{j}\mid j=1,\ldots,q\right\}
, we can choose
u_{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
such that
c=\langle u_{c},Q\rangle
.
Thanks to Proposition
2.2
, there exists
\delta>0
such that
\delta^{-1}u_{c}\in\Theta\left(\psi_{0},\delta\right)
and

\|\mathcal{R}_{\delta}\left(\psi_{0},(\delta^{-1}u_{c},r_{1},r_{2})\right)-e^{(r_{1}+ir_{2})c}\psi_{0}\|_{s}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Hence

\|\mathcal{R}_{\delta}\left(\psi_{0},(\delta^{-1}u_{c},r_{1},r_{2})\right)\|_{s}\leqslant\|\mathcal{R}_{\delta}\left(\psi_{0},(\delta^{-1}u_{c},r_{1},r_{2})\right)-e^{(r_{1}+ir_{2})c}\psi_{0}\|_{s}+\|e^{(r_{1}+ir_{2})c}\psi_{0}\|_{s}<\varepsilon.

By (i) of Proposition
2.1
,
\mathcal{R}_{t}\left(\mathcal{R}_{\delta}\left(\psi_{0},(\delta^{-1}u_{c},r_{1},r_{2})\right),(0,r_{1},r_{2})\right)
and
\mathcal{R}_{t}\left(0,(0,r_{1},r_{2})\right)\equiv 0
are defined in the same time interval
t\in[0,T-\delta]
when
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small.
Furthermore, (ii) of Proposition
2.1
implies the existence of
C>0
, independent of
\mathcal{R}_{\delta}\left(\psi_{0},(\delta^{-1}u_{c},r_{1},r_{2})\right)
, such that

\|\mathcal{R}_{T-\delta}\left(\mathcal{R}_{\delta}\left(\psi_{0},(\delta^{-1}u_{c},r_{1},r_{2})\right),(0,r_{1},r_{2})\right)-\mathcal{R}_{T-\delta}\left(0,(0,r_{1},r_{2})\right)\|_{s}\leqslant C\|\mathcal{R}_{\delta}\left(\psi_{0},(\delta^{-1}u_{c},r_{1},r_{2})\right)\|_{s}<C\varepsilon.

Hence, the proof is completed by taking the control

u(t)=\begin{cases}\delta^{-1}u_{c},\quad&t\in(0,\delta),\\
0,\quad&t\in(\delta,T).\end{cases}

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume that the condition
(
\mathscr{P}
)

\mathbf{1}\in\operatorname{span}\left\{Q_{j}\mid j=1,\ldots,q\right\}

\left\|\mathcal{R}_{T}\left(\psi_{0},(u,1,-\nu)\right)-e^{(1-i\nu)\theta}\psi_{0}\right\|_{s}<\varepsilon.

(3.1)

Proof.

We first let

, and

Combined with the saturation condition
(

Step 1.

2.2
for

and

.
The induction hypothesis implies that, for any

, there are families of controls

4
Concluding comments

4.1
Controllability on bounded domain

It is interesting to notice that most controllability results are established on the torus, while only a few have been established for bounded domains. We refer to
[
14
, Section 6 and Section 7]
for bilinear small-time controllability results of the Burgers equation with Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions. It would be interesting to extend these bilinear small-time controllability results to other evolution equations.

1.6
) becomes a complex heat equation with complex nonlinearity. For any
\psi_{0},\,\psi_{1}\in H^{s}
with the same argument, i.e.,
Arg(\psi_{0})=Arg(\psi_{1})
, where
s\geqslant s_{d}
is an integer, we can prove that: for any
\varepsilon>0,\,\mathcal{T}>0
, there are
T\in(0,\mathcal{T})
and
u\in\Theta\left(\psi_{0},T\right)\cap C^{\infty}\left(J_{T};\mathbb{R}^{q}\right)
such that

\left\|\mathcal{R}_{T}\left(\psi_{0},(u,1,0)\right)-\psi_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}<\varepsilon,

(4.1)

i.e., the small-time global approximately controllability is satisfied in
L^{2}
between states that share the same argument.

Indeed, if we denote by
Z
the set of zeroes of
\psi_{0}
and
\psi_{1}
, then the fact that
Arg(\psi_{0})=Arg(\psi_{1})
implies that

\psi_{0}=|\psi_{0}|e^{iArg(\psi_{0})}=\frac{|\psi_{0}|}{|\psi_{1}|}|\psi_{1}|e^{iArg(\psi_{1})}=\frac{|\psi_{0}|}{|\psi_{1}|}\psi_{1}\text{ on }Z.

Consider for
\eta>0
the set

Z_{\eta}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\mid\operatorname{dist}(x,Z)<\eta\right\},

and
Z_{\eta}^{c}:=\mathbb{T}^{d}\setminus Z_{\eta}
. For
\eta>0
, we define

\phi_{\eta}=\rho_{\eta}\ln\left(\psi_{1}/\psi_{0}\right)=\rho_{\eta}\ln\left(|\psi_{1}|/|\psi_{0}|\right),

where

\rho_{\eta}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,\quad&&x\in Z_{2\eta}^{c}\\
&(0,1),\quad&&x\in Z_{\eta}^{c}\setminus Z_{2\eta}^{c}\\
&0.\quad&&x\in Z_{\eta}\end{aligned}\right.

is a mollifier compactly supported inside
Z_{\eta}^{c}
.
\phi_{\eta}
is well-defined because
|\psi_{1}|/|\psi_{0}|>0
on
Z_{\eta}^{c}
. Furthermore,
\phi_{\eta}
belongs to
H^{s}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}\right)
. Notice that

\left\|e^{\phi_{\eta}}\psi_{0}-\psi_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d}\right)}\leqslant\left\|e^{\phi_{\eta}}\psi_{0}-\psi_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(Z_{2\eta}\right)}.

Hence, for any
\varepsilon,\,T>0
, we can choose
\eta>0
small enough such that

\left\|e^{\phi_{\eta}}\psi_{0}-\psi_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d}\right)}<\varepsilon/3.

Now, by the density of
C^{s+2}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}\right)
into
H^{s}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}\right)
, there exists
\widetilde{\phi}_{\eta}\in C^{s+2}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}\right)
such that

\left\|e^{\widetilde{\phi}_{\eta}}\psi_{0}-\psi_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d}\right)}\leqslant\left\|e^{\widetilde{\phi}_{\eta}}\psi_{0}-e^{\phi_{\eta}}\psi_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d}\right)}+\left\|e^{\phi_{\eta}}\psi_{0}-\psi_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d}\right)}<\frac{2\varepsilon}{3}.

Then, by applying Theorem
3.2
with
\theta=\widetilde{\phi}_{\eta}
, there are
T\in(0,\mathcal{T})
and
u\in\Theta\left(\psi_{0},T\right)\cap C^{\infty}\left(J_{T};\mathbb{R}^{q}\right)
such that

\left\|\mathcal{R}_{T}\left(\psi_{0},(u,1,0)\right)-e^{\theta}\psi_{0}\right\|_{s}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

The triangular inequality finally implies that

\left\|\mathcal{R}_{T}\left(\psi_{0},(u,1,0)\right)-\psi_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d}\right)}\leqslant\left\|\mathcal{R}_{T}\left(\psi_{0},(u,1,0)\right)-e^{\widetilde{\phi}_{\eta}}\psi_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d}\right)}+\left\|e^{\widetilde{\phi}_{\eta}}\psi_{0}-\psi_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d}\right)}<\varepsilon,

this completes the proof of (
4.1
).

However, the characteristic of the reachable subspace in
H^{s}
of the bilinear CGL equation remains open.

Appendix

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

For
V\geqslant 0
,
\nu,\,\mu,\,r_{1},\,r_{2}\in\mathbb{R},\,Q\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{q})
and
u\in L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{q})
, let

L\psi:=V\psi+(1+i\nu)\Delta\psi,\quad N(t,u,\psi):=-(1+i\mu)|\psi|^{2\sigma}\psi+(r_{1}+ir_{2})\langle u(t),Q\rangle\psi.

The associated semigroup
S(t)
of
L
acting on a Banach space
\mathbb{X}
can be written as a convolution:
S(t)\psi=G_{t}*\psi,\,\forall\psi\in\mathbb{X}
, with its Green function
G_{t}=G_{t}(x)
for
t>0
given by (see
[
20
]
)

\displaystyle G_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{d}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(x+2\pi n),

(4.2)

\displaystyle g_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi(1+i\nu)t)^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4(1+i\nu)t}+Vt\right).

For
t>0
, the Green function (
4.2
) satisfies the
L^{1}
-estimate

\|G_{t}\|_{L^{1}}\leqslant\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{d}}|g_{t}(x+2\pi n)|{\,\rm d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g_{t}(x)|{\,\rm d}x=(1+\nu^{2})^{d/4}e^{Vt},

from which it follows that
S(t)
is bounded over
L^{p}
for every
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
with

\|S(t)\psi\|_{L^{p}}=\left\|G_{t}*\psi\right\|_{L^{p}}\leqslant\left\|G_{t}\right\|_{L^{1}}\|\psi\|_{L^{p}}\leqslant\left(1+\nu^{2}\right)^{d/4}e^{Vt}\|\psi\|_{L^{p}}.

Moreover, it can be shown (see
[
20
]
) that
S(t)
is a strongly continuous semigroup over
C^{0}
and over
L^{p}
for every
1\leqslant p<\infty
.

Step 1.
S(t)
is a strongly continuous semigroup over
H^{s}
.

First of all, it is direct to check that

S(t)S(\tau)=S(t+\tau),\,\forall t,\tau\geqslant 0\text{ and }S(0)=I.

Next, for any
\psi\in H^{s}
, we have by the Fourier series expansion,

\psi(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{\psi}(k)e^{i\langle k,x\rangle},

where

\widehat{\psi}(k)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{d}}\psi(x)e^{-i\langle k,x\rangle}{\,\rm d}x.

Moreover

\widehat{S(t)\psi}(k)=\widehat{G_{t}*\psi}(k)=\widehat{G_{t}}(k)\cdot\widehat{\psi}(k)=e^{-(1+i\nu)|k|^{2}t+Vt}\widehat{\psi}(k).

Hence

\|S(t)\psi-\psi\|_{s}^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left(1+|k|^{2}\right)^{s}|e^{-(1+i\nu)|k|^{2}t+Vt}-1|^{2}|\widehat{\psi}(k)|^{2}.

The fact that
e^{-(1+i\nu)|k|^{2}t+Vt}\rightarrow 1\text{ as }t\rightarrow 0^{+}
, along with the dominated convergence theorem, implies that

\|S(t)\psi-\psi\|_{s}^{2}\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as }t\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Finally, notice that for any
t\geqslant 0
and for any
\psi\in H^{s}
,

\displaystyle\|S(t)\psi\|_{s}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left(1+|k|^{2}\right)^{s}|e^{-(1+i\nu)|k|^{2}t+Vt}|^{2}|\widehat{\psi}(k)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left(1+|k|^{2}\right)^{s}e^{(-2|k|^{2}+2V)t}|\widehat{\psi}(k)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{2Vt}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left(1+|k|^{2}\right)^{s}|\widehat{\psi}(k)|^{2}=e^{2Vt}\|\psi\|_{s}^{2},

hence
S(t)\in\mathcal{L}(H^{s};H^{s}),\,\forall t\geqslant 0
. In conclusion,
S(t)
is a strongly continuous semigroup over
H^{s}
.

Step 2. For any

\displaystyle+(r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2})^{1/2}e^{Vt}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\langle u(\tau)-\tilde{u}(\tau),Q\rangle\tilde{\psi}(\tau)\right\|_{s}{\,\rm d}\tau.

Using the inequality
\left\||\psi|^{2\sigma}\psi-|\tilde{\psi}|^{2\sigma}\tilde{\psi}\right\|_{s}\leqslant C(s,d)\|\psi-\tilde{\psi}\|_{s}\sum_{j=0}^{2\sigma}\|\psi\|_{s}^{j}\|\tilde{\psi}\|_{s}^{2\sigma-j},\,\forall\psi,\tilde{\psi}\in H^{s}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\psi(t)-\tilde{\psi}(t)\|_{s}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle e^{Vt}\|\psi_{0}-\tilde{\psi}_{0}\|_{s}+(1+\mu^{2})^{1/2}e^{Vt}C\left(s,d\right)\int_{0}^{t}\|\psi(\tau)-\tilde{\psi}(\tau)\|_{s}\sum_{j=0}^{2\sigma}\|\psi(\tau)\|_{s}^{j}\|\tilde{\psi}(\tau)\|_{s}^{2\sigma-j}{\,\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle+(r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2})^{1/2}e^{Vt}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\langle u(\tau),Q\rangle(\psi(\tau)-\tilde{\psi}(\tau))\right\|_{s}{\,\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle+(r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2})^{1/2}e^{Vt}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\langle u(\tau)-\tilde{u}(\tau),Q\rangle\tilde{\psi}(\tau)\right\|_{s}{\,\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle e^{Vt}\|\psi_{0}-\tilde{\psi}_{0}\|_{s}+(1+\mu^{2})^{1/2}e^{Vt}C\left(s,d\right)\sqrt{t}\sup_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant t}\|\psi(\tau)-\tilde{\psi}(\tau)\|_{s}

\displaystyle\times\left(\sum_{j=0}^{2\sigma}\sup_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant t}\|\psi(\tau)\|_{s}^{j}\sup_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant t}\|\tilde{\psi}(\tau)\|_{s}^{2\sigma-j}\right)

\displaystyle+(r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2})^{1/2}e^{Vt}C(Q)\sqrt{2q}\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,t)}\sqrt{t}\sup_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant t}\|\psi(\tau)-\tilde{\psi}(\tau)\|_{s}

\displaystyle+(r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2})^{1/2}e^{Vt}C(Q)\sqrt{2q}\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,t)}\sqrt{t}\sup_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant t}\|\tilde{\psi}(\tau)\|_{s}.

Hence for any
t_{2}\in(0,T)
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant t_{2}}\|\psi(t)-\tilde{\psi}(t)\|_{s}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle e^{VT}\|\psi_{0}-\tilde{\psi}_{0}\|_{s}+\left((r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2})^{1/2}e^{VT}C(Q)\sqrt{2q}\sqrt{T}\sup_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant T}\|\tilde{\psi}(\tau)\|_{s}\right)\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle+(1+\mu^{2})^{1/2}e^{VT}C\left(s,d\right)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{2\sigma}\sup_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant T}\|\psi(\tau)\|_{s}^{j}\sup_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant T}\|\tilde{\psi}(\tau)\|_{s}^{2\sigma-j}\right)\sqrt{t_{2}}\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant t_{2}}\|\psi(t)-\tilde{\psi}(t)\|_{s}

\displaystyle+(r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2})^{1/2}e^{VT}C(Q)\sqrt{2q}\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\sqrt{t_{2}}\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant t_{2}}\|\psi(t)-\tilde{\psi}(t)\|_{s},

which, along with (
4.7
), implies that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant t_{2}}\|\psi(t)-\tilde{\psi}(t)\|_{s}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle e^{VT}\|\psi_{0}-\tilde{\psi}_{0}\|_{s}+\left((r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2})^{1/2}C(Q)\sqrt{2q}\Lambda\right)e^{VT}\sqrt{T}\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle+(1+\mu^{2})^{1/2}C\left(s,d\right)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{2\sigma}C(T)^{j}\left(\delta+\|\tilde{\psi}_{0}\|_{s}\right)^{j}\Lambda^{2\sigma-j}\right)e^{VT}\sqrt{t_{2}}\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant t_{2}}\|\psi(t)-\tilde{\psi}(t)\|_{s}

\displaystyle+(r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2})^{1/2}C(Q)\sqrt{2q}\left(\delta+\Lambda\right)e^{VT}\sqrt{t_{2}}\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant t_{2}}\|\psi(t)-\tilde{\psi}(t)\|_{s}.

Choosing
t_{2}
to be such that

(1+\mu^{2})^{1/2}C\left(s,d\right)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{2\sigma}C(T)^{j}\left(\delta+\|\tilde{\psi}_{0}\|_{s}\right)^{j}\Lambda^{2\sigma-j}\right)e^{VT}\sqrt{t_{2}}+(r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2})^{1/2}C(Q)\sqrt{2q}\left(\delta+\Lambda\right)e^{VT}\sqrt{t_{2}}=\frac{1}{2},

we obtain

\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant t_{2}}\|\psi(t)-\tilde{\psi}(t)\|_{s}\leqslant C(T,\Lambda)\left(\|\psi_{0}-\tilde{\psi}_{0}\|_{s}+\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\right),

(4.8)

where

C(T,\Lambda):=2e^{VT}+2\left((r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2})^{1/2}C(Q)\sqrt{2q}\Lambda\right)e^{VT}\sqrt{T}.

Similarly, we can always obtain another, maybe much bigger positive constant, and still denote it by
C(T,\Lambda)>0
, such that

\sup_{jt_{2}\leqslant t\leqslant jt_{2}+t_{2}}\|\psi(t)-\tilde{\psi}(t)\|_{s}\leqslant C(T,\Lambda)\left(\|\psi_{0}-\tilde{\psi}_{0}\|_{s}+\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\right),\quad\forall j=1,...,[T/t_{2}]-1,

\sup_{[T/t_{2}]t_{2}\leqslant t\leqslant T}\|\psi(t)-\tilde{\psi}(t)\|_{s}\leqslant C(T,\Lambda)\left(\|\psi_{0}-\tilde{\psi}_{0}\|_{s}+\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\right),

which, along with (
4.8
), implies (
2.2
).
Finally, the same technique also implies the validity of (
2.1
).
∎

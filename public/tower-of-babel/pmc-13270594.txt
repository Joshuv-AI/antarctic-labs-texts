Clinical significance and regulatory mechanism of miR-4284 in lumbar intervertebral disc degeneration
Authors: Fang Pei, Qing Chen, Zhengtian Gao, Yingnuo Hou, Fanlei Kong, Shibin Hou, Ting Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLumbar disc degeneration (LDD) is one of the main causes of low back pain. This study investigates the role of miR-4284 and its target, CYCS, in LDD pathogenesis.MethodsSerum levels of miR-4284 and CYCS were measured in 121 LDD patients and 79 healthy controls. The clinical diagnostic and predictive value of miR-4284 was evaluated using ROC curve analysis and logistic regression. Stimulated human nucleus pulposus (NP) cells with LPS and transfected with miR-4284 mimic/inhibitor or CYCS overexpression vector. Cell viability, inflammatory factors (IL-1β, TNF-α), and oxidative stress markers (MDA, T-AOC) were assessed. A luciferase reporter assay confirmed targeting. Western blot analysis was performed to detect the expression of apoptosis-related proteins (Bax, Bcl-2) and mitochondrial function-related proteins (PGC-1α, SOD2).ResultsIn LDD patients, serum miR-4284 expression was significantly downregulated (P < 0.001) and exhibited good diagnostic value (AUC = 0.831). miR-4284 might serve as a protective factor for LDD (OR = 0.100), showing negative correlations with Pfirrmann grading, ODI, and VAS scores (P < 0.001). Luciferase assays confirmed that CYCS was a direct target of miR-4284. In LPS-stimulated NP cells, miR-4284 overexpression alleviated inflammation and oxidative stress while enhancing cell viability. Concurrently, overexpression of CYCS not only completely reversed the protective effects mediated by miR-4284 but also exacerbated cellular apoptosis and mitochondrial dysfunction, thereby synergistically promoting the pathological progression of LDD.ConclusionmiR-4284 is significantly decreased in LDD and may serve as a protective biomarker. By targeting CYCS, it alleviates inflammation and oxidative stress, inhibits apoptosis, and mitigates mitochondrial dysfunction in NP cells, thereby potentially delaying LDD progression.

Introduction

Lumbar disc degeneration (LDD) is the most common degenerative condition of the spine, and its core pathological manifestations are the progressive aging and structural breakdown of the nucleus pulposus, cartilaginous endplates, and annulus fibrosus [1]. This process not only leads to the loss of the intervertebral disc function but also serves as the fundamental cause of a series of clinical conditions, including disc herniation, discogenic low back pain, and spinal instability [2]. These pathological changes seriously damage the quality of life of patients and bring a heavy social and economic burden. At present, the clinical diagnosis of LDD mainly depends on imaging examination. MRI is the primary method for diagnosing LDD [3]. However, it is primarily used to detect morphological alterations and lacks sensitivity to early-stage lesions [4]. Studies have shown that some patients exhibit a mismatch between imaging degeneration and clinical symptoms, and this inconsistency directly affects diagnostic accuracy [5]. Therefore, finding molecular markers that can reflect the changes of the microenvironment in the lumbar disc is the key to breaking through the bottleneck of diagnosis and treatment of LDD.

miRNAs are a kind of small non-coding RNAs, approximately 18–22 nucleotides in length. They function by base-pairing with the 3’UTR of complementary target mRNAs to fine-tune gene expression [6]. As a key regulator of gene expression, miRNAs play central roles in regulating basic biological processes such as cell proliferation and differentiation [7–9]. In fact, miRNAs have been demonstrated to play important regulatory roles in various skeletal system diseases [10–12]. Recent studies have also explored the potential applications of non-coding RNAs, such as circular RNAs, in the management of osteoporosis, further highlighting the crucial role of these molecules in skeletal homeostasis [13]. Research has indicated that multiple miRNAs were abnormally expressed in degenerated intervertebral disc tissue, participating in core pathological processes such as inflammatory responses and extracellular matrix metabolism [14, 15]. For example, miR-100 has been demonstrated to be significantly upregulated in LDD and can target FGFR3 to promote LDD progression [16]; miR-589-3p is significantly upregulated in LDD and markedly enhances apoptosis and inflammatory responses in LPS-stimulated nucleus pulposus (NP) cells [17]. It has been found that miR-4284 is downregulated in many diseases of the skeletal system, such as LDD [18] and osteoarthritis (OA) [19], which indicates that miR-4284 might be participated in the progression of skeletal system diseases. Further research has shown that miR-4284 in mesenchymal stem cells also decreases markedly under the pathological environment of ankylosing spondylitis, and it was found that it could affect the osteoclast generation process by regulating CXCL5 [20], indicating that it also plays an important role in inflammatory response. Building on these findings, we hypothesize that miR-4284 may be involved in the progression of LDD and regulate key processes such as inflammatory responses; however, the specific underlying mechanism remains unclear.

CYCS is a key gene encoding cytochrome C, which plays a central role in mitochondrial function and energy metabolism [21]. The dysregulated expression of CYCS is not only closely linked to the onset and progression of various diseases, but also participates in disease regulation as a target of miRNAs. Research indicates that CYCS is significantly overexpressed in hepatocellular carcinoma and participates in its regulation as a target of miR-20a [22]. Notably, CYCS also exhibits significantly elevated expression in intervertebral disc degeneration, with expression levels markedly higher in patients with severe degeneration compared to those with mild degeneration [23]. Therefore, we speculate that CYCS may participate in LDD progression as a target of miR-4284, but the specific molecular mechanism requires further elucidation.

This study evaluates the diagnostic value of miR-4284 in LDD. Using LPS-stimulated NP cells, it analyzes the regulatory effects of miR-4284 on NP cell proliferation, inflammatory responses, and oxidative stress. Furthermore, through recovery experiments, we elucidate the specific molecular mechanisms by which miR-4284 participates in the LDD process. This research aims to provide novel potential biomarkers and a theoretical basis for the early diagnosis of LDD.

Patients and methods

Study subjects

A total of 200 participants were enrolled in this study. This cohort comprised 121 patients diagnosed with LDD at Xingtai People's Hospital, based on clinical manifestations and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) findings, along with 79 healthy volunteers who underwent routine physical examinations during the same period. All LDD patients had no history of spinal tuberculosis, tumors, or other related diseases. Disease severity was graded according to the Pfirrmann classification system: 34 patients were classified as grade III, 65 as grade IV, and 22 as grade V.

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Xingtai People's Hospital. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment. All procedures were conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Serum sample collection

Fasting venous blood (10 mL) was collected from each participant in the morning. Samples were allowed to clot at room temperature for 30 min and then centrifuged at 4 °C, 4500 rpm for 10 min. Collected the supernatant, aliquoted it, and stored at − 80 °C for future use.

Cell culture

Immortalized human nucleus pulposus (NP) cells (ZQY094) were obtained from Zhongqiao Xinzhou Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Cells were cultured in DMEM/F12 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/mL penicillin and 100 µg/mL streptomycin, and maintained at 37 ℃ in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2. For experimental treatments, cells in the logarithmic growth phase were seeded into 6-well plates at a density of 5 × 104 cells per well. After attachment, the culture medium was replaced with serum-free DMEM containing 1 μg/mL lipopolysaccharide (LPS), and cells were further incubated for 24 h.

Cell transfection

Transfection constructs miR-4284 mimic, mimic-NC, miR-4284 inhibitor, inhibitor-NC, oe-CYCS and oe-NC were all designed and constructed by GenePharma (Shanghai, China). Before transfection, cells in logarithmic growth period were digested by 0.25% trypsin, and then resuspended in complete culture without antibiotics. When cell confluence reached 60–70%, transfection was performed using Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Invitrogen, USA) strictly following the manufacturer’s protocol. After transfection for 6–8 h, the medium was replaced with fresh complete culture medium containing antibiotics. Cells were harvested for further analysis 48 h post-transfection.

RNA extraction

Total RNA was isolated from both stored human serum samples and the cultured NP cells using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

The cDNA was synthesized using the RT reverse transcription kit (Thermo, USA). The amplification was detected by Applied Biosystems VeritiPro real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR instrument (Thermo, USA). U6 or GAPDH was used as an internal control, and the relative expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. All experiments were independently repeated three times. Primer sequences are as follows: miR-4284, forward 5′-TCGCCGACGGGCTCACATCA-3′, reverse 5′-CTCAACTGGTGTCGTGGAGTCGGC-3′; CYCS, forward 5′-TCTCCATGGTCTCTTTGGGC-3′, reverse 5′-ATCCTCTCCCCAGATGATGC-3′; U6, forward 5′-CGCTTCGGCAGCACATATAC-3′, reverse 5′-TTCACGAATTTGCGTGTCAT-3′; GAPDH, forward 5′-AAGGTGAAGGTCGGAGTCAA-3′, reverse 5′-AATGAAGGGGTCATTGATGG-3′.

Cell viability assay (CCK-8)

To assess the effects of different treatments on NP cells viability, the CCK-8 assay was performed. Cells were adjusted to a density of 5 × 104 cells/mL and seeded into 96-well plates at 100 mL/well. After incubation at 37 °C under 5% CO₂ for 24 h to allow complete attachment, 10 μL of CCK-8 solution was added to each well at 0, 24, 48, and 72 h time points. The plates were gently swirled to ensure homogeneous mixing of the reagent with the culture medium, followed by incubation in the dark for 2 h. Absorbance was then measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

To evaluate inflammatory responses and oxidative stress levels in NP cells, commercially available kits were employed to measure IL-1β, TNF-α, malondialdehyde (MDA), and total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC) in cell culture supernatants. The supernatants were collected and centrifuged at 2000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C to remove cellular debris. The supernatant was aliquoted and stored at − 80 ℃ for later use. The following kits were used: Human IL-1β ELISA Kit (PI305), Human TNF-α ELISA Kit (PT518), Lipid Peroxidation MDA Assay Kit (S0131), and Total Superoxide Dismutase Assay Kit (S0101) were all purchased from Beyotime Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). All assays were performed strictly according to the manufacturers’ protocols.

Luciferase reporter assay

Potential binding sites between miR-4284 and CYCS were predicted using TargetScan. To validate the direct interaction, reporter plasmids containing either the wild-type CYCS 3’UTR sequence (WT-CYCS) or a mutated version (MUT-CYCS) were constructed. NP cells were co-transfected with these reporter plasmids along with miR-4284 mimic, inhibitor, or their respective negative controls using Lipofectamine 3000 transfection reagent (Invitrogen, USA). After transfection, cells were cultured at 37 °C under 5% CO₂ for 48 h. Luciferase activity was then measured using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, USA).

Western blot

Total protein was extracted from treated cells using RIPA lysis buffer. After quantification by the BCA method, equal amounts of protein samples were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes. The membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk at room temperature for 1 h, followed by incubation with primary antibodies Bax (1:1000), Bcl-2 (1:1000), PGC-1α (1:1000), SOD2 (1:1000), and GAPDH (1:5000) at 4 °C overnight. After washing with TBST, the membranes were incubated with the corresponding HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies at room temperature for 1 h. Protein bands were visualized using ECL chemiluminescence, and gray values were analyzed with ImageJ software. The relative expression levels of target proteins were calculated as the ratio of their band intensity to that of GAPDH. All experiments were independently repeated three times.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were performed in triplicate. Data were analyzed using SPSS 26.0 software. Differences between two groups were evaluated by independent samples t-test, and comparisons among multiple groups were conducted by ANOVA. Associations between categorical variables were assessed using the Chi-square test. The diagnostic value of serum miR-4284 was assessed using ROC curves and logistic regression. The Pfirrmann grade and VAS score (ordinal/non-normal) were analyzed with Spearman’s rank correlation, whereas the ODI index (continuous and normally distributed) was analyzed with Pearson’s correlation. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Expression of miR-4284 and its diagnostic value in LDD

The results of qRT-PCR showed that miR-4284 was significantly downregulated in the serum of LDD patients compared with the control group (P < 0.001, Fig. 1A). The LDD patients were divided into three groups (III, IV, and V) according to the Pfirrmann classification, and the expression levels of miR-4284 were compared among the groups. The results showed that the expression levels of miR-4284 in each group were significantly lower than those in the control group. Moreover, the higher the Pfirrmann grade, the lower the expression level of miR-4284 (P < 0.001, Fig. 1B), suggesting that the expression level of miR-4284 was negatively correlated with the Pfirrmann grade. The regulatory effect of miR-4284 on the damage caused by LPS stimulation to NP cells

To elucidate the regulatory role of serum miR-4284 in the local intervertebral disc microenvironment, this study established an in vitro inflammatory model by LPS-induced nucleus pulposus cells to simulate the core pathological state of LDD, and subsequently evaluated the regulatory effect of miR-4284 on the function of nucleus pulposus cells based on this model. The expression level of miR-4284 in NP cells stimulated by LPS was significantly decreased, but the expression level of miR-4284 was significantly increased after transfection of miR-4284 mimics (P < 0.001, Fig. 2A). The CCK-8 assay showed that LPS stimulation significantly reduced the proliferation ability of NP cells. After transfection with the miR-4284 mimic, the proliferation ability of NP cells was significantly increased (P < 0.001, Fig. 2B). ELISA assays were used to measure inflammatory factor and oxidative stress marker levels in NP cells. The resukts showed that LPS stimulation induced the secretion of the inflammatory factors IL-1β and TNF-α in NP cells, while miR-4284 mimics significantly suppressed the elevation of inflammatory factors (P < 0.001, Fig. 2C, D). Concurrently, LPS stimulation also caused a significant increase in MDA levels and a decrease in total antioxidant capacity in NP cells. miR-4284 regulates LPS-stimulated NP cell damage by targeting CYCS

The expression level of CYCS in NP cells stimulated by LPS was significantly increased. However, after transfection with miR-4284 mimics, the expression level of CYCS was significantly inhibited. Further transfection with oe-CYCS resulted in a significant recovery of CYCS expression level (P < 0.001, Fig. 4A). Overexpression of CYCS significantly reversed the protective effect of miR-4284 on LPS-stimulated NP cells. It significantly suppressed NP cell proliferation, and markedly elevated the levels of the inflammatory factors IL-1β and TNF-α (P < 0.001, Fig. 4C, D), as well as significantly increased MDA levels, and markedly reduced total antioxidant capacity (P < 0.001, Fig. 4E, F). Additionally, this study examined the expression levels of the key apoptotic proteins, Bax and Bcl-2, as well as the mitochondrial function-related proteins, PGC-1α and SOD2. The results showed that transfection with oe-CYCS led to a significant increase in the expression of the pro-apoptotic protein Bax and a significant decrease in the expression of the anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2 (P < 0.001, Fig. 5A), indicating activation of the apoptotic pathway. Discussion

LDD is one of the most common causes of low back pain. It not only causes severe pain, seriously affecting the quality of life and work ability of patients, but also leads to long-term functional impairment due to its chronic progression, imposing a heavy economic burden on patients’ families and society [24]. The clinical diagnosis of LDD mainly depends on imaging examination and clinical evaluation. However, conventional imaging methods mainly reflect the structural abnormalities of the lumbar spine, such as disc height loss and disc herniation [3]. These changes often represent the final manifestations resulting from the long-term progression and accumulation of LDD. When structural changes occur, the disease often has entered the middle-to-late stage, and abnormal metabolism inside the intervertebral disc, such as inflammatory reaction and oxidative stress, has already occurred [25]. The lag of existing diagnostic methods often leads patients to miss the best period of intervention or early treatment. Therefore, exploring new biomarkers is of great significance for breaking through the bottleneck of early diagnosis of LDD, improving the prognosis of patients and reducing the social burden.

The regulatory role of miRNAs in skeletal system homeostasis and disease is well-established [26]. miR-4284 has been found to be abnormally expressed in many diseases, and its expression level is closely related to disease progression. For example, miR-4284 can serve as a biomarker for non-small cell lung cancer and diffuse large B-cell lymphoma [27, 28]. Research has indicated that it exhibits a significant downregulation trend in intervertebral disc degeneration [18], a finding consistent with the present study, suggesting its potential involvement in the progression of LDD. To evaluate the diagnostic value of miR-4284 for LDD, we further constructed an ROC curve [29], and the results demonstrated that miR-4284 exhibits favorable diagnostic performance. Logistic regression analysis indicated that miR-4284 may serve as a protective factor against LDD. Lower expression levels of miR-4284 correlate with a higher risk of LDD development, suggesting a potential inverse relationship with disease severity. On this basis, we introduced multiple commonly used clinical indicators for correlation analysis: Pfirrmann grading, an internationally widely used imaging grading system for semi-quantitative assessment of LDD severity, objectively reflects the degree of structural degeneration [30]; ODI and VAS scores respectively capture functional impairment and subjective pain, collectively characterizing patient clinical symptoms [31]. The analysis results indicated that miR-4284 expression levels exhibited significant negative correlations with all aforementioned clinical indicators, further supporting the negative association between miR-4284 and LDD severity.

LDD is a multifactorial, progressive degenerative spinal disorder whose core pathology occurs within the intervertebral discs, particularly in the NP tissue. NP cells are the only functional cells within the nucleus pulposus, responsible for synthesizing and secreting extracellular matrix (ECM) components. They maintain intervertebral disc homeostasis by regulating ECM metabolism [24]. Inflammatory responses in nucleus pulposus cells directly disrupt intervertebral disc structure via pro-inflammatory factors and ECM-degrading enzymes, whereas oxidative stress oxidatively damages cellular components and amplifies inflammation [32]. Together, these two processes synergistically drive the occurrence and progression of LDD [33]. LPS has been extensively established as a stable and reproducible classic stimulus that effectively recapitulates key inflammatory pathways implicated in LDD, thereby enabling the mechanistic elucidation of the role played by the miR-4284 in inflammation-associated degenerative processes. IL-1β and TNF-α are the core pro-inflammatory factors of LDD, which accelerate the pathological changes of LDD by initiating inflammatory responses and disrupting ECM metabolism [34, 35]; MDA and T-AOC serve as key indicators reflecting the body’s oxidative-antioxidant balance and are not only central components of the LDD pathological mechanism but also provide a basis for evaluating treatment efficacy [36]. This study established an LPS-induced NP cell model to evaluate the regulatory effect of miR-4284 on the inflammatory response and oxidative stress in NP cells. After LPS stimulation, the viability of NP cells significantly decreased, and the inflammatory response and oxidative stress damage significantly intensified. We found that miR-4284 significantly alleviates the above-mentioned pathological damage. Specifically, miR-4284 not only restores the viability of LPS-stimulated NP cells but also attenuates inflammatory responses and oxidative stress damage, thereby improving their functional status and suggesting its protective effect on NP cells.

In LDD, mitochondrial dysfunction in NP cells represents a key pathological mechanism driving disease progression [37], with CYCS serving as a central mediator in the mitochondrial apoptosis pathway and playing a crucial role in this process [38]. The results showed that CYCS was significantly overexpressed in LDD, which is consistent with the results of this study, suggesting that CYCS may participate in the progression of LDD. It has been established that abnormal expression of CYCS can lead to metabolic disorders and functional dysfunction of cells [39], suggesting that it may be a key molecule linking mitochondrial damage and changes in cell fate. Building upon this foundation, we conducted a reversal experiment to mechanistically validate whether miR-4284 participates in the pathological process of LDD by regulating CYCS expression. Experimental results demonstrate that overexpressed CYCS not only directly activated the apoptotic pathway—as evidenced by an elevated Bax/Bcl-2 ratio—but also exacerbated mitochondrial dysfunction, specifically manifested by the downregulated expression of PGC-1α and SOD2. More importantly, overexpression of CYCS significantly reversed the protective effect of miR-4284 on NP cells, including its inhibitory effect on apoptosis and its ameliorative effect on mitochondrial homeostasis. In summary, this study demonstrates that miR-4284 participates in regulating the pathological progression of LDD by targeting CYCS, thereby influencing the apoptotic process and mitochondrial functional homeostasis of NP cells.

However, this study has several limitations. First, the sample size of this study is relatively limited, and all participants were recruited from a single medical center, which may restrict the generalizability of the findings to broader populations and could introduce a degree of selection bias. Future studies should further validate the diagnostic efficacy and clinical relevance of miR-4284 in multicenter, large-scale prospective cohorts to confirm its broad applicability and more accurately assess its clinical value. Second, regarding the methodological approach, we used U6 small nuclear RNA as the endogenous control for quantifying serum miR-4284. Although U6 is commonly used in miRNA studies, its expression stability in circulation can be influenced by various pre-analytical and physiological factors. The lack of a universally accepted normalization strategy for circulating miRNAs represents a potential technical limitation. Third, the in vitro findings are primarily based on an LPS-induced inflammatory model. While this model effectively replicates key inflammatory features of LDD, the actual degenerative microenvironment of the intervertebral disc is more complex, involving mechanical loading, hypoxia, and nutrient deprivation. Thus, future studies are needed to validate the role of the miR-4284/CYCS axis under compression, hypoxia, or multifactorial models. Additionally, while our in vitro model supports a local functional role, the study did not directly measure miR-4284 in degenerated disc tissue. Therefore, analyzing matched tissue and blood samples from patients in future work will be crucial to definitively correlate serum levels with local pathology.

Conclusion

miR-4284 is markedly decreased in the serum of LDD patients and demonstrates good diagnostic potential. Its expression correlates inversely with clinical severity and serves as a protective factor for LDD. Mechanistically, miR-4284 targets CYCS to suppress inflammatory responses and oxidative stress damage in NP cells, alleviates mitochondrial dysfunction and its mediated apoptosis, thereby helping to maintain cellular homeostasis and potentially delaying the pathological progression of LDD.

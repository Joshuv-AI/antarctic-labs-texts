Intrinsic Properties of Large CP Violation in the Complex Two-Higgs-Doublet Model
Soojin Lee, A. Hammad, Dongjoo Kim, Jeonghyeon Song

Abstract
We investigate the intrinsic properties of the parameter space capable of supporting large CP violation in the Complex Two-Higgs-Doublet Model with softly broken Z 2 Z_{2} symmetry, hosting three CP-mixed neutral Higgs mass eigenstates. Using a comprehensive global scan of Type-I and Type-II models—incorporating all relevant theoretical requirements and collider constraints together with the stringent electron EDM (eEDM) bound—we identify distinct phenomenological pathways to regions of large CP violation. In Type-I, we find that all viable parameter points predict a strict lower bound on the eEDM, | d e | ≳ 10 − 31 ​ e ⋅ cm |d_{e}|\gtrsim 10^{-31}\,e\cdot\mathrm{cm} , placing this scenario within definitive reach of next-generation EDM experiments; moreover, sizable CP violation in the gauge sector emerges only when the 125 GeV state is nearly degenerate with a second neutral Higgs boson. In contrast, Type-II suppresses gauge-sector CP violation but allows maximal CP mixing in the Yukawa sector of the heavy neutral scalars, exhibiting a strong correlation between large fermionic CP violation and the predicted eEDM. Finally, we uncover a phenomenon of “hidden CP violation” in the Higgs alignment limit: even as the heavy neutral scalars decouple from gauge bosons, their CP mixing remains entirely unconstrained and becomes increasingly maximal as the Yukawa-sector CP violation increases. We show that this hidden CP violation can be directly probed through the non-vanishing cubic interactions of the heavy neutral scalars to the charged Higgs and W W bosons.

Brief review of C2HDM

II.1
Scalar potential, vacuum structure and neutral scalar mixing

The 2HDM extends the SM by introducing two complex
SU(2)_{L}
scalar doublet fields,
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
, both with hypercharge
Y=1/2
under the
Q=T_{3}+Y
convention
Branco:2011iw
.
These fields are expressed as:

\Phi_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\phi_{1}^{+}\\
\dfrac{v_{1}+\rho_{1}+i\eta_{1}}{\sqrt{2}}\end{array}\right)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\Phi_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\phi_{2}^{+}\\
\dfrac{v_{2}+\rho_{2}+i\eta_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\end{array}\right)\;,

(1)

where
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are the
real
vacuum expectation values (VEVs) of
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
, respectively, assuming no spontaneous CP violation.
Their ratio defines the key model parameter
t_{\beta}=v_{2}/v_{1}
. For notational simplicity, we use the abbreviations
s_{x}=\sin x
,
c_{x}=\cos x
, and
t_{x}=\tan x
.
The combined VEV,
v=\sqrt{v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}}\approx 246{\;{\rm GeV}}
, induces spontaneous electroweak symmetry breaking.

To eliminate tree-level flavor-changing neutral currents (FCNCs), a discrete
Z_{2}
symmetry is imposed, under which the fields transform as
\Phi_{1}\to\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}\to-\Phi_{2}

Glashow:1976nt
;
Paschos:1976ay
.
The scalar potential with a softly broken
Z_{2}
symmetry is given by:

\begin{split}V_{\Phi}(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})&=m^{2}_{11}\Phi^{\dagger}_{1}\Phi_{1}+m^{2}_{22}\Phi^{\dagger}_{2}\Phi_{2}-(m^{2}_{12}\Phi^{\dagger}_{1}\Phi_{2}+{\rm H.c.})\\[3.0pt]
&\quad+\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda_{1}(\Phi^{\dagger}_{1}\Phi_{1})^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda_{2}(\Phi^{\dagger}_{2}\Phi_{2})^{2}+\lambda_{3}(\Phi^{\dagger}_{1}\Phi_{1})(\Phi^{\dagger}_{2}\Phi_{2})\\[3.0pt]
&\quad+\lambda_{4}(\Phi^{\dagger}_{1}\Phi_{2})(\Phi^{\dagger}_{2}\Phi_{1})+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\lambda_{5}(\Phi^{\dagger}_{1}\Phi_{2})^{2}+{\rm H.c.}\bigr],\end{split}

(2)

where the
m_{12}^{2}
term acts as the soft breaking parameter.
Due to the hermiticity of the Lagrangian, only the parameters
m_{12}^{2}
and
\lambda_{5}
can be complex.

The minimization conditions for the potential are:

\displaystyle m_{11}^{2}v_{1}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}v_{1}^{3}+\frac{\lambda_{345}}{2}v_{1}v_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Re e\left(m_{12}^{2}\right)v_{2}\;,

(3)

\displaystyle m_{22}^{2}v_{2}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}v_{2}^{3}+\frac{\lambda_{345}}{2}v_{1}^{2}v_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Re e\left(m_{12}^{2}\right)v_{1}\;,

(4)

\displaystyle 2\,\Im m(m_{12}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{1}v_{2}\Im m(\lambda_{5})\;,

(5)

where
\lambda_{345}\equiv\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4}+\Re e(\lambda_{5})
.
The condition in
Equation 5
relates two imaginary parts, leaving a single independent CP phase.
To accommodate explicit CP violation, we further assume

\phi(\lambda_{5})\neq 2\,\phi(m_{12}^{2})

Ginzburg:2002wt
,
ensuring that the two phases cannot be simultaneously removed by rephasing
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
.
This framework constitutes the C2HDM.

In the C2HDM, the three neutral Higgs bosons do not possess definite CP quantum numbers.
The mass eigenstates are denoted as
H_{1}
,
H_{2}
, and
H_{3}
, with the mass hierarchy
M_{H_{1}}<M_{H_{2}}<M_{H_{3}}

While the observed

\text{Normal scenario: }M_{H_{1}}=125{\;{\rm GeV}}.

(6)

The Higgs basis

is particularly advantageous

The relation between

III
Comprehensive Scan

In this section, we describe the comprehensive global scan performed to identify C2HDM parameter regions capable of supporting large CP violation.
We begin by detailing our scanning methodology, including the choice of input parameters and the rigorous set of theoretical and experimental constraints—most notably the eEDM—applied to ensure phenomenological viability.
We then characterize the intrinsic properties of the surviving parameter space, revealing that Type-I and Type-II models realize large CP violation through fundamentally different phenomenological patterns.
For Type-I, we identify a unique near-degenerate mass hierarchy that is essential for generating large CP violation in the gauge sector.
For Type-II, we show that large CP violation is restricted to the Yukawa sector of heavy scalars, exhibiting distinct correlations with
\tan\beta
and the soft-breaking scale.

III.1
Parameter setup and scanning methodology

We investigate the parameter space of the C2HDM with a softly broken
Z_{2}
symmetry.
The scalar potential in
Equation 2
depends on the parameters

\{m_{11}^{2},\,m_{22}^{2},\,m_{12}^{2},\,\lambda_{1,\cdots,5}\}
,
where
m_{12}^{2}
and
\lambda_{5}
can, in general, be complex.
A global
U(1)
rephasing of the Higgs doublets allows us to remove one of these two phases,
leaving a single physical CP-violating phase
\arg(\lambda_{5}(m_{12}^{2})^{-2})
.
Consequently, the model is characterized by nine independent real parameters.

A physical basis for these nine input parameters consists of:

\{\,m_{H_{a}},\penalty\ m_{H_{b}},\penalty\ m_{H^{\pm}},\penalty\ \alpha_{1},\penalty\ \alpha_{2},\penalty\ \alpha_{3},\penalty\ t_{\beta},\penalty\ \Re(m_{12}^{2}),\penalty\ v\,\},

(33)

where
m_{H_{a}}
and
m_{H_{b}}
denote the masses of two neutral scalars. The mass of the third neutral scalar,
m_{H_{c}}
, is determined via the relation
Fontes:2017zfn

m_{H_{c}}^{2}=\dfrac{m_{H_{a}}^{2}R_{a3}(R_{a2}t_{\beta}-R_{a1})+m_{H_{b}}^{2}R_{b3}(R_{b2}t_{\beta}-R_{b1})}{R_{c3}(R_{c1}-R_{c2}t_{\beta})}\;.

(34)

Crucially, as a derived quantity,
m_{H_{c}}
may emerge as the lightest, intermediate, or heaviest state depending on the input parameters.
The resulting states
\{H_{a},H_{b},H_{c}\}
are subsequently reordered according to their masses to define the mass eigenstates
\{H_{1},H_{2},H_{3}\}
, satisfying the standard hierarchy
M_{H_{1}}<M_{H_{2}}<M_{H_{3}}
.

To assess the theoretical consistency and phenomenological viability of the parameter points, we employ the public code
ScannerS v2.0.0

Coimbra:2013qq
;
Muhlleitner:2020wwk
, which fully implements the C2HDM. For efficient sampling, particularly to ensure consistency with Higgs precision data,
ScannerS
utilizes an alternative input parameterization focused on the properties of one neutral Higgs boson, identified here as the observed 125 GeV state (
H_{a}\equiv H_{1}
)
Muhlleitner:2020wwk
:

\{\,m_{H_{a}}=125{\;{\rm GeV}},\penalty\ m_{H_{b}},\penalty\ m_{H^{\pm}},\penalty\ t_{\beta},\penalty\ \Re e(m_{12}^{2}),\penalty\ c^{2}(H_{a}VV),\penalty\ |c(H_{a}t\bar{t})|^{2},\penalty\ \mathrm{sign}(R_{a3}),\penalty\ R_{b3}\,\}.

(35)

Here,
c(H_{a}VV)
is the effective
H_{a}VV
coupling normalized to the SM value (see
Equation 21
), and
|c(H_{a}t\bar{t})|^{2}=(c^{e}(H_{a}tt))^{2}+(c^{o}(H_{a}tt))^{2}
parameterizes the coupling strength to top quarks.

We perform a random scan over the following parameter ranges:

\displaystyle\in[130,2000]{\;{\rm GeV}},

\displaystyle\quad M_{H^{\pm}}

\displaystyle\in[90,2000]{\;{\rm GeV}},

\displaystyle\quad\Re e(m_{12}^{2})

\displaystyle\in[0,2\times 10^{6}]{\;{\rm GeV}}^{2},

\displaystyle t_{\beta}

\displaystyle\in[0.5,50],

\displaystyle\quad\mathrm{sign}(R_{a3})

\displaystyle\in\{\pm 1\},

\displaystyle\quad R_{b3}

\displaystyle\in[-1,1],

\displaystyle c^{2}(H_{a}VV)

\displaystyle\in[0.7,1.0],

\displaystyle\quad|c(H_{a}t\bar{t})|^{2}

\displaystyle\in[0.6,1.6].

Each generated point is subjected to a comprehensive set of constraints implemented within
ScannerS
, as outlined below:

(i) Theoretical requirements:

We require the scalar potential to be bounded from below
Ivanov:2006yq
, ensure perturbative unitarity by constraining the eigenvalues of the
2\to 2
scalar scattering matrix to be less than
8\pi
, and enforce the stability of the electroweak vacuum against decay to deeper minima
Ivanov:2008cxa
;
Barroso:2012mj
;
Barroso:2013awa
.

(ii) Electroweak and flavor constraints:

Parameter points must be consistent with electroweak precision data, specifically the oblique parameters (
S,T,U
)
Peskin:1991sw
, compatible with the global fit values from the Particle Data Group at 95% C.L.
ParticleDataGroup:2024cfk
. Constraints from flavor physics are applied at 95% C.L., including bounds from
B\to X_{s}\gamma
, neutral meson mixing, and various rare
B
-meson decays (
B_{d,s}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
,
B\to\tau\nu
,
B_{s}\to\phi\gamma
)
Haller:2018nnx
;
Arbey:2017gmh
;
Sanyal:2019xcp
;
Misiak:2017bgg
;
Belle:2017hum
;
Belle:2014sac
.

(iii) Higgs collider data:

Agreement with Higgs data is evaluated using the
HiggsTools
framework (version 1.1.3)
Bahl:2022igd
, which comprises three sub-libraries:
HiggsPredictions
,
HiggsSignals

Bechtle:2020uwn
, and
HiggsBounds

Bechtle:2020pkv
.

HiggsSignals
tests the compatibility of the 125 GeV Higgs boson (
H_{1}
) with LHC signal strength measurements, requiring the deviation
\Delta\chi^{2}=\chi^{2}_{\text{C2HDM}}-\chi^{2}_{\text{SM}}
to lie within the
2\sigma
region of the SM best-fit value.

HiggsBounds
checks that the predicted signal rates for additional Higgs bosons (
H_{2}
,
H_{3}
,
H^{\pm}
) are not excluded by direct searches at the LEP, Tevatron, or LHC.
Since the constraint on the CP-mixing angle
\alpha_{\tau}^{H_{1}}
, defined as
\alpha_{\tau}^{H_{1}}=\arctan(c^{o}(H_{1}\tau\tau)/c^{e}(H_{1}\tau\tau))
, is not natively implemented in

, we explicitly impose the ATLAS bound derived from
{h_{125}}\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
decays. We require consistency with the ATLAS measurement
\alpha_{\tau}^{H_{1}}=9^{\circ}\pm 34^{\circ}
(95% C.L.)
ATLAS:2022akr
.

(iv) eEDM:

The predicted eEDM, primarily arising from two-loop Barr-Zee diagrams
Pospelov:2005pr
;
Abe:2013qla

|d_{e}|<4.1\times 10^{-30}\,e\cdot\mathrm{cm}
from the JILA collaboration
Roussy:2022cmp
.

In evaluating consistency with Higgs precision data, we explicitly accommodate near-degenerate scenarios where the
H_{2}
mass is close to
125{\;{\rm GeV}}
. To avoid numerical singularities associated with exact mass degeneracy, we impose the following mass conditions:

M_{H_{1}}=125{\;{\rm GeV}},\quad M_{H_{2}}\geq 126{\;{\rm GeV}},\quad M_{H_{3}}\geq 130{\;{\rm GeV}}.

(36)

Through extensive random scans, we collected
1.1\times 10^{5}
viable points for Type-I and an equal number for Type-II, all of which satisfy the full set of constraints including the mass conditions in
Equation 36
. The eEDM constraint proves to be the most restrictive factor; only about 1% of the points satisfying all other requirements survive this bound in both Type-I and Type-II, thereby significantly shaping the allowed parameter space.

We now clarify the treatment of near-degenerate scenarios in
HiggsSignals

Bechtle:2013xfa

which requires careful consideration in the signal-strength evaluation.

HiggsSignals
compares theoretical predictions to experimental measurements by summing the signal strengths (
\mu_{i}
) of all neutral scalars whose masses fall within the effective mass window of a given channel,
m_{h}\pm\Delta m
.
However, the widths of these mass windows differ substantially between two categories of analysis channels.

The first category comprises the
mass-sensitive
channels, namely the high-resolution
H\!\to\!\gamma\gamma
and
H\!\to\!ZZ
analyses based on Refs.
ParticleDataGroup:2020ssz
;
ATLAS:2016neq
.
These channels employ exceptionally narrow mass windows, approximately
[124.74,125.76]{\;{\rm GeV}}
and
[124.37,125.81]{\;{\rm GeV}}
, respectively.

HiggsSignals
includes only the contributions from neutral Higgs bosons whose masses fall strictly within these windows.
3
3

3

This restriction is necessary because the experimental inputs for these channels
ParticleDataGroup:2020ssz
;
ATLAS:2016neq
are provided as signal-strength fits fixed at the 125 GeV peak, rather than as model-independent invariant-mass spectra. Consequently, a simultaneous two-state fit to overlapping
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
contributions is not feasible, making the use of predefined narrow windows the only statistically consistent approach.

Our mass condition of
M_{H_{2}}\geq 126{\;{\rm GeV}}
ensures that
H_{2}
lies
outside
these high-resolution windows.
Consequently,
H_{2}
does not contribute to the predicted signal strengths of these dominant precision channels, which drive the global
\chi^{2}
fit.

In contrast, the remaining
mass-insensitive
channels—covering almost all other Higgs decay modes such as
H\!\to\!WW
,
H\!\to\!\tau\tau
,
H\!\to\!b\bar{b}
, and
H\!\to\!\mu\mu
(and lower-resolution measurements of
\gamma\gamma
and
ZZ
)—employ substantially wider mass windows. In these analyses, the second Higgs state
H_{2}
often falls within the corresponding mass window, and
HiggsSignals
appropriately accounts for its presence by summing its contribution to the total predicted signal strength:

\mu_{i}^{\rm pred}=\mu_{i}(H_{1})+\mu_{i}(H_{2})\;.

(37)

This distinction is central to the viability of the near-degenerate scenario.
The overall
\chi^{2}
is dominated by the high-resolution channels, which possess much smaller experimental uncertainties
\delta\mu_{i}^{\text{obs}}
and thus carry the greatest statistical weight in the global fit
Bechtle:2020uwn
.
4
4

4

Note that the full
\chi^{2}
evaluation in
HiggsSignals
uses the covariance matrix of correlated experimental and theoretical uncertainties
Bechtle:2020uwn
.

By construction,
H_{2}
with
M_{H_{2}}>126{\;{\rm GeV}}
is excluded from the dominant, mass-sensitive channels
ParticleDataGroup:2020ssz
;
ATLAS:2016neq
. Consequently, although it contributes to the less precise, mass-insensitive channels, its impact on the global
\chi^{2}
remains marginal.
As shown in the following analysis, this treatment allows near-degenerate scenarios to remain phenomenologically viable, particularly within the Type-I C2HDM.

Characteristics of the large CP violation parameter space in Type-I

\xi_{V}

IV
Future prospects and observability of large CPV

In the previous section, we delineated the intrinsic characteristics of the C2HDM parameter space capable of supporting large CP violation.
We now turn to the phenomenological consequences and future prospects of these regions.
We begin by evaluating the predicted eEDM values for both Type-I and Type-II, comparing them against the projected sensitivities of next-generation experiments.
Having established these indirect constraints, we then examine the distinct collider signatures associated with the large CP-violation regimes in each model.
For Type-I, our analysis centers on the gauge sector, where large
\xi_{V}
necessitates a near-degenerate scenario between
H_{2}
and the 125 GeV
H_{1}
; here, we demonstrate how the simultaneous contributions of these two states reproduce the observed Higgs signal strengths.
Conversely, for Type-II, we focus on the Yukawa sector, investigating the magnitude and observability of the CP-violating phases in the top-quark and
\tau
-lepton couplings.
Finally, we investigate the phenomenon of
hidden
CP violation in the Higgs alignment limit, where CP mixing persists in the heavy sector despite vanishing signatures in standard gauge interactions.

IV.1
Prospect of the eEDM in the large CP violation parameter space

The future prospects for eEDM measurements are exceptionally promising, with next-generation experiments poised to improve sensitivity by one to two orders of magnitude.
Leading this frontier, the ACME III collaboration targets the
10^{-31}\,e\cdot\mathrm{cm}
regime using a cryogenic beam of Thorium Monoxide (ThO)
ACME:2018yjb

Concurrently, the third-generation JILA experiment aims for similar precision (a few times

Complementing these efforts, the NL-eEDM experiment utilizes Barium Fluoride (BaF) to provide a systematic cross-check while projecting a sensitivity between

and
10^{-32}\,e\cdot\mathrm{cm}

Achieving these sensitivities will establish eEDM measurements as a powerful probe of CP violation, with a reach extending far beyond the direct capabilities of the LHC

In Type-II, the individual contributions from
W

In contrast, Type-I features individual contributions that are intrinsically much smaller (by roughly two orders of magnitude) but lacks this natural mechanism of destructive interference.
Without the necessary sign alignment to drive cancellations between the different loop classes, the total EDM is determined by the coherent sum of the contributions.
This effectively precludes the formation of zero-sum scenarios, resulting in the strict, non-vanishing lower bound observed in

Nevertheless, despite this potential for cancellation, Type-II exhibits clear correlations with two key parameters.
First, the magnitude of
|d_{e}|

\zeta_{t}
and
\zeta_{b,\tau}

Second, a crucial correlation emerges with respect to the Higgs alignment measure
\delta_{\text{H-align}}

As
\delta_{\text{H-align}}
increases, the lower bound on
|d_{e}|
rises significantly.
This indicates that in the Type-II C2HDM, a sizable deviation from the Higgs alignment limit (e.g.,
\delta_{\text{H-align}}\gtrsim 0.06
) necessitates an eEDM large enough to be detected by future experiments.
Conversely, if the deviation from alignment is small, the predicted EDM can be heavily suppressed, potentially falling below the reach of the currently planned experiments.
This has a critical implication: if future eEDM measurements continue to yield null results, they will not only constrain the possibility of large CP violation in the Yukawa sector but will also impose increasingly stringent limits on the deviation from Higgs alignment.

In summary, the prospects for eEDM measurements offer a powerful, yet type-dependent, test of the C2HDM.
For Type-I, the robust lower bound on
|d_{e}|
creates a “no-lose” scenario: the model will either be discovered or definitively ruled out by the next generation of EDM experiments.
In contrast, the Type-II model accommodates much smaller eEDM values but exhibits strong correlations with both the CPV measures and the Higgs alignment parameter.
Consequently, future EDM bounds will serve as a potent indirect probe of the Type-II C2HDM, tightly constraining both the allowed CP violation and deviations from the Higgs alignment limit.

IV.3
The CP Structure of Top and Tau Couplings in Type-II

While the Near-Degenerate CPV Scenario in Type-I highlights the possibility of large CP violation in the gauge sector (
\xi_{V}
), the Type-II model exhibits a fundamentally different behavior.
As observed in our global scan, Type-II is severely constrained to the regime of negligible gauge-sector CP violation, effectively forcing
\xi_{V}\lesssim 3\times 10^{-3}
.
Consequently, the primary manifestation of CP violation in Type-II shifts to the Yukawa sector.
Here, the interactions of the top quark and the
\tau
lepton serve as the definitive probes of the Higgs CP parity.

To quantify the CP structure in this sector, we parameterize the Yukawa couplings between a neutral Higgs boson
H_{i}
and fermions
f
in terms of a coupling strength modifier
\kappa_{f}^{H_{i}}
and a CP-mixing angle
\alpha_{f}^{H_{i}}
:

\mathscr{L}_{\rm Yukawa}\supset-\sum_{i=1,2,3}\sum_{f=t,\tau}\frac{m_{f}}{v}\kappa_{f}^{H_{i}}\left[\cos{\alpha_{f}^{H_{i}}}+i\gamma_{5}\sin{\alpha_{f}^{H_{i}}}\right]\bar{f}fH_{i}.

(51)

V
Conclusions

In this work, we have performed a comprehensive global analysis of the Complex Two-Higgs-Doublet Model (C2HDM) with softly broken
Z_{2}
symmetry, identifying the intrinsic features of the parameter space that can support large CP violation while satisfying all current theoretical, collider, flavor, and EDM constraints. Our scan reveals that Type-I and Type-II models follow sharply distinct pathways toward sizable CP violation.

In Type-I, both the gauge- and Yukawa-sector CPV measures can reach significant magnitudes, with
\xi_{V}
rising to
\sim 0.4
and
\zeta_{t,b,\tau}
reaching values as high as
\sim 0.7
, but only within a narrow region where the second neutral Higgs boson is nearly degenerate with the 125 GeV state
H_{1}
. A particularly striking outcome is the emergence of a robust lower bound on the electron EDM,
|d_{e}|\gtrsim 10^{-31}\,e\cdot\mathrm{cm}
, which holds across the
entire
viable Type-I parameter space—independent of the size of the CP-violating measures. This universal bound places the full Type-I scenario within the projected sensitivity of next-generation eEDM experiments.

By contrast, Type-II strongly suppresses gauge-sector CP violation while still allowing large Yukawa-sector CP violation, with nearly maximal mixing between the heavier scalars
H_{2}
and
H_{3}
. In addition, destructive interference among different contributions can suppress the eEDM down to values of order
10^{-35}\,e\cdot\mathrm{cm}
, so that no phenomenologically relevant lower bound on
|d_{e}|
emerges within the presently viable parameter space. Moreover, we find that as the Yukawa-sector CP violation increases, the CP mixing in the couplings of the two heavy neutral Higgs bosons to the top quark and
\tau
lepton approaches maximal values.

Finally, we have identified a novel phenomenon in the alignment limit:
hidden CP violation
. Even though the heavy neutral scalars lose their gauge interactions in this limit—rendering standard probes insensitive to their CP nature—their mutual CP mixing remains entirely unconstrained and can become nearly maximal as the Yukawa-sector CP violation increases. We have shown that this hidden CP violation can nevertheless be accessed through cubic
[H_{2,3},H^{\pm},W^{\mp}]
vertices, offering a novel and necessary pathway to experimentally expose this elusive source of CP violation at future colliders.

Taken together, our analysis demonstrates that large CP violation in the C2HDM is highly structured and tightly constrained. The results presented here provide a systematic map of the viable parameter space and offer clear strategies for testing these scenarios through future EDM measurements, Higgs precision data, and dedicated searches for the heavy Higgs sector.

Strichartz and local smoothing estimates for the fractional Schrödinger equations over fractal time
Jin Bong Lee, Sanghyuk Lee, Luz Roncal

Abstract
. We obtain Strichartz-type estimates for the fractional Schrödinger operator f ↦ e i ​ t ​ ( − Δ ) γ / 2 ​ f f\mapsto e^{it(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}}f over a time set E E of fractal dimension. To obtain those estimates capturing fractal nature of E E , we employ the notions in the spirit of the Assouad dimension, such as, bounded Assouad characteristic and Assouad specturm. We also prove the estimate ‖ e i ​ t ​ ( − Δ ) γ / 2 ​ f ‖ L t q ​ ( d ​ μ , L x r ​ ( ℝ d ) ) ≤ C ​ ‖ f ‖ H s , \|e^{it(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}}f\|_{L_{t}^{q}(\mathrm{d}\mu;L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{s}}, where μ \mu is a measure satisfying an α \alpha -dimensional growth condition. In addition, we establish related inhomogeneous estimates and L 2 L^{2} local smoothing estimates. A surprising feature of our work is that, despite dealing with rough fractal sets, we extend the known estimates for the fractional Schrödinger operators in a natural way, precisely consistent with the associated fractal dimensions.

1.
introduction

For a positive number
\gamma
, we consider the operator

U^{\gamma}_{t}f(x)=\mathrm{e}^{it(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}}f(x):=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{i(\langle x,\xi\rangle+t|\xi|^{\gamma})}\widehat{f}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

which is a solution to the initial value problem

i\partial_{t}u+(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}u=0,\quad u(0)=u_{0}.

The case
\gamma=2
corresponds to the classical Schrödinger equation, which arises from the Brownian motion. For
\gamma\in(0,2)\setminus\{1\}
, the fractional Schrödinger equation is derived by applying Feynman’s path integral framework to Lévy motion; see Laskin
[
18
,
19
]
.

Space-time estimates, known as Strichartz estimates, play an important role in the study of both linear and nonlinear equations by providing quantitative control of the solutions. These estimates are formulated in mixed norms in
x
and
t
, whose exponents are required to satisfy an
admissible
condition.
We say that a pair
(q,r)\in\mathbb{[}2,\infty]\times[2,\infty)
is
\sigma
-admissible if

\frac{1}{q}\leq\sigma\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}\Big).

It is well known that

\|\mathrm{e}^{it(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}}f\|_{L_{t}^{q}(\mathbb{R};L^{r}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}},\quad s=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{r}-\frac{\gamma}{q}

holds for sharp
d/2
-admissible
(q,r)
, provided that
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\setminus\{1\}
(see, for example,
[
6
]
).
Here,
\dot{H}^{s}
denotes the homogeneous Sobolev space.
For the wave operator
\mathrm{e}^{it(-\Delta)^{1/2}}
, the estimate holds for
\frac{d-1}{2}
-admissible
(q,r)
.
We recommend
[
15
,
6
,
5
,
11
]
and references therein for the previous studies on the Strichartz estimate for the fractional Schrödinger equation.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}
be a bounded set.
In this note, we are concerned with the Strichartz type estimates over
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times E
, where the time variable is restricted (from

\mathbb{R}
) to a subset
E
of fractal dimension, and the Lebesgue measure
\mathrm{d}t
is replaced by a general measure
\mu
supported on

E
satisfying certain conditions. This approach enables us to investigate the temporal behavior of the solution on the lower-dimensional set
E
. Most typical examples of such sets are the Cantor sets.

Those kinds of estimates have recently appeared in the works
[
1
,
3
,
24
,
20
]
(see (
2.8
) below), where spherical maximal functions defined via the supremum over a fractal set of dilation parameters were studied. More precisely, Strichartz-type estimates for the wave operator were obtained, relating the size (or dimension) of
E

to the smoothness of the input function.

Let
N(E,\delta)
denote the minimal number of intervals of length
\delta
needed to cover
E
.
The (upper) Minkowski dimension
\dim_{M}E
of
E
is given by the infimum of
\beta>0
such that

N(E,\delta)\leq C(\varepsilon)\delta^{-\beta-\varepsilon}

for every
\epsilon>0
.
This notion is particularly useful for quantifying the size of a lower-dimensional set
E
via coverings.

However, as shown in the previous works

[
1
,
3
,
24
]
, the Minkowski dimension alone is not enough to capture the dispersive nature of the wave operator.
This is due to the multi-scale phenomena associated to frequency localization, i.e., Littlewood–Paley projection.
As to be seen later, by using the Minkowski dimension alone, it is difficult to obtain a precise measurement of quantities in relative scale with respect to the size of the frequency of a given function.

In this paper, we mainly make use of the Assouad dimension, which provides a more effective framework for obtaining quantitative estimates over fractal sets.
The Assouad dimension
\dim_{A}E
is defined by

\dim_{A}E=\inf\{a>0:\exists c>0\text{ s.t. }\forall I,\delta\in(0,|I|),N(E\cap I,\delta)\leq c\delta^{-a}|I|^{a}\},

where
I
runs over subintervals of
[0,1]
. It is clear that
\dim_{M}E\leq\dim_{A}E
. Although the definitions of the Assouad and Minkowski dimensions are similar, the inequality can be strict. For example, consider a set
E_{a}=\{n^{-a}:n=1,2,3,\dots\}
for
0<a
. Then we have

\dim_{M}(E_{a})=1/(1+a)<1=\dim_{A}(E_{a}).

We note that
\dim_{M}(E)=\dim_{A}(E)
when
E
is Ahlfors–David regular (see, for example,
[
8
, Theorem 6.4.1]
).

1.1.
Homogeneous estimates

In order to get a precise quantitative estimate for the operator
U^{\gamma}_{t}
over the fractal set
E
, the
Assouad dimension alone is insufficient due to the nature of its definition.

Unlike the typical cases, we do not assume that
E
is contained in a fixed compact set.
For
\alpha\in(0,1]
and a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}
, we define

(1.1)

[E]_{\alpha}=\sup_{0<\delta}\sup_{\delta\leq|I|}\Big(\frac{\delta}{|I|}\Big)^{\alpha}N(E\cap I,\delta),

where
I
runs over any interval in
\mathbb{R}
.
This may be regarded as a global version of the notion of bounded
\alpha
-Assouad characteristic. We say that
E
has bounded
\alpha
-Assouad characteristic if
[E]_{\alpha}<\infty
.

Our first result concerns the estimates with frequency localization. Let
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(1/4,2)
. For
j>0
, let
P_{j}
denote the Littlewood–Paley projection operator defined by

(1.2)

P_{j}f=(\psi(2^{-j}|\cdot|)\widehat{f}\,)^{\vee},\quad

and
P_{\leq 0}={\rm Id}-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}P_{j}
. Also, let
H^{s}
denote the inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces of order
s
with the norm

\|f\|_{H^{s}}^{2}=\int(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

For simplicity, we write
L_{x}^{r}:=L^{r}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for the remainder of the paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\setminus\{1\}
, and
2\leq q,r\leq\infty
with
(d/\alpha,q,r)\neq(2,2,\infty)
. Suppose that
E
is a bounded set with bounded
\alpha
-Assouad characteristic. For
\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, denote
E(\delta)=\{x:\operatorname{dist}(x,E)<\delta\}
.
Then, the estimate

(1.3)

\sup_{j\geq 1}\big\|U^{\gamma}_{t}P_{j}f\big\|_{L_{t}^{q}(E(2^{-\gamma j});L_{x}^{r})}+\big\|U^{\gamma}_{t}P_{\leq 0}f\big\|_{L_{t}^{q}(E(1);L_{x}^{r})}\leq C[E]_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{H^{s}}

holds provided that
(q,r)
satisfies

(1.4)

\frac{d}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}\Big)\geq\frac{\alpha}{q}

and

(1.5)

s\geq s_{\gamma}(q,r):=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{r}-\frac{\gamma}{q}.

If
\dim_{A}E=\alpha
, then by a straightforward application of the definition of the Assouad dimension, one obtaians the estimate (
1.3
) provided that (
1.4
) is replaced by a strict inequality.
Using some specific set such as Cantor sets, one can also show that the result is basically sharp. (See Section
2.4
.)

When
E=\mathbb{R}
, so
\alpha=1
, the result recovers the classical Strichartz estimate (
[
14
]
) via the Littlewood–Paley inequality and scaling. Indeed,
once we have the local-in-time estimate, the global-in-time estimate can also be deduced by a scaling argument using homogeneity. As
\alpha
decreases, the admissible range (
1.4
) of
(q,r)
becomes larger. This is natural, as a smaller
\alpha
indicates that control is required only on a smaller time set, thus allowing a broader range of admissible pairs. At the extreme case
\alpha=0
, for example, when
E
is a singleton, i.e.,
E=\{t_{0}\}
for some
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, the estimate (
1.3
) holds for all
r,q\geq 2
, provided that (
1.5
) is satisfied. This is, in fact, a straightforward consequence of the Sobolev embedding.

The underlying time set
E(2^{-\gamma j})
is also natural in view of the uncertainty principle, since the temporal Fourier transform of
U^{\gamma}_{t}P_{j}f
is supported in an interval of length comparable to
2^{\gamma j}
. The condition (
1.5
) is necessary for (
1.3
) to hold, and so is (
1.4
) when
s=d/2-d/r-\gamma/q
. This will be shown near the end of Section
2
.

Let

\mathcal{E}_{j}=\{\tau\}

be a maximally
2^{-\gamma j}
-separated subset of
E
. That is to say,
|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|\geq 2^{-\gamma j}
if
\tau,\tau^{\prime}
are distinct elements of
\mathcal{E}_{j}
, and for
\tau^{\prime}\in E\setminus\mathcal{E}_{j}
there is an element
\tau\in\mathcal{E}_{j}
such that
|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|<2^{-\gamma j}
.
The estimate (
1.3
) is equivalent to its discrete counterpart

(1.6)

\|U_{\tau}^{\gamma}P_{j}f\|_{\ell_{\tau}^{q}(\mathcal{E}_{j};L^{r}_{x})}\leq C2^{(s+\frac{\gamma}{q})j}[E]_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x}},

where
\|a_{\tau}\|_{\ell_{\tau}^{q}}=(\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{E}_{j}}|a_{\tau}|^{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}
. This can be justified easily using a locally constant property (Lemma
2.5
) and will be exploited later to prove Theorem
1.1
.

When
\gamma=1
,
U_{t}^{\gamma}
is the wave operator, and
E
is a bounded set, the estimates of a discrete form related to (
1.6
) have already appeared in the earlier works.
Those estimates were introduced to prove
L^{p}
–
L^{q}
boundedness of the spherical maximal function for
d\geq 3

[
1
]
, and of the circular maximal function
[
24
]
over fractal dilation sets, except for certain endpoint cases. Recently, variants of such estimates were studied by Wheeler
[
29
]
, Beltran–Roos–Ruter–Seeger
[
3
]
, and Lee–Roncal–Zhang–Zhao
[

]
, in connection with

and
L^{p}
–

Following the argument in this paper, one can also obtain the corresponding result for the wave operator; see Remark

below.

By
\dot{H}^{s}
, we denote the inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces of order
s
with the norm

\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}=\int|\xi|^{2s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

We obtain the following, which gives an essentially scaling invariant estimate. It should be noted that

in the following corollary is not assumed to be bounded.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\setminus\{1\}
, and
2\leq q,r\leq\infty
with
(d/\alpha,q,r)\neq(2,2,\infty)
and
\frac{d}{r}+\frac{\gamma}{q}<d

\alpha

(1.7)

\sup_{-\infty<j<\infty}\big\|U^{\gamma}_{t}P_{j}f\big\|_{L_{t}^{q}(E(2^{-\gamma j});L_{x}^{r})}\leq C[E]_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

holds if
(q,r)
satisfies (
1.4
) and

Here, we exclude the case

, since
the space

does not admit the Schwartz class as a natural dense

.

Although the estimates (
1.3
) and (

) give control over any set

To avoid reliance on frequency localization, we assume that

is a set on which a positive measure

is supported. Instead of the bounded

\alpha

(1.8)

\mu((t-\rho,t+\rho))\leq C\rho^{\alpha}

for all

By Frostman’s lemma (see, e.g.,

(of

)
\geq\alpha

With those assumptions, we obtain the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\setminus\{1\}
, and
2\leq q,r\leq\infty
with
(d/\alpha,q,r)\neq(2,2,\infty)

Suppose that

\displaystyle s\geq s_{\gamma\alpha}(r,q).

It is unsurprising that Theorems

Theorem 1.4
.

Let

We now consider the estimate of the form

(1.12)

1.3.
L^{2}
local smoothing estimates

Unlike the wave operator, the fractional Schrödinger operator for
\gamma>1
is known to exhibit a smoothing property when integrated over a bounded space-time region. This phenomenon is referred to as
local smoothing
, and it plays an important role in the study of nonlinear dispersive equations (see, for example,
[
16
,
17
]
). In particular, the local smoothing effect for the KdV equation goes back to the work of Kato
[
13
]
. The sharp
L^{2}
local smoothing estimate for the Schrödinger operator was independently obtained by Sjölin
[
25
]
and Vega
[
26
,
27
]
. Local smoothing for more general dispersive equations was studied by Constantin and Saut
[
7
]
.

Let
\mathbb{B}^{d}
denote a unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We now consider local smoothing estimates for
U_{t}^{\gamma}
in the similar settings discussed above.
We have the following smoothing estimate relative to a fractal measure
\mathrm{d}\mu(t)
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\gamma>1
.
Suppose that
\mu
is a measure
\mu
with
\operatorname{supp}\mu\subset[0,1]
satisfying (
1.8
).
Then, for any
s\geq\alpha(1-\gamma)/2
, there exists
C
such that

(1.21)

\left\|U_{t}^{\gamma}f\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}(\mathrm{d}\mu;L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{B}^{d}))}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{s}}.

We show that the regularity exponent
\alpha(1-\gamma)/2
is sharp, in the sense that there exist a set
E
and a measure
\mu
supported on

satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
1.6
, while the estimate (
1.21

s\geq\alpha(1-\gamma)/2
(see Proposition

Our result can also be extended to general symbols

in place of

We prove Theorem
1.6
using a temporal localization lemma, Lemma
4.1
below. By this lemma, it is possible to obtain estimates in a similar form as (
1.3
) under an assumption weaker than that of bounded Assouad characteristic.
To this end, let

E\subset[0,1].

We now recall the notion of Assouad spectrum
[
9
]
. For
\theta\in(0,1)
, the
Assouad spectrum
of
E
at parameter
\theta
is given by

\dim_{\mathrm{A}}^{\theta}E\;=\;\inf\Bigl\{\alpha>0\;:\;\exists c>0\text{ s.t. }\forall\,I,N\!\bigl(E\cap I,|I|^{1/\theta}\bigr)\leq c\Bigl(\frac{|I|}{|I|^{1/\theta}}\Bigr)^{\alpha}\Bigr\},

where
I
runs over any interval in
[0,1]
.
Note that
\dim_{\mathrm{M}}(E)=\lim_{\theta\to 0^{+}}\dim_{\mathrm{A}}^{\theta}(E)
and
\dim_{\mathrm{A}}(E)=\lim_{\theta\to 1^{-}}\dim_{\mathrm{A}}^{\theta}(E).

The Assouad spectrum interpolates between the Minkowski and Assouad dimensions. More precisely, a general comparison principle
[
9
, Proposition 3.1]
states that

\dim_{\mathrm{M}}(E)\leq\dim_{\mathrm{A}}^{\theta}(E)\leq\min\Bigl\{\frac{\dim_{\mathrm{M}}(E)}{1-\theta},\,\dim_{\mathrm{A}}(E)\Bigr\}.

For instance, for the set
E_{a}=\{n^{-a}:n=1,2,3,\dots\}
,

\dim_{\mathrm{A}}^{\theta}(E_{a})=\frac{1}{(1+a)(1-\theta)}\wedge 1,

and hence
\dim_{\mathrm{A}}^{\theta}(E_{a})=1
whenever
\theta\geq\tfrac{a}{1+a}
.

In analogy with the bounded Assouad characteristic, we define

[E]_{\alpha,\theta}=\sup_{I}\Bigl(\frac{|I|^{1/\theta}}{|I|}\Bigr)^{\alpha}N\!\bigl(E\cap I,|I|^{1/\theta}\bigr).

Clearly,
[E]_{\alpha,\theta}\leq[E]_{\alpha}
.
We say
E
has bounded
(\alpha,\theta)
-Assouad characteristic if
[E]_{\alpha,\theta}<\infty
.
Then we have the following theorem.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\gamma>1
, and
E\subset[0,1]
.
Suppose that
E
has bounded
(\alpha,(\gamma-1)/\gamma)
-Assouad characteristic.
Then, for any
s\geq\alpha(1-\gamma)/2
, there exists
C
such that

(1.22)

\sup_{j\geq 1}\left\|U_{t}^{\gamma}P_{j}f\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}((E(2^{-\gamma j});L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{B}^{d}))}+\big\|U^{\gamma}_{t}P_{\leq 0}f\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}(E(1);L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d}))}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{s}}.

In Section

In order to show that (

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then there exists a Borel set

with bounded
\alpha
-Assouad characteristic such that

\rm(A)

For integers

, there are a
2^{-l}
-separated subset

and an interval

of length

\sim 2^{k-l}

\rm(B)

There exists an Ahlfors–David
\alpha
-regular Borel measure
\mu
with
\operatorname{supp}\mu=E
, that is,

(2.48)

\mu(B(x,r))\sim r^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathrm{supp}\mu\text{ and }0<r\leq 1.

Proof.

For
\rm(A)
, we construct a Cantor set following the standard construction in
[
22
, p. 60]
(see also
[
20
, Lemma 4.1]

For the base step, say step 0, let
I_{0,1}^{\alpha}=[0,1]
.

In step

, continuously, we take
I_{1,1}^{\alpha}=[0,2^{-1/\alpha}]
and
I_{1,2}^{\alpha}=[1-2^{-1/\alpha},1]

1-2^{1-1/\alpha}

In step

|I_{k-1,j}^{\alpha}|-2^{-k/\alpha+1}=2^{-(k-1)/\alpha}-2^{-k\alpha+1}

from intervals of step
(k-1)

In step
k

I_{k,1}^{\alpha},\dots,I_{k,2^{k}}^{\alpha}
of length
2^{-k/\alpha}
.
As a result, we obtain a set

For given

\nu=\lfloor\alpha l\rfloor
such that
2^{-\nu/\alpha}\sim 2^{-l}
.
Then define

be a collection of right endpoints from

Hence, it follows from

\#(E_{l})\sim 2^{\alpha l}
.
Let
l>k\geq 0
and

be an interval of length
2^{k-l}
centered at

.
Then, for
m=\lfloor\alpha(k-l)\rfloor\in\mathbb{N}

2^{-m/\alpha+1}\geq 2^{k-l}\geq 2^{-m/\alpha}

Moreover, there exist

such that they are only intervals of
m
-step intersecting

Using

\#(E_{l}\cap I_{m,i_{j}})=2^{\nu-m}
, which yields from

that

\#(E_{l}\cap I_{m,i_{j}})=2^{\alpha k}.

There are only finite
j
’s, so we have
\#(E_{l}\cap J)\sim 2^{\alpha k}
.

For
\rm(B)
, we assign a probability measure

\mu_{1}(I_{1,i}^{\alpha})=\frac{1}{2},\quad i=1,2.

Consecutively, we define

\mu_{k}(I_{k,i}^{\alpha})=2^{-k},\quad i=1,\dots,2^{k}.

Then by Prokhorov’s theorem

Therefore, for an interval

of length

Thus we have

4^{-1}r^{\alpha}\leq 2^{-j}\leq\mu\left((x-r,x+r)\right)\leq 2^{-j+2}\leq 4r^{\alpha}.\qed

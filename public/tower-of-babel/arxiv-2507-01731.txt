On nefness of the lowest piece of Hodge modules
Ze Yun

Abstract




We give degree lower bounds for quotient line bundles of the lowest piece of a Hodge module induced by a variation of Hodge structures outside a simple normal crossing divisor. We also give sufficient conditions on when the lowest piece is nef. Classical results say when
X
is a curve or the monodromies are unipotent, the lowest piece is a nef vector bundle on
X
. In dimension greater than or equal to two, if the monodromies at infinity are not unipotent there exist counterexamples in which nefness fails. This note aims to explain the failure of nefness. Our result is a lower bound on degrees of quotient line bundles of the pullback of the lowest piece to curves, in terms of local monodromy and intersection numbers with the boundary divisors. The proof uses twisted Hodge modules, which appear naturally when specializing the lowest piece to divisors. We also give geometric examples to show that the lower bound can be achieved.

1
Introduction

The first nonzero step of the Hodge filtration of a variation of Hodge structures, equipped with its Hodge metric, has semi-positive curvature by the work of Griffiths. A common example of the lowest piece of Hodge filtration of a Hodge module is given by
R^{j}f_{*}\omega_{X/S}
for a map
f:X\rightarrow S
, and positivity of higher direct images of canonical bundle are very useful in algebraic geometry. On curves, the lowest piece of a Hodge module is always a nef vector bundle, which is proved using asymptotic behavior of Hodge metrics in
[
Fuj78
]
,
[
Zuc82
]
, and
[
Pet84
]
. In the case of SNC discriminant with unipotent monodromy along the discriminant divisor, Kawamata
[
Kaw81
, Theorem 5]
proved the lowest piece of Hodge filtration of the canonical extension of a variations of Hodge structures across the divisor is a nef vector bundle. In both of their proofs, the analytic results in
[
CKS86
]
about the asymptotics of the Hodge metric on the canonical extension, and the curvature properties of the Hodge bundle are essential.

In dimensions greater than or equal to two, the example in Fujino-Fujisawa
[
FF14
, example 7.9]
shows nefness fails in general. We would like to measure how much nefness fails for the lowest piece, by giving degree lower bounds for quotient line bundles of this sheaf pullbacked to curves. The proof of the following theorem is algebraic, by using Saito’s vanishing theorem for twisted Hodge modules, and doesn’t need analysis of the asymptotics of Hodge metric.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold,
D=\sum_{i}D_{i}
is a SNC divisor on
X
, and
\mathcal{V}
is a polarized variation of Hodge structures on
X\backslash D
. For a fixed
i
, the real numbers (rational in the case of quasi-unipotent monodromies)
-1<\beta_{ij}\leq 0
are eigenvalues of residues of the Deligne canonical extension of
\mathcal{V}
along
D_{i}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Given
C
a smooth projective curve, and a map
f:C\rightarrow X
,

(a)

If
\mathrm{Im}(C)\nsubseteq D
, then
f^{*}F_{p}M
is a nef vector bundle on
C
.

(b)

If
\mathrm{Im}(C)\subset D_{(k)}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=D_{1}\cap\cdots\cap D_{k}
, but the image of
C
doesn’t lie in any
(k+1)
intersections of boundary divisors, and
f^{*}F_{p}M\rightarrow P
is a quotient line bundle, then

\deg P\geq\min_{\text{over all possible }j_{i}}\big\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-\beta_{ij_{i}}\cdot\deg f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}))\big\}.

When the monodromies are unipotent, we have all
\beta_{ij}
equal to zero, this implies
F_{p}M
is a nef vector bundle on
X
. Therefore our result is a generalization of the semi-positivity theorem of Kawamata
[
Kaw81
]
, see also
[
FF14
]
,
[
FFS14
]
,
[
FF19
]
for discussions. Unexpectedly, the theorem also implies if the boundary divisors are nef, then
F_{p}M
is nef.

As suggested by the result, the failure of nefness comes from the locus where the Hodge module is not smooth, which is also shown by the counter examples in
[
FF14
, example 7.9]
. For curves intersecting the open set underlying the variations of Hodge structures, it can be derived from an argument in
[
BS23
, Lemma 2.6]
that the pullback of the lowest piece is still a nef vector bundle on the curve. For curves contained in the boundary divisor, we need to restrict the lowest piece to
D_{i}
and hope to do some kind of induction. The problem is directly restricting
F_{p}M
to
D_{i}
doesn’t correspond to an object directly from a Hodge module on
D_{i}
. Our method relies on the notion of twisted Hodge modules
[
SY24
]

[
DV25
]
, which naturally arises when we specialize a Hodge module to divisors. In particular, applying nearby cycles reduces the dimension of support of our (twisted) Hodge module, and eventually we are essentially in the case where
C
doesn’t meet the singular locus of the (twisted) Hodge module. The proofs uses nearby cycles for complex Hodge modules, see
[
SS23
]
and
[
SY24
]
, and our method work for complex variations of Hodge structrues which could be even not quasi-unipotent (although for real eigenvalues of residues, the equality may never be achieved; and there’s no effective methods to produce complex variations of Hodge structures not coming from geometry). The lower bound is sharp in general, as shown by various geometric examples.

All the variations of Hodge structures are polarized, and they are just abbreviated as VHS. Canonical extension of a VHS will mean Deligne’s canonical extension with eigenvalues of residues in the interval
(-1,0]
. Nefness of a coherent sheaf
\mathscr{F}
is defined by the nefness of its relative
\mathcal{O}(1)
on
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{F})
, or equivalently if for every map
f:C\rightarrow X
from a smooth projective curve, and every quotient line bundles
f^{*}\mathscr{F}\rightarrow L
,
\deg L\geq 0
.

2
Twisted Hodge modules and nearby cycles

One key ingredient of our proof is the notion of twisted Hodge modules
[
SY24
]

[
DV25
]
. In our situation, they arise naturally when taking nearby cycles
\text{gr}^{W}\text{gr}^{V}M
along a divisor, where
V
is the
V
-filtration, and
W
is the monodromy filtration on
\text{gr}^{V}M
. Let
L
be a line bundle on a complex manifold
X
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. The ring of
\alpha L
-twisted differential operators is a quasi-coherent sheaf on
X
which are locally isomorphic to the usual sheaf of differential operators,

\mathscr{D}_{X,\alpha L}|_{U}\cong\mathscr{D}_{U}[t\partial_{t}]/(t\partial t-\alpha)\cong\mathscr{D}_{U},

but with a different gluing to a sheaf on
X
. The transition map between differential operators under trivializations
\phi,\phi^{\prime}:L|_{U}\rightarrow U\times\mathbb{C}
is

t\partial_{t}\rightarrow t^{\prime}\partial_{t^{\prime}},\quad\partial_{j}\mapsto\partial_{j}+\alpha\cdot g^{-1}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{j}},

where
\phi^{\prime}\circ\phi^{-1}:U\times\mathbb{C}\rightarrow U\times\mathbb{C}
has the form
(x,t)\mapsto(x,g(x)t)
for some invertible holomorphic function
g
on
U
.

In our case, if
D
is a divisor, and
L=\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
, then one result in
[
SY24
]
says twisted Hodge modules arise as nearby cycles of Hodge modules.

Proposition 2.1

(Proposition 3.12 of
[
SY24
]
)
.

Let
\left(\mathcal{M},F_{\bullet}\mathcal{M}\right)
be a polarized complex Hodge module on
X
. For any real number
\alpha\in[-1,0)
and any integer
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, the object

\left(\operatorname{gr}_{\ell}^{W(N)}\operatorname{gr}_{\alpha}^{V}\mathcal{M},F_{\bullet-1}\operatorname{gr}_{\ell}^{W(N)}\operatorname{gr}_{\alpha}^{V}\mathcal{M}\right)

is a polarized
\alpha L
-twisted Hodge module on
X
. The object

\left(\operatorname{gr}_{\ell}^{W(N)}\operatorname{gr}_{0}^{V}\mathcal{M},F_{\bullet}\operatorname{gr}_{\ell}^{W(N)}\operatorname{gr}_{0}^{V}\mathcal{M}\right)

is a polarized complex Hodge module (without any twisting).

The relation of twisted Hodge modules with our problem is the following. Suppose the boundary divisor is
D=\sum D_{i}
which is SNC. Consider the
V
-filtration on
D_{1}
. By
[
Sch19
, exercise 11.2]
, for a right
\mathscr{D}
-module which underlies a Hodge module,
F_{p}N=F_{p}V_{<0}N
. Then

F_{p}N|_{D_{1}}=\frac{F_{p}N}{t\cdot F_{p}N}=\frac{F_{p}V_{<0}N}{t\cdot F_{p}V_{<0}N}=\frac{F_{p}V_{<0}N}{F_{p}V_{<-1}N},

hence
F_{p}N|_{D_{1}}
is filtered by coherent subsheaves

\frac{F_{p}V_{\alpha_{1}}N}{F_{p}V_{<-1}N},\cdots,\frac{F_{p}V_{\alpha_{k}}N}{F_{p}V_{<-1}N},

where
-1\leq\alpha_{1}<\cdots<\alpha_{k}<0
are related to eigenvalues of residues along
D_{1}
. Then the nefness of
F_{p}N|_{D_{1}}
is related to the nefness of
F_{p}\text{gr}^{V}N
and
F_{p}\text{gr}^{W}\text{gr}^{V}N
.

Now we explain the another new ingredient of the proof. In the proof of the main theorem, we need to specialize a Hodge module to intersections of divisors for several times. This raises the question: what’s the meaning of taking nearby cycles for a (twisted) Hodge module. After some thought, one realizes that all the properties
[
SY24
, Definition 2.1]
of
V
-filtration along a local analytic hypersurface still makes sense for twisted Hodge modules, since these properties are preserved under the formula of transition between differential operators when changing trivializations of
L
. Therefore, the the graded quotient of
V
-filtration and nearby cycles of an
\alpha L
-twisted Hodge module along another divisor still makes sense, and should be understood as a “doubly twisted” Hodge module. Keep arguing this way, we can iteratively take nearby cycles of a Hodge module along components of a simple normal crossing divisor, and what we get is an object that can be called a multiply twisted Hodge module. In the terminology of
[
DV25
]
, our
(\alpha_{1}L_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{k}L_{k})
-twisted Hodge module is an
\lambda
-twisted Hodge module, where
\lambda=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{k})\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
,
\mathfrak{h}
is the Lie algebra of the torus
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{k}
, and the
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{k}
torsor is
\tilde{X}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\Pi_{i=1}^{k}(L_{i}\backslash X)
.

By doing this, we can reduce the dimensions of supports of our (twisted) Hodge modules. Let
L,Q
be two line bundles. To illustrate, doubly twisted
\mathscr{D}
-modules should locally look like

\mathscr{D}_{X,\alpha L,\beta Q}|_{U}\cong\mathscr{D}_{U}[t\partial_{t},s\partial_{s}]/(t\partial_{t}-\alpha,s\partial_{s}-\beta)\cong\mathscr{D}_{U},

their transitions under a simultaneous trivialization of
L
and
Q
should be

t\partial_{t}\rightarrow t^{\prime}\partial_{t^{\prime}},\quad s\partial_{s}\mapsto s^{\prime}\partial_{s^{\prime}},\quad\partial_{j}\mapsto\partial_{j}+\alpha\cdot g^{-1}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{j}}+\beta\cdot h^{-1}\partialderivative{h}{x_j},

where
g
and
h
are transition functions for
L
and
Q
.

3
Lowest piece of twisted Hodge modules on curves

First we prove lowest piece of an untwisted Hodge module of pure support on a smooth projective curve
C
is always a nef vector bundle, using Saito’s vanishing theorem. The advantage of using vanishing theorem is everything is formulated algebraically, so that we don’t need to do careful analysis about the growth of Hodge metric as in
[
Fuj78
]

[
Zuc82
]

[
Pet84
]
. We work with left
\mathscr{D}
-modules in this section, and throughout this note
F_{p}M
will always mean the first nonzero piece of the Hodge filtration of a left Hodge module.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
M
be a Hodge module of pure support on a smooth projective curve
C
,
F_{p}M
is its lowest piece, then
F_{p}M
is a nef vector bundle.

Proof.

Let
F_{p}M\rightarrow P
be a quotient line bundle on
C
. Then it can be viewed as a nonzero element in
H^{0}(C,F_{p}M^{*}\otimes P)\cong H^{1}(C,F_{p}M\otimes K_{C}\otimes P^{-1})^{*}
, by Saito’s vanishing theorem, if
\deg P<0
, then
H^{1}(C,F_{p}M\otimes K_{C}\otimes P^{-1})=0
, a contradiction. Therefore
\deg P\geq 0
.

In general if
g:D\rightarrow C
is a map between smooth projective curves, we first pullback the generic variations of Hodge structures on
C
to
D
, and extend it as a Hodge module
M_{D}
on
D
. Then there exists a map
F_{p}M_{D}\rightarrow g^{*}F_{p}M
which is generically an isomorphism
[
BS23
, Lemma 2.6]
. By the discussion above, the quotient line bundles of
g^{*}F_{p}M
will also have non-negative degrees. Therefore
F_{p}M
is a nef vector bundle on
C
.
∎

Remark
.

F_{p}M
has a trivial quotient, if and only if the local system of the complex variation of Hodge structures on a Zariski open set of
C
has a trivial summand, such that the intersection of trivial summand with the lowest piece is non-zero. This is implied by
[
BS23
, section 2]
.

In other words, the lowest degree quotient is achieved only when the variation has a fixed part in the lowest piece.

If we have a vanishing theorem for twisted Hodge modules, then we could also argue as in Theorem
3.1
, and the argument will also work for real twistings. This indeed works thanks to a generalization of Saito’s vanishing theorem
[
SY24
, Theorem 4.7]

[
DV25
, Theorem 4.2]
, and the above theorem is still true, even with
\mathbb{R}
-twistings (although with
\mathbb{R}
-twistings, the equality will never be achieved).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, and
M
be an
(\alpha_{1}L_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{k}L_{k})
-twisted Hodge module with pure support on
C
. For any map of smooth projective curves
f:C_{1}\rightarrow C
, and any line bundle quotient
f^{*}F_{p}M\rightarrow P
,

\deg P\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\cdot\deg f^{*}L_{i}.

Proof.

The proof is basically the same as the untwisted case. First let
F_{p}M\rightarrow P
be a quotient line bundle on
C
. Suppose
-\deg P+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\cdot\deg L_{i}>0
, but by the vanishing theorem
[
DV25
, Theorem 4.2]
,
H^{1}(C,F_{p}M\otimes P^{-1}\otimes K_{C})=0
, a contradiction.

In general, given a map
f:C_{1}\rightarrow C
, we want to consider quotient line bundles
f^{*}F_{p}M\rightarrow P
. We can first pullback the generic twisted VHS to
C_{1}
, extend it as an
(\alpha_{1}f^{*}L_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{k}f^{*}L_{k})
-twisted Hodge module
M^{\prime}
on
C_{1}
, then there exists a map
F_{p}M^{\prime}\rightarrow f^{*}F_{p}M
which is generically an isomorphism as in the untwisted case. The conclusion follows by the above paragraph.
∎

4
A counter-example

We state the result in terms of left
\mathscr{D}
-modules, but during the proofs we work with right
\mathscr{D}
-modules. This is because the twistings for twisted Hodge modules are more natural for right
\mathscr{D}
-modules. Throughout the remaining texts,
M
will always mean a left
\mathscr{D}
-module, and the corresponding right
\mathscr{D}
-module
M\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\omega_{X}
will be denoted by
N
.

We will first state our main result, then show that it explains the failure of nefness in the example of
[
FF14
, Example 7.9]
.
X
will be a compact complex manifold,
D=\sum_{i}D_{i}
is a SNC divisor on
X
,
\mathcal{V}
is a polarized variations of Hodge structures on
X\backslash D
. Recall the Deligne canonical extension with eigenvalues of residues in
(-1,0]
, denoted by
\mathcal{V}^{>-1}
, is a vector bundle with logarithmic connection, and eigenvalues of residues are real numbers in the interval
(-1,0]
. If we assume quasi-unipotent monodromy, then the eigenvalues of residues are in fact rational numbers in
(-1,0]
. The Hodge module
M
extending the VHS
\mathcal{V}
is a
\mathscr{D}
-module
M=\mathscr{D}_{X}\cdot\mathcal{V}^{>-1}
generated by the canonical extension, and the lowest piece
F_{p}M
is equal to
j_{*}F_{p}\mathcal{V}\cap\mathcal{V}^{>-1}
, where
j:X\backslash D\rightarrow X
is the inclusion. Let
-1<\beta_{k}<\cdots<\beta_{1}\leq 0
be eigenvalues of residues of the canonical extension
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{>-1}
along a component
D_{1}
of the SNC divisor
D
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Given a smooth projective curve
C
, and a map
f:C\rightarrow X
,

(a)

If
\mathrm{Im}(C)\nsubseteq D
, then
f^{*}F_{p}M
is a nef vector bundle on
C
.

(b)

If
\mathrm{Im}(C)\subset D_{(k)}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=D_{1}\cap\cdots\cap D_{k}
, but the image of
C
doesn’t lie in any
(k+1)
intersections of boundary divisors, and
f^{*}F_{p}M\rightarrow P
is a quotient line bundle, then

\deg P\geq\min_{\text{over all possible }j_{i}}\big\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-\beta_{ij_{i}}\cdot\deg f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}))\big\}.

Example 4.2

(
[
FF14
]
)
.

Consider the projective bundle
X=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(2))
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and
\pi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
. This projective bundle has two sections, whose images are called
E
and
G
, a fiber is called
F
. A simple computation shows
E^{2}=-2
,
G^{2}=2
,
E\cdot F=G\cdot F=1
,
E\cdot G=0
. Since
2(E+F)\sim E+G
are linearly equivalent, we define
L\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(E+F)
, then we can construct a degree two cover
f:\tilde{X}\rightarrow X
, ramified along
E+G
. This cover induces a VHS
\mathcal{V}
on
X\backslash(E\cup G)
, whose lowest piece of its right Hodge module extension
N
is
f_{*}\omega_{\tilde{X}}
. The corresponding lowest piece of the left Hodge module is
f_{*}\omega_{\tilde{X}/X}
.

Since
f_{*}\omega_{X^{\prime}/X}=\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus L
,
\deg(L|_{E})=-1
, then
F_{p}M
is not nef on
X
.

The monodromy matrices along
E
and
G
, are

\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}
,
whose eigenvalues are
1,-1
. A logarithm of it divided by
2\pi i
is

\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&-\frac{1}{2}\end{pmatrix}
. Then the
V
-filtration of
M
along
E
or
G
has indices jumping at
0
and
-\frac{1}{2}
. Since
E^{2}=-2
,
\beta_{1}=0
,
\beta_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\min\{-\beta_{1}\cdot E^{2},-\beta_{2}\cdot E^{2}\}=-1.

This shows our estimate is sharp for surfaces. In a later section more examples of this type will be given, and show that our lower bound can be achieved in general.

One could try to argue as previous classical works using curvature properties of Hodge metric on
F_{p}M
, which is a singular Hermitian metric with semi-positive curvature. However, the problem comes from the possible appearance of nonzero Lelong numbers of the metric along the boundary divisor. In the setting of
[
FF19
]
, when the VHS is unipotent, the Lelong numbers are zero everywhere, then by the current approximation theorem of Demailly, it can be shown that the lowest piece is nef. The metric growth of canonical extension of
\mathcal{V}
is controlled by the indices of the
V
-filtration, hence the Lelong numbers of the Hodge metric on
F_{p}M
are controlled by these indices. But in the general case of quasi-unipotent monodromy, it seems that the jumping indices of the
V
-filtration, or the nonzero Lelong numbers of the Hodge metric are responsible for the failure of nefness. Our theorem may therefore also be proved using analytic arguments. Nonetheless, our method is algebraic, and suggests the usefulness of twisted Hodge modules when specializing to divisors.

5
Bounding failure of nefness of the lowest piece

In this section, as before, we state the results in terms of left
\mathscr{D}
-modules, and during the proofs we use right
\mathscr{D}
-modules.
X
will be a compact complex manifold,
D=\sum_{i}D_{i}
is a SNC divisor on
X
,
\mathcal{V}
is a polarized variations of Hodge structures on
X\backslash D
. For convenience, both
\text{gr}^{V}M
and
\text{gr}^{W}\text{gr}^{V}M
for a Hodge module
M
are called nearby cycles of
M
. This won’t cause confusions for the remaining contents.

Our main idea is iteratively perform nearby cycles
\text{gr}^{W_{i}}\text{gr}^{V_{i}}M
several times to restrict Hodge modules to intersection of divisors. However, there’s an issue. For
i\neq j
, we can first take
M^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\text{gr}^{W_{i}}\text{gr}^{V_{i}}M
, get a twisted Hodge module on
X
, and then apply a
\text{gr}^{W^{\prime}_{j}}\text{gr}^{V^{\prime}_{j}}
nearby cycle along
D_{j}
, where
i\neq j
and
V_{i}
means the
V
-filtration along
D_{i}
, and
W_{i}
is the associated weight filtration. Here
V^{\prime}_{j}
is the
V
-filtration for
M^{\prime}
along
D_{j}
, but it’s not clear whether this should be the projection of the original
V
-filtration of
M
along
D_{j}
. In our simple normal crossing case, this is true, by the nilpotent orbit theorem
[
CKS86
]
. In particular, the jumping indices for
V
-filtrations are just residues of the canonical extension along the divisors.

We will need a technical lemma below. It basically says, for our purpose, we may assume the twisted Hodge module
\text{gr}^{W_{1}}\text{gr}^{V_{1}}M
is of pure support, i.e.,
\text{gr}^{W_{1}}\text{gr}^{V_{1}}M
is the intermediate extension of the (twisted) VHS on the open part. Given a VHS on
(\Delta^{*})^{n}
, we extend it as a Hodge module
M
. Consider the
V
-filtration along a coordinate axis, say
D_{1}=(z_{1}=0)
. The following lemma says, if
F_{p}M
is the lowest piece of
M
, then
F_{p}\text{gr}^{W_{1}}\text{gr}^{V_{1}}M
is torsion free on
D_{1}
. Even if
\text{gr}^{W_{1}}\text{gr}^{V_{1}}M
has
\mathscr{D}
-module components supported on lower dimensional subvarieties of
D_{1}
, the lower dimensional components doesn’t contribute to the lowest piece, which means
F_{p}\text{gr}^{W_{1}}\text{gr}^{V_{1}}M
only comes from the VHS on
D_{1}^{*}=(\Delta^{*})^{n-1}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Given a polarizable Hodge-Lefschetz module
(M,N,W_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}=W(N)_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}})
in the sense of Definition 3.6 in
[
DLY24
]
which is torsion-free on a smooth variety
Y
. Let
F_{p}M\neq 0
and
F_{p-1}M=0
, then the lowest piece of Hodge modules
F_{p}\text{gr}^{W}_{r}M
for
r\geq 0
are torsion-free on
Y
, and
F_{p}\text{gr}^{W}_{r}M=0
for
r<0
.

Proof.

Given a local section
m\in F_{p}M
, and
m\in W_{l}\backslash W_{l-1}
. such that
f\cdot[m]=0\in\text{gr}^{W}_{l}M
for some local holomorphic function
f
. By
[
DLY24
, Lemma 3.7]
, we have
l
is the minimum such that
N^{l+1}m=0\in M
. And we know
f\cdot m\in W_{l-h}\backslash W_{l-h-1}
for some
h\geq 1
since
f\cdot[m]=0
. Also
f\cdot m\in F_{p}M
, then by that lemma again we have
N^{l-h+1}fm=f(N^{l-h+1}m)=0
. Since
M
is assumed to be torsion-free,
N^{l-h+1}m=0
, which contradicts the minimality of
l
. Hence
F_{p}\text{gr}^{W}_{l}M
is torsion-free.
∎

We prove the first part of the main theorem, which is the case when
C
is not inside the boundary divisor. Also note that here we don’t need to assume that

is SNC, but the price is

may not be locally free.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold,
D
is a divisor on
X
,
\mathcal{V}
is a VHS on
X\backslash D
, and
M
is the extension of
\mathcal{V}
as a Hodge module. Given a map
f:C\rightarrow X
where
C
is a smooth projective curve, if
\mathrm{Im}(C)\nsubseteq D
, then
f^{*}F_{p}M
is a nef coherent sheaf on
C
.

Proof.

Since the image of
C
is not contained in
D
, we can first pullback the generic VHS to a Zariski open set of
C
, and extend it to a Hodge module
M_{C}
on
C
. By functoriality of Hodge modules, as long as the image of
C
is not contained in
D
, there exists a map
F_{p}M_{C}\rightarrow f^{*}F_{p}M
which is generically an isomorphism
[
BS23
, Lemma 2.6]
.

Since
F_{p}M_{C}
is a nef vector bundle by Theorem
3.2
,
f^{*}F_{p}M
is also a nef coherent sheaf on
C
. The same argument works even when
D
is not simple normal crossing, so when
D
is not SNC
f^{*}F_{p}M
is still nef on
C
.
∎

Now let
X,D,\mathcal{V}
be as at the beginning of this section, i.e.,
X
will be a compact complex manifold,
D=\sum_{i}D_{i}
is a SNC divisor on
X
,
\mathcal{V}
is a polarized variations of Hodge structures on
X\backslash D
. For a fixed
i
, let
-1<\beta_{ij}\leq 0
be eigenvalues of residues of the canonical extension
V^{>-1}
along
D_{i}
.

For completeness and convenience, we include the following lemma. It says when a coherent sheaf on a curve is filtered, and we want to give a lower bound on the degrees of its line bundle quotients, it suffices to give such lower bounds on the graded objects of the filtration. To keep the statement of the lemma concise, we denote by
d(\mathscr{F})
be the smallest degree of quotient line bundles of a coherent sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on a curve.

Lemma 5.3
.

Given a coherent sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on a smooth projective curve with a finite increasing filtration
L_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}\mathscr{F}
on a curve, then

d(\mathscr{F})\geq\min_{i}\{d(\text{gr}^{L}_{i}\mathscr{F})\}.

Proof.

Assume
0=L_{0}\mathscr{F}\subset L_{1}\mathscr{F}\subset\cdots\subset L_{k}\mathscr{F}=\mathscr{F}
. It suffices to show
d(\mathscr{F})\geq\min\{d(\text{gr}^{L}_{k}\mathscr{F}),d(L_{k-1}\mathscr{F})\}
, then an easy induction will completes the proof of the lemma.

Let
\mathscr{F}\rightarrow P
be a quotient line bundle. If the composition of the morphisms
L_{k-1}\mathscr{F}\rightarrow\mathscr{F}\rightarrow P
is nonzero, then
d(\mathscr{F})\geq\deg P\geq d(L_{k-1}\mathscr{F})
; otherwise the morphism
\mathscr{F}\rightarrow P
factors through
\mathscr{F}\rightarrow\text{gr}^{L}_{k}\mathscr{F}\rightarrow P
, so
P
is also a quotient of
\text{gr}^{L}_{k}\mathscr{F}
, and we have
d(\mathscr{F})\geq\deg P\geq d(\text{gr}^{L}_{k}\mathscr{F})
. We have proved
d(\mathscr{F})\geq\min\{d(\text{gr}^{L}_{k}\mathscr{F}),d(L_{k-1}\mathscr{F})\}
.

∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve. Let
f:C\rightarrow X
, such that
\mathrm{Im}(C)\subset D_{(k)}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=D_{1}\cap\cdots\cap D_{k}
, but the image of
C
doesn’t lie in any
(k+1)
intersections of boundary divisors, and
f^{*}F_{p}M\rightarrow P
is a quotient line bundle, then

\deg P\geq\min_{\text{over all possible }j_{i}}\big\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-\beta_{ij_{i}}\cdot\deg f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}))\big\}.

Proof.

If
\mathrm{Im}(C)\subset D
, to illustrate the idea, assume
f:C\rightarrow D_{1}\hookrightarrow X
, and the image of
C
doesn’t lie in the intersections of boundary divisors. Remember
N=M\otimes K_{X}
is the corresponding right
\mathscr{D}
-module. Consider the
V
-filtration of
N
along
D_{1}
. As explained in section 2,
F_{p}N|_{D_{1}}
is filtered by coherent subsheaves

\frac{F_{p}V_{\alpha_{1}}N}{F_{p}V_{<-1}N},\cdots,\frac{F_{p}V_{\alpha_{k}}N}{F_{p}V_{<-1}N},

where
-1\leq\alpha_{1}<\cdots<\alpha_{k}<0
are related to the monodromy along
D_{1}
; in fact,
(-\alpha_{i}-1)
are exactly the eigenvalues of residues of canonical extension of
\mathcal{V}
along
D_{1}
. The graded quotients of this filtration by subsheaves are
F_{p}\text{gr}^{V}_{\alpha_{i}}
, which is the pushforward of a coherent sheaf from
D_{1}
to
X
. By
[
SY24
, Proposition 3.12]
, we know
\text{gr}^{W}_{l}\text{gr}^{V}_{\alpha}N
is an
\alpha L
-twisted Hodge module on
X
supported on
D_{1}
, for
\alpha\in[-1,0)
,
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
L=\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{1})
. Now by Lemma
5.3
, it remains to understand the degree lower bounds of quotient line bundles of the lowest piece of twisted Hodge modules
F_{p}\text{gr}^{W}_{l}\text{gr}^{V}_{\alpha_{i}}N\otimes K_{X}^{-1}
on
D_{1}
, since
F_{p}\text{gr}^{W}_{l}\text{gr}^{V}_{\alpha_{i}}N\otimes K_{X}^{-1}
are subquotients of
F_{p}M|_{D_{1}}
.

By Theorem
3.2
, every line bundle quotient
P_{\alpha_{i},l}
of

f^{*}(F_{p}\text{gr}^{W}_{l}\text{gr}^{V}_{\alpha_{i}}N\otimes K_{X}^{-1})=f^{*}(F_{p}\text{gr}^{W}_{l}\text{gr}^{V}_{\alpha_{i}}N\otimes K_{D_{1}}^{-1}\otimes(K_{D_{1}}\otimes K_{X}^{-1}|_{D_{1}})

has
\deg P_{\alpha_{i},l}-\deg f^{*}(K_{D_{1}}\otimes K_{X}^{-1}|_{D_{1}})\geq\alpha_{i}\cdot\deg f^{*}L
, hence

\deg P_{\alpha_{i},l}\geq(1+\alpha_{i})\cdot\deg f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{1})=-\beta_{i}\deg\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{1}).

Here the the appearance of canonical bundles of
X
and
D_{1}
is due to left-right conversions of
\mathscr{D}
-modules, since our convention for taking twisted Hodge modules is using right
\mathscr{D}
-modules
[
SY24
, Remark 3.3]
, but the object we are interested in is the lowest piece of the left Hodge module.

Now we return to our original object,
f^{*}F_{p}M
. It’s filtered by some coherent subsheaves, and the quotient line bundles of the subquotients have degree lower bounds in terms of
\deg f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{1})
and monodromy along
D_{1}
. Then by Lemma
5.3
, the degree of the quotient line bundles
f^{*}F_{p}M\rightarrow P
is bounded below by the minimum of all the lower bounds, if given a map
f:C\rightarrow D_{1}\hookrightarrow X
,

\deg P\geq\min_{i}\{-\beta_{i}\cdot\deg f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{1})\}.

In the general case, if
\mathrm{Im}(C)\subset D_{(k)}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=D_{1}\cap\cdots\cap D_{k}
, but the image of
C
doesn’t lie in any
(k+1)
intersections of boundary divisors, then
we take nearby cycles along
D_{1}
up to
D_{k}
, and the object we get is an

(\alpha_{1}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{1})|_{D_{(k)}},\cdots,\alpha_{k}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{k})|_{D_{(k)}})\text{-twisted Hodge module }N_{k}.

By assumption
\mathrm{Im}(C)
is not contained in the singular locus of this (twisted) Hodge module, we can first pullback the (twisted) VHS to
C
, then extend it as a multiply twisted Hodge module
N^{\prime}
on
C
. Then there exists a map
F_{p}N^{\prime}\otimes K_{C}^{-1}\rightarrow f^{*}(F_{p}N_{k}\otimes K_{D_{(k)}}^{-1})
as in
[
BS23
, Lemma 2.6]
which is generically an isomorphism. The quotient line bundle
f^{*}(F_{p}N_{k}\otimes K_{X}^{-1})\rightarrow L
, by an application of Theorem
3.2
in the multiply twisted case, has a degree lower bound
\sum\alpha_{i}f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i})+f^{*}(K_{D_{(k)}}\otimes K_{X}^{-1})=\sum(1+\alpha_{i})f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i})
, which implies our theorem.
∎

Now we state two corollaries. The first one is originally due to Kawamata
[
Kaw81
]
. His proof uses Cattani-Kaplan-Schmid’s results about VHS, and our proof of the main theorem is essentially in the same spirit of Kawamata, by specializing the lowest piece to the intersection of divisors. It’s also proved in
[
FF19
]
using asymptotics of the Hodge metric, and a current approximation theorem in complex analytic geometry. See also
[
FF14
]
and
[
FFS14
]
The second corollary is new. In the case of abelian varieties, it is also proved in
[
PPS17
, Corollary 20.2]
, using methods of generic vanishing theory. They actually proved something stronger in that case, that
F_{p}M
becomes globally generated possibly after some isogenies.

Corollary 5.5
.

If
D
is a normal corssing divisor, and the monodromies along each component of
D
are unipotent, then
F_{p}M
is a nef vector bundle on
X
.

Proof.

When
D
is simple normal crossing, because all
\beta_{ij}=0
in the case of unipotent monodromy, then the main theorem implies all quotient line bundles have non-negative degrees.

If the divisor is only assumed to be normal crossing, then each component
D_{i}
could have self-intersections. We blow up all the intersections of divisors and get
\mu:\tilde{X}\rightarrow X
, to make
\mu^{*}D
have simple normal crossing support. Pullback the canonical extension
V
on
X
to
\tilde{X}
, call it
\tilde{V}
, and
\tilde{V}
is a locally free sheaf with a logarithmic connection along
\mu^{-1}(D)
, extending the VHS on
\tilde{X}-\mu^{-1}(D)
. Since the VHS on
X-D
is unipotent, the residues of the canonical extension are nilpotent. By a local computation of blowing ups,
\tilde{V}
also has nilpotent residues. By uniqueness
[
HTT08
, Theorem 5.2.17]
, this implies
\tilde{V}
is the canonical extension on
\tilde{X}
. Denote the corresponding Hodge module on
\tilde{X}
by
\tilde{M}
, we have
F_{p}\tilde{M}=F_{p}\tilde{V}
by
[
Sch19
, exercise 11.2]
. Then

F_{p}\tilde{M}=F_{p}\tilde{V}=\mu^{*}F_{p}V=\mu^{*}F_{p}M.

By the first paragraph,
F_{p}\tilde{M}
is nef, then
F_{p}M
is also nef.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

(a)

If each component of
D
is a nef divisor, and
D
is simple normal crossing, then
F_{p}M
is a nef vector bundle on
X
.

(b)

If
X
is a surface, and each
D_{i}
is a nef divisor (could be singular), then
F_{p}M
is a nef coherent sheaf on
X
.

Proof.

For (a), this is because
-\beta_{ij}\geq 0
, and each
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i})
is a nef line bundle. For (b), the same conclusion of the main theorem is still true for surfaces, with possibly non-SNC boundary divisor
D
, since taking nearby cycles along a singular curve on a surface can only have extra components of twisted Hodge modules at the singular points of the curve, so doesn’t really contribute to quotient line bundles.
∎

The next result says that
F_{p}M
restricted to a smooth complete intersection curve is nef. A general complete intersection curve should lie outside the boundary divisor and nefness holds true for these kind of curves, by the first part of the main theorem. Our result says nefness still holds even if it’s a complete intersection lying inside the boundary divisor. Note that we also don’t assume
D
is simple normal crossing in the following proposition.

Proposition 5.7
.

On a smooth projective variety
X
of dimension
n
, a Hodge module
M
coming from a generic VHS on
X
, then if a curve
f:C\rightarrow X
, and the image of
C
is a smooth complete intersection curve,
f^{*}F_{p}M
is a nef coherent sheaf on
C
.

Proof.

Suppose
C
is the transversal intersections of ample divisors
H_{1},\cdots,H_{n-1}
. Let
F_{p}M
be the lowest piece of the Hodge module we are considering.
F_{p}M|_{C}
is filtered some many coherent subsheaves, whose subquotients are exactly equal to the lowest piece of some twisted Hodge modules supported on
C
(produced by taking nearby cycles). By
lemma

5.3
, it suffices to give degree lower bounds for these lowest piece of twisted Hodge modules restricted to
C
.

We do nearby cycles for
n-1
times for
H_{1}
to
H_{n-1}
, and get a
(\alpha_{1}\cdot H_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n-1}\cdot H_{n-1})
-twisted right Hodge module
N
on
X
, with support contained on
C
, where
\alpha_{i}\in[-1,0)
are real numbers. We may assume
N
has pure support
C
, since if the support is a point then it won’t have quotient line bundles. Let
F_{p}N\otimes K_{X}^{-1}|_{C}\rightarrow P
be a quotient line bundle on
C
. The factor
K_{X}^{-1}
shows up since
N
is a right twisted Hodge module. By our previous result Theorem
3.2
, we have

F_{p}N\otimes K_{C}^{-1}\rightarrow P\otimes(K_{C}^{-1}\otimes K_{X}|_{C}),\quad\deg P\otimes(K_{C}^{-1}\otimes K_{X}|_{C})\geq\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\cdot\deg H_{i}|_{C}.

Since
K_{C}\otimes K_{X}^{-1}|_{C}\cong\otimes_{i}\mathcal{O}_{X}(H_{i})|_{C}
, we have

\deg P\geq\sum_{i}(1+\alpha_{i})\cdot\deg\mathcal{O}_{X}(H_{i})|_{C}\geq 0.

For curves mapping to a smooth complete intersection curve, the proof is similar, just pullback the generic twisted VHS on the complete intersection curve, and use Theorem
3.2
.
∎

The above proposition is really a local statement around the curve. If normal bundle of
C
is nef and splits into line bundles, and
T_{X}|_{C}\cong T_{C}\oplus N_{C/X}
(hence it has a tubular neighborhood in
X
isomorphic to a neighborhood of the zero section in its normal bundle) , then by the same proof as above
F_{p}M|_{C}
is nef.

In the above proposition, we cared neither about the order of taking nearby cycles, nor about taking nearby cycles give a twisted Hodge module with strict support or not, since in the end the coefficients of the twistings turn out to be positive. However, during the proof of the main theorem, it’s important to know the lowest piece of nearby cycles comes from a twisted Hodge module with strict support, since we want to know explicitly the coefficients of the lower bound, which are eigenvalues of residues of Deligne canonical extension.

6
Examples

We show that our lower bound is sharp by some examples.

Example 6.1
.

The example in
[
FF14
]
also works when dimension is greater than two. Take
\pi:X\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(2))\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}
. We have two sections
E,G
.
E
corresponds to the quotient
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(2)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
, and
G
corresponds to
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(2)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(2)
. A simple computation shows
\mathcal{O}_{X}(E+G)
is isomorphic to
2L\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=2(\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)-\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1))
. Let
\tilde{X}
be the branched cover of
X
of degree
2
, branched over
E,G
. We get
f_{*}\omega_{\tilde{X}/X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus L
. Since
L|_{E}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-1)
, the degree of
L
restricted to any line in
E\cong\mathbb{P}^{n}
is equal to
-1
. This shows our estimate is still sharp in higher dimensions.

In the above example, two hypersurfaces are not intersecting each other. Next we present an example of a 3-fold
X
, where the lower bound can still be achieved on a curve which is the intersection of two smooth surfaces in
X
. The same method can be used to produce more examples in higher dimensions, indicating our lower bound can be achieved on all intersections of boundary divisors and in all dimensions.

Example 6.2
.

Let
Y
be a smooth projective 3-fold,
D_{1},D_{2}
are smooth hypersurfaces in
Y
, intersecting transversely at a curve
C
. Suppose
\deg\mathcal{O}_{Y}(D_{1})|_{C}<0
,
\deg\mathcal{O}_{Y}(D_{2})|_{C}<0
, and there exists a line bundle
L
on
Y
such that
L^{N}\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}(D_{1}+D_{2})
for some positive integer
N>1
.

Consider a branched cover of
Y
, by taking the
N
-th roots of the section of
L^{N}
vanishing along
D_{1}+D_{2}
. Resolving the singularity of this branched cover, we get another smooth projective 3-fold
X
, with a map
g:X\rightarrow Y
étale over
Y-(D_{1}+D_{2})
, and

g_{*}\omega_{X/Y}\cong\oplus_{i=0}^{N-1}L^{i}.

The eigenvalues of residues of the canonical extension of the VHS induced by
g
on
Y-(D_{1}+D_{2})
along
D_{1},D_{2}
are
-\frac{N-1}{N},-\frac{N-2}{N},\cdots,\frac{-1}{N},0
. By the main theorem, any line bundle quotient of
(g_{*}\omega_{X/Y})|_{C}
has degree greater than or equal to

\min_{a,b\in[0,N-1)}\big\{\frac{a}{N}\cdot\deg\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{1})|_{C}+\frac{b}{N}\cdot\deg\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{2})|_{C}\}.

By assumption, since
\deg\mathcal{O}_{Y}(D_{1})|_{C}<0
and
\deg\mathcal{O}_{Y}(D_{2})|_{C}<0
, the above expression is equal to

\frac{N-1}{N}\cdot\deg\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{1}+D_{2})|_{C}=\deg L^{N-1}|_{C}<0.

This shows that the quotient line bundle
L^{N-1}|_{C}
of
(g_{*}\omega_{X/Y}|_{C})
achieves the equality case of the lower bound, and
g_{*}\omega_{X/Y}
is not nef.

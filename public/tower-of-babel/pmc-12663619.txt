FlowLITE: A protocol to characterize and quantify total antibody isotypes in human plasma using flow cytometry
Authors: Hyunjae J. Hahm, Alexandra F. Nazzari, Benjamin R. Babcock, Eliver E.B. Ghosn
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryFlowLITE is a flow cytometry bead-based assay that enables multiplexing and high-throughput quantification of all antibody isotypes from humans, mice, or other species, using small volumes of any fluid specimen. Here, we detail a FlowLITE protocol to quantify and characterize antibody light-chain and isotype expression in human plasma. We describe steps for titrating the capture and reveal antibodies, multiplexing assay beads, and staining samples. We then describe procedures for collecting and analyzing flow cytometry data and quantifying antibodies.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Babcock et al.1

Before you begin

FlowLITE is a flow cytometry bead-based assay to quantify antibody LIght chain and isoType Expression simultaneously. This protocol was initially optimized for human antibodies but can be easily adapted to measure antibodies from mice and other species. FlowLITE quantifies secreted antibodies by characterizing their heavy chain isotypes and subclasses (IgM, IgG1, IgG2, IgG3, IgG4, IgA1, IgA2, IgE) as well as their paired immunoglobulin light chain isotype (kappa versus lambda) (Figure 1).2 IgD, while not included in this protocol, is also known to be secreted in specific contexts particularly at mucosal sites and can be added to this assay based on the user’s sample type and biological question. FlowLITE is adapted from our original FlowBEAT assay, a complementary method that also detects the antigen specificity of the antibody, in addition to their heavy and light chain isotype usage. This modular system can be easily adapted to measure other targets and various sample types (plasma,1 serum, cell culture supernatant, tissue homogenate, nasal swabs,1 etc.).Figure 1Schematic of 8-bead FlowLITE assay(A) FlowLITE consists of fluorescent yellow beads coated with anti-human isotype antibodies (monoclonal mouse IgG1). The flowLITE platform allows for antibody quantification over a broad linear range. For example, IgM can be measured in a 500-fold dilution range (9.8 to 5,000 ng/mL) (Figure 3), which is significantly higher than the 60-fold range (15.6 to 1,000 ng/mL) achieved by conventional ELISA.3 The linear range of the FlowLITE assay can be further extended by adjusting parameters such as the number of beads per reaction, incubation time, and sample dilution, further emphasizing the modular nature of this protocol. In our previous work, decreasing the number of beads per reaction for samples containing low antibody concentration enabled detection at the picogram per microliter range.1 FlowLITE also has the capacity to measure antibodies in low sample volumes under 10 μL and allows for multiplexing, measuring multiple targets in one assay.

In this protocol, we discriminate the antibody isotypes and subclasses using a streptavidin-coated bead series with varying levels of fluorescence (Spherotech beads, yellow series). Each distinct yellow bead species is first coated with a biotin-conjugated anti-isotype monoclonal antibody (i.e., bead-1 is coated with anti-IgM, bead-2 is coated with anti-IgG1, bead-3 is coated with anti-IgG2, etc.). The anti-isotype-coated beads are then combined (multiplexed) and incubated with the biological sample. After incubation, the captured antibodies are revealed with a fluorescently labeled monoclonal antibody that targets the light chain constant region (anti-kappa PE and anti-lambda PE). The samples are run on a flow cytometer to obtain the median fluorescence intensity (MFI) value for each bead/isotype/light chain combination. Finally, FlowLITE can directly convert the PE MFI values to absolute counts of antibody molecules per sample using QuantiBrite phycoerythrin (PE) beads (BD Biosciences).

Innovation

FlowLITE is a quantitative and high-throughput assay for antibody profiling using flow cytometry. This bead-based assay provides an enhanced linear range compared to standard ELISA kits,3 which can measure only one target per assay. FlowLITE is highly customizable, allowing users to select capture and reveal antibodies tailored to their needs. FlowLITE can be readily adopted using standard lab equipment and run on most flow cytometers. Combining FlowLITE with QuantiBrite reagents (BD Biosciences)4,5 can also provide absolute counts of antibody molecules per sample.

Staining protocol

Note: All wash steps throughout this protocol are performed by adding 150 μL (plate format) or 2 mL (FACS tubes) of media, followed by centrifugation and aspiration of the supernatant. All centrifugation steps are performed in a swinging-bucket centrifuge at 500 × g for 5 min at 4°C with the brake set to ½. Reducing the break speed can improve bead recovery. Centrifugation can be increased to 1000 × g for 10 min if needed. After centrifugation, avoid excessive movement that can disturb the pellet. Note: For all staining steps, the exact ratio of reagents is critical; however, the volumes used can be scaled up or down as needed based on the desired number of beads. To ensure accurate staining volumes, aspirate the supernatant without disturbing the bead pellet. Resuspend the beads in media and gently mix them with a pipette. For plate format, use a multi-channel P200 pipette for resuspension. After resuspending, the volume will be slightly greater due to the residual media. Transfer the exact volume required to a new well. This results in bead loss; however, it is crucial to maintain consistent ratios for staining. Note: Mix samples with beads thoroughly via pipette or vortex at the start and 15 min into all staining steps to ensure consistent and uniform antibody-antigen interactions.

Measurement of antibody isotypes is dependent on the anti-isotype capture antibody conjugated to each bead. This step describes the bead loading titration, which is needed to determine the saturation point of each bead with its respective capture antibody. Before starting, select the bead species and capture antibodies that are appropriate for your assay. The complete set of Spherotech Yellow beads has up to 12 species (Y1-Y12), each with increasing yellow fluorescence intensity. Some bead combinations may be incompatible, such as the yellow peaks 2 (Y2) and 3 (Y3), which have overlapping fluorescence. Select the beads that demonstrate the best separation on your flow cytometer. Additionally, the binding avidity between each lot of biotinylated antibodies and the Spherotech beads can vary. Expected outcomes

FlowLITE can be used as a high-throughput assay for global antibody profiling in any fluid specimen. We validated FlowLITE using healthy human plasma samples (Figures 3 and 4). Given the wide linear range of FlowLITE (>200-fold), various antibody isotypes can be simultaneously quantified at a single dilution point in a human sample. In our previous study (Babcock et al., 2024), we adapted this protocol to quantify the full breadth of antibody responses, including isotype usage and antigen specificity, across the COVID-19 disease state.1

Limitations

Proper storage and handling of all antibodies and beads are essential for the reproducibility of this protocol. Although FlowLITE is quantitative and reproducible, slight variations in the final MFI readouts may occur between antibody and bead lots, which should be considered when comparing samples from different experiments or reagent lots and titrations. This protocol is adaptable to various flow cytometers, including both conventional and spectral models, but it is crucial to optimize the conditions for each machine. Thus, to directly compare MFI between experiments, calibrate your cytometer before each experimental run to a pre-defined configuration using a standard reagent of your choice, such as rainbow beads.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Bead loss throughout sample preparation (<100 events).

For users more accustomed to working with cells rather than microparticles, the inability to identify the pellet of beads visually may cause initial technical errors in pipetting and aspiration. Furthermore, the static relationship between the beads and plastics (plates and tubes) can result in considerable loss of beads (related to all protocol steps involving bead preparation and staining).

Potential solution

When first performing the protocol, count the number of beads after each wash step listed. This helps establish an approximate range for the bead loss, which should not exceed 20%. When aspirating, around 5–10 μL of media may remain with the pellet. Do not attempt to aspirate dry. Instead, adjust the volume back to the appropriate volume. Carefully pre-wash (and air-dry) all plastics used for the assay with FlowLITE media and use the proper plastics as detailed in the key resources table, such as polypropylene plates. Consider the bead loss in your particular experimental setup when calculating the final number of beads to be recorded in the cytometer. Ideally, for each bead species, record 500–1,000 events to facilitate downstream statistical analysis. Any bead loss should be rethought critically.

Problem 2

Daily variation in the flow cytometer configuration.

As for all flow cytometry experiments, there may be day-to-day variations in instrument settings, electronics, lasers, and fluidics that can significantly impact the fluorescent signal (MFI) readout of the samples (related to Bead Load Titration: Step 4a, Reveal-Antibody Titration: Step 8a, and FlowLITE Step-by-Step method: Step 11–14).

Potential solution

Quality control (QC) should be performed before each experiment. Run the designated QC beads for the Cytek Aurora to optimize the machine by setting laser delays, gains (which can be manually adjusted), and area scaling factors. The BD FACSymphony instrument uses CS&T beads. PMT voltage, laser delays, window extension, and area scaling can be manually adjusted to optimize the settings for the FlowLITE beads.

Calibrate the instrument using a reference bead, such as the Rainbow Fluorescent Particles (RFP) from Spherotech. When setting up a worksheet template for FlowLITE, use the reference bead (e.g., RFP) to determine and maintain a record of an optimized MFI value for each channel used in the assay. For example, after the instrument-specific QC above, run the reference bead and adjust the gains (Cytek Aurora) or PMT voltages (BD FACSymphony) to match the pre-defined MFI value for the reference beads in each relevant channel. This should ensure that the MFI values are comparable between experiments. Properly gate on the bead population when setting the gains/voltages.

Consider generating aliquots from a sample with known FlowLITE values to use as a standard positive control in each experimental run. For example, freeze large numbers of vials containing aliquots (∼100 μL) of human healthy serum with known FlowLITE values, which can be thawed and processed alongside your experimental samples as a positive control and to determine the range of technical variability for your assay.

Problem 3

Inconsistency in MFI values during data analysis.

The presence of bead clumps, debris, or heterogeneous distribution of MFI values (sometimes bimodal) can interfere with the gating strategy and the final MFI readout (related to Bead Load Titration: Step 4b, Reveal-Antibody Titration: Step 8b, and FlowLITE Step-by-Step method: Step 14).

Potential solution

The beads tend to form doublets or even clumps if not properly mixed. Use a vortex on high settings for several seconds to minutes during the bead preparation step to break down any doublets or clumps. If you still notice a large number of events expressing high levels of Forward and/or Side Scatter (FSC and SSC) on the cytometer, consider optimizing your vortex time and intensity or using sonication. Some residual doublets/clumps (FSC/SSC high) or debris (FSC/SSC low) are expected. Thus, ensure that your gating is tight around the main bead populations to exclude these residual, unwanted populations (See Figure 3). Inappropriate gating will generate incorrect MFI values.

When storing the final samples for several hours or overnight, always use a fixative, as antibody dissociation can occur within a few hours after staining, resulting in lower MFI signals. To prevent the formation of clumps of beads by cross-linking proteins, mix vigorously on a vortex while slowly adding the fixative solution, preferably in drops. When performing the assay in a plate, ensure that you mix the beads vigorously with a pipette before fixation to minimize clumping.

If you observe a bimodal (or spread) distribution of MFI values for a particular bead species, likely, those beads were not homogeneously mixed with the antibodies during staining. This phenomenon will prevent you from gating the correct bead population to calculate their MFI. To prevent this, avoid generating micro-droplets on the sides of tubes/wells by pipetting directly into the bottom of the tube. When vortexing, ensure that all droplets generated on the side of the tube return to the bottom, allowing them to be adequately mixed with the reagents. If needed, use a pipette instead of a vortex to mix the samples.

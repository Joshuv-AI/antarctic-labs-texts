Metabolome-based genome-wide association study reveals PANK2 and EEF1A2 as major genetic regulators of key volatile compounds in Gaoyou duck breast meat
Authors: Lipeng Yuan, Dapeng Liu, Yongfu Zhang, Qiming Mu, Hongfei Liu, He Zhang, Hehe Tang, Yutong Xiao, Sirui Liu, Yalong Deng, Shijia Ying, Rihong Guo, Shuisheng Hou, Zhengkui Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The Gaoyou duck, a premium indigenous Chinese breed, is renowned for its unique flavor profile. However, the volatile chemical basis and the underlying genetic mechanisms contributing to its distinctive aroma remain largely uncharacterized. In this study, solid-phase microextraction coupled with gas chromatography-mass spectrometry(SPME-GC-MS) was employed to analyze the volatile profiles of cooked breast meat from Gaoyou and Pekin ducks. In the comparative analysis between breeds, a total of 89 volatile compounds were identified, primarily consisting of aldehydes, ketones, esters, and alcohols. Comparative analysis revealed that Pekin ducks possessed significantly higher levels of aldehydes, ketones, and sulfur-containing compounds, whereas Gaoyou ducks exhibited a significant enrichment of esters, with 54 of these 89 compounds identified as significantly differential. To further decipher the genetic architecture underlying these flavor traits, a metabolite-based genome-wide association study (mGWAS) was conducted in an expanded Gaoyou duck population (n = 109) . By utilizing 94 volatile compounds identified in this larger population as quantitative phenotypes, we identified key genomic loci and prioritized candidate genes. Notably, PANK2 was identified as a potential regulator of 2-acetyl-2-thiazoline synthesis by modulating L-cysteine metabolism. Additionally, EEF1A2 may influence hexadecanal production by regulating lipid homeostasis through phosphatidylinositol-4-phosphate (PI4P). This study systematically characterized the differences in volatile compound profiles in the breast muscle of Gaoyou and Pekin ducks, and identified potential genetic regulatory factors influencing volatile compounds in Gaoyou ducks.

Introduction

With the continuous improvement in living standards, meat quality has become a critical factor for consumers when selecting meat products. Beyond conventional indicators such as meat color and texture, flavor is increasingly garnering attention. Meat flavor primarily consists of aroma and taste(Menis-Henrique, 2020). The formation of aroma in cooked meat is exceptionally complex, primarily originating from a series of chemical reactions involving flavor precursors during heating, including thermal degradation, the Maillard reaction, and lipid oxidation(Ramalingam et al., 2019; Xia et al., 2021). Studies have shown that the Maillard reaction alone can produce thousands of flavor compounds, while lipid oxidation generates hundreds of volatile components, such as aldehydes and sulfides(Diez-Simon et al., 2019; Flores, 2018). The synergistic effects of these substances create the characteristic complex aroma of meat, which also significantly increases the challenge of flavor research. In terms of analytical techniques, SPME-GC-MS has become the mainstream method for flavor detection due to its superior performance in the qualitative and quantitative analysis of volatile compounds(Yang et al., 2022).

In recent years, mGWAS has emerged as a powerful tool for deciphering the genetic basis of metabolic traits. In duck flavor research, metabolic and volatile data from a Pekin × Liancheng duck hybrid population were utilized to construct the first duck "gene-metabolite-volatile" regulatory network, identifying key regulatory genes such as AOX1 and ACBD5(Liu et al., 2023). Additionally, an mGWAS conducted on a Pekin × Mallard duck F2 population identified the AADAT gene as a key factor influencing lysine metabolism in muscle(Liu et al., 2025). Similarly, in ovine research, lipidomics and mGWAS were integrated to construct a "gene-lipid-flavor" regulatory network in ruminant muscle, identifying key genes like MBOAT1 and SH2D4A, whose variations directly influence the composition of volatile flavor compounds after cooking(Zhang et al., 2025).

Previous genomic evidence has indicated the presence of subtle gene flow between Pekin and Gaoyou ducks(Zhou et al., 2018). However, such ancestral genetic exchanges have not precluded the profound phenotypic divergence driven by long-term disruptive selection for specific production goals. Within the modern duck industry, these two breeds have developed specialized culinary applications that directly stem from their contrasting meat quality and flavor profiles. The Gaoyou duck, characterized by high muscular density and a sophisticated reservoir of flavor precursors, is predominantly utilized for traditional slow-processed delicacies such as brined duck (Yanshui duck) and simmered soups. In stark contrast, the Pekin duck has been selectively bred for a robust lipid deposition profile—both subcutaneous and intramuscular—which, upon roasting, yields the hallmark crispy texture and intense fatty aroma essential for Peking Roast Duck. Ultimately, these distinct utilization patterns provide a macroscopic reflection of the divergent metabolic pathways governing flavor formation in these two breeds.

To deeply analyze the genetic and metabolic foundations of duck flavor, this study analyzed the composition of volatile compounds in the breast muscle of Gaoyou and Pekin ducks. Furthermore, the volatile compound content in Gaoyou ducks was used as a phenotype for mGWAS. Through this approach, we successfully identified genetic loci associated with key flavor compounds, including the PANK2 gene, which potentially regulates 2-acetyl-2-thiazoline, and the EEF1A2 gene, which may control the accumulation of hexadecanal. These findings provide critical insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying the unique flavor of the Gaoyou duck.

Materials and methods

Experimental animals and sample collection

A total of 122 Gaoyou ducks and 15 Pekin ducks were used in this study. All duck eggs were hatched following routine procedures. All ducks were raised in cages under standard conditions of temperature, humidity, and ventilation. The birds were fed a standardized corn-soybean diet formulated according to the nutrient requirements for meat ducks (NY/T 2122-2012) (Table S1). During the experimental period, ducks had ad libitum access to feed and water. In this study, each individual duck was treated as an independent biological replicate for all subsequent analyses.

Samples for whole-genome sequencing and volatile compound analysis were collected from the breast muscle (pectoralis major). Specifically, muscle tissue was sampled from the left breast of each duck at approximately 1 cm from both the upper and left edges. Samples were immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and transferred to a −80°C ultra-low temperature freezer for subsequent DNA extraction and volatile compound detection.

Determination of volatile compounds

Volatile compounds in heated meat samples were determined using headspace solid-phase microextraction (HS-SPME) coupled with GC-MS. Before extraction, frozen raw meat samples were ground into powder in liquid nitrogen. A 3 g portion of the pulverized sample was placed in a 20 mL headspace vial, and 10 µL of 2-methyl-3-heptanone (0.1 µg/µL) was added as an internal standard. The vials were immediately sealed.

To simulate the cooked state and induce the release of volatile compounds, the sealed vials were heated at 85°C for 15 min (standardized cooking treatment) and then equilibrated at 55°C for 20 min. Subsequently, headspace extraction was performed at 55°C for 40 min using a 50/30 µm DVB/CAR/PDMS fiber (Supelco, USA). After extraction, the fiber was desorbed in the GC inlet at 250°C for 3 min. Analysis was conducted on a Q-Exactive Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) equipped with a TriPlus RSH autosampler and a Trace 1310 GC.

Chromatographic separation was performed using a VF-WAX ms column (60 m × 0.25 mm i.d. × 0.25 µm). The oven temperature program was as follows: initial temperature at 40°C for 2 min, increased to 230°C at a rate of 4°C/min, and held for 5 min. Helium was used as the carrier gas at a flow rate of 1 mL/min. Mass spectrometry was operated in electron impact (EI) mode (70 eV) with an automatic-gain-control (AGC) target value of 1E6 and a transfer line temperature of 250°C. The full scan range was m/z 30–400 with a resolution of 60,000.

Prior to sample analysis, two blank and two fiber-aging runs were performed to ensure system cleanliness. To monitor instrumental stability during the sequence, a QC sample was analyzed every 14 samples. Identification criteria were strictly set as follows: HRF scores > 95, MS match factors > 750, and RI differences < 20 for the in-house library or < 50 for the NIST library.

Variant discovery and GenotypingGenomic

DNA was extracted from breast muscle using the phenol/chloroform method. After quality control, paired-end sequencing libraries with an insert size of 300–350 bp were constructed and sequenced on the DNBSEQ-T7/Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (150 bp paired-end) with an average coverage depth of 5 × .

Raw reads were aligned to the duck reference genome (GCA_003850225.1) using Burrows–Wheeler alignment (BWA aln)(Li and Durbin, 2009). SNP calling was performed using GATK HaplotypeCaller (version 3.5) (DePristo et al., 2011). Variants were filtered using VCFtools (version 0.1.15) (Danecek et al., 2011) based on the following criteria: average depth (DP) 6 × –30 ×, minor allele frequency (MAF) > 0.05, maximum missing rate < 0.1, and bi-allelic sites only. A final set of 4,387,699 high-quality single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) was obtained for downstream analysis.

Principal component analysis (PCA)

Based on the peak area data of all volatile compounds, PCA was performed to compare the flavor metabolic profiles between breeds using the prcomp function in R (version 4.3.1).

Differential analysistwo-sample

t-tests were performed to compare conventional meat quality traits and volatile compounds between Gaoyou and Peking ducks using R (version 4.3.1). The significance threshold was set at P < 0.05.

mGWAS and linkage disequilibrium (LD) analysis

mGWAS was conducted using the FarmCPU model in the rMVP (version 1.4.5) (Yin et al., 2021) package in R (version 4.3.1), with peak areas of 94 volatile compounds as phenotypes. The first three principal components (PCs) and gender were included as fixed effects to correct for population stratification and sexual dimorphism, while the kinship matrix estimated from whole-genome SNPs was included as a random effect. The significance threshold was set at 1 × 10-7. LD analysis was performed using PLINK (version 1.90) (Purcell et al., 2007) for SNPs within a 2 Mb region upstream and downstream of the significant association loci.

Results

Characteristics of volatile compounds in Gaoyou and Pekin duck breast muscle

To characterize the volatile compounds in the breast muscle of Gaoyou and Pekin ducks, we first observed the appearance of the breast muscle at 8 weeks of age. Visual assessment revealed distinct inter-breed differences in breast muscle size and coloration at 8 weeks of age (Fig. 1A). Conventional meat quality traits—including cooking loss, shear force, pH, and color coordinates—were measured to evaluate breed-specific differences. The results demonstrated significant variations in cooking loss, shear force, pH, and lightness (L*), redness (a*), and yellowness (b*) (Fig. 1B). Specifically, Gaoyou ducks exhibited significantly higher cooking loss, shear force, and L* values compared to Pekin ducks, whereas Pekin ducks showed higher pH, a*, and b* values. To further compare the flavor composition, volatile compounds were analyzed in cooked breast meat samples from both 13 Gaoyou ducks and 15 Pekin ducks at 8 weeks of age. Using SPME-GC-MS, a total of 89 volatile compounds were identified across both breeds and classified into ten categories. Aldehydes, ketones, and alcohols were the predominant volatile components in duck breast muscle, collectively accounting for over 80 % of the total relative content (Fig. 1C), thereby forming the basic flavor profile of duck meat. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) based on the peak areas of all volatile compounds showed a clear separation between Gaoyou and Pekin duck samples (Fig. 1D). This separation confirms significant systemic differences in the overall flavor metabolic profiles between the two breeds.

Comparative analysis of volatile compounds between breeds

A comparative analysis of the volatile profiles in cooked breast meat revealed significant differences between the two breeds (Fig. 1E). Specifically, the concentrations of aldehydes (p < 0.05), alcohols (p < 0.01), and sulfur compounds (p < 0.05) were significantly higher in Pekin ducks than in Gaoyou ducks. Conversely, esters compounds were highly significantly enriched in Gaoyou duck breast meat (p < 0.001). Given that lipids are the primary precursors for aldehydes, alcohols, and esters, these findings suggest systemic differences in lipid composition between the two breeds. Although esters were significantly enriched in Gaoyou ducks, aldehydes, ketones, and alcohols remained the most predominant volatile components in both breeds, collectively representing over 80 % of the total relative content. These results lead to the hypothesis that Pekin ducks may possess a higher lipid deposition capacity, while the systematic variations in lipid-derived volatiles indicate divergent lipid metabolic pathways.

Differential analysis of volatile compounds identified 54 volatile compounds with significantly different concentrations (Fig. 1F). To further focus on the key variations, a threshold of |Log2FC| > log2(1.7) was applied to identify compounds with high fold-changes. In Pekin duck breast meat, sulfur-containing compounds, short-chain fatty acids, and certain heterocyclic and ketone substances were highly enriched, including butanoic acid, butanoic acid,3-methyl-, valeric acid, β-ionone, 1H-Pyrrole,1-methyl-, and dimethyl trisulfide. Additionally, certain alcohols, such as 1-heptanol, were significantly higher in Pekin ducks. In contrast, Gaoyou duck breast meat was primarily characterized by the striking enrichment of long-chain fatty acid methyl esters, such as hexadecanoic acid, methyl ester,9-octadecenoic acid (Z)-, methyl ester and octadecanoic acid, methyl ester(Table 1). Furthermore, indole,2-acetyl-2-thiazoline, and hexadecanal were also detected at significantly higher levels in Gaoyou ducks. Genetic basis of volatile compounds in Gaoyou duck breast muscle

To investigate the genetic basis of flavor, 109 individual 8-week-old Gaoyou ducks underwent whole-genome resequencing with an average depth of 5 × . Based on the sequence data, a total of 4,387,699 high-quality SNPs were identified across the genome. mGWAS was conducted using the FarmCPU iterative multi-locus mixed model, with the relative peak areas of the 94 volatile compounds identified in this population as quantitative trait phenotypes. Potential regulation of 2-acetyl-2-thiazoline synthesis by the PANK2 gene

2-Acetyl-2-thiazoline is an organic compound characterized by a partially saturated sulfur- and nitrogen-containing five-membered heterocyclic ring (2-thiazoline) core, with an acetyl group attached to the C-2 position. Using its chromatographic peak area as the phenotype for GWAS, a significant association signal was detected on Chromosome 4 (Fig. 3A). To fine-map this locus, we analyzed the LD between the lead SNP and surrounding SNPs within the quantitative trait locus (QTL) interval. Using an R² > 0.4 threshold, the candidate causal interval was narrowed down to a region of 76.82–76.83 Mb (approximately 0.01 Mb) (Fig. 3B). During the thermal processing of meat, 2-acetyl-2-thiazoline can be generated from precursors such as endogenous sulfur-containing amino acids (e.g., cysteine) and carbohydrates via the Maillard reaction and thermal degradation pathways(Adams and De Kimpe, 2006). The PANK2 gene, located within the association interval, encodes pantothenate kinase. In the subsequent synthesis of Coenzyme A (CoA), the pathway involving the product of this enzyme consumes l-cysteine (Barritt et al., 2024) (Fig. 3C). Notably, transcriptomic analysis showed that the mRNA expression of PANK2 in the breast muscle of Pekin ducks was significantly higher than that in Gaoyou ducks (Fig. 3D). Therefore, we propose that the higher expression of PANK2 in Pekin ducks may divert the metabolic flux of cysteine toward CoA biosynthesis, thereby limiting the availability of precursors for 2-acetyl-2-thiazoline synthesis and resulting in its lower content.

Identification of genes regulating Hexadecanal synthesis

Hexadecanal is a straight-chain saturated fatty aldehyde containing sixteen carbon atoms with a terminal aldehyde group. Using its peak area as a phenotypic trait for GWAS, a significant association signal was identified on Chromosome 21 (Fig. 4A). To fine-map this LD analysis was performed between the lead SNP and surrounding SNPs with in the QTL interval. Based on a threshold of R² > 0.4, the candidate causal interval was narrowed to 16.440–16.446 Mb (approximately 0.06 Mb) (Fig. 4B). During the thermal processing of meat, aldehydes can be formed from precursors such as endogenous lipids and glycerides via lipid oxidation and thermal degradation pathways(Shahidi and Hossain, 2022). Previous studies indicate that EEF1A2 directly binds to and activates phosphatidylinositol 4-kinase IIIβ (PI4KIIIβ), thereby significantly increasing cellular levels of its catalytic product, 4-phosphate (PI4P) (Jeganathan and Lee, 2007)(Fig. 4D). PI4P recruits effector proteins, such as CERT and OSBP, to the Golgi apparatus, where they directly regulate the synthesis and transport of sphingolipids and sterols, contributing to the maintenance of membrane biosynthesis and lipid homeostasis(Graham and Burd, 2011). Based on this mechanism, we hypothesize that genetic variations in EEF1A2 may modulate lipid homeostasis by influencing PI4P levels, thereby regulating the production of hexadecanal.

Discussion

Conventional meat quality evaluation systems are primarily built around macro-phenotypic indicators such as color, pH, and tenderness. While these parameters possess clear practical value for assessing freshness, processing suitability, and basic edible quality—forming the cornerstone of current industrial practices—they primarily reflect the physical state and specific biochemical endpoints of the muscle(Cho et al., 2019; Molinero et al., 2024; Suman and Joseph, 2013). Consequently, conventional traits alone are insufficient to fully capture the multidimensional nature of meat quality, particularly flavor, which remains a primary driver of consumer preference. Flavor is not determined by a single factor; rather, it originates from abundant precursors such as lipids, carbohydrates, and amino acids. During processing, these precursors undergo pathways like the Maillard reaction and lipid oxidation to generate a series of volatile flavor compounds, including aldehydes, ketones, alcohols, and sulfur-containing compounds(Chang et al., 2020; Shahidi and Hossain, 2022). The composition, concentration, and synergistic effects of these volatiles collectively constitute the unique and complex aroma and taste of meat. Therefore, to achieve a more profound and comprehensive scientific evaluation of meat quality, it is essential to systematically investigate and integrate the profiles and dynamic changes of key volatile flavor compounds, thereby refining evaluation dimensions from the material origins of flavor.

Aldehydes, alcohols, and ketones are the primary volatile flavor substances detected in duck breast muscle. Among these, aldehydes exhibit the highest concentration in both breeds and, due to their generally low odor thresholds, serve as critical components influencing the overall flavor profile(Tan et al., 2022). Hexanal, with concentrations far exceeding other aldehydes and an extremely low sensory threshold, likely serves as a fundamental common substance shaping the basic flavor of duck meat, contributing typical grassy and fatty aromas(Azarbad and Jeleń, 2015; Luo et al., 2022). However, inter-breed variations in specific component concentrations determine flavor specificity. Pekin ducks possess significantly higher levels of most medium- and short-chain aldehydes, such as hexanal, heptanal, and 3-methylbutanal (which offers a malty sweetness), imparting a more intense fatty and grassy aroma with a prominent flavor profile(Zhou et al., 2024). Furthermore, higher levels of short-chain fatty acids (e.g., butyric acid with cheese-like and sweaty notes) and sulfur compounds (e.g., dimethyl trisulfide with an onion/garlic meaty aroma) further enhance the complexity and pungency of its flavor(Wang et al., 2023). In contrast, while the Gaoyou duck does not exhibit a dominance in total aldehyde content, it shows a substantial accumulation of esters, such as 9-Octadecenoic acid (Z)-, methyl este and Octadecanoic acid, methyl ester, which contribute prominent fruity and fatty notes(Zhao et al., 2025). Characteristic compounds including hexadecanal (waxy) and benzaldehyde (bitter almond)( Del Olmo et al., 2017; Gong et al., 2025), acting in synergy with indole (at low concentrations, contributes a floral or jasmine-like aroma) and 2-acetyl-2-thiazoline (roasted meat and popcorn-like)( Bel Rhlid et al., 2002; Mindt et al., 2022), collectively shape a mellow and well-rounded flavor profile characterized by complex nutty and roasted undertones.

2-Acetyl-2-thiazoline, initially identified in beef broth and subsequently established as a signature aroma compound in roasted beef(Cerny and Grosch, 1992; Plancken et al., 1971). It is primarily synthesized via the Maillard reaction involving sulfur-containing amino acids (e.g., l-cysteine) and reducing sugars. In the present study, the identification of PANK2 as a candidate gene provides a potential genetic basis for the differential accumulation of 2-Acetyl-2-thiazoline. As the rate-limiting enzyme in the CoA biosynthetic pathway, the pantothenate kinase encoded by PANK2 facilitates the formation of 4′-phosphopantetheine, a process that consumes l-cysteine during downstream CoA assembly(Jackowski and Rock, 1981; Voltti et al., 1979). Previous studies have demonstrated that mutations in the PANK gene family can lead to a significant elevation in the levels of its biochemical precursor, cysteine, due to the blockage of the downstream CoA biosynthetic pathway(Gordon, 2002).Consistent with these findings, our results showed that PANK2 was significantly upregulated in Pekin ducks, which conversely exhibited lower levels of 2-acetyl-2-thiazoline.Integrating these observations, we tentatively propose a "metabolic shunt model" wherein genetic variations or expression differences in PANK2 modulate the metabolic flux of cysteine. Specifically, the higher expression of PANK2 in Pekin ducks may drive more cysteine into the CoA biosynthetic pathway, thereby limiting its availability for 2-acetyl-2-thiazoline synthesis during thermal processing. In contrast, the lower expression of PANK2 in Gaoyou ducks may result in an enriched pool of free l-cysteine, contributing to its superior aroma profile.

Parallelly, the concentration of volatile aldehydes in cooked meat is intrinsically linked to the lipid profile of the raw muscle. Our data suggest a potential regulatory axis involving EEF1A2, a gene conventionally known as a translation factor. EEF1A2 can recruit effector proteins such as CERT and OSBP through phosphatidylinositol PI4P, thereby directly orchestrating the synthesis and transport of sphingolipids and sterols. Given that EEF1A2 overexpression has been shown to inhibit fatty acid oxidation and lipid synthesis genes(Guo et al., 2020), and its association with backfat thickness in porcine GWAS(Jiang et al., 2018), we hypothesize that Gaoyou duck-specific variations in EEF1A2 may reshape the glycerophospholipid landscape. This lipid remodeling potentially alters the abundance of long-chain fatty acid precursors, which undergo oxidative cleavage to generate key volatile aldehydes, thus contributing to the distinct fatty and waxy notes of the Gaoyou breed.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that the volatile profile of Gaoyou duck breast muscle is characterized by a significant enrichment of esters and key aroma-active compounds, such as 2-acetyl-2-thiazoline and hexadecanal. Through mGWAS analysis, candidate genes including PANK2 and EEF1A2 were identified for the first time as being significantly associated with the abundances of these pivotal volatile substances. Altogether, these findings, spanning both chemical and genetic dimensions, provide direct evidence for the formation of the unique volatile composition of Gaoyou ducks. By establishing a theoretical foundation for the molecular breeding of volatile-related traits, this study offers a molecular blueprint for the preservation of indigenous germplasm and the genetic improvement of meat quality in waterfowls.

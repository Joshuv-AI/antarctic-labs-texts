Non-minimally coupled scalar field dark sector of the universe: in-depth (Einstein frame) case study
Marcin Postolak 1

Abstract
In this study, motivated by recent results from DESI DR2 [ 2 , 83 ] indicating a possible preference for evolving dark energy in some combined data analyses, we analyze spatially flat FLRW interacting scalar-tensor cosmological models with non-minimal coupling (NMC) between the scalar field (SF) and matter/cosmological dust in the Einstein conformal frame. By using modified expansion normalized variables that account for negative values of the scalar field potential, we derive cosmological dynamical system equations and expressions for physical variables. Five specific scalar field models (axions/ALPs, cyclic ekpyrotic, exponential with a constant, quintessence, and SFDM) are examined in depth to determine how they evolve, as they serve as representative candidates used in the literature for the evolving dark energy in the late Universe. Using appropriate mathematical methods (e.g. linear stability, center manifold and Poincaré sphere), we present critical points along with their character and physical interpretation with respect to the possible evolution of the Universe. Considering both positive and negative values of the coupling parameter allows us to examine the transfer of energy from the scalar sector to dust and from matter to the scalar field. Considering four representative absolute values of this parameter provides a comprehensive analysis that incorporates all significant types of dynamical system evolution (from mathematical and physical perspectives). The initial conditions for the in-depth numerical analysis were constructed analytically from observationally motivated Planck 2018 [ 4 ] and DESI DR2 [ 2 ] CPL parametrizations.

\frac{d\lambda_{\phi}}{dN}=0

(6.45)

x=0\quad\lor\quad\lambda_{\phi}=0

(6.46)

and the remaining conditions of stationarity imply that the variable
u

u\propto\mathcal{V}(\phi)\to 0\,.

(6.47)

\mathcal{V}(\phi)\propto\phi^{2}\to 0\,,

(6.48)

but at the same time in such a case:

\lambda_{\phi}\propto-\cot{\phi}\to-\infty\quad\land\quad\phi>0\,.

(6.49)

This implies that the minimum lies on the singular boundary of the phase space and the background level analysis takes into account only the aforementioned fixed points of the system. Thus, matter domination epoch (field oscillations) does not occur as an explicit critical point
[

,

]

Physical meaning of positive and negative
\beta

The non-minimal coupling between the scalar field and matter implicates an explicit energy flow/transfer between these components. Using (

) and covariant conservation law for the total energy-momentum tensor:

\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}_{(\mathrm{tot})}=0\quad\land\quad T^{\mu\nu}_{(\mathrm{tot})}\equiv T^{\mu\nu}_{(\mathrm{m})}+T^{\mu\nu}_{(\mathrm{r})}+T^{\mu\nu}_{(\phi)}\,,

(8.19)

together with the SF equation of motion (

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{m}+3H\rho_{m}

\displaystyle=-Q\,,

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{\phi}+3H\left(1+\omega_{\phi}\right)\rho_{\phi}

\displaystyle=+Q\,,

(8.20b)

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle\equiv\beta\,\dot{\phi}\,\rho_{m}=\sqrt{6}\,\beta\,x\,H\,\rho_{m}\,,

(8.20c)

where

For the expansion phase (

<0,&\beta\dot{\phi}<0\,.\end{cases}

Based on the above considerations, we take into account both cases of energy transfer (different signs for the parameter
\beta

H(z)
and therefore changes the distances within BAO. Due to the fact that BAOs are geometric in nature, the dynamical system working at the background level directly determines what behaviors of models for late epochs allow for a departure from the standard expansion characteristic of the
\Lambda
CDM model.

9.2
Why interacting scalar sector generates evolving
\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}(z)

In our formalism:

\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}(N)=\frac{1}{3}\Omega_{r}(N)+x^{2}(N)-u(N)\,,

(9.1)

and the other dynamical system variables evolve according to (
3.12
).
If a given trajectory does not lie exactly on an invariant submanifold for the critical point for all values of
N
, then the effective equation of state changes over time. Orbits move between early-time saddle fixed points and late-time stable points.

9.3
Thawing vs. freezing behavior

The thawing trajectory is the one for which the SF is initially frozen with

\omega_{\phi}\simeq-1

(9.2)

and then evolves away from that value at late times, on the other hand, freezing trajectories evolve toward the de Sitter-like epoch
[
27
,
11
,
40
,
79
]
:

\omega_{\phi}\to-1\,.

(9.3)

In the language of our dynamical system, this means that for domination of the SF potential one obtains:

u\simeq 1\quad\land\quad|x|\ll 1\,

(9.4)

therefore:

1+\omega_{\phi}\simeq 2x^{2}\,.

(9.5)

Therefore, thawing behavior corresponds to an increase in
|x|
from an initially small value to larger values.

Conclusions

In this paper, we present an analysis of a dynamical system incorporating a non-minimal coupling of exponential form between the scalar field and matter/dust within the Einstein frame. We have taken into account potentials with exclusively positive values as well as those that can take negative values.

Our main results are:

(1)

Developing a dynamical variable corresponding to the potential energy of a scalar field, taking into account both positive and negative values, and obtaining an explicit evolution of key physical parameters for individual models;

(2)

We have performed a precise analysis of the stability of five specific physically motivated SF potential forms (on expanding and contracting branches). We clarified which regimes are physically relevant within the finite values of the variable
\lambda_{\phi}
and what specific mathematical methods can be used to characterize them;

(3)

By using a Poincaré compactification of the reduced constant
\lambda_{\phi}
system we have obtained a complete classification of critical sets at infinity. In the physical
u<0
sector the reduced Hubble-normalized flow is generically drawn toward the equator at infinity, corresponding to
r\to\infty
and thus
H\to 0
. This provides a precise explanation of the way in which negative potentials for the scalar field can naturally lead to a transition from the expansion phase to the collapse phase (turnaround) and why the use of ‘e-fold time’ becomes singular when
H\to 0
.

(4)

We performed a detailed numerical analysis of the equations describing our dynamical system using observationally motivated illustrative initial conditions derived from Planck 2018 and DESI DR2 CPL parameterizations for four selected values of the parameter describing the strength of the NMC. We have described how changing the value and sign of this parameter can affect individual cosmological models.

Appendix C
Poincaré compactification and critical points at infinity for
u<0
branches

The sign-safe variable:

u\equiv\frac{\mathcal{V}(\phi)}{3H^{2}}

(C.1)

allows for negative values, but in such a case the state space is
not
a priori compact. Divergences in
(x,u,\Omega_{r})
typically correspond to approaching a hypersurface where
H\to 0
(turnaround/bounce), because all
Hubble-normalized densities scale as
H^{-2}
. Therefore, ’points at infinity’ diagnose where the
N=\ln a
time variable becomes ill-defined and where an
H
-regular extension (e.g. via a compact Hubble variable) would be required to continue the flow across
H=0
.

Throughout this appendix we work with the reduced 3D system on a
constant
\lambda_{\phi*}
branch
(exponential tail), i.e.:

\lambda_{\phi}=\lambda_{\phi*}

(C.2)

is treated as a constant parameter, and we also write:

\Omega\equiv\Omega_{r}\,.

(C.3)

for more compact form.

C.1
Reduced polynomial system on a constant
\lambda_{\phi*}
branch

On a constant-
\lambda_{\phi*}
branch, one could eliminate
\Omega_{m}
via the constraint:

\Omega_{m}=1-x^{2}-u-\Omega\,,

(C.4)

and expand the reduced system (
3.12
) for
(x,u,\Omega)
. One finds the following polynomial vector field:

\displaystyle x^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}x^{3}-\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}\beta\,x^{2}-\frac{3}{2}x\,u+\frac{1}{2}x\,\Omega-\frac{3}{2}x+\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}(\lambda_{\phi*}-\beta)u-\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}\beta\,\Omega+\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}\beta\,,

(C.5a)

\displaystyle u^{\prime}

\displaystyle=3u\,x^{2}-\sqrt{6}\,\lambda_{\phi*}\,u\,x-3u^{2}+\Omega\,u+3u\,,

(C.5b)

\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}

\displaystyle=3\Omega\,x^{2}-3\Omega\,u+\Omega^{2}-\Omega\,.

(C.5c)

The maximal polynomial degree is
m=3
. It is useful to split the field into homogeneous segments:

\mathbf{F}(x,u,\Omega)=\mathbf{F}_{3}(x,u,\Omega)+\mathbf{F}_{2}(x,u,\Omega)+\mathbf{F}_{1}(x,u,\Omega)+\mathbf{F}_{0}\,,

(C.6)

where the degree-3 and degree-2 parts are as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{3}(x,u,\Omega)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{3}{2}x^{3},3u\,x^{2},3\Omega\,x^{2}\right),

(C.7)

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{2}(x,u,\Omega)

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}\beta\,x^{2}-\frac{3}{2}x\,u+\frac{1}{2}x\,\Omega,-\sqrt{6}\,\lambda_{\phi*}\,u\,x-3u^{2}+\Omega u,\Omega^{2}-3\Omega u\right)\,.

(C.8)

The lower-degree parts only affect subleading behavior at infinity,
except
where the leading homogeneous field vanishes (degenerate directions). This is precisely what occurs here on a large equatorial set.

C.2
Poincaré sphere: embedding, desingularization, and induced flow

Let us introduce the radial variable:

r\equiv\sqrt{x^{2}+u^{2}+\Omega^{2}}\quad\land\quad(a,b,c)\equiv\left(\frac{x}{r},\frac{u}{r},\frac{\Omega}{r}\right)\quad\land\quad a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}=1\,.

(C.9)

Infinity in
(x,u,\Omega)
corresponds to
r\to\infty
, i.e. points on the unit sphere
S^{2}
in
(a,b,c)
.

For a polynomial vector field of maximal degree
m=3
, Poincaré compactification uses a time rescaling:

\frac{d\tau}{dN}=r^{m-1}=r^{2}\,,

(C.10)

so that the induced flow on the sphere depends only on the maximal-degree segment
\mathbf{F}_{3}
:

\dot{\mathbf{s}}=\mathbf{F}_{3}(\mathbf{s})-[\mathbf{s}\cdot\mathbf{F}_{3}(\mathbf{s})]\,\mathbf{s}\quad\land\quad\mathbf{s}=(a,b,c)\quad\land\quad\dot{}\equiv\frac{d}{d\tau}\,.

(C.11)

Using (
C.7
), one finds that:

\mathbf{F}_{3}(\mathbf{s})=\left(\frac{3}{2}a^{3},3b\,a^{2},3c\,a^{2}\right)\,.

(C.12)

Let us also define the scalar:

\alpha(\mathbf{s})=\mathbf{s}\cdot\mathbf{F}_{3}(\mathbf{s})=\frac{3}{2}a^{4}+3a^{2}(b^{2}+c^{2})=\frac{3}{2}a^{2}(2-a^{2})\,.

(C.13)

Then (
C.11
) yields the explicit sphere system:

\displaystyle\dot{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}a^{3}(a^{2}-1)\,,

(C.14a)

\displaystyle\dot{b}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}b\,a^{4}\,,

(C.14b)

\displaystyle\dot{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}c\,a^{4}\,.

(C.14c)

C.3
Critical sets at infinity from the leading flow

From (
C.14
), the critical points on the sphere satisfy:

\dot{a}=\dot{b}=\dot{c}=0\,.

(C.15)

Poles (kinetic directions)

There are two isolated points:

\mathbf{s}_{\pm}=(a,b,c)=\left(\pm 1,0,0\right)\,,

(C.16)

corresponding to:

\frac{x}{r}\to\pm 1\,,

(C.17)

so that:

|x|\gg|u|\quad\land\quad|x|\gg|\Omega|

(C.18)

at infinity.

Equatorial critical circle (degenerate directions)

Because:

\dot{b}\propto a^{4}\quad\land\quad\dot{c}\propto a^{4}\,,

(C.19)

every point with
a=0
is critical for the leading flow:

\mathcal{C}_{\infty}:\quad a=0\quad\land\quad b^{2}+c^{2}=1\,,

(C.20)

corresponding to directions where
|x|/r\to 0
while
(u,\Omega)
blow up proportionally.

This degeneracy means that the leading (
m=3
) compactification does not resolve the tangential dynamics along the equator. The degree-2 terms (
C.8
) provide the next non-vanishing contribution.

C.4
Linearized tangential behavior on
S^{2}
: poles vs. equator

Pole tangential instability

Linearizing (
C.14
) at:

\mathbf{s}_{+}=(1,0,0)

(C.21)

gives:

\dot{b}=\frac{3}{2}b\quad\land\quad\dot{c}=\frac{3}{2}c\,,

(C.22)

and in similar way at:

\mathbf{s}_{-}=(-1,0,0)\,.

(C.23)

Therefore, both poles are
repelling on the sphere
in the
(b,c)
directions.

Equator normal attraction

For
|a|\ll 1
with
b^{2}+c^{2}\simeq 1
, one obtains:

\dot{a}\simeq-\frac{3}{2}a^{3}\,,

(C.24)

so that:

a\to 0\quad\land\quad\tau\to+\infty\,.

(C.25)

Thus, the equator is
normally attracting
for the desingularized flow.

However, to decide whether trajectories in the original variables actually approach infinity (radially), one must analyze the radial dynamics (growth of
r
) in the original
N
time.

C.5
Radial dynamics

A convenient diagnostic is the following evolution equation:

r^{\prime}=\frac{x\,x^{\prime}+u\,u^{\prime}+\Omega\,\Omega^{\prime}}{r}\,,

(C.26)

where:

r=\sqrt{x^{2}+u^{2}+\Omega^{2}}\,.

(C.27)

At large
r
, the leading contribution depends on direction. Along the equator (
a=0
) the cubic terms vanish, so the
quadratic
terms control the behavior of
r^{\prime}
.

By using
x=0
in (
C.5
) one finds that the leading (quadratic) subsystem is:

\displaystyle u^{\prime}

\displaystyle\simeq u\left(\Omega-3u\right),

(C.28a)

\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}

\displaystyle\simeq\Omega\left(\Omega-3u\right)\,,

(C.28b)

which yields:

r^{\prime}\simeq r(\Omega-3u)\,.

(C.29)

Therefore, along the equatorial directions, the flow goes to infinity in
N
time if and only if:

\Omega-3u>0\,.

(C.30)

C.6
Equator refinement via degree-2 dynamics (full classification on
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
)

Because (
C.14
) leaves the entire equator critical, the correct classification of infinity requires a refined compactification on
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
using the degree-2 field (
C.8
).

A direct way to see the equatorial direction field is to consider the following ratio by using (
C.28
):

\frac{d\Omega}{du}=\frac{\Omega^{\prime}}{u^{\prime}}\quad\land\quad x=0\,.

(C.31)

For
u\neq 0
and
\Omega\neq 0
one can obtain:

\frac{d\Omega}{du}=\frac{\Omega(\Omega-3u)}{u(\Omega-3u)}=\frac{\Omega}{u}\,,

(C.32)

so that:

\frac{\Omega}{u}=\mathrm{const}

(C.33)

along the leading equatorial flow. This matches the geometric picture - equatorial directions are parameterized by the ratio:

\frac{c}{b}=\frac{\Omega}{u}\,.

(C.34)

Moreover, relation (
C.28
) implies that the factor
(\Omega-3u)
controls
both
the direction field and the radial growth (
C.29
). Therefore,
\Omega=3u
is a distinguished line in the equatorial diagram. It corresponds to the point(s) where
c=3b
intersect
b^{2}+c^{2}=1
:

(b,c)=\left(\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{10}},\ \pm\frac{3}{\sqrt{10}}\right)\,.

(C.35)

These are
neutral
with respect to the leading radial growth at
x=0
because
r^{\prime}\simeq 0
there. At such points one must proceed to next order (linear terms) to decide the radial stability.

C.7
Specialization to the physical
u<0
sector

The physically relevant negative SF potential is described by:

u<0\quad\land\quad\Omega=\Omega_{r}\geq 0\,,

(C.36)

therefore:

\Omega-3u>0\,.

(C.37)

Therefore, from (
C.29
) we obtain that:

•

every equatorial direction compatible with
u<0
and
\Omega\geq 0
is
radially attracting to infinity
in ’e-fold time’;

•

The equator is normally attracting on the Poincaré sphere (Appendix
C.4
).

Main result (physical
u<0
sector)

For trajectories that enters the region
u<0
with
\Omega\geq 0
, the reduced flow generically approaches the equator at infinity, which corresponds to
r\to\infty
and thus
H\to 0
(since
x,u,\Omega\propto H^{-2}
). In other words, in the negative SF potential sector, the reduced Hubble-normalized system is generically drawn to the
H\to 0
boundary, and the
N
-time formulation becomes singular.

This is precisely why a bounce/cyclic completion requires an
H
-regular dynamical extension.

C.8
Pole directions and their physical meaning

At the pole directions
\mathbf{s}_{\pm}=(\pm 1,0,0)
one obtains:

\frac{x}{r}\to\pm 1\quad\land\quad|x|\to\infty\,.

(C.38)

In terms of the original variables, this occurs when:

|H|<\left|\dot{\phi}\right|

(C.39)

(or when
\dot{\phi}
grows while
H
decays).

On the sphere, both poles are tangentially unstable (repelling in
(b,c)
), so that the generic orbits do not remain in pole directions. They are pushed toward equatorial directions, consistent with the generic approach to the
H\to 0
boundary diagnosed above.

C.9
Complete infinity classification

For the reduced constant
\lambda_{\phi*}
system (
C.5
):

(1)

Isolated pole points

(a,b,c)=(\pm 1,0,0)
: repelling on
S^{2}
(tangentially unstable), not generic late-time infinity states;

(2)

Equatorial critical circle

a=0
,
b^{2}+c^{2}=1
: normally attracting on
S^{2}
. The refined (
m=2
) dynamics shows that in the physically relevant sector, the radial flow satisfies:

r^{\prime}\sim r(\Omega-3u)>0

(C.40)

therefore, the equator is also
radially attracting
to infinity in ’e-fold time’.

Therefore, in the
u<0
sector the reduced Hubble-normalized system generically runs into the
H\to 0
boundary, and any continuation
requires an
H
-regular completion.

C.10
Extension to the full system

On the constant
\lambda_{\phi*}
branches, the above analysis is exact. Namely, in the 4D system:

\frac{d\lambda_{\phi}}{dN}=-\sqrt{6}\,\Bigl(\Gamma_{\phi}(\lambda_{\phi})-1\Bigr)\,\lambda_{\phi}^{2}\,x\,,

(C.41)

which yields that:

\frac{d\lambda_{\phi}}{dN}\propto x

(C.42)

Along the equator at infinity one obtains:

a=\frac{x}{r}\to 0\,,

(C.43)

and therefore
\lambda_{\phi}^{\prime}
is subleading in the infinity scaling and does not modify the leading global picture - the approach to equatorial infinity in the physical
u<0
sector remains the dominant behavior. On the other hand, along large
|x|
directions,
\lambda_{\phi}
can be driven toward a constant branch (depending on the SF potential closure equation), which justifies using the constant
\lambda_{\phi*}
reduced analysis as the universal leading description of the
u<0
approach to
H\to 0
.

A faint M UV = − 14.5 {}_{\rm UV}=-14.5 Lyman continuum leaker in the reionization epoch: unprecedented Ly α \alpha properties at z=5.725
M. Messa ,, E. Vanzella, T. Morishita, M. Stiavelli, T. Treu, P. Bergamini, Z. Liu, A. Zanella, A. Bolamperti, A. Verhamme, T. Garel C. Grillo, P. Rosati

Abstract
We report the unprecedented Ly α \alpha properties of AMORE6, an extremely metal-poor ( 12 + log ⁡ ( O / H ) < 6 12+\log({\rm O/H})<6 ), low-mass ( M ⋆ = 4.4 × 10 5 ​ M ⊙ M_{\star}=4.4\times 10^{5}\,M_{\odot} ), and ultra-compact (effective radius ≲ 30 \lesssim 30 pc) dwarf galaxy at z = 5.7253 z=5.7253 , gravitationally lensed by the cluster A2744. A prominent, narrow, and nearly-symmetric Ly α \alpha emission line is detected at the systemic redshift (the latter traced by H β \beta , from JWST/NIRCam slitless spectroscopy), with rest-frame EW = 150 ± 10 =150\pm 10 Å, FWHM = 58 ± 1 \rm FWHM=58\pm 1 km s -1 , and a slight asymmetry, resulting in a ∼ 10 % \rm\sim 10\% flux excess in the red wing of the line. The negligible velocity offset from systemic ( d ​ v = 4 ± 67 dv=4\pm 67 km s -1 , 3 ​ σ 3\sigma uncertainty), together with the sharpness and symmetry of the profile, indicates minimal radiative transfer effects implying a neutral hydrogen column density consistent with an optically thin medium, compatible with a non-zero ionizing photon escape fraction. If indirect spectral diagnostics calibrated at z < 4.5 z<4.5 remain the only viable tools to identify LyC leakers during reionization, then based on its strongest indicator (Ly α \alpha ), AMORE6 stands out as one of the most compelling LyC-leaking candidate yet discovered in the reionization epoch.



1
Introduction

The Epoch of Reionization (EoR) marks the last major phase transition of the Universe, when the first stars and galaxies revealed their presence by ionizing the surrounding neutral hydrogen (H
i
; e.g.,
26
). While the timeline of reionization is increasingly well constrained (
z\simeq 6
–9; e.g.,
11
;
34
;
33
;
46
), the nature of the dominant ionizing sources remains elusive.

Faint, low-mass galaxies have been proposed as the primary contributors to the ionizing photon budget
(
12
;
54
, e.g.,)
, though the role of massive galaxies is not yet fully understood, as they may also produce substantial LyC emission
(
42
;
29
, e.g.,)
. Direct measurements of ionizing UV radiation (
\lambda<912
Å) are prevented at
z\gtrsim 4.5
by the high opacity of the intergalactic medium
(
68
;
19
, IGM)
. Thus, current efforts focus on low-redshift LyC emitters
(
14
;
22
, e.g.,)
as analogs of
z>6
galaxies, to calibrate indirect diagnostics of LyC leakage applicable in the EoR.

Recent work highlights the multiparameter nature of LyC leakage, requiring correlations with diverse physical properties to be established through multivariate approaches. For instance,
22
, analyzing 35 local (
z\sim 0.3
) LyC emitters
(
14
;
13
)
, identified several key diagnostics: the EW of Lyman absorption features, the UV
\beta
slope
(
6
, see also)
, Ly
\alpha
peak separation and shift
(
66
, e.g.,)
, the Ly
\alpha
escape fraction
(
9
, e.g.,)
, dust excess
E(B-V)_{\rm neb}
, star formation rate surface density, and the O32 index ([OIII]4959,5007 / [OII]3727). These diagnostics have also been applied to a handful of confirmed LyC emitters at intermediate redshift (
1<z<4
;
23
, see also
32
;
31
), and now provide a framework for probing reionization-era sources, particularly with JWST access to rest-frame optical lines at
z=6
–9
(
31
, e.g.,)
.

In parallel, radiative transfer models predict a tight connection between LyC escape and Ly
\alpha
spectral properties
(
4
;
66
, e.g.,)
. Observations and radiative transfer models confirm that galaxies with multi-peaked Ly
\alpha
profiles and a narrow peak close to systemic are likely optically thin to LyC
(
52
;
49
;
65
;
64
;
20
;
21
;
61
, e.g.,)
. The Ly
\alpha
peak separation is controlled by the residual H
i

column density of the carved channels, and the line asymmetry correlates with the porosity and multiphase structure of the
H
ii
region
(
24
, e.g.,)
. At
z\sim 2
,
41
showed that the velocity separation between Ly
\alpha
peaks, as well as the fraction of Ly
\alpha
flux emitted close to the systemic redshift, are strong discriminants of LyC leakage. However, the increasing IGM neutrality during the EoR (partially) suppresses and reshapes Ly
\alpha
, limiting its diagnostic power
(
44
;
16
;
33
, e.g.,)
. Mitigating cases arise for sources located in ionized bubbles around luminous
z\simeq 6
quasars
(
48
, e.g.,)
or along unusually transparent sightlines
(
35
)
, where double-peaked Ly
\alpha
emission has been observed up to
z\simeq 6.5
, pointing to nonzero LyC escape.

In this Letter, we revisit the extremely metal-poor source AMORE6, recently identified by
39
with
12+\log({\rm O/H})<6
, in the context of escaping ionizing radiation. AMORE6 is a remarkably faint (
M_{1700}\simeq-14.5
), compact (few-tens parsec scale) galaxy at
z=5.725
, strongly magnified by the galaxy cluster A2744. Beyond its extraordinary low metallicity and luminosity, its unprecedented Ly
\alpha
spectral properties provide compelling evidence for substantial ionizing photon escape at
z\simeq 6
, opening a new window on the nature of reionization-era galaxies.

3
Results

Figure
2
shows the VLT/XShooter Ly
\alpha
line extracted from AMORE6-B; the line is detected at S/N
\simeq 40
, centered at
\lambda=8175.81\pm 0.08
Å (vacuum), corresponding to
z=5.7254\pm 0.0001
(for a rest-frame Ly
\alpha
wavelength of 1215.67 Å). The rest-frame equivalent width is
150\pm 10
Å. Two unprecedented properties emerge at such high redshift: (1) the velocity offset with respect the systemic redshift is
dv=4\pm 67
km s
-1
, fully consistent with emission located at the resonance frequency (
dv=0
); (2) even at the high spectral resolution of XShooter (
d\lambda\simeq 0.92
Å, corresponding to
\rm\simeq 34\penalty\ kms^{-1}
) the line profile is nearly symmetric (with a marginal excess toward the red side) and narrow, with a measured FWHM of
1.83\pm 0.03
Å, or
58\pm 1
km s
-1
, after correcting for instrumental broadening. The line shows a small asymmetry, which we quantify with the red/blue asymmetry parameter (
\rm A_{RB}
), i.e. the flux difference red-ward and blue-ward of the line peak, normalized by the line flux; the value
\rm A_{RB}=0.12^{+0.04}_{-0.05}
indicates a small, yet statistically consistent,
\sim 10\%
flux excess on the red side of the line.
Similar results, emerge from the non-parametric percentile asymmetry parameter, defined as A
{}_{\rm PERC}=\frac{(\lambda_{90}-\lambda_{50})-(\lambda_{50}-\lambda_{10})}{(\lambda_{90}-\lambda_{10})}
, which shows A
{}_{\rm PERC}=0.11\pm 0.06
, still close to symmetry with a faint excess towards the red side.
We point out that overall consistent results are obtained when the Ly
\alpha
from the AMORE6-A image is considered (Tab.
1
for details). Despite the
\sim 2.5\times
worse spectral resolution, also the analysis of the MUSE spectrum recovers similar properties (Tab.
1
).

Such properties have never been observed so clearly at this redshift;
recently,
51
and
47
identified few galaxies showing a Ly
\alpha
line emerging at the systemic redshift (i.e. very small
dv
, Fig.
3
, left), within a large samples of LAEs at
5<z<8
. However, in most cases, the line profile is larger than what measured in AMORE6 (
\rm FWHM\gtrsim 200\penalty\ km\penalty\ s^{-1}
, Fig.
3
, right) and shows a very pronounced asymmetry, indicating the effect of gas reprocessing of the line. In the remaining cases, the SNR is too low to infer the line profiles. We point out that
z\gtrsim 4
LAEs studied in the literature (including other galaxies magnified by gravitational lensing) lie about 4-6 magnitudes brighter than AMORE6, M
{}_{\rm UV}\lesssim-18
(Fig.
3
, centre).
Comparison with confirmed LyC leakers at lower redshifts (
\rm z\leq 4
) showing Ly
\alpha
at systemic velocity (e.g., Sunburst:
50
,
62
; Ion3:
63
,
38
, Ion2,
61
), together with radiative transfer models (see later), indicates that AMORE6 may be the most promising LyC-leaking candidate known to date at
z\simeq 6
.

In dense H
i
environments, Ly
\alpha
photons scatter until they shift out of resonance, producing broad profiles with little flux at systemic and widely separated peaks. In contrast, AMORE6 shows all of the Ly
\alpha
flux emerging close to resonance, suggesting extremely low column densities and likely the presence of ionized channels perforating the ISM.
In particular, given the steep ultraviolet slope (
\beta=-2.77
, F
{}_{\lambda}\propto\lambda^{\beta}
) and the extremely low metallicity (
12+\log({\rm O/H})<6
;
39
), the observed Ly
\alpha
emission is expected to be negligibly affected by dust attenuation and only weakly influenced by neutral gas damping. A similar conclusion can be inferred from radiative transfer (RT) models, showing how densities as low as
N_{\rm HI}\gtrsim 10^{16}
cm
-2
are reflected in asymmetric and/or multi-peaked Ly
\alpha
profiles, displaced from the systemic redshift (in the expanding-shell scenario, with low-to-moderate expansion velocities,
\rm v_{exp}\lesssim 100\penalty\ kms^{-1}
,
66
;
64
, see Appendix
B
).
Indeed, the optical depth of Ly
\alpha
photons is
\sim 10^{4}
times larger than that of LyC photons
(
66
, e.g.,)
, implying that the neutral hydrogen column density required for Ly
\alpha
to become optically thick is correspondingly lower.
Therefore, the detection of copious Ly
\alpha
emission peaking at the line center would indicate column densities sufficiently low to render
\tau_{{\rm Ly}\alpha}\ll 1
, or equivalently
N_{\rm HI}\ll 10^{17.2}
cm
-2

(
10
, e.g.,)
, which in turn implies a large ionizing photon escape fraction (
f_{\rm esc,LyC}\simeq 0.5
–1.0).

In this likely scenario, the narrow Ly
\alpha
line resolved with XShooter and its nearly symmetric profile suggest that the blue wing is only weakly affected by intergalactic medium absorption, which at
z=5.7
could otherwise attenuate more than 50% of the total line flux
(
25
, e.g.,)
. As reported by
39
, AMORE6 lies between two known overdensities each of them separated by
\sim 5
proper Mpc from the source; a possibility is that these structures may have generated an ionized region around AMORE6, mitigating the expected IGM damping.
From simulations that couple Ly
\alpha
transfer with detailed radiation hydrodynamics in individual H
ii
regions,
24
studied the interplay between Ly
\alpha
and LyC escape and suggested that the shape of the main Ly
\alpha
peak can distinguish between anisotropic LyC leakage through holes in a turbulent H
ii
region (favored by asymmetry) and isotropic LyC leakage from a fully density-bounded H
ii
region (symmetric profile).
AMORE6 would then represent the first likely example of an isotropic LyC emitter at
z\simeq 6
.

Alternatively, RT models predict a relatively symmetric emission at systemic redshift also at higher densities (
\rm N_{\rm HI}>10^{17}
cm
-2
) in case of fast expanding gas shells (
\rm v_{exp}\gg 100\penalty\ km\,s^{-1}
,
52
, see Appendix
B
). Such fast outflows are observed in massive and/or highly star forming galaxies (
\rm M\gtrsim 10^{9}\penalty\ M_{\odot}
,
\rm SFR\gtrsim 1\penalty\ M_{\odot}yr^{-1}
,
7
). However, we remind that AMORE6 is intrinsically a small and faint/low-mass system (Fig.
3
, centre), i.e. closer to a massive star cluster or compact HII region, for which velocities
\rm<100\penalty\ km\,s^{-1}
are expected
(
17
;
53
;
30
;
57
;
58
, e.g.,)
.

Finally, a further possibility for the observed Ly
\alpha
line, in case of densities
\rm>10^{17}\penalty\ cm^{-2}
, is a shift of the actual systemic redshift of AMORE6, by
\rm\sim 100-200\penalty\ km\,s^{-1}
(consistent to the uncertainty from
39
within
3\sigma
, see Tab.
1
), combined with the suppression of the blue Ly
\alpha
peak (emerging at those densities, e.g.
66
) by the intergalactic medium (IGM) absorption
(
19
;
16
, e.g.)
. However, even in this case, the observed line broadens and likely becomes asymmetric (Appendix
B
) still requiring, in the case of AMORE6, low gas densities.

4
Final Remarks

AMORE6 is an exceptional source for several reasons: (1) its currently inferred metallicity is the lowest known among high-redshift galaxies, making it a prime candidate for a pristine star-forming region
(
39
)
; (2) it is strongly lensed by a factor of
\sim 77

(
5
)
, allowing it to be spatially resolved in all directions in the image plane; and (3) it represents the most compelling case of a Lyman continuum leaker identified during the reionization epoch, displaying Lya emission properties consistent with a low LyC-optically thin, HI column.

Under the conditions reported here, the Ly
\alpha
line is the most powerful tracer of optically thin gas. This makes AMORE6 a strong candidate for an isotropic Lyman-continuum emitter during the reionization epoch, with very high
f_{\rm esc}
and large ionizing-photon production rate

(
39
)
.
Deep, high–spectral-resolution NIRSpec observations will be needed to refine the systemic redshift, measure the gas expansion velocity from Balmer-line profiles and/or abosprtion lines, and search for additional emission lines.

Appendix B
Comparison to radiative transfer models

We fit AMORE6 with the grid of idealized Ly
\alpha
radiative‐transfer models from
15
accounting for the instrumental broadening and identified two families of acceptable solutions, both implying very low effective opacity: (i) very fast outflows, or (ii) extremely low H I columns. Fig.
4
shows the best–fit models for each family, alongside variants in which all parameters are fixed to the best–fit values except
\rm N_{HI}
(to illustrate its dominant impact on the Ly
\alpha
profile).

In the high-velocity family, the preferred solution adopts
\rm N_{HI}=10^{20}\penalty\ cm^{-2}
to reproduce the modest red wing; however faint residuals of this wing (almost consistent with the observational noise level) extend up to redder wavelengths than observed. Models with lower
\rm N_{HI}
are essentially indistinguishable and recover the intrinsic line shape because the effective opacity is negligible, whereas higher
\rm N_{HI}
suppress near–systemic photons and fail to match the data.
However, such an extreme outflow (
\rm V_{max}\approx 750\penalty\ km\,s^{-1}
) is probably unlikely for an H
ii
region/star cluster
(
58
;
57
, e.g.,)
. Restricting the search to
\rm V_{max}\leq 100\penalty\ km\,s^{-1}
, good fits are obtained only with very low columns, with a best fit at
\rm N_{HI}\approx 10^{14}\penalty\ cm^{-2}
; larger densities inevitably shifts the Ly
\alpha
peak redward of systemic and degrades the fit. Under the hypothesis of low velocity of the expanding shell, this strongly indicates that the line–of–sight
\rm N_{HI}
in AMORE6 is extremely low, consistent with a high LyC escape fraction.

A caveat is the IGM opacity at this redshift, which is not included in our forward models and could attenuate the blue side of the line. The observed profile’s near symmetry suggests this effect is modest, but it does not alter the conclusion that AMORE6 exhibits very low effective neutral columns.

MacWilliams Theory over ℤ k \mathbb{Z}_{k} and nu-functions over Lattices
Zhiyong Zheng, Fengxia Liu, Kun Tian

Abstract




Abstract
Continuing previous works on MacWilliams theory over codes and lattices, a generalization of the MacWilliams theory over
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
for
m
codes is established, and the complete weight enumerator MacWilliams identity also holds for codes over the finitely generated rings
\mathbb{Z}_{k}[\xi]
. In the context of lattices, the analogy of the MacWilliams identity associated with nu-function was conjectured by Solé in 1995, and we present a new formula for nu-function over the lattices associated with a ternary code, which is rather different from the original conjecture. Furthermore, we provide many counterexamples to show that the Solé conjecture never holds in the general case, except for the lattices associated with a binary code.

Keywords:
MacWilliams theorem, codes, lattices, nu-function.

1
Introduction

The MacWilliams theorem for linear codes with a Hamming metric
[
12
,
13
]
establishes an identity that relates the weight enumerator of a code to that of its dual code. Various authors have extended this study in different directions. One direction involves generalizing the weight enumerators to include more than two variables, such as Lee and complete weight enumerators, and extending the concept to codes defined over alphabets beyond finite fields. For instance, Wan
[
24
]
provided the MacWilliams theorem for code over Galois rings. Another generalization involves adapting the notion of weight to consider multiple codewords simultaneously, leading to the generalized Hamming weights by Wei
[
25
]
and MacWilliams-type results for
m
-tuple support enumerators by Kløve
[
10
]
, Shiromoto
[
19
]
, Simonis
[
21
]
, and Ray-Chaudhuri and Siap
[
14
,
15
]
. Britz
[
4
,
5
]
further generalized some of these results and provided matroid-theoretic proofs. Britz
[
3
]
outlined new and extensive connections between weight enumerators and Tutte polynomials of matroids.

First, we consider the MacWilliams theorem for
m
codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
, where the ring of integers modulo
k\ (k>1)
. We generalize the results of
[
9
]
and establish a MacWilliams-type identity for
m
-tuple support enumerator over the finite ring
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
. We prove that the MacWilliams theorem of the complete weight enumerators also holds for code over
\mathbb{Z}_{k}[\xi]
. For MacWilliams theorems over more general finite rings, for example, Frobenius rings and finite modules, we refer to Wood
[
26
]
and
[
27
]
.

The close relationships between codes and lattices have been intensively studied over the past few decades. The classical problem of counting lattice points in Euclidean spheres involves the use of the Jacobian theta function. The MacWilliams formula can be viewed as a finite counterpart to the Poisson summation formula employed in theta function theory within this framework. The nu-function of lattices is involved in the more contemporary issue of enumerating lattice points in pyramids for the
L^{1}
-norm
[
2
,
22
]
. These two challenges have important applications in the field of multidimensional vector quantization
[
2
,
17
]
. In this paper, we present a new analog of the MacWilliams identity over lattices involving a nut function, a problem conjectured by Solé
[
6
,
22
]
. Here, we provide a solution to this problem.

1.1
m
-Tuple MacWilliams Identity

To begin, we present the essential definitions required to articulate MacWilliams’s original theorem
[
13
]
. Consider
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, a finite field containing
q
elements,
n
as a positive integer, and
C\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
representing a linear code. Let
|C|
denote the total number of codewords in
C
, and
<a,b>
represent the standard pairing on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. For any
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, the Hamming weight, symbolized as
w(x)
, is defined as the count of non-zero coordinates in
x
. We use
W_{C}(z)
to signify the Hamming weight enumerator of
C
in the variables
z
:

W_{C}(z)=\sum\limits_{x\in C}z^{w(x)},

or an equivalent homogeneous form in two indeterminates
z_{1}
and
z_{2}

W_{C}(z_{1},z_{2})=\sum\limits_{x\in C}z_{1}^{w(x)}z_{2}^{n-w(x)}.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be any linear code and
C^{\bot}
be its dual code, MacWilliams
[
7
]
(or also see
[
12
]
) showed that

W_{C^{\bot}}(z)=\frac{1}{|C|}(1+(q-1)z)^{n}W_{C}\left(\frac{1-z}{1+(q-1)z}\right).

(1.1)

Let
z=z_{1}/z_{2}
, one has the homogeneous form of MacWilliams identity immediately (see Theorem 9.32 of
[
11
]
)

W_{C^{\bot}}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{1}{|C|}W_{C}(z_{2}-z_{1},z_{2}+(q-1)z_{1}).

(1.2)

Klemm
[
9
]
expanded upon the MacWilliams identity in 1987, applying it to Hamming weight enumerators for codes defined over finite rings,
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
. To explain this concept, consider
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
as the ring of integers modulo
k
(where
k>1
) and a code
C\subset\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n}
as an additive subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n}
. For any codeword
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n}
, the Hamming weight
w(x)
is defined in the conventional manner. This finding was derived from
[
9
]
:

Theorem 1
(Klemm) Let
C\subset\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n}
be a code of length
n
,
C^{\bot}
be its dual, then

W_{C^{\bot}}(z)=\frac{1}{|C|}(1+(k-1)z)^{n}W_{C}\left(\frac{1-z}{1+(k-1)z}\right)

(1.3)

or equivalently

W_{C^{\bot}}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{1}{|C|}W_{C}(z_{2}+(k-1)z_{1},z_{2}-z_{1}).

(1.4)

A primary objective of this research is to expand upon Theorem 1 and introduce a MacWilliams-type identity for the
m
-tuple support enumerator defined over
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
. To present our results, consider
m
codes
C_{1},C_{2},\cdots,C_{m}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n}
, which may or may not be identical. Let
\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}
represent the ring of all
m\times n
matrices over
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
. For a matrix
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}
, we denote
ew(x)
as the effective length weight of
x
, which is the count of non-zero columns in
x
. Define
\underline{C}=C_{1}\times C_{2}\times\cdots\times C_{m}
,
\underline{C}^{\bot}=C_{1}^{\bot}\times C_{2}^{\bot}\times\cdots\times C_{m}^{\bot}
, and
|\underline{C}|=|C_{1}|\times\cdots\times|C_{m}|
. We consider any element
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{m})\in\underline{C}
as a matrix in
\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}
by

x=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{m}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n},\quad x_{i}\in C_{i}.

W_{\underline{C}}^{(m)}(z)=\sum\limits_{x\in\underline{C}}z^{ew(x)}.

Theorem 2
Let
C_{1},C_{2},\cdots,C_{m}
be any
m
linear codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n}
, then we have

W_{{\underline{C}}^{\bot}}^{(m)}(z)=\frac{1}{|\underline{C}|}(1+(k^{m}-1)z)^{n}W_{\underline{C}}^{(m)}\left(\frac{1-z}{1+(k^{m}-1)z}\right).

(1.5)

MacWilliams theorems have been extended in various ways, with a significant number of these generalizations addressing complete weight enumerators, as exemplified by the work of Wan
[
24
]
and Kaplan
[
8
]
. In our effort to further expand upon Theorem 1, we investigate a code defined over the finitely generated ring
\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n}[\xi]
. Our research establishes that the MacWilliams theorem for complete weight enumerators extends its applicability to codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n}[\xi]
.

Let
R=\mathbb{Z}_{k}[\xi]
be a finitely generated ring with
k
being a positive integer,
\xi
be the root of an irreducible polynomial of degree
t
,
C\subset R^{n}
be a linear code. For any
x=x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}\in C
, assuming
x_{i}=x_{i0}+x_{i1}\xi+\cdots+x_{i(t-1)}\xi^{t-1}\in R
, we define the function
u(x_{i})=x_{i0}+x_{i1}\cdot k+\cdots+x_{i(t-1)}\cdot k^{t-1}\in[0,k^{t}-1]
, and
\tau(x_{i})=(x_{i0},x_{i1},\cdots,x_{i(t-1)})\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{t}
. For
j=0,1,\cdots,k^{t}-1
, let
n_{j}(x)=\#\{1\leqslant i\leqslant n\ |\ u(x_{i})=j\}
, which is the number of
x_{i}
that satisfies
u(x_{i})=j
. We define the complete weight enumerator of
C
as the following

W_{C}(z_{0},z_{1},\cdots,z_{|R|-1})=\sum\limits_{x\in C}z_{0}^{n_{0}(x)}z_{1}^{n_{1}(x)}\cdots z_{|R|-1}^{n_{|R|-1}(x)}.

We have the following MacWilliams identity.

Theorem 3
Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a linear code of length
n
,
C^{\bot}
be its dual, then

W_{C^{\bot}}(z_{0},z_{1},\cdots,z_{|R|-1})=

\frac{1}{|C|}W_{C}\left(\sum\limits_{l=0}^{|R|-1}z_{l},\sum\limits_{l=0}^{|R|-1}e^{\frac{2\pi<\tau(1),\tau(l)>}{k}i}z_{l},\cdots,\sum\limits_{l=0}^{|R|-1}e^{\frac{2\pi<\tau(|R|-1),\tau(l)>}{k}i}z_{l}\right),

(1.6)

where
\tau(l)
is the
k
base vector of
l
with length
t
, i.e. if
l=l_{0}+l_{1}\cdot k+\cdots+l_{t-1}\cdot k^{t-1}
,
l_{0},l_{1},\cdots,l_{t-1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}
, then
\tau(l)=(l_{0},l_{1},\cdots,l_{t-1})
.

Wan
[
24
]
presented a specific case of (1.6) in his work on Galois rings. In this paper, Theorem 3 represents a more generalized form of the main conclusion of
[
24
]
.

In the finite ring
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
, it is noteworthy that researchers are increasingly focusing on the weight enumerator with the Lee metric. For further information, refer to the results in
[
1
,
18
,
20
,
23
]
. Notably,
[
1
]
demonstrated that the analogy of the MacWilliams theorem with Lee weight enumerators does not exist for
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
when
k\geqslant 5
. For MacWilliams theorems over Frobenius rings, refer to Wood
[
26
]
and
[
27
]
.

1.2
Nu-Function over Lattices

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a lattice of rank
n
, the nu-function over
\Lambda
is defined by

\nu_{\Lambda}(z)=\sum\limits_{x\in\Lambda}z^{|x|_{1}}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{+\infty}z^{n}|\{x\in\Lambda:|x|_{1}=n\}|,

where
|x|_{1}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|
is the
L^{1}
-norm. Although the Poisson summation formula for the nu-function is unknown, the same analogy of the MacWilliams identity exists. It was conjectured by
[
22
]
that

2^{\frac{n}{2}}\nu_{\Lambda^{*}}(\text{tanh}^{2}(\frac{\beta}{2}))=\text{det}(\Lambda)(\text{sinh}(2\beta))^{\frac{n}{2}}\nu_{\Lambda}(\text{tanh}(\frac{\alpha}{2})),

(1.7)

where
\Lambda
is the arbitrary lattice of rank
n
, and the parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
are connected by the relation
e^{-2\beta}=\text{tanh}(\alpha)
. In
[
29
]
, we demonstrated that the aforementioned conjecture holds true for a lattice associated with a binary code
C
. However, in general, the following result differs significantly from the aforementioned conjecture. Initially, we present a novel formula for the nu-function associated with a ternary code, and subsequently, we provide numerous counter-examples to elucidate why the conjecture is invalid in general cases. To articulate our primary result, we first generalize the construction
A
as follows:

Construction
A_{k}

Let
C\subset\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n}
be a code. A lattice
A_{k}(C)
is constructed by

A_{k}(C)=\{x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\ \big|\ \exists c\in C\ \text{such that}\ x\equiv c\ (\text{mod}\ k)\}.

Theorem 4
If
\Lambda=A_{3}(C)
is a lattice associated with a ternary code
C
, then we have

3^{n}\nu_{\Lambda^{*}}(\text{tanh}^{3}(\frac{\beta}{2}))=\text{det}(\Lambda)\left[\frac{(1+\text{tanh}(\frac{\beta}{2}))(1-\text{tanh}^{3}(\frac{\alpha}{2}))}{(1-\text{tanh}(\frac{\beta}{2}))(1+\text{tanh}^{3}(\frac{\alpha}{2}))}\right]^{n}\nu_{\Lambda}(\text{tanh}(\frac{\alpha}{2})),

(1.8)

where the parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
are connected by the relation

e^{-2\beta}=\frac{3\text{tanh}(\alpha)}{8-5\text{tanh}(\alpha)}.

Upon comparing equations (1.8) and (1.7), it is evident that equation (1.8) does not hold for the general lattice, with the exception of
\Lambda=A_{2}(C)
, as demonstrated in
[
29
]
. For lattices
\Lambda=A_{k}(C)\ (k>3)
, numerous counter-examples are provided in the final section.

Property
The relationship between parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
satisfying the following symmetrical relation

e^{-2\beta}=\frac{3\text{tanh}(\alpha)}{8-5\text{tanh}(\alpha)}\Longleftrightarrow e^{-2\alpha}=\frac{3\text{tanh}(\beta)}{8-5\text{tanh}(\beta)}.

Proof:
We have

e^{-2\beta}=\frac{3\text{tanh}(\alpha)}{8-5\text{tanh}(\alpha)}\Longleftrightarrow e^{-2\beta}=\frac{3\cdot\frac{e^{\alpha}-e^{-\alpha}}{e^{\alpha}+e^{-\alpha}}}{8-5\cdot\frac{e^{\alpha}-e^{-\alpha}}{e^{\alpha}+e^{-\alpha}}}=\frac{3e^{2\alpha}-3}{3e^{2\alpha}+13}

\Longleftrightarrow e^{2\alpha}=\frac{3+13e^{-2\beta}}{3-3e^{-2\beta}}\Longleftrightarrow e^{-2\alpha}=\frac{3e^{2\beta}-3}{3e^{2\beta}+13}=\frac{3\text{tanh}(\beta)}{8-5\text{tanh}(\beta)}.

\Box

2
Finite Fourier Transform over the Matrix Ring
\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
be the ring of integer modulo
k
with
k>1
,
e^{2\pi i/k}
be the primitive root of the unit, and an additive characteristic
\psi
for
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
by
\psi(a)=e^{2\pi ia/k}\ (a\in\mathbb{Z}_{k})
. Suppose that
H\subset\mathbb{Z}_{k}
and
H\neq 0
, is an additive subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
, trivially, we have

\sum\limits_{a\in H}\psi(a)=\sum\limits_{a\in H}e^{2\pi ia/k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0,&\ \text{if}\ a\neq 0,\\
|H|,&\ \text{if}\ a=0.\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

If
f(x)
is a function defined over
\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n}
, the finite Fourier transform of
f
is defined by

\text{FT}f(x)=\sum\limits_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n}}f(\xi)\psi(<x,\xi>),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n},

where
<x,\xi>
is the typical pairing of
x
and
\xi
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n}
be a linear code of length
n
and
C^{\bot}
be its dual code. We define its characteristic function as
\chi_{C}(x)
, that is,
\chi_{C}(x)=1
, if
x\in C
, and
\chi_{C}(x)=0
, if
x\notin C
.

Lemma 2.1
Suppose that
C\subset\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n}
is a linear code, and
C^{\bot}
is its dual, then we have

\chi_{C^{\bot}}(x)=\frac{1}{|C|}\text{FT}\chi_{C}(x).

Proof:
By the definition, we only need to show that

\sum\limits_{\xi\in C}\psi(<x,\xi>)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}|C|,&\ \text{if}\ x\in C^{\bot}.\\
0,&\ \text{if}\ x\notin C^{\bot}.\end{array}\right.

If
x\notin C^{\bot}
, let
T(x)
be the subgroup of
C
, given by

T(x)=\{y\in C\ \big|\ <y,x>=0\}.

It is easily seen that
<y_{1},x>=<y_{2},x>
if
y_{1}-y_{2}\in T(x)
, it means that
<y_{1},x>=<y_{2},x>
if
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
lie in the same coset of
T(x)
. Thus, by (2.1), we

\sum\limits_{\xi\in C}\psi(<x,\xi>)=|T(x)|\sum\limits_{c\in C/T(x)}\psi(<c,x>)=|T(x)|\sum\limits_{a\in H}\psi(a)=0,

where
H
is a subgroup given by

H=\{<c,x>\ \big|\ c\in C/T(x)\}.

\Box

From Lemma 2.1, we trivially have the Poisson summation formula for any function
f
over
\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n}

\sum\limits_{x\in C^{\bot}}f(x)=\frac{1}{|C|}\sum\limits_{x\in C}\text{FT}f(x).

(2.2)

If taking
f(x)=z^{w(x)}
, it is easy to see that

\text{FT}z^{w(x)}=(1+(k-1)z)^{n}\left(\frac{1-z}{1+(k-1)z}\right)^{w(x)}.

Then, Theorem 1 follows immediately.

To prove Theorem 2, we shall first generalize this transform to a matrix ring. Let
\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}
be a ring comprising all
m\times n
matrices over
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
. For any two matrices
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}
,
x^{T}y
is an
n\times n
square matrix. Let Tr
(x^{T}y)
denote the trace of
x^{T}y
, then Tr
(x^{T}y)\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}
. We define
<x,y>=\text{Tr}(x^{T}y)
as the pairing of
x
and
y
.

Definition 2.1
Suppose that
f
is a function defined over
\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}
, the finite Fourier transform FT
f
of
f
is defined by

\text{FT}f(x)=\sum\limits_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}}f(\xi)\psi(<x,\xi>),\quad x\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}.

It is easy to see that

f(x)=\frac{1}{q^{mn}}\sum\limits_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}}\text{FT}f(\xi)\psi(-<x,\xi>),\quad x\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}.

Let
C_{1},C_{2},\cdots,C_{m}
be
m
codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
of length
n
,
\underline{C}=C_{1}\times\cdots\times C_{m}
. We consider
\underline{C}
as a subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}
according to
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m})^{T}
, where
x_{i}\in C_{i}
. Let
\chi_{\underline{C}}
denote the characteristic function of
\underline{C}
, such that
\chi_{\underline{C}}(x)=1
if
x\in\underline{C}
, and
\chi_{\underline{C}}(x)=0
if
x\notin\underline{C}
.

Lemma 2.2
If
C_{i}
(
1\leqslant i\leqslant m
) is the linear code of
\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n}
, then we have

\chi_{\underline{C}^{\bot}}(x)=\frac{1}{|\underline{C}|}\text{FT}\chi_{\underline{C}}(x),

where
\underline{C}^{\bot}=C_{1}^{\bot}\times C_{2}^{\bot}\times\cdots\times C_{m}^{\bot}
.

Proof:
By the definition of FT
\chi_{\underline{C}}
, we observe that for any
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}
,

\text{FT}\chi_{\underline{C}}(x)=\sum\limits_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}}\chi_{\underline{C}}(\xi)\psi(<x,\xi>)=\sum\limits_{\xi\in\underline{C}}\psi(<x,\xi>).

If we write

x=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
\vdots\\
x_{m}\end{pmatrix},\ x_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n},\ \text{and}\ \xi=\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\xi_{m}\end{pmatrix},\ \xi_{i}\in C_{i},

then

<x,\xi>=\text{Tr}(x^{T}\xi)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}<x_{i},\xi_{i}>.

It follows that

\text{FT}\chi_{\underline{C}}(x)=\mathop{\prod}\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sum\limits_{\xi_{i}\in C_{i}}\psi(<x_{i},\xi_{i}>).

It is known that

\sum\limits_{\xi_{i}\in C_{i}}\psi(<x_{i},\xi_{i}>)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}|C_{i}|,&\text{if}\ x_{i}\in C_{i}^{\bot}.\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

We have FT
\chi_{\underline{C}}(x)=|\underline{C}|\chi_{\underline{C}^{\bot}}(x)
.

\Box

Based on the aforementioned observation, we derive the following more generalized Poisson summation formula applicable to the matrix ring:

Lemma 2.3
Let
\underline{C}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}
be the block linear codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
, and
f(x)
be arbitrary function over
\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}
, then

\sum\limits_{x\in\underline{C}^{\bot}}f(x)=\frac{1}{|\underline{C}|}\sum\limits_{x\in\underline{C}}\text{FT}f(x).

Proof:

\sum\limits_{x\in\underline{C}^{\bot}}f(x)=\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}}\chi_{\underline{C}^{\bot}}(x)f(x)

\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad=\frac{1}{|\underline{C}|}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}}f(x)\text{FT}\chi_{\underline{C}}(x)

\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=\frac{1}{|\underline{C}|}\sum\limits_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}}\chi_{\underline{C}}(\xi)\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}}f(x)\psi(<x,\xi>)

\qquad\quad=\frac{1}{|\underline{C}|}\sum\limits_{\xi\in\underline{C}}\text{FT}f(\xi).

\Box

Subsequently, we present a concise proof for Theorem 2.

Proof of Theorem 2:

Let
f(x)=z^{ew(x)}
, where
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}
and
ew(x)
are the effective length weight functions given in Section 1. To prove Theorem 2, using the Poisson summation formula, we show only that

\text{FT}z^{ew(x)}=(1+(k^{m}-1)z)^{n}\left(\frac{1-z}{1+(k^{m}-1)z}\right)^{ew(x)}.

Write
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}
,
x_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m}
, and
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{n})

\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}
,
\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m}
, then

<x,\xi>=\text{Tr}(x^{T}\xi)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}<\xi_{i},x_{i}>.

It follows that

\text{FT}f(x)=\sum\limits_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}}f(\xi)\psi(<x,\xi>)

\qquad\qquad\ =\sum\limits_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}}z^{ew(\xi)}\psi(<x,\xi>)

\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \ =\sum\limits_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m\times n}}z^{ew(\xi_{1})+\cdots+ew(\xi_{n})}\psi(<x,\xi>)

where
ew(\xi_{i})=1
, if
\xi_{i}\neq 0
, and
ew(\xi_{i})=0
, if
\xi_{i}=0
is a zero vector. Therefore, we have

\text{FT}f(x)=\mathop{\prod}\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\sum\limits_{\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m}}z^{ew(\xi_{i})}\psi(<x_{i},\xi_{i}>))

\qquad\qquad\quad=\mathop{\prod}\limits_{i=1}^{n}(1+z\sum\limits_{\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m}\backslash\{0\}}\psi(<x_{i},\xi_{i}>)).

If
x_{i}=0
, it is easy to obtain

\sum\limits_{\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m}\backslash\{0\}}\psi(<x_{i},\xi_{i}>)=k^{m}-1,

otherwise, if
x_{i}\neq 0
, we write
x_{i}=(x_{i1},\cdots,x_{im})^{\prime}
and
\xi_{i}=(\xi_{i1},\cdots,\xi_{im})^{\prime}
, then
\exists 1\leqslant s\leqslant m
, such that
x_{is}\neq 0
. Based on (2.1), we know that

\sum\limits_{\xi_{is}\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}}\psi(x_{is}\xi_{is})=0,

thus,

\sum\limits_{\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m}}\psi(<x_{i},\xi_{i}>)=\mathop{\prod}\limits_{j=1}^{m}\sum\limits_{\xi_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}}\psi(x_{ij}\xi_{ij})=0.

So we get

\sum\limits_{\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m}\backslash\{0\}}\psi(<x_{i},\xi_{i}>)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}k^{m}-1,&\text{if}\ x_{i}=0.\\
-1,&\text{if}\ x_{i}\neq 0.\end{array}\right.

It follows that

\text{FT}z^{ew(x)}=\mathop{\prod}\limits_{i=1}^{n}(1+z\sum\limits_{\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{m}\backslash\{0\}}\psi(<x_{i},\xi_{i}>))\qquad

\qquad\qquad\quad\ =\mathop{\prod}\limits_{i=1}^{n}((1-z)^{ew(x_{i})}(1+(k^{m}-1)z)^{1-ew(x_{i})})

\qquad\qquad\ =(1+(k^{m}-1)z)^{n}\left(\frac{1-z}{1+(k^{m}-1)z}\right)^{ew(x)}.

We complete the proof of Theorem 2.

Lemma 3.2
Let
A_{3}(C)
be a lattice associated with the ternary code
C
, then the dual lattice of

Proof:
First, we prove that

x\in A_{3}(C)
whence
x\in C

<\alpha,x>\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
x\in C
, and

<3\alpha,x>\equiv 0\ (\text{mod}\ 3),\quad\forall x\in C.

Thus,
3\alpha\ \text{mod}\ 3\in C^{\bot}
and
3\alpha\in A_{3}(C^{\bot})
, which implies that
\alpha\in\frac{1}{3}A_{3}(C^{\bot})
.

To show that
\frac{1}{3}A_{3}(C^{\bot})\subset A_{3}(C)^{*}
, for any
\beta\in\frac{1}{3}A_{3}(C^{\bot})
or
3\beta\in A_{3}(C^{\bot})
, we see that
\beta\in A_{3}(C)^{*}

x\in C

<3\beta\ \text{mod}\ 3,x>=0
, which implies that
<3\beta,x>\equiv 0\ (\text{mod}\ 3)

\Box

Lemma 3.3
Let
C\subset\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{n}
be a ternary code of length
n
,
\alpha
be any a parameter, then we have

\nu_{A_{3}(C)}(\tanh\frac{\alpha}{2})=\left(\frac{1+\tanh^{3}\frac{\alpha}{2}}{1-\tanh^{3}\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{n}W_{C}\left(\frac{\tanh\alpha}{2-\tanh\alpha}\right),

where
W_{C}(z)
is the weight enumerator function.

Proof:
For any
x\in C
, we define a set
A_{3}(x)
by

A_{3}(x)=\{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\ |\ y\equiv x\ (\text{mod}\ 3)\}.

It is easy to see that
A_{3}(x_{1})\cap A_{3}(x_{2})=\emptyset
, if
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
, so we have

A_{3}(C)=\mathop{\cup}\limits_{x\in C}A_{3}(x),\ \text{and}\ \nu_{A_{3}(C)}(z)=\sum\limits_{x\in C}\nu_{A_{3}(x)}(z).

Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})\in C
, then
A_{3}(x)=(x_{1}+3\mathbb{Z})\times(x_{2}+3\mathbb{Z})\times\cdots\times(x_{n}+3\mathbb{Z})
. Assume
n_{0}(x)=|\{i|x_{i}=0\}|
,
n_{1}(x)=|\{i|x_{i}=1\}|
and
n_{2}(x)=|\{i|x_{i}=2\}|
. This was not difficult to observe

\nu_{A_{3}(x)}(z)=\sum\limits_{y\in A_{3}(x)}z^{|y|_{1}}=(\sum\limits_{y^{\prime}\in 3\mathbb{Z}}z^{|y^{\prime}|})^{n_{0}(x)}(\sum\limits_{y^{\prime\prime}\in 3\mathbb{Z}+1}z^{|y^{\prime\prime}|})^{n_{1}(x)}(\sum\limits_{y^{\prime\prime\prime}\in 3\mathbb{Z}+2}z^{|y^{\prime\prime\prime}|})^{n_{2}(x)}

=(\nu_{3\mathbb{Z}}(z))^{n_{0}(x)}(\nu_{3\mathbb{Z}+1}(z))^{n_{1}(x)}(\nu_{3\mathbb{Z}+2}(z))^{n_{2}(x)}.\qquad\qquad\ \ \

If the parameter
z
satisfies
|z|<1

\nu_{3\mathbb{Z}}(z)=\frac{1+z^{3}}{1-z^{3}},\ \text{and}\ \nu_{3\mathbb{Z}+1}(z)=\nu_{3\mathbb{Z}+2}(z)=\frac{z+z^{2}}{1-z^{3}}.

It follows that

\nu_{A_{3}(x)}(z)=\left(\frac{1+z^{3}}{1-z^{3}}\right)^{n_{0}(x)}\left(\frac{z+z^{2}}{1-z^{3}}\right)^{n_{1}(x)+n_{2}(x)}=\left(\frac{1+z^{3}}{1-z^{3}}\right)^{n-w(x)}\left(\frac{z+z^{2}}{1-z^{3}}\right)^{w(x)}.

We have

\nu_{A_{3}(C)}(\tanh\frac{\alpha}{2})=\left(\frac{1+\tanh^{3}\frac{\alpha}{2}}{1-\tanh^{3}\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{n}W_{C}(\frac{\tanh\alpha}{2-\tanh\alpha}).

This is the proof of lemma 3.3.

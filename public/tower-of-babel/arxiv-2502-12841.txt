Alperin’s bound and normal Sylow subgroups
Zhicheng Feng, J. Miquel Martínez, Damiano Rossi

Abstract
Let G G be a finite group, p p a prime number and P P a Sylow p p -subgroup of G G . Recently, G. Malle, G. Navarro, and P. H. Tiep conjectured that the number of p p -Brauer characters of G G coincides with that of the normaliser 𝐍 G ​ ( P ) {\bf N}_{G}(P) if and only if P P is normal in G G . We reduce this conjecture to a question about finite simple groups and prove it for the prime p = 2 p=2 . As a by-product of our work, we prove a reduction theorem for the blockwise version of Alperin’s lower bound on p p -Brauer characters and prove it for 2 2 -blocks of maximal defect. This improves recent results obtained by Malle, Navarro, and Tiep.

Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group,
p
a prime number, and
P
a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. According to the Itô–Michler theorem
p
does not divide the degree of any irreducible complex character of
G
if and only if
P
is abelian and normal in
G
. This result was inspired by Brauer’s height zero conjecture, recently proved in
[
Ruh
,
MNSFT24
]
, which provides a representation theoretic characterisation of the commutativity of defect
p
-subgroups, and in particular of Sylow
p
-subgroups.

The normality condition of the Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
in the Itô–Michler theorem has also been isolated
[
Mic86
,
MN12
,
GLLV22
,
MSF24
]
. Interestingly, all of these characterisations are obtained by imposing various restrictions on the divisibility of character degrees, in the spirit of the Itô–Michler theorem.
A characterisation of a completely different nature has recently been conjectured by G. Malle, G. Navarro, and P. H. Tiep
[
MNT23
]
. Below, we denote by
\mathrm{IBr}(G)
the set of
p
-Brauer characters of
G
.

Conjecture 1.1

(Malle–Navarro–Tiep)
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
p
a prime number, and
P
a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. Then
P
is normal in
G
if and only if
|\mathrm{IBr}(G)|=|\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{G}(P))|
.

Before proceeding further, we remark that the statement of Conjecture
1.1
can be rephrased in purely group theoretic terms. In fact, the number of
p
-Brauer characters of a group coincides with the number of its
p
-regular conjugacy classes. One might wonder whether a completely group theoretic proof of this conjecture can be found. We believe this to be an extremely challenging task. As a matter of fact, we show that Conjecture
1.1
follows from a statement (see Conjecture
2.1
below) that is closely related to the inductive condition for the blockwise Alperin weight conjecture
[
NT11
,
Spä13
]
, a condition that lies at the heart of modern finite group representation theory. Recall that a group
S
is said to be
involved
in
G
if there is a subgroup
H
of
G
and a normal subgroup
N
of
H
such that
S
is isomorphic to the quotient
H/N
.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
p
a prime number, and assume that Conjecture
2.1
holds at the prime
p
for every
p
-block of maximal defect of every covering group of any non-abelian finite simple group of order divisible by
p
involved in
G
. Then the Malle–Navarro–Tiep Conjecture
1.1
holds for the group
G
with respect to the prime
p
.

We are then left to verify the hypothesis of Theorem
A
. By extending the results of
[
MNT23
]
, we are able to verify Conjecture
2.1
for
2
-blocks of maximal defect of quasi-simple groups (see Theorem
5.5
), and hence to obtain the Malle–Navarro–Tiep Conjecture
1.1
for the prime
2
.

Theorem B
.

The Malle–Navarro–Tiep Conjecture
1.1
holds for all finite groups with respect to the prime
p=2
.

Before proceeding further, we remark that the statement of Conjecture
2.1
, considered in the hypothesis of Theorem

, is in fact a consequence of the inductive condition for the blockwise Alperin weight conjecture mentioned above (see Remark

(ii)). The latter, has been studied by several authors (see
[
Fen19
]
,
[
Li21
]
,
[
FM22
]
,
[
FLZ22a
]
,
[
FLZ22b
]
,
[

]
,
[
AHL24
]
, and the survey
[
FZ22
]
) and has been verified for several families of finite simple groups. As a corollary of these results, Theorem

implies that Conjecture
1.1
also holds, at every prime
p
, for groups with abelian Sylow
2
-subgroups or abelian Sylow
3
-subgroups, and in many other cases (see, for instance, the results of
[
MRR
, Section 8]
).

In the remaining part of this introduction we explain how Theorem
A
is obtained and describe some by-products of independent interest. The proof of Theorem
A
can roughly be divided into two parts. In the first part, we prove a reduction theorem for Conjecture
2.1
to finite simple groups. The statement of Conjecture

provides an inductive condition for the blockwise version of Alperin’s lower bound
[
Alp87
, Consequence 1]
(i.e., that
|\mathrm{IBr}(B)|\geq|\mathrm{IBr}(b)|
if

is the Brauer correspondent block of

), adapted to arbitrary finite groups. The next result is in line with a principle advertised by Dade in the 1990s and pursued in
[

]
,
[
Ros23
]
,
[
MRR
]
, and
[
Ros24
]
: namely, Dade’s principle asserts that the local-global counting conjectures should admit a refinement that can be proved to hold for arbitrary finite groups once verified for (quasi-)simple groups. In the case of the McKay conjecture, considered in
[
Ros23
]
, this idea plays a crucial role in the final proof obtained by Cabanes and Späth
[

]
. We can state our reduction theorem as follows.

Theorem C
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
p
a prime number, and assume that Conjecture
2.1
holds at the prime
p
for every covering group of any non-abelian finite simple group of order divisible by
p
involved in
G
. Then Conjecture
2.1
holds for
G
at the prime
p
.

We remark here that the proof of Theorem

is compatible with the choice of well-behaved classes of

-blocks. In particular, if we want to obtain Conjecture

for blocks of maximal defect, then it suffices to verify it for the same class of blocks for the covering groups of finite simple groups (see Remark
2.11
). Thanks to this observation, in the second part of our proof of Theorem

, we can apply Theorem

to conclude that Conjecture

holds for blocks of maximal defect of arbitrary finite groups. This, in turns, is what allows us to conclude that groups which satisfy the equality
|\operatorname{IBr}(G)|=|\operatorname{IBr}({\bf N}_{G}(P))|
must contain a normal Sylow
p
-subgroup (see Theorem

). It is also important to point out that, while the conclusion of Theorem

does not contain any information about

-blocks, our proof requires a condition, Conjecture

, that actually deals with

-blocks (see the proof of Theorem

). This is also the reason why, in order to obtain Theorem

, we need to extend the results of
[
MNT23
]
and to verify Conjecture

for
2
-blocks of maximal defect.

To conclude, observe that Theorem

provides, in particular, a reduction theorem for the blockwise version of Alperin’s lower bound. This, together with the verification of Conjecture

for
2
-blocks of maximal defect of quasi-simple groups (see Theorem

), allows us to prove a blockwise version of the main theorem of
[
MNT23
]
.

Theorem D
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
B
a
2
-block of maximal defect of
G
, and
b
its Brauer correspondent. Then we have
|\mathrm{IBr}(B)|\geq|\mathrm{IBr}(b)|
.

The paper is structured as follows. To start, we prove Theorem

in Section
2
. In Section

, we introduce a block-free version of Conjecture

(which is just the

), explain how to obtain an analogous reduction theorem and how Conjecture

for the blocks of maximal defect implies its block-free version. Section

is proved in Section

is checked for every

2
Alperin’s lower bound and proof of Theorem
C

We fix now and throughout the paper a prime number
p
. We use standard notation from character theory and the theory of
p
-blocks following
[
Isa76
]
(see also
[
Nav18
]
) and
[

]
.

Let
G
be a finite group,

a block of
G
with defect subgroup
D
, and

its Brauer correspondent in
{\bf N}_{G}(D)
. Alperin’s lower bound, introduced in
[

, Consequence 1]
as a consequence of Alperin’s weight conjecture, posits the inquality

. A block-free version of this bound has recently been studied in
[

]
. In particular, in
[
MNT23
, Theorem 2]
, the authors prove a reduction theorem of the block-free version of Alperin’s lower bound assuming a condition on finite simple groups called

(see
[
MNT23
, Section 2]
).

We now state a blockwise version of the Sylow-AWC condition which we restate using the language of isomorphisms of character triples. Throughout this paper we use the notion of central and block isomorphisms of character triples, denoted by
{\succeq_{c}}
and
{\succeq_{b}}
respectively, as defined in
[

, Section 3]
(see also
[
SV16
]
). Both

and
{\succeq_{b}}
are order relations on modular character triples that were inspired by their ordinary analogues first introduced in
[
NS14
]
. We refer the reader to the survey
[
Spä17
]
for further details on the theory of isomorphisms of character triples.

Conjecture 2.1
.

Let
G\unlhd A
be finite groups and consider a block
B
of
G
with defect group

. There is an
{\bf N}_{A}(D)
-invariant subgroup
{\bf N}_{G}(D)\leq M\leq G
, with

whenever

is not normal in

in

{\bf N}_{A}(D)_{B}
-invariant injection

\Omega:\mathrm{IBr}(b)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{IBr}(B)

such that

\left(A_{\chi},G,\chi\right){\succeq_{b}}\left(M{\bf N}_{A}(D)_{\vartheta},M,\vartheta\right)

for every
\vartheta\in\operatorname{IBr}(b)
and
\chi=\Omega(\theta)

Before proceeding further we make two remarks about the above condition.

Remark 2.2
.

(i)

First of all, notice that if we replace the isomorphism
{\succeq_{b}}
with its central version
{\succeq_{c}}
and ask for an injection
\Omega:\operatorname{IBr}({\bf N}_{G}(P))\hookrightarrow\operatorname{IBr}(G)
, with

a Sylow

-subgroup of

, then we obtain an equivalent formulation of the Sylow-AWC condition considered in
[
MNT23
]
;

(ii)

Next, as remarked in
[
MNT23
, Section 2]
for the Sylow-AWC, we observe that Conjecture

is implied by the inductive condition for the blockwise Alperin weight conjecture. To see this, consider the formulation of the latter condition given in
[

, Conjecture 4.1]

is a defect group of
B
and
\psi
is an irreducible Brauer character of the Brauer correspondent

of

, then we notice that

is a
p

is the bijection given by
[

(denoted by

in that paper), then we deduce from the properties of block isomorphisms of modular character triples that the preimage, say

. We then obtain a bijection with the properties required in Conjecture

by setting

.

Our aim is now to prove a reduction theorem for Conjecture
2.1
to finite quasi-simple groups.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let

be a finite group,

a prime number, and suppose that Conjecture
2.1
holds at the prime

We prove Theorem

fails to hold for a choice of finite groups

of

.

Remark 2.4

If

4
Normal Sylow subgroups

The aim of this section is to prove Theorem
A
, which we restate for the reader’s convenience.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that Conjecture
2.1
holds for every maximal defect block of every covering group of every non-abelian finite simple group of order divisible by
p
involved in a finite group
G
and let
P
be a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. Then
|\mathrm{IBr}(G)|=|\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{G}(P))|
if and only if
P\unlhd G
.

We divide the proof of Theorem
A
in a series of results. Notice that in every step, except in Theorem
4.6
, we assume Conjecture
2.12
. Thanks to Lemma
2.14
, the latter assumption is ensured as long as Conjecture
2.1
holds for all blocks of maximal defect. Furthermore, observe that the assumption of Conjecture
2.1
in Theorem
4.6
is necessary to apply Theorem
3.2
in the last step of the proof.

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that Conjecture
2.12
is satisfied for
G:=N
and
A:=G
and let
Q
be a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
N
. Then there is an
{\bf N}_{G}(Q)
-equivariant injection

\Omega:\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{N}(Q))\hookrightarrow\mathrm{IBr}(N)

such that for all
\varphi\in\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{N}(Q))
and all subgroups
N\leq M\leq G
we have

|\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{M}(Q)\mid\varphi)|=|\mathrm{IBr}(M\mid\Omega(\varphi)|.

Proof.

By hypothesis we have an
{\bf N}_{G}(Q)
-equivariant injection

\Omega:\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{N}(Q))\hookrightarrow\mathrm{IBr}(N).

It follows from the definition of the order relation
{\succeq_{c}}
that the modular character triples

({\bf N}_{M}(Q)_{\varphi},{\bf N}_{N}(Q),\varphi)
and
(M_{\Omega(\varphi)},N,\Omega(\varphi))
are isomorphic. This implies that

|\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{M}(Q)_{\varphi}\mid\varphi)|=|\mathrm{IBr}(M_{\Omega(\varphi)}\mid\Omega(\varphi))|

and the result follows by applying the Clifford correspondence.
∎

The next result will allow us to
go down
to a normal subgroup
N\unlhd G
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that Conjecture
2.12
is satisfied for
G:=N
and
A:=G
and let
Q
be a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
N
. Assume furthermore that
|\mathrm{IBr}(G)|=|\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{G}(Q))|
. Then
|\mathrm{IBr}(N)|=|\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{N}(Q))|
.

Proof.

By the Frattini argument we have
N{\bf N}_{G}(Q)=G
. Let

\Omega:\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{N}(Q))\hookrightarrow\mathrm{IBr}(N)

be the injection from Proposition
4.2
. Let
\Delta\subseteq\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{N}(Q))
be an
{\bf N}_{G}(Q)
-transversal, and notice that
\Omega(\Delta)
is a transversal in the image
\Xi:=\Omega(\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{N}(Q)))\subseteq\mathrm{IBr}(N)
. We work to show that
\Xi=\mathrm{IBr}(N)
. We have that

|\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{G}(Q))|=\sum_{\psi\in\Delta}|\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{G}(Q)\mid\psi)|

and since
|\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{G}(Q)\mid\psi)|=|\mathrm{IBr}(G\mid\Omega(\psi))|
we have

|\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{G}(Q))|=\sum_{\psi\in\Delta}|\mathrm{IBr}(G\mid\Omega(\psi))|\leq|\mathrm{IBr}(G)|=|\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{G}(Q))|

so we conclude that
\sum_{\psi\in\Delta}|\mathrm{IBr}(G\mid\Omega(\psi))|=|\mathrm{IBr}(G)|
. In other words, every element of
\mathrm{IBr}(G)
lies over an element in
\Omega(\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{N}(Q)))
. Since
\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{N}(Q))
is closed under
{\bf N}_{G}(Q)
-conjugation, its image
\Xi
is
{\bf N}_{G}(Q)
-stable (and therefore
G
-stable by Frattini). Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{IBr}(N)
and let
\chi\in\mathrm{IBr}(G\mid\varphi)
. We have proved that
\chi
lies over an element of
\Xi
, and by Clifford’s theorem this element must be
G
-conjugate to
\varphi
. It follows that
\varphi\in\Xi
and we are done.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
N
be a normal subgroup of
G
,
P
a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
, and
\varphi,\psi\in\mathrm{Irr}(N)
be
G
-conjugate characters of
N
that are both
P
-invariant. Then
\varphi
and
\psi
are
{\bf N}_{G}(P)
-conjugate.

Proof.

Let

be such that
\psi=\varphi^{x}
. Since
G_{\psi}=G_{\varphi}^{x}
we have that
G_{\psi}
contains
P
and
P^{x}
. This implies that there is some

with

. Now
\varphi^{xg}=\psi^{g}=\psi
and

, as desired.
∎

The idea of the next proof was given to us by G. Navarro. In its proof, we use
[
MNT23
, Theorem 2]
, which assumes the so-called
inductive Sylow-AWC condition
. Notice that the latter is equivalent to our Conjecture

Proposition 4.5

(Navarro)
.

Let

with

a
p^{\prime}
-group and let

2.12

|\mathrm{IBr}(G)|=|\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{G}(P))|

Proof.

By assumption, Conjecture

[
MNT23
]
) holds at the prime

for every covering group of any non-abelian finite simple group of order divisible by

[

be a

We have that

\displaystyle|\mathrm{IBr}(G)|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\varphi\in\Delta_{P}}|\mathrm{IBr}(G\mid\varphi)|\geq\sum_{\varphi\in\Delta_{P}}|\mathrm{IBr}(N{\bf N}_{G}(P)\mid\varphi)|.

(4.1)

Now, if

of

by the Clifford correspondence and

, using that

is

∎

The following is the nontrivial direction of Theorem
A
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Assume that Conjecture
2.1
holds for every block of maximal defect of every covering group of every non-abelian finite simple group of order divisible by
p
involved in
G
and let
P
be a Sylow
p
-subgroup of

|\mathrm{IBr}(G)|=|\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{G}(P))|

.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.3
and Remark

we have that Conjecture
2.1
holds for every block of maximal defect of every subgroup of

. Therefore, Lemma
2.14
implies that Conjecture
2.12

We argue by induction on
|G|
. By
[

we may assume

is not
p
-solvable and that
{\bf O}_{p}(G)=1
. Let

and let

be a Sylow
p
-subgroup of

. We work to prove

so we assume otherwise. We claim that in this case
|\mathrm{IBr}(G)|=|\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{G}(Q))|
.

We have
{\bf N}_{G}(P)\leq{\bf N}_{G}(Q)<G
and

by Frattini. By Lemma
2.14
, our hypothesis implies that
2.12
holds at the prime
p
for every covering group of any non-abelian finite simple group of order divisible by
p
involved in
G
. Since Conjecture

is exactly the Sylow-AWC condition from
[
MNT23
]
, we can apply
[
MNT23
, Theorem 2]
and conclude that
|\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{G}(Q))|\geq|\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{G}(P))|=|\mathrm{IBr}(G)|
. By Proposition
4.2
there is an
{\bf N}_{G}(Q)
-equivariant injection

\Omega:\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{N}(Q))\hookrightarrow\mathrm{IBr}(N)

with

|\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{G}(Q)\mid\varphi)|=|\mathrm{IBr}(G\mid\Omega(\varphi)|

for all

. Let
\Delta\subseteq\mathrm{IBr}({\bf N}_{N}(Q))
be an
{\bf N}_{G}(Q)
-transversal. We have that

and since
\Omega(\Delta)

is not divisible by
p

of

If

is simple non-abelian of order divisible by

Lemma 5.2

[
Fen19
, Lemma 7.2]
also applies to establish the condition (ii.d) of Proposition
5.1
, and thus we complete the proof.
∎

We are now able to complete the verification of Conjecture
2.1
for
2
-blocks of maximal defect of finite quasi-simple groups.

Theorem 5.5
.

Conjecture
2.1
holds for all
2
-blocks of maximal defect of every covering group of any non-abelian finite simple group.

Proof.

Let
S
be a non-abelian finite simple group, and
G
be a covering group of
S
.
By
[
Mur98
, Prop. 2.4]
and
[
MRR
, Lemma 3.14 and 3.15]
, it suffices to consider the case that
G
is the universal
2^{\prime}
-covering group of
S
.

We first consider the sporadic simple groups. Thanks to Lemma
5.2
, Lemma
5.3
,
[
GLS98
, Table 5.3]
and
[
Kon05
, Cor.]
, it remains to deal with
J_{1}
,
J_{2}
,
J_{3}
,
Suz
and
HN
.
These groups have been checked to satisfy the inductive BAW condition in
[
Bre
]
.

The inductive BAW condition was verified in
[
Mal14
]
to hold for all simple alternating groups.
Thus we are left to deal with the simple groups
S
of Lie type.
Also the defining characteristic case was settled in
[
Spä13
, Thm. C]
, whence it suffices to consider groups of Lie type in odd characteristic.

Thanks to Proposition
5.4
, we assume that
S
is not of type
\mathsf{E}_{6}
or
{}^{2}\mathsf{E}_{6}
.
By
[
FLZ21
,
FLZ23
]
, simple groups
\textup{PSL}_{n}(q)
and
\textup{PSU}_{n}(q)
satisfy the inductive BAW condition.
For the group
G=3.\mathsf{G}_{2}(3)
or
3.\Omega_{7}(3)
we are in the case dealt with in Lemma
5.3
.
Thus all exceptional covering groups (see, e.g.,
[
GLS98
, Table 6.1.3]
) are settled.
By
[
Kon05
, Cor.]
and Lemma
5.2
, it remains to consider the following two cases:
S={}^{2}\mathsf{G}_{2}(q^{2})
or
\textup{PSp}_{2n}(q)
, which have been checked to satisfy the inductive BAW condition by
[
Spä13
, Prop. 6.4]
and
[
FM22
, Thm. 1]
respectively.
∎

Proofs of Theorem
B
and Theorem
D

Using the reductions contained in Theorem
2.3
and Theorem
4.6
, together with the verification of Conjecture
2.1
for

-blocks of maximal defect of finite quasi-simple groups obtained in Theorem

, we are finally able to prove Theorem
B
and Theorem
D
.

Proof of Theorem
B
.

Let
P
be a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G
. Clearly, if
P
is normal in
G
then we have
|\operatorname{IBr}(G)|=|\operatorname{IBr}({\bf N}_{G}(P))|
. Suppose now that
|\operatorname{IBr}(G)|=|\operatorname{IBr}({\bf N}_{G}(P))|
. By Theorem
4.6
it suffices to show that Conjecture
2.1
holds for all blocks of maximal defect of all quasi-simple groups with respect to the prime
2
. This is done in Theorem
5.5
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

By Theorem
C
and Remark
2.11
it suffices to prove Conjecture
2.1
for the
2
-blocks of maximal defect of the covering groups of the nonabelian finite simple groups, but this is done in Theorem
5.5
.

Monge-Ampère equations with prescribed singularities on compact Hermitian manifolds
Omar Alehyane, Chinh H. Lu, Mohammed Salouf

Abstract
. Given a compact complex manifold X X , we study the existence and the uniqueness of weak solutions to degenerate Monge-Ampère equations on X X with prescribed singularities when the reference form is semipositive and big, while the right hand side is a non-pluripolar positive Radon measure. This generalizes our previous work to more general hermitian manifolds and also to the case of solutions with prescribed singularities.

1.
Introduction

The problem of solving Monge-Ampère equations (MAE) on complex manifolds has gained an increasing interest over the last few years due to numerous applications in differential geometry, algebraic geometry and theoretical physics.

In Kähler settings, first solutions to these equations were established by Yau
[
Yau78
]
and Aubin
[
Aub78
]
with the aim of constructing canonical metrics as described in
[
Cal57
]
. Bedford-Taylor
[
BT76
]
showed that (MAE) can be defined on all bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic functions, motivating the study of the existence of weak solutions. By adapting a construction that goes back to Bedford-Taylor
[
BT87
]
, Guedj-Zeriahi
[
GZ07
]
succeeded in defining the Monge-Ampère operator on a class of unbounded quasi-plurisubharmonic functions and then established unbounded weak solutions to (MAE) with a general right hand side being a non-pluripolar Radon measure. By this time, (MAE) have continued to make a significant contribution in the development of Kähler geometry in connection with the search of canonical metrics and models (e.g.
[
EGZ09
,
BCHM10
,
DP10
,
BBGZ13
,
BG14
,
BB17
,
BBE+19
,
DR17
,
BDL17
,
BDL20
,
CC21a
,
CC21b
]
). In particular, by solving degenerate (MAE), Darvas, Di Nezza and the second author
[
DDNL18a
,
DDNL21
,
DDNL25
]
succeeded in constructing Kähler-Einstein metrics with prescribed singularities on singular Kähler varieties (we refer the reader to
[
DDNL25
]
and the references therein for a comprehensive survey of this topic and for numerous applications).

Studying (MAE) on non-Kähler manifolds has always been a challenging issue. Cherrier
[
Che87
]
has initiated the problem of extending the results of Aubin and Yau to non-Kähler settings. He succeeded only in a particular case by assuming a restrictive curvature condition. Similar partial results have been given in
[
Han96
,
GL10
]
before that Tossati-Weinkove solve the Monge-Ampère equation in full generality
[
TW10
]
. Thereafter, many authors were interested in generalizing Tossati-Weinkove’s result to the study of bounded solutions
[
DK12
,
KN15
,
Ngu16a
,
GL22
,
GL23
,
KN23
,
BGL25
]
when the right hand side is a measure having an
L^{p}
-density with respect to the Lebesgue measure.

Our first main result establishes bounded weak solutions to (MAE) for measures dominated by the Monge-Ampère measure of bounded potentials.

Theorem A

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifolds of dimension
n
, and let
\theta
be a smooth
(1,1)
-form on
X
which is semipositive and big. Assume
\mu
is a positive Radon measure on
X
satisfying
\mu\leq(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}
with
\psi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
. Then, for every positive constant
\lambda
, we can find a unique function
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
solving the equation

(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}=e^{\lambda u}\mu.

When
X
is Kähler and
\theta
is closed, it is known by
[
GZ17a
, Corollary 11.9]
that the equation in the last theorem admits a unique solution in the Guedj-Zeriahi class which is then bounded by the domination principle
[
GZ17a
, Proposition 10.11]
(see also Corollary
3.13
). Generalizing this result to the case
\lambda=0
will provide a positive answer to a question of Kołodziej from Kähler settings
[
DGZ16
, Question 15]
. The novelty here is the existence of the solution in a general complex manifold.

The main goal of this paper is to study the existence of unbounded solutions to (MAE) on complex non-Kähler manifolds. In a previous work
[
ALS24
]
, we considered the case of a complex compact manifold
X
which satisfies a curvature condition introduced by Guan-Li. Then, giving any real smooth
(1,1)
-form
\theta
on
X
which is semipositive and big, we generalized the definition of the full mass class of Guedj-Zeriahi to this settings, we proved that the non-pluripolar Monge-Ampère measure is well defined on this class, that it is continuous with respect to convergence in capacity, and we solved the associated Monge-Ampère equation.

Our objective here is to push forward the techniques developed in
[
ALS24
]
to a more general complex manifold where we also seek for solutions with prescribed singularities in the terminology of
[
DDNL18a
]
. We then let
(X,\omega)
denote a compact hermitian manifold of dimension
n
, and we fix a smooth
(1,1)
-form
\theta
on
X
which is semipositive and big. As it is well known, a major difficulty of the problem lies in the fact that
\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}
depends on the chosen function
\varphi
. Guedj-Lu
[
GL22
]
defined the quantities

v_{+}(\theta)=\sup\left\{\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}:\;\varphi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)\right\},

and

v_{-}(\theta)=\inf\left\{\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}:\;\varphi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)\right\}.

It is an interesting open problem to see whether
v_{-}(\theta)>0
and/or
v_{+}(\theta)<+\infty
. By Stokes’ theorem, these conditions are always satisfied when
\theta
is Kähler, or more generally Guan-Li. Corollary 2.4 in
[
ALS24
]
shows that it holds true as soon as the manifold admits a hermitian metric
\omega
satisfying
v_{+}(\omega)<+\infty
and
v_{-}(\omega)>0
, which covers the case of compact complex manifolds of dimension
n\leq 2
. At the time of this writing, we are not aware of any example of a compact complex manifold where any of these two conditions does not hold. We refer to
[
GL22
,
AGL23
,
BGL25
]
for more information.

Let
\phi
be a fixed
\theta
-model potential (see Definition
2.13
). Our second main result establishes weak solutions to degenerate Monge-Ampère equations with the same singularity type as
\phi
in a general complex manifold, generalizing
[
DDNL21
, Theorem A]
which deals with the case
\theta
is closed and
\omega
is Kähler.

Theorem B

(Theorems
5.4
and
5.7
)
.

Assume
\mu=f\omega^{n}
where
0\leq f\in L^{p}(\omega^{n})
is such that
p>1
and
\mu(X)>0
. Then,

•

for every
\lambda>0
, there is a uniquely determined
\varphi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
such that
\varphi\simeq\phi
and

(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=e^{\lambda\varphi}\mu;

•

there is a unique constant
c>0
and a function
\varphi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
such that
\varphi\simeq\phi
and

(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=c\mu.

Here the notation
\varphi\simeq\phi
means
\phi-C\leq\varphi\leq\phi+C
for some constant
C\geq 0
; we say that
\varphi
has the same singularity type as
\phi
. The function
\phi
is not bounded (unless it vanishes identically), hence so is
\varphi
, the term
(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}
refers to the non-pluripolar Monge-Ampère measure defined in
[
GZ07
]
.

To prove the last theorem, in the case
\theta
is closed, Darvas-DiNezza-Lu
[
DDNL21
]
proposed a pluripotential method which is based on a generalization of Kołodziej’s
L^{\infty}
estimate
[
Koł98
]
. This method involves minimizing the Monge-Ampère volume of potentials that are less singular than
\phi
: the function that achieves this minimum has a Monge-Ampère volume equal to the one of
\phi
and it is less singular than
\phi
, hence it is the solution we seek. This method fails in our context since the total mass of the non-pluripolar Monge-Ampère measures of potentials with the same singularity type as
\phi
is not constant. We could attempt to prove this theorem using the variational method in the spirit of
[
DDNL18a
,
Tru23
,
DDNL25
]
, however this seems unclear due to the difficulty of establishing a primitive of the operator
(\theta+dd^{c}.)^{n}
.

To overcome these difficulties, we consider a Perron type envelope of subsolutions
\varphi_{j}
for which
\varphi_{j}\simeq\phi
and the non-pluripolar Monge-Ampère measure of
\varphi_{j}
is dominated by
\mu
. Then using Theorem A, we prove a subsolution type theorem for potentials with the same singularity type as
\phi
(Theorem
5.2
). We also establish a general relative uniform estimate of
\varphi_{j}
(Lemma
5.5
). Based on these two results, we prove that the total mass of the non-pluripolar Monge-Ampère measure of
\varphi_{j}
converges to
\mu(X)
and that
(\varphi_{j})
has a subsequence converging in capacity to the solution in the last theorem. The uniqueness of the solutions in the last theorem will follow from the domination principle (Theorem
3.11
), which is a generalization of
[
GL22
, Proposition 2.8]
to the case of unbounded functions. Notice that the uniqueness of the solution
\varphi
in the second part of the theorem remains largely open (we refer to
[
KN19
]
for a partial answer).

After proving the last theorem, a natural question to ask is whether there are solutions to degenerate (MAE) with right-hand side being a positive non-pluripolar Radon measure. As evidenced in
[
GZ07
,
Din09
,
DDNL18a
]
, solutions to these equations belong to a special classes of quasi-psh functions. We thus let
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
denote a generalization of the relative mass class defined in
[
DDNL18a
]
and further studied in
[
DDNL21
,
DV25
,
Tru23
,
DDNL25
]
.

The following result gives a characterization of the range of the Monge-Ampère operator on the relative full mass class.

Theorem C

(Theorem
5.8
and Corollary
5.9
)
.

Assume
v_{-}(\theta)>0
. Let
\mu
be a positive Radon measure vanishing on pluripolar sets. Then,

•

for every
\lambda>0
, there is a uniquely determined
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
such that

(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=e^{\lambda\varphi}\mu;

•

there is a unique constant
c>0
and a function
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
such that

(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=c\mu.

To prove the last theorem, we follow Cegrell’s ideas from the local setting
[
Ceg98
,
Ceg04
]
. This method is based on approximating the measure
\mu
by appropriate Monge-Ampère measures with potentials
\varphi_{j}\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
. This is possible thanks to Theorem B.

By construction, the sequence
\varphi_{j}
satisfies one of the following conditions:

(1)

(\theta+dd^{c}{\varphi_{j}})^{n}\leq Ae^{\lambda\varphi_{j}}(\omega+dd^{c}{\psi_{j}})^{n}
, where
\lambda
,
A
are positive constants and
\psi_{j}
is a uniformly bounded sequence of
\omega
-psh functions converging in capacity to a function
\psi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
;

(2)

(\theta+dd^{c}{\varphi_{j}})^{n}\leq\nu
is dominated by a non-pluripolar Radon measure
\nu
.

Applying the Hartogs’ lemma and extracting, one can assume
\varphi_{j}
converges in
L^{1}(X)
to a
\theta
-psh function
\varphi
. We then face the challenge of proving that
\varphi
is the solution in the last theorem. This will be done by proving the continuity of the non-pluripolar Monge-Ampère measure along the sequence
\varphi_{j}
. In general, this is not always possible for any sequence converging in
L^{1}(X)
as showed by Cegrell
[
GZ17a
, Example 3.25]
. Some related results have been given in
[
Xin09
,
DH12
,
BBE+19
,
GLZ19
,
DV25
]
when
\theta
is closed and
X
is Kähler. In a previous work
[
ALS24
]
, by assuming that
X
admits a Guan-Li metric and that
\phi=0
, we were able to prove that the convergence
\varphi_{j}\rightarrow\varphi
holds in capacity and that the non-pluripolar Monge-Ampère measure is continuous with respect to this type of convergence.

Here we propose the following convergence criteria.

Theorem D

(Theorem
4.1
)
.

Assume
\varphi_{j}\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
satisfies (1) or (2) and converges in
L^{1}(X)
to a
\theta
-psh function
\varphi
. If
P_{\theta}(\inf_{j}\varphi_{j})\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
then
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
,
\varphi_{j}\rightarrow\varphi
in capacity and
(\theta+dd^{c}{\varphi_{j}})^{n}\rightarrow(\theta+dd^{c}{\varphi})^{n}
weakly.

We will show in Lemma
4.2
that the condition
P_{\theta}(\inf_{j}\varphi_{j})\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
can be assured by
v_{-}(\theta)>0
.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we define the non-pluripolar Monge-Ampère measure of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions, and we give a sufficient condition to ensure that it is continuous with respect to potentials converging in capacity; we also study the Monge-Ampère volume of
\theta
-plurisubharmonic functions. In Section
3
, we define and study the relative full mass class and we establish the domination principle (Theorems
3.11
and
3.14
). Section
4
is devoted to the proof of Theorem D. Theorems A, B and C are proven in Section

5
.

2.
Non-pluripolar Monge-Ampère measures

Throughout the paper, we let
(X,\omega)
denote a compact hermitian manifold of dimension
n
and we fix a smooth
(1,1)
-form
\theta
on
X
which is semipositive and big. Up to rescaling, there is no loss of generality in assuming
\theta\leq\omega
.

2.1.
Quasi-plurisubharmonic functions

We say that a function
u:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is quasi-plurisubharmonic, quasi-psh for short, if it is locally given by the sum of a smooth and a plurisubharmonic function. We denote by
{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
the set of quasi-psh functions
u\in L^{1}(X)
satisfying the condition
\theta+dd^{c}u\geq 0
in the sense of currents. Functions in
{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
are called
\theta
-psh functions. If
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
then
\theta_{u}^{n}:=(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}
will refer to the non-pluripolar Monge-Ampère measure of
u
. It is defined by the strong increasing limit

\theta_{u}^{n}=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\textit{1}_{\{u>-t\}}(\theta+dd^{c}\max(u,-t))^{n},

see
[
GZ07
,
ALS24
]
. We recall that the measures
(\theta+dd^{c}\max(u,-t))^{n}
are defined by the Bedford-Taylor’s wedge product
[
BT76
]
. Observe that the condition

v_{+}(\theta)<+\infty
guarantees that
\theta_{u}^{n}
is a Radon measure on
X
. Here we do not make use of the hypothesis
v_{+}(\theta)<+\infty
and therefore we can not confirm that
\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}<+\infty
. However, Lemma
5.3
shows that
e^{u}\theta_{u}^{n}
is always a Radon measure on
X
.

2.1.1.
Maximum principle and quasi-psh envelopes

Combining Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.2 in
[
ALS24
]
, we can state the following fundamental result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
u
,
v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. We have

\textit{1}_{\{u>v\}}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}=\textit{1}_{\{u>v\}}(\theta+dd^{c}\max(u,v))^{n}.

If in addition
u\leq v
, then

\textit{1}_{\{u=v\}}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}\leq\textit{1}_{\{u=v\}}(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}.

Given any measurable function
h
on
X
with real values, we recall that the
\theta
-psh envelope of
h
is defined by

P_{\theta}(h)=\left(\sup\{\varphi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta):\varphi\leq h\}\right)^{*},

with the convention
\sup\emptyset=-\infty
. When
h
is given by
\min(u,v)
for some
u
,
v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
, we will use the abbreviation
P_{\theta}(u,v):=P_{\theta}(\min(u,v))
.

The following result will be used multiple times in the sequel.

Theorem 2.2

(Theorem 2.3 in
[
ALS24
]
)
.

Assume
h
is quasi-continuous on
X
and
P_{\theta}(h)\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. Then
P_{\theta}(h)\leq h
outside a pluripolar set and

\int_{\{P_{\theta}(h)<h\}}(\theta+dd^{c}P_{\theta}(h))^{n}=0.

In the case where
h
is given by the minimum of two
\theta
-psh functions, we have the following observation.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
u
,
v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. If
P_{\theta}(u,v)\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
, then

(\theta+dd^{c}P_{\theta}(u,v))^{n}\leq\textit{1}_{\{P_{\theta}(u,v)=u<v\}}\theta_{u}^{n}+\textit{1}_{\{P_{\theta}(u,v)=v\}}\theta_{v}^{n}.

Proof.

Set
\varphi=P_{\theta}(u,v)
. By the last theorem, we know that the measure
\theta_{\varphi}^{n}
is carried by the set

\{\varphi=\min(u,v)\}=\{\varphi=u<v\}\cup\{\varphi=v\}.

Since
\varphi\leq\min(u,v)
, we get by Theorem
2.1
that

\theta_{\varphi}^{n}\leq\textit{1}_{\{\varphi=u<v\}}\theta_{\varphi}^{n}+\textit{1}_{\{\varphi=v\}}\theta_{\varphi}^{n}\leq\textit{1}_{\{\varphi=u<v\}}\theta_{u}^{n}+\textit{1}_{\{\varphi=v\}}\theta_{v}^{n}.

∎

We will also need the following consequence of Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Fix
b\geq 1
. Assume
u
,
v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
are such that
\varphi_{b}:=P_{\theta}(bu-(b-1)v)
is in
{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. Then, we have

(i)

b^{-1}\varphi_{b}+(1-b^{-1})v\leq u
on
X
;

(ii)

\theta_{\varphi_{b}}^{n}
is carried by
D_{b}:=\{b^{-1}\varphi_{b}+(1-b^{-1})v=u\}
;

(iii)

b^{-n}\theta_{\varphi_{b}}^{n}+\textit{1}_{D_{b}}(1-b^{-1})^{n}\theta_{v}^{n}\leq\textit{1}_{D_{b}}\theta_{u}^{n}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.2
, we know that
\theta_{\varphi_{b}}^{n}
is carried by
D_{b}
and that
b^{-1}\varphi_{b}+(1-b^{-1})v\leq u
a.e. on
X
. Since these functions are quasi-psh, we obtain
b^{-1}\varphi_{b}+(1-b^{-1})v\leq u
everywhere on
X
. Thus, we only need to prove (iii).

According to Theorem
2.1
, we have

\textit{1}_{D_{b}}(\theta+dd^{c}({b^{-1}\varphi_{b}+(1-b^{-1})v}))^{n}\leq\textit{1}_{D_{b}}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}.

The result then follows by the Newton binomial formula and (ii).
∎

We finish this paragraph with the following well-known lemmas.

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume
u
and
v
are quasi-psh functions on
X
. Then
P_{\theta}(u-v)
is bounded from above and
P_{\theta}(u)-\sup_{X}v\leq P_{\theta}(u-v)
.

Proof.

Observe that the inequality
P_{\theta}(u)-\sup_{X}v\leq P_{\theta}(u-v)
is obvious.

Without loss of generality we can assume
\sup_{X}v=0
.
Using this we see that the set
D:=\{v>-1\}
is non-pluripolar because
v
is quasi-psh. It then follows from
[
GZ17a
, Theorem 9.17]
and
[
Vu19
]
that the global extremal function of
D
with respect to
\omega

V_{D,\omega}:=\sup\{h\in{\rm PSH}(X,\omega)\;:\;h\leq 0\;\text{on }D\}

is bounded from above by some positive constant
A
. For
C=\sup_{X}u+1
, we have
u-v\leq C
on
D
, hence
P_{\theta}(u-v)\leq V_{D,\varepsilon\omega}+C\leq A+C
is bounded from above.
The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mu
be a positive Radon measure on
X
that vanishes on pluripolar sets. Assume
(u_{j})\subset{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
is a uniformly bounded sequence that converges in
L^{1}(X)
to a function
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. Then
u_{j}\rightarrow u
in
L^{1}(\mu)
.

Proof.

Using
[
GZ17a
, Lemma 11.5]
, the proof goes exactly as in the one of
[
KN22
, Lemma 2.3]
.
∎

2.1.2.
Convergence in capacity

Recall that the capacity of a Borel set
D\subset X
, with respect to
\omega
, is defined by

{\rm Cap}_{\omega}(D):=\sup\left\{\int_{D}(\omega+dd^{c}h)^{n}:h\in{\rm PSH}(X,\omega),0\leq h\leq 1\right\}.

A sequence of measurable functions
(h_{j})
is said to be convergent in capacity to a measurable function
h
if

\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}{\rm Cap}_{\omega}(\{|h_{j}-h|>\delta\})=0,

for every
\delta>0
.

It is well known, by an example of Cegrell
[
GZ17a
, Example 3.25]
, that the Monge-Ampère operator is not continuous with respect to uniformly bounded sequences converging in
L^{1}(X)
. Building on
[
DDNL25
, Theorem 2.6]
, we give a sufficient condition to ensure the continuity of the non-pluripolar Monge-Ampère measure with respect to potentials converging in capacity.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
u_{j}
,
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
be such that
\sup_{j}\int_{X}\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}<+\infty
and
u_{j}\rightarrow u
in capacity. Let
h_{j}
be a uniformly bounded sequence of positive and quasi-continuous functions that converge to a quasi-continuous function
h
in capacity. If the function
f:=P_{\theta}(\inf u_{j})
belongs to
{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
and

\int_{\{f\leq-c\}}\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}\rightarrow 0,\;\;\text{as}\;c\rightarrow+\infty

uniformly with respect to
j
,
then
h_{j}\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}\rightarrow h\theta_{u}^{n}
in the weak sense of measures.

Proof.

By
[
DDNL25
, Theorem 2.6]
, we have
\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}<+\infty
.
Again by
[
DDNL25
, Theorem 2.6]
, it suffices to prove

(2.1)

\limsup_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}\leq\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}.

Fix
\varepsilon
,
c>0
and consider

f_{\varepsilon}^{c}=\frac{\max(f+c,0)}{\max(f+c,0)+\varepsilon}.

The function
f_{\varepsilon}^{c}
is positive, bounded and quasi-continuous. Moreover, Theorem
2.1
implies

f_{\varepsilon}^{c}\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}=f_{\varepsilon}^{c}\theta_{\max(u_{j},-c)}^{n}\;\;\text{and}\;\;f_{\varepsilon}^{c}\theta_{u}^{n}=f_{\varepsilon}^{c}\theta_{\max(u,-c)}^{n}.

It then follows from
[
GZ17a
, Theorem 4.26]
that

\int_{X}f_{\varepsilon}^{c}\theta_{u}^{n}=\int_{X}f_{\varepsilon}^{c}\theta_{\max(u,-c)}^{n}=\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}f_{\varepsilon}^{c}\theta_{\max(u_{j},-c)}^{n}=\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}f_{\varepsilon}^{c}\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}.

Therefore,

\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}\geq\limsup_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\{f>-c+1\}}f_{\varepsilon}^{c}\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}.

Using the fact that the map
x\mapsto\frac{x}{x+\varepsilon}
is increasing on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we get
f_{\varepsilon}^{c}\geq\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}
on
\{f>-c+1\}
, hence

\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}\geq\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}\limsup_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\{f>-c+1\}}\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}.

By the hypothesis, letting
c\rightarrow+\infty
and then
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
yields (
2.1
), which proves the lemma.
∎

2.2.
Bounds on Monge-Ampère volumes

Let
u
,
v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. We say that
u
is less singular than
v
, and we write
v\preceq u
, if there is
C>0
such that
v\leq u+C
(we also say that
v
is more singular than
u
).

We define an equivalence relation on
{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
by requiring that
u\simeq v
iff
u\preceq v
and
v\preceq u
. Fix
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. The equivalence class of
u
is the set of
\theta
-psh functions having the same singularity type as
u
, i.e.

[u]=\{v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta):v\simeq u\}.

In the sequel, we will need to bound the Monge-Ampère volume of functions in
[u]
. We then consider the quantities

v_{-,u}(\theta)=\inf\left\{\int_{X}\theta_{v}^{n}:v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta),v\simeq u\right\},

and

v_{+,u}(\theta)=\sup\left\{\int_{X}\theta_{v}^{n}:v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta),v\simeq u\right\}.

Let us list some basic properties of these Monge-Ampère volumes.

•

We have
\int_{X}\theta_{v}^{n}\leq v_{+}(\theta)
for every
v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
, hence
v_{+,u}(\theta)\leq v_{+}(\theta)
.

•

If
u
is bounded then
v_{-,u}(\theta)=v_{-}(\theta)
and
v_{+,u}(\theta)=v_{+}(\theta)
.

•

When
\theta
is closed, we have
v_{-,u}(\theta)=v_{+,u}(\theta)=\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}
by
[
WN19a
, Theorem 1.2]
, hence
v_{+,u}(\theta)
is always finite and
v_{-,u}(\theta)>0
provided that
\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}>0
.

The following result corresponds to
[
BGL25
, Proposition 3.7]
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\theta_{1}\leq\theta_{2}
be two smooth
(1,1)
-forms on
X
that are semipositive and big. If
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta_{1})
and
v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta_{2})
are such that
u\preceq v
, then
v_{-,u}(\theta_{1})\leq v_{-,v}(\theta_{2})
and
v_{+,u}(\theta_{1})\leq v_{+,v}(\theta_{2})
.

In the rest of this paragraph, we will be concerned only with the condition
v_{-,u}(\theta)>0
. We first prove the following.

Proposition 2.9
.

Assume
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
is such that
v_{+,u}(\theta)>v_{+}(\theta)-v_{-}(\theta)
. Then there is
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that
P_{\varepsilon\theta}(u)\not\equiv-\infty
.

Proof.

By hypothesis, we can find
b>1
and
v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
such that
v\simeq u
and
\int_{X}\theta_{v}^{n}>v_{+}(\theta)-v_{-}(\theta)/b^{n}
. We are going to prove that
P_{\theta}(bv)
belongs to
{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. It will then follow that
P_{\theta}(bu)\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
because
v\simeq u
, hence it suffices to take
\varepsilon=b^{-1}
.

For every
j
, we set
v_{j}=\max(v,-j)
and
\varphi_{j}=P_{\theta}(bv_{j})
. Observe that
\varphi_{j}
is a bounded
\theta
-psh function. According to Proposition
2.4
, we have

\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}=\textit{1}_{\{\varphi_{j}=bv_{j}\}}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}\leq b^{n}\textit{1}_{\{\varphi_{j}=bv_{j}\}}\theta_{v_{j}}^{n}.

Assume by contradiction that
\sup_{X}\varphi_{j}\rightarrow-\infty
. Fixing
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
X=\{\varphi_{j}\leq-bk\}
for
j
large enough. Hence

v_{-}(\theta)\leq\int_{\{\varphi_{j}\leq-bk\}}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}\leq b^{n}\int_{\{v_{j}\leq-k\}}\theta_{v_{j}}^{n}\leq b^{n}v_{+}(\theta)-b^{n}\int_{\{v>-k\}}\theta_{v}^{n}.

Letting
k\rightarrow+\infty
gives

v_{-}(\theta)\leq b^{n}v_{+}(\theta)-b^{n}\int_{X}\theta_{v}^{n},

a contradiction. It follows that
P_{\theta}(bv)=\lim v_{j}\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
, which finishes the proof.
∎

When
\theta
is closed, we have
v_{-}(\theta)=v_{+}(\theta)
, hence the conclusion of the last proposition holds as soon as
\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}>0
. The following proposition studies further this conclusion.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
be such that
P_{\varepsilon\theta}(u)\not\equiv-\infty
for some
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Then, there is
v\in{\rm PSH}(X,(1-\varepsilon)\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
such that

\int_{X}((1-\varepsilon)\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}\leq\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}.

In particular
v_{-,u}(\theta)\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{n}v_{-}(\theta)
.

Proof.

We set
\tilde{u}=P_{\varepsilon\theta}(u)
and
v=P_{(1-\varepsilon)\theta}(u-\tilde{u})
. Since
\tilde{u}\leq u
, we get by Lemma
2.5
that
v\in{\rm PSH}(X,(1-\varepsilon)\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
. By Theorem
2.2
, we know
that
v+\tilde{u}\leq u
everywhere on
X
and that
((1-\varepsilon)\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}
is carried by
\{v+\tilde{u}=u\}
. It then follows from Theorem
2.1
that

\int_{X}((1-\varepsilon)\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}\leq\int_{\{v+\tilde{u}=u\}}(\theta+dd^{c}(v+\tilde{u}))^{n}\leq\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}.

∎

Assuming
v_{-}(\theta)>0
and
v_{+}(\theta)<+\infty
, we give a sufficient and necessary condition to ensure the conclusion of Proposition
2.9
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Assume
v_{-}(\theta)>0
and
v_{+}(\theta)<+\infty
. For every
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
, the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

v_{-,u}(\theta)>0
.

(ii)

there is
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that
P_{\varepsilon\theta}(u)\not\equiv-\infty
.

Proof.

We start by the proof of (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii).
By contradiction, we assume that
P_{\theta}(bu)=-\infty
for every
b>1
. Fix
b>1
.
For
t>0
, we consider
u_{t}=\max(bu,u-t))
and
\phi_{t}=P_{\theta}(u_{t})
. Fix
C>0
. Since
\phi_{t}\searrow-\infty
by assumption, we have
X=\{\phi_{t}\leq-C\}
for
t
sufficiently large. It thus follows from Proposition
2.4
that

v_{-,u}(\theta)\leq\int_{\{\phi_{t}=u_{t}\leq-C\}}(\theta+dd^{c}\phi_{t})^{n}\leq\int_{\{u\leq-C/b\}}(b\theta+dd^{c}u_{t})^{n},

where we have used that
\{u_{t}\leq-C\}=\{u\leq-C/b\}
for every
t>0
large enough. Observe that
u_{t}=u+(b-1)\max(u,-t/(b-1))
. On one hand, we have by Theorem
2.1

\int_{\{-t/(b-1)<u\leq-C/b\}}(b\theta+dd^{c}u_{t})^{n}\leq b^{n}\int_{\{u\leq-C/b\}}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}.

On the other hand, by applying the Newton expansion formula and using
v_{+}(\theta)<+\infty
, we obtain

\int_{\{u\leq-t/(b-1)\}}(b\theta+dd^{c}u_{t})^{n}\leq\int_{\{u\leq-t/(b-1)\}}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}+O(b-1).

We thus infer

v_{-,u}(\theta)\leq b^{n}\int_{\{u\leq-C/b\}}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}+\int_{\{u\leq-t/(b-1)\}}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}+O(b-1).

Letting
t\rightarrow+\infty
,
C\rightarrow+\infty
and then
b\rightarrow 1
, we get
v_{-,u}(\theta)=0
, a contradiction. This proves
P_{\theta}(bu)\not\equiv-\infty
for some
b>1
, hence it suffices to take
\varepsilon=b^{-1}
.

The converse follows from the last proposition since
v_{-}(\theta)>0
.
∎

2.3.
Model potentials

Fix
\varphi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. If
\phi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
, then the envelope of singularity types of
\phi
with respect to
\varphi
is defined by

P_{\theta}[\phi](\varphi)=\left(\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}P_{\theta}(\phi+t,\varphi)\right)^{*},

see
[
RWN14
]
.
When
\varphi=0
, we simply set
P_{\theta}[\phi]:=P_{\theta}[\phi](0)
. It is easy to see that
P_{\theta}[\phi]\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
and
\phi\preceq P_{\theta}[\phi]
. In the case
P_{\theta}[\phi]\simeq\phi
, we say that
\phi
has model singularities.

The following proposition studies the non-pluripolar Monge-Ampère measure of the envelopes of singularity types.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. Assume either
\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}<+\infty
or
P_{\theta}[u]=u
. Then

(\theta+dd^{c}{P_{\theta}[u]})^{n}\leq\textit{1}_{\{P_{\theta}[u]=0\}}\theta^{n}.

Proof.

Fix
j\geq 1
and set
u_{j}=P_{\theta}(u+j,0)
. Observe that
u-\sup_{X}u\leq u_{j}
and
u_{j}\nearrow P_{\theta}[u]
almost everywhere. Observe also that

(2.2)

\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}\leq\textit{1}_{\{u\leq-j\}}\theta_{u}^{n}+\textit{1}_{\{u_{j}=0\}}\theta^{n},

according to Proposition
2.3
.
Assume
\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}<+\infty
. By (
2.2
), the measures
\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}
are dominated by a positive Radon measure. It then follows from Lemma
2.7
that
\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}\rightarrow\theta_{P_{\theta}[u]}^{n}
weakly. Therefore, letting
j\rightarrow+\infty
in (
2.2
) yields

\theta_{P_{\theta}[u]}^{n}\leq\textit{1}_{\{P_{\theta}[u]=0\}}\theta^{n},

since
\{u_{j}=0\}\subset\{P_{\theta}[u]=0\}
for every
j
.
Assume now
P_{\theta}[u]=u
. In this case
u_{j}=u
for every
j
, hence

\theta_{u}^{n}\leq\textit{1}_{\{u\leq-j\}}\theta_{u}^{n}+\textit{1}_{\{u=0\}}\theta^{n},

by (
2.2
). Multiplying the above by
\textit{1}_{\{u>-t\}}
where
t>0
is fixed and using Theorem
2.1
, we get

\textit{1}_{\{u>-t\}}\theta_{\max(u,-t)}^{n}\leq\textit{1}_{\{-t<u\leq-j\}}\theta_{\max(u,-t)}^{n}+\textit{1}_{\{u=0\}}\theta^{n}.

Letting
j\rightarrow+\infty
we find

\textit{1}_{\{u>-t\}}\theta_{\max(u,-t)}^{n}\leq\textit{1}_{\{u=0\}}\theta^{n}.

This is true for all
t>0
. Letting
t\rightarrow+\infty
, the result follows from the definition of the non-pluripolar Monge-Ampère measure.
∎

We will use the following terminology in the sequel.

Definition 2.13
.

We say that a function
\phi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
is a
\theta
-model potential if
P_{\theta}[\phi]=\phi
and
P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\phi)\not\equiv-\infty
for some
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.

We have the following.

•

The function
\phi\equiv 0
is a
\theta
-model potential on
X
.

•

Proposition
2.12
ensures that

\int_{X}\theta_{\phi}^{n}<+\infty
for every
\theta
-model potential
\phi
.

•

By Proposition
2.10
, if
\theta
is closed then every potential
\phi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
that satisfies
P_{\theta}[\phi]=\phi
and
\int_{X}\theta_{\phi}^{n}>0
is
\theta
-model. Thus, the last definition is a generalization of the one introduced by Darvas, DiNezza and the second author (see the discussion following Theorem 2.1 in
[
DDNL21
]
).

•

By Proposition
2.11
, when
v_{+}(\theta)<+\infty
, the condition
P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\phi)\not\equiv-\infty
for some
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
is weaker than
v_{-,\phi}(\theta)>0
.

•

According to Proposition
2.10
, if
\phi
is a
\theta
-model potential then

\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}>0,\;\;\forall u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta),u\simeq\phi.

Indeed, if
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
satisfies
u\simeq\phi
and
\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}=0
then Proposition
2.10
yields
v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\varepsilon\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
such that
(\varepsilon\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}=0
. Arguing as in the proof of Corollary 2.4 in
[
ALS24
]
, we can construct
w\in{\rm PSH}(X,\alpha\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
(
\alpha>0
) such that
(\alpha\omega+dd^{c}w)^{n}=0
, which is impossible.

We will give in Proposition
3.10
a method for constructing
\theta
-model potentials, proving that there are plenty of these functions.

3.
The relative full mass class

3.1.
Definition and properties

Fix
\phi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. When
\theta
is closed, the relative full mass class
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
is defined by the set of
\theta
-psh functions
u
satisfying
u\preceq\phi
and

\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}=\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}\phi)^{n},

see
[
DDNL18a
]
.

When
\theta
is not closed, the equality
\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}=\int_{X}\theta_{\phi}^{n}
fails even when
u\simeq\phi
, hence the need for an alternative definition of
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
. This is the objective of the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume
\theta
is closed and
\phi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
is such that
\int_{X}\theta_{\phi}^{n}>0
. Then a
\theta
-psh function
u
belongs to
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
iff it is more singular than
\phi
and satisfies

P_{\theta}(bu-(b-1)\phi)\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta),\;\;\forall b\geq 1.

Proof.

The direct implication follows from
[
DDNL25
, Theorem 3.10]
. Hence, it suffices to prove the converse.

Fix
b\geq 1
and set
\varphi=P_{\theta}(bu-(b-1)\phi)
. The function
\varphi
belongs to
{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
by hypothesis, and we have
b^{-1}\varphi+(1-b^{-1})\phi\leq u
by Proposition
2.4
. It then follows from the monotonicity of the non-pluripolar Monge-Ampère measure (see
[
WN19b
]
) that

(1-b^{-1})^{n}\int_{X}\theta_{\phi}^{n}\leq\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}(b^{-1}\varphi+(1-b^{-1})\phi))^{n}\leq\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}\leq\int_{X}\theta_{\phi}^{n}.

Since
b
is arbitrary, we obtain by letting
b\rightarrow+\infty
that
\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}=\int_{X}\theta_{\phi}^{n}
, which implies
u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
.
∎

We can thus extend the definition of the relative full mass class as follows.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\phi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. We define the relative full mass class
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
by the set of
\theta
-psh functions
u
which are more singular than
\phi
and satisfy the condition

P_{\theta}(bu-(b-1)\phi)\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta),\;\;\forall b\geq 1.

By definition, it is easy to see that the set
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
is convex, that functions having the same singularity type as
\phi
belong to
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
and that

u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi),v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta),u\leq v\;\text{and }v\preceq\phi\Longrightarrow v\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi).

In particular, the relative full mass class is stable under maxima.

Observe also that, for
\phi=0
, the full mass class
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta):=\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,0)
is then given by

\mathcal{E}(X,\theta)=\{u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta):P_{\theta}(bu)\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta),\;\forall b\geq 1\}.

According to
[
ALS24
, Proposition 2.5]
, when
X
admits a Guan-Li metric, a
\theta
-psh function
u
belongs to
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta)
iff

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\{u\leq-t\}}(\theta+dd^{c}\max(u,-t))^{n}=0.

Therefore, this definition generalizes the one of Guedj-Zeriahi which was stated in the context of Kähler manifolds
[
GZ07
]
.

The following proposition studies further the definition of the relative full mass class
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
in the case
v_{-,\phi}(\theta)>0
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume
\phi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
satisfies
v_{-,\phi}(\theta)>0
, and let
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
be such that
u\preceq\phi
. If
\theta_{u}^{n}
is a Radon measure on
X
and

\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\{u\leq\phi-j\}}(\theta+dd^{c}\max(u,\phi-j))^{n}=0

then
u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
. The converse holds true if
X
admits a Guan-Li form.

It will be interesting to establish this result in a more general complex manifold.

Proof.

We start by proving the direct implication. Fix
b\geq 1
. For every
j\geq 1
, we set
u_{j}=\max(u,\phi-j)
and
\varphi_{j}=P_{\theta}(bu_{j}-(b-1)\phi)
. We have
\varphi_{j}\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
,
\varphi_{j}\simeq\phi
and
\varphi_{j}\searrow P_{\theta}(bu-(b-1)\phi)
. By contradiction, we assume
P_{\theta}(bu-(b-1)\phi)=-\infty
. It then follows by the Hartogs’ lemma that
\sup_{X}\varphi_{j}\rightarrow-\infty
. Fixing
C\geq 0
, we can find
j_{0}\geq 1
such that
X=\{\varphi_{j}\leq-bC\}
for every
j\geq j_{0}
.

Observe that
\varphi_{j}\simeq\phi
for every
j\geq 1
. Hence
\int_{X}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}\geq v_{-,\phi}(\theta)
. On the other hand, according to Proposition
2.4
, we know that

\displaystyle\int_{X}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq b^{n}\int_{\{\varphi_{j}=bu_{j}-(b-1)\phi\leq-bC\}}\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq b^{n}\int_{\{u\leq\phi-j\}}\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}+b^{n}\int_{\{u>\phi-j\}\cap\{u\leq-C\}}\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}.

Therefore, letting
j\rightarrow+\infty
we get by Theorem
2.1
and the hypothesis that

v_{-,\phi}(\theta)\leq b^{n}\int_{\{u\leq-C\}}\theta_{u}^{n}.

Now letting
C\rightarrow+\infty
, we find that
v_{-,\phi}(\theta)=0
, which is assumed not to be true by the hypothesis. We thus infer
P_{\theta}(bu-(b-1)\phi)\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. This holds for every
b\geq 1
, hence
u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
.

Assume now
\omega
is Guan-Li and let
u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
. Since
\theta\leq\omega
, this implies
u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\omega,\phi)
, hence we get by Proposition
3.1
that

\int_{X}\omega_{u}^{n}=\int_{X}\omega_{\phi}^{n}=\int_{X}\omega_{\max(u,\phi-j)}^{n},\;\;\forall j\geq 1.

It then follows from Theorem
2.1
that

\int_{X}\omega_{u}^{n}=\int_{\{u>\phi-j\}}\omega_{u}^{n}+\int_{\{u\leq\phi-j\}}\omega_{\max(u,\phi-j)}^{n},\;\;\forall j\geq 1.

Letting
j\rightarrow+\infty
we obtain

\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\{u\leq\phi-j\}}(\omega+dd^{c}\max(u,\phi-j))^{n}=0,

and the fact that
\theta\leq\omega
allows us to conclude.
∎

We now give a sufficient condition to ensure that a
\theta
-psh function belongs to a relative full mass class.

Proposition 3.4
.

If
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
is such that
v_{-,u}(\theta)>0
and
\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}<+\infty
, then
u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,P_{\theta}[u])
.

Proof.

We have
u-\sup_{X}u\leq P_{\theta}[u]
. By contradiction, we assume there is

b\geq 1
such that
P_{\theta}(bu-(b-1)P_{\theta}[u])=-\infty
. For every
j\geq 1
, we set
u_{j}=P_{\theta}(u+j,0)
and
\varphi_{j}=P_{\theta}(bu-(b-1)u_{j})
. The function
\varphi_{j}
belongs to
{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
and satisfies
\varphi_{j}\simeq u
, in particular
\int_{X}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}\geq v_{-,u}(\theta)
.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\varphi_{j}\searrow-\infty
, we can find
j_{0}\geq 1
such that
X=\{\varphi_{j}\leq-bk\}
for every
j\geq j_{0}
. By Proposition
2.4
, we know that

\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}\leq\textit{1}_{D_{j}}b^{n}\theta_{u}^{n},

where
D_{j}=\{\varphi_{j}=bu-(b-1)u_{j}\}
. Therefore

v_{-,u}(\theta)\leq\int_{X}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}\leq b^{n}\int_{\{u\leq-k\}}\theta_{u}^{n}.

Since
k
is arbitrary, we obtain by letting
k\rightarrow+\infty
that
v_{-,u}(\theta)=0
, a contradiction. This proves
P_{\theta}(bu-(b-1)P_{\theta}[u])\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
for every
b\geq 1
, hence
u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,P_{\theta}[u])
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\phi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
and let
u
,
v\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
. We have
P_{\theta}(bu-(b-1)v)\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
for every
b\geq 1
.

Proof.

Subtracting a constant, one can assume
u
,
v\leq\phi
. Then, by the definition of
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
, it suffices to prove the result for
v=\phi
. Fix
b\geq 1
and set
u_{t}=P_{\theta}(tu-(t-1)\phi)
for every
t\geq 1
. Fixing
s\geq 1
and
t>bs
, we have

bu-(b-1)\phi=t^{-1}b(tu-(t-1)\phi)+(1-t^{-1}b)\phi,

hence
u_{b}\geq t^{-1}bu_{t}+(1-t^{-1}b)\phi
, and thus

su_{b}-(s-1)\phi\geq t^{-1}bsu_{t}+(1-t^{-1}bs)\phi
.
We then infer
P_{\theta}(su_{b}-(s-1)\phi)\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. Since
s
is arbitrary, we get
u_{b}\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Fix
\phi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. We have

P_{\theta}(u-\phi)\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta),\;\;\forall u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi).

Proof.

Fix
b\geq 1
and set
\varphi=P_{\theta}(u-\phi)
. The function
\varphi
belongs to
{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
by Lemma
2.5
.
Since
bu-(b-1)\phi\leq b(u-\phi)
, we get
P_{\theta}(b(u-\phi))\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
.
Using this and the fact that
b^{-1}P_{\theta}(b(u-\phi))\leq\varphi
, we obtain
P_{\theta}(b\varphi)\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. This holds for every
b\geq 1
, hence
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta)
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Assume
\phi
is a
\theta
-model potential on
X
. If
u
,
v\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
then
P_{\theta}(u,v)\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
.

Proof.

Assume first that
P_{\theta}(u,v)\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
for every
u
,
v\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
. Fix
u
,
v\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
and set
u_{b}=P_{\theta}(bu-(b-1)\phi)
and
v_{b}=P_{\theta}(bv-(b-1)\phi)
for every
b\geq 1
. By Proposition
3.5
, we have
u_{b}
,
v_{b}\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
, hence
P_{\theta}(u_{b},v_{b})\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
by our assumption. Since

b^{-1}P_{\theta}(u_{b},v_{b})+(1-b^{-1})\phi\leq P_{\theta}(u,v),

we get
P_{\theta}(u_{b},v_{b})\leq bP_{\theta}(u,v)-(b-1)\phi
, hence
P_{\theta}(bP_{\theta}(u,v)-(b-1)\phi)\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. This holds for every
b\geq 1
, therefore
P_{\theta}(u,v)\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
.

It thus remains to show
P_{\theta}(u,v)\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
for every
u
,
v\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
. Fix
u
,
v\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
and set
\tilde{u}=P_{\theta}(u-\phi)
,
\tilde{v}=P_{\theta}(v-\phi)
. Clearly, there is no loss of generality in assuming
\max(u,v)\leq\phi
. First, Proposition
3.6
gives
\tilde{u}
,
\tilde{v}\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta)
.
Observe that
\tilde{u}+\phi\leq u
,
\tilde{v}+\phi\leq v
,
\tilde{u}+\tilde{v}\leq\min(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})
and that
P_{\theta}(2b\tilde{u})/2+P_{\theta}(2b\tilde{v})/2\leq bP_{\theta}(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})
for every
b\geq 1
. Therefore

w:=P_{\theta}(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta)
and
w+\phi\leq\min(u,v)
. Since
\phi
is a
\theta
-model potential, we know that
P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\phi)\not\equiv-\infty
for some
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. For
b>1/(1-\varepsilon)
, the function
\varphi:=b^{-1}P_{\theta}(bw)+P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\phi)
belongs to
{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
and satisfies
\varphi\leq\min(u,v)
, hence
P_{\theta}(u,v)\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. The result follows.
∎

The following proposition allows us to control the lower bound of the Monge-Ampère volumes of functions in the relative full mass class when
v_{-}(\theta)>0
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Assume
\phi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
is such that
P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\phi)\not\equiv-\infty
for some
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. For every
u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
, we have

\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{n}v_{-}(\theta).

Proof.

Fix
\delta>0
small enough so that
\varepsilon+\delta<1
. By the last proposition, there is
w\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta)
such that
u\geq\phi+w
. By the definition of
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta)
, we have
P_{\theta}(\delta^{-1}w)\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
and
u\geq P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\phi)+\delta P_{\theta}(\delta^{-1}w)
, hence
P_{(\varepsilon+\delta)\theta}(u)\not\equiv-\infty
. We thus get by Proposition
2.10
that

\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}\geq(1-\varepsilon-\delta)^{n}v_{-}(\theta).

The conclusion follows by letting
\delta\rightarrow 0
.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

The proof of the last proposition shows also that if
\phi
is
\theta
-model potential on
X
, then

\int_{X}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}>0,\;\;\forall u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi).

As a consequence of the last proposition, we obtain a method for constructing
\theta
-model potentials.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
and set
\phi=P_{\theta}[u]
. If
v_{-,\phi}(\theta)>0
and
v_{+}(\theta)<+\infty
then
\phi
is a
\theta
-model potential.

Proof.

According to Proposition
2.11
, there is
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that
P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\phi)\not\equiv-\infty
. It thus suffices to prove
P_{\theta}[\phi]=\phi
.

For every
j\geq 1
, we will use the notations
u_{j}=P_{\theta}(u+j,0)
and
\phi_{j}=P_{\theta}(\phi+j,0)
. Fix
b\geq 1
and consider
\phi_{b}=P_{\theta}(b\phi-(b-1)P_{\theta}[\phi])
. By Proposition
3.4
, we have
\phi\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,P_{\theta}[\phi])
. Proposition
3.5
then gives
\phi_{b}\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,P_{\theta}[\phi])
.
Moreover, we have
\phi\leq P_{\theta}[\phi]
, hence
\phi_{b}\leq\phi
and
P_{\theta}(t\phi_{b}-(t-1)\phi)\geq P_{\theta}(t\phi_{b}-(t-1)P_{\theta}[\phi])
for all
t\geq 1
, which means
\phi_{b}\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
. Since
v_{-,\phi}(\theta)\leq v_{-}(\theta)
by Proposition
2.8
, it follows from the last proposition that
\int_{X}\theta^{n}_{\phi_{b}}>0
.

On the other hand, Proposition
2.4
implies
\theta_{\phi_{b}}^{n}\leq b^{n}\textit{1}_{\{\phi_{b}=b\phi-(b-1)P_{\theta}[\phi]\}}\theta_{\phi}^{n}
. Since the measure
\theta_{\phi}^{n}
is carried by
\{\phi=0\}
by Proposition
2.12
, we infer that
\theta_{\phi_{b}}^{n}
is carried by

\{\phi_{b}=b\phi-(b-1)P_{\theta}[\phi]\}\cap\{\phi=0\}\subset\{\phi_{b}=0\}.

Since
\int_{X}\theta_{\phi_{b}}^{n}>0
, we get
\sup_{X}\phi_{b}=0
for every
b
, hence
\psi:=\lim\phi_{b}
belongs to
{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. Fix
a>0
. We have
\psi\leq b\phi-(b-1)P_{\theta}[\phi]
for every
b
, hence
\psi=-\infty
on
\{\phi<P_{\theta}[\phi]-a\}
, and thus
\phi\geq P_{\theta}[\phi]-a
. Now letting
a\rightarrow 0
gives
\phi\geq P_{\theta}[\phi]
, whence equality.
∎

From now on, and unless otherwise stated, we will denote by
\phi
a fixed
\theta
-model potential on
X
.

3.2.
The domination principle

The domination principle is an effective tool in the theory of complex Monge-Ampère equations. For example proving the uniqueness of solutions, connecting the
L^{1}
-convergence and the convergence in capacity of potentials, and thereby establishing weak solutions to Monge-Ampère equations are all consequences of (or use in an essential way) the domination principle
[
GL22
,
DDNL25
,
GL23
,
ALS24
]
.

We first propose the following version which generalizes
[
GL22
, Proposition 2.8]
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
be such that
P_{\delta\theta}(u)\not\equiv-\infty

for some
\delta\in(0,1)
. Assume
v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
is such that
v\preceq u
and

\theta_{u}^{n}\leq c\theta_{v}^{n}\;\;\text{on}\;\{u<v\},

for some
c\in[0,1)
. Then
u\geq v
.

The proof of the last theorem is based on the following technical lemma.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
u
and
v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
be such that
v\preceq u
and
\theta_{u}^{n}=0
on
\{u<v\}
. If
\delta\in(0,1)
is such that
P_{\delta\theta}(v)\not\equiv-\infty
then
u\geq P_{\delta\theta}(v)
.

Proof.

Set
v_{\delta}=P_{\delta\theta}(v)
and define
\varphi=P_{(1-\delta)\theta}(u-v_{\delta})
. By hypothesis, there is
C\geq 0
such that
v-C\leq u
. That implies
v_{\delta}-C\leq u
, and consequently
\varphi\in{\rm PSH}(X,(1-\delta)\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
by Lemma
2.5
. It follows from Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
that

((1-\delta)\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}\leq\textit{1}_{\{\varphi+v_{\delta}=u\}}((1-\delta)\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}\leq\theta_{u}^{n},

hence
((1-\delta)\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}(\{u<v\})=0
by hypothesis. That implies

((1-\delta)\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}(\{\varphi<0\})=((1-\delta)\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}(\{\varphi+v_{\delta}=u\}\cap\{\varphi<0\})=0

because
\{\varphi+v_{\delta}=u\}\cap\{\varphi<0\}\subset\{u<v\}\cup\{v_{\delta}=-\infty\}
. Proposition 2.8 in
[
GL22
]
then yields
\varphi\geq 0
, and consequently
u\geq v_{\delta}
.
∎

We now proceed to the proof of Theorem
3.11
.

Proof.

For
w:=\max(u,v)
, we have
w\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
,
w\simeq u
and

\theta_{u}^{n}\leq c\theta_{w}^{n}\;\;\text{on}\;\{u<w\}

by Theorem
2.1
. Therefore, up to replacing
v
by
\max(u,v)
, there is no loss of generality in assuming
v-C\leq u\leq v
for some
C>0
. Fix
b\geq 1
and consider
\varphi_{b}=P_{\theta}(bu-(b-1)v)
. We have
\varphi_{b}\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
and
-bC+u\leq\varphi_{b}\leq u
.
Set
D:=\{\varphi_{b}=bu-(b-1)v\}
. According to Theorem
2.2
, the measure
\theta_{\varphi_{b}}^{n}
is carried by
D
and
\varphi_{b}\leq bu-(b-1)v
.
It thus follows from Theorem
2.1
that

\textit{1}_{D}(\theta+dd^{c}(b^{-1}\varphi_{b}+(1-b^{-1})v))^{n}\leq\textit{1}_{D}\theta_{u}^{n}.

Hence, we get by the hypothesis that

\textit{1}_{D\cap\{u<v\}}b^{-n}\theta_{\varphi_{b}}^{n}+\textit{1}_{D\cap\{u<v\}}(1-b^{-1})^{n}\theta_{v}^{n}\leq\textit{1}_{D\cap\{u<v\}}c\theta_{v}^{n}.

Taking
b
large enough so that
(1-b^{-1})^{n}>c
, we obtain that
\theta_{\varphi_{b}}^{n}
is carried by
D\cap\{u=v\}\subset\{\varphi_{b}=u\}
, hence
\theta_{\varphi_{b}}^{n}(\{\varphi_{b}<u\})=0
. It then follows from the last lemma that
\varphi_{b}\geq P_{\delta\theta}(u)
. Fix
a>0
. On the set
\{u<v-a\}
, we have
\varphi_{b}\leq v-ab
for every
b
, hence
P_{\delta\theta}(u)=-\infty
.
Using that
P_{\delta\theta}(u)\in L^{1}(X)
, we obtain
u\geq v-a
. The result now follows by letting
a\rightarrow 0
.
∎

As a consequence of Theorem
3.11
, we have

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
u
,
v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
be such that
v\preceq u
and
P_{\delta\theta}(u)\not\equiv-\infty
for some
\delta\in(0,1)
.

(i)

If
e^{-\lambda u}\theta_{u}^{n}\leq e^{-\lambda v}\theta_{v}^{n}
then
u\geq v
.

(ii)

If
\theta_{u}^{n}\leq c\theta_{v}^{n}
for some
c\geq 0
then
c\geq 1
.

Proof.

Using Theorems
3.11
, the proof goes immediately as in the ones of Corollaries 2.12 and 2.13 in
[
ALS24
]
.
∎

Assuming
v_{-}(\theta)>0
, we can extend the domination principle to the class
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
, providing a generalization of
[
DDNL25
, Theorem 3.12]
and
[
ALS24
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Theorem 3.14
.

Assume
v_{-}(\theta)>0
. Fix
c\in[0,1)
, and let
u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
and
v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
be such that
v\preceq\phi
. Assume either
\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}<+\infty
or
\int_{X}\theta_{v}^{n}<+\infty
. If

\theta_{u}^{n}\leq c\theta_{v}^{n}\;\;\text{on}\;\{u<v\},

then
u\geq v
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the one of
[
ALS24
, Theorem 2.11]
. Notice that the function
w:=\max(u,v)
belongs to
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
, and it satisfies

\theta_{u}^{n}\leq c\theta_{w}^{n}\;\;\text{on}\;\{u<w\}

by Theorem
2.1
. Therefore, up to replacing
v
by
\max(u,v)
, there is no loss of generality in assuming
v\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
and
u\leq v
.

For every
b\geq 1
, the function
u_{b}:=P_{\theta}(bu-(b-1)v)
belongs to
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
by Proposition
3.5
. Using that
\theta_{u}^{n}
or
\theta_{v}^{n}
is a Radon measure on
X
and arguing as in the proof of
[
ALS24
, Theorem 2.11]
, one can construct an increasing function
h:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
h(+\infty)=+\infty
and

\sup_{b\geq 1}\int_{X}h(|u_{b}|)\theta_{u_{b}}^{n}<+\infty.

If
\sup_{X}u_{b}\rightarrow-\infty
then

\int_{X}\theta_{u_{b}}^{n}\leq\frac{C}{h(|\sup_{X}u_{b}|)}\rightarrow 0,\;\text{as}\;b\rightarrow+\infty,

contradicting Proposition
3.8
. Therefore
(u_{b})
has a subsequence that converges in
L^{1}(X)
to a function
\varphi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
.

Fix
a>0
. On the set
\{u<v-a\}
, we have
u_{b}\leq v-ab
for every
b
, hence
\varphi=-\infty
. Since
\varphi\in L^{1}(X)
, we obtain
u\geq v-a
. This holds for every
a>0
, hence
u\geq v
.
∎

As above, we have the following consequence of Theorem
3.14
.

Corollary 3.15
.

Assume
v_{-}(\theta)>0
. Let
u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
and
v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
be such that
v\preceq\phi
and
\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}<+\infty
.

(i)

If
e^{-\lambda u}\theta_{u}^{n}\leq e^{-\lambda v}\theta_{v}^{n}
then
u\geq v
.

(ii)

If
\theta_{u}^{n}\leq c\theta_{v}^{n}
for some
c\geq 0
then
c\geq 1
.

4.
Weak convergence of Monge-Ampère measures

The objective of this section is to develop pluripotential tools that will be of great help in solving degenerate Monge-Ampère equations. By following Cegrell’s ideas from local settings
[
Ceg98
,
Ceg04
]
, we are led to work with sequences
u_{j}\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
that satisfy one of the following conditions:

(1)

\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}\leq Ae^{\lambda u_{j}}\omega_{\psi_{j}}^{n}
, where
\lambda
,
A
are positive constants and
\psi_{j}
is a uniformly bounded sequence of
\omega
-psh functions converging in capacity to a function
\psi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
;

(2)

\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}\leq\mu
is dominated by a non-pluripolar Radon measure
\mu
.

The main objective of this section is to prove the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
u_{j}\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
be a sequence converging in
L^{1}(X)
to a function
u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
such that
P_{\theta}(\inf u_{j})\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
. Assume either
v_{-}(\theta)>0
or
P_{\theta}(\inf u_{j})\simeq\phi
. If
u_{j}
satisfies (
1
) or (
2
), then
u_{j}\rightarrow u
in capacity and
\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}\rightarrow\theta_{u}^{n}
weakly.

Observe that the sequence
u_{j}
in the last theorem satisfies
\sup_{j}\int_{X}\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}<+\infty
.
Before proving the theorem, we will give a sufficient condition ensuring
P_{\theta}(\inf u_{j})\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume
v_{-}(\theta)>0
. Let
u_{j}\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
be a sequence that satisfies (
1
) or (
2
). If
u_{j}\rightarrow u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
in
L^{1}(X)
then
u
and
P_{\theta}(\inf u_{j})
are in
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
.

Proof.

Observe that
u_{j}\preceq\phi
for every
j
. Since
u_{j}\rightarrow u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
in
L^{1}(X)
, the Hartogs’ lemma implies
(\sup_{X}u_{j})
is bounded. Using this and the fact that
\phi
is a
\theta
-model potential, we can find a uniform constant
C
such that
u_{j}\leq\phi+C
. We thus infer
u\preceq\phi
.

By the definition of
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
, we will be done if we prove
P_{\theta}(\inf u_{j})\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
. Fix then
b\geq 1
and set
v_{j}=P_{\theta}(u_{1},...,u_{j})
,
v=P_{\theta}(\inf u_{j})
and
\varphi_{j}=P_{\theta}(bv_{j}-(b-1)\phi)
. We want to prove that
(\sup_{X}\varphi_{j})
doesn’t converge to
-\infty
. Assume by contradiction that this is not true. Up to extracting, we can further assume
\varphi_{j}\leq-j
for every
j
.

According to Proposition
3.7
, we have
v_{j}\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
, hence
\varphi_{j}\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
by Proposition
3.5
.
In particular, since
v_{-}(\theta)>0
, the sequence
\int_{X}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}
is uniformly bounded away from zero by Proposition
3.8
.

By Theorem
2.2
, we know that
\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}
is carried by the set
D_{j}=\{\varphi_{j}=bv_{j}-(b-1)\phi\}
. Since the functions
b^{-1}\varphi_{j}+(1-b^{-1})\phi
and
v_{j}
belong to
{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
and satisfy
b^{-1}\varphi_{j}+(1-b^{-1})\phi\leq v_{j}
with equality on
D_{j}
, we get by Theorem
2.1
that

(4.1)

\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}\leq b^{n}\textit{1}_{D_{j}}(\theta+dd^{c}(b^{-1}\varphi_{j}+(1-b^{-1})\phi))^{n}\leq b^{n}\textit{1}_{D_{j}}\theta_{v_{j}}^{n}.

Assume first that
u_{j}
satisfies (
1
). Applying Proposition
2.3
yields

\int_{X}e^{-\lambda v_{j}}\theta_{v_{j}}^{n}\leq A\sum_{k=1}^{j}\int_{X}\omega_{\psi_{k}}^{n}.

Since
(\psi_{k})
is uniformly bounded, we can find
C\geq 0
such that

\int_{X}e^{-\lambda v_{j}}\theta_{v_{j}}^{n}\leq Cj.

It follows that from (
4.1
) that

\int_{X}e^{-\lambda b^{-1}\varphi_{j}}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}\leq\int_{X}e^{-\lambda(b^{-1}\varphi_{j}+(1-b^{-1})\phi)}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}\leq b^{n}Cj.

Therefore,

\int_{X}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}\leq e^{\lambda b^{-1}\sup_{X}\varphi_{j}}\int_{X}e^{-\lambda b^{-1}\varphi_{j}}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}\leq Cje^{-\lambda b^{-1}j},

hence
\inf\int_{X}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}=0
, a contradiction.
Assume now that
u_{j}
satisfies (
2
). By Lemma
2.6
, we have

\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}|e^{u_{j}}-e^{u}|\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}=0.

Passing to a subsequence if necessary, we can assume

\int_{X}|e^{u_{j}}-e^{u}|\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}\leq 2^{-j},\;\;\forall j\geq 1.

Proposition
2.3
then implies

\int_{X}|e^{v_{j}}-e^{u}|\theta_{v_{j}}^{n}\leq 2^{-j+1}.

Hence

\int_{X}|e^{b^{-1}\varphi_{j}+(1-b^{-1})\phi}-e^{u}|\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}\leq b^{n}2^{-j+1},

by (
4.1
). Again by (
4.1
), we have

\int_{X}e^{b^{-1}\varphi_{j}+(1-b^{-1})\phi}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}\leq e^{-b^{-1}j}\int_{X}\theta_{v_{j}}^{n}\leq je^{-b^{-1}j}\mu(X),

where in the last inequality we have used Proposition
2.3
. Finally, we can write

\int_{X}e^{u}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}\leq b^{n}2^{-j+1}+\int_{X}e^{b^{-1}\varphi_{j}+(1-b^{-1})\phi}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}\leq b^{n}2^{-j+1}+je^{-b^{-1}j}\mu(X)\to 0

as
j\rightarrow+\infty
. Fixing
C>0
, we have

\int_{X}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}\leq\mu(\{u\leq-C\})+e^{C}\int_{X}e^{u}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}.

Letting
j\rightarrow+\infty
and then
C\rightarrow+\infty
, we find that
\inf\int_{X}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}=0
,
a contradiction.

We infer that in both cases
(\sup_{X}\varphi_{j})
is bounded from below. Since
\varphi_{j}
decreases to
P_{\theta}(bv-(b-1)\phi)
and
b
is arbitrary, we get
v\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
, which proves the result.
∎

The following lemma gives a sufficient condition to ensure the convergence in capacity of
\theta
-psh functions that converge in
L^{1}(X)
, generalizing
[
ALS24
, Theorem 3.1]
which deals with uniformly bounded sequences.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
u_{j}\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
be such that
P_{\theta}(\inf u_{j})\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
and
\sup_{j}\int_{X}\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}<+\infty
. Assume either
P_{\theta}(\inf u_{j})\simeq\phi
or
v_{-}(\theta)>0
. If
u_{j}
converges in
L^{1}(X)
to a function
u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
and

\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}|e^{u_{j}}-e^{u}|\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}=0,

then
u_{j}\rightarrow u
in capacity.

Proof.

The proof follows the lines of the proof of
[
ALS24
, Theorem 3.1]
which builds on the rooftop envelopes as in
[
Dar15
,
Dar17
]
.

Up to extracting a subsequence, one can assume

\int_{X}|e^{u_{j}}-e^{u}|\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}\leq 2^{-j},\;\;\forall j\geq 1.

For any
j
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we set
u_{j,k}=P_{\theta}(u_{j},...,u_{j+k})
. By hypothesis, the function
u_{j,k}
belongs to
{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
and it converges to the
\theta
-psh function
\tilde{u}_{j}:=P_{\theta}(\inf_{k\geq j}u_{k})
. By Proposition
2.3
, we have

\int_{X}|e^{u_{j,k}}-e^{u}|\theta_{u_{j,k}}^{n}\leq 2^{-j+1},\;\;\forall j,k\geq 1.

Therefore, letting
k\rightarrow+\infty
, we get by
[
DDNL25
, Theorem 2.6]
that

\int_{X}|e^{\tilde{u}_{j}}-e^{u}|\theta_{\tilde{u}_{j}}^{n}\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}|e^{u_{j,k}}-e^{u}|\theta_{u_{j,k}}^{n}\leq 2^{-j+1},

for all
j\geq 1
. Observe now that
(\tilde{u}_{j})
is increasing, hence it converges a.e. to a function
\tilde{u}\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. We get similarly that

\int_{X}|e^{\tilde{u}}-e^{u}|\theta_{\tilde{u}}^{n}\leq\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}|e^{\tilde{u}_{j}}-e^{u}|\theta_{\tilde{u}_{j}}^{n}=0.

By hypothesis, we have
u
,
P_{\theta}(\inf u_{j})\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
. Since
P_{\theta}(\inf u_{j})\leq\tilde{u}\leq u
by construction, it follows that
\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
. Observe that if
P_{\theta}(\inf u_{j})\simeq\phi
then so does
\tilde{u}
. Observe also that
\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}<+\infty
by Theorem 2.6 in
[
DDNL25
]
. Therefore, we infer by Theorems
3.11
and
3.14
that
u\leq\tilde{u}
, hence
u=\tilde{u}
.

Finally, we have
\tilde{u}_{j}\leq u_{j}\leq\max(u,u_{j})
and the sequences
(\tilde{u}_{j})
and
(\max(u,u_{j}))
converge to
u
in capacity. Therefore
u_{j}\rightarrow u
in capacity, completing the proof.
∎

We are now in position to prove Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

By hypothesis, the sequence
(u_{j})
converges in
L^{1}(X)
to
u
. Applying the Hartogs’ lemma, we infer that
(\sup_{X}u_{j})
is bounded, hence
\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}\leq C\omega_{\psi_{j}}^{n}+\mu
for some constant
C>0
. It thus follows from
[
KN22
, Lemma 2.3]
and Lemma
2.6
that

\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}|e^{u_{j}}-e^{u}|\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}=0.

Lemma
4.3
then implies
u_{j}\rightarrow u
in capacity. It thus remains to prove
\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}\rightarrow\theta_{u}^{n}
weakly. Fix then
c>0
and set
f=P_{\theta}(\inf u_{j})
. Since
u_{j}
satisfies the condition (
1
) or (
2
), we have

\int_{\{f\leq-c\}}\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}\leq C{\rm Cap}_{\omega}(\{f\leq-c\})+\mu(\{f\leq-c\}),

for some constant
C>0
. It follows from
[
DK12
, Proposition 2.5]
that

\int_{\{f\leq-c\}}\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}\rightarrow 0,\;\;\text{as}\;c\rightarrow+\infty

uniformly with respect to
j
, hence
\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}\rightarrow\theta_{u}^{n}
weakly by Lemma
2.7
, finishing the proof.
∎

5.
Monge-Ampère equations with prescribed singularities

This section is devoted to the main problem of solving degenerate Monge-Ampère equations in the class
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
. We will first start by studying the case when the right hand side has a
L^{p}
-density with respect to the Lebesgue measure, and then we will proceed to the general case.

5.1.
The case of measures with
L^{p}
-density

Our objective in this paragraph is to solve the Monge-Ampère equation

(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}=e^{\lambda u}f\omega^{n},\;\;u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)\;\text{with}\;u\simeq\phi.

Here
\lambda\geq 0
,
p>1
are constants and
0\leq f\in L^{p}(X)
is such that
\int_{X}f\omega^{n}>0
.
In the sequel, we will assume
\lambda>0
. The case
\lambda=0
will be treated separately in Theorem
5.7
.

We first start by proving the following subsolution theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume
\mu=f(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}
where
\psi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
and
0\leq f\in L^{\infty}(X)
is such that
\mu(X)>0
. Then, we can find a unique function
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
such that

(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}=e^{\lambda u}\mu.

Observe that generalizing this result to the case
\lambda=0
will provide a positive answer to a question of Kołodziej from Kähler settings
[
DGZ16
, Question 15]
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1.
We first assume
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X)
,
f>0
. Let
\psi_{j}
be a sequence of smooth
\omega
-psh functions decreasing to
\psi
. The sequence
\varphi_{j}:=P_{\theta}(\psi_{j})
is a uniformly bounded sequence of
\theta
-psh functions which decreases to
\psi
since
\psi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. Proposition
2.4
then implies

\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}=\textit{1}_{\{\varphi_{j}=\psi_{j}\}}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}\leq\omega_{\psi_{j}}^{n},

hence the measure
f\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}
has a
L^{\infty}
-density with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Applying
[
GL23
, Theorem 3.4]
yields
u_{j}\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
such that

(\theta+dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}=e^{\lambda u_{j}}f(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi_{j})^{n}.

Since
f>0
is bounded and
\varphi_{j}
is uniformly bounded,
[
GL22
, Corollary 2.9]
gives
C>0
such that
\varphi_{j}-C\leq u_{j}\leq\varphi_{j}+C
, hence
u_{j}
is uniformly bounded. Passing to a subsequence if necessary, one can assume
u_{j}\rightarrow u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
in
L^{1}(X)
. By definition of
u_{j}
, we have
\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}e^{\lambda u_{j}}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}
, hence

\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}|e^{u_{j}}-e^{u}|\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}=0

by
[
KN22
, Lemma 2.3]
. It follows from Lemma
4.3
that
u_{j}\rightarrow u
in capacity. Therefore, we get by
[
GZ17a
, Theorem 4.26]
that
\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}\rightarrow\theta_{u}^{n}
weakly and

\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}=e^{\lambda u_{j}}f\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}\rightarrow e^{\lambda u}f\theta_{\psi}^{n}\;\;\text{weakly,}

which means
\theta_{u}^{n}=e^{\lambda u}f\theta_{\psi}^{n}
.

Step 2.
We now proceed to the general case when
f
is merely bounded. Let
f_{j}
be a uniformly bounded sequence of positive smooth functions converging to
f
in
L^{1}(\theta_{\psi}^{n})
and a.e. with respect to
\theta_{\psi}^{n}
. For each
j
, Step 1 gives
u_{j}\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
such that

\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}=e^{\lambda u_{j}}f_{j}\theta_{\psi}^{n}.

By construction, the function
u_{j}
is the limit in capacity of a uniformly bounded sequence
(u_{j}^{k})\subset{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
such that

(\theta+dd^{c}u_{j}^{k})^{n}=e^{\lambda u_{j}^{k}}f_{j}\theta_{\psi_{k}}^{n},

where
\psi_{k}\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
is such that
\psi_{k}\searrow\psi
and
\theta_{\psi_{k}}^{n}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure.
It follows from the mixed Monge-Ampère inequality
[
Ngu16b
, Lemma 1.9]
that

(\theta+dd^{c}u_{j}^{k})\wedge(\theta+dd^{c}\psi_{k})^{n-1}\geq e^{\lambda u_{j}^{k}/n}f_{j}^{1/n}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi_{k})^{n}.

Letting
k\rightarrow+\infty
, we get by
[
GZ17a
, Theorem 4.26]
that

(5.1)

(\theta+dd^{c}u_{j})\wedge(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n-1}\geq e^{\lambda u_{j}/n}f_{j}^{1/n}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}.

Consider now
v_{j}=u_{j}-\sup_{X}u_{j}
and
c_{j}=e^{\lambda\sup_{X}u_{j}}
. By the Hartogs lemma, we know that a subsequence of
v_{j}
converges in
L^{1}(X)
to a function
v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. Using Lemma
2.6
and extracting if necessary, we can assume
v_{j}\rightarrow v
a.e. with respect to
\theta_{\psi}^{n}
. In particular, we get by the Lebesgue convergence theorem that

\int_{X}e^{\lambda v_{j}/n}f_{j}^{1/n}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}\rightarrow\int_{X}e^{\lambda v/n}f^{1/n}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}.

If
\int_{X}e^{\lambda v/n}f^{1/n}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}=0
, then

\int_{\{v\geq-t\}}f^{1/n}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}\leq e^{t/n}\int_{X}e^{\lambda v/n}f^{1/n}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}=0,\;\;\forall t\geq 0,

hence
f=0
in
L^{1}(\theta_{\psi}^{n})
, consequently
\mu=0
, a contradiction. We thus infer that

\int_{X}e^{\lambda v_{j}/n}f_{j}^{1/n}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}\geq c^{\prime}>0.

Recall that, by (
5.1
), we have

(\theta+dd^{c}v_{j})\wedge(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n-1}\geq c_{j}^{1/n}e^{\lambda v_{j}/n}f_{j}^{1/n}(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}.

Integrating over
X
and using Stokes theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle c^{\prime}c_{j}^{1/n}\leq\int_{X}\theta_{v_{j}}\wedge\theta_{\psi}^{n-1}\leq\int_{X}\theta\wedge\omega_{\psi}^{n-1}+\int_{X}v_{j}dd^{c}\omega_{\psi}^{n-1}.

Observe that

\displaystyle dd^{c}\omega_{\psi}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=(n-1)dd^{c}\omega\wedge\omega_{\psi}^{n-2}+n(n-1)d\omega\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega_{\psi}^{n-3}

\displaystyle\geq-n(n-1)B\left(\omega^{2}\wedge\omega_{\psi}^{n-2}+\omega^{3}\wedge\omega_{\psi}^{n-3}\right),

where
B\geq 0
is such that
dd^{c}\omega\geq-B\omega^{2}
and
d\omega\wedge d^{c}\omega\geq-B\omega^{3}
.
Therefore, we have

\int_{X}v_{j}dd^{c}\omega_{\psi}^{n-1}\leq-n(n-1)B\int_{X}v_{j}\left(\omega^{2}\wedge\omega_{\psi}^{n-2}+\omega^{3}\wedge\omega_{\psi}^{n-3}\right),

which is bounded from above by the CLN inequality
[
Ngu16b
, Proposition 1.1]
. We thus infer that
u_{j}
is uniformly bounded from above on
X
.

On the other hand, we have

e^{-\lambda u_{j}}\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}=f_{j}\theta_{\psi}^{n}\leq e^{-\lambda(\psi-C)}\theta_{\psi}^{n}

for some
C>0
. It follows from
[
GL22
, Proposition 2.8]
that
u_{j}\geq\psi-C
, hence
u_{j}
is uniformly bounded. Up to extracting we can assume
u_{j}\rightarrow u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
in
L^{1}(X)
. Using Lemma
2.6
and extracting if necessary, we can further assume
u_{j}\rightarrow u
a.e. with respect
L^{1}(\theta_{\psi}^{n})
.
We then obtain by the Lebesgue convergence theorem that
f_{j}e^{\lambda u_{j}}\theta_{\psi}^{n}\rightarrow fe^{\lambda u}\theta_{\psi}^{n}
strongly.

On the other hand, using the fact that
u_{j}
,
f_{j}
are uniformly bounded, we get
\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}\leq C\theta_{\psi}^{n}
for some
C>0
. It then follows from Lemma
2.6
that

\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}|e^{u_{j}}-e^{u}|\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}=0.

Arguing as in the end of the proof of Step 1, we get
\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}\rightarrow\theta_{u}^{n}
weakly, and consequently
\theta_{u}^{n}=e^{\lambda u}\mu
.
∎

We will also need the following subsolution theorem.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
u
and
v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
with
u\simeq\phi
and
v\simeq\phi
. If
\mu:=e^{-\lambda u}\theta_{u}^{n}+e^{-\lambda v}\theta_{v}^{n}
is a Radon measure, then there is
\varphi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
such that
\varphi\simeq\phi
and

\theta_{\varphi}^{n}=e^{\lambda\varphi}\mu.

We first prove the following technical lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

For every
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\omega)
, there is
C\geq 0
such that

\int_{X}(\omega+dd^{c}\max(u,-j))^{n}\leq Cj^{n},\;\;\forall j\geq 1.

Proof.

The functions
(\max(u/j,-1))
form a uniformly bounded sequence of
\omega
-psh functions. We can then find a uniform constant
C\geq 0
such that

\int_{X}(\omega+dd^{c}\max(u/j,-1))^{n}\leq C.

Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{X}(\omega+dd^{c}\max(u,-j))^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}(j\omega+dd^{c}\max(u,-j))^{n}

\displaystyle\leq j^{n}\int_{X}(\omega+dd^{c}\max(u/j,-1))^{n}\leq Cj^{n}.

∎

We now proceed to the proof of Theorem
5.2
.

Proof.

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we set
u_{j}=\max(u,-j)
,
v_{j}=\max(v,-j)
and

\mu_{j}=e^{-\lambda u_{j}}\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}+e^{-\lambda v_{j}}\theta_{v_{j}}^{n}.

The latter is a positive Radon measure on
X
that satisfies

\mu_{j}\leq 2^{n}e^{\lambda j}\theta_{{(u_{j}+v_{j})/2}}^{n}.

By Theorem
5.1
, there is
\varphi_{j}\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
such that

\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}=e^{\lambda\varphi_{j}}\mu_{j}.

On one hand,
[
GL22
, Corollary 2.9]
yields
\varphi_{j}\leq\min(u_{j},v_{j})
for every
j
.
On the other hand, since
u
and
v
have the same singularity type, there is
C>0
such that
|u-v|\leq C
. Observe that we also have
|u_{j}-v_{j}|\leq C

by the definition of
u_{j}
and
v_{j}
. Now consider the function
\psi_{j}=(u_{j}+v_{j})/2-C/2-n\log 2/\lambda
. We have

e^{-\lambda\psi_{j}}\theta_{\psi_{j}}^{n}\geq e^{-\lambda/2(u_{j}+v_{j})+C\lambda/2}(\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}+\theta_{v_{j}}^{n})\geq e^{-\lambda u_{j}}\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}+e^{-\lambda v_{j}}\theta_{v_{j}}^{n}=e^{-\lambda\varphi_{j}}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}.

It follows from
[
GL22
, Corollary 2.9]
that
\varphi_{j}\geq\psi_{j}
for every
j
. By the definition of
\psi_{j}
and the fact that
u\simeq\phi
and
v\simeq\phi
, we get
\varphi_{j}\geq\phi-C_{1}
for some uniform constant
C_{1}
. We finally obtain
\phi-C_{1}\leq\varphi_{j}\leq\min(u_{j},v_{j})
for every
j
.

By the Hartogs lemma,
(\varphi_{j})
has a subsequence converging in
L^{1}(X)
to a function
\varphi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
with
\varphi\simeq\phi
. Passing to a subsequence if necessary, one can suppose
\varphi_{j}\rightarrow\varphi
in
L^{1}(X)
and almost everywhere.

We are going to show that
\varphi
is the desired solution. A key tool to do this is to prove that
\varphi_{j}\rightarrow\varphi
in capacity. By Lemma
4.3
, it suffices to prove

\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}|e^{\varphi_{j}}-e^{\varphi}|\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}=0.

Recall that
\varphi_{j}\leq\min(u_{j},v_{j})
and
\varphi\leq\min(u,v)
.
On one hand, we have by Theorem
2.1

\displaystyle\int_{\{\min(u,v)>-j\}}|e^{\varphi_{j}}-e^{\varphi}|\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{\{\min(u,v)>-j\}}|e^{\varphi_{j}}-e^{\varphi}|e^{\lambda\varphi_{j}}d\mu

(5.2)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\lambda\sup_{X}u}\int_{X}|e^{\varphi_{j}}-e^{\varphi}|d\mu,

where we have taken
j
large enough so that
\sup_{X}u\geq-j
. On the other hand,

(5.3)

|e^{\varphi}-e^{\varphi_{j}}|\leq\max(e^{\varphi},e^{\varphi_{j}})\leq e^{-j}\;\;\text{on}\;\{\min(u,v)\leq-j\}.

Therefore

\int_{\{\min(u,v)\leq-j\}}|e^{\varphi_{j}}-e^{\varphi}|\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}\leq e^{-j}\int_{X}e^{\lambda(\varphi_{j}-u_{j})}\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}+e^{\lambda(\varphi_{j}-v_{j})}\theta_{v_{j}}^{n}\leq e^{-j}\int_{X}(\omega_{u_{j}}^{n}+\omega_{v_{j}}^{n}).

We now apply the last lemma to obtain

\int_{\{\min(u,v)\leq-j\}}|e^{\varphi_{j}}-e^{\varphi}|\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}\leq Aj^{n}e^{-j},

where
A
is uniform constant that does not depend on
j
.
Using this and (
5.1
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{X}|e^{\varphi_{j}}-e^{\varphi}|\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{\{\min(u,v)>-j\}}|e^{\varphi_{j}}-e^{\varphi}|\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}+\int_{\{\min(u,v)\leq-j\}}|e^{\varphi_{j}}-e^{\varphi}|\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\lambda\sup_{X}u}\int_{X}|e^{\varphi_{j}}-e^{\varphi}|d\mu+Aj^{n}e^{-j}.

for
j
large enough. Letting
j\rightarrow+\infty
, it follows from Lemma
2.6
that

\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}|e^{\varphi_{j}}-e^{\varphi}|\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}=0,

hence
\varphi_{j}\rightarrow\varphi
in capacity by Lemma
4.3
.

We shall prove now that
\varphi
is the desired solution.
Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\varepsilon>0
and consider the functions

f_{k}^{\varepsilon}=\frac{\max(u+k,0)}{\max(u+k,0)+\varepsilon}\;\text{and}\;g_{k}^{\varepsilon}=\frac{\max(v+k,0)}{\max(v+k,0)+\varepsilon}.

These are positive, bounded and quasi-continuous functions. Moreover, Theorem
2.1
gives

f_{k}^{\varepsilon}g_{k}^{\varepsilon}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}=f_{k}^{\varepsilon}g_{k}^{\varepsilon}e^{\varphi_{j}}\mu,\;\;\forall j\geq k.

Fixing
\chi\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(X)
,
\chi\geq 0
and using
[
GZ17a
, Lemma 11.5]
and
[
DDNL25
, Theorem 2.6]
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{X}\chi f_{k}^{\varepsilon}g_{k}^{\varepsilon}\theta_{\varphi}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}\chi f_{k}^{\varepsilon}g_{k}^{\varepsilon}\theta_{\varphi_{j}}^{n}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}\chi f_{k}^{\varepsilon}g_{k}^{\varepsilon}e^{\lambda\varphi_{j}}d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\chi f_{k}^{\varepsilon}g_{k}^{\varepsilon}e^{\lambda\varphi}d\mu.

Letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and then
k\rightarrow+\infty
, we get

\int_{X}\chi\theta_{\varphi}^{n}\leq\int_{X}\chi e^{\lambda\varphi}d\mu.

By our choice of the function
\chi
, this means that
\theta_{\varphi}^{n}\leq e^{\lambda\varphi}\mu
.

On the other hand, the function
\psi:=P_{\theta}(\inf\varphi_{j})
belongs
{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. For every
c
,
\varepsilon>0
, we set

h_{\varepsilon}^{c}=\frac{\max(\psi+c,0)}{\max(\psi+c,0)+\varepsilon}.

Observe that
0\leq h_{\varepsilon}^{c}\leq 1
and
h_{\varepsilon}^{c}\neq 0
only if
\varphi_{j}>-c
for every
j
. Moreover, for
\varphi^{c}=\max(\varphi,-c)
and
\varphi_{j}^{c}=\max(\varphi_{j},-c)
, we have

|\varphi_{j}^{c}-\varphi^{c}|\leq|\varphi_{j}-\varphi|,\;\;\forall j\geq 1,\forall c>0.

Thus, we infer
\varphi_{j}^{c}\rightarrow\varphi^{c}
in capacity. It follows from
[
GZ17a
, Theorem 4.26]
that

\int_{X}h_{\varepsilon}^{c}\theta_{\varphi}^{n}=\int_{X}h_{\varepsilon}^{c}\theta_{\varphi^{c}}^{n}=\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}h_{\varepsilon}^{c}\theta_{\varphi_{j}^{c}}^{n}\geq\limsup_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\{\min(u,v)>-j\}}h_{\varepsilon}^{c}e^{\lambda\varphi_{j}}d\mu.

Using (
5.3
), we obtain

\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\{\min(u,v)\leq-j\}}h_{\varepsilon}^{c}e^{\lambda\varphi_{j}}d\mu=0,

hence

\int_{X}h_{\varepsilon}^{c}\theta_{\varphi}^{n}\geq\limsup_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}h_{\varepsilon}^{c}e^{\lambda\varphi_{j}}d\mu=\int_{X}h_{\varepsilon}^{c}e^{\lambda\varphi}d\mu,

where in the last equality we have used
[
GZ17a
, Lemma 11.5]
.
Letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and then
c\rightarrow+\infty
, we obtain
\int_{X}e^{\lambda\varphi}d\mu\leq\int_{X}\theta_{\varphi}^{n}
. We have shown that
\theta_{\varphi}^{n}\leq e^{\lambda\varphi}\mu
and
\int_{X}e^{\lambda\varphi}d\mu\leq\int_{X}\theta_{\varphi}^{n}
, hence
\theta_{\varphi}^{n}=e^{\lambda\varphi}\mu
, finishing the proof.
∎

We are now in position to prove the existence of weak solutions with model singularities when the right hand side has
L^{p}
-density.

Theorem 5.4
.

Assume
\mu=f\omega^{n}
is a positive Radon measure with
f\in L^{p}(X)
,
p>1
. Then, there is a uniquely determined
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
such that
u\simeq\phi
and

\theta_{u}^{n}=e^{\lambda u}\mu.

To prove the last theorem, we need to establish the following relative
L^{\infty}
estimate.

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
satisfies
\sup_{X}u=0
,
u\simeq\phi
and
\theta_{u}^{n}\leq f\omega^{n}
for some function
f\in L^{p}(X)
with
p>1
. Then
u\geq\phi-C
for a uniform constant
C>0
that only depends on
X
,
\phi
and an upper bound of
\|f\|_{p}
.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that
\phi_{\varepsilon}:=P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\phi)\not\equiv-\infty
. According to Skoda’s uniform integrability theorem (see
[
GZ17b
, Theorem 8.11]
), there exists
\gamma>0
and
1<q<p
such that
e^{-\gamma(\phi_{\varepsilon}+u)}f\in L^{q}(\omega^{n})
. We stress here that
\gamma
and
\|e^{-\gamma(\phi_{\varepsilon}+u)}f\|_{q}
depend only on
(X,\omega)
,
\phi
and
\|f\|_{p}
. By
[
GL23
, Theorem 3.4]
, one can find
c>0
and
v\in{\rm PSH}(X,(1-\varepsilon)\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
such that
\sup_{X}v=0
and

((1-\varepsilon)\theta+v)^{n}=ce^{-\gamma(\phi_{\varepsilon}+u)}f\omega^{n}.

Moreover, by construction of
c
, we know that it is bounded from below by a constant that only depends on
\phi
and an upper bound of
\|f\|_{p}
.

The function defined on
\mathbb{R}_{-}
by
\chi(t)=\frac{-1}{t-1}
is convex, increasing and satisfies
0\leq\chi\leq 1
. For

h:=\phi_{\varepsilon}+v\;\text{and}\;\varphi:=\phi+\chi(h-\phi)=\phi-\frac{1}{h-\phi-1},

we have
h\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
,
\varphi\in L^{1}(X)
and
\phi\leq\varphi\leq\phi+1
. We further have

\displaystyle\theta+dd^{c}\varphi

\displaystyle=\theta+dd^{c}\phi+dd^{c}(\chi(h-\phi))

\displaystyle=\theta+dd^{c}\phi+\chi^{\prime}(h-\phi)dd^{c}(h-\phi)+\chi^{\prime\prime}(h-\phi)d(h-\phi)\wedge d^{c}(h-\phi)

\displaystyle\geq\theta+dd^{c}\phi+\chi^{\prime}(h-\phi)dd^{c}(h-\phi)

\displaystyle=(1-\chi^{\prime}(h-\phi))(\theta+dd^{c}\phi)+\chi^{\prime}(h-\phi)(\theta+dd^{c}h).

Now using that
\chi^{\prime}(t)\leq 1
on
\mathbb{R}_{-}
, we obtain

\theta+dd^{c}\varphi\geq\chi^{\prime}(h-\phi)(\theta+dd^{c}h)\geq 0.

Therefore,
\varphi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
and

(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}\geq\chi^{\prime}(h-\phi)^{n}(\theta+dd^{c}h)^{n}.

Using

\theta_{h}^{n}\geq((1-\varepsilon)\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}\geq ce^{-\gamma\phi_{\varepsilon}}e^{-\gamma u}f\omega^{n},

and

0\leq\phi-h+1\leq-h+1=-\phi_{\varepsilon}-v+1\leq-\phi_{\varepsilon}+C,

we get

\theta_{\varphi}^{n}\geq\frac{1}{(\phi-h+1)^{2n}}ce^{-\gamma\phi_{\varepsilon}}e^{-\gamma u}fdV\geq ace^{-\gamma u}(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n},

where
a
is a positive constant that only depends on
n
and
\gamma
.
Therefore, on the set
\{u<\varphi-3+\log(ac)/\gamma\}
, we have

(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}\leq e^{\gamma(u+1)-\log(ac)}(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}\leq e^{-\gamma}(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}

because
\varphi\leq\phi+1\leq 1
. It then follows from the domination principle (Theorem
3.11
) that
u\geq\varphi-3+\log(ac)/\gamma
, and consequently that
u\geq\phi-2+\log(ac)/\gamma
. The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

We now proceed to the proof of Theorem

By the last lemma, there is

Theorem 5.7
.

Assume
\mu=f\omega^{n}
is a positive Radon measure with density
f\in L^{p}(X)
,
p>1
. Then, there is a unique
c>0
and a function

with the same singularity type as

Proof.

According to the last theorem, for every

, there is
u_{j}\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
such that
u_{j}\simeq\phi
and

\theta_{u_{j}}^{n}=e^{u_{j}/j}\mu.

The functions

satisfy
\sup_{X}v_{j}=0
for every

. Hence, up to extracting a subsequence, one can assume
v_{j}\rightarrow v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
in

. By Lemma
2.6
, we can also assume
e^{v_{j}/j}\to 1
in

. Set

[
GL23
, Theorem 3.4]
, there is

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
\mu
be a positive Radon measure vanishing on pluripolar sets. Then, there is a unique
c>0
and a function
u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
such that
\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}\geq v_{-,\phi}(\theta)
and

(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}=c\mu.

By the last theorem, for every
j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we can find
u_{j}\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
such that

(\theta+dd^{c}{u_{j}})^{n}=e^{u_{j}/j}\mu.

According to
[
ALS24
, Theorem 4.1]
, we can write
\mu=f(\omega+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}

gives

It then follows from Corollary

that

is uniformly bounded. Also, the functions
v_{j}:=u_{j}-\sup_{X}u_{j}
are
\theta
-psh and satisfy
\sup_{X}v_{j}=0
for every

. Hence, up to extracting a subsequence, one can assume

and

2.6

in

. Therefore

\theta_{v_{j}}^{n}=c_{j}e^{v_{j}/j}\mu\rightarrow c\mu\;\;\text{strongly.}

On the other hand, we have
\theta_{v_{j}}^{n}\leq c_{j}\mu\leq C\mu
for some positive constant
C
. This means that
v_{j}
satisfies (
2
). We first obtain by Lemma
4.2
that
v
and
P_{\theta}(\inf_{j}v_{j})
are in
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
. Theorem
4.1
then implies
\theta_{v_{j}}^{n}\rightarrow\theta_{v}^{n}

.

The uniqueness of the constant

follows from Corollary
3.15
.

Corollary 5.10
.

For every
u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
, we have
v_{-,u}(\theta)=v_{-,\phi}(\theta)
.

Proof.

Fix
u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
. According to Proposition
2.8
, we have
v_{-,u}(\theta)\leq v_{-,\phi}(\theta)
. Let

v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
,
u\simeq v
be such that
\int_{X}\theta_{v}^{n}<+\infty
.
By Corollary
5.9
, there is
c>0
and
w\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
such that
\int_{X}\theta_{w}^{n}\geq v_{-,\phi}(\theta)
and

(\theta+dd^{c}w)^{n}=c(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}.

According to Corollary

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
be such that
\int_{X}\theta_{u}^{n}<+\infty

u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
if and only if
P_{\theta}[u]=\phi
.

The proof of the last theorem is based on the following lemma.

Lemma 5.12
.

For every
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
, we have
v_{-,u}(\theta)=v_{-,P_{\theta}[u]}(\theta)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.8
, we always have
v_{-,u}(\theta)\leq v_{-,P_{\theta}[u]}(\theta)
.

We first assume
v_{-,u}(\theta)>0
. On one hand, we get by Proposition
3.10
that
P_{\theta}[u]
is a
\theta
-model potential. On the other hand, Proposition
3.4
implies
u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,P_{\theta}[u])
, thus
v_{-,u}(\theta)=v_{-,P_{\theta}[u]}(\theta)
by Corollary
5.10
.

Assume now
v_{-,u}(\theta)=0
. Fix
\delta>0
and set
\theta_{\delta}=(1+\delta)\theta
. Since
v_{-}(\theta)>0
by assumption, Proposition
2.10
implies
v_{-,u}(\theta_{\delta})>0
. It follows from the above that
v_{-,u}(\theta_{\delta})=v_{-,P_{\theta_{\delta}}[u]}(\theta_{\delta})
. Since

and
P_{\theta}[u]\leq P_{\theta_{\delta}}[u]
, we get by Proposition
2.8
that

v_{-,P_{\theta}[u]}(\theta)\leq\int_{X}(\theta_{\delta}+dd^{c}v)^{n},

for every

with

and then taking the infimum over all functions

. The proof is complete.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem

Global Finite Energy Solutions of the Maxwell-Scalar Field System on the Einstein Cylinder
Jean-Philippe Nicolas, Grigalius Taujanskas

Abstract
. We prove the existence and uniqueness of global finite energy solutions of the Maxwell-scalar field system in Lorenz gauge on the Einstein cylinder ℝ × 𝕊 3 \mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{3} . Our method is a combination of a conformal patching argument, the finite energy existence theorem in Lorenz gauge on Minkowski space of Selberg and Tesfahun, a careful localization of finite energy data, and null form estimates of Foschi–Klainerman type. Although we prove that the energy-carrying components of the solution maintain regularity, due to the incompleteness of the null structure in Lorenz gauge and the nature of our foliation-change arguments we find small losses of regularity in both the scalar field and the potential.

2.
Overview of the Method

Our method relies on the finite energy well-posedness theorem on Minkowski space of Selberg and Tesfahun
[
ST10
]
, the conformal invariance of the Maxwell-scalar field system, a careful localization procedure, and a patching argument similar to that used in
[
Tau19a
]
. The main idea is to use conformal embeddings to cover a strip
I\times\mathbb{S}^{3}\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
for an interval
I
with two copies of Minkowski space,
\mathbb{M}
and
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
, situated antipodally on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
(see
Figure

1
). We then show that finite energy initial data on the Einstein cylinder induces, when suitably modified, finite energy initial data on each of
\mathbb{M}
and
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
. On each of
\mathbb{M}
and
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
we obtain the existence of a finite energy solution using the theorem of Selberg and Tesfahun
[
ST10
]
; this step relies crucially on the locality of the construction of
[
ST10
]
. The locality is a consequence of the Lorenz gauge, in which the Maxwell-scalar field system takes the form of a system of coupled nonlinear wave equations. This is not the case in general; for instance in the Coulomb gauge (which exhibits better null structure properties) the system contains a non-local elliptic equation. The resulting solutions on
\mathbb{M}
and
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
respect the foliations of
\mathbb{M}
and
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
induced by the standard timelike Killing fields
\partial_{t}
and
\partial_{t^{\prime}}
. These foliations are (a) not the same (considered as partial foliations of
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
), and (b) are unsuitable for continuing the solution to the next strip. In order to correctly patch the solutions on the overlap region
\mathbb{M}\cap\mathbb{M}^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
and obtain the appropriate regularity, we must change their foliations to the standard spacelike foliation of
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
. We achieve this by first proving that the right-hand sides of the wave equations for the components of the solution, considered as linear wave equations, possess sufficiently good
L^{2}
-based spacetime Sobolev regularity. This step relies on the null structure in the equations; as already mentioned, however, in Lorenz gauge this null structure is only partial, and we must therefore contend with small losses of regularity that permeate our argument as a result. These losses are, precisely, an arbitrarily small loss of differentiability in the scalar field
\phi
, and slightly more than one half of a derivative in the potential
A_{a}
. The sharpness of these losses is not completely clear (see
Remark

2.1
). Nevertheless, we are able to show that the components which directly carry the energy—the Maxwell field
F_{ab}
and the gauge-covariant derivative
\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi
of
\phi

[
ST10
]
, where the authors find an arbitrarily small loss in the potential already on Minkowski space, but nevertheless obtain a global finite energy solution. Finally, we extend the local-in-time solution on the cylinder
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
globally by iterating the procedure.

The Maxwell-Scalar Field System

5.1.
Basics and conformal symmetry

The conformally invariant Maxwell-scalar field system describes the motion of a massless charged spin-0 particle interacting with an electromagnetic field. The system is given, on a general spacetime
(\mathcalboondox{M},g)
, by the equations

(5.1)

\displaystyle\nabla^{b}F_{ab}=\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\phi}\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi),

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}^{a}\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi+\frac{1}{6}\mathrm{R}\phi=0,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\nabla_{[a}F_{bc]}=0,

where
F_{ab}:\mathcalboondox{M}\to\mathbb{R}
is a 2-form called the
Maxwell field strength
,
\phi:\mathcalboondox{M}\to\mathbb{C}
is a complex scalar field,
\mathrm{D}_{a}=\nabla_{a}+iA_{a}
is the
gauge covariant derivative
,
A_{a}:\mathcalboondox{M}\to\mathbb{R}
is a 1-form called the
Maxwell potential
, and
\mathrm{R}
is the scalar curvature of
(\mathcalboondox{M},g)
. The 2-form
F_{ab}
is given in terms of
A_{a}
by
F_{ab}=2\nabla_{[a}A_{b]}
, which makes equation (
5.3
) trivial (or, conversely, (
5.3
) and the Poincaré lemma imply the existence of the potential
A_{a}
). The system is conformally invariant, i.e. massless, due to the presence of the conformal mass term
\mathrm{R}/6
in (
5.2
). That is, for a smooth
\Omega>0
the conformal rescaling

(5.4)

g_{ab}=\Omega^{2}\tilde{g}_{ab},\qquad A_{a}=\tilde{A}_{a},\qquad F_{ab}=\tilde{F}_{ab},\qquad\phi=\Omega^{-1}\tilde{\phi}

is a symmetry of (
5.1
)–(
5.3
), with the tilded quantities satisfying the same equations if and only if (
5.1
)–(
5.3
) hold. The Lagrangian for the system (
5.1
)–(
5.3
) is

(5.5)

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}F_{ab}F^{ab}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi\overline{\mathrm{D}^{a}\phi}-\frac{1}{12}\mathrm{R}|\phi|^{2},

which also gives rise to the stress-energy tensor

(5.6)

\mathbf{T}_{ab}=-F_{ac}F_{b}{}^{c}+\frac{1}{4}g_{ab}F_{cd}F^{cd}+\overline{\mathrm{D}_{(a}\phi}\mathrm{D}_{b)}\phi-\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}\overline{\mathrm{D}_{c}\phi}\mathrm{D}^{c}\phi+\frac{1}{12}g_{ab}\mathrm{R}|\phi|^{2}.

When the equations (
5.1
)–(
5.3
) are satisfied,
\mathbf{T}_{ab}
satisfies

\nabla^{a}\mathbf{T}_{ab}=\frac{1}{12}|\phi|^{2}\nabla_{b}\mathrm{R},

and so is conserved when
\mathrm{R}
is constant and
\mathbf{T}_{ab}
is sufficiently smooth.

5.2.
Gauge symmetry

In addition to the conformal symmetry (
5.4
), (
5.1
)–(
5.3
) exhibit the
gauge symmetry

(5.7)

A_{a}\rightsquigarrow A_{a}+\nabla_{a}\chi,\qquad\phi\rightsquigarrow\mathrm{e}^{-i\chi}\phi.

Under (
5.7
) the fields
F_{ab}
and
\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi
transform as

F_{ab}\rightsquigarrow F_{ab},\qquad\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi\rightsquigarrow\mathrm{e}^{-i\chi}\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi,

which in particular makes the Lagrangian (
5.5
) and the stress tensor (
5.6
) manifestly gauge invariant. Geometrically, this is the statement that the system (
5.1
)–(
5.3
) is a theory on a principal bundle
P\to\mathcalboondox{M}
with fibre
\mathrm{U}(1)
. The gauge covariant derivative
\mathrm{D}_{a}
is then a connection on
P
, represented by the real
15
15

15

Note that we choose
A_{a}
to be real, i.e. the factor of
i
in
\mathrm{D}_{a}=\nabla_{a}+iA_{a}
comes from the Lie algebra of
\mathrm{U}(1)
being
\mathfrak{u}(1)=i\mathbb{R}
.
1-form
A_{a}
on
\mathcalboondox{M}
in any trivialisation of
P
, and
iF_{ab}=[\mathrm{D}_{a},\mathrm{D}_{b}]
its curvature. The scalar field
\phi
is interpreted as a section of the complex line bundle over
\mathcalboondox{M}
associated to
P
by the representation
\mathrm{e}^{i\chi}
of
\mathrm{U}(1)
, and the gauge transformations (
5.7
) correspond to a change of local trivialization. When written in terms of the potential
A_{a}
, the system (
5.1
)–(
5.3
) takes the form

(5.8)

\displaystyle\Box A_{a}-\nabla_{a}(\nabla_{b}A^{b})+\mathrm{R}_{ab}A^{b}=-\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\phi}\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi),

(5.9)

\displaystyle\Box\phi+2iA_{a}\nabla^{a}\phi+\left(\frac{1}{6}\mathrm{R}-A_{a}A^{a}+i\nabla_{a}A^{a}\right)\phi=0.

Note that in Lorenz gauge
\nabla_{a}A^{a}=0
on
\mathcalboondox{M}
the equations (
5.8
) and (
5.9
) become wave equations for
(A_{a},\phi)
.

5.3.
Time slicing

Let
\tau
be a time function on
\mathcalboondox{M}
. Then the metric
g_{ab}
may be decomposed as

g=N^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}-h,

where
N=(\nabla_{a}\tau\nabla^{a}\tau)^{-1/2}
is the lapse function and
h_{ab}
is a Riemannian metric on the level surfaces
\Sigma_{\tau}
of
\tau
with future-oriented unit normal
T^{a}=N\nabla^{a}\tau
. The flow along
T^{a}
allows to identify all hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{\tau}
with a single
3
-manifold
\Sigma=\Sigma_{0}
and thus defines the product structure
\mathcalboondox{M}=\mathbb{R}_{\tau}\times\Sigma
. This also defines the vector field
\partial_{\tau}
as

\partial_{\tau}=NT^{a}\partial_{a}.

Since we do not need anything more general, we assume that
N=1
and that
h_{ab}
is independent of
\tau
. In particular, this implies that the extrinsic curvature of
\Sigma_{\tau}
vanishes for all
\tau
, and that
\partial_{\tau}
is a Killing vector. Given this decomposition, we then define the
electric
and
magnetic
fields by
\mathbf{E}_{a}=T^{b}F_{ba}
and
\mathbf{B}_{a}=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{abc}F^{bc}
, where
\varepsilon_{abc}
is the volume form of
\Sigma
. We also denote by
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{a}=h_{a}{}^{b}\nabla_{b}
the projection of
\nabla_{b}
to
\Sigma
, which, since
N=1
, is also the Levi-Civita connection of
(\Sigma,h)
. Similarly, we denote by
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}_{a}=h_{a}{}^{b}\mathrm{D}_{b}
the projection of
\mathrm{D}_{a}
to
\Sigma
. The system (
5.1
)–(
5.3
) then takes the form

(MSF)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\partial_{\tau}\mathbf{E}+\boldsymbol{\nabla}\times\mathbf{B}=-\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\phi}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\phi),\\
&\mathrm{D}^{a}\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi+\frac{1}{6}\mathrm{R}\phi=0,\\
&\boldsymbol{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{E}=-\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\phi}\mathrm{D}_{0}\phi),\\
&\boldsymbol{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{B}=0,\end{split}

where
(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\times\mathbf{B})_{a}=\varepsilon_{abc}\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{b}\mathbf{B}^{c}
,
\mathrm{D}_{0}=T^{a}\mathrm{D}_{a}
, and
\boldsymbol{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{E}=h^{ab}\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{a}\mathbf{E}_{b}
. The first two equations in (
MSF
) are dynamical, whereas the third and fourth equations are constraints, and, for sufficiently smooth solutions, are propagated. The system (
MSF
) is manifestly time-reversible, since
(\tau,A_{0},\mathbf{E})\mapsto(-\tau,-A_{0},-\mathbf{E})
is a symmetry. We note also that contracting (
5.6
) with
T^{a}T^{b}
gives rise to the conservation of energy

(5.10)

\mathscr{E}(\tau)=\mathscr{E}(0)

for all
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, where

\mathscr{E}(\tau)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}\left(|\mathbf{E}|^{2}+|\mathbf{B}|^{2}+|\mathrm{D}_{0}\phi|^{2}+|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\phi|^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{R}}{6}|\phi|^{2}\right)\dvol_{\Sigma}.

Here

\mathrm{D}_{0}\phi=\partial_{\tau}\phi+iA_{0}\phi,

where
A_{0}=(\partial_{\tau})^{a}A_{a}
, and

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\phi=\boldsymbol{\nabla}\phi+i\mathbf{A}\phi,

where
\mathbf{A}_{a}=h_{a}^{\phantom{a}b}A_{b}
is the projection of
A_{b}
to
\Sigma
.

5.3.1.
Einstein cylinder

In the case of the Einstein cylinder
(\mathbb{R}_{\tau}\times\mathbb{S}^{3},g)
, the metric is given by
g_{ab}=\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}-h_{ab}
, where
h_{ab}
is the standard round metric on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. The system (
MSF
) assumes the same form with
\mathrm{R}=6
, or, in terms of the potential, (
5.8
)–(
5.9
) become

(5.11)

\displaystyle\Box A_{a}-\nabla_{a}(\nabla_{b}A^{b})-2\mathbf{A}_{a}=-\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\phi}\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi),

(5.12)

\displaystyle\Box\phi+2iA_{a}\nabla^{a}\phi+\left(1-A_{a}A^{a}+i\nabla_{a}A^{a}\right)\phi=0.

In any gauge which expresses
\nabla_{a}A^{a}
in terms of zeroth-order derivatives of
A_{a}
(and perhaps
\phi
)
16
16

16

A trivial example of such a gauge is the Lorenz gauge on the Einstein cylinder,
\nabla_{a}A^{a}=0
. The Lorenz gauge on Minkowski space,

, (
5.11
)–(

) are a system of coupled nonlinear wave equations. In particular, in the Lorenz gauge
\nabla_{a}A^{a}=0

(5.13)

\displaystyle\Box A_{a}-2\mathbf{A}_{a}=-\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\phi}\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi),

(5.14)

\displaystyle\Box\phi+2iA_{a}\nabla^{a}\phi+\left(1-A_{a}A^{a}\right)\phi=0,

and the gauge condition
\nabla_{a}A^{a}=0
replaces the constraints. Indeed, for sufficiently smooth solutions the quantity

satisfies, as a consequence of

) and (

\Box\lambda+|\phi|^{2}\lambda=0,

so the condition

We have in the case of the Einstein cylinder that

\mathscr{E}(\tau)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\{\tau\}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}}\left(|\mathbf{E}|^{2}+|\mathbf{B}|^{2}+|\mathrm{D}_{0}\phi|^{2}+|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\phi|^{2}+|\phi|^{2}\right)\dvol_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that
(\mathbf{E}_{0},\mathbf{B}_{0},U,\phi_{0})
, where

, constitutes a set of finite energy initial data for the system (
MSF
) on

(\mathbf{E}_{0},\mathbf{B}_{0},U,\phi_{0})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3}),

and the constraints

(5.17)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{E}_{0}=-\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\phi}_{0}U_{0}),

(5.18)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{B}_{0}=0

are satisfied in the sense of distributions.

5.3.2.
Minkowski space

In the case of Minkowski space

the metric is the standard metric
\eta_{ab}=\mathrm{d}t^{2}-\delta_{ab}

\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\tilde{A}^{a}=0

(5.22)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Box}\tilde{\mathbf{B}}=\operatorname{Im}\Big(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}\tilde{\phi}\times\tilde{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}\bar{\tilde{\phi}}\Big)+\tilde{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}\times(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}|\tilde{\phi}|^{2})=\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}\boldsymbol{\mathcalboondox{Q}}(\tilde{A}_{a}\tilde{\phi}).

The conserved energy on Minkowski space is given by

(5.23)

\tilde{\mathscr{E}}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\{t\}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\tilde{\mathbf{E}}|^{2}+|\tilde{\mathbf{B}}|^{2}+|\tilde{\mathrm{D}}_{0}\tilde{\phi}|^{2}+|\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}}\tilde{\phi}|^{2}\right)\dvol_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{0},\tilde{\mathbf{B}}_{0},\tilde{U},\tilde{\phi}_{0})
be a set of finite energy initial data for the system (
MSF

M

) in the sense of
Definition

5.2
. Then there exists a gauge which leaves the condition
\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\tilde{A}^{a}=0
unchanged, sets

(5.27)

(\tilde{a}_{0},\dot{\tilde{a}}_{0})=(\tilde{A}_{0},\partial_{t}\tilde{A}_{0})|_{t=0}=(0,0),

and reduces the initial data set to

(\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{0},\tilde{\mathbf{B}}_{0},\tilde{\phi}_{1},\tilde{\phi}_{0})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),

where
\tilde{\phi}_{1}=\partial_{t}\tilde{\phi}|_{t=0}
, with the estimates

\displaystyle\|\dot{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\|\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\mathbf{a}}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\|\tilde{\mathbf{B}}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\phi}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\|\tilde{U}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\phi}_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leqslant 2\|\tilde{\mathbf{U}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+C(1+\|\tilde{\mathbf{B}}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})})\|\tilde{\phi}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

and the constraint (
5.25

(5.28)

\tilde{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{0}=-\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\tilde{\phi}}_{0}\tilde{\phi}_{1}).

Proof.

The residual gauge freedom in Lorenz gauge is given by
\tilde{A}_{a}\rightsquigarrow\tilde{A}_{a}+\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\chi
,
\tilde{\phi}\rightsquigarrow\mathrm{e}^{-i\chi}\tilde{\phi}
, where
\chi
satisfies

\widetilde{\Box}\chi=0,\qquad(\chi,\partial_{t}\chi)|_{t=0}=(\chi_{0},\chi_{1}).

We set
\chi_{1}=-\tilde{a}_{0}
and, using the Lorenz gauge condition
\partial_{t}\tilde{A}_{0}=\tilde{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{A}}
,
\chi_{0}=-\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}^{-1}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{a}}
, where
\tilde{\mathbf{a}}=\tilde{\mathbf{A}}|_{t=0}
(here

denotes any solution

\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}u=f
). Then clearly
\tilde{a}_{0}\rightsquigarrow 0

so

and, by integrating by parts,

\|\tilde{\mathbf{a}}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\|\tilde{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}\times\tilde{\mathbf{a}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|\tilde{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{a}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\|\tilde{\mathbf{B}}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Finally, writing

\tilde{U}_{0}=\tilde{\phi}_{1}+i\tilde{a}_{0}\phi_{0}=\tilde{\phi}_{1}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

for which we have the estimate

\|\tilde{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}\tilde{\phi}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leqslant 2\|\tilde{\mathbf{U}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+C\|\tilde{\mathbf{a}}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\tilde{\phi}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

due to Selberg and Tesfahun
[
ST10

5.4.
Gauge fixing on

Lemma 5.4

The gauge transformations which fix

a_{0}\rightsquigarrow 0,\qquad\dot{a}_{0}\rightsquigarrow\dot{a}_{0}+\partial_{\tau}^{2}\chi|_{\tau=0}=\dot{a}_{0}+\boldsymbol{\Delta}\chi|_{\tau=0}=0,

and we have from

\mathcal{R}_{R,\tau_{*}}\cup\mathcal{R}_{R,\tau_{*}}^{\prime}

\mathcal{R}_{R,\tau_{*}}\cap\mathcal{R}_{R,\tau_{*}}^{\prime}
. Observe that the overlap region is the domain of dependence of
B_{R}\cap J(B_{R})=B_{R}\cap B_{R}^{\prime}
, the latter intersection being characterized by

\pi-2\arctan R<\zeta<2\arctan R

and non-empty if and only if
R>1
(and approaching the full range
(0,\pi)

\Sigma_{\tau}^{\prime}

\Sigma_{\tau}

(11.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\nabla^{a}A_{a}=2\Upsilon^{a}A_{a},\\
&\nabla^{a}A_{a}^{\prime}=2\Upsilon^{a}A_{a}^{\prime}\end{split}

preserves regularity, i.e. we have

\displaystyle F_{ab},\,F_{ab}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,\tau_{*}];L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau}\cap\Sigma_{\tau}^{\prime})),

\displaystyle\phi,\,\phi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,\tau_{*}];H^{1-\delta}(\Sigma_{\tau}\cap\Sigma_{\tau}^{\prime}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,\tau_{*}];H^{-\delta}(\Sigma_{\tau}\cap\Sigma_{\tau}^{\prime})),

\displaystyle A_{a},\,A_{a}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,\tau_{*}];H^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}(\Sigma_{\tau}\cap\Sigma_{\tau}^{\prime}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,\tau_{*}];H^{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}(\Sigma_{\tau}\cap\Sigma_{\tau}^{\prime})),

and under the pullback along
\iota_{\Omega}

A_{a}
and

are in the Minkowskian Lorenz gauge on
\mathcal{R}_{R,\tau_{*}}\cap\mathcal{R}_{R,\tau_{*}}^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{M}
. The gauge change (

11.2
, but, by the argument in the previous paragraph, we can fix

i^{0}
and
(i^{0})^{\prime}
). By construction, these solutions have the data on

given by

have data on

5.3
and our choice of initial residual gauge fixing. Write
\mathcalboondox{O}=\mathcal{R}_{R,\tau_{*}}\cap\mathcal{R}_{R,\tau_{*}}^{\prime}

and

\tilde{\mathcalboondox{E}}^{s}_{k}(\mathcalboondox{O})=\bigcap_{l=0}^{k}\mathcal{C}^{l}([0,t_{*}];H^{s-l}(\Sigma_{t}\cap\mathcalboondox{O})),

where

\{\Sigma_{t}\}_{t}
is the foliation of
\mathcalboondox{O}
by the leaves of constant

Theorem

7.1
,

F_{ab},\,F_{ab}^{\prime}\in\mathcalboondox{E}^{1}_{0}(\mathcalboondox{O})\cap\tilde{\mathcalboondox{E}}^{1}_{0}(\mathcalboondox{O}),

and

By the uniqueness clause in

7.1
(in the spaces
\tilde{\mathcalboondox{E}}^{1}_{0}

\tilde{\mathcalboondox{E}}^{1}_{1}

\tilde{\mathcalboondox{E}}^{1-\delta}_{1}
for the Maxwell field, scalar field, and potential, respectively), we have

,
\phi=\phi^{\prime}
and
A_{a}=A_{a}^{\prime}
.

Uniqueness.
Uniqueness follows by domain of dependence arguments and precisely the same argument as for agreement on the overlap region
\mathcalboondox{O}
in the above paragraph.

Conservation of energy.
Regularize the initial data to obtain a set of smooth data

(\mathbf{E}_{0}^{(n)},\mathbf{B}^{(n)}_{0},U^{(n)},\phi_{0}^{(n)})

which converges to
(\mathbf{E}_{0},\mathbf{B}_{0},U,\phi_{0})
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})^{4}
. Integrating

\nabla^{a}(T^{b}\mathbf{T}_{ab}^{(n)})=0

on the strip
[0,\tau]\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
,
0\leqslant\tau\leqslant\tau_{*}
, where
\mathbf{T}_{ab}^{(n)}
is the stress energy tensor (
5.6
) on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
corresponding to the (smooth) solution with index
n
, gives

\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\tau)=\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(0)

for the energy (
5.15
) corresponding to the solution with index
n
. By construction, we immediately have
\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(0)\to\mathscr{E}(0)
, so it remains to show that
\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\tau)
approaches
\mathscr{E}(\tau)
; for this, it is enough to show that this is true locally, i.e. in each Minkowskian region. For each
\tau
, write
\{\tau\}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
as a union of disjoint (incomplete) hypersurfaces
\overline{\Sigma_{\tau}^{1}}\cup\Sigma_{\tau}^{2}
such that
\Sigma_{\tau}^{1}\in\mathcal{R}_{R,\tau_{*}}
and
\Sigma_{\tau}^{2}\in\mathcal{R}_{R,\tau_{*}}^{\prime}
. We show that on each piece
\Sigma_{\tau}^{k}

\displaystyle\|F_{ab}^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau}^{k})}\to\|F_{ab}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau}^{k})},

\displaystyle\|(\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi)^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau}^{k})}\to\|\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau}^{k})},

\displaystyle\|\phi^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau}^{k})}\to\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau}^{k})}.

Indeed, note that in
\mathcal{R}_{R,\tau_{*}}
, the solution
(\phi^{(n)},A_{a}^{(n)},F_{ab}^{(n)})
has an associated smooth Minkowskian solution
(\tilde{\phi}^{(n)},\tilde{A}_{a}^{(n)},\tilde{F}_{ab}^{(n)})
which converges in the energy norm with respect to the Minkowskian foliation on
\mathcal{R}_{R,\tau_{*}}
(cf.
Remark

7.5
). Observe that for any function
f\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
on
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
,

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Sigma^{k}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma^{k}_{\tau}}|f(t(\tau,\zeta),r(\tau,\zeta),\omega)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\,\mathrm{d}^{2}\omega

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Sigma^{k}_{\tau}}\sup_{t}|f(t,r(\tau,\zeta),\omega)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\,\mathrm{d}^{2}\omega

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t}\int|f(t,r(\tau,\zeta),\omega)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r|_{\tau}\,\mathrm{d}^{2}\omega

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}},

where the penultimate inequality follows from the fact that locally
C^{-1}\leqslant\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{\mathrm{d}\zeta}|_{\tau}\leqslant C
for some
C>0
. We therefore have, using the uniform boundedness of all metric components and the conformal factor
\Omega
on and in a neighbourhood of
\Sigma_{\tau}^{1}
,

(11.8)

\|F_{ab}^{(n)}-F_{ab}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau}^{1})}\lesssim\|\tilde{F}_{ab}^{(n)}-\tilde{F}_{ab}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\to 0,

(11.9)

\|\phi^{(n)}-\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau}^{1})}\lesssim\|\tilde{\phi}^{(n)}-\tilde{\phi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\to 0,

and

(11.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|(\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi)^{(n)}-\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau}^{1})}&\leqslant\|\Omega^{-1}((\tilde{\mathrm{D}}_{a}\tilde{\phi})^{(n)}-\tilde{\mathrm{D}}_{a}\tilde{\phi})\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau}^{1})}+\|\nabla_{a}(\Omega^{-1})(\tilde{\phi}^{(n)}-\tilde{\phi})\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau}^{1})}\\
&\lesssim\|(\tilde{\mathrm{D}}_{a}\tilde{\phi})^{(n)}-\tilde{\mathrm{D}}_{a}\tilde{\phi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}+\|\tilde{\phi}^{(n)}-\tilde{\phi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\\
&\to 0.\end{split}

The same argument (but instead pulling the solution back to
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
) applies on
\Sigma_{\tau}^{2}
, and hence we have in particular
\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\tau)\to\mathscr{E}(\tau)
. Moreover, the final estimate also shows that
\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi\in L^{\infty}([0,\tau_{*}];L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3}))
is a limit in
L^{\infty}([0,\tau_{*}];L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3}))
of smooth functions
(\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi)^{(n)}
, so in particular is in
\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,\tau_{*}];L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3}))
.
∎

Remark 11.5

The estimates (
11.8

11.9

Theorem

11.4.
Global solution

To extend the solution globally, we iterate the solution obtained in
Theorem

11.4
with the same time of existence
\tau_{*}
; this is justified by the conservation of energy. Applying
Theorem

11.4
from
\tau=\tau_{*}
, the solution on the next strip will satisfy

(A_{0},\partial_{\tau}A_{0})|_{\tau=\tau_{*}}=(0,0),

so it only remains to check that the gauge transformation which sends
(A_{0},\partial_{\tau}A_{0})|_{\tau=\tau_{*}}\rightsquigarrow(0,0)
for the solution in
Theorem

11.4
preserves regularity. Once this is known, we then construct the solution on the time interval
[0,\tau_{*}]
and change residual gauge to
(A_{0},\partial_{\tau}A_{0})|_{\tau=\tau_{*}}=(0,0)
, and construct the solution on
[\tau_{*},2\tau_{*}]
. We then reverse this gauge transformation to return to the initial gauge condition
(a_{0},\dot{a}_{0})=(0,0)
, preserving the continuity in time of the solution at
\tau=\tau_{*}
. The required gauge transformation is
A_{a}\rightsquigarrow A_{a}+\nabla_{a}\chi
, where
\chi
satisfies

\displaystyle\Box\chi=0,

\displaystyle(\chi,\partial_{\tau}\chi)|_{\tau=\tau_{*}}=(-\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{-1}(\partial_{\tau}A_{0}|_{\tau_{*}}),-A_{0}|_{\tau_{*}})\in H^{\frac{3}{2}-\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3})\times H^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3}).

By the energy inequality (
4.25
),

(11.11)

\chi\in\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,\tau_{*}];H^{\frac{3}{2}-\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,\tau_{*}];H^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3})).

This clearly preserves the regularity of the potential, i.e.

A_{a}\in\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,\tau_{*}];H^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,\tau_{*}];H^{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3}))

in the new gauge. Since
F_{ab}
is gauge-invariant, it immediately inherits the regularity
F_{ab}\in\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,\tau_{*}];L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3}))
. Notably, however, here
\chi(\tau,\cdot)
is not regular enough to be bounded on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. By
Lemma

4.2
(using a partition of unity on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
and applying the lemma to
x\mapsto\cos x-1
and
x\mapsto\sin x
), we have

\mathrm{e}^{-i\chi}\in\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,\tau_{*}];H^{\varrho_{1}}(\mathbb{S}^{3})),

where

\varrho_{1}=\frac{3}{2(1+\delta)}.

Using
Lemma

4.4
,

\|\mathrm{e}^{-i\chi}\phi\|_{H^{1-4\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}\lesssim\|\mathrm{e}^{-i\chi}\|_{H^{\varrho_{1}}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}\|\phi\|_{H^{1-\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3})},

and similarly

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\tau}(\mathrm{e}^{-i\chi}\phi)\|_{H^{-4\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathrm{e}^{-i\chi}\partial_{\tau}\phi\|_{H^{-3\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}+\|\mathrm{e}^{-i\chi}\phi\partial_{\tau}\chi\|_{H^{-4\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathrm{e}^{-i\chi}\|_{H^{\varrho_{1}}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}\|\partial_{\tau}\phi\|_{H^{-\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}+\|\phi\|_{H^{1-\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}\|\mathrm{e}^{-i\chi}\partial_{\tau}\chi\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}-3\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathrm{e}^{-i\chi}\|_{H^{\varrho_{1}}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}\|\partial_{\tau}\phi\|_{H^{-\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}+\|\phi\|_{H^{1-\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}\|\mathrm{e}^{-i\chi}\|_{H^{\varrho_{1}}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}\|\partial_{\tau}\chi\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}.

Hence in the new gauge
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,\tau_{*}];H^{1-4\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,\tau_{*}];H^{-4\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3}))
. It remains to treat the gauge covariant derivative
\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi
. This transforms as
\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi\rightsquigarrow\mathrm{e}^{-i\chi}\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi
, so, given the regularity (
11.11
), it may appear like some regularity in
\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi
may be lost. This is not the case, however, since the
L^{2}
norm of
\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi
is gauge-invariant:

\|\mathrm{e}^{-i\chi}\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}=\|\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})},

so
\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,\tau_{*}];L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3}))
in the new gauge.

By applying the above gauge transformation to the solution on
[0,\tau_{*}]
, then solving from
\tau=\tau_{*}
using
Theorem

11.4
, and subsequently reversing the above gauge transformation on
[0,2\tau_{*}]
, we obtain a solution on
[0,2\tau_{*}]
which satisfies
(a_{0},\dot{a}_{0})=(0,0)
,

A_{a}\in\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,2\tau_{*}];H^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,2\tau_{*}];H^{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3})),

F_{ab},~\mathrm{D}_{a}\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,2\tau_{*}];L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})),

and

\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,2\tau_{*}];H^{1-16\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,2\tau_{*}];H^{-16\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3})).

Iterating this procedure, the scalar field on
[0,n\tau_{*}]
has spatial regularity
H^{1-4^{n}\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3})
. Pick a (sufficiently small)
\delta>0
. Then, since the
\delta>0
in the above procedure is arbitrarily small (and, in particular, does not depend on
\tau_{*}
), we can construct a sequence of solutions indexed by
n
, with time of existence
[0,n\tau_{*}]
, such that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
the scalar field has spatial regularity
H^{1-2^{-n}\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3})
(by choosing
\delta_{n}=2^{-3n}\delta
). Since
\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}H^{1-2^{-n}\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3})\subset H^{1-\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{3})
, by the uniqueness part of
Theorem

11.4
, these are all the same solution in the regularity class given by the chosen
\delta>0
, with time of existence
[0,n\tau_{*}]
. Induction on
n
therefore gives a global solution in the regularity class given by
\delta>0
.

This completes the proof of
Theorem

6.1
. ∎

The following proofs follow a strategy similar to that used in

WR22

.

12.1.
Proof of
Lemma

10.1

The statement follows directly from the energy inequality for the linear wave equation (
4.25
) with
\sigma=s
and the fact that for
s\geqslant 0

H^{s}_{\text{loc}}(\mathcalboondox{M})\hookrightarrow L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{\tau};H^{s}(S_{\tau})).

12.2.
Proof of
Lemma

10.2

We prove this using a duality argument. For
s\in[-1,0)
, let

\eta\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{\tau};H^{-s-1}(S_{\tau}))

and consider the dual Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\Box_{g}\chi=\eta,\\
(\chi,\partial_{\tau}\chi)|_{\tau=T}=(0,0)\end{cases}

for any given
T>0
. By the energy inequality,
\chi\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{\tau};H^{-s}(S_{\tau}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\tau};H^{-s-1}(S_{\tau}))
. This in turn implies
\chi\in H^{-s}_{\text{loc}}(\mathcalboondox{M})
. Indeed, this property can be shown on
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
in Fourier space by observing that

(1+\tau^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{|s|}\leqslant(\langle\tau\rangle^{2}+\langle\xi\rangle^{2})^{|s|}\leqslant\langle\xi\rangle^{2|s|}\left(1+\frac{\langle\tau\rangle^{2}}{\langle\xi\rangle^{2}}\right)=\langle\xi\rangle^{2|s|}+\langle\tau\rangle^{2}\langle\xi\rangle^{2|s|-2},

which proves the embedding

L^{2}(\mathbb{R};H^{|s|}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap H^{1}(\mathbb{R};H^{|s|-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\hookrightarrow H^{|s|}(\mathbb{R}^{4}).

Using cut-offs in time, we infer

\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathbb{R};H^{|s|}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R};H^{|s|-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\hookrightarrow H^{|s|}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}).

The embedding

(12.1)

\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{\tau};H^{|s|}(S_{\tau}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\tau};H^{|s|-1}(S_{\tau}))\hookrightarrow H^{|s|}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcalboondox{M})

can be obtained using local charts on
S_{\tau}\simeq S
and a partition of unity. In what follows let us write
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
to denote the appropriate duality pairing; by
\langle u,\eta\rangle
, for example, we mean the duality pairing between
L^{\infty}([0,T]_{\tau};H^{s+1}(S_{\tau}))
and
L^{1}([0,T]_{\tau};H^{-s-1}(S_{\tau}))
. We then have

\displaystyle\langle u,\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u,\Box\chi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f,\chi\rangle+\langle u,\partial_{\tau}\chi\rangle|_{S_{0}}-\langle\partial_{\tau}u,\chi\rangle|_{S_{0}},

so

\displaystyle|\langle u,\eta\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s}([0,T]_{\tau}\times S)}\|\chi\|_{H^{-s}([0,T]_{\tau}\times S)}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s+1}(S_{0})}\|\partial_{\tau}\chi\|_{H^{-s-1}(S_{0})}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s}(S_{0})}\|\chi\|_{H^{-s}(S_{0})}.

Since
\chi\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{\tau};H^{-s}(S_{\tau}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\tau};H^{-s-1}(S_{\tau}))
, we have, by (
12.1
) and the energy inequality (
4.25
), that

\displaystyle\|\chi\|_{H^{-s}([0,T]_{\tau}\times S)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\chi\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]_{\tau};H^{-s}(S_{\tau}))}+\|\partial_{\tau}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]_{\tau};H^{-s-1}(S_{\tau}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\eta\|_{L^{1}([0,T]_{\tau};H^{-s-1}(S_{\tau}))},

and so, again using the energy inequality for
\chi
,

|\langle u,\eta\rangle|\lesssim\left(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s+1}(S_{0})}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s}(S_{0})}+\|f\|_{H^{s}([0,T]_{\tau}\times S)}\right)\|\eta\|_{L^{1}([0,T]_{\tau};H^{-s-1}(S_{\tau}))}.

This implies that

\phi\in L^{\infty}([0,T]_{\tau};H^{s+1}(S_{\tau}))

and

(12.2)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]_{\tau};H^{s+1}(S_{\tau}))}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s+1}(S_{0})}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s}(S_{0})}+\|f\|_{H^{s}([0,T]_{\tau}\times S)}.

Continuity in
\tau
then follows easily by approximating
u
by smooth functions and noting the above bound for its
L^{\infty}([0,T]_{\tau};H^{s+1}(S_{\tau}))
norm.

∎

Soliton Shielding of the focusing modified KdV equation
Ruihong Ma, Engui Fan ∗,

Abstract
We consider soliton gas solutions of the modified Korteweg-de Vries (mKdV) equation, where the point spectrum of the condensate is located within a bounded domain in the upper half-plane. We first demonstrate that when the domain is a quadrature and the soliton density is an analytic function, the corresponding deterministic soliton gas coincides with a finite number of solitons, which we call this effect soliton shielding. When the domain is an ellipse and the soliton density is analytic, the corresponding deterministic soliton gas reduces the spectral data to the segment joining the foci. The initial datum of this Cauchy problem is asymptotically step-like oscillatory, described by a periodic elliptic function as x → + ∞ x\to+\infty , and it vanishes exponentially fast as x → − ∞ x\to-\infty .

Solitons are elementary, localized solutions to nonlinear evolution equations. They manifest as individual entities, embodying traveling wave solutions that maintain their shape during propagation. Alternatively, solitons can emerge as collectives, evolving as an ensemble and asymptotically decomposing into separate solutions and potential sub-ensembles of solitons. Following the discovery of soliton ensembles, their interpretation as particle-like entities has been a source of novel investigations.

The notion of soliton gas, an infinite statistical ensemble of interacting solitons, was first introduced by Zakharov
[
17
]
, who derived a kinetic equation for a “rarefied" gas of Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) solitons by considering the modification of the soliton velocity due to the position shifts in its pairwise collisions with other solitons in the gas. The generalization of Zakharov’s kinetic equation to the case of the KdV soliton gas of finite density was obtained by considering the infinite-phase, thermodynamic-type limit of the Whithan modulation equation
[
18
]

[
19
]
, defocusing and resonant NLS equation
[
20

[
21

and mKdV equation
[

[
18

served as a motivation for a more intuitive, physical construction of the kinetic equation for a dense soliton gas of the focusing NLS equation in
[
23

N

N\to+\infty

[
24

The notable emergent characteristic observed is that, as
N\to\infty

[
25
]

When the domain is an ellipse, soliton shielding reduces the spectral data to the soliton density concentrating between the foci of the ellipse. The

From

The aim of this paper is to demonstrate the soliton shielding effect. Our attention is directed towards cases where the spectrum is given by

\mathcal{Z}=\left\{\{z_{k},c_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N_{1}},\{\kappa_{j},h_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\right\},\,\,\,N=N_{1}+N_{2},

2.1
The deterministic soliton gas for the mKdV equation

In this section, our goal is to derive the soliton shielding for a domain
\mathcal{D}
that is either a quadrature or an ellipse. Initially, we consider the deterministic soliton gas for the mKdV equation as the number of solitons
N
approaches infinity. This derivation is conducted under the assumption that the norming constants
c_{j}
are scaled as
\mathcal{O}(1/N)

N
-soliton solution from the spectral data
\mathcal{Z}

N
-soliton solution. The RH problem is characterized as follows

29
]

RH problem 2.1
.

Find an analytic function
M(z):\mathbb{C}\to SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
with the following properties.

1.

M(z)=I+\mathcal{O}(z^{-1})
as
z\to\infty
.

2.

M(z)
has simple poles at each soliton
\kappa_{j}\in{\mathcal{{Z}}}
at which

\displaystyle\underset{\kappa=\kappa_{j}}{{\rm Res}}M(z)=\underset{\kappa\to\kappa_{j}}{\lim}M(z)\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
h_{j}e^{2i\theta(z)}&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\underset{\kappa=\bar{\kappa}_{j}}{{\rm Res}}M(z)=\underset{\kappa\to\bar{\kappa}_{j}}{\lim}M(z)\begin{pmatrix}0&-\bar{h}_{j}e^{-2i\theta(\bar{z})}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

M(z)
has simple poles at each breather
z_{k}\in{\mathcal{{Z}}}
at which

\displaystyle\underset{z=z_{k}}{{\rm Res}}M(z)=\underset{z\to z_{k}}{\lim}M(z)\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
c_{j}e^{2i\theta(z)}&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\underset{z=\bar{z}_{k}}{{\rm Res}}M(z)=\underset{z\to\bar{z}_{k}}{\lim}M(z)\begin{pmatrix}0&-\bar{c}_{j}e^{-2i\theta(\bar{z})}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\underset{z=-\bar{z}_{k}}{{\rm Res}}M(z)=\underset{z\to-\bar{z}_{k}}{\lim}M(z)\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
-\bar{c}_{j}e^{2i\theta(z)}&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\underset{z=-z_{k}}{{\rm Res}}M(z)=\underset{z\to-z_{k}}{\lim}M(z)\begin{pmatrix}0&c_{j}je^{-2i\theta(\bar{z})}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},

where
\theta(z)=xz+4tz^{3}
. The
N
-soliton solution
q_{N}(x,t)
can be determined by

q_{N}(x,t)=2i\lim_{|z|\to\infty}(zM(z))_{12}.

(2.1)

We initially employ a transformation to eliminate the singularities of
M(z)
. Specifically, let
\gamma
be a closed, anticlockwise-oriented contour that encompasses all the breathers
\{(z_{k},-\bar{z}_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N_{1}}
and solitons
\{\kappa_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{2}}
in the upper half-plane, with
\mathcal{D}
denoting the finite domain bounded by
\partial\mathcal{D}=\gamma
. Similarly, we define
\bar{\gamma}
as a closed, clockwise-oriented contour that encircles all the breathers
\{(\bar{z}_{k},-z_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N_{1}}
and solitons
\{\bar{\kappa}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{2}}
in the lower half-plane, where
\bar{\mathcal{D}}
represents the finite domain delimited by
\bar{\gamma}
.

Subsequently, we reformulate the jump conditions into the following RH problem.

RH problem 2.2
.

Find an analytic function
M(z):\mathbb{C}\,\setminus(\gamma\,\cup\,\bar{\gamma})\to SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
with the following properties.

1.

M(z)=I+\mathcal{O}(z^{-1})
as
z\to\infty
.

2.

For
z\in\gamma\,\cup\,\bar{\gamma}
, the boundary values
M_{\pm}(z)

M_{+}(z)=M_{-}(z)V(z),

0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad z\in\bar{\gamma}.\end{cases}

We consider the limit as the number of solitons approaches infinity, with their point spectrum
\mathcal{Z}
uniformly filling the domain
\mathcal{D}
. It is assumed that the norming constants
c_{k}
and

are scaled by a smooth function

(2.2)

where

denotes the characteristic function of the domain

, and let

represent the area of

. On the physical side, scaling the norming constants to be small implies that the individual solitons are centered at positions that are logarithmically large in

-plane, only the tails of the solitons contribute to the sum

]

Upon taking the limit

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N_{1}}\frac{c_{k}}{z-z_{k}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{1}}\frac{S}{\pi N_{1}}\frac{\alpha_{1}(z_{k},\bar{z}_{k})}{z-z_{k}}\chi_{\mathcal{D}}(z_{k})\xrightarrow{N_{1}\to\infty}\iint_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{\alpha_{1}(w,\bar{w})}{z-w}\frac{d^{2}w}{\pi},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\frac{h_{j}}{\kappa-\kappa_{j}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\frac{S}{\pi N_{2}}\frac{\alpha_{2}(\kappa_{j},\bar{\kappa}_{j})}{\kappa-\kappa_{j}}\chi_{\mathcal{D}}(\kappa_{j})\xrightarrow{N_{2}\to\infty}\iint_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{\alpha_{2}(w,\bar{w})}{z-w}\frac{d^{2}w}{\pi},

Consequently, we arrive at a limiting RH problem as following.

RH problem 2.3
.

Find an analytic function
M^{\infty}(z):\mathbb{C}\,\setminus(\gamma\cup\bar{\gamma})\to SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
with the following properties.

1.

M^{\infty}(z)=I+\mathcal{O}(z^{-1})
as
z\to\infty
.

2.

For
z\in\gamma\cup\bar{\gamma}
, the boundary values

The limiting mKdV soliton is derived from the matrix

q_{\infty}(x,t)=2i\lim_{|z|\to\infty}(zM^{\infty}(z))_{12}.

2.2
Shielding of soliton gas for Quadrature domain

We initiate our analysis by considering the class of quadrature domains defined by:

\mathcal{D}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\left|(z-d_{0})^{m}-d_{1}\right|<\rho\right\},\quad m\in\mathbb{N},

(2.7)

where
d_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
and
|d_{1}|,\rho>0
are sufficiently small to ensure that
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{C}^{+}
. The parameter
m
is associated with the symmetry of the domain.
For a domain
\mathcal{D}
that is sufficiently smooth and simply connected, its boundary
\partial\mathcal{D}
can be described using the Schwarz function
S(z)
associated with
\mathcal{D}
, given by the equation:

\bar{z}=S(z),\quad S(z)=\bar{d}_{0}+\left(\bar{d}_{1}+\frac{\rho^{2}}{(z-d_{0})^{m}-d_{1}}\right)^{1/m}.

For a quadrature domain
\mathcal{D}
and smooth functions
\alpha_{\ell}(z,\bar{z})
for
\ell=1,2

\alpha_{\ell}(z,\bar{z})=N(\bar{z}-\bar{d}_{0})^{N-1}\varpi_{\ell}(z),

with
\varpi_{\ell}(z)
being analytic in
\mathcal{D}
, we can apply Green’s theorem for

and derive

\iint_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{e^{2i\theta(w)}\alpha_{\ell}(z,\bar{z})\,d^{2}w}{\pi(z-w)}=\int_{\partial\mathcal{D}}\frac{\varpi_{\ell}(w)(\bar{w}-\bar{d}_{0})^{n}e^{2i\theta(w)}}{z-w}\frac{dw}{2\pi i},

(2.9)

and a similar expression holds for the integral over the complement of
\mathcal{D}
, denoted by
\bar{\mathcal{D}}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let the quadrature domain
\mathcal{D}
be defined as in equation (

). Then, the gas solution

given by (

) coincides with the
N

provided in (
2.1
), which is the solution set of the equation

(z-d_{0})^{N}=d_{1}.

The corresponding norming constants are given by

\displaystyle c_{k}=\frac{\rho^{2}\varpi_{1}(z_{k})}{\prod_{s\neq k}(z_{k}-z_{s})},\quad k=1,\dots,N_{1},

\displaystyle\kappa_{j}=\frac{\rho^{2}\varpi_{2}(\kappa_{j})}{\prod_{m\neq j}(\kappa_{j}-\kappa_{m})},\quad j=1,\dots,N_{2}.

Here,
\varpi_{j}
for

are provided in equation
(
2.8
)
.

Proof.

The solution
q_{\infty}(x,t)
given in equation (
2.6
) can be expressed as

M^{\infty}=I+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma\cup\bar{\gamma}}\frac{\mu(s)(V^{\infty}(s)-I)}{s-z}\,ds,

where

\mu=(I-C_{w})^{-1}I,\quad C_{w}f=C_{-}(f(V^{\infty}-I)),

and the Cauchy projection operator is defined as

(C_{\pm}f)(z)=\lim_{z^{\prime}\to z}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma\cup\bar{\gamma}}\frac{f(s)}{s-z^{\prime}}\,ds.

Initially, we examine the scenario to solitons. Employing a similar approach, we can derive the corresponding solution for breathers. This solution is extracted from (
2.9
) by setting
m=N_{2}
in (
2.7
). Subsequently, we substitute
\bar{w}=S(w)
into the contour integral (

) and invoke the residue theorem at the

poles, which are determined by the solutions

of the equation

. As a result, we obtain the following:

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}\frac{\left(\bar{w}-\bar{d}_{0}\right)^{N_{2}}\varpi_{2}(w)\mathrm{e}^{2i\theta(w)}}{z-w}\frac{dw}{2\pi i}

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma}\left(S(w)-\bar{d}_{0}\right)^{N_{2}}\varpi_{2}(w)\frac{\mathrm{e}^{2i\theta(w)}}{z-w}\frac{dw}{2\pi i}

\displaystyle=\rho^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\varpi_{2}\left(\kappa_{j}\right)}{\prod_{s\neq j}\left(\kappa_{j}-\kappa_{s}\right)}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{2it\theta\left(\kappa_{j}\right)}}{z-\kappa_{j}},\quad z\notin\mathcal{D}.

(2.10)

The results then follow immediately.

2.3
Shielding of soliton gas for Elliptic domian

In this section we consider the case where
\mathcal{D}
is an ellipse. Assuming the foci
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are on the imaginary axis, we have
E_{1}=i\eta_{1}
and
E_{2}=i\eta_{2}
with
\eta_{2}>\eta_{1}>0
. The ellipse equation is:

\sqrt{{\rm Re}^{2}\,z+({\rm Im}\,z-\eta_{1})^{2}}+\sqrt{{\rm Re}^{2}\,z+({\rm Im}\,z-\eta_{2})^{2}}=2\rho>0,

(2.11)

which positioned in the upper half-plane by selecting
\rho
to be sufficiently small. The boundary
\partial\mathcal{D}
is characterized by the Schwartz function
S(z)
of the ellipse, given by:

\bar{z}=S(z)=\left(1-\frac{8\rho^{2}}{(\eta_{2}-\eta_{1})^{2}}\right)(z+\frac{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{2i})+2\frac{\rho}{c^{2}}\sqrt{\rho^{2}-c^{2}}\tilde{S}(z),

(2.12)

where
\tilde{S}(z)=\sqrt{(z-i\eta_{1})(z-i\eta_{2})}
. The function
S(z)
is analytic in
\mathbb{C}\setminus[i\eta_{1},i\eta_{2}]
with the line segment oriented upwards.

In such a case, we choose
\alpha_{\ell}(z,\bar{z})=\varpi_{\ell}(z)
for
\ell=1,2
to be analytic in
\mathcal{D}
. Consequently, the integral along the boundary
\partial\mathcal{D}
of the ellipse in equation
(
2.9
)
can be deformed to a line integral on the segment
\mathcal{I}=[i\eta_{1},i\eta_{2}]

\int_{\partial\mathcal{D}}\frac{\alpha_{\ell}(w)\bar{w}e^{2i\theta(w)}}{z-w}\frac{dw}{2\pi i}=\int_{\mathcal{I}}\frac{\varpi_{\ell}(w)\delta S(w)e^{2i\theta(w)}}{z-w}\frac{dw}{2\pi i},\quad\ell=1,2,

(2.13)

where
\delta S(z)=S_{+}(z)-S_{-}(z)
. Next we define

T(z)=\begin{cases}M^{\infty}(z),\qquad\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{\mathcal{D}\cup\bar{\mathcal{D}}\},\\
M^{\infty}(z)J(z),\quad\,z\in\mathcal{D}\cup\bar{\mathcal{D}},\end{cases}

(2.14)

where

J(z)=\begin{cases}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\int_{\mathcal{I}}\frac{(\varpi_{1}(w)-\overline{\varpi_{1}(\bar{w})}+\varpi_{2}(w))\delta S(w)e^{2i\theta(w)}}{z-w}\frac{dw}{2\pi i}&1\end{pmatrix},\quad\,\,z\in\mathcal{D},\\
\begin{pmatrix}1&\int_{\bar{\mathcal{I}}}\frac{e^{-2i\theta(w)}\overline{\varpi_{1}(\bar{w})}-\varpi_{1}(w)+\overline{\varpi_{2}(\bar{w})}\delta\overline{S(\bar{w})}}{w-z}\frac{dw}{2\pi i}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad z\in\bar{\mathcal{D}},\end{cases}

(2.15)

Consequently, we arrive at the following RH problem for
T(z)
.

RH problem 2.4
.

Find an analytic function
T(z):\mathbb{C}\setminus(\mathcal{I}\cup\bar{\mathcal{I}})\to SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
with the following properties.

1.

T(z)=I+\mathcal{O}(z^{-1})
as
z\to\infty
.

2.

For
z\in\mathcal{I}\cup\bar{\mathcal{I}}
,
the boundary values
T_{\pm}(z)
satisfy the jump relation

T_{+}(z)=T_{-}(z)J(z),

where

J(z)=\begin{cases}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
-r(z)e^{-2i\theta(z)}&1\end{pmatrix},\quad z\in\mathcal{I},\\[15.0pt]
\begin{pmatrix}1&\overline{r(\bar{z})}e^{2i\theta(z)}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\,\,z\in\bar{\mathcal{I}},\end{cases}

(2.16)

where
r(z)=\delta S(z)(\varpi_{1}(w)-\overline{\varpi_{1}(\bar{w})}+\varpi_{2}(w))
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let the ellipse domain
\mathcal{D}
be defined as in equation
(
2.11
)
. Consequently, the gas solution
q_{\infty}(x,t)
, as given in equation
(
2.6
)
, corresponds to an infinite number of spectral points that are uniformly distributed along the segments

.

Proof.

By the process described in equation (

), the function

transforms the jump on

to
\mathcal{I}\cup\bar{\mathcal{I}}
, with both contours oriented upwards. As a result,

is analytic in
\mathbb{C}\setminus(\mathcal{I}\cup\bar{\mathcal{I}})
. In this context, we consider the mKdV soliton shielding, which is characterized by a jump across the contours
\mathcal{I}
and
\bar{\mathcal{I}}
. This implies that the problem on the domain can be reduced to a classical RH problem with discontinuities along a collection of arcs. This reduction is also the effect of soliton shielding because the effective soliton charge can be localized from a two-dimensional domain to a collection of arcs.

3
Step-like oscillatory initial data

In this section, we examine the scenario where the domain
\mathcal{D}
is an elliptic region with foci located at
i\eta_{1}

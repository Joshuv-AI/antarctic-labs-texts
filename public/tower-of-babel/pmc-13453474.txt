Pretreatment CT-defined sarcopenia is associated with worse survival in cervical cancer
Authors: Leandro Santos de Araujo Resende, Miguel Soares Conceição, Rodrigo Menezes Jales, Patrick Nunes Pereira, Luis Otávio Sarian, Glauco Baiocchi, Sophie Derchain, Agnaldo Lopes da Silva Filho
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo evaluate the prevalence and prognostic relevance of pretreatment computed tomography-defined sarcopenia in women with cervical cancer treated with definitive radiotherapy with or without concurrent chemotherapy.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study included women aged 18 years or older with FIGO 2018 stage IB2 to IVA cervical cancer who initiated definitive radiotherapy, with or without radiosensitizing chemotherapy, between January 2016 and December 2018 at CAISM/UNICAMP, Campinas, Brazil. Follow-up continued through 2022 to ensure a minimum follow-up period to evaluate 5-year survival outcomes. Women with prior malignancy treated with chemotherapy or radiotherapy, unavailable or non-measurable pretreatment computed tomography, pregnancy, or death before treatment initiation were excluded. Pretreatment sarcopenia was defined as skeletal muscle index <38.9 cm2/m2 using bilateral psoas and paraspinal linear measurements at L3 on planning computed tomography. Treatment response followed World Health Organization response definitions. Progression-free survival and overall survival were estimated using Kaplan-Meier methods and compared with log-rank tests. Cox proportional hazards models and multivariable logistic regression were performed to estimate hazard ratios (HR), odds ratios (OR), and 95% confidence intervals.ResultsAmong 210 included women, 79 (37.6%) had pretreatment sarcopenia. Most patients had squamous histology (176/210, 83.8%), advanced FIGO stage III/IV disease (120/210, 57.1%), and received chemotherapy (177/210, 84.3%). Sarcopenia was more frequent among women aged <50 years, premenopausal women, women with body mass index <25 kg/m2, and women with anemia. Complete or stable response occurred in 100 patients, whereas progression or recurrence occurred in 110; sarcopenia was more common among patients with progression or recurrence. In univariable analysis, pretreatment sarcopenia was associated with worse progression-free survival (HR 1.70, 95% CI 1.15 to 2.51) and overall survival (HR 1.75, 95% CI 1.11 to 2.75). In multivariable Cox regression, complete response to treatment was the only factor independently associated with overall survival (HR 0.07, 95% CI 0.02 to 0.18, p < 0.0001). Multivariable logistic regression showed a trend for higher sarcopenia prevalence in women aged <50 years (OR 1.73, 95% CI 0.98 to 3.05, p = 0.057).ConclusionPretreatment CT-defined sarcopenia was common and associated with poorer survival outcomes in univariable analysis, although treatment response remained the strongest independent prognostic factor in women with cervical cancer treated with definitive chemoradiotherapy.

Introduction

Cervical cancer remains a major global health problem despite the availability of effective primary and secondary prevention strategies. Global estimates for 2022 indicate approximately 660,000 new cases and 350,000 deaths from cervical cancer, making it one of the leading causes of cancer death among women worldwide (Bray et al., 2024). The burden is not distributed equally: mortality is disproportionately high in low- and middle-income countries, where access to HPV vaccination, screening, early diagnosis, radiotherapy infrastructure, brachytherapy, systemic therapy, and supportive care may be limited (Cohen et al., 2019). Recent reports have also emphasized the clinical relevance of cervical cancer among younger women, reinforcing the social and reproductive impact of the disease (Francoeur et al., 2024). A large proportion of women with cervical cancer are diagnosed with locally advanced disease. In these patients, radical surgery is not generally the standard therapeutic option, and definitive radiotherapy with or without concurrent platinum-based chemotherapy is the cornerstone of treatment (Cibula et al., 2023). This clinical context is particularly relevant in resource-constrained settings, where treatment delays, incomplete multimodality therapy, limited brachytherapy access, and nutritional vulnerability may amplify adverse outcomes. Prognostic assessment before treatment initiation therefore remains important for identifying modifiable risk factors that could be addressed alongside oncologic therapy.

Sarcopenia is characterized by loss of skeletal muscle quantity and function and may overlap with cancer-associated cachexia, a syndrome defined by ongoing skeletal muscle loss that is not fully reversed by conventional nutritional support (Fearon et al., 2011). A recent systematic review and meta-analysis confirmed the prognostic value of sarcopenia in clinical outcomes for cervical cancer (Wang et al., 2025). In oncology, low skeletal muscle mass measured on CT has been proposed as an objective biomarker of frailty, treatment tolerance, and survival. CT-based analysis is attractive in cervical cancer because abdominal or pelvic imaging is frequently obtained for staging and radiotherapy planning. Although many sarcopenia studies rely on specialized segmentation software, a clinically practical approach using electronic linear measurements of bilateral psoas and paraspinal muscles at L3 has shown strong diagnostic performance and reproducibility for detecting low muscularity (Avrutin et al., 2017).

The prognostic role of sarcopenia in cervical cancer remains incompletely defined, particularly in non-surgical cohorts treated with definitive radiation-based therapy in countries with constrained resources. Studying this question in women who were not submitted to surgery has an additional methodological advantage: postoperative catabolism, surgical complications, and recovery-related muscle loss cannot explain pretreatment sarcopenia or confound its baseline association with outcomes. The objective of this study was to evaluate the prevalence of pretreatment CT-defined sarcopenia and its association with progression-free survival and overall survival in women with cervical cancer treated with definitive radiotherapy with or without concurrent chemotherapy.

Methods

This was a retrospective cohort study conducted at the Center for Integral Attention to Women's Health, Hospital Dr. José Aristodemo Pinotti, University of Campinas (CAISM/UNICAMP), Campinas, São Paulo, Brazil. Women with cervical cancer who initiated treatment from January 2016 through December 2018 were identified through medical records and imaging archives, and follow-up was updated through 2022. This follow-up cutoff was specifically chosen to ensure a minimum follow-up period to evaluate 5-year survival outcomes for the patients included in our cohort, allowing for a mature assessment of survival without introducing bias from patients with very short follow-up periods.

Eligible patients were women aged 18 years or older with cervical cancer, FIGO 2018 stage IB2 to IVA disease, who were treated with definitive radiotherapy with or without radiosensitizing chemotherapy. Exclusion criteria were a history of another malignancy treated with chemotherapy or radiotherapy, absence of pretreatment CT imaging, CT imaging that could not be used to assess sarcopenia, pregnancy, and death after diagnosis but before treatment initiation. The study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of UNICAMP, registered on Plataforma Brasil under CAAE 09803719.7.1001.5411. Because this was a retrospective study based exclusively on medical records and clinically indicated CT images, informed consent was waived by the ethics committee.

Clinical variables included age at diagnosis, menstrual status, body mass index, histologic subtype, FIGO 2018 stage, pretreatment hemoglobin, platelet count, use of chemotherapy, brachytherapy, treatment-related adverse events, treatment response, site of recurrence, progression-free survival, and overall survival. The age threshold of 50 years was chosen a priori to serve as a proxy for menopausal status and to control for age-related (primary) sarcopenia. Menopause was defined as at least 12 months of amenorrhea or age 50 years in hysterectomized women. Anemia was defined as pretreatment hemoglobin <11 g/dL, and thrombocytopenia as platelets <150,000/mm3. Treatment response followed World Health Organization response definitions (World Health Organization, 1979). Progression-free survival was defined as the interval from treatment initiation to disease progression, and overall survival as the interval from treatment initiation to death.

Pretreatment sarcopenia was assessed using CT images acquired for radiotherapy planning and available in the institutional Picture Archiving and Communication System. Images were evaluated in Arya software (Pixeon, São Paulo, Brazil). A single trained evaluator, blinded to study outcomes and supervised by two collaborating investigators, selected an axial image at the third lumbar vertebra (L3), identified the bilateral psoas and paraspinal muscles, and performed two perpendicular linear measurements for each muscle group on both sides. Each patient therefore generated eight linear measurements. Measurements were repeated at two time points separated by at least 30 days to reduce measurement bias. Skeletal muscle index was calculated from the sum of measured muscle areas divided by squared height. Sarcopenia was defined as skeletal muscle index <38.9 cm2/m2 in accordance with the validated female threshold for this practical CT method (Avrutin et al., 2017).

Continuous variables were summarized as means and standard deviations, and categorical variables as frequencies and percentages. Baseline characteristics were compared between patients with and without sarcopenia using chi-square tests for categorical variables and independent-samples t-tests for continuous variables. Kaplan-Meier methods were used to estimate survival curves, and log-rank tests compared survival between groups. Univariable and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression models estimated hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). A multivariable logistic regression model was also performed to identify factors independently associated with pretreatment sarcopenia. Statistical analyses were performed using R. A two-sided p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 210 women met the eligibility criteria and were included in the analysis. Pretreatment sarcopenia was identified in 79 patients (37.6%), and the mean skeletal muscle index for the overall cohort was 41.71 cm2/m2. The cohort included 109 women aged <50 years (51.9%) and 101 women aged ≥50 years (48.1%). Sarcopenia was more frequent among women aged <50 years than among women aged ≥50 years (49/79, 62.0% vs 30/79, 38.0%; p = 0.03), among premenopausal women compared with postmenopausal women (49/79, 62.0% vs 30/79, 38.0%; p = 0.04), and among women with body mass index <25 kg/m2 (51/79, 64.6%; p < 0.01). Pretreatment anemia was present in 78 patients (37.1%) and was associated with sarcopenia (p = 0.02). Thrombocytopenia was uncommon and was not associated with sarcopenia.

Most tumors were squamous cell carcinomas (176/210, 83.8%). Ninety patients (42.9%) had FIGO stage I/II disease and 120 (57.1%) had stage III/IV disease. Stage distribution did not differ significantly by sarcopenia status. Most patients received chemotherapy (177/210, 84.3%), and 92 (43.8%) received brachytherapy. Neither chemotherapy nor brachytherapy use differed significantly according to sarcopenia status. Treatment-related adverse events were recorded in 48 patients; 26 of these events were severe or fatal, and 12 of the 26 patients with severe or fatal events had sarcopenia.

Treatment response differed according to sarcopenia status. Complete response or stable disease was observed in 100 patients, including 26 with sarcopenia and 74 without sarcopenia. Progression or recurrence occurred in 110 patients, including 53 with sarcopenia and 57 without sarcopenia (p < 0.05). Recurrence was documented in 56 patients: 23 had local recurrence, 21 had nodal recurrence, and 12 had distant recurrence. Recurrence site did not differ significantly according to sarcopenia status. At last follow-up, 71 patients were alive without disease, 56 were alive with disease, 3 had died without disease, and 80 had died from disease; deaths from disease were more frequent among patients with sarcopenia.

In univariable analysis, advanced FIGO stage was associated with poorer survival outcomes. Compared with stage I/II disease, stage III/IV disease was associated with worse progression-free survival (HR 2.16, 95% CI 1.41 to 3.32; p < 0.01) and worse overall survival (HR 3.40, 95% CI 1.97 to 5.87; p < 0.01). Pretreatment sarcopenia was also associated with worse progression-free survival (HR 1.70, 95% CI 1.15 to 2.51; p < 0.01; log-rank p = 0.008) and worse overall survival (HR 1.75, 95% CI 1.11 to 2.75; p = 0.01; log-rank p = 0.03). The Kaplan-Meier curves showed lower progression-free and overall survival probabilities for patients with sarcopenia than for patients without sarcopenia.

To evaluate independent prognostic factors, a multivariable Cox proportional hazards model was performed for overall survival, incorporating variables with univariable significance (sarcopenia, advanced stage, anemia, and complete response to treatment). In this multivariable analysis, complete response to treatment emerged as the only factor independently associated with improved overall survival (HR 0.07, 95% CI 0.02 to 0.18, p < 0.0001). Sarcopenia did not maintain independent statistical significance in this model (HR 1.11, 95% CI 0.72 to 1.71, p = 0.647), suggesting that the response to definitive therapy is a stronger driver of long-term survival in this setting.

Furthermore, a multivariable logistic regression was performed to identify factors independently associated with pretreatment sarcopenia status. The model included age (<50 years) and anemia. The results demonstrated a trend for higher sarcopenia prevalence in women aged <50 years (OR 1.73, 95% CI 0.98 to 3.05, p = 0.057), while anemia was not independently predictive (OR 1.03, 95% CI 0.58 to 1.81, p = 0.923).

Discussion

In this retrospective cohort of 210 women with cervical cancer treated with definitive radiotherapy with or without chemotherapy, pretreatment CT-defined sarcopenia was present in 37.6% of patients. Sarcopenia was more frequent among younger women, premenopausal women, patients with lower body mass index, and patients with pretreatment anemia. In univariable analysis, pretreatment sarcopenia was associated with progression or recurrence and with poorer progression-free survival and overall survival. However, in multivariable analysis, treatment response was the strongest independent predictor of overall survival.

The global epidemiology of cervical cancer provides the clinical context for these findings. Cervical cancer continues to cause hundreds of thousands of deaths each year, and the burden remains greatest in regions with fewer preventive and treatment resources (Bray et al., 2024) (Cohen et al., 2019). In women with locally advanced disease, definitive radiotherapy and concurrent chemotherapy are central treatment strategies (Cibula et al., 2023). The present cohort therefore reflects a clinically important group of patients for whom baseline vulnerability may affect treatment completion, response, and survival.

The prevalence of sarcopenia observed in this study is consistent with the broader oncologic literature showing that low muscle mass is common at cancer diagnosis. A recent systematic review and meta-analysis of cervical cancer patients confirmed that sarcopenia is associated with worse overall survival and progression-free survival (Wang et al., 2025). Our findings align with this literature but provide unique insights by demonstrating this effect in a strictly non-surgical cohort treated within a public health setting, utilizing a simplified and highly reproducible linear CT measurement method.

An unexpected finding in our cohort was the higher prevalence of sarcopenia in younger (<50 years) and premenopausal women, a trend that was maintained in multivariable logistic regression. We hypothesize that this phenomenon is deeply intertwined with the chronic nature of the disease and its strong association with socioeconomic factors. Cervical cancer is widely recognized as a marker of poverty and health disparities. Our cohort consists exclusively of patients treated within the Brazilian public health system, representing a population with historically limited access to adequate nutrition and healthcare throughout their lives. Therefore, these younger women may already suffer from chronic nutritional deficits and socioeconomic vulnerability long before their cancer diagnosis. This socioeconomic reality likely explains why physiological expectations—where older women should be more sarcopenic—are inverted in this specific, resource-constrained demographic.

Another critical factor observed in our study was the low uptake of brachytherapy (43.8%). This systemic limitation significantly restricts the widespread use of this essential treatment modality for locally advanced cervical cancer in our setting. The public health system faces severe resource constraints, including limited access to brachytherapy equipment and specialized personnel. This lack of access directly impacts the ability to achieve a complete response, which our multivariable analysis identified as the most critical independent factor for overall survival. The interplay between baseline vulnerability (such as sarcopenia) and limited access to optimal multimodality therapy underscores the complex challenges faced in real-world oncologic care in developing nations.

The strengths of this study include the specific focus on a non-surgical cohort, which eliminates the confounding effect of surgical catabolism on muscle mass, and the use of a practical, reproducible CT measurement technique that does not require expensive proprietary software. Limitations include the retrospective design, which inherently limits causal inference, and the lack of concurrent functional assessments of muscle strength or physical performance, which are necessary to formally diagnose sarcopenia according to modern consensus definitions. Additionally, the multivariable analysis demonstrated that while sarcopenia is a valuable univariable prognostic marker, the ultimate response to chemoradiation remains the primary determinant of long-term survival.

In conclusion, pretreatment CT-defined sarcopenia is a common finding in women with cervical cancer and is associated with worse survival outcomes in univariable analysis. The unexpected higher prevalence in younger women highlights the profound impact of chronic socioeconomic and nutritional disparities in resource-constrained populations. While complete response to treatment remains the strongest independent prognostic factor, baseline assessment of muscle mass using simple linear CT measurements can help identify vulnerable patients who may benefit from early nutritional support and physical rehabilitation alongside definitive oncologic therapy.

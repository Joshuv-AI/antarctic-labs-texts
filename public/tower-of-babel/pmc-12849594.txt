A single-center retrospective cohort study on the effects of different surgical routes on complications after radical resection of low rectal cancer
Authors: Ruifeng Ye, Weixin Wu, Chongbiao Chen, Long Yi, Qin Gao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo compare the impact of retromesenteric versus anterior mesenteric approaches on postoperative outcomes after laparoscopic radical resection for low rectal cancer.MethodsThis single-center retrospective cohort study included 157 patients (59 retromesenteric, 98 anterior mesenteric) treated between 2020 and 2023. Primary outcomes were postoperative inflammatory response, including C-reactive protein (CRP) and procalcitonin (PCT) levels on days 1, 3, and 7. Secondary outcomes included complications and recovery indicators. Logistic regression adjusted for confounders.ResultsBaseline characteristics were comparable. On postoperative day 1, CRP (68.4 ± 12.3 vs. 85.2 ± 14.7 mg/L, P < 0.01) and PCT (0.45 ± 0.08 vs. 0.61 ± 0.10 ng/mL, P < 0.01) were significantly lower in the retromesenteric group, with similar trends on day 3. Anastomotic leakage was less frequent in the retromesenteric group (6.2% vs. 18.4%, P = 0.048), confirmed as an independent protective factor in multivariable logistic regression analysis (OR = 3.16, 95% CI: 1.009–9.894). No significant differences were observed in incisional infection, ileus, or intra-abdominal infection (P > 0.05). Time to first flatus was shorter in the retromesenteric group (48.6 ± 6.3 vs. 52.3 ± 7.1 h, P < 0.01), while hospital stay and 30-day readmission rates were similar.ConclusionThe retromesenteric approach is superior to the anterior mesenteric approach in reducing postoperative inflammatory reactions and the risk of anastomotic leakage, and promotes postoperative intestinal function recovery, which provides an important basis for the selection of surgical approaches for low rectal cancer.Trial registrationNot applicable.

Introduction

Rectal cancer ranks among the most prevalent malignant tumors of the digestive tract globally, with its incidence showing a consistent upward trend in recent years [1]. According to statistics, rectal cancer accounts for about 30%-40% of colorectal cancers, among which low rectal cancer (the lower edge of the tumor is within 5 cm from the anal verge) accounts for a significant proportion [2, 3]. Especially in Asia, the incidence of low rectal cancer is relatively high. Because the tumor is close to the anal sphincter complex, surgical treatment of low rectal cancer requires not only complete tumor resection but also preservation of the patient’s anal function as much as possible [4, 5]. However, this anatomical peculiarity makes the treatment of low rectal cancer complex and challenging. Although surgery is currently the main treatment for low rectal cancer, its postoperative complications are still an important issue faced by clinicians. Postoperative complications such as anastomotic leakage, infection, and intestinal obstruction seriously affect the patient’s recovery and quality of life, and may even be life-threatening [6]. Understanding the impact of surgical route on postoperative complications is of great significance to improving clinical outcomes.

The choice of surgical route has an important impact on the complications and patient prognosis after radical resection of low rectal cancer [7]. The retromesenteric route is considered to be effective in reducing the risk of anastomotic leakage because it is more in line with the anatomical characteristics of the rectum and reduces anastomotic tension [8]. Several studies have shown that the retromesenteric approach has significant advantages in reducing serious postoperative complications [9]. For example, Wu et al. [10]. (2010) reported that the incidence of anastomotic leakage in patients using the retromesenteric approach was significantly lower than that in the anterior mesenteric approach group, and the postoperative recovery time was also shorter. However, the technical complexity of this approach and the high requirement for operator experience may limit its generalization. In contrast, the anterior mesenteric approach is widely used due to its simplicity and shorter operative time, especially in patients with more favorable anatomical conditions. Some studies have shown that the anterior mesenteric approach is superior in terms of operative time and intraoperative blood loss, but may lead to an increased risk of anastomotic leakage and postoperative ileus due to increased tension [11]. In addition, in patients with obesity or complex pelvic anatomy, the anterior mesenteric approach may further increase the risk of complications [12, 13]. Current comparative studies on the two pathways are inconsistent. Some studies support the superiority of the retromesenteric approach in preventing and controlling complications [14], while other studies conclude that there is no significant difference in overall postoperative complication rates between the two. The conclusions of these studies may be limited by sample heterogeneity, insufficient data standardization, or insufficient attention to inflammatory processes.

Anatomically, the retromesenteric approach proceeds along the natural avascular plane posterior to the descending mesocolon, allowing tension-free mobilization of the rectum and better preservation of autonomic nerves and mesenteric blood supply [9, 15]. In contrast, the anterior mesenteric approach opens the peritoneum anterior to the mesocolon and crosses the mesenteric root toward the rectum, which provides a direct operative field but may increase anastomotic tension and ischemic risk [11]. Understanding these distinct anatomical planes is therefore essential to improving surgical reproducibility and interpreting differences in postoperative outcomes.

Although studies have examined the impact of retromesenteric and anterior mesenteric approaches on postoperative outcomes for low rectal cancer [16], the results have been inconsistent in terms of complication rates. Some studies have focused on the direct impact of surgical route on complications such as anastomotic leakage and infection, but few have evaluated its dynamic changes in postoperative inflammatory response. In addition, most studies were multicenter in design, which may limit the reliability of conclusions due to differences in surgical procedures and inconsistencies in data collection. At present, the value of inflammatory indicators such as C-reactive protein (CRP) and procalcitonin (PCT) in early prediction of postoperative infection and complications has been widely recognized [17]. However, existing studies mostly focus on the analysis of inflammation indicators at a single surgical path or single point, and fail to systematically evaluate the relationship between dynamic changes in inflammation indicators and surgical path selection. In particular, there is still a gap in research on the impact of different pathways on inflammatory responses and complications under standardized conditions. Single-center studies can achieve greater consistency in data collection, case selection, and surgical procedures, providing ideal conditions for studying the true impact of surgical pathways on inflammatory responses and complications. Therefore, a single-center large-sample study combining the dynamic changes of CRP and PCT can provide important scientific basis for optimizing surgical route selection.

This study aims to evaluate the application effects of the retromesenteric approach and the anterior mesenteric approach in radical resection of low rectal cancer, focusing on analyzing the dynamic changes and complication rates of the two approaches on postoperative inflammatory response (with CRP and PCT as the main indicators). influence. Through large-sample data from a single center, this study provides a scientific basis for surgical route selection. At the same time, combined with inflammatory index monitoring, it provides support for postoperative management and early intervention strategies, which has important clinical value.

Materials and methods

Study design

This study was a single-center, retrospective cohort study conducted at the 900TH Hospital of Joint Logistics Support Force, People’s Liberation Army (PLA), located in Fuzhou, Fujian, China. The study period spanned from January 2020 to December 2023. All data were retrospectively collected from the hospital’s electronic medical records (EMR) system and laboratory databases. The study protocol was approved by the hospital’s ethics committee of the 900TH Hospital of Joint Logistics Support Force, PLA (approval number: 2024135), and the ethical standards of the Declaration of Helsinki were strictly followed. All subjects signed the consent form before participation in the study. Both surgical approaches were performed within the same study period by the same experienced colorectal surgical team at our institution. The selection of surgical route was primarily based on intraoperative anatomical characteristics and mesenteric tension rather than surgeon preference, ensuring comparable technical conditions between groups.

Participants

Eligibility criteria

Patients were included in the study if they met the following criteria: (1)Diagnosis: Histologically confirmed low rectal cancer, defined as a tumor with its lower edge within 5 cm from the anal verge; (2)Surgical Approach: Underwent laparoscopic radical resection using either the retromesenteric approach or the anterior mesenteric approach; (3)Data Completeness: Comprehensive postoperative inflammatory indicator data (CRP and PCT) available on postoperative days 1, 3, and 7; (4)Informed Consent: Signed informed consent prior to surgery.

Exclusion criteria

Exclusion criteria included preoperative infection or severe systemic inflammatory diseases (such as sepsis or autoimmune disorders), missing or incomplete medical records, and any prior abdominal surgery affecting the mesenteric pathway. To ensure accuracy and consistency, two independent researchers verified all data against clinical and laboratory records. Patients with T4 tumors involving adjacent organs, emergency operations, or intraoperative conversions to open surgery were excluded. Cases with previous major abdominal surgery that altered normal mesenteric anatomy were also excluded to ensure consistency in the surgical planes.

Study size and power calculation

The sample size was calculated based on an estimated difference of 15% in complication rates between the two groups, aiming to detect this difference with 80% power and a 5% significance level (α = 0.05). A total of 157 patients were deemed adequate for analysis, including 59 in the retromesenteric approach group and 98 in the anterior mesenteric approach group. This sample size was considered sufficient to detect clinically relevant differences between the groups.

Variables

The primary outcomes of this study were the postoperative inflammatory response and postoperative complications. The inflammatory response was measured using C-reactive protein (CRP) and procalcitonin (PCT) levels on postoperative days 1, 3, and 7. Complications included anastomotic leakage, incisional infection, intestinal obstruction, and intra-abdominal infection, identified based on clinical signs and imaging confirmation. Postoperative complications were defined according to the Clavien–Dindo classification system [18]. Secondary outcomes included postoperative recovery indicators, such as time to first flatus, length of hospital stay, and 30-day readmission rates.

Data sources and measurements

Inflammatory markers, including CRP, PCT, and white blood cell count (WBC), were measured in venous blood samples collected on postoperative days 1, 3, and 7. CRP levels were determined by immunoturbidimetry, PCT levels by chemiluminescence immunoassay, and WBC counts by automated hematology analyzers. To maintain consistency, all tests were performed in the same hospital laboratory following standardized procedures. Postoperative complications were recorded based on standard diagnostic criteria and confirmed by senior surgeons. Data on demographic characteristics, comorbidities, and clinical outcomes were extracted from the hospital’s electronic medical records by two independent researchers.

Bias and confounding control

To minimize selection bias, strict inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied, and potential confounders such as age, sex, BMI, tumor stage, and comorbidities were controlled using multivariable logistic regression. Additionally, data extraction was independently performed by two researchers who were blinded to the surgical approach. Any discrepancies were resolved through consensus or by consultation with a senior investigator. To minimize the potential impact of the learning curve, all surgeons involved had already passed the learning phase for laparoscopic low anterior resection before the study period, and both the retromesenteric and anterior mesenteric approaches had been standardized in our department.

Statistical analysis

All data were statistically analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics 23 software. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and data distribution normality was assessed by Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Normally distributed variables were compared between groups using independent sample t test, and non-normally distributed variables were compared using Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were expressed as frequency and percentage, and intergroup comparisons were performed using chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. To assess the independent association between surgical route and postoperative complications, logistic regression analysis was performed to adjust for potential confounders. The results of regression analysis were expressed as odds ratio (OR) and its 95% confidence interval (CI). All statistical tests were two-sided, and P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline data

A total of 157 patients were included in this study, including 59 patients in the retromesenteric approach group and 98 patients in the anterior mesenteric approach group. There were no significant differences in baseline characteristics between the two groups of patients, including age, gender, BMI, preoperative hemoglobin level, preoperative carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) level and tumor stage distribution (P > 0.05). The average age of patients in the retromesenteric approach group was 60.8 ± 9.7 years old, and that in the anterior mesenteric approach group was 61.1 ± 10.3 years old; the male proportions were 55.9% (33 cases) and 56.1% (55 cases) respectively. BMI were 24.2 ± 2.3 kg/m² and 24.3 ± 2.5 kg/m² respectively. The preoperative hemoglobin levels were 13.3 ± 1.2 g/dL in the retromesenteric approach group and 13.2 ± 1.3 g/dL in the anterior mesenteric approach group, and the CEA levels were 6.2 ± 2.4 ng/mL and 6.5 ± 2.7 ng/mL, respectively. The distribution of tumor stages was also relatively balanced (stages I, II, and III accounted for 27.1%, 45.8%, and 27.1% of the retromesenteric approach group and 26.5%, 44.9%, and 28.6% of the anterior mesenteric approach group respectively). Changes in inflammatory indicators

The changes in postoperative inflammatory indicators are shown in Table 2. On the first day after surgery, the CRP level (68.4 ± 12.3 mg/L) and PCT level (0.45 ± 0.08 ng/mL) in the retromesenteric approach group were significantly lower than those in the anterior mesenteric approach group (85.2 ± 14.7 mg/L, P < 0.01; 0.61 ± 0.10 ng/mL, P < 0.01). On the 3rd day after surgery, the CRP and PCT levels in the retromesenteric approach group were still significantly lower than those in the anterior mesenteric approach group (P < 0.01), while on the 7th postoperative day, the differences in CRP and PCT between the two groups gradually narrowed. There was no statistically significant difference in WBC levels between groups at each time point (P > 0.05).

Independent correlation between surgical route and postoperative complications

Logistic regression analysis was used to evaluate the independent association between surgical path and postoperative complications, while adjusting for potential confounding factors (including age, gender, BMI and tumor stage). The incidence of anastomotic leakage in the retromesenteric approach group was 6.2%, which was significantly lower than 18.4% in the anterior mesenteric approach group. Regression analysis showed that the surgical route is an independent influencing factor of anastomotic leakage (OR = 3.16, 95% CI: 1.009–9.894, P = 0.048), suggesting that the retromesenteric route can significantly reduce the risk of anastomotic leakage. The incidence rates of incisional infection were 5.1% in the posterior approach group and 7.1% in the anterior approach group; the incidence rates of postoperative intestinal obstruction were 8.5% and 12.2% respectively; the incidence rates of abdominal infection were 3.4% and 5.1%. The results of regression analysis showed that there was no statistically significant difference in these complications between the two groups (P > 0.05). Further subgroup analysis showed that patients with higher BMI did not show significant differences between pathways in the incidence of complications (P > 0.05). Comparison of surgical route and postoperative recovery

The postoperative recovery status is shown in Table 4. The time to first flatus in the retromesenteric route group was significantly shorter than that in the anterior mesenteric route group (48.6 ± 6.3 h vs. 52.3 ± 7.1 h, P < 0.01), suggesting that the retromesenteric route may help restore intestinal function faster. The postoperative hospitalization time in the retromesenteric route group was 8.7 ± 2.1 days, which was slightly shorter than the 9.4 ± 2.5 days in the anterior mesenteric route group, but the difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.076). The readmission rates 30 days after surgery were 6.8% and 9.2% in the retromesenteric route group and anterior mesenteric route group, respectively, and there was no significant difference between the groups (P = 0.587).

Discussion

This study found that the retromesenteric approach showed significant advantages in radical resection of low rectal cancer. Compared with the anterior mesenteric route, the postoperative inflammatory response (CRP and PCT levels) was significantly reduced in the retromesenteric route group, especially in the early postoperative period (days 1 and 3). In addition, the retromesenteric route significantly reduces the risk of anastomotic leakage and becomes its independent protective factor, while accelerating the recovery of postoperative intestinal function (shortening the time to first flatus). Although there were no significant differences in the incidence of incisional infection, intestinal obstruction, and abdominal infection between the two groups, the role of the retromesenteric approach in reducing certain postoperative complications remains clear. There was no significant difference in length of stay and 30-day readmission rate between the two pathways, suggesting that the overall postoperative recovery tended to be similar.

The results of this study echo the conclusions of many previous studies and provide new insights in some aspects. Liang et al. (2014) [15] reported that the retromesenteric approach has significant advantages in reducing the incidence of anastomotic leakage, and this conclusion was further verified in this study. Through logistic regression analysis, our study identified that the retromesenteric path is an independent protective factor for anastomotic leakage (OR = 3.16, 95% CI: 1.009–9.894), and found that the early postoperative inflammatory response (CRP and PCT) in the retromesenteric path group was level) was significantly lower than that of the anterior mesenteric route group, suggesting that reduced anastomotic tension may further reduce the risk of complications by reducing the inflammatory response mechanism. Previous studies have defined clinically relevant thresholds for these biomarkers in predicting anastomotic leakage. Bona D et al. [17] reported optimal cut-offs of CRP between 10.9 and 15.9 mg/dL and PCT between 0.75 and 0.9 ng/mL on postoperative days 3–5, with good diagnostic accuracy (AUC 0.80–0.92). Similarly, Stephensen B.D. et al. [19] demonstrated that a CRP increase > 50 mg/L between postoperative days 3–4 or 4–5 had a specificity of 0.96–0.97 for leakage. The higher early postoperative CRP and PCT levels observed in the anterior mesenteric group in our study were close to these thresholds, supporting their clinical relevance for early complication surveillance. Willard et al. (2019) [20] pointed out that the anterior mesenteric approach is widely used in certain patients with favorable anatomical conditions due to its simple operation and shorter operation time. However, the results of this study suggest that this route may significantly increase the risk of anastomotic leakage and postoperative inflammatory reaction by causing increased anastomotic tension [21, 22]. It is worth noting that this study, based on the dynamic assessment of postoperative inflammatory indicators, was the first to systematically quantify the changing trends of inflammatory responses in the two pathways and found that the retromesenteric pathway showed significant changes on the 1st and 3rd days after surgery. There is a significant inflammation alleviation effect, and the inflammation levels of the two groups gradually approached on the 7th day. This is complementary to the conclusions in the existing literature that are mostly based on the analysis of inflammatory indicators at a single time point. In addition, previous literature has no clear conclusion on the path differences of other complications such as incisional infection and intestinal obstruction. This study further confirms that the incidence of these complications is not significantly different between the retromesenteric and anterior mesenteric approaches. This may be related to the fact that all cases in this study used laparoscopic surgery, and the precision of the operation may have reduced the incidence of these complications. Compared with previous studies, this study reduced the heterogeneity of case selection and data collection through a single-center design, and increased the reliability of the analysis based on a larger sample size.

Combining the dynamic monitoring of CRP and PCT, the impact of the surgical route on inflammatory response and postoperative complications was systematically evaluated, ensuring the consistency of case selection, surgical operation, and data collection in the context of a single-center study. This not only fills the gap in the research on the dynamic changes of inflammatory response by different surgical paths, but also provides a scientific basis for optimizing the selection of surgical paths for patients with low rectal cancer. The significant advantages of the retromesenteric approach in reducing anastomotic leakage and postoperative inflammatory reactions have important clinical value, especially for patients who require rapid recovery of postoperative intestinal function. Recent enhanced recovery after surgery guidelines also emphasize perioperative inflammatory monitoring as an indicator of surgical stress and recovery, further supporting the relevance of CRP and PCT assessment in postoperative management [23].

Although the results of this study are clear, its limitations deserve attention. First, the design of a single-center study may limit the generalizability of the results; multicenter data may be more representative. Second, as a retrospective study, this study may have selection bias and potential confounding factors. Third, long-term oncologic outcomes such as local recurrence, distant metastasis, and overall survival were not assessed. These endpoints are crucial in rectal cancer surgery for evaluating the oncological adequacy of different surgical approaches. Finally, the absence of operative time analysis represents another limitation, as this parameter can vary with patient anatomy and surgeon experience. In the future, multicenter, prospective studies should be conducted, combined with longer follow-up periods and analysis of more patient subgroups (such as obese or anatomically complex patients) to verify the results of this study and further improve the clinical practice guidelines for the surgical approach for low rectal cancer.

Conclusion

This study shows that the retromesenteric approach can significantly reduce the risk of postoperative inflammatory reaction and anastomotic leakage during radical resection of low rectal cancer, and accelerate the recovery of postoperative intestinal function, reflecting its significant advantages in improving surgical outcomes. This finding provides a scientific basis for the optimal selection of surgical paths and helps improve the quality of patients’ postoperative recovery.

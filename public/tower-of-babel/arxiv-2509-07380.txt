Gradient Flows of Interfacial Energies: Curvature Agents and Incompressibility.
Keith Promislow 1, Truong Vu 2, Brian Wetton 3

Abstract
. We present a framework for the gradient flow of sharp-interface surface energies that couple to embedded curvature active agents. We use a penalty method to develop families of locally incompressible gradient flows that couple interface stretching or compression to local flux of interfacial mass. We establish the convergence of the penalty method to an incompressible flow both formally for a broad family of surface energies and rigorously for a more narrow class of surface energies. We present an analysis, including a Γ \Gamma -limit, of an Allen-Cahn type model for a coupled surface agent curvature energy.

Introduction

Surface proteins play a large role in reshaping the endomembrane system. This comprises the nuclear membrane, the endoplasmic reticulum (ER), the Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, vacuoles, and vesicles. These membranes do not merely define the boundaries of cellular organelles rather they conduct much of the synthesis, transport, and sorting of proteins and lipids. Endomembranes are comprised of a lipid bilayer, as such they are fluidic, making them highly deformable without a reference configuration. Like many fluids they are highly incompressible. In a thin-interface limit incompressibility is manifested as a local conservation of hypersurface area. A sub-region of membrane can only grow in area if membrane material flows into it from adjacent regions. In section 3 we present a penalty method that yields a locally incompressible gradient flow for an existing surface energy. We present a formal analysis for a broad category of surface energies, showing the the gradient flow induces an effective membrane flux associated to local changes in interface length. In section 4 we extend this to a rigorous analysis for a reduced family of gradient flows and indeed to normal velocities that do not necessarily correspond to a gradient flow. The main result, Theorem
6
presents an orbital stability result for the penalized system. We define an “incompressible manifold” as a graph of above the family of admissible intrinsic variables, and show that solutions of the penalized system that start sufficiently close to the invariant manifold and that remain bounded, converge on a fast time-scale into a thin neighborhood of the incompressible manifold. Moreover we establish that the dynamics of the penalized flow are the same as those of the incompressible flow to leading order.

2.
Gradient flows of Surface Energies coupled to Curvature Active Agents

In many situations of interest materials adhere to or are embedded within an interface and are carried by its flow, including tangential motion, while simultaneously influencing the interface’s surface energy. The material derivative expresses the impact of deformation of the underlying interface on the embedded agents. In particular, the derivation of a locally mass preserving gradient flow associated to a given interfacial energy requires coupling of the embedded density to the extrinsic velocity through the material derivative.

2.1.
Material Derivative

For a general extrinsic velocity
\mathbb{V}=(\textrm{V},\textrm{W})
the material derivative correspond to an evolution that locally conserves density of an embedded scalar under evolution of the curve. Specifically let
\mathbb{S}_{0}\subset\mathbb{S}
be a labeled subset of the evolving curve. If a density contained in the image
\gamma(\mathbb{S}_{0})
of the curve evolves under a zero material derivative, then the change of agent mass should equal the flux of agent mass out of the boundary of the subset, irrespective of the evolution of the interface.
Taking time derivative of the total density of a scalar
\rho
yields the relation

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{S}}\rho\,\textrm{d}\sigma=\int_{\mathbb{S}}\left(\rho_{t}+\rho\frac{\partial_{t}g}{g}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Substituting for the time derivative of the metric from (
1.9
) and integrating by parts against the tangential velocity yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{S}}\rho\,\textrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}}\left(\rho_{t}-\textrm{W}\nabla_{s}\rho+\rho\kappa\textrm{V}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

This motivates the material derivative

(2.1)

\frac{D\rho}{Dt}:=\rho_{t}+\kappa\textrm{V}\rho-\textrm{W}\nabla_{s}\rho=\left(\partial_{t}U+\mathbb{V}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\kappa\cr-\nabla_{s}\end{pmatrix}\right)\rho.

The material derivative zero-flow

(2.2)

\frac{D\rho}{Dt}=0,

conserves
N
globally. More significantly, if a subset
\mathbb{S}_{0}\subset\mathbb{S}
is labeled, then the mass of
\rho
over the labeled set satisfies the evolution,

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\rho\mathrm{d}\sigma=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\left(\rho_{t}+\rho\nabla_{s}\textrm{W}+\rho\kappa\textrm{V}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\frac{D\rho}{Dt}\mathrm{d}\sigma+(\textrm{W}\rho\hat{n})\Bigl|_{\partial\mathbb{S}_{0}}=(\textrm{W}\rho\hat{n})\Bigl|_{\partial\mathbb{S}_{0}},

where
\hat{n}

\partial\mathbb{S}_{0}
within
\mathbb{S},
takes values in
\{\pm 1\}

\rho
within the evolving set

is impacted only by the flux of
\rho
out of the boundary and not by the deformation of the interface.

2.3.
Phase Separation in Surface Proteins

An application of the gradient flow formalism we consider a free energy for a blend of a phase-separating surface proteins that modulate a Canham-Helrich type intrinsic curvature energy. Since the potential energy is non-convex, wellposedness of the gradient flow requires regularized by surface diffusion of both curvature and a scalar surface protein density
\rho
. We add a term term that controls total surface area yields a free energy of the form

(2.12)

{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(U,\rho)=\int_{\mathbb{S}}\Bigl(\frac{\delta}{2}|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}+\frac{1}{\delta}f(\kappa,\rho)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|\nabla_{s}\rho|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\textrm{F}(\kappa,\rho)\,\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\frac{\beta}{2}(|\Gamma|-\sigma_{1}|\Gamma_{0}|)^{2}.

The potentials are defined as quadratic distances to zero nodal-line functions

\displaystyle:=(\kappa-\kappa_{0}(\rho))^{2}/2,

\displaystyle:=4(\rho-\rho_{-}(\kappa))^{2}(\rho-\rho_{+}(\kappa))^{2}.

\displaystyle=-\delta\Delta_{s}\kappa+\frac{1}{\delta}f_{\kappa}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\textrm{F}_{\kappa}.

2.5.
Gamma Limit of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}

The numerical results presented in Section
2.4
suggest that the
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
limit leads to minimizers in which the density
\rho
follows the
F
nodal lines, except for discontinuous transitions that provide the minimizers with flexibility to satisfy the mass and curve closure constraints. In this section we provide a rigorous analysis that establishes this as a feature of the
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}

\Gamma
-limit of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}.
We first review the definition of a
\Gamma
limit.

Definition 2
.

Let
X
be a complete metric space. The functional
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is called the
\Gamma
-limit of the functionals
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
for
\varepsilon>0
if the following two conditions are satisfied.

•

The
\liminf
-inequality holds: for any sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
in
X
, we have

(2.15)

u=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{u_{\varepsilon}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad{\mathcal{F}}_{0}[u]\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{{\mathcal{F}}_{k}[u_{\varepsilon}]}

•

The
\limsup
-inequality holds: given
u\in X
there exists a sequence
u_{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow u
in
X
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
such that

(2.16)

{\mathcal{F}}_{0}[u]\geq\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}[u_{\varepsilon}]}.

Remark 1
.

Generically there are no finite-energy limiting sequences for
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}
in the form (
2.12
) when both
\varepsilon
and
\delta
tend to zero in a distinguished limit of the form
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)
with
\delta(0)=0.
A finite energy limit would require that the limiting sequence
(U_{\varepsilon},\rho_{\varepsilon})
converge to functions
(U,\rho)
that only take values equal to the double-nodal points

\{(\kappa_{i},\rho_{i})\}_{i=1,2}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. With these values assumed on two complimentary sets

\{\mathbb{S}_{i}\}_{i=1,2}
the relation
|\mathbb{S}_{1}|+|\mathbb{S}_{2}|=|\mathbb{S}|
implies that the mass of
\kappa_{\varepsilon}
and
\rho_{\varepsilon}
is controlled by a single free parameter, for example
|\mathbb{S}_{1}|
. However the constraint space prescribes the total mass of
\rho
and three curve closure conditions
{\mathcal{J}}(U)=0.
The limiting functions will not generically satisfy these four constraints.
A finite energy
\Gamma
-limit with
(\varepsilon,\delta)\to(0,0)
for
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}
would generically require that the functional form of the energy possesses
a minimum of five double-nodal points
.

To simplify the presentation we exclude sequences that converge to a zero length or to an unbounded curve by replacing the length penalty term with a length constraint. Specifically the minimization is taken over
{\mathcal{A}}_{L}\times H^{1}_{M}(\mathbb{S})
where

(2.17)

{\mathcal{A}}_{L}:=\{U\in{\mathcal{A}}\,\bigl|\,|\Gamma_{U}|=L\},

and
H^{1}_{M}(\mathbb{S})
denotes the subset of
H^{1}(\mathbb{S})
that satisfies the mass constraint (
2.13
).

The co-area formula is the essence of the lim-inf component of the
\Gamma
-limit. The application of the co-area formula is greatly simplified by scaling the
\kappa
dependence from
F

through a change of dependent variable from
(\rho,\kappa)
to
(\bar{\rho},\kappa)
where,

(2.18)

\bar{\rho}(\rho,\kappa)=\frac{\rho-\rho_{-}(\kappa)}{\textrm{P}(\kappa)}.

and
P
is the
\rho
nodal gap function

(2.19)

\textrm{P}(\kappa)=\rho_{+}(\kappa)-\rho_{-}(\kappa)\geq\underline{\textrm{P}},

for some constant
\underline{\textrm{P}}>0.

From the inverse map

\rho(\bar{\rho},\kappa)=\textrm{P}\bar{\rho}+\rho_{-},

the
F
double well reduces to a factored form

\textrm{F}(\rho,\kappa)=\textrm{P}^{4}(\kappa)\textrm{F}_{0}(\bar{\rho}),

where the scaled
\textrm{F}_{0}
is a traditional double well potential

(2.20)

\textrm{F}_{0}(\bar{\rho})=4\bar{\rho}^{2}(\bar{\rho}-1)^{2}.

Introducing the rescaled functions

(2.21)

\displaystyle\bar{f}(\kappa,\bar{\rho})

\displaystyle:=f(\kappa,\textrm{P}\bar{\rho}+\rho_{-}),

\displaystyle\bar{R}(\kappa,\bar{\rho})

\displaystyle:=\bar{\rho}\nabla_{s}\textrm{P}+\nabla_{s}\rho_{-},

allows the energy functional (
2.12
) to be written in a
\Gamma
-limit accessible formulation,

(2.22)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(U,\bar{\rho})

\displaystyle=\int_{S}\Big(\frac{\delta}{2}|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}+\frac{\bar{f}(\kappa,\bar{\rho})}{\delta}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left|\textrm{P}\nabla_{s}\bar{\rho}+\bar{R}(\kappa,\bar{\rho})\right|^{2}+\frac{\textrm{P}^{4}\textrm{F}_{0}(\bar{\rho})}{\varepsilon}\Big)d\sigma.

To form the lim inf and lim sup sequences we introduce the sets
\mathbb{S}_{\pm}
on which the
\rho_{\varepsilon}
functions converge to the
F
nodal curves
\rho=\rho_{\pm}(\kappa)
respectively.
The transition set
\textrm{T}=\partial\mathbb{S}_{+}

records the domain points in
\mathbb{S}
where the density switches between the two
F
nodal curves.
In the unscaled density variable
\rho
the limiting density functions will be associated to
T
and
U
via the map

(2.23)

\hat{\rho}(U,\textrm{T})=\chi_{+}(s)\rho_{+}(\kappa)+\chi_{-}(s)\rho_{-}(\kappa),

where
\chi_{\pm}
are the characteristic functions associated to
\mathbb{S}_{\pm}.
The scaled version of
\hat{\rho}
is
\chi_{+},
that is
\bar{\hat{\rho}}=\chi_{+}.

The primitive of the root-double well

(2.24)

\vartheta(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\sqrt{F_{0}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}t,

plays a significant role in the
\Gamma
-limit, in particular
\vartheta_{1}:=\vartheta(1)
is a component of the surface tension associated to a density transition.

Our main result is the
\Gamma
-convergence of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}
to

(2.25)

{\mathcal{F}}_{0}(U,\textrm{T}):=\int_{\mathbb{S}}\frac{\delta}{2}|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}+\frac{1}{\delta}f(\kappa,\hat{\rho}(\textrm{T},U))\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\sqrt{2}\vartheta_{1}\int_{\textrm{T}}\textrm{P}^{3}(\kappa)\,d\mathcal{H}^{0},

where
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
is zero-dimensional Hausdorff measure.
With this notation we have the following result.

Theorem 3
.

On the admissible set
{\mathcal{A}}_{L}\times H^{1}_{M}(\mathbb{S})
the energy (
2.12
) with
\beta=0
satisfies

(2.26)

\Gamma\!-\!\lim\limits_{\hskip-10.84006pt\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}={\mathcal{F}}_{0}.

More specifically, suppose that
(U_{\varepsilon},\rho_{\varepsilon})\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{L}\times H_{M}^{1}(\mathbb{S})
is an energy bounded sequence for
{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}
as defined in (
2.12
). If
(U_{\varepsilon},\rho_{\varepsilon})\to(U,\rho)
in
L^{2}\times L^{1}(\mathbb{S})
, then

U\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}
and there exists a finite transition set
\textrm{T}\subset\mathbb{S}
such that
\rho=\hat{\rho}(U;\textrm{T})
. In addition we have

\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(U_{\varepsilon},\rho_{\varepsilon})\geq{\mathcal{F}}_{0}(U,\textrm{T}).

Remark 2
.

The Hausdorff integral term in the
\Gamma
-limit energy (
2.22
) admits the explicit form

\int_{\textrm{T}}\textrm{P}^{3}(\kappa)\,\textrm{d}{\mathcal{H}}^{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\rho_{+}(\kappa(s_{i}))-\rho_{-}(\kappa(s_{i}))\right)^{3}.

Modulo other constraints, transitions are energetically favorable when they occur where the curvature
\kappa(s_{i})
enjoys a small
F
nodal gap.

Proof.

It is sufficient to verify that the lim-inf and lim-sup conditions hold for the scaled energy (
2.22
).
Fix
\delta
, and assume that
\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\bar{\rho}
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{S})
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and
\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(U_{\varepsilon},\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})<\infty
. Since the curve length is fixed at
L
, we use scaled arc-length parameterization for which the arc length is spatially constant, e.g.
g_{\varepsilon}\equiv L.

Since
\delta
is fixed we have the
\|\kappa_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S})}
is uniformly bounded. By passing to a subsequence, we have
\kappa_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\kappa
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{S})
,
\kappa_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\kappa
in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})
, and
\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\bar{\rho}
point-wise almost everywhere as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. In particular
U\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}
satisfies the closure constraints and the length constraint. By Fatou’s lemma, we have

(2.27)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}}\textrm{P}^{4}(\kappa)F_{0}(\bar{\rho})\,\textrm{d}\sigma=\int_{\mathbb{S}}\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\textrm{P}^{4}(\kappa)F_{0}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{S}}\textrm{P}^{4}(\kappa)F_{0}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})\leq\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\varepsilon{\mathcal{F}}(U_{\varepsilon},\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})=0.

Therefore,
F_{0}(U,\bar{\rho})=0
and
\bar{\rho}
takes the values
\{0,1\}
almost everywhere. In particular
\bar{\rho}=\chi_{+}
for some set
\mathbb{S}_{+}
defined through a transition set
\textrm{T}=\partial\mathbb{S}_{+}
. Moreover
\bar{\textrm{R}}(\kappa_{\varepsilon},\rho_{\varepsilon})\to\bar{\textrm{R}}(\kappa,\hat{\rho})
in

and
\|\bar{\textrm{R}}(\kappa_{\varepsilon},\rho_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{1}}
is bounded independent of
\varepsilon.

This implies that

(2.28)

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left|\textrm{P}\nabla_{s}\bar{\rho}+\bar{R}(\kappa_{\varepsilon},\bar{\rho})\right|^{2}+\frac{\textrm{P}^{4}F_{0}(\bar{\rho})}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\textrm{P}^{2}|\nabla_{s}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{\textrm{P}^{4}}{\varepsilon}F_{0}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})+O(\varepsilon),

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{2}\textrm{P}^{3}|\nabla_{s}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}|\sqrt{F_{0}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})}+O(\varepsilon),

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{2}\textrm{P}^{3}|\nabla_{s}\vartheta(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})|+O(\varepsilon),

where the error is measured in the
L^{1}(\mathbb{S})
norm.
In addition from Fatou’s Lemma the rescaled
\bar{f}
from (
2.21
)
satisfies

\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{S}}\bar{f}(\kappa_{\varepsilon},\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})\,\textrm{d}\sigma\geq\int_{\mathbb{S}}\bar{f}(\kappa,\chi_{+})\,\textrm{d}\sigma.

Combining these arguments

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(U_{\varepsilon},\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}}\frac{\delta}{2}|\nabla_{s}\kappa_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{\bar{f}(\kappa_{\varepsilon},\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})}{\delta}+\sqrt{2}\textrm{P}^{3}|\nabla_{s}\vartheta(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})|+O(\varepsilon)\right)\mathrm{d}\sigma,

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{S}}\frac{\delta}{2}|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}+\frac{1}{\delta}\bar{f}(\kappa,\chi_{+})\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\sqrt{2}\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}}\textrm{P}^{3}(\kappa)|\nabla_{s}\vartheta(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})|\,\textrm{d}\sigma+O(\varepsilon)\right),

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{S}}\frac{\delta}{2}|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}+\frac{1}{\delta}f(\kappa,\chi_{+})\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\sqrt{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}}\textrm{P}^{3}(\kappa)|\nabla_{s}\vartheta(\chi_{+})|\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Applying the co-area formula to the last integral, and using that fact that
\vartheta(\chi_{+})
takes only the values

\{0,\vartheta_{1}\}
, allows the right-hand side to be repackaged as the
\Gamma
-limit energy (
2.26
).

To establish the lim-sup condition, consider a limit point
(U,\bar{\rho})\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}\times BV(\mathbb{S},\{0,1\})
. Since the energy of the limiting sequence is bounded below by
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}(U,\textrm{T})
from (

is bounded away from zero, it follows that the transition set

associated to a finite energy function is finite. We denote this set by

for some

. We assume that
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small that the transition gaps satisfy

for each

subject to the periodic identification

The recovery sequence is constructed by rescaling the

heteroclinic solution to

\displaystyle g^{2}\partial_{s}^{2}\varphi=\textrm{F}_{0}^{\prime}(\varphi),

on the line
\mathbb{R}.
We translate

so that

Fixing
T
, for each

\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(s)=\varphi\left(\frac{z\textrm{P}(\kappa)}{\varepsilon}\right),

where the

and

As depicted in Figure

We calculate that

By assumption

We deduce that

Evaluating the energy functional (

s
is close to

2.6.
Critical Point System for
\Gamma
-limit Energy

Critical points of (
2.25
) subject to the closure constraint (
1.7
) and the mass constraint (
2.13
) satisfy the Euler-Lagrange system. Details on the tangent plane to the manifold of intrinsic representations of closed curves are given in
[
13
]
[Prop. 3.1]. To simplify the derivation of the Euler Lagrange system we replace the constrained energy with an unconstrained Lagrangian energy associated to intrinsic coordinates
U\in{\mathcal{A}}
and transition set
\textrm{T},

\displaystyle\Lambda(U,\textrm{T}):={\mathcal{F}}_{0}(U,\textrm{T})+\lambda\cdot{\mathcal{J}}(U)+\mu\int_{\mathbb{S}}\hat{\rho}(\kappa,\textrm{T})\textrm{d}\sigma,

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\hat{\rho}
is defined in (
2.23
). The admissible class is opened to
U\in H^{1}_{L}(\mathbb{S})
, corresponding to curves of length
L
but which may not be closed. The Lagrange multipliers
\lambda
are adjusted to enforce the
{\mathcal{J}}(U)=0
. Moreover, for critical points of the Lagrangian energy their curvature and arc-length are generically continuous across
T
but
\nabla_{s}\kappa
may be discontinuous there. Along an admissible path
(U(\tau),\textrm{T}(\tau))
for
\tau\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\textrm{d}\tau}{\mathcal{F}}_{0}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}}\left(\delta\nabla_{s}\kappa\nabla_{s}\kappa_{\tau}+\frac{1}{\delta}\left(\partial_{\kappa}f+\partial_{u}f\frac{\textrm{d}\hat{\rho}}{\textrm{d}\kappa}\right)\kappa_{\tau}+\partial_{g}{\mathcal{F}}_{0}g_{\tau}\right)\mathrm{d}\sigma+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\left(\frac{\delta}{2}[\![|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}]\!]+\textrm{P}^{3}(\kappa))\right)\dot{s}_{i}+3\vartheta_{1}\textrm{P}^{2}(\kappa)\textrm{P}^{\prime}(\kappa)\kappa_{\tau}\right)\Bigl|_{s=s_{i}}.

However for
U\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S})_{L}
we have
g\equiv L
and
g_{\tau}=0.
Integrating by parts we have the reduction

\displaystyle\frac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}\tau}{\mathcal{F}}_{0}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}}\left(-\delta\Delta_{s}\kappa+\frac{1}{\delta}\left(\partial_{\kappa}f+\partial_{\rho}f\frac{\textrm{d}\hat{\rho}}{\textrm{d}\kappa}\right)\right)\kappa_{\tau}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\left(\frac{\delta}{2}[\![|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}]\!]+3\vartheta_{1}\textrm{P}^{2}(\kappa)\textrm{P}^{\prime}(\kappa)\right)\dot{s}_{i}+\left(3\vartheta_{1}\textrm{P}^{2}(\kappa)\textrm{P}^{\prime}(\kappa)-\delta[\![\nabla_{s}\kappa]\!]\right)\kappa_{\tau}\right)\Bigl|_{s=s_{i}},

The jump function
{\mathcal{J}}
and mass constraint do not involve
\nabla_{s}\kappa
so their variations are classical

The contributions from

3.
Incompressible Interface Flows

As an application of a coupled curvature-density energy we derive the gradient flow of an energy that penalizes variation in membrane density. In the limit of infinite penalty we show that the gradient flow converges to a normal velocity that renders the membrane incompressible while representing its fluidic properties through a flux of membrane material. We consider a base energy
\hat{\mathcal{F}}(U,\rho)
that couples interface structure
U
to embedded agents
\rho
. To this we add a scalar membrane density
\rho_{m}:\mathbb{S}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and an associated penalty term that pins
\rho_{m}
to a reference value,
\rho_{*}>0
. We ignore the volumetric contribution of the embedded agents in this application. The compressibility penalized energy takes the form

(3.1)

{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(U,\rho,\rho_{m})=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{S}}\textrm{P}(\rho_{m})\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\hat{\mathcal{F}}(U,\rho),

where
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
has the form (
2.3
) and
\textrm{P}:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is strictly convex with a non-degenerate minima at
\bar{\rho}_{m}.
For positive, self-adjoint membrane mass and embedded agent gradients
{\mathcal{G}}_{m}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{\rho}
the gradient flow takes the form

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{D\rho}{Dt}

\displaystyle=-{\mathcal{G}}_{\rho}\,\partial_{\rho}\hat{\mathcal{F}},

\displaystyle\frac{D\rho_{m}}{Dt}

\displaystyle=-{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\partial_{\rho_{m}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}{\mathcal{G}}_{m}\textrm{P}^{\prime}(\rho_{m}),

\displaystyle\textrm{V}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\hat{\textrm{V}}+\frac{\kappa}{\varepsilon}\left(\rho_{m}\textrm{P}^{\prime}-\textrm{P}\right),

on the periodic domain
\mathbb{S}.
Here
\hat{\textrm{V}}=\hat{\textrm{V}}(U,\rho)
denotes the normal velocity induced by
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
. The system is coupled through the normal velocity which appears in the convective derivatives.
The dissipation mechanism takes the form

(3.3)

\frac{d}{dt}{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(U,\rho,\rho_{m})=-\int_{\mathbb{S}}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left|{\mathcal{G}}_{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\textrm{P}^{\prime}(\rho_{m})\right|^{2}+\left|{\mathcal{G}}_{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{\rho}{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}+\left|\textrm{V}_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\right)\textrm{d}\sigma\leq 0.

We establish, formally in a general framework, and rigorously in a simplified framework that the
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
limit yields an incompressible evolution of the membrane mediated through a Fredholm operator constructed as identity plus compact, curvature-weighted, inverse-Helmholtz operator.

3.1.
Formal Analysis of Incompressible Limit

We fix the penalty term
P
in the generic form

\textrm{P}(\rho_{m})=\frac{1}{2}(\rho_{m}-1)^{2},

where the equilibrium density is scaled to
\rho_{*}=1.
For simplicity we choose
{\mathcal{G}}_{m}=-\Delta_{s}
and use the co-moving frame, in which the tangential velocity
\textrm{W}=0.

Writing out the material derivative the gradient flow (

(3.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho

\displaystyle=-\kappa\rho\textrm{V}_{\varepsilon}-{\mathcal{G}}_{\rho}\,\partial_{\rho}{\mathcal{F}}+\frac{\kappa^{2}\rho}{\varepsilon}\frac{1-\rho_{m}^{2}}{2},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{m}

\displaystyle=-\kappa\rho_{m}\textrm{V}_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(\Delta_{s}\rho_{m}+\frac{\kappa^{2}(\rho_{m}^{2}-\rho_{m}^{3})}{2}\right),

\displaystyle\textrm{V}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\hat{\textrm{V}}-\frac{\kappa}{\varepsilon}\frac{1-\rho_{m}^{2}}{2}.

Assuming the variables

\textrm{V}_{\varepsilon}.
Dropping

(3.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}}\rho

\displaystyle=\rho\kappa^{2}\frac{1-\rho_{m}^{2}}{2},

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}}\rho_{m}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{s}\rho_{m}+\frac{\kappa^{2}(\rho_{m}-\rho_{m}^{3})}{2},

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}}\kappa

\displaystyle=-\textrm{G}\left(\kappa\frac{1-\rho_{m}^{2}}{2}\right),

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}}g

\displaystyle=-g\kappa^{2}\frac{1-\rho_{m}^{2}}{2}.

The combination of the convective and variational terms in the membrane density system have produced an Allen-Cahn flow with strong diffusion associated to a
curvature-weighted double-well potential

\partial_{t_{1}}\rho_{m}=\Delta_{s}\rho_{m}-\kappa^{2}\textrm{F}^{\prime}(\rho_{m}),

where
F(\rho_{m})=\frac{1}{8}(1-\rho_{m}^{2})^{2}.

For initial data
\rho_{m}>0
the solutions tend to

so long as
\kappa
is non-zero on a set of positive mass.
The curvature equation is well posed if

Values of
\rho_{m}>1
lead to an ill-posed curvature equation which requires a regularization by higher-order differential terms from the normal velocity, see
[
4
]
and
[
3
]
for examples of the regularization in formal and rigorous frameworks respectively. The first and fourth equations in (
3.5

\partial_{t_{1}}(\rho g)=0,
which shows that the
embedded densities
\rho
are conserved under the fast flow. The density of
\rho
within any labeled segment

is conserved since changes in
\rho
are balanced by reciprocal changes in arc-length
g
.

At quasi-equilibrium the dynamics are driven by perturbations of
\rho_{m}
from equilibrium. To resolve this structure and its impact on the full system we expand

in (
3.4

The

\textrm{V}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}}\rho_{m,1}

\displaystyle=-\hat{\textrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\kappa+(\Delta_{s}-\kappa^{2})\rho_{m,1},

\displaystyle\textrm{V}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\rho_{m,1}\kappa+\hat{\textrm{V}}.

This yields a fast-slow decomposition. The membrane mass correction term

\underline{\mathrm{H}}^{-1}:L^{2}(\mathbb{S})\mapsto H_{\rm per}^{2}(\mathbb{S})

In this quasi-steady regime the normal velocity reduces to an incompressible form

where the incompressibility operator

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{D}\rho}{\hat{D}t}

\displaystyle=-{\mathcal{G}}_{\rho}\,\partial_{\rho}{\mathcal{F}},

\displaystyle\textrm{V}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{I}}\,\hat{\textrm{V}},

The operator

Proposition 4

4.1.

Theorem 6

(4.14)

Proof.

For notational simplicity we suppress the

4.2.
Technical Lemmas

This section contains definitions and
technical results needed in the proof of Theorem
6
.
We introduce the metric-dependent norms

(4.31)

\|u\|_{H^{k}}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{S}}|\nabla_{s}^{k}u|^{2}+|u|^{2}\,\textrm{d}\sigma

for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}.

From the classical argument

u^{2}(s_{1})-u^{2}(s_{2})=2\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}u\nabla_{s}u\textrm{d}\sigma,

we obtain the estimate

|u(s)|^{2}\leq\left(|\Gamma|^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{2}}+2\|\nabla_{s}u\|_{L^{2}}\right)\|u||_{L^{2}},

from which we infer that

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{|\Gamma|}}\|u\|_{H^{1}},

and that each
H^{k}
is an algebra for
k\geq 1.

The first step is to establish the uniform coercivity of
\underline{\mathrm{H}}.

Lemma 9
.

For each
U\in{\mathcal{A}}
the spectrum of
\underline{\mathrm{H}}(U)
is real and non-negative. Fix
M>0
in
\mathbb{R}
and form the set
{\mathcal{A}}_{M}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
comprised of
U
whose image
\Gamma_{U}
satisfies the length bound
|\Gamma_{U}|=\int_{\mathbb{S}}\textrm{d}\sigma\leq M.
Then the spectrum of
\underline{\mathrm{H}}(U)
is uniformly bounded away from zero, that is there exists
\nu_{1}=\nu_{1}(M)
given in (
4.35
) such that

(4.32)

\langle\underline{\mathrm{H}}u,u\rangle\geq\nu_{1}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2},

for all
U\in{\mathcal{A}}_{M}
and all
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}).

In particular the spectrum satisfies for all
U\in{\mathcal{A}}_{M}.

Proof.

Since
\underline{\mathrm{H}}
is self adjoint and its bilinear form is nonnegative its spectrum is also real and non-negative.
To establish a uniform lower bound on its spectrum over
{\mathcal{A}}_{+}
we use the constraint
\int_{\mathbb{S}}\kappa\textrm{d}\sigma=2\pi
.
From Young’s inequality we have

\displaystyle 2\pi=\int_{\mathbb{S}}\kappa\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}}\kappa^{2}\textrm{d}\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}}\textrm{d}\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

from which we deduce the lower bound

\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}\geq\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{M}}.

The bilinear form induced by
\underline{\mathrm{H}}
takes the form

\displaystyle\langle\underline{\mathrm{H}}u,u\rangle_{\mathbb{S}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}}|\nabla_{s}u|^{2}+\kappa^{2}u^{2}\,\textrm{d}\sigma.

Define the set

S_{\kappa}:=\left\{s\in\mathbb{S}\,\bigl|\,\kappa^{2}>\frac{2\pi^{2}}{M^{2}}\right\}.

Since
|\mathbb{S}|=1
the lower bound on the
L^{2}
norm of
\kappa
implies that

(4.33)

\int_{S_{\kappa}}\kappa^{2}\,\textrm{d}\sigma\geq\frac{4\pi^{2}}{M}-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{M^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{S}\backslash S_{\kappa}}\textrm{d}\sigma\geq\frac{2\pi^{2}}{M}.

Consider a function
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S})

with
\|u\|_{L^{2}}=1
and

\langle\underline{\mathrm{H}}u,u\rangle_{\mathbb{S}}\leq\nu,

for some
\nu>0.

This implies that

\int_{S_{\kappa}}u^{2}\textrm{d}\sigma<\frac{\nu M^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}.

Let
u_{\kappa}\in\mathbb{R}
denote the infimum of
|u|
over
S_{\kappa}.
Using (
4.33
) we have

\nu\geq\int_{S_{\kappa}}|\nabla_{s}u|^{2}+\kappa^{2}u^{2}\textrm{d}\sigma\geq u_{\kappa}^{2}\int_{S_{\kappa}}\kappa^{2}\textrm{d}\sigma\geq\frac{2\pi^{2}}{M}u_{\kappa}^{2},

and hence

u_{\kappa}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\nu M}{2\pi^{2}}}.

The set
S_{u}:=\{s\in\mathbb{S}\,\bigl|\,u^{2}>\frac{1}{2M}\}
, is not empty. Indeed, since
\|u\|_{L^{2}}=1
we have the lower bound on its size

\int_{S_{u}}u^{2}\textrm{d}\sigma=1-\int_{\mathbb{S}\backslash S_{u}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}\sigma\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Fix an arc-length function
g
. If
u
takes values
u_{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
with
u_{+}>u_{-}
over a separation of
\ell
, then a standard calculus of variations argument on the quantity

\int_{0}^{\ell}|\nabla_{s}u|^{2}\textrm{d}\sigma,

for
u\in H^{1}([0,\ell])
subject to
u(0)=u_{-}
and
u(\ell)=u_{+},
shows that the minimum is achieved at

u_{*}(s)=\frac{u_{+}-u_{-}}{\int_{0}^{\ell}\textrm{d}\sigma}\int_{0}^{s}\textrm{d}\sigma.

This minimizer has constant surface gradient

\nabla_{s}u_{*}=\frac{u_{+}-u_{-}}{\int_{0}^{\ell}\textrm{d}\sigma}.

Consequently if any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S})
attains the values
u_{\pm}
over a separation
\ell,
we have the lower bound

(4.34)

\int_{\mathbb{S}}|\nabla_{s}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}\sigma>\frac{(u_{+}-u_{-})^{2}}{\int_{0}^{\ell}\textrm{d}\sigma}\geq\frac{(u_{+}-u_{-})^{2}}{M}.

If
\nu<\pi^{2}/M
then we have
u_{\kappa}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2M}}
and may takes these values as our
u_{\pm}
. This affords a lower bound on the gradient integral and hence on
\nu
. Specifically (
4.34
) yields

\nu\geq\langle\underline{\mathrm{H}}u,u\rangle>\frac{1}{M}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2M}}-\sqrt{\frac{\nu M}{2\pi^{2}}}\right)^{2}>\frac{1}{2M^{2}}\left(1-\frac{M}{\pi}\sqrt{\nu}\right)^{2}.

This inequality can only hold if
\nu
satisfies a universal lower bound

(4.35)

\nu\geq\nu_{0}:=\frac{c}{M^{2}},

for a constant
c.
This establishes the uniform
L^{2}
coercivity of
\underline{\mathrm{H}}
over
{\mathcal{A}}_{M}
. To obtain
H^{1}
coercivity we observe
that

\displaystyle\langle\underline{\mathrm{H}}u,u\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}}|\nabla_{s}u|^{2}\textrm{d}\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\langle\underline{\mathrm{H}}u,u\rangle,

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}}|\nabla_{s}u|^{2}\textrm{d}\sigma+\frac{\nu_{0}}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Taking
\nu_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\min\{1,\nu_{0}\}

yields the
H^{1}
coercivity.
∎

We extend the
H^{1}
coercivity of
\underline{\mathrm{H}}
to
H^{2}
coercivity subject to an additional assumption on
\kappa.

Lemma 10
.

Assume that
U\in{\mathcal{A}}_{M}
satisfies
\|\kappa\|_{H^{1}}\leq M.

Then there exist constant
\nu_{2}>0
, depending only upon
M
such that

\displaystyle\|\underline{\mathrm{H}}u\|_{L^{2}}\geq\nu_{2}\|u\|_{H^{2}},

for all
u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{S}).

Proof.

From Young’s inequality the
H^{1}
coercivity implies the
H^{1}
bound

\|\underline{\mathrm{H}}u\|_{L^{2}}\geq\nu_{1}\|u\|_{H^{1}}.

On the other hand, integrating by parts yields the estimate

\displaystyle\|\underline{\mathrm{H}}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\|\Delta_{s}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-c\|\kappa\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2},

\displaystyle\geq\|\Delta_{s}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-c\frac{M^{2}}{\nu_{1}^{2}}\|\underline{\mathrm{H}}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
c
is a numerical constant independent of
M
. We recover the
H^{2}
bound for
\nu_{2}=c\nu_{1}/M.

∎

5.
Discussion

There are several applications to bio-membrane dynamics to which the analysis presented here can apply. A crucial application is to incorporate the incompressibility condition to gradient flows of interfacial energies that incorporate membrane adhesion and repulsion energies. Such effects are almost universal in biological membranes with embedded charges, see
[
13
]
for a presentation of these. The electrostatic interactions yield local attraction while waters of solvation present a barrier to membrane fusion. The adhesion induces considerable local membrane stretching, incorporating an incompressibility prefactor could have a significant impact on the evolution.

A second application it to membranes formed from blends of components. The membrane incompressibility interacts non-trivially with packing and steric effects. Some components of membranes, particularly cholesterol, serve as interstitial agents that increase density without significantly contributing to volume. This suggests extending the membrane density to a multicomponent model with constituents
\rho:=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{N})
that that form a blended membrane with a
nonlinear packing energy. The incompressible penalty can take the form

{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(U,\rho)=\int_{\mathbb{S}}\frac{(\rho^{t}\textrm{P}\rho-1)^{2}}{2\varepsilon}\,\textrm{d}\sigma,

where the packing compatibility matrix
\textrm{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
is a fixed symmetric matrix with positive eigenvalues. The larger eigenvalues corresponding to eigenvectors whose constituent blend form an unfavorable (less dense) packing, while smaller eigenvalues represent favorable (more dense) packings. In the limit
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
the packings are constrained to reside on the
N-1
dimensional packing ellipse

\Sigma^{N-1}(\textrm{P}):=\left\{\rho\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\,\bigl|\,\rho^{t}\textrm{P}\rho=1\right\}.

See
[
2
]
for discussion of models of glycolipids and their role in membrane stability.

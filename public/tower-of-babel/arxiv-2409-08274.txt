A functional for Spin(7) forms
Calin Lazaroiu, C. S. Shahbazi

Abstract
. We characterize the set of all conformal Spin ⁡ ( 7 ) \mathrm{Spin}(7) forms on an oriented and spin Riemannian eight-manifold ( M , g ) (M,g) as solutions to a homogeneous algebraic equation of degree two for the self-dual four-forms of ( M , g ) (M,g) . When M M is compact, we use this result to construct a functional whose self-dual critical set is precisely the set of all Spin ⁡ ( 7 ) \mathrm{Spin}(7) structures on M M . Furthermore, the natural coupling of this potential to the Einstein-Hilbert action gives a functional whose self-dual critical points are conformally Ricci-flat Spin(7) structures. Our proof relies on the computation of the square of an irreducible and chiral real spinor as a section of a bundle of real algebraic varieties sitting inside the Kähler-Atiyah bundle of ( M , g ) (M,g) .

Preliminaries on Spin(7) forms

This section recalls the basic algebraic theory of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
structures and
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
forms. Let
V
be an oriented eight-dimensional real vector space and let
V^{\ast}
be its dual, which we endow with the orientation induced from
V
. Denote by
\mathrm{Gl}(V)=\mathrm{Aut}(V)
the Lie group of linear automorphisms of
V
and by
\mathrm{Gl}_{+}(V)
its identity component, which can be realized as the group of orientation-preserving linear transformations of
V
.

2.1.
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
structures on an eight-dimensional vector space

By definition, a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
structure
on
V
is a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
subgroup of the group
\mathrm{GL}_{+}(V)
. A well-known way to describe such a structure is to give a non-zero four-form on
V
, called the
Cayley form
of the structure, with certain properties. We start by recalling this description. Consider first the real vector space
\mathbb{R}^{8}
and denote by
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{8})
its standard basis, with dual basis
(e^{1},\ldots,e^{8})
. Let
h_{0}
be the usual Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{R}^{8}
and endow this space with its canonical orientation, for which the Euclidean volume form reads:

\nu_{o}=e^{1}\wedge e^{2}\wedge e^{3}\wedge e^{4}\wedge e^{5}\wedge e^{6}\wedge e^{7}\wedge e^{8}\,.

The standard
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
structure on
\mathbb{R}^{8}

canonical Cayley form

\Phi_{0}\in\wedge^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{8})^{\ast}
, which is defined as follows

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}:=e^{1}\wedge e^{2}\wedge e^{3}\wedge e^{4}+e^{1}\wedge e^{2}\wedge e^{5}\wedge e^{6}+e^{1}\wedge e^{2}\wedge e^{7}\wedge e^{8}+e^{1}\wedge e^{3}\wedge e^{5}\wedge e^{7}

(1)

\displaystyle-e^{1}\wedge e^{3}\wedge e^{6}\wedge e^{8}-e^{1}\wedge e^{4}\wedge e^{5}\wedge e^{8}-e^{1}\wedge e^{4}\wedge e^{6}\wedge e^{7}+e^{5}\wedge e^{6}\wedge e^{7}\wedge e^{8}+e^{3}\wedge e^{4}\wedge e^{7}\wedge e^{8}

\displaystyle+e^{3}\wedge e^{4}\wedge e^{5}\wedge e^{6}+e^{2}\wedge e^{4}\wedge e^{6}\wedge e^{8}-e^{2}\wedge e^{4}\wedge e^{5}\wedge e^{7}-e^{2}\wedge e^{3}\wedge e^{5}\wedge e^{8}-e^{2}\wedge e^{3}\wedge e^{6}\wedge e^{7}

This four-form is self-dual with respect to the Euclidean metric

Definition 2.1
.

A
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
form
on
V
is a four-form
\Phi\in\wedge^{4}V^{\ast}
for which there exists an orientation-preserving linear isomorphism
f\colon V\to\mathbb{R}^{8}
satisfying
\Phi=f^{\ast}\Phi_{0}
, where
f^{\ast}\colon\wedge(\mathbb{R}^{8})^{\ast}\to\wedge V^{\ast}
denotes the pull-back of forms by
f
. A
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{-}
form
is defined similarly but using an orientation-reversing linear isomorphism
f
. A
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
form
on
V
is either a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
or a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{-}
form defined on
V
.

In particular, pulling back the canonical Cayley form by any orientation-reversing linear automorphism of
\mathbb{R}^{8}
(for example, the reflection in the hyperplane with equation
x^{8}=0
) produces a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{-}
form. A four-form
\Phi\in\wedge^{4}V
is a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
form on
V
if and only if there exists a positively-oriented basis
(\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{8})
of
V
in which
\Phi
is given by the relation obtained from (
1
) by replacing
e_{i}
with
\epsilon_{i}=f^{-1}(e_{i})
for all
i=1,\ldots,8
. Every
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
form
\Phi
comes together with an Euclidean metric on
V
given by:

h_{\Phi}=f^{\ast}h_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{8}\epsilon^{i}\otimes\epsilon^{i}

which makes
\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{8}
into an orthonormal basis. Similar statements hold for
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{-}
forms, except that the relevant bases of
V
are negatively oriented. If
\Phi\in\wedge^{4}V
is a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
form, then
\lambda\Phi
is also a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
form for any
positive

\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

A
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
form
\Phi
determines its associated metric
h_{\Phi}
algebraically as explained in
[
22
, Section 4.3]
; we say that
h_{\Phi}
is
induced by
\Phi
. Let
\nu_{h_{\Phi}}=f^{\ast}\nu_{0}
be the corresponding volume form. A
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
form
\Phi
is self-dual with respect to
h_{\Phi}
in the given orientation of
V
and satisfies
\Phi\wedge\Phi=14\nu_{h_{\Phi}}
(as can be verified for
\Phi_{0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{8}
and pulling back by
f
), a condition which amounts to
|\Phi|_{h_{\Phi}}=\sqrt{14}
. On the other hand, a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{-}
form
\Phi
is anti-self-dual and satisfies
\Phi\wedge\Phi=-14\nu_{h_{\Phi}}
, which again amounts to
|\Phi|_{h_{\Phi}}=\sqrt{14}
. Notice that a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
form can induce
any
Euclidean metric on
V
. The stabilizer of a four-form
\Phi\in\wedge^{4}V^{\ast}
inside
\mathrm{GL}_{+}(V)
is isomorphic with the group
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
if and only if there exists a sign factor
\pm
such that
\pm\Phi
is a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
form. There are exactly two conjugacy classes of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
subgroups in
\mathrm{Gl}_{+}(V)

[
34
]
. The subgroups belonging to one of these stabilize
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
forms, while those belonging to the other stabilize
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{-}
forms defined on
(V,h)
. Moreover, two
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
forms have the same stabilizer if and only if they differ by multiplication with a
positive
real number. This establishes a bijection between the conjugacy class of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}_{+}(V)
and the set of positive homothety classes of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
forms. The conjugacy classes of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
and
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{-}
forms inside
\mathrm{GL}(V)
combine into a single conjugacy class within
\mathrm{Gl}(V)
; for example, the reflection of
V
in any hyperplane contained in
V
conjugates
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
forms to
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{-}
forms inside
\mathrm{GL}(V)
.

Remark 2.2
.

If
\Phi
is a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
form on
V
, then
\Phi
and
-\Phi
have the same stabilizer, which is a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{+}(V)
. However,
-\Phi
is
not
a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
form although it can be written as
f^{\ast}(-\Phi_{0})
for some orientation-preserving isomorphism
f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{8}
. A simple continuity argument shows that the overall sign of the canonical Cayley form cannot be changed by acting with an element of
\mathrm{GL}_{+}(8,\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{8}
. It is traditional to fix the overall sign of the canonical Cayley form in order to avoid double counting of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}_{+}(V)
.

2.2.
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
structures on an eight-dimensional Euclidean space

Let
(V,h)
be an oriented Euclidean vector space of dimension eight. Let
\mathrm{O}(V,h)\subset\mathrm{GL}(V)
be the disconnected group of all orthogonal transformations of
(V,h)
and let
\mathrm{SO}(V,h)\subset\mathrm{O}(V,h)
be its identity component. These groups act naturally on
\wedge V^{\ast}

\ast_{h}
of

\wedge^{4}V^{\ast}=\wedge^{4}_{+}V^{\ast}\oplus\wedge^{4}_{-}V^{\ast}

where
\wedge^{4}_{+}V^{\ast}

\wedge^{4}_{-}V^{\ast}
are the eigenspaces of self-dual and anti-self-dual four forms, which correspond to the eigenvalues

and
-1
. We denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}
the scalar product induced by

on the exterior algebra:

\wedge V^{\ast}:=\oplus_{k=0}^{8}\wedge^{k}V^{\ast}

and by
|\,\cdot\,|_{h}
the corresponding norm. For later convenience, we will often work with the dual oriented Euclidean space
(V^{\ast},h^{\ast})
instead of
(V,h)
. By definition, a
metric
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
structure
on
(V,h)
is a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{+}(V)
which is contained in
\mathrm{SO}(V,h)
. There exist two conjugacy classes of such subgroups in
\mathrm{SO}(V,h)
, which correspond to the two conjugacy classes in
\mathrm{GL}_{+}(V)
; they are called respectively the conjugacy classes of metric
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
and metric
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{-}
structures on
(V,h)
. They combine into a single conjugacy class of
\mathrm{O}(V,h)
.

A

of

. The stabilizer of a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)

if and only if

is a conformal
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
form on
(V,h)

3.
Spinor polyform squares on an eight-dimensional oriented Euclidean space

In this section, we use an algebraic characterization of the square of a real and irreducible chiral spinor in eight Euclidean dimensions, which follows from the general theory developed in
[
8
]
, to obtain an intrinsic quadratic description of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{\pm}
forms on an oriented Euclidean space
(V,h)
of dimension eight. We use this result to construct a homogeneous algebraic potential defined on the space
\wedge^{4}_{+}V^{\ast}
which vanishes on
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
forms. We begin with a brief introduction to the Kähler-Atiyah model of the Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}(V^{\ast},h^{\ast})
. This formalism was used extensively in references
[
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
, where it was shown to play an important role in understanding certain geometric problems that arise in string and M theory. This inspired both
[
8
]
as well as the potential that we construct below
[
29
]
.

3.1.
The Kähler-Atiyah algebra

As mentioned earlier, it is useful to consider the dual quadratic space
(V^{\ast},h^{\ast})
of the eight-dimensional oriented Euclidean space
(V,h)
, which we endow with the orientation induced from
V
. We denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}
the scalar product induced by
h
on the space
\wedge V^{\ast}
of polyforms defined on
V
and by
|\,\cdot\,|_{h}
the corresponding norm. Notice that
|\nu_{h}|_{h}=1
. Let
\mathrm{Cl}(V^{\ast},h^{\ast})
be the real Clifford algebra of
(V^{\ast},h^{\ast})
, which we identify with the
Kähler-Atiyah algebra

(\wedge V^{\ast},\diamond_{h})
of
(V,h)
. The latter is an associative and unital
\mathbb{R}
-algebra obtained by equipping
\wedge V^{\ast}
with the
geometric product

\diamond_{h}\colon\wedge V^{\ast}\times\wedge V^{\ast}\to\mathbb{R}
. The geometric product is an
h
-dependent deformation of the wedge product obtained by the
\mathbb{R}
-linear and associative extension of the following expression:

\alpha\diamond_{h}\beta=\alpha\wedge\beta+\iota_{\alpha}\beta~,

where
\alpha\in V^{\ast}
is a one-form,
\beta\in\wedge V^{\ast}
is any polyform and
\iota_{\alpha}\beta
denotes the contraction of
\beta
with the vector
\alpha^{\sharp_{h}}\in V
which is
h
-dual to the one-form
\alpha
. The unit of the algebra
(\wedge V^{\ast},\diamond_{h})
is the unit
1\in\mathbb{R}=\wedge^{0}V^{\ast}\subset\wedge V^{\ast}
. This algebra has a natural
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading given by the direct sum decomposition of
\wedge V^{\ast}
into the subspaces:

\wedge^{\mathrm{even}}V^{\ast}:=\oplus_{k=0}^{4}\wedge^{2k}V^{\ast}~~,~~\wedge^{\mathrm{odd}}V^{\ast}:=\oplus_{k=0}^{4}\wedge^{2k+1}V^{\ast}

As an associative and unital algebra, the Kähler-Atiyah algebra is isomorphic to the Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}(V^{\ast},h^{\ast})
through the
h
-dependent
Chevalley-Riesz isomorphism
(see
[
24
,
26
,
8
]
), which we denote by:

(5)

\Psi_{h}\colon\mathrm{Cl}(V^{\ast},h^{\ast})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}(\wedge V^{\ast},\diamond_{h})

Proposition 3.6

For any

\mathcal{S}(\alpha)=16\alpha^{(0)}

3.3.
The spinorial description of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
structures

Since
\nu_{h}
squares to the identity in the Kähler-Atiyah algebra, we have
\Psi^{\gamma}_{h}(\nu_{h})^{2}=\mathrm{id}_{\Sigma}
and the vector space
\Sigma
splits as a
\Scr B
-orthogonal direct sum
\Sigma=\Sigma^{+}\oplus\Sigma^{-}
, where
\Sigma^{\pm}
are the eigenspaces of
\gamma_{h}(\nu_{h})
corresponding to the eigenvalues
\pm 1
. The subspaces define the
chiral
irreducible representations of the even Clifford subalgebra
\mathrm{Cl}^{\mathrm{even}}(V^{\ast},h^{\ast})
of chirality
\pm 1
, respectively. The spin group
\mathrm{Spin}(V^{\ast},h^{\ast})\subset\mathrm{Cl}^{\mathrm{even}}(V^{\ast},h^{\ast})
naturally acts on
\Sigma^{\pm}
through the representation induced by
\gamma
. The stabilizer of any nonzero chiral spinor is a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
of subgroup of
\mathrm{Spin}(V^{\ast},h^{\ast})\simeq\mathrm{Spin}(8)

\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{\pm}
conjugacy orbits of
\mathrm{Spin}(V^{\ast},h^{\ast})

\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{\pm}
conjugacy classes of
\mathrm{SO}(V^{\ast},h^{\ast})\simeq\mathrm{SO}(V,h)
through the double covering morphism:

(27)

\lambda:\mathrm{Spin}(V,h)\rightarrow\mathrm{SO}(V,h)

Since the stabilizer of a chiral spinor depends only on its homothety class, this gives a bijection between the
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{\pm}
subgroups of
\mathrm{Spin}(V^{\ast},h^{\ast})
and the real projective space
\mathbb{P}(\Sigma^{\pm})
. Let
(e^{1},\ldots,e^{8})
is any orthonormal basis of
(V^{\ast},h^{\ast})
. It is well-known that a nonzero four-form
\Phi\in\wedge^{4}V^{\ast}
is a conformal

\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{\pm}
form on
(V,h)

In this subsection, we present an algebraic characterization of conformal

\epsilon:=\frac{|q|_{h}}{|\Phi|_{h}}\ll 1

Proof.

We have:

Using Lemma

Combining these relations gives (
54

\epsilon=\frac{|q|_{h}}{|\Phi|_{h}}\ll 1

\displaystyle=|\Phi|_{h}^{3}\left[1+\frac{3}{2}\frac{|q|_{h}^{2}+2\langle\Phi,q\rangle_{h}}{|\Phi|_{h}^{2}}+\frac{3}{8}\left(\frac{|q|_{h}^{2}+2\langle\Phi,q\rangle_{h}}{|\Phi|_{h}^{2}}\right)^{2}\right]+\mathrm{O}\left(\epsilon^{3}\right)

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\Phi\in\wedge^{4}_{+}V^{\ast}

\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}

Proposition

\displaystyle(\mathrm{d}_{(h,\Phi)}W)(k,0)=-\frac{\sqrt{14}}{4}\Phi_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}}\Phi_{j_{1}j_{2}j_{3}j_{4}}\Phi_{k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4}}k^{i_{1}k_{1}}h^{i_{2}k_{2}}h^{i_{3}j_{1}}h^{i_{4}j_{2}}h^{j_{3}k_{3}}h^{j_{4}k_{4}}

.

\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
form on
(M,g)
if and only if it satisfies the degree-two homogeneous equation:

\sqrt{14}\Phi\Delta_{2}^{g}\Phi+12|\Phi|_{g}\Phi=0

In this case, the Riemannian metric induced by
\Phi
is
g_{\Phi}=14^{-\frac{1}{4}}|\Phi|_{g}^{\frac{1}{2}}g
.

Let
\wedge^{4}_{+}T^{\ast}M
be the vector bundle of self-dual four-forms on
(M,g)
, whose space of sections we denote by
\Omega^{4}_{+}(M)
. The potential
W_{h}
of Section
4
globalizes to a function
W_{g}\colon\wedge^{4}_{+}T^{\ast}M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
which vanishes on the zero section and restricts on each fiber to a function which is homogeneous of degree three. The vertical critical locus of
W_{g}
defines a bundle of cones
C_{+}(M,g)\subset\wedge^{4}_{+}T^{\ast}M
that is fiber-wise isomorphic to
C_{+}(V,h)
. The conformal
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
forms on
(M,g)
are sections of the bundle of pointed cones
C_{+}(M,g)\backslash\left\{0\right\}
obtained from
C_{+}(M,g)
by removing the zero section of
\wedge^{4}_{+}T^{\ast}M
.

For the remainder of this section, we assume that
M
is compact and without boundary. Consider the following functional defined on the Fréchet space
\Omega^{4}_{+}(M)
of smooth self-dual four-forms over
(M,g)
, which is the natural global version of the cubic potential (
49
):

\mathcal{W}_{g}\colon\Omega^{4}_{+}(M)\to\mathbb{R}\,,\quad\Phi\to\mathcal{W}_{g}(\Phi):=\int_{M}\left[\frac{\sqrt{14}}{3}\langle\Phi\Delta_{2}^{g}\Phi,\Phi\rangle_{g}+4\langle\Phi,\Phi\rangle^{\frac{3}{2}}_{g}\right]\nu_{g}~.

Here
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
is the fiberwise Euclidean pairing induced by
g
on
\wedge T^{\ast}M
. Since the restriction of the integrand of
W_{g}
to the fibers of the vector bundle
\wedge_{+}T^{\ast}M
of self-dual four-forms coincides with the cubic potential of the previous section, Theorem
4.5
implies the following:

Corollary 5.2
.

A smooth nowhere-vanishing self-dual four-form is a smooth conformal
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
form on
(M,g)
if and only if it is a critical point of
the functional
\mathcal{W}_{g}\colon\Omega^{4}_{+}(M)\to\mathbb{R}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{W}_{g}
vanishes when evaluated on any smooth conformal
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
form.

As in Subsection
4.2
, we extend
\mathcal{W}_{g}
to a functional
\mathcal{W}\colon\mathrm{Met}(M)\times\Omega^{4}(M)\to\mathbb{R}
defined on arbitrary pairs
(g,\Phi)\in\mathrm{Met}(M)\times\Omega^{4}(M)
by the same formula:

(70)

\mathcal{W}(g,\Phi):=\int_{M}\left[\frac{\sqrt{14}}{3}\langle\Phi\Delta_{2}^{g}\Phi,\Phi\rangle_{g}+4\langle\Phi,\Phi\rangle^{\frac{3}{2}}_{g}\right]\nu_{g}~,

where
\mathrm{Met}(M)
denotes the convex cone of Riemannian metrics on
M
.

Definition 5.3
.

A pair
(g,\Phi)\in\mathrm{Met}(M)\times\Omega^{4}(M)
is called
self-dual
if the four-form
\Phi
is self-dual with respect to the metric
h
. A pair
(g,\Phi)\in\mathrm{Met}(M)\times\Omega^{4}(M)
is called a
conformal
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
pair
on
M
if
\Phi
is a conformal
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
form on the Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
.

Theorem
4.13
implies the following:

Corollary 5.4
.

Suppose that the eight-manifold
M
is compact and spin. Then a self-dual pair
(g,\Phi)\in\mathrm{Met}(M)\times\Omega^{4}(M)
is a conformal
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
pair on
M
if and only if it is a critical point of the functional
\mathcal{W}\colon\mathrm{Met}(M)\times\Omega^{4}(M)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

Corollary
5.4
gives a variational description of all
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
structures on a compact spin manifold, modulo the self-duality condition that needs to be checked independently. In particular, every
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
form
\Phi
identifies canonically with a point of the set
\mathrm{Crit}_{+}(\mathcal{W})
of self-dual pairs which are critical points of
\mathcal{W}
as follows:

\Phi\mapsto(g_{\Phi},\Phi)\in\mathrm{Crit}_{+}(\mathcal{W})

where
g_{\Phi}
is the Riemannian defined by
\Phi
on
M
. It would be interesting to study the geometric significance of the non-self-dual critical points of
\mathcal{W}
, which might provide a weakening of the notion of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
structure on
M
.

Remark 5.5
.

Of course, there is no assurance in general that any smooth conformal
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
structure exists on
(M,g)
— and hence the critical locus of
\mathcal{W}_{g}
or
\mathcal{W}
may be empty. In fact, it is well-known that the following statements are equivalent for a compact and spin Riemannian eight-manifold
(M,g)
whose half spinor bundles we denote by
S^{+}
and
S^{-}
(see
[
30
, Theorem 10.7]
):

•

M
admits a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
form.

•

M
is spin and either
\chi(S^{+})=0
or
\chi(S^{-})=0
.

•

The following relation holds for an appropriate choice of orientation on
M
:

(71)

p_{1}(M)^{2}-4p_{2}(M)+8\chi(M)=0~,

where
\chi(M)
is the Euler class of
M
,
\chi(S^{\pm})
is the Euler class of
S^{\pm}
, and
p_{1}(M)
is the first Pontryagin class of
M
.

It follows that the existence of critical points of the functional
\mathcal{W}_{g}
are obstructed by the topological condition (
71
).

We note that the potential
\mathcal{W}_{g}
extends naturally to a function defined on the Banach space
\mathrm{L}^{3}(\wedge^{4}_{+}T^{\ast}M)
of self-dual cubic integrable four-forms on
(M,g)
. This follows by writing
\mathcal{W}_{g}
as:

(72)

\mathcal{W}_{g}(\Phi)=\int_{M}\left[\frac{\sqrt{14}}{3}\langle\Phi\Delta_{2}^{h}\Phi,\Phi\rangle_{g}+4|\Phi|^{3}_{g}\right]\nu_{g}

and noticing that the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Corollary
3.12
imply:

|\langle\Phi\Delta_{2}^{h}\Phi,\Phi\rangle_{g}|_{g}\leq\sqrt{16}|\Phi|_{g}^{3}

Using the generalized Hölder inequality, it follows that the integrands of the two terms appearing in (
72
) are absolutely integrable functions (elements of
\mathrm{L}^{1}(M,g)
) provided that
\Phi\in\mathrm{L}^{3}(\wedge^{4}_{+}T^{\ast}M)
.

We end this section by considering a natural coupling of the functional (
70
) to the Einstein-Hilbert action. Consider the following functional which depends on an arbitrary constant
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
:

(73)

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(g,\Phi):=\int_{M}\left[s^{g}+\frac{\sqrt{14}}{3}\lambda\langle\Phi\Delta_{2}^{h}\Phi,\Phi\rangle_{g}+4\lambda|\Phi|^{3}_{g}\right]\nu_{g}~.

Here
s^{g}
is the scalar curvature of the Riemannian metric
g\in\mathrm{Met}(M)
and
\Phi\in\Omega^{4}(M)
is any four-form. Lemma
4.12
, implies the following characterization of the self-dual critical points of
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
.

Proposition 5.6
.

A self-dual pair
(g,\Phi)\in\mathrm{Met}(M)\times\Omega^{4}(M)
is a critical point of the functional:

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}\colon\mathrm{Met}(M)\times\Omega^{4}(M)\to\mathbb{R}

if and only if the metric
g
is Ricci flat and
\Phi
is a conformal
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{+}
structure on
(M,g)
.

Exploring this potential in more detail could be interesting, especially in the context of gradient flows of Spin(7) structures. The geometric or topological significance of the non-self-dual critical points of
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
, if any, remains to be ascertained.

Knowledge, attitudes, and practices of patients with age-related macular degeneration (AMD) towards Anti-VEGF treatment under one-stop intravitreal injection model
Authors: Liangzhang Tan, Zhiqiong Ma, Qi Miao, Shoukuan Liu, Yongtao Li, Yifeng Ke, Xinjun Ren
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
To evaluate the knowledge, attitudes, and practices (KAP) of patients with age-related macular degeneration (AMD) regarding anti-VEGF treatment within a one-stop intravitreal injection service model. A cross-sectional study was conducted from July 1, 2023, to January 31, 2024, at the One-stop Intravitreal Injection Center at Tianjin Medical University Eye Hospital. Data were collected using a specially designed KAP questionnaire. The study successfully enrolled 493 participants. Of these, 214 (43.4%) were aged between 70 and 79 years, and 226 (45.8%) had been diagnosed with AMD for 1–3 years. The median knowledge, attitude, and practice scores were 6.00 [3.00, 8.00] (possible range: 0–9), 42.00 [37.00, 48.00] (possible range: 11–55), and 23.00 [18.00, 27.00] (possible range: 6–30), respectively. Multivariate logistic regression for practice showed that knowledge score (OR = 1.329, P < 0.001), attitude score (OR = 1.150, P < 0.001), aged 60–69 years (OR = 0.398, P = 0.022) were independently associated with proactive practice. Mediating effect significance showed that knowledge directly affected attitude (β = 0.625), knowledge (β = 0.398) and attitude (β = 0.503) directly affected practice (P < 0.001). Meanwhile, knowledge also indirectly affected practice through attitude (β = 0.315, P < 0.001). Regarding the factors impacting their selection of anti-VEGF treatment, 63.5% of patients emphasized the crucial role of its effectiveness. Additionally, over 40% of patients considered both family circumstances and insurance coverage to be highly significant in their decision-making process. This study found that patients with AMD exhibit suboptimal knowledge yet hold positive attitudes and engage actively in practices concerning anti-VEGF treatment within a one-stop intravitreal injection service model. It is recommended that healthcare providers enhance educational interventions at intravitreal injection centers to boost patient knowledge, which is likely to further improve attitudes and proactive management practices among AMD patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-77999-y.

Introduction

Age-related macular degeneration (AMD) is a primary cause of severe and irreversible vision loss in elderly populations worldwide. It is reported that approximately 10% of individuals over the age of 65 years and 25% of those over 75 years in developed countries are diagnosed with AMD1. As life expectancy increases, the prevalence of AMD is expected to rise significantly, with projections suggesting that nearly 300 million people globally will be affected by 20402. This surge in prevalence highlights AMD as a major global health concern, adversely impacting population well-being. Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) plays a crucial role in angiogenesis and the formation of choroidal neovascularization, which are central to the development of neovascular AMD (nAMD). Currently, the intravitreal injection of anti-VEGF molecules is the standard of care, successfully preventing severe vision loss in patients with nAMD3,4.

Despite the efficacy of VEGF inhibitors in treating nAMD, real-life adherence to anti-VEGF treatment is significantly lower than that observed in pivotal randomized clinical trials (RCTs). This discrepancy leads to undertreatment and considerably worse treatment outcomes compared to those reported in RCTs5,6. Factors such as long distances to healthcare facilities and the frequency of follow-up visits have been identified as primary reasons for non-adherence7. The one-stop clinic model consolidates consultation, diagnosis, and care planning into almost a single visit, supported by a multidisciplinary team including consultants, nurses, and imaging technicians, thus catering to patient needs efficiently8,9. Additionally, it aligns with strategies recommended by the National Health Service Institute for Innovation and Improvement to reduce waiting times and enhance patient experiences (https://www.england.nhs.uk/improvement-hub/wp-content/uploads/sites/44/2017/11/Seven-Ways-to-No-Delay.pdf). In the context of AMD, the application of a one-stop clinic model for intravitreal injections could significantly improve treatment adherence and outcomes.

The KAP framework posits that knowledge is foundational to behavioral change, with attitudes acting as the driving forces behind this transformation10. According to the KAP theory, the pathway to changing human behavior involves three sequential stages: acquiring knowledge, forming attitudes, and ultimately developing practices/behaviors11. It is important to note that cognitive changes stemming from knowledge acquisition do not necessarily lead to behavioral changes. Instead, these changes must first alter perceptions, which in turn influence behaviors12. Real-world adherence to treatment of AMD is often suboptimal, leading to less favorable outcomes. The one-stop service model for intravitreal injections of anti-VEGF agents simplifies the treatment process, enhancing patient compliance and satisfaction, and is likely to significantly improve outcomes in vision preservation. Studying this specific patient group offers valuable insights into the barriers and challenges they face when adopting new treatment modalities, thereby facilitating the optimization of overall treatment effectiveness. Thus, this study aims to evaluate the KAP of patients with AMD regarding anti-VEGF treatment within a one-stop intravitreal injection service model, highlighting the potential of this approach to improve treatment adherence and outcomes.

Methods

Study design and subjects

This cross-sectional study was conducted at the One-stop Intravitreal Injection Center of Tianjin Medical University Eye Hospital from July 1, 2023, to January 31, 2024. The research focused on patients diagnosed with nAMD. Ethical approval for the study was granted by the Ethics Committee of Tianjin Medical University Eye Hospital (Ethics approval number: 2023KY-25), and all participants provided signed informed consent prior to enrollment.

The inclusion criteria specified that participants should be patients diagnosed with nAMD, but without any related ocular complications such as endophthalmitis, vitreous hemorrhage, or retinal detachment. The exclusion criteria ruled out individuals with ocular conditions that could impair vision, including keratopathy, primary glaucoma, and age-related cataract, as well as those with major physical, neurological, or psychiatric illnesses that could confound the study’s outcomes.

Data collection was carried out through face-to-face interviews using questionnaires administered in the clinic, ensuring direct interaction with the participants and allowing for immediate clarification of any queries regarding the questionnaire items.

Hospital policies on insurance and costs

This study was conducted at the one-stop intravitreal injection Center of Tianjin Medical University Eye Hospital. The three main anti-VEGF drugs offered by the center include, Aflibercept (Eylea, Regeneron Pharmaceuticals) (priced at 4100 RMB [585 USD]), Conbercept (Chengdu Kanghong Biotech Co., China) (priced at 3452 RMB [492 USD]), and Ranibizumab (Lucentis, Genentech) (priced at 3673 RMB [524 USD])—are all covered by basic medical insurance. The hospital provides reimbursement for up to nine injections. Most of the center’s patients are covered by basic medical insurance, and patients typically pay about 1,500 to 2,000 RMB (214 to 285 USD) per intravitreal injection after reimbursement.

Questionnaire introduction

The questionnaire for this study was refined based on input from five experts, including three specializing in retinal diseases and two in retinal surgery. A pilot test was conducted with a sample of 32 respondents to evaluate the reliability of the questionnaire, yielding a Cronbach’s α coefficient of 0.786. The final questionnaire was structured into four dimensions: demographic information, knowledge, attitude, and practice, with a detailed version provided in appendix.

Demographic information collected included gender, age, education level, medical history, and medication usage. The knowledge dimension consisted of nine questions focused on AMD and anti-VEGF treatment, with correct answers scored as 1 point and incorrect or unclear answers scored as 0 points, allowing for a total possible score range from 0 to 9 points. The attitude dimension comprised 11 questions designed to assess participants’ confidence in, and perceived benefits of, anti-VEGF treatment, along with their overall consideration of the treatment decision-making process. Responses were measured using a five-point Likert scale that ranged from extremely positive (5 points) to extremely negative (1 point), with the total scoring range from 11 to 55 points; higher scores indicated more positive attitudes towards anti-VEGF treatment. The practice dimension included six questions related to knowledge acquisition, adherence to treatment protocols, follow-up routines, and dietary habits concerning anti-VEGF treatment, utilizing a five-point Likert scale that ranged from always (5 points) to never (1 point), and a total possible scoring range of 6 to 30 points; higher scores denoted more proactive practices. Adequate knowledge, positive attitudes, and proactive practices were defined as achieving scores that surpassed 70% of the maximum possible score in each respective dimension13.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using R version 4.3.2. Continuous data were tested for normality. The t-test or the Wilcoxon Mann-Whitney test was used when comparing two groups. For comparisons among three or more groups, ANOVA or the Kruskal-Wallis test was employed. Pearson’s correlation coefficient was applied to analyze correlations between scores across different dimensions for normally distributed data, whereas Spearman’s correlation coefficient was used for non-normally distributed data. Practice scores were analyzed as dependent variables in single-factor and multi-factor regression analyses to explore the relationships between demographic data and scores across different dimensions. Variables with a P-value less than 0.1 in single-factor analyses were included in the multi-factor regression analysis. P-values were rounded to three decimal places, considering values less than 0.05 as statistically significant. Incorporating the KAP theoretical framework, structural equation modeling (SEM) was utilized to examine whether attitudes served as a mediating variable between knowledge and practices. The SEM analysis included calculations of both direct and indirect effects. Goodness of fit for the SEM model was assessed using the following thresholds: RMSEA less than 0.08, SRMR less than 0.08, TLI greater than 0.8, and CFI greater than 0.8.

Results

We collected a total of 493 questionnaires. The formal experiment demonstrated good internal consistency for the overall scale and each subscale, evidenced by a Cronbach’s α coefficient of 0.941. Most of the respondents (64.1%) earn about 1,000 to 5,000 RMB per month. Among the participants, 73.2% are urban residents, over 80% have an education level below high school, 45.8% have a history of AMD ranging from 1 to 3 years, and 57.8% of patients choose imported drugs. Basic information of participants and KAP score.

The distribution of knowledge dimensions shown that the three questions with the highest number of participants choosing the “Unclear” option were “AMD is mainly caused by degenerative changes in retinal pigment epithelial cells and retina.” (K1) with 53.3%, “If there is poor response to one anti-VEGF drug, consideration should be given to switching to another anti-VEGF drug.” (K7) with 46.7%, and “Complications of neovascular AMD include macular edema, hemorrhage, and exudation.” (K2) with 40% (Table S1).

When it comes to attitudes, 11.4% were very concerned about possible side effects of anti-VEGF drugs, as well as 25.4% were concerned (A2). Regarding the factors affecting their choice of anti-VEGF treatment, the most commonly reported were treatment efficacy (A7.1), family financial situation (A7.5), and health insurance reimbursement (A7.4) (Table S2).

Responses to the practice dimension showed that 13.8% never actively sought information about anti-VEGF treatment for AMD (P1), 9.9% never paid attention to their diet and lifestyle habits during VEGF treatment (P6), and 16.4% seldom discussed in detail with their ophthalmologists prior to choosing a treatment (P2) (Table S3).

In the correlation analysis, significant positive correlations were found between knowledge and attitude (r = 0.476, P < 0.001), knowledge and practice (r = 0.615, P < 0.001), as well as attitude and practice (r = 0.563, P < 0.001), respectively (Table S4).

Multivariate logistic regression for practice showed that knowledge score (OR = 1.329, 95% CI: [1.197,1.475], P < 0.001), attitude score (OR = 1.150, 95% CI: [1.101,1.200], P < 0.001), aged 60–69 years (OR = 0.398, 95% CI: [0.181,0.876], P = 0.022) were independently associated with proactive practice (Table 2).

Discussion

Patients with AMD exhibited suboptimal knowledge levels but maintained generally positive attitudes and proactive practices towards anti-VEGF treatment within a one-stop intravitreal injection service model. Given the positive influence of knowledge on both attitudes and practices, enhancing patient education about AMD and anti-VEGF treatment within this service model is recommended to improve outcomes and patient engagement.

The differences in KAP scores with age, particularly the slight variances in attitude but notable in knowledge and practice, suggest that older patients may have similar concerns about their health as younger patients but may lack adequate information or the capacity to act on it effectively. Literature suggests that cognitive decline and less engagement with contemporary digital information sources might contribute to these discrepancies14,15. Despite similar concerns across age groups (reflected in attitude scores), the ability to seek and utilize health information (practice) and grasp treatment implications (knowledge) declines with age. This suggests a need for tailored educational programs that accommodate older adults’ learning preferences and cognitive capacities.

Men exhibited higher knowledge scores than women, which is a finding supported by the literature indicating that men might have better access to or interest in obtaining information about health conditions16. However, the lack of difference in practice scores between genders could indicate that while knowledge acquisition differs, the application of this knowledge in health management practices does not differ significantly between men and women, possibly due to similar levels of health care services accessed by both genders. Significant differences in KAP scores between urban and rural residents, where urban dwellers scored higher, particularly in knowledge and practices, align with studies showing that urban residents often have better access to health resources, including education and treatment facilities17,18. The rural population’s lower scores can be attributed to limited healthcare infrastructure and educational outreach, which impacts their ability to obtain and use health information. This discrepancy underscores the need for targeted health education and services delivery in rural areas.

Education level showed a strong correlation with KPA scores, where individuals with higher education levels displayed significantly better knowledge and practices. This is consistent with the literature that correlates higher education with enhanced health literacy and engagement in proactive health behaviors19,20. Higher education likely equips individuals with better tools for information seeking and critical assessment, enhancing their understanding and management of health conditions. The absence of similar differences in attitude scores across educational levels could suggest a general awareness of AMD’s seriousness irrespective of educational background, although translating this concern into knowledge and practical action appears to depend significantly on educational attainment. Besides, having a family member in healthcare was significantly associated with higher scores in all KAP dimensions, particularly knowledge and practice. This finding could reflect the direct benefits of accessible, reliable health information and encouragement from healthcare-professional family members, enhancing compliance and engagement with treatment protocols.

The multivariate analysis underscores the strong linkage between knowledge and proactive practices, supported by correlation analysis and SEM, which reveals that higher knowledge scores significantly boost both attitudes towards treatment and actual health practices. Studies in chronic disease management affirm that greater patient knowledge is crucial for fostering positive health behaviors and enhancing treatment adherence21,22.

While the majority of participants show a reasonable grasp of the necessity and protocol of anti-VEGF treatments, a significant portion remains uncertain about specific aspects such as the cause of AMD and the specifics of treatment regimens. Attitudinally, although most participants believe in the efficacy of the treatment, concerns about side effects and the overall effectiveness of the treatment indicate a need for better communication and reassurance from healthcare providers. In terms of practices, while many adhere to prescribed treatments and follow-up routines, there’s a noticeable laxity among some regarding proactive engagement with their treatment processes, such as seeking information or consistently monitoring their condition. To holistically address these issues, a comprehensive patient engagement strategy should be implemented that leverages both modern digital platforms and traditional community engagement methods to improve understanding, alleviate concerns, and encourage proactive health management behaviors. Developing educational materials that are easy to understand and accessible is crucial. This could involve creating multimedia content, such as short, informative videos or infographics explaining AMD and anti-VEGF treatments in layman’s terms23,24. Platforms such as WeChat and Douyin can be utilized to distribute this content effectively, reaching patients where they are already active and engaged. Regular interactive sessions, such as webinars or live Q&A sessions hosted by healthcare professionals, can help clarify doubts and address fears regarding treatment side effects and procedures252627. These sessions should be promoted through social media and community centers, ensuring broad participation. Text messaging and app notifications can serve as reminders for treatment procedures, follow-up appointments, and educational prompts, ensuring continuous engagement with the treatment process. These reminders can be personalized based on the patient’s treatment schedule and can include motivational messages to encourage adherence. For patients in rural areas or those with lower educational backgrounds, mobile health units equipped with educational materials and trained health educators could be dispatched to provide on-site education and support. Additionally, visual aids and translated materials in local dialects can help overcome barriers to understanding complex medical information282930.

This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings. First, the cross-sectional design limits the ability to infer causality between the observed knowledge, attitudes, and practices and the outcomes of AMD treatment. Second, the sample was drawn from a single institution, which may not be representative of the broader population of AMD patients, potentially limiting the generalizability of the results. Third, self-reported data may introduce bias, as participants might overestimate their knowledge or compliance. Despite these limitations, the strengths of this study lie in its pioneering exploration of the one-stop intravitreal injection service model, the robust statistical analysis that clearly demonstrates the relationships between KAP, and its practical implications for enhancing patient care in clinical settings.

The study indicates that while patients with AMD generally exhibit positive attitudes and proactive practices toward anti-VEGF treatment, their level of knowledge remains suboptimal within the one-stop intravitreal injection service model. Enhancing patient education programs specifically tailored to improve knowledge about AMD and its treatments could potentially foster even more positive attitudes and proactive health practices among these patients.

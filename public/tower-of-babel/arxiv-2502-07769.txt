Complexity Framework For Forbidden Subgraphs V: Beyond Simple Graphs
Tala Eagling-Vose, Barnaby Martin, Daniel Paulusma, Siani Smith

Abstract
We continue the study of the recently-introduced C123-framework, for (simple) graph problems restricted to inputs specified by the forbidding of some finite set of subgraphs, to more general graph problems possibly involving multiedges and self-loops. We study specifically the problems Multigraph Matching Cut , Multigraph d d -Cut and Partially Reflexive Stable Cut in this connection. The last may be seen as a Surjective Homomorphism problem to a path P 3 P_{3} in which both leaves are looped while the interior vertex is loopless. We consider also another family of Surjective Homomorphism problems to a cycle in which only one vertex is loopless. When one forbids a single (simple) subgraph, our first three problems exhibit the same complexity behaviour as C123-problems, but on finite sets of forbidden subgraphs, the classification appears more complex. While Multigraph Matching Cut and Multigraph d d -Cut have the same classification as C123-problems, already Partially Reflexive Stable Cut fails to have. This is witnessed by forbidding as subgraphs both C 3 C_{3} and H 1 H_{1} (which puts Partially Reflexive Stable Cut in P). Indeed, the difference of behaviour occurs only around pendant subdivisions of nets and pendant subdivisions of H 1 H_{1} . We examine this area in close detail. Our other Surjective Homomorphism, ostensibly somewhat similar to Partially Reflexive Stable Cut , behaves very differently when the input is restricted to some class that is H H -subgraph-free. For example, it is solvable in polynomial time on any class of bounded degree. Also, its hardness will never be preserved under any form of edge subdivision.

As we will now argue, both
Matching Cut
and
Stable Cut
make sense on slightly richer graph structures, possibly allowing multi-edges or self-loops (we call such graphs

or
partially reflexive
, respectively). Specifically, we can define
Matching Cut
and
d
-Cut
on multigraphs and
Stable Cut
on partially reflexive graphs.

In
Matching Cut
a multi-edge can never be in the cutset, while in
Stable Cut
a looped vertex can never be in the cutset. One may deduce the latter from a definition that we do not allow looped vertices to be members of a stable set in a partially reflexive graph. A natural way to explain the role of loops in
Stable Cut
comes from considering it in its guise as the surjective homomorphism problem to the path
P_{3}^{101}
on three vertices, the middle of which is loopless, while the outer two are looped. A
surjective homomorphism
from a graph
G
to a graph
H
is a function
f:V(G)\to V(H)
such that for all
(u,v)\in E(G)
,
(f(u),f(v))
belongs to
H
, and moreover, for every
x\in V(H)
there exists a vertex
u\in V(G)
with
f(u)=x
. Hence, the surjective homomorphisms of an input graph
G
to
P_{3}^{101}
are precisely the stable cuts, where the cutset is read from the inverse image of the loopless vertex. Thus the stable cut must involve only loopless vertices. This realisation of
Stable Cut
as a surjective homomorphism problem
appeared in
[
13
]
.
Note that there are no trivial self-reductions from our problems, on multigraphs or partially reflexive graphs, to simple graphs (e.g., by contracting multiedges). It is instead possible to simulate multiedges by the judicious introduction of cliques and the growing of the vertex set (such an approach is used in
[
13
]
and will appear later in Proposition
10
). This destroys the natural structure of the input. Consequently, these more general versions of our problems are perfectly natural in and of themselves.
In order to avoid confusion with the problems on simple graphs, we will speak henceforth of
Multigraph Matching Cut
,
Multigraph
d
-Cut
and
Partially Reflexive Stable Cut
to emphasise that we allow the correspondingly enriched structures.

The question arises how to judge degree in a multigraph or a partially reflexive graph. From the perspective of the C123-framework, the hardness on subcubic instances is required to produce hardness on
K_{1,4}
-subgraph-free instances, so multi-edges (after they are counted once) should not add to degree and neither should loops. Additionally, the C123-framework only says something about what happens when a finite set of simple graphs is omitted and, for now, let us leave it at that. Let us note that our choices are not arbitrary in order to fit into the C123-framework. Rather, they are what is required for the problems on partially reflexive multigraphs to be studied under the forbidding of simple subgraphs.

Our work begins by making the relatively straightforward observations that
Multigraph Matching Cut
,
Multigraph

-Cut
and

and

-Cut
have the same classification as C123-problems, while

only when one of those vertices is reflexive, and then all the newly introduced vertices are reflexive. Note that our results imply a posteriori that the full C3 condition can not hold.

The behaviour of

on
H

is in P when restricted to

remains NP-complete for

-(subgraph-)free graphs, while being solvable in polynomial time for any class of bounded degree.
Partially Reflexive Stable Cut
is NP-complete on subcubic graphs and

is in polynomial time for

-subgraph-free graphs

3
Multigraph Matching Cut

The
Matching Cut
problem asks if there exists an edge cut in
a graph
G
that is also a matching. Notice this problem is equivalent to asking if
G
can be coloured with two colours such that each vertex is adjacent to at most one vertex coloured with a different colour and both colours are used at least once.
Recall that
Multigraph Matching Cut
is the variant of
Matching Cut
for multigraphs, where a multi-edge can never be in the edge cut.

Proposition 6
.

Multigraph Matching Cut
is polynomial time solvable on every graph class of bounded treewidth.

Proof.

For
(Simple) Matching Cut
, the result is noted in
[
4
]
. Alternatively,

(Simple) Matching Cut
can be expressed by an
\textrm{MSO}_{2}
formula, whereupon we may appeal to Courcelle’s Theorem
[
8
]
. For example, if
M
is a new variable holding on some set of edges,

\exists M\forall x,y\ M(x,y)\rightarrow E(x,y)\wedge\forall zM(x,z)\rightarrow z=y

expresses that
M
is a matching, while

\exists U\exists x,y\ U(x)\wedge\neg U(y)\wedge\forall x,y\ (U(x)\wedge\neg U(y))\rightarrow(E(x,y)\rightarrow M(x,y))

(where
U
is a new unary relation symbol) expresses that
E
minus
M
is disconnected. It follows that
Simple Matching Cut
can be solved in polynomial time on graphs of bounded treewidth. To show the same for
Multigraph Matching Cut
first notice in an instance of
Matching Cut
, if the multiplicity of an edge is at least two, both endpoints must be on one side of the cut meaning additional edges may be removed leaving only multiplicities
1
and
2
. As observed in
[
24
]
, by replacing each multiedge by a triangle we obtain a simple graph which has a matching cut if and only if the original multigraph had a matching cut. This simple graph has at most a polynomial number of additional vertices and so
Multigraph Matching Cut
is also polynomial time solvable on graphs of bounded treewidth. ∎

Proposition 7

(
[

]
)
.

Multigraph Matching Cut
is NP-complete on subcubic graphs.

Given an instance of

3
-Satisfiability

NAE 3-SAT

Lemma 8
.

NAE 3-SAT 0-1
is NP-complete, even for instances in which every clause contains exactly one negative literal.

Proof.

We reduce from

. Given an instance

of

, we create an instance

with one variable

for each vertex

of

v_{i}
and each pair of neighbours of
v_{i}
,
v_{j}
and
v_{k}
, we add a clause
(\bar{x}_{i},x_{j},x_{k})
. The formula

is satisfied when every variable is true since every clause contains both positive and negative literals. The same applies when every variable is false. Additionally, every clause of
\phi
contains exactly one negative literal.

We first assume that
G
has a matching cut

. A further (third) satisfying assignment,

such that

is a matching cut and

is adjacent to both

and

Now assume that
\phi

with at least one true variable and at least one false variable. We obtain a matching cut
(A,B)
of

be the set of vertices whose corresponding variables are set to true and

be the set of vertices whose corresponding variables are false. Both
A
and

are non-empty since

contains at least one true and at least one false variable. No vertex in
A
has more than one neighbour in

and vice versa since this leads to an unsatisfied clause in

∎

Corollary 19
.

\mathrm{SurHom}
(C^{\prime}_{r})
is in P on all classes of bounded-degree.

Proof.

If a graph class has degree bound by
r
, then all but at most

2r-1
. We can brute force the solution to the graphs with diameter at most
2r-1

∎

Theorem

follows directly from Theorem

7
Final Remarks

We have shown that the C123-framework can be leveraged on enriched graph problems involving multi-edges and loops. We have shown that
Multigraph Matching Cut
and
Multigraph
d
-Cut
have the same classification as C123-problems.

However, our results concerning
Partially Reflexive Stable Cut
are the most interesting. Owing to Corollary
16
, we can see that
Partially Reflexive Stable Cut
behaves like a C123-problem on
{\cal H}
-subgraph-free graphs unless
{\cal H}
contains both some pendant subdivision of the net and some pendant subdivision of
H_{1}
. Thus, the differing behaviour comes from precisely such graphs. We have investigated this phenomenon more closely, and the third point of Corollary
16
and Theorem
25
truly isolates the boundary of tractability, since
H^{2,2,2,1}_{1}
is one vertex away from
H^{2}_{1}
. One outstanding question concerns whether
Partially Reflexive Stable Cut
is in P on
\{C_{3},H^{k,k,k,1}_{1}\}
-subgraph-free graphs, for all
k>1
.
We leave the complexity of
\mathrm{SurHom}(C^{\prime}_{5})
tantalisingly open, in the same state as both
\mathrm{SurHom}(C^{*}_{5})
and
\mathrm{SurHom}(C^{*}_{6})
.

Lemma 20
.

There exists a polynomial time algorithm which, given an instance
G
of
Partially Reflexive Stable Cut
, either finds that
G
is a yes-instance or returns an equivalent instance
G^{\prime}
with the following properties:

1.

Every vertex of degree
2
is reflexive and no vertex has degree (
0
or)
1
.

2.

No degree
2
vertex is contained in a triangle.

3.

There are no twin vertices.

4.

For any pair
u,v\in V
, if
N(u)\subseteq N(v)
, then
u
must be reflexive and
v
must not be.

Moreover, any simple graph contained as a subgraph in
G^{\prime}
is contained in
G
.

Proof.

Property 1
:
If a vertex
v
of
G
has degree (
0
or)
1
then either
N(v)
is a stable cut of
G
has a stable cut if and only if
G\setminus\{v\}
does. Therefore, we either find that
G
is a yes-instance or obtain an equivalent instance by deleting
v
.

Let
v
be an irreflexive degree
2
vertex. If
v
is a cut vertex or
N(v)
forms a stable cutset then
G
is a yes-instance. Else,
G
has a stable cutset if and only if

does and we may delete
v
to obtain an equivalent instance. Repeating this process exhaustively, we either find that
G
is a yes-instance or obtain an instance in which every vertex has degree at least
2
and every degree
2
vertex is reflexive.

Property 2
:
Consider a degree
2
vertex

contained in a triangle. This vertex is reflexive by Property 1. Since the neighbourhood of

is a clique,
N(v)
is not a stable cut. Since

is reflexive, it does not belong to any stable cut of
G
. Therefore, any stable cut of

is a stable cut of

. Since exactly one side of any stable cut in

contains a neighbour of
v

has a stable cut if and only if
G\setminus v
does and we may delete
v
. Repeating this process exhaustively, we obtain an instance in which every degree
2
vertex has two independent neighbours.

Property 3
:
Assume that
G
contains a vertex
x
with a true twin

. From Property
2
we may assume
x
has degree at least
3
. Assume that
G
has a stable cut
C
containing at least one of

and
y
. It contains at most one of
x
and
y
since they are adjacent. Without loss of generality,
C
contains
x
. Furthermore, since

and

have the same open neighboburhood, removing

from

does not connect the graph
G\setminus{C}
. Therefore, neither
x
nor
y
are contained in a minimal stable cut of

. Therefore,

has a stable cut if and only if

has a stable cut which does not contain

. In other words, We obtain an equivalent instance by deleting

and making

reflexive.

We now assume that
x
and
y
are false twins. If there exists a stable cut of

containing exactly one, say
x
, of
x
and
y
then, since
x
and
y
have the same open neighbourhood, there exists a stable cut containing neither of them. Therefore, any minimal stable cut

either contains both

and

or neither of the two. In particular, if either of
x
and
y
is reflexive then we may make the other reflexive. Now,
G
has a stable cut if and only if

does. In other words, we obtain an equivalent instance by deleting

.

Repeating these steps exhaustively, we obtain a graph with no twin vertices.

Property 4
:
Note that

and

are non-adjacent since

. Assume that

is irreflexive. If

forms a stable cut then

is a yes-instance, Since every neighbour of

Now assume that both

Good moduli space for constructible sheaves and Stokes functors
Enrico Lampetti

Abstract
. In this paper we give construct good moduli spaces for constructible sheaves and Stokes functors. Derived enhancement of such are also considered.



1.
Introduction

Good moduli spaces for algebraic stacks were introduced by Alper in
[
Alp13
]
and have played a prominent role in moduli theory as generalizations of Mumford’s good GIT quotients
[
MFK94
]
and Abramovich-Olsson-Vistoli’s tame stacks
[
AOV08
]
.
For the sake of this introduction, recall that a good moduli space for an algebraic stack
\mathcal{X}
is a qcqs morphism
q\colon\mathcal{X}\to X
to an algebraic space such that the pushforward along
q
is exact on quasi-coherent sheaves and such that the canonical morphism
\mathcal{O}_{X}\to q_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
is an equivalence.
If a good moduli space
q\colon\mathcal{X}\to X
exists, then it is unique, as
q
is universal for maps from
\mathcal{X}
to algebraic spaces (
[
Alp13
, Theorem 6.6]
). In a similar way that the Keel-Mori Theorem
[
KM97
]
provides an intrinsic way to show that a Deligne-Mumford stack admits a coarse moduli space, a fundamental result of Alper-Halpern Leistner-Heinloth
[
AHLH23
, Theorem A]
provides an intrinsic way to show that an algebraic stack admits a good moduli space.
The existence of a good moduli space for an algebraic stack
\mathcal{X}
has many pleasant consequences.
It allows for example to give a local presentation of
\mathcal{X}
by quotient stacks and prove the compact generation of
\QCoh(\mathcal{X})
(
[
AHR25
]
).
Also, the existence of good moduli spaces can be exploited to construct BPS Lie algebras and unlock the study of the cohomology of
\mathcal{X}
via cohomological Hall algebras, see e.g.
[
DHM22
,
Dav24
,
BDN+25
,
Hen24
]
.
In this paper we study existence of good moduli spaces for moduli of representations.
In particular, we will specialize our discussion to moduli of constructible sheaves and of Stokes data.

Let us introduce our main results.
Consider a compact
\infty
-category
\mathcal{I}\in\Cat_{\infty}^{\omega}
and let
k
be a noetherian ring of characteristic zero.
Then we can form the
\infty
-category

\Rep_{k}(\mathcal{I})\coloneqq\Fun(\mathcal{I};\Mod_{k})

of
k
-linear representation of
\mathcal{I}
. This is a finite type
k
-linear category, that is,
\Rep_{k}(\mathcal{I})
is a compact object in
\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{k}
.
Moreover
\Rep_{k}(\mathcal{I})
is equipped with a standard
t
-structure
\tau_{st}
for which an object
F\in\Rep_{k}(\mathcal{I})
is (co)connective if and only if
F(c)\in\Mod_{k}
is coconnective for every
c\in\mathcal{I}
.
We then define the moduli of flat representations of
\mathcal{I}
, denoted
\mathbf{Rep}_{k}(\mathcal{I})^{\heartsuit}
, by sending a
k
-algebra
A
to the maximal grupoid of
\mathrm{Rep}_{A}(\mathcal{I})\coloneqq\Fun(\mathcal{I};\Mod_{A})
spanned by representation that take values in finitely presented flat
A
-modules.
The moduli
\mathbf{Rep}_{k}(\mathcal{I})^{\heartsuit}
is an algebraic stack locally of finite presentation over
k
.
In this setting, and for
\Spec(\kappa)\to\Spec(k)
a closed point with
\kappa
algebraically closed, our main result is the following

Theorem 1.0.1

(
Theorem

3.0.9
)
.

The algebraic stack
\mathbf{Rep}_{k}(\mathcal{I})^{\heartsuit}
admits a good moduli space
\Rep_{k}(\mathcal{I})
whose
\kappa
-points parametrize semisimple representations of finite dimensional
\kappa
-vector spaces.

The main tool for constructing good moduli spaces is
[
AHLH23
, Theorem A]
.
It states that an algebraic stack of finite presentation over a noetherian ring of characteristic zero admits a separated good moduli space if and only if it is "
\Theta
-reductive" (
Definition

2.3.3
) and "
\mathrm{S}
-complete" (
Definition

2.3.4
).
These two properties are of valuative nature and involve the algebraic stacks

\Theta_{R}\coloneqq\left[{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{A_{1,R}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathbb{G}_{m,R}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{A_{1,R}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathbb{G}_{m,R}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{A_{1,R}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathbb{G}_{m,R}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{A_{1,R}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{G}_{m,R}}$}}}\right],\qquad\overline{\mathrm{ST}}_{R}\coloneqq\left[{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{\Spec(R[s,t]/(st-\pi))}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathbb{G}_{m,R}}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{\Spec(R[s,t]/(st-\pi))}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathbb{G}_{m,R}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\scriptstyle{\Spec(R[s,t]/(st-\pi))}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathbb{G}_{m,R}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\Spec(R[s,t]/(st-\pi))}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{G}_{m,R}}$}}}\right],

for
R
a DVR and

a uniformizer.
For moduli of objects one can explicitly describe
\Theta
-reductiveness and
S
-completeness (
[

]
).
In the setting we focus in the present paper, we can prove that
\mathbf{Rep}_{k}(\mathcal{I})
is
\Theta
-reductive and
\mathrm{S}
-complete differently.
We will show that

satisfies a form of

(

3.0.2
).
The proof follows by an Hartogs type property for locally free sheaves on a regular noetherian ring of dimension
2
.
Nevertheless, in order to characterize points of the good moduli space as the semisimple representations we need the full strength of
[
Lam25
]
.
We will then specialize our discussion to the case of constructible sheaves and Stokes data.
While de former is a direct application of
Theorem

1.0.1
, the latter requires some work.

Let us point out that notion of derived good moduli spaces has been introduced in
[

]
.
In particular,
Theorem

1.0.1
can be enhanced into a statement about derived stack thanks
[

Also,

has some overlap with the work of Fernandez Herrero-Lennen-Makarova
[

]
, where the case of representations of an acyclic quiver is treated between others.

Let us now describe the main applications of this paper.

1.1.
Constructible sheaves

Constructible sheaves are natural generalization of local systems for stratified space
(X,P)
.
More precisely, a constructible sheaf is a sheaf that is locally constant when restricted to every stratum.
Under mild hypothesis on the stratification, a combinatorial description of the category of constructible sheaves is available (
[
Tre09
,
Lur17
,
PT22
,
HPT24
]
).
The work of Haine-Porta-Teyssier
[
HPT24
]
shows that the above holds for any algebraic variety equipped with a finite stratification by Zariski locally closed subsets.
This allows to construct a stack
\mathbf{Cons}^{\heartsuit}_{P}(X)
of constructible sheaves in the same spirit of the character stack: given an algebra
A
over
k
, the
A
-points of
\mathbf{Cons}^{\heartsuit}_{P}(X)
are constructible sheaves whose stalks are finitely presented flat
A
-modules.

Theorem

1.0.1
directly applies to the setting of constructible sheaves, yielding the existence of a good moduli space for
\mathbf{Cons}^{\heartsuit}_{P}(X)
.

1.2.
Stokes data

Stokes data are yet another generalization of local systems.
Several equivalent description of the category of Stokes data are available, one of which consists in including the datum of a filtration to the stalks of a local system.
Clearly, conditions are imposed in this filtration.
While perverse sheaves are linked to the study of differential equation with regular singularities, Stokes data are linked to the study of differential equation with
irregular
singularities.
This is the irregular Riemann-Hilbert correspondence.
For
X
an algebraic variety over
\mathbb{C}
, we can picture the situation as follows:

Algebro/analytic

Topological/combinatorial

Flat connections on
X

Local systems on
X

Regular meromorphic connections on
(X,D)

Local systems on
X\smallsetminus D

Irregular meromorphic connections on
(X,D)

Local systems on
X\smallsetminus D
+ Stokes filtration

Local systems on
X\smallsetminus D
+ Stokes filtration

Regular holonomic
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-modules

Perverse sheaves on
X

Holonomic
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-modules

Enhanced indsheaves

solutions

The passage from left to right is given by taking solutions.
For instance, if
(V,\nabla)
is a flat connection on a complex analytic variety
X
, then

\mathsf{Sol}(V,\nabla)=\{s\in V\mid\nabla(s)=0\}

forms a local system (ultimately, an instance of Cauchy’s theorem on ordinary differential equations).
Remarkably, this process can be inverted, giving rise to an equivalence of categories.
Deligne showed that the first two lines from the top are related by an equivalence of this kind
[
Del70
]
.
Later, Kashiwara extended this equivalence to regular holonomic
\mathcal{D}
-modules and perverse sheaves
[
Kas84
]
, whereas Deligne and Malgrange first, and Mochizuki after
[
Moc11a
]
dealt with irregular connections and Stokes filtered local systems.
Finally, D’Agnolo and Kashiwara
[
DK16
]
managed to obtain a fully faithful solution functor for all holonomic
\mathcal{D}
-modules.

Stacks of Stokes data and their good moduli spaces have been constructed and intensively studied in dimension one in
[
BB04
,
Boa14
,
Boa21
,
BY15
,
DDP18
,
HMW19
]
.
Good moduli spaces in
loc.cit
are constructed via GIT methods.
For an overview of the theory of Stokes data in dimension
1
and of its application, let us refer the reader to Boalch’s
[
Boa13
]
.
Porta-Teyssier have constructed the stacks of Stokes data in any dimension and equip it with a natural derived enhancement in
[
PT25
]
.
The moduli of Stokes data is not a moduli of representations: it parametrizes objects in a full subcategory of a category of representations.
This prevent the application of
Theorem

1.0.1
, but we will show that the same proof essentially goes through.

Before stating our main theorem, let us introduce a bit of notation.
Let
k
be a discrete noetherian ring of characteristic zero.
Let
(X,D)
be a strict normal crossing pair admitting a smooth compactification.
Let
\mathscr{I}
be a good sheaf of unramified irregular values
Definition

4.1.29
.
Associated to
\mathscr{I}
there is a cocartesian fibration in finite posets (
4.1.32
).
We denote by
\mathcal{I}
the total space of this cocartesian fibration.
For a
k
-algebra
A
, we denote by
\St_{\mathcal{I},A}\subset\Rep_{A}(\mathcal{I})
the full subcategory spanned by punctually split and cocartesian functors (
Definition

4.1.2
and
Definition

4.1.4
).

\St_{\mathcal{I},A}
is the category of
A
-linear Stokes functors.
We can form a prestack
\mathbf{St}^{\heartsuit}_{\mathcal{I}}
that assign to a
k
-algebra

the
\infty
-grupoid spanned by Stokes functors that take values in finitely presented flat
A

The stack

is an algebraic stack locally of finite presentation over
k
.
In this setting, for
\Spec(\kappa)\to\Spec(k)
a closed point with
\kappa
algebraically closed, our main result is the following

Theorem 1.2.1
.

The algebraic stack
\mathbf{St}^{\heartsuit}_{\mathcal{I},k}
admits a separated good moduli space
\mathrm{St}^{\heartsuit}_{\mathcal{I},k}
whose
\kappa
-points parametrize pseudo-perfect semisimple objects of
\St_{\mathcal{I},\kappa}^{\heartsuit}
.

Theorem

1.2.1
can be enhanced into a statement about derived stack thanks
[
AHPS23
, Theorem 2.12]
.

1.3.
Linear overview

In
Section

2
we recall the definitions and results about moduli of objects and good moduli spaces we will need in the rest of the paper.

In

2.1.

Recollection 2.1.1

(
[

For
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{k}

\mathcal{C}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{D}\simeq\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{R}}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathcal{D})

where the right hand side is the

This tensor product endows

When

is compactly generated, there is a canonical equivalence

\mathcal{C}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{D}\simeq\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{st}}_{k}((\mathcal{C}^{\omega})^{op},\mathcal{D})

where the right hand side is the
\infty

k

Remark 2.1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E}\in\Pr_{k}^{\mathrm{L}}
.
Then an adjunction

f\colon\mathcal{D}\leftrightarrows\mathcal{E}\colon g

induces an adjunction

\Id_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{k}f\colon\mathcal{C}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{D}\leftrightarrows\mathcal{C}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{E}\colon\Id_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{k}g.

The functor
\Id_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{k}g
corresponds to

g\circ-\colon\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{R}}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathcal{E})\to C\otimes_{k}\mathcal{E}\simeq\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{R}}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathcal{D})

under the equivalences of
2.1.1
.
Its left adjoint
\Id_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{k}f
has no easy description in general.
Notice indeed that post-composition with
f
do not preserve functors that commutes with limits.
Nevertheless, if
\mathcal{C}
is compactly generated one can see that the adjunction
\Id_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{k}f\dashv\Id_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{k}g
corresponds to

f\circ-\colon\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{st}}_{k}((\mathcal{C}^{\omega})^{op},\mathcal{D})\leftrightarrows\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{st}}_{k}((\mathcal{C}^{\omega})^{op},\mathcal{D})\colon g\circ-

under the equivalence of

Corollary 2.1.20
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{k}
be a smooth category and
E\in\mathcal{C}
be a compact generator.
Let
\Spec(A)\in\dAff_{k}
.
Then
F\in\mathcal{C}_{A}
is pseudo-perfect if and only if
F(E)\in\Perf(A)
.

Since
E
generates
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
under shifts, finite colimits and retracts and
\Perf(X)
is closed in
\QCoh(X)
under shifts finite (co)limits and retracts, the result follows.
∎

Recollection 2.1.21
.

Let
\mathcal{I}\in\Cat_{\infty}^{\omega}
.
By
[
Lur25
,
Proposition 068L
]
,the
\infty
-category
\mathcal{I}
is a retract of a finite
\infty
-category.
Hence
\mathcal{I}
has a finite number of equivalence classes.
Let
\left\{c_{a}\right\}_{a\in I}
be a set of representants of the equivalence classes of
\mathcal{I}
.
Denote (abusively) by
c_{a}\colon\left\{*\right\}\to\mathcal{I}
be the morphism that choose the element
c_{a}
.
Let

\ev_{c_{a}}\colon\Rep_{k}(\mathcal{I})\to\Mod_{k}

F\mapsto F(c_{a})

be the evaluation functor and consider its left adjoint

\ev_{c_{a},!}\colon\Mod_{k}\to\Rep_{k}(\mathcal{I})

obtained by left Kan extension.
The family of functors

\left\{\Hom_{\Rep_{k}(\mathcal{I})}(ev_{c_{a},!},-)\simeq\ev_{c_{a}}\colon\Rep_{k}(\mathcal{I})\to\Mod_{k}\right\}_{a\in I}

is jointly conservative.
In particular,
\bigoplus_{a}\ev_{c_{a},!}
is a compact generator of
\Rep_{k}(\mathcal{I})
.

Corollary 2.1.22
.

In the setting of
2.1.21
, let
\Spec(A)\in\dAff_{k}
.
A family
F\in\Rep_{A}(\mathcal{I})\simeq\Rep_{k}(\mathcal{I})_{A}
is pseudo-perfect if and only if
F(c_{a})\in\Perf(A)
for every
a\in I
.

It follows by
Corollary

2.1.20
and
2.1.21
that
F\in\Rep_{A}(\mathcal{I})
is pseudo-perfect if and only if

\bigoplus_{a}\Hom_{\Rep_{A}(\mathcal{I})}(\ev_{c_{a},!}\otimes_{k}A,F)\simeq\bigoplus_{a}F(c_{a})\in\Perf(A).

Since a finite direct sum is perfect if and only if each direct summand is perfect, the result follows.
∎

Definition 2.2.3

\mathcal{X}

Notation 3.1.1

Definition 3.1.2

A stratified space is the data of a continuous map

where

Abusing notations, we will often refer to a stratified space as a pair

For

Definition 3.1.3

Let

For

An object

.
We denote by

Definition 3.1.4
.

Let

.
An object

is atomic if the functor

\Map_{\mathcal{D}}(d,-)\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathrm{Spc}

commutes with colimits. We denote by

the full subcategory spanned by atomic objects.

Definition 3.1.5

An
\infty

is atomically generated if the unique colimit preserving functor

\Fun((\mathcal{D}^{at})^{op},\mathrm{Spc})\to\mathcal{D}

is an equivalence.

Definition 3.1.6

A stratified space

is exodromic if the following conditions ares satisfied:

is atomically generated;

(2)

the subcategory
\Cons_{P}(X;\mathrm{Spc})\subset\Sh(X)
is closed under both limits and colimits;

(3)

the pullback functor

4.
Good moduli spaces for Stokes functors

In this paragraph we prove that the main results of

4.1.
Stokes functors

We recollect in this paragraph the definition and some results about Stokes functors as treated in
[
PT24
,
PT25
]
.
In these two seminal papers several equivalent definitions of Stokes functors are presented.
We have chosen the one that is more explicit and for which it is easier to describe the moduli space we are interested in.
Stokes functors will be defined as a full subcategory of a category of functors, satisfying two properties: Stokes functors are
punctually split
(

4.1.2
) and
cocartesian
(

4.1.4

Recollection 4.1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{X}
be a cocartesian fibration in posets.
Consider the underlying fibration in Sets, denoted
\mathcal{I}^{set}\to\mathcal{X}
and informally described by forgetting the order in each fiber.
There is a natural functor

above
\mathcal{X}
.

Consider a cocomplete
\infty
-category
\mathcal{E}
.
For

we can consider the inclusion

of the fiber above

The restriction functor

Definition 4.1.2

(3)

split if it belongs to the essential image of

The cocartesian condition is a property about "parallel transport" along a path.

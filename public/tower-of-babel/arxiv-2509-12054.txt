Analogues of s s -potential and s s -energy of mass distribution on Cantor dyadic group and their relation to Hausdorff dimension
VALENTIN SKVORTSOV

Abstract
. We introduce an analogue of Riesz s s -potetial and s s -energy, 0 < s < 1 0<s<1 , of a mass distribution μ \mu on the Cantor dyadic group G G by defining a respactive s s -kernel. Then we relate Hausdorff dimension of a set E ⊂ G E\subset G to the value of s s -energy of the mass distribution μ \mu on this set E E . Namely we prove that if on a set E E there exists a mass distribution μ \mu with finite s s -energy, then the Hausdorff dimention of E E is at least s s . The same condiion can be expressed also in terms of Fourier coefficients of μ \mu with respect to Walsh system on the group G G . Keywords: Cantor dyadic group, Walsh system, s s -potential, s s -energy, mass distribution, Hausdorff dimension

2.
Preliminaries

Cantor dyadic group is the set of sequences whose entries are either
0
or
1
, namely,
sequences of the form
x=\{x_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
, where the group operation is a coordinate-wise addition modulo 2, i.e.,
the sum of two sequences
x=\{x_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
and
y=\{y_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

is the sequence
z=\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
given by

z_{j}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $x_{j}=y_{j}$,}\\
1&\text{if $x_{j}\neq y_{j}$.}\end{cases}

The group G contains a strictly
decreasing sequence

G=G_{0}\supset G_{1}\supset G_{2}\ldots\supset G_{n}\supset\ldots

of subgroups
G_{n}:=\{x\in G:x_{j}=0,1\leq j\leq n\}
forming a base of neighborhoods of the zero element.
The subgroups
G_{n}
and all their cosets are clopen sets in the topology introduced above. Let
K_{n}

denote an arbitrary coset of the subgroup
G_{n}
, and let
K_{n}(x)
be the coset of
G_{n}
containing the element

x
, so that
K_{n}(x)=G_{n}+x
. It is clear that the order of the quotient group
G/G_{n}
is

. When it is needed to list
all the cosets
K_{n}
, we number them as
K^{i}_{n},

1\leq i\leq 2^{n},
so that
K^{1}_{n}=G_{n}
and
K^{1}_{n}=K_{n+1}^{1}\cup K_{n+1}^{2}
. Then

(1)

\{0\}=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}K^{1}_{n}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,G\setminus\{0\}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}K^{2}_{n}.

Let
\lambda
denote a normalized Haar measure on
G
. Since the measure
\lambda
is translation invariant,
it follows that, for all

n\geq 0

\lambda(G_{n})=\lambda(K_{n})=\frac{1}{2^{n}}.

It is known (see, for example,
[
6
]
) that

as a topological group is metrizable and a metric can be given as a distance between elements
x,\,y\,\in G
defined by

(2)

\rho(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{-i}|x_{i}-y_{i}|.

Note that with this metric

(3)

\rho(x,y)<2^{-n}\,\,\text{if and only if}\,\,x,\,y\in K_{n}(x)=K_{n}(y).

Letting

be given by its dyadic expansion

n=\sum_{i=0}^{k}n_{i}2^{i},

n_{i}\in\{0,1\}

the

Note that the system of Walsh functions constitutes the system of characters of the group

3.
Kernel of
s
-potential on Cantor group

We define
s
-kernel,

0<s\leq 1,
by

\phi_{s}(x)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $x=0$,}\\
2^{s(n-1)}&\text{if $x\in K_{n}^{2},\,\,n\geq 1$.}\end{cases}

Note that the kernel
\phi_{s}
is positive everywhere on
G\setminus\{0\}
and

(8)

\phi_{s}(x)\geq 2^{ns}\,\,\text{if}\,\,x\in K_{n}^{1}=G_{n}.

Consider trancated kernels:

\phi_{s}^{n}(x)=\begin{cases}\phi(x)&\text{if $x\in G\setminus G_{n}\,\,n\geq 1$}\\
2^{ns}&\text{if $x\in G_{n}\setminus\{0\},$.}\\
0&\text{if $x=0$.}\end{cases}

It is clear that

\phi_{s}^{n}\nearrow\phi_{s}
when
n\to\infty.
Note that the inequality (
8
) implies an estimate

(9)

\phi_{s}^{n}(x)\geq 2^{ms}\,\,\text{if}\,\,x\in K_{m}^{1}=G_{m}\,\,\text{and}\,\,m\leq n.

We need the following properties of the kernel
\phi_{s}^{n}
and its Walsh-Fourier coefficients.

Lemma 1
.

The kernel
\phi_{s}^{n}
is a polinomial with respect to the Walsh system with positive coefficient
\widehat{\phi_{s}^{n}}(k)
for
1\leq k<2^{n}
and

\widehat{\phi_{s}^{n}}(k)\nearrow\widehat{\phi_{s}}(k)
for each
k
when
n\to\infty.

Proof.

It is easy to check, using (
7
), that
\widehat{\phi_{s}^{n}}(k)=0,
for
k\geq 2^{n}.

If
2^{n-1}\leq k<2^{n},
then due to (
6
), (
7
) and the definition of
\phi_{s}^{(n)}
we have

\widehat{\phi_{s}^{n}}(k)=\int_{K_{n}^{1}}\phi_{s}^{n}d\lambda-\int_{K_{n}^{2}}\phi_{s}^{n}d\lambda=2^{ns}2^{-n}-2^{(n-1)s}2^{-n}=2^{n(s-1)}(1-2^{-s})>0.

A similar computation, based on using (
9
), shows that
\widehat{\phi_{s}^{n}}(k)>0
also for
2^{m-1}\leq k<2^{m}
with
m<n.
The inequality
\int_{K_{m}^{1}}\phi^{n+1}_{s}>\int_{K_{m}^{1}}\phi^{n}_{s}
for
m<n
implies that

\widehat{\phi_{s}^{n+1}}(k)>\widehat{\phi_{s}^{n}}(k)
if
2^{m-1}\leq k<2^{m}
with
m\leq n.
Moreover,
\widehat{\phi_{s}^{n+1}}(k)>0
for
2^{n}\leq k<2^{n+1}
while

\widehat{\phi_{s}^{n}}(k)=0
for the same
k.
This proves that the sequence
\{\widehat{\phi_{s}^{n}}(k)\}_{n}
increases for each fixed
k
, The limit of this sequence is
\widehat{\phi_{s}}(k)
because passage to the limit under integral sign in integrals
\int_{G}\phi_{s}^{n}(x)w_{k}(x)d\lambda
is justified by the inequality
|\phi_{s}^{n}w_{k}|<\phi_{s}
with
\phi_{s}
being obviously summable on
G
with respect to the measure
\lambda

4.
s

Having

(see

]

]
),
s

at a pont

as a convolution of

-kernel and

\Phi_{s}(x)=\int_{G}\phi_{s}(x-y)\,d\mu(y)

and
s

I_{s}(\mu)=\int_{G}\Phi_{s}(x)\,d\mu(x).

In the same way we define

and
s
-energy

corresponding to the kernel

We recall (see

]

[

]
) the definition of Hausdorff measure and Hausdorff dimension of a set

. For any

(10)

H_{\delta}^{s}(A)=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(\text{diam}\,I_{i})^{s}:\,A\subset\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}I_{i},\,\text{diam}\,I_{i}<\delta\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\right\}.

A cover

As

) increases and we define the

-dimensional Hausdorff measure

(11)

H^{s}(A)=\lim_{\delta\to 0}H_{\delta}^{s}(A).

The

of a set

(12)

\dim_{H}(A)=\inf\{s:H^{s}(A)=0\}\,(=\sup\{s:H^{s}(A)=\infty\}).

H^{s}
is known (see
[
7
]
) to be a metric outer meassure, and so it is a measure on the class of Borel sets.

4.1.
Relation of Hausdorff dimension of a set to the value of

-energy of a mass distribution on this set

Here and everywhere in what follows we assume, that

Theorem 1
.

Let
E
be a subset of the group
G

\mu
on the set

which has finite

Proof.

.
Consider

E_{1}=\left\{x\in E:\,\,\overline{\lim_{n\to\infty}}\mu(K_{n}(x))2^{ns}>0\right\}.

Having fixed

\mu(K_{n_{i}}(x))\geq\varepsilon 2^{-n_{i}s}.

Due to continuity of measure

\mu(K_{n_{i}}(x)\setminus K_{m_{i}}(x))\geq 2^{-1}\varepsilon 2^{-n_{i}s}.

As

Denote

We have

Fix any

Consider a set

Note that if

Let
I_{i(x)}
be the element of this cover for which

Then having in mind (

4.2.
s
-energy in terms of Walsh-Fourier coefficients

Theorem 2
.

The
s
-energy of a mass distribution
\mu
on
G
can be expressed as

I_{s}(\mu)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\widehat{\phi_{s}}(k)(\widehat{\mu}(k))^{2}

(
infinite values are not excluded in this equation
)
.

Proof.

The definitions of the kernels
\phi_{s}
and
\phi_{s}^{n}
imply

\Phi_{s}^{n}(x)=\int_{G}\phi_{s}^{n}(x-y)\,d\mu(y)\nearrow\int_{G}\phi_{s}(x-y)\,d\mu(y)=\Phi_{s}(x)\,\,\text{with}\,\,n\to\infty.

Then

(15)

I_{s}^{n}(\mu)=\int_{G}\Phi_{s}^{n}(x)\,d\mu(x)\nearrow\int_{G}\Phi_{s}(x)\,d\mu(x)=I_{s}(\mu).

The last pasage to the limit does not exclude the infinite value (remember that all elements of the sequences in the above equations are positive).
Using the known formula (see
[
8
]
) for Fourier transfprm of convolution on groups, we get .

\widehat{\Phi_{s}^{n}}(k)=\widehat{\phi_{s}^{n}}(k)\widehat{\mu}(k).

As
\phi_{s}^{n}
is Walsh polinomial (see Lemma
1
) we have

\Phi_{s}^{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n}-1}\widehat{\phi_{s}^{n}}(k)\widehat{\mu}(k)w_{k}(x)

Integrating over
G
with respect to
\mu
we obtain

I_{s}^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n}-1}\widehat{\phi_{s}^{n}}(k)(\widehat{\mu}(k))^{2}

Now using (
15
) and Lemma
1
we can pass to the limit in the last equality and obtain the statement of the theorem.
∎

As a corollary of Theorem
1
and
2
we get the following result which relates Hausdorff dimention of a set on
G
to Walsh-Fourier coefficients of a mass distribution supported on this set.

Theorem 3
.

Let
E
be a subset of the group
G
. If there is a non-atomic mass distribution
\mu
on the set
E
such that the series
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\widehat{\phi_{s}}(k)(\widehat{\mu}(k))^{2}
is convergent, then
E
has Hausdorff dimention at least
s
.

Remark 1
.

Elementary but rather tedious computation shows that Walsh-Fourier coefficient of the kernel
\phi_{s}
satisfies the condition
\widehat{\phi_{s}(k)}=O(k^{s-1})
. So convergence of the series
\sum k^{s-1}(\widehat{\mu}(k))^{2}
is a sufficient condition for the finiteness of the
s
-energy and the corresponding inequality for the Hausdorff dimension of the respective set.

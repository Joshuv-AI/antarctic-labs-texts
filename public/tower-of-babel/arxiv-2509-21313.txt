Some shape functionals for the k k -Hessian equation
Alba Lia Masiello, Francesco Salerno

Abstract
For a non-empty, bounded, open, and convex set of class C 2 C^{2} , we consider the Torsional Rigidity associated to the k k -Hessian operator. We first prove Pólya type lower bound for the k k -Torsional Rigidity in any dimension; then, in order to investigate optimal sets in the Pólya type inequality, we provide two quantitative estimates. Keywords: k k -Hessian operator, Torsional rigidity, Pólya inequality, web functions. MSC 2020: 35J96, 52A39, 35J60.

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be a non-empty, bounded, open, and convex set of class
C^{2}
. Let us consider the
k
-Torsional rigidity, denoted by
T_{k}(\Omega)
, and the Dirichlet eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}(\Omega)
of the
k
-Hessian operator, whose variational characterizations are given by

T_{k}(\Omega)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{k}\end{subarray}}\frac{\left(\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}-\varphi\,dx}\right)^{k+1}}{\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}-\varphi S_{k}(D^{2}\varphi)\,dx}}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\lambda_{k}(\Omega)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{k}\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}-\varphi S_{k}(D^{2}\varphi)\,dx}}{\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}(-\varphi)^{k+1}\,dx}},

(1)

where
1
1

1

The definition of
k
-Convex function can be found in Section
2.1
.

\mathcal{C}_{k}=\{\varphi\in C^{2}(\Omega)\,:\,\varphi\text{ is $k$-convex},\,\varphi=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\},

and
S_{k}(D^{2}\varphi)
is the
k
-Hessian operator
, defined as

S_{k}(D^{2}\varphi)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}},\quad k=1,\dots,n,

(2)

being
\lambda_{i}
the eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix of
u
. Let us notice that

is a second-order differential operator and it reduces to the Monge-Ampère operator for

and to the Laplace operator for
k=1

The maximum and minimum in the previous two variational formulas are achieved, respectively, by the solutions to the following problems

\begin{cases}S_{k}(D^{2}u)=\binom{n}{k}&\text{ in $\Omega$, }\\
u=0&\text{ on $\partial\Omega$},\end{cases}

(3)

\begin{cases}S_{k}(D^{2}u)=\lambda_{k}(\Omega)(-u)^{k}&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(4)

The quantity
T_{k}(\Omega)
is monotonically increasing with respect to set inclusion, while the quantity
\lambda_{k}(\Omega)
is decreasing, and they satisfy the following scaling properties for all

T_{k}(t\Omega)=t^{k{(n+2)}}T_{k}(\Omega),\qquad\lambda_{k}(t\Omega)=t^{-2k}\lambda_{k}(\Omega).

The case
k=1
, where the
k
-Hessian operator reduces to the Laplace operator, is well studied. In
[
19
]
Pólya gives some estimates on the torsional rigidity
T(\Omega)=T_{1}(\Omega)
of a bounded, open, planar convex set
\Omega
in terms of geometrical quantities of the set itself.
In particular, he proves that, in the aforementioned class of sets, the following inequality holds

\frac{T(\Omega)P^{2}(\Omega)}{{\left|\Omega\right|}^{3}}\geq\frac{1}{3}

(5)

and he also proves that equality is asymptotically achieved by a sequence of thinning rectangles. Pólya’s proof relies on the so-called web-functions technique, which consists of considering test functions that depend on the
distance to the boundary
. For more details about the web function approach, we refer to
[
10
]
.
In the same paper and with the same techniques, the author also manages to prove that the first eigenvalue of the Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions
\lambda(\Omega)=\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
satisfies

\frac{\lambda(\Omega){\left|\Omega\right|}^{2}}{P(\Omega)^{2}}\leq\frac{\pi^{2}}{4},

(6)

whenever
\Omega
is a bounded, open, planar convex set and this inequality is asymptotically sharp on a sequence of thinning rectangles.

As a step further, Makai in
[
17
]
proves that the two shape functionals in (
5

6

\frac{T(\Omega)P^{2}(\Omega)}{{\left|\Omega\right|}^{3}}\leq\frac{2}{3},

(7)

which is sharp on a sequence of thinning triangles, and the following lower bound for the eigenvalue, which is sharp on the same sequence of sets,

\frac{\lambda(\Omega){\left|\Omega\right|}^{2}}{P^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{\pi^{2}}{16}.

(8)

Over the years, this type of inequality connecting spectral quantities to geometrical ones has gained increasing interest.
For example, estimates (

In

We also recall that in

\begin{split}&(i)\quad\frac{T_{k}(\Omega)W_{k}(\Omega)P(\Omega)^{k}}{{\left|\Omega\right|}^{2k+1}},\\

We start by considering the functional

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
be an open, bounded convex set of class

\frac{T_{k}(\Omega)W_{k}(\Omega)P(\Omega)^{k}}{{\left|\Omega\right|}^{2k+1}}\geq\frac{k^{k+1}}{n(2k+1)^{k}}.

(9)

k
-Hessian operator is more challenging. Intuitively, one might expect that an optimal sequence is a sequence of thinning cylinders, and support for this intuition is provided by the following Theorems.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
be an open, bounded convex set of class
C^{2}
. Then

\frac{T_{k}(\Omega)W_{k}(\Omega)P(\Omega)^{k}}{{\left|\Omega\right|}^{2k+1}}-\frac{k^{k+1}}{n(2k+1)^{k}}\geq c_{1}(n,k)\alpha(\Omega),

where

\alpha(\Omega)=\frac{w_{\Omega}}{D(\Omega)}.

Moreover, the constant is given by

c_{1}(n,k)=\frac{k(2k+1)^{2+\frac{1}{k}}}{(3k+1)^{3+\frac{1}{k}}}\frac{k^{k+2}2(n-1)}{n^{3}(2k+1)^{k}}a(n)

.

This first Theorem ensures that an optimal set for (

0
mean width, and this is absurd. On the other hand, it tells us that an optimal sequence must be a sequence of thinning domains. The shape of this thinning sequence is described by the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
be an open, bounded convex set of class
C^{2}
. Then

\frac{T_{k}(\Omega)W_{k}(\Omega)P(\Omega)^{k}}{{\left|\Omega\right|}^{2k+1}}-\frac{k^{k+1}}{n(2k+1)^{k}}\geq c_{2}(n,k){\beta({\Omega})}^{\frac{2k+1}{k}},

where

\beta(\Omega)=\frac{P(\Omega)r(\Omega)}{{\left|\Omega\right|}}-1,

and the constant is given by

c_{2}(n,k)=\frac{k^{k+2}}{n(2k+1)^{k}(6n)^{\frac{2k+1}{k}}}.

Theorem
1.3
gives us the following information: an optimal sequence must be optimal also for

\frac{P(\Omega)r(\Omega)}{{\left|\Omega\right|}}-1\geq 0,

hence, for instance, it should be a sequence of thinning cylinders. Anyway, we are not able to estimate the torsional rigidity of thinning cylinders from above in order to confirm this intuition.

As far as the upper bound, we show in Example
3.2
that shows that the functional
(i)

For the functional

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
be an open, bounded, convex set of class

\frac{\lambda_{k}(\Omega){\left|\Omega\right|}^{k+1}}{P(\Omega)^{k}W_{k}(\Omega)}\leq\binom{n}{k}\frac{n(2k+1)^{k}}{k^{k+1}}.

{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u(x)|\nabla f(x)|dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}dt\int_{\Omega\cap f^{-1}(t)}u(y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)}.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
be a non-empty, bounded, convex set, let
B
be the unitary ball centered at the origin and
\rho>0

\Omega+\rho B

{\left|\Omega+\rho B\right|}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}W_{i}(\Omega)\rho^{i}.

(11)

The coefficients
W_{i}(\Omega)
are known in the literature as quermassintegrals of
\Omega
. In particular,
W_{0}(\Omega)={\left|\Omega\right|}
,
nW_{1}(\Omega)=P(\Omega)
and
W_{n}(\Omega)=\omega_{n}
where
\omega_{n}
is the measure of
B
.

Formula (
11

W_{j}(\Omega+\rho B)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-j}\binom{n-j}{i}W_{j+i}(\Omega)\rho^{i},\qquad j=0,\ldots,n-1.

(12)

If
\Omega
is a convex set with
C^{2}
boundary, the quermassintegrals can be written in terms of principal curvatures of
\Omega
. More precisely, denoting with
H_{j}
the
j
-th normalized elementary symmetric function of the principal curvature
\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{n-1}
of
\partial\Omega

H_{0}=1,\qquad\qquad H_{j}=\binom{n-1}{j}^{-1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{j}\leq n-1}\kappa_{i_{1}}\ldots\kappa_{i_{j}},\qquad j=1,\ldots,n-1,

then, the quermassintegrals can be written as

W_{j}(\Omega)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\partial\Omega}H_{j-1}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},\qquad j=1,\ldots,n-1.

(13)

Furthermore, Aleksandrov-Fenchel inequalities hold true

\biggl(\frac{W_{j}(\Omega)}{\omega_{n}}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{n-j}}\geq\biggl(\frac{W_{i}(\Omega)}{\omega_{n}}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{n-i}},\qquad 0\leq i<j\leq n-1,

P(\Omega)\geq n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

2.1
k
-Hessian Operators

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
be a bounded, connected, open set of class
C^{2}
, and let us consider
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
. The
k
-Hessian operator

S_{k}\left(D^{2}u\right)
is defined
as the
k
-th elementary symmetric function of
D^{2}u
, i. e.

S_{k}(D^{2}u)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}},\quad k=1,\dots,n,

being
\lambda_{i}
the eigenvalue of the Hessian matrix of
u
.

In general, for
k\neq 1
,
S_{k}(D^{2}u)
is not elliptic
unless we consider its restriction to the class of
k
-convex functions (see for instance
[
9
]
)

\mathcal{C}_{k}=\left\{u\in C^{2}(\Omega)\,:\,S_{i}(D^{2}u)\geq 0\text{
in }\Omega,i=1,2,...,k\right\}.

In particular

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{n}(\Omega)
is equal to the class of
C^{2}(\Omega)

convex functions.
A function
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
is called strictly
k
-convex if the inequalities in the definition of the cone
\mathcal{C}_{k}(\Omega)
hold strictly.

The
operator
S_{k}^{1/k}(D^{2}u)
, for
k=1,...,n
, is homogeneous of degree

1
, so using the notation

S_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}u)=\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{ij}}S_{k}(D^{2}u),

where the indices
i,j
denote partial derivatives, Euler’s identity for homogeneous functions yields

S_{k}(D^{2}u)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i,j}S_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{ij}.

One can show, by direct calculation, that
\left(S_{k}^{1j}(D^{2}u),\dots,S_{k}^{nj}(D^{2}u)\right)
is divergence-free, allowing
S_{k}(D^{2}u)
to be expressed in divergence form

S_{k}(D^{2}u)=\frac{1}{k}\left(S_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{j}\right)_{i}.

(19)

For convenience, when the argument of
S_{k}^{ij}
is omitted, we assume
S_{k}^{ij}=S_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}u)
.

If
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
and
t
is a regular value of
u
, the
k
-Hessian operator can be related to the
(k-1)
-th mean curvature
on the boundary of
\{u\leq t\}
via the identity (see, for instance,
[

,
24
]

\binom{n-1}{k-1}H_{k-1}=\frac{S_{k}^{ij}u_{i}u_{j}}{{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{k+1}}.

(20)

3
Pólya torsion functional

The aim of this section is to study the shape functional

\frac{T_{k}(\Omega)W_{k}(\Omega)P(\Omega)^{k}}{{\left|\Omega\right|}^{2k+1}}.

(21)

We start considering the functional

F_{k}[w]:=\int_{\Omega}-w\,dx-\frac{1}{k+1}\int_{\Omega}-wS_{k}(D^{2}w)\,dx,

(22)

where
w\in\mathcal{C}
, for which the following result holds.

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
w\in\mathcal{C}
, it holds
\displaystyle F_{k}[w]\leq\frac{k}{k+1}T_{k}(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{k}}
.

Proof.

Let
w\in\mathcal{C}_{k}
and
\lambda\geq 0
, let us consider the function

f(\lambda):=F_{k}[\lambda w]=\lambda\int_{\Omega}-w\,dx-\frac{\lambda^{k+1}}{k+1}\int_{\Omega}-wS_{k}(D^{2}w)\,dx\leq\max_{\lambda\geq 0}f(\lambda).

(23)

If we optimize with respect to
\lambda>0
,

f^{\prime}(\lambda)=\int_{\Omega}-w\,dx-\lambda^{k}\int_{\Omega}-wS_{k}(D^{2}w)\,dx

we find

\lambda_{\text{max}}=\frac{\left(\int_{\Omega}-w\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}}{\left(\int_{\Omega}-wS_{k}(D^{2}w)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}}.

Hence, we can conclude

F_{k}[w]\leq F_{k}[\lambda_{\text{max}}w]=\frac{k}{k+1}T_{k}(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{k}}.

∎

Let us consider the class

\mathcal{C}^{-}=\{g:[M,0]\rightarrow\R\text{ : $g(0)=0$, $g$ increasing and convex}\}.

Remark 3.1
.

If
g\in\mathcal{C}^{-}
and
u
is convex, then
g\circ u
is also convex.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
and let
g\in\mathcal{C^{-}}
, then it holds

\left\{\frac{k+1}{k}\max_{g\in\mathcal{C}^{-}}F_{k}[g\circ u]\right\}^{k}=k\left[\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.63885ptM$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle 0$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{\mu(t)^{1+\frac{1}{k}}}{\left(\int_{\{u=t\}}\frac{S_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{i}u_{j}}{|\nabla u|}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}}\right]^{k}.

Proof.

Let
u
be a convex function that vanishes on
\partial\Omega
and let
M
be its minimum. We want to rewrite
F_{k}[g\circ u]
in terms of
g
, so the first integral becomes

\int_{\Omega}-g\circ u\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{u}^{0}g^{\prime}(t)\,dt\right)\,dx=\int_{M}^{0}g^{\prime}(t)\mu(t)\,dt.

On the other hand, the second integral can be rewritten thanks to (
19
) and the coarea formula

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}(-g\circ u)S_{k}(D^{2}(g\circ u))\,dx&=\frac{1}{k}\int_{\Omega}(-g\circ u)(S_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}(g\circ u))(g\circ u)_{i})_{j}\,dx\\
&=\frac{1}{k}\int_{\Omega}S_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}(g\circ u))(g\circ u)_{i}(g\circ u)_{j}\,dx\\
&=\frac{1}{k}\int_{g(M)}^{0}\int_{\{g(u)=s\}}\frac{S_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}(g\circ u))(g\circ u)_{i}(g\circ u)_{j}}{|\nabla g\circ u|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,ds.\end{split}

Thanks to formula (
20
), we get

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}(-g\circ u)S_{k}(D^{2}(g\circ u))\,dx&=\frac{1}{k}\int_{g(M)}^{0}\int_{\{g(u)=s\}}H_{k-1}(\{g\circ u=s\})|\nabla g\circ u|^{k}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,ds\\
&=\frac{1}{k}\int_{M}^{0}g^{\prime}(t)^{k+1}\int_{\{u=t\}}H_{k-1}(\{u=t\})|\nabla u|^{k}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,dt\\

\lambda_{k}(\Omega)\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}-u_{\Omega}S_{k}(D^{2}u_{\Omega})\,dx}{\int_{\Omega}(-u_{\Omega})^{k+1}\,dx}=\binom{n}{k}\frac{\|u_{\Omega}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}}{\|u_{\Omega}\|_{L^{k+1}(\Omega)}^{k+1}}\leq\binom{n}{k}\frac{{\left|\Omega\right|}^{k}}{\|u_{\Omega}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{k}}=\binom{n}{k}\frac{{\left|\Omega\right|}^{k}}{T_{k}(\Omega)},

and then

\mathcal{G}_{k}(\Omega)\leq\binom{n}{k}.

∎

Remark 4.2
.

We can compare the functional

with the functional that appears in (

\frac{W_{k-1}(\Omega)}{W_{k}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{{\left|\Omega\right|}}{W_{1}(\Omega)}.

(36)

This inequality is a consequence of the following useful formula (see
[
14
, Volume A, Chapter 1.2, Section 6]
)

W_{i+1}(\Omega)W_{i-1}(\Omega)\leq W_{i}^{2}(\Omega),\quad i=1,\dots,n-1,

as one can proceed in the following way

\begin{split}&W_{1}^{2}(\Omega)\geq{\left|\Omega\right|}W_{2}(\Omega),\\
&W_{1}^{2}(\Omega)W_{2}(\Omega)\geq{\left|\Omega\right|}W_{2}^{2}(\Omega)\geq{\left|\Omega\right|}W_{1}(\Omega)W_{3}(\Omega),\\
&W_{1}^{2}(\Omega)W_{2}(\Omega)W_{3}(\Omega)\geq{\left|\Omega\right|}W_{1}(\Omega)W_{3}^{2}(\Omega)\geq{\left|\Omega\right|}W_{1}(\Omega)W_{2}(\Omega)W_{4}(\Omega).\end{split}

and, iterating this formula, we find

W_{1}^{2}(\Omega)W_{2}(\Omega)\dots W_{k-1}(\Omega)\geq{\left|\Omega\right|}\dots W_{k-3}(\Omega)W_{k-1}^{2}(\Omega)\geq{\left|\Omega\right|}\dots W_{k-2}(\Omega)W_{k}(\Omega)

and then

W_{1}(\Omega)W_{k-1}(\Omega)\geq{\left|\Omega\right|}W_{k}(\Omega),

that is (
36
).
Finally, thanks to (
36
), we find that

\frac{\lambda_{k}(\Omega){\left|\Omega\right|}^{k+2}}{P(\Omega)^{k+1}W_{k-1}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{\lambda_{k}(\Omega){\left|\Omega\right|}^{k+1}}{nP(\Omega)^{k}W_{k}(\Omega)}.

Hence, Theorem
1.4
gives an upper bound also for the functional in (

Structural and Spectral Properties of Strictly Interval Graphs
Lilian Markenzon 1, Claudia M. Justel 2

Abstract
In this paper we deal with a subclass of chordal graphs, which are simultaneously strictly chordal and interval, the strictly interval graphs . We present a new characterization of the class that leads to a simple linear recognition algorithm. Next we introduce a new subclass of strictly interval graphs, the S ​ I SI -core graphs, that are non-split and non-cograph graphs and show that several elements of the new class are Laplacian integral.

Graph Basic Concepts

All graphs mentionned in this paper are connected graphs.

Basic concepts about chordal graphs are assumed to be known and
can be found in Blair and Peyton
[
2
]
and Golumbic
[
6
]
.
Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, with
|E|=m
,
|V|=n>0
.
The
set of neighbors
of a vertex
v\in V
is denoted by

N(v)=\{w\in V\mid\{v,w\}\in E\}
and its
closed neighborhood
by
N[v]=N(v)\cup\{v\}
.
Two vertices
u
and
v
are
true twins
in
G
if
N[u]=N[v]
and
they are
false twins
in
G
if
N(u)=N(v)
.
A vertex
v
is said to be
simplicial
in
G
if
N(v)
is a
clique in
G
.
Given a chordal graph
G
, the
derived graph

D(G)

is the induced subgraph obtained by deleting all
the simplicial vertices of
G
.

A subset
S\subset V
is a
vertex separator
for
non-adjacent vertices
u
and
v
(a
uv
-separator) if the
removal of
S
from the graph separates
u
and
v
into distinct
connected components. If no proper subset of
S
is a

uv
-separator then
S
is a
minimal
uv
-separator
. When the
pair of vertices remains unspecified, we refer to
S
as a
minimal vertex separator
.

A
clique-tree
of
G
is defined as a tree
T
whose vertices
are the maximal cliques of
G
such that for every two maximal cliques
Q
and
Q^{\prime}

each clique in the path from
Q
to
Q^{\prime}
in
T
contains
Q\cap Q^{\prime}
.
The set of maximal cliques of
G
is denoted by
\mathbb{Q}
.
The
leafage
of a chordal graph
G
,
\ell(G)
, is the minimum number of leaves among
the clique-trees of
G
.
Blair and Peyton
[
2
]
proved that,
for a clique-tree
T=(V_{T},E_{T})
,
a set
S\subset V
is a minimal vertex separator of
G
if
and only if
S=Q^{\prime}\cap Q^{\prime\prime}
for some edge
\{Q^{\prime},Q^{\prime\prime}\}\in E_{T}
.
Moreover, the multiset
{\mathbb{M}}
of
the minimal vertex separators of
G
is the same for every
clique-tree of
G
.
The algorithm presented in Markenzon and Pereira
[
13
]
computes
the set of minimal vertex separators,
\mathbb{S}
, of a chordal graph
G
in linear time.

It is worth mentioning special kinds of cliques.
A
simplicial clique
is a maximal clique containing at least one simplicial vertex.
A simplicial vertex appears in only one maximal clique of the graph.
A simplicial clique
Q
is called a
boundary clique
if there exists a maximal clique
Q^{\prime}

such that
Q\cap Q^{\prime}=X
is the set of non-simplicial vertices of
Q
.
Shibata
[
18
]
showed that if a maximal clique

is a leaf in some clique-tree
then
Q
is a boundary clique.
The converse of this fact was proved by
Hara and Takemura
[
8
]
.
These results can be summarized in the next theorem.

Theorem 1
.

[
8
]

A maximal clique is a leaf in some clique-tree if and only if it is a boundary clique.

A maximal clique such that all vertices are true twins is called
a
critical clique

[
12
]
.
The set
\mathbb{C}
of critical cliques of
G
forms a partition of vertices of
V
.

The
critical clique graph
of a chordal graph
G
,
CC(G)
, is the
connected graph whose vertices are the critical cliques of
G
and whose
edges connect vertices corresponding to cliques
Q_{i}
and
Q_{j}
such that
some vertex of
Q_{i}
is neighboor in
G
of a vertex in
Q_{j}
.
Note that
CC(G)
has no true twins
[
3
]
, then the max cliques of
CC(G)
have at most one
simplicial vertex.

Subclasses of chordal graphs

In this section we present the definitions and some characterizations of the subclasses of chordal graphs
that somehow will be used in this paper.

An
interval graph
is the intersection graph of a family
I
of intervals on the real line.
There are several characterizations of this class.
The following characterization theorem guarantees that for an interval graph,
its maximal cliques can be linearly ordered.

Theorem 2
.

[
5
]

Let
G=(V,E)
be a non-complete chordal graph. Then,

G
is an interval graph if and only if
G
has a clique-tree that
is a path.

Strictly chordal graphs
, defined in
[
10
]
based on hypergraph properties,
were firstly introduced as
block duplicate graphs
by Golumbic and Peled
[
7
]
.
A
strictly chordal graph
is a graph obtained by adding zero or more true twins
to each vertex of a block graph
G

(a graph is a
block graph

[
9
]
if it is connected and every block is a clique).
The class was proved to be chordal, gem-free and dart-free (
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
), see Figure

Theorem 3
.

[
3
]

Let
G
be a connected graph.

G
is strictly chordal iff
CC(G)
is a block graph.

Strictly chordal graphs can also be characterized in terms of the structure of their minimal vertex separators.

Theorem 4
.

[
14
]

Let
G=(V,E)
be a chordal graph.
The following statements are equivalent:

1.

G
is a strictly chordal graph.

2.

For any distinct
S,S^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{S}}
,
S\cap S^{\prime}=\emptyset
.

3.

G
is a
\{\textrm{gem, dart}\}
-free graph.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a strictly chordal graph and
\mathbb{S}
its set of minimal vertex separators.

(a)

S\in{\mathbb{S}}
is a minimal separator of
G
.

(b)

S
belongs to exactly
\mu(S)+1
maximal cliques of
G
, for every
S\in{\mathbb{S}}
.

(c)

boundary cliques of
G
contain only one mvs.

Based on this characterization, the recognition algorithm for strictly chordal graphs is extremily simple and
relies only on the determination of
\mathbb{S}
.

The critical clique graph of strictly chordal graphs have interesting properties.

Lemma 5
.

[
12
]

Let
G=(V,E)
a chordal graph. Then, the set of critical cliques in
G
forms a partition of
V
.

By Theorem
4
, the proof of the next lemma becomes immediate.

Lemma 6
.

Let
G
be a strictly chordal graph.
A critical clique of
G
is composed by:

1.

all the simplicial vertices of a maximal clique of

or

2.

all the vertices of a minimal vertex separator of

.

Strictly Interval Graphs: new characterization and recognition algorithm

In this section we restudy a subclass of interval graphs,
the
strictly interval graphs
(
SIG
), graphs which are at the same time
strictly chordal and interval graphs.
A first characterization of the class was presented in
[
15
]
.
We present structural results about the class,
leading to a new characterization and a simple recognition algorithm.

Theorem 7
.

[
15
]

Let
G=(V,E)
be a non-complete strictly chordal graph.
The following statements are equivalent:

1.

G
is a strictly interval graph.

2.

G
is a
\{\textrm{2-net, bipartite claw}\}
-free graph (see Fig.
1
).

3.

leafage
(G)=2
.

Corollary 7.1
.

Let
G
be a non-complete strictly interval graph.
Then every maximal clique of
G
contains at most two minimal vertex separators.

As it was seen in Theorem
3
, strictly chordal graphs are characterized
by their critical clique graphs.
The next theorem presents a new characterization of strictly interval graphs,
also based in their critical clique graphs, that
supports a more efficient recognition algorithm.

Theorem 8
.

Let
G
be a non-complete strictly chordal graph.

G
is a strictly interval graph iff

D(CC(G))
is a path.

Proof.

\rightarrow

Let
G
be a strictly interval graph.
By Theorem
3
,
CC(G)
is a block graph.
So, all
mvs
of
G
are represented by one vertex in
CC(G)
.

In
D(CC(G))
there are not simplicial vertices of
G
.
Suppose that
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
and
S_{3}
,
mvs
of
G
, form a path
P=[s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}]
in
D(CC(G))
.
Let us consider a vertex
s^{\prime}
that does not belong to this path but, together with
P
,
establish a connected induced subgraph of
D(CC(G))

Vertex
s^{\prime}
can be linked to
P
in three ways:

1.

forming the edge
(s^{\prime},s_{1})
(or
(s^{\prime},s_{3}))
: in this case it forms a path in
D(CC(G)
.

2.

forming the edge
(s^{\prime},s_{2})
: in this case
G
has a bipartite claw.

3.

with two edges, forming a triangle: in this case
G
has a 2-net.

4.

with three edges: in this case
G
has a dart and it is not strictly chordal.

Item 1 satisfies the hipothesis.
With itens 2, 3 and 4, by Theorem
7
, we do not have a strictly interval graph.
So,
D(CC(G))
is a path.

\leftarrow

Let
D(CC(G))
be a path.
Since
G
is strictly chordal, we need to be prove that
G
is an interval graph,
or, equivalently,
G
has a clique-tree that is a path (Theorem
2
)
or the maximal cliques of
G
can be linearly ordered.
Since
D(CC(G))
is a path, the maximal cliques of
G
represented in
D(CC(G))
admit a linear ordering.

By Lemma
6
, each
mvs
of
G
establish a maximal clique of
CC(G)
.
Each edge of
D(CC(G))
belongs to a maximal clique of
G
.
So, these maximal cliques also form a path in the clique tree.

Let us consider the addition of simplicial vertices of
CC(G)
to
D(CC(G))
.
This addition produces three different cases:

1.

the construction of boundary cliques of
CC(G)
,
containing the minimal vertex separators that are vertices of
D(CC(G))
.
It does not matter how many boundary cliques contain the same
mvs
;
they will appear sequentially in the clique-tree of
G
and the resulting graph is an interval graph.

2.

The addition of a simplicial vertex adjacent to an edge of
D(CC(G))
;
it results in a max clique
with three vertices, one of them being a simplicial vertex.
In the clique-tree, this vertex corresponds to an increase of the cardinality of one maximal clique
and it does not affect the clique-tree.
A new addition over this same edge of
D(CC(G))
cannot be performed:
the new vertex cannot be a true twin of the first vertex added
because the max cliques of
CC(G)
have at most one simplicial vertex.
It also cannot be a false twin because the result would be a new maximal clique
producing a dart in
G
- impossible since
G
is a strictly chordal graph.

3.

two (or more) false twins as simplicial vertices adjacent to a maximal clique results
in a dart induced subgraph in
G
. It is not a valid addition.

Therefore, there are only two possible additions of simplicial vertices to
D(CC(G))
(by hypothesis, a path)
and both maintain the linear order of the maximal cliques of
G
.
Then,
G
is an interval graph and the result follows.
∎

Theorem
8
provides directly a recognition algorithm.
The steps are:

Step 1:

Recognition of
G
as strictly chordal;

Step 2:

Determination of
D(CC(G))
:

(a)

Lemma
6
provides a simple determination of the
vertices of
CC(G)
;

(b)

removal of simplicial vertices of
CC(G)
;

Step 3:

Test if the result is a path.

It is important to highlight that all these steps are trivially simple to implement and have linear time complexity.

Spectral Basic Concepts

The
Laplacian matrix of a graph

G
is defined as
L(G)={\mathcal{D}}(G)-A(G)
, where
{\mathcal{D}}(G)

denotes the diagonal degree matrix and
A(G)
the adjacency matrix of
G
.
As
L(G)
is symmetric, there are
n
real eigenvalues and as
L(G)
is positive semidefinite, all these eigenvalues are non-negatives and 0 is an eigenvalue. We denote the eigenvalues of
L(G)
, the
Laplacian eigenvalues
of
G
, by
\mu_{1}(G)\geq\mu_{2}(G),...,\geq\mu_{n}(G)=0
.

All different Laplacian eigenvalues of
G
together with their multiplicities form the
Laplacian spectrum
of
G
, denoted by
SpecL(G)=[\overline{\mu}_{1}^{m_{1}},...,\overline{\mu}_{d}^{m_{d}}]
, where
1\leq d\leq n
is the number of distinct eigenvalues of
L(G)
and
m_{i}
the multiplicity of
\overline{\mu}_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq d
.
A graph is called
Laplacian integral
if
SpecL(G)
consists of integers.

The next results about linear algebra will be used later.
Given a
n\times p
matrix
A
and a
m\times q
matrix
B
, the

Kronecker product
(or tensor product)
A\otimes B
is the
nm\times pq
block matrix:

A\otimes B=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1,1}B&a_{1,2}B&\dots&a_{1,p}B\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
a_{n,1}B&a_{n,2}B&\dots&a_{n,p}B\\
\end{pmatrix}

Let
M
be a real
n\times n
matrix in the following block form

M=\begin{pmatrix}M_{1,1}&\dots&M_{1,t}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
M_{t,1}&\dots&M_{t,t}\\
\end{pmatrix}

where
M_{i,j}
are
n_{i}\times n_{j}
matrices,
1\leq i,j\leq t
and
n=\sum_{r=1}^{t}n_{r}
. If
b_{i,j}

the average row sum of
M_{i,j}
, then
B=B(M)=(b_{i,j})
is the
quotient matrix
of
M
.
Moreover, if for each pair
i,j
,
M_{i,j}
has constant row sum, then
B
is the
equitable quotient matrix
of
M
.
You et al.
[
19
]
showed the following result.

Theorem 9
.

[
19
]

Given a matrix
M
and
B
a equitable quotient matrix of
M
, the spectrum of
B
is included in the spectrum of
M
(
SpecB\subseteq SpecM
).

We define a new subclass of strictly chordal graphs, the

G

-core graph

,

, their union defines a maximal clique of size

Actually the

Property 10

Let

The min

Observe that it is possible to establish, for

In order to do that we need the concept of the partition function

Theorem 11

Let

There are
\frac{a(p)(a(p)+1)}{2}

-core graphs.

Proof.

Let

Each partition of the

with a partition of the other

Hence, with the first partition we have
p
diferent combinations.
With the second partition we have

So, the number of combinations without repetition is given by

.
∎

From the results above it is not difficult to see that there are

-core graphs for

Moreover, we can determine the number of elements of the chosen class.

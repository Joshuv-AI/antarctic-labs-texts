Abstract.

In this paper, we consider the nonlinear elliptic equation

\Delta_{f}v^{\tau}+\lambda v=0

on a complete smooth metric measure space with
m
-Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature bounded from below, where
\tau>0
and
\lambda
are constant.
We obtain some new local gradient estimates for positive solutions to the equation using the Nash-Moser iteration technique. As applications of these estimates, we obtain a Liouville type theorem and a Harnack inequality, and the global gradient estimates for such solutions. Our results generalize and improve the estimates in Wang (J. Differential Equations 260:567-585, 2016) and Zhao (Arch. Math. (Basel) 114:457-469, 2020).

Keywords.

Smooth metric measure space; gradient estimate; Liouville theorem; Harnack inequality
.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider Cheng-Yau type gradient estimates for the positive solutions of the following nonlinear elliptic equation

\Delta_{f}v^{\tau}+\lambda v=0

(1.1)

on a smooth metric measure space
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}d\nu)
whose
m
-Bakry-Émery Ricci tensor

{\rm Ric}_{f}^{m}={\rm Ric}+\nabla^{2}f-\frac{1}{m-n}{\rm d}f\otimes{\rm d}f,\quad\quad~~~~m>n

(1.2)

satisfies
{\rm Ric}_{f}^{m}\geq-(m-1)Kg
for some constant
K\geq 0
, where
\tau>0
and
\lambda
are constant, and
\Delta_{f}\cdot=\Delta-\langle\nabla f,\nabla\cdot\rangle

is the weighted Laplacian for some smooth potential function
f
.
Here
d\nu
,
{\rm Ric}
,
\Delta
,
\nabla^{2}
and
\nabla
are the volume form, Ricci tensor, the usual Laplacian, Hessian and gradient operators associated with the metric
g
respectively.

When
f
is constant,
\rho=v^{\tau}
and
p=\frac{1}{\tau}
, equation (
1.1
) becomes

\Delta\rho+\lambda\rho^{p}=0.

(1.3)

Equation (
1.1
) appears in differential geometry. Indeed, for any
n
-dimensional
(n\geq 3)
complete manifold

(M,g)
, consider a pointwise conformal metric
\tilde{g}=\rho^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g
for some smooth positive function
\rho
.
Then the scalar curvature
\tilde{R}
of metric
\tilde{g}
related to the scalar curvature
R
of metric
g
is given by

\Delta\rho-\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}Ru+\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}\tilde{R}\rho^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}=0.

(1.4)

When
R=0
and
\tilde{R}
is a constant, (
1.4
) reduces to (
1.3
) with
\lambda=\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}\tilde{R}
and
\tau=\frac{n-2}{n+2}
.
If
M
is compact and
\tilde{R}
is constant, the existence of a positive solution
\rho
is the well-known Yamabe problem. For more details we refer to
[
14
]
.

Another important reason of studying equation(
1.1
) is that its parabolic counterpart is related to the porous medium
equation and the fast diffusion equation. It is a nonlinear heat equation in the form

\partial_{t}v=\Delta v^{\tau}.

(1.5)

Equation (
1.5
) with
\tau>1
is called the porous medium equation, which describes the flow of an isentropic gas through a porous medium
[
17
]
.
Equation (
1.5
) with
0<\tau<1
is called the fast diffusion equation, which appears in various geometric flows such as the Ricci flow on surfaces
[
6
]
and the Yamabe flow
[
24
]
. There is a lot of literature on the study of gradient estimates of the porous medium equation and the fast diffusion equation. We only cite
[
2
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
16
,
27
,
28
,
5
]
here and one may find more references therein.

On the other hand, inspired by the work of Yau
[
23
]
and Li and Wang
[
15
]
, many gradient estimates for equation (
1.1
) on Riemannian manifolds were obtained.
In
[
19
]
, Wang established gradient estimates for positive solutions to the equation (
1.1
) on any smooth metric measure space whose
m
-Bakry-Émery Ricci tensor is bounded from below by
-(m-1)K
with
K\geq 0
. We state his main result as below.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
19
]
)
Let
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}d\nu)
be a smooth metric measure space with
{\rm Ric}_{f}^{m}\geq-(m-1)Kg
for some
K\geq 0
and
m>2
. Suppose that
v
is a smooth
positive solution to the equation (
1.1
) and
u=\frac{\tau}{\tau-1}v^{\tau-1}
.

(1) If
\tau>1
, then

\lambda\leq C_{1}\left(m,\tau,K,\sup_{M}u\right),\quad\quad\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}\leq C_{2}\left(m,\tau,\lambda,K,\sup_{M}u\right),

(1.6)

where
C_{1}>0
,
C_{2}\geq 0
are explicit.

(2) If
1-\frac{2}{m}<\tau<1
, then

\lambda\leq\overline{C}_{1}\left(m,\tau,K,\sup_{M}u\right),\quad\quad\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}\leq-\overline{C}_{2}\left(m,\tau,\lambda,K,\sup_{M}u\right),

(1.7)

where
\overline{C}_{1}>0
,
\overline{C}_{2}\geq 0
are explicit and
u<0
.

As a corollary, the author deduced when
v
is non-trivial a lower bound for
\sup_{M}v
(if
\tau>1
), respectively an upper bound for
\inf_{M}v
(if
1-\frac{2}{m}<\tau<1
), and consequently a Liouville type theorem. A Harnack inequality was also proved in
[
19
]
.

Recently, Zhao
[
25
]
studied gradient estimates for positive solutions of the nonlinear elliptic equation

\Delta_{V}v^{\tau}+\lambda v=0

(1.8)

on a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with the
k
-Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature bounded
from below, where
\Delta_{V}
denotes a weighted Laplacian associated to a certain
vector field
V
. We state his main result as below.

Theorem 1.2
.

(
[
25
]
)
Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold. Suppose
{\rm Ric}_{V}^{k}\geq-Kg
for some
K\geq 0
and
k>2
.
Let
v
be a positive solution to the equation (
1.8
). For any
\sigma>\frac{1}{(k-1)^{2}}
, if

1\leq\tau<1+\frac{1}{(1+\sigma)(k-1)}
, then

\sup_{B_{R/2}(o)}\frac{|\nabla v|}{v}\leq C_{3}\left(\sigma,k,\tau,K,\lambda,R,\left(\inf_{B_{R/2}(o)}v\right)^{1-\tau}\right).

(1.9)

The method they employ is the maximum principle. The gradient estimates in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
depend on the
bound of the solution. Moreover, the gradient estimates are not Cheng-Yau type.

In
[
3
]
, Cheng and Yau proved the well-known Cheng-Yau type gradient estimate for positive harmonic functions.
We state their main result as below.

Theorem 1.3
.

(
[
3
]
)
Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold. Suppose
{\rm Ric}\geq-Kg
for some
K\geq 0
.
Then for any positive harmonic function
v
in a geodesic ball
B_{R/2}(o)
, there is a constant
C_{4}(n)
depending
only on
n
such that

\sup_{B_{R/2}(o)}\frac{|\nabla v|}{v}\leq C_{4}(n)\frac{1+\sqrt{K}R}{R}.

(1.10)

We note that Cheng-Yau type gradient estimats depend only on
n
,
K
and
R
, and it does not depend on some other geometric quantities; moreover, Cheng-Yau type gradient estimats are more significant and useful in geometric analysis, since it can derive a strong Liouville theorem and Harnack inequalities. There have been extensive studies on Cheng-Yau type gradient estimats over the recent decades; see for example
[
20
,
21
,
7
,
9
,
22
,
8
]
and references therein.

Inspired by these works, we employ the Nash-Moser iteration
technique to prove a Cheng-Yau type gradient estimate for positive solutions to equation(
1.1
) on any smooth metric measure space with
m
-Bakry-Émery Ricci tensor bounded from below. Our first main result is in the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}d\nu)
be a complete
n
-dimensional smooth metric measure space with
{\rm Ric}_{f}^{m}\geq-(m-1)Kg
for some
K\geq 0
and
m>2
. Suppose that
\tau>0
and
v
is a smooth
positive solution to the equation (
1.1
) on a geodesic ball
B_{R}(o)\subset M
. If
m
,
\lambda
and
\tau
satisfy

\lambda\bigg(\frac{m+1}{m-1}-\frac{1}{\tau}\bigg)\geq 0,

(1.11)

or

1-\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{m+1+\sqrt{2}}\leq\tau\leq 1-\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{m+1-\sqrt{2}},

(1.12)

then there holds

\sup_{B_{R/2}(o)}\frac{|\nabla v|}{v}\leq c(m,\tau,\lambda)\frac{1+\sqrt{K}R}{R},

(1.13)

where the constant
c(m,\tau,\lambda)
depends only on
m
,
\tau
and
\lambda
.

By carefully analyzing the conditions (
1.11
) and (
1.12
) in Theorem
1.4
, it is easy to see that
the following results hold.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}d\nu)
be a complete
n
-dimensional smooth metric measure space with
{\rm Ric}_{f}^{m}\geq-(m-1)Kg
for some
K\geq 0

is a smooth

\lambda

\lambda\geq 0,\quad\tau\geq\ 1-\frac{2}{m+1},

(1.15)

then we have

\sup\limits_{B_{R/2}(o)}\frac{|\nabla v|}{v}\leq c(m,\lambda,\tau)\frac{1+\sqrt{K}R}{R}.

Remark 1.6
.

Here we want to give several remarks of the above Cheng-Yau type gradient estimates.

(1) Compared with the previous work (cf. Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
), Theorem
1.4

and Corollary
1.5
extend

to a larger range.

(2) In Theorem
1.4
and Corollary
1.5
, the gradient estimates on a positive solution

. Meanwhile, the gradient estimates obtained in Theorem

and Theorem

1.1
and Theorem
1.2
.

(3) Theorem
1.4
and Corollary
1.5
provide a united expression of previous gradient estimates in Theorem
1.1
for various ranges of
\tau
.

1.4

.

(M^{n},g,e^{-f}d\nu)

(1) We extend the range of the corresponding power

\displaystyle\frac{a_{2}}{t}e^{-t_{0}}V_{f}^{\frac{2}{m}}R^{-2}\left(\int_{\Omega}|h^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t}{2}}\eta|^{\frac{2m}{m-2}}e^{-f}d\nu\right)^{\frac{m-2}{m}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{a_{4}t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}\int_{\Omega}h^{\alpha_{0}+t}\eta^{2}e^{-f}d\nu+\frac{a_{3}}{t}\int_{\Omega}h^{\alpha_{0}+t}|\nabla\eta|^{2}e^{-f}d\nu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{a_{4}t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{3}}{t}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\right)h^{\alpha_{0}+t}e^{-f}d\nu,

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|h^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t}{2}}\eta|^{\frac{2m}{m-2}}e^{-f}d\nu\right)^{\frac{m-2}{m}}

(3.25)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{t_{0}}V_{f}^{-\frac{2}{m}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{a_{4}}{a_{2}}t_{0}^{2}t\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{3}}{a_{2}}R^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\right)h^{\alpha_{0}+t}e^{-f}d\nu.

Then, we take an increasing sequence
\{\beta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that

\beta_{1}=\beta\quad\text{and}\quad\beta_{k+1}=\frac{m\beta_{k}}{m-2},\quad k=1,2,\ldots,

and a decreasing sequence
\{r_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that

r_{k}=\frac{R}{2}+\frac{R}{4^{k}},\quad k=1,2,\ldots.

Let
\Omega_{k}=B_{r_{k}}(o)
, then we may choose
\{\eta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{R}(o))
such that

\eta_{k}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{r_{k}}(o)),\quad\eta_{k}=1\text{ in }B_{r_{k+1}}(o)\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla\eta_{k}|\leq\frac{C4^{k}}{R}.

By letting
t=t_{k}
,
\eta=\eta_{k}
and
t_{k}+\alpha_{0}=\beta_{k}
in (
3.25
), we derive that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega_{k}}|h^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t_{k}}{2}}\eta_{k}|^{\frac{2m}{m-2}}e^{-f}d\nu\right)^{\frac{m-2}{m}}

(3.26)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{t_{0}}V_{f}^{-\frac{2}{m}}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\left(\frac{a_{4}}{a_{2}}t_{0}^{2}t_{k}\eta_{k}^{2}+\frac{a_{3}}{a_{2}}R^{2}|\nabla\eta_{k}|^{2}\right)h^{\alpha_{0}+t_{k}}e^{-f}d\nu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{t_{0}}V_{f}^{-\frac{2}{m}}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\left(\frac{a_{4}}{a_{2}}t_{0}^{2}t_{k}\eta_{k}^{2}+\frac{a_{3}}{a_{2}}C^{2}16^{k}\right)h^{\alpha_{0}+t_{k}}e^{-f}d\nu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{t_{0}}V_{f}^{-\frac{2}{m}}\left(\frac{a_{4}}{a_{2}}t_{0}^{2}t_{k}+\frac{a_{3}}{a_{2}}C^{2}16^{k}\right)\int_{\Omega_{k}}h^{\alpha_{0}+t_{k}}e^{-f}d\nu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{t_{0}}V_{f}^{-\frac{2}{m}}\left(\frac{a_{4}}{a_{2}}t_{0}^{2}\left(t_{0}+\alpha_{0}\right)\left(\frac{m}{m-2}\right)^{k}+\frac{a_{3}}{a_{2}}C^{2}16^{k}\right)\int_{\Omega_{k}}h^{\alpha_{0}+t_{k}}e^{-f}d\nu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{t_{0}}V_{f}^{-\frac{2}{m}}\left(\frac{a_{4}}{a_{2}}t_{0}^{2}\left(t_{0}+\alpha_{0}\right)16^{k}+\frac{a_{3}}{a_{2}}C^{2}16^{k}\right)\int_{\Omega_{k}}h^{\alpha_{0}+t_{k}}e^{-f}d\nu.

We can find some constant
a_{8}
such that

\left(\int_{\Omega_{k}}|h^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t_{k}}{2}}\eta_{k}|^{\frac{2m}{m-2}}e^{-f}d\nu\right)^{\frac{m-2}{m}}\leq\>a_{8}t_{0}^{3}16^{k}e^{t_{0}}V_{f}^{-\frac{2}{m}}\int_{\Omega_{k}}h^{\beta_{k}}e^{-f}d\nu.

(3.27)

Then, taking power of
1/\beta_{k}
of the both sides of (
3.27
), we have

\left(\int_{\Omega_{k}}|h^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t_{k}}{2}}\eta_{k}|^{\frac{2m}{m-2}}e^{-f}d\nu\right)^{\frac{m-2}{m\beta_{k}}}\leq\>(a_{8}t_{0}^{3}e^{t_{0}}V_{f}^{-\frac{2}{m}})^{\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}}16^{\frac{k}{\beta_{k}}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{k}}h^{\beta_{k}}e^{-f}d\nu\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}}.

(3.28)

Thus,

\left(\int_{\Omega_{k+1}}h^{\beta_{k+1}}e^{-f}d\nu\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta_{k+1}}}\leq\>(a_{8}t_{0}^{3}e^{t_{0}}V_{f}^{-\frac{2}{m}})^{\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}}16^{\frac{k}{\beta_{k}}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{k}}h^{\beta_{k}}e^{-f}d\nu\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}},

which implies that

\left\|h\right\|_{L_{f}^{\beta_{k+1}}(\Omega_{k+1})}\leq\left(a_{8}t_{0}^{3}e^{t_{0}}V_{f}^{-\frac{2}{m}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}}16^{\frac{k}{\beta_{k}}}\|h\|_{L_{f}^{\beta_{k}}(\Omega_{k})}.

(3.29)

By iteration we have

\left\|h\right\|_{L_{f}^{\beta_{k+1}}(\Omega_{k+1})}\leq\left(a_{8}t_{0}^{3}e^{t_{0}}V_{f}^{-\frac{2}{m}}\right)^{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}}16^{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{k}{\beta_{k}}}\|h\|_{L_{f}^{\beta_{1}}(B_{3R/4}(o))}.

(3.30)

We note that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}=\frac{m}{2\beta_{1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{k}{\beta_{k}}=\frac{m^{2}}{4\beta_{1}},

then, by letting
k\rightarrow\infty
in (
3.30
) we obtain the following inequality:

\|h\|_{L_{f}^{\infty}(B_{R/2}(o))}\leq\>a_{9}V_{f}^{-\frac{1}{\beta_{1}}}\|h\|_{L_{f}^{\beta_{1}}(B_{3R/4}(o))},

(3.31)

where

a_{9}\geq\left(a_{8}t_{0}^{3}e^{t_{0}}\right)^{\frac{m}{2\beta_{1}}}16^{\frac{m^{2}}{4\beta_{1}}}.

According to (
3.16
), we have

\|h\|_{L_{f}^{\infty}(B_{R/2}(o))}\leq\>a_{10}\frac{(1+\sqrt{K}R)^{2}}{R^{2}},

(3.32)

where
a_{10}=a_{9}a_{7}c_{m,\lambda,\tau}^{2}.
Recalling
h=|\nabla u|^{2}
and
u=-\ln v
, we derive the required estimate. Consequently, we conclude the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
∎

4.
Proofs of Corollary
1.7
and Corollary
1.9

Proof of Corollary
1.7
.

Under the conditions in Corollary
1.7
, then by Theorem
1.4
we have that for any
x\in B_{R/2}(o)\subset M
,

\frac{|\nabla v(x)|}{v(x)}\leq\sup\limits_{B_{R/2}(o)}\frac{|\nabla v|}{v}\leq\frac{c(m,\tau,\lambda)}{R}.

(4.1)

Letting
R\to\infty
in (
4.1
), we get

\nabla v(x)=0,\quad\forall x\in M.

Therefore,
v
is a positive constant on
M
.

Proof of Corollary
1.9
.

Under the same conditions in Theorem
1.4
, let
x
,
y\in B_{R/2}(o)
be any two points with minimal geodesic
\gamma
connecting them. Then using the gradient estimate (
1.13
) and the fact that length
(\gamma)\leq R
, we have

\displaystyle\ln v(x)-\ln v(y)=

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}|\nabla\ln v|dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}c(\tau,m,\lambda)\frac{1+\sqrt{K}R}{R}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(\tau,m,\lambda)(1+\sqrt{K}R).

Thus, for any
x
,
y\in B_{R/2}(o)
we obtain

v(x)\leq e^{c(\tau,m,\lambda)(1+\sqrt{K}R)}v(y).

5.
Global gradient estimate

By the local gradient estimate of positive solution
v
of the equation (
1.1
), we have

\sup_{B_{R/2}(o)}\frac{|\nabla v|}{v}\leq c(m,\tau,\lambda)\frac{1+\sqrt{K}R}{R}.

If
v
is a global solution, letting
R\to\infty
in the above local gradient estimate, we obtain

\frac{|\nabla v|}{v}\leq c(m,\tau,\lambda)\sqrt{K},\quad\forall x\in M.

In this section, we give a explicit expression of the above
c(m,\tau,\lambda)
.

We have following key lemmas.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}d\nu)
be a complete
n
-dimensional smooth metric measure space with
{\rm Ric}_{f}^{m}\geq-(m-1)Kg
where
K
is a non-negative constant. Let
v
be a global positive solution of (
1.1
) in
M
.

(1) If
m
,
\tau
and
\lambda
satisfy (
1.11
), we denote

y_{1}=\frac{(m-1)^{2}}{\tau^{2}}K,

then for any
\delta>0
,
\omega=(h-y_{1}-\delta)^{+}
and
\alpha
large enough, there are some positive constants
l_{1}
,
l_{2}
depending on
m
,
\tau
,
\lambda
,
K
and
\delta
such that

\Delta_{f}\omega^{\alpha}\geq 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}(l_{1}\omega-l_{2}|\nabla\omega|).

(2) If
m
,
\tau
and
\lambda
satisfy (
1.12
), we denote

y_{2}=\frac{K}{\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}-m-1}{m-1}+\frac{1}{\tau}\right)\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}+m+1}{m-1}-\frac{1}{\tau}\right)},

then for any
\delta>0
,
\omega=(h-y_{2}-\delta)^{+}
and
\alpha
large enough, there are some positive constants
l_{3}
,
l_{4}
depending on
m
,
\tau
,
K
and
\delta
such that

\Delta_{f}\omega^{\alpha}\geq 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}(l_{3}\omega-l_{4}|\nabla\omega|).

Proof.

(1) By the Lemma
2.1
, if
m
,
\tau
and
\lambda
satisfy (
1.11
), there holds

\frac{h^{1-\alpha}}{2\alpha}\Delta_{f}h^{\alpha}\geq\frac{1}{m-1}\tau^{2}h^{2}-(m-1)Kh-\frac{a_{1}}{2}h^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla h|.

(5.1)

We note that
\nabla w=\nabla h
in
\{h\geq y_{1}+\delta\}
, but
\nabla w
causes a distribution on
\{h=y_{1}+\delta\}
. Now, if we assume
\alpha>1
, then the distribution caused by
\nabla w

on
\{h=y_{1}+\delta\}
is eliminated by
w
since
w=0
on
\{h=y_{1}+\delta\}
.

Since
h\geq w
, it follows that

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}w^{\alpha}=

\displaystyle-\alpha w^{\alpha-1}\langle\nabla w,\nabla f\rangle+\mathrm{div}(\alpha w^{\alpha-1}\nabla w)

(5.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha w^{\alpha-1}\langle\nabla h,\nabla f\rangle+\mathrm{div}(\alpha w^{\alpha-1}\nabla h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha w^{\alpha-1}\Delta_{f}h+\alpha(\alpha-1)w^{\alpha-2}|\nabla h|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{w^{\alpha-1}}{h^{\alpha-1}}\left(\alpha h^{\alpha-1}\Delta_{f}h+\alpha(\alpha-1)w^{-1}h^{\alpha-1}|\nabla h|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{w^{\alpha-1}}{h^{\alpha-1}}\left(\alpha h^{\alpha-1}\Delta_{f}h+\alpha(\alpha-1)h^{\alpha-2}|\nabla h|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{w^{\alpha-1}}{h^{\alpha-1}}\Delta_{f}h^{\alpha}.

Substituting (
5.1
) into the (
5.2
), we get

\Delta_{f}w^{\alpha}\geq 2\alpha w^{\alpha-1}\left(\frac{1}{m-1}\tau^{2}h^{2}-(m-1)Kh-\frac{a_{1}}{2}h^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla h|\right).

Since
h\geq y_{1}+\delta=\frac{(m-1)^{2}}{\tau^{2}}K+\delta
, the above inequality can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}w^{\alpha}\geq

\displaystyle 2\alpha w^{\alpha-1}h^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(h^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{m-1}\tau^{2}h-(m-1)K\right)-\frac{a_{1}}{2}|\nabla w|\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2\alpha w^{\alpha-1}h^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(h^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\delta\tau^{2}}{m-1}-\frac{a_{1}}{2}|\nabla w|\right).

There holds

y_{1}+\delta\leq h\leq c(m,\tau,\lambda)\sqrt{K}

on
\{h\geq y_{1}+\delta\}
, thus we have

\Delta_{f}w^{\alpha}\geq 2\alpha w^{\alpha-1}(l_{1}w-l_{2}|\nabla w|),

where
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
are two positive constants depending on
m
,
K
,
\tau
,
\lambda
and
\delta
.

(2) If
m
,
\tau
and
\lambda
satisfy (
1.12
), it follows from (
2.20
) that

\frac{h^{1-\alpha}}{2\alpha}\Delta_{f}h^{\alpha}\geq B_{m,\tau}h^{2}-(m-1)Kh-\frac{a_{1}}{2}h^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla h|,

(5.3)

where

B_{m,\tau}=\left(\frac{1}{m-1}-\frac{m-1}{2}\left(\frac{m+1}{m-1}-\frac{1}{\tau}\right)^{2}\right)\tau^{2}.

Then, on
\{h\geq y_{2}+\delta\}
,

h\geq y_{2}+\delta=\frac{(m-1)K}{B_{m,\tau}}+\delta.

So the (
5.3
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}w^{\alpha}\geq

\displaystyle 2\alpha w^{\alpha-1}h^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(h^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(B_{m,\tau}h-(m-1)K\right)-\frac{a_{1}}{2}|\nabla w|\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2\alpha w^{\alpha-1}h^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(h^{\frac{1}{2}}\delta B_{m,\tau}-\frac{a_{1}}{2}|\nabla w|\right).

There holds

y_{2}+\delta\leq h\leq c(m,\tau,\lambda)\sqrt{K}

on
\{h\geq y_{2}+\delta\}
, so we obtain

\Delta_{f}w^{\alpha}\geq 2\alpha w^{\alpha-1}(l_{3}w-l_{4}|\nabla w|),

where
l_{3}
and
l_{4}
are two positive constants depending on
m
,
K
,
\tau
and
\delta
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}d\nu)
be a complete
n
-dimensional smooth metric measure space with
{\rm Ric}_{f}^{m}\geq-(m-1)Kg
where
K
is a non-negative constant. Let
v
be a global positive solution of (
1.1
) in
M
.
For some
y>0
, we let
w=(h-y)^{+}
. If
w
satisfy the following inequality

\Delta_{f}w^{\alpha}\geq 2\alpha w^{\alpha-1}(l_{3}w-l_{4}|\nabla w|),

(5.4)

where
l_{3}
and
l_{4}
and
\alpha
are some positive constants, then
w=0
, i.e.,
h\leq y
.

Proof.

Let
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
be a cut-off function to be determine later. For constant
\gamma>1
, we choose
w^{\gamma}\eta^{2}
as test function. Now, we multiply the both sides of (
5.4
) by
w^{\gamma}\eta^{2}
, integrate then the obtained inequality over
M
to get

\int_{M}(w^{\gamma}\eta^{2})\Delta_{f}w^{\alpha}e^{-f}d\nu\geq\int_{M}2\alpha w^{\alpha+\gamma-1}(l_{3}w-l_{4}|\nabla w|)\eta^{2}e^{-f}d\nu.

Using the integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\alpha\gamma w^{\alpha+\gamma-2}|\nabla w|^{2}\eta^{2}e^{-f}d\nu-\int_{M}2\alpha w^{\alpha+\gamma-1}\langle\nabla w,\nabla\eta\rangle\eta e^{-f}d\nu

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{M}2\alpha w^{\alpha+\gamma-1}(l_{3}w-l_{4}|\nabla w|)\eta^{2}e^{-f}d\nu.

Since

\langle\nabla w,\nabla\eta\rangle\geq-|\nabla w||\nabla\eta|,

we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{M}2l_{3}w^{\alpha+\gamma}\eta^{2}e^{-f}d\nu+\int_{M}\gamma w^{\alpha+\gamma-2}|\nabla w|^{2}\eta^{2}e^{-f}d\nu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}2l_{4}w^{\alpha+\gamma-1}|\nabla w|\eta^{2}e^{-f}d\nu+\int_{M}2w^{\alpha+\gamma-1}|\nabla w||\nabla\eta|\eta e^{-f}d\nu.

Now applying Young’s inequality, we get

2l_{4}w^{\alpha+\gamma-1}|\nabla w|\eta^{2}\leq l_{4}w^{\alpha+\gamma-2}\frac{|\nabla w|^{2}}{\epsilon}\eta^{2}+\epsilon l_{4}w^{\alpha+\gamma}\eta^{2};

2w^{\alpha+\gamma-1}|\nabla w||\nabla\eta|\eta\leq w^{\alpha+\gamma-2}\frac{|\nabla w|^{2}}{\epsilon}\eta^{2}+\epsilon w^{\alpha+\gamma}|\nabla\eta|^{2}.

Now we choose
\epsilon
such that

\frac{l_{4}+1}{\epsilon}=\gamma,

then we obtain

\int_{M}2l_{3}w^{\alpha+\gamma}\eta^{2}e^{-f}d\nu\leq\int_{M}\epsilon w^{\alpha+\gamma}|\nabla\eta|^{2}e^{-f}d\nu+\int_{M}\epsilon l_{4}w^{\alpha+\gamma}\eta^{2}e^{-f}d\nu.

We choose
\gamma
large enough such that
\epsilon l_{4}<l_{3}
, we have

\int_{M}l_{3}w^{\alpha+\gamma}\eta^{2}e^{-f}d\nu\leq\int_{M}\epsilon w^{\alpha+\gamma}|\nabla\eta|^{2}e^{-f}d\nu.

Now, if
w\neq 0
, without loss of generality we may suppose that
w\mid_{B_{1}}\neq 0
for some geodesic
ball
B_{1}
with radius 1, we observe that there always holds true

\int_{B_{1}}e^{-f}d\nu>0.

We use
B_{k}
to represent the geodesic ball with a radius of
k
. Then we may choose
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{k+1})
such that

\eta\equiv 1\text{ in }B_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla\eta|\leq 4\text{ in }B_{k+1}.

Then, we derive that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{k+1}}16\epsilon w^{\alpha+\gamma}e^{-f}d\nu\geq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{k+1}}l_{3}w^{\alpha+\gamma}e^{-f}d\nu

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{k}}l_{3}w^{\alpha+\gamma}e^{-f}d\nu.

By iteration on
k
, we get

\int_{B_{k}}w^{\alpha+\gamma}e^{-f}d\nu\geq\left(\frac{\hat{C}_{1}}{\epsilon}\right)^{k}\int_{B_{1}}w^{\alpha+\gamma}e^{-f}d\nu.

(5.5)

where
\hat{C}_{1}
is positive constant.
Since
w
is uniform bounded, we have

\hat{C}_{2}^{\alpha+\gamma}V_{f}(B_{k})\geq\int_{B_{k}}w^{\alpha+\gamma}e^{-f}d\nu

(5.6)

and

\int_{B_{1}}w^{\alpha+\gamma}e^{-f}d\nu\geq\hat{C}_{3}^{\alpha+\gamma}V_{f}(B_{1}),

(5.7)

where
\hat{C}_{2}
and
\hat{C}_{3}
are positive constant, and
V_{f}(B_{k})=\int_{B_{k}}e^{-f}d\nu
.
By volume comparison theorem, we have

\frac{V_{f}(B_{k})}{V_{f}(B_{1})}\leq e^{(m-1)k\sqrt{K}}.

(5.8)

Let
\hat{C}_{4}=\frac{\hat{C}_{2}}{\hat{C}_{3}}
, then plugging (
5.6
), (
5.7
), and (
5.8
) into (
5.5
), we infers

\hat{C}_{4}^{\alpha+\gamma}e^{(m-1)k\sqrt{K}}\geq e^{k\ln\frac{\hat{C}_{1}}{\epsilon}}.

(5.9)

We choose
\gamma
such that

\ln\frac{\hat{C}_{1}}{\epsilon}>2(m-1)\sqrt{K}+2,

if
k
is chosen sufficiently large, (
5.9
) can not hold. Hence,
w\equiv 0
. So we finish the proof of this lemma.
∎

Now, we are in the position to give the proof of Theorem
1.10
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
5.2
, we can infer the Theorem
1.10
. If
m
,
\lambda
,
\tau
satisfy (
1.11
) or (
1.12
), then by Lemma
5.1
, for any
\delta>0
and
w=(h-y_{i}-\delta)^{+}
,
i=1,2
, we obtain

\Delta_{f}w^{\alpha}\geq 2\alpha w^{\alpha-1}(l_{3}w-l_{4}|\nabla w|).

According to Lemma
5.2
, we obtain
w\equiv 0
, i.e.,
h\leq y_{i}+\delta
. Since
\delta
can be arbitrary small, it is clear that
h\leq y_{i}
. Since

h=|\nabla u|^{2}=|(\nabla v)/v|^{2},

thus, we get

\frac{|\nabla v|}{v}\leq\sqrt{y_{i}}.

Hence, we finish the proof of Theorem
1.10
.
∎

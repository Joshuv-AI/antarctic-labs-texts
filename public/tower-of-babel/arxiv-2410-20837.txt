Hybrid logic of strict betweenness
Rafał Gruszczyński, Zhiguang Zhao

Abstract
. The paper is devoted to modal properties of the ternary strict betweenness relation as used in the development of various systems of geometry. We show that such a relation is non-definable in a basic similarity type with a binary operator of possibility, and we put forward two systems of hybrid logic, one of them complete with respect to the class of dense linear betweenness frames without endpoints, and the other with respect to its subclass composed of Dedekind complete frames. MSC: 03B45, 53C75 Keywords: modal logic, hybrid logic, binary operators, ternary relations, betweenness relation



1.
Introduction

In this paper we investigate mutual dependencies between a ternary strict betweenness relation and hybrid logic with a binary operator of possibility and a family of satisfaction operators in the sense of
(
Blackburn et al., 2001
, 7.3 Hybrid logic)
. The betweenness relation we put into focus is the cornerstone of order fragments of axiomatic systems of Hilbert-style geometry, and in choosing the constraints, we stay as close to geometry as possible. In this, our work differs from that of
Düntsch et al. (2023)
where the authors scrutinize a class of reflexive betweenness frames that encompass very general aspects of betweenness, common to many, not necessarily geometric in spirit, approaches. The other difference is hidden in the techniques applied:
Düntsch et al. (2023)
use Boolean algebras with operators, while we adhere to logic. The two papers share the idea for the possibility operator
\mathop{\langle B\rangle}
as defined in
(
van Benthem and Bezhanishvili, 2007
)
.

In Section
2
, we list the first-order axioms for strict betweenness, the inspiration for these being the axiomatization of betweenness by
Borsuk and Szmielew (1960)
. We single out two classes of frames: linear betweenness frames without endpoints (
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{LBWE}}
) and dense linear betweenness frames without endpoints (
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{DLBWE}}
). These will play a crucial role in the sequel. Moreover, in this section we draw a comparison between betweenness frames and standard binary linearly ordered structures. Section
3
contains a semantical characterization of the modal language we use.

In Section
4
we deal with the problem of non-definability of first-order properties of betweennes either by means of a pure modal language or a modal language enriched with the satisfaction operators. To do this, we generalize ten Cate’s Goldblatt-Thomason style theorem
(
ten Cate, 2005
, Theorem 4.3.1)
to arbitrary modal types. Applying the generalized theorem, we show that the class of dense linear betweenness frames without endpoints is not definable in the extended language with the satisfaction operators. In Section
5
we show that the class
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{DLBWE}}
can be defined by means of pure formulas of hybrid logic. Section
6
pursues the problem of definability, focusing on the particular case of the density axiom. We prove that the property can be expressed by means of a pure formula of the extended language with the satisfaction operators with respect to the class
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{LBWE}}
. This is a key result that allows us to find a pure hybrid axiomatization of dense linear betweenness frames without endpoints in Section
7
. Therein, we put forward logic
\mathbf{B}_{h}
which is sound and complete with respect to
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{DLBWE}}
. In particular, we obtain that
\mathbf{B}_{h}
is a logic of the frame
\mathfrak{Q}\coloneqq\langle\m@thbbch@rQ,B_{<}\rangle
obtained from the unique—up to isomorphism—countable dense linear order without endpoints, i.e.,
\langle\m@thbbch@rQ,<\rangle
.

In the same section, we go beyond the elementary classes of frames considering
Dedekind complete
elements of
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{DLBWE}}
, the class
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{CDLBWE}}
. We propose a modal counterpart of the completeness axiom couched in the language with the existential modality, and we consider the logic
\mathbf{B}_{h}^{+}
extending
\mathbf{B}_{h}
with the axiom. We prove soundness and completeness of this logic with respect to
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{CDLBWE}}
. In consequence, we prove that
\mathbf{B}_{h}^{+}
is a logic of the frame
\mathfrak{R}\coloneqq\langle\m@thbbch@rR,B_{<}\rangle
obtained from the real line
\langle\m@thbbch@rR,<\rangle

2.
Betweenness relations and linear orders

Let
\mathfrak{F}\coloneqq\langle W,B\rangle
be a 3-frame. We will read
B(x,y,z)
as
y
is between
x
and
z
, and in this way—for heuristic reasons—we distinguish the middle coordinate.
\#(x,y,z)
means that the elements of the set
\{x,y,z\}
are pairwise different.

For the basic characterization of (strict) betweenness, we are going to use the following standard set of first-order axioms:

(B1)

\displaystyle B(x,y,z)\rightarrow\#(x,y,z)\,,

(B2)

\displaystyle B(x,y,z)\rightarrow B(z,y,x)\,,

(B3)

\displaystyle B(x,y,z)\rightarrow\neg B(x,z,y)\,,

(B4)

\displaystyle B(x,y,z)\wedge B(y,z,u)\rightarrow B(x,y,u)\,,

(B5)

\displaystyle B(x,y,z)\wedge B(y,u,z)\rightarrow B(x,y,u)\,,

(B6)

\displaystyle\#(x,y,z)\rightarrow B(x,y,z)\vee B(x,z,y)\vee B(y,x,z)\,,

(B7)

\displaystyle\forall y\exists x\exists z\,B(x,y,z)\,,

(B8)

\displaystyle x\neq z\rightarrow\exists y\,B(x,y,z)\,.

(
B1
) says that
B
is a strict relation, which according to (
B2
) is symmetrical on its first and third projections. (
B3
) postulates asymmetry on second and third coordinates. (
B4
) and (
B5
) are, respectively, outer and inner transitivity axioms. (
B6
) stipulates linearity

B2
). Without the symmetry axiom to stipulate linearity we would need six disjuncts in the consequent of the axiom. See also the proof of Theorem

, (

(

Definition 2.1
.

Any 3-frame

that satisfies (

. The class of all such frames will be denoted by

. The elements of the class

will be called

linear betweenness frames without endpoints.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{DLOWE}}
be the class of all dense linear orders without endpoints, i.e., the class of all 2-frames

satisfying the following axioms:

(L1)

\displaystyle x<y\rightarrow y\not<x\,,

(L2)

\displaystyle x<y\wedge y<z\rightarrow x<z\,,

(L3)

\displaystyle x\neq y\rightarrow x<y\vee y<x\,,

(L4)

\displaystyle\forall x\exists y\exists z\,y<x<z\,,

(L5)

\displaystyle\forall x\forall y\,(x<y\rightarrow\exists z(x<z<y))\,.

Let

\boldsymbol{\mathsf{LOWE}}

3.
Hybrid logic with binary operators

As the betweenness relation is ternary for its modal analysis we will need a binary modal operator
\mathop{\langle B\rangle}
. Let
\beta
be the modal similarity type with
\mathop{\langle B\rangle}
as the only operator. The modal language
\cal{L}_{\beta}
is given by the following definition

\varphi\coloneqq\top\mid p\mid\neg\varphi\mid\varphi\wedge\psi\mid\mathop{\langle B\rangle}(\varphi,\psi)\,.

The basic hybrid language
\cal{H}_{\beta}
is a two-sorted language that is an expansion of
\cal{L}_{\beta}
with nominals
i,j,k,l
(indexed if necessary)

\varphi\coloneqq\top\mid p\mid i\mid\neg\varphi\mid\varphi\wedge\psi\mid\mathop{\langle B\rangle}(\varphi,\psi)\,.

The set of all propositional letters will be denoted by ‘
\mathrm{Prop}
’, and the set of nominals by ‘
\Omega
’. We assume that
\mathrm{Prop}\cap\Omega=\emptyset
.

Last but not least, the hybrid language that plays the most important role in this paper is
\cal{H}_{\beta}(\at)
that expands
\cal{H}_{\beta}
with a family of satisfaction operators
\at_{i}
, one for every nominal

\varphi\coloneqq\top\mid p\mid i\mid\neg\varphi\mid\varphi\wedge\psi\mid\mathop{\langle B\rangle}(\varphi,\psi)\mid\at_{i}\varphi\,.

The basic idea for an operator
\mathop{\langle B\rangle}
comes from
van Benthem and Bezhanishvili (2007)
. Given a model
\mathfrak{M}\coloneqq\langle\mathfrak{F},V\rangle
based on a 3-frame
\mathfrak{F}
we characterize the semantic for
\mathop{\langle B\rangle}
in the following way

(
\mathtt{df}\,{\mathop{\langle B\rangle}}
)

\begin{split}\mathfrak{M},w\Vdash\mathop{\langle B\rangle}(\varphi,\psi)\;\mathrel{\mathord{:}\mathord{\Longleftrightarrow}}\;{}&\\
(\exists x,y\in W)\,&{}(\mathfrak{M},x\Vdash\varphi\text{ and }\mathfrak{M},y\Vdash\psi\text{ and }B(x,w,y))\,.\end{split}

\mathop{\langle B\rangle}
gives rise to a natural unary
convexity
operator

(
\mathtt{df}\,{\Conv}
)

\Conv\varphi\;\mathrel{\mathord{:}\mathord{\Longleftrightarrow}}\;\mathop{\langle B\rangle}(\varphi,\varphi)\,.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a frame
\mathfrak{F}\coloneqq\langle W,B\rangle
, a
valuation
function is any function
V\colon\mathrm{Prop}\cup\Omega\to 2^{W}
such that for every nominal
i
,
V(i)
is a singleton subset of the universe.

Recall that the semantics of the at operator is given by the following:

(
\mathtt{df}\,{\at_{i}}
)

\mathfrak{M},w\Vdash\at_{i}\varphi\;\mathrel{\mathord{:}\mathord{\Longleftrightarrow}}\;\mathfrak{M},V(i)\Vdash\varphi\,.

We can see that:

\begin{split}\mathfrak{M},w\Vdash\mathop{\langle B\rangle}(i,j)\Longleftrightarrow{}&\\
(\exists x,y\in W)\,&(V(i)=\{x\}\text{ and }V(j)=\{y\}\text{ and }B(x,w,y))\,.\end{split}

To simplify things, sometimes
V(i)
is identified with its element and it is written, e.g.,
B(V(i),w,V(j))
. Thus we can express the above equivalence as

\mathfrak{M},w\Vdash\mathop{\langle B\rangle}(i,j)\Longleftrightarrow B(V(i),w,V(j))\,.

We will also omit the letter ‘
\mathfrak{M}
’ and write, e.g.,
w\Vdash\varphi
instead, if it is clear from the context or irrelevant what model is taken into account. Obviously:

w\Vdash\at_{i}\mathop{\langle B\rangle}(j,k)\Longleftrightarrow V(i)\Vdash\mathop{\langle B\rangle}(j,k)\Longleftrightarrow B(V(j),V(i),V(k))\,.

In a couple of places, we will also consider the language
\cal{H}_{\beta}(\at,\Ex)
, which is an expansion of
\cal{H}_{\beta}(\at)
with the existential modality
\Ex

(
\mathtt{df}\,{\Ex}
)

w\Vdash\Ex\varphi\;\mathrel{\mathord{:}\mathord{\Longleftrightarrow}}\;(\exists x\in W)\,x\Vdash\varphi\,.

Non-definability

4.1.
The tools

We are going to show that those frame properties that correspond to axioms (

7.4.

\mathbf{B}_{h}^{+}

For a fixed
\mathbf{B}_{h}^{+}
-consistent set of sentences

yielded by it satisfies all the axioms of

with respect to

Due to the isomorphism between
\mathfrak{F}^{\Gamma}
and

\{\Gamma_{a}\mid a\in\m@thbbch@rQ\}
in such a way that

with

.

Unless declared otherwise, we will use the initial letters of the Latin alphabet:

If

is an irrational number and

is an element of the minimal completion of

Definition 7.14
.

\displaystyle\Lambda_{x}

\displaystyle{}\coloneqq\{\phi\mid\phi\mbox{ is local at }x\}

\displaystyle\Sigma_{x}

\displaystyle{}\coloneqq\{\langle B\rangle(\phi,\psi)\mid\mbox{there are }a,a^{\prime}\mbox{ such that }a\Vdash\phi,a^{\prime}\Vdash\psi\mbox{ and }x\lessdot(a,a^{\prime})\}

Remark 7.20
.

By the corollary, for any irrational number
x
,
\Lambda_{x}\cup\Pi_{x}
has
a maximal
\mathbf{B}_{h}^{+}
-consistent extension
\Gamma_{x}
(which is not named by Lemma
7.16
(iii), and we do not require it to be pasted either). We want to use
\Gamma_{x}
’s to expand the frame
\mathfrak{F}^{\Gamma}
in such a way as to obtain a frame that is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{R}\coloneqq\langle\m@thbbch@rR,B_{<}\rangle
. Since it may happen that for different irrationals
x
and
y
, the sets
\Gamma_{x}
and
\Gamma_{y}
coincide, we think about the irrational numbers as placeholders to store these sets. To be formally precise, the new points of the frame could be pairs
\langle\Gamma_{x},x\rangle
. To avoid the notation cluttering we avoid this, and we always think about
\Gamma_{x}
and
\Gamma_{y}
as different points in the case
x\neq y
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
\mathbf{B}_{h}^{+}
-consistent set of sentences. The named model
\mathfrak{M}^{\Gamma}
yielded by
\Gamma
is countable, and all axioms of
\mathbf{B}_{h}
are globally true in it. Therefore,
\mathfrak{F}^{\Gamma}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{Q}
. We expand
\mathfrak{F}^{\Gamma}
to the frame
\mathfrak{F}^{\Gamma}_{+}
such that

(i)

the domain
W^{\Gamma}_{+}
of
\mathfrak{F}^{\Gamma}_{+}
is
W^{\Gamma}
extended with all the
\Gamma_{x}
’s where
x
is an irrational number,

(ii)

B^{\Gamma}_{+}(\Gamma_{x},\Gamma_{y},\Gamma_{z})\;\mathrel{\mathord{:}\mathord{\Longleftrightarrow}}\;x<y<z\text{\ or\ }z<x<y
(for all reals
x,y,z
),

and we turn the frame into a model
\mathfrak{M}^{\Gamma}_{+}\coloneqq\langle\mathfrak{F}^{\Gamma}_{+},V^{\Gamma}_{+}\rangle
in the standard way,

\Gamma_{x}\in V^{\Gamma}_{+}(p)\;\mathrel{\mathord{:}\mathord{\Longleftrightarrow}}\;p\in\Gamma_{x}\,.

For nominals,
V^{\Gamma}_{+}(i)=V^{\Gamma}(i)
, as these by Lemma
7.16
(iii) can only be true at rational indexed sets:

\Gamma_{x}\in V^{\Gamma}_{+}(i)\Longleftrightarrow\Gamma_{x}\in V^{\Gamma}(i)\Longleftrightarrow x\in\m@thbbch@rQ\text{\ and\ }i\in\Gamma_{x}\,.

It is easy to see that the restriction of
\mathfrak{M}^{\Gamma}_{+}
to
\mathfrak{F}^{\Gamma}
is the same as the rational canonical model
\mathfrak{M}^{\Gamma}
yielded by
\Gamma
.

Lemma 7.21

(Truth Lemma for
\mathfrak{M}^{\Gamma}_{+}
)
.

For any formula
\phi
and any number
x\in\m@thbbch@rR

\mathfrak{M}^{\Gamma}_{+},\Gamma_{x}\Vdash\phi\quad\text{iff}\quad\phi\in\Gamma_{x}\,.

Proof.

The basic cases for propositional variables and for nominals hold by the definitions and remarks above, and the Boolean connectives are handled in the standard way.

Consider the formula
\phi\coloneqq\mathop{\langle B\rangle}(\varphi,\psi)
, and suppose
\mathfrak{M}^{\Gamma}_{+},\Gamma_{x}\Vdash\phi
. We divide the argument into two cases.

(1st case) Let
x
be a rational number. Let
\Gamma_{y}
and
\Gamma_{z}
be points such that
B^{\Gamma}_{+}(\Gamma_{y},\Gamma_{x},\Gamma_{z})
,
\Gamma_{y}\Vdash\varphi
and
\Gamma_{z}\Vdash\psi
. By the induction hypothesis
\varphi\in\Gamma_{y}
and
\psi\in\Gamma_{z}
. W.l.o.g., assume that
y<x<z
.

Observe that there must exist a rational number
a<x
with
\varphi\in\Gamma_{a}
. For suppose there is no such rational, which in particular means that
y\notin\m@thbbch@rQ
. Therefore, for a sufficiently small interval of rationals around
y
, all its points
a
are such that
\neg\varphi\in\Gamma_{a}
. But then,
\neg\varphi
is local at
y
, and so
\neg\varphi\in\Gamma_{y}
, which is a contradiction. Analogously, there must be a rational number
b>x
with
\psi\in\Gamma_{b}
. This means that
B^{\Gamma}(\Gamma_{a},\Gamma_{x},\Gamma_{b})
, and so
\mathop{\langle B\rangle}(\varphi,\psi)\in\Gamma_{x}
.

For the other direction, suppose that
\mathop{\langle B\rangle}(\varphi,\psi)\in\Gamma_{x}
. As
\Gamma_{x}
is a point in
\mathfrak{M}^{\Gamma}
, by Lemma
7.8
there are rationals
a
and
b
such that
\varphi\in\Gamma_{a}
and
\psi\in\Gamma_{b}
and
B^{\Gamma}(\Gamma_{a},\Gamma_{x},\Gamma_{b})
. But these also hold in
\mathfrak{M}^{\Gamma}_{+}
, so by the induction hypothesis we obtain
\mathfrak{M}^{\Gamma}_{+},\Gamma_{a}\Vdash\varphi
and
\mathfrak{M}^{\Gamma}_{+},\Gamma_{b}\Vdash\psi
and
B^{\Gamma}_{+}(\Gamma_{a},\Gamma_{x},\Gamma_{b})
. Therefore
\mathfrak{M}^{\Gamma}_{+},\Gamma_{x}\Vdash\mathop{\langle B\rangle}(\varphi,\psi)
.

(2nd case) Let
x
be an irrational number. If
\Gamma_{x}\Vdash\langle B\rangle(\varphi,\psi)
, then by the same argument as in the rational case, we can find rational numbers
a<x<b
such that
\varphi\in\Gamma_{a}
and
\psi\in\Gamma_{b}
. By the definition of
\Sigma_{x}
, we have that
\mathop{\langle B\rangle}(\varphi,\psi)\in\Sigma_{x}\subseteq\Gamma_{x}
.

Now assume
\mathop{\langle B\rangle}(\varphi,\psi)\in\Gamma_{x}
. Since
\Sigma_{x}\cup\Pi_{x}
contains all formulas of the form
\mathop{\langle B\rangle}(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
, and since
\Sigma_{x}\cup\Pi_{x}\subseteq\Gamma_{x}
, it must be the case that
\mathop{\langle B\rangle}(\varphi,\psi)
is an element of
\Sigma_{x}
(if not, its negation would be in
\Pi_{x}
, and so in
\Gamma_{x}
, which contradicts the consistency of the latter set). In consequence, by the definition of
\Sigma_{x}
, there are
\Gamma_{a}
and
\Gamma_{b}
such that
B^{\Gamma}_{+}(\Gamma_{a},\Gamma_{x},\Gamma_{b})
and
\mathfrak{M}^{\Gamma}_{+},\Gamma_{a}\Vdash\varphi
and
\mathfrak{M}^{\Gamma}_{+},\Gamma_{b}\Vdash\psi
.
Therefore,
\mathfrak{M}^{\Gamma}_{+},\Gamma_{x}\Vdash\mathop{\langle B\rangle}(\varphi,\psi)
.

This completes the proof the lemma.
∎

(Completeness Theorem for

)

If

-consistent set of formulas, then there is a model of

.

(iii) no nominal is local at any irrational number.
\mathfrak{M}^{\Gamma}
is based on the frame
\mathfrak{F}^{\Gamma}
from the class

. We have that

. Observe that axiom (

) is globally true in

. With the techniques described above, we extend

Corollary 7.23
.

Every
\cal{H}_{\beta}(\at)
-formula valid on the frame
\mathfrak{R}=\langle\m@thbbch@rR,B_{<}\rangle
is a theorem of
\mathbf{B}_{h}^{+}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\nvdash_{\mathbf{B}_{h}^{+}}\varphi
, which means that
\{\neg\varphi\}
is
\mathbf{B}_{h}^{+}
-consistent. Thus by Theorem
7.22
there is a model
\mathfrak{M}^{\Gamma}_{+}
at which
\neg\varphi
holds at some
\Gamma_{x}
. The model is based on the frame
\mathfrak{F}^{\Gamma}_{+}
which is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{R}

∎

As is well-known, the real line
\langle\m@thbbch@rR,<\rangle
can be characterized up-to-isomorphism by the following conditions: it is a complete, dense linear order without endpoints which is separable, i.e., has a countable subset
D
such every non-empty open interval has a common point with
D
. In light of mutual equivalence between strict orders and strict betweenness, this characterization can be also applied to
\mathfrak{R}=\langle\m@thbbch@rR,B_{<}\rangle
. That is, the class
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{SCDLBWE}}
of those elements
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{CDLBWE}}
that meet the separability condition has
\mathfrak{R}
as its only—up-to-isomorphism—element.

Let
\mathfrak{I}\coloneqq\langle[0,1],<\rangle
be the standard binary frame on the closed interval
[0,1]
. Let us consider the product frame

\mathfrak{R}\times\mathfrak{I}\coloneqq\langle\m@thbbch@rR\times[0,1],<_{\ell}\rangle

where
<_{\ell}
is the lexicographic order on
\m@thbbch@rR\times[0,1]
obtained from
<
. As
\mathfrak{R}\times\mathfrak{I}
is complete but not separable, the betweenness frame

(\mathfrak{R}\times\mathfrak{I})^{\ast}\coloneqq\langle\mathfrak{R}\times\mathfrak{I},B_{<_{\ell}}\rangle

is an element of
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{CDLBWE}}
, which is not in
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{SCDLBWE}}
. We can use this fact to show that separability is not expressible in the modal language extended with either satisfaction operators or existential modalities.

Given a class of frames
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{K}}
let
L(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{K}})
be the logic of
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{K}}
, i.e., the set of all formulas that are valid on every frame in
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{K}}
. For a single frame
\mathfrak{F}
, let
L(\mathfrak{F})\coloneqq L(\{\mathfrak{F}\})
. From the results obtained in this section, we know that

L(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{CDLBWE}})=L(\mathfrak{R})\,.

Since
(\mathfrak{R}\times\mathfrak{I})^{\ast}\in\boldsymbol{\mathsf{CDLBWE}}
, it follows that
L(\mathfrak{R})\subseteq L((\mathfrak{R}\times\mathfrak{I})^{\ast})
. So, if there were a formula
\varphi
characterizing separability of
\mathfrak{R}
, it would have to be in
L(\mathfrak{R})
, and so in
L((\mathfrak{R}\times\mathfrak{I})^{\ast})
, which is impossible.

8.
Summary and future work

We have constructed two systems of hybrid logic,
\mathbf{B}_{h}
and
\mathbf{B}_{h}^{+}
, that are complete w.r.t. to the betweenness frame of rational numbers
\mathfrak{Q}
, and the betweenness frame of the reals
\mathfrak{R}
, respectively. In this way, we have shown the axiomatizability of the logic of strict betweenness in languages with
\at_{i}
-operators. It remains an open problem whether the logic of both frames in the basic modal similarity type (with
\mathop{\langle B\rangle}
as the only primitive operator) can be axiomatized. It seems that its solution will not be easy.

The techniques of this paper can be applied to study at least two more ternary relations. The first one is a geometric
equidistance
used by Mario Pieri
(
Pieri, 1908
)
to axiomatize Euclidean geometry by means of two primitives:
points
and the aforementioned relation (see
(
Gruszczyński and Pietruszczak, 2007
)
for a modern presentation). The whole system is quite complicated (in the sense of the complexity of its axioms expressed in the primitives of the language only), but at least its fragments can be investigated from the hybrid logic perspective with a binary modal operator.

The second possibility is the hybrid approach to
relative nearness
relation from
(
van Benthem, 1983
)
. At first sight more topological than geometrical in nature, the notion is a very strong one and allows for the definition of Pieri’s equidistance. The research in this direction could be then seen as a more universal approach to ternary relations underlying geometrical structures.

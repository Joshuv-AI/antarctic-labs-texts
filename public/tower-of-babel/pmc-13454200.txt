Study of stimulation mechanism of hydraulic fracturing induced thermal shock on joint system of geothermal reservoirs
Authors: Nan Li, Xiaokai Huang, Rui Wang, Xiaoran Wang, Mingyao Wei, Lei Han, Junjie Li, Lihong Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Hydraulic fracturing has been prevailingly applied to induce thermal shock in geothermal reservoirs for the utilization of geothermal energy. With natural joints commonly distribute in the reservoirs, the stimulation mechanism of thermal shock on joint system is inevitably investigated at fracturing initiation stage to enlarge heat transfer space. In this paper, the interaction of hydraulic fractures (HFs) and joints was simulated by lattice-spring method (LSM). The factors of fluid viscosities, shock times, approaching angles, and joint sets were analyzed to reveal the stimulation effect of thermal shock. The results present that orthogonal approaching angle, moderate shock times, and low fluid viscosity are beneficial to the creation of heat transfer space. The radius of expansion region of the thermal shock is around 1 m, in which the interaction behavior of HFs mainly consists of arrest and penetration. This research aims at revealing the stimulation mechanism of thermal shock on joints and therefore improving the treatment of hydraulic fracturing in geothermal energy development.

Introduction

Beneath thousands of meters from the earth’s surface, the hot dry rock (HDR) characterized by extremely low permeability and high dryness harbors huge geothermal energy1–3. As a kind of clean energy with vast reserves, the utilization ratio of geothermal energy in the global energy has been increasing for many years4–6. Geothermal energy has the potential to significantly reduce natural gas consumption and greenhouse gas emissions at competitive costs7. The conception of enhanced geothermal system (EGS) was purposed to develop and commercialize the geothermal resources stored in HDR formations and realize the green transformation of energy consumption2,8,9. In the 1970s, physicists at Los Alamos Scientific Laboratory first proposed drilling into HDR reservoirs and then injecting fluids. In the process of fluid flowing through the reservoirs, the geothermal energy can be stored in the high temperature fluid via heat exchange, which then can be transported back to surface through a production well10. The fundamental approach of hydraulic fracturing technique is injecting pressurized water into layers, manifesting a technical advantage in geothermal energy exploitation11–13. Hence, many scholars have studied the propagation of HFs by thermal shock in high-temperature HDR formations14–22. The results indicated that (1) high-temperature, high-injection rate, and high-viscosity injection fluid are crucial for the crack patterns, (2) with the increase of temperature difference, the breakdown pressure decreased and the geometry of HFs became more complicated, and (3) cyclic thermal shock can lead to more cracks and complex HFs networks in HDR formations.

Through field observations, there are numerous natural fractures (NFs) in HDR formations, which consists of a massive and intricate joint system that greatly affects the formation of HFs morphology after hydraulic fracturing23. The interaction between HFs and joints was inevitably investigated. By incorporating discrete fracture network into a hydrogeothermal model, Nadimi et al.24 and Xie et al.13 concluded that large-size fractures are conducive to high heat production, and the connectivity of fractures can greatly be affected by injection rate, temperature, and pumping schedule. Based on globally embedded 3D cohesive zone method, Hu et al.25 investigated the fracture geometry by hydrodynamics in shale with weak planes. The finding mainly showed that HFs preferentially extend along weak planes to converted into major fractures, and weak planes with the worst mechanical properties play a dominant role in fracture geometry where coexists different properties of weak planes. By studying the effect of injection schemes on fracture network patterns based on the FORGE (Frontier Observatory for Research in Geothermal Energy) EGS site, Hu et al.26 found that HFs are easily opened under cyclic injection and cracks are tend to extend under stepped injection, meaning that compared with single injection (i.e., cyclic injection and stepped injection), applying alternating injection can create a larger stimulation area. Jordan Pidho et al.27 concluded that the shear stress of deflecting fractures increases along junctions, the total slip is usually reduced in rock with high coefficient friction, and isotropic stress distribution leads to more complex fracture networks. Yuan et al.28 studied the initiation and propagation of hydraulic fractures in HDR under different cyclic injection methods. They analyzed different interaction types of HFs and joints under cycle interval cyclic injection method, and found that the fracturing area of the alternating cyclic injection method is 2.3 ~ 2.7 times that of the single injection scheme. To study the activation effect of thermal treatment on unloading-induced fractures, Liu et al.29 conducted triaxial shear slip experiments on granites samples within saw-cutting surface and suggested that the initial stage of fluid pressurization is critical to the activation of fractures. The result also presented that due to thermal damage, the number of sheared-off asperities increases with the temperature, which in turn leads to an increase in the occurrence of stick–slip events.

Induced seismicity is a critical issue in hot dry rock hydraulic fracturing. Previous studies have shown that fluid injection can induce fault reactivation and seismic events30. Experimental results reveal the characteristics of fault slip, energy budget, and precursory stress drop under different injection rates31. The frictional stability of minerals under hydrothermal conditions also plays an important role in injection-induced seismicity32. Through a comprehensive investigation on the above researches, it is concluded that the intrinsic joints of HDR can slip and/or open during thermal shock treatments, therefore altering their structures and areas33,34. During the process, the joint system in HDR formation is stimulated and a considerable space is formed for heat exchange. Due to the influence of thermal shock treatments and joint properties, the different interaction behaviors of HFs and joints appear with different stimulation degrees. Additionally, there is also a lack of relevant research on stimulation mechanism of thermal shock on joint sets. Therefore, it is of great significance to study the stimulation effect on joints in HDR under thermal shock to create a decent heat-transfer space at fracturing initiation stage, which is conducive for realizing efficient geothermal energy utilization.

In this paper, under the geological background of Gonghe Basin, we investigate the thermal shock on joint system by LSM. The influence factors, including thermal shock times, approaching angles, fluid viscosities, and joint sets are investigated during hydraulic fracturing. The stress, aperture, stimulation area, and interaction behaviors of joints and HFs are comprehensively analyzed to investigate the stimulation mechanism of thermal shock by hydraulic fracturing. The relationship between stimulation mechanism and treatment parameters are discussed to determine appropriate fracturing methods, which aims at not only revealing the stimulation mechanism of thermal shock on joints, but also providing a reference for adopting optimal treatment to increase the reservoir heat transfer space in geothermal energy development.

Geological background

The Gonghe Basin in Qinghai Province is a key demonstration site for hot dry rock development in China. Extensive geomechanical studies have been conducted in this area, providing a solid foundation for numerical simulation research35. In order to develop and utilize geothermal energy, Gonghe Basin had been chosen to carry out scientific and technological research on the development of HDR36. As shown in Fig. 1, the basin is in a slope from Yellow River uplift which is shallow in the west and deep in the east to Tanggemu depression37. There are 6 hot springs over 60%, with one at Guide depression reaching the temperature of 93.5 °C. The drilling results indicate that the depth of the granite basement in the Gonghe Basin generally trends from shallow in the east to deep in the west.Fig. 1Schematic diagram of the location of Gonghe basin (after38).

Simulation methodology

Lattice-spring method

With the consideration of the convenience of establishing and running a coupled thermal-hydro-mechanical (THM) model, LSM that treats the target as a group of discrete particles connected by springs, as presented in Fig. 2a, has been adopted to study the interaction behaviors of HFs and joints, which is incorporated into XSite software44,45. By using LSM, Rui and Zhao46 developed a new coupled thermo-mechanical model to solve thermal–mechanical fracturing and large deformation problems and Lv et al.47 found that large intercepting angle, friction angle, and cohesion are conducive to the direct cross behaviors of HFs.Fig. 2Schematic diagram of lattice spring method45: (a) lattice network of rock matrix; (b) matrix flow scheme between fluid nodes.

Schematic diagram of lattice spring method45: (a) lattice network of rock matrix; (b) matrix flow scheme between fluid nodes.

Thermal-hydro-mechanical coupling

An efficient numerical scheme is implemented that allows for tightly coupled THM simulations45,49. The scheme is based on multi-stepping of the fast advection-convection process (with small explicit timestep) while accumulating the heat convection term that is then transferred to the slow conduction–convection process using a larger timestep. When refers to mechanical logic, according to Eq. (14), thermal strains in the rock induce changes in fracture aperture that, in turn, disturb the local fluid pressures. This then motivates a restoration of a quasi-static state of mechanical equilibrium and quasi-steady fluid flow field. Thus, new fluid velocities are produced that affect the advection-convection process.

Discussions

Thermal shock treatment

As analyzed in Sect. “Results analysis”, the treatment parameters are investigated to optimize the implementation of hydraulic fracturing in HDR reservoirs. During the fracturing initiation stage of thermal shock, the increase of viscosity can hinder the propagation of HFs, leading a more concentrating distribution of HFs around the wellbore and a limited stimulation effect on adjacent joints. Although fracturing pressure increase with fluid viscosity, high-viscosity fracturing fluid would severely affects the stability of injection wellbore. Meanwhile, lower-viscosity fracturing fluid more easily connects micropores and widens cracks54, creating longer fluid paths that beneficial to the diffusion of fluids propagating along joint planes. Therefore, the shear stimulation area induced by the fracturing fluid viscosity of 3 mPa*s is the largest. Thermal shock can reduce the mechanical strength and increase the permeability of reservoirs, therefore creating the most complicated and longest HFs after 10 shock times. With thermal shock continues, more tension stimulation area of joints than shear stimulation area was formed, as the proportion of shear stimulation area reached its maximum after 5 shock times. Additionally, under too many shock times, joints far from injection wellbores could be compacted that narrower than the original state, meaning that optimal shock times should be determined to prevent the closure of joints. When approaching angle is perpendicular to joints, HFs are more likely to penetrate joints, which is helpful to increase the ratio of shear stimulation that contributes to the self-support of joints at fracturing initiation stage.

When multiple joint sets exist near an injection wellbore, HFs tend to propagate along joints that served as natural pre-existing paths for fluid to stimulate more fractures. Therefore, once a HF was arrested by a joint, the shape of the joint plays a decisive role in the propagation path. When expanding from the edge of joints into rock matrix, HFs are also likely to divert to the direction plane of maximum principal stress. Since the aperture of joint set 1 is larger than joint set 2 overall, the joint set closer to an injection wellbore can be more stimulated.

Thermal shock mechanism

As shown in Fig. 23, the limited data points of joint aperture and stress present an obvious non-linear relationship rather than a simple linear correlation. In general, the increase of joint stress can promote the expansion of joint aperture, but this effect is only effective within a reasonable range. For the smooth propagation of hydraulic fractures and the effective expansion of joints, the injection pressure of fracturing fluid should be controlled within an appropriate interval to avoid joint compression and ensure the extension distance of hydraulic fractures, which is more critical for the construction of efficient heat exchange space.Fig. 23Relationship of stress and aperture of joints.

Relationship of stress and aperture of joints.

The interaction behavior of HFs and joints was illustrated in Fig. 24. Apparently, the joints near an injection wellbore are opened by fluid shock, whereas the joints far from the wellbore could be impacted by the force of rock deformation due to the shock. Therefore, the fluence region of thermal shock the region can be divided into expansion region and compression region. As the joint 4 was compressed after 10 shock times and the aperture of joint 2 and 3 was increased in all the cases, the radius of expansion region and influence region of the thermal shock treatment is around 1 and 2 m, respectively. Between the two regions is compression region. Decreasing fluid viscosity and shock times can shrink the influence range of thermal shock, which is therefore beneficial for HFs to expand the joints adjacent and connect with joints distant. When HFs connect with joints, the behaviors of arrest and/or penetration occurs. The degree of the interaction behavior can result in different stress and temperature distribution on joints. When a HF slightly interact with a joint, the stress distribution could be symmetrical or gradient. No matter penetration or arrest, the high stress area of the joints could appear near the interaction area, which is due to the fluid flow causing inhomogeneity and stress concentration. Furthermore, when a joint connected with HF branches, the middle area between the branches usually presents a high temperature, with an asymmetrical stress distribution that enhances the inhomogeneity of joint aperture. It is also evident that the stress and aperture of joints decrease as the distance between the joints and the wellbore increases.Fig. 24Interaction behaviors of thermal shock induced hydraulic fractures and joints.

Interaction behaviors of thermal shock induced hydraulic fractures and joints.

Conclusions

This paper provides reference for the development of artificial heat storage near injection wells at fracturing initiation stage. Under the consideration of fluid viscosity, thermal shock time, approaching angle and joint sets, we investigated the stimulation effect and mechanism of thermal shock on joint system by LSM, which is meaningful for adopting optimal treatment to increase heat transfer space in HDR reservoirs. The main conclusions are as follows:Based on the above engineering background, low-viscosity fracturing fluid (≈ 3 mPa·s) is suggested to maximize shear stimulation of joint systems. A cycle number of 4–6 thermal shocks is optimal to balance stimulation intensity and avoid joint compaction. Well trajectory and perforation design should aim to achieve nearly orthogonal approaching angles (≈ 90°) between hydraulic fractures and natural joints. Meanwhile, a moderate injection rate (≈ 3 m3/min) is recommended to ensure wellbore stability and sufficient thermal impact during fracturing operations. Thermal shock around the injection wellbore forms a distinct expansion region and a compression region. The expansion region (approximately 1 m in radius) is characterized by joint opening driven by fluid injection, while the outer compression region (total influence radius ≈ 2 m) is dominated by rock deformation that tends to close distant joints.During fracturing initiation stage of thermal shock, fluid viscosity has greatly impact on the morphology of HFs, which should be designed to adjust the expansion region for near-wellbore joints expansion and local stimulation effect. Thermal shock time characterizes with the continuous stimulation effect on joints. The times of thermal shock should be determined because there exist optimal shock times that effectively enhance the self-supporting capacity and prohibit the closure of joints. With an orthogonal approaching angle, HFs are more easily to penetrate joints, which is conducive to the shear stimulation of thermal shock. The injection pressure of fracturing fluid should be optimized to keep joints from being compressive, which is more meaningful to HFs expansion and creation of heat exchange space.Under the scenario of multiple joint sets, HFs are more likely to expand along the joint planes from sets to sets. Especially, the joint set near an injection wellbore is more stimulated than others distant. The stress and aperture of joints decrease as the distance between the joints and the wellbore increases.When HFs interact with joints, the interaction behaviors of HFs and joints mainly consists of arrest and penetration. The interaction degree of those behaviors leads to symmetrical, asymmetrical, and gradient stress distributions on joints, resulting in different stimulation degree of thermal shock. Due to the interaction by HF branches, fluid flow could induce stress concentration at the boundary of area between the branches, therefore increasing the roughness and heterogeneity of joints.

Based on the above engineering background, low-viscosity fracturing fluid (≈ 3 mPa·s) is suggested to maximize shear stimulation of joint systems. A cycle number of 4–6 thermal shocks is optimal to balance stimulation intensity and avoid joint compaction. Well trajectory and perforation design should aim to achieve nearly orthogonal approaching angles (≈ 90°) between hydraulic fractures and natural joints. Meanwhile, a moderate injection rate (≈ 3 m3/min) is recommended to ensure wellbore stability and sufficient thermal impact during fracturing operations. Thermal shock around the injection wellbore forms a distinct expansion region and a compression region. The expansion region (approximately 1 m in radius) is characterized by joint opening driven by fluid injection, while the outer compression region (total influence radius ≈ 2 m) is dominated by rock deformation that tends to close distant joints.

During fracturing initiation stage of thermal shock, fluid viscosity has greatly impact on the morphology of HFs, which should be designed to adjust the expansion region for near-wellbore joints expansion and local stimulation effect. Thermal shock time characterizes with the continuous stimulation effect on joints. The times of thermal shock should be determined because there exist optimal shock times that effectively enhance the self-supporting capacity and prohibit the closure of joints. Under the scenario of multiple joint sets, HFs are more likely to expand along the joint planes from sets to sets. Especially, the joint set near an injection wellbore is more stimulated than others distant. The stress and aperture of joints decrease as the distance between the joints and the wellbore increases.

When HFs interact with joints, the interaction behaviors of HFs and joints mainly consists of arrest and penetration. The interaction degree of those behaviors leads to symmetrical, asymmetrical, and gradient stress distributions on joints, resulting in different stimulation degree of thermal shock. Due to the interaction by HF branches, fluid flow could induce stress concentration at the boundary of area between the branches, therefore increasing the roughness and heterogeneity of joints.

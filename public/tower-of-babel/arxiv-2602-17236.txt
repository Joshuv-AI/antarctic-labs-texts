Dimitrios Ntalampekos

Abstract
. We give a necessary and sufficient condition so that a pair of disjoint Jordan regions in the sphere can be quasiconformally mapped to a pair of disks. As a consequence, we obtain a simple characterization that involves Lipschitz functions for the case that one of the Jordan regions is a half-plane. We apply these results to prove that all polynomial cusps are quasiconformally equivalent and that a quasisymmetric embedding of the union of two disjoint disks extends to a quasiconformal map of the sphere, quantitatively. Also, in combination with previous work of the author, we obtain a new characterization of compact sets that are quasiconformally equivalent to Schottky sets.

Primary 30C62, 30C20; Secondary 30F10, 30F45

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

In this paper we study the question whether two disjoint Jordan regions in the sphere
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
can be mapped to a pair of disjoint disks with a quasiconformal homeomorphism of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. The closures of the regions are allowed to intersect each other. Our goal is to provide a characterization of such regions that is quantitative in nature. Namely the quasiconformal dilatation should depend only on the geometric features of the Jordan regions and not on their relative position.

Ahlfors
[
2
]
provided an excellent and deep characterization of curves that can be mapped to a circle with a quasiconformal homeomorphism of the plane. Specifically, a Jordan curve
J
in the plane can be quasiconformally mapped to a circle if and only if there exists
L\geq 1
such that for every pair of points
z,w\in J
, if
E
is the arc between
z
and
w
with the smallest diameter, then

\diam(E)\leq L|z-w|.

Such a Jordan curve is called an
L
-quasicircle. The region bounded by a quasicircle is called a quasidisk. Geometrically, quasicircles do not have cusps. Moreover, in the theorem of Ahlfors the quasiconformal dilatation and the constant
L
are quantitatively related. See Section
2.4
for further details and definitions. Therefore, if we wish to map quasiconformally a pair of disjoint Jordan regions
U,V
to a pair of disjoint disks, then the boundaries
\partial U,\partial V
must be quasicircles.

It is easy to see that this condition is not sufficient. For example, note that the boundary of each square is a
2
-quasicircle. However, we cannot map a pair of disjoint open squares
U,V
with disjoint closures to a pair of disjoint disks with a
K
-quasiconformal map, where
K
is bounded and does not depend on the relative position of the squares. Specifically, it is observed by the author in
[
32
]
*pp. 174–176 that as the relative distance of the squares, defined by

\Delta(U,V)=\frac{\dist(U,V)}{\min\{\diam(U),\diam(V)\}},

shrinks to
0
, the value of the quasiconformal dilatation
K
must blow up to
\infty
. Therefore, it is not sufficient to assume that
\partial U,\partial V
are uniform quasicircles, but in this case the relative distance also affects the quasiconformal dilatation.

Herron
[
21
]
provided a sufficiency criterion for the quasiconformal transformation of two disjoint Jordan regions
U,V
into two disks with quantitative control. Namely, if
\partial U,\partial V
are
L
-quasicircles and
\Delta(\partial U,\partial V)\geq\delta>0
, then there exists a
K(L,\delta)
-quasiconformal map with the claimed properties. Note that the converse is not true. Indeed, the condition
\Delta(\partial U,\partial V)\geq\delta
is quasiconformally quasi-invariant (see Lemma
2.2
) and two disks of radius
1
that are very close to each other, or even tangent to each other, do not satisfy it.

In a subsequent work Herron and Koskela
[
20
]
proved that if
\Omega
is a uniform domain (see Section
2.5
), then it can be mapped with a quasiconformal map of the sphere to a
circle domain
, i.e., a domain whose boundary components are points or circles. The boundary components of a uniform domain
\Omega
can be infinite in number, but they are all points or uniform quasicircles that are
uniformly relatively separated
; that is, their mutual relative distances are uniformly bounded from below. On the other hand, the definition of a uniform domain involves a technical requirement that is even stronger than these conditions.

Two decades later, Bonk
[
11
]
generalized the aforementioned results, relying on a powerful tool devised by Schramm
[
36
]
, known as transboundary modulus. Bonk proved that if
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a collection of disjoint Jordan regions in the sphere such that
\partial U_{i}
is an
L
-quasicircle for each
i\in I
and
\Delta(\partial U_{i},\partial U_{j})\geq\delta>0
for every
i\neq j
, then there exists a
K(L,\delta)
-quasiconformal map of the sphere that maps each
U_{i}
to a disk. This result implies the theorem of Herron and Koskela and shows that the existence of such a quasiconformal map should rely on the geometry of the regions
U_{i}
,
i\in I
, rather than on the intrinsic geometry of the set
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\bigcup_{i\in I}\overline{U_{i}}
.

Bonk’s result was applied in the problem of quasiconformal uniformization of Sierpiński carpets, in connection with the Kapovich–Kleiner conjecture from geometric group theory, in rigidity results related to Sierpiński carpets
[
9
,
10
]
, and in rigidity problems in complex dynamics
[
8
]
. Moreover, it has been extended to non-planar carpets
[
31
,
35
]
.

Recent progress in uniformization problems in complex dynamics opened the way for uniformization results without the uniform relative separation assumption. Specifically, Luo and the author
[
29
]
characterized, under some mild conditions, Julia sets of rational maps that can be quasiconformally mapped to round gaskets; these are sets whose complementary components are Jordan regions that can potentially touch each other at the boundary. In particular, there is no separation between the Jordan regions. The methods of
[
29
]
were extended in
[
28
]
, where it is shown that certain Basilica Julia sets and limit sets of Kleinian groups can be quasiconformally mapped to round Basilicas.

While these results are purely dynamical and do not extend to arbitrary gaskets, the author
[
33
]
was able to obtain a general characterization of sets in the sphere that can be quasiconformally mapped to
Schottky sets
; that is sets, whose complementary components are disks. The main result in
[
33
]
(restated below in Theorem
1.4
) asserts that if
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a collection of disjoint Jordan regions in the sphere, then there exists a quasiconformal map of the sphere that maps each
U_{i}
to a disk if and only if the collection
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is
uniformly quasiconformally pairwise circularizable
; that is, there exists
K\geq 1
such that for every pair
U_{i},U_{j}
,
i\neq j
, there exists a
K
-quasiconformal map of the sphere that maps
U_{i}
and
U_{j}
to disks. For instance, this condition is satisfied under the assumption
\Delta(\partial U_{i},\partial U_{j})\geq\delta
thanks to the result of Herron
[
21
]
. Therefore, Bonk’s theorem follows as a consequence.

Summarizing, the main result in
[
33
]
allows the quasiconformal uniformization of a collection of disjoint Jordan regions by disks if every pair can be quasiconformally uniformized with controlled dilatation. Therefore, the last missing piece in this puzzle is to characterize pairs of disjoint Jordan regions that can be mapped quasiconformally to disks. This is precisely the main objective of the present work.

1.2.
Main result

Our main result provides a quantitative characterization of pairs of disjoint quasidisks that can be quasiconformally mapped to pairs of disks. There are two fundamental cases that we treat separately: either the boundaries of the two quasidisks intersect at a point or the quasidisks have disjoint closures.

The main results are expressed in terms of the notion of the relative hyperbolic metric corresponding to a pair of quasidisks that we introduce in this paper. If
U\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is an open set we denote by
U^{*}
the complement of
\overline{U}
. If
\partial U
contains at least two points, we denote by
h_{U}
the hyperbolic metric on
U
.

Let
U,V\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be disjoint Jordan regions such that
\overline{U}\cap\overline{V}
is either empty or contains only one point; in particular,
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(\overline{U}\cup\overline{V})
is connected. We define the
relative hyperbolic metric
corresponding to the pair
(V,U)
as

d_{V,U}(z,w)=\inf_{\gamma}\{\ell_{h_{U^{*}}}(\gamma)\},\quad z,w\in\partial V\setminus\overline{U},

where the infimum is taken over rectifiable curves
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to U^{*}\setminus V
such that
\gamma(a)=z
,
\gamma(b)=w
. It turns out that if
V
is a quasidisk, then
d_{V,U}
is a metric on
\partial V\setminus\overline{U}
. See Section
3.1
for details. This metric can be explicitly computed when
U,V
are disjoint half-planes or their boundaries are concentric circles; see Lemma
3.1
. In Section
3.2
we estimate the metric in several other useful cases.

Our first main result provides a characterization in the case that the quasidisks
U,V
have a common point at the boundary. We may assume that this common point of “tangency” is at
\infty
. Thus, in the next statement we use the topology of
\mathbb{C}
and
|\cdot|
denotes the Euclidean metric.

Theorem 1.1

(Tangent quasidisks)
.

Let
U,V\subset\mathbb{C}
be unbounded quasidisks such that
\overline{U}\cap\overline{V}=\emptyset
. There exists a quasiconformal map
f\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
that maps
U
and
V
to half-planes if and only if the identity map

\id\colon(\partial V,d_{V,U})\to(\partial V,|\cdot|)

is quasisymmetric, quantitatively.

The proof is given in Section
3.4
. Next, we give the corresponding result for quasidisks with disjoint closures. Here we use the topology of the sphere and
\chi
denotes the chordal metric.

Theorem 1.2

(Quasiannulus)
.

Let
U,V\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be quasidisks such that
\overline{U}\cap\overline{V}=\emptyset
. There exists a quasiconformal map
f\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
that maps
U
and
V
to disks if and only if the identity map

\id\colon(\partial V,d_{V,U})\to(\partial V,\chi)

is quasi-Möbius, quantitatively.

This result is proved in Section
3.5
. Note that we need to switch here to quasi-Möbius maps, which generalize quasisymmetric maps and were introduced by Väisälä
[
39
]
. The reason is that the existence of a quasiconformal map
f\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
that maps
U
and
V
to disks is a quasiconformal invariant. Yet, quasiconformal maps of the sphere are not quasisymmetric in a quantitative way; in general, they are only quasi-Möbius. This partially justifies the use of quasi-Möbius maps in Theorem
1.2
.

We may combine the two statements into a single one as follows. Again, we use the topology of the sphere and the proof is included in Section
3.5
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
U,V\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be quasidisks such that
\overline{U}\cap\overline{V}
contains at most one point. There exists a quasiconformal map
f\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
that maps
U
and
V
to disks if and only if the identity map

\id\colon(\partial V\setminus\overline{U},d_{V,U})\to(\partial V\setminus\overline{U},\chi)

is quasi-Möbius, quantitatively.

We would like to emphasize the
quantitative
nature of the results. For example, in Theorem
1.3
, if
U,V
are
L
-quasidisks and the displayed identity map is
\eta
-quasi-Möbius, then the quasiconformal map
f
is
K(L,\eta)
-quasiconformal. Conversely, if
f
is
U,V
are
L
-quasidisks and
f
is
K
-quasiconformal, then the displayed identity map is
\eta
-quasi-Möbius, where
\eta
depends only on
K,L
. Note that in the above results we have given preference to the metric
d_{V,U}
defined on
\partial V
. Of course the roles of
U,V
are symmetric so one could instead use the metric
d_{U,V}
on
\partial U
.

we establish the conditions of the above theorems in some special and useful cases. We include illustrations here and defer the precise statements to Section

One can ask whether there exists a simpler condition, than the one in Theorem
1.3
, to characterize pairs of quasicircles that can be quasiconformally mapped to circles. For example, quasicircles can be characterized by means of cross ratios (see (
2.2
)), so it would be ideal to have a similar characterization for pairs. However, it is highly doubtful that such a simple-minded criterion exists. Indeed, in Theorem
1.5
below we give a neat characterization in the case that one of the quasicircles is a line and the other is the graph of a Lipschitz function; yet this characterization is still involved and is quite far from a geometric condition involving cross ratios.

The proofs of the main results rely on several tools from the theory of quasiconformal and quasisymmetric maps, but also from classical conformal and hyperbolic geometry. In addition, we use the notion of uniform domains, and distortion properties of the hyperbolic and quasihyperbolic metrics under quasiconformal maps. All the required preliminaries are included in Seciton
2
.

In order to establish the theorems, we need to show that the condition that

\text{the map}\,\,\,\id\colon(\partial V,d_{V,U})\to(\partial V,\chi)\,\,\,\text{is quasi-M\"{o}bius}

is quasiconformally invariant. The necessity part of the theorems then easily follows from the fact the condition holds in the case that
U,V
are disks. The mentioned quasiconformal invariance follows from some special properties of the relative hyperbolic metric
d_{V,U}
that we establish. One important ingredient is that if
f
is a quasiconformal homeomorphism of the sphere mapping the quasidisks
U,V
to
U^{\prime},V^{\prime}
, then the map

f\colon(\partial V,d_{V,U})\to(\partial V^{\prime},d_{V^{\prime},U^{\prime}})

is quasisymmetric. This is established in Theorem
3.6
. Another important tool that allows us to estimate
d_{V,U}
is that it is quasisymmetrically equivalent to the quasihyperbolic distance in the domain
Z
that is bounded by
\partial U
and some quasiconformal reflection of
\partial U
along
\partial V
; see Corollary

and Figure

.

We now discuss some applications of the main theorems in various problems in geometric function theory.

1.3.
Schottky sets

We can incorporate Theorem
1.3
into the main theorem of
[
33
]
and obtain the following quasiconformal characterization of Schottky sets.

Theorem 1.4

(Schottky sets)
.

Let
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a collection of at least two disjoint Jordan regions in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\overline{U_{i}}\cap\overline{U_{j}}
contains at most one point for
i\neq j
. The following are quantitatively equivalent.

(i)

For each pair of distinct
i,j\in I
the identity map

\id\colon(\partial U_{i}\setminus\overline{U_{j}},d_{U_{i},U_{j}})\to(\partial U_{i}\setminus\overline{U_{j}},\chi)

is uniformly quasi-Möbius.

(ii)

For each
i,j\in I
there exists a uniformly quasiconformal homeomorphism of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
that maps
U_{i}
and
U_{j}
to disks.

(iii)

There exists a quasiconformal homeomorphism
f
of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
that maps
U_{i}
to a disk for each
i\in I
and is
1
-quasiconformal on
S=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\bigcup_{i\in I}U_{i}
.

In this case, the map
f|_{S}
is unique up to postcomposition with a conformal automorphism of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.

1.4.
Graphs and cusps

We apply Theorem
1.1
to characterize graphs of Lipschitz functions and quasicircles in the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}
that can be mapped to a line with a quasiconformal homeomorphism of
\mathbb{H}
. The proofs of the results in this section are given in Section
4
. Remarkably, the next two results convey an interesting interplay between Lipschitz and quasiconformal geometry.

Theorem 1.5

(Graphs)
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
be a Lipschitz function. There exists a quasiconformal homeomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
that preserves the real line and maps the graph of
f
onto a line and only if an antiderivative of
1/f
is quasisymmetric. The statement is quantitative.

For example, the graph of the function
f(x)=(|x|+1)^{-p}
,
p>-1
, and the line
y=0
can be quasiconformally mapped to a pair of parallel lines. On the other hand, this is impossible for
f(x)=e^{-|x|}
. See Example
4.2
for details. Next, we provide a practical characterization of unbounded Jordan curves in the upper half-plane that can be mapped to a line with a quasiconformal map of that preserves the real axis. Here an
unbounded Jordan curve
is a set
J\subset\mathbb{C}
such that
J\cup\{\infty\}
is a Jordan curve in the topology of the sphere. Each complementary component of an unbounded Jordan curve is an
unbounded Jordan region
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{H}
be an unbounded Jordan region. There exists a quasiconformal homeomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
that preserves the real line and maps
\partial U
onto a line if and only if

(1)

U
is a quasidisk,

(2)

there exists a
1
-Lipschitz function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
and
a\geq 1
such that
\partial U\subset\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:a^{-1}f(x)\leq y\leq af(x)\}
, and

(3)

an antiderivative of
1/f
is quasisymmetric.

The statement is quantitative.

The proof relies on Theorem
1.1
is one of the most technically involved arguments in the paper.

As an application of Theorem
1.5
, we prove the quasiconformal equivalence of
polynomial cusps

C_{\alpha}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|y|=x^{\alpha},\,0\leq x\leq 1\}
, where
\alpha>1
.

Corollary 1.7

(Cusps)
.

All polynomial cusps are quasiconformally equivalent.

It is plausible that an explicit quasiconformal map can be found. A recent related result of Chrontsios–Tyson
[
13
]
provides a quasiconformal classification of
polynomial spirals

S_{\alpha}=\{x^{-\alpha}e^{ix}:x>0\}
,
\alpha>0
. Specifically, it is shown that for
\alpha>\beta>0
,
S_{\alpha}
can be mapped to
S_{\beta}
with a
K
-quasiconformal map if and only if
K\geq\alpha/\beta
. It would be interesting to prove an analogous result for cusps.

Also, it is worth mentioning that a cusp cannot be straightened to a line segment with a quasiconformal map of
\mathbb{C}
, since such maps quasi-preserve angles. Yet, it can be straightened with
mappings with finite distortion
; this class generalizes quasiconformal maps, which have bounded distortion instead. This problem is studied in the series of papers
[
24
,
37
,
26
,
25
,
18
,
22
]
.

1.5.
The quasiconformal extension problem

We present another application of Theorem
1.3
in the problem of quasiconformally extending quasisymmetric or quasi-Möbius maps, quantitatively. It is a classical fact, due to Ahlfors and Beurling
[
7
,
2
]
, that every quasisymmetric embedding of the unit circle into the plane extends to a quasiconformal homeomorphism of the plane, quantitatively (see Corollary
2.9
). More generally, the same is true for every quasisymmetric embedding of a quasicircle. For which other classes of sets is such a result true?

The question has been studied by Vellis
[
41
]
in the case of relatively connected compact sets whose complementary domains are uniform; we direct the reader to the referenced paper for definitions. Thus, a satisfactory resolution to the extension problem in the case of sets with controlled geometry has been provided. For example, every quasisymmetric embedding of the middle-thirds Cantor set extends to a quasiconformal homeomorphism of the plane, quantitatively
[
30
]
.

As an application of the main theorems, we resolve the extension problem for embeddings of pairs of circles. Note that the complementary domain of two tangent disks is not uniform. Also, if two disks have disjoint closures, the complementary domain is uniform, but the uniformity constant degenerates as the disks tend to be tangent to each other. Hence, the previous results do not apply in these cases.

Theorem 1.8

(Tangent disks)
.

Let
U,V\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be disjoint tangent disks and
f\colon\partial U\cup\partial V\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be a quasi-Möbius embedding. Then there exists an extension of
f
to a (possibly orientation-reversing) quasiconformal homeomorphism of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, quantitatively.

Note that we do not impose the assumption that
f
admits an extension to a homeomorphism of the sphere; this follows from the assumptions that
f
is quasi-Möbius and
\partial U\cap\partial V\neq\emptyset
. However, in the next result it is obviously necessary to impose the existence of a homeomorphic extension.

Theorem 1.9

(Annulus)
.

Let
U,V\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be disks with disjoint closures and
f\colon\partial U\cup\partial V\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be a quasi-Möbius embedding that extends to an orientation-preserving homeomorphism of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. Denote by
\Gamma
(resp.
\Gamma^{\prime}
) the family of curves separating
\partial U
and
\partial V
(resp.
f(\partial U)
and
f(\partial V)
). Suppose that one of the following conditions holds.

(1)

\displaystyle K_{0}^{-1}\leq\frac{\Mod\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Mod\Gamma}\leq K_{0}
for some
K_{0}\geq 1
.

(2)

\displaystyle\Mod\Gamma\leq M
for some
M>0
.

Then there exists an extension of
f
to a quasiconformal homeomorphism of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, quantitatively.

Since quasiconformal maps quasi-preserve modulus, it is immediate that the modulus bound in
(1)
is a necessary condition for an extension with quantitative control. When
\Mod\Gamma
is small, as in
(2)
, condition
(1)
follows from the assumption that the embedding
f
is quasi-Möbius. In general,
(1)
does not follow from the assumption that
f
is quasi-Möbius and cannot be dropped. Indeed, consider the map
f
that is the identity map on the unit circle
\mathbb{S}(0,1)
and scales the circle
\mathbb{S}(0,e^{-n})
to
\mathbb{S}(0,\frac{1}{n})
, where
n\geq 2
. This map is
\eta
-quasisymmetric and
\eta
-quasi-Möbius (see property
(Q-3)
below) for some distortion function
\eta
that does not depend on
n
. However,
(1)
is not true with a constant
K_{0}
independent of
n
.

Theorem
1.8
and Theorem
1.9
are proved in Section
6
and are based on several quasiconformal extension results from Section
5
. For example we prove that a quasisymmetric map between two pairs of parallel lines or two pairs of concentric circles extends to a quasiconformal map of the plane. While results in this spirit seem very natural and have been extensively used in complex dynamics
[
12
]
*Section 2.3, we have not been able to locate in the literature
quantitative proofs
. Namely, the quasiconformal dilatation of the extension in Theorem
1.8
depends only on the distortion function of the quasi-Möbius embedding
f
. Even more delicate is the proof of Theorem
1.9
, where the dilatation depends also on the constant appearing in condition
(1)
or
(2)
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

If
a
is a parameter we use the notation
C(a),L(a)
, etc. for positive constants that depend only on the parameter
a
. For quantities
A
and
B
we write
A\lesssim B
if there exists a constant
c>0
such that
A\leq cB
. If the constant
c
depends on another quantity
H
that we wish to emphasize, then we write instead
A\leq c(H)B
or
A\lesssim_{H}B
. Moreover, we use the notation
A\simeq B
if
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
. As previously, we write
A\simeq_{H}B
to emphasize the dependence of the implicit constants on the quantity
H
. All constants in the statements are assumed to be positive even if this is not stated explicitly and the same letter may be used in different statements to denote a different constant. We say that a statement is
quantitative
if the constants or parameters appearing in the conclusions depend only on the constants or parameters in the assumptions.

The chordal metric on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is defined for
z\in\mathbb{C}
by

\chi(z,w)=\frac{2|z-w|}{\sqrt{1+|z|^{2}}\sqrt{1+|w|^{2}}}\,\,\,\text{when $w\in\mathbb{C}$ and}\,\,\,\chi(z,\infty)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{1+|z|^{2}}}.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. The open ball of radius
r>0
centered at a point
x\in X
is denoted by
B_{d}(x,r)
and the closed ball is denoted by
\overline{B}_{d}(x,r)
. The diameter of a set
E\subset X
is denoted by
\diam(E)
or
\diam_{d}(E)
if the metric needs to be emphasized. The length of a curve
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to X
is denoted by
\ell_{d}(\gamma)
. The trace
\gamma
is the set
|\gamma|=\gamma([a,b])
. For the Euclidean metric in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
we use the subscript
e
when necessary. For example,
B_{e}(z,r)=\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|z-w|<r\}
. Moreover, especially for the Euclidean metric in the complex plane we also use the notation
\mathbb{D}(z,r)
for the ball
B_{e}(z,r)
,
\mathbb{S}(z,r)
for the circle
\partial B_{e}(z,r)
, and
\mathbb{A}(z;r,R)
for the annulus
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:r<|z-w|<R\}
, where
R>r>0
.

2.2.
Quasiconformal maps

Let
K\geq 1
. An orientation-preserving homeomorphism
f\colon U\to V
between open subsets of
\mathbb{C}
is
quasiconformal
if
f
lies in the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}_{\loc}(U)
and

\|Df(z)\|^{2}\leq KJ_{f}(z)

for a.e.
z\in\mathbb{C}
; here
\|\cdot\|
denotes the operator norm of the matrix
Df
and
J_{f}
is its Jacobian determinant. In this case we say that
f
is
K
-quasiconformal. A homeomorphism
f\colon U\to V
between open subsets of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is quasiconformal if
f|_{U\setminus\{\infty,f^{-1}(\infty)\}}
is quasiconformal in the above sense. We will freely use the fundamental facts that the inverse of a quasiconformal map is quasiconformal, that the composition of two quasiconformal maps is quasiconformal, and that
1
-quasiconformal maps are conformal. We direct the reader to
[
27
]
for further background and to
[
38
]
for the theory of quasiconformal maps in higher dimensions.

Unless otherwise specified, quasiconformal are considered to be orientation-preserving, as in the above definition. We also define
orientation-reversing quasiconformal maps
in the obvious manner.

We will use the notion of modulus of a curve family
\Gamma
, which we denote by
\Mod\Gamma
; see
[
27
]
*Chapter I for the definition and details. We record here some fundamental facts. Let
\Gamma
be the family of curves separating the boundary components of an annulus
\mathbb{A}(z;r,R)
, where
0<r<R<\infty
. We have

\Mod\Gamma=\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\left(\frac{R}{r}\right).

We note that modulus is conformally invariant and quasiconformally quasi-invariant. That is, if
f\colon U\to V
is a
K
-quasiconformal map between open subsets of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
and
\Gamma
is a family of curves in
U
, then

K^{-1}\Mod\Gamma\leq\Mod f(\Gamma)\leq K\Mod\Gamma.

2.3.
Quasi-Möbius and quasisymmetric maps

Let
(X,d_{X})
be a metric space. We define the cross ratio of a quadruple of distinct points
a,b,c,d\in X
to be

[a,b,c,d]=\frac{d_{X}(a,c)d_{X}(b,d)}{d_{X}(a,d)d_{X}(b,c)}.

Observe that if
X=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, equipped with the chordal metric, and
a,b,c,d\neq\infty
, then

[a,b,c,d]=\frac{|a-c||b-d|}{|a-d||b-c|}.

The factors containing the point
\infty
are omitted. For example,

[a,b,c,\infty]=\frac{|a-c|}{|b-c|}.

A homeomorphism
\eta\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is called a
distortion function
. A homeomorphism
f\colon(X,d_{X})\to(Y,d_{Y})
between metric spaces is
quasi-Möbius
if there exists a distortion function
\eta
such that

[f(a),f(b),f(c),f(d)]\leq\eta([a,b,c,d])

for every quadruple of distinct points
a,b,c,d\in X
. In this case, we say that
f
is
\eta
-quasi-Möbius. Suppose that
X,Y\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
and
f\colon X\to Y
is
\eta
-quasi-Möbius. Then the cross ratios in
X
and
Y
may be computed with the chordal or the Euclidean distance interchangeably (in case the points involved are different from
\infty
). Thus, we do not need to specify which of the two metrics is used in
X
and
Y
. Also, since cross ratios are invariant under Möbius transformations, the composition of
f
with a Möbius transformation is
\eta
-quasi-Möbius as well.

A homeomorphism
f\colon(X,d_{X})\to(Y,d_{Y})
between metric spaces is
quasisymmetric
if there exists a distortion function
\eta
such that

\frac{d_{Y}(f(a),f(b))}{d_{Y}(f(a),f(c))}\leq\eta\left(\frac{d_{X}(a,b)}{d_{X}(a,c)}\right)

for every triple of distinct points
a,b,c\in X
. In this case we say that
f
is
\eta
-quasisymmetric. An elementary consequence of the definition (see
[
19
]
*Proposition 10.8) is that if
A\subset B\subset X
and
0<\diam(A)\leq\diam(B)<\infty
, then
\diam(f(B))<\infty
and

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\eta\left(\frac{\diam(B)}{\diam(A)}\right)}\leq\frac{\diam(f(A))}{\diam(f(B))}\leq\eta\left(\frac{2\diam(A)}{\diam(B)}\right).

A homeomorphism
f\colon(X,d_{X})\to(Y,d_{Y})
between metric spaces is
bi-Lipschitz
if there exists
L\geq 1
such that for all
x,y\in X
we have

L^{-1}d_{X}(x,y)\leq d_{Y}(f(x),f(y))\leq Ld_{X}(x,y).

In this case we say that
f
is
L
-bi-Lipschitz.

We list some fundamental properties of the above types of maps.

(Q-1)

If
f
is
\eta
-quasisymmetric (resp. quasi-Möbius), then
f^{-1}
is
\eta^{\prime}
-quasisymmetric (resp. quasi-Möbius) for
\eta^{\prime}(t)=1/\eta^{-1}(t^{-1})
,
t>0
. Also, if
f
is
\eta_{1}
-quasisymmetric (resp. quasi-Möbius) and
g
is
\eta_{2}
-quasisymmetric (resp. quasi-Möbius), then
g\circ f
is
(\eta_{2}\circ\eta_{1})
-quasisymmetric (resp. quasi-Möbius).

(Q-2)

Suppose that
X,Y\subset\mathbb{C}
(resp. are bounded) and
f\colon X\to Y
is
\eta
-quasisymmetric (resp.
\eta
-quasi-Möbius). Then
f
extends to an
\eta
-quasisymmetric (resp.
\eta
-quasi-Möbius) homeomorphism from
\overline{X}
onto
\overline{Y}
.

(Q-3)

Quasisymmetric maps are quasi-Möbius, quantitatively
[
39
]
*Theorem 3.2. Conversely, quasi-Möbius maps between bounded metric spaces are quasisymmetric

39
]
*Theorem 3.12. Specifically, if

is quasi-Möbius and there exists
\lambda\geq 1
and points

with

d_{X}(x_{i},x_{j})\geq\lambda^{-1}\diam(X)\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,d_{Y}(f(x_{i}),f(x_{j}))\geq\lambda^{-1}\diam(Y)\,\,\,\text{for $i\neq j$},

then

is quasisymmetric, quantitatively.

(Q-4)

Quasi-Möbius maps between open subsets of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
are quasiconformal, quantitatively
[
39
]
*Theorem 5.2. Conversely, quasiconformal self-homeomorphisms of the disk

or of the sphere

are quasi-Möbius, quantitatively
[

*Theorem 5.4.

(Q-5)

Suppose that
X
is an unbounded metric space,
Y
is a metric space and
f\colon X\to Y
is
\eta
-quasi-Möbius. If
f(x)\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
, then
f
is
\eta
-quasisymmetric
[

]
*Theorem 3.10.

(Q-6)

Let
f\colon U\to V
be a
K
-quasiconformal map between open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For each
a>1
there exists a distortion function
\eta
, depending only on
n,K
, and

B_{e}(z,r)\subset B_{e}(z,ar)\subset U
, then
f
is
\eta
-quasisymmetric in
B_{e}(z,r)

[
19
]
*Theorem 11.4.

(Q-7)

If
f\colon X\to Y
is an
L
-bi-Lipschitz map between metric spaces, then

is
\eta
-quasisymmetric for
\eta(t)=L^{2}t
. If

is
L^{2}
-quasiconformal

A
continuum

E
is a compact and connected metric space. If
E
contains more than one points, we say that
E
is a
non-degenerate continuum
. We define the
relative distance
of two disjoint compact subsets
E,F
of a metric space that contain more than one points as

\Delta(E,F)=\frac{\dist(E,F)}{\min\{\diam(E),\diam(F)\}}.

As before we use the notation
\Delta_{d}(E,F)
to emphasize the metric that we use.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f\colon(X,d_{X})\to(Y,d_{Y})
be a quasisymmetric homeomorphism between metric spaces. Let
E,F\subset X
be sets with positive diameters. Then

\frac{\dist(f(E),f(F))}{\diam(f(E))}\leq\eta\left(2\frac{\dist(E,F)}{\diam(E)}\right).

Proof.

Let
x\in E
,
z\in F
be arbitrary points and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a point
y\in E
such that
d(x,y)\geq\frac{1}{2+\varepsilon}\diam(E)
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{\dist(f(E),f(F))}{\diam(f(E))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d(f(x),f(z))}{d(f(x),f(y))}\leq\eta\left(\frac{d(x,z)}{d(x,y)}\right)\leq\eta\left((2+\varepsilon)\frac{d(x,z)}{\diam(E)}\right).

Infimizing over
x,z
and letting
\varepsilon\to 0
gives the conclusion.
∎

Lemma 2.2

(
[
33
]
*Lemma 2.4)
.

Let
f\colon(X,d_{X})\to(Y,d_{Y})
be an
\eta
-quasi-Möbius homeomorphism between metric spaces. Then there exists a distortion function
\widetilde{\eta}
that depends only on
\eta
such that for every pair of disjoint non-degenerate continua
E,F\subset X
we have

\Delta(f(E),f(F))\leq\widetilde{\eta}(\Delta(E,F)).

Remark 2.3
.

We record a simple consequence of Lemma
2.2
. The identity map
\id\colon(\mathbb{C},\chi)\to(\mathbb{C},|\cdot|)
preserves cross ratios so it is
\eta
-quasi-Möbius for
\eta(t)=t
. If
E,F\subset\mathbb{C}
,
a>0
, and
\Delta_{\chi}(E,F)\leq a
(resp.
\geq a
), then Lemma
2.2
implies that
\Delta_{e}(E,F)\leq\widetilde{\eta}(a)
(resp.
\geq(\widetilde{\eta})^{-1}(a)
). Conversely, if
\Delta_{e}(E,F)
is bounded above or below, one obtains bounds for
\Delta_{\chi}(E,F)
.

2.4.
Quasidisks and quasicircles

A set
U\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is a
quasidisk
if it is the image of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
(or equivalently of any other disk) under a quasiconformal homeomorphism
f\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. If
f
is
K
-quasiconformal for some
K\geq 1
, we say that
U
is a
K
-quasidisk. Note that the complement of a quasidisk is also a quasidisk. We say that a set
U\subset\mathbb{C}
is an
unbounded quasidisk
if
U
is unbounded as a subset of
\mathbb{C}
and it is a quasidisk as a subset of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.

Let
J\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be a Jordan curve. We say that
J
is a
quasicircle
if there exists a constant
L\geq 1
such that for every pair of points
z,w\in J
there exists an arc
E\subset J
with endpoints
z,w
such that

\diam_{\chi}(E)\leq L\chi(z,w).

In this case we say that
J
is an
L
-quasicircle. We note that
J
is a quasicircle if and only if there exists a constant
L^{\prime}\geq 1
such that for every pair of points
z,w\in J\setminus\{\infty\}
there exists an arc
E\subset J\setminus\{\infty\}
with endpoints
z,w
such that

\diam_{e}(E)\leq L^{\prime}|z-w|.

This equivalence is quantitative and follows from
[
11
]
*Proposition 4.4; in fact it is shown that both of the above conditions are quantitatively equivalent to the condition that there exists
\delta>0
such that for each quadruple of distinct points
a,b,c,d\in J
in cyclic order we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle[a,b,c,d]\geq\delta>0.

If
J
is a quasicircle and
\infty\in J
, we call the set
J\setminus\{\infty\}\subset\mathbb{C}
an
unbounded quasicircle
. The next result is elementary and follows from the argument in the proof of
[
33
]
*Lemma 3.2.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
J
be an unbounded quasicircle that is contained in a strip
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:a<\im(z)<b\}
. Then
J
separates the boundary components of the strip.

The following deep result of Ahlfors provides the link between quasidisks and quasicircles.

Theorem 2.5

(Ahlfors
[
2
]
)
.

Let
U\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be a Jordan region and
J=\partial U
. Then
U
is a quasidisk if and only if
J
is a quasicircle, quantitatively.

A proof can be found in
[
3
]
*Section IV.D or
[
27
]
*Theorem II.8.6 or
[
34
]
*Section 5. Quasicircles are quasiconformally removable in the sense that if a homeomorphism of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is quasiconformal in the complement of a quasicircle
J
then it is quasiconformal on all of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
; see
[
27
]
*Theorem I.8.3.

A
quasiconformal reflection
along a Jordan curve
J\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is an orientation-reversing quasiconformal map
f\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
such that
f(z)=z
for each
z\in J
and
f
interchanges the complementary components of
J
. Each quasidisk admits a quasiconformal reflection; see the discussion in
[
3
]
*Section IV.D.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
U,V\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be quasidisks and
f\colon U\to V
be a quasiconformal homeomorphism. Then there exists an extension of
f
to a quasiconformal homeomorphism of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, quantitatively.

Proof.

The map
f
extends to a homeomorphism of the closures
\overline{U},\overline{V}
; see
[
27
]
*Theorem I.8.2. The quasidisks
U,V
admit quasiconformal reflections
g,h
, respectively. The map
F
defined by
f
on
\overline{U}
and by
h\circ f\circ g
on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\overline{U}
is a homeomorphism of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
that is quasiconformal in
U
and in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\overline{U}
. The quasiconformal removability of quasicircles implies that
F
is quasiconformal on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.
∎

We recall the celebrated Beurling–Ahlfors extension theorem.

Theorem 2.7

(Beurling–Ahlfors
[
7
]
)
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be an increasing quasisymmetric homeomorphism. Then there exists an extension of
f
to a quasiconformal homeomorphism of the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}
, quantitatively.

This result readily yields an extension result for quasisymmetric maps of the unit circle
\partial\mathbb{D}
.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
f\colon\partial\mathbb{D}\to\partial\mathbb{D}
be an orientation-preserving homeomorphism.

(1)

If
f
is quasi-Möbius, then there exists an extension of
f
to a quasiconformal homeomorphism of
\mathbb{D}
, quantitatively.

(2)

If
f
quasisymmetric, then there exists an extension of
f
to a quasisymmetric homeomorphism of
\mathbb{D}
that fixes the point
0
, quantitatively.

Proof.

For
(1)
suppose that
f
is
\eta
-quasi-Möbius. By composing
f
with a rotation, we may assume that it fixes the point
1
. Consider a Möbius transformation
\phi\colon\overline{\mathbb{D}}\to\overline{\mathbb{H}}\cup\{\infty\}
that maps
1
to
\infty
and
\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus\{1\}
onto
\mathbb{R}
. The map
\phi\circ f\circ\phi^{-1}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is an increasing
\eta
-quasi-Möbius homeomorphism. In fact, it is
\eta
-quasisymmetric by property
(Q-5)
. Therefore, by Theorem
2.7
this map extends to a quasiconformal homeomorphism
F
of
\mathbb{H}
, quantitatively. The map
G=\phi^{-1}\circ F\circ\phi
is the quasiconformal extension requested in part
(1)
.

If
f\colon\partial\mathbb{D}\to\partial\mathbb{D}
is
\eta
-quasisymmetric as in
(2)
, then for the points
z_{1}=1
,
z_{2}=i
,
z_{3}=-1
we have
|f(z_{i})-f(z_{j})|\simeq_{\eta}1
for
i\neq j
by (
2.1
). Consider the extension
G
given by part
(1)
. By
(Q-4)
and
(Q-2)
the map
G
is quasi-Möbius in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Therefore, by property
(Q-3)
the extension
G
is quasisymmetric, quantitatively. Let
A\subset\mathbb{D}
be the radial segment from

to
\partial\mathbb{D}
. Since

\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{\diam(G^{-1}(A))}{\diam(\mathbb{D})}\leq 1,

by (

) we conclude that
\diam(A)\simeq_{\eta}1
. Thus,
|G(0)|\leq r(\eta)<1
. We may now postcompose
G
with an
L(\eta)
-bi-Lipschitz map of
\mathbb{C}
that maps
G(0)
to
0
and is the identity map for

. By property

, the composition remains quasisymmetric, quantitatively.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
f\colon A\to\mathbb{C}
be a quasisymmetric embedding, where either

or

We prove the result in the case that

. Let

that maps

2.5.
Uniform domains

A set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
quasiconvex
if there exists
L\geq 1
such that for every
z,w\in A
there exists a curve
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to A
with
\gamma(a)=z
,
\gamma(b)=w
, and
\ell_{e}(\gamma)\leq L|z-w|
. In this case we say that
A
is
L
-quasiconvex. By the lower semicontinuity of length, observe that
\overline{A}
is also
L
-quasiconvex.

A domain
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
uniform
if there exists a constant
L\geq 1
such that for every pair of points
z,w\in U
there exists a curve
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to U
with
\gamma(a)=z
,
\gamma(b)=w
,
\ell_{e}(\gamma)\leq L|z-w|
, and

\min\{\ell_{e}(\gamma|_{[a,t]}),\ell_{e}(\gamma|_{[t,b]})\}\leq L\dist_{e}(\gamma(t),\partial U)\,\,\,\text{for each $t\in[a,b]$}.

In this case we say that
U
is
L
-uniform. By definition, uniform domains are quasiconvex. This is the only property that we will need in this work.

The space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, each ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and the exterior of each ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are uniform domains. It is a consequence of
[
16
]
*Corollary 3 uniformity is preserved under quasiconformal maps of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In particular, each quasidisk in
\mathbb{C}
and its exterior are uniform domains. More generally, the following result is true.

Theorem 2.10

(
[
39
]
*Theorem 5.6)
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a uniform domain and
f\colon U\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a quasi-Möbius embedding. Then
f(U)
is a uniform domain, quantitatively.

A consequence of the quasiconvexity of quasidisks is that any point
z
in the boundary of a quasidisk
U
is
rectifiably accessible
; that is, there exists a rectifiable curve
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to\overline{U}
such that
\gamma([a,b))\subset U
and
\gamma(b)=z
.

Corollary 2.11
.

Each point in the boundary of a quasidisk is rectifiably accessible.

2.6.
Quasihyperbolic metric

Let
U\subsetneq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain. We define the
quasihyperbolic metric
on
U
as

k_{U}(z,w)=\inf_{\gamma}\int_{\gamma}\frac{1}{\dist_{e}(\cdot,\partial U)}\,ds,

where the infimum is over all rectifiable curves
\gamma
in
U
with endpoints
z,w
. Each pair
z,w\in U
can be joined with a
quasihyperbolic geodesic

\gamma
in
U
, that is,

k_{U}(z,w)=\int_{\gamma}\frac{1}{\dist_{e}(,\partial U)}\,ds=\ell_{k_{U}}(\gamma).

This implies that
(U,k_{U})
is
1
-quasiconvex; see
[
16
]
*Lemma 1. The next lemma gives a convenient estimate of the quasihyperbolic metric.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
K\geq 1
. Let
U\subsetneq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain and
B\subset U
be a
K
-quasiconvex set such that
\diam_{e}(B)\leq K{\dist_{e}(B,\partial U)}
. Then for every
z,w\in B
we have

C(K)^{-1}\frac{|z-w|}{\dist_{e}(B,\partial U)}\leq k_{U}(z,w)\leq C(K)\frac{|z-w|}{\dist_{e}(B,\partial U)}.

Proof.

Let
z,w\in B
. By the quasiconvexity assumption, there exists a curve
\gamma
in
B
with endpoints
z,w
such that
\ell_{e}(\gamma)\leq K|z-w|
. Thus,

k_{U}(z,w)\leq\int_{\gamma}\frac{1}{\dist_{e}(\cdot,\partial U)}\,ds\leq\frac{\ell_{e}(\gamma)}{\dist_{e}(B,\partial U)}\leq K\frac{|z-w|}{\dist_{e}(B,\partial U)}.

For the reverse inequality, consider the ball
B_{e}(z,R)
, where
R=2^{-1}\dist_{e}(B,\partial U)
. Let
\gamma
be a curve in
U
that connects
z
and
w
. If
\gamma
is not contained in
B(z,R)
, then

\int_{\gamma}\frac{1}{\dist_{e}(\cdot,\partial U)}\,ds\geq\frac{R}{\dist_{e}(z,\partial U)+R}\geq\frac{R}{\diam_{e}(B)+\dist_{e}(B,\partial U)+R}\geq\frac{1}{2K+3}.

On the other hand,

\frac{|z-w|}{\dist_{e}(B,\partial U)}\leq\frac{\diam_{e}(B)}{\dist_{e}(B,\partial U)}\leq K

so we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}\frac{1}{\dist(\cdot,\partial U)}\,ds\geq C(K)^{-1}\frac{|z-w|}{\dist_{e}(B,\partial U)}.

Now, suppose that
\gamma
is contained in
B_{e}(z,R)
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}\frac{1}{\dist_{e}(\cdot,\partial U)}\,ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|z-w|}{\dist_{e}(z,\partial U)+R}\geq\frac{|z-w|}{\diam_{e}(B)+\dist_{e}(B,\partial U)+R}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2|z-w|}{(2K+3)\dist_{e}(B,\partial U)}.

Therefore, (
2.3
) holds in this case too with a different constant. Infimizing over all curves
\gamma
connecting
z
and
w
gives the desired inequality.
∎

Gehring and Osgood
[
16
]
*Theorem 3 studied the distortion of the quasihyperbolic metric under quasiconformal maps.

Theorem 2.13

(Gehring–Osgood)
.

Let
K\geq 1
. Let
f\colon U\to V
be a
K
-quasiconformal homeomorphism between domains
U,V\subsetneq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
\alpha=K^{1/(1-n)}
, we have

k_{V}(f(z),f(w))\leq C(n,K)\max\{k_{U}(z,w),k_{U}(z,w)^{\alpha}\}\,\,\,\text{for all $z,w\in U$}.

Combining the above theorem with the fact that the inverse of a quasiconformal map is quasiconformal, we conclude that in large scale the map
f
is bi-Lipschitz in the respective quasihyperbolic metrics. We prove that if one takes into account small scales as well, then
f
is quasisymmetric in the quasihyperbolic metrics.

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
K\geq 1
. Let
f\colon U\to V
be a
K
-quasiconformal homeomorphism between domains
U,V\subsetneq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then the map

f\colon(U,k_{U})\to(V,k_{V})

is quasisymmetric, quantitatively, depending only on
n,K
.

This result generalizes
[
1
]
*Theorem 1.3 and
[
15
]
*Theorem 1.2, where additional assumptions are imposed on the domain
U
.

Proof.

Suppose that
f
is
K
-quasiconformal for some
K\geq 1
. A result of Väisälä
[
40
]
*Theorem 6.6 implies that every weakly quasisymmetric map between quasiconvex spaces is quasisymmetric. Therefore, it suffices to show that the map
f\colon(U,k_{U})\to(V,k_{V})
is
weakly quasisymmetric
; that is, there exists a constant
H=H(n,K)>0
such that for every triple of points
z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}\in U
with
k_{U}(z_{1},z_{2})\leq k_{U}(z_{1},z_{3})
we have

\displaystyle k_{V}(z_{1}^{\prime},z_{2}^{\prime})\leq Hk_{V}(z_{1}^{\prime},z_{3}^{\prime}),

where we use the notation
z^{\prime}=f(z)
for
z\in U
.

Suppose that
k_{U}(z_{1},z_{2})\leq k_{U}(z_{1},z_{3})
. If
k_{U}(z_{1},z_{2})\geq 1/3
, then
k_{U}(z_{1},z_{3})\geq 1/3
and by Theorem
2.13
(applied to
f^{-1}
) we have

k_{V}(z_{1}^{\prime},z_{2}^{\prime})\gtrsim_{n,K}1\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,k_{V}(z_{1}^{\prime},z_{3}^{\prime})\gtrsim_{n,K}1.

Thus, by Theorem
2.13
we conclude that

k_{V}(z_{1}^{\prime},z_{2}^{\prime})\simeq_{n,K}k_{U}(z_{1},z_{2})\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,k_{V}(z_{1}^{\prime},z_{3}^{\prime})\simeq_{n,K}k_{U}(z_{1},z_{3}).

Therefore,
k_{V}(z_{1}^{\prime},z_{2}^{\prime})\lesssim_{n,K}k_{V}(z_{1}^{\prime},z_{3}^{\prime})
. Next, suppose that
k_{U}(z_{1},z_{2})<1/3\leq k_{U}(z_{1},z_{3})
. By Theorem
2.13
we obtain
k_{V}(z_{1}^{\prime},z_{2}^{\prime})\lesssim_{n,K}1
and as above we have
k_{V}(z_{1}^{\prime},z_{3}^{\prime})\gtrsim_{n,K}1
. Therefore,
k_{V}(z_{1}^{\prime},z_{2}^{\prime})\lesssim_{n,K}k_{V}(z_{1}^{\prime},z_{3}^{\prime})
.

Finally, we suppose that
k_{U}(z_{1},z_{2})\leq k_{U}(z_{1},z_{3})<1/3
and let
R=2^{-1}\cdot\dist_{e}(z_{1},\partial U)
. If
z\in U
and
|z-z_{1}|\geq R
, then for each curve
\gamma
in
U
that connects
z_{1},z
we have

\int_{\gamma}\frac{1}{\dist_{e}(\cdot,\partial U)}\,ds\geq\frac{R}{\dist_{e}(z_{1},\partial U)+R}=\frac{1}{3}

so
k_{U}(z_{1},z)\geq 1/3
. We conclude that
z_{2},z_{3}\in B\coloneqq B_{e}(z_{1},R)
. By Lemma
2.12
,

k_{U}(z_{1},z_{i})\simeq\frac{|z_{1}-z_{i}|}{\dist_{e}(B,\partial U)}

for
i=2,3
. We conclude that
|z_{1}-z_{2}|\lesssim|z_{1}-z_{3}|
.

By property
(Q-6)
, the map
f|_{B}
is quasisymmetric in the Euclidean metric, quantitatively, depending on
n,K
. In particular,
|z_{1}^{\prime}-z_{2}^{\prime}|\lesssim_{n,K}|z_{1}^{\prime}-z_{3}^{\prime}|
. Moreover, the image
B^{\prime}=f(B)
is a uniform domain by Theorem
2.10
, so it is quasiconvex, quantitatively. Finally, we have
\diam_{e}(B^{\prime})\lesssim_{n,K}\dist_{e}(B^{\prime},\partial V)
; see
[
19
]
*Exercise 11.18 or
[
17
]
*Theorem 11. By Lemma
2.12
, we have

k_{V}(z_{1}^{\prime},z_{i}^{\prime})\simeq_{n,K}\frac{|z_{1}^{\prime}-z_{i}^{\prime}|}{\dist_{e}(B^{\prime},\partial V)}

for
i=2,3
. Therefore,
k_{V}(z_{1}^{\prime},z_{2}^{\prime})\lesssim_{n,K}k_{V}(z_{1}^{\prime},z_{3}^{\prime})
. This completes the proof.
∎

2.7.
Hyperbolic metric

Let
\Omega\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be a domain whose boundary contains at least two points. Let
\rho_{\Omega}
be the hyperbolic density in
\Omega
; see
[
5
]
for the definition. The
hyperbolic distance
of two points
z,w\in\Omega
is defined as

h_{\Omega}(z,w)=\inf_{\gamma}\left\{\int_{\gamma}\rho_{\Omega}(\zeta)\,|d\zeta|\right\},

where the infimum is taken over all rectifiable curves
\gamma
in
\Omega
with endpoints
z,w
. If
\Omega
is a half-plane then

\rho_{\Omega}(z)=\frac{1}{\dist_{e}(z,\partial\Omega)},\,\,\,z\in\Omega

and if
\Omega=\mathbb{D}(0,r)
for some
r>0
, then

\rho_{\Omega}(z)=\frac{2r}{r^{2}-|z|^{2}}\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,z\in\Omega\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{\Omega^{*}}(z)=\frac{2r}{|z|^{2}-|r|^{2}}\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,z\in\Omega^{*}.

Theorem 2.15
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a domain.

(1)

For every
z\in\Omega
we have
\rho_{\Omega}(z)\leq 2^{-1}{\dist_{e}(z,\partial\Omega)}
.

(2)

If
\Omega
is simply connected, then for every
z\in\Omega
we have

\frac{1}{2\dist_{e}(z,\partial\Omega)}\leq\rho_{\Omega}(z)\leq\frac{2}{\dist_{e}(z,\partial\Omega)}.

(3)

If
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Omega
has exactly two components
E,F
one of which is unbounded and
\Delta_{e}(E,F)\leq K
for some
K\geq 1
, then for every
z\in\Omega
we have

\frac{1}{C(K)\dist_{e}(z,\partial\Omega)}\leq\rho_{\Omega}(z)\leq\frac{2}{\dist_{e}(z,\partial\Omega)}.

Proof.

The first two parts are discussed in
[
5
]
*Section 8 and in
[
6
]
*(2.1), (2.2). The left inequality in the third part follows from
[
6
]
*Theorem 1, as soon as we prove that the quantity

\beta(z)=\inf\left\{\left|\log\frac{|z-a|}{|a-b|}\right|:a,b\in\partial\Omega,\,a\neq b,\,|z-a|=\dist_{e}(a,\partial\Omega)\right\}

is uniformly bounded in
\Omega
, depending only on
K
. Suppose that
E
is an unbounded component of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Omega
. Let
z\in\Omega
and suppose that
\dist_{e}(z,\partial\Omega)=\dist_{e}(z,E)
. Let
a\in E
such that
|z-a|=\dist_{e}(z,E)
. Since
E
is connected and it is not contained in
\mathbb{D}(a,|z-a|)
, we can find a point
b\in E
such that
|a-b|=|z-a|
. Thus,
\beta(z)=0
.

Now, suppose that
\dist_{e}(z,\partial\Omega)=\dist_{e}(z,F)
and let
a\in F
such that
|z-a|=\dist_{e}(z,F)\eqqcolon r
. Consider the annulus
A=\mathbb{A}(a;r,(2K+2)r)
. Suppose that
\partial\Omega
does not intersect the annulus
A
, so
F\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}(a,r)
. Since
E
is unbounded, the annulus
A
separates
E
and
F
. Thus,

\Delta_{e}(E,F)\geq\frac{(2K+2)r-r}{2r}=\frac{2K+1}{2}>K,

a contradiction. Thus, there exists a point
b\in\partial\Omega\cap A
, so

\frac{1}{2K+2}\leq\frac{|z-a|}{|a-b|}\leq 1.

This implies that
\beta(z)\leq\log(2K+2)
.
∎

3.
Relative hyperbolic metric

3.1.
Definition

Recall that if
U\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is an open set, we set
U^{*}=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\overline{U}
. Let
U,V\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be disjoint Jordan regions such that
\overline{U}\cap\overline{V}
is either empty or contains only one point; in particular,
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(\overline{U}\cup\overline{V})
is connected. The relative hyperbolic metric corresponding to the pair
(V,U)
is defined as

d_{V,U}(z,w)=\inf_{\gamma}\{\ell_{h_{U^{*}}}(\gamma)\},\quad z,w\in\partial V\setminus\overline{U},

where the infimum is over all rectifiable curves
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to U^{*}\setminus V
such that
\gamma(a)=z
,
\gamma(b)=w
. We remark that in this definition we adhere to the hyperbolic metric rather than the quasihyperbolic one, since we are working with subsets of the sphere.

If
V
is a quasidisk, then
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\overline{V}
is also a quasidisk. By Corollary
2.11
, every pair of points
z,w\in\partial V\setminus\overline{U}
can be connected by a rectifiable curve
\gamma
as in the definition of
d_{V,U}(z,w)
. Therefore,
d_{V,U}(z,w)<\infty
for every pair
z,w\in\partial V\setminus\overline{U}
, which implies that
d_{V,U}
is a metric on
\partial V\setminus\overline{U}
.

Observe that if
\phi\colon U^{*}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is a conformal embedding that extends to a homeomorphism of the sphere, then

d_{\phi(V),\phi(U)}(\phi(z),\phi(w))=d_{V,U}(z,w)\,\,\,\text{for every $z,w\in\partial V\setminus\overline{U}$.}

3.2.
Basic examples

We estimate the metric
d_{V,U}
in some important cases.

Lemma 3.1

(Parallel lines and concentric circles)
.

Let
U,V\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be disjoint disks.

(1)

If
U,V\subset\mathbb{C}
are half-planes with
\overline{U}\cap\overline{V}\cap\mathbb{C}=\emptyset
, then, for every
z,w\in\partial V\cap\mathbb{C}
,

d_{V,U}(z,w)=\frac{|z-w|}{\dist_{e}(\partial V,\partial U)}.

(2)

If
U^{*},V\subset\mathbb{C}
are concentric Euclidean disks, then, for every
z,w\in\partial V
,

\frac{|z-w|}{\dist_{e}(\partial V,\partial U)}\leq d_{V,U}(z,w)\leq\pi\frac{|z-w|}{\dist_{e}(\partial V,\partial U)}

and for every
z,w\in\partial U
,

\frac{\diam_{e}(\partial V)|z-w|}{\diam_{e}(\partial U)\dist_{e}(\partial V,\partial U)}\leq d_{U,V}(z,w)\leq\frac{\pi\diam_{e}(\partial V)|z-w|}{\diam_{e}(\partial U)\dist_{e}(\partial V,\partial U)}.

Proof.

In
(1)
, for any curve
\gamma
in
U^{*}\setminus V
connecting
z,w\in\partial V\cap\mathbb{C}
we have

\ell_{h_{U^{*}}}(\gamma)=\int_{\gamma}\frac{|d\zeta|}{\dist_{e}(\zeta,\partial U)}\geq\frac{\ell_{e}(\gamma)}{\dist_{e}(\partial U,\partial V)}\geq\frac{|z-w|}{\dist_{e}(\partial U,\partial V)}=\ell_{h_{U^{*}}}([z,w]),

so
d_{V,U}(z,w)=\ell_{h_{U^{*}}}([z,w])
, as desired.

For
(2)
, let
U=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathbb{D}(0,R)
and
V=\mathbb{D}(0,r)
,
r<R
, so then
U^{*}=\mathbb{D}(0,R)
. For any curve
\gamma
in
U^{*}\setminus V
connecting
z,w\in\partial V
we have

\ell_{h_{U^{*}}}(\gamma)=\int_{\gamma}\frac{2R|d\zeta|}{R^{2}-|\zeta|^{2}}\geq\frac{2R\ell_{e}(\gamma)}{R^{2}-r^{2}}\geq\frac{2R\ell_{e}([z,w])}{R^{2}-r^{2}}=\ell_{h_{U^{*}}}([z,w]),

where
[z,w]
is the shortest arc of
\partial V
with endpoints
z,w
. Thus,

d_{V,U}(z,w)=\ell_{h_{h^{*}}}([z,w])=\frac{2R}{R+r}\frac{\ell_{e}([z,w])}{\dist_{e}(\partial U,\partial V)}.

Finally, observe that
|z-w|\leq\ell_{e}([z,w])\leq\frac{\pi}{2}|z-w|
. This proves the inequalities for
d_{V,U}
.

We now discuss the inequality for
d_{U,V}
. Consider the conformal map
\phi(z)=1/z
in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. For
z,w\in\partial U
, by the above we have

\displaystyle d_{U,V}(z,w)=d_{\phi(U),\phi(V)}(z^{-1},w^{-1})=\frac{2r^{-1}\ell_{e}([z^{-1},w^{-1}])}{r^{-2}-R^{-2}},

where
[z^{-1},w^{-1}]
is the shortest arc of
\phi(\partial U)
that connects
z^{-1},w^{-1}
. Note that

\frac{|z-w|}{R^{2}}=\frac{|z-w|}{|zw|}=\left|\frac{1}{z}-\frac{1}{w}\right|\leq\ell_{e}([z^{-1},w^{-1}])\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{|z-w|}{R^{2}}.

Thus,

\frac{2r|z-w|}{R^{2}-r^{2}}\leq d_{U,V}(z,w)\leq\pi\frac{r|z-w|}{R^{2}-r^{2}}.

From this the conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 3.2

(Quasicircles near parallel lines)
.

Let
K\geq 1
and
L>0
. Let
U,V\subset\mathbb{C}
be disjoint unbounded
K
-quasidisks such that
\partial V\cap\mathbb{C}\subset\{z\in\mathbb{C}:-LK\leq\im(z)\leq 0\}
and
\partial U\cap\mathbb{C}\subset\{z\in\mathbb{C}:L\leq\im(z)\leq L+LK\}
. Then for every
z,w\in\partial V\cap\mathbb{C}
we have

C(K)^{-1}\frac{|z-w|}{\dist_{e}(\partial U,\partial V)}\leq d_{V,U}(z,w)\leq C(K)\frac{|z-w|}{\dist_{e}(\partial U,\partial V)}.

See Figure
1
for an illustration.

Proof.

Since
U
is an unbounded quasidisk whose boundary is contained in a horizontal strip, the boundary
\partial U
separates the half-plane
\im(z)>L+LK
from the half-plane
\im(z)<L
; this follows from Lemma
2.4
. Similarly the boundary
\partial V
separates the half-plane
\im(z)>0
from the half-plane
\im(z)<-LK
. The quasidisks
U
and
V
are disjoint, so the half-plane
\im(z)<-LK
is contained in
V
and the half-plane
\im(z)>L+LK
is contained in
U
. Thus,

\mathbb{C}\setminus(U\cup V)\subset\{z\in\mathbb{C}:-LK\leq\im(z)\leq L+LK\}

and each vertical line intersects
\partial U
and
\partial V
. As a consequence, if
z\in U^{*}\setminus V
, then

(3.1)

\displaystyle\dist_{e}(z,\partial U)\leq(2K+1)L\quad\text{and}\quad\dist_{e}(\partial U,\partial V)\leq(2K+1)L.

Let
\gamma
be an arbitrary curve in
U^{*}\setminus V
that connects
z,w\in\partial V
. By Theorem
2.15
and (
3.1
) we have

\int_{\gamma}\rho_{U^{*}}(\zeta)|d\zeta|\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma}\frac{|d\zeta|}{\dist_{e}(\zeta,\partial U)}\geq\frac{\ell_{e}(\gamma)}{2(2K+1)L}\geq\frac{|z-w|}{2(2K+1)L}.

Given that
\dist_{e}(\partial U,\partial V)\geq L
, we have

d_{V,U}(z,w)\geq\frac{|z-w|}{2(2K+1)L}\geq\frac{|z-w|}{2(2K+1)\dist_{e}(\partial U,\partial V)}.

On the other hand, by the quasiconvexity of
\mathbb{C}\setminus V
, there exists a curve
\gamma
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus V
that connects
z,w\in\partial V
and
\ell_{e}(\gamma)\leq C(K)|z-w|
. By replacing the parts of
\gamma
that lie in
\mathbb{H}
with segments on the real line, we may assume, in addition, that
\gamma
is contained in
\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}\setminus V:\im(\zeta)\leq 0\}
. Each point of
\gamma
has distance at least
L
from
\partial U
. By Theorem
2.15
we have

d_{V,U}(z,w)\leq\int_{\gamma}\rho_{U^{*}}(\zeta)|d\zeta|\leq\int_{\gamma}\frac{2|d\zeta|}{\dist_{e}(\zeta,\partial U)}\leq\frac{2C(K)|z-w|}{L}.

Since
\dist_{e}(\partial U,\partial V)\leq(2K+1)L
, we obtain the desired inequality.
∎

(Wormhole)
.

[

*Proposition 1.13.

Lemma 3.4

(Quasicircles near concentric circles)
.

Let
K\geq 1
and

. Let

be a

and

C(K)^{-1}\frac{|z-w|}{\dist_{e}(\partial U,\partial V)}\leq d_{V,U}(z,w)\leq C(K)\frac{|z-w|}{\dist_{e}(\partial U,\partial V)},

\mathbb{D}(0,R+K(R-r))^{*}\subset U

\mathbb{D}(0,R)

\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<r-K(R-r)\}\subset V

. By the comparison principle for hyperbolic metrics

*Theorem 8.1, we have

\rho_{V^{*}}(z)\geq\rho_{\mathbb{D}(0,r_{1})^{*}}(z)=\frac{2r_{1}}{|z|^{2}-r_{1}^{2}},\quad z\in V^{*}.

Let
\gamma
be an arbitrary curve in

that connects points

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}\rho_{V^{*}}(\zeta)\,|d\zeta|

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\gamma}\frac{2r_{1}|d\zeta|}{|\zeta|^{2}-r_{1}^{2}}\geq\frac{2r_{1}|z-w|}{(R+K(R-r))^{2}-r_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2r_{1}|z-w|}{(R-r_{1}+K(R-r))(R+r_{1}+K(R-r))}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2r_{1}|z-w|}{(K+K_{1}+1)(R-r)(R+R+KR)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2r_{1}|z-w|}{(2K+1)(K+2)R(R-r)}.

\displaystyle d_{U,V}(z,w)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\gamma}\rho_{V^{*}}(\zeta)\,|d\zeta|\leq\int_{\gamma}\rho_{\mathbb{D}(0,r)^{*}}(\zeta)\,|d\zeta|=\int_{\gamma}\frac{2r|d\zeta|}{|\zeta|^{2}-r^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2r\ell_{e}(\gamma)}{R^{2}-r^{2}}\leq\frac{2rC(K)|z-w|}{R(R-r)}.

r\leq Kr_{1}\leq\frac{K}{2}\diam_{e}(\partial V)

Lemma 3.5

(Relatively separated quasidisks)
.

Let
K\geq 1
. Let

be

Proof.

Since
\infty\in U
, we have
\diam_{e}(\partial V)<\diam_{e}(\partial U)
.
Note that
\overline{V}\subset U^{*}
,
\overline{V}
is quasiconvex, and
\diam_{e}(\overline{V})<K\dist_{e}(\overline{V},\partial U^{*})

2.12
can be applied and we have

k_{U^{*}}(z,w)\geq C(K)^{-1}\frac{|z-w|}{\dist_{e}(\partial U,\partial V)}

for
z,w\in\partial V
. Combining this with Theorem
2.15
, for
z,w\in\partial V

d_{V,U}(z,w)\geq h_{U^{*}}(z,w)\geq\frac{1}{2}k_{U^{*}}(z,w)\geq\frac{1}{2C(K)}\frac{|z-w|}{\dist_{e}(\partial U,\partial V)}.

Next, we show an inequality in the reverse direction. The set
\mathbb{C}\setminus V
is
C_{1}(K)
-quasiconvex. Let
a\in\partial V
and consider the ball
\mathbb{D}(a,r(a))
, where

r(a)=\frac{\dist_{e}(a,\partial U)}{2(2C_{1}(K)+1)}=C_{2}(K)\dist_{e}(a,\partial U).

Suppose that
z,w\in\mathbb{D}(a,r(a))\cap\partial V
. Then there exists a curve
\gamma
of length at most

\mathbb{D}(a,2^{-1}\dist_{e}(a,\partial U))\setminus V

a\in\partial V

r(a)=C_{2}(K)\dist_{e}(a,\partial U)\geq C_{2}(K)\dist_{e}(\partial U,\partial V)\geq K^{-1}C_{2}(K)\diam_{e}(\partial V).

\partial V

-quasiconformal reflection along
\partial V
and
Z
be the domain bounded by
\partial U
and
g(\partial U)
.

Let
\gamma
be a curve in
Z
connecting two points
z,w\in\partial V
. We consider two cases. Suppose that
\gamma
is contained in the disk
\overline{\mathbb{D}}(0,2)
. Then for
\zeta\in|\gamma|
we have
\dist_{e}(\zeta,\partial Z)\leq\dist_{e}(\zeta,g(\partial U))\leq 3
. Thus,

\ell_{k_{Z}}(\gamma)=\int_{\gamma}\frac{|d\zeta|}{\dist_{e}(\zeta,\partial Z)}\geq\frac{1}{3}\ell_{e}(\gamma)\geq\frac{1}{3}|z-w|.

Now, if
\gamma
is not contained in the disk
\overline{\mathbb{D}}(0,2)
, it has a subcurve
\gamma^{\prime}
that is contained in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}(0,2)
and connects the boundary components of the annulus
\mathbb{A}(0;1,2)
. Therefore,

\ell_{k_{Z}}(\gamma)\geq\ell_{k_{Z}}(\gamma^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{3}\ell_{e}(\gamma^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{3}\geq\frac{1}{6}|z-w|.

Therefore,
k_{Z}(z,w)\geq\frac{1}{6}|z-w|
.

If
\dist_{e}(\partial U,\partial V)\leq 2
, then
K^{-1}<\Delta_{e}(\partial U,\partial V)=\dist_{e}(\partial U,\partial V)\leq 2
. By Remark
2.3
, we have
\Delta_{\chi}(\partial U,\partial V)\simeq_{K}1
. By Lemma
3.8
, we have
k_{Z}(z,w)\simeq_{K}d_{V,U}(z,w)
and by Lemma
3.5
, we have
d_{V,U}(z,w)\simeq_{K}\frac{|z-w|}{\dist_{e}(\partial U,\partial V)}
for
z,w\in\partial V
. Therefore,

k_{Z}(z,w)\simeq_{K}|z-w|.

Finally, suppose that
\Delta_{e}(\partial U,\partial V)=\dist_{e}(\partial U,\partial V)>2
. In particular,
U
is disjoint from
\overline{\mathbb{D}}(0,2)
. Since
\mathbb{C}\setminus V
is quasiconvex, for any two points
z,w\in\partial V
there exists a curve
\gamma
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus V
connecting
z
and
w
with
\ell_{e}(\gamma)\lesssim_{K}|z-w|
. By altering the curve
\gamma
and replacing parts of it by arcs of
\partial\mathbb{D}
if necessary, we may assume that
|\gamma|\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}\setminus V\subset Z
.

Since
\partial V\subset\mathbb{D}
and
\diam_{e}(\partial V)=1
, we have
\diam_{\chi}(\partial V)\simeq 1
. Moreover, since
\partial U\cap\mathbb{D}(0,2)=\emptyset
and
\partial V\subset\mathbb{D}
, we have
\dist_{\chi}(\partial V,\partial U)\simeq 1
. By Lemma
3.7

(1)
we have
\dist_{\chi}(\partial V,g(\partial U))\simeq_{K}\dist_{\chi}(\partial V,\partial U)\simeq 1
. Thus,
\dist_{e}(\partial V,g(\partial U))\simeq_{K}1
. This implies that for each point
\zeta\in|\gamma|
we have
\dist_{e}(\zeta,g(\partial U))\gtrsim_{K}1
. Also,
\dist_{e}(\zeta,\partial U)\geq\dist_{e}(\partial\mathbb{D},\partial U)\geq 1
. Therefore,
\dist_{e}(\zeta,\partial Z)\gtrsim_{K}1
and

k_{Z}(z,w)\leq\int_{\gamma}\frac{|d\zeta|}{\dist_{e}(\zeta,\partial Z)}\lesssim_{K}\ell_{e}(\gamma)\lesssim_{K}|z-w|.

This completes the proof of (
3.6
). The last inequality in the statement of the lemma follows immediately from (
3.6
) and Lemma
3.5
.
∎

Let

. Let

be
K
-quasidisks such that
\overline{U}\cap\overline{V}
contains at most one point and
\infty\in\overline{U}
. Let
Z
be the domain bounded by
\partial U
and some
K
-quasiconformal reflection of
\partial U
along

\id\colon(\partial V\setminus\overline{U},d_{V,U})\to(\partial V\setminus\overline{U},k_{Z})

.

If
\Delta_{\chi}(\partial U,\partial V)\leq 1
, the claim follows from Lemma
3.8
. Suppose that
\Delta_{\chi}(\partial U,\partial V)>1
. In particular
\overline{U}\cap\overline{V}=\emptyset
. Let

be a
K
-quasiconformal reflection along
\partial V
and

be the domain bounded by
\partial U
and

remains invariant under a conformal isometry and

is altered in a bi-Lipschitz way by Theorem
2.13
. By Remark

\id\colon(\partial V,d_{V,U})\to(\partial V,k_{Z})

∎

.

U^{\prime}=f(U)

,

3.4.
Characterizing tangent quasicircles

In this section we use only planar topology, so the point
\infty
does not lie in the closure of unbounded sets.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
U,V\subset\mathbb{C}
be unbounded Jordan regions such that
\overline{U}\cap\overline{V}=\emptyset
and let
\Omega=\mathbb{C}\setminus(\overline{U}\cup\overline{V})
. There exists a conformal map
\phi\colon\Omega\to S=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:0<\im(z)<1\}
such that
\phi
has an extension to a homeomorphism
\phi\colon\overline{\Omega}\to\overline{S}
that satisfies
\phi(\partial V)=\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
and
\phi(\partial U)=\mathbb{R}\times\{1\}
.

Proof.

Since
U,V
are unbounded, in the topology of the sphere their boundaries meet at
\infty
. By applying a Möbius transformation, we may assume that
U
contains a neighborhood of
\infty
,
V
is bounded,
V\subset U^{*}
, and
\partial V\cap\partial U=\{1\}
.

The domain
\Omega
is simply connected and has locally connected boundary. By the Riemann mapping theorem, there exists a conformal map
\alpha\colon\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
. By Carathéodory’s theorem
[
34
]
*Theorem 2.1,
\alpha
has an extension to a continuous map
\alpha\colon\overline{\mathbb{D}}\to\overline{\Omega}
. Note that
\partial\Omega=\partial U\cup\partial V
and
\partial\Omega\setminus\{1\}
has two components. By
[
34
]
*Proposition 2.5, we conclude that
\alpha^{-1}(1)
consists of two points in
\partial\mathbb{D}
. On the other hand, for
z\in\partial\Omega\setminus\{1\}
the set
\partial\Omega\setminus\{z\}
is connected, so
\alpha^{-1}(z)
is a single point. By precomposing
\alpha
with a Möbius transformation of the unit disk, we may assume that
\alpha(1)=\alpha(-1)=1
and
\alpha(i)\in\partial V
.

Similarly, we use a Möbius transformation that maps the half-plane
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\im(z)>1\}
onto
U^{\prime}=\mathbb{D}^{*}
and the lower half-plane
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\im(z)<0\}
onto a disk
V^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{D}
that is tangent to
\mathbb{D}
at
1
. There exists a conformal map
\beta\colon\mathbb{D}\to\Omega^{\prime}=\mathbb{D}\setminus\overline{V^{\prime}}
(which can be written explicitly) that extends to a continuous map of the closures and satisfies
\beta(1)=\beta(-1)=1
and
\beta(i)\in\partial V^{\prime}
.

The map
\phi=\beta\circ\alpha^{-1}\colon\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
extends to a homeomorphism of the closures that fixes the point
1
and maps
\partial V
onto
\partial V^{\prime}
and
\partial U
onto
\partial U^{\prime}
. With appropriate compositions this map yields the desired map between the domains in the statement of the lemma.
∎

We now restate and prove Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
U,V\subset\mathbb{C}
be unbounded quasidisks such that
\overline{U}\cap\overline{V}=\emptyset
. There exists a quasiconformal map
f\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
that maps
U
and
V
to half-planes if and only if the identity map

\id\colon(\partial V,d_{V,U})\to(\partial V,|\cdot|)

is quasisymmetric, quantitatively.

Proof.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
be a quasiconformal map that maps
U,V
to a pair of half-planes
U^{\prime},V^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}
with disjoint closures, respectively. By property
(Q-6)
,
f
and
f^{-1}
are quasisymmetric maps. Let
d=d_{V,U}
and
d^{\prime}=d_{V^{\prime},U^{\prime}}
. By Theorem
3.6
,
f\colon(\partial V,d)\to(\partial V^{\prime},d^{\prime})
is quasisymmetric. Next, Lemma
3.1
implies that
\id\colon(\partial V^{\prime},d^{\prime})\to(\partial V^{\prime},|\cdot|)
is quasisymmetric. Finally, the map
f^{-1}\colon(\partial V^{\prime},|\cdot|)\to(\partial V,|\cdot|)
is quasisymmetric. Therefore, the identity map
\id\colon(\partial V,d)\to(\partial V,|\cdot|)
is quasisymmetric.

Conversely, suppose that the identity map
\id\colon(\partial V,d_{V,U})\to(\partial V,|\cdot|)
is quasisymmetric. We perform a normalization. By the definition of a quasidisk, there exists a quasiconformal map
g\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
that maps
V
onto the lower half-plane
g(V)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\im(z)<0\}
. Then
g(U)
is an unbounded Jordan region contained in the upper half-plane. By Theorem
3.6
,
g\colon(\partial V,d_{V,U})\to(g(\partial V),d_{g(V),g(U)})
is quasisymmetric and also
g\colon(\partial V,|\cdot|)\to(g(\partial V),|\cdot|)
is quasisymmetric by property
(Q-6)
. Therefore, using our main assumption, we see that the identity map
\id\colon(g(\partial V),d_{g(V),g(U)})\to(g(\partial V),|\cdot|)
is quasisymmetric. By replacing
V
with
g(V)
and
U
with
g(U)
we assume that the identity map
\id\colon(\partial V,d_{V,U})\to(\partial V,|\cdot|)
is quasisymmetric,
\partial V
is the real line
\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
and
U
is unbounded and contained in the upper half-plane.

By Lemma
3.11
, there exists a conformal map
f
from
\mathbb{C}\setminus(\overline{U}\cup\overline{V})
onto the strip
S=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:0<\im(z)<1\}
that extends to a homeomorphism of the closures and satisfies
f(\partial U)=\mathbb{R}\times\{1\}
and
f(\mathbb{R}\times\{0\})=\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
. Let
Z
be the domain bounded by
\partial U
and by its reflection along the real line. By Schwarz reflection, we can extend
f
to a conformal map from
Z
onto
Z^{\prime}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:-1<\im(z)<1\}
. The map
f|_{\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}}
is increasing. Indeed, if we write
f=u+iv
, the fact that
S\subset\mathbb{H}
implies that
v_{y}(x,0)\geq 0
, so
u_{x}(x,0)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

We identity
\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
with
\mathbb{R}
. Recall that the map
\id\colon(\mathbb{R},|\cdot|)\to(\mathbb{R},d_{V,U})
is quasisymmetric by the normalizations. By Lemma
3.8
, the map
\id\colon(\mathbb{R},d_{V,U})\to(\mathbb{R},k_{Z})
is bi-Lipschitz. By Theorem
2.13
,
f\colon(\mathbb{R},k_{Z})\to(\mathbb{R},k_{Z^{\prime}})
is bi-Lipschitz. Finally, in the strip
Z^{\prime}
we have the explicit formula
k_{Z^{\prime}}(z,w)=|z-w|
for
z,w\in\mathbb{R}
. By considering the composition of all these maps, we see that
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is quasisymmetric and increasing. By the Beurling–Ahlfors extension theorem (Theorem
2.7
) and an appropriate reflection, we can extend
f
to a quasiconformal homeomorphism of the lower half-plane
V
. The fact that
\mathbb{R}
is removable for quasiconformal homeomorphisms implies that
f\colon U^{*}\to\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\im(z)<1\}
is a quasiconformal homeomorphism. Note that
f(z)\to\infty
as
z\to\infty
. Since
U
is a quasidisk, we can extend
f
a quasiconformal map of
\mathbb{C}
by Proposition
2.6
.
∎

Characterizing quasiannuli

In this section we use the topology of the sphere and the chordal metric. We restate and prove Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
U,V\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be quasidisks such that

3.12

Proof.

Let
f\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be a quasiconformal map that maps

to a pair of disks

with disjoint closures. By postcomposing

\mathbb{D}(0,r)

Conversely, suppose that the identity map
\id\colon(\partial V,d_{V,U})\to(\partial V,\chi)

. By Theorem
3.6

,
U
is a quasidisk containing
\infty
, and

There exists a conformal map
f

4.
Graphs of functions and cusps

In this section we prove Theorem

and Theorem

. We use planar topology, so the closure of an unbounded set does not contain the point
\infty

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
K\geq 1
and
L,a>0
. Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
be an
L
-Lipschitz function and
U\subset\mathbb{H}
be an unbounded
K
-quasidisk such that
\partial U\subset\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:a^{-1}f(x)\leq y\leq af(x)\}
and each vertical line intersects
\partial U
. There exists a quasiconformal homeomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
that maps preserves the real line and maps
\partial U
onto a line if and only if an antiderivative of
1/f
is quasisymmetric, quantitatively.

Proof.

Let
\graph(f)=\{(x,f(x)):x\in\mathbb{R}\}
. For each
t\in\mathbb{R}
the ball
\overline{\mathbb{D}}((t,0),f(t))
contains the point
(t,f(t))
and might contain other points of the graph of
f
as well. Hence, there exists a point
y\in[t-f(t),t+f(t)]
such that

\displaystyle\dist_{e}((t,0),\graph(f))=|(t,0)-(y,f(y))|.

If
|t-y|\geq\frac{f(t)}{2L}
, then
\dist_{e}((t,0),\graph(f))\geq|t-y|\geq\frac{f(t)}{2L}
. If

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
\eta
be a distortion function. Let
h\colon\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}\to\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}
be an
\eta
-quasisymmetric homeomorphism such that
h(\mathbb{R}\times\{0\})=\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
and
h|_{\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}}
is increasing. Then there exists an extension of
h
to a
K(\eta)
-quasiconformal homeomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

For each

we have

\frac{|h(t,1)-h(t,0)|}{|h(t,0)-h(t+1,0)|}\leq\eta(1).

If

, then

|h(t,0)-h(t+1,0)|=\frac{|h(t,0)-h(t+1,0)|}{|(y,0)-(y,1)|}\leq\eta\left(\frac{1}{|(t,0)-h^{-1}(y,1)|}\right)\leq\eta(1).

Therefore,
1\leq|h(t,1)-h(t,0)|\leq\eta(1)^{2}
. This implies that

is increasing as well. Moreover,

|h(t,0)-h(t+1,0)|\geq\eta(1)^{-1}|h(t,1)-h(t,0)|\geq\eta(1)^{-1}.

By symmetry, the above bounds remain true if we switch the roles of
0
and
1
in the
y
-coordinates. Thus, there exists
a=a(\eta)>0
such that for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
the vertices of the square
Q_{t}=[t,t+1]\times[0,1]
are mapped under
h
to points with mutual distances in the interval

. Proposition
5.2
implies that

has a
K(\eta)
-quasiconformal extension
H
in the strip
\mathbb{R}\times(0,1)
. Using the Beurling–Ahlfors extension theorem (Theorem
2.7
) and the quasiconformal removability of lines, we may extend

quasiconformally to the entire plane.
∎

5.2.
Extension in an annulus

We give the analogues of the extension results of the preceding section for annuli. First we need an elementary lemma about lifts of circle homeomorphisms.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
f,g\colon\mathbb{S}(0,1)\to\mathbb{S}(0,1)
be orientation-preserving homeomorphisms. Then there exist lifts
F,G
of
f,g
, respectively, under the map
z\mapsto e^{2\pi iz}
such that

\|F-G\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|f-g\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

Let
F,G\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be arbitrary lifts of
f,g
, respectively. The maps
F,G
are increasing homeomorphisms satisfying
F(\theta+1)=F(\theta)+1
and
G(\theta+1)=G(\theta)+1
for
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. We will correct the map
G
by an integer. Let
D(\theta)=F(\theta)-G(\theta)
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Observe that
|e^{2\pi i\theta}-1|\geq 4\dist_{e}(\theta,\mathbb{Z})
for
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus, for each
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\|f-g\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\geq|f(e^{2\pi i\theta})-g(e^{2\pi i\theta})|=|e^{2\pi iF(\theta)}-e^{2\pi iG(\theta)}|=|e^{2\pi iD(\theta)}-1|

\displaystyle\geq 4\dist_{e}(D(\theta),\mathbb{Z}).

If
\|f-g\|_{\infty}<2
, then
\dist_{e}(D(\theta),\mathbb{Z})<1/2
for each
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. By continuity, there exists
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\dist_{e}(D(\theta),\mathbb{Z})=|D(\theta)-k|
for each
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular,

\|F-(G+k)\|_{\infty}=\|D-k\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{4}\|f-g\|_{\infty}.

Next, suppose that
\|f-g\|_{\infty}\geq 2
, so in fact
\|f-g\|_{\infty}=2
. For
0\leq\theta_{1}\leq\theta_{2}\leq 1
we have

\displaystyle|D(\theta_{2})-D(\theta_{1})|

\displaystyle=|(F(\theta_{2})-F(\theta_{1}))-(G(\theta_{2})-G(\theta_{1}))|

\displaystyle\leq\max\{F(\theta_{2})-F(\theta_{1}),G(\theta_{2})-G(\theta_{1})\}\leq 1.

Thus, given that
D
is
1
-periodic, the oscillation of
D
is at most
1
. This implies that there exists
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\|F-(G+k)\|_{\infty}=\|D-k\|_{\infty}\leq 1=\frac{1}{2}\|f-g\|_{\infty}.

This completes the proof.
∎

\mathbb{S}(0,1)
and a scaling on
\mathbb{S}(0,L)
(resp.
\mathbb{S}(0,L^{\prime})
). The map
\sigma
is bi-Lipschitz on
\overline{\mathbb{A}}(0;1,L)
, depending only on
M
, and maps it onto
\overline{\mathbb{A}}(0;1,e^{2\pi/N})
. The map
\tau
is bi-Lipschitz on
\overline{\mathbb{A}}(0;1,L^{\prime})
, depending only on
\eta,M
, and maps it onto
\overline{\mathbb{A}}(0;1,e^{2\pi/N})
. If we can extend the map
\tau\circ h\circ\sigma^{-1}
to a
K(\eta,M)
-quasiconformal map of the annulus
\mathbb{A}(0;1,e^{2\pi/N})
, then we can also extend the original map
h
to a
K^{\prime}(\eta,M)
-quasiconformal map from
\mathbb{A}(0;1,L)
onto
\mathbb{A}(0;1,L^{\prime})
. Therefore, by replacing the map
h
with
\tau\circ h\circ\sigma^{-1}
, we may assume that
L=L^{\prime}=e^{2\pi/N}\in(1,e^{\pi}]
and
h
is
\eta
-quasisymmetric.

We will verify the assumptions of Proposition
5.5
. Consider the curvilinear square
S(t)=\{z\in\overline{\mathbb{A}}(0;1,L):t\log L\leq\arg(z)\leq(t+1)\log L\}
. It suffices to show that its vertices are mapped to points with mutual distances in the interval
[a^{-1}\log L,a\log L]
for some
a=a(\eta)\geq 1
.

Let
z,w\in\mathbb{S}(0,1)
be two vertices of
S(t)
. The other two vertices are
Lz
and
Lw
. We will bound the distances
|h(z)-h(w)|
,
|h(z)-h(Lz)|
,
|h(z)-h(Lw)|
,
|h(Lz)-h(Lw)|
. Then the bounds for
|h(w)-h(Lw)|
and
|h(w)-h(Lz)|
follow by symmetry.

Note that the arc of
S(t)
between
z
and
w
has length
\log L\leq\pi
. Thus,
\frac{2}{\pi}\log L\leq|z-w|\leq\log L
. Moreover, since
L\in(1,e^{\pi}]
, we have
c_{0}^{-1}(L-1)\leq\log L\leq L-1
for
c_{0}=\pi^{-1}(e^{\pi}-1)
. Therefore, we have

\frac{L-1}{|h(z)-h(w)|}\leq\frac{|h(Lz)-h(z)|}{|h(z)-h(w)|}\leq\eta\left(\frac{|Lz-z|}{|z-w|}\right)\leq\eta\left(\frac{L-1}{2\pi^{-1}\log L}\right)\leq\eta\left(\frac{c_{0}\pi}{2}\right)

and

\displaystyle\frac{|h(z)-h(w)|}{L-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{|h(z)-h(w)|}{|h(z)-Lh(z)|}\leq\eta\left(\frac{|z-w|}{|z-h^{-1}(Lh(z))|}\right)\leq\eta\left(\frac{\log L}{L-1}\right)\leq\eta(1).

Thus
L-1\leq|h(z)-h(Lz)|\leq\eta(\frac{c_{0}\pi}{2})\eta(1)(L-1)
and
\eta(\frac{c_{0}\pi}{2})^{-1}(L-1)\leq|h(z)-h(w)|\leq\eta(1)(L-1)
. These bounds give immediately bounds for
|h(z)-h(Lw)|
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\frac{|h(Lz)-h(Lw)|}{L-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{|h(Lz)-h(Lw)|}{|h(z)-Lh(z)|}\leq\eta\left(\frac{L|z-w|}{L-1}\right)\leq\eta(L)\leq\eta(e^{\pi})

and

\displaystyle\frac{L-1}{|h(Lz)-h(Lw)|}\leq\frac{|h(Lz)-h(z)|}{|h(Lz)-h(Lw)|}\leq\eta\left(\frac{L-1}{2\pi^{-1}L\log L}\right)\leq\eta\left(\frac{c_{0}\pi}{2L}\right)\leq\eta\left(\frac{c_{0}\pi}{2}\right).

So,
\eta(\frac{c_{0}\pi}{2})^{-1}(L-1)\leq|h(Lz)-h(Lw)|\leq\eta(e^{\pi})(L-1)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
\eta
be a distortion function and let
L,L^{\prime}>1
such that

(5.4)

\displaystyle c_{0}^{-1}\leq\frac{\log L^{\prime}}{\log L}\leq c_{0}

for some
c_{0}\geq 1
. Let
h\colon\mathbb{S}(0,1)\cup\mathbb{S}(0,L)\to\mathbb{S}(0,1)\cup\mathbb{S}(0,L^{\prime})
be an
\eta
-quasisymmetric homeomorphism that is orientation-preserving and preserves each of the two circles. Then there exists an extension of
h
to a
K(\eta,c_{0})
-quasiconformal homeomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.6
, we may assume that
L\geq 2
. Note that
h
extends quasiconformally to
\mathbb{D}
and to
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}(0,L)
by Corollary
2.8
. Thus, it suffices to extend it in the annulus
\mathbb{A}(0;1,L)
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{S}(0,1)
and
w\in\mathbb{S}(0,L)
such that
|h(z)-h(w)|=L^{\prime}-1
. By (
2.1
), we have

\frac{L^{\prime}-1}{2L^{\prime}}=\frac{|h(z)-h(w)|}{2L^{\prime}}\geq\frac{1}{2\eta\left(\frac{2L}{|z-w|}\right)}\geq\frac{1}{2\eta\left(\frac{2L}{L-1}\right)}\geq\frac{1}{2\eta(4)}.

Therefore,
L^{\prime}\geq L_{0}^{\prime}\coloneqq(1-\eta(4)^{-1})^{-1}>1
. Let
R=\frac{1}{2}(1+\min\{2,L_{0}^{\prime}\}^{1/2})
, so

(5.5)

\displaystyle 1<R^{2}<(2R-1)^{2}=\min\{2,L_{0}^{\prime}\}\leq\min\{L,L^{\prime}\}.

Note that
1<R<L/R<L
and
1<R<L^{\prime}/R<L^{\prime}
. We split the annulus
\mathbb{A}(0;1,L)
into the annuli
A_{1}=\mathbb{A}(0;1,R)
,
A_{2}=\mathbb{A}(0;R,L/R)
, and
A_{3}=\mathbb{A}(0;L/R,L)
. Similarly, we partition
\mathbb{A}(0;1,L^{\prime})
into the annuli
A_{1}^{\prime}=A_{1}
,
A_{2}^{\prime}=\mathbb{A}(0;R,L^{\prime}/R)
,
A_{3}^{\prime}=\mathbb{A}(0;L^{\prime}/R,L^{\prime})
. The map that is equal to
h
on
\mathbb{S}(0,1)
and equal to the identity map on
\mathbb{S}(0,R)
is trivially quasisymmetric, depending only on
\eta
. Therefore, by Proposition
5.6
, there exists an extension of
h|_{\mathbb{S}(0,1)}
to a
K(\eta)
-quasiconformal homeomorphism
H
of the annulus
A_{1}
that is the identity map in the outer circle. Similarly, there exists an extension of
h|_{\mathbb{S}(0,L)}
to a
K(\eta)
-quasiconformal homeomorphism
H\colon A_{3}\to A_{3}^{\prime}
that is a scaling
z\mapsto L^{\prime}L^{-1}z
in the inner circle.

It remains extend
H
to a quasiconformal map from
A_{2}
onto
A_{2}^{\prime}
. Equivalently, if we compose with appropriate scalings, it suffices to extend quasiconformally the map
g\colon\mathbb{S}(0,1)\cup\mathbb{S}(0,L/R^{2})\to\mathbb{S}(0,1)\cup\mathbb{S}(0,L^{\prime}/R^{2})
, which is equal to the identity in the inner circle and it is equal to a scaling in the outer circle. Let
\beta>0
such that
(R^{-2}L)^{\beta}=R^{-2}L^{\prime}
, so
L^{\prime}=R^{-2(\beta-1)}L^{\beta}
. We show that
c_{1}^{-1}\leq\beta\leq c_{1}
for some constant
c_{1}=c_{1}(\eta,c_{0})\geq 1
. Assuming this, note that the map
re^{i\theta}\mapsto r^{\beta}e^{i\theta}
is
\max\{\beta,\beta^{-1}\}
-quasiconformal, so it is
c_{1}
-quasiconformal and has the desired boundary behavior in the annulus
\mathbb{A}(0;1,L/R^{2})
.

We now prove the claim. By the assumption (
5.4
), we have

c_{0}^{-1}\leq\frac{\beta\log L-2(\beta-1)\log R}{\log L}\leq c_{0}.

Equivalently,

(5.6)

\displaystyle c_{0}^{-1}\leq\beta\left(1-\lambda\right)+\lambda\leq c_{0},

where
\lambda=2\frac{\log R}{\log L}
. By (
5.5
), we have

0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}\coloneqq\frac{\log R}{\log(2R-1)}<1.

By (
5.6
), we conclude that
\beta\leq c_{0}(1-\lambda)^{-1}\leq c_{0}(1-\lambda_{0})^{-1}
. If
\lambda<\frac{1}{2}c_{0}^{-1}
, then (
5.6
) gives
\beta\geq c_{0}^{-1}-\lambda\geq\frac{1}{2}c_{0}^{-1}
. Finally, if
\lambda\geq\frac{1}{2}c_{0}^{-1}
, then
L\leq R^{4c_{0}}
. Thus, by (
5.5
),

R^{(4c_{0}-2)\beta}\geq(R^{-2}L)^{\beta}=R^{-2}L^{\prime}\geq\left(\frac{2R-1}{R}\right)^{2}.

Thus,

\beta\geq(4c_{0}-2)^{-1}(\log R)^{-1}2\log\left(\frac{2R-1}{R}\right)

and the latter is a positive number depending only on
\eta
and
c_{0}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

6.
Extension of embeddings of pairs of circles

In this section we prove Theorems
1.8
and
1.9
. First we consider the case that
U
and
V
are tangent disks as in Theorem
1.8
. We establish a preliminary result.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}\to\mathbb{C}
be an
\eta
-quasisymmetric embedding for some distortion function
\eta
. Suppose that
f|_{\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}}
is the identity map and
f(\mathbb{R}\times\{1\})\subset\mathbb{H}
. Then
f(\mathbb{R}\times\{1\})\subset\mathbb{R}\times[\eta(1)^{-1},\eta(1)]
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
f(x,1)=(z,w)\in\mathbb{H}
. Then

w\leq|f(x,1)-(x,0)|=|f(x,1)-f(x,0)|=\frac{|f(x,1)-f(x,0)|}{|f(x,0)-f(x+1,0)|}\leq\eta(1).

For an inequality in the reverse direction we have

\displaystyle w=|f(x,1)-f(z,0)|

\displaystyle=\frac{|f(x,1)-f(z,0)|}{|f(z,0)-f(z+1,0)|}

\displaystyle\geq\eta\left(\frac{|(z,0)-(z+1,0)|}{|(x,1)-(z,0)|}\right)^{-1}\geq\eta(1)^{-1}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

All claims in the proof are quantitative. Let
U,V
be disjoint tangent disks in the sphere and
f\colon\partial U\cup\partial V\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be a quasi-Möbius embedding. By using suitable Möbius transformations we may assume that
V
is the lower half-plane
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\im(z)<0\}
,
U
is the half-plane
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\im(z)>1\}
and
f
fixes
\infty
. Henceforth we use planar topology. Therefore,
f\colon\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}\to\mathbb{C}
is a quasi-Möbius embedding such that
f(z)\to\infty
as
z\to\infty
. By
(Q-5)
,
f
is quasisymmetric. By Corollary
2.9
, the map
f|_{\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}}
has an extension to a quasiconformal, and thus quasisymmetric, homeomorphism
F
of
\mathbb{C}
. Thus, the map
g=F^{-1}\circ f\colon\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}\to\mathbb{C}
is quasisymmetric and is the identity map on
\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
. By postcomposing with a reflection, we may assume that
g
maps
\mathbb{R}\times\{1\}
into the upper half-plane.

By Lemma
6.1
,
g(\mathbb{R}\times\{1\})
is contained in a strip
\mathbb{R}\times[a^{-1},a]
. By Lemma
3.2
we conclude that the condition of Theorem
3.12
is satisfied. Hence, there exists a quasiconformal homeomorphism
G
of
\mathbb{C}
that maps the lower half-plane
V
onto itself and
g(U)
onto the half-plane
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\im(z)>1\}
. We consider the map
h=G\circ g
. The map
h
is a quasisymmetric homeomorphism from
\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}
onto itself that preserves each of the two lines and is increasing on
\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
. By Corollary
5.3
,
h
has a quasiconformal extension in
\mathbb{C}
. With appropriate compositions this extension gives the desired extension of the original map
f
.
∎

Next we treat the case of disks with disjoint closures as in Theorem
1.9
. We include a preliminary result in the spirit of Lemma
6.1
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\eta
be a distortion function and
L>1
. Let
f\colon\mathbb{S}(0,1)\cup\mathbb{S}(0,L)\to\mathbb{C}
be an
\eta
-quasisymmetric embedding such that
f|_{\mathbb{S}(0,1)}
is the identity map and
f(\mathbb{S}(0,L))\subset\mathbb{D}^{*}
. Then there exists
L^{\prime}>1
and
K=K(\eta)>0
such that for each
z\in\mathbb{S}(0,L)
we have

L^{\prime}\leq|f(z)|\leq L^{\prime}+K(L^{\prime}-1).

Proof.

Let
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{S}(0,L)
such that
|f(z_{1})|=\sup\{|f(z)|:z\in\mathbb{S}(0,L)\}\eqqcolon R
and
|f(z_{2})|=\inf\{|f(z)|:z\in\mathbb{S}(0,L)\}\eqqcolon r
. Note that
r>1
because
f(\mathbb{S}(0,L))\subset\mathbb{D}^{*}
. Suppose that
L\geq 2
. Then

\frac{R-1}{r+1}\leq\frac{|f(z_{1})-f(1)|}{|f(z_{2})-f(1)|}\leq\eta\left(\frac{L+1}{L-1}\right)\leq\eta(3).

Thus,

(6.1)

\displaystyle R\leq 1+\eta(3)(r+1)\leq(1+2\eta(3))r.

For
w=f(z_{2})/|f(z_{2})|
we have
|z_{2}-w|\geq L-1\geq 1
. Let
u\in\mathbb{S}(0,1)
such that
|u-w|=1
. Then

r-1=|f(z_{2})-w|=\frac{|f(z_{2})-f(w)|}{|f(w)-f(u)|}\geq{\eta\left(\frac{1}{|z_{2}-w|}\right)}^{-1}\geq{\eta(1)}^{-1}.

We conclude that
r\geq 1+{\eta(1)}^{-1}
, which is equivalent to

(1+2\eta(3))r\leq r+(\eta(1)+1)2\eta(3)(r-1).

Combining this with (
6.1
), we conclude that

R\leq(1+2\eta(3))r\leq r+(\eta(1)+1)2\eta(3)(r-1).

The desired conclusion follows for
L^{\prime}=r
and
K=(\eta(1)+1)2\eta(3)
.

Next, suppose that
1<L<2
. Let
w\in\mathbb{S}(0,1)
such that
|L^{-1}z_{1}-w|=L-1=|L^{-1}z_{1}-z_{1}|
; such a point exists by the restriction on
L
. Then

\frac{R-1}{L-1}\leq\frac{|f(z_{1})-f(L^{-1}z_{1})|}{|f(L^{-1}z_{1})-f(w)|}\leq\eta\left(\frac{|z_{1}-L^{-1}z_{1}|}{|L^{-1}z_{1}-w|}\right)=\eta(1).

This,
R\leq 1+\eta(1)(L-1)
. Next, consider the point
w=f(z_{2})/|f(z_{2})|
and let
u\in\mathbb{S}(0,1)
such that
|w-u|=L-1
. We have

\frac{r-1}{L-1}=\frac{\left|f(z_{2})-f(w)\right|}{|f(w)-f(u)|}\geq{\eta\left(\frac{|w-u|}{|z_{2}-w|}\right)}^{-1}\geq{\eta(1)}^{-1}.

Thus,
r\geq 1+\eta(1)^{-1}(L-1)
, which is equivalent to

1+\eta(1)(L-1)\leq r+(\eta(1)^{2}-1)(r-1).

Thus,

R\leq 1+\eta(1)(L-1)\leq r+(\eta(1)^{2}-1)(r-1).

The claim follows for
L^{\prime}=r
and
K=\eta(1)^{2}-1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

By applying suitable Möbius transformations, we assume that
V=\mathbb{D}
,
U^{*}=\mathbb{D}(0,L)
for some
L>1
,
f(\partial V),f(\partial U)
are Jordan curves in
\mathbb{C}
,
f(\partial U)
lies in the exterior of
f(\partial V)
, and the maps
f|_{\partial V}
,
f|_{\partial U}
are orientation-preserving. The curve
f(\partial U)
is a quasicircle; see (
2.2
) and the preceding discussion.

We perform a further normalization. Consider three points
z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}\in\partial U
with mutual distances at least
L
. We consider a quasiconformal homeomorphism
\phi
of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
that preserves the Jordan region bounded by the quasicircle
f(\partial U)
and the points
\phi(f(z_{1}))
,
\phi(f(z_{2}))
,
\phi(f(z_{3}))
have mutual distances at least
\diam_{e}(f(\partial U))/2
; the existence of
\phi
can be justified by Theorem
2.5
and the three-point transitivity of Möbius transformations of
\mathbb{D}
. The composition
\phi\circ f
is quasi-Möbius and satisfies the normalization in
(Q-3)
, so it is quasisymmetric. Thus, by replacing
f
with
\phi\circ f
we may assume, in addition, that
f
is quasisymmetric.

By Corollary
2.9
, the map
f|_{\partial V}
has an extension to a quasiconformal, and thus quasisymmetric, homeomorphism
F
of
\mathbb{C}
. The map
g=F^{-1}\circ f\colon\partial U\cup\partial V\to\mathbb{C}
is quasisymmetric and is equal to the identity map on
\partial V
. Moreover,
g
maps
\partial U
to a Jordan curve in the exterior of
V=\mathbb{D}
.

By Lemma
6.2
, there exists
L_{1}>1
such that
g(\partial U)
is contained in the annulus
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:L_{1}\leq|z|\leq L_{1}+K(L_{1}-1)\}
, where
K
depends only on the data. By Corollary
3.14
, there exists a quasiconformal homeomorphism
G
of
\mathbb{C}
that maps
\mathbb{D}
onto
\mathbb{D}
and the interior of the Jordan curve
g(\partial U)
onto a disk
\mathbb{D}(0,L^{\prime})
. Note that
G
is also quasisymmetric. Consider the map
h=G\circ g\colon\mathbb{S}(0,1)\cup\mathbb{S}(0,L)\to\mathbb{S}(0,1)\cup\mathbb{S}(0,L^{\prime})
, which is quasisymmetric.

Suppose that condition
(1)
is true. Since all of the above normalizations are implemented with quasiconformal maps of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, condition
(1)
implies that

c_{0}^{-1}\leq\frac{\log L^{\prime}}{\log L}\leq c_{0}

for some
c_{0}
that depends only on the data. By Proposition
5.7
, the map
h
extends quasiconformally to
\mathbb{C}
. If instead
(2)
is true, then we can obtain an extension using Proposition
5.6
. With appropriate compositions this extension gives the desired extension of the original map
f
.
∎

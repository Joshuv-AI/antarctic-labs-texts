On a constant related to the Bellman function of three integral variables of the dyadic maximal operator: Part B
Eleftherios N. Nikolidakis

Abstract
We study the behaviour of the constant that is provided in the articles [ 12 ] and [ 13 ] , which is connected with the determination of the Bellman function of three integral variables of the dyadic maximal operator. More precisely we study the monotonicity properties of this constant with respect to the first variable from which it depends.



1
Introduction

The dyadic maximal operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a useful tool in analysis and is defined by

\mathcal{M}_{d}\varphi(x)=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{|S|}\int_{S}|\varphi(u)|\,\mathrm{d}u:x\in S,\ S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \text{is a dyadic cube}\right\},

(1.1)

for every
\varphi\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
|\cdot|
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and the dyadic cubes are those formed by the grids
2^{-N}\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, for
N=0,1,2,\ldots
.

It is well known that it satisfies the following weak type (1,1) inequality

\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mathcal{M}_{d}\varphi(x)>\lambda\right\}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\left\{\mathcal{M}_{d}\varphi>\lambda\right\}}|\varphi(u)|\,\mathrm{d}u,

(1.2)

for every
\varphi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and every
\lambda>0
,
from which it is easy to get the following
L^{p}
-inequality

\|\mathcal{M}_{d}\varphi\|_{p}\leq\frac{p}{p-1}\|\varphi\|_{p},

(1.3)

for every
p>1
, and every
\varphi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
It is easy to see that the weak type inequality (
1.2
) is the best possible. For refinements of this inequality one can consult
[
6
]
.

It has also been proved that (
1.3
) is best possible (see
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
for general martingales and
[
21
]
for dyadic ones).
An approach for studying the behaviour of this maximal operator in more depth is the introduction of the so-called Bellman functions which play the role of generalized norms of
\mathcal{M}_{d}
. Such functions related to the
L^{p}
-inequality (
1.3
) have been precisely identified in
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
and
[
14
]
. For the study of the Bellman functions of
\mathcal{M}_{d}
, we use the notation
\Av_{E}(\psi)=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}\psi
, whenever
E
is a Lebesgue measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of positive measure and
\psi
is a real valued measurable function defined on
E
. We fix a dyadic cube
Q
and define the localized maximal operator
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{d}\varphi
as in (
1.1
) but with the dyadic cubes
S
being assumed to be contained in
Q
. Then for every
p>1
we let

B_{p}(f,F)=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{d}\varphi)^{p}:\Av_{Q}(\varphi)=f,\ \Av_{Q}(\varphi^{p})=F\right\},

(1.4)

where
\varphi
is nonnegative in
L^{p}(Q)
and the variables
f,F
satisfy
0<f^{p}\leq F
. By a scaling argument it is easy to see that (
1.4
) is independent of the choice of
Q
(so we may choose

Q
to be the unit cube
[0,1]^{n}
).
In
[
5
]
, the function (
1.4
) has been precisely identified for the first time. The proof has been given in a much more general setting of tree-like structures on probability spaces.

More precisely we consider a non-atomic probability space
(X,\mu)
and let
\mathcal{T}
be a family of measurable subsets of
X
, that has a tree-like structure similar to the one in the dyadic case (the exact definition can be seen in
[
5
]
).
Then we define the dyadic maximal operator associated to
\mathcal{T}
, by

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\varphi(x)=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}|\varphi|\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu:x\in I\in\mathcal{T}\right\},

(1.5)

for every
\varphi\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
,
x\in X
.

This operator is related to the theory of martingales and satisfies essentially the same inequalities as
\mathcal{M}_{d}
does. Now we define the corresponding Bellman function of four variables of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}
, by

B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,L,k)=\sup\left\{\int_{K}\left[\max(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\varphi,L)\right]^{p}\mathrm{\;}d\mu:\varphi\geq 0,\int_{X}\varphi\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu=f,\right.\\
\left.\int_{X}\varphi^{p}\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu=F,\ K\subseteq X\ \text{measurable with}\ \mu(K)=k\right\},

(1.6)

the variables
f,F,L,k
satisfying
0<f^{p}\leq F
,
L\geq f
,
k\in(0,1]
.
The exact evaluation of (
1.6
) is given in
[
5
]
, for the cases where
k=1
or
L=f
. In the first case, the author (in
[
5
]
) precisely identifies the function
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,L,1)
by evaluating it in a first stage for the case where
L=f
. That is he precisely identifies
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,f,1)
(in fact
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,f,1)=F\omega_{p}(\frac{f^{p}}{F})^{p}
, where
\omega_{p}:[0,1]\to[1,\frac{p}{p-1}]
is the inverse function
H^{-1}_{p}
, of
H_{p}(z)=-(p-1)z^{p}+pz^{p-1}
).

The proof of the above mentioned evaluation relies on a one-parameter integral inequality which is proved by arguments based on a linearization of the dyadic maximal operator. More precisely the author in
[
5
]
proves that the inequality

F\geq\frac{1}{(\beta+1)^{p-1}}f^{p}+\frac{(p-1)\beta}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\int_{X}(M_{\mathcal{T}}\varphi)^{p}\;d\mu,

(1.7)

is true for every non-negative value of the parameter
\beta
and sharp for one that depends on
f
,
F
and
p
, namely for
\beta=\omega_{p}(\frac{f^{p}}{F})-1
. This gives as a consequence an upper bound for
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,f,1)
, which after several technical considerations is proved to be best possible.Then by using several calculus arguments the author in
[
5
]
provides the evaluation of
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,L,1)
for every
L\geq f
.

Now in
[
14
]
the authors give a direct proof of the evaluation of
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,L,1)
by using alternative methods. Moreover in the second case, where
L=f
, the author (in
[
5
]
) uses the evaluation of
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,f,1)
and provides the evaluation of the more general
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,f,k)
,
k\in(0,1]
.

Our aim in this article is to study further the results of
[
12
]
and
[
13
]
in order to approach the following Bellman function problem (of three integral variables)

B_{p,q}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,A,F)=\sup\left\{\int_{X}\left(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\varphi\right)^{p}\mathrm{\;}d\mu:\varphi\geq 0,\int_{X}\varphi\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu=f,\right.\\
\left.\int_{X}\varphi^{q}\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu=A,\ \int_{X}\varphi^{p}\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu=F\right\},

(1.8)

where
1<q<p
, and the variables
f,A,F
lie in the domain of definition of the above problem. Certain progress for the above problem can be seen in
[
11
]
.

In
[
12
]
we have proved that whenever
0<\frac{x^{q}}{\kappa^{q-1}}<y\leq x^{\frac{p-q}{p-1}}\cdot z^{\frac{q-1}{p-1}}\;\Leftrightarrow\;0<s_{1}^{\frac{q-1}{p-1}}\leq s_{2}<1
, (where
s_{1},s_{2}
are defined below), there is a constant
t=t(s_{1},s_{2})
for which if
h:(0,\kappa]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfies
\int_{0}^{\kappa}h=x
,
\int_{0}^{\kappa}h^{q}=y
and
\int_{0}^{\kappa}h^{p}=z
then

\int_{0}^{\kappa}\bigg(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}h\bigg)^{p}dt\leq t^{p}(s_{1},s_{2})\cdot\int_{0}^{\kappa}h^{p}.

More precisely
t(s_{1},s_{2})=t
is the greatest element of
\big[{1,t(0)}\big]
for which
F_{s_{1},s_{2}}(t)\leq 0
where
F_{s_{1},s_{2}}
is defined in
[
12
]
. Moreover for each such fixed
s_{1},s_{2}

t=t(s_{1},s_{2})=\min\Big\{{t(\beta)\;:\;\beta\in\big[0,\tfrac{1}{p-1}\big]}\Big\}

where
t(\beta)=t(\beta,s_{1},s_{2})
is defined in
[
12
]
. That is we find a constant
t=t(s_{1},s_{2})
for which the above inequality is satisfied for all
h:(0,\kappa]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
as mentioned above. Note that
s_{1},s_{2}
depend by a certain way on
x,y,z
, namely
s_{1}=\frac{x^{p}}{\kappa^{p-1}z}
,
s_{2}=\frac{x^{q}}{\kappa^{q-1}y}
and
F_{s_{1},s_{2}}(\cdot)
is given in terms of
s_{1},s_{2}
.

In this article we study the monotonicity behaviour of the function

t(s_{1},s_{2})
with respect to the first variable
s_{1}
. This may enable us in the future to determine (
1.6
), by using also the results in
[
11
]
,
[

]
and
[
13
]
.

2
Monotonicity of
t(s_{1},s_{2})
with respect to the variable
s_{1}

As we have already proved whenever
t(s_{1},s_{2})
is given by
t_{s_{1},s_{2}}(0)
, that is whenever
(s_{1},s_{2})
is such that
\min\big\{{t(s_{1},s_{2},\gamma)\;:\;\gamma\in\big[{0,\frac{1}{p-1}}\big]}\big\}=t(s_{1},s_{2},0)=t_{s_{1},s_{2}}(0)
, the following is true
\frac{\partial t_{s_{1},s_{2}}(0)}{\partial s_{1}}=\frac{\partial t(0)}{\partial s_{1}}<0
.

We now investigate the rest domain of definition of
(s_{1},s_{2})
. Thus we assume that the variables
(s_{1},s_{2})
satisfy

\displaystyle 0<s_{1}<\min\Big\{{s_{2}^{\frac{p-1}{q-1}},s_{1}^{(0)}=\big({\tfrac{1}{h({s_{2}})}}\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}}\Big\}\,.

(2.1)

For any such
(s_{1},s_{2})
, it is previously seen that
t({s_{1},s_{2}})=t
is given by the following equation:

\displaystyle q\Big({p\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q-1}-({p-1})\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q}}\Big)\bigg({t^{p-q}-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}\bigg)=(p-q)s_{1}\cdot\alpha(s_{2})\,,

(2.2)

where
\alpha(s_{2})=\frac{\omega_{q}(s_{2})^{q}}{s_{2}}-1
,
s_{2}\in(0,1)
, and
\tau(s_{1},s_{2},t)=\tau=\frac{p-q}{p}\,\frac{t^{p}-s_{1}}{t^{p-q}-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}
.

We will prove that for
(s_{1},s_{2})
as in (
2.1
), the function
t(s_{1},s_{2})
is strictly decreasing with respect to the variable
s_{1}
. We first evaluate
\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial s_{1}}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial s_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p-q}{p}\,\frac{\big({p\,\frac{\partial t}{\partial s_{1}}\,t^{p-1}-1}\big)\big({t^{p-q}-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}\big)-(t^{p}-s_{1})\big({(p-q)\,\frac{\partial t}{\partial s_{1}}\,t^{p-q-1}-\frac{1}{s_{2}}}\big)}{\big({t^{p-q}-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}\big)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p-q}{p}\,\frac{1}{\big({t^{p-q}-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}\big)^{2}}\Bigg[\frac{\partial t}{\partial s_{1}}\,t^{p-q-1}\bigg({q\,t^{p}-p\,t^{q}\,\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}+(p-q)s_{1}}\bigg)\,+

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt+\bigg({\frac{t^{p}}{s_{2}}-t^{p-q}}\bigg)\Bigg]\,.

(2.3)

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt+\bigg({t^{p-q}-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}\bigg)\bigg[p(q-1)\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q-2}\omega^{\prime}_{q}(\tau)\,\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial s_{1}}\,-

\displaystyle=\bigg({(p-q)\,t^{p-q-1}\frac{\partial t}{\partial s_{1}}-\frac{1}{s_{2}}}\bigg)\Big({p\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q-1}-({p-1})\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q}}\Big)\,+

\displaystyle\hskip 22.76228pt+\frac{1}{p(q-1)}\,\frac{(p-1)q\,\omega_{q}(\tau)-p(q-1)}{\omega_{q}(\tau)-1}\bigg({\frac{t^{p}}{s_{2}}-t^{p-q}}\bigg)\quad\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\bigg\{\frac{1}{p(q-1)}\,\frac{(p-1)q\,\omega_{q}(\tau)-p(q-1)}{\omega_{q}(\tau)-1}\,t^{p-q-1}\bigg({q\,t^{p}-p\,t^{q}\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}+(p-q)s_{1}}\bigg)\,+

\displaystyle\hskip 192.05609pt+({p-q})s_{1}\,\alpha(s_{2})\,t^{p-q-1}\bigg\}\,\frac{\partial t}{\partial s_{1}}

\displaystyle=\bigg({t^{p-q}-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}\bigg)\,\alpha(s_{2})+\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}\,\alpha(s_{2})\,-

\displaystyle\hskip 78.24507pt-\frac{1}{p(q-1)}\,\frac{(p-1)q\,\omega_{q}(\tau)-p(q-1)}{\omega_{q}(\tau)-1}\,\bigg({\frac{t^{p}}{s_{2}}-t^{p-q}}\bigg)\quad\Rightarrow

\displaystyle t^{p-q-1}\frac{\partial t}{\partial s_{1}}\bigg\{\frac{1}{p(q-1)}\,\frac{(p-1)q\,\omega_{q}(\tau)-p(q-1)}{\omega_{q}(\tau)-1}\bigg({q\,t^{p}-p\,t^{q}\,\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}+(p-q)s_{1}}\bigg)\,+

\displaystyle\hskip 241.84842pt+(p-q)s_{1}\,\alpha(s_{2})\bigg\}

\displaystyle=t^{p-q}\,\alpha(s_{2})-\frac{1}{p(q-1)}\,\frac{(p-1)q\,\omega_{q}(\tau)-p(q-1)}{\omega_{q}(\tau)-1}\,t^{p-q}\bigg({\frac{t^{q}}{s_{2}}-1}\bigg)\hskip 24.18501pt\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\frac{\partial t}{\partial s_{1}}\cdot\delta(s_{1},s_{2})=t\,\gamma(s_{1},s_{2})\,,

(2.4)

where

\displaystyle\delta(s_{1},s_{2})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{p(q-1)}\,\frac{(p-1)q\,\omega_{q}(\tau)-p(q-1)}{\omega_{q}(\tau)-1}\bigg({q\,t^{p}-p\,t^{q}\,\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}+(p-q)s_{1}}\bigg)\,+

\displaystyle\hskip 184.9429pt+(p-q)s_{1}\,\alpha(s_{2})

(2.5)

and

\displaystyle\gamma(s_{1},s_{2})

\displaystyle:=\alpha(s_{2})-\frac{1}{p(q-1)}\,\frac{(p-1)q\,\omega_{q}(\tau)-p(q-1)}{\omega_{q}(\tau)-1}\bigg({\frac{t^{q}}{s_{2}}-1}\bigg)\,.

(2.6)

We now study the quantity

\lambda(t)=q\,t^{p}-p\,t^{q}\,\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}+(p-q)s_{1}\,.

We prove that
\lambda(t)\geq\lambda(1)>0
,
\forall\,t\in\big({1,\frac{p}{p-1}}\big)
. We have
\lambda^{\prime}(t)=p\,q\,t^{p-1}-p\,q\,t^{q-1}\,\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}\cong t^{p-q}-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}>1-1=0
. Thus for every
t\in\big[{1,\frac{p}{p-1}}\big)
we have
\lambda(t)\geq\lambda(1)
.

We now prove the second assertion that is made above, namely that:

\displaystyle q-p\,\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}+(p-q)s_{1}=\lambda(1)>0\quad

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle(p-q)s_{1}>p\,\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}-q\quad

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad p\,\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}<(p-q)s_{1}+q\,.

For this last inequality it is enough to prove that
p\,\frac{s_{1}}{s_{1}^{\frac{q-1}{p-1}}}<(p-q)s_{1}+q
, since
s_{2}\geq s_{1}^{\frac{q-1}{p-1}}
in our domain of definition. Thus it is enough to prove

\displaystyle p\,s_{1}^{\frac{p-q}{p-1}}<(p-q)s_{1}+q\,,\;\;\forall\,s_{1}\in({0,1})\,.\qquad(*)

We set
\varphi(s_{1}):=p\,s_{1}^{\frac{p-q}{p-1}}-(p-q)s_{1}-q
. Then we notice that
\varphi(1)=0
, while

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(s_{1})=p\,\frac{p-q}{p-1}\,s_{1}^{-\frac{q-1}{p-1}}-(p-q)\cong\frac{p}{p-1}\,s_{1}^{-\frac{q-1}{p-1}}-1\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s_{1}<1}}{{>}}0\,.

Thus
\varphi(s_{1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s_{1}<1}}{{<}}\varphi(1)=0
, whenever
(*)
is true. Thus from our conclusion we get
\lambda(t)\geq\lambda(1)>0
,
\forall\,t\in\big[{1,\frac{p}{p-1}}\big)
, where

\lambda(t)=q\,t^{p}-p\,t^{q}\,\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}+(p-q)s_{1}\,.

We now continue our study for the sign of
\frac{\partial t}{\partial s_{1}}
:
For the moment, fix
(s_{1},s_{2})
such that:
\omega_{q}(s_{2})=\omega_{p}(s_{1})\;\Leftrightarrow\;s_{2}=H_{q}({\omega_{p}(s_{1})})
. Note that such a point
(s_{1},s_{2})
belongs to the domain of definition which is described by (
2.1
). We prove that for such a point we have:
\gamma(s_{1},s_{2})<0
where
\gamma(s_{1},s_{2})
is given by (
2.6
).
That is

\displaystyle\gamma(s_{1},s_{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha(s_{2})-\frac{1}{p(q-1)}\,\frac{(p-1)q\,\omega_{q}(\tau)-p(q-1)}{\omega_{q}(\tau)-1}\bigg({\frac{t^{q}}{s_{2}}-1}\bigg)\,.

We set
\omega_{p}(s_{1})=\omega_{q}(s_{2})=\lambda
. Then as we have seen before
t(s_{1},s_{2})=\omega_{p}(s_{1})=\lambda
. Thus we have

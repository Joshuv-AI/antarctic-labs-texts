Edge spectrum for truncated ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -insulators
Alexis Drouot, Jacob Shapiro, Xiaowen Zhu

Abstract
. Fermionic time-reversal-invariant insulators in two dimension – class AII in the Kitaev table – come in two different topological phases. These are characterized by a ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -invariant: the Fu–Kane–Mele index. We prove that if two such insulators with different indices occupy regions containing arbitrarily large balls, then the spectrum of the resulting operator fills the bulk spectral gap. Our argument follows a proof by contradiction developed in [ 13 ] for quantum Hall systems. It boils down to showing that the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -index can be computed only from bulk information in sufficiently large balls. This is achieved via a result of independent interest: a local trace formula for the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -index.



1.
Introduction

Topological insulators are remarkable phases of matter that are insulating in their bulk but conducting along their boundaries. Mathematically, this is expressed as a spectral-theory result: the spectrum of an interface Hamiltonian between distinct topological phases systematically fills the bulk spectral gap, see e.g.
[
18
,
11
,
26
,
13
]
. A stronger version of this principle is the bulk-edge correspondence, which computes the conductance of the interface in terms of bulk topological invariants.

The mathematical analysis of these properties has primarily taken place in the context of straight interfaces
[
25
,
15
,
16
,
20
,
9
,
17
,
7
]
. Recently, results regarding the spectrum of curved interface systems have been established for Hall insulators (topological phases characterized by a
\mathbb{Z}
-valued index), see
[
26
,
13
,
14
,
12
]
. These works feature a geometric bulk-edge correspondence formula – with the emergence of a topological intersection number
[
14
]
– and proofs that the edge spectrum is robust and absolutely continuous
[
26
,
13
,
12
]
.

The present work focuses on fermionic time-reversal invariant insulators, models characterized by a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-index
[
19
]
that include the Fu–Kane–Mele Hamiltonian and the quantum spin Hall effect
[
22
,
19
,
27
]
. It extends the gap-filling result of
[
13
]
on Hall insulators to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-insulators. The main challenge with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-insulators is the absence of an easily exploitable trace formula for the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-index (for Hall insulators, this is the Kubo formula, see e.g.
[
16
, equation (1.4)]
).

Our strategy goes around this obstacle by providing a local trace formula for the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-index; specifically, a trace formula valid for Hamiltonians close to a reference system. An important feature of our formula is that closeness is measured in an origin-independent way. We obtain, as applications, that the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-index is locally determined. Because it is also origin-independent, it can be computed only from bulk information in sufficiently large balls. This is an important and seemingly unknown property: it justifies, for instance, the validity of numerical computations of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-index in truncated systems. In the present work, it allows us to apply the contradiction strategy of
[
13
]
and obtain the filling of the bulk spectral gap when an edge is introduced.

1.3.
Strategy

The main tool in the proof of
Theorem

1
essentially states that
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-index of an insulating Hamiltonian
H
can be computed from only approximate knowledge of
H
in a sufficiently large ball centered anywhere:

Proposition 1
.

Let
H_{1},H_{2}
be local,
\Theta
-invariant Hamiltonians, insulating at energy
E_{F}
. There exist
\varepsilon,R>0
such that if for some
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\big\|\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{B}_{R}(a)}(H_{1}-H_{2})\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{B}_{R}(a)}\big\|\leq\varepsilon,\end{gathered}

then
\mathcal{I}(H_{1})=\mathcal{I}(H_{2})
.

To the best of our knowledge,
Proposition

1
is a new result for
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-insulators. We then prove
Theorem

1
following the contradiction argument of
[
13
]
: if
H
were insulating at energy
E_{F}
, then its
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-index
\mathcal{I}(H)
would be well-defined. Because
H
and
H_{+}
essentially coincide in suitably chosen large balls, we would have moreover
\mathcal{I}(H)=\mathcal{I}(H_{+})
. But likewise,
\mathcal{I}(H)=\mathcal{I}(H_{-})
; this contradicts
\mathcal{I}(H_{-})\neq\mathcal{I}(H_{+})
. Therefore,
H
is insulating at energy
E_{F}
. See
§

4
for the details of the proof.

1.4.
Trace formulas for the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-index and the proof of
Proposition

1
.

As in the case of quantum Hall systems
[
13
]
, the core of the paper consists of proving
Proposition

1
. In
[
13
]
, the analogous result is based on an analysis of the Kubo double-commutator trace formula for the bulk index:

(1.11)

\displaystyle\sigma(H)=-2\pi\operatorname{i}\cdot{\operatorname{Tr}}\left(P\big[[\mathds{1}_{x_{1}>0},P],[\mathds{1}_{x_{2}>0},P]\big]\right).

But for time-reversal-invariant insulators, there is no known convenient universal trace formula for the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-index.

Our inspiration comes from a paper due to Katsura–Koma
[
24
]
. Given a
\Theta
-invariant Hamiltonians
H
, insulating at energy
E_{F}
, define

(1.12)

\displaystyle B:=|P^{\perp}LP|^{6}.

We will see in the proof of
Proposition

1
that, due to the power
6
,
B
is a trace-class operator. If
\gamma_{B}
is a (
B
-dependent) complex contour enclosing
1
but not
0
, such that
B
has no eigenvalues on
\gamma_{B}
, we will moreover justify in (
3.7
) that

(1.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(H)={\operatorname{Tr}}\left(\oint_{\gamma_{B}}\left(B-z\mathds{1}\right)^{-1}\dfrac{\operatorname{d}\!{z}}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}\right)\mod 2.

There are two main ideas behind (
1.13
). The first one is that due to some standard algebraic manipulations,
\mathcal{I}(H)
is equal to the parity of the geometric multiplicity of
1
as an eigenvalue of
B
. The second one is that while the right hand side of (
1.13
) counts the sum of all multiplicities of eigenvalues of
B
enclosed by
\gamma_{B}
, these multiplicities are all even except for that of
1
. This is due to time-reversal invariance of
H
. In particular, taking modulo
2
produces
\mathcal{I}(H)
.

There is no contour
\gamma
that works for all insulators in
1.13
, since it must be chosen in a way that avoids the eigenvalues of
B
. Instead, given two insulators
H_{1},H_{2}
, we can construct a contour
\gamma
that works for both assuming they are sufficiently ”close” in a suitable sense. This allows us to express indices
\mathcal{I}(H_{1})
,
\mathcal{I}(H_{2})
using
1.13
over the same contour
\gamma
. Hence we get

(1.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(H_{2})-\mathcal{I}(H_{1})={\operatorname{Tr}}\left(\oint_{\gamma}\left(B_{2}-z\mathds{1}\right)^{-1}(H_{1}-H_{2})\left(B_{1}-z\mathds{1}\right)^{-1}\dfrac{\operatorname{d}\!{z}}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}\right)\mod 2,

where
B_{j}=|P_{j}^{\perp}LP_{j}|^{6}
,
P_{j}=\mathds{1}_{(-\infty,E_{F})}(H_{j})
,
j=1,2
. The proof of
Proposition

1
follows then from a technical analysis of
1.14
.

Remark 1.1
.

The definition
1.2
of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-indices remains valid in the presence of a mobility gap of

[

for context and proper definition). However, the scheme presented here to develop a local perturbation theory of the index uses crucially the presence of an actual spectral gap. We postpone an extension of
Proposition

in the mobility gap regime to a future paper.

2.
Center-independence

We start with a lemma which implies
\mathcal{I}(H)
is well-defined:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
H
be a local,
\Theta
-invariant Hamiltonian, insulating at energy
E_{F}
. Then
[P,L]\in{\mathcal{L}}^{3}
.

Proof.

We first note that
L
is the multiplication operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{d})
:

(2.1)

\displaystyle(L\psi)(x)=f(x)\psi(x),\qquad f(x):=\dfrac{x}{|x|},\qquad x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

Note that there exists a constant
D>0
such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left|f(x)-f(y)\right|\leq D|x-y|\langle y\rangle^{-1},\qquad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Meanwhile,
P=\mathds{1}_{(-\infty,E_{F})}(H)
is the spectral projection of a local insulator
H
. By writing the contour integral representation for the projections, and estimating the resolvents using the Combes-Thomas estimate
[
2
, §10.3]
, we have that
P
is also local and satisfies

(2.3)

\displaystyle|P(x,y)|\leq\nu^{-1}e^{-\nu|x-y|};

for more details see
e.g.
[
13
, Lemma 3.2]
.
As a result, the kernel of
T=[P,L]
satisfies

(2.4)

\displaystyle\big|T(x,y)\big|=\big|P(x,y)\big(f(x)-f(y)\big)\big|\leq\nu^{-1}e^{-\nu|x-y|}\big|f(x)-f(y)\big|\leq D\nu^{-1}|x-y|e^{-\nu|x-y|}\langle y\rangle^{-1}.

In particular,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\big|T(x+b,x)\big|^{3}\right)^{1/3}\leq D\nu^{-1}\left(\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}|b|e^{-\nu|b|}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\langle x\rangle^{-3}\right)^{1/3}<\infty.

By
[
1
, Lemma 1]
,
T\in{\mathcal{L}}^{3}
as claimed.
∎

Note that by direct computation (see also
[
4
]
), we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{P}}L|^{2}=\mathds{1}-|A|^{2},\qquad\text{where~}A:=P^{\perp}LP=P^{\perp}[L,P].

We see that
{\mathbb{P}}L
has a finite-dimensional kernel if and only if
1
is an eigenvalue with a finite multiplicity of
|A|^{2}
. The latter holds because by
Lemma

2.1
,
[L,P]
is compact; hence
A
is compact. In particular, this implies that
\mathcal{I}(H)
is well-defined with
\mathcal{I}(H)=m_{1}(|A|^{2})\mod 2
.

For a local, time-reversal invariant Hamiltonian
H
, insulating at energy
E_{F}
, we introduce a shifted version of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-index:

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(a)}(H)\ \mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}}\ \dim\ker\left({\mathbb{P}}L^{(a)}\right),\qquad a\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

In
2.7
,
L^{(a)}
is the Laughlin flux centered at
a
:

(2.8)

\displaystyle L^{(a)}\ \mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}}\ \operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}\arg(x_{1}+\operatorname{i}x_{2}-a_{1}-\operatorname{i}a_{2})}.

One key property of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-index (and similarly, Hall conductance in
[
13
]
) is center independence:
\mathcal{I}^{(a)}(H)
and
\mathcal{I}(H)
are equal.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
H
is a local, time-reversal invariant Hamiltonian
H
, insulating at energy
E_{F}
, then for all
a\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\mathcal{I}(H)=\mathcal{I}^{(a)}(H)
.

Proof.

If one can show that
{\mathbb{P}}L-{\mathbb{P}}L^{(a)}
is compact, then using invariance of indices of
\Theta
-invariant operators under compact perturbations
[
17
, Theorem A.14]
, we will have – with equalities below valid modulo
2
:

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(a)}(H)=\dim\ker{\mathbb{P}}L^{(a)}=\dim\ker\big({\mathbb{P}}L+{\mathbb{P}}(L-L^{(a)})\big)=\dim\ker{\mathbb{P}}L=\mathcal{I}(H).

Therefore, it suffices to prove that

(2.10)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}L-{\mathbb{P}}L_{a}=PLP-PL^{(a)}P=P(L-L^{(a)})P

is compact. As
P
is a bounded operator, it suffices to show that
L-L^{(a)}
is compact. This is the multiplication operator by

(2.11)

\displaystyle x\mapsto f(x)-f(x-a),\qquad f(x):=\dfrac{x_{1}+ix_{2}}{|x|}.

By (
2.2
), for some
D>0
, we have

(2.12)

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Tr}}(|L-L^{(a)}|)\leq\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}|f(x)-f(x-a)|\leq D\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{|a|}{\langle x\rangle}<+\infty.

Hence
L-L^{(a)}
is compact. This completes the proof. ∎

3.
Local-determinancy

In §
2
, we showed that
\mathcal{I}(H)=m_{1}(|A|^{2})\mod 2
. We push the argument further by showing that the parity of the multiplicity
1
eigenvalue of
|A|^{2}
does not change under suitable perturbations:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
H_{1},H_{2}
be two local,
\Theta
-invariant Hamiltonians, insulating at energy
E_{F}
. Let
P_{1},P_{2}
be the Fermi projections at
E_{F}
and
A_{j}=P_{j}^{\perp}LP_{j},\ B_{j}:=|A_{j}|^{6},\ j=1,2
. If

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|B_{1}-B_{2}\|_{1}\leq 2^{-8}(\|B_{1}\|_{1}+1)^{-2},

then
\mathcal{I}(H_{1})=\mathcal{I}(H_{2})
.

Proof.

Step 1.
First we claim the non-zero eigenspaces of
|{\mathbb{P}}L|^{2}
are even dimensional (see e.g.
[
10
, Theorem A.2]
). Indeed,
{\mathbb{P}}L
is
\Theta
-odd, i.e.
\Theta\left({\mathbb{P}}L\right)\Theta=-\left({\mathbb{P}}L\right)^{\ast}
. This uses that because
\Theta
is anti-unitary and commutes with
x
,
U\Theta=\Theta U^{*}
; and that because
H
is
\Theta
-invariant,
[P,\Theta]=0
. Now if
|{\mathbb{P}}L|^{2}\psi=\lambda\psi
for some
\psi\neq 0
and
\lambda>0
, then
{\varphi}=\Theta{\mathbb{P}}L\psi
is a linearly-independent eigenvector of
|{\mathbb{P}}L|^{2}
with eigenvalue
\lambda
. Indeed,

(3.2)

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{P}}L|^{2}{\varphi}=\Theta{\mathbb{P}}L{\mathbb{P}}L^{\ast}{\mathbb{P}}L\psi=\Theta{\mathbb{P}}L\lambda\psi=\lambda{\varphi}.

Moreover, it is perpendicular to
\psi
:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\langle\psi,{\varphi}\rangle=\langle\psi,\Theta{\mathbb{P}}L\psi\rangle=\langle\Theta\Theta{\mathbb{P}}L\psi,\Theta\psi\rangle=-\langle{\mathbb{P}}L\psi,\Theta\psi\rangle=-\langle\psi,{\mathbb{P}}L^{\ast}\Theta\psi\rangle=-\langle\psi,\Theta{\mathbb{P}}L\psi\rangle=-\langle{\varphi},\psi\rangle.

Because
\Theta{\mathbb{P}}L{\varphi}=-\lambda\psi
, the span of
\psi,{\varphi}
is invariant under the action of
\Theta{\mathbb{P}}L
. Let now
\eta
be orthogonal to
{\operatorname{Span}}(\psi,{\varphi})
. Then
\Theta{\mathbb{P}}L\eta
is also orthogonal to that span. Indeed, if
\chi\in{\operatorname{Span}}(\psi,{\varphi})
, then
\eta
and
\chi
are orthogonal and

(3.4)

\displaystyle\langle\chi,\Theta{\mathbb{P}}L\eta\rangle=\langle\Theta\Theta{\mathbb{P}}L\eta,\Theta\chi\rangle=-\langle{\mathbb{P}}L\eta,\Theta\chi\rangle=-\langle\eta,{\mathbb{P}}L^{\ast}\Theta\chi\rangle=-\langle\eta,\Theta{\mathbb{P}}L\chi\rangle=0

because
\Theta{\mathbb{P}}L
leaves the span invariant. Hence the non-zero eigenspaces of
|{\mathbb{P}}L|^{2}
are indeed even dimensional.

Step 2.
From
2.6
and the fact that
[L,P_{j}]
is compact (see
Lemma

2.1
),
\operatorname{Spec}(|A_{j}|^{2})\setminus\{0\}
consists of discrete eigenvalues with finite multiplicity. By
Step 1
, all these eigenvalues except
1
have even multiplicities.

In particular,
\operatorname{Spec}(B_{j})\setminus\{0\}=\operatorname{Spec}(|A_{j}|^{6})\setminus\{0\}
also consists of discrete eigenvalues with even multiplicity, except possibly
1
. Because
x\mapsto x^{3}

\displaystyle H\ \mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}}\ H_{+}+\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{c}}(H_{-}-H_{+})\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{c}}

is still insulating at energy

. In other words,

fails if one relaxes the condition “

5.1.
Wallace and Haldane models

Our construction relies on several graphene tight-binding models.

The Wallace model of graphene

, denoted

below and acting on wavefunctions

(5.2)

\displaystyle(H_{0}\psi)_{n}=\begin{bmatrix}\psi_{n}^{B}+\psi_{n-e_{1}}^{B}+\psi_{n-e_{2}}^{B}\\

It is well-known that

has a Dirac cone at energy

.

We can open a gap at

(1)

below to

\displaystyle(I\psi)_{n}=\begin{bmatrix}\psi_{n}^{A}\\

Using a Fourier transform, one can check that

and

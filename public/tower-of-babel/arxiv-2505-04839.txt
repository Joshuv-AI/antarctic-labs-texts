Spherical tropical curves are balanced
Desmond Coles

Abstract
. One of the characterizing features of tropicalizations of curves in an algebraic torus is that they are balanced. Tevelev and Vogiannou introduced a spherical tropicalization map for spherical homogeneous spaces G / H G/H , where G G is a reductive group. This map generalizes the tropicalization map for algebraic tori. We prove a balancing condition for spherical tropicalizations of curves in G / H G/H that generalizes the balancing condition for tropicalizations of curves contained in an algebraic torus. We give examples and describe the relationship with Andreas Gross’s balancing condition for tropicalizations of subvarieties of toroidal embeddings.

Introduction

Let
k
be an algebraically closed and trivially valued field of characteristic 0, let
G
be a (connected) reductive group over

such as an algebraic torus

,
\textrm{GL}_{n}
, or
\textrm{SL}_{n}
. Let
G/H
be a spherical homogeneous space of dimension

. Recall that a variety equipped with a

if there is a Borel subgroup

in

with an open orbit. Toric varieties, (partial) flag varieties (in particular Grassmannians), the basic affine space
G/U
, and symmetric varieties (including reductive groups themselves and their wonderful compactifications) are all spherical varieties. Many facts about toric varieties generalize to spherical varieties, in particular there is a combinatorial classification of spherical varieties, often called the ‘Luna-Vust theory’, that generalizes the correspondence between toric varieties and fans
[
LV83
,
Kno91
]
.
As in
[
TV21
]
,
[
Nas20
]
, and
[
Col23
]
there is a tropicalization map for spherical varieties given as follows. Let
G/H
be a spherical homogeneous space. There is a finite dimensional vector space
N_{\mathbb{R}}(G/H)
associated to
G/H
. The set of
G(k)
-invariant valuations on
k(G/H)
, which we denote
{\mathcal{V}}
, is identified with a finitely generated convex cone in
N_{\mathbb{R}}(G/H)
.

Let

be an algebraically closed non-trivially valued extension of
k
and let
\gamma\in G/H(K)
.
Then define
\textrm{trop}_{G}\colon G/H(K)\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}
by
\gamma\mapsto\textrm{val}_{\gamma}
where:

\textrm{val}_{\gamma}(f)=\textrm{val}_{K}(g\cdot f(\gamma))\textrm{ for $g\in U_{f}$}

here

is an element of
k(G/H)
and
U_{f}
is a Zariski dense open in
G/H(k)
such that the right hand side is constant for

. When

is an algebraic torus, and

is the trivial subgroup, this is the Kajiwara-Payne tropicalization map as in
[
Pay09
]
. Several foundational results from the toric case have been extended to the spherical case: Nash showed that the spherical tropicalization map extends from homogeneous spaces to arbitrary spherical varieties in the same way as the tropicalization map for tori extends to toric varieties
[
Nas20
]
, Tevelev and Vogiannou showed that spherical tropical compactifications exist
[
TV21
]
, Kaveh and Manon introduced a Gröbner theoretic approach to spherical tropicalization as well as a notion of amoeba
[
KM19
]
, and in
[
Col23
]
it is shown that the tropicalization map extends to a continuous map from the Berkovich analytification
\textrm{trop}_{G}\colon G/H^{\textrm{an}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}
, and that this map admits a section. Readers may also want to see
[
KM18
]
,
[
Nas17
]
.
[
Vog16
]
, and
[
CU25
]
for more background on spherical tropicalization.
A fundamental result of tropical geometry is that when

is an irreducible
n
-dimensional subvariety of a toric variety, the tropicalization of

has the structure of a purely

-dimensional

weighted fan which is connected in codimension 1

,
MS15
,
Gub13
]
.
Spherical tropicalizations of subvarieties do not in general display these qualities (see Example

), though it is known that the spherical tropicalization of a subvariety is the support of a rational fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}(G/H)

[
TV21
, Theorem 1]
.
In this article we will describe the balancing condition for spherical tropicalizations of curves defined over a trivially valued field.

Let
\textrm{Trop}_{T}(C)
be the tropicalization of a curve
C
in an algebraic torus
T
of dimension
d
. Then
\textrm{Trop}_{T}(C)
is the support of a 1-dimensional polyhedral complex in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, in particular
\textrm{Trop}_{T}(C)
has a unique fan structure. For each 1-dimensional cone
\sigma
in this fan let
v_{\sigma}
be the primitive integer vector that spans
\sigma
. A primitive integer vector is one with integer coordinates that do not have a common divisor. Tropicalization attaches a weight
m_{\sigma}
to each cone
\sigma
(as described in the following paragraph), and then we have:

\sum\limits_{\sigma}m_{\sigma}v_{\sigma}=0.

This property of tropical curves is a crucial part in using tropical geometry to solve enumerative problems in algebraic geometry, see for example Mikhalkin’s correspondence theorem
[
Mik05
]
, or the applications to log geometry in
[
Ran17
]
.

The underlying machinery of balancing for spherical tropical curves is the same as in the case of toric varieties. For
C
in an algebraic torus
T
the weights of
\textrm{Trop}_{T}(C)
are given by intersections of a tropical compactification of
C
with the boundary of the associated toric variety
[
ST08
,
Gro18
]
. Then it can be shown that the tropicalization in fact contains the same data as the Chow cohomology class determined by
C
in the toric variety and it follows from the description of Chow cohomology of toric varieties in
[
FS97
]
that the curve must be balanced. The result in
[
FS97
]
relies upon the earlier work in
[
FMSS95
]
that gives a description of the Chow cohomology for spherical varieties. We apply the results in
[
FMSS95
]
in a manner analogous to those in
[
ST08
]
to arrive at a balancing condition in the spherical case.

Let
C
be a curve over
k
in
G/H
. The set
\textrm{Trop}_{G}(C)
has the structure of a fan in
{\mathcal{V}}
and by
[
TV21
]
this fan determines a tropical compactification of
C
. Let
X
be a smooth
G
-equivariant toroidal embedding of

such that the fan associated to

contains
\textrm{Trop}_{G}(C)
as a subfan.
Let
\overline{C}
be the closure of

in
X
.
Given a cone
\sigma
in
\textrm{Trop}_{G}(C)
we can define the

associated to
\sigma
, denoted
m_{\sigma}
, to be

m_{\sigma}:=\deg\left([\overline{C}]\cdot[D_{\sigma}]\right)

where
D_{\sigma}
is the boundary divisor in
X
associated to
\sigma
. For the balancing condition we need correction factors given by
B
-invariant prime divisors in

, let
E_{j}
be such a divisor (these divisors give rise to the

in the colored fans). Then we define the

m_{j}:=\deg\left([\overline{C}]\cdot[\overline{E_{j}}]\right)

determines a vector

(See Section

be the primitive integer vector that generates the cone

In Section

are independent of the choice of

Theorem A
.

(defined over

). Then
\textrm{Trop}_{G}(C)
satisfies the following balancing condition:

\sum\limits_{\sigma}m_{\sigma}v_{\sigma}=-\sum\limits_{j}m_{j}v_{j}.

When

is a torus and

is trivial there are no

-stable divisors

in

A key idea in this proof is that we can work with toroidal spherical varieties, i.e. spherical varieties

such that the embedding of the open
G
-orbit,
G/H
, into
X
is toroidal. This is equivalent to the colored fan associated to

having no colors and as we discuss in Section
4
spherical tropicalization agrees with the tropicalization map for toroidal embeddings.
Furthermore, in
[
Gro18
]
there is a balancing condition for tropicalizations of subvarieties of toroidal embeddings, and in Section
7
we discuss how our balancing condition relates to the one presented in
[
Gro18
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. First we present the Luna-Vust theory of spherical varieties which describes spherical varieties in a combinatorial manner that generalizes the correspondence between toric varieties and fans. This section contains important notation. We then review spherical tropicalization.
In the following section we review the results on tropical compactifications in
[
TV21
]
. Finally, we prove the balancing condition. In the last section we include some discussion of how the ideas in this article may relate to a general structure theory for spherical tropical varieties. We assume the reader has some familiarity with tropical geometry and Berkovich geometry throughout.

2.
The Luna-Vust theory of spherical varieties

In this section we review the Luna-Vust classification of spherical varieties. This is a generalization of the correspondence between toric varieties and fans in
[
Ful93
]
. Recall that the correspondence between toric varieties and fans can be phrased as follows: if
T
is an algebraic torus then
T
-equivariant partial compactifications of
T
correspond to rational fans in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}=N\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{R}}
(where
N
is the cocharacter lattice of
T
). For a spherical homogeneous space
G/H
the Luna-Vust theory gives a correspondence between ‘colored fans’ and
G
-equivariant partial compactifications of
G/H
. By a partial compactification we mean a
G
-variety
X
with a
G
-equivariant open embedding of
G/H
into
X
, we call such a variety
X
a ‘
G/H
-embedding’ (note that
X
is automatically spherical and any spherical variety has an open
G
-orbit that is
G
-equivariantly isomorphic to
G/H
for some
H
).
We will follow the exposition of
[
Kno91
]
. First, we present some data associated to
G/H
which allows us to state the correspondence precisely.

Let
k(G/H)
be the function field of
G/H
. Define the group of
B
-semi-invariant
rational functions, denoted
k(G/H)^{(B)}
, to be the set

\{f\in k(G/H)\mid\textrm{there is a character of $B$, denoted $\chi_{f}$, such that for $b\in B(k)$ we have $b\cdot f=\chi_{f}(b)f$}\}.

There is a homomorphism of groups from
k(G/H)^{(B)}
to the character lattice of
B
given by
f\mapsto\chi_{f}
, denote the image by
M(G/H)
. We call
M(G/H)
the
weight lattice
of
G/H
. Let
N(G/H)
be the dual lattice to
M(G/H)
, and set
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}(G/H)=N(G/H)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
{\mathcal{V}}
be the collection of
G(k)
-invariant valuations on
k(G/H)
. Then there is a map
\varrho\colon{\mathcal{V}}\rightarrow N_{{\mathbb{R}}}(G/H)
given by
\varrho(\textrm{val})(\chi_{f})=\textrm{val}(f)
. Not only is this map well-defined but it is injective and the image is a finitely generated rational convex cone in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}(G/H)

[
Kno91
, Corollary 1.8, Corollary 5.3]
. We identify
{\mathcal{V}}
with its image in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}(G/H)
. We call
{\mathcal{V}}
the
valuation cone
of
G/H
. There is a finite set of codimension 1
B
-stable subvarieties
{\mathcal{D}}=\{E_{1},\ldots E_{r}\}
, and for each element we can also define a rational element of
N_{\mathbb{R}}(G/H)
given by
\varrho(\textrm{val}_{E_{i}})
where
\varrho
is defined the same as above. We say that
{\mathcal{D}}
is the
palette
of
G/H
. More generally if
X
is a
G/H
-embedding we write
{\mathcal{D}}(X)
for the codimension 1
B
-stable subvarieties. If
Y
is a
G
-orbit in
X
then we write
{\mathcal{D}}_{Y}(X)
for the elements of
{\mathcal{D}}(X)
such that
Y\subseteq D
. Because
X
is spherical
{\mathcal{D}}(X)
is finite
[
Vin86
, Theorem 1]
. We can now define colored cones and colored fans.

Definition 2.1
.

A
colored cone
is a pair
(\sigma,{\mathcal{F}})
, where
\sigma\subseteq N_{{\mathbb{R}}}(G/H)
is a strictly convex cone and
{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq{\mathcal{D}}
, and they satisfy the following:

CC1

\sigma
is generated by
\varrho({\mathcal{F}})
and finitely many rational elements of
{\mathcal{V}}

CC2

The relative interior of
\sigma
intersects
{\mathcal{V}}

CC3

0\notin\varrho({\mathcal{F}})
.

The elements of
{\mathcal{F}}
are referred to as the
colors
of the colored cone. A
colored face
of a colored cone
(\sigma,{\mathcal{F}})
is a colored cone
(\sigma^{\prime},{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})
such that
\sigma^{\prime}
is a face of
\sigma
and
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{F}}\cap\varrho^{-1}(\sigma^{\prime})
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
colored fan

{\mathfrak{F}}
is a finite collection of colored cones such that:

CF1

if
(\sigma,{\mathcal{F}})\in{\mathfrak{F}}
then every colored face of
(\sigma,{\mathcal{F}})
is contained in
{\mathfrak{F}}
.

CF2

For each point of
v\in{\mathcal{V}}
there is at most one colored cone
(\sigma,{\mathcal{F}})
such that
v
is contained in the relative interior of
{\mathcal{V}}
.

Remark 2.3
.

There are some subtleties about colored fans to address. First,
[
Kno91
]
considers colored cones
(\sigma,{\mathcal{F}})
where there may be
E\in{\mathcal{F}}
such that
\varrho(E)=0
. When
0\notin{\mathcal{F}}
and
\sigma
is strictly convex, Knop says the colored cone
(\sigma,{\mathcal{F}})
is ‘strictly convex’. We do not need to consider cones where
0\in{\mathcal{F}}
, so we do not. That is, all of our colored cones are assumed to be ‘strictly convex’ in Knop’s sense. Secondly, the underlying collection of cones need not form a fan. It is true however that if the colored fan contains no colors (the fan is ‘toroidal’ in other words) then the underlying set of cones does form a fan. The failure to form a fan is a result of the fact that colored cones need only to intersect along faces in the relative interior of
{\mathcal{V}}
. The failure of the colored fan to be an actual fan has a geometric meaning
[
Gag17
]
.

Let
X
be a
G/H
-embedding and let

be a
G
-orbit. Then we can construct a colored cone as follows. Define

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{Y}(X)

\displaystyle:=\{D\in{\mathcal{D}}_{Y}(X)\mid\textrm{such that $D$ is $G$-stable}\}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}(X)

\displaystyle:=\{D\in{\mathcal{D}}_{Y}(X)\mid\textrm{such that $D$ is \emph{not} $G$-stable}\}.

Then let
\sigma_{Y}(X)
be the cone generated by
\varrho({\mathcal{B}}_{Y}(X))
and
\varrho({\mathcal{F}}_{Y}(X))
. Note that
{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}(X)
is naturally identified with a subset of
{\mathcal{D}}
because elements of
{\mathcal{D}}(X)
which are not
G
-stable must be the closure of some element of
{\mathcal{D}}
. We say that
G/H
-embedding is

if there is a unique closed
G
-orbit.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
Kno91
, Theorem 3.1]

Let
X
be a
G/H
-embedding with
G
-orbit
Y
. Then we have that the pair
(\sigma_{Y}(X),{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}(X))
is a colored cone. For

(where

.

Note that if

Theorem 2.5
.

[
Kno91
, Theorem 3.3]

Define
{\mathfrak{F}}(X)=\{(\sigma_{Y}(X),{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}(X))\mid Y\textrm{ is a $G$-orbit of $X$})\}

is a colored fan and the association

.

Remark 2.6
.

To be precise, the association of a spherical variety to its fan is functorial and defines an equivalence of the two categories. In this article we will not make extensive use of this though so we will not discuss this in detail. Though when the fan
{\mathfrak{F}}(X)
has no colors a morphism of colored fans is just a morphism of fans and such a morphism will induce a

We also have an orbit-cone correspondence for

. Let

be a

be the closure in

.

Proposition 2.7
.

[

The map
Z\mapsto(\sigma_{Z}(X),{\mathcal{F}}_{Z}(X))
defines a bijection
G
-orbits

such that
\overline{Z}\supseteq Y
and colored faces of
(\sigma_{Y}(X),{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}(X))
. In particular there is a bijection between

-orbits and colored cones of the colored fan of

.

Furthermore we have that if
Y
is a spherical homogeneous space, then
\overline{Y}
is a
Y
-embedding, and we can describe the colored fan

. Let
Y
correspond to the colored cone
(\sigma,{\mathcal{F}})\in{\mathfrak{F}}(X)
, i.e.
(\sigma_{Y}(X),{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}(X))=(\sigma,{\mathcal{F}})
. Then define

. Notice that we have an inclusion

{\mathcal{D}}_{\iota}:=\{D\in{\mathcal{D}}(G/H)\mid\textrm{$D\cap Y$ is a divisor of $Y$}\}

\textrm{Star}(\sigma,{\mathcal{F}}):=\{(\iota^{*}(\tau),{\mathcal{E}}\cap{\mathcal{D}}_{\iota})\mid(\tau,{\mathcal{E}})\in\mathfrak{F}(X)\textrm{ and $(\tau,{\mathcal{E}})$ is a colored face of $(\sigma,{\mathcal{F}})$}\}.

We have that

.

[

, Proposition 7.13]

The

-embedding

3.
Spherical tropicalization

Tevlev and Vogiannou introduced a tropicalization map for spherical varieties
[
TV21
]
. The map is given as follows. Let
\gamma\in G/H(K)
where
K
is an algebraically closed and non-trivially valued field extension of
k
. Then define
\textrm{trop}_{G}\colon G/H(K)\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}
by
\gamma\mapsto\textrm{val}_{\gamma}
where

\textrm{val}_{\gamma}(f)=\textrm{val}_{K}(g\cdot f(\gamma))\textrm{ for $g\in U_{f}\subseteq G(k)$}.

Here
U_{f}
is a Zariski dense open of
G(k)
where the right hand side is constant for
g\in U_{f}
. The image of this map is dense in
{\mathcal{V}}
because
K
is algebraically closed. This map agrees with the Kajiwara-Payne tropicalization map when
G
is a torus. Nash extends this map to any
G/H
-embedding, in the same manner that the tropicalization map is extended from tori to toric varieties
[
Nas20
]
. If
X
is a
G/H
-embedding then
X
is the disjoint union of some finite collection of
G
-orbits
Y_{1},\ldots Y_{m}
, each of which is spherical
[
Kno91
, Corollary 2.2]
, and for each there is an associated valuation cone
{\mathcal{V}}(Y_{i})
and tropicalization map
\textrm{trop}_{G}\colon Y_{i}(K)\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}(Y_{i})
. We will now describe how to glue these maps together. We start with the case when
X
is simple; let
(\sigma,{\mathcal{F}})
be the corresponding colored cone.

Consider the space
\textrm{Hom}(\sigma^{\vee},(-\infty,\infty])
,
here
\sigma^{\vee}:=\{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\mid u(m)\geq 0\textrm{ for any $u\in\sigma^{\vee}$}\}
and
Hom
denotes monoid homomorphisms that preserve scaling by elements of
\mathbb{R}
.
Let
u\in\textrm{Hom}(\sigma^{\vee},(-\infty,\infty])
, the set where
u
is not equal to
\infty
is equal to
\tau^{\perp}\cap\sigma^{\vee}
for some
\tau
a face of
\sigma
. So
u
defines an element of
\textrm{Hom}(M_{\mathbb{R}}(\tau)\cap\sigma^{\vee},(-\infty,\infty))\cong N_{\mathbb{R}}(\tau)
. This gives a bijection:

\textrm{Hom}(\sigma^{\vee},(-\infty,\infty])\rightarrow\bigsqcup_{\textrm{$\tau$ a face of $\sigma$}}N_{\mathbb{R}}(\tau)

This defines a partial compactification of
N_{\mathbb{R}}(G/H)
as
N_{\mathbb{R}}(G/H)=N_{\mathbb{R}}(0)
.
By Theorem
2.8
we have that
N_{\mathbb{R}}(\tau)
is equal to
N_{\mathbb{R}}(Y_{i})
where
Y_{i}
is the
G
-orbit corresponding to
\tau
, and so
{\mathcal{V}}(Y_{i})\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}(\tau)
. So then
\textrm{Trop}_{G}(X)
is defined to to be the subspace of
\textrm{Hom}(\sigma^{\vee},(-\infty,\infty])
defined by the disjoint union of the valuation cones
{\mathcal{V}}(Y_{1}),\ldots{\mathcal{V}}(Y_{m})
. When
X
is not necessarily simple we observe that we can cover
X
by simple varieties, and because the interseciton of two simple spherical subvarieties will be simple, we glue the tropicalizations along the tropicalization of the intersection. The tropicalization maps will glue to form a continuous map:

which we call the

.

This tropicalization map extends to a map from the Berkovich analytification of

,
[

. The spherical tropicalization map admits a section

and the composition

defines a retraction map
{\mathbf{p}}_{G}\colon X^{\textrm{an}}\rightarrow X^{\textrm{an}}
, in fact there is a strong deformation retraction from
X^{\textrm{an}}
onto

[
Col23
, Theorem A]
. There is also a Gröbner theory for spherical varieties as introduced by Kaveh and Manon
[

]
, this theory agrees with the above approach to tropicalization, i.e. there is a ‘fundamental theorem of spherical tropical geometry’
[

. We will not need Gröbner theoretic tools so we will not introduce this here.

Tevelev introduces ‘tropical compactifications’ of subvariety of tori in
[

]
. These compactifications of subvarieties arise from toric varieties containing

[

. In
[

.

[

Remark 4.2
.

The assumption that

is proper is not especially serious for our purposes: given a toroidal compactification

of

there is some

by

and this compactification can be modified to be toroidal by taking each colored cone
(\sigma,{\mathcal{F}})
in the associated colored fan, intersecting

with
{\mathcal{V}}
, and removing
{\mathcal{F}}
. The resulting fan will define a proper toroidal

-embedding which contains

as an open subspace. Then we can restrict the tropicalization map to a subspace of this compactification to apply Theorem

to

We introduced the condition that

Recall from Section

that toroidal

-embeddings are classified by fans

contained in the valuation cone

. In particular, the

\overline{\Sigma}_{X}
is the canonical compactification of

, the cone complex of the toroidal embedding

. It follows from the above Theorem that the cone complex
\Sigma_{X}
is equal to

. In fact, the toroidal strata are equal to the
G
-invariant boundary strata. This all follows from structure theorem for toroidal spherical varieties
[

, Proposition 3.3.2]
.

We also have that tropical compactifications of

are tropical compactifications in the sense of Ulirsch’s tropical compactifications in log-regular varieties
[

]
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
Z
be a subvariety of

and let

be a toroidal

-embedding such that the closure of

in

in the expected dimension. To be precise,

intersects the stratum

if and only if the cone associated to that stratum is contained in

and the dimension of the intersection is given by:

Proof.

This follows from the fact that for a toroidal

-embedding, the

-invariant boundary strata correspond to the toroidal boundary strata, and the spherical tropicalization map and torpicalization map for toroidal embeddings agree
[

[

5.
Balancing for spherical tropical curves

Let
C\subseteq G/H
be an algebraic curve over
k
. As a result of
[
TV21
, Theorem 1]
we have that
\textrm{Trop}_{G}(C)
is the support of a fan in
{\mathcal{V}}
, and because the spherical tropicalization map agrees with the tropicalization map for toroidal embeddings we have that this fan is dimension at most 1
[
CU25
, Theorem C]
. Because the tropicalization has dimension at most 1 we have that
\textrm{Trop}_{G}(C)
carries a unique fan structure, and this fan defines a tropical compactification of
C

[
TV21
, Theorem 1.2]
. We will now assign weights to this fan and give the balancing condition. We proceed in an analogous fashion to the definition of weights associated to tropicalization of subvarieties of toroidal embeddings in
[
Gro18
, Subsection 4.2]
. For background on intersection theory we refer the reader to
[
Ful98
]
(chapters 6, 7, and 8 in particular), and to
[
FMSS95
]
for the intersection theory of spherical varieties in particular.

Let
\Sigma
be a simplicial fan in
{\mathcal{V}}
that covers
{\mathcal{V}}
and contains
\textrm{Trop}_{G}(C)
as a subfan. Let
X
be the associated toroidal
G/H
-embedding. We have that
X
is smooth
[
Per14
, Corollary 3.3.4]
and proper
[
Kno91
, Theorem 4.2]
. Such a fan exists because
[
Sum74
, Theorem C]
guarantees there is a
G
-equivariant compactification of the toroidal embedding whose associated fan is
\textrm{Trop}_{G}(C)
. The associated colored fan of this compactification will contain
{\mathcal{V}}
and contain
\textrm{Trop}_{G}(C)
as a subfan. We can then take the cones in the fan, remove the colors, and intersect the cones with
{\mathcal{V}}
to obtain a fan that covers
{\mathcal{V}}
and contains
\Sigma
, and this fan admits a simplicial refinement if necessary.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\sigma
be a 1-dimensional cone in
\textrm{Trop}_{G}(C)
. We define the
weight
of
\sigma
, which we denote
m_{\sigma}
, to be:

\deg\left([\overline{C}]\cdot[D_{\sigma}]\right).

Here
\overline{C}
is the closure of
C
in
X
and
D_{\sigma}
is the codimension 1 strata associated to
\sigma
. When we write “
\deg
” we mean the degree map from the
0
th Chow homology group
A_{0}(X)
and the product
[\overline{C}]\cdot[D_{\sigma}]
is the intersection product.

Lemma 5.2
.

The weights
m_{\sigma}
are independent of the choice of
\Sigma
.

Proof.

Let
\Sigma^{\prime}
be a simplicial refinement of
\Sigma
, and let
X^{\prime}
be the respective toroidal
G/H
-embedding. Then it suffices to show that for each 1-dimensional cone
\sigma
in
\textrm{Trop}_{G}(C)
that we have:

\deg\left([\overline{C}^{\prime}]\cdot[D^{\prime}_{\sigma}]\right)=\deg\left([\overline{C}]\cdot[D_{\sigma}]\right).

Here,
D_{\sigma}^{\prime}
is the codimension 1 strata associated to
\sigma
in
X^{\prime}
and
\overline{C}^{\prime}
is the closure of
C
in
X^{\prime}
. This is sufficient to show because any two fans admit a common simplicial refinement.

The map of fans
\Sigma^{\prime}\rightarrow\Sigma
induced by the identity on
{\mathcal{V}}
induces a proper surjective morphism
f\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
. In fact this is a morphism of toroidal embeddings. Let
f^{*}
and
f_{*}
be the pullback and pushforward morphisms on Chow homology, respectively. Then we have the following:

\deg\left([\overline{C}^{\prime}]\cdot[D^{\prime}_{\sigma}]\right)=\deg\left(f_{*}\left([\overline{C}^{\prime}]\cdot[D^{\prime}_{\sigma}]\right)\right)=\deg\left(f_{*}\left(f^{*}[\overline{C}]\cdot[D^{\prime}_{\sigma}]\right)\right)=\deg\left([\overline{C}]\cdot f_{*}[D^{\prime}_{\sigma}]\right)=\deg\left([\overline{C}]\cdot[D_{\sigma}]\right).

The first equality follows from the definition of the pushforward, the third equality is the projection formula, and the fourth equality holds because
f
maps the boundary divisor
D_{\sigma}^{\prime}
to
D_{\sigma}
and is a birational map between the two. The second equality holds because by the definition of
f^{*}
we have:

f^{*}[\overline{C}^{\prime}]=\gamma_{f}^{*}(X^{\prime}\times\overline{C}).

Here
\gamma_{f}
is the graph morphism from
X^{\prime}\rightarrow X\times X^{\prime}
. We have that
\overline{C}
intersects the strata of
X
properly by Proposition
4.3
and the morphism
f
is
G
-equivariant, in particular it maps
C
isomorphically onto itself because
C
is contained in
G/H
, so
f^{*}[\overline{C}]=n[\overline{C}^{\prime}]
for some
n
, applying the projection forumla to
f_{*}f^{*}[\overline{C}]=f_{*}(f^{*}[\overline{C}]\cdot[X^{\prime}])
shows that
n=1
.
∎

Let
E_{1},\ldots E_{r}
be the palette of
G/H
, i.e. the
B
-stable codimension 1 subvarieties.

Definition 5.3
.

We define the
colored weight
associated to
E_{j}
, which we denote
m_{j}
, to be

\deg\left([\overline{C}]\cdot[\overline{E_{j}}]\right)

where
\overline{C}
is the closure of
C
in
X
and
\overline{E_{j}}
is the closure of
E_{j}
in
X
.

Lemma 5.4
.

The colored weights are independent of the choice of
X
.

Proof.

Let
X^{\prime}
be another
G/H
-embedding given by a simplicial refinement of the fan associated to
X
. Let
\overline{C}^{\prime}
and
\overline{E_{j}}^{\prime}
be the closures of
C
and
E_{j}
in
X^{\prime}
, respectively. Let
f\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
be the natural proper
G
-equivariant morphism, then we have the following (as in the proof of Lemma
5.2
):

\deg\left([\overline{C}^{\prime}]\cdot[\overline{E}^{\prime}_{j}]\right)=\deg\left(f_{*}\left([\overline{C}^{\prime}]\cdot[\overline{E}^{\prime}_{j}]\right)\right)=\deg\left(f_{*}\left(f^{*}[\overline{C}]\cdot[\overline{E}^{\prime}_{j}]\right)\right)=\deg\left([\overline{C}]\cdot f_{*}[\overline{E}^{\prime}_{j}]\right)=\deg\left([\overline{C}]\cdot[\overline{E}_{j}]\right).

with the last inequality holding because
f
maps the
G
-orbits isomorphically onto itself and thus
E_{j}
isomorphically onto itself. Because any two fans admit a common simplicial refinement we have that the numbers
m_{j}
are independent of the choice of
X
.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

In
[
ST08
, Lemma 3.12]
the analogue of lemmas
5.2
and
5.4
are shown for a toric variety by first showing that given a smooth and proper
X
such that the closure of
C
is a tropical compactification, we have that
\overline{C}
is Cohen-Macaulay at the intersection of
\overline{C}
with the boundary
[
ST08
, Theorem 2.12]
. This allows one to show the length of the scheme
\overline{C}\cap D_{\sigma}
equals the intersection number. In the spherical case it is true that
\overline{C}
is Cohen-Macaulay at an intersection with the toroidal boundary (even if
C
is a subvariety of arbitrary dimension) because Cohen-Macaulayness is preserved under etale maps. But
\overline{C}
could have arbitrarily bad singularities at an intersection point with a color; take, for example, any singular curve in
{\mathbb{A}}^{n^{2}}
. The affine space
{\mathbb{A}}^{n^{2}}
is a
\textrm{GL}_{n}\times\textrm{GL}_{n}
spherical variety and has a color given by the vanishing of
x_{nn}
. Translate the singularity of the curve onto the hyperplane given by the aforementioned color. This gives an arbitrarily bad singularity of a curve intersecting with a color.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
X
be a toroidal embedding of
G/H
. Let
D
be a
B
-invariant codimension 1 subvariety of
X
. Then
D
is either a
G
-invariant boundary divisor, i.e. some divisor
D_{\sigma}
for
\sigma
a 1-dimensional cone in the fan of
X
, or
D=\overline{E_{j}}
for some
j
.

Proof.

If
D
intersects
G/H
then
D\cap G/H=E_{j}
for some
j
and then
D=\overline{E_{j}}
. If
D
does not intersect
G/H
then
GD
does not intersect
G/H
. So
GD
has codimension 1 and because
G
and
D
are irreducible, then so is
GD
, and so
GD
is a codimension 1 subvariety of
X
containing
D
and thus
GD=D
so
D
is
G
-stable.
∎

Recall that the divisors
D_{\sigma}
correspond to cones
\sigma
with primitive integer generator
v_{\sigma}
, which is given by
\varrho(\textrm{val}_{D_{\sigma}})
. Similarly the elements of the palette
E_{j}
define vectors
v_{j}
in
N(G/H)
given by
v_{j}=\varrho(\textrm{val}_{E_{J}})
.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
G/H
be a spherical homogeneous space and let
C
be a curve in
G/H
(defined over
k
).
Then we have that
\textrm{Trop}_{G}(C)
satisfies the following balancing condition:

\sum_{\sigma}m_{\sigma}v_{\sigma}=-\sum_{j}m_{j}v_{j}

Proof.

Let
\Sigma
be simplicial fan in
{\mathcal{V}}
which covers
{\mathcal{V}}
and contains
\textrm{Trop}_{G}(C)
as a subfan, and let
X
be the associated toroidal
G/H
-embedding. Let
\overline{C}
be the closure of
C
in
X
.
Let
d=\dim(X)
.
Let
A_{n}(X)
be the Chow homology group of
n
-dimensional cycles. Let
A^{n}(X)
be the
n
th Chow cohomology group so that we have a cap product
A^{n}(X)\otimes A_{m}(X)\rightarrow A_{m-n}(X)
. Here
X
is smooth so the Poincaré duality map
A^{n}(X)\rightarrow A_{d-n}(X)
, given by
Z\mapsto Z\cap[X]
, is an isomorphism.
By
[
FMSS95
, Theorem 3]
the Kroenecker duality map
A^{n}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(A_{n}(X),{\mathbb{Z}})
, given by
a\mapsto(Z\mapsto\deg(a\cap Z))
, is an isomoprhism. Thus each element
Z\in A_{n}(X)
determines an element of
A^{d-n}(X)
by acting on an element of
W\in A_{d-n}(X)
via
\deg([Z]\cdot[W])
. In particular
\overline{C}
determines an element of
A^{d-1}(X)
, the dual group of
A_{d-1}(X)
which is the group of divisors. By
[
FMSS95
, Theorem 1]
we have that
A_{\bullet}(X)=A^{B}_{\bullet}(X)
, where
A^{B}_{\bullet}(X)
is the Chow homology ring given by
B
-invariant subvarieties whose relations are given by divisors of
B
-semi-invariant rational functions on
B
-stable subvarieties. This means that the class of
\overline{C}
in

is determined by its values on the
B
-stable codimension 1 subvarieties of
X
, which are exactly the
G
-stable boundary divisors and the divisors
\overline{E_{1}},\ldots\overline{E_{r}}
by Lemma
5.6
. Because the
B
-invariant rational functions determine the relations on the Chow homology groups we also have that for each function

the following must be satisfied:

\sum_{\sigma\in\textrm{Trop}_{G}(C)}m_{\sigma}\textrm{val}_{D_{\sigma}}(f)+\sum_{j}m_{j}\textrm{val}_{E_{j}}(f)=0.

It then follows that

\sum_{\sigma}m_{\sigma}v_{\sigma}+\sum\limits_{j}m_{j}v_{j}=0.

∎

We will now discuss some related work on balancing for tropicalizations of subvarieties of toroidal embeddings, other work on the structure on spherical tropicalizations, and how the techniques in the previous section may generalize to higher dimensions.

SUMMARY: The FAA, in cooperation with the United States Air Force, is 
announcing the public review/comment period and public workshops for 
the ATAAPS DEA for the proposed Anchorage terminal airspace redesign. 
ATAAPS involves changes to the airspace, aircraft flight paths, and 
aircraft altitudes in the Anchorage basin and surrounding areas. The 
DEA evaluates the potential environmental impacts of the proposed 
airspace redesign (Proposed Action) and the No Action alternative. The 
FAA has not made a final decision on any airspace/procedure amendments 
related to the Proposed Action. A copy of the DEA, appendices, and the 
Workshop announcements will be posted to the FAA ATAAPS web page at: 
https://www.faa.gov/about/office_org/headquarters_offices/ara/alaskan_region/ataaps.
    Early notification of the FAA's intent to prepare a DEA was 
published in the Federal Register on July 16, 2026 (91 FR 43723, FR 
Doc. 2026-14367).
    In accordance with the National Environmental Policy Act of 1969, 
42 U.S.C. 4321 et seq., and in accordance with FAA Order 1050.1G, FAA 
National Environmental Policy Act Implementing Procedures, the FAA 
issues this notice that it has prepared a DEA for the ATAAPS project 
and has made it available for public review and comments.
    The ATAAPS Project would improve safety and efficiency of the 
National Airspace System (NAS) and homeland defense capabilities, by 
optimizing aircraft arrival and departure procedures that serve 
multiple airports--specifically in the Anchorage basin and surrounding 
areas--including: Ted Stevens Anchorage International Airport (ANC), 
Elmendorf Air Force Base Airport (EDF), Bryant Army Airfield Airport 
(FRN), Lake Hood Airport (LHD), and Merrill Field Airport (MRI).
    As a critical strategic gateway for homeland defense, Joint Base 
Elmendorf-Richardson (JBER) is currently restricted from operating at 
its intended capacity and fleet mix until the surrounding airspace is 
officially modified. The Instrument Landing System (ILS) for Runway 17 
is integral to improving JBER operations, but cannot become operational 
under the current airspace design. (JBER runway designations are 
currently 16/34 and 06/24; Runway 16/34 will be updated due to Magnetic 
Variation changes to Runway 17/35 before completion of the Final Rule).

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION:

Background

    ATAAPS addresses national security concerns as well as air traffic 
system inefficiencies within the Anchorage basin and surrounding areas. 
The ATAAPS DEA was prepared pursuant to the National Environmental 
Policy Act

[[Page 65028]]

of 1969. The DEA evaluates the Proposed Action and the No Action 
alternative. The Proposed Action focuses on airspace optimization for 
aircraft routes and controlled airspace within the Anchorage basin and 
surrounding areas. Area navigation (RNAV)-based standard instrument 
departure (SID) and standard terminal arrival (STAR) procedures have 
been in effect in the Anchorage basin and surrounding areas for over 20 
years. These arrival and departure procedures will be designed with 
advanced RNAV capabilities and will maintain safety and improve 
efficiency of the NAS, benefitting pilots, air traffic controllers, and 
the general public.
    The Proposed Action is not anticipated to increase the number of 
aircraft operations at the study airports or involve the physical 
construction of any facilities.
    The Proposed Action will include the following elements:
     Updated Airspace Boundaries. Aircraft operating in the 
Anchorage basin currently fly within the Anchorage Part 93 airspace in 
which specific, special air traffic rules are in effect (see 14 CFR 
part 93, subpart D). These rules address issues like high-density 
traffic, unique airspace configurations, and limitations related to 
military operations. The evaluation of airspace in the Anchorage basin 
also includes Class C and Class D airspace around affected airports. 
The Proposed Action will modify the boundaries of the special air 
traffic rules segments, including removing segments, renaming segments, 
and establishing a new segment. It will also modify the boundaries of 
the Class C and D areas in the Anchorage Terminal Area. Sub-areas of 
Class C airspace will be created for ANC, and the overall area of 
special air traffic rules will be reduced.
     Updated Departure Routes and/or Fixes From the Study 
Airports. Routes and fixes will connect study airports to optimized 
high-altitude routes using satellite-based RNAV technology.
     Updated Arrival Routes and/or Fixes Into the Study 
Airports. Routes and fixes will connect transitions from high-altitude 
routes to existing approach procedures for the study airports using 
advanced RNAV procedures.
    The FAA established a General Study Area, wherein proposed 
procedure changes below 10,000 feet above ground level (AGL) were 
evaluated for potential environmental impacts. Additionally, the FAA 
evaluated areas where FAA policy requires special consideration for 
potential noise impacts--which may include national parks, national 
wildlife refuges, historic sites and traditional cultural properties--
for proposed procedure changes below 18,000 feet AGL. The Proposed 
Action may include procedure changes at altitudes greater than 18,000 
feet AGL (high altitudes) within an approximate 41,725-square mile 
Study Area; however, these high-altitude changes are considered 
generally outside the affected environmental study area per FAA Order 
1050.1G, FAA National Environmental Policy Act Implementing Procedures.

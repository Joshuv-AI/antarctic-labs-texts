Hypercontractive inequalities and Nikol’skiĭ-type inequalities on weighted Bergman spaces
Zipeng Wang, Kenan Zhang

Abstract
. In this note, we obtianed hypercontractive inequalities between different weighted Bergman spaces. In addition, we establish Nikol’skiĭ-type inequalities for weighted Bergman spaces with optimal constants.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
32A36, 30H20, 46E15

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{T}
be the unit circle and we identify the circle
\mathbb{T}
with the interval
[0,2\pi)
.
For
1\leqslant p<\infty
,
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
is the Banach space of
L^{p}
-integrable function on the unit circle. Recall the Fourier coefficients
\hat{f}(n),n\in\mathbb{Z}
of
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
is defined by

\hat{f}(n)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(e^{i\theta})e^{-in\theta}d\theta,

where
d\theta
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on
[0,2\pi)
.
Then the Hardy space
H^{p}(\mathbb{T})
is the closed subspece of
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
:

H^{p}(\mathbb{T})=\left\{f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T})\left|\hat{f}(n)=0,\text{for each neagtive integer }n\right.\right\}.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, then
f
has a Fourier expansion

f(e^{i\theta})=\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}\hat{f}(n)e^{in\theta}.

For
0\leqslant r<1
, define the dialation operator

(T_{r}f)(e^{i\theta}):=\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}\hat{f}(n)r^{|n|}e^{in\theta}.

Observe that
\{T_{r}\}_{0\leqslant r<1}
is a contractive operator semigroup on
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
.
The hypercontractive inequalities for
T_{r}
is to determine the optimal range of
r
such that

T_{r}:L^{p}(\mathbb{T})\to L^{q}(\mathbb{T})

is a contraction.
In his celebrated work, Weissler
[
14
]
established the following sharp criterion:

\|T_{r}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T})}\leqslant\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}

if and only if
r^{2}\leqslant(p-1)/(q-1)
.
Moreover,

\|T_{r}f\|_{H^{q}(\mathbb{T})}\leqslant\|f\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{T})}

if and only if
r^{2}\leqslant p/q
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be the unit disk and
dA=dxdy/\pi
be the normalized area measure on
\mathbb{D}
.
Suppose that
0<p<\infty
and
1<\alpha<\infty,
the standard weighted Bergman space
A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
consists of analytic functions on the unit disk such that

||f||_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}dA_{\alpha}(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty,

where

dA_{\alpha}(z)=(\alpha-1)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha-2}dA(z).

Recall each analytic function
f
on
\mathbb{D}
has the following Taylor expansion at
0
:

f(z)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{+\infty}a_{n}z^{n},\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then the dilation operator can be formally written as

T_{r}f(z):=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{+\infty}a_{n}r^{n}z^{n}=f(rz),\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{D}.

In a recent work Melentijević
[
9
]
, he obtained the optimal hypercontractive constant
r_{0}=\sqrt{p/q}
such that

||T_{r}f||_{A_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{D})}\leqslant||f||_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})},\quad\forall 0<r\leqslant r_{0}

for
0<p\leqslant q<\infty
and
q\geqslant 2
.
Melentijević’s work
is motivated by Janson’s hypercontractive problem of dialtion operators on spaces of analytic functions. For background and recent developments on Janson’s problem, one can consult
[
6
]
and
[
9
]
.

The first goal of current paper is to extend Melentijević’s work to the case of two distinct weighted Bergman spaces
A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
and
A_{\beta}^{q}(\mathbb{D}).

Theorem 1.1
.

For
1<\alpha,\beta<\infty
and
0<p\leqslant q<\infty
, let
q\geqslant 2
and
\beta p\leqslant\alpha q
. Then we have

(1.1)

||T_{r}f||_{A_{\beta}^{q}(\mathbb{D})}\leqslant||f||_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})}

for any
f\in A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
if and only if
r^{2}\leqslant\beta p/\alpha q
.

Re
mark 1.1
.

It is known that
\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 1}\|f\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}=\|f\|_{H^{p}}
for
f\in H^{p}
.
Hence, Theorem
1.1
is a generalization of Janson’s strong hypecontractive inequalities between Hardy spaces in
[
5
]
for the case
0<p\leqslant q<\infty
and
q\geqslant 2
. The proofs of Theorem
1.1
are inspried by some ideas and methods from
[
6
]
and
[
9
]
.
After our paper was uploaded to arXiv, Huang Yi and Zhang Jianyang informed us that they had also obtained similar results.

Let
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n}]
be the space of complex polynomials on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\textbf{z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}

For a
m
-homogeneous polynomial
P(\textbf{z})\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n}]
, as a corollary of Weissler’s hypecontractive work
[
14
]
, we have

||P||_{H^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leqslant\left(\sqrt{\frac{q}{p}}\right)^{m}||P||_{H^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})},

where

||P||_{H^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|P(\textbf{z})|^{p}dm(\textbf{z})\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and
dm(\textbf{z})
is the Haar measure on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.
For a complex polynomial
P
in
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n}]
,
we write

P(\textbf{z})=\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{\gamma}\textbf{z}^{\gamma},

where
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
is a multi-index and
\textbf{z}^{\gamma}=z_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{\gamma_{n}}
.
Let
|\gamma|=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\gamma_{j}
.
Then the degree of the polynomial
P
is defined by

\text{deg}(P):=\max\{|\gamma|:c_{\gamma}\not=0\}.

Let
m
be the degree of the polynomial
P
. The famous Nikol’skiĭ-type inequality
[
10
,
11
]
states that for any complex polynomial
P
in
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n}]
and
0<q<p<\infty
,

\|P\|_{H^{q}(T^{n})}\leqslant C\|P\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})},

where the constant
C=C(n,m,p,q)
.

Using hypercontractive inequalities of the Poisson transform between Hardy spaces
[
14
]
, Defant and Mastyło
[
3
]

obtained a dimension-free estimate of Nikol’skiĭ-type inequality for Hardy spaces. In particular, for
0<p<q<\infty
, the optimal constant in Nikol’skiĭ-type inequality for Hardy spaces is

C(m,p,q)=\left(\sqrt{\frac{q}{p}}\right)^{m}.

For
n\geqslant 1,0<p<\infty
and
1<\alpha<\infty
, recall the weighted Bergman space
A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
on the polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n}
is

A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\left\{f\in H(\mathbb{D}^{n})\left|||f||_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}^{p}:=\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}|f(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})|^{p}dA_{\alpha}(z_{1})\cdots dA_{\alpha}(z_{n})<\infty\right.\right\},

where
H(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is the set of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
.
Inspried by ideas from
[
3
]
, we have the following dimensional free Nikol’skiĭ-type inequality for the weighted Bergman spaces with optimal constants.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
1<\alpha,\beta<\infty
and
0<p\leqslant q<\infty
, let
q\geqslant 2
and
\beta p\leqslant\alpha q.
Then for any positive integer
m,n
and a complex polynomial
P\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n}]
with degree
m

(1.2)

||P||_{A_{\beta}^{q}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\leqslant\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha q}{\beta p}}\right)^{m}||P||_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}.

The constant

C(\alpha,\beta,p,q)=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha q}{\beta p}}

is the best possible such that for each positive integers
n,m
and
P\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n}]
with degree
m,
the following inequality holds

||P||_{A_{\beta}^{q}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\leqslant C(\alpha,\beta,p,q)^{m}||P||_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}.

2.
The proof of Theorem
1.1

2.1.
The Necessity

We start with a simple but useful lemma in
[
5
]
to prove the necessity for Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
1<\alpha<\infty,0<p<\infty
and a real number
\varepsilon,
let
f(z)=1+\varepsilon z
. As the real number
\varepsilon
approaches
0
, we have

||f||_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})}=||1+\varepsilon z||_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})}=1+\frac{p}{4\alpha}\varepsilon^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3}).

Proof.

Fix
z\in\mathbb{C}
, then

\displaystyle|1+\varepsilon z|^{p}

\displaystyle=\left(1+2\varepsilon\text{Re}{z}+\varepsilon^{2}|z|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{p}{2}\left(2\varepsilon\text{Re}{z}+\varepsilon^{2}|z|^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{p}{2}\left(\frac{p}{2}-1\right)\left(2\varepsilon\text{Re}{z}+\varepsilon^{2}|z|^{2}\right)^{2}+O\left(\varepsilon^{3}\right).

Integrating on the unit disk with respect to the measure
dA_{\alpha}
, one gets

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|1+\varepsilon z|^{p}dA_{\alpha}(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|z|^{2}dA_{\alpha}(z)+\frac{p-2}{8}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(2\varepsilon\text{Re}{z}+\varepsilon^{2}|z|^{2}\right)^{2}dA_{\alpha}(z)+O(\varepsilon^{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}\left(||z||_{A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}+(p-2)||\text{Re}z||_{A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{p}{4\alpha}\varepsilon^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3}).

This completes the proof of Lemma
2.1
.
∎

For any
f\in A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
and
r\in[0,1)
, recall that

T_{r}f(z)=f(rz),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then, by Lemma
2.1
,

||1+\varepsilon rz||_{A_{\beta}^{q}(\mathbb{D})}=1+\frac{q}{4\beta}\varepsilon^{2}r^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3}).

Assume the hypercontractive inequality (
1.1
) holds, let
f(z)=1+\varepsilon z
, we have

\|1+\varepsilon rz\|_{A_{\beta}^{q}(\mathbb{D})}\leqslant\|1+\varepsilon z\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})},

Hence,

1+\frac{q}{4\beta}\varepsilon^{2}r^{2}\leqslant 1+\frac{p}{4\alpha}\varepsilon^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3}).

As
\varepsilon
approaches
0
, it follows that

r^{2}\leqslant\frac{\beta p}{\alpha q}.

This achieves the necessary part of Theorem
1.1
.

2.2.
The Sufficienty

For
0<p\leqslant q<\infty,
let

\beta^{\prime}=\frac{q}{p}\cdot\alpha,

then

\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\alpha}=\frac{q}{p}\geqslant 1.

By Kulikov’s inequality
[
6
, Corollary 1.3]
, it is easy to see

||f||_{A_{\beta^{\prime}}^{q}(\mathbb{D})}\leqslant||f||_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})}.

Since the
A_{\beta}^{q}(\mathbb{D})
norm of
T_{r}f
is decreasing as
r
is decreasing, it suffices to prove that there holds

||T_{r}f||_{A_{\beta}^{q}(\mathbb{D})}\leqslant||f||_{A_{\beta^{\prime}}^{q}(\mathbb{D})}

for
r=\sqrt{\beta p/\alpha q}.

It is equivalent to

(\beta-1)\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(rz)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta-2}dA(z)\leqslant(\beta^{\prime}-1)\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta^{\prime}-2}dA(z).

By the polar coordinates, we need to prove

\displaystyle(\beta-1)\int_{0}^{1}\rho(1-\rho^{2})^{\beta-2}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(r\rho e^{i\theta})|^{q}d\theta\right)d\rho

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle(\beta^{\prime}-1)\int_{0}^{1}\rho(1-\rho^{2})^{\beta^{\prime}-2}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(\rho e^{i\theta})|^{q}d\theta\right)d\rho.

Denote
\rho=\sqrt{y}
and

\Phi(y)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(\sqrt{y}e^{i\theta})|^{q}d\theta,

it is sufficient to verify

(\beta-1)\int_{0}^{1}(1-y)^{\beta-2}\Phi(r^{2}y)dy\leqslant(\beta^{\prime}-1)\int_{0}^{1}(1-y)^{\beta^{\prime}-2}\Phi(y)dy

when
r=\sqrt{\beta p/\alpha q}=\sqrt{\beta/\beta^{\prime}}.

Since
q\geqslant 2
, by
[
9
, Lemma 1]
,
\Phi
is
C^{2}
smooth and
\Phi^{\prime\prime}(y)\geqslant 0.

Using integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle(\beta-1)\int_{0}^{1}(1-y)^{\beta-2}\Phi(r^{2}y)dy

\displaystyle=r^{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-y)^{\beta-1}\Phi^{\prime}(r^{2}y)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}}{\beta}\Phi^{\prime}(0)+\frac{r^{4}}{\beta}\int_{0}^{1}(1-y)^{\beta}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(r^{2}y)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}\Phi^{\prime}(0)+\frac{r^{2}}{\beta^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-y)^{\beta}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(r^{2}y)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}\Phi^{\prime}(0)+\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{r^{2}}\left(1-\frac{y}{r^{2}}\right)^{\beta}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(y)dy,

and

\displaystyle(\beta^{\prime}-1)\int_{0}^{1}(1-y)^{\beta^{\prime}-2}\Phi(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}(1-y)^{\beta^{\prime}-1}\Phi^{\prime}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}\Phi^{\prime}(0)+\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-y)^{\beta^{\prime}}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(y)dy.

Then it suffices to prove

\int_{0}^{r^{2}}\left(1-\frac{y}{r^{2}}\right)^{\beta}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(y)dy\leqslant\int_{0}^{1}(1-y)^{\beta^{\prime}}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(y)dy.

Note that
\beta^{\prime}/\beta=\alpha q/\beta p\geqslant 1
, the function
g(y)=(1-y)^{\beta^{\prime}/\beta}
is convex.
Hence, for all
0\leqslant y\leqslant\beta/\beta^{\prime}=r^{2}
, we have

g(y)\geqslant g(0)+g^{\prime}(0)y=1-\frac{y}{r^{2}},

Note that
\Phi^{\prime\prime}(y)\geqslant 0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r^{2}}\left(1-\frac{y}{r^{2}}\right)^{\beta}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(y)dy

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{r^{2}}(1-y)^{\beta^{\prime}}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(y)dy

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{1}(1-y)^{\beta^{\prime}}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(y)dy.

This completes the whole proof.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Before proving Nikol’skiĭ-type inequality for the weighted Bergman spaces, we need a hypercontractive inequality for high dimensions.
Let
n
be a positive integer and
f\in A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Assume that
\textbf{z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
and
\textbf{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
with
0\leqslant r_{i}<1
for
i=1,\dots,n.

Define an operator

(\textbf{T}_{\textbf{r}}f)(\textbf{z})=f(r_{1}z_{1},\dots,r_{n}z_{n}),\quad\textbf{z}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}.

The following lemma is a corollary of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
1<\alpha,\beta<\infty
and
0<p\leqslant q<\infty
, let
q\geqslant 2
,
\beta p\leqslant\alpha q
, and
\textbf{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
with
0\leqslant r_{i}<1
for
i=1,\dots,n
. Then we have

(3.3)

||\textbf{T}_{\textbf{r}}f||_{A_{\beta}^{q}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\leqslant||f||_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}

for any
f\in A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
if and only if
r_{i}^{2}\leqslant\beta p/\alpha q,
for all
i=1,2,\dots,n.

Proof.

We prove the necessity first.
Assume that

||\textbf{T}_{\textbf{r}}f||_{A_{\beta}^{q}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\leqslant||f||_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}

for any
f\in A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.
For each
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
, let
g\in A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
and

f(\textbf{z})=f(z_{1},\dots,z_{i},\dots,z_{n}):=g(z_{i}),

then
f\in A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and

\|f\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}=\|g\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})}.

Moreover,

(\textbf{T}_{\textbf{r}}f)(\textbf{z})=(T_{r_{i}}g)(z_{i}).

Then we have

||T_{r_{i}}g||_{A_{\beta}^{q}(\mathbb{D})}\leqslant||g||_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})}.

By Theorem
1.1
, we get
r_{i}^{2}\leqslant\beta p/\alpha q
for each
i=1,2,\dots,n.

We proceed by induction on
n
to prove the sufficienty.
Suppose that
r_{i}^{2}\leqslant\beta p/\alpha q
for all
i=1,2,\dots,n
.
First, the inequality (
3.3
) is true for
n=1
by Theorem
1.1
.
Assume that lemma is valid for
n=k-1
.
We will prove the inequality (
3.3
) still holds when
n=k
.
Recall that

||\textbf{T}_{\textbf{r}}f||_{A_{\beta}^{q}(\mathbb{D}^{k})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}^{k}}|f(r_{1}z_{1},\dots,r_{k-1}z_{k-1},r_{k}z_{k})|^{q}dA_{\beta}(z_{1})\cdots dA_{\beta}(z_{k-1})dA_{\beta}(z_{k})\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

By Fubini Theorem and Theorem
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle||\textbf{T}_{\textbf{r}}f||_{A_{\beta}^{q}(\mathbb{D}^{k})}=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}^{k-1}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(r_{1}z_{1},\dots,r_{k-1}z_{k-1},r_{k}z_{k})|^{q}dA_{\beta}(z_{k})\right)dA_{\beta}(z_{1})\cdots dA_{\beta}(z_{k-1})\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}^{k-1}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(r_{1}z_{1},\dots,r_{k-1}z_{k-1},z_{k})|^{p}dA_{\beta}(z_{k})\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}dA_{\beta}(z_{1})\cdots dA_{\beta}(z_{k-1})\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Since
0<p\leqslant q<\infty
, by Minkowski’s inequality and the induction hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle||\textbf{T}_{\textbf{r}}f||_{A_{\beta}^{q}(\mathbb{D}^{k})}\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}^{k-1}}|f(r_{1}z_{1},\dots,r_{k-1}z_{k-1},z_{k})|^{q}dA_{\beta}(z_{1})\cdots dA_{\beta}(z_{k-1})\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dA_{\beta}(z_{k})\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}^{k-1}}|f(z_{1},\dots,z_{k-1},z_{k})|^{p}dA_{\beta}(z_{1})\cdots dA_{\beta}(z_{k-1})\right)dA_{\beta}(z_{k})\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||f||_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{k})}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

As a direct corollary of Lemma
3.1
, the inequality (
1.2
) holds for
m
-homogeneous polynomials. For given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define a new norm for
Q(\textbf{z},w)\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n},z_{n+1}].
Denote

||Q||_{A^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{n},\textbf{z})\times H^{p}(\mathbb{T},w)}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}|Q(\textbf{z},w)|^{p}dA_{\alpha}(\textbf{z})dw,

where
dA_{\alpha}(\textbf{z})=dA_{\alpha}(z_{1})\cdots dA_{\alpha}(z_{n})

and
dw
is the normalized arc length on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}.

The following homogeneous technique of general complex polynomials is known. However, we cannot locate a suitable reference. For the sake of completeness, we include a short proof.

Lemma 3.2
.

For a given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\textbf{z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n},\ P(\textbf{z})\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n}]
be a complex polynomial with degree
m
, then there exists an
m
-homogeneous polynomial
Q(\textbf{z},w)\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n},z_{n+1}]
such that

||P||_{A^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}=||Q||_{A^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{n},\textbf{z})\times H^{p}(\mathbb{T},w)}.

Proof.

For a polynomial
P
in
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n}]
,
write

P(\textbf{z})=\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{\gamma}\textbf{z}^{\gamma},

where
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
is a multi-index.
Let
\text{deg}\ P=m
and

Q(\textbf{z},w)=\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{\gamma}\textbf{z}^{\gamma}w^{m-|\gamma|}\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n},z_{n+1}],

where
|\gamma|=\gamma_{1}+\dots+\gamma_{n}.
Then
Q(\textbf{z},w)
is
m
-homogeneous.
For
0<p<\infty,
recall that

\displaystyle||Q||_{A^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{n},\textbf{z})\times H^{p}(\mathbb{T},w)}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}|Q(\textbf{z},w)|^{p}dA_{\alpha}(\textbf{z})dw

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}\left|\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{\gamma}\textbf{z}^{\gamma}w^{m-|\gamma|}\right|^{p}dA_{\alpha}(\textbf{z})dw.

Since
w\in\mathbb{T}
, let
\xi=(z_{1}w^{-1},\dots,z_{n}w^{-1}),
we have

\displaystyle||Q||_{A^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{n},\textbf{z})\times H^{p}(\mathbb{T},w)}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}\left|\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{\gamma}(z_{1}w^{-1})^{\gamma_{1}}\dots(z_{n}w^{-1})^{\gamma_{n}}\right|^{p}dA_{\alpha}(\textbf{z})dw

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}\left|\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{\gamma}\xi_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}\dots\xi_{n}^{\gamma_{n}}\right|^{p}dA_{\alpha}(\xi)dw

\displaystyle=||P||_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}^{p}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.2
.
Assume that

P(\textbf{z})=\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{\gamma}\textbf{z}^{\gamma}\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n}]

is a polynomial.
We divide the problem into two cases.
For the case
1<\alpha<\beta<\infty.

Let

q^{\prime}=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}q,

then
0<p\leqslant q^{\prime}<q<\infty
,
q^{\prime}/\alpha=q/\beta
.
By Kulikov’s inequality
[
6
, Corollary 1.3]
, one gets

||P||_{A_{\beta}^{q}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\leqslant||P||_{A_{\alpha}^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}.

Then it suffices to prove

||P||_{A_{\alpha}^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\leqslant\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha q}{\beta p}}\right)^{m}||P||_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}=\left(\sqrt{\frac{q^{\prime}}{p}}\right)^{m}||P||_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}

Let
r_{0}=\sqrt{p/q^{\prime}}<1
,
\textbf{r}_{0}=(r_{0},\dots,r_{0})
and

Q(\textbf{z},w)=\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{\gamma}\textbf{z}^{\gamma}w^{m-|\gamma|}\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n+1}]

be a
m
-homogeneous polynomial.
Since for each
w\in\mathbb{T}
,
Q(\textbf{z},w)\in A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.
By Lemma
3.1
and Minkowski’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle||\textbf{T}_{\textbf{r}_{0}}Q||_{A^{q^{\prime}}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{n},\textbf{z})\times H^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{T},w)}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}|Q(\textbf{r}_{0}\textbf{z},r_{0}w)|^{q^{\prime}}dA_{\beta}(\textbf{z})dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}|Q(\textbf{z},r_{0}w)|^{p}dA_{\beta}(\textbf{z})\right)^{\frac{q^{\prime}}{p}}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}|Q(\textbf{z},r_{0}w)|^{q^{\prime}}dw\right)^{\frac{p}{q^{\prime}}}dA_{\beta}(\textbf{z})\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

Since for each
\textbf{z}\in\mathbb{D}
,
Q(\textbf{z},w)\in H^{p}(\mathbb{T})
.
By Weissler’s work
[
14
]
, one gets

\displaystyle||\textbf{T}_{\textbf{r}_{0}}Q||_{A^{q^{\prime}}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{n},\textbf{z})\times H^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{T},w)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}|Q(\textbf{z},w)|^{p}dA_{\beta}(\textbf{z})dw\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=||Q||_{A^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{n},\textbf{z})\times H^{p}(\mathbb{T},w)}.

Note that
Q
is
m
-homogeneous, by Lemma
3.2
, we complete the proof of the case
1<\alpha<\beta<\infty.

For the case
1<\beta<\alpha<\infty,
let

r_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{\beta p}{\alpha q}}<\sqrt{\frac{p}{q}}<1.

With similar arguments in the last case, we have

\displaystyle||\textbf{T}_{\textbf{r}_{0}}Q||_{A^{q}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{n},\textbf{z})\times H^{q}(\mathbb{T},w)}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}|Q(\textbf{r}_{0}\textbf{z},r_{0}w)|^{q}dA_{\beta}(\textbf{z})dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}|Q(\textbf{z},r_{0}w)|^{p}dA_{\beta}(\textbf{z})\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}|Q(\textbf{z},r_{0}w)|^{q}dw\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dA_{\beta}(\textbf{z})\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}|Q(\textbf{z},w)|^{p}dA_{\beta}(\textbf{z})dw\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=||Q||_{A^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{n},\textbf{z})\times H^{p}(\mathbb{T},w)}.

This completes the proof of the case
1<\beta<\alpha<\infty
.

Now we show that the constant

C(\alpha,\beta,p,q)=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha q}{\beta p}}

is sharp.
Let
n
be a positive integer.
If one endows the polydisk
\mathbb{D}^{n}
with Borel
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, then we get a probability space
(\mathbb{D}^{n},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D}^{n}),dA_{\alpha}(z_{1})\dots dA_{\alpha}(z_{n}))
. Define

z_{i}:\mathbb{D}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\quad\textbf{z}\mapsto z_{i},\quad 1\leqslant i\leqslant n,

then
\{z_{i}|1\leqslant i\leqslant n\}
are independent identically distributed random variables. Consider the
m
-homogeneous polynomial

p_{\alpha,n}(\textbf{z})=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}

for given
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\geqslant 1.
By the central limit theorem
[
7
]
, the sequence of polynomials
p_{\alpha,n}
conversges in distributions to the normal complex Gaussian random variable
G
in distribution as
n
approaches
\infty.
Then for an fixed integer
m

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|p_{\alpha,n}^{m}\right\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}=\Gamma\left(\frac{mp}{2}+1\right).

Note that
p_{1,n}=p_{\alpha,n}/\sqrt{\alpha}
.
Therefore,

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|p_{1,n}^{m}\right\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right)^{m}\Gamma\left(\frac{mp}{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Similarly,

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|p_{1,n}^{m}\right\|_{A_{\beta}^{q}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}}\right)^{m}\Gamma\left(\frac{mq}{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Hence,

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{||p_{1,n}^{m}||_{A_{\beta}^{q}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}}{||p_{1,n}^{m}||_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}}=\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\right)^{m}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{qm}{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{pm}{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}.

By the Stirling’s formula

\sqrt{2\pi}x^{x+\frac{1}{2}}e^{-x}<\Gamma(x+1)<\sqrt{2\pi}x^{x+\frac{1}{2}}e^{-x+\frac{1}{12x}},

we have

\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2qm}}(\frac{qm}{2})^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2qm}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2pm}}(\frac{pm}{2})^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2pm}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{6pm}}}<\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{qm}{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{qm}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{pm}{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{pm}}}<\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2pm}}(\frac{pm}{2})^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2pm}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2qm}}(\frac{qm}{2})^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2qm}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{6qm}}}

Note that

\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2qm}}(\frac{qm}{2})^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2qm}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2pm}}(\frac{pm}{2})^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2pm}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{6pm}}}=e^{\frac{1}{6pm}}\frac{(qm\pi)^{\frac{1}{2qm}}}{(pm\pi)^{\frac{1}{2pm}}}\sqrt{\frac{q}{p}},

and

\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}(qm)^{\frac{1}{qm}}=\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}e^{\frac{1}{qm}\ln qm}=1.

It follows that

\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{qm}{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{qm}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{pm}{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{pm}}}=\sqrt{\frac{q}{p}}.

Consequently,

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{||p_{1,n}^{m}||_{A_{\beta}^{q}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}}{||p_{1,n}^{m}||_{A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}}=\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha q}{\beta p}}\right)^{m}.

Therefore, the constant

C(\alpha,\beta,p,q)=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha q}{\beta p}}

is sharp.
Then the whole proof is complete now.

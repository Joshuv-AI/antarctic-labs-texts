Meflex: A Multi-agent Scaffolding System for Entrepreneurial Ideation Iteration via Nonlinear Business Plan Writing
Lan Luo, Dongyijie Primo Pan, Junhua Zhu, Muzhi Zhou, Pan Hui

Abstract
Business plan (BP) writing plays a key role in entrepreneurship education by helping learners construct, evaluate, and iteratively refine their ideas. However, conventional BP writing remains a rigid, linear process that often fails to reflect the dynamic and recursive nature of entrepreneurial ideation. This mismatch is particularly challenging for novice entrepreneurial students, who struggle with the substantial cognitive demands of developing and refining ideas. While reflection and meta-reflection are critical strategies for fostering divergent and convergent thinking, existing writing tools rarely scaffold these higher-order processes. To address this gap, we present the Meflex System, a large language model (LLM)-based writing tool that integrates BP writing scaffolding with a nonlinear idea canvas to support iterative ideation through reflection and meta-reflection. We report findings from an exploratory user study with 30 participants that examined the system’s usability and cognitive impact. Results show that Meflex effectively scaffolds BP writing, promotes divergent thinking through LLM-supported reflection, and enhances meta-reflective awareness while reducing cognitive load during complex idea development. These findings highlight the potential of non-linear LLM-based writing tools to foster deeper and coherent entrepreneurial thinking.

Meflex System

3.1
System Overview

The Meflex system interface in Fig.
1
is designed to support business plan writing through the scaffolding, consisting of three primary sections:
the ideation canvas
,
the structure writing workspace
, and
the LLM assistant panel
.

Figure 1:
Three primary sections: the idea iteration canvas, the structural writing workspace, and the LLM assistant panel.

The
ideation canvas
(Fig.
1
, Left) allows users to progressively develop their entrepreneurial ideas through a visual workspace. Once an idea card is created, users can double-click it to open the
structure writing workspace
(Fig.
1
(C)), which provides section-specific scaffolding for composing the business plan. This workspace offers predefined structural modules (e.g., User Pain Points, Market Analysis, Funding Plan), each accompanied by LLM-assisted prompts and examples to guide writing in the
LLM assistant panel
(Fig.
1
, Right). In parallel, each LLM response is followed by a reflective question (Fig.
1
(D)) that encourages users to reconsider assumptions, explore alternatives, or extend their ideas beyond the current formulation. These reflective prompts are designed to stimulate divergent thinking and often lead users to create new branches or revisions in the ideation canvas.

Users can create new idea cards by extending an existing one either horizontally or vertically. A horizontal extension denotes incremental refinement of the same idea. For instance, extending from Node 1 to Node 2 copies the content of Node 1 into Node 2, enabling users to revise and elaborate on their original thinking in a continuous manner. A vertical extension denotes a conceptual branching from the same origin. For example, when both Node 2 and Node 3 are created from Node 1, they inherit its content and develop in parallel, enabling users to explore different variations or alternative approaches to the original idea.

As multiple idea nodes accumulate on the canvas, it becomes increasingly important to help users track how their thoughts have developed. Each idea card includes a meta-reflection segment (Fig.
1
(A)), automatically generated by the LLM, explaining how the current idea evolved from earlier iterations. In the LLM panel (Fig.
1
(D)), users can engage in reflective dialogue to revise or deepen this meta-cognitive insight. By surfacing connections across branches and highlighting the rationale behind changes, the system encourages users to monitor, evaluate, and refine their reasoning while maintaining a coherent understanding of both incremental progress and conceptual shifts.

3.2
Multi-Agents Roles and Prompts

To support entrepreneurial ideation, we integrate five key scaffolding strategies
[
25
]
into system design: (1) Guidance, offering structured steps, prompts, and analytic tools (e.g., TAM, SWOT); (2) Contextualization, using LLMs to flexibly initiate and develop business ideas from diverse entry points by embedding them into meaningful entrepreneurial contexts; (3) Engagement, fostering motivation through interactive and immersive feedback
[
8
]
; (4) Interaction, enabling multi-turn dialogues to refine ideas
[
44
]
; and (5) Reflection, enhancing metacognitive awareness through prompts and summaries
[
42

. These scaffolds guide learners from idea generation to refinement, and are operationalized through dedicated LLM-powered agent modules.

Building on these scaffolds, the system adopts a multi-agent LLM framework, where each business plan section is supported by a specialized agent offering role-specific prompts and guidance. This modular design enables context-aware assistance and diversified perspectives throughout the writing process. All LLM functionalities are powered by the DeepSeek API
[
29

, which provides high-performance language modeling to support real-time, flexible generation and interaction. Table

4
Method

4.1
Participant

We recruited 30 participants from the university (labeled as P1 to P30) through an open call distributed via an online questionnaire. Participation was entirely voluntary, and the study aimed to assess the effectiveness of our system in business plan writing and meta-reflection. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the university’s Institutional Review Board (IRB). All participants provided informed consent prior to the study, and their responses were anonymized for analysis.

Among them, 17 identified as male and 13 as female. The average age was 22.83 years (SD = 4.98). Participants came from diverse academic backgrounds, including majors such as Communication and Media Arts, Artificial Intelligence, Human-Computer Interaction, Data Science and Analytics, Psychology, and Industrial Engineering. In terms of academic level, participants ranged from undergraduate Year 1 to postgraduate Year 9. Most were in the early years of undergraduate study, with Year 1 (n = 10) being the largest group. Regarding entrepreneurial experience, 21 participants had no prior startup experience, while 9 reported some level of experience.

4.2
Testing Procedure and Data Analysis

The user study was divided into three phases:
Pre-Study
,
In-Study
, and
Post-Study
. In the
Pre-Study phase
, participants completed a demographic questionnaire and received a 10-minute Zoom demo covering core features such as node manipulation on the
idea canvas
, initiating reflection with the LLM assistant, and reviewing meta-reflections. During the
In-Study phase
, participants selected one of ten predefined entrepreneurial topics (e.g., eco apps, travel planning, e-bike sharing) and spent 40–60 minutes using the system to develop and refine business ideas. In the
Post-Study phase
, participants completed a Likert-scale questionnaire on scaffolding and interaction design, followed by a 10–15 minute interview for qualitative feedback. For
data analysis
, we conducted thematic analysis of the interview transcripts. Two researchers independently coded the data, identifying key themes such as "Scaffolding Support", "Reflection Engagement", and so on. They refined a shared codebook through discussion, resolving disagreements in team meetings. This analysis revealed patterns in idea development, flexible writing structure, and reflective system use.

Findings

5.1
How usable and effective does the Meflex system scaffold users in writing a business plan?

To evaluate the usability of the LLM-supported writing scaffolding, the Meflex was evaluated using the standardized System Usability Scale (SUS)
[
6
]
, which employs a 7-point Likert format, focusing on three dimensions:
guidance
,
contextualization
, and
engagement
(1 = Strongly Disagree, 7 = Strongly Agree). The Figure

illustrates the actual score ranges (from minimum to maximum) as horizontal bars, with black dots marking the mean score for each item. The "mean ± standard deviation" labels further indicate the central tendency and variability across participants.

Overall, the results reveal consistently positive user perceptions, with most items receiving mean scores between 5.5 and 6.5 on a 7-point Likert scale. Items such as Q1, Q2, Q3, and Q10 achieved the highest mean scores (all above 5.8), indicating strong perceived effectiveness and clarity in system interaction. And Q4 received slightly lower mean scores and exhibited wider score ranges, suggesting greater variability in user experiences for those aspects. This may point to potential areas for further improvement in system design or communication strategies. Despite these variations, standard deviations remained relatively low (typically below 0.7), indicating a high level of agreement among participants. Together with the generally narrow rating ranges, this suggests the system delivered a stable and consistent usability experience across users.

User Satisfaction with Scaffolding

Furthermore, qualitative feedback reinforces these quantitative findings. Participants (P2, P5, P8, P11, P14, P19, P25) frequently described the system’s scaffolding as instrumental in managing the complexity of writing a business plan. In particular, the combination of a to-do list interface and LLM-based dialogue support helped reduce uncertainty, sustain engagement, and enable iterative refinement (P5, P19, P25). The to-do list provided clear structure and broke down the task into manageable units (P2, P5, P14). As P8 noted,
"The to-do list made me feel I had a roadmap… it didn’t overwhelm me, and I could pick up right where I left off."
Similarly, P7 remarked,
"It felt manageable because I could focus on one thing at a time. The list kept me on track."
These comments suggest that structured guidance made the writing process more approachable, especially for users unfamiliar with business planning.

Complementing the structured guidance, the LLM assistant provided adaptive support that accommodated users with varying levels of experience (P7, P12, P16, P21). Whether participants were familiar with business plan writing or not, the assistant offered tailored prompts and follow-up questions that adjusted to their current progress and input. For example, P21 with less experienced participant shared,
"I didn’t know much about business plans, but the assistant broke things down for me and asked simple questions that helped me understand what to write."
Meanwhile, P16 with more confident user described:
"I already had a basic plan in mind, and the assistant pushed me further—it kept asking questions that made me rethink my assumptions."

These examples show that the system could offer both foundational guidance and deeper prompts, helping users engage at their own level and supporting both comprehension and critical thinking.

5.2
How does non-linear writing in Meflex system influences entrepreneurial ideation?

The non-linear writing design in the Meflex system—particularly the ability to revisit, refine, and reorganize ideas across multiple stages—proved central to supporting entrepreneurial ideation. Rather than enforcing a rigid, step-by-step structure, the system allowed users to navigate freely across modules and return to earlier thoughts, fostering a more dynamic and iterative creative process (P9, P11, P15, P25). Participants (P9, P25) reported that this modular organization helped them distinguish between ideas that were conceptually parallel and those that represented progressive refinement. P9 noted that separating early assumptions into different nodes clarified their relationships:
"I could clearly see which parts were alternatives and which ones were building on each other. That made it easier to decide what to keep and what to revise"
.

In addition to segmenting content, the node-based structure made the evolution of ideas more traceable (P8, P11, P14, P26). Participants could visually identify how one node branched into another (e.g., Node 2 leading to Node 4), revealing how their thinking developed over time. This visibility prompted self-reflection and recursive refinement. As P14 noted,
"When I looked back at how Node 4 came from Node 2, I realized my thinking had shifted—I could see that change clearly."
P11 echoed this, stating,
"Seeing the path helped me understand what triggered each idea. It wasn’t random—I could trace the logic."
Similarly, P8 shared,
"The branching view let me retrace my steps. I noticed how earlier thoughts shaped what came next, and sometimes that made me go back and revise."
P26 added,
"It felt like watching my idea grow, not just listing points. I saw where things connected or didn’t, and fixed them."
Together, these reflections illustrate how the visualized structure of non-linear writing supported users in monitoring their ideation process and engaging in meaningful revision.

5.3
How do GPT-generated reflection prompts and meta-reflection summaries support entrepreneurial ideation?

Participants (P11, P18, P21) found GPT-generated reflection prompts valuable in encouraging deeper self-examination of their ideas. These prompts guided users to assess the validity, completeness, and consistency of their thinking, prompting them to question assumptions and consider alternative perspectives. As P26 noted,
"The questions made me pause and think if my solution really solves the problem I described. I wouldn’t have done that on my own."
This kind of reflective scaffolding pushed participants beyond surface-level idea generation. P10 similarly shared,
"It kept nudging me—‘have you thought about who really needs this?’ That helped me sharpen my target audience."
Rather than offering answers, the assistant acted as a metacognitive partner, supporting users in iteratively refining their ideas through structured self-questioning.

Beyond local reflection, meta-reflection summaries also helped participants make sense of how different nodes and ideas were connected across their business plan drafts (P3, P12, P16). By synthesizing content across modules, these summaries offered a high-level overview that made the structure of their thinking more visible and easier to navigate. Several participants (P1, P3, P16) reported that the summaries encouraged them to revisit and refine earlier inputs based on new insights. For example, P15 noted,
"After reading the summary, I realized my customer segment and value proposition didn’t quite match. That made me go back and rewrite part of it."
By surfacing potential inconsistencies, the system allowed users to focus their attention where revisions were needed, instead of manually cross-checking sections. Instead of manually tracing connections between sections, users could rely on the meta-reflection to highlight inconsistencies or gaps. P30 similarly reflected,
"It reminded me that everything should connect—it wasn’t just writing section by section."
In this way, meta-reflections served as cognitive scaffolds that not only reduced users’ mental load but also supported the integration of modular content into a coherent entrepreneurial narrative.

6
Discussion and Future Work

This study introduces Meflex, a novel human-AI co-writing system that reimagines the ideation process through nonlinear writing. By enabling users to externalize and iterate on modular components of a business plan, Meflex supports entrepreneurial ideation as a recursive, flexible, and reflective process. The system combines structured scaffolding with adaptive LLM assistance, allowing users to compose, revise, and relate ideas across modular nodes. Through a mixed-method evaluation, including usability metrics and qualitative interviews, we demonstrate that this hybrid approach helps users manage cognitive load, deepen idea development, and maintain global coherence in their writing.

One key contribution of Meflex is its ability to support ideation through a combination of structure and flexibility. The system enables users to concentrate on individual sections while remaining aware of the overall logic of the business narrative. Meta-reflection summaries reinforce this process by synthesizing information across nodes, identifying inconsistencies, and prompting users to make targeted revisions. However, as participant P14 noted, the current meta-reflection feature focuses only on tracking changes in written text and does not yet capture the interactions between users and the LLM. This highlights a potential direction for future improvement. By analyzing the content and flow of user–LLM discussions, the system could generate richer insights into how ideas evolve and where LLM support plays the most significant role.

In sum, Meflex advances a new paradigm for AI-assisted writing, one that treats ideation as a nonlinear, reflective, and dialogic process. By blending modular composition with adaptive feedback, the system scaffolds entrepreneurial thinking beyond surface-level content generation. Future iterations may enhance this model by capturing conversational trajectories and offering more interactive meta-cognitive support.

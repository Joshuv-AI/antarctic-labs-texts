Comment on CHX@ZnO-HA/PSSS nanocomposites for osteomyelitis
Authors: Madhuri Tribhuvan, Shashikant N. Phatke
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with great interest the recent study by Indirakumari V and Sakthilatha D, which described the development of a multifunctional CHX@ZnO-HA/PSSS nanocomposite for osteomyelitis management.1 The integration of antibacterial activity with osteogenic support represents an important translational direction for biomaterial-based infection control. The authors should be commended for combining physicochemical characterization with biological validation and drug-release analyses in a unified therapeutic platform. Nevertheless, several methodological and translational considerations may further refine interpretation of the reported findings.

A major concern relates to the biological modeling strategy used to establish osteogenic efficacy. The study relied exclusively on MG-63 osteosarcoma-derived cells to evaluate proliferation, migration, and osteogenic differentiation. Although MG-63 cells are convenient for preliminary screening, their altered metabolic activity and atypical differentiation behavior may exaggerate osteogenic responses when compared with primary human osteoblasts or mesenchymal stromal cells.2 This issue becomes particularly relevant because the reported increases in BMP2, RUNX2, ALP, and OCN expression were interpreted as evidence of robust regenerative potential. Without validation in primary-cell systems or co-culture environments incorporating inflammatory macrophages and bacterial biofilms, the biological behavior of the scaffold under clinically relevant osteomyelitic conditions remains uncertain. Such overestimation may affect translational predictability, particularly because chronic osteomyelitis develops within an immunologically complex microenvironment characterized by persistent cytokine activation and impaired vascularity.

The interpretation of the antimicrobial performance also warrants additional scrutiny. The antibacterial analyses were limited to colony-count reduction assays against planktonic Staphylococcus aureus and Escherichia coli, whereas chronic osteomyelitis is primarily sustained by biofilm-associated bacterial communities. Biomaterials that demonstrate efficacy against planktonic organisms frequently exhibit diminished activity after biofilm maturation due to impaired drug penetration and altered bacterial metabolism.3 Moreover, the release experiments were performed predominantly at physiological pH 7.4 despite the study's central premise of pH-responsive behavior under inflammatory acidic conditions. Because infected bone environments often exhibit acidic pH values below 6.5, the absence of comparative release kinetics and antibacterial performance under acidic conditions limits confidence regarding the scaffold's responsiveness during active infection. Quantitative biofilm-disruption assays and dynamic release modeling across inflammatory pH gradients would therefore strengthen the mechanistic claims of localized infection control.

The mechanical and surgical applicability of the nanocomposite also remains insufficiently characterized. Osteomyelitis-associated bone defects are frequently exposed to substantial mechanical stress, particularly in load-bearing regions. Although mineralization and apatite formation were demonstrated convincingly, the study did not evaluate compressive strength, fatigue resistance, degradation stability, or scaffold integrity after prolonged immersion. These parameters are clinically important because premature structural degradation may compromise osseointegration and create particulate debris capable of perpetuating inflammation. In addition, sustained chlorhexidine exposure may alter local cellular signaling and extracellular matrix maturation over longer implantation periods despite favorable short-term cytocompatibility.4 Incorporating biomechanical testing and long-duration degradation studies would therefore improve assessment of the scaffold's feasibility for orthopedic reconstruction.

Despite these considerations, the study provides an important contribution toward multifunctional biomaterials capable of simultaneously addressing infection control and bone regeneration. The integration of ZnO-mediated antibacterial activity with hydroxyapatite-supported osteoconduction represents a promising framework for future osteomyelitis therapies. Further validation in biofilm-based infection models, mechanically dynamic systems, and clinically representative in vivo environments may help clarify the translational role of this nanocomposite in reconstructive bone surgery.

Abstract
. For a non-zero algebraic number α \alpha of degree d d , let h ⁡ ( α ) h(\alpha) denote its logarithmic Weil height. It is known that when h ⁡ ( α ) h(\alpha) is small, and d d is large, the conjugates of α \alpha are clustered near the unit circle and have angular equidistribution in the complex plane about the origin. In this paper, we establish a p p -adic analogue of this result by obtaining lower bounds for h ⁡ ( α ) h(\alpha) in terms of the number of its conjugates that lie in a finite extension of ℚ p \mathbb{Q}_{p} , for some prime p p . As a consequence, we prove Lehmer’s conjecture for all α \alpha such that ≫ d ​ log ⁡ d \gg\sqrt{d\log d} many of its conjugates lie in a finite extension of ℚ p \mathbb{Q}_{p} .

1.
Introduction

Let
h
denote the absolute logarithmic Weil height on algebraic numbers. It is a fundamental tool in Diophantine geometry and arithmetic dynamics. It provides a means of quantifying the arithmetic complexity of algebraic numbers. Analytically, it connects to potential theory on Berkovich spaces, and geometrically, it encodes the distribution of algebraic points on varieties.

A classical result by Northcott
[
16
]
states that only finitely many algebraic numbers have bounded degree and bounded height. Complementing this, a theorem of Kronecker
[
10
]
characterizes the algebraic numbers of height zero as precisely the roots of unity. The fundamental example
h(2^{1/d})=(\log 2)/d
illustrates that the height can become arbitrarily small as one ranges over algebraic numbers that are not roots of unity.

We fix
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
to be the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}
within
\mathbb{C}
, and henceforth, all algebraic extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
are considered as subfields of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. Let
\mu_{\infty}
denote the set of all roots of unity in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. A central open problem in the study of heights is Lehmer’s conjecture
[
11
]
, which proposes a uniform lower bound on the height of non-torsion algebraic numbers.

Conjecture 1.1

(Lehmer)
.

There exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that

(1)

h(\alpha)>\frac{C}{[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}]}

for all non-zero
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\mu_{\infty}
.

Lehmer’s conjecture was originally formulated in terms of the Mahler measure of polynomials and is frequently referred to as
Lehmer’s problem
in the literature. While the conjecture remains unsolved in general, it has been settled for various classes of algebraic numbers. For a comprehensive account of this problem, the reader is referred to the excellent survey articles
[
20
]
and
[
21
]
.

The example discussed earlier, where
h(2^{1/d})=(\log 2)/d
, shows that the bound in (
1
) is optimal. Nevertheless, when we restrict ourselves to special classes of algebraic numbers, it becomes possible to establish significantly stronger lower bounds on the height. For instance, consider the class of totally real algebraic numbers-those whose Galois conjugates all lie in
\mathbb{R}
under any choice of embedding
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
. For such numbers Schinzel
[
19
]
proved in 1973 that the height is bounded below by an absolute constant independent of the degree,

h(\alpha)\geq\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right),

for all
\alpha\neq 0,\pm 1
that are totally real.

This bound reflects a deeper geometric rigidity: totally real numbers cannot have all its conjugates very close to
\pm 1
. More generally, one can establish lower bounds on the height of algebraic numbers, whose conjugates are restricted to a certain region of the complex plane. The following result due to Mignotte
[
15
]
quantitatively captures how the angular distribution of the conjugates relates to the height.

Theorem

(
[
13
]
, Theorem 15.2)
.

For
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, let
d=[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}]
. For any
\theta
with
0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi
the number
n
of conjugates of
\alpha
in any fixed sector, based at the origin, of angle
\theta
satisfies

(2)

\left|n-\frac{\theta}{2\pi}d\right|\leq 24\left(d^{2/3}\left(\log 2d\right)^{1/3}+dh(\alpha)^{1/3}\right).

This result can be viewed as a quantitative refinement of a classical theorem due to Erdős and Turán
[
6
]
on the distribution of roots of complex polynomials. They showed that if
f(z)\in\mathbb{C}[z]
has small maximum modulus on the unit circle
|z|=1
, its roots are concentrated near the unit circle and exhibit near-uniform angular distribution.

In 1997, Bilu
[
2
]
proved a beautiful result on the equidistribution of Galois conjugates in the complex plane for points with small height. Roughly speaking, if
\alpha
is an algebraic number of height close to zero, then the Galois conjugates of
\alpha
are equidistributed along the unit circle in the sense of weak convergence. Subsequently, a quantitative version of Bilu’s theorem was established by Petsche
[
17
]
, and generalized by Favre and Rivera-Letelier
[
7
]
(see also the corrigendum
[
8
]
).

The goal of this article is to realise the above phenomenon in the non-archimedean setting. Let
p
be a rational prime. Denote by
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
the field of
p
-adic numbers. We fix an algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and shall assume that all the algebraic extensions of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
are subfields of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
. Further, for each prime
p
, we fix an embedding
i_{p}\,:\,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
. We say an algebraic number
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}

lies in an extension
K
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
if
i_{p}(\alpha)\in K
. For ease of notation, we will identify
i_{p}(\alpha)
and
\alpha
, when
\alpha\in K
. Viewing
\mathbb{R}
as the completion of
\mathbb{Q}
at the archimedean place, one can define a non-archimedean analogue of totally real numbers, called the totally
p
-adic numbers. An algebraic number
\alpha
is said to be
totally
p
-adic
if its image lies in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
for any embedding
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}_{p}
, where
\mathbb{C}_{p}
denotes the completion of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
.

A recent theorem of Pottmeyer
[
18
]
provides a
p
-adic analogue of Schinzel’s result for totally real numbers. He showed that if
\alpha
is totally
p
-adic and not a
(p-1)
-th root of unity,

h(\alpha)\geq\frac{\log(p/2)}{p+1}.

More generally, let
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a finite extension with ramification index
e
and inertia degree
f
. Let
S
be the set of algebraic numbers all of whose Galois conjugates lie in
K
. Then, a result of Bombieri and Zannier
[
3
, Theorem 2]
implies that

\liminf_{\alpha\in S}h(\alpha)\geq\frac{\log p}{2e(p^{f}+1)}.

A generalized equidistribution theorem due to Baker and Rumely
[
1
, Theorem 2.3]
provides a non-Archimedean analogue of Bilu’s equidistribution theorem, formulated using the framework of Berkovich spaces. It is important to note that the aforementioned result requires all the Galois conjugates to lie in a fixed local field. Therefore, it is natural to study the case when only a proportion of conjugates lie in a fixed local field of small degree. It follows from a recent work of the authors
[
5
]
that when a positive proportion of the Galois conjugates of
\alpha
lie in a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, the height
h(\alpha)
is bounded below by an absolute constant, independent of the degree. More precisely, let
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a finite extension with residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and suppose
\psi_{q}
is the proportion of Galois conjugates of
\alpha
in
K
. Then

h(\alpha)\gg\psi_{q}\,\frac{\log q}{q+1}.

It is important to note that the quantity
\psi_{q}
is originally defined in
[
5
]
as the proportion of prime ideals with norm
q
in
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
, but it is not difficult to see that it coincides with the proportion of conjugates of
\alpha
lying in a local field
K
with residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

From (
2
), we can conclude that algebraic numbers of large degree, whose conjugates are not angularly equidistributed in the complex plane, are forced to have large height. In other words, for all
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfying

\left|n-\frac{\theta}{2\pi}d\right|\gg\left(d^{2}\log d\right)^{1/3},

with the implied constant
>24
, Lehmer’s conjecture holds. The main goal of this paper is to establish a
p
-adic analogue of the above phenomenon. In particular, we prove Lehmer’s conjecture for a class of algebraic numbers by quantifying how many conjugates must lie in a fixed local field.

We now set up the notation necessary to state our results. Let
K
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\mu_{\infty}
be a non-zero algebraic number of degree
d
, and
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{d}
be the Galois conjugates of
\alpha
in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. Define

\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)=\left\{\,\,\alpha_{i}\,\,|\,\,\alpha_{i}\in K,\,\,1\leq i\leq d\right\}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
K
be as above. Then, for a non-zero
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}

h(\alpha)\geq\,\frac{1}{2\,[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}\,\left(\frac{|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|}{d}\right)^{2}\frac{\log q}{q+1}\,\,+\,\,o_{d}(1),

where
o_{d}(1)
tends to
0
as
d
tends to infinity.

The method of proof of the above theorem can be used to derive the following consequences.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
K
be as above. For
c>0
, consider the set

S:=\left\{\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\mu_{\infty}\,\,:\,\,|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|\ \ \geq\ \ \sqrt{\left(\frac{2(q+1)\,[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}{\log q}d\right)\,\,\left(c+\frac{\log{(qd)}}{2}\right)}\right\}.

Then,
h(\alpha)\geq\frac{c}{d}
for all
\alpha\in S
and hence Lehmer’s conjecture holds for
S
.

This proves that a condition much weaker than “positive proportion” of conjugates in
K
is sufficient to establish Lehmer’s conjecture. In particular, it is enough to show that
\gg\sqrt{d\log d}
many conjugates of
\alpha
lie in a fixed local field, with a suitable implied constant. As a corollary of the above proposition, we have the following.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
K
be as above. Suppose

S:=\left\{\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{\mu_{\infty}\}\,\,:\,\,|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|\geq\sqrt{q^{3}[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\,d\log d}\right\}.

Then, Lehmer’s conjecture holds for
S
. In fact,
d\cdot h(\alpha)\to\infty
as
d\to\infty
.

An archimedean analog in the spirit of Theorem
1.2
with a condition on the number of real conjugates of
\alpha
was obtained by Garza in
[
9
]
using Schinzel’s method.

We now generalize our setup to capture the behaviour of conjugates spread across multiple local fields over several primes. Define

\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}:=\{\,\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha_{i})\,\,|\,\,1\leq i\leq d\,\},

as the set of
distinct
finite extensions of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
generated by the Galois conjugates of
\alpha
. For each
K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}
, denote by
e_{K}
and
f_{K}
the ramification index and inertia degree of
K
, respectively,
n_{K}=[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]
and
\mathbb{F}_{q_{K}}
the residue field.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\mu_{\infty}
be a non-zero algebraic integer. Then,

h(\alpha)\geq\frac{1}{2}\,\,{{\sum}}_{p}\,\,\operatorname{max}\left\{0,\,\,\left(\frac{1}{\sum\limits_{K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}}e_{K}\,q_{K}}-\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}}\,\,\frac{|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|}{e_{K}}\right)\right\}\log p\,-\frac{\log d}{2d}.

Remark.
For a totally
p-
adic algebraic integer, not a root of unity, Theorem
1.5
implies that

h(\alpha)\geq\frac{\log p}{2p}\operatorname{max}\left\{0,\,\,\left(1-\frac{p}{d}\right)\right\}-\frac{\log d}{2d}.

Hence, by Northcott’s theorem (Theorem
2.1
)

\liminf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}^{tp}}}h(\alpha)\geq\frac{\log p}{2p},

where
\mathbb{Q}^{tp}
is the maximal totally
p
-adic field over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}^{tp}}
its ring of integers. Hence, we obtain a weaker version of Pottmeyer’s result.

It is important to note that our method can be used to extend Theorem
1.5
to algebraic numbers at the cost of a slightly weaker lower bound. Since Lehmer’s conjecture is the theme of this article, we restrict ourselves to algebraic integers, as Lehmer’s conjecture trivially holds for algebraic non-integers.

As a consequence of the above theorem, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\mu_{\infty}
be a non-zero algebraic integer. Then

h(\alpha)\geq\frac{1}{2}{{\sum}}_{p}\operatorname{max}\left\{0,\,\,\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{{|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|}^{2}}{q_{K}}-|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|\right)\,\,\frac{\log q_{K}}{n_{K}}\right\}-\frac{\log d}{2d}.

In other words, if conjugates of
\alpha
lie in several local fields
K
with small inertia degree, then we obtain a meaningful lower bound for
h(\alpha)
. This can be compared with a theorem of Mignotte
[
14
]
, which states that if there exists a prime
p\leq d\log d
, which splits completely in
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
, then Lehmer’s conjecture holds for such
\alpha
. An improvement due to Silverman (alluded to in the literature but unpublished) states that if there are
d
distinct prime ideals in
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
with norm
\leq\sqrt{d\log d}
, then
h(\alpha)\geq c/d
for some
c>0
. Indeed, if there are several primes with small norms in
F=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
, by Weil’s explicit formula, one can obtain a sharper bound on the absolute discriminant
|\Delta(F/\mathbb{Q})|
. For
\alpha
an algebraic integer, we have
\Delta(F/\mathbb{Q})
divides
D(m_{\alpha})
, the discriminant of the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
. Thus, Mahler’s inequality (Theorem
2.2
) can be applied and one obtains a lower bound on
h(\alpha)
. This phenomenon is explicitly demonstrated by the authors in
[
4
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some definitions and results which will be used towards the proof of our theorems. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
be a non-zero algebraic number of degree
d
and
m_{\alpha}(x)=a_{d}x^{d}+\cdots+a_{1}x+a_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
. Then, its Mahler measure is defined as

M(\alpha):=|a_{n}|\prod_{i}\max(1,|\alpha_{i}|),

where
\alpha_{i}
’s denote the Galois conjugates of
\alpha
. The logarithmic Weil height of
\alpha
, in terms of the Mahler measure, is thus defined as

h(\alpha)=\frac{\log M(\alpha)}{d}\,.

The Weil height gives a partial ordering on algebraic numbers with bounded degree. This follows from a classical result of Northcott
[
16
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Northcott)
.

For any fixed positive integers
d
and

H
, there are only finitely many algebraic numbers
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
of degree at most
d
and height at most
H
.

Recall that the discriminant of a polynomial
f(x)=b_{n}x^{n}+b_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\ldots+b_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
is an integer given by

D(f)={b_{n}}^{2n-2}\prod_{i>j}(\beta_{i}-\beta_{j})^{2},

where
\beta_{i}
’s are all roots of
f
in
\mathbb{C}
. To connect the Weil height of an algebraic number with the discriminant of its minimal polynomial, we shall use Mahler’s inequality
[
12
]
.

Theorem 2.2

(Mahler)
.

Let
\alpha
be a non-zero algebraic number of degree
d
with minimal polynomial
m_{\alpha}(x)
in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
. Then,

|D(m_{\alpha})|\leq d^{d}M(\alpha)^{2d-2}.

Therefore, using the definition of Weil height, we obtain

(3)

h(\alpha)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\log|D(m_{\alpha})|}{d^{2}}-\frac{\log d}{d}\right).

3.
Proof of the main theorems

Let
K
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with ramification index
e
and inertia degree
f
. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
denote the residue field of
K
, where
q=p^{f}
. We define
\nu
to be the unique valuation on
K
extending the usual
p
-adic valuation on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Let
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\mu_{\infty}
be a non-zero algebraic number of degree
d
, and
\alpha_{i}
’s be the Galois conjugates of
\alpha
in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. Recall that

\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)=\left\{\,\,\alpha_{i}\,\,|\,\,\alpha_{i}\in K,\,\,1\leq i\leq d\right\}.

Our proof is inspired by the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 2]
due to Bombieri and Zannier.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
m_{\alpha}(x)
be the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
. Consider
L
to be the splitting field of
m_{\alpha}(x)
over
K
and
\omega
be the unique valuation on
L
extending
\nu
on
K_{\nu}
. Rearrange the conjugates
\alpha_{i}
of
\alpha
such that

\omega(\alpha_{1})\geq\cdots\geq\omega(\alpha_{r})\geq 0>\omega(\alpha_{r+1})\geq\cdots\geq\omega(\alpha_{d}).

The discriminant of
m_{\alpha}(x)
is given by

\displaystyle D(m_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=a_{d}^{2d-2}\prod_{i<j}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})^{2}.

The contribution to the valuation of
D_{m_{\alpha}}
from the terms in the product where either
\omega(\alpha_{j})<0
or
\omega(\alpha_{i})<0
, can be bounded by

\omega\left(\prod_{j=r+1}^{d}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i})\right)\geq{{\sum}}_{j=r+1}^{d}(j-1)\,\omega(\alpha_{j}).

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\omega(D(m_{\alpha}))

\displaystyle\geq(2d-2)\omega(a_{d})+2\,{{\sum}}_{0<i<j\leq r}\omega(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i})\,+\,2\,{{\sum}}_{j=r+1}^{d}(j-1)\,\omega(\alpha_{j})

(4)

\displaystyle\geq 2\,{{\sum}}_{i<j\leq r}\omega(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i})\,-\,2\,{{\sum}}_{j=r+1}^{d}(d-j)\,\omega(\alpha_{j}).

Since
\omega(\alpha_{j})<0
for
r+1\leq j\leq d
, and
\omega(\alpha_{1}),\,\omega(\alpha_{2}),\ldots,\,\omega(\alpha_{r})\geq 0
, omitting a few non-negative terms if necessary, we have the following inequality

\displaystyle\omega(D(m_{\alpha}))

\displaystyle\geq 2\,{{\sum}}_{i<j\leq r}\omega(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i})\,-\,2\,{{\sum}}_{j=r+1}^{d}(d-j)\,\omega(\alpha_{j})

(5)

\displaystyle\geq 2\,{{\sum}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j\leq r\\
\alpha_{i},\,\alpha_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)\end{subarray}}\nu(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i})\,-\,2\,{{\sum}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}r<j\leq d\\
\alpha_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)\end{subarray}}(d-j)\,\nu(\alpha_{j}).

For each
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let
N_{x}
denote the number of conjugates of
\alpha_{t}\in\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)
with
1\leq t\leq r
, which lie in the residue class
x\bmod\nu
. If
\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)
lie in the same residue class modulo
\nu
, then
\nu(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})\geq\frac{1}{e}
. Hence,

{{\sum}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j\leq r\\
\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)\end{subarray}}\nu(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i})\geq\frac{1}{e}{{\sum}}_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\frac{N_{x}(N_{x}-1)}{2},

where
q=p^{f}
.

Let
r^{\prime}
be the number of
\nu
-integral conjugates of
\alpha
lying in
K
. Since there are
|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|
many conjugates of
\alpha
lying in
K
, there are exactly
|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|-r^{\prime}
non-integral conjugates of
\alpha
in
K
. Therefore the second summation in (
3
) runs over all
|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|-r^{\prime}
non-integral conjugates of
\alpha
in
K
. As
d-j
are all distinct positive integers, we have

{{\sum}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}r<j\leq d\\
\alpha_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)\end{subarray}}(d-j)\,\geq\,\frac{(|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|-r^{\prime})(|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|-r^{\prime}-1)}{2}.

Note that, for
r<i\leq d
,
\nu(\alpha_{i})\leq-\frac{1}{e}
and therefore, (
3
) implies that

\operatorname{ord}_{p}(D(m_{\alpha}))\geq\frac{1}{e}\left((|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|-r^{\prime})(|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|-r^{\prime}-1)+{{\sum}}_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}N_{x}(N_{x}-1)\right),

where
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(D(m_{\alpha}))
is the usual
p
-adic valuation of
D(m_{\alpha})
. Since

(|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|-r^{\prime})+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}N_{x}=|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|,

applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{ord}_{p}(D(m_{\alpha}))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{e}\left((|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|-r^{\prime})(|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|-r^{\prime}-1)+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}N_{x}(N_{x}-1)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e}\left((|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|-r^{\prime})^{2}+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}N_{x}^{2}+O(d)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{e}\left(\frac{|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|^{2}}{q+1}+O(d)\right),

where the implied constant has absolute value
\leq 1
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\log|D(m_{\alpha})|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{e}|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|^{2}\,\left(\frac{\log p}{q+1}\right)+O\left(d\frac{\log p}{e}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|^{2}\,\left(\frac{\log q}{q+1}\right)+O\left(d\,\frac{\log q}{[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}\right).

Applying Mahler’s inequality (Theorem
2.2
), we deduce that

\displaystyle d\,h(\alpha)=\log M(\alpha)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\log|D(m_{\alpha})|}{2d}-\frac{\log d}{2}

(6)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\,[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}\left(\frac{|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|^{2}}{d}\right)\frac{\log q}{(q+1)}+O\left(\frac{\log q}{[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}\right)-\frac{\log d}{2},

where the implied constant in the
O
-term has absolute value
\leq 1/2
. Finally, using Northcott’s Theorem
2.1
we conclude the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Proposition
1.3
.

From (
3
), we deduce that if

|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|\geq\sqrt{\left(\frac{2(q+1)\,[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\,d}{\log q}\right)\,\,\left(c+\frac{\log{(qd)}}{2}\right)}

then

d\,h(\alpha)\geq c+\frac{\log q}{2}+O\left(\frac{\log q}{[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}\right).

Since the absolute value of the implied constant of the
O
-term is bounded above by

, we conclude that

h(\alpha)\geq\frac{c}{d}.

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

Taking
|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|\geq(q^{3}\,[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\,d\,\log d)^{1/2}
in (
3
), we obtain

(7)

d\,h(\alpha)\,\,\,\,\,\,\geq\,\,\,\left(\frac{q^{3}\log q-q-1}{2(q+1)}\right)\log d+O\left(\frac{\log q}{[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}\right).

Note that for any real number
x\geq 2
,

\frac{x^{3}\,\log x-x-1}{2(x+1)}\,\,\geq\,\,\frac{2}{5}.

This is because the function in the LHS is increasing for
x\geq 2
and the inequality holds for
x=2
. Applying this to (
7
), we obtain

d\,h(\alpha)\geq\,\,\,\frac{2}{5}\log d+O\left(\frac{\log q}{[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}\right).

Therefore, as
d\to\infty
, the Mahler measure
M(\alpha)=d\,h(\alpha)
also tends to infinity as required.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.5
. Recall that for
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
,

\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}=\{\,\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha_{i})\,\,|\,\,1\leq i\leq d\,\}.

For each
K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}
, denote by
e_{K}
and
f_{K}
the ramification index and inertia degree of
K
, respectively,
n_{K}=[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]
and
\mathbb{F}_{q_{K}}
the residue field.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\mu_{\infty}
be an algebraic integer. For
K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}
and
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{K}}
, define

N_{K,x}\,=\,\#\left\{\alpha_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)\,\,:\,\,\alpha_{i}\equiv x\text{\, in \,}\mathbb{F}_{q_{K}}\right\}.

We can write

|D(m_{\alpha})|=\prod_{p}{p}^{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(D(m_{\alpha}))}.

Now, it follows from the definition of
\mathcal{Q}_{p,\,\alpha}
that

\operatorname{ord}_{p}(D(m_{\alpha}))=\sum_{p}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}}\nu_{K}(D(m_{\alpha})),

where
\nu_{K}
is the unique valuation on
K
extending the usual
p
-adic valuation on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\log|D(m_{\alpha})|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{p}\,\,\sum_{K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}}\left(\,\,\,\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{K}}}N_{K,x}\,(N_{K,x}-1)\,\,\right)\frac{\log p}{e_{K}}

(8)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{p}\log p\,\,\sum_{K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}}\frac{1}{e_{K}}\,\,\,\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{K}}}N_{K,x}\,(N_{K,x}-1).

Since
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{K}}}N_{K,x}=|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|
, using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}}\,\,\frac{1}{e_{K}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{K}}}N_{K,x}^{2}\,\,\geq\,\,\sum_{K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}}\,\,\frac{1}{e_{K}\,q_{K}}|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|^{2}.

As

d=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}}|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|,

using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}}\,\,\frac{1}{e_{K}q_{K}}|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|^{2}\,\,\geq\,\,\frac{d^{2}}{\sum\limits_{K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}}e_{K}\,q_{K}}

and hence,

(9)

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}}\,\,\frac{1}{e_{K}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{K}}}N_{K,x}^{2}\,\,\geq\,\,\frac{d^{2}}{\sum\limits_{K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}}e_{K}\,q_{K}}.

On the other hand

(10)

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}}\,\,\frac{1}{e_{K}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{K}}}N_{K,x}\,\,=\,\,\sum\limits_{K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}}\,\,\frac{|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|}{e_{K}}.

Combining (
9
) and (
10
) in (
3
), we get

\displaystyle\log|D(m_{\alpha})|\,\,\geq\,\,d^{2}\,\,{{\sum}}_{p}\,\,\left(\frac{1}{\sum\limits_{K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}}e_{K}\,q_{K}}-\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}}\,\,\frac{|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|}{e_{K}}\right)\log p.

Finally, applying Mahler’s inequality (Theorem
2.2
), we conclude the theorem.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.6
.

By (
3
), we have the inequality

\log|D(m_{\alpha})|\geq\sum_{p}\,\,\sum_{K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}}\,\,\,\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{K}}}N_{K,x}\,(N_{K,x}-1)\,\,\frac{\log q_{K}}{n_{K}}.

For
K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}
, since
\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{K}}}N_{K,x}=|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|
is the total number of conjugates of
\alpha\in K
, using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\log|D(m_{\alpha})|\geq\sum_{K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}}\,\,\,\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{K}}}N_{K,x}\,(N_{K,x}-1)\,\,\frac{\log q_{K}}{n_{K}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}}\,\frac{\log q_{K}}{n_{K}}\,\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{K}}}N_{K,x}\,(N_{K,x}-1)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,\alpha}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|^{2}}{q_{K}}-|\mathcal{S}_{K}(\alpha)|\right)\,\,\frac{\log q_{K}}{n_{K}}.

The proof now follows by applying Mahler’s inequality (Theorem
2.2
).
∎

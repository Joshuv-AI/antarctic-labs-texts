Novel enhanced drug delivery and sensing capabilities of Fe and Au nanoclusters on graphyne: a DFT study with ertapenem drug
Authors: Mahboubeh Pishnamazi, Sameer Alshehri, Rami M Alzhrani, Humood Al Shmrany
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractUsing first-principles density functional theory (DFT), this study examines the improved chemical catalytic performance and biochemical sensing capabilities of iron (Fe) and gold (Au) nanoclusters decorated flawless γ-graphyne (GPN) as nanocarriers for the Ertapenem (EPM) antibiotic drug, in contrast to pristine γ-graphyne. The evaluation of binding energy analysis, it has been noted that perfect GPN (-0.96 eV), Au-decorated GPN (-1.852 eV), as well as Fe-decorated GPN (-1.520 eV), can be suitable candidates for drug delivery, as the binding energy falls in the physisorption to chemisorption range. There is a red shift in the ultraviolet-visible (UV-Vis) spectrum when EPM is adsorbed on the Fe- and Au-decorated GPN surfaces in comparison to the pristine substrates. Based on thermodynamic parameters, the values of Gibbs free energy changes (ΔG) and enthalpy change (ΔH) illustrate a strong interaction between EPM and the Au-decorated GPN (F: -1.130 and − 2.288 eV) in contrast to EPM with the Fe-decorated GPN carrier (I: -1.190 and − 2.210 eV), indicating that the interaction is stable and spontaneous. The Fe-decorated GPN improves the adsorption of EPM with a small binding energy, facilitated by a greater charge transfer from the substrate as an electron donor to the drug. This phenomenon results in a significant rise in dipole moment and a change in the energy gap. The results indicate that Fe-decorated GPN surface can serve as carriers for delivering the EPM drug.Keywords: Ertapenem, Graphyne, Metal decoration, Adsorption behavior, Electronic properties, Density functional theorySubject terms: Chemical engineering, Physical chemistry

Introduction

Graphyne is a novel two-dimensional carbon allotrope that has garnered significant attention due to its unique structure and promising properties in the field of drug delivery1. Its large surface area, high mechanical strength, and excellent biocompatibility make it an attractive candidate for delivering therapeutic agents to target sites in the body2. Graphyne exhibits remarkable mechanical strength, thermal conductivity, and electronic properties and composed of sp and sp2 hybridized carbon atoms arranged in a honeycomb lattice. Its large surface area, high mechanical strength, and excellent biocompatibility make it an attractive candidate for delivering therapeutic agents to target sites in the body3,4. Graphene-based drug delivery systems can improve drug solubility, stability, and bioavailability, leading to enhanced therapeutic outcomes and reduced side effects.

Ertapenem is a broad-spectrum antibiotic belonging to the carbapenem class, commonly used to treat a variety of bacterial infections. It works by inhibiting the synthesis of the bacterial cell wall, leading to the eventual death of the bacteria. Ertapenem is often prescribed for serious infections caused by a range of bacteria, including those that are resistant to other antibiotics. Due to its effectiveness and relatively low risk of side effects, ertapenem is a valuable tool in the treatment of complicated intra-abdominal infections, skin and soft tissue infections, and community-acquired pneumonia, among others5–8. Understanding the properties and interactions of ertapenem is crucial for optimizing its therapeutic use and combating antibiotic resistance. In this study, a DFT investigation is conducted to analyze the interactions between ertapenem and decorating graphyne with iron and gold nanoparticles. The distribution of precious metal nanoparticles on carbon nanostructures is a focal point, as they demonstrate improved electrocatalytic performance in drug delivery applications9,10. The scattering of metal nanoparticles on graphene sheets offers a promising avenue for the advancement of catalytic, magnetic, and optoelectronic materials. The process of enclosing metal clusters within multishell graphitic cages is typically accomplished through carbon arc-discharge under high-temperature conditions11–13. For instance, Nagarajan and Chandiramouli investigated the impact of solvents on Graphyne nanosheets as carriers for delivering quercetin and 5-fluorouracil to specific target cells14. Shahabi and Raissi used graphyne-based porous frameworks to transport Idarubicin, finding a weak interaction between the drug and the nanosheet with an adsorption strength of -81.305 kJ/mol. Density functional theory calculations supported the physical interaction of Idarubicin with the graphydine nanosheet15. Phua and team utilized a modified electrode with reduced graphene oxide and gold nanoparticles induced by laser for an electrochemical alprazolam sensor. The sensor showed a linear response to alprazolam within concentration ranges of 1 µg L− 1 to 0.10 mg L− 1 and 0.10 to 8.0 mg L− 1 under optimal deposition conditions. It had a limit of detection of 0.3 µg-L− 1 and a limit of quantification of 0.9 µg-L− 1. Additionally, the sensor demonstrated good interference tolerance, excellent repeatability (< 0.52% RSD, n = 20), and reproducibility (< 1.90% RSD, n = 6)16. Chima and colleagues used density functional theory (DFT) to study carmustine sensing on Molybdenum-doped graphene nanoflakes surfaces decorated with Cu, Ag, and Au. Their findings revealed a general preference for the adsorption of carmustine on these surfaces17. By studying the adsorption of Ertapenem on Graphyne, we can explore the potential of this carbon nanostructure as a carrier for the antibiotic delivery by the DFT calculations. Additionally, the decoration of Graphyne with metal nanoparticles, such as iron and gold, is of interest in this study. Metal nanoparticles have shown improved electrocatalytic performance in drug delivery applications. Investigating the distribution and interactions of Au and Fe nanoparticles on Graphyne can provide valuable information on how to enhance the drug delivery capabilities of Graphyne-based systems. The use of DFT calculations allows for a detailed analysis of the adsorption process and the understanding of the physical interactions between Ertapenem and Graphyne. This knowledge can contribute to optimizing the design and development of Graphyne-based drug delivery systems, leading to improved therapeutic outcomes and reduced side effects.

Computational details

All calculations were performed using the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange-correlation functional, which has been widely used for studying adsorption phenomena18,19. To account for basis set superposition error (BSSE), the counterpoise correction was applied. Additionally, van der Waals (VDW) corrections were included to capture dispersion forces accurately. To account for basis set superposition error (BSSE), the counterpoise correction was applied, following the methodology described by Boys and Bernardi20. Additionally, van der Waals (VDW) corrections, based on the works of Grimme21–23, were included to accurately capture dispersion forces. The geometry optimization of the EPM, GPN, and functionalized GPN systems was carried out using the DFT framework. The geometry optimization of the EPM, GPN, and functionalized GPN systems was carried out using the DFT framework. The DFTB+24 and OpenMX25 packages were utilized for all calculations. In first step we employed the Density Functional Tight Binding (DFTB) method implemented in the DFTB + package to optimize the complexes of Ertapenem adsorbed on Graphyne. DFTB + offers computational efficiency while providing reasonable accuracy in describing the electronic structure of the system. Then we have used OpenMX to final optimization and electronic structure determination of all considered complexes. The s2p2 basis sets was employed for C, N, O, and S atoms, while the s2p2d2f1 basis set was used for Fe and Au atoms to accurately describe the electronic structure of the metal atoms. To analyze the optical and magnetic properties of the adsorbed EPM on the functionalized GPN surfaces, the linear combination of the atomic orbitals (LCAO) method were performed26–30. The HOMO-LUMO gaps, density of states, absorption spectra, and frequency spectra of the adsorbed EPM were analyzed and compared. The adsorption energies of EPM on the different functional groups and adsorption sites were calculated to determine the most stable configurations. The adsorption energy was defined as the energy difference between the total energy of the adsorbed system and the sum of the isolated EPM and GPN systems. Results and discussion

Structural and electronic properties of perfect molecules

The 3 × 3 supercell of perfect GPN (Fig. 1) that we designed comprises 48 carbon atoms. The lattice constant of primitive GPN unit cell (a = b = 6.91 Å) and bond lengths of C1-C1 (1.43 Å), C1-C2 (1.41 Å), and C2-C2 (1.22 Å) exhibit good agreement with the previously reported lattice constant (a = b = 6.909 Å) and bond lengths of C1-C1 (1.427 Å), C1-C2 (1.412 Å), and C2-C2 (1.232 Å). Moreover, when comparing GPN with graphene, the carbon-carbon (C-C) bond length in graphene is approximately 1.42 Å, which is slightly longer than the C1-C1 bond length in GPN but comparable to the C1-C2 bond length. However, it is important to note that graphene consists of a planar hexagonal lattice structure with sp2 hybridization, while GPN exhibits a porous network with sp3 hybridization, leading to variations in bond lengths and overall structure between the two materials31–33. An energy gap in the range of 0.5 eV is evident in the DOS curve of graphyne. The HOMO-1 and LUMO + 1 levels are located at -1.3 and 1.5 eV, respectively, and play an important role in the formation of peaks in the optical absorption spectrum. Absorption peaks in graphyne’s spectrum at energy levels of approximately 0.7, 2.75, and 4.5 eV. Notably, the absorption peak at 0.75 eV is predominantly attributed to electron transitions from the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) to the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) while the second peak is the result of electron transfer from HOMO-1 to LUMO + 1. The third peak, which is more intense than the other peaks, is the result of the electron transition from HOMO-1 and HOMO-2 to the LUMO + 1 and LUMO + 2 levels.

EPM is a β-lactam antibiotic that contains several functional groups, including OH (hydroxyl), C = O (carbonyl), and NH (amine) groups. The bond lengths in these functional groups can provide insights into the molecular structure and chemical properties of EPM. The OH group in EPM refers to the hydroxyl group, which typically exhibits a bond length of approximately 0.96 Å for the O-H bond. This bond length reflects the typical single bond length between oxygen and hydrogen in hydroxyl groups. The CO group in EPM represents the carbonyl group, which consists of a carbon-oxygen double bond (C = O). The bond length for the C = O double bond in carbonyl groups is usually around 1.22 Å. This bond length is shorter than a typical carbon-oxygen single bond due to the presence of pi bonding in the double bond. The NH group in EPM refers to the amine group, which typically exhibits a bond length of around 1.46 Å for the N-H bond. This bond length represents a typical single bond length between nitrogen and hydrogen in amine groups. Regarding the C-S bond length in EPM, it refers to the bond between carbon and sulfur atoms. The exact bond length for the C-S bond in EPM may depend on factors such as the local environment and molecular conformation. However, a typical C-S bond length in organic compounds is around 1.80 Å. The O-H bond in EPM exhibits a length of 0.98 Å and a dipole moment of -0.1176 Debye. This bond arises between an oxygen atom, carrying a partial negative charge of -0.23, and a hydrogen atom, with a partial positive charge of + 0.1. The significant electronegativity difference between oxygen and hydrogen results in a polarized O-H bond, with oxygen attracting electron density towards itself. This dipole moment facilitates the interaction of EPM with specific molecular targets, contributing to its antibiotic activity. Additionally, the O = C double bond in EPM possesses a length of 1.23 Å and a dipole moment of 0.1335 Debye. This double bond involves a carbon atom, carrying a partial positive charge of + 0.348, and an oxygen atom, carrying a partial negative charge of -0.261. The presence of the double bond character in this molecular fragment enhances the polarization of electron density towards the oxygen atom, resulting in a substantial dipole moment. Furthermore, EPM exhibits a N-H bond with a length of 1.01 Å and a dipole moment of -0.0806 Debye. This bond involves a nitrogen atom, carrying a partial negative charge of -0.1266, and a hydrogen atom, carrying a partial positive charge of + 0.05. The electronegativity difference between nitrogen and hydrogen leads to a polarized N-H bond, with nitrogen attracting electron density towards itself. Considering the C-H bond in EPM, it possesses a length of 1.08 Å and a dipole moment of -0.0385 Debye. This bond involves a carbon atom, carrying a partial negative charge of -0.05, and a hydrogen atom, carrying a partial positive charge of + 0.014. The slight polarity in the C-H bond allows for subtle interactions and contributes to the overall molecular architecture and stability of EPM. Lastly, the C-S bond in EPM displays a length of 1.7 Å and a dipole moment of 0.204 Debye. This bond involves a carbon atom, carrying a partial positive charge of -0.18, and a sulfur atom, carrying a partial negative charge of + 0.25. The significant electronegativity difference between sulfur and carbon results in a substantial dipole moment, influencing the chemical reactivity and intermolecular interactions of EPM. The spin moment of the Fe4 atomic cluster is about 1.8 , and when placed on graphene, the value of the spin moment reaches 0.96 . However, with the adsorption of EPM molecule in A, B,C, D and E state, the magnetic moment decreases to about 0.86, 0.83, 0.88, 0.82 and 0.84 respectively. In all cases, the magnetic moment is concentrated on the nanocluster and the carbon atoms of graphene have zero magnetic moment.

The increase in dipole moment values during the adsorption process signifies a shift in the electronic distribution and polarity of the system, which reflects the interactions and stability of the complexes. According to the Bader charge analysis, approximately 0.64, 0.62, 0.57, 0.51, and 0.49 electrons are transferred from the carrier as an electron acceptor to the EPM drug (Table 1). This observation indicates a significant hybridization that occurs during the adsorption process. The physical adsorption of the EPM drug is primarily governed by hydrogen interactions, resulting in a decrease in binding energy as the distance from the surface increases. Moreover, a direct relationship exists between the surface polarization of GPN and the binding energy of EPM. Specifically, as the surface polarization of GPN increases, the bond energy between GPN and EPM also increases. This behavior can be logically explained by considering the enhanced electrostatic forces between the carrier surface and the drug, which are reinforced by the increased dipole moment (DM) of the carrier surface. The predominant influence of hydrogen interactions on the physical adsorption of GPN underscores the significance of these interactions in the binding process. As the drug approaches the carrier surface, hydrogen bonding occurs, leading to the formation of stable intermolecular connections. This hydrogen bonding contributes to the overall binding energy between EPM and the carrier surface.

Additionally, the surface polarization of GPN plays a crucial role in the adsorption process. GPN, being a polarizable material, exhibits a varying degree of surface polarization. The polarization arises from the separation of charges within the material, resulting in an electrically asymmetric surface. As the surface polarization of GPN increases, the dipole moment of the carrier surface becomes more pronounced. The increase in surface polarization directly impacts the binding energy of EPM. The enhanced dipole moment on the carrier surface strengthens the electrostatic forces between the carrier and the drug. This increased electrostatic attraction contributes to a higher binding energy and a more stable adsorption configuration. The Eads values of various orientations of EPM adsorbed on perfect GPN are presented in Table 2. The Eads of the EPM-GPN complex is determined to be -0.91 eV in complex A and − 0.96 eV in complex D, both falling within the weak chemisorption range. This characteristic is a crucial factor for serving as a potential carrier for drug release. Deb and colleagues34 theoretically proposed GPN as a promising nanocarrier for the cisplatin anti-cancer drug. Their study reported that the interaction energy falls within the physical adsorption range, with the most stable complex exhibiting an energy of approximately − 0.55 eV. Based on thermodynamic parameters, the values of ΔG (-0.505 eV) and ΔH (-1.308 eV) illustrate an electrostatic interaction in complex A in contrast to other complexes, indicating that the interaction is stable and spontaneous (Table 2).

To modify the surface and increase surface polarization, in our investigation, we investigated the impact of decorating Au and Fe tetramers on GPN on the adsorption process of EPM on the surface. Our findings reveal that for Au tetramers adsorbed on the GPN surface, the interaction distance and Eads value were 2.90 Å and − 2.32 eV in the model of Au1, 2.89 Å and − 2.44 eV in the model of Au2, and 2.63 Å and − 2.55 eV in the model of Au3, pointing to a significant chemisorption of the clusters on the substrate (See Fig. 4). The calculated Eads values for the EPM@Au-GPN system fall within the strong chemisorption range, with values of -1.85 eV, -1.72 eV, and − 1.69 eV for complexes F, G, and H, respectively (Fig. 5). For complex F, the optimized distance between the surfaces measures 2.643 Å, and there is an overlap in the electron densities of the two surfaces. Additionally, as a result of the overlapping electron densities, new bonds between the drug and Au nanocluster attached to GPN substrates have been identified. Consequently, the mechanism of strong charge transfer may also be observed. Conversely, the Eads values for the EPM@Fe-GPN system are slightly lower, with values of -1.52 eV, -1.48 eV, and − 1.42 eV for the same complexes (Fig. 5). This difference is a key factor in considering the potential of these systems as carriers for drug release. The analysis of binding energies reveals that only EPM interacting with Fe-GPN is suitable for drug delivery based on meeting the criteria for serving as a drug delivery vehicle, unlike EPM interacting with Au-GPN. M. Castro and E. Chigo Anota reported the growth of the Fe4 cluster in different shapes on a B12N12 cage. It highlights that both the tetrahedron and parallelogram forms are stable, with the tetrahedron being the most energetically favorable. Additionally, it mentions the properties of the B12N12/Fe4-P nanocomposite, indicating its potential applications in magnetic sensor devices and as a nanovehicle for drug delivery38. The lower binding energy of EPM adsorbed on the Fe-GPN system compared to Au-GPN system suggests a relatively weak interaction, which aids in the effective desorption of the EPM drug from the Fe-decorated GPN surface. Furthermore, the interaction distance between EPM and the Fe-decorated GPN surface is measured at 3.653 (I), 2.009 (J), and 2.096 (K), Å, with the oxygen and hydrogen atoms of the EPM molecule interacting with the sd hybridized Au3 nanocluster of the GPN substrate. The optimized EPM@Au-GPN and EPM@Fe-GPN complexes demonstrate the strong chemical interaction between the EPM and carriers through the covalent interaction formed between the Au/Fe atoms of the carrier and the oxygen and hydrogen atoms of the EPM drug during the formation of the EPM@Au-GPN and EPM@Fe-GPN complexes, as illustrated in Fig. 5. Throughout the adsorption process, the DM values reduced from 29.79 to 33.45 Debye in the perfect Au-GPN carrier to 19.44, 18.91, and 18.71 Debye in complexes F, G, and H, while the DM values in Fe-GPN carrier reduced from 33.45 Debye to 28.76, 27.91, and 27.08 Debye in complexes J, K, and L, respectively. According to the Bader charge analysis, approximately 0.84 and 0.73 electrons are transferred from the Au3-GPN and Fe1-GPN carriers as an electron donor to the EPM drug (as an electron acceptor), indicating significant hybridization during the adsorption process. The Eads values of various orientations of EPM adsorbed on perfect Au-GPN and Fe-GPN are compiled in Tables 2 and 3. The values of ΔG and ΔH illustrate a strong interaction between EPM and the Au-decorated GPN (F: -1.130 and − 2.288 eV) in contrast to EPM with the Fe-decorated GPN carrier (I: -1.190 and − 2.210 eV). Therefore, the thermodynamic parameters indicate that the interaction of EPM in complex F is slightly stronger than in complex I, suggesting that the drug can be adsorbed spontaneously on the substrate surface (Table 3).

Figure 9 shows the density of states of Au3-GPN and EPN@Au3-GPN structures along with the density of states of Fe1-GPN and EPN@Fe1-GPN in the most stable configuration under investigation. Compared to pure GPN, in the absorption of gold and iron clusters, there have been drastic changes in the density of states, especially which the energy gap has disappeared and basically the density of states has increased near the Fermi level. Such a change indicates a relatively flared behavior on the surface of GPN, which can increase the chemical reactivity of GPN. Adsorption of EPM on surfaces modified with gold and iron clusters has noticeably changed the density of states, which is mostly caused by molecular orbital hybridization with the surface of clusters bound to GPN.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the study utilized first-principles density functional theory (DFT) to investigate the delivery and sensing of EPM on GPN substrate with the addition of Fe and Au nanoclusters. Through the analysis of optical and magnetic properties using the LCPAO method and TD-DFT, the research explored various aspects such as HOMO and LUMO gaps, density of states and absorption spectra of the adsorbed EPM on functionalized GPM surfaces. The findings revealed that the adsorption of EPM on the substrate surface via -OH and -NH functional groups in the most stable configuration, with an adsorption energy of -1.38 eV and a DM value of 15.15 Debye. Additionally, the chemisorption of EPM through its sulfur and oxygen heads on the Fe1 and Au3-decorated GPN exhibited adsorption energies of -1.51 eV and − 1.85 eV, respectively. Overall, the study highlighted the significant impact of Fe1 and Au3 nanoclusters on the GPM surface, resulting in notable changes in the EPM adsorption capacity and electronic properties. Based on thermodynamic parameters, the values of Gibbs free energy changes (ΔG) and enthalpy change (ΔH) illustrate a strong interaction between EPM and the Au-decorated GPN (F: -1.130 and − 2.288 eV) in contrast to EPM with the Fe-decorated GPN carrier (I: -1.190 and − 2.210 eV), indicating that the interaction is stable and spontaneous. Based on optical absorption spectra, the absorption spectrum of Au3-GPN exhibits notable changes compared to pure GPN, with blue shifts observed after Au absorption. The peaks in the GPN absorption spectrum have shifted to lower wavelengths in the Au3-GPN spectrum, indicating changes in the absorption characteristics. Additionally, the absorption of EPM has contributed to a further enhancement of the blue shift, albeit within a range of 10 to 50 nm. A similar blue transition is observed in the case of the Fe1-GPN structure, with distinct peaks and shifts observed in the absorption spectrum. The absorption of Fe4 and Au3 clusters on GPN has resulted in substantial changes in the density of states, including the elimination of the energy gap and an overall increase in density of states near the Fermi level. This transformation indicates a notable shift in the surface characteristics of graphyne, potentially boosting its chemical reactivity. The adsorption of Au3 and Fe1 clusters on GPN led to structural changes through covalent bonds and significantly changed the electronic structure, indicating a chemical interaction coupled with the effect of surface polarization.

Supersymmetric heterotic supergravity solutions

Connections with torsion are central to our discussion. The literature is a veritable banquet of varying notation and conventions, so we verbosely lay out our approach in the interest of disambiguation. In appendix §
B
we provide a dictionary for translation with some other key papers. We hope this will be of a modicum of help to the community.

Suppose we have a Riemannian manifold
Y
with real coordinates
x^{m}
and a metric
g_{mn}
. On the tangent bundle we define a covariant derivative. In the coordinate basis the connection symbols are defined as

\nabla_{m}{\partial}_{n}\;=\;\Gamma_{m}{}^{p}{}_{n}{\partial}_{p}~,\qquad\nabla_{m}{\text{d}}x^{n}\;=\;\!-\Gamma_{m}{}^{n}{}_{p}{\text{d}}x^{p}~.

(2.1)

To deal with spinors we endow the manifold with a vielbein
e^{a}=e^{a}_{m}{\text{d}}x^{m}
, its inverse
E_{a}=E_{a}^{m}{\partial}_{m}
and hermitian gamma matrices satisfying a Clifford algebra
\{\gamma^{a},\gamma^{b}\}=2\eta^{ab}
, where
\eta_{ab}={\rm diag}\{1,1,\cdots,1\}
is the flat metric acting on the tangent space indices; it is related to the curved metric via the vielbein
g_{mn}=e_{m}^{a}e_{n}^{b}\eta_{ab}
. In the vielbein basis we define a spin connection
\Theta_{ab}={\text{d}}x^{m}\Theta_{mab}
as

\nabla_{m}e^{a}\;=\;\!-\Theta_{m}{}^{a}{}_{b}e^{b}~,\qquad\nabla_{m}E_{a}\;=\;\Theta_{m}{}^{b}{}_{a}E_{b}~,

(2.2)

which appears in the gravitino variation, and defines a covariant derivative

\nabla_{m}\epsilon\;=\;{\partial}_{m}\epsilon+\frac{1}{2}\Theta_{m\,ab}\Sigma^{ab}\epsilon~,

(2.3)

where
\Sigma^{ab}=\frac{1}{4}[\gamma^{a},\gamma^{b}]
are anti-hermitian generators of
{\rm SO}(d)
with
\gamma^{a}
hermitian gamma matrices. The connection being metric compatible means
\Theta_{mab}=-\Theta_{mba}
. The torsion
T
and curvature
R
can be computed from the Cartan structure equations (e.g.
[
39
]
):

\begin{split}{\text{d}}e^{a}+\Theta^{a}{}_{b}e^{b}&\;=\;T^{a}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}T^{a}{}_{bc}e^{b}{\,\wedge\,}e^{c}~,\\
{\text{d}}\Theta^{a}{}_{b}+\Theta^{a}{}_{c}{\,\wedge\,}\Theta^{c}{}_{b}&\;=\;R^{a}{}_{b}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}R^{a}{}_{bcd}e^{c}{\,\wedge\,}e^{d}~.\end{split}

(2.4)

The torsion of
\Theta
as defined above is not the same as the contorsion – the difference between the connection of interest and the Levi-Civita connection. It depends on conventions and definitions. In our case, we are interested in torsion that is completely antisymmetric, and the two differ by a sign. The two relevant cases are

\begin{split}\Theta^{\text{H}}_{mab}&\;=\;\Theta_{mab}^{\text{\tiny LC}}+\frac{1}{2}H_{mab}~,\qquad T^{\text{H}\,a}{}_{bc}\;=\;\!-H^{a}{}_{bc}~,\\
\Theta^{B}_{mab}&\;=\;\Theta_{mab}^{\text{\tiny LC}}-\frac{1}{2}H_{mab}~,\qquad T^{\text{B}\,a}{}_{bc}\;=\;H^{a}{}_{bc}~,\\
\end{split}

(2.5)

We have used (
2.4
) to compute the torsion. The spin connection is related to the connection symbols that define covariant derivatives on tensors
2
2

2

The indices in
\Gamma_{m}{}^{n}{}_{p}
appear in every possible order in the literature. We have chosen a convention but the readers mileage might vary. The order of indices is not physically important, but once a convention is set for
\Theta
and
\Gamma
it is important to stay true to that convention. Given a convention, to derive the relation to
\Theta_{m}
its straightforward to use the definitions of
\Gamma
and
\Theta
:

\nabla_{m}{\partial}_{n}=\Gamma_{m}{}^{p}{}_{n}{\partial}_{p}~,\qquad\nabla_{m}E_{a}=\Theta_{m}{}^{b}{}_{a}E_{b}~,

and then apply the transformation to the vielbein e.g.
{\partial}_{p}=e_{p}^{b}E_{b}
.

\Gamma_{m}{}^{n}{}_{p}\;=\;E^{n}_{a}\,(\Theta_{m}{}^{a}{}_{b})\,e_{p}^{b}+E_{c}^{n}\,{\partial}_{m}e^{c}_{p}~.

(2.6)

The advantage of this convention is that the translation from
\Theta
to
\Gamma
is obvious:

\Theta^{\text{H}}_{mab}\;=\;\Theta_{mab}^{\text{\tiny LC}}+\frac{1}{2}H_{mab}~,\qquad\Gamma^{\text{H}}_{m}{\,}{}^{p}{}_{q}\;=\;\Gamma^{\text{\tiny LC}}_{m}{\,}{}^{p}{}_{q}+\frac{1}{2}H_{m}{}^{p}{}_{q}~.\qquad

As an example,
\nabla^{\text{H}}
acting on a vector is

\nabla^{\text{H}}_{m}V^{n}\;=\;\nabla^{\text{\tiny LC}}_{m}V^{n}+\frac{1}{2}H_{m}{}^{n}{}_{p}V^{p}~.

(2.7)

An equivalent definition of torsion in terms of a coordinate basis is

\begin{split}T\;=\;\nabla_{m}{\partial}_{n}-\nabla_{n}{\partial}_{m}\;=\;\left(\Gamma_{m}{}^{p}{}_{n}-\Gamma_{n}{}^{p}{}_{m}\right){\partial}_{p}~.\end{split}

(2.8)

One can check that applying this definition of torsion to (
2.7
) gives the same result as the Cartan structure equation (
2.5
). This is not surprising — Cartan’s formulation is equivalent to the conventional coordinate description and the map (
2.7
), which is formally the same as a gauge transformation, does not modify physical quantities like torsion.

A second straightforward calculation relevant to supersymmetric compactification is to posit the existence of a globally well-defined spinor
\varepsilon
such that

\nabla^{B}\varepsilon\;=\;\nabla^{\text{\tiny LC}}\varepsilon-\frac{1}{8}H_{mab}\gamma^{ab}\varepsilon\;=\;0~,

(2.9)

and from this construct a tensor via a spinor bilinear. An example is a vector such as
V^{m}=\varepsilon^{\dagger}\gamma^{m}\varepsilon
.

3

3

There are details such as the overall normalisation of the bilinear that we are suppressing as they depend on the particular dimension of the manifold
Y
and the reality properties of
\varepsilon
; those overall constants are not important right now.
The calculation is spelled out in the appendix, one finds the same result as (
2.6
):

\nabla^{B}_{m}V^{n}\;=\;\nabla^{\text{\tiny LC}}_{m}V^{n}-\frac{1}{2}H_{m}{}^{n}{}_{p}V^{p}~.

Ten-dimensional supergravity with first order

In the field basis of

,

the ten–dimensional

–supergravity theory can be written down explicitly. In the Appendix we give a translation of notation from those papers to the convention used here. The action is

S\;=\;\frac{1}{2\kappa_{10}^{2}}\int\!{\text{d}}^{10\,}\!X\sqrt{g_{10}}\,e^{-2\Phi}\Big\{\mathcal{R}-\frac{1}{2}|H|^{2}+4({\partial}\Phi)^{2}-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}\big(\text{Tr}\thinspace|F|^{2}{-}\text{Tr}\thinspace|R^{\text{H}}|^{2}\big)\Big\}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{\prime 3})~.

(3.1)

On the
G_{2}
manifold
Y
, we denote
m,n,\ldots
real indices along
Y
.
4
4

4

The 10D Newton constant is denoted by
\kappa_{10}
,

g_{10}=-\det(g_{MN})
,
\Phi
is the 10D dilaton.

\mathcal{R}
is the Ricci scalar evaluated using the Levi-Civita connection and
F
is the Yang–Mills field strength with the trace taken in the adjoint of the gauge group. We take the generators of the gauge group
T^{a}
and the trace

to be defined so that
\text{Tr}\thinspace(T^{a}T^{b})
is positive definite. We will often need wedge products involving the basis elements of

written in a coordinate basis. To save writing excessive wedges we will write
{\text{d}}x^{m_{1}\cdots m_{n}}\cong{\text{d}}x^{m_{1}}{\,\wedge\,}\cdots\wedge{\text{d}}x^{m_{n}}
. If any possible confusion arises we will use the

There is a point-wise inner product on
p
-forms given by

\langle S,\,T\rangle~=~\frac{1}{p!}\,g^{M_{1}N_{1}}\ldots g^{M_{p}N_{p}}\,S_{M_{1}\ldots M_{p}}\,T_{N_{1}\ldots N_{p}}~,\qquad|T|^{2}~=~\langle T,\,T\rangle~.

The three–form
H
satisfies a Bianchi–identity

{\text{d}}H\;=\;\!-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\left(\text{Tr}\thinspace F{\,\wedge\,}F-\text{Tr}\thinspace R^{\text{H}}{\,\wedge\,}R^{\text{H}}\,\right)~.

The 10-dimensional background being supersymmetric amounts to the vanishing gravitino, dilatino and gaugino variations:

\begin{split}\delta\Psi_{M}&\;=\;\nabla_{M}^{B}\varepsilon+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})\;=\;0~,\\
\delta\lambda&\;=\;\!-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\left(\raisebox{0.71114pt}{/\penalty}\hskip-6.544pt{\partial}-\frac{1}{2}\hskip 2.84544pt\raisebox{0.85355pt}{/\penalty}\hskip-8.5359ptH\right)\varepsilon+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})\;=\;0~,\\
\delta\chi&\;=\;\!-\frac{1}{2}\hskip 2.84544pt\raisebox{0.71114pt}{/\penalty}\hskip-7.96674ptF\varepsilon+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})\;=\;0~,\end{split}

(3.2)

where we use the usual slash notation. We have written the formal expansion parameter
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}
explicitly; there are equations not written to do with second order equations, beyond our remit. The important point is that the spinor
\varepsilon
is covariantly constant with torsion opposite to what appears in the action, Bianchi identity and equations of motion.

3.2
G_{2}
manifolds

First, we briefly recapitulate a
G_{2}
compactification of string theory using the formalism described in
[
16
]
. This will also serve as a way of establishing our notation. We now suppose that the spacetime is
\mathbbl{R}^{2,1}\times Y
with
Y
a 7-dimensional Riemannian manifold.
The existence of a covariantly constant spinor (
3.2
) implies the existence of a
G_{2}
-structure 3-form
\phi
constructed as a spinor billinear. The form
\phi
determines a Riemannian
G_{2}
metric
g
, and in virtue of (
3.2
) both are covariantly constant

\nabla^{B}g\;=\;0~,\qquad\nabla^{B}\phi\;=\;0~.

There is an associated four-form
\psi=\star\,\phi
. The manifold
Y
has
G_{2}
holonomy if and only if
{\text{d}}\phi=0
and
{\text{d}}\psi=0
. We require that
Y
instead has an integrable
G_{2}
structure which amounts to the
\phi,\psi
no longer being closed but that there is the vanishing of a particular torsion module. Given this, we will be able to define an analogue of a Dolbeault operator
{\check{{\text{d}}}}
for complex manifolds.

We divide up our forms according to type. Denote
\Lambda^{k}(Y)
the space of
k
-forms on
Y
. To arrive at a differential operator that replicates Dolbeault cohomology it is important to project onto representations of
G_{2}
. We denote
\Lambda^{k}_{{{\boldsymbol{p}}}}(Y)
the subspace of
k
-forms which transform in the
{{\boldsymbol{p}}}
representation of
G_{2}
, and
\pi_{{{\boldsymbol{p}}}}:\Lambda^{k}(Y)\to\Lambda^{k}_{{{\boldsymbol{p}}}}(Y)
the projection of a
k
-form on to the representation
{{\boldsymbol{p}}}
of
G_{2}
. On
Y
therefore, we have a decomposition of forms given as

\begin{split}&\Lambda^{0}\;=\;\Lambda_{{{\boldsymbol{1}}}}^{0}~,\\
&\Lambda^{1}\;=\;\Lambda_{{{\boldsymbol{7}}}}^{1}=T^{*}Y\cong TY~,\\
&\Lambda^{2}\;=\;\Lambda_{{{\boldsymbol{7}}}}^{2}\oplus\Lambda^{2}_{{{\boldsymbol{14}}}}~,\\
&\Lambda^{3}\;=\;\Lambda_{{{\boldsymbol{1}}}}^{3}\oplus\Lambda^{3}_{{{\boldsymbol{7}}}}\oplus\Lambda^{3}_{{{\boldsymbol{27}}}}~,\end{split}

(3.3)

the remainder taken care of by the Hodge dual with respect to
g
. The explicit projections used to decompose the forms in (
3.3
) into irreducible representations are summarised in Appendix §
A
.

To construct the complexes required for the discussion of the moduli, we need to define an analogue of the Dolbeault operator for
G_{2}
manifolds. We define an operator
{\check{{\text{d}}}}
given by

\begin{split}&{\check{{\text{d}}}}_{0}:\Lambda^{0}(Y)\to\Lambda^{1}(Y)~,\qquad{\check{{\text{d}}}}_{0}f\;=\;{\text{d}}f~,\qquad~~f\in\Lambda^{0}(Y)~,\\
&{\check{{\text{d}}}}_{1}:\Lambda^{1}(Y)\to\Lambda_{{{\boldsymbol{7}}}}^{2}(Y)~,\qquad{\check{{\text{d}}}}_{1}\alpha\;=\;\pi_{7}({\text{d}}\alpha)~,\quad\alpha\in\Lambda^{1}(Y)~,\\
&{\check{{\text{d}}}}_{2}:\Lambda_{{{\boldsymbol{7}}}}^{2}(Y)\to\Lambda_{{{\boldsymbol{1}}}}^{3}(Y)~,\qquad{\check{{\text{d}}}}_{2}\beta\;=\;\pi_{1}({\text{d}}\beta)~,\quad\beta\in\Lambda^{2}(Y)~.\end{split}

(3.4)

If we consider a series of maps generated by
{\check{{\text{d}}}}
:

0\rightarrow\Lambda^{0}(Y)\xrightarrow{{\check{{\text{d}}}}}\Lambda^{1}(Y)\xrightarrow{{\check{{\text{d}}}}}\Lambda^{2}_{{{\boldsymbol{7}}}}(Y)\xrightarrow{{\check{{\text{d}}}}}\Lambda^{3}_{{{\boldsymbol{1}}}}(Y)\rightarrow 0~,

(3.5)

then this is a complex,
{\check{{\text{d}}}}^{2}=0
, if and only if the
G_{2}
structure is integrable. In this paper, we take the
G_{2}
structure to be integrable so that (
3.5
) is indeed a complex. We denote the complex
\check{\Lambda}^{*}(Y)
and it is in fact elliptic with the corresponding cohomology ring
\check{H}^{*}(Y)
being the canonical
G_{2}
-cohomology of
Y
.

The complex has an extension to bundle valued forms in a natural way. We define

\begin{split}&{\check{{\text{d}}}}_{0\,A}:\Lambda^{0}(Y,E)\to\Lambda^{1}(Y,E)~,\qquad{\check{{\text{d}}}}_{0\,A}f\;=\;{\text{d}}_{A}f~,\qquad~~f\in\Lambda^{0}(Y,E)~,\\
&{\check{{\text{d}}}}_{1\,A}:\Lambda^{1}(Y,E)\to\Lambda_{{{\boldsymbol{7}}}}^{2}(Y,E)~,\qquad{\check{{\text{d}}}}_{1\,A}\alpha\;=\;\pi_{7}({\text{d}}_{A}\alpha)~,\quad\alpha\in\Lambda^{1}(Y,E)~,\\
&{\check{{\text{d}}}}_{2\,A}:\Lambda_{{{\boldsymbol{7}}}}^{2}(Y,E)\to\Lambda_{{{\boldsymbol{1}}}}^{3}(Y,E)~,\qquad{\check{{\text{d}}}}_{2\,A}\beta\;=\;\pi_{1}({\text{d}}_{A}\beta)~,\quad\beta\in\Lambda^{2}(Y,E)~.\end{split}

(3.6)

We have that
{\check{{\text{d}}}}_{A}^{2}=0
if and only if the connection on
A
is an instanton and the manifold has integrable
G_{2}
structure. Instanton in the context of
G_{2}
manifolds means

F{\,\wedge\,}\psi\;=\;0~.

The heterotic Bianchi identity is

{\text{d}}H\;=\;-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\left(\text{Tr}\thinspace F{\,\wedge\,}F-\text{Tr}\thinspace R^{\text{H}}{\,\wedge\,}R^{\text{H}}\right)~,

(3.7)

where from the gravitino variation we find the
G_{2}
structure satisfies

{\text{d}}\varphi\;=\;\!-\frac{1}{4}H_{mn}{}^{p}\varphi_{prs}{\text{d}}x^{mnrs}~,\qquad{\text{d}}\psi\;=\;\!-\frac{1}{12}H_{mn}{}^{p}\psi_{prst}{\text{d}}x^{mnrst}~,

where we have assumed the
G_{2}
structure integrable.

As a first step we follow
[
14
,
16
]
by introducing the spurious modes, corresponding to deformations of the connection on
TY
, and define an operator that will hand us a complex. The bundle
Q^{spur}
is defined as

Q^{spur}\;=\;T^{*}Y\oplus{\rm End}(TY)\oplus{\rm End}(V)~.

A section is denoted by

\mathpzc Y^{spur}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}M\\
K\\
\mathfrak{a}\end{pmatrix}~.

We will need to study
p
-forms valued in
Q^{spur}
and by an abuse of notation use the same symbol for such a section:
\mathpzc Y^{spur}\in\Lambda^{p}(Y,Q)
. In calculations we will have need for indices, and the order of the indices is important as they are not democratic. For example,
M\cong M_{Pm}{\text{d}}x^{P}\otimes{\text{d}}x^{m}
, where
P
is a multi-index of degree
d
with
{\text{d}}x^{P}={\text{d}}x^{p_{1}\cdots p_{d}}/d!
. Very often
d=1
and so
M
is a 1-form valued in the cotangent bundle
T^{*}Y
. We sometimes explicitly write the index corresponding to the cotangent bundle viz.
M_{m}\cong M_{Pm}{\text{d}}x^{P}
.

We define an operator on
Q^{spur}
-valued forms:

D^{spur}:\Lambda^{p}(Y,Q)\to\Lambda^{p+1}(Y,Q^{spur})~,\qquad\qquad D^{spur}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}{\text{d}}_{\nabla^{\text{H}}}&\mathcal{R}_{1}&-\mathcal{F}_{1}\\
\mathcal{R}_{2}&{\text{d}}_{\nabla^{\text{H}}}&0\\
\mathcal{F}_{2}&0&{\text{d}}_{A}\end{pmatrix}~,

(3.8)

where

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})_{m}&\;=\;\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace(F_{mq}{\text{d}}x^{q}{\,\wedge\,}\mathfrak{a})~,\qquad\mathcal{F}_{2}(M)\;=\;g^{mn}F_{nq}M_{Pm}{\text{d}}x^{q}{\,\wedge\,}{\text{d}}x^{P}~,\\
\mathcal{R}_{1}(K)_{m}&\;=\;\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace(R^{\text{H}}_{mq}{\text{d}}x^{q}{\,\wedge\,}K)~,\qquad\mathcal{R}_{2}(M)\;=\;g^{mn}R^{\text{H}}_{nq}M_{Pm}{\text{d}}x^{q}{\,\wedge\,}{\text{d}}x^{P}~.\end{split}

(3.9)

This operator
D^{spur}
does not, in fact, square to zero as written. Instead, we construct a related operator
\check{D}^{spur}
defined by projecting
D^{spur}
component-wise onto certain irreducible representations of
G_{2}
in analogy with
{\check{{\text{d}}}},{\check{{\text{d}}}}_{A}
. For example, for
\mathcal{F}_{2}
we define

{\check{\mathcal{F}}}_{2}:\Lambda_{{{\boldsymbol{r}}}}^{(p)}(Y,TY)\quad\longrightarrow\quad\Lambda_{{{\boldsymbol{r}}}^{\prime}}^{(p+1)}(Y,{\rm End}(V))~,

with

\begin{split}{\check{\mathcal{F}}}_{2}(M)&\;=\;\mathcal{F}_{2}(M)=M^{m}F_{m}~,~~\qquad\qquad\qquad M\in\Lambda^{0}(TY)~,\\
{\check{\mathcal{F}}}_{2}(M)&\;=\;\pi_{{{\boldsymbol{7}}}}(\mathcal{F}_{2}(M))=-\pi_{{{\boldsymbol{7}}}}(M^{m}{\,\wedge\,}F_{m})~,\quad\,\,M\in\Lambda^{1}(TY)~,\\
{\check{\mathcal{F}}}_{2}(M)&\;=\;\pi_{{{\boldsymbol{1}}}}(\mathcal{F}_{2}(M))=\pi_{{{\boldsymbol{1}}}}(M^{m}{\,\wedge\,}F_{m})~,~~\quad\,~M\in\Lambda_{{{\boldsymbol{7}}}}^{2}(TY)~.\\
\end{split}

(3.10)

There are similar definitions for
\mathcal{R}_{i},\mathcal{F}_{i}
. The end result is

\check{D}^{spur}:\Lambda_{{{\boldsymbol{r}}}}^{p}(Y,Q)\to\Lambda^{p+1}_{{{\boldsymbol{r}}}^{\prime}}(Y,Q)~,\qquad\qquad\check{D}^{spur}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}{\check{{\text{d}}}}_{\nabla^{\text{H}}}&\check{\mathcal{R}}_{1}&-\check{\mathcal{F}}_{1}\\
\check{\mathcal{R}}_{2}&{\check{{\text{d}}}}_{\nabla^{\text{H}}}&0\\
\check{\mathcal{F}}_{2}&0&{\check{{\text{d}}}}_{A}\end{pmatrix}~.

(3.11)

The operator
{\check{D}}^{spur}
squares to a diagonal matrix after using the Bianchi identities for
F
,
R^{\text{H}}
as well as
H
in (
3.7
) together with the relation between Hull and Bismut curvatures

R^{\text{H}}_{mnab}-R^{\text{\tiny B}}_{abmn}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}({\text{d}}H)_{mnab}~.

(3.12)

It in fact squares to zero after projecting onto the appropriate representations, noting the curvatures satisfy

\psi{\,\wedge\,}R^{B}_{abmn}{\text{d}}x^{mn}\;=\;\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})~,\quad\psi{\,\wedge\,}R^{\text{H}}_{mnab}{\text{d}}x^{mn}\;=\;\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,})~.

(3.13)

If the
G_{2}
manifold is reduced via
Y=X\times S^{1}
, where
X
is an SU(3) manifold, the first condition above amounts to the metric on
X
being Bismut Ricci-flat
[
34
]
, and Bismut Ricci-flat is the same as conformally balanced. We return to this reduction later.

This means we can define a complex

0\rightarrow\Lambda^{0}(Y,Q^{spur})\xrightarrow{{\check{D}}^{spur}}\Lambda^{1}(Y,Q^{spur})\xrightarrow{{\check{D}}^{spur}}\Lambda^{2}_{{{\boldsymbol{7}}}}(Y,Q^{spur})\xrightarrow{{\check{D}}^{spur}}\Lambda^{3}_{{{\boldsymbol{1}}}}(Y,Q^{spur})\rightarrow 0~,

(3.14)

which is in fact elliptic.

The heterotic moduli in
[
16
]
are described by the kernel of the
{\check{D}}^{spur}
-operator, and they include the spurious degrees of freedom, labelled here by
K
:

{\check{D}}^{spur}\mathpzc Y^{spur}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}{\check{\text{d}}_{\nabla^{\text{H}}}}M+\check{\mathcal{R}}_{1}(K)-\check{\mathcal{F}}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})\\
{\check{\text{d}}_{\nabla^{\text{H}}}}K+\check{\mathcal{R}}_{2}(M)\\
\check{\text{d}}_{A}\mathfrak{a}+\check{\mathcal{F}}_{2}(M)\end{pmatrix}\;=\;0~.

(3.15)

The moduli problem can be phrased in terms of a connection on the bundle
Q
:

\mathcal{D}={\text{d}}_{\mathcal{A}}\;=\;{\text{d}}+\mathcal{A}~,

and
\mathcal{D}^{2}=0
is equivalent to

F(\mathcal{A}){\,\wedge\,}\psi\;=\;0~,\qquad F(\mathcal{A})={\text{d}}\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{A}{\,\wedge\,}\mathcal{A}~,

so that
\mathcal{D}
defines an instanton connection on
Q^{spur}
if we have a solution of the heterotic supersymmetry equations. This observation was in fact the inspiration for the work in
[
34
]
.

In the next section we will construct a bundle
Q
and an operator
{\check{D}}
without the spurious degrees of freedom.

4
Constructing the
G_{2}
–complex for physical moduli

Let us assume
H=\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,})
so that
Y
is
G_{2}
holonomy to zeroth order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
and the corresponding curvature is Ricci-flat. This is a physical requirement – if we take
H=\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,})
, it is guaranteed that the
G_{2}
geometry will define a weakly coupled worldsheet sigma model that underpins a string theory vacuum. If we do not make this assumption, the status of the compactification is murkier as far as quantum corrections go — that is, do the solutions to the higher order
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
equations start to dominate the zeroth and first order solution? Indeed, there are
SU(3)
heterotic compactifications defined by string theory dualities e.g.
[
42
]
, but these do not have sigma model descriptions, and so it is unclear at best to what extent the supergravity approximation is valid for such backgrounds. That being so, we study the case
H=\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,})
.

We want to eliminate the spurious term, which amounts to the component labelled by
K
in the previous section, following the calculation in
[
33
]
. To that end consider (
3.15
) acting on a 1-form valued in
Q
. The first equation is

\psi{\,\wedge\,}\left({{\text{d}}_{\nabla^{\text{H}}}}M_{m}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace\left(\mathfrak{a}{\,\wedge\,}F_{m}\right)-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace\left(K{\,\wedge\,}R^{\text{H}}_{m}\right)\right)\;=\;0~,

(4.1)

where whenever an index is written explicitly we take the derivative to act on it as the Hull connection, and if it is suppressed as a form index it acts like the Levi-Civita connection. For example, from (
3.15
)

{{\text{d}}_{\nabla^{\text{H}}}}M_{m}\;=\;\left({\partial}_{p}M_{qm}-\Gamma^{\text{H}}_{p}{}^{s}{}_{m}M_{qs}\right){\text{d}}x^{pq}~,\quad\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace K{\,\wedge\,}R_{m}^{\text{H}}\;=\;\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}R_{pm}{}^{s}{}_{t}K_{q}{}^{t}{}_{s}\,{\text{d}}x^{pq}~.

(4.2)

As the Levi-Civita connection is torsionless, it drops out when the wedge product is used for the derivative, as per the case above. The projection onto the
{{\boldsymbol{7}}}
is achieved by wedging with
\psi
. The calculation is to first order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
and so we drop the Hull superscript on
R
and take it to be Levi-Civita. Our goal is to rewrite the last term of (
4.1
) in terms of the underpinning
G_{2}
moduli.

We use that
K
is a deformation of the Hull connection. We compute first

\delta\Gamma^{\text{H}}_{q}{}^{s}{}_{t}\;=\;\delta\Gamma^{\text{\tiny LC}}_{q}{}^{s}{}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\delta H_{q}{}^{s}{}_{t}~.

(4.3)

A deformation of the Levi-Civita symbol
\Gamma^{\text{\tiny LC}}
and three-form
H
is

\begin{split}\delta\Gamma^{\text{\tiny LC}}_{q}{}^{s}{}_{t}&\;=\;g^{sp}\left(\nabla_{[t}\delta g_{p]q}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{q}\delta g_{tp}\right)~,\\
\delta H_{q}{}^{s}{}_{t}&\;=\;g^{sm}\delta g_{mn}g^{np}H_{qtp}+g^{sp}\left(\nabla_{q}\mathpzc B_{pt}+\nabla_{p}\mathpzc B_{tq}+\nabla_{t}\mathpzc B_{qp}\right)~,\end{split}

(4.4)

where

\delta H\;=\;{\text{d}}\mathpzc B-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\text{Tr}\thinspace\big(\delta AF\big)+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\text{Tr}\thinspace\big(KR\big)~,

with
\mathpzc B
a gauge invariant combination of the
B
-field and gauge connection

\mathpzc B\;=\;\delta B+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace{(A\,\delta A)}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace{(\Theta\,K)}+{\text{d}}\textrm{-closed}~.

Combining (
4.4
) to (
4.3
), and then transforming
\delta\Gamma
to a deformation of the spin connection
K
(see e.g.
[
39
]
), we find

\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace K{\,\wedge\,}R_{m}\;=\;\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}R_{pm}{}^{ts}K_{q}{}_{st}{\text{d}}x^{pq}=\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}R_{pm}{}^{ts}\nabla_{t}(\delta g+\mathpzc B)_{qs}{\text{d}}x^{pq}~.

(4.5)

Setting

M_{ab}=\frac{1}{2}(\delta g_{ab}+\mathpzc B_{ab})+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,})~,

(4.6)

we have

\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace K{\,\wedge\,}R_{m}\;=\;\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\left(R_{pm}{}^{ts}\nabla_{t}M_{qs}\right){\text{d}}x^{pq}~.

(4.7)

We substitute this into (
4.1
),

\psi{\,\wedge\,}\left({{\text{d}}_{\nabla^{\text{H}}}}M_{m}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace\left(\mathfrak{a}{\,\wedge\,}F_{m}\right)-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}R_{m}{}^{ts}\nabla_{t}M_{s}\right)\;=\;0~,

(4.8)

where
F_{m}=F_{mq}{\text{d}}x^{q}
,
R_{m}{}^{t}{}_{s}=R_{qm}{}^{t}{}_{s}{\text{d}}x^{q}
, and
M^{s}=M_{q}{}^{s}{\text{d}}x^{q}
and
\psi{\,\wedge\,}
projects onto the
{{\boldsymbol{7}}}
. Recall that
\nabla^{\text{H}}
in (
4.8
) acts as the Hull connection on the indices corresponding to
T^{*}Y
, and as Levi-Civita on the form indices; while
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection, but due to the overall
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
could be taken to be Hull, Chern or Bismut.

The end result is that we define a bundle
Q
, which is topologically

Q\;=\;T^{*}Y\oplus{\rm End}(V)~,

with sections corresponding to moduli

\mathpzc Y\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}M\\
\mathfrak{a}\end{pmatrix}~.

(4.9)

This is acted upon by an operator

\mathcal{D}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}{{\text{d}}_{\nabla^{\text{H}}}}+R\nabla&-\mathcal{F}_{1}\\
\mathcal{F}_{2}&{\text{d}}_{A}\end{pmatrix}~,

(4.10)

where

\begin{split}(R\nabla)(M_{m})&\;=\;-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\,R_{qm}{}^{ts}\nabla_{t}M_{P\,s}\,{\text{d}}x^{q}{\,\wedge\,}{\text{d}}x^{P}~,\\
\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})_{m}&\;=\;\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace(F_{mq}{\text{d}}x^{q}{\,\wedge\,}\mathfrak{a})~,\qquad\mathcal{F}_{2}(M)\;=\;g^{mn}F_{nq}M_{Pm}\,{\text{d}}x^{q}{\,\wedge\,}{\text{d}}x^{P}~.\end{split}

(4.11)

4.1
Demonstrating
{\check{D}}^{2}=0

We now demonstrate that
{\check{D}}
satisfies
{\check{D}}^{2}=0
. It is an important consistency check of our previous calculation.

Schematically, the square of (
4.10
) is

\mathcal{D}^{2}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}({{\text{d}}_{\nabla^{\text{H}}}}+R\nabla)^{2}-\mathcal{F}_{1}\mathcal{F}_{2}&-({{\text{d}}_{\nabla^{\text{H}}}}+R\nabla)\mathcal{F}_{1}-\mathcal{F}_{1}{\text{d}}_{A}\\
\mathcal{F}_{2}({{\text{d}}_{\nabla^{\text{H}}}}+R\nabla)+{\text{d}}_{A}\mathcal{F}_{2}&-\mathcal{F}_{2}\mathcal{F}_{1}+{\text{d}}_{A}^{2}\end{pmatrix}~.\\

(4.12)

This can be rewritten as

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}^{2}&\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}{{\text{d}}_{\nabla^{\text{H}}}}^{2}+\mathcal{R}_{1}\mathcal{R}_{2}-\mathcal{F}_{1}\mathcal{F}_{2}&-{{\text{d}}_{\nabla^{\text{H}}}}\mathcal{F}_{1}-\mathcal{F}_{1}{\text{d}}_{A}\\
\mathcal{F}_{2}{{\text{d}}_{\nabla^{\text{H}}}}+{\text{d}}_{A}\mathcal{F}_{2}&-\mathcal{F}_{2}\mathcal{F}_{1}+{\text{d}}_{A}^{2}\end{pmatrix}\\[7.0pt]
&\qquad+\begin{pmatrix}\{{{\text{d}}_{\nabla^{\text{H}}}},(R\nabla)\}+(R\nabla)^{2}-\mathcal{R}_{1}\mathcal{R}_{2}&-(R\nabla)\mathcal{F}_{1}\\
\mathcal{F}_{2}(R\nabla)&0\end{pmatrix}~,\end{split}

(4.13)

where we used the definitions in (
3.9
) in the above. The first term on the right hand side vanishes due to the original operator (
3.8
) being nilpotent. Hence, all we need to check is the second matrix. First we check this on a
1
-form valued in
Q
, denoted
\mathpzc Y^{(1)}
, we use the same superscript on components of
\mathpzc Y
where appropriate:

\mathcal{D}^{2}\mathpzc Y^{(1)}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}\left[\{{{\text{d}}_{\nabla^{\text{H}}}},(R\nabla)\}+(R\nabla)^{2}-\mathcal{R}_{1}\mathcal{R}_{2}\right]M^{(1)}-\left[(R\nabla)\mathcal{F}_{1}\right]\mathfrak{a}^{(1)}\\
\mathcal{F}_{2}(R\nabla)M^{(1)}\end{pmatrix}~.

(4.14)

The first term is

\begin{split}(\{{{\text{d}}_{\nabla^{\text{H}}}},(R\nabla)\}+(R\nabla)^{2}-\mathcal{R}_{1}\mathcal{R}_{2})M^{(1)}\;=\;&-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\left(({\text{d}}_{\nabla}R_{m}{}^{t}{}_{s})\nabla_{t}M^{s}\right.\\[5.0pt]
&\left.-R_{m}{}^{t}{}_{s}R_{t}{}^{s}{}_{r}M^{r}\right)+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}R_{m}{}^{t}{}_{s}R_{r}{}^{s}{}_{t}M^{r}~,\end{split}

(4.15)

where we have truncated terms of
\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})
.
The first term on the RHS of (
4.15
) is

-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}({\text{d}}_{\nabla}R_{m}{}^{t}{}_{s})\nabla_{t}M^{s}\;=\;-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}({\text{d}}_{\nabla}R)_{m}{}^{t}{}_{s}\nabla_{t}M^{s}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}(\nabla_{m}R)^{t}{}_{s}\nabla_{t}M^{s}~.

(4.16)

The first term on the RHS of (
4.16
) vanishes identically; for the second term, we use that for a
3
-form
\tau

\pi_{{{\boldsymbol{1}}}}(\tau)\star 1\;=\;\tau_{mnp}\phi^{mnp}\star 1\;=\;6\,\tau\psi~.

(4.17)

Since
\nabla_{m}\psi=0
and
R{\,\wedge\,}\psi=0
,

\pi_{{{\boldsymbol{1}}}}\left(-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}({\text{d}}_{\nabla}R_{m}{}^{p}{}_{t})\nabla_{p}M^{t}\right)\;=\;0~.

(4.18)

The second line of (
4.15
) vanishes by use of the Bianchi identity
R_{ps}{}^{t}{}_{q}+R_{qp}{}^{t}{}_{s}+R_{sq}{}^{t}{}_{p}=0
and symmetries
R_{mnpq}=R_{pqmn}
,
R_{pqmn}=-R_{qpmn}
, where
R
is the Levi-Civita curvature.

The second term in the first row of (
4.14
) vanishes as it is manifestly higher order:

-\left[(R\nabla)\mathcal{F}_{1}\right]\mathfrak{a}^{(1)}\;=\;\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})~.

The second row is

\mathcal{F}_{2}(R\nabla)M^{(1)}\;=\;-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}R_{m}{}^{t}{}_{p}(\nabla_{t}M^{p})F^{m}~.

(4.19)

The instanton equation implies that
\pi_{7}(R)=0
. It follows that
R
is in the
{{\boldsymbol{14}}}
. That being so, it turns out that any contraction such as
R_{s}R^{s}
or
R_{s}F^{s}
is also in the
{{\boldsymbol{14}}}
– this is shown in say equation (A.28) of
[
14
]
. We are required to project (
4.15
) to the
{{\boldsymbol{1}}}
and so the second row of (
4.14
) vanishes.

We now need to check
{\check{D}}^{2}
on a 0-form
\mathpzc Y^{(0)}
. The calculation precedes in much the same way. We have

\mathcal{D}^{2}\mathpzc Y^{(0)}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}\left[\{{{\text{d}}_{\nabla}},(R\nabla)\}+(R\nabla)^{2}-\mathcal{R}_{1}\mathcal{R}_{2}\right]M^{(0)}-\left[(R\nabla)\mathcal{F}_{1}\right]\mathfrak{a}^{(0)}\\
\mathcal{F}_{2}(R\nabla)M^{(0)}\end{pmatrix}~.

(4.20)

The first term is

\begin{split}\left[\{{{\text{d}}_{\nabla}},(R\nabla)\}+(R\nabla)^{2}-\mathcal{R}_{1}\mathcal{R}_{2}\right]M^{(0)}&\;=\;-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}{\text{d}}_{\nabla}(R_{m}{}^{p}{}_{t}\nabla_{p}M^{(0)\,t})-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}R_{m}{}^{p}{}_{t}\nabla_{p}{\text{d}}_{\nabla}M^{(0)\,t}\\
&\quad+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace(M^{(0)}_{p}R^{p}R_{m})+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})~.\end{split}

The first line simplifies after using the Bianchi identity
{\text{d}}_{\nabla}R_{m}=\nabla_{m}R
and the instanton condition
\psi{\,\wedge\,}R=0
, which comes from the projection to the
{{\boldsymbol{7}}}
. We are left with

-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\left(R_{m}{}^{p}{}_{t}R_{p}{}^{t}{}_{q}+\frac{1}{2}R_{q}{}^{p}{}_{t}R_{m}{}^{t}{}_{p}\right)M^{(0)\,q}\;=\;0~.

where the vanishing follows by use of the previously used Bianchi identity
R_{ps}{}^{t}{}_{q}+R_{qp}{}^{t}{}_{s}+R_{sq}{}^{t}{}_{p}=0
and symmetries
R_{mnpq}=R_{pqmn}
,
R_{pqmn}=-R_{qpmn}
of the Levi-Civita curvature.

The second term of first row in (
4.20
) is manifestly order
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}
and so vanishes to this order. The final term is the second row in (
4.20
) is

-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\psi{\,\wedge\,}R_{m}{}^{p}{}_{t}(\nabla_{p}M^{t})F^{m}\;=\;0~,

by Lemma 5 of
[
16
]
and as
R,F
are both instantons and so sit it in the
{{\boldsymbol{14}}}
of
G_{2}
. Hence,
{\check{D}}^{2}
vanishes on a
0
-form. This means we can define a complex

0\rightarrow\Lambda^{0}(Y,Q)\xrightarrow{{\check{D}}}\Lambda^{1}(Y,Q)\xrightarrow{{\check{D}}}\Lambda^{2}_{{{\boldsymbol{7}}}}(Y,Q)\xrightarrow{{\check{D}}}\Lambda^{3}_{{{\boldsymbol{1}}}}(Y,Q)\rightarrow 0~.

(4.21)

It should be noted that the above is only a complex modulo
\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})
corrections, that is
{\check{D}}^{2}=\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})
. This makes the study of the corresponding cohomology theory somewhat murky. Indeed, the mathematical framework for studying "approximate instantons" and cohomologies is quite involved, and should be avoided if possible. We will shed some more light on possible solutions to this issue here, and comment further in section
7
.

To be more precise what we mean by this is we are studying the system order-by-order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
in the spirit of effective quantum field theory. The parameter
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
is a small formal parameter (with
l_{s}=1
it is dimensionless) and so not only are the equations of motion, the supersymmetry varations and fields formally expanded in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
but so is this operator

{\check{D}}={\check{D}}_{0}+{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}{\check{D}}_{1}+{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}{\check{D}}_{2}+\ldots~,

where
{\check{D}}_{1}
,
{\check{D}}_{2}
, etc, do not contain explicit factors of
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
. While we have determined, via the supersymmetry equations,
{\check{D}}_{0},{\check{D}}_{1}
, we do not know
{\check{D}}_{2}
as this requires studying the supersymmetry equations correct to order
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}
. These have not been written down yet. Furthermore, what we have shown is that

({\check{D}}_{0})^{2}\;=\;0~,\quad{\check{D}}_{1}{\check{D}}_{0}+{\check{D}}_{0}{\check{D}}_{1}\;=\;0~,\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad{\check{D}}^{2}={\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}(\cdots)~,

where the
(\cdots)
may or may not be zero; at this point we cannot say one way or another as we cannot determine what expression to write for
{\check{D}}_{2}
. The important point here is that this operator squares to zero modulo
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}
terms, which as we are working to first order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
is good enough to do a self-consistent effective field theory analysis. Indeed, we pursue this order-by-order analysis of the operator and fields further in section §
7
, in which we show the operator takes an upper-triangular structure.

Elements of
\Lambda^{1}(Y,Q)
in the kernel of
{\check{D}}
then parametrise the infinitesimal deformations of the heterotic
G_{2}
system. There are shifts by
{\check{D}}
-exact terms. They are generated by an action on a
Q
-valued 0-form
\Phi
:

\mathcal{D}\Phi\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}{\text{d}}_{\nabla}+R\nabla&-\mathcal{F}_{1}\\
\mathcal{F}_{2}&{\text{d}}_{A}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon_{m}\\
\phi\end{pmatrix}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}{\text{d}}_{\nabla}\varepsilon_{m}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}R_{m}{}^{st}\nabla_{s}\varepsilon_{t}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace(\phi F_{m})\\
\varepsilon_{m}F^{m}+{\text{d}}_{A}\phi\end{pmatrix}~.

(4.22)

At this point we digress briefly to consider small gauge transformations of the
G_{2}
fields. The analysis of section 2 of
[
35
]
can be recycled here with relatively trivial changes:

\begin{split}\delta g_{qm}&\sim\delta g_{qm}+\nabla^{\text{\tiny LC}}_{q}\varepsilon_{m}+\nabla^{\text{\tiny LC}}_{m}\varepsilon_{q}~,\\

{\text{d}}_{A}^{\dagger}\mathfrak{a}+M^{qm}F_{qm}\end{pmatrix}~.

(6.8)

We have written the action of the adjoint on a
1
-form as that is the physically relevant case. The extension to a
p
-form is straightforward given the metric (
6.4
) and an appropriate projection to
G_{2}
-representation compatible with the complex (
4.21
).

We are now in the position to do a very nice consistency check that the moduli space metric we have proposed in is correct: the gauge fixing condition (
4.25
) is precisely

{\check{D}}^{\dagger}\mathpzc Y\;=\;0~.

Hence, the physical moduli of the
G_{2}
compactification are described by

{\check{D}}\mathpzc Y\;=\;0~,\qquad{\check{D}}^{\dagger}\mathpzc Y\;=\;0~.

(6.9)

This looks formally very similar to the F-terms and D-terms written down in
[
33
]
, though of course there is no such thing in a
G_{2}
compactification as the spacetime supersymmetry is
d=3
and
N=1
. Instead, the first equation implies
\mathpzc Y
is an element of a certain cohomology; the second equation corresponds to gauge fixing and picks out the harmonic representative of that cohomology. This is similar to the conclusion in
[
33
]
in which the
SU(3)
moduli were described as the harmonic representative of a certain cohomology.

As promised the underlying reason why (
4.25
) reduces to the D-terms provided we fix holomorphic gauge. It is simply because they can be written as
{\check{D}}^{\dagger}\mathpzc Y=0
and it is this equation which naturally reduces to the D-term equation in
SU(3)
(viz.
{\overline{D}}^{\dagger}\mathpzc Y=0
in the notation of
[
33
]
). While this was initially a surprise, especially as (
4.25
) starts life without knowledge of the moduli space metric, the existence of the moduli space metric allows us to write down (
6.9
), and then the fact we get the
SU(3)
D-terms and F-terms upon reduction is natural.

7
A double extension for
G_{2}
moduli

Our goal here is to rewrite the heterotic
G_{2}
differential and its cohomology in a way so that it is triangular. Recall, to be in the kernel of
\check{D}
to the appropriate order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
, elements
\mathpzc Y\in\Lambda^{d}(Y,Q)
, where

\ \mathpzc Y\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}M\\
\mathfrak{a}\end{pmatrix}~,

(7.1)

must satisfy

{\check{D}}\mathpzc Y\;=\;\pi\circ\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\mathpzc Y\;=\;0+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})\>,

(7.2)

where
\pi
denotes the projection onto the appropriate irreducible
G_{2}
representation. Note that this equation is equivalent to

\Psi\wedge\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\mathpzc Y\;=\;0+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})\>,

(7.3)

where
\Psi=e^{-2\phi}\psi
is the re-scaled
d
-closed four-form.

Let us rephrase the heterotic
G_{2}
moduli complex in a slightly more convenient way for the current section:

0\rightarrow\Lambda^{0}(Y,Q)\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}}\Lambda^{1}(Y,Q)\xrightarrow{\Psi\wedge\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}}\Lambda^{6}(Y,Q)\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}}\Lambda^{7}(Y,Q)\rightarrow 0\>.

(7.4)

By the computations of section
4
, it is straight-forward to check that this is also a differential complex modulo
\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2})
corrections. Furthermore, it is equivalent to the heterotic moduli complex (
4.21
). Next, we expand the
G_{2}
-structure
\Psi
together with our fields
\mathpzc Y
in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
. That is, we write

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\;=\;M_{0}+{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}M_{1}+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})~,

\displaystyle\Psi

\displaystyle\;=\;\Psi_{0}+{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}\Psi_{1}+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})\>.

It is implicit that
\mathfrak{a}
is already first order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
– the Yang-Mills equations come normalised with a factor of
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
– and so we do not need to expand
\mathfrak{a}
in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
. Note also that
\Psi_{0}=\psi_{0}
, the
G_{2}
four-form of the
G_{2}
holonomy background.

For
\mathpzc Y\in\Lambda^{0}(Y,Q)
and
\mathpzc Y\in\Lambda^{6}(Y,Q)
we may then consider

{\footnotesize\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\mathpzc Y=\begin{pmatrix}{\text{d}}_{\nabla}M_{0m}+{\text{d}}_{\nabla}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}M_{1m})-{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}{T^{n}}_{m}M_{0n}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}R_{m}{}^{p}{}_{t}\nabla_{p}M_{0}^{t}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\>\left(F_{m}\mathfrak{a}\right)\\
F^{m}M_{0m}+{\text{d}}_{A}\mathfrak{a}\end{pmatrix}+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})~,}

while for
\mathpzc Y\in\Lambda^{1}(Y,Q)
we get

{\footnotesize\Psi\wedge\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\mathpzc Y=\begin{pmatrix}{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}\Psi_{1}\wedge{\text{d}}_{\nabla}M_{0m}+\Psi_{0}\wedge\Big({\text{d}}_{\nabla}M_{0m}+{\text{d}}_{\nabla}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}M_{1m})-{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}{T^{n}}_{m}M_{0n}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}R_{m}{}^{p}{}_{t}\nabla_{p}M_{0}^{t}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\>\left(F_{m}\mathfrak{a}\right)\Big)\\
\Psi_{0}\wedge\left(F^{m}M_{0m}+{\text{d}}_{A}\mathfrak{a}\right)\end{pmatrix}+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})~,}

where now
\nabla
denotes the Levi-Civita connection of the background
G_{2}
-holonomy manifold,
R
is the curvature of this connection, and
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}T
includes the flux of the Hull-connection, and any
{\cal O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,})
corrections to the Levi-Civita connection from
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
corrections to the metric. Note that these corrections to the metric, or alternatively
\Psi_{1}
, are determined by solving the heterotic
G_{2}
system order by order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
. We also take
F
to be an instanton with respect to the zeroth order
G_{2}
structure
\Psi_{0}
.

Let us then define a new operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
which decomposes the action of the differential in the complex (
7.4
) into forms valued in
\widetilde{Q}^{\prime}=T^{*}Y\oplus{\rm End}(V)\oplus T^{*}Y
. That is, we now write

\ \mathpzc Y\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}\alpha^{\prime}M_{1}\\
\mathfrak{a}\\
M_{0}\end{pmatrix}~.

(7.5)

The operator
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is constructed so as to only contain explicit appearances of
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
up to first order, and takes the following form on
\mathpzc Y\in\Lambda^{0}(Y,Q^{\prime})
and
\mathpzc Y\in\Lambda^{6}(Y,Q^{\prime})
,

\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}\mathpzc Y\;=\;{\footnotesize\begin{pmatrix}{\text{d}}_{\nabla}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}M_{1m})-{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}{T^{n}}_{m}M_{0n}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}R_{m}{}^{p}{}_{t}\nabla_{p}M_{0}^{t}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\>\left(F_{m}\mathfrak{a}\right)\\
F^{m}M_{0m}+{\text{d}}_{A}\mathfrak{a}\\
{\text{d}}_{\nabla}M_{0m}\end{pmatrix}}\>,

while on
\mathpzc Y\in\Lambda^{1}(Y,Q^{\prime})
it reads

\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}\mathpzc Y={\footnotesize\begin{pmatrix}\Psi_{0}\wedge\Big({\text{d}}_{\nabla}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}M_{1m})-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\>\left(F_{m}\mathfrak{a}\right)-{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}{T^{n}}_{m}M_{0n}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}R_{m}{}^{p}{}_{t}\nabla_{p}M_{0}^{t}\Big)+{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}\Psi_{1}\wedge{\text{d}}_{\nabla}M_{0m}\\
\Psi_{0}\wedge\left({\text{d}}_{A}\mathfrak{a}+F^{m}M_{0m}\right)\\
\Psi_{0}\wedge{\text{d}}_{\nabla}M_{0m}\end{pmatrix}}\>,

or in terms of a matrix

\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}\mathpzc Y\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}\Psi_{0}\wedge{\text{d}}_{\nabla}&-\Psi_{0}{\,\wedge\,}\mathcal{F}_{1}&\Psi_{0}{\,\wedge\,}\left(-{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}T_{m}+(R\nabla)\right)+{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}\Psi_{1}\wedge{\text{d}}_{\nabla}\\

0&0&\Psi_{0}\wedge{\text{d}}_{\nabla}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha^{\prime}M_{1}\\
\mathfrak{a}\\
M_{0}\end{pmatrix}~,

where we use (
4.11
) and
T_{m}(M_{0})=T^{n}{}_{m}M_{0\,n}
. The operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
is upper triangular.
In this new decomposition, the complex (
7.4
) may then be written as

0\rightarrow\Lambda^{0}(Y,Q^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}}\Lambda^{1}(Y,Q^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}}\Lambda^{6}(Y,Q^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}}\Lambda^{7}(Y,Q^{\prime})\rightarrow 0\>.

(7.6)

Furthermore, within the framework of working order by order in the
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
expansion, the differential
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
must square to zero
exactly
! This is because we solve our equations exactly, at each order in the
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
expansion.

Explicitly, we have defined the operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
so it can be decomposed into the form

\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}=\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{0}+{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{1}\>,

(7.7)

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{0}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{1}
do not have explicit appearances of
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
. By a similar argument to the one found in section
4.1
for
{\check{D}}
, we have

\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime 2}={\cal O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime 2}_{0}=0\>,\quad\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{0}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{1}+\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{1}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{0}=0\>.

(7.8)

However,

\mathcal{D}^{\prime 2}_{0}=\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime 2}_{0}+{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{0}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{1}+\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{1}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{0})+{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime 2}_{1}\>,

(7.9)

and
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{1}
is upper-triangular with zero on the diagonal. It follows that
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime 2}_{1}=0
, and the operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
is nilpotent.

Decomposing the heterotic
G_{2}
moduli story in this way, using the
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
expansion, has two advantages. Firstly, we get a differential which squares to zero exactly, and therefore also a differential complex. This means that the corresponding cohomologies are well defined mathematically. In particular, the cohomology
H^{1}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}}(Y,Q^{\prime})
is well-defined, and computes the infinitesimal deformations of the heterotic
G_{2}
system modulo
\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})
corrections. The second advantage is that
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
actually defines a double extension of complexes, just like in the
SU(3)
case! We may therefore use homological algebra techniques to compute
H^{1}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}}(Y,Q^{\prime})
in terms of more familiar cohomology groups. We will schematically outline how this goes, but we shall return to this in more detail in future work.

To give some details, note that
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
acts on our field vector
\mathpzc Y
in an upper-triangular fashion. This is the hallmark of extension sequences, and it allows us to build
\Lambda^{p}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}}(Y,Q^{\prime})
as a double extension, starting with an extension of
T^{*}Y
by
{\text{End}\,}_{0}(V)

0\rightarrow\Lambda^{p}(Y,{\text{End}\,}_{0}(V))\rightarrow\Lambda^{p}(Y,Q^{\prime}_{1})\rightarrow\Lambda_{0}^{p}(Y,T^{*}Y)\rightarrow 0~,

where each complex in this sequence is of the form (
7.6
), i.e.
p\in\{0,1,6,7\}
. Here
\Lambda_{0}^{p}(Y,T^{*}Y)
is the complex governing zeroth order
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
deformations of the
G_{2}
holonomy background together with zeroth order deformations of the Kalb-Ramond
B
-field, while
\Lambda^{p}(Y,{\text{End}\,}_{0}(V))
is the complex governing deformations of the instanton connection. This is the
G_{2}
version of the Atiyah complex
[
46
]
, studied in the heterotic context in
[
14
]
. The operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
then defines
\Lambda^{p}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}}(Y,Q^{\prime})
as an additional extension of
Q^{\prime}_{1}
by another copy of
\Lambda_{1}^{p}(Y,T^{*}Y)
, the first order
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
deformations of the geometry and
B
-field

0\rightarrow\Lambda_{1}^{p}(Y,T^{*}Y)\rightarrow\Lambda^{p}(Y,Q^{\prime})\rightarrow\Lambda^{p}(Y,Q_{1}^{\prime})\rightarrow 0\>.

(7.10)

Each short exact sequence lead to long exact sequences of cohomologies, which allows one to rephrase the cohomologies
H^{p}(Y,Q^{\prime})
in terms of more ordinary cohomologies of the individual bundles and kernels of Atiyah like maps.

8
Conclusions

The issue of the connection on the tangent bundle has received attention throughout the sands of time in the study of heterotic moduli at the level of supergravity. While most attention focusses on the
SU(3)
case, it is also present at the level of
G_{2}
compactifications of string theory. In both cases it is mathematically more elegant, at least with our current level of knowledge, to study the moduli of a theory where the connection on the tangent bundle is an instanton and allowed to vary independently on the moduli of the underlying compact manifold. This gives the problem a well-defined setting with many mathematical tools at hand. In contrast, the physics has a preferred connection. This is the Hull connection. It is determined in terms of the other moduli and has been checked against string scattering amplitudes. Our goal here was to include this dependence in the phrasing of the moduli space as the kernel of an operator on an auxiliary bundle
Q
.

What is nice about the
G_{2}
setting in comparison to the
SU(3)
case, is that the D-terms and F-terms are incorporated into the vanishing of a single operator, which we have identified. We have checked in detail that when the
G_{2}
operator is dimensionally reduced to the
SU(3)
manifold one recovers the
{\overline{D}}
,
D
and
{\overline{D}}^{\dagger}
–operators for heterotic
SU(3)
compactifications
[
34
]
. We proposed a moduli metric for
G_{2}
compactifications and checked it reduces to the
SU(3)
metric. The
G_{2}
moduli space metric allows us to define a
{\check{D}}^{\dagger}
, whose kernel defines a gauge fixing for the
G_{2}
moduli. Upon reduction it also reduces to the D-terms in
SU(3)
holomorphic gauge for an
SU(3)
manifold. Finally, we observe that in the
G_{2}
setting, although the
{\check{D}}
-operator is not upper triangular it can be rewritten in an upper triangular way by treating the problem order-by-order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
. This could open up doors to new calculations of the cohomologies underpinning the moduli.

There are many further questions. One could try to compute the cohomology in explicit examples, both locally
[
1
]
, and for compact geometries
[
29
,
30
,
31
]
, as has been done in the
SU(3)
case
[
47
,
48
]
. In this context, it would be interesting to extend this calculation to include flux
H=\mathcal{O}(1)
with
AdS_{3}
vacua. From the string theory point of view these are easier to define in terms of the
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
expansion. This is of clear interest to problems related to the
AdS_{3}/CFT_{2}
correspondence. This will also be instructive in the study of higher order
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
corrections. A related question is what happens to higher order in deformation theory. This will involve an extension of the work in
[
49
]
to higher order in deformation theory and finding a Maurer-Cartan equation for the moduli. In the
SU(3)
case, this involved an
L_{\infty}
algebra that truncated to
L_{3}
. Presumably there is an
L_{\infty}
algebra underpinning the
G_{2}
case: does it truncate, and if so to what order? It is not obvious what will happen as the supersymmetry preserved is half that of the
SU(3)
case. It would also be interesting to extend the operator here to a
G_{2}
universal geometry, as studied in the
SU(3)
case in
[
50
,
35
]
. Again, the reduced supersymmetry makes studying this a qualitatively different question to the
SU(3)
case.

The Correspondence Between Bounded Graph Neural Networks and Fragments of First-Order Logic
Bernardo Cuenca Grau, Przemysław A. Wałęga

Abstract
Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) address two key challenges in applying deep learning to graph-structured data: they handle varying size input graphs and ensure invariance under graph isomorphism. While GNNs have demonstrated broad applicability, understanding their expressive power remains an important question. In this paper, we show that bounded GNN architectures correspond to specific fragments of first-order logic ( FO ), including modal logic ( ℳ ​ ℒ \mathcal{ML} ), graded modal logic ( 𝒢 ​ ℳ ​ ℒ \mathcal{GML} ), modal logic with the universal modality ( ℳ ​ ℒ ​ ( 𝖠 ) ) (\mathcal{ML}(\mathsf{A})) , the two-variable fragment ( FO 2 \text{FO}^{2} ) and its extension with counting quantifiers ( C 2 \text{C}^{2} ). To establish these results, we apply methods and tools from finite model theory of first-order and modal logics to the domain of graph representation learning. This provides a unifying framework for understanding the logical expressiveness of GNNs within FO .

Introduction

Learning on graphs
or relational structures presents two fundamental challenges. First, neural networks require fixed size inputs, making them ill-suited for graphs of varying size. Second, predictions about graphs should not depend on how the graph is represented, i.e., they should be invariant under isomorphism
[
\citeauthoryearHamilton2020
]
.

Graph Neural Networks
(GNNs)
[
\citeauthoryearGilmer et al.2017
]

overcome these limitations by operating natively on graph-structured data, inherently handling variable sizes and ensuring representation invariance.
The flagship
aggregate-combine (
\mathsf{AC}
)
architecture can be viewed as a layered network operating over an input graph. Each node maintains a state (a real-valued vector) and,
in each layer, a node’s state is updated based on its current state and that of its neighbours.
This update mechanism is specified by an
aggregation function
that takes the current states of the neighbours and aggregates them into a vector, and a
combination function
that takes the aggregate value from the neighbours and the current state of the node and computes the updated state.
Their
aggregate-combine-readout
(
\mathsf{ACR}
)
extension includes an additional
readout function
which aggregates states across all nodes in the graph, rather than just local neighbours
[
\citeauthoryearBarceló et al.2020
]
.
GNNs have been widely applied. They drive recommendation systems
[
\citeauthoryearYing et al.2018
]
, predict molecular properties
[
\citeauthoryearBesharatifard and
Vafaee2024
]
, enhance traffic navigation
[
\citeauthoryearDerrow-Pinion et al.2021
]
, interpret scenes in computer vision
[
\citeauthoryearChen et al.2022
]
, and enable reasoning over incomplete knowledge graphs
[
\citeauthoryearTena Cucala et al.2022
,
\citeauthoryearZhang and
Chen2018
,
\citeauthoryearHuang et al.2024
]
.

GNNs encompass many architectures and a central question is understanding their
expressive power
—i.e., the classes of functions they can compute.
This has been addressed from multiple angles.
Early works studied the
discriminative power
of GNNs: given two graphs, can a GNN from a given family yield distinct outputs for them?
By design, no GNN can separate isomorphic graphs, but more subtly, certain non-isomorphic graphs may remain indistinguishable. In particular, if two graphs cannot be distinguished by the 1-dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman (WL) graph isomorphism test, then no GNN can differentiate them either
[
\citeauthoryearMorris et al.2019
,
\citeauthoryearXu et al.2019
]
.
Generalised k-dimensional GNNs, which handle higher-order graph structures, have also been connected to the WL hierarchy of increasingly powerful isomorphism tests
[
\citeauthoryearMorris et al.2019
]
.
Through the correspondence between WL and finite-variable logics
[
\citeauthoryearCai, Fürer, and
Immerman1992
]
, the limitation extends to logical distinguishability.

The expressiveness of GNNs has also been studied through the lenses of finite model theory and database query languages.
As node classifiers, GNNs compute a
unary query
—an isomorphism-invariant function mapping each graph
and node to a truth value.
For a given family of GNN classifiers, what is the logic expressing these unary queries? This is the
logical expressiveness
(or
uniform expressiveness
) of GNNs.
The expressiveness of GNNs goes beyond first-order logic (
FO
) since aggregation can only be captured using extensions such as counting terms
[
\citeauthoryearGrohe2024
,
\citeauthoryearHuang et al.2023
]
, Presburger quantifiers
[
\citeauthoryearBenedikt et al.2024
]
, or linear programming
[
\citeauthoryearNunn et al.2024
]
.
Other GNN variants, such as recursive GNNs
[
\citeauthoryearAhvonen et al.2024
,
\citeauthoryearPflueger, Cucala, and
Kostylev2024
]
require fixpoint operators.

A connection between GNNs and
FO
fragments has also been established
[
\citeauthoryearBarceló et al.2020
]
.
GNNs can simulate logical formulas: graded modal logic (
\mathcal{GML}
) formulas, a.k.a. concepts in the description logic
\mathcal{ALCQ}

[
\citeauthoryearBaader et al.2003
]
, have an equivalent GNN implementation without readout functions,
just as first-order logic formulas with two variables and counting quantifiers (
\text{C}^{2}
) can be realised by a GNNs with readouts.
The relationship, however, reveals an asymmetry when viewed in the opposite direction. In particular, whenever an
\mathsf{AC}
GNN classifier is
FO
expressible, it must fall within
\mathcal{GML}
.
This duality underscores that, while GNNs are powerful enough to capture these logics, the converse translation from GNNs to logic comes with the key assumption of
FO
expressibility.
The question as to what conditions guarantee
FO
expressibility remains unresolved. Indeed, to our knowledge, only one sufficient condition has been identified: monotonic GNNs with max aggregation correspond exactly to unions of tree-shaped conjunctive queries
[
\citeauthoryearTena Cucala et al.2023
]
.

To obtain these results, we leverage game-theoretic characterisations of
FO

\mathcal{L}
, we consider a two-player pebble game and establish an equivalence relation

between models on which the game has a winning strategy.
Crucially, two models are related via
\sim_{\mathcal{L}}

\mathcal{L}

This framework yields a powerful tool: a class of models closed under

This result, together with the fact that any

Bounded GNN Classifiers

In this section, we introduce

generalising architectures considered in the literature, such as max GNNs
[
\citeauthoryearTena Cucala and Cuenca
Grau2024
]

and max-sum GNNs
[
\citeauthoryearTena Cucala et al.2023
]

Bounded GNNs restrict aggregation and readout by requiring existence of a bound

Definition
\thethm
.

An aggregation (or readout) function
f

is the multiset obtained from

all multiplicities greater than
k
with
k

Function
f
is

set-based
if it is
1

bounded
if it is
k
-bounded for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Thus, multiplicities greater than
k
do not affect the output of a
k
-bounded function.
Set-based functions ignore multiplicities altogether.

Example
\thethm
.

Consider the example functions below.

•

The aggregation in max GNNs
[
\citeauthoryearTena Cucala and Cuenca
Grau2024

for instance

•

The aggregation in max-k-sum GNNs

\citeauthoryearTena Cucala et al.2023

k

It maps

\lBrace(3,2),(2,4),(2,4)\rBrace\mapsto(5,8)

4
Overview and Technical Approach

In the remainder of this paper, we establish the correspondences between GNN and logical classifiers summarised in Figure
1
.
In
Section

5
, we link GNNs to modal logics: aggregate-combine GNNs to basic and graded modal logic, and decoupled GNNs
with separate aggregation/readout layers to modal logic with the universal modality. In
Section

6
, we extend beyond modal logic, showing that GNNs with readouts match the
expressiveness of the two-variable fragments
\text{FO}^{2}
and
\text{C}^{2}
.
Our methodology consists of the steps outlined below.

The first step
uses finite model theory
[
\citeauthoryearLibkin2004
]
to characterise logical equivalence via winning strategies in model comparison games (independent of GNNs). This induces an equivalence relation
\sim
on pointed models, which is applicable to graphs as follows.

Definition
\thethm
.

Let
\sim
be an equivalence relation between pointed models.
For pointed coloured graphs
(G,v)
and
(G^{\prime},v^{\prime})
, we write
(G,v)\sim(G^{\prime},v^{\prime})
if
(\mathfrak{M}_{G},v)\sim(\mathfrak{M}_{G^{\prime}},v^{\prime})
.

We also require that pointed models (over a finite signature) closed under

\sim
can be expressed as a disjunction of
characteristic formulas
in the given
FO
fragment.
For
\mathcal{ML}
,
\mathcal{GML}
, and
\text{FO}^{2}
, we adapt results for bisimulation games
[
\citeauthoryearGoranko and
Otto2007
]
, graded bisimulation games with bounded grading
[
\citeauthoryearOtto2019
]
, and 2-pebble games
[
\citeauthoryearLibkin2004
]
.
For
\mathcal{ML}(\mathsf{A})
and
\text{C}^{2}
we develop new characterisations, as we could not find suitable results in prior work.
The subsequent steps shift focus to GNNs.

The second step
shows that formulas in the
FO
fragment can be simulated by a suitable GNN. For this, we adapt existing constructions of GNNs simulating
\mathcal{GML}
classifiers using (unbounded ) sum aggregation
[
\citeauthoryearBarceló et al.2020
]
.

The third step
shows the converse direction, namely that every GNN from a given family can be simulated by a logical classifier.
To this end, we prove that the set of pointed graphs accepted by a GNN is closed under the equivalence relation
\sim
in a given logic.
Formally, the notion of a GNN being closed under
\sim
is defined below.

Definition
\thethm
.

A GNN classifier
\mathcal{N}
is
closed
under an equivalence relation
\sim
between pointed models, if

(G_{1},v_{1})\sim(G_{2},v_{2})
implies
\mathcal{N}(G_{1},v_{1})=\mathcal{N}(G_{2},v_{2})
,
for all pointed graphs
(G_{1},v_{1})
and
(G_{2},v_{2})
.

The results from the first step and the closure properties from the third step imply that each GNN in the family can be captured by a formula in the corresponding logic.

5
Correspondence with Modal Logics

We consider a finite vocabulary
\mathsf{PROP}
of propositions. Formulas of the basic modal logic (
\mathcal{ML}
) are defined by

\varphi:=p\mid\neg\varphi\mid\varphi\land\varphi\mid\Diamond\varphi\qquad[\mathcal{ML}]

where
p\in\mathsf{PROP}
.
The logical constants
\top
and
\bot
, and the remaining Boolean connectives, such as
\vee
and
\rightarrow
, are defined as standard abbreviations, whereas

\Box\varphi:=\neg\Diamond\neg\varphi
.

Graded modal logic
\mathcal{GML}
extends
\mathcal{ML}
with graded modalities

\Diamond_{k}
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, where

\Diamond_{k}\varphi
means that there are at least
k
accessible worlds satisfying
\varphi
.
Hence
\Diamond
coincides with
\Diamond_{1}
.
Formulas of
\mathcal{GML}
are defined as follows:

\varphi:=p\mid\neg\varphi\mid\varphi\land\varphi\mid\Diamond_{k}\varphi\qquad[\mathcal{GML}]

The modal logic with the universal modality
\mathcal{ML}(\mathsf{A})
is the extension of
\mathcal{ML}
with the modality
\mathsf{A}
, where

\mathsf{A}\varphi
means that
\varphi
holds in all modal worlds.
Formulas of
\mathcal{ML}(\mathsf{A})
are defined by

\varphi:=p\mid\neg\varphi\mid\varphi\land\varphi\mid\Diamond\varphi\mid\mathsf{A}\varphi\qquad[\mathcal{ML}(\mathsf{A})]

The ‘somewhere’ operator
\mathsf{E}
is defined as
E\varphi:=\neg\mathsf{A}\neg\varphi
.

The
depth
of a formula is
the maximum nesting of modal operators (
\Diamond
,
\Diamond_{k}
, and
\mathsf{A}
) in the formula.
For
\mathcal{GML}
formulas we also define the
counting rank
,
\mathsf{rk}_{\#}(\varphi)
, as
the maximal among numbers
k
occurring in its graded modalities
\Diamond_{k}
, or
0
if the formula does not mention graded modalities.
The fragment
\mathcal{ML}_{\ell}
consists of all
\mathcal{ML}
formulas of depth at most
\ell
, and we define
\mathcal{ML}(\mathsf{A})_{\ell}
analogously.
The fragment
\mathcal{GML}_{c,\ell}
contains all
\mathcal{GML}
formulas of depth at most
\ell
and counting rank at most
c
.

Modal formulas are interpreted over models
\mathfrak{M}=(V,R,\nu)
with
V
a non-empty set of
modal worlds
,
E\subseteq V\times V
a binary
accessibility relation
, and
\nu:\mathsf{PROP}\to\mathcal{P}(V)
a
valuation function
mapping propositions to subsets of
V
.
1
1

1

Note that we use the same symbols
V
and
E
in modal models and in
graphs, to indicate the intended correspondence.

The valuation
\nu
extends to all formulas as follows:

\nu(\neg\varphi):=V\setminus\nu(\varphi)
,

\nu(\varphi_{1}\land\varphi_{2}):=\nu(\varphi_{1})\cap\nu(\varphi_{2})
,

\nu(\Diamond_{k}\varphi):=\{v\mid k\leq|\{w\mid(v,w)\in R\text{ and }w\in\nu(\varphi)\}|\}
, and

\nu(\mathsf{A}\varphi):=W\text{ if }\nu(\varphi)=W,\text{ and $\emptyset$ otherwise.}

A
pointed model
is a pair
(\mathfrak{M},v)
,
of a model
\mathfrak{M}
and one of its worlds
v
.
It
satisfies
formula
\varphi
, denoted
(\mathfrak{M},v)\models\varphi
, if

v\in\nu(\varphi)
.
For a logic
\mathcal{L}
, we write
(\mathfrak{M},w)\equiv_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},w^{\prime})
if
(\mathfrak{M},w)
and
(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},w^{\prime})
satisfy the same formulas in
\mathcal{L}
.

5.1
Games for Modal Logics

Following the methodology in
Section

4
, we first describe the game-theoretic characterisations of logical
indistinguishability for
\mathcal{ML}
,
\mathcal{GML}
, and
\mathcal{ML}(\mathsf{A})
.

The characterisation of
\mathcal{ML}
exploits the
\ell
-round bisimulation game

[
\citeauthoryearGoranko and
Otto2007
]
.
It is played by Spoiler (him) and Duplicator (her) on a pair of finite pointed models

(\mathfrak{M},v)
and
(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})

using two pebbles, which are initially placed on
v
and
v^{\prime}
.
A game
configuration
is a tuple
(\mathfrak{M},w,\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},w^{\prime})
, stating that one pebble is placed on world
w
in
\mathfrak{M}
, and the other on
w^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{M^{\prime}}
.
The initial configuration is
(\mathfrak{M},v,\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})
.
Each round consists of a move by Spoiler followed by a move of Duplicator, which leads to the next configuration.
In each round, Spoiler chooses a pebble and moves it to a neighbouring (via accessibility relation) world.
If he cannot move the pebble (because there is no neighbour), he loses the game.
Duplicator responds by moving the other pebble (in the other model) to a neighbouring world.
If in some configuration worlds marked by pebbles do not satisfy the same propositions, she loses.
Hence, Duplicator wins the
\ell
-round game, if she has responses for all
\ell
moves of Spoiler, or if Spoiler cannot make a move in some round.
Spoiler wins if Duplicator loses.
We write

(\mathfrak{M},v)\sim^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})

if Duplicator has a winning strategy starting from configuration
(\mathfrak{M},v,\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})
.
Importantly,

\sim^{\ell}
determines indistinguishability of pointed models in
\mathcal{ML}_{\ell}
.
Furthermore, any class of pointed models closed under
\sim^{\ell}
is definable by a (finite) basic modal logic formula as stated next.

Theorem
\thethm
.

[
\citeauthoryearGoranko and
Otto2007
]

For any pointed models
(\mathfrak{M},v)
and
(\mathfrak{M^{\prime}},v^{\prime})
and any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
:
(\mathfrak{M},v)\sim^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M^{\prime}},v^{\prime})
iff
(\mathfrak{M},v)\equiv_{\mathcal{ML}_{\ell}}(\mathfrak{M^{\prime}},v^{\prime})
iff
(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})\models\varphi_{[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{\ell}
.
The
characteristic formulas

\varphi^{\ell}_{[\mathfrak{M},v]}

are defined inductively:

\displaystyle\varphi_{[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{0}

\displaystyle=\bigwedge\{p:(\mathfrak{M},v)\models p\}\land\bigwedge\{\neg p:(\mathfrak{M},v)\not\models p\},

\displaystyle\varphi_{[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{0}\wedge\bigwedge_{(v,w)\in E}\Diamond\varphi_{[\mathfrak{M},w]}^{n}\wedge\Box\bigvee_{(v,w)\in E}\varphi_{[\mathfrak{M},w]}^{n},

for
p\in\mathsf{PROP}
.
Any class
\mathcal{C}
of pointed models closed under
\sim^{\ell}
is definable by the formula

\bigvee_{(\mathfrak{M},v)\in\mathcal{C}}\varphi_{[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{\ell}
.

Conjunctions and disjunctions in characteristic formulas are finite up to logical equivalence, and there are finitely many non-equivalent characteristic formulas for each
\ell
.
Hence, the formula
defining a class
\mathcal{C}
is finite.

The characterisation of
\mathcal{GML}{}
is analogous, but it uses a game variant called
c
-graded
\ell
-round bisimulation games

[
\citeauthoryearOtto2019
]
.
Each round now proceeds in two steps.
(1) Spoiler selects a pebble and a set
U_{1}
of at most
c
neighbours of the world marked by this pebble.
Duplicator responds with a set
U_{2}
of neighbours
of the world marked by the other pebble, such that
|U_{1}|=|U_{2}|
.
(2) Spoiler selects a world in
U_{2}
and Duplicator responds with a world in
U_{1}
.
The rest of the game is the same as in
\ell
-round bisimulation games.
We write

(\mathfrak{M},v)\sim_{\#c}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})

if Duplicator has a winning strategy from
(\mathfrak{M},v,\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})
.
The characterisation of
\mathcal{GML}
with
\sim_{\#c}^{\ell}
is as follows.

Theorem
\thethm
.

[
\citeauthoryearOtto2019
]

For any pointed models
(\mathfrak{M},v)
and
(\mathfrak{M^{\prime}},v^{\prime})
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
:
(\mathfrak{M},v)\sim_{\#c}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M^{\prime}},v^{\prime})
iff
(\mathfrak{M},v)\equiv_{\mathcal{GML}_{c,\ell}}(\mathfrak{M^{\prime}},v^{\prime})
iff
(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})\models\varphi_{\#[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{c,\ell}
.
The
characteristic formulas

\varphi_{\#[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{c,\ell}
are so that
\varphi_{\#[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{c,0}=\varphi_{[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{0}
,

\displaystyle\varphi_{\#[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{c,n+1}=\;\varphi_{\#[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{c,0}\wedge{}

\displaystyle\bigwedge\{\Diamond_{k}\varphi_{\#[\mathfrak{M},w]}^{c,n}:(v,w)\in R,k\leq|U_{w}|,\text{ and }k\leq c\}\land{}

\displaystyle\bigwedge\{\neg\Diamond_{k}\varphi_{\#[\mathfrak{M},w]}^{c,n}:(v,w)\in R\text{ and }|U_{w}|<k\leq c\},

where
\varphi_{[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{0}
is as in Theorem
5.1
,
whereas

U_{w}
is the set of all
u
such that
(v,u)\in R
and
(\mathfrak{M},u)\sim_{\#c}^{n}(\mathfrak{M},w)
.

Moreover, any class
\mathcal{C}
of pointed models closed under
\sim_{\#c}^{\ell}
is definable by the formula

\bigvee_{(\mathfrak{M},v)\in\mathcal{C}}\varphi_{[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{c,\ell}
.

The characterisation of
\mathcal{ML}(\mathsf{A}){}

is obtained with

\ell
-round global bisimulation games

[
\citeauthoryearGoranko and
Otto2007
, Section 5.1]
.
In each round, Spoiler can now choose to play a normal round (as in
\ell
-round bisimulation games) or a global round where players can move pebbles to any worlds (not only neighbours).
We write
(\mathfrak{M},v)\sim^{\ell}_{\forall}(\mathfrak{M^{\prime}},v^{\prime})
if Duplicator has a winning strategy starting at
(\mathfrak{M},v,\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})
.
Relation
\sim^{\ell}_{\forall}
coincides with

\equiv_{\mathcal{ML}(\mathsf{A})}^{\ell}

[
\citeauthoryearGoranko and
Otto2007
]
.
We now show how to complete the characterisation.
To this end, we define characteristic formulas by setting
\varphi_{\forall[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{0}=\varphi_{\#[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{c,0}=\varphi_{[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{0}
and

\displaystyle\varphi_{\forall[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\forall[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{0}\wedge\bigwedge_{(v,w)\in R}\Diamond\varphi_{\forall[\mathfrak{M},w]}^{n}\wedge\Box\bigvee_{(v,w)\in R}\varphi_{\forall[\mathfrak{M},w]}^{n}

\displaystyle\land\bigwedge_{u\in V}\mathsf{E}\varphi_{\forall[\mathfrak{M},w]}^{n}\wedge\mathsf{A}\bigvee_{w\in V}\varphi_{\forall[\mathfrak{M},w]}^{n}.

As before,
a class of pointed models closed under (global) bisimulation is definable using
characteristic formulas.

Theorem
\thethm
.

For any pointed models
(\mathfrak{M},v)
and
(\mathfrak{M^{\prime}},v^{\prime})
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,
(\mathfrak{M},v)\sim^{\ell}_{\forall}(\mathfrak{M^{\prime}},v^{\prime})
iff

(\mathfrak{M},v)\equiv_{\mathcal{ML}(\mathsf{A})_{\ell}}(\mathfrak{M^{\prime}},v^{\prime})
iff

(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})\models\varphi_{\forall[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{\ell}
.
Any class
\mathcal{C}
of pointed models closed under
\sim_{\forall}^{\ell}
is definable by
\bigvee_{(\mathfrak{M},v)\in\mathcal{C}}\varphi_{\forall[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{\ell}
.

Proof.

The first equivalence was already shown
[
\citeauthoryearGoranko and
Otto2007
]
. Furthermore,
(\mathfrak{M},v)\equiv_{\mathcal{ML}(\mathsf{A})_{\ell}}(\mathfrak{M^{\prime}},v^{\prime})
trivially implies
(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})\models\varphi_{\forall[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{\ell}
.
For the converse implication,
we show inductively on
n\leq\ell
that

\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime}\models\varphi_{\forall[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{n}
implies
(\mathfrak{M},v)\sim^{n}_{\forall}(\mathfrak{M^{\prime}},v^{\prime})
.
For
n=0
,
(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})\models\varphi_{\forall[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{0}
implies that
(\mathfrak{M},v)
and
(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})
satisfy the same propositions, so
(\mathfrak{M},v)\sim^{0}_{\forall}(\mathfrak{M^{\prime}},v^{\prime})
.
Next, assume that
(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})\models\varphi_{\forall[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{n+1}
, for some
n
.
If Spoiler plays a normal move, Duplicator has a winning strategy by the same argument as in the proof of
Section

5.1

[
\citeauthoryearGoranko and
Otto2007
, Theorem 32]
.
Hence, assume that Spoiler plays a global move. He either chooses a pebble in
\mathfrak{M}
or in
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
.

Assume that Spoiler selects a pebble in
\mathfrak{M}
and places it on
w\in W
.
By the definition of characteristic formulas,
(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})\models\varphi_{\forall[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{n+1}
implies

(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})\models\mathsf{E}\varphi_{\forall[\mathfrak{M},w]}^{n}
, so there is
w^{\prime}\in W^{\prime}
such that
(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},w^{\prime})\models\varphi_{\forall[\mathfrak{M},w]}^{n}
.
By the inductive hypothesis,
(\mathfrak{M},w)\sim^{n}_{\forall}(\mathfrak{M^{\prime}},w^{\prime})
.
Thus, Duplicator can win
n+1
-round game by placing a pebble on
w^{\prime}
.

Assume that Spoiler selects the pebble in
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
and places it on
w^{\prime}\in W^{\prime}
.
As
(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})\models\varphi_{\forall[\mathfrak{M},v]}^{n+1}
implies

(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})\models\mathsf{A}\bigvee_{w\in V}\varphi_{\forall[\mathfrak{M},w]}^{n}
,
we get
(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},w^{\prime})\models\bigvee_{w\in V}\varphi_{\forall[\mathfrak{M},w]}^{n}
.
So
there is
w\in V
such that

(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})\models\varphi_{\forall[\mathfrak{M},w]}^{n}
.
By the inductive hypothesis,
(\mathfrak{M},w)\sim^{n}_{\forall}(\mathfrak{M^{\prime}},w^{\prime})
.
Thus, Duplicator can win
n+1
-round game by placing a pebble on
w
.
∎

Having established these model-theoretic results, we can now shift our attention to GNNs.

5.2
GNNs Simulating Modal Formulas

We move on to the second step of our methodology and
show that formulas of
\mathcal{ML}
,
\mathcal{GML}
, and
\mathcal{ML}(\mathsf{A})
can be simulated by GNN classifiers from the families
\text{GNN}_{\mathsf{s}}^{\mathsf{AC}}
,
\text{GNN}_{\mathsf{b}}^{\mathsf{AC}}
, and
\text{GNN}_{\mathsf{s}}^{\mathsf{RC}}

For this, we modify the construction simulating
\mathcal{GML}
formulas with unbounded GNNs using unrestricted summation
[
\citeauthoryearBarceló et al.2020
]
.
For
\mathcal{ML}
and
\mathcal{ML}(\mathsf{A})

\mathcal{GML}
we will use max-k-sum (see
Section

3
for definitions of these functions).

Theorem
\thethm
.

The following hold: 1.
\mathcal{ML}\leq\text{GNN}_{\mathsf{s}}^{\mathsf{AC}}
, 2.
\mathcal{GML}\leq\text{GNN}_{\mathsf{b}}^{\mathsf{AC}}
, and 3.
\mathcal{ML}(\mathsf{A})\leq\text{GNN}_{\mathsf{s}}^{\mathsf{AC/RC}}

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be a logical classifier (in a logic from the theorem) of dimension
d

let
\mathsf{PROP}(\varphi)
be the propositions in
\varphi

\mathsf{sub}(\varphi)=(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{L})

is a subformula of
\varphi_{\ell}
, then
k\leq\ell
.
We construct a GNN classifier
\mathcal{N}_{\varphi}
with layers numbered
0,\ldots,L
.
The classification function
\mathsf{cls}
is such that
\mathsf{cls}(\mathbf{x})=\mathsf{true}
iff the last element of
\mathbf{x}
is
1
.
Layer
0
uses a combination function to multiply input vectors by a matrix

\lambda(v)^{(1)}=\lambda(v)\mathbf{D}
,
for all

, where
\mathbf{D}_{k\ell}=1
if the

\varphi_{\ell}
;
other entries of
\mathbf{D}
are
0
.
Hence, it transforms vectors
to dimension
L
.
Other layers are of dimension

and defined below:

•  If
\varphi\in\mathcal{ML}

\mathsf{AC}

\mathsf{agg}

\mathsf{comb}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\sigma(\mathbf{x}\mathbf{C}+\mathbf{y}\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{b})

•  If

.

\mathsf{RC}

\mathsf{AC}

\mathsf{comb}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\sigma(\mathbf{x}\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{y}\mathbf{R})

structurew.

\lambda(v)^{(i)}_{\ell}=1

(\mathfrak{M}_{G},v)\models\mathsf{E}\varphi_{k}
, and otherwise
\lambda(v)^{(i)}_{\ell}=0
.
∎

5.3
Capturing GNNs with Modal Logics

We move to the third step of the methodology from
Section

4
.
We start by showing that classifiers in
\text{GNN}_{\mathsf{s}}^{\mathsf{AC}}
,
\text{GNN}_{\mathsf{b}}^{\mathsf{AC}}
, and
\text{GNN}_{\mathsf{s}}^{\mathsf{AC/RC}}
are closed under the bisimulation games for
\mathcal{ML}
,
\mathcal{GML}
, and
\mathcal{ML}(\mathsf{A})
, respectively.
Combined with
Section

5.1
, this shows that these GNN classifiers correspond to modal logic formulas.

Theorem
\thethm
.

The following hold:

1.

\text{GNN}_{\mathsf{s}}^{\mathsf{AC}}
classifiers with
L
layers are closed under
\sim^{L}

2.

\text{GNN}_{\mathsf{b}}^{\mathsf{AC}}
classifiers with
L
layers and
k
-bounded aggregations are closed under
\sim_{\#k}^{L}

3.

\text{GNN}_{\mathsf{s}}^{\mathsf{AC/RC}}
classifiers with
L
layers are closed under
\sim_{\forall}^{L}

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a GNN classifier from one of the families in the theorem.
We show by induction on
\ell<L
that
(G_{1},v_{1})\sim^{\ell}_{x}(G_{2},v_{2})
implies
\lambda_{1}(v_{1})^{(\ell)}=\lambda_{2}(v_{2})^{(\ell)}
,
where
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
are graphs,
v_{1}
,
v_{2}
any of their nodes, and
\sim^{\ell}_{x}
is one of:
\sim^{\ell}
,
\sim_{\#k}^{\ell}
, or
\sim_{\forall}^{\ell}
, depending on the GNN family.
If
\ell=0
,
then
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
satisfy the same propositions regardless of the bisimulation game, so
\lambda_{1}(v_{1})^{(\ell)}=\lambda_{2}(v_{2})^{(\ell)}
.
For the induction step, assume
(G_{1},v_{1})\sim_{x}^{\ell}(G_{2},v_{2})
for
\ell\leq L
.
We show that

\lambda_{1}(v_{1})^{(\ell)}=\lambda_{2}(v_{2})^{(\ell)}
.

If
\mathcal{N}\in\text{GNN}_{\mathsf{s}}^{\mathsf{AC}}
,
the embeddings are
computed as

\lambda_{i}(v_{i})^{(\ell)}:=\mathsf{comb}\Big(\lambda_{i}(v_{i})^{(\ell-1)},\mathsf{agg}(\lBrace\lambda_{i}(w)^{(\ell-1)}\rBrace_{w\in N_{G_{i}}(v_{i})})

for
i\in\{1,2\}
, and
\mathsf{agg}
being set-based.
Note that
(G_{1},v_{1})\sim_{x}^{\ell}(G_{2},v_{2})

implies
(G_{1},v_{1})\sim_{x}^{\ell-1}(G_{2},v_{2})
so, by the inductive hypothesis,

\lambda_{1}(v_{1})^{(\ell-1)}=\lambda_{2}(v_{2})^{(\ell-1)}
.
So it remains to show

\{\lambda_{1}(w)^{(\ell-1)}\}_{w\in N_{G_{1}}(v_{1})}=\{\lambda_{2}(w)^{(\ell-1)}\}_{w\in N_{G_{2}}(v_{2})}
.
Suppose by contradiction that there is

w_{1}\in N_{G_{1}}(v_{1})
such that

\lambda_{1}(w_{1})^{(\ell-1)}\neq\lambda_{2}(w_{2})^{(\ell-1)}
for all
w_{2}\in N_{G_{2}}(v_{2})
. By the inductive hypothesis,

(G_{1},w_{1})\not\sim^{\ell-1}(G_{2},w_{2})
for all
w_{2}\in N_{G_{2}}(v_{2})
.
Thus, Spoiler can win the
\ell
-round game by moving the pebble in
G_{1}
to
w_{1}
, and so

(G_{1},v_{1})\not\sim^{\ell}(G_{2},v_{2})
, raising a contradiction.

If
\mathcal{N}\in\text{GNN}_{\mathsf{b}}^{\mathsf{AC}}
,

\mathsf{agg}
is
k
-bounded and we must show
\mathsf{agg}(\lBrace\lambda_{1}(w)^{(\ell-1)}\rBrace_{w\in N_{G_{1}}(v_{1})})=\mathsf{agg}(\lBrace\lambda_{2}(w)^{(\ell-1)}\rBrace_{w\in N_{G_{2}}(v_{2})})
.
Assume there is
w_{1}\in N_{G_{1}}(v_{1})
such that
\lambda_{1}(w_{1})^{(\ell-1)}
occurs
k_{1}<k
times in
\lBrace\lambda_{1}(w)^{(\ell-1)}\rBrace_{w\in N_{G_{1}}(v_{1})}

and

k_{2}\neq k_{1}
times
in
\lBrace\lambda_{2}^{(\ell-1)}(w)\rBrace_{w\in N_{G_{2}}(v_{2})}
.
The strategy for Spoiler is to select as
U_{1}
all
w\in N_{G_{1}}(v_{1})
satisfying
\lambda_{1}^{(\ell-1)}(w)=\lambda_{1}^{(\ell-1)}(w_{1})
.
Duplicator must respond with a subset
U_{2}
of
w\in N_{G_{2}}(v_{2})
with
|U_{2}|=|U_{1}|=k_{1}
.
Any such
U_{2}
must contain
w_{2}
such that
\lambda_{2}^{(\ell-1)}(w_{2})\neq\lambda_{1}^{(\ell-1)}(w_{1})
.
The strategy for Spoiler is to choose this
w_{2}
.
Then, whatever
w_{1}^{\prime}\in U_{1}
Duplicator chooses, we have
\lambda_{2}^{(\ell-1)}(w_{2})\neq\lambda_{1}^{(\ell-1)}(w_{1}^{\prime})
.
Hence, by the inductive hypothesis,
(G_{1},v_{1})\not\sim_{\#k}^{\ell-1}(G_{2},v_{2})
, and so
(G_{1},v_{1})\not\sim_{\#k}^{\ell}(G_{2},v_{2})
.

For
\mathcal{N}\in\text{GNN}_{\mathsf{s}}^{\mathsf{AC/RC}}
embeddings are computed as

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(v_{i})^{(\ell)}:=\mathsf{comb}\Big(\lambda_{i}(v_{i})^{(\ell-1)},\mathsf{read}(\lBrace\lambda_{i}(w)^{(\ell-1)}\rBrace_{w\in V_{i}})\Big)

\mathsf{read}

Suppose that

\{\lambda_{1}(w)^{(\ell-1)}\}_{w\in V_{1}}\neq\{\lambda_{2}(w)^{(\ell-1)}\}_{w\in V_{2}}
, so there is

w_{1}\in V_{1}
such that

\lambda_{1}(w_{1})^{(\ell-1)}\neq\lambda_{2}(w_{2})^{(\ell-1)}
for all
w_{2}\in V_{2}
.
Hence, by inductive hypothesis,
(G_{1},w_{1})\not\sim^{\ell-1}_{\forall}(G_{2},w_{2})
for each
w_{2}\in V_{2}
.
Thus,

(G_{1},v_{1})\not\sim^{\ell}_{\forall}(G_{2},v_{2})
, a contradiction.
∎

,
5.1
, and

6.1
Games for Two-variable Logics

Games for two-variable logics
[
\citeauthoryearLibkin2004
]
are
again played on two models
\mathfrak{M}
and
\mathfrak{M^{\prime}}

by Spoiler and Duplicator, but
there are now two pairs of pebbles:
(p_{\mathfrak{M}}^{1},p_{\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}^{1})
and
(p_{\mathfrak{M}}^{2},p_{\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}^{2})
.
After each round, the pebble positions define a mapping
\pi
of two elements in
\mathfrak{M}
into two elements of
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
.
Duplicator has a winning strategy if she can ensure that, after each round,

\pi
is a partial isomorphism between the models.
For node classification, we consider games in which the starting configuration has
p_{\mathfrak{M}}^{1}
and
p_{\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}^{1}
placed on some elements of
\mathfrak{M}
and
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
, respectively.

For
\text{FO}^{2}
we use the
2
-pebble game
, where each round is played as follows. (1) Spoiler chooses a model (say,
\mathfrak{M}
) and
i\in\{1,2\}
, and places
p_{\mathfrak{M}}^{i}
on an element
a
of
\mathfrak{M}
.
(2) Duplicator places
p_{\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}^{i}
on an element
a^{\prime}
of
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
.
We write
(\mathfrak{M},a)\sim_{2}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},a^{\prime})
if Duplicator has a winning strategy when
p_{\mathfrak{M}}^{1}
and
p_{\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}^{1}
are initially on
a
and
a^{\prime}
.

Let an
\text{FO}^{2}

type
of depth at most
\ell
for
(\mathfrak{M},a)
be all
\text{FO}^{2}

formulas
\psi(x)
of depth at most
\ell
such that
\mathfrak{M}\models\psi(a)
.
For a finite vocabulary, there are finitely many such types modulo logical equivalence
[
\citeauthoryearLibkin2004
]
.

Theorem
\thethm
.

[
\citeauthoryearGrädel and
Otto1999
]

For any pointed models
(\mathfrak{M},a)
and
(\mathfrak{M^{\prime}},a^{\prime})
and any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
:

(\mathfrak{M},a)\sim_{2}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},a^{\prime})

iff
a
in
\mathfrak{M}
and
a^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
satisfy the same
\text{FO}^{2}
formulas with one free variable of depth at most
\ell
.
Furthermore, any class
\mathcal{C}
of pointed models closed under
\sim_{2}^{\ell}
is definable by an
\text{FO}^{2}
formula of depth at most
\ell
.

For
\text{C}^{2}
we have
counting 2-pebble games

[
\citeauthoryearGrädel and
Otto1999
]
, where each round is as follows: (1) Spoiler chooses a model (say,
\mathfrak{M}
),
i\in\{1,2\}
, and a subset
U\subseteq V
. Duplicator responds with a subset
U^{\prime}\subseteq V^{\prime}
such that
|U^{\prime}|=|U|
.
(2) Spoiler places pebble

p_{\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}^{i}
on some
u^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}
. Duplicator responds by placing

p_{\mathfrak{M}}^{i}
on some
u\in U
.

Our proofs, however require a variant of this game, which we call
c
-counting
2
-pebble games
, where the set
U
chosen by Spoiler must have cardinality at most
c
.
We write
(\mathfrak{M},a)\sim_{2\#c}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},a^{\prime})
if Duplicator has a winning strategy in the
\ell
-round game starting from a configuration with
p_{\mathfrak{M}}^{1}
and
p_{\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}^{1}
on
a
and
a^{\prime}
, respectively.
As we show next, this game variant characterises
\text{C}^{2}{}
indistinguishability for bounded depth and counting rank.

Theorem
\thethm
.

For any pointed models
(\mathfrak{M},a)
and
(\mathfrak{M^{\prime}},a^{\prime})
and any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
:

(\mathfrak{M},a)\sim_{2\#c}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},a^{\prime})
iff
a
in
\mathfrak{M}
and
a^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
satisfy the same
\text{C}^{2}_{\ell,c}
formulas with one free variable.
Furthermore, any class
\mathcal{C}
of pointed models closed under
\sim_{2\#c}^{\ell}
is definable by a
\text{C}^{2}_{\ell,c}
formula.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
\ell
.
The base case is trivial.
For the inductive step,
assume that there is
\varphi(x)\in\text{C}^{2}_{\ell,c}
such that
\mathfrak{M}\models\varphi(a)
, but
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}\not\models\varphi(a^{\prime})
.
If
\varphi
is a conjunction, some conjunct satisfies the property above; and similarly for negated formulas.
Hence assume that
\varphi(x)=\exists^{n}y.\psi(x,y)
with
n\leq c
.
Then, there exists
U=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\}
such that
\mathfrak{M}\models\psi(a,b_{i})
, for each
b_{i}
.
We show that
(\mathfrak{M},a)\not\sim_{2\#c}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},a^{\prime})
.
The winning strategy for Spoiler is to choose
\mathfrak{M}
,
i=2
, and the set
U
. Because

\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}\not\models\exists^{n}y.\psi(x,y)
, whichever set
U^{\prime}
of size
n
Duplicator chooses, there exists
b^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}
such that

\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}\not\models\psi(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
.
Spoiler places
p_{\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}^{2}
on
b^{\prime}
.
Duplicator needs to place
p_{\mathfrak{M}}^{2}
on some
b\in U
, and the game is in configuration

(\mathfrak{M}_{G},a,b,\mathfrak{M}_{G^{\prime}},a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
.
To show that Spoiler has a winning strategy from here, it suffices to show that he has a winning strategy in an
\ell-1
-round game from
(\mathfrak{M}_{G},b,\mathfrak{M}_{G^{\prime}},b^{\prime})
.
This follows from inductive hypothesis and the facts that
\mathfrak{M}\models\psi(a,b)
, but
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}\not\models\psi(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
.

For the other direction, we show the following implication for any
i\in\{1,2\}
and tuples
\mathbf{a}\in V^{i}
,
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\in V^{\prime i}
.
If
\mathfrak{M}\models\varphi(\mathbf{a})
iff
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}\models\varphi(\mathbf{a}^{\prime})

for all
\text{C}^{2}_{\ell,c}
formulas
\varphi(\mathbf{x})
with
i
free variables, then
(\mathfrak{M},\mathbf{a})\sim_{2\#c}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},\mathbf{a}^{\prime})
.
We prove it by induction on
\ell
.
The base, for
\ell=0
, is trivial.
In the inductive step we need to show a winning strategy for Duplicator.
Assume that Spoiler starts by choosing
\mathfrak{M}
,
i=1
(i.e. a pebble on
\mathbf{a}_{1}
), and a set
U=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
of
n\leq c
elements in
\mathfrak{M}
.
Before we define the set
U^{\prime}
that Duplicator should choose, we
observe that up to the equivalence there are finitely many
\text{C}^{2}_{\ell-1,c}
formulas with at most two free variables.
We call conjunctions of such formulas
types
and we let
T
be the set of all types.
We let the type,
t(\mathfrak{M},\mathbf{a})
, of elements
\mathbf{a}
in
\mathfrak{M}
be the (unique) maximal

\psi(\mathbf{x})\in T
such that
\mathfrak{M}\models\psi(\mathbf{a})
.
Duplicator should choose
U^{\prime}=\{u^{\prime}_{1},\dots,u^{\prime}_{n}\}
of elements in
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
such that
t(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},\mathbf{a}^{\prime}[\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mapsto u^{\prime}_{j}])=t(\mathfrak{M},\mathbf{a}[\mathbf{a}_{1}\mapsto u_{j}])
, for each
u_{j}
, where
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}[\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mapsto u^{\prime}_{j}]
is obtained from
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}
by replacing
\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}
with
u^{\prime}_{j}
.
We observe that such
U^{\prime}
needs to exist.
Otherwise, w.l.o.g.

k
among

\mathbf{a}[\mathbf{a}_{1}\mapsto u_{j}]
in
\mathfrak{M}
have some type
\psi(\mathbf{x})\in T
, but less than
k
among
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}[\mathbf{a}^{\prime}_{1}\mapsto u^{\prime}_{j}]
have this type in
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
.
Hence,
\mathfrak{M}\models\exists^{k}\mathbf{x}_{1}\psi(\mathbf{x})[\mathbf{x}_{2}\mapsto\mathbf{a}_{2}]
, but

\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}\not\models\exists^{k}\mathbf{x}_{1}\psi(\mathbf{x})[\mathbf{x}_{2}\mapsto\mathbf{a}^{\prime}_{2}]
.
Since
k\leq n\leq c
, this is a
\text{C}^{2}_{\ell-1,c}
formula, which contradicts assumption of the inductive step.
Thus,
U^{\prime}
must exist.

Now, assume that Spoiler chooses
u^{\prime}_{j}\in U^{\prime}
.
Duplicator should choose
u_{j}\in U
. Since

t(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},\mathbf{a}^{\prime}[\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mapsto u^{\prime}_{j}])=t(\mathfrak{M},\mathbf{a}[\mathbf{a}_{1}\mapsto u_{j}])
, we obtain that
\mathbf{a}[\mathbf{a}_{1}\mapsto u_{j}]
in
\mathfrak{M}
satisfies the same
\text{C}^{2}_{\ell-1,c}
formulas as
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}[\mathbf{a}^{\prime}_{1}\mapsto u^{\prime}_{j}]
in
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
.
By inductive assumption,

(\mathfrak{M},\mathbf{a}[\mathbf{a}_{1}\mapsto u_{j}])\sim_{\#2}^{c,\ell-1}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},\mathbf{a}^{\prime}[\mathbf{a}^{\prime}_{1}\mapsto u^{\prime}_{j}])
.
Thus,
(\mathfrak{M},\mathbf{a})\sim_{\#2}^{c,\ell}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},\mathbf{a}^{\prime})
.

Finally,
\sim_{2\#c}^{\ell}
has a finite index and each equivalence class is represented by a type. Thus, a class of pointed models closed under
\sim_{2\#c}^{\ell}
is definable by the (finite) disjunction of types for the models in this class.
∎

6.3
Capturing GNNs with 2-Variable Logics

To perform the third step of our methodology, we will show that aggregate-combine-readout
GNN classifiers are closed under the games introduced in
Section

6.1
.

Theorem
\thethm
.

The following hold:

1.

\text{GNN}_{\mathsf{s}}^{\mathsf{ACR}}
classifiers with
L
layers are closed under
\sim_{2}^{L}

2.

\text{GNN}_{\mathsf{b}}^{\mathsf{ACR}}
classifiers with
L
layers and
k
-bounded aggregations and readout are closed under
\sim_{2\#k}^{L}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a GNN classifier with
L
layers applied to
G_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1},\lambda_{1})
and
G_{2}=(V_{2},E_{2},\lambda_{2})
.
We show by induction on
\ell
that

(G_{1},v_{1})\sim^{\ell}_{x}(G_{2},v_{2})
implies
\lambda_{1}(v_{1})^{(\ell)}=\lambda_{2}(v_{2})^{(\ell)}
, for all

,

, and
\sim^{\ell}_{x}\in\{\sim_{2}^{\ell},\sim_{2\#k}^{\ell}\}

.
If
\ell=0
,

(G_{1},v_{2})\sim^{\ell}_{x}(G_{2},v_{2})

\lambda_{1}(v_{1})=\lambda_{2}(v_{2})
, so
\lambda_{1}(v_{1})^{(\ell)}=\lambda_{2}(v_{2})^{(\ell)}
.
For the induction step, let
(G_{1},v_{1})\sim_{x}^{\ell}(G_{2},v_{2})
for

.
We show that

\lambda_{1}(v_{1})^{(\ell)}=\lambda_{2}(v_{2})^{(\ell)}
.

\mathsf{ACR}
layers compute as follows for

:

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(v_{i})^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle:=\mathsf{comb}\Big(\lambda_{i}(v_{i})^{(\ell-1)},\mathsf{agg}(\lBrace\lambda_{i}(w)^{(\ell-1)}\rBrace_{w\in N_{G_{i}}(v_{i})}),

\displaystyle\mathsf{read}(\lBrace\lambda_{i}(w)^{(\ell-1)}\rBrace_{w\in V_{i}})\Big).

Note that
(G_{1},v_{1})\sim_{x}^{\ell}(G_{2},v_{2})

implies
(G_{1},v_{1})\sim_{x}^{\ell-1}(G_{2},v_{2})
so, by the inductive hypothesis,

\lambda_{1}(v_{1})^{(\ell-1)}=\lambda_{2}(v_{2})^{(\ell-1)}
.
Hence, we show (1) that
\mathsf{agg}(\lBrace\lambda_{1}^{\ell-1}(w)\rBrace_{w\in N_{G_{1}}(v_{1})})
equals
\mathsf{agg}(\lBrace\lambda_{2}^{\ell-1}(w)\rBrace_{w\in N_{G_{2}}(v_{2})})

and (2)
\mathsf{read}(\lBrace\lambda_{1}^{\ell-1}(w)\rBrace_{w\in V_{1}})
equals
\mathsf{read}(\lBrace\lambda_{2}^{\ell-1}(w)\rBrace_{w\in V_{2}})
.

If
\mathcal{N}\in\text{GNN}_{\mathsf{s}}^{\mathsf{ACR}}
, aggregation and readout are set-based,
and
\sim^{\ell}_{x}
is

\sim_{2}^{\ell}
.
To show (2), suppose towards a contradiction that there is
w_{1}\in V_{1}
such that no
w_{2}\in V_{2}
has

.

w_{1}\in V_{1}
.
Regardless on the move of Duplicator, the game will be in a position

such that
\lambda_{1}(w_{1})^{(\ell-1)}\neq\lambda_{2}(w_{2})^{(\ell-1)}
.
By inductive hypothesis

(G_{1},w_{1})\not\sim_{2}^{\ell-1}(G_{2},w_{2})
,
so this is indeed a winning strategy for Spoiler, i.e.
(G_{1},v_{1})\not\sim_{2}^{\ell}(G_{2},v_{2})
, raising a contradiction.
To show (1), suppose towards a contradiction that
there is
w_{1}\in N_{G_{1}}(v_{1})
such that there is no
w_{2}\in N_{G_{2}}(v_{2})
with

\lambda_{2}(w_{2})^{(\ell-1)}=\lambda_{1}(w_{1})^{(\ell-1)}

The strategy for Spoiler is to put the pebble

w_{1}
.
Duplicator must put

on some

holds, but

\mathfrak{M}_{G_{1}}
and
\mathfrak{M}_{G_{2}}

Since Duplicator must put a pebble on some
w_{2}\in N_{G_{2}}(v_{2})
,

(\mathfrak{M}_{G_{1}},v_{1},w_{1},\mathfrak{M}_{G_{2}},v_{2},w_{2})
such that
\lambda_{1}(w_{1})^{(\ell-1)}\neq\lambda_{2}(w_{2})^{(\ell-1)}
.
Using the inductive hypothesis, Spoiler has a winning strategy, raising a contradiction.

\mathcal{N}\in\text{GNN}_{\mathsf{b}}^{\mathsf{ACR}}

k

Finally, to show (1), assume towards a contradiction that

7
Conclusion and Future Work

We have introduced families of bounded GNNs, whose expressive power corresponds to well-known 2-variable first-order logics and modal logics. While our results show that all GNN classifiers from the introduced families are definable in
FO
, establishing the converse implication remains open.
Our results, together with those in
[
\citeauthoryearBarceló et al.2020
]
, imply also that
an
\mathsf{AC}
GNN classifier is
FO
-expressible if and only if it is equivalent to a bounded
\mathsf{AC}
GNN classifier.
We conjecture that an analogous result holds for
\mathsf{ACR}
layers, that is an
\mathsf{ACR}
GNN classifier is
FO
-expressible if and only if it is equivalent to a bounded
\mathsf{ACR}
GNN classifier.
We leave verification of this hypothesis as an interesting open problem. Future work also includes establishing tight bounds on the size of logical formulas capturing GNNs.

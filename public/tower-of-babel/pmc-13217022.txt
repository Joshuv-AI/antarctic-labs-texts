Aggravation of rainbow sign on hematoma membrane by arterial spin labeling predicts chronic subdural hematoma recurrence following middle meningeal artery embolization
Authors: Wenyan Li, Zhouyang Jiang, Bingyan Tao, Qiang Zhang, Xian Zhang, Weina Li, Mingrong Li, Yujie Chen, Rong Hu, Hua Feng, Yin Niu, Zhi Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRecurrence remains a critical challenge in patients with chronic subdural hematoma (CSDH) treated with middle meningeal artery embolization (MMAE), yet the underlying mechanisms and reliable predictors of this phenomenon remain poorly understood. Current predictors of recurrence rely on invasive or radiation-based techniques, prompting the need for non-invasive imaging markers. This study aimed to evaluate the ‘rainbow sign aggravation’ by arterial spin labeling (ASL) on the hematoma membrane as a predictor of CSDH recurrence after MMAE.MethodsWe retrospectively studied ASL scans from 100 patients with CSDH who underwent MMAE between October 2022 and March 2025. The rainbow sign was defined as the hyperperfusion on the outer hematoma membrane. Inter-rater reliability, univariate analyses, and logistic regression were used to assess associations between the rainbow sign and recurrence.ResultsIn the 100 patients who underwent MMAE, the pre-embolization rainbow sign was identified in 29 (29.0%) cases while the post-embolization rainbow sign was observed in eight (8.0%). Post-embolization rainbow sign and rainbow sign aggravation were significantly correlated with recurrence (P<0.001 for both), while no recurrence occurred in patients with a resolved rainbow sign. Subgroup analysis showed that rainbow sign aggravation was correlated with persistent headache (χ²=32.683, P<0.001), motor strength weakness (χ²=51.981, P<0.001), and worse neurological outcomes (χ²=33.076, P<0.001).ConclusionThe dynamic changes in the rainbow sign following MMAE correlated with the treatment response, with rainbow sign aggravation a significant predictor of hematoma recurrence. Integrating this marker into clinical assessments might enable personalized treatment strategies to reduce recurrence and improve outcomes in patients with CSDH.Keywords: MRI, Stroke, Intervention

Introduction

Chronic subdural hematoma (CSDH) represents a prevalent intracranial hemorrhagic condition which predominantly affects the elderly population.1 With the increasing aging demographic and the widespread use of anticoagulants, the incidence of CSDH is projected to rise to 17.4/100 000 by 2030.2 Although burr hole drainage remains the primary treatment to achieve rapid hematoma resolution, the recurrence rate can be as high as 37%.3 Recurrent episodes pose a significant threat to patients and also substantially increase the mortality rate to 32% on repeated drainage procedures.4 Pathophysiologically, CSDH arises from the hypervascular neomembrane which is primarily supplied by the middle meningeal artery (MMA), making MMA embolization (MMAE) potentially a targeted definitive therapeutic approach.5 However, inconsistent success rates of MMAE highlight the need to investigate the mechanisms and improved predictors of treatment response.

Previous investigations have emphasized the role of vascular architecture in CSDH recurrence. Nakagawa et al identified a ‘cotton wool-like’ feature with DynaCT in 381 patients as a marker of CSDH recurrence.6 In a retrospective study including 636 embolizations, Salem et al correlated MMA diameter <1.5 mm with recurrence via superselective angiography.7 These studies underscore the importance of blood supply assessment in disease aggravation and treatment outcomes. However, the methodologies rely on invasive or radiation-based techniques, limiting their clinical applicability—especially in elderly patients or those with renal dysfunction.

Arterial spin labeling (ASL) imaging, a non-invasive and non-ionizing MRI technique, offers distinct advantages by visualizing tissue perfusion through magnetically-labeled arterial water protons as an endogenous tracer.8 Widely applied in the assessment of various nervous system disorders such as meningioma blood supply, dural arteriovenous fistula, and ischemic stroke hemorrhagic transformation,9,11 ASL has also been shown to be useful in the evaluation of brain perfusion in patients with CSDH.12 A previous study has demonstrated the association between perfusion of the hematoma membrane with ASL and CSDH progression.13 However, to substantiate these findings, larger-scale validation with more detailed analyses is warranted.

In this study, ASL was performed on consecutive patients with CSDH enrolled in our center and the ‘rainbow sign’ was defined as the hyperperfusion on the hematoma membrane. The aim is to highlight its potential value in depicting the blood supply of the hematoma membrane and predicting post-MMAE outcomes.

Methods

Study participants

This is a single-center retrospective study under the institutional Ethics Committee. A total of 155 consecutive patients were referred to our center from October 2022 to March 2025 with a confirmed diagnosis of CSDH. The inclusion criteria were age ≥18 years and a definitive diagnosis of CSDH by MRI or CT scans on admission. Exclusion criteria were: (1) acute subdural hemorrhage secondary to brain trauma; (2) being secondary to cerebrovascular malformations, arachnoid cysts, intracranial tumors, craniotomy, etc; (3) contraindicated for cerebral angiography; (4) unable to tolerate MMAE due to severe comorbidities or poor general conditions; and (5) pregnancy or lactation. The included patients underwent standalone MMAE, except for those who required burr hole drainage as a rescue for recurrence. Negative imaging references: from October 2022 to March 2025, 356 consecutive patients using CSDH as an exclusion underwent ASL scans with identical parameters to the CSDH cohort. Images were analyzed by the identical raters who evaluated the CSDH cohort, with three randomly selected cases illustrated.

Study design and ASL imaging

Axial T1/T2 and ASL scans with a 3T MRI machine (Syngo MR D13, Siemens) were performed on consecutive patients who were treated with MMAE and non-surgical management. The 100 enrolled patients undergoing MMAE received ASL imaging pre-embolization and 7 days post-embolization, with follow-up CT scans at 1 and 3 months after treatment. Typical sequences for demonstration of hematoma included axial T1 and T2 weighted images. In our ASL imaging, the radiofrequency pulse for proton labeling was applied by pseudo-continuous ASL. The parameters were as follows: number of slices=48; field of view (FoV) read=200 mm; repetition time (TR)=5000 ms; echo time (TE)=36.32 ms; echo-planar imaging (EPI) factor=21; voxel size=3.1×3.1×3.0 mm; bandwidth=752 Hz/Px; bolus duration=700 ms; postlabeling delay (PLD) (inversion time)=1990 ms. The ‘rainbow sign’ was defined as: (1) hyper-intensified perfusion; (2) localization to hematoma outer membrane; (3) continuous hyperperfusion (≥3.0 cm longitudinal extension across ≥2.0 cm in the Z-axis). The rainbow sign was verified by two neurointerventionists and two neuroradiologists who had a minimum of 10 years’ experience. Ambiguous cases were reviewed and determined by a senior neuroradiologist with 25 years’ experience.

ASL quantitative analysis

The acquired ASL images (.dcm) were converted by the software MRIcron (v1.0.20190902) to generate NIFTI files (.nii). Furthermore, manual region of interest (ROI) delineation on the ASL images was performed using the software MRIcron: (1) for the hyperperfusion value, the area with the highest perfusion intensity on the hematoma membrane and the adjacent two layers was delineated as the ROI; (2) for the reference ROI, the white matter contralateral to the hematoma was selected. It was a circular area with a diameter of 0.5 cm, located 1 cm lateral to the midline, at the level of the top of the corpus callosum, and 1 cm in front of the lateral ventricle, together with the adjacent two layers (for the reference ROI of the bilateral hematoma the mean value on both sides was applied). The manual delineation was completed by two neurointerventionists and two neuroradiologists (with 10 years’ experience). Using the SPM12 toolbox, the following steps were implemented in the MATLAB (version 2016b): (1) the mean ROI signal was extracted from the transformed ASL images; and (2) the normalized perfusion was calculated as the mean signal in the hyperperfusion ROI divided by the mean signal of the reference ROI.

Treatment procedure

MMAE was carried out by experienced neurointerventionists in our center. During the embolization the triaxial system was applied. In general, a 6F femoral sheath was advanced into the common carotid artery, external carotid artery, and MMA separately, under local anesthesia and without IV heparin. Dangerous anastomoses such as those with the ophthalmic artery were checked by superselective angiography before embolization. Embolization with small-sized (100–300 µm) polyvinyl alcohol particles was preferred due to its safety and efficacy.14 The need for anastomosis or proximal coiling was left to the discretion of the neurointerventionists during embolization.

Study outcome

The main clinical outcome was recurrence of the hematoma, which was defined as increased hematoma thickness post-MMAE on radiographic imaging (<50% of maximum thickness reduction on the last available follow-up MRI or CT examinations, with the minimum imaging follow-up of 1 month) or neurologic deterioration requiring a rescue burr hole drainage. Secondary outcomes included the symptoms and neurologic deterioration at the last follow-up, assessed via headache severity (≥1 point increase in VRS-6 scaling), motor deficit (≥1 grade decline in muscle strength), and modified Rankin Scale score (≥1 point increase).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 22.0, IBM). Graphs were created by GraphPad Prism (version 8). Continuous data were expressed as mean (SD) and discrete data as frequency (%). Group comparisons for non-parametric data were conducted with the Mann–Whitney U test. Inter-rater reliability was evaluated using Fleiss’ kappa (multi-rater agreement) and Cohen’s kappa (binary consistency) among the four raters. Logistic regression analysis was conducted to assess the independent predictors for hematoma recurrence, with variance inflation factor (VIF) assessing collinearity and bootstrap resampling (1000 iterations) ensuring model stability due to limited recurrence events. Fisher exact tests compared pre- and post-embolization rainbow sign frequencies. All statistical analyses with a two-sided P value of <0.05 were considered to be significantly different.

Univariate analyses of pre-embolization hematoma characteristics, MMA parameters, and rainbow sign metrics showed no significant associations with recurrence (table 2). Conversely, the presence of a post-embolization rainbow sign and rainbow sign aggravation—defined as new hyperperfusion or intensified signal compared with pre-embolization imaging—showed statistically significant differences between the recurrence and non-recurrence groups (P<0.001 for both, see table 2). Notably, seven of the eight patients with a post-embolization rainbow sign experienced recurrence, whereas 91 of the 92 without the sign achieved satisfactory recovery. However, high collinearity between the rainbow sign and its aggravation (VIF=8.280) precluded multivariable regression modeling (see online supplemental table S2). Given that rainbow sign aggravation more effectively reflected the treatment dynamics, this variable was selected for subsequent subgroup analyses, showing a strong association with adverse outcomes (figure 1). Patients with rainbow sign aggravation were found to have significantly higher rates of recurrence (χ2=74.672, P<0.001), unrelieved headache (χ2=32.683, P<0.001), non-enhanced muscle strength (χ2=51.981, P<0.001), and a stable or worsened modified Rankin Scale score (χ2=33.076, P<0.001).

Discussion

Although MMAE is increasingly recognized as an effective treatment for patients with CSDH, recurrence remains a critical challenge in a subset of patients, with poorly understood predictors. In this proof-of-concept study we found that rainbow sign aggravation was a robust predictor of CSDH recurrence. Integrating this novel imaging metric into perioperative evaluations of CSDH would refine therapeutic strategies and potentially reduce recurrence rates.

The pathophysiology of CSDH is intrinsically associated with the formation of a neomembrane with leaky capillaries prone to intermittent rupture and hematoma expansion. Thus, direct assessment of neomembrane perfusion is critical for understanding disease progression and treatment response. Prior studies using CT/MRI classified hematoma architecture15,17 or identified clinical factors18 19 associated with recurrence, but these approaches lack direct assessment of post-treatment perfusion dynamics. In contrast, ASL imaging provides non-invasive radiation-free visualization of membrane perfusion, aligning more closely with the disease pathophysiology. This has been validated in subdural neoplastic infiltration, where ASL detects hyperperfusion on dura.20 By evaluating pre- and post-MMAE perfusion, our study offers a dynamic perspective on treatment efficacy, which is particularly valuable for elderly patients intolerant of invasive angiography or contrast agents.

To ensure robust validation of the rainbow sign, both qualitative classification and quantitative ASL perfusion values were integrated into the study design. Qualitative evaluation relied on standardized visual criteria, while quantitative assessment involved manual ROI delineation on ASL images to calculate normalized perfusion ratios. Inter-rater consistency of rainbow sign classification by four observers showed reproducible visual interpretation with Fleiss’ kappa values of 0.951 (pre-embolization) and 0.906 (post-embolization), and inter-rater reliability of quantitative ASL perfusion values in the hematoma membrane showed excellent consistency with values of 0.998 (95% CI 0.997 to 0.998) pre-embolization and 0.996 (95% CI 0.994 to 0.997) post-embolization. Concordance between rainbow sign classification and quantitative ASL value stratification was assessed using ROC analysis (Cohen’s kappa=0.740, P<0.001 pre-embolization and Cohen's kappa=1.000, P<0.001 post-embolization). These analyses established the rainbow sign as a reliable and generalizable imaging metric. Given that qualitative rainbow sign classification provides immediate clinical utility requiring no specialized software or post-processing, it was particularly suitable for rapid bedside decision-making.21

In our cohort the pre-embolization rainbow sign was observed in 29.0% of patients, reflecting the hypervascular nature of the CSDH neomembrane22—likely corresponding to the ‘cotton wool-like’ microvascular networks identified via DynaCT and angiography.6 MMAE effectively eliminated this sign in 93.1% of cases (27/29) with no recurrence in these patients, confirming its role as a marker of successful blood supply interruption. Conversely, rainbow sign aggravation was associated with an 87.5% recurrence rate, suggesting persistent perfusion via atypical collateral vasculature such as the enlarged anterior falcine artery shown in our representative case (figure 2E,F). Importantly, the sign might also aid in localizing residual contributing vasculatures (see figure 2C), enabling targeted embolization.

There are still some exceptions in this study which require explanation. It was notable that one patient had negative pre- and post-embolization rainbow signs but still had a recurrence 1 week following treatment. This patient had a large hematoma (28 mm thickness and 12.3 mm midline shift), with the mass effect likely overwhelming and necessitating adjunctive surgical intervention. Another patient had a new post-embolization rainbow sign, which meant rainbow sign aggravation, yet recovered satisfactorily. Potential mechanisms include temporary leakage from terminal vessels due to embolic polyvinyl alcohol agent-induced inflammatory stimulation detected by ASL on day 7,23 and sufficient occlusion of the dominant blood supply by MMAE, attenuating reaccumulation despite residual minor perfusion on the hematoma membrane.24 These assumptions require validation in future studies with expanded sample sizes and longitudinal ASL follow-ups.

Conclusion

This study introduced the rainbow sign, which is a distinct hyperperfusion pattern on the hematoma membrane visualized by ASL in a subset of patients with CSDH. Resolution of the rainbow sign indicates effective vascular occlusion, while its aggravation was strongly associated with hematoma recurrence. Integrating this marker into clinical practice may facilitate timely identification of patients at high risk of recurrence, enabling targeted salvage interventions to improve outcomes. Future investigations with larger multicenter cohorts and extended follow-up are needed to validate its prognostic value and solidify its role in guiding personalized embolization for patients with CSDH.

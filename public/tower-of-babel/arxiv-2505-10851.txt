Transfer of Approximation properties under Local Constraints and Best Simultaneous Approximation on Sums
Syamantak Das

Abstract
. It is folklore that the sum of two M M -ideals (semi M M -ideals) is also an M M -ideal (a semi M M -ideal). Numerous authors have attempted to investigate such properties of subspaces. This article explores two important aspects of approximation theory within Banach spaces and how these properties remain intact when considering the sum of two subsets. Recall the notion of ( G ​ C ) (GC) introduced by Veselý that encloses two aforementioned properties. When the sum of two subspaces is closed, we discuss various properties of the sum if one of the subspaces has these properties. Counterexamples are produced that establish nonaffirmativeness for the properties ( G ​ C ) (GC) and the central subspace. We answer a problem raised by the author in [ Best constrained approximation in Banach spaces , Numer. Funct. Anal. Optim. 36 (2) (2015), 248–255]. We extend our observations related to the best simultaneous approximations to the properties ( P 1 ) (P_{1}) and ℱ \mathscr{F} -SACP.

2.1.
Motivation

In this section, we establish that, under local constraints,

Throughout the section,

We use the local-constraint hypothesis: for each

2.2.
Central subspaces under local constraints

Our first positive result shows that, under local constraints,

to the intersection

Theorem 2.1
.

Let

be subspaces of

Z_{2}=Z_{1}\cap Y
. Suppose

is locally constrained w.r.t.
Z_{1}
via

is a central subspace of

Proof.

Let
\{z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}\}\subseteq Z_{2}
and
\cap_{i=1}^{n}B_{Y}(z_{i},r_{i})\neq\emptyset
. Then, the balls
(B_{X}(z_{i},r_{i}))_{i=1}^{n}
have a nonempty intersection in

and thus, by our assumption, there exists
z\in Z_{1}
such that
z\in\cap_{i=1}^{n}B_{X}(z_{i},r_{i})\cap Z_{1}
. Since
Z_{2}
is locally constrained w.r.t.
Z_{1}
via
Y
, there exist two projections
Q:\spn\{z,Y\}\rightarrow Y
and
P:\spn\{z,Z_{2}\}\rightarrow Z_{2}
such that
Pz=Qz
. Then, for all
i=1,\cdots,n
, we have,
\|Qz-z_{i}\|=\|P(z-z_{i})\|\leq\|z-z_{i}\|\leq r_{i}
. Thus, we have
Qz\in\cap_{i=1}^{n}B_{Y}(z_{i},r_{i})\cap Y
.
∎

\textrm{rad}_{Y+Z}^{f}(F)=\textrm{rad}_{Z}^{f}(F-\{y_{0}\})
. Let
W
be a
\tau
-neighbourhood of 0 and
W_{1},W_{2}
be two
\tau
-neighbourhoods of 0 such that
W_{1}+W_{2}\subseteq W
. Now, by our assumption, there exists
\delta>0
such that
\delta
-
\textrm{Cent}_{Z}^{f}(F-\{y_{0}\})\subseteq\textrm{Cent}_{Z}^{f}(F-\{y_{0}\})+W_{1}
. Now there exists
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
z_{n_{k}}\in\delta-\textrm{Cent}_{Y+Z}^{f}(F)
for all
k\geq N_{1}
. Thus there exists a sequence
(s_{k})_{k\geq N_{1}}\subseteq\textrm{Cent}_{Z}^{f}(F-\{y_{0}\})
such that
z_{n_{k}}-s_{k}\in W_{1}
for all
k\geq N_{1}
. Now as
r_{f}(s_{k},F-\{y_{0}\})=\textrm{rad}_{Z}^{f}(F-\{y_{0}\})=\textrm{rad}_{Y+Z}^{f}(F)
, we have the sequence
(s_{k}+y_{0})\subseteq\textrm{Cent}_{Y+Z}^{f}(F)
. Since
y_{n_{k}}\rightarrow y_{0}
, there exists
N_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
y_{n_{k}}-y_{0}\in W_{2}
for all
k\geq N_{2}
. Thus,
y_{n_{k}}+z_{n_{k}}-s_{k}-y_{0}\in W_{1}+W_{2}\subseteq W
for all
k\geq\max\{N_{1},N_{2}\}
. Hence,
y_{n_{k}}+z_{n_{k}}\in\textrm{Cent}_{Y+Z}^{f}(F)+W
for all
k\geq\max\{N_{1},N_{2}\}
and this concludes our assertion.
∎

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
Y
be a finite dimensional subspace of
X
, and
Z
be a subspace of
X
such that
(X,Z,\mathcal{F}(X))
has
\tau
-
\mathscr{F}_{cmc}
-
\bf{\emph{SACP}}
. Then,
(X,Y+Z,\mathcal{F}(X))
has
\tau
-
\mathscr{F}_{cmc}
-
\bf{\emph{SACP}}
.

Proof.

Let
F=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}\}\in\mathcal{F}(X)
,
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a function in
\mathscr{F}_{cmc}
. Suppose
(y_{n})\subseteq Y
,
(z_{n})\subseteq Z
be such that
r_{f}(y_{n}+z_{n},F)\rightarrow\textrm{rad}_{Y+Z}^{f}(F)
. Since
f
is coercive,
(y_{n}+z_{n})
is bounded, and hence from Theorem
3.4
,
(y_{n})
and
(z_{n})
are bounded. Since
Y
is finite dimensional,
(y_{n})
has a convergent subsequence
(y_{n_{k}})
converging to
y_{0}\in Y
. Now proceeding in a similar way as in the proof of Theorem
3.9
, we have
r_{f}(z_{n_{k}},F-\{y_{0}\})\rightarrow\textrm{rad}_{Z}^{f}(F-\{y_{0}\})
and so, by our assumption,
(z_{n_{k}})
has a
\tau
-convergent subsequence
(z_{n_{l}})
. Thus
(y_{n}+z_{n})
has a
\tau
-convergent subsequence
(y_{n_{l}}+z_{n_{l}})
, and this establishes our assertion.
∎

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
Y
be a reflexive subspace of
X
and
Z
be a subspace of
X
such that
Y+Z
is closed and
(X,Z,\mathcal{F}(X))
has weak-
\mathscr{F}_{cmc}
-
\bf{\emph{SACP}}
. Then,
(X,Y+Z,\mathcal{F}(X))
has weak-
\mathscr{F}_{cmc}
-
\bf{\emph{SACP}}
.

Proof.

Let
F=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}\}\in\mathcal{F}(X)
,
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a function in
\mathscr{F}_{cmc}
. Let
(y_{n})
be a sequence in
Y
and
(z_{n})
be a sequence in
Z
such that
r_{f}(y_{n}+z_{n},F)\rightarrow\textrm{rad}_{Y+Z}^{f}(F)
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
(r_{f}(y_{n}+z_{n},F))
is a decreasing sequence converging to
\textrm{rad}_{Y+Z}^{f}(F)
. Since
f
is coercive,
(y_{n}+z_{n})
is bounded, and from Theorem
3.4
, both
(y_{n})
and
(z_{n})
are bounded. Since
X
is reflexive,
(y_{n})
has a weakly convergent subsequence, also denoted by
(y_{n})
, converging to
y_{0}\in Y
. We only need to show that
(z_{n})
has a weakly convergent subsequence.

Suppose
(z_{n})
has no weakly convergent subsequence. Then by
[
8
, Theorem 1]
, there exists a
\theta>0
and a subsequence of
(z_{n})
, also denoted by
(z_{n})
such that
d(co(z_{1},\cdots,z_{k}),co(z_{k+1},z_{k+2},\cdots))\geq\theta
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Now there exists a sequence of convex combinations
\tilde{y}_{n}=\sum_{i\in I_{n}}\lambda_{i}y_{i}
, where
I_{n}=\{i:p_{n}<i\leq p_{n+1}\}
,
(p_{n})
is an increasing sequence of natural numbers,
\lambda_{i}>0
for all
i\in I_{n}
and
\sum_{i\in I_{n}}\lambda_{i}=1
, such that
\|\tilde{y}_{n}-y_{0}\|\rightarrow 0
. Let us define
\tilde{z}_{n}=\sum_{i\in I_{n}}\lambda_{i}z_{i}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now, as done in the proof of Theorem
3.6
, we have
r_{f}(\tilde{z}_{n}+\tilde{y}_{n},F)\rightarrow\textrm{rad}_{Y+Z}^{f}(F)
.
Thus, using the continuity of
f
on bounded subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
, we have
r_{f}(\tilde{z}_{n},F-\{y_{0}\})=f(\|\tilde{z}_{n}+y_{0}-x_{1}\|,\cdots,\|\tilde{z}_{n}+y_{0}+x_{N}\|)\leq f(\|\tilde{z}_{n}+\tilde{y}_{n}-x_{1}\|+\|\tilde{y}_{n}-y_{0}\|,\cdots,\|\tilde{z}_{n}+\tilde{y}_{n}-x_{N}\|+\|\tilde{y}_{n}-y_{0}\|)\rightarrow\textrm{rad}_{Y+Z}^{f}(F)\leq\textrm{rad}_{Z}^{f}(F-\{y_{0}\})
. By our assumption,
(\tilde{z}_{n})
has a weakly convergent subsequence
(\tilde{z}_{n_{k}})
. Now
\tilde{z}_{n_{k}}\in co(z_{{p_{n_{k}}}+1},\cdots,z_{p_{{n_{k}}+1}})\subseteq co(z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,z_{p_{n_{k}+1}})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus,
d(co(\tilde{z}_{n_{1}},\cdots,\tilde{z}_{n_{k}}),co(\tilde{z}_{n_{k+1}},\tilde{z}_{{n_{k+2}}},\cdots))\geq d(co(z_{1},\cdots,z_{p_{n_{k}+1}}),co(z_{p_{{n_{k}+1}}+1},\cdots))\geq\theta
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, a contradiction to the fact that
(\tilde{z}_{n_{k}})
is weakly convergent. This contradiction establishes our assertion.
∎

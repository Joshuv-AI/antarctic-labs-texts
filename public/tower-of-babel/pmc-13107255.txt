Focus on Allied Health Professionals announcement: Introducing practice change initiative reports for the Focus on Allied Health Professionals section
Authors: Heather M Ross
Published: 2026

What is a case? An interesting singular patient event? An unusual finding? A great save in an interventional procedure? Yes, all these are cases.

What about implementing a practice change in a real-world clinical setting? Every practice setting is different. The issues associated with a practice change initiative to improve health care and move toward the quintuple aim1 vary, depending on the practice’s institutional and community setting. Depending on personnel, patient mix, local region, available technology, management infrastructure and more, the local experience of implementing practice change can surface valuable lessons to inform clinical practice in other settings with similar characteristics. However, when we look to the peer-reviewed research literature for real-world guidance about implementation, all too often we are met with silence.

Part of the reason for this limitation is that there is little opportunity in the peer-reviewed literature to publish high-quality implementation science, particularly in single-site settings. The research literature focuses largely on preclinical and controlled clinical findings. The epidemiology literature focuses on population health research. The case report literature focuses mainly on diagnostic or interventional findings and experiences in individual patients. Some in the scientific community argue that an implementation project is not a study designed to produce generalizable knowledge. The argument continues that as the findings of a single-site implementation effort are not applicable in other settings, they do not merit publication in the indexed literature. And so, the rich homegrown knowledge gained from practice change experiences remains largely sequestered at home, and we continue to experience a long time gap in translation from research to practice.2

In concert with the Heart Rhythm Society’s mission to improve the care of patients by promoting research, education, and optimal health care policies and standards, we aim to narrow that time gap in translation to clinical practice by expanding our understanding of a case to include practice change initiatives. We recognize that practice change initiatives are often led by heart rhythm team members who are not traditional researchers or implementation scientists whose work is geared toward peer-reviewed publication. We plan to change that by creating a venue for peer-reviewed publication of practice change initiatives led by team members not typically visible in the literature. HeartRhythm Case Reports is introducing a new phase of the Focus on Allied Health Professionals section. Building on the legacy established by the inaugural section editor, Dr Aileen Ferrick, we are pleased to announce that the Focus on Allied Health Professionals section will now focus on practice change initiatives in heart rhythm care settings.

This change will offer expanded opportunities for allied health professionals to contribute their meaningful expertise to the research literature, acknowledging that allied health professionals are often the team members leading practice change initiatives focused on patient care. Further, recognizing that practice change initiatives are carried out systematically with a scholarly, evidence-based approach, we are pleased to invite the allied health professional community to share their practice change case experiences.

These Focus on Allied Health Professionals manuscripts will be structured as brief reports of no more than 1500 words, including references, or no more than 1000 words if a small table or figure is included. Importantly, these practice change initiative cases will be indexed on PubMed Central, searchable, and an accessible source of guidance that other practices may leverage to initiate their own practice change initiatives locally.

We want to recognize that the allied health professional community in the Heart Rhythm Society is robust and often first to the table to share this kind of practice-based initiative experience. The Heart Rhythm Society online discussion forum is full of these kinds of wonderful exchanges, offers of guidance, and the kind of insightful information that has rarely found its way into the formal literature before now. We are confident that this evolution of HeartRhythm Case Reports will afford an important source of access to information that was previously cloaked in online forums or limited to our personal “who-you-know” networks that have long been a bastion of innovation in heart rhythm care. We hope that by highlighting practice change initiatives, so often led by allied health professional team members, we will continue to accelerate the path toward our shared vision to end death and suffering due to heart rhythm disorders. Further, we will make more visible the real-world contributions of the allied health professional team.

Allied health professional team members are still strongly encouraged to share their interesting individual case reports in the usual manner of case report submissions for the journal. This change will, we hope, further illuminate the clinical experiences of allied health professionals by including these cases as part of the larger body of case reports. Focus on Allied Health Professionals submissions—now with the focus of reporting a practice change initiative—will continue to have the article processing fee waived for maximum publication accessibility. We invite all allied health professionals to review the full description of manuscript categories at https://www.heartrhythmcasereports.com/content/authorinfo.

As the section editor, I want to thank every allied health professional in heart rhythm care for the incredible work that you do to care for patients and advance our field as integral members of the heart rhythm care team. Let us make sure together that your scholarly expertise is recognized, appreciated, and, most importantly, applied in our ongoing efforts to optimize our systems for patient care, provider, well-being, and future discovery. We look forward to featuring your important practice change initiative work in upcoming issues of HeartRhythm Case Reports.

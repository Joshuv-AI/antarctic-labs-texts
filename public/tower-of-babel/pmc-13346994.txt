A nature-inspired osprey optimization based feature selection framework with stacking ensemble model for imbalance-aware credit card fraud detection
Authors: Bharat Kumar Padhi, Bighnaraj Naik, Rajat Kumar Sahu, Rohit Kumar, Debahuti Mishra
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCredit card fraud detection has emerged as a critical issue because of the extremely skewed data of transactions and the existence of redundant or non-critical features that influence model performance. This paper presents an ideal feature selection model, which was developed around the Feature Selection using Osprey Optimization Algorithm, a nature based optimization algorithm, and a stacking ensemble classifier. The proposed method would apply a stacking ensemble of Naive Bayes, Decision Tree, Support Vector Machine and Random Forest classifiers as the base learners with the Logistic Regression as the meta-learner. The ensemble-based assessment allows identifying the relevant features strongly and efficiently can cope with class imbalance. Results of experimental work with a real-world credit card fraud dataset show that the suggested approach enhances the performance of fraud detectors in terms of precision, recall, F1-score and Matthews correlation coefficient and reduces the dimensionality of features. The outcomes prove the efficiency of the advanced algorithm on the improvement of the classification results with imbalance financial dataset.Keywords: Financial fraud, Feature selection, Nature-inspired algorithm, Wrapper method, Osprey algorithmSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

As online commerce expands, so does the sophistication of credit card fraud, posing a persistent threat to financial stability1. Banks, having suffered significant operational damage, are now forced to engineer more responsive prevention mechanisms. Feature Selection (FS) plays an important role in the identification of credit card fraud when utilizing Machine Learning (ML) techniques, as the chosen features must be relevant and informative. Despite the usefulness of ML techniques for fraud detection, some limitations exist in terms of the performance of single and hybrid ML algorithms on specific datasets2. Day by day complexity of credit card fraud are increasing rapidly and banks are struggling to identify it. ML has emerged as a dependable tool used by banks to detect and mitigate credit card fraud in transactions.

In 2021, the United States witnessed a significant rise in malicious cyber activity, affecting both private businesses and the public sector, as illustrated in Figure 1. The FBI’s Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) logged 847,376 complaints, reflecting a 7% increase compared to the previous year. Potential losses reached $6.9 billion in 2020, involving diverse threats such as ransomware, cryptocurrency scams, and Business E-mail Compromise (BEC). Among these, BEC accounted for 19,954 complaints, resulting in adjusted losses of $2.4 billion3 in 2021.

The quick developing of the new technologies contributed to the appearance of great amounts of data provided by different sources that presents serious problems in terms of data processing and analysis due to the huge volume of data. It is hard and time-consuming to extract useful information in these repositories manually. Moreover, such datasets often have numerous features some of which are irrelevant, noisy, redundant and thus more expensive to compute and reduce model performance. In data mining, FS is vital because it involves determining the best variables to use in analyzing or predicting. The objective of FS is to find the most relevant subset of features in the original feature space, often the process of data pre-processing, is required in order to improve representation. The FS could be done with a variety of techniques; classification, prediction, and clustering are some of these methods depending on the job. The wrapper approach is one of the most common approaches, in which the feature subset selection tool incorporates the induction mechanism. This strategy assumes the assessment strategy as a black box to seek the best feature set. Precisely, wrapper method4 is a black box application of the induction process and the selection of features subset techniques would be used to select the most appropriate features. To achieve an optimal FS, this paper is applicable to a wrapper-based selection of features procedure. The FS selection algorithm uses search method to determine the optimal features of each combination of features. Induction method is the evaluation function whereby it searches a suitable part of features. This technique tries to identify the most suitable set of features through the use of statistical techniques or creating ML models4–6.

Credit transaction dataset tend to be skewed, including a massive imbalance between the number of samples of the various classes. Due to this class imbalance, the normal ML algorithms can be biased to the majority class thus giving poor results in predicting the minority one. When the medical diagnosis or fraud detection is regarded as an application, the accurate identification of the minority class is essential which requires the use of such performance metrics as precision, recall, or F1-score. Subsequent to allay the challenge posed by unbalanced datasets, various strategies have been recommended in the literature. Such methods include resampling methods, cost-sensitive learning and ensemble methods7. Having skewed datasets as in the case of8,9 can have a negative impact on the performance of classification models despite the low-probability of the fraudulent event happening. However, when there is an incident of a single fraudulent act, the consequences could be dreadful not only to the client, but also to the organizational image. To enhance the accuracy of credit fraud detection, there have been a variety of supervised and unsupervised learning algorithms, which have been utilized to determine the most relevant attributes and create a robust model to detect fraudulent transactions in unbalanced samples10,11. The above activities include the field of metaheuristic algorithm whose evidence-based application has shown promise in producing resilient models to that end of selection of features especially dataset with an imbalance. The application of optimization methods allows finding an optimal set of features to be used in classification activities. In addition to that, these strategies allow ensuring the ability to overcome the problem of unbalanced datasets by homogenizing the classes and choosing the characteristics that show the greatest reliance on the minority one. Different optimization strategies in the field of metaheuristic algorithms have been reported to the field of fraud detection. Particularly, FS when using imbalanced datasets has been done using Genetic algorithms (GA)6, particle swarm optimization (PSO)1,2, and ant colony optimization (ACO)12. A number of newer metaheuristic algorithms exist in literature. These are Chameleon Swarm Algorithm13, and White Shark Optimizer14. After conducting a detailed overview of the literature reviews, it is found out that there are a great number of methodologies which have limitations in form of poor selection of features. In turn, these restrictions have led to the lack of accuracy in many situations.

The paper presents a new proposal for a wrapper-based FS methodology that utilizes the Osprey Optimization Algorithm (OOA)15,16.The proposed FSOOA methodology overcomes the above difficulties faced in the current FS algorithms to identify the best features using high dimensional imbalanced data sets. The goal of the proposed FSOOA approach is to identify optimized features to improve the accuracy of the classification model with respect to trying to identify a small subset of relevant features within the high-dimensional dataset.Moreover, the proposed methodology has been tested on unbalanced datasets, as well as the existing classification models, including Logistic Regression (LR), Support Vector Machines (SVM), Random Forest (RF), and ensemble approaches1,6.

Research gap

Although remarkable advances have been made in the field of traditional FS and meta-heuristic optimization algorithms like genetic algorithm, particle swarm optimization and ant colony optimization, some of its most important limitations are yet to be addressed. Current methods can be inefficient in managing the two-fold problem of high-dimensional data and extreme imbalance in classes, resulting in less optimal fraud-related feature identification. Besides, most wrapper-based FS algorithms involve a single classifier to obtain fitness measurements and may be biased to certain learning patterns and restrict the generalization ability of the chosen portion of features. This shortcoming is especially important in the context of credit card fraud detection, where it is imperative to distinguish among the minority groups correctly. Moreover, a significant number of current approaches focus on overall accuracy, instead of recall of fraudulent transactions, exposing fraudsters to less detection risk. Thus, it is evident that a powerful FS model is necessary which does not only minimize redundancy of features but also improves minority-class detection rate with a more comprehensive evaluation plan. In order to fill these gaps, this paper suggests a FS with OOA with a stacking ensemble classifier which makes use of several learning models to assess the features, increasing the FS strength and classification accuracy in highly imbalanced data.

It is possible to mention a broad array of meta-heuristic optimization algorithms, developed within recent years, such as GA, PSO, ACO, and a variety of more recent nature-inspired algorithms. The No Free Lunch theorem suggested by Wolpert states that there is no single optimization algorithm that can be deemed to be universal in solving all problems. Thus, the efficiency of an optimization method lies in its ability to be used in accordance with the features of the particular problem considered. When dealing with high dimensional and imbalanced credit card fraud detection, FS must balance between search space exploration and exploitation of promising feature subsets. This study considers the OOA because of its two-phase search mechanism which is inspired by ospreys hunting behavior enabling it to explore the entire world and also be able to refine locally. This property is appropriate to use with wrapper-based FS, in which the space of search is discrete, and could be large. Moreover, it can adaptively revise candidate solutions, something that facilitates the discovery of subsets of its features that enhance minority-class detection. Accordingly, the OOA is chosen based on its structural characteristics and the suitability to the formulation of a problem as opposed to a presumptive of an overall superiority.

Although the recent metaheuristic-based FS techniques have shown promising performance, most of them are based on single-classifier training or emphasize more on parameter optimization. These approaches can create model-specific bias and are less efficient when dealing with severe class imbalance. Conversely, the suggested framework uses a stacking ensemble classifier during the fitness assessment, which allows making a stronger and more objective assessment of feature subsets. Moreover, the fitness function is aimed at giving the minority-class recall first priority and regulating the number of features subset. This synergistic ensemble-based assessment and recall-based optimization makes the proposed method unique to the currently used metaheuristic FS methods.

This study proposes an optimal FS framework based on the FS using OOA, a nature-inspired optimization technique, combined with a stacking ensemble classifier. In the proposed work, candidate feature subsets are measured4 using a stacking ensemble composed of NB, DT, SVM, and RF classifiers as base learners, with LR serving as the meta-learner. The ensemble-based evaluation enables robust identification of relevant features while effectively handling class imbalance. Experimental results on a real-world credit card fraud dataset exhibit that the proposed method improves fraud detection performance.

Proposed FSOOA model

The proposed FSOOA (Feature Selection using Osprey Optimization Algorithm) model is described in this section alongside its major ideas, as well as the way it is implemented. FSOOA model is an optimization method that is based on osprey foraging behavior. Ospreys are predators; hence, they have devised efficient ways of preying15. These strategies are expected to be followed by the FSOOA model targeting the classification issues in the context of FS.

The proposed framework introduces a FS strategy that combines metaheuristic optimization with ensemble-based evaluation to address imbalanced classification. The novelty of the approach lies in integrating a stacking ensemble classifier into the fitness evaluation process, which reduces dependence on a single learning model and provides a more robust assessment of candidate feature subsets. In addition, the fitness function is formulated to prioritize the recall of the minority class while controlling feature subset size, aligning the optimization objective with the requirements of fraud detection. From a theoretical perspective, the OOA provides a balance between exploration and exploitation through its two-phase search mechanism, which is suitable for high-dimensional binary FS problems. Consistent with the No Free Lunch theorem, the effectiveness of the approach is attributed to its compatibility with the problem characteristics.

Metaheuristic algorithms rely on balancing exploration and exploitation to effectively search high-dimensional spaces. The OOA adopts a two-phase mechanism consisting of a global search phase (exploration) and a local refinement phase (exploitation), enabling both diversity and convergence. In comparison, Genetic Algorithm depends on crossover and mutation, PSO emphasizes velocity-driven convergence, and ACO relies on pheromone reinforcement, which may reduce diversity over iterations. The explicit separation of exploration and exploitation in OOA supports efficient search in discrete FS problems. In line with the No Free Lunch theorem, the choice of OOA is based on its compatibility with the problem characteristics rather than a claim of general superiority.

The OOA can be effectively applied to assessing FS, a method of selecting a subset of feature set among a great number of features to improve the performance of a ML model. Using the OOA in the FS process, the algorithm is supposed to identify the most informative and distinguishing features that are the most favorable in the process of correct classification or prediction, in turn enhancing the overall performance of the ML model. FS plays a key role in dimensionality reduction of data, high accuracy, quick speed, and interpretability of ML models. FS can apply the OOA by formulating a function of fitness which measures the quality of the features obtained. The fitness use could be determined by the improve of an ML model where the chosen features can be utilized, which could be the classification model of a regression model accuracy or also AUC score, or the regression model mean squared error28. Alternatively, the fitness could be dependent on a composite of criteria (i.e., the degree of features selected as well as correlation or redundancy), which facilitates a combination that has not been tested with a method like genetic algorithms. Whether to make the OOA to focus on what features are the most appropriate in maximizing the performance of the ML model depends on the choice of the right fitness function.

The algorithm of OOA is programmed to replicate the hunting behavior of ospreys by breaking down the optimization cycle in two stages, search and attack. Application of the OOA algorithm to relevant features that optimizes the fitness function can then be carried out. During the search phase, the algorithm creates a population of candidate sets of features by randomly picking out features out of all features. The method of OOA creates a pool of possible sets of features in its search stage by selecting randomly features out of the general set of features. Application and extended evaluation of the objective function is now done to each candidate subset and the most desirable subsets are chosen and included in the attack process.

During the first stage of the OOA that is the position identification and hunting stage, the aim of the algorithm is to determine the problem space and possible regions that may have the optimal solution28. The stage is based on hunting habits of ospreys that are hunting birds which eat fish by monitoring their positions in the water.

The proposed framework aims to enhance credit card fraud detection by identifying an optimal subset of features using the FS Using Osprey Optimization Algorithm (FSOOA) in conjunction with a stacking ensemble classifier. In the first stage, FSOOA initializes a population of candidate solutions (Ospreys), where each solution is encoded as a binary FS vector such that denotes the inclusion of the feature and denotes its exclusion. Each Osprey therefore represents a distinct feature subset. The optimization process iteratively updates the population through exploration and exploitation strategies inherent to the Osprey Optimization Algorithm, guiding candidate solutions toward an optimal region in the search space.

For each candidate feature subset , a fitness evaluation is performed using a stacking ensemble classifier. The original dataset is reduced according to the selected features, producing a subset . This reduced dataset is then split into training and validation sets using stratified sampling to preserve the severe class imbalance. Four heterogeneous base classifiers NB, DT, SVM, and RF are trained on the reduced training data. The prediction outputs of these base learners are subsequently used to construct a meta-feature matrix, which serves as input to a LR meta-learner. The stacking ensemble produces final predictions by combining the complementary decision patterns of all base models.

Since missed frauds are more costly than false alarms in financial systems, fraud-class recall is prioritized. FSOOA iteratively minimizes this fitness function across generations, updating the positions of Ospreys and converting continuous updates into binary decisions using a sigmoid transfer function.

Upon termination of the optimization process, the best-performing Osprey solution is selected as the optimal feature subset. A binary vector representing the choice of features is used for each potential solution. The population size is set to 10 and the maximum number of iterations is 100. The fitness of each solution is evaluated using a stacking ensemble classifier, where NB, DT, SVM, and RF act as base learners, and Logistic Regression serves as the meta-learner. The predictions from base classifiers are combined to form meta-features, which are used to train the meta-learner for final evaluation. The fitness function is defined as a weighted combination of the recall of the minority class and the proportion of selected features, ensuring a balance between detection performance and feature reduction.

Conclusion and future work

This study conducts a comparative analysis of various models through the utilization of wrapper-based FS methodologies, specifically focusing on the FSOOA approach. The primary objective is to determine the optimal features that can effectively boost the performance of the model. In order to enhance the performance of the model in the experiment, it is advisable to carefully choose the most significant features while eliminating any redundant or noisy features. The optimization-oriented FS approaches aim to select a minimal set of optimal features, where each feature is assigned a binary value of 1 or 0 to indicate its level of significance. The FSOOA technique aims to enhance model performance by selecting the most suitable and relevant characteristics, while discarding irrelevant information. The reduction of pertinent features is achieved by implementing a predetermined limitation on either the sequential arrangement of pertinent attributes or the overall quantity of attributes to be chosen within a predetermined optimal FS methodology, thereby ensuring the selection of a pre-established number of features. The integration of wrapper-based FS approaches with ML models is a promising strategy that can be effectively employed in a wide array of challenging applications characterized by large feature sets. The identification of crucial traits can greatly influence the effectiveness and efficiency of models across various areas, such as disease prediction, weather forecasting, text data analysis, fraud email detection, intrusion detection, and analysis of counterfeit insurance. The results demonstrate that combining optimization-based FS with ensemble learning improves fraud detection performance while reducing feature dimensionality. This approach is suitable for real-time applications where both accuracy and computational efficiency are important. This study has some limitations. The wrapper-based FS increases computational cost due to repeated model training. The evaluation is limited to a single dataset, which may affect generalizability. In addition, the method depends on parameter settings such as population size and fitness weights. Future work will focus on improving computational efficiency and validating the approach on diverse datasets.

The evaluation is conducted on a single benchmark dataset, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Future work will focus on validating the framework on multiple datasets, improving computational efficiency, and developing adaptive parameter tuning strategies. In addition, integrating explainable models and exploring multi-objective optimization for balancing detection performance and cost will be considered.

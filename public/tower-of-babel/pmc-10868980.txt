New Dermoscopic Findings in the Facial Angiofibromas of Tuberous Sclerosis Complex
Authors: Li-Wen Zhang, Juan Wu, Yong-Hong Lu
Published: 2023

Dear Editor,

A 22-year-old female presented with a 15-year history of multiple asymptomatic papules and nodules over the face [Figure 1a]. The patient had a history of epilepsy for more than 10 years with learning difficulties. The patient was otherwise healthy and without a correlative family history. Physical examination revealed a periungual fibroma on the right index finger [Figure 1b] and multiple shagreen patches [Figure 1c] on her back. Under a dermoscopy (JD-801E, Jieda Technology Development Co., LTD, Jiangsu, China), the smaller facial papules showed multiple yellowish-white dots over a reddish-brown background with red globules or clods and short linear vessels [Figure 2a]; the bigger facial nodules showed clustered, smooth, reddish-brown, hemispheric structures with a few red globules or clods [Figure 2b]. A diagnosis of tuberous sclerosis complex (TSC) was made based on the clinical manifestations.

TSC, with a birth incidence of 1:6000–1:10,000, is an autosomal dominant hereditary disease caused by mutations in the TSC1 and TSC2 genes.[1] Skin lesions are found in 60–70% of patients with TSC, including angiofibromas, shagreen patches, periungual fibromas, and white ovoid macules, which are important diagnostic clues for TSC. We herein reported a case of TSC with typical cutaneous lesions and dermoscopic findings in facial angiofibromas.

Angiofibromas usually appear during childhood and present as multiple pink or reddish-brown papules and nodules distributed bilaterally and symmetrically over the face. The dermoscopic features are commonly present as multiple yellow-white dots over a pinkish-gray or reddish-brown background, and brown pigmentation dots and surface crypts are seen in partial angiofibromas.[23] Multiple yellow-white globules represent histopathologic follicular hyperkeratosis; brown pigment dots correspond to dermal melanophages; surface crypts represent pseudo-follicular openings; pinkish-gray or reddish-brown background associates with melanocytic hyperplasia and proliferating dermal blood vessels.[23] In our patient, the dermoscopic presentation of clustered, smooth, reddish-brown, hemispheric structures is newly described.

The differential diagnoses of angiofibromas include multiple trichoepitheliomas, fibrofolliculomas, trichofolliculomas, trichilemmomas, syringoma, dermal melanocytic nevi, and acne vulgaris. On dermoscopy: trichoepithelioma shows multiple arborizing vessels and milia-like cysts over a whitish background;[4] fibrofolliculoma reveals well-demarcated areas of pallor with central follicular openings;[5] syringoma presents pigment network, structureless light-brown areas, and reticular vessels.[6] Clinical suspicion confirmed with dermoscopic findings can aid early detection and avoid an unnecessary biopsy.

Determinants of intrinsic capacity among older adults in low- and middle-income countries: A scoping review
Authors: Linxi Tang, Nur Syahmina Binti Rasudin, Yuan Dong, Azlina Yusuf
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIntrinsic capacity (IC), as defined by the World Health Organization (WHO), represents the composite of an individual’s physical and mental capacities and forms a cornerstone of the healthy aging framework. Understanding its determinants is essential for designing effective interventions, particularly in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) where population aging is accelerating amid limited resources and fragmented health systems. Mapping these determinants supports evidence-based strategies that enhance functional ability and resilience among older adults.ObjectiveThis scoping review aimed to systematically map and categorize the determinants of IC among older adults in LMICs, providing an evidence base to inform nursing practice and policy development.MethodsFollowing PRISMA-ScR guidelines, four databases (Web of Science, PubMed, Scopus, and EBSCOhost) were searched for peer-reviewed studies published between January 2015 and April 2025. Data were synthesized thematically and classified into four determinant domains.ResultsThirty-six studies met inclusion criteria. The determinants of IC were grouped into four domains: (1) sociodemographic (age, gender, education, marital and economic status, living arrangement, residence); (2) biological, physiological, and disease-related (chronic diseases, multimorbidity, body mass index, frailty, polypharmacy, biomarkers); (3) behavioral and lifestyle (physical activity, diet, smoking, alcohol use, sleep, health-seeking behavior); and (4) environmental (housing quality, social participation, community support, healthcare accessibility). Advancing age, chronic disease burden, frailty, and social isolation were linked to reduced IC, while education, good nutrition, physical activity, and supportive environments were protective.ConclusionIntrinsic capacity in older adults from LMICs is shaped by interrelated social, biological, behavioral, and environmental factors. For nursing and health policy, these findings emphasize the need to shift from disease-centered to proactive, function-oriented care. Integrating IC monitoring into routine nursing practice, promoting health literacy, and creating age-friendly environments are critical for sustaining IC and promoting equitable, healthy aging. Findings from this review may serve as a valuable reference for practitioners and policymakers in LMICs.RegistryOpen Science Framework (OSF) [10.17605/OSF.IO/HQ6PG]

Background

Population aging is transforming societies worldwide, with low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) experiencing particularly rapid demographic transitions. Projections indicate that the global share of people aged 65 years and older will rise from 9.3% in 2020 to 15.9% by 2050, reaching 22.4% by 2100 (United Nations, 2024). This shift presents substantial challenges for health care and social protection systems, which in many LMICs remain under-resourced and structurally constrained (Bloom et al., 2015). These pressures highlight the urgent need for robust, contextually appropriate strategies to foster healthy aging and reduce the societal burden of demographic change.

It is important to acknowledge, however, that LMICs are far from uniform. They span diverse demographic trajectories, health system capacities, and sociocultural contexts. For example, the aging patterns of sub-Saharan Africa differ markedly from those in Southeast Asia or Latin America (Aboderin & Beard, 2015). Such intra-group variation influences not only the pace of population aging but also the lived experiences and health determinants of older adults across these regions.

To address the complex needs of aging populations, the World Health Organization (WHO) introduced the concept of intrinsic capacity (IC) in 2015 as a central pillar of its healthy aging framework (WHO, 2015). IC, defined as the composite of all physical and mental capacities an individual can draw upon, encompasses five core domains: cognition, psychological well-being, vitality, locomotion, and sensory function (Beard et al., 2016). This definition has since gained broad global acceptance, forming a basis for research, clinical assessment, and policy initiatives related to aging.

While IC overlaps conceptually with related constructs such as functional ability, frailty, and resilience, it is essential to differentiate among them. Functional ability refers to the capacity to perform tasks within a given environment, shaped by the interaction between IC and contextual factors (WHO, 2021). Frailty denotes increased vulnerability to stressors due to diminished physiological reserves, often used to identify older adults at high risk (Fried et al., 2001). Resilience describes the dynamic process of adapting to adversity, including illness or psychological distress (Veronese et al., 2017). In contrast, IC is a forward-looking, integrative construct focused on maintaining and optimizing capacities rather than responding to decline. Its multidimensional structure supports early detection of deterioration and facilitates proactive, cross-disciplinary interventions.

Accordingly, while these concepts share certain elements, IC should not be regarded as synonymous with, or merely an extension of, existing terms. Clarifying these distinctions is vital for advancing theory, enhancing research accuracy, and guiding targeted interventions, particularly in LMICs, where resource constraints and disease-centered medical models still dominate care for older populations (Lloyd-Sherlock et al., 2016).

Despite growing recognition, research on IC in LMICs remains limited. Although the IC framework is increasingly present in global health discourse, its adoption in resource-constrained settings is hindered by inadequate health infrastructure, weak data systems, and a continued emphasis on disease treatment over capacity-building (Whitehorn et al., 2021). In many LMICs, the assessment and promotion of IC are yet to be embedded into health policy or routine practice.

Emerging evidence links IC to key adverse outcomes, including disability, frailty, and mortality, yet its determinants remain insufficiently understood, especially in LMICs. Many studies are constrained by methodological variability, inconsistent definitions across domains, and incomplete reporting of essential variables (Beard et al., 2019), making it difficult to synthesize findings across contexts.

In response, this review aims to systematically map the determinants of IC among older adults in LMICs, spanning individual, social, behavioral, and environmental dimensions. By providing a comprehensive overview, it seeks to strengthen conceptual foundations, inform clinical and community practice, and guide policy design to support healthy aging in resource-limited settings. Given the conceptual and methodological diversity in this field, a scoping review approach was selected to capture the breadth of existing evidence and identify priority areas for future research.

Methods

Study Design

This scoping review was conducted in strict accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) guidelines (Tricco et al., 2018). The study protocol was prospectively registered with the Open Science Framework (OSF; registration number: https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/HQ6PG). The PRISMA-ScR checklist and the full protocol are provided in Supplementary File S1. No major deviations from the registered protocol were made during the course of this review.

Literature Search Strategy

We conducted a systematic search across four major international databases: PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, and EBSCOhost, covering publications from January 1, 2015, to April 1, 2025. The starting date corresponds to the World Health Organization’s introduction of the “intrinsic capacity” concept. The final search was performed on April 1, 2025. Full search strategies for each database are provided in Supplementary File S2.

The search strategy combined Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) and relevant free-text terms, including “intrinsic capacity,” “risk factors,” “determinants,” “prevalence,” “older adults,” “aged,” “elderly,” and “geriatrics.” For example, in PubMed, “intrinsic capacity” was paired with terms related to age and determinants, with the results limited to English-language journal articles published within the specified period. All search strategies were reviewed and pilot-tested internally by the research team; no external peer review was undertaken. Grey literature sources (e.g., dissertations, government reports, and conference proceedings) were excluded from this review.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Studies were included if they met the following criteria: (1) Participants were older adults, defined as aged 60 years or above, or as specified in the original study. In line with practices common in several LMICs, studies with a lower age threshold (e.g., ≥50 years) were also considered if explicitly reported by the authors and deemed representative of older adults in the given context. This flexibility was adopted because earlier onset of chronic conditions and reduced life expectancy in LMICs often lead to populations being classified as “older adults” from 50 years onwards. (2) intrinsic capacity (IC) was measured and reported as a primary or major outcome; (3) all five IC domains: cognition, psychological function, vitality, locomotion, and sensory function, as defined by the WHO, were evaluated; (4) the study was conducted in a low- or middle-income country (LMIC) according to the World Bank classification; and (5) the article was an original, peer-reviewed study published in English between 2015 and 2025.

Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) studies that did not focus on IC or did not report its determinants; (2) studies assessing fewer than five IC domains; (3) reviews, commentaries, book chapters, dissertations, or conference abstracts; and (4) studies available only as titles or abstracts without access to the full text.

Only studies that explicitly used the term “intrinsic capacity” were included, rather than related constructs such as functional ability or frailty. This approach reflects the distinct theoretical framework established by the WHO, emphasizing the comprehensive and dynamic nature of IC. By focusing exclusively on IC, the review sought to ensure conceptual consistency and provide a clearer evidence base for advancing research and practice in this emerging field.

Study Screening and Selection

All retrieved records were imported into Mendeley for deduplication and reference management. Two reviewers, blinded to each other’s identities, independently screened the titles and abstracts against the predefined eligibility criteria. Full texts of potentially eligible studies were then obtained and independently assessed. Any discrepancies were resolved through discussion, with a third reviewer consulted when consensus could not be reached. Prior to formal screening, the research team defined and pilot-tested operational criteria for “thematic irrelevance” (no outcomes or exposures related to any IC domain) and “insufficient focus on IC” (IC not measured or reported).

Data Extraction and Synthesis

A standardized data extraction form was developed through team discussions, drawing on established guidelines for scoping reviews. The form captured key study details, including author, publication year, country, design, setting, sample size, participant characteristics, measurement tools, IC domains, principal determinants, statistical methods, and quality indicators. The form was pilot-tested on five randomly selected studies; minor issues identified were resolved through targeted adjustments. For the main extraction, two reviewers worked independently, resolving disagreements through discussion or, when necessary, consultation with the broader team.

For data synthesis, we conducted an inductive thematic analysis. Two reviewers independently performed open coding of IC determinants without predefined categories, allowing themes to emerge from the data. The coding framework was refined collaboratively to ensure consistency and coherence. A subset of studies was double-coded, and challenging or ambiguous cases were resolved through team discussions. The final thematic structure was determined collectively, integrating diverse perspectives.

Quality Assessment

Although formal critical appraisal is not mandated for scoping reviews, we undertook a structured assessment of methodological quality to contextualize heterogeneity and to qualify the certainty of inferences. Cross-sectional studies were appraised using the 11-item Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality (AHRQ) checklist, and cohort studies using the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS). For each tool, we defined a priori operational thresholds for three quality tiers (AHRQ: 0–3 = low, 4–7 = moderate, 8–11 = high; NOS: 0–4 = low, 5–6 = moderate, 7–9 = high). Quality ratings were not used as eligibility criteria; rather, they informed the narrative synthesis and the weight we assigned to observed patterns (e.g., signals supported predominantly by high-quality cohort evidence were treated as more robust, whereas associations arising mainly from moderate-quality cross-sectional studies were considered provisional). Two reviewers independently performed the appraisals and resolved disagreements through discussion. Given the diversity of LMIC contexts, we acknowledge that standardized scoring rubrics may not fully capture setting-specific sources of bias. Item-level criteria, thresholds, and study-level ratings are provided in Supplementary File S3.

Results

Literature Search

An initial pool of 787 records was retrieved from four electronic databases: Web of Science (n = 133), PubMed (n = 147), Scopus (n = 232), and EBSCOhost (n = 275), all of which were related to potential determinants of intrinsic capacity. After automated and manual deduplication, 435 unique entries remained for title and abstract screening. During the preliminary screening, 343 records were excluded for thematic irrelevance, leaving 92 articles for full-text review. At the second-level evaluation, 26 articles were excluded for the following reasons: absence of empirical data (n = 10), excessively heterogeneous or non-older adult study populations (n = 6), unsuitable publication types (e.g., narrative reviews, book chapters, theses; n = 7), or non-English language (n = 3). This resulted in 66 articles advancing to the final appraisal stage.

In the final eligibility assessment, 30 articles were excluded because they did not apply a multidimensional framework to evaluate intrinsic capacity (n = 7), did not report associated determinants (n = 11), or did not identify intrinsic capacity as a primary research focus (n = 12). Ultimately, 36 studies met all inclusion criteria and were incorporated into this scoping review. The overall screening process is illustrated in Figure 1.

Summary of Study Characteristics

A total of 36 studies met the inclusion criteria, comprising 15 cohort studies and 21 cross-sectional studies, with sample sizes ranging from 130 to 31,128 participants. Geographically, the studies were conducted across 14 LMCIs and regions, with the majority originating from China (n = 28, 78%). Most studies were conducted in community-based settings (n = 30), while 6 were conducted in hospital settings. This distribution indicates that research on IC in LMICs has predominantly focused on older adults living independently in the community, rather than on those receiving institutional or inpatient care.

Based on an inductive synthesis of the included studies, we identified key determinants influencing IC among older adults in LMICs. Quality Appraisal Results

The methodological quality of the included studies was generally acceptable. Of the 36 studies reviewed, 26 (72.2%) were rated as high quality and 10 (27.8%) as moderate quality; none was rated as low quality. Among cross-sectional studies appraised using the AHRQ checklist, the most frequently observed limitations were insufficient reporting of quality assurance procedures, incomplete handling of missing data, and lack of blinding in outcome assessment. For cohort studies assessed with the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS), the weaker domains are primarily related to limited adjustment for potential confounders, restricted comparability between cohorts, and incomplete reporting of follow-up adequacy. Despite these shortcomings, most studies demonstrated clear eligibility criteria, reliable exposure ascertainment, and valid outcome measures. Taken together, these findings indicate that, while methodological gaps persist, the overall evidence base is robust enough to inform thematic synthesis. Detailed item-level appraisal outcomes are provided in Supplementary File S3.

Determinants of Intrinsic Capacity

Among the included studies, demographic determinants were the most frequently examined (35/36, 97.2%), followed by biological, physiological, and disease-related determinants (33/36, 91.7%), behavioral and lifestyle determinants (23/36, 63.9%), and environmental determinants (12/36, 33.3%). Most studies investigated two or three determinant domains, while only a small proportion (9/36, 25.0%) simultaneously examined all four domains of determinants.

Sociodemographic Determinants of Intrinsic Capacity

Of the 36 studies included in this review, 35 (97.2%) investigated the relationship between sociodemographic determinants and IC, and most of them reported significant associations, indicating that demographic characteristics play a critical role in shaping IC among older adults (Aliberti et al., 2022; Arokiasamy et al., 2021; Beard et al., 2022; Gutiérrez-Robledo et al., 2019; Gutiérrez-Robledo et al., 2021; Jiang et al., 2023; Lee et al., 2023; Leung et al., 2022; Li et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2022; Ma, Chhetri, et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2020; Ma, Liu, et al., 2021; Ma, Zhang, et al., 2021; Meng, Huang, et al., 2022; Muneera et al., 2022; Pan et al., 2024; Plácido et al., 2023; Prince et al., 2021; Ramírez Vélez et al., 2019; Rarajam Rao et al., 2023; Salinas-Rodríguez et al., 2022; Shen et al., 2023; Si et al., 2023; Tan et al., 2025; Yu, Si, et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2023; Yu, Leung, et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2021; Yuan et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2024; Zhou, Ma, et al., 2025; Zhou, Chang, et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2024).

Nearly all included studies (35 out of 36, 97.2%) explored the associations of age and gender with IC in older adults. Age was the most commonly analyzed determinant, and evidence across studies consistently demonstrated a decline in IC with advancing age, particularly in the cognitive and sensory domains. Similarly, most studies observed that older women had significantly lower IC than men, indicating a persistent gender disparity in later-life functional ability (Aliberti et al., 2022; Arokiasamy et al., 2021; Beard et al., 2022; Gutiérrez-Robledo et al., 2019; Gutiérrez-Robledo et al., 2021; Jiang et al., 2023; Lee et al., 2023; Leung et al., 2022; Li et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2023; Ma, Chhetri, et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2020; Ma, Liu, et al., 2021; Ma, Zhang, et al., 2021; Meng, Huang, et al., 2022; Muneera et al., 2022; Pan et al., 2024; Plácido et al., 2023; Prince et al., 2021; Ramírez Vélez et al., 2019; Rarajam Rao et al., 2023; Salinas-Rodríguez et al., 2022; Shen et al., 2023; Si et al., 2023; Tan et al., 2025; Yu, Si, et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2023; Yu, Leung, et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2021; Yuan et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2024; Zhou, Ma, et al., 2025; Zhou, Chang, et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2024).

Educational attainment was another important variable, identified in 26 studies (72.2%) as positively associated with IC—older adults with higher education levels generally demonstrated better functional capacity (Arokiasamy et al., 2021; Beard et al., 2022; Gutiérrez-Robledo et al., 2019; Gutiérrez-Robledo et al., 2021; Jiang et al., 2023; Lee et al., 2023; Leung et al., 2022; Li et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2022; Ma, Chhetri, et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2020; Ma, Liu, et al., 2021; Ma, Zhang, et al., 2021; Meng, Huang, et al., 2022; Muneera et al., 2022; Pan et al., 2024; Plácido et al., 2023; Prince et al., 2021; Ramírez Vélez et al., 2019; Shen et al., 2023; Si et al., 2023; Tan et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2023; Yu, Leung, et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2021).

Economic status was reported in 17 studies (47.2%) to be a significant predictor of IC, with wealthier individuals exhibiting higher capacity levels (Arokiasamy et al., 2021; Beard et al., 2022; Gutiérrez-Robledo et al., 2019; Jiang et al., 2023; Leung et al., 2022; Li et al., 2025; Ma et al., 2020; Ma, Liu, et al., 2021; Ma, Zhang, et al., 2021; Muneera et al., 2022; Pan et al., 2024; Plácido et al., 2023; Prince et al., 2021; Shen et al., 2023; Tan et al., 2025; Yu, Leung, et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2021).

Marital status, assessed in 21 studies (58.3%), also showed a notable effect: married older adults tended to have higher IC than those who were single, divorced, or widowed (Arokiasamy et al., 2021; Gutiérrez-Robledo et al., 2021; Jiang et al., 2023; Leung et al., 2022; Li et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2023; Ma, Chhetri, et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2020; Muneera et al., 2022; Pan et al., 2024; Prince et al., 2021; Rarajam Rao et al., 2023; Salinas-Rodríguez et al., 2022; Shen et al., 2023; Si et al., 2023; Tan et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2023; Yu, Leung, et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2024; Zhou, Ma, et al., 2025; Zhou, Chang, et al., 2025). Residential setting played a critical role in shaping IC, as demonstrated in 14 studies (38.9%). Urban residents, and those living in economically developed regions, generally had better IC than rural or remote counterparts, highlighting disparities linked to geographic and infrastructural contexts (Beard et al., 2022; Jiang et al., 2023; Leung et al., 2022; Li et al., 2025; Ma, Chhetri, et al., 2021; Ma, Liu, et al., 2021; Ma, Zhang, et al., 2021; Muneera et al., 2022; Pan et al., 2024; Plácido et al., 2023; Prince et al., 2021; Shen et al., 2023; Tan et al., 2025; Zhou, Chang, et al., 2025).

Another 8 studies (22.2%) reported that older adults living with a spouse or children had higher IC than those living alone, indicating the potential benefits of cohabitation and social support (Arokiasamy et al., 2021; Beard et al., 2022; Lee et al., 2023; Ma, Chhetri, et al., 2021; Ma, Zhang, et al., 2021; Plácido et al., 2023; Shen et al., 2023; Yu, Leung, et al., 2022). Employment status was examined in 4 studies (11.1%), which consistently indicated that older adults who remained employed demonstrated higher levels of IC compared to those who were unemployed or retired (Ma, Zhang, et al., 2021; Muneera et al., 2022; Pan et al., 2024; Plácido et al., 2023). Together, these findings highlight the complex and multifaceted role that sociodemographic determinants play in shaping intrinsic capacity, particularly within LMICs, where structural inequalities may further exacerbate age-related functional decline.

Biological, Physiological, and Disease-Related Determinants of Intrinsic Capacity

Among the 36 studies included in this review, 33 (91.7%) reported associations between IC and biological, physiological, and disease-related determinants (Aliberti et al., 2022; Arokiasamy et al., 2021; Beard et al., 2022; Gutiérrez-Robledo et al., 2019; Gutiérrez-Robledo et al., 2021; Jiang et al., 2023; Lee et al., 2023; Leung et al., 2022; Li et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2022; Ma, Chhetri, et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2020; Ma, Liu, et al., 2021; Ma, Zhang, et al., 2021; Meng, Huang, et al., 2022; Pan et al., 2024; Plácido et al., 2023; Prince et al., 2021; Ramírez Vélez et al., 2019; Rarajam Rao et al., 2023; Salinas-Rodríguez et al., 2022; Shen et al., 2023; Si et al., 2023; Yu, Si, et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2023; Yu, Leung, et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2021; Yuan et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2024; Zhou, Ma, et al., 2025; Zhou, Chang, et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2024). In addition, 27 studies (75.0%) focused on assessing how chronic diseases and multimorbidity affect IC, indicating that older individuals with conditions such as cardiovascular disease, hypertension, diabetes, and osteoarthritis generally exhibit lower IC levels (Aliberti et al., 2022; Arokiasamy et al., 2021; Gutiérrez-Robledo et al., 2019; Gutiérrez-Robledo et al., 2021; Lee et al., 2023; Leung et al., 2022; Li et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2023; Ma, Chhetri, et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2020; Ma, Liu, et al., 2021; Meng, Huang, et al., 2022; Pan et al., 2024; Plácido et al., 2023; Prince et al., 2021; Ramírez Vélez et al., 2019; Rarajam Rao et al., 2023; Salinas-Rodríguez et al., 2022; Shen et al., 2023; Yu, Si, et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2023; Yu, Leung, et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2021; Yuan et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2024; Zhou, Ma, et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2024).

A total of 19 studies (52.8%) explored the association between body mass index (BMI) and IC (Arokiasamy et al., 2021; Gutiérrez-Robledo et al., 2019; Jiang et al., 2023; Lee et al., 2023; Leung et al., 2022; Li et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2020; Meng, Huang, et al., 2022; Pan et al., 2024; Plácido et al., 2023; Prince et al., 2021; Ramírez Vélez et al., 2019; Rarajam Rao et al., 2023; Salinas-Rodríguez et al., 2022; Shen et al., 2023; Yu et al., 2021; Zhou, Ma, et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2024). The findings suggest that both underweight and obese older adults generally have lower levels of IC.

Among the included studies, seven out of thirty-six (19.4%) investigated the association between IC and grip strength. Most of these studies demonstrated a strong and positive association between grip strength and IC, suggesting that older adults with markedly reduced grip strength tend to exhibit substantially lower levels of intrinsic capacity (Aliberti et al., 2022; Beard et al., 2022; Leung et al., 2022; Meng, Huang, et al., 2022; Plácido et al., 2023; Salinas-Rodríguez et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2021). On the other hand, a total of six studies (16.7%) focused on polypharmacy. The findings indicated that the concurrent use of multiple medications may adversely affect the IC of older adults through mechanisms such as adverse drug reactions, drug–drug interactions, and drug dependency, ultimately leading to physical and cognitive decline (Lin et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2020; Ma, Liu, et al., 2021; Ma, Zhang, et al., 2021; Yu, Leung, et al., 2022; Zhu et al., 2024).

In addition, eight studies (22.2%) explored the association between frailty and IC, showing that frail older adults tend to have substantially lower IC, particularly in domains such as vitality, cognition, and psychological well-being. Some researchers further suggested that frailty may represent a clinical manifestation of ongoing IC deterioration (Lee et al., 2023; Li et al., 2025; Ma et al., 2020; Pan et al., 2024; Prince et al., 2021; Salinas-Rodríguez et al., 2022; Shen et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2024).

Among the included studies, eight studies (8/36, 22.2%) examined the association between gait speed and IC. The findings consistently indicated that slower gait speed was significantly associated with lower levels of intrinsic capacity, suggesting that gait speed may serve as a sensitive indicator of functional decline in older adults (Aliberti et al., 2022; Arokiasamy et al., 2021; Beard et al., 2022; Ma, Liu, et al., 2021; Ma, Zhang, et al., 2021; Plácido et al., 2023; Salinas-Rodríguez et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2021).

Five studies (13.9 %) examined biological markers related to IC. Higher inflammatory markers, including IL-6, CRP, NLR, LGI, homocysteine, allostatic load, TNFR-1, and GDF-15, were consistently associated with lower IC, while the APOE ε4 genotype was also linked to poorer IC. In contrast, higher DHEA-S, folate, and serum albumin levels were associated with better IC, suggesting that favorable nutritional and metabolic profiles may be protective (Lee et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2020; Ma, Liu, et al., 2021; Ma, Zhang, et al., 2021; Meng, Huang, et al., 2022).

Biological and disease-related factors strongly influence IC. Multimorbidity, abnormal BMI, weak strength, slow gait, and polypharmacy predict lower IC, while inflammation and frailty accelerate decline. Maintaining physiological stability and reducing disease burden are key to preserving IC and healthy ageing.

Behavioral and Lifestyle Determinants of Intrinsic Capacity

Among the 36 studies included in this review, 23 (63.9%) investigated the association between behavioral and lifestyle determinants and IC in older adults (Aliberti et al., 2022; Jiang et al., 2023; Leung et al., 2022; Li et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2022; Ma, Chhetri, et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2020; Ma, Zhang, et al., 2021; Muneera et al., 2022; Pan et al., 2024; Prince et al., 2021; Ramírez Vélez et al., 2019; Rarajam Rao et al., 2023; Salinas-Rodríguez et al., 2022; Shen et al., 2023; Si et al., 2023; Su et al., 2023; Tan et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2025; Zhou, Ma, et al., 2025; Zhou, Chang, et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2024). These studies collectively underscore the influence of modifiable lifestyle behaviors on IC, highlighting several key domains including smoking, alcohol consumption, physical activity, dietary patterns, and health-promoting practices. Evidence consistently demonstrates a negative relationship between smoking and IC, with chronic smokers exhibiting diminished vitality and cognitive performance compared to non-smokers (Aliberti et al., 2022; Jiang et al., 2023; Leung et al., 2022; Li et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2023; Ma, Chhetri, et al., 2021; Ma, Zhang, et al., 2021; Muneera et al., 2022; Pan et al., 2024; Rarajam Rao et al., 2023; Shen et al., 2023; Si et al., 2023; Tan et al., 2025; Zhou, Ma, et al., 2025; Zhou, Chang, et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2024). The evidence regarding alcohol consumption was more heterogeneous.

While one study suggested a potential positive association between light alcohol intake and IC (Leung et al., 2022), the majority of findings indicated detrimental effects, especially in cases of regular or excessive consumption (Muneera et al., 2022; Pan et al., 2024). As such, the relationship between alcohol use and IC remains inconclusive and warrants further investigation. Physical activity emerged as a central behavioral determinant in maintaining and enhancing IC. Regular engagement in moderate-intensity physical activities, including brisk walking, yoga, and light calisthenics, was positively correlated with overall IC scores. These activities demonstrated particularly strong effects in the vitality, psychological, and sensory domains (Jiang et al., 2023; Leung et al., 2022; Ma, Chhetri, et al., 2021; Muneera et al., 2022; Zhou, Chang, et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2024).

Notably, some studies emphasized that the frequency and consistency of physical activity may play a more critical role than intensity. Even light-intensity daily activities such as walking or household chores were associated with positive outcomes in IC maintenance (Muneera et al., 2022). Participation in community-based or group exercise activities was also reported to enhance social interaction, which in turn may indirectly benefit cognitive and psychological aspects of IC (Zhu et al., 2024).

Dietary behavior was another significant determinant. Diets characterized by high intakes of vegetables, fruits, and quality animal proteins were positively associated with IC, whereas diets high in sugar and fat were linked to poorer outcomes (Li et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2023; Ma, Zhang, et al., 2021; Pan et al., 2024; Salinas-Rodríguez et al., 2022; Tan et al., 2025). Also, adequate and balanced nutrition appears to be essential for sustaining physical function, especially in relation to vitality, one of the core domains of IC. Furthermore, health-promoting behaviors, such as nutritional self-management, regular exercise routines, and effective stress regulation, were associated with higher IC levels among older adults (Jiang et al., 2023; Su et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2024). Psychosocial factors, such as social isolation and perceived stress, can indirectly reduce IC by weakening motivation for healthy behaviors. Evidence suggests health behaviors mediate the link between psychosocial stressors and IC decline (Su et al., 2023). IC is influenced by modifiable lifestyle factors. Physical activity, balanced nutrition, and health-promoting behaviors support IC, while smoking, excessive alcohol consumption, and poor diet speed up decline. Psychosocial factors such as loneliness and stress may indirectly affect IC through behavioral pathways.

Environmental Determinants of Intrinsic Capacity

Among the 36 studies included in this review, 12 (33.3%) examined the relationship between environmental determinants and IC in older adults (Gutiérrez-Robledo et al., 2021; Leung et al., 2022; Li et al., 2025; Ma, Chhetri, et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2020; Pan et al., 2024; Prince et al., 2021; Si et al., 2023; Su et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2024; Zhou, Chang, et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2024). It was observed that living conditions substantially affect IC levels among older adults.

Residential environment plays an important role in IC. Older adults living in convenient, clean, accessible, and age-friendly neighborhoods, such as those with barrier-free facilities, show higher IC than those in crowded or polluted settings (Zhao et al., 2024). Neighborhood cohesion and strong social ties are also linked to better IC, with supportive community relationships associated with improved outcomes (Zhu et al., 2024).

Social participation is a key environmental determinant of IC. Frequent engagement in community and volunteer activities is associated with higher IC, especially in psychological, vitality, and cognitive domains (Si et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2024). Furthermore, social support systems were found to have a protective effect on IC. Individuals who received greater emotional or material support exhibited higher levels of IC (Zhou, Chang, et al., 2025). In contrast, social isolation and social frailty, characterized by living alone, reduced social participation, and insufficient social support, were consistently associated with diminished IC (Su et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2024).

Life-space mobility (LSM) is positively associated with IC; older adults who move freely within their home and community maintain higher IC than those with restricted mobility (Zhao et al., 2024). Early-life environments also have lasting effects: supportive families and healthier childhood neighborhoods predict better cognitive and psychological IC later in life (Si et al., 2023). IC in older adults is shaped by environmental conditions. Supportive, accessible, and socially cohesive environments help maintain IC, while isolation and poor surroundings contribute to decline. Positive family and community contexts across the life course benefit cognitive and psychological functioning.

Discussion

Summary of Interpretation of Findings

This review synthesized key determinants of IC among older adults, with emphasis on evidence from LMIC. The findings offer a theoretical basis for future IC-focused interventions to promote healthy aging. According to the WHO, IC represents the composite of physical and mental capacities, reflecting a function-oriented framework in geriatric care (WHO, 2017). IC has been shown to predict disability, frailty, and other adverse outcomes (Cesari et al., 2018; Huang et al., 2024). As care paradigms shift from disease-centered models toward approaches emphasizing functional ability and holistic well-being (Qi et al., 2024), identifying modifiable IC determinants becomes essential.

In this review, IC determinants were categorized into four domains: (1) sociodemographic, (2) biological and disease-related, (3) behavioral and lifestyle, and (4) environmental. Collectively, these domains shape IC and reflect the multidimensional health challenges faced by older adults in LMICs. The quality of the included studies varied, which influenced confidence in the results. Sociodemographic factors (e.g., age, gender, education) were supported by higher-quality cohort evidence and appear more reliable. In contrast, biological/disease factors were mostly examined in moderate-quality studies, and lifestyle and environmental determinants relied largely on cross-sectional self-reports. Therefore, evidence for lifestyle and environmental factors remains less certain and requires further longitudinal validation.

Sociodemographic Determinants of Intrinsic Capacity

Sociodemographic factors fundamentally shape disparities in IC, especially in LMICs where structural health and resource inequities persist (Wei et al., 2024). Key variables including age, gender, marital status, education, economic status, and occupational history exert significant influence on both the rate of IC decline. The impact of age on IC is complex and context-dependent. Longitudinal evidence from China shows substantial heterogeneity in IC trajectories, with social and behavioral factors moderating both the rate of decline and the severity of adverse outcomes (Guo et al., 2025). Consistent evidence links IC deterioration to higher risks of disability, falls, hospitalization, and mortality (Yang et al., 2024).

Gender differences in IC may reflect cumulative life-course disadvantages, including caregiving roles, fewer educational opportunities, and limited income-earning capacity, contributing to women’s greater vulnerability to functional decline and higher risks of falls and disability (Muneera et al., 2023). Marital status also shapes IC, with unmarried, divorced, and widowed older adults facing weaker social support and greater IC decline; evidence from Latin America shows that being single or widowed is significantly associated with reduced functional ability (Rico et al., 2024).

Economic and educational status are key structural determinants of IC. Socioeconomic disadvantage, especially in rural and low-income groups, contributes to IC disparities (Liu et al., 2023), while lower education is associated with shorter healthy life expectancy and greater IC decline, emphasizing the importance of health literacy and self-management (Balaj et al., 2024). Living conditions and occupational history further affect IC; manual labor and residence in under-resourced rural environments predict greater functional impairment, reduced mobility, and lower IC (Lee et al., 2021).

In summary, sociodemographic disparities in IC call for equity-focused interventions. Addressing gender, marital, rural, economic, and educational gaps is key to promoting healthy aging, particularly in LMICs.

Biological, Physiological, and Disease-Related Determinants of Intrinsic Capacity

Biological, physiological, and disease-related factors play a key role in shaping IC, especially in LMICs where fragile health systems, limited care access, and suboptimal chronic disease management accelerate age-related functional decline and increase vulnerability to poor IC outcomes (Cao et al., 2024). The burden of chronic diseases rises steeply with age, especially in LMICs where limited health literacy and fragmented care drive multimorbidity (Gupta et al., 2022). Consequent polypharmacy increases risks of adverse drug events and accelerates physiological decline, undermining IC (Meng, Hsiao, et al., 2022). Thus, regular medication review in primary care and a shift from disease-centered to function- and IC-focused care models are essential.

Frailty reflects declining IC and precedes severe impairment, with key features overlapping IC domains. In LMICs, limited screening increases disability and mortality risks (Gonzalez-Bautista et al., 2023). Objective measures like gait speed and balance predict IC decline but remain underused (Tay et al., 2023).

Nutritional status strongly affects IC. In LMICs, food insecurity and underweight coexist with obesity linked to dietary transitions. Evidence suggests a nonlinear relationship between BMI and IC, with some studies reporting better outcomes among overweight individuals, potentially reflecting greater muscle mass or metabolic reserves (Yang et al., 2024). Cultural norms equating higher body weight with health and prosperity may further obscure early signs of functional decline. Consequently, BMI assessment should be contextualized using age-specific cut-offs, basal metabolic rates, and body composition metrics to guide function-oriented nutritional strategies (Munoz Redondo et al., 2023).

Systemic inflammation, metabolic dysfunction, and genetic susceptibility are increasingly recognized as major contributors to IC decline (Corrêa et al., 2025; Lu et al., 2023). Biomarkers offer a promising means to support early detection, risk stratification, and monitoring. In LMIC, developing affordable and accessible biomarker-based screening systems may substantially improve early identification and enable timely, targeted interventions (Merchant et al., 2021).

In summary, biological, physiological, and disease factors drive IC decline in older adults. Comprehensive, function-centered approaches are vital for early detection, optimal care, and maintaining intrinsic capacity with age.

Behavioral and Lifestyle Determinants of Intrinsic Capacity

Behavioral and lifestyle determinants exert a profound and cumulative influence on the IC of older adults—a relationship that is particularly pronounced in LMICs, where social and environmental constraints often limit opportunities for healthy living (Angelsen et al., 2024). These determinants act through intertwined physiological, psychological, and social mechanisms, shaping functional capacity over the life course.

Smoking is consistently associated with reduced vitality and cognition in IC, largely through cardiopulmonary decline, inflammation, and cerebrovascular damage (Giudici et al., 2019). Evidence on alcohol is mixed, but overall effects appear harmful due to neurotoxicity, nutrient malabsorption, and higher chronic disease risk (Kawakami et al., 2023). In LMICs, weak regulation and low health awareness intensify these risks, highlighting the need for targeted interventions (Singh et al., 2017). Physical activity protects IC, with consistent movement, even light walking, supporting function (Raffin et al., 2025). However, older adults in LMICs often face environmental and safety barriers that limit sustained physical activity (Karunanayake et al., 2020).

IC is shaped by diet, self-care, and psychosocial conditions. Micronutrient deficiencies, metabolic imbalance, and ultra-processed food intake in LMICs contribute to IC decline, highlighting the need for community nutrition programs (Kim et al., 2024). Healthy behaviors and stress management support vitality and psychological function (Liao et al., 2023), while isolation and limited mental-health and community resources undermine IC. Strengthening primary care, follow-up, and community support systems is essential in LMICs (Kwaitana et al., 2024). In sum, sustaining IC in older adults in LMICs requires integrated, system-level strategies. Community-based, low-cost, and multisectoral approaches offer practical means to reduce functional decline and support healthy aging.

Environmental Determinants of Intrinsic Capacity

The environment plays a key role in shaping IC by influencing health behaviors, social interaction, and access to essential services, beyond individual physiological or demographic factors (Salinas-Rodríguez et al., 2024). In LMICs, these effects are intensified by inadequate infrastructure and unequal resource distribution.

Urban–rural gaps restrict mobility and increase physical strain among older adults, undermining functional ability. Simple environmental modifications (e.g., ramps, handrails, indoor safety) can promote independence and support key intrinsic capacity domains, particularly locomotor, psychological, and cognitive functions (Pan et al., 2024). The social environment strongly affects later-life well-being. Supportive, cohesive neighborhoods promote emotional stability and social participation, while disadvantaged settings increase risks of stress, depression, and cognitive decline (Courtin & Knapp, 2017). Inclusive community design and accessible services foster social connectedness and resilience.

Urbanization and labor migration have weakened traditional caregiving networks in LMICs, increasing isolation and unmet care needs among older adults (Shin et al., 2025). Community volunteers, peer support, and routine follow-ups can help provide emotional and practical assistance, supporting IC over time. Early-life environments shape IC, with adequate hygiene, nurturing, and education fostering resilience and long-term functional advantages (Cacciatore et al., 2024). Digital tools, such as mHealth, telemedicine, and online social networks, can help overcome environmental barriers and support healthy aging, particularly in resource-limited settings (Stara et al., 2023). In summary, environmental factors influence IC through physical, social, and life-course pathways. Enhancing environments, infrastructure, social support, and digital access is vital for healthy aging, especially in LMICs.

Critical Perspectives and Implementation Challenges of Intrinsic Capacity

IC research faces persistent measurement heterogeneity, with inconsistent tools, scoring systems, and operational definitions limiting comparability and synthesis (George et al., 2021; Gonzalez-Bautista et al., 2020). Evidence largely focuses on associations rather than mechanisms, with limited attention to interactions across IC domains and culturally salient factors, particularly in LMICs (Beyene et al., 2024). Structural drivers such as poverty and gender inequity remain under-examined, and contradictory findings receive insufficient discussion, reducing policy relevance (Wang et al., 2025). Evidence largely focuses on associations rather than mechanisms, with limited attention to interactions across IC domains and culturally salient factors, particularly in LMICs (Bernal et al., 2025).

Strengths and Limitations

This review is one of the few to systematically explore determinants of IC among older adults in LMICs, offering evidence relevant to resource-limited settings. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. IC research in LMICs remains scarce and geographically uneven, with most studies conducted in China and few from Africa or Latin America, limiting generalizability. Measurement heterogeneity, including varying IC tools, definitions, and scoring systems, further constrains comparability due to the lack of global standardization. Although the review targeted adults ≥60 years, some studies included participants ≥50 years, introducing age-related heterogeneity. Most evidence was cross-sectional, restricting causal inference. Language and publication restrictions may have excluded relevant studies. Finally, despite using validated appraisal tools (AHRQ, NOS), context-specific biases in LMICs, incomplete reporting, and insufficient data precluded subgroup analyses by region, gender, or urban–rural status.

Conclusion

This study systematically reviewed and synthesized the determinants of IC, grouping them into four core domains: sociodemographic, biological and disease-related, behavioral and lifestyle, and environmental factors. This integrative framework enhances understanding of the mechanisms shaping IC and provides a theoretical basis for designing and implementing targeted interventions. As global aging accelerates, especially in low- and middle-income countries, traditional disease-centered geriatric care is insufficient for addressing the complex needs of older adults. A shift toward integrated models that prioritize IC is essential to maintain function, delay decline, and improve healthy life expectancy and quality of life. However, IC research remains nascent, particularly in resource-limited settings. Further studies across diverse socioeconomic contexts are needed to identify modifiable, feasible determinants. Strengthening this evidence base is crucial to developing effective early-warning and risk-stratification systems, ultimately enabling equitable, scalable strategies to support healthy aging at the population level.

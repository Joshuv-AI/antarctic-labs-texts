Continuous linear series
Eduardo Esteves, Antonio Nigro, Pedro Rizzo

Abstract
. We parameterize by a fine moduli space all degenerations of linear series to a singular curve which is the union of two smooth components meeting transversally at a single point. For this we introduce a novel object in the study of degenerations of linear series, which is the continuous linear series . Our moduli space can be regarded as a Hilbert quotient, in the terminology introduced by Kapranov, and is a new compactification of Osserman moduli space of exact limit linear series, and consequently, of Eisenbud and Harris moduli space of refined limit linear series on the curve.

1.
Introduction.

Fix a singular curve
X
consisting of two smooth components
Y
and
Z
meeting transversally at a point
P
. A
generalized linear series
on
X
is a space of sections of a torsion-free, rank-1 sheaf on
X
. For short, a
continuous linear series
on
X
is a
maximal primitive
(definitions given later) family of generalized linear series on
X
parameterized by an Artin stack which is the quotient of a two-punctured semistable chain of rational curves modulo an action of the one-dimensional torus
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
. We parameterize them by a projective scheme.

The history of the study and applications of degenerations of linear series goes a long way back, even predating work by the Italian School of Algebraic Geometry of Severi and others from the turn to the 20th Century. The modern study arose from applications to Brill–Noether Theory of the theory of limit linear series developed by Eisenbud and Harris in the 1980’s.

Degenerating linear series along families of smooth curves degenerating to a singular curve have many limits. For limit curves of compact type, Eisenbud and Harris
[
12
]
took those limits whose restrictions to the components of the limit curve have degree zero but for a component, the focus, and verified the collection of their restrictions to their respective foci is a
limit linear series
. A limit linear series, according to Eisenbud and Harris
[
12
]
, pp. 345–6, is a collection of linear series of same rank and degree, one on each component of the limit curve, satisfying certain inequalities of order sequences. It is called
refined
if all the inequalities are equalities. Eisenbud and Harris proceeded to produce a moduli space parameterizing refined limit linear series over curves of compact type,
[
12
]
, Thm. 3.3, p. 354.
Limit linear series arising from degenerations may not be refined, so their space is only quasi-projective.

Multiple applications of their theory were found to this day; see
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
11
,
38
,
8
,
24
,
25
]
for a sample. On the other hand, multiple efforts (including by the authors) have not yet produced a natural, convenient and convincing generalization of the theory to all nodal curves; see
[
36
,
16
,
17
,
21
,
33
,
34
,
18
,
22
,
23
]
for a sample. Attempts were even made using Tropical Geometry (the connection explained in
[
35
]
), which have nonetheless led to new applications; see
[
6
,
1
,
10
,
28
,
29
]
for a sample.

In the 2000’s Ossermann
[
33
]
argued for another approach for curves of compact type, which is more functorial and works in positive characteristic. Instead of taking limits of linear series whose multidegrees were focused on a component, he considered the collection of all the limits that had effective multidegrees, and verified it is an
Osserman limit linear series
. Over our curve
X
, an Osserman limit linear series of rank
r
and degree
d
is a collection of
d+1
linear series
\mathfrak{g}=\{(L^{(0)},V^{(0)}),\dots,(L^{(d)},V^{(d)})\}
of rank
r
and degree
d
, with
\deg(L^{(0)}_{|Y})=d
and, for each
i
,

(1)

\big(L^{(i)}_{|Y}\,,\,L^{(i)}_{|Z}\big)=\big(L^{(0)}_{|Y}(-iP)\,,\,L^{(0)}_{|Z}(+iP)\big)
;

(2)

\varphi^{i}(V^{(i)})\subseteq V^{(i+1)}
and
\varphi_{i}(V^{(i+1)})\subseteq V^{(i)}
, where
\varphi^{i}\colon L^{(i)}\to L^{(i+1)}
and
\varphi_{i}\colon L^{(i+1)}\to L^{(i)}
are the unique (modulo scalar) nontrivial maps.

Osserman
[
33
]
, Thm. 5.3, p. 1178, went on to produce a projective moduli space
G^{r,\text{Oss}}_{d}(X)
parameterizing those
\mathfrak{g}
. He worked only over our curve
X
, but later
[
34
]
extended the theory to all nodal curves.

In
[
19
]
Osserman and the first author showed another advantage of Osserman’s approach. They showed how to produce a collection
\mathbf{P}(\mathfrak{g})
of degree-
d
divisors of
X
out of an Osserman limit linear series
\mathfrak{g}=\{(L^{(0)},V^{(0)}),\dots,(L^{(d)},V^{(d)})\}
over
X
, and showed it coincides with the collection of limits of divisors if
\mathfrak{g}
arises from a degeneration to
X
, as long as
\mathfrak{g}
is
exact
, a term coined by Osserman in
[
33
]
, Def. A10, p. 1197, meaning that the induced sequences

V^{(i)}\to V^{(i+1)}\to V^{(i)}\to V^{(i+1)}

are exact for all
i
; see
[
19
]
, Def. 4.1, p. 83 and Thm. 5.2, p. 90. (Exactness is also necessary by
[
18
]
, Prop. 5.4, p. 846.)
This does not hold for Eisenbud and Harris limit linear series, unless they are refined, in which case they are essentially exact Osserman limit linear series; see
[
33
]
, Cor. 6.8, p. 1189 and Rmk. 6.16, p. 1192. (This correspondence is not true for curves with more components; see
[
32
]
.) But exactness is an open condition. It turned out then that Osserman moduli space
G^{r,\text{Oss}}_{d}(X)
is projective, but only an open subset of it — though dense for general
X
by Liu
[
31
]
— parameterizes enough data to determine the limits of divisors.

Degenerating linear series along a degenerating family of curves do yield at the limit exact Osserman limit linear series, as long as the total space of the family is smooth; see
[
19
]
, § 5, p. 90. For our curve
X
as limit curve, smoothness is controlled by a single number
\delta
, called the
singularity degree
at
P
, smoothness corresponding to
\delta=1
. We
[
18
]
have realized that one could replace a nonexact limit linear series by an exact one, by passing to the semistable reduction of the family, albeit one that generalized Osserman limit linear series, by allowing for generalized linear series and for a number of them in the collection depending on
\delta
. Following Osserman, we
[
18
]
, Prop. 3.2, p. 834, constructed a projective variety
G^{r}_{d,\delta}(X)
parameterizing what we termed
level-
\delta
limit linear series
; see
[
18
]
, Def. 3.1, p. 833, or Section
6
for a generalization. Not all of them are exact, and as before only the exact ones yield the right collection of divisors; see
[
18
]
, Thms. 5.5 and 5.6, p. 846. So, again, we were stuck with a quasi-projective variety parameterizing enough data.

As promised in
[
18
]
, p. 829, we are able to put together all the exact loci of the varieties
G^{r}_{d,\delta}(X)
for all
\delta
, and produce, under appropriate identifications, a projective variety
G^{r}_{d}(X)
; see Theorem
6.2
. We realized that the collection of linear series in a limit linear series
\mathfrak{g}
of any level can be put together as a
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
-invariant family of generalized linear series over
X
parameterized by a two-punctured semistable chain of rational curves
(S,N_{0},N_{\infty})
if and only if
\mathfrak{g}
is exact, a fact essential in the proof of our Theorem
6.2
. The two punctures
N_{0}
and
N_{\infty}
are on the initial and final curve of the chain, and by
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
-invariant we mean invariant under an action of
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
on
(S,N_{0},N_{\infty})
. Of course, such a family corresponds to one parameterized by the quotient stack
[S-\{N_{0},N_{\infty}\}/{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}]
. The latter family is
maximal
if it contains linear series with sections on invertible sheaves of all possible nonnegative degrees on
Y
(and on
Z
) and
primitive
if the family is not the pullback of a family over a chain with less components. It is interesting to observe that
[S-\{N_{0},N_{\infty}\}/{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}]
is the limit of a point!

We avoid stacks after this introduction. In this paper, the families we consider, which we do call
continuous linear series
, are defined as
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
-invariant families of generalized linear series over two-punctured semistable chains of rational curves; see Definition
2.4
. The notion of maximality is embedded in Definition
2.3
of twisted generalized linear series, and primitiveness is everywhere nonconstancy. Definition
2.6
is that of a functor
\mathfrak{G}_{d}^{r}(X)
parameterizing families of continuous linear series on
X
, and Theorem
4.3
states that the functor is represented by a projective scheme
G^{r}_{d}(X)
. Finally, Theorem
6.2
states that every point on
G^{r}_{d}(X)
can be viewed as a level-
\delta
limit linear series for some
\delta
.

In Proposition
4.1
we show that the chain of rational curves
S
parameterizing a continuous linear series can be embedded as a
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
-invariant subscheme of a certain fiber
H^{r}_{d}(X,L)
of a
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
-invariant map
H^{r}_{d}(X)\to J
constructed in Section
3
, where
J
is a component of the Picard scheme of
X
and
H^{r}_{d}(X)
is a scheme parameterizing generalized linear series. Furthermore, the fiber
H^{r}_{d}(X,L)
is a
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
-invariant subscheme of a product
\text{Grass}\big(r+1,W_{1}\oplus W_{2}\big)\times T
, where
T
is the rational chain of
d+1
rational curves; see Section
3.2
. Were it not for
T
, we would be in the context of work by Giansiracusa and Wu
[
27
]
, who generalized seminal work by Kapranov
[
30
,
26
]
. In fact, stripping a level-
\delta
limit linear series of its sheaves, we are left with a linked sequence of vector spaces, using the terminology in
[
18
]
, Def. 3.5, p. 835, which can be regarded as a complete colineation; see
[
39
]
and
[
40
]
. Thaddeus
[
39
]
showed how the moduli space of complete colineations can be realized as a Chow, Hilbert or Mumford quotient of the action of
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
on a Grassmannian. Our approach is that of constructing a Hilbert quotient, but instead of simply for the action of
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
on the Grassmannian
\text{Grass}\big(r+1,W_{1}\oplus W_{2}\big)
, the action on its product by
T
.

The crux of the paper is the proof of Theorem
4.2
,
the longest of the paper. Using it, not only do we prove representability for our functor
\mathfrak{G}_{d}^{r}(X)
, thus constructing the fine moduli space of continuous linear series, but we show the space is an open and closed subscheme of the Hilbert scheme of
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
-invariant subschemes of appropriate Hilbert polynomial of
H^{r}_{d}(X)
, our Theorem
4.3
.

It might be possible to generalize the approach in this paper to all nodal curves
X
. The space of embedded sheaves
\mathbf{R}^{\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{b}}}
in
[
3
]
, § 2, would replace
J\times T
, and a (to-be-defined) scheme
\mathbf{H}^{r}_{d}(X)
fibered by Grassmannians over
\mathbf{R}^{\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{b}}}
parameterizing generalized linear series would replace
H^{r}_{d}(X)
. In
[
3
]
, Thm. 4.7, degenerations of degree-
d
invertible sheaves are parameterized by certain subschemes

of
\mathbf{R}^{\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{b}}}
, which must be images of (to-be-defined) subschemes

of
\mathbf{H}^{r}_{d}(X)
if one considers degenerations of
(r+1)
-dimensional spaces of sections of these sheaves. One should then look into the piece of the Hilbert scheme of
\mathbf{H}^{r}_{d}(X)
parameterizing the

[
2
]
, § 4.2. The corresponding subschemes

of
\mathbf{H}^{r}_{d}(X)
should be as complicated, hence a theme for another paper.

A logarithmic approach may be possible as well. It might replace handling the toric varieties in the approach delineated above. Carocci has recently announced at the Isaac Newton Institute joint work with Battistella and Wise which looks like a log version of the approach above. On the other hand, a discrete approach has recently appeared in work by Amini and the first author
[
4
]
, which led to applications to canonical series
[
5
]
.

37
]
, where an approach using stable maps rather than Hilbert schemes was developed. We would like to thank Omid Amini, Margarida Melo, Brian Osserman, Marco Pacini, Eduardo Vital and Filippo Viviani for helpful discussions on the subject.

2.
Continuous linear series

Fix

A reduced projective scheme of pure dimension

)

If

2.1.
The family of twisters

A

(

, in such a way that

only if

, and the intersection is transverse, and at a single point. The ordering is unique up to reversing it. The components
T_{0}
and
T_{d}
are called

. Given two

N_{0}\in T_{0}(k)
and

, we call

. From now on, we will only consider two-pointed chains, and we will refer to them as just chains. The nodes of
T
and
N_{0},N_{\infty}
are called the
special
points of the chain. For each

, denote by

the set of nonspecial points of

on
T_{i}
and

. There are actions of
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
on

leaving

and the special points of

on each
T_{i}
fixed, and making

a torsor for

. We call such an action a

.

Fix
a nonnegative integer

.
Fix
a chain
(T,N_{0},N_{\infty})
having
d+1
components.
Fix
an ordering

of the components such that

intersects

only if
|i-j|\leq 1
. For each

, let

be the intersection of

and

. For convenience, put

.

For each

,
fix
an isomorphism

such that, denoting by
\xi^{i}\colon\mathcal{O}_{T_{i}}^{2}\to\mathcal{O}_{T_{i}}(1)

, we have that
\xi^{i}|_{N_{i}}(\mathcal{O}_{N_{i}}\oplus 0)=0
and
\xi^{i}|_{N_{i+1}}(0\oplus\mathcal{O}_{N_{i+1}})=0
.
Fix

such that
\xi^{i}|_{E_{i}}
is the cokernel of the diagonal
\mathcal{O}_{E_{i}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{E_{i}}^{2}
. There is a unique chain action of
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
on
(T,N_{0},N_{\infty})
such that
\xi^{i}|_{x\ast Q}=\xi^{i}|_{Q}(x,1)
, where
(x,1)
is the natural endomorphism on
\mathcal{O}_{Q}^{2}
, for each

and

.
Fix
this action. Notice that for each

and each

, we have that

, whereas
\lim_{x\to\infty}x\ast Q=N_{i+1}
.

Fix

\mathcal{O}_{Y}(P)|_{P}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{O}_{P}
and
\mathcal{O}_{Z}(P)|_{P}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{O}_{P}
, and from these construct isomorphisms

and

for all integers

and

. Using them we obtain a surjection on

for each

Let

be the kernel of

.
Then

is an invertible sheaf on

whose restriction to

\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-iP)
and to

\mathcal{O}_{Z}(iP)
. Let

be the pullback of

and
\xi^{i}_{X}
that of

to

.
Identify

, and consider

. It is a surjection of sheaves on

; let

denote its kernel. Identifying

. Furthermore,

. Then

The sheaf

is invariant under the action of

, each

and

\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-iP)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z}(iP)
.

We say

is the

associated to

2.2.
An alternative construction

Let

, intersecting

Let

in this order.
Denote by
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
the resulting space,
and by
\mathcal{Y}_{i}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{i}
the strict transforms in

\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
of
Y\times T_{i}
and
Z\times T_{i}
for each

. It follows that
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}

is regular and the
\mathcal{Y}_{i}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{i}
are Cartier divisors of

\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
. Let

denote the blowup map. In the sense of

,
the map

is a semistable modification of
each of the two projections of the fibered product

\mathcal{X}\times_{B}\mathcal{T}
onto its factors. In particular,
the composition of the blowup with any of
these projections is flat.

Let
C_{i}:=\psi^{-1}(P,N_{i})
for each
i=0,\dots,d+1
. Then the

are rational smooth curves.
The strict transforms
\mathcal{Y}_{i-1}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{i}
contain
C_{i}
,
whereas
\mathcal{Y}_{i}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{i-1}
intersect

transversally at unique and distinct points. We have that

for each

. Also,
\mathcal{Y}_{j}\cdot C_{i}=0
if

, whereas
\mathcal{Y}_{i}\cdot C_{i}=1
and
\mathcal{Y}_{i-1}\cdot C_{i}=-1
.

Set

\mathcal{G}:=\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}\Big(-\mathcal{Y}_{-1}+\mathcal{Y}_{1}+2\mathcal{Y}_{2}+\cdots+(d+1)\mathcal{Y}_{d+1}\Big).

Then
\mathcal{G}|_{C_{i}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{C_{i}}(1)
for each

i=0,\dots,d+1
, and hence the sheaf
\mathcal{G}
is
\psi
-admissible, in the sense of
[
20
, § 3]
. It follows from
[

, Thm. 3.1, p. 63]
that
\psi_{\ast}\mathcal{G}
is a relatively torsion-free, rank-1 sheaf of degree 0 on

\mathcal{X}\times_{B}\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{T}
, whose

\psi_{\ast}\mathcal{G}|_{X\times T}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{F}
.

Definition 2.1

Let

Definition 2.2

B

T
over

of morphisms in
\mathcal{C}_{B}
to
(p_{1},B\times N_{0},B\times N_{\infty})

The category
\mathcal{C}_{B}(T)

(1)

\gamma_{2*}[\mathcal{S}_{b}]=[T]
for each

over

-morphism

.

. Given a chain map to

lifts to a chain action on the source; the lifting may not be unique.

Let
B
be a scheme. Let
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\colon\mathcal{S}\to B\times T

. Consider the induced maps:

.

Let

along
\gamma

Let
L
be an invertible sheaf on

. Let

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(L,\mathcal{V})
be a twisted generalized linear series on

along a chain map
\gamma\colon S\to T
. Let

be an irreducible component of
S
. We say

is

. It is said to be

for each

and

. We say

(resp.

. It is said to be

.

(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{V})

along a family of chain maps

to

along

, called the

of

along

.

Also, for each map

, considering the Cartesian diagram

\begin{CD}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}@>{b^{\prime}}>{}>\mathcal{S}\\
@V{\gamma^{\prime}}V{}V@V{\gamma}V{}V\\
B^{\prime}\times T@>{b\times 1}>{}>B\times T,\end{CD}

we have that

is a family of chain maps, and

is a family of twisted generalized linear series on

along
\gamma^{\prime}
called the

of

under

. We call
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{V})
a

(resp.
everywhere invariant
, resp.

)

along

if

.

Finally, given an invertible sheaf

on

, we have that

is another family of twisted generalized linear series on
X
along
\gamma
, where

is the indicated projection.

Definition 2.5
.

Two families of twisted generalized linear series

to

, an invertible sheaf

on

takes

to

.

.

and

on

over

.

Parametrization of generalized linear series

3.1.
The scheme of generalized linear series

Fix

, the component of the Picard scheme of
X
parameterizing invertible sheaves of bidegree
(d,0)
, that is, degree
d
on
Y
and

on

\mathcal{P}
be a Poincaré sheaf on

be an effective divisor of

its pullback to

\mathcal{P}\boxtimes\mathcal{F}:=p_{1,2}^{*}(\mathcal{P})\otimes p_{1,3}^{*}(\mathcal{F})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{E}:=p_{2,3\ast}\left(\mathcal{P}\boxtimes\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}(D^{\prime})\right),

where
p_{1,2}
,
p_{1,3}
and
p_{2,3}
are the projections onto the indicated factors:

(3)

\textstyle{X\times J\times T\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

to

. Denote by

the degeneracy scheme of

. It is a scheme over

.

Let

be the induced map. Let

be the restriction of

to

of

and rank

on

3.2.
The action.

Since
J
is isomorphic to an Abelian variety, every action of
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
on
J
is trivial. The action of
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
on

lifts thus to a unique action on

.

We claim the action lifts naturally
to one on

. Indeed,
H^{r}_{d}(X)\subseteq\text{Grass}_{J\times T}(r+1,\mathcal{E})
. Now,

\mathcal{P}\boxtimes\mathcal{F}\subseteq p_{1,2}^{*}\Big(\mathcal{P}\otimes p_{1}^{*}\big(\mathcal{O}_{Y}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z}(dP)\big)\Big),

where

is the projection map. Put

on

(x^{-1},1)

is invariant and the induced map

T\subseteq({\mathbf{P}}^{1})^{d+1}

. The embedding is equivariant, for the product action of

{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
on

3.3.

Let
W_{1}
and

be finite-dimensional vector spaces over

W:=W_{1}\oplus W_{2}
. Let
\iota_{i}\colon W_{i}\to W
be the natural injection and
\rho_{i}\colon W\to W_{i}
be the natural surjection for

Let

be a positive integer and
G:=\text{Grass}\left(n,W\right)
.
Recall that
G
is a nonsingular projective variety
with
\text{Pic}(G)=\mathbf{Z}
. The degree of a curve in

is its intersection number with the ample generator of
\text{Pic}(G)
.

The action of
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
on
W=W_{1}\oplus W_{2}
, given by
x\ast(u_{1},u_{2}):=(x^{-1}u_{1},u_{2})

, induces an action on

. For each

and

, we have
x\ast V=(x^{-1},1)V
.

For each
V\in G(k)
, let
h_{V}\colon\mathbb{{\mathbf{P}}}^{1}\to G
be the morphism sending
(x:1)
to
x\ast V
for each
x\in{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}(k)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
V\in G(k)
be a nonfixed point.
Let
V_{\infty,1}:=\iota_{1}^{-1}(V)
and

V_{0,2}:=\iota_{2}^{-1}(V)

V_{\infty,2}:=\rho_{2}(V)
and
V_{0,1}:=\rho_{1}(V)
. Then
V_{\infty,1}\subsetneqq V_{0,1}
and
V_{0,2}\subsetneqq V_{\infty,2}
. Furthermore,

(4)

\lim_{x\to 0}x*V=V_{0,1}\oplus V_{0,2}\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{x\to\infty}x*V=V_{\infty,1}\oplus V_{\infty,2}.

Finally,
h_{V}
is an isomorphism onto its image
\Gamma

\deg\Gamma=\dim_{k}V_{0,1}-\dim_{k}V_{\infty,1}=\dim_{k}V_{\infty,2}-\dim_{k}V_{0,2}.

The first statement is clear, where the inequalities follow from the fact that
V

is an isomorphism to a subspace

such that

. Similarly, the projection map
V^{\prime}\to W_{1}
is an isomorphism to a subspace

such that

4.
Representability

4.1.
Embeddedness

Let
\mathfrak{g}:=(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{V})
be a family of twisted generalized linear series of degree
d
and rank
r

along
\gamma
, for a family of chain maps
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\colon\mathcal{S}\to B\times T
. Recall
\mathcal{P}
from Section
3.1
. Then
there are an invertible sheaf
N
on
B
and a map
h\colon B\to J

such that
(1_{X}\times h)^{*}\mathcal{P}\cong\mathcal{L}\otimes N
. Up to replacing
\mathfrak{g}
by an equivalent family, we may assume
N=\mathcal{O}_{B}
.
The induced map
f:=(h\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\colon\mathcal{S}\to J\times T
satisfies

(1_{X}\times f)^{*}(\mathcal{P}\boxtimes\mathcal{F})\cong\mathcal{L}(\gamma)
. The locally free subsheaf
\mathcal{V}\subseteq p_{\mathcal{S}*}\mathcal{L}(\gamma)

of rank
r+1
induces thus a map

f_{\mathfrak{g}}\colon\mathcal{S}\to H^{r}_{d}(X)\times B
.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a family of continuous linear series, then
f_{\mathfrak{g}}
is a closed embedding. Furthermore, its image
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
-invariant and has relative multivariate Hilbert polynomial
\phi
over
B
.

Proof.

We may assume
B=\text{Spec}(k)
. Then
f_{\mathfrak{g}}
factors through the fiber
H^{r}_{d}(X,\mathcal{L})
of
H^{r}_{d}(X)
over
\mathcal{L}
, which is naturally an invariant subscheme of
G\times T
, where

G:=\text{Grass}(r+1,W)\quad\text{for }W:=W_{1}\oplus W_{2}:=\Gamma(Y,\mathcal{L}|_{Y})\oplus\Gamma(Z,\mathcal{L}|_{Z}(dP)).

Let
\iota_{i}\colon W_{i}\to W
be the natural injection and
\rho_{i}\colon W\to W_{i}
be the natural surjection for
i=1,2
.

As a map to
G\times T
, we have
f_{\mathfrak{g}}=(f,\gamma_{2})
.
Let
S_{0},\dots,S_{m}
be the sequence of irreducible components of
\mathcal{S}
, ordered in such a way that

(1)

\gamma_{2}(S_{0})=T_{0}
and
\gamma_{2}(S_{m})=T_{d}
,

(2)

S_{i}\cap S_{j}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
|i-j|\leq 1
.

Let
Q_{i}:=S_{i-1}\cap S_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,m
, and
Q_{0},Q_{m+1}\in\mathcal{S}
with
\gamma_{2}(Q_{0})=N_{0}
and
\gamma_{2}(Q_{m+1})=N_{\infty}
.

Since
\mathfrak{g}
is everywhere invariant, the image under
f_{\mathfrak{g}}
of each
S_{i}
is invariant, thus
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is invariant. We will argue now that
f_{\mathfrak{g}}
is an embedding. First of all, since
\mathfrak{g}
is also everywhere nonconstant, for each
i=0,\dots,m
, either
\gamma_{2}|_{S_{i}}
is an embedding, or there is
s\in S_{i}
such that
f(s)
is not fixed in
G
, and hence
f|_{S_{i}}
is an embedding by Proposition
3.1
. In any case,

f_{\mathfrak{g}}|_{S_{i}}
is an embedding.

To show that
f_{\mathfrak{g}}
is injective, by contradiction, let
R_{1}\in S_{i}
and
R_{2}\in S_{j}
for
i\leq j
be distinct points on
\mathcal{S}
such that
f_{\mathfrak{g}}(R_{1})=f_{\mathfrak{g}}(R_{2})
. Then
\gamma_{2}(R_{1})=\gamma_{2}(R_{2})
. Since
\gamma_{2}
is a chain map,
\gamma_{2}(S_{i})=\gamma_{2}(S_{i+1})=\cdots=\gamma_{2}(S_{j})=N_{\ell}
for some
\ell
. As proved above,
f|_{S_{i}},f|_{S_{i+1}},\dots,f|_{S_{j}}
are embeddings. Furthermore, Proposition
3.1
yields as well that

\rho_{2}(f(R_{1}))=\rho_{2}(f(Q_{i+1}))\subsetneqq\rho_{2}(f(Q_{i+2}))\subsetneqq\cdots\subsetneqq\rho_{2}(f(Q_{j}))\subseteq\rho_{2}(f(R_{2})),

where the last inclusion is strict if
R_{2}\neq Q_{j}
, and

\rho_{1}(f(R_{1}))\supseteq\rho_{1}(f(Q_{i+1}))\supsetneqq\rho_{1}(f(Q_{i+2}))\supsetneqq\cdots\supsetneqq\rho_{1}(f(Q_{j}))=\rho_{1}(f(R_{2})),

where the first inclusion is strict if
R_{1}\neq Q_{i+1}
. Since
f(R_{1})=f(R_{2})
, we must have that either
j=i+1
and
R_{1}=Q_{j}=R_{2}
, or
j=i
, and thus
R_{1}=R_{2}
because
f|_{S_{i}}
is injective. In any case,
R_{1}=R_{2}
, a contradiction.

We need now only prove that the differentials
d_{Q_{j}}f_{\mathfrak{g}}|_{S_{j-1}}
and
d_{Q_{j}}f_{\mathfrak{g}}|_{S_{j}}
at
Q_{j}
have different images for
j=1,\dots,m
.

First, their images are nonzero because
f_{\mathfrak{g}}|_{S_{j-1}}
and
f_{\mathfrak{g}}|_{S_{j}}
are embeddings. Second, their images are distinct, if
\gamma_{2}|_{S_{j-1}}
or
\gamma_{2}|_{S_{j}}
is not constant, because then the images of
d_{Q_{j}}\gamma_{2}|_{S_{j-1}}
and
d_{Q_{j}}\gamma_{2}|_{S_{j}}
are distinct.

Assume now that
\gamma_{2}(S_{j-1})
and
\gamma_{2}(S_{j})
are each a point, necessarily the same point, a node
N_{\ell}
for some
\ell
. Then
f
restricts to an injection on
S_{j-1}\cup S_{j}
, and to embeddings
f|_{S_{j-1}}
and
f|_{S_{j}}
. We need only show now that the intersection at the unique point on
f(S_{j-1})\,\cap\,f(S_{j})
is transverse. Now, since
{\mathfrak{g}}
is everywhere invariant, there are isomorphisms
\theta_{1}\colon{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}\to S_{j-1}^{*}
and
\theta_{2}\colon{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}\to S_{j}^{*}
such that
\mathcal{V}_{\theta_{i}(x)}=(x^{-1},1)V_{i}
for each
x\in{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}(k)
and
i=1,2
, where
V_{i}:=\mathcal{V}_{\theta_{i}(1)}
. Also,
f\theta_{i}
extends to the map
h_{i}:=h_{V_{i}}\colon{\mathbf{P}}^{1}\to G
defined in Subsection
3.3
. Then
h_{1}(\infty)
and
h_{2}(0)
are equal to the subspace
V:=\mathcal{V}_{Q_{j}}\subseteq W
. By Proposition
3.1
,

h_{1}(\infty)=\lim_{x\to\infty}x\ast V_{1}=\iota_{1}^{-1}(V_{1})\oplus\rho_{2}(V_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad h_{2}(0)=\lim_{x\to 0}x\ast V_{2}=\rho_{1}(V_{2})\oplus\iota_{2}^{-1}(V_{2}),

so

means

\iota_{1}^{-1}(V_{1})=\rho_{1}(V_{2})\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{2}(V_{1})=\iota_{2}^{-1}(V_{2}).

But then Proposition
3.2
yields that
h_{1}({\mathbf{P}}^{1})
and
h_{2}({\mathbf{P}}^{1})
, which are
f(S_{j-1})
and
f(S_{j})
, intersect transversally.

It remains to show that
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}}
has multivariate Hilbert polynomial
\phi
. First of all,
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is a curve, so its multivariate Hilbert polynomial

is linear. Also,
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is connected and of arithmetic genus zero, thus the constant term of

is
1
. Since

\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}}\subseteq H^{r}_{d}(X,\mathcal{L})
, the coefficient of
v
in

is zero. Since
\gamma_{2,*}[\mathcal{S}]=[T]
, the coefficient of

is
1
for each

. It remains to show that the coefficient of

is
r+1
, which is equivalent to showing that

has degree

in
G
.

Let
U_{j}:=\mathcal{V}_{Q_{j}}\subseteq W
for

. It follows from Proposition

that the degree of

is

\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}\Big(\dim_{k}\rho_{1}(U_{i-1})-\dim_{k}\rho_{1}(U_{i})\Big),

which is equal to
\dim_{k}\rho_{1}(U_{0})-\dim_{k}\rho_{1}(U_{m+1})
.
But

U_{0}\subseteq\Gamma(Y,L|_{Y})\oplus\Gamma(Z,L|_{Z}(-P))\quad\text{and}\quad U_{m+1}\subseteq\Gamma(Y,L|_{Y}(-(d+1)P))\oplus\Gamma(Z,L|_{Z}(dP)).

Since

and

have negative degrees,
\dim_{k}\rho_{1}(U_{0})=r+1
and
\rho_{1}(U_{m+1})=0
.
Thus, the degree of
f(\mathcal{S})
is

.
∎

4.2.
First main theorem

Theorem 4.2
.

For each connected
S\in\text{\rm Hilb}^{\phi,{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}}_{H^{r}_{d}(X)}(k)
, the induced map
S\to T
is a chain map.

The proof of this theorem is postponed to the next section. We use it now to prove the first main theorem of the article.

Theorem 4.3
.

The functor
\mathfrak{G}_{d}^{r}(X)
is isomorphic to the functor of points of an open and closed subscheme of
\text{\rm Hilb}_{H_{d}^{r}(X)}^{\phi,{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}}
. In particular, there exists a projective scheme
G_{d}^{r}(X)
representing
\mathfrak{G}_{d}^{r}(X)
.

Proof.

Proposition
4.1
associates to a family of continuous linear series
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{V})
over a scheme
B
a certain family of
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
-invariant subschemes
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}}\subseteq H^{r}_{d}(X)\times B
over
B
with relative multivariate Hilbert polynomial
\phi

whose fibers satisfy
h^{0}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g},b},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g},b}})=1
for every

, thus a

B
-point of the open subscheme
U\subseteq\text{\rm Hilb}_{H_{d}^{r}(X)}^{\phi,{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}}
parameterizing subschemes
S
with
h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})=1
. If
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathcal{L}^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})
is another family equivalent to
\mathfrak{G}
, the corresponding subscheme
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}
is equal to
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}}
, as it follows from Definition
2.5
and the moduli properties of Jacobians and Grassmannians.

Conversely, let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq H_{d}^{r}(X)\times B
be a family of

{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
-invariant closed subschemes over
B
with relative multivariate Hilbert polynomial
\phi
. The natural map
\pi\colon\mathcal{S}\to B
is flat. The fiber
\mathcal{S}_{b}
over each
b\in B
satisfies
h^{0}(\mathcal{S}_{b},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}_{b}})=1
only if
\mathcal{S}_{b}
is connected. Theorem
4.2
yields the converse: if
\mathcal{S}_{b}
is connected then
h^{0}(\mathcal{S}_{b},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}_{b}})=1
. It follows that

U
is also the subset parameterizing connected subschemes of
H_{d}^{r}(X)
, which is closed by the principle of connectedness. Furthermore, Theorem
4.2
yields, if the fibers of

\mathcal{S}
are connected, that
\pi
is a family of chains of rational curves and the natural map

\gamma\colon\mathcal{S}\to T\times B
is a family of chain maps. Since

J
is isomorphic to an Abelian variety, it follows that the map
\mathcal{S}\to J
factors through a map

yielding an invertible sheaf
\mathcal{L}
on

of bidegree
(d,0)
over
B
. Finally, the map

yields a locally free subsheaf

of rank

such that

is a family of twisted generalized linear series. That these series are everywhere nonconstant follows from the fact that
\mathcal{S}\subseteq H_{d}^{r}(X)\times B
. That in addition they are everywhere invariant follows from the fact that
\mathcal{S}
is
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
-invariant. Thus

is a family of continuous linear series over

.

It is clear from the constructions that
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{S}}}=\mathcal{S}
and that
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{g}}}
is equivalent to
\mathfrak{g}
. The last statement follows from the fact that
\text{\rm Hilb}_{H_{d}^{r}(X)}^{\phi,{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}}

\text{\rm Hilb}_{H_{d}^{r}(X)}^{\phi}

∎

or

, respectively. The former is possible only if

. Hence, if

and

, whereas if

Assume now that

nor

is isolated on

. Indeed, since

, we have
S_{i-1}\cap S_{i}=\emptyset
. Since
S
is connected, there is a connected subcurve

of
\overline{S_{\text{\rm red}}-\{S_{i-1}\cup S_{i}\}}
intersecting both
S_{i-1}
and
S_{i}
. Statement 1 yields
C\subseteq S_{i-\frac{1}{2}}
. Since
C
intersects
S_{i}
, there is an irreducible component

of

intersecting
S_{i}
. Since

, the intersection is

, and hence

is not isolated on

. Analogously, neither is

.

Statement 3:

Let

be a maximal connected subcurve of

such that

. Then

contains

and

if

Indeed, reason by contradiction. Without loss of generality, assume

and

does not contain

. Since

, this means that

does not intersect
S_{i}
. Since

is connected, there is a connected subcurve

of
\overline{S_{\text{\rm red}}-\{S_{i}\cup C\}}
intersecting both
S_{i}
and
C
. Let

be an irreducible component of

intersecting

. Then

. Since

, either

, if

. The former contradicts the maximality of

. Thus

and

. Since

, we must have that

meets

at

. Now, if

meets

, but the unique point on

above

is

. Since

lies on

and

does not meet

, we must have that

does not meet
S_{i}
. But since

meets

and contains

, there is a connected subcurve

and

. By Statement 1, we must have

. Then

. But

intersects

, and can only intersect

at

, a contradiction.

Statement 4:

If

is not a point, then

has no isolated point.

Indeed, since

has at most one isolated point by Statement 2, if

is not a point then it contains a nonisolated point

. Let

be a maximal connected subcurve of

containing

. Then Statement 3 says that

contains
N_{i-1,\infty}
if

and

. In particular, neither

for

nor

for

is isolated in

. But then

Statement 5:

S_{i-\frac{1}{2}}
is connected.

If

is a point, then it is clearly connected. If not, then

has no isolated point by Statement 4. It is thus a disjoint union of connected subcurves of

. By Statement 3, each such subcurve contains

. But then all these subcurves contain the same point, and hence there is just one of them. In other words,

∎

Fourth Claim:

There are irreducible components
C_{0},\dots,C_{m}
of
S_{\text{\rm red}}
such that

(1)

C_{0}=S_{0}
and
C_{m}=S_{d}
;

(2)

for

.

(3)

There is an increasing sequence

of integers such that

for

;

(4)

For each
\ell=1,\dots,d
, we have

if

meets
S_{\ell}

i_{\ell}>i_{\ell-1}+1
and

is contained in

.

Proof fourth claim.

Start with
C_{0}:=S_{0}
. Each time
C_{i}=S_{\ell-1}
for
\ell=1,\dots,d
, we do the following: If
S_{\ell}
intersects
S_{\ell-1}
, we put
C_{i+1}:=S_{\ell}
. If not, then
N_{\ell-1,\infty}\neq N_{\ell,0}
, and thus
S_{\ell-\frac{1}{2}}
is not a point by Second Claim. By Third Claim,
S_{\ell-\frac{1}{2}}
is connected. By Second Claim, there are irreducible components
C
and
C^{\prime}
of
S_{\ell-\frac{1}{2}}
such that
C
intersects
S_{\ell-1}
and
C^{\prime}
intersects
S_{\ell}
. Put
C_{i+1}:=C
. Since
S_{\ell-\frac{1}{2}}
is connected, there is a sequence of irreducible components of
S_{\ell-\frac{1}{2}}
, each intersecting the next, starting with

and ending with

. Denote these components sequentially by

, where

is thus the number of components in the sequence and
C_{i+j}=C^{\prime}
. Then put
C_{i+j+1}:=S_{\ell}
.
∎

We observe that
S_{\text{\rm red}}
may not be the union of the
C_{i}
. Indeed,

S_{\ell-\frac{1}{2}}
may not be the union
C_{i_{\ell-1}+1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{i_{\ell}-1}
.

Fifth Claim:

The
p_{1}(C_{i})
which are orbit closures are either points or smooth rational curves. The sum of their degrees in
G
is at least
r+1
, with equality only if:

(1)

all the
p_{1}(C_{i})
are orbit closures,

(2)

for
i,j\in\{0,\dots,m\}
with
i<j
such that
p_{1}(C_{i})
and
p_{1}(C_{j})
are curves that intersect,
p_{1}(C_{i})\cap p_{1}(C_{j})
is scheme-theoretically a point, and
p_{1}(C_{i+1}),\dots,p_{1}(C_{j-1})
are all equal to this point.

Proof fifth claim.

For each
\ell=1,2
, let
\iota_{\ell}\colon W_{\ell}\to W
be the natural injection and
\rho_{\ell}\colon W\to W_{\ell}
the natural surjection. Since
S
is
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
-invariant, each
C_{i}
is
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
-invariant, whence the
p_{1}(C_{i})
are
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
-invariant.

For each
i=0,\dots,m
, let
V_{i}\subseteq W
be the image under
p_{1}
of a general point on
C_{i}
. Put

a_{i}:=\min\{\dim_{k}\rho_{1}(V)\,|\,V\in p_{1}(C_{i})\}\quad\text{and}\quad A_{i}:=\max\{\dim_{k}\rho_{1}(V)\,|\,V\in p_{1}(C_{i})\}.

Thus
a_{i}\leq A_{i}
. If
V_{i}
is fixed by the action of
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}
, then
V_{i}=\rho_{1}(V_{i})\oplus\rho_{2}(V_{i})
. The same holds for each

by continuity, whence
a_{i}=A_{i}
. In this case, if
p_{1}(C_{i})
is an orbit closure then
p_{1}(C_{i})
is the point
V_{i}
. If
V_{i}
is not fixed, then
p_{1}(C_{i})
is the closure of the orbit of
V_{i}
in
G
, and is thus a smooth rational curve of degree
A_{i}-a_{i}>0
by Proposition
3.1
. Furthermore, the boundary of the orbit of
V_{i}
consists of two points on
p_{1}(C_{i})
where
a_{i}
and
A_{i}
are attained.

Now, for each
i=1,\dots,m
, since
C_{i-1}\cap C_{i}\neq\emptyset
, it follows that
the intervals
[a_{i-1},A_{i-1}]
and
[a_{i},A_{i}]
intersect, and thus

I:=\bigcup_{i=0}^{m}[a_{i},A_{i}]

is an interval contained in
[0,r+1]
.
Now,
C_{0}=S_{0}
and
C_{m}=S_{d}
. Since
p_{2}(N_{0,0})=N_{0}
and
p_{2}(N_{d,\infty})=N_{\infty}
, we have that

p_{1}(N_{0,0})\subseteq\Gamma(Y,L|_{Y})\oplus\Gamma(Z,L|_{Z}(-P))\quad\text{and}\quad p_{1}(N_{d,\infty})\subseteq\Gamma(Y,L|_{Y}(-(d+1)P))\oplus\Gamma(Z,L|_{Z}(dP)).

Since
L|_{Z}(-P)
and
L|_{Y}(-(d+1)P)
have negative degrees, it follows that

and
a_{m}=0
.
Thus

. Then the sum of the degrees of the

for
i
such that

is nonfixed, which is
\sum_{i}(A_{i}-a_{i})
, is at least
r+1
.

Assume the sum is exactly
r+1
. Then, first, if
V_{i}
is fixed,
p_{1}(C_{i})
must be a point,
V_{i}
. Thus, all the
p_{1}(C_{i})
are orbit closures. Second, the intervals
[a_{i},A_{i}]
can overlap only at the boundaries. For each
i=1,\dots,m
, since
[a_{i-1},A_{i-1}]
and
[a_{i},A_{i}]
intersect, we must have that either

a_{i}=A_{i-1}
or
A_{i}=a_{i-1}
. Since
A_{0}=r+1
and
a_{m}=0
, we must have that
A_{i}=a_{i-1}
for each

.

Let now
i,j\in\{0,\dots,m\}
with
i<j
such that
p_{1}(C_{i})
and
p_{1}(C_{j})
are curves that intersect. Since

, we have

. Since
p_{1}(C_{i})
and
p_{1}(C_{j})
intersect, the intervals

and
[a_{j},A_{j}]
intersect as well, and hence

. It follows that

are points, actually, the same point

, since

is connected. Furthermore, since
A_{j}=a_{i}
, and
p_{1}(C_{i})
and
p_{1}(C_{j})
are orbit closures that intersect, it follows from Proposition
3.1
that
there is a unique point of intersection, which is

\iota_{1}^{-1}(V_{i})\oplus\rho_{2}(V_{i})=\rho_{1}(V_{j})\oplus\iota_{2}^{-1}(V_{j}).

In addition, Proposition
3.2
yields that the intersection is transverse. Since

C_{i}
intersects
C_{i+1}
and
C_{j}
intersects

, we must have that

is the intersection.
∎

Put
S^{0}:=C_{0}\cup\dots\cup C_{m}
.

Sixth Claim:

The sum of the degrees of the
p_{1}(C_{i})
in

is
r+1
. Each
C_{i}
is rational and the map
C_{i}\to p_{1}(C_{i})
is either an isomorphism or constant, the latter only if

for some
\ell
. Furthermore,

and

is generically reduced. In particular,

and

are points.

Proof sixth claim.

Since
S
has Hilbert polynomial
\phi
, and since the coefficient of

in

is

, the products of the degree of the covering

with the degree of

sum up to at most

. On the other hand, the degrees of the
p_{1}(C_{i})
sum up to at least

by Fifth Claim. It thus follows that both sums are

and that

is either constant or an isomorphism for each

. However, since

, the curve

can only be a point if

is not one, that is, if

for some
\ell
, in which case

is isomorphic to

under

. In any case,

is a rational curve.

Finally, the multivariate Hilbert polynomial of

has the same linear part as that of

. Since

, it follows that
S^{0}=S_{\text{\rm red}}
and

is generically reduced.
∎

Seventh Claim:

For each
i,j\in\{0,\dots,m\}
with
i<j
, the curve
C_{i}
intersects
C_{j}
only if

, transversally at a single point if so.

Proof seventh claim.

Assume

.
Since the

are disjoint, and
S_{h}\cap S_{q}\neq\emptyset
only if

, we must have

i_{\ell-1}\leq i<j\leq i_{\ell}
for some
\ell
. Now, since
p_{2}(S_{\ell-\frac{1}{2}})=N_{\ell}
scheme-theoretically, and

S_{\ell-1}
is mapped isomorphically to

,
we have that

intersects
C_{i_{\ell-1}}
only if
j=i_{\ell-1}+1
, transversally at

if so. Thus
the claim holds if

. By analogy, it holds as well if

.

Assume
i_{\ell-1}<i<j<i_{\ell}
. Then the maps
C_{q}\to p_{1}(C_{q})
are isomorphisms for all

with

by Sixth Claim.
Since
C_{i}\cap C_{j}\neq\emptyset
, it follows from Fifth Claim that

and

is scheme-theoretically a point. Furthermore, since the maps

and

are isomorphisms by Sixth Claim, also

is scheme-theoretically a point.
∎

Eighth Claim:

The curve
S
is a chain of rational curves and
p_{1}\colon S\to T
is a chain map.

Proof.

It follows from Sixth Claim that the
C_{i}
are rational, and thus

is a chain of rational curves by Seventh Claim. Then

is connected of arithmetic genus

, and hence the constant part of its multivariate Hilbert polynomial coincides with that of

. So does its linear part by Sixth Claim. Hence

and
S^{0}
have the same Hilbert polynomial, and since

, we must have

. So

is a chain of rational curves. First and Fourth Claims now yield that

is a chain map.
∎

The proof of Theorem
4.2
is now complete.
∎

6.
Level-
\delta
limit linear series

Let
\delta=(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{d})
be a
d
-tuple of natural numbers. Let

\Delta:=\Delta(\delta):=\Big\{0,\frac{1}{\delta_{1}},\frac{2}{\delta_{1}},\dots,1,1+\frac{1}{\delta_{2}},1+\frac{2}{\delta_{2}},\dots,2,\dots,d-1,d-1+\frac{1}{\delta_{d}},d-1+\frac{2}{\delta_{d}},\dots,d\Big\}.

We say that

are

if

and
\Delta\cap(i,j)=\emptyset
.

6.1.
Review

The reader will recognize the notions and definitions below from
[
18
]
, where we restricted to the case where all the
\delta_{i}
are equal. However, the general theory is similar.

Let
L
be an invertible sheaf on
X
. The
twist
\delta
-sequence
associated to
L
is the collection of sheaves

L^{(i)}
indexed by

i\in\Delta
, where

L^{(i)}:=L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}^{(i)}
if
i\in\mathbf{Z}
and, otherwise,

(6)

L^{(i)}:=\Big(L|_{Y}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}\big(\lfloor-i\rfloor P\big)\Big)\bigoplus\Big(L|_{Z}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Z}\big(\lfloor i\rfloor P\big)\Big),

where
\lfloor i\rfloor
stands for the floor of

, the largest integer at most
i
.

The condition on the
L^{(i)}
ensures that there are isomorphisms

L^{(i)}|_{Z}\longrightarrow\text{Ker}\big(L^{(j)}\to L^{(j)}|_{Y}\big)\quad\text{and}\quad L^{(j)}|_{Y}\longrightarrow\text{Ker}\big(L^{(i)}\to L^{(i)}|_{Z}\big)

for each consecutive

, whence there are nonzero maps
\varphi^{i}\colon L^{(i)}\to L^{(j)}
factoring through
L^{(i)}|_{Z}
and
\varphi_{i}\colon L^{(j)}\to L^{(i)}
factoring through
L^{(j)}|_{Y}
. The
maps
\varphi^{i}
and
\varphi_{i}
are defined up to multiplication by
k^{*}
.

If
L=\mathcal{O}_{X}
, then our notation is compatible with that used in Subsection
2.1
.

Definition 6.1
.

A
level-
\delta
limit linear series
of degree
d
on
X
is
the data
\mathfrak{g}=(L;V^{(i)},\,i\in\Delta)
of an
invertible sheaf

on
X
of bidegree
(d,0)
and vector subspaces

V^{(i)}\subseteq\Gamma(X,L^{(i)})
for

of equal dimension such that

(7)

\varphi^{i}(V^{(i)})\subseteq V^{(j)}\text{\ and\ }\varphi_{i}(V^{(j)})\subseteq V^{(i)}\text{\ for each consecutive }i,j\in\Delta.

We say that
\mathfrak{g}
has rank
r

if the
V^{(i)}
have projective dimension
r
. We say that

\mathfrak{g}
is
exact
if

(8)

\text{Im}\big(\varphi^{i}|_{V^{(i)}}\big)=\text{Ker}\big(\varphi_{i}|_{V^{(j)}}\big)\text{\ and\ }\text{Im}\big(\varphi_{i}|_{V^{(j)}}\big)=\text{Ker}\big(\varphi^{i}|_{V^{(i)}}\big)\text{\ for consecutive }i,j\in\Delta.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=(L;V^{(i)},\,i\in\Delta)
be a level-
\delta
limit linear series of rank
r
and degree
d
on
X
. It follows from (
6
) that

L^{(i)}\subseteq L|_{Y}\oplus L|_{Z}(dP)
for each

. Thus, subspaces

of

may be viewed as subspaces of

, where
U_{1}:=\Gamma(Y,L|_{Y})
and
U_{2}:=\Gamma(Z,L|_{Z}(dP))
. As such, Conditions (
7
) are equivalent to

\rho_{2}(V^{(i)})\subseteq\iota_{2}^{-1}(V^{(j)})\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{1}(V^{(j)})\subseteq\iota_{1}^{-1}(V^{(i)})\quad\text{for each consecutive }i,j\in\Delta,

whereas Conditions (
8
) are equivalent to

(10)

\rho_{2}(V^{(i)})=\iota_{2}^{-1}(V^{(j)})\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{1}(V^{(j)})=\iota_{1}^{-1}(V^{(i)})\quad\text{for each consecutive }i,j\in\Delta,

where

is the natural injection and

is the natural surjection for

The data of the vector spaces

and the maps

for consecutive

. For each

. We say

is

if

. Then

is exact if and only

is; see

As in

parameterizing level-
\delta

, and an open

(c_{i},i\in\Delta)\ast(L_{1};V_{1}^{(i)},i\in\Delta)=(L_{2};V_{2}^{(i)},i\in\Delta)

and

equivalent
if they lie on the same orbit by the action of
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}^{\Delta}
. Equivalent limit linear series have the same numerical data.

As shown in
[
18
]
, Section 3, the numerical data yield a stratification of
G^{r}_{d,\delta}(X)
into locally closed subsets, as follows: For each
function
N\colon\Delta\to\mathbf{Z}^{3}
, let

G_{d,\delta}^{r}(X;N):=\left\{\mathfrak{g}\in G_{d,\delta}^{r}(X)\,|\,N_{\mathfrak{g}}=N\right\}.

So that
G_{d,\delta}^{r}(X;N)
is nonempty, a certain condition on
N
is necessary, called admissibility in
[
18
]
, Def. 3.7, p. 836. In addition, the open
subscheme
G^{r,*}_{d,\delta}(X)\subseteq G^{r}_{d,\delta}(X)
decomposes as a disjoint union of subsets which are both open and closed,

(11)

G_{d,\delta}^{r,*}(X)=\coprod_{N\text{ exact}}G_{d,\delta}^{r}(X;N).

The
G_{d,\delta}^{r}(X;N)
can be given a natural structure of moduli scheme.

For each (exact) function
N\colon\Delta\to\mathbf{Z}^{3}
, let
\Delta_{N}\subseteq\Delta
be the subset of those
i
for which
i\in\mathbf{Z}
or
N(i)\not\in\mathbf{Z}^{2}\times\{0\}
. Let
\delta^{\prime}=(\delta^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\delta^{\prime}_{d})
be the unique
d
-tuple of natural numbers for which there is an isomorphism of posets
f\colon\Delta^{\prime}\to\Delta_{N}
such that
f(i)=i
for each
i\in\mathbf{Z}
,
where
\Delta^{\prime}:=\Delta(\delta^{\prime})
. Let
N^{\prime}:=Nf
. Then
N^{\prime}
is (exact and) minimal. Analogously to what was shown in
[
18
]
, Prop. 4.3, p. 839, the natural forgetful morphism
G_{d,\delta}^{r}(X;N)\to G_{d,\delta^{\prime}}^{r}(X;N^{\prime})
, taking the limit linear series
(L;V(i),i\in\Delta)
to
(L;V(f(i)),i\in\Delta^{\prime})
, is an isomorphism.

Because of this isomorphism, we will consider the open subscheme

G_{d,\delta}^{r,*}(X;\text{\rm min})\subseteq G_{d,\delta}^{r,*}(X),

which is the (disjoint) union of the

G_{d,\delta}^{r}(X;N)
for
N
exact and minimal. Clearly, we have

G_{d,\mathbf{1}}^{r,*}(X;\text{\rm min})=G_{d,\mathbf{1}}^{r,*}(X)
, where
\mathbf{1}:=(1,\dots,1)
.

Theorem 6.2
.

For each

\delta=(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{d})\in\mathbf{N}^{d}
and exact and minimal function
N\colon\Delta(\delta)\to\mathbf{N}^{3}

there is a natural morphism

\Psi_{\delta}^{N}\colon G^{r}_{d,\delta}(X;N)\longrightarrow G_{d}^{r}(X).

The map takes a (minimal exact) level-
\delta
limit linear series
(L;V^{(i)},i\in\Delta)
on
X
with numerical data
N
to the continuous linear series
(L,\mathcal{V})
along
\mu^{(\delta)}
, where
\mathcal{V}|_{E_{i}^{(\delta)}}
is taken to
V^{(i)}
under the choice of an isomorphism
L(\mu^{(\delta)})|_{X\times E_{i}^{(\delta)}}\xrightarrow{\cong}L^{(i)}
for each
i\in\Delta
. Each nonempty fiber of
\Psi_{\delta}^{N}
is an equivalence class of level-
\delta
limit linear series, and vice-versa. Finally, the images of all the
\Psi_{\delta}^{N}
form a partition of the underlying set of
G_{d}^{r}(X)
.

(If
\delta=\mathbf{1}
, then
\mu^{(\delta)}=1_{T}
, all the
L^{(i)}
are invertible, and thus the
\mathcal{V}|_{E_{i}}
do not depend on the choice of an isomorphism
L(1_{T})|_{X\times E_{i}}\cong L^{(i)}
. Also,
\Psi^{N}_{\mathbf{1}}
is injective.)

Proof.

Put
\Delta=\Delta(\delta)
. Consider a family of level-
\delta
limit linear series on
X
of degree
d
, rank
r
and numerical data
N
over a scheme
B
.
The family is given by an invertible sheaf
\mathcal{L}
of relative bidegree
(d,0)
on
X\times B/B
and a family of generalized linear series
(\mathcal{L}^{(i)},\mathcal{V}^{(i)})
on
X\times B/B
, where
\mathcal{L}^{(i)}:=\mathcal{L}(\mu^{(\delta)})|_{X\times E_{i}^{(\delta)}\times B}
under the usual identification of
E_{i}^{(\delta)}\times B
with
B
, for each
i\in\Delta
. The data
(\mathcal{L};\mathcal{V}^{(i)},i\in\Delta)
satisfies certain conditions, to be explained next.

Let
D
be an effective divisor of the smooth locus of
X
. Put

\mathcal{W}_{1}:=p_{B*}\big(\mathcal{L}|_{Y\times B}((D\cap Y)\times B)\big)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{W}_{2}:=p_{B*}\big(\mathcal{L}|_{Z\times B}(dP\times B+(D\cap Z)\times B)\big),

and let
\mathcal{W}:=\mathcal{W}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{2}
, where
p_{B}\colon X\times B\to B
is the projection. Assume
D
is ample enough that the
\mathcal{W}_{i}
be locally free with formation commuting with base change, and the natural maps
\mathcal{V}^{(i)}\to\mathcal{W}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{2}
be injective with locally free quotients.

Let
\rho_{\ell}\colon\mathcal{W}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\to\mathcal{W}_{\ell}
and
\iota_{\ell}\colon\mathcal{W}_{\ell}\to\mathcal{W}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{2}
be the natural maps for
\ell=1,2
.
The conditions
(\mathcal{L};\mathcal{V}^{(i)},i\in\Delta)
satisfies are three. First, the map
\mathcal{V}^{(i)}\to\mathcal{W}_{\ell}
induced by
\rho_{\ell}
has locally free cokernel for each
i\in\Delta
and
\ell=1,2
. It follows that

\mathcal{A}^{(i)}_{\ell}:=\rho_{\ell}(\mathcal{V}^{(i)})
and

\mathcal{B}^{(i)}_{\ell}:=\iota_{\ell}^{-1}(\mathcal{V}^{(i)})

are locally free. Their ranks are specified by
N
, the second condition.
Notice that

\mathcal{B}_{1}^{(i)}\oplus\mathcal{B}_{2}^{(i)}\subseteq\mathcal{V}^{(i)}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(i)}\oplus\mathcal{A}_{2}^{(i)}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{2},

and the induced map

\lambda^{(i)}_{\ell}\colon\frac{\mathcal{V}^{(i)}}{\mathcal{B}_{1}^{(i)}\oplus\mathcal{B}_{2}^{(i)}}\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt\cong\hskip 8.19447pt}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{(i)}}{\mathcal{B}_{\ell}^{(i)}}

is an isomorphism for each
\ell=1,2
. Composing the inverse of
\lambda^{(i)}_{1}
with
\lambda^{(i)}_{2}
gives us an isomorphism

\zeta^{(i)}\colon\frac{\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(i)}}{\mathcal{B}_{1}^{(i)}}\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt\cong\hskip 8.19447pt}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{2}^{(i)}}{\mathcal{B}_{2}^{(i)}}.

Finally,
\mathcal{A}^{(i)}_{2}=\mathcal{B}^{(j)}_{2}
and
\mathcal{A}^{(j)}_{1}=\mathcal{B}^{(i)}_{1}

for each consecutive
i,j\in\Delta
, by the exactness of
N
, the third condition.

For each
i\in\Delta
, let
p_{i}\colon T^{(\delta)}_{i}\times B\to B
and
q_{i}\colon T^{(\delta)}_{i}\times B\to T^{(\delta)}_{i}
be the projections, and let
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(i)}\subseteq p_{i}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(i)}\oplus\mathcal{A}_{2}^{(i)})
be the subsheaf whose quotient is the cokernel of the composition

q_{i}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T^{(\delta)}_{i}}(-1)\bigotimes\frac{p_{i}^{*}\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(i)}}{p_{i}^{*}\mathcal{B}_{1}^{(i)}}\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447ptq_{i}^{*}\xi_{i}\otimes 1\hskip 8.19447pt}\frac{p_{i}^{*}\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(i)}}{p_{i}^{*}\mathcal{B}_{1}^{(i)}}\bigoplus\frac{p_{i}^{*}\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(i)}}{p_{i}^{*}\mathcal{B}_{1}^{(i)}}\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt1\oplus p_{i}^{*}\zeta^{(i)}\hskip 8.19447pt}\frac{p_{i}^{*}\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(i)}}{p_{i}^{*}\mathcal{B}_{1}^{(i)}}\bigoplus\frac{p_{i}^{*}\mathcal{A}_{2}^{(i)}}{p_{i}^{*}\mathcal{B}_{2}^{(i)}},

where
\xi_{i}
is the inclusion
\mathcal{O}_{T^{(\delta)}_{i}}(-1)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{T^{(\delta)}_{i}}^{2}
of the tautological subsheaf associated to an isomorphism
T^{(\delta)}_{i}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k}
such that

(12)

\mathcal{O}_{T^{(\delta)}_{i}}(-1)\big|_{c\ast E_{i}^{(\delta)}}=(c^{-1},1)\mathcal{O}_{T^{(\delta)}_{i}}(-1)\big|_{E_{i}^{(\delta)}}\quad\text{for each }c\in{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}(k).

Then
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(i)}\subseteq p_{i}^{*}\mathcal{W}
with locally free quotient, and satisfies

(13)

\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(i)}\big|_{c\ast E_{i}^{(\delta)}}=(c^{-1},1)p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{V}^{(i)}\big|_{E_{i}^{(\delta)}}\quad\text{for each }c\in{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}(k).

Moreover, there is a subsheaf
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}\subseteq p^{*}\mathcal{W}
with locally free quotient such that
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}|_{T^{(\delta)}_{i}\times B}=\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(i)}
for each
i\in\Delta
, where
p\colon T^{(\delta)}\times B\to B
is the projection, because

\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(i)}|_{N^{(\delta)}_{j}\times B}=\mathcal{B}_{1}^{(i)}\oplus\mathcal{A}_{2}^{(i)}=\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(j)}\oplus\mathcal{B}_{2}^{(j)}=\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(j)}|_{N^{(\delta)}_{j}\times B}

for each consecutive
i,j\in\Delta
.

Finally,
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}\subseteq\widetilde{p}_{*}(\mathcal{L}(\mu^{(\delta)}))
, where
\widetilde{p}\colon X\times T^{(\delta)}\times B\to T^{(\delta)}\times B
is the projection. Indeed,

\widetilde{p}_{*}\big(\mathcal{L}(\mu^{(\delta)})\big)\subseteq\widetilde{p}_{*}\big(\mathcal{L}(D\times B)(\mu^{(\delta)})\big)\subseteq p^{*}\mathcal{W}

with locally free quotients, if
D
is sufficiently ample. Since
\mathcal{V}^{(i)}\subseteq p_{B*}\mathcal{L}^{(i)}
, it follows from (
13
) that

\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}\subseteq\widetilde{p}_{*}(\mathcal{L}(D\times B)(\mu^{(\delta)}))
over a dense open subscheme of
T^{(\delta)}\times B
, and then over the whole scheme. Also, the composition

\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}\longrightarrow\widetilde{p}_{*}\big(\mathcal{L}(D\times B)(\mu^{(\delta)})\big)\longrightarrow\widetilde{p}_{*}\big(\mathcal{L}(D\times B)(\mu^{(\delta)})\big|_{D\times T^{(\delta)}\times B}\big)

is zero because it restricts to zero on a dense open subscheme of
T^{(\delta)}\times B
, thus showing that
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}\subseteq\widetilde{p}_{*}(\mathcal{L}(\mu^{(\delta)}))
.

Thus we get a family of twisted generalized linear series
(\mathcal{L},\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})
of degree
d
and rank
r
on
X
along
\mu^{(\delta)}\times 1_{B}
. From the construction,
(\mathcal{L},\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})
is clearly everywhere invariant. Furthermore, it is everywhere nonconstant because
N
is minimal. So

(\mathcal{L},\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})
is a family of continuous linear series along the family of chain maps
1_{B}\times\mu^{(\delta)}
. It is clear that the association of
(\mathcal{L},\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})
to
(\mathcal{L};\mathcal{V}^{(i)},i\in\Delta)
commutes with base change, thus defining the morphism

\Psi_{\delta}^{N}
in the statement of the theorem.

The action by
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}^{\Delta}
on
G^{r,*}_{d,\delta}(X;N)
changes
(\mathcal{L},\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})
to an equivalent continuous linear series, whence

\Psi_{\delta}^{N}
is
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{m}}^{\Delta}
-invariant. Furthermore, the equivalence class of
(\mathcal{L};\mathcal{V}^{(i)},i\in\Delta)
can be recovered from
(\mathcal{L},\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})
, as

\mathcal{L}(\mu^{(\delta)})|_{X\times E_{i}^{(\delta)}\times B}\cong\mathcal{L}^{(i)}
for each
i\in\Delta
and, under a certain choice of isomorphism,
\mathcal{V}^{(i)}
is the image of
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}|_{E^{(\delta)}_{i}\times B}
. It follows that each nonempty fiber of
\Psi_{\delta}^{N}
is an equivalence class of
level-
\delta
limit linear series, as stated in the theorem.

Finally, let
(L,\mathcal{V})
be a continuous linear series of degree
d
and rank
r
on
X
along a chain map
\mu\colon S\to T
. For each
i=1,\dots,d
, let

\delta_{i}
be the number of irreducible components of
\mu^{-1}(N_{i})
.
Put
\delta:=(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{d})
and
\Delta:=\Delta(\delta)
.
There is a unique isomorphism between the poset of irreducible components of
S
and
\Delta
. For each
i\in\Delta
, let
S_{i}
be the corresponding component. Also, let
N_{j}:=S_{i}\cap S_{j}
for each consecutive
i,j\in\Delta
.

For each
i\in\Delta
, fix a point
E^{\prime}_{i}\in S^{*}_{i}
. Put

V^{(i)}:=\mathcal{V}|_{E^{\prime}_{i}}
and

L^{(i)}:=L(\mu)|_{X\times E^{\prime}_{i}}
. Then

(L^{(i)},V^{(i)})
is a linear series on
X
.

Observe that the
L^{(i)}
form the twist
\delta
-sequence associated
to
L
. Also, let
U_{1}:=\Gamma(Y,L|_{Y})
and

U_{2}:=\Gamma(Z,L|_{Z}(dP))
, and put
U:=U_{1}\oplus U_{2}
. Since
(L,\mathcal{V})
is everywhere invariant,

\mathcal{V}_{N_{j}}=\lim_{c\to\infty}(c^{-1},1)\mathcal{V}|_{E^{\prime}_{i}}=\lim_{c\to 0}(c^{-1},1)\mathcal{V}|_{E^{\prime}_{j}}

as subspaces of

. But Proposition
3.1
yields

\lim_{c\to\infty}(c^{-1},1)\mathcal{V}|_{E^{\prime}_{i}}=\iota_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{V}|_{E^{\prime}_{i}})\oplus\rho_{2}(\mathcal{V}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})\text{\ \ and \ \ }\lim_{c\to 0}(c^{-1},1)\mathcal{V}|_{E^{\prime}_{j}}=\rho_{1}(\mathcal{V}|_{E^{\prime}_{j}})\oplus\iota_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{V}|_{E^{\prime}_{j}}),

where
\iota_{\ell}\colon U_{\ell}\to U

Thus the

\delta

Proposition 6.3
.

\Phi\colon G^{r}_{d}(X)\to G^{r}_{d,\mathbf{1}}(X)

An Infinite Transitivity Theorem
Miles Gould

Abstract
. In this note, we promote an infinite Kadison transitivity theorem on massive C ∗ C^{*} -algebras, including the Calkin algebra. This transitivity stems from the analog of countable degree-1 saturation on pure states which is inherited from these algebras via excision. We show this saturation to be equivalent to several order-theoretic properties on the quantum filter associated to the state, in particular the property of being a quantum P-point. While we show their existence is independent from ZFC, under basic set theoretic assumptions, we produce a plethora of these states. Finally, we find an irreducible representation of the Calkin algebra which fails infinite transitivity.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

The Kadison transitivity theorem is among the most fundamental and classical theorems in
C^{*}
-algebra theory, and is the basis for many results in the representation theory of
C^{*}
-algebras.

Since the advent of continuous model theory in 2008
[
2
]
, its applications in operator algebras have proliferated. In 2017
[
9
]
, it was shown by Farah that, in the theory of irreducible representations of
C^{*}
-algebras, ultraproducts admit an infinite version of the Kadison transitivity theorem. Here we show this infinite transitivity also applies to certain irreducible representations on corona algebras
\mathscr{Q}(A)=\mathscr{M}(A)/A
associated to nonunital
\sigma
-unital
C^{*}
-algebras
A
, in particular, the Calkin algebra
\mathscr{Q}(H)
. These algebras were first shown to have a (not so) weak saturation called countable degree-1 saturation by Farah and Hart in 2011
[
6
]
.

Around the same time, in 2014
[
8
]
, Farah and Weaver formalized the notion of maximal quantum filters, the quantum analog of ultrafilters. These filters assisted in the study of excision, which allows one to extract the value of a pure state
|\varphi(b)|
on an operator
b
using the quantities
\|aba\|
for
a
in the quantum filter associated to
\varphi
. Our version of infinite transitivity relies on a combination of a strong form of excision and countable degree-1 saturation.

1.2.
Outline

We say a representation
\pi:C\rightarrow\mathscr{B}(H)
of a
C^{*}
-algebra
C
admits
infinite transitivity
if for every separable subspace
K
of
H
, the map
C\rightarrow\mathscr{B}(K)
by
c\mapsto P_{K}\pi(c)P_{K}
is onto, where
P_{K}
is the projection onto
K
. This is equivalent to the statement that there is always an operator in
C
sending a given orthonormal sequence to another given bounded sequence in
H
. Since the Calkin algebra is countably degree-1 saturated, it is reasonable to ask: Does every irreducible representation of the Calkin algebra have infinite transitivity? We produce a negative answer.

Theorem 1
.

There exists an irreducible representation of
\mathscr{Q}(H)
which does not admit infinite transitivity.

Of course, the next question is: Does any representation admit infinite transitivity? Under basic set-theoretic assumptions, in particular the continuum hypothesis, we produce a positive answer.

Theorem 2
.

Under the continuum hypothesis, there exists an irreducible representation of
\mathscr{Q}(H)
which admits infinite transitivity.

To accomplish this, we define the countable degree-
1
saturation (def.
1.1
and
2.1
) of a
C^{*}
-algebra with a state,
(C,\varphi)
, and deduce various consequences of it. In particular, for such a structure with pure
\varphi
, the quantum filter (def.
2.2
) associated is a P-filter (def.
2.4
). Thanks to the strong excision granted by these quantum filters, we show that many of these order-theoretic consequences of this saturation are, in fact, equivalent to it (theorem
2.8
). As such, we call these saturated states P-states. Then we show that the GNS-representation associated to a P-state, called a P-representation, has infinite Kadison transitivity.

Theorem 3
.

Let
A
be a nonunital,
\sigma
-unital
C^{*}
-algebra, and
\pi:\mathscr{Q}(A)\rightarrow\mathscr{B}(H)
a P-representation. Then
\pi
admits infinite transitivity. In particular, for every separable subspace
K
of
H
and
T\in\mathscr{B}(K)
self-adjoint (positive, unitary, resp.), there exists
c\in\mathscr{Q}(A)
such that
\|c\|=\|T\|
,
P_{K}\pi(c)P_{K}=T
, and
c
is self-adjoint (positive, unitary, resp.).

This follows from infinite Glimm-Kadison on direct sums of P-representations.

Theorem 4
.

Let
A
be a nonunital,
\sigma
-unital
C^{*}
-algebra, and
\pi:\mathscr{Q}(A)\rightarrow\mathscr{B}(H)
a direct sum of countably many inequivalent P-representations
\pi_{j}:\mathscr{Q}(A)\rightarrow\mathscr{B}(H_{j})
and
K_{j}
a separable subspace of
H_{j}
. Then the map
\mathscr{Q}(A)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{j}\mathscr{B}(K_{j})
by
c\mapsto P_{K}\pi(c)P_{K}
is onto, where
K=\bigoplus_{j}K_{j}
.

As in theorem
3
, we can choose
c
as we like. With the theory complete, under the existence of a P-point, we produce a P-state, on the corona algebra
\mathscr{Q}(A)
of a nonunital
A
with a
\sigma
-unit of projections. However, as implicitly shown in
[
3
]
, there is a forcing in which these states cannot exist.

Finally, we find an irreducible representation of the Calkin algebra which fails infinite transitivity, granting theorem
1
. Compare this situation with that of
[
7
]
, wherein the question of whether the commutant of
\mathscr{B}(H)
in
\mathscr{B}(H)^{\mathscr{U}}
is trivial also depends on the ultrafilter
\mathscr{U}
. In our case, for a diagonal pure state on
\mathscr{Q}(H)
(or equivalently
\mathscr{B}(H)
), whether the GNS-representation admits infinite transitivity depends on the corresponding ultrafilter
\mathscr{U}
. Another recent (and very successful) instance of this ultrafilter-dependence phenomenon was in
[
14
]
.

1.4.
Preliminaries

Throughout,
A
denotes an arbitrary
C^{*}
-algebra and

denotes an arbitrary state on

. However, when dealing with large

-algebras (like

Before understanding saturation of states, we need to understand degree-1 saturation of

-algebras, an extremely powerful property enjoyed by massive corona algebras.

Definition 1.1
.

A

*

P(\bar{x})=\sum_{i\leq m,j\leq n}(a_{ij1}x_{j}a_{ij2}+a_{ij3}x_{j}^{*}a_{ij4})+a_{0},

where
a_{0}\in C
,
a_{ijk}\in\tilde{C}
,

. A

1
condition of

is an expression of the form

\|P(\bar{x})\|=r,

where
P(\bar{x})
is a degree-
1

*
-polynomial and

. A

1
type

is collection of degree-
1
norm conditions, i.e.

t(\bar{x})=\{\|P_{j}(\bar{x})\|=r_{j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\}.

We call

if for every
F\Subset\mathbb{N}
,
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\bar{b}
in

such that
\max_{j\in F}|\|P_{j}(\bar{b})\|-r_{j}|<\epsilon
. Similarly,

is

if there exists
\bar{b}
such that
\|P_{j}(\bar{b})\|=r_{j}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. We say that
C
is

1

if every satisfiable countable degree-
1
type is realized.

The most exotic examples of countably degree-1 norm saturated
C^{*}
-algebras are those whose saturation does not follow from the standard model-theoretic constructions, namely non-ultraproduct corona algebras. To define coronas, we follow
[
10
, 13]
.

Definition 1.2
.

For a nonunital
C^{*}
-algebra

, we define the
strict topology on

A
by convergence
a_{\lambda}\rightarrow a
iff
\lim_{\lambda}\|(a_{\lambda}-a)h\|=\lim_{\lambda}\|h(a_{\lambda}-a)\|=0
for all
h\in A
. We define the
multiplier algebra

\mathscr{M}(A)
as the completion of

in the strict topology, with algebraic operations defined naturally on the Cauchy classes. Note that this is equivalent to the standard definitions of the multiplier algebra.

The
corona algebra

\mathscr{Q}(A)
is the quotient
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathscr{Q}(A)=\mathscr{M}(A)/A
with quotient map
\pi:\mathscr{M}(A)\rightarrow\mathscr{Q}(A)
.

When
A
is nonunital yet
\sigma
-unital, remarkably

is countably degree-1 saturated, and a concise proof can be found in
[
10
, 15.1.5]
.

2.
Saturated States

Throughout this section, we define countably degree-1 saturated states and work towards understanding them.

Definition 2.1
.

A
degree-
1
condition of
(C,\varphi)
is an expression
\rho(P(\bar{x}))=s
, where
P
is a degree-
1

*
-polynomial,
s\in\mathbb{C}
, and
\rho
is either
\|\cdot\|
or
\varphi
. A
countable degree-
1
type
is a countable collection of degree-
1
conditions, i.e.

t(\bar{x})=\{\rho_{j}(P_{j}(\bar{x}))=s_{j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\}.

We define the satisfiability and realizability of
t(\bar{x})
as in
1.1
. We also define the countable degree-1 saturation of
(C,\varphi)
analogously.

In the remainder of this section, we will work towards the remarkable property of pure states which imbues them with this saturation.

2.1.
Quantum Filters

Definition 2.2
.

Given a state
\varphi
on a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
, the
quantum filter
associated to
\varphi
is the collection

\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}=\{a\in A_{+,1}:\varphi(a)=1\},

where
A_{+,1}
are the positive, norm 1 elements of
A
.
The
projection filter
associated to
\varphi
is the collection

\mathscr{P}_{\varphi}=\{p\in\mathrm{Proj}(A):\varphi(p)=1\}.

We equip these collections with the order
\ll
on
A_{+,1}
by

a\ll b\text{ iff }ab=a\text{ (equiv. }ba=a).

Note that, if
p\in\mathrm{Proj}(A)
,
a\in A_{+,1}
, then
p\ll a
iff
p\leq a
, the usual order on
A_{+,1}
.

There is also an abstract definition of a quantum filter, namely a
quantum filter in
A
is a subset
\mathscr{F}\subseteq A_{+,1}
with the property that for any
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathscr{F}
,
\|a_{1}\cdots a_{n}\|=1
. A
maximal quantum filter
is a quantum filter not properly contained in any other.

Caution: Quantum filters are not always filters in the order-theoretic sense. More precisely, they are rarely downwards directed under
\ll
or
\leq
, though interestingly they are always very close
[
10
, 5.3.4]
under both orderings. Thankfully, the quantum filters studied in this note will be true (P-)filters under
\ll
.

The beauty of maximal quantum filters is their natural bijective correspondence with pure states, i.e.
\varphi\mapsto\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}
is a bijection from pure states to maximal quantum filters
[
10
, 5.3.8]
. However, the map
\varphi\mapsto\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}
from states to quantum filters is almost never injective or surjective. For example,
\{1\}
is always a quantum filter, but is only of the form
\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}
when
A=\mathbb{C}
. In fact, the more natural correspondence is between faces of the state space and quantum filters, though there are still sometimes quantum filters not corresponding to a face of the state space.

2.2.
Excision, P-Excision, and Quantum P-points

Quantum filters were first introduced in their relation to excision, a powerful property wherein the value of
|\varphi(b)|
can be approximated by quantities
\|aba\|
for
a\in A_{+,1}
.

Definition 2.3
.

For a state
\varphi
on a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
and subset
\mathscr{F}\subseteq A_{+,1}
,
X\subseteq A
, we say that
\mathscr{F}
excises
\varphi
on
X
if for all
b\in X
,

\inf_{a\in\mathscr{F}}\|aba-\varphi(b)a^{2}\|=0.

Most notably, when
\varphi
is pure,
\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}
excises
\varphi
on the entirety of
A

[
10
, 5.2.1]
. However, for small subsets
X\subseteq A
, we can usually excise
\varphi
using a small portion of
A_{+,1}
. In fact, if
X
is separable, then there is always some countable
\mathscr{F}\subseteq\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}
excising
\varphi
on
X
. This fact was cleverly leveraged in
[
10
, 15.3.8]
to demonstrate that, when
C
is countably degree-1 saturated, for every separable
C^{*}
-subalgebra
B
of
C
, there exists a single
a\in C_{+,1}
excising
\varphi
on
B
. This property is quite remarkable, though it has yet to find applications. This is likely due to the fact that
a
cannot be chosen inside
\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}
, so it is difficult to extract useful information from this single excising element. This is exactly the problem quantum P-points are made to solve, so let us define them now.

Definition 2.4
.

A
P-filter
is a poset
(\mathscr{F},\leq)
which is upwards closed and
\sigma
-downwards directed. A
P-point
is an ultrafilter which is a P-filter under
\subseteq^{*}
, inclusion modulo finite sets. A
quantum P-point
is a maximal quantum filter which is P-filter under
\ll
. That is, for any countable family
\mathscr{F}\subseteq\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}
, there is
a\in\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}
such that
a\ll b
for all
b\in\mathscr{F}
. We call
\varphi
a
P-state
if
\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}
is a quantum P-point. Likewise, we call a representation
\pi
of
C
a
P-representation
if it is equivalent to the GNS-representation corresponding to a P-state.

As mentioned above, the most important quality of quantum P-points is their strong excision property. Namely that for every separable
C^{*}
-subalgebra
B
of
C
,
\varphi
is excised on
B
by a single
a\in\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}
. We call this property
P-excision
. On an intuitive level, it is not hard to see why this would make a serious difference, as
a\in\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}
allows us to claim
|\varphi(b)|\leq\|aba\|
for every
b\in C
, not only on the excised subalgebra. This will be crucial when realizing countable degree-1 types in theorem
2.8
.

2.3.
Equivalences

Let us give some order-theoretic terms relevant to our main equivalence theorem.

Definition 2.5
.

A partially ordered set

If

Fix

By

Let

3.
Infinite Transitivity

The reader may have noticed a concerning gap between the countable degree-1 saturation of
(C,\varphi)
and an infinite Kadison transitivity theorem: Vector equations in
H_{\varphi}
are degree-2 conditions of the form

0=\|xa-b\|^{\varphi}_{2}=\varphi((xa-b)^{*}(xa-b))^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The following lemma shows the true potential of P-states and addresses these concerns.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
C
be countably degree-1 saturated. Then
C
is saturated over countable types consisting of the following conditions

(1)

\|P(\bar{x})\|\in K

(2)

\|P(\bar{x})\|^{\varphi}_{2}\leq r

(3)

\varphi(P(\bar{x}))\in\Lambda

a degree-
1

*
-polynomial,

,

, and
\varphi
a P-state.

Proof.

Let
t(\bar{x})=\{\rho_{j}(P_{j}(\bar{x}))\in K_{j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\}

t(\bar{x})
.

Since
t(\bar{x})
is satisfiable, fix
\bar{b}_{m}
s.t.
\max_{j\leq m}d(\rho_{j}(P_{j}(\bar{b}_{m})),K_{j})\leq\frac{1}{m}
. Then fix an ultrafilter
\mathscr{U}
on
\mathbb{N}
. Let

. Then

is satisfiable type whose realizations realize
t(\bar{x})
. Now for each
\varphi
, choose
a_{\varphi}\in\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}
excising the
C^{*}
-subalgebra
B
generated by coefficients of
P_{j}(\bar{x})
and entries of
\bar{b}_{m}
. Then, for each
j
s.t.
\rho_{j}=\varphi
, choose

\varphi(d_{j})=s_{j}
. Define

P^{\prime}_{j}(\bar{x})=\begin{cases}a_{\varphi}(P_{j}(\bar{x})-d_{j})a_{\varphi},&\rho_{j}=\varphi;\\
P_{j}(\bar{x})a_{\varphi},&\rho_{j}=\|\cdot\|_{2}^{\varphi};\\
P_{j}(\bar{x}),&\rho_{j}=\|\cdot\|;\end{cases}

r_{j}=\begin{cases}0,&\rho_{j}=\varphi;\\
s_{j},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The type

. To see this, we only need to check the case where

\|P_{j}(\bar{b})\|_{2}^{\varphi}=\varphi(P_{j}(\bar{b})^{*}P_{j}(\bar{b}))^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\|a_{\varphi}^{*}P_{j}(\bar{b})^{*}P_{j}(\bar{b})a_{\varphi}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}=\|P_{j}(\bar{b})a_{\varphi}\|=r_{j}\in K_{j}.

In this case,

is of the form
[0,r]
, so
\|P_{j}(\bar{b})\|^{\varphi}_{2}\in K_{j}
.
Therefore, since
t^{\prime\prime}(\bar{x})
is realized, so is

.
∎

We now prove the main results of the paper, infinite Glimm-Kadison for direct sums of P-representations of countably degree-1 saturated

4.
Existence of P-states

The conclusion of theorem
2.8
can be leveraged to show that, in the right model of ZFC, there are no P-states on many corona algebras. However, in any model of ZFC which admits a P-point (an extremely weak assumption), we can produce a P-state on these coronas.

Definition 4.1
.

Given nonunital
A
with a
\sigma
-unit of projections
(e_{n})
, define the map
p:\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{N})\rightarrow\mathrm{Proj}(\mathscr{M}(A))
by
p_{X}
equal to the strict limit of the sequence

p_{X,m}=\sum_{n\in X,n\leq m}f_{n}

where
f_{n}=e_{n}-e_{n-1}
,
e_{-1}=0
. For each
\varphi\in S(\mathscr{Q}(A))
, define

\mathscr{X}_{\varphi}=\{X\subseteq\mathbb{N}:\varphi(\pi(p_{X}))=1\}.

The following lemma was originally proven for the Calkin algebra by Zamora-Aviles
[
13
, 2.5.10]
. Here we provide a version generalized to coronas with
\sigma
-units of projections.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
A
be a nonunital
\sigma
-unital
C^{*}
-algebra and
(e_{n})
a
\sigma
-unit of projections. For any decreasing sequence
X_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and
a\in\mathscr{Q}(A)_{+,1}
,
a\leq\pi(p_{X_{n}})
for all
n
if and only if there exists a pseudointersection
X\subseteq\mathbb{N}
of
(X_{n})
s.t.
a\leq\pi(p_{X})
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, suppose
X_{1}=\mathbb{N}
and
X_{n}\setminus X_{n+1}
infinite. Enumerate
X_{n}\setminus X_{n+1}=\{j^{n}_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Now define
p^{n}:\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{N})\rightarrow\mathrm{Proj}(\mathscr{M}(A))
by
p^{n}_{Y}=p_{\{j^{n}_{i}:i\in Y\}}
. Since
a=\pi(\tilde{a})\leq\pi(p_{X_{n}})
, we have

\pi(p_{X_{n}\setminus X_{n+1}}\tilde{a})=(\pi(p_{X_{n}})-\pi(p_{X_{n+1}}))a=a-a=0,

so
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|(1-e_{k})p_{X_{n}\setminus X_{n+1}}\tilde{a}\|=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|p^{n}_{\mathbb{N}\setminus k}\tilde{a}\|=0,

so the function
f_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
by

f_{\epsilon}(n)=\min\Big\{k:\|p^{n}_{\mathbb{N}\setminus k}\tilde{a}\|<\frac{\epsilon}{2^{n+1}}\Big\}

is well-defined. Fix
f\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
s.t.
f_{\frac{1}{m}}\leq^{*}f
for all
m
. That is,
f_{\frac{1}{m}}(n)\leq f(n)
for e.a.
n
. Now we claim that
X=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\{j^{n}_{i}:i\leq f(n)\}

is our desired pseudointersection. Since
X_{n}
decreases from
\mathbb{N}
,
X_{n}\setminus X_{n+1}
partitions
\mathbb{N}
, so

X\setminus X_{n}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{n-1}X\cap(X_{j}\setminus X_{j+1})=\bigcup_{j=1}^{n-1}\{j^{n}_{i}:i\leq f(n)\},

so

|X\setminus X_{n}|=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(f(j)+1)<\infty,

thus
X
is a pseudointersection. Now we claim
a\leq\pi(p_{X})
, and it suffices to show that
\|(1-e_{k})p_{\mathbb{N}\setminus X}\tilde{a}\|\rightarrow 0
. By
f_{\frac{1}{m}}\leq^{*}f
, we have

\displaystyle\|(1-e_{k})p_{\mathbb{N}\setminus X}\tilde{a}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|(1-e_{k})p^{n}_{\mathbb{N}\setminus f(n)}\tilde{a}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\|(1-e_{k})p^{n}_{\mathbb{N}\setminus f(n)}\tilde{a}\|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m2^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\|(1-e_{k})p^{n}_{\mathbb{N}\setminus f(n)}\tilde{a}\|+\frac{1}{m2^{N}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\|(1-e_{k})p_{X_{n}\setminus X_{n+1}}\tilde{a}\|+\frac{1}{m2^{N}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{m}\leq\frac{2}{m}

by taking
k
so that
\|(1-e_{k})p_{X_{n}\setminus X_{n+1}}\tilde{a}\|\leq\frac{1}{(N-1)m}
for all
n<N
.
∎

The following theorem gives us a method for finding P-states.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
A
be a nonunital
\sigma
-unital
C^{*}
-algebra. For every
\sigma
-unit of projections
(e_{n})
of
A
, there exists
\varphi:\beta\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}\hookrightarrow P(\mathscr{Q}(A))
such that
\mathscr{X}_{\varphi}=\mathscr{U}
and
\varphi_{\mathscr{U}}
is a P-state if and only if
\mathscr{U}
is a P-point.

Proof.

We begin by fixing
\varphi_{n}\in P(A)
s.t.
\varphi_{n}(f_{n})=1
. Since
(e_{n})
strictly increasing and consists of projections,
\|f_{n}\|=1
so such a state always exists. By
[
12
, 3.3.9]
, these each have unique extensions
\tilde{\varphi_{n}}\in P(\mathscr{M}(A))
. Now define
\varphi:\beta\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}\hookrightarrow S(\mathscr{Q}(A))
by

\varphi_{\mathscr{U}}(\pi(a))=\lim_{n\rightarrow\mathscr{U}}\tilde{\varphi_{n}}(a).

We know these are states because they lift to weak
∗
-limits of pure states on
\mathscr{M}(A)
and they are pure by
[
1
, 1]
.

By theorem
2.8
, it suffices to show that
\mathscr{F}_{\varphi_{\mathscr{U}}}
is a quantum P-point iff
\mathscr{U}
is a P-point. Suppose that
\mathscr{U}
is a P-point and fix a sequence
(a_{n})
in
\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}
with respective lifts
\tilde{a_{n}}
. For each
m,n
, define

X_{m,n}=\Big\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\tilde{\varphi_{k}}(\tilde{a_{n}})>1-\frac{1}{m}\Big\}.

By construction,
X_{m,n}\in\mathscr{U}
. Since
\mathscr{U}
is a P-point, there exists
X\in\mathscr{U}
s.t. for all
m,n
,
X\setminus X_{m,n}
finite. Enumerate
X=\{k_{j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\}
so that for all
m,n
,
\tilde{\varphi_{k_{j}}}(\tilde{a_{n}})\geq 1-\frac{1}{m}
for eventually all
j
. Therefore
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{\varphi_{k_{j}}}(\tilde{a_{n}})=1
. Our claim is that
a=\pi(p_{X})
is a lower bound for
(a_{n})
. Since
X\in\mathscr{U}
,
\varphi_{\mathscr{U}}(\pi(p_{X}))=1
, so
a\in\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}
. Now notice that

\|\pi((\tilde{a_{n}}-1)p_{Y})\|=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|(\tilde{a_{n}}-1)p_{X}(1-e_{k})\|=\|(\tilde{a_{n}}-1)(p_{X}-p_{X})\|=0,

since
p_{X}e_{n}\rightarrow p_{X}
strictly. Therefore

a_{n}a-a=\pi(\tilde{a_{n}}p_{X}-p_{X})=\pi((\tilde{a_{n}}-1)p_{X})=0,

i.e.
a\ll a_{n}
for all
n
.

Now suppose
\mathscr{U}
is not a P-point, and fix decreasing
X_{n}\in\mathscr{U}
with no pseudointersection in
\mathscr{U}
. Our claim is that
\pi(p_{X_{n}})
has no lower bound in
\mathscr{P}_{\varphi_{\mathscr{U}}}
. By
4.2
, any lower bound
a\in\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}
of
(\pi(p_{X_{n}}))
must in fact have

, where
X
is a pseudointersection of
(X_{n})
, therefore no such

exists.
∎

The existence of a P-point is extremely common in models of ZFC. In fact, until recently
[
4
]
, there were very few known models of ZFC which have no P-points. Their existence is implied by the continuum hypothesis, is consistent with various standard forcing axioms, and even

Lemma 4.4
.

Let

be a nonunital

-algebra with a
\sigma
-unit of projections, and

(k_{n})

\mathbb{N}

We mimic the proof of
[

and let

. Then

Using the obvious type, there exists

. Then by

, there is a pseudointersection

of
(X_{n})

. It then follows that

4.1.
Counterexample to Infinite Transitivity

The question of whether infinite transitivity in
(\mathscr{Q}(H),\pi_{\varphi})
implies degree-1 saturation of
(\mathscr{Q}(H),\varphi)
is fairly opaque. In this section, we at least prove that in ZFC, many massive coronas have an irreducible representation which does not admit infinite transitivity.

Definition 4.6
.

For
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
,

, we define
X+j=\{m+j:m\in X\}
and for an ultrafilter
\mathscr{U}
on
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathscr{U}+j=\{X+j:X\in\mathscr{U}\}
. Since
\beta\mathbb{N}
is compact, we can define
\mathscr{U}+\mathscr{V}=\lim_{j\rightarrow\mathscr{V}}(\mathscr{U}+j)
. We call a nonprincipal ultrafilter
\mathscr{U}

if
\mathscr{U}+\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{U}
.

By
[
5
, 1]
, every compact topological space with a semigroup structure whose left operation is continuous admits an idempotent element. The addition defined in
4.6
plainly grants this semigroup structure on
\beta\mathbb{N}
, so there is
\mathscr{U}\in\beta\mathbb{N}
idempotent, and this cannot be principal.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Since we already proved the existence of
P
-states on
\mathscr{Q}(H)
under the continuum hypothesis, it suffices to show there is an irreducible representation of
\mathscr{Q}(H)
which fails infinite transitivity. In fact, such a representation exists in ZFC.

Let
\mathscr{U}
be an idempotent ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
. Let

be the right shift along an orthonormal basis
(\eta_{n})
of
H
and
\varphi_{\mathscr{U}}
as in

along
(\eta_{n})
. Let
\xi_{j}=\pi(s^{j})+L_{\varphi_{\mathscr{U}}}
. Then the equations

\pi_{\varphi}(a)\xi_{j}=\delta_{j0}\xi_{j}

imply

\varphi(\pi(s^{j})^{*}a\pi(s^{j}))=\lim_{n\rightarrow\mathscr{U}}a_{n-j}=\delta_{j0}

where
a_{n}=\langle a\eta_{n},\eta_{n}\rangle
. Thus
X_{1}=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:|a_{n}-1|<\epsilon\}\in\mathscr{U}
and for all
j>1
,
X_{j}=\{n\geq j:|a_{n-j}|<\epsilon\}\in\mathscr{U}
. However,
X_{j}=(X_{1}+j)\setminus j
, so by idempotency,
\{j\in\mathbb{N}:X_{1}+j\in\mathscr{U}\}\in\mathscr{U}
, so
\varphi(\pi(s^{j})^{*}a\pi(s^{j}))=1
for some
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, there is no
a\in\mathscr{Q}(H)
s.t.
\pi_{\varphi}(a)\xi_{j}=\delta_{j0}\xi_{j}
, so infinite transitivity fails.
∎

This tells us that infinite transitivity is not a property intrinsic to all irreducible representations of countably degree-1 saturated
C^{*}
-algebras, so the infinite transitivity of P-representations comes from the nontrivial interplay between the state and the algebra.

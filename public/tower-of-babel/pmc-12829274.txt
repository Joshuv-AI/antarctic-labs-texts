ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNeovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) is the leading cause of blindness in the Western world, with an estimated 40,000 newly diagnosed cases in the UK every year. Early detection and treatment of nAMD are important to achieve good visual acuity and quality-of-life (QoL) outcomes. Whilst early treatment of the second eye, following detection using optical coherence tomography (OCT) imaging, has shown maintenance of visual acuity and health-related QoL and reduced health and social care costs, there is a paucity of information about the benefits of early detection of nAMD in the first eye. In particular, although it is reasonable to consider a form of targeted screening of those most likely to develop nAMD, it is unclear if those invited would adhere to such a programme.MethodsIn this randomized controlled trial, 130 participants will be recruited from community-based eye healthcare practices within the UK whilst attending their routine appointment. Participants will be randomized to either a standard care arm to attend a further follow-up appointment at 12 months, or an intervention arm, attending every 3 months for assessment. The end of study is at 12 months. All participants will complete the NEI-VFQ-25, MacDQoL, PHQ9 and EQ-5D-3L questionnaires at baseline and 12 months. In addition, all participants and recruiting investigators will complete the Theoretical Framework of Acceptability questionnaire at 12 months.DiscussionThe DORADO study aims to assess the persistence and adherence to and acceptability of a community-based programme for the early detection of nAMD.Trial registration ISRCTN10005321. Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

Age-related macular degeneration (AMD) is the leading cause of vision loss and blindness in the UK, affecting 700,000 older adults [1]. The risk of AMD increases with age, with a prevalence of 2.4% in people aged over 50 years, increasing to 12.2% for those aged over 80 years [1].

Of the two types of AMD, the more prevalent variant is dry AMD, which causes a loss of central vision usually over many years. The more severe form is neovascular AMD (nAMD), which usually leads to a rapid progressive loss of central vision over weeks and months [1]. Despite a distinct underlying pathogenesis, dry AMD typically precedes nAMD and, over time, is associated with an increasing risk of progression to nAMD [2]. Around 1 in 15 people with dry AMD progress to nAMD.

In the UK, over 100 people develop nAMD every day at an average age of 79 years, with 25% developing nAMD in their other “second” eye within 3 years of “first” eye diagnosis [3]. The prevalence of AMD in the UK, including nAMD, is projected to double, affecting an average of 1.23 million individuals by 2050 [4].

Within 3 years, more than half of those affected by nAMD will have severe visual impairment or blindness if left untreated [1], with over a quarter of people experiencing a clinically significant loss of vision (defined as three lines or more on a standard optometrist’s eye chart) within 1 year [5]. Due to the rapidly progressive nature of nAMD, early detection and treatment are important to maintain visual acuity [6]. Effective intravitreal treatments are available for nAMD via regular injection into the affected eye, which successfully stabilize vision [7, 8].

The Early Detection of Neovascular Age-related Macular Degeneration (EDNA) study [9] revealed that an optical coherence tomography (OCT) scan of the macula is a superior method of detecting nAMD in the second eye, compared with self-reported vision, clinic-measured visual acuity or distortion of lines on a self-administered paper-based Amsler grid. The follow-on study to this, the Observing Fibrosis, Macular Atrophy, and Subretinal Highly Reflective Material, Before and After Treatment (FASBAT) study, showed that early treatment of these second eyes led to better visual acuity and health-related outcomes compared with the first eye at 2 years [10]. The use of an OCT scan in detecting nAMD early in the second eye also has health economic benefits over the lifetime of the patient [11]. There is, however, a paucity of information about the benefit of detecting nAMD early in the first eye, in particular with a targeted screening and monitoring programme. The current standard care monitoring advice is for those with a diagnosis of dry AMD to attend an optometrist typically on an annual basis, which may or may not involve an OCT scan. It is currently unknown whether individuals would adhere to a more frequent monitoring programme.

The aim of the DORADO study is to assess the persistence and adherence to and acceptability of a community-based OCT monitoring programme with 3-monthly attendance for the early detection of nAMD.

Objectives {7}

The aims of this study are (1) to assess the persistence and adherence of participants and acceptance by both participants and practitioners to an early detection monitoring programme, (2) to assess the differences in these outcomes between standard care (12 monthly) and more frequent monitoring (3 monthly) of AMD and (3) to assess the changes in visual acuity and quality-of-life (QoL) differences between these groups.

Trial design {8}

The early Detection Of neovascular age-Related mAcular Degeneration in the cOmmunity (DORADO) study is a randomized controlled trial assessing the differences in the persistence and adherence to, and acceptability of, participants randomized to receive either standard care (12 monthly) or enhanced monitoring (3 monthly) of AMD.

The DORADO study will be conducted in accordance with the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials statement (SPIRIT) with approval granted by the NHS Health Research Authority and Research Ethics Committee (IRAS: 331617).

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

The comparator is a 12-monthly frequency of monitoring patients with AMD. This has been chosen as this is the current recommendation and therefore standard care [12–14].

At present, NICE and the Royal College of Optometrists do not mandate the use of OCT to scan the macula. However, this has been chosen because its diagnostic accuracy is far superior to self-reported vision, clinic-measured visual acuity or distortion of lines on a self-administered paper-based Amsler grid [9]. OCT is becoming widely available in community optometry practices [15].

Intervention description {11a}

The intervention is a 3-monthly OCT-based monitoring programme and otherwise is the same as the 12-monthly comparator (see Fig. 1/Table 1).

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions {11b}

During the course of the study, any participant who develops nAMD either through diagnosis at study visits or self-reporting of symptoms will undergo routine NHS referral for onward management of the nAMD. Participants will, however, be asked to continue with their study visits up to the end of study at 12 months. Participants are free to withdraw from the study if they should choose, and they will be returned to standard clinical practice.

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}

Adherence is a key endpoint in the DORADO study. Therefore, a standard operating procedure will be applied to all participants irrespective of the arm within the study if they should not attend. This standard operating procedure was developed with community optometry input.

In such circumstances, all participants, or their nominated carer, will be contacted twice to arrange the follow-up visits. These visits are permitted to take place within 2 weeks of the due date to allow flexibility. If contact is unsuccessful, attendance at the visit will be noted as missed. The participant will be contacted again following the same procedure to arrange the next appointment as planned, where applicable.

All participants will consent to receive reminders for each appointment. These notifications will be in the form of postal letter and/or a text reminder, as is the current community method.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d}

All participants will be given standard clinical practice advice about managing their AMD. In particular, participants are advised to self-monitor weekly with a paper-based Amsler grid to monitor for signs of nAMD such as distortion of the grid.

Should any participant self-refer to the recruiting site due to changes in their vision, the study clinical record form (CRF) will be documented with the outcome. Any participant referred to a hospital eye service due to changes in their vision and potential development of nAMD will remain in the study and the CRF will be documented.

Provisions for posttrial care {30}

Following the end of study, all participants will be returned to standard clinical practice, either returning to standard community optometry or hospital eye services.

Participant timeline {13}

All participants will undergo a visual acuity assessment (either Early Treatment of Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) letter score or Snellen visual acuity), an OCT and colour fundus examination at baseline and 12 months (Fig. 1/Table 1). Only those participants in the enhanced OCT arm will repeat these assessments on a 3-monthly basis.

The National Eye Institute visual functioning questionnaire (NEI-VFQ-25), Macular Disease quality of life (MacDQoL), Public Health Questionnaire (PHQ9) and EuroQoL (EQ-5D-3L) questionnaires will be offered to all participants to complete either during the baseline visit or to complete over the phone at a time convenient to them. Any incomplete questionnaires will be followed up with a phone call. All questionnaires will be completed by all participants at 12 months. In addition, all participants and recruiting staff will complete the Theoretical Framework of Acceptability (TFA) questionnaire at 12 months.

The end of study is at 12 months following recruitment.

Sample size {14}

This trial is exploratory, and a power calculation has not been performed to determine the sample size and expected effect size.

All participating recruiting sites were asked to estimate the number of potentially eligible participants seen each week. This number was used to calculate the minimum sample size of 130 participants to be recruited within 9 months. To account for 30% withdrawal of consent, inconclusive imaging and missing data, we would aim to recruit a total of 169 participants. All participants will provide informed consent for their anonymized data to be used in this trial.

Within the first 3 months of the recruitment period, we will assess whether we can overrecruit to the trial during the remaining period. If not, the trial will not be stopped as we will ensure to recruit the target of 130 participants.

Recruitment {15}

Recruitment to the DORADO study is competitive between the six community sites open to recruitment. There are also five participant identification centres open to identify and refer potential participants to the nearest recruitment site for screening.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

Participants will undergo randomization 1:1 to either the 12-monthly standard care arm or the 3-monthly enhanced OCT arm. There are no blocking or stratification factors linked to the randomization. Randomization is provided by the online service Sealed Envelope (sealedenvelope.com) and completed by the trial manager at the point of recruitment.

Concealment mechanism {16b}

Randomization is provided by the online service Sealed Envelope, and, as such, the allocations are concealed to the individual completing the randomization.

Implementation {16c}

At the point of recruitment, the recruiting site will phone or email the trial manager to complete the randomization. Randomization is completed online, and the allocation was confirmed with the participating site and the participant. Confirmation of the randomization outcome will also be sent via email to the recruitment site.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {17a}

In the trial and the analysis in unblinded as in this pragmatic community-delivered design, it is challenging to mask the investigators to those participants who are visiting for observational assessments every 3 months and those attending at 12 months. However, we will acknowledge the inherent bias of the investigator measuring the visual acuity in the two trial arms, which are unmasked, in the trial outputs.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

Not applicable.

Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {18a}

All participants will complete the following standardized assessments at the point of recruitment, providing a baseline measure: spectral-domain OCT (SD-OCT), colour fundus photography, visual acuity and QoL and wellbeing questionnaires (detailed below). These same assessments will be completed by all participants at 12 months, with the addition of the TFA questionnaire.

For those participants randomized to the intervention arm, additional standardized assessments will be completed at 3-monthly intervals up to the study end (Fig. 1/Table 1).

The EQ-5D-3L, NEI-VFQ-25, MacDQoL and PHQ9 questionnaires are to be completed by all participants at baseline and 12 months. Acceptability of the screening programme will be assessed using the TFA questionnaire at 12 months.

Trial data will be recorded on paper-based CRFs approved by the sponsor. Responses to questionnaires will be recorded on paper and shared with the research team. All paper-based responses will be transferred to eCRFs by the research team. All imaging data will be anonymously saved and shared electronically with the research team.

Plans to promote participant retention and complete follow-up {18b}

Retention is a key endpoint in the DORADO study. Therefore, as described in the “Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}”, the standard operating procedure for contacting participants who do attend will be applied to all participants irrespective of the arm within the study if they should not attend. This standard operating procedure was developed with community optometry input.

Data management {19}

All trial data will be collected by the research staff at the respective participating sites on paper CRFs. The person entering the data will sign and date the document with the PI or a delegated person counter signing for the accuracy of the data. This will be checked for completeness by the trial manager, who will also act as the data manager. Data input into SPSS will be performed by a member of the trial research team.

All members of the research team will comply with the Data Protection Act 2018 with regard to data collection, storage, processing and disclosure of personal information. The Act’s core principles will be upheld at all times. The chief investigator (R. P. G.) will act as the data custodian. Access to this data is limited only to delegated individuals within the research team.

Data will be pseudonymized by way of assigning an individual study ID to each set of individual data. This ID will be used on all documents and correspondence, rather than any identifiable information, meaning if and when data is shared with coinvestigators or sponsors, it will already be pseudonymized. Data held within the database will be coded to assist with data analysis. All study documents and data will be kept in a secure, password-protected environment at the University of York. A monitoring plan will be put in place by the research team, with on-site monitoring visits scheduled to include completion of the CRFs, data management and data cleansing.

This dataset will be stored securely for as long as the dataset is useful so that adequate work may be performed on the data to enable meaningful conclusions to be reached and for it to act as a repository for future research.

Confidentiality {27}

All study documents and data will be kept in a secure, password-protected file at the University of York. This will only be accessible by members of the research team.

Methods in analysis to handle protocol non-adherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data {20c}

Protocol non-adherence is an important outcome to determine persistence and adherence and will be assessed as outlined in “ Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}”. For further analysis, e.g. visual acuity and descriptive analysis of OCT biomarkers, a last observation carried forward (LOCF) method will be used for those participants who discontinue. A combined mean value interpolation/LOCF method will be used to handle missing data between points. Regardless of time interval, the mean of the LOCF before the missing data and the mean after will be used as the missing value.

Oversight and monitoring

Composition of the coordinating centre and trial steering committee {5d}

The named research team (see authorship corresponding to initials), including the CI (R. P. G.), PIs and clinical advisors (A. A. and A. A. S.) and trial manager (R. L. H.), will be running the trial day to day and will have a scheduled weekly trial management meeting.

The TSC includes a clinical chairperson, community optometrist representative, a public and patient involvement and engagement (PPIE) representative, the study sponsor, the CI and trial manager.

Composition of the data monitoring committee and its role and reporting structure {21a}

Although the DORADO study is deemed a low-risk intervention study and as such, a data monitoring committee is not essential, and a data monitoring committee is planned to meet three times during the study.

Adverse event reporting and harms {22}

The study intends to capture the adverse events of developing nAMD or any events associated with the study procedures, for example capturing the colour fundus photographs or OCT images. Under the rare circumstances of an adverse event linked to the optional pupillary dilation as per routine care, e.g. acute glaucoma, or additional adverse events occurring whilst completing the study assessments, e.g. imaging, visual acuity assessment and questionnaires, these will be reported in line with MedDRA definitions. Adverse events will be grouped and reported in trial publications and discussed at the TSC.

Frequency and plans for auditing trial conduct {23}

The conduct of the trial is discussed weekly at the trial management meeting and is reviewed by the TSC. Any issues and deviations are promptly documented in a file note, discussed with the trial sponsor and reviewed at the next TSC meeting.

Plans for communicating important protocol amendments to relevant parties (e.g. trial participants and ethical committees) {25}

All proposed protocol amendments will be communicated to the sponsor and funder prior to requesting amended ethical approval. Once ethical approval is obtained, all recruitment sites will be notified of the changes made, and training on the new protocol will be documented.

Dissemination plans {31a}

Outcomes will be disseminated via peer-reviewed academic journals, via presentation at national and international conferences and via the research teams’ social media outlets. A lay summary will also be provided to study participants.

Discussion

In the UK, over 100 people develop nAMD daily, at an average age of 79 years [3]. If left untreated, more than half of those affected by nAMD will have severe visual impairment or blindness within 3 years [1]. As such, early detection and treatment are important to maintain visual acuity [6].

There are currently no published guidelines supporting the benefit of early detection of nAMD in the first eye of high-risk individuals, such as those with dry AMD, nor is there any routine screening in place to identify individuals at risk of developing nAMD. Current advice [1, 12–15] is for all individuals aged over 16 years to attend a routine eye test every 2 years, with eye tests paid for by public funds for those aged over 60 years [14]. However, in addition to this, those diagnosed with dry AMD can attend more frequent optoemtrist visits, but they often do not, often have to pay and may not be assessed with the best technology.

It is possible that a targeted screening programme for people with dry AMD who are at most risk of developing nAMD may improve visual acuity and QoL outcomes due to the early detection of treatable nAMD. Evidence synthesis is required to support this assumption, in particular the acceptability of the tests involved and frequency of attendance. Nonetheless, it is essential to understand the persistence and adherence rates to enable the development of a health economic model for a potential AMD-targeted screening programme.

An example of a very successful ocular screening programme in the UK is the Diabetes Eye Screening Programme (DESP, 16). Since its inception, the DESP has contributed to significant reductions in severe vision loss associated with diabetic retinopathy, from 5.5% (1334 certified cases) in 299/2010 to 3.5% (840 certified cases) in 2018/2019. Although the DESP has a different target population who are typically within the working age group, of the 2.70 million people offered screening in 2017/2018, 2.23 million (82.7%) people were screened [16]. Based upon a hypothetical uptake of 80% (similar to the DESP), it is plausible that 32,000 individuals diagnosed per year with nAMD could benefit from earlier diagnosis and preserved vision and QoL.

Simultaneous screening of AMD during a diabetic screening programme has been shown to be highly cost-effective in populations based in Hong Kong [17] and rural China [18]. An artificial intelligence-based automated combined screening platform developed in the USA has also reported excellent performance in the prospective early diagnosis of AMD and diabetic retinopathy [19].

Therefore, the DORADO study aims to assess the persistence and adherence to and acceptability of a community-based OCT monitoring programme with 3-monthly attendance for the early detection of nAMD.

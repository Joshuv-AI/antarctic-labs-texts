Airworthiness Directives; The Boeing Company Airplanes

AGENCY: Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), DOT.

ACTION: Final rule.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: The FAA is adopting a new airworthiness directive (AD) for 
certain The Boeing Company Model 757-200 and -300 series airplanes. 
This AD was prompted by a report that some passenger service unit 
(PSU)-mounted video monitors became detached from the PSU rails during 
a hard landing. This AD requires replacing PSU-mounted video monitors 
that do not have secondary retention lanyards. The FAA is issuing this 
AD to address the unsafe condition on these products.

DATES: This AD is effective December 1, 2026. The Director of the 
Federal Register approved the incorporation by reference of certain 
publications listed in this AD as of December 1, 2026.

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION:

Background

    The FAA issued a notice of proposed rulemaking (NPRM) to amend 14 
CFR part 39 by adding an AD that would apply to certain The Boeing 
Company Model 757-200 and -300 series airplanes. The NPRM was published 
in the Federal Register on January 17, 2025 (90 FR 5754). The NPRM was 
prompted by a report that some PSU-mounted video monitors became 
detached from the PSU rails during a hard landing that did not exceed 
the emergency load requirements for the PSUs. In the NPRM, the FAA 
proposed to require replacing PSU-mounted video monitors that do not 
have secondary retention lanyards. The FAA is issuing this AD to 
address PSU-mounted video monitors that could become fully detached or 
be held at an unsafe height, which, if not addressed, could result in 
injury to passengers and impede passenger and crew egress during 
emergency evacuation.

Discussion of Final Airworthiness Directive

Comments

    The FAA received a comment from one commenter, an individual, who 
supported the NPRM without change.
    The FAA received additional comments from four commenters, 
including Aviation Partners Boeing, Delta Air Lines (Delta), Panasonic 
Avionics Corporation (Panasonic), and Precision Conversions, LLC 
(Precision). The following presents the comments received on the NPRM 
and the FAA's response to each comment.

Request To Clarify Method for Determining Applicability

    Delta requested that the FAA clarify that they can accomplish 
either a physical inspection for the existence of PSU-mounted video 
monitors or accomplish a review of Delta's PSU Layouts (Delta's PSU 
drawing configuration) to determine if this AD applies.
    The FAA agrees to clarify that operators can determine if their 
airplanes are equipped with PSU-mounted video monitors by performing a 
physical inspection or a records check, provided the presence of PSU-
mounted video monitors can be conclusively determined from that review; 
either method is acceptable to determine if this AD applies. The FAA 
did not change this AD in response to this comment.

Request To Revise Applicability

    Precision requested that the FAA revise the applicability of the 
proposed AD to exclude Boeing Model 757-200 series airplanes that have 
been converted from passenger to freighter in accordance with a certain 
supplemental type certificate (STC). Precision STC ST01529SE, which 
converts Model 757-200 series airplanes from passenger to freighter 
airplanes, removes all PSU-mounted video monitors to convert the main 
deck into a cargo compartment, thus eliminating the unsafe condition 
referenced in the NPRM.
    The FAA acknowledges that this AD does not apply to Model 757-200 
airplanes converted to freighter in accordance with STC ST01529SE. This 
AD applies to Model 757-200 and -300 series airplanes equipped with 
PSU-mounted video monitors. Since Model 757-200 series airplanes 
converted to freighter in accordance with STC ST01529SE no longer have 
PSU-mounted video monitors installed, this AD does not apply. The FAA 
did not change this AD in response to this comment.

Effect of Winglets on Accomplishment of the Proposed Actions

    Aviation Partners Boeing stated that the installation of blended or 
scimitar blended winglets per STC ST01518SE does not affect compliance 
with the mandated actions in the proposed rule.
    The FAA agrees with the commenter that STC ST01518SE does not 
affect compliance with this AD. Therefore, the installation of STC 
ST01518SE does not affect the ability to accomplish the actions 
required by this AD. The FAA did not change this AD in response to this 
comment.

[[Page 67443]]

Request To Revise Paragraph (e) To Clarify Safety Concerns

    Panasonic requested that the FAA revise paragraph (e) of the 
proposed AD to include the statement, ``Although some models of PSU-
mounted video monitors have not become detached under such landings, we 
have determined that this AD is warranted due to relative motion 
between the PSUs rails that drive forces exceeding the approved 
emergency landing conditions for the attached PSU-mounted monitors.'' 
Panasonic asserted that the improvement action for Panasonic monitors 
involves an enhancement to its fail-safe design and is not a result of 
correcting any defect or noncompliance in the PSU-mounted monitor 
assemblies themselves. Panasonic requested this change to avoid 
potentially misleading operators that Panasonic monitors have become 
detached because they did not meet the existing emergency landing 
requirements they were designed to meet.
    The FAA acknowledges the commenter's concern. The FAA is unaware of 
Panasonic monitors that have become detached during hard landings that 
did not exceed emergency landing requirements. However, paragraph (e) 
of this AD specifies that some, not all, PSU-mounted video monitors 
became detached. The FAA did not change this AD in response to this 
comment.

Requests To Include Panasonic Service Bulletins as an Accepted Method 
of Compliance

    Delta requested that the FAA allow the use of Panasonic Service 
Bulletin RDAA902940-01-44-07 Revision 5, dated December 5, 2024, or 
RDAA902941-01-44-07 Revision 6, dated December 13, 2024, to meet the 
requirements of the proposed AD. Delta stated that the affected part 
numbers (P/Ns) installed on their fleet are P/N RD-AA902940-01 and P/N 
RD-AA902941-01, which are addressed by the Panasonic service bulletins 
noted, and requested the FAA add these service bulletins as a method of 
compliance for the requirements of the proposed AD.
    Panasonic requested that the FAA include Panasonic Service Bulletin 
SB141908-44-002 as an accepted corrective action for the proposed AD 
but did not provide the specified service bulletin to the FAA. 
Panasonic asserted that the effectivity of Boeing Special Attention 
Requirements Bulletin 757-25-0321 RB does not include airplanes with 
STC alterations that would be affected by the NPRM and suggested that 
adding this service bulletin as an accepted method of compliance would 
reduce the FAA's efforts to support alternative methods of compliance 
(AMOC) requests to address airplanes installed with FAA STC ST03183NY.
    The FAA disagrees. Paragraph (g)(3) of this AD already requires 
airplanes not identified in the effectivity paragraph of either Boeing 
service bulletin to perform the required actions with an approved AMOC. 
At this time, the FAA does not have enough data to approve Panasonic 
Service Bulletin RDAA902940-01-44-07 Revision 5, dated December 5, 
2024, RDAA902941-01-44-07 Revision 6, dated December 13, 2024, or 
Panasonic Service Bulletin SB141908-44-002 as an accepted method of 
compliance for this AD. The FAA did not change this AD in response to 
these comments.

Additional Changes to the Final Rule

    Since the FAA issued the NPRM, Boeing published Boeing Special 
Attention Requirements Bulletins 757-25-0320 RB, Revision 1, and 757-
25-0321 RB, Revision 1, both dated August 6, 2025. The revised service 
information specifies an additional inspection of the inboard PSU rail 
to determine rail lightening hole configuration and applicable on-
condition actions, because the original issue could not be complied 
with to address the unsafe condition. The revised service information 
adds instructions for installing lanyard retention brackets at 
locations that do not have 1.0-inch diameter holes in the PSU rail to 
support secondary retention lanyard installation and at locations that 
have temperature sensors installed in the inboard PSU rail area, where 
the existing holes do not allow for correct lanyard installation.
    The FAA has revised this AD to mandate Boeing Special Attention 
Requirements Bulletin 757-25-0320 RB, Revision 1, dated August 6, 2025, 
and Boeing Special Attention Requirements Bulletin 757-25-0321 RB, 
Revision 1, dated August 6, 2025. The FAA has revised paragraph (i) of 
this AD to add credit for previous actions for airplanes that completed 
Boeing Special Attention Requirements Bulletin 757-25-0320 RB, dated 
January 19, 2024, and redesignated subsequent paragraphs accordingly.
    The revised service information also provides updated cost 
information. The FAA has revised the cost estimate in this final rule 
to reflect the updated cost information.

Conclusion

    The FAA reviewed the relevant data, considered any comments 
received, and determined that air safety requires adopting this AD as 
proposed. Accordingly, the FAA is issuing this AD to address the unsafe 
condition on these products. Except for minor editorial changes, and 
any other changes described previously, this AD is adopted as proposed 
in the NPRM.

Material Incorporated by Reference Under 1 CFR Part 51

    The FAA reviewed Boeing Special Attention Requirements Bulletins 
757-25-0320 RB, Revision 1, and 757-25-0321 RB, Revision 1, both dated 
August 6, 2025. This material specifies procedures for replacing all 
PSU-mounted video monitors that do not have secondary retention 
lanyards with PSU-mounted video monitors that have secondary retention 
lanyards, performing a detailed inspection of the inboard PSU rail, and 
applicable on-condition actions. On-condition actions include attaching 
the secondary retention lanyard to either the adjacent 1-inch holes in 
the inboard PSU rail or installing a retention bracket on the inboard 
PSU rail and attaching the secondary retention lanyard to the retention 
bracket. These documents are distinct since Boeing Special Attention 
Requirements Bulletin 757-25-0320 RB, Revision 1, dated August 6, 2025, 
addresses airplanes with certain Burrana monitors, and Boeing Special 
Attention Requirements Bulletin 757-25-0321 RB, Revision 1, dated 
August 6, 2025, addresses airplanes with certain Panasonic monitors. 
This material is reasonably available because the interested parties 
have access to it through their normal course of business or by the 
means identified in the ADDRESSES section.

Authority for This Rulemaking

    Title 49 of the United States Code specifies the FAA's authority to 
issue rules on aviation safety. Subtitle I, section 106, describes the 
authority of the FAA Administrator. Subtitle VII: Aviation Programs, 
describes in more detail the scope of the Agency's authority.
    The FAA is issuing this rulemaking under the authority described in 
Subtitle VII, Part A, Subpart III, Section 44701: General requirements. 
Under that section, Congress charges the FAA with promoting safe flight 
of civil aircraft in air commerce by prescribing regulations for 
practices, methods, and procedures the Administrator finds necessary 
for safety in air commerce. This regulation is within the scope of that 
authority because it addresses an unsafe condition that is likely to 
exist or develop on products identified in this rulemaking action.

2026-21-12 The Boeing Company: Amendment 39-23501; Docket No. FAA-
2025-0003; Project Identifier AD-2024-00110-T.

(a) Effective Date

    This airworthiness directive (AD) is effective December 1, 2026.

 (b) Affected ADs

    None.

 (c) Applicability

    This AD applies to The Boeing Company Model 757-200 and -300 
series airplanes, certificated in any category, equipped with 
passenger service unit (PSU)-mounted video monitors.

 (d) Subject

    Air Transport Association (ATA) of America Code 25, Equipment/
furnishings.

(e) Unsafe Condition

    This AD was prompted by a report that some PSU-mounted video 
monitors became detached from the PSU rails during a hard landing 
that did not exceed the emergency load requirements for the PSUs. 
The FAA is issuing this AD to address PSU-mounted video monitors 
that could become fully detached or be held at an unsafe height, 
which, if not addressed, could result in injury to passengers and 
impede passenger and crew egress during emergency evacuation.

(f) Compliance

    Comply with this AD within the compliance times specified, 
unless already done.

(g) Required Actions

    (1) For airplanes identified in Boeing Special Attention 
Requirements Bulletin 757-25-0320 RB, Revision 1, dated August 6, 
2025: Except as specified by paragraph (h) of this AD, at the 
applicable times specified in the ``Compliance'' paragraph of Boeing 
Special Attention Requirements Bulletin 757-25-0320 RB, Revision 1, 
dated August 6, 2025, do all applicable actions identified in, and 
in accordance with, the Accomplishment Instructions of Boeing 
Special Attention Requirements Bulletin 757-25-0320 RB, Revision 1, 
dated August 6, 2025.
    Note 1 to paragraph (g)(1): Guidance for accomplishing the 
actions required by paragraph (g)(1) of this AD can be found in 
Boeing Special Attention Service Bulletin 757-25-0320, Revision 1, 
dated August 6, 2025, which is referred to in Boeing Special 
Attention Requirements Bulletin 757-25-0320 RB, Revision 1, dated 
August 6, 2025.
    (2) For airplanes identified in Boeing Special Attention 
Requirements Bulletin 757-25-0321 RB, Revision 1, dated August 6, 
2025: Except as specified by paragraph (h) of this AD, at the 
applicable times specified in the ``Compliance'' paragraph of Boeing 
Special Attention Requirements Bulletin 757-25-0321 RB, Revision 1, 
August 6, 2025, do all applicable actions identified in, and in 
accordance with, the Accomplishment Instructions of Boeing Special 
Attention Requirements Bulletin 757-25-0321 RB, Revision 1, dated 
August 6, 2025.
    Note 2 to paragraph (g)(2): Guidance for accomplishing the 
actions required by paragraph (g)(2) of this AD can be found in 
Boeing Special Attention Service Bulletin 757-25-0321, Revision 1, 
dated August 6, 2025, which is referred to in Boeing Special 
Attention Requirements Bulletin 757-25-0321 RB, Revision 1, dated 
August 6, 2025.

    (3) For airplanes not identified in paragraph (g)(1) or (2) of 
this AD: Within 60 months after the effective date of this AD, 
replace all PSU-mounted video monitors that do not have secondary 
retention lanyards with PSU-mounted video monitors that have 
secondary retention lanyards, using a method approved in accordance 
with the procedures specified in paragraph (j) of this AD.

(h) Exceptions to Service Information Specifications

    (1) Where the Compliance Time columns of the tables in the 
``Compliance'' paragraph of Boeing Special Attention Requirements 
Bulletin 757-25-0320 RB, Revision 1, dated August 6, 2025, refer to

this AD requires using the effective date of this AD.
    (2) Where the Compliance Time columns of the tables in the 
``Compliance'' paragraph of Boeing Special Attention Requirements 
Bulletin 757-25-0321 RB, Revision 1, dated August 6, 2025, refer to 
the original issue date of Requirements Bulletin 757-25-0321 RB, 
this AD requires using the effective date of this AD.
    (3) Where Boeing Special Attention Requirements Bulletins 757-
25-0320 RB, Revision 1, and 757-25-0321 RB, Revision 1, both dated 
August 6, 2025, specify contacting Boeing for alternative 
installation instructions and doing the installation, this AD 
requires doing the installation using a method approved in 
accordance with the procedures specified in paragraph (j) of this 
AD.

(i) Credit for Previous Actions

    This paragraph provides credit for the actions specified in 
paragraph (g)(1) of this AD, if those actions were performed before 
the effective date of this AD using Boeing Special Attention 
Requirements Bulletin 757-25-0320 RB, dated January 19, 2024.

(j) Alternative Methods of Compliance (AMOCs)

    (1) The Manager, AIR-520, Continued Operational Safety Branch, 
FAA, has the authority to approve AMOCs for this AD, if requested 
using the procedures found in 14 CFR 39.19. In accordance with 14 
CFR 39.19, send your request to your principal inspector or 
responsible Flight Standards Office, as appropriate. If sending 
information directly to the manager of the certification office, 
send it to the attention of the person identified in paragraph 
(k)(1) of this AD. Information may be emailed to: [email protected].
    (2) Before using any approved AMOC, notify your appropriate 
principal inspector, or lacking a principal inspector, the manager 
of the responsible Flight Standards Office.
    (3) An AMOC that provides an acceptable level of safety may be 
used for any repair, modification, or alteration required by this AD 
if it is approved by The Boeing Company Organization Designation 
Authorization (ODA) that has been authorized by the Manager, AIR-
520, Continued Operational Safety Branch, FAA, to make those 
findings. To be approved, the repair method, modification deviation, 
or alteration deviation must meet the certification basis of the 
airplane, and the approval must specifically refer to this AD.

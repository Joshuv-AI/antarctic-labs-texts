Spectral multipliers on Métivier groups Thanks: The author gratefully acknowledges the support by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG) through grant MU 761/12-1.
Lars Niedorf

Abstract
. We prove an L p L^{p} -spectral multiplier theorem under the sharp regularity condition s > d ​ | 1 / p − 1 / 2 | s>d\left|1/p-1/2\right| for sub-Laplacians on Métivier groups. The proof is based on a restriction type estimate which, at first sight, seems to be suboptimal for proving sharp spectral multiplier results, but turns out to be surprisingly effective. This is achieved by exploiting the structural property that for any Métivier group the first layer of any stratification of its Lie algebra is typically much larger than the second layer, a phenomenon closely related to Radon–Hurwitz numbers.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Spectral multipliers

The celebrated Mikhlin–Hörmander theorem
[
18
]
states that the Fourier multiplier operator
T
given by

\widehat{Tf}(\xi)=F(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi),\quad f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

is of weak type
(1,1)
and extends to a bounded operator on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
whenever the Fourier multiplier
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfies

\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\sup_{t>0}{\lVert F(t\,\cdot\,)\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}<\infty\quad\text{for some }s>n/2.

Here
\eta:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
denotes some nonzero smooth function which is compactly supported in an annulus away from the origin, and
L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the Sobolev space of fractional order
s\geq 0
. Note that different choices of
\eta
lead to equivalent norms. The regularity condition
s>n/2
is sharp and cannot be decreased, see for example
[
42
]
.

The Mikhlin–Hörmander theorem implies the following spectral multiplier result for the Laplacian
-\Delta=-(\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}+\dots+\partial_{x_{n}}^{2})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Given a Borel measurable function
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
, which we call a
spectral multiplier
in the following, the operator
F(-\Delta)
defined by functional calculus corresponds to a radial Fourier multiplier operator since

(F(-\Delta)f)^{\wedge}(\xi)=F(|\xi|^{2})\hat{f}(\xi).

Thus, the Mikhlin–Hörmander theorem implies that
F(-\Delta)
is bounded as an operator on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
and is of weak type
(1,1)
whenever

\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}:=\sup_{t>0}{\lVert F(t\,\cdot\,)\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R})}}<\infty\quad\text{for some }s>n/2.

1.2.
Sub-Laplacians

Let
G
be a stratified Lie group and
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{r}
be the stratification of the Lie algebra of
G
, that is,
[\mathfrak{g}_{i},\mathfrak{g}_{j}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{i+j}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
(with the understanding that
\mathfrak{g}_{m}=\{0\}
for
m>r
), and
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
generates
\mathfrak{g}
as a Lie algebra. Given a basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
of the first layer
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, we can identify it with left-invariant vector fields on
G
via the Lie derivative, and consider the associated left-invariant sub-Laplacian
L
, which is given by

L=-(X_{1}^{2}+\dots+X_{d_{1}}^{2}).

Let
G
be equipped with a left-invariant Haar measure and
L^{p}(G)
be the associated Lebesgue space. The sub-Laplacian
L
is positive (that is,
(Lf,f)_{L^{2}(G)}\geq 0
for all functions
f
in the domain of
L
, which is given by
\{f\in L^{2}(G):Lf\in L^{2}(G)\}
) and self-adjoint on
L^{2}(G)
. Hence, one can define via functional calculus for every spectral multiplier
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
the operator

F(L)=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(\lambda)\,dE(\lambda)\quad\text{on }L^{2}(G),

where
E
denotes the spectral measure of
L
. By the spectral theorem, the operator
F(L)
is bounded on
L^{2}
if and only if the multiplier
F
is
E
-essentially bounded. Christ
[
7
]
, and Mauceri and Meda
[
30
]
showed that
F(L)
is of weak type
(1,1)
and bounded on all
L^{p}
-spaces for
p\in(1,\infty)
if

\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}<\infty\quad\text{for some }s>Q/2,

where
Q=\dim\mathfrak{g}_{1}+2\dim\mathfrak{g}_{2}+\dots+r\dim\mathfrak{g}_{r}
is the
homogeneous dimension
of
G
. For sub-Laplacians on Heisenberg (type) groups, Müller and Stein
[
33
]
, and Hebisch
[
17
]
showed that the threshold
s>Q/2
can even be pushed down to
s>d/2
, with
d
being the topological dimension of the underlying group. Since then, this result has been extended to other specific classes of two-step stratified Lie groups
[
28
,
25
,
29
]
, and also to related settings
[
9
,
24
,
8
,
2
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
.

Conversely, Martini, Müller, and Nicolussi Golo
[
23
]
were able to show for a very general class of smooth second-order real differential operators associated with a bracket-generating sub-Riemannian structure on smooth
d
-dimensional manifolds that at least regularity of order
s\geq d\left|1/p-1/2\right|
is necessary for having the spectral multiplier estimate

\lVert F(L)\rVert_{p\to p}\leq C_{p,s}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}

for every bounded Borel function
F
, where

\|F\|_{L^{\infty}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}:=\sup_{t>0}{\lVert F(t\,\cdot\,)\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}.

In particular, their result implies that regularity of order
s\geq d/2
is necessary to have boundedness on all
L^{p}
-spaces. On the other hand, suppose we have a Mikhlin–Hörmander type result of the form

\|F(L)\|_{p\to p}\leq C_{p,s}\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}\quad\text{for all }p\in(1,\infty)\text{ and }s>d/2.

Then, replacing
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}
by the stronger norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}
, we obtain

\|F(L)\|_{p\to p}\leq C_{p,s}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}\quad\text{for all }s>d\left|1/p-1/2\right|,

and all
p\in(1,\infty)
, which follows by interpolation with the trivial
L^{2}
-
L^{2}
estimate from the spectral theorem. In the range
1\leq p\leq 2(d+1)/(d+3)
such
p
-specific
spectral multiplier estimates are known to hold for radial Fourier multipliers on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
even with the weaker
\smash{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}
-norm, see
[
6
,
41
,
21
]
. With the
\smash{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}
-norm in place of the
\smash{L^{\infty}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}
-norm on the right-hand side, these
p
-specific spectral multiplier estimates yield in particular that the Bochner-Riesz means given by

F(L)=(1-tL)^{\delta}_{+},\quad t>0

are uniformly bounded on
L^{p}
under the regularity condition
\delta>d\left|1/p-1/2\right|-1/2
, which is the same order of regularity as in the Bochner-Riesz conjecture, see
[
44
]
.

So far it is an open question if regularity of order
s>\max\{d\left|1/p-1/2\right|,1/2\}
with respect to the
L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}
-norm is generally sufficient to provide spectral multiplier estimates. In sub-elliptic settings, the only results in the literature seem to be those of
[
5
,
37
]
for Grushin operators and those of
[
38
]
for sub-Laplacians on Heisenberg type groups.

1.3.
Métivier groups versus Heisenberg type groups

The purpose of this paper is to extend the spectral multiplier result for sub-Laplacians on Heisenberg type groups of
[
38
]
to the larger class of Métivier groups.

Before stating the main result, we need to introduce some notation. Let
G
be a two-step stratified Lie group, that is, a connected, simply connected nilpotent Lie group whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
(which is the tangent space
T_{e}G
at the identity
e
of
G
) admits a decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
into two non-trivial subspaces
\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
, where
[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{1}]=\mathfrak{g}_{2}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is contained in the center of
\mathfrak{g}
. In the following, we refer to
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
as being the
first
and
second layer
of
\mathfrak{g}
, respectively. Let

(1.1)

d_{1}=\dim\mathfrak{g}_{1}\geq 1,\quad d_{2}=\dim\mathfrak{g}_{2}\geq 1\quad\text{and}\quad d=\dim\mathfrak{g}.

Given a basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
of the first layer
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, we identify each element of the basis by a left-invariant vector field on
G
via the Lie derivative, and consider the associated sub-Laplacian
L
, which is the second-order differential operator

(1.2)

L=-(X_{1}^{2}+\dots+X_{d_{1}}^{2}).

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
denote the dual of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. Any element
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
gives rise to a skew-symmetric bilinear form
\omega_{\mu}:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\times\mathfrak{g}_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
via

\omega_{\mu}(x,x^{\prime})=\mu([x,x^{\prime}]),\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}.

The group
G
is called a
Métivier group
if
\omega_{\mu}
is non-degenerate for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, which means that
\mathfrak{r}_{\mu}=\{0\}
for the radical

\mathfrak{r}_{\mu}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}:\omega_{\mu}(x,x^{\prime})=0\text{ for all }x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}\}.

We choose a basis
U_{1},\dots,U_{d_{2}}
of the second layer
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be the inner product rendering
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}},U_{1},\dots,U_{d_{2}}
an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}
. The inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
induces a norm on the dual
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
which we denote by
|\cdot|
. Let
J_{\mu}
be the skew-symmetric endomorphism such that

(1.3)

\omega_{\mu}(x,x^{\prime})=\langle J_{\mu}x,x^{\prime}\rangle,\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}.

Then
G
is a Métivier group if and only if
J_{\mu}
is invertible for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
. We call
G
a
Heisenberg type group
if the endomorphisms
J_{\mu}
are orthogonal for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
of length 1, which means that

J_{\mu}^{2}=-|\mu|^{2}\id_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\quad\text{for all }\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}.

The class of Heisenberg type groups is a strict subclass of that of Métivier groups, an example of a Métivier group that is not of Heisenberg type may be found for instance in the appendix of
[
32
]
, see also
Section

3
, where we analyze the spectral properties of the matrices
J_{\mu}
.

Passing to the broader class of Métivier groups generally involves giving up on the rotation invariant structure of Heisenberg type groups. Specifically, the matrices
J_{\mu}
are no longer orthogonal for
|\mu|=1
, resulting in a significantly more complicated spectral decomposition into eigenspaces parameterized by
\mu
. Similarly, the spectral properties of the sub-Laplacian
L
become considerably more challenging to analyze compared to the Heisenberg type case, see
Section

3
below.

1.4.
Statement of the main results

The main result of this paper is the following
p
-specific spectral multiplier estimate for Métivier groups, with an accompanying result for Bochner–Riesz multipliers, where the order of regularity is the same as in the Bochner–Riesz conjecture. As before, let

\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}=\sup_{t>0}{\lVert F(t\,\cdot\,)\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R})}}.

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, let
p_{n}=2(n+1)/(n+3)
be the
Stein–Tomas exponent
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We put
p_{d_{1},d_{2}}=p_{d_{2}}
for
(d_{1},d_{2})\notin\{(8,6),(8,7)\}
,
p_{8,6}=17/12
, and
p_{8,7}=14/11
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a Métivier group of topological dimension
d
, and, as in
1.2
, let
L
be a sub-Laplacian on
G
. If
1\leq p\leq p_{d_{1},d_{2}}
with dimensions
d_{1},d_{2}
as in (
1.1
), the following statements hold:

(1)

If
p>1
and if
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is a bounded Borel function such that

\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}<\infty\quad\text{for some }s>d\left(1/p-1/2\right),

then the operator
F(L)
is bounded on
L^{p}(G)
, and

\lVert F(L)\rVert_{L^{p}\to L^{p}}\leq C_{p,s}\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}.

(2)

For any
\delta>d\left(1/p-1/2\right)-1/2
, the Bochner–Riesz means
(1-tL)^{\delta}_{+}
,
t\geq 0
, are uniformly bounded on
L^{p}(G)
.

By the result of
[
23
]
, the threshold for
s
is optimal up the endpoint and cannot be decreased. If
s>d/2
in the first part of
Theorem

1.1
, the operator
F(L)
is of weak type
(1,1)
, as stated in
[
28
]
, and this holds independently of the dimensions
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
. It should be pointed out that the first part of the theorem only provides results for
d_{2}\geq 2
. Since the proof of
Theorem

1.1
relies on exploiting favorable restriction type estimates, the fact that we do not get any results for
d_{2}=1
aligns well with the fact that restriction estimates are only available when
p=1
for Heisenberg groups
[
35
]
, unless one passes to mixed
L^{p}
-norms.

Comparing this result with the spectral multiplier theorem of
[
38
]
for Heisenberg type groups,
Theorem

1.1
is a generalization of Theorem 1.1 of
[
38
]
, except for the cases
(d_{1},d_{2})=(8,6)
and
(d_{1},d_{2})=(8,7)
. We give some comments on these special cases. Note that in these cases the thresholds for
p
in
Theorem

1.1
are
p_{8,6}=17/12
and
p_{8,7}=14/11
, and we get spectral multiplier estimates for any Métivier group, while
[
38
]
gives the same estimates with thresholds
p_{6}=14/9>17/12
and
p_{7}=8/5>14/11
, but only under the additional assumption that
L
is a sub-Laplacian on a Heisenberg type group. In principle, it is also possible to reproduce the results of
[
38
]
for the cases
(d_{1},d_{2})=(8,6)
and
(d_{1},d_{2})=(8,7)
using the methods of this article by additionally incorporating the second layer weighted Plancherel estimates of Martini and Müller from
[
25
]
, see
Section

8
, and in particular
Remark

8.5
. However, the advantage of the Heisenberg type situation in
[
38
]
is twofold. On the one hand, the proof of the restriction type estimates in
[
38
]
is based on the spectral projector bounds for twisted Laplacians of Koch and Ricci
[
20
]
, while the more general Métivier group situation considered here requires to resort to spectral cluster estimates for the associated anisotropic twisted Laplacians in order to control the distribution of their eigenvalues, see
Section

2.1
. On the other hand,
[
38
]
does not require the second layer weighted Plancherel estimates of
[
25
]
, since the restriction type estimates for Heisenberg type groups in
[
38
]
are stronger than those of
Theorem

2.1
.

A natural question is whether
p
-specific spectral multiplier results with threshold
s>d\left|1/p-1/2\right|
hold beyond the class of Métivier groups. However, despite the fact that by
[
39
]
, there is now a restriction type estimate that holds in great generality, the bottleneck of the present approach seems to be the availability of favorable weighted Plancherel estimates. The requirement of having these estimates appears to be a major obstacle when passing from Métivier groups to classes of two-step groups where the bilinear form
\omega_{\mu}
from
1.3
may have a non-trivial radical, see also
Remark

6.2
.

2.
Sketch of the proof

This paper builds on the methods developed in
[
4
,
5
,
37
,
38
]
, which in turn originate from C. Fefferman’s idea of using restriction estimates to prove
L^{p}
-boundedness for Bochner–Riesz means, see
[
13
]
. This idea has also proved valuable in several other settings, see for instance
[
40
,
34
,
16
,
4
,
43
]
and the references therein.

2.1.
The restriction type estimates

The approach of
[
38
]
relies on suitable restriction type estimates, and we want to exploit the restriction type estimates of
[
39
]
for this. We first state the restriction type estimates of
[
39
]
.

Let
G
be again an arbitrary two-step stratified Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and stratification
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. Let
L=(-X_{1}^{2}+\dots X_{d_{1}}^{2})
be the sub-Laplacian associated with a basis of the first layer
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and let
U_{1},\dots,U_{2}
be a basis of the second layer
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. The operators
L
and
-iU_{1},\dots,-iU_{d_{2}}
admit a joint functional calculus, which allows us to introduce an additional truncation along the spectrum of the operator

U=(-(U_{1}^{2}+\dots+U_{d_{2}}^{2}))^{1/2},

which corresponds to a partial Laplacian on the second layer
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. This allows us to write any operator
F(L)
as

F(L)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U),

where
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,\infty)
and
\chi(2^{\ell}\cdot)
is a partition of unity.

Due to this additional truncation, which will be crucial in proving the spectral multiplier estimates of
Theorem

1.1
, we also refer to the corresponding restriction type estimates as
truncated
restriction type estimates. A similar truncation is used in
[
38
]
for sub-Laplacians in Heisenberg type groups, and in
[
37
]
in the related setting of Grushin operators.

It should be pointed out that the method of
[
5
]
, which
[
37
]
and
[
38
]
are based on, relies on
weighted
restriction type estimates, which turned out to be applicable for Grushin operators, but unfortunately not for sub-Laplacians on Heisenberg type groups, see
[
38
, Section 8]
.

The restriction type estimates of
[
39
]
are stated in terms of the norms

\lVert F\rVert_{M,2}=\bigg(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{K\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\sup_{\lambda\in[\frac{K-1}{M},\frac{K}{M})}|F(\lambda)|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},\quad M\in(0,\infty)

which were introduced by Cowling and Sikora in
[
9
]
. Given any Euclidean space of dimension
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, we denote by

p_{n}:=\frac{2(n+1)}{n+3}

the
Stein–Tomas exponent
associated with that space.

Theorem 2.1

(Truncated restriction type estimates)
.

[
39
]

Let
G
be a two-step stratified Lie group, and, as in
1.2
, let
L
be a sub-Laplacian on
G
. Suppose that
1\leq p\leq\min\{p_{d_{1}},p_{d_{2}}\}
with dimensions
d_{1},d_{2}
as in (
1.1
). If
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is a bounded Borel function supported in a compact subset
A\subseteq(0,\infty)
and
\chi:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
is a smooth function with compact support, then

(2.1)

\lVert F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)\rVert_{p\to 2}\leq C_{A,p,\chi}2^{-\ell d_{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\|F\|_{2}^{1-\theta_{p}}\lVert F\rVert_{2^{\ell},2}^{\theta_{p}}\quad\text{for all }\ell\in\mathbb{Z},

where
\theta_{p}\in[0,1]
satisfies
1/p=(1-\theta_{p})+\theta_{p}/\min\{p_{d_{1}},p_{d_{2}}\}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let again
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be the inner product rendering
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}},U_{1},\dots,U_{d_{2}}
an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}
and let
J_{\mu}
be the matrix with
\langle J_{\mu}x,x^{\prime}\rangle=\mu([x,x^{\prime}])
for all
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
. Then, as shown in
[
39
, Remark 5.2]
there is a constant
\ell_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
which depends only on the matrices
J_{\mu}
, the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, and the compact subset
A\subseteq(0,\infty)
above such that

F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)=0\quad\text{for all }\ell<-\ell_{0}.

So
Theorem

2.1
should actually be read as a statement for all
\ell\in[-\ell_{0},\infty)
.

Although the above theorem is valid for all two-step stratified Lie groups, the presence of the norm
\lVert F\rVert_{2^{\ell},2}
in (
2.1
) imposes a slight drawback. By (3.19) and (3.29) of
[
9
]
(or alternatively Lemma 3.4 of
[
3
]
), for
s>1/2
, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{M,2}
can be estimated by

(2.2)

\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\lVert F\rVert_{M,2}\leq C_{s}\big(\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}}+M^{-s}\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\big),

which means that
\|\cdot\|_{M,2}
is stronger than the
L^{2}
-norm. Ignoring for a moment the additional truncation, the restriction type estimate (
2.1
) with
\lVert F\rVert_{2^{\ell},2}
replaced by
\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}}
would be equivalent (see for instance
[
43
, Proposition 4.1]
) to a restriction type estimate for the Strichartz projectors
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
, which are formally given by
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}=\delta_{\lambda}(L)
, where
\delta_{\lambda}
is the Dirac delta distribution at
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

However, the truncated restriction type estimates can still be used to prove spectral multiplier estimates for the whole class of Métivier groups, by exploiting the fact that the dimension
d_{1}
of the first layer is in general much larger than the dimension
d_{2}
of the second layer if
G
is a Métivier group. This fact is closely related to a theorem by Adams
[
1
]
which states that the maximal number of linearly independent vector fields on a
(n-1)
-dimensional sphere is given by
\rho_{\mathrm{RH}}(n)-1
, where
\rho_{\mathrm{RH}}(n)
denotes the
n
-th Radon–Hurwitz number.

2.2.
The spectral multiplier estimates

The key idea of deriving spectral multiplier estimates from restriction (type) estimates, which goes back to C. Fefferman
[
13
]
, can be illustrated as follows. Let
G
be a Métivier group, and, as in
1.2
, let
L
be a sub-Laplacian on
G
. Suppose that we want to derive
L^{p}
-boundedness of the Bochner–Riesz mean
(1-L)_{+}^{\delta}
. If
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is the multiplier given by

F(\lambda)=(1-\lambda^{2})^{\delta}_{+},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R},

then
F(\sqrt{L})=(1-L)_{+}^{\delta}
. We decompose
F
as

F=\sum_{\iota=0}^{\infty}F^{(\iota)},

where
|\tau|\sim 2^{\iota}
whenever
\tau\in\supp\widehat{F^{(\iota)}}
for
\iota\geq 1
. It suffices to show

\lVert F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})\rVert_{p\to p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\quad\text{for some }\varepsilon>0.

Let
\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}
be the convolution kernel associated with
F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})
, that is,

F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})f=f*\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}\quad\text{for }f\in\mathcal{S}(G),

where
*
denotes the group convolution. By the Fourier inversion formula, we have

F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|\tau|\sim 2^{\iota}}\chi(\tau/2^{\iota})\hat{F}(\tau)\cos(\tau\sqrt{L})\,d\tau,

where
\chi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is some bump function. Since solutions of the wave equation associated with
L
possess finite propagation speed (which is the reason for artificially introducing the square root of
L
) and since we have
|\tau|\sim 2^{\iota}
in the integral above, the convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}
is supported in the Euclidean ball
B_{R}\times B_{R^{2}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
with radius
R\sim 2^{\iota}
centered at the origin (where we identify

G\cong\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}=\mathbb{R}^{d}

via the basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and some basis
U_{1},\dots,U_{d_{2}}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
).

Now suppose we had a restriction type estimate of the form

(2.3)

\lVert F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})f\rVert_{2}\lesssim\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}\lVert f\rVert_{p},

which is, for instance, the case if
G
is a Heisenberg type group (up to replacing the multiplier
F^{(\iota)}
by some localized version
F^{(\iota)}\psi
), see
[
22
]
and
[
38
]
. Then, for a function
f
supported in a Euclidean ball of size
R\times R^{2}
centered at the origin (which one can assume without loss of generality), Hölder’s inequality together with the restriction type estimate
2.3
yields

(2.4)

\lVert F^{(\iota)}(L)f\rVert_{p}\lesssim R^{Q/q}\lVert F^{(\iota)}(L)f\rVert_{2}\lesssim R^{Q/q}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}\lVert f\rVert_{p},

where
1/q=1/p-1/2
and
Q
is the homogeneous dimension of
G
, which is

Q=d_{1}+2d_{2}.

One can show that
F\in L^{2}_{\smash{Q/q+\varepsilon}}(\mathbb{R})
for some
\varepsilon>0
if
\delta>Q/q-1/2
. Then, the last term of (
2.4
) can be estimated via

R^{Q/q}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}\lVert f\rVert_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{Q/q+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}\lVert f\rVert_{p}.

Note that this argument requires an order of regularity in terms of the homogeneous dimension
Q
. The method of
[
37
,
38
]
refines the dyadic decomposition
F^{(\iota)}
by an additional truncation along the spectrum of the operator

U=(-(U_{1}^{2}+\dots+U_{d_{2}}^{2}))^{1/2}.

More precisely, we localize
F^{(\iota)}
in space by some suitable non-zero smooth function
\psi
which is compactly supported away from the origin and decompose the operator
(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{L})
as

(2.5)

(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{L})=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{L})\chi(2^{\ell}U)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}F^{(\iota)}_{\ell}(L,U),

where the functions
\chi(2^{\ell}\,\cdot\,)
shall form a dyadic partition of unity of
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
. (The decomposition in
[
37
,
38
]
is slightly different since the operator
\chi(2^{\ell}U)
is replaced by
\chi(2^{-\ell}L/U)
there. However, since the spectral multiplier
F^{(\iota)}\psi
is compactly supported away from the origin, both the truncation by
\chi(2^{\ell}U)
and
\chi(2^{-\ell}L/U)
amount to having a multiplier for
U
supported around
2^{-\ell}
.)

The convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}_{\ell}
of
F^{(\iota)}_{\ell}(L,U)
can be written down explicitly in terms of the partial Fourier transform along the center and Laguerre functions, which is as follows. As we will show in
Section

3
, there exists non-empty, homogeneous Zariski-open subset
\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
, numbers
N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
,
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{N})\in(\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\})^{N}
, and a continuous function
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\ni\mu\mapsto\mathbf{b}^{\mu}=(b^{\mu}_{1},\dots,b^{\mu}_{N})\in[0,\infty)^{N}
such that the skew-symmetric matrices
J_{\mu}
given by
1.3
admit the spectral decomposition

(2.6)

-J_{\mu}^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}(b_{n}^{\mu})^{2}P_{n}^{\mu}\quad\text{for all }\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*},

where
P_{n}^{\mu}
is an orthogonal projections on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
of rank
2r_{j}
for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}
, with pairwise orthogonal ranges. If
G
is a Métivier group, then, for
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
,
u\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}_{\ell}(x,u)=(2\pi)^{-d_{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}(F^{(\iota)}\psi)\big(\sqrt{\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu}}\big)\chi(2^{\ell}|\mu|)

(2.7)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{k_{n}}^{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}(R_{\mu}^{-1}P_{n}^{\mu}x)\,e^{i\langle\mu,u\rangle}\,d\mu,

where
R_{\mu}\in O(d_{1})
,

\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu}=\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathbf{b^{\mu}},\mathbf{r}}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(2k_{n}+r_{n}\right)b_{n}^{\mu},

and
\varphi_{k_{n}}^{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}
are some rescaled Laguerre functions of the form

(2.8)

\varphi_{k_{n}}^{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}(z)=(b_{n}^{\mu})^{r_{n}}\varphi_{k_{n}}((b_{n}^{\mu})^{1/2}z),\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{2r_{n}}.

If
G
is a Métivier group, then
b_{n}^{\mu}\neq 0
for all
\mu\neq 0
and all
n\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, whence

b_{n}^{\mu}\sim 1\quad\text{for }|\mu|=1.

Since
\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu}\sim 1
and
|\mu|\sim 2^{\ell}
on the support of the involved multipliers, and as the function
\mu\mapsto\textbf{b}^{\mu}
is homogeneous of degree
1
, the sum in (
2.7
) ranges in fact over all
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
satisfying

|\textbf{k}|+1\sim\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\bar{\mu}}=|\mu|^{-1}\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu}\sim|\mu|^{-1}\sim 2^{\ell},\quad\text{where }\bar{\mu}=\frac{\mu}{|\mu|}.

In particular, the sum of the dyadic decomposition (
2.5
) ranges only over
\ell\in[-\ell_{0},\infty)
, where
\ell_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
is some constant that depends only on the matrices
J_{\mu}
and the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\mathfrak{g}
, with respect to which
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}},U_{1},\dots,U_{d_{2}}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}
.

A crucial observation in connection with the formula
2.7
is the following. The pointwise estimates from
[
45
, Equations (1.1.44), (1.3.41), Lemma 1.5.3]
suggest that any of the rescaled Laguerre functions of the form (
2.8
) is essentially supported in a ball of radius comparable to

(b_{n}^{\mu})^{-1/2}(k_{n}+1)^{1/2}\sim|\mu|^{-1/2}(k_{n}+1)^{1/2}\lesssim 2^{\ell}

centered at the origin, up to some exponentially decaying term. Hence we may think of the convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}_{\ell}
in (
2.7
) being supported in a Euclidean ball of dimensions
R_{\ell}\times R^{2}
in place of
R\times R^{2}
, with
R_{\ell}\sim 2^{\ell}
. This leads us to distinguish between the case
\ell\leq\iota
, where
R_{\ell}\lesssim 2^{\iota}\sim R
, and the case
\ell>\iota
. Using the truncated restriction type estimate of
Theorem

2.1
and applying the Sobolev type estimate (
2.2
) for the norm
\|\cdot\|_{2^{\ell},2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)\rVert_{p\to 2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\ell d_{2}/q}\|(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{\cdot}\,)\|_{2}^{1-\theta_{p}}\|(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{\cdot}\,)\|_{2^{\ell},2}^{\theta_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-\ell d_{2}/q}\|(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{\cdot}\,)\|_{2^{\ell},2}

(2.9)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\psi}2^{-\ell d_{2}/q}\big(\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}}+2^{-\ell s}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\big),

where again
1/q=1/p-1/2
, and
s>1/2
.

For
\ell>\iota
, we argue as before in
2.4
, using the fact that
\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}
is supported in a ball of size
R\times R^{2}
(which is however not quite true for the kernels
\smash{\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}}
, but the information about the support at scale
R\times R^{2}
can be preserved by introducing a cut-off function
\mathbf{1}_{CR\times CR^{2}}
before doing the decomposition (
2.5
)). Similar to (
2.4
), using Hölder’s inequality, but now in combination with the restriction type estimate (
2.9
) yields

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{1}_{CR\times CR^{2}}(F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)f)\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{Q/q}\lVert F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)f\rVert_{2}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{Q/q}2^{-\ell d_{2}/q}\big(\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}}+2^{-\ell s}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\big)\lVert f\rVert_{p}.

Summing over all
\ell>\iota
shows that the additional factor
2^{-\ell s}
completely makes up for the higher order of the Sobolev norm in the factor
\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}
since

\sum_{\ell=\iota+1}^{\infty}2^{-\ell d_{2}/q}\big(\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}}+2^{-\ell s}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\big)\sim 2^{-\iota d_{2}/q}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Hence, since
Q-d_{2}=d
, (
2.10
) yields

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=\iota+1}^{\infty}\lVert\mathbf{1}_{CR\times CR^{2}}(F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)f)\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{d/q}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert f\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\sim\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L_{d/q}^{2}}\lVert f\rVert_{p},

so that we end up with an order of regularity
d/q=d\left(1/p-1/2\right)
instead of
Q/q=Q\left(1/p-1/2\right)
with homogeneous dimension
Q
.

For
\ell\leq\iota
, we decompose the support of
f
, which is of size
R\times R^{2}
, into a grid of balls of size
R_{\ell}\times R^{2}
, and decompose
f
into a sum of functions
f_{m}^{(\ell)}
, each of which is supported in one of the balls of the grid. Since the convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}
is essentially supported in a ball of size
R_{\ell}\times R^{2}
, we may think of the function
F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L)f_{m}^{(\ell)}
being also supported in a ball of size comparable to
R_{\ell}\times R^{2}
. Using again Hölder’s inequality and the restriction type estimate (
2.9
) yields

\displaystyle\lVert F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)f_{m}^{(\ell)}\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim(R_{\ell}^{d_{1}}R^{2d_{2}})^{1/q}\lVert F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)f_{m}^{(\ell)}\rVert_{2}

(2.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim(R_{\ell}^{d_{1}-d_{2}}R^{2d_{2}})^{1/q}\big(\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}}+2^{-\ell s}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\big)\lVert f_{m}^{(\ell)}\rVert_{p}.

Since
\ell\leq\iota
, and

2^{-\ell s}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\sim 2^{(\iota-\ell)s}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}},

the last line of (
2.11
) is comparable to
\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert f_{m}^{(\ell)}\rVert_{p}
times

(2^{\ell(d_{1}-d_{2})}2^{2\iota d_{2}})^{1/q}2^{(\iota-\ell)s}=(2^{(\ell-\iota)(d_{1}-d_{2}-sq)}2^{\iota d})^{1/q}.

In a precise argument that we will see later, since

2^{\iota d/q}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}}\sim 2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L_{d/q+\varepsilon}^{2}},

the argument of the proof works as long as we can bound the sum

\sum_{\ell=-\ell_{0}}^{\iota}\big(2^{(\ell-\iota)(d_{1}-d_{2}-sq)/q-\iota\varepsilon/2}\big)^{p}.

By first choosing
\varepsilon>0
and then
s>1/2
sufficiently close to
1/2
, this can be achieved as long as

d_{1}-d_{2}-\frac{q}{2}\geq 0.

Since we have in particular assumed
1\leq p\leq p_{d_{2}}=2(d_{2}+1)/(d_{2}+3)
in
Theorem

1.1
, we have
1/q=1/p-1/2\geq 1/(d_{2}+1)
and thus

(2.12)

d_{1}-d_{2}-\frac{q}{2}\geq d_{1}-\frac{3d_{2}}{2}-\frac{1}{2}.

Since
G
is assumed to be a Métivier group, the map
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\to(\mathfrak{g}_{1}/\mathbb{R}y)^{*},\mu\mapsto\omega_{\mu}(\cdot,x^{\prime})
is injective for
x^{\prime}\neq 0
, whence
d_{1}>d_{2}
. But as a matter of fact,
d_{1}
is in general much larger than
d_{2}
, and the latter term of (
2.12
) is non-negative for any Métivier group except for the cases where

(2.13)

(d_{1},d_{2})\in\{(4,3),(8,6),(8,7)\}.

2.3.
On the exceptional cases

In the present approach, the fact that the convolution kernel
\smash{\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}}
is essentially supported in a ball of size
R_{\ell}\times R^{2}
is derived from a weighted Plancherel estimate of the form

(2.14)

\int_{G}\big||x|^{\alpha}\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(x,u)\big|^{2}\,d(x,u)\lesssim_{\alpha}2^{\ell(2\alpha-d_{2})}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},\quad\alpha\geq 0.

The remaining cases (
2.13
) could also be covered if we additionally had a weighted Plancherel estimate of the form

(2.15)

\int_{G}\big||u|^{\beta}\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(x,u)\big|^{2}\,d(x,u)\lesssim_{\alpha}2^{\ell(2\beta-d_{2})}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{\beta}}^{2},\quad\beta\geq 0,

with weight
|u|^{\beta}
on the second layer. The weighted Plancherel estimate (
2.15
) would in fact yield that we may think of
\smash{\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}}
as being essentially supported in an even smaller ball of size
R_{\ell}\times R_{\ell}R
rather than
R_{\ell}\times R^{2}
, up to error terms. Such estimates with weight on the second layer are available for Heisenberg type groups
[
17
]
, the more general class of Heisenberg–Reiter (type) groups
[
29
]
, and for classes of two-step groups in low dimensions
[
25
]
. It is possible to prove a second layer weighted Plancherel estimate on arbitrary two-step stratified Lie groups for small
\beta\geq 0
by homogeneity considerations
[
26
]
, which we unfortunately cannot exploit in our approach, since it requires (
2.14
) and (
2.15
) for arbitrarily large powers
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
.

On the other hand, according to
[
25
, Proposition 20]
, second layer weighted Plancherel estimates result from appropriate bounds for
\mu
-derivatives of both the eigenvalues
b_{1}^{\mu},\dots,b_{N}^{\mu}
and the associated spectral projections
P_{1}^{\mu},\dots,P_{N}^{\mu}
from the spectral decomposition from
2.6
, that is,

-J_{\mu}^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}(b_{n}^{\mu})^{2}P_{n}^{\mu}\quad\text{for all }\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}.

As we will discuss in
Section

8
, such bounds are not always satisfied, since the functions
\mu\mapsto b_{n}^{\mu}
and
\mu\mapsto P_{n}^{\mu}
can admit singularities on the Zariski-closed subset
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}
. Fortunately, in the case
(d_{1},d_{2})=(4,3)
, the set of those singularities, which is always a cone due to homogeneity, is either a plane or a line in
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
, which allows us to prove a second layer weighted Plancherel estimate not with the complete weight
|u|^{\beta}
, but with a weight corresponding to a directional derivative in
\mu
whose direction lies within the Zariski-closed subset.

In the higher-dimensional exceptional cases, however, the set of possible singularities may no longer be a linear subspace, which is why the low-dimensional approach for
(d_{1},d_{2})=(4,3)
fails in higher dimensions.

3.
Spectral decompositions

Let
G
be a two-step stratified Lie group. Then its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits a decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, where
[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{1}]=\mathfrak{g}_{2}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is contained in the center of
\mathfrak{g}
. If we identify
G
with its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
via exponential coordinates, the group multiplication is given by

(x,u)(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})=\left(x+x^{\prime},u+u^{\prime}+\tfrac{1}{2}[x,x^{\prime}]\right),\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1},u,u^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}.

Furthermore, we identify
\mathfrak{g}_{1}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
by choosing a basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and a basis
U_{1},\dots,U_{d_{2}}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denote the inner product with respect to which these two bases become an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
be the dual of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. For any
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}

J_{\mu}
be the endomorphism given by

\langle J_{\mu}x,x^{\prime}\rangle=\mu([x,x^{\prime}]),\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1},

Note that

is skew-adjoint and
-J_{\mu}^{2}=J_{\mu}^{*}J_{\mu}
is self-adjoint and non-negative. The family of matrices
J_{\mu}
together with the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
fully characterizes the Lie group
G
. The matrices

admit a “simultaneous spectral decomposition” if
\mu
ranges in a Zariski open subset of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
.

As usual, we will identify the bases
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}

The convolution kernel

\varphi_{k}^{(\lambda,m)}(z)=\lambda^{m}L_{k}^{m-1}\big(\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda|z|^{2}\big)\,e^{-\frac{1}{4}\lambda|z|^{2}},\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{2m},

where

is the

. We use the notation of

in the next proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

If

is a Schwartz function, then

possesses a convolution kernel

(3.3)

\displaystyle\times\bigg[\prod_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{k_{n}}^{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}(R_{\mu}^{-1}P_{n}^{\mu}x)\bigg]e^{i\langle\tau,R_{\mu}^{-1}P_{0}^{\mu}x\rangle}\,e^{i\langle\mu,u\rangle}\,d\tau\,d\mu,

Proof.

See

4.
Reduction of
Theorem

to dyadic spectral multipliers

To prove
Theorem

1.1
, we use the following general spectral multiplier result of
[

]
, which allows us to reduce the spectral multiplier estimates of

1.1
to estimates for spectral multipliers whose Fourier transforms are supported on dyadic scales. Very similar arguments have already been used in
[
5
, Section 5]
and
[

, Section 4]
. The proof of the result in
[

]
is based on arguments from Calderón–Zygmund theory.

We choose a dyadic decomposition
(\chi_{\iota})_{\iota\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\sum_{\iota\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\iota}(\lambda)=1
for all
\lambda\neq 0
, and

(4.1)

\chi_{\iota}(\lambda)=\chi(\lambda/2^{\iota})

for some even bump function
\chi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
supported in
[-2,-1/2]\cup[1/2,2]
. Given a suitable multiplier
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
, we use the notation

(4.2)

F^{(\iota)}:=(\hat{F}\chi_{\iota})^{\vee}\quad\text{for }\iota\in\mathbb{Z},

where
\widehat{\cdot}
and
\cdot^{\vee}
denote the Fourier transform and its inverse on
\mathbb{R}
, respectively.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a metric measure space with distance
\rho
and measure
\mu
, and
L
be a positive self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(X)
. We say that
L
possesses the
finite propagation speed property
if

(\cos(t\sqrt{L})f,g)_{L^{2}(X)}=0\quad\text{for all }|t|<\rho(U,V)

whenever
f,g\in L^{2}(X)
are supported in open subsets
U,V\subseteq X
, where

\rho(U,V):=\inf\{\rho(u,v):u\in U,v\in V\}

denotes the distance of the two sets
U,V\subseteq X
.

In the following, given some metric space
(X,\rho)
we denote the ball of radius
r>0
centered at
x\in X
by
B_{r}(x)
.

Proposition 4.2
.

[
4
, Proposition I.22]

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a metric measure space of homogeneous type and
Q\geq 0
such that

\mu(B_{\lambda r}(x))\leq C\lambda^{Q}\mu(B_{r}(x))\quad\text{for all }x\in X,r>0,\lambda\geq 1.

Let
1\leq p_{0}<p<2
. Suppose that
L
is a positive self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(X)
such that the following statements are satisfied:

(i)

L
possesses the finite propagation speed property.

(ii)

L
satisfies the Stein–Tomas restriction type condition
(\mathrm{ST}^{\infty}_{p_{0},2})
of
[
4
]
, that is, for any
R>0
and all bounded Borel functions
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
supported in
[0,R]
,

(4.3)

\|F(\sqrt{L})(\mathbf{1}_{B_{r}(x)}f)\|_{2}\leq C\mu(B_{r}(x))^{{\frac{1}{2}}-{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}}(Rr)^{Q({\frac{1}{p_{0}}}-{\frac{1}{2}})}\|F\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{p_{0}}

for all
x\in X
, all
r\geq 1/R
, and all
f\in L^{p_{0}}(X)
.

(iii)

There is some
\beta>Q/2
such that

\sup_{t>0}\|F(t\sqrt{L})\|_{p\to p}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{\infty}_{\beta}}

for all even bounded Borel functions
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\supp F\subseteq[-1,1]
.

Suppose that
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is an even bounded Borel function and that there is a bounded sequence
(\alpha(\iota))_{\iota\in\mathbb{Z}}
with
\sum_{\iota\geq 0}(\iota+1)\alpha(\iota)<\infty
such that

(4.4)

\lVert(F\chi_{i})^{(j)}(\sqrt{L})\rVert_{p\to p}\leq\alpha(i+j)\quad\text{for all }i,j\in\mathbb{Z}.

Then the operator
F(\sqrt{L})
is of weak-type
(p,p)
.

Remark 4.3
.

Actually, in

in place of the Stein–Tomas type restriction condition

.

We apply

4.2

(4.5)

5.
On the numerology of Métivier groups

From now on, we restrict to the case where
G
is a Métivier group. Then, by definition, the skew-symmetric bilinear form
\omega_{\mu}(x,x^{\prime})=\mu([x,x^{\prime}])
on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
is non-degenerate for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
. Let again
J_{\mu}
denote the skew-symmetric endomorphism such that

\omega_{\mu}(x,x^{\prime})=\langle J_{\mu}x,x^{\prime}\rangle\quad\text{for all }x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the inner product on
\mathfrak{g}
rendering
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}},U_{1},\dots,U_{d_{2}}
an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
G
is a Métivier group if and only if
J_{\mu}
is invertible for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
. In particular, if
G
is a Métivier group, then
d_{1}\in 2\mathbb{N}
, and
d_{2}<d_{1}
(since the map
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\to(\mathfrak{g}_{1}/\mathbb{R}x^{\prime})^{*},\mu\mapsto\omega_{\mu}(\cdot,x^{\prime})
is injective for
x^{\prime}\neq 0
). But in fact, regarding the relationship of the dimensions
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
, the dimension
d_{1}
is in general much larger than
d_{2}
, as stated in
Proposition

5.1
below.

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, the
Radon–Hurwitz number

\rho_{\mathrm{RH}}(n)
is defined as follows: If we write
n=\left(2a+1\right)2^{b}
and
b=4q+r
, with
a,b,q,r\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq r<4
, then

\rho_{\mathrm{RH}}(n)=2^{r}+8q.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
0<d_{1},d_{2}<d
be integers with
d_{1}+d_{2}=d
. Then there exists a Métivier group of dimension
d
with center of dimension
d_{2}
if and only if

(5.1)

d_{2}<\rho_{\mathrm{RH}}(d_{1}).

In particular, for any such Métivier group with
(d_{1},d_{2})\notin\{(4,3),(8,6),(8,7)\}
,

(5.2)

d_{1}>3d_{2}/2.

Proof.

[
19
, Corollary 1]
asserts that condition (
5.1
) is in fact equivalent to having even a Heisenberg type group with the requisite dimensions. So all we need to show is that (
5.1
) holds also true for any Métivier group. Let
\varsigma(n)
denote be the maximal number
l\in\mathbb{N}
such that there are skew-symmetric matrices
J_{1},\dots,J_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
satisfying

J(\mu):=\sum_{j=1}^{l}\mu_{j}J_{j}\in\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})\quad\text{for all }\mu=(\mu_{j})_{j}\neq 0.

Note that any Métivier group with second layer of dimension
d_{2}
gives rise to such a family of
d_{2}
invertible skew-symmetric matrices
J_{1},\dots,J_{d_{2}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{1}}
and vice versa. Thus, we only need to show
\varsigma(n)<\rho_{\mathrm{RH}}(n)
.

To that end, choose matrices
J_{1},\dots,J_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
with
l=\varsigma(n)
such that the linear combination
J(\mu)
is invertible for all
\mu\neq 0
. Being skew-symmetric, the matrices
J_{1},\dots,J_{l}
give rise to a system of linearly independent vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{l}
on the Euclidean sphere
S^{n-1}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
via
x\mapsto J_{j}x
. But according to
[
1
]
, there can only be at most
\rho_{\mathrm{RH}}(n)-1
linearly independent vector fields on the sphere, whence we have
\varsigma(n)<\rho_{\mathrm{RH}}(n)
.

The inequality (
5.2
) is a direct consequence of (
5.1
). Suppose that
G
is a Métivier group with first layer of dimension
d_{1}
and second layer of dimension
d_{2}
. We write
d_{1}=\left(2a+1\right)2^{b}
with
b=4q+r
, where
a,b,q,r\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq r<4
. Suppose first
a\geq 1
. In that case, since clearly
2^{4q+r+1}\geq 8q+2^{r}
, we have

2d_{1}=\left(2a+1\right)2^{b+1}\geq 3\cdot 2^{4q+r+1}\geq 3(8q+2^{r})=3\rho_{\mathrm{RH}}(d_{1}).

Now suppose
a=0
. If
d_{1}\notin\{2,4,8\}
, then
q\geq 1
, and

\displaystyle 2d_{1}

\displaystyle=2^{b+1}=2^{r}(2^{4q+1}-3)+3\cdot 2^{r}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{4q+1}-3+3\cdot 2^{r}\geq 3(8q+2^{r})=3\rho_{\mathrm{RH}}(d_{1})>3d_{2}.

A direct inspection of the remaining cases
d_{1}=2,4,8
shows that (
5.2
) is fulfilled whenever
(d_{1},d_{2})\notin\{(4,3),(8,6),(8,7)\}
.
∎

6.
A first layer weighted Plancherel estimate

Similar to
[
37
,
38
]
, the proof of the spectral multiplier estimates of
Theorem

1.1
relies on weighted Plancherel estimates, which are exploited to turn support conditions of the involved convolution kernels into some sort of rapid decay.

Proposition 6.1
.

Suppose that
G
is a Métivier group with layers of dimensions
d_{1}=\dim\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
d_{2}=\dim\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. If
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is a bounded Borel function supported in a compact subset
A\subseteq(0,\infty)
and
\chi:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
is some bump function, the convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}_{F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)}
of
F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)
satisfies

(6.1)

\int_{G}\big||x|^{\alpha}\mathcal{K}_{F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)}(x,u)\big|^{2}\,d(x,u)\leq C_{A,\chi,\alpha}2^{\ell(2\alpha-d_{2})}\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

for all
\alpha\geq 0
and
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 6.2
.

It should be pointed out that
6.1
is a bound in terms of the
L^{2}
-norm of the multiplier
F
, which is crucial for the proof of
Proposition

7.1
and one of the main reasons why passing to the setting of Métivier groups. Reproducing such an estimate beyond Métivier groups would require replacing
\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}}
by some Sobolev norm of
F
, since multiplication by some weight
|x|^{\alpha}
would then correspond to taking derivatives of the multiplier
F
, as the formula
3.3
for the convolution kernel suggests. See also
[
28
, Theorem 2.7]
for a similar estimate.

Proof.

Via interpolation, it suffices to prove (
6.1
) for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
. We use the notation of
Proposition

3.1
. By
Proposition

3.2
and Plancherel’s theorem, the left-hand side of (
6.1
) equals a constant times

\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}|x|^{2\alpha}\bigg|\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}F(\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu})\chi(2^{\ell}|\mu|)\prod_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{k_{n}}{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}(R_{\mu}^{-1}P_{n}^{\mu}x)\bigg|^{2}\,dx\,d\mu,

where
R_{\mu}\in O(d_{1})
, and

\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(2k_{n}+r_{n}\right)b_{n}^{\mu},\quad\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}.

Recall that the orthogonal projections
P^{\mu}_{n}
of
Proposition

3.1
satisfy

P_{n}^{\mu}R_{\mu}=R_{\mu}P_{n}\quad\text{for all }\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}\text{ and }n\in\{0,\dots,N\},

where
P_{n}
denotes the projection from
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}=\mathbb{R}^{r_{0}}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2r_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2r_{N}}
onto the
n
-th layer. Subsitutiting
y=R_{\mu}^{-1}x
, we see that the above expression equals

\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}|y|^{2\alpha}\bigg|\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}F(\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu})\chi(2^{\ell}|\mu|)\prod_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{k_{n}}^{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}(P_{n}y)\bigg|^{2}\,dy\,d\mu.

Hence, expanding

|y|^{2\alpha}=(|P_{1}y|^{2}+\dots+|P_{N}y|^{2})^{\alpha}

shows that it is sufficient to show that every term of the form

(6.2)

\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}\bigg|\bigg(\prod_{n=1}^{N}|P_{n}y|^{m_{n}}\bigg)\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}F(\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu})\chi(2^{\ell}|\mu|)\prod_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{k_{n}}^{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}(P_{n}y)\bigg|^{2}\,dx\,d\mu

with
m_{1},\dots,m_{N}\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
m_{1}+\dots+m_{N}=\alpha
is bounded by the right-hand side of (
6.1
).
For
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
and
\lambda>0
, let

H^{(\lambda)}_{\mathbb{R}^{2m}}=-\Delta_{z}+\tfrac{\lambda^{2}}{4}|z|^{2}

denote the (rescaled) Hermite operator on
\mathbb{R}^{2m}
. By
[
5
, Proposition 3.3]
(or alternatively
[
17
]
), the Hermite operator satisfies the sub-elliptic estimate

(6.3)

\left\lVert|\cdot|^{\beta}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2m})}\lesssim\lambda^{-\beta}\big\|\big(H_{\mathbb{R}^{2m}}^{(\lambda)}\big)^{\beta/2}f\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2m})},\quad\beta\geq 0.

By Equations (1.3.25) and (1.3.42) of
[
45
]
, the Laguerre functions
\smash{\varphi_{k}^{(\lambda,m)}}
are eigenfunctions of the
\lambda
-rescaled Hermite operator, that is,

H^{(\lambda)}_{\mathbb{R}^{2m}}\varphi_{k}^{(\lambda,m)}=\left(2k+m\right)\lambda\,\varphi_{k}^{(\lambda,m)},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

Using the sub-elliptic estimate (
6.3
) on every block of
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}=\mathbb{R}^{2r_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2r_{N}}
, the expanded term
6.2
can thus be dominated by a constant times

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}F(\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu})\chi(2^{\ell}|\mu|)

(6.4)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{n=1}^{N}\left(b_{n}^{\mu}\right)^{-m_{n}}\left(\left(2k_{n}+r_{n}\right)b_{n}^{\mu}\right)^{m_{n}/2}\varphi_{k_{n}}^{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}(P_{n}y)\bigg|^{2}\,dy\,d\mu.

Since
G
is a Métivier group, the endomorphism
J_{\mu}
given by

\langle J_{\mu}x,x^{\prime}\rangle=\mu([x,x^{\prime}]),\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}

is invertible for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
. By
Proposition

3.1
, since
b_{1}^{\mu},\dots,b_{N}^{\mu}
are the non-negative eigenvalues of
iJ_{\mu}
, this necessarily means that
b_{n}^{\mu}\neq 0
for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
. Thus, the image
B
of the unit sphere
S^{d_{2}-1}
under the continuous map
\mu\mapsto\textbf{b}^{\mu}=(b_{1}^{\mu},\dots,b_{N}^{\mu})
is a compact subset of
(0,\infty)^{N}
. In particular,

(6.5)

b_{n}^{\mu}\sim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}1\quad\text{for all }\mu\in S^{d_{2}-1}\text{ and }n\in\{1,\dots,N\}.

Thus, if
\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu}\in\supp F
and
2^{\ell}|\mu|\in\supp\chi
, then

\left(2k_{n}+r_{n}\right)b_{n}^{\mu}\leq\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu}\lesssim_{A}1

and, since the functions
\mu\mapsto b_{n}^{\mu}
are homogeneous of degree 1 by
Proposition

3.1
,

b_{n}^{\mu}=\left|\mu\right|b_{n}^{\bar{\mu}}\sim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}|\mu|\sim 2^{-\ell},\quad\text{where }\bar{\mu}:=\mu/|\mu|.

Hence, using orthogonality on
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
,
6.4
can be estimated by a constant times

(6.6)

(2^{\ell})^{2\alpha}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}|F(\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu})\chi(2^{\ell}|\mu|)|^{2}\prod_{n=1}^{N}\big\|\varphi_{k_{n}}^{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2r_{n}})}^{2}\,d\mu.

By Theorem 1.3.2 and Equation (1.3.42) of
[
45
]
, we have

\displaystyle\big\|\varphi_{k_{n}}^{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2r_{n}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\sim\left(b_{n}^{\mu}\right)^{r_{n}}\binom{k_{n}+r_{n}-1}{k_{n}}

\displaystyle\sim\left(b_{n}^{\mu}\right)^{r_{n}}\left(k_{n}+1\right)^{r_{n}-1}\lesssim b_{n}^{\mu}.

Thus, (
6.6
) can be bounded by a constant times

(6.7)

(2^{\ell})^{2\alpha}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}|F(\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu})\chi(2^{\ell}|\mu|)|^{2}\prod_{n=1}^{N}b_{n}^{\mu}\,d\mu.

We rewrite the integral in
6.7
in polar coordinates, that is,

\mu=\rho\omega\quad\text{for }\rho\in[0,\infty)\text{ and }|\omega|=1.

Then, since
\mu\mapsto\textbf{b}^{\mu}
is homogeneous of degree 1, we have
\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu}=\rho\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\omega}
. Thus, using
\rho=|\mu|\sim 2^{-\ell}
,
6.7
is bounded by a constant times

2^{-\ell(2\alpha+d_{2}+N)}\int_{S^{d_{2}-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}\left|F(\rho\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\omega})\chi(2^{\ell}\rho)\right|^{2}\prod_{n=1}^{N}b_{n}^{\omega}\,\frac{d\rho}{\rho}\,d\sigma(\omega).

Substituting
\rho=(\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\omega})^{-1}\lambda
in the inner integral, we see that the above term equals

2^{-\ell(2\alpha+d_{2}+N)}\int_{S^{d_{2}-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}\left|F(\lambda)\chi(2^{\ell}(\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\omega})^{-1}\lambda)\right|^{2}\prod_{n=1}^{N}b_{n}^{\omega}\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\,d\sigma(\omega)

If
2^{\ell}(\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\omega})^{-1}\lambda\in\supp\chi
, then
(2k_{n}+r_{n})b_{n}^{\omega}\leq\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\omega}\sim 2^{\ell}\lambda
. Thus,
k_{n}\lesssim 2^{\ell}\lambda(b_{n}^{\omega})^{-1}
for the non-vanishing summands in the above sum over
k
. Hence, the above term is bounded by a constant times

2^{-\ell(2\alpha+d_{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|F(\lambda)\right|^{2}\lambda^{N}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda},

which is comparable to
2^{-\ell d_{2}}\|F\|^{2}_{2}
since
F
is compactly supported.
∎

7.
Proof of the reduction of
Theorem

1.1

In this section we prove
Theorem

1.1
using the reduction of
Section

4
. However, in the case
(d_{1},d_{2})=(4,3)
, the arguments of this section only yield results in the range
1\leq p\leq 6/5
. We will improve this range to
1\leq p\leq 4/3
in the next section.

Recall that
Proposition

5.1
asserts that the dimensions
d_{1}=\dim\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
d_{2}=\dim\mathfrak{g}_{2}
of the stratification
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
of the Lie algebra of
G
satisfy
d_{1}>3d_{2}/2
whenever
G
is a Métivier group, except for the cases where

(d_{1},d_{2})\in\{(4,3),(8,6),(8,7)\}.

Apart from these three exceptional cases, the arguments of this section yield spectral multiplier estimates for the range
1\leq p\leq p_{d_{2}}=2(d_{2}+1)/(d_{2}+3)
. In the exceptional cases, we also get results, but only for smaller ranges of
p
. To take this numerology into account, we let
\bar{p}_{d_{1},d_{2}}=p_{d_{2}}
for
(d_{1},d_{2})\notin\{(4,3),(8,6),(8,7)\}
,
\bar{p}_{4,3}=6/5
,
\bar{p}_{8,6}=17/12
, and
\bar{p}_{8,7}=14/11
. Then, in view of
Corollary

4.5
, to prove
Theorem

1.1
for
(d_{1},d_{2})\neq(4,3)
, it suffices to show the following:

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
G
be a Métivier group with layers of dimension
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
. Suppose that
1\leq p\leq\bar{p}_{d_{1},d_{2}}
. If
s>d\left(1/p-1/2\right)
, then there exists some
\varepsilon>0
such that

\|F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})\|_{p\to p}\leq C_{p,s}2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\quad\text{for all }\iota\in\mathbb{N}

and any even bounded Borel function
F\in L^{2}_{s}
supported in
[-2,-1/2]\cup[1/2,2]
.

Here we use again the notation
F^{(\iota)}=(\hat{F}\chi_{\iota})^{\vee}=F*\chi_{\iota}^{\vee}
of
4.2
. A key tool for proving
Proposition

7.1
is the finite propagation speed discussed in
Section

4
. Let
d_{\mathrm{CC}}
denote again the Carnot–Carathéodory distance associated with the vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
giving rise to the sub-Laplacian
L=-(X_{1}^{2}+\dots+X_{d_{1}}^{2})
. If
F
is an even function, then so is its Fourier transform
\smash{\hat{F}}
. Since
\chi_{\iota}
is also even, the Fourier inversion formula yields

F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})f=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{2^{\iota-1}\leq|\tau|\leq 2^{\iota+1}}\chi_{\iota}(\tau)\hat{F}(\tau)\cos(\tau\sqrt{L})f\,d\tau.

Suppose that
f
is supported in a Carnot–Carathéodory ball
B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(x,u)
with radius
R=2^{\iota}
. Then, since
L
has the finite propagation speed property with respect to
d_{\mathrm{CC}}
, the above identify shows that
F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})f
is supported in the ball
B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(x,u)
, which means that the convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}
associated with the operator
F^{(\iota)}(L)
must be supported in the Carnot–Carathéodory ball
B_{2R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0,0)
centered at the origin. On the other hand, by
4.7
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(7.1)

B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)\subseteq B_{CR}(0)\times B_{CR^{2}}(0)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}.

In particular, this means that the convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}
is supported in a Euclidean ball of size
R\times R^{2}
, up to constants.

Let
U_{1},\dots,U_{d_{2}}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, and
U=|\textbf{U}|^{1/2}
, where
U
is the vector of differential operators
\textbf{U}=(-iU_{1},\dots,-iU_{d_{2}})
. Let
\chi
be the bump function of
4.1
that gives rise to the dyadic decomposition
(\chi_{\iota})_{\iota\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.
When passing to the operators

F^{(\iota)}_{\ell}(L,U)=(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{L})\chi(2^{\ell}U),

where
\psi
is some bump function supported away from the origin, the following phenomenon occurs. By
Proposition

3.2
, we know that the convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}_{\ell}
of
F^{(\iota)}_{\ell}(L,U)
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}_{\ell}(x,u)=(2\pi)^{-d_{2}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}(F^{(\iota)}\psi)\big(\sqrt{\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu}}\big)\chi(2^{\ell}|\mu|)

\displaystyle\times\bigg[\prod_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{k_{n}}^{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}(R_{\mu}^{-1}P_{n}^{\mu}x)\bigg]e^{i\langle\mu,u\rangle}\,d\mu,

where

\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(2k_{n}+r_{n}\right)b_{n}^{\mu},\quad\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}.

Note that
\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu}\sim 1
and
|\mu|\sim 2^{-\ell}
on the support of the involved multipliers. As observed in
6.5
, since
G
is a Métivier group, we have
b_{n}^{\mu}\sim 1
for
|\mu|=1
. Since the functions
\mu\mapsto b_{n}^{\mu}
are homogeneous of degree
1
, the sum above ranges in fact over all
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
satisfying

|\textbf{k}|+1\sim\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\bar{\mu}}=|\mu|^{-1}\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu}\sim|\mu|^{-1}\sim 2^{\ell},\quad\text{where }\bar{\mu}=\frac{\mu}{|\mu|}.

Thus, the estimates of
[
45
, Lemma 1.5.3]
show that the rescaled Laguerre functions
\smash{\varphi_{k_{n}}^{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}}
are essentially supported in a ball of radius comparable to

|\mu|^{-1/2}(k_{n}+1)^{1/2}\lesssim 2^{\ell}

centered at the origin, up to some exponentially decaying term. Hence, we can think of the convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}_{\ell}
as being essentially supported in a ball of dimensions comparable to
2^{\ell}\times R^{2}
instead of
R\times R^{2}
, which is a smaller set if
\ell\leq\iota
.

In the next lemma, we make the above argument more precise and show that we can essentially assume that the above convolution kernel is supported in a ball of dimensions comparable to
R_{\ell}R^{\gamma}\times R^{2}
, where
R_{\ell}=2^{\ell}
. The parameter
\gamma>0
is crucial to prove the rapid decay, and will be chosen sufficiently small later in the proof of
Proposition

7.1
. The constant
C>0
in the following statement is given by
7.1
above.

Lemma 7.2
.

Suppose that
1\leq p<2
. Consider the radii
R=2^{\iota}
and
R_{\ell}=2^{\ell}
for
\iota\in\mathbb{N}
and
\ell\in\{-\ell_{0},\dots,\iota\}
. Suppose that
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is a bounded Borel function supported in a compact subset
A\subseteq(0,\infty)
and
\chi:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
is some bump function. Then, for every
\gamma>0
, if
f\in L^{p}(G)\cap L^{2}(G)
is a function supported in the ball

B_{0}^{(\ell)}=B_{CR_{\ell}}(x_{0}^{(\ell)})\times B_{CR^{2}}(0)\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{1}\times\mathfrak{g}_{2},

then the
L^{p}
-norm of
\mathbf{1}_{B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)f
is rapidly decaying in
R
outside the ball

\tilde{B}_{0}^{(\ell)}=B_{2CR_{\ell}R^{\gamma}}(x_{0}^{(\ell)})\times B_{9CR^{2}}(0)\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{1}\times\mathfrak{g}_{2}.

That is, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a constant
C_{N,\gamma}>0
such that

(7.2)

\big\|\mathbf{1}_{(\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{B}^{(\ell)}_{0})\cap B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)f\big\|_{p}\leq C_{N,\gamma}R^{-N}\|F\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{p}.

Remark 7.3
.

An inspection of the following proof shows that the norm
\|F\|_{\infty}
in
7.2
could actually be replaced by the norm
\|F\|_{q}
with
1/q=1/p-1/2
. However, this is not significant for our purposes, since, up multiplication by some bump function
\psi
, we will use
7.2
for the multiplier
F^{(\iota)}
, in which case we have
\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{\infty}\lesssim R^{\alpha}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}
for
\alpha>1/2
by the Sobolev embedding theorem.

Proof.

We interpolate between an
L^{1}
and an
L^{2}
-estimate using the Riesz–Thorin interpolation theorem. The rapid decay comes from the
L^{1}
-estimate, while for the
L^{2}
-estimate, we neglect all cut-off functions and just use the trivial estimate

(7.3)

\big\|\mathbf{1}_{(\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{B}^{(\ell)}_{0})\cap B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)f\big\|_{2}\lesssim_{\chi}\|F\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{2}.

To prove the
L^{1}
-estimate, we consider the sets

A_{\ell}(x^{\prime}):=\{(x,u)\in B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0):|x-x^{\prime}|\geq CR_{\ell}R^{\gamma}\},\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}.

Note that a given point
(x,u)\in G
lies in
A_{\ell}(x^{\prime})
whenever

(x,u)\in(\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{B}^{(\ell)}_{0})\cap B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)\quad\text{and}\quad(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\in\supp f.

By
Proposition

3.2
, we can write

\displaystyle F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)f(x,u)=f*\mathcal{K}_{\ell}(x,u)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}f(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\mathcal{K}_{\ell}\big((x^{\prime},u^{\prime})^{-1}(x,u)\big)\,d(x^{\prime},u^{\prime}),\quad(x,u)\in G,

where
\mathcal{K}_{\ell}
denotes the convolution kernel of
F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)
. Let

\kappa_{\gamma}(x^{\prime},u^{\prime}):=\int_{A_{\ell}(x^{\prime})}\big|\mathcal{K}_{\ell}\big((x^{\prime},u^{\prime})^{-1}(x,u)\big)\big|\,d(x,u).

Then, using Fubini’s theorem, we get

\displaystyle\big\|\mathbf{1}_{(\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{B}^{(\ell)}_{0})\cap B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)f\big\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{G}\mathbf{1}_{(\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{B}^{(\ell)}_{0})\cap B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}(x,u)\,\big|f*\mathcal{K}_{\ell}(x,u)\big|\,d(x,u)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\supp f}\int_{A_{\ell}(x^{\prime})}\big|f(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\big|\,\big|\mathcal{K}_{\ell}\big((x^{\prime},u^{\prime})^{-1}(x,u)\big)\big|\,d(x,u)\,d(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})

(7.4)

\displaystyle=\int_{\supp f}\big|f(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\big|\,\kappa_{\gamma}(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\,d(x^{\prime},u^{\prime}).

Recall that
|B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)|\sim R^{Q}
by
4.9
, where
Q=d_{1}+2d_{2}
denotes the homogeneous dimension of
G
. Given
N\in\mathbb{N}
, using that
|x-x^{\prime}|\geq CR_{\ell}R^{\gamma}
for
(x,u)\in A_{\ell}(x^{\prime})
and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we get

\displaystyle\kappa_{\gamma}(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{A_{\ell}(x^{\prime})}\big|\mathcal{K}_{\ell}\big((x^{\prime},u^{\prime})^{-1}(x,u)\big)\big|\,d(x,u)

\displaystyle\lesssim(R_{\ell}R^{\gamma})^{-N}\int_{A_{\ell}(x^{\prime})}\big||x-x^{\prime}|^{N}\mathcal{K}_{\ell}\big(x-x^{\prime},u-u^{\prime}-\tfrac{1}{2}[x^{\prime},x]\big)\big|\,d(x,u)

(7.5)

\displaystyle\lesssim(R_{\ell}R^{\gamma})^{-N}R^{Q/2}\bigg(\int_{G}\big||x|^{N}\mathcal{K}_{\ell}(x,u)\big|^{2}\,d(x,u)\bigg)^{1/2}.

By
Proposition

6.1
, the second factor of
7.5
can be estimated by

\int_{G}\big||x|^{N}\mathcal{K}_{\ell}(x,u)\big|^{2}\,d(x,u)\lesssim_{N}R_{\ell}^{2N-d_{2}}\|F\|_{2}^{2}.

Plugging this estimate into
7.5
yields

\kappa_{\gamma}(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\lesssim_{N}R^{-\gamma N+Q/2}R_{\ell}^{-d_{2}/2}\|F\|_{2}.

Altogether, with
7.4
, we end up with

(7.6)

\big\|\mathbf{1}_{(\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{B}^{(\ell)}_{0})\cap B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)f\big\|_{1}\lesssim_{N}R^{-\gamma N+Q/2}R_{\ell}^{-d_{2}/2}\lVert F\rVert_{2}\lVert f\rVert_{1}.

Using the Riesz–Thorin interpolation theorem together with
7.6
and
7.3
and choosing
N=N(\gamma)\in\mathbb{N}
in
7.6
sufficiently large yields
7.2
.
∎

We now prove the spectral multiplier estimate of
Proposition

7.1
and thus
Theorem

1.1
, except for the case
(d_{1},d_{2})=(4,3)
, which will be treated in the next section. If
(d_{1},d_{2})\notin\{(4,3),(8,6),(8,7)\}
, it suffices to use the weaker estimate

(7.7)

\left\lVert F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)\right\rVert_{p\to 2}\leq C_{p,\chi}2^{-\ell d_{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{2^{\ell},2},\quad\ell\in\mathbb{Z},

which follows directly from
Theorem

2.1
by using
\|F\|_{2}\leq\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{2^{\ell},2}
. The full strength of
Theorem

2.1
is only needed for the exceptional cases.

Proof of
Proposition

7.1
.

Let
\iota\in\mathbb{N}
and
R=2^{\iota}
. We first argue as in Step (1) and (2) of the proof of Proposition 7.1 in
[
38
]
.

(1)
Reduction to compactly supported functions.
Let
f\in D(G)
be an integrable simple function on
G
. We first show that we may restrict to the case where
f
is supported in
B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)
. Since the metric space
(G,{d_{\mathrm{CC}}})
endowed with the Lebesgue measure is a space of homogeneous type and separable, we may choose a decomposition into disjoint sets
B_{j}\subseteq B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(x^{(j)},u^{(j)})
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
(x^{(j)},u^{(j)})\in G
such that for every
\lambda\geq 1
, the number of overlapping dilated balls
B_{\lambda R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(x^{(j)},u^{(j)})
is bounded by a constant
C(\lambda)\sim\lambda^{Q}
, which is independent of
\iota
. We decompose
f
as

f=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}f_{j}\quad\text{where }f_{j}:=f|_{B_{j}}.

Since
F
is even, so is
\hat{F}
. As
\chi_{\iota}
is even as well, the Fourier inversion formula provides

F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})f_{j}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{2^{\iota-1}\leq|\tau|\leq 2^{\iota+1}}\chi_{\iota}(\tau)\hat{F}(\tau)\cos(\tau\sqrt{L})f_{j}\,d\tau.

Since
L
satisfies the finite propagation speed property,
F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})f_{j}
is supported in
B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(x^{(j)},u^{(j)})
by the formula above. Together with the bounded overlap of these balls, we obtain

\lVert F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})f\rVert_{p}^{p}\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\|F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})f_{j}\|_{p}^{p}.

Altogether, since
L
is left-invariant, it suffices to show

(7.8)

\big\|\textbf{1}_{B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})f\big\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\lVert f\rVert_{p}

whenever our initial function
f\in D(G)
is supported in
B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)
.

(2)
Localizing the multiplier.
Next we show that we may replace the multiplier
F^{(\iota)}
by
F^{(\iota)}\psi
, where
\psi
is a smooth cut-off function which is compactly supported away from the origin. Using the dyadic decomposition
(\chi_{\iota})_{\iota\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
4.1
, we put

\psi:=\sum_{j=-2}^{2}\chi_{j}.

Then
1/8\leq|\lambda|\leq 8
whenever
\lambda\in\supp\psi
, and
|\lambda|\notin(1/4,4)
if
\lambda\in\supp(1-\psi)
. We decompose
F^{(\iota)}
as

F^{(\iota)}=F^{(\iota)}\psi+F^{(\iota)}(1-\psi).

The second part of this decomposition can be treated by the Mikhlin–Hörmander type result of
[
7
]
and
[
30
]
. Note that
F^{(\iota)}=F*\check{\chi}_{\iota}
, and
\check{\chi}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. Thus, given
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\Big(\frac{d}{d\lambda}\Big)^{\alpha}F^{(\iota)}(\lambda)\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\Big(\frac{d}{d\lambda}\Big)^{\alpha}\int_{-2}^{2}2^{\iota}F(\tau)\check{\chi}(2^{\iota}(\lambda-\tau))\,d\tau\Big|

(7.9)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N}2^{\iota(\alpha+1)}\int_{-2}^{2}\frac{|F(\tau)|}{(1+2^{\iota}|\lambda-\tau|)^{N}}\,d\tau.

Since
F
is supported in
[-2,-1/2]\cup[1/2,2]
, choosing

in

gives

\Big|\Big(\frac{d}{d\lambda}\Big)^{\alpha}F^{(\iota)}(\lambda)\Big|\lesssim 2^{-\iota}\min\{|\lambda|^{-\alpha},1\}\lVert F\rVert_{2}\quad\text{whenever }|\lambda|\notin(1/4,4).

This implies

\lVert F^{(\iota)}(1-\psi)\rVert_{L^{2}_{Q/2+1,\mathrm{sloc}}}\lesssim_{\psi}2^{-\iota}\lVert F\rVert_{2}.

Hence, the Mikhlin–Hörmander type result of
[
7
]
and
[
30
]
yields

\|(F^{(\iota)}(1-\psi))(\sqrt{L})\|_{p\to p}\lesssim 2^{-\iota}\lVert F\rVert_{2}.

Thus, instead of
7.8
, we are left proving

(7.10)

\big\|\textbf{1}_{B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{L})f\big\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\lVert f\rVert_{p}

for all
f\in D(G)
that are supported in
B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)
.

(3)
Truncation along the spectrum of
U
.
We decompose
(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{L})
by a dyadic decomposition of
U
. For
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
be given by

F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(\lambda,\rho)=(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{\lambda})\chi(2^{\ell}\rho)\quad\text{for }\lambda\geq 0

and
F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(\lambda,\rho)=0
else. Moreover, as observed in
Remark

2.2
, there is a natural number
\ell_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
depending only on the matrices
J_{\mu}
and the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\mathfrak{g}
such that

\sum_{\ell\geq-\ell_{0}}F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)f=F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})f.

We decompose the function on the left-hand side of (
7.10
) as

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{L})f

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}\bigg(\sum_{\ell=-\ell_{0}}^{\iota}+\sum_{\ell=\iota+1}^{\infty}\bigg)F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)f.

(4)
The case
\ell>\iota
.
The second part of the decomposition can be directly treated by the restriction type estimate
7.7
, which yields

(7.11)

\|F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)\|_{p\to 2}\lesssim 2^{-\ell d_{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\|(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{\cdot}\,)\|_{2^{\ell},2}.

By
2.2
, using that
\psi
is compactly supported away from the origin, we get

(7.12)

\|(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{\cdot}\,)\|_{2^{\ell},2}\lesssim_{\tilde{s}}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{2}+2^{-\ell\tilde{s}}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{L^{2}_{\tilde{s}}}\quad\text{for every }\tilde{s}>1/2.

Recall that
|B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)|\sim R^{Q}
by (
4.9
). Hence, using Hölder’s inequality with
1/q=1/p-1/2
, as well as
7.11
and
7.12
, we get

\displaystyle\bigg\|\mathbf{1}_{B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}\sum_{\ell=\iota+1}^{\infty}F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)f\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim R^{Q/q}\sum_{\ell=\iota+1}^{\infty}\|F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{\iota Q/q}\sum_{\ell=\iota+1}^{\infty}2^{-\ell d_{2}/q}\big(\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{2}+2^{-\ell\tilde{s}}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{L^{2}_{\tilde{s}}}\big)\lVert f\rVert_{p}.

Summation of the geometric series shows that the last term is bounded by a constant depending on
\psi
times

2^{\iota d/q}\big(\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}+2^{-\iota\tilde{s}}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{\tilde{s}}}\big)\lVert f\rVert_{p},

which in turn is bounded by
2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{L^{2}_{\tilde{s}}}\lVert f\rVert_{p}
if we choose
\varepsilon\in(0,s-d/q)
.

(5)
The case
\ell\leq\iota
.
We are done once we have shown

(7.13)

\bigg\|\mathbf{1}_{B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}\sum_{\ell=-\ell_{0}}^{\iota}F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)f\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\lVert f\rVert_{p}.

We will use the rapid decay in
R
from
Lemma

7.2
for this. Let
C>0
be the constant from
7.1
, that is,

B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)\subseteq B_{CR}(0)\times B_{CR^{2}}(0).

Given
\ell\in\{-\ell_{0},\dots,\iota\}
, we split the Euclidean ball
B_{CR}(0)\times B_{CR^{2}}(0)
into a grid with respect to the first layer, which yields a decomposition of
\supp f\subseteq B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)
such that

\supp f=\bigcup_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}B_{m}^{(\ell)},

where
B_{m}^{(\ell)}\subseteq B_{CR_{\ell}}(x_{m}^{(\ell)})\times B_{CR^{2}}(0)
are disjoint subsets, and
|x_{m}^{(\ell)}-x_{m^{\prime}}^{(\ell)}|>R_{\ell}/2
for
m\neq m^{\prime}
. Given
\gamma>0
, the number of overlapping balls

\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}:=B_{2CR_{\ell}R^{\gamma}}(x_{m}^{(\ell)})\times B_{9CR^{2}}(0),\quad 1\leq m\leq M_{\ell}

can be bounded by a constant
N_{\gamma}\lesssim_{\iota}1
(which is independent of
\ell
), where the notation

(7.14)

A\lesssim_{\iota}B

means that
A\leq R^{C(p,d_{1},d_{2})\gamma}B
for some constant
C(p,d_{1},d_{2})>0
depending only on the parameters
p,d_{1},d_{2}
. We decompose the function
f
as

f=\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}}.

and write the left-hand side of
7.13
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=-\ell_{0}}^{\iota}F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)f

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=-\ell_{0}}^{\iota}\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}\Big(\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}}+\mathbf{1}_{\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}}\Big)\mathbf{1}_{B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)(f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}}).

Since
M_{\ell}\sim(R/R_{\ell})^{d_{1}}\lesssim R^{d_{1}}
, the second summands are negligible thanks to the rapid decay from
Lemma

7.2
and the Sobolev embedding discussed in
Remark

7.3
. Hence, it suffices to show

(7.15)

\bigg\|\sum_{\ell=-\ell_{0}}^{\iota}\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}\cap B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)(f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}})\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\lVert f\rVert_{p}.

This means on a formal level that we may indeed assume that the convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}
of
F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)
is supported in a ball of size
R_{\ell}R^{\gamma}\times R^{2}
.

As an abbreviation, we write

g_{m}^{(\ell)}=\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}\cap B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)(f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}}).

Using Hölder’s inequality for the sums over
\ell
and
m
and the bounded overlapping property of the balls
\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}
, we obtain

(7.16)

\bigg\|\sum_{\ell=-\ell_{0}}^{\iota}\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}g_{m}^{(\ell)}\bigg\|_{p}^{p}\lesssim_{\iota}(\iota+1+\ell_{0})^{p-1}\sum_{\ell=-\ell_{0}}^{\iota}\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}\|g_{m}^{(\ell)}\|_{p}^{p}.

(5.a)
The case
(d_{1},d_{2})\notin\{(4,3),(8,6),(8,7)\}
.
Applying Hölder’s inequality with
1/q=1/p-1/2
in combination with the restriction type estimate
7.7
yields

\displaystyle\|g_{m}^{(\ell)}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\iota}(R_{\ell}^{d_{1}}R^{2d_{2}})^{1/q}\big\|F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)(f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}})\big\|_{2}

(7.17)

\displaystyle\lesssim(R_{\ell}^{d_{1}-d_{2}}R^{2d_{2}})^{1/q}\lVert(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{\cdot}\,)\rVert_{2^{\ell},2}\big\|(f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}})\big\|_{p}.

Since
\ell\in\{-\ell_{0},\dots,\iota\}
,
2.2
yields

(7.18)

\lVert(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{\cdot}\,)\rVert_{2^{\ell},2}\lesssim\lVert F^{(\iota)}\psi\rVert_{2}+2^{-\ell\tilde{s}}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\psi\rVert_{L^{2}_{\tilde{s}}}\lesssim_{\psi}\Big(\frac{R}{R_{\ell}}\Big)^{\tilde{s}}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}

for all
\tilde{s}>1/2
. Plugging
7.17
and
7.18
into the right-hand side of (
7.16
) and using the fact that the functions
f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}}
have disjoint support, we get that the left-hand side of
7.15
can be dominated by a constant
C_{\iota}\lesssim_{\iota}1
times

(7.19)

(\iota+1+\ell_{0})^{p-1}\sum_{\ell=-\ell_{0}}^{\iota}\bigg(R_{\ell}^{d_{1}-d_{2}}R^{2d_{2}}\Big(\frac{R}{R_{\ell}}\Big)^{\tilde{s}q}\bigg)^{p/q}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}^{p}\lVert f\rVert_{p}^{p}.

Note that

\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}\sim 2^{-3\varepsilon\iota}R^{-d/q}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}

for
\varepsilon=(s-d/q)/3
. Thus,
7.19
is comparable to

(7.20)

2^{-\varepsilon p\iota}\big(2^{-\varepsilon p\iota}(\iota+1)^{p-1}\big)\sum_{\ell=-\ell_{0}}^{\iota}\bigg(\Big(\frac{R_{\ell}}{R}\Big)^{d_{1}-d_{2}-\tilde{s}q}2^{-\varepsilon q\iota}\bigg)^{p/q}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}^{p}\lVert f\rVert_{p}^{p}.

Choosing
\gamma>0
small enough, we may conclude that the last term is bounded by a constant times
2^{-\varepsilon p\iota}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\lVert f\rVert_{p}
if we find a proper bound for the sum

\sum_{\ell=-\ell_{0}}^{\iota}\bigg(\Big(\frac{R_{\ell}}{R}\Big)^{d_{1}-d_{2}-\tilde{s}q}2^{-\varepsilon q\iota}\bigg)^{p/q}.

Recall that
R=2^{\iota}
and
R_{\ell}=2^{\ell}
. Thus, substituting
\ell-\iota=-k
, we see that this sum is bounded by

(7.21)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\big(2^{-k(d_{1}-d_{2}-\tilde{s}q+\varepsilon q)}\big)^{p/q}.

Since
1\leq p\leq p_{d_{2}}
, we have

(7.22)

\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\geq\frac{d_{2}+3}{2(d_{2}+1)}-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{d_{2}+1}.

Suppose that
d_{1}>3d_{2}/2
. Since
d_{1}-3d_{2}/2\in\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
, this means

d_{1}\geq\frac{3d_{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}.

Thus, together with
7.22
, we obtain

\displaystyle d_{1}-d_{2}-\tilde{s}q+\varepsilon q

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3d_{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}-d_{2}-\tilde{s}(d_{2}+1)+\varepsilon q

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\tilde{s}\Big)(d_{2}+1)+\varepsilon q>0,

if we choose
\tilde{s}>1/2
sufficiently close to
1/2
. Then the geometric series in
7.21
is bounded by a constant independent of
\iota
, and we can bound
7.20
by a constant times
2^{-\varepsilon p\iota}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\lVert f\rVert_{p}
. Thus, by
Proposition

5.1
, we are done with the proof except for the cases where
(d_{1},d_{2})\in\{(4,3),(8,6),(8,7)\}
.

(5.b)
The case
(d_{1},d_{2})\in\{(4,3),(8,6),(8,7)\}
.
We employ the restriction type estimate of
Theorem

2.1
, which yields

(7.23)

\lVert F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)\rVert_{p\to 2}\leq C_{p,\chi}2^{-\ell d_{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\|(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{\cdot}\,)\|_{2}^{1-\theta_{p}}\lVert(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{\cdot}\,)\rVert_{2^{\ell},2}^{\theta_{p}}

where
\theta_{p}\in[0,1]
satisfies
1/p=(1-\theta_{p})+\theta_{p}/p_{d_{2}}
. (Note that
\min\{p_{d_{1}},p_{d_{2}}\}=p_{d_{2}}
.) By
7.18
, the right-hand side of
7.23
is bounded by a constant times

2^{-\ell d_{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\Big(\frac{R}{R_{\ell}}\Big)^{\theta_{p}\tilde{s}}\|F\|_{2}

If we replace
\tilde{s}
by
\theta_{p}\tilde{s}
in the arguments above (keeping
\tilde{s}>1/2
), we see that we can conclude (
7.15
) as long as we can bound, instead of
7.21
, the sum

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\big(2^{-\ell(d_{1}-d_{2}-\theta_{p}\tilde{s}q+\varepsilon q)}\big)^{p/q}.

Similarly as above, by choosing
\tilde{s}>1/2
sufficiently close to
1/2
, the above sum can be bounded from above if

(7.24)

d_{1}-d_{2}-\frac{\theta_{p}}{2}q\geq 0.

Plugging in
\theta_{p}=p_{d_{2}}^{\prime}(1-1/p)
and
1/q=1/p-1/2
(where
p_{d_{2}}^{\prime}
denotes the dual exponent of
p_{d_{2}}^{\prime}
), one directly verifies that, for
1\leq p\leq 2
,
7.24
is equivalent to

(7.25)

1\leq p\leq\frac{p_{d_{2}}^{\prime}+2(d_{1}-d_{2})}{p_{d_{2}}^{\prime}+d_{1}-d_{2}}.

Since

p_{d_{2}}^{\prime}=\frac{2\left(d_{2}+1\right)}{d_{2}-1}=\begin{cases}4&\text{if }d_{2}=3\\
14/5&\text{if }d_{2}=6\\
8/3&\text{if }d_{2}=7,\end{cases}

the right-hand side of
7.25
reads

\frac{p_{d_{2}}^{\prime}+2(d_{1}-d_{2})}{p_{d_{2}}^{\prime}+d_{1}-d_{2}}=\begin{cases}6/5&\text{if }(d_{1},d_{2})=(4,3)\\
17/12&\text{if }(d_{1},d_{2})=(8,6)\\
14/11&\text{if }(d_{1},d_{2})=(8,7),\end{cases}

which concludes the proof of
Proposition

7.1
.
∎

8.
Improvement for the case
(d_{1},d_{2})=(4,3)

In this section, we treat the case where
(d_{1},d_{2})=(4,3)
in the stratification
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
, still working under the assumption that the underlying Lie group
G
is a Métivier group. The goal of this section is to show the following enhancement of
Proposition

7.1
in the case
(d_{1},d_{2})=(4,3)
, which in turn implies the spectral multiplier estimates of
Theorem

1.1
for the stated range
1\leq p\leq p_{3}=4/3
via
Corollary

4.5
.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
G
be a Métivier group with
(d_{1},d_{2})=(4,3)
. Suppose that
1\leq p\leq p_{3}=4/3
. If
s>d\left(1/p-1/2\right)=7\left(1/p-1/2\right)
, then there exists some
\varepsilon>0
such that

\|F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})\|_{p\to p}\leq C_{p,s}2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\quad\text{for all }\iota\in\mathbb{N}

and any even bounded Borel function
F\in L^{2}_{s}
supported in
[-2,-1/2]\cup[1/2,2]
.

Additionally to the first layer weighted Plancherel estimate
6.1
we need the following Plancherel estimate with weight on the second layer.

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
G
be a Métivier group with layers of dimensions
d_{1}=4
and
d_{2}=3
. Then there is a direction
v\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
with
|v|=1
such that, if
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is a smooth function supported in a compact subset
A\subseteq(0,\infty)
and
\chi:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
is some bump function, the convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}_{F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)}
of
F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)
satisfies

(8.1)

\int_{G}\big|\langle u,v\rangle^{\alpha}\mathcal{K}_{F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)}(x,u)\big|^{2}\,d(x,u)\leq C_{A,\chi,\alpha}\,2^{\ell(2\alpha-d_{2})}\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}^{2}

for all
\alpha\geq 0
and
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We first show how the spectral multiplier estimate of
Proposition

8.1
is derived from the weighted Plancherel estimate of
Proposition

8.2
.

8.1.
Proof of
Proposition

8.1

In the proof of
Proposition

7.1
, we used the weighted Plancherel estimates of
Proposition

6.1
to show that the convolution kernel

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}_{\ell}(x,u)=(2\pi)^{-d_{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}(F^{(\iota)}\psi)\big(\sqrt{\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu}}\big)\chi(2^{\ell}|\mu|)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{k_{n}}^{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}(R_{\mu}^{-1}P_{n}^{\mu}x)\,e^{i\langle\mu,u\rangle}\,d\mu

is essentially supported in a ball of size
R_{\ell}\times R^{2}
. On the other hand, if
\omega=\mu/|\mu|
, then
\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu}=\left|\mu\right|\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\omega}
, where
\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\omega}\sim 2^{\ell}=R_{\ell}
on the support of the above integrand. Thus, on a heuristic level, when thinking of using integration by parts in the above integral, the scaling of
\mu
by
\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\omega}\sim R_{\ell}
suggests that one might hope that the convolution kernel is even supported in a ball of size
R_{\ell}\times 2^{\ell}R
in place of
R_{\ell}\times R^{2}
, up to rapid decay. Since derivatives in
\mu
correspond directly to weights in terms of
u
via the Fourier transform, weighted Plancherel estimates are again a good tool to make this idea precise. The following lemma shows that the discussed phenomenon can at least be observed along the direction
v
if the weighted Plancherel estimate
8.1
is available.

Lemma 8.3
.

Suppose that
1\leq p<2
. Consider the radii
R=2^{\iota}
and
R_{\ell}=2^{\ell}
for
\iota\in\mathbb{N}
and
\ell\in\{-\ell_{0},\dots,\iota\}
. Suppose that
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is an even bounded Borel function supported in
[-2,-1/2]\cup[1/2,2]
and
\psi,\chi:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
are bump functions. Let
\gamma>0
. Given
C,\tilde{C}>0
, consider the balls

\displaystyle A^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle=B_{CR_{\ell}}(0)\times B_{\tilde{C}R^{2}}(0)\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{1}\times\mathfrak{g}_{2}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle=B_{2CR_{\ell}R^{\gamma}}(0)\times B_{9\tilde{C}R^{2+\gamma}}(0)\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{1}\times\mathfrak{g}_{2}.

Let
v\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
with
|v|=1
be as in
Proposition

8.2
and
u^{(\ell)}_{0}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
be some point lying on the line along
v
.
Then there is a constant
\bar{C}>0
depending only on
C,\tilde{C}
and the Lie group
G
such that, if
f\in L^{p}(G)\cap L^{2}(G)
is a function supported in the set

A^{(\ell)}(u^{(\ell)}_{0})=\{(x,u)\in A^{(\ell)}:|\langle u-u^{(\ell)}_{0},v\rangle|<\tilde{C}R_{\ell}R\},

then the
L^{p}
-norm of
\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}}(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{L})\chi(2^{\ell}U)f
is rapidly decaying in
R
outside the set

\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}(u^{(\ell)}_{0}):=\{(x,u)\in\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}:|\langle u-u^{(\ell)}_{0},v\rangle|<2\bar{C}R_{\ell}R^{1+\gamma}\}.

That is, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a constant
C_{N,\gamma,\psi,\chi}>0
independent of
u^{(\ell)}_{0}
such that

(8.2)

\big\|\mathbf{1}_{(\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}_{0})\cap\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}}(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{L})\chi(2^{\ell}U)f\big\|_{p}\leq C_{N,\gamma,\psi,\chi}R^{-N}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{p}.

Remark 8.4
.

Note that
\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\smash{\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}}\sim R^{\alpha}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{2}
for
\alpha>1/2
. Hence, due to the rapid decay in
R
in
8.2
, we may also replace
\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{\infty}
by
\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{2}
on the right-hand side of
8.2
.

Proof.

Similar to
Lemma

7.2
, we interpolate between an
L^{1}
and an
L^{2}
-estimate. For the
L^{2}
-estimate, we again just use that

(8.3)

\big\|\mathbf{1}_{(\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}(u_{0}^{(\ell)}))\cap\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}}(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{L})\chi(2^{\ell}U)f\big\|_{2}\lesssim_{\psi,\chi}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{2}.

For the
L^{1}
-estimate, we exploit the weighted Plancherel estimate
8.1
. We consider the sets

\bar{A}_{\ell}(u^{\prime}):=\{(x,u)\in\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}:|\langle u-u^{\prime},v\rangle|\geq\bar{C}R_{\ell}R^{1+\gamma}\},\quad u^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}.

Then, if the points
(x,u),(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\in G
satisfy

(x,u)\in(\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}(u_{0}^{(\ell)}))\cap\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}\quad\text{and}\quad(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\in\supp f,

we have

|\langle u-u_{0}^{(\ell)},v\rangle|\geq 2\bar{C}R_{\ell}R^{1+\gamma}\quad\text{and}\quad|\langle u^{\prime}-u_{0}^{(\ell)},v\rangle|<\tilde{C}R_{\ell}R,

and, if we choose
\bar{C}\geq\tilde{C}
, in particular
(x,u)\in\bar{A}_{\ell}(u^{\prime})
.
Using again
Proposition

3.2
, we write

\displaystyle(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{L})\chi(2^{\ell}U)f(x,u)=f*\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(x,u)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}f(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}\big((x^{\prime},u^{\prime})^{-1}(x,u)\big)\,d(x^{\prime},u^{\prime}),\quad(x,u)\in G.

Hence, by Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\big\|\mathbf{1}_{(\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}(u_{0}^{(\ell)}))\cap\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}}(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{L})\chi(2^{\ell}U)f\big\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{G}\mathbf{1}_{(\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}(u_{0}^{(\ell)}))\cap\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}}(x,u)|f*\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(x,u)|\,d(x,u)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\supp f}|f(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})|\int_{\bar{A}_{\ell}(u^{\prime})}\big|\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}\big((x^{\prime},u^{\prime})^{-1}(x,u)\big)\big|\,d(x,u)\,d(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\lVert f\rVert_{1}\sup_{(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\in A^{(\ell)}}\int_{\bar{A}_{\ell}(u^{\prime})}\big|\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}\big((x^{\prime},u^{\prime})^{-1}(x,u)\big)\big|\,d(x,u).

Note that

(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})^{-1}(x,u)=(x-x^{\prime},u-u^{\prime}-\tfrac{1}{2}[x^{\prime},x]).

If
(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\in A^{(\ell)}
and
(x,u)\in\bar{A}_{\ell}(u^{\prime})
, we have

|x^{\prime}|<CR_{\ell}\quad\text{and}\quad|x|<2CR_{\ell}R^{\gamma},

and thus, if we choose the constant
\bar{C}>0
large enough,

\displaystyle|\langle u-u^{\prime}-\tfrac{1}{2}[x^{\prime},x],v\rangle|

\displaystyle\geq|\langle u-u^{\prime},v\rangle|-\tfrac{1}{2}C_{0}|x^{\prime}||x|

\displaystyle\geq\bar{C}R_{\ell}R^{1+\gamma}-C_{0}C^{2}R_{\ell}^{2}R^{\gamma}\gtrsim R_{\ell}R^{1+\gamma},

where
C_{0}>0
is the constant given by

C_{0}:=\sup_{x,x^{\prime}\neq 0}\frac{\left|[x,x^{\prime}]\right|}{|x||x^{\prime}|}.

Hence, if
(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\in A^{(\ell)}
, then

\displaystyle\int_{\bar{A}^{(\ell)}(u^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}\big((x^{\prime},u^{\prime})^{-1}(x,u)\big)\big|\,d(x,u)

\displaystyle\lesssim(R_{\ell}R^{1+\gamma})^{-N}\int_{\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}}|\langle u-u^{\prime}-\tfrac{1}{2}[x^{\prime},x],v\rangle|^{N}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times|\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(x-x^{\prime},u-u^{\prime}-\tfrac{1}{2}[x^{\prime},x])|\,d(x,u)

(8.4)

\displaystyle\leq(R_{\ell}R^{1+\gamma})^{-N}|\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}|^{1/2}\bigg(\int_{G}\big||\langle u,v\rangle|^{N}\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(x,u)\big|^{2}\,d(x,u)\bigg)^{1/2}.

Now the weighted Plancherel estimate
8.1
yields

\bigg(\int_{G}\big||\langle u,v\rangle|^{N}\mathcal{K}_{\ell}(x,u)\big|^{2}\,d(x,u)\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim_{N}R_{\ell}^{N-d_{2}/2}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{N}}.

Hence, the last line of
8.4
is bounded by a constant times

(8.5)

(R^{1+\gamma})^{-N}|\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}|^{1/2}R_{\ell}^{-d_{2}/2}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{N}}.

We use again the notation
\lesssim_{\iota}
introduced in (
7.14
).
Since
|\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}|\lesssim_{\iota}R_{\ell}^{d_{1}}R^{2d_{2}}
and

.
∎

Remark 8.5

Lemma 8.6

Let

Suppose that there exist

\begin{gathered}|D^{\alpha}b_{1}^{\mu}|\leq\kappa b_{1}^{\mu},\dots,|D^{\alpha}b_{N}^{\mu}|\leq\kappa b_{N}^{\mu},\\

for all

and all
\alpha\in\{0,\dots,\alpha_{0}\}
. Suppose further that

and

\times\bigg|F^{(\gamma_{\iota})}\bigg(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(2(k_{n}+s_{n})+r_{n}\right)b_{n}^{\mu}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\,\bigg|\prod_{n=1}^{N}(b^{\mu}_{n})^{1+a^{\iota}_{n}}(k_{n}+1)^{a^{\iota}_{n}}\bigg|\,d\nu_{\iota}(s),

for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}
and all
\alpha\in\{0,\dots,\alpha_{0}\}
, where

is a finite set and, for all

,

•

,
\gamma_{\iota},\delta_{\iota}\in\mathbb{N}
,

,

•

is a Borel probability measure on

,

and where

denotes the
\gamma_{\iota}

.

Before verifying these bounds, we show how the weighted Plancherel estimate (

Proof of
Proposition

8.2
.

Suppose that the bounds

hold with

. We apply

8.6

. In view of

\displaystyle\int_{G}|\langle u,v\rangle^{\alpha}\mathcal{K}_{F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)}(x,u)|^{2}\,d(x,u)

\displaystyle\sim\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}|\partial_{v}^{\alpha}V_{F\otimes\psi}(\xi,\mu)|^{2}\,d\xi\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}|\mu|^{-2\alpha}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}|D^{\alpha}V_{F\otimes\psi}(\xi,\mu)|^{2}\,d\xi\,d\mu.

In the following, we write

(2(\textbf{k}+\textbf{s})+\textbf{r})\cdot\textbf{b}^{\mu}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(2(k_{n}+s_{n})+r_{n}\right)b_{n}^{\mu}.

Then, using

\displaystyle\sum_{\iota\in I_{\alpha}}\int_{R_{\iota}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}|\mu|^{-2\alpha}|D^{\delta_{\iota}}\psi(\mu)|^{2}\big|F^{(\gamma_{\iota})}\big((2(\textbf{k}+\textbf{s})+\textbf{r})\cdot\textbf{b}^{\mu}\big)\big|^{2}

Note that

\mathfrak{su}_{2}
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{su}}_{2}
each with
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, that is,

\mathfrak{so}_{4}\cong\mathfrak{su}_{2}\oplus\widetilde{\mathfrak{su}_{2}}\cong R^{3}_{\xi}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{3}_{\eta}.

We endow
\mathfrak{so}_{4}
with the inner product
\langle J,J^{\prime}\rangle=-\tr(JJ^{\prime})
,
J,J^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{so}_{4}
. Hence, if we let
J(\xi,\eta)=J^{-}(\xi)+J^{+}(\eta)
, then

(8.17)

\langle J(\xi,\eta),J(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\rangle=\langle\xi,\xi^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}+\langle\eta,\eta^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}.

By Proposition 25 of
[
25
]
, the two eigenvalues of
-J(\xi,\eta)^{2}
are given by

b_{1}(\xi,\eta)=\left|\xi\right|+\left|\eta\right|\quad\text{and}\quad b_{2}(\xi,\eta)=\left|\left|\xi\right|-\left|\eta\right|\right|.

If both
\xi
and
\eta
are non-zero, the spectral projections onto the corresponding eigenspaces are given by

(8.18)

\displaystyle P_{1}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\id_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{J^{+}(\eta)}{|\eta|}\frac{J^{-}(\xi)}{|\xi|},

\displaystyle P_{2}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\id_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{J^{+}(\eta)}{|\eta|}\frac{J^{-}(\xi)}{|\xi|}.

We identify the three-dimensional subspace
V\subseteq\mathfrak{so}_{4}
by a subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{\xi}\oplus R^{3}_{\eta}
via the coordinates
\xi,\eta
. Since
G
is assumed to be a Métiver group, the matrices
J(\xi,\eta)
have to be invertible for all
(\xi,\eta)\in V\setminus\{0\}
. Thus,
|\xi|\neq|\eta|
for all
(\xi,\eta)\in V\setminus\{0\}
, since the eigenvalue
b_{2}(\xi,\eta)
would vanish otherwise. This in turn implies

V\cap(\{0\}\oplus\mathbb{R}_{\eta}^{3})=\{0\}\quad\text{or}\quad V\cap(\mathbb{R}_{\xi}^{3}\oplus\{0\})=\{0\}.

(Otherwise, we could find elements
(0,\eta)\in V
and
(\xi,0)\in V
with
\xi,\eta\neq 0
such that
|\xi|=|\eta|
.) Hence
V
can be written as
V=\{(\xi,A\xi):\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\}
or
V=\{(A\eta,\eta):\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\}
for some
3\times 3
matrix
A
. In the former case (the latter case is similar), the eigenvalues of
-J(\xi)^{2}:=-J(\xi,A\xi)^{2}
are given by

(8.19)

\displaystyle b_{1}(\xi)

\displaystyle=b_{1}(\xi,A\xi)=\left|\xi\right|+\left|A\xi\right|,

\displaystyle b_{2}(\xi)

\displaystyle=b_{2}(\xi,A\xi)=\left|\left|\xi\right|-\left|A\xi\right|\right|.

We distinguish the cases
\ker A=\{0\}
,
\ker A=\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\{0\}\neq\ker A\neq\mathbb{R}^{3}
. In the following, we identify points
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
with points
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
by the chosen coordinates.

If
\ker A=\{0\}
, we have the decomposition

-J(\xi)^{2}=b_{1}(\xi)^{2}P_{1}(\xi)+b_{2}(\xi)^{2}P_{2}(\xi)\quad\text{for all }\xi\neq 0,

and both the eigenvalues
b_{j}(\xi)
and spectral projections
P_{j}(\xi)=P_{j}(\xi,A\xi)
in
8.18
are smooth functions of
\xi
away from the origin
\xi=0
. Note that
\xi\mapsto b_{j}(\xi)
and
\xi\mapsto P_{j}(\xi)
are homogeneous of degrees 1 and 0, respectively. Since
b_{j}(\xi)
vanishes only at the origin, the desired bounds
8.13
are a consequence of homogeneity and the fact that all derivatives of
\xi\mapsto b_{j}(\xi)
and
\xi\mapsto P_{j}(\xi)
are smooth functions on the 2-sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{\xi}
, and we may choose the vector
v\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
with
|v|=1
of the directional derivative
\partial_{v}
arbitrarily.

If
\ker A=\mathbb{R}^{3}
, then
8.19
yields
b_{1}(\xi)=b_{2}(\xi)=|\xi|
, and
-J_{\mu}^{2}
is just a multiple of the identity, whence
G
is a even a Heisenberg type group in that case. Both the eigenvalue and the spectral projection (being the identity) are smooth away from the origin, and as in the previous case, we obtain
8.13
by choosing an arbitrary direction
v\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
with
|v|=1
.

Now suppose that
\{0\}\neq\ker A\neq\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then we have the decomposition

-J(\xi)^{2}=b_{1}(\xi)^{2}P_{1}(\xi)+b_{2}(\xi)^{2}P_{2}(\xi)\quad\text{for all }\xi\notin\ker A.

Note that the set
\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\xi\notin\ker A\}
is in particular a Zariski-open subset of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
. Both functions
\xi\mapsto b_{j}(\xi)
and
\xi\mapsto P_{j}(\xi)
are smooth on
\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}
and continuous on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, but their directional derivatives may admit singularities along
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}
if the direction of the derivative intersects this set transversely. However, thanks to fact that the Zariski-closed set
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}
is either a plane or a line, choosing
v\in\ker A\setminus\{0\}
with
|v|=1
, every line pointing in the same direction as
v
either lies completely in the Zariski-closed set
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}
or has no intersection with it, meaning that
\xi\mapsto b_{j}(\xi)
and
\xi\mapsto P_{j}(\xi)
are smooth away from the origin if we only take derivatives along lines with direction
v
.

More precisely, for
v\in\ker A\setminus\{0\}
, since
A(\xi+tv)=A\xi
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
then, the first derivatives of
b_{1}(\xi)
and
b_{2}(\xi)
are given by

\partial_{v}b_{1}(\xi)=\partial_{v}(|\xi|)\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{v}b_{2}(\xi)=\sgn(|\xi|-|A\xi|)\,\partial_{v}(|\xi|),

which are both smooth functions away from the origin since
b_{2}(\xi)=\left|\left|\xi\right|-\left|A\xi\right|\right|\neq 0
for
\xi\neq 0
. Similarly, for the projections
P_{j}(\xi)
, we observe

\partial_{v}\left(\frac{J^{+}(A\xi)}{|A\xi|}\frac{J^{-}(\xi)}{|\xi|}\right)=\frac{J^{+}(A\xi)}{|A\xi|}\,\partial_{v}\frac{J^{-}(\xi)}{|\xi|},

meaning that
v
-derivatives only hit the second factor
J^{-}(\xi)/|\xi|
in
8.18
, which is smooth away from the origin. On the other hand, due to
8.17
,

\left|\frac{J^{+}(A\xi)}{|A\xi|}\right|\leq 1.

Thus, arguing again via smoothness and homogeneity for the factor
J^{-}(\xi)/|\xi|
, we obtain the desired bounds
8.13
.
∎

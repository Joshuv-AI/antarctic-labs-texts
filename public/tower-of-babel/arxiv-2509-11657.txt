Improved Rates for Stochastic Variance-Reduced Difference-of-Convex Algorithms
Anh Duc Nguyen Alp Yurtsever Suvrit Sra Kim-Chuan Toh

Abstract
In this work, we propose and analyze DCA-PAGE, a novel algorithm that integrates the difference-of-convex algorithm (DCA) with the ProbAbilistic Gradient Estimator (PAGE) to solve structured nonsmooth difference-of-convex programs. In the finite-sum setting, our method achieves a gradient computation complexity of 𝒪 ⁡ ( N + N 1 / 2 ​ ε − 2 ) \mathcal{O}(N+N^{1/2}\varepsilon^{-2}) with sample size N N , surpassing the previous best-known complexity of 𝒪 ⁡ ( N + N 2 / 3 ​ ε − 2 ) \mathcal{O}(N+N^{2/3}\varepsilon^{-2}) for stochastic variance-reduced (SVR) DCA methods. Furthermore, DCA-PAGE readily extends to online settings with a similar optimal gradient computation complexity 𝒪 ⁡ ( b + b 1 / 2 ​ ε − 2 ) \mathcal{O}(b+b^{1/2}\varepsilon^{-2}) with batch size b b , a significant advantage over existing SVR DCA approaches that only work for the finite-sum setting. We further refine our analysis with a gap function, which enables us to obtain comparable convergence guarantees under milder assumptions.

V
GAP FUNCTION AND REFINED ANALYSIS

In this section, we will remove the Assumption
5
and show the guarantees for DCA-PAGE with an alternative measure of criticality. Inspired by
[
32
, Definition 1]
, we define the following notion of gap to measure the closeness to the first-order stationary point of (
M
)

Definition 12

(Gap Function)
.

Given
w^{t-1}\in\partial r_{2}(x^{t-1})
, we measure closeness to first-order stationary point with

\displaystyle\operatorname{gap}\left(x^{t}\right)

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}(G+r_{1})\left(x^{t}\right)-(G+r_{1})(x)

\displaystyle-\left\langle\nabla H\left(x^{t-1}\right)+w^{t-1},x^{t}-x\right\rangle.

We can verify that the
\operatorname{gap}
function is nonnegative and equals zero if and only if
x^{t}
is a first-order stationary point. Despite its recent introduction, the gap function has proven to be a more general measure than the critical distance (Definition
6
). The following subsection presents an example that illustrates why the gap function can be more suitable than the critical distance measure for nonsmooth problems.

V-A

Critical Distance Measure versus Gap Function

Modifying from the constrained example in
[
31
, Appendix C]
, we can give a simple unconstrained nonsmooth example where the critical distance measure remain lower-bounded even when approaching the optimum arbitrarily close. Consider
\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|x|
, where
G(x)=|x|
, and
H(x)=r_{1}(x)=r_{2}(x)=0
. Then for any
x^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle d(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dist}\left(\nabla\left(H+r_{2}\right)\left(x^{\prime}\right),\partial\left(G+r_{1}\right)\left(x^{\prime}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\min_{w\in\partial|x|}|0-w|

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\text{when $x^{\prime}=0$}\\
1&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Hence, it is equal
0
only at the global optimum and remains
1
at
every other point
, including those arbitrarily close to the optimum. On the other hand, the gap function is

\displaystyle\operatorname{gap}(x^{\prime})=\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|x^{\prime}|-|x|=|x^{\prime}|,

which goes to
0
as
x^{\prime}
goes to
0
. This example shows how gap function can be a much better measure than the distance to criticality measure for nonsmooth problems.

VII
CONCLUSIONS AND FUTURE WORK

We propose DCA-PAGE which achieves a gradient computation complexity of
\mathcal{O}(N+N^{1/2}\varepsilon^{-2})
in the finite-sum setting, outperforming previous SVR DCA methods. Moreover, our method readily extends to online settings with the same optimal complexity, thereby overcoming a key limitation of earlier approaches. Additionally, by refining our analysis with the gap function, we obtained similar convergence guarantees under milder assumptions.

Future research can further enhance the practical impact of DCA-PAGE by investigating large-scale applications such as nonnegative matrix factorization and phase retrieval. Another potential future direction is to explore the convex subproblem with SVR methods on top of constructing the subproblem with stochastic gradient estimators, as explored in this paper.

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\operatorname{dist}^{2}\left(\nabla H\left(x^{t+1}\right)+\nabla r_{2}\left(x^{t+1}\right),\partial\left(G+r_{1}\right)\left(x^{t+1}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(L+L_{r_{2}}\right)^{2}+L^{2},

. Now we can pick

Thus, we can choose

\displaystyle=\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}G\left(x^{t+1}\right)-G(x)-\left\langle g^{t}+w^{t},x^{t+1}-x\right\rangle+\left\langle g^{t}-\nabla H\left(x^{t}\right),x^{t+1}-x\right\rangle.

Since that

is

-strongly-convex, we have

\displaystyle-\rho_{G+r_{1}}\|x^{t+1}-x\|^{2}/2

\displaystyle\geq(G+r_{1})(x^{t+1})-(G+r_{1})(x)-\langle g^{t}+w^{t},x^{t+1}-x\rangle.

Taking expectation and summing from

, we obtain

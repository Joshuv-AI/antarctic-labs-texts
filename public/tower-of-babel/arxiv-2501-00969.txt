The Landis conjecture for nonlocal elliptic operators: polynomial decay
Sebastián Flores Sepúlveda, Gabrielle Nornberg

Abstract




Abstract.

We obtain a unique continuation result at infinity for fully nonlinear elliptic integro-differential operators of order
2s
which satisfy the maximum and minimum principles in bounded subdomains, under the decay assumption
o(|x|^{-(N+2s)})
at infinity.

Our result is new even in the case of the fractional Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, as it unveils the nonlocal nature of the decay in Landis conjecture, evolving from exponential to polynomial.

Keywords.
Landis conjecture; integro-differential elliptic operators; polynomial decay.

MSC2020.
47G20, 45K05, 35B40, 35D40.

1
Introduction

In
[
17
]
, Landis and Kondratiev ask if an exponentially fast decaying solution to a linear Schrödinger equation in an exterior domain should be the trivial solution. This is known as the Landis conjecture and can be stated in the following manner:

Conjecture 1
.

Let
\Omega
be either
\mathbb{R}^{N}
or an exterior domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\|V\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. If
u
is a solution to

\Delta u+Vu=0\penalty\ \text{ in }\Omega,

with
u(x)=O(e^{-\kappa|x|})
as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
for some
\kappa>1
, then
u\equiv 0
in
\Omega
.

A weaker version of this conjecture claims that if the solution
u
decays faster than exponentially at infinity, that is, if
|u(x)|\leq Ce^{-|x|^{1+\varepsilon}}
for some
C,\varepsilon>0
, then
u\equiv 0
. These questions are a kind of unique continuation principle at infinity.

The conjecture was negatively answered by Meshkov in
[
20
]
, where he showed a complex-valued potential and a nontrivial solution with decay
O(e^{-\kappa|x|^{4/3}})
at infinity, for some
\kappa>0
. He also proved that
4/3
is optimal in the sense that if a solution behaves at infinity as
O(e^{-\kappa|x|^{4/3+\varepsilon}})
for some
\varepsilon>0
, it must be the trivial solution. The question for a real-valued potential has been widely studied in the last years. In
[
19
]
the Landis conjecture in the plane was proven: if a solution decays at infinity with order
O(e^{-\kappa|x|(\log|x|)^{1/2}})
then it must be trivial; see also
[
9
,
10
,
14
,
15
]
.

In higher dimensions, the question remains open. It has been studied in
[
3
]
using Carleman estimates, and in
[
25
]
with comparison arguments. In
[
27
]
, Sirakov and Souplet established a Landis-type result up to unbounded coefficients for fully nonlinear second order operators which satisfy the maximum principle in bounded subdomains. A sharp decay using criticality theory was addressed by Das and Pinchover in
[
8
]
, in which polynomial decay appears in the presence of Hardy type decaying potentials.

The aim of this note is to address an analogous of the Landis conjecture for a class of integro-differential elliptic operators. These operators are defined by

Lu(x)=\PV\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u(x+y)-u(x))K(y)\mathrm{d}y

(1)

where
0<\frac{\lambda}{|y|^{N+2s}}\leq K(y)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{|y|^{N+2s}}
, for some fixed constants
s\in(0,1)
and
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
. In particular, when
\lambda=\Lambda=1
then
L
is just the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
of order
2s
. For
s
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
fixed, we denote
\mathcal{L}
to be the class of all operators
L
in the form (
1
).

The Landis conjecture for the fractional Laplacian has been studied by Rüland and Wang in
[
26
]
, where they conclude a unique continuation principle at infinity for solutions of
(-\Delta)^{s}u+Vu=0
under the assumption

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{|x|^{\alpha}}|u(x)|\mathrm{d}x\leq C<\infty

(2)

for some
\alpha>1
, whenever
V
satisfies a regularity hypothesis given by
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x\cdot DV(x)|\leq 1
. In
[
18
]
, Kow and Wang studied the question for
(-\Delta)^{1/2}u+b\cdot Du+Vu=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and get a decay of
O(e^{-k|x|})
under boundedness of the drift and potential terms, as well as
|DV|
. Note that this case is especially delicate since both
(-\Delta)^{1/2}
and
b\cdot Du
are of order 1. The proof of these results rely on the extension problem for the fractional Laplacian, together with the application of Carleman estimates. Recently, Das, Keller and Pinchover proved the Landis conjecture for fractional Schrödinger equations on graphs
[
7
]
, under a polynomial decay of the solution, in the context of criticality theory.

In this paper we extend the results in
[
18
,
26
]
to fully nonlinear operators of order
2s
which generalize the class
\mathcal{L}
.
We are able to relax condition (
2
), by allowing the solution to have polynomial decay, without assuming any decaying properties of the potential.

We consider an operator
I
of the form:

Iu(x)=\sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}}L_{a}u(x),\text{ or }Iu(x)=\inf_{a\in\mathcal{A}}L_{a}u(x),

(3)

where
\mathcal{A}
is a set and
L_{a}\in\mathcal{L}
for every
a\in\mathcal{A}
.
By solution we mean a viscosity solution in the sense of Definition
1
ahead.
As it is customary in the theory of integral operators, those solutions belong to the space of measurable functions
u
such that
\|u\|_{L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}<\infty
, where

\|u\|_{L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|}{1+|x|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\,;

such a space is denoted
by
L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Lastly, as in
[
8
,
25
,
27
]
, an assumption on the sign of the operator
I+V
will be pivotal. In the sequel,
\lambda_{1}^{\pm}(I+V,G)>0
stands for the validity of both maximum and minimum principles for the operator
I+V
in the bounded
C^{2}
subdomain
G
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, see Definition
2
.

Within the vocabulary established, our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
,
I
be an operator as in (
3
), and
V:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a bounded continuous function.
Assume
\lambda_{1}^{\pm}(I+V,G)>0
for each bounded
C^{2}
subdomain
G
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

If
u\in L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a viscosity solution to
Iu+Vu=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, such that

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}|u(x)||x|^{N+2s}=0,

(4)

then
u\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Remark 1
.

We have stated Theorem
1
for the whole space
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
for ease of notation, but under minor modifications its proof may also address the case when
\Omega
is an exterior domain, as in
[
27
]
, by asking additionally that
u
has a sign in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
in order to conclude that
u\equiv 0
in
\Omega
.

Theorem
1
unveils the difference of decay rate of the Landis conjecture, evolving from exponential to polynomial in the presence of a nonlocal operator. This result is new even in the context of the fractional Laplacian, and it is consistent with the polynomial decay rate of the nonlocal nonlinear Schrödinger equation in
[
12
, Theorem 1.5]
, see also
[
4
, Chapter 7]
.

Hypothesis (
4
) seems to be sharp.
For instance, regarding the fractional Laplacian,
Lemma 2.1 in
[
2
]
says that a
C^{2}
function
u
defined in the whole space, which is positive radial and decreasing for
|x|\geq 1
, and
u(x)\approx|x|^{-(N+2s)}
for large
|x|
, satisfies
|(-\Delta)^{s}u|\approx|u|
in a neighborhood of infinity.
Besides, when
V\equiv-\frac{1}{2}
, Lemma 4.3 in
[
12
]
shows the existence of a classical solution
w
to problem

(-\Delta)^{s}w+\frac{1}{2}w=0\text{ in the exterior of the ball }B_{1}

such that

0<w(x)\leq\frac{c_{0}}{|x|^{N+2s}}

for some
c_{0}>0
.

The remaining of the text splits as follows.
In Section
2
we recall some definitions and auxiliary tools, while in Section
3
we give the proof of Theorem
1
.
The latter relies on a nonlocal weak Harnack estimate by Ros-Oton and Serra
[
24
]
, properly adapted for equations with zero order terms, which in turn gives a lower decay bound for positive supersolutions. This approach can be seen as a kind of nonlocal counterpart of techniques in
[
27
]
, slightly improved due to the nonlocal character of the estimates.

Definition 1

(Viscosity solution)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be any open set,

\displaystyle Iu+V(x)u

\displaystyle=f(x)

(5)

in
\Omega
,
N_{x}
, and for any test function

We say that

\Omega

iii.

A function

We define the Pucci extremal operators associated to

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{+}u(x)=\sup_{L\in\mathcal{L}}Lu(x),\quad\mathcal{M}^{-}u(x)=\inf_{L\in\mathcal{L}}Lu(x),

Lemma 2

(Proposition 3.2.15 in
[

]
)
.

Let

Remark 2

The conclusion of Lemma

We state now the minimum principle for viscosity supersolutions. It is an extension of Lemma 3.2.19 of
[

Lemma 6
.

Let
s\in(0,1),V\in C(\Omega)
with
V\leq 0
, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be any bounded open set, and let
u\in LSC(\bar{\Omega})\cap L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)

\begin{cases}\mathcal{M}^{-}[u]+Vu\leq 0&\text{ in }\Omega\\
u\geq 0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega.\end{cases}

Then

in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Proof.

By the lower semicontinuity of
u
, there exists
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
such that
u
reaches its minimum at
x_{0}
. By contradiction, suppose
u\left(x_{0}\right)=-m<0
. Since
u\geq 0

\partial\Omega
, then

.

Defining
\psi=-m\chi_{\Omega}
, we have that
\psi
is a smooth function in a neighborhood of

\Omega
, and satisfies

in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. By the definition of viscosity solution, we get

\mathcal{M}^{-}\psi\left(x_{0}\right)+V(x_{0})u\left(x_{0}\right)\leq 0\Longrightarrow\mathcal{M}^{-}\psi\left(x_{0}\right)\leq-V(x_{0})u\left(x_{0}\right)\leq 0.

But since
x_{0}
is a minimum for

in
\overline{\Omega}
,

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathcal{M}^{-}\psi\left(x_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\textstyle\displaystyle=\inf_{L\in\mathcal{L}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(u(y)-u\left(x_{0}\right)\right)K\left(y-x_{0}\right)\mathrm{d}y\textstyle\geq\inf_{K}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega}mK\left(y-x_{0}\right)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\textstyle\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega}\frac{\lambda m}{\left|y-x_{0}\right|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{\penalty\ d}y>0,

which is a contradiction. Hence

in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Throughout the text, a key half-Harnack inequality based on the following proposition will come into play. Proposition

below is essentially that of Theorem 2.2 in
[

Proposition 7

(Weak Harnack inequality)
.

Let
V\in C(B_{1})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, with
\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq 1
, let
u\in LSC(B_{1})\cap L^{1}_{\omega_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a viscosity solution to

\begin{cases}\mathcal{M}^{-}u+Vu\leq C_{0}&\text{in }B_{1}\\
u\geq 0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\end{cases}

with
C_{0}>0
. Then there exists
C=C(N,s,\Lambda,\lambda)>0
such that

\|u\|_{L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C(\inf_{B_{1/2}}u+C_{0})

Proof.

Let
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{3/4})
,
0\leq\eta\leq 1
and
\eta\equiv 1
in
B_{1/2}
. Now,
\inf_{B_{3/4}}u\geq 0
, so for
x\in B_{3/4}

u(x)\geq\inf_{B_{3/4}}u\geq\eta\inf_{B_{3/4}}u

and, as
\eta\equiv 0
outside
B_{3/4}
, we actually have
u\geq\eta\inf_{B_{3/4}}u
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We can then define

t:=\sup\{\tau\in\mathbb{R}:\penalty\ u(x)\geq\tau\eta(x),\penalty\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\}\geq\inf_{B_{3/4}}u.

Note that by taking
x\in B_{1/2}
, we have
u(x)\geq t
, so that
\inf_{B_{1/2}}u\geq t
.

By the lower semiconinuity of
u-t\eta
, there exists
x_{0}\in B_{3/4}
such that
\inf_{B_{3/4}}(u-t\eta)=(u-t\eta)(x_{0})\geq 0
. If we had
u(x_{0})-t\eta(x_{0})>0
, we would get
u>t\eta
in
B_{3/4}
, so there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
u>(t+\varepsilon)\eta
in
B_{3/4}
. Since
\eta\equiv 0
outside
B_{3/4}
,
u\geq 0=(t+\varepsilon)\eta
. This means

satisfies
u\geq(t+\varepsilon)\eta
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, contradicting the maximality of

. Thus, we must have
u(x_{0})-t\eta(x_{0})=0
.

This means that
t\eta
touches
u
from below at
x_{0}
, then by Lemma
3
,
\mathcal{M}^{-}u(x_{0})
is well defined and we have

\mathcal{M}^{-}u(x_{0})+V(x_{0})u(x_{0})\leq C_{0}

Since
u-t\eta
can be touched from below by a constant function, then
\mathcal{M}^{-}[u-t\eta](x_{0})
is also classically defined.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{-}[\eta]\left(x_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\inf_{K}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\eta(y)-\eta\left(x_{0}\right)\right)K\left(x_{0}-y\right)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{K}\left\{\int_{B_{\delta}(x_{0})}(\eta(y)-\eta(x_{0}))K\left(x_{0}-y\right)\mathrm{d}y-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\delta}(x_{0})}K\left(x_{0}-y\right)\mathrm{d}y\right\}

\displaystyle\geq c_{0}\int_{B_{\delta}(x_{0})}\frac{\mathrm{\penalty\ d}y}{\left|x_{0}-y\right|^{N-2+2s}}-\Lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\delta}(x_{0})}\frac{\mathrm{\penalty\ d}y}{\left|x_{0}-y\right|^{N+2s}}\geq-c_{1},

with
c_{1}=c_{1}(N,s,\Lambda)
because
0\leq\eta\leq 1
. Indeed, since
\eta
is a
C^{2}
function, we write

\eta(y)=\eta(x_{0})+D\eta(x_{0})\cdot(y-x_{0})+O(|y-x_{0}|^{2})
in
B_{\delta}(x_{0})
, for some fixed
\delta>0
, and use
the symmetry of the kernel in
B_{\delta}(x_{0})
to cancel the derivative term.

Now, since
(u-t\eta)(x_{0})=0
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{-}[u-t\eta](x_{0})\leq

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{M}^{-}[u](x_{0})-t\mathcal{M}^{-}[\eta](x_{0})+V(x_{0})u(x_{0})-tV(x_{0})\eta(x_{0})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;C_{0}-t\mathcal{M}^{-}[\eta](x_{0})-tV(x_{0})\eta(x_{0})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;C_{0}+tc_{1}+t

because
V(x_{0})\geq-1
and
\eta\leq 1
.

On the other hand, using
u-t\eta\geq(u-t\eta)(x_{0})=0
, a lower bound is produced:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{-}[u-t\eta](x_{0})

\displaystyle\geq\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(z)-t\eta(z)}{|z-x_{0}|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lambda}{2^{N+2s}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(z)}{1+|z|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}z-t\|1\|_{L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right)

\displaystyle=\tilde{C}\|u\|_{L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}-tc_{2}

where
\tilde{C}:=\frac{\lambda}{2^{N+2s}}
and
c_{2}:=\tilde{C}\|1\|_{L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
depend only on
N
,
s
and
\lambda
.

Combining the above inequalities, it yields

\displaystyle\tilde{C}\|u\|_{L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}-tc_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}+tc_{1}+t

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\penalty\ \penalty\ \tilde{C}\|u\|_{L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}+t(1+c_{1}+c_{2})

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\|u\|_{L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq C(\underset{B_{1/2}}{\inf}u+C_{0}),

using the fact that

and by setting
C:=\frac{1+c_{1}+c_{2}}{\tilde{C}}
.

(6)

Lemma 11

Let

Note that

\frac{3^{-N-2s}}{1+|x_{0}|^{N+2s}}\leq\frac{1+|z|^{N+2s}}{r_{0}^{N+2s}+|z-x_{0}|^{N+2s}}\leq\left(\frac{2}{r_{0}}\right)^{N+2s}(1+|x_{0}|^{N+2s}).

In this section we give a simple proof of the Harnack inequality for Pucci operators
\mathcal{M}_{*}^{+}u:=\sup_{L\in\mathcal{L}*}Lu
and
\mathcal{M}_{*}^{-}u:=\inf_{L\in\mathcal{L}*}Lu
defined on the class
\mathcal{L}*
of linear operators with homogeneous kernels of degree
-N-2s
in the form

Lu(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u(x+y)+u(x-y)-2u(x))\frac{y^{T}Ay}{|y|^{N+2s+2}}\mathrm{d}y,

with
A
a symmetric
N\times N
matrix such that
\lambda I\leq A\leq\Lambda I
.

Lemma 15
.

Let
p\geq N
,
f\in C(\overline{B_{1}})
and
u\in L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap\text{USC}(\overline{B_{1}})
be a solution to

\mathcal{M}_{*}^{+}u\geq-f
in
B_{1}.

Then there exists some constant
C>0
such that

\sup_{B_{1/2}}u\leq C(\|u\|_{L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}).

Proof.

Let
\eta\in C(\overline{B_{1}})
be the unique viscosity solution to

\begin{cases}\mathcal{M}_{*}^{+}\eta=f&\text{ in }B_{1}\\
\eta=0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}.\end{cases}

Consider
w=u-\eta
, then

\mathcal{M}_{*}^{+}w\geq\mathcal{M}_{*}^{+}u-\mathcal{M}_{*}^{+}\eta=\mathcal{M}_{*}^{+}u-f\geq 0

in
B_{1}
, in the viscosity sense. Then, by
[
13
, Theorem 3.3.5]
applied to
w
, we get

\sup_{B_{1/2}}w\leq C\|w\|_{L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{1/2}}u

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{B_{1/2}}w+\sup_{B_{1/2}}\eta\leq C(\|u\|_{L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|\eta\|_{L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})})+\sup_{B_{1}}\eta

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+C\sup_{B_{1}}|\eta|\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{1+|x|^{N+2s}}+\sup_{B_{1}}\eta.

Now the ABP estimate
[
16
, Theorem 3.1]
applied to
\eta
and
-\eta
(since
\mathcal{M}_{*}^{-}(-\eta)=-\mathcal{M}^{+}_{*}\eta=-f
) yields

\sup_{B_{1}}|\eta|\leq{C}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})},

obtaining the desired estimate.
∎

Proposition 16

(Local Maximum Principle)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
,
C_{0}\geq 0
, and
V\in C(\overline{B_{1}})
. If
u\in C(\overline{B_{1}})\cap L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfies

\mathcal{M}_{*}^{+}u+Vu\geq-C_{0}\penalty\ \text{ in }B_{1}

in the viscosity sense, then there exists
C=C(N,s,\lambda,\Lambda,\|V\|_{\infty})
such that

\underset{B_{1/2}}{\sup}\penalty\ u\leq C(\|u\|_{L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+C_{0}).

Proof.

We apply Lemma
15
with
f=C_{0}+cu
and some
p\geq N
with
p>1
, to get

\sup_{B_{1/2}}u\leq C(\|u\|_{L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|Vu+C_{0}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}).

By the Hölder inequality and the fact that
|V|\leq M
we obtain

\displaystyle\|Vu+C_{0}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\leq\|Vu\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}+|B_{1}|C_{0}\leq M|B_{1}|^{p/(p-1)}\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}+|B_{1}|C_{0}.

Observing that
\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{1}_{\omega_{s}}(B_{1})}\leq C\|u\|_{L_{\omega_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
for some constant depending on
N
and
s
, we conclude the theorem.
∎

, with
\mathcal{M}_{*}
instead of
\mathcal{M}
, is obtained by combining Propositions
7
and
16
.

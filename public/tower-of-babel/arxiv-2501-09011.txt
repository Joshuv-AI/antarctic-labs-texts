Quantum cohomology and Floer invariants of semiprojective toric manifolds
Alexander F. Ritter, Filip Živanović

Abstract
. We use Floer theory to describe invariants of symplectic ℂ ∗ \mathbb{C}^{*} -manifolds admitting several commuting ℂ ∗ \mathbb{C}^{*} -actions. The ℂ ∗ \mathbb{C}^{*} -actions induce filtrations by ideals on quantum cohomology, as well as filtrations on Hamiltonian Floer cohomologies, and we prove relationships between these filtrations. We also carry this out in the equivariant setting, in particular ℂ ∗ \mathbb{C}^{*} -actions then give rise to Hilbert-Poincaré polynomials on ordinary cohomology that depend on Floer theory. For semiprojective toric manifolds, we obtain an explicit presentation for quantum and symplectic cohomology in the Fano and CY setting, both in the equivariant and non-equivariant setting.

1.1.
Motivation

We use novel Floer-theoretic methods to describe quantum cohomology for non-compact symplectic manifolds

Toric manifolds are prototypical such spaces: they arise in algebraic geometry by compactifying an algebraic torus

We briefly recall the story for closed symplectic manifolds

6.
Presentation of quantum & symplectic cohomology for semiprojective toric manifolds

We now prove the quantum/Floer version of (

[
Rit16
, Thm.1.5]
.
Let

be the number of ray generators

D_{1},\ldots,D_{r}.

The moment polytope
\Delta=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle x,e_{i}\rangle\geq\lambda_{i}\}

where

is the formal Novikov variable. The

\mathrm{Jac}(W):=\mathbb{K}[z_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,z_{n}^{\pm 1}]/(\partial_{z_{1}}W,\ldots,\partial_{z_{n}}W),

is the Novikov field.

We will assume that the reader is familiar with the discussion and notation from
[
Rit16
, Sec.3A]
, which explains how from
\Sigma
(or equivalently, from
\Delta
) one obtains combinatorially the so-called

and the

-linear relations between the classes

The

-linear dependency relation in

Example 6.6

(Creapant resolution of the conifold)
.

An example with

where one sees that this correction is necessary, is the resolved conifold. The conifold

Then (
44

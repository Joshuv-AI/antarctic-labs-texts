Abstract
Understanding the dynamic reorganization of brain networks is critical for predicting cognitive decline, neurological progression, and individual variability in clinical outcomes. This work proposes a multimodal graph neural network (GNN) framework that integrates structural MRI, diffusion tensor imaging, and functional MRI to model spatiotemporal brain network reorganization. Brain regions are represented as nodes, and structural and functional connectivity as edges, forming longitudinal brain graphs for each subject. Temporal evolution is captured via fractional stochastic differential operators embedded within graph-based recurrent networks, enabling the modeling of long-term dependencies and stochastic fluctuations in network dynamics. Attention mechanisms are incorporated to fuse multimodal information and generate interpretable biomarkers, including network energy entropy, graph curvature, fractional memory indices, and modality-specific attention scores. These biomarkers are further combined into a composite prognostic index to quantify individual risk of network instability or cognitive decline. The proposed framework is evaluated on high-quality, longitudinal neuroimaging datasets, demonstrating both predictive accuracy and interpretability. Results highlight the potential of mathematically rigorous, multimodal graph-based approaches for deriving clinically relevant biomarkers from existing imaging data, without requiring new data collection.

IV
Methodology

Our proposed framework,
Multimodal Graph Neural Networks for Prognostic Modeling of Brain Network Reorganization
, integrates structural MRI, DTI, fMRI, PET, CSF biomarkers, and clinical scores into a unified, mathematically rigorous architecture. The methodology consists of four major components: data preprocessing, graph-based embeddings, deep learning fusion network, and prognostic risk modeling.

IV-A

Data Description and Preprocessing

We use multimodal datasets from the ADNI cohort and other clinical studies, comprising:

•

Structural MRI (T1-weighted):
Cortical thickness and regional volumes for
N=68
brain regions (Desikan-Killiany atlas), preprocessed using FreeSurfer.

•

Diffusion Tensor Imaging (DTI):
Fractional anisotropy (FA) and tractography generating structural adjacency matrices
A_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
.

•

Functional MRI (fMRI):
Resting-state BOLD time series, parcellated into 68 regions. Functional connectivity
A_{f}
computed via Pearson correlation.

•

PET:
Standardized uptake value ratios (SUVR), normalized across subjects.

•

CSF Biomarkers:
A
\beta
, Tau, p-Tau, z-normalized.

•

Clinical Scores:
MMSE, CDR, ADAS-Cog aligned to imaging sessions.

Dataset information (ADNI) and limitations.

We used the Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) public data release, which includes multimodal imaging, CSF, genetic and cognitive assessments across multiple visits. Typical sample sizes for multimodal cohorts vary by modality and inclusion criteria; in typical ADNI subset selections for multimodal longitudinal studies, the working cohort size ranges roughly from 800–1,200 subjects (depending on required modalities and baseline/follow-up availability)
[
13
,
4
]
. Limitations we explicitly handle: missing modalities/timepoints (imputed or excluded based on analysis policy), variable inter-scan intervals (handled by alignment and temporal windows), demographic biases (mitigated by stratified analyses), and class imbalance across diagnostic labels (handled via stratified sampling and balanced metrics). Preprocessing pipeline includes motion correction, bias-field correction, skull-stripping, segmentation (FreeSurfer), tractography (DTI), BOLD denoising (nuisance regressors + motion scrubbing), parcellation to the Desikan-Killiany atlas, z-normalization of numeric features, and alignment of visits for longitudinal graph construction.

IV-B

Graph-Based Embeddings

Each modality is represented as a graph
G=(V,E)
:

•

Nodes (
V
):
Brain regions (
N=68
).

•

Edges (
E
):
Weighted combination of structural (
A_{s}
) and functional (
A_{f}
) connectivity:

A=\alpha A_{s}+(1-\alpha)A_{f},\quad\alpha\in[0,1]

•

Graph Laplacian:

L=D-A,\quad D_{ii}=\sum_{j}A_{ij}

Temporal dependencies are captured via fractional stochastic differential equations on graph nodes:

D^{\alpha}_{t}X(t)=f(X(t))+\sigma dB_{t},\quad\alpha\in(0,1)

where
D^{\alpha}_{t}
is the Caputo derivative,
B_{t}
is Brownian motion, and
\sigma
is stochastic noise amplitude.

IV-C

Deep Learning Fusion Network

Each modality is processed through modality-specific deep learning pipelines:

•Structural MRI / DTI:
Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs)

\displaystyle H^{(l+1)}

\displaystyle=\sigma\!\left(\tilde{D}^{-1/2}\tilde{A}\tilde{D}^{-1/2}H^{(l)}W^{(l)}\right),\quad\tilde{A}=A+I

•fMRI:
Temporal GCN or Gated Graph Neural Networks to model connectivity dynamics.

•PET/CSF/Clinical Scores:
Fully connected layers with nonlinear activations, followed by attention layers:

\displaystyle H_{m}=\text{Attention}(\text{FC}(X_{m})),\quad m\in\{\text{PET},\text{CSF},\text{Clinical}\}

•Cross-Modality Attention Fusion:
Latent embeddings from all modalities are fused using self- and cross-attention:

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=HW_{Q},\quad K=HW_{K},\quad V=HW_{V}

\displaystyle A(Q,K,V)

\displaystyle=\text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}\right)V

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\text{Concat}(H_{\text{MRI}},H_{\text{fMRI}},H_{\text{PET}},H_{\text{CSF}},H_{\text{Clinical}})

\displaystyle\hat{y}_{\text{prognosis}}

\displaystyle=\text{FC}(A(Q,K,V)+Z)

IV-D

Prognostic Risk Modeling

Reorganization risk of brain networks is modeled as a diffusion over structural graphs:

D^{\alpha}_{t}X(t)=\beta AX(t)-\gamma X(t)

where
X(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is the node-level prognostic risk vector,
\beta
diffusion rate,
\gamma
decay, and
D^{\alpha}_{t}
fractional derivative. Numerically implemented using multiple GCN layers:

X(t+1)=\sigma(W_{gcn}AX(t)+b_{gcn}-\gamma X(t))

The resulting network embeddings are concatenated with other modality embeddings before final prediction.

IV-E

Biomarker Extraction and Interpretability

From the combined model, we compute interpretable biomarkers:

•

Network Energy:

E=\mathrm{trace}(X^{\top}LX)

•

Graph Curvature:

\kappa=\mathrm{trace}(R(X,Y)Y)

•

Attention Entropy:

H_{att}=-\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\log\alpha_{i}

•

Fractional Memory Index:
Derived from Caputo derivative trajectories

•

Diffusion Centrality:
Node-level contribution to network reorganization

IV-F

Loss Function and Optimization

The model is trained end-to-end with a composite loss:

L=L_{MSE}(\hat{y},y)+\lambda_{att}L_{entropy}+\lambda_{diff}\|D^{\alpha}_{t}X-(\beta AX-\gamma X)\|_{2}^{2}

where
L_{MSE}
is the regression loss for prognostic scores,
L_{entropy}
encourages informative attention, and the last term enforces accurate network diffusion modeling. Hyperparameters
\lambda_{att}
and
\lambda_{diff}
balance contributions. Optimization uses Adam (
\eta=0.001
), dropout
p=0.5
, L2 weight decay
10^{-4}
, and early stopping on validation loss.

V-F

Discussion

The results demonstrate that the proposed
Multimodal Graph Neural Network (MGNN)
consistently outperforms classical machine learning and unimodal deep learning baselines. Incorporating graph-based embeddings, temporal convolutional and Bi-LSTM layers, cross-modality attention, and structural-functional integration is crucial for accurately modeling brain network reorganization.

The spatial and temporal patterns captured by the MGNN indicate that network hubs in the default mode network, fronto-temporal regions, and subcortical structures contribute strongly to reorganization risk. The attention fusion mechanism provides interpretable insights into which regions drive the prognostic prediction.

While the model demonstrates improved performance, we adopt a cautious interpretation, acknowledging the following limitations:

•

The dataset, while multimodal and longitudinal, remains moderate in size. Validation on larger, multi-center cohorts is required to confirm generalizability.

•

Prediction metrics (MAE, RMSE, Pearson correlation, and AUC) indicate strong performance but do not fully eliminate errors, particularly for borderline risk subjects.

•

Derived biomarkers, including attention weights, network energy, and diffusion centrality, provide mechanistic insight, but prospective clinical validation is necessary for translational applications.

Overall, the MGNN establishes a principled graph-based and multimodal framework for prognostic modeling of brain network reorganization, providing both predictive accuracy and interpretability essential for neuroscience research and potential clinical decision support.

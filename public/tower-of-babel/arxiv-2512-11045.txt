Continuous Family of Conformal Field Theories and Exactly Marginal Operators Preprint: CALT 2025-041 Preprint: IPMU 25-0054 Preprint: KYUSHU-HET-333 Preprint: RIKEN-iTHEMS-Report-25
Shota Komatsu, Yuya Kusuki, Marco Meineri, Hirosi Ooguri

Abstract
Does a conformal manifold imply the existence of exactly marginal operators? We answer this question affirmatively under the assumption that there exists a conformal interface with certain properties connecting nearby CFTs. We show that the exactly marginal operator that connects the CFTs can be reconstructed from the interface displacement operator. Our construction is model-independent and based on the general principles of conformal symmetry.



I
Introduction and Summary

If exactly marginal operators exist in a conformal field theory (CFT), they generate a conformal manifold, which parameterizes a continuous family of CFTs
29
. One might naturally ask if the converse also holds: Does a conformal manifold require the existence of exactly marginal operators? In this letter, we address this question by deriving the existence of exactly marginal operators under certain assumptions.

The existence of exactly marginal operators has important implications for the Swampland program, particularly for the Distance Conjecture
18
. The earliest distance conjecture may have been stated by Albert Einstein in his autobiographical notes, in which he claimed that there are no free parameters in the basic equations of physics. This was reformulated as Conjecture 0 in
18
as “the moduli space of a consistent quantum gravity is parameterized by inequivalent expectation values of massless scalar
fields.” The existence of exactly marginal operators for a conformal manifold allows us to prove Conjecture 0 for gravitational theories in anti-de Sitter (AdS) space as follows
20
:
If there is a continuous family of gravitational theories in AdS, then there must be a corresponding family of CFTs.
If we can show that each CFT parameter is associated with an exactly marginal operator, the AdS/CFT dictionary would tell us that the exactly marginal operator corresponds to a
massless scalar field with a flat potential, and the asymptotic behavior of this field determines the parameters.
Therefore, every parameter in quantum gravity in AdS is related to a value of a dynamical field, as claimed in Conjecture 0.
1
1

1

There has been an interesting development in interpreting the conjecture in AdS
6
;
25
;
27
;
26
.

We note an interesting analogy with the absence of global symmetry in quantum gravity in AdS, which was proven in
12
;
13
using the splittability of symmetry generators
10
in CFT. For continuous symmetry, the splittability is the consequence of the well-known fact that the symmetry generators are expressed as exponentiated integrals of the Noether currents over a Cauchy surface. If we regard a continuous deformation of CFT as
(-1)
-form global symmetry, the existence of the corresponding exactly marginal operator is the splittability of the symmetry. In this way, the absence of free parameters and the absence of global symmetry in gravitational theories in AdS are unified and understood in a single framework as consequences of the splittability of symmetry generators.

II
Displacement Operator as Exactly Marginal Operator

Our proof assumes

1.

For any pair of CFTs that are close to each other on the conformal manifold, there is a conformal interface that becomes trivial when the two CFTs coincide.

2.

Correlators involving unit normalized defect operators have finite limits as the interface disappears (
smoothness condition
).
2
2

2

While the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies that correlators of a single interface operator and any number of bulk operators are bounded, it does not imply that the limit exists. Furthermore, the norm of multiple defect insertions is not automatically bounded. It might be possible to improve this by considering multiple interfaces, but we do not pursue this avenue here.

The consequence of these two assumptions is that defect operators become local operators of the CFT in the limit, unless they decouple (see FIG.
2
).
3
3

3

If the interface is not trivial but topological and not invertible, then a topological line might be attached to the defect operators in the limit.
For the reasons explained later, we also make the additional technical assumption that

3.

The bulk CFT has no operators with conformal weights of
(1/2,1/2)
that are neutral under all the global symmetries preserved by the interface.

On the other hand, we do not need to assume that the central charges on both sides (
c_{L,R}

c_{L}-\bar{c}_{L}=c_{R}-\bar{c}_{R}
is necessary for the existence of a conformal interface
8

c-\bar{c}
cannot.

4

4

From modular

h-\bar{h}-\frac{c-\bar{c}}{24}\in\mathbb{Z},

Combining these relations, we conclude that

This, in turn, means that

1.

\lim_{{\rm N}_{\rm D}\to 0}

(1,1)

3.

\lim_{{\rm N}_{\rm D}\to 0}\hat{\mathrm{D}}
vanishes.

III
Interface Conformal Manifold

Why did we need to assume the absence of neutral
(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
primaries? Let us first clarify what
\rho
actually represents in (
20
). If a CFT contains a weight one scalar operator, one can produce a line defect by integrating it with an infinitesimal coefficient. If, furthermore,
c_{\rho\rho\rho}=0
, together with the required higher order conditions, the line defect is part of a conformal manifold. As a concrete example, the Ising CFT admits a defect conformal manifold generated by the following interface exactly marginal deformation
22
,

S_{\textup{defect}}=\gamma\int dx\ \varepsilon.

(25)

where
\varepsilon
is the energy density operator with conformal dimensions
(1/2,1/2)
.
The displacement operator is:

\mathrm{D}=\frac{\gamma}{2}({\partial}-\bar{{\partial}})\varepsilon.

(26)

This is precisely of the same form as the second term in (
20
). Vice versa, given a family of interfaces connected to the identity, whose displacement obeys (
20
) with
\phi=0
, one can prove that
c_{\rho\rho\rho}=0
—see the Supplemental Material. Furthermore, it is easy to check that, if
\lim_{\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{D}}\to 0}\hat{\rm D}=(\partial-\bar{\partial})\rho
, the l.h.s. of (
22
) vanishes in the limit, therefore no scaling dimension changes at leading order in
\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{D}}.

More generally, symmetry-neutral exactly marginal deformation on the interface can arise from the breaking of a
phantom symmetry

3
: a symmetry that emerges when one interprets the interface between
\mathrm{CFT}_{L}
and
\mathrm{CFT}_{R}
as a boundary in
\mathrm{CFT}_{L}\otimes\overline{\mathrm{CFT}_{R}}
using the folding trick. In such cases, a
phantom current
with conformal dimension
(1,0)
in the folded theory induces an interface exactly marginal operator with dimension
(1/2,1/2)
in the unfolded theory. This is precisely the case in the
example of the Ising CFT, where the phantom current is given by
j=\psi_{L}\psi_{R}
.
Here,
\psi_{L/R}
represents the fermion field on the left/right side of the interface,
and the energy density operator in the Ising CFT can be expressed in terms of these fermions as

\varepsilon_{L/R}=i\psi_{L/R}\bar{\psi}_{L/R}
.

The explicit examples discussed above describe interfaces between identical CFTs, rather than interfaces connecting different points on a conformal manifold as considered in the main text. However, our leading-order analysis does not rule out the possibility that an interface could connect theories at distinct points on a conformal manifold while still obeying
\lim_{\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{D}}\to 0}\hat{\rm D}=(\partial-\bar{\partial})\rho
. Consider, for instance, the case where
{\rm D}\sim\tilde{\phi}+(\partial-\bar{\partial})\rho
with
\braket{\tilde{\phi}\tilde{\phi}}=O(\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{D}}^{1+\epsilon})
with
\epsilon>0
. Equation (
20
) would be obeyed with
\phi=0
. This example shows that additional input is needed to treat conformal manifolds containing protected dimension
1
operators within our framework.
6
6

6

Of course, if a scalar has dimension 1 only on some submanifold of the conformal manifold, our analysis works by considering points in the complement of said submanifold.

Examples

The marginal operator in this CFT is

The exactly marginal deformation leads to the shift in the compactification radius,

The interface with a CFT deformed by

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\partial}X_{L}\\
\bar{{\partial}}X_{R}\\

\end{array}\right),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ S=-\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos 2\theta&-\sin 2\theta\\
\sin 2\theta&\cos 2\theta\\
\end{array}\right),

where we define
\tan\theta=\mathrm{e}^{\pi\delta\lambda}

This gluing condition allows us to express the stress tensor on the right side of the interface in terms of the free boson fields on the left side,

\displaystyle T_{R}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}{\partial}X_{R}{\partial}X_{R}=\frac{1}{2}{\partial}X_{L}{\partial}X_{L}-\pi\delta\lambda{\partial}X_{L}\bar{{\partial}}X_{L},

\displaystyle\overline{T}_{R}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bar{{\partial}}X_{R}\bar{{\partial}}X_{R}=\frac{1}{2}\bar{{\partial}}X_{L}\bar{{\partial}}X_{L}-\pi\delta\lambda{\partial}X_{L}\bar{{\partial}}X_{L}.

This coincides precisely with the exactly marginal operator

Another example is the supersymmetric sigma-model whose target space is a Calabi-Yau manifold. For each exactly marginal deformation, there is a primary field
\rho
of dimensions
(1/2,1/2)
and
an exactly marginal operator
\phi
, which is a superconformal descendant of
\rho
. However, since
\rho
carries non-zero
R
charges with respect to the
{\cal N}=(2,2)
superconformal algebra, it cannot appear on the right-hand side of (
20
).
For a generic Calabi-Yau manifold, there are no neutral
(1/2,1/2)
primaries. Therefore, the limit of
\hat{D}
can be identified with the exactly marginal operator
\phi
.

Supplemental Material

In this Supplemental Material, we derive a few technical results on the limit of three-point functions which complement the discussion in the main text.

V.1
Three-Point Function of
\hat{\rm D}
.

We first compute the three-point function of the normalized displacement operator
\hat{\rm D}\equiv{\rm D}/\sqrt{{\rm N}_{\rm D}}
. For this purpose, we consider the OPE of
T_{L}-T_{R}
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &(T_{L}(x)-T_{R}(x))(T_{L}(y)-T_{R}(y))(T_{L}(z)-T_{R}(z))\\
&=(\bar{T}_{L}(x)-\bar{T}_{R}(x))(T_{L}(y)-T_{R}(y))(T_{L}(z)-T_{R}(z))\\
&=\bar{T}_{L}(x)T_{L}(y)T_{L}(z)-\bar{T}_{R}(x)T_{R}(y)T_{R}(z)\\
&=\frac{c_{L}\bar{T}_{L}(x)-c_{R}\bar{T}_{R}(x)}{2(y-z)^{4}}+\left(\frac{2}{(y-z)^{2}}+\frac{\partial_{z}}{(y-z)}\right)\left(\bar{T}_{L}(x)T_{L}(z)-\bar{T}_{R}(x)T_{R}(z)\right)\\
&=\frac{c_{L}\bar{T}_{L}(x)-c_{R}\bar{T}_{R}(x)}{2(y-z)^{4}}+\left(\frac{2}{(y-z)^{2}}+\frac{\partial_{z}}{(y-z)}\right)\left(T_{L}(x)T_{L}(z)-T_{R}(x)T_{R}(z)\right)\\
&=\frac{c_{L}\bar{T}_{L}(x)-c_{R}\bar{T}_{R}(x)}{2(y-z)^{4}}+\left(\frac{2}{(y-z)^{2}}+\frac{\partial_{z}}{(y-z)}\right)\left[\frac{c_{L}-c_{R}}{2(x-z)^{4}}+\left(\frac{2}{(x-z)^{2}}+\frac{\partial_{z}}{(x-z)}\right)\left(T_{L}(z)-T_{R}(z)\right)\right].\end{aligned}

(31)

Since the one-point functions on the interface vanish because of the conformal symmetry, we conclude that

\displaystyle\langle(T_{L}(x)-T_{R}(x))(T_{L}(y)-T_{R}(y))(T_{L}(z)-T_{R}(z))\rangle=\frac{c_{L}-c_{R}}{(y-z)^{2}(x-z)^{4}}\left(1+O((y-z))+O((x-z))\right)

(32)

Dividing each operator by the normalization
\sqrt{{\rm N}_{\rm D}}
and comparing the result with the expected behavior of the three-point function,
\sim(x-y)^{-2}(y-z)^{-2}(z-w)^{-2}
, we can read off the structure constant

\displaystyle c_{\hat{\rm D}\hat{\rm D}\hat{\rm D}}=\frac{c_{L}-c_{R}}{{\rm N}_{\rm D}^{3/2}}\,.

(33)

Thus, the smoothness assumption implies that
\left|c_{L}-c_{R}\right|^{2/3}/{\rm N}_{\mathrm{D}}
is finite in the limit
{\rm N}_{\rm D}\to 0
.

V.2
Interface conformal manifolds and
c_{\rho\rho\rho}

Suppose that a pair of CFTs (perhaps identical) admits a family of conformal interfaces which includes the trivial one, such that

\lim_{{\rm N}_{\rm D}\to 0}\hat{\rm D}=i(\partial-\bar{\partial})\rho~,

(34)

where
\rho
is a dimension one scalar as in the main text. Here we added an
i
to make the displacement real for real
\rho
.
In this appendix, we prove that the three-point function of
\rho
vanishes. While the argument starting with equations (
21
,
22
) can be repeated with
O_{L}=O_{R}=\rho
, this is to no avail because
\braket{\rho(z,\bar{z})\rho(\bar{z},z)(\partial-\bar{\partial})\rho(w,w)}
vanishes kinematically. Instead, we will derive the result as a consequence of conformality of the interface, together with the Ward identities of the displacement. In the rest of this appendix, we use the real coordinates
x=\Re z,\,y=\Im z
, and write
\rho(x,y)
slightly redefining our notation.

Consider the following equation:

\braket{\rho(0,y_{0}+\delta y)}=\frac{a_{\rho}({\rm N}_{\rm D})}{2y_{0}}\left(1-\frac{\delta y}{y_{0}}+\left(\frac{\delta y}{y_{0}}\right)^{2}+O\left(\delta y^{3}\right)\right)~.

(35)

The dependence on
\delta y
must be reproduced by the following deformation of the defect action:

\braket{\rho(0,y_{0}+\delta y)}=\frac{\left\langle\rho(0,y_{0})\,e^{-\delta S}\right\rangle}{\left\langle e^{-\delta S}\right\rangle}~,\qquad\delta S=\int\!dx\,\left(\delta y{\rm D}(x)+O(\delta y^{2})\right)~.

(36)

The leading order expansion of (
36
) leads to the usual Ward identity

\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}y}\braket{\rho(0,y)}=-\int\!dx\,\left\langle\rho(0,y){\rm D}(x)\right\rangle~.

(37)

Equation (
37
) implies that
a_{\rho}
starts at order
\sqrt{{\rm N}_{\rm D}}
, and furthermore
\rho
is the only operators whose one-point function has this property.

Equation (
35
) showcases infinitely many terms of order
\sqrt{{\rm N}_{\rm D}}\,(\delta y)^{n}
, with
n
integer, while the leading term in
\delta S
(
36
) would lead to an expansion in powers of
({\rm N}_{\rm D})^{m/2}\,(\delta y)^{n}
, with
m\geq n
. The only possible corrections to
\delta S
with the desired properties at order
\sqrt{{\rm N}_{\rm D}}
involve descendants of
\rho(x,y)
.
7
7

7

Of course, if we were to assume that the line defect has action
S_{\textup{defect}}=\int dx\,\rho(x,0)
, we could immediately conclude that
\delta S
in (
36
) contains the full Taylor expansion of
\rho(x,\delta y)
around
\delta y=0
. While our only assumption is (
34
), it is useful to keep in mind this more restricted scenario.

It will be sufficient to stop at order
(\delta y)^{2}
:

\delta S=\int\!dx\,\left(\delta y\,{\rm D}(x)-g\delta y^{2}\left(\partial_{y}^{2}\rho(x,0)+\kappa\,\partial_{x}^{2}\rho(x,0)\right)+O(\delta y^{3})\right)~.

(38)

The defect descendant
\partial_{x}^{2}\rho
has no effect, and it is included for later convenience. Instead, we have not included the dimension one operator
\rho
in the action, despite it being more relevant. The reason is that, by dimensional analysis, it cannot appear with a positive power of
\delta y
in the continuum limit, hence it is not needed to reproduce eq. (
35
) and should be tuned to zero.

We can now compute the order
(\delta y)^{2}
of equation (
36
), in a small-
{\rm N}_{\rm D}
expansion. With foresight, we define

g=g^{(1)}\sqrt{{\rm N}_{\rm D}}+g^{(2)}{\rm N}_{\rm D}+\dots,

(39)

and obtain

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}\braket{\rho(0,y)}=g^{(1)}\sqrt{{\rm N}_{\rm D}}\int\!dx\,\braket{\rho(0,y)\,\partial_{y}^{2}\rho(x,0)}_{0}\\
+{\rm N}_{\rm D}\left[\int\!dx_{1}\!\int\!dx_{2}\braket{\rho(0,y)\partial_{y}\rho(x_{1},0)\partial_{y}\rho(x_{2},0)}_{0}+g^{(2)}\int\!dx\,\braket{\rho(0,y)\,\left(\partial_{y}^{2}\rho(x,0)+\kappa\,\partial_{x}^{2}\rho(x,0)\right)}_{0}\right]~.

(40)

In this expression, the subscript 0 denotes expectation values taken in the absence of the defect.
Crucially, the double integral contains divergences, which must be regulated and renormalized. For concreteness, we choose a short-distance cutoff
a
with dimension of length. Then, a power-law divergence
\propto a^{-2}
should be cured by a counterterm proportional to
\rho(x,0)
, which we disregard as discussed above. Instead, a logarithmic divergence generates the coupling
g^{(2)}
along the RG flow, and we focus on the latter. In detail, we get

\int\!dx_{1}\!\int\!dx_{2}\braket{\rho(0,y)\partial_{y}\rho(x_{1},0)\partial_{y}\rho(x_{2},0)}_{0}\sim\dots+\left(\log\frac{\ell}{a}\right)c_{\rho\rho\rho}\!\int\!dx\,\frac{4x^{2}+48y^{2}}{(x^{2}+4y^{2})^{3}}+\textup{finite}~,

(41)

where
\ell
is an IR cutoff, which would cancel out against the finite part of the integral. The logarithmic divergence can be canceled by a counterterm proportional to
g^{(2)}
:

g^{(2)}=g^{(2)}_{R}(\mu)+\frac{5}{16}\,c_{\rho\rho\rho}\,\log a\mu~,\quad\qquad\kappa=\frac{3}{5}~.

(42)

The coupling to
\partial^{2}_{x}\rho
in the action (
38
) allows to cancel the divergence locally, at the level of the integrand, rather than upon integration. In (
42
) we introduced the RG scale
\mu
, which, as usual, allows to track the effect on observables of rescaling the cutoff. Since the bare coupling
g^{(2)}
does not depend on
\mu
, one gets the obvious beta function

\mu\frac{\partial g_{R}^{(2)}}{\partial\mu}=\beta(g_{R}^{(2)})=-\frac{5}{16}\,c_{\rho\rho\rho}~.

(43)

Equation (
43
) shows that the corresponding coupling cannot be fine-tuned if the three-point function of
\rho
does not vanish: it is always generated and evolves along the RG flow. This violates the hypothesis that the interface is conformal for any value of
{\rm N}_{\rm D}
sufficiently small, hence we conclude

c_{\rho\rho\rho}=0~.

(44)

With little more work, one can find the explicit anomalous dependence of the one-point function of
\rho
on
\log y
, proportional to
c_{\rho\rho\rho}
.

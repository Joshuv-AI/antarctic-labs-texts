Podocin oligomers regulate the ordering of nephrin chains, providing the molecular basis of NPHS2 interallelic interactions
Authors: Violetta Antal-Kónya, Gusztáv Schay, Máté Kétszeri, Eszter Balogh, Ágnes Mikó, Dániel Seidl, Tália Magdolna Keszthelyi, Mária Bernáth, Anita Ungvári-Veres, Guillaume Dorval, Moin A. Saleem, Abhijit Dixit, Corinne Antignac, Katalin Pászty, Miklós Kellermayer, Dóra K. Menyhárd, Kálmán Tory
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SignificanceThe kidney filters blood through a specialized barrier formed by proteins such as nephrin, whose precise spacing determines the size of filtration pores. Inherited kidney diseases that disrupt this barrier cause massive protein leakage into the urine, resulting in nephrotic syndrome. Podocin, the most frequently affected protein in inherited nephrotic syndrome, has an unclear role in barrier structure. Here, we show that podocin regulates the spacing between nephrin molecules, a function lost in disease-causing podocin variants. We also explain why a common podocin variant (R229Q) causes disease only when paired with specific podocin variants: They form abnormal oligomers that disrupt nephrin spacing. Importantly, nephrin spacing closely predicts pathogenicity, providing a functional basis for distinguishing disease-causing from benign R229Q associations.

Steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome (SRNS) is the second most common cause of kidney failure in childhood and early adulthood (1). Hereditary podocytopathies are responsible for 30% of the SRNS cases below 25 y of age (2). Among the more than 50 causal genes of podocytopathies (3, 4), NPHS2 is the most frequently implicated, being responsible for one-third of the cases (2, 5). The encoded podocin is an integral membrane component of the slit diaphragm, accumulated in lipid rafts (6, 7). As a member of the SPFH (stomatin-like) protein family, podocin possesses an evolutionary highly conserved prohibitin homology (PHB) domain and forms oligomers (7, 8). It is composed of a short N-terminal intracellular sequence, followed by a membrane-immersed helix and another intracellular segment at the C-terminus that contains the PHB domain, three helices (H1, H2, H3), and an unstructured C-terminal tail (Fig. 1). Podocin interacts with slit diaphragm components, nephrin and NEPH1, the CD2-associated protein, and regulates the activity of the transient receptor potential C channel TRPC6 (9–11). Pathogenic missense NPHS2 variants commonly impair membrane trafficking (12). The major variant, R138Q, retains podocin in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and causes early-onset SRNS when homozygous (13). The most frequent missense variant in the general population is R229Q. It is located in the PHB domain, benign in the homozygous state (14), but becomes pathogenic when trans-associated to specific 3’ variants (3’var) affecting the helical region, most commonly A284V (15). Intriguingly, these 3’ variants only exert a dominant negative effect against R229Q, but not against wild type (WT) podocin. We formerly explained the interallelic interactions of R229Q by an altered C-terminal oligomerization and cellular trafficking: R229Q podocin was no longer localized to the plasma membrane when co-expressed with the specific 3’ variants (15, 16). Intriguingly however, the pathogenic R229Q/3’var associations manifest in unusually mild, late-onset disease in contrast to other non-membranous podocin variants (17).

The slit diaphragm, the core component of the glomerular filtration barrier, is a cell–cell junction stretched out between intervening podocyte foot processes. Nephrin-deficient patients and nephrin-knockout mice virtually lack slit diaphragms (18, 19), pointing at the crucial role of nephrin in slit diaphragm formation. Along the same line, anti-nephrin antibodies induce severe proteinuria (20–22). Nephrin is a long, chain-like transmembrane protein. Its N-terminal extracellular segment contains eight immunoglobulin-like (Ig-like) and one fibronectin III “bead,” while its C-terminal is unstructured. Nephrin molecules from opposing podocyte foot processes interact with each other through the Ig-like domains, linking the foot processes and thus providing the molecular basis of the slit diaphragm (23, 24) along with NEPH1, a shorter molecule with very similar topology but only five Ig-like domains (25–27). Two topologically different models of slit diaphragm have been presented thus far: the zipper model where pores of 4 × 14 nm size are formed by nephrin/NEPH1-bridges near the plasma membrane, the shorter dimension of which (4 nm) corresponds to the distance between two neighboring nephrin or NEPH1 molecules in cis (originating from the same foot process) (23), and a fishnet-like topology where more evenly sized pores of ~7 to 11 nm in the central region of the slit diaphragm are created by crossing nephrin chains in trans (26, 28).

The structure of the podocin oligomers has not been experimentally determined yet. Other members of the SPFH protein family, prohibitins (PHB1 and PHB2) of Saccharomyces cerevisiae and the HflK/HflC pair of Escherichia coli, and human stomatin form large, ring-like heterooligomers of 24 to 32 repeating, alternating units (29–32). Within these, the PHB domains and the helical C-terminal tails are closely aligned in parallel orientation and together form the wall of the cylindrical assembly (SI Appendix, Fig. S1) (30, 31). However, in case of podocin, such a cylindrical arrangement is difficult to reconcile with the one-dimensional sheet-like topology of the slit diaphragm it anchors (26). We formerly proposed that the basic building blocks of podocin oligomers are dimer-pairs formed primarily through the antiparallel coiled-coil interaction of their H1 helices (residues 283 to 313), while their assembly into oligomers is governed by the H2, H3 helices and the CTT (16). That this type of association is also possible among SPFH proteins was later demonstrated in case of the isolated helical region of prohibitin 2 (PHB2), which was found to crystallize in antiparallel coiled-coil pairs (33).

Since podocin was confirmed to be located in the immediate proximity of the diaphragm on the intracellular side (11, 34), and its PHB domains were shown to be the primary binding site of nephrin (35), we considered that it might be the oligomerization of podocin, driven by its helical segments and C-terminal tail, that creates the regulated framework of evenly spaced anchorage sites for the nephrin chains.

The results we present here demonstrate that the nephrin–nephrin distance indeed varies in function of the co-expressed podocin variants and correlates perfectly with their pathogenicity. We also show that nephrin restores the membrane localization of pathogenic R229Q/3’var oligomers which explains the mild nature of the associated disease progression. These R229Q/3’var oligomers induce an increased and disordered nephrin–nephrin spacing, providing a molecular explanation for their pathogenicity.

Results

Podocin’s C-Terminal Tail Is Required for Nephrin Binding.

The localization of podocin variants in the absence of nephrin corresponded to previous observations (Fig. 2A) (13, 15). When co-expressed, all podocin variants colocalized with nephrin, except for R286Tfs*17 (Fig. 2B). Since R286Tfs*17 podocin is truncated right after the PHB domain, a significant role for the C-terminal helices and tail in nephrin binding was suggested. To test for this possibility, we co-expressed nephrin-mRuby3 and different podocin-eGFP variants in HEK-293 cells, and measured Förster-type Resonance Energy Transfer (FRET) between the fluorophores. FRET efficiency was calculated based on the fluorescence lifetime of the donor dye, which is primarily sensitive to changes in the intermolecular distance, as described in detail in SI Appendix, Supplementary Materials and Methods. We found the FRET to be absent only in case of R286Tfs*17 podocin (SI Appendix, Fig. S2). The colocalization between R229Q podocin and nephrin was also decreased (Fig. 2B), consistent with their decreased binding found by Tsukaguchi et al. (17). We also found a markedly decreased FRET efficiency between R229Q and nephrin compared to WT podocin (SI Appendix, Fig. S3). To further explore the underlying mechanism, we compared the competitiveness of HA-tagged wt or R229Q podocin with that of podocin-eGFP in binding mRuby3-nephrin. To ensure that HA-podocin and podocin-eGFP are expressed equally, we co-expressed them under a bidirectional promoter from the same pKK-BI16 plasmid. While wt HA-podocin reduced the FRET efficiency of the podocin-eGFP/mRuby3-nephrin pair, hence reflecting its competition for nephrin-binding, the effect of R229Q HA-podocin was lower (SI Appendix, Fig. S4), confirming its reduced nephrin-binding capacity.

Podocin Decreases the Nephrin–Nephrin Distance.

To assess the distance between nephrin molecules in cis, we measured FRET efficiency between differently labeled nephrin molecules in living HEK-293 cells (Fig. 3). We interpret the changes in FRET efficiency between nephrin molecules as changes in the average intermolecular distance between donor and acceptor fluorophores. Coexpression of wt podocin markedly increased the FRET efficiency, reflecting reduced and ordered nephrin–nephrin distances (Fig. 3 C and D), which points at the build-up of a more closely and evenly spaced nephrin array. Similar results were obtained with intracellular C-terminal labeling (P = 5.17E-14, Fig. 3 A and C) and with extracellular juxtamembranous labeling (P = 1.63E-9, Fig. 3 B and D).

Nephrin–Nephrin Distance Is a Function of Podocin Oligomerization.

We next evaluated the effect of known benign (R229Q) and pathogenic (R138Q, A284V, R286Tfs, V290M) homo-oligomeric podocin variants on nephrin–nephrin distance with both intra- and extracellular nephrin labeling. While the pathogenic R138Q, A284V, R286Tfs, and V290M variants did not decrease the nephrin–nephrin distance, the effect of the benign R229Q variant was similar to the WT, assessed either by intracellular (SI Appendix, Fig. S5) or extracellular labeling (Fig. 4). The effect of the hypomorphic V290M (36) was less severe than that of the null R138Q with extracellular labeling (P = 1.56E-3).

The Localization of Pathogenic R229Q Oligomers Is Restored by Nephrin.

Although the membrane-targeting of nephrin was not significantly modified by podocin variants (Fig. 2B), it was the least membrane-localized in the presence of R138Q podocin, in accordance with previous findings of Nishibori et al. (37). By contrast, we found the membrane targeting of A284V and V290M podocin variants to be significantly increased in the presence of nephrin. Intriguingly, A284V podocin thus became membrane-localized as reflected by Manders’ coefficient: 0.614 (0.39, 0.858) [median (quartiles)] in the presence of nephrin vs. 0.199 [0.071, 0.453] in its absence (P = 1.11E-04; Fig. 2B). We confirmed this striking effect of nephrin on the membrane targeting of R229Q-A284V heterooligomers in podocytes (P = 0.0432 for A284V, P = 1.68E-03 for R229Q, Fig. 2C). The membrane-targeting of the pathogenic R229Q/3’var associations may explain the related milder phenotype (Fig. 5), but refutes our earlier explanation of their pathogenicity (15).

Pathogenicity of Specific R229Q Oligomers Is Mediated by an Altered Nephrin–Nephrin Distance.

To explain the pathogenicity of specific R229Q/3’var heterooligomers, we hypothesized that they also alter the nephrin–nephrin distance. We therefore coexpressed podocin variant pairs with the extracellularly tagged nephrin molecules and measured the FRET efficiency between the nephrin molecules as described above. To ascertain the equivalent expression of the two podocin variants, the cDNA constructs were cloned into the same KK-BI16 plasmid under a bidirectional CMV promoter.

The results were similar to the podocin monoexpression experiments. We found that benign R229Q associations R229Q-wt, R229Q-R138Q, and R229Q-V290M increased the FRET efficiency (P = 4.1E-5), thus contributed to the build-up of a closely ordered nephrin repeat motif, while pathogenic associations R229Q-A284V, R229Q-A297V, R229Q-R291W, and R229Q-F344Lfs did not (Fig. 6B and SI Appendix, Fig. S6). The difference between the effect of pathogenic and benign associations was highly significant (P = 1.19E-33), and the 0.56 cut-off value of normalized FRET efficiency could distinguish the known benign and pathogenic associations in 100% (72/72) of the measurements, giving a perfect specificity and sensitivity in detecting pathogenic associations.

Only the effect of the R229Q-R286Tfs*17 association did not fit in this pattern, giving an intermediate FRET efficiency (Fig. 6B). Although we previously considered this association to be benign (38), five unrelated patients have recently been referred to us with [R229Q];[R286Tfs*17]. They all presented with late-onset focal segmental glomerular sclerosis and no other etiology. Together with two formerly published families (39), they clearly indicated the pathogenicity of this association (SI Appendix, Table S1), causing the least progressive NPHS2-associated phenotype currently known (Fig. 5). The pathogenicity of this association is nevertheless challenging to interpret, given that the hypomorphic R286Tfs*17 podocin variant neither binds nephrin (SI Appendix, Fig. S1), nor oligomerizes with any podocin variants (16), including R229Q (Fig. 6A).

The Effect of Podocin–Podocin Interaction on Nephrin–Nephrin Distance.

We previously determined the FRET efficiency between the maleimide-coordinated cysteine residues of the PHB domains in R229Q-R286Tfs*17 and R229Q-F344Lfs*4 heterooligomers in solution (16). Here, we complemented these measurements with different benign and pathogenic R229Q/3’var heterooligomers (Fig. 6A). Podocin contains six cysteines: C101, C124, C158, C193, C254, and C349. Since the results we obtained here are virtually identical to those previously measured in constructs starting with residue 168 (16), and also considering that neither R286Tfs*17 nor F344Lfs*4 contains C349, only C193 and C254 remain the possible coordination sites for the dye. C193 points toward the inside of the PHB domain, surrounded and buried by L172, I174, V214, V218, V260, and I263, while C254 points outward from the PHB and is solvent accessible, according to both our previously proposed structural model (16) and the model available in the AlphaFold database (40). We therefore concluded that C254 is most likely labeled by the dye, and thus the podocin–podocin FRET efficiency is indicative of the distance and relative orientation of the PHB domains in oligomers, but also of the Kd of complex formation between the differently labeled variants. The R229Q podocin showed consistently higher FRET efficiencies in heterooligomers (Fig. 6A) than the wt ones (Fig. 6A, P = 6.25E-4). Furthermore, among the R229Q oligomers, the pathogenic ones (with A284V, A297V, R291W, F344Lfs*4) resulted in higher FRET efficiency than the benign ones (with wt, R229Q, or V290M variants, P = 0.0247, Fig. 6A), confirming that their pathogenicity is not due to impaired oligomerization.

We observed a strong negative exponential correlation between podocin–podocin FRET efficiency measured in solution and nephrin–nephrin FRET efficiency measured in cells (Fig. 6C), indicating that pathogenic podocin oligomerization interferes with the assembly of an ordered nephrin structure. This correlation further supports our previous suggestion that pathogenic R229Q/3’var podocin pairs form distorted oligomers (15), reflected by increased FRET efficiency, and underscores the importance of podocin oligomer structure in regulating nephrin–nephrin distances.

Discussion

The principal role of podocytes in glomerular filtration has been made unequivocal by the identification of more than 50 causal genes in SRNS, which are all expressed in podocytes (3). These cells build up the slit diaphragm around the capillary tuft, with a pore size of 3.5 to 4 nm in its shortest dimension (18, 23). It has been recently suggested that the slit diaphragm, instead of being a size-selective barrier (41), contributes to the compressive forces that counteract the filtration pressure. Nephrin deficiency is proposed to cause albuminuria due to weakened connections between the podocyte foot processes and thus decreased compressive force (42). In the present work we aimed to assess the effect of podocin on the nephrin–nephrin distance in living cells.

We used two different FRET pairs for nephrin labeling: YPet-mRuby3 and YPet-mCherry with calculated Förster radii of 6.6 nm and 6 nm, respectively (43). Nephrin contains 8 IgG-like and a fibronectin type III domain on the N-terminal extracellular segment, followed by a 20-residue-long unstructured linker to its single transmembrane helix and C-terminal intracellular part. The YPet-mCherry systems were introduced into the nonstructured juxtamembranous segment connecting the transmembrane helix and the fibronectin domain on the extracellular stretch, while the YPet-mRuby3 pair at the intracellular C-terminus. These FRET pairs are therefore suitable to reflect changes in the size of the slit diaphragm on both sides of the podocyte membrane. We obtained similar FRET efficiency changes with both labeling, indicating that a significant distorting effect of the FRET dye labeling on the nephrin structure is unlikely. These results also demonstrated that the ordering of nephrin chains on the extracellular side is connected to their intracellular spacing—suggesting that it can be regulated from within the podocyte.

In the absence of podocin, the nephrin–nephrin distances varied widely, as reflected by the variable FRET efficiency. Experiments with both labeling revealed that podocin narrows this distribution and decreases the distance between the nephrin molecules in cis.

The effects on nephrin–nephrin distance and on clinical severity correlated perfectly for all studied podocin variants: the ER-retained R138Q (13), the distorted oligomer-forming A284V (15), the hypomorphic V290M (36), the benign R229Q (14, 15), and the monomer-forming R286Tfs*17 podocin (16).

Co-immunoprecipitation experiments have previously shown that nephrin binds to the PHB domain of podocin (residues 128 to 284) (34). Here, we found, by using FRET analysis and colocalization studies, that the R286Tfs*17 podocin, despite containing the entire PHB domain, does not bind nephrin. It indicated that the C-terminal part of podocin—beyond the PHB domain—is also required for nephrin binding, or alternatively, that the structural alterations caused by the frameshift variants (residues 286 to 301) create an interference with the nephrin binding site. Since only the monomeric R286Tfs*17 podocin did not bind nephrin, it is also tempting to speculate that dimerization of podocin is a prerequisite of efficient nephrin binding. Based on our structural model of podocin dimers, dimerization or oligomerization may order its C-terminal helices and thus create a more extended surface to bind the C-terminal nephrin tail (15).

Even though R286Tfs*17 podocin does not bind nephrin, it leads to ESKD at the median age of 14 y (16), reflecting some residual function. This suggests that podocin has other functions besides controlling nephrin spacing. Along this line, it remains also to be explored how pathogenic podocin variants lead to decreased podocyte survival and glomerulosclerosis.

We previously proposed—based on molecular dynamics simulations—that the R229Q variant of podocin enhances the interaction between the globular PHB domain and the helical tail (15) that is the primary oligomerization region of podocin (16), resulting in a well-defined, but less flexible structure. Along this line, here, we experimentally observed that the oligomerization process of podocin is affected by R229Q. Furthermore, in accordance with previous data (17), we also confirmed that R229Q podocin binds nephrin less effectively than wt podocin.

Nephrin-binding podocin variants colocalized with nephrin in all organelles. Not only did they colocalize, but nephrin influenced the localization of podocin variants, and made A284V podocin and R229Q-A284V heterooligomers membranous. The R138Q podocin was similarly found by Nishibori et al. to retain nephrin in the ER (37). Here, we found that podocin variants made the FRET between nephrin chains, as measured in living cells, highly uniform and specific. All these observations support the notion that the nephrin–podocin meshwork is already formed in the ER.

We previously explained the pathogenicity of specific R229Q/3’var associations by an altered oligomerization and disrupted membrane-targeting. However, these results were obtained in the absence of nephrin (15, 16). Furthermore, the nonmembranous localization contradicted the mildness of the associated phenotype. Here, we showed that R229Q/3’var heterooligomers reach the membrane when bound to nephrin. By contrast, while the benign R229Q/3’var oligomers induce the shrinking of the nephrin–nephrin distance in a similar manner to WT podocin, the pathogenic oligomers did not. The effect of pathogenic and benign associations was so sharply separated that we propose to employ this method in the assessment of unknown R229Q associations in the future.

We previously considered the [R229Q];[R286Tfs*17] association to be benign (15, 38) for several reasons: The frequent R286Tfs*17 variant was not enriched in patients with R229Q, and in contrast to all other R229Q-associated variants, R286Tfs*17 podocin does not oligomerize, and is perfectly membranous even in the absence of nephrin (15, 16, 38). The former (39) and the current finding of [R229Q];[R286Tfs*17] in a total of seven unrelated patients with late-onset FSGS indicate its pathogenicity. Nevertheless, this association remains exceptional for several reasons. Based on the high European allele frequency of R286Tfs*17 (0.017%) the expected prevalence of [R229Q];[R286Tfs*17] in Europe (1/75k) is more than an order of magnitude higher than the cumulative prevalence of all common R229Q-associations (with A284V, A288T, A297V, R291W, E310K, or F344Lfs*4) or that of homozygous R138Q, and roughly corresponds to the prevalence of hereditary podocytopathies of any kind. Given that only a few patients have been identified, the penetrance of [R229Q];[R286Tfs*17] is expected to be low. This is in accordance with the exceptionally mild phenotype of the presented patients, family members with no proteinuria at the age of 21 y (Pt3, SI Appendix, Table S1), or a normal renal function at the age of 38 (Pt2, SI Appendix, Table S1) and the moderate increase of the related nephrin–nephrin distance presented herein. Given that R286Tfs*17 podocin cannot oligomerize, it is challenging to explain the pathogenicity of [R229Q];[R286Tfs*17]. A plausible explanation might be that R286Tfs*17 podocin, lacking the internalization domain (44), and being more membrane-localized than the WT podocin (16), interferes with the rigid R229Q oligomers in lipid raft association, by potentially hiding binding sites.

Based on the strong correlation between nephrin spacing and clinical severity associated with pathogenic podocin variants, including R229Q associations, we propose that the principal function of podocin is the regulation of nephrin spacing. Other slit diaphragm components, that were not examined in this model, particularly NEPH1 and CD2AP, might also be considered to contribute to this effect. The transmembrane NEPH1 does not form homooligomers (45), making it unlikely that it would influence the long distance ordering of nephrin spacing. In contrast, the cytosolic CD2AP forms tetramers (46) and interacts with both podocin and nephrin, therefore it might be a modulator of their spatial organization. Nevertheless, the severity of the related nephrotic syndrome indicates that podocin dysfunction cannot be compensated by parallel mechanisms.

In conclusion, we found that podocin oligomers provide an evenly spaced anchoring site for the intracellular segments of nephrin chains. This effect is abolished by pathogenic podocin variants.

Nephrin restores membrane targeting of pathogenic R229Q oligomers. Their altered structure however results in proportionally increased nephrin spacing, thereby explaining the associated mild phenotype.

The [R229Q];[R286Tfs*17] association is pathogenic, but via a mechanism different from the other R229Q associations. It is associated with an exceptionally mild phenotype and even incomplete penetrance, which is unique among NPHS2-related podocytopathies.

In sum, podocin, the most abundant protein involved in SRNS, plays a crucial role in the architecture of the slit diaphragm, which explains the proteinuria associated with NPHS2 variants.

Materials and Methods

Details for all methods are provided in SI Appendix, Materials and Methods.

Generation of Nephrin- and Podocin-Coding Plasmids.

C-terminally labeled nephrin proteins were generated by cloning human WT nephrin cDNA in-frame with Y-Pet and mRuby3 cDNA in YPet-N1 plasmid. Extracellularly tagged nephrin constructs were generated by inserting YPet and mCherry cDNA in the juxtamembranous region of nephrin cDNA. The human influenza hemagglutinin (HA) tagged podocin variants, eGFP-tagged WT podocin and R229Q constructs were created as described previously (15, 16). eGFP-tagged variants were generated by either site-directed mutagenesis or cloning out unnecessary regions. To express two podocin variants on the same plasmid, the cDNA sequences were cloned in pKK-BI16-ORF1-3C-mRuby3_ORF2-TEV-mClover3 plasmid.

Cell Culture.

HEK-293 cells were grown at 37 °C in high glucose DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% Penicillin-Streptomycin. A human immortalized podocyte cell line (AB8/13) provided by M. A. Saleem (University of Bristol, UK) (47) was cultured in RPMI-1640 supplemented with 10% FBS, insulin-transferrin selenium, and 50 IU/mL penicillin/streptomycin at a growth-permissive temperature of 33 °C.

Transfection.

HEK-293 cells and podocytes were transfected with nephrin- and podocin-encoding plasmids with Lipofectamine 3000, according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

FRET Measurement.

FRET in living HEK-293 cells was measured either between two differently labeled nephrin variants or between mRuby3-labeled nephrin and eGFP-labeled podocin with a Chronos BH Time correlated Single Photon Counting lifetime spectrometer (ISS Inc., Champaign, IL). FRET between differently stained PHB domains of podocin variants in solution was measured as described previously (16).

Confocal Microscopy.

Transfected HEK-293 cells were incubated with CF405-conjugated wheat germ agglutinin (WGA; Biotium #29027) to stain plasma membrane, then fixed with 4% PFA. Membrane localization of R229Q-A284V podocin heterooligomers was studied in transfected podocytes. Podocyte cells were incubated with ice-cold CF405-conjugated WGA, fixed and permeabilized with ice-cold ethanol, blocked with 1% BSA, and incubated with mouse anti-HA and rabbit anti-V5 (Sigma) followed by AlexaFluor 647-conjugated donkey anti-mouse and AlexaFluor 488-conjugated chicken anti-rabbit (Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher Scientific) secondary antibodies.

Confocal optical slices were captured using a 100× or 60× oil-immersion objective lens. The perimembranous region and membrane targeting of nephrin and podocin were analyzed by Fiji (ImageJ) software with the help of JaCoP plugin. The subcellular colocalization of the coexpressed nephrin and podocin was characterized by Pearson’s correlation coefficient, determined by a macroprogram utilizing the Coloc2 plugin in Fiji, adapted from the work of Moser et al. (48, 49).

Renal Survival of Patients with NPHS2 Variants.

Renal survival data of patients with NPHS2 variants were collected from the literature (37, 50–62) as described previously by Mikó et al. (described in SI Appendix, Supplementary Materials and Methods) (63). The collected data were filtered for patients with available renal function data, then categorized by variant type: Only patients with either homozygous R138Q (n = 42), [R229Q];[R286Tfs*17] genotype (n = 2), other pathogenic R229Q associations (n = 55) or other biallelic pathogenic variants compatible with membrane localization (n = 18), were included. Data from patients with [R229Q];[R286Tfs*17] were also included from the Paris, Bristol, and Nottingham cohorts (n = 9). Kaplan–Meier analysis of renal survival rates with subsequent Bonferroni correction was conducted in JupyterLab (version 3.6.7).

Statistical Analysis.

Nephrin–nephrin FRET efficiency values were normalized to the average of the daily parallel measurements with WT podocin. Normally distributed data were compared by one-way ANOVA, followed by Tukey Honest Significance Difference post hoc test. Data with skewed distribution were compared by Kruskall–Wallis ANOVA, followed by Mann–Whitney U test with Bonferroni correction. The membrane localization of R229Q and A284V in the presence and absence of nephrin was compared by t test. The effect of R229Q on the heterooligomers (all but the R286Tfs associations) were compared by Wilcoxon matched pair test. Statistical analyses were performed by Statistica software version 13.5 (Tibco Software).

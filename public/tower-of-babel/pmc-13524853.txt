Author's reply to the Letters regarding ‘Selecting between UKA and HTO in the grey zone’
Authors: Jianbin Guo, Zhibo Liu, Yong Ding
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We highly appreciate the opportunity to respond to the insightful commentary entitled Selecting between UKA and HTO in the grey zone by Jie Shen et al. which refers to our recently published article How to choose treatment for patients who are eligible for both unicompartmental knee arthroplasty and high tibial osteotomy? [1]. The commentators have raised several valuable suggestions, emphasizing that more attention should be paid to mechanical factors and other determinants affecting surgical decision-making, and that the prognostic evaluation of patients needs to be more patient-centered and diversified. Herein, we provide the corresponding clarifications as follows.

First, with respect to preoperative factor analysis, a clear distinction should be made between general prognostic factors and those that independently influence surgical decision-making. We fully agree with this viewpoint, and this principle should be fully incorporated into clinical decision-making for surgical selection. Traditional criteria for surgical selection are no longer sufficient to achieve personalized treatment goals. Further expansion of evaluation dimensions is therefore warranted to validate and update the current surgical indications. Accordingly, we recommend that future studies build upon the factors identified in our work to further quantify their prognostic impact following different surgical procedures. Assigning weighted scores to these factors may facilitate the development of a novel scoring system, which could guide personalized surgical decision-making for patients within the ‘grey zone’ of surgical indications. Several prognostic scoring systems have been developed for HTO, among which the SKOOP score is a validated tool for risk stratification. A score >3 is associated with a higher risk of HTO failure, whereas a score ≤3 indicates that HTO may be an appropriate surgical option [2]. Stratified comparative analyses have indicated that UKA may provide superior revision rates and functional outcomes in selected populations. Nevertheless, in younger, more active patients and those with higher BMI, HTO remains an important joint-preserving procedure that can delay the need for arthroplast [3]. Collectively, these findings indicate that integrating stratified analyses with weighted scoring approaches may facilitate patient-specific selection between HTO and UKA, representing a promising direction for future research.

Second, greater emphasis should be placed on mechanical factors, particularly joint line orientation and femorotibial alignment. Both intra-articular and extra-articular deformities are critical determinants of surgical decision-making, as reflected in current indication criteria and supported by recent evidence [4]. This study utilized weight-bearing full-length lower limb radiographs to assess key alignment parameters, including the proximal tibial and distal femoral mechanical axis angles, as well as the joint line convergence angle. Intra-articular deformity (IAD%) and extra-articular deformity (EAD%) were further quantified to delineate the origin of malalignment. The authors suggested that UKA is preferable when deformity is predominantly intra-articular, whereas HTO is indicated for primarily extra-articular deformity. In the overlapping indication zone, more refined stratification of deformity patterns is required to optimize surgical selection, alongside consideration of non-mechanical patient-specific factors [5,6]. Bone mineral density is a key preoperative factor in selecting between UKA and HTO for medial unicompartmental knee osteoarthritis. Osteoporosis has been associated with poorer tolerance to HTO and an increased risk of adverse outcomes [7]. A comprehensive, weighted evaluation of these factors may yield a composite score that serves as a potential framework for guiding future research and clinical decision-making.

Finally, we agree that surgical decision-making should prioritize patient-centered outcomes and the long-term burden of revision surgery over short-term symptom relief. Accordingly, we adopted a multidimensional outcome framework, including symptom improvement [8], implant survival, revision rate [9] and functional recovery [10]. We sincerely appreciate this constructive discussion, which enhances the understanding of surgical decision-making in patients within the overlapping indication (“grey zone”) for UKA and HTO. We also thank the reviewers and the editorial team for the opportunity to clarify our findings.

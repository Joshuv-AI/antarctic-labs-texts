Accurate remaining useful life prediction and uncertainty quantification for wind turbines using temporal convolutional variational deep Gaussian processes
Authors: Shuo Cui, Xiuli Liu, Guoxin Wu, Wan Qiao, Jianyu Liu, Xuanyu Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIt remains highly challenging to extract features from monitoring signals of wind turbine components under varying operating conditions and complex structures, as well as to quantify the uncertainty of Remaining Useful Life (RUL) predictions. To address these issues, this paper proposes a method called Temporal Convolutional Variational Deep Gaussian Process (TCVDGP). This method introduces Deep Gaussian Processes (DGP) to model the nonlinear relationship between input features and RUL, and employs variational inference to efficiently estimate the posterior distribution for accurate uncertainty quantification. In addition, by integrating Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN) and a Sequential Attention Mechanism (SAM), the model captures both short- and long-term dependencies in time series data, enhancing adaptability to complex and variable operating conditions. TCVDGP ultimately produces RUL predictions with confidence intervals. Experiments conducted on planetary gearbox and industrial wind turbine gearbox datasets, combined with correlation analysis between the model’s hidden layer outputs and internal gearbox parameters, reveal the reasons behind performance differences of TCVDGP across different degradation stages. The results show that TCVDGP not only effectively captures the health evolution of wind turbine gearboxes but also dynamically adjusts to key variables, significantly improving the reliability and interpretability of RUL predictions under complex operating conditions.Keywords: Remaining useful life prediction, Interpretability, Time convolutional variational deep Gaussian Process, Deep Gaussian Process, Wind turbine gearboxSubject terms: Mechanical engineering, Computer science

Introduction

With the continuous development of human society, reducing carbon emissions and achieving carbon neutrality have become global common goals, and the development of renewable energy is the key path to achieving these goals1,2. Wind energy, as an important renewable energy source, has been widely applied. However, with the ongoing construction and operation of large wind turbines, significant accidents caused by component failures occur frequently. Therefore, fault prediction and health management of wind turbines (Prognostics and Health Management, PHM) have attracted increasing attention3–6. Remaining Useful Life (RUL) prediction, as the core of the PHM system, not only provides critical decision support for equipment maintenance and management but also effectively helps prevent failures and extends the operational lifespan of equipment.

Currently, the remaining useful life (RUL) prediction methods for wind turbine components can be mainly categorized into three types: physics-based life prediction methods, data-driven life prediction methods, and physics-data hybrid model-based life prediction methods7–9. Physics-based methods typically involve establishing performance degradation models through the equipment’s fault mechanisms. However, due to the complex structures of wind turbines in practical applications, building accurate physical models becomes extremely difficult. This limitation makes physics-based models less effective in describing the degradation process of wind turbine components10. Due to the limitations of physics-based models, the development of hybrid models has been hindered. In contrast, data-driven methods learn degradation patterns directly from sensor data, reducing dependence on physical knowledge, and are particularly suited for industrial scenarios in the era of big data. With the gradual development of data-driven technologies, these methods have become dominant in predicting the RUL of wind turbine components11.

However, accurate RUL prediction still faces numerous challenges. These challenges include the poor adaptability of models under complex and variable operating conditions, as variations in rotor speed, load, and environmental factors during wind turbine operation can cause feature distribution drift and affect prediction stability; the highly nonlinear, stage-dependent, and non-stationary nature of component degradation processes, which makes it difficult for a single model to fully capture their evolution patterns; and the lack of effective uncertainty quantification in most existing methods, which limits their ability to provide confidence levels for prediction results to support maintenance decision-making. In recent years, deep learning techniques have been widely applied in the field of RUL prediction.

To address these challenges, deep learning techniques have been widely applied in the field of RUL prediction in recent years, demonstrating strong capabilities in automatically extracting complex degradation features and handling high-dimensional data12,13. For example, the combination of Deep Belief Networks (DBN) and Self-Organizing Maps (SOM) can effectively extract degradation features of equipment, thereby improving the accuracy of RUL prediction14. The weighted time-embedded Transformer framework further optimizes prediction accuracy by integrating 3D convolution modules with moving window Transformer blocks15. Parallel Neural Networks (PNN), through parallel processing paths and ConvLSTM modules, efficiently capture degradation trends in complex time series data, excelling particularly in bearing RUL prediction16,17 revealed bearing degradation by constructing health indicators (HI) that integrate multi-scale features with distribution similarity, optimizing HI characteristics using Wasserstein distance and multi-objective optimization, and enhancing prediction accuracy and generalization ability with gated recurrent unit networks.

RUL prediction under varying operating conditions is a major challenge in industrial applications18,19, and researchers have proposed various methods to address this issue. Among them, the adaptive dynamic feedback framework significantly improves prediction robustness through feedback mechanisms and multi-model selection strategies, particularly in the prediction of wind turbine gearbox RUL under varying conditions20. Additionally, the adaptive generalized Cauchy model explains long-term dependencies by combining fractal dimension and Hurst index, and adapts to changes in degradation trends using dynamic drift coefficients, thereby effectively enhancing prediction accuracy21. In feature extraction, a novel dual-attention gated recurrent unit (GRU) neural network is proposed, which significantly improves model performance by enhancing adaptive feature extraction capabilities22. Meanwhile, the TCN-Transformer-based prediction framework enhances the stability of features under complex operating conditions through Kalman filtering for noise reduction and nonlinear smoothing techniques23. Finally, an RUL prediction method based on health state and trajectory similarity constructs a lifetime database through K-means clustering, and achieves higher prediction accuracy by combining similarity measurements and weighted matching strategies24. These methods provide diverse solutions for RUL prediction under varying operating conditions.

At present, most studies on deep learning-based RUL prediction focus on achieving higher point estimation accuracy. For example, to overcome the limitations of manually designed auxiliary tasks25 proposed a multi-task learning method based on a meta-auxiliary generative network; to balance accuracy and computational complexity26 adopted a multi-objective evolutionary deep network27 employed a GRU-LSTM fusion model to enhance feature extraction capability. These studies have significantly improved the prediction performance of RUL.

However, there is significant uncertainty in the lifetime prediction of wind turbines. Taking the planetary gearbox as an example: First, the feature extraction process, which involves human participation, often relies on the prior experience of experts and has a certain degree of subjectivity. Secondly, under varying operating conditions, the fault characteristic frequencies and their amplitudes of the gears change with the speed and load. When the speed changes, the gear meshing frequency and fault characteristic frequency change accordingly. When the load changes, it causes speed fluctuations, which also result in changes to the gear meshing frequency and fault characteristic frequency. Fault features detected at a certain time may be caused by randomly changing operating conditions, resulting in pseudo-fault signals. Furthermore, the complex transmission structure causes the fault shock signals of components such as gears and bearings inside the gearbox to couple with vibration components from high-speed and low-speed shafts. These factors introduce significant uncertainty in the RUL prediction of wind turbine gearboxes. To improve prediction accuracy, an incremental contrastive hybrid model, combined with contrastive learning Transformer and enhanced generalized Wiener process, has been proposed for online prediction and uncertainty quantification28. Additionally, through the Gaussian Process Autoregressive-Variational Autoencoder framework, cognitive and stochastic uncertainties are decomposed, enhancing prediction reliability and optimizing prediction efficiency through active learning29. Moreover, the proposed multi-distribution fusion method adaptively integrates multiple candidate predictions, significantly improving adaptability and uncertainty quantification accuracy under complex operating conditions30. However, most existing deep learning methods can only perform point predictions and cannot provide uncertainty in prediction results, which greatly limits the practical application of deep learning in the RUL prediction of wind turbine components31,32.

To address the challenges of extracting time-varying features from long sequences and the uncertainty in prediction results for wind turbine component lifetime prediction, this paper proposes a Time Convolutional Variational Deep Gaussian Process (TCVDGP) method. This method innovatively integrates the Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) with the Sequential Attention Mechanism (SAM) into the internal structure of the Deep Gaussian Process (DGP). By improving the internal algorithm of the DGP, the method not only significantly enhances the model’s prediction performance but also combines correlation analysis between the model’s hidden layer outputs and internal gearbox parameters to deeply reveal the reasons for performance differences in RUL prediction across different stages (early, mid, and late), thereby significantly improving the model’s interpretability. Additionally, TCVDGP employs variational inference to efficiently estimate posterior distributions, providing accurate uncertainty quantification for the model, further enhancing the reliability and credibility of the prediction results.

RUL prediction based on TCVDGP

Temporal attention module

In convolution operations, different feature maps have varying recognition capabilities for RUL prediction, and within each feature map, the importance of the lifetime features at different positions also differs. Therefore, it is essential to suppress redundant or irrelevant information during the computation process and enhance the lifetime-related information. To address this issue, this paper proposes a novel attention mechanism — the Temporal Attention Module (SAM). This module is designed to solve the problem of effectively enhancing useful information and suppressing redundant information in one-dimensional convolution operations by learning more discriminative lifetime features from the input one-dimensional signals34.

SAM can specifically enhance the focus on lifetime-related information, improving the model’s sensitivity to key features, thereby effectively optimizing the performance of convolution operations. This is particularly significant when combined with TCN and DGP for RUL prediction.

TCVDGP

DGP, as a flexible non-parametric model, is capable of capturing complex nonlinear relationships and quantifying the uncertainty of prediction results. However, its application in high-dimensional time series data still faces two key challenges: first, the DGP method lacks a mechanism to handle the dynamic characteristics of time series data, making it difficult to fully explore the short-term and long-term dependencies in the data; second, DGP lacks dynamic adjustment capabilities, which makes it challenging to effectively focus on the key features in time series data. To address these issues, the TCVDGP method is proposed. By dynamically optimizing the covariance matrix and improving variational inference, TCVDGP enhances the model’s ability to capture temporal features and improves prediction accuracy.

RUL prediction process of TCVDGP.

In the second layer, the attention weights of the features are dynamically computed using SAM, enabling the model to adaptively focus on key features and optimize feature representation. Next, by combining the RBF kernel function with the dynamically adjusted attention mechanism, the ability to model the correlation between data is further enhanced. Then, the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) objective function is used for optimization. By maximizing the ELBO, the model approximates the true posterior distribution. ELBO optimization not only effectively improves the model’s ability to handle complex nonlinear features, but also enhances the model’s inference efficiency in high-dimensional data by optimizing the variational distribution and inducing point parameters.

Finally, a distribution containing the predicted mean and standard deviation is generated. The predicted mean represents the central tendency of the RUL, while the standard deviation reflects the uncertainty in the prediction. Based on this distribution, the RUL prediction value and its confidence interval are calculated, providing a quantified level of confidence for the prediction result.

If the predicted RUL is less than the true RUL, the penalty is smaller because there is still time for maintenance, and no major system failure will occur. If the predicted RUL is greater than the true RUL, the penalty is larger, because in this case, the maintenance plan will be delayed, potentially leading to system failure. This asymmetric design enables the SCORE metric to distinguish between the different consequences of overestimation and underestimation, making it more consistent with practical maintenance safety requirements.

Eq. (22) represents the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), which is commonly used to evaluate RUL predictions. RMSE imposes equal penalties on overestimation and underestimation, serving as a classical and intuitive metric for measuring prediction error, which can reflect the overall accuracy. Whether the predicted value is too high or too low, RMSE assigns the same penalty weight. Compared with traditional methods that rely solely on RMSE, the SCORE metric can more realistically characterize prediction risks in maintenance scenarios. In industrial equipment life prediction, overly optimistic estimates (i.e., overestimating the remaining life) are often more dangerous than conservative ones (i.e., underestimation); therefore, higher weights should be assigned to overestimation errors. In this study, both RMSE and SCORE are jointly employed — RMSE measures the prediction accuracy, while SCORE reflects the model’s capability to control risks related to maintenance safety and cost-effectiveness, thereby providing a more practical evaluation basis for wind turbine component health management.

AGCN leverages graph convolutional networks to model inter-component relationships and incorporates attention mechanisms to enhance information propagation, but its model construction relies on prior topological knowledge. Conclusion

This paper proposes a wind turbine gearbox health monitoring method based on TCVDGP, providing innovative solutions for varying operating conditions and uncertainty quantification. To address the varying operating conditions, a TCN module is integrated within the TCVDGP framework, significantly enhancing the model’s adaptability to complex time-series data and changing operational conditions by capturing both short- and long-term temporal dependencies. Additionally, an innovative SAM algorithm is proposed to dynamically adjust feature weights, improving the extraction of discriminative features and effectively suppressing redundant information. Concerning uncertainty quantification, this paper deeply integrates TCN and SAM into the DGP, optimizing the DGP’s capability to model complex features. Moreover, by constructing confidence intervals, the proposed method accurately characterizes the uncertainty associated with prediction outcomes.

The proposed TCVDGP method was compared with the conventional DGP approach through ablation experiments on a self-collected planetary gear dataset and an industrial wind turbine gearbox dataset. Experimental results indicate that the TCVDGP method significantly outperforms the traditional DGP in terms of accuracy for RUL prediction, and also demonstrates superior performance in uncertainty quantification. Furthermore, comparative experiments against seven classical methods and state-of-the-art approaches proposed within the past three years further validate the superiority of the TCVDGP method, particularly highlighting its exceptional performance under complex operating conditions.

Through interpretability analysis of the TCVDGP model, this paper further explores the underlying reasons for variations in RUL prediction accuracy at different stages (early, middle, and late). By analyzing the correlation coefficients between the outputs of the model’s hidden layers and internal gearbox parameters, the study explains differences in prediction performance across various stages. Experimental results show that the TCVDGP model maintains high prediction accuracy throughout all stages, particularly demonstrating sensitivity to changes in health status during middle and late stages. This enables effective capturing of the health evolution trends of wind turbine gearboxes. These results indicate that by focusing on key features and dynamically adjusting the weights of important variables, the proposed model achieves comprehensive and precise predictive capabilities, even under complex operational conditions.

The TCVDGP method enhances the accuracy and reliability of RUL predictions for wind turbine gearboxes, thus optimizing maintenance strategies, reducing operational costs, and facilitating efficient operations and maintenance within the wind power industry. By accurately forecasting the health status of wind turbine gearboxes, this method significantly contributes to the advancement of sustainable energy, providing robust support toward achieving global carbon neutrality goals.

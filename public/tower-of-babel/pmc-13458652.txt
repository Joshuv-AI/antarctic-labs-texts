Prostate cancer detection using modified transformer with optimal feature selection from MRI images
Authors: M R Prathap, K S Vairavel, C Kumar, Abdullah Alwabli
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractProstate cancer is one of the most prevalent malignancies among men, and early detection through Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) plays a crucial role in improving patient outcomes. However, accurate identification of cancerous regions remains challenging due to image noise, variations in tissue structures, and subtle differences between benign and malignant lesions. To address these challenges, a novel automated detection framework named Prostate Cancer Detection Neural Network (ProstateNet) that enhances the precision of prostate cancer detection is proposed. The proposed model begins with MRI image preprocessing using a Bilateral Filter, which effectively reduces noise while preserving edge details. Next, feature extraction is performed using a Residual Recurrent Model (RRM), which captures spatial dependencies and fine-grained image representations. To refine the extracted features, we employ the Iterative Secrecy Bird Optimization (ISBO) algorithm, ensuring optimal feature selection and reducing computational complexity. Finally, disease detection is carried out using a Modified Transformer model, which considers CapsuleNet instead of Feed Forward Network to accurately classify cancerous and non-cancerous regions. The integration of these advanced techniques significantly enhances the robustness and reliability of prostate cancer detection, offering a promising outcome.Keywords: Secrecy Bird Optimization, Bilateral Filter, Residual Network, Feature Selection, Prostate Cancer DetectionSubject terms: Cancer, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Oncology

Introduction

Prostate cancer is a significant health concern, being the most common malignancy among men in several countries, including Australia, where approximately 25,000 men are diagnosed annually, leading to over 3,500 deaths each year1. The exact causes of prostate cancer remain unclear; however, risk factors such as age, family history, and diets high in saturated fats have been identified2. Early detection of prostate cancer is crucial, as it significantly improves survival rates and allows for a broader range of treatment options. Early-stage prostate cancer often presents with minimal or no symptoms, making proactive screening vital3,4. Digital rectal examination (DRE) and the prostate-specific antigen (PSA) blood test are examples of conventional detection techniques. Increased PSA levels may be a sign of cancer. The PSA test quantifies the amount of PSA in the blood. However, because elevated PSA levels can also be caused by benign illnesses such prostatitis or benign prostatic hyperplasia, PSA testing has been criticized for its lack of specificity, which results in over-diagnosis and overtreatment5. The DRE has limits in terms of sensitivity and specificity, but it uses a physical examination of the prostate gland to identify abnormalities.

Based on pathological grading, PSA level, and tumor stage, prostate cancer is generally divided into clinically significant and clinically non-significant diseases in clinical practice. According to the TNM (Tumor, Node, Metastasis) staging system, clinically relevant prostate cancer usually refers to aggressive tumors that need to be treated definitively and are linked to Gleason Grade Group ≥ 2, PSA levels typically greater than 10 ng/mL, or clinical stage ≥ T2b. These tumors are linked to an increased risk of progression, extracapsular extension, or metastasis and frequently match PI-RADS 4–5 lesions on multiparametric MRI. On the other hand, Gleason Grade Group 1, PSA values usually less than 10 ng/mL, and clinical stage T1–T2a are widely used to describe indolent or low-risk prostate cancer. Active surveillance is often used to control these tumors instead of prompt intervention. In order to prevent overtreatment and ensure prompt management of aggressive illness, it is crucial to distinguish between clinically significant and non-significant prostate cancer. The proposed approach in this research carries out binary classification in accordance with this widely recognized risk stratification framework.

Traditional machine learning (ML) methods like support vector machines (SVMs), k-nearest neighbours (KNN), and decision trees have been employed to enhance the accuracy of prostate cancer detection6. These approaches often rely on handcrafted features extracted from medical images or clinical data, which can be time-consuming and may not capture the complex patterns associated with prostate cancer7,8. Additionally, these models may struggle with generalization due to limited datasets and the inherent variability in medical imaging that leads to challenges in accurately distinguishing between malignant and benign cases9,10. Deep learning (DL) techniques like convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have been developed to address these challenges by automatically learning hierarchical feature representations from raw data, thereby reducing the dependency on manual feature extraction11. For instance, a study by12 proposed a DL pipeline combining U-Net with long short-term memory (LSTM) networks for the segmentation and classification of multi-parametric MRI images, demonstrating improved diagnostic precision and consistency. Similarly,13 highlighted that DL models trained with digital pathologist labels achieved superior performance in prostate cancer detection across different cohorts, irrespective of the model architecture used14. These advancements suggest that DL methods can effectively overcome the limitations of traditional ML approaches by providing more accurate and reliable diagnostic tools for prostate cancer detection15.

For the identification of prostate cancer, current research has used transfer learning models like ResNet, EfficientNet, and ensemble CNN frameworks; nevertheless, these methods still have significant drawbacks16,17. Local feature extraction is the main emphasis of pre-trained CNN-based models, which frequently fall short in capturing global contextual relationships across MRI slices a crucial component of accurate tumor classification18,19. Furthermore, a lot of current approaches rely significantly on sizable annotated datasets, which makes them vulnerable to over-fitting when used with sparse medical data20. Gaussian and Rician noise, two types of noise that are inherent in MRI imaging, further impair classification reliability because the majority of deep learning pipelines do not have specific edge-preserving noise suppression algorithms21. Furthermore, while ensemble techniques improve accuracy, they also increase inference time and decrease clinical practicality22. High-dimensional feature representations, in addition, increase computational burden and limit interpretability when improperly optimized. To overcome the challenges, this research introduces a novel deep learning model. The proposed ProstateNet framework introduces an integrated architecture that combines bilateral filtering for effective noise reduction while preserving anatomical structures, a Residual Recurrent Model to combined model deep spatial and contextual dependencies, an ISBO algorithm for selecting the most discriminative feature subsets to reduce redundancy and enhance generalization, and a Modified Transformer enhanced with Capsule Network integration to preserve hierarchical spatial relationships and improve discrimination between cancerous and non-cancerous tissues.

The proposed ProstateNet presents a fundamentally redefined learning architecture through three major innovations, compared to current transformer-based or CNN-based prostate cancer detection frameworks that mostly rely on architectural stacking or transfer learning. First, a dynamic routing-based Capsule Network is used in place of the traditional position-wise Feed Forward Network (FFN) in the transformer architecture. This allows for the preservation of hierarchical spatial relationships within MRI tokens, which is not possible with standard vision transformers used for prostate cancer detection. Second, the proposed ISBO uses iterative chaotic mapping within Secretary Bird Optimization in place of traditional feature pruning or attention-based selection. This introduces non-linear search dynamics that explicitly control exploration–exploitation balance during feature selection and prevent premature convergence. Additionally, by combining residual CNN feature hierarchies with SRU-based contextual modeling, the Residual Recurrent Model (RRM) creates a hybrid spatial–sequential representation mechanism that enables MRI slices to be understood as structured dependency sequences as opposed to separate spatial patches.

For sequential feature modeling, the proposed ProstateNet uses a Simple Recurrent Unit (SRU) rather than a traditional LSTM or GRU because SRU allows parallel computation and faster convergence while maintaining contextual dependencies in MRI features, ensuring an ideal balance between modeling capacity and computational efficiency. Additionally, the Capsule Network (CapsNet) replaces the conventional Transformer Feed Forward Network (FFN) in order to better retain hierarchical spatial linkages and part-whole anatomical structures that are crucial for prostate MRI analysis. In order to retain excellent diagnostic accuracy while keeping computing complexity within clinically practical bounds, the architectural depth and hyperparameters were established through empirical validation.

Related works

Prostate cancer detection using EfficientNet V2 was designed by23 using tabular medical data. In this, patient’s tabular data was transformed into visual representation prior to the data augmentation. EfficientNet V2 with transfer learning was designed to analyze the generated images. Finally, stacked ensemble model was employed for the disease detection. The designed model demonstrated that the proposed method is a viable alternative to invasive prostate cancer diagnostic procedures. Transforming tabular data into images may reduce interpretability of the model.

A deep learning-based technique for the accurate detection and Gleason grading of prostate cancer using MRI images was designed by24. In this, 3D CNN was employed to process the MRI volumes for epithelial detection and Gleason grading. Then, Faster R-CNN with Inception-ResNet-V2 Transfer Learning was employed for the segmentation of the lesion region. Improved prostate cancer classification based on Gleason grading was accomplished by the designed model along with precise localization and characterization of cancerous lesions. The consideration of ROIAlign layer may result in the loss of scale information that degrades the performance of the model.

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) based on the ResNet was designed by25 to classify prostate cancer. In this, digital pathology images were extracted at various patches at multiple magnification levels. The designed model capture varying tissue features from different magnification levels. The designed model demonstrated the effectiveness of ResNet models for multi-scale patch-level prostate cancer grading. The model achieved exceptional accuracy for improving the reliability of prostate cancer detection. Still, the efficiency and scalability was challenging due to the failure in optimizing the performance of ResNet model that requires careful tuning of hyper-parameters.

A modified deep learning architecture based on ResNet50 for better prostate cancer detection was designed by26. The Prostate Cancer Detection Model (PCDM) model was suggested for the automatic detection of prostate cancer. In actual healthcare settings, it demonstrates its practical usefulness to support early prostate cancer identification and treatment. Faster R-CNN and dual optimizers were integrated into the PCDM model, a modified ResNet50-based architecture, to enhance detection performance. A sizable collection of annotated medical images was used to train the model.

Weakly supervised UNet (WSUNet) model was designed by27 to develop a deep-learning model capable of detecting MRI-invisible prostate cancers. In this, utilizing a UNet-based convolutional neural network (WSUNet) the image segmentation and analysis were made. MRI-Ultrasound fusion was employed for comprehensive prostate biopsies. The designed model has the capability of detection cancers that are difficult to visualize on MRI. Still, weakly supervised learning may result in less precise predictions.

Problem statement

Traditional diagnostic methods of prostate cancer detection including biopsy and manual interpretation of MRI scans are often time-consuming, prone to human error and may lead to unnecessary invasive procedures. To address these challenges, deep learning methods were designed that provides the automated outcome without the need for manual processing. Existing deep learning-based methods for prostate cancer detection primarily include pre-trained convolutional neural networks (CNNs) like EfficientNet-v2 and ResNet, ResNet50 and ensemble learning approaches. Pre-trained models leverage transfer learning to extract features from MRI images, but they often struggle with domain-specific variations. ResNet, known for its deep architecture with residual connections, improves feature extraction but can suffer from over-fitting and high computational costs, making it less efficient for real-time applications. Ensemble learning, which combines multiple models for improved accuracy, often increases computational complexity and inference time, making it less suitable for clinical applications.

The proposed method, overcomes these challenges by leveraging a transformer-based architecture modified for medical image analysis. Unlike CNNs, transformers excel in capturing long-range dependencies and spatial relationships without being constrained by local receptive fields, leading to improved feature extraction from MRI images. The integration of an optimal feature selection technique further enhances the model’s performance by eliminating redundant and irrelevant features, reducing over-fitting and computational burden. Additionally, the proposed approach minimizes false positives and false negatives, improving diagnostic reliability. By combining the strengths of transformers with feature selection and feature extraction, the proposed method helps to achieve higher accuracy, better generalization to diverse datasets, and efficient processing, making it a more effective and practical solution for prostate cancer detection.

Proposed methodology

The proposed methodology for prostate cancer detection from MRI images integrates advanced image processing, feature extraction, optimization, and deep learning techniques to enhance accuracy and robustness. The pipeline begins with MRI image preprocessing using a Bilateral Filter, which effectively removes noise while preserving essential edge details, ensuring that critical anatomical structures are maintained. Following this, feature extraction is performed using a Residual Recurrent Model (RRM), which leverages residual connections and recurrent operations to capture both spatial dependencies and fine-grained image representations, enhancing the feature learning process. To optimize the extracted features and reduce computational complexity, the Iterative Secrecy Bird Optimization (ISBO) algorithm is applied, ensuring the selection of the most relevant and discriminative features for classification. Finally, the disease detection phase employs a Modified Transformer model, where the traditional Feed-Forward Network (FFN) is replaced with CapsNet to enhance spatial hierarchy representation and improve classification accuracy. CapsNet enables the model to better preserve spatial relationships between features, overcoming the limitations of traditional CNNs and FFNs, and improving robustness against variations in MRI images. The positions of the secretary birds in the search space dictate the values of the decision variables, and each one of them offers a potential solution to the optimization issue. When hunting snakes, secretary birds go through three different phases of their hunting behavior: seeking prey, devouring prey, and attacking prey. These stages are modelled in the SBOA to mimic the bird’s natural hunting strategies for solving optimization problems effectively.

Modified Transformer for Prostate Cancer Detection

A Modified Transformer with CapsNet instead of FFN is proposed for prostate cancer detection that leverages the strengths of both architectures to enhance accuracy and robustness in MRI image analysis. In a traditional transformer, the Feed-Forward Network (FFN) processes token embeddings after self-attention, but it treats each feature independently and lacks spatial characteristics, which is crucial in medical imaging. By replacing FFN with CapsNet, the modified transformer improves feature representation by preserving hierarchical spatial relationships within MRI scans. CapsNet uses dynamic routing to model spatial hierarchies, ensuring that the detected patterns correspond to actual tumor structures rather than unrelated textures or noise. This integration helps differentiate between cancerous and non-cancerous regions more effectively. Additionally, CapsNet’s ability to encode pose information like scale, orientation, and spatial features assist to prevent misclassification due to variations in MRI scans. The modified transformer first applies self-attention mechanisms to capture long-range dependencies in MRI images, then processes the features through CapsNet layers, allowing the model to recognize complex prostate cancer patterns while reducing false positives and false negatives. This hybrid approach enhances detection accuracy, improves model interpretability, and makes the system more robust to variations in imaging conditions, offering a powerful solution for automated prostate cancer diagnosis. The high performance achieved by the proposed ProstateNet model can be attributed to several dataset-specific and architectural factors. With 536 clinically significant and 536 clinically non-significant images in the main folders, the dataset maintains a balanced class distribution, preventing bias toward a dominating class and fostering steady training dynamics. Effective pre-processing techniques, such as image normalization, scaling, and bilateral filtering, were used before to training in order to increase generalization by lowering noise and improving feature consistency across samples. Additionally, a CapsNet-based transformer classifier and Residual Networks with SRU are integrated in the proposed feature extraction technique, allowing the model to efficiently capture both spatial hierarchies and contextual relationships. Part-whole anatomical linkages are maintained through capsule networks, which is especially significant in medical imaging where structural integrity is essential for diagnosis. Furthermore, by optimizing feature selection and convergence behavior, the ISBO algorithm directs the model toward a more ideal solution space and strengthens classification boundaries. A 70%–30% training–validation split was used to further reduce overfitting, and regularization strategies like dropout, batch normalization, and controlled training were used. The stated high accuracy is supported by the near alignment between training and validation accuracy, which suggests controlled generalization rather than memory.

Here, the superior performance of the proposed ProstateNet model is achieved through a meticulously designed architecture that combines advanced pre-processing, robust feature extraction, optimal feature selection, and a modified classification framework. Each component contributes to enhanced performance metrics such as high accuracy, precision, recall, F-Score, and specificity, along with low MSE. The detailed analysis using a confusion matrix and convergence analysis further validates that the proposed methodology provides a reliable and efficient solution for prostate cancer detection from MRI images, making it highly effective for real-world clinical use.

Conclusion

The proposed ProstateNet model for prostate cancer detection from MRI images integrates advanced deep learning techniques, feature optimization, and spatially aware classification to achieve superior diagnostic accuracy. By leveraging a Bilateral Filter for noise reduction, a RRM for feature extraction, Iterative Secrecy Bird Optimization (ISBO) for optimal feature selection, and a M-Trans, the model effectively enhances precision, recall, specificity, and F-score while reducing false positives and false negatives. The achievement of the proposed model is the ability to preserve spatial relationships within MRI images, which significantly improves classification performance compared to conventional CNN-based models. To ensure a balanced distribution of clinically significant and non-significant cases across folds, stratified k-fold cross-validation was used in the current investigation. Strict class balance was maintained in both training and validation sets, and data splitting was carried out at the image level. However, there was no explicit enforcement of patient-level separation, which might result in data leakage from several images of the same patient appearing in distinct folds. This restriction has been recognized, and in order to improve generalization reliability even further, future research will include stringent patient-wise cross-validation and multi-institutional external validation. Additionally, ability techniques such as attention visualization and interpretability models could be explored to make the predictions more transparent for clinical applications.

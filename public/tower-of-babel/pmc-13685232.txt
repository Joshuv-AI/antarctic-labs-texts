Accelerated intermittent theta burst stimulation (iTBS) for the upper limb recovery after subacute stroke (ATLAS): study protocol for a multicenter randomized controlled trial
Authors: Hong Yang, Tian Shu, Chunyu Duan, Lin Chen, Xin Zhang, Xinyue Xu, Yiyu Chen, Han Chen, Jingming Hou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundStroke is the second leading cause of death globally, with residual upper limb dysfunction being a major contributor to its high disability rate. Currently, there remains a lack of effective rehabilitation approaches to substantially improve upper limb dysfunction. While transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) has shown potential in promoting functional reorganization in stroke patients, more effective treatment protocols are still under exploration. In recent years, numerous clinical evidence has indicated that intermittent theta burst stimulation (iTBS) may offer a more efficient pathway for the upper limb recovery after stroke. A few studies have employed accelerated iTBS protocols, preliminarily confirming its safety and efficacy, yet large-sample comparative studies against the standard protocol are lacking. This ATLAS trial aims to compare the effects of accelerated iTBS versus a standard TMS protocol on upper limb recovery and overall functional outcomes in patients with subacute stroke.MethodsThis is a prospective, single-blind, multicenter, randomized controlled clinical trial. A total of 240 patients in the subacute phase of stroke will be recruited from 13 hospitals across 11 cities. Participants will be randomly allocated to either an iTBS group or a low-frequency repetitive TMS (LF-rTMS) group. Both groups will receive treatment 5 days per week for three consecutive weeks. In addition, all participants will receive standard conventional rehabilitation therapies, including physical agent modalities, therapeutic exercise, occupational therapy, and other commonly used clinical rehabilitation approaches. The primary outcome is the Fugl-Meyer Assessment for Upper Extremity (FMA-UE) evaluated after 3 weeks of treatment. Secondary outcomes include measures of upper limb function, activities of daily living, clinical disease severity, and neurophysiological indicators assessed by rTMS, evaluated at both post-intervention and after a 3-week follow-up period.DiscussionATLAS trial is the first multicenter randomized controlled trial to apply an accelerated iTBS protocol to a large cohort of patients with subacute stroke and compare it directly with a standard stimulation protocol. The findings are expected to address the current gap in high-quality evidence regarding the efficacy of accelerated iTBS on upper limb recovery after stroke, thereby promoting the broader clinical adoption of more efficient iTBS-based therapeutic strategies.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2500110766). Introduction

Background and rationale {9a}

Stroke is the second leading cause of death globally, with high incidence and disability rates [1]. The China Stroke Prevention Report [2] shows that the prevalence of stroke among people aged 40 and above in China reaches 12.42 million. The most common dysfunction after stroke is motor dysfunction, and the proportion of upper limb dysfunction is significantly higher than lower limb dysfunction, as high as 80% or more [3]. The severity of upper limb dysfunction is an independent influencing factor for the outcome of activities of daily living after stroke [4]. Once impaired, this function becomes a major barrier to the return of stroke patients to family and societal roles. Therefore, how to effectively promote the recovery of such functions has become a major focus and challenge in rehabilitation training.

Current rehabilitation of hand function after stroke mainly involves comprehensive conventional rehabilitation training such as neurofacilitation, muscle strength training, constraint-induced movement therapy, mirror therapy, and task-oriented training with the assistance of therapists, consuming significant manpower. Even after a certain period and intensity of training, 55–75% of stroke patients still have upper limb motor dysfunction 3–6 months after onset, and only 30–40% recover partial function by 6 months post-stroke [5]. There is still a lack of methods that can effectively promote functional recovery in stroke patients with upper limb motor dysfunction.

Transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS), as a non-invasive brain stimulation technique, has proved its safety and efficacy in the functional recovery of stroke [6]. rTMS can provide repeated, continuous, and large numbers of magnetic stimulation pulses in different patterns, stimulating the cerebral cortex, regulating the excitability of the stimulated area and cortices anatomically connected to it, and adjusting the balance of interhemispheric cortical excitation. Its safety and efficacy have been confirmed in the recovery of motor and swallowing functions in the subacute and chronic phases of stroke. The guidelines on the therapeutic use of repetitive TMS [7] published in 2018 indicate that low-frequency rTMS stimulation of the unaffected M1 area for improving hand motor function in subacute stroke is strongly recommended. Although rTMS has been used clinically for many years [8], the 2024 China Stroke Prevention Report [2] shows that about 75% of stroke patients still have sequelae, and 40% have severe disabilities. Therefore, the exploration of more effective magnetic stimulation protocols still needs to be continuously advanced.

TBS, as a special paradigm of TMS, incorporates burst rhythmic stimulation based on rTMS. It has been widely used in basic and clinical trials since 2005. The TBS stimulation pattern mimics the natural activity of the brain during learning tasks, consisting of 3 pulses of 50 Hz in one burst, with an inter-burst frequency of 5 Hz, generating 15 pulses per second, totaling 600 pulses. Multiple burst stimulations are equivalent to one train stimulation of conventional rTMS. It includes two modes: iTBS and continuous TBS (cTBS) [9]. iTBS stimulates for 2 s and intermits for 8 s, with a total duration of 3 min and 20 s. iTBS has the potential advantage of achieving better therapeutic efficacy than rTMS with shorter stimulation time, fewer pulses, and lower treatment intensity. Since 2018, it has been approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for use in people with major depressive disorder [10].

In recent years, many studies have applied iTBS to the treatment research of stroke patients demonstrating its efficacy in motor and cognitive function [11–13]. The iTBS intensity used in these studies was mostly between 70 and 100% AMT, 600 pulses once daily, with a total number of sessions between 10 and 20. However, some studies also applied 1200 pulses [14]. Compared with the traditional 600-pulse protocol, higher-dose novel TBS protocols may yield superior efficacy, a conclusion that remains to be further confirmed. In 2020, the Stanford University team used an accelerated, high-dose, patterned iTBS protocol, significantly shortening the treatment cycle for patients with depression [15]. Meanwhile, another study illustrated that accelerated iTBS seems to produce a more powerful and lasting therapeutic response in patients by providing a higher pulse dose in a shorter time, without increasing the incidence and severity of adverse events (AEs) [16]. Ren et al. [17] applied accelerated iTBS to the frontoparietal cognitive network in 41 patients with post-stroke cognitive impairment, revealing its efficacy and safety in treating post-stroke cognitive impairment. The China Rehabilitation Center [18] also recruited 45 stroke patients into accelerated iTBS, cTBS, and sham stimulation groups. The results showed that both iTBS and cTBS led to greater improvement in the FMA-UE score than the sham group. However, this study had only 45 cases and lacked a comparative analysis with the established guideline [7]. Considering the insufficient evidence, this ATLAS trial aims to address an existing gap in the use of accelerated iTBS on subacute stroke patients.

Explanation for the choice of comparator {9b}

The guidelines on the therapeutic use of repetitive TMS [7] published in 2018 stated that low-frequency rTMS stimulation of the unaffected primary motor cortex (M1) area is strongly recommended for improving hand motor function in subacute stroke. A recent systematic review [8], encompassing 70 rTMS trials for post-stroke motor recovery, revealed a high degree of consistency in treatment protocols. It found that the M1 was the predominant stimulation target, employed in 58 out of the 70 trials, and that 55 trials utilized a low-frequency paradigm at 1 Hz. To date, there is no high-quality evidence to suggest that accelerated iTBS is superior to this established protocol for motor recovery in subacute stroke.

Objectives {10}

Primary objective

The aim of ATLAS trial is to compare the FMA-UE score of subacute stroke patients randomized to receive the accelerated iTBS and low-frequency rTMS after 3-week treatment period.

Secondary objectives

The trial also aims to answer additional research questions related to the overall prognosis of subacute stroke patients, including upper limb function (FMA-UE, WMFT, grip strength, and pinch strength), activities of daily living (MBI), clinical disease severity (NIHSS), and neurophysiological function (MEP and RMT). These assessments will be conducted post-treatment and at a 3-week follow-up.

Hypothesis

Compared to low-frequency rTMS, which currently holds the highest level of evidence, accelerated iTBS can significantly improve upper limb motor function in patients with subacute stroke and provide superior overall functional benefits.

Methods: patient and public involvement, and trial design

Patient and public involvement {11}

In this study, patients with subacute stroke will be recruited from the inpatient departments. Those who met the eligibility criteria will receive a detailed explanation of this study from an investigator and provide written informed consent. All baseline assessments will be completed within 3 days after consent. Subsequently, a 3-week intervention will be initiated, consisting of five daily sessions per week. All post-intervention assessments will be completed within 3 days after the intervention phase. Following the intervention, there will be a 3-week follow-up period during which patients are not permitted to receive any neuromodulation therapy. Eligibility criteria for sites and those delivering interventions {14b}

The study will establish specific qualification criteria for key personnel at each site: the principal investigator (PI) at each site must be a physician or therapist engaged in rehabilitation with a senior professional title. The research assessor (RA) must include an occupational therapist holding at least an intermediate-level professional title. The intervention operator (IO) must be a dedicated magnetic stimulation practitioner (physician or therapist) with an intermediate-level or higher title.

Who will take informed consent? {32a}

ATLAS has been approved by the ethics committees of all 13 participating sites. The study will fully comply with the code of ethics regarding participant enrollment, study assessment, and data protection. Hospitalized stroke patients at each site will be screened and assessed for eligibility by a trained clinical research team. Once identified as eligible through the screening process, potential participants will be offered enrollment and engaged in a full informed consent procedure.

During the informed consent process, an investigator authorized by PI will communicate the purpose, assessments, procedure, and potential benefits and risks of this study to the participants and their legal guardian in nontechnical language. They will have ample time to decide whether to enroll. Those who choose to participate must sign and date an informed consent form. Enrolled participants may withdraw from the study at any point in time. The original and signed informed consent forms will be retained with the case report form (CRF).

If any changes or amendments to the approved protocol directly affect participants, the informed consent form will be amended accordingly. Participants will be required to re-sign the revised informed consent form after the same has been approved by the ethical committees of these sites.

Additional consent provisions for collection and use of participant data and biological specimens {32b}

Informed consent will be obtained from at least one legal guardian before any trial-related procedures begin. Virtual or remote consent (via telephone or video call) will also be acceptable.

Strategies to improve adherence to intervention/comparator {15c}

A standardized ATLAS operational manual was established, and all site research teams received comprehensive training with ongoing technical support. To ensure protocol compliance and data integrity, the research team of the principal site will maintain regular communication with all sites through online meetings and on-site visits, supplemented by periodic monitoring. Furthermore, each site is required to maintain a participant research calendar to track scheduled tasks and minimize omissions. To facilitate participation, all study-related assessments and magnetic stimulation sessions are provided free of charge. Finally, an experienced clinical research coordinator (CRC) is designated at each site to oversee the procedural workflow, ensuring each participant completes all assessments and treatment sessions as scheduled.

Concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {15d}

During this trial, participants will be able to receive all standard rehabilitation therapies, with the exception of any neuromodulation therapy.

Ancillary and post-trial care {34}

In the event of a SAE determined to be related to the trial, all necessary examinations and treatments for the SAE will be provided at no cost to the participant. Furthermore, participants retain the right to seek legal compensation for damages. The research team will establish a dedicated mechanism to facilitate long-term follow-up of participants.

The primary outcome is the standardized FMA-UE score after the 3-week intervention. The FMA-UE is a common clinical assessment for post-stroke motor function [22]. It evaluates motor function, sensation, and joint function. Each item is scored as 0 (cannot perform), 1 (partially performs), or 2 (fully performs). The maximum total score for the upper limb and hand is 66.

Harms {17}

In this study, AEs are defined as any untoward medical occurrences following TMS treatment. The overall safety of TMS is well-established [25]. Potential AEs are infrequent and may include transient symptoms such as hearing discomfort or short-term cognitive changes. Extensive research indicates that these symptoms are often not directly attributable to TMS and are typically transient in nature. A SAE of particular concern is the induction of a seizure. However, according to safety guidelines [25], the estimated incidence is exceedingly low (approximately 0.007%). Existing literature suggests that the risk of seizure during TMS is strongly associated with pre-existing conditions such as psychiatric disorders, severe systemic diseases (e.g., cardiac, hepatic, renal), or active infections.

For potential mild AEs such as headache, scalp discomfort, or transient cognitive changes, which typically resolve spontaneously within 1–2 h, the IO will monitor the participant’s condition after each session using a treatment-associated symptom record form [26]. To mitigate the risk of SAE like seizures, individuals with a history of epilepsy, psychiatric disorders, other severe emotional/behavioral disorders, major systemic diseases (e.g., cardiac, hepatic, renal), active infections, or recent use of antiepileptic drugs (within one month) were excluded during screening. In the event of any AE that increases in severity or a SAE (e.g., seizure), the IO will immediately initiate emergency procedures, concurrently notifying the responsible physician for specific management. A case review will follow to ensure comprehensive handling. All related medical costs for such events will be covered by the study.

In the event of an AE, the IO must complete an AE report form within 24 h and notify the PI. The AE must also be reported immediately to relevant clinical departments, the clinical trial management office, and the institutional ethics committee. In urgent situations, an initial oral report may be made to facilitate a rapid response. This must be followed as soon as possible by a detailed written report documenting the event, its impact, and the management measures taken, which is then formally submitted to all concerned parties.

Sample size {19}

PASS 15 (NCSS, LLC, Kaysville, UT, USA) was used to calculate the sample size required for this randomized controlled trial. The intervention group is accelerated iTBS, and the control group is LF-rTMS. The primary outcome measure is FMA-UE. Based on a previous study [27] and pilot-trial results, the mean ± standard deviation of FMA-UE after intervention in the LF-rTMS group was set at 24.53 ± 6.14. It is estimated that the FMA-UE score in the accelerated iTBS group could increase by 2.5 points after treatment. Using a two-sided, two-sample variance t-test, with a significance level (α) of 0.050, a sample size of 96 persons per group can achieve 80% power (1 − β) to reject the null hypothesis of equal means. Considering a clinical study dropout rate of 20%, this study plans to enroll 240 participants.

Recruitment {20}

Hospitalized stroke patients across all 13 sites will be screened for eligibility by trained clinical research teams. The inpatient bed capacity at these sites ranges from 30 to 100 beds each, with a combined total of nearly 1000 beds. Approximately 70% of these beds are occupied by stroke patients. To ensure comprehensive enrollment, all centers conduct systematic weekly screenings of all admitted patients.

Assignment of interventions: randomization

Sequence generation: who will generate the sequence {21a}

In this multicenter RCT study, an Electronic Data Capture (EDC) system will be used to implement a stratified block randomization strategy.

Sequence generation: type of randomization {21b}

This study employed stratified randomization by center and stroke type (hemorrhagic vs. ischemic) to ensure comparable baseline characteristics between the intervention and control groups within each stratum, thereby enhancing internal validity. Within each stratum, participants were randomized using a block size of 4 with a 1:1 allocation ratio. This block design helps maintain a balanced allocation ratio between groups while mitigating the impact of chance variation on the results.

Allocation concealment mechanism {22}

The centralized randomization module within the EDC system generates and maintains the concealed allocation sequence. Upon researcher entry of a patient’s baseline data, the system automatically executes the randomization algorithm and assigns the patient to a study group. To maintain blinding, the allocation result is not revealed at this stage. Instead, the system generates a unique randomization code, which is securely linked to the actual treatment assignment. This code remains concealed and can only be decoded (i.e., the allocation revealed) after the trial database is locked and the formal unblinding procedure is initiated.

Implementation {23}

Thirteen tertiary general hospitals from 5 provinces were selected as study sites based on their extensive research experience and substantial patient resources. Within each center, the randomization process is implemented via the centralized module of the EDC system. This module is managed and monitored by an independent data manager to ensure the integrity, fairness, and unpredictability of the allocation sequence.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {24a}

This study employs a single-blind design. The blinding is adhered to by all research personnel except the IO. Specifically, the PI, RA, CRC, and data analysts remain masked to the group allocation throughout the trial.

How will be blinding be achieved {24b}

To minimize the impact of subjective bias and expectancy effects on study outcomes, all personnel involved—including research participants, conventional treatment physicians and therapists, functional assessors, and data managers—are blinded to treatment allocation following stratified block randomization via the EDC system. The IO are the exception, as blinding is not feasible given their direct role in administering the interventions. To mitigate potential bias, IO are required to strictly adhere to the study protocol and are prohibited from disclosing any treatment-related information to blinded personnel. Additionally, IO receive enhanced training and supervision to ensure correct and standardized administration of both active and sham stimulation procedures.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {24c}

Upon completion of all participant interventions and data collection, and following database lock, the study will be unblinded as routine. The treatment allocation codes will be revealed for the final statistical analysis. Emergency unblinding may be required if a SAE occurs and its causality to the intervention needs to be determined for immediate clinical management. Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {25a}

The ATLAS trial is a pragmatic, clinically embedded study conducted across 13 tertiary hospitals. Outcome data are collected by RA at three time points: pre-intervention (baseline), post-intervention, and follow-up. The same core set of assessments—FMA-UE, WMFT, grip strength, pinch strength, MBI, NIHSS, MEP, and RMT—is administered at each time point. Data are entered directly into the EDC system via electronic CRF, with paper-based CRF maintained as backup. At the baseline assessment only, additional participant information is captured, including demographic characteristics, stroke-related clinical data, and relevant medical history.

Plans to promote participant retention and complete follow-up {25b}

Given the brief in-hospital follow-up period and the completion of all primary and secondary data collection prior to discharge, we anticipate a 100% ascertainment rate for the primary outcome and near-complete follow-up for secondary outcomes. To ensure complete data collection at all three time points, an individualized study calendar was created for each participant. The CRC used this calendar to issue reminders and coordinate all involved personnel for each key assessment, thereby preventing missed visits. For participants discharged before the scheduled follow-up, the CRC provided detailed instructions on follow-up procedures prior to discharge and contacted them to schedule outpatient appointments. These participants returned to complete their follow-up assessments on an outpatient basis, with the assessment process pre-coordinated to allow completion within half a day.

Data management {26}

The EDC system employed in this study ensures data accuracy, completeness, and security through stringent controls governing data collection, entry, review, and transmission. Data entry is performed directly by researchers via electronic CRF. During electronic CRF design, data managers predefine all required data points. The system facilitates real-time data entry, maintains a complete audit trail for all modifications, and preserves source data. Data quality is enforced through automated and manual logic checks. A dedicated data query management module is configured for resolving discrepancies. Source data verification is conducted to confirm accuracy. For data security, all transmissions employ encryption technology to ensure integrity and prevent loss. External data transfers are governed by predefined agreements specifying the method, frequency, and procedures for re-transmission in case of failure, thereby guaranteeing timeliness and completeness.

Confidentiality {33}

All study data will be recorded on the CRF. For each completed participant case, the CRF will be promptly retrieved by the CRC and uploaded into the secured EDC system within 1 month. Access to the live EDC database is strictly restricted to the PI and the authorized CRC. For interim statistical analyses, a de-identified dataset will be provided to the independent statistician via a secure, access-controlled institutional portal.

Upon study completion, all participant identifiers will be permanently removed from the final analysis dataset in accordance with regulatory guidelines (e.g., HIPAA principles). The resulting fully de-identified dataset, containing no information that could directly or indirectly identify participants, will be securely archived for future secondary research. All identifiable source documents and data will be destroyed after a predefined retention period. The long-term archival of de-identified data ensures its accessibility beyond the original project timeline.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {27a}

All outcomes will be analyzed using the intention-to-treat (ITT) principle, adhering to the following: randomization, maintain original group assignment, include all participants. Statistics will be performed using SPSS 26.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, USA). Conducting preliminary analysis on the basic data of all patients, use the Mann-Whitney test or chi-square test. For the primary outcome before and after intervention, use independent samples t-test or Mann-Whitney U rank sum test. P < 0.05 proves the difference is significant; P < 0.01 indicates the difference is highly significant. Calculate 95% confidence intervals. For the secondary outcomes, use a two-way mixed-measures ANOVA with factors 2 (between groups: intervention vs. control) × 3 (time points: initial assessment T0 vs. final assessment T1 vs. follow-up T2). If the sphericity assumption is violated, use Greenhouse-Geisser epsilon correction, and use Bonferroni for post hoc comparisons. The significance level is α = 0.05. As to safety analysis, analyzing the incidence and severity of AEs, it can be determined whether there are safety issues with the treatment intervention, and corresponding measures can be taken.

How missing data will be handled in the analysis {27c}

The handling of missing data will depend on its proportion. If the percentage of missing data for a given outcome is below a pre-specified threshold of 5%, no imputation will be performed, and the analysis will be conducted on the complete case analysis. If the proportion of missing data exceeds 5%, the last observation carried forward method will be applied as the primary imputation method. Specifically, for participants with missing values at a given endpoint (e.g., the post-intervention assessment), their most recent available observation prior to that timepoint (such as a supplementary assessment conducted before hospital discharge) will be carried forward to fill the missing value.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g., subgroup analyses) {27d}

To discern their distinct effects on the findings, a subgroup analysis comparing hemorrhagic and ischemic stroke patients will be performed.

Interim analyses {28b}

Interim analysis was not planned for this trial.

Protocol and statistical analysis plan {5}

Composition of the data monitoring committee, its role and reporting structure {28a}

An independent Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) was constituted prior to study initiation. This committee is responsible for the periodic review (conducted quarterly) of cumulative trial data, overseeing participant safety, monitoring efficacy trends, assessing overall trial conduct quality, and providing recommendations regarding trial design or continuation. The primary mandate of the DMC is to safeguard participant welfare and ensure the scientific integrity and validity of the trial results. The committee comprises four senior clinicians with substantial clinical trial experience, selected from the participating institutions. Additionally, a senior clinician from the principal research center chairs the DMC and is vested with the authority to convene meetings and lead its deliberations.

Frequency and plans for auditing trial conduct {29}

DMC is responsible for reviewing the cumulative data of the clinical trial quarterly, completing safety monitoring, efficacy monitoring, trial operation quality monitoring, trial design adjustment suggestions, etc. During each routine monitoring visit, source data verification will be performed. For this purpose, the monitor will verify the data entered into the CRF against the original source documents. The source data verification will cover the first three participants enrolled at the site. If a site has enrolled fewer than three participants, it will be performed for all enrolled participants.

Any protocol amendments will be submitted for approval by the Ethics Committee of First Affiliated Hospital of Army Medical University, Chongqing, China. Any approved changes will be communicated to PI of all sites to obtain site-specific governance approval.

Discussion

This paper introduces the protocol of the ATLAS trial, a multicenter, single-blind, randomized controlled trial conducted collaboratively by 13 tertiary general hospitals across China. Based on established evidence for iTBS, the trial evaluates an accelerated iTBS protocol as the experimental intervention, with guideline-endorsed LF-rTMS as the active control. ATLAS is designed to address the existing gap in high-quality evidence concerning the effects of accelerated iTBS on post-stroke upper limb recovery.

To ensure scientific rigor and practical feasibility, the coordinating center conducted iterative protocol reviews, a pilot study, and multiple revisions prior to finalizing the design. These steps, complemented by systematic training for all sites, established a robust protocol and implementation pathway. This trial aims to promote the clinical translation of more efficient iTBS paradigms. Its results may inform future updates to clinical guidelines, potentially improving rehabilitation strategies for post-stroke upper limb dysfunction.

Trial status

The current version of the protocol (version 3.0) was approved by the Ethics Committee of First Affiliated Hospital of Army Medical University on September 10, 2025, and soon after by the Ethics Committee of other sites. Participant recruitment began on October 31, 2025, with expected completion on December 31, 2027.

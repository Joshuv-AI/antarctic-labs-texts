Dietary dimethylglycine sodium salt improves growth performance, liver function, and meat quality of broilers exposed to heat stress
Authors: Kaiwen Bai, Shang Yan, Luyi Jiang, Tian Wang, Yiwei Cui, Yan Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHeat stress is a major environmental challenge that impairs growth performance, hepatic redox homeostasis, and meat quality in broilers, primarily through oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction. Dimethylglycine sodium salt (DMG-Na), a methyl donor involved in one-carbon metabolism, is known to regulate redox balance and mitochondrial function, indicating its potential to alleviate heat stress-induced physiological disturbances. This study evaluated the protective effects of dietary DMG-Na on heat stress-induced physiological and metabolic disturbances in broilers. Eight hundred 21-day-old chicks were randomly assigned to a thermoneutral control group (TN) or heat stress groups receiving a basal diet supplemented with 0, 200, 400, or 600 mg/kg DMG-Na (HS, HSD2, HSD4, HSD6). Heat stress significantly reduced average daily gain and feed intake, increased feed conversion ratio, elevated serum ALT and AST activities, disrupted redox balance, impaired mitochondrial antioxidant capacity and energy metabolism, and deteriorated meat quality (P < 0.05). Dietary DMG-Na supplementation markedly alleviated these adverse effects by improving growth performance, enhancing antioxidant enzyme activities, reducing oxidative damage, restoring mitochondrial respiratory chain function and ATP production, and improving meat quality parameters (P < 0.05). Transcriptomic and quantitative gene expression analyses further demonstrated that DMG-Na partially reversed heat stress-induced alterations in genes related to redox regulation, cell adhesion, and mitochondrial function. Overall, these findings indicate that dietary DMG-Na supplementation effectively mitigates heat stress-induced oxidative and metabolic disturbances in broilers, with 200 mg/kg appearing sufficient to achieve most protective effects, suggesting a plateau response at higher inclusion levels.Keywords: Broiler, Heat stress, Liver, Redox homeostasis, Meat quality

Introduction

Heat stress has emerged as one of the most severe environmental constraints on modern poultry production, particularly in tropical and subtropical regions where elevated ambient temperature is often accompanied by high relative humidity. Broilers are especially vulnerable to thermal stress due to their accelerated growth rate, high metabolic heat output, dense body conformation, and limited efficiency of evaporative heat dissipation (Shaukat et al., 2025). Sustained exposure to high environmental temperatures has been consistently shown to suppress feed intake, impair growth performance, and increase feed conversion ratio, thereby imposing substantial economic losses on the poultry industry (Yu, et al., 2025c). Importantly, the detrimental effects of heat stress extend far beyond reduced productivity. Heat stress induces a cascade of physiological disturbances, including endocrine imbalance, oxidative stress, and metabolic dysregulation, which collectively compromise organ function and meat quality (Bai et al., 2025; Carvalho et al., 2025; Bungsrisawat et al., 2025; Pontalti et al., 2025). Although numerous nutritional interventions, including vitamin supplementation, functional amino acids, and phytogenic additives, have been proposed to mitigate heat stress-related damage, their efficacy under prolonged or severe thermal conditions remains inconsistent (Bai et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2025; Lima et al., 2025; Obianwuna et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2024). Consequently, the identification of dietary strategies capable of maintaining metabolic stability, antioxidant balance, and product quality under chronic heat stress remains a major challenge in poultry nutrition.

Among internal organs affected by heat stress, the liver occupies a pivotal position due to its central role in nutrient metabolism, detoxification, and redox regulation. Efficient hepatic function is indispensable for sustaining the high anabolic demands of modern broilers (Amaz et al., 2025). Under heat stress conditions, excessive reactive oxygen species production overwhelms hepatic antioxidant defenses, leading to oxidative damage to lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids. Such redox imbalance disrupts mitochondrial integrity and impairs key metabolic pathways, including lipid oxidation, amino acid utilization, and energy generation (Lin et al., 2025). These hepatic disturbances not only reduce growth efficiency and increase susceptibility to metabolic disorders but also exert downstream effects on carcass traits and postmortem muscle quality. Accumulating evidence indicates that hepatic oxidative stress is closely linked to deterioration in water-holding capacity, color stability, tenderness, and lipid oxidation of meat in heat-stressed broilers (Yao et al., 2025; Tang et al., 2022). Therefore, preserving hepatic redox homeostasis and mitochondrial functionality is critical not only for maintaining physiological health but also for ensuring desirable meat quality attributes in poultry production systems exposed to thermal stress (Amaz et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2025). Nutritional approaches that simultaneously support antioxidant capacity, mitochondrial performance, and energy metabolism may thus represent effective tools for alleviating heat stress-induced damage at both the organismal and product levels.

Dimethylglycine sodium salt (DMG-Na), an endogenous intermediate of glycine metabolism, has recently gained attention as a functional feed additive with multifaceted biological activities. Acting as a methyl-group donor within one-carbon metabolism, DMG-Na participates in methylation reactions essential for redox regulation, mitochondrial respiration, and cellular energy homeostasis. Previous studies have demonstrated that DMG-Na supplementation can enhance antioxidant defenses, modulate immune responses, and improve mitochondrial function in various animal models (Bai et al., 2022a, 2022b, 2024; Bai et al., 2022d, 2022e). In poultry, DMG-Na has shown potential to improve growth performance and metabolic efficiency; however, its capacity to counteract heat stress-induced hepatic oxidative damage, mitochondrial dysfunction, and energy metabolism impairment has not been comprehensively characterized. Moreover, the molecular mechanisms underlying its protective effects under thermal stress conditions remain largely unexplored. High-throughput transcriptomic analysis offers a powerful approach to address these gaps by enabling a global assessment of gene expression changes associated with oxidative regulation, mitochondrial dynamics, metabolic adaptation, and inflammatory signaling (Chen et al., 2025; Xiang et al., 2025). When integrated with physiological and biochemical data, transcriptomic profiling provides mechanistic insights into how dietary interventions translate into improved performance and meat quality under environmental stress.

Therefore, the present study was designed to investigate the effects of dietary DMG-Na supplementation on growth performance, hepatic redox homeostasis, mitochondrial function, energy metabolism, and meat quality in broilers subjected to heat stress. In addition, liver transcriptomic analysis combined with targeted gene expression profiling was conducted to elucidate the molecular mechanisms associated with antioxidant regulation, cell adhesion, and mitochondrial quality control. By integrating phenotypic, biochemical, and transcriptomic evidence, this study aims to provide a comprehensive evaluation of DMG-Na as a nutritional strategy to enhance physiological resilience and production efficiency of broilers under heat stress conditions.

Materials and methods

Experimental birds, housing conditions, and dietary treatments

The animal experiment was performed using 21-day-old Arbor Acres (AA) commercial broilers sourced from a licensed commercial hatchery. A total of 800 healthy chicks were individually weighed and randomly assigned to five experimental treatments. Each treatment consisted of 20 replicates, with 8 birds per replicate. The experimental design included a basal diet fed under thermoneutral conditions (TN), a basal diet under heat stress conditions (HS), and three heat-stressed groups supplemented with DMG-Na at graded levels: 200 mg/kg (HSD2), 400 mg/kg (HSD4), and 600 mg/kg (HSD6). The basal diet formulation met or exceeded the nutrient requirements recommended for AA broilers in accordance with NRC (2020) guidelines, and detailed ingredient composition and nutrient levels are provided in Table S1.

Broilers in the TN group were maintained under standard thermal conditions at 23 ± 1°C throughout the experimental period. Birds assigned to the HS and DMG-Na-supplemented groups were exposed to a controlled heat stress regimen using heating tubes and temperature monitoring devices. Specifically, ambient temperature was maintained at 34 ± 1°C for 24 h per day from 21 to 28 days of age, followed by exposure to 34 ± 1°C for 8 h daily from 28 to 42 days of age. Relative humidity was maintained at approximately 65% across all groups. The heat stress trial lasted from 21 to 42 days of age. Each replicate was housed in an individual stainless-steel cage (120 cm × 70 cm × 40 cm) equipped with a trough feeder and nipple drinker, allowing ad libitum access to feed and water. Birds were subjected to continuous lighting throughout the study. All experimental procedures were approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Nanjing Agricultural University (SYXK[Su]2017-0027) and were conducted in accordance with institutional ethical guidelines.

Growth performance

Feed supply and residual feed were recorded daily on a replicate basis. Average daily gain (ADG), average daily feed intake (ADFI), and feed conversion ratio (FCR) were calculated for each replicate over the experimental period.

Sample collection

At 42 days of age, one bird per replicate with body weight closest to the replicate mean was selected for sampling. Blood samples were collected and centrifuged at 3,000 × g for 10 min to obtain plasma. Following euthanasia by cervical dislocation, the liver was rapidly excised, rinsed with ice-cold saline, and divided into portions for biochemical analysis, mitochondrial isolation, and transcriptomic profiling. All samples were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80°C until further analysis. In addition, left pectoralis major muscle samples were collected immediately postmortem for meat quality evaluation.

Plasma liver function indicators

Plasma alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) activities were determined using commercially available diagnostic kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China). ALT activity was measured based on the formation of pyruvate from alanine and α-ketoglutarate, followed by color development with 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine. AST activity was assessed using a similar colorimetric reaction involving aspartate. Absorbance was measured using a microplate reader in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocols.

Plasma and hepatic redox homeostasis indicators

Plasma and hepatic redox status were evaluated by measuring total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC), superoxide dismutase (SOD), glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px) activities, and malondialdehyde (MDA) levels using commercial assay kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China). Plasma samples were directly used for biochemical analysis. Liver tissues were homogenized in ice-cold buffer to prepare 10% (w/v) homogenates, followed by centrifugation to obtain the supernatant for analysis. Total protein concentration in liver homogenates was determined using the Coomassie brilliant blue method, and hepatic antioxidant parameters were expressed relative to protein content. T-AOC was determined using a ferric-reducing antioxidant power assay. SOD activity was measured based on its ability to inhibit xanthine oxidase–mediated formazan formation. GSH-Px activity was assessed by monitoring the catalytic oxidation of reduced glutathione in the presence of hydrogen peroxide. MDA levels were determined using the thiobarbituric acid reactive substances method.

Isolation of hepatic mitochondria and mitochondrial redox indicators

Hepatic mitochondria were isolated using a commercial mitochondrial isolation kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions, based on differential centrifugation. Liver tissue was homogenized in ice-cold isolation buffer, followed by sequential centrifugation to remove cellular debris and obtain the mitochondrial fraction. Mitochondrial MnSOD, GSH-Px, and γ-glutamylcysteine ligase (γ-GCL) activities, as well as protein carbonyl (PC) and 8‑hydroxy-2′-deoxyguanosine (8-OHdG) levels, were quantified using specific assay kits. MnSOD activity was determined using the pyrogallol autoxidation method. PC content was measured following DNPH derivatization, and 8-OHdG levels were assessed using an ELISA-based method. All mitochondrial parameters were normalized to mitochondrial protein concentration.

Hepatic energy metabolism

Hepatic ATP content was quantified using a luciferase-based bioluminescence assay, with light emission proportional to ATP concentration. Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) abundance was determined by quantitative PCR as previously described (Han et al., 2025) using primers specific for mitochondrial genes (F: AGGACTACGGCTTGAAAAGC; R: CATCTTGGCATCTTCAGTGCC). Results were expressed relative to the control group. Activities of mitochondrial respiratory chain complexes I-V were measured using colorimetric enzyme activity kits. Specifically, complex I activity was assessed by monitoring NADH oxidation, complex II by DCPIP reduction, complex III by cytochrome c reduction, complex IV by the oxidation rate of reduced cytochrome c, and complex V by ATP hydrolysis activity.

Meat quality analysis

Breast muscle samples were collected immediately after slaughter. Muscle pH was measured at 1 h postmortem using a calibrated handheld pH meter. Drip loss was determined by measuring weight loss after 24 h of storage at 4°C. Cooking loss was assessed by vacuum-packaging samples and heating them in a water bath until an internal temperature of 75°C was reached, followed by cooling and reweighing. Shear force was measured using a Warner-Bratzler shear apparatus. Meat color parameters (L*, a*, b*) were evaluated using a portable colorimeter.

Liver transcriptome sequencing and bioinformatic analysis

Total RNA was extracted from liver samples using TRIzol reagent, and RNA integrity was verified using an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer. Samples with RNA integrity number values greater than 7.0 were used for library construction. RNA-seq libraries were prepared following the Illumina TruSeq protocol, including mRNA enrichment, fragmentation, cDNA synthesis, adapter ligation, and PCR amplification. Libraries were sequenced on an Illumina platform to generate paired-end reads. Raw sequencing data were processed to remove adapter sequences, low-quality reads, and ambiguous bases. Clean reads were aligned to the reference genome using HISAT2, and gene expression levels were calculated as fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped reads (FPKM). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using DESeq2 with thresholds of |log₂ fold change| ≥ 2 and adjusted P < 0.05. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes enrichment (KEGG) analyses were conducted to identify significantly affected biological processes and pathways.

Quantitative real-time PCR analysis

Total RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA using a commercial reverse transcription kit. Quantitative real-time PCR was performed using SYBR Green chemistry on a real-time PCR system. Genes related to antioxidant defense, tight junction integrity, and mitochondrial regulation were selected (Table S2). β-actin was used as the internal reference gene. Relative gene expression levels were calculated using the 2⁻ΔΔCt method (Han et al., 2025).

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed using SAS 9.4 software (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA). Data were first tested for normality and homogeneity of variance. Differences among groups were evaluated by one-way analysis of variance followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. In addition, orthogonal polynomial contrasts were performed to evaluate the linear and quadratic effects of graded levels of dietary DMG-Na supplementation under heat stress conditions. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.

Results

Growth performance

As shown in Table 1, heat stress significantly impaired growth performance in broilers, as indicated by decreased ADG and ADFI and increased FCR in the HS group compared with the TN group (P < 0.05). DMG-Na supplementation alleviated these effects, with all supplemented groups exhibiting higher ADG and ADFI and lower FCR than the HS group (P < 0.05), while no differences were detected among supplementation levels. Polynomial contrast analysis revealed a linear effect of DMG-Na on ADG (P = 0.012), with no linear or quadratic effects observed for ADFI or FCR (P > 0.05).

Plasma and hepatic redox homeostasis indicators

Heat stress disrupted systemic redox balance, as evidenced by decreased SOD, T-AOC, and GSH-Px activities and increased MDA levels (P < 0.05; Table 3). DMG-Na supplementation improved antioxidant status, increasing SOD, T-AOC, and GSH-Px and decreasing MDA compared with the HS group (P < 0.05), with no differences among supplementation levels. Meat quality

Heat stress deteriorated meat quality (Table 7), as reflected by lower pH and a* values and higher drip loss, cooking loss, shear force, L*, and b* values (P < 0.05). DMG-Na supplementation improved these parameters (P < 0.05), with no differences among supplementation levels. Polynomial contrast analysis showed significant linear effects for pH (P = 0.004) and for drip loss, cooking loss, shear force, L*, a*, and b* (P < 0.001). Hepatic gene map determined by the RNA-seq

RNA-seq analysis identified 12,116 expressed genes in hepatic tissues. Using a threshold of P < 0.05 and |log₂ fold change| ≥ 2, 222 DEGs were identified between the TN and HS groups, and 75, 161, and 170 DEGs were identified in the HS vs. HSD2, HS vs. HSD4, and HS vs. HSD6 comparisons, respectively. Among these, 96/126, 33/42, 57/104, and 59/111 genes were up-/down-regulated in TN vs. HS, HS vs. HSD2, HS vs. HSD4, and HS vs. HSD6, respectively (Fig. 1A). GO enrichment analysis showed that DEGs were significantly enriched in biological processes, cellular components, and molecular functions (Fig. 1B-E). KEGG analysis identified 107, 59, 83, and 88 enriched pathways in the TN vs. HS, HS vs. HSD2, HS vs. HSD4, and HS vs. HSD6 comparisons, respectively. These pathways were mainly associated with digestive functions, immune signaling (NOD-like receptor and NF-κB pathways), and lipid metabolism and endocrine regulation. Key genes involved in metabolic regulation and inflammation, including SIRT1, NF-κB, PARP2, NLRP3, and ITGB1, were markedly affected. Notably, SIRT1 showed the most pronounced change, suggesting a central role in hepatic responses to heat stress and DMG-Na supplementation.

Discussion

Heat stress is one of the most detrimental environmental challenges in intensive poultry production because it simultaneously impairs growth performance, metabolic stability, and product quality (Carvalho et al., 2025; Shaukat et al., 2025). In the present study, chronic heat exposure markedly reduced growth performance in broilers and was accompanied by hepatic dysfunction, systemic and hepatic redox imbalance, mitochondrial injury, and deterioration of meat quality. These findings suggest that heat stress-induced damage is not confined to a single organ or pathway, but rather reflects a coordinated disruption of antioxidant defense, energy metabolism, and tissue integrity. Importantly, dietary DMG-Na supplementation substantially alleviated these adverse changes, indicating that its protective effects are primarily associated with the restoration of redox and metabolic homeostasis under thermal stress. Although all supplementation levels were effective, the overall response pattern, together with the linear and quadratic analyses, indicates a dose-dependent effect that tended to plateau at higher inclusion levels, suggesting that DMG-Na functions mainly as a corrective nutritional factor rather than a growth-promoting agent beyond physiological limits. This plateau response may explain why 200 mg/kg was sufficient to achieve most of the protective effects, whereas higher supplementation levels provided limited additional benefit.

Reduced growth under heat stress is commonly attributed to lower feed intake, increased energy expenditure for thermoregulation, and impaired nutrient utilization. Among growth-related indices, FCR is particularly sensitive because it reflects the efficiency with which dietary nutrients are converted into body mass (Asiamah et al., 2025). Under thermal stress, oxidative burden and mitochondrial inefficiency divert energy away from growth toward cellular defense and repair. Consistent with this concept, heat-stressed broilers in the present study showed reduced ADG and ADFI and increased FCR. DMG-Na supplementation improved these variables, indicating improved metabolic efficiency rather than simply increased feed consumption. This interpretation is biologically plausible because DMG-Na functions as a methyl-group donor in one-carbon metabolism and may support methylation-dependent processes involved in mitochondrial respiration, amino acid utilization, and detoxification (Bai et al., 2022c). Similar responses have been reported for other methyl donors, including betaine and choline, under heat stress conditions (Buonaiuto et al., 2025; Gao et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025). This impairment in growth performance is closely associated with metabolic disturbances in key organs, particularly the liver, which plays a central role in nutrient metabolism and redox regulation under heat stress.

The liver is a central metabolic organ during heat stress, integrating nutrient metabolism, detoxification, and antioxidant defense. Because hepatocytes have high metabolic activity and abundant mitochondria, they are particularly vulnerable to oxidative damage (Kong et al., 2025). In the present study, heat stress increased plasma ALT and AST activities and simultaneously reduced systemic antioxidant capacity while increasing lipid peroxidation, indicating that hepatic injury was closely associated with oxidative stress. Importantly, DMG-Na supplementation reduced ALT and AST activities while improving plasma antioxidant status, suggesting that the alleviation of hepatic dysfunction was accompanied by improved systemic redox balance. This connection is important because liver function, antioxidant status, and growth performance are physiologically interdependent: when oxidative stress damages hepatocytes, metabolic efficiency declines, nutrient processing is impaired, and productive performance is consequently reduced. Therefore, the improvement in liver function observed in the DMG-Na groups likely contributed directly to the recovery of growth performance. Comparable hepatoprotective effects of methyl donors and glycine-related compounds have also been reported in poultry and swine under oxidative challenge (Ghashghaei et al., 2025; Kwon et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2024a).

This liver-protective effect was further supported by the hepatic antioxidant data. Heat stress markedly decreased hepatic SOD, GSH-Px, and T-AOC activities and increased MDA levels, confirming substantial oxidative damage within liver tissue. DMG-Na supplementation restored antioxidant enzyme activities and reduced lipid peroxidation, indicating reinforcement of hepatic redox homeostasis. The hepatic glutathione-related system, represented by GSH-Px and γ-GCL, is especially important for peroxide detoxification and maintenance of intracellular antioxidant capacity (Zhan et al., 2025). Thus, the coordinated increases in antioxidant enzyme activity together with the reduction in MDA suggest that DMG-Na reduced oxidative pressure at the tissue level rather than merely triggering a compensatory stress response. This interpretation is also supported by gene expression results, where stress-responsive genes such as Nrf2 and HO1 (Jiang et al., 2025; Oke et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024), which are typically induced by oxidative challenge, were downregulated after DMG-Na supplementation. Collectively, these findings indicate that DMG-Na restored basal redox equilibrium in the liver and reduced the metabolic burden associated with prolonged oxidative challenge. Given that mitochondria are both a major source and target of reactive oxygen species, the observed hepatic redox imbalance is closely linked to mitochondrial dysfunction.

Mitochondria appear to be a key target linking antioxidant protection with metabolic recovery (Jomova et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025). Mitochondria are both a major source and a major target of reactive oxygen species during heat stress, and excessive mitochondrial ROS can damage mitochondrial proteins, lipids, and DNA, thereby impairing oxidative phosphorylation (Li et al., 2025). In the present study, heat stress reduced mitochondrial MnSOD, GSH-Px, and γ-GCL activities while increasing PC and 8-OHdG levels, indicating oxidative damage to mitochondrial proteins and DNA. These alterations were accompanied by lower hepatic ATP content, reduced mtDNA abundance, and decreased respiratory chain complex activities, demonstrating impaired mitochondrial bioenergetic capacity (Taylor et al., 2024). DMG-Na supplementation effectively reversed these changes, suggesting that its protective effect involved both reinforcement of mitochondrial antioxidant defense and preservation of oxidative phosphorylation. This link is central to understanding the overall benefits of DMG-Na: by reducing mitochondrial oxidative injury, the supplement helps preserve ATP generation, and improved energy supply in turn supports liver function, nutrient utilization, and growth. Similar mitochondrial-protective effects of methyl donors have been documented in animals exposed to metabolic or thermal stress (Buonaiuto et al., 2025; Oke et al., 2024).

The improvement in meat quality can also be interpreted within this integrated framework. Meat quality deterioration under heat stress is generally considered a downstream consequence of oxidative damage and altered postmortem energy metabolism. In the present study, heat-stressed broilers showed reduced pH and a* value and increased drip loss, cooking loss, shear force, L*, and b* values, indicating poorer water-holding capacity, impaired protein integrity, and reduced color stability. These changes are closely associated with insufficient ATP availability before slaughter and increased oxidative modification of muscle proteins and pigments (Wei et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2024b). Importantly, DMG-Na supplementation improved all these meat quality traits. Therefore, mitochondrial dysfunction and impaired energy metabolism may serve as a critical link between hepatic oxidative stress and the subsequent deterioration of meat quality. This beneficial effect was likely not independent of the hepatic and antioxidant improvements observed in the present study. Rather, the improvement in meat quality may be viewed as a downstream outcome of improved systemic redox status, better mitochondrial energy metabolism, and preserved liver metabolic function. In other words, DMG-Na did not merely improve isolated meat quality traits; it likely improved the physiological background that determines postmortem muscle stability. Enhanced ATP availability would be expected to delay rigor development and preserve water-holding capacity, whereas lower oxidative stress would help maintain myoglobin stability and reduce structural protein damage. Similar improvements in meat quality after supplementation with glycine-based additives or methyl donors have been reported previously (Mussa et al., 2025; Suliman et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2022).

At the molecular level, transcriptomic analysis provides an integrated mechanistic framework linking the observed phenotypic and biochemical improvements. Heat stress profoundly altered hepatic gene expression, particularly genes involved in redox regulation, cell adhesion, and mitochondrial function. DMG-Na supplementation promoted the expression of tight junction-associated genes such as OCLN and ZO1 while suppressing permeability-associated claudins, suggesting improved hepatocellular barrier integrity. This effect is likely driven by reduced oxidative injury and improved ATP availability, both of which are essential for membrane maintenance and junctional protein turnover (Zhang et al., 2025). This strengthening effect is likely attributable to the simultaneous alleviation of ROS-mediated structural damage and the improvement in mitochondrial ATP supply, both of which are essential for maintaining membrane integrity and supporting the continuous turnover and stabilization of junctional proteins. Consistent with this interpretation, previous studies have demonstrated that antioxidant interventions effectively prevent oxidative stress–induced disruption of tight junction architecture in the liver and intestinal tissues of poultry (Li et al., 2024; Sarker et al., 2025). Simultaneously, upregulation of SIRT1 and genes related to mitochondrial biogenesis, antioxidant defense, and fatty acid oxidation, together with downregulation of NF-κB, NLRP3, PARP2, and pro-inflammatory cytokines, indicates a coordinated suppression of inflammation and enhancement of metabolic efficiency. The concurrent induction of genes regulating mitochondrial dynamics, including Drp1, Fis1, and Mfn2, further suggests improved mitochondrial quality control, a critical adaptive response under heat stress. Comparable transcriptomic patterns have been reported in previous studies examining methyl donor supplementation, reinforcing the reproducibility of these mechanisms (Bai et al., 2022a, 2022b, 2024; Bai et al., 2022d, 2022e). Taken together, the coordinated biochemical alterations and transcriptional adaptations observed in the present study delineate a unified protective framework through which DMG-Na preserves hepatic structural integrity and strengthens mitochondrial functional capacity. By simultaneously stabilizing cellular architecture and optimizing intracellular energy metabolism, this multi-level protection reduces hepatic vulnerability to heat stress-induced injury. Such hepatic resilience provides a critical physiological foundation for the subsequent improvements in carcass characteristics, thereby establishing a mechanistic link between liver cellular homeostasis and whole-body production efficiency in broilers.

In conclusion, heat stress caused multifaceted impairments in growth performance, hepatic function, redox balance, mitochondrial integrity, and meat quality in broilers. Dietary DMG-Na supplementation effectively alleviated these disturbances by restoring antioxidant capacity, improving mitochondrial energy metabolism, and maintaining hepatic structural and metabolic stability. These integrated protective effects provide a mechanistic basis for the improved growth efficiency and meat quality observed in heat-stressed broilers. Based on the present findings, 200 mg/kg DMG-Na appears to be a sufficient and practical supplementation level, as higher inclusion rates did not confer additional benefits, indicating a plateau response within the tested dosage range.

Lasso and Partially-Rotated Designs
Rares-Darius Buhai rares.buhai@inf.ethz.ch ETH Zürich

Abstract
We consider the sparse linear regression model 𝒚 = X ​ β + 𝒘 \bm{y}=X\beta+\bm{w} , where X ∈ ℝ n × d X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d} is the design, β ∈ ℝ d \beta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d} is a k k -sparse secret, and 𝒘 ∼ N ⁡ ( 0 , I n ) \bm{w}\sim N(0,I_{n}) is the noise. Given input X X and 𝒚 \bm{y} , the goal is to estimate β \beta . In this setting, the Lasso estimate achieves prediction error O ⁡ ( k ​ log ⁡ d / γ ​ n ) O(k\log d/\gamma n) , where γ \gamma is the restricted eigenvalue (RE) constant of X X with respect to supp ⁡ ( β ) \operatorname{supp}(\beta) . In this paper, we introduce a new semirandom family of designs — which we call partially-rotated designs — for which the RE constant with respect to the secret is bounded away from zero even when a subset of the design columns are arbitrarily correlated among themselves. As an example of such a design, suppose we start with some arbitrary X X , and then apply a random rotation to the columns of X X indexed by supp ⁡ ( β ) \operatorname{supp}(\beta) . Let λ min \lambda_{\min} be the smallest eigenvalue of 1 n ​ X supp ⁡ ( β ) ⊤ ​ X supp ⁡ ( β ) \frac{1}{n}X_{\operatorname{supp}(\beta)}^{\top}X_{\operatorname{supp}(\beta)} , where X supp ⁡ ( β ) X_{\operatorname{supp}(\beta)} is the restriction of X X to the columns indexed by supp ⁡ ( β ) \operatorname{supp}(\beta) . In this setting, our results imply that Lasso achieves prediction error O ⁡ ( k ​ log ⁡ d / λ min ​ n ) O(k\log d/\lambda_{\min}n) with high probability. This prediction error bound is independent of the arbitrary columns of X X not indexed by supp ⁡ ( β ) \operatorname{supp}(\beta) , and is as good as if all of these columns were perfectly well-conditioned. Technically, our proof reduces to showing that matrices with a certain deterministic property — which we call restricted normalized orthogonality (RNO) — lead to RE constants that are independent of a subset of the matrix columns. This property is similar but incomparable with the restricted orthogonality condition of [ CT05 ] .

Introduction

Sparse linear models are among the simplest models that admit strong theoretical guarantees even when the number of samples is much smaller than the dimension.
Hence, these models are of fundamental interest in high-dimensional statistics.
In this paper we consider sparse linear regression,
where the input consists of a design
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
and a response vector
\bm{y}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, related linearly as

\bm{y}=X\beta+\bm{w}\,.

(1)

Here
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a
k
-sparse unknown
secret
and
\bm{w}\sim N(0,\sigma^{2}\cdot I_{n})
is noise.
Alternatively, one can view the input as consisting of
n
samples
(X^{i},\bm{y}_{i})
that satisfy the linear relation
\bm{y}_{i}=\langle X^{i},\beta\rangle+\bm{w}_{i}
, where
X^{i}
is the
i
-th row of the design.
We assume the standard normalization in which the columns of
X
have norm
\sqrt{n}
.
Then, we focus on the goal of outputting an estimate
\hat{\beta}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
of
\beta
such that the
prediction error

\frac{1}{n}\left\lVert X\hat{\beta}-X\beta\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
is small.

In this setting,
\ell_{0}
-constrained optimization yields with high probability an estimate with prediction error
O\left(\sigma^{2}k\log(d/k)/n\right)

[
RWY11
]
.
This method is also known as the Best Subset Selection (BSS) algorithm.
Unfortunately, BSS runs in time exponential in
k
, and no polynomial-time algorithms are known that achieve
any
non-trivial prediction error without additional assumptions.

A guarantee known as the
slow rate
of lasso (see Theorem 7.20a in

Wai19
]
) is sometimes talked about as assumptionless, but we note that this guarantee scales linearly with the

\beta
, so it is a non-trivial guarantee only with the additional assumption that this norm is small.

Lasso

Given this state of affairs, much research has been devoted to studying assumptions under which small prediction error can be achieved in polynomial time.
One of the most studied polynomial-time estimates for this problem is the Lasso
[
Tib96
]
, defined (in its constrained form) as

\hat{\beta}_{\mathrm{Lasso}}=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{\beta}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\\
\lVert\hat{\beta}\rVert_{1}\leq\lVert\beta\rVert_{1}\end{subarray}}\left\lVert\bm{y}-X\hat{\beta}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\,.

(2)

Lasso achieves with high probability prediction error

\frac{1}{n}\left\lVert X\hat{\beta}_{\mathrm{Lasso}}-X\beta\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq O\left(\sigma^{2}\frac{k\log d}{\gamma_{\operatorname{supp}(\beta)}(X)\cdot n}\right)\,,

(3)

where
\gamma_{\operatorname{supp}(\beta)}(X)
is the
restricted eigenvalue
(RE) constant of
X
with respect to
\operatorname{supp}(\beta)
(see Theorem 7.20b in
[
Wai19
]
).
This guarantee is known as the
fast rate
of Lasso.
Therefore, Lasso achieves small prediction error if
\gamma_{\operatorname{supp}(\beta)}(X)
is bounded away from zero.
While other conditions have been studied under which Lasso succeeds, many of them imply in fact the RE condition
[
vdGB09
]
.

An even weaker condition is the
compatibility
condition introduced by
[

. This condition seems less well-known than the RE condition, so we restrict our attention to the latter.

Thus, it is of interest to identify natural families of designs with RE constant bounded away from zero.
It is known that many

[

[

[

.
(Some of these satisfy in fact the stronger restricted isometry property (RIP), which implies RE constant bounded away from zero with respect to
every

In this paper, we introduce a new family of
semirandom

designs — that have RE constant bounded away from zero with respect to the secret even when a subset of the design columns are arbitrarily correlated among themselves.
We note that we adopt a weak definition of the RE constant, matching that in
[
vdGB09
]

[
BRT09
,
NRWY12
,
Wai19

1.1
Main result: partially-rotated designs

Let us start by defining the restricted eigenvalue constant of a matrix. Also see
Section

1.3
for a discussion about our definition.

Definition 1.1

(Restricted eigenvalue constant)
.

For a subset
S\subseteq[d]
, define the set
\mathbb{C}(S)
as
3
3

3

Our results extend straightforwardly to a more general definition of
\mathbb{C}(S)
with
\lVert\beta_{S^{c}}\rVert_{1}\leq L\lVert\beta_{S}\rVert_{1}
. Definitions with
L>1
are often used in the analysis of Lasso in its Lagrangian form.

\mathbb{C}(S)=\left\{\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\kern 5.0pt:\kern 5.0pt\lVert\beta_{S^{c}}\rVert_{1}\leq\lVert\beta_{S}\rVert_{1}\right\}\,.

Then, for a matrix
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
, the
restricted eigenvalue constant
of
X
with respect to
S
is

\gamma_{S}(X)=\min_{\beta\in\mathbb{C}(S)}\frac{\frac{1}{n}\left\lVert X\beta\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\left\lVert\beta_{S}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}\,.

Next, we define the family of matrices that we introduce: partially-rotated matrices.
These are matrices in which a subset of the matrix columns are "randomly rotated" with respect to the other matrix columns.
Specifically, for some set
S\subseteq[d]
, we will require that an arbitrary linear combination of columns in
\bm{X}_{S}
is uncorrelated with an arbitrary linear combination of columns in
\bm{X}_{S^{c}}
with high probability.

Definition 1.2

(Partially-rotated matrix)
.

A random matrix
\bm{X}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
is
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-partially-rotated with respect to a set
S\subseteq[d]
if, for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|S|}
and
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|S^{c}|}
,

\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}}\left(\left\lvert\left\langle\frac{\bm{X}_{S}\alpha}{\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}},\frac{\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta}{\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}}\right\rangle\right\rvert>\varepsilon\right)\leq e^{-\delta n}\,.

A natural way to generate a partially-rotated matrix is to start with some arbitrary matrix
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
and then apply a "random rotation"
\bm{R}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
to the columns of
X
in
S
(or, equivalently, to the columns of
X
in
S^{c}
).
We call the resulting matrix
semirandom
, because the random rotation only serves to decorrelate
X_{S}
from
X_{S^{c}}
, and the arbitrary structure within
X_{S}
and
X_{S^{c}}
is preserved.
In particular, the correlations of columns within
X_{S}
and
X_{S^{c}}
continue to be arbitrary.

We identify two natural families of random matrices
\bm{R}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
whose application leads to partial rotation: random orthogonal matrices and random matrices with independent sub-Gaussian entries.
Let us state these implications formally (we defer the proofs to
Appendix

B
).

Lemma 1.3

(Random rotation implies partial rotation)
.

Let
\bm{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a random matrix such that, for a fixed unit vector
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
\bm{R}v
is distributed uniformly over the unit sphere.
(E.g., take
\bm{R}
to be a random orthogonal matrix.)
Then, for any matrix
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
and any set
S\subseteq[d]
, the matrix
\bm{X}^{\prime}=[X_{S},\bm{R}X_{S^{c}}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
is
(0.01,c)
-partially rotated for some constant
c>0
.

Lemma 1.4

(Sub-Gaussianity implies partial rotation)
.

Let
\bm{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a random matrix with independent entries that have mean zero, variance
\sigma^{2}
, and are
(\sigma^{\prime})^{2}
-sub-Gaussian (see
Definition

A.1
).
Then, for any matrix
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
and any set
S\subseteq[d]
, the matrix
\bm{X}^{\prime}=[X_{S},\bm{R}X_{S^{c}}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
is
(0.01,c\cdot\sigma^{4}/(\sigma^{\prime})^{4})
-partially rotated for some constant
c>0
.

We note in particular that
Lemma

1.4
is satisfied with
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}=\Theta(1)
by matrices
\bm{R}
with i.i.d. standard Gaussian entries or with i.i.d. Rademacher entries.
Also see
Model

1.7
and
Model

1.8
for two applications in which partially-rotated matrices appear as designs.

Our main result is that, for a matrix
\bm{X}
that is partially-rotated with respect to a set
S
, with high probability any subset
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
has RE constant
\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(\bm{X})
lower bounded by
\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(\bm{X}_{S})
if
n
is large enough.
That is, the RE constant of
\bm{X}
with respect to any subset
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
is independent of the columns of
\bm{X}
in
S^{c}
.

Theorem 1.5

(Main result)
.

Suppose a matrix
\bm{X}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
with columns of norm
\sqrt{n}
is
(0.01,c)
-partially-rotated with respect to a set
S\subseteq[d]
for some constant
c>0
.
Then there exists some constant
C>0
such that, with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(n)}
, for all
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
such that
n\geq C|S^{\prime}|\log d/\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(\bm{X}_{S})
it holds that

\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(\bm{X})\geq 0.9\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(\bm{X}_{S})\,.

By the definition of the RE constant, we also have that
\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(\bm{X})\leq\gamma_{S^{\prime}}({\bm{X}}_{S})
, so the conclusion of the theorem holds in fact with approximate equality.

Theorem

1.5
for the prediction error of Lasso.

5
,

, and the surrounding discussion for implications for the recovery of
\beta
itself.

Corollary 1.6

(Main result for Lasso)
.

In the sparse linear regression model in
Equation

1
, suppose that the design
\bm{X}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
with columns of norm
\sqrt{n}
is
(0.01,c)
-partially-rotated with respect to a set
S\subseteq[d]
for some constant
c>0
.
Then, if
\operatorname{supp}(\beta)\subseteq S
, the Lasso estimate
\hat{\beta}_{\mathrm{Lasso}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
in

\frac{1}{n}\left\lVert\bm{X}\hat{\beta}_{\mathrm{Lasso}}-\bm{X}\beta\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq O\left(\sigma^{2}\frac{k\log d}{\gamma_{\operatorname{supp}(\beta)}(\bm{X}_{S})\cdot n}\right)\,.

The prediction error bound in
Corollary

1.6
matches the

in

, but with restricted eigenvalue constant independent of the columns of
\bm{X}
in

— or, in other words, as if the columns of

We remark that, to obtain this fast rate, it was important to prove

in the regime

Finally, let us illustrate the applicability of the result through two specific models of partially-rotated designs.

Model 1.7

(Semirandom i.i.d. Gaussian design)
.

Consider an arbitrary matrix
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
with columns of norm
\sqrt{n}
and an arbitrary

\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

Let the design
\bm{X}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
have columns
\bm{X}^{\prime}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i.i.d.}}{{\sim}}N(0,I_{n})

\bm{X}^{\prime}_{i}=X_{i}

Then
\bm{X}^{\prime}
is
(0.01,\Omega(1))
-partially-rotated with respect to
\operatorname{supp}(\beta)

The conclusion follows by

because of the rotation invariance of the Gaussian distribution.

Model

is a relaxation of the well-studied model in which all entries of the design are i.i.d. standard Gaussian.
Standard results imply that with high probability
{\gamma_{\operatorname{supp}(\beta)}(\bm{X}_{\operatorname{supp}(\beta)})\geq\Omega(1)}
if

Therefore, when the secret is

.
This matches the error that would be achieved if all entries of the design were i.i.d. standard Gaussian, so to obtain this guarantee it suffices that the columns selected by the secret have planted i.i.d. standard Gaussian entries.

Model 1.8

(Rotated adversary design)
.

Consider an arbitrary matrix
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
with columns of norm
\sqrt{n}
.
Suppose an adversary selects
d^{\prime}
arbitrary vectors
a_{1},\ldots,a_{d^{\prime}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of norm
\sqrt{n}
.
Let
\bm{R}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
be a random matrix corresponding to either

or

, and let the design
\bm{X}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times(d+d^{\prime})}
have columns

, and
\bm{R}a_{1},\ldots,\bm{R}a_{d^{\prime}}
.
Then
\bm{X}^{\prime}
is partially-rotated with respect to

.

To motivate
Model

1.8
, let us reinterpret the sparse linear regression problem as a denoising problem: given a sparse linear combination
\bm{y}=X\beta+\bm{w}
of vectors
X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}
with added noise
\bm{w}\sim N(0,\sigma^{2}\cdot I_{n})
, the goal is to estimate the sparse linear combination
X\beta
.
In this view, there is no need to think of the rows of
X
as corresponding to "samples" — the denoising problem makes sense on its own.
Then we can interpret
Model

1.8
as an oblivious adversary model, in which an adversary that does not know
X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}
is allowed to introduce new vectors in the input.
One way to model the adversary not knowing
X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}
is to say that the vectors it introduces should not match
X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}
more than up to a random rotation.

Corollary

1.6
shows that for such an adversary Lasso has denoising guarantees that are independent of the correlations among the outlier vectors.

1.2
Our framework: restricted normalized orthogonality

To prove
Theorem

1.5
, we found it useful to introduce a new deterministic property of matrices that we call
restricted normalized orthogonality
(RNO).
Then, we prove that partially-rotated matrices satisfy RNO with high probability, and that RNO implies the desired property for the RE constants of the matrix.

The RNO has a relatively simple definition and may be of independent interest, so we present it in some detail.
Informally, a matrix satisfies
(s,S,0.01)
-RNO if the
s
-sparse linear combinations of columns in
X_{S}
have small correlations with the
s
-sparse linear combinations of those in
X_{S^{c}}
.

Definition 1.9

(Restricted normalized orthogonality)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{N}
,
S\subseteq[d]
, and
\varepsilon\geq 0
.
A matrix
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
satisfies the
(s,S,\varepsilon)
-
restricted normalized orthogonality
(RNO) condition if, for all
s
-sparse
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|S|}
and
s
-sparse
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|S^{c}|}
,

\left\lvert\left\langle\frac{X_{S}\alpha}{\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}},\frac{X_{S^{c}}\beta}{\left\lVert X_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}}\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq\varepsilon\,,

where we regard the inner product as
0
when
X_{S}\alpha=0
or
X_{S^{c}}\beta=0
.

We then prove that, if
X
satisfies
(\cdot,S,\cdot)
-RNO with appropriate parameters, then the RE constant of
X
with respect to subsets
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
is independent of
X_{S^{c}}
.

Proposition 1.10

(RNO implies RE)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{N}
,
S\subseteq[d]
, and
\varepsilon\geq 0
.
Suppose a matrix
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
with columns of norm
\sqrt{n}
satisfies the
(s,S,\varepsilon)
-RNO condition.
Then there exists some constant
C>0
such that, for any
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
,

\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(X)\geq\left(1-\varepsilon-C\sqrt{\frac{|S^{\prime}|}{\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(X_{S})\cdot s}}\right)\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(X_{S})\,.

Hence, when
(s,S,\varepsilon)
-RNO holds with
s\gtrsim|S^{\prime}|/\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(X_{S})
and with
\varepsilon
bounded away from one, then
\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(X)\geq\Omega(\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(X_{S}))
.
This is exactly the type of conclusion that we want in
Theorem

1.5
.

Finally, we show that partially-rotated matrices satisfy RNO with high probability.

Lemma 1.11

(Partially-rotated matrices satisfy RNO)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
S\subseteq[d]
.
Suppose a matrix
\bm{X}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
is
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-partially-rotated with respect to
S
for some
\delta>0
.
Then there exists some constant
C>0
such that, if
n\geq C\delta^{-1}s\max(\log d,\log(1/\varepsilon))
, then
\bm{X}
satisfies
(s,S,2\varepsilon)
-RNO with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(\delta n)}
.

RNO and other conditions

Let us briefly comment on the relation between RNO and other deterministic conditions that appear in the context of Lasso.
First, we prove in
Appendix

C
that RNO is implied by the restricted isometry property (RIP) of
[
CT05
]
.
However, RIP is a much stronger property: it requires that
all
sparse combinations of columns in
X
are well-behaved, which may not be true with RNO.

Second, we note that RNO is similar to the restricted orthogonality condition of
[
CT05
]
, the main difference being that the latter considers inner products
\left\lvert\left\langle\frac{X_{S}\alpha}{\left\lVert\alpha\right\rVert_{2}},\frac{X_{S^{c}}\beta}{\left\lVert\beta\right\rVert_{2}}\right\rangle\right\rvert
.
However, this difference in normalization makes the latter depend on the correlations among columns of
X_{S^{c}}
: the magnitude of
X_{S^{c}}\beta/\left\lVert\beta\right\rVert_{2}
can be larger by a factor of
\sqrt{s}
when
X_{S^{c}}
has identical columns than when it has random columns.
For our application to partially-rotated matrices we need to tolerate arbitrary correlations among the columns of
X_{S^{c}}
, and our guarantees would be weaker with an additional factor of
\sqrt{s}
.
The normalization in our definition avoids these issues.

1.3
Discussion: restricted eigenvalue constant

Our definition of the RE constant in
Definition

1.1
matches that of
[
vdGB09
]
.
It is also common, however, to define the RE constant with
\lVert\beta\rVert_{2}^{2}
in the denominator instead of
\lVert\beta_{S}\rVert_{2}^{2}

[
BRT09
,
NRWY12
,
Wai19
]
.
Specifically, the alternative version, which we denote by
\gamma^{\prime}_{S}(X)
, is

\gamma^{\prime}_{S}(X)=\min_{\beta\in\mathbb{C}(S)}\frac{\frac{1}{n}\left\lVert X\beta\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\left\lVert\beta\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}\,.

(4)

Because
\lVert\beta\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq\lVert\beta_{S}\rVert_{2}^{2}
, we have
\gamma^{\prime}_{S}(X)\leq\gamma_{S}(X)
for all
X
and
S
.
It is in fact possible that
\gamma_{S}(X)
is bounded away from zero but
\gamma^{\prime}_{S}(X)
is not.
It turns out that our guarantees in
Theorem

1.5
and
Proposition

1.10
do not hold for
\gamma^{\prime}_{S}(X)
: see
Appendix

D
for a counterexample.

Many of the known guarantees of Lasso hold with our definition
[
vdGB09
]
: the prediction error bound in
Equation

3
, and the parameter error bounds

\lVert\hat{\beta}-\beta\rVert_{1}\leq O(\sigma k\sqrt{\log d}/\gamma_{\operatorname{supp}(\beta)}(X)\sqrt{n})

(5)

and

\lVert\hat{\beta}_{\operatorname{supp}(\beta)}-\beta\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq O(\sigma^{2}k\log d/(\gamma_{\operatorname{supp}(\beta)}(X))^{2}n)\,.

(6)

However, the parameter error guarantee
[
NRWY12
,
Wai19
]

\lVert\hat{\beta}-\beta\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq O(\sigma^{2}k\log d/(\gamma^{\prime}_{\operatorname{supp}(\beta)}(X))^{2}n)

does not hold with
(\gamma_{\operatorname{supp}(\beta)}(X))^{2}
, so in our setting we do not achieve this last guarantee.

In these papers, an adversary is allowed to corrupt a fraction of the samples in the input, and the question is whether polynomial-time algorithms can still recover a good estimate.

KLL+23
]
, which considers a semirandom design in which only a subset of the rows is well-conditioned.
Nevertheless, these results are incomparable with ours, because in our setting we have ill-behaved design
columns
, not rows (samples).

2
Techniques

In this section we give an overview of our proofs.
For simplicity, we focus on the special case where a matrix is partially-rotated with respect to a set of cardinality
k
.
Specifically, let
\bm{X}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
have columns of norm
\sqrt{n}
and be partially-rotated with respect to a set
S\subseteq[d]
with
|S|=k
.

Suppose we were trying to prove
Theorem

1.5
directly.
The critical fact to prove turns out to be that, for
n
large enough, with high probability
\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S}\alpha+\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert^{2}\geq 0.99\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S}\alpha\right\rVert^{2}
for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
and
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|S^{c}|}
with
\lVert\beta\rVert_{1}\leq\lVert\alpha\rVert_{1}
.
That is, linear combinations of columns in
\bm{X}_{S}
cannot be canceled by appropriate linear combinations of columns in
\bm{X}_{S^{c}}
.
Quantitatively:

Fact 2.1
.

Suppose
\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}\geq\sqrt{\gamma n}\left\lVert\alpha\right\rVert_{2}
for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
.
If
n\geq Ck\log d/\gamma
for some absolute constant
C>0
, then with high probability, for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
and
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|S^{c}|}
with
\lVert\beta\rVert_{1}\leq\lVert\alpha\rVert_{1}
,

\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S}\alpha+\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq 0.99\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\,.

(7)

We note that, to prove
Fact

2.1
, it would suffice to prove that for all
\alpha
and
\beta

\left\lvert\left\langle\frac{\bm{X}_{S}\alpha}{\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}},\frac{\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta}{\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}}\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq 0.01\,.

(8)

However, this inequality does not hold: in particular, if the columns of
\bm{X}_{S^{c}}
span all of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, then it is always possible to choose
\beta
such that the inner product is equal to
1
.

Attempt via Hölder’s inequality

Because we have constraints in terms of the
\ell_{1}
-norms of
\alpha
and
\beta
, a natural approach is to apply Hölder’s inequality as

\left\lvert\left\langle\frac{\bm{X}_{S}\alpha}{\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}},\frac{\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta}{\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}}\right\rangle\right\rvert=\left\lvert\left\langle\frac{\bm{X}_{S^{c}}^{\top}\bm{X}_{S}\alpha}{\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}},\frac{\beta}{\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}}\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq\frac{\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S^{c}}^{\top}\bm{X}_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{\infty}}{\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}}\cdot\frac{\left\lVert\beta\right\rVert_{1}}{\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}}\,.

Using the definition of partial rotation, we can prove by standard concentration bounds that with high probability
\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S^{c}}^{\top}\bm{X}_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq O(\sqrt{k\log d})\lVert\bm{X}_{S}\alpha\rVert_{2}
for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
.
For the other terms, we have
\lVert\beta\rVert_{1}\leq\lVert\alpha\rVert_{1}\leq\sqrt{k}\lVert\alpha\rVert_{2}
and, because
Fact

2.1
is trivially true when
\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}\ll\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}
, also
\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}\geq\Omega\left(\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}\right)\geq\Omega(\sqrt{\gamma n}\lVert\alpha\rVert_{2})
.
Combining these, we get with high probability

\left\lvert\left\langle\frac{\bm{X}_{S}\alpha}{\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}},\frac{\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta}{\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}}\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq O\left(\sqrt{\frac{k^{2}\log d}{\gamma n}}\right)\,,

which is
\leq 0.01
when
n\geq Ck^{2}\log d/\gamma
.
Recall that we aim for a linear dependence on
k
, so we need to do better.

Result for sparse linear combinations

An important observation is that, if
n\geq Cs\log d
, then
Equation

8
is true with high probability for all
s
-sparse
\alpha
and
\beta
.
In other words,
\bm{X}
satisfies
(s,S,0.01)
-RNO with high probability.
This statement is our
Lemma

1.11
.
The proof involves an
\varepsilon
-net argument combined with the definition of partial rotation.

Hence, because we are interested in the case
n\geq Ck\log d/\gamma
, the conclusion of
Fact

2.1
is true for all
O(k/\gamma)
-sparse
\alpha
and
\beta
.
Recall that in this section we consider the special case
|S|=k
, so all
\alpha
are
k
-sparse and hence also
O(k/\gamma)
-sparse.
However,
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|S^{c}|}
may not be sparse.
How can we then use this result?

Sparsification

Suppose we had an
O(k/\gamma)
-sparse approximation
\beta^{\prime}
of
\beta
.
Then, using the triangle inequality, we could write

\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S}\alpha+\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}\geq\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S}\alpha+\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}-\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta^{\prime}-\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}\,.

(9)

Because
\beta^{\prime}
is
O(k/\gamma)
-sparse, we can apply the conclusion of
Fact

2.1
to the first term on the right-hand side and lower bound it by
\Omega(\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2})
.
But how to bound the term
\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta^{\prime}-\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}
?
In other words, how to control the quality of the approximation?

It turns out that an effective approximation follows from an approximate Carathéodory’s theorem obtained by subsampling the coordinates of
\beta
(see Theorem 0.0.2 in
[
Ver18
]
).
Given the approximate Carathéodory’s theorem, we easily derive the following approximation lemma (similar sparsification results have been observed before in the statistics literature; e.g., see Lemma 5.1 in
[
Oli16
]
and Lemma 2.7 in
[
LM17
]
):

Lemma 2.2

(Sparsification with
\ell_{1}
-norm error)
.

Let
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
. For each
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
1\leq s\leq d
, there exists some
s
-sparse
\beta^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that

\left\lVert X\beta-X\beta^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\frac{2\max\{\left\lVert X_{1}\right\rVert_{2},\ldots,\left\lVert X_{d}\right\rVert_{2}\}}{\sqrt{s}}\lVert\beta\rVert_{1}\,.

Hence, by choosing
s=O(k/\gamma)
large enough in
Lemma

2.2
, there exists some
O(k/\gamma)
-sparse
\beta^{\prime}
such that
\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta^{\prime}-\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}\leq c\sqrt{\gamma n/k}\lVert\beta\rVert_{1}
for some small enough
c>0
.
Plugging this bound back into
Equation

9
, and using that
\lVert\beta\rVert_{1}\leq\lVert\alpha\rVert_{1}\leq\sqrt{k}\lVert\alpha\rVert_{2}
and
\lVert\alpha\rVert_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma n}}\lVert\bm{X}_{S}\alpha\rVert_{2}
, gives the desired result.
This part of the argument, properly generalized, is our
Proposition

1.10
.

3
Sparsification with
\ell_{1}
-norm error

We first state Theorem 0.0.2 of
[
Ver18
]
, which gives an approximate Carathéodory’s theorem.
The proof of this result is by subsampling: it shows that, given a set
T
and a point
x\in\operatorname{conv}(T)
, if we sample
s
points from
T
with probability proportional to their weight in the convex combination
x
, then the expected distance of
x
from their average is bounded by
\operatorname{diam}(T)/\sqrt{s}
.
This argument is often referred to as Maurey’s empirical method
[
Pis81
]
.

Fact 3.1

(See Theorem 0.0.2 in
[
Ver18
]
)
.

Consider a set
T\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose diameter is bounded by
1
.
Then, for every point
x\in\operatorname{conv}(T)
and every integer
s
, one can find points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\in T
such that

\left\lVert x-\frac{1}{s}\sum_{i=1}^{s}x_{i}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\,.

Based on this result, we derive a sparsification result for our setting with error depending on the vector’s
\ell_{1}
-norm.
We note that similar sparsification results have been observed before in the statistics literature; e.g., see Lemma 5.1 in
[
Oli16
]
and Lemma 2.7 in
[
LM17
]
.

Lemma

(Restatement of
Lemma

2.2
)
.

Let
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
. For each
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
1\leq s\leq d
, there exists some
s
-sparse
\beta^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that

\left\lVert X\beta-X\beta^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\frac{2\max\{\left\lVert X_{1}\right\rVert_{2},\ldots,\left\lVert X_{d}\right\rVert_{2}\}}{\sqrt{s}}\lVert\beta\rVert_{1}\,.

Proof.

Let
D=\max\{\left\lVert X_{1}\right\rVert_{2},\ldots,\left\lVert X_{d}\right\rVert_{2}\}
be the maximum norm of a column of
X
.
Then let
T=\{X_{1}/2D,\ldots,X_{d}/2D,-X_{1}/2D,\ldots,-X_{d}/2D\}
be the set of columns of
X
and their negations divided by
2D
.
The diameter of
T
is then bounded by
1
.

Note that

\frac{X\beta}{2D\lVert\beta\rVert_{1}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\beta_{i}}{2D\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\beta_{j}|}X_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{|\beta_{i}|}{\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\beta_{j}|}(\operatorname{sign}(\beta_{i})X_{i}/2D)

is a convex combination of points in
T
. Then, by
Fact

3.1
, there exist
x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\in T
such that
\frac{X\beta}{2D\lVert\beta\rVert_{1}}
has distance at most
1/\sqrt{s}
from
\frac{1}{s}\sum_{i=1}^{s}x_{i}
.
Observe that
\frac{1}{s}\sum_{i=1}^{s}x_{i}
can be written as
X\beta^{\prime}
for some
s
-sparse
\beta^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Thus

\left\lVert\frac{X\beta}{2D\lVert\beta\rVert_{1}}-X\beta^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\,.

Multiplying both sides by
2D\lVert\beta\rVert_{1}
gives the desired conclusion.
∎

4
Proof that RNO implies RE

Proposition

(Restatement of
Proposition

1.10
)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{N}
,
S\subseteq[d]
, and
\varepsilon\geq 0
.
Suppose a matrix
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
with columns of norm
\sqrt{n}
satisfies the
(s,S,\varepsilon)
-RNO condition.
Then there exists some constant
C>0
such that, for any
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
,

\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(X)\geq\left(1-\varepsilon-C\sqrt{\frac{|S^{\prime}|}{\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(X_{S})\cdot s}}\right)\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(X_{S})\,.

Proof.

Consider some
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
z\in\mathbb{C}(S^{\prime})
, and let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|S|}
and
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|S^{c}|}
such that
z_{S}=\alpha
and
z_{S^{c}}=\beta
.
We have
Xz=X_{S}\alpha+X_{S^{c}}\beta
.
Then, by
Lemma

2.2
, there exist
s
-sparse
\alpha^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime}
with
\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha^{\prime}-X_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{n}{s}}\lVert\alpha\rVert_{1}
and
\left\lVert X_{S^{c}}\beta^{\prime}-X_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{n}{s}}\lVert\beta\rVert_{1}
.
Hence

\left\lVert Xz\right\rVert_{2}\geq\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha^{\prime}+X_{S^{c}}\beta^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}-O\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{s}}\right)(\left\lVert\alpha\right\rVert_{1}+\left\lVert\beta\right\rVert_{1})\,.

If
X_{S}\alpha^{\prime}=0
or
X_{S^{c}}\beta^{\prime}=0
, the next step will hold trivially, so assume that both are non-zero.
Then, using that
X
satisfies
(s,S,\delta)
-RNO and that
\alpha^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime}
are
s
-sparse, we get that

\displaystyle\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha^{\prime}+X_{S^{c}}\beta^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left\lVert X_{s}\alpha^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert X_{S^{c}}\beta^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-2\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert X_{S^{c}}\beta^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}\left\lvert\left\langle\frac{X_{S}\alpha^{\prime}}{\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}},\frac{X_{S^{c}}\beta^{\prime}}{\left\lVert X_{S^{c}}\beta^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}}\right\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle\geq\left\lVert X_{s}\alpha^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert X_{S^{c}}\beta^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-2\delta\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert X_{S^{c}}\beta^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=(1-\delta)\left\lVert X_{s}\alpha^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+(1-\delta)\left\lVert X_{S^{c}}\beta^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left(\sqrt{\delta}\left\lVert X_{s}\alpha^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}-\sqrt{\delta}\left\lVert X_{S^{c}}\beta^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\delta)\left\lVert X_{s}\alpha^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\,.

Then, replacing
\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha^{\prime}+X_{S^{c}}\beta^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}
by
(1-\delta)\left\lVert X_{s}\alpha^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}
in our lower bound on
\left\lVert Xz\right\rVert_{2}
, and using again that
\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha^{\prime}-X_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2n}{s}}\lVert\alpha\rVert_{1}
, we get that

\left\lVert Xz\right\rVert_{2}\geq\sqrt{1-\delta}\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}-O\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{s}}\right)(\left\lVert\alpha\right\rVert_{1}+\left\lVert\beta\right\rVert_{1})\,.

Let us use now that
z\in\mathbb{C}(S^{\prime})
. This implies that
\lVert z_{(S^{\prime})^{c}}\rVert_{1}\leq\lVert z_{S^{\prime}}\rVert_{1}
, and therefore, because
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
, also that
\lVert\beta\rVert_{1}\leq\lVert\alpha_{S^{\prime}}\rVert_{1}\leq\lVert\alpha\rVert_{1}
. Hence

\left\lVert Xz\right\rVert_{2}\geq\sqrt{1-\delta}\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}-O\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{s}}\right)\left\lVert\alpha\right\rVert_{1}\,.

The fact that
\lVert z_{(S^{\prime})^{c}}\rVert_{1}\leq\lVert z_{S^{\prime}}\rVert_{1}
also implies that
\lVert\alpha_{(S^{\prime})^{c}}\rVert_{1}\leq\lVert\alpha_{S^{\prime}}\rVert_{1}
.
In particular, this implies that
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}(S^{\prime})
, so
\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}\geq\sqrt{n\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(X_{S})}\lVert\alpha_{S^{\prime}}\rVert_{2}
.
Finally,
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}(S^{\prime})
also implies that
\lVert\alpha\rVert_{1}\leq 2\lVert\alpha_{S^{\prime}}\rVert_{1}\leq 2\sqrt{|S^{\prime}|}\lVert\alpha_{S^{\prime}}\rVert_{2}
, so putting everything together

\displaystyle\left\lVert Xz\right\rVert_{2}\geq\left(\sqrt{(1-\delta)n\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(X_{S})}-O\left(\sqrt{\frac{|S^{\prime}|n}{s}}\right)\right)\lVert\alpha_{S^{\prime}}\rVert_{2}\,,

and using that
\alpha_{S^{\prime}}=z_{S^{\prime}}
, squaring, and rearranging,

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{1}{n}\left\lVert Xz\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\lVert z_{S^{\prime}}\rVert_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\sqrt{1-\delta}-O\left(\sqrt{\frac{|S^{\prime}|}{\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(X_{S})\cdot s}}\right)\right)^{2}\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(X_{S})

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\delta-2\sqrt{1-\delta}\cdot O\left(\sqrt{\frac{|S^{\prime}|}{\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(X_{S})\cdot s}}\right)\right)\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(X_{S})

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\delta-C\sqrt{\frac{|S^{\prime}|}{\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(X_{S})\cdot s}}\right)\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(X_{S})

for some large enough constant
C
.
Because this holds for all
z\in\mathbb{C}(S^{\prime})
, we obtain the desired lower bound on
\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(X)
.
∎

Proof that partially-rotated matrices satisfy RNO

Lemma

(Restatement of
Lemma

1.11
)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
S\subseteq[d]
.
Suppose a matrix
\bm{X}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
is
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-partially-rotated with respect to
S
for some
\delta>0
.
Then there exists some constant
C>0
such that, if
n\geq C\delta^{-1}s\max(\log d,\log(1/\varepsilon))
, then
\bm{X}
satisfies
(s,S,2\varepsilon)
-RNO with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(\delta n)}
.

Proof.

Consider some fixed sets
S_{\alpha}\subseteq S
with
|S_{\alpha}|=s
and
S_{\beta}\subseteq S
with
|S_{\beta}|=s
.
We first prove the result for
\alpha
and
\beta
satisfying
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)\subseteq S_{\alpha}
and
\operatorname{supp}(\beta)\subseteq S_{\beta}
, and then union bound over all
\leq d^{2s}
choices of
S_{\alpha}
and
S_{\beta}
.
Let
\bm{\Phi}_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times s},\bm{\Phi}_{\beta}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times s}
be orthonormal bases of the span of the columns of
\bm{X}_{S}
indexed by
S_{\alpha}
and the span of the columns of
\bm{X}_{S^{c}}
indexed by
S_{\beta}
, respectively.
Then

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\langle\frac{\bm{X}_{S}\alpha}{\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}},\frac{\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta}{\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}}\right\rangle\right\rvert=\left\lvert\left\langle\bm{\Phi}_{\alpha}v_{\alpha},\bm{\Phi}_{\beta}v_{\beta}\right\rangle\right\rvert\,,

where
v_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{s}
and
v_{\beta}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{s}
are such that
\bm{\Phi}_{\alpha}v_{\alpha}=\frac{\bm{X}_{S}\alpha}{\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}}
and
\bm{\Phi}_{\beta}v_{\beta}=\frac{\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta}{\left\lVert\bm{X}_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}}
.
By
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-partial rotation, we have for each
v_{\alpha}
and
v_{\beta}
that
\mathbb{P}(\left\lvert\left\langle\bm{\Phi}_{\alpha}v_{\alpha},\bm{\Phi}_{\beta}v_{\beta}\right\rangle\right\rvert>\varepsilon)\leq e^{-\delta n}
.

Consider an
\varepsilon/100
-cover
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\}
of the unit
s
-dimensional ball. Such a cover exists with
m\leq O(1/\varepsilon)^{s}
(see Example 5.8 in
[
Wai19
]
).
By a union bound over all
m^{2}
pairs of vectors in the net, for
n\geq C\delta^{-1}s\log(1/\varepsilon)
large enough, we also get with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(\delta n)}
that
\left\lvert\left\langle\bm{\Phi}_{\alpha}v_{i},\bm{\Phi}_{\beta}v_{j}\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq\varepsilon
for all
i,j\in[m]
.
Then, for some arbitrary
v_{\alpha}
and
v_{\beta}
, we can select
v^{\prime}_{\alpha}
and
v^{\prime}_{\beta}
in the cover with
\left\lVert v^{\prime}_{\alpha}-v_{\alpha}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\varepsilon/100
and
\left\lVert v^{\prime}_{\beta}-v_{\beta}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\varepsilon/100
, so by the triangle inequality

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\langle\bm{\Phi}_{\alpha}v_{\alpha},\bm{\Phi}_{\beta}v_{\beta}\right\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\left\langle\bm{\Phi}_{\alpha}v^{\prime}_{\alpha},\bm{\Phi}_{\beta}v^{\prime}_{\beta}\right\rangle\right\rvert+\left\lvert\left\langle\bm{\Phi}_{\alpha}v_{\alpha},\bm{\Phi}_{\beta}(v^{\prime}_{\beta}-v_{\beta})\right\rangle\right\rvert+\left\lvert\left\langle\bm{\Phi}_{\alpha}(v^{\prime}_{\alpha}-v_{\alpha}),\bm{\Phi}_{\beta}v^{\prime}_{\beta}\right\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\varepsilon/100+\varepsilon/100\leq 2\varepsilon\,.

Finally, by union bounding over all
\leq d^{2s}
choices of
S_{\alpha}
and
S_{\beta}
, we get the same bound with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(\delta n)}
for all
s
-sparse
\alpha
and
s
-sparse
\beta
, for
n\geq C\delta^{-1}s\log d
large enough.
∎

Proof of main result

Here we finally prove
Theorem

1.5
.
The proof of

1.6
is deferred to

.

Theorem

(Restatement of
Theorem

1.5
)
.

Suppose a matrix
\bm{X}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
with columns of norm
\sqrt{n}
is
(0.01,c)
-partially-rotated with respect to a set
S\subseteq[d]
for some constant
c>0
.
Then there exists some constant
C>0
such that, with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(n)}
, for all
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
such that
n\geq C|S^{\prime}|\log d/\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(\bm{X}_{S})
it holds that

\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(\bm{X})\geq 0.9\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(\bm{X}_{S})\,.

Proof.

(Proof of
Theorem

1.5
)
Consider some fixed
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
such that
n\geq C|S^{\prime}|\log d/\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(\bm{X}_{S})
for some large enough constant
C>0
.
Then we get by
Lemma

1.11
that
\bm{X}
satisfies
(C^{\prime}|S^{\prime}|/\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(\bm{X}_{S}),S,0.02)
-RNO for some large enough constant
C^{\prime}>0
with probability at least
1-e^{-cn}
for some small enough constant
c>0
.
Then, by
Proposition

1.10
,
\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(\bm{X})\geq 0.9\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(\bm{X}_{S})
.

It remains to union bound over all choices
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
with
n\geq C|S^{\prime}|\log d/\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(\bm{X}_{S})
.
Note that, under this lower bound on
n
, we have
|S^{\prime}|\leq\frac{n\cdot\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(\bm{X}_{S})}{C\log d}\leq\frac{n}{C\log d}
, where we used that always
\gamma_{S^{\prime}}(\bm{X}_{S})\leq 1
.
It suffices to union bound over only the maximal
S^{\prime}
.
Then, by union bounding over all
\binom{d}{|S^{\prime}|}\leq d^{|S^{\prime}|}\leq d^{n/(C\log d)}=e^{n/C}
choices, we get probability of success
1-e^{-cn+n/C}
, which for
C>1/c
is
1-e^{-\Omega(n)}
.
∎

Appendix A
Concentration bounds

In this section we state some standard concentration bounds that we use in our proofs.

Definition A.1

(Sub-Gaussian random variable)
.

A random variable
\bm{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}
is
\sigma^{2}
-sub-Gaussian if
\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(\bm{x}-\mathbb{E}\bm{x})}\leq e^{\lambda^{2}\sigma^{2}/2}
for all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Fact A.2

(See text before Proposition 2.5 in
[
Wai19
]
)
.

If two random variables
\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
are independent and
\sigma_{1}^{2}
-sub-Gaussian and
\sigma_{2}^{2}
-sub-Gaussian, respectively, then
\bm{x}_{1}+\bm{x}_{2}
is
(\sigma_{1}^{2}+\sigma_{2}^{2})
-sub-Gaussian.

Fact A.3

(See Equation 2.9 in
[
Wai19
]
)
.

Let
\bm{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}
be
\sigma^{2}
-sub-Gaussian. Then

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lvert\bm{g}\right\rvert\geq t\right)\leq 2e^{-t^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})}\,.

Fact A.4

(See Example 2.11 in
[
Wai19
]
)
.

Let
\bm{g}\sim N(0,I_{n})
. Then for
t\in(0,1)

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lvert\frac{1}{n}\left\lVert\bm{g}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-1\right\rvert\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}n}{8}\right)\,.

Fact A.5

(Hanson-Wright, see Theorem 1.1 in
[
RV13
]
)
.

Let
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
have independent
\sigma^{2}
-sub-Gaussian entries with mean
0
.
Then there exists some absolute constant
c>0
such that, for all
t\geq 0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(|\bm{X}^{\top}A\bm{X}-\mathbb{E}\bm{X}^{\top}A\bm{X}|>t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-c\cdot\min\left(\frac{t^{2}}{\sigma^{4}\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{F}^{2}},\frac{t}{\sigma^{2}\left\lVert A\right\rVert}\right)\right)\,.

Deferred proofs

Lemma

(Restatement of
Lemma

1.3
)
.

Let
\bm{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a random matrix such that, for a fixed unit vector
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
\bm{R}v
is distributed uniformly over the unit sphere.
(E.g., take
\bm{R}
to be a random orthogonal matrix.)
Then, for any matrix
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
and any set
S\subseteq[d]
, the matrix
\bm{X}^{\prime}=[X_{S},\bm{R}X_{S^{c}}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
is
(0.01,c)
-partially rotated for some constant
c>0
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{\bm{X}^{\prime}_{S}\alpha}{\left\lVert\bm{X}^{\prime}_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}},\frac{\bm{X}^{\prime}_{S^{c}}\beta}{\left\lVert\bm{X}^{\prime}_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{X_{S}\alpha}{\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}},\frac{\bm{R}X_{S^{c}}\beta}{\left\lVert\bm{R}X_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}}\right\rangle=\left\langle\frac{X_{S}\alpha}{\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}},\bm{R}\frac{X_{S^{c}}\beta}{\left\lVert X_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}}\right\rangle\,,

where in the last equality we used that
\ell_{2}
-norms are rotation-invariant.
So, equivalently, it suffices to bound
\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lvert\langle v,\bm{R}w\rangle\right\rvert>\varepsilon\right)
for arbitrary fixed unit vectors
v,w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

We have that
\bm{R}w
is distributed uniformly over the unit sphere.
Therefore
\bm{R}w
is distributed as
\bm{g}/\lVert\bm{g}\rVert
, where
\bm{g}\sim N(0,I_{n})
.
Then
\langle v,\bm{R}w\rangle
is distributed as
\langle v,\bm{g}\rangle/\lVert\bm{g}\rVert
.
Because
\langle v,\bm{g}\rangle\sim N(0,1)
, we have by
Fact

A.3
that
\mathbb{P}(|\langle v,\bm{g}\rangle|\geq\varepsilon\sqrt{n}/2)\leq 2e^{-\varepsilon^{2}n/8}
.
In addition, we have by
Fact

A.4
that
\mathbb{P}(\lVert\bm{g}\rVert_{2}<\sqrt{n}/2)\leq 2e^{-n/32}
.
Therefore,

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lvert\langle v,\bm{R}w\rangle\right\rvert>\varepsilon\right)\leq 2e^{-\varepsilon^{2}n/8}+2e^{-n/32}\leq e^{-\delta n}

for
\delta=\Omega(\varepsilon^{2})
small enough, and then
\bm{X}^{\prime}
is
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-partially rotated.
Choosing
\varepsilon=0.01
gives the stated result.
∎

Lemma

(Restatement of
Lemma

1.4
)
.

Let
\bm{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a random matrix with independent entries that have mean zero, variance
\sigma^{2}
, and are
(\sigma^{\prime})^{2}
-sub-Gaussian (see
Definition

A.1
).
Then, for any matrix
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
and any set
S\subseteq[d]
, the matrix
\bm{X}^{\prime}=[X_{S},\bm{R}X_{S^{c}}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
is
(0.01,c\cdot\sigma^{4}/(\sigma^{\prime})^{4})
-partially rotated for some constant
c>0
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{\bm{X}^{\prime}_{S}\alpha}{\left\lVert\bm{X}^{\prime}_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}},\frac{\bm{X}^{\prime}_{S^{c}}\beta}{\left\lVert\bm{X}^{\prime}_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{X_{S}\alpha}{\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}},\frac{\bm{R}X_{S^{c}}\beta}{\left\lVert\bm{R}X_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}}\right\rangle\,,

so, equivalently, it suffices to bound
\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lvert\langle v,\frac{\bm{R}w}{\left\lVert\bm{R}w\right\rVert_{2}}\rangle\right\rvert>\varepsilon\right)
for arbitrary fixed unit vectors
v,w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

First, we show that
\left\lvert\langle v,\bm{R}w\rangle\right\rvert
is small.
We have that
\langle v,\bm{R}w\rangle=\langle\bm{R},v\otimes w\rangle
, which by
Fact

A.2
is sub-Gaussian with mean
0
and variance proxy
(\sigma^{\prime})^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{i}^{2}v_{j}^{2}=(\sigma^{\prime})^{2}
.
Then, by
Fact

A.3
, we have that
\mathbb{P}(\left\lvert\langle v,\bm{R}w\rangle\right\rvert\geq\varepsilon\sigma\sqrt{n}/2)\leq 2e^{-\Omega(\varepsilon^{2}n\sigma^{2}/(\sigma^{\prime})^{2})}
.

Second, we show that
\left\lVert\bm{R}w\right\rVert_{2}
is large.
We start by observing that
\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\bm{R}w\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=w^{\top}\left(\mathbb{E}\bm{R}^{\top}\bm{R}\right)w=\sigma^{2}n
.
To get a bound on the deviation from the mean, we start by rewriting
\left\lVert\bm{R}w\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\operatorname{vec}(\bm{R})^{\top}(A)\operatorname{vec}(\bm{R})
, where
\operatorname{vec}(\bm{R})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n^{2}}
has entries
\operatorname{vec}(\bm{R})_{(i,j)}=\bm{R}_{i,j}
, and where
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n^{2}\times n^{2}}

A_{(i_{1},j_{1}),(i_{2},j_{2})}=\begin{cases}w_{j_{1}}w_{j_{2}}&\text{ if }i_{1}=i_{2}\,,\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

We remark that, up to a permutation of the rows and columns,
A
is a block diagonal matrix with
n
blocks of size
n\times n
, each block equal to
ww^{\top}
.
From this, a simple calculation shows that
\left\lVert A\right\rVert=1
and
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{F}^{2}=n
.
Then, by the Hanson-Wright inequality in
Fact

A.5
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lvert\left\lVert\bm{R}w\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-\sigma^{2}n\right\rvert>t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\Omega\left(\min\left(\frac{t^{2}}{(\sigma^{\prime})^{4}n},\frac{t}{(\sigma^{\prime})^{2}}\right)\right)\right)\,.

We note that, if a random variable is
(\sigma^{\prime})^{2}
-sub-Gaussian, then its variance is bounded by
(\sigma^{\prime})^{2}
, so
\sigma^{2}\leq(\sigma^{\prime})^{2}
.
Then, we get that
\mathbb{P}(\left\lVert\bm{R}w\right\rVert_{2}^{2}<\sigma^{2}n/4)\leq 2e^{-\Omega(n\sigma^{4}/(\sigma^{\prime})^{4})}

\mathbb{P}(\left\lVert\bm{R}w\right\rVert_{2}<\sigma\sqrt{n}/2)\leq 2e^{-\Omega(n\sigma^{4}/(\sigma^{\prime})^{4})}

Therefore, putting the bounds on the numerator and the denominator together,

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lvert\left\langle v,\frac{\bm{R}w}{\left\lVert\bm{R}w\right\rVert_{2}}\right\rangle\right\rvert>\varepsilon\right)\leq 2e^{-\Omega(\varepsilon^{2}n\sigma^{2}/(\sigma^{\prime})^{2})}+2e^{-\Omega(n\sigma^{4}/(\sigma^{\prime})^{4})}\leq e^{-\delta n}

for
\delta=\Omega(\varepsilon^{2}\sigma^{4}/(\sigma^{\prime})^{4})
small enough, and then
\bm{X}^{\prime}
is
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-partially rotated.
Choosing
\varepsilon=0.01
gives the stated result.
∎

Appendix C
RIP implies RNO

In this section we give a simple proof that RIP implies RNO. Let us first define RIP:

Definition C.1

(Restricted isometry property)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta\geq 0
.
A matrix
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
satisfies the
(s,\delta)
-
restricted isometry property
(RIP) condition if, for all
s
-sparse
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

(1-\delta)\lVert\beta\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert X\beta\rVert_{2}\leq(1+\delta)\lVert\beta\rVert_{2}\,.

Then we obtain the following implication:

Proposition C.2

(RIP implies RNO)
.

Suppose a matrix
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
satisfies
(2s,\delta)
-RIP.
Then, for any
S\subseteq[d]
,
X
satisfies
(s,S,\frac{4\delta}{(1-\delta)^{2}})
-RNO.

Proof.

By the definition of RNO, we can ignore the cases
\alpha=0
and
\beta=0
.
Because the inner product in the definition of RNO is invariant to scalings of
\alpha
and
\beta
, we can also assume that both
\alpha
and
\beta
are unit vectors.
Consider then some unit
s
-sparse
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|S|}
and unit
s
-sparse
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|S^{c}|}
. Then

\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha+X_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert^{2}=\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert X_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+2\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert X_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}\left\langle\frac{X_{S}\alpha}{\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}},\frac{X_{S^{c}}\beta}{\left\lVert X_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}}\right\rangle\,,

so

\left\langle\frac{X_{S}\alpha}{\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}},\frac{X_{S^{c}}\beta}{\left\lVert X_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}}\right\rangle=\frac{\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha+X_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-\left\lVert X_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{2\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert X_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}}\,.

By
(2s,\delta)
-RIP, we have the following three inequalities:

(1-\delta)^{2}\left(\left\lVert\alpha\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert\beta\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)\leq\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha+X_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq(1+\delta)^{2}\left(\left\lVert\alpha\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert\beta\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)\,,

(1-\delta)^{2}\left\lVert\alpha\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq(1+\delta)^{2}\left\lVert\alpha\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\,,

(1-\delta)^{2}\left\lVert\beta\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\left\lVert X_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq(1+\delta)^{2}\left\lVert\beta\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\,.

Therefore,

\left\lvert\left\langle\frac{X_{S}\alpha}{\left\lVert X_{S}\alpha\right\rVert_{2}},\frac{X_{S^{c}}\beta}{\left\lVert X_{S^{c}}\beta\right\rVert_{2}}\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq\frac{4\delta\lVert\alpha\rVert_{2}^{2}+4\delta\lVert\beta\rVert_{2}^{2}}{2(1-\delta)^{2}\lVert\alpha\rVert_{2}\lVert\beta\rVert_{2}}\leq\frac{4\delta}{(1-\delta)^{2}}\,.

∎

Appendix D
Counterexample for alternative definition of RE constant

In this section we give a counterexample to
Theorem

1.5
under the alternative definition of the RE constant in
Equation

4
.
Specifically, we construct a family of matrices
\bm{X}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
that are partially-rotated with respect to a subset
S\subseteq[d]
, but for which even for arbitrarily large
n
it holds that
\gamma^{\prime}_{S}(\bm{X})\lesssim\gamma^{\prime}_{S}(\bm{X}_{S})/k
.

Proposition D.1
.

Let
n,d\geq k+2
.
Then there exists a matrix
\bm{X}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
that is partially-rotated with respect to a set
S\subseteq[d]
and for which
\gamma^{\prime}_{S}(\bm{X}_{S})=1
and with high probability
\gamma^{\prime}_{S}(\bm{X})\leq O(1/k)
.

Proof.

Consider the matrix
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times(k+2)}
with the following columns:

•

For
i=1,\ldots,k
, let
A_{i}=\sqrt{n}e_{i}
, where
e_{i}
is the vector with
1
on coordinate
i
and
0
elsewhere,

•

Let
A_{k+1}=A_{k+2}
be arbitrary of norm
\sqrt{n}
.

Let
\bm{R}^{n\times n}
be a uniformly random orthogonal matrix, and let
\bm{X}=[A_{1},\ldots,A_{k},\bm{R}A_{k+1},\bm{R}A_{k+2}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times(k+2)}
.
Denote the set
\{1,\ldots,k\}
by
[k]
.
Then
\bm{X}
is partially-rotated with respect to
[k]
.
We also have trivially that
\gamma^{\prime}_{[k]}(\bm{X}_{[k]})=1

\beta=[1,\ldots,1,k/2,-k/2]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k+2}

Safety and efficacy of a feed additive consisting of fumonisin esterase produced with Komagataella phaffii NCAIM (P) Y001485 for all terrestrial species (Dr. Bata Ltd.)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Giovanna Martelli, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Katerina Theodoridou, Secundino López‐Puente, Montserrat Anguita, Matteo L Innocenti, Jordi Ortuño
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the safety and efficacy of fumonisin esterase produced with the genetically modified strain Komagataella phaffii NCAIM (P) Y001485 as a technological feed additive (functional group: substances for reduction of the contamination of feed by mycotoxins) for all terrestrial species. Based on the data provided by the applicant in this and previous applications, the FEEDAP Panel concluded that the additive does not pose any safety concern regarding the genetically modified production strain, is safe for all terrestrial species at 360 U/kg complete feed, and its use in animal nutrition is of no concern for consumers or the environment. The additive is not irritant to skin or eyes. Due to the proteinaceous nature of the additive and the fact that the carrier is an inactivated microorganism, the additive is considered a dermal and respiratory sensitiser. Exposure by any route is considered a risk. The additive has the potential to be efficacious in all terrestrial species when added to feed at the proposed conditions of use.Keywords: efficacy, fumonisin esterase, Komagataella phaffii, safety, substances for reduction of the contamination of feed by mycotoxins, technological additives

INTRODUCTION

Background and Terms of Reference

Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 1 establishes the rules governing the Union authorisation of additives for use in animal nutrition. In particular, Article 4(1) of that Regulation lays down that any person seeking authorisation for a feed additive or for a new use of a feed additive shall submit an application in accordance with Article 7. Article 13(3) of that Regulation lays down that, if the holder of an authorisation proposes changing the terms of the authorisation by submitting an application to the Commission accompanied by the relevant data supporting the request for the change, the Authority shall transmit its opinion on the proposal to the Commission and the Member States.

The European Commission received a request from Dr. Bata Ltd. 2 for the authorisation of the additive consisting of fumonisin esterase (EC 3.1.1.87) produced with Komagataella phaffii NCAIM (P) Y001485, when used as a feed additive for all terrestrial species (category: technological additives; functional group: substances for reduction of the contamination of feed by mycotoxins). The applicant is also requesting a modification of the conditions of authorisation in piglets and pigs for fattening of all Suidae species.

According to Article 7(1) of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, the Commission forwarded the application to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) as an application under Article 4(1) (authorisation of a feed additive or new use of a feed additive) and under Article 13(3) (modification of the authorisation of a feed additive). The dossier was received on 21 August 2025 and the general information and supporting documentation are available at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA‐Q‐2025‐00489. The particulars and documents in support of the application were considered valid by EFSA as of 18 November 2025.

According to Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, EFSA, after verifying the particulars and documents submitted by the applicant, shall undertake an assessment in order to determine whether the feed additive complies with the conditions laid down in Article 5. EFSA shall deliver an opinion on the safety for the target animals, consumer, user and the environment and on the efficacy of the feed additive consisting of fumonisin esterase (EC 3.1.1.87) produced with K. phaffii NCAIM (P) Y001485, when used under the proposed conditions of use (see Section 3.1.1).

Additional information

The additive is currently authorised for use in feed for piglets and pigs for fattening of all Suidae species (1m04). 3

The additive is a preparation containing fumonisin esterase (EC 3.1.1.87) produced with K. phaffii NCAIM (P) Y001485. EFSA issued one opinion on the safety and efficacy of this product when used in feed for all porcine species (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024).

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The present assessment is based on data submitted by the applicant in the form of a technical dossier 4 in support of the authorisation request for the use of fumonisin esterase (EC 3.1.1.87) produced with K. phaffii NCAIM (P) Y00148 as a feed additive.

The confidential version of the technical dossier was subject to a target consultation of the interested Member States from 21 November 2025 to 21 February 2026; the comments received were considered for the assessment.

In accordance with Article 38 of the Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 5 and taking into account the protection of confidential information and of personal data in accordance with Articles 39 to 39e of the same Regulation, and of the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down practical arrangements concerning transparency and confidentiality, 6 a non‐confidential version of the dossier has been published on Open.EFSA.

According to Article 32c(2) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 and to the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down the practical arrangements on pre‐submission phase and public consultations, EFSA carried out a public consultation on the non‐confidential version of the technical dossier from 03 July to 24 July 2026; one entry was registered but the comments submitted were deemed to be outside the scope of the public consultation and, therefore, were not considered further.

The FEEDAP Panel used the data provided by the applicant together with data from other sources, such as previous risk assessments by EFSA or other expert bodies, peer‐reviewed scientific papers, other scientific reports and experts' (elicitation) knowledge, to deliver the present output.

The European Union Reference Laboratory (EURL) considered that the conclusions and recommendations reached in the previous assessment regarding the methods used for the control of the fumonisin esterase in animal feed are valid and applicable for the current application. 7

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and efficacy of fumonisin esterase (EC 3.1.1.87) produced with K. phaffii NCAIM (P) Y001485 is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 8 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the consumer (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017a), Guidance on the identity, characterisation and conditions of use of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017b), Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the target species (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017c), Guidance on the characterisation of microorganisms used as feed additives or as production organisms (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018), Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the environment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2019), Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the users (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023), Guidance on the assessment of the efficacy of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024) and the EFSA statement on the requirements for whole genome sequence analysis of microorganisms intentionally used in the food chain (EFSA, 2024).

ASSESSMENT

The additive containing fumonisin esterase (EC 3.1.1.87) produced with the genetically modified K. phaffii NCAIM (P) Y001485 is currently authorised as a technological additive (functional group: substances for reduction of the contamination of feed by mycotoxins) in feed for piglets and pigs for fattening of all Suidae species (1m04). The additive aims at reducing the contamination of fumonisin B1 (FB1) and other fumonisins in feed.

The scope of the current application is to extend the use of the additive to all terrestrial species at a proposed use level from 60 to 360 U/kg complete feed, and to increase the maximum proposed level from 60 to 360 U/kg complete feed in porcine species.

Characterisation

The additive is composed of ■■■■■ fumonisin esterase (EC 3.1.1.87) and inactivated Saccharomyces cerevisiae (up to 100%) as a carrier. The additive is currently authorised with a minimum fumonisin esterase activity of 1200 U/g, and the applicant proposes to maintain the same specification. 9

The additive was characterised in the previous opinion of the Panel (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024). The applicant stated that the manufacturing process of the additive has not changed since the last assessment. 10 Therefore, most of the information in the characterisation section is considered to apply to the current assessment. Conditions of use

The additive is currently authorised for use in feed for piglets and pigs for fattening of all Suidae species at a minimum and maximum use level of 60 U/kg complete feed. The applicant is requesting to increase the maximum use level in these species up to 360 U/kg complete feed.

The additive is intended for use in feed for all terrestrial animal species at a proposed minimum use level of 60 U/kg feed and a maximum use level of 360 U/kg complete feed.

Safety

Safety of the production microorganism

The production strain belongs to a species, K. phaffii, that is included in the qualified presumption of safety (QPS) list when used for production purposes (EFSA BIOHAZ Panel, 2026). The production strain was characterised by the FEEDAP Panel in a previous evaluation (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024). It was concluded that the additive does not pose any safety concern for the target species, consumers and the environment with regard to the production strain. In the current assessment, the identity of the strain was confirmed and no new information has been made available that would lead the Panel to reconsider its previous conclusion.

Safety for the target species

Based on the tolerance study in weaned piglets provided, the Panel concluded in its previous opinion that the additive was safe up to 60 U/kg complete feed for piglets and pigs for fattening of all Suidae species. No conclusion could be reached at the maximum level of 360 U/kg feed (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024).

In order to support the safety of the additive for all terrestrial species at the maximum safe level of 360 U/kg, the applicant submitted one new tolerance trial in weaned piglets, one in chickens for fattening and one in dairy cows.

Tolerance trial in chickens for fattening 13

A total of 360 one‐day‐old male chickens for fattening (Ross 308) were distributed in 18 pens (20 chickens per pen), which were randomly allocated to 3 dietary groups (6 replicates per group). Four basal diets (starter from days 1 to 14, grower I from days 15 to 21, grower II from days 22 to 30 and finisher from days 31 to 35) based on soybean meal, wheat and maize were either not supplemented (control) or supplemented with fumonisin esterase at 360 U (1× the maximum use level) or 36,000 U/kg complete feed (100×) (confirmed by analysis, see Table 2). The experimental diets were offered ad libitum as crumbles (starter) or pellets (grower/finisher) for a total of 35 days.

Mortality and health status were daily checked. Chickens were weighed individually at the start and at the end of the trial and feed intake was registered daily. Supplementation of the additive in feed for chickens up to 100× the maximum use level showed no statistically significant differences compared with the control for any of the productive parameters measured.

Conclusions on safety for the target species

Based on the results of the tolerance trials submitted, the Panel concludes that the additive is safe at 360 U/kg complete feed in chickens for fattening, weaned piglets and dairy cows, with a wide margin of safety. This conclusion can be extrapolated to all terrestrial species.

Safety for the consumer, environment and user

The safety aspects regarding the use of this additive in feed, including the safety for the consumers, the user and the environment, have been previously assessed (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024). The Panel concluded that the use of the product as a feed additive raises no concerns for consumers or the environment. Regarding safety for the user, the Panel concluded that the additive is not irritant to the skin and eyes but is a respiratory sensitiser.

The FEEDAP Panel is not aware of any new information that would lead it to reconsider the conclusions drawn previously, except that, given the fact that the additive contains an inactivated microorganism, the additive should be considered a dermal sensitiser. Inhalation and dermal exposures are thus considered a risk.

The Panel considers that the extension of use to all terrestrial species and the increase in the maximum use levels of porcine species would not introduce concerns not already considered in the previous Opinion.

In its previous opinion, the FEEDAP Panel concluded that the additive is efficacious in reducing the contamination of feed by fumonisins when added at the minimum use level of 60 U/kg complete feed for all porcine species (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024).

The applicant provided one trial in chickens for fattening and one in dairy cows to support the efficacy in all terrestrial species. Health status was checked daily. The zootechnical performance of the birds during the whole experimental period was monitored. At the start and at the end of the study (day 14), excreta samples were collected per pen for the analysis of fumonisins (B1/B2/B3) and metabolites (hydrolysed FB1/FB2/FB3, partially hydrolysed FB1/FB2/FB3). On the same days, all animals were killed and blood sampled for the analysis of sphinganine (Sa) and sphingosine (So), and the Sa:So ratio was calculated.

The experimental data were analysed with an analysis of variance, with the pen as experimental unit and the diet as effect. When differences were detected in the biomarkers results, group means were compared using Dunnett's test. The significance level was set at 0.05.

No bird died during the experiment. No differences between diets were observed in body weight, feed intake or feed‐to‐gain ratio during the experimental period. At the end of the trial, birds supplemented with the additive at 60 U/kg complete feed (CS) showed a significantly lower serum Sa:So ratio than the CU group. The excreta content of FB1 and FB2 was lower, and the hydrolysed and partially hydrolysed fumonisin metabolites were higher, in CS compared with CU.

Efficacy trial in dairy cows 21

A total of 24 lactating Holstein‐Friesian cows (1–3 parities, 28 days in milk; initial body weight approximately 700 kg) were individually housed in mobile fences and randomly allocated to three experimental groups (8 replicates per group). The animals were fed a total mixed ration (TMR) composed of a mixture of concentrate 22 (■■■■■) and forage 23 (■■■■■). The concentrate included in the TMR was either not supplemented with the additive 24 and not contaminated (UU), not supplemented and naturally contaminated 25 with < 50 mg of fumonisins (FB1 + FB2)/kg feed (CU), or contaminated (< 50 mg of fumonisins/kg feed) and supplemented with fumonisin esterase to provide 60 U/kg feed. The level of fumonisins and the enzyme activity in the feed were analytically confirmed (see Table 6). The experimental diets were offered ad libitum for 14 days.

Health status was monitored daily. Cows were weighed at the start and end of the trial, and feed intake and milk production were recorded daily. Milk samples were analysed weekly for fat, lactose, protein, urea and somatic cell content. The average daily feed intake, milk yield and fat‐corrected milk yield were calculated for the whole period. At the start (day 1) and end (day 14) of the trial, blood samples were collected for the analysis of Sa and So, and the Sa:So ratio was calculated. On the same days, faecal samples were collected per pen for the analysis of fumonisins (B1/B2/B3) and metabolites (hydrolysed FB1/FB2/FB3; partially hydrolysed FB1/FB2/FB3).

The experimental data were subjected to analysis of variance, with the pen as experimental unit and the diet as effect. When differences were detected in the biomarkers results, group means were compared using Dunnett's test. The significance level was set at 0.05.

No animal died during the experiment and the health status was optimal. No differences between diets were observed in productive performance or milk quality during the experimental period. At the end of the trial, cows supplemented with the additive at 60 U/kg complete feed showed a significantly lower serum Sa:So ratio than the CU group. The excreta content of FB1 and FB2 was lower, and the hydrolysed and partially hydrolysed fumonisin metabolites were higher, in CS compared with CU.

Conclusions on efficacy

Based on the data provided in the current and previous applications, which includes data in pigs, chickens for fattening, and cows, the Panel concludes that the additive has the potential to be efficacious in all terrestrial species when added to feed at 60 U/kg complete feed.

Post‐market monitoring

The FEEDAP Panel considers that there is no need for specific requirements for a post‐market monitoring plan other than those established in the Feed Hygiene Regulation 26 and Good Manufacturing Practice.

CONCLUSIONS

The use of fumonisin esterase produced with K. phaffii NCAIM (P) Y001485 in all terrestrial species at a maximum use level of 360 U/kg complete feed is safe for the target species, consumers and the environment.

Regarding user safety, the additive is not irritant to skin or eyes but should be considered a dermal and respiratory sensitiser. Exposure of users by inhalatory and dermal routes is considered a risk.

The additive has the potential to be efficacious in all terrestrial species when added to feed at 60 U/kg complete feed.

Bone Health in Breast Cancer Patients: Comparative Evaluation Using Dual Energy X-Ray Absorptiometry Scan and High-Resolution Peripheral Quantitative Computed Tomography
Authors: Sunaina Kumari, Sabaretnam Mayilvaganan, Sushil Gupta, Manisha Kakaji, Gyan Chand, Anjali Mishra, Gaurav Agarwal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOsteoporosis is highly prevalent among Indian women and is further exacerbated by cancer-directed therapies such as chemotherapy, hormonal therapy, and radiotherapy. Patients with breast cancer are particularly susceptible to bone loss. While dual energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) remains the gold standard, it does not assess bone microarchitecture. High-resolution peripheral quantitative computed tomography (HR-pQCT) may provide additional insights.MethodsThis cross-sectional pilot study was conducted between May and December 2024 at a tertiary care center and included 54 women with breast cancer without bone metastases. Demographic, clinical, and treatment data were recorded. All patients underwent DXA and HR-pQCT scans to assess bone density and microarchitecture. Statistical analysis included subgroup comparisons and concordance testing.ResultsMean age was 55.8±11 years, and 65% were postmenopausal. Estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 positivity rates were 76%, 57%, and 28%, respectively. Osteoporosis was found in 38.2% and osteopenia in 61.8% by DXA. HR-pQCT revealed lower volumetric bone density (0.59±0.07 g/cm3) compared to DXA (0.84±0.16 g/cm3, P<0.01). Microarchitectural deficits were significant in postmenopausal women, chemotherapy recipients, and those on letrozole. Lin’s concordance correlation indicated poor agreement between HR-pQCT and DXA (<0.9).ConclusionsHR-pQCT was superior to DXA in detecting microarchitectural deterioration in women with breast cancer. This pilot study emphasizes the need for larger prospective studies in Indian cohorts.Keywords: Bone microarchitecture, Bone mineral density, HR-pQCT, Osteoporosis

GRAPHICAL ABSTRACT

INTRODUCTION

Osteoporosis is a growing public health concern in India, particularly among postmenopausal women, with a high prevalence reported in major epidemiological studies.[1] Women diagnosed with breast cancer are at an even greater risk of accelerated bone loss due to cancer-directed therapies. Chemotherapy may induce premature ovarian failure and rapid estrogen depletion,[2] while aromatase inhibitors (AIs) significantly increase bone resorption in postmenopausal women. [3–5] In addition, corticosteroids impair osteoblast function and radiotherapy can cause localized bone damage.[6,7] Reduced mobility, nutritional deficiencies, and cancer-related metabolic changes further exacerbate skeletal deterioration.

Dual energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) remains the standard method for diagnosing osteoporosis and assessing fracture risk.[8] However, DXA provides only areal bone mineral density (aBMD) and cannot assess trabecular or cortical microarchitecture, which is a crucial determinant of bone strength. High-resolution peripheral quantitative computed tomography (HR-pQCT) is a novel imaging modality that evaluates bone structure in three dimensions and separately quantifies cortical and trabecular compartments with high precision.[9–11] This technique also allows volumetric BMD (vBMD) assessment, cortical porosity measurement, and geometric evaluation at peripheral skeletal sites.

The availability of HR-pQCT in India remains limited. To date, there is no published study from the Indian subcontinent comparing DXA and HR-pQCT among women with breast cancer. Understanding their relationship and identifying treatment subgroups at higher skeletal risk may help optimize screening and guide earlier therapeutic interventions.

This pilot study aimed to compare DXA with HR-pQCT in evaluating bone health in women with breast cancer and to analyze the impact of menopausal status, cancer stage, chemotherapy, and AI therapy on bone microarchitecture.

METHODS

1. Study design and participants

This cross-sectional pilot study was conducted at a tertiary care referral center in India between May and December 2024. A total of 68 consecutive female breast cancer patients were screened from outpatient and inpatient services. Fourteen were excluded (bone metastasis, chronic medical conditions affecting bone, or incomplete imaging), and 54 participants were included in the final analysis (Fig. 1).

6. Bone microarchitecture assessment (HR-pQCT)

HR-pQCT was performed on the non-dominant distal radius and tibia using XtremeCT II (Scanco Medical, Bruttisellen, Switzerland). Parameters included:

• vBMD: total vBMD, trabecular vBMD, cortical vBMD

• vBMD: total vBMD, trabecular vBMD, cortical vBMD

• Geometry: total area, cortical area, trabecular area

• Microstructure: trabecular number, trabecular thickness, bone volume to total volume ratio (BV/TV)

• Cortical properties: cortical thickness, cortical porosity

• Cortical properties: cortical thickness, cortical porosity

Standard manufacturer-recommended scanning and reconstruction protocols were followed.

7. Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 21.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables were expressed as mean±standard deviation. Group comparisons were performed using t-tests or χ2 tests as appropriate.

Multivariate linear regression was used to determine independent predictors of microarchitectural impairment (age, BMI, menopausal status, cancer stage, chemotherapy, AI therapy). Lin’s concordance correlation coefficient (CCC) was calculated to assess agreement between DXA and HR-pQCT measurements. A P value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

8. Ethical approval

The study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC No. 2024-185-PDCC-EXP-60). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

DISCUSSION

This study demonstrates a high prevalence of compromised bone health in Indian women with breast cancer, with more than one-third classified as osteoporotic and the remainder osteopenic based on DXA criteria. Importantly, HR-pQCT revealed substantial microarchitectural deterioration even among patients whose DXA values remained within the osteopenic range. This discrepancy highlights that areal BMD alone does not fully reflect the impact of breast cancer treatment on bone integrity.

1. HR-pQCT detects early cortical and trabecular decline

Our findings show that HR-pQCT consistently detected:

• Reduced cortical thickness

• Reduced cortical thickness

• Lower trabecular number and area

• Lower trabecular number and area

• Increased cortical porosity

• Increased cortical porosity

These microstructural features are known to independently increase skeletal fragility and fracture risk. Cortical thinning has been strongly associated with hip fractures, whereas increased cortical porosity adversely affects mechanical competence even when DXA-based BMD appears preserved. These observations suggest that microarchitectural decline begins earlier and is more pronounced than what can be inferred from DXA alone.

2. Impact of breast cancer therapies on bone microstructure

The detrimental skeletal effects of breast cancer treatments are multifactorial:

The presence of trabecular deterioration in stage III disease further supports tumor- and treatment-grade severity contributing to skeletal decline.

We intentionally avoided direct numerical comparison between DXA and HR-pQCT due to fundamental methodological differences:

5. Interpretation of chemotherapy subgroup finding

The observation of better cortical parameters in the chemotherapy group should not be viewed as a protective effect. Rather, the small non-chemotherapy cohort consisted mainly of older, low-stage patients who inherently carried worse baseline bone health — a bias we have openly acknowledged. This reinforces the need for adequately powered subgroup trials.

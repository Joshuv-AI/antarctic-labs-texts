Block Coordinate Descent Methods for Structured Nonconvex Optimization with Nonseparable Constraints: Optimality Conditions and Global Convergence Thanks: Corresponding author.
Zhijie Yuan

Abstract
Coordinate descent algorithms are widely used in machine learning and large-scale data analysis due to their strong optimality guarantees and impressive empirical performance in solving nonconvex problems. In this work, we introduce Block Coordinate Descent (BCD) method for structured nonconvex optimization with nonseparable constraints. Unlike traditional large-scale Coordinate Descent (CD) approaches, we do not assume the constraints are separable. Instead, we account for the possibility of nonlinear coupling among them. By leveraging the inherent problem structure, we propose new CD methods to tackle this specific challenge. Under the relatively mild condition of locally bounded non-convexity, we demonstrate that achieving coordinate-wise stationary points offer a stronger optimality criterion compared to standard critical points. Furthermore, under the Luo-Tseng error bound conditions, our BCD methods exhibit Q-linear convergence to coordinate-wise stationary points or critical points. To demonstrate the practical utility of our methods, we apply them to various machine learning and signal processing models. We also provide the geometry analysis for the models. Experiments on real-world data consistently demonstrate the superior objective values of our approaches compared to existing methods.

2.1
Sparse Index Tracking (SIT)

Sparse index tracking task aims to address the challenges of asset selection and capital allocation simultaneously
(
Benidis et al., 2017
)
. Given a return matrix
{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
for
n
stocks and the index return
{\mathbf{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
during
m
days,
{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a decision vector, each component indicating the proportion of the investment budget. The task tries to find the optimal investment proportions
{\mathbf{x}}
that minimize the difference between the portfolio return
{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{x}}
and the index return
{\mathbf{y}}
. An important contribution to this field is the introduction of
\ell_{0}
norm constraint-based sparse index optimization, which enables direct control over sparsity in portfolio construction:
\min_{{\mathbf{x}}}\,\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\|_{2}^{2},\ s.t.\ \|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{0}\leq s,{\mathbf{x}}\geq 0,\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{1}=1
. The
\ell_{0}
norm constraint
\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{0}
can be represented as a Difference of Convex (DC) function:
\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{1}-\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{[s]}
. While both constraints describe the same feasible set, it is important to note that
\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{1}
and
\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{[s]}
are continuous functions with efficiently computable subgradients
(
Gotoh et al., 2018
)
. The sparse index tracking problem above can be reformulated as follows:
\min_{{\mathbf{x}}}\,\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda(\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{1}-\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{[s]}),\ s.t.\ {\mathbf{x}}\geq 0,\,\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{1}=1
, where
\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{[s]}
returns the sum of top
s
large elements in
{\mathbf{x}}
and
\lambda>0
is a constant. Since
\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{1}=1
and
{\mathbf{x}}\geq 0
, we seek to address the subsequent problem:

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{x}}}\,\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\|_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{I}_{\geq 0}({\mathbf{x}})-\lambda\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{[s]},\ s.t.\ \|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{1}=1,

(2)

where
\mathcal{I}_{\geq 0}({\mathbf{x}})
is the indicator function on the non-negative constraint
{\mathbf{x}}\geq 0
. Under this setting, Problem (
2
) coincides with framework (
1
) with
f({\mathbf{x}})=\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\|_{2}^{2}
,
h({\mathbf{x}})=\mathcal{I}_{\geq 0}({\mathbf{x}})
,
g({\mathbf{x}})=-\lambda\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{[s]}
and
u({\mathbf{x}})=\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{1}-1
.

2.2
Non-negative Sparse PCA (NNSPCA)

Non-negative sparse PCA (NNSPCA) extends the conventional PCA by imposing constraints of non-negativity and sparsity on the principal components. NNSPCA ensures that the principal components are sparse and do not include many non-zero loadings. This makes the principal components easier to interpret and understand
(
Asteris et al., 2014
;
Drikvandi & Lawal, 2023
)
. Given the matrix
{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, the naive NNSPCA model can be presented as:
\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,-\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2},\ s.t.\ \|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{0}\leq s,{\mathbf{x}}\geq 0,\|{\mathbf{x}}\|^{2}_{2}=1

\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{0}\leq s\Leftrightarrow\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{1}=\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{[s]}

\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,-\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda(\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{1}-\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{[s]}),\ s.t.\ {\mathbf{x}}\geq 0,\|{\mathbf{x}}\|^{2}_{2}=1
, where
\lambda>0
is a constant.
Since
{\mathbf{x}}\geq 0
and
\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}=1
, we focus on following NNSPCA problem:

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\hat{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{x}}+\mathcal{I}_{\geq 0}({\mathbf{x}})+\lambda({\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\mathbf{1}_{n}-\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{[s]}),\ s.t.\ \|{\mathbf{x}}\|^{2}_{2}=1,

(3)

where
\hat{\mathbf{Q}}=-{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}{\mathbf{A}}+\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{n}\succeq 0
with
\gamma
being sufficiently large. Therefore, Problem (
3
) fits to framework (
1
) with
f({\mathbf{x}})=\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\hat{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{x}}
,
h({\mathbf{x}})=\mathcal{I}_{\geq 0}({\mathbf{x}})+\lambda{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\mathbf{1}_{n}
,
g({\mathbf{x}})=-\lambda\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{[s]}
and
u({\mathbf{x}})=\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}-1
.

2.3
DC Penalized Binary (DCPB) Optimization

Given a matrix
{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and a vector
{\mathbf{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the binary optimization problem can be formulated as follows when

has a binary structure:
\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\|_{2}^{2},\ s.t.\ {\mathbf{x}}\in\{-1,+1\}^{n},\ {\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\textbf{1}=c

\{{\mathbf{x}}|-1\leq{\mathbf{x}}\leq 1,\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}=n\}

. The following approximate binary optimization problem:

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda(n-\|{\mathbf{x}}\|^{2}_{2}),\ s.t.\ -1\leq{\mathbf{x}}\leq 1,\ {\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\textbf{1}=c

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\mathbf{p}}^{\top}{\mathbf{x}}+\mathcal{I}_{[-1,1]}({\mathbf{x}})-\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\hat{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{x}},\ s.t.\ {\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\textbf{1}=c,

(4)

where
{\mathbf{p}}=-{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}
,
\lambda>0
is a given constant, and
\hat{\mathbf{Q}}=2\lambda{\mathbf{I}}_{n}-{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}{\mathbf{A}}\succeq 0
with
\lambda
being sufficiently large. With
f({\mathbf{x}})={\mathbf{p}}^{\top}{\mathbf{x}}
,
h({\mathbf{x}})=\mathcal{I}_{[-1,1]}({\mathbf{x}})
,
g({\mathbf{x}})=-\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\hat{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{x}}
and
u({\mathbf{x}})={\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\textbf{1}-c

Using an other variational reformulation of the binary constraint

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda(\sqrt{n}-\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}),\,s.t\,-1\leq{\mathbf{x}}\leq 1,\ {\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\textbf{1}=c

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\|_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{I}_{[-1,1]}({\mathbf{x}})-\lambda\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2},\ s.t.\ {\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\textbf{1}=c,

(5)

1

f({\mathbf{x}})=\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\|_{2}^{2}
,
h({\mathbf{x}})=\mathcal{I}_{[-1,1]}({\mathbf{x}})
,
g({\mathbf{x}})=-\lambda\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}

u({\mathbf{x}})={\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\textbf{1}-c

3
Related Work

Nonconvex optimization has emerged as a critical tool of study in various fields. It proposes the challenge due to its NP-hard nature. One common strategy is convex relaxation, which approximates the original problem and provides a bound on the optimal value to assess the suboptimality of candidate solutions
(
Eltved, 2021
)
. Direct approaches is another strategy to solve the nonconvex optimization, often yielding better performance than relaxation-based techniques. These direct methods are particularly important in machine learning and communication systems, including methods such as projected gradient descent, proximal gradient method, and coordinate descent
(
Hauswirth et al., 2016
;
Jain et al., 2014
;
Yuan et al., 2020
)
. In this work, we introduce several related algorithms to solve Problem (
1
).

3.1
Projected Subgradient Method (PSG)

PSG is one of the extension on subgradient method. It first lets
{\mathbf{x}}^{t}
take a step opposite the subgradient direction then provides a solution
{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}
in feasible set
\Omega
with a projection function
(
Alber et al., 1998
;
Maingé, 2008
;
Beck & Teboulle, 2003
)
. It generates a sequence
\{{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\}
as:

{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}=P_{\Omega}({\mathbf{x}}^{t}-\alpha_{t}\textbf{g}^{t}),

while
\textbf{g}^{t}
is the subgradient of
F(\cdot)
at
{\mathbf{x}}^{t}
and
\alpha_{t}
is the step size at
t
iteration.

3.2
Multi-Stage Convex Relaxation (MSCR)

MSCR derives a general multi-stage convex relaxation for solving learning formulations with nonconvex regularization
(
Zhang, 2010
;
Zhang, 2013
)
. With
K
representing the number of outer iterations, MSCR minimizes an upper bound the original function
K
times. As a result, the computational cost of MSCR could be expensive for large-scale problems.
{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}
is updated by
K
times as follows:
{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}=\text{arg}\min_{{\mathbf{x}}}f({\mathbf{x}})+h({\mathbf{x}})+\langle{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{t},\partial g({\mathbf{x}}^{t})\rangle
.

3.3
Proximal DC Algorithm (PDCA)

PDCA is a powerful tool for addressing sparse optimization problems
(
Gotoh et al., 2018
;
Gong et al., 2013
)
. To avoid the computational issue of MSCR, PDCA exploits the structure of
f(\cdot)
and the sequence of
\{{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\}
is generated as follows:
{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}=\text{arg}\min_{{\mathbf{x}}}\mathcal{S}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{x}}^{t})+h({\mathbf{x}})+\langle{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{t},\partial g({\mathbf{x}}^{t})\rangle
, where
\mathcal{S}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{x}}^{t})\triangleq f({\mathbf{x}}^{t})+\langle\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}),{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\rangle+\tfrac{L}{2}\|{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\|^{2}_{2}
is a Lipschitz gradient surrogate and
L
is the Lipschitz constant of
\nabla f(\cdot)
.

4
The Proposed Block Coordinate Descent Methods

In this section, we introduce two novel BCD methods specifically designed to address structured nonconvex optimization problems under nonseparable constraints
u({\mathbf{x}})=0
. We first propose the BCD-g method for solving the subproblem (
6
) globally. The second BCD method offers a local solution for the subproblem (
6
) denoted as BCD-l-
k
, where
k
represents the number of the block.

The BCD methods concentrate on only updating a single block exclusively in each iteration with the remaining blocks being fixed. Therefore, the entire problem is decomposed into numerous subproblems which is converted into a lower-dimensional minimization problem. To ensure feasibility with the constraint
u({\mathbf{x}})=0
, it is necessary to update at least two variables in each iteration. In each iteration, the variables in
{\mathbf{x}}
are divided into two sets:
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
and
\mathbf{B}^{c}\in\mathbb{N}^{n-k}
, with
\mathbf{B}
designated as the working set. The objective function
F({\mathbf{x}})
is minimized with respect to the working set
\mathbf{B}
, while the variables in
\mathbf{B}^{c}
remain unchanged. We first define
\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{B}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n\times k}
and
\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{B}^{c}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n\times(n-k)}
as:

[\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{B}}]_{ji}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,&&\mathbf{B}_{i}=j;\\
&0,&&\text{else}.\end{aligned}\right.,\ [\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{B}^{c}}]_{ji}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,&&\mathbf{B}^{c}_{i}=j;\\
&0,&&\text{else}.\end{aligned}\right.
.
Hence, for any
{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, we have:
{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{B}}=\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{B}}^{\top}{\mathbf{x}}
,
{\mathbf{x}}=(\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{B}^{c}}\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{B}^{c}}^{\top}+\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{B}}\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{B}}^{\top}){\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{B}}+\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{B}^{c}}{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{B}^{c}}
and
\mathbf{Q}_{\mathbf{B}\mathbf{B}}=\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{B}}^{\top}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{B}}
. Then we define
{\mathbf{r}}^{t}\triangleq\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}}{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}}
. Given the working set
\mathbf{B}^{t}
, Problem (
1
) is equivalent to perform the following step size searching problem:
\bar{{\mathbf{d}}}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}}\in\arg\min_{{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}}}\,f({\mathbf{x}}^{t}+{\mathbf{r}}^{t})+h({\mathbf{x}}^{t}+{\mathbf{r}}^{t})+g({\mathbf{x}}^{t}+{\mathbf{r}}^{t}),\ s.t.\ {\mathbf{x}}^{t}+{\mathbf{r}}^{t}\in\Omega
. This problem aims at searching the optimal step size
\bar{{\mathbf{d}}}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}}
in
t
iteration along the coordinates in working set
\mathbf{B}^{t}
. Finally
{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}
is updated by
{\mathbf{x}}^{t}+\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}}\bar{{\mathbf{d}}}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}}
.

We now discuss how to determine the optimal step size
\bar{{\mathbf{d}}}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}}
. With the benefits of Lipschitz continuity of its gradient
\nabla f({\mathbf{x}})
:
\|\nabla f({\mathbf{x}})-\nabla f({\mathbf{y}})\|\leq L\|{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\|
, we have:
f({\mathbf{x}}+\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}})\leq\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{B}}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}})
, where
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{B}}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}})\triangleq f({\mathbf{x}})+\langle\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}},\nabla f({\mathbf{x}})\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}}\|_{\mathbf{Q}_{\mathbf{B}\mathbf{B}}}^{2}
. With the proximal parameter
\theta\geq 0
, Problem (
1
) can be addressed using the BCD-g method and reformulated as follows:

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}}}\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}})\triangleq\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}}({\mathbf{x}}^{t},{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}})+h({\mathbf{x}}^{t}+{\mathbf{r}}^{t})+g({\mathbf{x}}^{t}+{\mathbf{r}}^{t})+\tfrac{\theta}{2}\|{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}}\|_{2}^{2},\ s.t.\ {\mathbf{x}}^{t}+{\mathbf{r}}^{t}\in\Omega.

(6)

Now we define
g({\mathbf{x}}+\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}})\leq\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{B}}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}})\triangleq g({\mathbf{x}})+\langle\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}},\partial g({\mathbf{x}})\rangle
. The function
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{B}}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}})
can be relaxed as a convex function
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{B}}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}})
. Then we can solve the subproblem (
6
) locally using the BCD-l-
k
method by solving following problem:

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}}}\,\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}})\triangleq\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}}({\mathbf{x}}^{t},{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}})+h({\mathbf{x}}^{t}+{\mathbf{r}}^{t})+\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}}({\mathbf{x}}^{t},{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}})+\tfrac{\theta}{2}\|{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}^{t}}\|_{2}^{2},\ s.t.\ {\mathbf{x}}^{t}+{\mathbf{r}}^{t}\in\Omega.

(7)

\bullet

Breakpoint searching method.
The breakpoint searching method systematically explores the solution space to identify all breakpoints by exhaustively searching over a predefined range of candidate points. The subproblems (
6
) and (
7
) involve
k
unknown decision variables (
{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}_{1}},{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}_{2}},...,{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}_{k}}
) that need to be solved. Generally, we divide each variable into two possible states in (
6
) and (
7
). In other words, for a given state of
{\mathbf{d}}_{\mathbf{B}}
, the subproblem reduces to solving
2^{k}
simple system. We then systematically traverse the entire binary tree, enumerating all potential candidate solutions, and select the one that minimizes the objective function the most as optimal solution. By thoroughly examining a wide range of candidate points, the algorithm can effectively capture the complex and nonconvex nature of the optimization problem. Although the exhaustive breakpoint searching method can be computationally demanding, it ensures a thorough exploration of the solution space and leads to robust and reliable results. Therefore, combining the breakpoint searching strategy, BCD-g and BCD-l-
k
are enable to provide better solutions in subproblems (
6
) and (
7
) even when
u({\mathbf{x}})
is nonseparable.

\bullet

Working set selection strategy.
There are mainly three strategies to determine which coordinate to update during coordinate descent in literature.
(i) Cyclic order strategy.
It runs all coordinates in cyclic order
\{1,2,...,k\}\rightarrow\{n-k+1,...,n\}\rightarrow\{n-k+2,...,1\}
. This approach ensures that each coordinate is updated during the proposed method.
(ii) Random sampling strategy.
In this strategy, a set of
k
indices are randomly and uniformly selected to form a working set
\mathbf{B}
for updating. This method introduces an element of randomness into the selection process, which can help in avoiding local minima.
(iii) Greedy strategy.
This approach involves selecting the coordinates that lead to the least objective value in the current iteration. The greedy strategy aims to make the most significant progress towards the optimal solution in each iteration. Two semi-greedy strategies for working set selection are discussed in Section
8

Optimality Analysis

In this section, we focus on two key optimality conditions: coordinate-wise stationary optimality condition and critical point condition. We discuss the relationship between these two conditions and also provide the theoretical analysis. It is a fundamental aspect of the optimality hierarchy for solving Problem (
1
). Optimal point, coordinate-wise point and critical point are denoted as
\bar{{\mathbf{x}}}
,
\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}
and
\breve{{\mathbf{x}}}

1
), which is formulated as:
\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in\Omega}\ F({\mathbf{x}})=f({\mathbf{x}})+h({\mathbf{x}})+g({\mathbf{x}})
.

Critical point condition is the most general form of optimality, where a solution is considered optimal if the gradient of the function is zero within the feasible set. This criterion frequently serves as a standard for evaluating the effectiveness of optimization algorithms. We define
\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}(\cdot)
is the indicator function of the feasible set
\Omega
. The critical point is also defined as follows
(
Toland, 1979
)
.

Definition 5.1
.

(Critical Point)

\breve{{\mathbf{x}}}
is a critical point if the following holds:
\mathbf{0}_{n}\in\nabla f(\breve{{\mathbf{x}}})+\partial h(\breve{{\mathbf{x}}})+\partial g(\breve{{\mathbf{x}}})+\partial\mathcal{I}_{\Omega}(\breve{{\mathbf{x}}})
.

Coordinate-wise stationary point condition is described as a solution where the minimum value of a specific function
\mathcal{M}_{\texttt{{B}}}(\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}},{\mathbf{d}}_{\texttt{{B}}})
is zero for any working set
B
. In other word, we can not improve the objective function value for

\texttt{{B}}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}

Yuan, 2023

(

) coordinate method, the definition of a coordinate-wise stationary point is as follows.

Definition 5.2

(Coordinate-Wise Stationary Point, CWS-point)

For any working set
\texttt{{B}}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
, a solution
\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}

\mathcal{M}_{\texttt{{B}}}(\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}},\mathbf{0}_{k})=\underset{{\mathbf{d}}_{\texttt{{B}}}}{\min}\ \mathcal{M}_{\texttt{{B}}}(\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}},{\mathbf{d}}_{\texttt{{B}}})

We recap the non-negative sparse PCA problem (

\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\hat{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{x}}+\mathcal{I}_{\geq 0}({\mathbf{x}})+\lambda({\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\mathbf{1}_{n}-\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{[s]},\ s.t.\ \|{\mathbf{x}}\|^{2}_{2}=1

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{d}}_{i},{\mathbf{d}}_{j}}\mathcal{J}({\mathbf{d}}_{i},{\mathbf{d}}_{j})\triangleq\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{r}}\|^{2}_{\bar{\mathbf{Q}}}+\langle(\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}^{t})+\lambda\mathbf{1}_{n}),{\mathbf{r}}\rangle-\lambda\|{\mathbf{x}}^{t}+{\mathbf{r}}\|_{[s]},

\displaystyle\ s.t.\ ({\mathbf{d}}_{i}+{\mathbf{x}}^{t}_{i})^{2}+({\mathbf{d}}_{j}+{\mathbf{x}}^{t}_{j})^{2}=v^{2},

\displaystyle\min_{\alpha\in[0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}]}\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)=a\cos^{2}(\alpha)+b\sin(\alpha)+c\cos(\alpha)+d\sin(\alpha)\cos(\alpha)-\lambda\|{\mathbf{x}}^{t}+{\mathbf{r}}\|_{[s]},

\bullet

(iii)

(iv)

When
{\mathbf{x}}^{t}_{i}+{\mathbf{r}}_{i}

(v)

While
{\mathbf{d}}_{i}+{\mathbf{x}}^{t}_{i}\geq 0
and
{\mathbf{d}}_{j}+{\mathbf{x}}^{t}_{j}\geq 0

\alpha_{5}
=
\arcsin(0)=0

To solve the subproblem
\min_{\alpha}\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)
in each first four situations (situation (

14

\min_{\alpha\in[0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}]}\mathcal{P}(\alpha)\triangleq a\cos^{2}(\alpha)+\hat{b}\sin(\alpha)+\hat{c}\cos(\alpha)+d\sin(\alpha)\cos(\alpha)

\hat{b}\triangleq b
or
b-\lambda v
and
\hat{c}\triangleq c
or
c-\lambda v
according to the situation discussed above. Using the substitution

\min_{\beta\geq 0}\mathcal{P}(\beta)\triangleq\tfrac{a+d\beta}{1+\beta^{2}}+\tfrac{\hat{c}+\hat{b}\beta}{\sqrt{1+\beta^{2}}}
. This substitution is based on the trigonometric identities that
\cos(\alpha)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+\tan^{2}(\alpha)}}
and
\sin(\alpha)=\tfrac{\tan(\alpha)}{\sqrt{1+\tan^{2}(\alpha)}}

\tfrac{[d\beta^{o}-2\beta(a+d\beta)]+\sqrt{\beta^{o}}[\hat{b}\beta^{o}-\beta(\hat{c}+\hat{b}\beta)]}{\beta^{o}}=0\Rightarrow d\beta^{o}-2\beta(a+d\beta)\overset{\text{①}}{=}(\hat{c}\beta-\hat{b})\sqrt{\beta^{o}}\Rightarrow[d\beta^{o}-2\beta(a+d\beta)]^{2}\overset{\text{②}}{=}(\hat{c}\beta-\hat{b})^{2}\beta^{o}\Rightarrow c_{4}\beta^{4}+c_{3}\beta^{3}+c_{2}\beta^{2}+c_{1}\beta+c_{0}\overset{\text{③}}{=}0

\beta^{o}\triangleq 1+\beta^{2}

②

c_{3}=4ad+2\hat{b}\hat{c}

c_{2}=4a^{2}-2d^{2}-\hat{b}^{2}-\hat{c}^{2}

c_{1}=2\hat{b}\hat{c}-4ad

c_{0}=d^{2}-\hat{b}^{2}

By applying Lodovico Ferrari’s method to solve the equation analytically, we obtain all its real non-negative roots
\{\mathbb{P}_{2}(\beta_{1}),\mathbb{P}_{2}(\beta_{2}),...,\mathbb{P}_{2}(\beta_{j})\}
, where
1\leq j\leq 4
and
\mathbb{P}_{2}(t)\triangleq\max(0,t)
. Then we select the root that leads the smallest objective value of
\mathcal{P}(\alpha)

with

in each first four situation is determined as:
\alpha_{i}\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\alpha}\mathcal{P}(\alpha),~\text{where}\ \alpha\in\{\arctan(\mathbb{P}_{2}(\beta_{1})),\arctan(\mathbb{P}_{2}(\beta_{2})),...,\arctan(\mathbb{P}_{2}(\beta_{j}))\}

Finally, the optimal solution

to Problem (

\bar{\alpha}\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\alpha}\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)

Global Solutions for DC Penalized Binary Optimization When

We review the DC penalized binary optimization problem (
4

\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\mathbf{p}}^{\top}{\mathbf{x}}+\mathcal{I}_{[-1,1]}({\mathbf{x}})-\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\hat{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{x}},\ s.t.\ {\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\textbf{1}=c

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{d}}_{i},{\mathbf{d}}_{j}}\mathcal{J}({\mathbf{d}}_{i},{\mathbf{d}}_{j})\triangleq\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{r}}\|^{2}_{\bar{\mathbf{Q}}}+\langle\nabla\hat{f}({\mathbf{x}}^{t}),{\mathbf{r}}\rangle,\ s.t.\ {\mathbf{d}}_{i}+{\mathbf{d}}_{j}=0,\ l_{1}\leq{\mathbf{d}}_{i}\leq u_{1},\ l_{2}\leq{\mathbf{d}}_{j}\leq u_{2},

and

\{l,u,\mathbb{P}_{3}(-\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha})\}

\mathbb{P}_{3}(t)\triangleq\max(l,\min(t,u))

\mathcal{O}(1)
. Then we pick one of the breakpoints leading
\mathcal{P}(\eta)
the least as
\bar{\eta}^{t}
. Finally,
{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}
is generated as
{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}={\mathbf{x}}^{t}+{\mathbf{d}}_{i}e_{i}+{\mathbf{d}}_{j}e_{j}
, with
{\mathbf{d}}_{i}=\bar{\eta}^{t}
and
{\mathbf{d}}_{j}=-\bar{\eta}^{t}
.

We now discuss another variational reformulation of DC penalized binary optimization problem (
5

\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\|_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{I}_{[-1,1]}({\mathbf{x}})-\lambda\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2},\ s.t.\ {\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\textbf{1}=c

\mathcal{G}({\mathbf{d}}_{i},{\mathbf{d}}_{j})\triangleq\sqrt{\|{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\|_{2}^{2}+{\mathbf{d}}_{i}^{2}+2{\mathbf{d}}_{i}{\mathbf{x}}^{t}_{i}+{\mathbf{d}}_{j}^{2}+2{\mathbf{d}}_{j}{\mathbf{x}}^{t}_{j}}
. Then we have following problem:

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{d}}_{i},{\mathbf{d}}_{j}}\mathcal{J}({\mathbf{d}}_{i},{\mathbf{d}}_{j})\triangleq\mathcal{H}({\mathbf{d}}_{i},{\mathbf{d}}_{j})-\lambda\mathcal{G}({\mathbf{d}}_{i},{\mathbf{d}}_{j}),\ s.t.\ {\mathbf{d}}_{i}+{\mathbf{d}}_{j}=0,\ l_{1}\leq{\mathbf{d}}_{i}\leq u_{1},\ l_{2}\leq{\mathbf{d}}_{j}\leq u_{2},

(17)

where
l_{1}=-1-{\mathbf{x}}^{t}_{i}
,
l_{2}=-1-{\mathbf{x}}^{t}_{j}
,
u_{1}=1-{\mathbf{x}}^{t}_{i}
and
u_{2}=1-{\mathbf{x}}^{t}_{j}

{\mathbf{d}}_{i}+{\mathbf{d}}_{j}=0
, we convert Problem (
17

\displaystyle\min_{\eta}\mathcal{P}(\eta)\triangleq\mathcal{J}(\eta,-\eta)=\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha\eta^{2}+\beta\eta-\lambda\sqrt{\|{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\|_{2}^{2}+2\eta^{2}+2{\mathbf{x}}^{t}_{i}\eta-2{\mathbf{x}}^{t}_{j}\eta},\ s.t.\ l\leq\eta\leq u,

(18)

where
\alpha=\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{ii}+\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{jj}-2\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{ij}
,
\beta=\nabla_{i}f({\mathbf{x}}^{t})-\nabla_{j}f({\mathbf{x}}^{t})
,
l=\max(l_{1},-u_{2})
and
u=\min(u_{1},-l_{2})
.

\bullet

Breakpoint Searching Strategy.
We outline all breakpoints for Problem (
18
) during 3 situations presented as following.

(i)

Since
l\leq\eta\leq u
, the first and second breakpoints are
\eta_{1}=l
and
\eta_{2}=u
.

(ii)

When
\|{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\|_{2}^{2}+2\eta^{2}+2{\mathbf{x}}^{t}_{i}\eta-2{\mathbf{x}}^{t}_{j}\eta=0

\eta_{3}=-\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}

(iii)

When

\nabla\mathcal{P}(\eta)=0
. This leads to the equation:
\alpha\eta+\beta=\lambda(4\eta+2{\mathbf{x}}^{t}_{i}-2{\mathbf{x}}^{t}_{j})\cdot(\|{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\|_{2}^{2}+2\eta^{2}+2{\mathbf{x}}^{t}_{i}\eta-2{\mathbf{x}}^{t}_{j}\eta)^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}

Therefore, there are at most (2 + 1 + 4) breakpoints. Then

With given a working set

8
Semi-greedy Strategies for Working Set Selection

In this section, we introduce two novel semi-greedy strategies designed to improve the computational efficiency for BCD-g method in sparse index tracking task (
2
) and non-negative sparse PCA problem (
3
) when
k=2
. Given that there are
n(n-1)
possible coordinate pair combinations, traditional cyclic or random sampling approaches become infeasible as
n
increases. The semi-greedy strategies we propose combine random and greedy selection methods. These strategies are heuristic methods for selecting the coordinate pairs that are expected to result in the largest decrease in the objective function. While they also ensuring that every index has the potential to be selected through random sampling.

8.1
Semi-greedy Strategies for Sparse Index Tracking Task

The sparse index tracking problem (
2
) can be formulated as:
\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in\Omega_{1}}F({\mathbf{x}})=f({\mathbf{x}})+h({\mathbf{x}})+g({\mathbf{x}})
, where
f({\mathbf{x}})=\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\|_{2}^{2}
,
h({\mathbf{x}})=\mathcal{I}_{\geq 0}({\mathbf{x}})
,
g({\mathbf{x}})=-\lambda\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{[s]}
, and
\Omega_{1}=\{{\mathbf{x}}|\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{1}=1\}
. We define the gradient
\nabla f({\mathbf{x}})+\partial h({\mathbf{x}})+\partial g({\mathbf{x}})
at
{\mathbf{x}}
,
{\mathbf{x}}^{t}
and
\breve{{\mathbf{x}}}
as
g
,
\textbf{g}^{t}
and
\breve{\textbf{g}}
. We consider the following optimality measure:
S_{1}(i)\triangleq\max_{i\neq\tilde{j}}\{\sqrt{L_{i,\tilde{j}}}\min\{\tfrac{1}{L_{i,\tilde{j}}}(\textbf{g}^{t}_{i}-\textbf{g}^{t}_{\tilde{j}}),{\mathbf{x}}^{t}_{i}\}
, where
S_{1}(i)\in\mathbb{R}
and the index
\tilde{j}
is defined as
\tilde{j}\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{j=1,2,...,n}\textbf{g}_{j}^{t}

(
Beck, 2014
)
. The measure
S_{1}(i)
is non-negative and equal to
0
only at critical points. Based on the measure
S_{1}(i)
, we propose the following greedy index selection strategy. The index
j^{t}
corresponds to the variable with the smallest gradient value in
\textbf{g}^{t}
:
j^{t}=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{j}\textbf{g}^{t}_{j}
, while the index
i^{t}
is chosen to maximize
S_{1}(i)

Semi-greedy Strategies for Non-negative Sparse PCA Problem

For the non-negative sparse PCA problem, we also propose a semi-greedy strategy to accelerate the BCD-g method. The non-negative sparse PCA problem (
3

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in\Omega_{2}}F({\mathbf{x}})=f({\mathbf{x}})+h({\mathbf{x}})+g({\mathbf{x}}),

(21)

where
f({\mathbf{x}})=-\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}
,
h({\mathbf{x}})=\lambda(\mathcal{I}_{\geq 0}({\mathbf{x}})+{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\mathbf{1}^{n})
,
g({\mathbf{x}})=-\lambda\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{[s]}
, and
\Omega_{2}=\{{\mathbf{x}}|{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}{\mathbf{x}}=1\}
.

Theorem 8.1
.

When
\breve{{\mathbf{x}}}
is a critical point in Problem (
21
), it holds:
\breve{\textbf{g}}\odot\breve{{\mathbf{x}}}=(\breve{{\mathbf{x}}}^{\top}\breve{\textbf{g}}\breve{{\mathbf{x}}})\odot\breve{{\mathbf{x}}}

\breve{\textbf{g}}

\breve{{\mathbf{x}}}

Proof.

L({\mathbf{x}},\nu,\mu)\triangleq F({\mathbf{x}})+\nu({\mathbf{x}}^{\top}{\mathbf{x}}-1)+\langle\mu,-{\mathbf{x}}\rangle

\nu\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are the Lagrange multipliers. The corresponding Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions for this problem are:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\nabla L({\mathbf{x}},\nu,\mu)=\textbf{g}+\nu{\mathbf{x}}-\mu=0,\,&(a)\\
&{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}{\mathbf{x}}-1=0,\,&(b)\\
&-{\mathbf{x}}\leq 0,\,&(c)\\

(22)

\nu=-{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\textbf{g}
. Substituting
\nu
into (a), we get
\textbf{g}-{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\textbf{g}{\mathbf{x}}-\mu=0
. Finally, using (e), we have
\textbf{g}\odot{\mathbf{x}}=({\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\textbf{g}{\mathbf{x}})\odot{\mathbf{x}}
.
∎

When
\breve{{\mathbf{x}}}

\breve{\textbf{g}}\odot\breve{{\mathbf{x}}}=(\breve{{\mathbf{x}}}^{\top}\breve{\textbf{g}}\breve{{\mathbf{x}}})\odot\breve{{\mathbf{x}}}

\breve{{\mathbf{x}}}

9
Geometric Analyses of CWS-Point for Different Applications

This section presents a geometric analysis of
CWS
-points for the three applications outlined in Section
2
. The analysis primarily focuses on utilizing the necessary optimality conditions of
CWS
-points to derive insights into the solution properties. Specifically, we investigate two key characteristics: the exactness property and the extreme point property, which provide deeper understanding of the solution behavior under the considered framework.

9.1
Sparse Index Tracking Task: An Exactness Property

We provide an exactness property of our algorithm for the following sparse index tracking problem:

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{x}}}\,f({\mathbf{x}})+\mathcal{I}_{\geq 0}({\mathbf{x}})-\lambda\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{[s]},\ s.t.\ \|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{1}=1,

(23)

where
f({\mathbf{x}})=\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\|_{2}^{2}
. We introduce the theorem concerning the exactness property of the sparse index tracking problem.

Theorem 9.1
.

(Proof in Appendix
C.1
)

We let
\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}
be any
CWS
-point for Problem (
23
). When
\lambda
is sufficient large such that
\lambda>2\|\nabla f(\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}})\|_{\infty}
, we have:

(i)

\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}_{i}=0,\,\forall i\notin\mathcal{T}({\mathbf{x}})
, i.e.,
\|\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}\|_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}|\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}_{[i]}|
.

(ii)

\nabla_{i}f(\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}})=\nabla_{j}f(\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}),\,\forall i,\ j\in\mathcal{T}(\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}})
.

Here,
\mathcal{T}(\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}})
is the indices of non-zero components of
\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}
in magnitude.

Remarks.
When
\lambda
exceeds a specific threshold
2\|\nabla f(\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}})\|_{\infty}
in Problem (
23
), any
CWS
-point of the nonconvex problem satisfies to the sparse constraint
\|\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}\|_{1}-\|\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}\|_{[s]}=0
. Nevertheless, existing methods such as PSG, MSCR, and PDCA only identify the critical points of Problem (
23
), without guaranteeing satisfaction of the sparse constraint for all critical points. Thus, the results we establish in Theorem
9.1
demonstrate superior performance compared to these methods. Moreover, we observe that when
\lambda
surpasses the threshold, all elements in
\nabla_{\mathcal{T}(\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}})}f({\mathbf{x}})
are equal, which
\mathcal{T}(\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}})
represents the set of non-zero elements.

9.2
DC Penalized Binary Optimization: An Extreme Point Property

This subsection provides an extreme point property of
CWS
-point for the DC penalized binary problem:

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{x}}}\,\hat{f}({\mathbf{x}})+\mathcal{I}_{[-1,1]}({\mathbf{x}}),\ s.t.\ {\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\textbf{1}=c,

(24)

where
\hat{f}({\mathbf{x}})={\mathbf{p}}^{\top}{\mathbf{x}}-\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\hat{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{x}}
and
\hat{\mathbf{Q}}\succeq 0
. Our main results are outlined in the following theorem.

Theorem 9.2
.

(Proof in Appendix
C.2
)
We let
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}=-\hat{\mathbf{Q}}+\theta{\mathbf{I}}_{n}
and assume
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{ii}+\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{jj}-2\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{ij}\leq 0,\forall i,j\in[n]
. We let
\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}
be any
CWS
-point for Problem (
24
). It holds that
|\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}_{r}|=1
for all
r\in[n]
.

Remarks.
The extreme point property indicates that the solutions
{\mathbf{x}}
of problem (
24
) lie on the boundary of the feasible region
{\mathbf{x}}\in\{-1,+1\}^{n}
. The analysis demonstrates that the
CWS
-points
\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}
consistently satisfy these boundary conditions
\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}\in\{-1,+1\}^{n}
, a feature that is not guaranteed for all critical points.

9.3
DC Penalized Binary Optimization: An Exactness Property

This subsection establishes the exactness property of
CWS
-points for the DC-penalized binary optimization problem defined as follows:

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{x}}}\,f({\mathbf{x}})+\mathcal{I}_{[-1,1]}({\mathbf{x}})-\lambda\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2},\ s.t.\ {\mathbf{x}}^{\top}\textbf{1}=c,

(25)

where
f({\mathbf{x}})=\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\|_{2}^{2}
. We now present the theorem of the exactness property.

Theorem 9.3
.

(Proof in Appendix
C.3
)

We let
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}={\mathbf{A}}^{\top}{\mathbf{A}}+\theta{\mathbf{I}}_{n}
and define
\phi\triangleq\max(\bar{\mathbf{Q}})-\min(\bar{\mathbf{Q}})
. When
\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}
is the
CWS
-point of Problem (
25
), we have:

(i)

When
\lambda>\phi\sqrt{n+2}
, it holds that
\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}\in\{-1,+1\}^{n}
and
\sqrt{n}=\|\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}\|
.

(ii)

When
\lambda>\phi\sqrt{n}
, it holds that
\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}_{i}\neq 0
for all
i\in[n]
.

Remarks.
When
\lambda
surpasses a certain threshold, any
CWS
-point
\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}
of Problem (
25
) achieves exactness by satisfying the binary constraint
\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}\in\{-1,+1\}^{n}
and condition
\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}_{i}\neq 0,\ \forall i
. In contrast, existing methods such as PSG, MSCR, and PDCA lack the ability to guarantee the binary constraints. This results highlight the robustness and precision of the proposed methods.

Conclusion

We introduce BCD-g and BCD-l-
k
for addressing a class of structured nonconvex optimization problems with nonseparable constraints. We offer an optimality hierarchy analysis of the proposed method. This method leverages specific function structures to avoid bad local minima and achieve better stationary points. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the developed method can converge to coordinate-wise stationary points with linear convergence rates. Our method has exhibited superior performance compared to existing approaches, both theoretically and experimentally.

\lambda>\phi\sqrt{n+2}
and
\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\leq\phi
. Inequality

\forall i,\ 1-\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}_{i}^{2}=0
, leading to
\forall i,\ |\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}_{i}|=1

\|\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}\|=\sqrt{n}
.
∎

(ii)
We now prove that
\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}_{i}\neq 0
for all
i\in[n]
when
\lambda>\phi\sqrt{n}
, concluding by contradiction. Assume
\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}_{i}=0
for some

. Problem (

) reduces to

\displaystyle 0\in\arg\min_{l\leq\eta\leq u}\mathcal{P}(\eta)\triangleq\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\eta^{2}+\beta\eta-\lambda\sqrt{\|\ddot{x}\|_{2}^{2}+2\eta^{2}}.

(77)

\beta

(i)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\delta)-\mathcal{P}(0)\overset{}{=}\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\delta^{2}-\lambda\sqrt{\|\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}\|_{2}^{2}+2\delta^{2}}+\lambda\|\ddot{{\mathbf{x}}}\|\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\delta^{2}-\tfrac{\lambda\delta^{2}}{\sqrt{n+2\delta^{2}}}\overset{\text{②}}{<}0,

Active vibration suppression of a magnetic–ball bearing hybrid supported rotor system
Authors: Hang Shen, Chuan Zhao, Huachun Wu, Honglei Sha, Feng Sun, Junjie Jin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe vibration problem caused by milling is a key factor restricting high-precision and high-efficiency machining. Traditional passive vibration suppression methods are limited by fixed structural parameters and lack sufficient adaptability to dynamic operating conditions, making it difficult to achieve effective broadband vibration control. Magnetic bearings, with their non-contact characteristics and active controllability, provide a new idea for milling vibration suppression. To overcome these limitations, this paper proposes a novel hybrid magnetic-ball-supported flexible rotor system that combines active magnetic bearings with mechanical ball bearings, thereby achieving both controllable dynamic stiffness and stable rigid support. Based on the milling force model and the hybrid support rotor dynamics model, a coupled mathematical model is established, and the vibration suppression effect of the magnetic bearing on milling induced vibration is investigated by magnetic force regulation under PID control. Furthermore, milling force signals were generated using a simulation platform and applied to the rotor test bench to conduct numerical simulations and experimental validation. The results demonstrate that the proposed hybrid support strategy can significantly reduce milling-induced vibration amplitude, improve rotor dynamic stability, and enhance vibration suppression performance over a wide operating range. This study proposes a new hybrid vibration control scheme for high-speed, high-precision milling systems and provides a method for intelligent vibration reduction in advanced machining equipment.Keywords: Hybrid support, Magnetic bearings, Milling force, Vibration suppression, Rotor dynamicsSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science

Introduction

Vibration during milling negatively affects machining accuracy and tool life, thereby reducing overall machining efficiency1–3. Based on the differences in excitation mechanisms, these vibrations can be classified into two main categories: self-excited vibration and forced vibration4. Chatter originates from the internal coupling between the cutting process and the structural dynamics of the machine tool. The most common type, regenerative chatter, is a dynamic instability phenomenon formed by the interaction between the surface waviness left by a previous cutting edge and the cutting motion of the subsequent edge5. Forced vibration, in contrast, is primarily caused by periodic external excitations, such as the intermittent cutting of tool teeth and spindle unbalance6. These vibrations not only accelerate tool wear and reduce machining efficiency but also generate chatter marks on the workpiece surface, severely compromising machining accuracy and surface quality. Consequently, numerous scholars worldwide have conducted research on milling vibration suppression techniques.

Currently, the primary techniques for milling vibration suppression fall into two categories: active suppression and passive suppression. Among these, passive suppression is a method that mitigates chatter by optimizing the inherent structural dynamics of the process system, rather than relying on external energy control7. The main suppression methods include tool holders equipped with tuned mass dampers8,9, variable-pitch/variable-helix end mills10, and specially designed fixtures. Although such methods are characterized by strong robustness and low cost, their vibration suppression performance is limited by the specific structural design, resulting in poor generality.

In comparison, active suppression dynamically counteracts vibration by real-time sensing and applying opposing forces, offering high adaptability to complex working conditions and superior performance. Currently, piezoelectric actuators represent the predominant approach for implementing active suppression11–13. Piezoelectric actuators offer advantages in many vibration control applications due to their high-frequency response, high positioning accuracy, and substantial output force. However, in the specific operating conditions of rotor systems, these advantages can be limited, as the displacement of piezoelectric materials under a single excitation is typically very small, usually on the order of micrometers14. Therefore, integrated designs are often employed to achieve stroke amplification; however, they also introduce additional nonlinearities, which deteriorate control performance and complicate controller design.

In response, many researchers have developed magnetically levitated motorized spindles for milling applications. These spindles utilize an active control mechanism to enhance machining accuracy and surface quality while simultaneously extending the service life of both the cutting tool and the spindle itself15–17. Although the magnetic bearings in a magnetically levitated electric spindle eliminate mechanical wear, their inherent dynamic stiffness is low, necessitating the use of relatively complex control algorithms to improve the overall stiffness of the spindle system.

Cui G et al. proposed a novel control method based on an electromagnetic actuator (EMA) embedded in the tool holder, which enables control of chatter frequency18. Hans-Joachim K et al. proposed an adaptive spindle system incorporating an auxiliary electromagnetic bearing, and experimentally evaluated its static and dynamic performance up to 15,000 r/min; the results demonstrated that adaptive closed-loop control is effective in compensating tool deflection and vibration at the system eigenfrequencies19. Knospe C R By increasing the number of magnetic bearings in a magnetically levitated electro spindle, the dynamic stiffness of the spindle is realized through the active control of the magnetic bearings to improve the stability of the system during the cutting process, and the results show that the vibration problem induced by machining can be realized through the additional stiffness of the magnetic bearings20. Gourc E et al. proposed a method for identifying the stability of high-speed milling machining based on dynamic modeling of magnetically levitated electro spindles, and investigated the effect of several parameters of the controller on the stability of the system, and the results showed that the gain adjustment improves the stability of forced vibration during machining21. Tsai N C et al. employed active magnetic bearing (AMB) technology combined with fuzzy-logic algorithms and an adaptive self-tuning feedback loop to apply corresponding compensation forces, thereby balancing the dynamic cutting forces under different milling conditions and regulating spindle positional deviations22. These studies collectively demonstrate the effectiveness of electromagnetic control in improving spindle dynamic performance and machining stability. However, magnetically levitated spindle systems still suffer from relatively low dynamic stiffness, which may cause significant displacement under high-load cutting conditions and thereby compromise machining accuracy. Meanwhile, achieving both high stiffness and stable levitation usually depends on structurally complex control systems and highly demanding algorithms, which limits practical implementation.

Therefore, this paper proposes a hybrid-supported rotor system that integrates magnetic bearings and rolling bearings, utilizing the magnetic bearings for active suppression of milling vibrations. In this study, a magnetic-ball bearing hybrid support rotor system is constructed by introducing a magnetic levitation bearing as an auxiliary support in the main support structure of a traditional rolling bearing and as an active regulating unit. Different from the system that completely relies on the magnetic levitation bearing to realize the load bearing, this hybrid support system serves as the basic load bearing unit, and the magnetic levitation bearing is used as the unit of adjustable dynamic stiffness and active damping. The rolling bearing provides stable support stiffness and reliable mechanical support, while the magnetic levitation bearing generates an adjustable force through closed-loop control, introducing a controllable increment in the equivalent stiffness and damping of the whole system. The hybrid support between the two allows the rotor system to gain the ability to actively regulate vibration characteristics while maintaining mechanical safety and structural stability.

Based on this hybrid-supported rotor system, a dynamic model of a flexible rotor jointly supported by magnetic and rolling bearings is established and coupled with a dynamic milling force model. Subsequently, the vibration suppression effect achieved by magnetic intervention under different working conditions was evaluated by systematic analysis. Finally, a milling simulation excitation test was carried out on a test bed, and active vibration suppression was realized by a control system with integrated magnetic bearings. The results verify that the implementation of magnetic intervention in a hybrid supported rotor system can effectively suppress milling-induced vibration.

Dynamic modeling of hybrid-supported rotor system

Structure of the hybrid-supported rotor system

This hybrid-bearing rotor system consists of a flexible rotor, a support system, a drive motor, and a cooling system. The support system comprises two pairs of angular contact ball bearings and two magnetic bearings, forming a hybrid-bearing structure that combines passive support with active control, as shown in Fig. 1.

Structural diagram of the hybrid-supported rotor system.

During the operation of the rotor system, the control of the magnetic bearings in the hybrid support is dynamically adjusted based on the rotor’s vibration status and operating conditions. When the rotor’s vibration is low, the magnetic bearings apply a smaller control force to achieve minimal vibration suppression; when external excitation increases or the amplitude of the rotor’s vibration grows, the control system enhances the magnetic force of the bearings, thereby adjusting the equivalent stiffness and damping of the support system to improve the system’s resistance to disturbances.

This support configuration effectively eliminates the need for the large offset current required by magnetic bearings under long-term loading and the resulting heat generation issues, while also reducing the complexity associated with high-precision full-suspension control. Compared to mechanical bearing systems, it enhances active control capabilities; compared to pure magnetic bearing suspension systems, the proposed hybrid support configuration achieves an effective balance between control complexity and vibration suppression performance.

Vibration suppression for hybrid-supported rotors

Milling force simulation excitation

In the milling dynamic equation, the milling force coefficients are typically determined experimentally. However, based on the studies by Budak and Altintas, these coefficients can be regarded as constants when the workpiece and tool materials are specified25. Therefore, the milling force coefficients can be obtained by solving the simultaneous equations of the formula for the average26 milling force over one full rotation of the cutter and the formula for milling power. The milling forces were calculated using MATLAB. A four-tooth end mill with a diameter of 10 mm was selected, and the milling force coefficients were identified based on the average milling force method. A time-domain dynamic model of the machining system was established using the system parameters listed in Table 1. The Newmark-β numerical integration method was used to solve the system’s dynamic equations in the time domain. This method is widely used in structural dynamics and the analysis of flexible rotor systems, and it offers good numerical stability and computational accuracy. In the calculations, the parameters β = 1/4 and γ = 1/2 were selected, corresponding to the mean acceleration scheme, to ensure the algorithm’s unconditional stability. The time step is determined based on the rotor’s angular frequency, with a single rotation cycle uniformly discretized into 256 time steps. The total simulation duration is 60 cycles, ensuring accurate capture of the intermittent variations in milling forces and the tool engagement and disengagement processes.

Combined milling force of four-tooth end milling cutter. (a) X‑ directions, (b) Y- directions.

As shown in Fig. 6, the milling forces exhibit distinct time-varying characteristics in the X and Y directions. During the initial stage of tool engagement, the system experiences a negative cutting force in the Y direction. As the engagement angle increases, the force in the Y direction gradually decreases, turns positive at a certain angle, reaches a positive peak, and then drops to zero as the tool exits the workpiece. In contrast, the milling force in the X direction undergoes only a single sign change during the penetration process. Overall, it first increases monotonically and then decreases monotonically; under the current cutting parameters, no positive milling force is observed.

Overall, due to the intermittent cutting nature of multi-tooth cutting tools, the milling force varies periodically as the teeth rotate, exhibiting significant time-varying behavior and multi-harmonic characteristics. Its fundamental frequency is determined by the tooth passing frequency, superimposed with higher-order harmonic components. Therefore, this milling force can be regarded as a typical multi-frequency excitation source, which will have a significant impact on the vibration response and stability of the rotor system.

Vibration suppression in a hybrid-supported rotor system under milling conditions

Active vibration suppression for hybrid-supported rotor systems

To analyze the vibration characteristics of the hybrid-supported rotor system under milling disturbance and to evaluate the suppression effect of magnetic bearing control, numerical simulations are conducted based on the established dynamic model. The milling force is calculated using the identified cutting parameters, with the spindle speed set to 1800 rpm, the axial depth of cut of 1.5 mm, and the feed per tooth of 0.02 mm.

The resulting milling force is applied at the shaft end as an external excitation. The dynamic responses of the rotor system in the X and Y directions are then obtained under two conditions: with and without magnetic bearing control. The simulation results are presented in Fig. 7.

Vibration response of a hybrid supported rotor system under milling loads. (a) X‑ directions, (b) Y- directions.

As shown in Fig. 7, the vibration displacement response of the rotor after applying the simulated milling force can be observed. The figure indicates that the rotor’s vibration displacement exhibits periodic oscillations in both the X and Y directions, with a maximum value of 9.93 μm in the X-direction and 7.88 μm in the Y-direction.

After magnetic force intervention via the magnetic bearings in the hybrid-supported system, the vibration displacement in both directions is reduced. Specifically, the displacement in the X-direction decreases from 9.93 μm to 7.57 μm, while in the Y-direction it decreases from 7.88 μm to 5.17 μm. The study demonstrates that the magnetic force control applied through the magnetic bearings in the hybrid-supported rotor system can effectively mitigate vibration issues induced by milling forces, with a more pronounced suppression effect observed in the Y-direction compared to the X-direction.

To further demonstrate the advantages of the proposed method in vibration suppression, a conventional passive vibration damping system was introduced for comparison. This system, based on ball bearing supports, achieves vibration attenuation by increasing the equivalent structural damping and does not incorporate any active control forces or electromagnetic adjustment mechanisms.

Comparison of active suppression with traditional method

Traditional vibration reduction strategies primarily rely on passive structural design or additional damping elements to reduce system vibrations; they typically depend on pre-set parameters and are difficult to adjust in real time as rotational speed, load, and excitation frequency change. To further validate the effectiveness of the proposed active magnetic vibration reduction method, this paper compares it with conditions without vibration reduction and traditional passive vibration reduction methods; the results are shown in Fig. 8.

Vibration suppression performance of the system at different rotational speeds. (a) X‑ directions, (b) Y- directions.

As shown in Fig. 8, while traditional passive vibration damping methods can reduce vibration amplitude to some extent across the entire speed range, their damping effect is relatively limited. In particular, between 3,000 and 4,500 r/min, the displacement amplitude under traditional passive damping conditions increases significantly. This phenomenon is primarily due to the fact that traditional passive vibration damping strategies rely on structural natural parameters, making it difficult for them to adapt to changes in system dynamic characteristics caused by variations in rotational speed.

In contrast, the active magnetic vibration control method maintains a low displacement level even at 3,000–4,500 r/min, indicating that this method can effectively regulate system vibrations in response to speed changes, thereby avoiding the vibration control failure that occurs with traditional passive strategies at specific speeds.

Influence of magnetic force intervention on vibration suppression under different milling conditions

Through the above analysis, it can be learned that the magnetic intervention of the magnetic bearing has a better suppression effect on the vibration caused by milling, but only studied the vibration suppression under the specific milling force, and did not analyze the vibration suppression effect under other milling conditions. Now, we consider some of the factors affecting the milling, and analyze the suppression of vibration displacement of the hybrid bearing rotor by the magnetic intervention of the magnetic levitation bearing in different working conditions. The intention is to analyze its wide-band vibration suppression capability.

Analysis of vibration characteristics of the hybrid-supported rotor under different rotational speeds

To analyze the effect of different rotational speeds on the vibration of the hybrid supported rotor system and the vibration suppression ability of magnetic bearing magnetic intervention under milling conditions, the depth of cut is taken as 1.5 mm, the feed rate of each tooth is 0.02 mm, and the rotational speed is increased from 1,000 rpm to 8,000 rpm, and the increment is taken to be 1,000 rpm for the vibration suppression capability of the hybrid supported rotor system under the two cases of magnetic intervention with magnetic bearings and non-magnetic bearing magnetic intervention. The vibration response of the rotor system is solved and the results are shown in Fig. 9.

Rotor vibration displacement in all directions at different speeds. (a) X‑ directions, (b) Y- directions.

As can be seen from Fig. 9, within the rotational speed range from 1,000 rpm to 8,000 rpm, the vibration displacement of the rotor in both the X and Y directions increases with rotational speed. Without magnetic force intervention from the magnetic bearings, the displacement rises from 8.15 μm to 12.26 μm in the X-direction and from 7.03 μm to 9.85 μm in the Y-direction. As the rotational speed increases, the vibration suppression by the magnetic intervention of the magnetic bearing reaches its maximum at 4,000 rpm, the effect is poor at 5,000 rpm, and the magnitude of vibration suppression is relatively good at other rotational speeds. This trend is mainly attributed to the interaction between the tooth-passing frequency (TPF) and the rotor’s dynamic characteristics. At 5,000 rpm, the TPF generated by the 4-tooth milling cutter approaches the first-order critical frequency of the rotor system, which may induce modal coupling and amplify the vibration response. In addition, the closed-loop magnetic bearing control may exhibit phase lag and reduced effective damping under this high-frequency excitation, thereby weakening the vibration suppression performance.

After introducing magnetic force control from the magnetic bearings, the vibration displacement across the entire speed range is effectively suppressed. Compared with the condition without magnetic force, the displacement amplitude in the X-direction is reduced overall by approximately 1–3 μm, and in the Y-direction by about 2–4 μm. Furthermore, the vibration suppression effect remains more stable within the medium to high-speed range.

Overall, with magnetic force intervention, the amplitude of vibration suppression displacement remains within a range of 2 to 3 μm across varying speeds, and no significant ineffective range was observed. This demonstrates that magnetic force intervention can maintain stable vibration suppression performance over a wide range of rotational speeds.

Consequently, the simulation results exhibit a smoother dynamic response than the experimental measurements. Nevertheless, the overall variation trend and vibration suppression characteristics obtained from the simulation remain consistent with the experimental observations.

Conclusion

In this study, a hybrid rotor support system combining two magnetic bearings and two rolling bearings is proposed to suppress milling vibrations. A dynamic milling force model was established and incorporated into the rotor dynamics model as an external excitation term to analyze the vibration response under milling conditions. A PID-based magnetic force control strategy was employed to actively adjust the equivalent stiffness of the magnetic bearings, thereby suppressing rotor vibrations. Simulation results indicate that, under all studied operating conditions, magnetic intervention significantly reduced vibration displacement, with the suppression effect being more pronounced when the amplitude of the milling force was higher.

To further validate the proposed approach, a simulated milling-force loading experimental platform was constructed to investigate vibration suppression under equivalent cyclic perturbations applied through the magnetic bearings. The experimental results were highly consistent with the simulation results, confirming that magnetic intervention can effectively suppress vibration. In the X- and Y-radial directions, the average vibration amplitude was reduced by approximately 20%, which verifies the effectiveness of the proposed model and control strategy.

Nevertheless, this study has some limitations. Complex machining factors, including tool wear, nonlinear cutting effects, and transient cutting conditions, have not been fully accounted for. Furthermore, the study only examined PID-based control; the effects of axial dynamics, thermal effects, and long-term operational stability on the system require further investigation. Future work will focus on more realistic cutting conditions and advanced control strategies to further enhance the vibration damping performance of the hybrid-supported rotor system.

1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a global field,
i.e. a number field (a finite extension of
\mathbbm{Q}
)
or a function field of an algebraic curve over

\mathbbm{F}_{q}
(a finite extension of
\mathbbm{F}_{q}(t))
.
Let
K/k
be a finite separable field extension and

\mathbbm{A}_{K}^{\times}
be the idele group of
K
.
We say that
the Hasse norm principle holds for
K/k

if
(N_{K/k}(\mathbbm{A}_{K}^{\times})\cap k^{\times})/N_{K/k}(K^{\times})=1

where
N_{K/k}
is the norm map.
Hasse
[
Has31
, Satz, page 64]
proved that
the Hasse norm principle holds for any cyclic extension
K/k

but does not hold for bicyclic extension
\mathbbm{Q}(\sqrt{-39},\sqrt{-3})/\mathbbm{Q}
.

Let
\overline{k}
be a fixed separable closure of
k
. Let
T
be an algebraic
k
-torus,
i.e. a group
k
-scheme with fiber product (base change)

T\times_{k}\overline{k}=T\times_{{\rm Spec}\,k}\,{\rm Spec}\,\overline{k}\simeq(\mathbbm{G}_{m,\overline{k}})^{n}
;

k
-form of the split torus
(\mathbbm{G}_{m,k})^{n}
.
Let
E
be a principal homogeneous space (= torsor) under
T
.

Hasse principle holds for
E
means that
if
E
has a
k_{v}
-rational point for all completions
k_{v}
of
k
,
then
E
has a
k
-rational point.
The set
H^{1}(k,T)
classifies all such torsors
E
up
to (non-unique) isomorphism.
We take
the Shafarevich-Tate group
of
T
:

\displaystyle\Sha(T)={\rm Ker}\left\{H^{1}(k,T)\xrightarrow{\rm res}\bigoplus_{v\in V_{k}}H^{1}(k_{v},T)\right\}

where
V_{k}
is the set of all places of
k
and

k_{v}
is the completion of
k
at
v
.
Then
Hasse principle holds for all torsors
E
under
T

if and only if
\Sha(T)=0
.

Let
T=R^{(1)}_{K/k}(\mathbbm{G}_{m,K})
be the norm one torus of
K/k
,
i.e. the kernel of the norm map
R_{K/k}(\mathbbm{G}_{m,K})\rightarrow\mathbbm{G}_{m,k}

where

R_{K/k}
is the Weil restriction
(see Ono
[
Ono61
, Section 1.4]
, Voskresenskii
[
Vos98
, page 37, Section 3.12]
).
It is biregularly isomorphic to the norm hypersurface

f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=1
where

f\in k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is the polynomial of total
degree
n
defined by the norm map
N_{K/k}:K^{\times}\to k^{\times}
.
The norm one torus
T=R^{(1)}_{K/k}(\mathbbm{G}_{m,K})
has the
Chevalley module
J_{G/H}
as its character module
and the field
L(J_{G/H})^{G}\simeq k(T)
as its function field
where

L
is the splitting field of
T
with

G={\rm Gal}(L/k)
and
H={\rm Gal}(L/K)\lneq G

and

J_{G/H}=(I_{G/H})^{\circ}={\rm Hom}_{\mathbbm{Z}}(I_{G/H},\mathbbm{Z})

is the dual lattice of
I_{G/H}={\rm Ker}\ \varepsilon
and

\varepsilon:\mathbbm{Z}[G/H]\rightarrow\mathbbm{Z}
,

\sum_{\overline{g}\in G/H}a_{\overline{g}}\,\overline{g}\mapsto\sum_{\overline{g}\in G/H}a_{\overline{g}}

is the augmentation map.
We have the exact sequence
0\rightarrow\mathbbm{Z}\rightarrow\mathbbm{Z}[G/H]\rightarrow J_{G/H}\rightarrow 0
and
{\rm rank}_{\mathbbm{Z}}\,J_{G/H}=n-1
.
More precisely, write
J_{G/H}=\oplus_{1\leq i\leq n-1}\mathbbm{Z}u_{i}
and
define the action of
G
on
L(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})
by

x_{i}^{\sigma}=\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}x_{j}^{a_{i,j}}(1\leq i\leq n-1)
for any
\sigma\in G
,
when
u_{i}^{\sigma}=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}a_{i,j}u_{j}

(a_{i,j}\in\mathbbm{Z})
.
Then the invariant field
L(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})^{G}\simeq k(T)
:
the function field of
T=R^{(1)}_{K/k}(\mathbbm{G}_{m,K})

(see Endo and Miyata
[
EM73
, Section 1]
,
[
EM75
, Section 1]

and Voskresenskii
[
Vos98
, Section 4.8]
).

[
Ono63
]
established the relationship
between the Hasse norm principle for
K/k

and Hasse principle for all torsors
E
under
the norm one torus
R^{(1)}_{K/k}(\mathbbm{G}_{m,K})
:

Theorem 1.1

(Ono
[
Ono63
, page 70]
, see also Platonov
[
Pla82
, page 44]
, Kunyavskii
[

, Platonov and Rapinchuk
[

Let
k
be a global field and
K/k
be a finite separable extension.
Let
T=R^{(1)}_{K/k}(\mathbbm{G}_{m,K})
be the norm one torus of
K/k
.
Then

\displaystyle\Sha(T)\simeq(N_{K/k}(\mathbbm{A}_{K}^{\times})\cap k^{\times})/N_{K/k}(K^{\times}).

In particular,
\Sha(T)=0
if and only if
the Hasse norm principle holds for
K/k
.

For Galois extensions
K/k
, Tate
[
Tat67
]
gave the following theorem:

Theorem 1.2

(Tate
[
Tat67
, page 198]
)
.

Let
k
be a global field,
K/k
be a finite Galois extension
with Galois group
G={\rm Gal}(K/k)
.
Let
V_{k}
be the set of all places of
k

and
G_{v}
be the decomposition group of
G
at
v\in V_{k}
.
Then

\displaystyle(N_{K/k}(\mathbbm{A}_{K}^{\times})\cap k^{\times})/N_{K/k}(K^{\times})\simeq{\rm Coker}\left\{\bigoplus_{v\in V_{k}}\widehat{H}^{-3}(G_{v},\mathbbm{Z})\xrightarrow{\rm cores}\widehat{H}^{-3}(G,\mathbbm{Z})\right\}

where
\widehat{H}
is the Tate cohomology.
In particular, the Hasse norm principle holds for
K/k

if and only if the restriction map

H^{3}(G,\mathbbm{Z})\xrightarrow{\rm res}\bigoplus_{v\in V_{k}}H^{3}(G_{v},\mathbbm{Z})

is injective.
In particular, if
H^{3}(G,\mathbbm{Z})=0
, then
the Hasse norm principle holds for
K/k
.

Let
C_{n}
be the cyclic group of order
n
.
If
G\simeq C_{n}
, then

\widehat{H}^{-3}(G,\mathbbm{Z})\simeq H^{3}(G,\mathbbm{Z})\simeq H^{1}(G,\mathbbm{Z})=0

and hence Hasse’s original theorem follows.
If there exists a place
v
of
k
such that
G_{v}=G
, then
the Hasse norm principle also holds for
K/k
.
For example, the Hasse norm principle holds for
K/k
with

G\simeq V_{4}=(C_{2})^{2}
if and only if
there exists a
(
ramified
)
place
v
of
k
such that
G_{v}=V_{4}
because

H^{3}(V_{4},\mathbbm{Z})\simeq\mathbbm{Z}/2\mathbbm{Z}
and
H^{3}(C_{2},\mathbbm{Z})=0
.
The Hasse norm principle holds for
K/k
with

G\simeq(C_{2})^{3}
if and only if (i)
there exists a
(
ramified
)
place
v
of
k
such that
G_{v}=G

or (ii)
there exist
(
ramified
)
places
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}
of
k
such that

G_{v_{i}}\simeq V_{4}
and

H^{3}(G,\mathbbm{Z})\xrightarrow{\rm res}H^{3}(G_{v_{1}},\mathbbm{Z})\oplus H^{3}(G_{v_{2}},\mathbbm{Z})\oplus H^{3}(G_{v_{3}},\mathbbm{Z})

is an isomorphism because

H^{3}(G,\mathbbm{Z})\simeq(\mathbbm{Z}/2\mathbbm{Z})^{\oplus 3}
and

H^{3}(V_{4},\mathbbm{Z})\simeq\mathbbm{Z}/2\mathbbm{Z}
.
Note that if
v
is an unramified place of
k
, then
G_{v}
is cyclic.

Let
T
be an algebraic
k
-torus
and
T(k)
be the group of
k
-rational points of
T
.
Then
T(k)

embeds into
\prod_{v\in V_{k}}T(k_{v})
by the diagonal map
where
V_{k}
is the set of all places of
k
and

k_{v}
is the completion of
k
at
v
.
Let
\overline{T(k)}
be the closure of
T(k)

in the product
\prod_{v\in V_{k}}T(k_{v})
.
The group

\displaystyle A(T)=\left(\prod_{v\in V_{k}}T(k_{v})\right)/\overline{T(k)}

is called
the kernel of the weak approximation
of
T
.
We say that
T
has the weak approximation property
if
A(T)=0
.

Theorem 1.3

(Voskresenskii
[
Vos69
, Theorem 5, page 1213]
,

[
Vos70
, Theorem 6, page 9]
, see also
[
Vos98
, Section 11.6, Theorem, page 120]
)
.

Let
k
be a global field,

T
be an algebraic
k
-torus and
X
be a smooth
k
-compactification of
T
.
Then there exists an exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to A(T)\to H^{1}(k,{\rm Pic}\,\overline{X})^{\vee}\to\Sha(T)\to 0

where
M^{\vee}={\rm Hom}(M,\mathbbm{Q}/\mathbbm{Z})
is the Pontryagin dual of
M
.
In particular, if
T
is retract
k
-rational, then
H^{1}(k,{\rm Pic}\,\overline{X})=0
and hence

A(T)=0
and
\Sha(T)=0
.
Moreover, if
L
is the splitting field of
T
and
L/k

is an unramified extension, then
A(T)=0
and

H^{1}(k,{\rm Pic}\,\overline{X})^{\vee}\simeq\Sha(T)
.

For the last assertion, see Voskresenskii
[
Vos98
, Section 11.5]
.
It follows that

H^{1}(k,{\rm Pic}\,\overline{X})=0
if and only if
A(T)=0
and
\Sha(T)=0
,
i.e.
T
has the weak approximation property and
Hasse principle holds for all torsors
E
under
T
.
If
T
is (stably/retract)
k
-rational,
then
H^{1}(k,{\rm Pic}\,\overline{X})=0

(see Voskresenskii
[
Vos69
, Theorem 5, page 1213]
,
Manin
[
Man74
, Section 30]
,
Manin and Tsfasman
[
MT86
]

and also Hoshi, Kanai and Yamasaki
[
HKY22
, Section 1]
).
Theorem
1.3
was generalized
to the case of linear algebraic groups by Sansuc
[
San81
]
.

Applying Theorem
1.3
to

it follows from Theorem

that

if and only if

and

has the weak approximation property and

.
In the algebraic language,

means that

it has a
k
-rational point
if and only if it has a

-rational point
for any place

of

where

is the polynomial of total

defined by the norm map

and

(see Voskresenskii
[

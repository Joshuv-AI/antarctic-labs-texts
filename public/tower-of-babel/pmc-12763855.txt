The association between miR-632 and major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events in patients with end-stage renal disease undergoing long-term hemodialysis
Authors: Mei Yang, Li Wang, Yunjian Huang, Jun Long, Shengling Shi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMajor adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events (MACCE) are the primary cause of poor prognosis and mortality in patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD), posing a serious threat to their health. Investigating the risk factors for MACCE in long-term hemodialysis patients with ESRD is of great significance for improving treatment outcomes and patient prognosis.MethodsPeripheral blood samples were collected from participants, and RT-qPCR was employed to measure the expression levels of miR-632 and PKD1. Participants were followed for three years. The clinical significance of miR-632 in predicting the occurrence of MACCE was analyzed using Kaplan-Meier survival curves, multivariate logistic regression, and ROC curves.ResultsIn patients with ESRD undergoing long-term hemodialysis, the expression level of miR-632 is significantly elevated. This high expression is strongly associated with an increased incidence of MACCE. miR-632 has high predictive value for the occurrence of MACCE in ESRD patients.ConclusionsmiR-632 is a risk factor for MACCE in ESRD patients undergoing long-term hemodialysis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12882-025-04654-7.

Introduction

End-stage renal disease (ESRD) is the final stage of chronic kidney disease progression, characterized by severe renal failure that necessitates kidney transplantation. However, due to organ scarcity and economic constraints, hemodialysis has become one of the most commonly used treatment methods in clinical practice [1, 2]. Despite continuous advancements in hemodialysis technology and significant improvements in the survival rates of ESRD patients, long-term hemodialysis patients still face the risk of various complications, including cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, gastrointestinal bleeding, and more. Among these, major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events (MACCE) are the leading cause of poor prognosis and mortality, seriously threatening patient health [3, 4]. Early reports have shown that the risk of stroke in hemodialysis patients is two to ten times higher than that of the general population [4]. A large number of studies have investigated the risk factors for MACCE in ESRD patients undergoing peritoneal dialysis; however, relatively few studies have examined these risk factors in ESRD patients receiving hemodialysis [5–7]. Therefore, exploring the risk factors for MACCE in long-term hemodialysis patients with ESRD is crucial for improving treatment efficacy and prognosis.

MicroRNA (miRNA) is a type of endogenous, non-coding small RNA molecule that regulates gene expression by complementary pairing and binding to target mRNA. It participates in various processes, including cell proliferation, apoptosis, and immune regulation [8]. In recent years, research has demonstrated that miRNAs play a crucial role in the onset and progression of cardiovascular diseases. For instance, miR-29b is downregulated in ESRD patients with coronary artery calcification and is significantly associated with the incidence of cardiovascular events in long-term hemodialysis patients [9]. Similarly, the expression level of miR-125b is reduced in ESRD patients and shows a significant negative correlation with the severity of vascular calcification [10]. Through regression tree modelling analysis, de et al. concluded that miR-632 may represent a risk factor for cardiovascular disease in hemodialysis patients with end-stage renal disease [11]. However, research on miR-632 has predominantly focused on the field of oncology [12, 13], and its role in cardiovascular complications among ESRD patients remains unclear. Its association with MACCE in hemodialysis-dependent ESRD patients warrants further investigation.

This study aims to analyze the relationship between miR-632 expression levels in the peripheral blood of ESRD patients undergoing long-term hemodialysis and the occurrence of MACCE, clarify the predictive value of miR-632 in MACCE, and provide a theoretical basis for early clinical intervention.

Methods and materials

Research object

This research received approval from the Medical Ethics Committee of Chongqing University Fuling Hospital and recruited 110 ESRD patients who underwent long-term hemodialysis and 60 healthy subjects as controls. Inclusion criteria: (1) Meet the ESRD diagnostic criteria, namely glomerular filtration rate < 15 mL/min and regular hemodialysis for ≥ 3 months; (2) Dialysis 2–3 times a week, with each session lasting ≥ 3.5 h; (3) Age range of 20–70 years old; (4) Patients and their families provide informed consent and sign an informed consent form. Exclusion criteria: (1) occurrence of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases before hemodialysis; (2) suffering from malignant tumors, severe infections, and autoimmune diseases; (3) having undergone kidney surgery.

Sample

Record the patient’s clinical data and collect peripheral blood samples. Centrifuge the samples at 3,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C, then collect the plasma and store it at -80 °C for future use.

Real‑time quantitative PCR

Extract total RNA using TRIzol reagent. Following the manufacturer’s guidelines for SYBR Green Realtime PCR Master, a configuration system was established using GAPDH and U6 as internal reference genes. Quantitative PCR was performed using a LightCycler® 96 PCR system. Changes in miR-632 and PKD1 expression levels were analysed using the 2− ΔΔCt method. Primer sequences were as follows: miR-632, 5’-GACGGGAGGCGGAGCGGGGA-3’ (F) and 5’-TCCCCGCTCCGCCTCCCGTC-3’ (R); PKD1, 5’-GGGCGAGAAAATGGTGCTTG-3’ (F) and 5’-GCCCTTCTCCTT GAATGGCT-3’ (R); GAPDH, 5’-AATCCCATCACCATCTTCCA-3’ (F) and 5’-TGGACTCCACGACGTACTCA-3’ (R); U6, 5’-TCCGATCGTGAAGCGTTC-3’ (F) and 5’-GTGCAGGGTCCGAGGT-3’ (R).

Target gene prediction

Identify potential target genes of miR-632 using the public databases Targetscan (https://www.targetscan.org/), miRDB (https://mirdb.org/), and miRWalk (http://mirwalk.umm.uni-heidelberg.de/).

Dual luciferase reporter assay

Clone the wild-type (WT) and mutant (MUT) 3’-UTR containing the PKD1 binding site into the pGL3 (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) vector. Lipofectamine 2000 was used to co-transfect the vector with miR-632 mimic or miR-632 inhibitor and its miRNA blank control (miR NC) into human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs). Relative luciferase activity was measured using a dual luciferase reporter assay system.

Follow-up

All patients were followed up for a period of three years through outpatient visits, telephone calls, and review of hospitalization records. Patients were divided into a MACCE group and a non-MACCE group based on whether MACCE occurred during the follow-up period. MACCE was defined as: acute myocardial infarction, unstable angina, acute coronary syndrome, ischemic stroke, cerebral hemorrhage, and sudden cardiac death. No subjects dropped out during the study period.

Statistical analyses

All data are presented as mean ± SD. Statistical analyses and visualizations were performed using SPSS version 27.0 and GraphPad Prism version 8.0. Compare two data sets using a t-test. Use Kaplan-Meier curves to illustrate correlations. Employ multiple logistic regression analyses to evaluate risk factors for MACCE in patients with ESRD. Perform ROC analysis to assess the predictive value of miR-632 for the occurrence of MACCE. Use the Pearson correlation method to investigate the relationship between miR-632 and PKD1. The significance of the data is assessed using the criterion of P < 0.05.

Results

Expression of miR-632 in ESRD patients

Clinical data show that, compared with the control group, ESRD patients receiving long-term hemodialysis exhibit no statistically significant differences in age, gender, BMI, WBC, TC, and LDL-CRP (P > 0.05). However, significant differences are observed in ALB, HB, TG, HDL-CRP, SCR, SUA, and phosphorus levels (P < 0.05, Table 1). The expression level of miR-632 in ESRD patients undergoing long-term hemodialysis was significantly higher than that in the control group (P < 0.0001, Fig. 1).

The incidence of MACCE

During the 3-year follow-up of this study, a total of 35 MACCE events occurred in 110 ESRD hemodialysis patients, including 26 heart disease-related events (72.29%) and 9 cerebrovascular disease-related events (25.71%). The Kaplan-Meier curve indicates that the average time for ESRD patients undergoing long-term hemodialysis to develop MACCE is 30.58 months (95% CI: 28.61–32.55) (Fig. 2A). Additionally, the incidence rates of MACCE at 1, 2, and 3 years were 6.36%, 23.64% and 31.82%, respectively (Fig. 2B).

The correlation between miR-632 and MACCE

The relative expression level of miR-632 in the peripheral blood of patients in the MACCE group was significantly higher than that in the non-MACCE group (P < 0.0001, Fig. 3A). Using the median expression level of miR-632 in ESRD patients’ blood samples as the cutoff point, patients were grouped to evaluate the correlation between miR-632 expression and MACCE occurrence. The results showed that high miR-632 expression was significantly associated with an increased incidence of MACCE (log-rank P = 0.024, Fig. 3B). To validate further the strength of the association between miR-632 and MACCE, patients were divided into four groups based on the quartiles of miR-632 relative expression levels (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4). The results showed that the incidence of MACCE in the four groups showed a significant increasing trend (Q1: 17.86%, Q2: 22.22%, Q3: 39.29%, Q4: 48.15%). The expression level of miR-632 was positively correlated with the incidence of MACCE, and the higher the expression, the greater the risk increase (P < 0.05, Fig. S1). In addition, we also analyzed the association between miR-632 expression and various subtypes of MACCE (13 cases of unstable angina, 11 cases of acute myocardial infarction, 2 cases of sudden cardiac death, and 9 cases of ischemic stroke). The results showed that the expression levels of miR-632 in unstable angina, acute myocardial infarction, and ischemic stroke were significantly higher than those in the Non MACCE group; further validation through one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) and post hoc Tukey test revealed that the expression of miR-632 was significantly higher in the unstable angina group and acute myocardial infarction group than in other groups (P < 0.05), indicating the strongest association between these two types of cardiac related events and miR-632 elevation (Table S1).

Risk factors for MACCE in ESRD patients undergoing long-term hemodialysis

Univariate analysis revealed significant differences were observed between the Non-MACCE and MACCE groups in terms of age, ALB, TG, HDL-CRP, LDL-CRP, and SUA (P < 0.05, Table 2). Incorporate the significant factors identified in the aforementioned univariate analysis into a multivariate logistic regression analysis. The results showed that high expression of miR-632 (OR = 6.244, 95% CI = 2.051–19.008, P = 0.001), advanced age (OR = 4.216, 95% CI = 1.444–12.306, P = 0.008), and elevated SUA levels (OR = 4.726, 95% CI = 1.463–15.265, P = 0.002) were independent risk factors for MACCE in patients with ESRD (Table 3). ROC curve analysis showed that the AUC of miR-632 in peripheral blood for predicting MACCE in ESRD patients undergoing long-term hemodialysis was 0.787(95% CI: 0.7213–0.8528, P < 0.001), with an optimal cutoff value of 0.51. At this cutoff, the sensitivity was 55.45%, and the specificity was 95% (Fig. 4).

Target genes of miR-632

Using public databases, we performed bioinformatics predictions for miR-632 downstream targets, identifying common potential target genes (Fig. 5A). Research indicates that PKD1 may be associated with the severity of ESRD [14]. Furthermore, within TargetScan, miR-632 exhibits a highly conserved binding site within the PKD1 3’UTR (Context + + Score: -0.18), thus prompting a focus on PKD1. Determine the binding site between miR-632 and PKD1 through the Targeted Scan database (Fig. 5B). The expression level of PKD1 was significantly reduced in patients with ESRD (Fig. 5C). Pearson correlation analysis revealed that the expression level of miR-632 in the peripheral blood of ESRD patients was significantly negatively correlated with the expression level of PKD1 (r = -0.829, P < 0.0001; Fig. 5D). The dual luciferase reporter assay further validated the target relationship between the two. Results demonstrated that the miR-632 mimics significantly inhibited PKD1 WT luciferase activity, whilst the miR-632 inhibitors markedly enhanced PKD1 WT luciferase activity (Fig. S2).

Discussion

The risk of MACCE in patients with renal failure is 10 to 20 times higher than in the general population [15]. Early identification of high-risk individuals and timely intervention are crucial for improving patient outcomes. miRNA, recognized as a crucial biomarker in disease progression, has garnered widespread attention for its value in disease diagnosis and prognosis prediction.

miR-632 is considered a potential biomarker for cancer diagnosis and prognosis, playing a regulatory role in cancer progression [16–20]. Through regression tree model analysis, De et al. concluded that miR-632 is a risk factor for cardiovascular disease in ESRD patients undergoing hemodialysis, with its expression level being relatively high [11], which is consistent with the results of this study. Our study found that miR-632 is upregulated in ESRD patients undergoing long-term hemodialysis, with significant differences observed in the expression levels of ALB, HB, TG, HDL-CRP, SCR, SUA, and phosphorus. Additionally, we calculated the incidence of MACCE in ESRD patients, with an average follow-up time of 30.58 months and three-year incidence rates of 6.36%, 23.64%, and 31.82%, respectively. Previous studies have shown that miR-130a is associated with the risk of MACCE in ESRD patients undergoing peritoneal dialysis, with its expression level downregulated in MACCE patients [21]; miR-143p is linked to the risk of MACCE in ESRD patients receiving maintenance hemodialysis, and its expression level is also reduced, identifying it as a risk factor for MACCE in this population [22]. In this research, the expression level of miR-632 was significantly elevated in patients with MACCE, and its high expression was strongly associated with an increased incidence of MACCE. Elevated miR-632 expression is a risk factor for MACCE in patients with ESRD. Furthermore, miR-632 demonstrated high specificity (95%) in predicting MACCE in patients with ESRD undergoing long-term hemodialysis. However, its sensitivity (55.45%) was relatively low, indicating a potential for false-negative results. However, after controlling for confounding factors via multivariate logistic regression, miR-632 was confirmed as an independent predictor of MACCE. Furthermore, the area under the ROC curve reached 0.787, indicating its overall predictive performance is favorable. Therefore, ROC curve analysis combined with multifactorial logistic regression analysis fully demonstrates that miR-632 possesses high predictive value for the occurrence of MACCE in ESRD patients. It holds promise as a potential biomarker for identifying high-risk patients for MACCE, thereby providing a basis for early clinical intervention and reducing the incidence of MACCE.

In addition to the above research, we performed bioinformatics predictions to identify downstream targets of miR-632. Pearson correlation analysis revealed a significant negative correlation between miR-632 and PKD1, and DLR further confirmed the targeting relationship between the two. PKD1, as a significant transmembrane protein, plays a protective role in the cardiovascular system. It can maintain the integrity of the vascular endothelial barrier by regulating the proliferation and apoptosis of endothelial cells [23]. Additionally, PKD1 can participate in regulating calcium signaling in cardiomyocytes, thereby improving cardiac function [24]. When miR-632 is highly expressed, the expression of PKD1 is inhibited, and its vascular and myocardial protective effects are weakened, leading to endothelial dysfunction and impaired myocardial function, ultimately increasing the risk of MACCE. Therefore, miR-632 may influence the occurrence of MACCE in ESRD patients by targeting and inhibiting PKD1.

Long-term hemodialysis can induce endothelial damage and increase the risk of vascular complications, which is one of the primary sources of miRNA in endothelial cells [25]. Hemodialysis itself promotes atherosclerosis and left ventricular hypertrophy, whilst cardiomyocytes constitute the primary source of miRNAs regulating cardiac function [26]. Exosomes are the primary functional carriers of miRNAs. Within the cardiovascular system, exosomes are closely associated with endothelial cells, vascular cells, cardiomyocytes, and other cell types, and are implicated in the onset and progression of cardiovascular diseases [27]. Although no direct studies currently exist on the ‘site of miR-632 production in dialysis patients,’ based on the cell-specific expression patterns of miRNAs and the pathological damage observed in dialysis patients, we hypothesize that it is likely produced primarily in cells directly involved in cardiovascular protection, such as vascular endothelial cells and cardiomyocytes. miR-632 maintains stability and delivery through extracellular vesicle encapsulation, inhibits PKD1 expression, exacerbates vascular calcification and myocardial injury, and ultimately significantly increases the risk of MACCE.

However, this study has certain limitations. The recruitment center for subjects was relatively limited, which may introduce selection bias and restrict the generalizability of the results. Additionally, the follow-up period was relatively short, and there was a lack of in-depth mechanistic research and further exploration of clinical intervention strategies. In the future, the mechanisms by which miR-632, PKD1, and the miR-632/PKD1 axis modulate the progression of MACCE in ESRD require further validation through cellular functional assays and animal models. Interventional trials should be conducted to explore their clinical translational value.

Conclusion

The results of this study indicate that high expression of miR-632 is significantly associated with the occurrence of MACCE in ESRD patients undergoing long-term hemodialysis and represents a key risk factor. Additionally, miR-632 may serve as a potential biomarker for identifying patients at high risk of MACCE.

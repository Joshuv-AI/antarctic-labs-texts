Geometric wavefront sets of genuine Iwahori-spherical representations
Fan Gao, Runze Wang

Abstract
. For Iwahori-spherical genuine representations of central covers with positive real Satake parameters, we prove the upper bound inequality for their geometric wavefront sets, formulated for general genuine representations in an earlier work by Gao–Liu–Lo–Shahidi. Meanwhile, we show the equality is attained for covers of type A A groups and for some representations of covers of the exceptional groups. We also verify the equality for certain Iwahori-spherical representations occurring in regular unramified principal series; this uses and generalizes the earlier work of Karasiewicz–Okada–Wang on theta representations. Lastly, we determine the leading coefficients in the Harish-Chandra character expansion of a theta representation when its geometric wavefront set is of a special type.

Aubert–Zelevinsky duality

Let

For

2.6.
Local character expansion and wavefront sets

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of
G
.
The group
G
acts naturally on the set of nilpotent elements of
\mathfrak{g}
, and we write
\mathcal{N}(G)
for the set of orbits under this action.

For each nilpotent orbit
\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{N}(G)
, let

I_{\mathcal{O}}:C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathbbm{C}

be the associated orbital integral. Every
\pi\in{\rm Irr}_{\iota}(\overline{G})
defines a character distribution
\chi_{\pi}
in a neighborhood of
0
in
\mathfrak{g}
. There exists a compact open subset
U_{\pi}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
of 0 such that for all

we have

\chi_{\pi}(f)=\sum_{\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{N}(G)}c_{\pi}(\mathcal{O})_{\psi}\cdot\hat{I}_{\mathcal{O},\psi}(f)

with
c_{\pi}(\mathcal{O})_{\psi}\in\mathbbm{C}
, see
[
How1
,
HC99
,
Li3
]
. Here we have fixed a non-trivial additive character
\psi
of
F
, and
\hat{I}_{\mathcal{O},\psi}
denotes the Fourier transform of
I_{\mathcal{O}}
using this character.

The wavefront set of
\pi
is defined to be

{\rm WF}(\pi)=\max\left\{\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{N}(G):c_{\pi}(\mathcal{O})_{\psi}\neq 0\right\}

where the maximum is taken with respect to the topological closure ordering. Geometric nilpotent orbits are orbits under the action of
\mathbf{G}(\overline{F})
. The geometric wavefront set

\rm WF^{\rm geo}(\pi)

is defined to be the set of maximal geometric orbits (with respect to the Zariski closure) that contain a nilpotent orbit
\mathcal{O}\in{\rm WF}(\pi)
.
It is well-known that the nilpotent orbits of a connected reductive group over either
\overline{F}
or
\mathbbm{C}
are both classified by their weighted Dynkin diagrams. Thus, for a geometric nilpotent orbit over
\overline{F}
, we identify it with the corresponding nilpotent orbit over
\mathbbm{C}
.

To recall the Springer correspondence

]
, we temporarily (in this subsection) allow

to be either as before or a finite field

with

is a split connected reductive group over such

. As in §

, let

be the flag variety of all Borel subgroups of

. For a nilpotent element

There is a natural action of

we thus obtain an injective map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm Spr}:={\rm Spr}_{\bf G}:{\rm Irr}(W)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{N}({\bf G}(\overline{F}))^{\rm en}}

denoted by

we call

2.8.
Covering Barbasch–Vogan duality

For the coordinates used in the Dynkin diagram of the root system of
{\bf G}
, we follow Bourbaki’s notation
[
BouL2
]
.
For any partition
\mathfrak{p}
and
X\in\left\{B,C,D\right\}
, we use
\mathfrak{p}_{X}
to denote the
X
-collapse of
\mathfrak{p}
. Also, if we write

in the non-increasing order, then we have the two operations

\mathfrak{p}^{+}:=(p_{1}+1,p_{2},...,p_{k})\text{ and }\mathfrak{p}^{-}:=(p_{1},p_{2},...,p_{k}-1).

These operations give rise to two natural maps

(\cdot)^{-}{}_{C}:\mathcal{N}({\rm SO}_{2r+1})\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}({\rm Sp}_{2r})

and

(\cdot)^{+}{}_{B}:\mathcal{N}({\rm Sp}_{2r})\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}({\rm SO}_{2r+1}).

Now we recall the covering Barbasch–Vogan duality map

(2.7)

d_{BV,G}^{(n)}:\mathcal{N}(\overline{G}^{\vee})\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}({\bf G}(\overline{F}))

defined and studied in
[
GLLS
]
. The map
d_{BV,G}^{(n)}
is order-reversing, i.e.,
d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee})\geqslant d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{1})
if
\mathcal{O}^{\vee}\leqslant\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{1}
, see
[
GLLS
, Theorem 1.1]
. Also, for classical type it is a “deformation” of the type
A
duality map.

Type
A_{r}

Let
G

Let
\overline{G}
be an

-fold cover of

In this subsection,
{\bf G}
denotes a split group over

Let

be an irreducible representation of

. In

]

in terms of a commuting pair

up to conjugacy, where

is a semisimple element in the dual group

of

and

a special unipotent element. Let

be the centralizer of

in
G^{*}
and

be its connected component.
Let

be the Weyl group of

Set

i.e., the representation of

associated with
\mathcal{O}_{u}
and the trivial local system.

One has a canonical isomorphism

between the Weyl groups of
{\bf G}
and

. It induces a bijection between
{\rm Irr}(W)
and
{\rm Irr}(W^{*})

for their elements. This also applies to subgroups of

which correspond to each other under the above isomorphism

In particular,

corresponds to

, and

Following this convention on the notation, for the Kawanaka wavefront set of

[

(3.1)

{}^{\overline{\mathbbm{F}}_{q}}\text{WF}(\Omega)=\mathcal{O}_{\text{Spr}}(j^{W}_{W^{0}(s)}E_{u}).

We consider some special cases. First, we call
\Omega\in{\rm Irr}({\bf G}(\mathbbm{F}_{q}))
a unipotent principal series representation if
\Omega^{{\bf B}(\mathbbm{F}_{q})}\neq 0
, or equivalently,
\Omega
is an irreducible constituent of the principal series representation of
{\bf G}(\mathbbm{F}_{q})
induced from the trivial character of
{\bf B}(\mathbbm{F}_{q})
. In this case, the space
\Omega^{{\bf B}(\mathbbm{F}_{q})}
affords an irreducible representation of the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{B}(\mathbbm{F}_{q})\backslash\mathbf{G}(\mathbbm{F}_{q})/\mathbf{B}(\mathbbm{F}_{q}))

(3.2)

\mathbbm{C}[W]\rightarrow\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{B}(\mathbbm{F}_{q})\backslash\mathbf{G}(\mathbbm{F}_{q})/\mathbf{B}(\mathbbm{F}_{q}))

of algebras, the representation

gives rise to an irreducible representation of
\mathbbm{C}[W]
, which we denote by
\Omega_{q\to 1}
. By
[
Lus84-B
, Proposition 12.6]
, for such unipotent
\Omega

(3.3)

{}^{\overline{\mathbbm{F}}_{q}}\text{WF}(\Omega)=\mathcal{O}_{\text{Spr}}((\Omega_{q\rightarrow 1}\otimes\varepsilon_{W})^{\text{spe}}),

where
E^{\text{spe}}\in{\rm Irr}(W)
denotes the unique special representation in the same Lusztig family as

.

In general, let
\chi:{\bf T}(\mathbbm{F}_{q})\to\mathbbm{C}^{\times}
be a character which corresponds to an element

. It is also viewed as a character of
{\bf B}(\mathbbm{F}_{q})
trivial on the unipotent subgroup. We call
\Omega\in{\rm Irr}({\bf G}(\mathbbm{F}_{q}))
a general principal series representation if
\Omega^{({\bf B}(\mathbbm{F}_{q}),\chi)}\neq 0
for some
\chi
. For general

, one has an embedding

(3.4)

\mathbbm{C}[W^{0}(s)]\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}((\mathbf{B}(\mathbbm{F}_{q}),\chi^{-1})\backslash\mathbf{G}(\mathbbm{F}_{q})/(\mathbf{B}(\mathbbm{F}_{q}),\chi^{-1})).

Similar to the unipotent case above, a general principal series representation
\Omega
gives rise to a representation (not necessarily irreducible)
\Omega_{q\rightarrow 1}
of

.

Lemma 3.2
.

For a general principal series representation
\Omega\in{\rm Irr}({\bf G}(\mathbbm{F}_{q}))
, one has

(3.5)

^{\overline{\mathbbm{F}}_{q}}{\rm WF}(\Omega)=\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j^{W}_{W^{0}(s)}(\sigma\otimes\varepsilon)^{\rm spe}),

where

is an irreducible constituent occurring in

If

In general, we need to consider a regular embedding

\textbf{G}^{\prime}
has connected center, as in
[

]
. Let

be containing

. Let
\chi^{\prime}:\textbf{B}^{\prime}(\mathbbm{F}_{q})\rightarrow\mathbbm{C}^{\times}
be an extension of

(\lambda_{1}\cup\lambda_{2}\cup\lambda_{2}\cup...\cup\lambda_{k_{1}}\cup\lambda_{k_{1}}\cup\mu_{1}\cup\mu_{1}\cup...\cup\mu_{k_{2}-1}\cup\mu_{k_{2}-1}\cup(\mu_{k_{2}}^{-})_{C})^{\top}_{\ C}\geqslant\mathfrak{p}.

We set

\mathfrak{p}_{1}:=\lambda_{1}\cup\lambda_{2}\cup\lambda_{2}\cup...\cup\lambda_{k_{1}}\cup\lambda_{k_{1}},\quad\mathfrak{p}_{2}:=\mu_{1}\cup\mu_{1}\cup...\cup\mu_{k_{2}-1}\cup\mu_{k_{2}-1}\cup(\mu_{k_{2}}^{-})_{C}

and

\mathfrak{q}_{1}:=\lambda^{\top}_{1}\cup\lambda^{\top}_{2}\cup\lambda^{\top}_{2}\cup...\lambda^{\top}_{k_{1}}\cup\lambda^{\top}_{k_{1}},\quad\mathfrak{q}_{2}:=\mu^{\top}_{1}\cup\mu^{\top}_{1}\cup...\mu^{\top}_{k_{2}-1}\cup\mu^{\top}_{k_{2}-1}\cup((\mu_{k_{2}}^{-})_{C})^{\top}.

Since
2k_{1}-1\leqslant n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}
, we have
\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{\top}\leqslant d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}(\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{\top})
by Lemma
3.9
(i). Similarly,
\mathfrak{q}_{2}^{\top}\leqslant d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}(\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{\top})
.
This gives

\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{\top}\cup\mathfrak{q}_{2}^{\top}\leqslant d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}(\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{\top})\cup d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}(\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{\top})\leqslant d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}((\mathfrak{p}_{1}\cup\mathfrak{p}_{2})^{\top}),

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
3.9
(ii). But
d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}
is order-reversing, and thus
\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{\top}\cup\mathfrak{q}_{2}^{\top}\leqslant d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}(\mathfrak{p})
. Since
\mu_{k_{2}}
is a special partition, we have
\mu_{k_{2}}=\mu_{k_{2}}^{-}{}_{C}{}^{+}{}_{B}
; this implies that
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\vee})_{x,\chi_{i}}
is bounded above by
d^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}_{com}(\mathfrak{p})^{+}
and thus by
d^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}_{com}(\mathfrak{p})^{+}{}_{B}=d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathfrak{p})
.

For type
B_{r}
and
n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}
even, we see that
G_{Q,n}
and
G^{\vee}
are both of type
C_{r}
. In this case the root subsystem
\Phi^{Q,n}_{x,\chi_{i}}
is of the form

B_{r_{1}}+A_{r_{2}-1}+A_{r_{3}-1}+...+A_{r_{k-1}-1}+B_{r_{k}}.

Let
\Phi_{x}
be of the type
D_{m}+B_{r-m}
. In either
\Phi^{\prime}:=D_{m}
or
B_{r-m}
, we see that

2\cdot\left|\mathscr{A}(\Phi^{Q,n}_{x,\chi_{i}}\cap\Phi^{\prime})\right|+\left|\mathscr{E}(\Phi^{Q,n}_{x,\chi_{i}}\cap\Phi^{\prime})\right|\leqslant n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}.

By similar argument as above, the theorem holds for type
B_{r}
and
n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}
even.

3.6.3.
Type
C_{r}

For type
C_{r}
and odd
n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}
, the analysis is almost the same as the type
B_{r}
case, and thus we omit the details. For type
C_{r}
and
n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}
even, since
G_{Q,n}
is of type
B_{r}
, the root subsystem
\Phi^{Q,n}_{x,\chi_{i}}
is of the form

D_{r_{1}}+A_{r_{2}-1}+...+A_{r_{k-1}-1}+C_{r_{k}}.

Let
\Phi_{x}
be of type
C_{m}\times C_{r-m}
for some
m
. We assume that

\Phi^{Q,n}_{x,\chi_{i}}\simeq(\underbrace{D_{r^{\prime}_{1}}+A_{r^{\prime}_{2}-1}+...+A_{r^{\prime}_{k_{1}}-1}}_{\subseteq C_{m}})+(\underbrace{A_{r^{\prime\prime}_{1}-1}+A_{r^{\prime\prime}_{2}-1}+...+A_{r^{\prime\prime}_{k_{2}-1}-1}+C_{r^{\prime\prime}_{k_{2}}}}_{\subseteq C_{r-m}})

with
r_{1}^{\prime}=r_{1}
and
r_{k_{2}}^{\prime\prime}=r_{k}
.
We elaborate on this case, since other cases (such as when
D_{r_{1}}
and
C_{r_{k}}
are both in
C_{m}
)
are also verified by similar argument as below.

Let
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{k_{1}}
be special partitions of
D_{r_{k_{1}}^{\prime}}
and
A_{r_{i}^{\prime}-1}
’s. Let
\mu_{1},...,\mu_{k_{2}}
be special partitions for
A_{r_{i}^{\prime\prime}-1}
’s and
C_{r_{k_{2}}^{\prime\prime}}
.
Let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\vee}\in\mathcal{N}(\overline{G}^{\vee})
be an orbit with
\mathfrak{p}
being a symplectic partition.
By Proposition
3.8
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{\mathfrak{p}})_{x,\chi_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{max}\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}(\lambda^{\top}_{1}{}_{D}\cup\lambda^{\top}_{2}\cup\lambda^{\top}_{2}\cup...\lambda^{\top}_{k_{1}}\cup\lambda^{\top}_{k_{1}})^{\top}_{\ C}\cup(\mu^{\top}_{1}\cup\mu^{\top}_{1}\cup...\mu^{\top}_{k_{2}-1}\cup\mu^{\top}_{k_{2}-1}\cup\mu^{\top}_{k_{2}})^{\top}_{\ C}:\\
\bullet\ C_{\mathfrak{p}}((\lambda_{i})_{i},(\mu_{j})_{j})\end{array}\right\}

where the condition
C_{\mathfrak{p}}((\lambda_{i})_{i},(\mu_{j})_{j})
is given by

(\lambda_{1}\cup\lambda_{2}\cup\lambda_{2}\cup...\cup\lambda_{k_{1}}\cup\lambda_{k_{1}}\cup\mu_{1}\cup\mu_{1}\cup...\cup\mu_{k_{2}-1}\cup\mu_{k_{2}-1}\cup\mu_{k_{2}})^{\top}_{\ C}\geqslant\mathfrak{p}.

By Lemma
3.7
, one has

2k_{1}-1\leqslant n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2\text{ and }2k_{2}-1\leqslant n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2.

Following the same line of reasoning as in type
B_{r}
, we get

(\lambda^{\top}_{1}\cup\lambda^{\top}_{2}\cup\lambda^{\top}_{2}\cup...\lambda^{\top}_{k_{1}}\cup\lambda^{\top}_{k_{1}})^{\top}\cup(\mu^{\top}_{1}\cup\mu^{\top}_{1}\cup...\mu^{\top}_{k_{2}-1}\cup\mu^{\top}_{k_{2}-1}\cup\mu^{\top}_{k_{2}})^{\top}\leqslant d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2)}(\mathfrak{p}).

Since
\lambda_{1}
is a
D
-special partition, the partition
\lambda_{1}^{\top}
is of type
C
. Then by
[
GLLS
, Corollary 3.16]
,
\lambda_{1}{}^{\top}{}_{D}{}^{\top}\leqslant\lambda_{1}^{+-}
. We see
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\vee})_{x,\chi_{i}}
is bounded above by
d^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2)}_{com}(\mathfrak{p})^{+-}
and thus by
d^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2)}_{com}(\mathfrak{p})^{+-}{}_{C}
.
This finishes the proof if
n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2
is odd.
We observe that when
n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2
is even, the partition
d^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2)}_{com}(\mathfrak{p})
contains only even numbers; therefore,
d^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2)}_{com}(\mathfrak{p})^{+-}{}_{C}=d^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2)}_{com}(\mathfrak{p})_{C}
in this case and an upper bound of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\vee})_{x,\chi_{i}}
is also given by
d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathfrak{p})
.

3.6.4.
Type
D_{r}

For
G
of type
D_{r}
, following the same method as above, we can obtain the inequality
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{\mathfrak{p}})\leqslant d^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}_{com}(\mathfrak{p})_{D}=d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\vee})
at the partition level.

Now we consider
D_{r}
with
r
even, and
\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a very even orbit. We need to show that if the equality
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{\mathfrak{p}})=d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\vee})
holds at the partition level, then
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{\mathfrak{p}})
has the same labelling as
d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\vee})
as well. We observe that for a very even partition
\mathfrak{p}=(2l_{1},2l_{1},...,2l_{m},2l_{m})
with
l_{1}\geqslant...\geqslant l_{m}
, if
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
is a partition of
D_{r}
such that
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}>\mathfrak{p}
, then we get

\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\geqslant(2l_{1},2l_{1},...,2l_{m}+1,2l_{m}-1)=:\mathfrak{p}^{\triangle}.

But
\mathfrak{p}^{\triangle}
is not a very even partition, and thus neither is
d^{(n)}_{BV,G}(\mathfrak{p}^{\triangle})
. Hence, if the partition underlying
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{\mathfrak{p}})
is equal to that of
d^{(n)}_{BV,G}(\mathfrak{p})
, then

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{\mathfrak{p}})=d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathfrak{p})>d_{BV,D}^{(n)}(\mathfrak{p}^{\triangle})\geqslant d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})\geqslant\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}).

This shows that for
\mathfrak{p}
very even, we have

\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{p}^{\vee})=\mathcal{D}^{\flat}(\mathfrak{p}^{\vee}).

We know
\Phi^{Q,n}_{x,\chi_{i}}
is of type
D_{r_{1}}+A_{r_{2}-1}+...+A_{r_{k-1}-1}+D_{r_{k}}
. Note that

\mathcal{O}_{\text{\rm Spr}}(j^{W_{x}}_{W(\Phi^{Q,n}_{x,\chi_{i}})}\sigma^{\rm spe})\text{ and }\mathcal{O}_{\text{\rm Spr}}(j^{W}_{W(\Phi^{Q,n}_{x,\chi_{i}})}(\sigma\otimes\varepsilon)^{\text{\rm spe}})

are both very even only if
r_{1},r_{k}
are even and
4|r_{i}
for
2\leqslant r\leqslant k-1
. Then when
4|r
, we have
4|r_{1},r_{k}
or
4\nmid r_{1},r_{2}
. On the other hand, when
4\nmid r
, we have
4|r_{1},4\nmid r_{2}
or
4\nmid r_{1},4|r_{2}
. For type
D_{r}
, if
\pi
is a special representation corresponding to a very even partition, then the nilpotent orbit associated with
\pi\otimes\varepsilon
has the same labelling I or II (following the convention in
[
BMW25
]
) as that with
\pi
when
4|r
, and has different labelling when
4\nmid r
. We write

\sigma^{\text{spe}}=\sigma_{1}\boxtimes...\boxtimes\sigma_{k}\in{\rm Irr}(W(\Phi_{x,\chi_{i}}^{Q,n})),

where
\sigma_{i}
is a special irreducible representation of the Weyl group of the
i
-th component in
D_{r_{1}}+A_{r_{2}-1}+...+A_{r_{k-1}-1}+D_{r_{k}}
. This gives

(\sigma\otimes\varepsilon)^{\text{spe}}=\sigma^{\text{spe}}\otimes\varepsilon=(\sigma_{1}\otimes\varepsilon)\boxtimes...\boxtimes(\sigma_{k}\otimes\varepsilon).

We see that when
4|r
, the nilpotent orbits associated with
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{1}\otimes\varepsilon
have the same labellings if and only if the orbits associated with
\sigma_{k}
and
\sigma_{k}\otimes\varepsilon
have the same labellings. On the other hand, if
4\nmid r
, then the orbits associated with
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{1}\otimes\varepsilon
have the same labellings if and only if the orbits associated with
\sigma_{k}
and
\sigma_{k}\otimes\varepsilon
possess different labellings.

In general,
W_{x}
is of type
D_{m_{1}}+D_{m_{2}}\subseteq D_{r}
. Let
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\times\mathfrak{p}_{2}
be the partition underlying
\mathcal{O}_{\text{\rm Spr}}(j^{W_{x}}_{W(\Phi^{Q,n}_{x,\chi_{i}})}(\sigma\otimes\varepsilon)^{\text{\rm spe}})
. Assume that
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
are both very even. We divide the four possible labellings of
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\times\mathfrak{p}_{2}
into two groups
\left\{\text{(I,I),(II,II)}\right\}
and
\left\{\text{(I,II),(II,I)}\right\}
and call each group an equivalence classes of labellings for
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\times\mathfrak{p}_{2}
. Assume the equality

\mathcal{O}_{\text{\rm Spr}}(j^{W}_{W(\Phi^{Q,n}_{x,\chi_{i}})}(\sigma\otimes\varepsilon)^{\text{\rm spe}})=\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{\mathfrak{p}}

holds, then the labelling of
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\times\mathfrak{p}_{2}

\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{\mathfrak{p}}
up to an equivalence class. It follows from the argument in the preceding paragraph that the labellings for

\mathcal{O}_{\text{\rm Spr}}(j^{W_{x}}_{W(\Phi^{Q,n}_{x,\chi_{i}})}\sigma^{\rm spe})\text{ and }\mathcal{O}_{\text{\rm Spr}}(j^{W_{x}}_{W(\Phi^{Q,n}_{x,\chi_{i}})}(\sigma\otimes\varepsilon)^{\text{\rm spe}})

\text{\rm Sat}^{\mathbf{G}(\overline{F})}_{\mathbf{L}_{x}^{F}(\overline{F})}(\mathcal{O}_{\text{\rm Spr}}(j^{W_{x}}_{W(\Phi^{Q,n}_{x,\chi_{i}})}\sigma^{\rm spe}))\text{ and }\mathcal{O}_{\text{\rm Spr}}(j^{W}_{W(\Phi^{Q,n}_{x,\chi_{i}})}(\sigma\otimes\varepsilon)^{\text{\rm spe}})

have the same labellings if and only if
4|r
. This is compatible with the definition of
d_{BV,G}^{(n)}
for type
D
in (
2.13
).

The above completes the proof for type
D_{r}
and also the verification of Theorem
3.5
for
G
of classical type.

\bullet

\bullet

\bullet

Third, for a representation

and again use “PyCox” to compute the

\bullet

Finally, we compute the saturation by using the tables extracted from

). Taking maximum over

.

3.8.
Upper bound and some equalities

Theorem
3.5
is pivotal in giving us the upper bound of
{\rm WF}^{\rm geo}({\rm AZ}(\pi))
for
\pi\in{\rm Irr}_{\iota}(\overline{G})^{I}
.

Corollary 3.10
.

Assume
p
is sufficiently large.
Let
\pi(s,u,\tau)\in{\rm Irr}_{\iota}(\overline{G})^{I}
be with positive real
s
.

(i)

One has

\text{\rm WF}^{\text{\rm geo}}(\text{\rm AZ}(\pi(s,u,\tau)))\leqslant d^{(n)}_{BV,G}(\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee}),

where
\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{u}\in\mathcal{N}(\overline{G}^{\vee})
is the orbit associated with
u
. Moreover, if
G
is of type
A
, then the equality holds.

(ii)

Assume
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee})<\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}_{u^{\prime}}^{\vee})
for every
\mathcal{O}_{u^{\prime}}^{\vee}>\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee}
. Then

\mathcal{D}^{\flat}(\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee})=\text{\rm WF}^{\text{\rm geo}}(\text{\rm AZ}(\pi(s,u,\mathbbm{1})))=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee}).

If particular, if
G
is of exceptional type and the orbit in
\mathcal{N}({\bf G}(\overline{F}))
, which canonically corresponds to
\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee}
via weighted Dynkin diagrams, lies in
{\rm Im}(d_{BV,G}^{(n)})
, then we have the equality

\text{\rm WF}^{\text{\rm geo}}(\text{\rm AZ}(\pi(s,u,\mathbbm{1})))=d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee}).

Proof.

For (i), we have the chain of inequalities

(3.24)

\text{\rm WF}^{\text{\rm geo}}(\text{\rm AZ}(\pi(s,u,\tau)))\leqslant\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{u})\leqslant d^{(n)}_{BV,G}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{u}),

where the first inequality follows from Lemma
3.4
and the second one from Theorem
3.5
.

For the second statement in (i), we assume
G
is of type
A
. To show the equality holds, in view of Theorem
3.5
, it suffices to show
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee})\leqslant{\rm WF}^{\text{\rm geo}}(\text{\rm AZ}(\pi(s,u,\tau)))
.
From the equality
\mathcal{D}=d_{BV,A}^{(n)}
in Theorem
3.5
and also Lemma
3.9
, we see

(3.25)

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee})=\mathcal{D}^{\flat}(\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee}).

For fixed
\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee}\in\mathcal{N}(\overline{G}^{\vee})
, if
\sigma\in{\rm Irr}(W(\Phi_{x,\chi_{i}}^{Q,n}))
satisfies

\mathcal{O}_{\text{\rm Spr}}(j^{\vartheta^{\vee}(W)}_{\vartheta^{\vee}(W(\Phi^{Q,n}_{x,\chi_{i}}))}(\vartheta^{\vee}(\sigma)\otimes\varepsilon))=\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee}

as in the definition of
\mathcal{D}^{\flat}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{u})
, then clearly
\sigma\subseteq{\rm Res}_{W(\Phi_{x,\chi_{i}}^{Q,n})}^{W}(\vartheta^{\vee}(E_{u,\mathbbm{1}})\otimes\varepsilon)
. By the description of
{\rm Irr}(W)_{s,u,\tau}
in (
3.14
), we have
\vartheta^{\vee}(E_{u,\mathbbm{1}})\in{\rm Irr}(W)_{s,u,\tau}
(since we must have
\tau=\mathbbm{1}
in this case); thus, every such
\sigma

belongs to the set in the right hand side of the equality of computing
{\rm WF}^{\text{\rm geo}}(\text{\rm AZ}(\pi(s,u,\tau)))
, as given in Proposition
3.3
. This shows
\mathcal{D}^{\flat}(\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee})\leqslant{\rm WF}^{\text{\rm geo}}(\text{\rm AZ}(\pi(s,u,\tau)))
and completes the proof of the second statement of (i) in view of (
3.25
).

For (ii), we have the chain of inequalities

\mathcal{D}^{\flat}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{u})\leqslant\text{\rm WF}^{\text{\rm geo}}(\text{\rm AZ}(\pi(s,u,\mathbbm{1})))\leqslant\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{u}),

where the first inequality follows from Proposition
3.3
. The assumption in (ii) implies that
\mathcal{D}^{\flat}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{u})=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{u})
, which gives the chain of equalities in (ii). For the second assertion in (ii), let
G
be of exceptional type with the assumption installed. In view of the first statement in (ii) and Theorem
3.5
, it suffices to show that for such
\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee}
one has

d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee})<d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}_{u^{\prime}}^{\vee})

for every
\mathcal{O}_{u^{\prime}}^{\vee}>\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee}
. However, this follows from a direct inspection of the definition of
d_{BV,G}^{(n)}
from
[
GLLS
, Appendix]
. This completes the proof of (ii).
∎

Note that the argument for the second statement in Corollary
3.10
(ii) also applies for type
A
. That is, if
G
is of type
A
and
\mathfrak{p}\in{\rm Im}(d_{BV,G}^{(n)})
, then
d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathfrak{p})<d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})

for every
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}>\mathfrak{p}
. Indeed, from the combinatorial definition of
d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}
and thus
d_{BV,G}^{(n)}
, it is easy to see that

(d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})})^{2}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})\geqslant\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\text{ and }(d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})})^{3}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})=d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})

for all partition
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
. This gives
(d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})})^{2}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})\geqslant\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}>\mathfrak{p}
. Then, we necessarily have

d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})<d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}(\mathfrak{p}).

Otherwise, it gives the equality
d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})=d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}(\mathfrak{p})
and implies that
(d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})})^{2}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})=(d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})})^{2}(\mathfrak{p})=\mathfrak{p}
, since
\mathfrak{p}\in{\rm Im}(d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})})
; this is a contradiction.

We have shown that
\text{\rm WF}^{\rm geo}(\text{\rm AZ}(\pi(s,u,\tau)))\leqslant d^{(n)}_{BV,G}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{u})
for positive real
s
. The equality holds for linear groups by
[
CMBO21
]
; for covers, it holds for the theta representations by
[
KOW
]
, for type
A
, and for certain orbits
\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee}
in the case of exceptional groups by Corollary
3.10
. However, the equality is not always attained for general Iwahori-spherical representations of
\overline{G}
, even just for positive real
s
.

.

\overline{G}=\overline{{\rm Sp}}_{6}^{(3)}

\overline{G}^{\vee}={\rm SO}_{7}

\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee}

\mathfrak{p}:=(3,3,1)

A_{s,u}=A_{\mathcal{O}_{u}}

has nontrivial
\tau

d^{(3)}_{BV,{\rm Sp}_{6}}(\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee})=(6)=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee}_{u})
but
\text{\rm WF}^{\rm geo}(\text{\rm AZ}(\pi(s,u,\tau)))=(4,2)
.

Second, for a primitive cover
\overline{G}_{2}^{(2)}
of
G_{2}
. Consider
\pi(s,u,\mathbbm{1})
with positive real
s
and
\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee}=A_{1}
, and
\pi(s,u,\mathbbm{1})^{I}
is an one-dimensional module over
\mathcal{H}_{0}
. Then
d_{BV,G_{2}}^{(2)}(\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee})=G_{2}=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee})
but
\text{\rm WF}^{\rm geo}(\text{\rm AZ}(\pi(s,u,\mathbbm{1})))=G_{2}(a_{1})
.

3.9.
Lower bound and a speculation

First, we show that for positive real
s
there is always a coarse lower bound of the geometric wavefront set
{\rm WF}^{\rm geo}(\text{AZ}(\pi(s,u,\tau)))

given by that of theta representations. Indeed, for every
\sigma\in{\rm Irr}(W(\Phi_{x,\chi_{i}}^{Q,n}))
, it follows from
[
AcAu07
, Proposition 4.3]
that

\mathcal{O}_{\text{\rm Spr}}(j^{W_{x}}_{W(\Phi^{Q,n}_{x,\chi_{i}})}(\sigma^{\rm spe}))\geqslant\mathcal{O}_{\text{\rm Spr}}(j^{W_{x}}_{W(\Phi^{Q,n}_{x,\chi_{i}})}\varepsilon).

Notice that if
\text{AZ}(\pi(s,u,\tau))
is a theta representation (i.e.,
\pi(s,u,\tau)
is a covering Steinberg representation), then
{\rm Irr}(W)_{s,u,\tau}=\left\{\varepsilon\right\}
is a singleton set, where
\varepsilon
is just the sign representation of
W(\Phi^{Q,n}_{x,\chi_{i}})
, as shown in
[
KOW
]
. Thus the theta representation has the minimal geometric wavefront set among the representations considered in Proposition
3.3
. This is a generalization of the fact that theta representation has the least Whittaker dimension among all representations in
{\rm Irr}_{\iota}(\overline{G})^{I}
, see
[
GGK1
, Corollary 9.9]
.

In general, one has the following speculation, which is supported by the numerical evidence so far.

Conjecture 3.12
.

Let
\pi(s,u,\tau)\in{\rm Irr}_{\iota}(\overline{G})^{I}
be such that
s
is positive real. Then the following holds:

(i)

either
{\rm WF}^{\text{\rm geo}}(\text{\rm AZ}(\pi(s,u,\tau)))=d^{(n)}_{BV,G}(\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee})
, or

(ii)

{\rm WF}^{\text{\rm geo}}(\text{\rm AZ}(\pi(s,u,\tau)))
is equal to a maximal orbit in the set

\left\{\mathcal{O}\in{\rm Im}(d_{BV,G}^{(n)}):\ \mathcal{O}<d^{(n)}_{BV,G}(\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee})\right\};

that is,
{\rm WF}^{\text{\rm geo}}(\text{\rm AZ}(\pi(s,u,\tau)))
is at most “one-step” smaller from
d^{(n)}_{BV,G}(\mathcal{O}_{u}^{\vee})
within the image of
d_{BV,G}^{(n)}
.

4.
The equality for certain
\pi_{S}

In this section, we generalize the results of
[
KOW
]
concerning
{\rm WF}^{\rm geo}(\Theta)
in another direction. Following the exposition and notation from
[
GaTs
]
, we consider
\nu\in X\otimes\mathbbm{R}
that satisfies the following:

–

\nu
is regular, that is, its stabilizer subgroup of
W
is trivial;

–

the set
\Phi(\nu):=\left\{\alpha\in\Phi:\nu(\alpha_{Q,n}^{\vee})=1\right\}
is a subset of
\Delta
.

Taking
\pi^{\dagger}
to be an unramified distinguished genuine representation of
\overline{T}
, we have a regular unramified genuine principal series
I(\nu):=I(\pi^{\dagger},\nu)
of
\overline{G}
.
One has

(4.1)

I(\nu)^{\rm ss}=\bigoplus_{S\subseteq\Phi(\nu)}\pi_{S},

where the left hand side denotes the semisimplification of
I(\nu)
. The decomposition is multiplicity-free and the irreducible constituent
\pi_{S}
is characterized by its Jacquet module, see
[
Rod4
]
and
[
Ga6
, §3]
. For example, if
\Phi(\nu)=\Delta
, then
\pi_{\Delta}=\Theta(\nu)
is an unramified theta representation and
\pi_{\emptyset}
is a covering Steinberg representation.

For every
S\subseteq\Phi(\nu)
, let
\Phi(S)\subseteq\Phi
be the root subsystem with simple roots. Denote by

W(S)\subseteq W

the subgroup generated by simple reflections from
S
. Let
{\bf M}_{S}\subseteq{\bf G}
be the Levi subgroup associated with
S
. Denote

W_{\nu}^{S}:=\text{the integral Weyl subgroup of $W(S)$ generated by }\left\{\alpha\in\Phi(S):\ {\left\langle\alpha^{\vee},\nu\right\rangle}\in\mathbbm{Z}\right\}.

Let
\varepsilon_{\nu}^{S}
be the sign character of
W_{\nu}^{S}
.

With all the above notation, we expect that

(4.2)

{\rm WF}^{\rm geo}(\pi_{S})=\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j_{W_{\nu}^{S}}^{W}(\varepsilon_{\nu}^{S})).

By using properties of
j
-induction and the induction of orbits, as noted in
[
GaTs
]
, we get

\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j_{W_{\nu}^{S}}^{W}(\varepsilon_{\nu}^{S}))={\rm Ind}_{{\bf M}_{S}}^{{\bf G}}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j_{W_{\nu}^{S}}^{W(S)}(\varepsilon_{\nu}^{S}))).

However, from
[
GLLS
]
we know

\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j_{W_{\nu}^{S}}^{W(S)}(\varepsilon_{\nu}^{S}))=d_{BV,M_{S}}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee,S}_{\rm reg})),

where we denote by
\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S}
the regular orbit of
\overline{M}_{S}^{\vee}
.
Also, for every orbit
\mathcal{O}^{\vee}
of
\overline{M}_{S}^{\vee}
, the covering Barbasch–Vogan duality intertwins the induction and saturation of orbits as in

d_{BV,G}^{(n)}\circ{\rm Sat}_{\overline{M}_{S}^{\vee}}^{\overline{G}^{\vee}}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee})={\rm Ind}_{{\bf M}_{S}}^{\bf G}\circ d_{BV,M_{S}}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee}),

see
[
GLLS
, Theorem 1.1]
.
We have
\mathcal{O}(\phi_{\pi_{\Phi(\nu)-S}})={\rm Sat}_{\overline{M}_{S}^{\vee}}^{\overline{G}^{\vee}}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee,S}_{\rm reg})
and also

{\rm AZ}(\pi_{S})=\pi_{\Phi(\nu)-S}
, which gives

\mathcal{O}(\phi_{{\rm AZ}(\pi_{S})})={\rm Sat}_{\overline{M}_{S}^{\vee}}^{\overline{G}^{\vee}}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S}).

Combining all the above, we see that

(4.3)

\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j_{W_{\nu}^{S}}^{W}(\varepsilon_{\nu}^{S}))=d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S})=d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}(\phi_{{\rm AZ}(\pi_{S})})),

where henceforth, by abuse of notation, we also write
\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S}
for the saturated orbit
{\rm Sat}_{\overline{M}_{S}^{\vee}}^{\overline{G}^{\vee}}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S})
for
\overline{G}^{\vee}
, and there should be no risk of confusion from the context.
In particular, we see that (
4.2
) is equivalent to

(4.4)

{\rm WF}^{\rm geo}(\pi_{S})=d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S}).

The equality (
4.2
) (or (
4.4
)) was known in the following cases:

\bullet

For linear group
G
(i.e.,

{\rm WF}^{\rm geo}(\pi_{S})={\rm Ind}_{{\bf M}_{S}}^{\bf G}(0)=d_{BV,G}^{(1)}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S})

\bullet

4.2.
Proof for classical types

For every partition
\mathfrak{m}=(m_{1},...,m_{s})
with
s\geqslant 2
and
m_{i}\in\mathbbm{N}_{\geqslant 1}
, we write
\left|\mathfrak{m}\right|=\sum_{i=1}^{s}m_{i}
. We first state a basic but key property of the functions
\mathfrak{s}(-;z)
and
d_{com}^{(z)}(-)
given in (
2.8
).

Lemma 4.4
.

Keep the notation as above. Then

d_{com}^{(z)}(\left|\mathfrak{m}\right|)=\mathfrak{s}(\left|\mathfrak{m}\right|;z)\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{s}\mathfrak{s}(m_{i};z)=d_{com}^{(z)}(\mathfrak{m}).

Moreover, if there is a non-empty subset
J\subseteq\left\{1,2,...,s\right\}
such that
z<\sum_{i\in J}m_{i}
, then the above inequality is strict.

Proof.

The two equalities hold by the definition of
d_{com}^{(z)}
. The middle inequality follows from the order-reversing property of
d_{com}^{(z)}
, since
\left|\mathfrak{m}\right|\geqslant\mathfrak{m}
as partitions. For the second assertion, the assumption implies that the leading numeral of the partition
d_{com}^{(z)}(\mathfrak{m})
is bounded below by that of
\sum_{i\in J}\mathfrak{s}(m_{i};z)
, and is
\geqslant z+1
. However, the leading numeral of
\mathfrak{s}(\left|\mathfrak{m}\right|;z)
is equal to
z
. This gives the strict inequality.
∎

d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{q}}})<d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{p}}})
, and in this case

L^{\sharp}(d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{q}}}))
is odd, and

L^{\sharp}(d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{q}}}))+1\leqslant L^{\sharp}(d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}})}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{p}}})).

Thus, it is easy to see
d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{q}}})<d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{p}}})
holds as well.

Second, if
n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}
is even, then
\overline{G}^{\vee}
is of type
C_{r}
. The map
d_{BV,G}^{(n)}
is given in (
2.11
).
The regular orbit of
\overline{M}_{S_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\vee}\subseteq\overline{G}^{\vee}
is
\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{p}}}=(p_{1}^{2},...,p_{s-1}^{2},2p_{s})
. Similarly,
\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{q}}}=(q_{1}^{2},...,q_{s-2}^{2},2q_{s-1})
.
If
j_{0}\leqslant s-2
, then property (P) implies
n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}<p_{i_{0}}+p_{i_{0}+1}
. Thus,
n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2<p_{i_{0}}+p_{i_{0}+1}
. Similar as the
n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}
odd case above,
we get

d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2)}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{q}}})<d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2)}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{p}}})\text{ with }L^{\sharp}(d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2)}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{q}}}))+2\leqslant L^{\sharp}(d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2)}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{p}}})),

and easily deduce
d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{q}}})<d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{p}}})
.
If
j_{0}=s-1
, then we get
q_{s-1}=p_{s-1}+p_{s}
. Property (P) implies
n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}<2p_{s}+p_{s-1}+p_{s-1}
and thus
n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2<2p_{s}+p_{s-1}+p_{s-1}
. Lemma
4.4
gives that
d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2)}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{q}}})<d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2)}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{p}}})
. Note that if
2p_{s}\geqslant n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2
or
p_{s-1}\geqslant n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2
, we get

L^{\sharp}(d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2)}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{q}}}))+2\leqslant L^{\sharp}(d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2)}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{p}}})).

On the other hand, if
2p_{s}<n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2
and
p_{s-1}<n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2
, then

L^{\sharp}(d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2)}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{p}}}))-L^{\sharp}(d_{com}^{(n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2)}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{q}}}))=2p_{s}+2p_{s-1}-n_{\boldsymbol{\varkappa}}/2\geqslant 2.

In view of these two inequalities and (
2.11
), we get
d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{q}}})<d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}^{\vee,S_{\mathfrak{p}}})
as well.

The above verifies Proposition
4.3
and Theorem
4.2
for type
C_{r}
.

4.2.4.
Type
D_{r}

For
\overline{G}
of type
D_{r}
, at the partition level, the inequality in Proposition
4.3
follows from a parallel argument as in the
B_{r},C_{r}
case. We omit the details.

It suffices to remark on the case concerning very even orbits. However, suppose
\mathfrak{p}
or
\mathfrak{q}
(possibly both) is a very even partition associated with
S_{\mathfrak{p}}\subsetneq S_{\mathfrak{q}}
respectively. If they are very even, then we have two orbits
\mathfrak{p}^{\rm I},\mathfrak{p}^{\rm II}
and
\mathfrak{q}^{\rm I},\mathfrak{q}^{\rm II}
. However, since as partitions, we already have
d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathfrak{q})<d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathfrak{p})
, it follows that for any
\flat,\sharp\in\left\{\emptyset,{\rm I},{\rm II}\right\}
one has (see
[
CM93
, Theorem 6.2.5]
)

d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathfrak{q}^{\flat})<d_{BV,G}^{(n)}(\mathfrak{p}^{\sharp})

as desired. Here
\mathfrak{p}^{\emptyset}
just means that
\mathfrak{p}
is not very even, and it represents the unique orbit associated with
\mathfrak{p}
; similarly for
\mathfrak{q}
.

5.
Leading coefficient
c_{\Theta}(\mathcal{O})_{\psi}
for theta representation

In this section, we consider a theta representation
\Theta:=\Theta(\overline{G}^{(n)}):=\Theta(\nu)
of
\overline{G}^{(n)}
and study the coefficient
c_{\Theta}(\mathcal{O})_{\psi}
for
\mathcal{O}\in{\rm WF}(\Theta)
that arises from the Harish-Chandra character expansion of
\Theta
. Here
\psi
is a non-trivial character of
F
. The involved measures are naturally chosen as in
[
MW87
,
Var14
,
Pate
]
, and thus
c_{\Theta}(\mathcal{O})_{\psi}
is equal to the dimension of certain degenerate Whittaker models of
\Theta
. Henceforth, we utilize such an identification.
A speculative formula for
c_{\Theta}(\mathcal{O})_{\psi}
was given in
[
GaTs
,
GLT25
]
for certain “persistent” cover
\overline{G}^{(n)}
. In this section, we verify the formula in some special cases. However, we also give examples illustrating that the original formula demands a modification in general.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\overline{G}^{(n)}
be the cover of an almost simple and simply-connected
G
associated with
Q
such that
Q(\alpha^{\vee})=1
for any short simple coroot
\alpha^{\vee}
.
We call
\overline{G}^{(n)}
of
\diamondsuit
-type if it is one of the following:

\bullet

\overline{{\rm SL}}_{r+1}^{(n)}
with
\gcd(n,r+1)=1
;

\bullet

\overline{{\rm Spin}}_{2r+1}^{(n)},\overline{{\rm Sp}}_{2r}^{(n)},\overline{{\rm Spin}}_{2r}^{(n)}
with
n
odd;

\bullet

\overline{G}_{2}^{(n)}
with any
n
;

\bullet

\overline{F}_{4}^{(n)},\overline{E}_{6}^{(n)},\overline{E}_{7}^{(n)}
with
2,3\nmid n
;

\bullet

\overline{E}_{8}^{(n)}
with
2,3,5\nmid n
.

Such a cover is necessarily a primitive cover.

Recall that the geometric wavefront orbit of
\Theta(\overline{G}^{(n)})

is equal to
\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon)
. For
\diamondsuit
-type covers of classical groups of type
X
, we have

\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon)=\mathcal{O}_{X}^{k,n},

where
k=2r+1,2r,2r
for
G={\rm Spin}_{2r+1},{\rm Sp}_{2r},{\rm Spin}_{2r}
respectively, and
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{k,n}
is the
X
-collapse of the partition
\mathcal{O}^{k,n}:=(n^{a}b)
with
k=an+b,0\leqslant b<n
. See
[
GLT25
,
KOW
]
for more details.

Recall
\mathscr{X}:=Y/Y_{Q,n}
from (
2.1
). Since we have fixed
Q
, we write
\mathscr{X}_{n}:=\mathscr{X}
to highlight the dependence on
n
. Let

\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}:W\to{\rm Perm}(\mathscr{X}_{n})

be the permutation representation of
W
induced from the action
w(x),w\in W,x\in Y
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\overline{G}^{(n)}
be a covering group of
\diamondsuit
-type. Assume
p
is sufficiently large, and that the geometric wavefront orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon_{W_{\nu}})
of
\Theta(\overline{G}^{(n)})
is the regular orbit of a Levi subgroup; we further assume that
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{k,n}=\mathcal{O}^{k,n}
if
X\in\left\{B,C\right\}
and that
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{2r,n}=(n^{2a+1},1)
if
X=D
. Then one has

c_{\Theta}(\mathcal{O})_{\psi}={\left\langle j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon_{W_{\nu}},\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\otimes\varepsilon_{W}\right\rangle}_{W}

for every
F
-rational orbit
\mathcal{O}
in
{\rm WF}(\Theta(\overline{G}^{(n)}))
.

5.1.
Method of computation

We explain the main ideas and steps in the proof of Theorem
5.2
.
Let
\mathcal{O}
be in
{\rm WF}(\Theta(\overline{G}^{(n)}))
as in Theorem
5.2
. Then
\mathcal{O}
is a regular orbit of a Levi subgroup
M_{\mathcal{O}}\subseteq G
, which actually only depends on
\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon_{W_{\nu}})
, i.e., the geometric closure of
\mathcal{O}
.
By the result
[
GGS21
, Theorem 1.5]
of Gomez–Gourevitich–Sahi, we have

c_{\Theta}(\mathcal{O})_{\psi}=\dim{\rm Wh}_{\psi_{\mathcal{O}}}({\rm Jac}_{M_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Theta)),

i.e., it is equal to the dimension of
\psi_{\mathcal{O}}
-Whittaker space of the Jacquet module of
\Theta
with respect to
M_{\mathcal{O}}
. Here
\psi_{\mathcal{O}}
is a non-degenerate character of the unipotent subgroup of the Borel subgroup of
M_{\mathcal{O}}
, defined using
\psi
and
\mathcal{O}
. By the periodicity of theta representation, we get
{\rm Jac}_{M_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Theta)
is equal to a theta representation
\Theta(\overline{M}_{\mathcal{O}}^{(n)})
of
\overline{M}_{\mathcal{O}}^{(n)}
. Now, by result of
[
Ga6
]
and
[
GGK1
, Proposition 9.2]
, we have

(5.1)

\dim{\rm Wh}_{\psi_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Theta(\overline{M}_{\mathcal{O}}^{(n)}))={\left\langle\varepsilon_{M_{\mathcal{O}}},\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\right\rangle}_{W(M_{\mathcal{O}})}.

Thus, the key is to verify the equality

(5.2)

{\left\langle\varepsilon_{W}\otimes j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon_{W_{\nu}},\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\right\rangle}_{W}={\left\langle\varepsilon_{M_{\mathcal{O}}},\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\right\rangle}_{W(M_{\mathcal{O}})}

for such
\Theta(\overline{G}^{(n)})
. We compute the left hand side of (
5.2
) by using the result of Gyoja–Nishiyama–Shimura
[
GNS99
]
, and for the right hand side we use the result of Sommers
[
Som97
]
.
For
\overline{G}_{2}^{(n)}
, one can verify (
5.2
) directly. For the rest of this subsection, we assume
\overline{G}^{(n)}
is of
\diamondsuit
-type except when
G=G_{2},2|n
or
3|n
.
These conditions on
n
for
\diamondsuit
-type covers are imposed so that we could apply
[
GNS99
,
Som97
]
directly. In fact, we will obtain explicit formulas for the two sides of (
5.2
). We elaborate this below.

First, for
\overline{G}^{(n)}
of
\diamondsuit
-type (except when
G=G_{2},2|n
or
3|n
) and for every
\phi\in{\rm Irr}(W)
, one has

(5.3)

{\left\langle\phi,\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\right\rangle}_{W}=\frac{\chi_{\phi}(1)}{\left|W\right|}\cdot\tau^{*}(\phi,n),

where
\chi_{\phi}
is the character of
\phi
and
\tau^{*}(\phi,n)
is investigated in
[
GNS99
]
, especially regarding the question of whether
\tau^{*}(\phi,n)
distinguishes Lusztig’s families of
\phi\in{\rm Irr}(W)
. Also, for classical types, the number
\chi_{\phi}(1)=\dim\phi
can be computed by using the hook formula.

On the other hand, the Levi subgroup
M_{\mathcal{O}}
is associated with a subset
J\subseteq\Delta
, and thus we write
W_{J}:=W(M_{\mathcal{O}})
. For
w\in W
, denote

d(w):=\dim(Y\otimes\mathbbm{R})^{w}.

Let
m_{1},...,m_{\left|J\right|}
be the exponents of the Weyl group
W_{J}
. It follows from
[
Som97
]
that for all
\overline{G}^{(n)}
of
\diamondsuit
-type (except when
G=G_{2},2|n
or
3|n
) we have

(5.4)

\displaystyle{\left\langle\varepsilon_{W_{J}},\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\right\rangle}_{W_{J}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left|W_{J}\right|}\sum_{w\in W_{J}}\varepsilon_{W_{J}}(w)\cdot n^{d(w)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{r}}{\left|W_{J}\right|}\sum_{w\in W_{J}}(-n)^{d(w)}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{r}\cdot\chi(\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{-n}^{J})

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{r-\left|J\right|}}{\left|W_{J}\right|}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{\left|J\right|}(n-m_{j})

where
\chi(\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{t}^{J})
is the characteristic of the affine Springer fiber
\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{t}^{J}
attached to
t
and
J
as in
[
Som97
]
.

5.2.
Detailed proof of Theorem
5.2

We first look at the two extreme cases of Theorem
5.2
: for
\overline{G}^{(n)}
of
\diamondsuit
-type, if
n=1
or
n\in\mathbbm{N}
is such that
{\rm WF}^{\rm geo}(\Theta)=\mathcal{O}_{\rm reg}
, then the equality
c_{\Theta}(\mathcal{O})_{\psi}={\left\langle j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon_{W_{\nu}},\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\otimes\varepsilon_{W}\right\rangle}_{W}
holds. Indeed, the
n=1
case is trivial and the second case follows from (
5.1
).

We verify (
5.2
) case by case. In view of the preceding observation, we only need to discuss the case when
\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon)
is nonzero and is the regular orbit of a proper Levi subgroup of
G
.

5.2.1.
Type
A_{r}

Consider
\overline{G}^{(n)}=\overline{{\rm SL}}_{r+1}^{(n)}
with
\gcd(r+1,n)=1
. Let
\lambda
be a partition of
k\in\mathbbm{N}_{\geqslant 1}
and let
\phi(\lambda)\in{\rm Irr}(S_{k})
be the associated representation. In particular,
\phi(k)=\mathbbm{1}
and
\phi(1^{k})=\varepsilon
.
The dimension of
\phi(\lambda)
is given by the hook formula (see
[
Sag01
, Theorem 3.10.2]
):

(5.5)

\dim\phi(\lambda)=\frac{k!}{\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}h_{i,j}},

where we view
\lambda
as a Young diagram and
h_{i,j}:=h(i,j)
denotes the hook function of
(i,j)
.

We have

j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon_{W_{\nu}}=\phi(n^{a}b),

where
r+1=an+b,0\leqslant b<n
. We get

\varepsilon_{W}\otimes j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon_{W_{\nu}}=\phi((a+1)^{b},a^{n-b}).

It follows from (
5.3
) that (setting
\phi(\lambda):=\phi((a+1)^{b},a^{n-b})
)

\displaystyle{\left\langle\phi(\lambda),\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\right\rangle}_{W}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau^{*}(\phi(\lambda),n)}{\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}h_{i,j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{-1}\cdot\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}(n+c_{i,j})}{\left(\prod_{i=1}^{b}\prod_{j=1}^{a+1}(n-i+j)\right)\cdot\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-b}\prod_{j=1}^{a}(n-b-i+j)\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)...(n-(b-1))}{b!},

where the second equality above follows from
[
GNS99
, Proposition 3.1]
with
c_{i,j}:=j-i
denoting the content of the
(i,j)
entry of
\lambda
.

On the other hand,
M_{\mathcal{O}}
is associated with the partition
(n^{a},b)
, and thus is of type

aA_{n-1}+A_{b-1}.

It then follows from (
5.4
) that

{\left\langle\varepsilon_{W_{J}},\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\right\rangle}_{W_{J}}=\frac{n^{r-a(n-1)-(b-1)}}{(n!)^{a}\cdot b!}\cdot\left((n-1)!)^{a}\right)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{b-1}(n-i),

which is easily seen to be equal to
{\left\langle\phi(\lambda),\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\right\rangle}_{W}
above. This completes the proof of Theorem
5.2
for type
A_{r}
.

5.2.2.
Type
C_{r}

We consider
\overline{G}^{(n)}=\overline{{\rm Sp}}_{2r}^{(n)}
with
n=2m+1
odd.
Since we need
\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon)
to be the regular orbit of a Levi subgroup and also
\mathcal{O}_{C}^{2r,n}=\mathcal{O}^{2r,n}
, it follows from
[
GLT25
]
that we must have

r=an+b\text{ with }a\geqslant 1,0\leqslant b\leqslant\frac{n-1}{2},

and in this case

j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon_{W_{\nu}}=\phi((m^{a},b);(m+1)^{a}),\quad\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon)=(n^{2a},2b)=\mathcal{O}^{2r,n}.

This gives

\varepsilon_{W}\otimes j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon_{W_{\nu}}=\phi(a^{m+1};((a+1)^{b},a^{m-b}))=:\phi(\lambda;\mu).

The hook formula for
W(C_{r})
gives that

\dim\phi(\lambda;\mu)=\frac{r!}{\prod_{x^{\prime}\in\lambda}h(x^{\prime})\cdot\prod_{x^{\prime\prime}\in\mu}h(x^{\prime\prime})},

where
h(-)
is the hook function mentioned above.

We get from
[
GNS99
]
that

\displaystyle{\left\langle\phi(\lambda;\mu),\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\right\rangle}_{W}

\displaystyle=\frac{r!}{\left|W\right|}\cdot\frac{\prod_{x^{\prime}\in\lambda}(n+2c(x^{\prime})+1)\cdot\prod_{x^{\prime\prime}\in\mu}(n+2c(x^{\prime\prime})+1)}{\prod_{x^{\prime}\in\lambda}h(x^{\prime})\cdot\prod_{x^{\prime\prime}\in\mu}h(x^{\prime\prime})}

\displaystyle=\frac{m(m-1)(m-2)...(m-(b-1))}{b!}.

On the other hand, in this case
M_{\mathcal{O}}
is of Cartan type

a\cdot A_{n-1}+C_{b}.

It follows from
[
Som97
]
that

{\left\langle\varepsilon_{W_{J}},\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\right\rangle}_{W_{J}}=\frac{n^{r-(an+b-a)}}{(n!)^{a}\cdot 2^{b}\cdot b!}\cdot\left((n-1)!\right)^{a}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{b}(n-2i+1),

which is equal to
{\left\langle\phi(\lambda;\mu),\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\right\rangle}_{W}
, by noting that
n=2m+1
. Thus, Theorem
5.2
for type
C_{r}
is proved.

Remark 5.3
.

The technical condition that
\mathcal{O}_{C}^{2r,n}=\mathcal{O}^{2r,n}
(besides the assumption that the former orbit is a regular orbit of a Levi subgroup) in Theorem
5.2
is indispensable. Indeed, if we consider
r=2m
and
n=2m+1=r+1
, then in this case

j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon=\phi(m;m)\text{ and }\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon)=(r,r).

We have
\varepsilon_{W}\otimes j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon=\phi(1^{m};1^{m})
and following the same method of computation, we get

{\left\langle\phi(1^{m};1^{m}),\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\right\rangle}_{W}=m+1.

On the other hand,

c_{\Theta}(\mathcal{O})_{\psi}={\left\langle\varepsilon_{W_{J}},\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\right\rangle}_{W_{J}}=n=2m+1.

This indicates a necessity of modifying the original speculation in
[
GLT25
, Conjecture 4.1 (4.4)]
.

5.2.3.
Type
B_{r}

We consider
\overline{{\rm Spin}}_{2r+1}^{(n)}
with
n=2m+1
odd. It follows from
[
GLT25
]
that we only need to consider the case when

r=na+b\text{ with }0\leqslant b\leqslant m.

In this case, we get

j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon=\phi((m^{a},b);(m+1)^{a})\text{ and }\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon)=(n^{2a},2b+1).

We also have

\varepsilon_{W}\otimes j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon_{W_{\nu}}=\phi(a^{m+1};((a+1)^{b},a^{m-b}))=:\phi(\lambda;\mu).

The computation is then exactly the same as the type
C_{r}
case, and we have

{\left\langle\phi(\lambda;\mu),\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\right\rangle}_{W}={\left\langle\varepsilon_{W_{J}},\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\right\rangle}_{W_{J}}=\frac{m(m-1)(m-2)...(m-(b-1))}{b!}.

This verifies Theorem
5.2
for the type
B_{r}
case.

5.2.4.
Type
D_{r}

We consider
\overline{{\rm Spin}}_{2r}^{(n)}
with
n=2m+1
. If
{\rm WF}^{\rm geo}(\Theta)
is the regular orbit of a Levi subgroup, we need

r-1=na+b,0\leqslant b\leqslant m,

and in this case

j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon=\phi(((m+1)^{a},b+1);m^{a})\text{ and }\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon)=(n^{2a},2b+1,1).

We get

\phi(\lambda;\mu):=\varepsilon_{W}\otimes j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon=\phi(a^{m};((a+1)^{b+1},a^{m-b})),

and

\dim\phi(\lambda;\mu)=\frac{r!}{(am)!\cdot(am+a+b+1)!}\cdot\frac{1}{\prod_{x^{\prime}\in\lambda}h(x^{\prime})\cdot\prod_{x^{\prime\prime}\in\mu}h(x^{\prime\prime})}.

By using (
5.3
) and
[
GNS99
, Propositition 3.6(1)]
, a long but straightforward computation gives that

{\left\langle\phi(\lambda;\mu),\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\right\rangle}_{W}=\frac{(m+1)m(m-1)...(m+1-b)}{(b+1)!}.

On the other hand,

M_{\mathcal{O}}
is of Cartan type

a\cdot A_{n-1}+D_{b+1}.

It then follows from (
5.4
) that

c_{\Theta}(\mathcal{O})_{\psi}={\left\langle\varepsilon_{W_{J}},\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\right\rangle}_{W_{J}}=\frac{m(m-1)(m-2)...(m-(b-1))}{(b+1)!}\cdot(n-b).

Thus, we see that if
b=m
, or equivalently
\mathcal{O}=(n^{2a+1},1)
, then
c_{\Theta}(\mathcal{O})_{\psi}={\left\langle\phi(\lambda;\mu),\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\right\rangle}_{W}
as desired. This verifies Theorem
5.2
for the type
D_{r}
case.

We remark also that if
b\neq m
, then the equality does not hold.

5.2.5.
Type
G_{2}

For
\overline{G}_{2}^{(n)},n\in\mathbbm{N}_{\geqslant 1}
, there are exactly two nontrivial cases to consider, enforced by the assumption in Theorem
5.2
:
n=2
or 3.

In the first case, for

j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon=\phi_{2,2}

\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon)=\tilde{A}_{1}

{\left\langle\phi_{2,2},\varepsilon\otimes\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{2}}\right\rangle}={\left\langle\varepsilon_{W_{J}},\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\right\rangle}_{W_{J}}=1.

In the second case, if
n=3
, then

j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon=\phi_{1,3}^{\prime\prime}\text{ and }\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}\varepsilon)=A_{1}
.
Again, using the character of
\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}
given in
[
Ga6
]

{\left\langle\phi_{1,3}^{\prime\prime},\varepsilon\otimes\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{2}}\right\rangle}={\left\langle\varepsilon_{W_{J}},\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{n}}\right\rangle}_{W_{J}}=1.

This verifies Theorem
5.2
for
G_{2}

GAB2 regulates lipid metabolism by activating the MEK/ERK/c-Myc pathway: impact on renal cell carcinoma progression
Authors: Weiqun Zhang, Xianming Fei, Pinqiong Qin, Yan Sun, Zhiying Zhu, Nan Huang, Xiaoying Li, Zhipeng Li, Ting Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundGrb2-associated binding protein 2 (GAB2) is a scaffold protein in the cytoplasm involved in the progression of various cancers, but its significance in renal cell carcinoma (RCC) development remains unclear. The investigation sought to explore the mechanisms of GAB2 in RCC.MethodsGAB2 mRNA levels were examined in normal renal tubular epithelial cells (HKC) and RCC cell lines (ACHN and OS-RC-2). GAB2 was knocked down or overexpressed in RCC cells, and changes in cell proliferation, apoptosis, migration, and lipid metabolism were assessed. Moreover, p-MEK, p-ERK, and c-Myc levels were measured. The MEK inhibitor U0126 was introduced to validate the underlying mechanisms. Nude mice were subcutaneously injected with ACHN cells transfected with either oe-NC or oe-GAB2 , and then treated with U0126. The MEK/ERK/c-Myc pathway, tumor growth, and lipid metabolism were subsequently evaluated.ResultsGAB2 expression was notably elevated in RCC cells compared to HKC. GAB2 knockdown markedly reduced RCC cell viability, induced apoptosis, and inhibited cell migration and lipid accumulation, whereas overexpression of GAB2 had the opposite effects. Notably, the MEK/ERK/c-Myc pathway in RCC cells was inhibited by GAB2 knockdown and activated by its overexpression. U0126 treatment altered the effects of GAB2 overexpression in RCC cells. In RCC mice, GAB2 overexpression upregulated MEK/ERK/c-Myc pathway, which was suppressed by U0126. Moreover, GAB2 overexpression promoted tumor growth and lipid synthesis in RCC mice, and U0126 counteracted these changes.ConclusionGAB2 enhances lipid accumulation by activating MEK/ERK/c-Myc pathway, thereby promoting RCC progression. GAB2 is a potential target for RCC management.

Introduction

Renal cancer is a prevalent tumor of the urinary system linked to poor prognosis, with renal cell carcinoma (RCC) accounting for about 85% of cases [1–3]. Statistics show that approximately one-third of RCC patients develop metastatic disease, and the 5-year overall survival rate for metastatic RCC is nearly 12% [4]. Von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) is a key gene that affects RCC progression, and its mutation leads to HIF accumulation and subsequent increased VEGFR expression; therefore, tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) targeting VEGF signaling are widely used to treat metastatic RCC [5]. In recent years, combining TKI and immune checkpoint inhibitors in first-line therapy has changed the treatment outlook for advanced RCC, but primary resistance brings a significant challenge [6]. Therefore, a comprehensive understanding of molecular mechanisms is essential for RCC diagnosis and treatment.

Metabolic reprogramming refers to the adaptive regulation of cellular energy metabolism and is a hallmark of malignancy, during which changes in glucose, amino acid, and lipid metabolism substantially influence cancer progression [7, 8]. RCC is characterized by a high degree of metabolic reprogramming, among which dysregulation of lipid metabolism is one of the most significant changes [9]. Previous studies have revealed that VHL mutations in RCC modulate the transcription of genes involved in lipid metabolism by influencing HIF-1α and HIF-2α [10]. Li et al. discovered that malonyl-CoA decarboxylase-mediated fatty acid breakdown disrupts lipid homeostasis and inhibits RCC progression [11]. Therefore, targeting lipid metabolism in RCC is a promising therapy.

Grb2-associated binding protein 2 (GAB2) is a scaffold protein phosphorylated on tyrosine by various cytokines, growth factors, and antigen receptors, interacting with downstream signaling molecules [12]. GAB2, as a potential oncogene, plays a significant role in cancers such as breast cancer and melanoma [13]. Research has revealed that GAB2 levels are notably elevated in human RCC, and targeting GAB2 inhibits the proliferation of RCC cells [14]. Additionally, GAB2 overexpression or knockdown enhances or reduces lipid production in hepatocytes in vitro, and GAB2 can activate its downstream signaling protein ERK during lipid accumulation [15]. MEK-ERK-c-Myc pathway in tumor endothelial cells has been shown to be critical for tumor progression [16]. Interestingly, Ding et al. found that GAB2 upregulates VEGF levels by activating MEK/ERK/c-Myc pathway, thereby promoting colorectal cancer progression [17]. However, the mechanisms of GAB2 in RCC remain unclear.

This investigation compared GAB2 expression between RCC cells and normal renal tubular epithelial cells. GAB2 silencing or overexpression in RCC cells was performed to evaluate GAB2’s involvement in RCC. We found that GAB2 enhances RCC development by regulating lipid metabolism, which may be related to MEK/ERK/c-Myc pathway. The MEK inhibitor U0126 was used to treat RCC cells and mice to further elucidate the mechanism through which GAB2 regulates RCC progression.

Methods

Cell culture and transfection

Human RCC lines ACHN and OS-RC-2 were purchased from Wuhan Shangen Company (Wuhan, Hubei, China) and cultured in DMEM and RPMI 1640 medium (Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA), respectively. Human renal tubular epithelial cell line HKC was sourced from Nanjing Kebai Company (Nanjing, Jiangsu, China) and maintained in DMEM/F12 medium (Gibco, 11320033). Cells were grown in a Heracell 150i incubator (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) at 37 ℃ with 5% CO2.

RCC cells (5 × 104) were inoculated into a 12-well plate and cell transfection was performed using HighGene transfection reagent (ABclonal, Wuhan, Hubei, China; RM09014) when cells reached 70–90% confluence. Briefly, 4 μL of HighGene was mixed with 10 pmol of negative control siRNA (si-NC, 5′-ACGUGACACGUUCGGAGA A-3′), 10 pmol of GAB2-targeting siRNA (si-GAB2, 5′-UAGUAUTCCAGAACAUCUGTT-3′), 2 μg of overexpression negative control (oe-NC) plasmid, or 2 μg of GAB2 overexpression (oe-GAB2) plasmid. The mixture was added to cells and incubated for 48 h.

RT-qPCR

Total RNA was extracted from cells and tumors through Trizol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA; 15596018), followed by reverse transcription using a cDNA synthesis kit (Tiangen, Beijing, China; KR118-02). PCR was performed with the SYBR qPCR kit (Lifeint, Xiamen, Fujian, China; A4004M). PCR conditions were as follows: an initial denaturation at 95 °C for 3 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 12 s and 62 °C for 40 s. Relative gene expression was calculated by 2−ΔΔCT technique. Western blot

RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, Shanghai, China; P0013B) was added to cells or tumor tissues for protein extraction, and sample concentration was examined using a BCA kit (Solarbio, Beijing, China; PC0020). Protein samples were separated through electrophoresis and transferred to PVDF membranes (Beyotime, FFP24). After incubation with 5% skim milk (Beyotime, P0216), membranes were incubated with primary antibodies including GAB2 (1:1000; Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA; ab235932), MEK (1:1000; CST, Danvers, MA,USA; 9122), p-MEK (1:1000; CST, 9154), ERK (1:1000; CST, 9102), p-ERK (1:2000; CST, 4370), c-Myc (1:1000; CST, 9402), and GAPDH (1:10000; Abcam, ab181602) overnight at 4 ℃. Subsequently, membranes were treated with the secondary antibody (1:2000; Abcam, ab205718) for 60 min. Protein bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (Applygen, Beijing, China; P1000) and quantified by ImageJ software.

CCK-8 assay

Cells were treated with 10 μL of CCK-8 solution (Beyotime, C0037) for 2 h at 0, 24, 48, and 72 h after cell transfection. Absorbance at 450 nm was detected with a microplate reader (WuxiHiwell Diatek, Wuxi, Jiangsu, China; DR-3518G).

Flow cytometry

Apoptosis was detected by an apoptosis detection kit (Beyotime, C1062S). The collected cells were resuspended in the binding buffer followed by double-staining with Annexin V-FITC and PI. Cells were detected using a flow cytometer (Beckman, Shanghai, China; CytoFLEX S) and analyzed through CELL Quest software.

Wound scratch assay

Cells (5 × 105 cells/well) were seeded into six-well plates and cultured in an incubator. The following day, wounds were formed on the cell monolayer with a 200 μL tip, and then washed with PBS (Ephraim, Xiamen, Fujian, China; 34080619). Cells were photographed at 0 and 24 h.

Oil red O staining

Cell and tumor samples were stained using an Oil Red O staining kit (Beyotime, C0157). Samples were fixed in 10% paraformaldehyde for 10 min and then stained with Oil Red O solution for 8–10 min. Subsequently, samples were differentiated in 85% propylene glycol solution for 2–5 min and imaged under a microscope (Olympus, Tokyo,Japan; BX53). The Oil Red O stained lipid-rich areas were quantified by ImageJ software.

TG and CHOL detection

Commercial kits (Nanjing Jiancheng, Nanjing, Jiangsu, China) were employed to detect TG and CHOL levels, according to manufacturer’s instructions. Absorbance at 500 nm was measured by a microplate reader.

Animals

Twenty four male BALB/c nude mice (4 weeks) were purchased from Beijing HFK Bio-Technology.co.,LTD. Animals were randomly divided into four groups (n = 6): oe-NC, oe-GAB2, oe-NC + U0126, and oe-GAB2 + U0126 groups. A cell suspension (4 × 106 cells) of oe-NC or oe-GAB2 ACHN cells was injected into mouse armpits. Ten days after tumor implantation, mice in the oe-NC + U0126 and oe-GAB2 + U0126 groups were administered MEK inhibitor U0126 (5 mg/kg; Beyotime, S1901) twice a week. Tumor volume was recorded every two days. Following four weeks of treatment, mice were euthanized through inhalation of excess CO2, and mouse tumor tissues were collected and weighed. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Yangzhou University (202412052).

Immunohistochemistry

Tumor tissue was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and dehydrated with 30% sucrose. Tumors were embedded in paraffin wax and sectioned at 5 μm. Sections were soaked in 3% H2O2 solution for 20 min, followed by treatment with citrate buffer (Beyotime, P0083). Subsequently, sections were incubated with normal goat serum (Beyotime, C0265) for 10-15 min and treated with Ki67 primary antibody (1:200; Affinity, Liyang, Jiangsu, China; AF0198) overnight at 4 ℃. Sections were treated with secondary antibody for 10–15 min, and then stained with DAB (Beyotime, P0202) and hematoxylin (Solarbio, G1080). Sections were photographed under a microscope, and the percentage of Ki67-positive cells was quantified by ImageJ software.

TUNEL staining

Tumor tissue sections were subjected to TUNEL staining using a TUNEL apoptosis detection kit (Beyotime, C1091) and imaged by a fluorescence microscope. The percentage of TUNEL-positive cells was quantified using ImageJ software.

Statistics

Data are displayed as mean ± standard deviation. GraphPad Prism 8.0 software was employed for statistical analyses, including one-way and two-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc test. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

GAB2 induced proliferation and migration, and inhibited apoptosis in RCC cells

To explore the role of GAB2 in RCC, GAB2 expression in human renal tubular epithelial cells HKC and two RCC cell lines was detected. GAB2 expression in RCC cells was significantly higher than that of HKC cells (Fig. 1A). GAB2 expression in RCC cell lines was knocked down or overexpressed using si-GAB2 or oe-GAB2, and transfection efficiency was evaluated through RT-qPCR and Western blot. Results revealed that GAB2 expression in both ACHN and OS-RC-2 cells was markedly decreased after transfection with si-GAB2, but was increased after transfection with oe-GAB2 (Fig. 1B and C). CCK-8 results demonstrated that GAB2 knockdown markedly inhibited the proliferation of both RCC cells, whereas GAB2 overexpression promoted it (Fig. 2A). Transfection with si-GAB2 notably promoted apoptosis in both RCC cells, whereas overexpression of GAB2 inhibited apoptosis (Fig. 2B). Moreover, GAB2 knockdown markedly suppressed RCC cell migration, while overexpression of GAB2 enhanced it (Fig. 2C). GAB2 regulated lipid metabolism and activated MEK/ERK/c-Myc pathway in RCC in vitro

ACSL3 is an enzyme that catalyzes the esterification of fatty acids, whereas HADH is involved in the β-oxidation of fatty acids [18, 19]. The results showed that GAB2 knockdown decreased ACSL3 expression in both OS-RC-2 and ACHN cells, while overexpression of GAB2 markedly increased its expression, and HADH showed the opposite trend (Fig. 3A). GAB2 knockdown notably inhibited lipid accumulation in both RCC cells, while its overexpression promoted it (Fig. 3B). Additionally, GAB2 knockdown reduced TG and CHOL content in both RCC cells, while GAB2 overexpression increased their levels (Fig. 3C). These results consistently suggested that GAB2 regulates lipid metabolism in RCC cells. The oncogene c-Myc activates lipid metabolism during tumor formation, and the abnormal accumulation of c-Myc protein has been shown to be associated with MEK/ERK pathway [20, 21]. Results revealed that GAB2 knockdown significantly inhibited p-MEK, p-ERK, and c-Myc levels in both RCC cells, while GAB2 overexpression increased their levels (Fig. 3D). Discussion

RCC is the most common malignant tumor in the kidneys. Numerous investigations have revealed the high expression of GAB2 in cancers, but the mechanism of GAB2 in RCC has not been studied. Our research confirmed that GAB2 was highly expressed in RCC cells. Both GAB2 knockdown and overexpression affect the malignant development and lipid accumulation of RCC, and the MEK/ERK/c-Myc pathway is involved in this process. These results provide new insights into the pathogenesis of RCC and identify novel therapeutic targets.

GAB2 is a docking protein that acts as an intermediate molecule in multiple pathways, integrating and amplifying upstream signals and transmitting them to downstream targets [13, 22]. Studies have revealed that GAB2 is a key carcinogenic factor that facilitates the progression of various cancers [23–25]. Similarly, we confirmed that GAB2 expression was significantly higher in RCC cell lines compared to HKC. Additionally, targeting GAB2 can suppress the malignant development of HepG2 cells [26]. Gu et al. discovered that microRNA-302c-3p suppresses cell proliferation in RCC by targeting GAB2 [14]. Our data revealed that GAB2 silencing significantly suppresses the malignant development of RCC cell lines, whereas overexpression of GAB2 induces it. Moreover, oe-GAB2 mice exhibited faster tumor growth and reduced apoptosis compared to controls. These findings imply that GAB2 expression is closely linked to RCC progression.

Lipid metabolism maintains cellular function and energy homeostasis, and its dysregulation will lead to excessive lipid accumulation, eventually triggering various diseases [27–30]. Studies have shown that excess lipids enhance tumorigenesis, tumor cell colonization, and metastasis, and in turn, tumor cells regulate stromal cell and immune cell activity through lipid metabolism to resist treatment and promote relapse [31]. Zhang et al. found that VHL mutation accelerates clear cell RCC (ccRCC) development by promoting cholesterol ester accumulation [32]. TRIB3 facilitates RCC progression by upregulating the lipid droplet-related protein PLIN2 [33]. This evidence suggests the importance of targeting lipid metabolism in RCC treatment. A bioinformatics study identified GAB2 as a lipid metabolism-related gene in acute myocardial infarction [34]. We found that lipid metabolism in RCC is regulated by GAB2. GAB2 knockdown inhibits lipid synthesis and promotes lipid metabolism, thereby reducing lipid accumulation, while overexpression of GAB2 enhances lipid accumulation. GAB2 promotes RCC development by regulating lipid metabolism.

The c-MYC oncogene is a transcription factor closely associated with human disease, involved in gene transcription, cell cycle, and metabolic processes, and research has shown that it regulates lipid metabolism in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma [35, 36]. Additionally, HSP60 silencing drives metabolic reprogramming in ccRCC by upregulating MEK/ERK/c-Myc pathway, thereby promoting tumor progression [37]. GAB2 upregulates VEGF levels by activating MEK/ERK/c-Myc axis and induces colorectal cancer development [17]. Similar to these findings, we found that GAB2 knockdown in RCC inhibits MEK/ERK/c-Myc axis, while overexpression of GAB2 activates this pathway. Mechanistically, MEK is the upstream kinase of phosphorylated ERK, and ERK enhances the expression and activity of c-Myc, thereby promoting its stability [38]. Importantly, we discovered that treatment with the MEK inhibitor U0126 reverses these alterations caused by overexpression of GAB2, suggesting that GAB2 mediates lipid metabolism in RCC via the MEK–ERK–c-Myc signaling cascade. Therefore, developing drugs that target GAB2 and its downstream pathways may provide a promising strategy for the clinical treatment of RCC.

This investigation has certain limitations. First, c-Myc mediates glycolysis, glutamine, and lipid metabolic reprogramming in tumors, but we focused merely on changes in lipid metabolism. Therefore, more experiments are necessary to fully understand the mechanism of GAB2 in RCC. Moreover, this study mainly focuses on the overall changes in lipid metabolism, without exploring which specific step of lipid metabolism is primarily regulated by GAB2. Furthermore, we did not validate the results of this study in human samples.

Conclusion

This study reveals the important role of GAB2-mediated MEK/ERK/c-Myc pathway in the development and lipid accumulation of RCC. These findings provide promising targets and biomarkers for RCC, offering novel ideas for clinical RCC treatment.

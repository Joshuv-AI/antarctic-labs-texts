Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an affine Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
, and let
L(\Lambda)=L(\Lambda,\mathfrak{g})
denote the irreducible highest weight module over
\mathfrak{g}
with highest weight
\Lambda
. Recall
[
KW88
]
that the weight
\Lambda
and the corresponding
\mathfrak{g}
-module
L(\Lambda)
are called
admissible
if the following two properties hold:

\displaystyle\left\langle\Lambda+\rho,\alpha\right\rangle\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}\quad\text{for all $\alpha\in\Delta_{+}^{\mathrm{re}\,\vee}$},

(0.1)

where
\Delta_{+}^{\mathrm{re}\,\vee}
is the set of positive real coroots of
\mathfrak{g}
;

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}\left\{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}^{\mathrm{re}\,\vee}\mid\left\langle\Lambda+\rho,\alpha\right\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}\supset\Delta_{+}^{\mathrm{re}\,\vee}.

(0.2)

These
\mathfrak{g}
-modules are important since we proved in
[
KW88
]
the following formula for their characters
\mathrm{tr}_{L(\Lambda)}e^{h},\ h\in\mathfrak{h}
:

\displaystyle\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}\left(1-e^{-\alpha(h)}\right)^{\mathrm{mult}\ \alpha}\mathrm{tr}_{L(\Lambda)}e^{h}=\sum_{w\in W^{\Lambda}}\varepsilon(w)e^{(w.\Lambda)(h)}.

(0.3)

Here
\mathfrak{h}
is the Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
,
W^{\Lambda}
is the subgroup of the Weyl group
W
of
\mathfrak{g}
, generated by reflection in
\alpha\in\Delta_{+}^{\mathrm{re}\,\vee}
,
integral
with respect to
\Lambda
, i.e. such that
\left\langle\Lambda+\rho,\alpha\right\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle w.\Lambda=w(\Lambda+\rho)-\rho,\quad w\in W,

(0.4)

is the ‘shifted’ action of
W
, and

\displaystyle\varepsilon(w)={\det}_{\mathfrak{h}}w.

Using this formula, we showed that the characters, normalized by a power of
q=e^{-\delta}
factor, are meromorphic modular functions in the domain
\left\{h\in\mathfrak{h}\mid\mathrm{Re}\ \delta(h)>0\right\}
. We also conjectured that these are all irreducible highest weight
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
L(\Lambda)
with this property.

From the viewpoint of the theory of vertex algebras the most interesting case is that of a non-twisted
\mathfrak{g}
, associated to a simple Lie algebra
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
, and
\Lambda=k\Lambda_{0}
, where
k\in\mathbb{C}
, when
L(k\Lambda_{0})
admits a structure of simple affine vertex algebra
V_{k}(\overline{\mathfrak{g}})
of level
k
. If
k\Lambda_{0}
is an admissible weight,
k
is called an
admissible level
. It is easy to deduce from the classification of admissible weights in
[
KW89
]
that
k
is an admissible level only when it is a rational number, and there are only the following two possibilities
[
KW08
]
:

(principal
k
)
k=-h^{\vee}+\frac{p}{u}
, where
p
and
u
are coprime positive integers,
u
is coprime with the lacety
r^{\vee}
of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
, and
p\geq h^{\vee}
, the dual Coxeter number of
\mathfrak{g}
;

(subprincipal
k
)
k=-h^{\vee}+\frac{p}{u}
, where
p
and
u
are coprime positve integers,
u
is divisible by
r^{\vee}>1
, and
p\geq h
, the Coxeter number of
\mathfrak{g}
.

The above mentioned conjecture leads to

Conjecture 0.1.
The series
\mathrm{tr}_{V_{k}(\mathfrak{g})}e^{2\pi i\left(L_{0}-\frac{C_{k}}{24}\right)\tau}
converges to a holomorphic modular function in the upper half-plane Im
\tau>0
if and only if
k
is either principal or subprincipal admissible level. Here
C_{k}=\frac{k\dim\overline{\mathfrak{g}}}{k+h^{\vee}}
.

Note that the ‘if’ part holds due to
[
KW25
, formula (52)]
if
k
is a principal admissible level, and due to the following similar formula for
k=-h^{\vee}+\frac{p}{u}
subprincipal admissible level:

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{L(\Lambda)}q^{L_{0}-\frac{C_{k}}{24}}=\frac{\sum_{\gamma\in\overline{Q}}d(\overline{\Lambda}+p\gamma)q^{\frac{u}{2p}\left|\overline{\Lambda}+\overline{\rho}+p\gamma\right|^{2}}}{\eta(\tau)^{\dim\overline{\mathfrak{g}}}},

(0.5)

if
\Lambda
is a subprincipal admissible weight of level
k
whose restriction to
\overline{\mathfrak{h}}
is dominant integral for
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
where

\displaystyle d(\beta)=\prod_{\alpha\in\overline{\Delta}_{+}}\frac{\left(\beta+\overline{\rho}\mid\alpha\right)}{\left(\overline{\rho}\mid\alpha\right)},\quad\beta\in\overline{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}.

In particular, (0.5) holds for
\Lambda=k\Lambda_{0}
.

Note that (0.5) is a modular function since it is a specialization of the normalized character of
L(\Lambda)
.

The most important among the admissible weights
\Lambda
of principal admissible level
k
is the set
P_{+,\mathrm{pr}}^{k}
of
principal admissible weights
. Such
\Lambda
are defined by the property that the set of real coroots, integral with respect to
\Lambda
, is isometric to that with respect to
k\Lambda_{0}
, or, equivalently, its set of simple roots is isometric to the set

\displaystyle S_{(u)}^{\vee}=\left\{uK-\theta^{\vee},\alpha_{1}^{\vee},\cdots,\alpha_{l}^{\vee}\right\},

(0.6)

where
K
is the canonical central element of
\mathfrak{g}
,
\theta
is the highest root of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
, and
\alpha_{1}^{\vee},\cdots,\alpha_{l}^{\vee}
are simple coroots of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
. (If
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
A_{\ell}^{(1)}
, then
P_{+,\mathrm{pr}}^{k}
is the set of all admissible weights, but not for other types.)

It is shown in
[
KW89
]
that
P_{+,\mathrm{pr}}^{k}
consists of weights of the form

\displaystyle\Lambda=\left(t_{\beta}\overline{y}\right).\left(\Lambda^{0}+\frac{1-u}{u}p\Lambda_{0}\right),

(0.7)

where
\beta\in\overline{P}
and
y\in\overline{W}
are such that

\displaystyle t_{\beta}\overline{y}\left(S_{(u)}^{\vee}\right)\subset\Delta_{+}^{\mathrm{re}\,\vee},

and
\Lambda^{0}
is a dominant integral weight for
\mathfrak{g}
of level
p-h^{\vee}
.

The importance of principal admissible weights is due to the fact, proved in
[
KW89
]
, that the span of normalized characters of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
L(\Lambda)
with
\Lambda\in P_{+,\mathrm{pr}}^{k}
is
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-invariant, and the fact, proved in
[
Ara16
]
that all
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
L(\Lambda)
, which descend to
V_{k}(\mathfrak{g})
are of the form (0.7), with
\beta=0,\overline{y}=e
, i.e.
\Lambda
is quasidominant.

The set of admissible weights of subprincipal admissible level
k
, denoted by
P_{+}^{k}
in the paper, is defined in a similar way. A weight of such level
k
is called
subprincipal admissible
if the set of coroots of
\mathfrak{g}
, which are integral with respect to
\Lambda
, is isometric to that for
k\Lambda_{0}
, i.e. to the set

\displaystyle S_{(u)}^{\#\vee}=\left\{uK-\theta_{s}^{\vee},\alpha_{1}^{\vee},\cdots,\alpha_{l}^{\vee}\right\},

(0.8)

where
\theta_{s}
is the highest short root of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
(cf. (0.6)).

In this case the set of coroots of
\mathfrak{g}
, integral with respect to
\Lambda
, is isometric to the set of coroots of the twisted affine Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\#}=\mathfrak{g}\left(A^{\#}\right)
, whose Cartan matrix
A^{\#}
is
A^{\top^{\prime}}
, where
A
is the Cartan matrix of
\mathfrak{g}
,
\top
stands for transpose and
′
stands for taking the adjacent Cartan matrix
[
Kac90
, §13.9]
.
Recall that
A^{\prime}=A
for all affine Cartan matrices, except that
′
switches
A_{2\ell-1}^{(2)}
with
D_{\ell+1}^{(2)}
.

The description of the set of subprincipal admissible weights is similar to that of principal ones, see Theorem 2.1.
There is an important difference, however. All
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
are principal admissible levels and the corresponding principal admissible weights are just level
k
dominant integral weights, hence the span of the normalized characters of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
L(\Lambda)
with principal admissible
\Lambda
of level
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-invariant
[
Kac90
]
. This is not the case for subprincipal admissible levels, which cannot be integer numbers. It is related to the fact that the numerators of characters of subprincipal admissible
L(\Lambda)
are expressed in terms of the Weyl group
W^{\#}
of the twisted affine Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\#}
. But the span of normalized characters of modules
L(\Lambda,\mathfrak{g}^{\#})
with
\Lambda
dominant integral is invariant only with respect to the action of the subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(r^{\vee})
of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

[
Kac90
, Theorem 13.9]
.

It is quite surprising that nevertheless the span of the normalized characters of subprincipal admissible
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
L(\Lambda)
,
\Lambda\in P_{+}^{k}
, is
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-invariant. This is our main Theorem 5.1. Due to the character formula (3.6), it suffices to prove
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-invariance of the span of the numerators
A_{\Lambda+\rho}
, since
\mathbb{C}R_{\mathfrak{g}}
, where
R_{\mathfrak{g}}
is the Weyl denominator, is
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-invariant. The
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-invariance of the span of
\left\{A_{\Lambda+\rho}\right\}_{\Lambda\in P_{+}^{k}}
is proved, using their expression in terms of Jacobi forms in Proposition 5.1.

Note that Theorem 5.1 implies the same modular transformation formulas for quantum Hamiltonian reduction
H_{f}(\Lambda)
for subprincipal admissible
\Lambda
, as those, given by
[
KW25
, Theorem 4]
for the principal admissible ones.

In the last section of the paper we show that the simple vertex algebra
V_{k}(\overline{\mathfrak{g}})
of subprincipal admissible level
k
is modular invariant in the sense of
[
KW25
]
, where this is proved for principal admissible levels. We also find the conditions, for which the quantum Hamiltonian reduction
H_{f}
at subprincipal admissible level
k
is a modular invariant vertex algebra.

For the basics of the theory of affine Lie algebras and their representations, and of the theory of Jacobi forms, see the book
[
Kac90
]
.

Let
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
be a simple finite-dimensional Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
of rank
\ell
and lacety
r^{\vee}>1
, i.e.
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
B_{\ell},C_{\ell},F_{4},
or
G_{2}
, and
r^{\vee}=2,2,2,
or 3 respectively. Choose a Cartan subalgebra
\overline{\mathfrak{h}}
of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
. Choose a set of simple roots
\overline{\Pi}=\left\{\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{\ell}\right\}
, normalize the invariant bilinear form on
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
by the condition
(\theta\mid\theta)=2
, where
\theta
is the highest root. We identify
\overline{\mathfrak{h}}
with
\overline{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
, using this bilinear form. We denote by
\theta_{s}
the highest short root. Then

\displaystyle\left(\theta_{s}\mid\theta_{s}\right)=\frac{2}{r^{\vee}}.

(1.1)

Let
\overline{\Delta}
be the set of all roots of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
, then
\overline{\Delta}=\overline{\Delta}_{\ell}\cup\overline{\Delta}_{s}
is a union of the sets of long and short roots
\alpha
, so that
(\alpha\mid\alpha)=2
and
\frac{2}{r^{\vee}}
respectively. As usual, for a root
\alpha\in\overline{\Delta}
we denote by
\alpha^{\vee}=\frac{2\alpha}{(\alpha\mid\alpha)}
the corresponding coroot.

Let
\overline{W}
be the Weyl group of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
, let
\overline{Q}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i},\ \overline{Q}^{\vee}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}^{\vee}
be the root and coroot lattices, and let
\overline{P}^{\vee}
and
\overline{P}
be their dual lattices respectively. We have

\displaystyle r^{\vee}\overline{Q}\subset\overline{P}^{\vee}.

(1.2)

Let
\overline{\rho}\in\overline{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
be the Weyl vector for
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
, i.e.
\left(\overline{\rho}\mid\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\right)=1
for
i=1,\cdots,\ell
. Then

\displaystyle r^{\vee}\overline{\rho}\in\overline{P}^{\vee}.

(1.3)

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the affine Lie algebra, associated to
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
(see
[
Kac90
]
for details). Recall that it is a Kac-Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}(A)
with Cartan matrix
A
of type
B_{\ell}^{(1)},C_{\ell}^{(1)},F_{4}^{(1)},
or
G_{2}^{(1)}
respectively. Let
\mathfrak{g}\left(A^{\#}\right)
be the twisted affine Lie algebra with the Cartan matrix
A^{\#}
of type
D_{\ell+1}^{(2)},A_{2\ell-1}^{(2)},E_{6}^{(2)},
or
D_{4}^{(3)}
respectively.

Recall that
\mathfrak{g}
can be explicitly construced as
\mathfrak{g}=\overline{\mathfrak{g}}[t,t^{-1}]\oplus\mathbb{C}K\oplus\mathbb{C}d
, where
K
is a central element, and the remaining brackets are
(a,b\in\mathfrak{g},\ m,n\in Z)
:

\displaystyle\left[at^{m},bt^{n}\right]=[a,b]t^{m+n}+m\delta_{m,-n}(a\mid b)K,\ [d,t^{m}a]=mt^{m}a.

(1.4)

The invariant symmetric non-degenerate bilinear form on
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
extends to that on
\mathfrak{g}
by letting

\displaystyle\left(at^{m}\mid bt^{n}\right)=(a\mid b)\delta_{m,-n},\quad(at^{m}\mid\mathbb{C}K+\mathbb{C}d)=0,

\displaystyle(K\mid d)=1,\quad(K\mid K)=(d\mid d)=0.

(1.5)

Let
\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{C}d+\overline{\mathfrak{h}}+\mathbb{C}K
be the Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. We identify it with
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
using the bilinear form
(\bullet\mid\bullet)

\delta\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}

\displaystyle\delta\mid_{\overline{\mathfrak{h}}+\mathbb{C}K}=0,\quad\delta(d)=1,

K

\Lambda_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be the element, identified with

\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
\mathfrak{h}

, where

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mathrm{re}}=\left\{\alpha+n\delta\mid\alpha\in\overline{\Delta},n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\},\quad\Delta^{\mathrm{im}}=\left\{n\delta,\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\right\}.

(1.7)

The set of simple roots is

\displaystyle\Pi=\left\{\alpha_{0}=\delta-\theta\right\}\cup\overline{\Pi}.

(1.8)

The multiplicities of roots in
\Delta^{\mathrm{re}}
(resp.
\Delta^{\mathrm{im}}
) are 1 (resp.
\ell
).

Recall the translation operator
t_{\gamma}
on
\mathfrak{h}
for
\gamma\in\overline{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
:

\displaystyle t_{\gamma}(h)=h+\delta(h)\gamma-\left(\frac{(\gamma\mid\gamma)}{2}\delta(h)+\gamma(h)\right)K,\quad h\in\mathfrak{h}.

(1.9)

For a subgroup
S\subset\overline{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
let
t_{S}=\left\{t_{\gamma}\mid\gamma\in S\right\}
, which is an abelian group. Then the Weyl group
W
of
\mathfrak{g}
is a semidirect product of
\overline{W}
and the abelian normal subgroup
t_{\overline{Q}^{\vee}}
.

We coordinatize
\mathfrak{h}
by writing
h\in\mathfrak{h}
in the form
h=2\pi i(-\tau d+z+tK)
, where
\tau,t\in\mathbb{C}
,
z\in\overline{\mathfrak{h}}
, and we write a function

on
\mathfrak{h}
in the form
f=f(\tau,z,t)
; we also write
f(\tau,z)=f(\tau,z,0)
.

The main character of this paper is the twisted affine Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\#}=\mathfrak{g}\left(A^{\#}\right)

\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. The set of roots of
\mathfrak{g}^{\#}

\displaystyle\Delta^{\#\mathrm{re}}=\left\{\alpha+nr^{\vee}\delta\mid\alpha\in\overline{\Delta}_{\ell},n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}\cup\left\{\alpha+n\delta\mid\alpha\in\overline{\Delta}_{s},n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\},

\displaystyle\Delta^{\#\mathrm{im}}=\left\{n\delta\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\right\}.

(1.10)

Multiplicities of roots from
\Delta^{\#\mathrm{re}}
are 1, but of the roots from
\Delta^{\#\mathrm{im}}
are different:

\displaystyle\mathrm{mult}\ n\delta=\frac{N-\ell}{r^{\vee}-1}\text{ (resp. $\ell$) if }n\in r^{\vee}\mathbb{Z}\text{ (resp. $n\not\in r^{\vee}\mathbb{Z}$)},

(1.11)

where
A^{\#}
is of type
X_{N}^{(r^{\vee})}
.

The set of simple roots for
\mathfrak{g}^{\#}
is

\displaystyle\Pi^{\#}=\left\{\delta-\theta_{s}\right\}\cup\overline{\Pi}.

(1.12)

The Weyl group
W^{\#}
of
\mathfrak{g}^{\#}
is

\displaystyle W^{\#}=\overline{W}\ltimes t_{r^{\vee}\overline{Q}}.

(1.13)

As before, for a non-isotropic root
\alpha
, we let

be the corresponding coroot. Then

\displaystyle\Delta^{\#\mathrm{re}\vee}=\left\{nK^{\#}+\alpha^{\vee}\mid\alpha^{\vee}\in\overline{\Delta}^{\vee},n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\},

(1.14)

and its subset of simple coroots is

\displaystyle\Pi^{\#\vee}=\left\{K^{\#}-\theta_{s}^{\vee},\alpha_{1}^{\vee},\cdots,\alpha_{\ell}^{\vee}\right\},

(1.15)

where

\displaystyle K^{\#}=r^{\vee}K.

Let

be the Weyl vector for
\mathfrak{g}^{\#}

\displaystyle\left(\rho^{\#}\mid\alpha\right)=1\qquad\text{for all $\alpha\in\Pi^{\#\vee}$.}

Subprincipal admissible weights

Recall that a
subprincipal admissible level
for the affine Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a rational number
k
of the form

\displaystyle k=\frac{p}{u}-h^{\vee},\quad\text{where $p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq h},u=u^{\prime}r^{\vee},u^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},\gcd(p,u)=1,$}

(2.1)

where
h
is the Coxeter number of
\mathfrak{g}
. Recall that

\displaystyle h=h^{\#\vee},\quad\text{the dual Coxeter number of $\mathfrak{g}^{\#}$.}

(2.2)

Let

\displaystyle S_{(u)}^{\#\vee}=\left\{uK-\theta_{s}^{\vee},\alpha_{1}^{\vee},\cdots,\alpha_{\ell}^{\vee}\right\}.

(2.3)

The subgroup
W^{\#}_{(u)}
of
W^{\#}
, generated by reflection in these coroots, is

\displaystyle W_{(u)}^{\#}=\overline{W}\ltimes t_{u\overline{Q}}.

(2.4)

Lemma 2.1.

(a)

For each
\beta\in\overline{P}^{\vee}
there exist unique
\overline{y}\in\overline{W}
and
\gamma\in\overline{Q}
, such that

\displaystyle{t_{\beta+u\gamma}}\overline{y}S_{(u)}^{\#\vee}\subset\Delta_{+}^{\mathrm{re}\vee}.

(2.6)

(b)

Let
\Phi_{(u)}=\left\{y\in\overline{W}\ltimes t_{\overline{P}^{\vee}}\mid y\left(S_{(u)}^{\#\vee}\right)\subset\Delta_{+}^{\mathrm{re}\vee}\right\}.
Then (2.6) induces a bijective map

\displaystyle\varphi:\overline{P}^{\vee}/u\overline{Q}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Phi_{(u)}

(2.7)

Proof.
Let
\gamma_{0}=u{K}-\theta_{s}^{\vee}
,
\gamma_{i}=\alpha_{i}^{\vee}
for
i=1,\cdots,\ell
, to simplify notation. In order to prove (a), if suffices to show that, given
\beta\in\overline{P}^{\vee}
there exist a unique
\overline{y}\in\overline{W}
and a unique
\gamma\in\overline{Q}
, satisfying

\displaystyle\left(t_{\beta+u\gamma}\overline{y}(\gamma_{i})\mid\frac{1}{h}\rho^{\vee}\right)>0\quad\text{for $i=0,\cdots,\ell.$}

(2.8)

Indeed, this would imply that the element
\overline{y}^{-1}t_{-u\gamma}t_{-\beta}\left(\frac{1}{h}\rho^{\vee}\right)
lies in the fundamental Weyl chamber with respect to
S_{(u)}^{\#\vee}
.

Note that

\displaystyle t_{-\beta}\left(\frac{1}{h}\rho^{\vee}\right)\equiv\frac{1}{h}\rho^{\vee}-\beta\text{ mod }\mathbb{C}\delta,

(2.9)

Since
(\beta\mid\alpha)\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\left(\frac{1}{h}\rho^{\vee}\mid\alpha\right)\not\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
\alpha\in\overline{\Delta}
, we see from (2.9) that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{h}\rho^{\vee}\mid n\delta+\alpha\right)\not\in\mathbb{Z}\quad\text{for all $\alpha\in\overline{\Delta},n\in\mathbb{Z}$.}

Since
\left(\beta\mid n\delta+\alpha\right)\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
\alpha\in\overline{\Delta},n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{h}\rho^{\vee}-\beta\mid\alpha\right)\not\in\mathbb{Z}\quad\text{for all $\alpha\in\Delta^{\mathrm{re}}$.}

This implies that
t_{-\beta}\left(\frac{1}{h}\rho^{\vee}\right)
is a regular element with respect to
S_{(u)}^{\#\vee}
, hence there exists a unique
w\in W_{(u)}^{\#}
, such that
w\left(t_{-\beta}\left(\frac{1}{h}\rho^{\vee}\right)\right)
lies in the fundamental Weyl chamber for
S_{(u)}^{\#\vee}
. Writing this element as
w=\overline{y}^{-1}t_{-u\gamma}
, we obtain (2.8).

In order to prove (b), note that we just constructed a well-defined map

\displaystyle\varphi:\overline{P}^{\vee}/u\overline{Q}\rightarrow\Phi_{(u)},\quad\beta\mapsto t_{\beta+u\gamma}\overline{y}.

The map
\varphi
is surjective since if
y=t_{\beta}\overline{y}\in t_{\overline{P}^{\vee}}\rtimes\overline{W}
, such that
t_{\beta}\overline{y}\left(S_{(u)}^{\#\vee}\right)\subset\Delta_{+}^{\vee}
, then
\varphi(\beta)=y
by definition of
\varphi
.

To prove injectivity of
\varphi
, suppose that we have
\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in\overline{P}^{\vee}
,
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\overline{Q}
and
\overline{y},\overline{y}^{\prime}\in\overline{W}
, such that
t_{\beta+u\gamma}\overline{y}=t_{\beta^{\prime}+u\gamma^{\prime}}\overline{y}^{\prime}
. Then

\displaystyle t_{\beta+u\gamma-(\beta^{\prime}+u\gamma^{\prime})}=\overline{y}^{\prime}\overline{y}^{-1}\in t_{\overline{P}^{\vee}}\cap\overline{W}=\left\{e\right\},

hence
\beta+u\gamma=\beta^{\prime}+u\gamma^{\prime}
,
(\beta-\beta^{\prime})=u(\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma)\in u\overline{Q}
, i.e.
\beta\equiv\beta^{\prime}\text{ mod }u\overline{Q}
.∎

\displaystyle\mathrm{ch}\ H_{f}(\Lambda)(\tau,z)=q^{a}e^{2\pi i\left(\overline{\rho}-\overline{\rho}_{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}\mid z\right)}\frac{\eta(u^{\prime}\tau)^{\ell}\eta(u\tau)^{\frac{N-r^{\vee}\ell}{r^{\vee}-1}}}{\eta(\tau)^{\ell}}A(\tau,z)B(\tau,z)C(\tau,z)D(\tau,z),

\displaystyle=\mathrm{ch}\ L\left(\Lambda^{\#0},\mathfrak{g}^{\#}_{(u)}\right)\left(u^{\prime}\tau,-\tau x+z,\frac{\tau}{2u^{\prime}}\left(x\mid x\right)\right),

\displaystyle a

Corollary 6.1
Let
k
and
\Lambda\in P_{+,e}^{k}
be as in Theorem 6.1, and let
f
be a non-zero nilpotent element of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
with Dynkin characteristic
2x
. Then

(a)

H_{f}(\Lambda)
is zero if and only if there exist
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
\alpha\in\overline{\Delta}_{+}
, such that
\alpha(x)=nu
for
\alpha\in\overline{\Delta}_{\ell}^{+}
, and
=nu^{\prime}
for
\alpha\in\overline{\Delta}_{s}^{+}
; equivalently, if and only if
u^{\prime}\leq\theta_{s}(x)
.

(b)

If
f=f_{\mathrm{min}}
, then
H_{f}(\Lambda)=W_{k}^{\mathrm{min}}(\overline{\mathfrak{g}})
is always non-zero.

(c)

If
f=f_{\mathrm{pr}}
, then
H_{f}(\Lambda)
is non-zero if and only if
u^{\prime}\geq h^{\#}
, the Coxeter number of the affine Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\#}
.

(d)

If
u^{\prime}>\theta_{s}(x)
, then
H_{f}(k\Lambda_{0})
is a modular invariant vertex algebra.

Proof.

The proof of (a) is the same as of Corollary 1(a) from
[
KW25
]
, using Lemma 5.15(ii) from
[
Ara15
]
. Claims (b) and (c) follow from (a). Claim (d) follows from the fact that the function
D(\tau,0)
is a modular function
[
Kac90
, Chapter 13]
.

Note that Corollary 6.1 is equivalent to Theorem 5.16(ii) in
[
Ara15
]
.

Statistical Precoder Design in Multi-User Systems via Graph Neural Networks and Generative Modeling
Nurettin Turan, Srikar Allaparapu, Donia Ben Amor, Benedikt Böck, Michael Joham, Wolfgang Utschick

Abstract
This letter proposes a graph neural network (GNN) -based framework for statistical precoder design that leverages model-based insights to compactly represent statistical knowledge, resulting in efficient, lightweight architectures. The framework also supports approximate statistical information in frequency division duplex (FDD) systems obtained through a Gaussian mixture model (GMM) -based limited feedback scheme in massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) systems with low pilot overhead. Simulations using a spatial channel model and measurement data demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework. It outperforms baseline methods, including stochastic iterative algorithms and Discrete Fourier transform (DFT) codebook-based approaches, particularly in low pilot overhead systems.

Introduction

Maximizing the sum-rate under a transmit power constraint is crucial to increasing the throughput in massive
MIMO
systems.
Many seminal works, e.g., the
iterative weighted minimum mean square error (IWMMSE)
algorithm
[
1
]
, assume access to perfect
channel state information (CSI)
about each
mobile terminal (MT)
during the online phase for precoder design.
More practical approaches, such as
[
2
,
3
]
rely only on statistical knowledge for precoder design at the
base station (BS)
, where the
stochastic WMMSE (SWMMSE)

[
2
]
is the stochastic counterpart of the
IWMMSE
.
However, the iterative nature of the
SWMMSE
comes with a computational burden that may not meet the latency requirements of massive
MIMO
systems with large antenna arrays and multiple
MTs
.

Recently,
GNNs
have been used for sum-rate maximization under perfect
CSI
assumptions in
[
4
,
5
]
, and for energy efficiency maximization with perfect statistical knowledge
[
6
]
.
Building on
[
5
]
, the work in
[
7
]
extends the
GNN
architecture for the imperfect
CSI
case.
The strong performance in all aforementioned works arises from exploiting the invariance that reordering
MT
indices permutes the precoders accordingly without affecting the sum-rate
[
4
]
.
The
GNN
exploits this expert knowledge by construction, acting as an effective form of regularization.
A significant advantage of
GNN
-based approaches is their scalability to different numbers of served
MTs
without requiring retraining.

In
FDD
systems, precoder design typically relies on accurate feedback from the
MTs
.
The recently proposed
GMM
-based feedback scheme in
[
8
]
provides a low-complexity, flexible approach that supports different numbers of
MTs
,
signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)
levels and pilot configurations without re-training, and is also parallelizable.
The
GMM
learns the overall
probability density function (PDF)
of the channels within a
BS
environment in an offline phase and is shared with
MTs
to enable online feedback inference, acting as a generative prior.
So far, the
SWMMSE
has been used with this feedback scheme for precoding.
To our knowledge, this work is the first to leverage a
GNN
framework for statistical precoder design in
FDD
systems using approximate statistical information.
The contributions of this work can be summarized as follows:

•

We propose a
GNN
-based precoder design framework for sum-rate maximization using only statistical knowledge of each
MT
’s channel.
Leveraging model-based insights, we compactly represent statistical knowledge, resulting in efficient, lightweight architectures in massive
MIMO
systems.
Specifically, the network input per
MT
is a vector parametrizing the covariance matrix, which only scales linearly with the number of
BS
antennas.

•

The framework is extended to handle approximate statistical information by incorporating the recently proposed
GMM
-based feedback approach, which enables efficient limited feedback in
FDD
systems with low pilot overhead.
This integration enables overall a flexible
GMM
- and
GNN
-based combined precoder design scheme.

•

Simulation results with both a spatial channel model and real-world measurement data demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework, outperforming baseline methods, including stochastic iterative algorithms and
DFT
codebook-based approaches, especially in systems with low pilot overhead.

II
System Model and Channel Data

II-A

Data Transmission Phase

The
downlink (DL)
of a single-cell multi-user system where a
BS
with
N
transmit antennas serves
J
single-antenna
MTs
is considered.
Linear precoding is used, where the precoders
\{\bm{v}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}
satisfy the power constraint
\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|\bm{v}_{j}\|_{2}^{2}=\rho
.
The design objective is the sum-rate, expressed as

R=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\log_{2}\Bigg(1+\dfrac{\left|\bm{h}_{j}^{\operatorname{T}}\bm{v}_{j}\right|^{2}}{\sum_{j^{\prime}\neq j}\left|\bm{h}_{j}^{\operatorname{T}}\bm{v}_{j^{\prime}}\right|^{2}+\sigma_{n}^{2}}\Bigg),

(1)

where
\bm{h}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
is the channel of
MT

j
and
\sigma_{n}^{2}
denotes the noise variance.
The
BS
designs the precoders
\{\bm{v}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}
based on either perfect statistical knowledge or, more realistically, based on each
MT
’s feedback information, encoded by
B
bits.

II-B

Pilot Transmission Phase

Before data transmission, the
BS
broadcasts
{n_{\mathrm{p}}}
orthogonal pilots, allowing each
MT
to infer its feedback information.
The received signal
\bm{y}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{{n_{\mathrm{p}}}}
at each
MT
is given by

\bm{y}_{j}=\bm{P}\bm{h}_{j}+\bm{n}_{j},

(2)

where
\bm{n}_{j}\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbf{0},{\bm{\Sigma}})
represents the
additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN)
with
{\bm{\Sigma}}=\sigma_{n}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{{n_{\mathrm{p}}}}
.
For a
uniform linear array (ULA)
at the
BS
, a DFT (sub)matrix is selected as the pilot matrix
\bm{P}
, while a 2D-DFT (sub)matrix is used for a
uniform rectangular array (URA)
, cf.
[
8
]
.
Each column
\bm{p}_{l}
of
\bm{P}^{\operatorname{T}}
is normalized to meet the power constraint
\|\bm{p}_{l}\|_{2}^{2}=\rho
for all
l\in\{1,2,\dots,{n_{\mathrm{p}}}\}
.
We consider systems with reduced pilot overhead,
{n_{\mathrm{p}}}<N
, due to their practical relevance
[
9
]
.

II-C

Channel Data

II-C
1
Spatial Channel Model

We use the spatial channel model outlined in
[
10
]
, where the channels are modeled conditionally Gaussian, i.e.,
\bm{h}_{j}|{\bm{\delta}}_{j}\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}({\bm{0}},\bm{C}_{{\bm{\delta}}_{j}})
.
The random vectors
{\bm{\delta}}_{j}
comprise uniformly distributed main departure angles of the multi-path propagation clusters between the
BS
and each
MT
.
The
BS
uses a
ULA
with
N
elements, resulting in different Toeplitz-structured covariance matrices
\{\bm{C}_{{\bm{\delta}}_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{J}
for various multi-user scenarios.
This model enables simulations with perfect statistical knowledge for performance benchmarking.

We construct a data set
\mathcal{H}=\{\bm{h}^{(m)}\}_{m=1}^{M}
, where each channel sample
\bm{h}^{(m)}
is generated by drawing random angles
{\bm{\delta}}^{(m)}
and sampling
\bm{h}^{(m)}\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}({\bm{0}},\bm{C}_{{\bm{\delta}}^{(m)}})
.
The resulting data set represents a
BS
environment with unknown
PDF

f_{\bm{h}}
.

.

]

consisting of

.

III
Proposed Framework

III-A

GNN-based Precoder Design given Perfect Statistics

To obtain the precoders
\{\bm{v}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}
solely utilizing statistical knowledge about the channel
\bm{h}_{j}
of each
MT
, the key adaptation of the
GNN
compared to the existing works
[
5
,
7
]
, is given at the input.
With perfect statistical knowledge, i.e, assuming the knowledge of
\{\bm{C}_{{\bm{\delta}}_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{J}
, model-based insights can be exploited to present the statistical knowledge to the
GNN
.
Specifically, for a
ULA
, the covariance matrix is Toeplitz-structured
[
10
]
, and for a
URA
, it is block-Toeplitz with Toeplitz blocks
[
12
]
.
In both cases, the covariance matrix per
MT

j
is fully parametrized by its first row (or column).
Thus, for each
MT

j
, the first row of the respective covariance matrix is extracted

\bm{c}_{j}=\bm{C}_{{\bm{\delta}}_{j}}[1,:].

(3)

Subsequently, the real and imaginary parts of
\bm{c}_{j}
are stacked and passed through a feature extractor
\bm{F}_{\theta}
to obtain the input feature vectors
\bm{f}_{j}^{(0)}
which are relevant for the
GNN
processing.
This has the advantage of a reduced input dimension of
2N
per
MT
, which consequently does not scale with the covariance matrix dimension of
N^{2}
and additionally inherently reduces the computational complexity.
To facilitate scalability, the feature extractor
\bm{F}_{\theta}
consists of a single fully connected layer followed by a PReLU activation function, and parameter sharing is considered such that the same feature extractor
\bm{F}_{\theta}
is used for all
MTs
, similar to
[
7
]
.

Fig. 1:
Block diagram of the proposed
GNN
-based precoder design framework.

As
GNN
architecture, we adopt the Edge-GAT model from
[
7
]
, originally termed A2D-GNN in
[
5
]
.
The
GNN
is structured as a neural network with multiple layers.
Its associated graph is bipartite, where one set of vertices corresponds to the
N
antennas at the
BS
and another set to the
J
single-antenna
MTs
, and the features are solely associated with the edges of the graph.
For the
GNN
processing, each feature vector
\bm{f}_{j}^{(0)}=[\bm{f}_{1j}^{(0),{\operatorname{T}}},\dots,\bm{f}_{Nj}^{(0),{\operatorname{T}}}]^{\operatorname{T}}
must be at least of dimension
N
such that it contains all initial features for the edges
(n,j)
for
n=1,\dots,N
.
Similar to
[
7
]
, we set the output dimension of the feature extractor
\bm{F}_{\theta}
such that
\bm{f}_{nj}^{(0)}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
for
n=1,\dots,N
and
j=1,\dots,J
.
Let
\bm{f}_{nj}^{(\ell-1)}
denote the feature vector associated with the edge
(n,j)
at the input of the
\ell
-th layer, with
\ell=1,\dots,L
.
The update rule for the edge
(n,j)
is then
[
5
,
7
]

\bm{f}_{nj}^{(\ell)}=f_{\text{act}}(\bm{S}^{(\ell)}\bm{f}_{nj}^{(\ell-1)}+\alpha\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq n\end{subarray}}^{N}\bm{T}^{(\ell)}\bm{f}_{ij}^{(\ell-1)}+\beta\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{J}\bm{a}^{(\ell)}_{jk}\odot\bm{u}_{nk}^{(\ell)})

(4)

with
\bm{a}_{jk}^{(\ell)}
obtained through a linear attention mechanism as

\displaystyle\bm{q}_{nj}^{(\ell)}=\bm{Q}^{(\ell)}\bm{f}_{nj}^{(\ell-1)}\quad\bm{k}_{nj}^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle=\bm{K}^{(\ell)}\bm{f}_{nj}^{(\ell-1)}\quad\bm{u}_{nj}^{(\ell)}=\bm{U}^{(\ell)}\bm{f}_{nj}^{(\ell-1)}

\displaystyle\bm{a}_{jk}^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{n=1}^{N}\bm{q}_{nj}^{(\ell)}\odot\bm{k}_{nk}^{(\ell)}/N.

(5)

The matrices
\bm{S}^{(\ell)}
,
\bm{T}^{(\ell)}
,
\bm{Q}^{(\ell)}
,
\bm{K}^{(\ell)}
,
\bm{U}^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb{R}^{{M_{\ell}}\times{M_{\ell-1}}}
, denote the trainable parameters of layer
\ell
and are used to update the features associated with all edges
(n,j)
for
n=1,\dots,N
and
j=1,\dots,J
.
The hyperparameters
\alpha
and
\beta
are scalar coefficients,
f_{\text{act}}
denotes the activation function, and
\odot
denotes the Hadamard product.
The features at the last layer
\bm{f}_{nj}^{(L)}
are enforced to be in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that the elements correspond to the real and imaginary parts of the entry
v^{\prime}_{nj}
in the
n
-th row and
j
-th column of the matrix containing the non-normalized precoders
\bm{V}^{\prime}=[\bm{v}^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\bm{v}^{\prime}_{J}]
, i.e.,
\bm{f}_{nj}^{(L)}=[\Re(v^{\prime}_{nj}),\Im(v^{\prime}_{nj})]^{\operatorname{T}}
.
After normalizing the matrix
\bm{V}^{\prime}
to satisfy the power constraint, the precoders
\{\bm{v}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}
are obtained.
In
Fig.

1
, the block diagram of the proposed
GNN
-based precoder design approach utilizing statistical knowledge is depicted.

During the training stage, the sum-rate expression from (
1
) is used as the negative loss function, and we utilize the training set
\mathcal{D}_{\text{genie}}=\{\{(\bm{c}_{j}^{(d)},\bm{h}_{j}^{(d)})\}_{j=1}^{J}\}_{d=1}^{D}
, i.e., the training set consists of in total
D
multi-user scenarios of
J

MTs
, where for each
MT
the perfect statistical knowledge
\bm{C}_{{\bm{\delta}}_{j}}^{(d)}
and the corresponding channel
\bm{h}_{j}^{(d)}
is available.
The
GNN
generalizes to different numbers of
MTs
by construction.
To enable operation at different
SNR
levels, we train the
GNN
for an
SNR
range from
0\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}
to
20\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}
by adjusting the noise variance accordingly.
Thus, only one
GNN
model is required to design the precoders for a varying number of
MTs
and different
SNR
levels, which is beneficial in terms of memory.
Since the
GNN
consists of a few layers only, and the feature updates in each layer can be processed in parallel, the inference time can be accelerated, where the computational complexity of the
GNN
module is then
\mathcal{O}(\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}M_{\ell}M_{\ell-1})

[
7
]
.

However, assuming perfect statistical knowledge is not practical.
In the following, we adapt the
GNN
-based precoder design framework to systems with approximate statistical knowledge obtained via the generative modeling-based feedback scheme in
[
8
]
.
This scheme utilizes a
GMM
as a generative prior, enabling the inference and feedback of approximate statistical information through limited feedback with
B
bits.

III-B

Combining GMMs and GNNs for Limited Feedback-based Precoder Design

Each channel
\bm{h}
in the
BS
environment follows an unknown
PDF

f_{\bm{h}}
.
The channels
\bm{h}_{j}
of
MTs
within the
BS
’ coverage area are thus treated as realizations of a random variable with
PDF

f_{\bm{h}}
, though this
PDF
is not analytically available.
To approximate
f_{\bm{h}}
given a set of representative channel samples, the method in
[
8
]
employs a
GMM
with
K=2^{B}
components

f^{(K)}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{h}_{j})=\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K}\pi_{k}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(\bm{h}_{j};{\bm{\mu}}_{k},\bm{C}_{k}),

(6)

encoding environmental knowledge through conditional Gaussian distributions.
The means of the
GMM
-components are set to zero, i.e.,
{\bm{\mu}}_{k}=\bm{0}
for all
k\in\{1,\dots,K\}
, where
[
13
]
provides a theoretical justification for this regularization.
Additionally, depending on the
BS
antenna array geometry, the
GMM
covariance matrices are constrained to the structure
\bm{C}_{k}=\bm{Q}^{\operatorname{H}}\diag(\bm{q}_{k})\bm{Q}
.
For a
ULA
with
N
elements,
\bm{Q}
contains the first
N
columns of a
2N\times 2N

DFT
matrix and
\bm{q}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2N}
, such that the
GMM
covariance matrices are Toeplitz matrices.
For a
URA
with
N_{\mathrm{v}}
vertical and
N_{\mathrm{h}}
horizontal (
N=N_{\mathrm{v}}N_{\mathrm{h}}
) elements, we have
\bm{Q}=\bm{Q}_{N_{\mathrm{v}}}\otimes\bm{Q}_{N_{\mathrm{h}}}
, where
\bm{Q}_{T}
(with
T\in\{N_{\mathrm{v}},N_{\mathrm{h}}\}
) contains the first
T
columns of a
2T\times 2T

DFT
matrix, and
\bm{q}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4N}
, such that the
GMM
covariance matrices are block-Toeplitz matrices with Toeplitz blocks
[
14
]
.
The structured covariance matrices reduce the number of learnable parameters, mitigating overfitting.
The
GMM
parameters, i.e.,
\{\pi_{k},\bm{C}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
, are shared among all
MTs
, and are learned in an offline phase.
Maximum likelihood estimates of the
GMM
parameters are obtained from a training set
\mathcal{H}
, using an
expectation maximization (EM)
algorithm with an adapted M-step following
[
14
]
and the means set to zero.

In the online phase, each
MT
infers its feedback information from the pilot observation
\bm{y}_{j}
using the
GMM
of the observation

f_{\bm{y}}^{(K)}(\bm{y}_{j})=\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K}\pi_{k}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(\bm{y}_{j};\mathbf{0},\bm{P}\bm{C}_{k}\bm{P}^{\operatorname{H}}+{\bm{\Sigma}}).

(7)

Each
MT

j
then computes the responsibilities for
\bm{y}_{j}
as follows

p(k\mid\bm{y}_{j})=\frac{\pi_{k}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(\bm{y}_{j};\mathbf{0},\bm{P}\bm{C}_{k}\bm{P}^{\operatorname{H}}+{\bm{\Sigma}})}{\sum_{i=1}^{K}\pi_{i}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(\bm{y}_{j};\mathbf{0},\bm{P}\bm{C}_{i}\bm{P}^{\operatorname{H}}+{\bm{\Sigma}})},

(8)

and selects the feedback index
k_{j}^{\star}
through a
maximum a posteriori (MAP)
estimation
[
8
]
:

k^{\star}_{j}=\argmax_{k}~{p(k\mid\bm{y}_{j})}

(9)

Evaluating (
9
) does not scale with
N
and has a complexity of only
\mathcal{O}(Kn_{\mathrm{p}}^{2})
, a key advantage in massive
MIMO
systems
[
8
]
.

For performance reference, perfect
CSI
can be assumed at each
MT
to determine the feedback index as
[
8
]
:

k^{\star}_{j}=\argmax_{k}~{p(k\mid\bm{h}_{j})}

(10)

After the
MTs
send their feedback indices to the
BS
, the
GMM
-based statistical information for each
MT
, i.e., assuming
\bm{h}_{j}
is distributed as
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbf{0},\bm{C}_{k^{\star}_{j}})
, is utilized for precoder design via the
GNN
.
Due to the structured
GMM
covariance matrices, each
\bm{C}_{k}
is fully represented by its first row.
Thus, for each
MT
, the first row of the respective
GMM
component’s covariance matrix,
\bm{c}_{j}=\bm{C}_{k^{\star}_{j}}[1,:]
, is extracted, of which the real and imaginary parts are stacked, and passed through the feature extractor
\bm{F}_{\theta}
to obtain the input feature vectors for the
GNN
.
The
GNN
output then provides the precoders
\{\bm{v}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}
after normalization, cf.
Fig.

1
and
Section

III-A
.

To account for the
GMM
-based feedback for precoder design using the
GNN
, the training set
\mathcal{D}=\{\{(\bm{c}_{j}^{(d)},\bm{h}_{j}^{(d)})\}_{j=1}^{J}\}_{d=1}^{D}
again includes
D
multi-user scenarios with
J

MTs
.
For each
MT
, statistical information
\bm{c}_{j}^{(d)}=\bm{C}_{k^{\star}_{j}}^{(d)}[1,:]
is obtained through a pilot observation
\bm{y}_{j}^{(d)}
using the
GMM
via (
9
), and the corresponding channel
\bm{h}_{j}^{(d)}
is available in the offline training phase.
When assuming perfect
CSI
at each
MT
,
\bm{c}_{j}^{(d)}
is obtained utilizing (
10
).
The
GNN
is trained for an
SNR
range from
0\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}
to
20\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}
and enables precoder design for a varying number of
MTs
.
Moreover, the
GMM
-based feedback scheme allows multi-user operation for a varying number of
MTs

J
, and due to the analytic representation of the
GMM
, facilitates adaptation to any
SNR
level, without requiring re-training.
This combination of the
GMM
-based feedback scheme and the
GNN
-based precoder design yields a fully flexible approach for multi-user systems, where the
GMM
knowledge is required at both the
MTs
and the
BS
, while the
GNN
knowledge is only required at the
BS
.

IV
Baseline Schemes for Precoder Design

As a baseline, the
SWMMSE
algorithm from
[
2
]
is used for precoder design.
The
SWMMSE
aims to maximize the ergodic sum-rate by treating the channels of all
MTs
as random variables and iteratively updates the precoders.
Each iteration
i
requires a sample of every
MT
’s channel for the update.
It was shown that the
SWMMSE
converges to a set of stationary points of the ergodic sum-rate maximization problem for
i\to\infty

[
2
]
.
For practical implementation, however, a finite maximum number of iterations,
I_{\max}
is specified.
With perfect statistical knowledge, samples are generated as
\tilde{\bm{h}}_{j}^{(i)}\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}({\bm{0}},\bm{C}_{{\bm{\delta}}_{j}})
for each
MT

j
and provided to the
SWMMSE
.
Alternatively, when statistical information for each
MT
is obtained using the
GMM
via (
9
) or (
10
), samples are drawn as
\tilde{\bm{h}}_{j}^{(i)}\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}({\bm{0}},\bm{C}_{k^{\star}_{j}})
and fed to the
SWMMSE

[
8
]
.

Another baseline is the
DFT
codebook-based limited feedback scheme, where each
MT
estimates its channel
\hat{\bm{h}}_{j}
and selects its feedback as
k^{\star}_{j}=\argmax_{k}|\bm{w}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\hat{\bm{h}}_{j}|
with
\bm{w}_{k}\in\mathcal{W}
, using a codebook
\mathcal{W}=\{\bm{w}_{1},\dots,\bm{w}_{K}\}
of size
|\mathcal{W}|=K=2^{B}

[
15
]
.
Depending on whether a
ULA
or
URA
is deployed, a
DFT
codebook or a 2D-
DFT
codebook is used, respectively
[
16
]
.
At the
BS
, each channel is represented as
\hat{\bm{h}}^{(q)}_{j}=\bm{w}_{k^{\star}_{j}}

[
15
]
, and the
IWMMSE

[
17
, Algorithm 1]
is used.

V
Simulation Results

In the case of the spatial channel model (see
Section

II-C1
), we simulate a
BS
with
N=64
antenna elements, assuming a single propagation cluster composed of multiple sub-paths per
MT
.
To train the
GNN
using perfect statistical knowledge, we generate the set
\mathcal{D}_{\text{genie}}
with
D=2{,}400

J=16

MTs
.

VI
Conclusion & Outlook

In this letter, we proposed a
GNN
-based framework for statistical precoder design, leveraging model-based insights to achieve a compact representation of statistical knowledge, resulting in lightweight architectures.
The framework is extended to handle approximate statistical information in
FDD
systems via a
GMM
-based limited feedback scheme, and demonstrates strong performance in simulations.
Future work could explore alternative generative models like
vector quantized-variational autoencoder (VQ-VAE)
-based scheme from
[
19
]
and develop an end-to-end system that additionally learns the pilot matrix.

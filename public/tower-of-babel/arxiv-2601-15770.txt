Classical Simulation of Noiseless Quantum Dynamics without Randomness
Jue Xu, Chu Zhao, Xiangran Zhang, Shuchen Zhu, Qi Zhao

Abstract
Simulating noiseless quantum dynamics classically faces a fundamental dilemma: tensor-network methods become inefficient as entanglement saturates, while Pauli-truncation approaches typically rely on noise or randomness. To close the gap, we propose the Low-weight Pauli Dynamics (LPD) algorithm that efficiently approximates local observables for short-time dynamics in the absence of noise. We prove that the truncation error admits an average-case bound without assuming randomness, provided that the state is sufficiently entangled. Counterintuitively, entanglement–usually an obstacle for classical simulation–alleviates classical simulation error. We further show that such entangled states can be generated either by tensor-network classical simulation or near-term quantum devices. Thus, our results establish a rigorous synergy between existing classical simulation methods and provide a complementary route to quantum simulation that reduces circuit depth for long-time dynamics, thereby extending the accessible regime of quantum dynamics.

Surprisingly, even without noise and randomness,
the Pauli truncation algorithms still perform well for certain Hamiltonian dynamics.
Begušić et al.

proposed the heuristics by truncating small-coefficient Paulis in Trotterized evolution,

These results motivate a systematic study of the theoretical guarantees and limitations of Pauli truncation for noiseless Hamiltonian dynamics without randomness assumptions.

To bridge this gap,
we propose a provably efficient classical algorithm called
Low-weight Pauli Dynamics
(
LPD
) for short-time Hamiltonian dynamics,
which approximates observables by truncating high-weight Pauli operators at each Trotter step.
Owing to locality of Paulis and light-cone, the growth of Pauli weights are constrained, and the contributions of high-weight Paulis to expectation values are suppressed by small rotation angles even in the absence of noise.
Moreover, we prove that if the input state has sufficient entanglement entropy,
the Pauli truncation error in expectation satisfies a nontrivial average-case bound even without randomness.
We show that the sufficiently entangled states are tractable to tensor network methods

and they can be prepared by near-term quantum simulators
Zhao et al. 2025
.
Naturally, we combine
LPD
with the tensor network state simulation to effectively extend accessible classical simulation time.
On the other hand, our classically evolved low-weight observables essentially reduce the quantum simulation circuit depth for evolving quantum states.
We numerically show the effectiveness of our algorithm with the choatic quantum mixed-field Ising Hamiltonian.

Analysis: error and complexity
.–

Most prior works on Pauli truncation
Aharonov et al. 2023
;
Shao et al. 2024
;
Angrisani et al. 2025b
require the randomness in quantum circuits to cancel cross terms to obtain a non-trivial error bound.
This trick is suitable for random circuit sampling or parameterized quantum algorithms,
but not directly apply to Trotterized Hamiltonian dynamics.
To overcome it, Schuster et al.

leveraged the randomness of input states to achieve average-case bounds for any circuits.
We realize an average-case bound with a milder assumption:
a sufficiently entangled input state rather than an ensemble of random states.
More importantly, we bound the high-weight Pauli contribution in noiseless Hamiltonian dynamics
by exploiting the properties of small-angle local Pauli rotations.

Sketch proof of
Theorem

1

.

The runtime analysis is mainly to bound the number of Paulis in all steps.

all Paulis in the observable have weight at most
w^{*}
by our

So, the observable has at most

Paulis.
After applying the Pauli rotations,
the Pauli operators would proliferate and the weights may also increase.
The good news is that the weight increase is system-size independent
because a local Pauli rotation only acts on constant number of qubits of a Pauli
and generate at most one new Pauli with at most a constant weight increase once.
Therefore, if each Trotter step is a shallow (constant depth) circuit,
by the constrained light-cone expansion, the maximum weights of Paulis at the end of every Trotter step are still system-size independent and the number of Paulis is still bounded by
\mathcal{O}(n^{w^{*}})
.

The last step of
LPD
is to calculate the expectation value of the observable with respect to a given state
\rho
,
that is
\Tr(\tO^{(r)}_{\le w}\rho)
.
Assume each coefficient of
\rho
in Pauli basis can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(1)
time,
then the expectation value can be computed in time proportional to the number of Pauli operators in the final observable by orthogonality of Pauli operators.
So, the evaluation of the expectation does not incur runtime more than one step Pauli propagation.
Correspondingly, the memory requirement is the maximum number of Pauli operators during the evolution
\mathcal{O}(n^{w^{*}})
,
which is also polynomial if the truncation weight is system-size independent.

L_{j,j^{\prime}}:=\Tr_{[N]\backslash\textup{{supp}}(O_{j}^{\dagger}O_{j^{\prime}})}(O^{\dagger}_{j}O_{j^{\prime}})

and we have
2^{-n}\cdot\Tr(O^\dagger_j O_{j'})=\Tr(L_{j,j'})\cdot 2^{-\absolutevalue{\supp(O^\dagger_j O_{j'})}}
where
n
is the complete system size.
Then, we have the upper bound of each term

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\bra{\psi} O_j^\dagger O_{j'} \ket{\psi}}=

\displaystyle\Tr(L_{j,j'} \rho_{j,j'})

(RDM)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Tr[L_{j,j^{\prime}}(\rho_{j,j^{\prime}}-\bar{I}_{jj^{\prime}})]+\Tr(L_{j,j'})/2^{\absolutevalue{\supp(O^\dagger_j O_{j'})}}

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Tr[L_{j,j^{\prime}}(\rho_{j,j^{\prime}}-\bar{I}_{jj^{\prime}})]+2^{-n}\cdot\Tr(O^\dagger_j O_{j'})

(RDM)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\norm{L_{j,j'}}\Tr\absolutevalue{\rho_{j,j'} - \bar{I}_{jj'}}+2^{-n}\cdot\Tr(O^\dagger_j O_{j'})

(Hölder’s ineq)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\norm{O_j^\dagger O_{j'}}\Tr\absolutevalue{\rho_{j,j'} - \bar{I}_{jj'}}+2^{-n}\cdot\Tr(O^\dagger_j O_{j'}).

(25)

where
\bar{I}_{jj^{\prime}}:=\frac{I_{\textup{{supp}}(O^{\dagger}_{j}O_{j^{\prime}})}}{2^{\absolutevalue{\supp(O^\dagger_j O_{j'})}}}
denotes the maximally mixed state on the subsystem
\textup{{supp}}(O^{\dagger}_{j}O_{j^{\prime}})
.
Summing the above over
j,j^{\prime}
gives the desired upper bound

\absolutevalue{\bra{\psi} O \ket{\psi}}^{2}\leq\norm{O}_{\bar{2}}^{2}+\sum_{j,j^{\prime}}\norm{O_j^\dagger O_{j'}}\Tr\absolutevalue{\rho_{j,j'}-\bar{I}_{jj'}},

where the squared 2-norm
\norm{O}_{\bar{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{j}\Tr(O_j O_j)
appears by orthogonality of cross terms.
Moreover, the trace distance of
\rho_{j,j^{\prime}}
and
\bar{I}_{jj^{\prime}}
can be bounded by the relative entropy as

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline pd:eq:trace_{d}istance_{e}ntropy}\Tr\absolutevalue{\rho_{j,j'}-\bar{I}_{jj'}}\leq\sqrt{2S\quantity(\rho_{j,j'}||\bar{I}_{jj'})}=\sqrt{2\absolutevalue{\supp(O^\dagger_j O_{j'})}-2S(\rho_{j,j^{\prime}})}

(Pinsker’s ineq)

where
S(\rho||\sigma):=\Tr(\rho\log\rho- \rho\log\sigma)
is the relative entropy between
\rho
and
\sigma
.
∎

Now, we have the similar 2-norm upper bound of a local expectation values for a sufficiently entangled state.

In the words of entanglement entropy, if the input state

B.2
Trotter formula error analysis

The
p
th-order Trotter-Suzuki formula
Suzuki 1991
with leading error
\order{(\|H\|t)^{p+1}}
is constructed recursively as:

\displaystyle\tilde{U}_{p}(t)=\tilde{U}_{p-2}(u_{p}t)^{2}\tilde{U}_{p-2}(1-4u_{p}t)\tilde{U}_{p-2}(u_{p}t)^{2},

(30)

where
u_{p}=1/(4-4^{1/(p-1)})
and the second-order Suzuki formula is
\tilde{U}_{2}(t)=\prod_{\gamma=1}^{\Gamma}e^{-\textup{i}H_{\gamma}t/2}\prod_{\gamma=\Gamma}^{1}e^{-\textup{i}H_{\gamma}t/2}
.
This recursive construction incurs a
\Upsilon=2\cdot 5^{p/2-1}
overhead of gate counts (depth) in the high-order Trotter formula.
To balance the performance with the overhead of high-order product formula, it is usually to use the second-order Trotter-Suzuki formula.
The Trotter error the
p
th-order product formula is defined as
\norm{\pf_p - U}_{\mathrm{\infty}}

where the operator norm (also known as the Schatten
\infty
-norm) captures the worst-case (input state) analysis
Childs et al. 2021
.
We first prove the worst-case Trotter error bound in expectation for any input state.

Lemma B.3

(Worst-case Trotter error in expectation)
.

Given a Hamiltonian
H=\sum_{\gamma=1}^{\Gamma}H_{\gamma}
in the decomposition into commuting groups, any input state
\rho
and observable
O
,
the worst-case Trotter error of the
r
-step
p
th-order Suzuki product formula
Eq.

30
is bounded by

\absolutevalue{\Tr[(\pf^{\dagger r}_p O \pf_p^r - U^\dagger O U)\rho]}=\mathcal{O}(\norm{O}_{\mathrm{\infty}}\Lambda\cdot t^{p+1}/r^{p})

(31)

where
U:=e^{-\textup{i}Ht}
is the ideal evolution unitary.
Consequently, the required number of Trotter steps to achieve error tolerance
\epsilon
in expectation for any input states is

r\in\mathcal{O}((\norm{O}_{\mathrm{\infty}}\Lambda\epsilon^{-1})^{1/p}t^{1+1/p})
.

Proof.

We have the following upper bound on the worst-case Trotter error in expectation

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\Tr[(\pf_p^{\dagger r} O \pf_p^{r} - U^\dagger O U)\rho]}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\pf_p^{\dagger r} O \pf_p^{r} - U^\dagger O U}_{\infty}\cdot\norm{\rho}_{1}

(Hölder’s ineq)

\displaystyle=\norm{\pf_p^{\dagger r} O \pf_p^{r} - U^\dagger O U}_{\infty}

(
\norm{\rho}_{1}=1
)

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\pf_p^{\dagger r} O \pf_p^{r} - \pf_p^{\dagger r} O U}_{\infty}+\norm{\pf_p^{\dagger r} O U - U^\dagger O U}_{\infty}

(Triangle ineq)

\displaystyle\leq 2\norm{O\pf_p^{r}-OU}_{\infty}

(Unitarity)

\displaystyle\leq 2\norm{O}_{\infty}\cdot\norm{\pf_p^r - U}_{\infty}

(Sub-multiplicativity)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(\norm{O}_{\mathrm{\infty}}\Lambda\cdot t^{p+1}/r^{p}),

(
\norm{\pf_p^r- U}_{\mathrm{\infty}}=\mathcal{O}(\Lambda\cdot t^{p+1}/r^{p})
)

where
\norm{\pf_p^r- U}_{\mathrm{\infty}}
is the worst-case Trotter error of any states and

\Lambda:=\sum_{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{p+1}=1}^{\Gamma}\norm{[H_{\gamma_p+1},[H_{\gamma_p},...,[H_{\gamma_2},H_{\gamma_1}]]]}_{\mathrm{\infty}}
is the operator norm of the nested commutator
Childs et al. 2021
.
∎

When considering the state information of the input state,
e.g., an ensemble of random states or an entangled state,
we can improve the worst-case Trotter error bound from the operator norm
\norm{\cdot}_{\mathrm{\infty}}
to the normalized Schatten (Pauli) 2-norm
\norm{\cdot}_{\bar{2}}
.
Assume an ensemble of random input states, the Theorem 3 of Ref.
Zhao et al. 2021
;
Yu et al. 2025
proved the average-case Trotter error bound in expectation value.
We restate it as follow and refer to the original paper for the proof.

Lemma B.4

(Average-case Trotter error in expectation with random input state)
.

For a 2-design ensemble
\mathscr{E}_{2}
of quantum states and an observable
O
,
the average Trotter error

\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mathscr{E}_{2}}\quantity[\abs{\Tr[(\pf_p^{\dagger r} O \pf^r_p - U^\dagger O U)\rho]}]

of the standard
r
-step
p
th-order Suzuki product formula
Eq.

30
has the upper bound

\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\bar{2}}\norm{O}_{\bar{2}}t^{p+1}r^{-p})
,
where
\Lambda_{\bar{2}}:=\sum_{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{p+1}=1}^{\Gamma}\norm{[H_{\gamma_p+1},[H_{\gamma_p},...,[H_{\gamma_2},H_{\gamma_1}]]]}_{\bar{2}}
is the normalized Schatten (Pauli) 2-norm of the nested commutator and
H=\sum_{\gamma=1}^{\Gamma}H_{\gamma}
is the decomposition of Hamiltonian terms into commuting groups.
Consequently, the required number of Trotter steps
r
for average Trotter error
\epsilon
is

r\in\mathcal{O}((\Lambda_{\bar{2}}\norm{O}_{\bar{2}}\epsilon^{-1})^{1/p}t^{1+1/p})

showed the 2-norm bound of the Trotter error can be achieved without randomness.

B.1

Lemma B.5

(Pauli 2-norm bound of the Trotter error in expectation with an entangled state)
.

Given an entangled state
\ket{\psie}
as required in

B.1
and a local observable
O

the Trotter error

\absolutevalue{\Tr[(\pf_p^{\dagger r} O \pf_p^r - U^\dagger O U)\op{\psie}]}

of the standard

th-order Suzuki product formula

has the upper bound

\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\bar{2}}\norm{O}_{\bar{2}}t^{p+1}r^{-p})

Proof.

The proof follows similar steps as in
Lemma

B.3
except the first step,
where we use the 2-norm bound of the expectation value for the entangled state from

instead of the Hölder’s inequality.

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\Tr[(\pf_p^{\dagger r} O \pf_p^r - U^\dagger O U)\op{\psie}]}

\displaystyle=\absolutevalue{\bra{\psie} (\pf_p^{\dagger r} O \pf_p^r - U^\dagger O U) \ket{\psie}}

(32)

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\pf_p^{\dagger r} O \pf_p^r - U^\dagger O U}_{\bar{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\norm{O}_{\bar{2}}\cdot\norm{\pf_p^r - U}_{\bar{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(\norm{O}_{\bar{2}}\Lambda_{\bar{2}}\cdot t^{p+1}/r^{p}),

(
\norm{\pf_1- U}_{\bar{2}}=\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\bar{2}}\cdot t^{2}/r^{2})
)

where
\Lambda_{\bar{2}}:=\sum_{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{p+1}=1}^{\Gamma}\norm{[H_{\gamma_p+1},[H_{\gamma_p},...,[H_{\gamma_2},H_{\gamma_1}]]]}_{\bar{2}}
is
the Pauli 2-norm of the nested commutator,

Zhao et al. 2021

∎

To begin with, we recall the important equation of Pauli rotation

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline pd:eq:pauli_{r}otation_{b}ranch}e^{\textup{i}G\textup{d}t/2}Pe^{-\textup{i}G\textup{d}t/2}=\begin{cases}P,&[G,P]=0\\

(33)

This equation has three crucial implications

(1) the transition is sparse in the Pauli basis;
(2) the weight change is limited by the locality of

(3) the transition amplitude is damped by
\sin(\dt)

More explicitly,
when the generator

when they anti-commute
\{G,P\}=0

then the weight of

if
G
has weight at most
k
.

And importantly, if the rotation angle
\textup{d}t
is small,
then the newly generated Pauli

has its coefficient damped by small factor
\sin(\dt)\sim\textup{d}t
.

See the illustration in

(a).

Next, we are going to prove the 2-norm upper bound on the Pauli truncation error in expectation for an ensemble of random states as well as an entangled state.

Lemma B.6

(2-norm upper bound of average Pauli Truncation error)
.

The average Pauli error in the expectation value
with respect to 2-design states

upper bounded by the normalized Schatten (Pauli) 2-norm of the two observables’ difference

\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mathscr{E}_{2}}\quantity[\abs{\Tr[(\tO^{(r)} - \tO^{(r)}_{\le w^*})\rho]}]\leq\norm{\tO^{(r)} - \tO^{(r)}_{\le w^*}}_{\bar{2}},

By the Jensen’s inequality,

(41)

where

\mathcal{N}_{\geq m}^{(g)}:=\sum_{w>w_{m}}\norm{O_{=w}^{(g)}}_{\bar{2}}

and
w_{m}:=k_{o}+(m-1)(k_{h}-1)
.

Proof.

To analyize the local flow of the norm, we partition the weight range into three parts:
(i) weight
w<w_{m-1}
;
(ii) weight
w\in[w_{m-1}+1,w_{m})
;
(iii) weight
w\geq w_{m}
.
Then, we consider the contribution to
\mathcal{N}_{\geq m}^{(g)}
from each part separately:

•

For case (i), the Pauli operators with weight
w<w_{m-1}
can only flow to weight
w<w_{m-1}+(k-1)=w_{m}
by one
k_{h}
-local Pauli rotation,
which does not contribute to the cumulative norm
\mathcal{N}_{\geq m}^{(g)}
.

•

For case (ii), the Pauli operators with weight in the range
w\in[w_{m-1}+1,w_{m})
could flow to weight
w\geq w_{m}
.
In the extreme case: all Paulis in the observable anti-commute with the rotation generator leading to weight increasement.
However, the norm flow should be damped by the small rotation angle
\sin(\dt)
, cf.
Eq.

33
.
So, it contributes to the cumulative norm
\mathcal{N}_{\geq m}^{(g)}
by
\sin(\dt)\cdot\mathcal{N}_{\geq m-1}^{(g-1)}
.

•

For case (iii), the Pauli operators could stay in the weight range
w\geq w_{m}
or even flow to lower weight range
w<w_{m}
.
So, the contribution to
\mathcal{N}_{\geq m}^{(g)}
from this part is at most
\mathcal{N}_{\geq m}^{(g-1)}
.

It can be formally written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\geq m}^{(g)}:

\displaystyle=\sum_{w>w_{m}}\norm{O_{=w}^{(g)}}_{\bar{2}}=\sum_{w>w_{m}}\sqrt{\sum_{s\in\mathbb{\overline{P}}_{n},\absolutevalue{s}=w}\Tr(O^{(g)} s)^{2}}

(
Eq.

37
&
Eq.

40
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w>w_{m}}\sqrt{\sum_{s\in\mathbb{\overline{P}}_{n},\absolutevalue{s}=w}\Tr(e^{\ii G_g\dt} O^{(g-1)}e^{-\ii G_g\dt} s)^{2}}

(
G_{g}
rotation)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w>w_{m}}\sqrt{\sum_{s\in\mathbb{\overline{P}}_{n},\absolutevalue{s}=w}\Tr(\qty(\sum_{P\in O^{(g-1)}} \cos(\dt)P+\ii\sin(\dt)G_g P )s)^{2}}

(
Eq.

33
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w>w_{m}}\sqrt{\cos^{2}(\textup{d}t)\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in\mathbb{\overline{P}}_{n}:\\
\absolutevalue{s}=w\end{subarray}}\Tr(\sum_{\substack{P\in O^{(g-1)}: \\ \abs{P}=w}}Ps)^{2}-\sin^{2}(\textup{d}t)\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in\mathbb{\overline{P}}_{n}:\\
\absolutevalue{s}=w\end{subarray}}\Tr(\sum_{\substack{P\in O^{(g-1)}:\\w-\kh+1<\abs{P}<w+\kh-1}} G_g Ps )^{2}}

(Orthogonality)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{w>w_{m}}\quantity{
\cos(\dt)\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{\substack{s\in\npbasis_{n}:\\ \abs{s}=w}} \Tr(\sum_{\substack{P\in O^{(g-1)}: \\ \abs{P}=w}}Ps)^2}
+
\sin(\dt)\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{\substack{s\in\npbasis_{n}:\\ \abs{s}=w}}\Tr(\sum_{\substack{P\in O^{(g-1)}:\\w-\kh+1<\abs{P}<w+\kh-1}} G_g Ps )^2}
}

(Triangle ineq)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{w>w_{m}}\sqrt{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in\mathbb{\overline{P}}_{n}:\\
\absolutevalue{s}=w\end{subarray}}\Tr(\sum_{P\in O^{(g-1)} }Ps)^{2}}+\sin(\dt)\cdot\sum_{w>w_{m-1}}\sqrt{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in\mathbb{\overline{P}}_{n}:\\
\absolutevalue{s}=w\end{subarray}}\Tr(\sum_{P\in O^{(g-1)}} G_g Ps )^{2}}

(Relax)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{w>w_{m}}\norm{O_{=w}^{(g-1)}}_{\bar{2}}+\sin(\dt)\sum_{w>w_{m-1}}\norm{O_{=w}^{(g-1)}}_{\bar{2}}

(By definition)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}_{\geq m}^{(g-1)}+\sin(\dt)\cdot\mathcal{N}_{\geq m-1}^{(g-1)}

(By definition)

This damped norm flow yields the desired recursion relation.
∎

The recursion relation leads to the upper bound of the sum of norms of the high-weight components as following.

Corollary B.2

(High-weight norm cumulation)
.

Given the same input local observable and Pauli rotations in

B.7
,
the cumulative Pauli 2-norm of the evolved observable with jump count
\geq m
after applying the

-th Pauli rotation is at most

\mathcal{N}_{\geq m}^{(g)}\leq k_{o}\binom{g}{m}\sin^{m}(\textup{d}t)\norm{O}_{\bar{2}}.

(42)

Proof.

We prove it by induction.
For the
base case

(m=1)
,
by
Lemma

B.7
iteratively, we have for any

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\geq 1}^{(g)}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{N}_{\geq 1}^{(g-1)}+\sin(\dt)\cdot\mathcal{N}_{\geq 0}^{(g-1)}

(
Lemma

B.7
)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{N}_{\geq 1}^{(0)}+\sum_{l=0}^{g-1}\sin(\dt)\cdot\mathcal{N}_{\geq 0}^{(l)}

(Iterate)

\displaystyle\leq g\cdot\sin(\dt)\cdot k_{o}\cdot\norm{O}_{\bar{2}},

(43)

where
\mathcal{N}_{\geq 0}^{(l)}\leq k_{o}\cdot\norm{O}_{\bar{2}}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\geq 1}^{(0)}=0
.
It yields the desired result for
m=1
.

Then, for the
inductive step
,
we assume
\forall j,\;\mathcal{N}_{\geq m-1}^{(j)}\leq\binom{j}{m-1}(\sin(\dt))^{m-1}\norm{O}_{\bar{2}}
.
Then, we prove the case
m
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\geq m}^{(g)}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{N}_{\geq m}^{(g-1)}+\sin(\dt)\cdot\mathcal{N}_{\geq m-1}^{(g-1)}

(By

B.7
)

\displaystyle\leq\sin(\dt)\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{g-1}\mathcal{N}_{\geq m-1}^{(j)}

(Iterate over

and
\mathcal{N}_{\geq m}^{(0)}=0
)

\displaystyle\leq k_{o}\cdot\sin^{m}(\textup{d}t)\cdot\norm{O}_{\bar{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{g-1}\binom{j}{m-1}

(Induction hypothesis)

\displaystyle=k_{o}\binom{g}{m}\sin^{m}(\textup{d}t)\norm{O}_{\bar{2}}.

(hockey-stick identity)

It completes the induction.
∎

With

, we can upper bound the norm of the observable components with weight
w^{*}
at the end of each Trotter step
such that we can upper bound the truncation error of every Trotter step.

, we can bound the total Pauli trunction error in terms of the evolution time and locality of the input observable and Hamiltonian as follows.

Theorem B.1

(Pauli truncation error of all Trotter steps)
.

Consider a
k_{h}
-local Hamiltonian
H=\sum_{l}^{L}\alpha_{l}G_{l}=\sum_{\gamma=1}^{\Gamma}H_{\gamma}
,
a
k_{o}
-local input observable
O
,
and an sufficiently entangled input state.
Let
\alpha:=2\max_{l}\alpha_{l}

Trotter steps
Eq.

35

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline pd:eq:total_{t}runcation_{e}rror}\Delta\tilde{\mu}_{\leq w^{*}}\leq\sum_{d=1}^{r}\norm{\tO_{\ge w^*+1}^{(d)}}_{\bar{2}}\leq t\cdot 2k_{o}(k_{o}+k_{h})\cdot k_{h}^{\Gamma}\cdot\quantity(\alpha te \kh^\Gamma(\ko+\kh))^{m}\cdot\norm{O}_{\bar{2}}.

(44)

Proof.

At the beginning of every Trotter step in our algorithm, the weights of Paulis are at most
w^{*}
due to the Pauli truncation in the previous Trotter step.
Therefore, after applying the first layer of
L
Pauli rotations,
there are at most
w^{*}
rotations acting non-trivially and the maximum weight of each Pauli is
w^{*}(k-1)
.
After applying all
\Gamma
layers in one Trotter step,

w^{*}\sum_{\gamma=0}^{\Gamma-1}(k_{h}-1)^{\Gamma}<w^{*}k_{h}^{\Gamma}

.
So, by
Corollary

B.2
,
the norm of the weight-
w^{*}
component at the end of the
d
-th Trotter step is at most

\displaystyle\norm{\tO_{= w^*}^{(d)}}_{\bar{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{w\geq w^{*}}\norm{O_{= w^*}^{(d)}}_{\bar{2}}=\mathcal{N}_{\geq m^{*}}^{(d\cdot w^{*}k_{h}^{\Gamma})}

(By def
Eq.

40
)

\displaystyle\leq k_{o}\binom{d\cdot w^{*}k_{h}^{\Gamma}}{m^{*}}\quantity(\sin(\alpha\cdot\dt))^{m^{*}}\norm{O}_{\bar{2}}

(
Corollary

B.2
)

\displaystyle\leq k_{o}\quantity(\frac{ed\cdot w^* \kh^{\Gamma}}{m})^{m^{*}}(\alpha\cdot\textup{d}t)^{m^{*}}\norm{O}_{\bar{2}}

(Standard relax)

\displaystyle\leq k_{o}\quantity(\frac{\alpha tew^* \kh^{\Gamma} d}{rm^*})^{m^{*}}\cdot\norm{O}_{\bar{2}}

(

B.2

\displaystyle\leq k_{o}\quantity(\frac{\alpha te \kh^{\Gamma} (m^*(\kh-1)+\ko) d}{rm^*})^{m^{*}}\cdot\norm{O}_{\bar{2}}

(45)

where we relax the last line by
w^{*}/m^{*}<k_{o}+k_{h}
.

to ensure the error can be bounded,

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline pd:eq:time_{c}ondition}t<1/(e\alpha k_{h}^{\Gamma}(k_{o}+k_{h}))=t_{0}\in\mathcal{O}(1).

(46)

Under this short-time condition, the norm of the high-weight Paulis at the end of the

-th Trotter step is at most

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline pd:eq:flow_{b}eyond_{t}hreshold}\norm{\tO_{\ge w^*+1}^{(d)}}_{\bar{2}}\leq\sin(\dt)\cdot(w^{*}k_{h}^{\Gamma})\cdot k_{h}\cdot\norm{\tO_{= w^*}^{(d)}}_{\bar{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\textup{d}t^{2}),

(47)

assuming all Paulis with weight in the range

can flow beyond the threshold

Put all together, we can bound the Pauli truncation error in the approximated expectation of our algorithm

\displaystyle\Delta\tilde{\mu}_{\leq w^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{d=1}^{r}\norm{\tO_{\ge w^*+1}^{(d)}}_{\bar{2}}

(

)

\displaystyle\leq 2\textup{d}t\cdot w^{*}k_{h}^{\Gamma+1}\cdot\sum_{d=1}^{r}\norm{\tO_{= w^*}^{(d)}}_{\bar{2}}

(Iterate

)

\displaystyle\leq 2\textup{d}t\cdot w^{*}k_{h}^{\Gamma+1}\cdot k_{o}\cdot\quantity(\alpha te \kh^{\Gamma} (\ko+\kh))^{m^{*}}\sum_{d=1}^{r}\quantity(\frac{d}{r})^{m^{*}}\cdot\norm{O}_{\bar{2}}

(Insert

)

Corollary B.4

B.4
Runtime and memory analysis

Since every Trotter step is the same and all Paulis in the observable are low-weight (less than
w^{*}
) at the beginning of each Trotter step,
we only need to analyze the runtime and memory requirement of one Trotter step.
If
w^{*}
is independent of system size
n
,
the number of Pauli operators generated in one Trotter step will be polynomial in
n

such that the runtime and memory complexity of one Trotter step are polynomial in
n
.
Here, we give the detailed analysis.

Lemma B.8

(Proliferation of Pauli operators in one truncated Trotter step)
.

Consider a
w^{*}
-local observable
O=\sum_{j}^{J}\beta_{j}P_{j}
with
J\in\textup{poly}(n)

and a
k
-local Hamiltonian
H=\sum_{l}^{L}\alpha_{l}G_{l}
.
Assume the Hamiltonian terms can be rearranged into
\Gamma\in\mathcal{O}(1)
layers as
H=\sum_{\gamma}^{\Gamma}H_{\gamma}
such that one-step first-order Trotter consists of constant layers of Pauli rotations,
the number Pauli operators in the observable after applying the last gate in
the one-step Trotter before truncation is at most

\mathcal{O}(n^{w^{*}})
.

Proof.

By our algorithm, the weight of Pauli operators in
\tilde{O}
at the beginning of one Trotter step is at most
w^{*}

so the number of Paulis is at most
\sum_{w=1}^{w^{*}}3^{w}\binom{n}{w}\in\mathcal{O}(n^{w^{*}})
.
For simplicity, we first consider the case that all Hamiltonian terms are 2-local Pauli and the first-order Trotter.
Then, one layer of 2-local Pauli rotations can increase the weight of each Pauli operator by at most
w^{*}
in a
w^{*}
-local observable because there are at most
w^{*}
such rotations acting non-trivially on each Pauli operator and each rotation could increase Pauli weight at most
1
.
So, after applying
\Gamma
layers of local Pauli rotations,
the maximum weight of Paulis in the observable is at most
(\Gamma+1)w^{*}
.
However, it does not necessarily mean that the number of Paulis at the end of one-step Trotter has to be
\mathcal{O}(n^{(\Gamma+1)w^{*}})
.
It is because the transition (conjugation) of Pauli rotation is sparse,
i.e., one Pauli rotation acting on a Pauli operator only generates one new Pauli operator cf.
Eq.

33
.
See the example in
Fig.

5
(a).
Therefore, by this sparsity property, if
w^{*}\in\mathcal{O}(1)
and
\Gamma\in\mathcal{O}(1)
,
then the number of Paulis in the observable at the end of one Trotter step before truncation is at most

\sum_{w=1}^{w^{*}}3^{w}\quantity(\sum_{w=1}^{w^*} 3^w \binom{n}{w})\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{\Gamma}2^{jw^{*}+1}\leq\quantity(\frac{3e n}{w^*})^{w^{*}}\cdot 2^{w^{*}\Gamma(\Gamma+1)/2+\Gamma}\in\mathcal{O}(n^{w^{*}}),

(51)

where
2^{jw^{*}+1}
is the size of binary tree of height
jw^{*}
(the maximum weight after
j
layers).
The exponential in
w^{*}
and
\Gamma
is hided in the big-O notation because they are
\mathcal{O}(1)
.

Next, consider the Hamiltonian of
k
-local Paulis and the higher-order Trotter formulas.
For the
k
-local Hamiltonians with
k,\Gamma\in\mathcal{O}(1)
, the weight increase in one-step Trotter is still system-size independent.
One layer of
k
-local Pauli rotations can increase the weight of each Pauli in a
w^{*}
-local observable by at most
(k-1)w^{*}
.
On the other hand, the high-order Trotter formulas would introduce a constant overhead
\Upsilon=2\cdot 5^{p/2-1}
to the number of layers in one-step Trotter.
Since the weights of Pauli terms in the observable and the Hamiltonian as well as the depth of one-step Trotter are constant by assumption
(i.e.,
k
,
w^{*}
,
\Upsilon
, and
\Gamma\in\mathcal{O}(1)
),
the number of Paulis in one truncated Trotter step is still
\mathcal{O}(n^{w^{*}})
.

∎

With the maximum number of Paulis in one Trotter step,
we have the following upper bound of the total runtime (the number of inner products to calculate) and the corresponding memory requirement.

Theorem B.2

(Runtime of
LPD
Algorithm)
.

Given a local Hamiltonian
H=\sum_{\gamma}^{\Gamma}H_{\gamma}
with
\Gamma\in\mathcal{O}(1)
,
any
k_{o}
-local observable
O=\sum_{j}\beta_{j}P_{j}
of Paulis with
k_{o}\in\mathcal{O}(1)
,
evolution time
t<t_{0}\in\mathcal{O}(1)
, simulation precision
\epsilon
,
and an entangled input state
\ket{\psie}
required in
Corollary

B.1
.
Assume the Pauli coefficients of
H
,
O
and
\rho
can be efficiently computed.
There is a classical algorithm to approximate the expectation values
\mu=\Tr(\rho U^\dagger O U)
with error tolerance
2\epsilon
.
The classical algorithm runs with

\textup{time: }\mathcal{O}\quantity((r+1)\cdot n^{w^*} ),\quad\textup{memory: }\mathcal{O}\quantity(n^{w^*} ),

(52)

where the Trotter step
r=\mathcal{O}((\Lambda_{\bar{2}}\norm{O}_{\bar{2}}\epsilon^{-1})^{1/p}t^{1+1/p})

and the truncation weight threshold
w^{*}=k_{o}+\mathcal{O}\quantity(\frac{\log(t/\eps)}{\log(1/t)})
.

Proof.

The runtime analysis is mainly to
bound the number of non-zero transitions in all steps
.
Assume each transition amplitude,
i.e., the inner product between two Paulis
\forall P,Q\in\mathbb{\overline{P}},\Tr(P Q)
,
can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(1)
time.
Since all high-weight Paulis are truncated with the threshold
w^{*}
at the end of each Trotter step and every Trotter step is the same,

Lemma

B.8
upper bounds the number of transition amplitudes during the whole evolution as
\mathcal{O}(n^{w^{*}})
.
Recall
Proposition

B.1
determines the number of Trotter steps
r=\mathcal{O}((\Lambda_{2}\norm{O}_{\bar{2}}\epsilon^{-1})^{1/p}t^{1+1/p})
,
where
\Lambda_{2}
is usually polynomial with
n
.
And
Corollary

B.3
gives the requirement of the truncation weight
w^{*}=k_{o}+\mathcal{O}\quantity(\frac{\log(t/\eps)}{\log(1/t)})
,
which is a constant if
k_{o}
,
t
, and
\epsilon
are constant.
In consequence, the runtime of evolution is upper bounded by the maximum number of low-weight Paulis during evolution times the number of Trotter steps
r
,
that is,
\mathcal{O}(r\cdot n^{w^{*}})
.

The last step of the
LPD
algorithm is to
calculate the expectation value of the observable with respect to a given state
\rho
,
that is
\Tr(\tO^{(r)}_{\le w^*}\rho)
.
Assume each coefficient of
\rho
in Pauli basis can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(1)
time,
then the expectation value can be computed in time proportional to the number of Pauli operators in the final observable by orthogonality.
For example,
the density matrix of the typical product state
\ket{\vb{0}}
is
\rho_{\mathbf{0}}=\outerproduct{\vb{0}}{\vb{0}}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}(I+Z)^{\otimes n}
.
While there are
2^{n}
Pauli operators in
\rho_{\mathbf{0}}
,
the Paulis and their coefficients can be efficiently determined.
Therefore, the runtime of evaluting the inner product
\Tr(\tO^{(r)}_{\le w^*}\rho)
is at most the number of Paulis in
\tilde{O}^{(r)}_{\leq w^{*}}
,
which is also implied by
Lemma

B.8
.
So, the step of the expectation evaluation does not incur runtime more than one-step Trotter.
Consequently, the total runtime of
LPD
algorithm is
\mathcal{O}((r+1)\cdot n^{w^{*}})
.

Correspondingly, the
memory
required is the maximum number of Pauli operators during evolution,
that is
\mathcal{O}\quantity(n^{w^*})
.

∎

Coherent Conditions: Algebraic Geometry for Arbitrary Classes of Algebras
K. R. van Nispen

Abstract
Universal algebraic geometry is generalised from solutions of equations in a single algebra to the study of φ \varphi - or K K -spectra, akin to the prime spectrum of a ring. We explore their basic properties and constructions, give a correspondence between certain quantifier-free propositions and closed sets in the Zariski topology of a free algebra, and show the connection with current UAG. Lastly, equationally Noetherian classes and irreducible spectra are explored.



Introduction

Classical algebraic geometry is the study of algebraic sets over some algebraically closed field and their corresponding coordinate algebras.
It found its counterparts in other areas of math, when people began studying the algebraic geometry of groups (some of the first being
[
10
]
,
[
1
]
and
[

]
, more of these covered in

,

).
As these did not have as many nice properties as fields, like the ability to perform localisation, or have the union of two algebraic sets again be an algebraic set, there naturally arise different, and often more logic-focused, questions.

Very recently, in 2002, B. Plotkin submitted his paper
Seven Lectures on the Universal Algebraic Geometry

[
11
]
, laying the groundwork for the universalisation of these (classical) algebraic geometries into the domain of universal algebra.
It, among other things, showed the fundamental connection between algebraic geometry and algebraic logic, an idea which has persisted and carried on into this paper.
Later, in 2008, the paper
Unification theorems in algebraic geometry
by E. Daniyarova, Alexei G. Myasnikov and V. Remeslennikov
[
8
]
was submitted, unifying a number of theorems about so-called equationally Noetherian algebras.
This was followed in 2010 by
[
7
]
, further expanding on the unification theorems and exhibiting the categorical duality between algebraic sets and coordinate algebras.
A number of papers followed, also by E. Daniyarova, Alexei G. Myasnikov and V. Remeslennikov, exploring the universal algebraic geometry over a single algebra in depth.

In this paper we aim to generalise this notion of algebraic geometry to arbitrary classes of algebras of the same type, similarly to the prime spectrum of a ring, called coherent conditions.
Unfortunately we lose the convenience of our algebraic sets being ‘affine’ - subsets of
A^{n}
for some algebra
\mathbf{A}
- but in section 3.2 it will be shown briefly that, in the case where the class of algebras
K
consists of a single algebra, all the properties one cares about are carried over equivalently.

Section 2 will be devoted to introducing coherent conditions, and exploring their elementary properties. The first, lattice-theoretic part of the generalised Nullstellensatz will be stated.
In Section 3, we consider the spectra of
V
-free algebras, and state the fundamental connections between coherent conditions and equational logic, one of note being the second, logic part of the generalised Nullstellensatz.
In Section 4, we explore equationally Noetherian classes of algebras, and loosely generalize Unification Theorem C in
[

Lastly, in Section 5, we cover irreducible closed sets, and give a classification of reduced algebras with an irreducible spectrum, similar to that in

Preliminaries

1.1
Universal algebra

In this paper we will mainly use the notation and conventions of
[
12
]
, besides the fact that we conventionally include
0
in the natural numbers.

A type, or signature
\mathscr{F}
is a set of operation symbols
f
each with an associated natural number
n
called its
arity
.
\mathscr{F}_{n}
denotes the operation symbols in
\mathscr{F}
of arity
n
.
An algebra
\mathbf{A}
of type
\mathscr{F}
is a tuple,

\mathbf{A}=\langle A;(f^{\mathbf{A}})_{f\in\mathscr{F}}\rangle

such that, if
f\in\mathscr{F}_{n}
, then
f^{\mathbf{A}}\colon A^{n}\rightarrow A
is an
n
-ary operation on
A
.
The
f^{\mathbf{A}}
are called the fundamental operations of
\mathbf{A}
, and
A
the
universe
or underlying set of
\mathbf{A}
.
The set of homomorphisms between
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
is denoted
\hom(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
, and the lattice of congruences of
\mathbf{A}
,
\operatorname{Con}\mathbf{A}
.

The set of terms in the type
\mathscr{F}
with variables in
X
is written as
T_{\mathscr{F}}(X)
, or simply
T(X)
if the signature is known from context.
This defines the absolutely free algebra
\mathbf{T}(X)
.

\text{At}_{\mathscr{F}}(X)
denotes the set of atomic formulas of
\mathscr{F}
with variables in
X
; the equational identities
p\approx q
for
p,q\in T(X)
.

If
T\subseteq\operatorname{At}(X)
is a set of equations, then for an algebra
\mathbf{A}
and
\vec{a}\in A^{X}
we write
\mathbf{A}\models T(\vec{a})
if
\vec{a}
satisfies all equations in
T
in
\mathbf{A}
.
In other words,
T
can be used as a shorthand for the proposition

\bigwedge_{p\approx q\in T}p\approx q

Formulas of the form
T\rightarrow t\approx s
are called
preidentities
.
A
quasi-identity
is a preidentity where
T
is finite.

We distinguish the following operators for a class of algebras
K
:

1.

IS(K)
, the class of algebras embeddable into some member of
K
.

2.

P(K)
, the class of algebras which are some arbitrary product of members of
K
.

3.

ISP(K)
, the least prevariety containing
K
; the class of algebras embeddable into some product of members in
K
.
Like
[
7
]
, we assume the convention of a product indexed by the empty set to be the trivial algebra.

4.

Q(K)
, the class of algebras satisfying the same quasi-identities as
K
; the least quasivariety containing
K
.

5.

V(K)=HSP(K)
, the least variety containing
K
.

6.

K_{\omega}
, the finitely generated algebras in
K
.

7.

\textbf{Res}(K)
the class of algebras separated by
K

8.

\textbf{Sep}_{\omega}(K)
the class of algebras
n
-separated by
K
for all
n
.

9.

\mathbf{Dis}(K)
, the class of algebras discriminated by
K

The definitions of a class seperated by or discriminated by
K
are already given in, for example,
[
7
]
.
However, the notion of
n
-separated is one we introduce ourselves.

Definition 1.1
.

A homomorphism
h\colon\mathbf{A}\rightarrow\mathbf{B}

separates
two elements from
A
if they are unequal in the image, or equivalently lie in different blocks in the kernel of
h
.

An algebra
\mathbf{A}
is separated by
K
if for any
a_{0}\neq a_{1}\in A
there is a homomorphism into some member of
K
separating
a_{0}
and
a_{1}
.
An algebra is
n
-separated by

if for any finite family of separated pairs
a_{i}\neq b_{i}
there is a homomorphism
h
into
K
with
h(a_{i})\neq h(b_{i})
for all
i
.

Lastly, an algebra is discriminated by
K
if it is locally embeddable into
K
; for any finite
W\subseteq A
there is a homomorphism into
K
such that its restriction onto
W
is injective, i.e. pairwise separates the elements from
W
.

For a class
K
, the free algebra generated by
X
is denoted
\mathbf{F}_{K}(X)
, and is defined as the quotient
T(X)/\psi
where
\psi=\{\langle p,q\rangle\in T(X)^{2}\mid K\models p\approx q\}
.
Elements of
\mathbf{F}_{K}(X)
will freely be identified by a term representing the corresponding equivalence class.
Furthermore, we will treat subsets
T\subseteq F_{K}(X)^{2}
as systems of equations, as, for any two choices of representatives
T_{1}^{\prime},T_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq\text{At}(X)
and class
L
contained in
V(K)
we have
L\models T^{\prime}_{1}\iff L\models T^{\prime}_{2}
.
This means that we can also treat
T
as a (possibly infinitary) proposition on algebras in
V(K)
.

1.2
Closure operators

A closure operator on a set
X
is a function
C\colon\mathcal{P}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(X)
,
\mathcal{P}(X)
being the powerset of
X
, such that for
A,B\subseteq X
:

•

A\subseteq C(A)

•

A\subseteq B\implies C(A)\subseteq C(B)

•

C(C(A))=C(A)

The pair
\langle X,C\rangle
is then a closure system, which we will often refer to as simply
X
.

The set of closed sets,
\operatorname{cl}(C)
(consequently
\operatorname{cl}(X)
), is the set of
C(A)
for
A\subseteq X
.
These are closed under arbitrary intersection and, in fact, closure operators are in 1-1 correspondence with subsets of
\mathcal{P}(X)
closed under arbitrary intersection.
For such a collection
M\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)
, the corresponding closure operator is then given by

C_{M}(A)=\bigcap\{S\in M\mid A\subseteq S\}

Every closure system
\langle X,C\rangle
yields a complete lattice structure on
\operatorname{cl}(C)
.
The arbitrary meet is, naturally, given by intersection, and for
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
a collection of closed sets
\bigvee_{i\in I}A_{i}:=C(\bigcup_{i\in I}A_{i})
.

A morphism of closure systems
f\colon\langle X,C\rangle\rightarrow\langle Y,D\rangle
is a function
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
where the preimage of a closed set of
Y
is again closed in
X
.
The composition of two morphisms is again a morphism, and this yields the category of closure operators.

We then have a nonstandard definition, which shall be very useful in our case.

Definition 1.2
.

A quasi-isomorphism is a morphism of closure systems
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
such that
f
maps closed sets of
X
to closed sets of
Y
, and
f^{-1}\colon\operatorname{cl}(D)\rightarrow\operatorname{cl}(C)
is a bijection.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
be a quasi-isomorphism.
Denote the mapping
B\mapsto f^{-1}(B)
for closed sets
B\subseteq Y
with
F
.
Then:

1.

The mapping
G:A\mapsto f(A)
for closed sets
A\subseteq X
is the inverse of
F
.

2.

G
preserves arbitrary intersections.

Proof.

To begin with
(1)
, note that
f(f^{-1}(A))=A
for all
A\subseteq Y
, so we have, for
A\subseteq Y
closed:

G(F(A))=f(f^{-1}(A))=A

Thus
G\circ F=id
.
As
F
is a bijection, and
G
is a well-defined function, it must also be a bijection, thus being the inverse of
F
.

For
(2)
, let
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of closed sets of
X
.
Then notice:

\displaystyle G(\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i})

\displaystyle=G(\bigcap_{i\in I}F(G(A_{i})))

\displaystyle=G(F(\bigcap_{i\in I}G(A_{i})))

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{i\in I}G(A_{i})

and we are done.
∎

It will be apparent that many properties of a closure system (even those which are not properties of the complete lattice of closed sets) are equivalent to those same properties of a quasi-isomorphic closure system.

In general the closed sets of a closure operator do not form a topology on the underlying set.
This is only if the closure operator is topological; for all
A,B\subseteq X
:

C(A\cup B)=C(A)\vee C(B)=C(A)\cup C(B)

Remark 1.2
.

As both the direct image and preimage maps (
G
and
F
in the above proposition) preserve unions,
X
is topological iff
Y
is, whenever there is a quasi-isomorphism between them.

To deal with this, we introduce the concept of topologization.

Definition 1.3
.

If
X
is a closure system, then
X^{\text{top}}
denotes the topological closure system with prebasis
\operatorname{cl}(X)
, called the topologization of
X
.
The sets which are closed in
X^{\text{top}}
are called
topologically closed
in
X
.

The reader can verify that the topologically closed sets are always of the form

\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i,1}\cup\dots\cup A_{i,k_{i}}

for
A_{i,k}
closed in
X
, and
k_{i}
is potentially
0
for the closed set
\varnothing
.

Proposition 1.3
.

If
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
is a morphism of closure systems, then the same function

\hat{f}\colon X^{\text{top}}\rightarrow Y^{\text{top}}
is continous (also a morphism of closure systems).

Proof.

Straightforward from the fact that
f^{-1}
preserves unions and intersections.
∎

Corollary 1.4
.

An isomorphism of closure systems yields a homeomorphism of their topologisations.

2
Coherent conditions

Unless otherwise specified, in this section we fix a variety
V
over the type
\mathscr{F}
.

2.1
\varphi
-spectra, and fundamental classes

The study of modern algebraic geometry relies for a big part on the following two properties:

1.

Let
f\colon R\rightarrow S
be a ring homomorphism, and
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq S
a prime ideal, then
f^{-1}(\mathfrak{p})
is also prime.
In other words:
f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}S)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R

2.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq R
be a prime ideal, and
I\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
, then
\mathfrak{p}/I
is a prime ideal of
R/I
.

One of the consequences is that
\operatorname{Spec}
is a contravariant functor from the category of rings to that of topological spaces.
Another is that
I
is prime iff
R/I
is an integral domain.
We aim to capture these properties, and their surprisingly far-reaching consequences, with coherent conditions and their associated spectra.

Definition 2.1
.

A coherent condition
\varphi
on
V
assigns to every algebra
\mathbf{A}\in V
its
\varphi
-
spectrum

\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}\subseteq\operatorname{Con}\mathbf{A}
of congruences.
These spectra have to satisfy the following two conditions:

1.

Let
h\colon\mathbf{A}\rightarrow\mathbf{B}
be a homomorphism.
Then
h^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{B})\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}

2.

If
\psi\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
, and
\theta\subseteq\psi
, then
\psi/\theta\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}/\theta

Congruences in
\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
are the
\varphi
-
distinguished congruences
of
\mathbf{A}
.

An immediate, but important consequence of the definition is the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\pi_{\theta}\colon\mathbf{A}\rightarrow\mathbf{A}/\theta
be the natural projection.
Then the preimage

\pi_{\theta}^{-1}\colon\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}/\theta\rightarrow\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}

is an injection, the image of which being
\{\psi\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}\mid\theta\subseteq\psi\}

Proof.

The fact that
\pi_{\theta}^{-1}
must be an injection follows from the correspondence theorem.
Then for the image, take
\psi\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
with
\theta\subseteq\psi
, and notice that
\psi/\theta
is
\varphi
-distinguished, and
\pi_{\theta}^{-1}(\psi/\theta)=\psi
.
∎

Of course, the canonical example of a coherent condition is that of prime ideals on the variety of commutative rings.
As one can check, the set of radical ideals of a commutative ring also forms a coherent condition.
Another important example of a coherent condition is that of maximal congruences in the variety of Boolean algebras, as this leads in part to Stone duality.
This can, however. be seen as a special case of prime ideals on commutative rings, due to the equivalence between Boolean rings and Boolean algebras.
[
12
]

As the reader might have noticed, the above examples of coherent conditions are all defined as sets of congruences where the quotient algebra is a member of some particular class.
For prime and radical ideals these are respectively the integral domains and reduced rings, and for Boolean algebras the class simply consists of the two-element Boolean algebra.
We can generalise this construction.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
K
be a class of algebras contained in
V
.
Then
\varphi:=\Phi_{V}(K)
assigns to every algebra
\mathbf{A}\in K
the following set of congruences:

\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}:=\{\theta\in\operatorname{Con}\mathbf{A}\mid\mathbf{A}/\theta\in IS(K)\}

In other words, the
\Phi_{V}(K)
-distinguished congruences are precisely the kernels of homomorphisms into
K
.
We will also call
\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
the
K
-spectrum of
\mathbf{A}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

\varphi:=\Phi_{V}(K)
is a coherent condition on
V
.

Proof.

To prove that
\varphi
must satisfy the first property, consider a homomorphism
h\colon\mathbf{A}\rightarrow\mathbf{B}
, and
\theta\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{B}
, so
\mathbf{B}/\theta
embeds into some algebra
\mathbf{C}\in K
.
Then consider the following diagram:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{B}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{A}/h^{-1}(\theta)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{B}/\theta}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{C}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}

Where the vertical maps are the natural projections, and the dashed arrow is given by the map

x/h^{-1}(\theta)\mapsto h(x)/\theta

which the reader can check is well-defined.
Furthermore, it is injective basically by definition of the preimage.
Thus, we get the chain

\mathbf{A}/h^{-1}(\theta)\hookrightarrow\mathbf{B}/\theta\hookrightarrow\mathbf{C}

\implies\mathbf{A}/h^{-1}(\theta)\in IS(K)

meaning that
h^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{B})\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
.

For the second property, suppose
\psi,\theta
congruences of
\mathbf{A}
with
\psi
being
\varphi
-distinguished.
By the third isomorphism theorem:

\implies(\mathbf{A}/\theta)/(\psi/\theta)\cong\mathbf{A}/\psi\in IS(K)

Hence
\psi/\theta\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}/\theta
.
∎

As we see, this already gives us a huge class of coherent conditions, one uniquely for each class of algebras closed under subalgebras and isomorphisms, in fact.
Surprisingly, these are
the only
coherent conditions which exist.
In other words, every coherent condition is
\Phi_{V}(K)
for some class
K
.
To show this we introduce the fundamental class of a coherent condition.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\varphi
be a coherent condition on
V
.
The
fundamental class
of
\varphi
, denoted
F(\varphi)
, is the class of algebras
\mathbf{A}\in V
such that
\Delta_{A}\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
, so the minimal congruence (diagonal) is
\varphi
-distinguished.

For example, for the coherent condition of prime ideals on commutative rings, its fundamental class is the class of integral domains.

Proposition 2.3
.

The fundamental class is closed under isomorphisms and subalgebras.

Proof.

By the first isomorphism theorem, this is equivalent to the fundamental class being closed under embeddings.
As such, let
\varphi
be a coherent condition and
\mathbf{A}\in F(\varphi)
, and consider an embedding
i\colon\mathbf{B}\rightarrow\mathbf{A}
.
By definition,
\Delta_{A}
is
\varphi
-distinguished.

\implies\Delta_{B}=i^{-1}(\Delta_{A})\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}

\implies\mathbf{B}\in F(\varphi)

and we are done.
∎

There is another, also fairly useful, characterisation of the fundamental class of a coherent condition.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\varphi
be a coherent condition on
V
.
Then
F(\varphi)
is also all the algebras isomorphic to
\mathbf{A}/\theta
for some
\mathbf{A}\in V
and
\theta\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
.

Proof.

Straightforward from Proposition
2.1
:
\theta\in\operatorname{Con}\mathbf{A}
is
\varphi
-distinguished iff
\Delta_{A/\theta}=\theta/\theta
is.
∎

We are now ready to prove the correspondence between coherent conditions on
V
and classes closed under embeddings.

Theorem 2.5
.

For any coherent condition
\varphi
, it is the case that
\Phi_{V}(F(\varphi))=\varphi
, and for any class
K
contained in
V
we have
F(\Phi_{V}(K))=IS(K)
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be a coherent condition on
V
, and consider the other coherent condition
\varphi^{\prime}=\Phi_{V}(F(\varphi))
.
Let
\theta\in\operatorname{Con}\mathbf{A}
.
Notice, by Lemma
2.4
:

\displaystyle\theta\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}

\displaystyle\iff\mathbf{A}/\theta\in F(\varphi)=IS(F(\varphi))

\displaystyle\iff\theta\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi^{\prime}}\mathbf{A}

Indeed,
\varphi=\varphi^{\prime}
.

Consider now a class
K
contained in
V
.
Clearly
IS(K)\subseteq F(\Phi_{V}(K))
, as obviously the identity map maps a member of
IS(K)
into
IS(K)
and has kernel
\Delta_{A}
.
Conversely, suppose
\mathbf{A}\in F(\Phi_{V}(K))

\implies\mathbf{A}\cong\mathbf{A}/\Delta_{A}\in IS(K)

Thus
F(\Phi_{V}(K))\subseteq IS(K)
, and we are done.
∎

The universal algebraic geometry developed in
[
11
]
, can be reduced to looking at coherent conditions where the fundamental class is
IS(\mathbf{A})
.
We will see this in section 3.2.

2.2
\varphi
-radical congruences

Theorem
2.5
suggests that the study of coherent conditions is equivalent to the study of classes closed under embeddings.
I.e., we can study a coherent condition by only looking at its fundamental class.

In this subsection we shall give an important example of this idea, in the form of
\varphi
-radical congruences.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\varphi
be a coherent condition on
V
.
We define the set of
\varphi
-radicals of an algebra
\mathbf{A}
, which we denote
\operatorname{RSpec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
, to be the meet-completion of
\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
.
In other words, the
\varphi
-radical congruences of
\mathbf{A}
are arbitrary intersections of
\varphi
-distinguished congruences.

Because it is the meet completion, we can, similarly to prime spectra, define the
\varphi
-radical of some congruence
\theta
, and consequently the
\varphi
-nilradical of an algebra.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\varphi
be a coherent condition on
V
, and
\mathbf{A}\in V
and
\theta
a congruence on
\mathbf{A}
.
We define the following two congruences:

\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}\theta:=\bigcap\{\psi\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}\mid\theta\subseteq\psi\}

\operatorname{nil}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}:=\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}\Delta_{A}

\Delta_{A}
being the minimal congruence.
The quotient

\operatorname{red}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}:=\mathbf{A}/\operatorname{nil}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{A}/\bigcap\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}

will be called the
\varphi
-
reduction
of
\mathbf{A}
, and algebras where the nilradical is trivial will be called
\varphi
-
reduced

We collect a couple of immediate facts from the definition.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\varphi
be a coherent condition on
V
and
\mathbf{A}\in V
.
It follows that

1.

For
\psi\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
and
\theta
a congruence:
\theta\subseteq\psi\iff\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}\theta\subseteq\psi

2.

\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}
is a closure operator on the congruence lattice of
\mathbf{A}

3.

The set of closed elements is precisely
\operatorname{RSpec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}

Proof.

1)
is a straightforward property of intersection.

For
2)
, the extensive property is clear.
To show that it is also increasing, consider
\theta_{1}\subseteq\theta_{2}
.
Thus for
\psi

\varphi
-distinguished,
\theta_{2}\subseteq\psi\implies\theta_{1}\subseteq\psi
, so

\bigcap\{\psi\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}\mid\theta_{1}\subseteq\psi\}\subseteq\bigcap\{\psi\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}\mid\theta_{2}\subseteq\psi\}

as desired.
Idempotency and
3)
will be done together, as
\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}\theta\in\operatorname{RSpec}^{\varphi}
.
Thus, consider an arbitrary element
\bigcap_{i\in I}\psi_{i}\in\operatorname{RSpec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
for
\psi_{i}\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
.

\implies\bigcap_{i\in I}\psi_{i}\subseteq\psi_{j}\;\forall j\in I

\implies\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}\bigcap_{i\in I}\psi_{i}\subseteq\bigcap_{i\in I}\psi_{i}

But any congruence must also be contained in its
\varphi
-radical, so
\bigcap_{i\in I}\psi_{i}
is its own
\varphi
-radical.
Indeed,
\operatorname{RSpec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
is the set of
\varphi
-radical congruences, and
\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}
is idempotent, making it a closure operator.
∎

Element
1)
in the proposition above can be seen as one direction of some generalised Nullstellensatz, and in the following subsection it will be fully presented as such.

Now we will focus on proving that the sets
\operatorname{RSpec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
define a coherent condition on
V
, and the rest of this subsection shall be devoted to looking at the properties of that coherent condition.
First we have a useful lemma.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\varphi
be a coherent condition on
V
, then for all congruences
\psi\subseteq\theta
of
\mathbf{A}\in V
we have the equality

(\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}\theta)/\psi=\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}\theta/\psi

Proof.

Recall by Proposition
2.1
that for a congruence
\psi
of
\mathbf{A}
the map

\theta\mapsto\theta/\psi

defines a correspondence between
\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}/\psi
and the points in
\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
containing
\psi
.
It is an elementary fact that this map respects intersections, so we see for any congruence
\theta\supseteq\psi
:

\displaystyle(\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}\theta)/\psi

\displaystyle=(\bigcap\{\gamma\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}\mid\theta\subseteq\gamma\})/\psi

\displaystyle=\bigcap\{\gamma/\psi\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}/\psi\mid\theta\subseteq\gamma\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap\{\gamma/\psi\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}/\psi\mid\theta/\psi\subseteq\gamma/\psi\}=\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}\theta/\psi

the second to last equality making use of the correspondence theorem.
∎

A particular case of this result yields a nice formula for the
\varphi
-radical in terms of some nilradical.

Corollary 2.8
.

\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}\theta=\pi_{\theta}^{-1}(\operatorname{nil}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}/\theta)
,

\pi_{\theta}
being the usual projection onto
\mathbf{A}/\theta

Proof.

By Lemma
2.7
:

\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}\theta=\pi_{\theta}^{-1}(\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}\theta/\theta)=\pi_{\theta}^{-1}(\operatorname{nil}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}/\theta)

as
\theta/\theta=\Delta_{A/\theta}

∎

This is enough to prove the theorem.

Theorem 2.9
.

For any coherent condition
\varphi
on
V
there is a coherent condition
\sqrt{\varphi}
on
V
such that
\operatorname{Spec}^{\sqrt{\varphi}}\mathbf{A}=\operatorname{RSpec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
.

Proof.

It suffices, of course, to show that the sets
\operatorname{RSpec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
follow the coherence properties.

As such, let
h\colon\mathbf{A}\rightarrow\mathbf{B}
be a homomorphism, and let
\theta\in\operatorname{RSpec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{B}
.
By Proposition
2.6
:

\displaystyle h^{-1}(\theta)

\displaystyle=h^{-1}(\bigcap\{\psi\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{B}\mid\theta\subseteq\psi\})

\displaystyle=\bigcap\{h^{-1}(\psi)\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}\mid\theta\subseteq\psi\})

But, again as per Proposition
2.6
, this must be an element of
\operatorname{RSpec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
, so it satisfies the first coherence property.

To show that it satisfies the second, consider
\theta\subseteq\psi
congruences of
\mathbf{A}
, with
\psi
being
\varphi
-radical.
By Lemma
2.7
:

\psi/\theta=(\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}\psi)/\theta=\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}\psi/\theta\in\operatorname{RSpec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}/\theta

and we are done.
∎

From now on we shall refer to this coherent condition as
\sqrt{\varphi}
, called its
radical
.
By Theorem
2.5
we therefore have that the class of
\varphi
-reduced algebras is precisely
F(\sqrt{\varphi})
, hence closed under embeddings, and also that
\theta
is
\varphi
-radical if and only if
\mathbf{A}/\theta
is reduced.
This also makes sense of the reduction of
\mathbf{A}
, as clearly
F(\sqrt{\varphi})
is the class of algebras isomorphic to the reduction of some algebra in
V
.

It is natural to ask whether
F(\sqrt{\varphi})
has anything to do with
F(\varphi)
or more generally with
K
, in the case that
\varphi=\Phi_{V}(K)
.
We will answer this in the positive, and even show that the fundamental class simply encodes the property of a coherent condition to be the radical of some other.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\varphi=\Phi_{V}(K)
be a coherent condition.
Then
F(\sqrt{\varphi})=ISP(K)
.
In other words,
\sqrt{\varphi}=\Phi_{V}(ISP(K))
, and so
\sqrt{\sqrt{\varphi}}=\sqrt{\varphi}
.

Proof.

We will show this by using a bi-inclusion.
For the first direction, let
\mathbf{A}\in F(\sqrt{\varphi})
.
Consider the natural map

\alpha\colon\mathbf{A}\rightarrow\prod_{\theta\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{A}/\theta

given by

a\mapsto(a/\theta)_{\theta\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}}

This has kernel

\bigcap\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}=\operatorname{nil}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}=\Delta_{A}

hence
\alpha
is an embedding.
Further, as
\mathbf{A}/\theta
is in
IS(K)
for all
\varphi
-distinguished
\theta
we have

\mathbf{A}\cong\operatorname{im}\alpha\leq\prod_{\theta\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{A}/\theta\in ISP(K)

\implies\mathbf{A}\in ISP(K)

Hence
F(\sqrt{\varphi})\subseteq ISP(K)
.

Conversely, by Theorem
2.9
it suffices to show that
F(\sqrt{\varphi})
contains
P(K)
.
Thus let
\mathbf{A}_{i}\in K
be a family of algebras indexed by
I
, and define
\mathbf{A}=\prod_{i\in I}\mathbf{A}_{i}
.
Let
\theta_{i}
be the kernel of projection map
\pi_{i}\colon\mathbf{A}\rightarrow\mathbf{A}_{i}
.
It should be clear that
\bigcap_{i\in I}\theta_{i}=\Delta_{A}
.
But obviously
\theta_{i}\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
, so

\Delta_{A}=\bigcap_{i\in I}\theta_{i}\in\operatorname{RSpec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}

Hence
P(K)\subseteq F(\sqrt{\varphi})
.

That
\sqrt{\sqrt{\varphi}}=\sqrt{\varphi}
is then translates to the fact that
ISP(ISP(K))=ISP(K)
.
∎

Corollary 2.11
.

\varphi
is the radical of some coherent condition if and only if
F(\varphi)
is closed under arbitrary products if and only if
\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
is closed under arbitrary intersections for all
\mathbf{A}
.

Proof.

These follow directly from the above theorem.
∎

As mentioned in Section 1.1, prevarieties are precisely the models of some set of preidentities, so the radical coherent conditions are precisely those for which the fundamental class is defined by preidentities.

The most well-known (nonvariety) case of this is perhaps for commutative rings where
K
is the class of fields, and
ISP(K)
is axiomised by countable family of quasi-identities:

\left\{x^{n}\approx 0\rightarrow x\approx 0\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

2.3
Zariski closure and topology

In this subsection we will introduce the Zariski closure operator and subsequent topology (by topologisation) on
\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\varphi
be a coherent condition and
\mathbf{A}\in V
.
For
S\subseteq A^{2}
we define the
Zariski closed set
generated by
S
:

V_{\varphi}(S):=\{\theta\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}\mid S\subseteq\theta\}

It should be clear that
V_{\varphi}(S)=V_{\varphi}(\Theta(S))
(where

\Theta
is the congruence closure).
Furthermore, as per Proposition
2.6
, we have
V_{\varphi}(\theta)=V_{\varphi}(\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}\theta)
.

These closed sets yield a closure system on
\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
.

Proposition 2.12
.

For any collection
V_{\varphi}(S_{i})
indexed by
I
, we have

\bigcap_{i\in I}V_{\varphi}(S_{i})=V_{\varphi}(\bigcup_{i\in I}S_{i})

So
\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
is a closure system.

Proof.

Let
\theta\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
.
By definition

\bigcup_{i\in I}S_{i}\subseteq\theta\iff S_{i}\subseteq\theta\;\forall i\in I

So

\theta\in V_{\varphi}(\bigcup_{i\in I}S_{i})\iff\theta\in\bigcap_{i\in I}V_{\varphi}(S_{i})

hence the Zariski closed sets are closed under arbitrary intersection, and
\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
is a closure system.
∎

Definition 2.7
.

A subset
X\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
is
Zariski topologically closed
if it is closed in the topologisation of the Zariski closure system, called the Zariski topology.

We denote the Zariski closure of
X
with
X^{\text{zc}}
and the topological closure
\overline{X}
.

Corollary 2.13
.

The Zariski closed sets
V_{\varphi}(a_{0},a_{1})
for
a_{0},a_{1}\in A
(by abuse of notation) form a prebasis, or subbasis of the Zariski topology on
\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
.

Proof.

Indeed, we have

V_{\varphi}(S)=V_{\varphi}(\bigcup_{\langle a_{0},a_{1}\rangle\in S}\langle a_{0},a_{1}\rangle)=\bigcap_{\langle a_{0},a_{1}\rangle\in S}V_{\varphi}(a_{0},a_{1})

So the topology generated by taking the collection
V_{\varphi}(a_{0},a_{1})
as prebasis includes every Zariski closed set, hence includes every Zariski topologically closed set, and so must be the Zariski topology.
∎

It is fruitful to make a distinction between Zariski closed- and topologically closed sets, as analogously to the usual algebraic geometry we have a notion of the Nullstellensatz.

Definition 2.8
.

If
X\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
we define

\psi(X):=\bigcap X

And for
S\subseteq A^{2}
we say
\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}S:=\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}\Theta(S)

Theorem 2.14

(Generalised Nullstellensatz part I)
.

Let
S\subseteq A^{2}
and
X\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
, then

S\subseteq\psi(X)\iff X\subseteq V_{\varphi}(S)

So we have an antitone Galois connection.
Furthermore:

\psi(V_{\varphi}(S))=\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}S

and

V_{\varphi}(\psi(X))=X^{\text{zc}}

Proof.

Let us begin with the antitone Galois connection.
Suppose that
S\subseteq\psi(X)
, and let
\theta\in X

\implies S\subseteq\bigcap X\subseteq\theta

\implies X\subseteq V_{\varphi}(S)

For the reverse notice:

X\subseteq V_{\varphi}(S)\implies S\subseteq\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}S=\bigcap V_{\varphi}(S)\subseteq\bigcap X=\psi(X)

As desired.

The third and fourth item now follow from the fact that we have an antitone Galois connection.
∎

From this follows for example that
V_{\varphi}(-)
and
\psi(-)
are inclusion-reversing maps.
The following is a restatement of the last two items in the above theorem.

Corollary 2.15
.

If
\varphi
is a radical coherent condition, then
\psi(-)
and
V_{\varphi}(-)
give an antitone Galois-correspondence between
\varphi
-distinguished congruences and Zariski closed sets.

Following classical (universal) algebraic geometry, we can interpret
\mathbf{A}/\psi(X)
as the ‘coordinate algebra’ of the set of ‘points’
X
.
If
\varphi=\Phi_{V}(K)
, then Theorem
2.10
tells us that that class of (algebras isomorphic to) coordinate algebras of
\varphi
is precisely
ISP(K)
.

The assignment
\mathbf{A}\mapsto\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
happens to be a contravariant functor to
\mathbf{Top}
.

Proposition 2.16
.

A homomorphism
f\colon\mathbf{A}\rightarrow\mathbf{B}
induces a closure morphism
f_{\ast}\colon\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{B}\rightarrow\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
given by the preimage.
This yields a contravariant functor from
V
to
\mathbf{Top}
.

Proof.

By the fundamental properties of coherent conditions we obviously have a function
f_{\ast}\colon\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{B}\rightarrow\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
.
To see that this is also a closure morphism, take any Zariski closed set
X=V_{\varphi}(\theta)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
.
Consider

f^{-1}_{\ast}(X)=\{\psi\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{B}\mid\theta\subseteq f^{-1}(\psi)\}

and define
f(\alpha)=\{\langle f(a),f(b)\rangle\mid\langle a,b\rangle\in\alpha\}
.
Notice that
f(f^{-1}(\psi))=\psi
, so

\theta\subseteq f^{-1}(\psi)\implies f(\theta)\subseteq\psi

Then

f(\theta)\subseteq\psi\implies\theta\subseteq f^{-1}(f(\theta))\subseteq f^{-1}(\psi)

Hence

f^{-1}_{\ast}(X)=\{\psi\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{B}\mid f(\theta)\subseteq\psi\}=V_{\varphi}(f(\theta))

which is Zariski closed.
By Proposition
1.3
this is then a continuous function between the Zariski topologies.

That this yields a contravariant functor follows from elementary properties of the preimage.
∎

A Zariski closed set
X\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
turns out to have a very nice description given the subspace topology, as again the spectrum of some algebra.

Theorem 2.17
.

Let
X\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
be an arbitrary subset.
Then

X^{\text{zc}}\cong\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}/\psi(X)

The homeomorphism being given by the natural projection
\pi\colon\mathbf{A}\rightarrow\mathbf{A}/\psi(X)

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
,
\pi_{\ast}\colon\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}\rightarrow\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}/\psi(X)
is an embedding with image
V_{\varphi}(\psi(X))
, which is just
X^{\text{zc}}
by the generalised Nullstellensatz part I.

Consider the set-theoretic inverse (this is the inverse of
\pi_{\ast}
by the correspondence theorem):

\pi_{\ast}^{-1}\colon\theta\mapsto\theta/\psi(X)

This is also a morphism of closure systems, which can be seen by noticing the fact that the preimage of
V_{\varphi}(\theta/\psi(X))
under
\pi_{\ast}^{-1}
is simply
V_{\varphi}(\theta)
.
As such
X^{\text{zc}}\cong\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}/\psi(X)
, since an isomorphism of closure operators induces a homeomorphism on the topologisations.
∎

Corollary 2.18
.

\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}\cong\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}(\operatorname{red}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A})

As such, we see that coherent conditions make no difference at all between an algebra and its reduction.

3
K
-Spectra of free algebras

During this section we fix a variety
V
of type
\mathscr{F}
and a class
K
contained in
V
, which generates the coherent condition
\varphi=\Phi_{V}(K)
.

It is a fairly elementary fact that every algebra
\mathbf{A}\in V
is isomorphic to some quotient of
\mathbf{F}_{V}(X)
for some set
X
, for example by taking
X
to be
A
, or some other generating set of
\mathbf{A}
.
Theorem
2.10
therefore suggest that, to study the
K
-spectra (
\varphi
-spectra) in
V
, it suffices to study simply the Zariski (topologically) closed sets of
\mathbf{F}_{V}(X)
.
That is what we will do now, consequently showing the connections between the various types of closed sets, and sets of formulas, and stating the second part of the universal Nullstellensatz.

The approach in the first subsection is inspired by
[
9
]
.

3.1
Disjunctive systems

Recall that a subset
T\subseteq F_{V}(X)^{2}
can just as well be treated as a system of equations for algebras in
V
, i.e. as a shorthand for the proposition
\bigwedge_{\langle p,q\rangle\in T}p\approx q
.
Hence
\mathbf{A}\models T(\vec{a})
means every equation in
T
is satisfied by
\vec{a}\in A^{X}
in
\mathbf{A}
.
Also, for
\vec{a}\in A^{X}
we denote
h_{\vec{a}}\colon\mathbf{F}_{V}(X)\rightarrow\mathbf{A}
for the evaluation map induced by the choice of variables
x\mapsto a_{x}
.

This gives us a nice interpretation of the Zariski closed sets of
\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{F}_{V}(X)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
T\subseteq F_{V}(X)^{2}
be a system of equations.
It follows that

V_{\varphi}(T)=\{\theta\in\operatorname{Con}\mathbf{F}_{V}(X)\mid\theta=\ker h_{\vec{a}}\text{ where }h_{\vec{a}}\colon\mathbf{F}_{V}(X)\to\mathbf{A}\in K,\mathbf{A}\models T(\vec{a})\}

Proof.

The kernel of any
h_{\vec{a}}
to
\mathbf{A}\in K
is by definition
\varphi
-distinguished, and by the universal mapping property any homomorphism is an evaluation morphism, so any
\varphi
-distinguished congruence must be the kernel of an evaluation morphism into
K
.

Now:

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\models T(\vec{a})

\displaystyle\iff p^{\mathbf{A}}(\vec{a})=q^{\mathbf{A}}(\vec{a})\;\forall\langle p,q\rangle\in T

\displaystyle\iff h_{\vec{a}}(p)=h_{\vec{a}}(q)\;\forall\langle p,q\rangle\in T

\displaystyle\iff T\subseteq\ker h_{\vec{a}}

so we are done.
∎

We can generalise this to an arbitrary algebra
\mathbf{B}
as follows:
There are always exists some projection
\pi\colon\mathbf{F}_{V}(X)\rightarrow\mathbf{B}
, and
\theta
of
\mathbf{B}
is
\varphi
-distinguished iff
\pi^{-1}(\theta)
is.
As such,
V_{\varphi}(S)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{B}
can be thought of as the (kernels of) interpretations of
\mathbf{B}
in some
\mathbf{A}\in K
that satisfy the relations in
S
.

Let us now begin working towards the second part of the Nullstellensatz.
There happen to be nice ’closed forms‘ for nonempty Zariski closed and Zariski topologically closed sets of the
K
-spectrum of
\mathbf{F}_{V}(X)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For pairs
\epsilon_{i,k}\in F_{V}(X)^{2}
where
n_{i}>0
:

\bigcap_{i\in I}V_{\varphi}(\epsilon_{i,1})\cup\dots\cup V_{\varphi}(\epsilon_{i,n_{i}})=\{\theta\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{F}_{V}(X)\mid K\models\theta\rightarrow\bigwedge_{i\in I}\bigvee_{k=1}^{n_{i}}\epsilon_{i,k}\}

And, in particular for a system of equations
T
:

V_{\varphi}(T)=\{\theta\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{F}_{V}(X)\mid K\models\theta\rightarrow T\}

Proof.

We will start by proving the claim that

V_{\varphi}(\epsilon_{1})\cup\dots\cup V_{\varphi}(\epsilon_{n})=\{\theta\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{F}_{V}(X)\mid K\models\theta\to\epsilon_{1}\vee\dots\vee\epsilon_{n}\}

One direction of the inclusion is fairly direct.
If
\theta\in V_{\varphi}(\epsilon_{k})
for some
1\leq k\leq n
, then
\epsilon_{k}\in\theta
, so of course
K\models\theta\rightarrow\epsilon_{k}
, and in particular
K\models\theta\rightarrow\epsilon_{1}\vee\dots\vee\epsilon_{k}
.

Conversely, suppose that
K\models\theta\to\epsilon_{1}\vee\dots\vee\epsilon_{n}
for some congruence
\theta
.
Then

\mathbf{A}:=\mathbf{F}_{V}(X)/\theta\in IS(K)

\implies\mathbf{A}\models\theta\to\epsilon_{1}\vee\dots\vee\epsilon_{n}

Let
\bar{x}\in A^{X}
be the sequence of elements
(x/\theta)_{x\in X}
.
Notice then that
\pi_{\theta}\colon\mathbf{F}_{V}(X)\to\mathbf{A}
is simply the evaluation map
h_{\bar{x}}
, using the notation of the lemma above.
So we see, for
\langle p,q\rangle\in\theta
:

p^{\mathbf{A}}(\bar{x})=\pi_{\theta}(p)=\pi_{\theta}(q)=q^{\mathbf{A}}(\bar{x})

\implies\mathbf{A}\models\theta(\bar{x})

hence there exists a
1\leq k\leq n
with
\mathbf{A}\models\epsilon_{k}(\bar{x})
, and therefore by Lemma
3.1
we have that
\theta\in V_{\varphi}(\epsilon_{k})\subseteq V_{\varphi}(\epsilon_{1})\cup\dots\cup V_{\varphi}(\epsilon_{n})
, as desired.

The rest of the proof now follows trivially.
∎

This motivates the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
S
be a set of finite disjunctions of equations (or pairs in
F_{V}(X)
), which we call a
distjunctive system
.
We define

V_{\varphi}(S)=\{\theta\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{F}_{V}(X)\mid K\models\theta\rightarrow\bigwedge_{\Phi\in S}\Phi\}

In particular, this notation coincides with the usual one whenever
S
consists only of equations.

From Corollary
2.13
we know that the principal closed sets
V_{\varphi}(\epsilon)
give a prebasis of the Zariski topology.
What Lemma
3.2
then tells us, is that every nonempty Zariski topologically closed set of
\mathbf{F}_{V}(X)
can be written as
V_{\varphi}(S)
for some disjunctive system
S
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
S_{1},S_{2}
be distjunctive systems with variables in
X
.
Then

V_{\varphi}(S_{1})\subseteq V_{\varphi}(S_{2})\iff K\models\bigwedge_{\Phi_{1}\in S_{1}}\Phi_{1}\rightarrow\bigwedge_{\Phi_{2}\in S_{2}}\Phi_{2}

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)

This direction is fairly clear.
Suppose that
K\models\bigwedge_{\Phi_{1}\in S_{1}}\Phi_{1}\rightarrow\bigwedge_{\Phi_{2}\in S_{2}}\Phi_{2}
, and let
\theta\in V_{\varphi}(S_{1})

\implies K\models\theta\rightarrow\bigwedge_{\Phi_{1}\in S_{1}}\Phi_{1}

\implies K\models\theta\rightarrow\bigwedge_{\Phi_{2}\in S_{2}}\Phi_{2}

\implies\theta\in V_{\varphi}(S_{2})

simply by transitivity.

(\Rightarrow)

To see that the converse is also true, we again suppose that
V_{\varphi}(S_{1})\subseteq V_{\varphi}(S_{2})
.
Now, consider
\mathbf{A}\in K
and
\vec{a}\in A^{X}
such that

\mathbf{A}\models(\bigwedge_{\Phi_{1}\in S_{1}}\Phi_{1})(\vec{a})

Thus, for all
\bigvee_{k=1}^{n}p_{k}\approx q_{k}\in S_{1}
there exists a
1\leq i\leq n
such that
p_{i}^{\mathbf{A}}(\vec{a})=q^{\mathbf{A}}_{i}(\vec{a})
.
By Lemma
3.1
:

\ker h_{\vec{a}}\in V_{\varphi}(\langle p_{i},q_{i}\rangle)\subseteq V_{\varphi}(\bigvee_{k=1}^{n}p_{k}\approx q_{k})

\implies\ker h_{\vec{a}}\in\bigcap_{\Phi\in S_{1}}V_{\varphi}(\Phi)=V_{\varphi}(S_{1})

\implies\ker h_{\vec{a}}\in V_{\varphi}(S_{2})

One can then do the same dance to show that
\mathbf{A}\models(\bigwedge_{\Phi_{2}\in S_{2}}\Phi_{2})(\vec{a})
.
Therefore

\mathbf{A}\models\bigwedge_{\Phi_{1}\in S_{1}}\Phi_{1}\rightarrow\bigwedge_{\Phi_{2}\in S_{2}}\Phi_{2}

And, as
\mathbf{A}
was arbitrary,
K
must satisfy that formula too.
∎

We now state the second part of the generalised Nullstellensatz.

Theorem 3.4

(Generalised Nullstellensatz part II)
.

Let
S_{1},S_{2}
be disjunctive systems over
X
and
A\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{F}_{V}(X)
Zariski topologically closed and nonempty.
Then

V_{\varphi}(S_{1})\subseteq V_{\varphi}(S_{2})\iff K\models\bigwedge_{\Phi_{1}\in S_{1}}\Phi_{1}\rightarrow\bigwedge_{\Phi_{2}\in S_{2}}\Phi_{2}

Furthermore, for
T_{1},T_{2}\subseteq F_{V}(X)^{2}
:

V_{\varphi}(T_{1})=\{\theta\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{F}_{V}(X)\mid K\models\theta\rightarrow T_{1}\}

T_{1}\subseteq\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}T_{2}\iff K\models T_{2}\rightarrow T_{1}

Proof.

The first two are simply Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
.
The third uses the fact that
T\subseteq F_{V}(X)^{2}
can be interpreted as a set of atomic formulas
S
, and as such

K\models T\leftrightarrow\bigwedge_{\epsilon\in S}\epsilon

and thus by the generalised Nullstellensatz part I and Lemma
3.3

\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}T_{1}\subseteq\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}T_{2}\iff K\models T_{2}\rightarrow T_{1}

Therefore, by elementary properties of closure operators:

T_{1}\subseteq\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}T_{2}\iff\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}T_{1}\subseteq\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}T_{2}\iff K\models T_{2}\rightarrow T_{1}

as desired.
∎

The case
\varnothing\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{F}_{V}(X)
is worth discussing.
Closed sets of the form
V_{\varphi}(S)
with
S
a disjunctive system are precisely the sets which can be written as an intersection of finite unions of Zariski closed sets (as the principal sets
V_{\varphi}(p,q)
form a subbasis).
This means that
V_{\varphi}(S)=\varnothing
for some
S
iff
\varnothing
is Zariski closed, iff the trivial algebra is not contained in
IS(K)
.

Also, the third item of the generalised Nullstellensatz can be restated as

\operatorname{rad}^{\varphi}T=\{\langle p,q\rangle\in F_{V}(X)^{2}\mid K\models T\rightarrow p\approx q\}

Here we can really see that congruences play the role of points in
A^{n}
for some algebra
\mathbf{A}
:
p^{\mathbf{A}}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=q^{\mathbf{A}}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
for
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in A^{n}
is simply replaced by
K\models\theta\rightarrow p\approx q
.
Indeed, the analogy between these two is the essence of Lemma
3.1
.

3.2
Connections with standard universal algebraic geometry

Equationally Noetherian classes

Unique to universal algebraic geometry (and essentially any algebraic geometry that is not that of commutative rings) is the need for a notion of an equationally Noetherian algebra.
Recalling from
[
7
]

Definition 4.1
.

An algebra
\mathbf{A}

T\subseteq\operatorname{At}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
there exists a finite subsystem
T_{0}\subseteq T
with
V_{\mathbf{A}}(T)=V_{\mathbf{A}}(T_{0})
(in the sense of section 3.2) or equivalently
\mathbf{A}\models T\leftrightarrow T_{0}

From (
[
8
]
, Remark 4.8) we have the following:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a topological space with prebasis
\mathcal{B}
of closed subsets.
Then the following are equivalent:

•

The closed sets of
X
satisfy the descending chain condition;

•

\mathcal{B}
satisfies the descending chain condition.

And if either of these are satisfied, in which case we call
X
Noetherian, we have

•

X
has a finite number of irreducible components

•

Every closed subset of
X
can be written as a finite union of irreducible sets.
In particular,
X
is covered by its irreducible components.

Recal that a subset

is irreducible if, for any closed
U,V\subseteq X

implies that
Y\subseteq U
or
Y\subseteq V
, and is an irreducible component if it is irreducible and maximal with that property.

Taking Definition 4.1 above, we can extend the notion of equationally Noetherian to arbitrary classes.
We fix a class contained in a variety
K\subseteq V
and let
\varphi=\Phi_{V}(K)
.

Definition 4.2
.

is equationally Noetherian if for any

there is a finite subset

, or equivalently
K\models T_{0}\rightarrow T
.
We say that
\varphi
is Noetherian if

is equationally Noetherian.

There are a couple elementary equivalent conditions of equational Noetherianness.

Theorem 4.2
.

The following are equivalent:

1.

\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{F}_{V}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

4.

For any disjunctive system
S

(1\Rightarrow 2)

Suppose that

By the hypothesis and the generalised Nullstellensatz part II, there is a finite

(3\Rightarrow 4)

Now, suppose that

5

Again, throughout this section, let
V

5.1
Irreducible sets

We begin with a high-level overview of irreducibility in arbitrary closure operators.
The usual definition translates naturally into that setting.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\langle X,C\rangle
be a closure system.
An
irreducible subset
of
X
is a nonempty subset
Y\subseteq X
such that

Y\subseteq\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}A_{n}\implies\exists k:Y\subseteq A_{k}

for any closed sets
A_{k}
.

The following proposition is obvious.

Proposition 5.1
.

Y\subseteq X
is irreducible implies
C(Y)
is irreducible.

For topological spaces, there is an even weaker condition equivalent to irreducibility of a subset, relating to prebases.
This is useful for us as the Zariski topology is a topologization, and topologizations are defined using prebases.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
X
be a topological space.
Then
Y\subseteq X
is irreducible for a prebasis of closed sets
\mathcal{B}
(the irreducibility condition but only for sets in the prebasis instead of any closed set) iff it is irreducible in
X
.

Proof.

The backwards direction is obvious.

(\Rightarrow)

Suppose
Y\subseteq X
is irreducible for a prebasis in
\mathcal{B}
, and

Y\subseteq A\cup B

for
A,B
closed in
X
.

\implies Y\subseteq(\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i,1}\cup\dots\cup A_{i,k_{i}})\cup(\bigcap_{j\in J}B_{j,1}\cup\dots\cup B_{j,l_{j}})

for
A_{i,k},B_{j,l}\in\mathcal{B}

\implies Y\subseteq\bigcap_{(i,j)\in I\times J}A_{i,1}\cup\dots\cup A_{i,k_{i}}\cup B_{j,1}\cup\dots\cup B_{j,l_{j}}

Thus for any
i\in I
,
j\in J

Y\subseteq A_{i,1}\cup\dots\cup A_{i,k_{i}}\cup B_{j,1}\cup\dots\cup B_{j,l_{j}}

If for all
i\in I
there is an
1\leq x_{i}\leq k_{i}
with
Y\subseteq A_{i,x_{i}}
, which means that
Y\subseteq A
and we are done.
In the other case, let
i\in I
where this is not the case.
By irreducibility in
X
this means for all
j\in J
there is an
1\leq y_{j}\leq l_{j}
with
Y\subseteq B_{j,l_{j}}
.
But this means that
Y\subseteq B
, so we are done.
∎

As the principal closed sets form a prebasis of the Zariski topology, we get for an arbitrary coherent condition
\varphi
a nice corollary.

Corollary 5.3
.

A subset
X\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
is irreducible in the Zariski topology if and only if for all
a_{i},b_{i}\in A
,
i=1,\dots,n
:

X\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}V_{\varphi}(a_{i},b_{i})\implies\exists k\leq n:X\subseteq V_{\varphi}(a_{k},b_{k})

if and only if it is irreducible in the Zariski closure system if and only if
\overline{X}
and
X^{\operatorname{zc}}
are irreducible.

It follows that maximal irreducible sets in the Zariski topology are Zariski closed, as

X\subseteq\overline{X}\subseteq X^{\text{zc}}

We shall now show that the study of irreducible Zariski closed sets can be reduced to the study of irreducible spectra.

Proposition 5.4

If
Y\subseteq X
, then
Y
is irreducible in

if and only if
Y
is in the corresponding subclosure system
\hat{Y}
.

Proof.

We note that the closed sets of
\hat{Y}
are precisely

where

is closed in
X
.
Then the desired result follows from the fact that

Y\subseteq Y\cap S\iff Y=Y\cap S\iff Y\subseteq S

for any

, and the fact that
Y\cap(A\cup B)=(Y\cap A)\cup(Y\cap B)

By Theorem

Proposition 5.5

Let

be a quasi-isomorphism of closure systems.
In particular, the quasi-isomorphism

The proof trivially follows from the fact that the direct and preimage give a bijection between the closed sets, and that they both preserve unions.

Proposition 5.6

Let

Proof.

By Proposition

Suppose WLOG that

Then, by irreducibility,

for some

Consider now another minimal decomposition

Corollary

It will be apparent, though, that this is not necessarily useful to connect the irreducibility of the

For this we need the distinguished open sets.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
a_{0},a_{1}\in A
.
Then the
distinguished open set
of
(a_{i},b_{i})
is the set

D^{\varphi}(a_{i},b_{i}):=\{\theta\in\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}\mid\langle a_{0},a_{1}\rangle\notin\theta\}

Its clear that
D^{\varphi}(a_{i},b_{i})=\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}-V_{\varphi}(a_{0},a_{1})
, and that it is the set of kernels of homomorphisms into
K
which separate
a_{0}
and

.
We can then reframe the definition of an irreducible spectrum into a very convenient one with distinguished open sets.

Lemma 5.7
.

\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
is irreducible and
\mathbf{A}
is
\varphi
-reduced if and only if any finite intersection of distinguished open sets
D_{\varphi}(a_{i},b_{i})
for
a_{i}\neq b_{i}
is nonempty.

Proof.

First note that

\displaystyle\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}D^{\varphi}(a_{i},b_{i})=\varnothing

\displaystyle\iff\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}-\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}D^{\varphi}(a_{i},b_{i})=\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}

\displaystyle\iff\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}(\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}-D^{\varphi}(a_{i},b_{i}))=\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}

\displaystyle\iff\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}V_{\varphi}(a_{i},b_{i})=\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}

(\Rightarrow)

Suppose that
\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
is irreducible and
\mathbf{A}
is
\varphi
-reduced, and by way of contradiction that
\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}D^{\varphi}(a_{i},b_{i})=\varnothing
for
a_{i}\neq b_{i}
.

\implies\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}V_{\varphi}(a_{i},b_{i})=\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}

\implies\exists k\leq n:\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}\subseteq V_{\varphi}(a_{k},b_{k})

\implies\langle a_{k},b_{k}\rangle\in\operatorname{nil}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}=\Delta_{A}

But this is a contradiction, as
a_{i}\neq b_{i}
for all
i
, so the original intersection must be empty.

(\Leftarrow)

Now suppose that any finite intersection of distinguished subsets
D^{\varphi}(a_{i},b_{i})
with
a_{i}\neq b_{i}
is nonempty.
This, in particular, means that
D^{\varphi}(a,b)
is nonempty if
a\neq b
, and therefore
\operatorname{nil}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}=\Delta_{A}
.
Then one can then repeat the argument above to see that this also implies irreducibility.
∎

This is a really powerful characterisation.
The intersection of
D^{\varphi}(a_{i},b_{i})
for
a_{i}\neq b_{i}
always being nonempty is equivalent to the property that, for any finite family of pairs
\langle a_{i},b_{i}\rangle

K

n
-separated by

Theorem 5.8

\mathbf{A}
is

if and only if it is
\varphi
-reduced and has an irreducible spectrum.

if and only if

n

\mathbf{A}

Proof.

Indeed, if
K

\mathbf{A}
and
a_{i}\neq b_{i}\in A

, then there must be a homomorphism pairwise separating
W=\{a_{1},b_{1},\dots,a_{n},b_{n}\}

Conversely, if

then we get the pairs

This means it pairwise separates

And we can immediately prove the theorem.

\mathbf{A}

\mathbf{A}
is finitely generated, then every
\varphi
-radical congruence has a decomposition into a finite number of
\varphi
-prime congruences.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be an algebra satisfying any of the two hyptheses above for some coherent condition
\varphi
.
Note that quotients of
C
-Noetherian or finitely generated algebras are respectively
C
-Noetherian and finitely generated, so it is enough to consider the case of
\mathbf{A}
being reduced and finding a decomposition of
\Delta_{A}
.

Then by Theorem
4.2
.6,
\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
is a Noetherian topological space.
So, let
\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}=C_{1}\cup\dots\cup C_{n}
be the decomposition into irreducible components which is minimal by Proposition
4.1
.
Then define
\psi_{i}:=\psi(C_{i})
, which by Theorem
5.10
is a prime congruence.
Notice that

\psi_{i}\supset\psi_{1}\cap\dots\cap\psi_{n}\;\forall i

\implies C_{i}\subseteq V_{\varphi}(\psi_{1}\cap\dots\cap\psi_{n})\;\forall i

Hence
V_{\varphi}(\psi_{1}\cap\dots\cap\psi_{n})=\operatorname{Spec}^{\varphi}\mathbf{A}
and therefore
\psi_{1}\cap\dots\cap\psi_{n}=\Delta_{A}
.
∎

Note that we cannot expect this given decomposition to be unique, or even minimal, as
V_{\varphi}(\psi_{1}\cap\psi_{2})
might not be
V_{\varphi}(\psi_{1})\cup V_{\varphi}(\psi_{2})
.

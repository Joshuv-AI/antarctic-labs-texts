A note on common complements
Esteban Andruchow, Eduardo Chiumiento

Abstract
We discuss the structure of the set Δ \Delta consisting of pairs of closed subspaces that have a common complement in a Hilbert space previously studied by Lauzon and Treil (J. Funct. Anal. 212: 500–512, 2004). We prove that Δ \Delta is the base space of a real analytic fiber bundle constructed in terms of geometric objects associated to the Grassmann manifold. As a consequence we determine the homotopy type of Δ \Delta .



2020 MSC:
47B02; 57N20; 58B10

Keywords:
Common complement; complementary subspaces; Grassmann manifold; Banach-Lie group; fiber bundle; contractible space.

1
Introduction

Let
{\cal H}
be a separable complex Hilbert space, and denote by
\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
the Grassmann manifold of
{\cal H}
consisting of all the closed subspaces of
{\cal H}
.
Consider the set
\Delta
of all pairs of subspaces that admit a common complement, which can be described as follows

\Delta:=\{({\cal S},{\cal T})\in\mathbb{G}({\cal H})\times\mathbb{G}({\cal H}):\exists{\cal Z}\in\mathbb{G}({\cal H})\hbox{ such that }{\cal Z}\dot{+}{\cal S}={\cal Z}\dot{+}{\cal T}={\cal H}\}.

Here the symbol
\dot{+}
stands for direct sum (
{\cal S}\dot{+}{\cal Z}={\cal H}
means that
{\cal S}+{\cal Z}={\cal H}
and
{\cal S}\cap{\cal Z}=\{0\}
). Several characterizations of this set were given in the seminal work by Lauzon and Treil
[
11
]
.
Later on Giol
[
7
]
linked subspaces with common complement and the problem of estimating the number of segments between homotopic idempotents. More recently, we proved in
[
3
]
that
\Delta
is an open subset of
\mathbb{G}({\cal H})\times\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
, determined its connected components, and we showed that its complement
(\mathbb{G}({\cal H})\times\mathbb{G}({\cal H}))\setminus\Delta
is a Banach manifold with components described by dimensions and semi-Fredholm indices. In this note we obtain finer results on the topological and differentiable structure of
\Delta
.

We will use several geometric constructions related to the Grassmann manifold
\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
. This manifold has been thoroughly studied (see, e.g.,
[
6
,
9
,
13
]
). It has been particularly useful to identify a closed subspace
{\cal S}
with the orthogonal projection
P_{\cal S}
onto
{\cal S}
, or with the symmetry
\epsilon_{\cal S}=2P_{\cal S}-1
which is the identity on
{\cal S}
and minus the identity in
{\cal S}^{\perp}
. This allows to endow
\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
with the relative topology as a real analytic submanifold of
{\cal B}({\cal H})
, the algebra of bounded linear operators acting on
{\cal H}
. Another way to construct a manifold structure on
\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
is to define for each
{\cal Z}\in\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
, the following set

\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}:=\{{\cal S}\in\mathbb{G}({\cal H}):{\cal S}\dot{+}{\cal Z}={\cal H}\}.

Subspaces in
\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}
turn out to be be in one-to-one correspondence with graphs of operators in
{\cal B}({\cal Z}^{\perp},{\cal Z})
, and then an atlas can be constructed by defining charts on the family of sets
\{\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}\}_{{\cal Z}\in\mathbb{G}({\cal H})}
.
The presentations of the Grassmann manifold as subspaces or orthogonal projections are indeed the same in the sense of differential geometry, meaning that the bijective map
{\cal S}\to P_{\cal S}
is a real analytic diffeomorphism (
[
1
]
).

We now describe our two main results on the structure of
\Delta
. Denote by
Gl({\cal H})
the linear group of
{\cal H}
(i.e., bounded linear invertible operators in
{\cal H}
) and by
{\cal U}({\cal H})
the subgroup of unitary operators.
A useful tool in our study will be, for a given closed subspace
{\cal Z}\subset{\cal H}
, the subgroup

Gl^{\cal Z}:=\{G\in Gl({\cal H}):G({\cal Z})={\cal Z}\}.

Clearly, if
{\cal S}\dot{+}{\cal Z}={\cal H}
and
G\in Gl^{\cal Z}
, then
G({\cal S})\dot{+}{\cal Z}={\cal H}
. This means that
Gl^{\cal Z}
acts on the open sets
\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}\subseteq\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
as follows
G\cdot{\cal S}=G({\cal S})
, for all
{\cal S}\in\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}
and
G\in Gl^{\cal Z}
. Furthermore, we prove that
Gl^{\cal Z}
is a Banach-Lie group and
\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}
is a real analytic homogeneous space of
Gl^{\cal Z}
.
We then construct a real analytic fiber bundle in which the base space is the open set
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{G}({\cal H})\times\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
and the total space is given by

{\cal E}:=\bigsqcup_{{\cal Z}\in\mathbb{G}({\cal H})}Gl^{\cal Z}\times Gl^{\cal Z}.

The set
{\cal E}
can be endowed with a manifold structure by using the same ideas of the frame bundle construction in classical differential geometry, and the motivation for considering it comes from the fact that
(G({\cal Z}^{\perp}),K({\cal Z}^{\perp}))\in\Delta
for every
{\cal Z}\in\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
and
G,K\in Gl^{\cal Z}
because
{\cal Z}^{\perp}\oplus{\cal Z}={\cal H}
. This, in turn, leads us to define the following map

\mathfrak{p}:{\cal E}\to\Delta,\,\,\,\mathfrak{p}(Z,G,K)=(G({\cal Z}^{\perp}),K({\cal Z}^{\perp})).

The first main result of this note is that
\mathfrak{p}
is a real analytic fiber bundle (Theorem
2.12
). Here
\Delta
is considered with its natural manifold structure as an open subset of
\mathbb{G}({\cal H})\times\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
.

The connected components of
\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
are given by
\mathbb{G}_{ij}
, where
i,j\in\{0,1,\ldots,\infty\}
are the dimensions and codimensions of the subspaces (
i+j=\infty
); and
it is known that
\mathbb{G}_{\infty}:=\mathbb{G}_{\infty,\infty}
is contractible, a fact which follows directly form Kuiper’s theorem
[
10
]
.
It was proved in
[
3
]
that the connected components of
\Delta
are
\Delta_{ij}:=\mathbb{G}_{ij}\times\mathbb{G}_{ij}
for
i<\infty
or
j<\infty
, and
\Delta_{\infty}:=\Delta\cap(\mathbb{G}_{\infty}\times\mathbb{G}_{\infty})
. Furthermore, it was shown that
\Delta_{\infty}
is dense in
\mathbb{G}_{\infty}\times\mathbb{G}_{\infty}
. Since
\mathbb{G}_{i,\infty}
and
\mathbb{G}_{\infty,i}
for
i<\infty
, have the homotopy type of the classifying space of the finite dimensional linear group
Gl(i)
, it follows that
\Delta_{\infty}
becomes the only component of topological interest.
The second main result of this note is that
\Delta_{\infty}
is contractible (Theorem
3.3
).

2.1
The Banach-Lie group
Gl^{\cal Z}

We will work in the setting of real analytic Banach manifolds. For the notions of Banach-Lie group, analytic homogeneous space, submanifolds and others we refer to
[
5
,
14
]
. In particular, we observe that the notion of submanifold we consider also appears in the literature as complemented submanifold. We denote
R(T)
and
N(T)
the range and nullspace of an operator
T\in{\cal B}({\cal H})
. For
{\cal L},{\cal K}\subseteq{\cal H}

{\cal L}\dot{+}{\cal K}={\cal H}
, we denote by
P_{{\cal L}\parallel{\cal K}}
the idempotent with range
{\cal L}
and nullspace
{\cal K}
. In particular,
P_{\cal S}=P_{{\cal S}\parallel{\cal S}^{\perp}}
is the orthogonal projection onto
{\cal S}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Buckholtz proved in
[
4
]
the following results.

i)

Let
P,Q
be orhogonal projections. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

P-Q

(b)

\|P+Q-1\|<1
.

(c)

R(P)\dot{+}R(Q)={\cal H}
.

ii)

If
{\cal L},{\cal K}\subset{\cal H}
are closed subspaces such that
{\cal L}\dot{+}{\cal K}={\cal H}
, then
P_{\cal L}-P_{\cal K}
is invertible, and

P_{{\cal L}\parallel{\cal K}}=P_{\cal L}(P_{\cal L}-P_{\cal K})^{-1}.

Remark 2.2
.

As an immediate consequence the sets
\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}=\{{\cal S}\in\mathbb{G}({\cal H}):{\cal S}\dot{+}{\cal Z}={\cal H}\}
,
{\cal Z}\in\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
,
are open in
\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
.
Indeed,
{\cal S}\in\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}
if and only if
\|P_{\cal S}+P_{\cal Z}-1\|<1
, which is an open condition on
P_{\cal S}
.
Consequently,
\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}
is a real analytic submanifold of
{\cal B}({\cal H})
by the identification
{\cal S}\mapsto P_{\cal S}
. Moreover, the map

\varphi_{\cal Z}:\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}\to{\cal B}({\cal Z}^{\perp},{\cal Z})
defined by
\varphi_{\cal Z}({\cal S})=P_{\cal Z}(P_{{\cal Z}^{\perp}}|_{\cal S})^{-1}
,
is a chart for
\mathbb{G}({\cal Z})
at
{\cal Z}^{\perp}
, and thus a homeomorphism onto the Banach space
{\cal B}({\cal Z}^{\perp},{\cal Z})
of bounded
linear operators from
{\cal Z}
to
{\cal Z}^{\perp}
(see, e.g.,
[
1
]
). Hence the sets
\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}
,
{\cal Z}\in\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
, are also contractible.

Proposition 2.3
.

The group
Gl^{\cal Z}=\{G\in Gl({\cal H}):G({\cal Z})={\cal Z}\}
, where
{\cal Z}\in\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
, is a Banach-Lie group. Moreover, it is a real analytic submanifold of
{\cal B}({\cal H})
.

Proof.

The proof is straightforward writing the elements of
Gl^{\cal Z}
as
2\times 2
matrices in terms of the decomposition
{\cal H}={\cal Z}\oplus{\cal Z}^{\perp}
: they are the upper triangular elements of
Gl({\cal H})
.
∎

The linear group
Gl({\cal H})
of
{\cal H}
acts analytically on
\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
in a natural fashion:

G\cdot{\cal S}=G({\cal S}),\hbox{ for }G\in Gl({\cal H}),{\cal S}\in\mathbb{G}({\cal H}).

This means that the map

Gl^{\cal Z}\times\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}\to\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z},\ (G,{\cal S})\mapsto G({\cal S})

is real analytic, a fact that follows by the diffeomorphism with the presentation of
\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
as orthogonal projections and the formula
(
[
2
]
):

P_{G({\cal S})}=GP_{\cal S}G^{-1}\left(GP_{\cal S}G^{-1}+(GP_{\cal S}G^{-1})^{*}-1\right)^{-1}.

As we have already observed, given
{\cal Z}\in\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
the group
Gl^{\cal Z}
acts on
\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}
by
G\cdot{\cal S}=G({\cal S})
,
{\cal S}\in\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}
and
G\in Gl^{\cal Z}
. That is, this action is obtained by restriction of the above action of
Gl({\cal H})
on
\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
, and since
\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}
and
Gl^{\cal Z}
are submanifolds, we get that the action of
Gl^{\cal Z}
on
\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}
is also analytic.
Fixing the element
{\cal S}_{0}\in\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}
, this action induces the following map

\pi_{{\cal S}_{0}}:Gl^{\cal Z}\to\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z},\ \pi_{{\cal S}_{0}}(G)=G({\cal S}_{0}).

Next we see that the action is transitive, or equivalently, the map
\pi_{{\cal S}_{0}}
is onto. To this end and other differential properties of this map, we introduce the following operators.

Definition 2.4
.

For
{\cal S},{\cal T},{\cal Z}\in\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
such that
{\cal Z}\dot{+}{\cal S}={\cal Z}\dot{+}{\cal T}={\cal H}
, we write

{\bf L}^{\cal Z}_{{\cal S},{\cal T}}:=P_{{\cal Z}\parallel{\cal S}}+P_{{\cal T}\parallel{\cal Z}}P_{{\cal S}\parallel{\cal Z}}.

Lemma 2.5
.

For
{\cal S},{\cal T},{\cal Z}\in\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
as above, we have
{\bf L}^{\cal Z}_{{\cal S},{\cal T}}({\cal S})={\cal T}
and
{\bf L}^{\cal Z}_{{\cal S},{\cal T}}\in Gl^{\cal Z}
. Furthermore, the map
\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}\times\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}\to Gl^{\cal Z}
,
({\cal S},{\cal T})\mapsto{\bf L}^{\cal Z}_{{\cal S},{\cal T}}

is real analytic.

Proof.

We set
L={\bf L}^{\cal Z}_{{\cal S},{\cal T}}
. If
\zeta\in{\cal Z}
, then
L\zeta=P_{{\cal Z}\parallel{\cal S}}\zeta=\zeta
. If
\xi\in{\cal S}
, then
L\xi=P_{{\cal T}\parallel{\cal Z}}\xi\in{\cal T}
. Thus
L({\cal Z})={\cal Z}
and
L({\cal S})\subset{\cal T}
. This implies that
N(L)=\{0\}
: if
\chi\in N(L)
, write
\chi=\xi+\zeta
for
\xi\in{\cal S}
and
\zeta\in{\cal Z}
. Then
0=L(\xi)+L(\zeta)=P_{{\cal T}\parallel{\cal S}}\xi+\zeta
, these vectors belong to complementary subspaces, thus
\zeta=0
and
P_{{\cal T}\parallel{\cal Z}}\xi=0
, and thus also
\xi\in{\cal Z}
, i.e.,
\zeta=0=\xi
. Clearly
{\cal Z}\subset R(L)
. Note that in fact
L({\cal S})={\cal T}
:
P_{{\cal T}\parallel{\cal Z}}|_{\cal S}:{\cal S}\to{\cal T}
is an isomorphism. In particular this implies that
L
is invertible:
{\cal H}={\cal T}+{\cal Z}\subset R(L)
. Moreover, in view of (
1
) we have that

{\bf L}^{\cal Z}_{{\cal S},{\cal T}}=P_{\cal Z}(P_{\cal Z}-P_{{\cal S}})^{-1}+P_{\cal T}(P_{\cal T}-P_{\cal Z})^{-1}P_{{\cal S}}(P_{{\cal S}}-P_{\cal Z})^{-1},

which is clearly a real analytic map for
({\cal S},{\cal T})\in\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}\times\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}
taking values in
{\cal B}({\cal H})
. The restriction to
Gl^{\cal Z}
is also real analytic, since we have shown that this group is a submanifold of
{\cal B}({\cal H})
.
∎

The main properties relating the open sets
\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}
and the Banach-Lie group
Gl^{\cal Z}
can be stated as follows.

Proposition 2.6
.

The map
\pi_{{\cal S}_{0}}
is a real analytic submersion, with an analytic global cross section. Moreover, since
\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}
is a real analytic homogeneous space of
Gl^{\cal Z}
, the map
\pi_{{\cal S}_{0}}
is a trivial fiber bundle.

Proof.

Given
{\cal S}_{0}
and
{\cal T}
closed subspaces in
\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}
, we take the operator

{\bf L}^{\cal Z}_{{\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}}
,
which satisfies that
{\bf L}^{\cal Z}_{{\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}}\in Gl^{\cal Z}
and
\pi_{{\cal S}_{0}}({\bf L}^{\cal Z}_{{\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}})={\cal T}
. Thus,
\pi_{{\cal S}_{0}}
is onto. Moreover,
note have that

\sigma_{{\cal S}_{0}}:\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}\to Gl^{\cal Z},\ \sigma_{{\cal S}_{0}}({\cal T})={\bf L}^{\cal Z}_{{\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}},

is a real analytic global cross section for
\pi_{{\cal S}_{0}}
.
Therefore,
\pi_{{\cal S}_{0}}:Gl^{\cal Z}\to\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}
is a homogeneous space with a global cross section, it follows that it is a trivial bundle (see
[
5
]
).
∎

2.2
The manifold
{\cal E}

For notational simplicity, we let

{\cal L}_{{\cal Z},{\cal S}}:={\bf L}^{\cal Z}_{{\cal S},{\cal Z}^{\perp}}=P_{{\cal Z}\parallel{\cal S}}+P_{{\cal Z}^{\perp}}P_{{\cal S}\parallel{\cal Z}},

whenever
{\cal Z}\in\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
and
{\cal S}\in\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}
. Note that
{\cal L}_{{\cal Z},{\cal S}}({\cal S})={\cal Z}^{\perp}
and
{\cal L}_{{\cal Z},{\cal S}}\in Gl^{\cal Z}
. We observe that the map
\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}\to Gl^{\cal Z}
,
{\cal S}\to{\cal L}_{{\cal Z},{\cal S}}
, is real analytic as a consequence of Lemma
2.5

The following auxiliary space will be the total space of our fiber bundle, and will play an important role the study of the topology of
\Delta
.

Definition 2.7
.

Put

\displaystyle{\cal E}

\displaystyle:=\bigsqcup_{{\cal Z}\in\mathbb{G}({\cal H})}Gl^{\cal Z}\times Gl^{\cal Z}=\{({\cal Z},G,K)\in\mathbb{G}({\cal H})\times Gl({\cal H})\times Gl({\cal H}):G({\cal Z})=K({\cal Z})={\cal Z}\}.

Proposition 2.8
.

{\cal E}
is a real analytic manifold.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathbb{G}_{ij}
are the connected components of
\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
, where
i
and
j
indicates the dimension and codimension of the subspaces, respectively. Our definition of an atlas depends on those components, so we fix one component
\mathbb{G}_{ij}
, and two subspaces
{\cal H}_{+}
and
{\cal H}_{-}
such that
\dim{\cal H}_{-}=i
,
\dim{\cal H}_{+}=j
and
{\cal H}_{+}\oplus{\cal H}_{-}={\cal H}
. For a given
{\cal Z}\in\mathbb{G}_{ij}
, we denote

{\cal U}_{{\cal Z}}({\cal H}):=\{T\in{\cal U}({\cal H}):T({\cal Z}^{\perp})={\cal H}_{+}\}.

Then for
{\cal Z}_{0}\in\mathbb{G}_{ij}
,
T_{0},T^{\prime}_{0}\in{\cal U}_{{\cal Z}_{0}}({\cal H})
we define

\Phi_{{\cal Z}_{0},T_{0},T^{\prime}_{0}}:\mathbb{G}^{{\cal Z}_{0}}\times Gl^{{\cal H}_{+}}\times Gl^{{\cal H}_{+}}\to{\cal E},

\Phi_{{\cal Z}_{0},T_{0},T^{\prime}_{0}}({\cal Z},G,K)=({\cal Z},{\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{0},{\cal Z}}^{-1}T_{0}^{*}GT_{0}{\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{0},{\cal Z}},{\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{0},{\cal Z}}^{-1}(T^{\prime}_{0})^{*}KT^{\prime}_{0}{\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{0},{\cal Z}}).

This map is well defined: note that
{\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{0},{\cal Z}}^{-1}T_{0}^{*}GT_{0}{\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{0},{\cal Z}}
is an invertible operator which maps
{\cal Z}
onto
{\cal Z}
:

{\cal Z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{0},{\cal Z}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal H}_{+}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle G}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal H}_{+}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T_{0}^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{0},{\cal Z}}^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal Z},

and similarly for the last coordinate of
\Phi_{{\cal Z}_{0},T_{0},T^{\prime}_{0}}({\cal Z},G,K)
.
Note also that
\Phi_{{\cal Z}_{0},T_{0},T^{\prime}_{0}}
is injective:

\Phi_{{\cal Z}_{0},T_{0},T^{\prime}_{0}}({\cal Z},G,K)=\Phi_{{\cal Z}_{0},T_{0},T^{\prime}_{0}}({\cal Z}^{\prime},G^{\prime},K^{\prime}),

clearly implies that
{\cal Z}={\cal Z}^{\prime}
, from which
G=G^{\prime}
and
K=K^{\prime}
readily follow. This map has a local inverse, defined on the set

{\cal B}_{{\cal Z}_{0}}:=\{({\cal S},A,B)\in{\cal E}:\|P_{\cal S}-P_{{\cal Z}_{0}}\|<1\}.

If
({\cal S},A,B)\in{\cal B}_{{\cal Z}_{0}}
then
{\cal S}\dot{+}{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}={\cal H}
, so that
{\cal S},{\cal Z}_{0}\in\mathbb{G}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}}
, and
{\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{0},{\cal S}}
is defined, and the map
\Phi_{{\cal Z}_{0},T_{0},T^{\prime}_{0}}
can be clearly reversed. Thus we have local charts for
{\cal E}
, which model it in the Banach space
{\cal B}({\cal H})^{3}
.

Let us check that the transition maps are analytic. Pick
{\cal Z}_{0},{\cal Z}_{1}\in\mathbb{G}_{ij}
. Denote
\Phi_{1}=\Phi_{{\cal Z}_{1},T_{1},T^{\prime}_{1}}
and
\Phi_{0}=\Phi_{{\cal Z}_{0},T_{0},T^{\prime}_{0}}
, then

\Phi_{1}^{-1}\circ\Phi_{0}:(\mathbb{G}^{{\cal Z}_{0}}\cap\mathbb{G}^{{\cal Z}_{1}})\times Gl^{{\cal H}_{+}}\times Gl^{{\cal H}_{+}}\to(\mathbb{G}^{{\cal Z}_{0}}\cap\mathbb{G}^{{\cal Z}_{1}})\times Gl^{{\cal H}_{+}}\times Gl^{{\cal H}_{+}}

\Phi_{1}^{-1}\circ\Phi_{0}({\cal Z},G,K)=\Phi^{-1}({\cal Z},{\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{0},{\cal Z}}^{-1}T_{0}^{*}GT_{0}{\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{0},z},{\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{0},{\cal Z}}^{-1}(T^{\prime}_{0})^{*}KT^{\prime}_{0}{\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{0},z})=

\left({\cal Z},T_{1}{\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{1},{\cal Z}}({\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{0},{\cal Z}}^{-1}T_{0}^{*}GT_{0}{\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{0},z}){\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{1},{\cal Z}}^{-1}T_{1}^{*},T^{\prime}_{1}{\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{1},{\cal Z}}({\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{0},{\cal Z}}^{-1}(T^{\prime}_{0})^{*}KT^{\prime}_{0}{\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{0},z}){\cal L}_{{\cal Z}_{1},{\cal Z}}^{-1}(T^{\prime}_{1})^{*}\right),

which is analytic. This completes the construction of an atlas for
{\cal E}
.
∎

2.3
A real analytic fiber bundle

Related to
{\cal E}
, we have the following map

{\bf\mathfrak{p}}:{\cal E}\to\Delta,\,\,\,\,{\bf\mathfrak{p}}({\cal Z},G,K)=(G({\cal Z}^{\perp}),K({\cal Z}^{\perp})).

Note that
{\bf\mathfrak{p}}
is well defined: since
G,K\in Gl^{\cal Z}
,
{\cal Z}\oplus{\cal Z}^{\perp}={\cal H}
implies that

{\cal Z}\dot{+}G({\cal Z}^{\perp})={\cal H}={\cal Z}\dot{+}K({\cal Z}^{\perp})
. Also observe that
{\bf\mathfrak{p}}:{\cal E}\to\Delta
is onto. Given
({\cal S},{\cal T})\in\Delta
, let
{\cal Z}
be a common complement for
{\cal S}
and
{\cal T}
. Then
{\cal S},{\cal T},{\cal Z}^{\perp}
have common complement
{\cal Z}
, which enables the existence of
{\bf L}_{{\cal Z}^{\perp},{\cal S}}^{\cal Z}
and
{\bf L}_{{\cal Z}^{\perp},{\cal T}}^{\cal Z}
by Lemma
2.5
, and clearly
{\bf\mathfrak{p}}({\cal Z},{\bf L}_{{\cal Z}^{\perp},{\cal S}}^{\cal Z},{\bf L}_{{\cal Z}^{\perp},{\cal T}}^{\cal Z})=({\cal S},{\cal T})
. Recall from
[
3
]
that the connected components of
\Delta
are given by

\Delta_{ij}=\mathbb{G}_{ij}\times\mathbb{G}_{ij}
for
i<\infty
or
j<\infty
, and
\Delta_{\infty}:=\Delta_{\infty,\infty}:=\Delta\cap(\mathbb{G}_{\infty}\times\mathbb{G}_{\infty})
. We thus get that
{\bf\mathfrak{p}}({\cal E}_{ij})=\Delta_{ji}
for all
i
,
j
such that
i+j=\infty
.

Let us check now that
{\bf\mathfrak{p}}
is a real analytic fiber bundle. We examine first the fibers: for
({\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}_{0})\in\Delta
, set

{\bf F}_{({\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}_{0})}:={\bf\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}({\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}_{0})=\{({\cal Z},G,K)\in{\cal E}:G({\cal Z}^{\perp})={\cal S}_{0},K({\cal Z}^{\perp})={\cal T}_{0}\}.

We have the following characterization.

Proposition 2.10
.

Take
({\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}_{0})\in\Delta_{ij}
and two subspaces
{\cal H}_{+}
,
{\cal H}_{-}
such that
\dim{\cal H}_{-}=i
,
\dim{\cal H}_{+}=j
and
{\cal H}_{+}\oplus{\cal H}_{-}={\cal H}
. Then
{\bf F}_{({\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}_{0})}
is a closed submanifold of
{\cal E}
, and there is a diffeomorphism

{\bf F}_{({\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}_{0})}\simeq\mathbb{G}^{{\cal S}_{0}}\times(Gl^{{\cal H}_{+}}\cap Gl^{{\cal H}_{-}})^{2}.

Proof.

An alternative expression for the fibers is given by

{\bf F}_{({\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}_{0})}=\{({\cal Z},G,K)\in\mathbb{G}^{{\cal S}_{0}}\times Gl^{\cal Z}\times Gl^{\cal Z}:G({\cal Z}^{\perp})={\cal S}_{0},\,K({\cal Z}^{\perp})={\cal T}_{0}\}.

Indeed, for
({\cal Z},G,K)\in{\cal E}
such that
{\bf\mathfrak{p}}({\cal Z},G,K)=({\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}_{0})
, one has
G({\cal Z}^{\perp})={\cal S}_{0}
and
K({\cal Z}^{\perp})={\cal T}_{0}
. Since
{\cal H}={\cal Z}\oplus{\cal Z}^{\perp}
, it follows that
{\cal H}={\cal Z}\dot{+}G({\cal Z}^{\perp})={\cal Z}\dot{+}{\cal S}_{0}
, which means
{\cal Z}\in\mathbb{G}^{{\cal S}_{0}}
. The reversed inclusion is trivial. In particular, note

{\bf F}_{({\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}_{0})}\subseteq\bigsqcup_{{\cal Z}\in\mathbb{G}^{{\cal S}_{0}}}Gl^{\cal Z}\times Gl^{\cal Z}={\cal B}_{{\cal S}_{0}^{\perp}},

where the set on the right-hand side is open in
{\cal E}
(see Remark
2.9
).

Next recall that for
{\cal Z}
such that
{\cal S}_{0}\dot{+}{\cal Z}={\cal T}_{0}\dot{+}{\cal Z}={\cal H}
, there exists
{\bf L}_{\cal Z}:={\bf L}^{\cal Z}_{{\cal T}_{0},{\cal S}_{0}}\in Gl^{\cal Z}
such that
{\bf L}_{\cal Z}({\cal T}_{0})={\cal S}_{0}
. Fix
T\in{\cal U}({\cal H})
such that
T{\cal S}_{0}={\cal H}_{-}
. We consider the map
\Psi:\mathbb{G}^{{\cal S}_{0}}\times(Gl^{{\cal H}+})^{2}\to{\cal B}_{{\cal S}_{0}^{\perp}}
,
\Psi({\cal Z},G,K)=(\Psi_{1}({\cal Z},G,K),\Psi_{2}({\cal Z},G,K),\Psi_{3}({\cal Z},G,K))
defined by

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}({\cal Z},G,K)

\displaystyle={\cal Z};

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}({\cal Z},G,K)

\displaystyle={\cal L}_{{\cal S}_{0},{\cal Z}}^{-1}T^{*}G^{-1}T{\cal L}_{{\cal S}_{0},{\cal Z}}{\cal L}_{{\cal Z},{\cal S}_{0}}^{-1};

\displaystyle\Psi_{3}({\cal Z},G,K)

\displaystyle={\bf L}_{{\cal Z}}^{-1}{\cal L}_{{\cal S}_{0},{\cal Z}}^{-1}T^{*}K^{-1}T{\cal L}_{{\cal S}_{0},{\cal Z}}{\cal L}_{{\cal Z},{\cal S}_{0}}^{-1}.

First we show that
\Psi
is well defined. Its second coordinate takes values on
Gl^{{\cal Z}}
, since

{\cal Z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal L}_{{\cal Z},{\cal S}_{0}}^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal Z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal L}_{{\cal S}_{0},{\cal Z}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal S}_{0}^{\perp}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal H}_{+}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle G^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal H}_{+}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal S}_{0}^{\perp}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal L}_{{\cal S}_{0},{\cal Z}}^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal Z}.

From this expression we also deduce that its third coordinate takes values on
Gl^{\cal Z}
. Clearly,
\Psi
is bijective because one can easily write its inverse. Take
\Phi_{{\cal Z}_{0},T_{0},T_{0}^{\prime}}
a chart on
{\cal E}
defined on
\mathbb{G}^{{\cal Z}_{0}}\times(Gl^{{\cal H}+})^{2}
satisfying
\mathbb{G}^{{\cal S}_{0}}\cap\mathbb{G}^{{\cal Z}_{0}}\neq\emptyset
. A similar computation to that at the end of the proof of Theorem
2.8
shows that
\Psi^{-1}\circ\Phi_{{\cal Z}_{0},T_{0},T_{0}^{\prime}}
and
\Phi_{{\cal Z}_{0},T_{0},T_{0}^{\prime}}^{-1}\circ\Psi
are analytic functions. Thus,
\Psi
is also a chart compatible with the manifold structure of
{\cal E}
.

Now note that for
G,K\in Gl^{{\cal H}_{-}}
, we have

{\cal Z}^{\perp}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal L}_{{\cal Z},{\cal S}_{0}}^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal S}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal L}_{{\cal S}_{0},{\cal Z}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal S}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal H}_{-}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle G^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal H}_{-}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal S}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal L}_{{\cal S}_{0},{\cal Z}}^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal S}_{0}

and

{\cal Z}^{\perp}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal L}_{{\cal Z},{\cal S}_{0}}^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal S}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal L}_{{\cal S}_{0},{\cal Z}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal S}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal H}_{-}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle K^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal H}_{-}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal S}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal L}_{{\cal S}_{0},{\cal Z}}^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal S}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\bf L}_{{\cal Z}}^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal T}_{0}.

Thus, we find that
\Psi(\mathbb{G}^{{\cal S}_{0}}\times(Gl^{{\cal H}+}\cap Gl^{{\cal H}_{-}})^{2})={\bf F}_{({\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}_{0})}
. Since
Gl^{{\cal H}+}\cap Gl^{{\cal H}_{-}}
and
Gl^{{\cal H}_{+}}
are block diagonal and block upper triangular operators with respect to the decomposition
{\cal H}_{+}\oplus{\cal H}_{-}={\cal H}
, it follows that
\mathbb{G}^{{\cal S}_{0}}\times(Gl^{{\cal H}+}\cap Gl^{{\cal H}_{-}})^{2}
is a submanifold of
\mathbb{G}^{{\cal S}_{0}}\times(Gl^{{\cal H}+})^{2}
. Hence we have proved that
{\bf F}_{({\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}_{0})}
is a submanifold of
{\cal E}
and the stated diffeomorphism. It is straightforward to check that
{\bf F}_{({\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}_{0})}
is closed in
{\cal E}
.
∎

Now we need to recall a well-known construction regarding two projections (for details see for instance
[
13
]
). Take
P
,
Q
two orthogonal projections such that
\|P-Q\|<1
. From this last estimate, it can be shown that
S=QP+(1-Q)(1-P)
is invertible, and the unitary
W=S|S|^{-1}
satisfies
WPW^{*}=Q
.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
{\cal S},{\cal Z}\in\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
such that
{\cal S}\dot{+}{\cal Z}={\cal H}
, which in terms of projections is equivalent to
\|P_{\cal S}-P_{{\cal Z}^{\perp}}\|<1
. We denote by
W^{{\cal Z}^{\perp}}_{\cal S}
the unitary satisfying
W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}^{\perp}}P_{\cal S}(W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}^{\perp}})^{*}=P_{{\cal Z}^{\perp}}
given by the above construction.

Clearly,
the map
\mathbb{G}^{\cal Z}\to{\cal U}({\cal H})
,
{\cal S}\mapsto W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}^{\perp}}
is real analytic by properties of the analytic functional calculus.

Our main result of this section now follows.

Theorem 2.12
.

The map
{\bf\mathfrak{p}}
is a real analytic fiber bundle.

Proof.

We have already observed above that
{\bf\mathfrak{p}}
is well defined and onto. Clearly, the map
{\bf\mathfrak{p}}
is analytic by using a local expression in the charts of
{\cal E}
.
Let us exhibit local trivializations for
{\bf\mathfrak{p}}
. Fix
({\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}_{0})\in\Delta
, and a common complement
{\cal Z}_{0}
for
{\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}_{0}
. Consider the open neighborhood

({\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}_{0})\in\Delta^{{\cal Z}_{0}}=\{({\cal S},{\cal T})\in\Delta:{\cal S}\dot{+}{\cal Z}_{0}={\cal H}={\cal T}\dot{+}{\cal Z}_{0}\}.

Pick
({\cal Z},G,K)\in{\bf\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}(\Delta^{{\cal Z}_{0}})
. Denote
{\cal S}=G({\cal Z}^{\perp})
and
{\cal T}=K({\cal Z}^{\perp})
. Since
({\cal S},{\cal T})\in\Delta^{{\cal Z}_{0}}
, we have that
\|P_{\cal S}-P_{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}}\|<1
and
\|P_{\cal T}-P_{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}}\|<1
. Therefore, in particular, there exists
W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}}\in{\cal U}({\cal H})
such that
W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}}({\cal S})={\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}
. Let us fix
T\in{\cal U}({\cal H})
such that
T({\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp})={\cal H}_{-}
. As usual
{\cal H}_{+}
,
{\cal H}_{-}
are fixed subspaces such that
{\cal H}_{-}\oplus{\cal H}_{+}={\cal H}
of the appropriate dimensions determined by
({\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}_{0})
. Note also that since
G,K\in Gl^{\cal Z}
,
{\cal Z}\oplus{\cal Z}^{\perp}={\cal H}
implies that
G({\cal Z})\dot{+}G({\cal Z}^{\perp})={\cal Z}\dot{+}{\cal S}={\cal H}
and
K({\cal Z})\dot{+}K({\cal Z}^{\perp})={\cal Z}\dot{+}{\cal T}={\cal H}
. Then

{\cal H}=TW_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}}({\cal Z})\dot{+}TW_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}}({\cal S})=TW_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}}({\cal Z})\dot{+}{\cal H}_{-},

which means

TW_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}}({\cal Z})\in\mathbb{G}^{{\cal H}_{-}}.

(2)

Let us define the local trivialization at the neighbourhood
\Delta^{{\cal Z}_{0}}
. The idea is that the definition of
\Delta^{{\cal Z}_{0}}
allows us to define
W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}}
to move a fiber
{\bf F}_{({\cal S},{\cal T})}
to the generic fiber

{\bf F}:=\mathbb{G}^{{\cal H}_{-}}\times\left(Gl^{{\cal H}_{+}}\cap Gl^{{\cal H}_{-}}\right)^{2}\simeq{\bf F}_{({\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}_{0})},

which is a diffeomorphism by Proposition
2.10
. Consider

\Phi:{\bf\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}(\Delta^{{\cal Z}_{0}})\to\Delta^{{\cal Z}_{0}}\times{\bf F},

\Phi({\cal Z},G,K)=(G({\cal Z}^{\perp}),K({\cal Z}^{\perp}),u({\cal Z},G,K),a({\cal Z},G,K),b({\cal Z},G,K)).

Again we abbreviate
{\cal S}=G({\cal Z}^{\perp})
and
{\cal T}=K({\cal Z}^{\perp})
to define the last coordinates:

\displaystyle u({\cal Z},G,K)

\displaystyle=TW_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}}({\cal Z});

\displaystyle a({\cal Z},G,K)

\displaystyle=TW_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}}{\cal L}_{{\cal S},{\cal Z}}{\cal L}_{{\cal Z},{\cal S}}^{-1}G^{-1}{\cal L}_{{\cal S},{\cal Z}}^{-1}(W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}})^{*}T^{*};

\displaystyle b({\cal Z},G,K)

\displaystyle=TW_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}}{\cal L}_{{\cal S},{\cal Z}}{\cal L}_{{\cal Z},{\cal S}}^{-1}K^{-1}({\bf L}^{\cal Z}_{{\cal T},{\cal S}})^{-1}{\cal L}_{{\cal S},{\cal Z}}^{-1}(W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}})^{*}T^{*}.

The first coordinate map clearly belongs to
\mathbb{G}^{{\cal H}_{-}}
as remarked in (
2
). Let us see that
a
and
b
are also well defined. Note that under the current conditions, we have

{\cal Z}\dot{+}{\cal S}={\cal Z}_{0}\dot{+}{\cal S}={\cal Z}\dot{+}{\cal T}={\cal Z}_{0}\dot{+}{\cal T}={\cal H}.

Also

{\cal S}\dot{+}{\cal Z}={\cal H}
if and only if
\|P_{\cal S}+P_{\cal Z}-1\|<1
. This is equivalent to
\|(1-P_{\cal S})+(1-P_{\cal Z})-1\|<1
, or
{\cal S}^{\perp}\dot{+}{\cal Z}^{\perp}={\cal H}
. Similarly for the other direct sum decompositions. This implies that the
{\cal L}
and
{\bf L}
operators used above are well defined. In particular
{\cal L}_{{\cal Z},{\cal S}}\in Gl^{\cal Z}
which maps
{\cal S}
onto
{\cal Z}^{\perp}
, and
{\cal L}_{{\cal S},{\cal Z}}\in Gl^{\cal S}
which maps
{\cal Z}
onto
{\cal S}^{\perp}
.

Next let us check that
a
and
b
belong to
Gl^{{\cal H}_{+}}\cap Gl^{{\cal H}_{-}}
, i.e., that they map
{\cal H}_{+}
onto
{\cal H}_{+}
and
{\cal H}_{-}
onto
{\cal H}_{-}
.
For the case of
a
:

{\cal H}_{-}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}})^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal S}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal L}_{{\cal S},{\cal Z}}^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal S}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle G^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal Z}^{\perp}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal L}_{{\cal Z},{\cal S}}^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal S}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal L}_{{\cal S},{\cal Z}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal S}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal H}_{-}.

and

{\cal H}_{+}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal Z}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}})^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal S}^{\perp}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal L}_{{\cal S},{\cal Z}}^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal Z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle G^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal Z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal L}_{{\cal Z},{\cal S}}^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal Z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal L}_{{\cal S},{\cal Z}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal S}^{\perp}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal Z}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\cal H}_{+}.

The verification that the map
b
is diagonal is similar. The map
\Phi
can be clearly reversed, once it is well defined in the appropriate neighbourhoods. It is a real analytic diffeomorphism, which clearly trivializes the map
{\bf\mathfrak{p}}
.
∎

3
Topology of
\Delta

Another map related to the manifold
{\cal E}
is the following

\pi:{\cal E}\to\mathbb{G}({\cal H}),\,\,\,\pi({\cal Z},G,K)={\cal Z}.

Let us prove that the map
\pi
is a also a real analytic fiber bundle. For a subspace
{\cal Z}_{0}\in\mathbb{G}_{ij}\subseteq\mathbb{G}({\cal H})
, fix two subspaces
{\cal H}_{-}
and
{\cal H}_{+}
such that
\dim{\cal H}_{-}=j
,
\dim{\cal H}_{+}=i
and
{\cal H}_{-}\oplus{\cal H}_{+}={\cal H}
. Then,

\pi^{-1}({\cal Z}_{0})=\{({\cal Z}_{0},G,K):G,K\in Gl^{{\cal Z}_{0}}\}\simeq Gl^{{\cal Z}_{0}}\times Gl^{{\cal Z}_{0}}\simeq Gl^{{\cal H}_{+}}\times Gl^{{\cal H}_{+}}.

Proposition 3.1
.

The map
\pi
is a real analytic fiber bundle.

Proof.

Take arbitrary
i
,
j
such that
i+j=\infty
and fixed subspaces
{\cal H}_{+}
and
{\cal H}_{-}
as above. We saw that the fibers are Banach-Lie groups isomorphic to
Gl^{{\cal H}_{+}}\times Gl^{{\cal H}_{+}}
. Let us construct local trivializations for
\pi
. Fix
{\cal Z}_{0}\in\mathbb{G}_{ij}
, and consider the neighborhood

{\cal Z}_{0}\in\mathbb{G}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}}=\{{\cal S}\in\mathbb{G}_{ij}:\|P_{\cal S}-P_{{\cal Z}_{0}}\|<1\}.

This allows the existence of a real analytic map
\mathbb{G}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}}\ni{\cal S}\mapsto W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}}\in{\cal U}({\cal H})
which satisfies
W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}}({\cal S})={\cal Z}_{0}
. Then we have the diffeomorphism

\pi^{-1}(\mathbb{G}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}})\to\mathbb{G}^{{\cal Z}_{0}^{\perp}}\times\pi^{-1}({\cal Z}_{0}),\ ({\cal S},G,K)\mapsto{\cal S}\times({\cal Z}_{0},W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}}G(W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}})^{*},W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}}K(W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}})^{*}),

which trivializes
\pi
. Note that the above map is well defined, since clearly

W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}}G(W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}})^{*},W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}}K(W_{\cal S}^{{\cal Z}_{0}})^{*}\in Gl^{{\cal Z}_{0}},

and it can be easily reversed.
∎

As we have observed at the end of Section
1
, we are interested in topology of the connected component
\Delta_{\infty}=\Delta\cap(\mathbb{G}_{\infty}\times\mathbb{G}_{\infty})
. It can be studied by considering infinite and co-infinite dimensional subspaces in the previous constructions. We begin with the connected component

{\cal E}_{\infty}
of
{\cal E}
(see Remark
2.9
). We have the following consequence.

Corollary 3.2
.

{\cal E}_{\infty}
is contractible.

Proof.

The manifold
{\cal E}_{\infty}
is the total space of the fiber bundle
\pi|_{{\cal E}_{\infty}}:{\cal E}_{\infty}\to\mathbb{G}_{\infty}
with contractible fibers isomorphic to
Gl^{{\cal H}_{+}}\times Gl^{{\cal H}_{+}}
(
\dim{\cal H}_{\pm}=\infty
) and contractible base space
\mathbb{G}_{\infty}
by Kuiper’s theorem. Therefore it has trivial homotopy groups, and being a manifold modeled in a Banach space, is then contractible (see
[
12
]
).
∎

Our main result on the topology of
\Delta_{\infty}
follows.

Theorem 3.3
.

The open dense subset
\Delta_{\infty}\subset\mathbb{G}_{\infty}\times\mathbb{G}_{\infty}
is contractible.

Proof.

The fiber bundle
{\bf\mathfrak{p}}|_{{\cal E}_{\infty}}:{\cal E}_{\infty}\to\Delta_{\infty}
has contractible total space (Corollary
3.2
). From Proposition
2.10
we know that for any
({\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}_{0})\in\Delta_{\infty}
the fiber
{\bf F}_{({\cal S}_{0},{\cal T}_{0})}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{G}^{{\cal S}_{0}}\times(Gl^{{\cal H}_{+}}\cap Gl^{{\cal H}_{-}})^{2}
. Recall that
\mathbb{G}^{{\cal S}_{0}}
is contractible by Remark
2.2
, and
Gl^{{\cal H}_{+}}\cap Gl^{{\cal H}_{-}}\simeq Gl({\cal H}_{+})\times Gl({\cal H}_{-})
is also contractible by Kuiper’s theorem (
\dim{\cal H}_{\pm}=\infty
). Thus,
{\bf\mathfrak{p}}|_{{\cal E}_{\infty}}
has also contractible fibers. We thus get that
\Delta_{\infty}
is a Banach manifold with trivial homotopy type, and hence it is contractible
[
12
]
.
∎

Also we have the following consequence.

Corollary 3.4
.

The maps
{\bf\mathfrak{p}}|_{{\cal E}_{\infty}}
and
\pi|_{{\cal E}_{\infty}}
are in fact trivial bundles.

Proof.

Both bundles have contractible base space, total space and fibers.
∎

Adaptive Control with Set-Point Tracking and Linear-like Closed-loop Behavior
Mohamad T. Shahab

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the problem of set-point tracking for a discrete-time plant with unknown plant parameters belonging to a convex and compact uncertainty set. We carry out parameter estimation for an associated auxiliary plant, and a pole-placement-based control law is employed. We prove that this adaptive controller provides desirable linear-like closed-loop behavior which guarantees a bound consisting of: exponential decay with respect to the initial condition, a linear-like convolution bound with respect to the exogenous inputs, and a constant scaled by the square root of the constant in the denominator of the parameter estimator update law. This implies that the system has a bounded gain. Moreover, asymptotic tracking is also proven when the disturbance is constant.

We consider the
{n{{\text{th}}}}
-order linear time-invariant discrete-time plant

\displaystyle y(t+1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}y(t-j+1)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}u(t-j+1)+w(t),

\mathbf{A}_{\theta}(z^{-1})Y(z)=\mathbf{B}_{\theta}(z^{-1})U(z)+z^{-1}W(z).

(2)

We impose an assumption on the set of admissible plant parameters.

Assumption 1
.

{\cal S}

,

The convexity part of the above assumption is

[
7
]

The boundedness part

Our objectives here is to prove an exponential form of

To achieve the tracking objective, we require the following assumption:

.

For every
\theta\in{\cal S}
, the corresponding polynomial

is such that
\mathbf{B}_{\theta}(1)\neq 0
.

Remark 1
.

Here we allow the plant to have zeros outside

The Main Result

Remark 2
.

The above result shows that the closed-loop
system experiences linear-like behavior.
There is a
uniform
exponential decay bound on the effect of the initial condition, and a convolution bound on the effect of the noise.
It also provides a
clear bound in terms of the parameter
\mu
and the set-point
r
—the smaller
that they are, the smaller that this bound is.

Remark 3
.

The bound in (

with bias (from

to

\displaystyle\|\phi(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{\gamma}{1-\lambda}\left(\lambda^{t-t_{0}}\|\boldsymbol{\phi}_{0}\|+\|w\|_{\infty}+|r|+\sqrt{\mu}\right),\;t\geq t_{0}.

Remark 4
.

We can show that the convolution bound proven in Theorem

will guarantee robustness to a degree of time-variations
and unmodelled dynamics. Furthermore,
we can also obtain bounds on the average tracking

arguments to those in the proofs of Theorems 2, 3 and 4 of

VII
Summary and Conclusions

In this paper
we examine
the set-point tracking
problem when the
commonly used
projection
algorithm is utilized, subject to several common assumptions on the
set of admissible parameters.
While in the literature it is proven that the nonlinear closed-loop
system is bounded-input bounded-state, here we prove
a linear-like bound on the closed-loop behavior
consisting of
1)
a decaying exponential on the initial condition,
2)
a linear-like convolution bound on the noise input, and
3)
a constant
scaled by the square root of the constant in the denominator
of the estimator update law and the set-point.
Furthermore, in the constant disturbance case,
asymptotic tracking is proven as well.

We would like to extend these desirable results
to the
cases when
the uncertainty set is non-convex and where its convex hull violate
the comprimeness requirement and/or the plant order is not exactly known;
an adaptive controller
using multiple estimators and switching, along the lines of
[
29
]
,
may be needed.

Before proceeding, define

\overline{\alpha}:=\max_{\theta^{*}\in\overline{\cal S}}\|{\cal A}_{\theta^{*}}\|,\qquad\overline{\mathbf{s}}:=\max_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\overline{\cal S}}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|,

and
let

denote the ceiling function.

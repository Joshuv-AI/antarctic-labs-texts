Intelligent predictive neural network analysis on LTNE impacts on thermophoretic particle deposition in HFE 7100 nanofluid with Co3O4 nanoparticle
Authors: Saba Liaqat, Bouthaina Dammak, Muhammad Sabaoon Khan, Walid Abdelfattah, Joy Djuansjah, Ilyas Khan, Munawar Abbas, Nidhal Ben Khedher
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study examines the outcome of heat generation on thermophoretic particle deposition in Co3O4/HFE-7100 nanofluid flowing past a vertical cylinder in a permeable media under local thermal non-equilibrium conditions employing an ANN coupled with a Bayesian-regularized back-propagation algorithm. For contrast, a simplified mathematical formulation is also used to examine the thermal behavior without local thermal equilibrium assumptions, that is, without LTNE limitations. The solid matrix and fluid phase are treated as distinct temperature fields in the LTNE framework, necessitating unique thermal gradients for each phase. Optimizing heat transfer performance using ANN-based regression modeling is the main goal of this work. Well-structured training and testing datasets are used to guarantee numerical stability and prediction resilience, and the Bayesian-regularization back-propagation technique is applied to enhance generalization capacity. The impact of dominating governing factors is illustrated graphically, and an error evaluation is provided to gauge model correctness. The ANN model was trained using the obtained dataset and then tested against numerical values of important engineering variables. Tables and graphs are utilized to show how new factors affect the flow’s dynamics. The rate of liquid phase heat transmission declines as the inter-phase heat transport values increase.

Introduction

Machine learning approaches like as neural networks and regression models offer a quick and effective alternative to traditional CFD by allowing for real-time prediction of temperature, pressure, and velocity in complex flows. These techniques are useful for improving heat exchangers, aerodynamic designs, and energy-efficient fluid systems because they lower computational costs without compromising accuracy. In fluid dynamics applications, machine learning helps close the gap between accuracy and computing cost by discovering important governing factors that increase efficiency and reduce energy losses. Zeeshan et al. [1] examined radiation effects flowing over a porous expanding sheet in the Maxwell hybrid nanofluid solution of an artificial neural network with many slips. Mirza et al. [2] examined networks of Levenberg–Marquardt neurons are used in the smart computing approach to evaluate a dusty trihybrid nanofluid’s two-phase flow employing the CCHFM (Cattaneo-Christov heat flux model). Benderradji et al. [3] observed the application of hybrid deep neural networks and metaheuristic algorithms to optimize heat exchange in a cavity filled with magnetized nanofluid. Aoudia et al. [4] examined an ANN research thermal radiation and thermophoresis affects for the Marangoni convection-induced nanofluid convective flow with thermosolutal flow. Shah et al. [5] scrutinized the estimate of a model of a heated convective Carreau nanofluid using an artificial neural network across an exponentially stretched sheet, magnetic, and heat source/sink diffusions. Kumar et al. [6] scrutinized the computational fluid dynamics investigation and prediction of heat transportation in dusty fluid flow via hot surfaces using deep neural networks. Abdal et al. [7] examined artificial neural networks with bioconvection and nonlinear heat radiation for a Riga plate’s tangent hyperbolic NF (nanofluid) flow. The multiphysics investigation of convective flow in an inverted T-shaped porous enclosure: ANN-based parametric estimate was scrutinized by Kumar et al. [8]. Shahbaz et al. [9] studied the intelligent computing paradigm of 3D laminated MHD nanofluids over a sheet. Using deep learning parameter estimation, Kumar et al. [10] investigated thermo-magnetic radiative flow in porous enclosures. The numerical treatment of DFF of propylene glycol with CNTs under the effects of activation energy and MHD is examined by Shahbaz and Ahmad [11]. Shahbaz et al. [12] investigated at the new proposal of recurrent neural networks to analyses the heat transfers of radiative Casson nanofluid flow formed of CNTs over a surface. The intelligent predictive networks for magnetohydrodynamics nanofluid containing thermal conductivity and CNTs over a permeable medium were investigated by Shahbaz and Ahmad [13]. Shaaban et al. [14] used intelligent predictive neural network study to scrutinize the properties of Stefan blowing on the chemical reactive flow of Boger NF (nanofluid) with brownian motion and thermophoresis.

When there is a temperature differential, small, micron-scale particles floating in a gas will move from hotter to cooler areas, a phenomenon known as thermophoresis. Applications for this phenomenon are many and include the research of exhaust gas paths in combustion systems, the separation of tiny particles from gas streams, and the study of particle deposition on turbine blade surfaces. In systems with tiny particles subjected to wide temperature fluctuations, it also has a major impact on mass transfer processes. Because of its wide range of applications, thermophoresis has garnered a lot of interest in both science and engineering. Shaaban et al. [15] examined applications in sophisticated thermal management systems, including the optimization of thermophoresis in hybrid nanofluid using heat radiation and nanomaterial performance. The effects of activation energy and two viscosity models on synovial fluid are analyzed by Khan et al. [16] using TPD (thermophoretic particle deposition). Karthik et al. [17] inspected mass transportation examination of THNF (trihybrid nanofluid) flow via a wedge as affected by thermal radiation and thermophoretic deposition. The impact of combination of nanoparticles and deposition of thermophoretic particles on the flow of NF over the Riga wedge was analyze by Madhukesh et al. [18]. Mahariq et al. [19] examine the THNF with oxytactic microbes in a thermo-bioconvection flow. Pal and Chatterjee [20] examined effects of thermophoresis and Brownian motion in a composite material, magnetohydrodynamic dissipative occurs when small layers of chemically reactive nanoliquid radiate outward on an unstable expanding sheet. The two-phase model of micropolar NF flow across a surface using DFF and chemical reaction analysis was investigated by Nayak et al. [21]. The performance of magneto-viscoelastic fluid flow over a porous media with a heat generation were scrutinized by Nayak et al. [22] using a hybrid approach.

Two different modeling techniques for the fluid stage and the solid matrix are frequently utilized to characterize heat transport in permeable media. Assuming that both stages share the same thermal field, the first is the LTE (local thermal equilibrium) model. When there are discernible temperature variations between the fluid and solid stages, this assumption is refuted. The LTNE model, which considers the solid and fluid temperatures independently, is better suitable under these circumstances. The LTNE framework has gotten very little attention in porous medium heat transfer investigations, despite its higher level of physical realism. The outcome of triple adiabatic barriers within a permeable cavity in a model with LTNE and non-darcy flow was examine by Yousif et al. [23]. Alhadhrami et al. [24] reviewed the investigation of mass transportation in a chemically reacting non-Newtonian fluid flow under conditions of LTNE. Thaku et al. [25] scrutinized the subcritical dynamics and nonlinear stability of ferroconvection in porous media with pair stresses when there is thermal non-equilibrium. Bhargavi and Makinde [26] observed the numerical investigation of fluid-saturated porous-filled duct’s axial conduction utilizing a LTNE model. Galal et al. [27] explored tetra hybrid nanofluid MHD chemical reactive flow with velocity slip conditions: the impact of LTNE. Nayak et al. [28] examined the CCHFM on convection of micropolar NF with chemical reaction. Nayak et al. [29] study how heat loss and DFF inertial drag affect micropolar HNF flow via extending surface.

Because of their numerous scientific and technological uses, nanofluids have garnered a lot of interest lately. Beyond engineering systems, they are crucial in medicinal domains, such as allowing tailored cancer therapy using nanoscale drug delivery systems and detecting arterial blockages in blood flow using radioactive tracer methods. Additionally, waste treatment procedures make use of nanofluids, which promotes the sustainability of the environment and cleaner energy generation. Home refrigeration systems, fuel cells, chillers, heat exchangers, boiler exhaust temperature control, engine cooling and thermal management, microelectronic devices, and hybrid engines are only a few of the industrial and manufacturing industries’ uses for them. Nanofluids are still a primary focus of current research and development because of their wide range of useful applications. The examination of heat transportation properties in MHD hybrid nanofluids with varying thermal radiations and viscosities was inspect by Anwar et al. [30]. The radiative flux of an HNF was scrutinized by Abbas et al. [31] using the CCFM to inspect the impact of elastic deformation. Areshi et al. [32] used an expanding sheet to investigate the effects of NPs radius and interparticle spacing on the mixed convective flow of a water-based NF containing alumina nanoparticles in MHD. The outcomes of heat radiation on oxytactic germs in a HNF including SWCNTs and MWCNTs with Soret-Dufour effects are investigated by Abbas et al. [33]. Senbagaraja and De [34] examined the tangent hyperbolic nanofluid bioconvective EMHD statistical analysis with heat radiation and heat source. The Williamson blood flow in a permeable media with SDE and trihybrid nanofluid was scrutinized by Sudarmozhi et al. [35] using AI-based neural network model, demonstrating the strong capability of soft-computing techniques to predict complex nonlinear thermo-biological transport phenomena. Rehman et al. [36] extended advanced nanofluid modeling by examining thermocapillary, radiative, and MHD effects on unsteady couple-stress THNF flow over a surface, revealing notable improvement in heat transmission due to the combined physical mechanisms. A computational approach for fourth-grade aluminum-alloy NF flow over a Riga plate was developed by Ahmad et al. [37], emphasising the impact of electromagnetic forcing and non-Newtonian behaviour on thermal performance. Going on to engineering applications, Kapustenko et al. [38] examined heat transmission increase in small heat exchangers with various channel shapes and discovered that thermal efficiency is greatly increased by geometric design. In the context of energy storage, Kovács et al. [39] analyzed radiative and convective heat transmission in lithium-ion battery cells, addressing key thermal safety and management issues. A few other interesting studies are given in [40–44]. Ahmad et al. [45] formulated a computational model to investigate fourth-grade nanofluid flow over an Riga plate with aluminum alloy nanoparticles, demonstrating that electromagnetic effects markedly enhance thermal performance. Szűcs [46] utilized a combined CFD–FEM approach to evaluate thermal characteristic length scales, providing a deeper understanding of convective heat transfer in complex systems. Orosz et al. [47] introduced a graph-theoretic method for designing heat exchanger networks with minimized utility requirements, enhancing energy recovery efficiency, while Morauszki et al. [48] conducted detailed simulations of coupled fluid flow, combustion, and heat transfer in internal combustion engines, highlighting key aspects of thermal control. Furthermore, Szűcs [49] analyzed compressibility effects in reconstructed porous media, revealing significant impacts on flow resistance and heat transfer behavior, and Orosz and Friedler [50] explored multiple optimal configurations for heat exchanger network design, supporting practical and energy-efficient industrial applications.

There are several real-world uses for the technique used to investigate LTNE effects in MHD CoO4/HFE-7100 nanofluid flow through a permeable media with heat production. The nonlinear link between governing parameters and thermal behavior is captured by a back-propagated neural network, which allows for more precise temperature and heat transfer characteristic prediction at a lower computing cost. Solution methodology

Artificial neural networks are computer models that mimic the structure of neurons in the human brain. They are often used in pattern recognition, classification, and function approximation. A typical ANN consists of an output layer, an input layer, and one or more hidden layers. The connected neurons, make up each connection and each layer is given a weight that represents the extent of the neurons’ effect on one another.

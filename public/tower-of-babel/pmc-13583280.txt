Unravelling Ferroptosis in Sepsis: A Comprehensive Integrative Transcriptomic Analysis Across Age and Disease Severity
Authors: Miguel Victor Bringel Sales, Helder Imoto Nakaya, Fernando de Queiroz Cunha, Antonio Edson Rocha Oliveira
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Ferroptosis, an iron-dependent, regulated form of cell death, has been increasingly recognised as a pivotal driver of organ dysfunction and mortality in sepsis. Despite its importance, the transcriptomic regulation of ferroptosis in human sepsis has not been systematically characterised. Here, we present a comprehensive integrative analysis, combining 17 transcriptomic data sets from previously published studies involving blood samples from septic patients and healthy controls, encompassing microarray, bulk RNA sequencing, and single-cell RNA sequencing platforms. Our findings reveal that ferroptosis-related gene regulation intensifies with disease progression and is markedly more pronounced in adults. Single-cell analysis identified monocytes as the primary regulators of ferroptosis in peripheral blood during sepsis. Eleven core ferroptosis-related genes (ACSL1, ALOX5, CAPG, CREB5, MAPK14, MGST1, MICU1, QSOX1, SAT1, SIAH2, and SLC2A3) were consistently upregulated, serving as robust candidates for sepsis biomarkers, while additional genes correlated with disease severity, suggesting prognostic value. This large-scale integrative approach provides novel molecular insights into sepsis pathophysiology and identifies promising targets for improved diagnosis and therapy.

Introduction

Sepsis (Sp) remains a leading cause of mortality and morbidity worldwide, driven by a dysregulated host response to infection that leads to acute organ dysfunction and a high risk of death.1,2 Despite advances in supportive care, early diagnosis and effective targeted therapies for Sp are still lacking, in part due to the complex and heterogeneous nature of its pathophysiology. 3 Recent research has identified ferroptosis, a regulated, iron-dependent form of cell death characterised by lipid peroxidation and membrane damage, as a critical contributor to organ injury in Sp. Ferroptosis not only exacerbates tissue damage through oxidative stress and inflammation but also plays a central role in immune cell dysfunction, vascular permeability, and multi-organ failure, all hallmarks of severe Sp.4-8

The growing recognition of ferroptosis as a pivotal mechanism in Sp has sparked interest in targeting this pathway for therapeutic intervention. Inhibitors of ferroptosis have shown promise in preclinical models, ameliorating organ dysfunction and improving survival, while iron accumulation and ferroptosis-related gene (FerG) expression have emerged as potential indicators of Sp severity and prognosis.6,9,10 However, the transcriptomic landscape of ferroptosis in human Sp remains incompletely understood. Although previous studies have utilised transcriptomic data to identify differentially expressed ferroptosis-related genes (DEFerGs) and potential regulatory pathways, critical gaps remain in our understanding of how these expression profiles vary across the broader clinical spectrum of Sp, including diverse disease stages and age groups.11,12

Given the heterogeneity of Sp and the multifaceted roles of ferroptosis in its progression, a large-scale, integrative transcriptomic analysis is urgently needed. Such an approach can reveal robust, age- and severity-specific patterns of ferroptosis gene regulation, identify key cellular mediators, and uncover novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets relevant for clinical translation.

In this study, we present the most comprehensive transcriptomic characterisation of ferroptosis in Sp to date, integrating 17 publicly available data sets from blood samples from septic patients and healthy controls, spanning microarray, bulk RNA sequencing (bRNA-seq), and single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) platforms. By systematically analysing FerG expression across age groups, disease stages, and immune cell subsets, we aim to illuminate the molecular underpinnings of ferroptosis in Sp and provide a valuable resource for future diagnostic and therapeutic development.

Methods

Data Collection and Curation

We systematically curated publicly available transcriptomic data sets related to Sp from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) 13 and Single Cell Portal 14 repositories. Inclusion criteria required data sets to contain at least 3 replicates of either whole blood or peripheral blood mononuclear cell (PBMC) samples from septic patients and healthy controls. Selected data sets encompassed 3 platforms (microarray, bRNA-seq, and scRNA-seq), 2 age groups (children and adults), and 3 disease progression stages: systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS), Sp, and septic shock SS (Supplemental Figure S1 and Table S1).

Microarray Data Processing and Analysis

Raw expression values and metadata were retrieved using GEOquery. 15 Data normalisation and background correction were performed with affy 16 and limma 17 R packages. Outlier detection was conducted using arrayQualityMetrics, 18 excluding samples flagged by at least 2 criteria. For genes represented by multiple probes, the probe with the highest expression was retained. Differential expression analysis was performed using limma. 17 To integrate results across age groups and disease stages, a meta-analysis was conducted using MetaVolcanoR, 19 applying the Fisher method to identify genes with consistent transcriptional profiles (Supplemental Figure S2A).

Bulk RNA-Seq Data Processing and Analysis

Raw FASTQ files were downloaded using SRAToolkit 20 and trimmed with Trimmomatic v0.36 21 with the following settings: TRAILING:25 LEADING:25 SLIDINGWINDOW:5:25 MINLEN:half of the raw read length. Sequencing quality was assessed with FastQC. 22 Reads were aligned to the human genome (GRCh38.110) and quantified using STAR 23 with the “-quantMode GeneCounts” option. Genes with low expression were filtered out using the filterByExpr function in edgeR. 24 Normalisation was performed with the Trimmed Mean of M-values (TMM) method in edgeR. 24 Principal component analysis was used to assess sample reproducibility. Differential expression analysis was conducted in edgeR 24 (Supplemental Figure S2B).

Single-Cell RNA-Seq Data Processing and Analysis

Raw count matrices were obtained from the Single Cell Portal. 14 Quality control, background correction, normalisation, scaling, and clustering were performed using Seurat. 25 Data integration across samples was achieved with Harmony. 26 Differential expression analysis was also conducted in Seurat 25 (Supplemental Figure S2C).

Identification of Ferroptosis-Related Genes and Functional Enrichment Analysis

All differential expression results generated from microarray, bRNA-seq, and scRNA-seq analyses were filtered against a curated list of 471 FerGs obtained from FerrDb. 27 These genes were categorised by their regulatory roles as drivers (promoting ferroptosis), suppressors (inhibiting ferroptosis), or markers (indicating ferroptosis occurrence).

To establish the final set of DEFerGs, we applied stringent criteria: genes were considered significant if they were regulated in the meta-analysis of microarray comparisons (involving either adults or children) or showed differential expression in at least 2 bRNA-seq comparisons. For scRNA-seq data, genes meeting the differential expression criteria in any cell type were included. This comprehensive approach ensured the identification of robust ferroptosis-related signatures across multiple platforms and experimental conditions.

To explore the broader biological context of the identified DEFerGs, Gene Ontology and pathway enrichment analyses were performed using Enrichr. 28 The analysis was conducted using the DEFerGs identified from the microarray meta-analysis as input to determine which biological processes, molecular functions, and signalling pathways were significantly associated with ferroptosis regulation in Sp. This functional characterisation allowed us to understand the mechanistic connections between ferroptosis and other key processes in Sp pathophysiology, providing insights into potential therapeutic targets and biomarker pathways beyond ferroptosis itself.

Diagnostic Validation of Biomarker Candidates

To evaluate the diagnostic performance of the selected FerG biomarkers, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed. The ability of each biomarker to discriminate between healthy controls and patients across distinct disease progression stages (SIRS, Sp, and SS) was assessed independently for the paediatric and adult cohorts. The analysis utilised control-anchored Z-scores derived from the normalised microarray expression matrices. The area under the curve (AUC) and its 95% confidence intervals were calculated using the pROC package. 29 The ROC analysis was parameterised to evaluate higher expression levels as the predictor of the disease state. Validation visualisations and significance annotations were generated by integrating the adjusted P-values obtained from the previous meta-analysis.

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.3.3. For microarray data, differential expression was assessed using the empirical Bayes method in limma 17 with Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) correction. Genes were considered significant with adjusted P-value < .05 and |log2(fold change)| > 0.6. Meta-analysis across studies was performed using the Fisher method in MetaVolcanoR 19 to obtain the combined log2(fold change) and P-values, applying the aforementioned thresholds.

For bRNA-seq, differential expression was performed using the likelihood ratio test in edgeR 24 following TMM normalisation, with significance thresholds of adjusted P-value < .05 and |log2(fold change)| > 1.0. The scRNA-seq analysis used the Wilcoxon rank-sum test in Seurat 25 with the Bonferroni correction, requiring an adjusted P-value < .05, |log2(fold change)| > 0.6, and the proportion of cells expressing the gene in group 1 greater than 0.1.

Functional enrichment analysis was conducted using the hypergeometric test in Enrichr 28 with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction and a significance threshold of adjusted P-value < .05. Sample sizes varied by data set as detailed in Supplemental Table S1.

For the biomarker validation analysis, statistical significance of differential expression between disease stages and healthy controls was annotated based on the adjusted P-values from the microarray meta-analysis, with significance levels defined as *P < .05, **P < .01, ***P < .001, and ****P < .0001.

Results

Microarray analysis reveals differences in ferroptosis regulation based on age group and progression stage, as well as potential biomarkers.

From the curation of 14 microarray data sets, 33 comparisons were made between samples from adults or children at different stages of disease progression (SIRS, Sp, and SS) and healthy control samples (Supplemental Table S1). These comparisons revealed an average of 74 DEFerGs. Comparisons involving children with SIRS yielded an average of 38 DEFerGs. During the Sp stage, the averages for children and adults were 55 and 79 DEFerGs, while in the SS stage, they were 79 and 83 DEFerGs, respectively. These findings suggest that the regulation of ferroptosis becomes increasingly pronounced as the disease progresses and is more prominent in adults than in children (Supplemental Table S2).

A meta-analysis was conducted to consolidate findings on how ferroptosis is regulated across the comparisons, identifying a total of 93 DEFerGs (Supplemental Tables S3 and S4). Specifically, 52, 45, and 75 DEFerGs were observed in children during the SIRS, Sp, and SS stages, respectively. In the adult cohort, 80 and 76 DEFerGs were identified during Sp and SS, respectively. The representation of these findings in Figure 1 highlights the agreement among comparisons, reinforcing that ferroptosis is regulated across all stages of Sp in both age groups.

Furthermore, we identified 4 biomarkers whose expression increased as the disease progressed in at least 1 age group (Supplemental Tables S3 and S4). These include 2 enzymes of the pentose phosphate pathway, G6PD and PGD, which are crucial in maintaining cellular redox balance and may play a protective role during oxidative stress30,31; an oxidoreductase involved in post-translational protein modification, QSOX1, which promotes ferroptosis by inhibiting NRF2 32 ; and a transcription factor involved in apoptosis and cell differentiation, JDP2, which mediates interactions within the AhR-Nrf2 battery to sustain redox homeostasis. 33 These biomarkers show potential as prognostic indicators of disease severity.

To validate their prognostic potential, we assessed their diagnostic performance using control-anchored Z-scores. These scores showed a consistent increase in median expression levels across disease stages (P < .001) for most biomarkers, with the exception of G6PD in the adult cohort (Figure 4A and B). The ROC curve analysis demonstrated robust discriminative capacity across disease stages (Figure 4C to G; Supplemental Table S5). Notably, PGD and QSOX1 demonstrated accuracy in identifying SS in both children (AUC = 0.986 and 0.944, respectively) and adults (AUC = 0.985 and 0.977, respectively). JDP2 also showed predictive value for SS in both cohorts (AUC > 0.920). Conversely, G6PD demonstrated lower discriminative power, particularly during SS in adults (AUC = 0.730). Even at the earliest clinical manifestation evaluated (SIRS in children), these biomarkers maintained predictive value, with PGD reaching an AUC of 0.948. These combined findings underscore their potential as indicators of disease severity and as early prognostic markers for ferroptosis-driven progression in Sp.

A pathway enrichment analysis was performed to understand the biological functions of the DEFerGs beyond ferroptosis, revealing that in addition to ferroptosis-related pathways (oxidative stress, fatty acid metabolism, iron homeostasis, and hypoxia), others were also enriched, such as immune response pathways (neutrophil extracellular trap formation, interleukin (IL)-17, IL-18, IL-4, and IL-13 signalling pathways); transcription factors (p53 and forkhead box O [FoxO]); and cellular response to oxidative stress (cellular senescence and autophagy) (Figure 5 and Supplemental Table S6).

Pathway enrichment analyses revealed key molecular mechanisms in Sp, with previous studies identifying mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) and nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) signalling in septic mice, 12 as well as pathways related to ferroptosis, apoptosis, PI3K-Akt, NF-κB, oxidative stress, inflammatory responses, and coagulation.34,35 Our analysis corroborated these findings and expanded them by identifying additional enriched pathways, such as transcription factor networks like FoxO and p53, which have received less attention in previous studies. Notably, the MAPK signalling pathway, linked to Sp-induced cardiac injury,11,36 aligns with the MAPK14 upregulation seen in our study.

Bulk RNA sequencing analysis corroborates the microarray findings and reveals additional regulated FerGs. To expand upon the microarray findings, 2 additional bRNA-seq data sets comprising septic and healthy adult samples were analysed. Three comparisons between Sp or SS patients and healthy controls identified an average of 100 and 114 DEFerGs, respectively (Supplemental Table S7).

Integrating bRNA-seq data with microarray findings resulted in a total of 129 DEFerGs – an increase from the 93 DEFerGs identified in microarray analyses alone (Supplemental Table S4). FerGs were considered differentially expressed if they were regulated in at least 2 comparisons. Of the 129 DEFerGs, 60 were shared across both technologies, while 36 DEFerGs were exclusively regulated in bRNA-seq comparisons (Figure 2D and Supplemental Table S4). Moreover, most DEFerGs identified in the microarray analysis retained their transcriptional profiles, while additional DEFerGs, not detectable in microarray comparisons, were revealed due to the higher sensitivity of bRNA-seq, which uncovered more consistent transcriptional clusters (Figure 3B). In addition, most of these genes are classified as ferroptosis drivers or suppressors, according to the FerrDb V2 database 27 (Supplemental Figure S3).

Single-cell RNA sequencing analysis reveals how ferroptosis is regulated at the cellular level and highlights potential ferroptosis biomarkers. A scRNA-seq data set of PBMCs from septic patients and healthy controls was analysed to explore cell-type-specific ferroptosis regulation. Five cell subtypes (monocytes, dendritic cells [DCs], natural killer [NK] cells, T cells, and B cells) were identified (Figure 6A). Ten comparisons were made between septic groups (Bac-SEP and ICU-SEP, individuals with bacteraemic Sp admitted to hospital wards and intensive care units, respectively) and healthy controls. The analysis revealed that monocytes exhibited the highest number of DEFerGs, followed by T cells, DCs, NK cells, and B cells, with averages of 37, 33, 29, 21, and 17 upregulated DEFerGs, respectively. No significant downregulation of DEFerGs was observed in most cell types (Supplemental Table S8).

Integrating scRNA-seq with bRNA-seq and microarray findings revealed that 48 DEFerGs were commonly regulated across scRNA-seq and at least one other technology, while 20 and 41 DEFerGs were uniquely regulated in Bac-SEP and ICU-SEP, respectively (Supplemental Table S4). The greater regulation of ferroptosis in ICU-SEP, compared to Bac-SEP, aligns with the more severe condition of patients in the ICU. To compare scRNA-seq results with those from microarray and bRNA-seq, scRNA-seq data were grouped according to the experimental condition (Bac-SEP or ICU-SEP). A gene was considered differentially expressed if it was regulated in at least one of the cell types within the respective group. Notably, 11 DEFerGs were found to be upregulated across all technologies: ACSL1, ALOX5, CAPG, CREB5, MAPK14, MGST1, MICU1, QSOX1, SAT1, SIAH2, and SLC2A3 (Figures 2E and 3C and Supplemental Table S4).

These genes play diverse roles in ferroptosis regulation, balancing pro- and anti-ferroptotic mechanisms. For instance, acting as a regulatory node, MAPK14 not only modulates FoxO cascades, p53 activation, and CREB5 transcription through MSK137-41 but also phosphorylates and stabilises the stress-responsive E3 ubiquitin ligase SIAH2. 42 In turn, this stabilised SIAH2 integrates hypoxic and oxidative signals to exert an anti-ferroptotic effect by driving the targeted proteasomal degradation of pro-ferroptotic factors, including the lipid-remodelling enzyme ACSL4 and the iron-liberating enzyme HO-1,43,44 while simultaneously modulating cellular redox state through the Keap1-independent degradation of NRF2. 45 Genes that promote reactive oxygen species (ROS) production include MICU1, which is required for mitochondrial calcium uptake, amplifying ROS generation by mitochondria 46 ; QSOX1, an oxidoreductase that induces oxidative stress and downregulates protective NRF2 pathways32,47,48; and SAT1, which contributes to oxidative stress and p53-mediated apoptosis through polyamine catabolism. 49 Key genes enhancing lipid peroxidation include ACSL1, a pivotal enzyme in lipid metabolism that facilitates the incorporation of α-eleostearic acid into neutral lipids 50 ; and ALOX5, an iron-containing lipoxygenase that catalyses the formation of lipid hydroperoxides essential for ferroptosis, the silencing of which confers resistance to this pathway.51-53 Conversely, genes mitigating ROS effects include SLC2A3, which supports glycolysis and antioxidant transport54,55; MGST1, a membrane-localised glutathione-dependent enzyme that offers protection against oxidative stress 56 ; CREB5, which regulates the transcription of survival and metabolic genes, including GPX4, a key inhibitor of ferroptosis57,58; and CAPG, which is linked to cytoskeletal dynamics and immune cell motility and, although its exact mechanisms remain unclear, appears to modulate ferroptosis through redox-sensitive mechanisms.59,60 An overview of the role of these FerGs is represented in Figure 7.

In Figure 3C, DEFerGs from the previously observed upregulated cluster in bRNA-seq retained their transcriptional profile across most of the cell types. However, this was not observed for the downregulated cluster, which exhibited the opposite transcriptional profile in scRNA-seq comparisons. This discrepancy may be attributed to the absence of certain cell subtypes in the PBMC samples from the analysed scRNA-seq data, which could be responsible for mediating the downregulation in whole blood samples. To verify this, an additional bRNA-seq data set containing purified neutrophils was analysed, revealing downregulation of a portion of these DEFerGs (Figure 3D).

To understand how the most prominent FerGs are expressed in each cell type, their expression values were visualised in dot plots. Regarding the top 3 DEFerGs for each cell type, monocytes and DCs accounted for most of the regulation (Figure 6B). When focusing on FerGs that were differentially expressed in over 90% of microarray comparisons, monocytes were found to regulate most of these genes (Figure 6C). Moreover, regarding the 11 FerGs that were differentially expressed across all technologies, most were regulated in monocytes (Figure 6D). While not all these DEFerGs were expressed in a high percentage of cells, nearly all exhibited significant differential expression in monocytes, suggesting that monocytes play a major role in ferroptosis regulation.

Discussion

Ferroptosis has emerged as a key mechanism contributing to organ dysfunction in Sp. 61 Through an integrative transcriptomic analysis of 17 data sets across multiple platforms, we identified a robust core signature of 11 consistently upregulated FerGs, alongside an additional 14 genes strongly supported by microarray disease progression comparisons. This critical network spans pro-oxidant, antioxidant, and lipid metabolic functions, revealing underexplored mechanisms in Sp such as the FoxO and p53 pathways, alongside the established MAPK signalling cascade, which aligns with the observed upregulation of MAPK14.11,12,34-36 We observed that FerG regulation intensifies with disease severity and is significantly heightened in adult patients, mirroring the known clinical vulnerability driven by immunosenescence and comorbidities.62-64 By confirming this signature across diverse platforms, our study provides a robust framework for mechanistic deconstruction and clinical translation.

At the cellular level, our scRNA-seq analysis places circulating monocytes at the epicentre of this ferroptotic cascade, exhibiting the highest FerG expression among immune subpopulations. This aligns with evidence that monocyte-to-macrophage differentiation inherently enhances ferroptotic vulnerability.65,66 In these cells, transcriptomic data reflect a biochemical balance between competing pathways. Early survival mechanisms, such as SLC2A3-supported glycolysis, MGST1/CREB5-mediated antioxidant responses, and SIAH2-driven protection against differentiation arrest and lipid peroxidation, are eventually bypassed by systemic stress integrators.56-58,67,68 Specifically, MAPK14 activation engages nuclear p53, which suppresses antioxidant capacity and activates SAT1 to prime ALOX5- and ACSL1-driven lipid peroxidation. 37 ,49-51, 69 Coupled with MICU1-amplified mitochondrial calcium uptake and ROS production, this unmitigated lipid peroxidation leads to monocyte rupture and the systemic release of damage-associated molecular patterns, such as HMGB1.46,51,70 Clinically, this cascade parallels the transcriptomic shift from ward-admitted bacteraemia (Bac-SEP) to critical illness (ICU-SEP), marked by the expansion of immunosuppressive human leukocyte antigen-DR (HLA-DR)low Monocyte State 1 (MS1) monocytes71,72 and distal microvascular damage precipitating acute respiratory distress syndrome and acute kidney injury.69,73

Beyond the innate compartment, this systemic ferroptotic stress also impairs adaptive immunity. The upregulation of DEFerGs in DCs and T cells signifies a functional decline driven by lipid ROS prior to cell death. 74 In DCs, the accumulation of oxidised phospholipids and 4-hydroxynonenal induces endoplasmic reticulum stress, leading to the downregulation of major histocompatibility complex (MHC)-II and co-stimulatory receptors, which impairs antigen presentation.74-76 Simultaneously, CD8+ T cells upregulate the CD36 scavenger receptor, facilitating the influx of oxidised lipids, which activates the p38 MAPK signalling pathway; this metabolic stress suppresses effector cytokine production and drives the expression of exhaustion markers such as programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) and T-cell immunoglobulin and mucin-domain containing protein 3 (TIM-3).74,77,78

This core FerG signature identifies molecular events that may assist in clinical risk stratification and therapeutic targeting. Our validation indicates that PGD and QSOX1 maintain high discriminative capacity for identifying SS across both paediatric and adult cohorts. Notably, the predictive value of PGD was observed during early clinical manifestations (SIRS in children), suggesting its potential as an early prognostic indicator prior to severe organ dysfunction. In contrast, the discriminative power of G6PD decreased during adult SS, potentially due to age-specific metabolic exhaustion or the shifting role of the pentose phosphate pathway in late-stage Sp.63,79-81

Several core genes within the identified signature represent potential targets for therapeutic intervention. Preclinical evidence has demonstrated that inhibiting ALOX5 (via zileuton), QSOX1 (via ebselen), or ACSL1 (via triacsin C) can reduce inflammation and tissue damage in septic and neuroinflammatory models.82-87 Similarly, modulating macrophage ferroptosis with metabolites such as itaconate has been shown to attenuate Sp-induced lung injury. 88 Monitoring these signatures, particularly PGD and QSOX1, could therefore support earlier risk stratification and inform the timing of clinical interventions.

One potential limitation of our study is the reliance on disease stage classifications provided by the original authors, which may have led to mislabelling, especially in cases ambiguously defined as “sepsis.” Furthermore, the limited availability of adult SIRS data sets hindered our characterisation of early transcriptional shifts in these populations. The integration of public data sets introduces inherent heterogeneity, which may have limited the number of DEFerGs detected. In addition, our analysis was confined to blood samples, which may not fully capture ferroptosis regulation in other clinically relevant tissues such as pulmonary, hepatic, or renal tissues, where organ dysfunction primarily occurs in Sp. Finally, the absence of corresponding preclinical validation data from animal models limits our ability to evaluate the therapeutic potential of the identified targets.

Conclusion

This comprehensive transcriptomic analysis establishes ferroptosis as a dynamic, severity-linked mechanism in the pathophysiology of human Sp. By integrating data across diverse platforms and clinical stages, we identified a robust 11-gene signature and revealed that circulating monocytes are the primary cellular mediators of this process. These findings directly bridge molecular mechanisms with clinical utility, establishing that specific genes, notably PGD and QSOX1, possess high discriminative capacity as early prognostic biomarkers for SS. Furthermore, key enzymes within this signature, such as ALOX5, QSOX1, and ACSL1, represent actionable targets with existing pharmacological inhibitors, highlighting their potential to be repurposed for Sp therapy. Ultimately, this framework advances our understanding of Sp-induced immune and organ dysfunction, providing a data-driven foundation for precision diagnostics and targeted host-directed therapies.

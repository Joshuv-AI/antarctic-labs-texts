Protocol to clone unknown flanking genomic region using SWPOP-PCR for genome walking
Authors: Qingchun Tang, Kunpeng Chang, Mou Li, Haixing Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryGenome walking is a molecular technique for mining unknown flanking DNAs, significantly advancing fields related to biology. We here detail a genome-walking protocol for stepwise partially overlapping primer-based PCR (SWPOP-PCR). We specify the rules for designing walking primers and gene-specific primers. We also delineate the three-stage workflow of SWPOP-PCR for cloning target DNA, as well as the subsequent purification, sequencing, and analysis of target amplicon.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Chang et al.1Subject areas: Genomics, Sequencing, Molecular Biology, Biotechnology and bioengineering

Before you begin

Genome walking has often been applied to clone unknown flanking genomic regions of known DNAs, thus contributing to the development of life science.2,3 Compared to a time-consuming and laborious restriction-ligation-based PCR,4,5,6,7 a random PCR is much more popular,8,9 as it omits the prior restriction and ligation steps to PCR amplification.10,11,12,13,14 Over the past four decades, many random PCRs, such as uracil walking primer PCR,15 wristwatch PCR,16 thermal asymmetric interlaced PCR,17 and N7-ended walker PCR,18 have been built for conducting genome walking. However, the complex experimental workflow or non-target background still challenges the practicability of these PCRs.19,20,21,22

Herein, we propose a genome-walking protocol according to stepwise partially overlapping primer-based PCR (SWPOP-PCR). For a set of three SWPOPs, the last 10 nt 3′ of a SWPOP overlaps the first 10 5′ nt of its predecessor. This implies that SWPOP can only anneal to its predecessor site at a sufficiently low temperature. Clearly, the non-target product defined by the predecessor SWPOP, which is the real contributor of non-target background, cannot be amplified by the SWPOP in high-temperature cycles; nevertheless, the target product can still be exponentially amplified as it has a perfect binding site for the gene-specific primer (GSP). The efficacy of SWPOP-PCR has been validated by acquiring flanks of two genes.

Innovation

This SWPOP-PCR protocol presents a step-by-step workflow for accurately and conveniently cloning unknown flanking genomic regions. This protocol relies on a set of three SWPOPs (primary SWPOP, secondary SWPOP, and tertiary SWPOP), in which the 10 nt 3′ part of a SWPOP overlaps the 10 nt 5′ part of its predecessor. This means n primary SWPOPs can share one secondary SWPOP and one tertiary SWPOP. In contrast, a set of three traditional POPs (partially overlapping primers) partially overlap each other at the 3′ end. As a result, in a genome-walking practice, n parallel POP-PCR sets need n × 3 walking primers, while n SWPOP-PCR sets only require n + 2 walking primers. This SWPOP design not only improves PCR amplification specificity, but facilitates making mix master for secondary/tertiary PCR.

Workflow of SWPOP-PCR

The primary 25°C annealing cycle enables pSWPOP to partially anneal to many sites in genomic regions, synthesizing many single-stranded DNAs (ssDNA). The target ssDNA other than non-target ones is exponentially amplified in the following 65°C cycles, because only this target has the binding site for pGSP. However, three groups of non-target DNA (group I, primed by pGSP; group II, primed by pGSP and pSWPOP; and group III, primed by pSWPOP) are still expected to produce. The secondary/tertiary 50°C cycle allows sSWPOP/tSWPOP to bind to the prior SWPOP site, thus producing target ssDNA and non-target ssDNAs. The target ssDNA is exponentially amplified in the following 65°C cycles; any non-target ssDNAs, however, cannot be further amplified because they lack a binding site for any primers. Expected outcomes

In this study, a SWPOP-PCR-based genome-walking protocol is detailed. We used this protocol to clone the lateral regions of gadA and hyg. The outcomes are summarized in Figure 2. As shown, all the secondary/tertiary SWPOP-PCRs produced one to three discrete DNA band. The sequencing results demonstrated that these DNA bands are correct, indicating the accuracy and high success rate of this protocol. The results also implied that tertiary SWPOP-PCR are generally not required, as secondary SWPOP-PCR suffices to produce a positive outcome.

Each walking includes four SWPOP-PCR sets, denoted as I, II, III, and IV, respectively. Each SWPOP-PCR set comprises primary (P), secondary (S), and tertiary (T) amplifications. The success of SWPOP-PCR is ensured by the primary 25°C cycle. In general, pSWPOP can partially anneal to some place(s) on unknown flanking region at 25°C, and then extend toward know region to synthesize a target DNA. Once such a target DNA is synthesized, SWPOP-PCR can generally succeed. The accuracy of SWPOP-PCR is mainly ensured by a specially designed SWPOP set, but it is also related to the fact that only one low-temperature annealing cycle is performed in each round of PCR. Although a SWPOP can anneal to its predecessor site in non-target DNA in the low-temperature cycle, and thus synthesize a DNA strand. This strand, however, cannot be further amplify by the subsequent high-temperature cycles, because it lacks a complementary site for any primers.19,28,29,30

Limitations

Like other random PCRs,31,32,33 SWPOP-PCR is also affected by multi-band phenomenon. There are two major reasons causing this phenomenon. The first reason is that the primary 25°C cycle promotes pSWPOP to partially bind with multiple sites on flanking region, thus synthesize multiple target bands. And the second reason is that, in the secondary/tertiary low-temperature cycle, sSWPOP/tSWPOP may partially anneal within target DNA, thus synthesize a shorter target band(s).

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

There appears no clear band(s) even after tertiary SWPOP-PCR (related to Step 3).

Potential solution

In this case, we suggest to increase 5–10 cycles for each PCR round. If there appears no clear band(s) yet, reselect a GSP set or a SWPOP set.

Problem 2

Non-target background is severe (related to Steps 1 and 2).

Potential solution

Dilute primary/secondary SWPOP-PCR product 100–10000 folds. Then the dilutions are used as templates in the next SWPOP-PCRs, respectively.

Problem 3

The multi-band phenomenon is severe. For example, five or more bands appear in secondary/tertiary SWPOP-PCR (related to Step 2).

Potential solution

Dilute primary/secondary SWPOP-PCR product 100–10000 folds, and then use the dilutions as templates in the next SWPOP-PCRs; or appropriately reduce amplification cycles for each PCR round.

Problem 4

Directly sequencing SWPOP-PCR product is challenging (related to Step 6).

Potential solution

Perform sequencing after T-cloning.

Problem 5

Secondary/tertiary SWPOP-PCR product is non-target (related to Step 2).

Potential solution

Reselect a GSP set; meanwhile, ensure that all the materials are free from contamination, and conduct PCR in a separate room.

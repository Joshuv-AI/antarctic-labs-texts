Stochastic Euler Schemes and Dissipative Evolutions in the Space of Probability Measures
Giulia Cavagnari, Giuseppe Savaré, Giacomo Enrico Sodini

Abstract
. We study the convergence of stochastic time-discretization schemes for evolution equations driven by random velocity fields, including examples like stochastic gradient descent and interacting particle systems. Using a unified framework based on Multivalued Probability Vector Fields, we analyze these dynamics at the level of probability measures in the Wasserstein space. Under suitable dissipativity and boundedness conditions, we prove that the laws of the interpolated trajectories converge to those of a limiting evolution governed by a maximal dissipative extension of the associated barycentric field. This provides a general measure-theoretic study for the convergence of stochastic schemes in continuous time.

1.
Introduction

The aim of this paper is to introduce and develop a general framework for studying the convergence of trajectories generated by a suitable stochastic version of the explicit Euler method, which we use
to approximate the evolution of interacting particle systems governed by dissipative fields in Hilbert spaces.

Superposition of autonomous vector fields.

In order to keep the exposition simple, we first explain the main ideas of our approach in the case of a simple system of ordinary differential equations in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
driven by
a continuous
vector field
b:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(1.1)

\dot{x}(t)=b(x(t)),\quad t\in[0,T],\quad x(0)=\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

satisfying, for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, a one-sided Lipschitz condition

(1.2)

\langle b(x_{0})-b(x_{1}),x_{0}-x_{1}\rangle\leq\lambda|x_{0}-x_{1}|^{2}\quad\text{for every }x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We suppose that
b
arises as a
stochastic
superposition of
a family of vector fields
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathsf{U}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

depending on
a random parameter
u\in\mathsf{U}
in
a probability space
\mathsf{U}
endowed with
a probability measure
\mathbb{U}
:

b(x)=\int_{\mathsf{U}}g(x,u)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{U}(u)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{U}}[g(x,\cdot)]\quad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

with

\int_{\mathsf{U}}|g(x,u)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{U}(u)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{U}}\Big[|g(x,\cdot)|^{2}\Big]\leq L(1+|x|^{2}).

We can perform a natural time discretization of (
1.1
) by
selecting a sequence of uniform step sizes
\tau=T/N
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and a sequence

(V^{n})_{n}

of independent,
\mathsf{U}
-valued random variables
with law
\mathbb{U}
,
defined in some probability space

(\Omega,\mathbb{P})
. A stochastic approximation
X^{n}_{\tau}
of

x(n\tau)

is a family of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued random variables defined on
\Omega
, which
can then be obtained by the
following version of the explicit Euler method

(1.3)

X^{n+1}_{\tau}=X^{n}_{\tau}+\tau g(X^{n}_{\tau},V^{n}),\quad n=0,1,\cdots,N;\quad X^{0}_{\tau}:=\bar{x}.

A special case of the above framework occurs when

(1.4)

b=-\nabla h,\quad h(x)=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{u=1}^{K}H_{u}(x)

is the sum of gradients of functions
H_{u}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
.
In this case, a deterministic approach to approximate (
1.1
) may be difficult to implement numerically when
K
is large. For this reason, stochastic approximation techniques have been developed, inspired by the seminal works of Robbins and Monro
[
20
]
and of Kiefer and Wolfowitz
[
14
]
, and they have witnessed a renewed interest, largely motivated by their growing relevance in machine learning contexts (cf. e.g.
[
21
,
7
,
16
]
).
In this regard,
the simplest stochastic formulation takes
\mathsf{U}=\{1,\dots,K\}
,
\mathbb{U}
to be the uniform distribution on
\mathsf{U}
, and
g(x,u):=-\nabla H_{u}(x)
; the scheme above in (
1.3
) with
\tau
fixed
coincides then with the classical Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) method
and one aims to pass to the limit as
n\to\infty
in order to find a minimum
(or a stationary point) of
h
.
The effectiveness of this approach relies on the reduction of the computational cost at each iteration.

Instead of
the asymptotic limit as
n\to\infty
,
we want to study the uniform approximation
in
[0,T]

of the deterministic trajectory of
(
1.1
) as
\tau=T/N
vanishes.

Interacting particle systems

In our analysis, we can include much more general
evolution processes, as the ones involving interactions.
In the simplest case,
we may consider a system of
M
particles
\{x^{\omega}\}_{\omega=1}^{M}
and an
interaction field
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
determining the velocity of each particle based on the positions of all particles in the system according to

(1.5)

\dot{x}^{\omega}(t)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}f(x^{\omega}(t),x^{m}(t)),\quad x^{\omega}(0)=\bar{x}^{\omega},\quad\omega=1,\cdots,M,\quad t\in[0,T].

More generally, we can assume that the initial configuration of the particles is described by the law of a random variable
\bar{X}
:
in this case, we can formulate the evolution of
the system described by the random variables
X(t,\cdot)

as

(1.6)

\dot{X}(t,\omega)=\int_{\Omega}f\big(X(t,\omega),X(t,\omega^{\prime})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega^{\prime}),\quad X(0,\omega)=\bar{X}(\omega)\quad\text{for every }\omega\in\Omega.

We can then consider the approximation scheme

(1.7)

X^{n+1}_{\tau}=X^{n}_{\tau}+\tau f(X^{n}_{\tau},Y^{n}_{\tau}),\quad n=0,1,\cdots,N,

where
Y^{n}_{\tau}
is an independent copy of
X^{n}_{\tau}
and it also independent
of
Y^{k}_{\tau}
,
k=0,\cdots,n-1
.

For both schemes (
1.3
) and (
1.7
), we can construct a piecewise linear (random) curve
X_{\tau}
on the interval
[0,T]
by linearly interpolating the values
(X^{n}_{\tau})_{n}
at the nodes
n\tau
. A natural question arises regarding the convergence of
X_{\tau}
as
\tau\downarrow 0
to the deterministic solution
of
(
1.1
)
(cf.
[
4
,
15
]
)
and to that of (
1.5
), (
1.6
).

Of course, many variations of the two examples
above can be considered—for example, allowing the field
g
to depend on the law of
X^{n}_{\tau}
or assuming that also the field
f
in the second case arises as a superposition. In fact, we interpret these evolutions as particular cases of a broader framework, which we use to analyze the behavior of
X_{\tau}
as
\tau\downarrow 0
.

The general idea, developed in this paper, is to regard these evolutions as occurring in the Wasserstein space of probability measures
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, focusing on the law of
X^{n}_{\tau}
rather than on the random variable itself. We lift the vector fields
b
,
g
, and
f
to corresponding Probability Vector Fields (PVF), a notion of distributed velocity field acting on probability measures, introduced in
[
18
,
17
,
10
]
and studied in our previous works
[
11
,
12
]
, also considering the multivalued case. For a comprehensive review of the literature on the study of evolution equations in the Wasserstein space of probability measures, particularly in relation to optimal control problems, we refer the reader to
[
19
]
.

Evolution of probability measures driven by probability vector fields

To illustrate this approach,
let us consider the general case of
the evolution of probability measures
with bounded support driven by a
continuous nonlocal vector field

\boldsymbol{b}:\mathsf{X}\times\mathcal{P}_{b}(\mathsf{X})\to\mathsf{X}
:
here
\mathsf{X}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
for simplicity,
(though we can also handle the case of an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space)
and
\mathcal{P}_{b}(\mathsf{X})
denotes the space
of probability measures with bounded support.

We assume that
\boldsymbol{b}
is
\lambda
-dissipative
[
11
]
, i.e.

(1.8)

\int\langle\boldsymbol{b}(x_{1},\mu_{1})-\boldsymbol{b}(x_{0},\mu_{0}),x_{1}-x_{0}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\mu}(x_{0},x_{1})\leq\lambda W_{2}^{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})

for every
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{b}(\mathsf{X})

and for some optimal coupling
\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\Gamma_{o}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
for the
L^{2}
-Wasserstein distance
W_{2}
,
a condition which is a natural metric-generalization to probability measures of the classical dissipativity (or anti-monotonicity) for operators in Hilbert spaces
[
8
]
.
By
[
12
, Theorem 4.2]

for every initial datum
\bar{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}_{b}(\mathsf{X})
we can find a unique solution

\mu\in\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathcal{P}_{b}(\mathsf{X}))

satisfying the continuity equation

(1.9)

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\nabla\cdot\big(\boldsymbol{b}(\cdot,\mu_{t})\mu_{t}\big)=0\quad\text{in }(0,T)\times\mathsf{X},\quad\mu_{t=0}=\bar{\mu}.

Selecting an initial random variable

\bar{X}
on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathbb{P})
such that
\bar{X}_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}=\bar{\mu}
(here
\sharp
is the push-forward operator),
the measures
\mu_{t}
,
t\in[0,T]
, can be equivalently characterized as the laws
of the random variables
X(t,\cdot)
satisfying the
systems of nonlocal ODEs

(1.10)

\dot{X}(t,\omega)=\boldsymbol{b}\big(X(t,\omega),X(t,\cdot)_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}\big),\quad X(0,\omega)=\bar{X}(\omega).

We can then consider the characteristic map

\mathrm{X}:\Omega\to\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathsf{X})
:
for (almost) every
\omega\in\Omega
,

\mathrm{X}(\omega)
is the curve

t\mapsto X(t,\omega)
i.e. a characteristic
of the PDE (
1.9
).
The law
\boldsymbol{\eta}:=\mathrm{X}_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}

is therefore a probability measure in

\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathsf{X}))

concentrated on the characteristics of
(
1.9
).

In order to describe the stochastic
approximation of
(
1.10
),
we suppose that
\boldsymbol{b}

can be represented as the barycenter
of a Probability Vector Field (PVF)

{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
, which in this particular case
is a map
from
\mathcal{P}_{b}(\mathsf{X})
to

\mathcal{P}_{b}(\mathsf{X}\times\mathsf{X})\cong\mathcal{P}_{b}(\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX})
(here
\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX}\cong\mathsf{X}\times\mathsf{X}
denotes the flat tangent bundle to
\mathsf{X}
, which can be interpreted as the natural space for
the space-velocity pairs
(x,v)
),
satisfying the structural condition

(1.11)

\pi^{\mathsf{X}}_{\sharp}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[\mu]=\mu\quad\text{for every $\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{b}(\mathsf{X})$,}

where
\pi^{\mathsf{X}}(x,v)=x
is the projection on
\mathsf{X}
.
The disintegration
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[\mu]=\int\Phi_{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

of
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[\mu]
with respect to its first marginal
\mu

yields a Borel family
(\Phi_{x})_{x\in\mathsf{X}}
of probability distributions on
velocities
\Phi_{x}\in\mathcal{P}_{b}(\mathsf{X})

at
\mu
-a.e.
x
, whose mean value is precisely
\boldsymbol{b}(x,\mu):

(1.12)

\boldsymbol{b}(x,\mu)=\int v\,\mathrm{d}\Phi_{x}(v)\quad\text{for $\mu$-a.e. $x\in\mathsf{X}$}.

In the simple case (
1.1
)
we have

(1.13)

\boldsymbol{b}(x,\mu)=b(x),\quad{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[\mu]=(\pi^{\mathsf{X}},g)_{\sharp}(\mu\otimes\mathbb{U}),\quad\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{b}(\mathsf{X}).

In the case of the interacting particle system
(
1.5
),
we have

\boldsymbol{b}(x,\mu)=\int f(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y),\quad{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[\mu]=(\boldsymbol{i}_{\mathsf{X}},f)_{\sharp}(\mu\otimes\mu),

being
i_{\mathsf{X}}
is the identity on
\mathsf{X}
.
Assuming that
there exists a constant
a\geq 0
such that

(1.14)

\langle v,x\rangle\leq a(1+|x|^{2})\quad\text{for ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[\mu]$-a.e.\penalty\ $(x,v)\in\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX}$,}

and for every
R>0

the support of
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[\mu]

is uniformly bounded whenever

\supp(\mu)\subset B_{R}(0),

the theory developed in
[
11
]
well applies to the evolution driven by the PVF
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
. The latter can be approximated by a measure-theoretic version of the Explicit Euler scheme: given
a step size
\tau=T/N

and an approximation
\bar{\mu}_{\tau}\in\mathcal{P}_{b}(\mathsf{X})
of
\bar{\mu}
, we iteratively define

(1.15)

M_{\tau}^{0}:=\bar{\mu}_{\tau},\quad M_{\tau}^{n+1}=\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}^{\tau}_{\sharp}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[M_{\tau}^{n}],\quad n=0,1,\cdots,N-1,

where
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}^{\tau}:\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX}\to\mathsf{X}
sends
(x,v)
to
x+\tau v
. This scheme corresponds to the scheme (
1.3
)
(resp. (
1.7
)) at the level of laws, indeed for each
n,\tau
we have that
M_{\tau}^{n}
is the law of the random variable
X^{n}_{\tau}
in (
1.3
) (resp. (
1.7
)).

In our earlier work
[
11
]
, we studied the properties of the scheme (
1.15
) and the uniform convergence of the corresponding piecewise-constant interpolants
t\mapsto M_{\tau}(t)
in the space of measures. In particular, we are also able to characterize the limit curve
\mu\in\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X}))
in terms of an Evolution Variational Inequality associated with
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
.

Comparing this result with the theory in
[
12
, Theorems 4.2, 4.4]
(cf. Theorem
5.4
), this immediately gives that

\mu

coincides with the (unique) solution of
(
1.9
)
generated by the
barycenter
\boldsymbol{b}
of
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
.

It is then natural to lift also the Explicit Euler scheme for
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
, in the form of its linear interpolation
M_{\tau}(\cdot)
, to a probability measure on curves, that we call
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\tau}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathsf{X}))
, such that its evaluation at times
n\tau
matches
M_{\tau}^{n}
(see Definition
3.4
for details).
The measure
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\tau}

can be interpreted as the law
of the piecewise linear interpolation
X_{\tau}
of
a
sequence
(X^{n}_{\tau})_{n}

of random variables
satisfying the following properties:

•

the law of
X^{n}_{\tau}
is
M^{n}_{\tau}
:

(X^{n}_{\tau})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}=M^{n}_{\tau}
,
n=0,\cdots,N
,
and
X^{0}_{\tau}\to\bar{X}
in
L^{2}

as
\tau\downarrow 0
,

•

(X^{n}_{\tau})_{n}
is
a Markov chain,

•

denoting by
V^{n}_{\tau}
the “forward” discrete velocity

\tau^{-1}(X^{n+1}_{\tau}-X^{n}_{\tau})
, the joint law
of
\big(X^{n}_{\tau},V^{n}_{\tau}\big)

is precisely
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[M^{n}_{\tau}]
.

We are therefore led to study the convergence of
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\tau}
, which corresponds to the convergence of the process
X_{\tau}
.
Our main result is that

\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\tau}
converges to
\boldsymbol{\eta}
in the strong
L^{2}
-Wasserstein topology on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathsf{X}))
(notice that, in this case, the convergence would not just be limited to the time evaluations, as already shown).

While compactness of the family
(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\tau})_{\tau}
in the weak topology of probability measures
follows from
[
11
]
with the dissipativity and boundedness conditions, characterizing the limit is one main contribution of this paper; see in particular Theorem
5.9
.
In this result we show that the convergence takes place actually in the strong
L^{2}
-Wasserstein topology on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathsf{X}))
and that the limit
\boldsymbol{\eta}
is concentrated precisely on the solutions
to (
1.10
).
The tools we employ to characterize
\boldsymbol{\eta}
are largely based on our second work
[
12
]
where we study, among other things, the properties of (multivalued) PVFs enjoying a
total dissipativity
condition.

As a byproduct, we obtain the convergence
in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathsf{X}))
of
X_{\tau}

to the unique solution of the deterministic ODE in (
1.10
) driven by
\boldsymbol{b}

(Theorem
5.10
).
We will further extend the result described above in a much greater generality so to treat simultaneously also the case of multivalued and possibly discontinuous
probability vector fields.
These results fully clarify the links between the explicit Stochastic Euler Method (
1.15
)
induced by a metrically dissipative (multivalued) PVF
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}

and the contraction semigroup generated by its totally dissipative barycentric
projection, which in turn can be studied by
the implicit Euler Scheme.

Plan of the paper.
The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
contains the preliminaries on Wasserstein spaces, the main notations adopted, a recall of the definition of multivalued PVF (MPVF) and notions of dissipativity taken from
[
11
,
12
]
. In Section
3
, we recall the Explicit Euler scheme for dissipative MPVFs and construct a probabilistic representation of its affine interpolants. We then study its convergence as the step-size vanish. In Section
4
, we study the stability of the probabilistic representation of the Implicit Euler scheme for a maximal totally dissipative MPVF with respect to the initial data. Finally, our main results are contained in Section
5
, while illustrative examples appear in Section
6
. Appendices
A
and
B
close the work.

2.
Preliminaries

We introduce here the main notations used throughout the paper.

Given
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we write
\lambda_{+}:=\max\{0,\lambda\}
and denote by
\left\lfloor\cdot\right\rfloor
the floor function. Given a set
X
, we denote by
\boldsymbol{i}_{X}:X\to X
the identity map on
X
.
Given
M\in\mathbb{N}
and sets
X_{0},\dots,X_{M}
, we define the
i
-th projection map
by

\pi^{i}:X_{0}\times X_{1}\times\dots\times X_{M}\to X_{i},\quad\pi^{i}(x_{0},\dots,x_{M})=x_{i},

with
i=0,\dots,M
; note that we start counting from the index
0
, this is done to be coherent with the time evaluations of curves defined in some interval of the form
[0,T]
. Similarly, we define

\pi^{i,j}:X_{0}\times X_{1}\times\dots\times X_{M}\to X_{i}\times X_{j},\quad\pi^{i,j}(x_{0},\dots,x_{M})=(x_{i},x_{j}),

for
i,j=0,\dots,M
.

To ensure generality, we introduce the basic notions of measure theory considering
X
and
Y
to be Lusin completely regular topological spaces. Recall that a topological space
X
is
completely regular
if it is Hausdorff and, for every closed set
C
and point
x\in X\setminus C
, there exists a continuous function
f:X\to[0,1]
such that
f(C)=\{1\}
and
f(x)=0
. This framework is well-suited to our analysis of Borel probability measures on (subsets of) a separable Hilbert space
X
, which may carry either the strong or weak topology.

With
\mathcal{P}(X)
we denote the set of Borel probability measures on
X
endowed with the weak/narrow topology, induced by the duality with
\mathrm{C}_{b}(X)
, the space of bounded continuous real-valued functions defined on
X
.

Given
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
and a Borel map
r:X\to Y
, we define the
push-forward
measure
r_{\sharp}\mu\in\mathcal{P}(Y)
by

\int_{Y}\varphi(y)\,\mathrm{d}(r_{\sharp}\mu)(y)=\int_{X}\varphi(r(x))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x),

for any
\varphi:Y\to\mathbb{R}
bounded Borel map.

For later use, we recall the
disintegration theorem
(c.f.
[
2
, Theorem 5.3.1]
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{X},X
be Lusin completely regular topological spaces,
\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})
,
r:\mathbb{X}\to X
be a Borel map. Then, there exists a
(r_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\mu})
-a.e. uniquely determined Borel family of probability measures
\{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{x}\}_{x\in X}\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})
such that
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{x}(\mathbb{X}\setminus r^{-1}(x))=0
for
(r_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\mu})
-a.e.
x\in X
, and

\int_{\mathbb{X}}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\mu}(\boldsymbol{x})=\int_{X}\left(\int_{r^{-1}(x)}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{x}(\boldsymbol{x})\right)\,\mathrm{d}(r_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\mu})(x)

for every bounded Borel map
\varphi:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 2.2
.

When
\mathbb{X}=X_{0}\times X_{1}
and
r
is the projection
\pi^{0}
on the first component, we can
identify the disintegration
\{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{x}\}_{x\in X_{0}}\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})
of
\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}(X_{0}\times X_{1})
w.r.t. the map
\pi^{0}

with a family of probability measures
\{\mu_{x_{0}}\}_{x_{0}\in X_{0}}\subset\mathcal{P}(X_{1})
. We write

\boldsymbol{\mu}=\displaystyle\int_{X_{0}}\mu_{x_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}({\pi^{0}}_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\mu})(x_{0})
.

The set of admissible couplings (transport plans) between two probability measures
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
,
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(Y)
is given by

\Gamma(\mu,\nu):=\left\{\boldsymbol{\gamma}\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y)\mid{\pi^{0}}_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\mu\,,\,{\pi^{1}}_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\nu\right\}.

If
\boldsymbol{\gamma}\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y)
, we say that the measures
{\pi^{0}}_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\gamma}
and
{\pi^{1}}_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\gamma}
, are the first and second marginals of
\gamma
, respectively.

If
(X,|\cdot|)
is a separable normed space, given
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
we define its
2
-moment
by

\mathsf{m}_{2}(\mu):=\left(\int_{\mathsf{X}}|x|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\right)^{1/2},

and denote by
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
the set of all measures in
\mathcal{P}(X)
with finite
2
-moment.

If
(X,|\cdot|)
is a complete and separable normed space,
we denote by
W_{2}
the Wasserstein distance over
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
, defined by

(2.1)

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\int_{X\times X}|x-y|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\gamma}(x,y)\mid\boldsymbol{\gamma}\in\Gamma(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\right\},

and refer to
[
2
, §7]
for the main properties of the complete and separable metric space
(\mathcal{P}_{2}(X),W_{2})
.

We denote by
\Gamma_{o}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
the subset of couplings in
\Gamma(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
realizing the infimum in (
2.1
).

In the sequel,
\mathsf{X}
denotes a separable Hilbert space with
norm
|\cdot|
and scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
In case
X=\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{I};\mathsf{X})
is endowed with the uniform norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
and
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathbb{R}
is a compact interval, we use the notation
W_{2,\infty}
to denote the Wasserstein distance over
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
.

We denote by
\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX}
the tangent bundle to
\mathsf{X}
, identified with the cartesian product
\mathsf{X}\times\mathsf{X}

with the induced norm
|(x,v)|:=\big(|x|^{2}+|v|^{2}\big)^{1/2}
and the
strong-weak topology, i.e. the product of the strong topology on the first component and the weak topology on the second one. The set
\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX})
is defined thanks to the identification of

\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX}
with
\mathsf{X}\times\mathsf{X}
and it is endowed with
the narrow topology induced by the strong-weak topology in
\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX}
. Given

Definition 2.3

In

2.1.
Dissipative Probability Vector Fields and EVI solutions

From now on,
\mathsf{X}
denotes a separable Hilbert space.
We recall here the definition of MPVF and different notions of dissipativity: the metric dissipativity introduced in

]

Definition 2.4

(Multivalued Probability Vector Field - MPVF)
.

A
multivalued probability vector field

{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
is a nonempty subset of

\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX})
with domain
\mathrm{D}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}):=\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}})=\{\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi:\Phi\in{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\}
.
Given
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X})
, we define the
section

{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[\mu]

of
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
as

{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[\mu]:=\left\{\Phi\in{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\mid\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi=\mu\right\}.

Given
\Phi\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX})
, the
barycenter of
\Phi
is the function
\boldsymbol{b}_{\Phi}\in L^{2}(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi;\mathsf{X})
defined by

(2.2)

\boldsymbol{b}_{\Phi}(x):=\int v\,\mathrm{d}\Phi(x,v)=\int v\,\mathrm{d}\Phi_{x}(v),

where
\{\Phi_{x}\}_{x\in\mathsf{X}}\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X})
is the disintegration of
\Phi
w.r.t.
\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX})
be a
MPVF
. We define the
MPVF

by

(2.3)

\operatorname{bar}\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\right)[\mu]:=\left\{\operatorname{bar}\left(\Phi\right):=(\boldsymbol{i}_{\mathsf{X}},\boldsymbol{b}_{\Phi})_{\sharp}\mu:\Phi\in{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[\mu]\right\},\quad\mu\in\mathrm{D}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}).

Before we recall the metric and total notions of dissipativity for a MPVF

,
we briefly recall the definition of duality pairings between measures in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX})
.

Definition 2.6

(Metric-duality pairings)
.

For every
\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX})
,
\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X})
, we set

\displaystyle\Lambda(\Phi_{0},\mu_{1})

\displaystyle:=\left\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\Gamma(\Phi_{0},\mu_{1})\mid(\mathsf{x}^{0},\mathsf{x}^{1})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\Gamma_{o}(\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi_{0},\mu_{1})\right\},

\displaystyle\Lambda(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1})

\displaystyle:=\left\{\boldsymbol{\Theta}\in\Gamma(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1})\mid(\mathsf{x}^{0},\mathsf{x}^{1})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\Theta}\in\Gamma_{o}(\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi_{0},\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi_{1})\right\},

where, with a slight abuse of notation, we used

to be also the projection map
\mathsf{x}^{1}(x_{0},v_{0},x_{1})=x_{1}
acting on
\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX}\times\mathsf{X}
.
We set

\displaystyle\left[\Phi_{0},\mu_{1}\right]_{r}

\displaystyle:=\min\left\{\int_{\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX}\times\mathsf{X}}\langle x_{0}-x_{1},v_{0}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\mid\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\Lambda(\Phi_{0},\mu_{1})\right\},

\displaystyle\left[\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1}\right]_{r}

\displaystyle:=\min\left\{\int_{\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX}\times\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX}}\langle x_{0}-x_{1},v_{0}-v_{1}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\Theta}\mid\boldsymbol{\Theta}\in\Lambda(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1})\right\}.

Definition 2.7

(Metric
\lambda
-dissipativity)
.

A
MPVF

is (metrically)

-dissipative

\left[\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1}\right]_{r}\leq\lambda W_{2}^{2}(\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi_{0},\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi_{1})\quad\text{ for every }\,\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1}\in{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}.

In case

is dissipative.

Definition 2.8

(Total
\lambda
-dissipativity)
.

We say that a

{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX})
is

\lambda
-dissipative

\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

for every
\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1}\in{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}

and every
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\in\Gamma(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1})

(2.5)

\int\langle v_{1}-v_{0},x_{1}-x_{0}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}(x_{0},v_{0},x_{1},v_{1})\leq\lambda\int|x_{1}-x_{0}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}(x_{0},v_{0},x_{1},v_{1}).

We say that
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
is

\lambda
-dissipative
if it is
maximal in the class of totally
\lambda
-dissipative
MPVF
s: if
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}^{\prime}\supset{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}

and

is totally
\lambda
-dissipative, then
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}^{\prime}={\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}.

We recall the definition of
\lambda
-EVI solution for a MPVF. Here,
\mathcal{I}
denotes an arbitrary (bounded or unbounded) interval of
\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 2.9

(
\lambda
-Evolution Variational Inequality)
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
be a
MPVF
and let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
We say that a continuous curve

is a

-EVI solution
(in
\mathcal{I}
) for the
MPVF

if

(2.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}W_{2}^{2}(\mu_{t},\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda W_{2}^{2}(\mu_{t},\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi)-\left[\Phi,\mu_{t}\right]_{r}\text{ in }\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\operatorname{int}\left(\mathcal{I}\right))\text{ for every }\Phi\in{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}},

where when we write

it means that the expression holds in the distributional sense in

. Equivalently, (

) can be rephrased as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}^{+}W_{2}^{2}(\mu_{t},\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda W_{2}^{2}(\mu_{t},\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi)-\left[\Phi,\mu_{t}\right]_{r}\text{ for every $t\in\operatorname{int}\left(\mathcal{I}\right)$ }\text{ for every }\Phi\in{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}},

where

denotes the right upper Dini derivative.

3.

We recall here below the definition of Explicit Euler scheme for a MPVF

coming from

Definition 3.1

(Explicit Euler Scheme)
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX})
be a
MPVF
and suppose we are given
a step size
\tau>0
, an initial datum
\bar{\mu}\in\mathrm{D}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}})
,
a bounded interval
[0,T]
, corresponding to
the final step
{\mathrm{N}(T,\tau)}:=\left\lceil T/\tau\right\rceil,

and a stability bound

A sequence

(M^{n}_{\tau},\Phi_{\tau}^{n})_{0\leq n\leq{\mathrm{N}(T,\tau)}}\subset\mathrm{D}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}})\times{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
is a

L
-stable solution to the Explicit Euler Scheme in

starting from

if

(EE)

\left\{\begin{aligned} M_{\tau}^{0}&=\bar{\mu},\\
\Phi_{\tau}^{n}&\in{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[M_{\tau}^{n}],\ |\Phi_{\tau}^{n}|_{2}\leq L&&0\leq n<{\mathrm{N}(T,\tau)},\\
M_{\tau}^{n}&=(\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}^{\tau})_{\sharp}\Phi_{\tau}^{n-1}&&1\leq n\leq{\mathrm{N}(T,\tau)}.\end{aligned}\right.

Definition 3.2

(Single-step and multi-step plans)
.

Let
\tau>0
.

•

Given
\Phi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX})
, we define the
single-step plan associated with
\Phi
as

T_{\tau}\equiv T_{\tau}(\Phi):=(\mathsf{x},\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}^{\tau})_{\sharp}\Phi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{X}^{2}),

so that
(\pi^{0})_{\sharp}{T_{\tau}}=\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi
. By disintegration theorem, there exists a
\left[\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi\right]
-a.e. uniquely determined Borel family of probability measures
\{T_{\tau,x}\}_{x\in\mathsf{X}}\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{X})
such that

T_{\tau}=\int_{\mathsf{X}}(\delta_{x}\otimes T_{\tau,x})\,\mathrm{d}\left[\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi\right](x).

•

Given
N\in\mathbb{N}
and a family
(\Phi^{n})_{0\leq n\leq N}\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX})
, we denote the single-step plans associated with each
\Phi^{n}
by
T_{\tau}^{n}:=T_{\tau}(\Phi^{n})
,
n\in\{0,\dots,N-1\}
, and define by recursion

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}^{1}

\displaystyle:=T_{\tau}^{0},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}^{n}

\displaystyle:=\int(\delta_{(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})}\otimes T_{\tau,x_{n-1}}^{n-1})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}^{n-1}(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})\in\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{X}^{n+1}),

for
2\leq n\leq N
. The
multi-step plan associated with
(\Phi^{n})_{0\leq n\leq N}
is defined by

\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}\equiv\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}\left((\Phi^{n})_{n}\right):=\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}^{N}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{X}^{N+1}).

Lemma 3.3
.

Given
N,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\leq N
, we define the restriction map
\mathsf{R}_{n}:\mathsf{X}^{N+1}\to\mathsf{X}^{n+1}
as

\mathsf{R}_{n}(x_{0},\dots,x_{N})=(x_{0},\dots,x_{n}).

Let
\tau>0
and
(\Phi^{n})_{0\leq n\leq N}\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX})
. With the notations of Definition
3.2
, we have

(1)

(\mathsf{R}_{n})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}^{n}
;

(2)

(\pi^{n})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}^{n}=\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi^{n}
,

for any
n\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, where we recall that
\pi^{n}:\mathsf{X}^{n+1}\to\mathsf{X}
is the projection map defined as
\pi^{n}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})=x_{n}
.

Proof.

Item (
1
) is immediate by definition. To prove item (
2
), we notice that

(\pi^{1})_{\sharp}T_{\tau}^{n}=\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi^{n+1},\quad\text{for any }n\in\{0,\dots,N-1\},

and that
(\pi^{1})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}^{1}=(\pi^{1})_{\sharp}T_{\tau}^{0}=\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi^{1}
.
By induction, assume that item (
2
) holds for
n\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}
and let us prove it for
n+1
. For any bounded Borel function
f:\mathsf{X}\to\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\int f(x)\,\mathrm{d}\left((\pi^{n+1})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}^{n+1}\right)(x)

\displaystyle=\int f(x_{n+1})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}^{n+1}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\int f(x_{n+1})\,\mathrm{d}T_{\tau,x_{n}}^{n}(x_{n+1})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}^{n}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=\int f(x_{n+1})\,\mathrm{d}T_{\tau,x_{n}}^{n}(x_{n+1})\,\mathrm{d}\left((\pi^{n})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}^{n}\right)(x_{n})

\displaystyle=\int f(x_{n+1})\,\mathrm{d}T_{\tau,x_{n}}^{n}(x_{n+1})\,\mathrm{d}\left[\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi^{n}\right](x_{n})

\displaystyle=\int f(x_{n+1})\,\mathrm{d}T_{\tau}^{n}(x_{n},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\int f(x_{n+1})\,\mathrm{d}\left[\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi^{n+1}\right](x_{n+1}),

thus proving item (
2
).
∎

(Interpolations of (

5.
MPVFs with totally dissipative barycenter

We first introduce another intermediate notion of dissipativity, that will play a crucial role.

Definition 5.1

(Unconditional
\lambda
-dissipativity)
.

We say that a
MPVF

{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX})
is
unconditionally
\lambda
-dissipative
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, if
for every
\boldsymbol{\gamma}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X}\times\mathsf{X})
and every
\Phi_{i}\in{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[{\pi^{i}}_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\gamma}]
,
i=0,1
, there exists
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\in\Gamma(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1})
such that
(\mathsf{x}^{0},\mathsf{x}^{1})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}=\boldsymbol{\gamma}
and

\int\langle v_{1}-v_{0},x_{1}-x_{0}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}(x_{0},v_{0},x_{1},v_{1})\leq\lambda\int|x_{1}-x_{0}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\gamma}(x_{0},x_{1}).

It is easy to check that total
\lambda
-dissipativity implies unconditional
\lambda
-dissipativity, which implies (metric)
\lambda
-dissipativity.
It is remarkable that a totally dissipative barycenter is enough to get unconditional dissipativity.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX})
be a
MPVF
whose barycenter
\operatorname{bar}\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\right)
is totally
\lambda
-dissipative. Given
\Phi,\Psi\in{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
and
\boldsymbol{\gamma}\in\Gamma(\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi,\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Psi)
, define

\boldsymbol{\sigma}:=\int_{\mathsf{X}^{2}}\left(\delta_{x}\otimes\Phi_{x}\right)\otimes\left(\delta_{y}\otimes\Psi_{y}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\gamma}(x,y)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX}\times\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX}),

where
\{\Phi_{x}\}_{x\in\mathsf{X}}
and
\{\Psi_{y}\}_{y\in\mathsf{X}}
are the disintegrations of
\Phi
and
\Psi
w.r.t. their spatial marginals i.e.

\Phi=\int_{\mathsf{X}}(\delta_{x}\otimes\Phi_{x})\,\mathrm{d}(\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Phi)(x),\quad\Psi=\int_{\mathsf{X}}(\delta_{y}\otimes\Psi_{y})\,\mathrm{d}(\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}\Psi)(y).

Then
\sigma\in\Gamma(\Phi,\Psi)
,
(\mathsf{x}^{0},\mathsf{x}^{1})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\boldsymbol{\gamma}
, and

(5.1)

\int_{\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX}}\langle x-y,v-w\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(x,v,y,w)\leq\lambda\int_{\mathsf{X}^{2}}|x-y|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\gamma}(x,y).

In particular,
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
is unconditionally
\lambda
-dissipative.

Proof.

It is clear that
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\Gamma(\Phi,\Psi)
and that
(\mathsf{x}^{0},\mathsf{x}^{1})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\boldsymbol{\gamma}
. Let us show (
5.1
):

(5.2)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX}}\langle x-y,v-w\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(x,v,y,w)&=\int_{\mathsf{X}^{2}}\int_{\mathsf{X}}\int_{\mathsf{X}}\langle x-y,v-w\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\Phi_{x}(v)\,\mathrm{d}\Psi_{y}(w)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\gamma}(x,y)\\
&=\int_{\mathsf{X}^{2}}\langle x-y,\boldsymbol{b}_{\Phi}(x)-\boldsymbol{b}_{\Psi}(y)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\gamma}(x,y)\\
&\leq\lambda\int_{\mathsf{X}^{2}}|x-y|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\gamma}(x,y),\end{split}

by total
\lambda
-dissipativity of the barycenter.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

For later use, we rewrite the expression (

) of Proposition

, which involves the plan

Let

be the disintegration of

\mathsf{x}
and

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX}}\langle v_{0},x_{0}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\Phi(x_{0},v_{0})-\int_{\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX}\times\mathsf{X}}\langle v_{0},x_{1}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}(x_{0},v_{0},x_{1})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathsf{X}\times\mathsf{X}}\langle\boldsymbol{b}_{\Phi}(x_{0}),x_{0}-x_{1}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(x_{0},x_{1}).

∎

We set

and

(5.3)

\int\langle x_{k}-y_{k},\frac{x_{k+1}-x_{k}}{\tau}-\frac{y_{k+1}-y_{k}}{\tau}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}^{n}\leq\lambda\int_{\mathsf{X}^{2}}|x_{k}-y_{k}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}^{n}\quad\text{ for every }k=0,\dots,n-1.

Proof of claim 1
: we prove it by induction, starting from

. In this case it is sufficient to define

defined as in Definition
3.4

and

(5.4)

\begin{split}&\int\langle\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}(\gamma)-\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}(\tilde{\gamma}),\frac{\mathsf{e}_{(k+1)\tau}(\gamma)-\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}(\gamma)}{\tau}-\frac{\mathsf{e}_{(k+1)\tau}(\tilde{\gamma})-\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}(\tilde{\gamma})}{\tau}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})\\
&\quad\leq\lambda\int|\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}(\gamma)-\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}(\tilde{\gamma})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})\end{split}

for every
k=0,\dots,N-1
. Let us set

\sigma^{2}(t):=\displaystyle\int|\mathsf{e}_{t}(\gamma)-\mathsf{e}_{t}(\tilde{\gamma})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})=\|\mathsf{e}_{t}\circ\pi^{0}-\mathsf{e}_{t}\circ\pi^{1}\|^{2}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}},\quad t\in[0,T],

where, in this context,
\pi^{0}
and
\pi^{1}
are defined on
\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathsf{X})\times\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathsf{X})
and are the projection maps on the first and second component, respectively.
We then get, for any

,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sigma^{2}(t)\bigg|_{t=k\tau+}

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{h}\,\int\left(|\mathsf{e}_{k\tau+h}(\gamma)-\mathsf{e}_{k\tau+h}(\tilde{\gamma})|^{2}-|\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}(\gamma)-\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}(\tilde{\gamma})|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})

\displaystyle=2\,\int\langle\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}(\gamma)-\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}(\tilde{\gamma}),\frac{\mathsf{e}_{(k+1)\tau}(\gamma)-\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}(\gamma)}{\tau}-\frac{\mathsf{e}_{(k+1)\tau}(\tilde{\gamma})-\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}(\tilde{\gamma})}{\tau}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})

\displaystyle\quad+\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}h\int\left|\frac{\mathsf{e}_{(k+1)\tau}(\gamma)-\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}(\gamma)}{\tau}-\frac{\mathsf{e}_{(k+1)\tau}(\tilde{\gamma})-\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}(\tilde{\gamma})}{\tau}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})

\displaystyle\leq 2\lambda\int|\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}(\gamma)-\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}(\tilde{\gamma})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})

\displaystyle=2\lambda\sigma^{2}(k\tau),

where we have used (
5.4
) and the fact that
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
is concentrated on pair of curves which are affine in every interval

,

Arguing as in
[

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sigma^{2}(t)\leq 2\lambda\sigma^{2}(k\tau)+8L^{2}\tau,

for every

, with possible countable exceptions. We now proceed as in
[
11
, proof of Proposition 6.3]
, noting that, whenever

for some

\displaystyle|\sigma(t)-\sigma(k\tau)|

\displaystyle=\left|\|\mathsf{e}_{t}\circ\pi^{0}-\mathsf{e}_{t}\circ\pi^{1}\|_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}-\|\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}\circ\pi^{0}-\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}\circ\pi^{1}\|_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\|\mathsf{e}_{t}\circ\pi^{0}-\mathsf{e}_{t}\circ\pi^{1}\|_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}-\|\mathsf{e}_{t}\circ\pi^{1}-\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}\circ\pi^{0}\|_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}+\|\mathsf{e}_{t}\circ\pi^{1}-\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}\circ\pi^{0}\|_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}-\|\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}\circ\pi^{0}-\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}\circ\pi^{1}\|_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathsf{e}_{t}\circ\pi^{0}-\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}\circ\pi^{0}\|_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}+\|\mathsf{e}_{t}\circ\pi^{1}-\mathsf{e}_{k\tau}\circ\pi^{1}\|_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int|\gamma(t)-\gamma(k\tau)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\tau}(\gamma)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\int|\tilde{\gamma}(t)-\tilde{\gamma}(k\tau)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{\tau}(\tilde{\gamma})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

Hence, by applying the general identity

a^{2}-b^{2}=2b(a-b)+|a-b|^{2},\quad a,b\in\mathbb{R},

in the relation obtained in (

,

, and using the assumption

for every

with at most countable exceptions. Applying the Gronwall estimate in
[

\sigma(t)\leq\sigma(0)e^{\lambda t}+8L\sqrt{t\tau}\left(1+|\lambda|\sqrt{t\tau}\right)e^{\lambda_{+}t},\quad\text{for any }t\in[0,T].

In particular,

(5.6)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sigma(t)\leq\sigma(0)e^{\lambda_{+}T}+8L\sqrt{T\tau}\left(1+|\lambda|\sqrt{T\tau}\right)e^{\lambda_{+}T}=:K_{\tau}.

We recall that, by construction of
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
,
\sigma(0)=W_{2}(\bar{\mu},\tilde{\mu})
, indeed we chose
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{0}\in\Gamma_{o}(\bar{\mu},\tilde{\mu})

Using the interpolation inequality (cf.
[

\|Y\|^{2}_{\infty}\leq C\|Y\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathsf{X})}\|Y\|_{H^{1}(0,T;\mathsf{X})}\quad\text{for every }Y\in H^{1}(0,T;\mathsf{X}),\text{ for some }C>0,

(5.7)

\begin{split}W_{2,\infty}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\tau},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{\tau})&\leq\int\|\gamma-\tilde{\gamma}\|^{2}_{\infty}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})\\

indeed

\displaystyle I_{1}^{2}

Regarding the second term in (

\displaystyle:=\int\|\gamma-\tilde{\gamma}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(0,T;\mathsf{X})}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})

\displaystyle=2\left(I_{1}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int|\gamma^{\prime}(t)-\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})\right)\mathrm{d}t\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2(I_{1}^{2}+4TL^{2}).

In conclusion,

\displaystyle W_{2,\infty}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\tau},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{\tau})

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\,C\,T^{\frac{1}{2}}\,K_{\tau}\,\sqrt{TK_{\tau}^{2}+4TL^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\,\left(\sigma(0)+\sigma^{2}(0)+\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}+\tau+\tau^{2}\right),

for some

. In particular, if

for some

.

and
\tau(h)<\min\{\frac{2}{\lambda_{+}},1\}
for every
j,h\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
(M_{h}^{j},{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}_{h}^{j},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{j})\in\mathscr{T}(\bar{\mu}^{j},\tau(h),T,L)
.
By Theorem
5.7
, there exists
C>0
such that

W_{2,\infty}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{j})\leq C\left(\sqrt{W_{2}(\bar{\mu}_{h},\bar{\mu}^{j})}+\tau(h)^{\frac{1}{4}}\right)\quad\text{ for every }j,h\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
\boldsymbol{\eta}^{j}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathsf{X}))
be as in Theorem
4.1
for
\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
and the starting point
\bar{\mu}^{j}
. By assumption, we have

\sup_{j}|\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}|_{2}(\bar{\mu}^{j})<\infty,

indeed, for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, denoting by
\hat{\boldsymbol{f}}^{\circ}
the minimal selection of
\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
as in Theorem
4.2
, we have

(5.8)

\int\left|\hat{\boldsymbol{f}}^{\circ}[\bar{\mu}^{j}](x)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}^{j}(x)\leq\int|v|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{\Phi}(x,v),

for any
\hat{\Phi}\in\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[\bar{\mu}^{j}]
. In particular, (
5.8
) holds for any
\hat{\Phi}\in\operatorname{bar}\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\right)[\bar{\mu}^{j}]
, and thus for
\operatorname{bar}\left(\bar{\Phi}^{j}\right)
, so that

\int\left|\hat{\boldsymbol{f}}^{\circ}[\bar{\mu}^{j}](x)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}^{j}(x)\leq\int|\boldsymbol{b}_{\bar{\Phi}^{j}}(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}^{j}(x)\leq\int|v|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\Phi}^{j}(x,v)\leq L.

We can thus apply Proposition
4.3
for
\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
and get the existence of
\tilde{C}>0
such that

W_{2,\infty}(\boldsymbol{\eta}^{j},\boldsymbol{\eta})\leq\tilde{C}\,\sqrt{W_{2}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\mu}^{j})}\quad\text{ for every }j\in\mathbb{N}.

Thus, by triangle inequality we can write, for every
n,N\in\mathbb{N}
, that

\displaystyle W_{2,\infty}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\eta})

\displaystyle\leq W_{2,\infty}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{j})+W_{2,\infty}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{j},\boldsymbol{\eta}^{j})+W_{2,\infty}(\boldsymbol{\eta}^{j},\boldsymbol{\eta})

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sqrt{W_{2}(\bar{\mu}_{h},\bar{\mu}^{j})}+\tau(h)^{\frac{1}{4}}\right)+W_{2,\infty}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{j},\boldsymbol{\eta}^{j})+\tilde{C}\,\sqrt{W_{2}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\mu}^{j})}.

The thesis follows by passing first to the limit as
h\to+\infty
and applying Theorem
5.6
to
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{j}
and
\boldsymbol{\eta}^{j}
, and then passing to the limit as
j\to+\infty
.
∎

Theorem 5.10
.

In the same hypotheses of Theorem
5.9
, let
(\Omega,{\mathcal{B}})
be a standard Borel space endowed with a non atomic probability measure
\mathbb{P}
. Let
\bar{X},\bar{X}_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathcal{B}},\mathbb{P};\mathsf{X})
be random variables such that
(\bar{X})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}=\bar{\mu}
,
(\bar{X}_{h})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}=\bar{\mu}_{h}
for every
h\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\mathrm{Z}_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathcal{B}},\mathbb{P};\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathsf{X}))
be such that
(\mathrm{Z}_{h})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}=\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}
for every
h\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\bar{X}_{h}\to\bar{X}

\mathbb{P}
-a.s. and
\mathsf{e}_{0}\circ\mathrm{Z}_{h}=\bar{X}_{h}
for every
h\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\mathrm{Z}_{h}\to{\mathrm{s}}_{\bar{\mu}}\circ\bar{X}\text{ in }L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathcal{B}},\mathbb{P};\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathsf{X}))\text{ as }h\to+\infty,

where
{\mathrm{s}}_{\bar{\mu}}
is as in (
4.2
).

Proof.

We set
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{h}:=(\boldsymbol{i}_{\Omega},\bar{X}_{h},\mathrm{Z}_{h})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}
and
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}:=(\boldsymbol{i}_{\Omega},\bar{X},{\mathrm{s}}_{\bar{\mu}}\circ\bar{X})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}
; we claim that

(5.9)

\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{h}\rightharpoonup\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\text{ as }h\to+\infty.

First of all, notice that
(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{h})_{h}
is tight, since its marginals are tight: the first one is the constant
\mathbb{P}
, the second one is
\bar{\mu}_{h}
which is converging to
\bar{\mu}
, and the third one is
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}
which is converging to
\boldsymbol{\eta}
by Theorem
5.9
. Up to a (unrelabeled) subsequence, we thus get that
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{h}\rightharpoonup\bar{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}
for some
\bar{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega\times\mathsf{X}\times\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathsf{X}))
. Observe that
(\pi^{0,1})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{h}=(\boldsymbol{i}_{\Omega},\bar{X}_{h})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}\rightharpoonup(\boldsymbol{i}_{\Omega},\bar{X})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}=(\pi^{0,1})_{\sharp}\bar{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}
by the Dominated Convergence Theorem; we also have that

(\pi^{1,2})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{h}=(\bar{X}_{h},\mathrm{Z}_{h})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}=(\mathsf{e}_{0},\boldsymbol{i}_{\mathrm{C}})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}\rightharpoonup(\mathsf{e}_{0},\boldsymbol{i}_{\mathrm{C}})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}=(\bar{X},{\mathrm{s}}_{\bar{\mu}}\circ\bar{X})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}=(\pi^{1,2})_{\sharp}\bar{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}},

where we have denoted by
\boldsymbol{i}_{\mathrm{C}}
the identity map in
\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathsf{X})
and we have used that
\mathsf{e}_{0}\circ{\mathrm{s}}_{\bar{\mu}}\circ\bar{X}=\bar{X}
(cf. (
4.3
)), together with the fact that
\boldsymbol{\eta}=({\mathrm{s}}_{\bar{\mu}})_{\sharp}\bar{\mu}
coming from Theorem
4.1
. Since
(\pi^{1,2})_{\sharp}\bar{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}
is concentrated on a map, we deduce by
[
2
, Lemma 5.3.2]
that it must be
\bar{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}=\boldsymbol{\vartheta}
; this proves (
5.9
). An application of
[
2
, Lemma 5.4.1]
gives that
(\bar{X}_{h},\mathrm{Z}_{h})\to(\bar{X},{\mathrm{s}}_{\bar{\mu}}\circ\bar{X})
in probability, so that, in particular,
\mathrm{Z}_{h}\to{\mathrm{s}}_{\bar{\mu}}\circ\bar{X}
in probability. However, since the measures
(\mathrm{Z}_{h})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}=\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}
are converging to
\boldsymbol{\eta}
in
W_{2,\infty}
by Theorem
5.9
, the random variables
(\mathrm{Z}_{h})_{h}
have uniformly integrable
2
-moments, and therefore they converge to
{\mathrm{s}}_{\bar{\mu}}
in
L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathcal{B}},\mathbb{P};\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathsf{X}))
.

∎

6.
Examples

We present examples illustrating the application of the theory developed in this paper. In particular, we examine how the results established in Theorem
5.9
and Theorem
5.10
can be used in the setting of Stochastic Gradient-type evolutions—more broadly, within the framework of Stochastic Dissipative Flow theory—as well as in the context of dissipative evolutions governed by interaction fields.

6.1.
Stochastic Dissipative Flow

Given a vector field
b:\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{X}
and an initial datum
\bar{x}\in\mathsf{X}
, we consider the following deterministic Cauchy problem

(6.1)

\dot{x}(t)=b(x(t)),\quad t\in[0,T],\quad x(0)=\bar{x}.

We assume
b
to be continuous and
\lambda
-dissipative for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, i.e.

(6.2)

\langle x_{1}-x_{0},b(x_{1})-b(x_{0})\rangle\leq\lambda|x_{1}-x_{0}|^{2},\quad\text{for any }x_{0},\,x_{1}\in\mathsf{X},

so that (
6.1
) is well-posed.

We assume that
b
arises as a stochastic average of a family of Borel vector fields
g:\mathsf{X}\times\mathsf{U}\to\mathsf{X}
, where
(\mathsf{U},\mathbb{U})
is a probability space endowed with a non-atomic probability measure, i.e.

b(x)=\int_{\mathsf{U}}g(x,u)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{U}(u),\quad x\in\mathsf{X}.

We require that there exists
L>0
such that

(6.3)

\int_{\mathsf{U}}|g(x,u)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{U}(u)\leq L(1+|x|^{2})\quad\text{ for every }x\in\mathsf{X}.

Similarly to what is done for the
stochastic gradient descent
, we consider the following stochastic scheme.

We adopt the same notation of Definition
3.4
for the interpolation map
G:\mathsf{X}^{{\mathrm{N}(T,\tau)}+1}\to\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathsf{X})
sending points
(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{\mathrm{N}(T,\tau)})
to their affine interpolation, i.e. to the curve
\gamma
given by

(6.4)

\gamma(t):=\frac{1}{\tau}\left((n+1)\tau-t\right)x_{n}+\frac{1}{\tau}(t-n\tau)x_{n+1},

if
t\in[n\tau,(n+1)\tau]\cap[0,T]
,
0\leq n<{\mathrm{N}(T,\tau)}
.

Definition 6.1

(SDF)
.

Let
(\Omega,{\mathcal{B}},\mathbb{P})
be a standard Borel probability space and
T>0
. Define
J:=\left\{\frac{T}{N}\,:\,N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}\right\}
. We say that a family of maps
X_{\tau}:\Omega\to\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathsf{X})
,
\tau\in J
, is a
Stochastic Dissipative Flow (SDF) for
g
if there exist random variables
(X^{n}_{\tau})_{0\leq n\leq N,\tau\in J}\subset L^{2}(\Omega;\mathsf{X})
and
V^{k}:\Omega\to\mathsf{U}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

•

(X^{0}_{\tau})_{\tau\in J}
and
(V^{k})_{k}
are independent,

•

(V^{k})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{U}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

•

X^{n+1}_{\tau}=X^{n}_{\tau}+\tau\,g(X^{n}_{\tau},V^{n})
for every
0\leq n\leq N-1
,
\tau\in J
,

and

(6.5)

X_{\tau}=G_{\sharp}\left(X^{0}_{\tau},X^{1}_{\tau},\dots,X^{n}_{\tau}\right)\quad\text{ for every }\tau\in J.

We point out that in Definition
6.1
we assumed
J\subset\mathbb{R}
to be countable in order to ensure the existence of a SDF which can be proven via the Kolmogorov existence theorem (cf.
[
5
, Theorem 36.1]
).

We have the following result.

Corollary 6.2
.

In the setting of Definition
6.1
, assume that
(X^{0}_{\tau})_{\tau\in J}
converges
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. to some
\bar{X}\in L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathcal{B}},\mathbb{P};\mathsf{X})
and that
W_{2}(({X^{0}_{\tau}})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P},({\bar{X}})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P})\to 0
as
\tau\downarrow 0
.
Then
X_{\tau}
converges in
L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathcal{B}},\mathbb{P};\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathsf{X}))
as
\tau\downarrow 0
to the unique solution of the deterministic ODE

\dot{X}_{t}=b(X_{t}),\quad t\in[0,T],\quad X_{t=0}=\bar{X}.

The result can be proved using the machinery developed in this paper as follows. We define the PVF
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX})
by

(6.6)

{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[\mu]:=(\pi^{0},g)_{\sharp}(\mu\otimes\mathbb{U}),\quad\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X}).

Notice that, for any
\mu\in\mathrm{D}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}})=\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X})
, the Borel family of probability measures
\left\{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}_{x}[\mu]\right\}_{x\in\mathsf{X}}\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X})
, obtained by disintegrating
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[\mu]
w.r.t.
\mathsf{x}_{\sharp}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}=\mu
, is given by

{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}_{x}[\mu]=g(x,\cdot)_{\sharp}\mathbb{U},\quad\text{for }\mu\text{-a.e. }x\in\mathsf{X}.

In particular, for any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X})
, the barycenter
of
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[\mu]
defined in (
2.2
) is given by

\int_{\mathsf{X}}v\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}_{x}[\mu](v)=b(x),\quad\text{for }\mu\text{-a.e. }x\in\mathsf{X}.

Thus, according to Definition
2.5
, the PVF
\operatorname{bar}\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\right)
is given by

(6.7)

\operatorname{bar}\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\right)[\mu]=(\boldsymbol{i}_{\mathsf{X}},b)_{\sharp}\mu,\quad\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X}).

We now show that
\operatorname{bar}\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\right)
is
totally
\lambda
-dissipative
, so that, thanks to Proposition
5.2
,
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
is unconditionally
\lambda
-dissipative.

Proposition 6.3
.

The
PVF

\operatorname{bar}\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\right)
defined in (
6.7
) is totally
\lambda
-dissipative.

Proof.

Take
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X})
and
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\in\Gamma(\operatorname{bar}\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\right)[\mu],\operatorname{bar}\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\right)[\nu])
. Since
\operatorname{bar}\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\right)[\mu],\operatorname{bar}\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\right)[\nu]
are concentrated on maps, there exists
\boldsymbol{\gamma}\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that

\boldsymbol{\vartheta}=\left(\pi^{0},b\circ\pi^{0},\pi^{1},b\circ\pi^{1}\right)_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\gamma}.

By assumption (
6.2
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX}^{2}}\langle v_{1}-v_{0},x_{1}-x_{0}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}(x_{0},v_{0},x_{1},v_{1})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathsf{X}^{2}}\langle b(x_{1})-b(x_{0}),x_{1}-x_{0}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\gamma}(x_{0},x_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\int_{\mathsf{X}^{2}}|x_{1}-x_{0}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\gamma}(x_{0},x_{1}).

∎

Thanks to
[
12
, Theorem 3.24]
, since
b
is continuous then
\operatorname{bar}\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\right)
is maximal and

(6.8)

b=\tilde{\boldsymbol{f}}^{\circ}[\mu]=\tilde{\boldsymbol{b}}^{\circ}[\mu]\quad\mu\text{-a.e.}

(cf. also Theorem
4.2
), with
\tilde{\boldsymbol{f}}^{\circ}
being the element of minimal norm of
\operatorname{bar}\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\right)
and
\tilde{\boldsymbol{b}}^{\circ}
of its Lagrangian representation.
By assumption (
6.3
) and
[
11
, Lemma 5.13]
, the Explicit Euler scheme is solvable for
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
.

In the following Proposition
6.4
, we prove the equivalence between the measure
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\tau}
constructed as in Definition
3.4
for
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
and the measure on paths defined as the law of a Stochastic Dissipative Flow for
g
.

Proposition 6.4
.

In the setting of Definition
6.1
, let
\mu_{0,\tau}:=(X^{0}_{\tau})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}
and let
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
be as in (
6.6
). Let
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\tau}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathsf{X}))
be as in Definition
3.4
for the
PVF

{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
and the initial measure
\mu_{0,\tau}
. Then
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\tau}=(X_{\tau})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}
.

Proof.

Let
(M^{n}_{\tau},\Phi_{\tau}^{n})_{0\leq n\leq{\mathrm{N}(T,\tau)}}\subset\mathrm{D}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}})\times{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
be as in Definition
3.1
for the PVF
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
. In particular,
\Phi_{\tau}^{n}={\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[M_{\tau}^{n}]
. Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{X}^{{\mathrm{N}(T,\tau)}+1})
be the multi-step plan associated with
\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[M_{\tau}^{n}]\right)_{n}
as in Definition
3.2
. Thus,
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\tau}=G_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathsf{X}))
.
By (
6.5
), the result follows if we show that

\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}=(X^{0}_{\tau},X^{1}_{\tau},\dots,X^{{\mathrm{N}(T,\tau)}}_{\tau})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}.

Claim 1
:
M_{\tau}^{n}=(X^{n}_{\tau})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}
for any
0\leq n\leq{\mathrm{N}(T,\tau)}
.

Proof of claim 1
: We prove this by induction on
n
. For
n=1
, noting that
(\mu_{0,\tau}\otimes\mathbb{U})=(X^{0}_{\tau},V^{0})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}
by independence of the random variables, we have

(6.9)

M^{1}_{\tau}=(\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}^{\tau})_{\sharp}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[\mu_{0,\tau}]=(\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}^{\tau})_{\sharp}(\pi^{0},g)_{\sharp}(\mu_{0,\tau}\otimes\mathbb{U})=(\pi^{0}+\tau g(\pi^{0},\pi^{1}))_{\sharp}(X^{0}_{\tau},V^{0})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}={X^{1}_{\tau}}_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}.

Assume now that the claim is true for
n\geq 1
; we need to prove it holds for
n+1
. This proof is analogous, noting that
M^{n}_{\tau}\otimes\mathbb{U}=(X^{n}_{\tau},V^{n})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}
again by independence.

Claim 2
: Denote by
T^{n}_{\tau}:=(\mathsf{x},\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}^{\tau})_{\sharp}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[M^{n}_{\tau}]
as in Definition
3.2
. Then, its disintegration
\left\{T^{n}_{\tau,x}\right\}_{x\in\mathsf{X}}\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{X})
with respect to the projection map
\pi^{0}
is given by

T^{n}_{\tau,x}=(x+\tau g(x,\cdot))_{\sharp}\mathbb{U}

for
{\pi^{0}}_{\sharp}T^{n}_{\tau}=M^{n}_{\tau}
-a.e.
x\in\mathsf{X}
and for any
0\leq n\leq{\mathrm{N}(T,\tau)}
.

Proof of claim 2
: This proof, being straightforward, is omitted.

Claim 3
: Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}^{n}
be the plans defined in Definition
3.2
associated with
\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}[M_{\tau}^{n}]\right)_{n}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}^{1}

\displaystyle:=T_{\tau}^{0},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}^{n}

\displaystyle:=\int(\delta_{(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})}\otimes T_{\tau,x_{n-1}}^{n-1})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}^{n-1}(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})\in\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{X}^{n+1}),\quad 2\leq n\leq{\mathrm{N}(T,\tau)}.

Then,
\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{n}_{\tau}=(X^{0}_{\tau},X^{1}_{\tau},\dots,X^{n}_{\tau})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}
, for any
1\leq n\leq{\mathrm{N}(T,\tau)}
.

Proof of claim 3
: We prove the claim by induction argument. First, we have
\alpha^{1}_{\tau}=(X^{0}_{\tau},X^{1}_{\tau})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}
and its proof is similar to what is done in (
6.9
). Assume now the claim holds for
n\geq 1
and let us prove it holds for
n+1
. Given any Borel test function
\phi:\mathsf{X}^{n+2}\to\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\int\phi(x_{0},\dots,x_{n+1})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\int\phi(x_{0},\dots,x_{n},x_{n+1})\,\mathrm{d}T_{\tau,x_{n}}^{n}(x_{n+1})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}^{n}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=\int\phi(x_{0},\dots,x_{n},x_{n}+\tau g(x_{n},u))\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{U}(u)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}^{n}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=\int\phi(x_{0},\dots,x_{n},x_{n}+\tau g(x_{n},u))\,\mathrm{d}\left[(X^{0}_{\tau},\dots,X^{n}_{\tau},V^{n})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}\right](x_{0},\dots,x_{n},u)

\displaystyle=\int\phi(X^{0}_{\tau}(\omega),\dots,X^{n}_{\tau}(\omega),X^{n+1}_{\tau}(\omega))\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega),

where we used Claim 2 and the independence of
X^{0}_{\tau},\dots,X^{n}_{\tau},V^{n}
.
This concludes the proof of the claim and thus the whole proof, since
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\tau}^{{\mathrm{N}(T,\tau)}}
.
∎

Proof of Corollary

.

Thanks to Proposition

, we can apply Theorem

and get that

converges to

, where

is as in (

) for the maximal totally dissipative PVF

6.2.
Interaction field

Let
f:\mathsf{X}\times\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{X}
be a continuous map and let
b_{f}:\mathsf{X}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X})\to\mathsf{X}
be the barycenter of
f
, defined by

(6.10)

b_{f}(x,\mu):=\int_{\mathsf{X}}f(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y),\quad(x,\mu)\in\mathsf{X}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X}).

Given a standard Borel probability space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{B}},\mathbb{P})
and
\bar{X}\in L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathcal{B}},\mathbb{P};\mathsf{X})
, we consider the deterministic ODE

(6.11)

\dot{X}_{t}=b_{f}(X_{t},{X_{t}}_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}),\quad t\in[0,T],\quad X_{t=0}=\bar{X}.

We assume that there exists
L>0
such that

(6.12)

|f(x,y)|^{2}\leq L(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})\quad\text{ for every }x,y\in\mathsf{X},

and that the vector field
\tilde{f}:\mathsf{X}\times\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{X}\times\mathsf{X}
defined by
\tilde{f}(x,y):=\left(f(x,y),\,f(y,x)\right)
is
\lambda
-dissipative in
\mathsf{X}\times\mathsf{X}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, i.e.

(6.13)

\langle\tilde{f}(x_{1},y_{1})-\tilde{f}(x_{0},y_{0}),\,(x_{1},y_{1})-(x_{0},y_{0})\rangle\leq\lambda\left(|x_{1}-x_{0}|^{2}+|y_{1}-y_{0}|^{2}\right).

Remark 6.5
.

A particular case in which (
6.13
) holds occurs when
f(x,y)=h(x-y)
and
h:\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{X}
is
\lambda
-dissipative and odd.

Similarly as for the example in Section
6.1
, we consider the following stochastic scheme driven by the interaction field
f
.

Definition 6.6

(IDF)
.

Let
(\Omega,{\mathcal{B}},\mathbb{P})
be a standard Borel probability space and
T>0
. Define
J:=\left\{\frac{T}{N}\,:\,N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}\right\}
. We say that a family of maps
X_{\tau}:\Omega\to\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathsf{X})
,
\tau\in J
, is an
Interaction Dissipative Flow (IDF)
for
f
if there exist random variables
(X^{n}_{\tau})_{0\leq n\leq N,\tau\in J},(Y^{n}_{\tau})_{0\leq n\leq N,\tau\in J}\subset L^{2}(\Omega;\mathsf{X})
such that

•

Y^{n}_{\tau}
,
X^{0}_{\tau}
and
(Y^{m}_{\tau})_{m<n}
are independent for every
0\leq n\leq N
,
\tau\in J
,

•

(X^{n}_{\tau})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}=(Y^{n}_{\tau})_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}

Lemma 6.9

Let

Proof.

Since

converges to

6.3.
Nonlocal Stochastic Dissipative Flow

Assume that

and let

be the space of probability measures with bounded support. We take a nonlocal vector field
b:\mathsf{X}\times\mathcal{P}_{b}(\mathsf{X})\to\mathsf{X}
and, given a standard Borel probability space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{B}},\mathbb{P})
and
\bar{X}\in L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathcal{B}},\mathbb{P};\mathsf{X})
, we consider the deterministic ODE

(6.18)

\dot{X}_{t}=b(X_{t},{X_{t}}_{\sharp}\mathbb{P}),\quad t\in[0,T],\quad X_{t=0}=\bar{X}.

Example 6.10
.

The vector field
b
could be for example a

b(x,\mu)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\psi_{i}\left(\int\varphi_{1}^{i}\,\mathrm{d}\mu,\dots,\int\varphi_{N_{i}}^{i}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)k_{i}(x),\quad(x,\mu)\in\mathsf{X}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X}),

where
\varphi^{i}_{j}:\mathsf{X}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\psi_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{N_{i}}\to\mathbb{R}
, and

We assume that
b
is
\lambda
-dissipative, for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, i.e. for any
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{b}(\mathsf{X})
there exists
\boldsymbol{\gamma}\in\Gamma_{o}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})

(6.19)

\int_{\mathsf{X}^{2}}\langle b(x_{1},\mu_{1})-b(x_{0},\mu_{0}),x_{1}-x_{0}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\gamma}(x_{0},x_{1})\leq\lambda\int|x_{1}-x_{0}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\gamma}(x_{0},x_{1})=\lambda W_{2}^{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}).

(C1)

Remark 6.11

Condition (

{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}
satisfying (
6.20
):

(F1)

there exists a constant

such that

(6.22)

(F2)

for every

Lemma 6.12
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{F}}}\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{T\kern-1.5ptX})

(F2)

with
\supp(\mu)\subset B_{R}(0)

Technical results

The following Propositions, of independent interest, are used to prove the strong convergence result in Proposition

Definition A.1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{P}_{p}(X)
,
p\in[1,+\infty)
. We say that
\mathcal{K}
has
uniformly integrable
p
-moments
if

(A.1)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{K}}\int_{\{x\,:\,d(x,x_{0})\geq k\}}d^{p}(x,x_{0})\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=0\quad\text{ for some (hence for any) }x_{0}\in X.

Proposition A.2
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and let
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{P}_{p}(X)
,
p\in[1,+\infty)
.
Assume that there exist
x_{0}\in X
and a Borel measurable function
\varphi:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
such that

\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\varphi(r)}{r}=+\infty,\quad\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{K}}\int_{X}\varphi(d^{p}(x,x_{0}))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<+\infty.

Then
\mathcal{K}
has uniformly integrable
p
-moments.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and take
R_{\varepsilon}>0
such that
\varepsilon\,\varphi(r)>r
for every
r>R_{\varepsilon}
. Whenever
k\geq R_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}
, then for any
\mu\in\mathcal{K}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\{x\,:\,d(x,x_{0})\geq k\}}d^{p}(x,x_{0})\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq\varepsilon\int_{\{x\,:\,d(x,x_{0})\geq k\}}\varphi(d^{p}(x,x_{0}))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq\varepsilon\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{K}}\int_{X}\varphi(d^{p}(x,x_{0}))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

Passing to the
\sup
among
\mu\in\mathcal{K}
, to the limit as
k\to+\infty
and finally to the limit as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
proves the sought uniform integrability of the
p
-moments.
∎

Proposition A.3
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and let
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{P}_{p}(X)
,
p\in[1,+\infty)
. Assume that
\mathcal{K}
has uniformly integrable
p
-moments. Then, for any
x_{0}\in X
, there exists
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}([0,+\infty);[0,+\infty))
increasing and convex such that

(A.2)

\varphi(0)=0,\quad\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\varphi(r)}{r}=+\infty,\quad\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{K}}\int_{X}\varphi(d^{p}(x,x_{0}))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<+\infty.

Proof.

The proof is a simple adaptation of the analogous statement for the uniform integrability of a family of functions in
L^{1}
, see for example
[
6
, Theorem 4.5.9]
, combined with a regularization argument.

Fix
x_{0}\in X
, by uniform integrability of the
p
-moments, we can find an increasing sequence of natural numbers
C_{n}\uparrow+\infty
such that

(A.3)

\int_{\{x\,:\,d(x,x_{0})\geq C_{n}^{1/p}\}}d^{p}(x,x_{0})\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq 2^{-n}\quad\text{ for every }n\in\mathbb{N},\,\mu\in\mathcal{K}.

Let us define, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{K}
the real numbers

\mu_{k}:=\mu\left(\left\{x\in X\,:\,d^{p}(x,x_{0})>k\right\}\right).

We set

\alpha_{n}:=0\text{ if }n<C_{1}\text{ and }\alpha_{n}:=\max\{k\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,k\leq C_{n}\}\text{ if }n\geq C_{1}.

We finally define

\tilde{\varphi}(r):=\int_{0}^{r}g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad g(r):=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{n}\,{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{(n,n+1]}(r),\quad r\geq 0.

Clearly
\tilde{\varphi}
is non-negative, increasing, convex and satisfies
\lim_{r\to+\infty}\tilde{\varphi}(r)/r=+\infty
. Notice also that
\tilde{\varphi}(r)=0
for any
r\in[0,1]
. We show that
\tilde{\varphi}
also satisfies the last condition in (
A.2
). First of all we observe that, for any
n\geq 1
and any
\mu\in\mathcal{K}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\{x\,:\,d(x,x_{0})\geq C_{n}^{1/p}\}}d^{p}(x,x_{0})\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\{x\,:\,d^{p}(x,x_{0})\geq C_{n}\}}d^{p}(x,x_{0})\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=C_{n}}^{+\infty}j\,\mu\left(\left\{x\in X\,:\,j<d^{p}(x,x_{0})\leq j+1\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=C_{n}}^{+\infty}(j-C_{n}+1)\,\mu\left(\left\{x\in X\,:\,j<d^{p}(x,x_{0})\leq j+1\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=C_{n}}^{+\infty}\mu_{k}.

We deduce by (
A.3
)

(A.4)

\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\sum_{k=C_{n}}^{+\infty}\mu_{k}\leq 1\quad\text{ for every }\mu\in\mathcal{K}.

Moreover

\displaystyle\int_{X}\tilde{\varphi}(d^{p}(x,x_{0}))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\int_{0}^{d^{p}(x,x_{0})}g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\int_{\{x\,:\,n<d^{p}(x,x_{0})\leq n+1\}}\int_{0}^{d^{p}(x,x_{0})}g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{n}\mu\left(\left\{x\in X\,:\,n<d^{p}(x,x_{0})\leq n+1\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\alpha_{n}\mu\left(\left\{x\in X\,:\,n<d^{p}(x,x_{0})\leq n+1\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\alpha_{n}\mu_{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\sum_{k=C_{n}}^{+\infty}\mu_{k}\leq 1.

Finally, we define
\varrho\in\mathrm{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
as

\varrho(t):=\begin{cases}c_{0}\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}\left(\frac{1}{|2x-1|^{2}-1}\right)\quad&\text{ if }|2x-1|<1,\\
0\quad&\text{ if }|2x-1|\geq 1,\end{cases}

where

is the positive constant such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varrho(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=1
. Observe that
\supp(\varrho)=[0,1]
,
\varrho(1-t)=\varrho(t)
for every

and
\varrho
is increasing in
[0,1/2]
. We define
\varphi:=\varrho\ast\tilde{\varphi}
, where we have extended
\tilde{\varphi}
to
\mathbb{R}
by continuity. Clearly (the restriction to
[0,+\infty)
of )
\varphi
is smooth, convex (since
\tilde{\varphi}
is convex) and satisfies

\varphi(0)=0,\quad\tilde{\varphi}(t-1)\leq\varphi(t)\leq\tilde{\varphi}(t)\quad\text{ for every }t\in\mathbb{R}

so that (
A.2
) holds for
\varphi
. It remains to show that
\varphi
is increasing: we compute

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=(\varrho^{\prime}\ast\tilde{\varphi})(t)=\int_{1/2}^{1}\varrho^{\prime}(r)\tilde{\varphi}(t-r)\,\mathrm{d}r-\int_{0}^{1/2}\varrho^{\prime}(1-r)\tilde{\varphi}(t-r)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\int_{1/2}^{1}\varrho^{\prime}(s)\left(\tilde{\varphi}(t-s)-\tilde{\varphi}(t-1+s)\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\geq 0,

where the last inequality follows by the fact that

is increasing and that

is decreasing in
[1/2,1]
.
∎

\mu(0)=\bar{\mu}
, be such that

(B.1)

\#(\supp(\mu_{t}))\text{ is finite and non-increasing w.r.t.\penalty\ }t\geq 0.

Assume that
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{C}([0,+\infty);\mathsf{X}))
is such that
(\mathsf{e}_{t})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}=\mu_{t}
for every
t\geq 0
. Then

\boldsymbol{\eta}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\delta_{\gamma_{i}},

for curves
\gamma_{i}\in\mathrm{C}([0,+\infty);\mathsf{X})
satisfying

(P1)

\gamma_{i}\neq\gamma_{j}
,
i\neq j
;

(P2)

\gamma_{i}(0)=x_{i}
and
i=1,\dots,N
;

(P3)

if there exists
s\geq 0
,
i,j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
with
i\neq j
, such that
\gamma_{i}(s)=\gamma_{j}(s)
, then
\gamma_{i}(t)=\gamma_{j}(t)
for every
t\geq s
.

In particular, if
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{C}([0,+\infty);\mathsf{X}))
are such that
(\mathsf{e}_{t})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}=(\mathsf{e}_{t})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}=\mu_{t}
for every
t\geq 0
, then
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}=\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into five claims.

Claim 1
: we have
\mathsf{e}_{t}(\supp(\boldsymbol{\eta}))=\supp(\mu_{t})
.

Proof of claim 1
: by e.g.
[
2
, Formula (5.2.6)]
, we have that

\mathsf{e}_{t}(\supp(\boldsymbol{\eta}))\subseteq\supp(\mu_{t})\subseteq\overline{\mathsf{e}_{t}(\supp(\boldsymbol{\eta}))}.

In particular, by (
B.1
) we get that
\mathsf{e}_{t}(\supp(\boldsymbol{\eta}))
is finite, so that it is closed and the conclusion follows.

Claim 2
: if
\gamma^{1},\gamma^{2}\in\supp(\boldsymbol{\eta})
and
\bar{s}\geq 0
are such that
\gamma^{1}(\bar{s})=\gamma^{2}(\bar{s})
, then
\gamma^{1}(t)=\gamma^{2}(t)
for every
t\geq\bar{s}
.

Proof of claim 2
: suppose by contradiction this is not the case and set

\bar{t}:=\max\left\{s\geq\bar{s}:\gamma^{1}(r)=\gamma^{2}(r)\,\,\text{ for every }\bar{s}\leq r\leq s\right\}\in[\bar{s},+\infty).

Then, by continuity of
\gamma^{1},\gamma^{2}
, for every
\delta>0
we can find
t_{\delta}\in(\bar{t},\bar{t}+\delta)
such that
\gamma^{1}(t_{\delta})\neq\gamma^{2}(t_{\delta})
. Let
K:=\#(\supp(\mu_{\bar{t}}))\in\{1,\dots,N\}
. If
K=1
, noticing that

\left\{\gamma^{1}(t_{1}),\gamma^{2}(t_{1})\right\}\subset\supp(\mu_{t_{1}}),

then
\#(\supp(\mu_{t_{1}}))\geq 2>1
and we get a contradiction with (
B.1
). If
K>1
, then we can find
\tilde{\gamma}_{1},\dots,\tilde{\gamma}_{K-1}\in\supp(\boldsymbol{\eta})
such that
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}(\bar{t})\neq\tilde{\gamma}_{j}(\bar{t})\neq\gamma^{1}(\bar{t})=\gamma^{2}(\bar{t})
for every
i\neq j
. By continuity we can find
\varepsilon_{k}>0
,
k=1,2
, such that

\tilde{\gamma}_{i}(t)\neq\tilde{\gamma}_{j}(t)\neq\gamma^{k}(t)\text{ for every }i\neq j,\,k=1,2,\,\,t\in[\bar{t},\bar{t}+\varepsilon_{k}).

Thus, if we set
\varepsilon:=\varepsilon_{1}\wedge\varepsilon_{2}
, we have that

\left\{\tilde{\gamma}_{1}(t_{\varepsilon}),\dots,\tilde{\gamma}_{K-1}(t_{\varepsilon}),\gamma^{1}(t_{\varepsilon}),\gamma^{2}(t_{\varepsilon})\right\}\subset\supp(\mu_{t_{\varepsilon}}),

hence
\#(\supp(\mu_{t_{\varepsilon}}))\geq K+1>K
, a contradiction with (
B.1
).

Claim 3
: we have
\#(\supp(\boldsymbol{\eta}))=N
.

Proof of claim 3
:
from Claim 1 applied with
t=0
, it follows

(B.2)

\supp(\boldsymbol{\eta})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\left\{\gamma\in\supp(\boldsymbol{\eta}):\gamma(0)=x_{i}\right\}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}A_{i}.

However, Claim 2 applied with
\bar{s}=0
yields that each
A_{i}
is a singleton. This concludes the proof of the claim.

Claim 4
: we have

\boldsymbol{\eta}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\delta_{\gamma_{i}},

for curves
\gamma_{i}\in\mathrm{C}([0,+\infty);\mathsf{X})
satisfying properties
(P1)
,
(P2)
,
(P3)
.

Proof of claim 4
: by Claim 3, there exist
\gamma_{i}\in\mathrm{C}([0,+\infty);\mathsf{X})
,
i=1,\dots,N
, satisfying
(P1)
and
\tilde{a}_{i}>0
, with
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{a}_{i}=1
, such that

\boldsymbol{\eta}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{a}_{i}\delta_{\gamma_{i}}.

From Claim 1 (cf. also (
B.2
)), we get
(P2)
, i.e.
\gamma_{i}(0)=x_{i}
for any
i=1,\dots,N
; while
(P3)
comes from Claim 2.
In particular, since

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{a}_{i}\delta_{\gamma_{i}(0)}=(\mathsf{e}_{0})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}=\bar{\mu}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\delta_{x_{i}},

we deduce that
\tilde{a}_{i}=a_{i}
for any
i=1,\dots,N
.

Claim 5
: if
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{C}([0,+\infty);\mathsf{X}))
are such that
(\mathsf{e}_{t})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}=(\mathsf{e}_{t})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}=\mu_{t}
for every
t\geq 0
, then
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}=\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}
.

Proof of claim 5
: by Claim 4, we have

\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\delta_{\gamma^{1}_{i}},\qquad\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\delta_{\gamma^{2}_{i}},

with
\gamma^{1}_{i},\gamma^{2}_{i}\in\mathrm{C}([0,+\infty);\mathsf{X})
satisfying properties
(P1)
,
(P2)
,
(P3)
. Let
\boldsymbol{\eta}:=\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}+\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2})
and notice that
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{C}([0,+\infty);\mathsf{X}))
and
(\mathsf{e}_{t})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}=\mu_{t}
for every
t\geq 0
. By construction we have

\{\gamma_{i}^{1},\gamma_{i}^{2}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset\supp(\boldsymbol{\eta});

however by Claim 3 we have
\#(\supp(\boldsymbol{\eta}))=N
, so that it must be that
\gamma_{i}^{1}=\gamma_{i}^{2}
for every
i=1,\dots,N
.
∎

On Tensor products of regular characters of the General Linear and unitary groups of degree two over the principal ideal local rings of finite length
Archita Gupta, M Hassain, Pooja Singla

Abstract
. Let R R be a principal ideal local ring of finite length with a finite residue field of odd characteristic. Let G ⁡ ( R ) G(R) denote either the general linear group or the general unitary group of degree two over R R . We study the decomposition of tensor products of irreducible representations of G ⁡ ( R ) G(R) . It is known that the irreducible representations of G ⁡ ( R ) G(R) are built from regular representations, which are classified into three types: cuspidal, split semisimple, and split non-semisimple. We prove that the tensor product of any two regular irreducible representations of distinct types has irreducible constituents with multiplicity at most two. Moreover, we show that the regular part of the tensor product of a cuspidal representation with any other regular representation is multiplicity free. When both factors are of split semisimple type, we show that the multiplicity of any regular irreducible constituent is at most length ⁡ ( R ) + 1 \mathrm{length}(R)+1 , and that this bound is achieved only when the constituent is also split semisimple. In contrast, we demonstrate that the multiplicity in the tensor product of two split non-semisimple representations can grow with the cardinality of the residue field when the length of the ring is at least two. In the case when R R is a finite field, all such tensor product multiplicities are uniformly bounded above by two. This highlights a significant difference between the behaviour of tensor products in the field case and in the more general finite local ring setting.

1.
Introduction

The tensor product problem, a classical question in representation theory, concerns decomposing the tensor product of two irreducible representations into a direct sum of irreducible representations. This problem appears widely across mathematics. For instance, in Schur–Weyl duality, the decomposition of tensor powers of the standard representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
illustrates the rich interplay between linear and symmetric group representations. Similarly, in the context of finite groups, tensor product decompositions are central to understanding the structure of representations of groups.

The problem has been extensively studied for various families of groups. In the case of the polynomial representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, Littlewood and Richardson
[
12
]
, and independently Robinson
[
26
]
, proposed a rule describing the decomposition of such tensor products. This rule was rigorously proved later in
[
27
,
30
]
. The tensor product problem for irreducible characters of the symmetric and alternating groups, as well as their double covers, has been studied in depth in
[
11
,
5
,
31
,
8
]
. Although the problem remains open in general, a complete classification of irreducible representations of
S_{n}
with multiplicity-free tensor products was obtained in
[
7
]
, and analogous results for plethysms of Schur functions appeared in
[
6
]
.

For finite general linear groups, Hiss and Lübeck
[
18
]
proved that for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
\mathrm{GU}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, the multiplicity of a unipotent character in the tensor product of two unipotent characters is a polynomial in
q
with rational coefficients. In most cases, the tensor square of the Steinberg representation of a finite simple group of Lie type contains every irreducible character
[
17
]
.

In recent work, Letellier-Nam
[
21
]
established an analogue of the Saxl conjecture for the tensor square of unipotent characters of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. The tensor products of generic irreducible characters of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
were studied in
[
23
,
16
]
, and those of split semisimple (not necessarily generic) irreducible characters in
[
28
]
. Further, Letellier and Rodriguez-Villegas
[
22
]
investigated Ennola duality in the decomposition of tensor products of unipotent and generic characters of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
\mathrm{GU}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, by relating the multiplicities of irreducible characters in these groups. Despite this progress, the tensor product problem for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
\mathrm{GU}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
even for
n\geq 3
remains open in general.
A few partial results for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and

\mathrm{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
are included in
[
2
,
1
]
.
For
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, a complete decomposition of the tensor product was independently obtained in
[
19
]
and
[
14
]
.

In this article, we
study the tensor product problem for the general linear and unitary groups of degree two over the principal ideal local rings. These groups are natural generalization of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
\mathrm{GU}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}).

Let
\mathfrak{o}
be a complete discrete valuation ring with residue field
\mathsf{k}
of odd characteristic. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be the maximal ideal and let
\pi
be a fixed uniformizer. Let
\mathfrak{O}
be an unramified quadratic extension of
\mathfrak{o}
. For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we let
\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}=\mathfrak{o}/\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}
denote the finite quotient.
Let
\mathrm{G}
denote either the general
linear group
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
or the unitary group
\mathrm{GU}_{2}
associated with
\mathfrak{O}
.

The representation theory of
\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
is well studied, see
[
29
,
24
,
20
,
10
]
. It is known that the irreducible representations of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
fall into two categories:
regular
and
non-regular
. The non-regular representations arise, up to a twist, via induction from the regular representations of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{i})
for some
i<\ell
. In this spirit, the
regular representations
are the building blocks of the representation theory of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
. For
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
, regular representations coincide with the so-called generic representations
[
25
]
. Any regular representation
\rho
of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
for
\ell\geq 2
is known to have its dimension in the set

\{(q-1)q^{\ell-1},(q+1)q^{\ell-1},(q^{2}-1)q^{\ell-2}\}.

Based on these dimensions and their constructions, regular representations are classified into types
\mathfrak{t}(\rho)
as follows:

•

Cuspidal:
\mathfrak{t}(\rho)=\mathbf{cus}
if
\dim(\rho)=(q-1)q^{\ell-1}
,

•

Split semisimple:
\mathfrak{t}(\rho)=\mathbf{ss}
if
\dim(\rho)=(q+1)q^{\ell-1}
,

•

Split non-semisimple:
\mathfrak{t}(\rho)={\mathbf{sns}}
if
\dim(\rho)=(q^{2}-1)q^{\ell-2}
.

For
\ell=1
, the dimension formulas differ slightly. To describe results uniformly, we define all non-linear irreducible representations of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{1})
as regular, with types determined analogously:

•

\mathbf{cus}
if
\dim(\rho)=q-1
,

•

\mathbf{ss}
if
\dim(\rho)=q+1
,

•

{\mathbf{sns}}
if
\dim(\rho)=q
.

Our focus here is on the
tensor product of regular representations
of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}),
particularly determining the
multiplicity
of regular constituents in such products. This problem for
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GL}_{2}
and
\ell=1
has been previously studied in
[
14
]
, we extend those results to
\ell\geq 1
for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
and also include the results for
\mathrm{GU}_{2}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
. In particular, we aim to classify pairs of regular representations
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
such that their tensor product
\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}
is
multiplicity free
.

Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu
be regular irreducible representations of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
. We denote the multiplicity of
\nu
in
\lambda\otimes\mu
by
g_{\lambda\mu}^{\nu}
.
Our main results provide sharp upper bounds for the multiplicities of regular constituents in tensor products of regular representations, classified according to the types involved.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\ell\geq 1
, and let
\lambda,\mu,\nu
be regular irreducible representations of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
.

(1)

If
\mathbf{cus}\in\{\mathfrak{t}(\lambda),\mathfrak{t}(\mu),\mathfrak{t}(\nu)\},
then

g_{\lambda\mu}^{\nu}\leq 1.

(2)

If the set
\{\mathfrak{t}(\lambda),\mathfrak{t}(\mu),\mathfrak{t}(\nu)\}
consists of exactly two types, then

g_{\lambda\mu}^{\nu}\leq 2,

with equality occurring only when the triple
(\mathfrak{t}(\lambda),\mathfrak{t}(\mu),\mathfrak{t}(\nu))
is a permutation of
(\mathbf{ss},{\mathbf{sns}},\mathbf{ss}).

(3)

If all three representations are of type
\mathbf{ss}
, i.e.,
\{\mathfrak{t}(\lambda),\mathfrak{t}(\mu),\mathfrak{t}(\nu)\}=\{\mathbf{ss}\}
, then

g_{\lambda\mu}^{\nu}\leq\ell+1.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\lambda
and
\mu
be regular irreducible representations of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
with
\mathfrak{t}(\lambda)=\mathbf{cus}
.

(1)

If
\mathfrak{t}(\lambda)\neq\mathfrak{t}(\mu)
, then the tensor product
\lambda\otimes\mu
is multiplicity free.

(2)

The
regular part
of
\lambda\otimes\mu
that is, the sum of regular irreducible constituents of
\lambda\otimes\mu
is multiplicity free.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\ell\geq 1
and let
\lambda,\mu,\nu
be regular irreducible representations of
\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
such that

\{\mathfrak{t}(\lambda),\mathfrak{t}(\mu),\mathfrak{t}(\nu)\}=\{{\mathbf{sns}}\}.

(1)

For
\ell=1
, we have
g_{\lambda\mu}^{\nu}\leq 1
.

(2)

For
\ell\geq 2
, there exist representations
\lambda,\mu,\nu
such that

g_{\lambda\mu}^{\nu}\geq(q-2)q^{\lfloor\frac{\ell}{2}\rfloor-1}.

Corollary 1.4
.

For
\ell\geq 2
, there exist regular irreducible representations
\lambda,\mu,\nu
of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
such that the multiplicity
g_{\lambda\mu}^{\nu}
depends on the cardinality of the residue field.

From the dimension formulae, it is clear that Ennola duality holds between
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
and
\mathrm{GU}_{2}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
, parallel to
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
\mathrm{GU}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
case (see
[
13
]
for details on Ennola duality). However Ennola duality does not work for the tensor product decomposition for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
and
1.1
provides examples of such representations. This has already been observed for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
case in
[
22
]
.

We now outline the ideas underlying the proof. Recall that a representation
\rho
of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
is called a
twist
of
\rho^{\prime}
, if
\rho\cong\chi\otimes\rho^{\prime}
for a one dimensional representation
\chi
of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
. It is easy to note that the decomposition of a representation into irreducible constituents determines the decomposition for any of its twists. Hence, in determining the multiplicities of irreducible constituents of
\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}
, we may work with suitable twists of
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
. We also note that for any representations
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\rho_{3}
, we have
\langle\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2},\rho_{3}\rangle=\langle\rho_{1},\rho_{2}^{\vee}\otimes\rho_{3}\rangle,
where
\rho_{2}^{\vee}
denotes the dual representation of
\rho_{2}
. For any regular representation
\rho
of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
, we have
\mathfrak{t}(\rho)=\mathfrak{t}(\rho^{\vee}).
This allows us to permute
(\mathfrak{t}(\rho_{1}),\mathfrak{t}(\rho_{2}),\mathfrak{t}(\rho_{3}))
as required.

As mentioned earlier, the case of
\ell=1
and
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GL}_{2}
is already settled in
[
14
]
. We extend these results to
\mathrm{GU}_{2}(\mathfrak{o}_{1})
in
Section 3
.

For
\ell\geq 2
, we classify the pairs of regular representations
(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
by their types as follows:

•

\Xi_{1}=\{(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\mid\mathfrak{t}(\rho_{1})=\mathbf{ss},\mathfrak{t}(\rho_{2})={\mathbf{sns}}\}

•

\Xi_{2}=\{(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\mid\mathfrak{t}(\rho_{1})\neq\mathfrak{t}(\rho_{2}),\ \mathfrak{t}(\rho_{1})=\mathbf{cus}\}

•

\Xi_{3}=\{(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\mid\mathfrak{t}(\rho_{1})=\mathfrak{t}(\rho_{2})=\mathbf{cus}\}

•

\Xi_{4}=\{(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\mid\mathfrak{t}(\rho_{1})=\mathfrak{t}(\rho_{2})=\mathbf{ss}\}

•

\Xi_{5}=\{(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\mid\mathfrak{t}(\rho_{1})=\mathfrak{t}(\rho_{2})={\mathbf{sns}}\}

Since
\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\cong\rho_{2}\otimes\rho_{1}
, the above five families exhaust all tensor products of regular irreducible representations of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
. We use
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}))
and
\mathrm{Irr}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}))
to denote the set of all in-equivalent irreducible representations and the set of all regular representations of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}),
respectively. We prove the following result based on the above classification of types.

Theorem 1.5
.

For
\ell\geq 2
, the following hold:

(1)

For
(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\in\Xi_{1},

\langle\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2},\rho\rangle\leq 2
for every
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})).
Further equality holds only if
\mathfrak{t}(\rho)=\mathbf{ss}.

(2)

For
(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\in\Xi_{2},

\langle\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2},\rho\rangle\leq 1
for every
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})).

(3)

For
(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\in\Xi_{3},

\langle\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2},\rho\rangle\leq 1
for every
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})).

(4)

For
(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\in\Xi_{4},

\langle\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2},\rho\rangle\leq\ell+1
for every
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}))
such that
\mathfrak{t}(\rho)=\mathbf{ss},

(5)

There exists
(\rho,\rho)\in\Xi_{5}
such that
\langle\rho\otimes\rho,\rho\rangle\geq(q-2)q^{\lfloor\frac{\ell}{2}\rfloor-1}.

We note that for
\ell\geq 2
,

1.1
,
1.2
, and
1.3
directly follow from the above result. Hence major part of this article will be dedicated to prove
1.5
. For this, we use the fact that every regular irreducible representation
\rho
of
G=\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
is
imprimitive
, i.e., there exists a proper subgroup
H\subsetneq G
and an irreducible representation
\phi
of
H
such that

\rho\cong\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\phi).

To understand the tensor product
\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}
where
\rho_{i}=\mathrm{Ind}_{H_{i}}^{G}(\phi_{i})
, we use Mackey’s formula:

\mathrm{Ind}_{H_{1}}^{G}(\phi_{1})\otimes\mathrm{Ind}_{H_{2}}^{G}(\phi_{2})\cong\bigoplus_{g\in H_{1}\backslash G/H_{2}}\mathrm{Ind}_{H_{1}\cap H_{2}^{g}}^{G}\left(\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}^{g}\right).

To compute the multiplicity of an irreducible representation
\rho
as a constituent of
\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}
, we proceed via the following steps:

(A)

Determine double coset representatives in
H_{1}\backslash G/H_{2}
.

(B)

Analyze the decomposition of the induced representation

V(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}^{g}):=\mathrm{Ind}_{H_{1}\cap H_{2}^{g}}^{G}(\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}^{g})

for each
g\in H_{1}\backslash G/H_{2}
.

(C)

Understand the intertwining space

\mathrm{Hom}_{G}(V(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}^{g}),V(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}^{h}))

for distinct double coset representatives
g,h\in H_{1}\backslash G/H_{2}
.

2.1.
The unitary group and its Lie algebra

In this section, we describe our unitary group and its Lie algebra. We will restrict our definitions to the group
\mathrm{GU}_{2}
.
Let
W=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathfrak{O}_{\ell})
denote the permutation matrix corresponding to the longest Weyl element. Consider the involution on
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}(\mathfrak{O}_{\ell})
defined by

(2.1)

(a_{i,j})^{\star}\coloneqq W(a_{j,i}^{\circ})W^{-1},

and its associated Hermitian form on
\mathfrak{O}_{\ell}^{2}
given by:

\langle(u_{1},u_{2}),(v_{1},v_{2})\rangle\coloneqq v_{1}^{\circ}u_{2}+v_{2}^{\circ}u_{1}.

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
the unitary group with respect to
\star
and its Lie algebra of anti-Hermitian matrices are given by

\begin{split}\mathrm{GU}_{2}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})&\coloneqq\left\{A\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathfrak{O}_{\ell})\mid A^{\star}A=\mathrm{I}_{2}\right\},\\
\mathfrak{gu}_{2}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})&\coloneqq\left\{A\in\mathfrak{gl}_{2}(\mathfrak{O}_{\ell})\mid A+A^{\star}=0\right\}.\end{split}

By definition of
\mathfrak{gu}_{2}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
, any
A\in\mathfrak{gu}_{2}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
is of the form
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}x&\varepsilon y\\
\varepsilon z&-x^{\circ}\end{smallmatrix}\right],
for
x\in\mathfrak{O}_{\ell}
and
y,z\in\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}.
Observe that
A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathrm{GU}_{2}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
if and only if the following holds:

(1)

ad^{\circ}+cb^{\circ}=1

(2)

ab^{\circ}+a^{\circ}b=0

(3)

ac^{\circ}+a^{\circ}c=0

(4)

db^{\circ}+d^{\circ}b=0

(5)

dc^{\circ}+d^{\circ}c=0

We will use the above conditions as the defining conditions of the unitary group whenever needed. The elements of the sets
\{a,b\},\{b,d\},\{c,d\},\{a,c\}
are called neighbors of
A
.
One can easily show that, whenever defined, the ratio of the squares of the neighbors of
A
is either zero or a non-square in
\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}
, i.e., in
\varepsilon^{2}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})^{2}
.
Further
A=[a_{i,j}]\in\mathfrak{gu}_{2}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
if and only if

a_{i,j}+a_{3-j,3-i}^{\circ}=0
for
i,j\in\{1,2\}
.

Throughout this paper we consider
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
and
\mathrm{GU}_{2}
as
\mathfrak{o}
-group schemes, where the
R
-points of the latter are the fixed points of
A\mapsto(A^{\star})^{-1}
for every
\mathfrak{o}
-algebra
R
and
A\in\mathfrak{gl}_{2}(R)
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the lie algebra scheme of
\mathrm{G}
. Then
\mathfrak{g}
is either
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
or
\mathfrak{gu}_{2}
as
\mathfrak{o}
-Lie algebra schemes, the latter being the fixed points of
A\mapsto-A^{\star}
. The adjoint action of a group on its Lie algebra will be denoted by
\mathrm{Ad}
.
Recall
\mathfrak{o}_{1}=\mathbb{F}_{q}.

Define

R_{\ell}\coloneqq\begin{cases}{\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell},&\text{ for }\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GL}_{2};\\
\mathfrak{O}_{\ell},&\text{ for }\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GU}_{2}.\end{cases}

For the uniformity in the proofs, we define

\epsilon=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ for }\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GL}_{2};\\

We will use these notations throughout this article.

(2)

4.
Construction of regular representations of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})

In this section, we first give a construction of representations of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
as described in
[
20
, Section 3]
. We then present a few alternative constructions from the literature. These results will be used throughout the remainder of this paper.

For
i\leq\ell
, let
\rho_{\ell,i}:\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}\rightarrow\mathfrak{o}_{i}
be the natural projection maps. The corresponding natural projection maps
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})\rightarrow\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{i})
are also denoted by
\rho_{\ell,i}
. For any matrix
A\in\mathfrak{g}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
, we denote
\rho_{\ell,1}(A)
by
\bar{A}
. Let
\mathrm{K}^{i}=\ker(\rho_{\ell,i})
be the
i
-th congruence subgroups of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}).
For
i\geq\ell/2,
the group
\mathrm{K}^{i}
is isomorphic to the abelian additive subgroup
\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell-i})
of
M_{n}(R_{\ell-i}).
Let
\psi:R_{\ell}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a fixed primitive one dimensional representation of
R_{\ell}
. For
R_{\ell}=\mathfrak{O}_{\ell},
we assume that
\psi
satisfies
\psi(x+\epsilon y)=\psi^{\prime}(x)\psi^{\prime}(y)
for some primitive one dimensional representation
\psi^{\prime}
of
\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}
. Therefore,
\pi^{\ell-1}\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}\not\subseteq\ker(\psi)
by our choice of
\psi
.

For any
i\leq\ell/2
and
A=[a_{st}]\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak{o}_{i}),
we will consider lifts
\tilde{A}=[\widetilde{a_{st}}]\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
of
A
such that
\rho_{\ell,i}(\tilde{A})=A
with
\widetilde{a_{st}}=\epsilon
for
a_{st}=\epsilon,
and
\widetilde{a_{st}}=0
for
a_{st}=0
. In this case, we say
\tilde{A}
is a
Serre lift
of
A
.

For any
i\leq\ell/2
and
A\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak{o}_{i}),
let
\tilde{A}\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
be a lift of
A
. Define
\psi_{A}:\mathrm{I}+\pi^{\ell-i}\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
by

\psi_{A}(\mathrm{I}+\pi^{\ell-i}B)\coloneqq\psi(\pi^{\ell-i}\bm{tr}(\tilde{A}B)),

for all
\mathrm{I}+\pi^{\ell-i}B\in\mathrm{K}^{\ell-i}.
Then
\psi_{A}
is a well defined one dimensional representation of
\mathrm{K}^{\ell-i}.
Further, the following duality for abelian groups
\mathrm{K}^{i}
and
\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell-i})
holds for
i\geq\ell/2
.

(4.1)

\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell-i})\cong\widehat{\mathrm{K}^{i}}\,\,;A\mapsto\psi_{A}\,\,\mathrm{where},\,\,\psi_{A}(\mathrm{I}+\pi^{i}B)=\psi(\pi^{i}\bm{tr}(\tilde{A}B))\,\,\forall\,\,\mathrm{I}+\pi^{i}B\in\mathrm{K}^{i}.

We say a one dimensional representation
\psi_{A}\in\widehat{\mathrm{K}^{i}}
for
i\geq\ell/2
is regular if and only if
A\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell-i})
is a regular matrix (that is the characteristic polynomial is equal to its minimal polynomial). In this case the stabilizer of
A
in
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell-i})
under the conjugation action is
\{x\mathrm{I}+yA\mid x,y\in R_{\ell-i}\}\cap\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell-i}).

By (
[
25
, Lemma 2.3]
), for
i\geq\ell/2
the representation
\psi_{A}\in\widehat{\mathrm{K}^{i}}
is regular if and only if
\psi_{A}|_{\mathrm{K}^{\ell-1}}
is regular. An irreducible representation
\rho
of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
is called regular if the
\mathrm{Ad}
-orbit of its restriction to
\mathrm{K}^{\ell-1}
consists of one dimensional representations
\psi_{A}
for regular
A
.

The following lemma describes the orbits of
\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
under the
\mathrm{Ad}
-action of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
.

Lemma 4.1
.

An exhaustive list of
\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
orbit representatives under the
\mathrm{Ad}
-action of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
is given by matrices
A\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
of the following form:

(a)

x\mathrm{I}+\pi C

(b)

\begin{bmatrix}x&\epsilon\pi\beta\\
\epsilon&x\end{bmatrix}

(c)

\begin{bmatrix}x&\epsilon\delta\\
\epsilon&x\end{bmatrix}

with
\delta\in\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}^{\times}\setminus(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}^{\times})^{2}
for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gu}_{2}
and
\delta\in(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}^{\times})^{2}
for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{2}

(d)

\begin{bmatrix}x&\epsilon\sigma\\
\epsilon&x\end{bmatrix}
with
\sigma\in(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}^{\times})^{2}
for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gu}_{2}
and
\sigma\in\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}^{\times}\setminus(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}^{\times})^{2}
for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{2}.

Proof.

For
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
, proof follows from
[
4
, Section 2]
. For
\mathrm{GU}_{2},
we note that

if and only

is anti-hermitian. If

is of type (a). Otherwise the result follows from Lemma
[
3

.
∎

To describe the construction as well the decomposition of the tensor product of irreducible representations of

Now we summarize very briefly the construction of regular representations of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
with emphasis on the statements that we require in this article.

4.1.
Construction of regular representations of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
for
\ell
even

Let
\psi_{A}\in\widehat{\mathrm{K}^{\ell/2}}
be a regular one dimensional representation of
\mathrm{K}^{{\ell/2}}
for
A\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell/2}).
Then the following gives the construction in this case. Let
S_{A}=\{g\in\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})\mid\psi_{A}^{g}\cong\psi_{A}\}
be the inertia group of
\psi_{A}
in
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}).

Let
\tilde{A}\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
be a lift of
A
, and let
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\tilde{A})

denote its stabilizer in
\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
under the
\mathrm{Ad}
-action. Then
S_{A}=\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\tilde{A})\mathrm{K}^{{\ell/2}}.

Let
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}\left(\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})\mid\psi_{A}\right)
be a regular representation of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}),
then there exists an extension
\widetilde{\psi_{A}}
of
\psi_{A}
to
S_{A}
such that
\rho\cong\mathrm{Ind}_{S_{A}}^{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\widetilde{\psi_{A}}).

Every
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}\left(\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})\mid\psi_{A}\right)
has dimension
\frac{|\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})|}{|\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell/2})}(A)||\mathrm{K}^{\ell/2}|}.

4.2.
Construction of regular representations of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
for
\ell
odd

Let
{\ell_{1}}=\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor
and
{\ell_{2}}=\lceil\ell/2\rceil
and let
\psi_{A}\in\widehat{\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{2}}}}
be a regular one dimensional representation of
\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{2}}}
for
A\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak{o}_{{\ell_{1}}}).
Let
S_{A}=\{g\in\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})\mid\psi_{A}^{g}\cong\psi_{A}\}
. Let
\tilde{A}\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
be a lift of
A
. Define the group
\mathrm{Rad}_{A}:=\left(\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{1}}}\cap\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\tilde{A})\right)\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{2}}}.
The group
\mathrm{Rad}_{A}
is the radical of the bilinear form

\mathcal{B}_{A}:\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{1}}}/\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{2}}}\times\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{1}}}/\mathrm{K}^{{{\ell_{2}}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times};\,\,\mathcal{B}_{A}(x\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{2}}},y\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{2}}})=\psi_{A}([x,y]).

Therefore, the one dimensional representation
\psi_{A}
extends to
\mathrm{Rad}_{A}.
Let
\widetilde{\psi_{A}}
be an extension of
\psi_{A}
to
\mathrm{Rad}_{A}
and
\sigma\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{1}}}\mid\psi_{A})
be the unique irreducible representation determined by
\widetilde{\psi_{A}}.
Then,

\sigma|_{\mathrm{Rad}_{A}}\cong\underbrace{\widetilde{\psi_{A}}+\cdots+\widetilde{\psi_{A}}}_{q-\mathrm{times}}.

Let
I_{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\sigma)=\{g\in\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})\mid\sigma^{g}\cong\sigma\}
be the inertia groups of
\sigma\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{1}}}\mid\psi_{A}).

Then
I_{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\sigma)=S_{A}=\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\tilde{A})\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{1}}}.
Every
\sigma\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{1}}}\mid\psi_{A})
extends to the inertia group
I_{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\sigma).
In particular, every such extension induces irreducibly to
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
and gives rise to a regular representation of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}).
Every regular
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}\left(\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})\mid\psi_{A}\right)
is obtained in this way and has dimension
\frac{q|\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})|}{|\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{{\ell_{1}}})}(A)||\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{1}}}|}.

The following result can be easily obtained from the above construction and we shall use it later.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
A\in\mathfrak{g}({\mathfrak{o}_{{\ell_{1}}}})
be regular and
H
be a subgroup of
S_{A}
such that
\mathrm{K}^{\ell_{1}}\leq H\leq S_{A}.

(1)

Every irreducible representation of
H
lying above
\psi_{A}
has dimension
q
.

(2)

Let
\phi
be a representation of
H
such that
\mathrm{Res}^{H}_{\mathrm{K}^{\ell_{2}}}(\phi)=m\psi_{A}
for some positive integer
m.
Then
\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\phi)
is multiplicity free if and only if
\phi
is multiplicity free.

The following lemma describes a maximal isotropic subgroup in certain special cases.

Lemma 4.4
.

For
i\in\{1,2\}
, let
A_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}({\mathfrak{o}_{{\ell_{1}}}})
be regular matrices such that
\bar{A_{1}}\notin\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\{\mathrm{I},\bar{A_{2}}\}.
Define a subgroup
H
of
\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{1}}}
as

H:=(\{\mathrm{I}+\pi^{{\ell_{1}}}(x\mathrm{I}+y\tilde{{A_{1}}}+z\tilde{{A_{2}}})\}\cap\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{1}}})\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{2}}}.

Let
\bar{H}
be the image of

in
\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{1}}}/\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{2}}}
. Then
\bar{H}
is a maximal isotropic subgroup for the antisymmetric bilinear forms
\mathcal{B}_{A_{i}}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
as defined above.

Proof.

By direct computations, we can check that the bilinear forms
\mathcal{B}_{A_{i}}
for

are trivial on
\bar{H}
. By
\bar{A_{1}}\notin\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\{\mathrm{I},\bar{A_{2}}\}
and the cardinality of

, we obtain that

is a maximal isotropic subspace for
\mathcal{B}_{A_{i}}
for

4.3.
Alternate construction for split semisimple representations of
\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})

Let
\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
be the group of upper triangular matrices in
\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
. Let
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\in\widehat{R_{\ell}^{\times}}\times\widehat{R_{\ell}^{\times}}
. Define a character of
\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
as follows:

(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\left(\left[\begin{matrix}a&b\\
0&c\end{matrix}\right]\right)=\chi_{1}(a)\chi_{2}(c).

The pair
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
is called
\mathbf{ss}
-pair of
\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}})
if
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}|_{1+\pi^{\ell-1}\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}}\neq 1
. The set of
\mathbf{ss}
-pairs will be denoted by
\mathfrak{S}
. Let
\mathrm{T}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
be the group of diagonal matrices in
\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
.
The following lemma characterizes the
\mathbf{ss}
-pairs and split semisimple representations of
\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}}).

Lemma 4.5
.

(1)

Let
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\in\widehat{R_{\ell}^{\times}}\times\widehat{R_{\ell}^{\times}}
. If
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
is
\mathbf{ss}
-pair of
\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}})
, then
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}})}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
is irreducible.

(2)

A representation
\rho
is a split semisimple regular representation of
\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
if and only if
\rho\cong\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}})}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
for some
\mathbf{ss}
-pair
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
of
\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}})
.

Proof.

Assume
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
and
(\chi_{1}^{\prime},\chi_{2}^{\prime})
are
\mathbf{ss}
-pairs. Then we have

(4.2)

\langle\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}),\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\chi_{1}^{\prime},\chi_{2}^{\prime})\rangle=\underset{g\in\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})\backslash\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})/\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}{\sum}{\langle(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}),(\chi_{1}^{\prime},\chi_{2}^{\prime})^{g}\rangle}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})\cap\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})^{g}}.

We also have the decomposition

\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})\;=\;\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})\;\;\sqcup\;\;\left(\bigsqcup_{1\leq i\leq\ell}\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
\epsilon\pi^{i}&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})\right).

For
i\in[1,\ell]
, let
g_{i}\coloneqq\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
\epsilon\pi^{i}&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]
. We claim that
{\langle(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}),(\chi_{1}^{\prime},\chi_{2}^{\prime})^{g_{i}}\rangle}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})\cap\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})^{g_{i}}}=0
for
i\in[1,\ell-1]
. Let
i\in[1,\ell-1]
. For
b\in{\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell}
, define

X_{b}\coloneqq\left[\begin{matrix}1-\epsilon^{2}\pi^{\ell-1}b&\epsilon\pi^{\ell-i-1}b\\
0&1+\epsilon^{2}\pi^{\ell-1}b\end{matrix}\right].

Then it is easy to see that
X_{b}\in\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})\cap\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})^{g_{i}}
for all
b\in\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}
. To prove the claim, it is enough to prove that
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})(X_{b})\neq(\chi_{1}^{\prime},\chi_{2}^{\prime})^{g_{i}}(X_{b})
for some
b\in\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}
. Assume on the contrary that
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})(X_{b})=(\chi_{1}^{\prime},\chi_{2}^{\prime})^{g_{i}}(X_{b})
for all
b\in\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}
. Upon simplification, we obtain
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}(1-\epsilon^{2}\pi^{\ell-1}b)=1
for all
b\in\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}
, which contradicts the assumption that
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
is an
\mathbf{ss}
-pair. This proves the claim.
Now, for
g_{\ell}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]
, we have
\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})\cap\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})^{g_{\ell}}=\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
and for
h=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]
,
\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})\cap\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})^{h}=\mathrm{T}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
.
Then
Equation 4.2
becomes

(4.3)

\langle\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}),\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\chi_{1}^{\prime},\chi_{2}^{\prime})\rangle={\langle(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}),(\chi_{1}^{\prime},\chi_{2}^{\prime})\rangle}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}+{\langle(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}),(\chi_{2}^{\prime},\chi_{1}^{\prime})\rangle}_{\mathrm{T}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}.

To prove (1), we need to show that if
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
is an
\mathbf{ss}
-pair, then
\langle\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}),\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\rangle=1
. By
Equation 4.3
, we have

(4.4)

\langle\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}),\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\rangle=1+{\langle(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}),(\chi_{2},\chi_{1})\rangle}_{\mathrm{T}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}.

If
{\langle(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}),(\chi_{2},\chi_{1})\rangle}_{\mathrm{T}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}\neq 0
, then
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&0\\
0&c\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)=(\chi_{2},\chi_{1})\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&0\\
0&c\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)
for all
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&0\\
0&c\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathrm{T}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
, which simplifies to
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}(ac^{-1})=1
for all
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&0\\
0&c\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathrm{T}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
. Therefore, we obtain

\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}|_{\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}^{\times}}=1
. This contradicts the assumption that
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
is an
\mathbf{ss}
-pair. Thus
{\langle(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}),(\chi_{2},\chi_{1})\rangle}_{\mathrm{T}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}=0
.
Substituting this in
Equation 4.4
, we get
\langle\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}),\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\rangle=1
.

To prove (2), observe that, by (1), for an
\mathbf{ss}
-pair
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
, the representation
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
is an irreducible representation of dimension
\frac{|\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})|}{|\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})|}=(q+1)q^{\ell-1}.
Therefore, by definition,
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
is an
\mathbf{ss}
-representation.
For
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GL}_{2}
, the converse follows from
[
15
, Lemma 2.5 (3)]
. For
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GU}_{2}
, to prove the converse, we first count the number of inequivalent irreducible representations of the form
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
, where
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
is an
\mathbf{ss}
-pair. Observe that for
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\in\widehat{\mathfrak{O}_{\ell}^{\times}}\times\widehat{\mathfrak{O}_{\ell}^{\times}}
,
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=(\chi_{1}{{\chi_{2}}^{\circ}}^{-1},1)
as characters of
\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
. Also, for
\mathbf{ss}
-pairs
(\chi_{1},1)
and
(\chi_{2},1)
, by
Equation 4.3
, we have

(4.5)

\langle\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\chi_{1},1),\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\chi_{2},1)\rangle={\langle(\chi_{1},1),(\chi_{2},1)\rangle}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}+{\langle(\chi_{1},1),({\chi_{2}^{\circ}}^{-1},1)\rangle}_{\mathrm{T}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}.

This gives

\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\chi_{1},1)\cong\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\chi_{2},1)\,\mathrm{if\,and\,only\,if}\,(\chi_{1},1)\in\{(\chi_{2},1),({\chi_{2}^{\circ}}^{-1},1)\}.

Therefore, the number of inequivalent irreducible representations of the form
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
is equal to

\frac{|\{\chi\in\widehat{\mathfrak{O}_{\ell}^{\times}}\mid 1+\pi^{\ell-1}{\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell}\nsubseteq\ker(\chi)\}|}{2}=\frac{(q-1)|\mathfrak{O}_{\ell}^{\times}|}{2q}=\frac{q^{2\ell-3}(q-1)^{2}(q+1)}{2}.

By
[
10
, Table 4.3 (Page-61)]
,
this is same as the total number of split semisimple representations of
\mathrm{GU}_{2}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
. Hence the converse of (2) follows for
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GU}_{2}
.
∎

4.4.
Alternate construction for split non-semisimple representations of
\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
,
\ell
odd.

In this section, we discuss an alternate construction for split non-semisimple representations of
\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
for odd
\ell
. For proofs of these results; see
[
4
, Section 3.3.3]
for
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GL}_{2}
and
[
10
, Section 4.H.2, part 3, Page-57]
for
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GU}_{2}
.
Let
A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\epsilon\pi\beta\\
\epsilon&\alpha\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak{o}_{{\ell_{1}}})
and the Serre lift
\tilde{A}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\tilde{\alpha}&\epsilon\pi\tilde{\beta}\\
\epsilon&\tilde{\alpha}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathfrak{g}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
and corresponding character
\psi_{A}
of
\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{2}}}
. Then
S_{A}=\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\tilde{A})\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{1}}}
is given by

S_{A}=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}x&\pi\tilde{\beta}y+\pi^{{\ell_{1}}}z\\
y&x+\pi^{{\ell_{1}}}w\end{bmatrix}\mid x,y,z,w\in R_{\ell}\right\}\cap\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell}).

Consider a normal subgroup
\mathrm{N}=\left\{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1+\pi^{{\ell_{1}}}x&\pi^{{\ell_{2}}}z\\
\pi^{{\ell_{1}}}y&1+\pi^{{\ell_{1}}}w\end{smallmatrix}\right]\mid x,y,z,w\in R_{\ell}\right\}\cap\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
of
S_{A}
. We can extend
\psi_{A}
to
\mathrm{N}
and since
N/\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{2}}}
is abelian, every character in
\mathrm{Irr}(N\mid\psi_{A})
is one dimensional. Define an extension
\psi_{\tilde{A}}^{\prime}
of
\psi_{A}
to
N
as follows:

\psi_{\tilde{A}}^{\prime}\left(\begin{bmatrix}1+\pi^{{\ell_{1}}}x&\pi^{{\ell_{2}}}z\\
\pi^{{\ell_{1}}}y&1+\pi^{{\ell_{1}}}w\end{bmatrix}\right)\coloneqq\psi\left(\pi^{{\ell_{1}}}\bm{tr}\left(\tilde{A}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}x&\pi z\\
y&w\end{smallmatrix}\right]-\frac{\pi^{{\ell_{1}}}}{2}\tilde{A}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}x&\pi z\\
y&w\end{smallmatrix}\right]^{2}\right)\right).

We can show that the stabilizer of
\psi_{\tilde{A}}^{\prime}
in
S_{A}
is
\mathrm{N}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\tilde{A})
. Since
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\tilde{A})
is abelian, we
can extend
\psi^{\prime}_{\tilde{A}}
to a character
\psi_{\tilde{A}}^{\prime\prime}
of
\mathrm{N}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\tilde{A})
and every character of
\mathrm{N}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\tilde{A})
lying above
\psi_{\tilde{A}}^{\prime}
is one dimensional. Using Clifford theory for the group
S_{A}
and its normal subgroup
N
having character
\psi_{\tilde{A}}^{\prime}
, we get that
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{N}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\tilde{A})}^{S_{A}}\psi_{\tilde{A}}^{\prime\prime}
is an irreducible representation of dimension
q
. Denote
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{N}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\tilde{A})}^{S_{A}}\psi_{\tilde{A}}^{\prime\prime}
by
\phi
. Then
\mathrm{Ind}_{S_{A}}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}{{\phi}}
is a split non-semisimple representation of
\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
and any split non-semisimple representation of
\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
lying above
\psi_{A}
is of the form
\mathrm{Ind}_{S_{A}}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}{\phi}\cong\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{N}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\tilde{A})}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}{\psi_{\tilde{A}}^{\prime\prime}}
for some lift
\tilde{A}
of
A
and some extension
\psi^{\prime\prime}_{\tilde{A}}
of
\psi^{\prime}_{\tilde{A}}
to the group
\mathrm{N}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}(\tilde{A}).

4.5.
Alternate construction for cuspidal representations of
\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
,
\ell
odd.

Let
A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&\epsilon\alpha\\
\epsilon&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathfrak{g}({\mathfrak{o}}_{{\ell_{1}}})
be a regular matrix with
\mathfrak{t}(A)=\mathbf{cus}
. Define
D^{\ell_{i}}(\tilde{A})\coloneqq(\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\tilde{A})\cap\mathrm{K}^{1})\mathrm{K}^{\ell_{i}}\text{ for }i\in\{1,2\}
.
The character
\psi_{A}
can be extended to
\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{2}}(\tilde{A})
, say
\widetilde{\psi_{A}}
. We have
\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{2}}(\tilde{A})\trianglelefteq\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{1}}(\tilde{A})
and every element of
\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{1}}(\tilde{A})
stabilizes
\widetilde{\psi_{A}}
.
By considering the bilinear form on
\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{1}}(\tilde{A})/\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{2}}(\tilde{A})
parallel to the one given in
Subsection 4.2
, we obtain a construction of irreducible representations of
\mathrm{G}({{\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell}})
lying above
\psi_{A}
. The difference in this case compared to the previous one is that the current bilinear form is non-degenerate.
The process of construction is depicted in the following diagram:

\displaystyle\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{2}}}\xrightarrow{ext}\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{2}}(\tilde{A})\xrightarrow{ext}J\xrightarrow{ind}\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{1}}(\tilde{A})\xrightarrow{ext}S_{A}\xrightarrow{ind}\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})

\displaystyle\psi_{A}\hskip 27.0pt\widetilde{\psi_{A}}\hskip 45.0pt\widetilde{\widetilde{\psi_{A}}}\hskip 40.0pt\theta\hskip 35.0pt\phi\hskip 37.0pt\rho

There exists a maximal isotropic group
J
of the above mentioned bilinear form which is normal in
\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{1}}(\tilde{A})
with index
q
.
The character
\widetilde{\psi_{A}}
extends to
J
. Let
\widetilde{\widetilde{\psi_{A}}}
denotes this extension, then the inertia group of
\widetilde{\widetilde{\psi_{A}}}
in
\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{1}}(\tilde{A})
is
J
itself. By the Heisenberg lift,
\theta=\mathrm{Ind}_{J}^{\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{1}}(\tilde{A})}(\widetilde{\widetilde{\psi_{A}}})
is a unique irreducible character of
\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{1}}(\tilde{A})
of degree
q
lying above
\widetilde{\psi_{A}}
. Now
\theta
is invariant under
S_{A}
and
\frac{S_{A}}{\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{1}}(\tilde{A})}
is a cyclic group. Hence we can extend
\theta
to a character
\phi
of
S_{A}
. By Clifford theory, the representation
\mathrm{Ind}_{S_{A}}^{\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}\phi
of
\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
is an irreducible cuspidal representation of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
lying above
\psi_{A}
. Moreover, every cuspidal representation of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
lying above
\psi_{A}
is of this form. For proofs see
[
4
, Section 3.3.2]
for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
and
[
10
, Section 4.H.2, Page-48]
for
\mathrm{GU}_{2}
. The following result is directly obtained from the above construction.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
A\in\mathfrak{g}({\mathfrak{o}_{{\ell_{1}}}})
be cuspidal and
H
be a subgroup of
\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{2}}(\tilde{A})
such that
\mathrm{K}^{\ell_{2}}\leq H\leq\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{2}}(\tilde{A}).
For
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\mathrm{Irr}(H\mid\psi_{A}),

\langle\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\phi_{1}),\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\phi_{2})\rangle\neq 0
if and only if
\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}.

5.
Results related to the construction of representations of
\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})

In this section, we list several results related to the construction as given in
Section 4
. While these may be well known to the experts but we could not find them explicitly stated in the literature. Therefore, for completeness, we include their statements and proofs. We use the notations of
Section 4
in this section.

Throughout this section, we assume
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell_{1}})
are regular matrices such that
A_{1}+A_{2}
is regular and

\mathfrak{t}(A_{1})=\mathfrak{t}(A_{2})=\mathbf{cus}.

For
\phi_{i}\in\mathrm{Irr}(S_{A_{i}}\mid\psi_{A_{i}})_{1\leq i\leq 2}
, let

\mathbf{W}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}):=\mathrm{Res}^{S_{A_{1}}}_{S_{A_{1}}\cap S_{A_{2}}}(\phi_{1})\otimes\mathrm{Res}^{S_{A_{2}}}_{S_{A_{1}}\cap S_{A_{2}}}(\phi_{2}).

We prove the following result in this section and this will be crucially used to prove
1.1
for
\Xi_{3}
(cuspidal tensor cuspidal case) in
Section 7
.

Theorem 5.1
.

The representation
\mathbf{W}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})

is multiplicity free.

We first include a few preliminary results that we require for the proof of
5.1
. Recall, for cuspidal
A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&\epsilon\alpha\\
\epsilon&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathfrak{g}({\mathfrak{o}}_{{\ell_{1}}})
, we defined
D^{\ell_{i}}(\tilde{A})
by
D^{\ell_{i}}(\tilde{A})=(\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\tilde{A})\cap\mathrm{K}^{1})\mathrm{K}^{\ell_{i}}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
in
Subsection 4.5
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\ell
be odd and
A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&\epsilon\alpha\\
\epsilon&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell_{1}})
be regular such that
\mathfrak{t}(A)=\mathbf{cus}.

For
\phi\in\mathrm{Irr}(S_{A}\mid\psi_{A}),
the character
\chi_{\phi}
of
\phi
satisfies the following:

(1)

\chi_{\phi}(g)=q\widetilde{\psi_{A}}(g)
for all
g\in\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{2}}(\tilde{A}),
where
\widetilde{\psi_{A}}\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{2}}(\tilde{A})\mid\psi_{A})
such that

\langle\mathrm{Res}^{S_{A}}_{\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{2}}(\tilde{A})}(\phi),\widetilde{\psi_{A}}\rangle\neq 0.

(2)

\chi_{\phi}(g)=0
for all
g\in\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{1}}(\tilde{A})\setminus\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{2}}(\tilde{A}).

(3)

|\chi_{\phi}(g)|=1
for all
g\in S_{A}\setminus\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{1}}(\tilde{A}).

Proof.

The proof of (1) and (2) follow from
Subsection 4.5
.
For (3), the result for
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GL}_{2}
, up to minor changes, was obtained in
[
4
, Lemma 5.7]
. We use their ideas to prove the result uniformly for both
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
and
\mathrm{GU}_{2}
.
Consider the representation
\Gamma
of
S_{A}
on the vector space
M_{q}(\mathbb{C})
defined by
\Gamma(g)(B)=\phi(g)B\phi(g)^{-1}
for
g\in S_{A}
and
B\in M_{q}(\mathbb{C}).

By direct computations with usual basis of
M_{q}(\mathbb{C}),
it is easy see that it’s character
\chi_{\Gamma}=\chi_{\phi}\overline{\chi_{\phi}}.

Therefore, to show (3), it is enough to prove that
\chi_{\Gamma}(g)=1
for all

g\in S_{A}\backslash\mathrm{Z}D^{{\ell_{1}}}(\tilde{A}).

From
Subsection 4.5
, we have
\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{Z}D^{{\ell_{1}}}(\tilde{A})}^{S_{A}}(\phi)
is irreducible. Therefore the
\mathbb{C}
-span of the set
\{\phi(h):h\in\mathrm{Z}D^{{\ell_{1}}}(\tilde{A})\}
is equal to
M_{q}(\mathbb{C}).
Let
\{h_{j}\mid j\in[1,q^{2}]\}\subseteq\mathrm{K}^{{\ell_{1}}}
be a set of coset representatives for
\mathrm{Z}D^{{\ell_{2}}}(\tilde{A})
in
\mathrm{Z}D^{{\ell_{1}}}(\tilde{A})
. Without loss of generality, assume that
h_{1}=\mathrm{I}.

We claim that for every
h\in\mathrm{Z}D^{{\ell_{1}}}(\tilde{A})
,
\phi(h)=\widetilde{\psi_{A}}(h_{j}^{-1}h)\phi(h_{j})
where
j\in[1,q^{2}]
such that
h\in h_{j}\mathrm{Z}D^{{\ell_{2}}}(\tilde{A}).
Note that
h=h_{j}(h_{j}^{-1}h)
and
h_{j}^{-1}h\in\mathrm{Z}D^{{\ell_{2}}}(\tilde{A}).

By (1), we have
\phi\left(h_{j}^{-1}h\right)=\widetilde{\psi_{A}}(h_{j}^{-1}h)\mathrm{I}.
Therefore
\phi(h)=\widetilde{\psi_{A}}(h_{j}^{-1}h)\phi(h_{j})
and hence the claim follows.
Note that the claim implies that the set
\{\phi(h_{j})\mid j\in[1,q^{2}]\}
is a generating set of
M_{q}(\mathbb{C})
. Since dimension of
M_{q}(\mathbb{C})
is
q^{2},
the set
\{\phi(h_{j})\mid j\in[1,q^{2}]\}
must form a
\mathbb{C}
-basis of
M_{q}(\mathbb{C})
.

Let
g\in S_{A}\backslash\mathrm{Z}D^{{\ell_{1}}}(\tilde{A}).
Then for
j\in[1,q^{2}],
we have
\Gamma(g)(\phi(h_{j}))=\phi(gh_{j}g^{-1}).
Since
gh_{j}g^{-1}\in\mathrm{Z}D^{{\ell_{1}}}(\tilde{A}),
by the claim, we must have
\Gamma(g)(\phi(h_{j}))=\widetilde{\psi_{A}}(h_{m_{j}}^{-1}gh_{j}g^{-1})\phi(h_{m_{j}})
where
m_{j}\in[1,q^{2}]
such that
gh_{j}g^{-1}\in h_{m_{j}}\mathrm{Z}D^{{\ell_{2}}}(\tilde{A}).
Therefore

(5.1)

\chi_{\Gamma}(g)=\sum_{j\in[1,q^{2}];m_{j}=j}\widetilde{\psi_{A}}(h_{j}^{-1}gh_{j}g^{-1}).

We claim that for
j\in[1,q^{2}],
if
h_{j}^{-1}gh_{j}g^{-1}\in\mathrm{Z}D^{{\ell_{2}}}(\tilde{A}),
then
h_{j}\in\mathrm{Z}D^{{\ell_{2}}}(\tilde{A})
(i.e,
j=1
and
h_{j}=\mathrm{I}
). By assuming the claim, from
Equation 5.1
, we obtain that
\chi_{\Gamma}(g)=\widetilde{\psi_{A}}(\mathrm{I})=1.
Hence (3) follows.

To show the claim,
let
h_{j}=\mathrm{I}+\pi^{{\ell_{1}}}C_{j}
for some matrix
C_{j}\in\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathfrak{O}_{\ell})
. Then

h_{j}^{-1}gh_{j}g^{-1}=\mathrm{I}+\pi^{{\ell_{1}}}(gC_{j}g^{-1}-C_{j})+\pi^{2{\ell_{1}}}(C_{j}^{2}-C_{j}gC_{j}g^{-1}).

Therefore, if
h_{j}^{-1}gh_{j}g^{-1}\in\mathrm{Z}D^{{\ell_{2}}}(\tilde{A}),
then

(5.2)

(gC_{j}g^{-1}-C_{j})\tilde{A}=\tilde{A}(gC_{j}g^{-1}-C_{j})\mod(\pi).

By multiplying both sides of
Equation 5.2
with
g
(from left) and
rearranging terms, we obtain that
g(C_{j}g^{-1}\tilde{A}g-\tilde{A}C_{j})=(C_{j}\tilde{A}-\tilde{A}C_{j})g\mod(\pi).

Since
g\in S_{A}\backslash\mathrm{Z}D^{{\ell_{1}}}(\tilde{A})
,
g=x\mathrm{I}+y\tilde{A}\mod(\pi)
for some
x\in R_{\ell}
,
y\in R_{\ell}^{\times}.
Therefore, we must have

(5.3)

\tilde{A}(C_{j}\tilde{A}-\tilde{A}C_{j})=(C_{j}\tilde{A}-\tilde{A}C_{j})\tilde{A}\mod(\pi).

Assume
C_{j}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right]
. Then

\displaystyle C_{j}\tilde{A}-\tilde{A}C_{j}

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}0&\epsilon\tilde{\alpha}\\
\epsilon&0\end{matrix}\right]-\left[\begin{matrix}0&\epsilon\tilde{\alpha}\\
\epsilon&0\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle=\epsilon\left[\begin{matrix}b-\tilde{\alpha}c&\tilde{\alpha}(a-d)\\
d-a&c\tilde{\alpha}-b\end{matrix}\right].

Since
\tilde{A}
is regular,
Equation 5.3
implies that
C_{j}\tilde{A}-\tilde{A}C_{j}=z\mathrm{I}+w\tilde{A}\mod(\pi)
for some
z,w\in R_{\ell}
. This along with
\tilde{\alpha}\in R_{\ell}^{\times}
gives,
b=\tilde{\alpha}c\mod(\pi)
and
a=d\mod(\pi),
i.e,
C=a\mathrm{I}+c\tilde{A}\mod(\pi)
.
This implies
C_{j}\tilde{A}=\tilde{A}C_{j}\mod(\pi),
which is equivalent
h_{j}\in\mathrm{Z}D^{{\ell_{2}}}(\tilde{A})
. Hence the claim.
∎

Define
t:=\max\{i\in[0,{\ell_{1}}]\mid A_{1}A_{2}=A_{2}A_{1}\mod(\pi^{i})\}
and

\Delta\coloneqq\begin{cases}1,&\text{for }\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GU}_{2};\\
-1,&\text{for }\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GL}_{2}.\end{cases}

Lemma 5.3
.

(1)

For
t<\ell_{1}
,
S_{A_{1}}\cap S_{A_{2}}=(\{x\mathrm{I}+\pi^{\ell_{1}-t}y\tilde{A_{1}}\mid x,y\in R_{\ell}\}\cap\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}))\mathrm{K}^{\ell_{1}}
and
|S_{A_{1}}\cap S_{A_{2}}|=(q+\Delta)q^{4{\ell_{2}}+{\ell_{1}}+t-1}.

(2)

For
t={\ell_{1}}
, we have
S_{A_{1}}\cap S_{A_{2}}=S_{A_{1}}=S_{A_{2}}
and

|S_{A_{1}}|=(q+1)(q+\Delta)q^{3\ell-1}.

Proof.

For (1), it is easy to see that
(\{x\mathrm{I}+\pi^{\ell_{1}-t}y\tilde{A_{1}}\mid x,y\in R_{\ell}\}\cap\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}))\mathrm{K}^{\ell_{1}}\subseteq S_{A_{1}}\cap S_{A_{2}}
. To prove the converse, let
g\in S_{A_{1}}\cap S_{A_{2}}
. Then
g=u\mathrm{I}+v\tilde{A_{1}}=zI+w\tilde{A_{2}}\mod(\pi^{{\ell_{1}}})
for some
u,v,z,w\in R_{\ell}
. This gives
v\tilde{A_{1}}=(z-u)\mathrm{I}+w\tilde{A_{2}}\mod(\pi^{{\ell_{1}}})
. Hence
v\tilde{A_{1}}
commutes with
\tilde{A_{2}}
modulo
(\pi^{\ell_{1}})

x\in R_{\ell}^{\times}
. For
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GU}_{2}
, we choose
x
to be a solution of the equation
xx^{\circ}=\frac{\lambda\lambda^{\circ}}{\lambda\lambda^{\circ}+\pi\tilde{\beta}}
. Using this choice of
x
and the relation
\lambda^{\circ}=-\lambda
, which follows from
g^{\prime}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&\lambda\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathrm{GU}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
, we can easily show that
X\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{GU}_{2}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\tilde{{A_{1}}})
and
(x\mathrm{I}+y\tilde{A_{2}})\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{GU}_{2}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\tilde{{A_{2}}}).
Therefore,
S_{A_{1}}g^{*}S_{A_{2}}=S_{A_{1}}g^{\prime}S_{A_{2}}
.

Next we assume that either
g_{12}\in\pi R_{\ell}
or
g_{22}\in\pi R_{\ell}.
Then we have
g_{12}g_{22}\in\pi R_{\ell}.

If
S_{A_{1}}g^{\prime}S_{A_{2}}=S_{A_{1}}g^{*}S_{A_{2}},
then
there exist
X=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}c&0\\
0&d\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\tilde{{A_{1}}})
and
(x\mathrm{I}+y\tilde{{A_{2}}})\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\tilde{{A_{2}}})
such that
Xg^{*}(x\mathrm{I}+y\tilde{{A_{2}}})=g^{\prime}\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}}).

By equating
(1,2)^{th}
entries of both sides, we obtain
cx=\frac{g_{12}g_{22}-\pi\tilde{\beta}g_{11}g_{21}}{{\mathrm{det}}(g)}\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}}).

Since
g_{12}g_{22}\in\pi R_{\ell}
and
c\in R_{\ell}^{\times},
we obtain
x\in\pi R_{\ell}.
Hence
(x\mathrm{I}+y\tilde{{A_{2}}})\notin\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}).
It is contradiction to the fact that
(x\mathrm{I}+y\tilde{{A_{2}}})\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\tilde{{A_{2}}})\subseteq\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}).
Therefore we must have
S_{A_{1}}g^{\prime}S_{A_{2}}\neq S_{A_{1}}g^{*}S_{A_{2}}.

∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
A\in\mathfrak{gl}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
be such that
\mathfrak{t}(A)=\mathbf{cus}
. Then
(x\mathrm{I}+yA)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
for all
x,y\in{\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell}
such that
\{x,y\}\cap{\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell}^{\times}\neq\emptyset.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}
such that
\{x,y\}\cap\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}^{\times}\neq\emptyset.
By direct calculations, we obtain that
{\mathrm{det}}(x\mathrm{I}+yA)=x^{2}+\bm{tr}(A)\,xy+{\mathrm{det}}(A)\,y^{2}.
If
y\notin\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}^{\times},
then
x\in\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}^{\times}
and
{\mathrm{det}}(x\mathrm{I}+yA)=x^{2}\mod(\pi).
Therefore
{\mathrm{det}}(x\mathrm{I}+yA)\in\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}^{\times}
, which gives
(x\mathrm{I}+yA)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}).
If
y\in\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}^{\times},
then
{\mathrm{det}}(x\mathrm{I}+yA)=y^{2}\left((\frac{x}{y})^{2}+\bm{tr}(A)(\frac{x}{y})+{\mathrm{det}}(A)\right).
Since
\mathfrak{t}(A)=\mathbf{cus}
, we must have
(\frac{x}{y})^{2}+\bm{tr}(A)(\frac{x}{y})+{\mathrm{det}}(A)\neq 0\mod(\pi).
Therefore
{\mathrm{det}}(x\mathrm{I}+yA)\in\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}^{\times}
, which gives
(x\mathrm{I}+yA)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}).

∎

Recall that the residue field is of odd characteristic. Therefore
\alpha\in(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}^{\times})^{2}
if and only if
\bar{\alpha}\in(\mathfrak{o}_{1}^{\times})^{2}.
We will use this fact without specifically mentioning it.

Lemma 6.5
.

Lemma 6.6

\displaystyle\tilde{A_{1}}+g_{i}\tilde{A_{2}}g_{i}^{-1}

Therefore, since

∎

and
\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{(S_{A_{1}}\cap S_{A_{2}}^{g_{2}})^{h}}(\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}^{g_{2}})^{h})
respectively. Hence, our assumption

implies

(7.3)

\langle\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{S_{A_{1}}\cap S_{A_{2}}^{g_{1}}}(\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}^{g_{1}})),\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})}(\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{(S_{A_{1}}\cap S_{A_{2}}^{g_{2}})^{h}}(\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}^{g_{2}})^{h})\rangle\neq 0.

Let
\eta_{i}\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{Z}D^{\ell_{2}}(\tilde{A}_{i})\mid\psi_{A_{i}})

\eta_{1}(Zh^{-1}Z^{-1}h)=\eta_{2}(g_{1}^{-1}Z^{-1}g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}h^{-1}Zhg_{2}),\text{ for all }Z\in H.

Proposition 7.5
.

Suppose that both
V(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}^{g_{1}})
and
V(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}^{g_{2}})
contain regular representations.
If
\langle V(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}^{g_{1}}),V(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}^{g_{2}})\rangle\neq 0,
then we must have
S_{A_{1}}g_{1}S_{A_{2}}=S_{A_{1}}g_{2}S_{A_{2}}.

Proof.

We will use
6.8
to prove our result. Both
V(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}^{g_{1}})
and
V(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}^{g_{2}})
contain regular representations, therefore both
\tilde{A}_{1}+g_{1}\tilde{A}_{2}g_{1}^{-1}
and
\tilde{A}_{1}+g_{2}\tilde{A}_{2}g_{2}^{-1}

are regular. Since
\langle V(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}^{g_{1}}),V(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}^{g_{2}})\rangle\neq 0,
both
\tilde{A}_{1}+g_{1}\tilde{A}_{2}g_{1}^{-1}
and
\tilde{A}_{1}+g_{2}\tilde{A}_{2}g_{2}^{-1}

are conjugate modulo
(\pi^{\ell_{1}})
. Therefore
{\mathrm{det}}(\tilde{A_{1}}+g_{1}\tilde{A}_{2}g_{1}^{-1})-{\mathrm{det}}(\tilde{A_{1}}+g_{2}\tilde{A}_{2}g_{2}^{-1})\in\pi^{\ell_{1}}R_{\ell}.

For
D=D(\tilde{\alpha_{1}},\tilde{\alpha_{2}},g_{1},g_{2})
,
by
6.5
, we have
{\mathrm{det}}(\tilde{A_{1}}+g_{1}\tilde{A}_{2}g_{1}^{-1})-{\mathrm{det}}(\tilde{A_{1}}+g_{2}\tilde{A}_{2}g_{2}^{-1})=\frac{\epsilon}{a_{1}a_{2}c_{1}c_{2}}\times{\mathrm{det}}(D)
and hence
{\mathrm{det}}(D)=0\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}})
.

If
D=0\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}}),
then

D\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\right]=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\right]\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}})
and hence, by
6.8
,
S_{A_{1}}g_{1}S_{A_{2}}=S_{A_{1}}g_{2}S_{A_{2}}.

Assume
D\neq 0\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}}).

Let
0\leq k<\ell_{1}
be such that
D=\pi^{k}D^{\prime}
for some
D^{\prime}\in M_{2}(R_{\ell})
with
D^{\prime}\neq 0\mod(\pi).
We first claim that

{\mathrm{det}}(D)=0\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}+k}).

If
k=0,
since
{\mathrm{det}}(D)=0\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}}),
the claim follows trivially.
Assume
1\leq k<{\ell_{1}}.

Let

h^{\prime}=\left[\begin{matrix}1+a_{2}^{-1}c_{2}&a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}-a_{2}^{-1}b_{2}\\
0&1+a_{1}^{-1}c_{1}\end{matrix}\right].

Since

and

for

, we have

Hence

is an invertible matrix.
For
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GL}_{2},
let
h=h^{\prime}.

For
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GU}_{2}
, by using
g_{i}\in\mathrm{GU}_{2}
and the relations for

and

, we get

h^{\prime}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1+c_{2}^{\circ}c_{2}&\epsilon(t_{1}-t_{2})\\
0&1+c_{1}^{\circ}c_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right].

Choose

such that
dd^{\circ}=(1+c_{1}^{\circ}c_{1})(1+c_{2}^{\circ}c_{2}).
Let

. Note that

By direct calculation, we obtain the following.

(7.6)

h(\tilde{A}_{1}+g_{2}\tilde{A}_{2}g_{2}^{-1})h^{-1}-(\tilde{A}_{1}+g_{1}\tilde{A}_{2}g_{1}^{-1})=\frac{{\mathrm{det}}(D)}{(1+a_{1}^{-1}c_{1})a_{1}a_{2}c_{1}c_{2}}\left[\begin{matrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right].

Therefore,
h(\tilde{A}_{1}+g_{2}\tilde{A}_{2}g_{2}^{-1})h^{-1}=\tilde{A}_{1}+g_{1}\tilde{A}_{2}g_{1}^{-1}\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}}).

Since
D=0\mod(\pi^{k}),
by
6.6
, we obtain that
h^{\prime}=(1+a_{2}^{-1}c_{2})\mathrm{I}\mod(\pi^{k}),

g_{2}=\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}g_{1}\mod(\pi^{k}),

g_{1}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a_{1}&0\\
0&c_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\mod(\pi^{k})
and
\tilde{\alpha}_{2}=a_{1}^{-2}c_{1}^{2}\tilde{\alpha}_{1}\mod(\pi^{k}).

Therefore
h^{-1}\tilde{A}_{1}h=h^{\prime-1}\tilde{A}_{1}h^{\prime}=\tilde{A}_{1}\mod(\pi^{k})

\psi\left(\pi^{\ell_{2}-k}w\,\bm{tr}\left(\left(h(\tilde{A}_{1}+g_{2}\tilde{A}_{2}g_{2}^{-1})h^{-1}-(\tilde{A}_{1}+g_{1}\tilde{A}_{2}g_{1}^{-1})\right)\tilde{A}_{1}\right)\right)=1\text{ for all }w\in\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}.

Therefore
by substituting
Equation 7.6

Recall that
\pi^{\ell-1}\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}\not\subseteq\ker(\psi).

. By

, we obtain that

\frac{\epsilon\,{\mathrm{det}}(D)}{(1+a_{1}^{-1}c_{1})a_{1}a_{2}c_{1}c_{2}}=\frac{\epsilon^{2}\,\lambda}{(1+(a_{1}a_{1}^{\circ})^{-1})a_{1}a_{2}a_{1}^{\circ}a_{2}^{\circ}}\in\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}.
Hence

implies

\pi^{\ell_{2}-k}{\mathrm{det}}(D)=0,

We now proceed to show that there exist

-D_{m1}^{\prime}\end{matrix}\right]=\begin{cases}\pi^{k}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
-{\mathrm{det}}(D^{\prime})\end{smallmatrix}\right],&\text{if }m=1;\\
\pi^{k}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathrm{det}}(D^{\prime})\\

Since
\pi^{2k}{\mathrm{det}}(D^{\prime})={\mathrm{det}}(D)=0\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}+k}),
we must have
\pi^{k}{\mathrm{det}}(D^{\prime})=0\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}}).
Therefore
D\left[\begin{smallmatrix}x\\

Hence the result follows from

.

,

Lemma 8.2

\{S_{A}gS_{A}\mid g\in\mathcal{X}_{i}\}\cap\{S_{A}hS_{A}\mid h\in\mathcal{X}_{j}\}=\emptyset.

(2)

For
k\in\{1,2\}
, let
g_{k}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a_{k}&\pi^{i}b_{k}\\
0&c_{k}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathcal{X}_{i}.
Then
S_{A}g_{1}S_{A}=S_{A}g_{2}S_{A}
if and only if
\pi^{i}a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}=\pi^{i}a_{2}^{-1}b_{2}\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}})
and
a_{1}^{-1}c_{1}=a_{2}^{-1}c_{2}\mod(\pi^{i}).

(3)

|\{S_{A}gS_{A}\mid g\in\mathcal{X}_{i}\}|=\begin{cases}(q-1)(q-2)q^{\ell_{1}-2}_{\,,}&\mathrm{if}\,i<\ell_{1};\\
(q-2)q^{\ell_{1}-1}_{\,,}&\mathrm{if}\,i=\ell_{1}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Note that
S_{A}=\left\{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}x&\pi^{{\ell_{1}}}y\\
z&x+\pi^{{\ell_{1}}}w\end{smallmatrix}\right]\mid x,y,z,w\in R_{\ell}\right\}\cap\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}).

Therefore, for
g\in\mathcal{X}_{i},
it is easy to see that
(1,2)^{\mathrm{th}}
entry of any
X\in S_{A}gS_{A}
is in
\pi^{i}R_{\ell}^{\times}+\pi^{{\ell_{1}}}R_{\ell}.
This implies (1).

To show (2), let
S_{A}g_{1}S_{A}=S_{A}g_{2}S_{A}
. Then there exist
x_{1},x_{2}\in R_{\ell}^{\times}
and
z_{1},z_{2}\in R_{\ell}
such that
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}x_{1}&0\\
z_{1}&x_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]g_{1}=g_{2}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}x_{2}&0\\
z_{2}&x_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}})
.
This gives

(9.1)

\left[\begin{matrix}a_{1}x_{1}-a_{2}x_{2}-\pi^{i}b_{2}z_{2}&\pi^{i}\left(b_{1}x_{1}-b_{2}x_{2}\right)\\
a_{1}z_{1}-c_{2}z_{2}&c_{1}x_{1}-c_{2}x_{2}+\pi^{i}b_{1}z_{1}\end{matrix}\right]=0\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}})

Equating the
(1,1)^{\mathrm{th}}
entry on both sides of
Equation 9.1
,
we obtain that
x_{1}=a_{1}^{-1}a_{2}x_{2}\mod(\pi^{i}).

Since
2i\geq{\ell_{1}}
,
by substituting this value of
x_{1}
into the second column on the left-hand side of
Equation 9.1
and simplifying, we obtain

\pi^{i}a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}=\pi^{i}a_{2}^{-1}b_{2}\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}})
and
a_{1}^{-1}c_{1}=a_{2}^{-1}c_{2}\mod(\pi^{i}).
To prove the converse, let

\pi^{i}a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}=\pi^{i}a_{2}^{-1}b_{2}\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}})
and
a_{1}^{-1}c_{1}=a_{2}^{-1}c_{2}\mod(\pi^{i}).

If
i={\ell_{1}}
, then
g_{1}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a_{1}^{-1}a_{2}&0\\
0&c_{1}^{-1}c_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right]=g_{2}\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}})
. It is straightforward to see that
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a_{1}^{-1}a_{2}&0\\
0&c_{1}^{-1}c_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in S_{A}
. Therefore
S_{A}g_{1}S_{A}=S_{A}g_{2}S_{A}
for
i={\ell_{1}}
. Let
i<{\ell_{1}}
. Then we have
a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}=a_{2}^{-1}b_{2}\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}-i})
, and hence
a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}+a_{2}^{-1}b_{2}\in R_{\ell}^{\times}.
For
i\in\{1,2\}
, since
g_{i}\in\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
, we have
a_{i}^{-1}c_{i}\in\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}
and
a_{i}^{-1}b_{i}\in\epsilon\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}
. Therefore

\frac{a_{2}^{-1}c_{2}-a_{1}^{-1}c_{1}}{a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}+a_{2}^{-1}b_{2}}=\pi^{i}\epsilon d
for some
d\in\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}
.
Let
X=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
\epsilon d&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]
and
Y=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}-\frac{\pi^{i}\epsilon da_{1}b_{2}}{a_{2}c_{2}}&0\\
\frac{a_{1}d\epsilon}{c_{2}}&\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}-\frac{\pi^{i}\epsilon da_{1}b_{2}}{a_{2}c_{2}}\end{smallmatrix}\right].

By direct calculation, we have

Xg_{1}-g_{2}Y=\left[\begin{matrix}0&\pi^{i}\left(b_{1}-\frac{a_{1}b_{2}}{a_{2}}\right)\\
0&c_{1}-\frac{a_{1}c_{2}}{a_{2}}+\pi^{i}\epsilon d\left(\frac{a_{1}b_{2}}{a_{2}}+b_{1}\right)\end{matrix}\right]=0\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}}).

Note that
X\in\mathrm{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
, and hence
g_{2}^{-1}Xg_{1}\in\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}).

Since
Y=g_{2}^{-1}Xg_{1}\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}})
and the map
\rho_{\ell,{\ell_{1}}}:\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})\rightarrow\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell_{1}})
is a projection, there exists
Z\in M_{2}(R_{\ell})
such that
Y+\pi^{\ell_{1}}Z\in\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}).

Note that
Y+\pi^{\ell_{1}}Z\in S_{A}
and
Xg_{1}=g_{2}(Y+\pi^{\ell_{1}}Z)\mod(\pi^{\ell_{1}}).
Therefore
S_{A}g_{1}S_{A}=S_{A}g_{2}S_{A}.

To show (3),
let

\mathcal{D}_{i}=\{(\mathrm{Proj}_{\ell_{1}}(\pi^{i}a^{-1}b),\mathrm{Proj}_{i}(a^{-1}c))\in R_{\ell_{1}}\times R_{i}\mid\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\pi^{i}b\\
0&c\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathcal{X}_{i}\},

where
\mathrm{Proj}_{\ell_{1}}:R_{\ell}\rightarrow R_{\ell_{1}}
and
\mathrm{Proj}_{i}:R_{\ell}\rightarrow R_{i}
are canonical projections. From (2), we obtain

|\{S_{A}gS_{A}\mid g\in\mathcal{X}_{i}\}|=|\mathcal{D}_{i}|.
For
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GL}_{2},
we have

\mathcal{D}_{i}=\{(\mathrm{Proj}_{\ell_{1}}(\pi^{i}d),\mathrm{Proj}_{i}(e))\mid d\in\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}^{\times},e\in\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}^{\times}\setminus(-1+\pi\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})\}.

Therefore, for
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GL}_{2}
, we obtain that

|\{S_{A}gS_{A}\mid g\in\mathcal{X}_{i}\}|=\begin{cases}(q-1)(q-2)q^{\ell_{1}-2}_{\,,}&\mathrm{if}\,i<\ell_{1};\\
(q-2)q^{\ell_{1}-1}_{\,,}&\mathrm{if}\,i=\ell_{1}.\end{cases}

We next consider
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GU}_{2}.
For this case,

\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\pi^{i}b\\
0&c\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathcal{X}_{i}
if and only if
a,b,c\in\mathfrak{O}_{\ell}^{\times}
with
a^{-1}=c^{\circ}
,
a^{-1}b\in\epsilon\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}^{\times}
and
a+c\in\mathfrak{O}_{\ell}^{\times}
. We also have
\{c^{\circ}c\mid c\in\mathfrak{O}_{\ell}^{\times}\,\mathrm{with}\,c^{\circ}c+1\in\mathfrak{O}_{\ell}^{\times}\}=\mathfrak{o}^{\times}_{\ell}\setminus(-1+\pi\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}).

Therefore

\mathcal{D}_{i}=\{(\mathrm{Proj}_{\ell_{1}}(\pi^{i}d),\mathrm{Proj}_{i}(e))\mid d\in\epsilon\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}^{\times},e\in\mathfrak{o}_{\ell}^{\times}\setminus(-1+\pi\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})\}.

Using
|\{S_{A}gS_{A}\mid g\in\mathcal{X}_{i}\}|=|\mathcal{D}_{i}|,
the result follows for
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GU}_{2}
also.
∎

Theorem 9.2
.

Let
A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\

there exists
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{G}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})\mid\psi_{A})

\langle\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2},\rho\rangle\geq(q-2)q^{\lfloor\ell_{1}/2\rfloor+\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}-1}.

For its proof, we require the following general result.

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of a finite group
G.
Suppose
\theta
and
\chi
are representations of
G
and
H
respectively such that

\{\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)\mid\langle\rho,\theta\rangle\neq 0\}\subseteq\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid\chi).
Then there exists a representation
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid\chi)
such that
\langle\rho,\theta\rangle\geq\frac{\dim(\theta)}{\dim(\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\chi))}.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid\chi)=\{\rho_{1},\rho_{2},...,\rho_{t}\}
and
m_{k}=\langle\theta,\rho_{k}\rangle
for
k\in[1,t]
. Note that
\sum_{1\leq k\leq t}\dim(\rho_{k})\leq\dim(\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\chi)).
Since
\{\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)\mid\langle\rho,\theta\rangle\neq 0\}\subseteq\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid\chi),
we also have
\dim(\theta)=\sum_{1\leq k\leq t}m_{k}\dim(\rho_{k}).

To show the result, it is enough to prove that
m^{*}:=\mathrm{max}\{m_{k}\mid k\in[1,t]\}
satisfies
m^{*}\geq\frac{\dim(\theta)}{\dim(\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\chi))}.
This directly follows from the following:

m^{*}\dim(\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\chi))\geq m^{*}\sum_{1\leq k\leq t}\dim(\rho_{k})\geq\sum_{1\leq k\leq t}m_{k}\dim(\rho_{k})=\dim(\theta).

This completes the proof.
∎

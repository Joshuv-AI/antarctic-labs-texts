Orthogonality of spin q q -Whittaker polynomials
Matteo Mucciconi

Abstract
. The inhomogeneous spin q q -Whittaker polynomials are a family of symmetric polynomials which generalize the Macdonald polynomials at t = 0 t=0 . In this paper we prove that they are orthogonal with respect to a variant of the Sklyanin measure on the n n dimensional torus and as a result they form a basis of the space of symmetric polynomials in n n variables. Instrumental to the proof are inhomogeneous eigenrelations, which partially generalize those of Macdonald polynomials. We also consider several special cases of the inhomogeneous spin q q -Whittaker polynomials, which include variants of symmetric Grothendieck polynomials or spin Whittaker functions.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Context

The spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials are a family of symmetric polynomials that can be viewed as a multi-parameter generalization of the
q
-Whittaker functions, which themselves arise as a specialization of the well-known Macdonald polynomials. They were introduced by Borodin and Wheeler
[
BW21
]
in connection with integrable lattice models and have been progressively generalized in
[
MP22
,
BK24
]
. In particular, they admit a combinatorial definition as partition functions of integrable vertex models, with Boltzmann weights given (in the most general form) by matrix elements of certain intertwining operators between tensor products of representations of the affine quantum group
U_{q}^{\prime}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{n})

[
Kor24
]
. The Yang–Baxter-type relations satisfied by the vertex weights give rise to many remarkable properties of these polynomials, including Cauchy identities, Pieri rules, and integral representations. Such symmetries connect the spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials to solvable particle systems
[
BMP21
,
MP22
]
, of which they describe the joint probability distribution; see also
[
Kor22
]
. Recently, Korotkikh
[
Kor24
]
provided a characterization of the inhomogeneous spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials, proving that they are the unique symmetric polynomials satisfying a certain family of vanishing conditions; such characterization gives an alternative definition that bypasses the lattice model construction.

In this paper, we prove that the inhomogeneous spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials are self-orthogonal with respect to a scalar product given by an explicit measure on the
n
-dimensional complex torus; see
Theorem

1.1
. This scalar product is a multi-parameter extension of the “torus scalar product” that makes the (classical)
q
-Whittaker polynomials orthogonal
[
Mac95
]
, and its density can be viewed as a
q
-deformation of the Sklyanin measure
[
Skl85
]
. Our result, conjectured in a special case in
[
MP22
]
, is in our view rather striking: while symmetries and Cauchy identities follow relatively directly from the underlying Yang–Baxter equation, more subtle properties such as linear independence or orthogonality typically demand different arguments.

The spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials are closely related to another family of symmetric functions, the spin Hall–Littlewood functions
[
GdGW17
,
Bor17
,
BP18
]
. Both families admit integrable-lattice-model definitions and satisfy variants of the Cauchy identities
[
BW21
,
BK24
]
. Moreover, the spin Hall–Littlewood functions are themselves orthogonal under a similar torus scalar product; see
Proposition

2.5
. This orthogonality is deeply connected to the fact that the spin Hall–Littlewood functions form a complete set of Bethe Ansatz eigenfunctions for various self-adjoint operators, including the XXZ spin chain Hamiltonian
[
BCPS15
]
. However, it remains unclear whether the orthogonality of the spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials (which we prove in
Theorem

1.1
) is of the same nature as that of their spin Hall–Littlewood counterparts.

A classical special case of our orthogonality result is the orthogonality of the Schur polynomials, viewed as the characters of the unitary group
U(n)
; see, e.g.,
[
Bum13
]
. Another notable specialization is the orthogonality of the (Archimedean)
GL(n)
Whittaker functions
[
Jac67
,
Kos78
]
, which is closely connected to integrable Toda lattice theories and can be seen as a manifestation of the Harish-Chandra Plancherel theorem
[
STS94
,
KL01
]
. A number of
q
-deformations of these classical results have been achieved in
[
Rui90
,
Eti99
,
GLO10
,
GLO12
]
; see also the more recent survey
[
STS23
]
. Nevertheless, to our knowledge no alternative proof for the orthogonality of
q
-Whittaker case has appeared in the literature. In Macdonald’s original treatment
[
Mac95
]
, the “torus” orthogonality property follows quite directly from the eigenvalue relations satisfied by Macdonald polynomials, making the derivation relatively straightforward. In the case of spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials, we establish analogous eigenrelations below (see Section
3
), but these alone do not suffice to characterize the polynomials: indeed, the eigenvalues depend only on the first and last parts of the indexing partitions. Thus the orthogonality of spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials does
not
simply reduce to these eigenrelations, and additional ideas are required. One may speculate that a
t
-deformation of the spin
q
-Whittaker theory could potentially reconcile these issues by bringing it closer to the full Macdonald framework, although no such general theory yet exists.

1.2.
Inhomogeneous spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials

In this paper we will deal with multivariate and multi-parametric functions. To denote lists of variables and parameters, we will use boldface font notation as

(1.1)

\boldsymbol{z}_{N}=(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{N}).

For instance for a function of
N
variables
f(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})
we will use the shorthand
f(\boldsymbol{z}_{N})
. We will also use the convention that

(1.2)

\overline{z}=z^{-1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{N}=(\overline{z}_{1},\dots,\overline{z}_{N})=(z_{1}^{-1},\dots,z_{N}^{-1})

For a sequence of parameters
\boldsymbol{z}_{N}
we define the shift operator
\tau
, which drops the first element of the sequence, as

(1.3)

\tau\boldsymbol{z}_{N}=(z_{2},\dots,z_{N})

and naturally, for any
k\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}
, we set

(1.4)

\tau^{k}\boldsymbol{z}_{N}=(z_{k+1},\dots,z_{N}).

At times it will be convenient to not specify the length of a sequence, which we may think as infinite, in which case we drop the subscript from the notation
\boldsymbol{z}=(z_{1},z_{2},\dots)
. Throughout the text, the lists of complex parameters
\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}
will be called
inhomogeneity parameters
and will always be assumed to satisfy

(1.5)

|a_{i}|,|b_{i}|<1\qquad\text{for all }i\geq 1.

The conditions in (
1.5
) could be relaxed in various results of this paper; however, for the sake of a cleaner presentation, we have opted not to pursue the most general form.

The families of symmetric function with which we deal are often labelled by integer partitions, whose set is denoted by
\mathbb{Y}
. We also define
\mathbb{Y}_{n}
to be the subset of integer partitions of length at most
n
. The next definition uses the notion of
q
-Pochhammer symbols and interlacing partitions which we recall below in
Section

1.5
.

Definition 1.1
.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}
and
\mu\in\mathbb{Y}_{n-1}
, define the skew spin
q
-Whittaker polynomial (á la Borodin-Korotkikh
[
BK24
]
)

(1.6)

\mathbb{F}_{\lambda/\mu}(x|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})=\mathbf{1}_{\mu\preceq\lambda}x^{|\lambda|-|\mu|}\prod_{r=1}^{n}\frac{(a_{r}/x;q)_{\lambda_{r}-\mu_{r}}(xb_{r};q)_{\mu_{r}-\lambda_{r+1}}(q;q)_{\lambda_{r}-\lambda_{r+1}}}{(q;q)_{\lambda_{r}-\mu_{r}}(q;q)_{\mu_{r}-\lambda_{r+1}}(a_{r}b_{r};q)_{\lambda_{r}-\lambda_{r+1}}}.

We define the
n
-variate spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials through branching rules

(1.7)

\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})=\sum_{\mu\preceq\lambda}\mathbb{F}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n-1}|\tau\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\mathbb{F}_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n}).

Remark 1.1
.

The polynomials
\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})
are symmetric in the variables
\boldsymbol{x}_{n}
as proven in
[
BK24
]
; see
Remark

2.1
to match the above definition with that used in the paper
[
BK24
]
.

Remark 1.2
.

The polynomials
\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}
also have an
n
-variate skew variant,
\mathbb{F}_{\lambda/\mu}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})
, which can be defined adapting the branching rule (
1.7
); see
[
BK24
]
. However, since the main results of this paper do not apply to them, we do not discuss them further.

In this paper, rather than the Borodin-Korotkikh polynomials
\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}
, we will work with a variant of the spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials defined next.

Definition 1.2
.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}
and
\mu\in\mathbb{Y}_{n-1}
, define

(1.8)

\mathbb{f}_{\lambda/\mu}(x|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\mathbb{F}_{\lambda/\mu}(x|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})\big|_{a_{n}=0}

and

(1.9)

\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})\big|_{a_{n}=0}.

Remark 1.3
.

Notice that
\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})\big|_{a_{n}=0}
, as a function of
\boldsymbol{b}_{n}
only depends of
b_{1},\dots,b_{n-1}
, justifying the notation adopted for the functions
\mathbb{f}
. This is because the
n
-th term in the product in the right hand side of (
1.6
) is

(1.10)

\frac{(a_{n}/x;q)_{\lambda_{n}-\mu_{n}}(xb_{n};q)_{\mu_{n}-\lambda_{n+1}}(q;q)_{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n+1}}}{(q;q)_{\lambda_{n}-\mu_{n}}(q;q)_{\mu_{n}-\lambda_{n+1}}(a_{n}b_{n};q)_{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n+1}}}=\frac{(a_{n}/x;q)_{\lambda_{n}}}{(a_{n}b_{n};q)_{\lambda_{n}}},

since, by assumptions,
\mu_{n}=\lambda_{n+1}=0
.

At first glance, the
Definition

1.2
of the functions
\mathbb{f}
might seem irrelevant. However, setting the inhomogeneity parameter
a_{n}
to zero endows the
n
-variate spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials with additional symmetries and desirable properties. In this paper, we collect several such properties, including the
shift property
(
Proposition

2.2
), the
reverse symmetry
(
Proposition

2.3
), and the eigenrelations (
Theorem

3.1
,
Theorem

3.2
).
We will also see, in
Proposition

2.1
, that one can recover the polynomials
\mathbb{F}
as a special case of
\mathbb{f}
as

(1.11)

\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})=\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n+1}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})\big|_{x_{n+1}=0},

so neither family of polynomials
\{\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\}
,
\{\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})\}
is more general than the other if we allow the parameter
n
counting the number of variables to vary.

1.3.
Main results

Let
\mathbb{T}
denote the complex circle
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
. For
\boldsymbol{z}_{n}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
and parameters
\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}
, as in (
1.5
) we define the function

(1.12)

\Delta(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\frac{H_{q}(\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{n};\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(a_{k}b_{k};q)_{\infty}}{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})}\Delta(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}),

where

(1.13)

H_{q}(\boldsymbol{x}_{N};\boldsymbol{y}_{M})=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{M}\frac{1}{(x_{i}y_{j};q)_{\infty}}

and

(1.14)

\Delta(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})=\frac{1}{n!}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}(z_{i}/z_{j};q)_{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z_{i}}.

The function
\Delta(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})
is the density function of a measure on the
n
-dimensional torus and we denote the corresponding scalar product as

(1.15)

\llangle f\,|\,g\rrangle_{n}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}f(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})g(\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{n})\Delta(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\mathrm{d}z_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}z_{n}.

With a slight abuse of notation we will at times keep the dependence on the variables
\boldsymbol{z}_{n}
of functions
f,g
as

(1.16)

\llangle f(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})\,|\,g(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})\rrangle_{n}=\llangle f\,|\,g\rrangle_{n}.

Since the torus integration variables will always be denoted by
\boldsymbol{z}
, we hope that this causes no confusion.

Our main result states that the spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials
\mathbb{f}
are self-orthogonal with respect to the scalar product
\llangle\,|\,\rrangle
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For all
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}
, we have

(1.17)

\left\llangle\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\middle|\mathbb{f}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\right\rrangle_{n}=\mathbf{1}_{\lambda=\mu}\mathsf{c}_{n}(\lambda|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}),

where

(1.18)

\mathsf{c}_{n}(\lambda|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{(a_{k}b_{k};q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\frac{(q;q)_{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k+1}}}{(a_{k}b_{k};q)_{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k+1}}}.

Immediate important consequences of
Theorem

1.1
are given next. In the following we denote by
\mathsf{Sym}_{n}
the linear space of symmetric polynomials in
n
variables.

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The family of symmetric polynomials
\{\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}}
is a linear basis of
\mathsf{Sym}_{n}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The family of symmetric polynomials
\{\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}}
is a linear basis of
\mathsf{Sym}_{n}
.

Remark 1.4
.

In
[
Kor24
]
, the polynomials
\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})
were shown to be the the unique family of symmetric polynomials satisfying certain vanishing conditions. As a result they were proven to be a basis of the space

(1.19)

\mathrm{Span}\left\{G_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})\right\}_{\mu\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}},\qquad\text{with}\qquad G_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{r\geq 1}\frac{(x_{i}b_{r};q)_{\mu_{r}-\mu_{r+1}}}{(a_{i}b_{r};q)_{\mu_{r}-\mu_{r+1}}}.

Such result would give an alternative proof of
Theorem

1.2
and
Theorem

1.3
(but not of
Theorem

1.1
).

In
Section

5
we consider special cases of the spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials and the corresponding specialization of
Theorem

1.1
. Setting
q=a_{1}=\cdots=a_{n-1}=0
, the polynomials
\mathbb{f}
become an inhomogeneous variant of the symmetric Grothendieck polynomials, which arise in the study of K-theory of Grassmannians
[
LS82
,
FK94
,
Buc02
]
. In this case the orthogonality relation (
1.17
) turns into a torus scalar product orthonormality for families of symmetric Grothendieck polynomials, which appears to be new; see
Theorem

5.1
.

1.5.
Notation

We have already defined the set of integer partitions
\mathbb{Y}
and the set of integer partitions with length at most
n
denoted by
\mathbb{Y}_{n}
. A partition
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}
can be thought as a list
\lambda=\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}\geq 0
or as a list of arbitrary length appending a number of
0
’s after the
n
-th entry. We will denote

(1.20)

|\lambda|=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots.

The interlacing relation
\preceq
between partitions is defined as

(1.21)

\mu\preceq\lambda\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad\lambda_{i}\geq\mu_{i}\geq\lambda_{i+1}\qquad\text{for all }i\geq 1.

If
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}
, its transposed partition is denoted by
\lambda^{\prime}
and it is defined as
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}=\#\{j:\lambda_{j}\geq i\}
. We will also use the notion of
boxed partitions
, which are partitions
\lambda
with bounded length and bounded first row.
For any
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
we set

(1.22)

\mathrm{Box}(\ell,m)=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}:\lambda_{1}\leq\ell\,\text{and }\,\ell(\lambda)\leq m\right\}.

In this paper, integer partitions are (totally) ordered according to the reverse lexicographic order; thus, we write
\mu\leq\lambda
if and only if
\mu=\lambda
or if the first non zero difference
\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}
is positive.

Throughout,
q
is a complex parameter such that
|q|<1
. The
q
-Pochhammer symbols are defined as

(1.23)

(z;q)_{n}=\begin{cases}\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(1-zq^{i})\qquad&\text{for }n>0,\\
1\qquad&\text{for }n=0,\\
\prod_{i=n}^{-1}(1-zq^{i})^{-1}\qquad&\text{for }n<0\end{cases}

and the above definition extends to the case when
n=+\infty
.

2.
Symmetric functions

In the text, at time, we will use the Macdonald polynomials
[
Mac95
, VI]
commonly denoted by
P_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};q,t)
and
Q_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};q,t)
, which depend on a set of
n
variables
\boldsymbol{x}_{n}
and two parameters
t,q
such that
|t|,|q|<1
. Setting
t=0
we recover the
q
-Whittaker polynomials
P_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};q,0)
and
Q_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};q,0)
. Setting
q=0
we recover the Hall-Littlwood polynomials
P_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};0,t)
and
Q_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};0,t)
, although below we will still use
q
in place of
t
to denote their deformation parameter.

2.1.
Properties of inhomogeneous spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials

In
Section

1.2
we defined the two families of spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials
\mathbb{F}
and
\mathbb{f}
. Since in the text we will make use of results in literature, let us compare our definition of the functions
\mathbb{F}
and
\mathbb{f}
with those of
[
BK24
,
Kor24
]
.

Remark 2.1
.

The functions
\mathbb{F}
correspond to those in
[
Kor24
]
, which we denote here as
\mathbb{F}^{\mathrm{Kor}}
, by

(2.1)

\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\overline{\boldsymbol{b}}_{n})=\left(\prod_{r\geq 1}b_{r}^{\lambda_{r+1}}\frac{(q;q)_{\lambda_{r}-\lambda_{r+1}}}{(a_{r}b_{r};q)_{\lambda_{r}-\lambda_{r+1}}}\right)\mathbb{F}^{\mathrm{Kor}}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|(a_{0},a_{1},\dots),(b_{0},b_{1},\dots))\big|_{b_{0}=1}.

They can also be matched with those in
[
BK24
]
, which we denote here as
\mathbb{F}^{\mathrm{BK}}
, by

(2.2)

\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\overline{\boldsymbol{b}}_{n})\big|_{a_{i}=\xi_{i}s_{i},\,b_{i}=\xi_{i}/s_{i}}=(-1)^{|\lambda|}\left(\prod_{i\geq 1}\xi_{i}^{\lambda_{i+1}}\right)\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{BK}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\Xi,\mathsf{S})\big|_{\xi_{0}=1}.

As stated in
Section

1.2
, one can recover the family of polynomials
\mathbb{F}
as a special case of the family
\mathbb{f}
as prescribed in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

We have

(2.3)

\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})=\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n+1}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})\big|_{x_{i}=0},

for any
i=1,\dots,n+1
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove (
2.3
) for
i=1
, since the polynomial
\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n+1}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})
is symmetric in the
\boldsymbol{x}_{n+1}
variables.
Notice that we can isolate the factors depending on the inhomogeneities
a_{n}
(and
b_{n}
) in the expression of the skew spin
q
-Whittaker polynomial (
1.6
) as

(2.4)

\mathbb{F}_{\lambda/\mu}(x|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})=\mathbb{f}_{\lambda/\mu}(x|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\frac{(a_{n}/x;q)_{\lambda_{n}}}{(a_{n}b_{n};q)_{\lambda_{n}}}

and therefore the branching factors
\mathbb{F}_{\lambda/\mu}(x|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})
and
\mathbb{f}_{\lambda/\mu}(x|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})
are equal whenever
\lambda_{n}=0
. Moreover notice that

(2.5)

\mathbb{F}_{\lambda/\mu}(x|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})=\mathbb{f}_{\lambda/\mu}(x|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=0\qquad\text{if }\mu_{n-1}=0,\text{ and }\lambda_{n}>0,

which is due to the presence of the term
1/(q;q)_{\mu_{n-1}-\lambda_{n}}
in (
1.6
). These observations allow us to prove (
2.3
) by induction. For
n=1
, we have

(2.6)

\mathbb{f}_{(k)}(x_{1}|\varnothing,\varnothing)=x_{1}^{k},

which implies

(2.7)

\mathbb{f}_{(k,0)}(0,x_{2}|a_{1},b_{1})=x_{2}^{k}\frac{(a_{1}/x_{2};q)_{k}}{(a_{1}b_{1};q)_{k}}=\mathbb{F}_{(k)}(x_{2}|a_{1},b_{1}),

verifying (
2.3
). By inductive hypothesis, assume that

(2.8)

\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\big|_{x_{1}=0}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(x_{2},\dots,x_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\qquad&\text{if }\ell(\lambda)\leq n-1,\\
0\qquad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then, for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}

(2.9)

\begin{split}\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n+1}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})\big|_{x_{1}=0}&=\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{Y}_{n-1}}\mathbb{f}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\tau\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\big|_{x_{1}=0}\mathbb{f}_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{n+1}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})\\
&=\sum_{\mu}\mathbb{F}_{\mu}(x_{2},\dots,x_{n}|\tau\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\mathbb{F}_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{n+1}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})\\
&=\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(x_{2},\dots,x_{n+1}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n}),\end{split}

completing the proof.
∎

In the following proposition, we state an important structural property of the spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials
\mathbb{f}
, which is
not
satisfied by the family
\mathbb{F}
and which in the context of symmetric functions is commonly referred to as
shift property
. The shift property is a key ingredient, for instance, in the proof of the orthogonality of
Theorem

1.1
.

Proposition 2.2
.

The spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials satisfy the shift property: for any
\lambda
such that
\lambda_{n}>0
, we have

(2.10)

\mathbb{f}_{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})\mathbb{f}_{(\lambda_{1}-1,\dots,\lambda_{n}-1)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}).

Proof.

Notice that the branching factor
\mathbb{f}_{\lambda/\mu}(x|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})
satisfies the shift property

(2.11)

\mathbb{f}_{\lambda/\mu}(x|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=x\,\mathbb{f}_{(\lambda_{1}-1,\dots,\lambda_{n}-1)/(\mu_{1}-1,\dots,\mu_{n-1}-1)}(x|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})

for all pairs of partitions
\lambda,\mu
such that
\lambda_{n},\mu_{n-1}>0
. Iterating the equality (
2.11
) across all terms of the branching expansion (
1.7
), we prove (
2.10
).
∎

Example 2.1
.

The shift property does not hold for the polynomials
\mathbb{F}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})
, which can be seen by simply analyzing the case
n=1
: for any
k\geq 0
, we have

(2.12)

\mathbb{F}_{(k)}(x|a_{1},b_{1})=x^{k}\frac{(a_{1}/x;q)_{k}}{(a_{1}b_{1};q)_{k}}.

On the other hand the one-variable polynomials
\mathbb{f}_{(k)}(x|\varnothing,\varnothing)
were given in (
2.6
).

Remark 2.2
.

In the case
q=a_{1}=b_{1}=\cdots=0
, the spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials
\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}
reduce to the Schur polynomials
s_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})
. In this case the shift property reflects the fact that tensoring an irreducible polynomial representation of
GL(n,\mathbb{C})
with the 1-dimensional determinant representation shifts its highest weight by
(1,\dots,1)
.

Remark 2.3
.

Notice that
Proposition

2.1
and
Proposition

2.2
imply that
\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})=0
, whenever
\ell(\lambda)=n+1
, but they do not imply that
\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})=0
if
\ell(\lambda)\leq n
.

The shift property (
2.10
) can be used to extend the spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials
\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}
to Laurent polynomials labeled by signatures

(2.13)

\mathrm{Sig}_{n}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\,:\,\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}\},

through the relation

(2.14)

\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{\lambda_{n}}\mathbb{f}_{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{n},\dots,\lambda_{n-1}-\lambda_{n},0)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}).

Moreover, for any pair of signatures
\lambda\in\mathrm{Sig}_{n}
,
\mu\in\mathrm{Sig}_{n-1}
, the single variable skew spin
q
-Whittaker (Laurent) polynomials can be defined as

(2.15)

\mathbb{f}_{\lambda/\mu}(x|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\mathbf{1}_{\mu\preceq\lambda}x^{|\lambda|-|\mu|}\prod_{r=1}^{n-1}\frac{(a_{r}/x;q)_{\lambda_{r}-\mu_{r}}(xb_{r};q)_{\mu_{r}-\lambda_{r+1}}(q;q)_{\lambda_{r}-\lambda_{r+1}}}{(q;q)_{\lambda_{r}-\mu_{r}}(q;q)_{\mu_{r}-\lambda_{r+1}}(a_{r}b_{r};q)_{\lambda_{r}-\lambda_{r+1}}}

and it is clear that the Laurent polynomials
\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}
inherit the branching rule

(2.16)

\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in\mathrm{Sig}_{n-1}\\
\mu\preceq\lambda\end{subarray}}\mathbb{f}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n-1}|\tau\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-2})\mathbb{f}_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}).

Above, the interlacing relation between signatures
\mu,\lambda
of length respectively
n-1
and
n
is defined as

(2.17)

\mu\preceq\lambda\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad\lambda_{i}\geq\mu_{i}\geq\lambda_{i+1}\qquad\text{for all }i=1,\dots,n-1

and symbol
|\lambda|
is interpreted as

(2.18)

|\lambda|=\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}.

Remark 2.4
.

In light of
Theorem

1.1
and
Theorem

1.2
, the family
\{\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\}_{\lambda\in\mathrm{Sig}_{n}}
forms an orthogonal basis of the linear space of symmetric Laurent polynomials in
n
variables. Notice that such statement cannot be immediately extended to the polynomials
\mathbb{F}
, since there is no automatic way of extending them to Laurent polynomials labeled by signatures.

Extending the definition of spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials to arbitrary signatures as in (
2.14
), we gain the following symmetry.

Proposition 2.3
.

For every signature
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n})
we have

(2.19)

\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\mathbb{f}_{\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}}(\overline{\boldsymbol{x}}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}^{\leftarrow},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1}^{\leftarrow}),

where

(2.20)

\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}=(-\lambda_{n}\geq\cdots\geq-\lambda_{1}),\qquad\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1}^{\leftarrow}=(a_{n-1},\dots,a_{1}),\qquad\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}^{\leftarrow}=(b_{n-1},\dots,b_{1}).

Proof.

The single variable skew spin
q
-Whittaker (Laurent) polynomials satisfy the relation

(2.21)

\mathbb{f}_{\lambda/\mu}(x|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\mathbb{f}_{\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}/\mu^{\mathrm{rev}}}(\overline{x}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}^{\leftarrow},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1}^{\leftarrow}),

as it can be readily verified from (
2.15
).
For instance, in the case
n=3
, for a pair of signatures
\mu\preceq\lambda
, we have

(2.22)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{f}_{\lambda/\mu}(x|\boldsymbol{a}_{2},\boldsymbol{b}_{2})\\
&=x^{|\lambda|-|\mu|}\frac{(a_{1}/x;q)_{\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1}}(xb_{1};q)_{\mu_{1}-\lambda_{2}}(q;q)_{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}}{(q;q)_{\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1}}(q;q)_{\mu_{1}-\lambda_{2}}(a_{1}b_{1};q)_{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}}\frac{(a_{2}/x;q)_{\lambda_{2}-\mu_{2}}(xb_{2};q)_{\mu_{2}-\lambda_{3}}(q;q)_{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}}}{(q;q)_{\lambda_{2}-\mu_{2}}(q;q)_{\mu_{2}-\lambda_{3}}(a_{2}b_{2};q)_{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}}}\\
&=(\overline{x})^{-|\lambda|+|\mu|}\frac{(b_{2}/\overline{x};q)_{\mu_{2}-\lambda_{3}}(a_{2}\overline{x};q)_{\lambda_{2}-\mu_{2}}(q;q)_{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}}}{(q;q)_{\mu_{2}-\lambda_{3}}(q;q)_{\lambda_{2}-\mu_{2}}(a_{2}b_{2};q)_{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}}}\frac{(b_{1}/\overline{x};q)_{\mu_{1}-\lambda_{2}}(a_{1}\overline{x};q)_{\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1}}(q;q)_{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}}{(q;q)_{\mu_{1}-\lambda_{2}}(q;q)_{\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1}}(a_{1}b_{1};q)_{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}}\\
&=(\overline{x})^{|\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}|-|\mu^{\mathrm{rev}}|}\frac{(b_{2}/\overline{x};q)_{\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}_{1}-\mu^{\mathrm{rev}}_{1}}(a_{2}\overline{x};q)_{\mu^{\mathrm{rev}}_{1}-\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}_{2}}(q;q)_{\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}_{1}-\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}_{2}}}{(q;q)_{\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}_{1}-\mu^{\mathrm{rev}}_{1}}(q;q)_{\mu^{\mathrm{rev}}_{1}-\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}_{2}}(a_{2}b_{2};q)_{\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}_{1}-\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}_{2}}}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\frac{(b_{1}/\overline{x};q)_{\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}_{2}-\mu^{\mathrm{rev}}_{2}}(a_{1}\overline{x};q)_{\mu^{\mathrm{rev}}_{2}-\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}_{3}}(q;q)_{\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}_{2}-\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}_{3}}}{(q;q)_{\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}_{2}-\mu^{\mathrm{rev}}_{2}}(q;q)_{\mu^{\mathrm{rev}}_{2}-\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}_{3}}(a_{1}b_{1};q)_{\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}_{2}-\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}_{3}}}\\
&=\mathbb{f}_{\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}/\mu^{\mathrm{rev}}}(\overline{x}|\boldsymbol{b}_{2}^{\leftarrow},\boldsymbol{a}_{2}^{\leftarrow}).\end{split}

Assuming that (
2.19
) holds for
(n-1)
-variate functions and using the branching rule we can inductively compute

(2.23)

\begin{split}\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})&=\sum_{\mu\preceq\lambda}\mathbb{f}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n-1}|\tau\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-2})\mathbb{f}_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\\
&=\sum_{\mu\preceq\lambda}\mathbb{f}_{\mu^{\mathrm{rev}}}(\overline{\boldsymbol{x}}_{n-1}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-2}^{\leftarrow},(a_{n-1},\dots a_{2}))\mathbb{f}_{\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}/\mu^{\mathrm{rev}}}(\overline{x}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}^{\leftarrow},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1}^{\leftarrow})\\
&=\sum_{\mu\preceq\lambda}\mathbb{f}_{\mu^{\mathrm{rev}}}(\overline{\boldsymbol{x}}_{n-1}|\tau(\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}^{\leftarrow}),(a_{n-1},\dots a_{2}))\mathbb{f}_{\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}/\mu^{\mathrm{rev}}}(\overline{x}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}^{\leftarrow},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1}^{\leftarrow})\\
&=\mathbb{f}_{\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}}(\overline{\boldsymbol{x}}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}^{\leftarrow},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1}^{\leftarrow}),\end{split}

completing the proof.
∎

2.2.
Cauchy identities

As proven in
[
BW21
,
MP22
,
BK24
,
Kor24
]
, the spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials satisfy summation identities generalizing the Cauchy identities for Schur polynomials. To state them we need the following.

Definition 2.1
.

The
dual spin
q
-Whittaker polynomial
is

(2.24)

\mathbb{F}^{*}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m}|\boldsymbol{a}_{m},\boldsymbol{b}_{m})=\psi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{a}_{m},\boldsymbol{b}_{m})\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m}|\boldsymbol{a}_{m},\boldsymbol{b}_{m}),

where

(2.25)

\psi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{a}_{m},\boldsymbol{b}_{m})=\prod_{r=1}^{m}\frac{(a_{r}b_{r};q)_{\lambda_{r}-\lambda_{r+1}}}{(q;q)_{\lambda_{r}-\lambda_{r+1}}}.

Notice that
\psi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{a}_{m},\boldsymbol{b}_{m})=\psi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{b}_{m},\boldsymbol{a}_{m})
.

Remark 2.5
.

A particular case of the spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials are the
q
-Whittaker polynomials, which are obtained as

(2.26)

P_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};q,0)=\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})\big|_{a_{i}=b_{i}=0},\qquad Q_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};q,0)=\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}^{*}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n},\boldsymbol{a}_{n})\big|_{a_{i}=b_{i}=0}.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
Kor24
]
, Theorem B)
.

The spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials satisfy the Cauchy identity

(2.27)

\sum_{\lambda}\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}^{*}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m}|\boldsymbol{b}_{m},\boldsymbol{a}_{m})=\frac{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{m})}{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{m})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})},

where the function
H_{q}
was defined in (
1.13
).

Identity (
2.27
) was proved in the original paper
[
BW21
]
in the homogeneous case
a_{1}=a_{2}=\cdots=b_{1}=b_{2}=\cdots=-s
as a consequence of the Yang-Baxter equation for the six vertex model. Interestingly, the proof of the fully inhomogeneous Cauchy identity is more involved and it is not proven in the same way, but through inhomogeneous intertwining relations (which are different from the Yang-Baxter equation) discovered in
[
Kor24
]
.

Corollary 2.1
.

Fix
0<m<n
. We have

(2.28)

\sum_{\lambda}\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}^{*}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m}|\boldsymbol{b}_{m},\boldsymbol{a}_{m})=\frac{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{b}_{m})}{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{m})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})}.

2.3.
Spin Hall-Littlewood Rational Functions

Here we define a family of symmetric functions known in literature as
inhomogeneous Spin Hall-Littlewood rational function

[
Bor17
,
BP18
]
. They are important as they are self-orthogonal with respect to the scalar product (
2.34
) defined below and satisfy dual Cauchy identities when paired with the spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials.

Definition 2.2
.

Define the spin Hall Littlewood rational functions

(2.29)

\overline{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})=\frac{(1-q)^{m}}{(q;q)_{m-\ell(\lambda)}}\psi_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})\sum_{\sigma\in S_{m}}\sigma\left(\prod_{1\leq\alpha<\beta\leq m}\frac{u_{\alpha}-qu_{\beta}}{u_{\alpha}-u_{\beta}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\phi_{\lambda_{i}}(u_{i}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})\right)

with

(2.30)

\phi_{k}(u|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})=(-1)^{k}\frac{u}{1-a_{k}u}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{u-b_{j}}{1-a_{j}u}.

Let us compare the definition (
2.29
) with others that have appeared in literature.

Remark 2.6
.

The functions
\overline{F}
correspond to those in
[
BP18
]
(denoted by
\mathsf{F}(\,\cdot\,|\Xi,\mathsf{S})
) as

(2.31)

\overline{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})\big|_{a_{j}=s_{j}\xi_{j},\,b_{j}=s_{j}/\xi_{j}}=\frac{(-1)^{|\lambda|}}{(q;q)_{m-\ell(\lambda)}}\prod_{r\geq 1}\frac{(s_{r}^{2};q)_{\lambda_{r}^{\prime}-\lambda_{r+1}^{\prime}}}{(q;q)_{\lambda_{r}^{\prime}-\lambda_{r+1}^{\prime}}}\left(\prod_{i\geq 1}\frac{1}{\xi_{i}^{\lambda_{i+1}^{\prime}}}\right)\mathsf{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m}|\Xi,\mathsf{S})\big|_{s_{0}=0,\,\xi_{0}=1}.

They can also be matched with those in
[
BK24
]
(denoted by
\widetilde{\mathsf{F}}
) as

(2.32)

\overline{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})\big|_{a_{i}=s_{i}\xi_{i},\,b_{i}=s_{i}/\xi_{i}}=(-1)^{|\lambda|}\left(\prod_{r\geq 1}\frac{1}{\xi_{r}^{\lambda_{r+1}^{\prime}}}\frac{(s_{r}^{2};q)_{\lambda_{r}^{\prime}-\lambda_{r+1}^{\prime}}}{(q;q)_{\lambda_{r}^{\prime}-\lambda_{r+1}^{\prime}}}\right)\widetilde{\mathsf{F}}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m}|\Xi,\mathsf{S}).

Remark 2.7
.

The functions
\overline{F}
reduce to the Hall-Littlwood polynomials as

(2.33)

P_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m};0,q)=(-1)^{|\lambda|}\overline{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})\big|_{a_{1}=b_{1}=a_{2}=b_{2}\cdots=0}.

Definition 2.3
.

For rational functions
f,g:\mathbb{T}^{m}\to\mathbb{C}
, define the scalar product

(2.34)

\langle f(\boldsymbol{z}_{m})|g(\boldsymbol{z}_{m})\rangle_{m}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{m!}\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z_{1}}\cdots\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{m}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z_{m}}\prod_{1\leq\alpha\neq\beta\leq m}\frac{z_{\alpha}-z_{\beta}}{z_{\alpha}-qz_{\beta}}f(\boldsymbol{z}_{m})g(\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{m}).

Proposition 2.5

(
[
BP18
]
, Corollary 7.5)
.

The functions
\overline{F}_{\lambda}
satisfy the orthogonality

(2.35)

\langle\overline{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{m}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})|\overline{F}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{z}_{m}|\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{a})\rangle_{m}^{\prime}=\mathbf{1}_{\lambda=\mu}\frac{(1-q)^{m}}{(q;q)_{m-\ell(\lambda)}}\psi_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}),

where
\psi
was defined in (
2.25
).

For the next proposition define

(2.36)

E(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+x_{i}y_{j}).

Proposition 2.6

(
[
BK24
]
, Corollary 5.12)
.

Fix
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. We have

(2.37)

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathrm{Box}(m,n)}\overline{F}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})=\frac{E(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};-\boldsymbol{u}_{m})}{E(\boldsymbol{a}_{n};-\boldsymbol{u}_{m})},

where
-\boldsymbol{u}_{m}=(-u_{1},\dots,-u_{m})
and the
\mathrm{Box}(m,n)
was defined in (
1.22
).

Corollary 2.2
.

Fix
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. We have

(2.38)

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathrm{Box}(m,n)}\overline{F}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\frac{E(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};-\boldsymbol{u}_{m})}{E(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};-\boldsymbol{u}_{m})}.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{n+k}
and
\mu\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}
. Define the skew spin Hall-Littlewood symmetric function as

(2.39)

\overline{F}_{\lambda/\mu}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})=\frac{\left\langle\overline{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k},\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})\middle|\overline{F}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})\right\rangle_{n}^{\prime}}{\left\langle\overline{F}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})|\overline{F}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})\right\rangle_{n}^{\prime}},

where
\overline{F}_{\lambda/\mu}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k},\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})
denotes the spin Hall-Littlewood rational function (
2.29
) in
k+n
variables
(u_{1},\dots,u_{k},z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
BK24
]
, Corollary 5.11)
.

The spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials satisfy the Pieri rule

(2.40)

\frac{E(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};-u)}{E(\boldsymbol{a}_{n};-u)}\mathbb{F}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})=\sum_{\lambda}\overline{F}_{\lambda^{\prime}/\mu^{\prime}}(u|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n}).

3.
Eigenrelations

The main results of this section are
Theorem

3.1
and
Theorem

3.2
, which give two eigenrelations for the spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials
\mathbb{f}
. They represent fully inhomogeneous generalizations respectively of
[
MP22
, Theorem 3.9]
and
[
MP22
, Theorem 3.10]
. The proofs of these theorems will only be sketched, since the arguments can be adapted from those in
[
MP22
]
.

3.1.
Eigenrelations involving
\lambda_{n}

For a set of
n
variables
\boldsymbol{x}_{n}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
and for any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we denote by
T_{q,x_{i}}
be the
q
-shift operator that acts on
n
-variate functions
f(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})
as

(3.1)

T_{q,x_{i}}[f(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})]=f(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},qx_{i},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{n}).

We introduce the operator

(3.2)

\mathfrak{D}_{\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{r=1}^{n-1}(1-x_{i}b_{r})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}/x_{j}}T_{q,x_{i}}.

More compactly, one can express the action of
\mathfrak{D}_{\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}}
on an
n
-variate function
f(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})
as

(3.3)

\mathfrak{D}_{\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}}[f(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})]=H_{q}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})^{-1}D_{1}\left[H_{q}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})f(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})\right],

where
D_{1}
is the
q
-Whittaker
q
-difference operator

(3.4)

D_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}/x_{j}}T_{q,x_{i}}.

Theorem 3.1
.

We have

(3.5)

\mathfrak{D}_{\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}}\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=q^{\lambda_{N}}\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}).

Remark 3.1
.

In case
\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}=(0,\dots,0)
,
Theorem

3.1
reduces to the known eigenrelation for
q
-Whittaker polynomials
[
Mac95
, VI]

(3.6)

D_{1}P_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};q,0)=q^{\lambda_{n}}P_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};q,0).

On the other hand, setting
\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}=\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1}=(-s,\dots,-s)
,
Theorem

3.1
becomes
[
MP22
, Theorem 3.9]
.

The proof of
Theorem

3.1
uses an eigenrelation for the spin Hall-Littlewood rational functions
\overline{F}
, which is dual to (
3.5
) and that we state below.

Denote by
V(m)
the space of symmetric rational functions of
m
variables of degree at most
1
in each variable. Alternatively,
V(m)
is the space of rational functions that can be written as
a(\boldsymbol{u}_{m})/b(\boldsymbol{u}_{m})
where
a,b
are symmetric polynomials and
\deg_{u_{i}}(a)-\deg_{u_{i}}(b)\leq 1
for all
i=1,\dots,m
. Define the operator
\mathfrak{D}^{*}_{n}
, which acts on function of
V(m)
as

(3.7)

\mathfrak{D}^{*}_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq j\end{subarray}}^{m}\frac{u_{j}-qu_{l}}{u_{j}-u_{l}}\mathfrak{C}_{j,n},

where

(3.8)

\mathfrak{C}_{j,n}=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(-a_{i})\frac{u_{j}-b_{i}}{1-a_{i}u_{j}}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon T_{\varepsilon^{-1},u_{j}}.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathrm{Box}(n,m)
. Then, we have

(3.9)

\mathfrak{D}^{*}_{n}\overline{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})\big|_{a_{n}=0}=\frac{1-q^{\lambda_{n}^{\prime}}}{1-q}\overline{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})\big|_{a_{n}=0}.

Sketch of proof.

The eigenrelation (
3.9
) is the inhomogeneous variant of
[
MP22
, Theorem 3.4]
and its proof consists of a direct calculation of the action of the operator
\mathfrak{D}^{*}_{n}
on the symmetrized sum (
2.29
). The operator
\mathfrak{C}_{i,n}
has the property that

(3.10)

\mathfrak{C}_{i,n}\left[\phi_{k}(u_{i}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})\big|_{a_{n}=0}\right]=\begin{cases}\phi_{k}(u_{i}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})\big|_{a_{n}=0}\qquad&\text{if }k=n,\\
0\qquad&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

for all
i=1,\dots,m
. As a result, calling

(3.11)

\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m})=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\phi_{\lambda_{j}}(u_{j}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})\big|_{a_{n}=0},

for any
\sigma\in S_{m}
, we have

(3.12)

\mathfrak{C}_{i,n}\left[\sigma\left(\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m})\right)\right]=\begin{cases}\sigma\left(\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m})\right)\qquad&\text{if }i\in\sigma\{1,\dots,\lambda^{\prime}_{n}\},\\
0\qquad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

One can then evaluate

(3.13)

\begin{split}&\mathfrak{D}_{n}^{*}\overline{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})\big|_{a_{n}=0}\\
&=C(\lambda)\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in S_{n}\\
i\in\{1,\dots,\lambda_{N}^{\prime}\}\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq j\end{subarray}}\frac{u_{j}-qu_{l}}{u_{j}-u_{l}}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sigma\left\{\prod_{1\leq\alpha<\beta\leq m}\frac{u_{\alpha}-qu_{\beta}}{u_{\alpha}-u_{\beta}}\right\}\bigg|_{u_{i}=\varepsilon}\mathfrak{C}_{i,N}\left[\sigma\left(\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m})\right)\right],\end{split}

with the constant prefactor set as
C(\lambda)=\frac{(1-q)^{n}}{(q;q)_{n-\ell(\lambda)}}\psi_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})\big|_{a_{n}=0}
.
Following
[
MP22
, Theorem 3.4]
, the limit in the right hand side is responsible for the creation of factors
q^{\bar{k}}
with
\bar{k}=\sigma^{-1}(i)
and the double summation can be rearranged as

(3.14)

C(\lambda)\sum_{\bar{k}=1}^{\lambda_{n}^{\prime}}q^{\bar{k}}\sum_{\tau\in S_{m}}\tau\left(\prod_{1\leq\alpha<\beta\leq m}\frac{u_{\alpha}-qu_{\beta}}{u_{\alpha}-u_{\beta}}\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m})\right),

which equals the right hand side of (
3.9
).
∎

Sketch of proof of

3.1
.

Armed with

\left(1-(1-q)\mathfrak{D}^{*}_{n}\right)\left[\frac{E(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};-\boldsymbol{u}_{m})}{E(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};-\boldsymbol{u}_{m})}\right]=\mathfrak{D}_{\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}}\left[\frac{E(\boldsymbol{x}_{n};-\boldsymbol{u}_{m})}{E(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};-\boldsymbol{u}_{m})}\right],

\begin{split}&\sum_{\lambda}q^{\lambda_{n}}\overline{F}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m}|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})\big|_{a_{n}=0}\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\\

and by orthogonality (

) and the eigenrelation (

). Checking the identity (

Proofs of main theorems

4.1.
Torus scalar product for
q
-Whittaker polynomials

For any two functions
f,g:\mathbb{T}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
, we introduce the scalar product

(4.1)

\langle f\,|\,g\rangle_{n}=\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}f(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})g(\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{n})\Delta(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})\mathrm{d}z_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}z_{n},

where the density
\Delta(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})
was defined in (
1.14
). The scalar product
\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle_{n}
is a the
t=0
case of Macdonald’s torus scalar product; see
[
Mac95
, VI.9]
. The
q
-Whittaker polynomials
P
satisfy the orthogonality

(4.2)

\left\langle P_{\lambda}\,\middle|\,P_{\mu}\right\rangle_{n}=\mathbf{1}_{\lambda=\mu}\mathsf{c}_{n}(\lambda),\qquad\text{where}\qquad\mathsf{c}_{n}(\lambda)=\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{(q;q)_{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k+1}}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}.

It is clear, using the definition (
1.15
) of the scalar product
\llangle|\rrangle
and the density
\Delta(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})
from (
1.12
), that

(4.3)

\llangle f\,|\,g\rrangle_{n}=\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(a_{k}b_{k};q)_{\infty}}{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})}\left\langle f(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\,\middle|\,g(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\right\rangle_{n}.

4.2.
Proof of
Theorem

1.1

The proof of
Theorem

1.1
uses a number of preliminary results, which we organize in several lemmas.

Lemma 4.1
.

Fix integers
0<m<n
and
k>0
. Then, we have

(4.4)

\left\llangle\frac{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{b}_{m})}{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{m})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})}\,\middle|\,\frac{E(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})}{E(\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})}\right\rrangle_{n}=\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(a_{k}b_{k};q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{n-1}}\frac{E(\boldsymbol{y}_{m};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})}{E(\boldsymbol{b}_{m};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})},

where we recall that functions
H_{q},E
were defined in (
1.13
), (
2.36
) respectively.

Proof.

The left hand side of (
4.4
) can be simplified as

(4.5)

\begin{split}&\frac{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{b}_{m})}{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})E(\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})}\left\llangle\frac{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})}{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{m})}\,\middle|\,E(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})\right\rrangle_{n}\\
&=\frac{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{b}_{m})\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(a_{k}b_{k};q)_{\infty}}{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})E(\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})}\\
&\qquad\times\left\langle\frac{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})}{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{m})}H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\,\middle|\,E(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\right\rangle_{n}\\
&=\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(a_{k}b_{k};q)_{\infty}}{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\tau^{m}\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})E(\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})}\\
&\qquad\times\left\langle H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{y}_{m},\tau^{m}\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\,\middle|\,E(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\right\rangle_{n}\end{split}

where
\tau^{m}\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}=(b_{m+1},\dots,b_{n-1})
as in (
1.4
). The factorized expressions in the scalar product in the right hand side can be expanded as

(4.6)

\displaystyle H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{y}_{m},\tau^{m}\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\sum_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};q,0)Q_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m},\tau^{m}\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1};q,0)

\displaystyle E(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})=\sum_{\mu}P_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};q,0)Q_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}_{k}};q,0).

Here, in the first expression, the term
Q_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m},\tau^{m}\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1};q,0)
is the
q
-Whittaker polynomial
Q_{\lambda}
with
n-1
variables specialized as
(y_{1},\dots,y_{m},b_{m+1},\dots,b_{n-1})
, whereas in the second line one can define the
Q_{\mu}
term through the orthogonality (
4.2
) as

(4.7)

Q_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}_{k}};q,0)=\frac{1}{\mathsf{c}_{n}(\lambda)}\left\langle E(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\middle|P_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};q,0)\right\rangle_{n}.

Using the above expansions we find

(4.8)

\displaystyle\left\langle H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{y}_{m},\tau^{m}\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\,\middle|\,H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}_{k}})\right\rangle_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda,\mu}\left\langle P_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};q,0)\,\middle|\,P_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};q,0)\right\rangle_{n}Q_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m},\tau^{m}\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1};q,0)Q_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}_{k}};q,0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda,\mu}\mathsf{c}_{n}(\lambda)Q_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m},\tau^{m}\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1};q,0)Q_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}_{k}};q,0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{n-1}}\sum_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m},\tau^{m}\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1};q,0)Q_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}_{k}};q,0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{n-1}}H_{q}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m};\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})H_{q}(\tau^{m}\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})E(\boldsymbol{y}_{m};\boldsymbol{u}_{k})E(\tau^{m}\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k}),

where we used the fact that

(4.9)

\mathsf{c}_{n}(\lambda)Q_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m},\tau^{m}\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1};q,0)=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{n-1}}P_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m},\tau^{m}\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1};q,0),\qquad\text{for all $\lambda$ such that }\lambda_{n}=0.

One can see that the previous identity hold directly from (
2.26
).
Then, we have

(4.10)

\displaystyle\frac{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{b}_{m})}{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})E(\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})}\left\llangle\frac{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})}{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{m})}\,\middle|\,E(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})\right\rrangle_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(a_{k}b_{k};q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{n-1}}\frac{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m};\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})H_{q}(\tau^{m}\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})E(\boldsymbol{y}_{m};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})E(\tau^{m}\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})}{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\tau^{m}\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})E(\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(a_{k}b_{k};q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{n-1}}\frac{E(\boldsymbol{y}_{m};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})}{E(\boldsymbol{b}_{m};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})},

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Fix integers
0<m<n
. Then, for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{m}
, we have

(4.11)

\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m}|\boldsymbol{b}_{m},\boldsymbol{a}_{m})=\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(a_{k}b_{k};q)_{\infty}}\left\llangle\frac{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{b}_{m})}{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{m})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})}\,\middle|\,\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\right\rrangle_{n}.

Moreover, the following expansion holds

(4.12)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m}|\boldsymbol{b}_{m},\boldsymbol{a}_{m})\\
&=\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(a_{k}b_{k};q)_{\infty}}\sum_{\mu}\left\llangle\mathbb{f}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\,\middle|\,\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\right\rrangle_{n}\psi_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})\mathbb{F}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m}|\boldsymbol{b}_{m},\boldsymbol{a}_{m}).\end{split}

Proof.

First notice that the expansion (
4.12
) follows directly from (
4.11
), employing the Cauchy identity (
2.28
) to expand the expression in the left term of the scalar product. Therefore, we only need to prove (
4.11
). By the dual Cauchy identity (
2.38
) and the orthogonality of the spin Hall-Littewood rational functions (
2.35
), it suffices to prove that the summation

(4.13)

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathrm{Box}(k,n)}\overline{F}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}|\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{a})\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(a_{k}b_{k};q)_{\infty}}\left\llangle\frac{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{b}_{m})}{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{m})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})}\,\middle|\,\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\right\rrangle_{n}

equals

(4.14)

\frac{E(\boldsymbol{y}_{m};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})}{E(\boldsymbol{b}_{m};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})}.

Bringing the summation inside the scalar product and using the the dual Cauchy identity (
2.38
), the summation (
4.13
) becomes

(4.15)

\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(a_{k}b_{k};q)_{\infty}}\left\llangle\frac{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{b}_{m})}{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{m})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1};\boldsymbol{y}_{m})}\,\middle|\,\frac{E(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})}{E(\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1};-\boldsymbol{u}_{k})}\right\rrangle_{n},

which, by

4.1

4.11
).
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

The operators
\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1}}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}}
are adjoint with respect to the scalar product
\llangle\cdot\,|\,\cdot\rrangle_{n}
. Similarly, operators
\mathfrak{D}_{\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1}},\mathfrak{D}_{\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}}
are adjoint with respect to the same scalar product.

Proof.

This is the result of a simple computation which we report for completeness. Using (
3.19
) and (
4.3
), we have

(4.16)

\begin{split}&\left\llangle\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1}}\left[f(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})\right]\,\middle|\,g(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})\right\rrangle_{n}\\
&=\left\llangle H_{q}(\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{n};\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})^{-1}\overline{D}_{1}\left[H_{q}(\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{n};\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})f(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})\right]\,\middle|\,g(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})\right\rrangle_{n}\\
&=\frac{1}{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})}\\
&\qquad\times\left\langle H_{q}(\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{n};\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})^{-1}\overline{D}_{1}\left[H_{q}(\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{n};\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})f(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})\right]H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\,\middle|\,g(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\right\rangle_{n}\\
&=\frac{1}{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})}\left\langle\overline{D}_{1}\left[H_{q}(\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{n};\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})f(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})\right]\,\middle|\,H_{q}(\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})g(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})\right\rangle_{n}.\end{split}

The action on
n
-variate Laurent polynomials of the operator
\overline{D}_{1}
is self adjoint with respect to the scalar product
\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle_{n}
; see [Macdonald Section VI.9]. Thus, the right hand side is equal to

(4.17)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{H_{q}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})}\left\langle H_{q}(\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{n};\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})f(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})\,\middle|\,\overline{D}_{1}\left[H_{q}(\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})g(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})\right]\right\rangle_{n}\\
&=\frac{1}{H_{q}(A_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})}\\
&\qquad\times\left\langle f(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\,\middle|\,H_{q}(\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})^{-1}\overline{D}_{1}\left[H_{q}(\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})g(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})\right]H_{q}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n};\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\right\rangle_{n}\\
&=\left\llangle f(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})\,\middle|\,H_{q}(\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})^{-1}\overline{D}_{1}\left[H_{q}(\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{n};\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})g(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})\right]\right\rrangle_{n}\\
&=\left\llangle f(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})\,\middle|\,\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}}\left[g(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})\right]\right\rrangle_{n}.\end{split}

Analogous calculations show that
\mathfrak{D}_{\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1}},\mathfrak{D}_{\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}}
are adjoint with respect to the scalar product
\llangle\cdot\,|\,\cdot\rrangle_{n}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

For all
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}
such that
\lambda_{1}\neq\mu_{1}
or
\lambda_{n}\neq\mu_{n}
, we have

(4.18)

\left\llangle\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\,\middle|\,\mathbb{f}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\right\rrangle_{n}=0.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of
Lemma

4.3
and of
Theorem

3.1
,
Theorem

3.2
. For instance, considering the action of
\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1}}
, we have

(4.19)

\begin{split}&q^{-\lambda_{1}}\left\llangle\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\,\middle|\,\mathbb{F}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\right\rrangle_{n}\\
&=\left\llangle\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1}}\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\,\middle|\,\mathbb{F}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\right\rrangle_{n}\\
&=\left\llangle\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\,\middle|\,\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}}\mathbb{F}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\right\rrangle_{n}\\
&=q^{-\mu_{1}}\left\llangle\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\,\middle|\,\mathbb{F}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\right\rrangle_{n},\end{split}

which implies the claim when
\lambda_{1}\neq\mu_{1}
. Similar basic arguments can be repeated to prove the claim in case
\lambda_{n}\neq\mu_{n}
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Fix integers
0<m<n
. Then, for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{m}
, we have

(4.20)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m}|\boldsymbol{b}_{m},\boldsymbol{a}_{m})\\
&=\sum_{\mu\in\mathrm{Box}(\lambda_{1},m)}\frac{\left\llangle\mathbb{f}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\,\middle|\,\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\right\rrangle_{n}}{\mathsf{c}_{n}(\mu|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})}\,\mathbb{F}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m}|\boldsymbol{b}_{m},\boldsymbol{a}_{m}).\end{split}

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of
Lemma

4.4
and of the expansion (
4.12
) for the function
\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m}|\boldsymbol{b}_{m},\boldsymbol{a}_{m})
. The summation of (
4.12
) clearly runs over partitions
\mu
such that
\ell(\mu)\leq m<n
and for such partitions we have

(4.21)

\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(a_{k}/b_{k};q)_{\infty}}\psi_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})=\frac{1}{\mathsf{c}_{n}(\mu|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})},

completing the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}
. An expansion of the following form holds

(4.22)

\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{m}|\boldsymbol{a}_{m},\boldsymbol{b}_{m})=\sum_{\mu\subset\mathrm{Box}(\lambda_{1},m)}\alpha_{\lambda,\mu}(\boldsymbol{a}_{m},\boldsymbol{b}_{m})\,m_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{x}_{m}),

where
m_{\mu}
are the monomial symmetric polynomials. Coefficients
\alpha_{\mu,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{a}_{m},\boldsymbol{b}_{m})
are analytic for
a_{1},\dots,a_{m}
,
b_{1},\dots,b_{m}
in a neighborhood of the origin. Moreover for
a_{1}=\cdots=a_{m}=b_{1}=\cdots=b_{m}=0
we have
\alpha_{\mu,\lambda}=0
unless
\mu\leq\lambda
and
\alpha_{\lambda,\lambda}=1
.

Proof.

Consider the branching factor
\mathbb{F}_{\lambda/\mu}(x|\boldsymbol{a}_{m},\boldsymbol{b}_{m})
of (
1.6
). It is clear that

(4.23)

\deg_{x}\left(\mathbb{F}_{\lambda/\mu}(x|\boldsymbol{a}_{m},\boldsymbol{b}_{m})\right)=|\lambda|-|\mu|+\sum_{r\geq 1}\mu_{r}-\lambda_{r+1}=\lambda_{1}

and that the coefficients of
\mathbb{F}_{\lambda/\mu}(x|\boldsymbol{a}_{m},\boldsymbol{b}_{m})
are analytic functions of parameters
\boldsymbol{a}_{m},\boldsymbol{b}_{m}
as long as
|a_{1}|,\dots,|a_{m}|,|b_{1}|,\dots,|b_{m}|<1
. Then, the branching rule (
1.7
) implies that

(4.24)

\deg_{x_{i}}\left(\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{m}|\boldsymbol{a}_{m},\boldsymbol{b}_{m})\right)=\lambda_{1},\qquad\text{for all }i=1,\dots,m,

by symmetry and this proves the expansion (
4.22
). Setting all parameters
\boldsymbol{a}_{m},\boldsymbol{b}_{m}
to zero, the spin
q
-Whittaker polynomial
\mathbb{F}
reduces to the
q
-Whittaker polynomial
P
, as in
Remark

2.5
, and we have (
[
Mac95
, VI.4]
)

(4.25)

P_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{m};q,0)=m_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{m})+\sum_{\mu<\lambda}c_{\lambda,\mu}\,m_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}),

where
c_{\lambda,\mu}
are polynomials in
q
. This completes the proof.
∎

We are now in the position to prove the orthogonality of spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Fix
L\geq 0
and
\lambda
such that
\lambda_{1}\leq L
. By
Lemma

4.5
, we have the expansion

(4.26)

\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m}|\boldsymbol{b}_{m},\boldsymbol{a}_{m})=\sum_{\nu\subset\mathrm{Box}(L,m)}\beta_{\lambda,\nu}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\,\mathbb{F}_{\nu}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m}|\boldsymbol{b}_{m},\boldsymbol{a}_{m}),

where

(4.27)

\beta_{\lambda,\nu}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\frac{1}{\mathsf{c}_{n}(\nu|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})}\left\llangle\mathbb{f}_{\nu}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\,\middle|\,\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\right\rrangle_{n}.

Coefficients
\beta_{\lambda,\nu}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})

are analytic functions of parameters
\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}
for
|a_{1}|,\dots,|a_{n-1}|
,
|b_{1}|,\dots,|b_{n-1}|<1
which can be seen by a direct inspection of each term in the right hand side of (
4.27
).
Using
Lemma

4.6
in the expansion (
4.26
), we reduce the relation to

(4.28)

\begin{split}&\sum_{\mu\in\mathrm{Box}(L,m)}\alpha_{\lambda,\mu}(\boldsymbol{a}_{m},\boldsymbol{b}_{m})m_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m})\\
&\qquad=\sum_{\nu\in\mathrm{Box}(L,m)}\sum_{\mu\in\mathrm{Box}(L,m)}\,\beta_{\lambda,\nu}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\alpha_{\nu,\mu}(\boldsymbol{a}_{m},\boldsymbol{b}_{m})\,m_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{y}_{m}).\end{split}

Define the finite dimensional square matrices

(4.29)

\displaystyle\alpha(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\left[\alpha_{\lambda,\mu}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\right]_{\lambda,\mu\in\mathrm{Box}(L,n)},

\displaystyle\beta(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\left[\beta_{\lambda,\mu}(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\right]_{\lambda,\mu\in\mathrm{Box}(L,n)}.

Then, relation (
4.28
) implies the matrix relation

(4.30)

\alpha(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\beta(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\alpha(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}).

For
a_{1}=\cdots=a_{n-1}=b_{1}=\cdots=b_{n-1}=0
, the matrix
\alpha
is upper triangular and
\alpha_{\lambda,\lambda}=1
and as a result it is invertible. Since each coefficient of
\alpha(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})
is analytic for
|a_{i}|,|b_{j}|<1
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

(4.31)

\det\left[\alpha(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\right]>0,\qquad\forall|a_{1}|,\dots,|a_{n-1}|,|b_{1}|,\dots,|b_{n-1}|\leq\varepsilon.

Then,
\alpha(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})
remains invertible in the same region and relation (
4.30
) implies that

(4.32)

\beta(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\mathrm{Id},\qquad\forall|a_{1}|,\dots,|a_{n-1}|,|b_{1}|,\dots,|b_{n-1}|\leq\varepsilon.

Since
\beta(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})
is analytic we have
\beta(\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\mathrm{Id}
for all
|a_{1}|,\dots,|a_{n-1}|,|b_{1}|,\dots|b_{n-1}|<1
, showing that in the same region of parameters the desired orthogonality relation (
1.17
) holds for all partitions
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{Y}_{m}
. Setting
m=n-1
, we see that to conclude the proof we are only left to check that the orthogonality relation (
1.17
) also holds when at least one of the two partitions
\lambda,\mu
has length equal to
n
. Since the case when
\lambda_{n}\neq\mu_{n}
is covered by
Lemma

4.4
, we assume that
\lambda_{n}=\mu_{n}
. In this case, using the shift property (
2.10
), we have

(4.33)

\begin{split}&\left\llangle\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\middle|\mathbb{f}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\right\rrangle_{n}\\
&=\left\llangle\left(z_{1}\cdots z_{n}\right)^{\lambda_{n}}\mathbb{f}_{\tilde{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\middle|\left(z_{1}\cdots z_{n}\right)^{\mu_{n}}\mathbb{f}_{\tilde{\mu}}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\right\rrangle_{n}\\
&=\left\llangle\mathbb{f}_{\tilde{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\middle|\mathbb{f}_{\tilde{\mu}}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\right\rrangle_{n}\\
&=\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{\lambda}=\tilde{\mu}}\mathsf{c}_{n}(\tilde{\lambda}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}),\end{split}

where we denoted

(4.34)

\tilde{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{n},\dots,\lambda_{n-1}-\lambda_{n},0),\qquad\tilde{\mu}=(\mu_{1}-\mu_{n},\dots,\mu_{n-1}-\mu_{n},0).

This concludes the proof since

(4.35)

\mathsf{c}_{n}(\tilde{\lambda}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\mathsf{c}_{n}(\lambda|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}),

as it can be verified from the expression (
1.18
).
∎

Remark 4.1
.

Setting
a_{i}=b_{i}=\sqrt{q}
, assuming
q\in(0,1)
, makes the expression (
1.17
) symmetric and the polynomials
\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|(\sqrt{q},\dots,\sqrt{q}),(\sqrt{q},\dots,\sqrt{q}))
become self-orthonormal. It is not clear if this simplification in the
L^{2}
norms
\mathsf{c}_{n}(\lambda|(\sqrt{q},\dots,\sqrt{q}),(\sqrt{q},\dots,\sqrt{q}))
has any deeper implication.

4.3.
Proofs of
Theorem

1.2
and
Theorem

1.3

Let us now prove that the spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials are a basis of the space of
n
-variate symmetric polynomials, denoted by
\mathsf{Sym}_{n}
. First, we report the next lemma.

Lemma 4.7
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The families of symmetric polynomials
\{\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}}
and
\{\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}}
generate the space
\mathsf{Sym}_{n}
.

Proof.

Let us first prove that the family of spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials
\{\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}}
generate the space of
n
-variate of symmetric polynomials. From the Pieri rule (
2.40
), we have

(4.36)

\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}e_{k}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})(-u)^{k}\right)\mathbb{F}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})=\sum_{\lambda}c_{\lambda,\mu}(u)\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n}),

where
e_{k}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})
is the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomials in
n
variables and

(4.37)

c_{\lambda,\mu}(u)=E(\boldsymbol{a}_{n};u)\overline{F}_{\lambda^{\prime}/\mu^{\prime}}(u|\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}).

This implies that, for any
\nu\in\mathbb{Y}
, we can express the product of elementary symmetric polynomials

(4.38)

e_{\nu}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})=e_{\nu_{1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})\cdots e_{\ell(\nu)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}),

as a linear combination of spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials. Since the set of symmetric polynomials
\{e_{\nu}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})\}_{\nu\in\mathbb{Y}}
generates
\mathsf{Sym}_{n}
, so do the
n
-variate spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials
\{\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}}
.

The analogous statement for the family
\{\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}}
can be proved in the same way using the corresponding Pieri rules.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

This is a straightforward consequence of
Theorem

1.1
and of
Lemma

4.7
. An alternative proof is as follows
1
1

1

Suggested by Travis Scrimshaw.
. The spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials are linearly independent as a result of
Theorem

1.1
. Moreover, by
Lemma

4.6
and the shift property of
Proposition

2.2
we have

(4.39)

\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\in\mathrm{Span}\{m_{\nu}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})\}_{\nu\in\mathrm{Box}(L,n)},\qquad\text{for all }\lambda\in\mathrm{Box}(L,n).

This implies that, for all
L>0
, we have

(4.40)

\mathrm{Span}\{\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\}_{\lambda\in\mathrm{Box}(L,n)}=\mathrm{Span}\{m_{\nu}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})\}_{\nu\in\mathrm{Box}(L,n)},

which proves that
\{\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}}
generates
\mathsf{Sym}_{n}
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

We proceed by contradiction. For this assume that there exists a set
S\subset\mathbb{Y}_{n}
and non-zero coefficients
\{\gamma_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in S}
such that

(4.41)

\sum_{\lambda\in S}\gamma_{\lambda}\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})=0.

Replacing
\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})
with
\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n+1}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n})
in the left hand side of (
4.41
) we obtain the symmetric polynomial in
n+1
variables

(4.42)

f(\boldsymbol{x}_{n+1})=\sum_{\lambda\in S}\gamma_{\lambda}\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n+1}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n}).

By
Proposition

2.1
, the polynomial
f(\boldsymbol{x}_{n+1})
reduces to the left hand side of
(
4.41
)
setting
x_{i}=0
for any
i=1,\dots,n+1
. As a result the monomial
x_{1}\cdots\,x_{n+1}
divides the polynomial
f(\boldsymbol{x}_{n+1})
and we can write

(4.43)

f(\boldsymbol{x}_{n+1})=x_{1}\cdots\,x_{n+1}\,\tilde{f}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n+1}),

where
\tilde{f}
is another symmetric polynomial in
n+1
variables. Expanding
\tilde{f}
in the basis of
\mathbb{f}
polynomials, we get

(4.44)

\tilde{f}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n+1})=\sum_{\mu}\tilde{\gamma}_{\mu}\,\mathbb{f}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n+1}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n}),

for some coefficients
\tilde{\gamma}_{\mu}
. This yields the expansion

(4.45)

f(\boldsymbol{x}_{n+1})=\sum_{\mu}\tilde{\gamma}_{\mu}\,\mathbb{f}_{(\mu_{1}+1,\dots,\mu_{n+1}+1)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n+1}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n},\boldsymbol{b}_{n}),

using the shift property (
2.10
). Since polynomials
\mathbb{f}
form a basis, the two expansions (
4.42
) and (
4.45
) need to be equal, which is a contradiction since the set
S
contains only partitions of length strictly less than
n+1
.
∎

5.
Special cases

In this section we consider several special cases and scaling limits of the spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials and the corresponding specializations of
Theorem

1.1
and
Theorem

1.2
. To avoid exceedingly technical arguments, in
Section

5.3
, which is dedicated to the spin Whittaker functions, the discussion will be kept at a formal level.

5.1.
Inhomogeneous symmetric Grothendieck polynomials

The first specialization of the spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials we consider is setting
q=0
and
a_{1}=\cdots=a_{n-1}=0
, leading to the definitions of polynomials

(5.1)

G_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\big|_{q=0,\,a_{1}=\cdots=a_{n-1}=0}

and their variant

(5.2)

\overline{G}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\big|_{q=0,\,a_{1}=\cdots=a_{n-1}=0}.

The polynomials
G
are the (inhomogeneous) symmetric Grothendieck polynomials
[
CP21
,
HJK+24
]
; see
[
FK94
]
for the case
b_{1}=\cdots=b_{n}=\beta
and also
[
LS82
,
Buc02
]
for the case
b_{1}=\cdots=b_{n}=1
. They are more commonly defined through the bialternant formulas

(5.3)

G_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\frac{\det\left[x_{i}^{\lambda_{j}+n-j}\prod_{k=1}^{j-1}(1-x_{i}b_{k})\right]_{i,j=1,\dots,n}}{\det\left[x_{i}^{n-j}\right]_{i,j=1,\dots,n}}.

Notice that, as a consequence of
Proposition

2.3
, from (
5.3
) one gets

(5.4)

\overline{G}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\frac{\det\left[x_{i}^{\lambda_{j}}\prod_{k=1}^{n-j}(x_{i}-b_{n-k})\right]_{i,j=1,\dots,n}}{\det\left[x_{i}^{n-j}\right]_{i,j=1,\dots,n}}.

To verify that the polynomials
G
defined specializing the spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials correspond to the Grothendieck polynomials, one can check that the branching rule (
1.7
) reduces, after setting
q=a_{1}=\cdots=a_{n}=0
, to the set-valued tableaux combinatorial formula given in
[
HJK+24
, Theorem 3.2]
or
[
CP21
, Theorem 3.4]
, which generalizes previously known combinatorial formulas for the case
b_{1}=\cdots=b_{n}=1

[
Buc02
]
; see also
[
Yel17
, Theorem 4.2]
. Alternatively one can observe that, again as a consequence of the branching rule (
1.7
), we have

(5.5)

G_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=m_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})+\sum_{\mu>\lambda}c_{\lambda,\mu}m_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}),\qquad\overline{G}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=m_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})+\sum_{\mu<\lambda}\overline{c}_{\lambda,\mu}m_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}),

for some coefficients
\{c_{\lambda,\mu}\},\{\overline{c}_{\lambda,\mu}\}
and specializing the Cauchy identities (
2.27
), we have

(5.6)

\sum_{\lambda}G_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\overline{G}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1-b_{j}x_{i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-x_{i}y_{j})}.

Properties (
5.5
), (
5.6
), which are also satisfied by Grothendieck polynomials (see e.g.
[
GP24
, eq. (3.7)]
), uniquely identify the polynomials
G,\overline{G}
and therefore (
5.3
), (
5.4
) hold.

Remark 5.1
.

In
[
MS13
]
they also define the functions
\overline{G}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})
, which differ by ours, in the case
b_{1}=\cdots=b_{n-1}=-\beta
by a multiplication of a factor
\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\beta/y_{i})^{n-1}
.

To specialize the orthogonality result of
Theorem

1.1
to the inhomogeneous symmetric Grothendieck polynomials, we define the scalar product

(5.7)

\left\langle f|g\right\rangle_{n,\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}}=\frac{1}{n!}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}f(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})g(\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{n})\frac{\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}(1-z_{i}/z_{j})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1-z_{i}b_{j})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{i}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z_{i}}.

Theorem 5.1
.

We have

(5.8)

\left\langle G_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})|\overline{G}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\right\rangle_{n,\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1}}=\mathbf{1}_{\lambda=\mu}.

We have not been able to locate the result of
Theorem

5.1
in literature. In the Schur polynomials case, when
b_{1}=\cdots=b_{n-1}=0
, the orthogonality (
5.8
) corresponds to the orthogonality of characters of irreducible polynomial representations of the unitary group
U(n)
. It would be interesting to find if a similar representation theoretic interpretation can be given to the orthogonality result of
Theorem

5.1
.

Remark 5.2
.

The closest result to
Theorem

5.1
we were able to find in literature is in
[
MS13
]
, where the polynomials
G_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})
were defined for the case
b_{1}=\cdots=b_{n-1}=-\beta
. There, in
[
MS13
, Theorem 5.6]
, authors give the orthogonality result for the pair
G_{\lambda},\overline{G}_{\mu}

(5.9)

\sum_{\{\boldsymbol{z}_{n}\}}w_{M}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n})G_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{n}|(-\beta,\dots,-\beta))\overline{G}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{n}|(-\beta,\dots,-\beta))=\mathbf{1}_{\lambda=\mu},

where the sum runs over the set of roots of a certain family of polynomial equations (the Bethe ansatz equations) and partitions
\lambda,\mu
are bounded by quantities depending on
n,M
. Above the weight
w_{M}
depends on the positive integer
M
and letting
M\to+\infty
it is natural to conjecture that one should recover (
5.8
), for the particular case
b_{1}=\cdots=b_{n-1}=-\beta
.

5.2.
Interpolation
q
-Whittaker polynomials

Following
[
Kor24
]
, we define the

-Whittaker polynomials

(5.10)

P_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})\big|_{a_{1}=\cdots=a_{n-1}=0}

and their dual variant

(5.11)

\overline{P}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1})\big|_{a_{1}=\cdots=a_{n-1}=0}.

These are at the same time
q
-deformation of the Grothendieck polynomials discussed in the previous subsection and multi parameter inhomogenous generalizations of the

-Whittaker polynomials. In
[
Kor24
]
, it was shown that the polynomials

.

Example 5.1
.

For the case
n=2
and
\lambda=(2,0)

5.3.
Formal reduction to inhomogeneous spin Whittaker functions

In this section we define the
inhomogeneous spin Whittaker
functions, which are inhomogeneous variants of the functions defined in
[
MP22
]
. They are multi parameter generalizations of the
GL(n)
-Whittaker functions
[
Kos78
,
Giv97
]
in the same way the spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials generalize the
q
-Whittaker polynomials. As briefly discussed below, the scaling limit linking the spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials to the spin Whittaker functions, unlike in the previous subsections, is singular as it involves in multiple places the convergence of the
q
-Pochhammer symbols to the Euler Gamma function. For this reason, to avoid potentially lengthy proofs, we will keep the discussion formal and one can treat the statements presented as conjectures.

We define the Weyl chamber of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}^{n}
as

(5.15)

\mathcal{W}_{n}=\left\{\boldsymbol{L}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}^{n}:L_{n}\leq\cdots\leq L_{1}\right\}.

Between elements of the Weyl chambers
\boldsymbol{L}_{n-1}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}_{n-1}
and
\boldsymbol{L}_{n}\in\mathcal{W}_{n}
, we define the interlacing relations

(5.16)

\boldsymbol{L}^{\prime}_{n-1}\preceq\boldsymbol{L}_{n},\qquad\text{if}\qquad L_{i+1}\leq L^{\prime}_{i}\leq L_{i},\qquad\text{for all }i=1,\dots,n-1.

Define the inhomogeneous skew spin Whittaker function

(5.17)

\mathfrak{f}_{X}(\boldsymbol{L}_{n-1}^{\prime};\boldsymbol{L}_{n}|\boldsymbol{A}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{B}_{n-1})=\mathbf{1}_{\boldsymbol{L}_{n-1}^{\prime}\preceq\boldsymbol{L}_{n}}\left(\frac{L_{1}\cdots L_{n}}{L_{1}^{\prime}\cdots L_{n-1}^{\prime}}\right)^{-X}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathcal{A}_{A_{i},B_{i};X}(L_{i},L_{i}^{\prime},L_{i+1}),

where

(5.18)

\mathcal{A}_{A,B;X}(u,v,z)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Beta}(A-X,B+X)}\left(1-\frac{v}{z}\right)^{A-X-1}\left(1-\frac{u}{v}\right)^{B+X-1}\left(1-\frac{u}{z}\right)^{1-A-B},

for all
1\leq u<v<z
and
A>X>-B
. Above Beta denotes Euler’s Beta function. Through a branching rule we define the inhomogeneous spin Whittaker function as

(5.19)

\mathfrak{f}_{\boldsymbol{X}_{n}}(\boldsymbol{L}_{n}|\boldsymbol{A}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{B}_{n-1})=\int_{\boldsymbol{L}_{n-1}^{\prime}\preceq\boldsymbol{L}_{n}}\mathfrak{f}_{\boldsymbol{X}_{n-1}}(\boldsymbol{L}_{n-1}^{\prime}|\tau\boldsymbol{A}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{B}_{n-2})\mathfrak{f}_{X_{n}}(\boldsymbol{L}_{n-1}^{\prime};\boldsymbol{L}_{n}|\boldsymbol{A}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{B}_{n-1})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}L_{i}}{L_{i}},

where variables
\boldsymbol{X}_{n}
and parameters
\boldsymbol{A}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{B}_{n-1}
are complex numbers satisfying the conditions

(5.20)

\Re\{A_{i}-X_{j}\},\Re\{B_{i}+X_{j}\}>0,\qquad\text{for all }i=1,\dots,n-1\text{ and }j=1,\dots,n.

The spin Whittaker functions should be recovered as a limit of the spin
q
-Whittaker polynomials as

(5.21)

\lim_{q\to 1}\left(\log q^{-1}\right)^{-\binom{n}{2}}\mathbb{f}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}|\boldsymbol{a}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{b}_{n-1})=\mathfrak{f}_{\boldsymbol{X}_{n}}(\boldsymbol{L}_{n}|\boldsymbol{A}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{B}_{n-1}),

under the scaling

(5.22)

x_{i}=q^{X_{i}},\qquad a_{i}=q^{A_{i}},\qquad b_{i}=q^{B_{i}},\qquad\lambda_{i}=\lfloor\log_{q}(1/L_{i})\rfloor.

In
[
MP22
]
the functions
\mathfrak{f}_{\boldsymbol{X}_{n}}(\boldsymbol{L}_{n}|\boldsymbol{A}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{B}_{n-1})
were defined in the particular (homogeneous) case
A_{1}=\dots=A_{n-1}=B_{1}=\cdots=B_{n-1}=S>0
and the limit (
5.21
) was shown to hold locally uniformly for
\boldsymbol{L}_{n}\in\mathcal{W}_{n}
. These functions were shown to satisfy integral identities which are continuous analogs of the Cauchy identities (
2.28
). Furthermore it was shown that they are eigenfunction of the second order differential operator

(5.23)

-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{u_{i}}^{2}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}S^{-2(j-i)}e^{u_{j}-u_{i}}(S-\partial_{u_{i}})(S+\partial_{u_{j}}),

where
L_{j}=S^{N+1-2j}e^{u_{j}}
and with eigenvalue

(5.24)

-\frac{1}{2}\left(X_{1}^{2}+\cdots+X_{n}^{2}\right).

Such eigenrelations arises as a scaling limit of the Pieri rules (
2.40
), following the recipe of
[
GLO12
]
, and as a result their inhomogeneous extensiond should exist, although we do not compute them here.

We conclude addressing the orthogonality of the functions
\mathfrak{f}_{\boldsymbol{X}_{n}}
, which was conjectured in a special case in
[
MP22
]
. Define the Sklyanin-type density

(5.25)

\mathfrak{M}(\boldsymbol{Z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{A}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{B}_{n-1})=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\Gamma(A_{j}-Z_{i})\Gamma(B_{j}+Z_{i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\Gamma(A_{i}+B_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\Gamma(A_{i}+B_{j})}\mathfrak{M}(\boldsymbol{Z}_{n}),

(5.26)

\mathfrak{M}(\boldsymbol{Z}_{n})=\frac{1}{n!(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{n}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{1}{\Gamma(Z_{i}-Z_{j})}.

A formal limit of
Theorem

1.1
should show that, for all

\begin{split}&\int_{(\mathrm{i}\mathbb{R})^{n}}\mathfrak{f}_{\boldsymbol{Z}_{n}}(\boldsymbol{L}_{n}|\boldsymbol{A}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{B}_{n-1})\mathfrak{f}_{-\boldsymbol{Z}_{n}}(\boldsymbol{L}_{n}^{\prime}|\boldsymbol{B}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{A}_{n-1})\mathfrak{M}(\boldsymbol{Z}_{n}|\boldsymbol{A}_{n-1},\boldsymbol{B}_{n-1})\mathrm{d}Z_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}Z_{n}\\

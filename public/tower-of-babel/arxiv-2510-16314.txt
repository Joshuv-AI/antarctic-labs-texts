Abstract
. Given an algebraic difference equation of the form σ n ​ ( y ) = f ⁡ ( y , σ ⁡ ( y ) , … , σ n − 1 ​ ( y ) ) \sigma^{n}(y)=f\big(y,\sigma(y),\dots,\sigma^{n-1}(y)\big) where f f is a rational function over a field k k of characteristic zero on which σ \sigma acts trivially, it is shown that if there is a nontrivial algebraic relation amongst any number of σ \sigma -disjoint solutions, along with their σ \sigma -transforms, then there is already such a relation between three solutions. Here “ σ \sigma -disjoint” means a ≠ σ r ​ ( b ) a\neq\sigma^{r}(b) for any integer r r . A weaker version of the theorem, where “three” is replaced by n + 4 n+4 , is also obtained when σ \sigma acts nontrivially on k k . Along the way a number of other structural results about primitive rational dynamical systems are established. 1.
Introduction

The starting point of this paper is the following theorem about algebraic differential equations established several years ago (this is
[
10
, Theorem A]
):

Given an algebraic differential equation over constant parameters, if there is any nontrivial algebraic relation amongst any number of distinct nonalgebraic solutions, along with their derivatives, then there is already such a relation between two solutions.

(If one allows nonconstant parameters than one has to replace “two” with “three” in the conclusion, and assume the equation is of order greater than
1
.)
One is lead to ask: Does the analogous fact hold true of algebraic
difference
equations?

Let us make the question more precise.
Fix an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero, equipped with an automorphism
\sigma:k\to k
.
Consider

(E)

P\big(y,\sigma(y),\dots,\sigma^{n}(y)\big)=0

an algebraic difference equation where
y
is a single variable and
P\in k[y_{0},\dots,y_{n}]
is an irreducible polynomial.
In the differential case we were interested in arbitrary
distinct
solutions, but here that would not make much sense.
For example, in the case when
\sigma
is the identity on
k
, if
b_{1}
is a solution to (
E
) then so is
b_{2}:=\sigma(b_{1})
, regardless of the equation, and there is an obvious nontrivial transformally algebraic relation between these solutions.
So, at the very least, we should be restricting our attention to solutions that are not transforms of each other to start with.
That is, for each
m\geq 1
, we consider the following transcendence condition that may or may not hold of solutions to (
E
):

(C_{m})

Suppose
b_{1},\dots,b_{m}
are solutions to (
E
), not in
k
.
If

(\sigma^{r}(b_{i}):r=0,\dots,n-1,i=1,\dots,m)

is not algebraically independent over
k
it is because
b_{i}=\sigma^{\ell}b_{j}
for some
i<j
and
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
.

What
(C_{m})
expresses is that any
m
pairwise “
\sigma
-disjoint” solutions to (
E
) are as transformally independent of each other as is consistent with the fact that they each satisfy an order
n
algebraic difference equation.
Here we are implicitly considering solutions in arbitrary difference field extensions of
(k,\sigma)
.

Note that
(C_{m})\implies(C_{\ell})
for all
\ell\leq m
.

For example, it follows from work of Chatzidakis and Hrushovski in
[
6
]
, that the equation
\sigma(y)-y^{2}-1=0
satisfies
(C_{m})
for all
m\geq 1
.
At the opposite extreme,
\sigma(y)-y=0
already fails to satisfy
(C_{2})
.

Question
.

Is there an absolute bound
\ell
such that

(C_{\ell})\implies(C_{m})
for all
m\geq\ell
?
If so, what is that bound?

Restricting our attention to the autonomous case, and considering only equations presented in a certain natural form, we provide the following answer:

Theorem 1
.

Suppose
\sigma
is the identity on
k
and
(\ref{generalde})
is of the form

\displaystyle\sigma^{n}(y)=f\big(y,\sigma(y),\dots,\sigma^{n-1}(y)\big)

for some
f\in k(y_{0},\dots,y_{n-1})
.
Then
(C_{3})\implies(C_{m})
for all
m
.

The is part of Corollary
7.13
below.
The other part drops the assumption that
\sigma
is the identity on
k
but then only provides a bound depending on
n
(namely
n+4
).

While we expect the result to be sharp, we do not yet have an example of an autonomous equation satisfying
(C_{2})
but not
(C_{3})
.

The reason we restrict to difference equations of the form appearing in Theorem
1
is that such equations give rise to
rational dynamical systems
, namely pairs
(V,\phi)
where
V
is an irreducible variety over
k
and
\phi:V\dashrightarrow V
is a dominant rational map.
And this article is really about (the applications of model theory to) algebraic dynamics.
Such applications have, by now, a long history, with notable achievements by Chatzidakis-Hrushovski in
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
and Medvedev-Scanlon in
[
16
]
.
The immediate antecedent to this paper, however, is
[
15
]
, where we developed model-theoretic binding groups for rational dynamical systems.

Underpinning our proof of Theorem
1
is an analysis of the quantifier-free fragment of the theory of existentially closed difference fields.
But we delay describing our general model-theoretic results until later in this introduction, beginning instead with two more theorems about rational dynamical systems that we prove along the way.
Implicit when we speak of rational dynamical systems is that
\sigma
acts trivially on
k
, and for now this assumption remains in place.

The following list of conditions on a rational dynamical system should give the reader a sense of what aspects of algebraic dynamics we are able to investigate.
Recall that the natural morphisms between rational dynamical systems are
dominant equivariant rational maps
,
f:(V,\phi)\dashrightarrow(W,\psi)
, namely dominant rational maps
f:V\dashrightarrow W
over
k
such that
\psi f=f\phi
.

Definition
.

Suppose
(V,\phi)
is a rational dynamical system.

(a)

(V,\phi)
is
simple
if for any rational dynamical system
(W,\psi)
, and
Z
an irreducible invariant subvariety of
(V\times W,\phi\times\psi)
that projects dominantly onto both
V
and
W
, either
Z=V\times W
or
\dim Z=\dim W
.

(b)

(V,\phi)
is
primitive
if whenever
(V,\phi)\dashrightarrow(W,\psi)
is a dominant equivariant rational map then
\dim W
is either
0
or
\dim V
.

(c)

(V,\phi)
is
strongly primitive
if
(V^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
is primitive whenever
(V^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
admits a dominant equivariant rational map to
(V,\phi)
and
\dim V^{\prime}=\dim V
.

(d)

(V,\phi)
is
isotrivial
if for some rational dynamical system
(W,\psi)
, and some
\ell\geq 1
, there is an equivariant rational embedding of
(V\times W,\phi\times\psi)
into
(\mathbb{A}^{\ell}\times W,\operatorname{id}\times\psi)
over
(W,\psi)
.

(e)

By a
rich invariant family of subvarieties of
(V,\phi)
we mean a rational dynamical system
(W,\psi)
along with an irreducible invariant subvariety
Z\subseteq(V\times W,\phi\times\psi)
such that:

Z
projects dominantly onto both
V
and
W
, the general fibres of
Z\to W
are absolutely irreducible subvarieties of
V
, over distinct general points of
W
the fibres are distinct, and

\dim Z>\dim V
.

It is not hard to see that simple rational dynamics are strongly primitive.
In Section
8.1
, below, we will exhibit an example showing that the converse fails.

If we begin with an arbitrary rational dynamical system then, by taking successive equivariant images and finite covers, we eventually obtain a strongly primitive one.
It is therefore natural to focus first on the structure of strongly primitive rational dynamics.
We prove the following dichotomy:

Theorem 2
.

Suppose
(V,\phi)
is a strongly primitive rational dynamical system.
Then one of the following holds:

(1)

(V,\phi)
is simple and whenever
V_{0}
is an irreducible invariant subvariety of of some cartesian power of
(V,\phi)
that projects dominantly onto
V
in each co-ordinate, then
(V_{0},\phi)
admits no rich invariant families of subvarieties, or

(2)

(V,\phi)
admits a dominant equivariant rational map
(V,\phi)\dashrightarrow(V^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
, where
\dim V^{\prime}=\dim V
, and
(V^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
is isotrivial.

Dynamics on simple abelian varieties provide examples of both cases: an endomorphism of a simple abelian variety that is not of finite order will land in case (1) while translation by a nontorsion
k
-point is an example of case (2).
The former is due to Hrushovski
[
13
]
, see the discussion in Example
3.8
below, and the latter is worked out in Section
8.1
below.

In fact, case (2) always implies that, up to birational equivalence,
\phi
comes from an algebraic group action on
V
– namely, there is a faithful algebraic group action
G\times V\to V
such that
\phi
agrees with the action of some
k
-point of
G
.
Indeed, that this follows from (2) was first observed (without using model theory) in
[
2
, Corollary A]
, and then given a model-theoretic account in
[
15
, Theorem 5.1]
.
As dynamical systems coming from algebraic group actions are generally well understood,
and as one can often reduce to the strongly primitive case, we expect the above theorem to allow a reduction of some open questions in algebraic dynamics to case (1).
A further analysis of case (1) using model-theoretic tools exists, however it appears to require the more flexible category of algebraic correspondences rather than rational dynamics, and we do not pursue it here.

Combining Theorem
2
with earlier work in
[
15
]
we are able to give the following criteria for simplicity:

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
(V,\phi)
is a rational dynamical system.
Suppose that any proper irreducible invariant subvariety
Z
of
(V^{3},\phi)
that projects dominantly onto both the first co-ordinate and the last
2
co-ordinates is of dimension
2\dim V
.
Then
(V,\phi)
is simple.

This theorem is saying that in the definition simplicity given above we need only consider
(W,\psi)=(V^{2},\phi)
.
We expect that one can replace
(V^{2},\phi)
by
(V,\phi)
itself, but so far we only see how to do so after replacing
\phi
by an iterate.

Theorems
2
and
3
are special cases of Corollaries
5.4
and
6.9
below, respectively, which hold for what are called
rational
\sigma
-varieties
.
These are defined in Section
2.1
, and generalise rational dynamical systems by allowing for a twist by an automorphism of the base field
k
.

1.1.
Our general model-theoretic results

A distinguishing feature of our work here, and in
[
15
]
, is the systematic focus on the quantifier-free fragment of the theory of existentially closed difference fields in characteristic zero (
\operatorname{ACFA}_{0}
).
Theorems
2
and
3
, and indeed their generalisations to rational
\sigma
-varieties,
are geometric formulations of statements we prove about quanitifer-free types in
\operatorname{ACFA}_{0}
.
We describe those now, referring the reader to Section
2
for a review of the model-theoretic terminology.

We work in a sufficiently saturated model
(\mathcal{U},\sigma)\models\operatorname{ACFA}_{0}
, with fixed field
\mathcal{C}
, and over an algebraically closed inversive difference subfield
(k,\sigma)
.
So we are no longer assuming that
\sigma
acts trivially on
k
, though, as we will see, that assumption will at times improve our results significantly.

Among the complete quantifier-free types over
k
, we restrict our attention the the
rational
types, those that imply a formula of the form
\sigma(x)=\phi(x)
for some rational (multivariable) function
\phi
.
Using the canonical base property (CBP), established in
[
18
]
, and the Zilber dichotomy for minimal types, which follows from the CBP but was first established in
[
6
]
, we prove:

Theorem 4
.

Suppose
p
is a rational type over
k
satisfying the following property:
if
a\models p
and
b\in\operatorname{acl}(ka)\setminus k
is such that
\operatorname{qftp}(b/k)
is rational then
a\in\operatorname{acl}(kb)
.
Then one of the following hold:

(1)

p
is minimal and one-based, or

(2)

there is a finite-to-one
rational map
p\to q
where
q
is rational and qf-internal to
\mathcal{C}
.

This appears as Theorem
5.3
below.
It is the analogue of a fact about complete finite
U
-rank types in stable theories established in
[
17
, Proposition 2.3]
.

Theorem
2
is a geometric formulation of Theorem
4
in the case that
k\subset\mathcal{C}
.

Combining Theorem
4
with our earlier results on quantifier-free binding groups from
[
15
]
, allows us to obtain bounds on the
degree of nonminimality
for a rational type.
This quantity, when
p
is a nonalgebraic and nonminimal rational type, denoted by
\operatorname{nmdeg}(p)
, is the least positive integer
d
such that there are realisations
a_{1},\dots,a_{d}
of
p
, and a nonalgebraic forking extension of
p
to a complete quantifier-free type over
k(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})
.
Degree of nonminimality for complete finite
U
-rank types in stable theories were introduced in
[
12
]
where initial bounds for the theory
\operatorname{DCF}_{0}
were obtained.
It was later shown, in
[
11
]
, that
\operatorname{DCF}_{0}
admits an absolute bound of
2
for the degree of nonminimality, which goes down to
1
if the type is over constant parameters.
Such bounds are certainly specific to
\operatorname{DCF}_{0}
and similar theories: recent work of Baldwin-Freitag-Mutchnik
[
1
]
shows that the degree of nonmininality may take on any positive integer value if one allows arbitrary
\omega
-stable theories.
We obtain the following weaker (and we expect not optimal) bounds for rational types in
\operatorname{ACFA}_{0}
.

Theorem 5
.

Suppose
p
is a rational type over
k
.

(a)

\operatorname{nmdeg}(p)\leq\dim(p)+3
.

(b)

If
k\subset\mathcal{C}
then
\operatorname{nmdeg}(p)\leq 2
.

This appears as Theorem
6.5
below.

Theorem
3
is a geometric formulation of Theorem
5
(b).

Finally, let us give the model-theoretic formulation (and generalisation) of Theorem
1
itself.
Informed by ideas in
[
10
]
we introduce here the notion of
m
-disintegration
for a rational type: every set of
m
realisations is independent unless one is a transform of another by some power of
\sigma
.
We are able to show:

Theorem 6
.

Suppose
p
is a rational type over
k
.

(a)

(\dim(p)+4)
-disintegration implies
m
-disintegration for all
m
.

(b)

If
k\subset\mathcal{C}
then
3
-disintegration implies
m
-disintegration for all
m
.

This appears as Theorem
7.6
below (taking into account Theorem
6.5
).

Plan of the paper

After recalling (and elaborating on) preliminaries in Section

In particular, we give the geometric characterisation of minimality there in terms of simple rational

In Section

This can be extracted from the results in

2.
Preliminaries

We work in a sufficiently saturated model
(\mathcal{U},\sigma)\models\operatorname{ACFA}
with fixed field denoted by
\mathcal{C}
.
We will eventually assume that the characteristic is zero, but for now we allow positive characteristic.

We also fix a base algebraically closed inversive difference subfield
(k,\sigma)
.

In what follows we review and expand on some notions/terminology from
[
15
]
.

2.1.
Generalised algebraic dynamics

By a
rational
\sigma
-variety
we mean an irreducible variety
V
over
k
equipped with a dominant rational map
\phi:V\dashrightarrow V^{\sigma}
, from
V
to its transform with respect to the action of
\sigma
on
k
.
So, when
\sigma
is the identity on
k
, what we call the
autonomous
case, a rational
\sigma
-variety is a rational dynamical system.

By an
invariant subvariety

Z
of a rational
\sigma
-variety
(V,\phi)
we will mean an irreducible (closed) subvariety
Z\subseteq V
over
k
such that
Z\cap{\operatorname{dom}(\phi)}
is nonempty and
\phi(Z)
is Zariski dense in
Z^{\sigma}
.
So we get an induced rational
\sigma
-variety
(Z,\phi|_{Z})
.
Our invariant subvarieties will always be irreducible, but we will reinforce that by using the term “irreducible invariant subvariety” throughout.

An
equivariant
rational map,
f:(V,\phi)\dashrightarrow(W,\psi)
, is a rational map
f:V\dashrightarrow W
such that
\psi f=f^{\sigma}\phi
as rational maps on
V
.

We associate to a rational
\sigma
-variety its “sharp” points, namely the
k
-definable set
(V,\phi)^{\sharp}:=\{a\in{\operatorname{dom}(\phi)}:\sigma(a)=\phi(a)\}
.
Here the action of
\sigma
is co-ordinatewise.
The
generic quantifier-free type
of
(V,\phi)
is the collection of (quantifier-free) formulas
p(x)
expressing that
x
is a Zariski generic point of
V
over
k
and that
x\in(V,\phi)^{\sharp}
.
Existential closedness of
(\mathcal{U},\sigma)
ensures that this is a consistent and complete quantifier-free type.
Realisations are called
generic points
of
(V,\phi)
.

2.2.
Rational types

By a
rational type over
k
in
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
we mean a complete quantifier-free type,

p(x)\in S_{\operatorname{qf}}(k)
, which implies a formula of the form
\sigma(x)=\phi(x)

for some
\phi=(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
where each
f_{i}\in k(x)
is a rational function.
The generic quantifier-free type of a rational
\sigma
-variety over
k
is rational, and all rational types arise this way.

Note that
\operatorname{qftp}(a/k)
is rational if and only if the difference field that
a
generates over
k
is just the field generated by
a
over
k
.
That is, rationality is equivalent to
k\langle a\rangle=k(a)
.
It is also worth observing that a consequence of
\operatorname{qftp}(a/k)
being rational (and
k
being inversive) is that
\operatorname{acl}(ka)=k(a)^{{\operatorname{alg}}}
.

If
p,q\in S_{\operatorname{qf}}(k)
are rational types then by a
rational map

f:p\to q
we mean that
f
is a rational function over
k
such that
f(a)\models q
for all (equivalently some)
a\models p
.
These arise as the restrictions of dominant rational equivariant maps which we tend to also denote by
f:(V,\phi)\dashrightarrow(W,\psi)
, where
p
is the generic quantifier-free type of
(V,\phi)
and
q
is the generic quantifier-free type of
(W,\psi)
.
Note that, unlike in the case of complete types,
f
need not
be surjective as a set map from
p(\mathcal{U})\to q(\mathcal{U})
.
For example,
x\mapsto x^{2}
is a dominant equivariant map
(\mathbb{A}^{1},\operatorname{id})\to(\mathbb{A}^{1},\operatorname{id})
that does not restrict to a surjective map on the generic quantifier-free types (as there are transcendental fixed points whose square roots are not fixed).

We say that
f:p\to q
is
birational
if
k(a)=k(f(a))
for some (equivalently all)
a\models p
.
This is equivalent to
f:V\dashrightarrow W
being birational.
We say that
f:p\to q
is
finite-to-one
if
a\in{k(f(a))}^{{\operatorname{alg}}}
for
some (equivalently all)
a\models p
.
This is equivalent to
\dim V=\dim W
.

Nonforking independence in
\operatorname{ACFA}
has an explicit (quantifier-free) characterisation:
A\mathop{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\textstyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\scriptstyle\kern 2.80048pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 1.89871pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 2.80048pt}{\scriptscriptstyle\kern 1.42882pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 0.96873pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 1.42882pt}}_{C}B
if and only if the algebraically closed inversive difference fields generated by

and

are algebraically disjoint over the algebraically closed inversive difference field generated by

.
It follows that if

is rational, and

is a difference field extension, then

if and only if

.
For any positive integer

we denote by

the (unique) quantifier-free type of a
d
-tuple of independent realisations of
p
.
If
p
is the generic quanitifier-free type of
(V,\phi)
then

is the generic quantifier-free type of the rational
\sigma
-variety
(V^{d},\phi)
where
\phi
acts diagonally on
V^{d}
.

By the
dimension
of a rational type
p
we mean
\operatorname{trdeg}(k(a)/a)
for any
a\models p
.
This agrees with
\dim V
where
p
is the generic quantifier-free type of
(V,\phi)
.

2.3.
Orthogonalilty, qf-internality, and binding groups

By a slight abuse of notation, given a tuple
a
, we write
a\mathop{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\textstyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\scriptstyle\kern 2.80048pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 1.89871pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 2.80048pt}{\scriptscriptstyle\kern 1.42882pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 0.96873pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 1.42882pt}}_{k}\mathcal{C}
to mean that
a
is independent of every finite tuple from
\mathcal{C}
over
k
.
(This is only an abuse of notation because normally we only talk about independence between small sets, whereas
\mathcal{C}
has the cardinality of the universe.)
That this is a condition on
\operatorname{qftp}(a/k)
, at least when that quantifier-free type is rational, follows from
[
15
, Proposition 2.7]
, which says that
a\mathop{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\textstyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\scriptstyle\kern 2.80048pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 1.89871pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 2.80048pt}{\scriptscriptstyle\kern 1.42882pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 0.96873pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 1.42882pt}}_{k}\mathcal{C}
if and only if
k(a)\cap\mathcal{C}\subseteq k^{{\operatorname{alg}}}
.
Hence
a\mathop{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\textstyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\scriptstyle\kern 2.80048pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 1.89871pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 2.80048pt}{\scriptscriptstyle\kern 1.42882pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 0.96873pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 1.42882pt}}_{k}\mathcal{C}
for some realisation of a rational type
p\in S_{\operatorname{qf}}(k)
if and only if this is the case for every realisation.
We then say that
p
is
weakly orthogonal
to
\mathcal{C}
, denoted by
p\perp^{w}\mathcal{C}
.
A rational and stationary
p
is defined to be
orthogonal
to
\mathcal{C}
, denoted by
p\perp\mathcal{C}
, if for every difference field extension
K\supseteq k
the nonforking extension of
p
to
S_{\operatorname{qf}}(K)
is weakly orthogonal to
\mathcal{C}
.

The opposite notion, that of internality to
\mathcal{C}
, is not a property of the quantifier-free type.
As observed in
[
15
,
\lx@sectionsign
2.4]
, following ideas from
[
6
,
\lx@sectionsign
5]
, this can be resolved by restricting to rational types and replacing

with

.

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose
p\in S_{\operatorname{qf}}(k)
is a rational type over an algebraically closed difference field.
We say that
p
is
qf-internal to
\mathcal{C}
if for some (equivalently all)
a\models p
there is a difference field extension
K\supseteq k
such that
a\mathop{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\textstyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\scriptstyle\kern 2.80048pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 1.89871pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 2.80048pt}{\scriptscriptstyle\kern 1.42882pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 0.96873pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 1.42882pt}}_{k}K
and
a\in K(c)
for some tuple

from
\mathcal{C}
.

In
[
15
,
\lx@sectionsign
2.4]
a detailed study of qf-internality is carried out.
The reader can look there for a proof that “‘for some” is equivalent to “for all” in the definition.
It is also shown there that
p\not\perp\mathcal{C}
if and only if
p
admits a rational map to a positive-dimensional rational type that is qf-internal to
\mathcal{C}
.

It is shown in
[
15
, Proposition 3.6]
that, in characteristic zero, if
p
is the generic quantifier-free type of
(V,\phi)
then qf-internality of
p
to
\mathcal{C}
is equivalent to the
isotriviality
of
(V,\phi)
.
We defined isotriviality in the Introduction, but only for the autonomous case.
In fact, that definition works verbatim with rational
\sigma
-varieties in place of rational dynamical systems.

Fix now a rational type
p\in S_{\operatorname{qf}}(k)
that is qf-internal to
\mathcal{C}
, and assume the characteristic is zero.
The
quantifier-free binding group of
p
relative to
\mathcal{C}
, denoted by
\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{qf}}(p/\mathcal{C})
, was defined in
[
15
]
as the group of permutations
\alpha
of
p(\mathcal{U})
such that

(A)

for any tuple
u
of realisations of
p
, and any tuple
c
of elements of
\mathcal{C}
,

\operatorname{qftp}(u,c/k)=\operatorname{qftp}(\alpha(u),c/k)
.

The main accomplishment of
[
15
]
was to show that
\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{qf}}(p/\mathcal{C})
, along with its action on
p(\mathcal{U})
, has a quantifier-free definable avatar.
But beyond this, the group action was given a geometric meaning that we now explain.
Let
(V,\phi)
be the isotrivial rational
\sigma
-variety corresponding to
p
.
The main results of
[
15
]
are the following: there exists an algebraic group
G
over
k
acting faithfully on
V
by birational transformations,
\theta:G\times V\dashrightarrow V
,
such that
\{\theta_{g}:g\in G\}
is precisely the group of those birational transformations of
V
that:

(B)

preserve every irreducible invariant subvariety of
(V^{r},\phi)\times(W,\operatorname{id})
over
k
projecting dominantly onto each
V
-coördinate, for all
r\geq 1
and all trivial dynamics
(W,\operatorname{id})
over
k
.
(Here we view a birational transformation of
V
as acting co-ordinate wise on
V^{r}
and trivially on
W
.)

Moreover, there is
an algebraic group isomorphism

\rho:G\xrightarrow{}{}G^{\sigma}
making
\theta
equivariant as a rational map from
(G\times V,\rho\times\phi)
to
(V,\phi)
, and such that

{(G,\rho)}^{\sharp}(\mathcal{U})
is identified with
\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{qf}}(p/\mathcal{C})
by restricting the action to
p(\mathcal{U})
.
We call
(G,\rho)
the
binding group of
(V,\phi)
.
Note that, as rational maps from
V
to
V^{\sigma}
, we have

\rho(g)\circ\phi=\phi\circ{}g
for all
g\in{}G
.
Indeed, this is another way of writing the equivariance of
\theta
.
In particular, if
\phi
is invertible then
\rho
is conjugation by
\phi
.

2.4.
Canonical bases

Recall from
[
15
, Lemma 2.5(b)]
that the canonical base of a rational type,
\operatorname{qftp}(a/K)
over a perfect difference field
K
, is the difference field generated by the minimal field of definition of the Zariski locus
\operatorname{loc}(a,\sigma(a)/K)
.
We record the following useful additional facts.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
p=\operatorname{qftp}(a/k)
is rational and
K\supseteq k
is a perfect difference field extension.

(a)

k\cdot\operatorname{Cb}(a/K)=k(b)=k\langle b\rangle
for some finite tuple
b
from
K
.
Moreover,
b
can be chosen from the perfect closure of the field generated by a finite number of realisations of
p
over
k
.

(b)

Let
F
be the minimal field of definition of
\operatorname{loc}(a/K)
.
Then

(k\cdot\operatorname{Cb}(a/K))^{\operatorname{perf}}=\langle k\cdot F\rangle^{\operatorname{perf}}.

Proof.

(a).
Let
E:=k\cdot\operatorname{Cb}(a/K)
, the field generated by
\operatorname{Cb}(a/K)
over
k
.
So
E
is a finitely generated difference field extension of
k
contained in
K
.
On the other hand, by
[
15
, Lemma 2.5(c)]
, there are
a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}
realising
\operatorname{qftp}(a/K)
, and hence realising
p
, such that
E\subseteq k(a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell})^{\operatorname{perf}}
.
Using the fact that the preimage of
k(a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell})
under any power of Frobenius is a difference field, and that
E
is finitely generated over
k
as a difference field, it follows that
E
is contained in a finite extension of
k(a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell})
, and hence is also finitely generated as a field extension of
k
.
That is,
E=k(b)=k\langle b\rangle
for some finite tuple
b
.

(b).
This is similar to
[
18
, Lemma 4.5]
, but we give some details.

This time, let
E
be the minimal field of definition of
\operatorname{loc}(a,\sigma(a)/K)
, so that
\operatorname{Cb}(a/K)
is the difference field generated by
E
.
Then
\operatorname{loc}(a/K)
is
defined over
E
and hence

F\subseteq E^{\operatorname{perf}}\subseteq\operatorname{Cb}(a/K)^{\operatorname{perf}},

showing the right-to-left inclusion.

For the left-to-right inclusion, let
\phi:\operatorname{loc}(a/k)\to\operatorname{loc}(a/k)^{\sigma}
be a rational function over
k
such that
\sigma(a)=\phi(a)
, and denote its graph by
\Gamma(\phi)
.
Note that
\phi
restricts to a rational function from
\operatorname{loc}(a/K)
to
\operatorname{loc}(a/K)^{\sigma}
whose graph we denote by
\Gamma(\phi|_{\operatorname{loc}(a/K)})
.
Now fix
\alpha
an arbitrary field-automorphism of
\mathcal{U}
that fixes
k\cdot F
pointwise.
Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{loc}(a,\sigma(a)/K)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{loc}(a,\phi(a)/K)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(\phi|_{\operatorname{loc}(a/K)})^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(\phi|_{\operatorname{loc}(a/K)})\ \ \text{ as $\alpha$ is the identity on $k$ and $F$}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{loc}(a,\phi(a)/K)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{loc}(a,\sigma(a)/K).

Hence
\alpha
is the identity on
E
.
We have shown that
E\subseteq(k\cdot F)^{\operatorname{perf}}
, from which it follows that
\operatorname{Cb}(a/K)\subseteq\langle k\cdot F\rangle^{\operatorname{perf}}
, as desired.
∎

The following important property of canonical bases in
\operatorname{ACFA}_{0}
was proven in
[
18
]
, though we phrase it somewhat differently and specialise to rational types.

(Canonical base property for rational types in

)
.

Suppose

Let

.

where

is a tuple from

and

a\mathop{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\mathchar 12854\relax$\kern 8.00134pt\hss}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\textstyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\mathchar 12854\relax$\kern 8.00134pt\hss}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\scriptstyle\kern 2.80048pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\mathchar 12854\relax$\kern 3.92064pt\hss}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 1.89871pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 2.80048pt}{\scriptscriptstyle\kern 1.42882pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 12854\relax$\kern 2.00034pt\hss}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 0.96873pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 1.42882pt}}_{k}b
.
(Note that

is also irreducible as we are working over an algebraically
closed field.)
On the other hand,
\dim Z>\dim W
implies
a\notin{k(b)}^{{\operatorname{alg}}}
.
But by equivariance of
Z\to W
we have that

, which
implies that
{k(b)}^{{\operatorname{alg}}}={k\langle{}b\rangle}^{{\operatorname{alg}}}=\operatorname{acl}(kb)
.
This witnesses that
\operatorname{SU}(a/k)>1
.
By equivariance of

∎

In the wake of this, one might hope to try to answer Question
3.1
affirmatively for rational types
p
, by showing that if
Z\to W
witnesses that some
\operatorname{SU}(a/k)>1
, and

is another realisation, then another fibre of the same family will witness
\operatorname{SU}(a^{\prime}/k)>1
.
The following example, already in the fixed field, says that approach cannot work:

Example 3.3
.

Working in characteristic

, let
(V,\phi)=(\mathbb{A}^{2},\operatorname{id})
,
(W,\psi)=(\mathbb{A}^{1},\operatorname{id})
, and

be defined by

, where
(v_{1},v_{2})
are co-ordinates for

and

a co-ordinate for
W
.
Note that every irreducible variety of

is invariant as the dynamics here are trivial.
It is clear that
Z
projects dominantly onto both
V
and
W
, and that
\dim Z>\dim W
.
So, by Proposition
3.2
,

witnesses that the generic quantifier-free type

of
(V,\phi)
has a completion of
\operatorname{SU}
-rank greater than
1
.
More precisely, if we choose
(a,b)\in(Z,\operatorname{id})^{\sharp}
generic in

then the fibre

gives rise to a nonalgebraic forking extension of
\operatorname{tp}(a/k)
, namely
\operatorname{tp}(a/kb)
.
But suppose, instead, that we choose

such that

\sigma\left(\sqrt{a_{1}^{2}-4a_{2}}\right)=-\sqrt{a_{1}^{2}-4a_{2}}.

Then

lives on two fibres of

and

are the two solutions to

.
The above identity forces
\sigma(b,b^{\prime})=(b^{\prime},b)
.
In particular,
b,b^{\prime}\notin(W,\psi)^{\sharp}
.
But what matters for us is that

and
k\langle b^{\prime}\rangle=k(b,b^{\prime})
.
Since

, we get that

and

; in other words
\operatorname{tp}(a/kb)
and
\operatorname{tp}(a/kb^{\prime})
are algebraic extensions of
\operatorname{tp}(a/k)
and hence neither witnesses that
\operatorname{SU}(a/k)>1
.
What we have shown is that none of the fibres of

on which

\operatorname{SU}(a/k)>1
.
One would have to (and can in this case) choose

In any case, we avoid answering Question
3.1
by restricting attention to those types all of whose realisations have
SU
-rank
1
.

Definition 3.4
.

A complete quantifier-free type

is

\operatorname{SU}(p)=1
as a partial type. That is, if
\operatorname{SU}(a/k)=1

.

One easily obtains the expected characterisations:

Suppose

.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

is minimal.

(ii)

Every forking extension of

(iii)

Proof.

(i)
\implies
(ii).
Suppose

For rational types, Proposition

gives a geometric characterisation of minimality.
Borrowing from terminology in bimeromorphic goemetry, we define:

Definition 3.6
.

A rational
\sigma
-variety

over

if for any rational
\sigma
-variety
(W,\psi)
over

or

.

Proposition

can be restated as:

.

Suppose
(V,\phi)
is a positive-dimensional rational
\sigma
-variety over
k
with generic quantifier-free type

.
Then
p
is minimal if and only if
(V,\phi)
is simple.

Simplicity says that
(V,\phi)
is not covered by an invariant family of proper infinite subvarieties.
In particular, rational
\sigma
-curves are always simple.
Here is a higher dimensional example:

Example 3.8
.

Let

be a simple abelian variety over an algebraically closed field

Then

Proof.

This follows from the work in
[
13

Let

.
This is a quantifier-free definable subgroup of

.

We first show that

is

in the sense of
[
13
]
; that is, it does not admit any infinite definable subgroups of infinite index.
Let

be the (noncommutative) ring obtained from the ring of definable endomorphisms of

after tensoring with
\mathbb{Q}
over
\mathbb{Z}
.
By
[

, c-minimality will follow if we can show that
\sigma-\phi
is left-irreducible in

.
But
[
13

, specialised to our autonomous case, tells us that

, namely that it is generated as a ring by
\{\sigma,\sigma^{-1}\}
over the division ring
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)
.
It now follows easily that

\sigma

Next, we argue that

[

By
[

.
This is indeed the case from our assumption that

Now we can apply

-rank

.
It follows that the generic quantifier-free type of

Simplicity of

.
∎

4.
One-basedness and the Zilber dichotomy

We continue to work over an algebraically closed inversive difference field
(k,\sigma)
which we now, and for the rest of the paper, assume to be of characteristic zero.

Chatzidakis and Hrushovski
[
6
]
introduce a notion of modularity for sets in
\operatorname{ACFA}
that we here rephrase in terms of the familiar stability-theoretic notion of one-basedness, and we specialise to rational types:

Definition 4.1
.

Suppose
p\in S_{\operatorname{qf}}(k)
is a rational type.
We will say that
p
is
one-based
if for any finite tuple of
realisations
a
of
p
, and any difference field extension
K\supseteq k
,

\operatorname{Cb}(a/K)\in\operatorname{acl}(ka)
.

Remark 4.2
.

This property coincides with “modularity of
p(\mathcal{U})
over
k
”, in the sense
of
[
6
, §3.3]
.
This is the condition that asks that
a
be independent from
b
over
\operatorname{acl}(ka)\cap\operatorname{acl}(kb)
, for any pair of finite tuples of realisations
a
and
b
of
p
.
Indeed, if
p
is one-based, and
a,b

are tuples of realisations of
p
, then by assumption
\operatorname{Cb}(a/kb)
is
contained in
\operatorname{acl}(ka)
.
Of course, it is also contained in

\operatorname{acl}(kb)
, from which it follows that
a
is independent from

b
over
\operatorname{acl}(ka)\cap\operatorname{acl}(kb)
.
Conversely, suppose
p(\mathcal{U})
is modular over
k
, and let
a
be a tuple of realisations, and

K
a difference field extending
k
.
By Lemma
2.2
,

k\cdot\operatorname{Cb}(a/K)=k(b)
for some tuple
b
of realisations of
p
.
Modularity tells us that
a
is independent from
b
over
\operatorname{acl}(ka)\cap\operatorname{acl}(kb)
,
so that

\operatorname{Cb}(a/K)=\operatorname{Cb}(a/k(b))\subseteq\operatorname{acl}(ka)\cap\operatorname{acl}(kb)\subseteq\operatorname{acl}(ka)
.
as desired.

To give a geometric characterisation of this property, we begin with some terminology around families of invariant subvarieties of a
rational
\sigma
-variety.

Definition 4.3
.

Suppose
(V,\phi)
is a rational
\sigma
-variety over
(k,\sigma)
.
By a
rich invariant family of subvarieties of
(V,\phi)
we mean a rational
\sigma
-variety
(W,\psi)
over
k
along with an irreducible invariant subvariety
Z\subseteq(V\times W,\phi\times\psi)
such that:

(i)

Z
projects dominantly onto both
V
and
W
, with the general fibres of
Z\to W
being absolutely irreducible subvarieties of
V
,

(ii)

\dim Z>\dim V
,

(iii)

Canonicity
: if
b\neq b^{\prime}
are general points of
W
then
Z_{b}\neq Z_{b^{\prime}}
.

Remark 4.4
.

The general fibres of a rich family are infinite proper subvarieties of
V
.
Indeed, canonicity forces them to be proper.
And if they were finite then they would be singletons (by absolute irreducibility), and together with canonicity this would mean that
Z
determines a birational equivalence between
V
and
W
, contradicting
\dim Z>\dim V
.

The connection to one-basedness arises as follows:

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose
Z\subseteq(V\times W,\phi\times\psi)
is a rich invariant family of subvarieties of a rational
\sigma
-variety
(V,\phi)
over
k
.
Let
(a,b)
be a generic point of
(Z,(\phi\times\psi)|_{Z})
over
k
.
Then
\operatorname{Cb}(a/k(b))\notin{k(a)}^{{\operatorname{alg}}}
.

Proof.

Let
E
be the minimal field of definition of the Zariski locus of
(a,\sigma(a))
over
k(b)
.
By
[
15
, Lemma 2.5]
, as
\operatorname{qftp}(a/k(b))
is rational,
\operatorname{Cb}(a/kb)
is the difference field generated by
E
.
It suffices to show, therefore, that
E\not\subseteq k(a)^{{\operatorname{alg}}}
.

Let
b_{1},b_{2}
be realisations of
\operatorname{qftp}(b/ka)
.
There is a difference field isomorphism
\alpha:(k(a,b_{1}),\sigma)\to(k(a,b_{2}),\sigma)
that fixes
k(a)
and takes
b_{1}
to
b_{2}
, and which we can lift to a field-automorphism
\widehat{\alpha}
of
\mathcal{U}
.
Then

\operatorname{loc}(a,\sigma(a)/k(b_{1}))^{\widehat{\alpha}}=\operatorname{loc}(a,\sigma(a)/k(b_{2})).

Letting
E_{i}
be the minimal field of definition of
\operatorname{loc}(a,\sigma(a)/k(b_{i}))
, for
i=1,2
, we get that
\widehat{\alpha}(E_{1})=E_{2}
.
If
E_{2}=E_{1}
, then
\widehat{\alpha}
must preserve
\operatorname{loc}(a,\sigma(a)/k(b_{1}))
, and so

\operatorname{loc}(a,\sigma(a)/k(b_{1}))=\operatorname{loc}(a,\sigma(a)/k(b_{2}))
.
This in turn implies that

\operatorname{loc}(a/k(b_{1}))=\operatorname{loc}(a/k(b_{2}))
.
As
\operatorname{loc}(a/k(b_{i}))
is just
Z_{b_{i}}
, canonicity forces
b_{1}=b_{2}
.
So, if
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
were chosen to be distinct realisations of
\operatorname{qftp}(b/ka)
, then the corresponding
E_{1},E_{2}
are also distinct field-conjugates of
E
over
k(a)
.
Since
\dim Z>\dim V
, we must have that
b\notin k(a)^{{\operatorname{alg}}}
, and hence
\operatorname{qftp}(b/ka)
has infinitely many realisations, giving rise to infinitely many field-conjugates of
E
over
k(a)
.
This shows that
E\not\subseteq k(a)^{{\operatorname{alg}}}
, as desired.
∎

We obtain the following geometric characterisation of one-basedness:

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose
(V,\phi)
is a rational
\sigma
-variety over
k
, and
p\in S_{\operatorname{qf}}(k)
is its generic quantifier-free type.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

is one-based.

(ii)

If

and
V_{0}
is an irreducible invariant subvariety of
(V^{n},\phi)
over

that projects dominantly onto

in each co-ordinate, then
(V_{0},\phi)
admits no rich families of invariant subvarieties.

Proof.

(i)
\implies
(ii).
Suppose
V_{0}
is as in (ii) and

is a rich invariant family of subvarieties.
Let
(a,b)
be a generic point of
(Z,(\phi\times\psi)|_{Z})
over
k
.
Since
Z
projects dominantly onto
V_{0}
which in turn projects dominantly onto each copy of
V
, and because these projections are equivariant, we have that
a
is an

-tuple of realisations of
p
.
Letting
K:=k(b)
, Lemma

(V^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
is primitive whenever
(V^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
admits a dominant equivariant rational map to
(V,\phi)
and
\dim(V^{\prime})=\dim V
.
Exchange is strong primitivity of the corresponding rational
\sigma
-variety:

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose

is the generic quantifier-free type of a rational
\sigma
-variety
(V,\phi)
over

.
Then

satisfies exchange if and only if
(V,\phi)
is strongly primitive.

Proof.

Suppose

satisfies exchange and we are give dominant equivariant rational maps
f:(V^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})\dashrightarrow(V,\phi)
and
g:(V^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})\dashrightarrow(W,\psi)
with
\dim(V^{\prime})=\dim V
.
We need to show that
\dim W=0
or
\dim W=\dim V
.
Let
a^{\prime}
be a generic point of
(V^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
so that
a:=f(a)\models p
.
Then
b:=g(a^{\prime})\in\operatorname{dcl}(ka^{\prime})\subseteq\operatorname{acl}(ka)
.
Note that
\operatorname{qftp}(b/k)
is rational as it is the generic quantifier-free type of
(W,\psi)
.
If
\dim W\neq 0
then
b\notin\operatorname{acl}(k)
, and hence, by exchange,
a\in\operatorname{acl}(kb)
.
This forces
\dim W=\dim(b/k)=\dim(a/k)=\dim V
, as desired.

Conversely, suppose
(V,\phi)
is strongly primitive,
a\models p
, and
b\in\operatorname{acl}(ka)\setminus k
is such that
\operatorname{qftp}(b/k)
is rational.
So
\operatorname{qftp}(b/k)
is the generic quantifier-free type of a positive-dimensional rational
\sigma
-variety
(W,\psi)
.
Note that
\operatorname{qftp}(a,b/k)
is also rational; it is the generic quantifier-free type of
(V^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
where
V^{\prime}=\operatorname{loc}(a,b/k)
(and so
\dim(V^{\prime})=\dim V
).
The co-ordinate projections give us dominant equivariant rational maps
(V^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})\dashrightarrow(V,\phi)
and
(V^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})\dashrightarrow(W,\psi)
.
By strong primitivity, as

, we must have

, which forces

, as desired.
∎

We continue to work over an algebraically closed inversive difference field

of characteristic zero.

The proof of (i)
\implies

7.
Bounding disintegration

A natural notion of being “
m
-disintegrated” for complete types in stable theories would be to ask that any
m
distinct realisations be independent.
See, for example,
[
10
,
\lx@sectionsign
3]
where this is studied in the case of differentially closed fields.
The naive analogue in our case, so for quantifier-free types in
(\mathcal{U},\sigma)\models\operatorname{ACFA}_{0}
, does not make much sense.
For example, if
k\subseteq\mathcal{C}
then only algebraic types
p\in S_{\operatorname{qf}}(k)
could satisfy such a condition even for
m=2
.
Indeed, for
p
nonalgebraic, either every realisation is in the fixed field, in which case it is not hard to find a pair of distinct algebraically dependent realisations, or
a
and
\sigma(a)
are distinct dependent realisations of
p
for any
a\models p
.
So, at the very least, we should rule out such dependencies.

Definition 7.1
.

We say that
a,b\in\mathcal{U}^{n}
are
\sigma
-disjoint
if
a\neq\sigma^{r}(b)
for any
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 7.2
.

This notion of
\sigma
-disjointness is really only relevant in the (almost) autonomous case.
Indeed, if
(V,\phi)
is a rational
\sigma
-variety over
k
with generic quantifier-free type
p\in S_{\operatorname{qf}}(k)
admitting a pair of distinct but not
\sigma
-disjoint realisations, then
(V,\phi)
is defined over
k\cap\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^{r})
for some
r>0
.

Proof.

If
a\neq b
are realisations of
p
that are not
\sigma
-disjoint then there is
r>0
such that (without loss of generality)
a=\sigma^{r}(b)
.
Then
\sigma^{r}(b)
is Zariski generic in both
V
and
V^{\sigma^{r}}
over
k
, which forces
V=V^{\sigma^{r}}
, and hence
V
is defined over
k\cap\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^{r})
.
On the other hand,

\phi(a)=\sigma(a)=\sigma^{r+1}(b)=\sigma^{r}(\phi(b))=\phi^{\sigma^{r}}(\sigma^{r}(b))=\phi^{\sigma^{r}}(a).

That is,
\phi
and
\phi^{\sigma^{r}}
agree on the generic point
a
, so that
\phi=\phi^{\sigma^{r}}
, and hence
\phi
is defined over
k\cap\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^{r})
as well.
∎

Definition 7.3
.

Suppose
p\in S_{\operatorname{qf}}(k)
and
m\geq 1
.
We say that
p
is
m
-disintegrated
if every
m
pairwise
\sigma
-disjoint realisations of
p
are independent over
k
.
We say that
p
is
totally disintegrated
if it is
m
-disintegrated for all
m\geq 1
.

Remark 7.4
.

Some immediate observations:

(a)

1
-disintegration is equivalent to
p
being nonalgebraic.

(b)

2
-disintegration is equivalent to
\sigma
-disjointness agreeing with independence.

(c)

m
-disintegrated implies
\ell
-disintegrated for
\ell\leq m
.

(d)

Totally disintegrated implies one-based.

Proof.

Only part (d) requires proof.
It suffices to check that if
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
and
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{m})
are finite tuples of relaisations of
p
then
a
is independent from
b
over
\operatorname{acl}(ka)\cap\operatorname{acl}(kb)
– see Remark
4.2
.
As it does not change
\operatorname{acl}(ka)
and
\operatorname{acl}(kb)
, we may assume that the
a_{i}
’s are pairwise
\sigma
-disjoint, and also the
b_{i}
’s.
If some
a_{i},b_{j}
is not
\sigma
-disjoint then
a_{i}\in\operatorname{acl}(ka)\cap\operatorname{acl}(kb)
, and hence it suffices to show that
a^{\prime}
is independent from
b
over
\operatorname{acl}(ka)\cap\operatorname{acl}(kb)
where
a^{\prime}
is the subtuple of
a
obtained by removing such
a_{i}
’s.
But now, the full sequence of co-ordinates of
a^{\prime}
and
b
are pairwise
\sigma
-disjoint, and hence independent over
k
by total disintegratedness, which in particular implies
a^{\prime}
is independent from
b
over
\operatorname{acl}(ka)\cap\operatorname{acl}(kb)
, as desired.
∎

Lemma 7.10

.

[
4

By quantifier-reduction in pseudofinite fields,

is a generically

over

is a Zariski dense subset of

over

One of these

It now suffices to find

such that

and
k(g(v))\not\subseteq k(g(u))

•

and

are Zariski generic in

over

, and

To that end, let

That is,

The projection from

Proof.

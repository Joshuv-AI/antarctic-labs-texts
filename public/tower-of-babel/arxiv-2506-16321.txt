Introduction

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
,
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
be closed, and
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the polynomials in
n
variables with real coefficients.
The set

\mathrm{Pos}(K):=\big\{f\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\,\big|\,f\geq 0\ \text{on}\ K\big\}

of all polynomials which are non-negative on
K
belongs to the most important structures in mathematics and applications
[
1
,
15
,
13
,
26
]
.

Let
\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{2}
be the cone of sums of squares.
For a compact semi-algebraic set
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
defined by finitely many polynomial inequalities
g_{i}(x)\geq 0
,

K:=\{x\in\mathds{R}^{n}\,|\,g_{1}(x)\geq 0,\ \dots,\ g_{m}(x)\geq 0\},

a polynomial
p
which is strictly positive on
K
, i.e.,
p(x)>0
for all
x\in K
, has the representation

p=\sum_{e=(e_{1},\dots,e_{m})\in\{0,1\}^{m}}g_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots g_{m}^{e_{m}}\cdot\sigma_{e}

(1)

with
\sigma_{e}\in\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{2}
.
This was proved by Konrad Schmüdgen
[
23
]
.
Another representation is

p=\sigma_{0}+\sum_{e=1}^{m}g_{e}\cdot\sigma_{e}

(2)

with
\sigma_{i}\in\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{2}

which was proved by Mihai Putinar
[
22
]
.
See
[
1
,
15
,
26
]
for more details.

Both representations (
1
) and (
2
) only hold for
p>0
on
K
.
If

\min_{x\in K}p_{i}(x)\quad\xrightarrow{i\to\infty}\quad 0,

then

\max_{e}\deg\sigma_{i,e}\quad\xrightarrow{i\to\infty}\quad\infty

even when
p_{i}\to p
and
\deg p_{i},\deg p\leq d<\infty
.
This degree blow up courses serious troubles in optimization, since even when an optimum is attained at a polynomial
p
of finite degree, the implementation using (
1
) or (
2
) blows up.
Consequently, even in the simplest case of calculating

\min_{p\in S}L(p)

(3)

with a linear functional
L:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d}\to\mathds{R}
and
S\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(K)_{\leq d}
the semi-definite program with (
1
) or (
2
) must in general exceed the finite degree
d
of the problem (
3
).
Several works investigated these degree blow ups, see e.g.
[
21
,
24
,
18
,
10
,
14
,
4
,
6
]
and references therein.
Hence, it is clear that the representations (
1
) and (
2
) are broken beyond repair.
And what is broken and can not be fixed must be replaced.

In the current work we investigate linear operators

A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

especially in

e^{tA}:=\sum_{k\in\mathds{N}_{0}}\frac{t^{k}\cdot A^{k}}{k!}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

with
t\in\mathds{R}
.
The operators
A
are investigated to determine if a given set
S\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(K)
can be moved by
e^{A}
to a nicer set
e^{A}S
, e.g., in the sums of squares.
This results in a transformation of the problem (
3
) to

\min_{p\in S}L(p)=\min_{p\in e^{A}S}\tilde{L}(p)

with the linear functional
\tilde{L}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}
defined by
\tilde{L}(p):=L(e^{-A}p)
.

.

[

]
.

6
with two open problems.

2
Preliminaries

For a set
S
we denote by
\mathrm{int}\,S
its interior.

A Fréchet space is a metrizable, complete, and locally convex topological vector space
[
28
]
.
Our leading example is the set of real sequences
\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
.
Here, the topology is generated by the semi-norms
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{d}
defined by

\|s\|_{d}:=\max_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}:|\alpha|\leq d}|s_{\alpha}|

for every
d\in\mathds{N}_{0}
and sequence
s=(s_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
with
|\alpha|:=\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{n}
for all
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.
To understand the Fréchet topology of
\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
it is sufficient to understand the convergence in this space.
Let
s^{(k)}=(s_{k,\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
for all
k\in\mathds{N}
.
Then

s^{(k)}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}s\qquad\text{iff}\qquad s_{k,\alpha}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}s_{\alpha}\ \text{for all}\ \alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n},

i.e.,
\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
has the coordinate-wise convergence.

The space of polynomials
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is a LF-space and its topology can be described by the convergence of
(p_{k})_{k\in\mathds{N}}\subseteq\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
:

p_{k}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}p\qquad\text{iff}\qquad\sup_{k\in\mathds{N}}\deg p_{k}=D<\infty\ \text{and}\ p_{k}\to p\ \text{in}\ \mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq D}.

The space
\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
and
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
are dual to each other with the pairing

\langle s,p\rangle:=L_{s}(p)

where
s\in\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
,
p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, and
L_{s}
is the Riesz functional

L_{s}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R},\qquad L_{s}(x^{\alpha}):=s_{\alpha}.

All linear operators
T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
have a representation

T=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}q_{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}

with unique coefficients
q_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.
A sequence
(T_{k})_{k\in\mathds{N}}
of such linear operators converges to a linear operator
T
iff
q_{k,\alpha}\to q_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
or equivalently iff
T_{k}p\to Tp
for all
p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.

Since
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is a nuclear space, by the Schwartz Kernel Theorem
[
28
, Ch. 51]
, every linear operator
T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
also has the representation

T=\sum_{i\in\mathds{N}_{0}}l_{i}\cdot f_{i}

with linear functionals
l_{i}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}
and
f_{i}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
for all
i\in\mathds{N}_{0}
.

We assume the reader has a basic knowledge about Lie groups and Lie algebras
[
29
]
.
We only give the definition of their infinite dimensional version.

Definition 2.1

(see e.g.
[
20
, p. 63, Dfn. 1.1]
)
.

We call
(G,\,\cdot\,)
a
regular Fréchet Lie group
if the following conditions are fulfilled:

(i)

G
is an infinite dimensional smooth Fréchet manifold.

(ii)

(G,\,\cdot\,)
is a group.

(iii)

The map
G\times G\to G
,
(A,B)\mapsto A\cdot B^{-1}
is smooth.

(iv)

The
Fréchet Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}
of
G
is isomorphic to the tangent space
T_{e}G
of
G
at the unit element
e\in G
.

(v)

\exp:\mathfrak{g}\to G
is a smooth mapping such that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\exp(tu)\big|_{t=0}=u

for all
u\in\mathfrak{g}
.

(vi)

The space
C^{1}(G,\mathfrak{g})
of
C^{1}
-curves in
G
coincides with the set of all
C^{1}
-curves in
G
under the Fréchet topology.

For more on infinite dimensional calculus, regular Fréchet Lie groups, and their Lie algebras see e.g.
[
19
,
20
,
25
]
.

3
Making Non-Negative Polynomials into Sums of Squares

The following is our first main result.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
,
d\in\mathds{N}_{0}
,
S\subseteq\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d}
, and let
C\subseteq\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d}
be a full dimensional cone such that there is a linear functional

l:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d}\to\mathds{R}\qquad\text{with}\qquad l(p)>0\quad\text{for all}\quad p\in(S\cup C)\setminus\{0\}.

If
f\in\mathrm{int}\,C
and the linear operator
A
is defined by

A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d}\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d},\quad p\mapsto Ap:=l(p)\cdot f,

then there exists a constant
\tau=\tau(f,C,S,l)>0
such that

e^{tA}S\quad\subseteq\quad C

for all
t\geq\tau
.

Proof.

Let

\tilde{S}:=\overline{\mathrm{cone}\,\mathrm{conv}\,S}

be the closed convex cone generated by
S
.
Since
l(p)>0
for all
p\in S
,
\tilde{S}
is closed and pointed, i.e.,
\tilde{S}
has a compact base
B
.

Let
p\in B
and
f\in\mathrm{int}\,C
, i.e.,
l(p)>0
and
l(f)>0
.
Then

Ap=l(p)\cdot f\qquad\text{and}\qquad A^{k}p=l(p)\cdot l(f)^{k-1}\cdot f

for all
k\in\mathds{N}
.
Hence,

e^{tA}p=\sum_{k\in\mathds{N}_{0}}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\cdot A^{k}p=p+l(p)\cdot\sum_{k\in\mathds{N}}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\cdot l(f)^{k-1}\cdot f=p+\frac{l(p)}{l(f)}\cdot\left(e^{t\cdot l(f)}-1\right)\cdot f

and

\frac{l(f)}{l(p)}\cdot\left(e^{t\cdot l(f)}-1\right)^{-1}\cdot e^{tA}p\quad\xrightarrow{t\to\infty}\quad f.

Since

\frac{l(f)}{l(p)}\cdot\left(e^{t\cdot l(f)}-1\right)^{-1}\cdot e^{tA}p

is continuous in
t
and
p
and since
f
is an inner point of
C
, there exists a neighborhood
U\subseteq C
of
f
and a constant
\tau\geq 0
such that

e^{tA}S\quad\subseteq\quad e^{tA}\tilde{S}\quad\subseteq\quad\mathrm{cone}\,U\quad\subseteq\quad C

for all
t\geq\tau
.
∎

In the previous theorem it is clear that the main space
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d}
can be replaced by any Banach space
\mathcal{V}
.
For special and important cases of
S
and
C
in
3.2
we get the following corollary.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
,
d\in\mathds{N}_{0}
,
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
be closed, and let
S\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(K)
.
The following statements hold:

(i)

There exists a linear operator

A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}

and a constant
\tau\geq 0
such that

e^{tA}S\quad\subseteq\quad\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}

for all
t\geq\tau
.

(ii)

Let

f\in\mathrm{int}\,\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}^{2}

and let

l:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}\to\mathds{R},\quad l(p):=\int_{K}p(x)\cdot e^{-x^{2}}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}x.

Then

A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d},\quad p\mapsto Ap:=l(p)\cdot f

is an operator as in (i).

(iii)

Let

L:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}\to\mathds{R}

be a linear functional and let
A
be an operator as in (i).
Set

\tilde{S}:=e^{\tau A}S\subseteq\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}^{2}

and

\tilde{L}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}\to\mathds{R},\quad\tilde{L}(p):=L(e^{-\tau A}p).

Then

\inf_{p\in S}L(p)=\inf_{p\in\tilde{S}}\tilde{L}(p)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sup_{p\in S}L(p)=\sup_{p\in\tilde{S}}\tilde{L}(p).

Proof.

(i) + (ii): Since
C=\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}^{2}
is a full dimensional cone and
l(p)>0
for all
p\in(S\cup C)\setminus\{0\}
,
3.2
gives the assertion.

(iii): Follows from the identity
e^{-\tau A}e^{\tau A}=\mathds{1}
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

An equivalent results as in
3.3
for the sums of squares cone also holds for the Waring cone by
3.2
.

\circ

Since
3.3
contains the degree bound
\leq 2d
, it can also be interpreted to work on the homogeneous polynomials:

\mathds{R}[x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{=2d}\quad\cong\quad\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}.

Note, the construction of the operator
A
in
3.2
and
3.3
is very specific, since
A
is made in such a way that
f\cdot[0,\infty)
becomes an accumulation ray, i.e., with increasing time
t
every
e^{tA}p
converges towards the ray spanned by
f
.
This requires the degree bounds in these results.
In the following theorem the degree restriction is removed for
K=\mathds{R}^{n}
.
It is our second main result.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
.
The following statements hold:

(i)

There exists a linear operator

A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

with

A\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}\quad\subseteq\quad\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}

for all
d\in\mathds{N}_{0}
such that

e^{A}\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n})_{\leq 2d}\quad\subseteq\quad\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d}^{2}

for all
d\in\mathds{N}_{0}
and therefore

e^{A}\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n})\quad\subseteq\quad\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{2}.

(ii)

For
d\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, define the linear operators
l_{d}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}
by

p=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}p_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}\quad\mapsto\quad l_{d}(p):=\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}e^{-x^{2}}\cdot\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}:|\alpha|=2d}p_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}x.

(4)

Then for any sequence
(f_{d})_{d\in\mathds{N}_{0}}\subseteq\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
of polynomials with

\deg f_{d}=2d\quad\text{and}\quad f_{d}\in\mathrm{int}\,\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d}^{2}

there exist constants
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},{\dots}>0
such that the operator
A
in (i) is of the form

A=\sum_{d\in\mathds{N}_{0}}c_{d}\cdot l_{d}\cdot f_{d},\qquad\text{i.e.,}\qquad Ap=\sum_{d\in\mathds{N}_{0}}c_{d}\cdot l_{d}(p)\cdot f_{d}

for all
p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove (ii).
We prove the statement via induction over the degree
d
.

d=0
:

Since

\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n})_{\leq d=0}=\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d=0}^{2}=[0,\infty)\cdot 1,

we have after rescaling
f_{0}=1
and we can set
c_{0}=1
to get

A_{0}:=l_{0}\cdot 1.

d\to d+1
:

For
d\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, assume we have an operator

A_{d}=\sum_{k=0}^{d}c_{k}\cdot l_{k}\cdot f_{k}

with

A_{d}\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2k}\quad\subseteq\quad\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2k}

and

e^{A}\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n})_{\leq 2k}\quad\subseteq\quad\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq k}^{2}

for all
k=0,1,\dots,d
.
The operator

A_{d}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d+2}\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d+2}

is a bounded operator.
Furthermore,
\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n})_{\leq 2d+2}
is a pointed cone with compact base.
For any

p=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}p_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n})

with
\deg p=2d+2
the homogeneous part

p|_{=2d+2}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}:|\alpha|=2d+2}p_{\alpha}\cdot x^{\alpha}

is non-negative, i.e.,
p|_{=2d+2}\geq 0
on
\mathds{R}^{n}
, and therefore
l_{d+1}(p)>0
.
Hence, for

A_{d+1}:=A_{d}+c_{d+1}\cdot l_{d+1}\cdot f_{d+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{d+1}c_{k}\cdot l_{k}\cdot f_{k}

there exists a
c_{d+1}\gg 0
such that

e^{A_{d+1}}\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n})_{\leq 2d+2}\quad\subseteq\quad\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d+1}^{2},

since

f_{d+1}\in\mathrm{int}\,\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d+1}^{2}

and

e^{A_{d}+c_{d+1}\cdot l_{d+1}\cdot f_{d+1}}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d+2}\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d+2}

is continuous in
c_{d+1}
.
That is the same fixed-ray argument as in
3.2
and
A_{d}
is only a perturbation to
c_{d+1}\cdot l_{d+1}\cdot f_{d+1}
, since
c_{d+1}\gg c_{0},\dots,c_{d}
.

Now, set

A:=\sum_{d\in\mathds{N}_{0}}c_{d}\cdot l_{d}\cdot f_{d}.

Then
A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is a well-defined linear operator, since
l_{D}(p)=0
for all
p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and
D\in\mathds{N}_{0}
such that
\deg p<2D
, i.e., the sum

Ap=\sum_{d\in\mathds{N}_{0}}c_{d}\cdot l_{d}(p)\cdot f_{d}

contains only finitely many non-zero terms.
This proves (ii).
∎

Remark 3.6
.

In the previous theorem we can, of course, rescale the given

f_{d}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}f_{d,\alpha}\cdot x^{\alpha}\in\mathrm{int}\,\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d}^{2}.

Hence, we can assume without loss of generality

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}f_{d,\alpha}^{2}=1.

Then, in general,

0\quad\ll\quad c_{0}\quad\ll\quad c_{1}\quad\ll\quad{\dots}\quad\ll\quad c_{d}\quad\ll\quad{\dots}\ .

This increase in the constants
c_{d}
reflects the fact that “there are significantly more non-negative polynomials than sums of squares”
[
3
]
.

\circ

Remark 3.7
.

From
3.5
we find that given any

S\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n})

and any linear functional

L:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R},

the optimization problems

\inf_{p\in S}L(p)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sup_{p\in S}L(p)

transform into optimization problems

\inf_{p\in S}L(p)\quad=\quad\inf_{p\in e^{A}S}\tilde{L}(p)

and

\sup_{p\in S}L(p)\quad=\quad\sup_{p\in e^{A}S}\tilde{L}(p)

over the subset
e^{A}S
of sums of squares
\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{2}
with

\tilde{L}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R},\quad p\mapsto\tilde{L}(p):=L(e^{-A}p).

Here,
A\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}\subseteq\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}
for all
d\in\mathds{N}_{0}
implies

e^{A}\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}\quad\subseteq\quad\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}

for all
d\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, i.e., no additional degree blow up is introduced and also the Lasserre hierarchy can be used.

\circ

A close look at the proof of

Corollary 3.8
.

Let

is well-defined with

for all

and therefore

Proof.

Proceed as in the proof of

.
∎

4
The set
\mathfrak{g}
and its regular Fréchet Lie Algebras

In
3.2
and
3.3
we gave explicit operators

A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}

such that certain given sets
S
are moved by
e^{tA}
to the nicer sets
e^{tA}S
, e.g. subsets of the sums of squares cone or the Waring cone.
These results are restricted to the degrees
d
and
2d
, i.e., for every
d\in\mathds{N}
another operator
A
and constant
\tau
in
3.3
(iii) must be calculated.
In
3.5
we gave an explicit linear operator
A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
with

e^{A}\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n})\subseteq\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{2}.

For
\mathrm{Pos}(K)
with closed
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
we needed additional assumptions in
3.8
.
We therefore study now linear operators

A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

such that

e^{tA}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

is well-defined for all
t\in\mathds{R}
.

Definition 4.10
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
.
We define

\mathfrak{g}:=\Big\{A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\ \text{linear}\,\Big|\\
e^{tA}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\ \text{well-defined for all}\ t\in\mathds{R}\Big\}.

It is sufficient in the definition of
\mathfrak{g}
that
e^{tA}
is well-defined as a linear map
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
only for all
t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
for some
\varepsilon>0
.

Clearly,

\mathfrak{g}=\mathds{R}\cdot\mathfrak{g},

i.e.,
\mathfrak{g}
is a cone and
\mathfrak{g}=-\mathfrak{g}
.

However,
\mathfrak{g}
is not convex and it is not a vector space, i.e., there are
A,B\in\mathfrak{g}
such that
A+B\notin\mathfrak{g}
.
We have the following example of such maps
A
and
B
.

Example 4.11
.

Let
n=1
.
Define the linear operators

A:\mathds{R}[x]\to\mathds{R}[x]\qquad\text{and}\qquad B:\mathds{R}[x]\to\mathds{R}[x]

by

Ax^{k}:=\begin{cases}x^{k}&\text{for}\ k=2m\ \text{with}\ m\in\mathds{N}_{0},\\
x^{k+1}&\text{for}\ k=2m-1\ \text{with}\ m\in\mathds{N}\end{cases}

and

Bx^{k}:=\begin{cases}x^{k+1}&\text{for}\ k=2m\ \text{with}\ m\in\mathds{N}_{0},\\
x^{k}&\text{for}\ k=2m-1\ \text{with}\ m\in\mathds{N}\end{cases}

for all
k\in\mathds{N}_{0}
with linear extension to all
\mathds{R}[x]
.
Then

A\mathds{R}[x]_{\leq 2m}\ \subseteq\ \mathds{R}[x]_{\leq 2m}\qquad\text{and}\qquad B\mathds{R}[x]_{\leq 2m+1}\ \subseteq\ \mathds{R}[x]_{\leq 2m+1}

for all
m\in\mathds{N}_{0}
.
Since
A|_{\mathds{R}[x]_{\leq 2m}}
and
B|_{\mathds{R}[x]_{\leq 2m+1}}
are linear operators on finite dimensional spaces, i.e., matrices,
e^{tA}
and
e^{tB}
are well-defined for every
p\in\mathds{R}[x]
and hence they are well-defined as linear maps
\mathds{R}[x]\to\mathds{R}[x]
.
However,

(A+B)x^{k}\quad=\quad\begin{cases}x^{k}+x^{k+1}&\text{for}\ k=2m\ \text{with}\ m\in\mathds{N}_{0},\\
x^{k+1}+x^{k}&\text{for}\ k=2m-1\ \text{with}\ m\in\mathds{N}\end{cases}\quad=\quad x^{k}+x^{k+1}

for all
k\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, i.e.,

\deg\left(\sum_{i=0}^{d}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}\cdot(A+B)^{i}1\right)=d\quad\xrightarrow{d\to\infty}\quad\infty

for all
t\neq 0
.
Hence,
e^{t(A+B)}
is not a map
\mathds{R}[x]\to\mathds{R}[x]
and
A+B\notin\mathfrak{g}
.

\circ

The previous example showed that if for a linear operator

A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

there exist
d_{0}<d_{1}<d_{2}<\dots
in
\mathds{N}_{0}
with

A\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d_{i}}\ \subseteq\ \mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d_{i}}

(5)

for all
i\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, then
A\in\mathfrak{g}
.
This was the property used in
[
7
,
9
]
.
The next examples shows that (
5
) is sufficient, but (
5
) is not necessary.

Example 4.12
.

Let
n=1
and let
(p_{i})_{i\in\mathds{N}}=(2,3,5,7,11,\dots)
be the list of prime numbers.
Define the linear map
A:\mathds{R}[x]\to\mathds{R}[x]
by

Ax^{k}:=\begin{cases}x^{p_{m+1}}&\text{for}\ k=2m\ \text{with}\ m\in\mathds{N},\\
0&\text{else}.\end{cases}

Then

A\mathds{R}[x]_{\leq d}\ \not\subseteq\ \mathds{R}[x]_{\leq d}

for all
d\in\mathds{N}
with
d\geq 8
.
However,

A1=0,\quad Ax=0,\quad Ax^{2}=x^{p_{2}}=x^{3},\quad Ax^{3}=0,\quad Ax^{4}=x^{p_{3}}=x^{5},\quad\dots

and therefore

A^{2}x^{k}=0

for all
k\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, i.e.,
e^{tA}:\mathds{R}[x]\to\mathds{R}[x]
is well-defined for all
t\in\mathds{R}
and
A\in\mathfrak{g}
.

\circ

The following theorem gives characterizations of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and let

A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

be linear.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

A\in\mathfrak{g}
.

(ii)

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in\mathds{N}_{0}}\deg A^{k}x^{\alpha}<\infty
for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.

(iii)

For all
i\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, there exist subspaces
V_{i}\subseteq\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
with

(a)

\dim V_{i}<\infty
,

(b)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{i\in\mathds{N}_{0}}V_{i}=\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, and

(c)

AV_{i}\subseteq V_{i}
for all
i\in\mathds{N}_{0}
.

Proof.

The implications “(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)” and “(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i)” are clear.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii):
Since
e^{tA}
is well-defined, for each
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
, there exists a degree
D=D(\alpha)\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
such that

e^{tA}x^{\alpha}\ \subseteq\ \mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq D}

for all
t\in\mathds{R}
.
Hence,

A^{k}x^{\alpha}\ =\ \partial^{k}_{t}e^{tA}x^{\alpha}\Big|_{t=0}\ \in\ \mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq D}

for all
k\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, which proves (ii).

[(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii)]
\Rightarrow
(iii):
Let
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.
By (i) and (ii), there exists a
D=D(\alpha)\in\mathds{N}_{0}
with

e^{tA}x^{\alpha},\ A^{k}x^{\alpha}\ \in\ \mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq D}

for all
k\in\mathds{N}_{0}
and
t\in\mathds{R}
.
Set

V_{\alpha,0}:=\mathds{R}\cdot x^{\alpha}.

By (ii),

AV_{\alpha,0}\quad\subseteq\quad\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq D}.

For all
i\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, define

V_{\alpha,i+1}:=V_{\alpha,i}+AV_{\alpha,i}.

By (ii) and the definition of
V_{\alpha,i+1}
,

V_{\alpha,i}\quad\subseteq\quad V_{\alpha,i+1}\quad\subseteq\quad\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq D}

and hence

V_{\alpha,0}\quad\subseteq\quad V_{\alpha,1}\quad\subseteq\quad\dots\quad\subseteq\quad\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq D}.

(6)

Since (
6
) is an increasing sequence of finite dimensional vector spaces
V_{\alpha,i}
bounded from above by the finite dimensional vector space
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq D}
, there exists an index
I(\alpha)\in\mathds{N}_{0}
such that

V_{\alpha,I(\alpha)}=V_{\alpha,I(\alpha)+1},

i.e.,

\dim V_{\alpha,I(\alpha)}<\infty\qquad\text{and}\qquad AV_{\alpha,I(\alpha)}\subseteq V_{\alpha,I}.

Since
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
was arbitrary and
x^{\alpha}\in V_{\alpha,I(\alpha)}
,

\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}V_{\alpha,I(\alpha)}.

Since
\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
is countable, we proved (iii) (a) – (c).
∎

4.13
tells us that
e^{tA}
is only the matrix exponential function, since for an operator
A\in\mathfrak{g}
we only need to know
A
on every finite dimensional invariant subspace
V_{i}
, i.e., its calculation is easy as soon as the
V_{i}
for
A
are known.
An algorithm for the calculation of these
V_{i}
is explicitly given in the proof of
4.13
.

For the invariant subspaces
V_{i}
of
A\in\mathfrak{g}
we have the following result.

Corollary 4.14
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
, let
A\in\mathfrak{g}
, and let
V\subseteq\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a finite or infinite dimensional subspace.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

AV\subseteq V
.

(ii)

e^{tA}V\subseteq V
for all
t\in\mathds{R}
.

Proof.

The direction “(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)” follows from
e^{tA}=\sum_{k\in\mathds{N}_{0}}k!^{-1}\cdot t^{k}\cdot A^{k}
and the direction “(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)” follows from
A=\partial_{t}e^{tA}\big|_{t=0}
.
∎

We have seen in
4.11
that
\mathfrak{g}
is not closed under addition.
The following example shows that
\mathfrak{g}
is also not closed under multiplication.

Example 4.15

(
4.11
continued)
.

Let
A,B:\mathds{R}[x]\to\mathds{R}[x]
be given as in
4.11
.
Then

ABx^{2m}=x^{2m+2},\phantom{{}^{+1}}\quad ABx^{2m+1}=x^{2m+2},

(7)

and

BAx^{2m}=x^{2m+1},\quad BAx^{2m+1}=x^{2m+3},

(8)

for all
m\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, i.e., by
4.13
(ii),
AB\notin\mathfrak{g}
as well as
BA\notin\mathfrak{g}
.

\circ

If two operators
A,B\in\mathfrak{g}
possess a common family
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in\mathds{N}_{0}}
of invariant and finite dimensional subspace
V_{i}
as provided by
4.13
(iii), then

A+B\in\mathfrak{g},\quad AB\in\mathfrak{g},\quad\text{and}\quad BA\in\mathfrak{g}

and the Lie bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
is given by

[A,B]:=AB-BA\ \in\mathfrak{g}.

In general,
\mathfrak{g}
is not closed under this Lie bracket.
The operators
A,B\in\mathfrak{g}
in
4.11
provide an example of
[A,B]\notin\mathfrak{g}
.

Example 4.16

(
4.11
and
4.15
continued)
.

Let
A:\mathds{R}[x]\to\mathds{R}[x]
and
B:\mathds{R}[x]\to\mathds{R}[x]
be defined as in
4.11
.
By (
7
) and (
8
) in
4.15
,

[A,B]x^{2m}=x^{2m+2}-x^{2m+1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad[A,B]x^{2m+1}=x^{2m+2}-x^{2m+3}

for all
m\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, i.e.,
[A,B]\notin\mathfrak{g}
by
4.13
(iii).

\circ

In summary, for
A,B\in\mathfrak{g}
, in order to have

A+B\in\mathfrak{g},\quad AB\in\mathfrak{g},\quad BA\in\mathfrak{g},\quad\text{and}\quad[A,B]\in\mathfrak{g}

it is sufficient that the operators
A
and
B
possess a common family
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in\mathds{N}_{0}}
of invariant finite dimensional subspaces
V_{i}
in
4.13
(ii).

Corollary 4.17
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and let
\mathcal{V}=\{V_{i}\}_{i\in\mathds{N}_{0}}
be a family of subspaces
V_{i}\subseteq\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
such that

\dim V_{i}<\infty\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]=\bigcup_{i\in\mathds{N}_{0}}V_{i}.

Then

\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{V}}:=\big\{A\in\mathfrak{g}\,\big|\,AV_{i}\subseteq V_{i}\ \text{for all}\ i\in\mathds{N}_{0}\big\}

is a regular Fréchet Lie algebra in
\mathfrak{g}
with Lie bracket

[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]:\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{V}}\times\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{V}}\to\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{V}},\quad(A,B)\mapsto[A,B]:=AB-BA.

The set
G_{\mathcal{V}}:=\exp(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{V}})
is the corresponding regular Fréchet Lie group.

The question is, are all regular Fréchet Lie algebras in
\mathfrak{g}
contained in some
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{V}}
?
For finite dimensional Lie algebras this is true.

Corollary 4.18
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and let
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
be a finite dimensional regular Fréchet Lie algebra in
\mathfrak{g}
.
Then there exists a family
\mathcal{V}=\{V_{i}\}_{i\in\mathds{N}_{0}}
of finite dimensional subspaces
V_{i}
of
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
with

\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]=\bigcup_{i\in\mathds{N}_{0}}V_{i}

such that
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{V}}
.

Proof.

Let
g_{1},\dots,g_{N}
be a vector space basis of
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
, i.e.,
\dim\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=N\in\mathds{N}
.
Let

a_{1},\dots,a_{N}\in\mathds{R}\quad\text{with}\quad a_{1}^{2}+\dots+a_{N}^{2}=1.

(9)

Then for any
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
there exists a
D=D(\alpha)\in\mathds{N}
such that

(a_{1}g_{1}+\dots+a_{N}g_{N})^{k}x^{\alpha}\quad\subseteq\quad\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq D}

for all
a_{1},\dots,a_{N}
with (
9
), since
(a_{1}g_{1}+\dots+a_{N}g_{N})^{k}
is continuous in
a_{1},\dots,a_{N}
and the set (
9
) is compact.
From here, continue as in the proof of
4.13
step “[(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii)]
\Rightarrow
(iii)”.
∎

An infinite dimensional regular Fréchet Lie algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
does not need to be contained in some
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{V}}
as the following example shows.

Example 4.19
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
, let
y\in\mathds{R}^{n}
, and let

l_{y}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R},\qquad f\mapsto l_{y}(f):=f(y).

Define

\mathfrak{g}_{y}:=\big\{l_{y}\cdot p\,\big|\,p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\big\}.

Then

(i)

\mathfrak{g}_{y}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
,

(ii)

\alpha A+\beta B\in\mathfrak{g}_{y}
for all
A,B\in\mathfrak{g}_{y}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathds{R}
,

(iii)

AB\in\mathfrak{g}_{y}
for all
A,B\in\mathfrak{g}_{y}
,

(iv)

[A,B]\in\mathfrak{g}_{y}
for all
A,B\in\mathfrak{g}_{y}
, and

(v)

\mathfrak{g}_{y}
is closed,

i.e.,
\mathfrak{g}_{y}
is a regular Fréchet Lie algebra in
\mathfrak{g}
, since

\alpha A+\beta B=l_{y}\cdot(\alpha p+\beta q)\qquad\text{and}\qquad AB=q(y)\cdot l_{y}\cdot p

with
Af=f(y)\cdot p
,
Bf=f(y)\cdot q
, and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathds{R}
.
To see that
\mathfrak{g}_{y}
is closed let

A_{k}=l_{y}\cdot p_{k}\qquad\text{with}\qquad A_{k}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\big[A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\big].

Hence,
A_{k}1=p_{k}\to p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
as
k\to\infty
and
A=l_{y}\cdot p\in\mathfrak{g}_{y}
.
But since
\deg p
in
A=l_{y}\cdot p
is not bounded,
\mathfrak{g}_{y}
is not contained in any
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{V}}
.

\circ

In
2.1
we gave the definition of a regular Fréchet Lie group and its Lie algebra.
The Examples
4.11
,
4.15
, and
4.16
show that
\mathfrak{g}
in the
4.10
is not a regular Fréchet Lie algebra of any regular Fréchet Lie group
G
.
It is too large and not even a vector space.
However, since it contains all operators
A
such that
e^{tA}
is well-defined, it contains all regular Fréchet Lie algebras in our case.

The set
\mathfrak{g}
and its regular Fréchet Lie algebras are therefore not only of practical interest in the Theorems
3.2
and
3.5
and
3.3
, but they can also serve as simple toy examples to further study regular Fréchet Lie groups and their Lie algebras.

5
Three-Line-Proof of Stochel’s Theorem

Recall from
Section

2
, for a sequence
s=(s_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
and a polynomial

p(x)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}p_{\alpha}\cdot x^{\alpha}\quad\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}],

the Riesz functional
L_{s}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}
is defined by

L_{s}(p)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}p_{\alpha}\cdot s_{\alpha}.

We get the following three-line-proof of Stochels Theorem
[
27
]
.

Theorem 5.20
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
closed.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

s
is a
K
-moment sequence.

(ii)

For all
d\in\mathds{N}_{0}
,
s|_{\leq d}:=(s_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}:|\alpha|\leq d}
is a truncated
K
-moment sequence.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Clear.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i): For
p\in\mathrm{Pos}(K)
,
L_{s}(p)\overset{\deg p\leq d}{=}L_{s|_{\leq d}}(p)\overset{\text{(ii)}}{\geq}0
and, by Haviland’s Theorem
[
12
]
,
s
is a
K
-moment sequence.
∎

Note, in (ii) actually a weaker property is used:

(ii’)

For all
d\in\mathds{N}_{0}
and
p\in\mathrm{Pos}(K)_{\leq d}
,
L_{s|_{\leq}d}(p)\geq 0
.

The truncated sequences
s|_{\leq d}
do not need to be truncated
K
-moment sequences.

.

L_{s}(p)\overset{\deg p\leq d}{=}L_{s|{{}_{\leq}d}}(p)

and

\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]=\bigcup_{d\in\mathds{N}_{0}}\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d}.

Conclusions and Open Problems

In
[
7
,
9
,
8
]
we used the theory of regular Fréchet Lie groups and algebras to investigate linear maps

T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

and

e^{tA}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}].

We extended previous results
[
11
,
5
,
16
]
about positivity preserver.
Especially in
[
9
]
we claimed that the maps
e^{tA}
could be used in linear optimization.
This is shown in the current work by the Theorems
3.2
and
3.5
.

In
3.3
we give for every
d\in\mathds{N}_{0}
and subset
S\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(K)_{\leq 2d}
an explicit linear operator

A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}

such that
e^{tA}S
is a subset of the sums of squares for all
t\geq\tau\geq 0
.
In the optimization

\min_{p\in S}L(p)

over a linear functional
L:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}\to\mathds{R}
, using the Schmüdgen or the Putinar Positivstellensatz can lead to degree blow ups in the
\sigma_{e}
in (
1
) and (
2
), even though the original problem lives only in
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d}
.
This is circumvented by
3.3
(iii), since the Schmüdgen and the Putinar Positivstellensatz are removed.

But
3.2
and
3.3
depend on the fixed degree
d\in\mathds{N}_{0}
.
Hence, it is natural to ask the following questions which are open.

Open Problem 1
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
,
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
be closed, and
S\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(K)
.
Exists a linear operator

A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

such that

(a)

there exists
d_{0}<d_{1}<\dots
in
\mathds{N}_{0}
and constants
\tau_{0}\leq\tau_{1}\leq\dots
such that

e^{\tau_{d_{i}}A}\big(S\cap\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d_{i}}\big)\quad\subseteq\quad\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d_{i}}^{2}

for all
i\in\mathds{N}_{0}

or does there even exists such an operator with

(b)

e^{A}S\subseteq\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{2}
?

The
1
is solved in
3.5
for
K=\mathds{R}^{n}
and
S=\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n})
.
In
3.8
a condition is formulated such that
1
(b) is solvable for
S=\mathrm{Pos}(K)
with
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
.

The second open problem is the following.

Open Problem 2
.

Exists a (good or useful) description of the sets

e^{\tau_{d_{i}}A}\big(S\cap\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d_{i}}\big)\qquad\text{or}\qquad e^{A}S

in
\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2d_{i}}^{2}
and
\sum\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{2}
, respectively?

To attack these problems we looked in
Section

4
at the structure of the set
\mathfrak{g}
of all linear maps
A
such that
e^{tA}
is well-defined.
We characterized all such maps
A
in
\mathfrak{g}
.
We showed with explicit examples that
\mathfrak{g}
is not a vector space (
4.11
),
\mathfrak{g}
is not closed under multiplication (
4.15
), and
\mathfrak{g}
is not closed under the Lie bracket (
4.16
).
We looked at special regular Fréchet Lie algebras in
\mathfrak{g}
in
4.18
and
4.19
.
We have to work here in the full setting of non-commutative regular Fréchet Lie groups and algebras, where the theory is much more evolved than in the commutative case in
[
7
]
.

With respect to
2
one also has to mention, that
\mathrm{Pos}(K)
or general subsets
S\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(K)
are not spectrahedra or spectrahedral shadows
[
17
]
.
This property is preserved under linear maps like
e^{tA}
, i.e., an NP-hard optimization problem remains NP-hard and “only” the degree blow ups in the Schmüdgen and the Putinar Positivstellensatz (
1
) and (
2
) are removed.

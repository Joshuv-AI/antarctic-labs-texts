Dipyridamole as a Perioperatively Safe Aurora Kinase A Inhibitor for Neoadjuvant Therapy in Esophageal Squamous Cell Carcinoma
Authors: Jiawen Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionAurora kinase A (AURKA) is overexpressed in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) and correlates with chemoresistance and poor prognosis. Dedicated AURKA inhibitors show potent target engagement but cause severe myelosuppression incompatible with perioperative esophagectomy. We aimed to identify a compound with meaningful AURKA-binding activity and an established perioperative safety profile through drug repurposing.MethodsWe screened ~2500 FDA-approved drugs against the AURKA co-crystal structure (PDB: 5EW9) using hierarchical Glide docking with MM-GBSA rescoring, followed by 200-ns molecular dynamics simulations, normal mode analysis, and ADMET profiling. The top candidate was validated using NanoBRET target engagement assays and CCK-8 viability assays in three ESCC cell lines (KYSE-150, TE-1, EC109) and normal esophageal epithelial cells (Het-1A).ResultsDipyridamole, an established antiplatelet agent, ranked first with an MM-GBSA binding energy of −77.11 kcal/mol, exceeding all other drugs by >15 kcal/mol. MD simulations confirmed stable complex formation over 200 ns. NanoBRET assays validated direct AURKA binding in intact living cells (IC50 ≈ 2.8 μM). CCK-8 assays demonstrated selective antiproliferative activity (IC50: KYSE-150 = 12.4 μM; TE-1 = 18.7 μM; EC109 = 25.3 μM at 48 h) with a selectivity index of 6.3 versus normal cells (Het-1A IC50 = 78.5 μM).ConclusionDipyridamole is an AURKA-engaging compound with an established perioperative safety profile, validated cellular target engagement, and selective anti-ESCC activity, warranting further preclinical investigation as a potential neoadjuvant adjunct.

Introduction

Esophageal cancer constitutes a major global health burden, ranking as the seventh most common malignancy and the sixth leading cause of cancer-related mortality, with over 600,000 new diagnoses estimated in 2022.1 In China, esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) accounts for approximately 87--90% of all esophageal cancers, with an age-standardized incidence rate of 12.4 per 100,000 person-year.2 Despite the integration of neoadjuvant concurrent chemoradiotherapy (nCRT) with paclitaxel/carboplatin and subsequent minimally invasive esophagectomy, 5-year overall survival for locally advanced ESCC (cT2-T4a, N0-N3, M0) remains in the range of 36–47%, with pathological complete response (pCR) rates of only 25–49%.3,4 This substantial cohort of patients who derive suboptimal oncological benefit underscores an urgent need for molecularly targeted adjuncts that can safely enhance pCR rates and facilitate R0 resection without compromising perioperative fitness.

Aurora kinase A (AURKA) is a serine/threonine kinase that orchestrates centrosome maturation, bipolar spindle assembly, G2/M transition, and accurate chromosome segregation.5 Multiple independent studies have demonstrated that AURKA is markedly overexpressed in ESCC tissues relative to adjacent normal mucosa, correlating significantly with advanced TNM stage, lymph node positivity, lymphovascular invasion, and dismal patient prognosis.6–9 Mechanistically, hyperactive AURKA promotes ESCC pathogenesis through activation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR and Wnt/β-catenin axes, upregulation of anti-apoptotic BCL-2 family members, p53 destabilization, and conferral of resistance to cisplatin and 5-fluorouracil, which are the backbone agents of current neoadjuvant regimens.6,10 Genetic silencing or pharmacological inhibition of AURKA induces robust G2/M cell cycle arrest, caspase-dependent apoptosis, and ferroptosis in ESCC cell lines and patient-derived xenograft models.7,8,11

These compelling preclinical data have spurred development of dedicated AURKA inhibitors such as alisertib (MLN8237) and MK-5108, which have progressed to Phase I/II evaluation.12 However, their clinical utility is severely constrained by dose-limiting toxicities, most notably grade 3–4 neutropenia (incidence 43–68%), febrile neutropenia, and stomatitis.13 These adverse events pose particular hazards in the perioperative context of thoracic surgery, where adequate hematopoietic reserve and mucosal integrity are prerequisites for safe esophagectomy involving thoracotomy, esophageal anastomosis, and the systemic inflammatory response of major surgery. Consequently, the clinical translation of AURKA inhibition in ESCC has stalled.

This translational impasse defines the central question of our study: whether a compound can be identified that combines meaningful AURKA inhibitory activity with a perioperative safety profile compatible with esophagectomy. Drug repurposing, defined as the systematic identification of novel indications for approved agents with well-characterized safety profiles, offers a compelling solution.14 By leveraging existing safety data, repurposing can compress the timeline from bench to bedside by 3–7 years while dramatically reducing development costs.

In the present study, we report an integrated computational and experimental campaign targeting AURKA in ESCC. Through structure-based virtual screening, 200-ns molecular dynamics simulations, normal mode analysis, and ADMET profiling, we identified dipyridamole, a pyrimidopyrimidine antiplatelet and vasodilatory agent in clinical use for over five decades, as the foremost AURKA-binding candidate among ~2500 FDA-approved drugs, with an unprecedented binding free energy of −77.11 kcal/mol.15–17 Critically, we then experimentally validated this computational prediction using NanoBRET cellular target engagement assays and CCK-8 cell viability assays, demonstrating that dipyridamole directly engages AURKA in living cells and selectively inhibits ESCC cell proliferation. Dipyridamole possesses several attributes particularly advantageous for ESCC neoadjuvant use: an established perioperative safety profile without increased surgical bleeding risk, no clinically significant myelosuppression, and documented chemosensitization capacity in gastrointestinal cancers.18–20 To our knowledge, this constitutes the first study combining systematic computational evidence with experimental target engagement validation to provide a preclinical rationale for exploring dipyridamole as a candidate for further investigation in the ESCC neoadjuvant setting.

Beyond its established cardiovascular indications, dipyridamole has demonstrated antitumor activity across multiple cancer types. In prostate cancer, dipyridamole impairs autophagic flux and inhibits cell growth.21 In colon cancer, it synergizes with trametinib through combined targeting of HMGCS1 and the MEK pathway.22 In breast cancer, dipyridamole has been reported to suppress carcinogenesis and enhance methotrexate cytotoxicity.23,24 In colorectal cancer, combined administration with aspirin induces apoptosis through an unfolded protein response-dependent mechanism.25 Dipyridamole has also been evaluated as a chemosensitizer in urothelial and gastric malignancies, augmenting the cytotoxicity of cisplatin and 5-fluorouracil in preclinical bladder cancer models and in clinical regimens for advanced gastric cancer.26,27 This body of evidence supports the broader oncological rationale for repositioning dipyridamole and contextualizes the present investigation of its activity in ESCC.

Materials and Methods

Target Protein Preparation and Binding Site Definition

The high-resolution crystal structure of human AURKA in complex with the selective inhibitor MK-5108 (PDB ID: 5EW9; resolution: 2.18 Å; space group: P3121) was retrieved from the RCSB Protein Data Bank.28 Protein preparation was conducted using the Protein Preparation Wizard module within Schrödinger Suite 2023–4. The workflow comprised: deletion of crystallographic water molecules located beyond 5 Å from the co-crystallized ligand; assignment of bond orders and formal charges; addition of hydrogen atoms; modeling of missing side chains and loop regions employing Prime homology refinement; optimization of hydrogen-bond networks using the OPLS4 force field; and restrained all-atom energy minimization to convergence (heavy-atom RMSD threshold ≤0.30 Å). Protonation states were determined at physiological pH 7.0 ± 2.0 using PROPKA 3.1.

The receptor docking grid centroid was placed at the center of mass of the co-crystallized MK-5108 ligand, with an outer grid box of 30 × 30×30 Å and an inner docking sampling box of 20 × 20×20 Å. Protocol validation was confirmed by redocking of the native MK-5108 ligand, achieving an RMSD of 1.12 Å relative to the crystallographic pose.

FDA-Approved Drug Library Preparation and Hierarchical Virtual Screening

A comprehensive library of FDA-approved small-molecule drugs (~2500 compounds; DrugBank version 5.1.12, April 2025) was prepared using LigPrep as implemented in Schrödinger Suite 2023–4, with ionization states generated at pH 7.0 ± 2.0 using Epik (version 6.4), retaining a maximum of eight ionization and tautomeric states per compound; the most probable ionization state at physiological pH was selected for all subsequent docking calculations. Low-energy 3D conformers were generated using the OPLS4 force field, followed by desalting. After quality filtering (molecular weight 100--1000 g/mol; ≤5 undefined stereocenters), 2436 molecular entities remained.

Virtual screening employed a three-tier hierarchical Glide docking cascade (Glide version 11.4): Stage 1, High-Throughput Virtual Screening (HTVS, top 10% retained); Stage 2, Standard Precision (SP, top 10% retained); Stage 3, Extra Precision (XP) with full post-docking minimization. The top 50 XP hits were rescored using Prime MM-GBSA binding free energy calculations (Prime version 6.4, VSGB 2.0 solvation model, OPLS4 force field, full flexibility within 5 Å of the docked pose). MM-GBSA ΔG values served as the primary ranking criterion.

All-Atom Molecular Dynamics Simulations

The top three virtual screening candidates (dipyridamole, SN-38, and cefamandole nafate sodium), together with the reference inhibitor MK-5108, were subjected to 200-ns all-atom explicit-solvent MD simulations using GROMACS 2023.3. For dipyridamole, three independent 200-ns replicate simulations were performed with distinct initial velocity seeds to assess sampling robustness, bringing the total simulation time for this compound to 600 ns. All replicates were initiated from the same NPT-equilibrated starting structure with independently randomized velocities. Protein topology was assigned using CHARMM36m;29 ligand topologies were generated via CGenFF and cross-validated with SwissParam. Topology quality for dipyridamole was verified using the CGenFF web server (https://cgenff.umaryland.edu/), yielding a Param Penalty of 4.6 and a Charge Penalty of 4.2, both within the accepted thresholds (Param < 10; Charge < 50) recommended by the CHARMM force field developers (Vanommeslaeghe et al, 2010), confirming accurate assignment of dihedral and partial charge parameters.

The equilibration protocol comprised: (1) steepest descent energy minimization; (2) 100-ps NVT equilibration at 300 K with position restraints (1000 kJ/mol/nm2); (3) 100-ps NPT equilibration (Parrinello--Rahman barostat, 1.0 bar) with stepwise restraint release; and (4) 1-ns unrestrained NPT pre-production. Production trajectories (200 ns each) used 2-fs timesteps with LINCS constraints, PME electrostatics (1.2 nm cutoff), and van der Waals cutoff at 1.2 nm with force switching. Snapshots were saved every 10 ps.

Trajectory analysis included backbone and ligand RMSD, per-residue RMSF, radius of gyration (Rg), SASA, hydrogen-bond occupancy, PCA on Cα coordinates (last 150 ns), 2D Gibbs free energy landscape, and MM-GBSA binding free energy from 500 evenly spaced snapshots over the final 100 ns.

Normal Mode Analysis

NMA was performed on energy-minimized apo-AURKA and dipyridamole-bound AURKA structures using iMODS (http://imods.chaconlab.org).30 Cα coarse-grained elastic network models were computed with default parameters. Outputs included main-chain deformability plots, NMA-derived B-factors (compared against experimental 5EW9 B-factors), eigenvalue spectra, variance decomposition, residue-residue covariance matrices, and elastic network spring maps. Pearson correlation between theoretical and experimental B-factors was calculated using SciPy 1.11.

ADMET Profiling

Comprehensive ADMET characterization of dipyridamole was conducted using three independent platforms (accessed April 2025): SwissADME (physicochemical properties, drug-likeness, CYP profiles),31 pkCSM (quantitative ADME-toxicity predictions including Caco-2 permeability, HIA, BBB/CNS permeability, clearance, acute/chronic toxicity, AMES mutagenicity, hERG liability),32 and ADMETlab 3.0 (DILI probability, eye/skin/respiratory toxicity). All inputs employed the canonical SMILES representation of dipyridamole.

NanoBRET Cellular Target Engagement Assay

To validate the computational prediction of direct AURKA binding, NanoBRET target engagement assays were performed using the Promega NanoBRET TE Intracellular Kinase Assay platform. HEK293T cells were transiently transfected with an NanoLuc-AURKA fusion construct. After 24 h, cells were treated with increasing concentrations of dipyridamole, SN-38, cefamandole nafate sodium, or MK-5108 (positive control) in the presence of a cell-permeable fluorescent AURKA tracer. BRET ratios were measured after 2 h of compound incubation. Competitive displacement of the tracer by the test compound results in decreased BRET signal, allowing quantification of intracellular target engagement. All experiments were performed in three independent biological replicates, each conducted on a separate day, with two technical replicates per condition. IC50 values were derived by fitting the normalized BRET ratio data to a four-parameter logistic curve using GraphPad Prism 10.

CCK-8 Cell Viability Assay

Three ESCC cell lines (KYSE-150, TE-1, EC109) and one normal esophageal epithelial cell line (Het-1A), all obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA), were used to assess the antiproliferative activity and tumor selectivity of dipyridamole. Cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 5×103 cells/well and cultured for 24 h. Cells were then treated with dipyridamole at concentrations ranging from 1 nM to 10 mM for 48 h and 72 h. Cell viability was determined using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8; Dojindo, Kumamoto, Japan) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm. IC50 values were calculated by nonlinear regression using a variable-slope four-parameter model (GraphPad Prism 10). Selectivity indices (SI) were calculated as IC50 (Het-1A)/IC50 (ESCC cell line). All experiments were conducted in three independent biological replicates with six technical replicates per concentration point. Results are expressed as mean ± SD. Statistical comparisons between treatment groups were performed using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test, with statistical significance defined as p < 0.05.

Western Blot Analysis

KYSE-150 cells were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 3×105 cells per well and cultured overnight. Cells were treated with dipyridamole (0, 10, 25, or 50 μM) or alisertib (100 nM, positive control) for 24 hours. Cells were washed twice with ice-cold PBS and lysed in RIPA buffer supplemented with protease inhibitor cocktail and phosphatase inhibitor (PhosSTOP, Roche). Lysates were centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 15 minutes at 4°C, and supernatants were collected. Protein concentrations were determined using the BCA assay. Equal amounts of protein (30 μg per lane) were separated by 10% SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk (for total protein detection) or 5% BSA (for phosphoprotein detection) in TBST for 1 hour at room temperature, followed by overnight incubation at 4°C with the following primary antibodies: anti-p-AURKA (Thr288) (Cell Signaling Technology, #3079, 1:1000), anti-AURKA (Abcam, ab52973, 1:2000), anti-p-Histone H3 (Ser10) (Cell Signaling Technology, #3377, 1:1000), anti-Histone H3 (Cell Signaling Technology, #4499, 1:2000), and anti-β-actin (Cell Signaling Technology, #4970, 1:5000). After washing with TBST, membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 hour at room temperature and visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL).

Ethical Statement

This study did not involve human participants, human tissue, patient data, or animal experiments. All experiments were performed using commercially available cell lines. Therefore, ethical approval was not required.

Results

Virtual Screening Identifies Dipyridamole as the Foremost AURKA-Binding FDA-Approved Drug

The AURKA kinase domain sequence alignment highlights the conserved ATP-binding pocket architecture (Figure 1A). The three-tier hierarchical virtual screening cascade progressively enriched the compound library from 2436 input molecules to 244 (HTVS stage), 24 (SP stage), and ultimately 10 candidates advanced to XP docking. Structural analysis confirmed that dipyridamole inserts deeply into the ATP-competitive binding cleft of AURKA (Figure 1B), occupying the adenine sub-pocket, the ribose pocket, and a portion of the phosphate-binding region, with key residues visualized in the close-up binding pocket view (Figure 1C). Subsequent MM-GBSA rescoring of the top-50 XP-docked compounds generated a definitive binding affinity ranking. Dipyridamole consistently occupied the apex position with an MM-GBSA binding free energy of −77.11 kcal/mol, which was the strongest binding affinity observed among all FDA-approved drugs screened and exceeded the second-ranked compound (SN-38: −61.94 kcal/mol) by more than 15 kcal/mol (Figure 1D). Molecular Interaction Analysis Reveals a Multimodal Binding Mechanism

Detailed 3D and 2D interaction analysis illuminated distinct binding mechanisms for the top-ranked compounds (Figure 2). Dipyridamole established an unusually rich interaction network: three direct hydrogen bonds with Glu260 (C=O acceptor; N--H donor distance 2.91 Å) and Asn261 (N--H donor; estimated occupancy ~62% during MD); π-cation interactions with the aromatic systems of Tyr212 and Phe275; and an extensive hydrophobic network involving Leu139, Val147, Leu210, Ala213, Pro214, and Leu263 (Figure 2A). By contrast, cefamandole nafate sodium (MM-GBSA: −58.47 kcal/mol) and SN-38 (MM-GBSA: −61.94 kcal/mol) displayed fewer overall contact points (Figure 2B and C), rationalizing the superior computed binding free energy of dipyridamole. Molecular Dynamics Simulations Confirm Exceptional Complex Stability

200-ns all-atom MD simulations provided convergent evidence of exceptional binding stability. MM-GBSA binding free energy calculations were performed independently on each of three replicate simulations using 500 snapshots from the final 100 ns. The mean ΔG values were −50.86, −42.47, and −52.08 kcal/mol for Replicates 1, 2, and 3 respectively, with an overall mean ± SD of −48.47 ± 4.27 kcal/mol across replicates, confirming reproducible thermodynamically favorable binding across independent simulations (Figure 3A). The secondary structure element (SSE) evolution plot confirmed preservation of helical and β-strand architecture (Figure 3B). The 2D Gibbs free energy landscape demonstrated a single dominant low-energy basin (Figure 3C). Across three independent replicate simulations, protein backbone RMSD equilibrated within 30 ns and remained stable throughout, while ligand RMSD similarly converged to a stable value after an initial adjustment phase, consistent across all replicates (Figure 3D). SASA (~323,000 Å2; Figure 3E) and Rg (~1.88 nm; Figure 3F) remained stable, indicating maintenance of compact protein structure. PCA scatter plot showed tight conformational clustering (Figure 3G), with PC1 and PC2 accounting for ~48% of total variance (Figure 3H). Ramachandran analysis confirmed stereochemically valid backbone geometry (Figure 3I), and per-atom ligand RMSF revealed uniform flexibility consistent with tight pocket anchoring (Figure 3J). Normal Mode Analysis Demonstrates Ligand-Induced Restriction of Hinge-Region Dynamics

NMA on apo and holo structures revealed that dipyridamole binding produced pronounced and selective reduction in conformational flexibility at two functionally critical loci: the hinge segment A213/P214 (~42% reduction) and the DFG-adjacent activation loop region E260/N261 (~38% reduction; Figure 4A). NMA-derived B-factors showed excellent agreement with experimental values (Pearson r = 0.87; Figure 4B). The first normal mode (λ1 = 1.618×10−4) captured hinge-bending motion, and the first three modes accounted for ~65% of total variance (Figure 4C and D). The covariance matrix revealed correlated N-lobe/C-lobe co-motions and anti-correlated P-loop/activation loop movements consistent with canonical DFG-in dynamics (Figure 4E). The elastic network spring map confirmed dense connectivity and high local rigidity in the ATP pocket region (Figure 4F). ADMET Profiling Supports Perioperative Safety and Clinical Translatability

Dipyridamole’s ADMET profile was evaluated across three platforms. The molecular structure and key physicochemical descriptors are summarized as shown (Figure 5A). The drug-likeness radar chart demonstrated near-ideal pharmacological space occupancy, with two permissible Lipinski violations (MW \> 500 g/mol; H-bond acceptors = 12; Figure 5B). SwissADME confirmed acceptable consensus LogP (0.82), and a synthetic accessibility score of 4.03 confirming commercial availability (Figure 5C). pkCSM predicted moderate human intestinal absorption (66.31%), low BBB permeability (log BB: −1.412), which minimizes central neurotoxicity risk, and low CNS permeability (Figure 5D). No mutagenicity (AMES negative) or significant hepatotoxicity flags were raised. ADMETlab 3.0 confirmed manageable DILI probability and low eye/skin sensitization risk, consistent with decades of clinical use. Experimental Validation of AURKA Target Engagement and Antiproliferative Activity

To experimentally validate the computational predictions, we performed NanoBRET target engagement assays and CCK-8 cell viability assays. In the NanoBRET assay (Figure 6A), dipyridamole demonstrated dose-dependent displacement of the AURKA tracer with an IC50 of approximately 2.8 µM in cell culture conditions where the drug is essentially unbound (free fraction 75–100% in 10% FBS medium). MK-5108 (positive control) showed an IC50 of 0.12 µM. SN-38 and cefamandole nafate sodium exhibited only weak target engagement at high concentrations (IC50 ≈ 18 µM and 85 µM, respectively). The binding rank order was consistent with the computational MM-GBSA predictions. This target engagement readout is independent of dipyridamole’s phosphodiesterase and nucleoside transporter inhibitory activities. CCK-8 assays revealed dose-dependent antiproliferative activity of dipyridamole in all three ESCC cell lines at 48 h (Figure 6B) and 72 h (Figure 6C), with IC50 values of 12.4 µM (KYSE-150), 18.7 µM (TE-1), and 25.3 µM (EC109) at 48 h. At 72 h, IC50 values decreased by approximately 1.5-2-fold, consistent with time-dependent effects of cell cycle--targeted agents. The concentration gradient across assays reflects the requirement for substantial target occupancy before phenotypic effects manifest.

To directly assess on-target AURKA inhibition, Western blot analysis was performed in KYSE-150 cells treated with dipyridamole (0, 10, 25, and 50 μM) or alisertib (100 nM, positive control) for 24 hours (Figure 6D). Dipyridamole reduced p-AURKA (Thr288) levels in a dose-dependent manner. p-Histone H3 (Ser10) was concordantly suppressed in a dose-dependent fashion.

Discussion

The fundamental challenge in translating AURKA-targeted therapy to ESCC is not a lack of potent inhibitors, but a lack of safe inhibitors suitable for the perioperative context. Alisertib and MK-5108 demonstrate excellent AURKA binding, yet their severe myelosuppression makes them incompatible with the physiological demands of esophagectomy, one of the most extensive operations in thoracic surgery.12,13 Our study addresses this translational impasse by identifying and experimentally validating dipyridamole as a compound that combines AURKA-binding activity with an established perioperative safety profile derived from decades of clinical use in cardiovascular indications, a combination that no dedicated AURKA inhibitor currently offers.

The NanoBRET data demonstrate that dipyridamole directly engages AURKA in intact living cells with an IC50 of 2.8 μM. Direct comparison of this value to total plasma Cmax is not appropriate, as dipyridamole is extensively bound to plasma proteins (free fraction approximately 1.9–3.5% in human plasma). Notably, the NanoBRET assay was performed in cell culture medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum, where the free fraction of dipyridamole is 75–100% (Creaven et al, 1991), and the measured IC50 therefore reflects intrinsic cellular target engagement rather than a plasma pharmacokinetic parameter. Whether sufficient intracellular concentrations can be achieved in ESCC tumor tissue in vivo requires dedicated pharmacokinetic investigation. The pharmacologically relevant comparison with MK-5108 is not one of binding affinity but of perioperative tolerability, a criterion that only dipyridamole satisfies.

The difference between the MM-GBSA binding free energy (−77.1 kcal/mol) and the NanoBRET IC50 (2.8 μM) reflects the well-documented tendency of implicit-solvent end-point methods to overestimate absolute binding affinities, particularly for large or highly charged ligands.33,34 MM-GBSA was used here solely as a rank-ordering tool across 2436 compounds,35 and dipyridamole’s first-place ranking by a margin exceeding 15 kcal/mol is robust to this systematic bias. All pharmacological inferences are based on the experimentally determined NanoBRET IC50. CGenFF parameterization of dipyridamole yielded a Param Penalty of 4.6 and a Charge Penalty of 4.2, both below the threshold of 10 at which validation is recommended, reducing the likelihood of artifactual inflation of MM-GBSA scores from poor force field parameterization.36,37

The CCK-8 results demonstrate selective antiproliferative activity across ESCC cell lines, with normal esophageal epithelial cells substantially less sensitive. The sensitivity hierarchy (KYSE-150 > TE-1 > EC109) correlates with reported AURKA expression levels across these cell lines, consistent with, though not proof of, a contribution from AURKA inhibition alongside dipyridamole’s established phosphodiesterase and nucleoside transporter inhibitory activities. Direct pharmacodynamic attribution requires mechanistic marker data, which are provided by the NanoBRET and Western blot analyses described above. The selectivity index of 6.3, while encouraging for an initial repurposing candidate, is modest and will require optimization in future medicinal chemistry studies.

The NMA findings provide mechanistic insight beyond simple binding affinity. The selective attenuation of deformability at A213/P214 (hinge entrance) and E260/N261 (DFG-motif flank) suggests that dipyridamole does not merely occupy the ATP binding site but allosterically dampens conformational transitions required for catalytic competence. This dual mechanism of ATP competition and conformational restriction mirrors type I½ kinase inhibitor behavior and implies that cellular AURKA inhibition may be achieved at lower intracellular drug concentrations than required for pure competitive occupancy, which is a pharmacologically favorable property.38

From the perspective of ESCC neoadjuvant strategy, dipyridamole presents several features that merit further investigation. Its documented chemosensitization of cisplatin and 5-fluorouracil through nucleoside transport inhibition and modulation of apoptotic signaling could augment the cytotoxicity of standard neoadjuvant regimens.15,19 Emerging evidence of radiation sensitization via AURKA pathway suppression provides a mechanistic basis for synergism with concurrent chemoradiotherapy.20 Furthermore, AURKA inhibition would impair mitotic spindle assembly and activate the spindle assembly checkpoint, amplifying the effects of paclitaxel, a standard neoadjuvant agent, through complementary mechanisms of mitotic interference.

In contrast to dedicated AURKA inhibitors, dipyridamole carries an established clinical safety record accumulated over decades of use in cardiovascular indications, though it should be noted that perioperative safety in the ESCC surgical context was not directly evaluated in the present study. Based on its historical profile, dipyridamole has no clinically significant myelosuppressive potential; its mild antiplatelet effect has not been associated with increased surgical bleeding risk at standard doses; and its low CNS penetration is expected to minimize postoperative neurocognitive complication risk. These characteristics, combined with its low cost and global generic availability, suggest that dipyridamole may face lower barriers to perioperative use than investigational AURKA inhibitors, though formal evaluation in the esophagectomy setting will be necessary before any such inference can be confirmed.

Several limitations warrant candid acknowledgment. First, although NanoBRET confirms direct cellular target engagement, the selectivity of dipyridamole for AURKA versus the broader kinome requires characterization through kinase panel profiling. Preliminary computational docking of dipyridamole against the human AURKB crystal structure (PDB: 4AF3) yielded an MM-GBSA binding free energy of −24.0±3.6 kcal/mol, compared to −33.7±4.1 kcal/mol for AURKA, suggesting lower predicted affinity for AURKB at the computational level.39 Experimental AURKB counter-screening is planned as an immediate priority in the follow-up study. Second, Western blot analysis in KYSE-150 cells presented in this study demonstrates dose-dependent suppression of p-AURKA (Thr288) and p-Histone H3 (Ser10) following dipyridamole treatment, providing initial on-target pharmacodynamic evidence. Further mechanistic delineation including cell cycle analysis, apoptosis assays, and validation across additional ESCC cell lines is planned as part of the follow-up study. Additionally, dipyridamole’s plasma protein binding (free fraction approximately 1.9–3.5% in human plasma) raises an important pharmacokinetic consideration: whether intracellular concentrations sufficient to engage AURKA can be achieved in tumor tissue in vivo requires dedicated pharmacokinetic investigation. Third, synergy assessment with cisplatin/5-FU/paclitaxel using Chou--Talalay combination indices and in vivo validation in ESCC xenograft or patient-derived xenograft models are essential next steps. Fourth, dipyridamole’s classification as a P-glycoprotein substrate deserves attention in ESCC where MDR1 overexpression is documented, potentially limiting intracellular accumulation. Fifth, while ADMET predictions from validated platforms guide expectations, empirical pharmacokinetic characterization in ESCC patients remains necessary.

Looking forward, the translational roadmap encompasses three parallel tracks: (i) expanded experimental validation including kinase selectivity profiling, mechanistic Western blot studies, cell cycle/apoptosis analyses, and combination studies with standard neoadjuvant agents; (ii) murine pharmacodynamic and efficacy studies in ESCC PDX models; and (iii) design of an early-phase clinical study to evaluate the feasibility and preliminary efficacy of dipyridamole as an adjunct to standard neoadjuvant therapy in locally advanced resectable ESCC, contingent on satisfactory outcomes from the preclinical program. Dipyridamole’s established safety profile and generic availability may facilitate such an investigation relative to investigational AURKA inhibitors, though formal clinical translation requires completion of the preclinical validation steps outlined above.

Conclusions

Through an integrated computational and experimental approach, encompassing structure-based virtual screening, 200-ns molecular dynamics, normal mode analysis, ADMET profiling, NanoBRET target engagement validation, and CCK-8 antiproliferative assays, we have identified and validated dipyridamole as an AURKA-engaging compound with an established perioperative safety profile based on decades of clinical use, direct cellular target engagement, and preferential antiproliferative activity against ESCC. Dipyridamole occupies a unique therapeutic niche: it is not the most potent AURKA inhibitor, but it is the only one that combines meaningful AURKA-binding activity with a safety profile compatible with esophagectomy. This work provides a computational and preliminary in vitro basis for further investigation of dipyridamole in the ESCC neoadjuvant context. Expanded mechanistic validation, in vivo efficacy studies, and early-phase clinical investigation are warranted pending confirmation of the preclinical findings reported here.

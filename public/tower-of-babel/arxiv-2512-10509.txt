Flat groups of automorphisms of totally disconnected, locally compact groups
George A. Willis

Abstract
A group, ℋ \mathscr{H} , of automorphisms of a totally disconnected locally compact group, G G , is flat if there is a compact open U ≤ G U\leq G such that the index [ α ( U ) : U ∩ α ( U ) ] [\alpha(U):U\cap\alpha(U)] is mininimized for every α ∈ ℋ \alpha\in\mathscr{H} . The stabilizer of U U in ℋ \mathscr{H} is a normal subgroup, ℋ u \mathscr{H}_{u} ; the quotient ℋ / ℋ u \mathscr{H}/\mathscr{H}_{u} is a free abelian group; and the rank of ℋ \mathscr{H} is the rank of this free abelian group. Each singly generated group ⟨ α ⟩ \langle\alpha\rangle is flat and has rank either 0 0 or 1 1 . Higher rank groups may be seen in Lie groups over local fields and automorphism groups of buildings. Flat groups of automorphisms exhibit many of the features of these special examples, including analogues of roots and a factoring of U U into analogues of root subgroups. New proofs of improved versions of these results are presented here.

Introduction

These notes describe the structure of flat groups of automorphisms of a totally disconnected, locally compact (t.d.l.c.) group. Cartan subgroups of semi-simple
p
-adic Lie groups, or more precisely the groups of inner automorphisms they induce, are motivating examples flat groups and it is seen that flat groups share some of the features of Cartan subgroups.

A group,
\mathscr{H}
, of automorphisms of
G

, of

that is minimizing for every
\alpha\in\mathscr{H}
. For this notion to have any content, it is necessary that

be non-discrete, for the trivial subgroup is compact, open and minimizing for every automorphism otherwise. For it to be meaningful for a t.d.l.c. group, it is necessary that the group have compact open subgroups , and that was shown in general by van Dantzig,
[
26
]
, see
[
16
, Theorem 7.7]
. Since

is minimizing for
\alpha
if and only if it is tidy for
\alpha
, see
[
27
,
29
]
, results about subgroups tidy for a single automorphism are used to study flat groups. Relevant results about tidy subgroups are collected from the literature in the first section.

The second section gives complete proofs that a flat group
\mathscr{H}
is abelian modulo the stabilizer of a minimizing subgroup and that there exist surjective homomorphisms
\mathscr{H}\to\mathbb{Z}
that are analogous to roots of a Cartan subgroup. It is also shown that tidy subgroups factor into analogues of root subgroups. These things were shown in

Minimizing subgroups and the scale

This section gives the definitions and summarizes basic results about the scale and minimizing subgroups associated with a single endomorphism of a t.d.l.c. group. We begin with endomorphisms even though the later results on flatness only concern automorphisms. Definition 1.1.1

Suppose that

1.2
Subgroups associated with automorphisms

The minimizing, or tidy, subgroups associated with an endomorphism are generally not unique. For example, if
U
is tidy for the automorphism
\alpha

Definition 1.2.1
.

Let

\displaystyle\parb(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\bigl\{x\in G\mid(\alpha^{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mbox{ has an accumulation point}\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\parb(\alpha)\cap\parb(\alpha^{-1})

\displaystyle=\bigl\{x\in G\mid\alpha^{n}(x)\to\id_{G}\mbox{ as }n\to\infty\bigr\}

\displaystyle\mbox{ and }\ \nub(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\overline{\con(\alpha)}\cap\lev(\alpha)=\overline{\con(\alpha)}\cap\parb(\alpha^{-1}).

These sets are subgroups of

That

Lemma 1.2.5
.

Let

Step 2

Step 3

Adjoin

‘Adjoining’

1.3
The tree representation theorem and scale groups

The following representation of
\widehat{U}_{\alpha+}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
as an isometry group of a regular tree is not needed for the description of flat groups to come. It does apply to the analogues of root subgroups, however, and shows that these abstract defined subgroups of general t.d.l.c. groups may be represented as concrete groups of isometries of a tree. The regular tree in which every vertex has degree
q
is denoted by
T_{q}
.

Theorem 1.3.1
(Tree Representation Theorem
[
6
]
).

Let
\alpha\in\Aut(G)
and
U\in\COS(G)
be tidy for
\alpha
. Suppose that
s(\alpha)=s
. Then there is a homomorphism
\phi:\widehat{U}_{\alpha+}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle\to\Isom(T_{s+1})
such that:

1.

\phi(\widehat{U}_{\alpha+}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle)
fixes an end,
\omega
, of
T_{s+1}
and is transitive on the vertices of
T_{s+1}
;

2.

\phi(\widehat{U}_{\alpha+})
is contained in the set of elliptic elements of
\Isom(T_{s+1})
(that is, the tree isometries having finite orbits) and
\phi(\alpha)
is a hyperbolic isometry (that is, has infinite orbits) that has
\omega
as an attracting end and which translates its axis by distance
1
;

3.

\phi(\widehat{U}_{\alpha+}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle)
is a closed subgroup of
\Isom(T_{s+1})
and the kernel of
\phi
is the largest compact normal subgroup of
\widehat{U}_{\alpha+}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
; and

4.

there is a vertex,
v_{0}
, on the axis of
\phi(\alpha)
such that
\phi(U_{\alpha+})
is the subgroup of
\phi(\widehat{U}_{\alpha+}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle)
that fixes
v_{0}
, and hence also fixes every vertex on the ray
[v_{0},\omega)
.

The tree representation theorem is a particular case of the Bass-Serre theory that represents a graph of groups on a regular tree. This comes about because
\alpha^{-1}
is an endomorphism
U_{\alpha+}\to U_{\alpha+}
such that
\alpha^{-1}(U_{\alpha+})
has index
s
in
U_{\alpha+}
and thus gives rise to an HNN extension
U_{\alpha+}\ast_{\alpha^{-1}}
. However, the tree
T_{s+1}
may be defined directly in this case because we already have the group
\widehat{U}_{\alpha+}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
and do not need to define it using the HNN extension. For this, let the vertex set be the coset space
(\widehat{U}_{\alpha+}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle)/U_{\alpha+}
and the edge set be
\left\{\{x\alpha^{n}U_{\alpha+},x\alpha^{n+1}U_{\alpha+}\}\mid x\in\widehat{U}_{\alpha+},\ n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
. Then the translation action of
\widehat{U}_{\alpha+}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
on cosets represents the group as tree isometries.

As stated in Theorem
1.3.1
,
\phi(\widehat{U}_{\alpha+}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle)
is a closed group of isometries that is transitive on the vertices and fixes an end of
T_{s+1}
. Such groups are called
scale groups
in
[
34
]
. Every scale group is a t.d.l.c. group and all scale groups occur in this manner. A close connection between scale groups and self-replicating groups, see
[
20
]
, is seen in
[
34
]
.

2
Flat Groups of Automorphisms

Groups of automorphisms of a t.d.l.c. group for which there exist a common tidy subgroup are the main topic of these notes. Such groups of automorphisms are called
flat
(a name suggested by U. Baumgartner), and it is seen here that they have a particular structure that leads to refinements of the tidiness conditions and the scale.

The name ‘flat’ follows from the structure of the groups, namely: a group of automorphisms,
\mathscr{H}\leq\Aut(G)
, sharing a tidy subgroup,
U\leq G
, is free abelian modulo the normaliser of
U
; and the free abelian group
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is geometrically flat in the sense that it is cocompact in the euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The exponent
d
is called the
rank
of the flat group
\mathscr{H}
. Further evidence for the aptness of the name ‘flat’ is seen in
[
4
]
, where it is shown that maximal flat groups of inner automorphisms of the isometry group of a building translate a (metrically flat) apartment of the building and that the rank of the group is equal to the dimension of the apartment; and also in
[
3
]
, where it is shown that flat groups of inner automorphisms of hyperbolic t.d.l.c. groups can have rank at most
1
. The geometric interpretation of flatness of a group of automorphisms is given a partial converse in
[
5
]
.

The tidiness conditions of Theorem
1.1.2
resolve into conditions that
U
satisfies for every automorphism
\alpha\in\mathscr{H}
: tidiness above now expresses
U
as a product of subgroups, each of which is expanded or shrunk by every
\alpha\in\mathscr{H}
; and tidiness below says that each of the factors of
U
dilates under the action of
\mathscr{H}
to a closed subgroup of
G
. The amount by which
\alpha\in\mathscr{H}
expands a given factor of
U

Flatness of groups of automorphisms was introduced in

2.2
Roots for a flat group and the scaling subgroups associated with them

Roots of a flat group,
\mathscr{H}
, of automorphisms of
G
and subgroups of
G
that are scaling for
\mathscr{H}
are key concepts for describing the structure of
\mathscr{H}
and its action on
G
.

Definition 2.2.1
.

A closed, non-compact,
\mathscr{H}
-stable subgroup
H\leq G
that has a compact, relatively open group
U\leq H
such that

\displaystyle\alpha\in\mathscr{H}\implies

\displaystyle\text{ either }\alpha(U)\geq U\text{ or }\alpha(U)\leq U

\displaystyle\text{ and }H=

\displaystyle\ \bigcup\left\{\alpha(U)\mid\alpha\in\mathscr{H}\right\}

is a
scaling subgroup
for
\mathscr{H}
.

Scaling subgroups are distinct from scale groups, see
[
34
]
and §
1.3
above. A scale group is a closed group of isometries of a regular tree
T_{s+1}
that fixes an end of the tree and is transitive on the vertices, whereas saying that a subgroup is scaling refers to its behaviour under the action of a flat group
\mathscr{H}
. Scale groups are thus a class of concrete t.d.l.c. groups, while scaling subgroups are contained in general abstract t.d.l.c. groups. The connection is that every scaling subgroup has a representation with compact kernel as a scale group, see
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
. An understanding of scale groups will contribute to knowledge about scaling groups and, through the results shown in these notes, to knowledge of flat groups of automorphisms.

The condition that either
\alpha(U)\geq U
or
\alpha(U)\leq U
implies that
U
is tidy for
\mathscr{H}|_{H}
and hence that
\mathscr{H}|_{H}
is flat. This condition also implies that the set
\left\{\alpha(U)\mid\alpha\in\mathscr{H}\right\}
is totally ordered by inclusion. Since
\alpha(U)
is compact and open for each
\alpha\in\mathscr{H}
, the index
[\beta(U):\alpha(U)]
is a positive integer for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{H}
with
\alpha(U)\leq\beta(U)
and so there is a minimum such index bigger than
1
. (Note that non-compactness of
H
implies that there is
\alpha\in\mathscr{H}
such that
\alpha(U)>U
.) Choose
\beta_{*}\in\mathscr{H}
such that
[\beta_{*}(U):U]
is equal to this minimum index. Then

\left\{\alpha(U)\mid\alpha\in\mathscr{H}\right\}=\left\{\beta_{*}^{n}(U)\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

Hence,
N:=\bigcap_{\alpha\in\mathscr{H}}\alpha(U)=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\beta_{*}^{n}(U)
is a compact,
\mathscr{H}
-stable subgroup of
H
and, since
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\beta_{*}^{n}(U)=H
, we have that
H=\con(\alpha|_{H}/N)
for every
\alpha\in\mathscr{H}
such that
\alpha(U)<U
.

The modular function on the restriction of
\mathscr{H}
to
H
is a homomorphism
\mathscr{H}\to(\mathbb{Q}^{+},\times)
given by

\triangle_{H}(\alpha|_{H})=\begin{cases}[\alpha(U):U],&\text{ if }\alpha(U)\geq U\\
[U:\alpha(U)]^{-1},&\text{ if }\alpha(U)\leq U\end{cases}

and is a power of
[\beta_{*}(U):U]
for every
\alpha\in\mathscr{H}
.

2.5
Contraction modulo the nub

Arguments to follow in §

will apply the results in §

repeatedly by restricting

\mathscr{H}
for each

;

•

\mathscr{H}
to

•

\mathscr{H}
is abelian modulo

.

The proof that

\mathscr{H}/\mathscr{H}_{u}
is a free abelian group.

Proof.

Each root
\rho\in\red(\mathscr{H})
is a homomorphism and hence so is
R
. The range of
R
is contained in
\bigoplus_{\rho\in\red(\mathscr{H})}\mathbb{Z}
because
\supp_{\mathscr{H}}(\alpha)
is finite for every
\alpha
, by Proposition
2.6.10
. The kernel of
R
contains
\mathscr{H}_{u}
because each
\rho
sends
\mathscr{H}_{u}
to
0
. If
\alpha\in\mathscr{H}\setminus\mathscr{H}_{u}
, then at least one of
s(\alpha^{-1})
and
s(\alpha)
is greater than
1
and so there is a root,
\ell
, in
\supp(\alpha)
such that
\rho(\alpha)\neq 0
, by Proposition
2.6.10
. Hence the kernel of the homomorphism is equal to
\mathscr{H}_{u}
. It follows that
\mathscr{H}/\mathscr{H}_{u}
is isomorphic to a subgroup of the free abelian group
\bigoplus_{\rho\in\red(\mathscr{H})}\mathbb{Z}
, and is therefore free abelian itself by
[
19
, Theorem 7.3]
.

Corollary 2.7.2
.

1.

If
\mathscr{H}\leq\Aut(G)
is flat, then the rank of
\mathscr{H}/\mathscr{H}_{u}
is at most equal to the number of roots for
\mathscr{H}
.

2.

There is no upper bound for the number of roots in terms of the rank of
\mathscr{H}/\mathscr{H}_{u}
, but the rank is finite if and only if the number of roots is finite.

3.

If

has infinite rank, then that rank and the number of roots have the same cardinality.

Proof.

1

–

The claims in

is supported on only finitely many roots, as shown in Proposition

Definition 2.7.3

The
rank
of the flat group

Proposition 2.7.5
.

Let
\mathscr{H}\leq\Aut(G)
be flat and suppose that
\{\gamma_{i}\mathscr{H}_{u}\}_{i\in I}
is a generating set for
\mathscr{H}/\mathscr{H}_{u}
. Then
\red(\mathscr{H})=\bigcup\{\supp_{\mathscr{H}}(\gamma_{i})\mid i\in I\}
.

Proof.

By definition,
\supp_{\mathscr{H}}(\gamma_{i})
is a subset of
\red(\mathscr{H})
for each
i\in I
and so it remains only to show that
\red(\mathscr{H})\subseteq\bigcup\{\supp_{\mathscr{H}}(\gamma_{i})\mid i\in I\}
. Suppose that
\rho\in\red(\mathscr{H})
. Then there is
\alpha\in\mathscr{H}
such that
\rho(\alpha)>0
and, since
\{\gamma_{i}\mathscr{H}_{u}\}_{i\in I}
generates
\mathscr{H}/\mathscr{H}_{u}
, there are
i_{1},\dots,i_{s}\in I
such that

\alpha+\mathscr{H}_{u}=\gamma_{i_{1}}+\dots+\gamma_{i_{s}}+\mathscr{H}_{u}.

Since it must be that
\rho(\gamma_{i_{j}})>0
for at least one
\gamma_{i_{j}}
,

\rho\in\supp_{\mathscr{H}}(\gamma_{i_{1}})\cup\dots\cup\supp_{\mathscr{H}}(\gamma_{i_{s}})

and the claim is established.

For the next lemma, recall from Definition
2.1.6
that for a flat group
\mathscr{H}
, the Levi subgroup is
\lev(\mathscr{H})=\left\{x\in G\mid\{\alpha(x)\}_{\alpha\in\mathscr{H}}^{-}\mbox{ is compact}\right\}
and this subgroup is closed by Lemma
2.1.8
.

Lemma 2.7.6
.

Let
\mathscr{H}\leq\Aut(G)
be flat and suppose that
\mathscr{K}\triangleleft\mathscr{H}
. Let
U
be tidy for
\mathscr{H}
. Then
\lev(\mathscr{K})
is
\mathscr{H}
-stable, and
U_{\mathscr{K}0}
is a compact open subgroup of
\lev(\mathscr{K})
that is tidy for the restriction of
\mathscr{H}
to
\lev(\mathscr{K})
.

Proof.

By definition of flatness, it suffices to show for every
\alpha\in\mathscr{H}
that
\lev(\mathscr{K})
is stabilised by
\alpha
and
U_{\mathscr{K}0}
is tidy for
\alpha
.

Consider
x\in\lev(\mathscr{K})
, so that
\{\beta(x)\mid\beta\in\mathscr{K}\}
has compact closure. Then

\left\{\beta(\alpha(x))\mid\beta\in\mathscr{K}\right\}=\left\{\alpha(\alpha^{-1}\beta\alpha(x))\mid\beta\in\mathscr{K}\right\}=\alpha\left(\left\{\beta(x)\mid\beta\in\mathscr{K}\right\}\right)

because
\mathscr{K}
is normal in
\mathscr{H}
. Since the latter set has compact closure,
\alpha(x)
belongs to
\lev(\mathscr{K})
and
\lev(\mathscr{K})
is
\alpha
-stable.

Since
U_{\mathscr{K}0}
is compact and
\mathscr{K}
-stable, it is contained in
\lev(\mathscr{K})
. Moreover,
U_{\mathscr{K}0}=U\cap\lev(\mathscr{K})
by Lemma
2.1.8
and is therefore open. Proposition
1.1.12
shows that
U_{\mathscr{K}0}
is tidy below for
\alpha|_{\lev(\mathscr{K})}
and it remains to show that it is tidy above.

Consider
u\in U_{\mathscr{K}0}
. Then
\mathscr{K}
-stability of
U_{\mathscr{K}0}
implies that
u\in\beta(U)
for every
\beta\in\mathscr{K}
and so, since
\beta(U)
is tidy for
\alpha
,
u=u_{+}u_{-}
with
u_{\pm}\in\beta(U)_{\alpha\pm}
. In other words,

C_{\beta}:=\left\{(u_{+},u_{-})\in\beta(U)_{\alpha\pm}\mid u=u_{+}u_{-}\right\}

is non-empty for every
\beta\in\mathscr{K}
, and
C_{\beta}
is a closed, and therefore compact, subset of
\beta(U)
. Since the intersection of finitely many tidy subgroups is tidy,
\left\{C_{\beta}\mid\beta\in\mathscr{K}\right\}
has the finite intersection property. Therefore
\bigcap\left\{C_{\beta}\mid\beta\in\mathscr{K}\right\}\neq\emptyset
. If
(u_{+},u_{-})
lies in this intersection, then
u_{\pm}\in U_{\mathscr{K}0}
and
u_{+}u_{-}=u
. Furthermore,
\alpha^{\mp k}(u_{\pm})\in\beta(U)
for every
k\geq 0
and every
\beta\in\mathscr{K}
, which implies that
u_{\pm}\in(U_{\mathscr{K}0})_{\pm}
. Therefore
U_{\mathscr{K}0}
is tidy above for
\alpha
as claimed.

The final result in this section completes the proof of Theorem
B
.
2
.

Proposition 2.7.7
.

Let
\mathscr{H}\leq\Aut(G)
be flat and suppose that
\rk(\mathscr{H})
is finite. Let
U
be tidy for
\mathscr{H}
. Then the roots in
\red(\mathscr{H})
may be ordered as
\rho_{1}
,…,
\rho_{q}
so that

U=U_{\mathscr{H}0}U_{\rho_{1}}\dots U_{\rho_{q}}=U_{\mathscr{H}0}\prod_{\rho\in\red(\mathscr{H})}\left(U\cap\con(\mathscr{A}_{\rho}/\nub(\mathscr{H}))\right).

Proof.

Since
U_{\rho_{i}}=U_{\mathscr{H}0}\left(U\cap\con(\mathscr{A}_{\rho_{i}}/\nub(\mathscr{H}))\right)
for each
\rho_{i}\in\red(\mathscr{H})
, by Proposition
2.2.7
, and since
U_{\mathscr{H}0}
normalizes
\con(\mathscr{A}_{\rho_{i}}/\nub(\mathscr{H}))
, it suffices to show that
U
is equal to the second product.

The proof is by induction on
\rk(\mathscr{H})
, that is, on the number of generators of
\mathscr{H}/\mathscr{H}_{u}
. The base case when
\rk(\mathscr{H})=0
holds because we then have
U=U_{\mathscr{H}0}
and
\red(\mathscr{H})=\emptyset
.

Assume that the claim has been shown for all flat
\mathscr{K}
with
\rk(\mathscr{K})\leq k
. Let
\mathscr{H}
be flat with rank
k+1
and suppose that
\gamma_{1}\mathscr{H}_{u}
, …,
\gamma_{k+1}\mathscr{H}_{u}
generate
\mathscr{H}/\mathscr{H}_{u}
. Then Corollary
2.5.3
shows that
\mathscr{K}=\langle\gamma_{k+1},\mathscr{H}_{u}\rangle
is a normal subgroup of
\mathscr{H}
and Propositions
1.2.10
and
1.2.13
show that

U=U_{\mathscr{K}0}D_{\gamma_{k+1}+}D_{\gamma_{k+1}-}

with
D_{\gamma_{k+1}\pm}=U\cap\con(\gamma_{k+1}^{\mp 1}/\nub(\mathscr{H}))
. (We have also used that
U_{\mathscr{K}0}
normalises
\con(\gamma_{k+1}/\nub(\mathscr{H}))
and
\con(\gamma_{k+1}^{-1}/\nub(\mathscr{H}))
.) Lemma
2.7.6
shows that
U_{\mathscr{K}0}
is a compact open subgroup of
\lev(\mathscr{K})
and is tidy for
\mathscr{H}|_{\lev(\mathscr{K})}
. Since
\gamma_{k+1}
is uniscalar on
\lev(\mathscr{K})
, it follows that
\rk(\mathscr{H}|_{\lev(\mathscr{K})})\leq k
and we may apply the induction hypothesis. Hence the roots in
\red(\mathscr{H}|_{\lev(\mathscr{K})})
may be ordered as
\rho_{1}
, …,
\rho_{r}
such that, abbreviating
U\cap\con(\mathscr{A}_{\rho}/\nub(\mathscr{H}))
by
{C}_{\rho}
and denoting
\bigcap\left\{\alpha|_{\lev(\mathscr{K})}(U_{\mathscr{K}0})\mid\alpha\in\mathscr{H}\right\}
by
\widetilde{U}
,

U_{\mathscr{K}0}=\widetilde{U}C_{\rho_{1}}\dots{C}_{\rho_{r}}.

Then the fact that
\mathscr{H}|_{\lev(\mathscr{K})}
is a quotient of
\mathscr{H}
and
\rho_{1}
, …,
\rho_{r}
pull back to roots of
\mathscr{H}
, combined with the identities

\displaystyle U_{\mathscr{K}0}

\displaystyle=\bigcap\left\{\gamma_{k+1}^{n}(U)\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}

\displaystyle\mbox{ and }\mathscr{H}

\displaystyle=\langle\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k}\rangle\mathscr{K},

imply that
\widetilde{U}=U_{\mathscr{H}0}
. Hence, noting that

U_{\mathscr{K}0}\cap\con(\mathscr{A}_{\rho}/\nub(\mathscr{H}))=U\cap\con(\mathscr{A}_{\rho}/\nub(\mathscr{H}))

if
\rho\in\red(\mathscr{H}|_{\lev(\mathscr{K})})
because
\langle\gamma_{k+1}\rangle\subset\mathscr{A}_{\rho}
in this case, we have that

U=U_{\mathscr{H}0}C_{\rho_{1}}\dots C_{\rho_{r}}D_{\gamma_{k+1}+}D_{\gamma_{k+1}-}.

Next, Theorem
2.5.2
shows that
\con(\gamma_{k+1}^{-1}/\nub(\mathscr{H}))
and
\con(\gamma_{k+1}/\nub(\mathscr{H}))
are stable under
\mathscr{H}
and Propositions
2.6.5
and
2.6.10
show that these subgroups are products of groups
\con(\mathscr{A}_{\rho}/\nub(\mathscr{H}))
with
\rho\in\red(\mathscr{H})
in the support of
\gamma_{k+1}
. Writing these roots in order
\rho_{r+1}
, …,
\rho_{q}
according to the order they appear as factors in
\con(\gamma_{k+1}^{-1}/\nub(\mathscr{H}))
and
\con(\gamma_{k+1}/\nub(\mathscr{H}))
respectively and abbreviating
U\cap\con(\mathscr{A}_{\rho}/\nub(\mathscr{H}))
by
{C}_{\rho}
, yields

D_{\gamma_{k+1}+}D_{\gamma_{k+1}-}=C_{\rho_{r+1}}\dots C_{\rho_{q}}

by Corollary
2.6.6
. Hence

U=U_{\mathscr{H}0}C_{\rho_{1}}\dots C_{\rho_{q}}

with
\rho_{1}
, …,
\rho_{q}
the roots of
\mathscr{H}
. (Note that every root in
\red(\mathscr{H})
appears: those in
\supp_{\mathscr{H}}(\gamma_{k+1})
are in the list
\rho_{r+1}
, …,
\rho_{q}
, and those in the complement of
\supp_{\mathscr{H}}(\gamma_{k+1})
are in the list
\rho_{1}
, …,
\rho_{r}
.) Therefore the claim of the induction holds with

.

2.8
The lower nub for a flat group

The nub subgroup for an automorphism
\alpha\in\Aut(G)
is simultaneously the largest subgroup of
G
contained in all subgroups tidy for
\alpha
, by Theorem
1.2.3
, and the smallest
\alpha
-stable subgroup,
C
, such that
\con(\alpha/C)
is closed, by Proposition
1.2.13
. The nub subgroup,
\nub(\mathscr{H})
for flat
\mathscr{H}
is the largest group contained in all subgroups tidy for
\mathscr{H}
. However, although
\con(\alpha/\nub(\mathscr{H}))
is closed for every
\alpha\in\mathscr{H}
,
\nub(\mathscr{H})
might not be the smallest
\mathscr{H}
-invariant group with this property.

The nub of a flat group of automorphisms was first studied by Colin Reid in
[
23
]
. He also introduced what he called the
lower nub
, denoted by
\lnub(\mathscr{H})
, which is the closed subgroup generated by all groups
\nub(\alpha)
with
\alpha\in\mathscr{H}
. Since
\beta(\nub(\alpha))=\nub(\beta\alpha\beta^{-1})
and
\mathscr{H}
normalises itself,
\lnub(\mathscr{H})
is
\mathscr{H}
-stable. Since a subgroup tidy for
\mathscr{H}
is tidy for every
\alpha\in\mathscr{H}
, Theorem
1.2.3
implies the following.

Proposition 2.8.1
.

Suppose that
\mathscr{H}\leq\Aut(G)
is flat. Then

\langle\nub(\alpha)\mid\alpha\in\mathscr{H}\rangle\leq\nub(\mathscr{H}).

Hence
\lnub(\mathscr{H})\leq\nub(\mathscr{H})
and
\lnub(\mathscr{H})
is compact.

Since
\lnub(\mathscr{H})
is compact and
\mathscr{H}
-invariant, the contraction group for
\alpha
modulo
\lnub(\mathscr{H})
, see Definition
1.2.7
, is well-defined.

Corollary 2.8.2
.

The contraction group
\con(\alpha/\lnub(\mathscr{H}))
is closed for every
\alpha\in\mathscr{H}
and
\lnub(\mathscr{H})
is the smallest closed group with this property. Furthermore,
\con(\alpha/\nub(\mathscr{H}))
is closed for every
\alpha\in\mathscr{H}
.

Example 4.1 in
[
23
]
shows that the inclusion
\lnub(\mathscr{H})\leq\nub(\mathscr{H})
in Proposition
2.8.1
may be strict. The flat group in that example is abelian and not finitely generated. Here are a couple of additional examples. The first takes a different approach and gives a finitely generated flat group, while the second is based on the idea in
[
23
]
but incorporates additional features which illustrate arguments in earlier sections.

Example 2.8.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated (discrete) group and
F
be a finite group. Then
G:=F^{\Gamma}
is a compact group and the translation action embeds
\Gamma
as a flat subgroup of
\Aut(G)
. Since the action of
\Gamma
on itself by translation is transitive,
G
itself is the smallest subgroup tidy for
\Gamma
and so
\nub(\Gamma)=G
. However, if every element of
\Gamma
has finite order, as constructed in
[
1
,
13
,
14
]
for instance, then
\nub(\gamma)=\{\id\}
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
and hence
\lnub(\Gamma)=\{\id\}
.

Example 2.8.4
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{p}
be the additive group of the field of order
p
and let
B=\mathbb{F}_{p}(\!(t)\!)\times\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}}
be the abelian group that is the direct product of the additive group of the field of formal Laurent series over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and the product over
\mathbb{Z}
of copies of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Denote elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}(\!(t)\!)
by
f=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}f(m)t^{m}
. For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
u_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}}
be given by
u_{n}(i)=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{ if }i=n\\
0,&\mbox{ otherwise}\end{cases}
. Define, for each
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, an automorphism
\beta_{m,n}
of
B
by

\beta_{m,n}(f,g)=(f,g+f(m)u_{n}),\quad f\in\mathbb{F}_{p}(\!(t)\!),\ g\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}}.

Then
\mathscr{B}:=\langle\beta_{m,n}\mid m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\rangle
is a flat subgroup of
\Aut(B)
with every compact open subgroup of
B
containing
\{0\}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}}
being invariant under
\mathscr{B}
and therefore tidy. Every subgroup tidy for
\mathscr{B}
must contain
\{0\}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}}
. Hence
\nub(\mathscr{B})=\{0\}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}}
. On the other hand, since every element of
\mathscr{B}
has order
p
,
\nub(\beta)=\{0\}\times\{0\}
for every
\beta\in\mathscr{B}
and so
\lnub(\mathscr{B})=\{0\}\times\{0\}
.

Next, define
\tau\in\Aut(B)
by
\tau(f,g)=(tf,g)
, so that
\tau
is the shift on the first coordinate and the identity on the second. Then
\mathscr{H}:=\langle\tau,\mathscr{B}\rangle
is a flat subgroup of
\Aut(B)
with
t^{m}\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![t]\!]\times\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}}
tidy for
\mathscr{H}
every
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence
\nub(\mathscr{H})=\{0\}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}}
. Once again we have that
\nub(\beta)=\{0\}\times\{0\}
for every
\beta\in\mathscr{H}
.

Further define
\sigma\in\Aut(B)
by
\sigma(f,g)=(f,g^{(1)})
, where
g^{(1)}(n)=g(n-1)
is the shift of
g
. Then
\mathscr{K}:=\langle\sigma,\tau,\mathscr{B}\rangle
is flat but now we have that
\nub(\sigma)=\{0\}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\nub(\mathscr{K})=\lnub(\mathscr{K})
.

Example 2.8.5
.

Let

B=\mathbb{F}_{p}(\!(t)\!)\times\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}},\ \mathscr{B}=\langle\beta_{m,n}\mid m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\rangle\mbox{ and }\mathscr{H}=\langle\tau,\mathscr{B}\rangle\leq\Aut(B)

G=B_{1}\times B_{2}

\mathscr{H}^{(1)}\times\mathscr{H}^{(2)}

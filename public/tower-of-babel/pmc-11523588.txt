Temporal trends of the burden of ischemic stroke attributable to high low-density lipoprotein cholesterol in China from 1999 to 2019
Authors: Zhenzhen Deng, Huilan Li, Jianglin Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHigh levels of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) can lead to the occurrence and development of atherosclerosis, which is one of the important risk factors for ischemic stroke (IS). However, details regarding the evolution of the IS burden attributable to high LDL-C in China has not been available. The objective of this study was to examine the changes over time, from 1990 to 2019, in the burden of IS attributed to high levels of LDL-C in China and to estimate the individual impacts of age, period, and cohort on the burden of IS associated with high LDL-C.MethodsDetailed data on IS burden attributable to high LDL-C from 1990 to 2019 in China were extracted from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) Study 2019. The numbers and age-standardized rates of IS-related mortality and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) attributable to high LDL-C were assessed by age and sex. The annual percentage change (APC) and average annual percentage change (AAPC) in the burden of IS due to high LDL-C were analyzed using Joinpoint regression model. The age-period-cohort analysis was carried out to assess the individual impacts of age, period, and cohort on the trends over time of mortality and DALY rate.ResultsThe number of IS-related deaths and DALYs due to high LDL-C in China were 182.7 thousand and 4.43 million in 2019, respectively, both more than double the corresponding numbers reported in 1990. However, despite these increased, the age-standardized mortality rate (ASMR) and age-standardized DALY rate (ASDR) remained unchanged. In 2019, men under 84 years of age had higher death and under 79 years of age had higher DALYs than women. However, above these ages, the gender disparities were reversed. Furthermore, age-specific rates of death, as well as DALY of IS attributable to high LDL-C in 2019 increased with age for both women and men, except for death in adults over 95 years old; and were greater in men than in women. From 1990 to 2019, males consistently had a higher ASMR and ASDR than females in China. During 1990–2019, the ASMR in women slightly decreased before 1997, sharply increased from 1997 to 2004, and then continuously decreased from 2004 to 2019, with an overall AAPC value of -0.4% (95% CI -0.8%, -0.0%). However, for the ASMR of men in China, the overall trend is upward and the AAPC is 0.5%(95% CI 0.1%, 0.8%). The ASDR in women and men had the similar trend as the ASMR over the time, with an AAPC of -0.4% (95% CI -0.7%, -0.1%) and 0.3% (95% CI 0.1%, 0.5%), respectively. The age-period-cohort analysis indicated a rise in period effects and a decline in cohort effects on mortality and DALY rate associated with IS caused by high LDL-C in both genders. Age effect on mortality rates from IS due to high LDL-C showed an exponential increased with age in all women and men except for the 60–69 age group and over 95 age group. The age relative risk of IS DALY rate due to high LDL-C increased with age in both genders except for 65–74 age group and over 95 age group.ConclusionsFrom 1990 to 2019, the burden of IS caused by high LDL-C in China significantly increased among both sexes combined and among men, while significantly decreased among women. Elderly have a substantial burden of IS attributable to high LDL-C. Therefore, effective and tailored strategies based on gender and age for IS primary prevention and management of IS and high LDL-C are urgently needed in China.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-024-20461-5.

Introduction

Globally, stroke is a major public health issue, ranking as the second-leading cause of death and the third for combined death and disability [1, 2]. The Global Burden of Diseases, Injuries, and Risk Factors Study (GBD) 2019 reported a significant rise in stroke cases, with incident strokes increasing by 70%, deaths by 43%, and stroke-related disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) by 32% from 1990 to 2019, reaching 12.2 million cases, 6.55 million deaths, and 143 million DALYs in 2019 [1]. Stroke-related deaths in China, accounting for over one-third of the global total, present significant public health challenges. In 2019, stroke continues to be the first leading cause of DALYs in China, with deaths and DALYs reaching 2.19 million and 45.9 million, respectively [3]. Ischemic stroke (IS), which makes up 60–85% of all strokes [4, 5], saw one of largest increases in China’s age-standardized incidence rates (ASIRs) from 1990 to 2019. IS-related deaths and DALYs in 2019 were 1.71 and 1.39 times higher than in 1990, respectively, contributing to a growing economic burden due to increasing prevalence and post-stroke care needs [6, 7].

High low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) is a modifiable risk factor for IS, and lipid-lowering therapy, such as statins, are recommended to prevent IS occurrence and recurrence [5, 8]. The Stroke Prevention by Aggressive Reduction in Cholesterol Level trial demonstrated that high-dose atorvastatin reduced stroke incidence by 33% in those with carotid stenosis and reduced recurrent stroke by 16% compared to placebo [9, 10]. Compared to patients with high LDL-C (2.6 mmol/L), those with low LDL-C (<1.8 mmol/L) had a 28% lower stroke risk [11], and every 1.0 mmol/L reduction in LDL-C reduced stroke risk by 20% [12]. The Treat Stroke to Target trial showed further risk reduction for subsequent cardiovascular events after an IS when LDL-C was below 1.8 mmol/L [13]. A recent meta-analysis confirmed that individuals with LDL-C levels above 1.8 mmol/L have a higher IS risk than those with levels below1.3 mmol/L [14]. It also found that lipid-lowering agents, such as statins, reduce IS risk in those achieving LDL-C below 1.8 mmol/L.

Despite the well-known benefits of controlling LDL-C to reduce cardiovascular disease risk, with the improvement of China’s economic level and lifestyle changes, high-energy, high-cholesterol, and high-fat diets are becoming increasingly common among people, coupled with lack of exercise and increased life stress, leading to a sustained rise in LDL-C levels [15]. A survey of 160,000 Chinese individuals revealed that 74.5% of high-risk and 93.2% of very high-risk atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease patients failed to meet LDL-C treatment target (<2.6 and <1.8 mmol/L, respectively), with only 5.5% and 14.5% receiving treatment [16]. In 2019, stroke-related DALYs attributed to high LDL-C in China reached 4.43 million, with an age-standardized DALY rate (ASDR) of 232.8 per 100,000 [3]. A GBD 2019 stroke analysis identified high LDL-C as the third leading risk factor for IS in East Asia, after high systolic blood pressure and ambient particulate matter pollution [1]. A related study on stroke burden in China and its attributable risk factors highlighted high LDL-C as the strongest risk factor for IS, aside from common factors for all stroke subtypes (elevated systolic blood pressure, environmental particulate matter pollution, smoking, and a high-sodium diet) [3].

While previous studies have explored the burden of stroke and its risk factors globally and in China, the burden of IS globally, and the global burden of diseases attributed to high level of LDL-C [1–3, 6, 17, 18], none have specifically analyzed long-term trends in IS burden attributed to high LDL-C in China from 1990 to 2019, by age group and sex. This study aims to fill that gap, using Joinpoint regression analysis and age-period-cohort model to explore the temporal trends and independent effects of chronological age, time period, and birth cohort on the IS burden attributable to high LDL-C in China.

Methods

Data sources

The data for this study were sourced from the GBD 2019 datasets, available through the Global Health Data Exchange source tool (http://ghdx.healthdata.org/gbd-results-tool). The GBD 2019 provided annual epidemiological estimates for incidence, prevalence, mortality, years lived with disability(YLDs), years of life lost(YLLs), DALYs and other epidemiological measures for 369 diseases and injuries, 286 causes of death, and 87 risk factors across 204 countries and territories, 21 regions, and seven super-regions from 1990 to 2019. The methodology of GBD 2019 has been detailed in prior publications [19, 20].

Definitions of IS and high LDL-C

IS was defined based on WHO criteria as an episode of neurological dysfunction caused by focal cerebral, spinal, or retinal infarction [21], with diagnostic codes from International Classification of Diseases (ICD) version 10 (G45-G46.8, I63-I63.9, I65-I66.9, I67.2-I67.848, I69.3-I69.4) and ICD version 9 (433-435.9, 437.0-437.1, 437.5-437.8). High LDL-C was considered as levels exceeding 1.3 mmol/L or 50 mg/dl, as established by a previous study [20].

Estimation of IS burden due to high LDL-C

The methodology used in the GBD 2019 study to evaluate the burden of IS attributed to high LDL-C involved three primary analytical steps: (1) gathering raw data through a systematic review; (2) producing adjusted and standardized clinical data inputs; and (3) modelling the processed data to generate estimates by age, sex, location, and year, using three main standardized tools (Cause of Death Ensemble model (CODEm), spatiotemporal Gaussian process regression (ST-GPR), and DisMod-MR 2.1) [1, 3, 19]. YLLs were calculated as the number of deaths caused by IS and a standard life expectancy at the age of death, while years YLDs were determined by multiplying IS prevalence with the corresponding disability weights. DALYs were the sum of YLDs and YLLs. To determine the IS burden attributable to high LDL-C, the GBD 2019 study used a comparative risk assessment conceptual framework, that involved several steps: collation of the LDL-C-IS pairs based on multiple epidemiological studies; estimation of relative risk for the high LDL-C-IS pair using meta-regression-Bayesian, regularized, and trimmed (MR-BRT) method; modelling of LDL-C exposure levels and distributions by age, sex, location, and year by using either DisMod-MR 2.1 or ST-GPR; determination of the theoretical minimum risk exposure level (TMREL); assessment of the population attributable fractions(PAFs) for LDL-C-IS pair; consideration the potential mediating effect; and estimation of the burden of IS attributable to high LDL-C by multiplying the YLLs, YLDs, and deaths for IS with the PAF for the high LDL-C risk factor [1, 3, 19].

Statistical analysis

Joinpoint regression analysis, as proposed by Kim and colleagues, was used to assess the trends in age-standardized mortality rate (ASMR) and ASDR [22]. The annual percentage change (APC) for each segment and average annual percentage change (AAPC) for overall trend with 95% confidence interval (CI) were calculated to determine the magnitude and direction of trends in ASMR and ASDR. If the lower limit of the 95% CI for the APC/AAPC is greater than zero, the corresponding ASMR and ASDR show an increasing trend, and vice versa; if it includes 0, the IS burden owing to high LDL-C is stable. Joinpoint regression was performed using Joinpoint software (Version 4.9.1.0).

An age-period-cohort model analysis was conducted to estimate the effects of age, period, and cohort on IS mortality and DALY rate attributable to high LDL-C. The age effect captured changes in physiological, pathological and social status with aging; the period effect reflects changes in the burden of IS attributable to high LDL-C, influenced by human factors such as advances in diagnosis, treatments, healthcare reforms, etc.; and the cohort effect refers to the lifestyle and risk factors exposure variations across generations. Mortality and DALY rate data for IS attributable to high LDL-C and population data were arranged into successive 5 years (from the 25–29 age group to the 90–94 age group and the 95 plus age group), continuous 5-year period (from 1990 to 1994 to 2015–2019), and corresponding consecutive 5-year birth cohort groups (starting from 1895 to 1899 to 1995–1999). The estimation of the coefficients for the age, period, and cohort effects were calculated by the age-period-cohort model analysis with intrinsic estimator method [23]. The exponential values of the coefficients represent the relative risk (RR) of IS burden attributed high LDL-C of a given age, period, and birth cohort, relative to the reference groups. The age-period-cohort model analysis and plot drawing were performed using Stata (version 16.0, StataCorp LP, TX, UA) and R (version 4.2.2), respectively. The rest of the analysis and visualization in this study were produced by R software. P values below 0.05 (two-tailed) were considered statistically significant.

Results

Analysis of IS deaths and DALYs attributable to high LDL-C in China

The number of IS deaths and DALYs attributable to high LDL-C increased more than twice in China from 67.8 thousand in 1990 to 182.7 thousand in 2019 and from 1.95 million to 4.43 million in 2019 (Table 1), respectively. In 2019, the ASMR and ASDR of IS due to high LDL-C were 11.01 per 100,000 and 232.83 per 100,000 (Table 1). High LDL-C accounted for 17.71% (95% UI 5.91%, 37.88%) and 20.28% (95% UI 10.73%, 36.19%) of the ASMR and ASDR of IS, respectively. The ASMR, ASDR and its age-standardized PAF remained unchanged compared to those in 1990. Between 1990 and 2019, the number of deaths and DALYs, ASMR, and ASDR of IS due to high LDL-C were consistently higher among men than women (Fig. 1). Prior to 2004, there were no significant gender differences in mortality or DALY rates. However, since then, the gender differences in mortality and DALY rates have gradually increased, with male rates being higher than those among females. Furthermore, between 1990 and 2019, the number of deaths and DALYs due to IS attributed to high levels of LDL-C increased in both men and women, with the increase being faster in men than in women. This gender difference was also observed in the mortality and DALY rates. While the ASMR and ASDR of IS attributable to high LDL-C slightly increased in men, they remained stable or slightly decreased in women (Supplementary Figure S1A and B).

Figure 2 displays the number and rate of IS deaths and DALYs attributed to high LDL-C, stratified by gender and age group, in 2019. The mortality estimate was higher in men than in women and increased with age, except for men older than 94 years. The mortality peaked in women over 95 years and men aged 90–94 years. In addition, the death rate increased tremendously after age 70 in both genders. The number of deaths increased with age, peaking at 80–84 years age in both genders, then, declined in older group. Gender difference in the number of deaths across different age groups in 2019 were observed, with more deaths in men under the 85–89 age group, but more in women thereafter. DALYs showed a bimodal distribution with age in both genders, peaking at the 60–64 age group and the 80–84 age group. Furthermore, before the 80–84 age group, males are more affected by IS attributable to high LDL-C, while females were more affected afterward. The DALY rate pattern by sex closely mirrored mortality trends across age groups.

Temporal trends for the burden of IS attributable to high LDL-C in China by gender

The segmental trends of the ASMR and ASDR for IS attributable to high LDL-C from 1990 to 2019 in China were divided using Joinpoint analysis (Fig. 3; Table 2). Overall, ASMR and ASDR for men and women combined remained stable from 1990 to 2019. The AAPC for ASMR and ASDR in the entire period fluctuated around zero, with values of 0% (95% CI -0.6%, 0.5%) and − 0.1% (95% CI -0.3%, 0.1%), respectively. Surprisingly, the trend in ASMR and ASDR among men was exactly the opposite of that among women. From 1990 to 2019, the ASMR and ASDR among females decreased by 0.4% (95% CI -0.8%, -0.0%) and 0.4% (95% CI -0.7%, -0.1%) annually, respectively. However, in contrast to that, the ASMR and ASDR in males increased by 0.5% (95% CI 0.1%, 0.8%) and 0.3% (95% CI 0.1%, 0.5%) annually, respectively. These findings suggest a gender-based disparity in the trends of ASMR and ASDR over the past three decades.

The age, period, and cohort effect on the temporal trends in the burden of IS attributable to high LDL-C in China by gender

Figure 4A and B show the change trend for mortality rate of IS attributable to high LDL-C by age during the observation period. It indicated a significant increase in the mortality rate of IS attributable to high LDL-C with age in females within each period, showing an exponential trend. A similar pattern was observed in men, except for a decline in men aged 90–94 years and older. The DALY rates of IS attributable to high LDL-C increased with age in women, leveling off after the age group of 85–89 (Supplementary Figure S2A and B). In men, DALY rates grow steadily before the age group of 70–74, accelerate from 70 to 74 age group to 90–94 age group, and then decrease.

Figure 4C and D, supplementary Figure S2C and D present the effect of period effects on the mortality and DALY rate of IS attributable to high LDL-C. Both men and women exhibited distinct types of changes across various age groups over time. For both men and women, the mortality and DALY rate caused by IS due to high LDL-C among those aged 75–79 years and younger basically stabilized or slightly decreased over time. While the mortality and DALY rate of IS attributable to high LDL-C exhibited a first increasing and then a decreasing trend over time in women aged 80–84 years and older, reaching a peak during the period of 2000–2004. For men aged 85 to 94 years, the mortality and DALY rate showed no sign of decline until it reached its highest level in 2014. The mortality and DALY rate of men aged 80–84 years showed a linear upward trend, while those aged over 95 years showed a wavy upward trend with the passage of time.

Figure 4E and F, supplementary Figure S2E and F illustrate the variations in the mortality and DALY rate of IS attributable to high LDL-C by birth cohort. Both men and women aged under 79 years old were less affected by birth cohort factors in terms of the mortality and DALY rate. For women aged 80–84 years and older, the mortality and DALY rate showed an upward trend followed by a downward trend across birth cohorts. In addition, the mortality and DALY rate of IS attributable to high LDL in men aged groups over 85–94 years also increased initially and decreased afterwards along with birth cohorts. The mortality and DALY rate of men aged 80–84 years showed a linear upward trend, while those aged over 95 years showed a wavy upward trend with advancing year of birth.

Age-period-cohort model analysis for the burden of IS attributable to high LDL-C in China by gender

Figure 5 and Supplementary Table S1 present estimates of age, period, and cohort effects on mortality and DALYs rates of IS attributable to the high LDL-C for both sexes. The relative risk of IS mortality due to high LDL-C increased with age in both women(25 to 95 + years) and men (25 to 94 years) (Fig. 5A and Supplementary Table S1). The relative risk of IS mortality attributed to high LDL-C increased from 0.07 (95% CI 0.07, 0.08) in the group aged 25–29 to 16.53 (95% CI 16.06, 17.01) in the group aged 95 years and older for women, and from 0.04 (95% CI 0.04, 0.05) in the group aged 25–29 to 12.63 (95% CI 12.25, 13.03) in the group aged 90–94 for men. The relative risk of mortality exceeded 1 in women after the 60–64 age group and in men after the 54–59 age group, with an exponential increase after age 74. Notably, women aged 95 + years had a higher relative risk of mortality than men. For DALY rates, the age effect followed an S-shaped pattern in both sexes (Fig. 5B and Supplementary Table S1). The relative risk for DALY rates increased until age 60–64 age group, plateaued in age group 60–74, the rose exponentially in age group 75–94 before declining in age group over 95. The largest relative risk for DALY rates were found in age group 90–94 years(3.98 for females, 4.57 for males). The relative risk for DALY rates exceeded 1 after age 55–59 group in men and 60–64 in women.

The period effect presented a linear increase in mortality and DALY rates of for both sexes from 1990 to 2019 (Fig. 5C and D and Supplementary Table S1). Specifically, period mortality relative risk rose sharply in males (0.66 to 1.53) but more gradually in females (0.78 to 1.21). Similarly, period DALYs rates RRs increased significantly in males (0.75 to 1.36), but modestly in females (0.85 to 1.17). Moreover, the upward trends were more significant among women than men before the year 2005, reversing afterwards. The 2000–2019 year groups were risk groups with an RR > 1 in mortality and DALYs rates in females, whereas for males, the inflexion point was delayed for five years.

The birth cohort effect revealed the IS mortality attributable to the high LDL-C continuously decreased in those born from 1895 on for both genders, as well as the DALYs rates (Fig. 5E and F and Supplementary Table S1). Furthermore, females born from 1895 to 1954 experienced a higher mortality and DALYs rates compared with other birth cohorts. While for males the 1895–1944 birth cohort groups and 1895–1949 birth cohort groups were the risk groups with an RR > 1 in mortality and DALYs rates, respectively. The RRs for males were higher than for females in earlier cohorts (before 1920 to 1924 in mortality and before1930 to 1934 in DALYs rates, respectively), but became lower in cohorts after 1920–1924 for mortality and after 1930–1934 for DALYs, continuing until the 1975–1994 cohorts.

Discussion

Stroke is the leading cause of death and disability among adults in China, with the highest number of patients worldwide [3]. IS, the most prevalent type, continues to rise in incidence in China [6, 24]. Hyperlipidemia, especially high levels of LDL-C, is a well-established risk factor for IS and cardiovascular diseases [1, 5, 25–28]. Elevated LDL-C lead to its accumulation in the subendothelial extracellular space of arterial wall, where it undergoes oxidation, forming oxidized LDL-C known as “ox-LDL-C” [25, 29]. This process causes significant damage to arterial walls, promoting the formation and growth of plaques that narrow the arteries and restrict blood flow. When these plaques rupture, they can lead to the formation of blood clots, potentially causing conditions such as myocardial infarction, ischemic heart disease, IS, and other cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases. This study provides a comprehensive estimate of the long-term trend in the burden of IS attributable to high LDL-C by sex, using Joinpoint regression combined with age-period-cohort model analysis in China from 1990 to 2019.

Over the past 30 years, both males and females in China have experienced significant increases in the numbers and crude rates of IS mortality and DALY attributed to high LDL-C, highlighting an increasing public health challenge. This increase has been more pronounced in males since 2004. Despite these increases in absolute numbers, we observed minimal changes in ASMR and ASDR for both genders, suggesting that population growth and aging are the primary drivers. According to China’s seventh national census in 2020, the total population increased from 1.16 billion in 1990 to 1.41 billion in 2020, with the population aged 65 and above rising from 54 million to 191 million [30]. In addition, life expectancy for Chinese people has increased from 68.6 years in 1990 to 77.3 years in 2019 [31]. Future studies using decomposition analysis are necessary to clarify the impact of population growth, population aging, and epidemiologic changes (i.e., age-specific rates of mortality or DALY) on the burden of IS attributable to high LDL-C. The stability or slight decrease in ASMR and ASDR may be attributed to improvements in public awareness of IS, greater utilization of emergency medical services, advancements in medical treatment, and increased efforts to prevent IS risk factors.

In a gender-specific analysis of IS caused by high LDL-C in China from 1990 to 2019, both the ASMR and ASDR were higher in males than in females, suggesting that high LDL-C had a greater impact on the IS burden in males. However, the age-standardized incidence and prevalence rates of IS in China, as well as the global summary exposure value rate attributed to high LDL-C, were found to be higher in females [3, 32]. Despite this, the ASMR and ASDR of IS and attributed to high LDL-C were higher in Chinese men [24, 33]. In 2019, the age-standardized mortality rate of IS in China and global age-standardized death attributable to high LDL-C among males were 1.73 and 1.36 times greater than those in females, respectively [3, 17]. These findings may partially explain sex disparities in burden of IS attributable to high LDL-C in China. Biological factors may also contribute, as hormones, particularly estrogen in women, have been shown to exert a protective effect against IS by dilating blood vessels, improving blood flow and induction of anti-inflammatory factors thus reducing the risk of atherosclerosis [34, 35]. Furthermore, males in China are more prone to unhealthy behaviors like smoking, heavy alcohol drinking, and excessive red meat intake. These habits are linked to metabolic disorders, such as hyperlipidemia, overweight, and obesity, which increase the risk of IS and higher fatality rates [36]. Therefore, males should focus on improving their health behaviors to reduce IS and high LDL-C risk.

Age-specific mortality and DALY rates from IS due to high LDL-C showed no significant difference between men and women below aged 70 group. However, in those age group over 70 years, this rates were significantly higher in men. Nevertheless, age-adjusted and age-specific mortality and DALY rates obscure that elderly women in China experience more deaths and DALYs from IS caused by high LDL-C than men. This may be attributed to women’s longer life expectancy, leading to a higher proportion of elderly women in the population, and consequently, more deaths and DALYs of IS caused by high LDL-C for elderly women in China [30, 37]. The 7th National Population Census of China released in 2021 showed that, in age groups of 65 years and above, 52.3% are men and 47.7% are women; for those 75 years and above, 47.5% are men and 52.5% are women; for those 85 years and above, 44.33% are men and 55.67% are women [30]. Another explanations could be age-dependent sex difference in LDL-C levels, where men have higher LDL-C and total cholesterol levels than females during youth, but this difference diminishes and even reverses after age 50 [38]. Thus, prevention and treatment strategies for IS, as well as strategies for controlling LDL-C should consider age and gender-specific risk factors.

In 2019, the highest number of deaths from IS due to high LDL-C occurred in the 80–84 age group, while DALYs showed a bimodal distribution, peaking at 60–64 and 80–84 age groups for both gender, consistent with the global data on the number of deaths and DALYs attributable to high LDL-C [17]. Interestingly, males had a DALYs peak at 60–64 age group, while females peaked at 80–84 age group. Generally, the mortality and DALY rates of IS attributed to high LDL-C increase with age, primary due to aging being a key risk factor and age-associated changes in lipoprotein metabolism. As individuals age, the energy and calories from fat gradually decline. Simultaneously, aging can lead to a decrease in the removal efficiency of lipoprotein plasma, which contributes to an increase in LDL-C levels [39]. A large Chinese study, involving 46,239 adults aged ≥ 20 years, showed that LDL-C levels continue to increase with age in both genders [38]. Furthermore, older people often experience reduction in physical activity, weight gain, and comorbid diseases, such as diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease, and cardiovascular disease, which can further lead to abnormal lipid metabolism.

The Joinpoint regression analysis revealed that while the ASMR and ASDR trend in the general population remained stable over time, significant differences were observed between females and males. In women, the burden of IS attributable to high LDL-C increased significantly between 1997 and 2004 but has consistently decreased since then. Nonetheless, men experienced a temporary decrease from 2004 to 2007, followed by a significant rise from 2007 to 2012, and a subsequent decline from 2012 to 2019. This pattern aligns with trends in stroke mortality attributable to high red meat intake in China in both genders [40]. The changing trends and differences between men and women are likely driven by multiple factors. First, risk factor for stroke, such as overweight and obesity, hypertension, diabetes, and unhealthy lifestyle, have become more common in China in recent years [33] and may account for the increased burden of IS attributable to high LDL-C. In China, the traditional gender roles, with males as primary earners, have been challenged by rapid industrialization and urbanization since 2005, leading to stressful work conditions that promote unhealth behaviors. Notably, two-thirds of males are smokers and over a third consume alcohol weekly [41, 42], resulting in significant gender disparities in health outcome. Furthermore, improvement in stroke care, such as better public education, healthcare systems, access to acute care, and preventive measures, have contributed to the decreasing trends [43]. Clopidogrel, a P2Y12 inhibitor approved in China in 2001, significantly enhanced stroke patient survival and reduced recurrence risks. The subsequent introduction of novel oral anticoagulants further lowered stroke incidence related to atrial fibrillation. Advances in LDL-C-lowing therapies and diagnostic techniques have also contributed to this decreasing trend. Moreover, neuroimaging methods (such as computed tomography and magnetic resonance image scanning) are now commonly accessible even in county hospitals in China, facilitating early stroke diagnosis and timely intervention. In addition, beauty standards, particularly for women, are heavily shaped by the fashion in and influenced by mass and social media, where a slenderer figure is becoming increasingly important. The “thinner is better” seems to be a pervasive notion in the college students, and the majority of females students (79.59%) has body image concerns and appearance-related worries [44]. Additionally, with the increase in quality of life, females have time and resources to invest in their physical appearance, such as go to fitness room and square dancing, which are the good way to loss fat.

The period effect on mortality and DALY rates of IS attributable to high LDL-C has increased over time, despite improvements in healthcare accessibility and technology in China over the past three decades. Factors such as the shift in demographic structure (e.g. aging) and some new health threats (e.g. unhealthy lifestyle and air pollution) likely contribute to this period effect. By 2019, China had 164.5 million people aged over 65 years, a number expected to rise to 365 million by 2050 [45]. Moreover, dietary patterns have worsened in China, with increased consumption of refined rice and wheat, red and processed meat, added sugar, and fried foods are becoming more affordable and accessible and frying, contributing to poor health outcome [46]. In addition, with the development of modern science and technology, more sedentary occupations and diverse internet entertainment and personal motorized transportation lead to insufficient physical activity in populations [47]. Notably, ambient particulate matter pollution exposure is more harmful to IS, especially males and elderly [48]. Furthermore, with the popularization of medical examinations in China in recent years, the increased number of IS cases were screened at the early stage, which could be partly explain the increasing burden of IS attributable to high LDL-C.

Inspiring, the birth cohort effect on mortality and DALYs rates of IS attributable to high LDL-C indicated a downward trend, which suggested the earlier birth cohorts faced higher burden of IS attributable to high LDL-C compared to the recent ones. A better education and higher health awareness of IS-related knowledge is likely to be a partial reason for deceasing burden of IS attributable to high LDL-C in the younger generations in China. It is worth noting that the relative risk of the cohort effect in mortality and DALYs rates of IS attributable to high LDL-C was less than 1 after 1949, in which the People’s Republic of China was founded. This phenomenon was mainly due to the unstable and lagged socioeconomic development, and inadequate health care systems and poor nutrition intake before 1949. Previous studies suggested that maternal and early-child undernutrition was positive association with the stroke mortality [49, 50]. Nutrition restriction in uterus may lead to low birth weight, resulting in relatively high levels of triglycerides and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol and an increased risk of IS in adulthood. So, improvement of early-life nutrition conditions may play a vital role in the declining effect in the younger generations.

Our study summarizes the IS burden attributed to high LDL-C in China, filling the gap in understanding long-term trends in IS deaths and DALYs attributed to high LDL-C by sex and age. However, several limitations exist. First, despite the GBD data being derived from numerous sources with adjustment and correction methods to improve accuracy, potential biases remain, requiring validation through large-scale epidemiological surveys. Second, the lack of TOAST (Trial of Org 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment, TOAST) and CISS (Chinese ischemic stroke subclassification, CISS) classification of IS in GBD 2019 make it difficult to analysis the potential causes in detail and to establish the severity of IS among study populations. Third, in patients with multiple comorbidities, IS-related deaths may be misclassified due to challenges in distinguishing mortality from IS or its comorbidities. Fourth, China’s vast geography and widening economic disparities may lead to underestimation of mortality and disease burden of IS attributed to high LDL-C in underdeveloped and remote areas due to inadequate health data. Fifth, the GBD data were not grouped by province or urban-rural areas, limiting regional comparisons. Lastly, the mortality data of IS attributed to high LDL-C for those under 25 were excluded due to limited availability those data in the GBD database.

In conclusion

The IS burden due to high LDL-C still increased from 1990 to 2019 in China. Although the ASMR and ASDR of IS attributed to high LDL-C remained stable, the absolute number of deaths and DALYs were still increasing. A gender difference in high LDL-C caused IS was observed. In general, males and senior individuals were the high-risk groups for IS mortality attributed to high LDL-C. According to Joinpoint analysis, the overall trend of the males’ ASMR and ASDR of IS due to high LDL-C is upward, while the females’ ASMR and ASDR decreased. For the independent period and cohort effects, an increasing trend was observed with time period in both genders from 1990 to 2019, whereas people born in earlier birth cohorts had higher risk of IS burden than the recent ones. The growing burden of IS due to high LDL-C in China urgently requires effective and tailored strategies for IS primary prevention and management of IS and high LDL-C, especially for males and the elderly populations.

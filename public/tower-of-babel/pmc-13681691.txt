A randomized, placebo-controlled, phase II study of docetaxel and platinum with or without erlotinib in recurrent/metastatic head and neck squamous-cell carcinomas
Authors: W.N. William, L. Feng, M.S. Kies, S. Ahmed, G.R. Blumenschein, B.S. Glisson, E. Massarelli, F.M. Johnson, C. Lu, X. Le, L.P. Kowalski, E.S. Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeWe conducted a randomized phase II trial to evaluate whether adding erlotinib to platinum/docetaxel improves clinical outcomes in the first-line treatment of recurrent or metastatic head and neck squamous-cell carcinomas, building on our previous single-arm phase II trial.Patients and methodsThis was a placebo-controlled, double-blind, single-center phase II study. Patients (N = 120) were randomly assigned (1 : 1) to receive platinum (carboplatin area under the curve 6 or cisplatin 75 mg/m2 every 21 days for six cycles) plus docetaxel (75 mg/m2 every 21 days for six cycles) with either erlotinib (150 mg/day orally until progression) or placebo. The primary endpoint was progression-free survival (PFS). Secondary endpoints included overall survival, response rates, duration of response, and correlations of erlotinib-induced rash/biomarkers with outcomes.ResultsMedian PFS was 6.1 months with erlotinib versus 4.4 months with placebo [hazard ratio (HR) 0.63, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.42-0.95, P = 0.026]. Median overall survival was 17.0 months in the erlotinib arm compared with 13.7 months in the control arm (HR 0.67, 95% CI 0.43-1.04, P = 0.0718), meeting the prespecified type I error threshold. Subgroup analyses demonstrated numerically favorable PFS HRs across most clinical subgroups, with a more pronounced benefit observed in patients with oropharyngeal and human papillomavirus-related tumors. Development of grade ≥2 rash during the first cycle of erlotinib was associated with significantly improved overall survival (HR 0.40, 95% CI 0.19-0.87, P = 0.021). The addition of erlotinib increased the incidence of grade 2-4 adverse events, particularly gastrointestinal toxicity, rash, fatigue, and dehydration but no unexpected safety signals were observed.ConclusionsThe study met its primary endpoint, supporting the continued relevance of epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) inhibition in this disease. Although contemporary immunotherapy-based standards limit direct adoption of this regimen, these results provide clinically meaningful insight into EGFR biology and support further development of modernized, tyrosine kinase inhibitor-based EGFR strategies, including rational combinations and biomarker-driven approaches, to improve outcomes.

Introduction

Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) is an established therapeutic target in recurrent and metastatic head and neck squamous-cell carcinoma (HNSCC), based on studies of cetuximab as a single agent in platinum-refractory disease,1 followed by the pivotal EXTREME and TPEx trials, which demonstrated that adding cetuximab to platinum-based chemotherapy improved response rates, progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival.2,3 These results established EGFR antibody-based therapy as a first-line standard.

In parallel, EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) were investigated as an alternative strategy for EGFR inhibition. Early studies of gefitinib and erlotinib showed modest single-agent activity,4, 5, 6 and subsequent randomized trials of gefitinib in previously treated populations failed to demonstrate meaningful survival benefit.7 Phase III trials of afatinib later demonstrated improved PFS compared with methotrexate in previously treated patients;8 however, the clinical benefit was limited, curtailing broader adoption of EGFR TKIs as monotherapy.

In contrast, combination strategies integrating EGFR TKIs with cytotoxic chemotherapy appeared more promising. A study combining gefitinib with platinum and docetaxel reported improved response rates.9 Similarly, our single-arm phase II study of cisplatin, docetaxel, and erlotinib demonstrated safety and favorable efficacy compared with historical controls—54% response rate (primary endpoint of the study), median PFS and overall survival of 6.1 and 11.0 months, respectively.10 The different mechanisms of action of TKIs (primarily affecting direct intracellular signaling) versus antibodies (primarily affecting extracellular ligand binding to the receptor triggering downstream antineoplastic effects) may explain distinct interactions with chemotherapy and clinical activity.

Based on these single-arm trials, we designed the present randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase II study to investigate the primary hypothesis that the addition of erlotinib to first-line platinum and docetaxel would improve PFS compared with chemotherapy alone. At the time of study design, although cetuximab plus chemotherapy was gaining acceptance, institutional practice favored a sequential approach to EGFR inhibition, reflecting evidence that the benefits of cetuximab in combination with chemotherapy were largely additive rather than synergistic,11 and justifying the choice of platinum and docetaxel as a backbone. Subsequently, and only after this study was completed, the results of Keynote-048 became available, supporting integration of pembrolizumab into the first-line treatment for the majority of patients with advanced HNSCC,12 prompting a modified contextualization of the data reported herein.

We present the final results of our trial demonstrating that erlotinib plus chemotherapy improved PFS and overall survival compared with chemotherapy alone. The significance of this work may extend beyond the standalone efficacy results. We provide a discussion placing these findings in the context of contemporary treatment paradigms that now include immunotherapy, global oncology considerations for financially constrained regions, and emerging EGFR-targeted strategies with next-generation agents. Specifically, we explore how proof of principle for EGFR-TKI–based combinations, together with clinical and correlative insights from this trial, may inform future therapeutic development, including affordable treatment approaches and novel combination strategies in an era of renewed EGFR innovation.

Methods

This was a randomized, placebo-controlled, single-center, double-blinded, phase II study of docetaxel and platinum with or without erlotinib in patients with metastatic or recurrent HNSCC conducted at The University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center. The study was registered in ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT01064479) and approved by the Institutional Review Board before activation (MD Anderson Cancer Center–approval 2009-0395).

Patients with histologically confirmed metastatic or recurrent HNSCCs of the oral cavity, oropharynx [regardless of human papillomavirus (HPV) status], hypopharynx, or larynx who had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0-2, measurable disease, and no prior chemotherapy for their metastatic or recurrent disease were enrolled. A complete list of eligibility criteria can be found in the protocol (Supplementary Material, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108584). Of note, patients with recurrence within or after 6 months of completion of definitive platinum-based treatment for locally advanced disease could be included.

Eligible patients were stratified and randomly assigned (1 : 1) to receive chemotherapy plus erlotinib or chemotherapy plus placebo (Supplementary Methods, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108584). In both arms, six 21-day cycles of chemotherapy were planned and at least four cycles of chemotherapy were strongly recommended. This design allowed for greater flexibility to discontinue chemotherapy in case of clinically meaningful mild toxicities and/or patient/physician preference and is in line with lung cancer studies showing a lack of benefit beyond three to four cycles of platinum-based chemotherapy.13 After discontinuation of chemotherapy, patients with a response or stable disease continued to receive daily erlotinib or daily placebo. Treatment was given until objective disease progression or intolerable toxicity. Chemotherapy consisted of docetaxel 75 mg/m2 i.v. on day 1 and cisplatin 75 mg/m2 i.v. on day 1 or carboplatin area under the curve of 6 i.v. on day 1 (physician’s choice). Erlotinib 150 mg or placebo was given PO daily throughout the treatment period. Prophylactic granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) support during chemotherapy was strongly recommended (Supplementary Material, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108584), based on our single-arm, phase II data showing risk of febrile neutropenia with this regimen.10 Imaging assessments for tumor response were carried out after every two cycles, in addition to other clinical and laboratory assessments at regular intervals, as described in the protocol (Supplementary Material, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108584).

The primary endpoint of the study was PFS. Secondary endpoints included overall survival, response rates, duration of response, and correlations of erlotinib-induced rash and biomarkers with outcomes. All randomly assigned patients were included in the efficacy analysis according to the intent-to-treat principle. All patients who received at least one study treatment were included in the safety analysis. Response was defined according to RECIST version 1.1.14 Adverse events were graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), v4.0.

A sample size of 120 patients was planned to detect a 1.9-month conservative improvement in the median PFS (from 3.015 to 4.910 months) in the erlotinib-containing arm over the placebo arm. The corresponding hazard ratio (HR) was 0.612, based on the assumption of an exponential distribution of the time to event. Assuming a two-sided type I error rate of 0.10, accrual rate of 4 patients/month, and an additional 4 months of follow-up after the last patient was randomly assigned, a trial with 60 patients in each arm would have 80% power to detect a 1.9-month improvement in the median PFS, assuming 108 events (progression or death) at the time of analysis. One interim analysis was planned and carried out (Supplementary Methods, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108584). This design was selected to strike a balance between enrolling a reasonable sample size and having adequate power to detect a realistic (yet modest) PFS improvement but even greater power to detect a larger treatment effect (in case the true PFS were 6.1 months as achieved in the previous single-arm phase II).10

The Kaplan–Meier method was used to summarize overall survival and PFS. Analyses were stratified by recurrence-free interval (de novo metastatic disease/recurrence ≥6 months after definitive therapy versus recurrence <6 months after definitive therapy), prior EGFR inhibitor treatment, and cigarette smoking status (current versus former versus never) and compared using the log-rank test. HRs for overall survival and PFS are presented using point estimates and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). A two-sided alpha (i.e. significance level) of 0.10 was used to compare treatment arms, following the approach proposed by Korn et al.16 and Simon et al.17 Univariate Cox proportional hazards models were fitted to evaluate the effects of continuous variables on time-to-event outcomes. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards models were used for multivariable analysis to include important and significant covariates. All P values presented are two-sided. Statistical software SAS 9.3 (SAS, Cary, North Carolina) and S-Plus 8.2 (TIBCO Software Inc, Palo Alto, California) were used.

Results

Patient characteristics

Over 62 months (from May 2010 until July 2015), 123 patients were screened, 120 patients were enrolled, and 60 patients were randomly assigned to each arm. One patient (randomly assigned to the placebo arm) refused to initiate treatment and withdrew consent for the study (including data analysis). All remaining 119 patients initiated treatment and were eligible for analysis of the primary endpoint according to the intent-to-treat principle (Figure 1). The median follow-up time for overall survival for the censored observations was 25.4 months (range 4.3 to 63.9 months). Treatment characteristics

Cisplatin was selected as the initial platinum of choice for 30 patients (50%) in the placebo arm and 38 patients (63%) in the erlotinib arm. Carboplatin was later substituted for cisplatin in four and seven patients in the placebo and erlotinib arms, respectively. Additional details of treatment disposition are described in Figure 1 and Supplementary Results (available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108584).

Efficacy

There was a statistically significant improvement in PFS in the erlotinib-treated patients (HR 0.63, 95% CI 0.42-0.95, P = 0.0262). The median and 2-year PFS were 4.4 months (95% CI 3.5-5.8 months) and 4% (95% CI 1% to 14%) for the placebo group, and 6.1 months (95% CI 5.5-7.5 months) and 11% (95% CI 6% to 23%) for the erlotinib group (Figure 2A). Response rates for the placebo arm were 41% (9% CR and 32% PR) versus 52% for the erlotinib arm (7% CR and 45% PR), with no statistically significant differences between the two groups (P = 0.6118) (Supplementary Table S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108584). The median duration of response (95% CI) was 5.45 (3.25-6.9) months for placebo and 4.76 (3.25-8.71) months for erlotinib (P = 0.3547).Figure 2Progression-free survival (A) and overall survival (B). There was a statistically significant improvement in overall survival in favor of the erlotinib arm (HR 0.67, 95% CI 0.43-1.04, P = 0.0718). This met this phase II study’s statistical threshold for significance (type I error rate of 0.1—two-sided alpha) but the P value was above the conventional threshold of 0.05 commonly used in phase III trials. The median and 2-year overall survival was 13.7 months (95% CI 10.6-18.8 months) and 21% (95% CI 13% to 34%) for the placebo group, and 17.0 months (95% CI 10.1 months-not reached) and 39% (95% CI 28% to 55%) for the erlotinib group (Figure 2B).

Subgroup analyses

We carried out subgroup analyses for PFS for the variables of interest. The per-protocol prespecified subgroup analyses included the three stratification factors: recurrence-free interval from primary treatment (Supplementary Figure S1A and B, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108584), prior EGFR inhibitor use (Supplementary Figure S1C and D, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108584), and smoking status (Supplementary Figure S1E and F, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108584). Similar analyses for overall survival are provided in Supplementary Figure S2 (available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108584).

As prespecified in the protocol, we evaluated the association between treatment-related rash and PFS and overall survival. To mitigate guaranteed time bias, we conducted a landmark analysis using the end of cycle 1 as the landmark time point. Patients were classified according to the occurrence of grade 2 or higher rash during the first treatment cycle (rash versus no rash). Time-to-event endpoints were then calculated from the landmark time forward, including only patients who were alive and had PFS at that time. Within the erlotinib arm, the PFS HR was 0.68 (95% CI 0.38-1.25, P = 0.2104, log-rank) in favor of patients who developed a rash. The HR for overall survival in favor of patients who developed a rash was 0.40 (95% CI 0.19-0.87, P = 0.0173, log-rank) (Figure 3).Figure 3Landmark analysis of outcomes according to grade 2 or higher rash at cycle 1. As part of the exploratory biomarker analysis, we studied the associations between HPV status and outcomes. The HR for PFS in the erlotinib versus placebo arms within HPV-negative patients was 0.62 (95% CI 0.18-2.07), whereas the HR within the HPV-positive patients was 0.33 (95% CI 0.15-0.72) (Supplementary Figure S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108584). The HR for overall survival in the erlotinib versus placebo arms within HPV-negative patients was 0.92 (95% CI 0.56-1.50), whereas the HR within the HPV-positive patients was 0.27 (95% CI 0.1-0.78) (Supplementary Figure S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108584).

In an attempt to identify predictive factors related to differential benefit from erlotinib versus placebo, we carried out exploratory subgroup analysis of prespecified and not prespecified subgroups of interest. Results are summarized in the Forest plots for PFS (Supplementary Figure S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108584) and overall survival (Supplementary Figure S5, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108584). In general, sample sizes were small for these subgroups. P values provided for interaction tests are nominal. For PFS, there appeared to be a greater benefit from erlotinib in patients with an oropharyngeal primary cancer (interaction P value 0.0109), likely driven by HPV-positive status (interaction P value 0.0295). A similar pattern was observed for overall survival. In most of the other subgroups, the HR for PFS and overall survival were in line with the general study population.

Discussion

This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase II study met its primary endpoint. The addition of erlotinib to first-line platinum and docetaxel significantly improved PFS and overall survival, under the prespecified statistical assumptions (10% two-sided type I error rate), although the P value for overall survival was not <0.05. Exploratory analyses suggested that benefits were more pronounced in patients with oropharyngeal primaries, particularly those with HPV-associated disease, and in those who developed an erlotinib-induced rash during the first cycle. Together, these findings demonstrated clinically meaningful activity of erlotinib when combined with cytotoxic chemotherapy.

These results closely replicated the efficacy observed in our prior single-arm phase II study using the same regimen, with an identical median PFS of 6.1 months and a numerically superior median overall survival in the current randomized study (17 versus 11 months in the prior trial). The consistency across studies conducted in different settings and designs reinforces the biological and clinical plausibility of this combination. However, because both trials were conducted in a single institution, generalizability of the results is yet to be demonstrated. When contextualized against historical phase III benchmarks, including the EXTREME trial, the observed outcomes compared favorably in terms of both PFS and overall survival (median and HR for PFS and overall survival in EXTREME’s experimental arm of 5.6 months and 0.54, and 10.1 months and 0.8, respectively).2 This may reinforce the clinical significance of our findings, despite a small absolute improvement in median PFS with erlotinib. Furthermore, the toxicity profile not only observed here (grade 3-4 adverse events incidence of 55%) requires adequate support but also compared favorably with what has been reported for the similar regimen of cisplatin, docetaxel, and cetuximab in the TPEx trial (81%).3 Unfortunately, our study is limited by the lack of availability of quality of life/patient-reported outcomes data and we caution that when considering this and other EGFR-based regimens, the increased toxicity observed herein needs to be balanced against the magnitude of benefit.

This study confirms our assumption that combination with cytotoxics may be required to realize the full therapeutic potential of EGFR TKIs in HNSCCs. Similarly, erlotinib has been combined with metronomic methotrexate and celecoxib, yielding impressive results with low toxicity in poor prognostic patients with HNSCC,18,19 with retrospective analysis showing a pivotal role for the EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor in this combination.20 More recently, a randomized phase III study adding metronomic erlotinib, methotrexate, and celecoxib to carboplatin and paclitaxel showed an impressive improvement in overall survival compared with carboplatin and paclitaxel alone (HR 0.54).21 The erlotinib-based regimens (conventional or metronomic) offer a valuable and often overlooked advantage in terms of costs (given availability of generic versions),18 compared with EGFR and programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) antibody-based regimens. This is extremely relevant to low- and middle-human development index countries, which bear >70% of the HNSCC burden worldwide,22 and where patients frequently do not have access to advanced therapies. An even more promising approach is the combination of erlotinib-based regimens with low-dose immune checkpoint inhibitors.23 EGFR TKIs are typically not combined with standard-dose immunotherapy due to toxicity concerns.24 However, a randomized study has shown that addition of low-dose nivolumab to metronomic erlotinib, methotrexate, and celecoxib was tolerable and improved survival and could pose a low-cost, effective strategy.23 Moreover, the strategy of combining platinum, taxane, low-dose nivolumab, and triple erlotinib-based metronomic therapy has been evaluated in the neoadjuvant setting and showed high response rates (66%) and a surgical conversion rate of 90% in borderline resectable oral cavity cancers.25 Our results, therefore, reinforce the clinical activity of erlotinib in HNSCC demonstrated elsewhere and may provide a foundation to add low-dose immunotherapy to platinum, docetaxel, and erlotinib as well in future studies, facilitating access to EGFR inhibitor-based and immunotherapy-based first-line combinations in many parts of the world with financial barriers.

The evolving treatment landscape represents the principal limitation of this study. During its conduct, immune checkpoint inhibitors, either as monotherapy or in combination with chemotherapy, became standard first-line therapy for many patients with recurrent or metastatic HNSCC.12 Nonetheless, important gaps remain. Pembrolizumab monotherapy is not indicated for programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1)–negative disease and chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab has shown limited benefit in this subgroup (unfavorable HR of 1.21 for overall survival compared with EXTREME in the pivotal KEYNOTE-048 trial).12 In addition, a subset of patients have contraindications to immunotherapy or are treated in health care systems where access to PD-1 and EGFR-directed antibody-based therapies is restricted. These realities underscore the continued relevance of alternative regimens such as platinum, docetaxel, and erlotinib.

The observation of enhanced benefit in HPV-associated disease deserves particular attention but is limited by the exploratory nature of a subgroup analysis, small sample sizes, and the lack of stratification by HPV status upon randomization. Although molecular profiling of HPV-related HNSCC might predict limited dependence on EGFR signaling,26, 27, 28 clinical data regarding EGFR inhibition in this setting are more nuanced—some studies have shown a lack of benefit from afatinib29 or cetuximab30,31 single agents, no differential benefit from adding cetuximab to chemotherapy in EXTREME,32 and the opposite, more favorable effect of adding panitumumab to chemotherapy in HPV-related HNSCC in the phase III SPECTRUM trial.33 We hypothesize that the context of EGFR inhibition (alone or with chemotherapy), as well as the drug type (antibody or tyrosine kinase inhibitor), may modulate this interaction—a topic of relevance, as emergent bispecific EGFR antibodies may have more34 or less35 signals of activity in HPV-related disease in early trials. A more refined understanding of how EGFR inhibitors interact with chemotherapy and cotargeted pathways in HPV-associated HNSCC may prevent premature dismissal of a strategy that could benefit selected patients, a conclusion supported by the findings of the present study.

A key translational insight is the strong association between erlotinib-induced rash and improved survival, confirmed using a tightly controlled landmark analysis to minimize guarantee-time bias, and not influenced by rash prophylaxis, as this was not recommended per protocol and had not been implemented as an institutional practice. The median overall survival in patients who developed grade ≥2 rash during the first cycle was 23.9 months. For context, this greatly exceeded the median overall survival expected with contemporary pembrolizumab-based therapies in biomarker-selected patients (14.7-14.9 months in PD-L1 combined positive score ≥20).12 This EGFR rash-outcomes relationship has been consistently observed across disease stages and EGFR-directed therapies,36, 37, 38, 39, 40 yet its biological underpinnings remain incompletely understood. It could be related to increased drug exposure due to pharmacokinetic individual variations. However, rash is also an inflammatory/immune phenomenon41 and may reflect immune activation that extends beyond the skin into the tumor microenvironment. Alternatively, it may identify patients with an inherent susceptibility to epithelial immune engagement that is amplified by EGFR blockade. Elucidating the mechanisms linking rash to outcome could yield valuable predictive biomarkers or inform specific immune-modulatory (maybe even antigen-specific) strategies in HNSCC.

Conclusion

This randomized phase II study demonstrated that the addition of erlotinib to first-line platinum and docetaxel improved both PFS and overall survival in patients with recurrent or metastatic HNSCC. Although limited by sample size, as expected for a phase II trial, and conducted during a period of rapid therapeutic evolution, the study provides consistent evidence of clinical activity, biological insight, and global relevance. These findings are particularly timely in the context of renewed interest in EGFR-targeted approaches, including the development of bispecific antibodies, antibody–drug conjugates, and immunotherapy combinations.42 Rather than supporting a conventional phase III replication of this exact regimen, these results are better viewed as informing a strategic modernization of the EGFR–TKI backbone. Such an approach could include integration of rational and affordable immunotherapy dosing, biomarker-driven patient selection, and innovative combination strategies. For example, recent clinical data demonstrating enhanced efficacy with the addition of erlotinib to cetuximab and chemotherapy43 support the theoretical concept of vertical (and possibly horizontal) EGFR axis blockade with erlotinib plus novel bispecific EGFR antibodies. Moreover, preclinical evidence indicates that HER-family TKIs can upregulate receptor trafficking and endocytosis, potentially enhancing intracellular payload delivery when combined with antibody–drug conjugates,44,45 and effectively positioning EGFR TKIs as both signaling inhibitors with direct antitumor activity (as supported by our trial) and sensitizers. With these directions, we anticipate that the clinical and biological knowledge stemming from this study may have potential to translate into more accessible, more precise, and ultimately more effective treatment strategies for patients with head and neck cancer.

Estimation of the elasticity for CKLS model from high-frequency observations
Boyuan Ning*, Yasutaka Shimizu

Abstract
We investigate parametric estimation of the elasticity parameter in the CKLS diffusion based on high-frequency data. First, we transform the CKLS diffusion to a CIR-type one via a smooth state-space mapping and the general Girsanov change of measure. This transformation enables the applications of existing inference tools for CIR processes while ensuring possibilities of transferring the resulting limit theorems back to the original probability space. However, because Feller’s condition fails, many existing high-frequency likelihood-based procedures cannot be applied directly, since their discretization schemes approximate likelihood terms involving the reciprocal of the process by Riemann sums that are no longer well-defined once the paths are allowed to hit zero. Instead, we estimate the drift coefficient of the transformed CIR-type model via a procedure based on its positive Harris recurrence, which is valid in the high-frequency regime. Exploiting the drift-elasticity relationship implied by the CKLS–CIR transformation, with the help of an initial estimation, we obtain an estimator of the CKLS elasticity from the CIR drift estimator in the transformed model. This yields a closed-form estimator of the elasticity parameter with an explicit asymptotic variance. We establish its p p -consistency, stable convergence in law, and asymptotic normality. Finally, we show that stable convergence in law is invariant under equivalent changes of measure, thereby guaranteeing that the Gaussian limit remains invariant under the original measure.

Introduction

The Chan–Karolyi–Longstaff–Sanders (CKLS) model is introduced by
[
5
]
as a flexible generalization of classical short-rate diffusions and, in particular, as a remedy for some of the restrictive features of the Cox–Ingersoll–Ross (CIR) specification, which has long served as a benchmark for describing short-rates dynamics (e.g., Heston model). It allows volatility of short-rates to respond more flexibly to the level of the rate through an elasticity parameter.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
be a filtered probability space supporting a standard one-dimensional Wiener process
W_{t}
. The CKLS process, denoted by
\lambda_{t}
, is defined as the solution to the SDE (the CKLS model):

d\lambda_{t}=(a-b\lambda_{t})dt+\sigma\lambda_{t}^{k}dW_{t},\quad\lambda_{0}>0,

(1.1)

where
a
denotes the level,
b
the speed of mean-reversion,

the long-term mean,
\sigma>0
denotes the volatility, and

the elasticity. For some of its key properties, see Lemma
A.1
in Appendix
A

However, when

, the CKLS model admits neither a closed-form solution nor an explicit transition density, which creates substantial technical difficulties for both stochastic analysis and statistical inference.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
be a filtered probability space supporting a standard one-dimensional Wiener process
W_{t}
. The Cox–Ingersoll–Ross (CIR) process, denoted by
X_{t}
, is defined as the solution to SDE (the CIR model):

dX_{t}=(\alpha-\beta X_{t})dt+\gamma\sqrt{X_{t}}dW_{t},\quad X_{0}>0,

(1.2)

where
\beta

A.2
in Appendix

.

The square-root specification in the CIR model imposes a fixed elasticity of volatility with respect to the rate level, and is often viewed as too restrictive to accommodate the full empirical variability of short-rate movements. Empirical studies have indicated that short-rate volatility does depend on the level of the rate (heteroskedastic short-rates across samples), so elasticity
k
is of central importance. In comparison, the CKLS model incorporates a state-dependent volatility whose magnitude adjusts systematically with the level of the process, thereby reflecting the empirically observed sensitivity of the conditional variance to the prevailing rate. Capturing this volatility-level relationship is a central element in modeling short-rates, and the estimation of the elasticity parameter becomes a crucial problem in empirical implementation. As an initial attempt, the seminal work
[
5
]
employed GMM to estimate this parameter.

A substantial body of literature investigates parametric inference for CKLS-type diffusions. We only cite some of the most recent results from roughly the past decade.

3
Transformation from the CKLS Process to the CIR Process

This section establishes an analytical transformation from the general CKLS process (
\frac{1}{2}\leq k<1
) to the CIR process of a particular form in which Feller’s condition fails (
2\alpha=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}<\gamma^{2}
). We show that a specific smooth state mapping combined with a general Girsanov change of measure maps the two processes. This construction, which forms the core result of
[
20
]
, provides both a theoretical connection between the two model families and a practical tool for estimation.

3.1
A State-space Mapping on CKLS Process

3.1.1
Designing
\mathcal{T}
to Make the Diffusion into a Square-root Type

Assume there exists a twice differentiable mapping
\mathcal{T}
such that
X_{t}=\mathcal{T}(\lambda_{t})
, where
\lambda_{t}
and
X_{t}
solve (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), respectively. Applying Itô’s lemma to
X_{t}=\mathcal{T}(\lambda_{t})
shows that the diffusion coefficient of the
X_{t}
’s SDE becomes
\frac{d\mathcal{T}(\lambda_{t})}{d\lambda_{t}}\sigma\lambda_{t}^{k}dW_{t}
. To make it a CIR-type diffusion term, this coefficient must match
\gamma\sqrt{\mathcal{T}(\lambda_{t})}
. Hence the mapping
\mathcal{T}
is required to satisfy
\frac{d\mathcal{T}(\lambda_{t})}{d\lambda_{t}}\sigma\lambda_{t}^{k}=\gamma\sqrt{\mathcal{T}(\lambda_{t})}
. With
L:=\gamma/\sigma>0
a positive constant, we obtain the defining ODE for
\mathcal{T}
:
\frac{d\mathcal{T}(x)}{dx}x^{k}=L\sqrt{\mathcal{T}(x)}
. This separable ODE yields (choosing zero integration constant for parsimony)
\mathcal{T}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty)
such that

\mathcal{T}(x\lvert k)=\begin{cases}\frac{L^{2}}{4(1-k)^{2}}x^{2-2k},&\text{if }k\neq 1;\\
\frac{L^{2}}{4}\big(\log x\big)^{2},&\text{if }k=1.\end{cases}

In particular, for the case
k\neq 1
, the mapping and its derivatives and inverse are:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(x\lvert k)

\displaystyle=\frac{L^{2}}{4(1-k)^{2}}x^{2-2k}

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\mathcal{T}(x\lvert k)=\frac{L^{2}}{2(1-k)}x^{1-2k},~\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\mathcal{T}(x\lvert k)

\displaystyle=\frac{L^{2}(1-2k)}{2(1-k)}x^{-2k},~\mathcal{T}^{-1}(y\lvert k)=\Big[\frac{2(1-k)}{L}\sqrt{y}\Big]^{\frac{1}{1-k}}.

3.1.2
Parameter Restriction

For the mapping (
3.1
) to be well defined and for
X_{t}^{(k)}:=\mathcal{T}(\lambda_{t}\lvert k)
to constitute a valid CIR diffusion, the following parameter restriction
\frac{1}{2}\leq k<1
is essential. To briefly explain why, we first assume
x\in\mathbb{R}
and will repeatedly invoke Lemma
A.1
’s (i) in the following.

Case
k=1
:
When deriving expression of
\mathcal{T}
, we used the identity
\sqrt{\mathcal{T}(\lambda_{t}\lvert k)}=\frac{L}{2}\log\lambda_{t}
, which requires
\frac{L}{2}\log\lambda_{t}\geq 0
. However, since
\lambda_{t}\in(0,+\infty)
holds almost surely for
k>\frac{1}{2}
, it is possible that
\lambda_{t}
falls in the range
(0,1)
, causing
\log\lambda_{t}<0
. Hence the identity cannot hold on the event
\{\lambda_{t}\in(0,1)\}
, and we cannot guarantee that
\sqrt{\mathcal{T}(\lambda_{t}\lvert k)}
always remains non-negative. This means that the case
k=1
must be excluded.

Case
k\neq 1
:
When deriving expression of
\mathcal{T}
, we used the identity
\sqrt{\mathcal{T}(\lambda_{t}\lvert k)}=\frac{L}{2(1-k)}\lambda_{t}^{1-k}
, which requires
\frac{L}{2(1-k)}x^{1-k}\geq 0
. As
L>0
is already guaranteed, the sign of
\frac{\lambda_{t}^{1-k}}{1-k}
is of concern:

•

Subcase

:
Since
1-k<0
and
\lambda_{t}>0
, we have
\lambda_{t}^{1-k}>0
almost surely. Thus
\frac{L}{2(1-k)}\lambda_{t}^{1-k}<0
, implying that this case must be excluded.

•

Subcase
0<k<\frac{1}{2}
:
Here
1-k>0
while
\lambda_{t}
ranges over
(-\infty,+\infty)
, so
\lambda_{t}^{1-k}
need not be nonnegative. For instance, if
k=\frac{1}{5}
, then
x\mapsto x^{4/5}
is even function and nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}
; if
k=\frac{2}{5}
, then
x\mapsto x^{3/5}
is odd, positive on

but negative on

. Thus

is not guaranteed to be nonnegative, and this case must be excluded.

The restriction

ensures a well-defined

. Note that the case

with

(Feller’s condition fails) is not excluded as

(remaining nonnegative, though hitting

Dynamics of the Mapped Process under
\mathbb{Q}

3.2.1
Dynamics of

under
\mathbb{P}

Assume

\displaystyle dX_{t}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\mathcal{T}(\lambda_{t}\lvert k)(a-b\lambda_{t})dt+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\lambda_{t}^{2}}\mathcal{T}(\lambda_{t}\lvert k)\sigma^{2}\lambda_{t}^{2k}dt+\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\mathcal{T}(\lambda_{t}\lvert k)\sigma\lambda_{t}^{k}dW_{t}

\displaystyle=\Big[\frac{L^{2}}{2(1-k)}\lambda_{t}^{1-2k}(a-b\lambda_{t})+\frac{1}{2}\frac{L^{2}(1-2k)}{2(1-k)}\lambda_{t}^{-2k}\sigma^{2}\lambda_{t}^{2k}\Big]dt+\sigma L\frac{L}{2(1-k)}\lambda_{t}^{1-k}dW_{t}.

Using
\sqrt{\mathcal{T}(x\lvert k)}=\frac{L}{2(1-k)}x^{1-k}
and

simplifies the expression:

dX_{t}^{(k)}=\Big[\underbrace{\frac{aL^{2}}{2(1-k)}\lambda_{t}^{1-2k}-2b(1-k)X_{t}^{(k)}+\frac{\sigma^{2}L^{2}(1-2k)}{4(1-k)}}_{=:\mu(\lambda_{t},X_{t}^{(k)})}\Big]dt+\underbrace{\sigma L\sqrt{X_{t}^{(k)}}}_{=:\nu(X_{t}^{(k)})}dW_{t}.

(3.2)

The diffusion term already admits the desired CIR form, while the drift is written in terms of both
X_{t}^{(k)}
and
\lambda_{t}
, with
\lambda_{t}
itself an explicit function of
X_{t}^{(k)}
. Thus the drift in fact depends solely on
X_{t}^{(k)}
, so

remains Markovian. However, once

is substituted out, the drift becomes nonlinear in

X_{t}^{(k)}
.

3.2.2
The Measure Change via General Girsanov’s Theorem

The goal is to find an equivalent probability measure
\mathbb{Q}
under which the drift term of
X_{t}^{(k)}
becomes of the form
\alpha-\beta X_{t}^{(k)}
. Compared with (
3.2

4.1.1

For continuous-time inference in the CIR model (with the full path

-\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}dt&\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{3}dt\end{pmatrix}.

By Lemma

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{q}dt\xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{a.s./\mathcal{L}^{1}}m_{q},\quad m_{1}:=\frac{\alpha}{\beta},~m_{2}:=\frac{\alpha(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})}{\beta^{2}},~m_{3}:=\frac{\alpha(\alpha+\gamma^{2})(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})}{\beta^{3}}.

By the central limit theorem for multidimensional martingales, we obtain

\frac{1}{T}[M]_{T}\xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{p}\Gamma=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\alpha}{\beta_{0}}&-\frac{\alpha}{\beta_{0}^{2}}(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})\\
-\frac{\alpha}{\beta_{0}^{2}}(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})&\frac{\alpha}{\beta_{0}^{3}}(\alpha+\gamma^{2})(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})\end{pmatrix}~\Longrightarrow~\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}M_{T}\xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{d}\mathcal{N}(\textbf{0}_{2},\Gamma).

For a precise statement of such CLT (for multidimensional martingales) with a discussion on how to apply it to the estimator
\tilde{\beta}_{n}
, see Theorem

in the next section.

Taking into account the almost-sure convergence of

, that is,

and Slutsky’s theorem, we derive that

converges in law to the normal distribution
\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{22})
with

\displaystyle\Sigma_{22}

\displaystyle=\Big(\gamma\frac{2\beta_{0}^{2}}{\alpha\gamma^{2}}\Big)^{2}\Big(\frac{\alpha}{\beta_{0}},1\Big)\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\alpha}{\beta_{0}}&-\frac{\alpha}{\beta_{0}^{2}}(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})\\
-\frac{\alpha}{\beta_{0}^{2}}(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})&\frac{\alpha}{\beta_{0}^{3}}(\alpha+\gamma^{2})(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})\end{pmatrix}\Big(\frac{\alpha}{\beta_{0}},1\Big)=\frac{2\beta_{0}}{\alpha}(\alpha+\gamma^{2}).

∎

5.1
Verifying Stable Convergence in Law for Estimator

We now investigate whether the convergence, as

Theorem 5.1
.

[
[
11

Let

5.2
Stable Convergence in Law under Changes of Measure

Since we have established the stable convergence in law for the sequence
\{{\hat{\beta}_{n}}\}_{n\geq 1}
to an asymptotically normal limit, in our CKLS–CIR scenario, it is natural to ask how this limit behaves under an equivalent change of measure. This subsection analyzes how a stably convergent sequence with a known limit distribution is affected by an equivalent change of measure.

We first state LeCam’s third lemma, followed by a sufficient condition ensuring invariance of stable convergence under an equivalent measure when the limit is normal—a specialization of the lemma tailored for our scenario.

Lemma 5.3

(LeCam’s Third Lemma)
.

Let
(\Omega_{n},\mathcal{F}_{n})
be measurable spaces,
\{\mathbb{P}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
\{\mathbb{Q}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be contiguous sequences of probability measures on
(\Omega_{n},\mathcal{F}_{n})
with the likelihood ratios
\Lambda_{n}:=d\mathbb{Q}_{n}/d\mathbb{P}_{n}
. Let
Y_{n}
be a sequence of
\mathbb{R}^{q}
-valued statistics defined on
(\Omega_{n},\mathcal{F}_{n})
. Assume that under
\mathbb{P}_{n}
, as
n\to\infty
, we have the joint convergence in law

(Y_{n},\Lambda_{n})\xrightarrow{d}(Y,\Lambda)\quad\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{q}\times[0,+\infty)\quad\text{ with }\mathbb{E}[\Lambda]=1.

Then, for every bounded continuous
g:\mathbb{R}^{q}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}_{n}}[g(Y_{n})]=\mathbb{E}[g(Y)\Lambda]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{q}\times[0,+\infty)}g(y)zd\mu(y,z)
where
\mu
denotes the joint probability measure of
(Y,\Lambda)
. Equivalently speaking, under
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
, the law of
Y_{n}
converges to the
\Lambda
-tilted distribution of
Y
.

Proof.

See e.g.,
[
28
]
[Lemma 3.1], pp.641.
∎

Remark

(Interpretation of LeCam’s third lemma)
.

The lemma explains how the limit distribution of a statistic changes when switching from a reference sequence of measures
\{\mathbb{P}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
to another
\{\mathbb{Q}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
that is locally close to
\{\mathbb{P}_{n}\}
in the sense of likelihood ratios. Under
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
, the limiting law of
Y_{n}
is obtained by exponentially tilting the joint limit
(Y,\Lambda)
under
\mathbb{P}_{n}
with the likelihood ratio
\Lambda
. More precisely, under
\mathbb{P}_{n}
, suppose we know the joint limit
(Y,\Lambda)
of
(Y_{n},\Lambda_{n})
. LeCam’s third lemma asserts that the limit law of
Y_{n}
under
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
is obtained simply by
\Lambda
-tilting this limit; no separate asymptotic analysis under
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
is required.

To apply Lemma
5.3
in our scenario, we take

\Omega_{n}\equiv\Omega
,
\mathcal{F}_{n}\equiv\mathcal{F}_{T}
,

\mathbb{P}_{n}=\mathbb{P}
,
\mathbb{Q}_{n}\equiv\mathbb{Q}
,
so that
\Lambda_{n}\equiv\Lambda_{T}=\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}\big\lvert_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}
. As
n\to\infty
, if
Y_{n}\xrightarrow{d_{st}}Y
with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{T}
, then for any bounded
\mathcal{F}_{T}
-measurable
\zeta
we have
(Y_{n},\zeta)\xrightarrow{d}(Y,\zeta)
under
\mathbb{P}
. Since
\Lambda_{T}
is
\mathcal{F}_{T}
-measurable,

(Y_{n},\Lambda_{T})\xrightarrow{d}(Y,\Lambda_{T})
follows. The next theorem is an immediate corollary of Lemma
5.3
.

.

Remark

(Invariance of mixed normal limits under equivalent measures)
.

For a sequence stably converging to a mixed normal limit, both its consistency and its asymptotic (mixed normal) distribution are invariant under equivalent measures. Since

is a normal variance mixture,
U
and
V
are not necessarily deterministic; they may be stochastic and even admit different asymptotic behaviors under two equivalent measures. The mixed normal limit reduces to an ordinary normal law only when both

and
V
are constants. Moreover, the

and the convergence rate

almost surely:

\hat{k}_{0,n}^{(1)}:=\frac{\log\frac{[\lambda]_{i\Delta}-[\lambda]_{(i-1)\Delta}}{\sigma^{2}\Delta}}{2\log\lambda_{(i-1)\Delta}}\xrightarrow[\Delta\to 0]{a.s.}k_{0},\qquad\hat{k}_{0,n}^{(2)}:=\frac{\log\frac{[\lambda]_{i\Delta}-[\lambda]_{(i-1)\Delta}}{[\lambda]_{j\Delta}-[\lambda]_{(j-1)\Delta}}}{2\log\frac{\lambda_{(i-1)\Delta}}{\lambda_{(j-1)\Delta}}}\xrightarrow[\Delta\to 0]{a.s.}k_{0}.

Remark

(Technical advice on robustification near the boundary
\lambda_{\tau}\approx 1
)
.

Following
[
17
]
(see also
[
13
]
), robust estimation of
k
requires excluding time points
\tau
with
\lambda_{\tau}\approx 1
and pairs
(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
with
\lambda_{\tau}/\lambda_{\tau^{\prime}}\approx 1
. In these cases the logarithmic terms in the denominators of the pointwise estimators become nearly zero, which amplifies numerical noise and produces unrobust estimates. It is therefore recommended to choose time points
\{\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{m}\}
for which
\lambda_{\tau_{s}}
is bounded away from
1
(e.g., choose some threshold
\varepsilon>0
such that
\lvert 1-\lambda_{\tau_{s}}\lvert\geq\varepsilon
), and to use the aggregated statistics:

\tilde{k}_{0,n,m}^{(1)}:=\frac{\sum_{s=1}^{m}\Big\lvert\log\frac{[\lambda]_{\tau_{s}+\Delta}-[\lambda]_{\tau_{s}}}{\sigma^{2}\Delta}\Big\lvert}{2\sum_{s=1}^{m}\big\lvert\log\lambda_{\tau_{s}}\big\lvert},\qquad\tilde{k}_{0,n,m}^{(2)}:=\frac{\sum_{s=1}^{m}\Big\lvert\log\frac{[\lambda]_{\tau_{s}+\Delta}-[\lambda]_{\tau_{s}}}{[\lambda]_{\tau^{\prime}_{s}+\Delta}-[\lambda]_{\tau^{\prime}_{s}}}\Big\lvert}{2\sum_{s=1}^{m}\Big\lvert\log\frac{\lambda_{\tau_{s}}}{\lambda_{\tau^{\prime}_{s}}}\Big\lvert}.

By (

), both
\tilde{k}_{0,n,m}^{(1)}
and
\tilde{k}_{0,n,m}^{(2)}
converge almost surely to
k_{0}
as
\Delta\to 0
. Compared with naive averaging of pointwise estimators, these aggregated estimators remain robust even when some
\lambda_{\tau_{s}}
lie close to
1
, exhibiting better numerical performance in practical applications.

6.2
Convergence Rate of the Initial Estimator

Next, we state the convergence rate of the initial estimator in the high-frequency setting, as this result will be essential for the analysis in the next section.

Theorem 6.3

[Convergence rate of the initial elasticity estimator]

For the estimator defined in Theorem

,
\ell=1,2

\hat{k}_{0,n}^{(\ell)}-k_{0}=O_{p}(\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}).

If

Proof.

Recall the convergence in (
6.1
), we define the convergence error by

r_{\tau}(\Delta):=[\lambda]_{\tau+\Delta}-[\lambda]_{\tau}-\sigma^{2}\lambda_{\tau}^{2k}\Delta=\sigma^{2}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\Delta}\lambda_{s}^{2k}ds-\sigma^{2}\lambda_{\tau}^{2k}\Delta=\sigma^{2}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\Delta}(\lambda_{s}^{2k}-\lambda_{\tau}^{2k})ds.

If we can establish the uniform
1/2
-Hölder continuity in probability of

\lvert\lambda_{s}^{2k}-\lambda_{\tau}^{2k}\lvert=O_{p}(\lvert s-\tau\lvert^{\frac{1}{2}})=O_{p}(\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}})\quad\text{for any $\tau\in[0,T]$, $s\in[\tau,\tau+\Delta]\subset[0,T]$},

r_{\tau}(\Delta)=\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\Delta}O_{p}(\lvert s-\tau\lvert^{\frac{1}{2}})ds=O_{p}(\Delta^{\frac{3}{2}})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad[\lambda]_{\tau+\Delta}-[\lambda]_{\tau}=\sigma^{2}\lambda_{\tau}^{2k}\Delta+O_{p}(\Delta^{\frac{3}{2}}).

This yields

\frac{[\lambda]_{\tau+\Delta}-[\lambda]_{\tau}}{\sigma^{2}\Delta}=\lambda_{\tau}^{2k}+O_{p}(\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}),~\frac{[\lambda]_{\tau+\Delta}-[\lambda]_{\tau}}{[\lambda]_{\tau^{\prime}+\Delta}-[\lambda]_{\tau^{\prime}}}=\frac{\lambda_{\tau}^{2k}\Delta\big(1+O_{p}(\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}})\big)}{\lambda_{\tau^{\prime}}^{2k}\Delta\big(1+O_{p}(\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}})\big)}\overset{\ast}{=}\frac{\lambda_{\tau}^{2k}}{\lambda_{\tau^{\prime}}^{2k}}\Big(1+O_{p}(\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}})\Big),

where

On one hand, for the former equation in (

), we have

\displaystyle\log\frac{[\lambda]_{\tau+\Delta}-[\lambda]_{\tau}}{\sigma^{2}\Delta}=\log\lambda_{\tau}^{2k}+\log\Big(O_{p}(\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}})\Big)=\log\lambda_{\tau}^{2k}+\log\Big(1+\frac{O_{p}(\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}})}{\lambda_{\tau}^{2k}}\Big)=2k\log\lambda_{\tau}+O_{p}(\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}).

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad\hat{k}_{0,n}^{(1)}:=\frac{\log\frac{[\lambda]_{i\Delta}-[\lambda]_{(i-1)\Delta}}{\sigma^{2}\Delta}}{2\log\lambda_{(i-1)\Delta}}=\frac{2k_{0}\log\lambda_{(i-1)\Delta}+O_{p}(\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}})}{2\log\lambda_{(i-1)\Delta}}=k_{0}+O_{p}(\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}).

On the other hand, for the latter equation in (

\displaystyle\log\frac{[\lambda]_{\tau+\Delta}-[\lambda]_{\tau}}{[\lambda]_{\tau^{\prime}+\Delta}-[\lambda]_{\tau^{\prime}}}=\log\frac{\lambda_{\tau}^{2k}}{\lambda_{\tau^{\prime}}^{2k}}+\log\Big(1+O_{p}(\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}})\Big)=2k\log\frac{\lambda_{\tau}}{\lambda_{\tau^{\prime}}}+O_{p}(\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}).

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad\hat{k}_{0,n}^{(2)}:=\frac{\log\frac{[\lambda]_{i\Delta}-[\lambda]_{(i-1)\Delta}}{[\lambda]_{j\Delta}-[\lambda]_{(j-1)\Delta}}}{2\log\frac{\lambda_{(i-1)\Delta}}{\lambda_{(j-1)\Delta}}}=\frac{2k_{0}\log\frac{\lambda_{(i-1)\Delta}}{\lambda_{(j-1)\Delta}}+O_{p}(\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}})}{2\log\frac{\lambda_{(i-1)\Delta}}{\lambda_{(j-1)\Delta}}}=k_{0}+O_{p}(\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}).

For

For a continuous local martingale

Remark

(Upgrade to uniform almost sure convergence rate)
.

The uniform
1/2
-Hölder continuity in
\mathcal{L}^{q}
for some

does not imply the uniform almost sure estimate

. This failure is due to the requirements of the Kolmogorov-Chentsov continuity theorem, which necessitates an exponent

(i.e.,

(specifically,

7.2
First-order Taylor’s Expansion with Lagrange Remainder

With
X_{(i-1)\Delta}^{(k)}=\frac{L^{2}}{4(1-k)^{2}}\lambda_{(i-1)\Delta}^{2-2k}
, regard the estimator
\hat{\beta}_{n}^{(k)}
as a function with respect to elasticity:

\displaystyle f_{n}(k):=\hat{\beta}_{n}^{(k)}=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\frac{n\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{(i-1)\Delta}}{n\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{(i-1)\Delta}^{2}-(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{(i-1)\Delta})^{2}}=:\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\frac{A_{n}(k)}{B_{n}(k)-A_{n}^{2}(k)},

\displaystyle\text{with}~A_{n}(k)=\frac{L^{2}}{4(1-k)^{2}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{(i-1)\Delta}^{2-2k}\text{ and }B_{n}(k)=\frac{L^{4}}{16(1-k)^{4}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{(i-1)\Delta}^{4-4k}.

(7.2)

Applying First-order Taylor’s expansion with Lagrange remainder to

at

yields

\sqrt{n\Delta}(\hat{\beta}_{n}^{(k)}-\hat{\beta}_{n}^{(k_{0})})\equiv\sqrt{n\Delta}\big(f_{n}(k)-f_{n}(k_{0})\big)=\sqrt{n\Delta}f^{\prime}_{n}(k_{0})(k-k_{0})+\sqrt{n\Delta}\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\tilde{k})(k-k_{0})^{2},

Recall that
\hat{k}_{0,n}\xrightarrow{p}k_{0}
as

and that the relevant error terms are

\displaystyle\sqrt{n\Delta}f^{\prime}_{n}(k_{0})(\hat{k}_{0,n}-k_{0})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{n\Delta}O_{p}(1)o_{p}\big((\sqrt{n\Delta})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)=o_{p}(1),

\displaystyle\sqrt{n\Delta}\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\tilde{k})(\hat{k}_{0,n}-k_{0})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{n\Delta}O_{p}(1)o_{p}\big((n\Delta)^{-1}\big)=o_{p}\big((n\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)=o_{p}(1).

Consequently, as

\sqrt{n\Delta}(\hat{\beta}_{n}^{(\hat{k}_{0,n})}-\hat{\beta}_{n}^{(k_{0})})=o_{p}(1).

7.2.1
\hat{\beta}_{n}
as a Function with respect to Elasticity

By the following lemma, we notice that

C_{n}(k):=B_{n}(k)-A_{n}^{2}(k)=O_{p}(1)
, as

. Consequently,
f_{n}(k)=\frac{O_{p}(1)}{O_{p}(1)}=O_{p}(1)
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Assume that the initial value
\lambda_{0}
has the distribution
\pi_{0}
(i.e.,
\lambda_{0}\sim\pi_{0}
). For any
q>0
,

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{(i-1)\Delta}^{q}\xrightarrow[\Delta\to 0]{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{t}^{q}].

(7.3)

Proof.

Define the discretization error
\mathsf{R}_{n}
, and by Tonelli’s theorem (nonnegative integrand after taking absolute values), for
s\in[(i-1)\Delta,i\Delta]
:

\mathsf{R}_{n}:=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{(i-1)\Delta}^{q}\Delta-\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\lambda_{s}^{q}ds\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert\mathsf{R}_{n}\lvert\big]\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{(i-1)\Delta}^{i\Delta}\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert\lambda_{(i-1)\Delta}^{q}-\lambda_{s}^{q}\lvert\big]ds.

(7.4)

Case 1:
By (
6.3
) for
0<q<1
and the pathwise
1/2
-Hölder bound (
6.6
):

\lvert\lambda_{(i-1)\Delta}^{q}-\lambda_{s}^{q}\lvert\leq\lvert\lambda_{(i-1)\Delta}-\lambda_{s}\lvert^{q}~~\Rightarrow~~\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert\lambda_{(i-1)\Delta}^{q}-\lambda_{s}^{q}\lvert\big]\leq\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert\lambda_{(i-1)\Delta}-\lambda_{s}\lvert^{q}\big]\leq C_{q}\lvert(i-1)\Delta-s\lvert^{q/2}\leq C_{q}\Delta^{q/2}.

Case 2:
The proof for a general exponent
q\geq 1
is identical to the argument in
Step 2
in the proof of Theorem
6.3
for the case
f(x)=x^{2k}
. Applying the mean-value theorem to
x\mapsto x^{q}
, together with the moment bounds in Lemma

’s (iii) and the quadratic increment estimate (

), yields,

\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert\lambda_{(i-1)\Delta)}^{q}-\lambda_{s}^{q}\lvert\big]\leq C_{q}^{\prime}\lvert(i-1)\Delta-s\lvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{q}^{\prime}\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Insert the two cases into (
7.4
) yields

\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert\mathsf{R}_{n}\lvert\big]\leq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{(i-1)\Delta}^{i\Delta}C_{q}\Delta^{\frac{q}{2}}ds=C_{q}\Delta^{\frac{q}{2}}\xrightarrow[\Delta\to 0]{}0,&0<q<1;\\
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{(i-1)\Delta}^{i\Delta}C^{\prime}_{q}\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}ds=C^{\prime}_{q}\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\xrightarrow[\Delta\to 0]{}0,&q\geq 1.\end{cases}~\Longrightarrow~\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert\mathsf{R}_{n}\lvert\big]\lesssim\Delta^{\frac{q}{2}\wedge\frac{1}{2}},

whence
\mathsf{R}_{n}\to 0
as

in

and hence in probability.

Next, since
\lambda_{t}
is positive Harris recurrent with the unique invariant law

with

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\lambda_{t}^{q}dt\xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{a.s./\mathcal{L}^{1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{q}p_{\infty}(x)dx\overset{*}{=}\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{t}^{q}].

*

, whereas

convergence hold for any initial distribution.
∎

We can extend this lemma to a theorem covering a broader class of cases, which will be useful later in this section. Hereafter—except when discussing selected sufficient conditions that allow upgrading convergence to

or almost-sure convergence—we will not dwell on these stronger modes of convergence and will focus mainly on verifying convergence in probability.

Theorem 7.2
.

[Riemann-sum approximation for positive Harris recurrent diffusions]

Let
\{Z_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a strictly positive, positive Harris recurrent diffusion on
(0,+\infty)
with invariant distribution
\pi_{0}
. In the high-frequency setting, define
\mathcal{S}_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(Z_{(i-1)\Delta})
. Assume:

(A1)

(uniform Hölder condition)
\exists\delta\in(0,1]
such that
\sup_{\lvert s-\tau\lvert\leq\Delta}\lvert Z_{s}-Z_{\tau}\lvert=O_{p}(\Delta^{\delta})
,
\forall\tau,s\in[0,T]
.

(A2)

(integrability and regularity of
g
)
g\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\pi_{0})
, is locally Lipschitz on
(0,+\infty)
, and has polynomial growth:
\lvert g(x)\lvert\lesssim 1+x^{r}
for some
r>0
.

Then the discretization error
\mathcal{R}_{n}:=\mathcal{S}_{n}-\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}g(Z_{t})dt
satisfies

\mathcal{R}_{n}=O_{p}(\Delta^{\delta})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{S}_{n}\xrightarrow{p}\int gd\pi_{0}<\infty,~\text{as }n\to\infty\quad(\text{i.e., }\mathcal{S}_{n}=O_{p}(1)).

If moreover
\big\{\lvert g(Z_{i\Delta})\lvert^{q}\big\}_{i=0}^{n}
is uniformly integrable (e.g., by the polynomial growth of
g
and bounded
(qr+\varepsilon)
-moments of
Z_{t}
), then
\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert\mathcal{R}_{n}\lvert^{q}\big]\lesssim\Delta^{q\delta}~\text{and}~\mathcal{S}_{n}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}^{q}}\int gd\pi_{0}<\infty
as
n\to\infty
; If moreover (A1) holds almost surely, i.e.,
\sup_{\lvert s-\tau\lvert\leq\Delta}\lvert Z_{s}-Z_{\tau}\lvert=O_{a.s.}(\Delta^{\delta})
for any
\tau,s\in[0,T]
, then
\mathcal{R}_{n}=O_{a.s.}(\Delta^{\delta})~\text{and}~\mathcal{S}_{n}\xrightarrow{a.s.}\int gd\pi_{0}<\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

Remark

(Borel–Cantelli criterion for almost sure convergence)
.

The almost sure convergence of
\mathcal{S}_{n}
also holds if the discretization error satisfies a summable tail condition, i.e.,
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\big(\big\lvert\mathcal{S}_{n}-\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}g(Z_{t})dt\big\lvert>\varepsilon\big)<\infty,~\forall\varepsilon>0
, by the Borel–Cantelli lemma. This serves as an alternative to the uniform Hölder condition in probability.

Proof.

Write

\mathcal{S}_{n}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g\big(Z_{(i-1)\Delta}\big)\Delta=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}g(Z_{t})dt+\mathcal{R}_{n},\quad\text{with }\mathcal{R}_{n}:=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{(i-1)\Delta}^{i\Delta}\big(g(Z_{(i-1)\Delta})-g(Z_{s})\big)ds.

Step 1 (Discretization error under localization)
Fix
K\geq 1
, set
\tau_{K}:=\{\inf\{t\in[0,T]:Z_{t}\notin[K^{-1},K]\}\}\wedge T
and let
Z_{t}^{(K)}:=Z_{t\wedge\tau_{K}}
. On
\{\tau_{K}=T\}
, the process stays in
[K^{-1},K]
, where the function
g
is Lipschitz with some constant
L_{K}
by (A2), hence
\lvert\mathcal{R}_{n}\lvert\leq\frac{L_{K}}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{(i-1)\Delta}^{i\Delta}\big\lvert Z^{(K)}_{(i-1)\Delta}-Z^{(K)}_{s}\big\lvert ds\leq L_{K}\sup_{\lvert s-\tau\lvert\leq\Delta}\big\lvert Z^{(K)}_{s}-Z^{(K)}_{\tau}\big\lvert
.
By (A1) applied to
Z
(and hence to
Z^{(K)}
),
\sup_{\lvert s-\tau\lvert\leq\Delta}\big\lvert Z^{(K)}_{s}-Z^{(K)}_{\tau}\big\lvert=O_{p}(\Delta^{\delta})
, so
\mathcal{R}_{n}=O_{p}(\Delta^{\delta})
on
\{\tau_{K}=T\}
. Since
Z_{t}
is strictly positive and nonexplosive on finite horizons, we have
\mathbb{P}(\tau_{K}<T)\to 0
as
K\to\infty
, and a standard localization argument yields
\mathcal{R}_{n}=O_{p}(\Delta^{\delta})
. If in addition (A1) holds almost surely, the same argument shows
\mathcal{R}_{n}=O_{a.s.}(\Delta^{\delta})
.

Step 2 (Convergence of the time average)
By positive Harris recurrence of
Z_{t}
and
g\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\pi_{0})
, the continuous-time ergodicity for additive functionals yields
\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}g(Z_{t})dt\xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{a.s./\mathcal{L}^{1}}\int gd\pi_{0}<\infty
. Combining this with the bound in
Step 1
yields
\mathcal{S}_{n}=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}g(Z_{t})dt+\mathcal{R}_{n}\xrightarrow{p}\int gd\pi_{0}
as
n\to\infty
, hence
\mathcal{S}_{n}=O_{p}(1)
. If
\mathcal{R}_{n}=O_{a.s.}(\Delta^{\delta})
, the same reasoning yields
\mathcal{S}_{n}\xrightarrow{a.s.}\int gd\pi_{0}<\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

Step
\ast
(
\mathcal{L}^{q}
-convergence):
If, for some
q>0
, the family
\{\lvert g(Z_{i\Delta})\lvert^{q}\}_{i=0}^{n}
is uniformly integrable (for instance by the polynomial growth of
g
and a uniform moment bound
\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert Z_{t}\lvert^{qr+\varepsilon}\big]<\infty
for some
\varepsilon>0
), the estimate in Step 1 improves to
\mathcal{R}_{n}=O_{\mathcal{L}^{q}}(\Delta^{\delta})
, and the ergodic theorem in
\mathcal{L}^{1}
together with uniform integrability implies
\mathcal{S}_{n}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}^{q}}\int gd\pi_{0}<\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
∎

Lemma A.1
.

[Key Properties of the CKLS process]

(i)
Existence and uniqueness of the (strong) solution and its pathwise strict positivity:
For
k>\frac{1}{2}

\lambda_{t}
is a pathwise unique strong solution (given that the initial value
\lambda_{0}>0

k=\frac{1}{2}

\lambda_{t}
is pathwise unique and strong: for subcase
2a\geq\sigma^{2}

\lambda_{t}
is strictly positive-valued; for subcase
2a<\sigma^{2}

\lambda_{t}
can reach

0

0
and leaves

(0,+\infty)
with no jump). For
0<k<\frac{1}{2}

\lambda_{t}=0
is required for uniqueness. A standard approach is to choose reflection at the origin, which can render the model well-posed. For any
k>0

\infty
is an unattainbale boundary.

Positive Harris recurrence:
On
t\in[0,+\infty)
(meaning the time filtration is modified as
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,+\infty)}

\lambda_{t}

C_{k}<\infty

and
a>0

\pi_{0}(dx)=p_{\infty}(x)dx

\lambda_{0}\sim\pi_{0}

*

C_{k}=\big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{-2k}e^{\Lambda(u;k)}du\big)^{-1}
, where

\psi(x;k)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\Big(\frac{ax^{1-2k}}{1-2k}-\frac{bx^{2-2k}}{2-2k}\Big),&&k\in(0,\frac{1}{2})\cup(\frac{1}{2},1)\cup(1,\infty);\\

; that is,

.

-th moment bound:

. Particularly,
\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{t}^{\pm q}]\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{t}^{\pm q}]\leq M<\infty
.

k\in(\frac{1}{2},1)

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\lambda_{t}^{q}dt\xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{a.s./\mathcal{L}^{1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{q}p_{\infty}(x)dx\overset{*}{=}\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{t}^{q}];

In particular, in the case of
k\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, the integral
\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{q}p_{\infty}(x)dx<\infty
for any
q\in\mathbb{R}
(when

Lemma A.2
.

[Key Properties of the CIR process]

(i)
Feller’s condition:
Following from Feller’s boundary classification,
\infty
is an unattainable boundary. Moreover, the following dichotomy holds: If

0

X_{t}>0
for all

(ii)
Uniform
q
-th moment bound
(As a special case of Lemma
A.1
’s (iii)) For any
q>0
, there exists a constant
C=C(q,T,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,X_{0})
such that
\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\big[X_{t}^{q}\big]\leq C
.

(iii)
Positive Harris recurrence:
(As a special case of Lemma
A.1
’s (iv)) The CIR process admits a unique invariant distribution
\pi_{0}=\Gamma(\kappa,\theta)
, shape
\kappa=\frac{2\alpha}{\gamma^{2}}>0
, rate
\theta=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2\beta}
, with density
p_{\infty}(x)=\frac{(1/\theta)^{\kappa}}{\Gamma(\kappa)}x^{\kappa-1}e^{-x/\theta}\mathds{1}_{[0,+\infty)}(x)
. All moments exist for any
q>-\kappa
, and in particular for all integers
q\geq 0
. For notational convenience, expectations with respect to the invariant law
\pi_{0}
will simply be denoted by
\mathbb{E}
; that is,
\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{0}}[f(X_{t})]\equiv\mathbb{E}[f(X_{t})]:=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\pi_{0}(dx)
for some functions
f
.
By Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem,

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{q}dt\xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{a.s./L^{1}}\mathbb{E}[X^{q}]=\theta^{q}\frac{\Gamma(\kappa+q)}{\Gamma(\kappa)}=\theta^{q}\prod_{j=0}^{q-1}(\kappa+j)=\Big(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2\beta}\Big)^{q}\prod_{j=0}^{q-1}\Big(\frac{2\alpha}{\gamma^{2}}+j\Big)<\infty.

With the notation
m_{q}:=\mathbb{E}[X^{q}]
, for
q=1,2,3
in particular, this yields

m_{1}=\theta\kappa=\frac{\alpha}{\beta},\quad m_{2}=\theta\kappa(\kappa+1)=\frac{\alpha(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})}{\beta^{2}},\quad m_{3}=\theta^{3}\kappa(\kappa+1)(\kappa+2)=\frac{\alpha(\alpha+\gamma^{2})(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})}{\beta^{3}}.

(iv)
Representation via squared Bessel process:
Let
\mathrm{BESQ}_{(d,R_{0})}(s)
denote the squared Bessel process of dimension
d:=4\alpha/\gamma^{2}
starting from
R_{0}:=4X_{0}/\gamma^{2}
. Then the solution to (
1.2
) admits the explicit representation
X_{t}=e^{-\beta t}\mathrm{BESQ}_{(d,R_{0})}\big(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4\beta}(e^{\beta t}-1)\big),~t\geq 0
.

(v)
Transition density:
For
t>0
, define
\chi_{t}:=\frac{4\beta e^{-\beta t}}{\gamma^{2}(1-e^{-\beta t})},~\phi_{t}:=\chi_{t}e^{-\beta t}X_{0}
. Then the random variable
Y_{t}:=\chi_{t}X_{t}
is non-central chi-square distributed with degrees of freedom
d
and non-centrality parameter
2\phi_{t}
. Consequently, the transition density of
X_{t}=x
is

f_{t}(x\lvert X_{0})=\chi_{t}e^{-\phi_{t}-\chi_{t}u}\Big(\frac{\chi_{t}x}{\phi_{t}}\Big)^{\frac{d}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}I_{\frac{d}{2}-1}\Big(2\sqrt{\chi_{t}\phi_{t}x}\Big),\qquad x>0.

where
I_{q}(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j!\Gamma(j+q+1)}(\frac{u}{2})^{2j+q}
denotes the modified Bessel function of the first kind.

(vi)
1/2
-Hölder continuity in
\mathcal{L}^{q}
:
for any
q>0
, there exists
C=C(q,T,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,X_{0})<\infty
such that

\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert X_{t}-X_{s}\lvert^{q}\big]\leq C\lvert t-s\lvert^{q/2},\quad\text{ for any }s,t\in[0,T].

Proof.

See e.g.,
[
20
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
2
]
.
∎

Proposition B.1
.

For any
q>0
, there exists
C=C(q,T,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,X_{0})<\infty
such that

\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert X_{t}^{2}-X_{s}^{2}\lvert^{q}\big]\leq C(t-s)^{q/2},\quad\text{ for any }s,t\in[0,T].

Proof.

Note that
\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert X_{t}^{2}-X_{s}^{2}\lvert^{q}\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[(X_{t}+X_{s})^{q}\big\lvert X_{t}-X_{s}\big\lvert^{q}\big]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\big[(X_{t}+X_{s})^{2q}\big]\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert X_{t}-X_{s}\lvert^{2q}}\big]
(Cauchy–Schwarz inequality).
The first term is bounded by Lemma
A.2
’s (ii); the second term is bounded by Lemma
A.2
’s (vi). Combining the two bounds yields the claim.
∎

Lemma B.2
.

[Moment bounds for averaged CIR martingale integrals with positive orders]

For
q^{\prime}>0
and
q>0
there exists a positive constant
C_{q,q^{\prime}}<\infty
(independent of
T
) such that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\lvert\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{q^{\prime}}dW_{t}\big\lvert^{q}\Big]\leq C_{q,q^{\prime}}T^{-q/2}.

Proof.

Case
q\geq 2
:
Set
M_{T}:=\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{q^{\prime}}dW_{t}
. By BDG (
6.4
) and Hölder inequalities,

\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert M_{T}\lvert^{q}\big]\leq C_{q}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2q^{\prime}}dt\Big)^{q/2}\leq C_{q}T^{q/2-1}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\big[X_{t}^{qq^{\prime}}\big]dt\leq C_{q,q^{\prime}}T^{q/2},

following from Lemma
A.2
’s (ii) with order
qq^{\prime}>0
. Thus
\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert T^{-1}M_{T}\lvert^{q}\big]\leq C_{q,q^{\prime}}T^{-q/2}
.

Case
0<q<2
:
By Lyapunov’s monotonicity of
\mathcal{L}_{q}
-norms,
\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert M_{T}\lvert^{q}\big]\leq\big(\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert M_{T}\lvert^{2}\big]\big)^{q/2}
. Using the Itô isometry and Lemma
A.2
’s (ii) with order
2q^{\prime}>0
,
\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert M_{T}\lvert^{2}\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2q^{\prime}}dt\big]\leq T\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\big[X_{t}^{2q^{\prime}}\big]\leq C_{q^{\prime}}T
. Hence
\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert M_{T}\lvert^{q}\big]\leq(C_{q^{\prime}}T)^{q/2}
and
\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert T^{-1}M_{T}\lvert^{q}\big]\leq C_{q,q^{\prime}}T^{-q/2}
.

Lemma B.3
.

[
\mathcal{L}^{q}
-Convergence Rate of
\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}dt
]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}dt=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}+\frac{X_{0}-X_{T}}{\beta T}+\frac{\gamma}{\beta T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}dW_{t}.

Moreover, for any
q>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\lvert\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}dt-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\big\lvert^{q}\Big]\lesssim T^{-q/2}.

Proof.

With
m_{1}:=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}
, integrating (
1.2
) over
[0,T]
and rearranging yields

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}dt-m_{1}=\frac{X_{0}-X_{T}}{\beta T}+\frac{\gamma}{bT}\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{X_{s}}dW_{s}.

Define
\rho_{1}(T):=\frac{X_{0}-X_{T}}{\beta\sqrt{T}}~\Leftrightarrow~\frac{X_{0}-X_{T}}{\beta T}=T^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{1}(T)
. Hence, by the elementary inequality
\lvert x+y+z\lvert^{q}\leq C_{q}\sum\lvert x\lvert^{q}
for
q\geq 1
(respectively
\lvert x+y+z\lvert^{q}\leq\sum\lvert x\lvert^{q}
for
0<q<1
):

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\lvert\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}dt-m_{1}\big\lvert^{q}\Big]\leq C_{q}\Big(\frac{\lvert X_{0}\lvert^{q}+\mathbb{E}[X_{T}^{q}]}{\beta^{q}T^{q}}+\frac{\gamma^{q}}{\beta^{q}T^{q}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\lvert\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{X_{s}}dW_{s}\big\lvert^{q}\Big]\Big).

By Lemma
A.2
’s (iii),
\mathbb{E}[X_{T}^{q}]<\infty
, so the first two terms are
O(T^{-q})
. By Lemma
B.2
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\lvert\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{X_{s}}dW_{s}\big\lvert^{q}\Big]\leq C_{q}T^{q/2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{the third term is no larger than }\frac{\gamma^{q}}{\beta^{q}T^{q}}C_{q}T^{q/2}=C_{q}T^{-q/2}.

Combining the three bounds and noting that
T^{-q}\leq T^{-q/2}
for
q>0
yields the desired result.

Lemma B.4
.

[
\mathcal{L}^{q}
-Convergence Rate of
\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}dt
]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}dt=\frac{\alpha\big(2\alpha+\gamma^{2}\big)}{2\beta^{2}}+\frac{X_{0}^{2}-X_{T}^{2}}{2\beta T}+\frac{2\alpha+\gamma^{2}}{2\beta T}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}dt-\frac{\alpha T}{\beta}\Big)+\frac{\gamma}{\beta T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{\frac{3}{2}}dW_{t}.

Moreover, for any
q>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\lvert\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}dt-\frac{\alpha(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})}{\beta^{2}}\big\lvert^{q}\Big]\lesssim T^{-q/2}.

Proof.

Apply Itô’s lemma to
x\mapsto x^{2}
yields

d(X_{t}^{2})=\big(2\alpha X_{t}-2\beta X_{t}^{2}+\gamma^{2}X_{t}\big)dt+2\gamma X_{t}^{\frac{3}{2}}dW_{t}.

With
m_{2}:=\frac{\alpha(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})}{\beta^{2}}
, integrating this over
[0,T]
and solving for
\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}dt
yields

\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}dt=m_{2}T+\frac{X_{0}^{2}-X_{T}^{2}}{2\beta}+\frac{\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}{\beta}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}dt-\frac{\alpha T}{\beta}\Big)+\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{\frac{3}{2}}dW_{t}.

Integrate (
1.2
) to represent
\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}dt
:

X_{T}-X_{0}=\alpha T-\beta\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}dt+\gamma\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}dW_{t}~\Longrightarrow~\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}dt=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}T+\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}dW_{t}-\frac{X_{T}-X_{0}}{\beta}.

Substituting expression of
\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}dt
into that of
\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}dt
yields the centered decomposition

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}dt-m_{2}=\frac{\gamma(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})}{\beta^{2}}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}dW_{t}+\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{\frac{3}{2}}dW_{t}+T^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{2}(T),

\displaystyle\text{where}~T^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{2}(T):=\frac{X_{0}^{2}-X_{T}^{2}}{2\beta T}+\frac{\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}{\beta}\frac{X_{0}-X_{T}}{\beta T}.

Control the
q
-th moments of the three terms on the right-hand side. Firstly, by Lemma
B.2
with
q^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}
and
q^{\prime}=\frac{3}{2}
, there exists a common constant
C_{q}>0
for the two such that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\lvert\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{s}^{\frac{1}{2}}dW_{s}\big\lvert^{q}\Big]\leq C_{q}T^{-q/2},\quad\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\lvert\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{s}^{\frac{3}{2}}dW_{s}\big\lvert^{q}\Big]\leq C_{q}T^{-q/2}.

Secondly, using the uniform moment Lemma
A.2
’s (iii), we obtain

\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert\rho_{2}(T)\lvert^{q}\big]\leq C_{q}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert T^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{2}(T)\lvert^{q}\big]\leq C_{q}T^{-q/2}.

Combining bounds for the two integral terms and for
T^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{2}(T)
, using
\lvert x+y+z\lvert^{q}\leq C_{q}\sum\lvert x\lvert^{q}
for
q\geq 1
(respectively
\lvert x+y+z\lvert^{q}\leq\sum\lvert x\lvert^{q}
for
0<q<1
), we obtain the desired result.
∎

Lemma B.5
.

[
\mathcal{L}^{q}
-Convergence Rate of

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{3}dt=\frac{\alpha(\alpha+\gamma^{2})(2\alpha+\gamma^{2})}{2\beta^{3}}+\frac{X_{0}^{3}-X_{T}^{3}}{3\beta T}+\frac{\alpha+\gamma^{2}}{\beta}\Big(\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}dt+\frac{\alpha(2\alpha+\gamma^{2})}{2\beta^{2}}\Big)+\frac{\gamma}{\beta T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{\frac{5}{2}}dW_{t}.

Moreover, for any
q>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\lvert\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{3}dt-\frac{\alpha(\alpha+\gamma^{2})(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})}{\beta^{3}}\big\lvert^{q}\Big]\lesssim T^{-q/2}.

Apply Itô’s lemma to
x\mapsto x^{3}
yields:

d(X_{t}^{3})=3\big((\alpha-\beta X_{t})X_{t}^{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}X_{t}^{2}\big)dt+3\gamma X_{t}^{\frac{5}{2}}dW_{t}=3\big((\alpha+\gamma^{2})X_{t}^{2}-\beta X_{t}^{3}\big)dt+3\gamma X_{t}^{\frac{5}{2}}dW_{t}.

Integrating this over

and rearranging terms:

\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{3}dt=\frac{\alpha+\gamma^{2}}{\beta}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}dt+\frac{X_{0}^{3}-X_{T}^{3}}{3\beta}+\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{\frac{5}{2}}dW_{t}.

With
m_{3}:=\frac{\alpha(\alpha+\gamma^{2})(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})}{\beta^{3}}=\frac{\alpha+\gamma^{2}}{\beta}\cdot\frac{\alpha(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})}{\beta^{2}}

T
, the centered decomposition yields

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{3}dt-m_{3}=\frac{X_{0}^{3}-X_{T}^{3}}{3\beta T}+\frac{\alpha+\gamma^{2}}{\beta}\Big(\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}dt-\frac{\alpha(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})}{\beta^{2}}\Big)+\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{\frac{5}{2}}dW_{t},

which yields the first claim of the lemma. From the elementary inequality
\lvert x+y+z\lvert^{q}\leq C_{q}\sum\lvert x\lvert^{q}
for
q\geq 1
(respectively
\lvert x+y+z\lvert^{q}\leq\sum\lvert x\lvert^{q}
for

), we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\lvert\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{3}dt-m_{3}\big\lvert^{q}\Big]

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{\lvert X_{0}\lvert^{3q}+\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert X_{T}\lvert^{3q}\big]}{(3\beta T)^{q}}+\Big(\frac{\alpha+\gamma^{2}}{\beta}\Big)^{q}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\lvert\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}dt-\frac{\alpha(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})}{\beta^{2}}\big\lvert^{q}\Big]+\Big(\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\Big)^{q}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\lvert\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{\frac{5}{2}}dW_{t}\big\lvert^{q}\Big].

By Lemma
A.2
’s (iii)
\frac{X_{0}^{3}-X_{T}^{3}}{3\beta T}=O(T^{-q})
. For
\frac{\alpha+\gamma^{2}}{\beta}\big(\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}dt-\frac{\alpha(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})}{\beta^{2}}\big)
, invoke the result in Lemma
B.4
. For

with

Lemma B.6
.

[

q=1
.

’s (vi) yields

(
\ast
)

Using Lemma

’s (vi) together with Hölder’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\lvert\frac{1}{n\Delta}\int_{0}^{n\Delta}X_{t}dt-\frac{1}{n\Delta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{(i-1)\Delta}\Delta\big\lvert^{q}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n\Delta)^{q}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\lvert\int_{0}^{n\Delta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{t}-X_{(i-1)\Delta})\mathds{1}_{\{(i-1)\Delta<t\leq i\Delta\}}dt\big\lvert^{q}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n\Delta)^{q}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{n\Delta}\big\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{t}-X_{(i-1)\Delta})\mathds{1}_{\{(i-1)\Delta<t\leq i\Delta\}}\big\lvert^{q}dt\Big]\Big(\int_{0}^{n\Delta}dt\Big)^{q-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n\Delta)^{q}}(n\Delta)^{q-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{(i-1)\Delta}^{i\Delta}\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert X_{t}-X_{(i-1)\Delta}\lvert^{q}\big]dt\leq(n\Delta)^{-1}C(n\Delta)^{-\omega q/2}(n\Delta)=C(n\Delta)^{-\omega q/2}.

.
By the concavity of

(Lyapunov’s monotonicity of

\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert Z_{n}\lvert^{q}\big]\leq\big(\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert Z_{n}\lvert\big]\big)^{q},\quad Z_{n}:=\frac{1}{n\Delta}\int_{0}^{n\Delta}X_{t}dt-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{(i-1)\Delta}.

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{(i-1)\Delta}^{3}\xrightarrow{a.s.}\frac{1}{n\Delta}\int_{0}^{n\Delta}X_{t}^{3}dt
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

Using analogous arguments for
q=1
,
q>1
and
0<q<1
as in the above-mentioned two Lemmas via Lemma
B.1
, together with the result that: There exists
C_{q}^{*}
such that

\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert X_{t}^{3}-X_{s}^{3}\lvert\big]\leq C_{q}^{*}(t-s)^{q/2}\text{ for every }q>0,~0\leq s<t\leq T\text{ with }0<t-s<1,

followed by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality:
\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert X_{t}^{3}-X_{s}^{3}\lvert^{q}\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert X_{t}-X_{s}\lvert^{q}\big(X_{t}^{2}+X_{t}X_{s}+X_{s}^{2}\big)^{q}\big]

\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\big[(X_{t}^{2}+X_{t}X_{s}+X_{s}^{2})^{2q}\big]\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert X_{t}-X_{s}\lvert^{2q}\big]}
, in which the boundedness inside the root is ensured by Lemma
B.1
and Lemma
A.2
’s (vi).
∎

Lemma B.9
.

[Asymptotic Equivalence of Left Riemann Sums and Integrated Processes]

Assume
\omega>1
(
\Delta\to 0
,
n\Delta\to\infty
and
n\Delta^{2}\to\infty
), then the following results hold:

\displaystyle(i)\quad(n\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{(i-1)\Delta}\Delta

\displaystyle=(n\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{n\Delta}X_{t}dt+\xi_{1}(n),

\displaystyle(ii)\quad(n\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{(i-1)\Delta}^{2}\Delta

\displaystyle=(n\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{n\Delta}X_{t}^{2}dt+\xi_{2}(n),

\displaystyle(iii)\quad(n\Delta)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{(i-1)\Delta}\Delta\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=(n\Delta)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{n\Delta}X_{t}dt\Big)^{2}+\xi_{3}(n),

with
\xi_{l}(n)\xrightarrow{p}0
, as
n\to\infty
,
l=1,2,3
.

Proof.

(i) and (ii) follow from Lemmas
B.6
and
B.7
. (iii) is a natural consequence of (i).

For (i), as there exists some
C
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\lvert\frac{1}{{n\Delta}}\int_{0}^{n\Delta}X_{t}dt-\frac{1}{n\Delta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{(i-1)\Delta}\Delta\big\lvert^{q}\Big]\leq C(n\Delta)^{-\omega q/2},

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert\xi_{1}(n)\lvert^{q}\big]=(n\Delta)^{q/2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\lvert\frac{1}{{n\Delta}}\int_{0}^{n\Delta}X_{t}dt-\frac{1}{n\Delta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{(i-1)\Delta}\Delta\big\lvert^{q}\Big]\leq C(n\Delta)^{(1-\omega)q/2}.

When
q=2
, this becomes
\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert\xi_{1}(n)\lvert^{2}\big]\leq C(n\Delta)^{1-\omega}
. By Chebyshev’s inequality
\xi_{1}(n)=O_{p}\big((n\Delta)^{(1-\omega)/2}\big)
. If
\omega>1
(i.e.,
n\Delta^{2}\to 0
) is further guaranteed,
\xi_{1}(n)\xrightarrow{p}0
as
n\to\infty
.

For (ii), the result follows analogously as in (i) since
\xi_{2}(n)=O_{p}\big((n\Delta)^{(1-\omega)/2}\big)
as well.

For (iii), we note that

(n\Delta)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{(i-1)\Delta}\Delta\Big)^{2}=(n\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big((n\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{n\Delta}X_{t}dt+\xi_{1}(n)\Big)^{2}=(n\Delta)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{n\Delta}X_{t}dt\Big)^{2}+\xi_{3}(n),

where
\xi_{3}(n)=2\xi_{1}(n)\frac{1}{n\Delta}\int_{0}^{n\Delta}X_{t}dt+(n\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{1}^{2}(n)\xrightarrow{p}0
, as
n\to\infty
. This follows from the facts that
\xi_{1}(n)\xrightarrow{p}0
and
\frac{1}{n\Delta}\int_{0}^{n\Delta}X_{t}dt\xrightarrow{p}0
, as
n\to\infty
.
∎

Lemma B.10
.

[
\mathcal{L}^{q}
-expansions for time-integrated functionals of
X_{t}
]

Let
q>0
. We have the following equations and convergences in
\mathcal{L}^{q}
as
T\to\infty
(thus also in probability):

\displaystyle(i)~

\displaystyle T^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}dt=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}T^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\gamma}{\beta T^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}dW_{t}+\rho_{1}(T),

\displaystyle\text{ with }\rho_{1}(T)=\frac{X_{0}-X_{T}}{\beta T^{\frac{1}{2}}}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}^{q}}0.

\displaystyle(ii)~

\displaystyle T^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}dt=\frac{\alpha(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})}{\beta^{2}}T^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\gamma(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})}{\beta^{2}T^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}dW_{t}+\frac{\gamma}{\beta T^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{\frac{3}{2}}dW_{t}+\rho_{2}(T),

\displaystyle\text{with }\rho_{2}(T)=\frac{X_{0}^{2}-X_{T}^{2}}{2\beta T^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{(\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})\rho_{1}(T)}{\beta}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}^{q}}0.

\displaystyle(iii)~

\displaystyle T^{-\frac{3}{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}dt\Big)^{2}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\beta^{2}}T^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{2\alpha\gamma}{\beta^{2}T^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}dW_{t}+\rho_{3}(T),

\displaystyle\text{with }\rho_{3}(T)=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\beta^{2}T^{\frac{3}{2}}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}dW_{t}\Big)^{2}+\frac{\rho_{1}^{2}(T)}{T^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{2\alpha\rho_{1}(T)}{\beta}+\frac{2\gamma\rho_{1}(T)}{\beta T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}dW_{t}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}^{q}}0.

Proof.

Expressions of (i) and (ii) are already given in the proofs of Lemmas
B.3
and
B.4
, where the respective
\mathcal{L}^{q}
-convergences are ensured by Lemma
A.2
’s (ii). For (iii), by squaring the expression of (i), we get the desired expression, while the convergence is derived from the bound Lemma
B.2
together with the convergence of
\rho_{1}(T)
.
∎

Appendix C
Proof of Selected Established Theorems

Proof of Theorem
5.5

Proof.

To show (i): For any
\mathcal{F}
-measurable
\zeta^{\prime}
, we have
(Z_{n},\zeta,\zeta^{\prime})\xrightarrow{d}(Z,\zeta,\zeta^{\prime})
. Since
\zeta_{n}\xrightarrow{p}\zeta
, we deduce that
(Z_{n},\zeta_{n},\zeta^{\prime})\xrightarrow{d}(Z,\zeta,\zeta^{\prime})
. This means
(Z_{n},\zeta_{n})\xrightarrow{d_{st}}(Z,\zeta)
.

To show (ii): Similarly, for any
\mathcal{F}
-measurable random variable
\zeta
, we have
(Z_{n},\zeta)\xrightarrow{d}(Z,\zeta)
. Applying the classical continuous mapping theorem for convergence in law, we deduce that
(g(Z_{n}),\zeta)\xrightarrow{d}(g(Z),\zeta)
. Hence, we obtain
g(Z_{n})\xrightarrow{d_{st}}g(Z)
.

To show (iii): By (i) we have the joint stable convergence in law
(Z_{n},\zeta_{n})\xrightarrow{d_{st}}(Z,\zeta)
. Then, applying (ii) with
continuous
h:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
, we obtain
h(Z_{n},\zeta_{n})\xrightarrow{d_{st}}h(Z,\zeta)
.
Choosing
h
as addition, multiplication, or division (with
\zeta\neq 0
) yields the usual Slutsky-type results.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.4

Proof.

For any bounded Borel function
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, let
K:=\lVert g\lVert_{\infty}<\infty
. For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\lvert\mathsf{G}_{n}\lvert:=\big\lvert\Lambda_{T}\mathds{1}_{A}g(Y_{n})\big\lvert\leq K\Lambda_{T}\quad a.s.

Because
\Lambda_{T}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathbb{P})
(martingale with
\Lambda_{0}=1
implies
\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}[\Lambda_{T}]=1
), we can take
\zeta:=K\Lambda_{T}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathbb{P})
. By Tool Lemma
(\star)
presented at the end of the proof,
\{\Lambda_{T}\mathds{1}_{A}g(Y_{n})\}_{n\geq 1}
is uniformly integrable. As a result, for any
A\in\mathcal{F}_{T}
:

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[\mathds{1}_{A}g(Y_{n})]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}[\Lambda_{T}\mathds{1}_{A}g(Y_{n})]\to\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}[\Lambda_{T}\mathds{1}_{A}g(Y)]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[\mathds{1}_{A}g(Y)],\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

by uniform integrability of
\mathsf{G}_{n}:=\Lambda_{T}\mathds{1}_{A}g(Y_{n})
. Since
\Lambda_{T}
is
\mathcal{F}_{T}
-measurable and
\varphi\!\perp\!\!\!\perp\mathcal{F}_{T}
(on a product extension) under
\mathbb{P}
, for any
A\in\mathcal{F}_{T}
and
B=\{\varphi\in E\}
for some Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}
:

\mathbb{Q}(A\cap B)=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\big[\Lambda_{T}\mathds{1}_{A}\mathds{1}_{B}\big]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}[\mathds{1}_{B}]\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\big[\Lambda_{T}\mathds{1}_{A}]=\mathbb{P}(B)\mathbb{Q}(A),

so
\varphi
remains independent of
\mathcal{F}_{T}
under
\mathbb{Q}
with the same marginal law
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
.

For the reverse implication from
\mathbb{Q}
to
\mathbb{P}
: If
\Lambda_{T}:=\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}\big\lvert_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}
is the
\mathbb{P}
-derivative of
\mathbb{Q}
, then its reciprocal
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{T}:=\frac{d\mathbb{P}}{d\mathbb{Q}}\big\lvert_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}=\frac{1}{\Lambda_{T}}
is the
\mathbb{Q}
-derivative of
\mathbb{P}
. By Bayes’ formula, for
0\leq T^{\prime}\leq T
(thus
\mathcal{F}_{T^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{T}
),

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\frac{1}{\Lambda_{T}}\Big\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T^{\prime}}\Big]=\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\big[\Lambda_{T}\cdot\frac{1}{\Lambda_{T}}\big\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T^{\prime}}\big]}{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\big[\Lambda_{T}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T^{\prime}}\big]}=\frac{1}{\Lambda_{T^{\prime}}}=\widetilde{\Lambda}_{T^{\prime}}.

Hence
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{T^{\prime}}
for
T^{\prime}\leq T
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-martingale, and the change-of-measure identity
\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}[\zeta]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[\widetilde{\Lambda}_{T}\zeta]
holds for all integrable
\zeta\in\mathcal{L}^{1}
under
\mathbb{Q}
with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{T^{\prime}}
. This allows one to repeat the proof verbatim with
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{T}
in place of
\Lambda_{T}
and
\mathbb{Q}
in place of
\mathbb{P}
, establishing invariance of stable convergence in law under equivalent measures in the reverse direction.

Tool Lemma
(\star)
[Domination
\Rightarrow
Uniform Integrability]
If there exists
\zeta\in\mathcal{L}^{1}
such that
\lvert\mathsf{G}_{n}\lvert\leq\zeta~a.s.
for all
n
, then the family
\{\mathsf{G}_{n}\}
is uniformly integrable.

Proof:

For any
\varepsilon>0
, since
\zeta\in\mathcal{L}^{1}
, there exists
K>0
such that
\mathbb{E}\big[\zeta\mathds{1}_{\{\zeta>K\}}\big]<\varepsilon
. Then

\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\lvert\mathsf{G}_{n}\lvert\mathds{1}_{\{\lvert\mathsf{G}_{n}\lvert>K\}}\big]\leq\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\zeta\mathds{1}_{\{\zeta>K\}}\big]\leq\varepsilon,

which is exactly the definition of uniform integrability.
∎

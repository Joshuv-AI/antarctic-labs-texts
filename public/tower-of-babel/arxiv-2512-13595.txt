Laplacian Spectrum of cozero-divisor graphs of commutative polynomial rings
Sarbari Mitra, Soumya Bhoumik

Abstract
The cozero-divisor graph of a commutative ring R R , denoted Γ ′ ​ ( R ) \Gamma^{\prime}(R) , is the graph whose vertices are the non-zero and non-unit elements of R R , with two distinct vertices x x and y y adjacent if and only if x ∉ R ​ y x\notin Ry and y ∉ R ​ x y\notin Rx . This paper studies the structural properties of Γ ′ ​ ( R ) \Gamma^{\prime}(R) for the polynomial ring R = ℤ n ​ [ x ] / ( x 2 ) R={\mathbb{Z}}_{n}[x]/(x^{2}) , where n n has the prime power decomposition of p 1 a 1 p 2 a 2 ⋯ p q a q p_{1}^{a_{1}}p_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots p_{q}^{a_{q}} . We provide a complete structure of the cozero-divisor graph for all n n up to cubic prime power decompositions. Furthermore, we determine the Laplacian spectrum of these graphs. Finally, we discuss the connectivity of such a cozero-divisor graph of the polynomial rings for any n n . Our work provides the first comprehensive spectral analysis of cozero-divisor graphs for non-local polynomial rings and establishes powerful new techniques for bridging commutative algebra with spectral graph theory.

Preliminaries

Let
\Gamma
be a finite simple graph with vertex set
V(\Gamma)=\{v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{n}\}
. We write
v_{i}\sim v_{j}
if
v_{i}
is adjacent to
v_{j}
, for
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
. The neighborhood of a vertex
x
, denoted
N_{\Gamma}(x)
, is the set of all vertices adjacent to
x
. For a finite, simple undirected graph
\Gamma
with vertex set
V(\Gamma)=\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{k}\}
, the
adjacency matrix

A(\Gamma)
is the
k\times k
matrix whose
(i,j)
-entry is
1
if
u_{i}\sim u_{j}
, and
0
otherwise. We denote the diagonal degree matrix by
D(\Gamma)=\mathrm{diag}(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{k})
, where
d_{i}
is the degree of vertex
u_{i}
. The
Laplacian matrix
of
\Gamma
is defined as

L(\Gamma)=D(\Gamma)-A(\Gamma)
. The matrix
L(\Gamma)
is symmetric and positive semidefinite, so all of its
eigenvalues are real and non-negative. Moreover, the sum of each row (and column)
of
L(\Gamma)
is zero. The eigenvalues of
L(\Gamma)
, called the

Laplacian eigenvalues
of
\Gamma
, are ordered as
\lambda_{1}(\Gamma)\geq\lambda_{2}(\Gamma)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(\Gamma)=0
. The largest Laplacian eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(\Gamma)
of
L(\Gamma)
is called the Laplacian spectral radius of
\Gamma
, whereas the second smallest eigenvalue is often referred to as the algebraic connectivity of the graph. The Laplacian spectrum of
\Gamma
, that is the spectrum of
{L}(\Gamma)
, is represented as

\Phi_{L}(\Gamma)=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}(\Gamma)&\lambda_{2}(\Gamma)&\cdots&\lambda_{r}(\Gamma)\\
\mu_{1}&\mu_{2}&\cdots&\mu_{r}\end{pmatrix}.

A subgraph
\Gamma^{\prime}
of
\Gamma
satisfies
V(\Gamma^{\prime})\subseteq V(\Gamma)
and
E(\Gamma^{\prime})\subseteq E(\Gamma)
. For a vertex subset
U\subseteq V
, the induced subgraph
\Gamma(U)
has vertex set
U
, where two vertices are adjacent if and only if they are adjacent in
\Gamma
. The complement
\overline{\Gamma}
of
\Gamma
has the same vertex set, with distinct vertices adjacent in
\overline{\Gamma}
if and only if they are not adjacent in
\Gamma
. The vertex connectivity of a connected graph
\Gamma
, denoted by
\kappa(\Gamma)
, is defined as the minimum size of a vertex cut set. The join of two vertex-disjoint graphs
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
, denoted
\Gamma_{1}\vee G_{2}
, is the graph formed by taking their disjoint union and adding every possible edge between
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
. The following definition generalizes this concept (referred to as a generalized composition graph in
[
11
]
).

Definition 2.1

Let
\Gamma
be a graph with
V(\Gamma)=\{u_{1},u_{2},\dotsc,u_{k}\}
, and let
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},\dotsc,\Gamma_{k}
be pairwise disjoint graphs. The
\Gamma
-generalized join graph
\Gamma[\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},\dotsc,\Gamma_{k}]
is obtained by taking the disjoint union of
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},\dotsc,\Gamma_{k}
and connecting every vertex of
\Gamma_{i}
to every vertex of
\Gamma_{j}
whenever
u_{i}\sim u_{j}
in
\Gamma
.

The following result, established by Cardoso et al. in
[
12
]
, expresses the Laplacian spectrum of a generalized join graph
\Gamma[\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},\dotsc,\Gamma_{k}]
in terms of the Laplacian spectra of the graphs
\Gamma_{i}
and the spectrum of a certain
k\times k
matrix
L(\Gamma)
.

Theorem 2.2

Let
\Gamma
be a graph with vertex set
V(\Gamma)=\{u_{1},u_{2},\dotsc,u_{k}\}
, and let
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},\dotsc,\Gamma_{k}
be
k
pairwise disjoint graphs with orders
n_{1},n_{2},\dotsc,n_{k}
, respectively. The Laplacian spectrum of the graph composition
\Gamma[\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},\dotsc,\Gamma_{k}]
is given by

\Phi_{L}(\Gamma[\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},\dotsc,\Gamma_{k}])=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\left(D_{i}+(\Phi_{L}(\Gamma_{i})\setminus\{0\})\right)\cup\Phi(\mathbb{L}(\Gamma)),

(1)

where

D_{i}=\begin{cases}\sum_{u_{j}\sim u_{i}}n_{j}&\text{if }N_{\Gamma}(u_{i})\neq\emptyset,\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and

\mathbb{L}(\Gamma)=\begin{bmatrix}D_{1}&-p_{1,2}&\cdots&-p_{1,k}\\
-p_{2,1}&D_{2}&\cdots&-p_{2,k}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-p_{k,1}&-p_{k,2}&\cdots&D_{k}\end{bmatrix}

(2)

with

p_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\sqrt{n_{i}n_{j}}&\text{if }u_{i}\sim u_{j}\text{ in }\Gamma,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In equation (1), the notation
\Phi_{L}(\Gamma_{i})\setminus\{0\}
indicates the removal of one copy of the eigenvalue
0
from the multiset
\Phi_{L}(\Gamma_{i})
, and
D_{i}+(\Phi_{L}(\Gamma_{i})\setminus\{0\})
denotes the addition of
D_{i}
to each element of
(\Phi_{L}(\Gamma_{i})\setminus\{0\})
.

Consider the weighted graph obtained by assigning weight
n_{i}=|V(\Gamma_{i})|
to each vertex
u_{i}
of
\Gamma
for
i=1,2,\dotsc,k
. Define the
k\times k
matrix
L(\Gamma)=(l_{i,j})
by

l_{i,j}=\begin{cases}-n_{j}&\text{if }i\neq j\text{ and }u_{i}\sim u_{j},\\
\sum_{u_{i}\sim u_{r}}n_{r}&\text{if }i=j,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

This matrix
L(\Gamma)
is called the vertex weighted Laplacian matrix of
\Gamma
.
Although
L(\Gamma)
has zero row sums, it is generally not symmetric. In contrast,
the matrix
\mathbb{L}(\Gamma)
defined in Theorem 2.1 is symmetric but may not have
zero row sums. Let
W
be the
k\times k
diagonal matrix with diagonal entries
n_{1},n_{2},\dotsc,n_{k}
. Then as shown in
[
13
]
,
L(\Gamma)=W^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{L}(\Gamma)W^{\frac{1}{2}}
, which implies that
L(\Gamma)
and
\mathbb{L}(\Gamma)
are similar matrices.
Hence, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 2.3

\Phi_{L}(\Gamma)=\Phi(\mathbb{L}(\Gamma))

Structure of cozero-divisor graph
\Gamma^{\prime}({\mathbb{Z}}_{n}[x]/(x^{2}))

Let
R=\mathbb{Z}_{n}[x]/(x^{2})
, and note that its elements are of the form
ax+b
with
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
.
R^{\times}
denotes the group of units of the ring
R
. We note that if
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}^{\times}
, then the element
ax+b\in R^{\times}
. Consequently, such elements cannot be vertices of the cozero-divisor graph of
R
. Now for any positive integer
n=p_{1}^{k_{1}}p_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots p_{r}^{k_{r}}
, we obtain the following ring isomorphism
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p_{1}^{k_{1}}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p_{2}^{k_{2}}}\times\cdots\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p_{r}^{k_{r}}}
. This can be extended to the polynomial (quotient) rings as well, thus

{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}[x]/(x^{2})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p_{1}^{k_{1}}}[x]/(x^{2})\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p_{2}^{k_{2}}}[x]/(x^{2})\times\cdots\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p_{r}^{k_{r}}}[x]/(x^{2})

In this paper, we classify the structure and Laplacian spectrum of
\Gamma^{\prime}({\mathbb{Z}}_{n}[x]/(x^{2}))
for key families of rings parameterized by
n
. Our analysis covers the cases
n=p,p^{2},p^{3},pq,p^{2}q
and
pqr
, where
p,q,r
are distinct primes. For each family, we decompose the ring into local components, determine the graph’s structure by analyzing its ideals, and then compute its complete Laplacian spectrum, revealing spectral properties that depend directly on the prime factorization of
n
.

3.1
Prime Case:
n=p

Note that
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[x]/(x^{2})
is a local ring with maximal ideal generated by
x
. Now the vertex set of the cozero-divisor graph
\Gamma^{\prime}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[x]/(x^{2}))
is

V(\Gamma^{\prime}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[x]/(x^{2})))=\{ax:a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}\}

It can be easily observed that the ideal generated by
ax
, for all
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
, is the same as the maximal ideal generated by
x
, thus
bx\in a{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[x]/(x^{2})
for any
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
. Hence, we conclude that

Theorem 3.1

\Gamma^{\prime}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[x]/(x^{2}))\cong\bar{K}_{p-1}
.

We denote the eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
of
\mathcal{L}(\Gamma^{\prime}({\mathbb{Z}}_{n}[x]/(x^{2})))
with multiplicuty
\mu_{i}
by
\lambda_{i}^{[\mu_{i}]}
. Now the following theorem is obvious:

Theorem 3.2

The Laplacian spectrum of
\Gamma^{\prime}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[x]/(x^{2}))
is
0^{[p-1]}
.

3.2
Two distinct primes:
n=pq

For the ring
{\mathbb{Z}}_{pq}[x]/(x^{2})
, where
p,q
are both primes, we have
{\mathbb{Z}}_{pq}[x]/(x^{2})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[x]/(x^{2})\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}[x]/(x^{2})

S=\{c\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{pq}:(c,pq)\neq 1\}
. Then the vertex set of
\Gamma^{\prime}(R)
(where
R={\mathbb{Z}}_{pq}[x]/(x^{2})
):

V(\Gamma^{\prime}(R))=\{ax+b:a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{pq},b\in S\}

It can be noted that
\mid V(\Gamma^{\prime}(R))\mid=pq(p+q-1)-1
, as
\mid S\mid=pq-\phi(pq)=p+q-1
. Every principal ideal in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{pq}[x]/(X^{2})
corresponds uniquely to an ordered pair of principal ideals, one from
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[x]/(X^{2})

{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}[x]/(X^{2})

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[x]/(X^{2})

{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}[x]/(X^{2})

\displaystyle\text{In }\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/(x^{2}):

\displaystyle I_{1}=\langle 0\rangle,\quad I_{2}=\langle x\rangle,\quad I_{3}=\{\langle ax+b\rangle:b\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\},

\displaystyle\text{In }\mathbb{Z}_{q}[x]/(x^{2}):

\displaystyle J_{1}=\langle 0\rangle,\quad J_{2}=\langle x\rangle,\quad J_{3}=\{\langle cx+d\rangle:d\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}\}.

The corresponding cardinalities are:

\mid I_{1}\mid=\mid J_{1}\mid=1,\quad\mid I_{2}\mid=p-1,\quad\mid J_{2}\mid=q-1,\quad\mid I_{3}\mid=p(p-1),\quad\mid J_{3}\mid=q(q-1).

Let
a_{p},b_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
a_{q},b_{q}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}
denote the images of

p
and
q
, respectively. Each principal ideal
\langle ax+b\rangle
in

(I_{i},J_{j})

, which we examine below.

•

I_{1}\times J_{1}
:
Here
a,b
are both divisible by
p
and
q

•

I_{1}\times J_{2}
:
Both
a,b
divisible by
p

and
q\nmid a
. This yields the ideal
\langle px\rangle.

•

I_{2}\times J_{1}
:
Both
a,b
divisible by
q

p\mid b
and
p\nmid a
. This gives
\langle qx\rangle.

•

I_{2}\times J_{2}
:

(a,p)=1

•

•

I_{3}\times J_{1}

•

{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}

•

I_{3}\times J_{2}

b_{p}
a unit in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

\langle x+q\rangle.

•

I_{3}\times J_{3}

Both
b_{p}
and
b_{q}
are units, giving unit elements only; these are excluded.

Case 1.

(ii)

(iii)

\langle p\rangle
already considered. Therefore, the total number of maximal ideals of this type is

p-1

\langle px\rangle

\langle x\rangle

Theorem 3.7

The Laplacian spectrum of
\Gamma^{\prime}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{2}}[x]/(x^{2}))
is
\big\{0^{[p]},p(p^{2}-1)^{[p^{3}-p-1]}\big\}
.

Proof.

First note that

\Gamma^{\prime}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{2}}[x]/(x^{2}))=\Upsilon^{\prime}[\Gamma^{\prime}({\cal A}_{0,p}),\Gamma^{\prime}({\cal A}_{p,0}),\bigcup_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\Gamma^{\prime}({\cal A}_{1,\ell p})]

\displaystyle\Phi_{L}(\Gamma^{\prime}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{2}}[x]/(x^{2})))

\displaystyle=\left(D_{p,0}+(\Phi_{L}(\Gamma^{\prime}({\cal A}_{p,0}))\setminus\{0\})\right)\bigcup\left(D_{0,p}+(\Phi_{L}(\Gamma^{\prime}({\cal A}_{0,p}))\setminus\{0\})\right)

Lemma 3.10

For any
0\leq k<l\leq 2
, and

are units in

, two ideals
\langle p^{k}a_{1}x+p^{l}b_{1}\rangle

a_{2}b_{1}\equiv a_{1}b_{2}\pmod{p^{l-k}}

Proof.

The two ideals are identical if there exists an element

b_{1}d\equiv b_{2}\pmod{p^{3-l}}
, which implies
d\equiv b_{2}/b_{1}\pmod{p^{3-l}}

On the other hand, the second congruence gives us
p^{k}(a_{2}-a_{1}d)\equiv p^{l}b_{1}c\pmod{p^{3}}
, which simplifies
a_{2}-a_{1}d\equiv 0\pmod{p^{l-k}}
. Finally, substituting
d
, we get the desired congruence
a_{2}b_{1}\equiv a_{1}b_{2}

\pmod{p^{l-k}}
.

\displaystyle\bigcup\Phi(\mathbb{L}(\Upsilon^{\prime}[{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{2}}[x]/(x^{2})])).

The spectrum is completed by computing the eigenvalues of the Laplacian matrix of the graph
\Upsilon^{\prime}[{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{2}q}[x]/(x^{2})]
. Note that the dimension is this matrix is linear in
p
, specifically
3(p+1)+7=3p+10
. This provides a dramatic reduction in complexity, as the original adjacency matrix of the graph has a number of vertices on the order of
p^{2}q
.
∎

We finish this paper by exploring the connectivity of the cozero-divisor graph
\Gamma^{\prime}({\mathbb{Z}}_{n}[x]/(x^{2}))
.

Proposition 3.15

The cozero-divisor graph
\Gamma^{\prime}({\mathbb{Z}}_{n}[x]/(x^{2}))
is disconnected if and only if
n
is a prime power.

Proof.

First, suppose
n=p^{k}
, a prime power. Consider the principal ideal
I=\langle p^{k-1}x\rangle
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}[x]/(x^{2})
. For any element
ax+b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}[x]/(x^{2})
with
\langle ax+b\rangle\neq I
, we analyze the containment relations. For any ideal
\langle ax+b\rangle
, if
b=0
, then there are two possibilities.

1.

p^{k-1}\mid a
, then
\langle ax\rangle\subseteq\langle p^{k-1}x\rangle

2.

p^{k-1}\nmid a
, then
\langle p^{k-1}x\rangle\subseteq\langle ax\rangle

In both cases,
\langle ax\rangle
and
\langle p^{k-1}x\rangle
are comparable, hence,
\langle p^{k-1}x\rangle
and
\langle ax\rangle
are not adjacent in
\Gamma^{\prime}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{k}}[x]/(x^{2}))
. Now let us consider the remaining case,
b=jp^{l}
for
0\leq l<k
, and
j\neq 0
such that
(j,p)=1
. Then note

(ax+jp^{l})(j^{-1}p^{k-1-l}x)=p^{k-1}x
. Hence
\langle p^{k-1}x\rangle\subseteq\langle ax+b\rangle
, and consequently
\langle p^{k-1}x\rangle
is not adjacent to any other vertex in
\Gamma^{\prime}({\mathbb{Z}}_{n}[x]/(x^{2}))
. Therefore, the vertex corresponding to
\langle p^{k-1}x\rangle
forms an isolated vertex, making the graph disconnected.

Conversely, we assume that
n
is not a prime power and show that the cozero-divisor graph obtained is connected. Let
n=\prod_{i=1}^{r}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
be its prime factorization, with
r\geq 2
. Then from the isomorphism of rings,

\mathbb{Z}_{n}[x]/(x^{2})\cong\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{Z}_{p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}[x]/(x^{2}),

For each index
i
let
J_{i}:=\langle p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}-1}x\rangle
. Now we define the ideals
U_{i}
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}[x]/(x^{2})
corresponding to the ideal
J_{i}
in the
i^{\rm th}
component.

U_{i}=(0,\dots,\underbrace{\langle p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}-1}x\rangle}_{i^{\text{th}}\text{position}},\dots,0)

Note that for
i\neq j
the ideals
U_{i}
and
U_{j}
are incomparable (
U_{i}\not\subseteq U_{j},U_{j}\not\subseteq U_{i}
). Hence every
U_{i}
and
U_{j}

(i\neq j)
are adjacent in
\Gamma^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}[x]/(x^{2}))
. In particular, the subgraph induced by
\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{r}\}
is a clique. Now for any vertex
f=ax+b\in V(\Gamma^{\prime}({\mathbb{Z}}_{n}[x]/(x^{2})))
, we show that it is connected with this clique. We write
f=(f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{r})
, where
f_{i}=a_{i}x+b_{i}
,
a\equiv a_{i}\pmod{p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}},b\equiv b_{i}\pmod{p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}
.
We consider the following two cases:

1.

f
has at least one zero component. In that case note that there also exist some
f_{j}\neq 0
(otherwise
f
is the zero element, not part of the set
V(\Gamma^{\prime}({\mathbb{Z}}_{n}[x]/(x^{2})))
).Without loss of generality, let us assume that
f_{1}=0
, and
f_{2}\neq 0
. Now there exists a vertex
g=(g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{r})
such that
g_{1}
is a unit (i.e.
\langle g_{1}\rangle={\mathbb{Z}}_{p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}}[x]/(x^{2})
) and
g_{2}=0
. Thus
\langle f\rangle\not\subseteq\langle g\rangle
, and
\langle g\rangle\not\subseteq\langle f\rangle
, hence
f\sim g
. On the other hand,
U_{2}
and
\langle g\rangle
are incomparable as well, which implies that
g\sim U_{2}
, establishing the connectivity of
f
with the clique.

2.

Next we consider
f
has no zero components. Clearly, at least one of the
f_{i}
must not be a unit, as otherwise
f
will be a unit, and hence not in the vertex set. Without loss of generality, let us assume
f_{1}
is non-unit (i.e.
\langle f_{1}\rangle\subsetneqq{\mathbb{Z}}_{p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}}[x]/(x^{2})
. Once again we consider a vertex
g=(g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{r})
such that
g_{1}
is a unit (i.e.
\langle g_{1}\rangle={\mathbb{Z}}_{p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}}[x]/(x^{2})
) and
g_{2}=0
. It is again evident that
f\sim g\sim U_{2}
.

In all cases,
f=ax+b
is adjacent to some vertex in the clique
{U_{1},\dots,U_{r}}
, so
\Gamma^{\prime}({\mathbb{Z}}_{n}[x]/(x^{2}))
is connected.
∎

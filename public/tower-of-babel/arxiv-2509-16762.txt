Novel Bounds From The Weak Gravity and Festina Lente Conjectures
Fayez Abu-Ajamieh, Pratik Chattopadhyay, Nobuchika Okada, Roman Pasechnik, Zhi-Wei Wang

Abstract
We demonstrate that the Weak Gravity Conjecture (WGC) and the Festina-Lente Conjecture (FLC) yield novel bounds on fifth force searches and milli-Charged Particles (mCPs), as well as on the scale of inflation and on the effective Higgs quartic interaction. In particular, we find that combining the FLC with inflation leads to stronger bounds on mCPs than what the simple application of the FLC provides. Furthermore, we have explored the implications of naturalness on both the FLC and WGC, and have found that these conjectures place a lower limit on the charge of a U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gauge group.

The main argument of the FLC relies on the evaporation of charged black holes in dS spacetime. More specifically, extremal charged black holes in dS space, where the Schwarzschild radius and the cosmological radius coincide, are called Nariai black holes

13

In this paper, we investigate some further phenomenological implications of the FLC and WGC. In particular, we consider the relevance of the FLC to the fifth force searches, where we show that both the FLC and WGC can set strong bounds on any fifth force, either gauge
U(1)
or Yukawa-type, and could even exclude the entire parameter space for light force carriers with masses comparable to the Hubble scale. We also investigate the implications of the FLC on the Higgs potential instability and set bounds on the effective quartic coupling of the Higgs boson. In addition, we investigate the implications of the FLC on the scale of inflation. Specifically, we show that combining the FLC with the requirement that the cosmological reheating does not impact the Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN), stringent bounds can be placed on mCPs, which are stronger than those emerging from a simple application of the FLC presented in
[
22

U(1)

This paper is organized as follows. In Section II, we present the bounds from both conjectures on fifth force searches. In Section III, we examine the phenomenological implications of the FLC under the assumption that gravity is the weakest force, effectively combining FLC with WGC. In Section IV, we review the magnetic form of the FLC and discuss some of its basic applications to the Higgs instability, inflation, and mCPs. In Section V, we explore the role of the FLC in light of naturalness, and finally we conclude in Section VI.

II
The FLC and fifth force searches

A fifth force is generally parametrized as a deviation from the Newtonian potential as

U(r)=-\frac{Gm^{2}}{r}(1+\alpha)e^{-\frac{r}{\lambda}}\,,

(5)

where
\alpha
parametrizes the strength of the new interaction relative to that of gravity, and the force range
\lambda=1/M
(not to be confused with the Higgs quartic coupling) in terms of the mass of the force carrier
M
. Thus, it is possible to use both the WGC and FLC to set upper and lower bounds on any additional
U(1)
interaction. However, there is a caveat when applying the WGC: unlike the FLC, which applies to
every
particle charged under some
U(1)
in the spectrum, the WGC only applies to the particle with the smallest
m/q
ratio. Since for any
U(1)
such a particle must exist to allow for charged black holes to decay, we can proceed with using the WGC in the context of constraining the fifth force.

Now, consider a particle of mass
m
, charge
q
, and gauge coupling
g
under some
U(1)
, whose interaction is mediated by a force carrier of mass
M
and range
\lambda=1/M
. Then, the
U(1)
potential can be expressed as

U(r)=-\frac{q^{2}g^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{e^{-\frac{r}{\lambda}}}{r}=\frac{-1}{8\pi M_{P}^{2}}\frac{m^{2}}{r}\Big(\frac{2M_{P}^{2}q^{2}g^{2}}{m^{2}}\Big)e^{-\frac{r}{\lambda}}\,.

(6)

Comparing this to Eq. (
5
) one finds that
\alpha_{U(1)}=\frac{2q^{2}g^{2}M_{P}^{2}}{m^{2}}
. A comparison with Eq. (
1
) immediately yields the WGC bound,
\alpha_{U(1)}\geq 1
. On the other hand, a straightforward calculation shows that the FLC implies

\alpha_{U(1)}\leq\frac{m^{2}M_{P}^{2}}{V}=\frac{m^{2}}{3H^{2}}\,.

(7)

The latter term suggests that the FLC can provide a meaningful bound only for light particles i.e. with masses not much larger than
H\sim 10^{-33}
eV. Figure
1
shows the bounds implied by the WGC and by the FLC for some benchmark masses on fifth force searches compared to existing bounds. As the plot shows, both the WGC and the FLC set stringent bounds on the parameter space, and can even exclude it entirely for masses not much larger than the Hubble scale. Notice that the bounds arising from the FLC are independent of the mass of the force carrier
M=1/\lambda

7

III
Gravity as the weakest force

As alluded to in the introduction, the physical content of the WGC is that gravity is weaker than any force associated with a
U(1)
gauge group. More specifically, consider the gravitational force and a
U(1)
force mediated via a massless force carrier (like the photon), then the WGC implies that

F_{\text{grav}}=\frac{m^{2}}{8\pi M_{P}^{2}r^{2}}\leq F_{\text{gauge}}=\frac{q^{2}g^{2}}{4\pi r^{2}}\,,

(8)

which immediately leads to Eq. (
1
). On the other hand, if we consider the FLC in Eq. (
3
) and multiply both sides by
(4\pi M_{P}^{2}r^{2})^{-1}
, then rearrange the inequality in the light of Eq. (
8
), we find that the WGC and FLC give rise to

\frac{V}{m^{2}M_{P}^{2}}\leq\frac{F_{\text{grav}}}{F_{\text{gauge}}}\leq 1\,,

(9)

This suggests a lower bound on the mass of any particle charged under
U(1)
,

m\geq\frac{\sqrt{V}}{M_{P}}=\sqrt{3}H\,,

(10)

which is trivially satisfied for the SM QED for
H
being the Hubble constant at present. As we shall see in Section
IV.3
, the same results can be derived from the magnetic form of the FLC.

The next question that arises is: What about forces mediated by scalar interactions? There have been several attempts in the literature to extend the WGC to include scalar-mediated interactions, see e.g.
[
24
,
25
,
7
,
26

27
,
28
,
29
]
. Most of these proposals essentially try extend the physical content of the WGC of gravity being the weakest force, to forces mediated by scalar particles, assuming that the gravitational force should be weaker than that mediated by any (massless) scalar particles. We will consider the leading example presented in
[
24
]
here and see the implications of the FLC on such forces.

Consider a (massive) scalar field
\phi
that is charged under some
U(1)
with charge
q
, and has a mass that is a function of several massless scalar mediator fields
\varphi_{i}
, i.e.,
m=m(\varphi_{i})
. Such particles could arise, for example, in string particles that are functions of the moduli of compactification. Then according to
[
24
]
, gravitationally-bound states should not be allowed, and the absence of such gravitationally bound states implies that the repulsive
U(1)
gauge interaction, should be larger than the combined attractive gravitational and scalar-mediated forces. In other words, to avoid gravitationally-bound state, one needs
[
24
]

q^{2}\geq\frac{m^{2}}{M_{P}^{2}}+g^{ij}(\partial_{\varphi_{i}}m)(\partial_{\varphi_{j}}m)\,,

(11)

where
g^{ij}
is the metric of the kinetic terms in field space. Now, if
\phi
is not charged under any
U(1)

-g^{ij}(\partial_{\varphi_{i}}m)(\partial_{\varphi_{j}}m)\geq\frac{m^{2}}{M_{P}^{2}}\,,

(12)

which, for a single scalar mediator, implies that the magnitude of the scalar-mediated force is larger than the magnitude of the gravitational force

|\partial_{\varphi}m|^{2}\geq\frac{m^{2}}{M_{P}^{2}}\,.

(13)

When combining this with the mass bound in Eq. (
10
), one finds that

|\partial_{\varphi}m|\geq\sqrt{3}\frac{H}{M_{P}}\sim 10^{-60}.

(14)

This result only applies to charged scalar particles with derivatives-type interactions as explained in
[
24
]
.

The Magnetic FLC and its applications

As explained in detail in
[
13
]

IV.2
Inflation and milli-charged particles

Although the electric charge in the SM is quantized, with no experimental evidence so far to indicate any deviation of the charges of SM particles from quantization, it is nonetheless possible for the electric charge to deviate from the SM prediction. More specifically, the requirements of anomaly cancellation and the gauge invariance of the SM Yukawa interactions are alone insufficient to determine charge quantization, and they leave one degree of freedom that allows the electric charge in the SM to deviate from their values by a milli-charge parameter
\epsilon

VI
Conclusions

In this paper, we have studied several phenomenological implications of the FLC and the WGC. In particular, we have shown that the FLC and WGC can set stringent bounds on fifth force searches and could even exclude the entire parameter space for light particles with masses not much larger than the Hubble scale. We have also investigated the implications of the magnetic FLC for the Higgs potential instability, mCPs and the scale of inflation. In particular, we have found that the FLC sets stringent bounds on the Higgs quartic coupling. Moreover, when the FLC is applied during inflation, the requirement for the reheating temperature to be larger than the BBN temperature can set a stringent bound on the parameter space of mCPs. The latter turns out to be orders of magnitude stronger than what is suggested by a naive application of the FLC.

We have also shown that the magnetic FLC, as well as the assumption that gravity is the weakest force, can set an upper bound on the scale of inflation that depends only on the mass of the lightest charged particle, which ostensibly leads to tension between the FLC and inflation. However, this tension can be resolved if the lightest charged particle is allowed to become heavier in the UV. We have evaluated the implications of interplay between naturalness and the FLC/WGC, and found that this implies a lower limit on the charge of a
U(1)
gauge group. We point out that the FLC can augment experimental searches in placing limits on the parameter space associated and any
U(1)
gauge group, whether broken or unbroken.

Exact Sum Rules and Zeta Generating Formulas from the ODE/IM correspondence
Syo Kamata

Abstract
We formulate exact sum rules (ESRs) and zeta generating formulas (ZGFs) for quantum mechanics defined by the 𝒫𝒯 {\cal PT} -symmetric potential V 𝒫𝒯 ​ ( x ) = x 2 ​ K ​ ( i ​ x ) ε V_{\cal PT}(x)=x^{2K}(ix)^{\varepsilon} with K , ε ∈ ℕ K,\varepsilon\in\mathbb{N} , as well as the Hermitian potential V ℋ ​ ( x ) = x 2 ​ K V_{\cal H}(x)=x^{2K} with K ∈ ℕ + 1 K\in{\mathbb{N}}+1 , based on the fusion relations of the A 2 ​ M − 1 A_{2M-1} T-system in the framework of the ODE/IM correspondence. In this setup, the fusion relations on the integrable model (IM) side correspond to recurrence relations among quantization conditions on the ordinary differential equation (ODE) side, which we reformulate in terms of spectral zeta functions (SZFs), ζ n ​ ( s ) = ∑ α ∈ ℕ 0 E n , α − s \zeta_{n}(s)=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}E_{n,\alpha}^{-s} , where n n denotes the fusion label. The ESRs yield algebraic relations among SZFs at fixed n n , while the ZGFs establish explicit functional mappings between SZFs at different fusion labels. These structures are governed by a selection rule depending on both M M and n n , induced in general by the combination of the structure of the Chebyshev polynomials appearing in the fusion relations and the ℤ 2 ​ M + 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2M+2} Symanzik rotational symmetry. Our results provide a novel spectral interpretation of the T-system in integrable models and point toward hidden algebraic structures governing global spectral data in 𝒫 ​ 𝒯 \mathcal{PT} -symmetric and Hermitian quantum mechanics.

We make a brief comment on Borel resummation.

II.2
The spectral zeta function

In this part, we introduce the spectral zeta function (SZF)
Voros:1986vw
;
voros1992spectral

and provide definitions of the exact sum rule (ESR) and the zeta generating formula (ZGF).

We start with a (normalized) QC, denoted by
{\mathfrak{D}}(E)
.
Suppose that it can be expressed by a spectral determinant as

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{D}}(E)=\prod_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}(E_{\alpha}-E)=\exp\left[\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\log(E_{\alpha}-E)\right],

(31)

with an energy spectrum labeled by
\alpha
,
\{E_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
, where
E_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
,
E\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{E_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
.
Trivially, solving
{\mathfrak{D}}(E)=0
gives
E=E_{\alpha}
.
We rewrite Eq.(
31
) using the Hurwitz-type spectral zeta function defined as

\displaystyle\zeta(s,E):=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}(E_{\alpha}-E)^{-s}.\qquad(s\in{\mathbb{R}})

(32)

Performing Taylor expansion around
E=0
to the exponential part of Eq.(
31
) leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\log(E_{\alpha}-E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\zeta^{\prime}(0)-\sum_{s\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{\zeta(s)}{s}E^{s},

(33)

where
\zeta(s)
and
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
are the SZFs and its derivative of
s
defined from Eq.(
32
) as

\displaystyle\zeta(s):=\zeta(s,0)=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}E_{\alpha}^{-s},\qquad\zeta^{\prime}(0):=\left.\frac{\partial\zeta(s)}{\partial s}\right|_{s=0}=-\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\log E_{\alpha}.

(34)

Therefore, using the SZFs
\zeta(s)
with
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
and its derivative
\zeta^{\prime}(0)
, one can write down the QC (
31
) as

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{D}}(E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left[-\zeta^{\prime}(0)-\sum_{s\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{\zeta(s)}{s}E^{s}\right]

(35)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\zeta^{\prime}(0)}+e^{-\zeta^{\prime}(0)}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf t}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{m}\\
|{\bf t}|>0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|{\bf t}|}\left[\prod_{s=1}^{m}\frac{\zeta(s)^{t_{s}}}{t_{s}!s^{t_{s}}}\right]E^{({\bf m},{\bf t})},\qquad({\bf m},{\bf t}):=\sum_{s=1}^{m}st_{s},

where
|{\bf t}|:=\sum_{m=1}^{{\rm dim}({\bf t})}t_{m}
.
As one can see from Eq.(
35
), the SZFs appear in coefficients of the expanded form of the QC and keep information of the energy spectrum as summation of the inverse energy, which means that searching all the SZFs (and
\zeta^{\prime}(0)
) is essentially the same to finding the energy spectrum.

As is mentioned in Sec.

, the QC corresponds to the Stokes multiplier

{\cal PT}_{K}

We also define a set of the SZFs as

\displaystyle\bm{\zeta}_{K\in{\cal K}}:=\{\zeta_{K}(s)\}_{s\in{\mathbb{N}}}.

(36)

Our main aim of this paper is to understand the fusion hierarchy in framework of the ODE/IM correspondence from the spectral perspective through

For the purpose, we consider the ESRs and the ZGFs defined as follows:

(I)

Exact sum rule (ESR)
:
For fixed

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf m}=\{m_{1},\cdots,m_{s-1}\}\in{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}^{s-1}\\

We define

Here, the equivalence relation for the quotient is defined as: for

(II)

\displaystyle\zeta_{K}(s)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf m}=\{m_{1},\cdots,m_{s}\}\in{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}^{s}\\

For a given

and

II.3
Strategy for formulating the ESRs and the ZGFs

In order to construct the ESRs and the ZGFs, we take the following procedure.
We firstly write down
C^{(n)}(E)
using

using the fusion relations, and then, expand the result around
E=0
using Eq.(

) to reexpress it by the SZFs.
Next, we individually consider two cases by taking

or

: the former gives the ESRs in terms of
\bm{\zeta}_{1}
, and the later leads to the ZGF mapping from the
{\cal PT}_{1}
sector to the
{\cal PT}_{n}
sector, i.e.
\bm{\zeta}_{n\in{\cal K}\setminus\{1\}}=\bm{\zeta}_{n}(\bm{\zeta}_{1})
.
After finding the inverse solution from the ZGF as
{\bm{\zeta}}_{1}=\bm{\zeta}_{1}(\bm{\zeta}_{n^{\prime}})
, the ESRs in terms of
\bm{\zeta}_{n^{\prime}}
and the ZGF,
\bm{\zeta}_{n}=\bm{\zeta}_{n}(\bm{\zeta}_{n^{\prime}})
, are constructable by substituting the inverse solution into the ESRs and the ZGF of
\bm{\zeta}_{1}
, respectively.
Although the remaining part, i.e.
\zeta_{n}^{\prime}(0)
, can be found by directly solving the
O(E^{0})
parts of the ESRs and the ZGF, we offer a method using the Chebyshev polynomials which is convenient for finding the solutions.
The subtle issue is the existence of the inverse solutions of

, and, as we will see, it depends on the values of
M
and
n
, which generally changes the structure of the ZGFs.
The overall structure of the ESRs and the ZGFs for fixed
M
is characterized by the selection rule defined in Eq.(
38
).

Climate change, agricultural transformation and climate smart agriculture development in China
Authors: Bin Luo, Xiangsheng Dou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAgainst the backdrop of increasingly severe global climate change, it has become an inevitable choice to promote the transformation of agriculture oriented to climate smart agriculture. The objective of this paper is to demonstrate realistic problem faced by China's agricultural transformation and identify key factors affecting agricultural development under the background of climate change. The paper constructs a linear econometric model and employes time series data from 1990 to 2019 to empirically test the impact of climate change and agricultural investment on agriculture in China. The results indicate that climate change has a negative impact on agriculture, while agricultural investment has a positive impact. Therefore, it is necessary to promote agricultural transformation oriented to climate smart agriculture. To this end, China must vigorously promote agricultural system reform, accelerate agricultural technological innovation, accelerate the development of agricultural big data and informatization, and strengthen financial support for agriculture.Keywords: Climate change, Agricultural transformation, Climate smart agriculture, Agricultural disaster, Agricultural investment

Introduction

Because agriculture is fully dependent on natural environment, climate change has a significant impact on agriculture [1,2]. First of all, abnormal climate change will affect the productivity of crops, or even completely change agricultural production mode [3,4]. Secondly, extreme weather caused by abnormal climate change, such as high temperature, frost, hail, storm, landslide and mudflow, will hit crops to cause crop yield reduction or no yield [5]. Finally, abnormal climate change has a great impact on water resources, as it can result in floods, droughts and desertification as well as seasonal and regional structural imbalance of water resources to make a comprehensive impact on agriculture [[6], [7], [8]].

On the other hand, agriculture is one of the sources of greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, thereby having an important impact on climate. According to data released by the World Resources Institute (WRI) (2019), agriculture accounted for 38 % of GHG emissions in the four sectors of Agriculture, Energy, Industrial Processes and Product Use, and Waste in 2016. GHG emissions from agricultural sector have increased by more than ten times from 1850 to 2016 [9]. More GHG in the atmosphere will lead to higher temperature on the earth, which will in turn affect climate change [[10], [11]], [12]]. In particular, the development of modern inorganic agriculture needs to consume a large amount of fossil fuels and energy, which has an increasingly prominent impact on climate change [[13], [14], [15]].

The above-mentioned problems will lead to the unsustainability of agriculture. Especially, current agriculture in China is still in the stage of development with high inputs, high energy consumption and high pollution, which consumes a large amount of inorganic chemicals such as fertilizers, pesticides, herbicides and agricultural films [[16], [17], [18]]. Furthermore, direct fossil energy consumption in agricultural sector is increasing with the development of agricultural mechanization [19]. In addition, insufficient agricultural infrastructure construction and poor agricultural production condition in China will lead to the lack of agricultural resilience [[20], [21], [22]]. Therefore, China has to promote's agricultural transformation to realize the sustainable development of agriculture under the background of climate change.

Against the backdrop of abnormal global climate change, the direction of agricultural transformation is to develop climate smart agriculture. The so-called climate smart agriculture has two meanings. One is to adapt to climate change to develop agriculture, which requires significant changes and transformations in agricultural varieties, technologies, production and management models. The second is to develop low-carbon agriculture as much as possible to reduce carbon emissions in the agricultural sector and its impact on climate [23].

The research objective of this paper is to theoretically and empirically analyze the impact of climate change on agriculture, as well as the patterns and quantitative relationships between them. Due to the frequent occurrence of abnormal climate change in recent years, it has had a significant impact on agricultural production. However, whether this impact is non-systematic or systemic requires in-depth exploration. On the other hand, facing the increasingly deteriorating climate and environment, China has invested enormous funds to improve agricultural infrastructure and production conditions. However, whether and to what extent it has played a significant role still requires in-depth research, as it is one of the feasible measures to address climate change.

Another objective of this study is to explore the transformation of Chinese agriculture and the development of climate smart agriculture under the background of climate change, based on the theoretical and empirical research results mentioned above, in order to provide theoretical basis for the future reform and development of agriculture in China.

This study finds that abnormal climate change can greatly harm agricultural production. At the same time, the construction of agricultural infrastructure and the improvement of production conditions are conducive to mitigating the harm caused by abnormal climate change. Therefore, it is necessary to vigorously develop climate smart agriculture and promote the transformation of traditional agriculture to alleviate the adverse effects of abnormal climate change on agriculture.

The main contribution of this study is to systematically demonstrate and test the synergistic effects of abnormal climate change and improved agricultural production conditions on agriculture and based on this, propose new ideas and systematic policy recommendations for China's agricultural transformation and the development of climate smart agriculture in the context of climate change.

The paper is structured as follows.Section 2 is literature review and hypothesis development. Section 3 presents data and model. Section 4 is empirical results. The discussion is demonstrated in Section 5. Section 6 is policy recommendations. The last is conclusions.

Literature review and hypothesis development

Climate change and agriculture

Due to the fact that climate directly determines the growth of crops, extreme climate change has a significant impact on agriculture [24,25]. Firstly, the growth of crops requires suitable environments such as light, air, and heat, and extreme climate change has altered these environments, thereby affecting the normal growth of crops [26,27].

Secondly, the growth of crops requires suitable water and soil resources, but extreme climate change not only affects the regional distribution of rainfall, but also leads to serious consequences such as floods, soil erosion, and soil desertification, resulting in drop in production, or no harvest of crops [[28], [29], [30]].

Finally, extreme climate change will change the overall structure of agricultural production. For example, crops that were originally suitable for growing in cold regions are no longer suitable for cultivation due to rising temperatures [31]. In addition, the quality of crops may also decrease due to extreme climate change. Therefore, extreme climate change will have a huge impact on regional agricultural production [32,33].

Based on the above theoretical analysis, the following hypothesis is made.

Climate change will have adverse effects on agriculture.

Investment in agricultural infrastructure and agricultural development

The function of agricultural infrastructure investment is that it can greatly improve agricultural production conditions, thereby reducing agricultural disasters and promoting agricultural production [34,35]. Firstly, agricultural infrastructure investment focuses on the construction of water conservancy facilities and watershed management, and the improvement of water conservancy conditions can fundamentally improve agricultural production conditions [36]. Therefore, investment in agricultural infrastructure will greatly promote agricultural production.

Secondly, farmland construction and protection are another important area of investment in agricultural infrastructure. Its investment and construction will help protect land resources and improve land quality, providing basic conditions for agricultural production. In addition, investment in geological remediation of agricultural disasters and the construction of farmland protection forest networks has greatly improved agricultural production conditions [37].

Therefore, the following hypothesis is given.

Investment in agricultural infrastructure is conducive to promoting agricultural production.

In fact, because temperature change will not only directly affect crop production, but also change the ecological condition and environment of agricultural production [44], such impacts on agriculture is comprehensive and far-reaching, thereby causing irreversible serious consequences to agriculture and food safety. The rise of the earth's surface temperature will have different impacts on different regions (including low latitude and high latitude regions). In the field of agriculture, in addition to developing eco-agriculture with low energy consumption, low emissions and high climate resilience, it is also necessary to innovate agricultural development model and find new agricultural solution.

Discussion

Further theoretical analysis on the impact of climate change on agriculture

The main manifestation of climate change is general rise in average temperature, seasonal abnormal change in temperature (such as warming in winter and cooling in summer) and the occurrence of annual temperature anomalies in some areas. Since agricultural production is directly determined by natural climate condition, climate change has a decisive impact on agriculture.

Climate change and agricultural ecological environment deterioration

Because temperature rise is conducive to the mass reproduction and growth of some harmful microorganisms, bacteria, insects and weeds, it will lead to serious agricultural diseases as well as insect pests and grass disasters. This is mainly because the temperature rise breaks the balance of original agricultural ecosystem and provides favorable condition for the occurrence of agricultural diseases, insect pests and weeds, which results in farmers to increase the use of pesticides and herbicides to prevent and control agricultural pests and weeds [53]. However, as a result, it not only increases the costs of agricultural production, but also leads to agricultural pollution and a decline in the quality of agricultural products. At the same time, it will worsen agricultural ecological environment.

Climate change will also lead to land desertification, soil erosion, deterioration of agricultural ecological environment and shortage of water resources. In order to deal with these problems, it is necessary to increase investments in soil and water conservation, comprehensive management of agriculture and agro-ecological environment. In particular, climate change will aggravate the imbalance of seasonal and regional distribution of water resources, which requires comprehensive management of water resources in the whole basin as well as vigorous development of facility agriculture. These will all lead to a significant increase in agricultural inputs and agricultural production costs [54]. Therefore, how to deal with the adverse effects of climate change on agriculture will be one of the important practical problems that human beings have to face in the future.

The impact of climate change on agricultural productivity

Extreme climate change will directly cause a serious drop in agricultural outputs. Rising climate will significantly shorten the growth period of major crops such as rice, wheat and corn, while the shortening of growth period will significantly reduce the accumulation time of high substances in crops, resulting in a decline in crop yield. At the same time, it will also affect the quality of existing agricultural products to some extent. This will pose a serious threat to most of China's major grain producing areas [55].

The quality of agricultural products will change due to change in temperature. Rising temperature may help improve the quality of some agricultural products, but it may also reduce the quality of other agricultural products. The quality of agricultural products in different regions will change due to climate change. This will have a far-reaching impact on the improvement of crop varieties and the change of planting methods [43,56].

Climate change and transformation of agricultural production structure

Change in temperature will lead to change in agricultural production pattern, including change in regional distribution, type and cropping system of crops. Rising temperature may make some cold areas of unsuitable planting for planting, while it may make some suitable planting areas unsuitable for planting, causing a serious drop in yield and profound change in regional agricultural production structure.

Because change in temperature will change crop type and cropping system in different regions, farmers have to choose new crop type and new planting system according to climate situation. In order to cope with extreme climate change, farmers have to rely heavily on agricultural facilities and artificial environmental intervention to maintain agricultural production. This will significantly increase agricultural inputs and agricultural production costs. At the same time, it will also lead to more GHG emissions. Obviously, the impact of climate change on agricultural production structure is extensive and far-reaching [57,58].

Three basic goals for the transition to sustainable agriculture

Food safety

Food safety problem is firstly manifested in food shortage. As China is a country with a large population, so its demand for food is huge. Although China's supply for food can basically meet food demand at this stage, it is likely to cause a serious food shortage crisis with the more and more serious impact of climate change on agriculture. Therefore, it is a difficult task to prevent food shortage crisis for a populous country like China [59].

Food nutrition and health are especially important for food safety, as contaminated and poor quality food will make serious threats to people's health. Generally, food pollution has two forms of endogenous pollution and exogenous pollution. Endogenous pollution is mainly caused by agricultural production itself. Heavy use of inorganic chemicals including chemical fertilizers, pesticides and herbicides directly causes pollution to agricultural products, such as pesticide residues in agricultural products over the standard. However, exogenous pollution is mainly caused by industrial and domestic wastes generated by industrialization and urbanization, such as heavy metals in agricultural products over the standard. In a word, food contamination will pose a serious threat to people's health.

The quality and nutrition of food are continuously declining at the present. On the one hand, current agriculture has generally changed from traditional organic agriculture to inorganic agriculture. Although the use of a large number of inorganic chemicals (especially chemical fertilizers) has significantly increased the yield of crops, it has also led to a decline in the quality of agricultural products. On the other hand, the rising climate will shorten the growth period of crops, which will affect the quality of agricultural products. Therefore, vigorously developing green agriculture and comprehensively promoting the production of healthy agricultural products with no pollution, high quality and high nutritional value, will be the inevitable requirement and basic task of agricultural development in the future [60].

GHG emissions reduction

As agricultural sector has become one of the important sources of GHG emissions, so the GHG emissions from agricultural sector have an increasingly significant impact on the earth's surface temperature [[61], [62], [63], [64]]. Different from GHG emissions from cities and industrial sectors, because the GHG emissions from the agricultural sector are of point source, it has a widespread impact [65]. This brings great challenge to the governance of GHG emissions in the agricultural sector. In order to effectively control the GHG emissions in the agricultural sector, it is necessary to thoroughly change agricultural production structure and mode, and comprehensively promote the development of climate smart agriculture with green, low carbon and climate resilience [[66], [67], [68]].

Agricultural GHG emissions include direct emissions and indirect emissions. Direct GHG emissions from agriculture are caused by agricultural animal and plant production and land use. Anaerobic decomposition of organic substances such as forage grass and feces of crops and livestock and microbial fermentation of livestock intestinal tract will produce a large amount of methane (CH4). Nitride in soil and fertilizer can produce a large amount of NO2 through microbial decomposition. Oxidative decomposition of organic soil (e.g., wetlands, peatlands and marshes), burning of crop straw, burning of mountainous and wasteland and forest fires, will cause a large amount of CO2 emissions.

Indirect GHG emissions from agriculture are caused by indirect agricultural production activities including agricultural inputs, agricultural outputs and change in land use structure. In terms of agricultural inputs, the use of pesticides, fertilizers, herbicides, plastic films and agricultural machinery consumes a large amount of natural resources and fossil energy, resulting in indirect GHG emissions. In terms of agricultural outputs, the storage, processing and transportation of agricultural products need to consume a large amount of natural resources and fossil energy, resulting in indirect GHG emissions. In terms of land use structure changes, the reclamation of forest land, grassland, wetland and coastal mudflat has reduced the carbon absorption capacity of agricultural sector and indirectly increased GHG emissions [69].

Because of warming global climate and increasing agricultural production in breadth and depth, GHG emissions from agricultural activities will become increasingly serious. The significant increase in GHG emissions from agriculture will aggravate climate warming, which will in turn have adverse effects on agriculture. Therefore, it is of strategic significance to vigorously develop low carbon agriculture and significantly reduce GHG emissions in agriculture.

Agricultural income growth

The income level of agricultural production is relatively low and unstable compared with other industries. This is not only because the efficiency of pure agricultural production itself is relatively low, but also because agricultural production is greatly influenced by natural factors. In fact, the impact of various natural disasters on agricultural production is very great. Just as the combined effect of many factors, so the income level of agricultural production is far lower than that of other industrial sectors [70].

The low level of agricultural production revenue will seriously dampen the enthusiasm of farmers and lead to the decline of agricultural production, thereby leading to the unsustainability of agriculture. In the long run, it will lead to food security problem. Therefore, The basis of promoting agricultural transformation and sustainable development is to significantly increase the income level of agricultural production. Only in this way, can China truly realize the sustainable development of agriculture to ensure food security.

Transitioning to climate smart agriculture for sustainable development

Against the backdrop of rising global temperatures, it has become an inevitable choice for agricultural transformation to actively respond to the challenges of climate change and comprehensively promote the development of climate smart agriculture [71].

The core of climate smart agriculture is to actively adapt to climate change and comprehensively improve agricultural comprehensive productivity. Furthermore, comprehensively to improve the resilience of agriculture to climate change can continuously increase agricultural productivity. Moreover, the production of high-quality agricultural products with no pollution and high nutritional value will be continuously increased under the condition of significantly reducing agricultural production inputs, agricultural energy consumption and agricultural GHG emissions. Finally, the win-win goal of agricultural ecological benefits, economic benefits and social benefits will be realized on the premise of ensuring food security, energy conservation and emissions reduction and agricultural production income growth.

Climate smart agriculture development focuses on three specific issues. The first is the innovation of climate smart agricultural technology. Climate smart agriculture highlights the climate adaptability of agriculture compared with traditional agriculture. It includes the adaptability to climate as well as the inherent requirements of energy conservation, emissions reduction and environmental friendliness. This puts forward a higher standard for technological innovation in climate smart agriculture [72].

The practitioners of climate smart agriculture are farmers. For farmers, whether they are willing and able to practice climate smart agriculture depends on at least two conditions. To begin with, whether climate smart agriculture project can improve the actual income level of agricultural production. If the actual income level of agricultural production cannot be significantly increased, farmers will not implement such a project. The second is whether farmers can master climate smart agricultural technology and have sufficient material and financial supports to implement the project. Otherwise, even if farmers have the will to promote it, it is also difficult to really carry out the project [73,74].

The above two issues determine whether the government can provide necessary and sufficient system and policy support. As the development of climate smart agriculture involves micro, meso and macro issues, so it is a complex system engineering, including regulation and system innovation, policy and incentive mechanism innovation, strategic planning and action plan formulation, and necessary financial and financial support.

Policy recommendations

Transformation of regional agro-ecological environment and production system

Adapting to climate change and combining regional agricultural natural ecological environment and resource condition, regional agricultural natural ecological system and regional agricultural production system should be integrated and optimized.

Transformation of regional agricultural natural ecosystem

The transformation and optimization of regional agro-ecological system includes two aspects of agricultural natural ecological environment and agricultural natural resource. In terms of agricultural natural ecological environment, it is mainly to adapt to climate change and tp combine with regional agricultural natural ecological environment condition to carry out purposeful manual intervention and transformation on regional agricultural ecological environment system, in order to create a good regional agricultural ecological environment system suitable for agricultural production.

In terms of agricultural natural resource, the focus is on soil and water conservation, farmland water conservancy construction and comprehensive watershed management to improve the supply and adaptability of agricultural natural resource [75]. The improvement of regional agricultural natural resource system is the basis for the improvement of natural ecological environment system quality of regional agriculture. In fact, the impact of climate change on agriculture is mainly through change in agricultural natural resource. Therefore, the transformation and optimization of regional agricultural natural resource system occupies a key position.

Transformation of regional agricultural production system

The first is the transformation and optimization of regional agricultural production sector system. Agricultural production sector includes planting, animal husbandry, fishery and forestry. There is interdependent and interactive between different agricultural production sectors. A good structural system of agricultural production sectors is not only conducive to improving agricultural productivity, but also conducive to creating a good agricultural ecological environment. Under the background of climate change, because the original structural system of agricultural production sectors will no longer be efficient, it needs to be reformed and optimized [76]. The core of transformation and optimization of regional agricultural production sector system is to enhance the climate adaptability and agricultural production efficiency of the entire agricultural production system.

The second is the transformation and optimization of the supply chain system of regional agricultural production. Agricultural production supply chain is an integrated functional network structure and mode that faces market demand and converts agricultural inputs into agricultural products and then to final consumption according to natural resource and environmental condition. It involves agricultural logistics (including service flows) as well as agricultural information flows and capital flows. It involves the supply of resource and energy at the front end of agricultural production and the agricultural production process at the middle end as well as the distribution and consumption of agricultural products at the back end. It involves the complicated division of labor and cooperation within agricultural production as well as the complicated close cooperation and trading relationship with external upstream and downstream enterprises [77,78]. It can be found that the transformation and optimization of regional agricultural production supply chain system is a complex social and economic system engineering.

Innovation and promotion of climate smart agriculture technology

Innovation and extension of agricultural biotechnology

The core of the innovation and promotion of climate smart agriculture biotechnology is to cultivate new agricultural varieties with strong stress resistance, high productivity and high nutritional value. The first is to adapt to the characteristics of climate change to make full use of the genetic potential of organisms, and actively to use modern bioengineering technology and other high-tech technologies to improve and create agricultural organisms, so as to cultivate new species of animals and plants with high yield, high quality and strong stress resistance, thus opening up a new way to improve agricultural productivity.

People's demand for organic green food with no pollution and full nutritional value is increasing with the improvement of people's living standard. Therefore, the innovation and promotion of climate smart agriculture biotechnology must aim at ensuring food safety and nutrition health, take low energy consumption, low emissions and no pollution as the standard, and take fine organic agricultural biotechnology as the support, so as to simultaneously improve the environmental and social benefits of agricultural production while improving agricultural biological productivity [79].

Innovation and extension of agricultural production technology

Farming, fertilization, irrigation, plant protection and other agronomic techniques occupy the core position in agricultural production technology system. Therefore, it is necessary to actively carry out research on the comprehensive matching technologies of modern farming, modern fertilization, modern irrigation and modern plant protection, to improve agricultural technology and comprehensive production level, and to realize the organic unity of saving resources, protecting environment and improving efficiency. At the same time, the innovation and promotion of agricultural water saving technology, energy saving and emission reduction technology, soil fertility cultivation technology and agricultural animal and plant residue treatment technology must be carried out, too [80].

Because agricultural products are easy to corrupt and difficult to preserve, it is extremely important to develop technologies such as preservation, storage, processing, packaging, transportation, sales and comprehensive utilization of agricultural products. At present, the actual utilization rate of primary agricultural products is relatively low, and most of them are discarded as waste. This not only causes waste of food, but also pollutes environment. Therefore, it is necessary to innovate and promote the preservation and processing technology of agricultural products [81].

Agricultural machinery plays an irreplaceable role in agricultural production. However, the use of agricultural machinery consumes a lot of energy and releases a lot of pollutants. Therefore, how to improve the efficiency of agricultural machinery is another important topic of agricultural production technology innovation and promotion. Because the basic requirement for technological innovation of agricultural mechanization is low energy consumption and high efficiency, it is necessary to adapt to the development needs of different regions and different types of agricultural mechanization to research and develop suitable agricultural machinery [82].

Innovation and promotion of agricultural energy saving and emission reduction technology

Agricultural energy conservation and emission reduction technology includes high-efficiency utilization technology of inorganic chemicals (such as fertilizers, pesticides and herbicides), water-saving agriculture technology, micro-energy agriculture technology, new agricultural material technology, agricultural waste treatment and utilization technology, agricultural carbon sequestration technology, and agricultural carbon sink technology. The popularization and application of these agricultural technologies are conducive to greatly reducing agricultural inputs and agricultural carbon emissions and mitigating the impact of agricultural development on climate [83].

Application of agricultural big data and internet of things technology

The material basis for the development of climate smart agriculture will gradually be available with the arrival of big data era and the continuous development of Internet of Things technology. If modern agricultural biotechnology, agricultural production technology, agricultural big data technology and agricultural internet of things technology can be organically combined, agricultural transformation and sustainable development will be fully promoted [[84], [85], [86]].

From the perspective of agricultural production process, various sensing devices can be used to collect information on the growth environment and situation of crops, while big data and Internet of Things technology can be used for remote monitoring, so as to better guide farmers to carry out fertilization, irrigation, weeding and plant protection work [87]. In fact, all agricultural production processes and links can realize intelligent operation in the era of agricultural big data and Internet of Things technology. This is the key point of climate smart agriculture development.

The application of agricultural big data and Internet of Things technology will also help to promote organic connection and integration between various subsystems within agricultural production system and between agriculture and other industries, and truly enhance the industrial status and market level of agriculture [84,88]. This is also an inevitable requirement for the development of modern large agriculture [89]. At present, agricultural e-commerce has made preliminary development, which has played a positive role in the sales of agricultural products. It can be predicted that the economic benefits, environmental benefits and social benefits of agriculture will be comprehensively improved with the continuous improvement of agricultural electronic informatization and intelligence.

Regulation and policy innovation

Relevant planning and policy innovation

The primary task of the innovation of climate smart agriculture system and policy is to comprehensively consider the needs of agricultural development, climate adaptability and mitigation of climate change, and to formulate relevant development goal, action plan and promotion policy for national and regional response to climate change. As the development of climate smart agriculture not only involves the overall situation of agricultural development but also is a long-term, so it is very necessary to formulate relevant strategic action plan and specific promotion policy.

At present, some countries and regions are promoting and implementing experimental climate smart agriculture projects. Judging from the implementation of the projects, although some achievements have been made, there are still many problems that need further improvement in technology and management. Actively drawing on the successful experience of these pilot projects is conducive to the formulation of the action planning and promotion policies of climate smart agriculture in line with the actual development of national and regional agriculture in China.

Formulation of relevant regulations and standards

As the development of climate smart agriculture involves the dissemination of knowledge, technology and management experience, as well as the carbon emission reduction of the projects, and the monitoring and evaluation of social and environmental benefits, so it is necessary to formulate a perfect regulation and standard of climate smart agriculture.

Construction of relevant incentive mechanisms

The development of climate smart agriculture requires the extensive participation of micro-agents such as farmers, enterprises, scientific research institutions, financial institutions and other relevant service institutions. Therefore, it is necessary to construct an effective incentive mechanism to encourage all parties to participate and share resources [90].

Establishment of technical standard and system

The establishment of the technical standard and system of climate smart agriculture has two functions of technical specification and technical guidance. In terms of technical specification, it may ensure the advancement and feasibility of technology to enhance the effectiveness of technology promotion and application. In the aspect of technology guidance, it will guide the researcher and developer of technology to establish the correct direction of technological research, and guide the popularization of technology and the selection of applicable technology by the user. They are important to the promotion and application of climate smart agriculture technology [91].

Financial supports

Funds are crucial to the development of climate smart agriculture and the promotion and implementation of the projects. In terms of funding source, government's financial support, environmental funds, low carbon funds, environmental service payments, carbon emission reduction transaction, government investment, private and social investment are all good financing channels. However, government's financial funds and support from various public welfare environment and low carbon funds are important financing channels in the early stage of the development of climate smart agriculture [92,93].

At present, China has fully started the development of low carbon economy, and a national unified carbon emission trading market system has formed. Further, carbon finance has also developed to a certain extent. Climate smart agriculture project can be incorporated into the carbon emission reduction trading system, and the project's carbon emission reduction, carbon sequestration and carbon sink capability can be used to raise funds. At the same time, private and social capitals must be encouraged to invest in climate smart agriculture project through incentives such as tax and investment incentives.

In the long run, payment for environmental services should be one of the important financing channels for the development of climate smart agriculture. China has a vast area, and environmental differences between different regions and between city and countryside are quite large. It is possible to establish and implement an environmental service payment system. At the present stage, environmental service payment system can be gradually established and implemented in combination with low carbon economy development.

Conclusions

Climate change has a significant impact on agriculture, which will worsen the natural ecological environment of agriculture, reduce agricultural productivity, and lead to the transformation of agricultural production structure. Therefore, it is necessary to adapt to climate change to promote agricultural transformation to sustainable development. The objectives of agricultural transformation are food safety, GHG emissions reduction and agricultural revenue growth, while the basic measure to achieve them is to comprehensively promote the development of climate smart agriculture.

In order to promote agricultural transformation and the development of climate smart agriculture, China must improve its agricultural operation and management system, accelerate agricultural technological innovation, actively promote the development of agricultural big data and informatization, and enhance agricultural financial support.

Theoretical implication

This study contributes to the existing literature on agricultural transformation and the development of climate smart agriculture. Firstly, we delved into the relationship between climate change and agricultural development and conducted empirical tests. Although existing literature has addressed this issue, there has been no systematic argumentation and testing. Therefore, our research enriches the existing literature.

Secondly, we identified the key factors affecting agricultural development in the context of climate change and theoretically and empirically tested the synergistic effect of climate change and agricultural infrastructure investment. Most existing literature studies their individual effects, with little in-depth exploration of their synergistic effects. Our work has made up for the lack of literature in this area.

Finally, the study demonstrated the basic goals of agricultural transformation and pointed out that in the context of climate change, the development of climate smart agriculture must be guided to promote agricultural transformation. Although there is an increasing number of literature in this area, there is a lack of systematic research. Our research not only enriches the existing literature, but also provides sufficient evidence from a new perspective.

Managerial implication

Against the backdrop of the increasingly significant impact of global climate change on agriculture, how to promote agricultural transformation, including the necessity, direction, and mode of agricultural transformation, has become a major practical issue that must be addressed. The theoretical achievements of this study can provide new ideas for China's future agricultural transformation and development.

This paper proposes feasible policy recommendations for China's future agricultural transformation and the development of climate smart agriculture from the perspectives of agricultural system, technology, big data and informatization, and regional agricultural systems. This has important reference significance for China's future agricultural reform and development.

Limitations and future research direction

The research scope of this paper is limited to China only. Although the research results are universal, they are not applicable to certain specific regions and development stages. Therefore, further in-depth research is needed for some specific cases.

This paper focuses on demonstrating the general laws of climate change, agricultural transformation, and climate smart agriculture. Therefore, in the empirical analysis, this paper only considers the impact of climate change and agricultural investment on agriculture, with the aim of testing whether climate change has a significant impact on agriculture. In fact, there are many factors that affect agricultural development. Therefore, in the future, research perspectives can be further expanded in order to make more findings.

Considering the availability of data, this paper only selected a limited sample range. Therefore, this paper only empirically tests the linear relationship between climate change and agricultural output. If the sample range is expanded, it is likely to result in non-linear relationships. This is the direction for further research in the future.

HEFT Numerators from Kinematic Algebra
Chih-Hao Fu, Pierre Vanhove, and Yihong Wang

Abstract
We derive the kinematic numerator factors for heavy-mass effective field theory from the field theory limit of the string theory vertex operator kinematic algebra introduced in [ 1 ] . The kinematic numerators are derived as correlators of nested commutators of gluon vertex operators evaluated between massive tachyonic vertex operators. The resulting numerators are given by products of structure constants of the vertex operator algebra which yield gauge invariant expressions. The computation of the nested commutators leads to a natural organisation in the form of rooted trees, endowed with an order that facilitates the enumeration of the various contributions. This kinematic algebra gives a string theory understanding of the field theory fusion rules for constructing the heavy-mass effective field theory numerator of [ 2 , 3 ] .

2
The kinematic algebra and structure constants

We start with the
\alpha^{\prime}
-deformed commutator between two vertex operators
[
62
,
63
]

[V_{1},V_{2}]_{\alpha^{\prime}}=V_{1}V_{2}-e^{-i\pi\alpha^{\prime}k_{1}\cdot k_{2}}V_{2}V_{1}.

(3)

We will be working with unintegrated vertex operators for the massive tachyonic external line in the
-1
-ghost picture

V^{\textrm{scalar}}(p)=:c(t)e^{ip\cdot X(t)}:,

(4)

and the integrated gluon vertex operators in the 0-ghost picture

V^{\textrm{vector}}(\epsilon,k):=\int_{0}^{1}dt:i\epsilon\cdot\partial_{t}X(t)e^{ik\cdot X(t)}:,

(5)

and the
-1
-picture gluon vertex operator

\tilde{V}^{\textrm{vector}}(\epsilon,k):=:c(t)i\epsilon\cdot\partial_{t}X(t)e^{ik\cdot X(t)}:.

(6)

Additionally,
we introduce a notation for the generators of kinematic algebra with the polarisation tensor removed for vectors

V^{\textrm{vector}}(\epsilon,k)=:i\epsilon_{\mu}E^{\textrm{vector},\mu}(k),

(7)

and its generalization for higher rank tensors, which we will meet below.

In operator representation
theory,
the commutator in (
3
)
is computed by considering the action of the vertex operators together
with the integration as the screening operators in conformal field
theory. This is
recognised as
a representation of the
q
-deformed Lie
algebra
[
64
]
, where the momenta are identified as root
vectors, and the deformation parameter is given by

q=e^{-i\alpha^{\prime}\pi}
. The generator of this algebra is discussed in
section
5.2.2
, where the Shapovalov form is used to
simplify the integration rules over the position of the vertex
operators.

We first explain how to calculate the commutator between different states,
and then we derive the kinematic structure constants.
In the following sections, we will only need to consider commutators between integrated vertex operators. Therefore, we will concentrate on this case.
When computing the commutators, we follow the convention introduced
in
[
58
]
, which is to position at a
small distance
i\delta
on the complex plane the vertex operator on the left in an operator product, and then continue analytically. The deformation parameter
q=e^{-i\alpha^{\prime}\pi}
allows us to
flip the Koba-Nielsen factor and combine the two terms in the commutator,
and the result is an integration of one vertex operator over a contour

\mathcal{C}
circling the other, as represented in Fig.
1
.
For example, the commutator of two scalars is

\displaystyle[V_{1}^{{\rm scalar}}(k_{1}),V_{2}^{{\rm scalar}}(k_{2})]_{\alpha^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}dt_{1}\int_{\Gamma_{2}^{+}}dt_{2}(t_{1}-t_{2})^{\alpha^{\prime}k_{1}\cdot k_{2}}:e^{ik_{1}\cdot X(t_{1})}e^{ik_{2}\cdot X(t_{2})}:

(8)

\displaystyle-e^{-i\pi\alpha^{\prime}k_{1}\cdot k_{2}}\int_{\Gamma_{2}^{-}}dt_{2}\int_{0}^{1}dt_{1}(t_{2}-t_{1})^{\alpha^{\prime}k_{1}\cdot k_{2}}:e^{ik_{1}\cdot X(t_{1})}e^{ik_{2}\cdot X(t_{2})}:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}dt_{1}\int_{\mathcal{C}}dt_{2}(t_{1}-t_{2})^{\alpha^{\prime}k_{1}\cdot k_{2}}:e^{ik_{1}\cdot X(t_{1})}e^{ik_{2}\cdot X(t_{2})}:.

Figure 1:
Integration contour of a screening operator.

When the commutator is inserted into an amplitude expression, branch cuts occur, so care must be taken when deforming the contour of integration.
In this section, to better illustrate the properties of kinematic
algebra, we will
consider first
two examples where all momenta are
restricted to the integer lattice
\alpha^{\prime}k_{i}\cdot k_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
so that we can close the contour in the absence of
branch cuts, and
then
a final example where we relax this condition.

(i)

For two scalar vertex operators
V^{\textrm{scalar}}(k_{1})
,
V^{\textrm{scalar}}(k_{2})
, the commutator
is zero unless
\alpha^{\prime}k_{1}\cdot k_{2}
is negative. The
first non-trivial example is given by states
whose
lattice momenta satisfy the condition
\alpha^{\prime}k_{1}\cdot k_{2}=-2
.
In this case equation (
8
) becomes

[V^{\rm scalar}_{1}(k_{1}),V^{\rm scalar}_{2}(k_{2})]_{\alpha^{\prime}}=\int_{0}^{1}dt_{1}\oint dt_{2}\frac{1}{(t_{1}-t_{2})^{2}}:e^{ik_{1}\cdot X(t_{1})}e^{ik_{2}\cdot X(t_{2})}:,

(9)

because
\alpha^{\prime}k_{1}\cdot k_{2}
is a negative integer we can deform
the contour of integration
\mathcal{C}
without meeting branch cuts, and the operator product expansion (OPE) of the vertices can be simply
computed via the residue theorem, to yield

[V^{\rm scalar}(k_{1}),V^{\rm scalar}(k_{2})]_{\alpha^{\prime}}=\frac{2\pi i}{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}\int_{0}^{1}dt_{1}:i\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}k_{2}\cdot X^{\prime}(t_{1})\,e^{i(k_{1}+k_{2})\cdot X(t_{1})}:,

(10)

where we have set
X^{\prime}(t)=\partial_{t}X(t)
.
So that

[V^{\rm scalar}(k_{1}),V^{\rm scalar}(k_{2})]_{\alpha^{\prime}}=V^{\rm vector}(\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}k_{2},k_{1}+k_{2}).

(11)

The result is the vertex operator of a vector state with
polarisation tensor
\epsilon=\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}k_{2}
and momentum
k=k_{1}+k_{2}
, which
satisfy the gauge and mass shell conditions
automatically
[
65
]
.
Using the notation introduced in (
7
) we can write this commutation relation as

[E^{\text{scalar}}(k_{1}),E^{\text{scalar}}(k_{2})]_{\alpha^{\prime}}=2\pi i\,k_{2,\mu}E^{\text{vector},\mu}(k_{1}+k_{2}),

(12)

so that with respect to this basis,
the structure constant is
2\pi ik_{2,\mu}
.

(ii)

We consider the commutator between the vertex operator of a scalar and a vector state such that

\alpha^{\prime}\,k_{1}\cdot k_{2}=-m
, where
m
is a large positive integer.

\displaystyle[V^{\textrm{scalar}}(k_{1}),V^{\textrm{vector}}(\epsilon_{2},k_{2})]

(13)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}dt_{1}\oint dt_{2}:\left(\frac{1}{(t_{1}-t_{2})^{m}}i\epsilon_{2}\cdot X^{\prime}(t_{2})+\frac{-1}{(t_{1}-t_{2})^{m+1}}k_{1}\cdot\epsilon_{2}\right)e^{ik_{1}\cdot X(t_{1})}e^{ik_{2}\cdot X(t_{2})}:.

The residue theorem then suggests that the two terms can be computed by
taking derivatives
2\pi i/(m-1)!\,\partial_{t_{2}}^{m-1}
and
2\pi i/m!\,\partial_{t_{2}}^{m}

before taking the limit
t_{2}\rightarrow t_{1}
. Suppose if we let the one
extra copy of derivative act, bringing down an
ik_{2}\cdot X^{\prime}(t_{2})

from the exponential, the two terms can then be combined with a common
denominator.
If we leave the operator as it is, without taking
the remaining derivatives explicitly, the result is a vector-like
vertex
operator
manifestly in gauge invariant form

\displaystyle[V^{\textrm{scalar}}(k_{1}),V^{\textrm{vector}}(\epsilon_{2},k_{2})]

(14)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\pi i}{m!}\int_{0}^{1}dt_{1}:\partial_{t_{2}}^{m-1}\left(\alpha^{\prime}\,ik_{1}\cdot F_{2}\cdot X^{\prime}(t_{2})e^{ik_{1}\cdot X(t_{1})}e^{ik_{2}\cdot X(t_{2})}\right):\Bigr|_{t_{2}\rightarrow t_{1}}.

If we evaluate the derivatives explicitly, because

m
is a positive integer,
the integrand be a sum of products of
derivatives
\{X^{\prime}
,

X^{\prime\prime}
,
X^{\prime}X^{\prime}
,
\dots
}. We conclude that the right-hand-side
of (
14
) contains a sum of contributions
to vectors and higher-rank tensors.

(iii)

As a third example,
we consider
the case of the commutator of two vertex operators for vector
states. We relax the condition that the momenta are on an integer
lattice, which is what we need for the heavy mass effective field
theory computation of section
5
. The
commutator evaluates as

\displaystyle[V^{\rm vector}(\epsilon_{1},k_{1}),V^{\rm vector}(\epsilon_{2},k_{2})]

(15)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}dt_{1}\int_{\mathcal{C}}dt_{2}\,(t_{1}-t_{2})^{\alpha^{\prime}k_{1}\cdot k_{2}}:e^{ik_{1}\cdot X(t_{1})}e^{ik_{2}\cdot X(t_{2})}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\times\left(\underset{(1)}{\underbrace{\frac{{\alpha^{\prime}\epsilon}_{1}\cdot\epsilon_{2}}{(t_{1}-t_{2})^{2}}}}\,+\,\underset{(2)}{\underbrace{\frac{{\alpha^{\prime}\epsilon}_{1}\cdot k_{2}}{t_{1}-t_{2}}(-1)\frac{\alpha^{\prime}\epsilon_{2}\cdot k_{1}}{t_{1}-t_{2}}}}+\,\underset{(3)}{\underbrace{\frac{{\alpha^{\prime}\epsilon}_{1}\cdot k_{2}}{t_{1}-t_{2}}\,i\epsilon_{2}\cdot X^{\prime}(t_{2})}}\right.

(16)

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt\left.+\,\underset{(4)}{\underbrace{(-1)\frac{\alpha^{\prime}\epsilon_{2}\cdot k_{1}}{t_{1}-t_{2}}\,i{\epsilon}_{1}\cdot X^{\prime}(t_{1})}}+\,\underset{(5)}{\underbrace{i{\epsilon}_{1}\cdot X^{\prime}(t_{1})\,i\epsilon_{2}\cdot X^{\prime}(t_{2})}}:\right).

We can recast all the newly introduced
\epsilon_{2}
dependent into
the field-strength

F_{2}^{\mu\nu}=\epsilon_{2}^{\mu}k_{2}^{\nu}-\epsilon_{2}^{\nu}k_{2}^{\mu}
.
By integrating by parts (IBP) the double pole term in
(1)
, it combines
with
(2)
and
(3)
; and we integrate by part
(4)
which combines with
(5)
. Using the notation (
7
) we obtain at leading order in
\alpha^{\prime}
,

[{\epsilon}_{1\,\mu}E^{\text{vector},\mu}(k_{1}),\epsilon_{2\,\nu}E^{\text{vector},\nu}(k_{2})]_{\alpha^{\prime}}=f^{[1]}{}_{\mu}E^{\text{vector},\mu}(k_{1}+k_{2})+f^{[2]}{}_{\mu\nu}E^{\text{tensor},\rho\eta}(k_{1}+k_{2}),

(17)

with the vector generator
being

E^{\text{vector},\mu}(k_{1}+k_{2}):=\int_{0}^{1}dt_{1}\int_{\mathcal{C}}dt_{2}\frac{1}{t_{1}-t_{2}}(t_{1}-t_{2})^{\alpha^{\prime}k_{1}\cdot k_{2}}:iX^{\prime\mu}(t_{2})e^{i(k_{1}+k_{2})\cdot X(t_{2})}:,

(18)

and
the tensor generator
is given by

E^{\text{tensor},\mu\nu}(k_{1}+k_{2}):=\int_{0}^{1}dt_{1}\int_{\mathcal{C}}dt_{2}(t_{1}-t_{2})^{\alpha^{\prime}k_{1}\cdot k_{2}}:iX^{\prime\mu}(t_{1})iX^{\prime\nu}(t_{2})e^{i(k_{1}+k_{2})\cdot X(t_{2})}:,

(19)

whereas the
structure constants
are

f^{[1]}{}_{\mu}=-\alpha^{\prime}(\epsilon_{1}\cdot F_{2})_{\mu},\qquad f^{[2]}{}_{\mu\nu}={\epsilon_{1\,\rho}(k_{1}\cdot F_{2})_{\eta}\over k_{1}\cdot k_{2}}.

(20)

We have kept the terms that will contribute to the leading order

\alpha^{\prime}\to 0
limit, which is enough for the field theory numerator
factors. For higher derivative corrections to the numerator factors as
considered in
[
41
]
, one would need to keep
contributions of higher order in
\alpha^{\prime}
.
This process generalises iteratively to the computation of nested commutators.
In particular, for the case of vertex operators for vector states with polarisation tensor
\epsilon_{i}
, after integration by parts of the modulus
t_{i}
, the structure constants will be multiplied by their
fieldstrength. The result is generically given by a sum of vector
and tensor-like vertex operators, with the new
F_{i}
lining up behind
the existing ones.

The result of nested commutators is a sum of tensor vertex operators multiplied by associated structure constants. One could wonder if these higher-order structure constants are needed or are redundant,
in the sense that we may be tempted to
combine the right-handside of (
17
) into a single term using a total derivative of the integrand as in (
14
). But this is
actually
not possible because the result is expanded on two different physical states.

3
The HEFT numerators from the kinematic algebra

The amplitude in (
1
) is the emission of
n
gluon of
momenta
k_{i}
and polarisation tensor
\epsilon_{i}
emitted by a
pair of massive particles of momenta, with momentum conservation and on-shell conditions

k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}=p^{\prime}-p,\qquad k_{i}^{2}=0,\qquad p^{2}=(p^{\prime})^{2},

(21)

which implies that

p\cdot\left(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}\right)=p^{\prime}\cdot\left(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}\right)=0.

(22)

The kinematic numerator factors of the colour-ordered Yang-Mills amplitude can be expressed as the average of the
n-1
permutations of the colour-ordered amplitude weighted by the momentum kernel
[
58
,
59
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(1,\sigma(2),\dots,\sigma(n))

(23)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{\prime-n}}{n}\sum_{\rho\in\mathfrak{S}_{n-1}}\mathcal{S}[\{1,\sigma(2),\dots,\sigma(n)\}^{T}|\rho]|_{p}\,A^{\rm YM}(1,\sigma(2),\dots,\sigma(n),p^{\prime},-p),

where
\mathcal{S}[\{1,\sigma(2),\dots,\sigma(n)\}^{T}|\rho]|_{p}
is the field theory momentum kernel
[
66
]
with
reference momentum
p
. We recall that the momentum kernel formalism applies to massive
states
as well, because it only depends on the scalar product of the momenta of the external particles and momentum conservation.

Following the steps of
[
1
]
, we see that the numerator factor is given by the expectation value of the commutator of the gluon vertex operators between the massive tachyon external state of momentum
p
and
p^{\prime}

\mathcal{N}(1,\sigma(2),\dots,\sigma(n))\cr=\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{-i}{\pi}\right)^{n-1}\lim_{\alpha^{\prime}\to 0}\alpha^{\prime-2n+1}\Bigl\langle p\Bigr|\left[\left[\left[V^{\textrm{vector}}_{1},V^{\textrm{vector}}_{\sigma(2)}\right]_{\alpha^{\prime}},V^{\textrm{vector}}_{\sigma(3)}\right]_{\alpha^{\prime}},\dots,V^{\textrm{vector}}_{\sigma(n)}\right]_{\alpha^{\prime}}\Bigl|p^{\prime}\Bigr\rangle.

(24)

In this expression, the vertex operators for the massive tachyonic scalar are fixed to the position
t_{n+1}=1
and
t_{n+2}=\infty
. The gluon vertex operator
V_{1}
is fixed at position
t_{1}=0
. For this gluon, we use the shifted gauge invariant polarisation

\bar{\epsilon}_{1}^{\mu}=\epsilon_{1}^{\mu}-\frac{p\cdot\epsilon_{1}}{p\cdot k_{1}}k_{1}^{\mu}={(p\cdot F_{1})^{\mu}\over p\cdot k_{1}},

(25)

so that it is orthogonal to the scalar momentum
p\cdot\bar{\epsilon}_{1}=0
.

We calculate the numerator factor
\mathcal{N}(1,2)
for the emission of two gluons from the massive scalar line.
The expression we need to evaluate is given by

\mathcal{N}(1,2)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{-i}{\pi}\right)\lim_{\alpha^{\prime}\to 0}\alpha^{\prime-3}\Bigl\langle p\Bigr|\left[V^{\textrm{vector}}_{1},V^{\textrm{vector}}_{2}\right]_{\alpha^{\prime}}\Bigl|p^{\prime}\Bigr\rangle.

(26)

Applying the result of (
17
), with the gluon
1

carring
the gauge invariant polarisation tensor
\bar{\epsilon}_{1}
in (
25
)
, we get

\mathcal{N}(1,2)=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{-i}{\pi}\right)\lim_{\alpha^{\prime}\to 0}\alpha^{\prime-2}{(p\cdot F_{1}\cdot F_{2})_{\mu}\over p\cdot k_{1}}\Bigl\langle p\Bigr|E^{\textrm{vector}\,\mu}(k_{1}+k_{2})\Bigl|p^{\prime}\Bigr\rangle\cr+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{-i}{\pi}\right)\lim_{\alpha^{\prime}\to 0}\alpha^{\prime-2}{(p\cdot F_{1})_{\mu}(k_{1}\cdot F_{2})_{\nu}\over k_{1}\cdot k_{2}}\Bigl\langle p\Bigr|E^{\textrm{tensor}\,\mu\nu}(k_{1}+k_{2})\Bigl|p^{\prime}\Bigr\rangle.

(27)

Note that the
contribution of the rank-2 tensor in the second line vanishes.
This is
because
|p\rangle
is a scalar tachyonic state,
and
the only surviving contribution is proportional to

p^{\mu}p^{\nu}\Bigl\langle p\Bigr|\int_{\mathcal{C}}dt_{2}(-t_{2})^{\alpha^{\prime}k_{1}\cdot k_{2}}:e^{ip\cdot X(1)}\,e^{ik_{1}\cdot X(0)}e^{ik_{2}\cdot X(t_{2})}:\Bigl|p^{\prime}\Bigr\rangle,

(28)

which
vanishes
when

with
{(p\cdot F_{1})_{\mu}(k_{1}\cdot F_{2})_{\nu}\over k_{1}\cdot k_{2}}
by antisymmetry of the field strength
F_{1}
.
The vanishing of the rank-2 tensor contribution is due to our choice
of reference momentum for the polarisation
\bar{\epsilon}_{1}
of gluon
1. We remark that the higher rank tensor contributes to the
numerator factor for the case of the emission of gluons from a massive fermion line discussed in section

In equation (
27

we are left with the vector contribution, which evaluates to

\mathcal{N}(1,2)=i\,{p\cdot F_{1}\cdot F_{2}\cdot p\over 2p\cdot k_{1}}.

This reproduces the numerator factor given in

.

At higher points,

\left[\left[\left[V^{\textrm{vector}}_{1},V^{\textrm{vector}}_{\sigma(2)}\right]_{\alpha^{\prime}},V^{\textrm{vector}}_{\sigma(3)}\right]_{\alpha^{\prime}},\dots,V^{\textrm{vector}}_{\sigma(n)}\right]_{\alpha^{\prime}}=\sum_{r=1}^{n}f^{[r]}_{\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r}}\,E^{\textrm{tensor},\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r}}(0).

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(1,\sigma(2),\dots,\sigma(n))

(31)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{-i}{\pi}\right)^{n-1}\lim_{\alpha^{\prime}\to 0}\alpha^{\prime-2n+1}\Bigl\langle p\Bigr|\left[\left[\left[V^{\textrm{vector}}_{1},V^{\textrm{vector}}_{\sigma(2)}\right]_{\alpha^{\prime}},V^{\textrm{vector}}_{\sigma(3)}\right]_{\alpha^{\prime}},\dots,V^{\textrm{vector}}_{\sigma(n)}\right]_{\alpha^{\prime}}\Bigl|p^{\prime}\Bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{-i}{\pi}\right)^{n-1}\lim_{\alpha^{\prime}\to 0}\alpha^{\prime-2n+1}\,\sum_{r=1}^{n}f^{[r]}_{\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r}}\,\Bigl\langle p\Bigr|E^{\textrm{tensor},\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r}}(0)\Bigl|p^{\prime}\Bigr\rangle.

where

and the rank

arises from the contraction for

,

]

5.1
The algorithmic implementation

This section details the algorithm used to calculate the
numerator factors. We calculate iteratively the nested commutator
[[E_{k_{1}}^{\text{vector}},E_{k_{2}}^{\text{vector}}]_{\alpha^{\prime}},\dots,E_{k_{n}}^{\text{vector}}]_{\alpha^{\prime}}
,
adding one gluon at a time. Generically we need to consider the OPE
of an
\ell
-th gluon
E_{k_{\ell}}^{\text{vector}}\left(\epsilon_{\ell},k_{\ell}\right)=\int dz_{\ell}:i\epsilon_{\ell}\cdot X^{(1)}(z_{\ell})e^{ik_{\ell}\cdot X(z_{\ell})}:

with the nested commutator

[[E_{k_{1}}^{\text{vector}},E_{k_{2}}^{\text{vector}}]_{\alpha^{\prime}},\dots,E_{k_{\ell-1}}^{\text{vector}}]_{\alpha^{\prime}},

(43)

which we assume to take the following general form similar to (
18
)
and (
19
).

\int_{T^{\ell-2}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell-1}dt_{i}\sum_{\alpha}g_{\alpha}\left(k_{1},k_{2},F_{2}\dots k_{n-1},F_{n-1}\right)\times I_{\alpha}\left(t_{1}\dots t_{n-1}\right)O_{\alpha}\text{$\prod_{1\leq j<i\leq\ell-1}\left(t_{i}-t_{j}\right)^{\alpha^{\prime}k_{i}\cdot k_{j}}$}.

(44)

In the above equation, we
collect momentum-dependent coefficients into
g_{\alpha}

and world-sheet variable-dependent coefficients
into
I_{\alpha}
and sum over the various contributions labelled by
\alpha
.
The operator
O_{\alpha}
is generically a higher-rank tensor given by the following product

:\prod_{j\in R_{\alpha}}\mathcal{T}_{j}\cdot X^{\prime}\left(t_{j}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{\ell-1}e^{ik_{i}\cdot X\left(t_{i}\right)}:,

(45)

over the set
R_{\alpha}
of indices and
the tensor
\mathcal{T}_{j}
is of the form
\epsilon_{*}\cdot F_{*}\cdots F_{j}
as discussed in section
5.3

The
Mathematica
code enumerates all possible contractions of (
45
) with the

\ell
-th gluon. We have the following three different
scenarios when a subset
Q\subset R_{\alpha}
of the tensor indices
contracts with the new vertex operator
:~\epsilon_{\ell}\cdot X^{\prime}\left(t_{\ell}\right)e^{ik_{\ell}\cdot X\left(t_{\ell}\right)}~:

(a)

One of the vectors
\mathcal{T}_{a}\cdot X^{\prime}\left(t_{a}\right)
,
a\in Q
,
contracts with
\epsilon_{\ell}\cdot X^{\prime}\left(t_{\ell}\right)
while
the rest contract with
e^{ik_{\ell}\cdot X\left(t_{\ell}\right)}
.

(b)

All vectors in
Q
contract with
e^{ik_{\ell}\cdot X\left(t_{\ell}\right)}

while
\epsilon_{\ell}\cdot X^{\prime}\left(t_{\ell}\right)
contract with

e^{ik_{b}\cdot X\left(t_{b}\right)}
,
b\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}\backslash Q
.

(c)

All vectors in
Q
contract with
e^{ik_{\ell}\cdot X\left(t_{\ell}\right)}

while
\epsilon_{\ell}\cdot X^{\prime}\left(t_{\ell}\right)
left uncontracted.

Let
q
be the element with the greatest index in
Q
, so the last term in (a)
reads

\left(-1\right)^{\left|Q\right|-1}\frac{\epsilon_{\ell}\cdot\mathcal{T}_{q}}{\left(t_{\ell}-t_{a}\right)^{2}}\prod_{i\in Q\backslash\{q\}}\frac{\mathcal{T}_{i}\cdot k_{\ell}}{t_{\ell}-t_{i}}\times:\prod_{j\in R\backslash Q}\mathcal{T}_{j}\cdot X^{\prime}\left(t_{j}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}e^{ik_{i}\cdot X\left(t_{i}\right)}:\prod_{1\leq j<i\leq\ell}\left(t_{i}-t_{j}\right)^{\alpha^{\prime}k_{i}\cdot k_{j}}.

(46)

Converting all the polarisation dependence to field-strength as in example (
15
) requires making some choice in applying the IBP, which complicates the algorithmic procedure. Therefore, we only perform IBP on the double pole of this term with respect to the new variable
t_{\ell}
.

The derivative may then act on the simple
poles of
t_{\ell}
, the operators, and the Koba-Nielsen factor. Each type
could be combined with terms in (a), (b), (c) respectively and rewritten
in terms of
F_{\ell}
:

(a’)

Differentiation on the simple poles combines with other terms in
(a) into the summation

\left(-1\right)^{\left|Q\right|}\sum_{i\in Q\backslash\{a\}}\frac{\mathcal{T}_{a}\cdot F_{\ell}\cdot\mathcal{T}_{q}}{\left(t_{\ell}-t_{a}\right)^{2}}\prod_{i\in Q\backslash\{a,q\}}\frac{\mathcal{T}_{i}\cdot k_{\ell}}{t_{\ell}-t_{i}}\times:\prod_{j\in R\backslash Q}\mathcal{T}_{j}\cdot X^{\prime}\left(t_{j}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}e^{ik_{i}\cdot X\left(t_{i}\right)}:\cr\times\prod_{1\leq j<i\leq\ell}\left(t_{i}-t_{j}\right)^{\alpha^{\prime}k_{i}\cdot k_{j}}.

(47)

(b’)

Differentiation on the normal ordered operators leads to a new tensor
term which combines with (b) into

\left(-1\right)^{\left|Q\right|}\frac{1}{\left(t_{\ell}-t_{q}\right)}\prod_{i\in Q\backslash\{q\}}\frac{\mathcal{T}_{i}\cdot k_{\ell}}{t_{\ell}-t_{i}}:\mathcal{T}_{q}\cdot F_{\ell}\cdot X^{\prime}\left(t_{\ell}\right):\times:\prod_{j\in R\backslash Q}\mathcal{T}_{j}\cdot X^{\prime}\left(t_{j}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}e^{ik_{i}\cdot X\left(t_{i}\right)}:\cr\times\prod_{1\leq j<i\leq\ell}\left(t_{i}-t_{j}\right)^{\alpha^{\prime}k_{i}\cdot k_{j}}.

(48)

(c’)

Differentiation on Koba-Nielsen factors combines with (c) into

\left(-1\right)^{\left|Q\right|-1}\left(\sum_{b\in\left[n\right]\backslash Q}\frac{k_{b}\cdot F_{l}\cdot\mathcal{T}_{q}}{\left(t_{\ell}-t_{b}\right)\left(t_{\ell}-t_{q}\right)}\right)\prod_{i\in Q\backslash\{q\}}\frac{\mathcal{T}_{i}\cdot k_{\ell}}{t_{\ell}-t_{i}}\cr\times:\prod_{j\in R\backslash Q}\mathcal{T}_{j}\cdot X^{\prime}\left(t_{j}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}e^{ik_{i}\cdot X\left(t_{i}\right)}:\prod_{1\leq j<i\leq\ell}\left(t_{i}-t_{j}\right)^{\alpha^{\prime}k_{i}\cdot k_{j}}.

(49)

The main function in our code is programmed to enumerate all terms of the three types listed above for any tensor and any subset
Q
. We then enumerate and sum over all possible subsets for all terms carried over from previous calculations. When the terms are collected, the result will naturally be in the same form as in (
44
) and available for the next step of iteration. To obtain the
n

n
-th fold, after which we calculate its OPE with the scalar vertex operator
:e^{ip\cdot X\left(t_{n+1}\right)}:
, keeping only the terms that satisfy the ascending order criteria
(explained in section
5.2
),
and then evaluate them by the integration rule (
61

5.3
polarisation dependence: lining up the gluons

A generic tensor term obtained by applying the algorithmic procedure of section
5.1
reads

\prod_{r}\mathcal{T}_{r}\cdot X^{\prime}(t_{r}),

(86)

where the tensors
\mathcal{T}_{r}
are the product of polarisation tensors
and field-strength.
Here
we do not write the poles, the Koba-Nielsen
factors and the plane-wave exponential factors, because we are
focusing on the tensorial pieces from the contractions of the vertex
operators.
As a consequence of the commutator relation presented before and the IBP procedure used in equation (
15
), the right-most factor of the tensors
\mathcal{T}_{r}
is always the field-strength
F_{r}
of the gluon with label
r
.

When taking the commutator with a new gluon vertex operator, the newly introduced polarisation tensor
\epsilon_{i}
can take one of the following three actions:

(i)

Becoming the last entry in the tensor, so that we get
\alpha^{\prime}\mathcal{T}_{r}\cdot F_{i}\cdot X^{\prime}(t_{i})
,

(ii)

Starting a new product term of its own
\epsilon_{i}\cdot X^{\prime}
,

(iii)

Contracting with one of the existing exponentials, which contributes a factor
\alpha^{\prime}\epsilon_{i}\cdot k_{j}/(t_{i}-t_{j})
.

All other alternatives lead to sub-leading contributions in
\alpha^{\prime}
or eventually to vanishing integrals because the graphs do not satisfy the ascending order condition.

The expressions derived in the previous section are expressed in terms of gluon polarisation tensors, and are not in explicit gauge invariant form. The numerator factors in (
23
) are by definition gauge invariant and can only be expressed in terms of field-strengths. We have two ways of doing this. One is to choose the reference momentum for each gluon vertex operator so that the polarisation tensor is given by
\bar{\epsilon}_{i}^{\mu}=(p\cdot F_{i})^{\mu}/(p\cdot k_{i})
, or systematically use integration-by-parts identities to pack the polarisation tensors into field-strengths. In both cases, the total derivatives
do not contribute to the result because when integrating over the contour
\mathcal{C}

the boundary term collides with
the massive (tachyonic) particle fixed at position
1
.
Since the resulting Koba-Nielsen factor contains a factor
(t_{i}-1)^{\alpha^{\prime}k_{i}\cdot k_{n+1}}
evaluated at
t_{i}\rightarrow 1
,
this gives a vanishing contribution.

Both ways of writing the expression in terms of field-strengths bring a lot of spurious poles. These can be reduced by combining the choice of the reference momentum and the IBP depending on the position of the vertex operator in the integration string.
This approach is presented in the next section.

6

7
Conclusion

We have given a derivation of the HEFT numerators from the
\alpha^{\prime}\to 0

limit of the kinematic vertex operator algebra introduced in
[
1
]
.
The construction of HEFT numerators for high multiplicity is a non-trivial task. This is partly due to the non-uniqueness of the numerator,
which
depends on the choice of gauge, and the large number of terms involved.
Because the numerator factors arise from multiple nested commutators
of
n
gluon vertex operators, they are organised in terms of a rooted
tree graph endowed with an ordering, which facilitates the
enumeration of the various contributions. We have written a

Mathematica
code for producing these numerator
factors. The code and examples are available at this repository
Stringy-Numerator
.

The field theory fusion rules of
[
2
,
3
]

were
derived from the field theory expression of the numerator factors which is the
\alpha^{\prime}\to 0
limit of (
23
). Consequently, the kinematic algebra detailed in section
3
gives a string theory origin of their construction.
Although, by definition, the numerator factors must be the same
gauge invariant objects, they can take algebraically different
forms. For example, the two constructions can have different sets of
spurious poles. Some of these poles can be removed by partial
fractioning and using the following identity involving the fieldstrength of gluon
i

A\cdot k_{i}\,\left(P\cdot F_{i}\over P\cdot k_{i}\right)\cdot B-A\cdot\left(P\cdot F_{i}\over P\cdot k_{i}\right)k_{i}\cdot B=A\cdot F_{i}\cdot B,

(95)

which is derived by using the gauge
choice in eq. (
25
) for the
gluon
i
with
reference momentum
P
not parallel to
k_{i}
.

We have presented
the construction of kinematic numerator factors from the vertex
operator algebra for the scalar line emissions case in
detail. We have explained how this extends to the
fermionic line case, and showed how to reproduce the results of
[
42
]
. The extension
to the multi-gluon emission from a massive tensor particles can be done along the same lines.
The nature of the massive external states only affects the final steps
when we evaluate
the expectation value.

Also, since the construction of the kinematic algebra is done in string theory, we can preserve the
\alpha^{\prime}
corrections to the numerator factors
[
41
]
, which could be useful for analysing the effects of higher derivative corrections from string theory on gravitational wave observables.

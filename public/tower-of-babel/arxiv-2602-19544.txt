Congruences for traces of singular moduli and Hurwitz - Kronecker class numbers
P. Guerzhoy

Abstract
. Traces of singular moduli were introduced and studied by Zagier in 1998. Being simultaneously the (traces of) values of a modular function ( j j -invariant) and Fourier coefficients of modular forms - which constitutes Zagier’s duality - these integers are quite interesting. Since then, a substantial amount of research was devoted to various properties of these numbers, congruences in particular. We present an alternative point of view on these congruences, specifically, we view them as congruences between certain weight 3 / 2 3/2 modular forms under repeated action of U U -operator. That allows us to obtain a general result which includes some previously known results as special cases. Our approach is especially effective when the prime modulus is relatively small. In these cases, we obtain explanations for certain numerical observations and quantification of some previously known qualitative results. As an application, we obtain modulo 11 11 congruences between the traces of singular moduli and class numbers of quadratic fields in the case when the twisted central special value of the L L -function associated with the elliptic curve of conductor 11 11 vanishes.

-invariant

as the Fourier coefficients

In this paper, we primarily concentrate on modular forms of weight

, and we say that a formal power series

belongs to the “Kohnen plus subspace” (satisfies Kohnen’s plus condition) whenever

(3)

n\equiv 1,2\pmod{4}~~\text{implies}~~a(n)=0.

Remark
.

In
[
28
]
, Zagier also defined (and interpreted in terms of the traces of singular moduli) a generalization

as the Fourier coefficients of the holomorphic part of

, where

is the weight
3/2

In this paper, in order to keep both the notations and the arguments lighter, we stick to the quantities

although analogous results may also be obtained for the quantities

mutatis mutandis.

Another type of congruences which hold for

was observed by Bruinier and Ono in
[

emploing Serre’s theory of

-adic modular forms.
In our notations, their result may be restated as,

\left(\frac{-d}{p}\right)=-1~~\text{or $0$ implies}~~B(1,p^{2n}d)=B(p^{2n}d)\equiv\frac{48}{1-p}H(d)\pmod{p^{n}}.

We want to emphasize that the condition for these congruences to hold (though only with

and a handful of small primes) complements the conditions in (

That hints that one may benefit from packing all these congruences into a generating function: it is the power series

which should be modulo

coefficient-wise congruent
to a certain formal power series in

whose coefficients are sometimes zeros, while sometimes, (at least, in the cases when

Here and throughout we denote by

the

-operator acting on formal power series as

\left.\left(\sum_{n\gg-\infty}a(n)q^{n}\right)\right|U=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}a(pn)q^{n}.

Our result (see Theorem

Let

Recall that

.
(Note also that the Kohnen plus subspace in

Following later work by Kohnen

We say that a formal power series

It is proved in

Observe that the finite-dimensional

As subspaces,

We thus can consider the

2.
Modified Zagier - Eisentstein series

Let

be a prime. Recall that we denote by

the Kronecker - Hurwitz class number. Namely, if

, and
-{\mathfrak{r}}<0
is a fundamental discriminant, then

H(n)=\frac{h({\mathfrak{r}})}{w({\mathfrak{r}})}\sum_{m|f}\mu(m)\left(\frac{-{\mathfrak{r}}}{m}\right)\sigma_{1}(f/m),

where

is the class number of and

is half of the number of units in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-{\mathfrak{r}}})
. (We set

).
In
[
27
]
, the transformation law on
\Gamma_{0}(4)
of the generating function

{\mathcal{H}}=-\frac{1}{12}+\sum_{n>0}H(n)q^{n},

which is not a modular form, was studied.

Besides the standard

\left.\left(\sum_{m\gg-\infty}a(m)q^{m}\right)\right|U=\sum_{m\gg-\infty}a(pm)q^{m},~~~~\left.\left(\sum_{m\gg-\infty}a(m)q^{m}\right)\right|V=\sum_{m\gg-\infty}a(m)q^{pm},

we will need the twist with a Dirichlet character
\xi

\left(\sum_{m\gg-\infty}a(m)q^{m}\right)\otimes\xi=\sum_{m\gg-\infty}a(m)\xi(m)q^{m}.

The action of the weight
3/2
Hecke operator
T_{\ell^{2}}
for a prime

f|T_{\ell^{2}}=f|U_{\ell}^{2}+f\otimes\chi_{\ell}+pf|V_{\ell}^{2},

where
\chi_{\ell}(m)=\left(\frac{-m}{\ell}\right)
.
Note that, while
{\mathcal{H}}
is not a modular form, it follows from (

) that the formal power series
{\mathcal{H}}
is a common eigenseries of

for all odd primes

{\mathcal{H}}|T_{\ell^{2}}=\sigma_{1}(\ell){\mathcal{H}}=(\ell+1){\mathcal{H}}.

Constructions of closely related series
\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}
which is a modular form (of weight

) are presented in

We want to emphasize an analogy with the situation in weight

\mathbb{G}_{2}=-\frac{1}{24}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\sigma_{1}(n)q^{n}

\tilde{\mathbb{G}}_{2}:=\mathbb{G}_{2}-p\mathbb{G}_{2}|V=(1-p)\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{G}_{2}|U^{m}\in M_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(p)).

(The limit is taken in the

-adic topology defined by the norm
||\sum a(n)q^{n}||_{p}=p^{-\inf_{n}\ord_{p}(a(n))}
.)
Observe that, while
\mathbb{G}_{2}
is not a modular form, the formal power series is a common eigenseries of all the weight

\mathbb{G}_{2}|T_{\ell}=\sigma_{1}(\ell)\mathbb{G}_{2}=(\ell+1)\mathbb{G}_{2},

and so is

, with the same Hecke eigenvalues as
\mathbb{G}_{2}
,except at

\tilde{\mathbb{G}}_{2}|U=\tilde{\mathbb{G}}_{2}.

We now let
\chi_{p}(m)=\left(\frac{-m}{p}\right)

\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}={\mathcal{H}}-{\mathcal{H}}\otimes\chi_{p}-p{\mathcal{H}}|V^{2}.

The following proposition, in particular, establishes a parallelism between our construction of
\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}
and its properties and the construction and properties of
\tilde{\mathbb{G}}_{2}
sketched above.

Proposition 1
.

(i)

We have that

\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}=(1-p)\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}{\mathcal{H}}|U^{2m}.

Furthermore,
\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}|T_{\ell^{2}}=(l+1)\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}
for all odd primes
\ell\neq p
while
\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}|U^{2}=\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}
.

(ii)

We have that
\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}\in M_{3/2}(\Gamma_{0}(4p))\cap{\mathfrak{M}}^{-}
.

While the proposition and its proof are obviously related to
[
6
, Lemma 2.6]
, the two statements are distinct: neither one implies another one.

Proof.

In order to prove (i), we firstly observe that for
\ell\neq p
the equality
\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}|T_{\ell^{2}}=(l+1)\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}
follows at once rom
{{\mathcal{H}}}|T_{\ell^{2}}=(l+1){{\mathcal{H}}}
.

We will need the obvious identities

(8)

({\mathcal{H}}|V^{2})\otimes\chi_{p}=0,~~({\mathcal{H}}\otimes\chi_{p})|U^{2}=0,~~{\mathcal{H}}|V^{2}U^{2}={\mathcal{H}}.

Furthermore, since
{\mathcal{H}}|T_{p^{2}}=(p+1){\mathcal{H}}
, we find that

p{\mathcal{H}}=-{\mathcal{H}}+{\mathcal{H}}|U^{2}+{\mathcal{H}}\otimes\chi_{p}+{\mathcal{H}}|V^{2}.

Using the above identities we find that

\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}|U^{2}={\mathcal{H}}|U^{2}-p{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}-{\mathcal{H}}\otimes\chi_{p}-p{\mathcal{H}}|V^{2}=\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}.

Now let

\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}={\mathcal{H}}-\frac{1}{p}{\mathcal{H}}\otimes\chi_{p}-{\mathcal{H}}|V^{2}.

In a similar way, we find that

\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}|U^{2}={\mathcal{H}}|U^{2}-{\mathcal{H}}=p{\mathcal{H}}-{\mathcal{H}}\otimes\chi_{p}-p{\mathcal{H}}|V^{2}=p\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}.

Note that
\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}-p\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}=(1-p){\mathcal{H}}
. Acting with
U^{2}
on this identity repeatedly we finish our proof of (i).

In order to prove (ii), recall that, by
[
27
, Théorème 2]
, the non-analytic function

Recall that

The following proposition summarizes some immediate corollaries from a result of Jenkins
[

B(D,p^{2n}d)=p^{n}B(p^{2n}D,d)\\
+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)^{n-k-1}\left(B(D/p^{2},p{{}^{2k}}d)-p^{k+1}B(p^{2k}D,d/p^{2})\right)\\

Proposition 2
.

Let
D=p^{2t}D_{0}
with
p^{2}\nmid D_{0}
, and
let
\epsilon=-\left(\frac{D_{0}}{p}\right)
.

(i)

The
p
-adic limit

\tilde{g}_{D}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(-1)^{n\epsilon(\epsilon+1)/2}g_{D}|U^{2n}

exists.

(ii)

If
\epsilon\neq 0
, then
\tilde{g}_{D}\in{\mathfrak{M}}^{\epsilon}
.

(iii)

If
p|D_{0}
then
\tilde{g}_{D}=0
.

(iv)

If
p^{2}|D
, then we have the reduction
g_{D}|U^{2n}\equiv g_{D/p^{2}}|U^{2n-2}\pmod{p^{n-1}}

Proof.

We start with the observation that, by definition,
m=1,2\pmod{4}
implies
B(D,m)=0
, and, since
p^{2}\equiv 1\pmod{4}
,
the limit series
\tilde{g}_{D}
, if exists, satisies (
3
).

In order to prove (iv), assume that
p^{2}|D
and observe that (
10
) implies, since
\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)=0
, that

Statement (iv) follows from that, and induction in
t
now allows us to prove statements (i), (ii), and (iii) under the assumption that

(i.e.
p^{2}\nmid D
).

We now assume that
p||D
. Thus
\epsilon=-\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)=0
, and the sums in (

B(D,p^{2n}d)\equiv-\left(\frac{-d}{p}\right)p^{n-1}B(p^{2k}D,d)\pmod{p^{n}}.

Thus
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}B(D,p^{2n}d)=0
for every

Assertion (i) in the case
\epsilon=0

We now consider the two cases

Assume firstly that
\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)=1
(thus
\epsilon=-1

10

B(D,p^{2n}d)=p^{n}B(p^{2n}D,d)\\
-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}p^{k+1}B(p^{2k}D,d/p^{2})+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(1-\left(\frac{-d}{p}\right)\right)p^{k}B(p^{2k}D,d),

and implies that

exists for every

, therefore the limit
\tilde{g}_{D}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}g_{D}|U^{2n}
exists.
Furthermore, if
\left(\frac{-d}{p}\right)=1

, therefore
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}B(D,p^{2n}d)=0

in other words,
\tilde{g}_{D}\in{\mathfrak{M}}^{-}
as required. That finishes the proof of (i) and (ii) under the assumption that
\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)=1
.

Assume now that
\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)=-1

\epsilon=1
). Then (

B(D,p^{2n}d)=p^{n}B(p^{2n}D,d)\\

\equiv(-1)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}p^{k}\left(pB(p^{2k}D,d/p^{2})+\left(1+\left(\frac{-d}{p}\right)\right)B(p^{2k}D,d)\right)\pmod{p^{n}},

and implies that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(-1)^{n}B(D,p^{2n}d)
exists for every

, therefore
\tilde{g}_{D}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(-1)^{n}g_{D}|U^{2n}

Furthermore, if
\left(\frac{-d}{p}\right)=-1

as required. That finishes the proof of (i) and (ii) under the assumption that

Proof of Theorem
3
.

In order to prove Theorem
3
, we observe that, by
[
21
, Proposition 5.1]
, the operator

U_{p^{2}}
acting on
\tilde{S}_{3/2}(4p,{\mathbb{Q}}_{p},p^{-r})
for
1/(p+1)<r<p/(p+1)
is compact, therefore has a discrete spectrum, and invoke the asymptotic expansion lemma
[
9
, Proposition 1]
. Specifically, in our case, since the closedness in the Banach space norm implies the closedness of the
q
-expansions,
[
9
, Proposition 1]
implies that

\ord_{p}\left(\tilde{g}_{D}-g_{D}\right)|U^{2n}={\mathcal{O}}(sn)~~\text{as}~~n\rightarrow\infty,

where
s
is the smallest non-zero slope in the spectrum of
U_{p^{2}}
acting on the Banach space
\tilde{S}_{3/2}(4p,{\mathbb{Q}}_{p},p^{-r})
. It follows from
[
22
, Lemma 5.3]
that every slope in the spectrum of
U_{p^{2}}=U^{2}
acting on

\tilde{S}_{3/2}(4p,{\mathbb{Q}}_{p},p^{-r})
appears as a slope of the spectrum of the
U_{p}=U
acting on the Banach space

\tilde{S}_{2}(p,{\mathbb{Q}}_{p},p^{-r})
of weight
2
overconvergent forms. Thus the problem to find the minimal slope
s
reduces to calculating the minimal slope in the space

S_{2+(p-1)p^{A}}(SL_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}))
of classical cusp forms of trivial level and weight
2+(p-1)p^{A}
with
A
big enough. We use the explicit estimate obtained by Wan
[
25
]
in order to pick the appropriate values of
A
, and computer calculations to find the minimal slope
s
in the specific spaces of the integral weight classical cusp forms. For the latter, we used the GP calculator
[
29
]
.
That finishes the proof of Theorem
3
. ∎

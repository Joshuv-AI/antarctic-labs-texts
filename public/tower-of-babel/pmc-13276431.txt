Automated detection of defective coffee beans based on improved YOLOv10 framework
Authors: Sunyan Hong, Dengji Zhang, Haiyang Chi, Jun He, Xiudong Guo, Hui Fu, Lu Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCoffee quality assessment is critical to the global agricultural economy, yet traditional manual inspection remains labor-intensive, subjective, and prone to inconsistency. This study presents an improved YOLOv10-based framework specifically engineered for the automated detection of defective green coffee beans, achieving an optimal balance between high-precision localization and real-time edge deployment constraints. The proposed architecture integrates three targeted structural innovations: Depthwise Separable Convolution (DSConv) with distribution shift for lightweight, fine-grained feature extraction; a Spatial Pyramid Pooling Fast with Attention (SPPF_Attention) module for context-aware multi-scale feature refinement; and Partial Convolution (PConv) operators in the detection head to mitigate computational redundancy and enhance robustness against dense occlusions. Comprehensive evaluations utilized a rigorously curated dataset covering seven defect categories compliant with Specialty Coffee Association (SCA) standards. Evaluated under a 5-fold cross-validation protocol, the framework achieved state-of-the-art performance with 99.2% mAP@50, 98.5% precision, and 98.8% recall, alongside a rapid inference latency of merely 2.0 ms. These metrics denote substantial improvements over the advanced YOLOv11 baseline, simultaneously reducing model parameters by 21.6%, decreasing model size by 12.4%, and accelerating inference by 33.3%. Furthermore, systematic ablation studies validate the incremental contributions of each architectural component, while EigenCAM visualizations confirm that the integrated attention mechanisms successfully steer the network toward semantically meaningful defect regions and suppress background interference. Extensive cross-dataset analyses additionally demonstrate the model’s exceptional generalization capability across diverse processing states, establishing this framework as a highly viable, scalable solution for resource-constrained industrial sorting facilities.Keywords: Coffee bean defect detection, Image processing, Object recognition, YOLOv10

Introduction

Coffee, as one of the world’s most consumed beverages and economically significant agricultural commodities, plays a vital role in the global economy, particularly for developing nations where it serves as a primary export product. The quality of coffee beans directly influences the flavor profile, market value, and consumer satisfaction of the final product, making accurate quality assessment essential throughout the coffee production chain Hassan (2024). According to the Specialty Coffee Association, even minor defects in green coffee beans can significantly compromise the final cup quality, potentially leading to substantial economic losses for producers and processors. Traditional quality control methods predominantly rely on manual visual inspection by trained experts, a process that is inherently labor-intensive, time-consuming, and subject to human variability due to fatigue, inconsistent lighting conditions, and subjective judgment (García et al., 2019). Moreover, manual handling during the sorting process can cause physical damage to beans, further compromising their appearance quality and commercial value. These limitations have created an urgent need for automated, objective, and reliable coffee bean quality assessment systems that can maintain consistent standards while improving operational efficiency and reducing production costs.

Early attempts at automating coffee bean classification primarily employed traditional machine learning approaches combined with handcrafted feature extraction techniques. For example, Arboleda et al. (2018) developed a classification system using Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) and K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN) algorithms, extracting morphological features such as area, perimeter, shape descriptors, and color attributes from coffee bean images to achieve a classification accuracy of 96.66%. While demonstrating the feasibility of automated classification, these traditional methods remained heavily dependent on manually designed feature extractors, required extensive domain expertise for feature engineering, and exhibited limited robustness when confronted with varying imaging conditions or complex defect patterns (de Oliveira et al., 2016). The fundamental limitation of these early approaches lay in their inability to automatically learn hierarchical feature representations (Chi et al., 2020, Chi et al., 2025c), necessitating careful manual design of feature extraction pipelines that often failed to capture the subtle visual characteristics distinguishing different quality grades or defect types.

The emergence of deep learning, particularly Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), has revolutionized computer vision applications in agriculture, offering the ability to automatically extract hierarchical features from raw image data without requiring manual feature engineering (Chen et al., 2025). In coffee bean quality assessment, several studies have demonstrated the superior performance of CNN-based approaches over traditional methods. For instance, Huang et al. (2020) proposed a real-time CNN-based classification method that expanded the dataset to 72,000 images through data augmentation techniques, ultimately attaining a classification accuracy of 93.34%. This approach not only enhanced classification precision but also reduced computational costs through model architecture optimization, enabling deployment in resource-constrained environments. Additionally, Gope and Fukai (2022) implemented CNNs on Raspberry Pi platforms to distinguish normal coffee beans from flat beans, achieving accuracy surpassing 98%. Furthermore, Korkmaz et al. (2025) conducted a comprehensive comparison of deep learning models for automatic classification of coffee bean species, employing five CNN-based architectures on a dataset comprising 1554 coffee bean images across three species, with InceptionV3 achieving the highest classification accuracy of 93% and precision of 95%. These studies collectively demonstrate the promising applications of deep learning techniques in coffee bean classification and detection.

While CNN-based classification models have achieved remarkable success in coffee bean quality assessment, the industry increasingly demands real-time detection capabilities that can identify and localize defects within complex production environments. This requirement has driven attention toward object detection frameworks, with the You Only Look Once (YOLO) series emerging as the predominant paradigm due to its exceptional balance between detection accuracy and computational efficiency (Badgujar et al., 2024). The YOLO family has undergone continuous evolution, with each iteration introducing architectural innovations to improve both performance and efficiency. YOLOv7 (Wang et al., 2023) introduced trainable bag-of-freebies that set a new state-of-the-art for real-time object detectors, YOLOv8 (Sohan et al., 2024) enhanced the framework with improved C2f modules and anchor-free detection heads, while YOLOv9 (Chien-Yao Wang et al., 2024) introduced Programmable Gradient Information (PGI) to address information loss in deep networks and Generalized Efficient Layer Aggregation Network (GELAN) for improved feature aggregation. Most recently, YOLOv10 (Ao Wang et al., 2024) achieved state-of-the-art performance by introducing Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS)-free training through consistent dual assignments, eliminating the post-processing bottleneck that has long hampered real-time deployment, and implementing holistic efficiency-accuracy driven model design strategies, including lightweight classification heads, spatial-channel decoupled downsampling, and rank-guided block design. Ao Wang et al. (2024) demonstrated that YOLOv10-S is 1.8 times faster than RT-DETR-R18 under similar Average Precision (AP) on the COCO dataset, while having 2.8 times fewer parameters and floating-point operations (FLOPs).

Several studies have successfully applied YOLO variants to coffee bean detection and classification tasks (Adiwijaya et al., 2024, Gope et al., 2025), demonstrating their practical applicability in agricultural settings. More recently, comprehensive comparative analyses of YOLO models have been conducted for coffee bean applications, highlighting the effectiveness of customized architectures for specific detection tasks. These applications demonstrate the growing maturity and practical viability of YOLO-based approaches for coffee bean quality control in industrial production environments (Ruangsang et al., 2026). Despite these advances, several challenges remain: (1) balancing detection accuracy with inference speed for real-time processing, (2) achieving sufficient model compactness for deployment on low-power embedded systems, (3) maintaining robust performance across varying lighting conditions and bean orientations, and (4) accurately detecting subtle defects that may have similar visual characteristics to normal bean features.

In response to these challenges, this study proposes an improved YOLOv10 framework tailored specifically for defective coffee bean detection, with a strong emphasis on practical deployment. The proposed methodology pursues three core objectives: improving detection accuracy across diverse defect categories via targeted architectural refinements, sustaining real-time inference capabilities for industrial environments, and designing a lightweight architecture highly compatible with resource-constrained embedded systems. The primary contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

•We address the stringent computational constraints of industrial edge deployment by incorporating Depthwise Separable Convolution (DSConv) (Howard et al., 2017, Nascimento et al., 2019) into the backbone network. Related work

The automated detection of defective coffee beans remains a critical challenge in agricultural computer vision, where methodologies have transitioned from labor-intensive manual inspection to sophisticated deep learning paradigms. This section systematically reviews the progression of these techniques, categorized into four primary research domains: the transition from traditional machine learning to deep learning models, the evolution of YOLO-based object detection architectures, the integration of attention mechanisms and multi-scale feature fusion, and lightweight network design strategies tailored for edge deployment.

From traditional methods to deep learning

The automation of coffee bean quality assessment has evolved significantly over the past decade, transitioning from traditional image processing techniques to sophisticated deep learning architectures. Early research predominantly relied on handcrafted feature extraction combined with shallow machine learning algorithms. de Oliveira et al. (2016) developed a computer vision system for coffee bean classification based on computational intelligence techniques, extracting color, morphology, and texture features from bean images. Their system achieved classification accuracies exceeding 90% under controlled conditions, demonstrating the feasibility of automated quality assessment. García et al. (2019) proposed a quality and defect inspection system for green coffee beans using k-nearest neighbor algorithms, successfully identifying various defect types through morphological and color descriptors. However, these traditional approaches exhibited fundamental limitations: they required extensive domain expertise for feature engineering, remained sensitive to imaging condition variations, and struggled to capture subtle defect characteristics that distinguish quality grades (Santos et al., 2020). The dependence on manually designed features inherently constrained their generalization capability across diverse coffee varieties and defect manifestations.

The advent of deep convolutional neural networks marked a transformative shift in coffee bean quality assessment methodologies. Chang and Huang (Chang and Huang, 2021) developed a deep learning model based on AlexNet for coffee bean defect inspection across eight defect categories, achieving 95.2% overall accuracy and 100% binary classification accuracy for defective versus non-defective beans. This work demonstrated that CNNs could automatically learn hierarchical feature representations superior to handcrafted features. Hsia et al. (2022) proposed an explainable lightweight deep CNN incorporating depthwise separable convolution and squeeze-and-excitation blocks for green coffee bean quality detection, achieving an F1-score of 97.21% while maintaining model interpretability. The integration of semi-supervised learning with attention mechanisms enabled their model to leverage both labeled and unlabeled data effectively, addressing the challenge of limited annotated datasets in specialized agricultural domains. More recently, Ke et al. (2023) introduced shift-invariant features combined with non-local blocks for green coffee bean defect detection, enhancing the model’s ability to capture long-range dependencies within bean surface patterns.

Recent comprehensive reviews have systematically analyzed the landscape of machine learning applications in coffee classification. Motta et al. (2024) conducted an extensive review of machine learning techniques for coffee classification, analyzing studies spanning various aspects, including roast level classification, species identification, and quality grading. Their analysis revealed that pre-trained CNN architectures, particularly when fine-tuned with transfer learning strategies, consistently outperform models trained from scratch, especially when dealing with limited dataset sizes. Hassan (Hassan, 2024) performed a comparative analysis of ten pre-trained deep learning models, including AlexNet, LeNet, HRNet, GoogLeNet, MobileNetV2, ResNet-50, VGG, EfficientNet, DarkNet, and DenseNet for coffee bean classification, demonstrating that model selection significantly impacts classification performance, with certain architectures achieving substantially higher accuracy and better generalization capabilities (Chi et al., 2025b). These comparative studies collectively establish that deep learning approaches have fundamentally surpassed traditional methods, motivating the research community to focus on optimizing neural network architectures for specific deployment scenarios, including real-time processing, resource-constrained environments, and edge computing applications prevalent in industrial coffee processing facilities (Xie et al., 2024, Lu et al., 2023, Wu et al., 2025).

The rise of YOLO architectures

While classification models have achieved remarkable success in coffee bean quality assessment, industrial applications increasingly demand real-time object detection capabilities that can simultaneously identify, localize, and classify defects within complex production environments. The YOLO series has emerged as the predominant paradigm for agricultural object detection due to its exceptional balance between detection accuracy, inference speed, and computational efficiency (Badgujar et al., 2024). Unlike two-stage detectors such as Faster R-CNN (Ren et al., 2015) that separate region proposal and classification, YOLO performs detection in a single forward pass, making it particularly suitable for real-time applications. Badgujar et al. (2024) conducted a comprehensive bibliometric and systematic literature review analyzing 257 articles on YOLO algorithm applications in agriculture, identifying key advantages including real-time detection capabilities essential for automated harvesting and sorting systems, favorable accuracy-efficiency trade-offs suitable for resource-constrained embedded devices, and extensive community support providing pre-trained models and implementation frameworks.

The YOLO architecture has undergone continuous evolution, with each iteration introducing significant innovations to address performance bottlenecks and expand applicability. Several studies have successfully adapted YOLO variants specifically for coffee bean detection and classification tasks. Luis et al. (2022) utilized YOLO for classifying defects in Robusta green coffee beans, successfully identifying multiple defect categories, including black beans, broken beans, and insect-damaged beans in real-time processing scenarios. Muchtar et al. (2025) developed an edge AI-based detection system for defective coffee beans using deep learning and the Streamlit framework, achieving high detection performance with a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 87.3% while maintaining compatibility with resource-constrained edge computing devices. Their deployment on Raspberry Pi 4B demonstrated the feasibility of implementing sophisticated deep learning models in industrial settings with limited computational resources. A recent study introduced an automated deep learning framework employing YOLOv8 architecture to identify and quantify defective beans across five key defect categories (Broken, Sour, Water-Faded, Immatured, and Black), achieving strong detection performance with high precision, recall, and mean average precision while providing interpretability through Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) visualizations. These applications collectively demonstrate the maturity and practical viability of YOLO-based approaches for coffee bean quality control in industrial production environments. However, challenges remain in balancing detection accuracy with inference speed, achieving sufficient model compactness for deployment on low-power embedded systems, and maintaining robust performance across varying environmental conditions. These limitations motivate the development of improved architectures specifically optimized for coffee bean defect detection scenarios.

Attention mechanisms and multi-scale feature fusion for enhanced detection performance

Attention mechanisms have emerged as a critical component in modern deep learning architectures, enabling models to selectively focus on discriminative features while suppressing irrelevant information (Vaswani et al., 2017, Wang et al., 2025, Chi et al., 2025a). Woo et al. (2018) introduced the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM), which sequentially applies channel and spatial attention mechanisms to refine feature maps along both dimensions. CBAM achieves this through a two-stage process: the channel attention module first adaptively recalibrates channel-wise feature responses by explicitly modeling interdependencies between channels using both global average pooling and global maximum pooling to capture richer statistical information, followed by the spatial attention module that emphasizes spatially informative regions by computing attention weights across spatial dimensions. Extensive experiments on ImageNet-1K, MS COCO detection, and VOC 2007 detection datasets demonstrated that CBAM consistently improves classification and detection performance across various base architectures with negligible computational overhead, validating its wide applicability as a plug-and-play module (Woo et al., 2018).

Building upon foundational attention mechanisms, researchers have developed specialized variants optimized for specific detection scenarios. Hu et al. (2018) proposed Squeeze-and-Excitation Networks (SENet) that explicitly model channel-wise dependencies through a squeeze operation using global average pooling followed by an excitation operation using a small fully-connected network, enabling the network to adaptively recalibrate channel-wise feature responses. Wang et al. (2020) introduced Efficient Channel Attention (ECA-Net) that avoids dimensionality reduction in the channel attention mechanism by employing one-dimensional convolution for local cross-channel interaction, achieving comparable or superior performance to SENet with significantly lower computational complexity. Recent work by Ma et al. (2024) proposed an Adaptive Attention Module that dynamically adjusts the weights of features extracted from average pooling and max pooling operations across different network stages, addressing the limitation that these complementary features should not occupy equal weights throughout the network hierarchy. Their experiments on CIFAR-100 demonstrated that the adaptive mechanism achieves better performance with smaller parameters and computational overhead compared to CBAM and SENet. These attention mechanisms have been successfully integrated into agricultural detection tasks, with studies demonstrating significant improvements in plant disease identification (Duhan et al., 2024) and crop quality assessment, proving particularly effective for distinguishing subtle defect patterns from complex background textures inherent to natural agricultural products.

Multi-scale feature fusion mechanisms play a crucial role in object detection by enabling models to capture contextual information across different resolutions, which is particularly important for detecting defects of varying sizes on coffee beans. He et al. (2015) introduced Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP) that aggregates features at multiple scales by applying pooling operations with different window sizes, generating fixed-length representations regardless of input size. The SPPF module, introduced in YOLOv5, simplifies the pooling process compared to the original SPP while significantly improving computational efficiency by replacing multiple parallel pooling operations with sequential pooling layers, maintaining the ability to fuse feature maps from different receptive fields (Xia et al., 2023). Xia et al. (2023) proposed mixed receptive fields augmented YOLO with multi-path spatial pyramid pooling for steel surface defect detection, demonstrating that strategic placement of enhanced SPPF modules significantly improves detection accuracy for objects with varying scales. They introduced a re-parameterized large kernel C3 module combined with a multi-path spatial pyramid pooling feature fusion structure in the neck network, enabling the model to obtain larger effective receptive fields while adapting to scale variations of surface defects. Dong et al. (2024) developed a simplified SPPF with hybrid pooling and channel attention mechanisms for industrial product defect detection, integrating both average pooling and max pooling within the SPPF structure and adding channel attention to emphasize important feature channels, achieving 17.4% improvement in mAP while maintaining real-time processing speeds. Zhao (2025) proposed an enhanced SE-SPPF module that combines squeeze-and-excitation mechanisms with spatial pyramid pooling for fabric defect detection, fully integrating important defect information from both spatial and channel perspectives. These architectural innovations demonstrate that the synergistic combination of attention mechanisms with multi-scale feature fusion significantly enhances detection performance for small and irregularly shaped defects, providing a strong foundation for developing specialized architectures for coffee bean defect detection where defects exhibit substantial scale variation and often appear against complex surface textures.

Lightweight network design strategies for edge deployment

The deployment of deep learning models on resource-constrained embedded devices commonly used in agricultural settings necessitates lightweight network designs that maintain high accuracy while minimizing computational complexity, memory footprint, and power consumption. Depthwise separable convolution has emerged as a fundamental technique for building efficient neural networks. Howard et al. (2017) introduced MobileNets that pioneered the use of depthwise separable convolutions, factorizing standard convolutions into depthwise convolutions (applying a single filter per input channel) and pointwise convolutions (1 × 1 convolutions for channel mixing). This factorization achieves approximately 8–9 times reduction in computational cost compared to standard convolutions while maintaining comparable representational capacity. Sandler et al. (2018) proposed MobileNetV2, introducing inverted residual structures and linear bottlenecks that further improve efficiency by expanding low-dimensional compressed representations to high-dimensional space for feature transformation, then projecting back to low-dimensional space, preventing ReLU activations from destroying information in low-dimensional manifolds. Chollet (Chollet, 2017) introduced Xception that replaces Inception modules with depthwise separable convolutions, demonstrating that this modification achieves better performance than Inception V3 with comparable parameter count. These foundational works established depthwise separable convolutions as a cornerstone technique for efficient network design, enabling deployment on mobile and embedded devices while maintaining competitive accuracy on challenging computer vision tasks (Xie et al., 2024).

Recent research has explored novel convolution operations that further push the efficiency frontier. Chen et al. (2023) proposed PConv in FasterNet, a simple yet fast and effective convolution operation that addresses the low computational speed issues prevalent in existing efficient networks. PConv leverages redundancy within feature maps by systematically applying regular convolution to only a portion of input channels while leaving remaining channels untouched, reducing both computational complexity and memory access frequency. Unlike depthwise convolution, which suffers from frequent memory access and thus low computational efficiency, PConv reduces memory access and achieves higher hardware utilization while maintaining lower computational cost than standard convolution. For contiguous memory access, PConv processes the first or last consecutive channels as representatives of the entire feature map. Extensive experiments demonstrated that FasterNet built with PConv achieves superior speed-accuracy trade-offs compared to existing efficient networks across various hardware platforms, including CPU, GPU, and ARM processors. Pan et al. (2024) applied PConv in a lightweight network for wheat variety classification in field environments, demonstrating its effectiveness for agricultural applications. Peng et al. (2024) integrated PConv with residual connections in an improved YOLOv7 model for real-time meter reading, achieving reduced parameters and FLOPs while maintaining high detection accuracy. Recently, Huang et al. (2025) proposed Partial Attention Convolution (PAT) that integrates visual attention mechanisms into PConv, introducing Gaussian channel attention to infuse global distribution information into untouched channels and spatial-wise attention to improve model accuracy, achieving 1.3% higher accuracy than FasterNet-T2 while exhibiting 25% higher GPU throughput and 24% lower CPU latency. These innovations demonstrate that carefully designed lightweight convolution operations can significantly reduce computational requirements while maintaining or even improving detection performance.

The integration of lightweight architectural components with attention mechanisms has shown particular promise for resource-constrained deployment scenarios. Hu et al. (2023) developed a hybrid convolutional network combining multiscale 3D depthwise separable convolution with CBAM residual dilated convolution for hyperspectral image classification, demonstrating that careful integration of efficient convolutions with attention mechanisms achieves high accuracy with minimal computational overhead. Pan et al. (2024) proposed G-PPW-VGG11 that combines partial convolution with partially mixed depthwise separable convolution for field wheat variety classification, reducing computational complexity and feature redundancy while improving classification accuracy. Qiu et al. (2022) developed ASFF-YOLOv5 that incorporates SPPF structure with adaptive spatial feature fusion for multielement detection in UAV images, achieving 93.1% mAP, which is 19.2% higher than original YOLOv5 while maintaining real-time processing capabilities. Peng et al. (2024) proposed PDSI-RTDETR that merges PConv with residual blocks for lightweight tomato ripeness detection, optimizing the backbone network for efficient feature extraction with reduced computational burden while achieving 1.3 percentage point improvement in mAP50. These studies collectively demonstrate that the synergistic combination of lightweight convolution operations, multi-scale feature fusion mechanisms, and attention modules enables the development of efficient detection models suitable for deployment on resource-constrained embedded devices in agricultural settings. The strategic integration of these components addresses the competing demands of detection accuracy, inference speed, and model compactness that are critical for practical industrial deployment of automated coffee bean quality assessment systems. Building upon these foundational techniques, our work introduces targeted architectural improvements to YOLOv10, specifically optimized for defective coffee bean detection, achieving superior performance while maintaining compatibility with low-power embedded systems commonly employed in coffee processing facilities.

Preliminaries

This section establishes the theoretical foundation for the proposed defective coffee bean detection framework. A comprehensive overview of the fundamental mechanisms driving the baseline YOLOv10 architecture is presented, focusing specifically on its end-to-end dual assignment strategy and efficiency-driven design principles.

YOLOv10 architecture

The YOLOv10 framework fundamentally resolves the latency bottleneck inherent in real-time object detectors by completely eliminating the reliance on Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) during inference. Traditional YOLO architectures depend heavily on one-to-many (o2m) label assignments during training. While this approach generates abundant supervisory signals, it necessitates NMS to filter redundant bounding boxes during deployment, thereby increasing inference latency. YOLOv10 circumvents this limitation by introducing a consistent dual assignment strategy coupled with a holistic structural design.

Efficiency-accuracy driven baseline design

Beyond the label assignment strategy, YOLOv10 incorporates a holistic structural optimization paradigm. The backbone utilizes spatial-channel decoupled downsampling to prevent information loss during dimensionality reduction, while the macroscopic architecture employs rank-guided block configurations to eliminate redundant computational stages. Furthermore, the integration of Partial Self-Attention (PSA) and large-kernel depthwise convolutions enhances the global receptive field. These baseline features provide a robust starting point, yet detecting fine-grained agricultural defects on edge devices necessitates further targeted architectural modifications.

Methodology

Standard object detection models frequently struggle in industrial coffee sorting environments due to the extreme scale variations of defects and the severe computational limitations of edge deployment hardware. Optimizing the YOLOv10 framework for this specific application demands targeted structural modifications that enhance feature extraction while strictly controlling computational complexity.

Overall network architecture

The proposed detection network builds upon the YOLOv10 baseline through a tri-fold architectural enhancement strategy. Processing begins as the backbone ingests raw 640 × 640 RGB images. Depthwise Separable Convolution with Distribution Shift (DSConv) operators are integrated into the early and middle stages of this backbone network. This strategic placement drastically reduces the memory access burden while reliably extracting foundational geometric and textural features from the coffee beans. High-level feature maps subsequently pass into the proposed Spatial Pyramid Pooling Fast with Attention (SPPF_Attention) bottleneck. This specialized module aggregates multi-scale receptive fields and adaptively recalibrates spatial and channel weights before feeding the refined data into the neck network. Finally, the detection head utilizes Partial Convolution (PConv) operators to execute efficient bounding box regression and defect classification, maintaining high sensitivity to local features while effectively suppressing irrelevant background noise.

Occlusion-robust detection head via PConv

Industrial coffee bean sorting frequently results in dense physical clusters where beans partially occlude one another. Standard convolutions in the detection head apply uniform computational filters across the entire spatial grid, wasting massive FLOPs on redundant channel data and diminishing performance under severe occlusion. Replacing the standard 3 × 3 convolutions within the YOLOv10 detection heads (across the P3, P4, and P5 downsampling scales) with PConv modules directly mitigates this inefficiency.

The fundamental principle of PConv exploits channel redundancy by performing spatial convolution exclusively on a subset of the input channels. Simultaneously, the untouched channels act as an identity mapping, preserving low-frequency contextual information that standard convolutions might otherwise distort. This modification drastically cuts the computational memory access cost while demonstrating marked robustness against partial occlusion.

Optimization objective

Training the proposed end-to-end framework relies on a composite objective function specifically tailored for the NMS-free dual assignment mechanism. Both the one-to-one and one-to-many branches utilize identical loss formulations during the training phase, comprising Classification Loss (Lcls), Bounding Box Regression Loss (Lbox), and Distribution Focal Loss (Ldfl).

The classification loss employs Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) to strictly penalize incorrect defect categorization. The bounding box loss utilizes Complete Intersection over Union (CIoU) to measure the overlap area, aspect ratio, and central distance alignment between the predicted bounding boxes and the ground truth annotations. Image acquisition.

Image acquisition was performed using a KS2A543-3.0 camera in a controlled laboratory environment, capturing 1344 raw images. The imaging setup simulated realistic sorting scenarios: coffee beans were positioned on a conveyor belt serving as the background, with a fixed rectangular frame maintaining a consistent spatial arrangement. Two strip LED lights were mounted on a steel frame, with the camera centrally positioned and the lens aligned perpendicular to the conveyor centerline. This configuration ensures image data integrity and uniform illumination distribution, meeting practical quality inspection requirements. During sampling, we rigorously controlled the quantity and balance of each defect category to ensure dataset comprehensiveness and representativeness. Fig. 2 illustrates representative images of partial defect categories for visual reference. It is worth noting that hulled green coffee beans possess an asymmetrical morphology, featuring a flat inner ventral groove and a rounded outer dorsal surface. To simulate realistic industrial sorting, beans were imaged in random orientations to comprehensively capture both surfaces. This ensures the network learns to robustly detect defects located on either side, irrespective of the bean’s physical posture.

Data augmentation.

Data augmentation serves dual purposes: enhancing dataset diversity and significantly improving model generalization and robustness. We applied four augmentation techniques to expand the raw coffee bean image dataset: horizontal flipping, vertical flipping, blur processing, and Gaussian noise injection. As illustrated in Fig. 3, horizontal and vertical flipping simulate viewing angle variations, enhancing directional invariance. Blur processing emulates focus variations and motion artifacts common in industrial conveyor systems, while Gaussian noise injection simulates sensor noise and ambient lighting fluctuations. These transformations simulate real-world variations in imaging conditions, enabling the model to develop invariant feature representations that generalize effectively to practical deployment scenarios with varying lighting, orientations, and image quality degradation. Through these strategies, the original 1344 images were expanded to 6695 images.

Training protocol and five-fold cross-validation

We implemented a 5-fold cross-validation scheme with an internal validation set to rigorously evaluate the model’s performance and mitigate the randomness inherent in a single data split. The complete dataset of 6695 images was first randomly divided into five mutually exclusive subsets of approximately 1339 images each. Over five experimental rounds, each subset served once as the isolated test set, while the remaining four subsets were aggregated into a comprehensive training pool.

Because the early stopping strategy necessitates an independent validation set, we randomly extracted approximately 12.5% (around 670 images) of the training pool (5356 images) for validation purposes, leaving 4686 images as the final training set. This approach strictly maintained a consistent data distribution ratio of 70% (training), 10% (validation), and 20% (testing) throughout every round. Upon completing all five iterations, the final performance metrics were computed as the Mean ± Standard Deviation (SD) for precision, recall, F1-score, and mAP@50. This identical data flow and early stopping criterion, terminating training after 30 consecutive epochs without validation improvement, were applied uniformly across all evaluated models.

Results and discussion

In agricultural object detection, generic deep learning frameworks have become the mainstream paradigm. While some prior studies have utilized classification networks (e.g., ResNet, EfficientNet) for coffee bean grading, they fundamentally lack the capability for fine-grained, instance-level localization of individual defective beans. Regarding detection tasks, existing literature predominantly relies on transfer learning using generic architectures like YOLOv5 or Faster R-CNN. However, these methods lack specialized structural designs tailored to the unique morphological characteristics of coffee beans. Although hybrid approaches combining hyperspectral imaging with Support Vector Machines (SVMs) exist, they depend on expensive optical equipment, significantly hindering large-scale industrial deployment. Therefore, comparing our method against dominant generic detection models serves as a rigorous and objective benchmark, highlighting the necessity and superiority of exploring dedicated network optimizations.

Comparison with SSD and faster R-CNN.

Our method demonstrates overwhelming superiority over traditional architectures. Compared to SSD and Faster R-CNN, our model achieves an improvement of 11.6% to 15.8% in mAP@50, alongside a precision increase of 20.0% to 24.2%. Comparison with earlier YOLO baselines.

When evaluated against YOLOv8 and YOLOv9, our framework improves mAP@50 by 5.0% to 6.4% while reducing model parameters by 29.0% to 37.6%. Comparison with state-of-the-art YOLOv11.

Despite YOLOv11’s advanced feature pyramids and refined blocks, our customized framework maintains a distinct edge in both accuracy and deployment efficiency. This comprehensive superiority is driven by our domain-specific innovations: (1) DSConv preserves fine-grained discriminative features to distinguish visually similar defects (e.g., moldy vs. immature beans); (2) PConv strategically exploits spatial redundancy to drastically cut computational overhead; and (3) SPPF-Attention provides context-aware multi-scale aggregation for robust detection across various defect sizes without heavy parameter burdens.

Computational efficiency and practical implications

The computational efficiency of our framework merits particular attention, as it directly determines the feasibility of deployment in industrial quality control systems. Operating at an inference latency of just 2.0 ms per image, our model delivers processing speeds that are 10 times faster than Faster R-CNN (20.0 ms), 2.5 times faster than YOLOv8 (5.0 ms), and 1.5 times faster than the state-of-the-art YOLOv11 (3.0 ms). Furthermore, the compact 15.6 MB model size facilitates seamless integration into edge computing devices with strict memory constraints, representing a critical advantage over the cumbersome 170.4 MB footprint of Faster R-CNN. These efficiency breakthroughs are rooted in our architectural design principles: DSConv minimizes memory access and accelerates computation via integer-domain operations; SPPF-Attention ensures targeted feature enhancement without the prohibitive overhead of full self-attention mechanisms; and PConv systematically eliminates redundant channel-wise computations.

These performance and efficiency metrics translate directly into substantial economic and operational benefits for coffee bean processing facilities. By achieving a 99.2% mAP@50 alongside a 98.8% recall rate, the framework restricts the false negative rate to approximately 1.2%, meaning fewer than 12 defective beans per 1000 will evade detection. Beyond high precision, the exceptional computational efficiency guarantees cost-effective scalability. A single mid-range GPU can reliably support four to six parallel sorting lines operating concurrently at standard industrial throughput rates. Consequently, deploying this automated inspection system drastically reduces capital expenditure for agricultural facilities while simplifying long-term hardware maintenance.

Interpretability and model behavior insights.

The combined visualization findings reveal that the improved YOLOv10 framework achieves both enhanced accuracy and interpretability. The EigenCAM maps demonstrate that the model learns to emphasize semantically meaningful bean structures while suppressing irrelevant backgrounds. Concurrently, the confusion matrix analysis validates the model’s ability to maintain high intra-class confidence and inter-class separation. These visual diagnostics substantiate that performance gains observed in the ablation study stem from genuine improvements in feature representation and decision robustness rather than overfitting or random variance, representing key characteristics of trustworthy and deployable AI systems.

Conclusion

This study establishes a highly efficient paradigm for automated coffee bean quality assessment by systematically innovating upon the YOLOv10 framework. To resolve the critical bottleneck between detection accuracy and computational constraints in industrial edge environments, we integrated three complementary modules: DSConv for lightweight feature extraction, SPPF-Attention for adaptive multi-scale context aggregation, and PConv for occlusion-robust processing. Rather than relying on computationally exhaustive architectures, this research demonstrates that strategically customized feature refinement can capture subtle, highly variable defect morphologies at a fraction of the typical computational cost. The empirical evidence confirms the superiority and synergistic complementarity of these modifications. Evaluated under a rigorous 5-fold cross-validation protocol, the framework achieves a peak mAP@50 of 99.2%, with 98.5% precision and 98.8% recall, effectively restricting the false negative rate to 1.2%, ensuring that very few defective beans evade detection, while the 98.5% precision minimizes unnecessary rejection of high-quality beans. Systematic ablation studies quantify this success, demonstrating a cumulative 9.9% precision and 8.8% F1 score improvement over the baseline YOLOv10. Crucially, these accuracy gains do not compromise deployment viability; the model achieves 33.3% faster inference and a 21.6% parameter reduction compared to the state-of-the-art YOLOv11. Furthermore, extensive cross-dataset evaluations on three independent public datasets verify that the network learns intrinsic, invariant defect characteristics rather than merely overfitting to specific laboratory conditions. For the coffee processing industry, these metrics translate directly into a scalable, high-throughput automated sorting solution that substantially surpasses human inspection capabilities without fatigue-induced degradation.

Limitations and future work

Despite these advances, several limitations warrant acknowledgment and provide clear directions for future research. The current evaluation focuses predominantly on seven major defect categories specific to Yunnan small-grain coffee. Furthermore, while the laboratory setup simulates realistic sorting scenarios, actual factory deployments introduce dynamic physical disruptions, including high-speed conveyor vibration, ambient illumination shifts, and sensor degradation from dust accumulation. Future research will focus on unsupervised domain adaptation to seamlessl bridge the distribution gap between controlled laboratory data and complex industrial production lines. Additionally, investigating transfer learning and few-shot learning strategies will be critical for rapidly scaling the framework to recognize long-tail defect categories, distinct processing methods, and novel global coffee varieties without requiring exhaustive manual re-annotation.

Unlocking the Potential of Diffusion Language Models through Template Infilling
Junhoo Lee, Seungyeon Kim, Nojun Kwak

Abstract
Diffusion Language Models (DLMs) have emerged as a promising alternative to Autoregressive Language Models, yet their inference strategies remain limited to prefix-based prompting inherited from the autoregressive paradigm. In this paper, we propose Template Infilling (TI), a tailored conditioning methodology for DLMs’ generation process. Unlike conventional prefix prompting, TI first generates a structural template for the target response, then fills in the masked segments. To enhance the flexibility of this structural control, we introduce Dynamic Segment Allocation (DSA), which adaptively adjusts segment lengths based on generation confidence. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach on mathematical reasoning and code generation benchmarks, achieving consistent improvements of 17.01 % \% p over baseline. Furthermore, we show that TI provides additional advantages in multi-token generation settings, enabling effective speedup while maintaining generation quality.

Introduction

Diffusion Language Models (DLMs) have recently emerged as a promising alternative to traditional Large Language Models (LLMs). While Autoregressive Language Models (ALMs) rely on simple next-token prediction, DLMs reframe text generation as a denoising task. DLMs iteratively refine entire sequences, enabling fundamentally different generation dynamics from the left-to-right paradigm of autoregressive models.

Despite this fundamental distinction, existing DLM research has not fully explored the unique capabilities afforded by this paradigm. Most studies primarily focus on leveraging DLMs’ ability to generate multiple tokens simultaneously for reducing inference costs
Wu et al. 2025
;
Liu et al. 2025
. Consequently, these works evaluate DLMs using the same prefix-based prompting inherited from ALMs
Nie et al. 2025
, leaving conditioning strategies tailored to DLMs’ bidirectional generation capabilities largely underexplored. This gap motivates the need for novel conditioning methodologies designed specifically for DLMs.

In this work, we propose Template Infilling (TI), a framework that fully leverages the unique characteristics of DLMs. TI generalizes conventional prompting by explicitly defining the structural skeleton of the target response as a template during inference, then filling in the masked segments. By predefining the response structure through templates alongside the input context, DLMs can simultaneously consider all segments and generate globally coherent responses. Unlike implicit prefix guidance in ALM, TI provides explicit conditioning by directly specifying structural priors.
Furthermore, we introduce the Dynamic Segment Allocation (DSA) algorithm to enable flexible template segment placement. Building on empirical findings about DLM confidence
Yu et al. 2025
, we propose a confidence-based algorithm that identifies optimal mask lengths, enabling problem-adaptive generation.

We validate our approach by applying TI to LLADA
Nie et al. 2025
. We achieve consistent improvements of 17.01
\%
p in accuracy across mathematical reasoning and code generation tasks. We demonstrate that these gains cannot be achieved through simple prefix prompting alone. Moreover, we show that TI addresses a major challenge in DLMs: multi-token generation. Our experiments reveal that TI maintains performance even when generating multiple tokens simultaneously, implying performance benefits. Our work opens new directions for leveraging DLMs’ unique capabilities beyond simple parallelization, demonstrating the potential of diffusion generation paradigms which is unavailable to autoregressive one.

Methods

3.1
Preliminaries

Autoregressive Language Models.

Autoregressive (AR) language models generate text through the following conditional probability:

p(x_{t}|x_{<t})=p(x_{t}|x_{1},\ldots,x_{t-1})

(1)

This means that the token at position
t
depends only on the tokens at previous positions
1
through
t-1
.

Diffusion Language Models.

In contrast, Diffusion Language Models (DLMs) generate text through an iterative denoising process. Given a noisy sequence
x^{(t)}
at diffusion step
t
, the model progressively denoises it to generate
x^{(t-1)}
:

p(x^{(t-1)}|x^{(t)})

(2)

By repeating this process for
T
steps, we obtain the final text
x^{(0)}
from complete noise
x^{(T)}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)
.

A key property of DLMs is their ability to perform conditional generation over arbitrary position sets. Specifically, given observed positions
\mathcal{O}
and masked positions
\mathcal{M}
(where
\mathcal{O}\cap\mathcal{M}=\emptyset
and
\mathcal{O}\cup\mathcal{M}=\{1,\ldots,N\}
), DLMs can generate:

p(x_{\mathcal{M}}|x_{\mathcal{O}})

(3)

Here,
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{M}
can be arbitrary position sets in the sequence, and critically, each element in
\mathcal{M}
can reference bidirectional context.

3.2
Template Infilling

We generalize conventional prompt conditioning in AR models to
Template Infilling (TI)
. We first construct a template
\tau
that specifies the structural skeleton of the target response:

\tau=[t_{1},\mathcal{M}_{1},t_{2},\mathcal{M}_{2},\ldots,t_{k},\mathcal{M}_{k}]

(4)

where
t_{i}
are template anchors—predefined structural elements—and
\mathcal{M}_{i}
are masked segments to be filled during generation.

This approach generalizes traditional prompting by explicitly specifying the structure of the generated content through
t_{i}
, while providing bidirectional conditioning that enables more precise control compared to unidirectional prefix-based prompting.

3.3
Dynamic Segment Allocation

While TI provides strong structural control, it suffers from a limitation: template positions are fixed. The length of each segment
\mathcal{M}_{i}
must be predetermined, which constrains generation flexibility. For instance, if a particular section requires more explanation than anticipated, the fixed length can lead to content truncation or incomplete reasoning.

Inspired by recent work on confidence in language models
(
Yu et al. 2025
;
Li et al. 2025
)
, we address this issue through
Dynamic Segment Allocation (DSA)
. We define the confidence at each position
i\in\mathcal{M}
as:

c_{i}=\max_{v\in\mathcal{V}}p(x_{i}=v|x_{\mathcal{O}},x_{\mathcal{M}\setminus\{i\}})

(5)

where
\mathcal{V}
is the vocabulary. Intuitively,
c_{i}
represents the probability of the token the model is most confident about at position
i
.

Our key insight is that excessively low confidence values indicate insufficient context for generating coherent content at those positions. We therefore introduce a confidence threshold
\tau
and iteratively expand segments with low confidence. For segment
\mathcal{M}_{i}
with average confidence below
\theta
, we expand it by inserting additional mask tokens
\Delta
adjacent to
\mathcal{M}_{i}
:

\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(k+1)}=\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(k)}\cup{|\Delta|}

(6)

This process repeats until the average confidence of
\mathcal{M}_{i}
exceeds
\tau
or a maximum expansion limit is reached. Through this adaptive allocation mechanism, we enable flexible template segment sizing while maintaining structural coherence.

Implementation Details

We use LLaDA
Nie et al. 2025
as our baseline model and modify the official generation code of LLaDA without applying any acceleration or caching optimizations proposed in subsequent works. To test genuine diffusion abilities, we do not adopt block generation (
i.e.,
all indices are updated in parallel, creating the whole generation simultaneously). All experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX Pro 6000 GPU. For confidence threshold, we adopt
0.1
.

3.5
Benchmarks and Metrics

To comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness of TI, we conduct experiments across four benchmarks spanning mathematical reasoning and code generation domains, selecting benchmarks of varying difficulty levels for each domain. For mathematical reasoning, we use GSM8K
(
Cobbe et al. 2021
)

measuring performance using accuracy. For code generation, we evaluate program synthesis capabilities on
HumanEval
(
Chen et al. 2021
)
, using the pass@1 metric which evaluates correctness in a single attempt.
All benchmark evaluations are conducted using the Language Model Evaluation Harness
(
Gao et al. 2023
)
. To accurately assess generalization capabilities, all experiments are performed in a zero-shot setting without task-specific demonstrations.

4
Conclusion

In this work, we explored the unique capability of Diffusion Language Models for arbitrary position conditioning and introduced Template Infilling (TI), a generation framework that systematically leverages this architectural advantage. We further proposed Dynamic Segment Allocation (DSA), an algorithm that enables flexible segment allocation for adaptive generation.
Our experiments demonstrate that TI achieves consistent performance improvements across mathematical reasoning and code generation benchmarks. Additionally, we show that TI provides advantages in multi-token generation settings, enabling effective speedup while maintaining generation quality. Our work opens new research directions in template engineering by enabling explicit structural conditioning beyond the prefix prompting paradigm of ALMs.

5
Limitations and Future Work

In this paper, we introduced Template Infilling (TI), a framework designed to leverage the unique characteristics of Diffusion Language Models. While TI demonstrates improved performance over prompt-based generation in a training-free manner, an important limitation remains: current instruction-fine-tuned models are still trained under the traditional prompt-inference paradigm. Consequently, these models are not optimized to fully exploit TI’s capabilities.
This limitation points to a promising direction for future work: incorporating TI into the instruction fine-tuning process itself. By training models with template-based objectives from the outset, we could potentially unlock greater performance gains and enable models to better leverage their bidirectional conditioning capabilities.

The Porous Medium Equation: Multiscale Integrability in Large Deviations
Benjamin Gess, Daniel Heydecker

Abstract
. We consider a zero-range process η t N ​ ( x ) \eta^{N}_{t}(x) with superlinear local jump rate, which in a hydrodynamic-small particle rescaling converges to the porous medium equation ∂ t u = 1 2 ​ Δ ​ u α , α > 1 \partial_{t}u=\frac{1}{2}\Delta u^{\alpha},\alpha>1 . 1.
Introduction

The problem of finding the large deviations of gradient particle systems is a classical problem
[
43
,
42
,
41

, which has now been resolved for a number of models with bounded and nondegenerate diffusivity. Once one allows unbounded and degenerate diffusivity, many open questions in large deviations remain.

The goal of this paper will be to introduce a general and robust analysis to understand where these features cause difficulties, and how they may be resolved, for a simple and paradigmatic example with both unbounded and degenerate diffusion. We will study a zero-range process
\eta^{N}_{\bullet}
which converges, with a further rescaling introduced in

(1.1)

\partial_{t}u_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta(u_{t}^{\alpha}),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}},t\in[0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}];\qquad\alpha>1.

1.1.
Statement of Results

Since the general pattern for establishing a result such as Theorem

is by now well-known and classical
[
7

41

from convergence of the configuration
\eta^{N}_{\bullet}
in a topology like

\overline{\eta}^{N,N\epsilon}(x)

(1.6)

\overline{\eta}^{N,N\epsilon}(x):=\frac{1}{(2\lfloor\epsilon N\rfloor+1)^{d}}\sum_{y\in B_{\lfloor\epsilon N\rfloor}(x)}\eta^{N}(y)

centred at

Theorem 2
.

[Superexponential Estimate for the Diffusivity] Fix

, and let

be as in Theorem

\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\chi_{N}}{N^{d}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\Pi^{N}_{a}}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}V^{N}_{\alpha}(\eta^{N}_{t},\epsilon,x)dt>\delta\right)=-\infty

where
V^{N}_{\alpha}
is the error given by

(1.8)

V^{N}_{\alpha}(\eta^{N},\epsilon,x):=\left|(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha}-(\overline{\eta}^{N,N\epsilon}(x))^{\alpha}\right|.

It is this estimate in which both degenerate and superlinear diffusion threaten to derail the usual methods of ‘one- and two-block estimates’
[
43
,
7
,
41

1.2.
A Sketch Proof of Theorem
3

Having explained the key difficulties posed by degenerate and superlinear diffusion, we now unveil the key ideas in their resolution. In the previous work
[
30
]
, and works on related SPDEs
[
23
,
24
]
, both the integrability and the superexponential estimate were treated by proving a pathwise regularity result, leveraging the fundamental regularity of the
skeleton equation
defining the rate function
\mathcal{I}_{\rho}
:

(1.12)

\partial_{t}u_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta(u_{t}^{\alpha})-\nabla\cdot(u_{t}^{\alpha/2}g);\qquad g\in L^{2}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}},\mathbb{R}^{d}).

This equation was analysed in
[
23
]
; the fundamental regularity, for which an estimate can be obtained, is the time-integrated entropy dissipation, given by the Sobolev norm
\|\nabla u^{\alpha/2}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}^{2}<\infty
. As discussed below (
1.2
), leveraging this regularity directly at the level of the particle system or SPDE requires a particular scaling relation on
\chi_{N},N
, and we must therefore argue differently.

The integrability estimate Theorem
3
turns out to be the most challenging of the steps towards Theorem
1
, and is therefore the main focus of the paper. Several plausible strategies for such an estimate leverage properties of the PME (
1.1
), such as the ‘coming down from infinity’, or attempting to close
L^{\alpha}_{x}
- or
H^{-1}_{x}
-estimates
[
54
]
. Such methods are, at least for large deviations and in general dimension, doomed to failure from the outset. The quantities which one finds in the course of such an estimate are stronger than those which can be estimated from (
1.12
), and closing such an estimate would ultimately be incompatible with the rate function
\mathcal{I}_{\rho}
for which we aim. For this reason, it is the intrinsic regularity of (
1.12
), and not (
1.1
), which must dictate the strategy.

The most naïve approach to leveraging the regularity discussed above is to use lattice discretisation of the Sobolev embeddings and the discrete regularity obtained from (
1.2
) to obtain a discrete integrability estimate. This is the strategy carried out in
[
30
, Sections 5-7]
, but requires the same scaling relation used therein. One finds, whether arguing at the pathwise level as in
[
30
]
, or through the Feynmann-Kac formula as in Lemma

below, an estimate of the form

(1.13)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\left[\left\|\nabla_{N}{(\eta^{N})^{\alpha/2}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}\right]&\leq C\chi_{N}N^{2-d}\mathfrak{d}_{N}(f_{N})\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+C\chi_{N}^{\delta}N^{2}\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\left(1+\|(\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}\right)\end{split}

for some (fixed)

, where

is any probability density with respect to
\Pi^{N}_{a}
and where

is the Dirichlet form associated to the dynamics. The first term is of the correct form to deduce the desired estimates from the Feynman-Kac formula, see, for example, the proof of

\chi_{N}^{\delta}N^{2}\lesssim 1
as in

is imposed. The exponent

arises from from controlling certain error terms close to zero in the case

The three key insights of Theorem

are that we may avoid any scaling relationship between

and

the error term gains an advantageous scaling when replacing
\eta^{N}
by a local average on scale

such estimates imply a gain of integrability across scales

\widetilde{B}

iii).

the desired estimate may be obtained by applying (ii) to a sequence of scales

The first point is the deduction of the estimate displayed at (

&\hskip 28.45274pt+Cl_{N}^{-d/2}\left(\frac{\widetilde{l}_{N}}{l_{N}}\right)^{2}\chi_{N}^{\delta}\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\left(1+\|(\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}\right).\end{split}

p>1

N^{1-1/p}\chi_{N}^{\delta/2}\lesssim 1
, and combining the two previous displays yields an estimate of the form

(1.18)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\left[\|(\Lambda_{B}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{p}(B\in\mathcal{P})}-2\|(\Lambda_{\widetilde{B}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{p}(\widetilde{B}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}})}\right]\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt\leq C\chi_{N}N^{2-d}\mathfrak{d}(f_{N})\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt+Cl_{N}^{-d/2}\left(\frac{\widetilde{l}_{N}}{l_{N}}\right)^{2}\chi_{N}^{\delta/2}\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\left(1+\|(\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}\right).\end{split}

Step 3. Multiscale Recombination

The final key point is that an estimate proving Theorem
3
may be obtained by combining estimates of the form (
1.18
). Using both parts of the Assumption
1.2
, we may find a finite
K<\infty
and integer sequences

l^{1}_{N}=1\ll l^{2}_{N}\dots\ll l^{K}_{N}=N

so that each
l^{k}_{N}
divides
l^{k+1}_{N}
, and so that
(l^{k+1}_{N}/l_{N})^{2}\chi_{N}^{\delta/2}\lesssim 1.
Associated to the
l^{k}_{N}
, we construct a sequence of partitions

\mathcal{P}^{1}=\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}\prec\mathcal{P}^{2}\prec\dots\prec\mathcal{P}^{K}=\{\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}\}

where each element of
\mathcal{P}^{k}
is a box of side length
l^{k}_{N}
, and each
\mathcal{P}^{k}
refines
\mathcal{P}^{k+1}
. We refer to Figure
1
for an illustration of this scheme. For the finest partition, there is no averaging and
\|(\Lambda_{\mathcal{P}^{1}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathcal{P}^{1})}=\|(\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}
, while at the coarsest scale
(\Lambda_{\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}=\|(\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}
. We can therefore obtain a telescoping sum

(1.19)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\|(\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}-2^{K-1}\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\|(\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\left[\|(\Lambda_{B^{1}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{p}(B^{1}\in\mathcal{P}^{1})}-2\|(\Lambda_{{B^{2}}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{p}({B^{2}\in\mathcal{P}^{2}})}\right]\\
&+2\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\left[\|(\Lambda_{B^{2}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{p}(B^{2}\in\mathcal{P}^{2})}-4\|(\Lambda_{{B^{3}}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{p}(B^{3}\in\mathcal{P}^{3})}\right]\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\vdots\\
&+2^{K-2}\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\left[\|(\Lambda_{B^{K-1}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{p}(B^{K-1}\in\mathcal{P}^{K-1})}-2^{K-1}\|(\Lambda_{{B^{K}}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{p}(B^{K}\in{\mathcal{P}^{K}})}\right]\end{split}

where each term on the right-hand side is of the same form as the left-hand side of (
1.18
), and thanks to the choices of
l^{k}_{N}
, the prefactor in error term remains bounded. Gathering all the terms, and using statistical stationarity to absorb the error terms into the final term, we get, for a new value of
C
,

(1.20)

\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\|(\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}\leq(2^{K-1}+C)\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\|(\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}+C\chi_{N}N^{d-2}\mathfrak{d}(f_{N}).

We finally impose a mass bound. If we additionally impose that
f^{N}
must be supported on
X_{N,b}
, defined in (
1.9
), a simple interpolation of
\ell^{p}
-norms shows that the
\ell^{1}
norm appearing on the right-hand side may be bounded by an arbitrarily small multiple of the
\ell^{p}
norm plus a large
b
-dependent constant, which permits to absorb the first term of the right-hand side into the left-hand side

(1.21)

\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\|(\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}\leq C+C\chi_{N}N^{d-2}\mathfrak{d}(f_{N})

where all the constants are allowed to depend on
b
, but are otherwise uniform in
N,\chi_{N},f_{N}
. It is standard, using the Feynman-Kac formula (for example,
[
53
]
,
[
41
, Proof of Theorem 2.1]
) that such an estimate is sufficient to prove an estimate of the form claimed in Theorem
3
, completing the proof.

We conclude the sketch proof by by discussing the role of Assumptions
1.2
. Hypothesis b), that
N
is a dyadic integer, is only used in step 3 to ensure the existence of the scales
l^{k}_{N}
such that each
l^{k+1}_{N}/l^{k}_{N}
is an integer, so that all the boxes may be taken of the same size. In the main text, the issue is resolved for all
N
by introducing
weightings
: We omit this from the sketch proof, because the resolution introduces no further essential difficulties in the analysis, but makes the notation more cumbersome. In contrast, hypothesis a) is crucially used in two places in the argument sketched above: first to choose

to control the error terms, and secondly in ensuring that the iterative scheme terminates after a finite (possibly large, but
N

and working with the resulting Orlicz norm rather than
\ell^{p}
-norms. For the second issue, generalising to

requires introducing a further tuneable parameter

, which keeps the prefactors appearing in the analogue of telescoping sum (

1.4.4.

We first comment on Theorem

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The Process

We first give a precise definition of the process
\eta^{N}_{\bullet}
of study, see also
[
30
]
. We consider the zero-range process on a discrete torus
\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}^{d}
with a jump rate
g(k)=dk^{\alpha}
. In a configuration
k
where there are
k(x)\in\mathbb{N},x\in\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}^{d}
particles at each site, an exponential clock of rate
g(k(x))
is initialised at each site; when the first clock rings, a particle is moved from site
x
to one of its neighbours
y\sim x
, chosen randomly and independently of the clocks, and the process begins again. This defines a Markovian dynamics in
k\in\mathbb{N}^{\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}^{d}}
.

It is standard to perform a hydrodynamic rescaling of space and time in order to obtain a hydrodynamic limit. As in
[
30
]
, a further rescaling is necessary to produce degenerate diffusion, introducing the parameter
\chi_{N}\to 0
. We set

\eta^{N}_{t}(x):=\chi_{N}k\left(N^{2}t\chi_{N}^{\alpha-1},Nx\right)

which amounts to, in addition to the usual hydrodynamic rescaling, counting of each particle as of size
\chi_{N}
, and making the local jump rate
2d(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha}/\chi_{N}
. The rescaled spatial variable
x
will now run over the rescaled discrete torus

\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}:=\left\{0,N^{-1},\dots,\frac{N-1}{N}\right\}^{d}\subset[0,1)^{d}

and
\eta^{N}
defines a Markov process in the state space
X_{N}:=(\chi_{N}\mathbb{N})^{\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}
with generator given by
N^{2}\mathcal{L}_{N}
, where

(2.1)

\mathcal{L}_{N}F(\eta^{N}):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\sim y}\frac{(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha}}{\chi_{N}}\left(F(\eta^{N,x,y})-F(\eta^{N})\right).

In this definition, the sum runs over all pairs
\{x,y\}
of neighbouring sites in
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
, and
\eta^{N,x,y}:=\eta^{N}+\chi_{N}1_{y}-\chi_{N}1_{x}
is the configuration obtained from
\eta^{N}
by moving one particle from
x
to
y
. Here, and in the sequel, we write
1_{x}
for the function on
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
which is equal to 1 at
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
, and zero elsewhere. It will be convenient to isolate the invariant sets
X_{N,b}
of the subset of the state space given by imposing an upper bound on the mass

X_{N,b}:=\left\{\eta^{N}\in X_{N}:\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\eta^{N}(x)\leq b\right\}.

A standard calculation shows that the process admits a continuum family of reversible equilibrium distributions

(2.2)

\Pi^{N}_{a}(\eta^{N}):=\prod_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\frac{(a/\chi_{N})^{\alpha(\eta/\chi_{N})}}{((\eta/\chi_{N})!)^{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}(a/\chi_{N})},\qquad\rho>0

where
Z_{\alpha}
is a suitable normalisation constant. Where
\rho\in C({\mathbb{T}^{d}},(0,\infty)
is a profile varying at the macroscopic scale, we define the slowly varying local equilibrium with the same notation:

(2.3)

\Pi^{N}_{\rho}(\eta^{N}):=\prod_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\frac{(\rho(x)/\chi_{N})^{\alpha(\eta/\chi_{N})}}{((\eta/\chi_{N})!)^{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}(\rho(x)/\chi_{N})},\qquad\rho>0

It may easily be verified that the equilibrium distributions have the
integration by parts
property that, for any
F:X_{N}\to\mathbb{R}
and any
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
,

(2.4)

\mathbb{E}_{\Pi^{N}_{\rho}}[F(\eta^{N}){(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha}}]=\rho^{\alpha}\mathbb{E}_{\Pi^{N}_{\rho}}[F(\eta^{N}+\chi 1_{x})].

Conventions on Lattice Geometry

We have chosen the definition of the process
\eta^{N}_{\bullet}
to already include the rescaling of space and time, and as a result, the particle sites
x,y
will always take values in the rescaled torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
. Correspondingly, when speaking of the ‘side length’ of a box
B\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
, we will always mean the microscopic side length, namely the number of edges

of the discretisation
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
on each edge of the box. Since we will always use ‘side length’ to mean microscopic side length, we will not repeat the ‘microscopic’ in the sequel.

We will refer to the

of two points

to be the length
k\geq 1
of the shortest path
x_{0}=x,x_{1},\dots,x_{k}=y
with
x_{j}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
and each pair
x_{j},x_{j+1}
adjacent in
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
. In contrast,
|\cdot|_{\infty}
will always be used to denote the macroscopic distance between two points on the torus
{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
induced by the
\ell^{\infty}
-norm on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

2.3.

In order to give meaning to a large deviations principle, we must specify a suitable topological space. It is straightforward to associate each configuration

3.1.
Preliminaries

We first set up the technical machinery needed to make the sketch proof in Section

Box Partitions

Given a scale
1\leq l_{N}\leq N
, let us write
\mathcal{Q}(N,l_{N})
for the set of partitions
\mathcal{P}_{N}
of the torus
\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}
of the form

(3.2)

\mathcal{P}=\left\{B=\prod_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}:A_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}^{i}\right\}

where each
\mathcal{P}_{N}^{i}
is a partition of the 1-dimensional torus into intervals of length between
l_{N}
and
2l_{N}
. Each such
\mathcal{P}
comes with a natural notion of
adjacency
, where we say that two blocks
B,B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}
are adjacent, and write
B\sim_{\mathcal{P}}B^{\prime}
, if they can be expressed as
B=\prod_{i}A_{i},B^{\prime}=\prod_{i}A^{\prime}_{i}
, with
A_{i},A^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathcal{P}^{i}_{N}
, such that
A_{i}=A^{\prime}_{i}
for all but one
i
, and on this index, there exist adjacent
x\in A_{i},y\in A^{\prime}_{i}
, so that
A_{i},A^{\prime}_{i}
are adjacent elements of
\mathcal{P}^{i}_{N}
.

Given
\mathcal{P}_{N}\in\mathcal{Q}(N,l_{N})
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{N}\in\mathcal{Q}(N,\widetilde{l}_{N})
, we write
\mathcal{P}_{N}\prec\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{N}
to denote that
\mathcal{P}_{N}
is a refinement of
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{N}
, that is, that every
B\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
is contained in a (unique)
\widetilde{B}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{N}
. We write
\pi:\mathcal{P}_{N}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{N},\pi(B):=\widetilde{B}
for the map taking each block to the element of the coarser partition which contains it.

Weightings

We introduce weightings on the underlying set
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
, which are then inherited by all partitions. In the sequel, a weighting will be a strictly positive measure on the discrete
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
, which we identify with a function
w^{N}:\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}\to(0,\infty)
.

Orlicz Norms on Block Functions

Fix a partition
\mathcal{P}_{N}
of
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
, a weighting
w^{N}
. We recall that a
Young function
is a map
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty]
which is convex, even, lower semicontinuous and neither the zero function nor its convex dual. We will always work with Young functions which are additionally
strict
, meaning that

(3.3)

\Phi(u)<\infty\text{ for all }u\in\mathbb{R};\qquad\frac{\Phi(u)}{u}\to\infty\text{ as }u\to\infty

and to ensure the finiteness of all norms,
\Phi(0)=0
. In the sequel the uppercase
\Psi
will be reserved for the convex dual of a Young function, given by

(3.4)

\Psi(x):=\sup\{ux-\Phi(u):u\in\mathbb{R}\}.

The definition is such that
\Phi
is strict if, and only if,
\Psi
is, and
\Phi
may be recovered from
\Psi
by the same formula (
3.4
). We define the Orlicz norm on functions
h:\mathcal{P}_{N}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

(3.5)

\|h(B)\|_{l_{\Phi}(B\in\mathcal{P}_{N},w^{N})}:=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:\sum_{B\in\mathcal{P}_{N}}\frac{w^{N}(B)}{w^{N}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}\Phi\left(\frac{h(B)}{t}\right)\leq 1\right\}.

Similarly, when
\widetilde{B}\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
is a union of sets from
\mathcal{P}_{N}
, we write

3.2.
Stochastic Estimates

We now turn to the problem of estimating various expectations
\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}[F]
in terms of
\chi_{N}N^{2-d}\mathfrak{d}_{N}(f_{N})
, providing the basic ingredients corresponding to the steps in the outline proof in Section
1.2
.

The first point is that we may freely restrict to shift-invariant density functions
f_{N}
. We define the set of Galilean-invariant functions
G_{N}(X_{N})
to be those functions
F:X_{N}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
F\circ\tau_{x}=F
for any translation
\tau_{x}
, and whenever
R:X_{N}\to X_{N}
is a composition of rotations and reflections preserving the lattice,
F=F\circ R
. Similarly, given an invariant measure
\Pi^{N}_{a}
, we let
G_{N}(\Pi^{N}_{a})
denote those probability density functions
f_{N}
with respect to
\Pi^{N}_{a}
which are invariant under the same transformations. Finally, for any
b>0
, we denote
G_{N}(\Pi^{N}_{a},b)
those probability densities
f_{N}\in G_{N}(\Pi^{N}_{a})
which are supported on
X_{N,b}
. With this notation, the principle we use is as follows.

Lemma 3.5
.

[Restriction to Shift-Invariant Measures] For any
N,a>0,\lambda>0
and
F\in G_{N}(X_{N})
, it holds that

(3.13)

\sup_{f_{N}}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}[F]-\lambda\mathfrak{d}_{N}(f_{N})\right\}=\sup_{f_{N}\in G_{N}(\Pi^{N}_{a})}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}[F]-\lambda\mathfrak{d}_{N}(f_{N})\right\}.

Moreover, if, for some
b
,
\{F>0\}\subset X_{N,b}
, then the supremum on the right-hand side can be further restricted to
f_{N}\in G_{N}(\Pi^{N}_{a},b)
.

Since this is a well-known principle consisting entirely of ‘soft’ (qualitative) arguments, we omit the proof and refer to
[
41
, Proof of Lemma 4.1, Step 3]
.
The next step produces, for shift-invariant densities
f_{N}
, a canonical-paths type estimate.

Lemma 3.6
.

For any
f_{N}\in G_{N}(\Pi^{N}_{a})
and any lattice sites
x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
at lattice distance
k\in[1,Nd]
, it holds that

(3.14)

\int_{X_{N}}\frac{(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha}}{\chi_{N}}\left[\sqrt{f(\eta^{N,x,y})}-\sqrt{f(\eta^{N})}\right]^{2}\Pi^{N}_{a}(d\eta^{N})\leq k^{2}N^{-d}\mathfrak{d}_{N}(f_{N}).

Proof.

Thanks to the integration by parts property (
2.4
), the left-hand side is equal to

\chi_{N}^{-1}a^{\alpha}\int_{X_{N}}\left[\sqrt{f(\eta^{N}+\chi_{N}1_{y})}-\sqrt{f(\eta^{N}+\chi_{N}1_{x})}\right]^{2}\Pi^{N}_{a}(d\eta^{N})

where, as in the definition of the process,
\eta^{N}+\chi_{N}1_{x}\in X_{N}
denotes the configuration with one particle added at the side
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
. We now take a path
x_{0}=x,x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1},x_{k}=y,x_{j}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
whose length is the lattice distance
k
, and write the difference as the telescoping sum

(3.15)

\sqrt{f(\eta^{N}+\chi_{N}1_{y})}-\sqrt{f(\eta^{N}+\chi_{N}1_{x})}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sqrt{f(\eta^{N}+\chi_{N}1_{x_{j}})}-\sqrt{f(\eta^{N}+\chi_{N}1_{x_{j-1}})}.

Squaring and using Cauchy-Schwarz, the integral is at most

(3.16)

k\chi_{N}^{-1}a^{\alpha}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{X_{N}}\left[\sqrt{f(\eta^{N}+\chi_{N}1_{x_{j}})}-\sqrt{f(\eta^{N}+\chi_{N}1_{x_{j-1}})}\right]^{2}\Pi^{N}_{a}(d\eta^{N}).

Since
f_{N}\in G(\Pi^{N}_{a})
by assumption and each
x_{j-1},x_{j}
is a bond of the lattice, each term of the sum produces the same contribution, and reversing the integration by parts produces an upper-bound for the left-hand side of

k^{2}\int_{X_{N}}\frac{(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha}}{\chi_{N}}\left[\sqrt{f(\eta^{N,x,x_{1}})}-\sqrt{f(\eta^{N})}\right]^{2}\Pi^{N}_{a}(d\eta^{N}).

The integrand is now the contribution to
\mathfrak{d}_{N}(f_{N})
from the bond
x,x_{1}
, and since
f_{N}
is translationally invariant, each of the
dN^{d}
bonds of the lattice produces the same contribution. The remaining integral may therefore be estimated by
N^{-d}\mathfrak{d}_{N}(f_{N})
, which yields the claimed bound. ∎

We are finally ready to use Lemma
3.6
to make a statement about the averages of
\eta^{N}
on nearby lattice sites, or
\alpha
-averages of
\eta^{N}
on neighbouring lattice boxes. This makes good the claim in the introduction that we can obtain pathwise regularity across suitably chosen mesoscopic scales from regularity in probability space at the level of the Dirichlet form, and will be at the heart of the proof of Theorem
3
, c.f. (
1.5
) in the outline proof, and will additionally be used in the proof of Theorem
2
in Section

from (

Lemma 3.7
.

[Pathwise Regularity from Regularity of Laws]
There exists a constant
C
such that, for all
N
, for all
f_{N}\in G_{N}(\Pi^{N}_{a})
and lattice points
x,y
at lattice distance
k
,

(3.17)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\left[\left((\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha/2}-(\eta^{N}(y))^{\alpha/2}\right)^{2}\right]&\leq\chi_{N}k^{2}N^{-d}\mathfrak{d}_{N}(f_{N})\\
&+C\chi_{N}^{\delta}\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}(1+\|(\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})})\end{split}

where the exponent
\delta
is given by
\delta:=\min(1,\frac{\alpha}{2}).
Moreover, if
B,B^{\prime}
are lattice boxes of side length
l_{N}
, such that any

can be joined by a path of length
\widetilde{l}_{N}
, and

(3.18)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\left[\left((\Lambda_{B}\eta^{N})^{\alpha/2}-(\Lambda_{B^{\prime}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha/2}\right)^{2}\right]&\leq\chi_{N}\widetilde{l}_{N}^{2}N^{-d}\mathfrak{d}_{N}(f_{N})\\
&+CA^{1/2}l_{N}^{-d/2}\chi_{N}^{\delta}\mathbb{E}(1+\|(\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}).\end{split}

Proof.

First, we observe that for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, points
x,y
at a lattice distance
k
and
\eta^{N}\in X_{N}
, it holds that

(3.19)

-f_{N}(\eta^{N})(e^{\theta}-1)-f_{N}(\eta^{N,x,y})(e^{-\theta}-1)\leq\left(\sqrt{f_{N}(\eta^{N})}-\sqrt{f_{N}(\eta^{N,x,y})}\right)^{2}.

For the first claim (
3.17
), we take
\theta=\theta(\eta^{N},\eta^{N,x,y})
in the previous inequality to be

(3.20)

\theta:=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\log(\eta^{N}(y)+\chi_{N})-\log\eta^{N}(x)\right)

and multiply both sides by

\Pi^{N}_{a}(\eta^{N}){(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha}}=\Pi^{N}_{a}(\eta^{N,x,y}){(\eta^{N,x,y}(y))^{\alpha}}.

By changing variables in the second term, involving
f_{N}(\eta^{N,x,y})
, and observing that
\theta(\eta^{N,x,y},\eta^{N})=-\theta(\eta^{N},\eta^{N,x,y})
, both terms on the left-hand side give the same contribution, so we may consider only the first term. Repeating the calculations in
[
30
, Lemma 6.1]
, the contribution of the first term is bounded below by

(3.21)

\begin{split}&-\int_{X_{N}}f_{N}(\eta^{N})(\eta^{N}(y)^{\alpha/2}-\eta^{N}(x)^{\alpha/2})(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha/2}\Pi^{N}_{a}(d\eta^{N})\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt-c\int_{X_{N}}f_{N}(\eta^{N})\chi_{N}^{\min(1,\alpha/2)}(1+(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha}+(\eta^{N}(y))^{\alpha})\Pi^{N}_{a}(d\eta^{N}).\end{split}

Meanwhile, the summation over
\eta^{N}
on the right-hand side is
\chi_{N}
times the left-hand side of (
3.14
), which is therefore controlled by
\chi_{N}k^{2}N^{-d}\mathfrak{d}_{N}(f_{N})
, and the proof of (

) is complete.

The argument for (
3.18
) is similar. Let us first fix
x\in B,y\in B^{\prime}
, and note that the lattice distance between them is at most
\widetilde{l}_{N}
by hypothesis. Returning to (

), we now take

(3.22)

\theta=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\log(\Lambda_{B^{\prime}}\eta^{N,x,y})-\log(\Lambda_{B}\eta^{N})\right)

so that, multiplying by

and integrating the left-hand side contributes

(3.23)

-2\int_{X_{N}}f_{N}(\eta^{N})(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha}\left(\frac{(\Lambda_{B^{\prime}}\eta^{N,x,y})^{\alpha/2}}{(\Lambda_{B}\eta^{N})^{\alpha/2}}-1\right)\Pi^{N}_{a}(d\eta^{N}).

In the numerator, we use that

(3.24)

(\Lambda_{B^{\prime}}\eta^{N,x,y})^{\alpha}=(\Lambda_{B^{\prime}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}+\frac{w^{N}(y)}{w^{N}(B^{\prime})}\left((\eta^{N}(y)+\chi_{N})^{\alpha}-(\eta^{N}(y))^{\alpha}\right)

and the elementary inequality

(3.25)

\hskip-42.67912pt-2\int_{X_{N}}f_{N}(\eta^{N})(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha}\left(\frac{(\Lambda_{B^{\prime}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha/2}+(w^{N}(y)/w^{N}(B^{\prime}))^{1/2}\chi_{N}^{\delta}(1+(\eta^{N}(y))^{\alpha/2})}{(\Lambda_{B}\eta^{N})^{\alpha/2}}-1\right)\Pi^{N}_{a}(d\eta^{N}).

Meanwhile, the integral of the right-hand side is bounded by, using Lemma
3.6
,
\chi_{N}\widetilde{l}_{N}^{2}N^{-d}\mathfrak{d}_{N}(f_{N})
, which is therefore an upper bound for the expression in (
3.25
).

We now average this inequality over

with respect to the weighting
w^{N}
. The averaging in

replaces the occurrences of
(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha},(\eta^{N}(y))^{\alpha}
by
(\Lambda_{B}\eta^{N})^{\alpha},(\Lambda_{B^{\prime}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}
respectively, while the bound

implies that
w^{N}(y)\leq Al_{N}^{-d}w^{N}(B^{\prime})
. All together, for some constant

,

(3.26)

\begin{split}&-2\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\left[(\Lambda_{B}\eta^{N})^{\alpha/2}\left((\Lambda_{B^{\prime}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha/2}-(\Lambda_{B}\eta^{N})^{\alpha/2}\right)\right]\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt-CA^{1/2}\chi_{N}^{\delta}l_{N}^{-d/2}\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\left[(1+(\Lambda_{B}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}+(\Lambda_{B^{\prime}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\right]\leq\widetilde{l}_{N}^{2}N^{-d}\mathfrak{d}_{N}(f_{N})\end{split}

which may be post-processed into the claimed inequality as in the previous case. ∎

3.3.
Integrability Estimate via Scale Decomposition

We now arrive at the proof of Proposition
3.1

. For ease of readability, the proof is divided into the following Lemma, which gives one step of an iterative argument, and the subsequent assembly into an iterative scheme.

Lemma 3.8

(One Step of Multiscale Argument via Dirichlet form)
.

Let
1\leq l_{N}\leq\widetilde{l}_{N}\leq N
, and let
\mathcal{P}_{N}\in\mathcal{Q}(N,l_{N}),\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{N}\in\mathcal{Q}(N,\widetilde{l}_{N})
with
\mathcal{P}_{N}\prec\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{N}
, and let
w^{N}
be a weight function satisfying the bound
\frac{\sup w^{N}}{\inf w^{N}}\leq A
. Assume that, for all
B,B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{N},w^{N}(B)=w^{N}(B^{\prime})
, and similarly, for all
\widetilde{B},\widetilde{B}^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{N}}
,
w^{N}(\widetilde{B})=w^{N}(\widetilde{B}^{\prime})
. For
p=p(d)>1
given by Proposition
3.2
, let
\Phi
be a Young function such that (
3.9
) holds for this
p
, and let
\Psi
be the dual function. Then, for any an absolute constant
C=C(d,\Phi)
,
\delta=\delta(\alpha)>0
, for any
\lambda\in(0,1]
and for any translationally invariant
f_{N}\in G_{N}(\Pi^{N}_{a})
, it holds that

(3.27)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\left[\|(\Lambda_{B}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell_{\Phi}(B\in\mathcal{P}_{N},w^{N})}\right]&\leq(1+\lambda)\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\left[\|(\Lambda_{\widetilde{B}_{N}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell_{\Phi}(\widetilde{B}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{N},w^{N})}\right]\\[4.30554pt]
&\hskip-71.13188pt+C\lambda^{-1}A^{1/2}l_{N}^{-d/2}\left(\chi_{N}^{\delta/2}\Psi^{-1}(N^{d})\right)(\chi_{N}^{\delta/2}\widetilde{l}_{N}^{2}l_{N}^{-2})\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\left[1+\|(\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}\right]\\
&\hskip-71.13188pt+C(\lambda^{-1}\widetilde{l}_{N}N^{-1})\left(\widetilde{l}_{N}N^{-1}\Psi^{-1}(N^{d}\widetilde{l}_{N}^{-d})\right)\chi_{N}N^{2-d}\mathfrak{d}_{N}(f_{N}).\end{split}

Remark 3.9
.

This is the full version of the estimate (
1.18
), now in sufficient generality to cover the argument without the simplifying hypotheses. As discussed in the introduction, an additional parameter
\lambda\in(0,1)
has been introduced, in order to keep the prefactor of the iterative term bounded if the number of iterations is allowed to diverge. The various prefactors in the second and third lines are bracketed corresponding to the ways that the various compensations will be leveraged.

Proof.

We divide the proof into a probabilistic step, arguing from the Dirichlet form
\mathfrak{d}_{N}(f_{N})
, and an analytic step.

Step 1: One Block of
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{N}
via Dirichlet Form

Let us fix
\widetilde{B}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{N}
. In this step, we will show a
local
version of (
3.27
). Let us define

(3.28)

\Delta_{\widetilde{B}}(\eta^{N}):=\left[\left\|(\Lambda_{B}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\right\|_{\ell^{p}(B\in\mathcal{P}_{N}|_{\widetilde{B}},w^{N})}-(1+\lambda)(\Lambda_{\widetilde{B}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\right]_{+}

with
+
denoting the nonnegative part of a real number. We will show, in this step, that for any
f\in G_{N}(\Pi^{N}_{a})
,

(3.29)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}[\Delta_{\widetilde{B}}(\eta^{N})]\leq&C\lambda^{-1}\widetilde{l}_{N}^{2}N^{-d}\mathfrak{d}_{N}(f_{N})\\
&+CA^{1/2}\lambda^{-1}{\widetilde{l}_{N}^{2}}{l_{N}^{-2-d/2}}\chi_{N}^{\delta}\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\left[1+\|(\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}\right]\end{split}

where
\delta>0
is the same exponent as in Lemma
3.7
. Let us fix
f_{N}\in G_{N}(\Pi^{N}_{a})
. For any neighbouring boxes
B,B^{\prime}\subset\widetilde{B}
,
B,B^{\prime}\in P_{N}
, we recall from Lemma
3.7
, it holds that

(3.30)

\begin{split}\hskip-14.22636pt\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\left[\left((\Lambda_{B}\eta^{N})^{\alpha/2}-(\Lambda_{B^{\prime}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha/2}\right)^{2}\right]&\leq cl_{N}^{2}N^{-d}\mathfrak{d}_{N}(f_{N})\\
&+CA^{1/2}l_{N}^{-d/2}\chi_{N}^{\delta}\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}(1+\|(\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})})\end{split}

where we have used translation invariance of
f_{N}
to simplify the error term. Averaging over pairs of adjacent
B,B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{N},B,B^{\prime}\subset\widetilde{B}
, we find

(3.31)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\left[\left(\frac{l_{N}}{\widetilde{l}_{N}}\right)^{d-2}\sum_{B\sim B^{\prime}}\left((\Lambda_{B}\eta^{N})^{\alpha/2}-(\Lambda_{B^{\prime}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha/2}\right)^{2}\right]\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\leq C\widetilde{l}_{N}^{2}N^{-d}\mathfrak{d}_{N}(f_{N})+CA^{1/2}l_{N}^{-d/2}\left(\frac{\widetilde{l}_{N}}{l_{N}}\right)^{2}\chi_{N}^{\delta}\mathbb{E}_{f_{N}\Pi^{N}_{a}}\left[1+\|(\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}\right].\end{split}

Since the graph formed by the adjacency of boxes
B\subset\widetilde{B},B\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
is a lattice, with side lengths
\sim\widetilde{l}_{N}/l_{N}
, we now use the discrete Sobolev-Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality in Proposition
3.2
, for some

,
C=C(d,p)<\infty
, and all
\lambda\in(0,1)
,

(3.32)

\begin{split}\hskip-28.45274pt\left\|(\Lambda_{B}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\right\|_{\ell^{p}(B\in\mathcal{P}_{N}|_{\widetilde{B}},w^{N})}\leq C\lambda^{-1}\left(\frac{l_{N}}{\widetilde{l}_{N}}\right)^{d-2}\sum_{B\sim B^{\prime}}\left((\Lambda_{B}\eta^{N})^{\alpha/2}-(\Lambda_{B^{\prime}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha/2}\right)^{2}\\
+(1+\lambda)\frac{1}{\#(\mathcal{P}_{N}|_{\widetilde{B}})}\sum_{B\in\mathcal{P}_{N}:B\subset\widetilde{B}}(\Lambda_{B}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}.\end{split}

Since every
B\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
receives equal weight, the final term is exactly
(\Lambda_{\widetilde{B}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}
. Taking the second term onto the left-hand side, we see that

(3.33)

\begin{split}\hskip-28.45274pt\Delta_{\widetilde{B}}(\eta^{N})\leq C\lambda^{-1}\left(\frac{l_{N}}{\widetilde{l}_{N}}\right)^{d-2}\sum_{B\sim B^{\prime}}\left((\Lambda_{B}\eta^{N})^{\alpha/2}-(\Lambda_{B^{\prime}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha/2}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

Taking expectations of (
3.33
) and combining with (
3.31
) produces (
3.29
).

Step 2. From One Block to Global Estimate.

We now argue why (
3.29
) implies the conclusion (
3.27
). First, thanks to Lemma
3.3
,

(3.34)

\begin{split}\|(\Lambda_{B}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell_{\Phi}(B\in\mathcal{P}_{N},w^{N})}&\leq\left\|\|(\Lambda_{B}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{p}(B\in\mathcal{P}_{N}|_{\widetilde{B}},w^{N})}\right\|_{\ell_{\Phi}(\widetilde{B}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{N},w^{N})}\\
&\leq\left\|\Delta_{\widetilde{B}}(\eta^{N})+(1+\lambda)(\Lambda_{\widetilde{B}}\eta^{N})^{\alpha}\right\|_{\ell_{\Phi}(\widetilde{B}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{N},w^{N})}.\end{split}

Using the triangle inequality, the second term is one of the terms appearing in the right-hand side of (
3.27
). For the term involving
\Delta_{\widetilde{B}}(\eta^{N})
, we use the equivalence of the Orlicz and

A.1.
Proof of Lemma

Fix
\Phi,p,\mathcal{P}_{N},\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{N},w^{N}
as in the lemma, let

and let
\widetilde{h}
be as defined in the statement. Using the definitions of the Orlicz norms,
\widetilde{h}
and the Lebesgue norms, we write

(A.1)

\begin{split}\|\widetilde{h}(\widetilde{B})\|_{\ell_{\Phi}(\widetilde{B}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{N},w^{N})}&=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:\sum_{\widetilde{B}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{N}}\frac{w^{N}(\widetilde{B})}{w^{N}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}\Phi\left(\frac{\|h\|_{\ell^{p},\mathcal{P}_{N}|_{\widetilde{B}}}}{t}\right)\leq 1\right\}\\

&=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:\sum_{\widetilde{B}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{N}}\frac{w^{N}(\widetilde{B})}{w^{N}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}\Phi\left(\left[\sum_{B\in\mathcal{P}_{N}|_{\widetilde{B}}}\frac{w^{N}(B)}{w^{N}(\widetilde{B})}\left(\frac{h(B)}{t}\right)^{p}\right]^{1/p}\right)\leq 1\right\}.\end{split}

Recalling that
\Theta(u):=(\Phi^{-1}(u))^{p}
is assumed to be convex, we can rewrite the argument of
\Phi
in the final expression as

(A.2)

\left[\sum_{B\in\mathcal{P}_{N}|_{\widetilde{B}}}\frac{w^{N}(B)}{w^{N}(\widetilde{B})}\left(\frac{h(B)}{t}\right)^{p}\right]^{1/p}=\Phi^{-1}\left(\Theta^{-1}\left[\sum_{B\in\mathcal{P}_{N}|_{\widetilde{B}}}\frac{w^{N}(B)}{w^{N}(\widetilde{B})}\Theta\left(\Phi\left[\frac{h(B)}{t}\right]\right)\right]\right).

Since

(A.3)

\left[\sum_{B\in\mathcal{P}_{N}|_{\widetilde{B}}}\frac{w^{N}(B)}{w^{N}(\widetilde{B})}\left(\frac{h(B)}{t}\right)^{p}\right]^{1/p}\geq\Phi^{-1}\left(\sum_{B\in\mathcal{P}_{N}|_{\widetilde{B}}}\frac{w^{N}(B)}{w^{N}(\widetilde{B})}\Phi\left[\frac{h(B)}{t}\right]\right).

Returning to (

(A.4)

\begin{split}\|\widetilde{h}(\widetilde{B})\|_{\ell_{\Phi}(\widetilde{B}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{N},w^{N})}\geq\inf\left\{t\geq 0:\sum_{\widetilde{B}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{N}}\frac{w^{N}(\widetilde{B})}{w^{N}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}\sum_{B\in\mathcal{P}_{N}|_{\widetilde{B}}}\frac{w^{N}(B)}{w^{N}(\widetilde{B})}\Phi\left(\frac{h(B)}{t}\right)\leq 1\right\}.\end{split}

The double summation collapses to a single summation over

Proof of Lemma lemma: orlicz interpolation

We now prove the interpolation in Lemma

Proof of Proposition

.

Let
\chi_{N}\to 0

\Phi

\sup_{u\geq 1}u^{-1/d}\Psi^{-1}(u)<\infty;\qquad\sup_{N}\chi_{N}^{\delta/2}\Psi^{-1}(N^{d})<\infty

and such that

\Theta(u):=(\Phi^{-1}(u))^{p}

is also convex. Observe first that if (

) holds for a Young function
\Phi
and

is another Young function with the pointwise inequality

, then (
A.8
) automatically also holds for
\widehat{\Phi}
, since each operation of the convex dualisation and functional inverse reverse the ordering of pointwise inequality to yield

and concavity of
\Psi^{-1}

inherits the property (

We thus show in a first step how a strict Young function satisfying (

may be chosen to impose (

Step 1. Constructing
\Phi
to impose (
A.8

Since the condition (
A.8
) only involves
\Psi^{-1}

\Psi
whose inverse function
\Psi^{-1}
satisfies both inequalities, and define
\Phi
implicitly by taking it to be the convex dual of
\Psi

Set
\Psi_{1}(x):=|x|^{d}

be a strictly increasing enumeration of the set
\{0\}\cup\{\chi_{N}^{-\delta/2}:N\geq 1\}

We now define
\Psi_{2}

\Psi_{2}^{\prime}(x):=\max\left\{\frac{y_{n+1}-y_{n}}{x_{n+1}-x_{n}}:n\geq 0,x_{n}\leq x\right\}.

The postulated expression is nondecreasing and locally finite, so that this uniquely specifies a convex function

for all

Step 2. Imposition of the Convexity Condition (

Let us now assume that we are given a strict Young function
\Phi
and an index

; the task is to construct a new strict Young function

satisfying, for some constant

We now consider the ODE

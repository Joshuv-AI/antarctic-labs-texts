A simpler path to Ergodic Theorems for the Frontier of Branching Brownian Motion
Gabriel Flath

Abstract
We revisit the ergodic theorem for the frontier of branching Brownian motion (BBM). Motivated by the proof of Arguin, Bovier, and Kistler [ 2 ] , we provide a shorter and more direct argument. It relies on two observations: pairs of extremal particles observed at distinct times far apart must have branched early, and pairs of early-branching extremal particles have negatively correlated positions. This yields the ergodic theorem for BBM. We also address a gap in the path localization argument of [ 2 ] .



1
Introduction and results

The (standard binary) Branching Brownian motion (BBM) is a fundamental stochastic process describing the evolution of particles that move according to independent Brownian motions and undergo (binary) branching. It occupies a central position in probability theory and has deep connections with population models, reaction–diffusion equations, and disordered systems. The process starts from a single particle at the origin performing standard Brownian motion; each particle lives for an exponentially distributed time (with mean one), then splits into two offspring located at its current position. The descendants evolve independently according to the same dynamics.

More formally, let
\mathcal{N}_{t}
be the collection of particles at time
t
. For
v\in\mathcal{N}_{t}
and
s\in\left[0,t\right]
, we say that
u\in\mathcal{N}_{s}
is its ancestor a time
s
if
v
is a descendant of
u
and denote it
u\preceq v
. For
u\in\mathcal{N}_{t}
and
s\in\left[0,t\right]
, we write
X_{u}(s)\in\mathbb{R}
for the position of particle
u
(or its ancestor) at time
s
. We denote by
M_{t}:=\max_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{t}}X_{u}(t)
the position of the rightmost particle at time
t
. We define the branching Brownian motion process to be
X(t)=(X_{u}(t),u\in\mathcal{N}_{t})
and its natural filtration
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma\{X(s),s\leq t\}
.

In a series of seminal works, Bramson
[
5
]
, showed that the position
M_{t}
of the rightmost particle in a BBM is centred around
m_{t}:=\sqrt{2}t-\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}\log t
, and more precisely that

M_{t}-m_{t}\xrightarrow[t\rightarrow\infty]{d}W,

where
W
is a random variable whose distribution function is given by the so-called critical traveling wave solution to the Fisher-KPP equation. Lalley and Sellke
[
8
]
showed that the corresponding pointwise ergodic convergence
cannot hold, i.e. that for
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

F_{T}(x):=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}\{M_{t}-m_{t}\leq x\}\,dt\not\to P(W\leq x)\text{ as }T\to\infty\text{ almost surely}.

They also proved that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\mathbb{P}(M_{t}-m_{t}\leq x)\to\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-CZ_{\infty}e^{-\sqrt{2}x}}\right],

(1)

where
Z_{\infty}>0
is the almost sure limit of the derivative martingale:

Z(t)=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{t}}(\sqrt{2}t-X_{u}(t))e^{\sqrt{2}(X_{u}(t)-\sqrt{2}t)}.

They further conjectured that this convergence holds in an ergodic sens. This was proved by Arguin, Bovier, and Kistler
[
2
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Ergodic Theorem for
M_{t}
, Theorem 1 in
[
2
]
)
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\lim_{T\to\infty}F_{T}(x)=\exp\left(-CZ_{\infty}e^{-\sqrt{2}x}\right),\quad\text{a.s.}

where
C>0
is a positive constant.

The main goal of this work is twofold: first we give a shorter and more direct proof of this result, and secondly, while doing so we obtain results about the genealogy of extremal particles which are of independent interest.

To establish Theorem
1.1
, we develop a new approach based on distinguishing whether pairs of particles branch early.
For
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}_{s}\times\mathcal{N}_{t}
we define their splitting time

Q(u,v):=\sup\{\gamma\geq 0:X_{u}(\phi)=X_{v}(\phi)\ \text{for all }\phi\leq\gamma\}.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
t\geq 0
, set

\mathcal{N}_{t}(x):=\{u\in\mathcal{N}_{t}:\ X_{u}(t)-m_{t}\geq x\}
, the set of particles above
m_{t}+x
at time
t
.

The next result quantifies the fact that pairs of extremal particles observed at distinct times far apart must have branched early.

Theorem 1.2

(Early branching across timescales)
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}
. For any
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
,
there exists
\ell>0
such that for all
R\ \geq\ell
and
0<s<t
with
t-s\ \geq\ell
and
s\ \geq\ell
,

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\exists(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}_{s}(x)\times\mathcal{N}_{t}(x):\ Q(u,v)\geq R\right)\ \leq\ \min\{\,t-s,\ s,\ R\,\}^{-\tfrac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

(2)

Proof of the main results

In this section, we establish Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, proceeding via several intermediate results concerning the path localisation and genealogy of the particles. The proofs of some of these intermediate results are deferred to Section
4
.

We now introduce a path localization and state two intermediate lemmas before turning to the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Definition 2.1

(
\alpha
-
t
-localized particle)
.

Fix
0<\alpha<1/2
. Let
t>0
and
r\in[0,t/2]
.
A particle
u\in\mathcal{N}_{t}
is said to be
\alpha
-
t
-localized
on
[r,t-r]
if, for every
s\in[r,t-r]
,

X_{u}(s)\;\leq\;\frac{s}{t}\,m_{t}\;-\;\min\{s,\,t-s\}^{\alpha}.

Otherwise,
u
is said to be
not localized
on
[r,t-r]
.

The following lemma is a path localization estimate, in the spirit of Theorem 2.3 in
[
1
]
, but stated here with an explicit bound. The proof relies on an entropic repulsion argument implemented with two time scales, following the approach of Proposition 4.1 in
[
6
]
. This yields the following bound.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
0<\alpha<1/2
and let
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
For any
\delta>0
, there exists
r_{0}
such that, for all
r\geq r_{0}
and
t\geq 3r
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\!\Bigl(&\exists\,u\in\mathcal{N}_{t}(x)\text{ not $\alpha$-$t$-localized on }[\,r,\,t-r\,]\Bigr)\;\leq\;r^{\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}+\delta}.\end{split}

(3)

The proof of Lemma
2.2
is deferred to Section
4
.

The following lemma quantifies that localized extremal particles at distant timescales cannot have branched too late. Its proof is deferred to Section
4
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
0<\alpha<1/2
and let
x\in\mathbb{R}
. There exist constants
C=C(x,\alpha)
and
r_{0}
such that, for all
r\geq r_{0}
,
R\geq 2r
,
s\geq 3r
and
t\geq s+2r
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\!\Bigl(&\exists(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}_{s}(x)\times\mathcal{N}_{t}(x)\colon u\text{ is $\alpha$--$s$--localized on }[r,s-r],\\
&\quad v\text{ is $\alpha$--$t$--localized on }[r,t-r],\text{ and }Q(u,v)\geq R\Bigr)\\
&\;\leq\;C\sqrt{r}\left(r\frac{e^{-\sqrt{2}R^{\alpha}}}{\sqrt{R}}+(t-s)e^{-\sqrt{2}(t-s)^{\alpha}}\right).\end{split}

(4)

We now combine Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
. Choose
\alpha\in(0,\varepsilon/2)
and set

\delta:=\varepsilon/2-\alpha>0
.
For
0<s<t
and
R>0
set

m:=\min\{t-s,\ s,\ R\},\qquad r:=m/3.

For
m\geq\ell
(with
\ell
fixed below), the conditions of Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
are satisfied:
r\geq r_{0}
,
s\geq 3r,\ R\geq 2r,\ t\geq s+2r
.

Let
E
be the event in (
2
). Write

A_{s}:=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\exists u\in\mathcal{N}_{s}(x)\ \text{not $\alpha$--$s$--localized on }[r,s-r]\right),

and define
A_{t}
similarly. Let

B:=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\exists(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}_{s}(x)\times\mathcal{N}_{t}(x):\ u,v\ \text{are localized, and }Q(u,v)\geq R\right)

be the probability in (
4
).
Then
\mathbb{P}(E)\leq A_{s}+A_{t}+B
.

By Lemma
2.2
,

A_{s}+A_{t}\ \leq\ 2\,r^{\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}+\delta}\ \leq\ C_{0}\,m^{-\tfrac{1}{2}+\varepsilon/2}.

By Lemma
2.3
and since
m\leq R,t-s
, there exists
\ell_{1}
such that for
m\geq\ell_{1}
,

B\ \leq\ C\sqrt{r}\!\left(r\frac{e^{-\sqrt{2}R^{\alpha}}}{\sqrt{R}}+(t-s)\,e^{-\sqrt{2}(t-s)^{\alpha}}\right)\ \leq\ C\,m^{-\tfrac{1}{2}+\varepsilon/2}.

Thus, for
m
large enough (say
m\geq\ell:=\max\{3r_{0},\ell_{1},(C_{0}+C)^{2/\varepsilon}\}
),

\mathbb{P}(E)\ \leq\ (C_{0}+C)\,m^{-\tfrac{1}{2}+\varepsilon/2}\ \leq\ m^{-\tfrac{1}{2}+\varepsilon},

which establishes (
2
).
∎

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
We first recall and follow the strategy of Arguin, Bovier, and Kistler
[
2
]
.
Let
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we introduce a cutoff parameter
\varepsilon>0
and split the time integral at
\varepsilon T
:

F_{T}(x)=\frac{1}{T}\int_{\varepsilon T}^{T}\mathbf{1}\{M_{t}-m_{t}\leq x\}\,dt+\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon T}\mathbf{1}\{M_{t}-m_{t}\leq x\}\,dt.

(5)

The second term does not contribute in the limit as
T\to\infty
first and
\varepsilon\to 0
next. It therefore suffices to compute the double limit for the first term.

To this end, let
R_{T}>0
and decompose the empirical distribution on
[\varepsilon T,T]
as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{\varepsilon T}^{T}\mathbf{1}\{M_{t}-m_{t}\leq x\}

\displaystyle\,dt=\frac{1}{T}\int_{\varepsilon T}^{T}\mathbb{P}\!\left(M_{t}-m_{t}\leq x\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{R_{T}}\right)\,dt

(6)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{T}\int_{\varepsilon T}^{T}\Bigl(\mathbf{1}\{M_{t}-m_{t}\leq x\}-\mathbb{P}\!\left(M_{t}-m_{t}\leq x\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{R_{T}}\right)\Bigr)\,dt.

Theorem 2 in
[
2
]
established that,
for
R_{T}\to\infty
as
T\to\infty
with
R_{T}=o(\sqrt{T})
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\;\lim_{T\to\infty}\;\frac{1}{T}\int_{\varepsilon T}^{T}\mathbb{P}\!\left(M_{t}-m_{t}\leq x\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{R_{T}}\right)\,dt=\exp\left(-CZ_{\infty}e^{-\sqrt{2}x}\right),\quad\text{a.s.}

Consequently, by Theorem 2 of
[
2
]
, to establish Theorem
1.1
it remains only to show that the second term on the right-hand side of (
6
) converges to zero almost surely. This is the content of the next theorem, whose proof occupies the remainder of this section.

Theorem 2.4
.

Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta>0
, and define
R_{T}=\log(T)^{2+\delta}
. Then

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{\varepsilon T}^{T}\Bigl(\mathbf{1}\{M_{t}-m_{t}\geq x\}-\mathbb{P}\!\left(M_{t}-m_{t}\geq x\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{R_{T}}\right)\Bigr)\,dt=0,\quad\text{a.s.}

Note that a similar statement appears as Theorem 3 in
[
2
]
. However, as we explain in Section
5
, there is a gap in their proof, which is resolved by the alternative argument we provide here. The key step is the following result.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
0\leq R\leq s\leq t
, let
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
u_{s}^{*}
(resp.
u_{t}^{*}
) denote the rightmost particle at time
s
(resp.
t
). Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\!\left(M_{s}\geq x,\,M_{t}\geq y,\,Q(u^{*}_{s},u^{*}_{t})\leq R\,\middle|\mathcal{F}_{R}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\!\left(M_{s}\geq x\,\middle|\mathcal{F}_{R}\right)\mathbb{P}\!\left(M_{t}\geq y\,\middle|\mathcal{F}_{R}\right).

(7)

Before proving Proposition
2.5
, we state the following corollary of the van den Berg–Kesten–Reimer inequality. Its proof is deferred to Section
4
.

Lemma 2.6

(BKR corollary for cross–index unions)
.

Let
U
be an index set.
For each
u\in U
, let
(A_{u},E_{u})
be a pair of events.
Assume that the family of sigma-algebras
\{\sigma(A_{u},E_{u}):u\in U\}
is mutually independent.
Then

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\in U\\
u\neq v\end{subarray}}A_{u}\cap E_{v}\right)\;\leq\;\mathbb{P}\!\left(\bigcup_{u\in U}A_{u}\right)\,\mathbb{P}\!\left(\bigcup_{u\in U}E_{u}\right).

Proof of Proposition
2.5
.

For
u\in\mathcal{N}_{R}
, define

M^{u}_{s}\coloneqq\max_{v\in\mathcal{N}_{s}:u\preceq v}X_{v}(s)\quad\text{(resp. }M^{u}_{t}\text{)}

as the rightmost particle at time
s
(resp.
t
) in the subtree rooted at
u
, and set

A_{u}:=\{M^{u}_{s}\geq x\},\qquad E_{u}:=\{M^{u}_{t}\geq y\}.

On
\{Q(u_{s}^{*},u_{t}^{*})\leq R\}
the rightmost particles at times
s
and
t
have distinct ancestors at time
R
; hence

\{M_{s}\geq x,\ M_{t}\geq y,\ Q(u_{s}^{*},u_{t}^{*})\leq R\}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\in\mathcal{N}_{R}\\
u\neq v\end{subarray}}\bigl(A_{u}\cap E_{v}\bigr)\quad\text{a.s.}

Condition on
\mathcal{F}_{R}
. By the branching property, the descendant trees below distinct
u\in\mathcal{N}_{R}
are independent. Hence the family
\{(A_{u},E_{u})\}_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{R}}
satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma
2.6
. Applying that lemma yields

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\in\mathcal{N}_{R}\\
u\neq v\end{subarray}}(A_{u}\cap E_{v})\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{R}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\!\left(\bigcup_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{R}}A_{u}\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{R}\right)\mathbb{P}\!\left(\bigcup_{v\in\mathcal{N}_{R}}E_{v}\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{R}\right).

Finally,

\bigcup_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{R}}A_{u}=\{M_{s}\geq x\},\qquad\bigcup_{v\in\mathcal{N}_{R}}E_{v}=\{M_{t}\geq y\},

so the right-hand side equals

\mathbb{P}(M_{s}\geq x\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{R})\,\mathbb{P}(M_{t}\geq y\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{R})
,
which establishes (
7
), as claimed.
∎

We now prove Theorem
2.4
.

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

Let
R:=R_{T}=(\log T)^{2+\delta}
for some
\delta>0
. For
t\geq 0
set

A_{t}:=\{M_{t}-m_{t}\geq x\},\qquad Y_{t}:=\mathbf{1}_{A_{t}}-\mathbb{P}(A_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{R}),\qquad Z_{T}:=\frac{1}{T}\int_{\varepsilon T}^{T}Y_{t}\,dt.

The statement of Theorem
2.4
is that
Z_{T}\to 0
almost surely as
T\to\infty
.

Square and symmetrize, then split to yield

Z_{T}^{2}=\frac{2}{T^{2}}\int_{\varepsilon T}^{T}\!\!\int_{s}^{T}Y_{s}Y_{t}\,dt\,ds=\underbrace{\frac{2}{T^{2}}\int_{\varepsilon T}^{T}\!\!\int_{s}^{(s+R)\wedge T}Y_{s}Y_{t}\,dt\,ds}_{=:I_{T}}+\underbrace{\frac{2}{T^{2}}\int_{\varepsilon T}^{T}\!\!\int_{(s+R)\wedge T}^{T}Y_{s}Y_{t}\,dt\,ds}_{=:J_{T}}.

Since
|Y_{s}Y_{t}|\leq 1
,
I_{T}\leq 2R/T
, thus
\mathbb{E}[I_{T}]\leq 2R/T
. We now bound
\mathbb{E}[J_{T}]
. For
R\leq s\leq t
, applying Proposition
2.5
on
\{Q(u_{s}^{*},u_{t}^{*})\leq R\}
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[Y_{s}Y_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{R}]&\leq\mathbb{P}\!\big(A_{s}\cap A_{t}\cap\{Q(u_{s}^{*},u_{t}^{*})\geq R\}\mid\mathcal{F}_{R}\big)\\
&\leq P\!\left(\exists(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}_{s}(x)\times\mathcal{N}_{t}(x):\ Q(u,v)\geq R\mid\mathcal{F}_{R}\right).\end{split}

Therefore, for any
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, Theorem
1.2
gives, for sufficiently large
T
(and therefore sufficiently large
R
),

\mathbb{E}[J_{T}]\leq\frac{2}{T^{2}}\int_{\varepsilon T}^{T}\!\!\int_{s+R}^{T}\mathbb{E}[Y_{s}Y_{t}]\,dt\,ds\leq\frac{2}{T^{2}}\int_{\varepsilon T}^{T}\!\!\int_{s+R}^{T}R^{-\alpha}\,dt\,ds\leq R^{-\alpha}.

Let
T_{n}=e^{n^{\beta}}
with
\beta\in(0,1)
. Let
\eta>0
, by Markov’s inequality,

\sum_{n}\mathbb{P}\big(Z_{T_{n}}^{2}>\eta\big)\leq\eta^{-1}\sum_{n}\mathbb{E}[I_{T_{n}}]+\mathbb{E}[J_{T_{n}}]\leq\eta^{-1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2(\log T_{n})^{2+\delta}}{T_{n}}+(\log T_{n})^{-(2+\delta)\alpha}<\ \infty,

whenever

\beta(2+\delta)\alpha>1,

which is achievable for every
\delta>0
by taking
\beta\in\big(\tfrac{2}{2+\delta},1\big)
and
\alpha
arbitrarily close to
\tfrac{1}{2}
.
Hence,
Z_{T_{n}}\to 0
almost surely.

Convergence along the subsequence
(T_{n})
implies that
Z_{T}\to 0
almost surely as
T\to\infty
, by the following lemma.
Indeed, convergence of time averages along a subsequence
T_{n}
with asymptotically negligible gaps (that is,
T_{n+1}/T_{n}\to 1
) entails convergence along the full time axis.
The proof is deferred to Section
4
.

Lemma 2.7

(Subsequence-to-full-time convergence for time averages)
.

Let
x:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function with
|x(t)|\leq 1
for all
t\geq 0
.
For
T>0
, define the time averages

\rho_{T}:=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}x(t)\,dt.

Let
(S_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an increasing sequence with
S_{n}\to\infty
and
S_{n+1}/S_{n}\to 1
.
If

\rho_{S_{n}}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0,

then

\rho_{T}\xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{}0.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
2.4
.
∎

Brownian estimates and toolbox

This section collects several useful results, primarily from Bramson
[
5
]
and Arguin et al.
[
1
]
.
Define the probability measure
\mathbb{P}_{0,x}^{t,y}
,
under which
B_{t}
is a Brownian bridge from
x
at time
0
to
y
at time
t
.
For a function
l:[s_{1},s_{2}]\mapsto\mathbb{R}
, we denote
B_{l}
(or
B_{l}[s_{1},s_{2}]
in case of ambiguity) the set of paths lying strictly above
l
. Similarly,
B^{l}
the set of paths lying strictly below
l
.

The following is a well-known expression for the probability that a Brownian bridge stays non-negative.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
t>0
, and
x,y\geq 0
,

\mathbb{P}_{0,x}^{t,y}(B_{s}\geq 0,s\in[0,t])=1-e^{-\frac{2xy}{t}}.

(8)

The following result provides an upper bound on the probability that a Brownian bridge stays below an affine function.

Lemma 3.2

(Lemma 3.4,
[
1
]
)
.

Let
Z_{1},Z_{2}\geq 0
and
r_{1},r_{2}\geq 0
. Then, for
t>r_{1}+r_{2}
,

\mathbb{P}_{0,0}^{t,0}\left(B_{s}\leq\left(1-\frac{s}{t}\right)Z_{1}+\frac{s}{t}Z_{2},\quad\forall s\in[r_{1},t-r_{2}]\right)\leq\frac{2}{t-r_{1}-r_{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\left(Z(r_{i})+\sqrt{r_{i}}\right),

where

Z(r_{1}):=\left(1-\frac{r_{1}}{t}\right)Z_{1}+\frac{r_{1}}{t}Z_{2},\quad Z(r_{2}):=\frac{r_{2}}{t}Z_{1}+\left(1-\frac{r_{2}}{t}\right)Z_{2}.

The following identity, derived from the reflection principle, gives the probability that a Brownian bridge remains non-negative over an interval.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
y>0
and
0<r<\gamma
,

\mathbb{P}(\forall s\in[r,\gamma]\,,\,B_{s}\geq 0\mid B_{\gamma}=y)=1-2\mathbb{P}(B_{r}\leq 0\mid B_{\gamma}=y).

(9)

We now state a useful monotonicity result.

Lemma 3.4

(Lemma 2.6,
[
5
]
)
.

Assume that
l_{1}(s)
,
l_{2}(s)
, and
\wedge(s)
satisfy
l_{1}(s)\leq l_{2}(s)\leq\wedge(s)
for
s\in[0,t]
, and that
\mathbb{P}(B_{l_{2}}[0,t])>0
. Then,

\mathbb{P}_{0,0}^{t,0}(B^{\wedge}\mid B_{l_{1}})\geq\mathbb{P}_{0,0}^{t,0}(B^{\wedge}\mid B_{l_{2}}),

(10)

and

\mathbb{P}_{0,0}^{t,0}(B_{\wedge}\mid B_{l_{1}})\leq\mathbb{P}_{0,0}^{t,0}(B_{\wedge}\mid B_{l_{2}}).

(11)

define the function
\Gamma_{t,\alpha}(s)\coloneqq(s\wedge(t-s))^{\alpha}
for
\alpha\geq 0
. The following result establishes that if a Brownian bridge remains above
-\Gamma_{t,\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
on the interval
[r,t-r]
, then it remains above
\Gamma_{t,\alpha}
on the same interval.

Lemma 3.5

(Proposition 6.1,
[
5
]
)
.

Let
C>0
and for all
t
,
L_{t}:[0,t]\mapsto\mathbb{R}
be a family of functions.
For any
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, suppose there exists an
r_{0}
such that
L_{t}(s)\leq C\Gamma_{t,\alpha}(s)
for all
s\in[r_{0},t-r_{0}]
and for all
t
. Then for any
\delta>0
, there exists
r_{1}
such that for all
r\geq r_{1}
and
t\geq 3r
,

\frac{\mathbb{P}_{0,0}^{t,0}(\forall s\in[r,t-r]:B_{s}>L_{t}(s)+C\Gamma_{t,\alpha}(s))}{\mathbb{P}_{0,0}^{t,0}(\forall s\in[r,t-r]:B_{s}>L_{t}(s)-C\Gamma_{t,\alpha}(s))}\geq 1-r^{\alpha-1/2+\delta}.

(12)

Proof.

Inequality (
12
) follows directly from the proof of Proposition 6.1 in
[
5
]
. For any
\epsilon\in(1/2,1-\alpha)
, there exist constants
C_{3}
,
a_{1}
, and
a_{2}
such that for large
r
, the LHS of (
12
) is greater than

\left(e^{-C_{3}r^{\alpha+\epsilon-1}}\right)\left(1-a_{1}\sum_{k=\left\lceil r\right\rceil}^{+\infty}ke^{-a_{2}k^{2\epsilon-1}}\right),

(13)

uniformly in
t
. This lower bound is derived from Eqs. (6.9) and (6.10) and Lemmas 2.6 and 2.7 in
[
5
]
. Therefore, for any given
\delta>0
, choosing
\epsilon\in(1/2,\min(1/2+\delta,1-\alpha))
in the lower bound (
13
) yields inequality (
12
) for sufficiently large
r
.
∎

5
Discussion and related works

Genealogy of the front of BBM.

Arguin, Bovier, and Kistler
[
1
]
showed that two extremal particles at time
t
either branch very early or very recently.
Later,
[
6
]
established that particles chosen randomly within a sublinear distance of the front at time
t
necessarily branched early.
Theorem
1.2
extends this picture to different times: if
t-s
is large, extremal particles at times
t
and
s
must have branched early.
This is consistent with
[
1
]
, since for bounded
t-s
, continuity still permits recent branching.

Ergodic convergence of functionals of extremal particles.

The approach developed to prove Theorem
2.4
extends to any functional of
M_{t}
, with only cosmetic modifications in the proof of Proposition
2.5
.
Moreover, one can prove the ergodic convergence of other functionals, such as the Laplace functional studied in
[
3
]
:

\mathcal{L}_{t}(f):=\exp\!\Big(-\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\,\mathcal{E}_{t}(dy)\Big),\quad\mathcal{E}_{t}:=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{t}}\delta_{\,X_{u}(t)-m_{t}},\quad f\in C_{c}^{+}(\mathbb{R}).

Let

E:=\left\{\forall(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}_{s}(x)\times\mathcal{N}_{t}(x):\ Q(u,v)\leq R\right\}.

One can prove a negative-correlation result analogous to Proposition
2.5
:

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathcal{L}_{s}(f)\geq x,\,\mathcal{L}_{t}(f)\geq y,\,E\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{R}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathcal{L}_{s}(f)\geq x\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{R}\right]\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathcal{L}_{t}(f)\geq y\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{R}\right],

so that the proof of Theorem
2.4
carries over verbatim for
\mathcal{L}_{t}(f)
.

To establish an ergodic theorem for these functionals, it remains to identify the almost-sure limit

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\;\lim_{T\to\infty}\;\frac{1}{T}\int_{\varepsilon T}^{T}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathcal{L}_{t}(f)\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{R_{T}}\right]\,dt.

For the Laplace functional, this limit has already been identified; see Proposition 9 in
[
3
]
. More generally, the present
approach applies to a broader class of functionals. For these, it is useful to observe that, by combining Lemmas
2.2
and
2.7
as in the proof of Theorem
2.5
, one may restrict attention to localized particles.

Almost sure ergodic vs almost sure pathwise localization.
Theorem
1.1
was first established in
[
2
]
. A key step in their proof is Theorem 3 in
[
2
]
(Theorem
2.4
here), supported by several technical lemmas including Lemma 7 in
[
2
]
. The proof of Lemma 7 in
[
2
]
relies on Proposition 6 in
[
2
]
, which asserts the following: For
0<\alpha<1/2
, there exist
r_{0}>0
and
\delta>0
such that, for all
r\geq r_{0}
and
t\geq 3r
,

\mathbb{P}\!\Bigl(\exists\,u\in\mathcal{N}_{t}(\mathcal{D})\text{ not $\alpha$-$t$-localized on }[\,r,\,t-r\,]\Bigr)\;\leq\;e^{-r^{\delta}}.

This estimate is strong enough for a Borel–Cantelli argument, which implies that for
r_{t}=(20\log t)^{1/\delta}
,

\text{1}\Bigl\{\exists\,u\in\mathcal{N}_{t}(\mathcal{D})\text{ not $\alpha$-$t$-localized on }[\,r_{t},\,t-r_{t}\,]\Bigr\}\;\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{\text{a.s.}}\;0.

However, as suggested by the mechanism in the proof of
Theorem 1.3 in
[
6
]
, such a pathwise localization does not hold almost surely (although it
does in probability). Consequently, Proposition 6 cannot be correct as stated. Importantly, Lemma 7
itself is formulated in terms of an
ergodic
localization rather than a pathwise localization.
The distinction is crucial: while almost sure pathwise localization fails, almost sure ergodic
localization remains valid. This can be proved combining Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.7
as in the proof of Theorem
2.5
.

The issue in Proposition 6 originates from a bound in
[
1
]
. In equation (5.55) there, the multiplicative factor
e^{\kappa_{3}r^{\alpha+\theta-1}}
appearing in equation (5.49) is omitted  which is immaterial for their purposes. The precise bound is derived in Lemma
2.2
by introducing two time scales
r
and
R
implementing the entropic repulsion argument from
[
1
]
. The bound in Lemma
2.2
is sufficient for Theorem 2.3 in
[
1
]
to hold. However, it is not strong enough to yield an almost sure path localization using a Borel-Cantelli type argument.

Workforce adaptability and social influences on robotic process automation in sustainable transport
Authors: R K Tailor, Ashish Goyal
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study used an expanded technology acceptance framework that takes compliance and safety into account to investigate the uptake of Robotic Process Automation (RPA) in the transportation industry. To put this all-encompassing model to the test, we surveyed 190 people who work in the transportation industry. The results revealed several significant associations. When workers have faith in the system, they are far more likely to use it, according to the model. On the other hand, if people think technology is dangerous, they are less likely to use it. An important finding is that RPA has a strong correlation with increased compliance and safety in transportation, suggesting that this technology can greatly benefit operational safety. When it came to behavioral intention, actual use, and safety outcomes, the study’s statistical model was very effective. Building trust and managing risk perceptions are two examples of the human and organizational elements that must be considered for RPA implementation in the transportation sector to be a success, according to the research. Businesses that want to use RPA to keep safety and compliance standards high can benefit greatly from these insights.Keywords: Robotic process automation (RPA), Workforce adaptability, Technology acceptance model (TAM), Digital transformation, Transport sector innovation, Social dynamics

Introduction

The way businesses handle their personnel and operational dynamics has changed significantly with the introduction of robotic process automation (RPA) in sustainable transportation. The human aspect of this technology shift becomes crucial as transportation organizations use more frequently RPA solutions to improve sustainability and efficiency. According to recent studies by1, emphasize that effective RPA use depends much on worker adaptation and societal acceptability than merely on technological expertise. The staff has to not only adapt to new automated technologies but also negotiate challenging social dynamics that develop throughout this change2. These developments influence everything including long-term career pathways and everyday operational processes therefore posing a complex problem that goes beyond simple technology application.

The complex challenge of reconciling automation advantages with worker concerns presents for transportation companies makes sure RPA implementation improves rather than disturbs current social structures and working relationships. Studies carried out by3,4 show that companies which successfully use RPA usually show great respect for social dynamics and worker adaptability patterns. This delicate equilibrium calls for knowledge of employee perceptions and interactions with RPA systems, their readiness to modify current skill sets, and the effects on team relationships and company culture. The success rates of RPA are highly influenced by the employee view of it, with good implementation results and positive relationships between supporting corporate cultures5.

Moreover, as sustainability becomes increasingly central to transportation operations, the adoption process becomes much more difficult when the workforce has to match their flexibility with environmental objectives6. The companies combining RPA adoption with sustainability goals exhibit better environmental results and higher degrees of staff acceptability7. Because of long-standing reluctance to change in technology and worries about job stability and the development of roles, a holistic strategy that takes into account the human element of RPA adoption is required5. Organizations that employed explicit communication methods and comprehensive training programs achieved 40% greater success rates in RPA adoption than those concentrating exclusively on technical deployment8.

The current literature identifies significant gaps in the comprehension of workforce dynamics amid the adoption of robotic process automation in sustainable transportation. Organizations that adopt collaborative strategies for the adoption of RPAs engage employees in the implementation process and achieve sustainability objectives at 35% are more likely to achieve this goal13.

Contemporary research2,13 emphasizes the changing dynamics of workforce problems in the deployment of RPA, especially within sustainable transportation frameworks. Their longitudinal analysis involving 50 transportation firms demonstrates that the successful adoption of RPA is significantly correlated with adaptable corporate cultures and powerful social support networks11. Moreover, studies conducted by14, organizations that cultivate a culture of continuous learning and adaptation have 45% greater success rates in RPA adoption, concurrently sustaining elevated worker satisfaction levels.

This intersection of workforce adaptability15, social dynamics and sustainable approaches establishes a distinctive framework for examining how firms might proficiently navigate technological transitions12 while fostering a supportive and adaptable work environment. Recent findings13, the successful RPA adoption in sustainable transportation necessitates a comprehensive approach that encompasses both technological infrastructure and social infrastructure, along with workforce development initiatives11,16. Methodology

The study used a thorough mixed-method approach that included both quantitative and qualitative data gathering techniques, as well as sophisticated statistical modeling, to investigate RPA adoption patterns among 190 personnel in the transportation sector. The sample size of 190 was determined using G*Power estimation, which considered parameters such as effect size, statistical power (0.80), and significance level (α = 0.05), ensuring that the sample was statistically adequate to detect meaningful relationships. This approach guarantees sufficient power for advanced analyses like regression and structural equation modeling, minimizes the risk of Type II errors, and aligns with best practices in similar studies, thereby ensuring the robustness and generalizability of the findings.

Sample information

The study population was representative of a wide range of demographics, including income stratification across four brackets (below $35,000: 20%, $35,000–$50,000: 35%, $50,001–$75,000: 30%, above $75,000: 15%), professional experience levels ranging from entry to expert (1–5 years: 30%, 6–10 years: 35%, 11–15 years: 25%, and > 15 years: 10%), and age distribution ranging from 20 to 60 years divided into four groups (20–30 years: 35%, 31–40 years: 30%, 41–50 years: 25%, and 51–60 years: 10%).

Instruments and procedure of data collection

The study adopted a comprehensive mixed-method approach that included both quantitative and qualitative data collection techniques, along with advanced statistical modeling, to examine RPA adoption patterns among personnel in the transportation sector. A total sample size of 190 respondents was taken for the study, which was determined through G*Power estimation considering parameters such as effect size, statistical power (0.80), and significance level (α = 0.05). This ensured that the sample size was statistically adequate to detect meaningful relationships and supported the use of sophisticated analyses like regression and structural equation modeling. For the quantitative component, a structured questionnaire based on a 5-point Likert scale was employed. Prior to the main survey, a pilot study with 15 participants was conducted to test the instrument’s reliability and validity. The questionnaire demonstrated strong internal consistency reliability with Cronbach’s alpha exceeding 0.80, confirming its suitability for further research. All research procedures were conducted in accordance with relevant ethical guidelines and regulations. The authors confirm that informed consent was obtained from all participants, and the experimental protocol received ethical approval from Manipal University Jaipur, India. Following the successful pilot testing and necessary refinements, the questionnaire was deemed appropriate for the full-scale study. It is also confirmed that all methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations of Manipal University Jaipur, India.

Data analysis

SmartPLS 4.0, which was selected due to its capacity to manage complicated model specifications and non-normal data distributions, was used to build Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) to evaluate the intricate correlations between demographic characteristics and RPA adoption variables. First, the measurement model was evaluated using composite reliability (threshold > 0.70), convergent validity (average variance extracted > 0.50), and discriminant validity (Heterotrait-Monotrait ratio < 0.85). A second step involved evaluating the structural model using path coefficients, R2 values, and effect sizes (f2). It was bootstrapped with 5000 samples to test for the significance of path coefficients and indirect effects in the model, which considered both reflective and formative constructs. Exogenous variables stood for demographic constructs while endogenously built RPA adoption measures were included in the model.

Multi-group analysis performed aimed to ascertain significant variance across demographic groups, while importance-performance matrix analysis identified major areas for managerial attention. Semi-structured interviews of 45–60 min’ length were conducted with a sample of 25 respondents, representing each demographic group. The interviews were analyzed using NVivo 14 software for their qualitative content. Data triangulation with quantitative data and qualitative findings, with special reference towards qualitative interpretations for explaining correlation through PLS-SEM analysis, greatly contributed to mixed-methodology. With an attrition rate of 92%, the study enhanced attrition through advanced retention techniques throughout the longitudinal phase. Further improvement of the PLS-SEM treatment of longitudinal data came through growth curve modeling, which highlighted trends in trajectories of adoption across demographic groups. Quality control by systematic data audits, protocols for missing data through full information maximum likelihood approach, and strict response validation checks accompanied data collection. Additional moderator variables in the PLS-SEM model included such as training received and system access levels, while mediating variables included organizational support and technological self-efficacy. By that measure, the study design comprehensively represents the adoption process. Such thorough methodological approach assures both scientific rigor and practical relevance that allows for rigorous assessment of how demographic variables impact RPA adoption in the transportation sector. It achieved all this by integrating sophisticated statistical models with rich qualitative findings.

Results and discussion

The excellent model fit indices obtained in this study provide strong empirical support for the extended technology acceptance framework in transport sector RPA adoption. The SRMR value of 0.048 demonstrates superior fit compared to recent transport technology studies by20, who reported SRMR values ranging from 0.062 to 0.085. This improved fit suggests that incorporating safety and compliance factors enhances model specification in the transport context. The NFI value of 0.935 aligns with21 criteria for robust model validation in safety–critical operations, while exceeding the fit indices reported in27 meta-analysis of transport automation studies (NFI range: 0.885–0.912). The strong R2 values for endogenous constructs (ranging from 0.482 to 0.568) demonstrate substantial explanatory power that compares favorably with recent technology adoption studies. The R2 of 0.568 for Behavioral Intention exceeds values reported in24 study of transport automation adoption (R2 = 0.485) and investigation of operational technology acceptance (R2 range: 0.425–0.512)25. These higher R2 values support assertion that context-specific model extensions can significantly enhance explanatory power in technology adoption studies. The model’s strong predictive relevance, evidenced by Q2 values ranging from 0.265 to 0.342, validates framework for assessing technology adoption models in operational contexts24.

The significant path coefficients and substantial effect sizes support recent theoretical developments in technology adoption research. The strong relationship between Behavioral Intention and Actual Use (β = 0.512, f2 = 0.325) aligns25 findings on adoption mechanisms in operational technologies. The significant influence of Trust in Technology (β = 0.345) supports emphasis on trust-building in automated operations6, while the negative effect of Perceived Risk (β = − 0.298) risk perception framework26. The substantial relationship between Actual Use and Transport Safety & Compliance (β = 0.485, R2 = 0.485) provides empirical support for proposition that technology adoption can directly enhance safety outcomes5. This finding extends work on safety performance in automated operations24. The excellent measurement properties, including high AVE values (0.642–0.698) and strong factor loadings (0.792–0.858), exceed quality criteria established in recent methodological studies.

This study offers strong empirical support for the effective adoption and implementation of Robotic Process Automation in the transport sector by thoroughly analyzing nine interconnected constructs. The robust explanatory capability of the extended model is evidenced by impressive fit indices (SRMR = 0.048, NFI = 0.935) and significant R2 values between 0.482 and 0.568, affirming the theoretical framework and providing clear practical direction for implementation. The findings clearly demonstrate that RPA adoption in transport operations is influenced by a complex mix of technological, organizational, and human factors, which have important implications for operational efficiency and safety outcomes.

The study offers significant theoretical insights into the literature on adoption of technology. Initially, it effectively enhances conventional technology acceptance models by integrating transport-specific elements and safety results, offering a more thorough framework for comprehending RPA adoption in safety–critical settings. Secondly, it confirms an integrated trust-risk framework within the realm of transport automation, highlighting the essential equilibrium between fostering trust (β = 0.345) and addressing risk perceptions (β = − 0.298). Third, it establishes clear empirical connections between technology adoption and safety performance, addressing a notable gap in the literature through the robust relationship between Actual Use and Transport Safety & Compliance (β = 0.485).

The measurement model exhibits strong psychometric properties across all constructs, with composite reliability values between 0.895 and 0.928 and AVE values ranging from 0.642 to 0.698, surpassing the established thresholds for construct validity. The high consistency along with the high factor loadings of 0.792–0.858 and acceptable values of VIF 2.085–2 input into the measurement approach while validating the structural relationships with significant path coefficients and effect sizes that run from 0.192 to 0.325, not as an afterthought, strengthens the argument for reliance on the results and their real-world applications.

The study establishes a robust empirical link between RPA taking up and safety outcomes in transport, proving that the technology will make operations safer and more compliant. The rather impressive coefficient of determination, R2 = 0.485 for Transport Safety & Compliance, shows that RPA can be applied to improve safety performance adequately, hence making a strong case for investing in such technology for transport operations. The mediation analysis places behavior intention as a whole mediator for all causative factors on actual usage, stressing the criticality to engender positive adoption intention through comprehensive facilitation mechanisms.

The findings give clear direction to transport organizations adopting RPA systems. Results have shown that balanced implementing strategies considering technical and social aspects of adoption are essential. Trust must be built, along with high and positive perceptions towards risks, transforming it with training and environment-supportive activities. The powerful relationship with safety outcomes inspires RPA applications to be viewed as part of a broader view of safety improvements as facilitating condition (β = 0.328) and social influence (β = 0.315) account so much towards adoption.

Theoretical and managerial implications

This research makes a huge contribution to theory and practice while leaving much room for potentially future research areas. Longitudinal studies can look at the patterns of adoption and safety outcomes over time, while cross-cultural research might investigate how findings would translate to different settings of operation. The investigation of other transport technologies, together with an analysis of well-defined mechanisms for safety, could improve our understanding of RPA in transport operations. Furthermore, exploring the possible moderating influences of organizational and environmental factors may yield more profound understanding of adoption dynamics. In summary, this research offers a thorough insight into RPA adoption within the transport sector, backed by robust empirical evidence and distinct practical implications. The findings indicate that effective RPA implementation necessitates a well-rounded strategy that considers technological, organizational, and human elements, all while prioritizing safety outcomes. The robust empirical evidence backing the theoretical framework, along with distinct practical implications, establishes a firm basis for future research and the implementation of initiatives in the adoption of RPA within the transport sector. This study enhances our theoretical understanding of technology adoption in safety–critical environments and provides valuable guidance for practitioners aiming to utilize RPA capabilities while improving operational safety and efficiency.

Limitations and future direction

Despite this study making important contributions related to workforce adaptability and social relations for RPA uptake for sustainable transportation using an integrated UTAUT-TAM framework, we recognize some limitations. First, the study is based on specific time frame. Second, the sample was limited to personnel that were recruited from the same geographical region and sector, which may limit the generalizability of the findings in relation to other industries and cultures. Furthermore, the reliance on self-reported data, while good for gaining subjective perspectives, may negate some validity due to social desirability or perceptions. Future research can address this by utilizing longitudinal designs to measure behavior change over time, conduct collaboration with several cross-schema participants across multiple regional pools and transportation sub-sectors, and incorporating objective performance data. Future studies can also increase the explanatory power of the UTAUT-TAM framework linked to RPA uptake by including moderating variables such as organizational culture, digital literacy, and policy interventions. The study was focused on a very specific, niche group within the transport sector, such as technical executives, specialized engineers and managers of transport systems.

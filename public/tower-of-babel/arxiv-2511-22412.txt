Abstract
We study chiral symmetry restoration in hot-and-dense quark matter using the von Neumann chirality entropy within the in-medium Nambu-Jona-Lasinio (NJL) model. Starting from the lesser Green’s function G < ​ ( k ) G^{<}(k) , the reduced correlator C L = P L ​ G < ​ P L C_{L}=P_{L}G^{<}P_{L} is constructed, and the associated entropy S χ = − Tr ⁡ [ C L ​ ln ⁡ C L + ( 1 − C L ) ​ ln ⁡ ( 1 − C L ) ] S_{\chi}=-\mathrm{Tr}[C_{L}\ln C_{L}+(1-C_{L})\ln(1-C_{L})] is introduced to quantify the entanglement between left- and right-handed quark sectors. The dynamical quark mass M q ​ ( T , μ q ) M_{q}(T,\mu_{q}) obtained from the gap equation exhibits the expected QCD-like phase structure: A second-order transition in the chiral limit and a smooth crossover for finite m q m_{q} . The chirality entropy S χ S_{\chi} increases monotonically with temperature and chemical potential, approaching a maximal value as M q → 0 M_{q}\to 0 . We also explore the critical exponents and scaling behavior of those quantities, yielding β S χ ≃ 1 \beta_{S_{\chi}}\simeq 1 . This demonstrates that S χ S_{\chi} serves as an information-theoretic probe for chiral symmetry restoration, linking dynamical mass generation to quantum entanglement in strongly interacting matter.



I
Introduction

The spontaneous breakdown (SBCS) and restoration of chiral symmetry constitute a fundamental aspect of Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) and play a central role in the mass generation mechanism of hadrons and in the formation of the quark-gluon plasma (QGP) at high temperature or density
Nambu:1961fr
;
Klevansky:1992qe
;
Hatsuda:1994pi
;
Buballa:2003qv
;
Fukushima:2010bq
. In the QCD vacuum, chiral symmetry is dynamically broken, leading to a large constituent quark mass and giving rise to light pseudoscalar mesons as Nambu-Goldstone bosons. As temperature or baryon density increases, the quark condensate
\langle\bar{q}q\rangle
melts, the constituent mass decreases, and chiral symmetry is partially restored. Lattice QCD simulations have established that this restoration occurs through a smooth crossover at vanishing chemical potential for physical quark masses
Aoki:2006we
;
Borsanyi:2010cj
. At the same time, effective models such as the Nambu–Jona-Lasinio (NJL), Polyakov-NJL (PNJL), and instanton-based models successfully reproduce the qualitative features of the QCD phase diagram
Klevansky:1992qe
;
Hatsuda:1994pi
;
Ratti:2005jh
;
Fukushima:2008wg
;
Nam:2009nn
.

Recently, concepts from quantum information theory have been applied to strongly interacting systems to characterize their microscopic coherence and correlations in terms of entanglement entropy
Vedral:2002zz
;
Amico:2007ag
;
Calabrese:2009qy
;
Casini:2014aia
. In QCD and other field-theoretical systems, entanglement-based observables have been used to probe confinement, deconfinement, topological ordering, and spin structures
Levin:2006zz
;
Ryu:2006bv
;
Kharzeev:2017qzs
;
Nam:2025hei
. The von Neumann entropy, in particular, provides a quantitative measure of quantum coherence loss and has been proposed as a diagnostic tool for phase transitions in gauge theories and many-body systems.

In this work, we formulate the
von Neumann chirality entropy
as an information-theoretic probe of chiral symmetry restoration within the in-medium NJL model. Starting from the lesser Green’s function
G^{<}(k)
in the real-time formalism
KadanoffBaym1962
;
Keldysh:1964ud
, we construct the reduced left-handed correlator
C_{L}(\bm{k})=P_{L}G^{<}(k)P_{L}
and define the entropy

S_{\chi}=-\mathrm{Tr}\,[C_{L}\ln C_{L}+(1-C_{L})\ln(1-C_{L})].

This entropy measures the degree of quantum entanglement between left- and right-handed quark sectors, which is dynamically generated through the mass term
M_{q}(\bar{q}_{L}q_{R}+\bar{q}_{R}q_{L})
. In the chirally broken phase (
M_{q}\neq 0
), the left and right components are polarized and coherence is maintained, while in the restored phase (
M_{q}\to 0
), they become maximally mixed, leading to a large
S_{\chi}
.

We demonstrate that
S_{\chi}(T,\mu_{q})
increases monotonically with both temperature and chemical potential,
reflecting the progressive loss of chiral coherence and the transition to a maximally mixed state. The resulting behavior mirrors the thermodynamic phase structure of the NJL model: A second-order transition in the chiral limit and a smooth crossover for finite current quark mass. Thus, the von Neumann chirality entropy serves as an
information-theoretic probe
that connects the dynamical mechanism of mass generation to the microscopic structure of quantum entanglement in QCD matter.

Most existing entanglement-related studies in QCD address spatial bipartition entropy, Rényi entropies, or color entanglement in Euclidean or lattice formulations. In contrast, the present work develops an information-theoretic quantity associated with the chiral internal degree of freedom, constructed directly from the real-time lesser Green function. The resulting chirality-reduced entropy captures dynamical changes in the quasiparticle spectrum and offers a new, complementary perspective on the chiral phase transition beyond conventional order parameters and previously studied entanglement measures.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Sec. II, we outline the theoretical framework of the finite-temperature Nambu-Jona-Lasinio model and introduce the lesser Green’s function in the real-time formalism, which provides the foundation for the subsequent definition of the reduced correlator. In Sec. III, we derive the left-handed reduced correlator
C_{L}(\bm{k})
and analyze its connection to the dynamical mass and chiral mixing. In Sec. IV, the von Neumann chirality entropy is formulated and its physical interpretation as an information-theoretic measure of chiral entanglement is discussed in detail. Section V presents the numerical results for the dynamical quark mass and chirality entropy as functions of temperature and chemical potential, together with a detailed physical discussion of the QCD-like phase structure. Finally, Sec. VI summarizes the main conclusions and remarks on possible extensions of the present approach, including applications to the PNJL model and to lattice-QCD analyses of the entanglement in hadronic matter.

II
In-medium NJL Model

The NJL model is based on a four-fermion interaction that preserves global chiral symmetry in the massless limit. Its Lagrangian reads:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{NJL}}=\bar{q}(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-m_{q})q+G\left[(\bar{q}q)^{2}+(\bar{q}i\gamma_{5}\bm{\tau}q)^{2}\right],

(1)

where
q=(u,d)^{T}
represents the light-flavor quark fields,
m_{q}
is the current quark mass (assumed to be small but nonzero), and
G
is a dimensionful coupling constant characterizing the strength of the effective scalar and pseudoscalar interactions. The second term in the Lagrangian contains chirally invariant combinations of bilinear quark operators, reflecting the underlying
SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}
symmetry.

The NJL model does not confine quarks, yet it successfully captures dynamical mass generation. When the interaction strength
G
exceeds a critical value, the vacuum becomes unstable against the formation of a quark condensate
\langle\bar{q}q\rangle
, which serves as an order parameter for SBCS. This results in the generation of a constituent quark mass
M_{q}
, given by the gap equation:

\displaystyle M_{q}=m_{q}-2G\langle\bar{q}q\rangle.

(2)

Here, the dynamical mass
M_{q}
significantly exceeds the current mass
m_{q}
, reflecting the nonperturbative nature of the QCD vacuum. This mechanism also leads to the emergence of massless Nambu-Goldstone bosons, identified with the pions in the chiral limit, associated with the broken axial generators of the chiral symmetry. The NJL model thus provides a minimal yet powerful framework for exploring the interplay between chiral symmetry and the fermionic vacuum structure in low-energy QCD. III
Lesser Green’s Function and Chiral Correlator Framework

In the Schwinger-Keldysh real-time formalism
Keldysh:1964ud
, two correlation functions are defined as

\displaystyle G^{>}(x,y)=-i\langle q(x)\bar{q}(y)\rangle,\,\,\,\,G^{<}(x,y)=i\langle\bar{q}(y)q(x)\rangle.

(4)

The symbols
>
and
<
denote contour ordering along the Keldysh time path. Physically,
G^{<}
measures correlations among occupied states and serves as the momentum-space one-body density matrix.

In equilibrium, these are related to the spectral function
A(p)
and the Fermi distribution
n_{F}(p^{0})
:

\displaystyle G^{<}(p)=i\,n_{F}(p^{0})A(p),\,\,\,\,G^{>}(p)=-i[1-n_{F}(p^{0})]A(p),

(5)

where
A(p)=i[G^{R}(p)-G^{A}(p)]
. Thus,
G^{<}
directly encodes the occupied part of the spectral density.

Within the mean-field NJL model, the lesser propagator is

\displaystyle G^{<}(k)=2\pi i\Big[n_{+}\,\Lambda_{+}(\bm{k})\,\delta(k_{0}-E_{k})+(1-n_{-})\,\Lambda_{-}(\bm{k})\,\delta(k_{0}+E_{k})\Big],

(6)

The positive
(+)
and negative
(-)
energy projectors are given by

\Lambda_{\pm}(\bm{k})=\frac{1}{2}\left[1\pm\frac{\gamma^{0}(\bm{\gamma}\cdot\bm{k}+M_{q})}{E_{k}}\right].

(7)

They satisfy
\Lambda_{\pm}^{2}=\Lambda_{\pm}
,
\Lambda_{+}\Lambda_{-}=0
,
\Lambda_{+}+\Lambda_{-}=1
, and

\gamma^{0}\Lambda_{\pm}(\bm{k})\gamma^{0}=\frac{\hbox to0.0pt{/\hss}{k}\pm M_{q}}{2E_{k}},

(8)

using ther energy eigenvalue euqations
\gamma^{0}u_{\pm}(\bm{k})=\pm\,u_{\pm}(\bm{k})
. Integrating over
k_{0}=\pm E_{k}
gives a compact on-shell representation:

G^{<}(k)=n_{+}(E_{k})\,\Lambda_{+}(\bm{k})+[1-n_{-}(E_{k})]\,\Lambda_{-}(\bm{k}).

(9)

In the limit of
M_{q}\to 0
,
G^{<}
becomes diagonal in chirality, recovering the massless thermal propagator
in_{F}(E_{k}-\mu)\hbox to0.0pt{/\hss}{k}/(2E_{k})
. Finite
M_{q}
introduces left-right mixing, and
G^{<}
thereby encodes coherence loss between chiral sectors. Because
0\leq n_{\pm}\leq 1
, the eigenvalues of
G^{<}
lie in
[0,1]
, consistent with probabilistic normalization. Detailed derivation of the above relations is given in Appendix A.

IV
Chirality Decomposition and Reduced Correlator

The Dirac field can be decomposed into its left- and right-handed components,

q=q_{L}+q_{R},\,\,\,\,q_{L,R}=P_{L,R}q,\,\,\,\,P_{L,R}=\frac{1\mp\gamma^{5}}{2}.

(10)

Here,
P_{L,R}
are projection operators in chiral space that separate fermionic degrees of freedom with definite handedness. The chiral symmetry of the Lagrangian ensures that, in the absence of a mass term, the left- and right-handed
fields evolve independently. The mass term introduces mass-induced mixing between left- and right-handed components of the Dirac field, so that a nonzero constituent mass
M_{q}
acts as a
quantum mixing source
linking the two sectors. In the chiral limit
M_{q}\to 0
, the mass coupling vanishes, and the Lagrangian decouples into independent left- and right-handed parts, leading to no direct mass-induced entanglement.

To quantify this coupling, we construct the left-handed reduced correlator by projecting the lesser Green’s function
G^{<}(k)
onto the left-handed subspace:

C_{L}(\bm{k})=P_{L}G^{<}(k)P_{L}=n_{+}\,P_{L}\Lambda_{+}(\bm{k})P_{L}+(1-n_{-})\,P_{L}\Lambda_{-}(\bm{k})P_{L}.

(11)

Here
n_{\pm}=n_{F}(E_{k}\mp\mu_{q})
are the Fermi-Dirac distribution functions, and
\Lambda_{\pm}(\bm{k})
denote the positive- and negative-energy projectors as discussed previously. The first term represents the contribution of occupied quark states, while the second encodes the vacuum and antiquark components. Thus
C_{L}(\bm{k})
contains information on how many left-handed states are occupied and how strongly they are mixed with right-handed ones through the mass term.
C_{L}
is used as the projected correlation matrix, and it may clarify that it is not the thermal Bloch matrix but the Gaussian reduced state. Justification for using
C_{L}=P_{L}G^{<}P_{L}
as a reduced density matrix is discussed in Appendix B.

These relations demonstrate that the mass term
M_{q}
controls the weight of left-handed components in positive- and negative-energy sectors. When
M_{q}/E_{k}\ll 1
, the projectors become nearly identical and the left-right asymmetry is suppressed, signaling partial restoration of chiral symmetry.

Substituting these results into the expression for
C_{L}(\bm{k})
, we obtain

C_{L}(\bm{k})=\nu_{k}\,\mathbb{I}_{2\times 2},\,\,\,\,\nu_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(1-\frac{M_{q}}{E_{k}}\right)n_{+}+\left(1+\frac{M_{q}}{E_{k}}\right)(1-n_{-})\right].

(13)

The coefficient
\nu_{k}
plays the role of an
effective occupation probability
for the left-handed subspace.
It incorporates both the thermal population (
n_{\pm}
) and the dynamical left-right mixing determined by
M_{q}/E_{k}
.
From the positivity of
P_{L}\Lambda_{\pm}P_{L}
and
0\leq n_{\pm}\leq 1
, it follows that
0\leq\nu_{k}\leq 1
, guaranteeing a valid probabilistic interpretation.

Physically,
\nu_{k}
quantifies the degree of chirality coherence at momentum
k
:

•

For
\nu_{k}\simeq 1
or
0
, the state is dominantly left- or right-handed,
indicating strong chiral polarization and negligible entanglement.

•

For
\nu_{k}\simeq\tfrac{1}{2}
, left- and right-handed components are equally mixed,
reflecting maximal chirality entanglement and partial restoration of chiral symmetry.

In characteristic limiting cases at finite chemical potential
\mu_{q}
, we find

\displaystyle M_{q}\to 0:

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\nu_{k}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(n_{+}+1-n_{-}\right),

\displaystyle\text{(chiral-symmetric limit)},

(14)

\displaystyle T\to 0:

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\nu_{k}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{M_{q}}{E_{k}}\right),

\displaystyle\text{(zero-temperature vacuum)},

(15)

\displaystyle T\to\infty:

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\nu_{k}=\tfrac{1}{2},

\displaystyle\text{(maximal thermal mixing)}.

(16)

Hence,
\nu_{k}
evolves smoothly between the chirally broken and chirally symmetric regimes. In the vacuum, the finite constituent mass
M_{q}
skews
\nu_{k}
toward unity, signifying intense chiral polarization, while at high temperature or vanishing mass
\nu_{k}\to 1/2
, indicating the recovery of left-right symmetry and maximal entanglement.

Physically,
\nu_{k}
quantifies the degree of left-right mixing in the quark spinor basis. When
M_{q}/E_{k}\ll 1
, the mass-induced coupling between left- and right-handed sectors weakens, yielding
\nu_{k}\simeq 1/2
and indicating nearly maximal chirality entanglement. Conversely, for larger
M_{q}/E_{k}
, the occupation probability skews toward
\ nu_{k}\simeq 1
or
0
, signifying dominant chiral polarization and diminished entanglement. This transition from polarized to mixed chirality states encapsulates the dynamical mechanism of chiral symmetry restoration: as the dynamical mass decreases with increasing temperature or chemical potential,
\nu_{k}
evolves continuously toward
1/2
, marking the onset of maximal quantum mixing between the two chiral sectors.

V
Von Neumann Entropy and Chirality Entanglement

The von Neumann entropy for the left-chiral subsystem quantifies the
loss of quantum coherence

between left- and right-handed components due to chiral mixing:

S_{\chi}=-\mathrm{Tr}\left[C_{L}\ln C_{L}+(1-C_{L})\ln(1-C_{L})\right].

(17)

This expression is formally identical to the von Neumann entropy in quantum information theory, but here the reduced density operator is replaced by the left-handed correlator
C_{L}
. The eigenvalues of
C_{L}
encode how strongly the chiral subspaces are entangled. If
C_{L}
is idempotent (
C_{L}^{2}=C_{L}
), the state is pure, and the entropy vanishes, whereas any deviation from idempotency indicates mixedness arising from chiral correlations.

For isotropic and spin-averaged matter, the correlator becomes diagonal,
C_{L}(\bm{k})=\nu_{k}\,\mathbb{I}_{2\times 2}
, simplifying the trace to

\frac{S_{\chi}}{V}=2N_{c}N_{f}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[-\nu_{k}\ln\nu_{k}-(1-\nu_{k})\ln(1-\nu_{k})\right],

(18)

where
\nu_{k}
is given by Eq. (
13
). The integrand is positive and real for
0\leq\nu_{k}\leq 1
, representing the entropy contribution from each momentum mode
k
. The integration over all
\bm{k}
combines these microscopic contributions into the macroscopic entanglement entropy density,
S_{\chi}/V
.

The quantity inside the integral can be identified as the
single-mode chirality entropy
,

s_{\chi}(k)=-2\left[\nu_{k}\ln\nu_{k}+(1-\nu_{k})\ln(1-\nu_{k})\right].

(19)

This expression is formally equivalent to the Shannon entropy of a binary probability distribution with occupation probabilities
\nu_{k}
and
1-\nu_{k}
. It measures the degree of uncertainty or mixing between the left- and right-handed components
for a given momentum mode:

•

s_{\chi}(k)=0
when
\nu_{k}=0
or
1
, corresponding to a purely left- or right-handed state with perfect chirality coherence (no entanglement).

•

s_{\chi}(k)
reaches its maximum at
\nu_{k}=\tfrac{1}{2}
, indicating complete loss of chirality information and maximal entanglement.

As the constituent mass
M_{q}
decreases, the mixing between chiral sectors becomes stronger, driving
\nu_{k}
toward
\frac{1}{2}
and enhancing
s_{\chi}(k)
. Consequently, the total entropy
S_{\chi}
increases, reflecting the progressive loss of left-right coherence as the system approaches the chirally symmetric phase. In the chiral-symmetric limit
M_{q}\to 0
,
\nu_{k}\to\tfrac{1}{2}
, and the entropy attains its maximum value, corresponding to full quantum mixing between left and right components. In contrast, for the fully chirality-conserved vacuum (
M_{q}\neq 0
,
T=\mu=0
), the left and right subspaces remain orthogonal and unentangled, so that
\nu_{k}\to 1
or
0
and
S_{\chi}\to 0
.

Hence,
S_{\chi}(T,\mu)
serves as an
information-theoretic probe
for chiral symmetry restoration:
Its rise with temperature or chemical potential signals the degradation of chiral coherence, while its vanishing at zero temperature and finite mass characterizes the chiral-broken, coherent vacuum. For reference, several limiting forms are listed below: At
T\!\to\!0
,
\nu_{k}=\theta(\mu_{q}-E_{k})
and
s_{\chi}(k)=0
. At
T\!\to\!\infty
,
n_{F}\!\to\!\tfrac{1}{2}
so
\nu_{k}=\tfrac{1}{2}
and
s_{\chi}(k)=\ln 2
. In the chiral limit
M_{q}\!\to\!0
,
P_{L}\Lambda_{\pm}P_{L}=\tfrac{1}{2}P_{L}
,
giving
\nu_{k}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,n_{F}(E_{k})
and
s_{\chi}(k)=-\tfrac{1}{2}\!\big[n_{F}\ln n_{F}+(1-n_{F})\ln(1-n_{F})\big]
. These limits show that
s_{\chi}(k)
vanishes for fully occupied or empty modes, approaches
\ln 2
at high temperature, and varies smoothly near the chiral critical region.

VII
Summary and Outlook

In this work, we have developed a consistent framework to quantify the quantum entanglement between left- and right-handed quark sectors within the finite-temperature and density Nambu–Jona-Lasinio (NJL) model. Starting from the lesser Green’s function
G^{<}(k)
in the real-time formalism, we derived the left-handed reduced correlator
C_{L}(\bm{k})=P_{L}G^{<}(k)P_{L}
and introduced the von Neumann chirality entropy,
S_{\chi}=-\mathrm{Tr}\!\left[C_{L}\ln C_{L}+(1-C_{L})\ln(1-C_{L})\right]
, as an information-theoretic measure of chiral coherence, this entropy characterizes the degree of mixing between the two chiral components, thereby providing a direct link between dynamical mass generation and the quantum information content of QCD matter.

The self-consistent solutions of the NJL gap equation reveal a QCD-like chiral phase structure: A second-order transition in the chiral limit and a smooth crossover for finite current quark mass. The dynamical quark mass
M_{q}(T,\mu_{q})
decreases continuously with temperature and chemical potential, signaling progressive chiral symmetry restoration. Using this behavior, we computed the chirality entanglement entropy density
S_{\chi}/V
and found that it increases monotonically with both
T
and
\mu_{q}
, approaching a maximal value as
M_{q}\to 0
. Thus,
S_{\chi}
mirrors the order-parameter behavior of
M_{q}
, i.e., it is nearly zero in the broken phase, where the system is chirally polarized, and maximal in the restored phase, where left- and right-handed components are thoroughly mixed.

A key aspect of this work is the scaling analysis of
S_{\chi}
near the second-order critical line in the chiral limit. The dynamical mass exhibits the mean-field behavior
\beta_{M_{q}}\simeq 1/2
, being consistent with the standard Landau universality of the NJL model. In contrast, the entropy provides
\beta_{S_{\chi}}\simeq 1
, i.e., a distinctly larger exponent. This difference arises because
S_{\chi}
is the temperature derivative of the free energy. At the same time, the critical part of the latter is dominated by the order-parameter amplitude squared,
\Omega_{\rm crit}\sim M_{q}^{2}\sim(T_{c}-T)^{1}
. Consequently, the entropy inherits the
M_{q}^{2}
scaling rather than
M_{q}
, leading to an exponent approximately twice that of the order parameter. This confirms that
S_{\chi}
is not an order parameter itself, but a thermodynamic entanglement response that nevertheless remains tightly correlated with the underlying critical dynamics.

The monotonic increase of
S_{\chi}(T,\mu_{q})
therefore encodes the loss of left-right quantum coherence as the system evolves from a strongly correlated vacuum to a thermally randomized quark ensemble. In this sense,
S_{\chi}
serves as an
information-theoretic probe
for chiral symmetry restoration, complementing the traditional condensate

\langle\bar{q}q\rangle
by emphasizing the quantum entanglement structure of the quark sector. The framework developed here thus bridges thermal quantum field theory and quantum information theory, allowing symmetry restoration to be interpreted as the emergence of maximal quantum mixing in the chiral subspace.

The present study opens several directions for future work. Incorporating the Polyakov loop or explicit color interactions would enable a combined analysis of deconfinement and chirality entanglement. Extensions to PNJL or Dyson-Schwinger frameworks could clarify the interplay among confinement, dynamical mass, and information entropy. In confining models such as the PNJL framework, color-singlet constraints suppress single-quark excitations below the deconfinement temperature, reducing the accessible Hilbert-space volume and lowering
S_{\chi}
in the confined phase. This suggests that confinement delays the onset of large chirality entanglement until color screening becomes effective near
T_{c}
. Future lattice-QCD simulations may directly evaluate fermionic correlation matrices or reduced chiral projectors to obtain
S_{\chi}
nonperturbatively and thus test our entanglement-based interpretation of chiral symmetry restoration, since
C_{L}(\bm{k})
is constructed from fermionic two-point functions and the same reduced correlator can in principle be evaluated on the lattice. Such developments would further establish the von Neumann chirality entropy as a universal, information-based diagnostic of phase transitions in strongly interacting quantum systems. Related works will appear elsewhere.

VII.1
Validity range of the equilibrium assumption

The equilibrium relations used in Eqs. (
4
)-(
9
),

G^{<}(p)=in_{F}(p^{0})A(p),\,\,\,\,G^{>}(p)=-i[1-n_{F}(p^{0})]A(p),

(24)

are valid when the system has reached local thermal equilibrium and its relaxation time
\tau_{\mathrm{rel}}
is much longer than the microscopic interaction time. In this regime, quasiparticle excitations are well defined, and the spectral function
A(p)
is sharply peaked at the mass shell,

A(p)\simeq 2\pi\mathrm{sgn}(p^{0})\,\delta(p^{2}-M_{q}^{2}),

(25)

so that the finite width of
A(p)
can be neglected.
This narrow-width (mean-field) limit justifies the use of the on-shell forms in
Eqs. (6)-(9).

If a finite-width spectral function is employed,

A(p)=\frac{-2\mathrm{Im}G^{R}(p)}{\pi},

(26)

the definition of the lesser propagator and the reduced correlator,
C_{L}=P_{L}G^{<}P_{L}
, remains unchanged, but the occupation factor
\nu_{k}
and the chirality entropy
S_{\chi}
include an additional integration over the off-shell energy
distribution:

\nu_{k}=\int\frac{dp^{0}}{2\pi}n_{F}(p^{0})\mathrm{Tr}\left[P_{L}A(p)P_{L}\right].

(27)

Consequently,
S_{\chi}
measures the mixedness of the chiral subspace, including the effects of quasiparticle broadening, and reduces to the simple on-shell expression of Eq. (
18
) in the narrow-width limit. In this sense, the inclusion of a finite spectral width can be interpreted as an
entanglement broadening
effect, reflecting the enhanced mixing of chiral
degrees of freedom due to thermal smearing and quasiparticle damping.

VII.2
Justification for using
C_{L}=P_{L}G^{<}P_{L}
as a reduced density matrix

In equilibrium, the lesser Green’s function
G^{<}(k)
describes the occupied part of the single-particle spectrum:

C(\bm{k})=i\int\frac{dk^{0}}{2\pi}G^{<}(k),\qquad 0\leq C\leq 1,

(28)

which defines the equal-time correlation matrix for a Gaussian (mean-field) fermionic state. Such a state is completely characterized by
C
, since higher-order correlations follow from Wick’s theorem.

Projecting onto the left-handed subspace via
P_{L}=(1-\gamma_{5})/2
, we obtain

C_{L}=P_{L}CP_{L}=i\int\frac{dk^{0}}{2\pi}P_{L}G^{<}(k)P_{L},

(29)

which represents the restriction of the full correlation matrix to the algebra generated by
\psi_{L}=P_{L}\psi
and
\psi_{L}^{\dagger}
. The eigenvalues of
C_{L}
satisfy
0\leq\nu_{k}\leq 1
and play the role of effective occupation probabilities within the left-chiral sector.

For fermionic Gaussian states, the von Neumann entropy of the restricted (or “reduced”) subsystem is given by

S_{\chi}=-\mathrm{Tr}\left[C_{L}\ln C_{L}+(P_{L}-C_{L})\ln(P_{L}-C_{L})\right],

(30)

which coincides with the entropy of a true reduced density matrix. Hence, within the mean-field or quasiparticle approximation,
C_{L}
serves as a legitimate surrogate for the reduced density operator of the left-handed sector. Its trace and spectrum are invariant under unitary basis changes, ensuring that
S_{\chi}
is physically well-defined and basis independent.

This construction remains valid for any Gaussian equilibrium state with a positive spectral function
A(k)
such that
G^{<}=in_{F}(k^{0})A(k)
. For interacting non-Gaussian cases, Eq. (
30
) provides the entropy of the Gaussian reference state sharing the same two-point correlator, serving as an upper bound to the exact subalgebra entropy.

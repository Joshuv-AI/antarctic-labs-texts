Maintenance of clinical remission with cyclosporine after discontinuation of prednisolone in a patient with tubulointerstitial nephritis with IgM-positive plasma cells
Authors: Kazuki Yamada, Shiori Nakao, Minori Ikeda, Reina Suetsugu-Ishizawa, Nozomi Hayashi, Hirofumi Sakuma, Motoki Matsuki, Naoki Takahashi, Masayuki Iwano, Tadashi Toyama, Yayoi Ogawa, Naoki Nakagawa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTubulointerstitial nephritis (TIN) with IgM-positive plasma cells (IgMPC-TIN) is an inflammatory disease characterized by the infiltration of IgM and CD138 dual-positive plasma cells into the renal interstitium. Steroid treatment is effective for many cases of IgMPC-TIN. However, the optimal dose and duration of steroid therapy remain poorly understood. In the present case, IgMPC-TIN was diagnosed in a 36-year-old woman with femoral head osteonecrosis. After administration of prednisolone (PSL, 20 mg/day), several markers of disease activity gradually decreased. The PSL dose was tapered to 5 mg/day for one year without TIN recurrence. To avoid exacerbation of bone lesions due to prolonged administration of PSL, cyclosporine was added to PSL. Combination treatment with 5 mg/day PSL and 100–125 mg/day cyclosporine stabilized the TIN. The patient remained stable without recurrence after further reduction and discontinuation of PSL. This case provides original evidence regarding the optimal treatment regimen for patients with IgMPC-TIN who are unsuitable for prolonged PSL administration.Keywords: Tubulointerstitial nephritis, Prednisolone, Cyclosporine

Introduction

Tubulointerstitial nephritis (TIN) is an inflammatory disease that affects the renal tubules and interstitium without glomerular lesions and is caused by numerous genetic and environmental factors [1]. The most common cause of TIN is drug induction, while other causes include systemic diseases, such as Sjögren’s syndrome, sarcoidosis, and immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4)-related kidney disease [1]. Pathological findings revealed a pronounced infiltration of lymphocytes, macrophages, and plasma cells into the renal interstitium [2]. Although infiltration of IgG-positive plasma cells is a common finding in TIN, some cases of TIN with IgM-positive plasma cells (IgMPC) have recently been reported [3–5]. These cases, characterized by high serum IgM levels, positive anti-mitochondrial antibodies (AMA), distal tubular acidosis, Fanconi syndrome, and primary biliary cholangitis (PBC), have been termed as “IgMPC-TIN” by Takahashi et al. [3]. Although the pathogenesis of IgMPC-TIN remains unclear, an intermediate dose of prednisolone (PSL) is highly effective in most cases [3–7]. However, the optimal PSL dose and duration for IgMPC-TIN have not been well established. Furthermore, long-term use of PSL should be undertaken with caution because of its various side effects, such as infection, osteoporosis, and osteonecrosis of the femoral head (ONFH) [8].

Here, we report the case of a patient with IgMPC-TIN with a history of ONFH in which PSL was discontinued with the use of cyclosporine.

Case report

A 36-year-old Japanese woman was diagnosed with bilateral ONFH by an orthopedic surgeon seven months prior to being referred to our department. The patient continued to experience predominantly right hip pain and underwent conservative management. She visited a general hospital because of nausea and vomiting for 2 weeks. Urinary abnormalities and renal dysfunction were detected at the hospital; thereafter, the patient was referred to our department. She had not undergone annual health checkups since she was 30 years old. Her family, medical, and pharmacological histories were unremarkable. Laboratory tests revealed severe renal dysfunction, hypokalemia, metabolic acidosis, proteinuria, urinary occult blood, and glycosuria. The patient was then admitted for further examination.

On admission, the patient had a blood pressure of 102/90 mmHg and pulse of 95 beats per minute with a regular rhythm. Physical examination revealed no abnormalities. Chest radiography and electrocardiography results were normal. Table 1 shows her laboratory findings upon admission. Her serum IgM level was markedly elevated at 1036 mg/dL, and IgG and IgA levels were normal. Serum electrophoresis showed anIgM-λ peak, and urine protein electrophoresis did not detect Bence Jones protein. Concentrations of serum light-chain kappa and lambda were 61.6 and 38.5 mg/L, respectively, and the free-light-chain ratio was normal (1.60; reference range 0.26, 1.65). Bone marrow aspiration revealed no significant findings. Thus, the possibility of monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance or multiple myeloma was excluded. Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) revealed bone mineral density values of 1.122 g/cm2 at the lumbar spine (L2–L4) with a Z-score of 0.0 and 0.722 g/cm2 at the right femoral neck with a Z-score of − 1.3, indicating no osteoporosis. Tests for anti-Sjögren’s antibodies A and B were negative, whereas the patient tested positive for AMA and AMA-M2 with normal levels of hepatobiliary enzymes. A liver biopsy was performed 12 days after admission owing to elevated AMA-M2 levels. Biopsy revealed focal necrosis, microabscesses, and granuloma formation in the hepatic lobular area, which met the criteria for primary biliary cirrhosis (PBC). Therefore, she was prescribed oral ursodeoxycholic acid. Her urinalysis showed heavy proteinuria (urinary protein-to-creatinine ratio, 1.6 g/gCr), renal glycosuria, and elevated levels of urinary β2-microglobin (9.48 µg/mL [normal range: ≤ 0.22 µg/mL]) and N-acetyl-β-D-glucosaminidase/Cr (9.4 U/gCr [normal range: < 8.0 U/gCr]). Additionally, glycosuria, hyperphosphaturia, and impaired tubular reabsorption of uric acid, potassium, and sodium were observed, suggestive of Fanconi syndrome. In the differential diagnosis of hypophosphatemia, intact PTH and 25(OH) vitamin D levels were within the normal range, suggesting renal tubular phosphate wasting rather than abnormalities in PTH- or vitamin D-related metabolism. Further analysis revealed normal anion gap metabolic acidosis with persistently elevated urinary pH (> 5.5), suggestive of distal renal tubular acidosis. Figures 1 and 2 show the renal biopsy findings obtained 18 days after admission. Light microscopy revealed 14 glomeruli without any glomerular abnormalities (Fig. 1a–c). Significant infiltration of inflammatory cells, including lymphocytes and plasma cells, was observed in the renal interstitium (Fig. 1d–f). Notably, these cells were positively stained for CD3, suggesting a diffuse interstitial distribution of CD3-positive T lymphocytes. Dual immunostaining with IgM and CD138 revealed that IgM and CD138 dual-positive plasma cells were markedly observed in the renal interstitium (Fig. 2a–b). Immunofluorescence staining did not reveal immunoglobulins or complement components. Therefore, the patient was diagnosed as having IgMPC-TIN. Additionally, based on X-ray and MRI findings, an orthopedic surgeon diagnosed ONFH stage I type C1, indicating a severe risk of progression to bone collapse. Although total hip arthroplasty could be considered, conservative management was prioritized because of her relatively young age.

Forty days after the renal biopsy, the patient was treated with oral PSL at a dose of 20 mg/day (0.3 mg/kg body weight). After induction of PSL, her serum creatinine, IgM, urinary proteinuria, glycosuria, and urinary β2-microglobin levels gradually decreased (Fig. 3). The PSL dose was tapered to 5 mg/day over the course of one year (Fig. 3). She had no relapse during steroid tapering. There were concerns regarding the exacerbation of bone lesions with the prolonged administration of PSL. Therefore, cyclosporine was administered orally. Then, PSL was tapered and discontinued, and the patient was switched to cyclosporine. Additionally, hepatobiliary enzyme levels did not increase during the tapering or discontinuation of steroids. Cyclosporine therapy was monitored by measuring blood cyclosporine concentrations, which were maintained at approximately 600 ng/mL. Remission was successfully maintained with cyclosporine monotherapy for at least one year after discontinuation of PSL (Fig. 3).

Clinical course. After the patient began treatment with prednisolone (PSL, 20 mg/day: 0.3 mg/kg body weight), there was a gradual decrease in the levels of serum creatinine, IgM, urinary β2-microglobin, proteinuria, and glycosuria. We gradually tapered the dose of PSL with maintenance of remission. Upon reduction of the dose of PSL to 5 mg/day, cyclosporine was added to the PSL. The patient continued on a combined regimen of 5 mg/day of PSL and 100–125 mg/day of cyclosporine, with its blood concentration around 600 ng/mL. Finally, she successfully maintained remission on cyclosporine monotherapy, even after the PSL was tapered and discontinued in the follow-up period

Discussion

This study presents the case of a 36-year-old woman with ONFH who was diagnosed with IgMPC-TIN and switched from PSL to cyclosporine, which was subsequently continued to maintain remission.

The prevalence of IgMPC-TIN is rare, estimated to be approximately 0.06% among renal biopsy cases [3]. The age of onset is often middle age or older. Pathological findings are characterized by the infiltration of IgM-positive plasma cells into the renal interstitium with mild or minor glomerular abnormalities such as mesangial proliferation. Double staining for IgM and CD138 is indispensable for accurate diagnosis of IgMPC-TIN. The defined thresholds for IgM-positive plasma cells were established at 13 cells per high-power field (HPF), yielding a sensitivity of 100% and a specificity of 93.2% [3]. In the present case, the average number of IgM-positive plasma cells per HPF was extremely high (142), indicating a clinically significant increase in serum IgM concentration. The pathological findings in this case also revealed diffuse infiltration of CD3-positive T-lymphocytes in the renal tubules, predominantly in the proximal tubules, suggesting that the case was complicated by Fanconi syndrome. Fanconi syndrome causes proximal tubular dysfunction, resulting in urinary wasting of phosphate, bicarbonate, glucose, urate, amino acids, and low molecular weight proteins [9]. Bone disorders are well recognized in Fanconi syndrome, mainly because of hypophosphatemia and vitamin D deficiency related to tubular dysfunction. Several risk factors were not identified in the present case of ONFH, although the disease is generally associated with steroid use, alcohol consumption, smoking, and certain underlying diseases [10]. In this case, we considered that ONFH may have been either attributable to bone metabolic abnormalities associated with Fanconi syndrome, or idiopathic. Bone metabolic abnormalities and related symptoms may provide important clues regarding underlying Fanconi syndrome.

Although an optimal treatment strategy for IgMPC-TIN has not been established yet, an intermediate dose of PSL is effective. Our patient responded to steroid treatment, followed by a decrease in several disease activity markers. However, long-term use of PSL was undesirable in this case because of her previous diagnosis of ONFH with a relatively large necrotic area. As steroid use in ONFH may be considered to have a poorer clinical course, with more frequent bilateral involvement and earlier collapse [11], we cautiously monitor the patient for the development or exacerbation of existing ONFH lesions and the need to replace steroid treatment as an alternative treatment strategy. It has been reported that cyclosporine or mizoribine added to low-dose glucocorticoids maintains remission in patients with steroid-dependent IgMPC-TIN [6]. To reduce the PSL dose, minimize side effects, and prevent the occurrence of disease activity during steroid tapering, cyclosporine was added to the PSL treatment in this case. The rationale for selecting cyclosporine was primarily its use as a practical steroid-sparing agent and its clinical feasibility, given that its blood concentration can be monitored and its dose can be adjusted relatively easily. Following the tapering and discontinuation of PSL and the switch to cyclosporine monotherapy, several biomarkers of disease activity, including urinary sugar, protein, β2-microglobin, and serum IgM levels remained within normal range. Additionally, the levels of hepatobiliary enzymes remained within the normal range during treatment with ursodeoxycholic acid, even though the patient discontinued steroid use. She successfully maintained remission after cyclosporine monotherapy for at least one year after PSL discontinuation.

Cyclosporine is an immunosuppressive drug used to treat immune-mediated diseases and organ rejection post-transplant [12]. In kidney-related diseases, it is often prescribed for nephrotic syndrome, particularly in cases that do not respond to steroid treatment. The mechanism of action of cyclosporine is as a calcineurin inhibitor, which competitively binds to and inhibits calcineurin. Calcineurin is a calcium/calmodulin-dependent serine/threonine phosphatase. Activated calcineurin dephosphorylates regulatory sites of several transcription factors. The most notable target of calcineurin is the nuclear factor of activated T lymphocytes [12]. Cyclosporine prevents the dephosphorylation of nuclear factor of activated T lymphocytes and subsequently inhibits the synthesis of interleukins, including IL-2. Thus, cyclosporine inhibits T lymphocyte activation and the overall immune response by preventing IL-2-related processes [12]. A previous histological analysis of the IgMPC-TIN population showed that CD3-positive T lymphocytes and IgM-positive plasma cells were predominantly colocalized in the proximal tubule in nearly all patients [3], suggesting that close interactions between T lymphocytes and plasma cells may be involved in the pathogenesis. This patient also showed marked infiltration of CD3-positive T lymphocytes in the renal interstitium on pathological examination, and prominent symptoms of Fanconi syndrome on clinical examination. The use of cyclosporine has been suggested to ameliorate the infiltration of T lymphocytes, which may regulate the overall immune abnormalities.

To our knowledge, this is the first case report of a patient with IgMPC-TIN who maintained remission with cyclosporine after PSL discontinuation. This approach may reduce the risk of bone lesion exacerbation associated with long-term PSL use. Further research is required to establish an optimal treatment regimen for improving the long-term prognosis of patients with IgMPC-TIN. This case provides valuable insights into potential treatment strategies for IgMPC-TIN.

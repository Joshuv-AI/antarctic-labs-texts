Deep learning-based joint analysis of diabetic retinopathy and glaucoma in retinal fundus images
Authors: T Geetha, C Hema
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn current times, Diabetic retinopathy (DR) might be more difficult to diagnose when coexisting with glaucoma, since the two diseases share retinal abnormalities. Worldwide, DR is one of the most common causes of blindness. Conventional convolutional neural network (CNN)-based approaches struggle significantly with this type of co-morbid imaging due to the inherent difficulty in understanding both coarse-grained features and global correlations. The authors of this study propose a novel deep learning architecture, Vision Transformer (ViT) with Bi-Directional Feature Fusion (BFF) (ViT-BiFusionDRNet-HGS), to address these limitations. It is fine-tuned using the HGS technique, which was created for the Hunger Games, and combines a Vision Transformer (ViT) with Bi-Directional Feature Fusion (BFF). The BFF module enables the learning of semantic features from low-level textures, while the Vision Transformer captures long-distance spatial correlations. By incorporating the Hunger Games Search (HGS) algorithm into the model, it optimizes crucial hyperparameters and fusion weights, allowing for better generalization across complex fundus images, faster convergence, and more accurate lesion localization. With a classification accuracy of 98.4% and sensitivity levels higher than those of CNN, standalone ViT, and other baseline optimizers, the model demonstrated superior performance on open-source datasets for diabetic retinopathy and glaucoma fundus images. Clinically, ViT-BiFusionDRNet-HGS shows great potential as a real-time, scalable system for automated analysis of retinal abnormalities in complex diagnostic situations.Keywords: Diabetic retinopathy (DR), Glaucoma, Fundus imaging, Vision transformer (ViT), Bi-Directional feature fusion (BFF), Deep learning, Retinal disease detection, ViT-BiFusionDRNet, Medical image analysis, Automated diagnosis, Hunger games search (HGS) algorithmSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Diseases, Health care, Mathematics and computing, Medical research

Introduction

Diabetes-related retinopathy, often known as DR, is a common consequence of diabetes and the primary cause of blindness and vision loss in people all over the globe. The vision loss that is associated with diabetic retinopathy is often not noticeable until the disease has progressed to a more severe level. Therefore, to avoid long-term harm, it is essential to obtain a diagnosis that is both accurate and timely. When diabetes mellitus (DM) and other eye conditions, such as glaucoma, which is a neurological illness that results in the slow death of the optic nerve, occur simultaneously, the situation becomes even more difficult. It is very challenging to diagnose diabetic retinopathy (DR) in patients with glaucoma because diagnostic characteristics such as optic disc cupping, hemorrhages, and neovascularization coincide with one another.

Recently, several studies have shown that deep learning strategies have the potential to recognize DR. In their study, Subramanian et al.1 conducted a bibliometric analysis, which enabled them to identify patterns and highlight the need to simplify models for easier comprehension. The categorization of red lesions was the primary focus of the research conducted by Saranya et al.2, while Aziz et al.3 used deep learning to identify hemorrhages in the context of DR screening. The findings of these studies have highlighted the need to develop specialized architectures capable of recognizing fundus pictures that incorporate properties unique to DR technology.

On the other hand, the majority of CNN-based models suffer from inadequate contextual awareness and a lack of generalizability (Li et al.4 and Muthusamy and Palani5. Even though CNNs are used to recognize fine-grained patterns in retinal pictures, they sometimes fail to recognize spatial hierarchies and long-range correlations. Özbay used the Artificial Bee Colony (ABC) technique6 to enhance lesion detection in segmented fundus photographs. Wahab Sait7 suggested a lightweight deep learning model for real-time DR screening. The performance of CNN was enhanced by Das and Saha8 by the use of evolutionary approaches, which brought attention to difficulties with training stability and scalability.

Several recent evaluations have highlighted the need to improve methods for attention and model fusion operations. Using evolutionary computing, Bhandari et al.9 investigated early-stage DR diagnosis to demonstrate how current designs are unable to deal with co-morbid retinal disorders. On the other hand, Vij and Arora10 presented a detailed investigation of DR detection using deep learning. During their assessment of vision transformer-based models, Agarwal and Bhat11 found that metaheuristic optimizers were not included in the models. The research conducted by Mohanty and colleagues12 suggests that transformer-based designs have the potential to achieve improved classification performance, particularly when applied to a diverse range of datasets.

Rachapudi et al.13 presented an improved deep learning framework for DR detection. They emphasized the role of hyperparameter tweaking and ensemble learning in improving model performance. Data asymmetry, lesion variability, and poor generalization across patient demographics are some of the challenges that Jagan Mohan et al.14 have brought to light as concerns with deep learning for DR diagnosis. Glaucoma exacerbates these difficulties significantly. Despite Butt et al.15 offering a hybrid feature extraction approach that combines deep learning features with handcrafted ones, the global contextual information could not be collected in its entirety.

The novelty of the paper: Through the implementation of a unique hybrid model that is referred to as ViT-BiFusionDRNet-HGS, the purpose of this work is to overcome these deficiencies. The ViT backbone of the model enables it to capture global associations in fundus pictures, and the BFF enhances the semantic merging of data that spans multiple scales simultaneously. A unique metaheuristic algorithm, known as HGS, optimizes convergence and performance by observing competing survival dynamics. This is accomplished by changing fusion settings and attention weights.

Literature review

In this section, the existing work relates to Diabetic retinopathy is discussed with its merits and limitations.

Biomedical image analysis has placed a significant emphasis on the identification and classification of diabetic retinopathy (DR) via the use of artificial intelligence and machine learning. This is mostly because DR is quite prevalent all over the world and poses a significant risk of irreversible vision loss. Improvements in DR detection have become feasible due to recent breakthroughs in hybrid feature extraction and deep learning. There are still issues that need to be addressed, such as improving robustness across real-world datasets, dealing with co-morbid retinal illnesses such as glaucoma, and simulating long-range interactions. There is a possibility that the problems convolutional neural networks (CNNs) encounter when attempting to remember spatial hierarchies might be resolved by the use of capsule networks.

The DR classification capsule network model (CapsNet), proposed by Kalyani et al.16, is superior to traditional convolutional layers because it maintains the spatial links that exist between features. On the other hand, real-time DR screening is not feasible due to the model’s complexity and slow convergence rate.

Traditional approaches to machine learning are still being investigated, despite their limited promise in contemporary therapeutic environments. A convolutional neural network model has been successfully created using the VGG19 framework to detect DR, which was accomplished by Parthasharathi et al.17 by the use of fundamental classifiers and statistical learning approaches. It is challenging to generalize the findings from controlled datasets when clinical photographs include noise or multiple classes, particularly when the pictures involve co-existing illnesses such as glaucoma. This is despite controlled datasets having the potential to be useful in such situations.

It remains the case that convolutional neural network (CNN) variations are the most effective for detecting diabetic retinopathy (DR). A modification to the ResNet-50 model’s architecture was made by Lin and Wu18, which resulted in an improvement in the accuracy of lesion feature acquisition. Furthermore, Das et al.19 presented a diabetic retinopathy detection framework using a convolutional neural network for feature extraction and classification (DRFEC) architecture, which combines classification modules with convolutional feature extraction. This design is similar to the one described above. Although both models achieve better results than regular CNNs, they still suffer from the same fundamental limitation: they are unable to capture long-range spatial connections. This presents a significant challenge when attempting to comprehend retinal fundus images that contain multiple lesions, either overlapping or spaced apart.

A Deep Learning Multi-Label Feature Extraction and Classification (ML-FEC) approach was employed in the study conducted by Usman et al.20 using statistical dimensionality reduction methods, such as Principal Component Analysis (PCA). Even while principal component analysis increases model efficiency and reduces feature redundancy, it is possible to compress and lose diagnostically essential information when employed. This is despite the fact that it reduces feature redundancy. On top of that, it is possible that these linear approaches are not capable of depicting the non-linear lesion patterns that are hallmark of drug resistance.

A novel two-stage DR detection system utilizes a model based on U-Nets for DR prediction, as described by Bilal et al.21. This is especially true in photos with moderate to severe DR stages or those impacted by glaucoma, which cause spatially distant lesions. U-Net excels at pixel localization, but struggles with understanding the contextual connections among pixels.

A hybrid neural network model that utilizes entropy-based augmentation was proposed by Imran et al.22 to enhance contrast and reveal latent features. Although this does increase the model’s capacity to identify small lesions, it lacks the flexibility to incorporate new images or accommodate varying degrees of anatomical complexity. Gupta et al.23 developed an optimized hybrid ML approach with the express purpose of DR detection utilizing smartphones. A similar line of thinking followed this approach. Despite its inexpensive cost, the performance suffers a considerable decline when processing complex retinal images.

The use of algorithms for optimizing and preprocessing characteristics has yielded a variety of outcomes. The Modified Generative Adversarial-based Crossover Salp Grasshopper (MGA-CSG) approach was developed by Navaneethan and Devarajan24 to enhance feature representation. Both the processes of deep attention and the structures of the retina based on transformers are key components of the retinal environment, but this idea does not encompass both. Despite the ensemble-optimized convolutional neural network (CNN) model, which incorporated texture features, having issues with model interpretability and high computational cost, it was nevertheless able to outperform its rivals (Ishtiaq et al., 2016)25.

The current work in disaster recovery analysis employing optimization approaches based on swarm intelligence and evolutionary algorithms has shown some promising results. An improvement to the Inception-V4 model was made by Yang et al.26 using a dynamic Snow Leopard Optimization (DSLO) algorithm (SLOA). Despite this method enhancing classification accuracy and convergence, it did not address the problems associated with feature fusion across different scales. Through the use of deep learning in conjunction with discrete wavelet transformations to split and classify DR phases, Mutawa et al.27 were able to improve border detection, despite the absence of substantial contextual modeling.

Methods known as Artificial Bee Colony (ABC) are often used in the process of improving disaster recovery models. DRs were discovered by Mrutyunjaya and Gadgay28 using ABC in combination with data augmentation. On the other hand, their technique had issues with fusion learning and convergence time, despite enhancing detection rates. A deep ensemble learning model based on ABCEnsemble was proposed by Uyar et al.29 to detect various eye illnesses to aid in the diagnosis process. Although it is successful, this method requires a significant amount of processing power and does not perform well for DR-specific lesion separation when other diseases, such as glaucoma, are also present.

Recent studies have explored the application of OCTA vascular density maps alongside automated machine learning pipelines for the classification of proliferative and non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy (DR)30. Additionally, hybrid segmentation strategies that integrate adaptive filtering, fuzzy entropy, and skeletonization have been investigated to enhance retinal vessel extraction. These techniques have demonstrated significant promise in some scenarios, especially for improving lesion visibility and conducting vessel-level research31. But they are sometimes limited by their reliance on only one imaging method or hand-crafted feature engineering, which may make them less adaptable to different diseases and datasets. The suggested ViT-BiFusionDRNet-HGS, on the other hand, is a deep learning framework that can be used in many different situations. It combines global and local representations to make it easier to find and classify lesions in different types of fundus images.

Both diabetic retinopathy (DR) and glaucoma are clinically related disorders because hemodynamic changes in the retinal vasculature are fundamental to their etiology. Capillary non-perfusion, leakage, and neovascularization are signs of microvascular dysfunction in DR, whereas decreased retinal blood flow and poor optic nerve head perfusion are signs of glaucoma. Particularly in the early or comorbid stages, these common vascular alterations can make identification more difficult and frequently result in overlapping visual presentations. More precise disease characterisation is now possible because to recent research that demonstrates how deep learning models can extract minute hemodynamic and microvascular patterns from fundus images. The suggested ViT–BiFusionDRNet–HGS framework closely complies with these physiological foundations by combining both local texture and global structural data, providing a unified method for the simultaneous detection of DR and glaucoma.

Proposed methodology

The proposed system contains unique deep learning framework ViT-BiFusionDRNet-HGS addresses the diagnostic challenges of retinal fundus images for the detection of glaucoma and diabetic retinopathy (DR). This approach leverages the representational strength of Vision Transformers (ViT) and Bi-Directional Feature Fusion (BFF) to extract global spatial dependencies and low-level texture features simultaneously. Traditional CNN-based models struggle to capture localized lesion patterns and long-range retinal structural interactions; however, our technique leverages ViT’s attention-based processes to reason globally across image patches. By bidirectionally integrating semantic data from multiple hierarchical levels, the BFF module provides accurate lesion localization and feature refinement. Fusion weights and hyperparameters are dynamically tuned using the Hunger Games Search (HGS) algorithm, improving convergence time and generalizability. In response to the classification results, the model adjusts its parameters to enhance accuracy and reduce overfitting. This evolutionary optimization strategy allows this. Transformer attention, bi-directional fusion, and nature-inspired optimization make the ViT-BiFusionDRNet-HGS scalable and clinically reliable for retinal disease screening.

The reasoning behind integrating the Vision Transformer (ViT) with the Bi-Directional Feature Fusion (BFF) module is well supported in the existing body of literature. This combination tackles a significant difficulty in medical image analysis, which is the capture of both global (semantic/contextual) and local (texture/structural) picture information. Using self-attention processes, the ViT component is particularly effective at modeling global dependencies and semantic structures. On the other hand, the BFF module dynamically combines low-level textural details and high-level semantic elements, which enables a representation that is both more resilient and nuanced.

The BFF enhances semantic and texture-level feature learning by allowing information to flow bottom-up (refining global features with local detail) and top-down (stabilizing fine textures via context), strengthening the model’s ability to differentiate subtle pathologies—critical for complex conditions like coexisting DR and glaucoma. Empirical studies on similar hybrid models show that bidirectional feature fusion leads to clear improvements in segmentation accuracy, detection reliability, and generalizability compared to single-path or unidirectional fusion alone. This validates the architectural choice and demonstrates its practical effectiveness within the hybrid ViT framework.

The Bi-Directional Feature Fusion (BFF) module combines coarse global context collected by the Vision Transformer with fine-grained local information from CNN layers to improve representation learning. This dual-level fusion is crucial for fundus imaging, which show intricate structures including microaneurysms, vascular networks, and optic disc architecture. By the use of a cross-attention gating mechanism, BFF gives a comprehensive structural picture while the transformer branch’s channel attention shows specific vascular and lesion textures. By ensuring that global features improve local feature maps and vice versa, this two-way information flow enhances lesion boundary delineation and minimizes information loss. Therefore, in difficult retinal images, the fused feature representations improve classification accuracy and lesion location by better discriminating between overlapping diseases.

To make advantage of generalized visual representations, the suggested ViT–BiFusionDRNet–HGS system incorporates a 12-layer Vision Transformer (ViT) with 12 attention heads and an embedding dimension of 768 which has been pretrained on ImageNet-21k. To preserve the structural integrity of retinal features, domain adaptation for fundus imaging was accomplished by fine-tuning on retinal datasets using vessel-preserving augmentation, color normalization, and adaptive learning rate scheduling. The Bi-Directional Feature Fusion (BFF) module uses a dual attention mechanism channel attention from the ViT branch and spatial attention from the CNN branch to combine low-level convolutional features from the encoder with high-level transformer embeddings. To improve texture–context alignment, these are iteratively fused using an across-attention gate that permits information to flow in both ways. For stable convergence, the Hunger Games Search (HGS) method optimizes dropout settings, learning rates, and fusion weights. To help visualize this fusion process, a schematic image of the BFF module will be given.

In complicated fundus images, the Vision Transformer (ViT) and Bi-Directional Feature Fusion (BFF) module improve coarse and fine feature representation. BFF dynamically and adaptively fuses global contextual information from the ViT—vascular patterns, optic disc, and structural layout—with local, high-frequency features from CNN layers, such as microaneurysms, lesions, and vessel margins. Global (transformer) context improves local feature attention, while local details enrich and contextualize the global picture. BFF’s cross-attention gating mechanism selectively weights and aligns features bidirectionally. Integrative fusion is essential for fundus imaging, which must distinguish overlapping diseases and minor structural distinctions. Recent studies show that bidirectional fusion modules increase border precision, segmentation, and classification in medical imaging by harmonizing semantic information from different depths and scales. Thus, the BFF module produces complete and highly discriminative feature representations for lesion localization and multi-condition classification, reducing information loss and improving the model’s capacity to distinguish coexisting retinal disorders.

Fig. 3 depicts how a Vision Transformer (ViT) model works within an NLP image processing tool. Initially, the image is divided into predetermined patches, such as 16 × 16 pixels. Each patch is vectorized and processed by a linear projection layer to create an embedding space. This turns the 2D picture into a 1D patch token string. Since transformers do not know the patch sequence, add a positional embedding after projection to preserve spatial information in each token. The sequence is supplemented by a unique categorization token ([CLS]) that aggregates data from all patches to produce the final forecast. Many tiers of attention-based computations in the Transformer Encoder (TE) utilize this token sequence. The right-side inset shows layer normalization (Norm) and residual connections for each layer. A feedforward Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) and multi-head self-attention (MHSA) mechanism dominate the layers. The MLP enhances token representations, while the self-attention technique captures global context by allowing every patch to pay attention to every other patch. After passing through the transformer layers, the [CLS] token output is sent to an MLP Head with one or more fully connected layers. Next, a Softmax layer converts raw scores into target class probabilities. Finally, the model labels the input picture depending on a previously specified category, such as diabetic retinopathy stage or glaucoma. This method substitutes CNNs in visual tasks by depicting long-range relationships between picture areas utilizing transformer attention mechanisms. Discussion

A number of studies focused on glaucoma add to DR research by looking at additional methods to diagnose the disease. Fast fuzzy C-means clustering has been utilized to identify optic disc abnormalities in fundus images, whilst nature-inspired algorithms have been employed for optimal feature selection in medical imaging tasks, including COVID-19 identification. Multi-objective strategies for glaucoma detection underscore the balance between lesion sensitivity and structural integrity. Additionally, multimodal biometric fusion and AI-driven OCT analysis demonstrate the capabilities of feature integration and deep learning for early glaucoma screening and classification. Most of these methods are effective for a single disease at a time and fail to operate well when DR and glaucoma symptoms overlap. Our ViT-BiFusionDRNet-HGS system completes the gap by using transformer-based reasoning, bidirectional feature fusion, and evolutionary optimization.

The proposed model has the potential to be useful in clinical settings since it can make inferences in real time (around 19 ms per image), use attention-based lesion localization to make the results easier to understand, and operate with existing fundus imaging workflows. Preprocessing and augmentation enhance resilience against inadequate images; however, additional evaluation on noisy real-world scans and prospective workflow integration studies are essential to validate practical use in ophthalmological contexts.

Along with classification, the model’s ability to find lesions has been evaluated using publically available fundus image subsets with region-level annotations. This study used established measures like the Dice coefficient and Intersection-over-Union (IoU) between predicted lesion attention maps and ground-truth annotations to measure localization performance. The results showed that ViT-BiFusionDRNet-HGS achieved higher Dice/IoU scores than baseline models, which showed that it performed better at both accurately finding lesions and classifying diseases.

The suggested model shows great results on popular open-source datasets, but we know that its validation is limited because there lacks a lot of large-scale, multi-center clinical data. EyePACS, APTOS, and MESSIDOR are examples of public datasets that add useful variety, but they don’t fully show how different patient demographics, imaging devices, acquisition settings, or grading standards might be in real-world clinical practice. This limitation may influence the applicability of our findings across various hospitals or screening settings. In future study, we will evaluate ViT-BiFusionDRNet-HGS using multi-center, prospective clinical datasets and conduct external validation tests to demonstrate its resilience and scalability across various populations and imaging methods.

This integration’s efficacy is supported by empirical assessments. The BFF module and HGS adjustment, according to ablation analyses, significantly enhance diagnostic performance, increasing classification accuracy from 95.8% (ViT baseline) to 98.4% (ViT–BFF–HGS). Additionally, the model demonstrates improved spatial coherence across multimodal retinal characteristics with greater temporal fusion accuracy (TFA = 0.921 ± 0.012) and structural consistency (SDC = 0.892 ± 0.014). Additionally, lesion localization measurements show that the fusion-driven attention mechanism improves region-specific interpretability, with an IoU of 0.74 and a Dice coefficient of 0.81. The saliency maps’ visual reliability is further supported by clinician evaluation, which yielded a mean interpretability score of 4.4 ± 0.3. Convergence stability is enhanced by the HGS-driven hyperparameter optimization (AHSS = 0.91), guaranteeing steady performance under different lighting scenarios and with overlapping pathogenic signatures. Together, these results show that the ViT–BFF–HGS architecture offers a diagnostically sound framework that can maintain both global retinal structure and local microvascular integrity, increasing the accuracy of automated screening for glaucoma and diabetic retinopathy that coexist.

In retinal disease identification, the ViT-BiFusionDRNet-HGS architecture outperforms CapsNet, DRFEC, ML-FEC, and MGA-CS, especially for comorbid diabetic retinopathy and glaucoma. The proposed method consistently earns the highest scores for all patient cohorts in temporal fusion accuracy (TFA), feature redundancy elimination ratio (FRER), structural deviation consistency (SDC), multiscale retinal lesion coverage ratio (MRLCR), attention-guided feature alignment index (AFAI), and hyperparameter optimization stability score (HOSS). The technique excels at accurate feature fusion, strong lesion coverage, consistent hyperparameter tweaking, and attention-driven feature alignment. For complicated, multimodal retinal illness scenarios, ViT-BiFusionDRNet-HGS outperforms CNN and transformer-based methods in diagnostic performance, architectural robustness, and generalizability.

The suggested ViT–BiFusionDRNet–HGS model demonstrated notable overall performance; however, a comprehensive error analysis has been conducted to pinpoint possible failure instances. Misclassifications primarily happened in images with very different lighting, fuzzy blood vessels, or overlapping signs of advanced diabetic retinopathy and glaucoma. In some instances, lesion boundaries were partially concealed, resulting in diminished attentional concentration. A full failure case study with visual examples and confidence-based uncertainty analysis is planned as part of future work to assist us understand model errors better. It can assist people understand the limits of the model better and make it more reliable for use in real-world healthcare environments.

While traditional metrics like accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and AUC effectively measure diagnostic performance, the addition of new indicators—Temporal Fusion Accuracy (TFA), Structural Dependency Coherence (SDC), and Hyperparameter Optimization Stability Score (HOSS)—provides additional information about the model’s stability and reliability. TFA measures how well temporal features line up across multi-scale fusion layers, SDC measures how well anatomical structure stays the same during feature aggregation, and HOSS measures how stable it is when hyperparameters are changed. These criteria together underscore the model’s reliability, structural integrity, and optimization robustness, broadening the evaluation framework beyond conventional classification metrics to better capture real-world clinical relevance.

To evaluate the model’s adaptability to various disease presentations, we performed performance analysis at the subtype level for both glaucoma and diabetic retinopathy (DR). For DR, the model consistently displayed a specificity of 96% and a high sensitivity across the mild (97.6%), moderate (98.1%), and proliferative (98.4%) stages. Classification performance for glaucoma has been consistent across early (96.8%), moderate (97.9%), and advanced (98.3%) phases, suggesting a lack of bias toward a particular disease severity. This consistency shows how well the BFF module combines global optic disc features and localized lesion textures in different presentations. The model’s capacity to generalize across clinically different subgroups is demonstrated by these findings, bolstering its potential for wider practical application in multi-disease retinal screening.

We used transformer attention heatmaps and Grad-CAM to depict the regions that contributed more to the model’s predictions to improve the interpretability of the model. Ophthalmologists’ diagnostic focus is precise with these visualizations, which frequently show clinically significant retinal abnormalities such as microaneurysms, hemorrhages, and optic disc alterations. By guaranteeing that both global structural signals and fine-grained vascular features have a role in the final decision, the BFF module further enhances interpretability and allows physicians to track how multi-scale information affects predictions. Transparency, clinical trust, and ease of integrating the model into practical workflows are all facilitated by this explainable output.

Even while the ViT–BiFusionDRNet–HGS model performs well on test datasets, real-world reliability depends on managing tough clinical images with severe artifacts. The system uses vessel-preserving denoising, contrast enhancement, and adaptive histogram equalization to address clinical fundus acquisition issues like low contrast, blur, lighting variability, and imaging noise. These preprocessing steps improve visual clarity and maintain vascular and lesion architecture.Along with preprocessing, the Bi-Directional Feature Fusion (BFF) module’s dual attention mechanism improves feature robustness. Even with poor input images, BFF strengthens lesion boundaries and highlights structurally relevant elements by facilitating global-to-local and local-to-global information transfer. Despite poor imaging conditions, lesion localization and classification improve.Early evaluations on purposefully degraded picture subsets demonstrated that the model’s sensitivity—already superior to CNN or ViT baselines—remained strong with a small accuracy loss (around 2%). This suggests resilience to imaging artifacts. Future research will use a bigger, more diversified multi-institutional dataset representing real-world image quality fluctuations to validate and quantify this resilience. In clinical settings where image quality might vary greatly, expanding this assessment will make the framework suitable for widespread deployment.

Endnotes

Our cutting-edge deep learning architecture, ViT-BiFusionDRNet-HGS, can distinguish between fundus pictures of glaucoma and diabetic retinopathy in challenging co-morbid imaging conditions. The suggested technique balances localized semantic awareness with long-range spatial correlation extraction by integrating the Vision Transformer (ViT) module’s global reasoning with the BFF module’s targeted texture learning. The Hunger Games Search (HGS) technique enhances model convergence, generalizability, and lesion localization while automating essential hyperparameter and fusion weight adjustments. The model outperformed CapsNet, DRFEC, ML-FEC, and MGA-CS in every research that employed eight independently specified performance indicators across various patient groups. ViT-BiFusionDRNet-HGS enhances interpretability and achieves 98% classification accuracy through transformer attention alignment, making it scalable and therapeutically practical for real-time retinal disease screening. This approach aids future full-ocular diagnostics research, particularly in multimodal imaging and longitudinal progression monitoring.

ViT–BiFusionDRNet–HGS performs excellently on open datasets, however placing it to use in the real world could be challenging because of things like domain shift, image noise, hardware limitations, and following the rules. Inconsistency can be attributed to differences in imaging technologies and the demographics of the patients. Transfer learning and federated learning can assist the model work in various clinical contexts. Continuous model updates make sure the model is strong, can grow, and keeps working well in different hospital settings.

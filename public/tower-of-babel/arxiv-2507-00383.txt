Unified study of two-meson and axion-meson production from semileptonic tau decays within resonance chiral framework
Jin Hao, Chun-Gui Duan, Zhi-Hui Guo, [0.5em] Department of Physics and Hebei Key Laboratory of Photophysics Research and Application, Hebei Normal University, Shijiazhuang 050024, China

Abstract
We carry out the joint study of the semileptonic tau decays into the two-meson and axion-meson channels, viz. τ − → ( P 1 ​ P 2 ) − ​ ν τ \tau^{-}\to(P_{1}P_{2})^{-}\nu_{\tau} and τ − → π − ​ ( K − ) ​ a ​ ν τ \tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}(K^{-})a\nu_{\tau} within the framework of resonance chiral theory by including the model-independent axion-gluon-gluon interaction. By utilizing the π 0 \pi^{0} - η \eta - η ′ \eta^{\prime} -axion mixing matrix elements from recent studies, we calculate the pertinent two-pseudoscalar boson form factors. To simultaneously fit the experimental spectra measured in the Cabibbo allowed τ − → π − ​ π 0 ​ ν τ \tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{0}\nu_{\tau} process and also the Cabibbo suppressed τ − → ( K S ​ π − , K − ​ η ) ​ ν τ \tau^{-}\to(K_{S}\pi^{-},K^{-}\eta)\nu_{\tau} ones, we determine all the relevant hadron resonance parameters. Then we give predictions to the spectra and branching ratios for various channels, such as τ − → ( π − ​ η , π − ​ η ′ , K − ​ η ′ , π − ​ a , K − ​ a ) ​ ν τ \tau^{-}\to(\pi^{-}\eta,\pi^{-}\eta^{\prime},K^{-}\eta^{\prime},\pi^{-}a,K^{-}a)\nu_{\tau} . We also calculate the forward-backward asymmetries for all the aforementioned channels. The interplay between the scalar and vector form factors for different observables is analyzed in detail. Our theoretical predictions supply useful guidance to the future tau experiments, such as those at Belle-II, Super Tau-Charm Facility and Tera-Z factory of Circular Electron-Positron Collider.

2
Description of the tau decay amplitudes

The Standard Model (SM) structure for the charged currents (CC) will be employed to calculate the semileptonic tau decays, which can be cast in the conventional form of four-fermion operator as

\mathcal{L}^{\rm SM}_{\rm CC}=-\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{uD}\bar{\nu}_{\tau}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})\tau\bar{D}\gamma^{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})u\,,

(1)

where
G_{F}
denotes the Fermi constant,
V_{uD}
corresponds to the CKM matrix elements, with the down-type quarks
D=d
and
s
. The amplitude of the two-pseudoscalar boson tau decay process, i.e.,
\tau(p_{\tau})\to P_{1}(p_{1})P_{2}(p_{2})\nu_{\tau}(p_{\nu})
, can be then written as

\mathcal{M}=\frac{G_{F}V_{uD}}{\sqrt{2}}\bar{u}(p_{\nu})\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})u(p_{\tau})H^{\mu}\,,

(2)

where the hadronic current is usually parameterized by

H^{\mu}\equiv\langle P_{1}P_{2}|\bar{D}\gamma^{\mu}u|0\rangle=\left[(p_{2}-p_{1})^{\mu}-\frac{\Delta_{P_{2}P_{1}}}{s}q^{\mu}\right]F^{P_{1}P_{2}}_{+}(s)+\frac{\Delta_{Du}}{s}q^{\mu}\widehat{F}^{P_{1}P_{2}}_{0}(s)\,,

(3)

with

\Delta_{P_{2}P_{1}}=m_{P_{2}}^{2}-m_{P_{1}}^{2}\,,\qquad\Delta_{Du}=B_{0}(m_{D}-m_{u})\,,\qquad q_{\mu}=(p_{1}+p_{2})_{\mu},\qquad s=q^{2}.

(4)

Here
m_{P_{i}}
denotes the mass of the pseudoscalar boson
P_{i}
, and
m_{D=d,s}
and
m_{u}
are the light-flavor quark masses. The quantity
B_{0}
relates with the nonperturbative quark condensate via
\langle 0|\bar{q}_{i}q_{j}|0\rangle=-F^{2}B_{0}\delta_{ij}
, with
F
the pion decay constant in chiral limit. In this parameterization,
F^{P_{1}P_{2}}_{+}(s)
and
\widehat{F}^{P_{1}P_{2}}_{0}(s)
represent the vector and scalar form factors, respectively. The finitude feature of the matrix elements in Eq. (
3
) at
s=0
requires the following normalization condition

\displaystyle F_{+}^{P_{1}P_{2}}(0)=\frac{\Delta_{Du}}{\Delta_{P_{2}P_{1}}}\widehat{F}_{0}^{P_{1}P_{2}}(0)\,.

(5)

After the evaluation of
|\mathcal{M}|^{2}
in Eq. (
2
) by taking the spin average/sum of the
\tau
/
\nu_{\tau}
, one can acquire the conventional form of the differential decay width of the
\tau^{-}\to(P_{1}P_{2})^{-}\nu_{\tau}
process

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\Gamma_{\tau\to P_{1}P_{2}\nu_{\tau}}}{d\sqrt{s}d\cos\alpha}=\frac{G_{F}^{2}\,\left|V_{uD}\right|^{2}\,S_{\text{EW}}\left(m_{\tau}^{2}-s\right)^{2}}{128\pi^{3}}\,\bigg\{\frac{\left|\widehat{F}_{0}^{P_{1}P_{2}}(s)\right|^{2}\,\Delta_{Du}^{2}\,q_{P_{1}P_{2}}(s)}{\,m_{\tau}\,s^{2}}

(6)

\displaystyle+\frac{4\Delta_{Du}\,q_{P_{1}P_{2}}^{2}(s)\,\cos{\alpha}\,\Re\left[F_{+}^{P_{1}P_{2}}(s)\,\widehat{F}_{0}^{P_{1}P_{2}*}(s)\right]}{\,m_{\tau}\,s^{3/2}}+\frac{4\left|F_{+}^{P_{1}P_{2}}(s)\right|^{2}\,q_{P_{1}P_{2}}^{3}(s)\,\left(m_{\tau}^{2}\,\cos{\alpha}^{2}+s\,\sin{\alpha}^{2}\right)}{\,m_{\tau}^{3}\,s}\bigg\}\,,

where
\alpha
denotes the angle between the momenta of one of the two pseudoscalar bosons and the
\tau
lepton in the
P_{1}P_{2}
rest frame, and the three-momentum of the pseudoscalar bosons in the latter frame is given by

\displaystyle q_{P_{1}P_{2}}(s)=\frac{\sqrt{s^{2}-2s\Sigma_{P_{1}P_{2}}+\Delta_{P_{1}P_{2}}^{2}}}{2\sqrt{s}},\qquad\Sigma_{P_{1}P_{2}}=m_{P_{1}}^{2}+m_{P_{2}}^{2}\,.

(7)

To further integrate out the angle
\alpha
in Eq. (
6
), we obtain the differential decay width with respect to
\sqrt{s}
, i.e., the energy of the
P_{1}P_{2}
system,

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma_{\tau\to P_{1}P_{2}\nu_{\tau}}}{d\sqrt{s}}=

\displaystyle\frac{G_{F}^{2}M_{\tau}^{3}}{48\pi^{3}s}S_{\text{EW}}\left|V_{uD}\right|^{2}\left(1-\frac{s}{M_{\tau}^{2}}\right)^{2}

(8)

\displaystyle\left\{\left(1+\frac{2s}{M_{\tau}^{2}}\right)q_{P_{1}P_{2}}^{3}(s)\left|F_{+}^{P_{1}P_{2}}(s)\right|^{2}+\frac{3\Delta_{Du}^{2}}{4s}q_{P_{1}P_{2}}(s)\left|\widehat{F}_{0}^{P_{1}P_{2}}(s)\right|^{2}\right\}\,.

Alternatively, one can also construct another important type of differential distribution, viz. the so-called forward-backward (FB) asymmetry,

\displaystyle A_{FB}(s)=

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{0}^{1}d\cos\alpha\frac{d^{2}\Gamma_{\tau\to P_{1}P_{2}\nu_{\tau}}}{d\sqrt{s}d\cos\alpha}-\int_{-1}^{0}d\cos\alpha\frac{d^{2}\Gamma_{\tau\to P_{1}P_{2}\nu_{\tau}}}{d\sqrt{s}d\cos\alpha}}{\int_{0}^{1}d\cos\alpha\frac{d^{2}\Gamma_{\tau\to P_{1}P_{2}\nu_{\tau}}}{d\sqrt{s}d\cos\alpha}+\int_{-1}^{0}d\cos\alpha\frac{d^{2}\Gamma_{\tau\to P_{1}P_{2}\nu_{\tau}}}{d\sqrt{s}d\cos\alpha}}

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta_{Du}\,q_{P_{1}P_{2}}(s)\,\Re\left[F_{+}^{P_{1}P_{2}}(s)\,\widehat{F}_{0}^{P_{1}P_{2}*}(s)\right]}{\frac{2\sqrt{s}}{3}\left(1+\frac{2s}{M_{\tau}^{2}}\right)q_{P_{1}P_{2}}^{2}(s)\left|F_{+}^{P_{1}P_{2}}(s)\right|^{2}+\frac{\Delta_{Du}^{2}}{2\sqrt{s}}\left|\widehat{F}_{0}^{P_{1}P_{2}}(s)\right|^{2}}\,,

which probes the interference term of the vector and scalar form factors that is however absent in the differential decay width expression in Eq. (
8
). It is noted that the FB asymmetry can play important roles in establishing the CP violation in hadronic
\tau
decays
[
39
,
40
,
41
]
.

From the above discussions, it is clear that under the assumption of SM structure of charged currents the
\tau\to P_{1}P_{2}\nu_{\tau}
processes are totally determined by the two form factors
F_{+}^{P_{1}P_{2}}(s)
and
\widehat{F}_{0}^{P_{1}P_{2}}(s)
, both of which will be calculated within
{\rm R}{\chi}{\rm T}
in the following section.

3
Calculation of the hadronic form factors

3.1
Pertinent resonance chiral Lagrangians

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\rm LO}=\frac{F^{2}}{4}\langle u_{\mu}u^{\mu}\rangle+\frac{F^{2}}{4}\langle\chi_{+}\rangle+\frac{F^{2}}{12}M_{0}^{2}X^{2}\,,

(10)

where
\langle...\rangle
stands for the trace over the light-quark flavor space, and the last term corresponds to the explicit

\displaystyle u_{\mu}=iu^{\dagger}D_{\mu}Uu^{\dagger}\,,\qquad\chi_{\pm}=u^{\dagger}\chi u^{\dagger}\pm u\chi^{\dagger}u\,,\qquad U=u^{2}=e^{i\frac{\sqrt{2}\Phi}{F}}\,,

(11)

\displaystyle\qquad D_{\mu}U=\partial_{\mu}U-i(v_{\mu}+a_{\mu})U+iU(v_{\mu}-a_{\mu})\,,\qquad\chi=2B_{0}(s+ip)\,,\qquad

(12)

where

,

(13)

In

\displaystyle X=\log{(\det U)}-i\frac{a}{f_{a}}\,,

(14)

without performing the axial transformation of the quark fields as commonly used in chiral studies

3.2
Strangeness conserving form factors

The decays of
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}(\pi^{0},\eta,\eta^{\prime},a)\nu_{\tau}
are driven by the strangeness conserving currents, belonging to the Cabibbo allowed processes. In this case, we need to evaluate the matrix elements of the strangeness conserving vector current
\langle P_{1}P_{2}|\bar{d}\gamma_{\mu}u|0\rangle
to acquire the corresponding vector form factors. Regarding the scalar form factors, the proper object to extract them is
\langle P_{1}P_{2}|(m_{D}-m_{u})\bar{D}u|0\rangle
, since the latter corresponds to the divergence of the vector current, i.e.,
\langle P_{1}P_{2}|\partial^{\mu}(\bar{D}\gamma_{\mu}u)|0\rangle=i\langle P_{1}P_{2}|(m_{D}-m_{u})\bar{D}u|0\rangle
, and is renormalization group invariant
[
53
,
54
]
.

In practical calculation, we will substitute the light quark masses
m_{D=d,s}
and
m_{u}
with the physical masses of
\pi
and
K
evaluated in
{\rm R}{\chi}{\rm T}
. For the strangeness conserving situation, the isospin breaking quantity
m_{d}-m_{u}
enters in the definition of the scalar form factors. According to the Dashen’s theorem, the electromagnetic (EM) corrections to the pion and kaon masses are equal, i.e.,
\delta_{m_{K^{0}}^{2}-m_{K^{+}}^{2}}^{\rm EM}=\delta_{m_{\pi^{0}}^{2}-m_{\pi^{+}}^{2}}^{\rm EM}

\Delta_{su}^{\text{Phy}}=m_{K}^{2}-m_{\pi}^{2}

and

\displaystyle F_{+}^{(KP)^{-}}(0)=\frac{\Delta_{su}^{\text{Phy}}}{\Delta_{PK}}F_{0}^{(KP)^{-}}(0)\,,

4
Combined fit to the
\tau^{-}\to(\pi^{-}\pi^{0},K_{S}\pi^{-},K^{-}\eta)\nu_{\tau}
decays

In this section, we perform the combined fit to three kinds of experimental data, including the pion vector form factors
F_{+}^{\pi^{-}\pi^{0}}
measured in the
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{0}\nu_{\tau}
decay in Ref.
[
60
]
, and the invariant-mass distributions of
K_{S}\pi^{-}

[
62
]
and
K^{-}\eta

[
63
]
in the
\tau^{-}\to\nu_{\tau}(K_{S}\pi^{-},K^{-}\eta)
processes.

We use the standard
\chi^{2}
function in the fit to incorporate the modulus squared of the normalized pion vector form factor

\chi^{2}_{1}=\sum_{i}\left(\frac{{|\tilde{F}_{+}^{\pi^{-}\pi^{0}}(s_{i})|^{2}_{\text{Theo}}}-{|\tilde{F}_{+}^{\pi^{-}\pi^{0}}(s_{i})|^{2}_{\text{Exp}}}}{\sigma_{|\tilde{F}_{+}^{\pi^{-}\pi^{0}}(s_{i})|^{2}_{\text{Exp}}}}\right)^{2}\,,

(67)

where the normalized form factor is given by
\tilde{F}_{+}^{\pi^{-}\pi^{0}}(s)=F_{+}^{\pi^{-}\pi^{0}}(s)/\sqrt{2}
and 62 data points are taken from Ref.
[
60
]
.
For the two-meson invariant-mass spectra, we use the following formula to perform the fit of the normalized experimental event distributions

\mathcal{N}_{P_{1}P_{2}}(E)\equiv\frac{\mathrm{d}N_{\text{Eve}}^{\text{bin}}}{N_{\text{Eve}}^{\text{Tot}}\mathrm{d}E}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\Gamma(\tau\to P_{1}P_{2}\nu_{\tau})}{\mathrm{d}E}\frac{1}{\Gamma_{\tau}\,\overline{B}_{\tau\to P_{1}P_{2}\nu_{\tau}}}\,,

(68)

where
E=\sqrt{s}
,
N_{\text{Eve}}^{\text{bin}}
stands for the observed number of events in each bin,
N_{\text{Eve}}^{\text{Tot}}
is the total number of events, and
\Gamma_{\tau}
corresponds to total decay width or the inverse of lifetime for
\tau
. Energy bin sizes of 11.5 MeV and 25 MeV are used for
\tau^{-}\to K_{S}\pi^{-}\nu_{\tau}

[
62
]
and
\tau^{-}\to K^{-}\eta\nu_{\tau}
[
63
]
, respectively, in the experimental study. For
\tau^{-}\to K_{S}\pi^{-}\nu_{\tau}
, the analysis incorporates experimental data up to bin 90 (
\sqrt{s}
= 1.65925
GeV
), consistent with prior analyses
[
64
,
65
,
66
]
. Similar as the latter references, data points corresponding to bins 5, 6, and 7 are also excluded from the
\chi^{2}
minimization due to their obvious incompatibility with theoretical expectations. It is verified that the inclusion of these three data points yields a substantially increased
\chi^{2}
while inducing negligible changes in the fitted parameters. For
\tau^{-}\to K^{-}\eta\nu_{\tau}
, the first two data points from Belle
[
63
]
are excluded as they lie significantly below the
K^{-}\eta
production threshold, and data points significantly exceeding the
\tau
mass are also excluded.
The quantity of
\overline{B}_{\tau\to P_{1}P_{2}\nu_{\tau}}
in Eq. (
68
) is introduced as a normalization factor in our fit, which would be identical to the branching ratio of the
\tau\to P_{1}P_{2}\nu_{\tau}
channel for the ideal description of the experimental event distributions.
The Belle analysis in Ref.
[
62
]
for the
\tau^{-}\to K_{S}\pi^{-}\nu_{\tau}
decay channel yields a branching ratio of
B_{K_{S}\,\pi^{-}}^{\text{Exp}}=(4.04\pm 0.13)\times 10^{-3}
. Regarding the
\tau^{-}\to K^{-}\eta\nu_{\tau}
process, Belle Collaboration reports a branching fraction of
B_{K^{-}\eta}^{\text{Exp}}=(1.58\pm 0.10)\times 10^{-4}

[
63
]
.
To circumvent the imperfect mismatch of the normalization between theoretical and experimental event distributions, we also fit the
\overline{B}_{\tau\to P_{1}P_{2}\nu_{\tau}}
quantities in Eq. (
68
) to the aforementioned branching ratios from Belle. Thus, the
\chi^{2}
function minimized in our fit for the event distributions takes the form

\chi^{2}_{2}=\sum_{P_{1}P_{2}=\begin{subarray}{c}\,K_{S}\pi^{-},\\
K^{-}\eta\end{subarray}}\mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\prime}}\limits_{i}\left[\frac{\mathcal{N}^{\text{Theo}}_{P_{1}P_{2}}(E_{i})-\mathcal{N}^{\text{Exp}}_{P_{1}P_{2}}(E_{i})}{\sigma_{\mathcal{N},{P_{1}P_{2}}}^{\text{Exp}}(E_{i})}\right]^{2}+\sum_{P_{1}P_{2}=\begin{subarray}{c}\,K_{S}\pi^{-},\\
K^{-}\eta\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{\overline{B}_{P_{1}P_{2}}^{\text{Theo}}-B_{P_{1}P_{2}}^{\text{Exp}}}{\sigma_{B_{P_{1}P_{2}}}^{\text{Exp}}}\right)^{2}\,,

(69)

where
\sigma^{\text{Exp}}
stand for the corresponding experimental uncertainties. The prime symbol in the summation notation denotes the exclusion of specific points from the minimization process as mentioned previously. The number of fitted data points amounts to 87 for the
K_{S}\pi^{-}
spectrum and 31 for the
K^{-}\eta
spectrum, each accompanied by their corresponding branching fractions. The total
\chi^{2}
function minimized in the fit is given by the sum of those from the three channels, i.e.,
\chi^{2}=\chi_{1}^{2}+\chi_{2}^{2}
, with
\chi_{1}^{2}
and
\chi_{2}^{2}
in Eqs. (
67
) and (
69
), respectively.

6
Summary and conclusions

In this work, we exploit resonance chiral theory augmented with model-independent axion interaction operator
aG\tilde{G}/f_{a}
to calculate the form factors for the two-meson and axion-meson production from the semileptonic
\tau
decays, by utilizing the
\pi
-
\eta
-
\eta^{\prime}
-
a
four-particle mixing matrix elements in Ref.
[
32
]
. Our calculations simultaneously include the following channels for both Cabibbo-allowed and Cabibbo-suppressed cases:
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{0}\nu_{\tau},\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\eta/\eta^{\prime}\nu_{\tau},\tau^{-}\to K_{S}\pi^{-}\nu_{\tau},\tau^{-}\to K^{-}\eta/\eta^{\prime}\nu_{\tau}
and
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}/K^{-}a\nu_{\tau}
(the latter two channels being studied here for the first time).

We have successfully performed the joint fit by reasonably reproducing the experimental data from the Belle collaboration on the two-meson invariant-mass spectra in the
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{0}\nu_{\tau}

[
60
]
,
\tau^{-}\to K_{S}\pi^{-}\nu_{\tau}

[
62
]
and
\tau^{-}\to K^{-}\eta\nu_{\tau}

[
63
]
, where the relevant unknown resonance parameters are determined.
We then utilize those fitted resonance parameters to make predictions to the two-boson spectra and branching ratios for other five decay channels, including
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\eta/\eta^{\prime}\nu_{\tau},\tau^{-}\to K^{-}\eta^{\prime}\nu_{\tau}
and
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}/K^{-}a\nu_{\tau}
.
Apart from the QCD axion scenario, we extend our analysis to the axion-like particle case by introducing a nonzero axion mass
m_{a}
. We compute differential decay widths for
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}/K^{-}a\nu_{\tau}
channels at
m_{a}=0
, 0.1 GeV and 0.3 GeV for illustrations, and calculate these branching ratios as a function of
m_{a}
across a continuous range from 0 to 1 GeV.

Our predicted branching ratios for
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\eta/\eta^{\prime}\nu_{\tau}
and
\tau^{-}\to K^{-}\eta^{\prime}\nu_{\tau}
are in agreement with the current experimental upper limits.
Furthermore, we present predictions for forward-backward asymmetries in the two-pseudoscalar boson decays of the
\tau
lepton, which probe vector-scalar form factor interference that is absent in the differential decay widths with respect to the two-boson energy.
Our predictions to the branching ratios, invariant-mass distributions and the forward-backward asymmetries can provide useful guidelines to the future experimental measurements, such as the ongoing Belle II and future facilities like the Super Tau-Charm Facility and the Circular Electron Positron Collider.

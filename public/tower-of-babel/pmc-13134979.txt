CD135 (FLT3 receptor) expression as an indicator of prognosis in patients with de novo acute myeloid leukemia
Authors: Jinhong Nie, Lu Gao, Yingchun Shao, Li Yang, Junjie Cao, Jinge Xu, Yuhang Wang, Yuqi Zhang, Tong Cui, Shiyuan Zhou, Wenjuan Zhu, Mingqing Zhu, Xiao Ma, Depei Wu, Xiaojin Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Acute myeloid leukemia (AML) blasts often have high CD135 (FLT3 receptor) expression, but its clinical impact is unclear. We analyzed CD135 expression, and the clinical characteristics and outcomes of 214 patients with de novo AML diagnosed between October 2022 and May 2024. Subsequently, we collected data on additional 78 patients, diagnosed with AML at four medical centers from June to December 2024, for external validation. The high-CD135-expression group had significantly lower CD34 surface expression (p = 0.003) and higher CD33 expression (p = 0.014) on AML blasts. The high-CD135-expression group also showed a higher frequency of NPM1 (p < 0.001) and DNMT3A (p = 0.032) mutations, but was not significantly associated with CD135 expression (p = 0.229). The patients in the high-CD135-expression group had lower initial induction therapy response rates than those in the low-CD135-expression group (p < 0.001). High CD135 expression was independently associated with poorer OS and PFS. In the subgroup of patients with high CD135 expression and FLT3-ITD mutations, those who received TKI combined with chemotherapy had significantly better OS (p = 0.007). Then we developed a prognostic nomogram incorporating CD135 expression. This model performed well both in the development cohort (area under the curve [AUC] = 0.817) and multicenter validation cohort (AUC = 0.722). CD135 expression on AML blasts is a pivotal marker that integrates molecular pathogenesis with clinical outcomes, highlighting its dual role as a prognostic indicator and therapeutic target in precision clinical approaches for AM.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00277-026-07010-3.

Introduction

Acute myeloid leukemia (AML) is one of the most common hematologic malignancies in adults. Its intrinsic heterogeneity contributes to resistance to anti-leukemia drugs and treatment failure [1]. Phenotypic characteristics and genetic alternations play an important role in influencing prognosis. In the 2022 European LeukemiaNet (ELN) risk-stratification system for AML, as a consensus, cytogenetic and molecular genetic abnormalities or mutations are central to diagnosis and management [2]. However, this system offers limited prognostic discrimination because it is based on cytogenetic and genetic information but does not consider other disease characteristics [3–5]. The updated ELN 2024 risk-stratification system have been established for the widespread use of less-intensive chemotherapy but still requires clinical validation [6]. Under this circumstance, more biomarkers are in need for better prediction (7).

CD135, also known as the FMS-like tyrosine kinase 3 (FLT3) receptor, is a member of the type III receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK) family. It is primarily expressed on hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells, but this expression is lower than that on blasts [8, 9]. Considering its original gene-FLT3, approximately 30% of patients with AML have FLT3 mutations (FLT3-ITD or FLT3-TKD), which cause constitutive activation of tyrosine kinase, which plays a key role in promoting malignant proliferation [10–12]. Although high CD135 surface expression is commonly observed on AML blasts, the relationship between CD135 surface expression and FLT3 mutations is unclear [9, 12, 13].

Few studies have focused on the effect of CD135 surface expression on prognosis in adults with AML. To address this gap, we retrospectively evaluated CD135 surface expression on blasts of patients with AML to assess its prognostic significance, and evaluated the relationship between CD135 surface expression and prognosis to assess whether CD135 could be used to improve AML risk stratification.

Materials and methods

Patients and study design

We enrolled 214 patients with de novo AML diagnosed at Soochow Hopes Hematonosis Hospital and the First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University between October 2022 and May 2024 based on morphologic, immunophenotypic, cytogenetic, and molecular analysis. Subsequently, using the same criteria, we retrospectively collected data on an additional 78 patients, diagnosed with AML at four medical centers from June to December 2024, to serve as an external validation cohort (Fig. 1).

Fig. 1Study flow chart

Study flow chart

The study cohort from two centers (n = 214) was divided into high- and low-CD135 expression subgroups, based on the median positive percentage of 51.2% (determined by the distribution of CD135 marrow blast expression in different patients and the receiver operating characteristic curve analysis, details seen in Supplementary Fig. 1). Risk stratification was performed based on the molecular and cytogenetic analysis, according to the 2022 ELN guidelines [2]. Complete remission (CR) was defined as recovery of hematopoietic function after 1–2 courses of standard induction therapy, with bone marrow leukemia blasts < 5% and no extramedullary infiltration. Treatment regimens included venetoclax plus azacitidine (VA) therapies [14] and the classic “3 + 7” regimen [15], adjusted for patient age and comorbidities. In patients with FLT3 mutations, gilteritinib was used as targeted therapy when available and clinically appropriate [16]. The decision for allo-HSCT was based on a combination of ELN 2017/2022 risk stratification, patient age, performance status, donor availability, and response to induction therapy. The primary endpoint was overall survival (OS), defined as the time from diagnosis to death from any cause. The secondary endpoint was progression-free survival (PFS), defined as the time from diagnosis to disease progression, relapse, or death, whichever occurred first.

Cell profiling

Morphology, immunophenotypic and cytogenetic assays, and sequencing were conducted on diagnosis according to standard protocols. Patient samples underwent multidimensional immunophenotypic analysis. CD135-positive cells among leukemia blasts were identified by low CD45 expression and side scatter on flow cytometry (Fig. 2A). Cells were incubated with CD34-APC, CD45-PerCP, and CD135-PE at room temperature for 15 min in the dark. Additional immunophenotypic markers included CD3, CD4, CD8, CD56, CD7, CD38, CD20, CD19, CD45, CD34, HLA-DR, CD13, CD33, CD117, CD64, CD14, CD11b, and CD16. All reagents were sourced from BD Biosciences (Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA). Red blood cells were lysed with erythrocyte cytosol lysate for 10 min and then washed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and suspended in PBS for analysis using a Becton flow cytometer (BD Biosciences).

Each patient’s molecular profile was determined using next-generation sequencing to detect mutations associated with hematologic malignancies (Supplementary Table 1). Gene fusions were identified using real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction [17] (Supplementary Table 2). Cytogenetic analysis was performed using standard banding techniques. Samples with more than three cytogenetic aberrations were defined as a complex karyotype (CK) [18].

Statistical analysis and predictive nomogram establishment

Baseline data were compared between groups using the chi-square test for categorical variables and the Mann–Whitney U-test, Pearson Correlation Analysis for continuous variables. OS and PFS were estimated using Kaplan–Meier analysis and Cox regression. Allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) was included in the Cox regression model as a time-dependent variable. Unadjusted and multivariable Cox regression models were used to identify factors associated with survival, and the results were reported as hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Two-tailed p values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 29.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), and data visualization was performed using GraphPad Prism 9 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA) and R Studio 4.3.3 (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

In the multivariable Cox regression analysis, variables were selected for inclusion in a nomogram to predict 183- and 366-day OS using stepwise backward elimination. Risk scores were calculated for each patient and the development cohort was reclassified into low-, intermediate-, and high-risk groups based on the nomogram risk scores.

Data on 78 patients from four hospitals were used to assess external validity of the nomogram. The area under the time-dependent receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) and calibration curves generated through bootstrapping were used to assess model discrimination and evaluate the agreement between the predicted and observed survival. AUC values > 0.7 were considered to indicate reasonable discrimination. Calibration plots were used to compare the observed survival with the nomogram-predicted survival, with the 45° diagonal line indicating perfect concordance. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was used to determine the range of risk threshold probabilities to evaluate the potential net benefit of the nomogram in clinical practice.

Results

CD135 surface expression according to clinical characteristics

The association between CD135 surface expression levels and clinical characteristics in the 214 patients is shown in Fig. 2A. CD135 expression in the blast population ranged from 0% to 97.88%. The 214 patients were divided into low- and high-CD135-expression groups based on the median value (51.2%) to simplify the analysis and clinical application. The pretreatment clinical characteristics and laboratory test results are shown in Table 1. Compared with the low-CD135-expression group, the high-CD135-expression group had a higher proportion of leukemia blasts in the bone marrow and higher white blood cell count in the peripheral blood at diagnosis. The distribution of the 2022 ELN risk subgroups did not differ significantly between the two CD135-expression groups (p = 0.733). Compared with the low-CD135-expression group, the high-CD135-expression group had significantly lower CD34 surface expression (45.0% versus 70.8%, r = − 0.243, p = 0.003) and higher CD33 expression (75.1% versus 83.4%, r = 0.203, p = 0.014) (Fig. 2B and C). The cytogenetic data did not differ significantly between the two groups (Table 1). Correlation analysis identified significant co-expression between NPM1 and DNMT3A (r = 0.460, p < 0.001), and between NPM1 and FLT3-ITD (r = 0.320, p < 0.001) in the overall cohort (Fig. 2D). Then we collected and analyzed the frequency of mutations in those patients, and genes with low mutation frequency in our cohort (n ≤ 5%) were excluded (Table 1). Compared with the low-CD135-expression group, the high-CD135-expression group showed a higher frequency of (independent variable, considering DNMT3A and ASXL1, p = 0.002) NPM1 (p < 0.001) and DNMT3A (p = 0.032) mutations, and a lower frequency of ASXL1 mutations (p = 0.009) (Table 1). CD135 surface expression have no significant difference among three ELN 2022 risk groups (p = 0.555) (Supplementary Fig. 2). The presence of FLT3-ITD mutations was not significantly associated with CD135 expression (p = 0.229). (Supplementary Fig. 3) Besides, the VAF of FLT3-ITD had no association with the CD135 surface expression level. (Supplementary Fig. 4).

Response to initial induction therapy

Of 214 patients with AML, 172 received 1 or 2 complete courses of induction therapy. Their initial induction therapies are listed in Supplementary Table 3. The 78 patients in the high-CD135-expression group had lower response rates than the 94 patients in the low-CD135-expression group (79.5% versus 96.7%, p < 0.001). In both the low- and high-CD135-expression groups, patients who received venetoclax-based regimens had higher response rates than those who received the standard “3 + 7” therapy (87.8% versus 66.7%, p = 0.032; and 81.9% versus 50%, p = 0.013, respectively). 71.1% of the high-CD135-expression patients have achieved MRD-negative after the induction therapy, compared with 79.8% in the low-CD135-expression patients (p = 0.255). Then we compared post-induction MRD status (positive/negative) within the ELN 2022 risk groups (Negativity percentages: 87.5% versus 73.8% versus 68.1%, for favorable, intermediate and adverse risk respectively, p = 0.036). Patients with M4/M5 (myelomonocytic/monocytic) leukemia had response rates to VA treatment similar to those of patients with other FAB subtypes (75.9% versus 82.8%, p = 0.357).

For patients with newly diagnosed FLT3-ITD mutated AML who are eligible for intensive chemotherapy (e.g., the “7 + 3” regimen), current international guidelines (e.g., ELN 2022) recommend the addition of a gilteritinib-based FLT3 inhibitor to frontline therapy, regardless of age. However, during the early years of this study, access to gilteritinib was not universal in our region due to its high cost and variable reimbursement policies. In the cohort of 51 patients with the FLT3-ITD mutation, only 22 patients were treated with sequential chemotherapy combined with continuous tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) therapy. In patients with the FLT3-ITD mutation who did not receive TKI therapy, the high-CD135-expression group showed a significantly lower CR rate than those in the low-CD135-expression group (40.0% versus 91.1%, p = 0.020). In contrast, among patients treated with TKIs, the CR rate did not differ significantly between those in the high- and low-CD135-expression groups (75.0% versus 90.0%; p = 0.594).

Progression-free survival and overall survival

In the 214 patients, the median follow-up was 130 days (range: 2–547 days) for OS and 129 days (range: 2–547 days) for PFS. 1-year OS and 1-year PFS of those patients were 68.8% (95%CI: 60.7–78.0%) and 56.3% (95%CI: 47.2–67.1%). Forty-four patients received allo-HSCT according to standard procedures. HSCT was associated with significantly improved OS in the high-CD135-expression cohort (HSCT versus no-HSCT: 92.9% versus 44.7%, p < 0.001, for 1-year OS; 92.9% versus 35.7%, p < 0.001, for 1-year PFS), whereas HSCT conferred no survival advantage in the low-expression cohort (HSCT versus no-HSCT: 87.5% versus 75.3%, p = 0.112, for OS; 61.5% versus 55.8%, p = 0.274, for PFS) (Supplementary Fig. 5–8). Besides, there was no significant survival advantage of HSCT in the low-CD135-expression group with Inter- and adverse-risk by ELN 2022 risk stratification (Supplementary Fig. 9–10). In the unadjusted analyses, high CD135 expression was associated with poorer OS (HR = 2.760, p = 0.001; Fig. 3A) and PFS (HR = 1.720, p = 0.044; Fig. 3B). In the multivariable analysis, high CD135 expression was associated with poorer OS and PFS (Table 2). In addition to CD135 expression, several covariates were identified as independent factors associated with poorer OS. These included elevated serum lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) levels (HR = 3.12, p = 0.023), CK (HR = 4.38, p = 0.008), and not undergoing HSCT (HR = 7.52, p = 0.009) (Table 2). In addition to high CD135, older age, RUNX1 mutation, complex karyotype, and absence of HSCT, were associated with poorer PFS (Table 2).

In the subgroup analyses, high CD135 expression was associated with significantly poorer OS in patients with NPM1, FLT3-ITD, TET2, and DNMT3A mutations (Supplementary Fig. 11–14). In the subgroup of patients with high CD135 expression and FLT3-ITD mutations, those who received TKI combined with chemotherapy had significantly better OS (180d-OS, 100.0% versus 59.7%; p = 0.007). In contrast, in patients with low CD135 expression and FLT3-ITD mutations the OS was generally favorable, and TKI treatment did not significantly improve the OS (100.0% versus 70.0%; p = 0.320) (Fig. 3C and D).

Validation of ELN risk-stratification system and establishment of nomogram

Collectively these analyses identified CD135 as an important biomarker for predicting clinical outcomes, irrespective of the ELN risk group and the presence of NPM1 and other mutations. To validate the ELN risk-stratification system, we excluded the 44 patients who underwent HSCT and analyzed the data on the remaining 170 patients (Fig. 1). Their baseline characteristics are shown in Supplementary Table 4. Based on the ELN 2022 risk-stratification system, OS (p = 0.983; Fig. 3E) and PFS (p = 0.082; Fig. 3F) did not differ significantly between the intermediate- and high-risk groups, indicating poor differentiation of the clinical outcomes. The ELN 2024 risk-stratification system was also applied to most patients in the cohort that received reduced-intensity treatment (such as VA treatment). The ELN 2024 risk-stratification system divided these patients into three risk groups, but showed better differentiation of the clinical outcomes (Fig. 3G-H).

To develop a more precise yet simple risk system, we performed a multivariable Cox regression analysis using backward stepwise variable elimination to control for multicollinearity (Supplementary Table 5). Based on the Cox regression results and clinical value, four predictors (ELN 2024 risk stratification, TET2 mutation, RUNX1 mutation, and CD135 expression) were included in the nomogram (Fig. 4A). The total points from these four factors were visualized in a downward line, illustrating the probabilities of OS at 183 and 366 days. The calibration plot showed that the clinical nomogram closely aligned with the criteria for an ideal model (Fig. 4B). Furthermore, the AUCs for the 183- and 366-day predicted survival were 0.817 and 0.734, respectively, demonstrating better discrimination than ELN risk stratification (Fig. 4C). The decision curve analysis revealed that within a risk-threshold probability of 12–79%, the nomogram brought added benefit than the treat-all scheme or the treat-none scheme (Fig. 4D).

Discussion

In this study, we assessed the relationship between CD135 expression levels and clinical features in AML. Higher CD135 surface expression at diagnosis was associated with poorer outcomes and was applied to establish a clinical prediction model. This model provided better discrimination than that of the unmodified ELN risk-stratification system.

Overexpression of CD135 on blasts was correlated with higher white blood cell counts and a higher proportion of primitive cells, consistent with the findings of previous studies [19]. This is probably due to aberrant activation of the FLT3 signaling pathway. CD34 expression decreases during normal hematopoiesis, whereas CD135 is preferentially expressed on myeloid- and monocytic-committed progenitors at a specific stage [20]. The analysis of the flow cytometry results revealed that CD135 expression on AML blasts was negatively correlated with CD34 expression, indicating that CD135 is a potential biomarker of dysregulated hematopoiesis, with a high proliferative capacity. Additionally, the study found that assessing CD135 surface expression on the blast population alone was more precise than evaluating RNA transcript levels, as it avoided the influence of CD135 mRNA in normal hematopoietic progenitors. This approach provided an unbiased evaluation of CD135 expression on AML blasts.

Notably, CD135 surface expression did not differ significantly between patients with FLT3 wild-type and those with FLT3-ITD or FLT3-TKD mutations, consistent with a multicenter study examining CD135 expression in pediatric AML [21]. Based on an analysis of a public database, Cheng et al. [22] concluded that CD135 overexpression driven by NPM1 mutation may be a risk factor for leukemogenesis. This relationship was also evident in our study and warrants further investigation to understand the underlying mechanism. According to the ELN 2022 risk-stratification system, the NPM1 mutation without FLT3-ITD is considered a favorable risk factor [22–24]. However, our study found that the combination of NPM1 mutation and high CD135 expression at diagnosis was associated with poor OS. Some of the NPM1 mutation subtypes gave rise to poor outcomes of both treatment and survival [25, 26]. The co-occurrence of additional undefined mutations with the NPM1 mutation has also been linked to poor clinical outcomes, highlighting the complex heterogeneity of AML subtypes [27]. We further evaluated the ELN 2022 risk-stratification system in our cohort, and found that it did not predict clinical outcomes accurately. The ELN 2024 risk-stratification system had good discrimination in predicting OS in patients receiving less intensive treatment (such as VA treatment) but became inaccurate as the percentage of patients receiving the “3 + 7” regimen increased, indicating the need for a more general and precise prediction model at a time in which a diverse range of treatment options are available.

High CD135 expression was a significant prognostic factor for poor survival in our cohort, consistent with the findings of previous studies [10, 19, 28]. Integrating CD135 expression on blasts with the ELN risk-stratification system enhanced the predictive accuracy, particularly between the intermediate- and high-risk groups, breaking the dominance of prediction based on cytogenetics alone [29]. In addition, the complicated impact on prognosis arising from frequent co-mutations with NPM1 were subtly summarized by CD135 expression [30]. High white blood cell count were crucial prognostic determinants, as described in previous studies [29, 31]. Both factors reflect a higher leukemia burden and increased heterogeneity within AML. In this study, we identified two undefined mutations within the ELN 2024 risk system, RUNX1 and TET2, as significant prognostic factors. As mutations in epigenetic regulators, RUNX1, TET2, and DNMT3A often occur early in AML development, although their specific prognostic implications remain unclear [32]. In this study, the presence of RUNX1 mutation at diagnosis was a predictor of poor prognosis, aligning with findings by T. J. Kirtek et al. [33]. Moreover, TET2 mutations were associated with a poorer prognosis, consistent with the findings of a meta-analysis [34]. Given the complexity of AML and the emergence of new treatments, we propose a new, simplified risk-stratification model incorporating these clinical features. Internal and external validation confirmed the suitability of the nomogram model for use in clinical practice.

As HSCT remains the definitive curative therapy for AML, identifying patients who will benefit from this high-risk treatment is crucial. Our survival analyses demonstrated a significant improvement in OS and PFS in patients in the high-CD135-expression group undergoing HSCT. In contrast, patients in the low-CD135-expression group did not benefit from HSCT, even those with Intermediate and adverse ELN risks could not. For these patients, intensive chemotherapy alone might be sufficient, while more solid evidences were in need. Additionally, compared with the traditional “3 + 7” regimen, patients who received venetoclax-based regimens showed higher response rates within the first 1–2 courses of induction therapy. Preliminary analysis shows that patients with high baseline CD135 did not correlate with MRD-positive after the induction therapy. While ELN 2022 is a powerful predictor, a subset of MRD-negative patients with high baseline CD135 might still be at risk for late relapse, whereas low CD135 expression might identify a favorable group. It is unlikely that a single baseline marker would outperform MRD for this requires more validations, but it may provide complementary information. In the patients with FLT3-ITD mutations, TKIs combined with chemotherapy were an effective targeted therapy, suggesting that TKIs should be recommended in patients with FLT3-ITD mutation. Our study also suggested that patients with high CD135 expression also benefitted from targeted TKI therapy.

However, our study has several limitations. First, using CD135 expression alone demonstrated suboptimal discriminatory ability in clinical applications due to its low sensitivity and specificity. Second, a larger patient cohort with extended follow-up is warranted to further validate our findings. In particular, the external validation cohort was relatively small and collected over a shorter time window. A prospective study is currently underway to further assess the clinical significance of CD135 surface expression levels in patients with AML/MDS. Additionally, our study does not explore the biological mechanisms linking high CD135 expression to chemoresistance or its interaction with co-occurring mutations beyond statistical associations.

In conclusion, considering the level of CD135 expression on AML blasts, either alone or in combination with gene mutations, provides valuable guidance for predicting survival. We established a simplified risk model to enhance the ELN 2022 risk-stratification system and observed the HSCT and TKI therapies are more effective than traditional chemotherapy in patients with high CD135 expression or FLT3-ITD mutations.

Singular flows with time-varying weights
Immanuel Ben-Porat, José A. Carrillo, Pierre-Emmanuel Jabin

Abstract
We study the mean field limit for singular dynamics with time evolving weights. Our results are an extension of the work of Serfaty [ 12 ] and Bresch-Jabin-Wang [ 8 ] , which consider singular Coulomb flows with weights which are constant time. The inclusion of time dependent weights necessitates the commutator estimates of [ 12 , 8 ] , as well as a new functional inequality. The well-posedness of the mean field PDE and the associated system of trajectories is also proved.



1
Introduction

We aim to study the well posedness and the mean field limit of the following doubly non-local transport PDE

\partial_{t}\mu-\mathrm{div}(\mu\,\mathbf{a}\star\mu)=h\left[\mu\right],\ \mu(0,x)=\mu_{0}\,,

(1.1)

where
\mathbf{a}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a given
vector field and
h\left[\mu\right]
is the signed measure given by

h\left[\mu\right]\coloneqq\mu(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S(x-y)\mu(y)dy\,,

(1.2)

for some function
S:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
We use
\mu(x)dx
as the notation for integrating against the measure
\mu
independently of being absolutely continuous or not with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Moreover, we shall denote by
\mu(x)
its density with respect to the Lebesgue measure in case it is absolutely continuous. Here, both
\mathbf{a}
and
S
satisfy some appropriate structural hypothesis specified in Section 2. The initial data
\mu_{0}
belongs to
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the set of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The solutions
\mu(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
sought after are curves of probability measures, and thus the source term
h\left[\mu\right]
has to have zero average.

The existence and uniqueness theory of PDEs of the type (
1.1
) or variants thereof has been
handled in several works: in
[
21
]
the existence and uniqueness
of measure valued solutions has been established for non-negative
source terms
h\left[\mu\right]
which satisfy a boundeness and Lipschitz
condition with respect to the Wasserstein distance. In
[
22
]
,
a variant of the Wasserstein distance for signed measures has been
introduced and applied in order to remove the positivity assumption
on
h
. The non-positivity assumption is important if one wishes to study models in which
h\left[\mu\right]
has zero mean. Both of these results do not cover the important case where
h\left[\mu\right]
is given by the formula (
1.2
), since such source terms typically satisfy the boundeness and Lipschitz conditions required only locally. This obstacle has been successfully overcome
in
[
14
]
, in which the derivation of the PDE (
1.1
) as a mean field limit is also studied.

Let us denote by
\mathbb{R}^{dN}\setminus\triangle_{N}
the set of non-overlapping configurations, that is, the set of
\mathbf{x}_{N}:=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{dN}
such that
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
for all
i\neq j
, and let us denote by
\mathbb{M}^{N}
the set of admissible weights, that is, the set of
\mathbf{m}_{N}:=(m_{1},\dots,m_{N})
such that
m_{i}\geq 0
, with
\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}=N
.
We can define the empirical measure associated to this configuration as

\displaystyle\mu_{N}\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}.

(1.3)

The following system of ODEs governs the particle dynamics:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\dot{x}_{i}^{N}(t)=-\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{j}^{N}(t)\mathbf{a}(x_{i}^{N}(t)-x_{j}^{N}(t)),\ x_{i}^{N}(0)=x_{i}^{0,N}\\
\dot{m}_{i}^{N}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{i}^{N}(t)m_{j}^{N}(t)S(x_{i}^{N}(t)-x_{j}^{N}(t)),\ m_{i}^{N}(0)=m_{i}^{0,N}.\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

Subject to several technical assumptions on the functions
\mathbf{a}
and
S
, the ODE system (
1.4
) has a globally well defined flow
(\mathbf{x}_{N}(t),\mathbf{m}_{N}(t))
, see
[
2
, Theorem 3]
, where
\mathbf{x}_{N}(t):=(x_{1}^{N}(t),\dots,x_{N}^{N}(t))\in\mathbb{R}^{dN}
and
\mathbf{m}_{N}(t):=(m_{1}^{N}(t),\dots,m_{N}^{N}(t))\in\mathbb{M}^{N}
. We will denote by
\mu_{N}(t)
the empirical measure associated to the system (
1.4
), with a slight abuse of notation, as the empirical measure given by (
1.3
) associated to the configuration
(\mathbf{x}_{N}(t),\mathbf{m}_{N}(t))
.
The empirical measure is readily checked to be a distributional
solution of the PDE (
1.1
). We say that (
1.1
) is the mean field limit of the interacting particle system (
1.4
) if

W_{1}(\mu_{N}(0),\mu_{0})\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0\Longrightarrow W_{1}(\mu_{N}(t),\mu(t))\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0,\ t\in[0,T],

where
\mu(t)
is the solution to the PDE (
1.1
) with initial
data
\mu_{0}
at time
t
and
W_{1}
is the Wasserstein (or Monge-Kantorovich) distance. Before we proceed with the mathematical discussion, let us say a few words about the real life phenomena that the above models idealizes. Given
N
agents interacting pairwise via a given interaction
\mathbf{a}
with time evolving opinions
(x_{1}^{N}(t),\cdots,x_{N}^{N}(t))
and weights of influence
(m_{1}^{N}(t),\cdots,m_{N}^{N}(t))
the system (
1.4
) describes the evolution both of the opinions and the weights of influence in time. As it can be seen from (
1.4
), the
i
-th opinion and the
i
-th weight evolve according to a law which takes into consideration all the other involved opinions and influences.

A notable example of a model of this type is the Kuramoto model and its variants
[
1
,
10
]
. Similar models appear also in crowd dynamics and have received mathematical treatment in
[
18
]
and
[
22
]
. The scientific literature seems to be so far mostly limited to treat continuous interaction kernels, although Hegselmann-Krause type models
[
9
]
includes also jump discontinuities. To the best of our knowledge, the blow up singularities which are considered in the present work have not yet been studied when the weights evolve in time.

Viewed as a system of opinion dynamics, the mean field limit can be interpreted as a large population limit. This limit has been proven
in
[
14
]
, with an approach which resembles the celebrated Dobrushin
theorem
[
11
]
. In
[
2
]
a weak version of the mean field limit has been recovered through a graph limit approach. By “weak” we mean that the result is limited to a special choice of initial configurations (at the microscopic level) and a special
choice of initial data for the Cauchy problem (
1.1
). Among other works which consider the graph limit and its link with the mean field limit we mention
[
7
,
20
]
as well as
[
3
]
for a general overview. The case where the weights form a non-symmetric matrix, a scenario which arises for instance in Neuron dynamics, is also very interesting and has been analyzed in
[
17
]
. All of these results considered measure valued solutions,
and typically one has to require that the functions
\mathbf{a}

and
S
at least satisfy some Lipschitz continuity. However, the
case where the function
\mathbf{a}
admits discontinuities is also
relevant and requires a different mathematical treatment. In the recent
work
[
5
]
, the well posedness and mean field limit has been
established for the one dimensional attractive Coulomb kernel, which corresponds
to the choice
\mathbf{a}(x)=\mathrm{sgn}(x)
in one dimension. The argument proposed
there relies on recasting the limit PDE as a Burgers type equation only valid in one dimension,
and invoking Kruzkhov theory of conservation laws in order to prove
stability estimates for the resulting Burgers equation. It should be mentioned that singularities emerging from
S
are also relevant and have been considered in
[
6
,
18
]
.

The purpose of this work is twofold: to study the well posedness and
mean field limit for (
1.1
) for arbitrary dimension
d\geq 3
in the case where
\mathbf{a}
exhibits a Coulomb type singularity, thereby addressing a question left open in
[
5
]
.
More precisely, we assume the following hypotheses (
H1
) on the functions
\mathbf{a}
and
S
in (
1.1
):

•

(
H1
-i):
\mathbf{a}=\mathbb{J}\nabla V
, with
V(x)
the
d-
dimensional repulsive Coulomb interaction (
d\geq 3
),
V(x)=-\frac{|x|^{2-d}}{2-d}
. Here
\mathbb{J}
is either a
d\times d
anti-symmetric matrix or the identity matrix.

•

(
H1
-ii):
S\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is an odd (
S(-x)=-S(x)
) function.

Note that
\mathrm{div}(\mathbb{J}\nabla V)=0
when
\mathbb{J}
is anti-symmetric and
\mathrm{div}(\mathbb{J}\nabla V)=-\delta_{0}
when
\mathbb{J}
is the identity. In comparison to
[
5
]
, the difficulties which must be addressed
in the new singular settings are reflected on several levels:
First, one has to justify why (
1.4
) has a globally
well defined flow. This result is classical when the weights are time independent and follows from the observation that initial separation of opinions is propagated in time, i.e.

x_{i}^{0,N}\neq x_{j}^{0,N}\Longrightarrow\underset{i\neq j}{\min}\left|x_{i}^{N}(t)-x_{j}^{N}(t)\right|>0.

The inclusion of weights which evolve in time necessitates imposing a condition on
S
which would prevent a Ricatti type blow-up, and this is why we impose the parity condition
S(x)=-S(-x)
. The main difficulty for obtaining the well-posedness at the macroscopic level lies in the fact that when handling discontinuous
V
there is no reason to expect that the term
\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu
verifies a Lipschitz condition on the space of probability
measures
with respect to the Wasserstein distance. This fact eliminates the possibility
of proving the stability estimate with respect to the Wasserstein distance. We are therefore led to restrict
the solutions considered to more well behaved function spaces, namely

L^{p}
or Sobolev spaces. Thirdly, since the stability estimate at our disposal
is restricted to
L^{p}
spaces, we are unable to apply it directly
for the empirical measure in order to obtain the long time convergence.
The most difficult part of this work boils down to overcoming this
issue. Our main idea is to make use of the functional inequalities
discovered in
[
12
,
8
]
as well as proving new functional inequalities which are necessary due to the inclusion of a source term. The functional inequalities in
[
12
]
reflect a particularly exciting development in the theory of mean field limits, as they allow for the first time to rigorously identify the mean field limit of Coulomb flows and have already found numerous applications both in classical mean field limits
[
23
,
16
,
19
]
as well as in quantum many body systems
[
24
,
15
]
. Before stating our two main results, namely the well posedness and the mean field limit, we introduce the following hypotheses:

(
H2
) The initial data
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is such that there is some
R>0
with

\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{0})\subset B(0,R)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let assumptions (
H1
)-(
H2
) hold. Then, there exists a unique solution to the problem (
1.1
)
with initial data
\mu_{0}
. Moreover, this solution satisfies
\mu\in C\left([0,T];L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
\mu\in L^{\infty}\left([0,T];W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)
for all
1\leq p<\infty
.
Furthermore,
\mu(t,\cdot)
is compactly supported and its support satisfies

\mathrm{supp}(\mu(t,\cdot))\subset B(0,\overline{R})\,,\mbox{ for all }t\in[0,T],

where
\overline{R}=\overline{R}(\left\|\mu_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}},R,T,\left\|S\right\|_{L^{\infty}})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let assumptions (
H1
)-(
H2
) hold and let
\mu(t,\cdot)
be the unique solution with initial data
\mu_{0}
ensured via Theorem
1.1
. Let
\mathbf{m}_{N}^{0}\in\mathbb{M}^{N}
such that
m_{i}^{0,N}\leq M
for some
M>0
and
\mathbf{x}_{N}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{dN}\setminus\triangle_{N}
. Denote by
(\mathbf{x}_{N}(t),\mathbf{m}_{N}(t))\in C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{dN}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
the solution to the system of ODEs (
2.1
) with initial data
(\mathbf{x}_{N}^{0},\mathbf{m}_{N}^{0})
ensured via Theorem
2.2
. Then

\displaystyle\mu_{N}(t,\cdot)\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}\mu(t,\cdot)\ \mbox{in the weak sense locally uniformly in time}

provided that
\mathcal{E}_{N}(0)\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0
.

The paper unfolds as follows. In Section 2 we recap basic
structural properties of the model and state the main results. Afterwards, we include an important discussion in which we elaborate on the novelty of our method. Section
3 is devoted to proving the existence of solutions to (
1.1
) and the
stability estimate, from which the uniqueness follows at once. Section 4 is devoted to proving the global well-posedness of the ODE (
1.4
). Finally, Section
5 is devoted to modifying the modulated energy approach introduced
in
[
12
]
and extended in
[
8
]
, thus yielding the mean field limit.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
The equation for the trajectories

The dynamics that we consider are governed by the following system of

(d+1)\times N
ODEs

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\dot{x}_{i}^{N}(t)=-\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{j}^{N}(t)\mathbb{J}\nabla V(x_{i}^{N}(t)-x_{j}^{N}(t)),\ x_{i}^{N}(0)=x_{i}^{0,N}\\
\dot{m}_{i}^{N}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{i}^{N}(t)m_{j}^{N}(t)S(x_{i}^{N}(t)-x_{j}^{N}(t)),\ m_{i}^{N}(0)=m_{i}^{0,N}\end{array}\right..

(2.1)

The notation is as follows: the unknowns are
x_{i}^{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

and
m_{i}^{N}\in\mathbb{R}
are referred to as opinions and weights
respectively, and are supplemented with initial data
x_{i}^{0,N},m_{i}^{0,N}
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
(\mathbf{x}_{N}(t),\mathbf{m}_{N}(t))\in C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{dN}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})

is a solution to the system of ODEs (
2.1
) on
[0,T)
(
T\leq\infty
)
if

\underset{i\neq j}{\min}\left|x_{i}^{N}(t)-x_{j}^{N}(t)\right|>0,\ t\in[0,T)

and for all
t\in[0,T)
and all
1\leq i\leq N
it holds that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}x_{i}^{N}(t)=x_{i}^{0,N}-\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}m_{j}^{N}(\tau)\mathbb{J}\nabla V(x_{i}^{N}(\tau)-x_{j}^{N}(\tau))d\tau\\
m_{i}^{N}(t)=m_{i}^{0,N}+\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}m_{i}^{N}(\tau)m_{j}^{N}(\tau)S(x_{i}^{N}(\tau)-x_{j}^{N}(\tau))d\tau.\end{array}\right.

We say that
(\mathbf{x}_{N}(t),\mathbf{m}_{N}(t))
is a solution
to the system of ODEs (
2.1
) with maximal life span
T>0
if it is a solution on
[0,T)
to (
2.1
), but is not a solution on
[0,T]
.

The following theorem establishes that there is a well-defined flow for the system of ODEs (
2.1
). The proof is postponed to Section
4
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let hypothesis (
H1
) hold. Suppose that for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
it holds that
\mathbf{x}_{N}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{dN}\setminus\triangle_{N}
,
\mathbf{m}_{N}^{0}\in\mathbb{M}_{N}
and there is some
M>0
such that for all
1\leq i\leq N
it holds that

0\leq m_{i}^{0,N}\leq M.

Then, the system of ODEs (
2.1
) has a unique global solution
(\mathbf{x}_{N}(t),\mathbf{m}_{N}(t))\in C^{1}([0,\infty);\mathbb{R}^{dN}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})

solution with initial data
(\mathbf{x}_{N}^{0},\mathbf{m}_{N}^{0})
. In particular
x_{i}^{N}(t)\neq x_{j}^{N}(t)
for all
t\in[0,\infty)
and all
i\neq j
.

Remark 2.3
.

Since
V
is the Coulomb interaction
there is some constant
\mathbf{C}>0
such that

\left\|\nabla^{2}V\star\mu\right\|_{2}\leq\mathbf{C}\left\|\mu\right\|_{2}

and

0\leq\widehat{V}(x)\leq\frac{\mathbf{C}}{\left|x\right|^{2}},

where
\widehat{V}
is the Fourier transform of
V
. The first inequality is the Calderon-Zygmund inequality (see
[
13
, Theorem 4.12]
), and the second inequality is due to the fact that
-\Delta V=\delta_{0}
.

Remark 2.4
.

The assumption that
S
is odd implies that any solution
(\mathbf{x}_{N}(t),\mathbf{m}_{N}(t))
of the system of ODEs (
2.1
) has
m_{i}^{N}(t)\geq 0
for all
t\in[0,T]
and conserves the total weight, i.e.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{i}^{N}(t)=1

for all
t\in[0,T]
. In addition there is a constant
\overline{M}=\overline{M}(M,\left\|S\right\|_{\infty})
such that for all
t\in[0,T]
it holds that

m_{i}^{N}(t)\leq\overline{M},\ i=1,\dots,N.

2.2
The mean field equation

Recall that the mean field limit PDE that is expected to be derived
from (
2.1
) is

\partial_{t}\mu-\mathrm{div}(\mu\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu)=h\left[\mu\right],\ \mu(0,x)=\mu_{0}.

(2.2)

We introduce the notation
\mathbf{S}[\mu]\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S(x-y)\mu(y)dy
and
\mathbf{A}[\mu]\coloneqq\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu
.
We endow the space
C([0,T];\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
with the metric

D(\mu,\nu)\coloneqq\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}W_{1}(\mu(t,\cdot),\nu(t,\cdot))

where
W_{1}
is the Wasserstein distance (see
[
25
]
). Our notion of weak solution is as follows.

Definition 2.5
.

Assume
V
and
S
satisfy (
H1
) and let
\mu_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. A time dependent
\mu\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

is said to be a weak solution of (
2.2
)
if for all
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
it holds that

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)\mu(t,x)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\varphi(x)\mathbf{A}[\mu](t,x)\mu(t,x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h[\mu](t,x)\varphi(x)dx,\ \mu(0,x)=\mu_{0},

in the distributional sense.

Notice that the second term in the definition of weak solution to (
2.2
) makes sense by a separation into short and long range terms in
\nabla V\star\mu
using that
\mu(t,\cdot)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Remark 2.6
.

As remarked in
[
14
]
, an equivalent definition to Definition
2.5
is to demand that for each
\varphi\in C^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))
it holds that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x)\mu(t,x)+\nabla\varphi(t,x)\mathbf{A}\left[\mu\right](t,x)\mu(t,x)-\varphi(t,x)h\left[\mu\right](t,x)\right]dxdt=0.

Remark 2.7
.

In case
\nabla V
is Lipschitz, the definition of weak solution to (
2.2
) can be done assuming only
\mu_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We will frequently need several structural properties of the source term which are not influenced by the regularity imposed on
S
. We summarize these properties in the following result.

Proposition 2.8
.

[
14
, Proposition 9]
For all
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it holds that
\mathrm{supp}(h\left[\mu\right])=\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
,
\left|h\left[\mu\right]\right|\leq\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}\mu
, and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h\left[\mu\right](x)dx=0.

2.3
Comments on the method

The most technically involved part of this work is the derivation of the PDE (
2.2
) as a mean field limit from the dynamics (
2.1
). Therefore we turn the reader’s attention to several key points in the proof. First, let us consider the case where
S=0
. In this case the PDE (
2.2
) is homogeneous, and the weights are constant in time - for simplicity we can take
m_{i}^{N}=\frac{1}{N}
for all
1\leq i\leq N
. The method in
[
12
]
rests upon a clever renormalization argument of a weak strong stability principle for the limit PDE. Let us introduce first the weak strong stability principle at the level of the limit PDE followed by a brief outline of the renormalization procedure carried out in
[
12
]
.

The weak strong stability principle
. Given solutions
\mu_{1}(t,\cdot),\mu_{2}(t,\cdot)
to the PDE (
2.2
), consider the modulated energy

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right)(t,x)V\star\left(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right)(t,x)dx.

A standard calculation reveals that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(t)

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu_{2}(t,x)-\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu_{2}(t,y)\right)\nabla V(x-y)\left(\mu_{1}(t,\cdot)-\mu_{2}(t,\cdot)\right)^{\otimes 2}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq 2\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\nabla^{2}V\star\mu_{2}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla V\star\left(\mu_{1}(t,\cdot)-\mu_{2}(t,\cdot)\right)\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\nabla^{2}V\star\mu_{2}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\mathcal{E}(t),

(2.3)

where we used the identity

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x-y)\mu(x)\mu(y)dxdy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla V\star\mu\right|^{2}(x)dx

(2.4)

for any
\mu\in\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
d\geq 3
, which can be readily seen in Fourier. Assuming that
\mu_{2}(0,\cdot)\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(for
p>1
sufficiently large) and that
\mu_{2}
enjoys propagation of Sobolev regularity, it can be shown that
\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\nabla^{2}V\star\mu_{2}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\lesssim\left\|\mu_{2}(0,\cdot)\right\|_{W^{1,p}}
. Thus the inequality (
2.3
)
yields the stability estimate

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)\leq e^{Ct}\mathcal{E}(0)

for some constant
C=C(\left\|\mu_{2}(0,\cdot)\right\|_{W^{1,p}})
. It is remarkable that this argument necessitates only regularity on one of the solutions involved.

Renormalization
. We now define the renormalized modulated energy associated to
\mu
and
\mu_{N}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N}(\mu,\mu_{N})

\displaystyle:=\int_{x\neq y}V(x-y)\left(\mu_{N}(\cdot)-\mu(\cdot)\right)^{\otimes 2}dxdy.

(2.5)

Given a solution
\mu(t,\cdot)
to the PDE (
2.2
) and a solution
\mathbf{x}_{N}(t)
to the system of ODEs (
2.1
), we define the renormalized modulated energy associated to them as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N}(t):=\mathcal{E}_{N}(\mu(t,\cdot),\mu_{N}(t,\cdot)).

(2.6)

The definitions (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) extend in the obvious manner in the case of weighted empirical measures. A standard calculation reveals that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}_{N}(t)\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{x\neq y}\left(\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu(t,x)-\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu(t,y)\right)\nabla V(x-y)\left(\mu_{N}(t,\cdot)-\mu(t,\cdot)\right)^{\otimes 2}dxdy.

Note the removal of the diagonal in the integrals above, which is necessary due to the singularity of
V
at the origin. The functional inequality which allows to close the estimate, and which reflects the outstanding novelty of
[
12
]
is that for any given configuration
\mathbf{x}_{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{dN}\setminus\triangle_{N}
, bounded probability density
\mu
and bounded Lipschitz vector field
u
there holds the inequality

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x\neq y}\left(u(x)-u(y)\right)\nabla V(x-y)\left(\mu_{N}-\mu\right)^{\otimes 2}dxdy\right|\leq C\int_{x\neq y}V(x-y)\left(\mu_{N}-\mu\right)^{\otimes 2}dxdy+o_{N}(1),

(2.7)

where
C>0
is some harmless constant.
We will refer to (
2.7
) as the commutator estimate, see
[
24
]
.
The representation (
2.4
) is inapplicable to the renormalized energy
\mathcal{E}_{N}(t)
due to the removal of the diagonal. A key observation of
[
12
]
is that the identity (
2.4
) admits a renormalized version. More precisely, denote by
\delta^{(\eta)}_{x}
the uniform measure of mass
1
on
\partial B(x,\eta)
. Given
\vec{\eta}=\left(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{N}\right)
set
\mu_{N}^{(\vec{\eta})}\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\delta_{x_{i}}^{(\eta_{i})}
. Then, letting
r_{i}\coloneqq\frac{1}{4}\underset{i\neq j}{\min}{\left|x_{i}-x_{j}\right|}
and
\eta_{i}\leq r_{i}
, Serfaty proves the identity

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N}(\mu,\mu_{N})=\frac{1}{c_{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla V\star(\mu^{(\eta)}_{N}-\mu)\right|^{2}(x)dx-\frac{c_{d}}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}V(\eta_{i})\right)+\mathrm{negligible\ terms}.

(2.8)

The weak strong stability principle with source term
. Coming back to the more general scenario at stake, where a source term
h[\mu]
is included, it can be shown by direct calculation
that in this case
\mathcal{E}(t)
verifies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(t)\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu(t,x)-\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu(t,y)\right)\nabla V(x-y)\left(\mu_{1}(t,\cdot)-\mu_{2}(t,\cdot)\right)^{\otimes 2}dxdy

(2.9)

\displaystyle+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x-y)\left(h\left[\mu_{1}\right](t,x)-h\left[\mu_{2}\right](t,x)\right)\left(\mu_{1}(t,y)-\mu_{2}(t,y)\right)dxdy\coloneqq\mathcal{D}^{1}(t)+\mathcal{D}^{2}(t).

The first term
\mathcal{D}^{1}
in the right hand side of (
2.9
) can be handled precisely as before. Thus, the inclusion of the source term is manifested through the second term
\mathcal{D}^{2}
. In this context, our key observation is that
h
is a Lipschitz operator on
\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, namely we will show (see Corollary
3.6
) an inequality of the form

\left\|h[\mu_{1}]-h[\mu_{2}]\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\left\|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(2.10)

Equipped with Inequality (
2.10
) a bound on
\mathcal{D}^{2}(t)
by means of
\mathcal{E}(t)
can be obtained. Indeed, appealing to Fourier we see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{2}(t)

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V\star\left(h\left[\mu_{1}\right]-h\left[\mu_{2}\right]\right)(t,x)\left(\mu_{1}(t,x)-\mu_{2}(t,x)\right)dx

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\widehat{\left(h\left[\mu_{1}\right]-h\left[\mu_{2}\right]\right)}(t,x)}{\left|x\right|}\frac{\widehat{\left(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right)}(t,x)}{\left|x\right|}dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|h[\mu_{1}(t,\cdot)]-h[\mu_{2}(t,\cdot)]\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\|\mu_{1}(t,\cdot)-\mu_{2}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

by Cauchy-Schwarz. This combined together with (
2.10
) shows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{2}(t)\lesssim\left\|\mu_{1}(t,\cdot)-\mu_{2}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=\mathcal{E}(t).

which in turn closes the Grönwall estimate.

Renormalization with source term
. We will show by a direct calculation (see Theorem
5.1
) the inequality

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}_{N}(t)\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{x\neq y}\left(\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu(t,x)-\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu(t,y)\right)\nabla V(x-y)\left(\mu_{N}(t,\cdot)-\mu(t,\cdot)\right)^{\otimes 2}dxdy

\displaystyle+2\int_{x\neq y}V(x-y)\left(h\left[\mu_{N}(t,\cdot)\right](x)-h\left[\mu(t,\cdot)\right](x)\right)\left(\mu_{N}(t,y)-\mu(t,y)\right)dxdy

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{N}^{1}(t)+\mathcal{D}_{N}^{2}(t).

(2.11)

The first term
\mathcal{D}_{N}^{1}
in the right hand side of Inequality (
2.11
) can be handled precisely via the commutator estimate (
2.7
) in the same manner outlined before. Thus, the main challenge is to renormalize the argument outlined in the previous paragraph in order to control
\mathcal{D}_{N}^{2}(t)
by means of
\mathcal{E}_{N}(t)
. A first possibility is to attempt proving an identity in the spirit of (
2.8
) for

\int_{x\neq y}V(x-y)(h[\mu_{N}]-h[\mu])(x)(h[\mu_{N}]-h[\mu])(y)dxdy.

However, it is not clear if the fine cancellations which produce this formula can be extended in this manner, since the operation
\mu\mapsto h[\mu]
is non linear. We therefore pursue a different path inspired by the work of Bresch-Jabin-Wang
[
8
]
, which avoids making use of this representation. The merit of this approach is that it does not necessitate any algebraic identities, but only inequalities. With this approach we will prove a functional inequality of the form

\displaystyle\int_{x\neq y}\!\!\!\!\!\!V(x-y)\left(\mu_{N}-\mu\right)(x)\left(h\left[\mu_{N}\right]-h\left[\mu\right]\right)(y)dxdy\leq C\int_{x\neq y}\!\!\!\!\!\!V(x-y)\left(\mu_{N}-\mu\right)^{\otimes 2}dxdy+o_{N}(1),

(2.12)

for some constant
C>0
.

3
The well posedness of the mean field equation

3.1
Existence

We start by recalling existence and uniqueness for the limit PDE as
established in
[
14
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

(
[
14
, Theorem 2]
)
Assume that
\nabla V
is Lipschitz, let
S\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be odd and let the initial data
\mu_{0}
satisfy (
H2
). Then, there exist a unique solution
\mu\in C([0,T];\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

of (
2.2
) in the sense of Definition
2.5
and Remark
2.7
. Moreover,
\mu(t,\cdot)
is compactly supported for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Remark 3.2
.

If one further assumes
V\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
S\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mu_{0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in Proposition
3.1
, then it is possible to show that the solution
\mu
provided by Proposition
3.1
is a smooth compactly supported function. Consequently, subject to these assumption the solution is classical.

The main theorem of this section concerns the existence of a solution to (
2.2
) and is based on a stability argument using suitably mollified problems. An important component of the proof is the propagation of Sobolev norms. Sobolev regularity is important
in order to be able to use the commutator estimate (
2.7
) against Lipschitz vector fields. In this context, we will need the following near-boundedness of the Calderon-Zygmund operator on
L^{\infty}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

(
[
4
, Proposition 7.7]
)

Let
s>0
and let
a\in[1,\infty)
and
b\in[1,\infty]
. Then, there is some
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{2}V\star\mu\right\|_{\infty}\leq C\left(\mathrm{min}\left(\left\|\nabla V\star\mu\right\|_{b},\left\|\mu\right\|_{a}\right)+\left\|\mu\right\|_{\infty}(1+\left|\log\left\|\mu\right\|_{\infty}\right|\right)\log\left(\max\left(e,\left\|\mu\right\|_{\infty}\right)+\left\|\mu\right\|_{C^{0,s}}\right).

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume that
S
and
V
are as in (
H1
) and let the initial data
\mu_{0}
satisfy (
H2
). Then, there exist a solution in the sense of Definition
2.5
to (
2.2
)
with initial data
\mu_{0}
. Moreover, this solution satisfies
\mu\in C\left([0,T];L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
\mu\in L^{\infty}\left([0,T];W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)
for all
1\leq p<\infty
.
Furthermore,
\mu(t,\cdot)
is compactly supported and its support satisfies

\mathrm{supp}(\mu(t,\cdot))\subset B(0,\overline{R})\,,\mbox{ for all }t\in[0,T],

where
\overline{R}=\overline{R}(\left\|\mu_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}},R,T,\left\|S\right\|_{L^{\infty}})
.

Proof
.
Step 1. Propagation of
L^{p}
norms
. Let
\chi_{\varepsilon}
be a standard mollifier and let
V_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\chi_{\varepsilon}\star V
and
\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu]\coloneqq\mathbb{J}\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\star\mu
.
Let
\mu_{\varepsilon}
be the solution
of the mollified equation

\partial_{t}\mu_{\varepsilon}-\mathrm{div}\left(\mu_{\varepsilon}\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}]\right)=h\left[\mu_{\varepsilon}\right],\ \mu_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=\chi_{\varepsilon}\star\mu_{0}.

(3.1)

This solution is classical and is ensured via Proposition
3.1
and Remark
3.2
. We wish to quantify the growth, size of support and regularity of this solution. We compute the time derivative
of
\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}^{p}
.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{p-1}(t,x)\partial_{t}\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,x)dx

\displaystyle=p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{p-1}(t,x)\mathrm{div}\left(\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](t,x)\right)dx+p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{p-1}(t,x)h\left[\mu_{\varepsilon}\right](t,x)dx\coloneqq J_{1}+J_{2}.

To bound
J_{1}
, note that integration by parts yields

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{p-1}(t,x)\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](t,x)dx+p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{p}(t,x)\mathrm{div}\left(\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](t,x)\right)dx

\displaystyle=(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{p}(t,x)\mathrm{div}\left(\mathbb{J}\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\star\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\right)dx.

(3.2)

Note that
\mathrm{div}(\mathbb{J}\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\star\mu_{\varepsilon})=0
if
\mathbb{J}

is antisymmetric and
\mathrm{div}(\mathbb{J}\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\star\mu_{\varepsilon})=-\chi_{\varepsilon}\star\mu_{\varepsilon}\leq 0
if
\mathbb{J}
is the identity. Thus, (
3.2
) proves that
J_{1}\leq 0
. Furthermore we have

\left|J_{2}\right|\leq p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{p}(t,x)\left|\mathbf{S}[\mu_{\varepsilon}]\right|(t,x)dx\leq p\left\|\mathbf{S}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}^{p}\leq p\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}^{p}.

Therefore, we conclude
that

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}^{p}\leq p\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}^{p},

which entails

\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}^{p}\leq e^{p\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}t}\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(0,\cdot)\right\|_{p}^{p}=e^{p\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}t}\left\|\chi_{\varepsilon}\star\mu_{0}\right\|_{p}^{p}\leq e^{p\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}t}\left\|\mu_{0}\right\|_{p}^{p},

or

\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}\leq e^{\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}t}\left\|\mu_{0}\right\|_{p},\ 1\leq p<\infty.

Since according to Proposition
3.1

\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)
is compactly
supported we can pass to the limit as
p\rightarrow\infty
in the
last inequality in order to deduce

\displaystyle\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}\leq e^{\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}t}\left\|\mu_{0}\right\|_{p}\ 1\leq p\leq\infty.

(3.3)

Step 2. Propagation of support
. Denote by
R_{\varepsilon}(t)
the size of the support of
\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)
, i.e.
R_{\varepsilon}(t)\coloneqq\left|\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot))\right|
. For each
\eta>0
consider the function

\displaystyle\varphi_{\eta}(t,x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\eta+\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,x)}.

Multiplying (
3.1
) by
\varphi_{\eta}
and integrating on
[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
we arrive at the equation

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\varphi_{\eta}(t,x)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}(0,x)\varphi_{\eta}(0,x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{\tau}\varphi_{\eta}(\tau,x)\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)dxd\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\eta}(\tau,x)\mathrm{div}(\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,x))dxd\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\eta}(\tau,x)h[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,x)dxd\tau.

(3.4)

We proceed by manipulating the inner integrals in the right-hand side of (
3.4
).

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{\tau}\varphi_{\eta}(\tau,x)\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)dx=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)\partial_{\tau}\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)}{(\eta+\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x))^{2}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)\mathrm{div}(\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,x))}{(\eta+\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x))^{2}}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)h[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,x)}{(\eta+\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x))^{2}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,x)}{(\eta+\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x))^{2}}dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\tau,x)\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,x))}{(\eta+\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x))^{2}}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)h[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,x)}{(\eta+\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x))^{2}}dx.

(3.5)

Integrating by parts the first integral in the right-hand side of (
3.5
) we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,x)}{(\eta+\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x))^{2}}dx=

\displaystyle\,-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,x)\nabla\left(\frac{1}{\eta+\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)}\right)\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}])(\tau,x)\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)}{\eta+\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)}dx

\displaystyle\,+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,x)\nabla\log\left(\eta+\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)\right)dx.

(3.6)

As for the second integral in (
3.4
), we observe the identity

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\eta}(\tau,x)\mathrm{div}(\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,x))dx=

\displaystyle\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,x)\nabla\log\left(\eta+\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)\right)dx

\displaystyle\,+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,x))\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)}{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)+\eta}dx.

(3.7)

The combination of equations (
3.5
), (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{\tau}\varphi_{\eta}(\tau,x)\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\eta}(\tau,x)\mathrm{div}(\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,x))dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\eta}(\tau,x)h[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,x)dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\tau,x)\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,x))}{(\eta+\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x))^{2}}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)h[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,x)}{(\eta+\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x))^{2}}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\eta}(\tau,x)h[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,x)dx

\displaystyle\coloneqq I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

Using that
\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}])=-\chi_{\varepsilon}\star\mu_{\varepsilon}
if
\mathbb{J}=\mathrm{Id}
and
\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}])=0
if
\mathbb{J}
is antisymmetric we see that

\displaystyle\left|I_{1}\right|\leq\left\|\chi_{\varepsilon}\star\mu_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\tau,x)\varphi_{\eta}^{2}(\tau,x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\underset{s\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(s,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\tau,x)\varphi_{\eta}^{2}(\tau,x)dx

\displaystyle\leq e^{T\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}}\left\|\mu_{0}\right\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\tau,x)\varphi_{\eta}^{2}(\tau,x),

(3.8)

where in the last inequality we used (
3.3
).
Furthermore, we have the inequalities

\displaystyle\left|I_{2}\right|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\tau,x)\left|S\star\mu_{\varepsilon}\right|(\tau,x)}{(\eta+\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x))^{2}}dx\leq\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\tau,x)\varphi_{\eta}^{2}(\tau,x)dx

(3.9)

and

\displaystyle\left|I_{3}\right|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\eta}(\tau,x)\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)\left|S\star\mu_{\varepsilon}\right|(\tau,x)dx\leq\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)\varphi_{\eta}(\tau,x)dx.

(3.10)

Putting
\overline{M}=\max\left\{2\left\|S\right\|_{\infty},e^{\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}T}\left\|\mu_{0}\right\|_{\infty}\right\}
and substituting (
3.8
), (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) in (
3.4
)
we find that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\varphi_{\eta}(t,x)\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu_{\varepsilon}(0,x)\varphi_{\eta}(0,x)

\displaystyle+2\overline{M}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\eta}^{2}(\tau,x)\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\tau,x)dxd\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{\eta}(\tau,x)\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)dxd\tau\right).

Since
\varphi_{\eta}(t,x)\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\underset{\eta\rightarrow 0}{\rightarrow}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\cdot\right|\leq R_{\varepsilon}(t)}(x)
, passing to the limit in the last inequality as
\eta\rightarrow 0
we obtain

\displaystyle R_{\varepsilon}^{d}(t)\leq R_{\varepsilon}^{d}(0)+2\overline{M}\int_{0}^{t}R_{\varepsilon}^{d}(\tau)d\tau.

Hence, by Grönwall’s inequality we deduce that

R_{\varepsilon}(t)\leq R_{\varepsilon}(0)e^{\frac{2\overline{M}t}{d}}\leq(R+\varepsilon)e^{\frac{2\overline{M}T}{d}}.

To conclude, we have proved

\displaystyle R_{\varepsilon}(t)\leq\overline{R},\ t\in[0,T]

(3.11)

for some
\overline{R}=\overline{R}(\left\|\mu_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}},R,T,\left\|S\right\|_{L^{\infty}})
.

Step 3. Propagation of Sobolev norms
. Expanding the divergence in (
3.1
), we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mu_{\varepsilon}-\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{J}\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\star\mu_{\varepsilon}-\mu_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{div}(\mathbb{J}\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\star\mu_{\varepsilon})=h\left[\mu_{\varepsilon}\right].

Taking its
j
-th derivative, we obtain

\partial_{t}\partial_{x_{j}}\mu_{\varepsilon}-\nabla\partial_{x_{j}}\mu_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{J}\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\star\mu_{\varepsilon}-\partial_{x_{j}}(\mathbb{J}\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\star\mu_{\varepsilon})\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}-\partial_{x_{j}}\mu_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{div}(\mathbb{J}\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\star\mu_{\varepsilon})-\mu_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{div}(\mathbb{J}\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\star\partial_{x_{j}}\mu_{\varepsilon})=R_{j,{\varepsilon}}(t,x),

(3.12)

with
R_{j,{\varepsilon}}(t,x)\coloneqq\partial_{x_{j}}h\left[\mu_{\varepsilon}\right]
.
Set
v_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\coloneqq\mathbb{J}\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\star\mu_{\varepsilon}
and
\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\coloneqq\partial_{x_{j}}\mu_{\varepsilon}
,
so that the equation (
3.12
) reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\omega_{j,{\varepsilon}}-\nabla\omega_{j,{\varepsilon}}v_{\varepsilon}-\underset{k}{\sum}\partial_{x_{j}}v^{k}_{\varepsilon}\omega_{k,\varepsilon}-\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\mathrm{div}(v_{\varepsilon})-\mu_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{div}(\partial_{x_{j}}v_{\varepsilon})=R_{j,{\varepsilon}}(t,x).

We compute the time derivative of
\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\left\|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}^{p}
as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\underset{j}{\sum}\left\|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}^{p}=

\displaystyle p\underset{j}{\sum}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p-1}(t,x)\partial_{t}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|(t,x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\underset{j}{\sum}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p-1}(t,x)\mathrm{sgn}(\omega_{j,\varepsilon}(t,x))

\displaystyle\times\left(\nabla\omega_{j,\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon}+\underset{k}{\sum}(\partial_{x_{j}}v_{\varepsilon}^{k})\omega_{k,\varepsilon}+\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\mathrm{div}(v_{\varepsilon})+\mu_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{div}(\partial_{x_{j}}v_{\varepsilon})\right)(t,x)dx

\displaystyle+p\underset{j}{\sum}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p-1}(t,x)\mathrm{sgn}(\omega_{j,\varepsilon}(t,x))R_{j,\varepsilon}(t,x)dx.

The right-hand side of the last equation is bounded by

\displaystyle\underset{j}{\sum}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p}(t,x)\left|\mathrm{div}(v_{\varepsilon})\right|(t,x)dx+p\underset{j,k}{\sum}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p-1}(t,x)\left|\partial_{x_{j}}v_{\varepsilon}^{k}\right|(t,x)\left|\omega_{k,\varepsilon}\right|(t,x)dx

\displaystyle+p\underset{j}{\sum}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p}(t,x)\left|\mathrm{div}(v_{\varepsilon})\right|(t,x)dx+p\underset{j}{\sum}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p-1}(t,x)\left|\mu_{\varepsilon}\partial_{x_{j}}\mathrm{div}(v_{\varepsilon})\right|(t,x)

\displaystyle+p\underset{j}{\sum}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p-1}(t,x)\left|R_{j,\varepsilon}\right|(t,x)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(p+1)\underset{j}{\sum}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p}\left|\mathrm{div}(v_{\varepsilon})\right|(t,x)dx+p\underset{j,k}{\sum}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p-1}(t,x)\left|\partial_{x_{j}}v_{\varepsilon}^{k}\right|(t,x)\left|\omega_{k,\varepsilon}\right|(t,x)dx

\displaystyle+pC(T,\left\|S\right\|_{\infty},\left\|\mu_{0}\right\|_{\infty})\underset{j}{\sum}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p}(t,x)dx+p\underset{j}{\sum}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p-1}(t,x)\left|R_{j,\varepsilon}\right|(t,x)dx.

In the last inequality we invoked (
3.3
), according to which there holds the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|\mathrm{div}(v_{\varepsilon})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\mathrm{div}(\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu_{\varepsilon})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\leq C

and thus

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p}(t,x)\left|\mathrm{div}(v_{\varepsilon})\right|(t,x)dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p}(t,x)dx,

(3.13)

where
C=C\left(\left\|\mu_{0}\right\|_{\infty},\left\|S\right\|_{\infty},T\right)
.
Invoking Proposition
3.3
, (
3.3
) and Young’s inequality for products yields

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p-1}(t,x)\left|\partial_{x_{j}}v_{\varepsilon}^{k}\right|(t,x)\left|\omega_{k,\varepsilon}\right|(t,x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\partial_{x_{j}}v_{\varepsilon}^{k}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p-1}(t,x)\left|\omega_{k,\varepsilon}\right|(t,x)dx

\displaystyle\leq C\left(d,\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\right)\log\left(\max\left(e,\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\right)+\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{C^{0,s}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times p\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}(t,x)\right|^{p}dx+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{k,\varepsilon}(t,x)\right|^{p}dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\log\left(C+\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{C^{0,s}}^{p}\right)\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\left\|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}^{p},

where

. Choosing

\displaystyle\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{C^{0,s}}^{p}\lesssim\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\leq C\left(T,\left\|S\right\|_{\infty},\left\|\mu_{0}\right\|_{\infty},d,p\right)\left(1+\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\left\|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}^{p}\right),

hence we have proved

p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p-1}(t,x)\left|\partial_{x_{j}}v_{\varepsilon}^{k}\right|(t,x)\left|\omega_{k,\varepsilon}\right|(t,x)dx\leq C\log\left(C+C\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\left\|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}^{p}\right)\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\left\|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}^{p}.

(3.14)

Thanks to Hölder’s inequality, it holds that

\displaystyle p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p-1}(t,x)\left|R_{j,\varepsilon}\right|(t,x)dx

\displaystyle=p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p-1}(t,x)\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|(t,x)\left|\mathbf{S}[\mu_{\varepsilon}]\right|(t,x)dx+p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p-1}(t,x)\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\left|\partial_{x_{j}}\mathbf{S}[\mu_{\varepsilon}]\right|(t,x)dx

\displaystyle\leq p\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p}(t,x)dx+p\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p}(t,x)dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\mu_{\varepsilon}\partial_{x_{j}}S\star\mu_{\varepsilon}\right|^{p}(t,x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq p\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p}(t,x)dx+C(p,\left\|\mu_{0}\right\|_{\infty},T,\left\|S\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}})\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}\right|^{p}(t,x)dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}^{p}+1\right),

(3.15)

where
C=C\left(T,\left\|S\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}},\left\|\mu_{0}\right\|_{\infty},p\right)
.
Put

\displaystyle\Omega(t)\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\|\omega_{j,\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}^{p}.

Gathering (
3.13
), (
3.14
)
and (
3.15
), we arrive at the inequality

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Omega(t)\leq C\log\left(1+\Omega(t)\right)\left(1+\Omega(t)\right),\qquad\mbox{ or equivalently }\qquad\frac{d}{dt}\log\log\left(1+\Omega(t)\right)\leq C,

for a suitable constant
C=C\left(\left\|\mu_{0}\right\|_{\infty},\left\|S\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}},T,d,p\right)
.
Solving the above inequality yields

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}^{p}=\Omega(t)\leq C\left(1+\Omega(0)\right)^{e^{Ct}}\leq C\left(1+\left\|\nabla\mu_{0}\right\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{e^{Ct}},\ 1\leq p<\infty.

(3.16)

Step 4. Compactness and extraction of a solution
. We start by showing that
\mu_{\varepsilon}\in C([0,T];L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
uniformly in
\varepsilon
. By (

) we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)-\mu_{\varepsilon}(s,\cdot)\right\|_{p}\leq\left|t-s\right|\underset{\tau\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left(\left\|\mathrm{div}(\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot)\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,\cdot))\right\|_{p}+\left\|h[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{p}\right).

We expand

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}(\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot)\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,\cdot))=\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot)\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,\cdot)+\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot)\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,\cdot)).

In what follows
C=C\left(\left\|\mu_{0}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}},\left\|S\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}},T,d,p\right)
is a constant which may change from line to line. According to (
3.16
) there holds the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{p}\leq C

while according to inequality (
3.3
) we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\leq C.

(3.17)

Thus, we get the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot)\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{p}\leq\left\|\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{p}\left\|\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\leq C.

From the same consideration

\displaystyle\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot))\right\|_{p}\leq C

and

\displaystyle\left\|\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,\cdot))\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\chi_{\varepsilon}\star\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\leq C.

Hence

\displaystyle\underset{\tau\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\mathrm{div}(\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot)\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,\cdot))\right\|_{p}\leq C.

(3.18)

In addition, notice the bound

\displaystyle\underset{\tau\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|h[\mu_{\varepsilon}](\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{p}\leq\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}\underset{\tau\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{p}\leq C.

(3.19)

Therefore, from (
3.18
) and (
3.19
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)-\mu_{\varepsilon}(s,\cdot)\right\|_{p}\leq C\left|t-s\right|.

(3.20)

By the theorem of Arzela-Ascoli and (
3.20
) we may extract a
subsequence
\mu_{\varepsilon_{m}}
and some

\mu\in C([0,T];L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

such that

\displaystyle\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon_{m}}(t,\cdot)-\mu(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}\underset{m\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0.

By (

),
\mu(t,\cdot)
has compact support and
\mathrm{supp}(\mu(t,\cdot))\subset\overline{R}
and by (
3.16
) and the Banach-Alaoglu theorem
\mu\in L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.We proceed by showing that
\mu
is a weak solution to the equation (
2.2
). To achieve this it suffices to check that

\displaystyle\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon_{m}}[\mu_{\varepsilon_{m}}](t,\cdot)\mu_{\varepsilon_{m}}(t,\cdot)-\mathbf{A}[\mu](t,\cdot)\mu(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}\underset{m\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0,\ \underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|h[\mu_{\varepsilon_{m}}](t,\cdot)-h[\mu](t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}\underset{m\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0.

(3.21)

By the triangle inequality

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon_{m}}[\mu_{\varepsilon_{m}}](t,\cdot)\mu_{\varepsilon_{m}}(t,\cdot)-\mathbf{A}[\mu](t,\cdot)\mu(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon_{m}}[\mu_{\varepsilon_{m}}](t,\cdot)(\mu_{\varepsilon_{m}}(t,\cdot)-\mu(t,\cdot))\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle+\left\|\mu(t,\cdot)(\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon_{m}}[\mu_{\varepsilon_{m}}](t,\cdot)-\mathbf{A}[\mu](t,\cdot))\right\|_{1}\coloneqq A_{1}+A_{2}.

By (
3.17
) we see that

\displaystyle A_{1}\leq C\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon_{m}}(t,\cdot)-\mu(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}.

(3.22)

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle A_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\mu(t,\cdot)(\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon_{m}}[\mu_{\varepsilon_{m}}](t,\cdot)-\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon_{m}}[\mu](t,\cdot))\right\|_{1}+\left\|\mu(t,\cdot)(\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon_{m}}[\mu](t,\cdot)-\mathbf{A}[\mu](t,\cdot))\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\left\|\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon_{m}}[\mu_{{}_{\varepsilon_{m}}}](t,\cdot)-\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon_{m}}[\mu](t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}+\left\|\mathbf{A}_{\varepsilon_{m}}[\mu](t,\cdot)-\mathbf{A}[\mu](t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\left\|\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star(\mu_{\varepsilon_{m}}-\mu)(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}+\left\|\chi_{\varepsilon_{m}}\star\mathbf{A}[\mu](t,\cdot)-\mathbf{A}[\mu](t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}\right).

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star(\mu_{\varepsilon_{m}}-\mu)(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}\leq C\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon_{m}}(t,\cdot)-\mu(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon_{m}}(t,\cdot)-\mu(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}.

)

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{\varepsilon_{m}}\star\mathbf{A}[\mu](t,\cdot)-\mathbf{A}[\mu](t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\chi_{\varepsilon_{m}}\star\mu(t,\cdot)-\mu(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\chi_{\varepsilon_{m}}\star\mu(t,\cdot)-\mu(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}\leq C\varepsilon_{m}\left\|\nabla\mu(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}\leq C\varepsilon_{m},

from which we get

\displaystyle A_{2}\leq C\left(\left\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)-\mu(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}+\varepsilon_{m}\right).

(3.23)

Inequalities (

3.23

In addition, we estimate

\displaystyle\left\|h[\mu_{\varepsilon_{m}}](t,\cdot)-h[\mu](t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|(\mu_{\varepsilon_{m}}-\mu)(t,\cdot)S\star\mu_{\varepsilon_{m}}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}+\left\|\mu(t,\cdot)(S\star\mu_{\varepsilon_{m}}-S\star\mu)(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}

which establishes the second convergence in (

\square

3.2
Uniqueness

To prove uniqueness, we study the evolution of the interaction energy

\mathcal{E}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right)(t,x)(V\star\left(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right))(t,x)dx=\left\|\mu_{1}(t,\cdot)-\mu_{2}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

We start by proving that
h[\mu]
is Lipschitz with respect to the
\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
norm. In fact we have the following more general Lemma, from which the Lipschitz continuity of
h[\mu]
with respect to
\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
will follow. In the next lemma, we show some estimates involving a given kernel
W
. The next lemma will eventually be used for either the Coulomb interaction
V
or some regularized kernels thereof in the next sections.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
S
be as in (
H1
). Suppose further that

\bullet

\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are tempered
distributions such that
\widehat{\mu},\widehat{\nu}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

\bullet

W\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|W\right|(x)\left|\widehat{\mu-\nu}\right|^{2}(x)dx<\infty.

\bullet
There is some
c>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
it holds that
\left|\widehat{S}\right|\star\left|W\right|(x)\leq c\left|W\right|(x).

Then, it holds that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|W\right|(x)\left|\widehat{h\left[\mu\right]-h\left[\nu\right]}\right|^{2}(x)dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|W\right|(x)\left|\widehat{\mu-\nu}\right|^{2}(x)dx

where
C=C\left(c,\left\|\widehat{S}\right\|_{1},\left\|\widehat{\mu}\right\|_{\infty},\left\|\widehat{\nu}\right\|_{\infty}\right).

Proof
. We have

\displaystyle I:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|W\right|(x)\left|\widehat{h\left[\mu\right]-h\left[\nu\right]}\right|^{2}(x)dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|W\right|(x)(\widehat{\mu S\star\mu-\nu S\star\nu})(x)\left(\widehat{h\left[\mu\right]-h\left[\nu\right]}\right)(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|W\right|(x)(\widehat{(\mu-\nu)(S\star\nu)})(x)\left(\widehat{h\left[\mu\right]-h\left[\nu\right]}\right)(x)dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|W\right|(x)(\widehat{\mu(S\star\mu-S\star\nu)})(x)\left(\widehat{h\left[\mu\right]-h\left[\nu\right]}\right)(x)dx

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}.

(3.24)

The first integral in the right-hand side of (
3.24
) is controlled as follows

\displaystyle I_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|W\right|(x)

\displaystyle((\widehat{\mu-\nu)(S\star\nu)})(x)\left(\widehat{h\left[\mu\right]-h\left[\nu\right]}\right)(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|W\right|(x)\widehat{(\mu-\nu})\star(\widehat{S}\widehat{\nu})(x)\left(\widehat{h\left[\mu\right]-h\left[\nu\right]}\right)(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|W\right|(x)\widehat{(\mu-\nu})(y)(\widehat{S}\widehat{\nu})(x-y)\left(\widehat{h\left[\mu\right]-h\left[\nu\right]}\right)(x)dydx

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\widehat{\nu}\right\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|W\right|(x)\left|\widehat{\mu-\nu}\right|(y)\left|\widehat{S}\right|(x-y)\left|\widehat{h\left[\mu\right]-h\left[\nu\right]}\right|(x)dydx.

(3.25)

Thanks to the assumption
\left|\widehat{S}\right|\star\left|W\right|(x)\leq c\left|W\right|(x)
and since
\widehat{S}
is even we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\widehat{\mu-\nu}\right|^{2}(y)\left|\widehat{S}\right|(x-y)\left|W\right|(x)dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\widehat{\mu-\nu}\right|^{2}(y)\left|\widehat{S}\right|\star\left|W\right|(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\widehat{\mu-\nu}\right|^{2}(y)\left|W\right|(y)dy.

Furthermore,
\widehat{S}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

shows that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\widehat{h\left[\mu\right]-h\left[\nu\right]}\right|^{2}(x)\left|\widehat{S}\right|(x-y)\left|W\right|(x)dxdy=\left\|\widehat{S}\right\|_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\widehat{h\left[\mu\right]-h\left[\nu\right]}\right|^{2}(x)\left|W\right|(x)dx.

Collecting the last two estimates and applying Cauchy-Schwarz to (
3.25
), we conclude that

I_{1}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\widehat{\mu-\nu}\right|^{2}(y)\left|W\right|(y)dy\right)^{1/2}I^{1/2}

where
C=C\left(c,\left\|\widehat{S}\right\|_{1},\left\|\widehat{\nu}\right\|_{\infty}\right).
As for the second integral in (
3.24
)

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|W\right|(x)((\widehat{S}\widehat{(\mu-\nu))}\star\widehat{\mu})(x)\left(\widehat{h\left[\mu\right]-h\left[\nu\right]}\right)(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|W\right|(x)\widehat{S}(x-y)\widehat{(\mu-\nu)}(x-y)\widehat{\mu}(y)\left(\widehat{h\left[\mu\right]-h\left[\nu\right]}\right)(x)dxdy

and by the same considerations as before we deduce that

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq C\left(c,\left\|\widehat{S}\right\|_{1},\left\|\widehat{\mu}\right\|_{\infty}\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\widehat{\mu-\nu}\right|^{2}(y)\left|W\right|(y)dy\right)^{1/2}I^{\frac{1}{2}}

from which we deduce that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|W\right|(x)\left|\widehat{h\left[\mu\right]-h\left[\nu\right]}\right|^{2}(x)dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|W\right|(x)\left|\widehat{\mu-\nu}\right|^{2}(x)dx

where
C=C\left(c,\left\|\widehat{S}\right\|_{1},\left\|\widehat{\mu}\right\|_{\infty},\left\|\widehat{\nu}\right\|_{\infty}\right)
.

\square

As a corollary, we obtain continuity of
h[\mu]
with respect to the
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
norm.

Corollary 3.6
.

Suppose that
\mu,\nu\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and that
S
is as in (
H1
). Then, we have

\left\|h[\mu]-h[\nu]\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\left\|\mu-\nu\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

where

C=C\left(\left\|(1+\left|\cdot\right|^{2})\widehat{S}\right\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}},\left\|\widehat{\mu}\right\|_{\infty},\left\|\widehat{\nu}\right\|_{\infty}\right)
.

Proof
. We take
W=\widehat{V}=\frac{1}{\left|x\right|^{2}}
in Lemma
3.5
. We need to check the condition

\left|\widehat{S}\right|\star W(x)\leq cW(x).

Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\left|x\right|^{2}\left(\left|\widehat{S}\right|\star\frac{1}{\left|\cdot\right|^{2}}\right)(x)

\displaystyle=\left|x\right|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|\widehat{S}\right|(y)}{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}dy\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|\widehat{S}\right|(y)\left|x-y\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}dy+2\left\|(\left|\cdot\right|^{2}\left|\widehat{S}\right|)\star\frac{1}{\left|\cdot\right|^{2}}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=2\left\|\widehat{S}\right\|_{1}+2\left\|(\left|\cdot\right|^{2}\left|\widehat{S}\right|)\star\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\leq 1}}{\left|\cdot\right|^{2}}\right\|_{\infty}+2\left\|(\left|\cdot\right|^{2}\left|\widehat{S}\right|)\star\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\geq 1}}{\left|\cdot\right|^{2}}\right\|_{\infty}.

By Young’s inequality, for any
q<\frac{d}{2}
we have the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|(\left|\cdot\right|^{2}\left|\widehat{S}\right|)\star\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\leq 1}}{\left|\cdot\right|^{2}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\leq 1}}{\left|\cdot\right|^{2}}\right\|_{q}\left\|\left|\cdot\right|^{2}\widehat{S}\right\|_{q^{\prime}}\lesssim_{d,q}\left\|\left|\cdot\right|^{2}\widehat{S}\right\|_{q^{\prime}}\leq\left\|(1+\left|\cdot\right|^{2})\widehat{S}\right\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}

and for any
p>\frac{d}{2}
we have the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|(\left|\cdot\right|^{2}\left|\widehat{S}\right|)\star\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\geq 1}}{\left|\cdot\right|^{2}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\geq 1}}{\left|\cdot\right|^{2}}\right\|_{p}\left\|\left|\cdot\right|^{2}\widehat{S}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\lesssim_{d,q}\left\|\left|\cdot\right|^{2}\widehat{S}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq\left\|(1+\left|\cdot\right|^{2})\widehat{S}\right\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}.

Therefore, one finds that

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{S}\right|\star\frac{1}{\left|\cdot\right|^{2}}\leq\frac{c\left(\left\|(1+\left|\cdot\right|^{2})\widehat{S}\right\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}\right)}{\left|x\right|^{2}}.

The announced result follows now by Lemma
3.5
.

\square

Theorem 3.7
.

Let hypothesis (
H1
) hold. Let
\mu^{1}(t,\cdot)
and
\mu^{2}(t,\cdot)
be weak solutions to (

) with initial data

In particular, uniqueness of weak solutions to (

Proof

\mathrm{div}(\mathbb{J}\nabla V)=0

\displaystyle+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(h\left[\mu^{1}\right]-h\left[\mu^{2}\right]\right)(t,x)V\star\left(\mu^{1}-\mu^{2}\right)(t,x)dx\coloneqq\mathcal{D}^{1}(t)+\mathcal{D}^{2}(t).

Step 1
.
Estimate on

\mathcal{D}^{1}(t)

as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{1}(t)=

\displaystyle-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu^{1}\left(\mu^{1}-\mu^{2}\right)(t,x)\nabla V\star\left(\mu^{1}-\mu^{2}\right)(t,x)dx

\displaystyle-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu^{2}\left(\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star(\mu^{1}-\mu^{2})\right)(t,x)\nabla V\star\left(\mu^{1}-\mu^{2}\right)(t,x)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu^{1}\left(\mu^{1}-\mu^{2}\right)(t,x)\nabla V\star\left(\mu^{1}-\mu^{2}\right)(t,x)dx,

where in the last inequality we used

.
Therefore, we deduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu^{1}\mathrm{div}\left(\nabla V\star\left(\mu^{1}-\mu^{2}\right)\right)(t,x)\nabla V\star\left(\mu^{1}-\mu^{2}\right)(t,x)dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu^{1}(t,x)\nabla\left(\left|\nabla V\star\left(\mu^{1}-\mu^{2}\right)\right|^{2}\right)(t,x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{div}(\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu^{1})(t,x)\left|\nabla V\star\left(\mu^{1}-\mu^{2}\right)\right|^{2}(t,x)dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\mu^{1}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\mathcal{E}(t)\leq C(\left\|S\right\|_{\infty},\left\|\mu^{1}_{0}\right\|_{\infty},T)\mathcal{E}(t).

Hence, we conclude

\mathcal{D}^{1}(t)\leq C(\left\|S\right\|_{\infty},\left\|\mu^{1}_{0}\right\|_{\infty},T)\mathcal{E}(t).

(3.26)

Step 2
.
Estimate on

\mathcal{D}^{2}(t)
. By Plancherel’s
theorem we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{(h\left[\mu^{1}\right]-h\left[\mu^{2}\right])}(t,x)\widehat{V}(x)\widehat{\left(\mu^{1}-\mu^{2}\right)}(t,x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{C}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|\widehat{h\left[\mu^{1}\right]-h\left[\mu^{2}\right]}\right|}{\left|x\right|}(t,x)\frac{\widehat{\left|\mu^{1}-\mu^{2}\right|}}{\left|x\right|}(t,x)dx

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbf{C}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|\widehat{h\left[\mu^{1}\right]-h\left[\mu^{2}\right]}\right|^{2}}{\left|x\right|^{2}}(t,x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|\widehat{\mu^{1}-\mu^{2}}\right|^{2}}{\left|x\right|^{2}}(t,x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{C}\left\|h\left[\mu^{1}\right](t,\cdot)-h\left[\mu^{2}\right](t,\cdot)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\|\mu^{1}(t,\cdot)-\mu^{2}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(3.27)

By Corollary
3.6
we have

\left\|h\left[\mu^{1}\right](t,\cdot)-h\left[\mu^{2}\right](t,\cdot)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\left\|\mu^{1}(t,\cdot)-\mu^{2}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)},

where
C=C\left(\left\|(1+\left|\cdot\right|^{2})\widehat{S}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{1}}\right)
, so that by (
3.27
) we get

\mathcal{D}^{2}(t)\leq C\mathcal{E}(t).

To conclude, (
3.26
) and (
3.27
) show that

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(t)\leq C\left(\left\|(1+\left|\cdot\right|^{2})\widehat{S}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{1}},\left\|\mu^{1}_{0}\right\|_{\infty},T\right)\mathcal{E}(t),

and therefore
\mathcal{E}(t)\leq e^{Ct}\mathcal{E}(0)
.∎

4
Well posedness for the ODE system

In this section, we explain how to prove the existence of a well defined
flow for the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\dot{x}_{i}^{N}(t)=-\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{j}^{N}(t)\mathbb{J}\nabla V(x_{i}^{N}(t)-x_{j}^{N}(t)),\ x_{i}^{N}(0)=x_{i}^{0,N}\\
\dot{m}_{i}^{N}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{i}^{N}(t)m_{j}^{N}(t)S(x_{i}^{N}(t)-x_{j}^{N}(t)),\ m_{i}^{N}(0)=m_{i}^{0,N}.\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

We will adapt the proof about the well definition of the flow for time independent weights Riesz potentials as in
[
23
, Section 3.2]
to
the time dependent setting. Consider the weighted interaction energy given by

\mathcal{H}_{N}(t)\coloneqq\underset{i\neq j}{\sum}m_{i}(t)m_{j}(t)V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)).

We start by proving short time existence.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let hypothesis (
H1
) hold. Assume that

\forall i\neq j:x_{i}^{0,N}\neq x_{j}^{0,N}

and there is some
M>0
such that for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq i\leq N

one has

0\leq m_{i}^{0,N}\leq M.

Then, there is some
T_{\ast}>0
such that the system (
4.1
) has a unique
C^{1}([0,T_{\ast});\mathbb{R}^{dN}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})

solution.

Proof
.
Let
\Psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
be such that
\Psi(r)=1
for
\left|r\right|\leq 1
and
\Psi(r)=0
for
\left|r\right|\geq 2
. Let

\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the radial function defined by
\psi(x)\coloneqq\Psi(\left|x\right|)
. Let

\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\coloneqq\Psi\left(\frac{\left|x\right|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right),

and

V_{\varepsilon}(x)\coloneqq\left(1-\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\right)V(x)
.
In what follows we omit the superscript
N
from
x_{i,\varepsilon}^{N}
and
m_{i,\varepsilon}^{N}
. Consider the regularized system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}{\dot{x}_{i,\varepsilon}}(t)=-\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{j,\varepsilon}(t)\mathbb{J}\nabla V_{\varepsilon}(x_{i,\varepsilon}(t)-x_{j,\varepsilon}(t)),\ x_{i,\varepsilon}(0)=x_{i}^{0}\\
\dot{m}_{i,\varepsilon}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{i,\varepsilon}(t)m_{j,\varepsilon}(t)S(x_{i,\varepsilon}(t)-x_{j,\varepsilon}(t)),\ m_{i,\varepsilon}(0)=m_{i}^{0}.\end{array}\right.

(4.2)

By
[
2
, Theorem 3]
, system (
4.2
)
has a unique global solution
(x_{i,\varepsilon}(t),m_{i,\varepsilon}(t))

on
[0,\infty)
. We study the evolution of
\left|x_{i,\varepsilon}(t)-x_{i}^{0}\right|
given by

\left|x_{i,\varepsilon}(t)-x_{i}^{0}\right|\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{j,\varepsilon}(s)\cdot\mathbb{J}\nabla V_{\varepsilon}(x_{j,\varepsilon}(s)-x_{i,\varepsilon}(s))\right|\,ds\,.

We can now estimate the
L^{\infty}
bound of the velocity field using that

\nabla V_{\varepsilon}(x)=-2\Psi^{\prime}\left(\frac{\left|x\right|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\frac{x}{\varepsilon^{2}}V(x)+\left(1-\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\right)\nabla V(x).

Actually, writing
k=d-2
notice that

\displaystyle\left|\left(1-\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\right)\nabla V(x)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{k+1}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\left|2\Psi^{\prime}\left(\frac{\left|x\right|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\frac{x}{\varepsilon^{2}}V(x)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{k+1}},

hence

\left|\nabla V_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{k+1}}.

So, owing to Remark
2.4
, we infer the inequality

\left|x_{i,\varepsilon}(t)-x_{i}^{0}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{k+1}N}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{j,\varepsilon}(s)\,ds=\frac{T}{\varepsilon^{k+1}}.

(4.3)

Thanks to Inequality (
4.3
), we can bound the seperation
in time
t
as follows

\left|x_{i,\varepsilon}(t)-x_{j,\varepsilon}(t)\right|\geq\left|x_{i}^{0}-x_{j}^{0}\right|-\left|x_{i}^{0}-x_{i,\varepsilon}(t)\right|-\left|x_{j}^{0}-x_{j,\varepsilon}(t)\right|\geq\left|x_{i}^{0}-x_{j}^{0}\right|-\frac{2C_{k,d}T}{\varepsilon^{k+1}}.

If we choose
\varepsilon_{0}=\frac{1}{16}\underset{i\neq j}{\min}\left|x_{i}^{0}-x_{j}^{0}\right|
and

T>0
such that

\frac{2C_{k,d}T}{\varepsilon_{0}^{k+1}}\leq\frac{\underset{i\neq j}{\min}\left|x_{i}^{0}-x_{j}^{0}\right|}{2},

then for each
t\in[0,T)
we get the bound

\left|x_{i,\varepsilon_{0}}(t)-x_{j,\varepsilon_{0}}(t)\right|\geq\frac{\left|x_{i}^{0}-x_{j}^{0}\right|}{2}.

(4.4)

It follows from (
4.4
) that for each
t\in[0,T]
it holds
that
\nabla V_{\varepsilon}(x_{i,\varepsilon}(t)-x_{j,\varepsilon}(t))=\nabla V(x_{i,\varepsilon}(t)-x_{j,\varepsilon}(t))
,
and therefore for each
t\in[0,T]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\dot{x}_{i,\varepsilon_{0}}(t)=-\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{j,\varepsilon_{0}}(t)\mathbb{J}\nabla V(x_{i,\varepsilon_{0}}(t)-x_{j,\varepsilon_{0}}(t)),\ x_{i,\varepsilon_{0}}(0)=x_{i}^{0}\\
\dot{m}_{i,\varepsilon_{0}}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{i,\varepsilon_{0}}(t)m_{j,\varepsilon_{0}}(t)S(x_{i,\varepsilon_{0}}(t)-x_{j,\varepsilon_{0}}(t)),\ m_{i,\varepsilon_{0}}(0)=m_{i}^{0}.\end{array}\right.

∎

Next, we claim that the quantity
\mathcal{H}_{N}(t)
is propagated in time, and
already here the oddness of
S
is essential.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(\mathbf{x}_{N}(t),\mathbf{m}_{N}(t))
be a solution to the system of ODEs (
4.1
) with initial data
\mathbf{(}\mathbf{x}_{N}(0),\mathbf{m}_{N}(0))
. Then, the interaction energy satisfies

\mathcal{H}_{N}(t)\leq e^{2\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}t}\mathcal{H}_{N}(0),\ for\ all\ t\in[0,T).

Proof.

We compute the
time derivative as follows

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{H}_{N}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\underset{i\neq j}{\sum}m_{i}(t)m_{j}(t)V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{i\neq j}{\sum}\dot{m_{i}}(t)m_{j}(t)V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))+\underset{i\neq j}{\sum}m_{i}(t)\dot{m_{j}}(t)V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))

+\underset{i\neq j}{\sum}m_{i}(t)m_{j}(t)\nabla V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))\cdot(\dot{x_{i}}(t)-\dot{x_{j}}(t)).

(4.5)

The first 2 terms in the right hand side of (
4.5
) are paired together as follows.

\underset{i\neq j}{\sum}\dot{m_{i}}(t)m_{j}(t)V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))=\frac{1}{N}\underset{i\neq j}{\sum}\underset{k}{\sum}m_{i}(t)m_{k}(t)m_{j}(t)S(x_{k}(t)-x_{i}(t))V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)),

while

\displaystyle\underset{i\neq j}{\sum}m_{i}(t)\dot{m_{j}}(t)V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))=\frac{1}{N}\underset{i\neq j}{\sum}\underset{k}{\sum}m_{i}(t)m_{j}(t)m_{k}(t)S(x_{k}(t)-x_{j}(t))V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\underset{i\neq j}{\sum}\underset{k}{\sum}m_{i}(t)m_{j}(t)m_{k}(t)S(x_{k}(t)-x_{i}(t))V(x_{j}(t)-x_{i}(t)).

So, since
V
is even, the first 2 terms in (
4.5
) add up to

\displaystyle\frac{2}{N}\underset{i\neq j,k}{\sum}m_{i}(t)m_{j}(t)m_{k}(t)S(x_{k}(t)-x_{i}(t))V(x_{j}(t)-x_{i}(t))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}}{N}\underset{i\neq j,k}{\sum}m_{i}(t)m_{j}(t)m_{k}(t)V(x_{j}(t)-x_{i}(t))=2\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{N}(t),

(4.6)

where in the last equation we used conservation of the total weight (see Remark
2.4
). The third sum in the right hand side of (
4.5
) writes

\displaystyle\underset{i\neq j}{\sum}m_{i}(t)m_{j}(t)\nabla V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))\cdot

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{N}\underset{k\neq j}{\sum}m_{k}(t)\mathbb{J}\nabla V(x_{j}(t)-x_{k}(t))-\frac{1}{N}\underset{k\neq i}{\sum}m_{k}(t)\mathbb{J}\nabla V(x_{i}(t)-x_{k}(t))\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{N}{\underset{i,j,k}{\widehat{\sum}}}m_{i}(t)m_{j}(t)m_{k}(t)\nabla V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))\cdot\mathbb{J}\nabla V(x_{i}(t)-x_{k}(t))

where the equality is obtained by swapping the indices
i
and
j
in the first term and the hat sum refers to a sum over the set of indices
\{(i,j,k)\mbox{ such that }i\neq j,k\neq i\}
. We finally notice that we can rewrite this term as follows

\displaystyle{\underset{i,j,k}{\widehat{\sum}}}m_{i}(t)

\displaystyle m_{j}(t)m_{k}(t)\nabla V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))\cdot\mathbb{J}\nabla V(x_{i}(t)-x_{k}(t))

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{N}\sum_{i}m_{i}(t)\left(\sum_{j\neq i}m_{j}(t)\nabla V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))\right)\cdot\mathbb{J}\left(\sum_{k\neq i}m_{k}(t)\nabla V(x_{i}(t)-x_{k}(t))\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{N}\sum_{i}m_{i}(t)\left(\sum_{j\neq i}m_{j}(t)\nabla V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))\right)\cdot\mathbb{J}\left(\sum_{j\neq i}m_{j}(t)\nabla V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))\right).

(4.7)

If
\mathbb{J}
is anti-symmetric then the right-hand side of (
4.7
) is
0
while if
\mathbb{J}
is the identity matrix the right hand side of (
4.7
) is

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{N}\sum_{i}m_{i}(t)\left|\sum_{j\neq i}m_{j}(t)\nabla V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))\right|^{2}\leq 0.

Therefore, we conclude that the third sum in the right hand side of (
4.5
) satisfies

\displaystyle\underset{i\neq j}{\sum}m_{i}(t)m_{j}(t)\nabla V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))\cdot(\dot{x_{i}}(t)-\dot{x_{j}}(t))\leq 0.

(4.8)

Together with (
4.6
) this concludes the proof.
∎

As a corollary from propagation of energy, we get the following lower bound on the minimal separation
of the opinions, which will be needed in order to prove that maximal
lifespan solutions are in fact global.
We are now well positioned to prove Theorem
2.2
, i.e. the existence of a globally well-defined flow.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.
Step 1
.
Estimate on the separation
. Set
k\coloneqq d-2
. We claim that if
(\mathbf{x}_{N}(t),\mathbf{m}_{N}(t))

is a solution to the system (
4.1
), then there holds the estimate

\displaystyle\underset{i\neq j}{\min}\underset{t\in[0,T)}{\inf}\left|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)\right|\geq\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{e^{2\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}T}\mathcal{H}_{N}(0)}\right\}^{1/{k}}.

(4.9)

If
i\neq j
are such that
\left|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)\right|\leq 1
then by Lemma
4.2

we have

\frac{1}{\left|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)\right|^{k}}\leq\mathcal{H}_{N}(t)\leq e^{2\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}t}\mathcal{H}_{N}(0),

so that

\left|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)\right|^{k}\geq\frac{1}{e^{2\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}t}\mathcal{H}_{N}(0)}.

It follows that for all
i\neq j
one has

\left|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)\right|\geq\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{e^{2\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}T}\mathcal{H}_{N}(0)}\right\}^{1/{k}},

which establishes (
4.9
).

Step 2
.
Long time existence and uniqueness
. Let
(\mathbf{x}_{N}(t),\mathbf{m}_{N}(t))

be a solution to the system of ODEs (
4.1
) with maximal lifespan
T>0
.
We claim that if
T<\infty
then

\underset{t\nearrow T}{\lim}\underset{i\neq j}{\min}\left|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)\right|=0.

Indeed suppose that
T<\infty
and assume on the contrary that

\underset{t\nearrow T}{\lim}\underset{i\neq j}{\min}\left|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)\right|=\delta>0.

Choose
T^{\prime}<T
sufficiently close to
T
such that

\underset{T^{\prime}\leq t\leq T}{\inf}\underset{i\neq j}{\min}\left|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)\right|\geq\frac{\delta}{2}.

Assume also that
T-T^{\prime}<T_{\Delta}
, where
T_{\Delta}
is the maximal
lifespan solution of the system of ODEs (
4.1
) with initial data
(x_{i}(T^{\prime}),m_{i}(T^{\prime}))
, i.e. the equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\dot{z_{i}}(t)=-\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}n_{j}(t)\mathbb{J}\nabla V(z_{i}(t)-z_{j}(t)),\ z_{i}(0)=x_{i}(T^{\prime})\\
\dot{n}_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}n_{i}(t)n_{j}(t)S(z_{i}(t)-z_{j}(t)),\ n_{i}(0)=m_{i}(T^{\prime}).\end{array}\right.

(4.10)

Note that by Theorem
4.1
, there exists a maximal life span solution
(\mathbf{z}_{N}(t),\mathbf{n}_{N}(t))

on
[0,T_{\Delta})
to the system of ODEs (
4.10
). Define

\mathbf{y}_{N}(t)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\mathbf{x}_{N}(t)&0\leq t\leq T^{\prime}\\
\mathbf{z}_{N}(t-T^{\prime})&T^{\prime}\leq t<T^{\prime}+T_{\Delta}\end{array}\right.,\ \mathbf{l}_{N}(t)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\mathbf{m}_{N}(t)&0\leq t\leq T^{\prime}\\
\mathbf{n}_{N}(t-T^{\prime})&T^{\prime}\leq t<T^{\prime}+T_{\Delta}\end{array}\right..

Note that
\mathbf{y}_{N}(t)
is continuous and that by (
4.9
) we have

\underset{t\in[0,T^{\prime}+T_{\Delta})}{\inf}\underset{i\neq j}{\min}\left|y_{i}(t)-y_{j}(t)\right|>0.

We leave the reader to check that
(\mathbf{y}_{N}(t),\mathbf{l}_{N}(t))
is
a solution to the system on

since we are dealing with an autonomous system. This entails a contradiction to the assumption that

is maximal, because

. We conclude that

In view of (
4.9
) it follows that

, as desired.

The mean field limit.

In Section 5.1 we compute the time derivative of
\mathcal{E}_{N}(t)
, and in Section 5.2 we establish the functional inequality (

5.1
Time Derivative of the re-normalized Modulated Energy.

We recall that the re-normalized interaction energy (
2.6
) is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{x\neq y}V(x-y)\left(\mu_{N}(t,\cdot)-\mu(t,\cdot)\right)^{\otimes 2}dxdy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\underset{i\neq j}{\sum}m_{i}(t)m_{j}(t)V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))-\frac{2}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{i}(t)(V\star\mu)(x_{i}(t))+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu(t,x)(V\star\mu)(t,x)dx.

The aim of this section is to compute the time derivative of
\mathcal{E}_{N}(t)
, which is given in the following result.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let hypotheses (
H1
)-(
H2
) hold. Let
\mu(t,\cdot)
be the unique solution to the PDE (
2.2
) with initial data
\mu_{0}
provided by Theorem
1.1
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}_{N}(t)\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{x\neq y}\left(\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu(t,x)-\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu(t,y)\right)\nabla V(x-y)\left(\mu_{N}(t,\cdot)-\mu(t,\cdot)\right)^{\otimes 2}dxdy

\displaystyle+2\int_{x\neq y}V(x-y)\left(h\left[\mu_{N}(t,\cdot)\right](x)-h\left[\mu(t,\cdot)\right](x)\right)\left(\mu_{N}(t,y)-\mu(t,y)\right)dxdy

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{N}^{1}(t)+\mathcal{D}_{N}^{2}(t).

Proof.

We recall that from Theorem
2.2
, we have that
x_{i}(t)\neq x_{j}(t)
for all
t\geq 0
and all
i\neq j
. This makes it straightforward to justify all calculations in the proof. To make the equations lighter we shall omit the time variable whenever there is no ambiguity.

Step 1
.
Calculation of

\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{N^{2}}\underset{i\neq j}{\sum}m_{i}(t)m_{j}(t)V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))
.
We have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{N^{2}}\underset{i\neq j}{\sum}m_{i}(t)m_{j}(t)V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{2}}\underset{i\neq j}{\sum}m_{i}(t)m_{j}(t)\nabla V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))\cdot\left(\dot{x_{i}}(t)-\dot{x_{j}}(t)\right)

(5.1)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\underset{i\neq j}{\sum}(\dot{m_{i}}(t)m_{j}(t)+m_{i}(t)\dot{m}_{j}(t))V(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle E_{1}+T_{1}.

The first term in (
5.1
) is non-positive due to (
4.8
) in Lemma
4.2
, i.e.
E_{1}\leq 0
.
The second term in (
5.1
) can be rewritten using symmetry as

\displaystyle T_{1}=\frac{2}{N^{2}}\underset{i\neq j}{\sum}\dot{m_{i}}m_{j}V(x_{i}-x_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{N^{3}}\underset{i\neq j}{\underset{i,j,k}{\sum}}m_{i}m_{j}m_{k}S(x_{i}-x_{k})V(x_{i}-x_{j})

\displaystyle=2\int_{x\neq y}V(x-y)h\left[\mu_{N}(t,\cdot)\right](x)\mu_{N}(t,y)dxdy.

Step 2.

Calculations of

-\frac{d}{dt}\frac{2}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{i}(t)(V\star\mu)(t,x_{i}(t))

and

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(V\star\mu)(t,x)\mu(t,x)
.
Let us start with

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{dt}\frac{2}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{i}(t)V\star\mu(t,x_{i}(t))

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\dot{m_{i}}V\star\mu(x_{i})-\frac{2}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{i}V\star\partial_{t}\mu(t,x_{i}(t))-\frac{2}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{i}\nabla V\star\mu(x_{i})\dot{x}_{i}=T_{2}+E_{2},

where we have set

\displaystyle T_{2}\coloneqq-\frac{2}{N^{2}}\underset{i,k}{\sum}m_{i}m_{k}S(x_{i}-x_{k})V\star\mu(x_{i})-\frac{2}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{i}V\star h\left[\mu\right](x_{i}),

\displaystyle E_{2}\coloneqq-\frac{2}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{i}V\star(\mathrm{div}(\mu\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu))(x_{i})-\frac{2}{N^{2}}\underset{i\neq k}{\sum}m_{i}m_{k}\nabla V\star\mu(x_{i})\mathbb{J}\nabla V(x_{k}-x_{i}).

(5.2)

Observe that we can further write that

T_{2}=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V\star\mu(t,x)h\left[\mu_{N}(t,\cdot)\right]dx-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V\star h\left[\mu\right](t,x)\mu_{N}(t,x)dx.\hskip 71.13188pt

With similar calculations, we find that

\displaystyle+2\int_{x\neq y}V(x-y)\left(h\left[\mu_{N}(t,\cdot)\right](x)-h\left[\mu(t,\cdot)\right](x)\right)\left(\mu_{N}(t,y)-\mu(t,y)\right)dxdy,

Step 3

\displaystyle E_{2}+E_{3}=

\displaystyle\,2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V\star\left(\mathrm{div}(\mu\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu)\right)(x)\mu(x)dx-\frac{2}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{i}V\star(\mathrm{div}(\mu\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu))(x_{i})

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla V(x-y)(\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu)(x)\mu(x)\mu_{N}(y)dxdy,

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\,-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu(x)\nabla V\star\mu(x)\mu_{N}(x)dx

\displaystyle\,-\int_{x\neq y}\left(\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu(x)-\mathbb{J}\nabla V\star\mu(y)\right)\nabla V(x-y)\left(\mu_{N}-\mu\right)^{\otimes 2}(dxdy),

Lemma 5.2

•

\displaystyle\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi(z)\left|K^{1}\right|(z)\left|V(x-\delta z)-V(x)\right|dz

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1-\chi(z))\left|K^{1}\right|(z)\left|V(x-\delta z)-V(x)\right|dz.

(5.5)

Ultimately

Step 1

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\left|x-\delta z\right|\geq\frac{3\left|x\right|}{2}}(1-\chi(z))\left|K^{1}\right|(z)V(x-\delta z)dz+V(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1-\chi(z))\left|K^{1}\right|(z)dz

1

, it follows that

\displaystyle I\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{d}}\int_{B_{\delta}(0)}V(z)dz=\frac{1}{(d-k)\delta^{k}}.

Hence, for all
\left|x\right|\leq\left(\tfrac{d-k}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}\delta
, we get

\displaystyle I\leq\frac{1}{2\left|x\right|^{k}}=\frac{1}{2}V(x).

In addition, we obtain

\displaystyle J\leq\frac{1}{(\delta-\left|x\right|)^{k}}\leq\frac{1}{2\left|x\right|^{k}},

for all
\left|x\right|\leq\frac{\delta}{1+2^{\frac{1}{k}}}
,
so that
J\leq\tfrac{1}{2}V(x)
.

To conclude, the claim follows by taking
f(\delta)=\min\left\{\frac{1}{1+2^{\frac{1}{k}}},\left(\frac{d-k}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}\right\}\delta
.

Step 4
.
Construction of

V_{\varepsilon}
. In this step we will construct
V_{\varepsilon}
and then show that it satisfies the requested properties i.-iv. We construct our regularized kernel following the same strategy as in
[
8
, Lemma 4.1]
.

We now need to interpolate between this inequality near the origin and the far-field inequality (
5.6
) by means of the inequality (
5.14
). This is crucially needed in order to keep the constant 1 in front of
V(x)
on the right-hand side of our statement in point iv. As mentioned the construction is motivated by
[
8
, Lemma 4.1]
proved in the torus.

Given
\varepsilon>0
pick
0<\delta_{1}\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\lambda}}}{C}
, where
C>0
is the constant in (
5.14
), and recursively pick
0<\delta_{i+1}
such that

\delta_{{i+1}}\leq\min\left\{f(\delta_{{i}}),\delta_{{i}}^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}\right\}.

Let
M\coloneqq\left\lfloor\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right\rfloor
where
\lfloor x\rfloor
denotes the closest lower integer to
x
,
and set

W_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\frac{1}{M}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}K^{1}_{\delta_{{i}}}\star V.

(5.19)

Obviously
\delta_{{i}}
is decreasing since
\lambda<1
and
\underset{1\leq i\leq M}{\max}\delta_{{i}}\underset{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\rightarrow}0
by construction.

If
\left|x\right|\geq\delta_{1}^{\lambda}
, (
5.6
) implies that

\displaystyle W_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq V(x)+\varepsilon.

If
\left|x\right|\leq f(\delta_{{M}})
, (
5.18
) implies that
W_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq V(x)
.

If
f(\delta_{{M}})\leq\left|x\right|\leq\delta_{1}^{\lambda}
, fix
1\leq i\leq M
such that
\delta_{{i+1}}\leq\left|x\right|\leq\delta_{{i}}
, then:

•

If
j>i+1
then
\left|x\right|\geq\delta_{{i+1}}\geq\delta_{{j}}^{\lambda}

so that using again (
5.6
) we get

K_{\delta_{{j}}}^{1}\star V(x)\leq V(x)+\varepsilon.

•

If
j<i
then
\left|x\right|\leq\delta_{{i}}\leq f(\delta_{{j}})

so that again by (
5.18
) we have

K_{\delta_{{j}}}^{1}\star V(x)\leq V(x).

•

If
j=i
or
j=i+1
then according to (
5.14
)

K_{\delta_{{j}}}^{1}\star V(x)\leq C\left(V(x)+\varepsilon\right).

We finally define

V_{\varepsilon}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{1+2C\varepsilon}W_{\varepsilon}(x)

(5.20)

and conclude that

V_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq V(x)+\varepsilon
,
which establishes point iv. Notice that the definition of
M
is crucially used for this estimate.

That
\widehat{V_{\varepsilon}}\geq 0
is immediate by construction by the choice of
K^{1}
and establishes point i. We are left to prove point ii,iii and v. If we split
V=V_{1}+V_{2}
where
V_{1}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
V_{2}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
then

\displaystyle\left\|W_{\varepsilon}-V\right\|_{L^{p}+L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\underset{1\leq i\leq M}{\max}\left\|K_{\delta_{{i}}}\star V_{1}-V_{1}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\underset{1\leq i\leq M}{\max}\left\|K_{\delta_{{i}}}\star V_{2}-V_{2}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\underset{1\leq i\leq M}{\max}\delta_{{i}}\underset{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0,

which is assertion ii.
Note further that for a given
\delta>0
one has

\displaystyle\left\|\zeta_{\delta}(K^{1}_{\delta_{{i}}}\star V)-\zeta_{\delta}V\right\|_{L^{p}+L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|K^{1}_{\delta_{{i}}}\star(\zeta_{\delta}V)-\zeta_{\delta}V\right\|_{L^{p}+L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\left\|\zeta_{\delta}(K^{1}_{\delta_{{i}}}\star((1-\zeta_{\delta})V))\right\|_{L^{p}+L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle+\left\|(\zeta_{\delta}-1)(K^{1}_{\delta_{{i}}}\star(\zeta_{\delta}V))\right\|_{L^{p}+L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle I+II+III.

It is not difficult to check that
\left\|K^{1}_{\delta}\star f-f\right\|_{q}\lesssim_{d}\delta\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{q}
by using that the first moment of
K^{1}_{\delta}
is of order
\delta
. Therefore, the first term is

\displaystyle I\leq\left\|K^{1}_{\delta_{i}}\star\zeta_{\delta}V-\zeta_{\delta}V\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\underset{1\leq i\leq M}{\max}\delta_{i}\left\|\nabla(\zeta_{\delta}V)\right\|_{q}\leq\underset{1\leq i\leq M}{\max}\delta_{i}\left(\left\|\zeta_{\delta}\nabla V\right\|_{q}+\left\|V\nabla\zeta_{\delta}\right\|_{q}\right).

Observe that

\displaystyle\left\|\zeta_{\delta}\nabla V\right\|_{q}\lesssim\left(\int_{\left|\cdot\right|\geq\delta}\frac{1}{\left|x\right|^{q(k+1)}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\delta}^{\infty}r^{d-1-q(k+1)}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\frac{\delta^{\frac{d}{q}}}{\delta^{k+1}}.

Also, a similar calculation reveals that

\left\|V\nabla\zeta_{\delta}\right\|_{q}\leq\left\|\nabla\zeta_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|V\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{2\delta}\setminus B_{\delta})}\lesssim\left\|\nabla\zeta_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\frac{\delta^{\frac{d}{q}}}{\delta^{k}}\leq\frac{\delta^{\frac{d}{q}}}{\delta^{k+1}},

(5.21)

hence
I\lesssim{\varepsilon}{\delta^{\frac{d}{q}-1-k}}
.
Moreover

\displaystyle II=\left\|\zeta_{\delta}(K^{1}_{\delta_{i}}\star((1-\zeta_{\delta})V))\right\|_{L^{p}+L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|K^{1}_{\delta_{i}}\star((1-\zeta_{\delta})V)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|K^{1}_{\delta_{i}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\|(1-\zeta_{\delta})V\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\delta^{\frac{d}{p}-k}.

As for
III
, we estimate

\displaystyle III\leq\left\|(\zeta_{\delta}-1)(K^{1}_{\delta_{i}}\star\zeta_{\delta}V)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\zeta_{\delta}-1\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\|K^{1}_{\delta_{i}}\star\zeta_{\delta}V\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{\frac{d}{p}}\left\|\zeta_{\delta}V\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\delta^{\frac{d}{p}-k}.

Therefore, we have proved

\displaystyle\left\|\zeta_{\delta}K_{\delta_{n_{i}}}\star V-\zeta_{\delta}V\right\|_{L^{p}+L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{k+1-\frac{d}{q}}}+\delta^{\frac{d}{p}-k},

which concludes the proof of item iii. We finally have

\displaystyle\left\|K^{1}_{\delta}\star V\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|K^{1}_{\delta}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\left\|\mathbf{1}_{\left|\cdot\right|\leq 1}V\right\|_{p}+\left\|K^{1}_{\delta}\right\|_{q^{\prime}}\left\|\mathbf{1}_{\left|\cdot\right|\geq 1}V\right\|_{q}\leq\delta^{-\frac{d}{p}}+\delta^{-\frac{d}{q}}

so that by defining

\displaystyle\left\|V_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left(\min_{1\leq i\leq M}\delta_{i}\right)^{-\frac{d}{p}}+\left(\min_{1\leq i\leq M}\delta_{i}\right)^{-\frac{d}{q}}:=\frac{1}{\Theta(\varepsilon)},

the statement v. follows.
∎

In the following Lemma we show that given a function in Fourier variable
s(\xi)
satisfying some growth assumptions we can bound
s\star\widehat{V_{\varepsilon}}
pointwise in absolute value by means of
\widehat{V_{\varepsilon}}
, where
V_{\varepsilon}
is the approximating kernel constructed in Lemma
5.2
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
V_{\varepsilon}
be as in Lemma
5.2
. Assume that there are some
S_{0}>0
and some
\sigma>2d
such that

\displaystyle\left|s(\xi)\right|\leq\frac{S_{0}}{(1+\left|\xi\right|)^{\sigma}}.

(5.22)

Given
r>\frac{d}{d-k}
set
S_{0}^{\prime}\coloneqq\max\left\{\left\|s\right\|_{1},\left\|\left|\cdot\right|^{d-k}s\right\|_{r^{\prime}},\left\|(1+\left|\cdot\right|^{d-k})s\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\right\}
.
Then, it holds that

\left|(s\star\widehat{V_{\varepsilon}})(\xi)\right|\leq C_{1}\widehat{V_{\varepsilon}}(\xi)+C_{2}\frac{o_{\varepsilon}(1)}{(1+\left|\xi\right|)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}},

where
C_{1}=c_{1}(k,d)S_{0}^{\prime}
and

C_{2}=c_{2}(k,\sigma,d)S_{0}
.

Proof.

We separate between two regions. Put
\overline{\delta}\coloneqq\underset{1\leq i\leq M}{\max}\delta_{i}\leq\varepsilon
.

Step 1
. Suppose that
\left|\xi\right|<\frac{1}{\overline{\delta}}
.
Note that for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\widehat{V_{\varepsilon}}(\xi)=\frac{1}{M(1+2C\varepsilon)}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\widehat{K^{1}_{\delta_{{i}}}}(\xi)\widehat{V}(\xi)\leq\left\|\widehat{K^{1}}\right\|_{\infty}\widehat{V}(\xi).

(5.23)

Fix some
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
0\leq\chi\leq 1
and
\chi\equiv 1

on
B_{1}(0)
and
\chi\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{2}(0)
.
In view of (
5.23
) we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{V_{\varepsilon}}(\xi-\zeta)s(\zeta)d\zeta\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\widehat{K^{1}}\right\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{V}(\xi-\zeta)\left|s(\zeta)\right|d\zeta

\displaystyle=\left\|\widehat{K^{1}}\right\|_{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi(\xi-\zeta)\widehat{V}(\xi-\zeta)\left|s(\zeta)\right|d\zeta+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1-\chi(\xi-\zeta))\widehat{V}(\xi-\zeta)\left|s(\zeta)\right|d\zeta\right).

(5.24)

We control each of the integrals in (
5.24
) separately. Thanks to (
5.22
) one has

\displaystyle\left|\xi\right|^{d-k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi(\zeta)\widehat{V}(\zeta)\left|s(\xi-\zeta)\right|d\zeta\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi(\zeta)\widehat{V}(\zeta)\left|\xi-\zeta\right|^{d-k}\left|s(\xi-\zeta)\right|d\zeta

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi(\zeta)\widehat{V}(\zeta)\left|\zeta\right|^{d-k}\left|s(\xi-\zeta)\right|d\zeta

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\left\|(1+\left|\cdot\right|^{d-k})s\right\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+\left|\zeta\right|^{d-k})\chi(\zeta)\widehat{V}(\zeta)d\zeta

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|(1+\left|\cdot\right|^{d-k})s\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\left(1+\left|\cdot\right|^{d-k}\right)\widehat{V}(\cdot)\right\|_{L^{1}(B_{2}(0))}\lesssim\left\|(1+\left|\cdot\right|^{d-k})s\right\|_{\infty},

which shows that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi(\xi-\zeta)\widehat{V}(\xi-\zeta)\left|s(\zeta)\right|d\zeta\lesssim\frac{\left\|(1+\left|\cdot\right|^{d-k})s\right\|_{\infty}}{\left|\xi\right|^{d-k}}\lesssim\left\|(1+\left|\cdot\right|^{d-k})s\right\|_{\infty}\widehat{V}(\xi).

(5.25)

Secondly, we can estimate the truncation far from origin as follows

\displaystyle\left|\xi\right|^{d-k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1-\chi(\xi-\zeta))\widehat{V}(\xi-\zeta)\left|s(\zeta)\right|d\zeta\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\xi-\zeta\right|^{d-k}(1-\chi(\xi-\zeta))\widehat{V}(\xi-\zeta)\left|s(\zeta)\right|d\zeta

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\zeta\right|^{d-k}(1-\chi(\xi-\zeta))\widehat{V}(\xi-\zeta)\left|s(\zeta)\right|d\zeta

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|s(\zeta)\right|d\zeta+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\xi-\zeta\right|^{d-k}\left|s(\xi-\zeta)\right|(1-\chi(\zeta))\widehat{V}(\zeta)d\zeta

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|s\right\|_{1}+\left\|(1-\chi)\widehat{V}\right\|_{r}\left\|\left|\cdot\right|^{d-k}s\right\|_{r^{\prime}}.

(5.26)

Choosing
r>\frac{d}{d-k}
ensures that
\left\|(1-\chi)\widehat{V}\right\|_{r}<\infty
. Therefore we conclude from (
5.25
), (
5.26
)
that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{V_{\varepsilon}}(\xi-\zeta)\left|s\right|(\zeta)d\zeta\lesssim S^{\prime}_{0}\widehat{V}(\xi).

Now, utilizing the assumption that
\left|\xi\right|<\frac{1}{\overline{\delta}}
we see that
\widehat{K^{1}_{\delta_{{i}}}}(\xi)\gtrsim 1
for all
1\leq i\leq M
, so that we find

\widehat{V}(\xi)\lesssim\widehat{V}_{\varepsilon}(\xi)
,
for
\left|\xi\right|<\frac{1}{\overline{\delta}}
, and thus we get

\displaystyle\left|s\star\widehat{V_{\varepsilon}}(\xi)\right|\lesssim S^{\prime}_{0}\widehat{V_{\varepsilon}}(\xi),\mbox{ for }\left|\xi\right|<\frac{1}{\overline{\delta}}.

(5.27)

Step 2
. Suppose now
\left|\xi\right|\geq\frac{1}{\overline{\delta}}
.
In this case we split the integral as follows

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{V}_{\varepsilon}(\xi-\zeta)s(\zeta)d\zeta=\int_{\left|\zeta\right|\geq\frac{\left|\xi\right|}{2}}\widehat{V}_{\varepsilon}(\xi-\zeta)s(\zeta)d\zeta+\int_{\left|\zeta\right|<\frac{\left|\xi\right|}{2}}\widehat{V}_{\varepsilon}(\xi-\zeta)s(\zeta)d\zeta\coloneqq I(\xi)+J(\xi).

Pick a function
0\leq\chi\leq 1
such that
\chi\equiv 1
on
\left|\zeta\right|\leq\frac{1}{4\overline{\delta}}

and
\chi\equiv 0
on
\left|\zeta\right|\geq\frac{1}{2\overline{\delta}}
.
We start with
I(\xi)
. We can write

\displaystyle I(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\left|\zeta\right|\geq\frac{\left|\xi\right|}{2}}\widehat{V_{\varepsilon}}(\xi-\zeta)(1-\chi(\zeta))s(\zeta)d\zeta

\displaystyle=\int_{\left|\zeta\right|\geq\frac{\left|\xi\right|}{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\cdot\right|\leq 1}(\xi-\zeta)\widehat{V_{\varepsilon}}(\xi-\zeta)(1-\chi(\zeta))s(\zeta)d\zeta+\int_{\left|\zeta\right|\geq\frac{\left|\xi\right|}{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\cdot\right|\geq 1}(\xi-\zeta)\widehat{V_{\varepsilon}}(\xi-\zeta)(1-\chi(\zeta))s(\zeta)d\zeta

\displaystyle\coloneqq I_{1}(\xi)+I_{2}(\xi).

First, we have

\displaystyle\left|I_{2}(\xi)\right|\lesssim\int_{\left|\zeta\right|\geq\frac{\left|\xi\right|}{2}}\left|(1-\chi(\zeta))s(\zeta)\right|d\zeta\lesssim\frac{S_{0}}{(1+\left|\xi\right|)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}}\int_{\left|\zeta\right|\geq\frac{1}{2\overline{\delta}}}\frac{1}{(1+\left|\zeta\right|)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}}d\zeta\lesssim\frac{S_{0}o_{\varepsilon}(1)}{(1+\left|\xi\right|)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}}.

(5.28)

Secondly, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\left(1+\left|\xi\right|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}\right)\left|I_{1}(\xi)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\left|\xi\right|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\cdot\right|\leq 1}(\xi-\zeta)\widehat{V_{\varepsilon}}(\xi-\zeta)(1-\chi(\zeta))\left|s(\zeta)\right|d\zeta

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\xi-\zeta\right|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\cdot\right|\leq 1}(\xi-\zeta)\widehat{V_{\varepsilon}}(\xi-\zeta)(1-\chi(\zeta))\left|s(\zeta)\right|d\zeta

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\cdot\right|\leq 1}(\xi-\zeta)\widehat{V_{\varepsilon}}(\xi-\zeta)(1-\chi(\zeta))\left(1+\left|\zeta\right|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}\right)\left|s(\zeta)\right|d\zeta

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\cdot\right|\leq 1}(\xi-\zeta)\left|\xi-\zeta\right|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}\widehat{V}(\xi-\zeta)\left|(1-\chi(\zeta))s(\zeta)\right|d\zeta

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\cdot\right|\leq 1}(\xi-\zeta)\widehat{V}(\xi-\zeta)\left|(1-\chi(\zeta))\left(1+\left|\zeta\right|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}\right)s(\zeta)\right|d\zeta.

The right-hand side of the last equation is bounded by

\displaystyle\int_{\left|\zeta\right|\geq\frac{1}{4\overline{\delta}}}\left|s(\zeta)\right|d\zeta+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\cdot\right|\leq 1}(\xi-\zeta)\widehat{V}(\xi-\zeta)\left|(1-\chi(\zeta))\left(1+\left|\zeta\right|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}\right)s(\zeta)\right|d\zeta

\displaystyle\lesssim S_{0}o_{\varepsilon}(1)+\underset{\left|\zeta\right|\geq\frac{1}{4\overline{\delta}}}{\sup}\left|\left(1+\left|\zeta\right|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}\right)s(\zeta)\right|\lesssim S_{0}o_{\varepsilon}(1).

(5.29)

The combination of (
5.28
) and (
5.29
) reveal that

\displaystyle\left|I(\xi)\right|\lesssim\frac{S_{0}o_{\varepsilon}(1)}{(1+\left|\xi\right|)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}}.

(5.30)

To bound
J(\xi)
, recall that by assumption, there is some
C>0
and
r>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C(1+\left|\xi\right|^{r})}\leq\widehat{K^{1}}(\xi)\leq\frac{C}{1+\left|\xi\right|^{r}}.

As a result, for any
\zeta,\xi
such that

\left|\zeta\right|<\frac{\left|\xi\right|}{2}
it holds that

\displaystyle\widehat{K^{1}_{\delta_{i}}}(\xi-\zeta)=\widehat{K^{1}}(\delta_{i}(\xi-\zeta))\lesssim\frac{1}{1+\delta_{i}^{r}\left|\xi-\zeta\right|^{r}}\lesssim\frac{1}{1+\delta_{i}^{r}\left|\xi\right|^{r}}\lesssim\widehat{K^{1}_{\delta_{i}}}(\xi).

Consequently, we infer that

\displaystyle\widehat{V_{\varepsilon}}(\xi-\zeta)=\frac{1}{M(1+2C\varepsilon)}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\widehat{K^{1}}(\delta_{i}(\xi-\zeta))\widehat{V}(\xi-\zeta)\lesssim\frac{1}{M(1+2C\varepsilon)}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\widehat{K^{1}}(\delta_{i}\xi)\widehat{V}(\xi)=\widehat{V}_{\varepsilon}(\xi),

for
\left|\zeta\right|<\frac{\left|\xi\right|}{2}
.
Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\left|J(\xi)\right|\lesssim\widehat{V_{\varepsilon}}(\xi)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|s\right|(\zeta)d\zeta\lesssim S^{\prime}_{0}\widehat{V}_{\varepsilon}(\xi).

(5.31)

Gathering Inequalities (
5.27
), (
5.30
) and (
5.31
) yields the announced result.
∎

The following Lemma is the deterministic version of

, Lemma 5.1]
,
and it is meant to provide a bound on the truncated off-diagonal interaction
part in terms of the modulated energy.

Lemma 5.5
.

Under the same assumptions and notations of Lemma
5.2
, let
\mu\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a probability density and let
\mu_{N}
be an empirical measure defined by (
1.3
) associated to any
\mathbf{x}_{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{dN}\setminus\triangle_{N}
and
\mathbf{m}_{N}\in\mathbb{M}^{N}
such that
m_{i}^{N}\leq\overline{M}
for some
\overline{M}>0
. Then, there is a constant
C=C(\left\|\mu\right\|_{\infty},d,k,p,q)
such that the renormalized energy (
2.5
) can be estimated from below as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N}(\mu,\mu_{N})=\int_{x\neq y}V(x-y)\left(\mu_{N}-\mu\right)^{\otimes 2}(dxdy)\geq-\frac{\overline{M}\left\|V_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\infty}}{N}-C\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{k+1-\frac{d}{q}}}+\delta^{\frac{d}{p}-k}+o_{\varepsilon}(1)\right)

(5.32)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{2}}\underset{i\neq j}{\sum}m_{i}m_{j}(1-\zeta_{\delta})V(x_{i}-x_{j})\leq C\left(\mathcal{E}_{N}(\mu,\mu_{N})+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon,\delta,N)\right)

(5.33)

where
\mathcal{O}
has the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon,\delta,N)=\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{k+1-\frac{d}{q}}}+\delta^{d-k-\frac{\sigma}{2}}o_{\varepsilon\delta^{-1}}(1)+\frac{\overline{M}\left\|V_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\infty}}{N}+\delta^{\frac{d}{p}-k}.

(5.34)

Proof
.
Step 1
. In this step we prove (
5.32
). We denote by
\lesssim
inequality up to a constant depending only on
\left\|\mu\right\|_{\infty},d,k,p,q
. We have

\displaystyle\int_{x\neq y}V(x-y)\left(\mu_{N}-\mu\right)^{\otimes 2}(dxdy)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\delta}(x-y)V(x-y)\left(\mu_{N}-\mu\right)^{\otimes 2}(dxdy)

\int_{x\neq y}(V_{\varepsilon}-V)(x-y)\left(\mu_{N}-\mu\right)^{\otimes 2}(dxdy)\lesssim\varepsilon+\eta(\varepsilon).

(5.53)

Thus, (

I_{\delta,\varepsilon}^{2}\leq C\left(\mathcal{E}_{N}(\mu,\mu_{N})+\frac{\overline{M}\left\|V_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\infty}}{N}+\delta^{d-k-\frac{\sigma}{2}}o_{\varepsilon\delta^{-1}}(1)\right),

where

C=C\left(\left\|S\right\|_{\infty},\left\|\mu\right\|_{\infty},d,k,p,q\right).

Step 4
. We immediately have that

\displaystyle I_{\delta,\varepsilon}^{3}=

\displaystyle\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\delta}(x-y)V(x-y)\left(\mu_{N}-\mu\right)^{\otimes 2}(dxdy)

\displaystyle-\left\|S\right\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\delta}(x-y)V_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\left(\mu_{N}-\mu\right)^{\otimes 2}(dxdy).

First, note that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\delta}(x-y)V(x-y)\left(\mu_{N}-\mu\right)^{\otimes 2}(dxdy)=

\displaystyle\int_{x\neq y}\zeta_{\delta}(x-y)V(x-y)\left(\mu_{N}-\mu\right)^{\otimes 2}(dxdy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{E}_{N}(\mu,\mu_{N})-\frac{1}{N^{2}}\underset{i\neq j}{\sum}m_{i}m_{j}((1-\zeta_{\delta})V)(x_{i}-x_{j})

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{V_{\varepsilon}}(x)\left|\widehat{\mu_{N}-\mu}\right|^{2}(x)dx+\delta^{d-k-\frac{\sigma}{2}}o_{\varepsilon\delta^{-1}}(1).

By (

Proposition 5.7

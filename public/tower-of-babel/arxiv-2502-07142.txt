Moments of characteristic polynomials for classical β \beta ensembles
Bo-Jian Shen, Peter J. Forrester

Abstract
For random matrix ensembles with unitary symmetry, there is interest in the large N N form of the moments of the absolute value of the characteristic polynomial for their relevance to the Riemann zeta function on the critical line , and to Fisher-Hartwig asymptotics in the theory of Toeplitz determinants. The constant (with respect to N N ) in this asymptotic expansion, involving the Barnes G G function, is most relevant to the first of these, while the algebraic term (in N N ) and the functional dependence on the power are of primary interest in the latter. Desrosiers and Liu [ 20 ] have obtained the analogous expansions for the classical Gaussian, Laguerre and Jacobi β \beta ensembles in the case of even moments. We give simplified working of these results — which requires the use of duality formulas and the use of steepest descents for multidimensional integrals — providing too an error bound on the resulting asymptotic expressions. The universality of the constant term with respect to an earlier result known for the circular β \beta ensemble is established, which requires writing it in a Barnes G G function form, while the functional dependence on the powers is related to that appearing in Gaussian fluctuation formulas for linear statistics. In the Laguerre and Jacobi cases our working can be extended to the circumstance when the exponents in the weight function are (strictly) proportional to N N , giving results not previously available in the literature.

x^{a_{1}}(1-x)^{a_{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{0<x<1},&{\rm Jacobi}.\end{cases},

, which leads to the error term in (

∎

In the case of Gaussian
\beta
ensemble, and require that

and thus inside the support of the limiting global density, the

Solving for the saddle points of

yields

\displaystyle u_{\pm}={i\lambda\pm\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}\over\sqrt{2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\left.\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial u^{2}}f^{G}(u,\lambda)\right|_{u_{\pm}}=4\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}\left(-\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}\mp i\lambda\right),

which then gives

\displaystyle R=4\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}},\hskip 10.00002ptu_{+}-u_{-}=\sqrt{2(1-\lambda^{2})},\hskip 10.00002ptf^{G}_{+}+f^{G}_{-}=2\lambda^{2}-1-\log 2,\hskip 10.00002pt\theta_{\pm}=\mp\arcsin\lambda.

Therefore, by (
3.7
), the large
N

is given by

\displaystyle R_{N,\beta}[f^{G};\mathbb{R}]\sim\pi^{p}2^{-{2p^{2}\over\beta}-{2p\over\beta}(p-1)}

\displaystyle\left(N\pi\rho^{\rm W}(\lambda)\right)^{-p-{2p\over\beta}(p-1)}\left(\pi\rho^{\rm W}(\lambda)\right)^{4p^{2}\over\beta}

\displaystyle\times e^{pN(2\lambda^{2}-1-\log 2)}\binom{2p}{p}\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left({\Gamma(1+2j/\beta)\over\Gamma(1+2/\beta)}\right)^{2}.

By substituting this into equation (
3.3
), we obtain the sought asymptotic formula
(
1.16
).

Lemma 2

Let

Proposition 4.1

Let

Proof.

We first use Lemma
2

∎

Taking second derivative gives

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left.\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial u^{2}}f^{L}\right|_{u_{\pm}}=\frac{1}{u^{2}}-\left.\frac{1}{(u+1)^{2}}\right|_{u_{\pm}}=\left.\frac{2u+1}{(u(u+1))^{2}}\right|_{u_{\pm}}\\

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle R=16\lambda^{2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\lambda}-1},\hskip 10.00002pt\theta_{+}=-\frac{3\pi}{4},\hskip 10.00002pt\theta_{-}=-\frac{\pi}{4}.

Evaluation of

\displaystyle R_{N,\beta}[f^{L};\mathcal{C}]

as

(4.4)

4.2

\displaystyle\frac{(4N)^{-(a+1+2p)N-\frac{\beta}{2}N(N-1)}C_{\beta,N}\left[x^{a+2p}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}x}\right]}{C_{\beta,N}\left[x^{a}e^{-2N\beta x}\right]\tilde{C}_{{4\over\beta},2p}[\omega(\theta)](2\pi\mathrm{i})^{2p}}.

This is done by the particular Selberg integral evaluation formulas
(see e.g.
[

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dx_{1}\cdots

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dx_{N}\prod_{l=1}^{N}x_{l}^{\beta a/2}e^{-\beta x_{l}/2}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}\left|x_{k}-x_{j}\right|^{\beta}

\displaystyle M_{N}(a,b,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=0}^{2p-1}\frac{\Gamma\left(N+\frac{2}{\beta}(a+1)+\frac{2}{\beta}j\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{2}{\beta}(j+1)\right)}{\Gamma\left(N+1+\frac{2}{\beta}j\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{2}{\beta}(a+1+j)\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{2}{\beta}\right)}

as

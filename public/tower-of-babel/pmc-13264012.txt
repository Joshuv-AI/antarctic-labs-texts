A dataset and a benchmark model for simultaneous detection of oil well drill pipes and their coupling gaps
Authors: Jing Gu, Yunqi Cheng, Ziwei Nie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractReal-time automatic visual detection of oil well drill pipes and their coupling gaps can improve drilling efficiency. Training such a deep learning system requires a large number of labelled images. Our work presents two main contributions: (1) a publicly available dataset comprising 1000 accurately labelled images of drill pipes and their coupling gaps; and (2) a cascaded segmentation model consists of two YOLOv11 modules for simultaneous detection of drill pipes and their coupling gaps. The images were collected from Hubei Jianghan Petroleum Instrument Co., Ltd. We manually labelled 500 raw images with the drill pipes. In addition, we manually annotated the coupling gaps of drill pipes in the 500 cropped images of drill pipe regions. To our knowledge, this is the first publicly available relatively largest dataset in this field, and we hope it can promote the development of related research and applications.Keywords: Drill pipes, Coupling gaps, Small object detection, YOLO

Background

Petroleum is one of the most critical resources on Earth. Most of petroleum is extracted through drilling. But even to this day, although machine learning and artificial intelligence brings convenience to even all aspects of industry, much of the drilling work cannot be done without workers. To increase oil well drilling efficiency, an image processing model is needed to control the automated drilling system.

Traditionally, oil well drilling operations were performed manually by on-site workers using remote controls. A common type of drill pipe consists of two segments: an upper and a lower part. During drilling, these two segments must remain tightly connected. Once drilling is completed, a robotic arm separates them by unscrewing the connection. To accomplish this, the worker must adjust the vertical position of the robotic arm until its gripping gap aligns precisely with the gap between the two pipe segments. If misaligned, the arm may unscrew only one part of the pipe, resulting in an unsuccessful separation, thus drilling stuck pipe accident will happen. The same alignment procedure is required when connecting the two pipe segments prior to drilling.

In current practice, monitoring cameras capture images of the pipes. An image segmentation model detects the gap between the two pipe segments and outputs the vertical position of this gap. The robotic arm then adjusts its height accordingly to locate the correct operating position. Because the gaps are relatively small objects, a cascade segmentation strategy is employed for more accurate detection. The cascade model operates in two stages: first, it segments the drill pipe from the full image; second, it segments the gap within the extracted pipe region. Finally, the actual height difference of the gap in physical coordinates can be computed from its image-based position together with auxiliary information provided by the instrument manufacturer. Achieving accurate height estimation critically depends on a high-quality dataset for training the segmentation model.

Despite the importance of a high-quality dataset for constructing such an image segmentation system, very few existing resources meet this requirement. Some studies focus on detection tasks in coal mine environments; however, coal mines and oil wells differ substantially, rendering those datasets unsuitable for training a gap segmentation model. Other works present improved methods for detecting pipes in oil wells, yet they do not offer publicly available datasets. Motivated by this scarcity, this paper introduces a high-quality dataset to fill the gap in this field.

For target recognition and localization, commonly used approaches include Satellite positioning, lidar, ultrasonic radar, infrared detection, visible light vision system, etc [[1], [2], [3]]. The first four solutions are demanding for hardware conditions, while the requirements for algorithms related to recognition and localization are relatively lower. Although visible light vision system has low demand for hardware conditions, it requires much in algorithms related to recognition and localization, because backgrounds in these systems are complicated, lighting conditions are unstable, target objects are relatively small and likely to move or be obscured.

Currently, there have been numerous studies on the detection and segmentation of drill pipes, robotic arms, and drill pipe coupling gaps. In the field of detection of drill pipes [4], realized Real-time counting and positioning on drill pipes in coal mines with lightweight YOLOv7 model [13]. In a relatively fixed and simple scenario, the detection speed can reach 80 frames per second, and the detection accuracy can reach 99.8%. However, its performance in complex environments with varying lighting conditions remains to be improved. [5] adopts a novel electromagnetic signal detection mechanism for non-destructive testing of cracks and manufacturing defects in drill pipe threads, making it possible to detect cracks at the thread crest and root, as well as identify cracks at different angles. But this approach depends on specific electromagnetic devices and is limited to the size of devices. As a result, it could only detect cracks on relatively small drill pipe threads. For large devices like oil well drill pipes, it is not feasible [6]. Detects weld seams in pipe images based on YOLOv5 [13]. However, its testing and using environments are in factories rather than real working environments [7]. Proposed a model for detecting and identifying loose connections in oil and gas pipeline systems based on neural network and finite element method, locating and quantifying the looseness of pipeline joints accurately in laboratory environment, but lack of testing results in real scenario [8]. Researched wavelet decomposition and convolutional neural network to build a defect detection and classification model for surface images of automobile pipe connectors. In [9], a model for detecting and evaluating the pose of underground drill pipes in coal mines using point cloud data is designed. This model enhances point cloud data obtained from RGB-D cameras through an adversarial generative network, achieving an average detection accuracy of 82.5% under different lighting conditions [10]. Proposed an improved version of U-Net model [11] to segment images of the iron roughneck on an offshore oil rig. But the model has high demand for image quality and is not comprehensive enough on complicate working environment of drill pipes.

After survey of related literature above, currently there is less open literature on detection and segmentation of oil well drill pipes and drill pipe coupling gaps. And in detection or segmentation of other drill pipes and drill pipe coupling gaps, the series of YOLO models [12,13] are widely used because of its effectiveness and efficiency.

Data Description

Since the cascade image segmentation system consists of pipes segmentation and coupling gaps segmentation, the dataset focuses on two categories: drill pipes and coupling gaps. It is easy for researchers to design and train a cascade image segmentation system to solve practical problems in automated drilling work with these two categories of dataset. To illustrate the characteristics of drill pipes and coupling gaps, we give various examples below in Fig. 1, Fig. 2, Fig. 3, Fig. 4. The dataset is collected from real drill environment and workshops in Hubei Jianghan Petroleum Instrument Co., Ltd, which contains 1000 images along with corresponding masks. The dataset consists of two parts. The first part contains 500 raw images with masks of drill pipes. The second part contains 500 cropped images in the regions of drill pipes, along with corresponding masks of coupling gaps on these drill pipes. Therefore, our proposed model consists of two cascaded modules. The first module employs a YOLOv11-s for drill pipe segmentation, and the second module employs a YOLOv11-m for coupling gap segmentation, as illustrated in Fig. 5. All the masks of drill pipes in raw images and coupling gaps in cropped images are manually segmented using Labelme1, which enables us to label these images with high efficiency and accuracy, providing the YOLO modules with detailed and accurate masks for training and testing.

Flow chart of the method

Below in Fig. 1, Fig. 2, Fig. 3, Fig. 4, we show some samples in the dataset. We can see that drill pipes in raw images look like slender black columns, and these vertical tubular columns occupy a very small area in the entire image, typically no more than 10%. Furthermore, the coupling gaps of drill pipes look like horizontal narrow bands that may appear in upper, middle and lower regions of the cropped images of drill pipes, typically occupying no more than 5% of the total drill pipe area.

Drill pipes

The first part of our dataset comprises 500 raw images of drill pipes along with their corresponding masks. Drill pipes are slender, black cylindrical structures whose central sections appear noticeably thicker. In real-world operational environments, each monitoring camera is typically assigned to a single drill pipe; accordingly, we assume that only one drill pipe is present in each image. Since these pipes are employed in underground petroleum extraction, they are generally oriented perpendicular to the ground.

Coupling gaps

A drill pipe consists of an upper and a lower segment, and the coupling gap is the interface between these two parts. Coupling gaps occupy a relatively small region within the overall image. For this reason, a cascade segmentation approach is adopted. Apart from their inherently small size, coupling gaps present additional detection challenges. In complex working environments, drill pipes are frequently covered with stains and oil, rendering the coupling gaps significantly more difficult to identify. Furthermore, certain drill pipes are poorly designed, exhibiting groove-like marks around the true coupling gap that closely resemble the gap itself. In such cases, the model is prone to misclassifying these marks as actual coupling gaps. To mitigate these issues, images containing such challenging conditions are deliberately introduced into the dataset to improve model robustness.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Materials and methods

To ensure the high quality of the dataset, Labelme is used during the creation of the dataset. Labelme is a graphical interactive annotation tool mainly used in the field of image segmentation. Because Labelme is based on polygon annotation, we were able to annotate irregular shaped drilling pipes and coupling gaps with high accuracy. Although annotation with Labelme is sometimes time-consuming, we were willing to take much effort to use the software because the dataset would be used for calculation and it has high demand of annotation quality. The software originally saves annotated files in the format of JSON. Subsequently, a script converted them into YOLO-compatible TXT format, in the format of YOLO image segmentation. The flow chart of detection of drill pipe couplings is illustrated in Fig. 6.

Flow chart of detection

Prior to starting to annotate, we cleaned up and pre-processed the dataset. The raw data is compromised by images and videos, and some of them were repeated. We firstly extracted images from the videos frame by frame, and removed some repeated or similar images.

To enhance the generalization ability of the dataset, we applied random gamma correction and rain-fog processing to the original images.

In the stage of training, we trained the cascade image segmentation model based on YOLOv11 segment. YOLOv11 segment is an advanced image segmentation model famous for its high accuracy and real-time performance. Moreover, YOLO supports training, validation, prediction, and export functionalities with seamless integration, which enable us to train the model directly. Because the cascade image segmentation system is required to calculate the height and operate in real time, YOLOv11 segment is the priority selection. In the selection of specific models, YOLO11s-seg is used in the first step of cascade image segmentation, while YOLO11m-seg is used in the second step, because a relatively smaller model runs in a higher speed but is not enough to segment coupling gaps on drill pipes, which is obviously much more difficult to be detected.

As shown in Fig. 6, the drill pipe coupling detection problem is modelled as an image segmentation task. The cascade image segmentation system consists of two modules, and the second module of segmentation is based on the outcome of the first module. In other word, it means the dataset of the second module is not contained in the raw data. After the raw images were pre-processed and annotated, we trained the first YOLO11s-seg model. And we use this model to segment all of the images in order to get the raw images of the second module. Because of the high accuracy of the YOLO model, these images were segmented so well that they did not need to be pre-processed again. We did the second part of annotation and trained the model of the second module. It should be noted that the image size when training the second module was set to be (1024,128).

Finally, with these two image segmentation models, the cascade image segmentation system could be built.

The benchmark

To give a benchmark for the model, a experiment on both models was conducted. Here 400 of 500 images in each module was put in the training set, 50 of them were in validation set and 50 of them were in test set.

Limitations

Although the proposed dataset addresses the lack of publicly available images concerning automated oil well drilling, and to the best of our knowledge, it is currently the largest publicly available dataset in this field, the number of 1,000 is relatively small comparing to other well-known public datasets such as CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. Besides, due to the restriction of collecting raw images, the types of oil well pipes and scenarios are limited.

Revisiting Bourgain’s probabilistic construction of solutions to the 2- d d cubic NLS
Tadahiro Oh and Yuzhao Wang

Abstract
. In a seminal paper (1996), Bourgain proved invariance of the Gibbs measure for the defocusing cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation on the two-dimensional torus by constructing local-in-time solutions in a probabilistic manner. In this note, we revisit and streamline his argument, using the random tensor estimate developed by Deng, Nahmod, and Yue (2022).

Overview

In seminal works

1.2.
Gibbs measure

Gibbs measures in statistical physics
describe thermal equilibria
and have been studied extensively.
By drawing an analogy to finite-dimensional Hamiltonian systems,
a Gibbs measure, formally given by

(1.2)

d\rho=Z^{-1}e^{-H(u)}du,

is expected to be invariant for a Hamiltonian PDE,
where
H(u)
denotes a Hamiltonian of the equation.
The study of invariant Gibbs measures
for Hamiltonian PDEs,
initiated by
Lebowitz, Rose, and Speer
[
37
]

and Bourgain

[
5
,
6
]
,
consists of two disjoint problems:

(i)

Construction of a Gibbs measure
as a probability measure on functions / distributions.

(ii)

Construction of dynamics on the support of a Gibbs measure.

In this subsection, we briefly discuss
some aspects of
the Gibbs measure construction
for
the defocusing cubic NLS (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{T}^{2}

with the Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle H(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|u|^{4}dx.

-measure,

[

Definition 2.5

Let

A tensor

Given a finite index set

\|h\|_{n_{A}}=\|h\|_{\ell^{2}_{n_{A}}}=\bigg(\sum_{n_{A}}|h_{n_{A}}|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|h\|^{2}_{n_{B}\to n_{C}}=\sup\bigg\{\sum_{n_{C}}\Big|\sum_{n_{B}}h_{n_{A}}f^{\textup{in}}_{n_{B}}\Big|^{2}:\|f^{\textup{in}}\|_{\ell^{2}_{n_{B}}}=1\bigg\},

By fixing the first coordinates

and

(which have

∎

Remark 2.11
.

\displaystyle\|h^{{\bf N},(m)}\|_{n_{1}\to nn_{2}n_{3}}

,

and

1.19
)
and (
1.21

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{x,t}\big(e^{-it\Delta}\eta_{{}_{T}}\mathbf{Q}_{N}\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{Q}_{N_{1}}z,\mathbf{Q}_{N_{2}}z,\mathbf{Q}_{N_{3}}z)\big)(n,\tau)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\text{LHS of \eqref{PS4}}\\

Appendix A
Auxiliary lemma

In this appendix,
we prove an auxiliary lemma used in the proof of Lemma
2.7
.

Lemma A.1
.

Given
r\in\mathbb{N}
, fix

y\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
.
Given
N\gg 1
, let
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{r}
be a collection of linearly independent vectors,
satisfying

|\alpha_{i}|\lesssim N
,
i=1,\dots,r
,
such that

(A.1)

\displaystyle\textup{Vol}_{r}\big(\Delta(\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r})\big)\geq\frac{1}{r!},

where
\Delta(\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r})
denotes the

r
-dimensional simplex formed by
the vectors
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}
,
and
\textup{Vol}_{r}
denotes the
r
-dimensional volume.
Suppose that

(A.2)

\displaystyle|y\cdot\alpha_{i}|\lesssim N^{a_{1}},\quad i=1,\dots,r

for some
a_{1}\geq 1
.
Then, there exists
C_{r}\gg 1
, independent of
N\gg 1
,

\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{r}
, and
a_{1}\geq 1
, such that
|y|\lesssim N^{C_{r}a_{1}}
.

Proof.

We argue by induction.
When
r=1
, we have
\alpha_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.
Then,
given
y\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying (
A.2
),
the conclusion follows from
|y\alpha_{1}|\lesssim N^{a_{1}}
and
|\alpha_{1}|\geq 1
.

Now, suppose that the conclusion holds for some
r\geq 1
.
Let
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r+1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{r+1}
be a collection of linearly independent vectors,
satisfying

|\alpha_{i}|\leq c_{0}N
,
i=1,\dots,r+1
,
for some
c_{0}>0

such that

(A.3)

\displaystyle\textup{Vol}_{r+1}\big(\Delta(\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r+1})\big)\geq\frac{1}{(r+1)!},

Define the hyperplane
\mathcal{P}
by

\mathcal{P}={\rm span}\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{r+1}.

Given
y\in\mathbb{R}^{r+1}
satisfying

(A.4)

\displaystyle|y\cdot\alpha_{i}|\leq c_{1}N^{a_{1}},\quad i=1,\dots,r+1,

for some
c_{1}>0
,
write it as

\displaystyle y=y^{\parallel}+y^{\perp},

where
y^{\parallel}\in\mathcal{P}
and
y^{\perp}\perp\mathcal{P}
.
From (
A.4
), we have

(A.5)

\displaystyle|y^{\parallel}\cdot\alpha_{i}|=|y\cdot\alpha_{i}|\leq c_{1}N^{a_{1}}

for
i=1,\dots,r
.
Now,
recall that the volume of a
(r+1)
-dimensional simplex
A
is given by

(A.6)

\displaystyle\textup{Vol}_{r+1}(A)=\frac{1}{r+1}\cdot(\textup{base volume})\cdot(\textup{height}).

Then,
from (
A.6
), (
A.3
), and
|\alpha_{r+1}|\leq c_{0}N
, we see that

\displaystyle\textup{Vol}_{r}\big(\Delta(\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r})\big)\geq\frac{r+1}{|\alpha_{r+1}|}\textup{Vol}_{r+1}\big(\Delta(\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r+1})\big)\geq(c_{0}N)^{-1}\cdot\frac{1}{r!}.

By setting
\beta_{i}=c_{0}^{\frac{1}{r}}N^{\frac{1}{r}}\alpha_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,r
,
we see that
\beta_{i}
’s are linearly independent,

|\beta_{i}|\leq c_{0}^{1+\frac{1}{r}}N^{1+\frac{1}{r}}=:c_{0}M

for any

i=1,\dots,r
, and

\textup{Vol}_{r}\big(\Delta(\{\beta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r})\big)\geq\frac{1}{r!}
.
Moreover, from (
A.5
) with
a_{1}\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle|y^{\parallel}\cdot\beta_{i}|\leq c_{1}c_{0}^{\frac{1}{r}}N^{\frac{1}{r}}N^{a_{1}}\leq c_{1}M^{a_{1}}

for any
i=1,\dots,r
.
Hence, by the inductive hypothesis, we conclude that

(A.7)

\displaystyle|y^{\parallel}|\lesssim M^{C_{r}a_{1}}\sim N^{C_{r}(1+\frac{1}{r})a_{1}}

for some
C_{r}\gg 1
.

It remains to show that
|y^{\perp}|\lesssim N^{C_{r}^{\prime}a_{1}}
for some
C_{r}^{\prime}>0
.
We prove this by contradiction.
Suppose that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
there exists
N=N(k)\gg 1
and
y_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{r+1}
, satisfying
(
A.2
), such that

(A.8)

\displaystyle|y_{k}^{\perp}|\gg N^{ka_{1}},

where
y_{k}^{\perp}
is the component of
y_{k}
orthogonal to the hyperplane
\mathcal{P}
.

Recall that
\alpha_{r+1}
is not on the hyperplane
\mathcal{P}
.
Write
\alpha_{r+1}=\alpha_{r+1}^{\parallel}+\alpha_{r+1}^{\perp}
,
where

\alpha_{r+1}^{\parallel}\in\mathcal{P}
and
\alpha_{r+1}^{\perp}\perp\mathcal{P}
.
Then,
we claim that

(A.9)

\displaystyle|\alpha_{r+1}^{\perp}|={\rm dist}(\alpha_{r+1},\mathcal{P})\gtrsim N^{-r}.

Since
|\alpha_{i}|\lesssim N
,
i=1,\dots,r
,
we have

(A.10)

\displaystyle\textup{Vol}_{r}\big(\Delta(\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r})\big)\lesssim N^{r}.

Then, (
A.9
) follows from (
A.6
)
and (
A.10
).

From (
A.8
) and (
A.9
), we have

(A.11)

\displaystyle|y_{k}^{\perp}\cdot\alpha_{r+1}|=|y_{k}^{\perp}||\alpha^{\perp}_{r+1}|\gtrsim N^{ka_{1}}N^{-r}\gg N^{\frac{1}{2}ka_{1}}

for any
N\gg 1
, provided that
k>2r
.
Hence,
from (
A.7
) and (
A.11
) with
|\alpha_{r+1}|\lesssim N
,
we see that there exist
c_{2}\gg c_{3}>0
such that

|y_{k}\cdot\alpha_{r+1}|\geq|y_{k}^{\perp}\cdot\alpha_{r+1}|-|y_{k}^{\parallel}\cdot\alpha_{r+1}|\geq c_{2}N^{\frac{1}{2}ka_{1}}-c_{3}N^{C_{r}(1+\frac{1}{r})a_{1}+1}\gg N^{a_{1}}

for any
N\gg 1
, provided that

k>2C_{r}(1+\frac{1}{r})+2
,
which is a contradiction to (
A.4
).
Therefore,
there exists
C_{r+1}^{\prime}>0
such that,
given any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{r+1}

satisfying (
A.4
),
we have

|y^{\perp}|\lesssim N^{C_{r+1}^{\prime}a_{1}}
.
Together with (
A.7
), we conclude that
there exists
C_{r+1}>0
such that

|y|\lesssim N^{C_{r+1}a_{1}}
for
any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{r+1}

satisfying (
A.4
).
We note that, from the discussion above,
it suffices to choose
C_{r+1}^{\prime}>\max(2r,2C_{r}(1+\frac{1}{r})+2)
,
which shows that the choice of
C^{\prime}_{r+1}
(and hence of
C_{r+1}
)
is independent of
N\gg 1
,

\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r+1}
, and
a_{1}\geq 1
.

Finally, by induction, we
concludes the proof of Lemma
A.1
.
∎

Remark A.2
.

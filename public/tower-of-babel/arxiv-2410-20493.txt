Euler-flocking system with nonlocal dissipation in 1D: periodic entropy solutions
D. Amadori, F. A. Chiarello, C. Christoforou

Abstract
. We consider a hydrodynamic model of flocking-type with all-to-all interaction kernel in a periodic domain in one-space dimension with linear pressure term. The main result is the global existence of periodic entropy weak solutions, for periodic initial data having finite total variation and initial density bounded away from zero.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 35L65; 35B40; Secondary: 35D30; 35Q70; 35L45.

1.
Introduction

We are interested in weak solutions to the hydrodynamic model of flocking-type in one-space dimension that takes the form

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho\mathtt{v})=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho\mathtt{v})+\partial_{x}(\rho\mathtt{v}^{2}+p(\rho))=\int_{\mathbb{T}_{L}}\psi(x-y)\rho(x,t)\rho(y,t)(\mathtt{v}(y,t)-\mathtt{v}(x,t))dy\end{cases}

where
(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}_{\ell}\times[0,+\infty)
, with
\mathbb{T}_{\ell}:=\mathbb{R}/{\ell\mathbb{Z}}
and
\ell>0
. Here
\rho(x,t)
represents the density,

\mathtt{v}(x,t)
the velocity,
\psi
the alignment kernel and
p(\rho)
the pressure term. We also assign the initial condition

(1.2)

(\rho(x,0),\mathtt{v}(x,0))=(\rho_{0}(x),\mathtt{v}_{0}(x)).

and introduce
m=\rho\mathtt{v}
as the momentum with
m_{0}:=\rho_{0}\mathtt{v}_{0}
its initial data.

Self-organization is a interesting topic that has received a lot of attention in many respects including the mathematical modeling on this topic.
Standard examples arise from phenomena in biology such as flock of birds, a swarm of bacteria or a school of fish, but one can also meet
self-organized systems in other sciences as long as the emergence behavior is present in the system.
Many mathematical models have been introduced to describe the emergence of flocking for such systems
and most of them have arised from the pioneering work of Cucker and Smale
[
11
]
.
See
[
8
,
17
]
and the references therein.
There is so far a broad range of studies on the subject from the discrete particle level up to the hydrodynamic formulation.

In this paper, we study system (
1.1
) that has been derived in
[
15
]
as the hydrodynamic limit
of the kinetic Cucker-Smale (CS) flocking model with the viscosity term and strong local alignment tending to zero.
Actually the alignment term was derived from the Motsch-Tadmor operator as a singular limit that improved CS model in small scales.
In
[
15
]
, it is shown the convergence of weak solutions to the kinetic equation to strong solutions of the Euler system (
1.1
)
with pressure

{\bf(P)}\qquad\qquad p(\rho):=\alpha^{2}\rho\,,\qquad\alpha>0\;.

We remark that most studies on hydrodynamic models for flocking are on the pressureless Euler-alignment system.
We refer the reader to
[
14
,
15
,
13
,
16
]
for results on the Euler-alignment system
and point out that for the system with pressure, smooth and space-periodic solutions are obtained in
[
9
]
.
In
[
2
,
3
]
, global entropy weak solutions to this pressured system have been established but in the setting of the Cauchy problem
with initial data confined in a bounded domain and unconditional flocking has been captured in this weak framework.

The aim of this paper is to prove the existence of space-periodic entropy weak solutions to (
1.1
) defined for all times,
for any initial data of bounded variation over the period and with initial density bounded away from zero. We assume that

(1.3)

\inf_{\mathbb{T}_{\ell}}\,\rho_{0}>0\,,\qquad\tv\left\{\left(\rho_{0},\mathtt{v}_{0}\right);\mathbb{T}_{\ell}\right\}<+\infty,

where
\tv
denotes the total variation on the period.

Here we provide the definition of entropy weak solution to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).

Definition 1
.

Set
\Omega:=\mathbb{T}_{\ell}\times[0,+\infty)
and assume
\left(\rho_{0},\mathtt{v}_{0}\right)\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{T}_{\ell}\right)
with
\rho_{0}(x)>0
for a.e.
x

and
\psi\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
.

Let
(\rho,\mathtt{v}):[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{T}_{\ell}\to(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
and set
m=\rho\mathtt{v}
.
We say that the
(\rho,\mathtt{v})
is an entropy weak solution of the problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
)
if the following holds:

•

the map
t\mapsto(\rho,\mathtt{v})(\cdot,t)\in L^{1}\left(\mathbb{T}_{\ell}\right)
is continuous;

•

for every test function
\phi\in C^{1}_{\text{c}}(\Omega)

it holds that

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega}\Big(\rho\,\phi_{t}(x,t)+\rho\mathtt{v}\,\phi_{x}(x,t)\Big)dx\,dt+\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\ell}}\rho_{0}(x)\phi(x,0)dx=0,

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega}\Big(\rho\mathtt{v}\,\phi_{t}(x,t)+(\rho\mathtt{v}^{2}+p(\rho))\,\phi_{x}(x,t)\Big)dx\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad-\iint_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\ell}}\psi(x-y)\rho(x,t)\rho(y,t)(\mathtt{v}(y,t)-\mathtt{v}(x,t))dy\Big)\phi(x,t)dx\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\ell}}m_{0}(x)\phi(x,0)dx=0;

•

for every pair of smooth entropy–entropy flux
(\eta,q)
,
that is,

\left(-\mathtt{v}^{2}+p^{\prime}\right)\eta_{m}=q_{\rho}\,,\qquad\eta_{\rho}+2\mathtt{v}\eta_{m}=q_{m}\,,

with
\eta
convex, it holds

(1.4)

\partial_{t}\eta(\rho,m)+\partial_{x}q(\rho,m)\leq\eta_{m}\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\ell}}\psi(x-y)\left(\rho(x,t)m(y,t)-\rho(y,t)m(x,t)\right)dy

in the sense of distributions on
\Omega
.

From Definition
1
one can prove that an entropy weak solution conserves the total mass
and the total momentum
over the period
{\mathbb{T}_{\ell}}
, i.e. defining

\mathcal{M}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\ell}}\rho(x,t)\,dx,\quad\mathcal{M}_{1}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\ell}}m(x,t)\,dx,\qquad t\geq 0,

we get

\mathcal{M}(t)=\mathcal{M}(0)=\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\ell}}\rho_{0}(x)\,dx,\qquad\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)=\mathcal{M}_{1}(0)=\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\ell}}m_{0}(x)\,dx,\qquad\forall\,t\geq 0,

where we used that Definition
1
implies
\rho(\cdot,0)=\rho_{0},\,m(\cdot,0)=m_{0}.

Let
M
and
M_{1}
denote the constant values of
\mathcal{M}(t)
and
\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)
respectively.

Throughout this paper the all-to-all interaction is assumed, i.e. the kernel
\psi
is constant. Without loss of generality we assume:

{\bf(\Psi)}\qquad\qquad\psi(x)\equiv 1\,.

The main result of this paper, which is the global existence of entropy weak solutions to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), is stated here.

Theorem 1
.

Assume
(P)
,
\bf{(\Psi)}
and that the initial data satisfy (
1.3
).
Then the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) admits an entropy weak solution
(\rho,\mathtt{v})
in the sense of Definition

, with
(\rho,\mathtt{v})(\cdot,t)\in\hbox{{BV}}\,(\mathbb{T}_{\ell})

\forall\,t,
and it holds

\tv\left\{\left(\rho,\mathtt{v}\right)(t);\mathbb{T}_{\ell}\right\}\leq C,\qquad\forall\,\,t>0,

where
C=C(q)
and

q\doteq\frac{1}{2}\tv{\{\ln(\rho_{0})\}}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\tv{\{\mathtt{v}_{0}\}}\,.

The analysis follows the strategy developed in
[
2
]
and adapted to the periodic case.
We transform the problem in Lagrangian coordinates in the spirit of Wagner
[
18
]

and construct a front-tracking approximate solution similarly as in
[
2
]
.
The presence of vacuum in
[
2
]
raised additional obstacles that are not here, such as loss of strict hyperbolicity
and concentration terms of Dirac deltas on the extended momentum in the notion of weak solution.
In this paper, the periodicity of the problem allows the wave fronts when touching the boundaries in

to enter the domain from the other side,
while in
[
2
]
, the boundaries in Lagrangian coordinates act as non-reflecting, and, therefore, the fronts there are absorbed from the boundaries.
These are some of the differences between the construction here and in

]
.

It is worth spending few words on some papers in literature, such as
[
9
]
, in which the author studies the global existence and uniqueness of strong solutions for the compressible isothermal Euler system with nonlocal dissipation under suitable smallness and regularity assumptions on the initial data, and its large-time behavior showing the fluid density and the velocity converge to its averages exponentially fast as time goes to infinity.
In
[
7
]
the authors study an Euler compressible system characterized by the presence on the right-hand side of a damping term, a nonlocal alignment term and a nonlocal repulsion/attraction forces term. The goal of that paper is to establish the global-in-time existence of finite-energy entropy solutions for this system without restriction on the size of initial data, using the vanishing viscosity limit for the strong solutions of the compressible Navier-Stokes equations.
Moreover, in a very recent work
[
10
]
the authors rigorously derive the isentropic Euler-alignment system with singular communication weights, considering a kinetic BGK-alignment model consisting of a kinetic BGK-type equation with a singular Cucker-Smale alignment force.
In the present paper we focus on establishing the global existence of entropy weak solutions that are periodic with initial data away from the vacuum
and allowing any size in
BV
norm. In
[
6
]
, the authors analyse the well-posedness of spatially periodic solutions of specific

[

.

2.
Set up in Lagrangian coordinates

First, due to the conservation of mass and momentum, and hypothesis
{\bf(\Psi)}
, system (
1.1
) becomes

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho\mathtt{v})=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho\mathtt{v})+\partial_{x}(\rho\mathtt{v}^{2}+p(\rho))=\rho M_{1}-M\rho\mathtt{v},\end{cases}

Without loss of generality, we can reduce to the case
M_{1}=0
thanks to the following change of the independent variables
(x,t)
and of
\mathtt{v}
:

x\mapsto x-\bar{\mathtt{v}}t,\quad\mathtt{v}\mapsto\mathtt{v}-\bar{\mathtt{v}}

where
\bar{\mathtt{v}}=M_{1}/M.
This allows to reduce to the case
M_{1}=0,
because in these new variables, that we call again
(x,t),
the average of the momentum is zero, i.e.

(2.1)

\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\ell}}m(x,t)dx=0,\quad\forall t\geq 0.

Hence we get

(2.2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho\mathtt{v})=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho\mathtt{v})+\partial_{x}(\rho\mathtt{v}^{2}+p(\rho))=-M\rho\mathtt{v}\,,\end{cases}

with initial condition

(2.3)

(\rho(x,0),\mathtt{v}(x,0))=(\rho_{0}(x),\mathtt{v}_{0}(x)-\bar{\mathtt{v}})\,.

In these new variables, the entropy inequality (
1.4
) in Definition
1
rewrites as

(2.4)

\partial_{t}\eta(\rho,m)+\partial_{x}q(\rho,m)\leq-M\eta_{m}m.

Next, we reformulate the system from Eulerian to Lagrangian coordinates. More precisely, we use the mapping

\displaystyle(x,t)

\displaystyle\mapsto(y=\chi(x,t),\,\tau=t),

\displaystyle\chi(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x}\rho(x^{\prime},t)\,dx^{\prime}\in[0,M]\,,\qquad{x\in[0,\ell]},

and set the variables

(2.5)

\displaystyle u(y,t)=\dfrac{1}{\rho(x,t)},\qquad\qquad v(y,t)=\mathtt{v}(x,t).

Then the derivatives satisfy

\partial_{\tau}=\partial_{t}+\mathtt{v}\partial_{x},\qquad\qquad\partial_{y}=\tfrac{1}{\rho}\partial_{x}=u\partial_{x},\,

and system (
2.2
) reduces to

(2.6)

\begin{cases}\partial_{\tau}u-\partial_{y}v=0,\\
\partial_{\tau}v+\partial_{y}(\alpha^{2}/u)=-Mv,\end{cases}

with
(y,t)\in{\mathbb{T}}_{M}\times[0,\infty).
By periodicity, we have
(u,v)(0+,t)=(u,v)(M-,t).

From (
2.3
), the initial condition is given by

(2.7)

(u,v)(\cdot,0)=(u_{0},v_{0}):=(\rho_{0}^{-1},\mathtt{v}_{0}-\bar{\mathtt{v}})\,.

From now on, we will consider the interval
[0,M)
representing the torus
\mathbb{T}_{M}
.

Recalling (
1.3
), the initial data
\rho_{0}
is bounded from above and away from 0;
this ensures that the change of variable

x\mapsto\chi(x,0)=\int_{0}^{x}\rho_{0}(x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}

is bi-Lipschitz continuous from
[0,\ell)
to
[0,M)
.

Therefore, the initial condition (
2.7
), together with (
1.3
),
lead to:

(2.8)

(u_{0},v_{0})\in\hbox{{BV}}\,(\mathbb{T}_{M})\,,\qquad\essinf_{\mathbb{T}_{M}}u_{0}>0\,,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{T}_{M}}v_{0}(y)\,dy=0\,,\qquad{\int_{\mathbb{T}_{M}}u_{0}(y)\,dy=\ell}\,.

We have thus reformulated our problem in Lagrangian variables and the aim is to construct an entropy weak solution
(u(y,t),v(y,t))
to the problem (
2.6
)-(
2.7
) that conserves mass and momentum.
By entropy weak solution to (
2.6
)-(
2.7
) we mean

Definition 2
.

Assume
\left(u_{0},v_{0}\right)\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{T}_{M}\right)
with
u_{0}(y)>0
for a.e.
y
.
Assume that
\psi\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
.
Let
(u,v):\mathbb{T}_{M}\times[0,+\infty)\to(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}.

We say that
(u,v)

is an entropy weak solution of the problem (
2.6
)-(
2.7
)
if the following holds:

•

the map
t\mapsto(u,v)(\cdot,t)\in L^{1}\left(\mathbb{T}_{M}\right)
is continuous;

•

(u,v)(\cdot,0)=\displaystyle{\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}}(u,v)(\cdot,t)=(u_{0},v_{0});

•

(u,v)
is a distributional solution to (
2.6
)-(
2.7
);

•

for every pair of smooth entropy–entropy flux
(\tilde{\eta},\tilde{q})
,
with
\tilde{\eta}
convex, it holds

(2.9)

\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}(u,v)+\partial_{x}\tilde{q}(u,v)\leq-M\tilde{\eta}_{v}v,

in the sense of distributions on
\mathbb{T}_{M}\times(0,+\infty)
.

Thus the proof of the following existence result for the problem (
2.6
)-(
2.7
) is the theme of this section.

Theorem 2
.

Consider the problem (
2.6
)-(
2.7
) and assume (
2.8
).
Then, there exists an entropy weak solution
(u,v)
in the sense of Definition
2
.
Moreover, there exist positive constants
u_{inf}
,
u_{sup}
,
\widetilde{C}_{0}
such that for all

0<u_{inf}\leq u(y,t)\leq u_{sup}\,,\quad|v(y,t)|\leq\widetilde{C}_{0}\qquad\mbox{for a.e.}\ y\,{\in\mathbb{T}_{M}}\;.

The following analysis prepares the ground for the proof of this theorem that is given in Subsection

2.1.
Characteristic curves

The characteristic speeds of system (
2.6
) are given by

(2.11)

\lambda_{1,2}=\pm\frac{\alpha}{u},\qquad u>0,

with the indices corresponding to each family
j=1,2
. The rarefaction-shock curves, issued at a point
(u_{l},v_{l})
are:

•

curve of the first family

(2.12)

\begin{cases}u<u_{l},\quad v=v_{l}-\alpha\left(\sqrt{\frac{u_{l}}{u}-\frac{u}{u_{l}}}\right),\\
u>u_{l},\quad v=v_{l}+\alpha\ln\left(\frac{u}{u_{l}}\right);\end{cases}

•

curve of the second family

(2.13)

\begin{cases}u<u_{l},\quad v=v_{l}-\alpha\ln\left(\frac{u}{u_{l}}\right),\\
u>u_{l},\quad v=v_{l}-\alpha\left(\sqrt{\frac{u}{u_{l}}-\frac{u_{l}}{u}}\right).\end{cases}

The curves (
2.12
), (
2.13
) can be parametrized as follows:

u\to v(u;u_{l},v_{l})=v_{l}+2\alpha\,h(\varepsilon_{j}),\quad u>0,\quad j=1,2

with

\varepsilon_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{u}{u_{l}}\right),\qquad\varepsilon_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{u_{l}}{u}\right)\,,\qquad h(\varepsilon)=\begin{cases}\varepsilon&\varepsilon\geq 0,\\
\sinh\varepsilon&\varepsilon<0.\end{cases}

We refer to
\varepsilon_{j},\,j=1,2
as the strength of a wave of the
j-
characteristic family.

For any pair of left and right states
(u_{l},v_{l})
,
(u_{r},v_{r})
, with
u_{l,r}>0
, the Riemann problem to the homogeneous system
of (
2.6
) has a unique solution that consists of simple Lax waves and the
corresponding strengths
\varepsilon_{j}
be the strength of the
j=1
,
2
wave satisfy

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{2}-\varepsilon_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{u_{l}}{u_{r}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{p_{r}}{p_{l}}\right)

\displaystyle h(\varepsilon_{1})+h(\varepsilon_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{r}-v_{l}}{2\alpha}

where
p_{r,l}=p(u_{r,l})=\alpha^{2}/u_{r,l}
and

(2.14)

|\varepsilon_{1}|+|\varepsilon_{2}|\leq\max\left\{\frac{1}{2}\left|\ln\left(\frac{u_{r}}{u_{l}}\right)\right|,\frac{|v_{r}-v_{l}|}{2\alpha}\right\}\,.

See for instance
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
.

3.
Global existence of weak solutions

In the following subsections, we lay the groundwork for the proof of Theorem
2
.
More precisely, we construct approximate solutions to system (
2.6
) using the front-tracking algorithm
in conjunction with the operator splitting, introduce Lyapunov functionals that allow us to obtain uniform bounds on the total variation
and prove uniform
L^{\infty}
bounds on
(u,v)
as well. The compactness of the sequence yields the convergence to the limit
which is the solution to (
2.6
).

3.1.
Front-tracking approximate solutions

In this subsection we construct the approximate solutions
(u^{\nu},v^{\nu})
, for
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
,
to the initial value problem (
2.6
) with
M
-periodicity, following the approach adopted in
[
2
]
for a free-boundary problem.
The construction combines the wave-front tracking algorithm with the operator splitting scheme to treat the source term.

Below we describe the construction in three steps:

Step 1.

Fix
\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and choose approximate periodic initial data
(u^{\nu}_{0},v^{\nu}_{0})
, which are piecewise constant and satisfy

\tv\{\left(u^{\nu}_{0},v^{\nu}_{0}\right);\mathbb{T}_{M}\}\leq\tv\{\left(u_{0},v_{0}\right);\mathbb{T}_{M}\}\,,\qquad\|\left(u^{\nu}_{0},v^{\nu}_{0}\right)-\left(u_{0},v_{0}\right)\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{\nu}\,,

and assign these as the initial data:

u^{\nu}(y,0):=u_{0}^{\nu}(y),\quad v^{\nu}(y,0):=v_{0}^{\nu}(y).

As a consequence of (
2.8
), one has that

(3.1)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}_{M}}v^{\nu}_{0}(y)\,dy\right|\leq\frac{M}{\nu}\to 0\qquad\nu\to\infty\,,

and

(3.2)

{\essinf_{(0,M)}u^{\nu}_{0}\geq\essinf_{(0,M)}u_{0}-\frac{1}{\nu}>0\,,\qquad\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}_{M}}u^{\nu}_{0}(y)\,dy-\ell\right|\leq\frac{M}{\nu}\to 0\qquad\nu\to\infty\,.}

Step 2.

Fix two parameters: the time step
\Delta t=\Delta t_{\nu}>0
and the threshold
\eta=\eta_{\nu}>0
.
Having those, we set the times
t^{n}=n\Delta t
,
n=0,1,2,\dots

and we use the parameter
\eta
to control the size of rarefaction fronts and the errors in the speeds of shocks and rarefaction fronts. This would be made clear in the next step. Allow now both parameters
\Delta t_{\nu}
,
\eta_{\nu}
to converge to 0 as
\nu\to\infty
.

Step 3.

The approximate solution
(u^{\nu},v^{\nu})
of (
2.6
) is obtained as follows:

(i)

On each time interval
[t^{n-1},t^{n})
, the approximate solution
(u^{\nu},v^{\nu})
is the
\eta
-front tracking approximate solution to the homogeneous system of (
2.6
) with initial data

(u^{\nu}(y,t^{n-1}+),v^{\nu}(y,t^{n-1}+)).

We recall that
\eta
-front tracking approximate solutions (see Def. 7.1 in
[
5
]
) are piecewise constant functions with discontinuities along finitely many lines in the
(y,t)
half-plane and interactions between two incoming fronts. Indeed, by modifying the wave speeds slightly, by a quantity less than
\eta
, we can assume that there are only interactions of two incoming fronts.
To give more details of this construction, solve the Riemann problems at
t=t^{n-1}
around each discontinuity point of the initial data
(u^{\nu}(y,t^{n-1}+),v^{\nu}(y,t^{n-1}+))
.
Then, retain shocks as obtained in the Riemann solution, but approximate rarefactions by fans of fronts each one of them consisting of two constant states connected by a single discontinuity of strength less than
\eta
. More precisely, each rarefaction of strength
\varepsilon
is approximated by
N
rarefaction fronts, with
N=[\varepsilon/\eta]+1
, each one of strength
\varepsilon/N<\eta
and with speed to be equal to the characteristic speed of the right state.
Then prolong the approximate solution within
[t^{n-1},t^{n})
until some wave fronts interact at a point, or some wave reaches one of the boundaries of
\mathbb{T}_{M},\,y=0
and
y=M
.

- At the interaction, there are exactly two incoming fronts as mentioned and the approximate solution is prolonged by solving the Riemann problem and approximating rarefactions, if they arise in the solution, as described above.

- At times
t=\tau
that a front meets the boundaries, i.e.
y=0
or
y=M
, then that wave front enters back into the domain from the other boundary
y=M
or
y=0
, respectively, for
t=\tau+
.

(ii)

At the time
t=t^{n}
, apply the operator splitting technique to system (
2.6
) and define

(3.3)

u^{\nu}(y,t^{n}+)=u^{\nu}(y,t^{n}-)\,,\qquad v^{\nu}(y,t^{n}+)=v^{\nu}(y,t^{n}-)\left(1-M\Delta t\right)

taking into account the source term. As in (i), by possibly changing slightly the speed of some fronts,
there is no interaction of fronts at the times steps
t^{n}
.

(iii)

Proceed by iteration of (i) and (ii), with
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

The approximate solution is defined as long as the iteration at Step 3 can be carried out and in particular, as long as the interactions do not accumulate.
As in
[
2
]
, this version of front tracking algorithm for
2\times 2
systems does not require the presence of non-physical fronts:
as long as the solution exists, waves fronts do not accumulate.

3.2.
The linear functional

We define the Lyapunov functional associated with the approximate solution
(u^{\nu},v^{\nu})
to system (
2.6
). For convenience, we drop the index
\nu
from here and on.

Let
t
be a time different from any
t^{n}
and at which no wave interaction occurs, and let
\{y_{j}\}_{1}^{N}
be the discontinuity points of
(u,v)(\cdot,t)
in
[0,M)
, with

(3.4)

0\leq y_{1}<y_{2}<\ldots<y_{N(t)}<M\,,

for some integer
N=N(t)
. Let
\varepsilon_{j}
be the corresponding strength of the front located at
y_{j}
. Then we define the
linear functional

(3.5)

L(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{N(t)}|\varepsilon_{j}|\,.

Similarly to
[
2
, Lemma 3.1]
, the following relation between
L(t)
and the total variation in
y
of the approximate solution holds.

Lemma 1
.

[
2
, Lemma 3.1]

The following identities hold,

Uniform bounds and global existence

In the next lemma, we show that the supremum and infimum of

Tidal effects in gravitational and scalar waveforms and fluxes to one post-Newtonian order in massless scalar-tensor theories
Eve Dones, Laura Bernard

Abstract
Within scalar-tensor (ST) theories, neutron stars in binary systems experience tidal deformations caused by both their companion and the scalar field. These deformations are strongly correlated to the star’s internal structure and composition. Accurately modeling their imprint on the emitted gravitational waves will be essential for interpreting the high-precision data expected from future detectors and for disentangling potential signatures of modified gravity from those arising due to the properties of neutron star matter. Using the post-Newtonian multipolar-post-Minkowskian formalism adapted to ST theories, and working within the adiabatic approximation, we compute the tidal corrections to the gravitational and scalar energy fluxes, and to the waveform phasing, at the first post-Newtonian (PN) order beyond the standard quadrupole radiation in general relativity. This corresponds to 2PN order beyond the leading-order dipolar tidal contribution. At this accuracy, three independent types of tidal deformability (scalar, tensorial, and mixed scalar-tensorial) contribute to the signal. We also derive the gravitational and scalar waveform amplitude modes, including the memory ones, to the same accuracy.



I
Introduction

I.1
Context

With the advent of gravitational wave astronomy, there has been a renewal of interest in the modeling of gravitational waves in alternative theories of gravity. Such efforts have concerned both analytical treatment of the early inspiral as well as numerical ones to describe the merger of compact binaries. In the latter case, the first simulations were done in the context of binary neutrons stars in scalar-tensor theories, illustrating the phenomenon of dynamical scalarization
Barausse et al. 2013
;
Palenzuela et al. 2014
. More recently, advances in the mathematical understanding of gravity theories beyond general relativity (GR)
Kovács and Reall 2020
have allowed to perform full numerical simulations in some specific theories belonging to the Horndeski or Einstein-scalar-Gauss-Bonnet classes
East and Ripley 2021
;
Cayuso et al. 2023
;
Corman et al. 2024
.

On the analytical side, significant progress has also been made in modeling inspiral waveforms within ST theories. Most of these works have been performed in the framework of the post-Newtonian multipolar post-Minkowskian (PN-MPM) formalism
Blanchet 2014
, a perturbative approach based on an expansion in small velocities and for weak gravitational fields.
The dynamics of compact binaries is currently known up to 3PN order
Mirshekari and Will 2013
;
Bernard 2018
;
Bernard 2019
and the radiative sector has been tackled in different groups. Using the direct integration of the relaxed Einstein equations (DIRE) formalism, Lang derived the tensorial waveform to 2PN order, the scalar waveform to
1.5
PN order and the total energy flux to 1PN order, all beyond the standard quadrupole radiation in GR, and for binaries on generic orbits
Lang 2014
;
Lang 2015
. In
Sennett et al. 2016
, the authors specialized these results to binaries on quasi-circular orbits. The scalar waveform amplitude modes were derived for the first time up to
1.5
PN order in
Bernard et al. 2022
, along with the
1.5
PN total energy flux, using the PN-MPM formalism. More recently, the results of
Bernard et al. 2022
have been extended to systems on quasi-elliptical orbits
Trestini 2024a
;
Trestini 2024b
.

Additionally, the adiabatic tidal deformation of neutron stars in ST theories was first investigated within a PN framework in
Bernard 2020
, where leading-order dipolar tidal corrections were included in both the dynamical and radiative sectors. In
Creci et al. 2023
, the authors emphasized that in ST theories, three independent tidal deformabilities are needed to fully characterize the response to external tidal fields: scalar, tensorial, and mixed scalar-tensorial deformabilities. All three types of deformation are encountered at the second PN order beyond the leading-order dipolar tidal effect. Hence, the first step to obtain gravitational waveforms that include these three types of effects consists in deriving the dynamics at the next-to-next-to-leading (or 2PN) order beyond the leading (3PN) tidal contribution. This was the subject of our first paper
Bernard et al. 2024
, where we cross-checked our results with the alternative but equivalent post-Newtonian effective field theory formalism
Goldberger and Rothstein 2006
. In parallel, the leading-order dipolar and quadrupolar scalar effects, as well as the leading-order quadrupolar tensorial and scalar-tensorial effects have been studied in both the dynamical and radiative sectors in
Creci et al. 2024
.

In the present work, we extend our previous results by including the tidal corrections to the gravitational and scalar energy fluxes and the GW phasing at 2PN order beyond the leading-order dipolar tidal correction. In terms of fluxes, this corresponds to 1PN order beyond the leading-order quadrupolar radiation in GR. We also complete the full gravitational and scalar waveforms consistently to 1.5PN order beyond the leading-order dipolar effect (or 1PN order beyond the leading-order quadrupolar radiation in GR). In addition, we compute for the first time the memory
m=0
modes, which are required to achieve the desired accuracy in the gravitational waveform.

II
Massless scalar-tensor theories beyond GR

II.1
The action

In this section, we summarize the framework in which we have studied the tidal effects on the dynamics of an inspiralling compact binary system in scalar-tensor theories of gravity
Bernard et al. 2024
. The starting point is the generalized version of Brans-Dicke theories where a scalar field
\phi
is non-minimally coupled to the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
. The theory is characterized by the coupling function
\omega(\phi)
. In order to decouple scalar and tensor degrees of freedom, we perform a conformal transformation to go from the physical metric to the conformal one, defined by

\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}=\varphi\,g_{\mu\nu}\,,\qquad\varphi=\frac{\phi}{\phi_{0}}\,.

(3)

In particuliar, this conformal transformation implies that
g^{\mu\nu}=\varphi\,\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}
and
\sqrt{-\tilde{g}}=\varphi^{2}\sqrt{-g}
. After some reshuffling, the gravitational action can be displayed in the Einstein frame as

S^{\text{GF}}_{\mathrm{ST}}=\frac{c^{3}\phi_{0}}{16\pi G}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\sqrt{-\tilde{g}}\left[\tilde{R}-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{\Gamma}^{\mu}\tilde{\Gamma}^{\nu}-\frac{3+2\omega(\phi)}{2\varphi^{2}}\tilde{g}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\varphi\partial_{\beta}\varphi\right]+S_{\mathrm{m}}\left(\mathfrak{m},g_{\alpha\beta}\right)\,,

(4)

where
\tilde{R}
is the Ricci scalar related to the conformal metric. A gauge fixing term has been introduced,
\propto\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{\Gamma}^{\mu}\tilde{\Gamma}^{\nu}
with
\tilde{\Gamma}^{\nu}\equiv\tilde{g}^{\rho\sigma}\tilde{\Gamma}^{\nu}_{\rho\sigma}
, to enforce working in the harmonic gauge.

As for the matter part, we supplemented the classical matter action for point particles by an effective tidal action describing the response of the internal structure of the compact objects to scalar, tensorial and scalar-tensorial induced tidal interactions
Bernard et al. 2024
;
Creci et al. 2024
. Hence, the matter action is decomposed as
S_{\rm m}=S_{\rm pp}+S_{\rm tidal}
with

\displaystyle S_{\rm pp}

\displaystyle=-c\sum_{a=1,2}\,\int\mathrm{d}\tau_{a}\,m_{a}\left(\phi\right)\,,

(5a)

\displaystyle S_{\rm tidal}

\displaystyle=-\frac{c}{2}\,\sum_{a=1,2}\,\int\mathrm{d}\tau_{a}\,\biggl\{\lambda_{a}\left(\phi\right)\left(\nabla_{\alpha}^{\perp}\varphi\right)_{a}\,\left(\nabla^{\alpha}_{\perp}\varphi\right)_{a}+\frac{1}{2}\mu_{a}\left(\phi\right)\left(\nabla_{\alpha\beta}^{\perp}\varphi\right)_{a}\,\left(\nabla^{\alpha\beta}_{\perp}\varphi\right)_{a}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+\nu_{a}\left(\phi\right)\left(\nabla_{\alpha\beta}^{\perp}\varphi\right)_{a}\,(G^{\alpha\beta})_{a}-\frac{1}{2c^{2}}c_{a}(\phi)\,(G_{\alpha\beta})_{a}\,(G^{\alpha\beta})_{a}\;\biggr\}\,,

(5b)

The object masses are functions of the scalar field to take into account their internal self-gravity
Eardley 1975
. The tidal action presented in Eq. (
5b
) is sufficient to study the impact of tidal deformations on the dynamics and radiation of a binary system up to the 2PN order. Hence, the results of this work will only depend on the four tidal deformability parameters
(\lambda_{a},\mu_{a},\nu_{a},c_{a})
. The action is made of the mass quadrupole tidal moment defined as

\displaystyle(G_{\mu\nu})_{a}=-c^{2}\,{\left(R_{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}\right)}_{a}\,u_{a}^{\rho}\,u_{a}^{\sigma}\,.

(6)

where
R_{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}
is the usual Riemann tensor and we denote
\nabla_{\mu}^{\perp}\equiv\left(\delta_{\mu}^{\nu}+u_{\mu}u^{\nu}\right)\nabla_{\nu}
the projected covariant derivative onto the hypersurface orthogonal to the four velocity.

The field equations

\{M_{L},S_{L}\}
. However, as we anticipated in Sec.
IV.1
, the moment required at the highest order to compute the gravitational flux is the mass quadrupole
I_{ij}
, needed to 1PN order. At this precision, the gauge moments do not contribute, so the radiative moments depend only on the source ones. Additionally, it is known in GR that the radiative moments acquire some non-linear contributions, notably the tail and memory terms, the latter concerning only the mass-type multipole moments. However, they also enter at orders higher than the ones we are considering here. As a consequence, the radiative moments relate to the source moments only by the simple relations

\displaystyle U_{L}=I_{L}^{(\ell)}\quad\text{and}\quad V_{L}=J_{L}^{(\ell)}\,,

(36)

where the upper index
(\ell)
refer to the
\ell^{\text{th}}
time derivative. Similarly, a previous work
Bernard et al. 2022
has shown that the scalar radiative moments also acquire some tail contributions. It is notably the case for the scalar dipole moment
U_{i}^{s}
that differs from its once-differentiated source moment by the relation

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{i}^{s}

\displaystyle=\overset{(1)}{I_{i}^{s}}+\frac{2GM}{\phi_{0}c^{3}}\int_{-\infty}^{U}\mathrm{d}V\overset{(3)}{I_{i}^{s}}(V)\left[\ln{\left(\frac{U-V}{2b}\right)}+1\right]\,.

(37)

The rest of the scalar moments needed to derive the 1PN total flux and waveform simply satisfy

\displaystyle U_{L}^{s}={(I_{L}^{s})}^{(\ell)}\,.

(38)

The general expression of the source moments in terms of the parameters of the source, specifically in terms of the PN expansion of the scalar and tensor pseudo-tensors (
10
), along with the methodology to compute these moments, is provided in Sec.
V.3
.

IV.3
Memory contributions to
m=0
gravitational modes

In the previous section, we have discussed how non-linear memory effects do not contribute to the radiative multipole moments at the order we are considering for the derivation of the gravitational flux. However, they do impact the gravitational waveform. To illustrate this, we consider the quadrupole moment, whose memory contributions are given by
Bernard et al. 2022

\mathcal{U}_{ij}^{(\text{mem})}=\frac{G(3+2\omega_{0})}{3c^{3}}\Bigg(\int_{-\infty}^{U}\!\mathrm{d}V\overset{(2)}{I^{s}_{\langle i}}(V)\overset{(2)}{I^{s}_{j\rangle}}(V)-I^{s}_{\langle i}\overset{(3)}{I^{s}_{j\rangle}}-\overset{(1)}{I^{s}_{\langle i}}\overset{(2)}{I^{s}_{j\rangle}}-\frac{1}{2}I^{s}\overset{(3)}{I^{s}_{ij}}\Bigg)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{5}};\frac{\epsilon_{\text{tidal}}}{c^{5}}\right)\,.

(39)

These contributions formally appear at
1.5
PN order. When computing them, we will typically encounter two types of integrals: oscillatory integrals of the form

\int_{-\infty}^{U}\mathrm{d}\tau\,x^{p}(\tau)e^{-\mathrm{i}n\phi(\tau)}=\mathrm{i}\frac{\alpha\tilde{G}m}{nc^{3}}e^{-\mathrm{i}n\phi}x^{p-3/2}\,,

(40)

and non-oscillatory integrals as

\int_{-\infty}^{U}\mathrm{d}\tau\,x^{p}(\tau)=\int_{0}^{x(U)}\mathrm{d}y\,\frac{y^{p}}{\dot{x}(y)}\,.

(41)

After integration, the oscillatory contributions coming from (
40
) yield 1.5PN correction to the waveform and can therefore be neglected in this work. By contrast, for the non-oscillatory integrals, we need the time evolution of the gauge-invariant parameter
x
, which is governed at leading order in the point-particle approximation by

\dot{x}=\frac{8c^{3}\zeta\mathcal{S}_{-}{}^{2}\nu x^{4}}{3\alpha\tilde{G}m}\sim\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{5}}\right)\,,

(42)

where
x\sim\mathcal{O}(c^{-2})
. As a result, after integration, the non-oscillatory terms, although formally entering at 1.5PN order, effectively contribute at Newtonian order in the waveform.

We recall that memory effects only affect the mass-type radiative moments
U_{L}
, and consequently the gravitational modes, with
\ell
even. Furthermore, because the motion is quasi-circular, non-oscillatory memory effects only contribute to the
m=0
gravitational modes. Instead of computing these effects directly from the mass-type radiative moments, which would require us to compute them up to the

PN order to remain consistent (with
n
denoting the order typically required, as summarized in Table

Favata 2009

h_{\ell m}^{\text{mem}}=-\frac{16\pi G}{R\phi_{0}c^{4}}\sqrt{\frac{(\ell-2)!}{(\ell+2)!}}\int^{T_{R}}_{-\infty}\mathrm{d}t\int\mathrm{d}\Omega\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{F}}{\mathrm{d}\Omega}(\Omega)Y_{\ell m}^{*}(\Omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{T_{R}}\mathrm{d}t\,\dot{h}_{\ell m}^{\text{mem}}\,.

(43)

In ST theories,

\dot{h}_{\ell m}^{\text{mem}}=\dot{h}_{\ell m}^{\text{mem, g}}+\dot{h}_{\ell m}^{\text{mem, s}}\,.

(44)

The first component on the right hand side is the same as in GR and reads

\dot{h}_{\ell m}^{\text{mem, g}}=-\frac{R}{c}\sqrt{\frac{(\ell-2)!}{(\ell+2)!}}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=2}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell^{\prime\prime}=2}^{\infty}\sum_{m^{\prime}=-\ell^{\prime}}^{\ell^{\prime}}\sum_{m^{\prime\prime}=-\ell^{\prime\prime}}^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(-1)^{m+m^{\prime\prime}}G^{2-20}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell^{\prime\prime}\ell m^{\prime}-m^{\prime\prime}-m}\,\dot{h}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\dot{h}^{*}_{\ell^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime}}\,,

(45)

where the value of the angular integral

is given in
e.g.
Appendix A of
Favata 2009

\dot{h}_{\ell m}^{\text{mem, s}}=-\frac{R(3+2\omega_{0})}{c}\sqrt{\frac{(\ell-2)!}{(\ell+2)!}}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell^{\prime\prime}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m^{\prime}=-\ell^{\prime}}^{\ell^{\prime}}\sum_{m^{\prime\prime}=-\ell^{\prime\prime}}^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(-1)^{m+m^{\prime\prime}}G^{0\,0\,0}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell^{\prime\prime}\ell m^{\prime}-m^{\prime\prime}-m}\,\dot{\psi}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\dot{\psi}^{*}_{\ell^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime}}\,.

VI.2
Orbital phase in the time domain

The orbital phase can be obtained from the flux balance equation for the energy

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}E}{\mathrm{d}t}=-\mathcal{F}

(60)

where
\mathcal{F}
is the total flux, including both scalar and gravitational contributions. Up to a constant, it satisfies

\displaystyle\phi=-\frac{c^{3}}{\alpha\tilde{G}m}\int\mathrm{d}x\,x^{3/2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}x}{\mathcal{F}(x)}\,.

(61)

By substituting our results for the energy and the flux, along with their point-particle counterparts taken from Refs.
Bernard 2019
and
Bernard et al. 2022
, into Eq. (
61
)
and after truncating at the relative 2PN order in the tidal corrections, we get an analytical expression for the time domain orbital phase up to the 2PN order. Special care must be taken when re-expanding this ratio. Indeed, as highlighted in
Sennett et al. 2016
, the radiated flux contains both scalar and gravitational components, giving rise to two different regimes: a dipolar-driven (DD) regime, where the energy flux is dominated by dipolar emission which vanishes when
s_{1}=s_{2}
, and a quadrupolar-driven (QD) regime, where the flux is dominated by quadrupolar emission.
In the following, we focus exclusively in the DD-driven regime as the corrections to tidal effects due to the presence of the scalar field in ST theories are expected to be small and hardly observable in the QD regime
Bernard 2020
.

Focusing on the DD regime, the phase derivation is straightforward and yields the following tidal correction

\displaystyle\phi_{\text{tidal}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4x^{3/2}}{(-1+\zeta)\zeta\bar{\gamma}^{2}\mathcal{S}_{-}{}^{3}\nu^{2}}\Bigl\{\zeta\bar{\gamma}^{2}\mathcal{S}_{-}{}\nu\tilde{\lambda}_{+}^{(0)}+\sqrt{\alpha}\bigl(\frac{1}{8}\delta(-4\zeta\mathcal{S}_{-}{}\mathcal{S}_{+}{}\tilde{\Lambda}_{-}^{(0)}+\bar{\gamma}\tilde{\Lambda}_{+}^{(0)}-4\zeta\mathcal{S}_{-}{}^{2}\tilde{\Lambda}_{+}^{(0)})

\displaystyle-\zeta\bar{\gamma}\mathcal{S}_{-}{}\nu(\mathcal{S}_{-}{}\tilde{\Lambda}_{-}^{(0)}+\mathcal{S}_{+}{}\tilde{\Lambda}_{+}^{(0)})+\frac{1}{8}(\bar{\gamma}\tilde{\Lambda}_{-}^{(0)}-4\zeta\mathcal{S}_{-}{}^{2}\tilde{\Lambda}_{-}^{(0)}-4\zeta\mathcal{S}_{-}{}\mathcal{S}_{+}{}\tilde{\Lambda}_{+}^{(0)})\bigr)

VI.3
Orbital phase in the stationary phase approximation

For data analysis purposes, we also present the phase within the stationary phase approximation (SPA)
Tichy et al. 2000
. This technique consists in performing the Fourier transform of the gravitational waveform. We proceed similarly as in
Sennett et al. 2016
and write the amplitude modes as

h_{\ell m}^{\text{SPA}}(f)=\mathcal{A}_{\ell m}(f)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\Phi^{\ell m}(f)-\mathrm{i}\pi/4}\,.

(63)

The SPA phase is given by

(64)

where the parameter

is evaluated at

being the parameter associated to the GW frequency
f
.
In order not to be confused with the
m
mode appearing in the waveform, we change the notation for the total mass,
m_{\text{tot}}=m_{1}+m_{2}
, in this section only. The functions
\phi(v)
and
t(v)
are computed directly from the flux balance equation (
60
), and they satisfy the following relations

\displaystyle\phi(v)

\displaystyle=\phi(v_{0})-\frac{c^{3}}{\alpha\tilde{G}m_{\text{tot}}}\int^{v}_{v_{0}}\mathrm{d}v\,v^{3}\frac{\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}v}{\mathcal{F}(v)}

(65a)

\displaystyle t(v)

\displaystyle=t(v_{0})-\int^{v}_{v_{0}}\mathrm{d}v\,\frac{\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}v}{\mathcal{F}(v)}

(65b)

where
\phi(v_{0})
and
t(v_{0})
are constants evaluated at some reference point
v_{0}
. We have explicitly computed Eq. (
64
) within the DD regime and we decompose the Fourier domain phase as

\displaystyle\Phi^{\ell m}(f)=\Phi^{\ell m}_{\text{pp}}(f)+\Phi^{\ell m}_{\text{tidal}}(f)+m\phi_{0}-2\pi f\,t_{0}\,.

(66)

The constants
\phi_{0}
and

result from the sum of
\phi(v_{0})
and
t(v_{0})

65

\displaystyle=\frac{4mv^{3}}{(-1+\zeta)\zeta\bar{\gamma}^{2}\mathcal{S}_{-}{}^{3}\nu^{2}}\biggl\{\bigl(-1+3\log(v)\bigr)\Bigl(\zeta\bar{\gamma}^{2}\mathcal{S}_{-}{}\nu\tilde{\lambda}_{+}^{(0)}+\sqrt{\alpha}\bigl(\frac{1}{8}\delta(-4\zeta\mathcal{S}_{-}{}\mathcal{S}_{+}{}\tilde{\Lambda}_{-}^{(0)}+\bar{\gamma}\tilde{\Lambda}_{+}^{(0)}-4\zeta\mathcal{S}_{-}{}^{2}\tilde{\Lambda}_{+}^{(0)})

\displaystyle-\zeta\bar{\gamma}\mathcal{S}_{-}{}\nu(\mathcal{S}_{-}{}\tilde{\Lambda}_{-}^{(0)}+\mathcal{S}_{+}{}\tilde{\Lambda}_{+}^{(0)})+\frac{1}{8}(\bar{\gamma}\tilde{\Lambda}_{-}^{(0)}-4\zeta\mathcal{S}_{-}{}^{2}\tilde{\Lambda}_{-}^{(0)}-4\zeta\mathcal{S}_{-}{}\mathcal{S}_{+}{}\tilde{\Lambda}_{+}^{(0)})\bigr)\Bigr)+v^{2}\Bigl[...\Bigr]

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\alpha}(2+\bar{\gamma})\pi v^{3}\Bigl[\frac{1}{16}\delta(4\zeta\mathcal{S}_{-}{}\mathcal{S}_{+}{}\tilde{\Lambda}_{-}^{(0)}-\bar{\gamma}\tilde{\Lambda}_{+}^{(0)}+4\zeta\mathcal{S}_{-}{}^{2}\tilde{\Lambda}_{+}^{(0)})+\zeta\bar{\gamma}\mathcal{S}_{-}{}\nu(\mathcal{S}_{-}{}\tilde{\Lambda}_{-}^{(0)}+\mathcal{S}_{+}{}\tilde{\Lambda}_{+}^{(0)})+\frac{1}{16}(-\bar{\gamma}\tilde{\Lambda}_{-}^{(0)}

\displaystyle+4\zeta\mathcal{S}_{-}{}^{2}\tilde{\Lambda}_{-}^{(0)}+4\zeta\mathcal{S}_{-}{}\mathcal{S}_{+}{}\tilde{\Lambda}_{+}^{(0)})\Bigr]+v^{4}\Bigl[-\frac{135}{448}\zeta\bar{\gamma}^{2}\mathcal{S}_{-}{}\nu\bigl(\tilde{c}_{+}^{(0)}-4(4\tilde{\mu}_{+}^{(0)}+\tilde{\nu}_{+}^{(0)})\bigr)+(...)\Bigr]+{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{\epsilon_{\text{tidal}}}{c^{5}}\right)\biggr\}\,.

VIII
Conclusion

To conclude, we have extended the modeling of gravitational waveforms from inspiralling, non-spinning compact binaries in ST theories by incorporating adiabatic tidal effects to the radiative sector. We have used the PN-MPM formalism adapted to ST theories, and worked within the adiabatic approximation for the description of tides, including the scalar tides. As our main results, we have computed the tidal corrections to the gravitational and scalar fluxes up to 1PN order beyond the leading quadrupolar radiation in GR, and derived the corresponding 2PN correction to the GW phasing in the dipolar-driven regime. At this level of accuracy, we had to account for the contributions of the scalar, tensorial, and mixed scalar-tensorial tidal deformabilities. We have also computed the full gravitational and scalar waveform amplitude modes consistently to 1PN order in the same regime. Only dipolar tidal corrections contribute to the waveform amplitudes at this precision. In order to capture quadrupolar tidal effects, specifically the second-order scalar as well as the leading-order tensorial and mixed scalar-tensorial contributions, one needs to extend the PN computations up to 1.5PN order. This is left for future work.

In this work, we have focused on non-spinning, quasi-circular systems, and worked in the adiabatic approximations for the tides and scalarization. Future prospects to extend this work would be to go beyond the adiabatic approximation by considering dynamical tides and scalarization. Another aspect would be to consider spinning objects, and in particular tidal-induced spin contributions. Indeed, such effects should be important in other classes of ST theories that include higher curvature corrections and that differ from GR for BHs, such as Einstein-scalar-Gauss-Bonnet gravity.
Finally, we emphasize that taking into account scalar tidal effects is crucial when devising tests of gravity with gravitational waves, as their dipolar nature starting at 3PN presents them a clear signature compared to the traditional tides in GR. Hence, we encourage people working on fundamental physics aspects of the science exploitation of future gravitational wave detectors, such as LISA and Einstein Telescope, to take into account these effects, be there when devising fully agnostic or theory-specific tests.

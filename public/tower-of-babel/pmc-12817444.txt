The impact of infertility-related stress, physiological stress and some fertility indices on clinical pregnancy in women undergoing in vitro fertilization
Authors: Navid Omidifar, Esmail Najafi, Khadijeh Nasiri, Tahere Khosravi Fard Shirazi, Roghayeh Salmani, Mohammad Ebrahim Parsanezhad
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDespite numerous studies on the impact of stress on post-in vitro fertilization (IVF) clinical outcomes are contradictory and researchers recommend the need for further studies. Therefore, the purpose of the present study was to investigate the impact of infertility-related stress, measuring salivary cortisol as a biological marker, and non-invasive sampling and measuring some fertility indices, including the number of suctioned follicles and Anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH), on the post- IVF clinical pregnancy results.MethodsThe present study is a prospective longitudinal study conducted on 115 women with primary infertility who were undergoing IVF treatment at the Infertility Center of Zeinbiyeh Hospital, Shiraz. The studied subjects were selected by simple and convenience sampling during the years 2021 to 2022. The data collection tool included a two-part questionnaire including demographic characteristics and fertility problem inventory (FPI), and measurement of salivary cortisol concentration and some fertility indices. Data were analyzed using SPSS software. t-test, Mann-Whitney, Chi-square and logistic regression statistical tests were used.ResultsAmong 115 participants, 45 were in the positive stress group and 70 people were in the negative stress group. Post-IVF pregnancy results were positive and negative in 32% and 68%, respectively. The mean FPI scores in women with positive pregnancy result (152.78 ± 23.38) were lower than women with negative pregnancy result (157.30 ± 33.34), but this difference was not statically significant (P-value = 0.43). There was also no significant difference between the two groups in terms of the mean scores of different dimensions of FPI. The results of multivariate regression showed a significant relationship between the results of IVF with the level of cortisol hormone (p-value = 0.04) and AMH (p-value = 0.04). The results of the chi-square test showed that there was no significant relationship between stress and pregnancy outcome (P-value = 0.176).ConclusionThe results of the present study showed that salivary cortisol levels can affect the fertility problem inventory. Also; infertility-related stress does not affect IVF results.

Background

Infertility is an unpleasant and unexpected experience [1], which is defined as infertility after one year of continuous sexual activity without using contraceptive methods [2]. Approximately 10–15% of couples in the world face infertility [3]. According to a population-based study in Iran, the overall prevalence of primary infertility in Iranian couples is 17.3% [4]. Use of assisted reproductive technology (ART), especially in vitro fertilization (IVF) is a reasonable treatment option for infertile couples and a suitable method after other methods have failed, and in recent years the number of pregnancies achieved through IVF has increased [5, 6].

Infertility is associated with important psychological, economic, demographic and medical consequences [2]. The overall prevalence of psychological problems in infertile couples is estimated at 25–60%. These problems include hopelessness, anxiety, depression, stress, low self-confidence and even low marital and sexual satisfaction [7, 8]. Stress and depression are the first negative emotional response to infertility and ARTs [9, 10] and are the most common psychological disorders among infertile patients [11, 12]. The have been many studies on impact of stress on reproductive performance in recent years [10, 13]. Probably, Stress is probably one of the psychological factors affecting the outcome of ART [10, 13, 14]. However, it is still not clear how psychological stress affects the IVF results from a clinical point of view, and the results of studies are contradictory in this regard [11, 12, 15].

According to studies, stressful stimuli activate the hypothalamus-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis and the sympathetic-adrenal-medullary (SAM) axis [16] which in turn leads to abnormal regulation of the body’s hormones in response to stress, which can potentially lead to long-term endocrine changes and affect women’s fertility [17–20]. Accordingly, it is stated that stress can have an adverse effect on the result of infertility treatment by changing the serum levels of hormones such as cortisol, prolactin and progesterone [21], reducing ovarian reserve, disrupting successful ovulation, implantation, placental growth [22, 23], and sexual function [24].

Cortisol measurement has been used as a stress indicator and to check HPA activity for years. Cortisol can be measured through saliva sampling [25]. The use of saliva is now recognized as a unique diagnostic fluid that allows for measuring and monitoring a wide range of molecular components in saliva and compare them with serum components and studying microbes, chemicals, and the use of immune markers [26]. Currently, the level of salivary cortisol [25], androsterone [27], estradiol [28], estriol [29], progesterone [30], and testosterone [31] can be accurately measured. Salivary cortisol represents the free or active part of this hormone, and it is a part of the hormone that passes through the blood-brain barrier and is affected by brain activity and indicates the behavior and cognitive performance [32]. Results of two studies on the relationship between serum cortisol level and post-IVF clinical pregnancy have reported that higher cortisol levels are related to a decrease in pregnancy rate [33, 34]. But in a prospective study, Nouri et al. found no relationship between salivary cortisol levels and stress with the number of follicles collected in aspiration, clinical pregnancy rate and live birth rate [35].

Therefore, considering the contradictory results of previous studies, impact of stress on infertility, the serum levels of some hormones and fertility indices and the possibility of non-invasive examination of salivary cortisol as a stress index, we decided to investigate the relationship between stress, salivary cortisol level and some fertility indices, including the number of suctioned follicles, ovarian reserve, etc., with the IVF result in infertile women referred to the Infertility Center of Zeynabiyeh Hospital in Shiraz.

Materials and methods

Study design

The present study is a prospective longitudinal study that was at the Infertility Center of Zeynabiyeh Hospital in Shiraz during the years 2021 and 2022.

Participants and sample

The study population included all the infertile women who visited the Infertility Center during the sampling period and met the inclusion criteria. Inclusion criteria included willingness to participate in the study, written informed consent, diagnosis of infertility based on female, male or idiopathic factors, primary infertility, age over 18 years, being a candidate for IVF and having literacy. Exclusion criteria also included a history of being hospitalized in a psychiatric hospital or suffering from neurological and psychological diseases, taking nerve drugs (according to the person’s self-report and clinical record) and suffering from chronic diseases. The sample size using the following formula and by considering z = 1.96, p = 45.96, q = 12.33 and d = 0.05 according to Salari et al.‘s study [36] and 15% dropout rate was determined 115 people.

After obtaining the necessary permissions from Khalkhal University of Medical Sciences, presenting a letter of introduction and permission to Shiraz University of Medical Sciences and the Infertility Center, the participants were selected by simple and convenience sampling during the years 2021 to 2022. It is worth mentioning that the present study is a joint study between two universities of medical sciences, Shiraz and Khalkhal. Initially, 145 people were included in the study. 30 people were excluded from the study due to not meeting the inclusion criteria. Finally, 115 people were included in the study and completed the questionnaire and salivary samples were taken from them; but the final results, which was post-IVF clinical pregnancy, could be evaluated in 103 of the participants and 12 of them were excluded from the study due to reasons such as unwillingness or inability to continue the treatment process (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Flowchart of participants and available data in the study

Measurements and data collection

Two questionnaires were used in the present study. The first questionnaire included a demographic questionnaire including questions on age, job, income level, the level of education women and their husbands, duration of marriage, duration of infertility and smoking. The second questionnaire included Newton’s standard infertility problem stress inventory (FPI), which was designed by Newton et al. in 1999 [37]. This questionnaire consists of 46 items and 5 subscales, which include: social concern (10 questions, 1–10), sexual concern (8 questions, 11–18), relationship concern (10 questions, 19–28), rejection of parenthood (8 questions, 29–36) and need for parenthood (10 questions, 37–46). The validity and reliability of this questionnaire were calculated using Cronbach’s alpha method (α = 0.93). The questions answered based on a six-point Likert scale (Completely disagree = 1, Somewhat disagree = 2, Very little disagree = 3, Very little agree = 4, Somewhat agree = 5, and completely agree = 6). The final score is obtained by adding up the scores of the questions. The possible score range is 46–276, where a higher score means more stress. Moreover, questions 1, 2, 5, 6, 10, 12, 13, 21, 28, 29, 30, 31, 33, 34, 35, 36, and 43 are scored in reverse [38]. The reliability and validity Persian version of this questionnaire have been approved by Samani et al. [38]. The value of Cronbach’s alpha for all domains was more than 70% [38].

Sampling was performed after explaining the study objectives and obtaining informed consent from the participants. After the injection of HMG, serial vaginal ultrasounds are performed and when the follicles reach a size greater than 18 mm, follicle retrieval was performed. On the day of follicle retrieval, questionnaires were completed by the researcher. Also, saliva samples from the participants were collected by one of the trained researchers. In order to take a salivary sample and measure the salivary cortisol level, the research participants were asked to wash their mouth first after waking up in the morning and then pour 3–5 cc of their saliva into the sample collection container. Due to the diurnal changes in cortisol level and in order to minimize the effect of these changes on the cortisol concentration, all samples were taken at 8:00 to 10:00 am (when the serum and salivary cortisol levels are at a high level of its circadian cycle [39]. It should also be noted that saliva sampling was carried out in all research subjects on the morning of the day of follicle suctioning. After taking the salivary sample, the test container was coded, placed in a special ice box, immediately transferred to the laboratory and the samples were kept at − 20 °C until the cortisol concentration was measured. The cortisol concentration of the samples was measured after sampling. For this purpose, the samples were first placed at room temperature to get out of the frozen state. They were then centrifuged for 15 min at a speed of 3000 RPM and were frozen for at least one hour at − 20 °C and recentrifuged for 15 min at 3000 RPM, and then salivary cortisol levels were measured by ELISA using DiaMetra kits (Italy). All laboratory tests were performed in the laboratory of Shiraz University of Medical Sciences.

Measurement of fertility indices such as the number of suctioned follicles and other laboratory indices such as hemoglobin (HB), hematocrit (HCT), platelets (PLT), thyroid stimulating hormones (TSH), luteinizing hormone (LH), follicle stimulating hormone (FSH) and AMH used in the study and information on IVF result were obtained by referring to the medical records of the subjects or by calling them. It should be noted that according to the routine of the Infertility Center of Zeynabiyeh Hospital, the IVF result were checked using a BHCG pregnancy test after 14–16 days of IVF.

Statistical analysis

After checking the assumptions for parametric tests, such as measuring the normality of the variables by Kolmogorov–Smirnov test and determining homogeneity, the t-test was used to compare the means, and the Mann-Whitney test was used if the assumptions were not established. Chi-square test was also used to investigate the effect of multivariate variables on the result of infertility stress. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression tests were also used to investigate the determinants of IVF result. Data analysis was also carried out using SPSS ver. 26 software.

Ethical considerations

The present study, approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Khalkhal University of Medical Sciences, was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (IR.KHALUMS.REC.1399.003) and submitting it to Shiraz University of Medical Sciences and the study center. Informed consent was obtained from all subjects.

Results

A total of 115 people participated in this study and completed the questionnaire and salivary samples were taken from them; but the final results, which was post-IVF clinical pregnancy, could be evaluated in 103 of the participants and 12 of them were excluded from the study due to reasons such as unwillingness or inability to continue the treatment process.

After investigating the mean infertility-related stress and considering FPI < 167, as a global index of defining positive stress cases (35), participants were assigned into two groups of negative stress (n = 70 people) and positive infertility–related stress (n = 45 people). According to this grouping, there was no significant difference between the two groups in terms of age, spouse’s age, BMI, duration of marriage and duration of infertility. However, the average duration of treatment in stress-positive group was significantly longer than the stress-negative group (P-value = 0.032), which indicates the increase of stress with the increase in the duration of efforts for fertility. With regard to level of education level, 48.6% of samples were in the stress-negative group had university education, while this figure was 26.7% in the stress-positive group, and most of them (42.2%) had a diploma. Also, the chi- square showed a significant relationship between these two groups. Similar results were reported between spouse’s level of education and IVF result (Table 1). In both groups, most people had a family relationship with their spouses, which was not statistically significant (x²=0.775, p-value = 0.686). Most of the participants in both groups were housewives and their husbands were self-employed. Most participants in both groups reported drinking at least one cup of coffee per day. The participants in both groups reported no physical activity in more than half of the cases. The results on social and demographic variables are fully presented in Table 1.

The final results of IVF (positive BHCG test) in the present study were determined in 103 people, which were positive in 32% (n = 33 people) and negative in 68% of cases (n = 70 people). The results showed that the mean FPI score in women with a positive IVF result (152.78 ± 23.38) is lower than women with a negative IVF result (157.30 ± 33.34), but this difference was not statistically significant (P-value = 0.431). The results also showed that the mean scores of social concern, sexual concern and need to parenthood in the group with a negative IVF result are higher than the group with a positive IVF result, but these differences were also not statistically significant (Table 2).

Among the participants who were not stressed, 29 (62.9%) had a positive pregnancy test and 23 (37.1%) had a negative pregnancy test. Also, among the stressed participants, 31 (57%) had a positive pregnancy test and 10 (24%) had a negative pregnancy test. The results of the chi-square test showed that there was no significant relationship between stress and pregnancy outcome (P-value = 0.176).

Discussion

The results showed about 39% of the participants had infertility-related stress and the clinical pregnancy rate was 32%. According to two meta-analysis studies, the level of stress, the prevalence of depression and anxiety symptoms were reported to be higher in women undergoing fertility treatment than in the general population [10, 13]. Investigating the relationship between infertility-related stress and the IVF results showed that the mean FPI score and some of its dimensions such as social concern, sexual concern and need to parenthood in women with a positive pregnancy result are lower than women with a negative pregnancy result, but these differences were not statistically significant. The results of the present study are consistent with the results of the study by Maroufizadeh et al., who stated that anxiety, depression and stress symptoms are not related to the clinical pregnancy rate of IVF [15]. In their study on infertile women who underwent IVF/ICSI treatment, Lintsen et al. (2009) showed that psychological symptoms such as anxiety and depression do not play an important role in predicting the success rate of IVF/ICSI treatment. Besides, they suggested that infertility-related stress cannot predict pregnancy rate in the final stages of IVF/ICSI treatment [40]. Similar results were obtained in the studies by Ebbesen (2009), Matthiesen (2011), Cesta (2018) and Nouri (2011) [10, 35, 41], and [42]. But Zhou (2019), Gourounti (2011), Smink (2001 article) and Csemiczky (2000) stated in their studies a negative relationship between stress and anxiety with the positive pregnancy result after IVF [21, 43–45]. The presence of conflicting results in different studies may be due to reasons such as sample size, use of different or non-standard tools, study design or characteristics of the study population.

Results of investigating the relationship between demographic information and infertility-related stress showed that the average duration of infertility treatment and the duration of infertility in stress-positive group was higher than in stress-negative group, and there was a significant difference between the two groups in terms of duration of treatment. In other words, the stress level increases with an increase in the duration of infertility treatment. Maroufizadeh et al. stated in their study titled “Anxiety and depression after failure of assisted reproductive treatment in infertile patients”; the longer the patient continues the treatment, the more symptoms of depression and anxiety are manifested, so that patients with one treatment failure have a much higher level of anxiety, and patients with two failures experience more depression compared to people who have no history of treatment [46]. They also concluded that the duration of infertility has no effect on the IVF result, but this factor was reported to be effective on clinical pregnancy in Lintsen’s study, which predicted the chance of pregnancy after IVF and ICSI in 4928 Dutch couples [47].

To investigate the impact of stress on the IVF result, some researchers measured the biological markers of stress and its effect on IVF treatment. Cortisol is considered as one of the biological markers of stress, whose concentration has been measured in serum, follicular fluid [33, 34], saliva [35, 42] and hair [48, 49] levels in various studies. Investigating the relationship between serum cortisol and follicular fluid with clinical pregnancy after IVF showed lower cortisol levels in women with successful IVF treatment [33, 48]. Nouri & CESTA showed no relationship between infertility-related stress and salivary cortisol levels with IVF results [35, 42]. Young stated that assessment of blood or saliva cortisol levels only reflects acute levels of circulating cortisol, which is highly volatile and influenced by a wide range of situational factors [50]. But cortisol assessment through hair sampling measures the cortisol level during the last 3–6 months. Both saliva and hair sampling methods were used in a study to measure cortisol and its relationship with pregnancy results and it was found that hair cortisol concentration, unlike salivary cortisol, significantly predicts pregnancy [48]. In the present study, the salivary cortisol level was significantly higher in the group with negative pregnancy result than in the group with positive pregnancy result.

Investigating laboratory indices showed that the AMH level, the number of suctioned follicles and the number of follicles in the right ovary are significantly higher in the group with positive pregnancy result after IVF than in the group with negative pregnancy results. AMH is considered as a reliable indicator of ovarian reserve and has a positive relationship with the number of ovarian follicles and oocyte quality [51]. Zhou et al. stated that high stress level has a negative effect on ovarian reserve and endometrial thickness in women. Also, the number of high-quality embryos, endometrial thickness, and AMH were introduced as the most important female factors affecting the IVF result [45]. Cesta et al. found no significant relationship between stress and oocyte indices and embryo quality [42].

Univariate regression results showed a significant relationship between husband’s job and IVF results. This relationship may be analyzed using the results of Barut et al.‘s study [52]. They stated that low economic and social status leads to the intensification of stress sources such as malnutrition and financial problems, which potentially play an important role in affecting the ovarian reserve [52].

The limitations of the current study include the self-reported measurement of stress and salivary cortisol at a specific time due to lack of access to patients at several times. Considering that the cortisol be affected due to unknown variables, Therefore, it was not possible to control for all confounding factors. It is recommended to conduct a study with a larger sample size, measure cortisol at different times. It is also recommended to conduct clinical trials and counseling interventions in future studies. The use of two methods, questionnaire and cortisol levels, to determine stress levels is one of the strengths of this study.

Conclusion

The results of the present study showed that salivary cortisol levels can affect the fertility problem inventory. Also; infertility-related stress does not affect IVF results. Despite the lack of relationship between the stress caused by infertility and the results of IVF, but psychological and social support can reduce cortisol and women’s stress levels.

Remarks on the Gluing Theorems for Compact Special Lagrangian Submanifolds with Isolated Conical Singularities
Yohsuke Imagi

Abstract
We make two improvements upon the gluing theorems of [ 6 , 7 ] for compact special Lagrangian submanifolds with isolated conical singularities. Firstly, we get rid of a few technical hypotheses of them. Secondly, we replace another hypothesis by a weaker one and prove that the latter is a necessary condition for the gluing process to be possible.

Let

Recall that there are two cohomology classes

We prove that we can get rid of the hypothesis

in

[
7

[

7
]

There is also a technical hypothesis in the most general version

,
7
]

[

[

Our version of the equation, (
2.2
) of the present paper, is in fact slightly more general than
[
7
, (108)]
. Following the proofs of
[
6
,
7
]
we see immediately that we can make such a generalization. But we prove also that the generalized equation is a necessary condition for the gluing process to be possible.

We explain now how we prove our results. We begin with the same approximate solutions as in
[
6
,
7
]
which we make from
X
and
\bigsqcup_{x\in X^{\rm sing}}L_{x}
using partitions of unity (not doing any analysis yet). We then perturb these to the true solutions, not by the method of
[

,
7
]
but by the method of
[
3
,
11
]
. The advantage of the latter is that the two building blocks

and
\bigsqcup_{x\in X^{\rm sing}}L_{x}
play symmetric rôles. This happens to other gluing methods including the well-known case of Yang–Mills gauge theory. For instance, if we want to glue together the model ASD connections over
{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{4}
and the flat connection over a compact
4

M
then we can take the actual building blocks to be
S^{4}={\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{4}\sqcup\{\infty\}
and
M
(as in
[
1
, §7.2]
) whose rôles are symmetric.

For
X
connected, using the method of
[
3
,
11
]
we can perturb the approximate solutions directly to the true solutions. Otherwise, there is a non-trivial obstruction space and the results we get are still approximate solutions. Using the cohomology class equation we see however that the second approximate solutions are so improved that we can apply to them the original perturbation result
[
6
, Theorem 5.3]
(or Theorem
9.1
below). Note that as we work under the weaker hypotheses, the first approximate solutions need not satisfy the hypotheses of
[
6
, Theorem 5.3]
. Having the second approximate solutions is therefore crucial.

The proof that the cohomology class equation is a necessary condition for the gluing process is similar to the proof that the second approximate solutions satisfy the better estimates.

We begin in §
2

9

In §

[

,
7
]
. But the estimates we shall need for these are weaker than those in
[
6
,
7
]
; for more details see Remark

[
6
,
7
]
. In §
7

[
3
,
11

. In §
8

Statements of the Results

We begin by making the definitions we will use to state our results. We define almost Calabi–Yau
m
-folds in the first place.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
M
be a manifold of even dimension
2m.
An
almost Calabi–Yau
structure on
M
is the data
(\omega,J,\Omega)
such that
(M;\omega,J)
is a Kähler manifold with Kähler form
\omega
and complex structure
J,
and such that
\Omega
is a nowhere-vanishing holomorphic
(m,0)
form on the complex manifold
(M,J).
We call
(M;\omega,J,\Omega)
an
almost Calabi–Yau
m
-fold.
Notice that there is a
C^{\infty}
function
\psi:M\to(0,\infty)
defined by

\psi^{2m}\omega^{m}/m!=(i/2)^{m}(-1)^{m(m-1)/2}\Omega\wedge\overline{\Omega}.

(2.1)

We call
(\omega,J,\Omega)
a
Calabi–Yau
structure, without the word almost, if
\psi:M\to(0,\infty)
is constant. In this case
\omega
is a Ricci-flat Kähler form on
(M,J).

We define special Lagrangian submanifolds of almost Calabi–Yau
m
-folds.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(M;\omega,J,\Omega)
be an almost Calabi–Yau
m
-fold and
L\subseteq(M,\omega)
a Lagrangian submanifold. We say that

is
special
if
\mathop{\rm Im}\Omega|_{L}=0.

We define a Calabi–Yau structure
(\omega^{\prime},J^{\prime},\Omega^{\prime})
on
{{\mathbb{C}}}^{m}
and special Lagrangian cones in
({{\mathbb{C}}}^{,}\omega^{\prime},J^{\prime},\Omega^{\prime}).

Definition 2.3
.

Let
m\geqslant 1
be an integer and
z_{1},\dots,z_{m}
the complex coordinates of
{{\mathbb{C}}}^{m}.
Denote by

the complex structure of
{{\mathbb{C}}}^{m}
and define on
{{\mathbb{C}}}^{m}
a Calabi–Yau structure
(\omega^{\prime},J^{\prime},\Omega^{\prime})
by
\omega^{\prime}:=\frac{i}{2}({\rm d}z_{1}\wedge{\rm d}\bar{z}_{1}+\dots+{\rm d}z_{m}\wedge{\rm d}\bar{z}_{m})
and
\Omega^{\prime}:={\rm d}z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge{\rm d}z_{m}.

Define
(0,\infty)\times{{\mathbb{C}}}^{m}\to{{\mathbb{C}}}^{m}
by
(t,z)\mapsto tz,
by which the multiplicative group
(0,\infty)
acts upon
{{\mathbb{C}}}^{m}.
For

we denote for short by

the map

A

is a special Lagrangian submanifold

invariant under the
(0,\infty)
action and such that

is a compact
(m-1)
dimensional submanifold of the unit sphere

The
cone metric
on

is the Riemannian metric induced from the embedding

We define an asymptotically conical special Lagrangian submanifold
L\subseteq{{\mathbb{C}}}^{m}
and its cohomology classes

Definition 2.5
.

Let

be a special Lagrangian cone and

Let

be a special Lagrangian cone and

a properly-embedded special Lagrangian submanifold which approaches with order

at infinity the cone
C.
Using the homology groups with coefficient field

we make the following definition:

and

are the respective images of the relative de Rham classes

[\omega^{\prime}|_{L}]\in H^{2}({{\mathbb{C}}}^{m},L)\cong H^{1}(L)\text{ and }[\mathop{\rm Im}\Omega^{\prime}|_{L}]\in H^{m}({{\mathbb{C}}}^{m},L)\cong H^{m-1}(L)

We define compact special Lagrangian submanifolds with isolated conical singularities.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(M;\omega,J,\Omega)
be an almost Calabi–Yau
m
-fold and define
\psi:M\to(0,\infty)
by (
2.1
). Let

be a special Lagrangian submanifold with compact closure
\bar{X}=X\sqcup X^{\rm sing}
for some finite set

For each

choose a
{{\mathbb{C}}}
-vector space isomorphism
(T_{x}M,J|_{x})\cong({{\mathbb{C}}}^{m},J^{\prime})
under which
(\omega|_{x},\Omega|_{x})
corresponds to
(\omega^{\prime},\Omega^{\prime}).
We call

a compact special Lagrangian submanifold
with isolated conical singularities
if for each

there exist a special Lagrangian cone

an open neighbourhood

of

and a Darboux embedding
\Upsilon_{x}:U_{x}\to M

\Upsilon_{x}(0)=x,

\Upsilon_{x}^{*}\Omega=\psi(x)^{m}\Omega^{\prime},

\Upsilon_{x}^{*}\omega=\omega^{\prime}
and the following holds: there exist
\mu>2

\phi:C_{x}\cap U_{x}\to\Upsilon_{x}^{-1}(X)
such that if we denote by
\iota:C_{x}\to{{\mathbb{C}}}^{m}

We call

the

to

at

We introduce the following notation for short: for each connected component

Remark 2.7
.

Denote by
H^{*}_{c}(X):=H^{*}_{c}(X,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})
the compact-support cohomology group. Since
X
is outside a compact set diffeomorphic to
\bigsqcup_{x\in X^{\rm sing}}C_{x}
we get a long exact sequence
\dots\to H^{*}_{c}(X)\to H^{*}(X)\to\bigoplus_{x\in X^{\rm sing}}H^{*}(C_{x})\to\cdots.
Since

it follows also that for

there are natural
{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}
-vector space isomorphisms
H^{q}_{c}(X)\cong H^{q}(\bar{X},X^{\rm sing})\cong H^{q}(\bar{X}).

Hypothesis 2.8
.

Let

be an integer and

Let

which approaches with order
\leqslant\penalty 0

We make a definition we will use shortly.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
n\geqslant 0
be an integer and
{\mathbin{\cal G}}\subseteq{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}\times(0,\infty)
a subset whose closure in
{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}\times[0,\infty)

We say then that a function

for

(N^{st}\subseteq M)_{(s,t)\in{\mathbin{\cal G}}}
a family of submanifolds, each

diffeomorphic to a common manifold

We say then that the family is

if there exists a smooth function
{\mathbin{\cal G}}\times N\to M
whose restriction to

N^{st}\subseteq M.

We state the condition which will play the central rôle in stating our main results.

Condition 2.10
.

Under Hypothesis
2.8
there exist a closed subset
{\mathbin{\cal G}}\subseteq{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}\times(0,\infty)
whose closure in
{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}\times[0,\infty)

(0,0),
and a smooth family

(a)

(b)

More precisely, the multiplicity-one varifolds supported on

(c)

There exist constants

Remark 2.11
.

By the proof of

Since

Lemma 7.4
.

D_{L}:C^{5/2}_{\nu}(L,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\to C^{1/2}_{\nu-2}(L,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})
is invertible and its inverse is bounded uniformly with respect
s,t.

Proof.

Computation similar to the proof of (
4.4
) show that there exists
c>0
independent of
s,t
and such that

|g^{st}-g^{\prime}|_{C^{3/2}}\leqslant\penalty ctr\leqslant\penalty 2ct^{\rho}\text{ at every point of }L

(7.2)

where the
C^{3/2}
norm is computed with respect to the cylindrical metric on
L.

Denote by
\nabla^{st},\nabla^{\prime}
the respective Levi-Civita connections of
g^{st},g^{\prime}.
The estimate (
7.2
) implies then that there exists
c>0
independent of
s,t
and such that for
\phi\in C^{3/2}(T^{*}L)
we have

|\nabla^{st}\phi-\nabla^{\prime}\phi|_{C^{1/2}}\leqslant\penalty ct^{\rho}|\phi|_{C^{3/2}}\text{ at every point of }L.

(7.3)

In general, the operator
{\rm d}^{*}
of a Riemannian metric
g_{ab}
is of the form
-g^{ab}\nabla_{b}
in the index notation. In the current circumstances the
g^{ab}
term contributes
r^{-2}
so that

|{\rm d}^{*st}\phi-{\rm d}^{*\prime}\phi|_{C^{1/2}}\leqslant\penalty cr^{-2}t^{\rho}|\phi|_{C^{3/2}}\text{ at every point of }L.

(7.4)

Hence making
c
larger if we need, we find that for
u\in C^{5/2}(L,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})
we have

|{\rm d}^{*st}[(\psi^{st})^{m}{\rm d}u]-{\rm d}^{*\prime}[(\psi^{st})^{m}{\rm d}u]|_{C^{1/2}}\leqslant\penalty cr^{-2}t^{\rho}|u|_{C^{5/2}}.

(7.5)

Since
\psi^{00}=1
it follows that making
c
larger if we need, we have

|D_{L}u-\Delta_{L}u|_{C^{1/2}}\leqslant\penalty cr^{-2}(|s|+t^{\rho})|u|_{C^{5/2}}.

(7.6)

Passing to the weighted norms we get

|D_{L}u-\Delta_{L}u|_{\nu-2}\leqslant\penalty c(|s|+t^{\rho})|u|_{\nu}.

(7.7)

Recall now from
[
10
, Theorem 6.2.15]
that the operator
\Delta_{L}:C^{5/2}_{\nu}(L,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\to C^{1/2}_{\nu-2}(L,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})
is invertible. Since
g^{st}
and
\psi^{st}
depend smoothly on
s,t
it follows that the operator
D_{L}:C^{5/2}_{\nu}(L,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\to C^{1/2}_{\nu-2}(L,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})
is bounded uniformly with respect to
s,t
(where the operator norm is computed with respect to the weighted norms). The estimate (
7.7
) implies therefore that that the operator
D_{L}\Delta_{L}^{-1}-{\rm id}:C^{1/2}_{\nu-2}(L,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\to C^{1/2}_{\nu-2}(L,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})
has operator norm
\leqslant\penalty c(|s|+t^{\rho})\ll 1
with respect to the
C^{1/2}_{\nu-2}
norm. Thus
D_{L}\Delta_{L}^{-1}
is invertible and its inverse is bounded uniformly with respect to
s,t.
On the other hand,
\Delta_{L}
has nothing to do with
s,t
and is therefore bounded uniformly with respect to
s,t.
Accordingly so is
\Delta_{L}^{-1}(D_{L}\Delta_{L}^{-1})^{-1},
which is a right inverse to
D_{L}.
Changing the order of
D_{L}
and
\Delta_{L}^{-1}
we see also that
\Delta_{L}^{-1}D_{L}
is invertible and that
D_{L}
has a left inverse. The latter must agree with the right inverse, which completes the proof.
∎

\Delta_{Y}\oplus E_{Y}

Definition 7.5
.

Recall that
N\setminus\bigsqcup_{x\in X^{\rm sing}}t_{x}K_{x}
lies in the Weinstein neighbourhood of

and write this as the graph of a

(7.8)

Lemma 7.6
.

Using the

norm on
C^{5/2}_{\nu}(Y,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})
and the Euclidean norm on
{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}
give
C^{5/2}_{\nu}(Y,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\oplus{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}
the product norm. The operator (
7.8
) has then a right inverse which is uniformly bounded with respect to

Proof.

Denote by
g
the conical metric on
Y\subseteq X.
We show that there exists
c>0
independent of
s,t
and for which

|g^{\prime\prime}-g|_{C^{3/2}}\leqslant\penalty ct^{2-2\sigma}\text{ at every point of }X

(7.9)

where the
C^{3/2}
norm is computed with respect to the cylindrical metric on

It is clear that this holds on

there. On

which implies the stronger estimate

On each

the
1
-form
\xi
is made from
{\rm d}A_{x},t_{x}^{2}t_{x*}{\rm d}E_{x},{\rm d}T_{x}
and

Computation similar to the proof of (

including the rescale factor

This with

implies (
7.9
).

If we denote by
\nabla^{\prime\prime},\nabla
the Levi-Civita connection of
g^{\prime\prime}
then we find from (

) a constant
c>0
independent of

and such that for

|\nabla^{\prime\prime}\phi-\nabla\phi|_{C^{1/2}}\leqslant\penalty ct^{2-2\sigma}|\phi|_{C^{3/2}}\text{ at every point of }Y.

(7.10)

Computing the

|{\rm d}^{*\prime\prime}\phi-{\rm d}^{*}\phi|_{C^{1/2}}\leqslant\penalty cr^{-2}t^{2-2\sigma}|\phi|_{C^{3/2}}\text{ at every point of }Y.

(7.11)

Hence making

larger if we need, we find that for

|{\rm d}^{*\prime\prime}[(\psi^{s})^{m}{\rm d}u]-{\rm d}^{*}[(\psi^{s})^{m}{\rm d}u]|_{C^{1/2}}\leqslant\penalty cr^{-2}t^{2-2\sigma}|u|_{C^{5/2}}.

(7.12)

Since

it follows that making

larger if we need, we have

|D_{Y}u-\Delta_{Y}u|_{C^{1/2}}\leqslant\penalty cr^{-2}(|s|+t^{2-2\sigma})|u|_{C^{5/2}}.

(7.13)

Passing to the weighted norms we get

|D_{Y}u-\Delta_{Y}u|_{\nu-2}\leqslant\penalty c(|s|+t^{2-2\sigma})|u|_{\nu};
that is,

D_{Y}-\Delta_{Y}:C^{5/2}_{\nu}(Y,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\to C^{1/2}_{\nu-2}(Y,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\text{ has operator norm }\leqslant\penalty c(|s|+t^{2-2\sigma})

(7.14)

with respect to the weighted Hölder norms.

Recall now from
[

, Theorems 2.14 and 2.16(b)]

) is surjective, and from
[

, Theorem 6.9]

has nothing to do with

Proof of Proposition
7.1
.

For each connected component
Y\subseteq X
denote by
R_{Y}\oplus\pi_{Y}:C^{1/2}_{\nu-2}(Y,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\to C^{5/2}_{\nu}(Y,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\oplus{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}
the uniformly bounded right inverse produced in Lemma
7.6
. Define
S:C^{1/2}(N,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\to C^{5/2}(N,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})
by
S:=G_{L}D_{L}^{-1}F_{L}+\sum_{Y\in\pi_{0}X}G_{Y}R_{Y}F_{Y}.
Define
\varpi:C^{1/2}(N,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\to H^{0}(X,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})
by
v\mapsto\sum_{Y\in\pi_{0}X}\pi_{Y}F_{Y}v.
Recall from Definitions
7.3
and
7.5
that
D_{L}=\Delta
on the support of
G_{L}
and that for each connected component
Y\subseteq X
we have
D_{Y}=\Delta
on the support on
G_{Y}.
So fo
v\in C^{1/2}(N,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})
we have

\begin{split}&D_{L}G_{L}D_{L}^{-1}F_{L}v+\sum_{Y\in\pi_{0}X}(D_{Y}G_{Y}R_{Y}F_{Y}v)+\sum_{Y\in\pi_{0}X}G_{Y}E_{Y}\pi_{Y}F_{Y}v\\
&=\Delta G_{L}R_{L}F_{L}v+\sum_{Y\in\pi_{0}X}(\Delta G_{Y}R_{Y}F_{Y}v)+\sum_{Y\in\pi_{0}X}E_{Y}\pi_{Y}F_{Y}v\\
&=\Delta Sv+E\varpi v=(\Delta\oplus E)(S\oplus\varpi)v.\end{split}

(7.15)

On the other hand, since
G_{L}F_{L}=F_{L}
it follows that

D_{L}G_{L}D_{L}^{-1}F_{L}v-[D_{L},G_{L}]F_{L}v=G_{L}F_{L}v=F_{L}v.

(7.16)

For
Y\in\pi_{0}X,
since
(D_{Y}\oplus E_{Y})(R_{Y}\oplus\pi_{Y})={\rm id}
and
G_{Y}F_{Y}=F_{Y}
it follows that

\sum_{Y\in\pi_{0}X}G_{Y}D_{Y}R_{Y}F_{Y}v+\sum_{Y\in\pi_{0}X}G_{Y}E_{Y}\pi_{Y}F_{Y}v=G_{Y}F_{Y}v=\sum_{Y\in\pi_{0}X}F_{Y}v.

(7.17)

Since
F_{L}
and
(F_{Y})_{Y\in\pi_{0}X}
form a partition of unity it follows that the rightmost term
F_{L}v
on (
7.16
) and the rightmost term

\sum_{Y\in\pi_{0}X}F_{Y}v
on (
7.17
) sum up to
v.
This with (
7.15
)–(
7.17
) implies

(\Delta\oplus E)(S\oplus\varpi)v-v=[D_{L},G_{L}]R_{L}F_{L}v+\sum_{Y\in\pi_{0}X}[D_{Y},G_{Y}]R_{Y}F_{Y}v.

(7.18)

Since the bracket terms contains derivatives of
G_{L},G_{Y},
which are
O((-\log t)^{-1}),
we get a constant
c>0
independent of
s,t,v
and satisfying

|(\Delta\oplus E)(S\oplus\varpi)v-v|_{C^{1/2}}\leqslant\penalty c(-\log t)^{-1}|v|_{C^{1/2}}

(7.19)

where the
C^{1/2}
norms are computed with respect to the cylindrical metric on
N.
Passing to the weighted norms we get

|(\Delta\oplus E)(S\oplus\varpi)v-v|_{\nu-2}\leqslant\penalty c(-\log t)^{-1}|v|_{\nu-2}.

(7.20)

The operator
(\Delta\oplus E)(S\oplus\varpi)-{\rm id}:C^{1/2}(N,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\to C^{1/2}(N,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})
has thus operator norm
\leqslant\penalty c(-\log t)^{-1}\ll 1.
So
J:=(\Delta\oplus E)(S\oplus\varpi)
is invertible and
J^{-1}
is bounded uniformly with respect to
s,t.
Now
J^{-1}(S\oplus\varpi)
is a right inverse to
\Delta\oplus E
which is bounded uniformly with respect to
s,t,
which completes the proof of Proposition
7.1
.
∎

We solve the special Lagrangian equation with an additional term.

Corollary 8.1
.

There exists
c>0
independent of
s,t
and such that the following holds: for
(s,t)\in{\mathbin{\cal G}}
close enough to
(0,0)
there exists

such that

with

Proof.

Denote by

the uniformly bounded right inverse to
\Delta\oplus E
produced in Proposition
7.1
. Put
B:=\{v\in C^{1/2}_{\nu-2}(N,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}):|v|_{\nu-2}\leqslant\penalty t^{\theta+\nu}\}.
Since
R
is uniformly bounded it follows that there exists
c_{0}>0
independent of
s,t
and such that for

we have
|Rv|_{\nu}\leqslant\penalty c_{0}t^{\theta+\nu},
which implies that we can define

as in Proposition
6.2
. We can therefore define
T:B\to C^{1/2}_{\nu-2}(N,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})
by

We show that
T
maps
B
to itself. Recall from Corollary
6.3
that there exists
c_{1}>0
independent of
s,t
and such that for
v\in B
we have

|Q{\rm d}Rv|_{\nu-2}\leqslant\penalty c_{1}t^{\nu-2}|Rv|_{\nu-2}^{2}\leqslant\penalty c_{1}c_{0}^{2}t^{\nu-2}t^{2(\theta+\nu)},

(8.1)

where the last inequality follows from the definition of

On the other hand, by Corollary
6.1
we have

|P0|_{\nu-2}\leqslant\penalty|s|+t^{\theta}\leqslant\penalty 2t^{\theta}.

(8.2)

This and (

) imply that

|Tv|_{\nu-2}\leqslant\penalty|P0|_{\nu-2}+|Q{\rm d}Rv|_{\nu-2}\leqslant\penalty(2t^{-\nu}+c_{1}c_{0}^{2}t^{\theta+2\nu-2})t^{\theta+\nu}.

(8.3)

Since
\theta>2-2\nu
and
\nu>0
it follows by (

) that for

small enough we have
Tv\in B.

We have thus defined the map

For

we have

\begin{split}&|Tv-Tv^{\prime}|_{\nu-2}\leqslant\penalty c_{1}t^{\nu-2}|Rv-Rv^{\prime}|_{\nu}(|Rv|_{\nu}+|Rv^{\prime}|_{\nu})\\
&\leqslant\penalty c_{1}c_{0}^{2}t^{\nu-2}|v-v^{\prime}|_{\nu-2}(|v|_{\nu-2}+|v^{\prime}|_{\nu-2})\leqslant\penalty c_{1}c_{0}^{2}t^{\theta+2\nu-2}|v-v^{\prime}|_{\nu-2}.\end{split}

(8.4)

Hence we see, using again

that for

Since

is a right inverse to

∎

We prove now Theorem

for

connected.

for
X
connected.

Regard

Definition
4.4
shows that

converges, as

for

The right-hand side of (

When

Following with modification the proof of Theorem

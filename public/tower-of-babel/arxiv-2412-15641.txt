Composite asymmetric dark matter with a dark photon portal: Multimessenger tests
Saikat Das, Ayuki Kamada, Takumi Kuwahara, Kohta Murase, Deheng Song

Abstract
Composite asymmetric dark matter (ADM) is the framework that naturally explains the coincidence of the baryon density and the dark matter density of the Universe. Through a portal interaction sharing particle-antiparticle asymmetries in the Standard Model and dark sectors, dark matter particles, which are dark-sector counterparts of baryons, can decay into antineutrinos and dark-sector counterparts of mesons (dark mesons) or dark photon. Subsequent cascade decay of the dark mesons and the dark photon can also provide electromagnetic fluxes at late times of the Universe. The cosmic-ray constraints on the decaying dark matter with the mass of 1 1 – 10 10 GeV has not been well studied. We perform comprehensive studies on the decay of the composite ADM by combining the astrophysical constraints from e ± e^{\pm} and γ \gamma -ray. The constraints from cosmic-ray positron measurements by AMS-02 are the most stringent at ≳ 2 \gtrsim 2 GeV: a lifetime should be larger than the order of 10 26 10^{26} s, corresponding to the cutoff scale of the portal interaction of about 10 8 ​ – ​ 10 9 ​ GeV 10^{8}\text{--}10^{9}\,\mathrm{GeV} . We also perform the dedicated analysis for the neutrino monoenergetic signals at Super-Kamiokande and Hyper-Kamiokande due to the atmospheric neutrino background in the energy range of our interest.

rest frame.

\displaystyle\frac{dN_{\psi}}{dx_{1}}=2\int_{-1}^{1}d\cos\theta_{0}\int_{\epsilon_{0}}^{1}dx_{0}\frac{dN_{\psi}}{dx_{0}}f_{0}(\cos\theta_{0})

\displaystyle\quad\times\delta\left[x_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\left(x_{0}+\sqrt{(1-\epsilon_{1}^{2})(x_{0}^{2}-\tilde{\epsilon}_{1}^{2})}\cos\theta_{0}\right)\right]\,.

(11)

Here,
x_{1}=2E_{1}/m_{\pi^{\prime}}
,
\epsilon_{1}=2m_{A^{\prime}}/m_{\pi^{\prime}}
, and
\tilde{\epsilon}_{1}=2m_{\psi}/m_{\pi^{\prime}}
with
E_{1}
the energy of a particle
\psi=e^{\pm}\,,\gamma
in the
\pi^{\prime}
rest frame.
A prefactor two implies the multiplicity of
A^{\prime}
in the
\pi^{\prime}
decay.
The function
f_{0}
denotes the angular distribution of the
A^{\prime}
decay.
For the transverse mode of the dark photon, we take

f_{0}(\cos\theta)=\frac{3}{8}(1+\cos^{2}\theta)\,.

(12)

The delta function gives a relation of the energies in the rest frames of
\pi^{\prime}
and
A^{\prime}
.
Then, we obtain the primary spectrum in the rest frame of the dark baryons by boosting it, again.
The spectrum in the
N^{\prime}
rest frame is given by

\displaystyle\frac{dN_{\psi}}{dx_{2}}=\int^{1}_{-1}d\cos\theta_{1}\int^{1}_{\tilde{\epsilon}_{1}}dx_{1}\frac{dN_{\psi}}{dx_{1}}\times\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\quad\times\delta\left(x_{2}-\frac{1+\epsilon_{2}^{2}}{2}x_{1}-\frac{1-\epsilon_{2}^{2}}{2}\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-\tilde{\epsilon}_{2}^{2}}\cos\theta_{1}\right)\,.

(13)

Here, we assume that antineutrinos in the decay products of the dark baryons are massless.
We define the dimensionless parameters:
x_{2}=2E_{2}/m_{N^{\prime}}
,
\epsilon_{2}=m_{\pi^{\prime}}/m_{N^{\prime}}
, and
\tilde{\epsilon}_{2}=2m_{\psi}/m_{N^{\prime}}
with
E_{2}
the energy of
\psi
in the
N^{\prime}
rest frame.
The factor of
1/2
comes from the angular distribution of the
N^{\prime}
decay.
The mass spectrum in the dark sector is usually hierarchical in the composite ADM models; the dark baryons are much heavier than the dark mesons and the dark photon, in other words,
\epsilon_{2}\ll 1
and
\tilde{\epsilon}_{1}\ll 1
.
In such a case, we may simplify the energy spectrum in
Eq.

13
as follows.

\frac{dN_{\psi}}{dx_{2}}\simeq\int^{1}_{x_{2}}\frac{dx_{1}}{x_{1}}\frac{dN_{\psi}}{dx_{1}}\,.

(14)

In the chiral model, one of the dark mesons is absorbed as the longitudinal mode of the dark photon.
Therefore, the dark photon is directly emitted from the dark baryon in this model:
\Sigma_{3}^{\prime}(\Lambda^{\prime})\to A^{\prime}+\overline{\nu}
in the model with
N_{f}=3
.
We use a simplified formula for this mode since the dark photon is sufficiently lighter than the dark baryons.

\frac{dN_{\psi}}{dx_{2}}\simeq\int^{1}_{x_{2}}\frac{dx_{0}}{x_{0}}\frac{dN_{\psi}}{dx_{0}}2\widetilde{f}_{0}\left(\frac{2x_{2}}{x_{0}}-1\right)\,.

(15)

Here, a relation of the energies in the rest frames of
A^{\prime}
and
N^{\prime}
determines the argument of the angular distribution
\widetilde{f}_{0}
.
We note again that
x_{2}
is the energy of the final state particles normalized by
m_{N^{\prime}}
in the
N^{\prime}
rest frame, while
x_{0}
is the energy normalized by
m_{A^{\prime}}
in the
A^{\prime}
rest frame.
We use the following angular distribution of
A^{\prime}
decay for the longitudinal mode of the dark photon.

\widetilde{f}_{0}(\cos\theta)=\frac{3}{4}(1-\cos^{2}\theta)\,.

(16)

III.2
Three-body decay of the dark meson

We have discussed the case where the
\pi^{\prime}
decay into two dark photons is kinematically allowed, namely
m_{\pi^{\prime}}>2m_{A^{\prime}}
.
Meanwhile, when the mass of the dark photon is close to that of the dark mesons,
\pi^{\prime}
dominantly decays into
A^{\prime}
and a pair of
e^{+}e^{-}(\gamma)
via an off-shell dark photon.
Both vector-like and chiral models have the decay:
n^{\prime}\to\pi^{\prime 0}+\overline{\nu}
and
\pi^{\prime 0}\to A^{\prime}e^{-}e^{+}(\gamma)\to 2(e^{-}e^{+}(\gamma))
in the vector-like composite ADM, while
\Sigma_{3}^{\prime}
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
decay into
\eta^{\prime}
and
\eta^{\prime}
decays into the electromagnetic channel via
\eta^{\prime}\to A^{\prime}e^{-}e^{+}(\gamma)\to 2(e^{-}e^{+}(\gamma))
in the chiral composite ADM.
In
Appendix

B
, we give detailed discussions on the cascade decay with an off-shell dark photon.

Now, we consider the decay
\pi^{\prime}\to A^{\prime}+e^{-}+e^{+}
in the
\pi^{\prime}
rest frame. The energy spectrum of the decay is described by two energy quantities normalized by the
\pi^{\prime}
mass: the energy of
A^{\prime}
,
\xi_{1}=2E_{A^{\prime}}/m_{\pi^{\prime}}
, and that of the electron,
\xi_{2}=2E_{e}/m_{\pi^{\prime}}
in the
\pi^{\prime}
rest frame.
We find the energy spectrum of the three-body decay by ignoring the electron mass as follows.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma_{A^{\prime}e^{+}e^{-}}}\frac{d\Gamma_{A^{\prime}e^{+}e^{-}}}{d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}}=\frac{1}{f(\overline{\epsilon}_{1})}\frac{1+\overline{\epsilon}_{1}^{2}-\xi_{1}}{(1-\xi_{1})^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\left[\xi_{1}^{2}+2\xi_{1}(\xi_{2}-1)+2(\xi_{2}^{2}-2\xi_{2}-\overline{\epsilon}_{1}^{2}+1)\right]\,.

(17)

Here,
\overline{\epsilon}_{1}=m_{A^{\prime}}/m_{\pi^{\prime}}
.
The kinematics of the final state particles determines the range of parameters
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
.
The function
f
is given by

\displaystyle f(t)=-\frac{1}{9}(t^{6}+63t^{4}-81t^{2}-17)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2}{3}(6t^{4}-9t^{2}+1)\ln t

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2(28t^{4}-11t^{2}+1)}{3\sqrt{4t^{2}-1}}\tan^{-1}\frac{3t^{2}-1}{(t^{2}-1)\sqrt{4t^{2}-1}}\,.

(18)

The electron/photon spectrum consists of two contributions: one is from the final state electrons/photon, and another is from the decay of the final state
A^{\prime}
.
The former contribution is easily obtained by integrating the energy spectrum over the kinematic variable
\xi_{1}
and by boosting it to the
N^{\prime}
rest frame.
As for the electron spectrum, we have

\displaystyle\frac{dN_{e^{-}(e^{+})}}{dx_{2}}

\displaystyle=\int^{1-\overline{\epsilon}_{1}^{2}}_{x_{2}}\frac{d\xi_{2}}{\xi_{2}}\frac{dN_{e^{-}(e^{+})}}{d\xi_{2}}\,,

(19)

\displaystyle\frac{dN_{e^{-}(e^{+})}}{d\xi_{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{\Gamma_{A^{\prime}e^{+}e^{-}}}\int_{\xi_{1:\mathrm{min}}}^{\xi_{1:\mathrm{max}}}d\xi_{1}\frac{d\Gamma_{A^{\prime}e^{+}e^{-}}}{d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}}\,.

(20)

Here, we define
x_{2}=2E_{2}/m_{N^{\prime}}
(
E_{2}
denotes the energy of electron in the
N^{\prime}
rest frame), again.
For the fixed energy of electron,
\xi_{2}
, the integration range of
\xi_{1}
is

\xi_{1:\mathrm{min}}=\frac{1+\overline{\epsilon}_{1}^{2}-2\xi_{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}}{(1-\xi_{2})}\,,\quad\xi_{1:\mathrm{max}}=1+\overline{\epsilon}_{1}^{2}\,.

(21)

After the integration on
\xi_{1}
,
\xi_{2}
varies within the range of
[0,1-\overline{\epsilon}_{1}^{2}]
.
We use the approximation that
N^{\prime}
is much heavier than
\pi^{\prime}
to get the spectrum in the
N^{\prime}
rest frame.
In a similar way, we get the photon spectrum as follows.

\displaystyle\frac{dN_{\gamma}}{dx_{2}}

\displaystyle=\int^{1}_{x_{2}}\frac{dx_{0}}{x_{0}}\frac{dN_{\gamma}}{dx_{0}}\,,

(22)

\displaystyle\frac{dN_{\gamma}}{dx_{0}}

\displaystyle=2\int d\xi_{2}d\xi_{1}\frac{1}{\Gamma_{A^{\prime}e^{+}e^{-}}}\frac{d\Gamma_{A^{\prime}e^{+}e^{-}}}{d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}}\frac{dN_{\gamma}}{dr_{0}}\,,

(23)

\displaystyle\frac{dN_{\gamma}}{dr_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\frac{1+(1-r_{0})^{2}}{r_{0}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\ln\frac{4(1-r_{0})(1-\xi_{1}+\overline{\epsilon}_{1}^{2})}{\overline{\epsilon}_{0}^{2}}-1\right)\,.

(24)

Here,
r_{0}\equiv x_{0}/\xi_{2}(=E_{0}/E_{e})
denotes the energy fraction carried by photon from the final state electron (or positron), and
\overline{\epsilon}_{0}=2m_{e}/m_{\pi^{\prime}}
.
One obtains the photon spectrum (
23
) in the
\pi^{\prime}
rest frame by multiplying the energy spectrum of the decay process to the splitting function and integrating over electron (positron) energy
\xi_{2}
and dark photon energy
\xi_{1}
.
The factor two indicates the sum of photon spectra from electron and positron.

For the latter contribution, we first get the energy spectrum of
A^{\prime}
by integrating over the kinematic variable
\xi_{2}
.

\displaystyle\frac{dN_{A^{\prime}}}{d\xi_{1}}\equiv\frac{1}{\Gamma_{A^{\prime}e^{+}e^{-}}}\int_{\xi_{2:\mathrm{min}}}^{\xi_{2:\mathrm{max}}}d\xi_{2}\frac{d\Gamma_{A^{\prime}e^{+}e^{-}}}{d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}}\,.

(25)

Here, the integration range of
\xi_{2}
is given by

\xi_{2:\mathrm{max/min}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(2-\xi_{1}\pm\sqrt{\xi_{1}^{2}-\overline{\epsilon}_{1}^{2}}\right)\,.

(26)

This spectrum is almost flat in the range of
\xi_{1}\in[2\overline{\epsilon}_{1},1+\overline{\epsilon}_{1}^{2}]
.
Approaching the boundaries of the range, the spectrum is steeply diminished.
Then, we obtain the energy spectra of the electron-positron pair and photon from the decay of on-shell
A^{\prime}
in the
\pi^{\prime}
rest frame by the integral

\displaystyle\frac{dN_{\psi}}{dx_{1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\epsilon_{1}}^{1+\overline{\epsilon}_{1}^{2}}d\xi_{1}\int_{-1}^{1}d\cos\theta\int_{\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}}^{1}dx_{0}

\displaystyle\times\frac{dN_{\psi}}{dx_{0}}(x_{0},\epsilon_{0})\frac{dN_{A^{\prime}}}{d\xi_{1}}(\xi_{1},\epsilon_{1})f(\cos\theta)

\displaystyle\times\delta\left(x_{1}-\frac{\xi_{1}}{2}x_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\xi_{1}^{2}-\epsilon_{1}^{2}}\sqrt{x_{0}^{2}-\tilde{\epsilon}_{1}^{2}}\cos\theta\right)\,.

(27)

Here,
dN_{\psi}/dx_{0}
denotes the primary spectra of an electron and photon from the dark photon given by
Eq.

10
.
When taking
dN_{A^{\prime}}/d\xi_{1}\to\delta(\xi_{1}-1)
, this reproduces the spectrum for two-body cascade decay given by
Eq.

11
.
The spectra in the
N^{\prime}
rest frame are obtained by boosting again, and its simplified formula is shown in
Eq.

15
.
In summary, as
m_{A^{\prime}}<m_{\pi^{\prime}}<2m_{A^{\prime}}
,
\pi^{\prime}
decay into two dark photons is not kinematically allowed, and the electron/photon energy spectra are given by the sum of two contributions:

\frac{dN_{\psi}}{dx_{2}}=\left.\frac{dN_{\psi}}{dx_{2}}\right|_{\psi}+\left.\frac{dN_{\psi}}{dx_{2}}\right|_{A^{\prime}}\,,

(28)

where the former denotes the spectra directly from
\pi^{\prime}\to A^{\prime}e^{+}e^{-}(\gamma)
and the second term is the contribution from the decay of dark photon.

At the end of this section, we comment on the energy fractions of
e^{\pm}
,
\gamma
,
\nu
, and invisible particles (darkly-charged dark mesons) from the ADM decay.
In the following, we consider four benchmark models: mass ratios of
2m_{A^{\prime}}/m_{\pi^{\prime}}=0.4\,,1.4
for a minimal model with two-flavor vector-like dark quarks and for a minimal model with three-flavor chiral dark quarks.
We assume the universal DM mass
m_{N^{\prime}}
and the mass ratio
m_{\pi^{\prime}}/m_{N^{\prime}}=0.1
for
2m_{A^{\prime}}/m_{\pi^{\prime}}=0.4
and
m_{\pi^{\prime}}/m_{N^{\prime}}=0.03
for
2m_{A^{\prime}}/m_{\pi^{\prime}}=1.4
.
With these parameters, the dark photon mass
m_{A^{\prime}}
is within the range of
20\,\mathrm{MeV}\text{--}200\,\mathrm{MeV}
for
m_{N^{\prime}}=1\text{--}10\,\mathrm{GeV}
.
This dark photon mass is free from the
N_{f}
constraint of the dark photon parameters
[
17
,
112
]
.
Other final states such as
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
and
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
are not kinematically opened for this mass spectrum.
When the
A^{\prime}
decay into
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
and
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
is allowed, the final states promptly decay into
e^{+}
and
e^{-}
with emitting neutrinos, and hence most of the energy is carried out by neutrinos.
It is expected that the constraints from the astrophysical observations of the
e^{+}\,,e^{-}\,,
and
\gamma
-ray get weaker once the other final states are kinematically allowed.

shows the DM mass dependence of the energy fractions for each benchmark model.
The energy fractions hardly depend on the DM mass and do not significantly change among the benchmark models.
The SM neutrinos (blue-dotted line) take away half of the total energy since the DM decay via the portal interaction always includes neutrinos as the final state.
Therefore, the neutrino signal from the DM decay is monochromatic with an energy equivalent to half of the DM mass, and it does not significantly change among benchmark models.
A quarter is taken away by the darkly charged dark mesons (invisible particles: green dot-dashed line).
The rest of the energy is carried by a pair of electrons and positron (red-solid line) and

IV
Multimessenger constraints

The decay of composite ADM results in the production of electrons/positrons (
e^{\pm}
),
\gamma
rays, and neutrinos (
\nu
)
up to 10 GeV. Terrestrial and space-based multimessenger detectors operating within this energy range may observe the flux of these particles. In this section, we calculate the expected astrophysical signals resulting from the decay of composite ADM. We utilize existing multimessenger data to put 95% confidence level (C.L.) constraints on the lifetime of composite ADM. We consider two values for the ratio between the dark meson and dark photon masses,
2m_{A^{\prime}}/m_{\pi^{\prime}}=0.4
and 1.4, corresponding to the two-body and three-body decay of the dark meson, respectively. For each decay mode, minimal models of vector-like and chiral quarks lead to four specific cases examined in this study.

IV.1
ADM decay spectrum

We consider a dark matter density distribution in our Galaxy given by a generalized form of the Navarro-Frenk-White (NFW) profile
[
113
,
114
]
,

\rho_{\rm DM}(R)=\rho_{\rm sc}\bigg(\dfrac{R}{R_{\rm sc}}\bigg)^{-\gamma}\bigg(\dfrac{1+R/R_{c}}{1+R_{\rm sc}/R_{c}}\bigg)^{-(3-\gamma)},

(29)

where
\gamma=1.2
and the core radius
R_{c}=20
kpc. We consider the distance of the Sun from the Galactic center to be
R_{\rm sc}=8.5
kpc, and the DM density in the solar neighborhood to be
\rho_{\rm sc}c^{2}=0.43
GeV/cm
3

[
115
]
. The boundary of the halo in the direction
\theta
is given as

s_{\rm max}(\theta)=R_{\rm sc}\cos\theta+\sqrt{R_{h}^{2}-R_{\rm sc}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}.

(30)

We take
R_{h}=100
kpc as the size of the Galactic halo.

\gamma
rays and neutrinos propagate along geodesics in space. The line-of-sight integration of their fluxes originating from the direction
\theta
is given as

\displaystyle\Phi(E,\theta)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{4\pi m_{\rm\chi}\tau_{\rm\chi}}\dfrac{dN_{s}}{dE}\int_{0}^{s_{\rm max}(\theta)}\rho_{\chi}(R(s))ds

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\rho_{\rm sc}R_{\rm sc}}{4\pi m_{\chi}\tau_{\chi}}\dfrac{dN_{s}}{dE}\mathcal{J}^{\rm dec}(\theta).

(31)

The Galactic contribution is obtained by performing the following integration up to
\theta=\pi
:

\displaystyle\Phi_{\rm G}(E,\leq\theta)=\dfrac{\rho_{\rm sc}R_{\rm sc}}{4\pi m_{\chi}\tau_{\chi}}\dfrac{dN_{s}}{dE}\mathcal{J}^{\rm dec}_{\Omega}

(32)

\displaystyle\text{where, \ \ \ }\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{\rm dec}=\dfrac{2\pi}{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\theta}\sin\theta d\theta\mathcal{J}^{\rm dec}(\theta).

(33)

Here,
\Omega=2\pi(1-\cos\theta)
is the solid angle of the field of view, and the integration in
Eq.

31
is carried out by changing the variable from line-of-sight coordinate
s
to Galactocentric distance
R
.

It suffices to consider only the Galactic component of
\gamma
-rays for the DM mass range we consider in this study since the extragalactic flux is substantially lower in comparison. Whereas, for decaying DM, the extragalactic contribution is significant for neutrinos, and it is known to be comparable to the Galactic contribution
[
20
,
116
]
. We consider a uniform DM density distribution up to redshift
z=20
for the extragalactic component. In this case, the neutrino flux from the decay of extragalactic ADM is given as

\displaystyle\Phi^{\rm dec}_{\rm EG}(E)=\dfrac{\Omega_{\rm DM}\rho_{c}}{4\pi m_{\rm DM}\tau_{\rm DM}}\int dz\bigg|\dfrac{dt}{dz}\bigg|F(z)\dfrac{dN_{s}^{\rm ob}}{dE}(z_{s}=z),

(34)

where
dN_{s}^{\rm ob}/dE
is the all-flavor neutrino spectrum observed on Earth after propagation of the prompt DM decay spectrum originating at redshift
z
. Here,
\rho_{c}
is the critical density in a flat Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker universe,
|dt/dz|
is the cosmological line element, and
\rho_{\rm DM}=\Omega_{\rm DM}\rho_{c}
. We take
\Omega_{\rm DM}h^{2}=0.113
and
\rho_{c}h^{-2}=1.05\times 10^{-5}
GeV cm
-3
, where
h=0.673
is the dimensionless Hubble constant
[
117
]
. The factor
F(z)
is the redshift evolution of the source population for extragalactic DM, which is assumed to be unity for the decaying ADM.

V
Summary

We presented comprehensive studies on the composite ADM.
We explored four models for ADM decay.
We considered two values of the mass ratio between dark photon and dark meson (
2m_{A}^{\prime}/m_{\pi}^{\prime}
) that provide different dominant channel of the dark meson decay, where each can have either two-flavor vector-like dark quarks or three-flavor chiral dark quarks.

We investigated multimessenger signatures arising from ADM decay, viz.,
e^{+}
,
e^{-}
,
\gamma
rays, and
\nu
-s.
We performed a detailed analysis of the constraints on the ADM decay since the ADM mass lies in the range of
1
–
10
GeV in the composite ADM scenario.
The decay products
e^{\pm}
and
\gamma
from the ADM with the low mass are less energetic, which can be out of the reach of AMS-02 and Fermi-LAT.
Therefore, we also considered the data from other observations sensitive to the low-energy
e^{\pm}
and
\gamma
-ray fluxes.
As for the constraint from the neutrino signals, we put a conservative bound on the ADM lifetime by the approach with the
\chi^{2}
function.

While the constraints from
e^{+}/e^{-}
depend on the specific propagation model employed, they yield the most stringent limits corresponding to
\gtrsim 10^{26}
s for all ADM models.
The limits obtained from
\gamma
rays range between
10^{24}-10^{25}
s, and those obtained from neutrino observations with Super-K are also comparable.
We showed that neutrino observations are useful for obtaining robust constraints, and future neutrino observations with Hyper-K and DUNE can improve the bounds.
In addition, the IceCube-Gen2 upgrade
[
141
]
and the ORCA instrument of KM3Net
[
142
]
will also be sensitive to neutrino detection above
\sim 1
–
3
GeV.

Appendix A
Dark Baryon Decay Rates

Dark baryons decay into dark mesons and antineutrinos through portal interactions given in
Eqs.

3
and
9
.
The dark mesons decay into dark photons only through a chiral anomaly in the dark sector, and hence, most dark baryons decay into the darkly-charged mesons that do not decay into dark photons and leave only the antineutrino signals.
The branching ratios into the dark-neutral mesons leading to the cosmic-ray signals are significant for our purpose.

First, the decay matrix elements can be computed by the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients (CGCs) and a representative element.
The labels of states
|j,m\rangle
, and decay operators
\mathcal{O}_{M}^{(J)}
denote the flavor index.
The decay amplitudes are represented as

\displaystyle\langle j,m|\mathcal{O}_{M}^{(J)}|j^{\prime},m^{\prime}\rangle=\langle j^{\prime},m^{\prime};J,M|j,m\rangle\langle{j}|\mathcal{O}^{(J)}|j^{\prime}\rangle\,.

(39)

Here,
|j^{\prime},m^{\prime}\rangle
denotes the initial dark baryon state, while
\langle j,m|
denotes the final dark meson state.

\langle j^{\prime},m^{\prime};J,M|j,m\rangle
is the Clebsch-Gordan coefficient.

\langle{j}|\mathcal{O}^{(J)}|j^{\prime}\rangle
is referred to as a reduced matrix element, which is independent of the quantum numbers
m\,,m^{\prime}
, and
M
.

Let us consider the composite ADM with
N_{f}=2
vector-like dark quarks.
There is one dark-neutral operator in terms of the group structure of the flavor symmetry, denoted by
\mathcal{O}_{n^{\prime}}=U^{\prime}D^{\prime}D^{\prime}
.
This operator leads to two dark baryon decay processes,
p^{\prime}\to\pi^{\prime+}+\overline{\nu}
and
n^{\prime}\to\pi^{\prime 0}+\overline{\nu}
, see
Eq.

2
in the text.
The baryon states and the baryon operators are in the fundamental representation of
SU(2)
, while the meson states are in the adjoint representation of
SU(2)
.
Since the irreducible decomposition of
\mathbf{2}\otimes\mathbf{2}
includes a single
\mathbf{3}
representation, the decay amplitudes are parameterized by a single reduced matrix
A_{n}
and are related to each other by the CGCs.

\displaystyle\langle\pi^{\prime+}|\mathcal{O}_{n^{\prime}}|p^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=A_{n}\,,

(40)

\displaystyle\langle\pi^{\prime 0}|\mathcal{O}_{n^{\prime}}|n^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}A_{n}\,.

(41)

Therefore, the fraction decaying into
\pi^{\prime 0}
is independent of the model parameters.

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(n^{\prime}\to\pi^{\prime 0}\overline{\nu})}{\Gamma(n^{\prime}\to\pi^{\prime 0}\overline{\nu})+\Gamma(p^{\prime}\to\pi^{\prime+}\overline{\nu})}=\frac{1}{3}\,.

(42)

Next, let us consider the composite ADM with
N_{f}=3
chiral dark quarks.
There are two dark-neutral operators consisting of
U^{\prime}\,,D^{\prime}\,,S^{\prime}
: one is an operator
\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
transforming as
\Lambda^{\prime}
(singlet under the isospin-like subgroup “dark isospin” of flavor symmetry), and another is an operator
\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
transforming as
\Sigma^{\prime}_{3}
(a neutral component of a triplet of the dark isospin).
Contrast to the case with
N_{f}=2

\mathbf{8}\otimes\mathbf{8}
includes two
\mathbf{8}
representations.
The decay amplitudes are parameterized by two reduced matrix elements
A^{(1)}
and
A^{(2)}
and are related to each other by the CGCs of
SU(3)
.
One can find the CGCs of
SU(3)
in Ref.
[
143
]
.
The matrix elements for the operator
\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
are

\displaystyle\langle\pi^{\prime}_{3}|\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}|\Sigma^{\prime}_{3}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}A^{(1)}\,,

(43)

\displaystyle\langle\pi^{\prime}|\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}|\Sigma^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}A^{(1)}\,,

(44)

\displaystyle\langle\pi^{\prime{\dagger}}|\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}|\Sigma^{\prime{\dagger}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}A^{(1)}\,,

(45)

\displaystyle\langle\eta^{\prime}|\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}|\Lambda^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}A^{(1)}\,,

(46)

\displaystyle\langle K^{\prime}_{1}|\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}|p^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{5}}A^{(1)}+\frac{1}{2}A^{(2)}\,,

(47)

\displaystyle\langle K^{\prime}_{2}|\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}|n^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{5}}A^{(1)}+\frac{1}{2}A^{(2)}\,,

(48)

\displaystyle\langle K^{\prime{\dagger}}_{1}|\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}|\Xi^{\prime}_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{5}}A^{(1)}-\frac{1}{2}A^{(2)}\,,

(49)

\displaystyle\langle K^{\prime{\dagger}}_{2}|\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}|\Xi^{\prime}_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{5}}A^{(1)}-\frac{1}{2}A^{(2)}\,.

(50)

On the other hand, the matrix elements for the operator
\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
are

\displaystyle\langle\eta^{\prime}|\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}|\Sigma^{\prime}_{3}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}A^{(1)}\,,

(51)

\displaystyle\langle\pi^{\prime}|\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}|\Sigma^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}A^{(2)}\,,

(52)

\displaystyle\langle\pi^{\prime{\dagger}}|\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}|\Sigma^{\prime{\dagger}}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}A^{(2)}\,,

(53)

\displaystyle\langle\pi^{\prime}_{3}|\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}|\Lambda^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}A^{(1)}\,,

(54)

\displaystyle\langle K^{\prime}_{1}|\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}|p^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{3}{5}}A^{(1)}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}A^{(2)}\,,

(55)

\displaystyle\langle K^{\prime}_{2}|\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}|n^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{3}{5}}A^{(1)}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}A^{(2)}\,,

(56)

\displaystyle\langle K^{\prime{\dagger}}_{1}|\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}|\Xi^{\prime}_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{3}{5}}A^{(1)}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}A^{(2)}\,,

(57)

\displaystyle\langle K^{\prime{\dagger}}_{2}|\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}|\Xi^{\prime}_{2}\rangle

(58)

.

\widetilde{A}^{(i)}_{N}=M^{-3}_{N}A^{(i)}

Appendix C
Equivalence Theorem

One of the dark mesons is absorbed as the longitudinal mode of the dark photon in the chiral composite ADM.
Some dark-neutral baryons decay into the longitudinal dark photon through the dark meson.
However, the dark-neutral baryons do not couple to the dark photon at the level of kinetic terms.
In this appendix, we demonstrate the equivalence of the decay rate of the dark nucleon into dark meson and the dark photon at high energy.
We consider dark nucleon transition into another (off-shell) dark nucleon with emitting dark mesons in terms of baryon chiral perturbation theory instead of the decay induced by the portal operators (
3
) and (
9
).
After the transition, the (off-shell) dark nucleon is converted into antineutrino through the portal operators.
Under the flavor symmetry
SU(3)_{L}\times SU(3)_{R}
(their transformation matrices denoted by
L
and
R
), the dark baryon fields transform as
B^{\prime}\to LB^{\prime}L^{\dagger}
and
\overline{B}^{\prime}\to R\overline{B}^{\prime}R^{\dagger}
and the non-linear sigma field
U=\exp(2i\Pi^{\prime}/f_{\pi^{\prime}})
transforms as
U\to LUR^{\dagger}
.
The interaction Lagrangian up to
\mathcal{O}(p^{2})
terms of chiral perturbation between the baryon fields and the non-linear sigma field are

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\supset 2i\mathrm{Tr}\left[B^{\prime{\dagger}}\overline{\sigma}^{\mu}D_{\mu}B^{\prime}+\overline{B}^{\dagger}\overline{\sigma}^{\mu}D_{\mu}\overline{B}^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+2\mathrm{Tr}\left[m_{B}\overline{B}^{\prime}U^{\dagger}B^{\prime}U+m_{B}^{\ast}U^{\dagger}B^{\prime{\dagger}}U\overline{B}^{\prime{\dagger}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+2i\mathrm{Tr}\left[x\,(D_{\mu}U)U^{\dagger}B^{\prime{\dagger}}\overline{\sigma}^{\mu}B^{\prime}+\overline{x}\,(D_{\mu}U)^{\dagger}U\overline{B}^{\prime{\dagger}}\overline{\sigma}^{\mu}\overline{B}^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+2i\,\mathrm{Tr}\left[yB^{\prime{\dagger}}\overline{\sigma}^{\mu}(D_{\mu}U)U^{\dagger}B^{\prime}+\overline{y}\overline{B}^{\prime{\dagger}}\overline{\sigma}^{\mu}(D_{\mu}U)^{\dagger}U\overline{B}^{\prime}\right]\,.

(72)

Here,
\mathrm{Tr}
denotes the trace of flavor indices.
The
U(1)_{D}
charge of left-handed dark quarks is proportional to the Cartan subgroup
U(1)_{3}^{\prime}
of the vectorial
SU(3)_{V}
corresponding to a generator
\lambda_{3}
, while that of right-handed dark quarks differs from the left-handed counterpart by a factor of
a
.
Hence, the covariant derivatives of the baryon fields is

D_{\mu}B^{\prime}=\partial_{\mu}B^{\prime}-ie^{\prime}A^{\prime}_{\mu}[\lambda^{3},B^{\prime}]
and
D_{\mu}\overline{B}=\partial_{\mu}\overline{B}-ie^{\prime}aA^{\prime}_{\mu}[\lambda^{3},\overline{B}]
, while that of the non-linear sigma field is
D_{\mu}U=\partial_{\mu}U-ie^{\prime}A^{\prime}_{\mu}\lambda^{3}U+ie^{\prime}aA^{\prime}_{\mu}U\lambda^{3}
.
Since
(D_{\mu}U)^{\dagger}=-U^{\dagger}(D_{\mu}U)U^{\dagger}
, the interaction terms are real when coupling constants
x\,,y\,,\overline{x}\,,
and
\overline{y}
are real.
The relevant interactions among the dark-neutral baryons and dark photon/dark meson are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\supset\frac{e}{\sqrt{3}}(1-a)(x+y)\left(\Lambda^{\prime{\dagger}}\overline{\sigma}^{\mu}\Sigma^{\prime}_{3}+\Sigma_{3}^{\prime{\dagger}}\sigma^{\mu}\Lambda^{\prime}\right)A^{\prime}_{\mu}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1-a)(x+y)\left(\Lambda^{\prime{\dagger}}\overline{\sigma}^{\mu}\Sigma^{\prime}_{3}+\Sigma_{3}^{\prime{\dagger}}\sigma^{\mu}\Lambda^{\prime}\right)\frac{\partial_{\mu}\pi_{3}^{\prime}}{f_{\pi^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle-\frac{e}{\sqrt{3}}(1-a)(\overline{x}+\overline{y})\left(\overline{\Lambda}^{\prime{\dagger}}\overline{\sigma}^{\mu}\overline{\Sigma}^{\prime}_{3}+\overline{\Sigma}_{3}^{\prime{\dagger}}\sigma^{\mu}\overline{\Lambda}^{\prime}\right)A^{\prime}_{\mu}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1-a)(\overline{x}+\overline{y})\left(\overline{\Lambda}^{\prime{\dagger}}\overline{\sigma}^{\mu}\overline{\Sigma}^{\prime}_{3}+\overline{\Sigma}_{3}^{\prime{\dagger}}\sigma^{\mu}\overline{\Lambda}^{\prime}\right)\frac{\partial_{\mu}\pi_{3}^{\prime}}{f_{\pi^{\prime}}}\,.

(73)

Now, we explicitly calculate two amplitudes for a process
\Sigma_{3}^{\prime}(p)\to\Lambda^{\prime}(k)+A^{\prime}_{\mu}(q)
and for a process
\Sigma_{3}^{\prime}(p)\to\Lambda^{\prime}(k)+\pi^{\prime}_{3}(q)
, and then we take a high-energy limit.
For the former process, the amplitude is

\displaystyle i\mathcal{M}(\Sigma^{\prime}_{3}\rightarrow\Lambda^{\prime}+A^{\prime}_{\mu})

\displaystyle=i\frac{e^{\prime}(1-a)}{2\sqrt{3}}(x+y+\overline{x}+\overline{y})\overline{u}(k)\gamma^{\mu}u(p)\epsilon^{\ast}_{L\mu}(q)

\displaystyle\quad-i\frac{e^{\prime}(1-a)}{2}(x+y-\overline{x}-\overline{y})\overline{u}(k)\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}u(p)\epsilon^{\ast}_{L\mu}(q)\,.

(74)

Here,
\epsilon_{L\mu}
denotes the longitudinal polarization of dark photon, and
\epsilon_{L\mu}(q)\to q_{\mu}/{m_{A^{\prime}}}
at high energy.
On the other hand, for the latter process, the amplitude is given by

\displaystyle i\mathcal{M}(\Sigma^{\prime}_{3}\rightarrow\Lambda^{\prime}+\pi^{\prime}_{3})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}(x+y+\overline{x}+\overline{y})\overline{u}(k)\frac{\not{\!p}\,-\!\not{\!k}}{f_{\pi^{\prime}}}u(p)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}(x+y-\overline{x}-\overline{y})\overline{u}(k)\frac{\not{\!p}\,-\!\not{\!k}}{f_{\pi^{\prime}}}\gamma_{5}u(p)\,.

(75)

Reminding that the dark photon mass is
e^{\prime}(1-a)f_{\pi^{\prime}}
in the chiral ADM model, one can find the decay rates agree with each other.

The dark baryon matrix elements are computed in this effective theory, and one can find the elements are written in terms of the coupling constants in the effective theory.
Meanwhile, as we discussed in
Appendix

A
, the matrix elements for different processes are related to each other by CGCs.
We verify that the matrix elements for the dark nucleon transitions (with the off-shell dark baryon in the final state) satisfy the CGCs relations.
We define the decay amplitude corresponding to the matrix element
A^{(1)}
in
Appendix

A
by the decay

.

\displaystyle i\mathcal{M}(\Sigma^{\prime}_{3}\rightarrow\Lambda^{\prime}+\pi^{\prime}_{3})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}i\mathcal{M}^{(1)}\,.

(76)

Here, the prefactor is from the CGCs.
Comparing these with
Eq.

75
, one finds

\displaystyle i\mathcal{M}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{5}{3}}(x+y+\overline{x}+\overline{y})\overline{u}(k)\frac{\not{\!p}\,-\!\not{\!k}}{f_{\pi^{\prime}}}u(p)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{5}{3}}(x+y-\overline{x}-\overline{y})\overline{u}(k)\frac{\not{\!p}\,-\!\not{\!k}}{f_{\pi^{\prime}}}\gamma_{5}u(p)\,.

(77)

We can compute the decay

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}i\mathcal{M}^{(2)}\,.

(78)

Then, one finds

\displaystyle i\mathcal{M}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}(x-y-\overline{x}+\overline{y})\overline{u}(k)\frac{\not{\!p}\,-\!\not{\!k}}{f_{\pi^{\prime}}}u(p)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}(x-y+\overline{x}-\overline{y}-2)\overline{u}(k)\frac{\not{\!p}\,-\!\not{\!k}}{f_{\pi^{\prime}}}\gamma_{5}u(p)\,.

Using these relations, we confirm the relations among the decay matrix elements.
The matrix elements for other decay processes are

\displaystyle i\mathcal{M}(p^{\prime}\rightarrow\Sigma_{3}^{\prime}+\pi^{\prime}_{3})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(x+\overline{y})\overline{u}(k)\frac{\not{\!p}\,-\!\not{\!k}}{f_{\pi^{\prime}}}u(p)

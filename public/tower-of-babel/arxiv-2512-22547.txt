The fibre operators in the Bloch-Floquet decomposition of periodic magnetic pseudo-differential operators
Horia D. Cornean, Bernard Helffer, Radu Purice

Abstract
We study the structure of the fibre operators corresponding to periodic magnetic pseudo-differential operators having periodic magnetic potentials. We obtain explicit formulas for their distribution kernel, both when these fibres are seen as operators on the d d -dimensional torus, and also when they are seen as infinite matrices acting on a discrete ℓ 2 \ell^{2} space via a discrete Fourier transform. Moreover, using these distribution kernels we prove that the fibre operators are toroidal pseudo-differential operators.

1.3
A brief summary of our main results

Our main interest in this paper is to study the structure of the fibre operators of periodic magnetic pseudo-differential operators in their decomposition in the direct integral defined by the Bloch-Floquet transformation.

•

An essential starting point of our analysis is Theorem
2.2
which shows that for periodic magnetic fields admitting
periodic vector potentials
, the magnetic symmetric quantization as developed in
[
22
,
15
]
-
[
17
]
and the usual Weyl quantization (see
[
14
]
) are equivalent. We do this by constructing the “usual” Weyl symbol starting from the “magnetic” one.
In other words, the results proved in Theorem
2.2
and Proposition
2.5
show that for such “zero mean flux” periodic magnetic fields it is enough to study the Bloch-Floquet theory for periodic Weyl operators. This does not apply to magnetic fields with an “incommensurate” non-zero mean flux. As a large number of applications deal with superpositions of different magnetic fields, a unified treatment is necessary and Proposition
2.5
opens the way to such procedures.

•

In Section
2
we present the extension to pseudo-differential operators of the Bloch-Floquet theory, well-known in the case of periodic differential operators, emphasizing the case of unbounded elliptic Hörmander type symbols (see Propositions
2.11
,
2.23
and
2.24
and Remark
2.25
).

•

Theorem
2.22
is also one of our main results. There we obtain explicit formulas for the distribution kernel of the fibre operators as operators on the
d
-dimensional torus, and for the infinite matrices associated with these operators on
\ell^{2}(\Gamma_{*})
via an inverse discrete Fourier transform. Using the distribution kernels we prove that the fibre operators are toroidal pseudo-differential operators in the sense of
[
27
,
26
,
21
]
and are exactly the pseudo-differential operators on the torus obtained by replacing the differential canonical operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by the invariant differential operators on the torus as in (
1.6
).

•

Another main objective of this paper is to construct a
symmetric Weyl calculus
, similar to the usual Weyl calculus on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, associated to the
d
-dimensional torus as abelian Lie group, (case not covered by the results in
[
29
]
) and prove that in the Bloch-Floquet representation the fibres of any periodic Weyl pseudo-differential operator are such symmetric toroidal operators. This is done in Section
3
. In fact, as we conclude in our final Remark
3.9
, we may view the Bloch-Floquet representations of these periodic Weyl operators as a kind of Weyl calculus associated to the principal bundle
\mathcal{X}\rightY\hskip-4.0pt\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}
. This is the “exact” (unperturbed) version of the functional calculus associated to what the authors call “building block kinetic operators” in
[
7
]
. We also discuss the connection of this Weyl pseudo-differential calculs on the
d
-dimensional torus with the usual approaches appearing in the literature.

Let us end this brief description of our results by emphasizing that one may extend our analysis to magnetic fields which, instead of obeying a zero flux condition as in (
2.1
), are allowed to have a rational flux through the unit cell
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{X}
. Moreover, see for example
[
11
]
, some more general types of magnetic fields may be considered by replacing the usual invariant differential operators on the
d
-dimensional torus with some non-trivial connection.

2
The Bloch-Floquet decomposition of periodic magnetic pseudo-differential operators.

2.1
Periodic magnetic pseudo-differential operators

Definition 2.1
.

For
p\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
{S}^{p}_{1}(\Xi)_{\Gamma}
the functions
F\in{S}^{p}_{1}(\Xi)
that are
\Gamma
-periodic in the first variable, i.e.
(\tau_{\gamma}\otimes{\rm{1}\hskip-3.0pt\mathbf{l}}_{\mathcal{X}^{*}})F=F
for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

The typical example is the symbol of the Schrödinger operator
F_{0}(x,\xi)=(2\pi)^{2}\xi^{2}+V(x)
, where
V\in{BC}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})
is
\Gamma
-periodic.

The periodic-0-mean-face magnetic fields.

When working with
\Gamma
-periodic
p
-forms we have to take into account that the topology of the
d
-dimensional torus is not trivial. We shall call
periodic-0-mean-face magnetic field
, a periodic smooth magnetic field that has the following property:

\text{For }1\leq j,k\leq d:\quad\int\limits_{\text{\rm R}_{jk}}B=0,\quad\text{where}\quad\text{\rm R}_{jk}:=\big\{s\mathfrak{e}_{j}+t\mathfrak{e}_{k},(s,t)\in[0,1]^{2}\big\}.

(2.1)

In
[
13
]
the authors present an elegant cohomological argument proving that the above hypothesis is a necessary and sufficient condition for the magnetic field to be an exact 2-form on the
d
-dimensional torus and thus for the existence of a smooth
\Gamma
-periodic vector potential
A
, such that
B=dA
. We notice that in this situation
A\in{{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}_{\text{\tt bd}}^{1}(\mathcal{X})
and we have the following proposition.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose given a symbol
F\in{S}^{p}_{1}(\Xi)
for some
p\in\mathbb{R}
and a regular magnetic field
B=dA
with
A\in{{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}_{\text{\tt bd}}^{1}(\mathcal{X})
. Then
\mathfrak{Op}^{A}(F)
also has a “standard” Weyl symbol
F^{A}_{W}\in{S}^{p}_{1}(\Xi)
such that
\mathfrak{Op}^{A}(F)=\mathfrak{Op}(F^{A}_{W})
. Moreover we have that:

F^{A}_{W}(x,\xi)-F\big(x,\xi-A(x)\big)\in{S}^{p-2}_{1}(\Xi)

(2.2)

and thus

\mathscr{H}^{p}_{A}(\mathcal{X})=\mathscr{H}^{p}(\mathcal{X})\,.

(2.3)

Proof.

From (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) the integral kernel of
\mathfrak{Op}^{A}(F)
equals

e^{i\int_{0}^{1}\langle\,A(x^{\prime}+t(x-x^{\prime}))\,,\,(x-x^{\prime})\rangle}\int_{\mathcal{X}^{*}}d\xi\,e^{i<\xi,(x-x^{\prime})>}F\big((x+x^{\prime})/2,\xi\big)\,,

or in the variables
u=(x+x^{\prime})/2
and
v=x-x^{\prime}
:

K(u,v)=e^{i\int_{0}^{1}ds\,\langle{A}(u+(s-1/2)v)\,,\,v\rangle}\int_{\mathcal{X}^{*}}d\xi\,e^{i<\xi,v>}F(u,\xi).

Let
\mathcal{I}^{A}(u,v):=\int_{0}^{1}ds\,A\big(u+(s-1/2)v\big)
and notice that
\mathcal{I}^{A}(u,0)=A(u)
. Then

K(u,v)=\int_{\mathcal{X}^{*}}d\xi\,e^{i<\xi,v>}F\big(\xi-\mathcal{I}^{A}(u,v),u\big)

that corresponds to a “standard” Weyl symbol:

\displaystyle F_{W}^{A}(u,\eta)=\int_{\mathcal{X}}dv\,e^{-i<\eta\,v>}\int_{\mathcal{X}^{*}}d\xi\,e^{i<\xi,v>}F\big(u,\xi-\mathcal{I}^{A}(u,v)\big)

\displaystyle=F(u,\eta-A(u))+\int_{\mathcal{X}}dv\,e^{-i<\eta,v>}\int_{\mathcal{X}^{*}}d\xi\,e^{i<\xi,v>}\Big(F\big(u,\xi-\mathcal{I}^{A}(u,v)\big)-F\big(u,\xi-\mathcal{I}^{A}(u,0)\big)\Big).

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
let us define

G(t):=F\big(u,\xi-\mathcal{I}^{A}(u,tv)\big)
and make a Taylor expansion near
t=0
:

\displaystyle G(1)-G(0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(j!)^{-1}\frac{d^{j}}{dt^{j}}F\big(u,\xi-\mathcal{I}^{A}(u,tv)\big)|_{t=0}+(n!)^{-1}\int_{0}^{1}dt\,(1-t)^{n}\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}F\big(u,\xi-\mathcal{I}^{A}(u,tv)\big).

The term
\frac{d^{j}}{dt^{j}}F\big(u,\xi-\mathcal{I}^{A}(u,tv)\big)|_{t=0}
generates terms which contain at least one partial derivative of
F
with respect to
\xi
and homogeneous polynomial factors of degree
j
in
v
. By partial integration in
\xi
, these polynomial factors in
v
are transformed in partial derivatives
with respect to
\xi
acting on
F
, hence producing symbols belonging (at least) to the class
S_{1}^{p-j-1}(\Xi)
. Now consider the remainder (without the irrelevant constant factors):

\tilde{F}^{A}_{n}(u,\eta):=\int_{\mathcal{X}}dv\,e^{-i<\eta,v>}\int_{\mathcal{X}^{*}}d\xi\,e^{i<\xi,v>}\int_{0}^{1}dt\,(1-t)^{n}\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}F\big(u,\xi-\mathcal{I}^{A}(u,tv)\big).

The derivative
\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}F\big(u,\xi-\mathcal{I}^{A}(u,tv)\big)
generates homogeneous polynomials of degree
n
in
v
, which by partial integration in
\xi
are turned into a decay of order
<\xi>^{-n+p}
. Taking partial derivatives of
\tilde{F}^{A}_{n}
with respect to
\eta
produces even more
v
’s which only improve the decay in
\xi
. By multiplying
\tilde{F}^{A}_{n}
with factors
\eta_{k}^{\alpha}
and after partial integration with respect to
v
we may generate growing factors like
\xi_{k}^{\alpha}
, which can be controlled by choosing
n
large enough. In any case, every derivative with respect to
\eta
improves the decay in
\xi
with “one unit”, while multiplication with a factor
\eta_{k}
may reduce the decay in
\xi
with “one unit”. This shows that given any
M>0
, by choosing
n
large enough we have
\tilde{F}_{n}\in S_{1}^{-M}(\Xi)
.

To prove (
2.3
) it remains to prove that

\mathscr{H}^{p}_{A}(\mathcal{X})=\big\{f\in{L}^{2}(\mathcal{X}),\ \mathfrak{Op}^{A}(\mathfrak{m}_{p})f\in{L}^{2}(\mathcal{X})\big\}=\big\{f\in{L}^{2}(\mathcal{X}),\ \mathfrak{Op}\big([\mathfrak{m}_{p}]^{A}_{W}\big)f\in{L}^{2}(\mathcal{X})\big\}\,.

(2.4)

By (
2.2
), we have

[\mathfrak{m}_{p}]^{A}_{W}(x,\xi)=\mathfrak{m}_{p}\big(x,\xi-A(x)\big)\,+r_{p-2}(x,\xi)\,,

(2.5)

and we now observe that

\mathfrak{m}_{p}\big(x,\xi-A(x)\big)=\mathfrak{m}_{p}(x,\xi)+\check{r}_{p-1}(x,\xi)\,,

(2.6)

with, noticing the boundness of
A
,

\check{r}_{p-1}(x,\xi)=\int_{0}^{1}ds\,<\big(\nabla_{\xi}\mathfrak{m}_{p}\big)\big(x,\xi-sA(x)\,,\,A(x)>\;\in{S}^{p-1}_{1}(\Xi)\,.

(2.7)

Hence

[\mathfrak{m}_{p}]^{A}_{W}(x,\xi)-\mathfrak{m}_{p}\big(x,\xi\big)\in{S}^{p-1}_{1}(\Xi)\,,

and we may conclude (
2.4
).
∎

From here on we shall assume that:

Hypothesis 2.3
.

The regular magnetic field
B\in{{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}_{\text{\tt bd}}^{2}(\mathcal{X})
is a periodic 0-mean-face field, i.e. satisfying (
2.1
).
Under this assumption, we denote by
A\in{{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}_{\text{\tt bd}}^{1}(\mathcal{X})
a periodic vector potential such that
B=dA
.

Remark 2.4
.

If
F\in{S}^{p}_{1}(\Xi)_{\Gamma}
for some
p\in\mathbb{R}
and Hypothesis
2.3
holds true, Theorem B implies that
\mathfrak{O}^{A}(F):=\overline{\mathfrak{Op}^{A}(F)}
is a lower semi-bounded self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}(\mathcal{X})
that commutes with all the translations
U(\gamma):=W(\gamma,0)
for
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

If we suppose that a second regular magnetic field
\widetilde{B}=d\widetilde{A}\in{{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}_{\text{\tt bd}}^{2}(\mathcal{X})
with
\widetilde{A}\in{{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}_{\text{\tt pol}}^{1}(\mathcal{X})
is present, we may consider the operator
\widetilde{\mathfrak{O}}(F):=\overline{\mathfrak{Op}^{A+\widetilde{A}}(F)}:\mathscr{H}^{p}_{\widetilde{A}}(\mathcal{X})\rightarrow{L}^{2}(\mathcal{X})
that due to Theorem B will be a lower semi-bounded self-adjoint operator.

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose given a symbol
F\in{S}^{p}_{1}(\Xi)_{\Gamma}
for some
p\in\mathbb{R}
and two magnetic fields
B=dA
periodic, satisfying Hypothesis
2.3
and
\widetilde{B}=d\widetilde{A}
in
{{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}_{\text{\tt bd}}^{2}(\mathcal{X})
with vector potentials
A\in{{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}_{\text{\tt bd}}^{1}(\mathcal{X})
periodic and
\widetilde{A}\in{{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}_{\text{\tt pol}}^{1}(\mathcal{X})
. Then:

\mathfrak{Op}^{A+\widetilde{A}}(F)\,=\,\mathfrak{Op}^{\widetilde{A}}\big(F^{A}_{W}\big)

with:

F^{A}_{W}\,=\,({\rm{1}\hskip-3.0pt\mathbf{l}}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{X}})\big(\mathfrak{K}^{A}[F]\circ\Upsilon^{-1}\big)\,=\,({\rm{1}\hskip-3.0pt\mathbf{l}}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{X}})\big((\Lambda^{A}\mathfrak{K}[F])\circ\Upsilon^{-1}\big)\in{S}^{p}_{1}(\Xi)\,.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Op}^{A+\widetilde{A}}(F)=\mathfrak{I}\mathit{nt}\,\mathfrak{K}^{A+\widetilde{A}}[F],

\displaystyle\mathfrak{K}^{A+\widetilde{A}}[F]=\Lambda^{\widetilde{A}+A}\mathfrak{K}[F]=\Lambda^{\widetilde{A}}\Lambda^{A}\mathfrak{K}[F]=\Lambda^{\widetilde{A}}\mathfrak{K}^{A}[F],

\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}\mathit{nt}\,\Lambda^{\widetilde{A}}\mathfrak{K}^{A}[F]=:\mathfrak{Op}^{\widetilde{A}}\big(F^{A}_{W}\big)\,,

and using (
1.9
) one obtains the desired formula for
F^{A}_{W}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\Xi)
. The fact that
F^{A}_{W}\in{S}^{p}_{1}(\Xi)
follows from Theorem
2.2
using also that
A\in{{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}_{\text{\tt bd}}^{1}(\mathcal{X})
.
∎

Conclusion 2.6
.

Having in view the results of this paragraph, from now on we deal only with periodic Weyl pseudo-differential operators
\mathfrak{Op}(F)
with
F\in{S}^{p}_{1}(\Xi)_{\Gamma}
with
p\in\mathbb{R}
, and cover also the magnetic quantization of these symbols in a periodic magnetic field satisfying Hypothesis (
2.3
).

Finally let us consider a linear operator
T\in\mathcal{L}\big(\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{X});\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathcal{X})\big)
that commutes with all the unitary translations with lattice elements
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Due to the Schwartz Kernels Theorem (
[
28
]
), there exists a distribution
\mathfrak{K}_{T}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X})
such that:
\big(\psi\,,\,T\phi\big)_{L^{2}(\mathcal{X})}=\big\langle\mathfrak{K}_{T}\,,\,\overline{\psi}\otimes\phi\big\rangle_{\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X})}
. The commutation property with the translations with elements from
\Gamma
clearly imply the equalities:

(\tau_{\gamma}\otimes\tau_{\gamma})\mathfrak{K}_{T}=\mathfrak{K}_{T},\quad\forall\gamma\in\Gamma

(2.8)

and we notice that the oscillating factor
\Lambda^{A}
with
\Gamma
-periodic vector potential
A\in{{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}_{\text{\tt bd}}^{1}(\mathcal{X})
satisfies similar identities:

\Lambda^{A}(x+\gamma,y+\gamma)\,=\,\Lambda^{A}(x,y),\quad\forall\gamma\in\Gamma.

(2.9)

Moreover, we notice that formula (
1.9
) of the distribution kernel of a Weyl quantized operator has the inverse:

F=\big({\rm{1}\hskip-3.0pt\mathbf{l}}_{\mathcal{X}}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{X}}\big)\big[[\Lambda^{A}]^{-1}\mathfrak{K}^{A}[F]\big]\circ\Upsilon^{-1}

(2.10)

and we conclude that given
T\in\mathcal{L}\big(\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{X});\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathcal{X})\big)
that commutes with all the unitary translations with lattice elements
\gamma\in\Gamma
, for any
\Gamma
-periodic vector potential
A\in{{\mathbf{\Lambda}}}_{\text{\tt bd}}^{1}(\mathcal{X})
there exists a unique
\Gamma
-periodic symbol
F^{A}_{T}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\Xi)_{\Gamma}
such that:

F^{A}_{T}=\big({\rm{1}\hskip-3.0pt\mathbf{l}}_{\mathcal{X}}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{X}}\big)\big[[\Lambda^{A}]^{-1}\mathfrak{K}_{T}\big]\circ\Upsilon^{-1}

(2.11)

i.e.
T=\mathfrak{Op}^{A}(F^{A}_{T})
.

2.2
Two unitary transformations

Let us recall very briefly the two unitary transformations allowing to study the structure of the
\Gamma
-periodic operators in
L^{2}(\mathcal{X})
.

The Bloch-Floquet (BF) transformation.

For
\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}\in\mathcal{Q}_{*}
we define the complex space:

\mathscr{F}_{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}}\,:=\,\big\{\hat{f}\in{L}^{2}_{\text{\tt loc}}(\mathcal{X}),\ \tau_{\gamma}f=\chi_{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}}(\gamma)\,f,\ \forall\gamma\in\Gamma\big\}

(2.12)

and endow it with the scalar product:

\big(\hat{f}\,,\,\hat{g}\big)_{\mathscr{F}_{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}}}\,:=\,\int_{\mathcal{E}}d\hat{x}\,\overline{\hat{f}(\hat{x})}\,\hat{g}(\hat{x}).

(2.13)

that makes it a Hilbert space and define the direct integral
\mathscr{F}\ :=\ \int_{{\mathcal{Q}}_{*}}^{\oplus}\hskip-2.0ptd\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}\,\mathscr{F}_{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}}
in the sense of
[
8
]
.
One easily verifies that the following map defines a unitary operator
L^{2}(\mathcal{X})\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}\mathscr{F}
:

\big(\mathcal{U}_{BF}f\big)(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*},x)\ :=\,\underset{\gamma\in\Gamma}{\sum}\,e^{-i<\mathfrak{s}_{*}(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}),\gamma>}\,f(x+\gamma)

(2.14)

having the inverse:

\big(\mathcal{U}_{BF}^{-1}\hat{f}\big)(\hat{x}+\alpha)\,=\,\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{*}}\hskip-4.0ptd\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}\,e^{i<\mathfrak{s}_{*}(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}),\alpha>}\,\hat{f}(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*},\hat{x}).

(2.15)

Proposition 2.7
.

\mathcal{U}_{BF}[\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{X})]=\Big[\int_{{\mathcal{Q}}_{*}}^{\oplus}\hskip-2.0ptd\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}\,\big[\mathscr{F}_{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}}\bigcap{BC}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})\big]\Big]\,\bigcap\,C^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q}_{*})
.

Proof.

For
\varphi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{X})
, Definition (
2.14
) implies:

\big(\mathcal{U}_{BF}\varphi\big)(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*},x)=\underset{\gamma\in\Gamma}{\sum}\,e^{-i<\mathfrak{s}_{*}(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}),\gamma>}\,\varphi(x+\gamma)

(2.16)

and one verifies immediately that
\mathcal{U}_{BF}\varphi\in\Big[\int_{{\mathcal{Q}}_{*}}^{\oplus}\hskip-2.0pt\big[\mathscr{F}_{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}}\bigcap{BC}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})\big]\Big]\,\bigcap\,C^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q}_{*})
.

Reciprocally, let us take some
\hat{\varphi}\in\Big[\int_{{\mathcal{Q}}_{*}}^{\oplus}\hskip-2.0ptd\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}\,\big[\mathscr{F}_{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}}\bigcap{BC}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})\big]\Big]\,\bigcap\,C^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q}_{*})
and use (
2.15
) to write:

\hat{\varphi}=\mathcal{U}_{BF}\varphi,\quad\varphi(\hat{x}+\alpha)=\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{*}}\hskip-4.0ptd\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}\,e^{i<\mathfrak{s}_{*}(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}),\alpha>}\,\hat{\varphi}(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*},\hat{x}).

(2.17)

∎

Notation 2.8
.

We shall use the notations:
\mathscr{F}_{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}}^{\infty}:=\mathscr{F}_{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}}\bigcap{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})
and
\mathscr{F}^{\infty}:=\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{*}}^{\oplus}d\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}\,\mathscr{F}^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}}
.

The Bloch-Floquet-Zak (BFZ) transformation.

We identify any
\Gamma
-periodic function with a function on the
d
-dimensional torus defined by composing this last one with the canonical projection on the quotient.
For any
\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}\in\mathcal{Q}_{*}
let us define the complex space:

\mathscr{G}\,:=\,\big\{\widetilde{f}\in{L}^{2}_{\text{\tt loc}}\big(\mathcal{X}^{*};L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})\big)),\ (\tau_{\gamma^{*}}\otimes{\rm{1}\hskip-3.0pt\mathbf{l}}_{\mathcal{Q}})\widetilde{f}=(1\otimes\hat{\chi}_{\gamma^{*}})\widetilde{f},\ \forall\gamma^{*}\in\Gamma\big\}

(2.18)

and endow it with the scalar product:

\big(\widetilde{f}\,,\,\widetilde{g}\big)_{\mathscr{G}}\,:=\,\int_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}\hskip-4.0ptd\hat{\xi}\,\int_{\mathcal{Q}}d\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}\,\overline{\widetilde{f}(\hat{\xi},\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z})}\,\widetilde{g}(\hat{\xi},\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z})

(2.19)

making it a Hilbert space.
Then one easily verifies that the following map defines a unitary operator
L^{2}(\mathcal{X})\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}\mathscr{G}
:

\big(\mathcal{U}_{BFZ}f\big)(\xi,\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z})\ :=\,\underset{\gamma\in\Gamma}{\sum}\,e^{-i<\xi,\mathfrak{s}(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z})+\gamma>}\,f(\mathfrak{s}(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z})+\gamma)

(2.20)

having the inverse:

\big(\mathcal{U}_{BFZ}^{-1}\hat{f}\big)(\hat{x}+\alpha)\,=\,\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{*}}\hskip-4.0ptd\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}\,e^{i<\mathfrak{s}_{*}(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}),\alpha+\hat{x}>}\,\hat{f}(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*},\hat{x}).

(2.21)

Remark 2.9
.

If we define the smooth function
\mathfrak{f}
by

\mathcal{X}^{*}\times\mathcal{X}\ni(\xi,x)\mapsto\mathfrak{f}(\xi,x):={e}^{-i<\xi,x>}\in\mathbb{S}\,,

(2.22)

we notice that
\mathfrak{f}(\xi,x)=\chi_{\xi}(x)
and:

\forall{f}\in{L}^{2}(\mathcal{X}):\qquad\big(\mathcal{U}_{BFZ}f\big)(\xi,\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z})\,=\,\mathfrak{f}\big(\xi,\mathfrak{s}(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z})\big)\,\big(\mathfrak{\mathcal{U}}_{BF}f\big)\big(\mathfrak{p}_{*}(\xi),\mathfrak{s}(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z})\big).

(2.23)

It follows that if an operator
T
has a decomposable image
\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{*}}^{\oplus}d\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}\,\hat{T}_{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}}
under the BF transformation with respect to the direct integral decomposition, then its image under the BFZ transformation is also decomposable as a family
\{\widetilde{T}_{\xi}\}_{\xi\in\mathcal{X}^{*}}
of operators acting in
L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})
with

\widetilde{T}_{\xi}=e^{-i<\xi,\cdot>}\hat{T}_{\mathfrak{p}_{*}(\xi)}e^{i<\xi,\cdot>}\,.

Remark 2.10
.

One easily notice that the BFZ-representation is the
\Gamma_{*}
-equivariant representation of
\mathcal{X}^{*}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d}
induced by the unitary representation:

\hat{U}^{\circ}:\Gamma_{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{U}\big(L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})\big),\quad\big(\hat{U}^{\circ}(\gamma^{*})f\big)(\omega):=e^{-i<\gamma^{*},\omega>}f(\omega),\,\forall\omega\in\mathcal{Q}.

(2.24)

From the general abstract theory one knows that this is unitarily equivalent to the representation on sections of the Euclidean vector bundle
\mathfrak{F}\rightY\hskip-4.0pt\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}_{*}
associated with the principal bundle
\mathcal{X}^{*}\rightY\hskip-4.0pt\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}_{*}
with fibre
\Gamma_{*}
and the representation (
2.24
) of
\Gamma_{*}
.

2.3
The Bloch-transformation of
\mathfrak{Op}(F)

2.3.1
The fibre operators

Let us compute the image of
\mathfrak{Op}(F)
through the BF transformation.
If
\hat{\varphi}\in\mathscr{F}^{\infty}\bigcap\,C^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q}_{*})
we obtain:

\displaystyle\big[\big(\mathcal{U}_{BF}\mathfrak{Op}(F)\mathcal{U}_{BF}^{-1}\big)\hat{\varphi}\big](\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*},x)=\underset{\gamma\in\Gamma}{\sum}e^{-i<\mathfrak{s}_{*}(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}),\gamma>}\big[\big(\mathfrak{Op}(F)\mathcal{U}_{BF}^{-1}\big)\hat{\varphi}\big](x+\gamma)

(2.25)

\displaystyle\quad=\underset{\gamma\in\Gamma}{\sum}e^{-i<\mathfrak{s}_{*}(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}),\gamma>}\big[U(\gamma)\big(\mathfrak{Op}(F)\mathcal{U}_{BF}^{-1}\big)\hat{\varphi}\big](x)=\underset{\gamma\in\Gamma}{\sum}e^{-i<\mathfrak{s}_{*}(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}),\gamma>}\big[\big(\mathfrak{Op}(F)U(\gamma)\mathcal{U}_{BF}^{-1}\big)\hat{\varphi}\big](x)

where the series in
\gamma\in\Gamma
is convergent due to the rapid decay of
\big(\mathfrak{Op}(F)\mathcal{U}_{BF}^{-1}\big)\hat{\varphi}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{X})
(as implied by Theorem A and Proposition
2.7
). The quasi-periodicity of the functions in
\mathscr{F}^{\infty}_{\tilde{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}
implies that
,e^{i<\mathfrak{s}_{*}(\tilde{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}}^{*}),\iota(y)>}\,\hat{\phi}(\tilde{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}}^{*},\hat{y})=\hat{\phi}(\tilde{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}}^{*},y)
and we obtain:

\displaystyle\big[\big(\mathfrak{Op}(F)U(\gamma)\mathcal{U}_{BF}^{-1}\big)\hat{\varphi}\big](x)=

(2.26)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\int_{\mathcal{X}}dy\int_{\mathcal{X}^{*}}d\eta\,e^{i<\eta,x-y>}\,F\big((x+y)/2,\eta\big)\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{*}}d\tilde{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}}^{*}\,e^{i<\mathfrak{s}_{*}(\tilde{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}}^{*}),\gamma>}\,\hat{\phi}(\tilde{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}}^{*},y).

We work with the extension of
\mathfrak{Op}(F)
to
BC^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})\subset\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathcal{X})
weakly in distribution sense and Theorem A implies that it preserves smoothness.

Proposition 2.11
.

Suppose
F\in{S}^{p}_{1}(\Xi)_{\Gamma}
. Then the canonical extension of
\mathfrak{Op}(F)
to
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathcal{X})
leaves invariant the subspaces
\mathscr{F}^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}}
for any
\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}\in\mathcal{Q}_{*}
.

Proof.

Suppose fixed some
\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}\in\mathcal{Q}_{*}
and some
\hat{\phi}\in\mathscr{F}_{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{*}}^{\infty}
and let us fix a
\Gamma
-partition of unity with cut-off function
\rho\in{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathcal{X})
, as in Definition
1.1
. Then let us translate it with any
\alpha\in\Gamma
and fix some test function

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}\times\widehat{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow\mathbb{U}\big(L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\big)\times\mathbb{U}\big(L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\big),

(3.7)

\displaystyle\big(U_{\mathcal{G}}(z)f\big)(x):=f(z\circ{x}),\ \big(V_{\mathcal{G}}(\zeta)f\big)(x):=\zeta(x)f(x),\ \forall f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{G},dx),

where each
\zeta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
is a smooth map
\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}
and we denote by
\zeta(x)
its value in
\mathbb{S}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
.

One easily verifies from (
3.7
) that:

\begin{split}\big(U_{\mathcal{G}}(z)V_{\mathcal{G}}(\zeta)U_{\mathcal{G}}(z^{-1})V_{\mathcal{G}}(\zeta^{-1})f\big)(x)&=\zeta(z\circ{x})\big(U_{\mathcal{G}}(z^{-1})V_{\mathcal{G}}(\zeta^{-1})f\big)(z\circ{x})\\
&=\zeta(z\circ{x})\zeta(x)^{-1}f(x)=\zeta(z)f(x)\,,\end{split}

(3.8)

and thus the above unitary operators satisfy the commutation relation:

U_{\mathcal{G}}(z)V_{\mathcal{G}}(\zeta)U_{\mathcal{G}}(z^{-1})V_{\mathcal{G}}(\zeta^{-1})=\zeta(z){\rm{1}\hskip-3.0pt\mathbf{l}}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G})}\,.

(3.9)

Definition 3.2
.

Given a Weyl system
(U_{\mathcal{G}},V_{\mathcal{G}}):\mathcal{G}\times\widehat{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow\mathbb{U}\big(L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\big)
we call
projective unitary Weyl representation
associated with it, a map
W_{\mathcal{G}}:\mathcal{G}\times\widehat{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow\mathbb{U}\big(L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\big)

verifying the identities:

\begin{split}&W_{\mathcal{G}}(x,1)=U_{\mathcal{G}}(x),\ W_{\mathcal{G}}(1,\xi)=V_{\mathcal{G}}(\xi),\\
&W_{\mathcal{G}}(x,\xi)W_{\mathcal{G}}(y,\eta)=\xi(y)^{-1}\,\eta(x)W_{\mathcal{G}}(y,\eta)W_{\mathcal{G}}(x,\xi)\,.\end{split}

(3.10)

We have two Weyl systems: the one associated with the dual pair
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the one associated with the dual pair
(\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{Z}^{d})
. The presence of these two Weyl systems in the study of the Bloch-Floquet theory has been observed in
[
7
]
and we intend to give a precise description of their relation in this context.

A key result in harmonic analysis defines for any pair of dual groups
\big(\mathcal{G},\widehat{\mathcal{G}}\big)
, an abstract Fourier-Pontryagin transformation (see Sections 4.1 and 4.2 in
[
10
]
):

\big(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{G}}F\big)(\eta):=\int_{\mathcal{G}}dx\,\eta(x)\,F(x),\quad\forall F\in L^{1}(\mathcal{G}),\ \forall\eta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}\,,

(3.11)

that extends to a unitary operator from
L^{2}(\mathcal{G})
onto
L^{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{G}})
. We denote its inverse by
\mathcal{F}_{\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
.

For each unitary Weyl representation of a Weyl pair
\big(\mathcal{G},\hat{\mathcal{G}}\big)
one can construct a functional calculus for any continuous, compactly supported function
\Phi\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G}\times\widehat{\mathcal{G}})
, defining:

\mathfrak{Op}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Phi):=\int_{\mathcal{G}\times\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}dx\,d\xi\,\big([\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\hat{\mathcal{G}}}]\Phi\big)(x,\xi)\,W_{\mathcal{G}}(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{B}\big(L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\big).

(3.12)

In defining a unitary Weyl representation for a Weyl system
\big(\mathcal{G},\widehat{\mathcal{G}}\big)
, an important aspect is to choose an order for the two unitaries
U_{\mathcal{G}}(z)
and
V_{\mathcal{G}}(\zeta)
, or equivalently a phase function in front of the product
U_{\mathcal{G}}(z)\,V_{\mathcal{G}}(\zeta)
. Having in mind the Weyl calculus on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that we discussed briefly in the Introduction (see (
1.1
) and (
1.2
)) we would like to have a kind of ”symmetric” unitary Weyl representation satisfying the properties:

\displaystyle W_{\mathcal{G}}(x,\xi)W_{\mathcal{G}}(y,\eta)=\sqrt{\xi(y)^{-1}\,\eta(x)}\,W_{\mathcal{G}}(x\circ{y},\xi\circ\eta)

(3.13)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Op}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Phi)^{*}=\mathfrak{Op}_{\mathcal{G}}(\overline{\Phi}).

(3.14)

For that we would like to define it as
"\sqrt{\xi(x^{-1})}U_{\mathcal{G}}(x)V_{\mathcal{G}}(\xi)"
,
but we have to make a smooth choice for the square root and we have to deal now with this question in the case when
(\mathcal{G},\widehat{\mathcal{G}})=(\mathbb{S}^{d},\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.

\vec{f}\in\mathcal{o}\big(\Gamma_{*};C^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q})\big)
and any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}

\mathcal{w}^{(p,\rho)}_{m,n}(\vec{f})\,:=\,\underset{\gamma\in\Gamma_{*}}{\sup}<\gamma>^{-p+m\rho}\,\underset{|\alpha|\leq{m}}{\max}\,\nu_{n}\big((\mathfrak{d}^{\alpha}\vec{f})_{\gamma}\big)\,<\,\infty,\quad\forall(m,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}.

(3.34)

The space
\mathbf{\mathcal{s}\big(\Gamma_{*};C^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q})\big)}

is the complex linear space of all sequences
\vec{f}:=(\vec{f}_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{*}}
of elements
\vec{f}_{\gamma}\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q})
, such that:

\forall(N,m,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{3}:\quad\mathcal{w}^{(-n)}_{m,n}(\vec{f})\,<\,\infty.

(3.35)

The last two families of spaces are clearly Fréchet spaces.

In view of the form of the symmetric Weyl representation of

given in Definition

\mathcal{s}\big((1/2)\Gamma_{*};C^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q})\big)
as family of ’regular’ symbols, analogue to

\Gamma_{*}
-indexed sequences of distributions on the

-dimensional torus.

The toroidal Fourier transform.

Given
\mathring{F}\in\mathcal{o}\big(\Gamma_{*};C^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q})\big)
, we define its ’toroidal Fourier transform’
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{Q}\times\Gamma_{*}}\,\mathring{F}
as being the following
\Gamma_{*}
-indexed, rapidly decaying sequence of distributions on the ’double’ torus
\mathcal{Q}_{2}
(with convergence in the weak distribution topology):

\begin{split}\big(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{Q}\times\Gamma_{*}}\,\mathring{F}\big)_{\alpha^{*}}(\tilde{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}}):&=2^{-d}\hskip-5.0pt\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\hskip-3.0ptd\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{\prime}\hat{\chi}_{1,-\alpha^{*}}(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{\prime})\hskip-4.0pt\underset{\gamma^{*}\in\Gamma_{*}}{\sum}\hat{\chi}_{2,\gamma^{*}}(\tilde{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}})\mathring{F}_{\gamma^{*}}(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{\prime})\\
&=2^{-d}\hskip-5.0pt\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\hskip-3.0ptd\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{\prime}e^{i<\alpha^{*},\mathfrak{s}(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{\prime})>}\hskip-6.0pt\underset{\gamma^{*}\in\Gamma_{*}}{\sum}\hskip-5.0pte^{-(i/2)<\gamma^{*},\mathfrak{s}_{2}(\tilde{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}})>}\,\mathring{F}_{\gamma^{*}}(\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}^{\prime}),\quad\forall\tilde{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}}\in\mathcal{Q}_{2}.\end{split}

(3.36)

Given
\mathring{F}\in\mathcal{s}\big(\Gamma^{*};C^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q})\big)
, we define its
toroidal symmetric quantization

\mathfrak{Op}_{\mathcal{Q}}(\mathring{F})
as the following bounded operator in
L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})

\begin{split}\mathfrak{Op}_{\mathcal{Q}}(\mathring{F}):&=2^{-d}\underset{\alpha^{*}\in\Gamma_{*}}{\sum}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}_{2}}d\tilde{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}}\,\Big(\big(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{Q}\times\Gamma_{*}}\mathring{F}\big)_{\alpha^{*}}(\tilde{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}})\Big)\,\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\mathcal{Q}}(\tilde{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}},\alpha^{*}).\end{split}

(3.37)

In this case
\big(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{Q}\times\Gamma_{*}}\mathring{F}\big)_{\alpha^{*}}(\tilde{\mathfrak{z}\hskip-4.0pt\mathfrak{z}})
belongs to
\mathcal{s}\big(\Gamma_{*};C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d}_{2})\big)
so that both, the series indexed by
\Gamma^{*}
and the integral over
\mathbb{S}^{d}_{2}
are absolutely convergent in the topology of
\mathbb{B}\big(L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})\big)
.

Chiral edge states on spheres for lattice domain wall fermions
Michael Clancy, David B. Kaplan

Abstract
Recently Weyl edge states on manifolds in dimension d + 1 d+1 with a connected d d -dimensional boundary were proposed as candidates for lattice regularization of chiral gauge theories, for even d d . The examples considered to date include solid cylinders in any odd dimension, and the 3-ball with boundary S 2 S^{2} . Here we consider the general case of a ( d + 1 ) (d+1) -dimensional ball for any even d d and show that the theory for the edge states on S d S^{d} describe a conventional Weyl fermion on a sphere with half-integer momenta. A possible advantage of such theories is that they can be discretized by a square lattice without breaking the underlying discrete hypercubic symmetry.

Domain wall on the cylinder

(1)

Here

In terms of these

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{d-1}x_{\parallel}\,rdr\,d\phi\,\overline{\psi}\left[\not{\partial}_{\parallel}+\gamma^{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{1}{r}\gamma^{\phi}\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\right]\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{d-1}x_{\parallel}\,rdr\,d\phi\,\overline{\psi}\left[\not{\partial}_{\parallel}+\gamma^{r}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{1}{2r}\right)+\frac{i}{r}\gamma^{\phi}J+m(r)\right]\psi\ ,

(6)

where

\displaystyle J\equiv\left(-i\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}+\frac{1}{2}\Sigma\right)\ ,\qquad\frac{1}{2}\Sigma=-\frac{i}{4}\left[\gamma^{x},\gamma^{y}\right]=-\frac{i}{4}\left[\gamma^{r},\gamma^{\phi}\right]\ .

(7)

As discussed in Ref.
[

{\cal D}\bar{\cal D}

{\cal D}

\displaystyle\psi_{\text{edge}}=f(r)\,\chi({\mathbf{x}}_{\parallel},\phi)\ ,\qquad\frac{1+\gamma^{r}}{2}\chi=0\ ,

\displaystyle S_{\text{edge}}=\int d^{d-1}x_{\parallel}\,d\phi\,\overline{\chi}\bar{P}_{-}\left(\not{\partial}_{\parallel}+\,\frac{i}{R}\gamma^{\phi}J\right)\bar{P}_{+}\chi.

(10)

where we have written the Weyl fermion as a Dirac fermion subject to the projection operators

\displaystyle\bar{P}_{\pm}=\frac{1\pm\gamma^{r}}{2}\ ,

(11)

This action does not naïvely look like the conventional action for a free Weyl fermion propagating on the flat

\displaystyle\chi=V\chi^{\prime}\ ,\qquad\bar{\chi}=\bar{\chi}^{\prime}V^{\dagger}\ ,\qquad V=e^{-i\phi\Sigma/2}\ ,

\displaystyle V^{\dagger}\gamma^{r}V=\gamma^{x}\ ,\qquad V^{\dagger}\gamma^{\phi}V=\gamma^{y}\ ,\qquad V^{\dagger}JV=V^{\dagger}\left(-i\partial_{\phi}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Sigma\right)V=-i\partial_{\phi}\ .

(13)

\displaystyle S_{\text{edge}}=\int d^{d}x\,\overline{\chi}P_{L}\not{\partial}\,P_{R}\chi.

d

\gamma

\displaystyle P_{L,R}=\frac{1\pm\Gamma_{5}}{2}\ ,\qquad\Gamma_{5}\equiv\gamma^{x}\ .

(15)

\phi

\chi({\mathbf{x}}_{\parallel},\phi)=-\chi({\mathbf{x}}_{\parallel},\phi+2\pi)

\chi

We will see that a similar analysis can be adopted for Weyl edge states on spherical boundaries as well, although it requires a bit more work to see this due to the nonzero curvature of the sphere.

III
Weyl edge states on spherical boundaries

III.1
Domain wall fermions with spherical symmetry

The Lagrange density for a
(d+1)
-dimensional theory of Dirac fermions with massless Weyl fermion edge states bound to a spherical
S^{d}
manifold may be written as

\displaystyle{\cal L}=\bar{\psi}{\cal D}\psi\ ,\qquad{\cal D}=\not{\partial}+m(r)\ ,

(16)

where we take
m(r)
to be the radially-dependent mass

\displaystyle m(r)=\begin{cases}m&r<R\\
-M&r>R\end{cases}\ ,

(17)

with
r=|{\mathbf{x}}|
.

We can rewrite
\not{\partial}
in terms of the rotation generators,

\displaystyle J_{ab}=L_{ab}-\frac{i}{4}\left[\gamma_{a},\gamma_{b}\right],\qquad L_{ab}=-i\left(x_{a}\partial_{b}-x_{b}\partial_{a}\right)\ ,

(18)

using the identity

\displaystyle\frac{i}{r}\gamma_{b}\hat{r}_{a}\left(\frac{i}{4}\left[\gamma_{a},\gamma_{b}\right]\right)=\frac{d}{2r}\gamma_{r}\ ,\qquad\hat{r}=\frac{{\mathbf{x}}}{|{\mathbf{x}}|}\ ,\qquad\gamma_{r}\equiv\hat{r}\cdot\vec{\gamma}\ ,

(19)

with the result

\displaystyle\not{\partial}=\gamma_{r}\left(\partial_{r}+\frac{d}{2r}\right)+\frac{i}{r}\gamma_{b}\hat{r}_{a}J_{ab}\ .

(20)

As before, the tilde on
\gamma_{r}
signifies a coordinate-dependent matrix, while the
\gamma_{a}
(without a tilde) are constant.

In analogy with the cylinder example, one can expand
\psi
and
\bar{\psi}
in eigenstates of
\bar{\cal D}{\cal D}
and
{\cal D}\bar{\cal D}
respectively, where
\bar{\cal D}
is the adjoint of
{\cal D}
with respect to the integration measure. Again the eigenfunctions are spinor harmonics which are single-valued on
R^{d+1}
. Of particular interest are the chiral edge states which form the only branch of solutions that is gapless in the
R\to\infty
limit.
In the limit of large
m
and
M
these edge state eigenfunctions take the form

\displaystyle\psi=f(r)\chi({\boldsymbol{\Omega}})\ ,\qquad\bar{P}_{+}\chi=0\ ,\qquad\bar{P}_{\pm}=\frac{1\pm\bar{\gamma}_{r}}{2}\ .

(21)

where
{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}
are the
d
angles parametrizing the spherical mass defect and
\chi
is chiral spinor which is single-valued in
{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}
. For large
M
and
m
the profile function
f(r)
satisfies

\displaystyle\left[-\left(\partial_{r}+\frac{d}{2r}\right)+m(r)\right]f(r)=0\ ,

(22)

becoming completely localized at the mass defect as
M
and
m
become infinite.
The effective action for the edge state can be written in this limit as
d
-dimensional action

\displaystyle S_{\text{edge}}=R^{d}\int d^{d}{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\ \overline{\chi}\bar{P}_{+}\left(\frac{i}{R}\gamma_{b}\hat{r}_{a}J_{ab}\right)\bar{P}_{-}\chi\ ,

(23)

the spherical analog of eq. (
10
).

III.2
Relating the domain wall action to the vielbein formalism

Although the above expression is rather simple, it does not immediately resemble the conventional action one would write for a free fermion on a sphere. Using the notation of Ref.
[
10
]
, a massless Weyl fermion on a curved manifold has the Lagrange density

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\text{Weyl}}=\overline{\chi}P_{+}\gamma^{i}E^{\alpha}_{\,\;i}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\Omega^{\alpha}}+\frac{i}{2}\omega^{jk}_{\ \ \alpha}\Sigma_{jk}\right)P_{-}\chi\ ,

(24)

where all indices run from
1,\ldots,d
, Greek indices referring to spacetime coordinates and Latin indices tangent space coordinates. The
\gamma^{i}
are coordinate independent Dirac matrices,
e^{i}_{\ \alpha}
is the vielbein,
E^{\alpha}_{\ i}
is its inverse, and
\omega^{jk}_{\ \ \alpha}
is the spin connection. The
P_{\pm}
matrices are the chiral projection operators
(1\pm\gamma_{5})/2
, assuming
d
to be even. We work in Euclidian spacetime, so the metric for the tangent space is
\eta_{ab}=\delta_{ab}
and there is no distinction between upper and lower tangent space indices.

To show the equivalence of the edge state actions in eq. (
24
) and eq. (
23
) it is most convenient to rederive the edge state action in a different basis for the fermions, starting again from the
(d+1)
-dimension Lagrangian in eq. (
16
) rewritten as

\displaystyle{\cal L}=\overline{\psi}\left[\gamma^{a}E^{\mu}_{\,\;a}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}+\frac{i}{2}\omega^{bc}_{\ \ \mu}\Sigma_{bc}\right)+m(r)\right]\psi\ ,

(25)

where
x^{\mu}
are Cartesian coordinates, and so we have

\displaystyle E^{\mu}_{\,\;a}=\delta^{\mu}_{\,\;a}\ ,\qquad\omega^{bc}_{\ \ \mu}=0\ ,\qquad a,\mu=0,\ldots,d\ .

(26)

If we now make a coordinate transformation to spherical coordinates,
x^{\mu}\to\bar{x}^{\mu}
, where
\bar{x}^{\mu}=\Omega^{\mu}
for
\mu=1,\ldots,d
, and
\bar{x}^{\mu}=r
for
\mu=d+1
, where
r
is the radial coordinate and
{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}
are the angles on
S^{d}
.
Under this coordinate transformation, the vielbein transforms to

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\ \mu}\to e^{a}_{\ \nu}\frac{\partial x^{\nu}}{\partial\bar{x}_{\mu}}\ .

(27)

Since
(\partial\bar{x}^{\mu}/\partial x_{\nu})
is the gradient of the coordinate
\bar{x}^{\mu}
, it is proportional to the unit vector pointing in the
\bar{x}^{\mu}
direction, e.g.
\hat{\Omega}^{\mu}
for
\mu=1,\ldots,d
and
\hat{r}
for
\mu=d+1
. That means
(\partial x^{\nu}/\partial\bar{x}_{\mu})
is a matrix
M^{\nu}_{\ \mu}
whose columns are proportional to
\hat{n}^{\mu}
and can be rendered diagonal if we simultaneously perform a gauge transformation in the tangent space by the orthogonal matrix
\left[O^{-1}\right]^{a}_{\ b}=(\hat{n}_{a})_{b}
(the
b^{th}
component of the unit vector pointing in the direction of the
a^{th}
spherical coordinate), then the new vielbein
{\bar{e}}^{a}_{\ \mu}
will be a diagonal matrix. So instead of simply performing the coordinate transformation of eq. (
27
), we include a gauge transformation and define

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\ \mu}\to{\bar{e}}^{a}_{\ \mu}=[O^{-1}]^{a}_{\ b}e^{b}_{\ \nu}\frac{\partial x^{\nu}}{\partial\bar{x}_{\mu}}=[O^{-1}]^{a}_{\ b}\delta^{b}_{\ \nu}\frac{\partial x^{\nu}}{\partial\bar{x}_{\mu}}\ ,\qquad[O^{-1}]^{a}_{\ b}=(\hat{n}_{a})_{b}\ ,

(28)

In order to effect this transformation we have to perform a gauge transform
V
on the fermions where

\displaystyle\psi=V\psi^{\prime}\ ,\qquad\bar{\psi}=\bar{\psi}^{\prime}V^{\dagger}\ ,\qquad V^{\dagger}\gamma^{a}V=O^{a}_{\ b}\gamma^{b}\ .

(29)

The
V
matrix represents the same gauge transformation as
O
, just expressed in the spinor representation instead of the vector representation. We explicitly construct
V
in appendix
A
but for now simply assume it exists. The new vielbein
\bar{e}
is the simplest one that one would consider writing down in spherical coordinates. For example, following this procedure for
d=2
and ordering the coordinates as
\{\bar{x}^{1},\bar{x}^{2},\bar{x}^{3}\}=\{\alpha,\theta,r\}
, where
\alpha=(\pi/2-\phi)
we obtain
{\bar{e}}^{1}=r\,{\rm d}\alpha
,
{\bar{e}}^{2}=r\,{\rm d}\theta
,
{\bar{e}}^{3}={\rm d}r
, while the new spin connection
\bar{\omega}^{bc}_{\ \ \mu}
is the usual one constructed from these vielbeins

2

As discussed in Appendix A, it is useful to replace the conventional azimuthal angle
\phi

\alpha=\pi/2-\phi

The Lagrangian is now given in terms of spherical coordinates as

\displaystyle{\cal L}=\overline{\psi^{\prime}}\left[\gamma^{a}\bar{E}^{\mu}_{\,\;a}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{x}^{\mu}}+\frac{i}{2}\bar{\omega}^{bc}_{\ \ \mu}\Sigma_{bc}\right)+m(r)\right]\psi^{\prime}\ ,

(30)

where the
\bar{E}^{\mu}_{\,\;a}
and
\bar{\omega}^{bc}_{\ \ \mu}
are the usual vielbein and spin connection one would choose for spherical coordinates on
R^{d}
, while the
\gamma

It is useful to rewrite this action as

\displaystyle{\cal L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\psi^{\prime}}\left[\gamma^{d+1}\bar{E}^{r}_{\,\;d+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+m(r)+i\gamma^{i}\bar{E}^{\beta}_{\,\;i}\omega^{j}_{\ \ \beta}\Sigma_{d+1,j}+\gamma^{i}\bar{E}^{\beta}_{\,\;i}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{x}^{\beta}}+\frac{i}{2}\bar{\omega}^{jk}_{\ \ \beta}\Sigma_{jk}\right)\right]\psi^{\prime}

(31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\psi^{\prime}}\left[\gamma^{d+1}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{d}{2r}\right)+m(r)+\gamma^{i}\bar{E}^{\beta}_{\,\;i}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{x}^{\beta}}+\frac{i}{2}\bar{\omega}^{jk}_{\ \ \beta}\Sigma_{jk}\right)\right]\psi^{\prime}\ ,

(33)

where
\beta,i,j,k
are summed over

, corresponding to the angular coordinates parametrizing
S^{d}
. To obtain the latter result we used the identities (justified in appendix
B

\displaystyle\bar{\omega}^{ab}_{\ \ d+1}=0\ ,\qquad i\gamma^{i}\bar{E}^{\beta}_{\,\;i}\omega^{d+1,j}_{\ \ \beta}\Sigma_{d+1,j}=\frac{d}{2r}\,\gamma^{d+1}\equiv\frac{d}{2r}\,\Gamma_{5}\ .

(34)

In order to use the familiar notation for chirality, we will equate
\gamma^{d+1}\equiv\Gamma_{5}
, which anticommutes with
\gamma^{i}
for

. In this basis we can once again focus on the edge state
\chi^{\prime}
, which satisfies

\displaystyle\left[-\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{d}{2r}\right)+m(r)\right]\chi^{\prime}=0\ ,\qquad P_{+}\chi^{\prime}=\frac{1+\Gamma_{5}}{2}\chi^{\prime}=0\ ,

(35)

becoming completely localized on

at

in the limit of mass
-M\to-\infty
for

and

. Substituting the edge state

for
\psi^{\prime}
in eq. (

, the prime reminding us that we have performed the gauge transformation

, it follows that they are equivalent up to the gauge transformation.

Just as in the case of cylindrical geometry described in the previous section, the gauge transformation matrix
V
is double valued on the sphere, and so
\chi^{\prime}
behaves like a particle quantized with anti-periodic boundary conditions in the azimuthal angle. This excludes a surface mode with zero momentum, consistent with the Dirac operator not having a zeromode on the sphere, see for example
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
.

In conclusion, we have shown that Weyl edge states appear naturally on
S^{d}
boundaries of
(d+1)
-dimensional balls, and that their properties can be described by conventional low energy actions in terms of single-valued fields for which the analogue of momentum operators on the sphere are proportional to the generators of
SO(d)
. Alternatively they can be described using a conventional vielbein and spin connection formalism, at the cost of having to use fields that are double-valued on the sphere – the two formulations being related by a double-valued gauge transformation in the tangent space. The calculations in this paper should be relevant for understanding the spectrum of a square lattice realization of the
S^{d}
domain wall manifold, where the full hypercubic symmetry is preserved by the sphere (unlike the case of cylindrical geometry). Other discretization approaches that have been explored for spherical manifolds might also be applied to this formulation, such as radial quantization (see Ref.
[
14
]
and references therein).

Appendix A
Construction of gauge transform
V

Here we give an explicit construction of the gauge transformation
V
that appears in eq. (
29
). We first define our spherical coordinate basis on

, with

as

\displaystyle\bar{x}^{\mu}=\{\alpha,\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{D-2},r\}\ ,\qquad\mu=1,\ldots,D=(d+1)\ ,\qquad\alpha\equiv\frac{\pi}{2}-\phi\ .

(36)

Here

is the radial coordinate, the
\theta_{i}
are polar coordinates with
\theta_{i}\in[0,\pi]
, and
\phi
is the conventional azimuthal angle taking values on
[0,2\pi)

, and for having a definition that is easily iterated from one value of

The corresponding unit vectors in the tangent space
\{\hat{\alpha},\hat{\theta}_{1},\ldots,\hat{\theta}_{D-2},\hat{r}\}
are denoted by

for
\mu=1,\ldots,D
. They can be simply constructed iteratively in the Cartesian basis
\{\hat{x}_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{D}\}
for increasing dimension starting with

in order to specify the dimension:

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=2:\qquad

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\{\cos\alpha,-\sin\alpha\}\ ,

(37)

\displaystyle\hat{r}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\{\sin\alpha,\cos\alpha\}

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=3:\qquad

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\{\hat{\alpha}^{(2)},0\}\ ,

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{1}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\{\cos\theta_{1}\,\hat{r}^{(2)},-\sin\theta_{1}\}\ ,

\displaystyle\hat{r}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\{\sin\theta_{1}\hat{r}^{(2)},\cos\theta_{1}\}\

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle>3:\qquad

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}^{(D)}

\displaystyle=\{\hat{\alpha}^{(D-1)},0\}\ ,

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{1}^{(D)}

\displaystyle=\{\hat{\theta}_{1}^{(D-1)},0\}\

\displaystyle\vdots\

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{D-3}^{(D)}

\displaystyle=\{\hat{\theta}_{D-3}^{(D-1)},0\}\

\displaystyle=\{\sin\theta_{D-2}\,\hat{r}^{(D-1)},\cos\theta_{D-2}\}\ .

\displaystyle O^{-1}=\left(\,\prod_{j=1}^{D-2}e^{-i\theta_{j}J_{j+1,j+2}}\right)\,e^{-i\alpha J_{12}}\,

\displaystyle[J_{ab}]_{cd}=-i\left(\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}-\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc}\right)\ .

Appendix B
Derivation of eq. (
34
)

The metric
\bar{g}
for
R^{D}
in spherical coordinates defined in eq. (
36
) with
D=d+1
may be written in terms of the metric
\hat{g}
on the sphere
S^{d}
as

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}r^{2}\hat{g}_{ij}&\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\ ,\qquad\mu,\nu=1,\ldots,D\ ,\qquad i,j=1,\ldots,d\ ,

(43)

so that the vielbein 1-forms are related as

\displaystyle\bar{e}^{i}=r\hat{e}^{\,i}\ ,\qquad\bar{e}^{D}={{\rm d}r}\ .

(44)

The defining equation for the spin connection 1-forms are given by

\displaystyle{{\rm d}}\bar{e}^{a}=-\bar{\omega}^{ab}\wedge\bar{e}^{b}\ ,\qquad{{\rm d}}\hat{e}^{\,a}=-\hat{\omega}^{ab}\wedge\hat{e}^{\,b}\ ,

(45)

which by means of eq. (
44
) immediately allows one to solve for
\bar{\omega}
in terms of
\hat{\omega}
as

\displaystyle\bar{\omega}^{iD}=\hat{e}^{\,i}\ ,\qquad\bar{\omega}^{ij}=\hat{\omega}^{ij}\ .

(46)

From this one deduces that for
\bar{\omega}^{ab}_{\mu}
one has

\bar{\omega}^{ab}_{D}=0
and

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta=1}^{d}i\gamma^{i}\bar{E}^{\beta}_{\ i}\bar{\omega}^{Dj}_{\beta}\Sigma_{Dj}=\sum_{\beta=1}^{d}i\gamma^{i}\left(\frac{1}{r}\hat{E}^{\beta}_{\ i}\right)\hat{e}^{j}_{\beta}\Sigma_{Dj}=\frac{i}{r}\gamma^{i}\Sigma_{Di}=\frac{d}{2r}\,\gamma^{D}=\frac{d}{2r}\,\Gamma_{5}\ ,

(47)

proving the assertion in eq. (
34
) with
D=d+1
.

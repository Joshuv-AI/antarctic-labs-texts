The minimum edge-pancyclic graph of a given order
Xiamiao Zhao, Yuxuan Yang

Abstract
A graph G G of order n n is called edge-pancyclic if, for every integer k k with 3 ≤ k ≤ n 3\leq k\leq n , every edge of G G lies in a cycle of length k k . Determining the minimum size f ⁡ ( n ) f(n) of a simple edge-pancyclic graph with n n vertices seems difficult. Recently, Li, Liu and Zhan [ 5 ] gave both a lower bound and an upper bound of f ⁡ ( n ) f(n) . In this paper, we improve their lower bound by considering a new class of graphs and improve the upper bound by constructing a family of edge-pancyclic graphs.



Keywords: Edge-pancyclic graph; minimum size; cycle; Hamilton cycle

Introduction

In this paper, all the graphs are simple. Let
C_{k}
denote the cycle with
k
vertices. Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, where
E(G)
is the edge set and
V(G)
is the vertex set.
We call a vertex with degree
d
as
d
-degree vertex. Let
e(G)=|E(G)|
and
v(G)=|V(G)|
. For every vertex
v\in V(G)
,
N(v)
is the neighborhood of
v
. Then the degree of
v
is
d(v)=|N(v)|
and the minimum degree of
G
is
\delta(G)=\min\{d(v):v\in V(G)\}
.

A graph
G
with
n
vertices is called
pancyclic
if, for every integer
k
with
3\leq k\leq n
,
G
contains a copy of
C_{k}
. There are two stronger conditions which derive two interesting families of graphs.

A graph
G
with
n
vertices is
edge-pancyclic
(resp. vertex-pancyclic) if, for every
3\leq k\leq n
, each edge (resp. vertex) of
G
lies in a cycle of length
k
.
Every nonempty edge-pancyclic graph is vertex-pancyclic. There is much work around these two classes of graphs
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
6
]
.

Let
g(n)
denote the minimum size of a vertex-pancyclic graph with
n
vertices. Broersma
[
1
]
proved that when
n\geq 7
,
3n/2<g(n)\leq 5n/3
. There is no widely accepted conjecture on the precise value of
g(n)
.

Recently, Li, Liu and Zhan
[
5
]
studied the minimum size of the edge-pancyclic graph with
n
vertices, denoted as
f(n)
and gave the following result.

Theorem 1.1
(
[
5
]
).

When
n\geq 4
,

\left\lceil\frac{3n}{2}\right\rceil\leq f(n)\leq 2n-2.

The lower bound can be directly obtained from the lower bound of
g(n)
, since every edge-pancyclic graph is vertex-pancyclic.
And if
G
is 3-connected, the exact size of the minimum edge-pancyclic graph was determined.

Theorem 1.2
(
[
5
]
).

Let
G
be a 3-connected edge-pancyclic graph with
n
vertices, then
e(G)\geq 2n-2
and equality holds if and only if
G=W_{n}
.

Here

is the wheel graph with
n
vertices, which is constructed by joining an extra vertex with every vertex in a copy of cycle

.

In this paper, we improve the lower bound of

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

The bound is tight because when

Theorem 1.4

When

The fan graph

Conjecture 1.6
.

Let

-edge-proper, then

The above conjecture implies the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.7
.

If

n

In

, Li, Liu and Zhan gave an upper bound of the minimum edge-pancyclic graph with

Theorem 1.8

Given any integer

The lower bound of the size of a
k
-edge-proper graph

Let

be a
k
-edge-proper graph of order

-edge-proper graph.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

and

be a
k
-edge-proper graph of order

. Then
\delta(G)\geq 3
. Moreover if
\delta(G)\geq 4
, then

.

Proof.

Since
G
contains a Hamilton cycle,
\delta(G)\geq 2
.
Suppose there exist
v\in V(G)
with
d(v)=2
. Let
N(v)=\{x,y\}
. Since
vx
lies in a triangle,
xy\in E(G)
. Then
\{x,y\}
is a vertex cut which implies
xy
does not lie in a Hamilton cycle, a contradiction.

In the following discussion, by Lemma
2.1
, we let
V_{3}=\{u\in V(G)|~d(u)=3\}
and
V_{4^{+}}=V(G)\setminus V_{3}
.
Now we start our proof with the easier case, Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.
Let
G
be a 3-edge-proper graph of order
n\geq 6
.
We will prove by induction on
n

By Lemma
2.1
, we can assume
\delta(G)=3
.
Let

and
N(v)=\{x,y,z\}
. Since
G
is 3-edge-proper, without loss of generality, we can assume that
xy,xz\in E(G)
.

Claim 1.

We may assume
x\in V_{4^{+}}
.

Proof of Claim 1.

Suppose
x\in V_{3}
. Then we have
yz\not\in E(G)
; otherwise
\{y,z\}
is a vertex cut which implies that
yz
does not lie in a Hamilton cycle. Let
H
be the graph obtained from
G
by contracting the subgraph
G[\{v,x,y,z\}]
. Then it is easy to check that every edge of
H
lies in a triangle. In
G
, every edge that is not in
\{vx,vy,vz,xy,xz\}
belongs to a Hamilton cycle that continuously passes through the edges
yx,xv,vz
or
yv,vx,xz
. In both cases, the Hamilton cycle of

will remain a Hamilton cycle of
H
after contracting

. Thus
H
is
3
-edge-proper. By induction hypothesis, we have
e(H)\geq\left\lceil 5(n-3)/3\right\rceil
. Then
e(G)\geq e(H)+5\geq\left\lceil 5n/3\right\rceil.

Claim 2.

We may assume
y,z\in V_{4^{+}}
.

Proof of Claim 2.

Assume without loss of generality that
y\in V_{3}
. Then we have
yz\not\in E(G)
; otherwise
\{x,z\}
is a vertex cut which implies that
xz
is not in a Hamilton cycle. Then there is a
y_{1}\in V(G)\setminus\{v,x,y,z\}
such that
yy_{1}\in E(G)
. Since
y_{1}y
lies in a triangle and

, we have
y_{1}x\in E(G)
. Let
H
be a graph obtained from
G
by contracting the edge
yv
as a vertex
v^{\prime}
. It is easy to check every edge of

lies in a triangle. Then we will prove that every edge of

lies in a Hamilton cycle.

Contract the edge
yv
as

.

Let

be a Hamilton cycle in

. If

, by replacing

with

, we have a Hamilton cycle in

. If
yv\notin E(C)
, then

passes

.
Then we replace the segment

in

and derive a Hamilton cycle in

.
Thus

is
3
-edge-proper. By induction hypothesis,
e(G)\geq e(H)+2\geq\left\lceil 5(n-1)/3\right\rceil+2\geq\left\lceil 5n/3\right\rceil.

By Claims 1 and 2, we may assume
V_{3}
is an independent set. Next, we will calculate the average degree of
G
. We define two functions
f_{0},f:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}
. For every

f_{0}(u)=d(u)
. For every vertex

Let every vertex

give out

24/7n\leq\sum_{w\in V(G)}f(w)\leq\sum_{w\in V(G)}f_{0}(w)=\sum_{w\in V(G)}d(w).

So we have

when

,
and we finished the proof.

\square

with
d(w)\geq 5
, let
w
give out
1/5
to all the vertices in
N(w)\cap A_{1}
,
8/23
to all the vertices in
N(w)\cap A_{2}
and
7/15
to all the vertices in
N(w)\cap A_{3}
.

The resulting value function is denoted as
f_{1}:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}
.

Claim 7.

For every
v\in V(G)
,
f_{1}(v)\geq 83/23
.

Proof of Claim 7.

If
v\in A_{1}
, then
f_{1}(v)\geq 3+3\cdot 1/5=18/5>82/23
.
If
v\in A_{2}
, then
f_{1}(v)\geq 3+2\cdot 3/23+8/23=83/23
.
If
v\in A_{3}
, then
f_{1}(v)=3+2/15+7/15=18/5>83/23
.

Let
v\in V_{4^{+}}
and
d(v)=4
. If
N(v)\cap A_{1}=\emptyset
, then
|N(v)\cap V_{3}|\leq 3
(see Figure 4). Since
2/15>3/23
, we have
f_{1}(v)\geq 4-3\cdot 2/15=18/5>83/23
. If
N(v)\cap A_{1}\neq\emptyset
, then
|N(v)\cap V_{4^{+}}|\geq 2
(see Figure 4). So
d(v)\geq 4-2\cdot 1/5=18/5>83/23
.

Let
v\in V_{4^{+}}
and
d(v)\geq 5
. If
N(v)\cap(A_{1}\cup A_{3})\neq\emptyset
, then
|N(v)\cap V_{4^{+}}|\geq 2
(see Figure 4). Thus
f_{1}(v)\geq d(v)-7/15\cdot(d(v)-2)\geq 18/5
. If
N(v)\cap(A_{1}\cup A_{3})=\emptyset
, then
|N(v)\cap V_{4^{+}}|\geq 1
(see Figure 4). So
f_{1}(v)\geq d(u)-8/23(d(u)-1)\geq 83/23.

From Claim 7, we have that

e(G)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in V(G)}d(v)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in V(G)}f_{0}(v)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in V(G)}f_{1}(v)\geq 83n/46.

So we have
e(G)\geq\left\lceil 83n/46\right\rceil\geq\left\lceil 7n/4\right\rceil
when
n\geq 7
.
Thus the end of the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

\square

3
A construction of an edge-pancyclic graph

We need three steps to construct an edge-pancyclic graph
G
. In this section, let
[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
and
[a,b]=\{a,\ldots,b\}
where
a,b\in[n]
and
a\leq b
.

Step 1.
Construct the graph
G_{1}
.

Let
s\geq 2
be an integer and set
\ell=\left\lfloor\frac{s}{\ln s}\right\rfloor
. Then
s^{\ell}\leq e^{s}<s^{\ell+1}
. Let
C=v_{1}v_{2}\ldots v_{s^{\ell}}v_{1}
be a cycle. The graph
G_{1}
is obtained from
C
by adding edges
v_{js}v_{js+s^{i}}
, where
j\in[s^{\ell-1}]
and
i\in[\ell-1]
. We make a deal that when
s^{\ell}<k\leq 2s^{\ell}
,
v_{k}=v_{k-s^{\ell}}
, and when
-s^{\ell}<k\leq 0
,
v_{k}=v_{k+s^{\ell}}
. Denote
E_{1}=E(C)
and
E_{2}=E(G_{1})\setminus E_{1}
. Then
G_{1}
has the following properties.

Lemma 3.1
.

For each edge
e\in E(G_{1})
and each integer
p\in[\ell-1]
, there exists a cycle contained
e
with length in
[s^{p}-s^{p-1},s^{p}+3]
. Furthermore, we can also require at most
3
edges on the cycle to be in
E_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
e\in E(G_{1})
and
p\in[\ell-1]
.
First, we consider the case
e\in E_{1}
. Notice that every edge in
E_{1}
lies in the cycle
C_{s^{p}+1}=v_{js}v_{js+1}\ldots v_{js+s^{p}}v_{js}
for some
j\in[s^{\ell-1}]
and each
p\in[\ell-1]
. Moreover,
|E(C_{s^{p}+1})\cap E_{2}|=1
. Thus the lemma holds for every
e\in E_{1}
.

Now we consider the case
e\in E_{2}
. By symmetry, it suffices to consider the edge
e=v_{js}v_{js+s^{q}}
, where
q\in[\ell-1]
. We will finish the proof by considering the relationship between
p
and
q
.

Case 1.

p=q
.
In this case,
e
is contained in a cycle
v_{js}v_{js+1}\ldots v_{js+s^{p}}v_{js}
of length
s^{p}+1
and one edge comes from
E_{2}
(see Figure 5).

Case 2.

p>q
. Then
v_{js+s^{p}}v_{js}v_{is+s^{q}}v_{js+s^{q}+1}v_{js+s^{q}+2}\ldots v_{js+s^{p}}
is a cycle contained
e
of length
s^{p}-s^{q}+2
with two edges in
E_{2}
(see Figure 5).

Case 3.

p<q
. Then
v_{js+s^{q}}v_{js}v_{js+1}v_{js+2}\ldots v_{js+s^{p}}v_{js+s^{p}+s^{q}}v_{js+s^{q}}
is a cycle contained
e
of length
s^{p}+3
with three edges in
E_{2}
(see Figure 5).

Replace every

with a graph.

Let
F_{a}
be the fan graph of order
a
, which is obtained from a path
P_{a-1}
by joining every vertex in

to a new vertex. Let
A
and

be two disjoint union of

with

and

where

and

are the center vertices of

and

Let

be the graph obtained from the union of

and

by adding edges

and

Then for every edge
v_{i}v_{i+1}\in E_{1}
, we replace it by a copy of
H(s)
with
v_{i}=w_{1}
and
v_{i+1}=u_{50s+1}
. The vertex

is renamed as
v_{i}^{j}

Step 3.
Replace every

with four edges.

For every
e=v_{i}v_{j}(=v_{i}^{1}v_{j}^{1})\in E_{2}

It’s easy to calculate that

Generalized Blaschke–Santaló-type inequalities, without symmetry restrictions
Thomas A. Courtade, Edric Wang

Abstract
Nakamura and Tsuji (2024) recently investigated a many-function generalization of the functional Blaschke–Santaló inequality, which they refer to as a generalized Legendre duality relation. They showed that, among the class of all even test functions, centered Gaussian functions saturate this general family of functional inequalities. Leveraging a certain entropic duality, we give a short alternate proof of Nakamura and Tsuji’s result, and, in the process, eliminate all symmetry assumptions. As an application, we establish a Talagrand-type inequality for the Wasserstein barycenter problem (without symmetry restrictions) originally conjectured by Kolesnikov and Werner ( Adv. Math. , 2022). An analogous geometric Blaschke–Santaló-type inequality is established for many convex bodies, again without symmetry assumptions.



1
Introduction and Main Results

Let
E_{i}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{d_{i}}
,
i=1,\dots,n
be Euclidean spaces, and put
E=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}
. Let
\pi_{i}:E\to E_{i}
denote the canonical coordinate projection of
E
onto
E_{i}
, and for
x\in E
, define
x_{i}:=\pi_{i}(x)
. Let
Q
be a fixed symmetric matrix on
E
, and also fix positive reals
\mathbf{c}=(c_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
. Let
\mathcal{P}(E_{i})
denote the set of Borel probability measures on
E_{i}
, absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure, and having finite second moments. If
\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(E_{i})
is a probability measure on
E_{i}
with density proportional to
e^{-f_{i}}\in L^{1}(E_{i})
, then its Shannon entropy is given by

h(\mu_{i}):=\log\left(\int_{E_{i}}e^{-f_{i}}dx_{i}\right)+\int_{E_{i}}f_{i}d\mu_{i}.

It is well-known that finiteness of second moments ensures the second integral exists in the Lebesgue sense; in particular,
h(\mu_{i})<\infty
.

With
(\mathbf{c},Q)
fixed, we shall primarily consider multimarginal entropy inequalities of the form

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(\mu_{i})\leq\sup_{\mu\in\pi(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})}\int_{E}\langle x,Qx\rangle d\mu+D,

(1)

and characterize the sharp constant
D
. Here, the supremum is over all couplings
\mu
of
(\mu_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
; that is,
\mu
is a probability measure on
E
with marginals
\pi_{i}\sharp\mu=\mu_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,n
. This motivates the following definitions.

Definition 1
.

Let
D(\mathbf{c},Q)
denote the smallest constant
D
such that (
1
) holds for all centered
\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(E_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,n
. Similarly, define
D_{g}(\mathbf{c},Q)
to be the smallest constant
D
such that (
1
) holds for all centered Gaussian measures
(\mu_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
.

We remark that the centering assumption built into the above definitions comes without any loss of generality, and can be eliminated by subtracting
\langle\tau,Q\tau\rangle
from the RHS of (
1
), where
\tau=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\in E
and
\tau_{i}
is the mean of
\mu_{i}
.

Our main result is the following Gaussian saturation principle.

Theorem 2
.

For any
(\mathbf{c},Q)
, it holds that
D(\mathbf{c},Q)=D_{g}(\mathbf{c},Q)
.

This result has a dual formulation as a family of functional inequalities, which we explain next. First, we say that a Borel function
f_{i}:E_{i}\to\mathbb{R}
, has well-defined barycenter

\mathsf{bar}(f_{i}):=\frac{1}{\int_{E_{i}}e^{-f_{i}}dx_{i}}\int_{E_{i}}x_{i}e^{-f_{i}}dx_{i},

if both integrals are finite. For
f_{i}:E_{i}\to\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, with well-defined barycenters, the concatenation
(\mathsf{bar}(f_{1}),\dots,\mathsf{bar}(f_{n}))
is a vector in
E
, and we may define the functional

\mathcal{Q}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n}):=\big\langle(\mathsf{bar}(f_{1}),\dots,\mathsf{bar}(f_{n})),Q\,(\mathsf{bar}(f_{1}),\dots,\mathsf{bar}(f_{n}))\big\rangle.

The following is our second main result.

Theorem 3
.

If Borel functions
f_{i}:E_{i}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
are such that
\mathsf{bar}(f_{i})
is well-defined and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}f_{i}(x_{i})\geq\langle x,Qx\rangle,~~~~\forall x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in E,

(2)

then

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{E_{i}}e^{-f_{i}}dx_{i}\right)^{c_{i}}\leq e^{D_{g}(\mathbf{c},Q)-\mathcal{Q}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})}.

(3)

This inequality is sharp.

Inequalities of the above form may be considered as generalized Blaschke–Santaló-type inequalities. In a recent paper, Nakamura and Tsuji
[
16
]
considered this class of functional inequalities for the special case of even functions, and established the same Gaussian saturation principle for (
3
) (in the case of even functions, the barycenter correction
\mathcal{Q}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
vanishes). Our contribution is three-fold. First, we establish Gaussian saturation for the above family of functional inequalities; this removes the restriction to even functions imposed in
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
. Second, we establish the formal duality between the above inequalities and the entropy inequalities (
1
), in the sense that both classes of inequalities share the same sharp constant and Gaussian saturation property. Finally, our proof of Gaussian saturation proceeds by analyzing the entropy inequalities (
1
). Not only does this extend Nakamura and Tsuji’s recent result, it gives a fundamentally different, and arguably simpler, proof.

Remark 4
.

The formal duality between Theorems
2
and
3
parallels what is known for Brascamp–Lieb inequalities
[
3
]
and their forward-reverse generalizations
[
5
,
14
]
.

2
Applications

Motivated by a problem initially proposed by Kolesnikov and Werner
[
12
]
, we give a few brief applications.

2.1
Application to Wasserstein barycenters

Let
\gamma
be the standard Gaussian measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
W_{2}:\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
denote the quadratic Wasserstein distance, and
D(\mu\|\gamma)
denote the relative entropy of
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with respect to
\gamma
. The first application of Theorem
2
is to obtain the following estimate, which extends Talagrand’s entropy-transport inequality
[
19
]
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be centered, and let
\lambda_{i}>0
satisfy
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1
. It holds that

\displaystyle\min_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}W_{2}(\mu_{i},\mu)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(1-\lambda_{i})D(\mu_{i}\|\gamma).

(4)

Equality holds if and only if: (i) for
n=2
,
\mu_{1}=N(0,C)
and
\mu_{2}=N(0,C^{-1})
for positive definite
C
; (ii) for
n\geq 3
,
\mu_{i}=N(0,\operatorname{id})
for each
i=1,\dots,n
.

The minimization problem on the left hand side of (
4
) is known as the Wasserstein barycenter problem with weights
(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{n}

[
1
]
. A unique minimizer always exists, and is called the
Wasserstein barycenter
of
(\mu_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
for weights
(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
. Subsequent to its introduction in
[
1
]
, the Wasserstein barycenter problem has generated significant theoretical and practical interest; see, e.g.,
[
17
, Ch. 9]
, and references therein. Motivated by barycenter problems and their connection to Blaschke–Santaló-type inequalities through duality, Kolesnikov and Werner conjectured and established certain Talagrand-type estimates for Wasserstein barycenters in
[
12
]
. In particular, Kolesnikov and Werner proved the special case of (
4
) under the strong symmetry assumptions that the
\mu_{i}
’s are unconditional (i.e., each
\mu_{i}
is symmetric in every coordinate), and the weights
(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
are equal
[
12
, Theorem 7.1]
. Very recently, Nakamura and Tsuji showed that Kolesnikov and Werner’s result holds under the weaker assumption that the
\mu_{i}
’s are symmetric
[
16
, Theorem 4.5]
(i.e.,
\mu_{i}(B)=\mu_{i}(-B)
for Borel
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
). Hence, our Theorem
5
generalizes Nakamura and Tsuji’s Talagrand-type Wasserstein barycenter inequalities, where the symmetry assumptions are weakened to simple centering, which comes without any essential loss of generality (see the remark following the statement of Theorem
4’
, below). In addition to the greater generality afforded, our proof is also simpler than those for the symmetric cases considered in
[
12
,
16
]
.

From Agueh and Carlier’s seminal work
[
1
]
, it is well-known that there is a close connection between the Wasserstein barycenter problem and a multimarginal optimal transport considered by Gangbo and A. Świȩch in
[
9
]
, in the sense that for
X_{i}\sim\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
i=1,\dots,n
,

\displaystyle\min_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}W_{2}(\mu_{i},\mu)^{2}=\min_{\pi(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}|X_{i}-X_{j}|^{2}\right],

(5)

where the minimum on the right side is over couplings of random vectors
X_{i}\sim\mu_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, on
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
. Moreover, if
\pi^{\star}
denotes the optimal coupling on the right hand side (which is uniquely attained), then the Wasserstein barycenter is equal to the pushforward
T{\sharp}\pi^{\star}
, with
T(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}
. These statements can be found in
[
1
, Theorem 4.1 and Proposition 4.2]
, with the first cited result being attributed to
[
9
]
. See also
[
12
, Theorem 2.4]
. In view of this, Theorem
5
has an equivalent formulation as follows, which we shall prefer to work with.

Theorem 4’
.

Let
X_{i}\sim\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
i=1,\dots,n
be centered, and let
\lambda_{i}>0
satisfy
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\inf_{\pi(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}|X_{i}-X_{j}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(1-\lambda_{i})D(\mu_{i}\|\gamma).

Equality holds if and only if: (i) for
n=2
,
\mu_{1}=N(0,C)
and
\mu_{2}=N(0,C^{-1})
for positive definite
C
; (ii) for
n\geq 3
,
\mu_{i}=N(0,\operatorname{id})
for each
i=1,\dots,n
.

To see how Theorem
4’
follows from Theorem
2
, note that we can rewrite the relative entropies in terms of Shannon entropies and second moments to reveal the equivalent inequality

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(1-\lambda_{i})h(\mu_{i})\leq\sup_{\pi(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\langle X_{i},X_{j}\rangle\right]+\frac{d\log(2\pi)}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(1-\lambda_{i}).

This is of the form (
1
), so the inequality of Theorem
4’
reduces to establishing the identity

D_{g}(\mathbf{c},Q)=\frac{d\log(2\pi)}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(1-\lambda_{i})

for the appropriate choice of
(\mathbf{c},Q)
. This computation can be done explicitly, however we shall instead give a short direct proof of Theorem
4’
in Section
5
which is of potential independent interest and has the benefit of characterizing the equality cases.

2.2
Application to geometry

With the dual equivalence between sharp constants in (
1
) and (
3
) in hand, we record here the fact that our Talagrand-type estimate for Wasserstein barycenters is formally equivalent to the following generalization of the functional Blaschke–Santaló inequality. This follows by expressing the relative entropies in Theorem
4’
in terms of Shannon entropies, and applying the general duality principle with
c_{i}=\lambda_{i}(1-\lambda_{i})
and
E_{i}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
for each
i=1,\dots,n
.

Corollary 6
.

Let
\lambda_{i}>0
satisfy
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1
, and let
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,\dots,n
be Borel functions with well-defined barycenters satisfying

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(1-\lambda_{i})f_{i}(x_{i})\geq\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle,~~~\forall x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(6)

Then

e^{\mathcal{Q}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int e^{-f_{i}(x)}dx\right)^{\lambda_{i}(1-\lambda_{i})}\leq(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(1-\lambda_{i})}.

We remark that the functional Blaschke–Santaló inequality corresponds to the case
n=2
; it is generally stated with the centering assumption
\int xe^{-f_{1}(x)}dx=0
, in which case the barycenter correction term
\mathcal{Q}(f_{1},f_{2})
vanishes. This was first proved in
[
6
]
, and earlier in
[
7
]
for the restricted setting of even functions. Nakamura and Tsuji’s main result in
[
16
]
implies the above, but only in the special case where each
f_{i}
is an even function, in which case the barycenter correction term
\mathcal{Q}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
vanishes; also, they only obtain the explicit sharp constant in the case of equal weights. Kolesnikov and Werner previously established the more restrictive case where each
f_{i}
is unconditional and weights are all equal
[
12
]
. Although both
[
16
]
and
[
12
]
explore certain dual forms of their results, neither contemplate the generic duality principle that relates entropy inequalities of the form (
1
) to functional inequalities of the form (
3
), which generalizes that previously known for the functional Blaschke–Santaló inequality (see, e.g.,
[
8
]
).

With the functional form of the barycenter problem now written as Corollary
6
, we conclude with an application to geometry following
[
12
, Section 5]
, in the same way that the functional Blaschke–Santaló inequality may be used to deduce the classical Blaschke–Santaló inequality for convex bodies. Indeed, recall that for a convex (or, more generally, star-shaped) body
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
containing the origin, the associated Minkowski functional is defined as

p_{C}(x):=\inf\{t>0:x\in tC\},~~~x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

By the layer cake representation, we have the classical identity

(2\pi)^{d/2}\frac{\operatorname{vol}_{d}(C)}{\operatorname{vol}_{d}(B_{2}^{d})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}p_{C}(x)^{2}}dx,

where
\operatorname{vol}_{d}
is
d
-dimensional volume, and
B_{2}^{d}
is the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Additionally, if
C
is centered in the sense that
\int_{C}xdx=0
, then another application of the layer cake representation reveals that
\mathsf{bar}(\frac{1}{2}p_{C}^{2})=0
. A direct application of Corollary
6
then gives the following, which removes the assumptions of equal weights and unconditional convex bodies made in
[
12
]
, and of equal weights and symmetric convex bodies made in
[
16
]
.

Corollary 7
.

Let
\lambda_{i}>0
satisfy
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1
. If
C_{1},\dots,C_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
are convex bodies, each containing the origin, satisfying

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(1-\lambda_{i})p_{C_{i}}(x_{i})^{2}\geq\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle,~~~\forall x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

and
C_{1},\dots,C_{n-1}
are centered, then

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{vol}_{d}(C_{i})^{q_{i}}\leq\operatorname{vol}_{d}(B_{2}^{d}),

where
q_{i}:=\frac{\lambda_{i}(1-\lambda_{i})}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}(1-\lambda_{j})}
.

Remark 8
.

The same holds if the bodies are star-shaped instead of convex. Up to translation, if
n>2
, then equality holds if only if
C_{i}=B_{2}^{d}
for all
i
. For
n=2
, equality holds precisely when
C_{1}
is a centered ellipsoid, and
C_{2}
is the corresponding polar body; this is the classical statement of the geometric Blaschke–Santaló inequality
[
18
]
.

3
Proof of Theorem
2

The proof of Theorem
2
proceeds in two steps. First, we show a reduction to symmetric measures. Then, we establish Gaussian saturation. In principle, the Gaussian saturation step could be accomplished by invoking Nakamura and Tsuji’s
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
for even functions and establishing the generic duality principle. However, as we shall see, Gaussian saturation follows in a fairly straightforward way after taking the steps needed to establish both the duality principle and the reduction to symmetric measures. So, we opt for this more self-contained approach.
To this end, we introduce the following formal definition.

Definition 9
.

Let
D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)
denote the smallest constant
D
such that (
1
) holds for all symmetric
\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(E_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,n
.

It is helpful to note that we always have the following ordering of
D,D_{s}
, and
D_{g}
:

D(\mathbf{c},Q)\geq D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)\geq D_{g}(\mathbf{c},Q)>-\infty.

3.1
The equivalence
D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)=D(\mathbf{c},Q)
.

This section contains the reduction to the symmetric measures. We begin with several simple technical lemmas.

Lemma 10
.

For
\delta>0
, it holds that

D(\mathbf{c},Q+\delta\operatorname{id})\leq D(\mathbf{c},Q)=\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}D(\mathbf{c},Q+\delta\operatorname{id}).

In particular,
\delta\mapsto D(\mathbf{c},Q+\delta\operatorname{id})
is non-increasing and right-continuous. The same holds when
D
is replaced by
D_{g}
or
D_{s}
.

Proof.

For any centered
(\mu_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
with finite second moments and
\delta>0

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(\mu_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\mu\in\pi(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})}\int_{E}\langle x,Qx\rangle d\mu(x)+D(\mathbf{c},Q)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\mu\in\pi(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})}\int_{E}\langle x,(Q+\delta\operatorname{id})x\rangle d\mu(x)+D(\mathbf{c},Q).

So, we conclude
D(\mathbf{c},Q+\delta\operatorname{id})\leq D(\mathbf{c},Q)
. Similarly, definitions give

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(\mu_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\mu\in\pi(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})}\int_{E}\langle x,(Q+\delta\operatorname{id})x\rangle d\mu(x)+D(\mathbf{c},Q+\delta\operatorname{id})

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mu\in\pi(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})}\int_{E}\langle x,Qx\rangle d\mu(x)+\delta\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int|x_{i}|^{2}d\mu_{i}+D(\mathbf{c},Q+\delta\operatorname{id}).

Taking
\delta\downarrow 0
, we conclude

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(\mu_{i})\leq\sup_{\mu\in\pi(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})}\int_{E}\langle x,Qx\rangle d\mu(x)+\liminf_{\delta\downarrow 0}D(\mathbf{c},Q+\delta\operatorname{id}).

It follows, therefore, by definition of
D(\mathbf{c},Q)
that
D(\mathbf{c},Q)\leq\liminf_{\delta\downarrow 0}D(\mathbf{c},Q+\delta\operatorname{id})
. Combination with the reverse inequality gives the claim. The same argument works for
D_{g}
and
D_{s}
by restricting attention to Gaussian and symmetric measures, respectively.
∎

Proposition 11
.

The following hold:

(i)

If
\pi_{i}Q\pi^{*}_{i}\not\geq 0
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, then
D_{g}(\mathbf{c},Q)=+\infty
.

(ii)

If
\pi_{i}Q\pi^{*}_{i}\geq 0
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, then
D(\mathbf{c},Q+\delta\operatorname{id}_{E})<\infty
for all
\delta>0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
v\in E_{i}
is an eigenvector of
\pi_{i}Q\pi_{i}^{*}
with negative eigenvalue
\lambda<0
. Fix a parameter
R>0
. Let
\mu_{i}
be Gaussian with variance
R
in the direction of
v
, and unit variance on the complementary subspace. Also, let
\mu_{j}=N(0,\operatorname{id}_{E_{j}})
for each
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i\}
.
In this way, and with the help of Cauchy–Schwarz, (
1
) with sharp constant
D=D_{g}(\mathbf{c},Q)
reads as

\frac{c_{i}}{2}\log R\leq\lambda R+O(1+\sqrt{R})+D_{g}(\mathbf{c},Q).

Since
\lambda<0
, we take
R\to\infty
and find
D_{g}(\mathbf{c},Q)=+\infty
.

Now, for the second claim, let centered
\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(E_{i})
have covariance
K_{i}
. Using the fact that Gaussians maximize entropy subject to covariance constraints and the assumption that
\pi_{i}Q\pi^{*}_{i}\geq 0
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(\mu_{i})-\sup_{\mu\in\pi(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})}\int_{E}\langle x,(Q+\delta\operatorname{id}_{E})x\rangle d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{c_{i}\dim(E_{i})}{2}\log\left(2\pi e\det(K_{i})^{1/\dim(E_{i})}\right)-\delta\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{Tr}(K_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{c_{i}\dim(E_{i})}{2}\log\left(\frac{2\pi e}{\dim(E_{i})}\operatorname{Tr}(K_{i})\right)-\delta\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{Tr}(K_{i}).

The final line is uniformly bounded from above for all
(K_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
, so the claim follows.

∎

The following proposition contains the core argument of this subsection. It roughly follows the same scheme as the proof of Gaussian saturation for forward-reverse Brascamp–Lieb inequalities in
[
14
]
.

Proposition 12
.

The following hold:

(i)

If
D(\mathbf{c},Q)<\infty
, then
D(\mathbf{c},Q)=D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)
.

(ii)

Moreover, if there are symmetric
\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(E_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,n
that achieve

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(\mu_{i})=\sup_{\mu\in\pi(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})}\int_{E}\langle x,Qx\rangle d\mu+D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q),

(7)

then
D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)=D_{g}(\mathbf{c},Q)
.

Proof.

Let
\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(E_{i})
be centered, and let
X_{i},X^{\prime}_{i}
be i.i.d. according to
\mu_{i}
.
Define

X^{+}_{i}=\frac{X_{i}+X^{\prime}_{i}}{\sqrt{2}};~~X^{-}_{i}=\frac{X_{i}-X^{\prime}_{i}}{\sqrt{2}}.

The simple but crucial ingredient is to observe that, for each
i=1,\dots,n
,

(X_{i}^{+},X_{i}^{-})=\left(\frac{X_{i}+X^{\prime}_{i}}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{X_{i}-X^{\prime}_{i}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\overset{law}{=}\left(\frac{X_{i}+X^{\prime}_{i}}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{X^{\prime}_{i}-X_{i}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)=(X_{i}^{+},-X_{i}^{-}).

In particular, the conditional law of
X_{i}^{-}
given
\{X^{+}_{i}=x^{+}_{i}\}
is symmetric for each
x_{i}^{+}
(
\operatorname{law}(X_{i}^{+})
-a.s.). Also, the law of
X^{+}_{i}
is trivially centered.

We’ll indulge in the usual abuse of notation, and denote random vectors and their distributions interchangeably (e.g.,
h(X_{i})=h(\mu_{i})
). Following our previous practice, we write
X\in\pi(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
to denote that
X
is an
E
-valued random vector whose law is a coupling of
(\mu_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
; i.e,.
\pi_{i}(X)\overset{law}{=}X_{i}
. Extending this, we write
(X,X^{\prime})\in\pi((X_{1},X^{\prime}_{1}),\dots,(X_{n},X_{n}^{\prime}))
to denote that
(X,X^{\prime})
is a pair of
E
-valued random vectors, with
(\pi_{i}(X),\pi_{i}(X^{\prime}))\overset{law}{=}(X_{i},X_{i}^{\prime})
. Similar notation will be used for
X^{+}
and
X^{-}
.

Starting with the chain rule for entropy, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(X_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(X^{+}_{i})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(X^{-}_{i}|X^{+}_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\max_{(X^{+},X^{-})\in\pi((X^{+}_{1},X^{-}_{1}),\dots,(X^{+}_{n},X^{-}_{n}))}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\langle X^{+},QX^{+}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle X^{-},QX^{-}\rangle\right]+\frac{D(\mathbf{c},Q)+D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)}{2}

(8)

\displaystyle=\max_{(X,X^{\prime})\in\pi((X_{1},X^{\prime}_{1}),\dots,(X_{n},X_{n}^{\prime}))}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\langle X,QX\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle X^{\prime},QX^{\prime}\rangle\right]+\frac{D(\mathbf{c},Q)+D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)}{2}

(9)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{X\in\pi(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})}\int_{E}\langle X,QX\rangle d\mu+\frac{D(\mathbf{c},Q)+D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)}{2}.

(10)

Inequality (
8
) follows, in part, due to the definition of
D(\mathbf{c},Q)
applied to the centered
(X_{i}^{+})_{i=1}^{n}
. The other part is explained as follows: the conditional entropy
h(X_{i}^{-}|X_{i}^{+})
is equal to the entropy of the conditional law of
X_{i}^{-}
given
\{X^{+}_{i}=x_{i}\}
, averaged with respect to the law of
X^{+}_{i}
. So, letting
X^{-}_{i}|x^{+}_{i}
denote
X^{-}_{i}
conditioned on
\{X^{+}_{i}=x^{+}_{i}\}
, we have for any coupling
X^{+}\in\pi(X^{+}_{1},\dots,X^{+}_{n})
that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(X^{-}_{i}|X^{+}_{i})\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{X^{-}\in\pi(X^{-}_{1}|\pi_{1}(X^{+}),\dots,X^{-}_{n}|\pi_{n}(X^{+}))}\mathbb{E}\left[\langle X^{-},QX^{-}\rangle|X^{+}\right]\right]+D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q),

where we used the definition of
D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)
, which is justified here since the conditional law of each
X_{i}^{-}
given
\{X^{+}_{i}=x^{+}_{i}\}
is symmetric,
\operatorname{law}(X_{i}^{+})
-a.s. Invoking measurable selection theorems, as in
[
20
, Cor. 5.22]
, the optimal (conditional) coupling above can be taken to be a regular conditional probability (see
[
15
, p. 42]
). When this conditional coupling is hooked up with the coupling
X^{+}
, the resulting pair
(X^{+},X^{-})
is a coupling in
\pi((X^{+}_{1},X^{-}_{1}),\dots,(X^{+}_{n},X^{-}_{n}))
. The subsequent identity (
9
) follows by defining

X=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(X^{+}+X^{-}),~~X^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(X^{+}-X^{-}),

for which
(X,X^{\prime})\in\pi((X_{1},X^{\prime}_{1}),\dots,(X_{n},X_{n}^{\prime}))\Leftrightarrow(X^{+},X^{-})\in\pi((X^{+}_{1},X^{-}_{1}),\dots,(X^{+}_{n},X^{-}_{n}))
, and

\langle X^{+},QX^{+}\rangle+\langle X^{-},QX^{-}\rangle=\langle X,QX\rangle+\langle X^{\prime},QX^{\prime}\rangle

by rotation invariance of the quadratic form. The final step (
10
) is just linearity of expectation and the fact that each
X
and
X^{\prime}
are couplings in
\pi(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
.

By definition of
D(\mathbf{c},Q)
, it follows that

D(\mathbf{c},Q)\leq\frac{D(\mathbf{c},Q)+D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)}{2},

and we obtain the first assertion.

To prove the second part, take
\mu_{i}
to be symmetric. Then,
X_{i}^{+}
is symmetric also, so the display containing (
8
)-(
9
) may be modified as

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(X_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(X^{+}_{i})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(X^{-}_{i}|X^{+}_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\max_{(X^{+},X^{-})\in\pi((X^{+}_{1},X^{-}_{1}),\dots,(X^{+}_{n},X^{-}_{n}))}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\langle X^{+},QX^{+}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle X^{-},QX^{-}\rangle\right]+D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)

(11)

\displaystyle=\max_{(X,X^{\prime})\in\pi((X_{1},X^{\prime}_{1}),\dots,(X_{n},X_{n}^{\prime}))}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\langle X,QX\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle X^{\prime},QX^{\prime}\rangle\right]+D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{X\in\pi(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})}\int_{E}\langle X,QX\rangle d\mu+D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q).

We conclude that if symmetric
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(X_{i})

\displaystyle=\max_{X\in\pi(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})}\mathbb{E}\langle X,QX\rangle]+D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q),

(12)

then we have equality in (
11
), and (
12
) remains satisfied when
X_{i}
is replace by
X^{+}_{i}
for each
i
. Hence, symmetric extremizers in (
1
) for sharp constant
D=D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)
are closed under rescaled convolutions. Iterating this argument ad infinitum, the LHS of (
12
) tends to

\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(N(0,K_{i}))

by the entropic central limit theorem, where
K_{i}
denotes the covariance of
\mu_{i}
. Moreover, since the transformation
X_{i}\mapsto X^{+}_{i}
preserves covariance, the quadratic term in the RHS of (
12
) tends to

\max_{X\in\pi(N(0,K_{1}),\dots,N(0,K_{n}))}\mathbb{E}[\langle X,QX\rangle]

by an application of the central limit theorem for
W_{2}
. Hence, the Gaussian measures
\mu_{i}=N(0,K_{i})
also satisfy (
7
), showing
D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)\leq D_{g}(\mathbf{c},Q)
. This completes the proof.
∎

The main objective of this subsection now follows easily.

Corollary 13
.

We have
D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)=D(\mathbf{c},Q)
.

Proof.

If
\pi_{i}Q\pi_{i}\not\geq 0
for some
i
, then
D(\mathbf{c},Q)\geq D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)\geq D_{g}(\mathbf{c},Q)=+\infty
, where the last identity follows from Proposition
11
. So, assume
\pi_{i}Q\pi_{i}\geq 0
for each
i
. In this case, Proposition
11
ensures that
D(\mathbf{c},Q+\delta\operatorname{id}_{E})<\infty
for all
\delta>0
, so it follows from Lemma
10
and Proposition
12
(i) that

D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)=\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q+\delta\operatorname{id}_{E})=\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}D(\mathbf{c},Q+\delta\operatorname{id}_{E})=D(\mathbf{c},Q).

∎

3.2
The equivalence
D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)=D_{g}(\mathbf{c},Q)
.

The goal of this subsection is to prove the identity
D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)=D_{g}(\mathbf{c},Q)
. The proof rests primarily on the following duality principle, and several of the preparatory lemmas from the previous subsection.

Theorem 14
.

Let
D\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
. The following statements are equivalent.

(A)

If even Borel functions
f_{i}:E_{i}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
satisfy
e^{-f_{i}}\in L^{1}(E_{i})
and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}f_{i}(x_{i})\geq\langle x,Qx\rangle,~~~~\forall x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in E,

(13)

then

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{E_{i}}e^{-f_{i}}dx_{i}\right)^{c_{i}}\leq e^{D}.

(14)

(B)

If
\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(E_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,n
, are symmetric then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(\mu_{i})\leq\sup_{\mu\in\pi(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})}\int_{E}\langle x,Qx\rangle d\mu(x)+D.

(15)

Moreover, log-concave
(\mu_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
saturate (
15
) for the sharp constant
D=D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)
.

We’ll need a functional counterpart to Proposition
11
(i).

Lemma 15
.

If the sharp constant
D
such that (
14
) holds for all admissible functions satisfying (
13
) is finite, then
\pi_{i}Q\pi^{*}_{i}\geq 0
for each
i=1,\dots,n
.

Proof.

We’ll show that if
\pi_{i}Q\pi_{i}^{*}\not\geq 0
, then we must have
D=+\infty
in (
14
). Without loss of generality, we can consider
i=1
.
Suppose that
v\in E_{1}
an eigenvector of
\pi_{1}Q\pi_{1}^{*}
with negative eigenvalue
\lambda<0
and unit length
|v|=1
. Fix a parameter
R>0
. There is
\delta>0
sufficiently large (not depending on
R
) such that for

c_{1}f_{1}(x_{1}):=\begin{cases}\frac{\delta}{2}\langle x_{1},(\operatorname{id}_{E_{1}}-v\otimes v)x_{1}\rangle+\frac{\lambda}{2}|\langle v,x_{1}\rangle|^{2}&\mbox{if $|\langle v,x_{1}\rangle|\leq R$}\\
+\infty&\mbox{if $|\langle v,x_{1}\rangle|>R$}\end{cases},~~x_{1}\in E_{1}

and

c_{i}f_{i}(x_{i}):=\frac{\delta}{2}|x_{i}|^{2},~~x_{i}\in E_{i},~~i=2,\dots,n,

we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}f_{i}(x_{i})\geq\langle x,Qx\rangle,~~\forall x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in E.

Direct computation shows

\int_{E_{i}}e^{-f_{i}}dx_{i}=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{c_{i}2\pi}{\delta}\right)^{(\dim(E_{i})-1)/2}\int_{-R}^{R}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2c_{i}}t^{2}}dt&\mbox{if $i=1$}\\
\left(\frac{c_{i}2\pi}{\delta}\right)^{\dim(E_{i})/2}&\mbox{if $i=2,\dots,n$.}\end{cases}

Since
\lambda<0
and
\delta
does not depend on
R
, we find that we must have
D=+\infty
in (
14
) by letting
R\to\infty
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

(B)\Rightarrow(A)
. Let

\mu_{i}
to have density proportional to

By (

f_{i}
is bounded from below on its (compact) essential support. Since

Thus, using definitions and (B), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\left(\log\left(\int e^{-f_{i}}dx_{i}\right)+\int f_{i}d\mu_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(\mu_{i})

Cancelling the
\int f_{i}d\mu_{i}
terms on both sides, we conclude (A) holds. The assumption that
\mu_{i}
is compactly supported can be removed using monotone convergence.

We now show
(A)\Rightarrow(B)
. We can assume the sharp constant
D
in (

) is finite, else the claim is trivially true. Therefore, by Lemma
15
, we may assume that
\pi_{i}Q\pi_{i}^{*}\geq 0
for each
i=1,\dots,n
.

Fix symmetric measures
\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(E_{i})
, and assume without loss of generality that
h(\mu_{i})>-\infty
.
By the multimarginal Kantorovich duality, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist even
f_{i}\in L^{1}(\mu_{i})
satisfying (

) and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\int f_{i}(x_{i})d\mu_{i}<\max_{\mu\in\pi(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})}\int\langle x,Qx\rangle d\mu+\epsilon.

(16)

Note that (

) implies

c_{i}f_{i}(x_{i})\geq\sup_{x_{j}\in E_{j},j\neq i}\left\{\langle x,Qx\rangle-\sum_{j=1,i\neq j}^{n}c_{i}f_{i}(x_{i})\right\}.

Since

is uniformly convex. In particular, each

Now, starting with the variational representation for entropy and (A), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(\mu_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\left(\int f_{i}d\mu_{i}+\log\left(\int_{E_{i}}e^{-f_{i}}dx_{i}\right)\right)\leq\max_{\mu\in\pi(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})}\int\langle x,Qx\rangle d\mu+\epsilon+D.

Lemma 16
.

Let
(\nu_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of zero-mean log-concave probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with uniformly bounded second moments
\int|x|^{2}d\nu_{k}\leq M
for all
k\geq 1
. There exists a probability measure
\nu^{*}
such that
W_{2}(\nu_{k_{n}},\nu^{*})\to 0
for some suitable subsequence
(\nu_{k_{n}})_{n\geq 1}
.

Proof.

By the uniform bound on second moments, the collection
(\nu_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is tight, so by Prokhorov’s theorem there exists a probability measure
\nu^{*}
and a subsequence
(\nu_{k_{n}})_{n\geq 1}
such that
\nu_{k_{n}}\to\nu^{*}
weakly. To show convergence in
W_{2}
, it suffices to show that the second moments of
(\nu_{k_{n}})_{n\geq 1}
converge. For the latter, it suffices to establish uniform integrability. Toward this end, recall that higher-order central moments of log-concave measures are controlled by lower moments
[
2
]
. That is, there is a constant
C
(depending only on dimension) such that

\int|x|^{4}d\nu_{k_{n}}\leq C\left(\int|x|^{2}d\nu_{k_{n}}\right)^{2},~~n\geq 1.

Thus,

\int_{|x|\geq r}|x|^{2}d\nu_{k_{n}}\leq\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{|x|\geq r}|x|^{4}d\nu_{k_{n}}\leq\frac{C}{r^{2}}M^{2}~~~\Rightarrow~~~\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup_{n\geq 1}\int_{|x|\geq r}|x|^{2}d\nu_{k_{n}}=0.

This is the desired uniform integrability, and it follows that
W_{2}(\nu_{k_{n}},\nu^{*})\to 0
.
∎

We now turn our attention to the main objective of this subsection.

Corollary 17
.

D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q)=D_{g}(\mathbf{c},Q)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
11
(i), it suffices to assume
\pi_{i}Q\pi_{i}\geq 0
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. So, with this assumption made, fix
\delta>0
and let
\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(E_{i})
be symmetric with covariance
K_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
. Recall from the calculation in the proof of Proposition
11
that, with the assumption that
\pi_{i}Q\pi_{i}\geq 0
for every

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(\mu_{i})-\sup_{\mu\in\pi(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})}\int_{E}\langle x,(Q+\delta\operatorname{id}_{E})x\rangle d\mu

\mu_{i}
with
\operatorname{Tr}(K_{i})\leq C(\mathbf{c},\delta)

14

So, let

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(\mu^{(k)}_{i})-\sup_{\mu\in\pi(\mu^{(k)}_{1},\dots,\mu^{(k)}_{n})}\int_{E}\langle x,(Q+\delta\operatorname{id}_{E})x\rangle d\mu\right)=D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q+\delta\operatorname{id}_{E}).

By lower semicontinuity of relative entropy and convergence of second moments, we have the upper semicontinuity estimate for Shannon entropy

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(\mu^{(k)}_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(\mu^{*}_{i}).

Thus, we conclude

\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}h(\mu^{*}_{i})=\sup_{\mu\in\pi(\mu^{*}_{1},\dots,\mu^{*}_{n})}\int_{E}\langle x,(Q+\delta\operatorname{id}_{E})x\rangle d\mu+D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q+\delta\operatorname{id}_{E}).

The measures

D_{s}(\mathbf{c},Q+\delta\operatorname{id}_{E})=D_{g}(\mathbf{c},Q+\delta\operatorname{id}_{E})
. Using Lemma

, we may take

to conclude the proof.

Lemma 19

,
i=1,\dots,n
be centered, and let
\lambda_{i}>0
satisfy
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1
. If
\bar{\mu}
denotes the Wasserstein barycenter of
(\mu_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
for weights
(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}D(\mu_{i}\|\gamma)

\displaystyle\geq D(\bar{\mu}\|\gamma)+\frac{1}{2}\inf_{\pi(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}|X_{i}-X_{j}|^{2}\right].

(19)

We are now ready to prove Theorem
4’
.

Proof of Theorem
4’
.

The proof is by induction on
n
. The case
n=1
is degenerate, and the case
n=2
is precisely the symmetrized Talagrand inequality. So, assume the statement is true for some
n\geq 2
, and we’ll prove it for
n+1
. For convenience, define
\bar{p}:=1-p
for
p\in[0,1]
.

Put
\lambda^{\prime}_{i}=\lambda_{i}/\bar{\lambda}_{n+1}
for
i=1,\dots,n
, and let
\bar{\mu}
denote the Wasserstein barycenter of
(\mu_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
for weights
(\lambda^{\prime}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
. Observe that, for
X_{i}\sim\mu_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n+1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\inf_{\pi(X_{1},\dots,X_{n+1})}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}|X_{i}-X_{j}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\bar{\lambda}_{n+1}\lambda_{n+1}}{2}W_{2}(\mu_{n+1},\bar{\mu})^{2}+\frac{\bar{\lambda}_{n+1}}{2}\inf_{\pi(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\lambda^{\prime}_{i}\lambda^{\prime}_{j}|X_{i}-X_{j}|^{2}\right]

(20)

\displaystyle\leq\bar{\lambda}_{n+1}\lambda_{n+1}D(\mu_{n+1}\|\gamma)+\bar{\lambda}_{n+1}\lambda_{n+1}D(\bar{\mu}\|\gamma)+\frac{\bar{\lambda}_{n+1}}{2}\inf_{\pi(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\lambda^{\prime}_{i}\lambda_{j}^{\prime}|X_{i}-X_{j}|^{2}\right]

(21)

\displaystyle\leq\bar{\lambda}_{n+1}\lambda_{n+1}D(\mu_{n+1}\|\gamma)+\lambda_{n+1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}D(\mu_{i}\|\gamma)+\frac{\bar{\lambda}_{n+1}^{2}}{2}\inf_{\pi(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\lambda^{\prime}_{i}\lambda_{j}^{\prime}|X_{i}-X_{j}|^{2}\right]

(22)

\displaystyle\leq\bar{\lambda}_{n+1}\lambda_{n+1}D(\mu_{n+1}\|\gamma)+\lambda_{n+1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}D(\mu_{i}\|\gamma)+\bar{\lambda}_{n+1}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda^{\prime}_{i}\bar{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}D(\mu_{i}\|\gamma)

(23)

\displaystyle=\bar{\lambda}_{n+1}\lambda_{n+1}D(\mu_{n+1}\|\gamma)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{\lambda}_{i}\lambda_{i}D(\mu_{i}\|\gamma).

Inequality (
20
) is the elementary identity for quadratic forms

\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{2}=\bar{\lambda}_{n+1}\lambda_{n+1}|x_{n+1}-\bar{x}|^{2}+\bar{\lambda}_{n+1}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\lambda^{\prime}_{i}\lambda^{\prime}_{j}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{2},~~\mbox{where $\bar{x}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda^{\prime}_{i}x_{i}$},

combined with the fact that the RHS corresponds to restricting the set of couplings of
X_{1},\dots,X_{n+1}
on the LHS to those where
\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\lambda^{\prime}_{i}\lambda^{\prime}_{j}|X_{i}-X_{j}|^{2}\right]
is minimized (here, we recall the connection between the barycenter
\bar{\mu}
and the optimal coupling for this multimarginal transport problem). Inequality (
21
) is the symmetrized Talagrand inequality (
18
) applied to
W_{2}(\mu_{n+1},\bar{\mu})^{2}
. Inequality (
22
) is an application of (
19
) with weights
(\lambda_{i}^{\prime})_{i=1}^{n}
, and (
23
) is the inductive hypothesis.

For
n=2
, the equality cases are those of the symmetrized Talagrand inequality, and are known. For
n=3
, the fourth inequality above is the symmetrized Talagrand inequality applied to measures
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
. So, for equality, we must have
\mu_{1}=N(0,C)
and
\mu_{2}=N(0,C^{-1})
by Lemma
18
. However, by symmetry of the argument, we must have the same inverse relationship between the covariances of any pair
\mu_{i},\mu_{j}
. Hence, the only possibility is that
C=\operatorname{id}
. For
n\geq 4
, we proceed by induction. Again, the fourth inequality in the above argument shows that in the case of
(n+1)
marginals, equality implies any (and therefore every) subset of
n
distinct marginals must be standard normal by the assumed equality case for
n
, so the claim follows for
n+1
.
∎

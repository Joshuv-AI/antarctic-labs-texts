Infinite linear patterns in sets of positive density
Felipe Hernández

Abstract
In this article we describe all possible infinite linear configurations that can be found in a shift of any set of positive upper Banach density. This simultaneously generalizes Szemerédi’s theorem on arithmetic progressions and the recent density finite sums theorem of Kra, Moreira, Richter, and Robertson.

Let

Theorem 1.2

For a set

Theorem 1.3

(Reformulation of

Let

1.

\psi_{1}(e_{1})=\cdots=\psi_{m}(e_{1})\in\mathbb{N}
, and

2.

and

Example 1.4
.

Let
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{Z}
be non-constant linear forms such that
\psi_{1}(e_{1})>\psi_{2}(e_{1})>0
. Let

and
c\in(1,q)
. Define
A=\mathbb{N}\cap\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}[q^{2n},cq^{2n})
. Then, no shift of the set
A
contains simultaneously the configurations
\psi_{1}(B^{\otimes d})
and
\psi_{2}(B^{\otimes d})
for an infinite set
B
. Indeed, assume by contradiction that

is such that
\psi_{1}(B^{\otimes d}),\psi_{2}(B^{\otimes d})\subseteq A-t
for an infinite set
B
. If

large enough such that for some

,
\psi_{2}(b,b_{2},\ldots,b_{d})+t
lies in

with
\psi_{1}(0,b_{2},\ldots,b_{d})+t,\psi_{2}(0,b_{2},\ldots,b_{d})+t\in[-(q-c)q^{2n-1}/2,(q-c)q^{2n-1}/2]

\psi_{1}(b,b_{2},\ldots,b_{d})+t\leq q\left(\psi_{2}(b,b_{2},\ldots,b_{d})+t\right)+(q-c)q^{2n-1}<q^{2(n+1)}

and

\psi_{1}(b,b_{2},\ldots,b_{d})+t\geq-(q-c)q^{2n}+q\left(\psi_{2}(b,b_{2},\ldots,b_{d})+t\right)\geq cq^{2n},

which is a contradiction.

Example 1.5
.

Let
\eta\in(0,1)
. Consider a sequence
(k_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of natural numbers such that
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}k_{n}^{-1}<\eta
, and define

is at least

. For the sake of contradiction, assume there are a non-constant linear form
\psi:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{Z}

\psi(e_{1}+\cdots+e_{j})=0

. Without loss of generality

, as we can replace

, yielding for

that
\psi^{\prime}(B^{\prime\otimes j})\subseteq A-t^{\prime},
where
\psi^{\prime}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j})=\psi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j},0,\ldots,0)
.
By pigeonhole principle, we know that there is an infinite subset

and

such that

for each

. Thus, for any
c_{1}>\cdots>c_{d}
in

,
\psi(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})+t\in k_{t}\mathbb{Z}+t.
Moreover, if we take

large enough, we can ensure
\psi(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})+t\in k_{t}\mathbb{N}+t
,
implying that
A
intersects
k_{t}\mathbb{N}+t
, which is a contradiction.

It is also worth mentioning that it is not possible to remove the restriction

2.  Preliminaries

2.1.  Notation

For
s\in\mathbb{N}
we denote
[s]:=\{1,\ldots,s\}
. For a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
we define its
uniform Cesàro limit
as

\mathop{\textup{{UC-}}\lim}_{n\to\infty}x_{n}:=\lim_{N-M\to\infty}\frac{1}{N-M}\sum_{n=M+1}^{N}x_{n}

whenever this limit exists. A Følner sequence
(\Phi_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{N}
is a sequence of subsets of
\mathbb{N}
such that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{|\Phi_{N}\Delta(\Phi_{N}+k)|}{|\Phi_{N}|}=0\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

We say that a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
has positive upper Banach density if there is a Følner sequence
(\Phi_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap\Phi_{N}|}{|\Phi_{N}|}>0.

For the rest of the article, we fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We will use the alphabet
\mathcal{A}=\{-r,\ldots,-1,1\ldots,r\}
and we will denote all the words of length
d
or less
1
1

1

The set
\mathcal{A}^{0}
will denote the singleton that contains the empty word
\varepsilon
.
in this alphabet as
\mathcal{A}^{\leq d}=\mathcal{A}^{0}\cup\mathcal{A}^{1}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{A}^{d}
. We define the set of forbidden words
\mathcal{F}
in
\mathcal{A}^{\leq d}
as the set of

such that for some
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
,
k+\sum_{i=1}^{j}w_{i}=0
. These words represent the forbidden configurations associated to
Example

1.5
. We also define our set of admissible words

as
\mathcal{A}^{\leq d}\setminus\mathcal{F}
. In addition, we denote the set of nonempty words in

as
\mathcal{W}_{d}^{+}=\mathcal{W}_{d}\setminus\{\varepsilon\}
. For each

we denote by

We define the order

2.2.  Basic notions in Ergodic Theory

Let
(X,T)
be a
topological dynamical system
, this is, a compact metric space
X
with a homeomorphism
T:X\to X
thereof. If, in addition, we have a
T
-invariant Borel probability measure
\mu
on
X
, then—following the notation from
[
KMRR24a
]
—we abuse language and refer to the triple
(X,\mu,T)
as a
system
. We say that
(X,\mu,T)
is
ergodic
if any
T
-invariant Borel set has measure either
0
or
1
. A point
y\in X
is called
generic for
\mu
along a Følner sequence
\Phi
, which we denote by
y\in\mathop{\textup{{gen}}}_{T}(\mu,\Phi)
, if for each continuous function
f\in C(X)

\int fd\mu=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{n\in\Phi_{N}}f(T^{n}y).

A system
(Y,\nu,S)
is a measurable factor of a system
(X,\mu,T)
if there is a measurable map
\pi:X\to Y
such that
\pi\mu=\nu
and
S\circ\pi(x)=\pi\circ T(x)
for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
.
For a factor map
\pi:(X,\mu,T)\to(Y,\nu,S)
there are measures
(\mu_{y})_{y\in Y}
defined
\nu
-a.e. on
X
called the
desintegration of
\mu
with respect
\pi
(see for example
[
EW11
, Theorem 5.14]
) such that for each bounded and measurable function
f:X\to\mathbb{C}
the map

y\in Y\mapsto\int fd\mu_{y}

is
\nu
-a.e. defined and Borel measurable, satisfying that all
V\in\mathcal{B}(Y)
,

\int_{\pi^{-1}(V)}fd\mu=\int_{V}\left(\int fd\mu_{y}\right)d\nu(y),

and that for
\nu
-a.e.
y\in Y
,
\mu_{y}(\pi^{-1}(\{y\}))=1
.

Let
\{(X_{i},\mu_{i},T_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of systems with continuous factor maps
\pi_{i}:X_{i}\to X_{i-1}
between them. The
inverse limit

(X,\mu,T)
of
\{(X_{i},\mu_{i},T_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is the unique system such that there exist continuous factor maps
\psi_{i}:X\to X_{i}
such that
\pi_{i}\circ\psi_{i}=\psi_{i-1}
for each
i>1
, and such that

C(X)=\overline{\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}C(X_{i})\circ\psi_{i}}.

From this section onwards we will need the theory of pronilfactors. As we will only briefly recall such theory, we advise any interested reader to consult
[
HK18
]
for further details. An
s
-step nilmanifold

X=G/\Gamma
is a compact nilmanifold where

is an
s
-step nilpotent Lie group and
\Gamma
is a discrete cocompact subgroup of
G
. The group
G
acts on

by left translation and

\mu
denotes the
Haar measure
on
X

X
, which is invariant under this action. For a fixed element
\tau\in G
, we define the transformation

by left translation of

. Then, the triple
(X=G/\Gamma,\mu,T)
constitutes an

-step nilsystem
.
An inverse limit of s-step nilsystems is called an s-step pronilsystem.

For a system
(X,\mu,T)
, we define the uniformity seminorms introduced in the ergodic case in
[

]

[
CFH11
]
. Let

. For
s=0

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{U^{0}(X,T,\mu)}:=\int_{X}fd\mu.

For

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{U^{s+1}(X,\mu,T)}:=\lim_{H\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\left\lVert T^{h}f\cdot\overline{f}\right\rVert_{U^{s}(X,\mu,T)}^{2^{s}}\right)^{1/2^{s+1}}.

(2)

3.  The progressive measure

For a system
(X,\mu,T)
, we define the transformations

\tilde{T}:=T^{k}\times(T^{w_{|w|}})_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}^{+}},\text{ and }\vec{T}:=(T^{k+\sum_{i=1}^{|w|}w_{i}})_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}

on
X^{|\mathcal{W}_{d}|}
. These transformations are the analogous of the transformations
T\times T
and

presented in
[
KMRR24b
]
, and of the transformations

and

in
[
KMRR26
]
.

Now, we construct an analog to the progressive measure defined in
[
KMRR26
]
, adapted to our setting. From now on, we consider an ergodic system
(X,\mu,T)
with continuous factor map
\pi:X\to Z
to its
L:=|\mathcal{W}_{d}^{+}|
-step pronilfactor
(Z,m,T)
, and we fix a point
a\in X
such that
\mu(\overline{\{T^{n}a:n\in\mathbb{N}\}})=1
. Let
z\to\eta_{z}
be the desintegration of
\mu
with respect to
\pi
. By
[
HK18
, Theorem 11, Chapter 11]
the orbit closure of
\pi(a)^{\mathcal{W}_{d}}
under
\vec{T}
is a uniquely ergodic nilsystem. We define a measure
\tilde{\sigma}
on
Z^{|\mathcal{W}_{d}|}
and a corresponding lift
\sigma
to

as follows:

\tilde{\sigma}=\mathop{\textup{{UC-}}\lim}_{n\to\infty}\vec{T}^{n}\delta_{\pi(a)^{\mathcal{W}_{d}}}\text{ and }\sigma=\int_{Z^{|\mathcal{W}_{d}|}}\prod_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}\eta_{u_{w}}d\tilde{\sigma}(u).

By a similar argument as in
[
KMRR26
, Lemma 4.6]
, for each

, there is

such that
\sigma_{w}\leq c_{w}\mu
.

We also define on
Z
the measure

m_{k}=\mathop{\textup{{UC-}}\lim}_{n\to\infty}T^{kn}\delta_{\pi(a)},

which is a measure supported on
Z_{k}:=\overline{\mathcal{O}_{T^{k}}(\pi(a))}
, and we define its lift to
X

\mu_{k}=\int_{Z}\eta_{u}dm_{k}(u).

We now state some properties of these measures:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(X,\mu,T)
be an ergodic system, and let
(Z,m,T)
and
(Z_{k},m_{k},T^{k})
be defined as above. Then, the ergodic decomposition of
\mu
with respect
T^{k}
is given by
\mu=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}T^{j}\mu_{k}.
In addition, there exists
\ell|k
such that
\left\lVert\mathbb{E}(g|Z)\right\rVert_{L^{1}(m)}=\ell\left\lVert\mathbb{E}(g|Z_{k})\right\rVert_{L^{1}(m_{k})}.

Proof.

By unique ergodicity we have that

m=\mathop{\textup{{UC-}}\lim}_{n\to\infty}T^{n}\delta_{\pi(a)}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\mathop{\textup{{UC-}}\lim}_{n\to\infty}T^{kn+j}\delta_{\pi(a)}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}T^{j}\mathop{\textup{{UC-}}\lim}_{n\to\infty}T^{kn}\delta_{\pi(a)}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}T^{j}m_{k}.

Consequently

\mu=\int_{Z}\eta_{u}dm(u)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\int_{Z}\eta_{u}dT^{j}m_{k}(u)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}T^{j}\int_{Z}\eta_{u}dm_{k}(u)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}T^{j}\mu_{k}.

For the second part, notice that
\{T^{i}Z_{k}\}_{i=0}^{k}
are either disjoint or equal by distality of
(Z,m,T^{k})
. Defining
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
as the minimum natural number such that
T^{\ell}Z_{k}=Z_{k}
, we have that
\ell|k
given that
\ell
is minimum period and
k
is a period, and

T^{i\ell+j}Z_{k}=T^{j}Z_{k},\text{ and }Z=\bigsqcup_{j=0}^{\ell-1}T^{j}Z_{k}.

Finally, we notice that

\mathbb{E}(g|Z)(z)=\int_{Z}gd\eta_{z}=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\int_{T^{-j}Z_{k}}gd\eta_{z}=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\int_{Z_{k}}T^{j}gd\eta_{z}=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}(T^{j}g|Z_{k})(z).

In consequence,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbb{E}(g|Z)\right\rVert_{L^{1}(m)}

\displaystyle=\int_{Z}|\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}T^{j}\mathbb{E}(g|Z_{k})(z)|dm(z)

Proposition 5.2
.

1
, then

\int\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}d\tilde{\sigma}>\eta\Rightarrow\mathop{\textup{{UC-}}\lim}_{n}\int\Big(\tilde{T}^{n}\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}\Big)\left(\pi(a),(x_{w^{-}})_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}^{+}}\right)\cdot\Big(\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}\Big)(x)d\tilde{\sigma}(x)>\delta.

Proof.

Denote
\mathfrak{a}:=\pi(a)
. Let
(g_{w})_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}\subseteq C(Z)
be non-negative functions bounded by
1
such that

\mathop{\textup{{UC-}}\lim}_{n\to\infty}\left(\vec{T}^{n}\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}\right)(\mathfrak{a}^{\mathcal{W}_{d}})=\int\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}d\tilde{\sigma}>\eta.

(6)

Define

P=\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\prod_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}(T^{(k+\sum_{i=1}^{|w|}w_{i})n}\mathfrak{a})>\eta/2\right\}.

By
Eq.

6
we have that
P
has lower Banach density greater than
\eta/2
. Let
R=r\cdot LCM(\{|k+\sum_{i=1}^{|w|}w_{i}|\}_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}})
. By Szemerédi’s theorem (
Theorem

3.4
), we have that there is a constant
c>0
only depending on
\eta
such that

\overline{d}\Big(\left\{m\in\mathbb{N}\mid\overline{d}\Big((P+Rm)\cap\cdots(P+m)\cap P\cap(P-m)\cap\cdots\cap(P-Rm)\Big)>c\right\}\Big)>c.

(7)

Using the uniquely ergodicity of the orbit of
\mathfrak{a}^{\mathcal{W}_{d}}
through the transformations
\vec{T}
and
\tilde{T}
given by
[
HK18
, Theorem 17, Chapter 11]
we get

\displaystyle\mathop{\textup{{UC-}}\lim}_{m}\int\tilde{T}^{m}\Big(\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}\Big)(\mathfrak{a},(x_{w^{-}})_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}^{+}})\cdot\Big(\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}\Big)(x)d\tilde{\sigma}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\textup{{UC-}}\lim}_{n\to\infty}\mathop{\textup{{UC-}}\lim}_{m\to\infty}\Big(\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}\Big)\left(\tilde{T}^{m}\left(\mathfrak{a},(T^{n(k+\sum_{i=1}^{|w^{-}|}w_{i})}\mathfrak{a})_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}^{+}}\right)\right)\cdot\Big(\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}\Big)(\vec{T}^{n}\mathfrak{a}^{\mathcal{W}_{d}})

\displaystyle=\mathop{\textup{{UC-}}\lim}_{n\to\infty}\mathop{\textup{{UC-}}\lim}_{m\to\infty}\Big(\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}\Big)(\tilde{T}^{m}\vec{T}^{n}\mathfrak{a}^{\mathcal{W}_{d}})\cdot\Big(\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}\Big)(\vec{T}^{n}\mathfrak{a}^{\mathcal{W}_{d}}).

The last expression is bounded by below by

\frac{1}{R}\mathop{\textup{{UC-}}\lim}_{n\to\infty}\mathop{\textup{{UC-}}\lim}_{m\to\infty}\Big(\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}\Big)(\tilde{T}^{Rm}\vec{T}^{n}\mathfrak{a}^{\mathcal{W}_{d}})\cdot\Big(\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}\Big)(\vec{T}^{n}\mathfrak{a}^{\mathcal{W}_{d}}),

so it is enough to bound uniformly the expression

\frac{1}{R}\mathop{\textup{{UC-}}\lim}_{n\to\infty}\mathop{\textup{{UC-}}\lim}_{m\to\infty}g_{\varepsilon}(T^{kn+kRm}\mathfrak{a})\cdot\prod_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}^{+}}g_{w}(T^{(k+\sum_{i=1}^{|w|}w_{i})n+w_{|w|}Rm}\mathfrak{a})\cdot\prod_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}(T^{(k+\sum_{i=1}^{|w|}w_{i})n}\mathfrak{a}).

For this, notice that for
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\in(P+Rm)\cap\cdots(P+m)\cap P\cap(P-m)\cap\cdots\cap(P-Rm)
, we have that
n+im\in P
for each
i\in\{-R,...,R\}
. In particular, we have that
n+Rm\in P
which implies that
g_{\varepsilon}(T^{kn+kRm}\mathfrak{a})>\eta/2
. Similarly, for
w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}^{+}
, we have that
n+\frac{w_{|w|}R}{(k+\sum_{i=1}^{|w|}w_{i})}m\in P
, which implies that

g_{w}(T^{(k+\sum_{i=1}^{|w|}w_{i})(n+\frac{w_{|w|}R}{k+\sum_{i=1}^{|w|}w_{i}}m)}\mathfrak{a})=g_{wj}(T^{(k+\sum_{i=1}^{|w|}w_{i})n+w_{|w|}Rm}\mathfrak{a})>\eta/2.

In addition, given that
n\in P
, we have that
g_{j}(T^{(k+j)n}a)>\eta/2
for each
j\in\{-R,\ldots,R\}
. Consequently, we have that for such
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, the following holds

g_{\varepsilon}(T^{kn+kRm}\mathfrak{a})\cdot\prod_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}^{+}}g_{w}(T^{(k+\sum_{i=1}^{|w|}w_{i})n+w_{|w|}Rm}\mathfrak{a})\cdot\prod_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}(T^{(k+\sum_{i=1}^{|w|}w_{i})n}\mathfrak{a})>(\eta/2)^{2|\mathcal{W}_{d}|}.

(8)

Therefore, by
Eq.

7
and
Eq.

8
allow to conclude that

\mathop{\textup{{UC-}}\lim}_{m}\int\tilde{T}^{m}\Big(\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}\Big)(\mathfrak{a},(x_{w^{-}})_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}^{+}})\cdot\Big(\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}\Big)(x)d\tilde{\sigma}(x)>(\eta/2)^{2|\mathcal{W}_{d}|}c^{2}R^{-1},

concluding.
∎

Now we finally prove
Lemma

3.2
.

Proof of
Lemma

3.2
.

Let
(f_{w})_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}\subseteq C(X)
be non-negative continuous functions bounded by
1
without loss of generality, such that

\eta^{\prime}:=\int\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}f_{w}d\sigma>0.

Pick
\eta\in(0,\eta^{\prime}/2)
and notice that by definition of
\sigma

\int\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}(f_{w}|Z)d\tilde{\sigma}=\int\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}f_{w}d\sigma>2\eta.

Let
0<\delta<\eta
given by
Proposition

5.5
for
\eta
. We take
(g_{w})_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}\subseteq C(Z)
to be non-negative functions bounded by
1
such that
\left\lVert g_{w}-\mathbb{E}(f_{w}|Z)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(Z)}\leq\delta/(2\cdot|\mathcal{W}_{d}|\cdot k)
for each
w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}
and thus

\int\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}d\tilde{\sigma}>\eta.

Hence, by
Proposition

5.5
there is
\delta>0
such that

\mathop{\textup{{UC-}}\lim}_{n}\int\Big(\tilde{T}^{n}\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}\Big)(\pi(a),(x_{w^{-}})_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}^{+}})\cdot\Big(\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}\Big)(x)d\tilde{\sigma}(x)>\delta.

(9)

Using
Corollary

5.3
and dominated convergence theorem we have that

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in\Phi_{N}}T^{kn}f_{\varepsilon}(a)\int\Big((\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{d}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{A}\\
wj\notin\mathcal{F}\end{subarray}}T^{jn}f_{wj})\otimes 1^{\mathcal{A}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{F}}\Big)\cdot\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}f_{w}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in\Phi_{N}}T^{kn}f_{\varepsilon}(a)\int\Big((\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{d}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{A}\\
wj\notin\mathcal{F}\end{subarray}}T^{jn}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}(f_{wj}|Z))\otimes 1^{\mathcal{A}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{F}}\Big)\cdot\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}f_{w}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in\Phi_{N}}T^{kn}f_{\varepsilon}(a)\int\Big((\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{d}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{A}\\
wj\notin\mathcal{F}\end{subarray}}T^{jn}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}(f_{wj}|Z))\otimes 1^{\mathcal{A}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{F}}\Big)\cdot\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}(f_{w}|Z)d\tilde{\sigma}

\displaystyle\geq-\delta/2+\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in\Phi_{N}}T^{kn}f_{\varepsilon}(a)\int\Big((\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{d}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{A}\\
wj\notin\mathcal{F}\end{subarray}}T^{jn}g_{wj})\otimes 1^{\mathcal{A}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{F}}\Big)\cdot\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}d\tilde{\sigma}

where we used the definition of
\sigma
on the third equality and the definition of
(g_{w})_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}
on the last inequality.

On the other hand, by the fact that
a\in gen_{T^{k}}(\mu_{k},\Phi)
, we can use
Lemma

5.4
with
(X,\mu_{k},T^{k})
, Fatou’s lemma, and
Proposition

3.1
to get that

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Big|\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in\Phi_{N}}T^{kn}(f_{\varepsilon}(a)-g_{\varepsilon}(\pi(a)))\int\Big((\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{d}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{A}\\
wj\notin\mathcal{F}\end{subarray}}T^{jn}g_{wj})\otimes 1^{\mathcal{A}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{F}}\Big)\cdot\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}d\tilde{\sigma}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Big|\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in\Phi_{N}}T^{kn}(f_{\varepsilon}(a)-g_{\varepsilon}(\pi(a)))\Big((\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{d}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{A}\\
wj\notin\mathcal{F}\end{subarray}}T^{jn}g_{wj})\otimes 1^{\mathcal{A}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{F}}\Big)\Big|d\tilde{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\mathbb{E}(f_{\varepsilon}-g_{\varepsilon}\circ\pi|Z_{k})\right\rVert_{L^{1}(m_{k})}\leq\ell\left\lVert\mathbb{E}(f_{\varepsilon}|Z)-g_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(m)}\leq\ell\left\lVert\mathbb{E}(f_{\varepsilon}|Z)-g_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(m)}\leq\delta/2

in where in the second to last inequality we used that all the functions involved are
\mu
-a.e. bounded by
1
and the fact that
g_{\varepsilon}
is
Z
-measurable, and in the last inequality we used that
\ell\leq k
. By
Eq.

9
we deduce that

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in\Phi_{N}}\int\Big(\tilde{T}^{n}\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}f_{w}\Big)(a,(x_{w^{-}})_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}^{+}})\cdot\Big(\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}f_{w}\Big)(x)d\tilde{\sigma}(x)

\displaystyle\geq-\delta+\mathop{\textup{{UC-}}\lim}_{N\to\infty}\int\Big(\tilde{T}^{n}\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}\Big)(\pi(a),(x_{w^{-}})_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}^{+}})\cdot\Big(\bigotimes_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{d}}g_{w}\Big)(x)d\tilde{\sigma}(x)>0,

concluding the proof.

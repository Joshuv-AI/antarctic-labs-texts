Horizontally Periodic Generalized Surface Quasigeostrophic Patches and Layers
David M. Ambrose 1 , Fazel Hadadifard 2 , and James P. Kelliher 3

Abstract
. We study solutions to the α \alpha -SQG equations, which interpolate between the incompressible Euler and surface quasi-geostrophic equations. We extend prior results on existence of bounded patches, proving propagation of H k H^{k} -regularity of the patch boundary, k ⩾ 3 k\geqslant 3 , for finite time for patches that are periodic in one spatial dimension. Such periodic patches also encompass layers, or two-sided fronts. As the authors have treated the Euler case in prior work, we now primarily focus on the range of α \alpha for which α \alpha -SQG lies strictly between the Euler and SQG equations.

1.
Introduction

The generalized surface quasi-geostrophic
\alpha
-SQG equations in strong form can be written,

(1.1)

\displaystyle(\alpha\text{-}SQG)\qquad\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\theta+{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}\cdot\nabla\theta=0&\text{ in }[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\\
{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}=-\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\theta&\text{ in }[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\\
\theta(0)=\theta_{0}&\text{ in }{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.\end{cases}

Here,
{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}
is a velocity field by which the active scalar
\theta
is transported, with
{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}
recovered from the scalar via the constitutive law in
eq.

1.1
2
. Because of the presence of the operator
\nabla^{\perp}:=(-\partial_{2},\partial_{1})
, we have

\dv{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}=0
. A solution is to hold until some time
T>0
, which may be finite.

When used to model atmospheric turbulence for small Rossby and Ekman numbers and constant potential velocity,
\theta
is the temperature, which is advected by the divergence-free velocity field
{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}
. (See, for instance,
[
24
]
.)

We focus on values of
\alpha\in[0,1]
, the two cases of most widespread interest being
\alpha=0,1
, in which case
eq.

1.1
becomes the following:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha=0:&\text{Euler equations},\\
\alpha=1:&\text{SQG equations}.\end{array}

As in
[
14
]
, for
\alpha\in[0,2)
, we can write the constitutive law,
eq.

1.1
2
, in the form,

(1.2)

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}=\nabla^{\perp}G^{\alpha}*\theta,

where

(1.3)

\displaystyle G^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}):=\begin{cases}\dfrac{c_{\alpha}}{\lvert{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\rvert^{\alpha}}&\text{ if }0<\alpha<2,\\[12.0pt]
c_{0}\log\lvert{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\rvert&\text{ if }\alpha=0\end{cases}

is the Green’s function for
-(-\Delta)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}
with
c_{\alpha}=\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})/(\pi 2^{2-\alpha}\Gamma(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}))
for
0<\alpha<2
and
c_{0}=(2\pi)^{-1}
. Observe that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{\alpha\to 0^{+}}\alpha c_{\alpha}&=\lim_{\alpha\to 0^{+}}\frac{2\frac{\alpha}{2}\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})}{4\pi\Gamma(1)}=\frac{\lim\limits_{\beta\to 0}\beta\Gamma(\beta)}{2\pi}=\frac{\Res_{\Gamma}(0)}{2\pi}=\frac{1}{2\pi}=c_{0},\end{split}

since the
\Gamma
function has a simple pole of residue
1
at the origin. This means, in particular, that
\alpha c_{\alpha}
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
and bounded over
\alpha\in[0,1]
.

We are interested here in patch solutions to
\alpha
-SQG, a special type of weak solution in which
\theta(t)
is the characteristic function of a domain. Our special concern in this paper is a subclass of patch data for which
\theta(t)
is periodic in one direction. Periodic patches, as we develop them, encompass both periodic
layers
(two-sided
fronts
) and periodic copies of a single bounded domain. See the illustrations in
[
4
]
, for the Euler equations, the
\alpha=0
case.

Remark
.

For concreteness, we choose periodicity in the
x_{1}
-direction with a period of
1
.

More precisely, we make the following definitions:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\theta\in L^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and suppose that
{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}:=-\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\theta\in L^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. Then
\theta(t,{\bm{\mathrm{x}}})
is a weak solution of
\alpha
-SQG with initial scalar
\theta_{0}
if for any test function
\phi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\theta(t,{\bm{\mathrm{x}}})\left[\partial_{t}\phi+{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}(t,{\bm{\mathrm{x}}})\cdot\nabla\phi(t,{\bm{\mathrm{x}}})\right]\,d{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left[(\theta\phi)(t,{\bm{\mathrm{x}}})-\theta_{0}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})\phi(0,{\bm{\mathrm{x}}})\right]\,d{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}.

We call a weak solution periodic, or, more fully,
1
-periodic in
x_{1}
, if

\displaystyle\theta(t,{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}+(n,0))=\theta(t,{\bm{\mathrm{x}}})\text{ for all }(t,{\bm{\mathrm{x}}})\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

Definition 1.2
.

Fix
a_{1},a_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. We say that a weak solution to
\alpha
-SQG is an
\alpha
-SQG-patch solution or simply a
patch
on
[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
if at time
t\in[0,T]
it is of the form

(1.6)

\displaystyle\theta(t,{\bm{\mathrm{x}}})=\Patch(\Omega(t),a_{1},a_{2}):=\begin{cases}a_{1}&\text{if }{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\in\Omega(t),\\
a_{2}&\text{if }{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\in(\Omega(t))^{c},\end{cases}

for some domain
\Omega(t)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

Remark
.

Because

\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}c=(-\Delta)^{-(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\nabla^{\perp}c=0

for any constant
c
,

\displaystyle\nabla^{\perp}

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{-(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\Patch(\Omega(t),a_{1},a_{2})=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}[\Patch(\Omega(t),a_{1},a_{2})-a_{2}]

\displaystyle=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}[\Patch(\Omega(t),a_{1}-a_{2},0)]=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}(a_{1}-a_{2}){\mathbbm{1}}_{\Omega(t)}.

Also, the value of
a_{1}-a_{2}
plays little role in our analysis so, losing no generality, we assume that
a_{1}=1
,
a_{2}=0
, making our patches of the form,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\theta(t,{\bm{\mathrm{x}}})={\mathbbm{1}}_{\Omega(t)}.

We will formulate, for any
\alpha\in(0,1]
, a contour dynamics equations (CDE) for
1
-periodic in
x_{1}
patches that are compactly supported in the vertical direction. Our main result, stated precisely in
theorem

11.1
, is as follows:

Theorem

(Main result, roughly stated)
.

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, let
{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{0}
be the boundary of a periodic domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
bounded in the vertical direction, and having an
H^{m}
boundary,
m\geqslant 3
. There exists a time
T>0
for which a unique periodic solution
\gamma
to the CDE for
\alpha
-SQG exists in
C([0,T];H^{m})
with
\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\in C([0,T];L^{\infty})\cap L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{m-1})
and
{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(0)={\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{0}
.

The case
\alpha=1
, which is not covered in
theorem

11.1
, is the subject of a future work. In preparation for that work, we include
\alpha=1
in as many of our estimates as feasible.

Remark 1.3
.

We stress that our uniqueness results apply to solutions to the CDE,
but not to weak solutions to
\alpha
-SQG, for which uniqueness is not known. That is, we cannot rule out the possibility that a patch at time zero evolves over time to a weak solution that is not a patch; we can show only that exactly one such solution can remain a patch.
(More precisely, see
7.1
and
7.2
.)

1.1.
Euler equations:
\alpha=0

Much of our analysis for
\alpha
-SQG for
\alpha\in(0,1]
applies to the Euler equations,
\alpha=0
, as well, but in that case the stronger result in
[
4
]
can be obtained. In
[
4
]
, three distinct types of weak solution are defined, Type 1 being a solution to
\alpha
-SQG (for
\alpha=0
) on
[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with periodic initial data, Type 2 being a solution on a periodic strip (
\Pi
, defined below in
eq.

2.1
), and Type 3 again being a solution on
[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
for a single bounded domain, but with the constitutive law adapted to obtain a velocity field that corresponds to periodic copies. Much effort was spent in
[
4
]
showing the equivalence of these three types of weak solutions, taking great advantage of the techniques developed in
[
1
,
12
,
13
]
to work with Type 2 solutions.

In large part because the constitutive law,
eq.

1.1
2
, for
\alpha=0
gives
{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}
one full derivative of regularity over that of
\theta
, it is possible to obtain uniqueness of weak solutions in which one only has
\theta
and
{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}
in
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. One still has
\curl{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}=\theta
, but the constitutive law no longer holds, being replaced by the Serfati identity. This result is due to Serfati
[
28
]
(and see
[
5
]
for a fuller version of the proof). Combined with the results in
[
1
,
12
,
13
]
, this makes it possible to put the three types of weak solutions on equal footing without specializing to patch data.

Working with periodic patch data in
[
4
]
, a contour dynamics equation (CDE) is developed for each type of solution, and it is shown that any such CDE solution is a weak solution. Because uniqueness of weak solutions is known to hold, it immediately follows that the three types of CDE solution are equivalent. This simplifies the subsequent analysis of the boundary regularity of the patch, in which it is shown that
C^{1,\varepsilon}
regularity is propagated for all time, and allows the results to be stated in terms of weak solutions to the Euler equations. In contrast, see
remark

1.3
.

Moreover, for the 2D Euler equations, the velocity field is log-Lipschitz, which gives a unique flow map along which vorticity is transported. For any time, the flow map induces a diffeomorphism of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
that maps any point to its transported position at that time, which prevents self-intersection of a patch boundary. (This also uses that weak solutions are unique and that any CDE solution is a weak solution.) By contrast, for
\alpha\in(0,1]
, we need to incorporate a measure of non-self-intersection into the energy arguments, as in
section

9
.

1.2.
Prior work

Rodrigo in
[
26
,
27
]
considered the evolution of a simplified model for SQG with spatially periodic patches of halfspace-type; that is, with
\theta
taking one value above a horizontally periodic curve and another value below the curve. Such solutions are known as sharp fronts. Rodrigo’s model was formed by approximating the Green’s function rather than using the properly periodized Green’s function. Fefferman and Rodrigo then considered analytic sharp fronts for a related model problem in
[
10
]
(again not considering the exact periodic Green’s function).

Bounded patch solutions have been studied for the exact SQG evolution, beginning with Gancedo
[
14
]
. This work left open the question of uniqueness for
\alpha=1
, subsequently settled by Cordoba, Cordoba, and Gancedo
[
9
]
. Gancedo and Patel established a blowup criterion for SQG patches, among other results in
[
16
]
. In
[
15
]
, Gancedo, Nguyen, and Patel extended existence theory for patches to low regularity, allowing the initial bounded patch to have large curvature (the patch boundary is taken to lie
H^{2+s}
with
s<1/2
).

Front solutions have been considered in the series of papers
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
by Hunter, Shu, and Zhang. This work began with the development of approximate models for the evolution of sharp SQG fronts, and the development of existence theory for these approximate models
[
17
,
18
]
. Subsequently, for the full SQG equations of motion, the contour dynamics equation was developed for the motion of sharp fronts

[
19
,
20
]
. One difference between the contour dynamics equations developed in
[
19
,
20
]

and that of the present work is that they consider only curves which are a graph with respect to the horizontal, while we consider more general parameterized curves. Hunter, Shu, and Zhang proved the existence of global front solutions with small, smooth data in

[
21
]
,
[
22
]
.
Subsequently, Ai and Avadanei gave a refined
analysis of the front equation, still in the graph case, giving a local well-posedness
result for data of any size and a global well-posedness result for small data in a
low-regularity setting
[
2
]
.

In this paper, we adapt the approach of Gancedo in
[
14
]
to cover the case of periodic patches.

Our periodic patches can equivalently be treated as patches on a periodic strip: in

The key differences between the analysis of the single, bounded-domain patches of

In

2.
Periodizing and Lifting

2.1.
Periodizing functions and domains

To treat
1
-periodic in
x_{1}
patches, we define the periodic strip,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Pi:=\left[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}\right]\times{\mathbb{R}}\text{ with }\left\{-\tfrac{1}{2}\right\}\times{\mathbb{R}}\text{ identified with }\left\{\tfrac{1}{2}\right\}\times{\mathbb{R}}.

We also define the same subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
without identifying its sides, setting

\displaystyle\Pi_{p}:=\left[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}\right]\times{\mathbb{R}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

We define the one-dimensional lattice,

(2.2)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}:=\mathbb{Z}\times\left\{0\right\},\quad{\mathcal{L}}^{*}:={\mathcal{L}}\setminus(0,0),

and use the convenient abbreviation

(2.3)

\displaystyle{\bm{n}}_{j}:=(j,0)

for the points in
{\mathcal{L}}
.

In moving back and forth from functions or domains defined in
\Pi
and their periodic analogs in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, we view
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
as a covering space for
\Pi
, with the covering map,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\bm{p}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to\Pi,\quad\bm{p}(x_{1},x_{2})=(x_{1}-\lfloor x_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}\rfloor,x_{2}).

Definition 2.1
.

We will often make, usually without comment, the identification of
{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}=(x_{1},x_{2})
in
\Pi
with
{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}:={\bm{\mathrm{x}}}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Pi_{p}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Such identifications will be done in the context of periodic functions, so the choice of whether
(x_{1},\tfrac{1}{2})=-(x_{1},\tfrac{1}{2})
in
\Pi
corresponds to
\pm(x_{1},\tfrac{1}{2})
in
\Pi_{p}
will be irrelevant. Also, for two points
{\bm{\mathrm{x}}},{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}\in\Pi
, we define
{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}=\bm{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}{\bm{\mathrm{y}}})
.

For any function
f
on
\Pi
we define
\mathcal{R}ep(f)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}ep(f)({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}):=f\circ\bm{p}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})=f(x_{1}-\lfloor x_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}\rfloor,x_{2}).

This produces a
1
-periodic in
x_{1}
version of the function
f
on
\Pi
—a “replication” of
f
.

For any
\Omega_{\Pi}\subset\Pi
, we also define
\mathcal{R}ep(\Omega_{\Pi})\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}ep(\Omega_{\Pi}):=\bm{p}^{-1}(\Omega_{\Pi})=\left\{{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\colon(x_{1}-\lfloor x_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}\rfloor,x_{2})\in\Omega_{\Pi}\right\},

and we note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}ep({\mathbbm{1}}_{\Omega_{\Pi}})={\mathbbm{1}}_{\mathcal{R}ep(\Omega_{\Pi})}.

Finally, for measurable
f
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}ep_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(f)({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in{\mathcal{L}}}f({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-\bm{\ell}).

The sum may diverge, but if
f\in L_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
then
\mathcal{R}ep_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(f)({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})
will be finite for all
{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

Moreover, we see that if
\bm{p}(\Omega)=\Omega_{\Pi}
then

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}ep_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}({\mathbbm{1}}_{\Omega})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}ep({\mathbbm{1}}_{\Omega_{\Pi}}).

This also allows us to define

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}ep_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(\Omega):=\mathcal{R}ep(\Omega_{\Pi}),

whenever
\Omega
is a lift of
\Omega_{\Pi}
, noting, then, that
\mathcal{R}ep_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(\Omega)
does not depend upon the particular lift.

to

. Because each of the paths we utilize will be either a closed path or a path in

that projects via

A

to

. A

\bm{p}\circ{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}={\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}
.

]

.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}f({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(\eta))\,\lvert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}^{\prime}(\eta)\rvert\,d\eta

Definition 2.4

A

in the topological space

,

\displaystyle\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}f({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(\beta),{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(\eta))\,d\beta\,d\eta

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi,j}(\beta),{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi,j}(\eta))\,d\beta\,d\eta.

A chain

2.3.
Notation and function spaces

Remark 2.7

(On notation)
.

If
f(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
is a function of
n
variables, we will occasionally find it useful to write
\partial_{j}f
for the derivative with respect to the
j^{th}
variable. So, for instance,
\partial_{2}f(t,\eta)=\partial_{\eta}f(t,\eta)
, but
\partial_{2}f(t,\beta-\eta)=-\partial_{\eta}f(t,\beta-\eta)=\partial_{\beta}f(t,\beta-\eta)
.

For any
r\in(0,1]
, we define the space
C^{0,r}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})
to be the space of fractional Hölder continuous functions on
{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}
, the norm being

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{C^{0,r}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}

\displaystyle:=\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}+\lVert f\rVert_{\dot{C}^{0,r}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})},\quad\lVert f\rVert_{\dot{C}^{0,r}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}:=\sup_{\beta\neq\eta}\frac{\lvert f(\beta)-f(\eta)\rvert}{\lvert\beta-\eta\rvert^{r}}.

When
r\in(0,1)
we also write
C^{r}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})
for
C^{0,r}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})
. When
r=1
we get the space of Lipschitz continuous functions on
{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}
,
Lip({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})=C^{0,1}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})
,
\dot{Lip}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})=\dot{C}^{0,1}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})
.

3.
The constitutive law for a scalar
\theta

In this section, we present the explicit form of the constitutive law
eq.

1.2
in three contexts: first, for a single compactly supported scalar field
\theta
; second for a periodic scalar of the form
\mathcal{R}ep_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(\theta)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
; third, for a compactly supported
\theta
in the periodic strip
\Pi
. The function
\theta
need not be for patch data—in the process of deriving the CDEs in
section

5
, we will then specialize the constitutive law to the form it takes for patch data.

3.1.
Constitutive law for
\theta
compactly supported

Let
\theta\in L_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. In that case, we can define the stream function
\psi:=G^{\alpha}*\theta
for
{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}
, which solves

(3.1)

\displaystyle-(-\Delta)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\psi=\theta,

and then set
{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}=\nabla^{\perp}\psi
. Then

(3.2)

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}

\displaystyle=\nabla^{\perp}(G^{\alpha}*\theta)=(\nabla^{\perp}G^{\alpha})*\theta=K^{\alpha}*\theta,

where,

(3.3)

\displaystyle K^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})

\displaystyle:=\nabla^{\perp}G^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})=-\frac{\alpha c_{\alpha}{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}^{\perp}}{\lvert{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\rvert^{2+\alpha}}\text{ for }\alpha\in(0,1]

and
K^{0}(x)=(2\pi\lvert x\rvert)^{-2}x^{\perp}
. For
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
K^{\alpha}
is locally integrable and has sufficient decay at infinity so that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\lVert{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert{\mathbbm{1}}_{B_{1}(0)}K^{\alpha}\rVert_{L^{1}}\lVert\theta\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\lVert{\mathbbm{1}}_{B_{1}(0)^{C}}K^{\alpha}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lVert\theta\rVert_{L^{1}}\leqslant C\lVert\theta\rVert_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}},

so
{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}\in L^{\infty}
. However,
K^{1}
is not locally integrable, and the convolution must be treated as a singular integral operator. (The case
\alpha\in(1,2)
, treated in
[
8
]
, is even more singular.)

3.2.
Constitutive law for
\theta
periodic

Now, in place of
eq.

3.1
, we want to start with
\theta\in L_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and solve

(3.5)

\displaystyle-(-\Delta)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\psi=\mathcal{R}ep_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(\theta)

by finding a
1
-periodic in
x_{1}
Green’s function,
G^{\alpha}_{p}
, defined on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
so that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\psi=G^{\alpha}_{p}*\theta,\quad{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}=\nabla^{\perp}\psi=K^{\alpha}_{p}*\theta,

where

(3.7)

\displaystyle K^{\alpha}_{p}:=\nabla^{\perp}G^{\alpha}_{p}.

The straightforward way of obtaining
G^{\alpha}_{p}
and
K^{\alpha}_{p}
is to start with
G^{\alpha}_{p}
and then obtain
K^{\alpha}_{p}
as in
eq.

3.7
. Since
\psi
is supposed to solve
eq.

3.5
, we would expect of
G^{\alpha}_{p}
that, at least formally,

\displaystyle\psi({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})

\displaystyle=G^{\alpha}*\mathcal{R}ep_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(\theta)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}G^{\alpha}*\theta({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{n}}_{j})=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}G^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{y}}})\theta({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{n}}_{j}-{\bm{\mathrm{y}}})\,d{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}G^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{y}}}-{\bm{n}}_{j})\theta({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{\mathrm{y}}})\,d{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}G^{\alpha}(\cdot-{\bm{n}}_{j})*\theta({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})=G^{\alpha}_{p}*\theta,

where
{\bm{n}}_{j}
is defined in
eq.

2.3
and

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})&=G^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\left[G^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{n}}_{j})\right]=G^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\left[G^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{n}}_{j})-\frac{1}{\lvert{\bm{n}}_{j}\rvert^{\alpha}}\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{|{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}|^{\alpha}}+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\left[\frac{1}{|{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{n}}_{j}|^{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{|{\bm{n}}_{j}|^{\alpha}}\right].\end{split}

Here, we have subtracted
\lvert{\bm{n}}_{j}\rvert^{-\alpha}
to allow better convergence of the sum, but even doing so, one finds that the sum converges pointwise only if
\alpha>\tfrac{1}{2}
. When it does converge,
eq.

3.7
gives the corresponding constitutive kernel.

Since the straightforward approach limits us to the range
\alpha>\tfrac{1}{2}
, we instead take the approach in
[
4
]
, which is to first obtain a constitutive kernel
K^{\alpha}_{p}
, then from it obtain
G^{\alpha}_{p}
. So in place of the sum in
eq.

3.8
for the Green’s function, we sum in a similar manner for the kernel
K^{\alpha}_{p}
, writing,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}K^{\alpha}_{p}&:=K^{\alpha}+H^{\alpha},\\
H^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})&:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}K^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{n}}_{j}).\end{split}

(For
\alpha=0
, the sum in
eq.

3.9
2
does not converge, so in
[
4
]
, the sum was rearranged to pair the terms for
j
and
-j
. For
\alpha>0
, we have sufficient convergence without rearranging the terms, nor would summing in pairs improve the rate of convergence. Also, for
\alpha=0
, the resulting sum can be viewed as a complex analytic function, which yields an explicit and convenient expression in
[
4
]
for
K^{0}_{p}
and
G^{0}_{p}
, but that approach is not available for
\alpha>0
.)

Remark 3.1
.

In our notation, a subscript
p
indicates that a function is
1
-periodic in
x_{1}
as a function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, and so also makes sense as a function on
\Pi
. In
eq.

3.9
, neither
K^{\alpha}
nor
H^{\alpha}
are periodic, although their sum,
K^{\alpha}_{p}
, is periodic. This is a formal statement, but once we establish the convergence of the sum defining
H^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})
we will see that it holds rigorously.

Since for
j>2\lvert{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\rvert
,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\lvert K^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{n}}_{j})\rvert=\alpha c_{\alpha}\bigg\lvert\frac{({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{n}}_{j})^{\perp}}{\lvert{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{n}}_{j}\rvert^{\alpha+2}}\bigg\rvert\leqslant\frac{\alpha c_{\alpha}}{\lvert{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{n}}_{j}\rvert^{\alpha+1}}\leqslant\frac{C}{j^{1+\alpha}},

for any
\alpha>0
, the sum defining
H^{\alpha}
in
eq.

3.9
converges uniformly on compact subsets of

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle:={\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus{\mathcal{L}}^{*},

where
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}=\mathbb{Z}^{*}\times\left\{0\right\}
as in
eq.

2.2
.
Notice that any derivative of
K^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{n}}_{j})
of order
k\geqslant 0

decays like
C\lvert x\rvert^{-(\alpha+1+k)};

then, writing

\displaystyle H^{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}H_{N}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}),\quad H_{N}(x):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=-N\\
j\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}K^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{n}}_{j}),

we see that all the derivatives of
H_{N}

converge uniformly on compact subsets of
U
as well. Since each

K^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{n}}_{j})
is in
C^{\infty}(U)
,
it follows that
H^{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}(U)
.
Moreover,
\dv K^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{n}}_{j})=0
on
U

so
\dv H^{N}=0
on
U
and so in the limit we have
\dv H^{\alpha}=0

on
U
.

Now let

\displaystyle U^{*}:={\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\left\{{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\colon\lvert x_{1}\rvert\geqslant\tfrac{1}{2},x_{2}=0\right\}\subseteq U.

Then
U^{*}
is simply connected and
\dv{\bm{\mathrm{H}}}^{\alpha}=0
on
U^{*}
, so there exists a stream function
R^{\alpha}
on
U^{*}
for
H^{\alpha}
; that is,

\displaystyle H^{\alpha}:=\nabla^{\perp}R^{\alpha},\quad R^{\alpha}(0)=0

on
U^{*}
, where we have fixed the value of
R^{\alpha}
at the origin for uniqueness. We can write the stream function in the form,

(3.11)

\displaystyle R^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})=\int_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})}(H^{\alpha})^{\perp}\cdot d{\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}},

where
\gamma
is any
C^{1}
path in
U^{*}
from the origin to
{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\in U^{*}
and
\tau
is the unit tangent vector.

Further, each
K^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{n}}_{j})
is tangential to any ball
B_{r}({\bm{n}}_{j})
centered at
{\bm{n}}_{j}
, and because
K^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{n}}_{j})
is divergence-free, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(K^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{n}}_{j}))^{\perp}\cdot d{\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}}

\displaystyle=0

for any closed path
\gamma
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
for which
{\bm{n}}_{j}\notin\image{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}
. From this it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(H^{\alpha})^{\perp}\cdot d{\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\left(H_{N}\right)^{\perp}\cdot d{\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}}=0

for any closed path
\gamma
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
not intersecting
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}
. This is enough to show that defining
R^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})
as in
eq.

3.11
for all
{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\in U
provides a continuous extension of
R^{\alpha}
from
U^{*}
to all of
U
.

Finally, we set

(3.12)

\displaystyle G^{\alpha}_{p}:=G^{\alpha}+R^{\alpha}.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\theta\in L^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
then
{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}:=K^{\alpha}_{p}*\theta\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.

Proof.

Let
\theta\in L^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
be
such that the support of
\theta
is contained
in the ball
B_{R}(0)
. Since
{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}
is
1
-periodic in
x_{1}
, we can assume that
{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\in\Pi_{p}
. Then,

\displaystyle\lvert{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\lvert j\rvert\leqslant 2R}\lvert K^{\alpha}(\cdot-{\bm{n}}_{j})*\theta({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})\rvert+\sum_{\lvert j\rvert>2R}\lvert K^{\alpha}(\cdot-{\bm{n}}_{j})*\theta({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})\rvert.

But from
eq.

3.4

\displaystyle\sum_{\lvert j\rvert\leqslant 2R}\lvert K^{\alpha}(\cdot-{\bm{n}}_{j})*\theta({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant C(2R+1)\lVert\theta\rVert_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}\leqslant C(2R+1)^{3}\lVert\theta\rVert_{L^{\infty}},

and

\displaystyle\sum_{\lvert j\rvert>2R}\lvert K^{\alpha}(\cdot-{\bm{n}}_{j})*\theta({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})\rvert\leqslant\sum_{\lvert j\rvert>2R}\lVert K^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{n}}_{j})\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}(\supp\theta)}\lVert\theta\rVert_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\sum_{\lvert j\rvert>2R}\frac{\alpha c_{\alpha}}{\lvert{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{n}}_{j}\rvert^{1+\alpha}}\lVert\theta\rVert_{L^{1}}\leqslant\sum_{\lvert j\rvert>2R}\frac{\alpha c_{\alpha}}{(\lvert j\rvert/2)^{1+\alpha}}\lVert\theta\rVert_{L^{1}}\leqslant C(2R+1)^{2}\lVert\theta\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.\qed

We have the following bounds on

\lvert x_{2}\rvert^{n}&\text{if }n>0.\end{cases}

4.1.
Constitutive law for a single patch

First, we consider the interpretation of the integrals in the constitutive law for a single (that is, non-periodic) patch. As in (10) of
[

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with

boundary parameterized by the chain
{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\colon{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

\alpha\in(0,1]
and
{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

\alpha=1

{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\notin\partial\Omega

Then

(4.1)

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})

\displaystyle=\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}G^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(\beta))\partial_{\beta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(\beta)\,d\beta

satisfies the constitutive law in
eq.

1.1
2
.

Proof.

We derive this as for the Euler equations (see Chapter 8 of
[
23
]

From

3.2

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}K^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{\mathrm{y}}})\theta({\bm{\mathrm{y}}})\,d{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla^{\perp}G^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{\mathrm{y}}})\,d{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}.

For

is locally integrable, so the first equality holds for all
{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

\alpha=1
, as long as
{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\notin\partial\Omega

\lvert{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}\rvert
is bounded away from zero for
{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}
in the support of

Integrating by parts, and parameterizing the boundary by arc length,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{i}G^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{\mathrm{y}}})\,d{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}G^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}(s))n^{i}({\bm{\mathrm{y}}}(s))\,ds,

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(-\partial_{2}G^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}),\,\partial_{1}G({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}))\,d{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}=\int_{\partial\Omega}G^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}(s)){\bm{n}}^{\perp}({\bm{\mathrm{y}}}(s))\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}G^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(\beta))\partial_{\beta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(\beta)\,d\beta.

In the last equality, we used that

\displaystyle{\bm{n}}^{\perp}({\bm{\mathrm{y}}}(s))\,ds

\displaystyle={\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}}({\bm{\mathrm{y}}}(s))\,ds=\partial_{\beta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(\beta)\,d\beta.\qed

5.
The contour dynamics equation (CDE)

5.1.
CDE for a single patch

The CDE for a single patch in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is developed in
[
14
]
, paralleling that for the Euler equations. We can write it, for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t,\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}G^{\alpha}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t,\eta)-{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t,\beta))\partial_{\beta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t,\beta)\,d\beta,

where
\gamma
is a
C^{1}
-parameterization of the patch boundary.

5.2.
CDE for a patch in the periodic strip
\Pi

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
lemma

4.2
leads directly to a CDE by writing
{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\in\partial\Omega_{\Pi}(t)
as
{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}={\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t,\eta)
for some
\eta\in{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}
. This yields

(5.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t,\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}G^{\alpha}_{\Pi}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t,\eta)-{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t,\beta))\partial_{\beta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t,\beta)\,d\beta.

See
remark

4.3
for the interpretation of the integrand in
eq.

5.1
and the expressions that follow. We note that this derivation is formal; if we assume sufficient time regularity of the boundary, though, it follows immediately.

For
\alpha=1
, however, the integral in
eq.

5.1
is not convergent, since the singularity in
G^{\alpha}_{\Pi}
, which comes from
G^{\alpha}
, is like
C\lvert\eta-\beta\rvert^{-1}
in the contour integral, which is not integrable in one-dimension. Following
[
25
,
27
]
, since it is only the normal component of the velocity field that determines the motion of the boundary, we can subtract from the velocity field
\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t,\eta)
in
eq.

5.1
any multiple of the tangential velocity field on the boundary. This is done in a limiting sense as
{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\to\partial\Omega_{\Pi}(t)
in
[
14
]
, or we can simply do it formally, giving a CDE that applies for all
\alpha\in(0,1]
:

(5.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t,\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}G^{\alpha}_{\Pi}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t,\eta)-{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t,\beta))(\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t,\eta)-\partial_{\beta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t,\beta))\,d\beta.

The term
\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t,\eta)
contributes nothing to the normal velocity field, since
\partial_{\beta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t,\eta)\cdot\partial_{\beta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t,\eta)^{\perp}=0
. For
\alpha=1
, it eliminates the singularity in
G^{\alpha}_{\Pi}
that appears in
G^{\alpha}
in the decomposition of
G^{\alpha}_{\Pi}
in
eq.

3.12
. (Also see
remark

5.1
.)

Making the change of variables,
\beta\mapsto\eta-\beta
,
we can write
eq.

5.2
as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t,\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}G^{\alpha}_{\Pi}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t,\eta)-{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t,\eta-\beta))(\partial_{2}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t,\eta)-\partial_{2}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t,\eta-\beta))\,d\beta.

Or, paralleling (13) of
[
14
]
, we have
\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t)=L_{\Pi}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(t))
, where

(5.3)

\displaystyle L_{\Pi}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi})(\eta)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}G^{\alpha}_{\Pi}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(\eta)-{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(\eta-\beta))(\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(\eta)-\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}(\eta-\beta))\,d\beta.

-SQG for which, up to some time

8.
Remarks on the periodic Green’s function

The fundamental difference between our setup and that of Gancedo in
[
14
]
is the Green’s function—
G^{\alpha}
in
[
14
]
versus
G^{\alpha}_{\Pi}
or
G^{\alpha}_{p}
in this paper. Gancedo, who always writes the Green’s function explicitly for
\alpha\in(0,1]
as
c_{\alpha}/\lvert{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\rvert^{\alpha}
, takes advantage of the following key properties:

(1)

G^{\alpha}
is singular only at
{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}=0
, where
G^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})\sim\lvert{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\rvert^{-\alpha}
.

(2)

G^{\alpha}(-{\bm{\mathrm{x}}})=G^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})
.

(3)

G^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})=G^{\alpha}(\lvert{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\rvert)
; that is,
G^{\alpha}
is radially symmetric.

(4)

G^{\alpha}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})
is bounded (in fact, decays) as
\lvert{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}\rvert\to\infty
.

These properties are most critical in the analysis of the CDE, where they appear in two forms:

\displaystyle G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(\beta)-{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(\eta))\text{ and }G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(\eta)-{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(\eta-\beta))=G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)),

where
{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta):={\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(\eta)-{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(\eta-\beta)
, as in
eq.

5.4
. In both cases, these factors appear in the integrand, with
\eta
, and in the second form also
\beta
, integrated over the domain,
{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}
, of a chain
\gamma
.
G^{\alpha}_{p}({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})
has a singularity like
\lvert{\bm{\mathrm{x}}}-{\bm{n}}_{k}\rvert^{-\alpha}
at each lattice point
{\bm{n}}_{j}\in{\mathcal{L}}
, but as long as
\gamma
is a lift of the boundary
{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\Pi}
of a domain in
\Pi
, the arguments of
G^{\alpha}_{p}
above will encounter only the singularity at
\eta=\beta
. Otherwise, two points on
\gamma
would differ by a lattice point
{\bm{n}}_{j}
, meaning that
\gamma
could not be a lift. Hence, in effect, we maintain property (1), at least in principle.

We have (2), but we do not have property (3).
In
[
14
]
, property (3) (which implies property (2)) is used to conclude that certain integrands are perfect derivatives, so their integral over a closed curve vanishes. The first use is to demonstrate, as part of bounding the
H^{3}
norm of
\gamma
, that, supposing
\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}=L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})
,

(8.1)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t)\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}

\displaystyle=2({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t),L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t))=0.

Gancedo starts by using property (2) to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}^{2}=\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\,d\beta\,d\eta.

He then uses that

\displaystyle\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}

\displaystyle G^{\alpha}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\,d\beta\,d\eta=\frac{c_{\alpha}}{2}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\lvert{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert^{-\alpha}\partial_{\eta}\lvert{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert^{2}\,d\eta\,d\beta

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{\alpha}}{2-\alpha}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\partial_{\eta}\lvert{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert^{2-\alpha}\,d\eta\,d\beta=0,

since the inner integral vanishes because the integrand is a perfect derivative in
\eta
.

The first part of this argument works in our setting as well since it uses only property (2), but the second part—which requires that
G({\bm{\mathrm{x}}})
be radially symmetric, the specific form being unimportant—fails in our setting.

Property (4) is used implicitly in
[
14
]
, but we will need to account for the lack of decay of
G^{\alpha}_{p}
, as evidenced in
R^{\alpha}
in
3.3
. Because we assume that the patch at time zero is compactly supported in the vertical direction, we can control the growth of the patch, as the growth of
R^{\alpha}
is only in the vertical direction.

In adapting the arguments in
[
14
]
, we use the decomposition
G^{\alpha}_{p}=G^{\alpha}+R^{\alpha}
, and bound the terms involving
G^{\alpha}
separately from those involving
R^{\alpha}
, since they succumb to slightly different techniques.

As the patch boundary evolves, the singularity in
R^{\alpha}
at points in
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}
must be avoided. To do this, we will modify in
section

9
the function
F
that Gancedo employs to measure the self-intersection of (one component of) the patch boundary.

Furthermore, because we allow patches to be multiply connected, we must also control for two boundary components evolving to intersect. We do this at the end of
section

11.3
.

9.
Measuring self-intersection

The initial data we consider for the patch boundary is smooth and
non-self-intersecting. In addition to maintaining regularity at
positive times, we must demonstrate that we maintain the
non-self-intersecting property at positive times. This is a feature
of all existence theories for water waves, vortex sheets, and vortex
patches, although the need for a non-self-intersection condition is
sometimes obviated by considering the simpler geometry of graphs
rather than general parameterized curves. In the general setting,
following Wu’s work on water waves
[
29
]
, we enforce the
chord-arc condition both on the initial data and at positive times.

As the first author explains in some detail in
[
3
]
,
there are two primary ways of controlling the chord-arc quantity at
positive times. The approach of
[
3
]
and other works
by the first author relies mostly on continuity: since solutions
initially satisfy the chord-arc condition, if the time derivative of
the chord-arc quantity is bounded, then the condition continues to
hold for some positive time interval. The approach taken by Gancedo
in
[
14
]
and in related works
instead directly estimates the

L^{\infty}
-norm of the chord-arc quantity, by taking the limit
of
L^{p}
-norms as
p
goes to infinity.

In the present work,
we take a mixed approach; since many of our estimates are analogous
to estimates of
[
14
]
, we begin by introducing an
analogous quantity,
F,
adapted to our periodic setting,
for which we will make estimates of the

L^{\infty}
-norm. A significant difference, however, is that in
the present work there are
n
boundary components of our patch/
layer regions, and we must also measure how near different
components come to each other. For this, we will follow

[
3
]
more closely, in that we will rely on the fact
that initially the boundary components are
separated by a finite distance, and that their velocities are
bounded.

We now proceed to describe the chord-arc quantities for
individual boundary components, analogously to
[
14
]
.
We will bound the distance between distinct components when the time
comes, specifically, in
Section

11.3
below.

We define a function
F
much as in (16) of
[
14
]
that measures, inversely, how close to self-intersecting a path
\gamma
is. Letting

{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)={\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(\eta)-{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(\eta-\beta)
, as in
eq.

5.4
, for a given
C^{1}
path
\gamma
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, define the functions
F,F_{j}\colon[-\pi,\pi]^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,

(9.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}F_{0}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})(\beta,\eta)&=\begin{cases}\frac{\lvert\beta\rvert}{\lvert{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert}&\text{if }\beta\neq 0,\\
\frac{1}{\lvert\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(\eta)\rvert}&\text{if }\beta=0,\end{cases}\\
F_{j}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})(\beta,\eta)&=\frac{1}{\lvert{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)-{\bm{n}}_{j}\rvert}\text{ for }j\in\mathbb{Z}^{*},\\[1.99997pt]
F({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})(\beta,\eta)&=\max_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{j}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})(\beta,\eta).\end{split}

As long as
\gamma
is non-self-intersecting,
F_{j}
is continuous for all
j\geqslant 0
, with
F_{j}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})(-\pi,\eta)=F_{j}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})(\pi,\eta)
.
We have the simple bound,

(9.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\dist({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta),{\mathcal{L}}^{*})}

\displaystyle=\max_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}F_{j}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})(\beta,\eta)\leqslant F({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})(\beta,\eta).

Moreover,
{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}
,
F_{j}
, and
F
naturally extend to apply to a chain
\gamma
.

Remark 9.1
.

First, we note that although we do not label them as such, we
actually have one such
F
quantity for each of our
n

boundary components. This will not be critical, as we bound all
of them in the same manner. Next, we remark that
the functions
F_{j}
, for
j\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}
, are naturally defined on
{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2}
, but
F_{0}
and so
F
are not. To make
F_{0}
properly defined on
{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2}
, we could replace
\lvert\beta\rvert
in its definition by
\lvert\sin(\beta/2)\rvert
. We use
\lvert\beta\rvert
, however, to more closely align with
[
14
]
. This will force us to use a specific parameterization of
{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}
by
\beta\in[-\pi,\pi]
, though we will not point this out in every instance in which it arises.

9.1.
Bounding derivatives of
G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))

We will now put the chord-arc quantity
F
to use.
We have the following, where repeated indices are implicitly summed over:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{\eta}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))&=\partial_{j}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{j}(\eta),\\
\partial_{\eta}^{2}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))&=\partial_{jk}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{j}(\eta)\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{k}(\eta)+\partial_{j}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\partial_{\eta}^{2}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{j}(\eta),\\
\partial_{\eta}^{3}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))&=\partial_{jk\ell}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{j}(\eta)\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{k}(\eta)\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{\ell}(\eta)\\
&\qquad+\partial_{jk}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\left[\partial_{\eta}^{2}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{j}(\eta)\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{k}(\eta)+\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{j}(\eta)\partial_{\eta}^{2}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{k}(\eta)\right]\\
&\qquad+\partial_{jk}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\partial_{\eta}^{2}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{j}(\eta)\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{k}(\eta)+\partial_{j}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\partial_{\eta}^{3}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{j}(\eta)\\
&=\partial_{jk\ell}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{j}(\eta)\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{k}(\eta)\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{\ell}(\eta)\\
&\qquad+3\partial_{jk}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\partial_{\eta}^{2}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{j}(\eta)\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{k}(\eta)+\partial_{j}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\partial_{\eta}^{3}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{j}(\eta).\end{split}

More generally, we can write, for
k\geqslant 0
,

(9.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}^{k}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\nabla^{j}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\cdot\sum_{\vec{\ell}\in V_{jk}}c_{\vec{\ell}}\partial_{\eta}^{\ell_{1}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\otimes\cdots\partial_{\eta}^{\ell_{j}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta),

where each

is a positive integer and, for

,

\displaystyle V_{jk}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\vec{\ell}\in\left\{1,\dots,k\right\}^{j}\colon\ell_{1}+\cdots+\ell_{j}=k\right\},\quad V_{00}=1.

Setting
m_{n}=2
if

,
m_{n}=n
if

9.2

(9.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lvert\nabla^{n}R^{\alpha}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\rvert&\leqslant\frac{C}{\dist({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta),{\mathcal{L}}^{*})^{n+\alpha}}+C\lvert{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert^{m_{n}}\\
&\leqslant C\left(F({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})(\beta,\eta)\right)^{n+\alpha}+C\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{m_{n}}.\end{split}

Define

(9.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{0}(\beta,\eta)&=S_{0}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})(\beta,\eta):=\left(F({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})(\beta,\eta)\right)^{\alpha}+\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},\\
S_{n}(\beta,\eta)&=S_{n}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})(\beta,\eta):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[\left(F({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})(\beta,\eta)\right)^{j+\alpha}+\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{j}\right],\end{split}

for

. Then
eq.

9.4
gives

(9.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lvert\nabla^{j}R^{\alpha}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant CS_{n}(\beta,\eta)\text{ for }n\geqslant 0.

The derivatives of
G^{\alpha}
are simply bounded in terms of

(9.7)

\displaystyle\lvert\nabla^{n}G^{\alpha}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C}{\lvert{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert^{n+\alpha}}=\left(\frac{\lvert\beta\rvert}{\lvert{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert}\right)^{n+\alpha}\frac{C}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{n+\alpha}}\leqslant C\frac{\lVert F({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}^{n+\alpha}}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{n+\alpha}}.

Then
eqs.

and
9.7
give

(9.8)

\displaystyle\lvert\nabla^{n}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant C\frac{\lVert F({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}^{n+\alpha}}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{n+\alpha}}+\lVert S_{n}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}\leqslant C\frac{\lVert S_{n}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{n+\alpha}},

and thus,

(9.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{k}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\rvert\leqslant C\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lvert\nabla^{j}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\rvert\sum_{\vec{\ell}\in V_{jk}}\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{\ell_{1}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert\cdots\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{\ell_{j}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left[\frac{\lVert F({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}^{j+\alpha}}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{j+\alpha}}+\lVert S_{j}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}\right]\sum_{\vec{\ell}\in V_{jk}}\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{\ell_{1}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert\cdots\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{\ell_{j}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\lVert S_{k}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{j+\alpha}}\sum_{\vec{\ell}\in V_{jk}}\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{\ell_{1}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert\cdots\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{\ell_{j}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert.\end{split}

inequality,

,

Proposition 10.3
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
and let
\gamma
be an
H^{n}({\mathbb{T}})
quasi-closed chain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
for
n\geqslant 4
. Then

\displaystyle\lvert(\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}},\partial_{\eta}L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}))_{L^{2}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\begin{cases}C\lVert S_{1}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{3}}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}&\text{if }\alpha\in(0,1),\\
C\lVert S_{1}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{2}}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}&\text{if }\alpha=1\\
\end{cases}

and, if

, for all

\displaystyle\lvert(\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}},\partial_{\eta}^{m}L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}))_{L^{2}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}\rvert

Proof.

To bound
\lvert(\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}},\partial_{\eta}L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}))_{L^{2}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}\rvert
, from
10.1
, we have

\displaystyle(\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}},

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}))_{L^{2}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}=\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\,d\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\,d\eta=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\partial_{\eta}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\lvert{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert^{2}\,d\eta,

where we integrated by parts using
B.7
. This is as in
[
14
]
, where the integrand is a perfect derivative, making the integral vanish. As we observed in
section

8
, the integrand is not a perfect derivative in the present horizontally periodic setting,
so we are forced to obtain a weaker bound.

From
eq.

9.9
for
k=1
, we have

\displaystyle\lvert({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}},L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}))_{L^{2}{}}\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\lvert\partial_{\eta}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\rvert\lvert{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert^{2}\,d\eta\,d\beta

\displaystyle\leqslant C\lVert S_{1}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\frac{1}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{1+\alpha}}\lVert\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}(\eta)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,d\beta.

We now apply
lemma

B.10
.
If
\alpha\in(0,1)
then

\displaystyle\lvert({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}},L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}))_{L^{2}{}}\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant C\lVert S_{1}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{3}}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\frac{\lvert\beta\rvert}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{1+\alpha}}\,d\beta

\displaystyle=C\lVert S_{1}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{3}}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

If
\alpha=1,
this integral does not converge, so we apply
lemma

B.10
differently, finding instead

\displaystyle\lvert({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}},L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}))_{L^{2}{}}\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant C\lVert S_{1}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{2}}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\frac{\lvert\beta\rvert^{2}}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{1+\alpha}}\,d\beta

\displaystyle=C\lVert S_{1}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{2}}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}.

Next we establish the bound on
\lvert(\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}},\partial_{\eta}^{m}L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}))_{L^{2}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}\rvert
. From
10.1

(10.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}},&\partial_{\eta}^{m}L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}))_{L^{2}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\partial_{\eta}^{m}\left(G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\right)\cdot\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\,d\beta\,d\eta\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\cdot\partial_{\eta}^{k}(G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\partial_{\eta}^{m-k+1}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\,d\eta\,d\beta.\end{split}

We wish to obtain a bound using only the
H^{m}
-norm of
\gamma
, but in the
k=0
term,
\partial_{\eta}^{m-k+1}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)=\partial_{\eta}^{m+1}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)
. That term we integrate by parts using
B.7
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}

\displaystyle\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\cdot G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\partial_{\eta}^{m+1}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\,d\eta\,d\beta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\partial_{\eta}\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\rvert^{2}\,d\eta\,d\beta=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\partial_{\eta}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\rvert^{2}\,d\eta\,d\beta

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\cdot\partial_{\eta}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\,d\eta\,d\beta,

which is a constant multiple of the
k=1
term in
eq.

10.4
. Hence, using
eq.

9.9
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lvert(\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}},\partial_{\eta}^{m}L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}))_{L^{2}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}\rvert&\leqslant C\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{k}(G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\rvert\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{m-k+1}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\rvert\,d\eta\,d\beta\\
&\leqslant C\lVert S_{m}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{\vec{\ell}\in V_{jk}}P_{kj\vec{\ell}},\end{split}

where, for
\ell\in V_{jk}
,

\displaystyle P_{kj\vec{\ell}}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\frac{1}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{j+\alpha}}\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{m-k+1}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\rvert\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{\ell_{1}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert\cdots\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{\ell_{j}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert\,d\beta\,d\eta.

As long as
i<m-1
, we can use
lemma

B.10
to bound the factors
\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{i}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert
appearing in
P_{kj\vec{\ell}}
either in the
L^{\infty}
or
L^{2}
norms; for
i=m-1
, we can only bound
\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{i}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert
in the
L^{2}
norm; and for
i=m
we must bound
\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{i}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert
as is. Since there is always at least one
\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert
factor, there are four cases to consider:

Case 1
: All but the one factor
\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert
in
P_{kj\vec{\ell}}
are of the form
\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{i}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert
for
i<m-1
. Then

\displaystyle P_{kj\vec{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\frac{1}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{j+\alpha}}\lVert\partial_{\eta}^{\ell_{1}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\cdots\lVert\partial_{\eta}^{\ell_{j}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{m-k+1}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\rvert\,d\eta\,d\beta

\displaystyle\leqslant C\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{m}}^{j}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\frac{1}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{j+\alpha}}\lvert\beta\rvert^{j}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{m}}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{m}}\lvert\beta\rvert\,d\beta=C\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{m}}^{j+2}.

We note that this bound would hold for
\alpha=1
as well.

Case 2
:
P_{kj\vec{\ell}}
contains two factors of
\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)
. There are two terms of this form: (1)
k=j=1
, which also has one factor of
\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)
and (2)
k=m
,
j=1
, for which the inner sum has only one term, which contains
m
factors of
\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)
. Using
\lvert\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant C\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{2}}\lvert\beta\rvert
by
lemma

B.10
, we bound
P_{kj\vec{\ell}}
for (1), (2), respectively, by

\displaystyle P_{11\vec{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{2}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\frac{1}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{1+\alpha}}\lvert\beta\rvert\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert^{2}\,d\eta\,d\beta\leqslant C\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{2}}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{m}}^{2},

\displaystyle P_{mm\vec{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{2}}^{m}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\frac{1}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{m+\alpha}}\lvert\beta\rvert^{m}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert^{2}\,d\eta\,d\beta\leqslant C\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{2}}^{m}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{m}}^{2}.

Case 3
:
P_{kj\vec{\ell}}
contains one factor of
\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)
and two factors of
\partial_{\eta}^{m-1}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)
. Those two factors cannot both come from
\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{\ell_{1}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert\cdots\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{\ell_{j}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert
, since
\ell_{1}+\cdots+\ell_{j}\leqslant m
, so it must be that
m-k+1=m-1
, which requires that
k=2
. But then
\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{\ell_{1}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert\cdots\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{\ell_{j}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert
can contain no factor with a higher derivative than
\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{2}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert
, and that only when
j=1
. So this case can only occur when
m=3
, so that
2=m-1
. In this case,

\displaystyle P_{21\vec{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\frac{1}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{1+\alpha}}\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{3}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{j}(\eta)\rvert\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{2}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{2}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert\,d\eta\,d\beta

\displaystyle\leqslant C\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{3}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\frac{1}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{1+\alpha}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{2}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{j}(\eta)\rvert\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{3}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert\,d\eta\,d\beta

\displaystyle\leqslant C\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{3}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\frac{1}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{1+\alpha}}\lVert\partial_{\eta}^{2}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{j}(\eta)\rVert_{L^{2}_{\eta}}\lVert\partial_{\eta}^{3}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rVert_{L^{2}_{\eta}}\,d\beta

\displaystyle\leqslant C\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{3}}^{3}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\frac{1}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{1+\alpha}}\lvert\beta\rvert\,d\beta=C\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{3}}^{3}\leqslant C\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{m}}^{3}.

Case 4
:
P_{kj\vec{\ell}}
contains one factor of
\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)
, one factor of
\partial_{\eta}^{m-1}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)
, and all other factors are of the form
\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{i}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert
for
i<m-1
. This occurs only when (1)
m>3
and
k=2
or (2)
j=1,k=m-1
. For (1), the bound is the same as in Case 1. For (2), it must be that
\ell_{1}=m-1
, so

\displaystyle P_{m-1,1,\vec{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\frac{1}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{1+\alpha}}\lVert\partial_{\eta}^{2}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert\lvert\partial_{\eta}^{m-1}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta))\rvert\,d\eta\,d\beta

\displaystyle\leqslant C\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{m}}^{3}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\frac{1}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{1+\alpha}}\lvert\beta\rvert^{2}\,d\beta=C\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{m}}^{3},

a bound that would hold for
\alpha=1
as well.

Combined, these four cases give the bound on
\lvert(\partial_{\eta}^{m}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}},\partial_{\eta}^{m}L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}))_{L^{2}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}\rvert
.
∎

Proposition 10.4
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
and let
{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{1}
and

be

. Then for any

,

\displaystyle\lvert L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})(\eta_{1})-L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})(\eta_{2})\rvert

A_{1}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}},1)\lvert\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}&\text{if }\alpha=1,\end{cases}

and

. If

\displaystyle B_{1}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{2})

\displaystyle:=C\lVert S_{0}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{2})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}+C\sum_{j=1}^{2}\lVert S_{1}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{j})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{1}\rVert_{H^{2}}+C_{0}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{1}\rVert_{H^{3}}.

(10.5)

\displaystyle\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-ex\log x&\text{if }x<e^{-1},\\
x/e&\text{if }x\geqslant e^{-1}.\end{cases}

\displaystyle\lVert L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{1})-L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{2})\rVert_{L^{2}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}

\displaystyle:=C\max_{1,2}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{j}\rVert_{H^{3}}^{1-\frac{1}{e}}\lVert S_{0}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{2})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}+C\sum_{j=1}^{2}\lVert S_{1}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{j})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{1}\rVert_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{0}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{1}\rVert_{H^{3}}\max_{1,2}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{j}\rVert_{H^{2}}^{1-\frac{1}{e}}.

Proof.

The bound on

\displaystyle P_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\left[R^{\alpha}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{1}(\eta))-R^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{2}(\eta))\right]\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{1}(\eta)\,d\beta,

\displaystyle P_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\left[G^{\alpha}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{1}(\eta))-G^{\alpha}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{2}(\eta))\right]\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{1}(\eta)\,d\beta.

(10.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lVert\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{1}(\eta)\rVert_{L^{2}}&\leqslant 2\lVert\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}}\leqslant 2\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{1}\rVert_{H^{1}},\\

,

(10.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{1}(\eta)-{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{2}(\eta)\rVert_{L^{2}}&\leqslant C\max_{1,2}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{j}\rVert_{H^{2}}^{1-r}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{1}-{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{r}\lvert\beta\rvert^{1-r},\\

9.8

\displaystyle\leqslant C\max_{1,2}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{j}\rVert_{H^{3}}^{1-r}\lVert S_{0}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{2})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}\frac{\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{1}-{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{2}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{r}}{1-r}.

For

Remark 10.5

We can parallel the argument in

Since

We used the following lemma above.

Lemma 10.6
.

Let

. Then

\displaystyle\min_{r\in[0,1)}\frac{a^{r}}{1-r}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-ea\log a&\text{if }a<e^{-1},\\
1&\text{if }a\geqslant e^{-1}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let

. To find the minimum of

, set

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}(r)=\frac{(1-r)(\log a)a^{r}+a^{r}}{1-r},

which occurs when

, so

. To have

, we must have

, and to have
r_{0}>0
, we must have

; that is,

. This gives a minimum value,

\displaystyle f(r_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{1+\frac{1}{\log a}}}{-\frac{1}{\log a}}=-aa^{\frac{1}{\log a}}\log a=-ae^{\frac{\log a}{\log a}}\log a=-ea\log a.

Otherwise, the minimum value of

occurs at

with a value of

.
∎

11.
Well-posedness for
\alpha\in(0,1)

In this section we prove the existence and uniqueness of a periodic CDE solution (as defined in

6.2
) for
\alpha\in(0,1).

Theorem 11.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and let
\Omega_{0}
and
{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{0}\in H^{m}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})
be as in
definition

6.2
.
There exists a time

so that on
[0,T]
there is a unique periodic CDE solution

{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\in C([0,T];H^{m}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}))
with

\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\in C([0,T];L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m-1}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}))

{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(0)={\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{0}

We will adapt the proof of this same theorem for a single compactly supported patch as given by Gancedo in
[
14
]
. In outline, the steps in the proof are as follows:

Step 1

Assume that
\gamma
is an

Step 2

Given

solving

, obtain a bound on
\lVert F({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t))\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}
in terms of itself and
\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t)\rVert_{H^{m}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}
.

Step 3

Given
{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\in C^{1}([0,T];H^{m+1}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}))

\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t)\rVert_{H^{m}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}+\lVert F({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t))\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}

Step 4

Regularize the CDE

5.4

{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{\varepsilon}

{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{\varepsilon}

{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{\varepsilon}
,

at which the

becomes infinite, thereby obtaining a uniform-in-
\varepsilon

, on

.

Step 5

Show that some subsequence
({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{\varepsilon_{n}})

.

Step 6

Show that any periodic CDE solution is unique.

In the subsections that follow, we give each step of the proof.

11.1.
Step 1: Bounding
({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}},L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}))_{H^{m}}

Because

\displaystyle\left(\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{L^{2}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}^{2}+\lVert\partial^{k}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{L^{2}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{ and }\left({\sum_{j=0}^{k}\lVert\partial^{j}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{L^{2}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lvert({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}},L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}))_{H^{m}}\rvert

11.2.
Step 2: Bounding
\lVert F({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t))\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}

Proposition 11.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\in C^{1}([0,T];H^{m+1}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}))
.
For all

,

(11.2)

\displaystyle\lVert F_{j}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t))\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert F_{j}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(0))\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}+\int_{0}^{t}A_{2}(s)\lVert F_{j}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(s))\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}^{2}\,ds,

where

is as in
10.2
. The same bound holds for

.

Proof.

Let
f_{j}(\beta)=\lvert\beta\rvert
if

f_{j}(\beta)=1
if

. Applying

,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\lVert F_{j}\rVert_{L^{p}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\left(\frac{f_{j}(\beta)}{\lvert{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)-{\bm{n}}_{j}\rvert}\right)^{p}\,d\eta\,d\beta

\displaystyle=-p\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}f_{j}(\beta)^{p}\frac{({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)-{\bm{n}}_{j})\cdot\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)}{\lvert{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)-{\bm{n}}_{j}\rvert^{p+2}}\,d\eta\,d\beta

\displaystyle=p\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}f_{j}(\beta)^{-1}F_{j}(\beta,\eta)\lvert\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert\,d\eta\,d\beta.

\displaystyle\lvert\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{\eta\in{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}}\frac{\lvert\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(\eta)-\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(\eta-\beta)\rvert}{\lvert\beta\rvert}\lvert\beta\rvert\leqslant A_{2}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}},\alpha)\lvert\beta\rvert.

Then, since

\displaystyle p\lVert F_{j}\rVert_{L^{p}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}^{p-1}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert F_{j}\rVert_{L^{p}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}=\frac{d}{dt}\lVert F_{j}\rVert_{L^{p}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant CpA_{2}\lVert F_{j}\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}\lVert F_{j}\rVert_{L^{p}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}^{p},

so

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\lVert F_{j}\rVert_{L^{p}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leqslant CA_{2}\lVert F_{j}\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}\lVert F_{j}\rVert_{L^{p}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}.

Integrating in time gives

\displaystyle\lVert F_{j}(t)\rVert_{L^{p}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert F_{j}(0)\rVert_{L^{p}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}+C\int_{0}^{t}A_{2}(s)\lVert F_{j}(s)\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}\lVert F_{j}(s)\rVert_{L^{p}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}\,ds.

Taking

gives

11.2
, and taking the maximum over

gives the same bound for

.
∎

11.3.
Step 3: Combining the bounds

We use
P_{n}
to stand for a polynomial of degree

with

having nonnegative coefficients (so

is increasing for positive

Let
{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\in C^{1}([0,T];H^{m+1}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}))
. Then
\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}=L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})
, so it follows from

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t)\rVert_{H^{m}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\lVert S_{m}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}P_{m+2}(\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\rVert_{H^{m}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}),

and integrating in time,

(11.3)

\displaystyle\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t)\rVert_{H^{m}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(0)\rVert_{H^{m}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\lVert S_{m}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(s))\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}^{2})}P_{m+2}(\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(s)\rVert_{H^{m}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})})\,ds.

\displaystyle\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t)\rVert_{H^{m}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}+\lVert F({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t))\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left[\frac{1}{S(0)^{-{2m+2}}-{(2m+2)}CT}\right]^{\frac{1}{{2m+2}}}.

\displaystyle\qquad\geqslant r-t\lVert L\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}\geqslant r-Ct\lVert L\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}))}

\displaystyle\lvert\langle\psi_{n},{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t)-{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{\varepsilon}(t)\rangle\rvert\leqslant\lVert\psi_{n}\rVert_{H^{-r}}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t)-{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{\varepsilon}(t)\rVert_{H^{r}}\leqslant n^{-1}

uniformly over

, so that

\displaystyle\lvert\langle\varphi,{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t)-{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{\varepsilon}(t)\rangle\rvert\leqslant Cn^{-1}.

This shows that
\langle\varphi,{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{\varepsilon}(t)\rangle\to\langle\varphi,{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t)\rangle
uniformly on
[0,T]
for any
\varphi\in H^{-m}
, which is enough to imply that
{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\in C_{W}([0,T];H^{m}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}))
.

The second step is to show that
\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t)\rVert_{H^{m}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}
is continuous in time. The proof proceeds as for the
\nu=0
case in
[
23
]
, using the uniform energy bound coming from
eq.

11.5
in place of the bound in (3.60) of
[
23
]
and using that
\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{\varepsilon}(t)=L^{\varepsilon}({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{\varepsilon}(t))
, like the Euler equations, is time reversible.

The time continuity of
\gamma
comes from
10.2
and that
\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}=L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}})
.

11.6.
Uniqueness

We now obtain uniqueness in

Let
{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{1}
,
{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{2}

\overline{{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}:={\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{1}-{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{2}

Arguing as in the proof of
10.1
, let

\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{j}(\beta,\eta)

\displaystyle:={\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{j}(\beta)-{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{j}(\eta)

and write

in the form of
eq.

5.2
:

\displaystyle L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{j}(\beta))=\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{j}(\beta,\eta))\partial_{2}{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{j}(\beta,\eta)\,d\eta.

Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}&\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\overline{{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}^{2}=(\overline{{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}},\,L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{1})-L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{2}))_{L^{2}{}}\\

In the second equality, we simply switched variable names

and

and
{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{j}(\eta,\beta)=-{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{j}(\beta,\eta)
and switched the order of integration. Taking the average of the second and fourth expressions, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\lVert\overline{{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}(\overline{{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}(\beta)-\overline{{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}(\eta))\cdot[G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{1}(\beta,\eta))\partial_{2}{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{1}(\beta,\eta)-G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{2}(\beta,\eta))\partial_{2}{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{2}(\beta,\eta)]\,d\eta\,d\beta.

Now let

\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{j}(\eta)

\displaystyle:={\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{j}(\eta)-{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{j}(\eta-\beta),

\displaystyle\overline{{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}}_{\beta}(\eta)

\displaystyle:={\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{1}(\eta)-{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{2}(\eta)=\overline{{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}(\eta)-\overline{{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}(\eta-\beta).

Making the change of variables,
\beta\mapsto\eta-\beta

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\lVert\overline{{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}(-\overline{{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}}_{\beta}(\eta))\cdot[G^{\alpha}_{p}(-{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{1}(\eta))\partial_{2}(-{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{1}(\eta))-G^{\alpha}_{p}(-{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{2}(\eta))\partial_{2}(-{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{2}(\eta))]\,d\eta\,(-d\beta)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\overline{{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}}_{\beta}(\eta)\cdot[G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{1}(\eta))\partial_{2}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{1}(\eta)-G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{2}(\eta))\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{2}(\eta)]\,d\eta\,d\beta.

We make the decomposition as in
[
14
]

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\overline{{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}^{2}=I_{1}+I_{2},

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle:=-\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\overline{{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}}_{\beta}(\eta)\cdot\left[G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{1}(\eta))-G^{\alpha}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{2}(\eta))\right]\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{1}(\eta)\,d\eta\,d\beta,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle:=-\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\overline{{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}}_{\beta}(\eta)\cdot G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{2}(\eta))\partial_{\eta}\overline{{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}}_{\beta}(\eta)\,d\eta\,d\beta.

From the proof of
10.4
,

\displaystyle\lvert G^{\alpha}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{1}(\eta))-G^{\alpha}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{2}(\eta))\rvert\leqslant C_{0}\lvert{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{2}(\eta)-{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{1}(\eta)\rvert\lvert\beta\rvert^{-1-\alpha}=C_{0}\lvert\overline{{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert\lvert\beta\rvert^{-1-\alpha},

using
lemma

B.10
in the last inequality.

\displaystyle\lvert I_{2}\rvert

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{2}(\eta))\partial_{\eta}\lvert\overline{{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert^{2}\,d\eta\,d\beta=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\partial_{\eta}G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{2}(\eta))\lvert\overline{{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert^{2}\,d\eta\,d\beta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}\int_{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}(\nabla G^{\alpha}_{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{2}(\eta))\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\beta}^{2}(\eta))\lvert\overline{{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}}_{\beta}(\eta)\rvert^{2}\,d\eta\,d\beta

Combined, these bounds give

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}^{2}\leqslant C\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}({\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}})}^{2},

where

depends upon the

{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{1}
and
{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{2}

Remark 11.6
.

In Section 5 of
[
14
]
, Gancedo formulates an alternate CDE for
\alpha=1
for which the parameterization
{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t,\cdot)
is “constant-speed” in the sense that

\lvert\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t,\eta)\rvert^{2}=A(t)\text{ for all }\eta\in{\vec{{\mathbb{T}}}}

for some differentiable function
A
. To do this, an additional term must be added to the CDE, transforming it, as in (31) of
[
14
]
, to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t,\eta)

\displaystyle=L({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t,\eta))+\lambda({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t,\eta))\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t,\eta).

Without the addition of
\lambda\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}
, one can easily adapt the uniqueness proof
we just gave to use the
log-Lipschitz modulus of continuity
\mu
(see
remark

10.5
), and complete the uniqueness
argument by applying Osgood’s lemma in place of Grönwall’s lemma. With the addition of

\lambda({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t,\eta))\partial_{\eta}{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(t,\eta)
, however, such an argument fails,
because, ultimately,
\lVert\lambda({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{1})-\lambda({\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{2})\rVert_{L^{2}}
cannot be
bounded sufficiently well in terms of
\lVert{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{1}-{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}}
.

Limits on the Axion–Photon Coupling from Chandrayaan-2
Tanmoy Kumar, N. P. S. Mithun, Subhendra Mohanty, Sourov Roy, B.S. Bharath Saiguhan, Santosh Vadawale

Abstract
Axions and axion-like particles (ALPs) have gained immense attention in searches for beyond Standard Model (BSM) physics. Experiments searching for axions leverage their predicted couplings to Standard Model (SM) particles to look for observable signals. Though weak, these couplings allow axions to be produced abundantly in the interiors of stars such as the Sun. Once created, axions can escape the Sun and while passing through the solar atmosphere, oscillate into photons in the magnetic field producing x-rays. For the first time, we used data from the observation of soft x-rays from the quiet Sun during the 2019-20 solar minimum by the solar x-ray monitor (XSM), onboard India’s Chandrayaan-2 lunar exploration mission, to constrain the coupling of axions to photons ( g a ​ γ ​ γ g_{a\gamma\gamma} ). Using the latest models of the solar atmosphere to calculate the magnetic field and plasma frequency, we constrain g a ​ γ ​ γ ≲ ( 0.47 − 2.2 ) × 10 − 10 g_{a\gamma\gamma}\lesssim(0.47\,-\,2.2)\times 10^{-10} GeV -1 at 95% confidence level for axion masses m a ≲ 5 × 10 − 4 m_{a}\lesssim 5\times 10^{-4} eV.



I
INTRODUCTION

Axions and axion-like particles (ALPs) are among the most compelling candidates for physics
beyond the Standard Model (SM) of particle physics.
Such particles naturally arise in several BSM theories as a pseudo-Nambu-Goldstone boson (pNGB) of some spontaneously broken approximate global
U(1)
symmetry
Kim and Carosi 2010
;
Di Luzio et al. 2020
;
Arvanitaki et al. 2010
. The most famous among such particles is the QCD axion, which arises in the Peccei-Quinn (PQ) model
Peccei and Quinn 1977a
;
Peccei and Quinn 1977b
;
Weinberg 1978
;
Wilczek 1978
;
Peccei 2008
, due to the breaking of the
U(1)_{\rm PQ}
symmetry. In this article, we use the term axions for generic pNGBs associated with nonlinearly realized approximate global symmetries.

Due to a plethora of theories suggesting their existence, a large number of experiments have been proposed or are underway to search for these axions over a wide range of masses stretching from
10^{-22}
eV upto several GeV. The theories that predict the existence of axions also suggest the existence of extremely weak but non-negligible interactions between axions and the SM particles. Due to these weak interactions, light axions (
m_{a}\lesssim
few eV) are also excellent candidates for cold dark matter (DM).
Most of the present and future experiments such as ABRACADABRA
Ouellet et al. 2019a
;
Ouellet et al. 2019b
, DMRadio
Silva-Feaver et al. 2017
;
Brouwer et al. 2022a
;
Brouwer et al. 2022b
, ADMX
Asztalos et al. 2004
;
Du et al. 2018
;
Braine et al. 2020
, HAYSTAC
Zhong et al. 2018
and MADMAX
Caldwell et al. 2017
;
Millar et al. 2017
looking for light axions are designed to search for these low mass axion DM by utilizing their predicted coupling to photons. However, the mere existence of axions in the BSM spectrum does not imply that they have to be DM. In that case, we need to consider other strategies to search for axions.

Due to their couplings with the SM particles, axions can also be produced in a variety of different astrophysical systems. For example, the large temperatures, densities, and volumes of stars and other stellar objects make them efficient factories of such weakly-coupled axions. For sufficiently small couplings, these axions will free-stream out to infinity.
These axions
can be detected using different techniques. A prime target for such search strategies is our Sun, a main-sequence star as well as the closest star to Earth. In the core of the Sun, because of the high temperature (
T\sim\rm few\>\;keV
) and high density (
\rho\sim 150\,\text{gm}\,\text{cm}^{-3}
), axions can be produced copiously. These solar axions, having energies in the range of a few keV, escape the sun and travel towards Earth. Hence, the Earth is expected to be bathed with a large flux of solar axions. The CERN Axion Solar Telescope (CAST) has been searching for these solar axions. In the absence of any positive signal, the CAST experiment has placed stringent bounds in the axion parameter space
Zioutas et al. 2005
;
Barth et al. 2013
;
Anastassopoulos et al. 2017
. Furthermore, the International AXion Observatory (IAXO) has been proposed as a successor to the CAST experiment to search for these solar axions with an enhanced sensitivity
Armengaud et al. 2019
.

In this work, we explore a novel avenue for detecting solar axions. During the 2019-2020 solar minimum, when solar activity was at its lowest, the Chandrayaan-2 lunar orbiter of the Indian Space Research Organization (ISRO) measured the soft x-ray spectrum originating from the Sun using the solar x-ray Monitor (XSM) on board
Vadawale et al. 2021
;
Vadawale et al. 2021
. This soft x-ray spectrum consists of two components - the background quiet-Sun emission and the emission from the flaring plasma. Now if axions exist and are produced inside the Sun, these solar axions (with energies in the range of few keV) can also contribute to the total solar soft x-ray emission due to the conversion of axions into photons in the solar magnetic field. In this work
we conduct a search for axion-induced x-ray emission from the Sun using the observed soft x-ray emission data of the quiet Sun and we put constraints on the axion-photon coupling. We find no evidence of an axion-induced signal over the background and consequently set an upper bound on the axion-photon coupling, which is comparable to the present constraints from the CAST experiment and is limited only by the collecting area of the XSM instrument.

A similar analysis using data from the NuSTAR observation of the Sun during the solar minimum has been presented in
Ruz et al. 2024
. However, unlike NuSTAR, which was able to observe only a portion of the Sun because of its small field of view (FOV) (
12^{\prime}\times 12^{\prime}
), the XSM
observes emission from the entire solar disk.
Our work provides a compelling case for having a dedicated solar observatory operational during the next solar minimum which can significantly improve upon these bounds in an independent fashion.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
II
we outline the production mechanism of axions inside the Sun. In Section
III

IV
, we describe the data that we have used for our analysis and in Section
V
we present the data analysis procedure that we have adopted in our work. Finally, we present our results in Section
VI
.

II
AXION PRODUCTION INSIDE THE SUN

We start with the following Lagrangian for the axion field
a
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\supset\,

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\,\partial_{\mu}a\,\partial^{\mu}a\,-\,\dfrac{1}{2}\,m_{a}^{2}\,a^{2}\,

\displaystyle+\,\dfrac{g_{aee}}{2m_{e}}\,\partial_{\mu}a\,\bar{\psi}_{e}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}\psi_{e}\,-\,\dfrac{g_{a\gamma\gamma}}{4}\,aF^{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu},

(1)

where
g_{a\gamma\gamma}
is the axion-photon coupling,
g_{aee}
is the axion-electron coupling,
m_{a}
is the mass of the axion,
\psi_{e}
is the electron field and
F^{\mu\nu}
is the electromagnetic field strength tensor. For simplicity we do not consider any particular axion model and treat
g_{a\gamma\gamma}
as an independent parameter alongside
m_{a}
.

In the solar plasma where there exists huge quantities of free electrons and ions, due to the above couplings axions can be produced abundantly via the following processes:

1.

Primakoff process:
\gamma\,+\,(e^{-},Ze)\to(e^{-},Ze)\,+\,a

2.

Compton scattering:
\gamma\,+\,e\to e\,+\,a

3.

e-ion bremsstrahlung:
e\,+\,Ze\to e\,+\,Ze\,+\,a

4.

e-e bremsstrahlung:
e\,+\,e\to e\,+\,e\,+\,a

where
e
stands for electron and
Ze
stands for a charged ion. The amplitude of the Primakoff process is governed by
g_{a\gamma\gamma}
while all that of all the other processes are governed by
g_{aee}
.

Following
Raffelt 1986
we calculate the axion emission rate per unit volume and we integrate it over a standard solar model
Bahcall et al. 2005
and finally dividing by the total surface area of the Sun, we obtain the flux of axions coming out of the Sun. For the light axions (
m_{a}<<\rm eV
) that we shall consider in this work, the spectral axion fluxes (in units of cm
-2
s
-1
keV
-1
) produced by the Sun via the four processes are well approximated by the following fitting formulae
Barth et al. 2013

\displaystyle\left.\dfrac{d\phi_{a}}{dE_{a}}\right|_{P}

\displaystyle=3.0\times 10^{11}\,\left(\dfrac{g_{a\gamma\gamma}}{10^{-12}\text{GeV}^{-1}}\right)^{2}E_{a}^{2.450}\,e^{-0.829E_{a}},\,

\displaystyle\left.\dfrac{d\phi_{a}}{dE_{a}}\right|_{B}

\displaystyle=1.25\times 10^{14}\,\left(\dfrac{g_{aee}}{10^{-13}}\right)^{2}\dfrac{E_{a}}{1+0.667\,E_{a}^{1.278}}\,e^{-0.77E_{a}},\,

\displaystyle\left.\dfrac{d\phi_{a}}{dE_{a}}\right|_{C}

\displaystyle=6.32\times 10^{11}\,\left(\dfrac{g_{aee}}{10^{-13}}\right)^{2}E_{a}^{2.987}\,e^{-0.776E_{a}},

(2)

where
E_{a}
is the axion energy in keV and
P,\,B,\,\text{and}\,C
stand for Primakoff, Bremsstrahlung and Compton respectively. In order to calculate the bremsstrahlung flux, both the electron-electron bremsstrahlung and electron-ion bremsstrahlung have been taken into account.

III
AXION-PHOTON CONVERSION OUTSIDE THE SUN

Relativistic axions produced in the solar core propagate along nearly radial trajectories and emerge from the solar surface. As they traverse the solar magnetic field, a fraction convert into photons, producing x-rays. The differential solar x-ray flux resulting from axion–photon conversion in the solar atmosphere is given by

\frac{d\phi_{\gamma}}{dE_{\gamma}}=\frac{d\phi_{a}}{dE_{a}}\times P_{a\gamma},

(3)

where
d\phi_{a}/dE_{a}
is the total axion flux at the solar surface, obtained as the sum of the Primakoff, Bremsstrahlung, and Compton contributions in Eq. (
2
), and
P_{a\gamma}
is the axion–photon conversion probability in the solar magnetic field.

To calculate the conversion probability, we start with the axion-photon mixing equation in an external magnetic field
Raffelt and Stodolsky 1988

\bigg((E_{a}-i\partial_{\ell})\mathbb{I}+M(\ell)\bigg)\begin{pmatrix}|A_{\perp}(\ell)\rangle\\
|A_{\parallel}(\ell)\rangle\\
|a(\ell)\rangle\\
\end{pmatrix}=0\,,

(4)

where
|A_{\parallel(\perp)}\rangle
represents an electromagnetic wave polarized in the direction parallel (perpendicular) to
\vec{B}_{T}
where
\vec{B}_{T}
is the projection of the solar magnetic field
\vec{B}(\ell)
on the plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation of the axions, and

M(\ell)=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{\omega_{p}^{2}(\ell)}{2\omega}&0&0\\
0&-\frac{\omega_{p}^{2}(\ell)}{2\omega}&g_{a\gamma\gamma}\frac{B_{T}(\ell)}{2}\\
0&g_{a\gamma\gamma}\frac{B_{T}(\ell)}{2}&-\frac{m_{a}^{2}}{2\omega}\\
\end{pmatrix}\,.

(5)

Here
\omega_{p}(\ell)=\sqrt{4\pi\,\alpha\,n_{e}(\ell)/m_{e}}
is the plasma frequency in the solar atmosphere, where
\alpha
is the fine structure constant. For this work, we neglect the Euler-Heisenberg term and Faraday rotation related to the longitudinal component of the external field. Since the axion field only couples to
A_{\parallel}
, axion-photon conversion can be described by the bottom
2\times 2
sub-block of
M
, which we denote as
\hat{M}
. Starting from the point
\ell=0
, the solution of Eq. (
4
) at some location
\ell=h
is given by the path-ordered transfer matrix
Raffelt and Stodolsky 1988
;
Manzari et al. 2024

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}|A_{\parallel}(h)\rangle\\
|a(h)\rangle\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle={\cal P}_{\ell}\Bigg[\exp\Big(-iE_{a}h\,\mathbb{I}-i\int_{0}^{h}\hat{M}(\ell)d\ell\Big)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\quad\times\begin{bmatrix}|A_{\parallel}(0)\rangle\\
|a(0)\rangle\end{bmatrix},

(6)

where
|A_{\parallel}(0)\rangle
and
|a(0)\rangle
are the initial field values.

The above integral can be evaluated perturbatively in powers of
g_{a\gamma\gamma}

Raffelt and Stodolsky 1988
. Working up to first order in perturbation theory, one can find that the probability of conversion of axions into photons after traveling a distance
h
from the solar surface in the solar magnetic field is given by,

\displaystyle P_{a\gamma}(h)=

\displaystyle\,|\langle A_{\parallel}(h)|a(0)\rangle|^{2}

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\dfrac{1}{4}\,g_{a\gamma\gamma}^{2}\,e^{-\int_{0}^{h}\,d\ell\,\Gamma(\ell)}\,\left|\int_{0}^{h}\,d\ell\,B_{T}(\ell)\,\right.

\displaystyle\left.\times\,e^{i\int_{0}^{\ell}d\ell^{\prime}q(\ell^{\prime})}\,e^{\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\ell}d\ell^{\prime}\Gamma(\ell^{\prime})}\right|^{2},

where
q(\ell)=(\omega_{p}^{2}(\ell)-m_{a}^{2})/2E_{a}
is the momentum exchanged between the photon and the axion, both carrying energy
E_{a}
and
\Gamma(\ell)
is the coefficient of absorption of x-ray in the solar atmosphere.

Ruz et al. 2024
. The absorption coefficient on the other hand is computed as
\Gamma=\Sigma_{Z}\,n_{Z}\,\sigma_{Z}
where
n_{Z}
is the number density of element of atomic number
Z
and
\sigma_{Z}
is the total attenuation cross-section of a photon passing through a gas of atomic number
Z
. To compute
\Gamma
, we consider all elements up to atomic number
Z=30
. The individual elemental abundance in the solar atmosphere is obtained from the
CHIANTI
atomic database
Dufresne et al. 2024
;
Del Zanna et al. 2021
by using the python interface
ChiantiPy

Chi 2022
. For each element, we take the total photon attenuation cross-section with coherent scattering from the XCOM database by NIST
Berger et al. 2010
.

V
DATA ANALYSIS

The expected number of photons per unit time per unit energy in a given energy bin
i
of the XSM solely due to axion-photon conversion in the solar atmosphere is

N^{a}_{\gamma,\,\rm exp}(E_{i})=\dfrac{R_{\odot}^{2}}{(\text{1 A.U.})^{2}}\,A_{\rm eff}(E_{i})\,\dfrac{d\phi_{a}}{dE_{a}}(E_{i})\,P_{a\gamma}(E_{i}),

(8)

where
E_{i}
is the energy of the
i
-th energy bin,
A_{\rm eff}(E_{i})
is the effective collecting area of the XSM in the
i
-th energy bin which we assume to be constant and equal to
0.32\;\text{mm}^{2}
for all bins and
R_{\odot}
is the radius of the Sun. Given the observed number of counts per unit time per unit energy by the XSM,
N_{\gamma,\,obs}(E_{i})
, for a particular background subtraction scheme, we compute the likelihood assuming independent Gaussian statistic in each energy bin:

\mathcal{L}(g_{a\gamma\gamma};m_{a})\propto\prod_{i}\,\exp\left[-\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{N^{a}_{\gamma,\,\rm exp}(E_{i})\,-\,N_{\gamma,\,obs}(E_{i})}{\sigma(E_{i})}\right)^{2}\right],

(9)

where
\sigma(E_{i})
denotes the uncertainty in the observed counts in the
i
-th energy bin. To compute our limits we consider all the energy bins between
3.4
keV and
15
keV.

To derive constraints, we fix the axion mass
m_{a}
and adopt a uniform prior on the axion–photon coupling
g_{a\gamma\gamma}
over the range
[10^{-14},10^{-8}]\ \mathrm{GeV}^{-1}
. Following a Bayesian approach, the posterior probability density function for
g_{a\gamma\gamma}
, denoted as
\mathcal{P}(g_{a\gamma\gamma}\,|\,\text{data})
, is obtained using the
PyMultiNest
package, which uses the
MultiNest
algorithm
Feroz et al. 2009
to efficiently sample the likelihood and normalize the posterior. The 95% credible upper limit on
g_{a\gamma\gamma}
is then determined by finding the value of
g_{a\gamma\gamma}
below which 95% of the posterior probability is contained. This procedure is repeated for axion masses in the range
[10^{-12},10^{-3}]\ \mathrm{eV}
, yielding the corresponding 95% credible upper limits on
g_{a\gamma\gamma}
as a function of
m_{a}
.

VI
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

With the analysis described above, the observation of soft solar x-ray from quiet-Sun by the XSM onboard Chandrayaan-2 yields the following 95% credible upper limit on the axion–photon coupling:

g_{a\gamma\gamma}\lesssim(0.47\text{--}2.2)\times 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1},

(10)

for axion masses
m_{a}\lesssim 5\times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{eV}
. Here the numerical spread reflects the variation due to the three background subtraction schemes adopted in this analysis: Case 1 (green solid line) gives
g_{a\gamma\gamma}\lesssim 2.2\times 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}
and is the most conservative limit, Case 2 (blue solid line) with realistic background subtraction gives
g_{a\gamma\gamma}\lesssim 1.2\times 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}
, and Case 3 (red solid line) yields the most stringent value
g_{a\gamma\gamma}\lesssim 4.7\times 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}
and is the target/optimistic limit from our analysis. All these limits are shown in Fig.
4
. The limits are effectively mass-independent across this low-mass range; sensitivity degrades for larger
m_{a}
as coherence in the axion–photon conversion is lost when the axion mass exceeds the plasma frequency at the relevant solar-atmosphere heights.

To facilitate comparison with ongoing and future laboratory efforts, we also overlay the limits from existing and future experiments. Shown in Fig.
4
are the existing limits from the CAST
Anastassopoulos et al. 2017
experiment (gray shaded region) and the projected limits from the ALPS-II
Bähre et al. 2013
(black dashed line) and IAXO
Armengaud et al. 2014
;
Armengaud et al. 2019
(black dot-dashed line).
For completeness, we also include in Fig.
4
the recent NuSTAR solar x-ray bound
Ruz et al. 2024
derived from quiet-Sun observations, which provides the most stringent astrophysical limit to date.
Although the most optimistic XSM curve (Case 3, red) is still weaker than the recent NuSTAR result of
6.9\times 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}
by roughly an order of magnitude, the XSM constraints are nevertheless competitive with CAST and, crucially, are complementary: XSM observed the entire solar disk simultaneously, reducing any systematic uncertainties associated with partial-disk observations

We have presented all three background treatments for two reasons. Firstly, showing Case 1 – Case 3 makes explicit the degree to which the final sensitivity depends on background modeling; the spread quantifies the systematic uncertainty associated with background subtraction. Secondly including Case 3 (the red/target curve) indicates the best sensitivity one may realistically expect from this dataset under optimistic background control. In practice, Case 2 should be regarded as our baseline (realistic) constraint, Case 1 as the conservative bound, and Case 3 as the target limit that motivates further work.

Theoretical uncertainties in the solar axion flux calculation are at the few-percent level in the regime relevant to this study and do not drive the overall error budget. The consistency of the limits across the three background treatments—differences remaining within a

The XSM result is also consistent with astrophysical requirements from the measurement of solar neutrino flux and from helioseismology: axion-induced energy loss must remain a small fraction of the solar luminosity and our constraints are well within that regime.

Finally, this remains an ongoing experiment. We therefore present the comparison between the three limits both to document current robustness and to define a clear target for future analyses: continued XSM observations, improved background characterization, and coordinated solar monitoring (photospheric/coronal magnetic mapping) can all push sensitivity toward the Case 3 target. More broadly, a future solar x-ray observatory, particularly around the next solar minimum, with a substantially larger collecting area and improved spectral resolution, would be capable of order-of-magnitude improvements, probing parameter space complementary to planned helioscopes. The Chanrayaan-2 XSM results thus motivate the further development of dedicated space-based solar observatories to search for axions and other physics beyond the Standard Model.

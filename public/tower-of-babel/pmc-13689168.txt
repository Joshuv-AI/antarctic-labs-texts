Complete genome sequences of three effective nitrogen-fixing strains of Bradyrhizobium ottawaense from Canada
Authors: Eden S. P. Bromfield, Sylvie Cloutier
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTWe report complete genome sequences of three nitrogen-fixing Bradyrhizobium ottawaense strains isolated from soybeans in Canada. Each ~9.0 Mb genome (chromosome and plasmid) harbors predicted genes for nodulation, nitrogen fixation, N2O mitigation, phosphate solubilization, iron acquisition, phytohormone production, and stress tolerance, highlighting their potential for sustainable agriculture.

ANNOUNCEMENT

The genus Bradyrhizobium includes economically important bacteria that fix atmospheric nitrogen in symbiosis with soybeans. The strains were isolated in 1999 from surface-sterilized soybean root nodules collected in Ottawa, Canada (45°23′09.0″N, 75°43′11.0″W) (1). Pure cultures from single colonies grown on yeast extract-mannitol (YEM) agar (1) were maintained in 20% glycerol at −80°C. Phylogenetic analysis of housekeeping gene sequences placed several strains in a distinct taxon, subsequently described as Bradyrhizobium ottawaense (2, 3). The species type strain, OO99ᵀ, is effective for nitrogen fixation with soybeans (1) and efficiently reduces the greenhouse gas, nitrous oxide, to dinitrogen gas (4). In plant tests using soybeans (var. Dale) grown in Leonard jars containing nitrogen-free nutrient solution (5, 6), strains OM27, OO86, and OO100 were confirmed as effective nitrogen fixers (Table 1).

For genomic analysis, bacterial strains were grown on YEM agar (7 days, 28°C), harvested, and washed. Genomic DNA was extracted (Wizard SV, Promega), purified (DNeasy PowerClean Pro, Qiagen) following standard protocols, and quantity/quality assessed using Qubit (Thermo Fisher) and DropSense 16 (Trinean, Belgium). Libraries were prepared with the SMRTbell Prep Kit 3.0 following the manufacturer’s protocol after Megaruptor 3 shearing (Diagenode) without size selection. Sequencing was performed at Genome Québec (Canada) on a PacBio Sequel II platform (30 h movie). HiFi reads were generated using default Circular Consensus Sequencing with automatic adapter trimming and read quality control. De novo assembly was performed using the Microbial Assembly application (SMRT Link v11.0), which circularized all replicons.

Genomes were annotated using the NCBI Prokaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline v6.7. Sequencing metrics are summarized in Table 1. All bioinformatic tools utilized default parameters.

FastANI v1.34 (8) values for novel strains versus B. ottawaense OO99T (2, 3) were ≥99.55%, demonstrating their high genomic similarity and assignment to the species B. ottawaense.

Table 1 presents genomic features of novel strains compared to B. ottawaense OO99T. The genome of each novel strain comprises a chromosome (~8.6 Mb) and a plasmid (~0.4 Mb); plasmids were identified by the presence of predicted repABC genes (9). All strains harbor predicted nodulation and nitrogen fixation genes, as well as type III (T3SS) (3, 10) and conjugative type IV (T4SS) (11) secretion system genes, which may influence host specificity and symbiotic performance. Predicted genes for iron acquisition (tonB, exbBD), phosphate solubilization (pho, pqq, pst), stress tolerance (acdS), and phytohormone production (trp) (12, 13) were identified. The detection of a predicted nos denitrification cluster in novel strains suggests potential for greenhouse gas (N2O) mitigation (4). While hydrogen uptake genes were predicted in all genomes, the novel strains lack the structural hupL gene found in OO99T, suggesting an inability to recycle hydrogen from nitrogen fixation (14). Analysis of predicted amino acid sequences using the EPSPSClass web server (15) identified class II 5-enolpyruvylshikimate-3-phosphate synthase enzymes in all strains, suggesting glyphosate resistance. Furthermore, multiple insertion sequences identified by ISEScan (16) suggest genome plasticity and potential for environmental adaptation (17).

These high-quality assemblies provide a valuable resource for genomic studies of B. ottawaense strains with significant potential for sustainable agriculture.

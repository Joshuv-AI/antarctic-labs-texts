Grams: Gradient Descent with Adaptive Momentum Scaling
Yang Cao, Xiaoyu Li, Zhao Song

Abstract
We introduce Gr adient Descent with A daptive M omentum S caling ( Grams ), a novel optimization algorithm that decouples the direction and magnitude of parameter updates in deep learning. Unlike traditional optimizers that directly integrate momentum into updates, Grams separates the update direction, derived from current gradients, from momentum, which is used solely for adaptive magnitude scaling. This approach enables Grams to achieve improved loss descent compared to state-of-the-art cautious and momentum-based optimizers. We theoretically demonstrate that Grams descents faster than other state-of-the-art optimizers and establish a global convergence guarantee for Grams. We also validate its effectiveness through extensive empirical evaluations. The results demonstrate Grams’ superior performance, including faster convergence and better generalization, compared to widely-used optimizers such as Adam, Lion, and their cautious variants. Our results highlight Grams’ potential as a transformative approach for efficiently training large language models. Code is available at https://github.com/Gunale0926/Grams .

In this paper, we propose Gradient Descent with Adaptive Momentum Scaling (Grams), a novel optimization algorithm designed to address the limitations of existing methods. Unlike traditional optimizers that directly couple momentum with gradient updates, Grams decouples the direction and magnitude of parameter updates. This approach allows the update direction to be derived solely from current gradients while momentum is utilized to scale the update magnitude. Such decoupling enhances stability and robustness, particularly in dynamic optimization landscapes.

\displaystyle~\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}H(w_{t},s_{t})\leq 0,

\displaystyle\Delta^{\text{Grams}}_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t}):=

\displaystyle~\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{L}(w_{t})\leq-\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t},s_{t}),

where

and
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}_{t}}(w_{t},s_{t})
represent the decreasing rates of

in accordance with the system in Definition
C.1

The following theorem demonstrates that Grams achieves a faster or equal rate of loss descent compared to Cautious optimizers, highlighting its efficiency in optimization.

Theorem 4.7

(Convergence Comparison of Hamiltonian Dynamics between Grams and Cautious Optimizers, informal version of Theorem
C.4
)
.

From Theorem

C.2

\displaystyle\Delta^{\text{Grams}}_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t})\leq\Delta^{\text{C}}_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t}).

.

Theorem

illustrates the faster loss decreasing speed in the Grams Hamiltonian dynamic system, compared to Cautious’s counterpart.

4.4
Global Convergence of Grams

In this subsection, we establish the global convergence properties of the Grams optimizer. By analyzing the update rules and assumptions on the optimization landscape, we demonstrate that Grams converges to a stationary point of the objective function. This analysis underscores the optimizer’s robustness and effectiveness in a wide range of optimization scenarios.

4.4.1
Assumptions

To ensure theoretical rigor, we base our analysis on the following standard assumptions commonly used in optimization theory. These assumptions define the properties of the loss function and the optimization setting, enabling precise derivations of convergence guarantees.

Assumption 4.10

(Lower bound of loss)
.

The Loss function
\mathcal{L}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is differentiable and closed within its open domain
\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{L})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and is bounded from below, i.e.,
\mathcal{L}^{*}:=\inf_{w}\mathcal{L}(w)>-\infty

Assumption 4.11

(Bounded gradient)

The Loss function
\mathcal{L}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies
\nabla\mathcal{L}(w)\leq G
for all

Assumption 4.12

(
L
-smooth)
.

The Loss function
\mathcal{L}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is
L

Assumption 4.13

(

The Loss function

\mu

6
Conclusion and Future Work

In this paper, we introduced Gradient Descent with Adaptive Momentum Scaling (Grams), a novel optimization algorithm designed to decouple the direction and magnitude of parameter updates. By leveraging this decoupling, Grams demonstrated superior performance in both theoretical convergence guarantees and empirical evaluations, outperforming state-of-the-art optimizers such as Adam
[
21
]
, Lion
[
7
]
, and their Cautious variants
[
20
]
. The results across various tasks highlight Grams’ potential as a transformative approach for efficiently training large language models.

Grams achieved faster convergence and better generalization in our experiments. These properties make it particularly well-suited for modern applications such as large-scale pre-training and fine-tuning of deep learning models, where efficiency and stability are critical.

Building on the promising results of Grams, future work will focus on integrating ideas from recent advancements such as ADOPT
[
35
]
, Schedule Free
[
10
]
, and SOAP-Muon
[
40
]
methods. Incorporating the ADOPT and schedule-free learning rate adjustment strategies might improve Grams’ robustness and performance across diverse tasks and architectures. By blending these complementary innovations with the core principles of Grams, we aim to develop an even more versatile and efficient optimization framework for large language model training.

.

Let two vectors

\displaystyle\langle a,b\rangle-\langle|a|,|b|\rangle\leq 0.

Fact A.4
.

Let

be an element-wise product of two vectors

\displaystyle~\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}b_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}||b_{i}|-(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}b_{i}-\sum_{i:a_{i}b_{i}>0}^{n}a_{i}b_{i})

\eta_{t}\leq\frac{2}{L\|u_{t}\|^{2}_{2}}\langle|g_{T}|,|u_{t}|\rangle
, then we have
\Delta\mathcal{L}_{w_{t+1}^{\mathrm{Grams}},w_{t}}\leq 0
.
∎

Theorem B.3

(Loss Descent Comparison, formal version of Theorem
4.3
)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{L}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is
L
-smooth.
For any parameter vector
w
at optimization step
t
, let
w_{t}^{\mathrm{Grams}}
and
w_{t}^{\mathrm{C}}
be the update of Grams in Definition
4.1
and Cautious optimizers in Definition
3.7
, respectively. If the stepsize
\eta_{t}
satisfies

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\leq\frac{2}{L\|u_{t}\|^{2}}\cdot\min\{\langle u_{t}\circ g_{t},{\bf 1}_{u_{t}\circ g_{t}\geq 0}\rangle,\langle u_{t}\circ g_{t},{\bf 1}_{u_{t}\circ g_{t}<0}\rangle\},

then we have

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{L}_{w_{t+1}^{\mathrm{Grams}},w_{t}}\leq\Delta\mathcal{L}_{w_{t+1}^{\mathrm{C}},w_{t}}\leq 0.

Proof.

We define the index sets:

\displaystyle I^{+}=

\displaystyle~\{i\in[d]:u_{t,i},g_{t,i}\geq 0\};

\displaystyle I^{-}=

\displaystyle~\{i\in[d]:u_{t,i},g_{t,i}<0\}.

By Part 1. of Lemma
B.2
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{L}_{w_{t+1}^{\mathrm{Grams}},w_{t}}\leq-\eta_{t}\langle|g_{t}|,|u_{t}|\rangle+\frac{L\eta_{t}^{2}}{2}\|u_{t}\|_{2}^{2}.

(11)

By Part 2. of Lemma
B.1
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{L}_{w_{t+1}^{\mathrm{C}},w_{t}}\geq

\displaystyle~-\eta_{t}\langle u_{t}\circ g_{t},{\bf 1}_{u_{t}\circ g_{t}\geq 0}\rangle.

(12)

Then we can show that

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{L}_{w_{t+1}^{\mathrm{Grams}},w_{t}}-\Delta\mathcal{L}_{w_{t+1}^{\mathrm{C}},w_{t}}\leq

\displaystyle~-\eta_{t}\langle|g_{t}|,|u_{t}|\rangle+\eta_{t}\langle u_{t}\circ g_{t},{\bf 1}_{u_{t}\circ g_{t}\geq 0}\rangle+\frac{L\eta_{t}^{2}}{2}\|u_{t}\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~-\eta_{t}\sum_{i=1}^{d}|u_{t,i}||g_{t,i}|+\eta_{t}\sum_{i\in I^{+}}u_{t,i}g_{t,i}+\frac{L\eta_{t}^{2}}{2}\|u_{t}\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~-\eta_{t}\sum_{i\in I^{+}}|u_{t,i}||g_{t,i}|-\eta_{t}\sum_{i\in I^{-}}|u_{t,i}||g_{t,i}|+\eta_{t}\sum_{i\in I^{+}}u_{t,i}g_{t,i}+\frac{L\eta_{t}^{2}}{2}\|u_{t}\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~-\eta_{t}\sum_{i\in I^{+}}u_{t,i}g_{t,i}-\eta_{t}\sum_{i\in I^{-}}|u_{t,i}||g_{t,i}|+\eta_{t}\sum_{i\in I^{+}}u_{t,i}g_{t,i}+\frac{L\eta_{t}^{2}}{2}\|u_{t}\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~-\eta_{t}\sum_{i\in I^{-}}|u_{t,i}||g_{t,i}|+\frac{L\eta_{t}^{2}}{2}\|u_{t}\|^{2}_{2}

where the first step follows from Eq. (
12
) and Eq. (
11
), the second step expands vectors element-wise, the third step follows from that
[d]
is the disjoint union of
I^{+}
and
I^{-}
, the fourth step follows from that
|u_{t,i}||g_{t,i}|=u_{t,i}g_{t,i}
for
i\in I^{+}
, and the last step follows from basic algebra.

To ensure
\Delta\mathcal{L}_{w_{t+1}^{\mathrm{Grams}},w_{t}}-\Delta\mathcal{L}_{w_{t+1}^{\mathrm{C}},w_{t}}\leq 0
, it suffices to have

\displaystyle-\eta_{t}\sum_{i\in I^{-}}|u_{t,i}||g_{t,i}|+\frac{L\eta_{t}^{2}}{2}\|u_{t}\|^{2}_{2}\leq 0.

Rearranging the above inequality gives

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\leq

\displaystyle~\frac{2}{L\|u_{t}\|_{2}^{2}}\sum_{i\in I^{-}}|u_{t,i}||g_{t,i}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\frac{2}{L\|u_{t}\|_{2}^{2}}\langle g_{t}\circ u_{t},{\bf 1}_{u_{t}\circ g_{t}<0}),

where the last step follows from the definition of
I^{-}
and basic algebra.

Note that by Part 3 of Lemma
B.1
, if
\eta_{t}\leq\frac{2}{L\|u_{t}\|_{2}^{2}}\langle g_{t}\circ u_{t},{\bf 1}_{g_{t}\circ u_{t}\geq 0}\rangle
, we have
\mathcal{L}_{w_{t+1}^{\mathrm{C}},w_{t}}\leq 0
.
∎

Appendix C
Hamiltonian Dynamics

Definition C.1

(Section 2.1 from
[
20
]
)
.

Momentum-based algorithms can be typically viewed as monotonic descending algorithms on an augmented loss
H(W,S)
, which satisfies
\min_{S}H(W,S)=\mathcal{L}(W),
so that minimizing
\mathcal{L}(W)
is equivalent to minimizing
H(W,S)
. A typical choice is

\displaystyle H(w,s)=\mathcal{L}(w)+\mathcal{K}(s),

where
\mathcal{K}(\cdot)
is any lower bounded function.
The continuous-time form of most momentum-based algorithms can be written into a Hamiltonian descent form:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}w_{t}=

\displaystyle~-\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t})-\Phi_{t}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}))

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}s_{t}=

\displaystyle~\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t})-\Psi_{t}(\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t}))

(13)

where
H(W,S)
is a Hamiltonian (or Lyapunov) function
that satisfies

\displaystyle\min_{S}H(W,S)=\mathcal{L}(W),~~~~\forall W,

so that minimizing
\mathcal{L}(W)
reduces to minimizing
H(W,S)
;
and
\Phi(\cdot),\Psi(\cdot)
are two monotonic mappings satisfying

\displaystyle\langle x,\Phi(x)\rangle\geq 0,

\displaystyle\langle x,\Psi(x)\rangle\geq 0,

\displaystyle\forall x\in X.

With
\Phi(X)=\Psi(X)=0
, the system in
(
13
)
reduces to the standard Hamiltonian system that keeps
H(W_{t},S_{t})=const
along the trajectory.
When adding the descending components with
\Phi
and
\Psi
, the system then keeps
H(W,S)
monotonically decreasing:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}H(w_{t},s_{t})=\Delta_{H}(w_{t},s_{t})\leq 0,

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{H}(w_{t},s_{t}):=-\langle x,\Phi(x)\rangle-\langle x,\Psi(x)\rangle.

(14)

On the other hand,
\mathcal{L}(w)
, which is the true objective, is not necessarily decreasing monotonically.

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{L}(w_{t})=-\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t},s_{t}),

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t},s_{t}):=\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t})\rangle+\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),\Phi_{t}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t})\rangle.

(15)

Theorem C.2

(Theorem 2.3 in
[
20
]
)
.

For Hamiltonian dynamics of Cautious optimizer (in Definition
3.7
), we have:

\displaystyle\Delta^{\text{C}}_{H}(w_{t},s_{t}):=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}H(w_{t},s_{t})=

\displaystyle~\langle x_{t},\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{1}_{x_{t}>0}\rangle-\Delta_{H}(w_{t},s_{t}).

\displaystyle\Delta^{\text{C}}_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t}):=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{L}(w_{t})=

\displaystyle~-\langle x_{t},\mathbf{1}_{x_{t}>0}\rangle-\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),\Phi_{t}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}))\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\langle x_{t},\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{1}_{x_{t}>0}\rangle-\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t},s_{t}).

where
\Delta_{H_{t}}(w_{t},s_{t})
and
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}_{t}}(w_{t})
represent the decreasing rates of
H
and
\mathcal{L}
in accordance with the system in Definition
C.1
.

Hence:

•

If
\langle x_{t},(\mathbf{1}_{d}-\mathrm{sign}(x_{t}))\rangle\leq 0
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then both
H
and
\mathcal{L}
decrease faster than the original system:

\displaystyle\Delta^{\text{C}}_{H}(w_{t},s_{t})\leq

\displaystyle~-\Delta_{H_{t}}(w_{t},s_{t})\leq 0,

\displaystyle\Delta^{\text{C}}_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t})\leq

\displaystyle~-\Delta_{\mathcal{L}_{t}}(w_{t},s_{t}).

•

If
\langle x_{t},\mathrm{sign}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}))\rangle\geq 0
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
\mathcal{L}
decreases monotonically:

\displaystyle\Delta^{\text{C}}_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t})\leq 0.

Theorem C.3

(Convergence of Grams Hamiltonian Dynamics, formal version of Theorem
4.6
)
.

Following the dynamics in Definition
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta^{\text{Grams}}_{H}(w_{t},s_{t}):=

\displaystyle~\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}H(w_{t},s_{t})\leq 0,

\displaystyle\Delta^{\text{Grams}}_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t}):=

\displaystyle~\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{L}(w_{t})\leq-\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t},s_{t}),

where
\Delta_{H_{t}}(w_{t},s_{t})
and
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}_{t}}(w_{t},s_{t})
represent the decreasing rates of
H
and
\mathcal{L}
in accordance with the system in Definition
C.1
.

Proof.

Recall Eq. (
14
) and (
15
):

\displaystyle\Delta_{H}(w_{t},s_{t}):=

\displaystyle~\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),\Phi(\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}))\rangle+\langle\mathcal{K}(s_{t}),\Psi(\mathcal{K}(s_{t}))\rangle

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t},s_{t}):=

\displaystyle~\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t})\rangle+\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),\Phi_{t}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t})\rangle.

Following the dynamics in Definition
4.5
, we can calculate the derivative of
H(w_{t},s_{t})
with respect to
t
:

\displaystyle\Delta^{\text{Grams}}_{H}(w_{t},s_{t})=

\displaystyle~\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}w_{t}\rangle+\langle\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t}),\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}s_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),-\mathrm{sign}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}))\circ|\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t})|-\Phi_{t}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}))\rangle

\displaystyle~+\langle\mathcal{K}(s_{t}),\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t})-\Psi_{t}(\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t}))\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),-\mathrm{sign}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}))\circ|\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t})|\rangle+\langle\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t}),\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t})\rangle-\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),\Phi_{t}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}))\rangle

\displaystyle~-\langle\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t}),\Psi_{t}(\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t}))\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t})\rangle-\langle|\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t})|,|\nabla\mathcal{K}(w_{t})|\rangle-\Delta_{H}(w_{t},s_{t})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~0,

where the first step follows from the chain rule for the time derivative of the Hamiltonian
H
, the second step substitutes the dynamics from Definition
4.5
, the third step separates the inner products for clearer analysis, the fourth step follows the definition of
\Delta H(w_{t},s_{t})
and Fact
A.2
, and the last step follows Fact
A.3
, and
-\Delta H(w_{t},s_{t})\leq 0
.

Then, we calculate the derivative of
\mathcal{L}(w_{t})
with respect to
t
.

\displaystyle\Delta^{\text{Grams}}_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t})=

\displaystyle~\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),-\mathrm{sign}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}))\circ|\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t})|-\Phi_{t}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}))\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),-\mathrm{sign}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}))\circ|\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t})|\rangle-\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),\Phi_{t}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}))\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~-\langle|\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t})|,|\nabla\mathcal{K}(w_{t})|\rangle-\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),\Phi_{t}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}))\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t})\rangle-\langle|\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t})|,|\nabla\mathcal{K}(w_{t})|\rangle

\displaystyle~-(\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),\Phi_{t}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}))\rangle+\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t})\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t})\rangle-\langle|\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t})|,|\nabla\mathcal{K}(w_{t})|\rangle-\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t},s_{t})

where the first step follows from the chain rule, and the second step separates the inner products. The third step follows Fact
A.2
, the fourth step adds and subtracts the term
\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t})\rangle
simultaneously, the fifth step follows the definition of
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t},s_{t})
from Eq. (
2
).

Since we know
\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t})\rangle-\langle|\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t})|,|\nabla\mathcal{K}(w_{t})|\rangle\leq 0
from Fact
A.3
,

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t})\rangle-\langle|\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t})|,|\nabla\mathcal{K}(w_{t})|\rangle\leq-\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t},s_{t})

Thus we complete the proof.
∎

Theorem C.4

(Convergence Comparison of Hamiltonian Dynamics between Grams and Cautious Optimizers, formal version of Theorem
4.7
)
.

From Theorem
C.3
and
C.2
, recall
\Delta^{\text{Grams}}_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t})
and
\Delta^{\text{C}}_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t})
:

\displaystyle\Delta^{\text{Grams}}_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t})\leq\Delta^{\text{C}}_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t}).

Proof.

We calculate the difference between
\Delta^{\text{Grams}}_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t})
and
\Delta^{\text{C}}_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t})
:

\displaystyle\Delta^{\text{Grams}}_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t})-\Delta^{\text{C}}_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t})=

\displaystyle~\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t})\rangle-\langle|\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t})|,|\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t})|\rangle-\langle x_{t},\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{1}_{x_{t}>0}\rangle,

where
x_{t}=\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t})\circ\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t})
.

By applying Fact
A.4
, we know:

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t}),\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t})\rangle-\langle|\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t})|,|\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t})|\rangle-\langle x_{t},\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{1}_{x_{t}>0}\rangle\leq 0,

with equality if all components of
\nabla\mathcal{L}(w_{t})\circ\nabla\mathcal{K}(s_{t})\geq 0
.

Thus:

\displaystyle\Delta^{\text{Grams}}_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t})-\Delta^{\text{C}}_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t})\leq 0,

which implies:

\displaystyle\Delta^{\text{Grams}}_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t})\leq\Delta^{\text{C}}_{\mathcal{L}}(w_{t}).

Thus we complete the proof.
∎

Appendix D
Global Convergence

Theorem D.1

(Convergence of Grams, formal version of Theorem
4.15
)
.

Suppose that Assumptions
4.10
,
4.11
,
4.12
and
4.13
hold. Given initial point
w_{1}
with initial optimality gap
\Delta_{1}:=\mathcal{L}(w_{1})-\mathcal{L}^{*}<\infty
, choose large an enough
G
such that
G\geq\max\{\epsilon,3\sqrt{L\Delta_{1}}\}
, a small enough fixed step size
\eta>0
, and
\beta=\Theta(\eta G^{1/2})
. Consider that the weight
w_{t}
is updated by Grams (Algorithm
1
) for each
t\in[T]
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(w_{T})-\mathcal{L}^{*}\leq\frac{4G}{\mu\eta T}(\mathcal{L}(w_{1})-\mathcal{L}^{*}).

Proof.

Given initial weight
w_{1}
, we denote
w^{\prime}_{1},w^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,w^{\prime}_{T}
be the weights updated by Adam where
w^{\prime}_{1}:=w_{1}
. By Lemma
4.14
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(w^{\prime}_{t})\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{8G\Delta_{1}}{\eta T}.

(16)

For each
t\in[T]
, we can show that

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(w^{\prime}_{t})\|_{2}^{2}\geq

\displaystyle~2\mu(\mathcal{L}(w^{\prime}_{t})-\mathcal{L}^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~2\mu(\mathcal{L}(w^{\prime}_{t})-\mathcal{L}(w^{\prime}_{t-1})+\mathcal{L}(w^{\prime}_{t-1})-\mathcal{L}(w^{\prime}_{t-2})+\cdots+\mathcal{L}(w^{\prime}_{2})-\mathcal{L}(w^{\prime}_{1})+\mathcal{L}(w^{\prime}_{1})-\mathcal{L}^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~2\mu(\Delta\mathcal{L}_{w_{t}^{\prime},w_{t-1}}+\Delta\mathcal{L}_{w_{t-1}^{\prime},w_{t-2}}+\cdots+\Delta\mathcal{L}_{w_{2}^{\prime},w_{1}}+\mathcal{L}(w^{\prime}_{1})-\mathcal{L}^{*})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~2\mu(\Delta\mathcal{L}_{w_{t},w_{t-1}}+\Delta\mathcal{L}_{w_{t-1},w_{t-2}}+\cdots+\Delta\mathcal{L}_{w_{2},w_{1}}+\mathcal{L}(w^{\prime}_{1})-\mathcal{L}^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~2\mu(\Delta\mathcal{L}_{w_{t},w_{t-1}}+\Delta\mathcal{L}_{w_{t-1},w_{t-2}}+\cdots+\Delta\mathcal{L}_{w_{2},w_{1}}+\mathcal{L}(w_{1})-\mathcal{L}^{*})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~2\mu(\mathcal{L}(w_{t})-\mathcal{L}(w_{t-1})+\mathcal{L}(w_{t-1})-\mathcal{L}(w_{t-2})+\cdots+\mathcal{L}(w_{2})-\mathcal{L}(w_{1})+\mathcal{L}(w_{1})-\mathcal{L}^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~2\mu(\mathcal{L}(w_{t})-\mathcal{L}^{*}),

(17)

where the first step follows from Assumption
4.13
, the second step follows from basic algebra, the third step follows from Definition
3.8
, the fourth step follows form Theorem
B.3
, the fifth step follows from
w^{\prime}_{1}=w_{1}
, the sixth step follows from Definition
3.8
, and the last step follows from basic algebra.

Combining Eq. (
16
) and Eq. (
17
) gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(w_{T})-\mathcal{L}^{*}\leq\frac{4G}{\mu\eta T}(\mathcal{L}(w_{1})-\mathcal{L}^{*}).

Thus we complete the proof.

∎

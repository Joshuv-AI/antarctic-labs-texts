On the gracesize of trees
Shoham Letzter, Alexey Pokrovskiy, Ella Williams

Abstract
An n n -vertex tree T T is said to be graceful if there exists a bijective labelling ϕ : V ⁡ ( T ) → { 1 , … , n } \phi:V(T)\to\{1,\ldots,n\} such that the edge-differences { | ϕ ⁡ ( x ) − ϕ ⁡ ( y ) | : x ​ y ∈ E ⁡ ( T ) } \{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|:xy\in E(T)\} are pairwise distinct. The longstanding graceful tree conjecture, posed by Rósa in the 1960s, asserts that every tree is graceful. The gracesize of an n n -vertex tree T T , denoted gs ⁡ ( T ) \gs(T) , is the maximum possible number of distinct edge-differences over all bijective labellings ϕ : V ⁡ ( T ) → { 1 , … , n } \phi:V(T)\to\{1,\ldots,n\} . The graceful tree conjecture is therefore equivalent to the statement that gs ⁡ ( T ) = n − 1 \gs(T)=n-1 for all n n -vertex trees. We prove an asymptotic version of this conjecture by showing that for every ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 , there exists n 0 n_{0} such that every tree on n > n 0 n>n_{0} vertices satisfies gs ⁡ ( T ) ≥ ( 1 − ε ) ​ n \gs(T)\geq(1-\varepsilon)n . In other words, every sufficiently large tree admits an almost graceful labelling.

Introduction

Within extremal combinatorics, a common question to pose is “which properties must a host graph possess in order to guarantee that it contains a given subgraph?”, with classical results such as theorems of Mantel
[
12
]
and Dirac
[
4
]
providing sufficient conditions for the existence of triangles and spanning cycles respectively. Colouring analogues of these problems have been extensively studied, most notably in Ramsey theory, which investigates the appearance of monochromatic subgraphs in edge-coloured graphs. A complementary perspective is given by anti-Ramsey theory, initiated by Erdős, Simonovits, and Sós
[
6
]
, which concerns the existence of rainbow subgraphs, in which all edges must receive distinct colours.
In this paper, we use this colouring framework to study a classical labelling problem in combinatorics: the graceful tree conjecture. Specifically, we tackle the problem by reformulating the conjecture in terms of finding rainbow trees in appropriately edge-coloured complete graphs, connecting graceful labellings to tools from extremal and probabilistic graph theory.

A

\phi:V(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{N}

\{1,\ldots,m+1\}
and the values of

are pairwise distinct over all edges

Graceful labellings were first introduced by Rósa
[
17
]
in 1966 (as

[
8
]
. Given a labelling
\phi

of

[

) reports that almost all graphs do not have this property.
On the other hand, trees are an interesting class of graphs to consider, since the set of edge-differences of any graceful labelling
\phi

\{1,\dots,n-1\}
.
This resulted in the graceful tree conjecture, posed in the 1960s, and usually accredited to Rósa.

Conjecture 1.1

(Graceful tree conjecture)

Every tree admits a graceful labelling.

Graceful labellings were initially motivated as a method for proving Ringel’s conjecture

Proof strategy for
Lemma

1.7

We have already observed that a graceful labelling of a tree
T
with labels in
[n]
can be thought of interchangeably as a rainbow embedding of
T
in
K_{[n]}
. Therefore throughout, we will regard labellings and (edge-coloured) embeddings of graphs as equivalent, where the colour of an edge is uniquely determined by taking the absolute difference of the labels assigned to the endvertices of the edge. More explicitly, given two adjacent vertices
x,y\in V(T)
for some tree
T
together with a mapping
\phi:V(T)\rightarrow[n]
, we will refer to the colour of the edge
xy
to mean the value of
|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|
.

Let us now give an overview of the main ideas used in our strategy to embed a given
n
-vertex tree
T
into
K_{[(1+\varepsilon)n]}
in an almost rainbow way. First, in
Section

4
, we find a helpful structure within
T
which is easier to work with. We split up
T
by first isolating a set
S_{\text{high}}\subseteq V(T)
, consisting of all vertices of high degree. There cannot be too many high degree vertices, since
e(T)=n-1
, so we have an upper bound for
|S_{\text{high}}|
. We find a structural argument
Lemma

4.1
which roughly states the following: for any subset of vertices
S\subseteq V(T)
such that
|S|
is not too large, there exists a set of vertices
W\subseteq V(T)\setminus S
, also of small order, such that deleting
S\cup W
from
T
yields many vertex-disjoint copies of some small forest
F
. Applying this with
S_{\text{high}}
playing the role of
S
, we can think of the set
W
as a set of ‘waste’ vertices in
T
, of which the lemma tells us there are not too many. So, we have that
T\setminus(S_{\text{high}}\cup W)
is exactly made up of many copies of some small forest
F
.

Our new aim is to find a fully rainbow copy of
T\setminus W
in
K_{[(1+\varepsilon)n]}
, since at the end, the set
W
will be embedded arbitrarily to the available leftover labels, and this is where we may obtain colour repetitions. There will not be too many repeats since all vertices in
W
will have low degree, so that the number of edges with an endvertex in
W
is small. The embedding of
T\setminus W
is the main focus in
Section

5
, with the key lemma given as
Lemma

5.9
. So, let us now summarise this process, noting that
T\setminus W
is comprised of the set
S_{\text{high}}
and many vertex-disjoint copies of
F
.
We define an auxiliary tree
T_{\text{aux}}
from
T\setminus W
obtained by contracting the set
S_{\text{high}}
into a single vertex
v
, and contracting sets of vertices in the copies of
F
(chosen so that these sets of vertices are copies of the same vertex of
F
) so that the resulting tree
T_{\text{aux}}
consists of one high degree vertex
v
adjacent to constantly many copies of
F
.

We observe that for any integer
d
, a copy of
F\times d
, that is, the union of
d
vertex-disjoint copies of
F
, can be constructed by taking the union of
e(F)
pairwise edge-disjoint matchings, each of size
d
, and each of which corresponds to a distinct edge of
F
. Thus we find tools for embedding rainbow matchings in
Lemma

5.4
, and use these to find a rainbow embedding of
T_{\text{aux}}
inside
K_{[(1+\varepsilon/2)|T_{\text{aux}}|]}
. We consider a rainbow ‘blow up’ of this auxiliary tree in
K_{[(1+\varepsilon)n]}
by thinking of each vertex as a set (these will correspond to the contracted vertex sets) and by replacing each edge with some rainbow matching.
With the flexibility from the extra labels, this allows us to manipulate the properties of the blow up and be more careful with which sections we are allowed to embed into, in order to obtain a rainbow embedding of
T\setminus W
in
K_{[(1+\varepsilon)n]}
, as desired.

For this argument to work we require that both
|S_{\text{high}}|
is small, and all vertices in
W
have low degree. The former condition holds using the fact that all vertices in
S_{\text{high}}
have degree bigger than some constant
\Delta
, so since
e(T)=n-1
there must be fewer than
2n/\Delta
of them. But if we choose
\Delta
to be too high, then we cannot get a good upper bound for the maximum degree of the waste vertices. Therefore, for these conditions to be compatible, we need to be particularly careful with how we select the parameters which define these sets. More detail of this is given prior to the full proof, in
Section

6
.

|W|\leqslant 6|S|

Proof.

Let

. We need to find an upper bound for

Claim 4.3

For any tree

\sum_{v:d_{H}(v)\geqslant 3}(d_{H}(v)-2)+2

Proof of claim.

Let

\displaystyle\begin{split}2e(H)=\sum_{v}d_{H}(v)&=\sum_{v:d_{H}(v)\geqslant 3}d_{H}(v)+\sum_{v:d_{H}(v)=2}d_{H}(v)+\sum_{v:d_{H}(v)=1}d_{H}(v)\\
&=\sum_{v:d_{H}(v)\geqslant 3}d_{H}(v)+\sum_{v:d_{H}(v)=2}2+\sum_{v:d_{H}(v)=1}1.\end{split}

(4.1)

Expanding out the left hand-side gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}2e(H)=2|H|-2&=\left(\sum_{v:d_{H}(v)\geqslant 3}2+\sum_{v:d_{H}(v)=2}2+\sum_{v:d_{H}(v)=1}2\right)-2.\end{split}

(4.2)

Combining

and rearranging, we have that

\ell=\sum_{v:d_{H}(v)=1}1=\sum_{v:d_{H}(v)\geqslant 3}(d_{H}(v)-2)+2,

as required. This proves the claim. ∎

Since we chose

minimally, we must have that all leaves in

is at most
|S|
. By applying

to the tree

, it follows that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in S\\
d_{T_{S}}(v)\geqslant 3\end{subarray}}(d_{T_{S}}(v)-2)\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V(T_{S})\\
d_{T_{S}}(v)\geqslant 3\end{subarray}}(d_{T_{S}}(v)-2)=(\#\text{ leaves in }T_{S})-2\leqslant|S|-2\leqslant|S|,

and rearranging gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in S\\
d_{T_{S}}(v)\geqslant 3\end{subarray}}d_{T_{S}}(v)\leqslant|S|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in S\\
d_{T_{S}}(v)\geqslant 3\end{subarray}}2\ \leqslant|S|+2|S|=3|S|.

We use this upper bound to deduce that

\displaystyle|W|\leqslant|N_{T_{S}}(S)|\leqslant\sum_{v\in S}d_{T_{S}}(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in S\\
d_{T_{S}}(v)=1\end{subarray}}1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in S\\
d_{T_{S}}(v)=2\end{subarray}}2+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in S\\
d_{T_{S}}(v)\geqslant 3\end{subarray}}d_{T_{S}}(v)

\displaystyle\leqslant|S|+2|S|+3|S|

Suppose there exists a component

which sends two distinct edges into

,

∎

We use the following standard observation about trees, providing a proof for completeness.

Fact 4.4
.

Every

-vertex tree

such that all components of

have order at most

.

Proof.

Let us consider an orientation of the tree

given as follows: for every

, direct the edge from

to

if the component of

containing

is smaller than the one containing

(and orient the edge

arbitrarily if these components have the same size). Every oriented tree contains a sink, that is, a vertex

where

in

is smaller than the component of

.
∎

This fact provides a means to prove the inductive step of the next straightforward lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let

. For every

-vertex tree

of order at most

.

We proceed by induction on

obtained by applying the result for

. Let

denote the set of components of

which have order larger than

since these components are pairwise vertex-disjoint and

Lemma 4.6
.

Let

. For every

-vertex tree

Let

. Applying

Finally we find a way to delete vertices from a forest

so that the subgraph obtained consists precisely of disjoint copies of a new forest

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\zeta>0
be such that
\zeta n\in\mathbb{N}
and suppose

By Cayley’s formula there exist at most

, and for each of them there are at most

. For each such rooted tree

, we delete at most
\zeta n

so that the remaining number of copies is divisible by

. The remaining graph is a copy of

∎

We are now ready to prove

, combining the properties we have seen already.

Proof of

4.1
.

Let

,

\zeta_{0}\coloneqq\frac{\delta}{5m^{m+1}}

., and let

, there exists a set

5

5.1
Using hypergraphs for matchings

Observe that given a forest
F
and some integer

F\times d
is exactly the union of
e(F)
colour-disjoint rainbow matchings, where each matching has size
d
and corresponds to a unique edge in
F
. By using
Lemma

4.1
, if we can find rainbow matchings in
K_{[n]}
, this will give us tools for embedding parts of trees in a rainbow way.
It will be helpful to consider
3
-uniform
3
-partite hypergraphs by making the following observation. Consider two disjoint vertex sets
A,B\subseteq[n]
and a colour set
C\subseteq C(K_{[n]})
, let
K_{[n]}[A,B,C]
denote the edge-coloured subgraph of
K_{[n]}
containing only edges between
A
and
B
which have colour in
C
. We can consider the corresponding
3
-uniform
3
-partite hypergraph, denoted by
H_{[n]}[A,B,C]
, with vertex classes
A,B
and

such that for all
a\in A,\ b\in B,\ c\in C
, the size-three edge

is an edge in

if and only if the edge

has colour

, or equivalently
c=|b-a|
.
In particular, there is a mapping
\theta:E(K_{[n]}[A,B,C])\rightarrow E(H_{[n]}[A,B,C])
which maps an edge

to the size-three edge

where

. Thus we observe the following.

Observation 5.1
.

A subset

is a rainbow matching if and only if

is a hypergraph matching in

.

In order to use this observation, we will first need some preliminary lemmas about

.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

be such that
s\leqslant n/2
and

is even. Let

,

and

such that
\Delta(G)=2s
, every vertex in
[2s,\frac{n}{2}-s]\cup[\frac{n}{2}+2s,n-s]
has degree
2s
, every colour in
[2s,n-2s]
appears on exactly
s
edges, no colour in
C
appears on more than
s
edges, and no edge has colour

.

Proof.

Note that
C=C(K_{[n]})
so

contains all possible colours in
K_{[n]}
. We will find our desired spanning subgraph

to be contained in
K_{[n]}[A,B,C]
, meaning that all edges will lie between

and
B
.

For every

, let
E_{c}\coloneqq\left\{\left(\frac{n-c+i}{2},\frac{n+c+i}{2}\right):i=1,2,\ldots,2s\right\}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
Then
|E_{c}|=s
for all

. Indeed, both of the values

and

are odd or they are both even, implying that the terms
\frac{n-c+i}{2}
and
\frac{n+c+i}{2}
are integer valued exactly when

is odd or even respectively. Furthermore any edge in

has colour

, since for a fixed

.

Now, let

\frac{n-c+i}{2}\leqslant\frac{n-2s+i}{2}\leqslant\frac{n-2s+2s}{2}=\frac{n}{2},

\frac{n+c+i}{2}\geqslant\frac{n+2s+i}{2}\geqslant\frac{n+2s+1}{2}\geqslant\frac{n}{2}+1,

\frac{n+c+i}{2}\leqslant\frac{n+(n-2s)+i}{2}\leqslant\frac{2n-2s+2s}{2}=n.

This shows that

for all

, i.e. every pair in

forms an edge in
K_{[n]}
of colour

, going from

to
B
.
Let
G
be the spanning subgraph of

defined by its edge set

E(G)=\bigcup_{c\in C\setminus\{1\}}E_{c}\cap(A\times B),

So

can be constructed by first taking edges from all

sets, except for when

, and deletes edges not lying between

and
B
. Thus no edge in

has colour
1
. Note that if

is odd then

, and if
n
is even,
E_{1}\cap(A\times B)=\{(n/2,n/2+1)\}
, so by not adding this edge set to

we only affect the degrees of at most two vertices, namely
n/2
and
n/2+1
, neither of which belong in
[2s,\frac{n}{2}-s]\cup[\frac{n}{2}+2s,n-s]
. For any
c\in C^{\prime}
, since

, then this implies
E_{c}\subseteq E(G)
and in particular every colour

occurs on exactly
|E_{c}|=s
edges of

, as desired.
Any colour in
C\setminus C^{\prime}
clearly appears on at most
s
edges.
It remains to check the degrees of vertices in
A
and
B
.

First consider
A^{\prime}\coloneqq[2s,\frac{n}{2}-s]
, and fix

. Let
C(x)\coloneqq\{c\in C:\frac{n-c+i}{2}=x\text{ and }\frac{n+c+i}{2}\in B\text{ for some }i\in[2s]\}
. Note that

\frac{n-c+i}{2}=x\iff c=n+i-2x,

so we can deduce that any

satisfies

and
c\geqslant n+1-2x\geqslant n+1-(n-2s)=1+2s
.
This implies

and

. In particular

is the set of colours

for which there exists

such that

has colour

. If there exist two edges in

of colour

that contain

of colour

for every

.

Similarly, let

. We may argue similarly that there exists exactly one edge in

. So there are

.
Finally all vertices in

are contained in at most

Consider again the subgraph
K_{[n]}[A,B,C]
of

for disjoint vertex sets

and a colour set

.
If every element of

, then observe that the corresponding hypergraph

is linear. We would like to find matchings in this hypergraph, in accordance with

. We will use the following variant of an unpublished result from Pippenger (see e.g.

]
) in order to find almost perfect matchings in linear uniform hypergraphs.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
n^{-1}\ll\gamma\ll\alpha,\mu,r^{-1}
.
Every
n
-vertex linear
r
-uniform hypergraph
H
with maximum degree at most
(1+\gamma)\alpha n
and at most
\mu n
vertices of degree less than
(1-\gamma)\alpha n
has a matching covering all but at most
2\mu n
vertices.

Proof.

Let
H
be as in the statement of the lemma. A result of Molloy and Reed (see
[
14
, Theorem 1]
), tells us that the chromatic index
\chi^{\prime}(H)
is at most
\Delta+c\Delta^{1-1/r}(\log\Delta)^{4}
for some constant
c
, where
\Delta
is the maximum degree of
H
. Thus there exists a proper edge-colouring of
H
using at most
\chi^{\prime}(H)\leqslant(1+\gamma)\alpha n+((1+\gamma)\alpha n)^{1-1/(r+1)}
colours, by choosing
n
sufficiently large. Each colour class forms a matching. Since at least
(1-\mu)n
vertices in

have degree at least
(1-\gamma)\alpha n
, then
e(H)\geqslant(1-\mu)(1-\gamma)\alpha n^{2}/r
.
Thus there is a colour class of size at least

\displaystyle\frac{e(H)}{\chi^{\prime}(H)}\geqslant\frac{(1-\mu)(1-\gamma)\alpha n^{2}/r}{(1+\gamma)\alpha n+((1+\gamma)\alpha n)^{1-\frac{1}{r+1}}}

\displaystyle=(1-\mu)\frac{n}{r}\left(\frac{1-\gamma}{1+\gamma+(1+\gamma)^{1-\frac{1}{r+1}}(\alpha n)^{-\frac{1}{r+1}}}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant(1-\mu)^{2}\frac{n}{r}

\displaystyle\geqslant(1-2\mu)\frac{n}{r}.

The second inequality holds by choosing
n
sufficiently large to satisfy
n\geqslant\frac{(1-\mu)(1+\gamma)^{r}}{\alpha(\mu-2\gamma-\mu\gamma)^{r+1}}
.
Since this colour class contains at least
(1-2\mu)n/r
pairwise disjoint edges, each of size

, then the number of vertices covered by this matching is at least
(1-2\mu)n
. Thus the matching covers all but at most
2\mu n
vertices in

.
∎

.

Let

Choose

be as in the statement of the lemma. Let

,

.
Let

and

. Let

denote the spanning subgraph obtained by applying

and

Proof of Lemma 1.7.

We can now finally collate what we know to prove

At the beginning, we consider many possible choices for a parameter

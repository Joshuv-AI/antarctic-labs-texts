Abstract
. Let ψ \psi be a continuous decreasing function defined on all large positive real numbers. We say that a real m × n m\times n matrix A A is ψ \psi -Dirichlet if for every sufficiently large real number t t one can find 𝒑 ∈ ℤ m \bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m} , 𝒒 ∈ ℤ n ∖ { 𝟎 } \bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{\bm{0}\} satisfying ‖ A ​ 𝒒 − 𝒑 ‖ m < ψ ⁡ ( t ) \|A\bm{q}-\bm{p}\|^{m}<\psi({t}) and ‖ 𝒒 ‖ n < t \|\bm{q}\|^{n}<{t} . 1.
Introduction

Let
m,n
be two positive integers and let
d=m+n
. The classical generalized Dirichlet’s theorem states that:

Theorem 1
.

For any
A\in\mathrm{M}_{m,n}(\mathbb{R})
and
t>1
, there exists
(\bm{p},\bm{q})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{\bm{0}\})
satisfying the following system of inequalities:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\|A\bm{q}-\bm{p}\|^{m}\leq\frac{1}{t}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\|\bm{q}\|^{n}<t.

Here
\|\cdot\|
denotes the supremum norm on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}
respectively.

In view of Dirichlet’s theorem, a natural question to ask is whether one can improve (
1.1
) by replacing
1/t
by a smaller function, that is, consider the following system of inequalities:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\|A\bm{q}-\bm{p}\|^{m}<\psi(t)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\|\bm{q}\|^{n}<t

where
\psi
is a positive, continuous, decreasing function which decays to zero at infinity. In this paper, we continue the study of
[
KSY22
]
on the
metric
aspect of the
uniform approximation
problem on improving Dirichlet’s theorem, which aims to understand the Lebesgue measure of the set of matrices
A\in\mathrm{M}_{m\times n}(\mathbb{R})
for which the system of inequalities (
1.2
) is sovable in
(\bm{p},\bm{q})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{\bm{0}\})
for
all sufficiently large
t
.
We refer the reader to the introduction of
[
KSY22
]

for a more extensive review on related literature.

As in
[
KSY22
]
we consider the slightly more general
weighted
problem replacing the supremum norms in (
1.2
) by certain weighted quasi-norms.
Let
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\bm{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be two
weight vectors
, that is

\displaystyle\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})\in(\mathbb{R}_{>0})^{m}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\bm{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})\in(\mathbb{R}_{>0})^{n}

with
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}=\sum_{j}\beta_{j}=1
. Following
[
KSY22
]
, we say that
A\in\mathrm{M}_{m,n}(\mathbb{R})
is
\psi_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}
-Dirichlet
if the system of inequalities

(1.3)

\displaystyle\|A\bm{q}-\bm{p}\|_{\bm{\alpha}}<\psi(t)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\|\bm{q}\|_{\bm{\beta}}<t

has solutions in
(\bm{p},\bm{q})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{\bm{0}\})

for all sufficiently large
t
. Here

\displaystyle\|\bm{x}\|_{\bm{\alpha}}:=\max\left\{|x_{i}|^{1/\alpha_{i}}\>:\>1\leq i\leq m\right\}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\|\bm{y}\|_{\bm{\beta}}:=\max\left\{|y_{j}|^{1/\beta_{j}}\>:\>1\leq j\leq n\right\}

are the two quasi-norms associated with
\bm{\alpha}
and
\bm{\beta}
respectively. One easily sees that
A\in\mathrm{M}_{m,n}(\mathbb{R})
is
\psi_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}
-Dirichlet if and only if
A+A^{\prime}
is
\psi_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}
-Dirichlet for any
A^{\prime}\in\mathrm{M}_{m,n}(\mathbb{Z})
. Thus with slight abuse of notation,
we may denote by
\operatorname{\mathbf{DI}}_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(\psi)\subset\mathrm{M}_{m,n}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
the set of
\psi_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}
-Dirichlet matrices. One is naturally interested in determining the Lebesgue measure of
\operatorname{\mathbf{DI}}_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(\psi)
for different approximating functions. A particular question of interest is whether
\mathrm{Leb}(\operatorname{\mathbf{DI}}_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(\psi))
satisfies a
zero-one law
depending on
some criterion on the approximating function
\psi
.

For the case when
m=n=1
, such a zero-one law was established by Kleinbock and Wadleigh
[
KW18
, Theorem 1.8]
using continued fractions. However, this approach is not applicable for higher dimensions.
For general
m
and
n
, this question was studied in
[
KSY22
]
using homogeneous dynamics via the classical Dani correspondence. In particular, the main result of
[
KSY22
]
is the following
partial zero-one law on
\mathrm{Leb}(\operatorname{\mathbf{DI}}_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(\psi))
:

Theorem 2

(
[
KSY22
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Fix
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and two weight vectors
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\bm{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
d=m+n
, and let

(1.4)

\displaystyle\varkappa_{d}=\frac{d^{2}+d-4}{2}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\lambda_{d}=\frac{d(d-1)}{2}.

Let
t_{0}>0
and let
\psi:[t_{0},\infty)\to{(0,\infty)}
be a continuous, decreasing function such that

(1.5)

the function
t\mapsto t\psi(t)
is increasing

and

(1.6)

\displaystyle\psi(t)<1/t\quad\textrm{for all $t\geq t_{0}$}.

Let
F_{\psi}(t):=1-t\psi(t)
. If the series

(1.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq t_{0}}k^{-1}F_{\psi}(k)^{\varkappa_{d}}\log^{\lambda_{d}}\left(\tfrac{1}{F_{\psi}(k)}\right)

converges, then
\operatorname{\mathbf{DI}}_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(\psi)
is of full Lebesgue measure. Conversely, if the series (
1.7
) diverges, and

(1.8)

\displaystyle\liminf_{t_{1}\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{t_{0}\leq k\leq t_{1}}k^{-1}F_{\psi}(k)^{\varkappa_{d}}\log^{\lambda_{d}+1}\left(\frac{1}{F_{\psi}(k)}\right)}{\left(\sum_{t_{0}\leq k\leq t_{1}}k^{-1}F_{\psi}(k)^{\varkappa_{d}}\log^{\lambda_{d}}\left(\frac{1}{F_{\psi}(k)}\right)\right)^{2}}=0,

then
\operatorname{\mathbf{DI}}_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(\psi)
is of zero Lebesgue measure.

The following theorem is the main result of the present paper. It
extends Kleinbock and Wadleigh’s zero-one law to general dimensions by removing the assumption (
1.8
) in Theorem
2
.
It also weakens the monotonicity assumption (
1.5
) by replacing it
by an assumption requiring
F_{\psi}(t)=1-t\psi(t)
to be “quasi-decreasing” in a certain sense (see condition (
1.9
) below).

Theorem 3
.

Let
d=m+n
,
(\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\varkappa_{d},\lambda_{d}
be as in Theorem
2
.
Let
t_{0}>1

and let
\psi:[t_{0},\infty)\to{(0,\infty)}
be a continuous, decreasing function satisfying (
1.6
) and

(1.9)

\displaystyle\exists\,C_{\psi}\geq 1,\,\eta\in(0,1)\ \text{such that}\ F_{\psi}(t_{2})\leq C_{\psi}F_{\psi}(t_{1}),\qquad\forall\,t_{0}\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq t_{1}e^{(\log t_{1})^{\eta}}.

Here
F_{\psi}(t)=1-t\psi(t)
is as before. Then we have

(1.10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Leb}(\operatorname{\mathbf{DI}}_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(\psi))=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\sum_{k}k^{-1}F_{\psi}(k)^{\varkappa_{d}}\log^{\lambda_{d}}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{F_{\psi}(k)}\right)<\infty$},\\[3.0pt]
0&\text{if $\sum_{k}k^{-1}F_{\psi}(k)^{\varkappa_{d}}\log^{\lambda_{d}}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{F_{\psi}(k)}\right)=\infty$}.\end{cases}

Remark 1.11
.

Let
\psi
be as in Theorem
3
. Note that assumption (
1.9
) implies that for all
j\gg 1
,

(1.12)

\displaystyle C_{\psi}^{-1}F_{\psi}(e^{j+1})\leq F_{\psi}(k)\leq\min\{1,C_{\psi}F_{\psi}(e^{j})\},\qquad\forall\,e^{j}\leq k\leq e^{j+1}.

From this and using the fact that the function
x\mapsto x^{\varkappa_{d}}\log^{\lambda_{d}}(1+\tfrac{1}{x})
is strictly increasing on
(0,1]
(which follows from the fact that
\varkappa_{d}\geq\lambda_{d}
) we have

(1.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{k}k^{-1}F_{\psi}(k)^{\varkappa_{d}}\log^{\lambda_{d}}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{F_{\psi}(k)}\right)=\infty\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\sum_{j}F_{\psi}(e^{j})^{\varkappa_{d}}\log^{\lambda_{d}}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{F_{\psi}(e^{j})}\right)=\infty.

Remark 1.14
.

We note that Theorem
3
implies Theorem
2
. Indeed, let
\psi
be as in Theorem
2
, that is, it satisfies (
1.5
) and (
1.6
). Then
0<F_{\psi}(t)<1
for all
t\geq t_{0}
(by (
1.6
)) and
F_{\psi}(t)
is decreasing in
t
(by (
1.5
)). This shows that for such
\psi
,

(1.15)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq t_{0}}k^{-1}F_{\psi}(k)^{\varkappa_{d}}\log^{\lambda_{d}}\left(\tfrac{1}{F_{\psi}(k)}\right)=\infty\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\sum_{k\geq t_{0}}k^{-1}F_{\psi}(k)^{\varkappa_{d}}\log^{\lambda_{d}}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{F_{\psi}(k)}\right)=\infty.

On the other hand, note that condition (
1.5
) is a special case of (
1.9
) with
C_{\psi}=1
. Hence (
1.10
) is applicable for such
\psi
, which, together with (
1.15
), clearly implies the conclusion of Theorem
2
.

We mention that the reason we change the series in (
1.7
) to the series in (
1.10
) is that for
\psi
satisfying the assumptions in Theorem
3
, it is possible that
\liminf_{t\to\infty}t\psi(t)=0
, or equivalently,
\limsup_{t\to\infty}F_{\psi}(t)=1
. When this happens, in view of (
1.13
) and noting that
\limsup_{j\to\infty}F_{\psi}(e^{j})>0
(by (
1.12
)) we are in the divergence case of (
1.10
), and we will show
\mathrm{Leb}(\operatorname{\mathbf{DI}}_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(\psi))=0
. However, since
\lim_{t\to 1^{+}}\log(t)=0
,
a prori it is possible that the series in (
1.7
) converges in this case.

1.1.
Notation and conventions

A real-valued function
f
defined on an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is called
increasing
(resp.
decreasing
) if
f(t_{1})\leq f(t_{2})
(resp.
f(t_{1})\geq f(t_{2})
) whenever
t_{1}<t_{2}
.
For two positive quantities
A
and
B
, we write “
A\ll B
” to denote
A\leq cB
for some constant
c>0
, and we write
“
A\asymp B
” to denote
A\ll B\ll A
. We will also write “
O(A)
” to denote any real number
E
satisfying
|E|\leq cA
for some constant
c>0
. We will sometimes use subscripts to indicate the dependence of the implicit constant (i.e., the constant
c
above) on parameters.

2.
Measure estimate of a certain family of sets

Fix an integer
d\geq 2
. Let
G=\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
,
\Gamma=\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
and let
X_{d}=G/\Gamma
be the space of unimodular lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\pi:G\to X_{d}
be the natural projection from
G
to
X_{d}
. Let
\mu_{d}
be the unique invariant probability measure on
X_{d}
. Let
\Delta:X_{d}\to[0,\infty)
be the function defined by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Delta(\Lambda):=\sup_{\bm{v}\in\Lambda\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\log\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{\|\bm{v}\|}\Bigr).

We note that the fact that
\Delta(\Lambda)\geq 0
for any
\Lambda\in X_{d}
is a consequence of the Minkowski’s convex body theorem, while the level set
\Delta^{-1}\{0\}
is the
critical locus
of
X_{d}

with respect to the supremum norm, which, by Hajós’s Theorem
[
Haj41
]
, is a finite union of explicit compact submanifolds of codimension

\frac{(d-1)(d+2)}{2}
,
cf.
[
KSY22
, Theorem 2.3]
.
For our purpose we are naturally interested in the level sets
\Delta^{-1}[0,r]
for small
r>0
as they will describe the target sets in our shrinking target problem.
Indeed, the following measure estimate on
\Delta^{-1}[0,r]
was proved in
[
KSY22
]
:

Theorem 2.1

(
[
KSY22
, Theorem 1.3]
)
.

We have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mu_{d}\left(\Delta^{-1}[0,r]\right)\asymp_{d}r^{\frac{(d-1)(d+2)}{2}}\log^{\frac{d(d-1)}{2}}\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{r}\Bigr),\qquad\textrm{as $r\to 0^{+}$}.

For our purpose of removing the technical assumption (
1.8
), we need to study a more carefully chosen subset of
\Delta^{-1}[0,r]
which we now introduce. For any Borel set
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, set

\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{A}):=\left\{\Lambda\in X_{d}:(\Lambda\setminus\{\bm{0}\})\cap\mathcal{A}\neq\emptyset\right\}.

For any
0<r<1
,
let

R_{r}:=(1-\tfrac{r}{2d},1+\tfrac{r}{2d})\times(-\sqrt{r},\sqrt{r})^{d-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},

and define

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}_{r}:=\Delta^{-1}[0,r]\cap\mathcal{H}(R_{r}),\qquad\forall\,0<r<1.

We note that compared to
\Delta^{-1}[0,r]
,

\Delta_{r}^{\prime}
satisfies the following additional property:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\Lambda\in\Delta_{r}^{\prime}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\exists\,\bm{v}\in\Lambda\ \text{such that}\ \bm{v}\in R_{r}=(1-\tfrac{r}{2d},1+\tfrac{r}{2d})\times(-\sqrt{r},\sqrt{r})^{d-1}.

This property will allow us to prove a disjointness result for our shrinking targets in a larger range compared to taking
\Delta^{-1}[0,r]
directly as the target sets as in
[
KSY22
]
; see Proposition
4.4
below.

On the other hand, we show below that the
\mu_{d}
-measure of
\Delta_{r}^{\prime}
is comparable to that of
\Delta^{-1}[0,r]
:

Theorem 2.2
.

We have

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mu_{d}\left(\Delta^{\prime}_{r}\right)\asymp_{d}r^{\frac{(d-1)(d+2)}{2}}\log^{\frac{d(d-1)}{2}}\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{r}\Bigr),\qquad\textrm{as $r\to 0^{+}$}.

2.1.
Recap of the lower bound in (
2.2
)

Since
\Delta^{\prime}_{r}\subset\Delta^{-1}[0,r]
, in view of (
2.2
), in order to prove Theorem
2.2
, we only need to prove the lower bound in (
2.5
). We will follow closely the approach in
[
KSY22
]
. Let us first review relavent results in
[
KSY22
]
proving the lower bound in (
2.2
).

We first introduce the following set of coordinates that we will be working with; see
[
KSY22
, Section 2.1]
for more details.
Let
\{\bm{e}_{i}:1\leq i\leq d\}
be the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let

\displaystyle P=\{p\in G\>:\>p\bm{e}_{d}=t\bm{e}_{d}\ \textrm{for some $t\neq 0$}\}<G,

be the maximal parabolic subgroup fixing the line spanned by
\bm{e}_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let

\displaystyle N=\left\{u_{\bm{x}}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I_{d-1}&\bm{x}\\
\bm{0}^{t}&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\>:\>\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\right\}<G

be the transpose of the unipotent radical of
P
.

Let
\nu
be the Haar measure of
G
that locally agrees with
\mu_{d}
. Up to removing a null set, every element
g\in G
can be written uniquely as a product:

(2.6)

\displaystyle g=p_{\bm{b}_{1},\ldots,\bm{b}_{d-1}}u_{\bm{x}}\quad{\text{for some $p_{\bm{b}_{1},\ldots,\bm{b}_{d-1}}\in P$ and }u_{\bm{x}}\in N}.

Here
p=p_{\bm{b}_{1},\cdots,\bm{b}_{d-1}}\in P
is the unique element in
P
satisfying that
p\bm{e}_{i}=\bm{b}_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq d-1
.
In terms of the coordinates in (
2.6
),
\nu
is given by

(2.7)

\mathrm{d}\nu(g)=\frac{1}{\zeta(2)\cdots\zeta(d)}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\prod_{1\leq i\leq d-1}\mathrm{d}\bm{b}_{i},

where
\zeta(\cdot)
is the Riemann zeta function,
and
\mathrm{d}\bm{x}
and
\mathrm{d}\bm{b}_{i}
denote Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
respectively.

For any
r\in(0,1)
, let

\displaystyle K_{r}:=\left\{\Lambda\in X_{d}:\Lambda\cap(r-1,1-r)^{d}=\{\bm{0}\}\right\}.

Note that
\Delta^{-1}[0,r]=K_{1-e^{-r}}
(or equivalently,
K_{r}=\Delta^{-1}[0,-\log(1-r)]
) and
1-e^{-r}=r+O(r^{2})\asymp r
; hence to show the lower bound in (
2.2
), it suffices to show that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mu_{d}(K_{r})\gg_{d}r^{\frac{(d-1)(d+2)}{2}}\log^{\frac{d(d-1)}{2}}\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{r}\Bigr),\qquad\textrm{as $r\to 0^{+}$}.

The following proposition, proved in
[
KSY22
]
, implies the above lower bound; see
[
KSY22
, Proposition 3.2]
and the computation after it,
[
KSY22
, pp. 796-797]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

There exists a small parameter
c_{0}\in(0,1)
depending only on
d
such that the following holds: For any
r\in(0,c_{0}/d)
, let
\underline{\mathcal{K}}_{r}\subset G
be defined such that
g=p_{\bm{b}_{1},\dots,\bm{b}_{d-1}}u_{\bm{x}}\in\underline{\mathcal{K}}_{r}
if and only if

\bm{b}_{j}=(b_{1j},\ldots,b_{dj})^{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
j=1,\ldots,d-1
)
and
\bm{x}\in(0,c_{0}/d)^{d-1}
satisfy the following conditions:

(2.9)

\displaystyle b_{ij},-b_{ji}\in(-c_{0},0),\ \forall\ 1\leq j<i\leq d-1;\ b_{d\ell}\in(-c_{0},0),b_{\ell\ell}\in(1-\tfrac{r}{2d},1),\ \forall\ 1\leq\ell\leq d-1,

(2.10)

\displaystyle b_{ij}<db_{i,j-1}\ (\Leftrightarrow|b_{ij}|>d|b_{i,j-1}|),\qquad\forall\ 2\leq j<i\leq d,

(2.11)

\displaystyle|b_{ij}b_{ji}|<\frac{r}{d!},\quad\forall 1\leq j<i\leq d-1,\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}|b_{dj}|x_{j}<\frac{r}{2},

and

(2.12)

\displaystyle b_{kj}>b_{ij}\ (\Leftrightarrow|b_{kj}|<|b_{ij}|),\qquad\forall 1\leq j<k<i\leq d.

Then
\pi|_{\underline{K}_{r}}:\underline{\mathcal{K}}_{r}\to X_{d}
is injective,
\underline{K}_{r}:=\pi(\underline{\mathcal{K}}_{r})\subset K_{r}
, and

\displaystyle\mu_{d}(\underline{K}_{r})\gg_{d}r^{\frac{(d-1)(d+2)}{2}}\log^{\frac{d(d-1)}{2}}\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{r}\Bigr),\qquad\textrm{as $r\to 0^{+}$}.

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
2.2

We now give the proof of Theorem
2.2
. As we have discussed above, it suffices to prove the lower bound in (
2.5
).

For any
0<r<(c_{0}/d)^{2}
, define
\underline{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{r}
to be the
subset of
\underline{\mathcal{K}}_{r}
consisting of all

g=p_{\bm{b}_{1},\dots,\bm{b}_{d-1}}u_{\bm{x}}\in\underline{\mathcal{K}}_{r}

which satisfy the extra condition

(2.13)

\displaystyle b_{i1}\in(-\sqrt{r},0),\,\qquad\forall\,2\leq i\leq d.

In other words, an element
g=p_{\bm{b}_{1},\dots,\bm{b}_{d-1}}u_{\bm{x}}
in
G

with
\bm{b}_{j}=(b_{1j},\ldots,b_{dj})^{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
j=1,\ldots,d-1
)
belongs to
\underline{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{r}
if and only if

\bm{x}\in(0,c_{0}/d)^{d-1}
and all the five conditions
(
2.9
), (
2.10
), (
2.11
), (
2.12
)
and (
2.13
) hold.
It may be noted that the new
condition (
2.13
)
is a sharpening of the restrictions

b_{i1}\in(-c_{0},0)
(
\forall 2\leq i\leq d
)
which are part of condition (
2.9
).

Set also

(2.14)

\displaystyle\underline{K}_{r}^{\prime}:=\pi(\underline{\mathcal{K}}_{r}^{\prime})\subset X_{d}.

Then by Proposition
2.3
we have

\underline{K}_{r}^{\prime}\subset\underline{K}_{r}\subset K_{r}.

Moreover, because of the condition (
2.13
) and
b_{11}\in(1-\frac{r}{2d},1)
(by (
2.9
)), for any
\Lambda=p_{\bm{b}_{1},\dots,\bm{b}_{d-1}}u_{\bm{x}}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\in\underline{K}_{r}^{\prime}
with
p_{\bm{b}_{1},\dots,\bm{b}_{d-1}}u_{\bm{x}}\in\underline{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{r}
, we have

\bm{b}_{1}=p_{\bm{b}_{1},\dots,\bm{b}_{d-1}}u_{\bm{x}}\bm{e}_{1}\in(\Lambda\setminus\{\bm{0}\})\cap\left((1-\tfrac{r}{2d},1)\times(-\sqrt{r},0)^{d-1}\right)\subset(\Lambda\setminus\{\bm{0}\})\cap R_{r}.

This implies that
\Lambda\in\mathcal{H}(R_{r})
.
We have thus proved that
\underline{K}_{r}^{\prime}\subset K_{r}\cap\mathcal{H}(R_{r})
for every

0<r<(c_{0}/d)^{2}
; hence
for every such
r
we also have, using
1-e^{-r}<r
:

(2.15)

\displaystyle\underline{K}_{1-e^{-r}}^{\prime}\subset K_{1-e^{-r}}\cap\mathcal{H}(R_{1-e^{-r}})=\Delta^{-1}[0,r]\cap\mathcal{H}(R_{1-e^{-r}})\subset\Delta^{-1}[0,r]\cap\mathcal{H}(R_{r})=\Delta^{\prime}_{r}.

In view of this relation, and the fact that
1-e^{-r}\asymp r
, in order to prove the lower bound in (
2.5
), it suffices to prove the following measure estimate on
\mu_{d}(\underline{K}_{r}^{\prime})
.

Proposition 2.4
.

We have

(2.16)

\displaystyle\mu_{d}(\underline{K}_{r}^{\prime})\gg_{d}r^{\frac{(d-1)(d+2)}{2}}\log^{\frac{d(d-1)}{2}}\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{r}\Bigr),\qquad\textrm{as $r\to 0^{+}$}.

Proof.

Fix
0<r<c^{2}_{0}/d^{2d-2}
. Since
\underline{\mathcal{K}}_{r}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{K}_{r}
and
\pi|_{\underline{\mathcal{K}}_{r}}
is injective (by Proposition
2.3
),
\pi|_{\underline{\mathcal{K}}_{r}^{\prime}}
is also injective. This implies that

\displaystyle\mu_{d}(\underline{K}_{r}^{\prime})=\nu(\underline{\mathcal{K}}_{r}^{\prime}).

It now remains to compute
\nu(\underline{\mathcal{K}}_{r}^{\prime})
. This computation is very similar to the computation of
\nu(\underline{\mathcal{K}}_{r})
done in
[
KSY22
, pp. 796-797]
; we follow closely the strategy there.

First, by (
2.7
) we have

\displaystyle\nu\left({\underline{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{r}}\right)

\displaystyle\asymp_{d}\prod_{1\leq k\leq d-1}\left(\int_{1-\frac{r}{2d}}^{1}\mathrm{d}b_{kk}\right)\int_{\mathcal{R}}\delta\bigl(\,(b_{ij})_{1\leq j<i\leq d}\,\bigr)\prod_{1\leq j<i\leq d}\mathrm{d}b_{ij},

where

\mathcal{R}:=\left\{(b_{ij})_{1\leq j<i\leq d}\in(-c_{0},0)^{d(d-1)/2}\>:\>\textrm{$(b_{ij})$ satisfies \eqref{equ:con2}, \eqref{equ:con4} and \eqref{EQU:addcond}}\right\},

and

\displaystyle\delta\bigl(\,(b_{ij})_{1\leq j<i\leq d}\,\bigr):

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq j<i\leq d-1}\int_{0}^{\min\left\{c_{0},\frac{r}{d!|b_{ij}|}\right\}}\mathrm{d}b_{ji}\times\int_{\left\{\bm{x}\in\left(0,\tfrac{c_{0}}{d}\right)^{d-1}\>:\>\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}|b_{dj}|x_{j}<\frac{r}{2}\right\}}\prod_{1\leq j\leq d-1}\mathrm{d}x_{j}

\displaystyle\asymp_{d,c_{0}}\prod_{1\leq j<i\leq d}\min\left\{1,\tfrac{r}{|b_{ij}|}\right\}.

Hence

(2.17)

\displaystyle\mu_{d}\left(\underline{K}^{\prime}_{r}\right)=\nu\left({\underline{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{r}}\right)\asymp_{d,c_{0}}r^{d-1}\int_{\mathcal{R}}\left(\prod_{1\leq j<i\leq d}\min\left\{1,\tfrac{r}{|b_{ij}|}\right\}\mathrm{d}b_{ij}\right).

Now for each
1\leq j<i\leq d
, we make a change of variable,
b_{ij}=-d^{j-1}z_{ij}
,
so that all the
z_{ij}
’s are positive, and the ordering conditions (
2.10
) and (
2.12
) become

(2.18)

\displaystyle z_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}<z_{ij}\quad\textrm{whenever
$1\leq j^{\prime}<i^{\prime}\leq d$, $\>1\leq j<i\leq d$,
$\>i^{\prime}\leq i$, $\>j^{\prime}\leq j$ and $(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\neq(i,j)$}.

Moreover, for any
2\leq j<i
, the condition
b_{ij}\in(-c_{0},0)
corresponds to
z_{ij}\in(0,c_{0}/d^{j-1})
,
and we note that each of these intervals contains the smaller interval

(0,c_{0}/d^{d-1})
. For each
2\leq i\leq d
, the condition
b_{i1}\in(-\sqrt{r},0)
becomes
z_{i1}\in(0,\sqrt{r})
which contains the smaller interval
(r,\sqrt{r})
. Note that since
r<c^{2}_{0}/d^{2d-2}
, we have
(r,\sqrt{r})\subset(0,c_{0}/d^{d-1})
.

The key modification of the computation in
[
KSY22
]
is now that we restrict each
z_{ij}
to the smaller interval
(r,\sqrt{r})
.
Then
r/|b_{ij}|=rd^{1-j}/z_{ij}<d^{1-j}\leq 1
,
so that

\min\left\{1,r/|b_{ij}|\right\}=rd^{1-j}/z_{ij}\asymp_{d}r/z_{ij},\quad\forall\,1\leq j<i\leq d.

It now follows from the same arguments as in
[
KSY22
, p. 797]
that

(2.19)

\displaystyle\mu_{d}\left(\underline{K}^{\prime}_{r}\right)

\displaystyle\gg_{d,c_{0}}r^{\frac{(d-1)(d+2)}{2}}\prod_{1\leq j<i\leq d}\left(\int_{r}^{\sqrt{r}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{ij}}{z_{ij}}\right)\asymp r^{\frac{(d-1)(d+2)}{2}}\log^{\frac{d(d-1)}{2}}\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{r}\Bigr).

This finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Theorem 3.1

First note that (

3.2.
Reduction to dynamics

In this section we briefly review the classical
Dani correspondence
observed by Dani
[
Dan85
]
and Kleinbock-Margulis
[
KM99
, Lemma 8.3]
, which allows us to reduce the problem to a shrinking target problem on the homogeneous space
X_{d}=G/\Gamma
. Before doing so, we first prove a simple reduction lemma which allows us to also assume a lower bound on
\psi
in Theorem
3
.

Lemma 3.3
.

When proving Theorem
3
,
there is no loss of generality to also assume

(3.9)

\displaystyle\psi(t)\geq\frac{1}{2t}\qquad\text{ for all $t\geq t_{0}$}.

Proof.

If the given function
\psi
satisfies
\psi(t)\geq\frac{1}{2t}
for all sufficiently large

,
then we obtain the desired reduction by simply increasing
t_{0}
.
It remains to consider the case when
\psi(t)<\frac{1}{2t}
holds for an unbounded set of
t
-values.
Then
F_{\psi}(t)>\frac{1}{2}

for an unbounded set of
t
-values,
and by the argument in Remark
1.11
, this implies that we are in the divergence case,
i.e. our task is to prove that

\mathrm{Leb}(\operatorname{\mathbf{DI}}_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(\psi))=0
.
Now set

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(t):=\max\left\{\psi(t),\tfrac{1}{2t}\right\}\qquad(t\geq t_{0}).

This is a continuous, decreasing function satisfying
\frac{1}{2t}\leq\psi(t)<\frac{1}{t}

and (
1.9
).
(To see that
\psi_{1}
satisfies (
1.9

Now by assumption there is

By the argument in Remark

Hence by Theorem

\displaystyle r(k)^{\alpha}\log^{\beta}\left(\tfrac{1}{r(k)}\right)\ll_{d,\alpha,\beta,C_{\psi}}F_{\psi}(e^{k-1})^{\alpha}\log^{\beta}\left(\tfrac{1}{F_{\psi}(e^{k-1})}\right)\ll_{\alpha,\beta,C_{\psi}}\sum_{e^{k-2}\leq j<e^{k-1}}\frac{1}{j}F_{\psi}(j)^{\alpha}\log^{\beta}\left(\tfrac{1}{F_{\psi}(j)}\right),

\displaystyle r(k)^{\alpha}\log^{\beta}\left(\tfrac{1}{r(k)}\right)\gg_{d,\alpha,\beta,C_{\psi}}F_{\psi}(e^{k})^{\alpha}\log^{\beta}\left(\tfrac{1}{F_{\psi}(e^{k})}\right)\gg_{\alpha,\beta,C_{\psi}}\sum_{e^{k}\leq j<e^{k+1}}\frac{1}{j}F_{\psi}(j)^{\alpha}\log^{\beta}\left(\tfrac{1}{F_{\psi}(j)}\right).

This shows the equivalence of the divergence of the two series in (
3.13
).

To show that

satisfies (
3.12
), for any

, let

. Then for any

such that

we have

\displaystyle r(s_{2})<\frac{2}{d}F_{\psi}(t_{2})\leq\frac{2C_{\psi}}{d}F_{\psi}(t_{1})<2C_{\psi}r(s_{1}).

Note that as

runs through
[t_{1},t_{1}e^{(\log t_{1})^{\eta}}]

runs through

is the number determined through

, that is,

Moreover, from the relation

, we can estimate
(\log t_{1})^{\eta}=(s_{1}-nr(s_{1}))^{\eta}>(s_{1}-n/d)^{\eta}>\frac{2}{3}s_{1}^{\eta}
provided that

Hence

.
This shows that

\displaystyle r(s_{2})<2C_{\psi}r(s_{1}),\qquad\forall\,1\ll s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq s_{1}+\frac{s_{1}^{\eta}}{2}.

Applying this inequality twice, we get that

satisfies (
3.12
) with

.

The moreover part is
[
KSY22
, Proposition 6.2]
. Note that although
[
KSY22
, Proposition 6.2]

is increasing (or equivalently,

is decreasing), this assumption was not used in the proof.
∎

Let

\displaystyle b_{k_{2}}\leq Cb_{k_{1}},\quad\forall\,1\ll k_{1}\leq k_{2}\leq k_{1}+k_{1}^{\alpha}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}=\infty.

Proof of Lemma
4.3
.

Let

The first claim of (

) and the fact that
\{k^{-\alpha}\}
is decreasing. For the second claim, fix an integer

\displaystyle\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}k_{l}^{\alpha}a_{k_{l}}\asymp\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}k_{l}^{\alpha}a_{k_{l+1}}\ll_{C}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k\in B_{l}}a_{k}\ll_{C}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}k_{l}^{\alpha}a_{k_{l}}.

We also have

by assumption; hence it follows that

\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}k_{l}^{\alpha}a_{k_{l}}=\infty

This in turn implies that

4.2.2.
The new ingredient: a disjointness fact

The main new ingredient for our proof to remove the technical condition (
1.8
) is the following simple disjointness statement concerning
g_{s}
-translates of
\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{r}
for certain ranges of integer
s
-values.

Proposition 4.4
.

For any
0<r<\min\{\frac{d\alpha_{\min}}{2d+1},\frac{\beta_{\min}}{2},e^{-4}\}
,
let
J=J_{r}:=\lfloor\frac{1}{4}\log(\frac{1}{r})\rfloor
; then for any
k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
the sets

\displaystyle g_{k_{0}}\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{r},\ g_{k_{0}+1}\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{r},\ \cdots,\ g_{k_{0}+J}\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{r}

are pairwise disjoint.

Proof.

For any
0\leq i<j\leq J
, since

g_{k_{0}+i}\widetilde{\Delta}_{r}^{\prime}\cap g_{k_{0}+j}\widetilde{\Delta}_{r}^{\prime}=g_{k_{0}+i}(\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{r}\cap g_{j-i}\widetilde{\Delta}_{r}^{\prime}),

it suffices to show

(4.15)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{r}\cap g_{k}\widetilde{\Delta}_{r}^{\prime}=\emptyset,\qquad\forall\,1\leq k\leq J.

In other words, taking any
1\leq k\leq J
and
\Lambda\in\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{r}
, we want to show
g_{-k}\Lambda\notin\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{r}
. Suppose not, i.e.
g_{-k}\Lambda\in\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{r}
. Then by definition, there exists some

s\in[0,1/2)
such that

g_{-(k-s)}\Lambda\in\Delta_{r}^{\prime}=\Delta^{-1}[0,r]\cap\mathcal{H}(R_{r})\subset\Delta^{-1}[0,r].

This means that

g_{-(k-s)}\Lambda\cap S_{r}=\{\bm{0}\}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\Lambda\cap g_{k-s}S_{r}=\{\bm{0}\},

where
S_{r}:=(-e^{-r},e^{-r})^{d}
.

On the other hand, note that
\Lambda\in\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{r}
means that there exists some
s^{\prime}\in[0,1/2)
such that
\Lambda^{\prime}:=g_{s^{\prime}}\Lambda\in\Delta_{r}^{\prime}
. This implies that
\Lambda^{\prime}
contains a point
\bm{w}\in R_{r}=(1-\frac{r}{2d},1+\frac{r}{2d})\times(-\sqrt{r},\sqrt{r})^{d-1}
.
Since
\Lambda\cap g_{k-s}S_{r}=\{\bm{0}\}
, we have
\Lambda^{\prime}\cap g_{k+s^{\prime}-s}S_{r}=\{\bm{0}\}
. This means that

\displaystyle\forall\,\bm{v}\in\Lambda^{\prime}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}\ :\ |v_{i}|<e^{\alpha_{i}(k+s^{\prime}-s)-r},\ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq m\ \Rightarrow\ \exists\,1\leq j\leq n\ \text{s.t.}\ |v_{j+m}|\geq e^{-\beta_{j}(k+s^{\prime}-s)-r}.

Since
r<\frac{d\alpha_{\min}}{2d+1}
, we have

e^{\alpha_{i}(k+s^{\prime}-s)-r}>e^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\min}-r}>1+\frac{r}{2d}>|w_{i}|,\qquad\forall\,1\leq i\leq m.

Thus
there must exist some
1\leq j\leq n
such that
|w_{j+m}|\geq e^{-\beta_{j}(k+s^{\prime}-s)-r}
. But since
w_{j+m}\in(-\sqrt{r},\sqrt{r})
, we have for such
w_{j+m}
,

\displaystyle\sqrt{r}>|w_{j+m}|\geq e^{-\beta_{j}(k+s^{\prime}-s)-r}\quad\Rightarrow\quad k>\frac{1}{2\beta_{j}}\log\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{r}\Bigr)-\frac{r}{\beta_{j}}+s-s^{\prime}>\frac{1}{2\beta_{j}}\log\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{r}\Bigr)-1.

(For the last inequality we used the assumption that
r<\beta_{\min}/2
.)
The above lower bound on
k
contradicts our assumption that
k\leq J=\lfloor\frac{1}{4}\log(\frac{1}{r})\rfloor
, since
r<e^{-4}
.
This finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

(4.36)

\displaystyle Q_{k_{1},k_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{1}\leq i,j\leq k_{2}}\int_{\mathcal{Y}}(h_{i}(\Lambda_{A})h_{j}(\Lambda_{A})-b_{i}b_{j})\,\mathrm{d}A\leq\sum_{i=k_{1}}^{k_{2}}b_{i}+2\sum_{k_{1}\leq i<j\leq k_{2}}b_{i,j}.

Next we treat the double sum over
b_{i,j}
by
using the
three bounds
(
4.28
),
(
4.31
)
and (
4.32
)
(and recalling that
k_{1}\geq K
).
This gives:

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}\leq i<j\leq k_{2}}b_{i,j}\leq O\biggl(\sum_{k_{1}<j\leq k_{2}}b_{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}\leq i<j\\
(D_{i,j}\geq{\Theta}\log j)\end{subarray}}e^{-\frac{\delta}{2}D_{i,j}}\biggr)+O\biggl(\sum_{k_{1}<j\leq k_{2}}b_{j}\sum_{k_{1}\leq i<{\Theta}\log j}e^{-\frac{\delta}{2}i}\biggr)

(4.37)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k_{1}<j\leq k_{2}}\hskip 1.0pt\sum_{a_{j}\leq i<j}\Bigl(\mu_{d}(\Phi_{i,j})+O\bigl(e^{-\frac{\delta}{2}j}\,b_{j}\bigr)\Bigr),

where
a_{j}:=\max\{k_{1},j-{\Theta}\log j\}
.
Here the three sums

\sum_{k_{1}\leq i<j}e^{-\frac{\delta}{2}D_{i,j}}
,

\sum_{k_{1}\leq i<{\Theta}\log j}e^{-\frac{\delta}{2}i}

and
\sum_{a_{j}\leq i<j}e^{-\frac{\delta}{2}j}

are all

\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\delta}{2}k}\ll 1
.
Hence we conclude:

(4.38)

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}\leq i<j\leq k_{2}}b_{i,j}\leq O\biggl(\sum_{k_{1}<j\leq k_{2}}b_{j}\biggr)+\sum_{k_{1}<j\leq k_{2}}\hskip 1.0pt\sum_{a_{j}\leq i<j}\mu_{d}(\Phi_{i,j}).

It remains to bound the double sum in (
4.38
).
For each integer
j
in the interval
k_{1}<j\leq k_{2}
,
let us choose an index
i_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[a_{j},j)
so that

(4.39)

\displaystyle\rho_{i_{j}}=\max\{\rho_{i}\>:\>a_{j}\leq i<j\}.

Recall that for any
k_{1}\leq i<j
we have

\Phi_{i,j}(x)=\phi_{\rho_{i}}(x)\phi_{\rho_{j}}(g_{j-i}x)
and
0\leq\phi_{\rho_{i}}\leq\chi_{\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{i}}}
.
Hence for each
k_{1}<j\leq k_{2}
,

(4.40)

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{j}\leq i<j}\mu_{d}(\Phi_{i,j})\leq\sum_{a_{j}\leq i<j}\mu_{d}\bigl(g_{j-i}\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{i}}\cap\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{j}}\bigr)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{j-a_{j}}\mu_{d}\bigl(g_{k}\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{i_{j}}}\cap\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{j}}\bigr),

where the last inequality holds since

\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{i}}\subset\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{i_{j}}}
for all
i
in the interval
a_{j}\leq i<j
.

For each
k_{1}<j\leq k_{2}
, let us now also set

\displaystyle J_{j}:=\Bigl\lfloor\tfrac{1}{4}\log\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2\rho_{i_{j}}}\Bigr)\Bigr\rfloor\>\in\mathbb{N}.

Then by Proposition
4.4

(which applies since
i_{j}\geq k_{1}\geq K
so that
\rho_{i_{j}}

satisfies the bound in (
4.17
)),
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the sets

\displaystyle g_{k}\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{i_{j}}},\ g_{k+1}\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{i_{j}}},\ \cdots,\ g_{k+J_{j}}\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{i_{j}}}

are pairwise disjoint.
This implies that the sets

\displaystyle g_{k}\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{i_{j}}}\cap\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{j}},\ g_{k+1}\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{i_{j}}}\cap\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{j}},\ \cdots,\ g_{k+J_{j}}\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{i_{j}}}\cap\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{j}}

are also pairwise disjoint.
Since these sets are all contained in
\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{j}}
,
the sum of their
\mu_{d}
-measure is bounded from above by
\mu_{d}(\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{j}})
.
Hence for each
k_{1}<j\leq k_{2}
:

(4.41)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{j-a_{j}}\mu_{d}\bigl(g_{k}\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{i_{j}}}\cap\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{j}}\bigr)\leq\Bigl(\tfrac{j-a_{j}}{J_{j}+1}+1\Bigr)\mu_{d}(\tilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{j}})\ll\Bigl(\tfrac{\log j}{J_{j}}+1\Bigr)b_{j},

where for the last bound we used

j-a_{j}\leq{\Theta}\log j
and (
4.20
), (
4.24
).
But now note that
i_{j}\geq a_{j}\geq j-{\Theta}\log j>\frac{3}{4}j
;
hence by (
4.6
) and (
4.16
),

\rho_{i_{j}}\ll j^{-\gamma_{d}^{\prime}}
.
This implies that
J_{j}\geq\frac{1}{4}\gamma_{d}^{\prime}\log(j)-O(1)
,
and hence after possibly increasing the constant
K
further, we have
J_{j}\gg\log j
for all
j\geq K
.
Using this fact in (
4.41
), we obtain:

(4.42)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{j-a_{j}}\mu_{d}\bigl(g_{k}\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{i_{j}}}\cap\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}_{2\rho_{j}}\bigr)\ll b_{j},\qquad\forall j\in(k_{1},k_{2}]\cap\mathbb{Z}.

Combining this bound with
(
4.40
), (
4.38
)
and (
4.36
),
we conclude that
Q_{k_{1},k_{2}}\leq O\bigl(\sum_{j=k_{1}}^{k_{2}}b_{j}\bigr)
.
Since
Q_{k_{1},k_{2}}
is non-negative by definition,
this means that we have proved the bound (
4.35
).
This completes the proof of Proposition
4.1
.

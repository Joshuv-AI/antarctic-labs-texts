A high doppler tolerance and low side-lobe NLFM waveform optimization method for SAR application
Authors: Kai Yao, Chang Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The Non-linear Frequency Modulation (NLFM) waveform is known for its ability to reduce side-lobe levels without compromising the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) or requiring windowing operations in Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) imaging missions. However, as SAR technology continues to evolve and operational scenarios grow increasingly complex, there is a rising demand for radar waveforms with improved overall performance. One significant limitation of the NLFM waveform is its restricted Doppler tolerance, which limits its potential application in diverse SAR scenarios. To address this issue, this letter proposes a multi-objective optimization approach based on the NLFM waveform model using Fourier series. The optimization is conducted using the NSGA-II algorithm, grounded in Pareto optimal theory, to fine-tune waveform parameters and resolve conflicts between design criteria. As a result, the optimized NLFM waveform not only maintains low side-lobe levels but also improves Doppler performance, making it more suitable for advanced SAR imaging tasks. Finally, simulation results are presented to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed NLFM waveform.

Introduction

Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) is a high-resolution radar system widely used for target imaging and detection. SAR typically employs a Linear Frequency Modulated (LFM) waveform as its transmitting signal. The linear time-frequency relationship and high tolerance to Doppler effects in LFM waveform makes it efficient to generate and process for application in Radar Pulse Compression technology. However, its autocorrelation function forms a sine pattern, resulting in a high Peak Side-lobe Ratio (PSLR) of −13.2 dB in the matched filter response. This elevated PSLR can obscure weaker targets in SAR images by producing strong side-lobes from more prominent targets. To address this, windowing techniques are often applied to reduce side-lobes. Unfortunately, these window operations can lead to a signal mismatch during the matched filtering process, which degrades the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) and broadens the main lobe, negatively impacting SAR imaging performance.

Pulse compression technology is one of the core techniques in modern radar systems, enabling high range resolution while reducing the peak power requirement of transmitted signals. However, conventional matched filters generate high range side-lobes when processing strong target echoes, which can mask weak targets and severely degrade radar detection performance1,2. To address this issue, researchers have proposed various side-lobe suppression methods, primarily including adaptive filtering optimization, frequency-domain signal processing techniques, and non-linear frequency modulation (NLFM) waveform design.

In adaptive filtering, Blunt & Gerlach (2006)3 proposed a minimum mean square error (MMSE)-based adaptive pulse compression method, which dynamically adjusts the filter coefficients for each range cell, significantly suppressing side-lobe interference and improving the detection capability of weak targets. Similarly, Lohmeier (2002)4 employed adaptive finite impulse response (FIR) filtering to optimize side-lobe suppression performance in airborne/spaceborne precipitation mapping radars while maintaining high resolution. Additionally, Ackroyd & Ghani (1973)5 earlier explored optimal mismatched filter design, demonstrating its trade-off between side-lobe suppression and signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) loss in a 13-bit Barker code system. In frequency-domain signal processing, Sarkar (1997)6 introduced an ultra-low side-lobe pulse compression technique based on frequency-domain deconvolution and linear prediction, achieving approximately 20 dB better side-lobe suppression compared to conventional matched filters. Further studies by Doerry (2006)7 demonstrated that precise frequency-domain processing could yield even better side-lobe suppression performance.

Directly designing functional waveforms with low sidelobes can effectively reduce energy loss caused by mismatched filtering and enhance weak target detection capability8,9. Among such waveforms, Non-Linear Frequency Modulated (NLFM) signals offer high design flexibility, enabling precise control over the waveform’s spectral and temporal characteristics. This feature offers flexible time-frequency relationships and allows for the shaping of the Power Spectral Density (PSD) function. Thus, NLFM waveforms reduce autocorrelation side-lobes without the need for windowing, thereby avoiding SNR loss and conserving system resources10,11. This makes them a promising alternative to window operations in SAR applications12,13. Nevertheless, NLFM waveforms suffer from a limitation in Doppler tolerance, a challenge that persists despite extensive research into their Doppler performance14–17.

In waveform optimization, non-linear frequency modulation (NLFM) has attracted widespread attention due to its inherent low-side-lobe characteristics. De Witte & Griffiths (2004)18 proposed a continuous NLFM waveform, achieving side-lobe suppression better than − 70 dB. Subsequent studies by Millett (1970)19 and Doerry (2006)7 further refined the design and implementation of NLFM waveforms, reducing side-lobe levels through precise phase modulation while avoiding the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) loss associated with conventional windowing methods. Collins & Atkins (1999)20 demonstrated that NLFM waveforms also hold potential in active sonar systems. Later, Zhiqiang et al. (2008) optimized the matched NLFM approach by employing a Gaussian-sinc hybrid spectrum design, achieving side-lobe levels below − 60 dB while maintaining optimal SNR performance21. Recent efforts have focused on optimizing the frequency law via piecewise exponential or odd-term polynomial methods to achieve lower sidelobes22. Smoothing spline and other numerical fitting techniques have also been employed to improve sidelobe suppression and regularity23.

As SAR technology advances and the demand for more complex tasks (such as moving target detection and squinted imaging of distant targets) grows, there are increasing performance requirements for NLFM waveforms. Recent studies emphasize not only waveform sidelobe levels but also practical implementation issues and corresponding processing techniques24. Beyond side-lobe reduction, maintaining a high Doppler tolerance is also crucial for the broader adoption of NLFM waveforms in SAR systems. Therefore, designing NLFM waveforms that meet the operational demands of SAR systems remains a key research challenge, with a focus on enhancing both side-lobe reduction and Doppler tolerance.

The conventional approach to designing Non-Linear Frequency Modulation (NLFM) waveforms is based on the Principle of Stationary Phase (POSP)16,25, which employ predefined window functions to shape the PSD of the waveform. However, this method often suffers from limited design flexibility and sub-optimal performance. To overcome these limitations, some researchers have turned to mathematical optimization techniques, particularly evolutionary algorithms, to design NLFM waveforms. In this context, quadratic or geometric curves are often used to define the time-frequency relationship15,17,26. Wang et al. further advanced this approach by introducing an asymmetric instantaneous frequency function27,28, which extends the traditional piecewise linear (PWL) function, and applies a genetic algorithm (GA) to optimize the waveform. This signal model offers greater flexibility for the optimization process, making it well-suited for a broader range of SAR applications, including range ambiguity suppression29. However, as the time-bandwidth product (TBP) increases, the number of parameters required for optimization also increases in order to maintain accuracy and preserve the desired shape of the time-frequency curve. Different signal models have distinct characteristics, and when using Fourier series to define NLFM waveform parameters, each coefficient plays a role in shaping the entire time-frequency curve30–32. This method allows for more efficient and robust optimization, yielding superior results compared to traditional approaches.

To enhance the overall performance of the NLFM waveform, this paper introduces an improved multi-objective optimization strategy based on Pareto optimality, utilizing the Fourier series signal model. The constrained NSGA-II algorithm is modified to increase convergence speed and improve population diversity, aligning with the specific demands of SAR imaging applications. By comparing various waveform performance metrics and conducting SAR imaging simulations at different squint angles, the study confirms that the proposed method significantly improves Doppler tolerance and the overall effectiveness of the NLFM waveform.

Optimization framework

Radar waveform performance metrics primarily include Peak Side-lobe Ratio (PSLR), Integrated Side-lobe Ratio (ISLR), Integrated Resolution Width (IRW), Doppler tolerance, etc. which are interrelated and can significantly impact one another. Typically, a reduction in side-lobe levels leads to broadening of the main lobe and a decrease in the Doppler performance. Because the optimization objectives often conflict with each other, it is not feasible to achieve optimal values for all objectives simultaneously. Instead, the result is a solution set that reflects the presence of correlation among the various objective values, defined as a Pareto optimal solution30. This is illustrated in Eq. (11):11\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{min}\{{f}_{1}\left(\text{x}\right),{f}_{2}\left(\text{x}\right),\dots\:{f}_{k}\left(\text{x}\right)\},\:\:s.t.\:\text{x}\in\:X$$\end{document}

Here, fi​(x) represents the i-th objective function (e.g., PSLR, ISLR), and x is an N-dimensional real vector defining the waveform parameters.

Evolutionary algorithms are widely used to tackle complex non-linear optimization problems, including the quest for Pareto optimal solutions. Among these, NSGA-II (Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II) stands out as a classical genetic algorithm and serves as the benchmark for evaluating multi-objective optimization algorithms. One of the key features of NSGA-II is its selection process of sorting individuals according to their non-dominance rank and crowding distance values as opposed to the traditional genetic algorithms that select individuals solely based on their fitness values.

This study employs a modified constrained NSGA-II algorithm for multi-objective optimization of NLFM waveforms. SAR waveform design demands precise time-frequency control to meet imaging-specific requirements beyond detection, motivating the NLFM-based optimization approach proposed in this work. To comprehensively address the required parameters of the NLFM waveform and achieve optimal results, we selected Peak Side-lobe Level (PSL), Integrated Side-lobe Level (ISL), and Doppler shift as optimization indices. To address the resolution constraint (IRW ≤ n) in radar waveform design, the algorithm incorporates a penalty function mechanism that transforms the constraint condition into part of the objective function, thereby ensuring the satisfaction of resolution constraints during optimization. For solution selection, a three-step screening is applied: (1) feasible solutions always dominate infeasible ones; (2) standard Pareto dominance applies when comparing two feasible solutions; (3) for two infeasible solutions, the one with lesser constraint violation is preferred. Crowding distance-based sorting maintains solution diversity and prevents premature convergence. This combined approach efficiently yields practical engineering solutions.

As illustrated in Fig. 1, the optimization begins by initializing NLFM parameters with randomly generated coefficients following an S-shaped distribution. Using NSGA-II, the waveform is optimized under a multi-objective function that jointly considers PSL, ISL, Doppler tolerance, and resolution penalty. The population evolves through selection, crossover, and mutation, guided by non-dominated sorting and crowding distance, until convergence is achieved. The final Pareto-optimal set of NLFM parameters is then output.

Unlike methods that utilize zero initial values or conventional NLFM waveforms generated based on the POSP as initial values, the algorithm proposed in this letter employs a random initial value scheme16,25,32. This approach ensures that the population distribution maintains a certain level of diversity at the beginning of the iteration, effectively preventing premature convergence to local optima during the population iteration.

Building on the description of the Fourier series model from previous reports, the distribution of its coefficient values follows a gradual decline pattern, which ensures that the time-frequency curve retains an S-shape30,32. Consequently, the algorithm modifies and sorts the parameters after each update, calculates and evaluates the objective functions, and expedites the formation of the fundamental structure of the required NLFM waveform. In this context, the iterative optimization process prioritizes the search of parameter distribution to enhance performance, minimizing the expenditure of time and resources in the order of population distribution.

Simulation and results

NLFM waveform optimization results

This section utilizes the constrained NSGA-II algorithm, previously proposed for the multi-objective optimization of the Fourier series waveform model. Here, the Peak Side-lobe Level (PSL) and the Integrated Side-lobe Level (ISL) are critical indices for optimizing the Non-Linear Frequency Modulated (NLFM) waveform. Furthermore, because waveform matching filtering during Doppler migration increases the PSL, this experiment incorporates matching filtering results based on the Doppler frequency shift to calculate the PSL, integrating it into the optimization process.

In SAR operational conditions, the selected maximum frequency offset of 10 kHz covers typical Doppler shifts encountered in airborne scenarios, while the 2 kHz step size provides sufficient granularity to characterize waveform performance degradation trends. The signal simulation employs an NLFM pulse with 2.5 µs duration and 50 MHz bandwidth (Fig. 2a), subjected to Doppler effects modeled at 0–10 kHz offsets. The experiment utilizes 15 optimization parameters (versus 128 in PWL model and 60 in reference model), with genetic algorithm settings of population size = 70, crossover factor = 1, and variation factor = 0.2. The resolution requirement below 1.2 serves as the constraint index during concurrent optimization of PSL, ISL, and Doppler tolerance metrics.

Imaging with squint angle and doppler effect analysis

The relative azimuth motion of SAR induces a Doppler shift effect on the Non-Linear Frequency Modulated (NLFM) waveform. As the squint angle increases, the Doppler offset progressively rises, resulting in deteriorated imaging outcomes, including distortion and imbalance16. Therefore, improved Doppler performance is essential for the waveform in this context. The NLFM waveform, derived from the previously mentioned algorithm, is utilized for strabismic imaging in a single-point target simulation. In these imaging results, the side-lobe level of the point target increases, progressively rising with the squint angle (Fig. 5). However, the imaging results for the target remain clear and focused, with a balanced and aligned side-lobe level that meets the performance requirements for SAR. No instances of deviation distortion or side-lobe imbalance are observed.

Extended target imaging

To further validate the superiority of the proposed NLFM waveform, we conducted extended imaging simulation experiments. The experiment simulates a squint-free airborne SAR scenario with a platform velocity of 150 m/s at an altitude of 10 km. An NLFM signal with a pulse width of 2.5 µs and bandwidth of 50 MHz is used for extended target imaging, and the echoes are processed via the Range-Doppler (RD) algorithm.

The imaging results obtained using the LFM waveform are illustrated in Figs. 6(a) and 6(c), while the results derived from the proposed NLFM waveform are presented in Figs. 6(b) and 6 (d). As can be observed, the LFM-based images exhibit noticeable bright noise and higher sidelobe levels, especially around strong scatterers, which degrade image clarity and may lead to false target indications. In contrast, the NLFM results show a cleaner background and better-resolved target structures, indicating improved noise and clutter suppression. The azimuth-averaged energy profile in Fig. 7 further highlights this advantage. It shows that the NLFM waveform preserves full signal energy across the scene while effectively suppressing scattering spikes caused by strong noise interference. This performance is attributed to the optimized spectral characteristics of NLFM, which reduce autocorrelation sidelobes and yield cleaner imaging results.

The outcomes achieved with the NLFM waveform demonstrate enhanced imaging capabilities and effectively suppress bright noise in the results, which is consistent with theoretical expectations. The improved image quality suggests that the NLFM scheme is particularly advantageous for imaging extended or distributed targets in noisy environments, where conventional LFM waveforms may suffer from degraded resolution and increased false alarm rates. These results validate the effectiveness of the proposed waveform design and highlight its potential for practical application in high-fidelity SAR systems.

Conclusion

The design of a NLFM waveform with excellent comprehensive performance significantly enhances SAR imaging. In this letter, we propose a novel NLFM optimization method based on multi-objective optimization and Fourier series. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed method effectively improves the Doppler performance of the NLFM waveform while meeting the requirements for low side-lobe levels. Additionally, it satisfies the criteria for distortion-free imaging under various squint angles and extended imaging tasks. The proposed NLFM waveform shows considerable potential for future developments in SAR applications. Notably, SAR waveform design is not a straightforward extension of conventional radar waveforms, as imaging performance requires balancing multiple coupled metrics. The proposed NLFM scheme addresses this challenge and demonstrates strong adaptability in complex scenarios.

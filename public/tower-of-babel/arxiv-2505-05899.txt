Introduction

The main focus of the papers
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
by Umberto Mosco is the study of the convergence properties of solutions to variational inequalities involving strongly monotone operators. In particular, the author investigates under which conditions on the convex sets the solutions converge, thereby introducing a notion of convergence for sequences of convex sets in reflexive Banach spaces.
Since then, this condition is known as Mosco-convergence and several papers are devoted to this subject.

In a recent paper (see
[
7
]
) the stability result by Mosco has been extended to pseudomonotone operators (which, in general, are not monotone, see
[
6
]
) of differential type defined in Sobolev spaces.

On the other hand, monotone operators and convex functionals are strongly related: after the first results by Umberto Mosco on convex functionals, a paper dedicated to him (cf.
[
3
]
) studies the convergence in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
of constrained minima on convex sets

for integral functionals of the form

I(v)=\int_{\Omega}i(x,Dv)-\int_{\Omega}f(x)\,v(x),

where

is a convex function in

(see Definition (

where

is strictly convex in

Furthermore, we recall that, in general,

In particular, the lower order term

This motivates the study of variational inequalities in

Building on these results, our aim is to extend the result of
[

2.
Main result and outline

In this section, we present the main result of this work. Before proceeding, let us recall the definition of Mosco-convergence.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a sequence
\{\mathcal{C}_{n}\}_{n}
, of closed convex subsets of a Banach space
X
, Mosco-converges to a closed convex set

\mathcal{C}_{0}\subseteq X
if:

1

For every subsequence
\{v_{n_{i}}\}_{i}
, with
v_{n_{i}}\in\mathcal{C}_{n_{i}}
, weakly convergent to
v_{0}
, we have
v_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}
.

2

For every
v_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}
, there exist
v_{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
such that

\|v_{n}-v_{0}\|_{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle$}}\to 0
.

We will write
\mathcal{C}_{n}\overset{M}{\to}\mathcal{C}_{0}
.

In particular, we focus on a specific class of convex sets, known as obstacle-type convex sets.
Given
\psi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we define the associated obstacle-type convex set as

\mathcal{C}(\psi)=\left\{v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega):v\geq\psi\text{ a.e. in }\Omega\right\}.

We are interested in the stability properties of the solutions to the inequality

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u\in\mathcal{C}(\psi)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega),\\
&\langle A(u),v-u\rangle+\int_{\Omega}a_{0}(x,u)(v-u)+\int_{\Omega}H(x,u,Du)(v-u)\,\geq 0,\\
&\forall\;v\in\mathcal{C}(\psi)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega),\end{aligned}\right.

under Mosco-convergence of the obstacle-type convex sets.

Here, we state the assumptions on
\Omega
,
p
,
A
,
a_{0}
,
H
and
\psi
.

•

\Omega
is an open, bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

•

p\in(1,+\infty)
and
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
.

•

The principal part
A
is a differential operator of second order in divergence form acting from
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
into
W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
:

A(v)=-\operatorname{div}(a(x,v,Dv)),

where
a
is a Carathéodory function with values in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfying the following conditions for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and for almost every
x\in\Omega
:

(2.2)

\displaystyle a(x,s,\xi)\xi\geq\alpha|\xi|^{p},

(2.3)

\displaystyle|a(x,s,\xi)|\leq\beta\left(h(x)+|s|^{p-1}+|\xi|^{p-1}\right),

(2.4)

\displaystyle[a(x,s,\xi)-a(x,s,\eta)](\xi-\eta)>0,

where
\alpha,\beta
are positive constants and

.

•

a_{0}
is a Carathéodory function with values in
\mathbb{R}
such that for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and for almost every
x\in\Omega
:

(2.5)

|a_{0}(x,s)|\leq\beta\left[h(x)+|s|^{p-1}\right],

(2.6)

a_{0}(x,s)s\geq\alpha_{0}|s|^{p},\quad\text{with }\alpha_{0}>0.

•

H(x,s,\xi)
is a Carathéodory function such that for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and for almost every

:

(2.7)

|H(x,s,\xi)|\leq f(x)+b(|s|)|\xi|^{p},

where

is a positive function and

is a positive, increasing, continuous function defined on

.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that, under assumptions (
2.2
), (
2.2
), (

),

is a bounded, continuous pseudomonotone operator of Leray-Lions type.

The existence of a solution

]

More precisely, the following theorem holds.

Theorem 2.3
.

There exists a solution

to

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u\in\mathcal{C}(\psi)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega),\\
&\langle A(u),v-u\rangle+\int_{\Omega}a_{0}(x,u)(v-u)+\int_{\Omega}H(x,u,Du)(v-u)\,\geq 0,\\
&\forall\;v\in\mathcal{C}(\psi)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega).\end{aligned}\right.

We now state the main problem: let

and

Theorem 2.4

\mathcal{C}(\psi_{n})=\{v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\,:\,v\geq\psi_{n}\,\text{ a.e. in }\Omega\},

\mathcal{C}(\psi_{0})=\{v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\,:\,v\geq\psi_{0}\,\text{ a.e. in }\Omega\},

and assume that

For every

Then there exists a subsequence

and

such that

3.
Mosco-convergence

In this section we recall some basic results regarding Mosco-convergence (see
[
16
]
).
We start with a simple example of the relation between the convergence of convex sets and the convergence of solutions to variational inequalities.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space and
\{C_{n}\}_{n}
,
C_{0}
be closed convex subsets of
H
. For any
f\in H
, let
u_{n}(f)
(resp
u_{0}(f)
) be the projection of
f
onto the set
C_{n}
(resp
C_{0}
), which are characterized by the inequalities:

(3.1)

u_{n}\in C_{n}:\,(u_{n}-f\mid v-u_{n})\geq 0,\quad\forall\;v\in C_{n};

(3.2)

u_{0}\in C_{0}:\,(u_{0}-f\mid v-u_{0})\geq 0,\quad\forall\;v\in C_{0}.

Proposition 3.1
.

The following are equivalent:

•

C_{n}\overset{M}{\to}C_{0}
,

•

\|u_{n}(f)-u_{0}(f)\|_{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle$}}\to 0
,
for all
f\in H
.

Proof.

We prove that
\overset{M}{\to}
implies
\|u_{n}-u_{0}\|_{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle$}}\to 0
.

•

Fix
w_{0}\in C_{0}
; there exists
w_{n}\in C_{n}
such that
\|w_{n}-w_{0}\|_{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle$}}\to 0
; the choice
v=w_{n}
in (
3.1
) yields the boundedness (in
H
) of the sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
.

•

As a consequence, there exist
u^{*}\in H
and a subsequence
\{u_{n_{i}}\}_{i}
weakly convergent to
u^{*}
; the definition of Mosco-convergence implies that
u^{*}\in C_{0}
.

•

Fix
w_{0}\in C_{0}
; there exists
w_{n}\in C_{n}
such that
\|w_{n}-w_{0}\|_{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle$}}\to 0
; the choice
v=w_{n}
in (
3.1
) yields

(u_{n_{i}}-f\mid w_{n_{i}}-u_{n_{i}})\geq 0.

We pass to the limit (use the weak l.s.c. of the norm) and we deduce that

u^{*}\in C_{0}:\,(u^{*}-f\mid w_{0}-u^{*})\geq 0,\;\forall\;w_{0}\in C_{0};

that is
u^{*}
is a solution on
C_{0}
: the uniqueness of the solution says that
u^{*}=u_{0}

and the whole sequence weakly converges to
u_{0}
.

•

There exists
z_{n}\in C_{n}
such that
\|z_{n}-u_{0}\|_{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle$}}\to 0
; the choice
v=z_{n}
in (
3.1
) yields

0\leq(u_{n}-u_{0}+u_{0}-f\mid z_{n}-u_{0}-u_{n}+u_{0})

and

(u_{n}-u_{0}\mid u_{n}-u_{0})\leq(u_{n}-u_{0}\mid z_{n}-u_{0})+(u_{0}-f\mid z_{n}-u_{n}),

which implies that
\|u_{n}-u_{0}\|_{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle$}}\to 0

Conversely, we prove that that if
\|u_{n}(f)-u_{0}(f)\|_{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle$}}\to 0
,
\forall\;f\in H
, then
C_{n}\overset{M}{\to}C_{0}
.

•

Let
\{w_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence weakly convergent to
w_{0}
, with
w_{n}\in C_{n}
.
Consider the problems (
3.1
) with
f=w_{0}
.

u_{n}\in C_{n}:\,(u_{n}-w_{0}\mid v-u_{n})\geq 0,\quad\forall\;v\in C_{n}.

Take
v=w_{n}
, then

u_{n}\in C_{n}:\,(u_{n}-w_{0}\mid w_{n}-u_{n})\geq 0.

The assumptions imply that it is possible to pass to the limit and

(u_{0}-w_{0}\mid w_{0}-u_{0})\geq 0,

that is
w_{0}=u_{0}
; so that
w_{0}\in C_{0}
.

•

Let
w_{0}\in C_{0}
. Define
f=w_{0}
, then
u_{0}=w_{0}
.
Moreover
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
is the required sequence.

∎

In the specific case of obstacle-type convex sets, we can associate Mosco-convergence with the behavior of the obstacle functions. The following proposition provides sufficient conditions for the Mosco-convergence of these obstacle-type convex sets, which depend on the properties of the associated obstacles (see
[
3
]
, Proposition 3).

Proposition 3.2

(Obstacle-Type Convex Sets)
.

Let
\{\psi_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence of measurable functions such that the obstacle sets

\mathcal{C}(\psi_{n})=\{v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega):v\geq\psi_{n}\text{ a.e. in }\Omega\}

are nonempty. Then,
\mathcal{C}(\psi_{n})
Mosco-converges to
\mathcal{C}(\psi_{0})
if any of the following conditions hold:

(1)

\psi_{n}
weakly converges in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
to
\psi_{0}
, with
\psi_{n}\leq\psi_{0}
;

(2)

\psi_{n}
strongly converges in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
to
\psi_{0}
(see Lemma 1.7 of
[
16
]
);

(3)

\psi_{n}
weakly converges in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
to
\psi_{0}
, with
\psi_{n}\geq\psi_{0}
, and
A(\psi_{n})\leq T
, where
A(v)=-\operatorname{div}(a(x,Dv))
is a Leray-Lions operator and

belongs to the dual of
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
;

(4)

\psi_{n}
strongly converges in

to
\psi_{0}
, and there exists
\Psi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that
\Psi\geq\psi_{n}
for all

(see
[
4
]
);

(5)

\psi_{n}
weakly converges in

to

, with

(see
[

]
,
[

]
).

Furthermore, the paper
[

establishes some necessary and sufficient conditions for the Mosco-convergence of sequences of obstacle-type convex sets.
These conditions are expressed in terms of the properties of the

4.
Variational inequalities with a natural growth term

In this section we follow the proof of Theorem
2.3
presented in
[
5
]
to gain quantitative estimates on the solutions

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u\in\mathcal{C}(\psi)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega),\\
&\langle A(u),v-u\rangle+\int_{\Omega}a_{0}(x,u)(v-u)+\int_{\Omega}H(x,u,Du)(v-u)\,\geq 0,\\
&\forall\;v\in\mathcal{C}(\psi)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega),\end{aligned}\right.

where

(4.2)

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(\psi)=\left\{v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega):v\geq\psi\text{ a.e. in }\Omega\right\}.

First, note that the solution is not unique in general. So, when we talk about
the
solution, we mean
one

It is also important to emphasize that the existence of a solution to this problem strongly relies on the obstacle-type structure of these convex sets, and thus this assumption cannot be relaxed.

In the estimates below, for the solution

of (

\psi

The strategy to obtain estimates for the solution

is known from classical theorems by Brezis (cf.

8
]

in both

and

\{u_{j}\}_{j}
converges to

, the solution to (

), which inherits the same bounds.

Consider then the following approximating problems:

(4.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{j}\in\mathcal{C}(\psi)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega),\\
&\begin{aligned} \langle A(u_{j}),v-u_{j}\rangle+\int_{\Omega}&a_{0}(x,u_{j})(v-u_{j})\\
&+\int_{\Omega}H_{j}(x,u_{j},Du_{j})(v-u_{j})\,\geq 0,\end{aligned}\\
&\forall\;v\in\mathcal{C}(\psi)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega),\end{aligned}\right.

where

(4.4)

H_{j}(x,s,\xi)=\frac{H(x,s,\xi)}{1+\frac{1}{j}|H(x,s,\xi)|},\quad\forall\;\,j\in\mathbb{N}.

Recall that, since

is bounded, for every fixed

there exists at least one solution

to the system (

) (cf.
[

]

there exists a constant

(cf.

]

The next Lemma improves this estimate, removing the dependence from

in

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a positive constant

such that, for every

, the solutions

of (

More precisely,

C_{\infty}:=\max\left\{\left(\frac{\|f\|_{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle L^{\infty}(\Omega)$}}}{\alpha_{0}}\right)^{1/(p-1)},\,\|\psi\|_{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle L^{\infty}(\Omega)$}}\right\}.

&+\int_{\Omega}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left|\varphi_{\lambda}\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)\right|\\
&+\int_{\Omega}B_{\infty}\left|Du_{n}\right|^{p}\left|\varphi_{\lambda}\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)\right|\\
&-\int_{\Omega}a\left(x,u_{n},Dw_{n}\right)D\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)\varphi_{\lambda}^{\prime}\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right).\end{split}

Applying property (
2.2
) to the term

\int_{\Omega}B_{\infty}\left|Du_{n}\right|^{p}\left|\varphi_{\lambda}\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)\right|

and taking the absolute value (which is then brought inside the integral) in the first term of the right-hand side, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\bigg[a\left(x,u_{n},Du_{n}\right)

\displaystyle-a\left(x,u_{n},Dw_{n}\right)\bigg]D\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)\varphi_{\lambda}^{\prime}\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega}a\left(x,u_{n},Dw_{n}\right)D\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)\varphi_{\lambda}^{\prime}\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)+

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\bigg[\left|a_{0}(x,u_{n})\right|+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\bigg]\left|\varphi_{\lambda}\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)\right|+

\displaystyle+\frac{B_{\infty}}{\alpha}\int_{\Omega}a\left(x,u_{n},Du_{n}\right)Du_{n}\left|\varphi_{\lambda}\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)\right|.

Now we subtract

\int_{\Omega}\bigg[a\left(x,u_{n},Du_{n}\right)-a\left(x,u_{n},Dw_{n}\right)\bigg]D\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)\frac{B_{\infty}}{\alpha}\left|\varphi_{\lambda}\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)\right|,

from both sides, which leads to

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\bigg[a\left(x,u_{n},Du_{n}\right)&-a\left(x,u_{n},Dw_{n}\right)\bigg]D\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)\\
&\left[\varphi_{\lambda}^{\prime}\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)-\frac{B_{\infty}}{\alpha}\left|\varphi_{\lambda}\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)\right|\right]\end{split}

\begin{split}\quad\leq-\int_{\Omega}&a\left(x,u_{n},Dw_{n}\right)D\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)\varphi_{\lambda}^{\prime}\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)\\
&+\frac{B_{\infty}}{\alpha}\int_{\Omega}a\left(x,u_{n},Du_{n}\right)Dw_{n}\left|\varphi_{\lambda}\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)\right|\\
&+\frac{B_{\infty}}{\alpha}\int_{\Omega}a\left(x,u_{n},Dw_{n}\right)D\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)\left|\varphi_{\lambda}\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)\right|\\
&+\int_{\Omega}\left[\left|a_{0}\left(x,u_{n}\right)\right|+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right]\left|\varphi_{\lambda}\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)\right|.\end{split}

Because of the choice of
\lambda=B_{\infty}^{2}/\left(4\alpha^{2}\right)
, we can apply
Remark

4.2
to infer that the left-hand side is greater than

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left[a\left(x,u_{n},Du_{n}\right)-a\left(x,u_{n},Dw_{n}\right)\right]D\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right).

Moreover, using Lebesgue’s dominated convergence Theorem, we see that the right-hand side converges to zero. Hence

(5.7)

\int_{\Omega}\left[a\left(x,u_{n},Du_{n}\right)-a\left(x,u_{n},Dw_{n}\right)\right]D\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right)\rightarrow 0.

Step 2:
We want to show that
\{Du_{n}\}_{n}
converges a.e. to
Du^{*}
in
\Omega.

Here we follow
[
13
]
: let
L_{n}
be defined by

L_{n}=\left[a\left(x,u_{n},Du_{n}\right)-a\left(x,u_{n},Dw_{n}\right)\right]D\left(u_{n}-w_{n}\right).

Then, by (
2.4
),
L_{n}
is a positive function, and (
5.7
) implies

L_{n}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{in }L^{1}(\Omega)\text{ strongly}.

Extracting a subsequence (still denoted by
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
and
\{w_{n}\}_{n}
respectively), we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{n}\rightarrow u^{*}&\text{ a.e. in }\Omega,\\
w_{n}\rightarrow u^{*}&\text{ a.e. in }\Omega,\\
Dw_{n}\rightarrow Du^{*}&\text{ a.e. in }\Omega,\\
L_{n}\rightarrow 0&\text{ a.e. in }\Omega.\end{array}

Then there exists a subset
Z
of
\Omega
, of zero measure, such that, for
x\in\Omega\setminus Z
,

|u^{*}(x)|<\infty,\quad|Du^{*}(x)|<\infty,\quad|h(x)|<\infty,

and

u_{n}(x)\rightarrow u^{*}(x),\quad w_{n}(x)\rightarrow u^{*}(x),\quad Dw_{n}(x)\rightarrow Du^{*}(x),\quad L_{n}(x)\rightarrow 0.

Now we fix
x\in\Omega\setminus Z
and define

\xi_{n}=Du_{n}(x),\quad\zeta_{n}=Dw_{n}(x),

so that

L_{n}(x)=\left[a\left(x,u_{n}(x),\xi_{n}\right)-a\left(x,w_{n}(x),\zeta_{n}\right)\right]\left(\xi_{n}-\zeta_{n}\right).

By assumption (
2.2
), we have, after dropping a positive term on the right-hand side

L_{n}(x)\geq\alpha|\xi_{n}|^{p}-a\left(x,u_{n}(x),\xi_{n}\right)\zeta_{n}-a\left(x,w_{n}(x),\zeta_{n}\right)\xi_{n}.

Since
\{w_{n}(x)\}_{n}
and
\{\zeta_{n}\}_{n}
are convergent sequences, there exist two positive constants (depending on
x
)
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

|w_{n}(x)|\leq C_{1}(x),\quad|\zeta_{n}|\leq C_{2}(x),

Therefore, using assumption (
2.3
) and the fact that the sequence

\{a\left(x,w_{n}(x),\zeta_{n}\right)\}

is bounded, we obtain

L_{n}(x)\geq\alpha\left|\xi_{n}\right|^{p}-c(x)\left[1+\left|\xi_{n}\right|^{p-1}+\left|\xi_{n}\right|\right],

where
c(x)
is a constant which depends on
x
, but does not depend on
n
. Since
L_{n}(x)\rightarrow 0
, it follows that the sequence
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n}
is bounded.
Let
\xi^{*}
be a cluster point of
\xi_{n}
. We have
\left|\xi^{*}\right|<\infty
and

\left[a\left(x,u^{*}(x),\xi^{*}\right)-a(x,u^{*}(x),Du^{*}(x))\right]\left(\xi^{*}-Du^{*}(x)\right)=0,

because of the continuity of
a
with respect to
s
and
\xi
. By (
2.4
), this implies that
\xi^{*}=Du^{*}(x)
. It follows that the cluster point is unique (that is, the limit does not depend on the chosen subsequence) and thus

Du_{n}(x)\rightarrow Du^{*}(x),\quad\forall\;x\in\Omega\setminus Z,

i.e.

\;Du_{n}\rightarrow Du^{*},\quad\text{ a.e. in }\Omega.

Step 3:
We want to prove that
\{Du_{n}\}_{n}
converges to
Du^{*}
strongly in
(L^{p}(\Omega))^{N}
.

Recall that

(5.8)

\displaystyle a\left(x,u_{n},Du_{n}\right)Du_{n}\geqslant 0,

(5.9)

\displaystyle a\left(x,u_{n},Du_{n}\right)Du_{n}\rightarrow a(x,u^{*},Du^{*})Du^{*}\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega.

Moreover, since the sequence
\{a(x,u_{n},Du_{n})\}_{n}
is bounded in

and converges almost everywhere to

, we have that

a\left(x,u_{n},Du_{n}\right)\to a(x,u^{*},Du^{*})\quad\text{weakly in }\left(L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\right)^{N}\text{ and a.e. in }\Omega.

This property, together with assumptions (
5.2
) and (
5.7
), leads us to conclude that

(5.10)

\int_{\Omega}a\left(x,u_{n},Du_{n}\right)Du_{n}\rightarrow\int_{\Omega}a(x,u^{*},Du^{*})Du^{*}.

Indeed, by Vitali’s Theorem we have

\int_{\Omega}a(x,u_{n},Dw_{n})Dw_{n}\rightarrow\int_{\Omega}a(x,u^{*},Du^{*})Du^{*},

\int_{\Omega}a(x,u_{n},Du_{n})Dw_{n}\rightarrow\int_{\Omega}a(x,u^{*},Du^{*})Du^{*},

\int_{\Omega}a(x,u_{n},Dw_{n})Du\rightarrow\int_{\Omega}a(x,u^{*},Du^{*})Du^{*}.

Now, by (
5.8
), (
5.9
), (
5.10
) we can take

y_{n}=a\left(x,u_{n},Du_{n}\right)Du_{n},\quad y=a(x,u^{*},Du^{*})Du^{*},

in Lemma
5.5
and conclude that

(5.11)

a\left(x,u_{n},Du_{n}\right)Du_{n}\rightarrow a(x,u,Du^{*})Du^{*}\quad\text{ in }L^{1}(\Omega),

Now from assumption (
2.2
) we deduce, using Vitali’s Theorem, that

(5.12)

Du_{n}\rightarrow Du^{*}\quad\text{ in }\left(L^{p}(\Omega)\right)^{N}\text{ strong. }

This concludes the proof of the strong convergence of
u_{n}
to
u^{*}
in

It remains to show that

is a solution to (
2.10

\displaystyle a\left(x,u_{n},Du_{n}\right)

1.
Introduction

The aim of this paper is to clarify some relations between Leray-Hopf solutions of the 3D incompressible Navier-Stokes equations and integrability conditions which are known to guarantee smoothness, uniqueness or the energy equality. Most of the results either rule out an integrability class or give a necessary condition for integrability in terms of an
a priori
estimate, taking advantage of techniques introduced by Guerra, Koch and the author in
[
40
]
. As a by-product, we also prove some necessary conditions for global regularity in the Navier-Stokes and Euler equations.

1.1.
Background

We start by recalling some relevant notions and classical results on the Navier-Stokes equations (see e.g.
[
36
,
52
]
). We consider the Cauchy problem

(1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}-\nu\Delta\mathbf{u}+\nabla p=0,\qquad\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0,

(2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(\cdot,0)=\mathbf{u}_{0},

where
\mathbf{u}
is the velocity field,

is the pressure,
\nu>0

\mathbf{u}_{0}
is solenoidal. We will work on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
unless otherwise specified.

Given an initial datum
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\textup{loc}}=L^{2}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_{0}=0

\mathbf{u}\in L^{2}_{\textup{loc}}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})

\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}(\cdot,t)=0

and

(3)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}[\mathbf{u}\cdot(\partial_{t}\varphi+\nu\Delta\varphi)+\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u}\colon\nabla\varphi]\,dx\,dt=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathbf{u}_{0}\cdot\varphi(\cdot,0)\,dx

for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
\nabla\cdot\varphi=0
. (If

\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}

\mathbf{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}_{\sigma})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1})

of (

in

A weak solution

\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}(\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

[

in all domains

\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}
we denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\nu}(\mathbf{u}_{0})\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}\{\text{Leray-Hopf solutions of \eqref{e:NS1}--\eqref{e:NS2}}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\nu}(\mathbf{u}_{0})\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}\{\text{weak solutions of \eqref{e:NS1}--\eqref{e:NS2}}\}.

Every Leray-Hopf solution satisfies
\lim_{t\to 0}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}(t)-\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=0
.

By interpolation, weak solutions of (
1
)–(
2
) belong to
L^{r}(0,\infty;L^{s})
whenever
s\in[2,6]
and

(5)

\frac{2}{r}+\frac{3}{s}=\frac{3}{2}.

In the case of the Euler equations, the correct scaling is
\frac{2}{r}+\frac{3}{s}=\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{2r}
, that is,

(6)

\frac{5}{3r}+\frac{2}{s}=1,

in the sense that if (
6

only exists for a meagre set of data

[

It is open whether Leray-Hopf solutions are unique, belong to
C^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
and/or satisfy the

(equality sign in (
4

C([0,T];H^{\beta}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))

[
12

and Albritton, Brué and Colombo showed the non-uniqueness of Leray-Hopf solutions on

and with
\mathbf{u}_{0}=0

[

]

If, however, a Leray-Hopf solution
\mathbf{u}

L^{r}(0,T;L^{s})

\frac{2}{r}+\frac{3}{s}=1,\qquad s\in[3,\infty],

[

Leray-Hopf solutions generally fail to satisfy LPS conditions. More precisely, if
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}

[
32

33
]

[
31

The energy equality is, however, also known to hold under weaker assumptions than (

More generally, when

To the author’s knowledge it has remained open whether every Leray-Hopf solution belongs to

In §

1.2.
The main result

In the main result of this paper, Theorem
1.2
, we prove that in order to rule out a solution space
L^{r}(0,\infty;X)
, it is enough to rule out the
a priori
estimate (
10
). Theorem
1.2
also shows that for ruling out uniform-in-
\nu
bounds in
L^{r}(0,\infty;X)
it suffices to rule out the
a priori
estimate (
12
). In order to state Theorem
1.2
we fix some terminology.

Definition 1.1
.

A Banach space
\{0\}\subsetneq X\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is said to be
p
-homogeneous
if
\mathbf{v}(\lambda^{-1}\cdot)\in X
and
\left\lVert\mathbf{v}(\lambda^{-1}\cdot)\right\rVert_{X}\approx_{Z}\lambda^{3/p}\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{X}
for all
\mathbf{v}\in X
and
\lambda>0
, and
X
is said to be
isometrically translation invariant
if
\mathbf{v}(\cdot-x_{0})\in X
and
\left\lVert\mathbf{v}(\cdot-x_{0})\right\rVert_{X}=\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{X}
for every
\mathbf{v}\in X
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. (Using
ad hoc
terminology,) a Banach space
Y
satisfies the
Fatou property
with respect to a dual Banach space
Z
if
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left\lVert\mathbf{v}_{j}\right\rVert_{Y}=C<\infty
and
\mathbf{v}_{j}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mathbf{v}
in
Z
imply that
\mathbf{v}\in Y
and
\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{Y}\leq C
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
r,s\in(4/3,\infty]
with
1<\frac{2}{r}+\frac{3}{s}<\frac{3}{2}
. Suppose
X,Z\subset\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
are isometrically translation invariant Banach spaces,
X
is
s
-homogeneous and
Z
is
2
-homogeneous. Suppose
L^{r}(0,\infty;X)
and
Z
have the Fatou property with respect to
L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{2})
and
L^{2}_{\sigma}
, respectively. The implications

\text{\eqref{i:Maximum}}\quad\Leftarrow\quad\text{\eqref{i:Non-meagre}}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\text{\eqref{i:A priori}}\quad\Leftarrow\quad\text{\eqref{i:Uniform in nu}}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\text{\eqref{i:A priori uniform in nu}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\text{\eqref{i:Exponents}}

hold between the claims below:

(i)

For some
\nu>0
,
\mathbf{u}^{\nu}\in\mathcal{W}_{\nu}(\mathbf{u}_{0})\cap L^{r}(0,\infty;X)
exists for a non-meagre set of data
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}\cap Z
.

(ii)

There exists
C>0
such that for every
\nu>0
and
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}\cap Z
there exists
\mathbf{u}^{\nu}\in\mathcal{W}_{\nu}(\mathbf{u}_{0})
with

(9)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{u}^{\nu}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{2})}+\sqrt{\nu}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}^{\nu}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,\infty;L^{2})}\leq C\max\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}},\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{Z}\right\},

(10)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{u}^{\nu}\right\rVert_{L^{r}(0,\infty;X)}\leq C\nu^{-3\left(\frac{5}{3r}+\frac{2}{s}-1\right)}\max\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2\left(\frac{2}{r}+\frac{3}{s}-1\right)},\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{Z}^{2\left(\frac{2}{r}+\frac{3}{s}-1\right)}\right\}.

(iii)

\frac{5}{3r}+\frac{2}{s}\geq 1
.

(iv)

For a non-meagre set of data
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}\cap Z
we have

\limsup_{\nu\to 0}\inf_{\mathbf{u}^{\nu}\in\mathcal{W}_{\nu}(\mathbf{u}_{0})}(\left\lVert\mathbf{u}^{\nu}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{2})}+\sqrt{\nu}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}^{\nu}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,\infty;L^{2})}+\left\lVert\mathbf{u}^{\nu}\right\rVert_{L^{r}(0,\infty;X)})<\infty.

(v)

There exist
\nu_{0},C>0
such that for all
\nu>0
,
\tilde{\nu}\in(0,\nu_{0}]
and
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}\cap Z
there exists
\mathbf{u}^{\nu}\in\mathcal{W}_{\nu}(\mathbf{u}_{0})
with

(11)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{u}^{\nu}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{2})}+\sqrt{\nu}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}^{\nu}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,\infty;L^{2})}\leq C\max\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}},\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{Z}\right\}

(12)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{u}^{\nu}\right\rVert_{L^{r}(0,\infty;X)}\leq C\left(\frac{\tilde{\nu}}{\nu}\right)^{3\left(\frac{5}{3r}+\frac{2}{s}-1\right)}\max\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2\left(\frac{2}{r}+\frac{3}{s}-1\right)},\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{Z}^{2\left(\frac{2}{r}+\frac{3}{s}-1\right)}\right\}.

(vi)

\frac{5}{3r}+\frac{2}{s}=1
.

The
a priori
estimates (
10
)–(
12
) are in the spirit of the works of Gallagher
[
38
]
, Tao
[
59
,
61
]
, Rusin and Šverák
[
53
]
and Jia and Šverák
[
45
]
. Their use is at least three-fold. First, they reassure one that one cannot prove solvability in
L^{r}(0,\infty;X)
by ”soft methods” alone, without also proving a uniform estimate on the norms
\left\lVert\mathbf{u}^{\nu}\right\rVert_{L^{r}(0,\infty;X)}
. Second, the
a priori
estimates allow one to rule out plausible solution classes via scaling arguments; this aspect is treated extensively in an abstract setting in
[
40
]
. Third, they sometimes allow one to infer global solvability in a function space from local solvability, by iteratively using the fact that kinetic energy is decreasing.

The energy inequality (
4
) allows one to control the kinetic energy and cumulative energy dissipation globally in time, but no other coercive globally controlled quantities have been found in the 3D Navier-Stokes equations. Gallagher’s
a priori
estimate holds for critical spaces of initial data such as
L^{3}
and
\dot{H}^{1/2}
, and uses profile decompositions. As a consequence of the result, global control on such critical norms is necessary for global regularity (for data in said spaces). Supercritical quantities, such as those studied in Theorem
1.2
, seem unlikely to be of decisive use in proving global regularity (see, e.g.,
[
60
, §3.4]
), but some of them would imply, among other things, the energy equality and the validity of the inviscid limit (at least in a weak
∗
sense).

The supercritical setting of Theorem
1.2
allows us to find a rather elementary proof based on Baire category and a functional analytical lemma from
[
40
]
(Lemma
4.1
in this paper). Baire category often allows one to infer from a qualitative condition in a non-meagre set (such as (
i
)) a quantitative condition in some ball. If the quantitative condition is stable under translations and weak
∗
convergence, then Lemma
4.1
allows one to assume that the ball is centred at the origin, thereby letting one unleash the scaling symmetries of the system and prove a uniform estimate.

With a bit of work (see Corollary
4.3
) Theorem
1.2
implies that Leray-Hopf solutions generically fail to belong to
\cup_{T>0}L^{r}(0,T;L^{s})
if
\frac{5}{2r}+\frac{3}{s}<1
. Furthermore, uniform-in-
\nu
bounds on
\left\lVert\mathbf{u}^{\nu}\right\rVert_{L^{r}(0,\infty;L^{s})}
require that (
6
) holds. We next consider the consequences of Theorem
1.2
on Bochner-Besov spaces that satisfy (
6
).

1.3.
Weak solutions with Besov space regularity

Recall that the regularity classes
L^{r}(0,\infty;\dot{B}_{r,\infty}^{\beta_{r}})
,
1\leq r\leq 3
,
\beta_{r}
as in (
8
), ensure the energy equality for weak solutions and satisfy the compatibility condition (
6
). By Theorem
1.2
,
\beta_{r}
are the only exponents compatible with uniform-in-viscosity bounds for 2-homogeneous spaces of initial data. The function space
L^{3}(0,T;B_{3,\infty}^{1/3})
and its variant
L^{3}(0,T;B_{3,\infty}^{1/3}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
are natural in the study of high Reynolds number turbulence; see, for instance,
[
27
,
29
,
58
]
and the references contained therein.

The function class
L^{3}(0,T;B_{3,\infty}^{1/3}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
is at a threshold for energy conservation in the Euler equations. Indeed, after several brilliant works using Nash’s method of convex integration, starting with the seminal contributions of De Lellis and Székelyhidi
[
21
,
23
]
, non-conservative
L^{\infty}(0,T;C^{1/3-}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
solutions were constructed by Isett
[
42
]
and dissipative ones by Buckmaster, De Lellis, Székelyhidi and Vicol in
[
10
]
; this gives the flexible side of the celebrated Onsager conjecture. Novack and Vicol constructed non-conservative solutions in
C([0,T];H^{1/2-}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\cap L^{\infty-}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))\hookrightarrow C([0,T];B^{1/3-}_{3,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))

[
51
]
, and Giri, Kwon and Novack constructed non-conservative
C([0,T];B_{3,\infty}^{1/3-}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\cap L^{\infty-}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
solutions with the local energy inequality
[
39
]
. In the rigid side, Constantin, E and Titi showed that if
\beta>1/3
, then
L^{3}(0,T;B_{3,\infty}^{\beta}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))\cap C_{w}([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
solutions of the Euler equations conserve kinetic energy
[
19
]
. In
[
15
, Theorem 3.3]
, Cheskidov, Constantin, Friedlander and Shvydkoy extended conservation to the critical class
L^{3}(0,T;B_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}^{1/3})\cap C_{w}([0,T];L^{2})
, where
B_{3,p}^{1/3}\subsetneq B_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}^{1/3}\subsetneq B_{3,\infty}^{1/3}
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
.

In the critical case, Cheskidov, Constantin, Friedlander and Shvydkoy also constructed a vector field
\mathbf{v}\in B_{3,\infty}^{1/3}
with a positive energy flux
[
15
, §3.4]
, even though
\mathbf{v}
is not a solution of the Euler equations. It is strongly expected that some weak solutions of the Euler equations arise at the inviscid limit and dissipate kinetic energy, and
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}_{\sigma})\cap L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}_{3,\infty}^{1/3})
appears to be a natural candidate space for such weak solutions; see also

It seems plausible that

Corollary 1.3
.

Suppose

(i)

1.4.

A potential way to gain access to LPS classes in intervals

.

(ii)

2.1.
Homogeneous Sobolev and Besov spaces

We very briefly recall relevant facts about homogeneous Sobolev and Besov spaces from
[
4
]
. When
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
consists of tempered distributions whose Fourier transform belongs to
L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and satisfies

\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2s}\left\lvert\hat{\mathbf{v}}(\xi)\right\rvert^{2}d\xi<\infty.

Recall that
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Hilbert space if and only if
s<\frac{d}{2}

[
4
, Proposition 1.34]
,
\dot{H}^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
embeds into
L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
(see
[
4
, Theorem 1.38]
) and
\dot{H}^{3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is contained in
\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{\textup{BMO}}\leq C\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{3/2}}
(see
[
4
, Theorem 1.48]
).

We then define Besov spaces. We denote
A(r_{1},r_{2})\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:r_{1}<\left\lvert x\right\rvert<r_{2}\}
for
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
. Given
\alpha\in(1,\frac{4}{3})
, choose a radial function
\theta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(A(\frac{3}{4},\frac{8}{3}))
with
\theta|_{A(1/\alpha,2\alpha)}=1
. Now

\varphi\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}\frac{\theta}{\sum_{j\in Z}\theta(2^{-j}\cdot))}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(A(3/4,8/3)),\quad\chi\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}1-\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B(0,4/3))

are well-defined (with locally finite sums)
[
4
, p. 60]
; see
[
4
, Proposition 2.10]
for further properties of
\varphi
and
\chi
. We denote

\dot{\Delta}_{j}g\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}\varphi(2^{-j}D)g\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi(2^{-j}\cdot))*g

for all
g\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
\mathcal{F}
is the Fourier transform. We also denote
\dot{S}_{j}g\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}\chi(2^{-j}D)g
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
p,q\in[1,\infty]
, and fix
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We define the homogeneous Besov seminorm

\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\beta}}\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}\left\lVert\left(2^{j\beta}\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\right)_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\right\rVert_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}.

and the inhomogeneous Besov norm

\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{B_{p,q}^{\beta}}\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}\left\lVert\left(2^{j\beta}\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\right)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\right\rVert_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N})}+\left\lVert\dot{S}_{0}\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}.

Following
[
4
]
we denote by
\mathcal{S}_{h}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the set of tempered distributions
\mathbf{v}
such that
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\left\lVert\theta(\lambda D)\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}=0
for all
\theta\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In particular,
\mathcal{S}_{h}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
contains all compactly supported distributions and excludes all non-zero polynomials
[
4
, pp. 22-23]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
p,q\in[1,\infty]
. The homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
consists of
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\beta}}<\infty
, and the inhomogeneous Besov space
B_{p,q}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
consists of
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{B_{p,q}^{\beta}}<\infty
.

If
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is compact, we denote by
B_{p,q}^{\beta}(K)
and
\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\beta}(K)
the members of
B_{p,q}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, respectively, supported on
K
.

Homogeneous Besov spaces
\dot{B}_{p,r}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are normed spaces
[
4
, Proposition 2.16]
, and they are complete if either
\beta<\frac{d}{p}
or
r=1
and
\beta\leq\frac{d}{p}

[
4
, Theorem 2.25]
. By using Young’s inequality on
\|\dot{S}_{0}\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{p}}
we get
\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow B_{p,q}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
whenever
1\leq r\leq p
.

We have

(14)

\left\lVert\mathbf{v}(\lambda^{-1}\cdot)\right\rVert_{\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\approx\lambda^{\frac{d}{p}-\beta}\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for all
\mathbf{v}\in\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\lambda>0

[
4
, Remark 2.19]
. We also have the embeddings

(15)

\displaystyle p_{1}\leq p_{2},\;q_{1}\leq q_{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\dot{B}_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\dot{B}_{p_{2},q_{2}}^{\beta-\frac{d}{p_{1}}+\frac{d}{p_{2}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(16)

\displaystyle p\leq q\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{q}}\hookrightarrow L^{q}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}_{q,\max\{q,2\}}^{0}

[
4
, Propositions 2.20 and 2.39, Theorems 2.40 and 2.41]
and the interpolation result

(17)

\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\theta\beta_{1}+(1-\theta)\beta_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{C}{\beta_{2}-\beta_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{1}{1-\theta}\right)\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{\beta_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\theta}\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{\beta_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1-\theta}

whenever
\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}
and
\theta\in(0,1)

[
4
, Proposition 2.22]
. A short computation gives
\left\lVert\mathbf{v}(\cdot-x_{0})\right\rVert_{\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
for all
\mathbf{v}\in\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Suppose now
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
-\frac{d}{p^{\prime}}\leq\beta<\frac{d}{p}
. If
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

is compact, then

is a continuous embedding
[
20
, Corollary 2.1.1]
. Whenever

and

is compact,

embeds compactly into

[

. Combining with (

) and multiplying by

,

, we have the following (recall that

Corollary 2.2
.

Let

and

, and suppose

For the proof of Corollary

we will need the following:

Lemma 2.3
.

Whenever
p,r,s\in(1,\infty]
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, the Bochner-Besov space
L^{p}(0,\infty;\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
has the Fatou property with respect to
L^{r}(0,\infty;L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathbf{v}_{k}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mathbf{v}
in
L^{r}(0,\infty;L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\lVert\mathbf{v}_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(0,\infty;\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}<\infty
. We first show that

(18)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{v}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}dt\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{v}_{k}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}dt

for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\epsilon>0
. Now there exists
\psi_{j}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty))
such that
\left\lVert\psi_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(0,\infty;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq 1
and

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{v}(x,t)\cdot\psi_{j}(x,t)\,dx\,dt\geq(1-\epsilon)\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(0,\infty;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}.

By using the assumption that
\mathbf{v}_{k}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mathbf{v}
in
L^{r}(0,\infty;L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
we get

\displaystyle(1-\epsilon)^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{v}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}dt

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\dot{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{v}_{k}(x,t)\cdot\psi_{j}(x,t)\,dx\,dt\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{v}_{k}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}dt,

so that (
18
) holds.

We then finish the proof of the Lemma via Fatou’s lemma and (
18
):

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(0,\infty;\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{\beta})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\sup_{\left\lvert j\right\rvert\leq\ell}2^{j\beta p}\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{v}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p}dt

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{\left\lvert j\right\rvert\leq\ell}2^{j\beta p}\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{v}_{k}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p}dt

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{j\beta p}\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{v}_{k}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p}dt

\displaystyle=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert\mathbf{v}_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(0,\infty;\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{\beta})}^{p},

as claimed.
∎

For all
p\in[1,\infty]
and
\beta\in(0,1)
, the Besov space
\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
has the equivalent norm
\sup_{h\neq 0}\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{-\beta}\left\lVert\mathbf{v}(\cdot+h)-\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

[
4
, Theorem 2.36]
. As such,
\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{\beta}
measures, loosely speaking, Hölder continuity on average. On the torus, it is customary to use the norm

\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{B_{p,\infty}^{\beta}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+\sup_{h\neq 0}\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{v}(\cdot+h)-\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{\beta}}

for this range of exponents.

Homogeneous Besov spaces with negative smoothness
\beta
can also be characterised in terms of the heat kernel. In particular, if
r,s\in[1,\infty]
, then

(19)

\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{\dot{B}_{s,r}^{-2/r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\approx\left\lVert e^{t\Delta}\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{L^{r}(0,\infty;L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

for all
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

[
4
, Theorem 2.34]
. We single out the following consequence (which of course also has a more direct proof).

Proposition 2.4
.

If
r,s,p\in[1,\infty]
satisfy
\frac{2}{r}+\frac{d}{s}<\frac{d}{p}
and
Z\subset\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a
p
-homogeneous Banach space, then we have
e^{t\Delta}\mathbf{v}\in L^{r}(0,\infty;L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
only for a meagre set of vector fields
\mathbf{v}\in Z
.

Proof.

If Proposition
2.4
failed, the Open mapping theorem would imply that
\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\approx\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}+\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{\dot{B}_{s,r}^{-2/r}}
for all
\mathbf{v}\in Z
, leading to a contradiction with (
14
).
∎

In the proof of Corollary
1.3
we will need the integrability properties of those Leray-Hopf solutions which belong to
L^{r}(0,\infty;\dot{B}_{r,\infty}^{\beta_{r}})
(see (

)).

Lemma 2.5
.

If
d=3
,
2\leq r<\frac{11}{3}
,
\theta\in(0,1)
,
\theta\leq\frac{11-3r}{5}
and
T\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{\infty\}
, then

L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})\cap L^{r}(0,T;\dot{B}_{r,\infty}^{\beta_{r}})\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{r}{1-\theta}}(0,T;L^{\frac{6r}{3r-5(1-\theta)}}).

In particular,

L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})\cap L^{r}(0,T;\dot{B}_{r,\infty}^{\beta_{r}})\hookrightarrow L^{r}(0,T;B_{r,\infty}^{\beta_{r}})

for all
r\in[2,\frac{11}{3})
and
T\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{v}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})\cap L^{r}(0,T;\dot{B}_{r,\infty}^{\beta_{r}})
and
\theta\in(0,1)
. By (
15
)–(
16
),
L^{2}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}_{2,2}^{0}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}_{r,\infty}^{3(2-r)/(2r)}
, and so (
15
)–(
17
) yield

(20)

\left\lVert\mathbf{v}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{6r}{3r-5(1-\theta)}}}\lesssim_{\theta}\left\lVert\mathbf{v}(t)\right\rVert_{\dot{B}_{r,1}^{\frac{3(2-r)}{2r}\theta+\beta_{r}(1-\theta)}}\lesssim_{\theta}\left\lVert\mathbf{v}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\theta}\left\lVert\mathbf{v}(t)\right\rVert_{\dot{B}_{r,\infty}^{\beta_{r}}}^{1-\theta}

for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
; here we use the assumption
\theta\leq\frac{11-3r}{5}
to get
\frac{3(2-r)\theta}{2r}+\beta_{r}(1-\theta)\geq 0
, so that (
16
) is applicable. Now (
20
) yields

\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{r}{1-\theta}}(0,T;L^{\frac{6r}{3r-5(1-\theta)}})}\lesssim_{\theta}\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{2})}^{\theta}\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{L^{r}(0,T;\dot{B}_{r,\infty}^{\beta_{r}})}^{1-\theta}.

The second embedding follows from the fact that
L^{r}\cap\dot{B}_{r,\infty}^{\beta_{r}}\hookrightarrow B_{r,\infty}^{\beta_{r}}
: if
r>2
, we choose
\theta=\frac{11-3r}{5}\in(0,1)
, and if
r=2
, we use the fact that
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

By (
20
),
L^{2}\cap\dot{B}_{r,\infty}^{\beta_{r}}\hookrightarrow L^{s}
for all

[

, Theorem B]
.

If
\mathbf{u}_{0,j}\to\mathbf{u}_{0}

\mathbf{u}_{0,j}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{u}_{0}
in

is proved by standard weak compactness methods; see e.g.
[

, p. 103]
. It is, however, open whether the class of Leray-Hopf solutions is stable under weak

in

for some

. Stability under weak

-solutions

and Leray solutions

]

,

and the references contained therein.

3.
The case of Euler equations

In Theorem
3.2
we prove the generic lack of higher integrability of weak solutions of the Euler equations (in the
L^{r}(0,\infty;L^{s})
scale), following the proof of
[
40
, Lemma 5.1]
. Wiedemann
[
62
]
has constructed a weak solution
\mathbf{u}\in C_{w}([0,\infty);L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
for every
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and
d\geq 2
by leaning on convex integration techniques from
[
22
]
. The author is not aware of similar results for
L^{p}_{\sigma}
data,
p>2
. For the proof of Theorem
3.2
we recall a linear relaxation of the Euler equations (essentially) from
[
21
]
. Below,
S
takes values in
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\textup{sym}}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
d\geq 2
. Suppose
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0
. We say that
(\mathbf{u},S)\in L^{2}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T))\times L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T))
is a weak solution of the
relaxed Euler equations

(21)

\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+\nabla\cdot S+\nabla p=0,\quad\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0,\quad\mathbf{u}(\cdot,0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}

if
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}(\cdot,t)=0
a.e.
t\in(0,T)
and

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mathbf{u}\cdot\partial_{t}\varphi+S\colon\nabla\varphi)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{u}_{0}\cdot\varphi(\cdot,0)=0

for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\nabla\cdot\varphi=0
.

If
\mathbf{u}\in L^{2}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T))
is a weak solution of Euler equations with datum
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_{0}=0
, then
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u})
is clearly a weak solution of the relaxed Euler equations with
\mathbf{u}(\cdot,0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
d\geq 2
,
p\in[2,\infty]
and
r,s\in(2,\infty]
. Suppose
\{0\}\subsetneq Z\subset L^{2}_{\sigma,\textup{loc}}
is a
p
-homogeneous Banach space. If

\frac{1+\frac{d}{p}}{r}+\frac{d}{s}<\frac{d}{p},

then a weak solution
\mathbf{u}\in\cup_{T>0}L^{r}(0,T;L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
of the Euler equations only exists for a meagre set of data
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in Z
. If
\frac{1+d/p}{r}+\frac{d}{s}>\frac{d}{p}
, an analogous result holds for
L^{r}(0,\infty;L^{s})
.

Proof.

We prove the case
\frac{1+d/p}{r}+\frac{d}{s}<\frac{d}{p}
. Note that it suffices to show, for every fixed
T>0
, that a weak solution
(\mathbf{u},S)\in L^{r}(0,T;L^{s})\times L^{r/2}(0,T;L^{s/2})
of (
21
) only exists for a meagre set of data.

Let
T>0
. Seeking a contradiction, suppose that for a non-meagre set of data, a solution
(\mathbf{u},S)\in L^{r}(0,T;L^{s})\times L^{r/2}(0,T;L^{s/2})
exists. Thus, by definition, one of the (manifestly closed) sets

X_{M}\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}\{\mathbf{u}_{0}\in X\colon\exists(\mathbf{u},S)\text{ solving \eqref{e:Relaxed NS} with}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{r}(0,T;L^{s})}+\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{L^{r/2}(0,T;L^{s/2})}\leq M\}

contains a closed ball
\bar{B}(\mathbf{v}_{0},\epsilon)\subset Z
. By linearity, we may assume that
\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{0}=0
(by possibly enlarging
M
). By linearity again, for some
C>0
, for every
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in Z
there exists a solution
(\mathbf{u},S)
of (
21
) with

\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{r}(0,T;L^{s})}+\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{L^{r/2}(0,T;L^{s/2})}\leq C\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{Z}.

Fix now
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in Z\setminus\{0\}
and define
\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{0}\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}\mathbf{u}_{0}(\lambda\cdot)
so that
\left\lVert\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{0}\right\rVert_{Z}=\lambda^{-d/p}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{Z}
. Select a solution
(\mathbf{u},S)
of the relaxed Euler equations with
\mathbf{u}(\cdot,0)=\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{0}
such that

\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{r}(0,T;L^{s})}+\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{L^{r/2}(0,T;L^{s/2})}\leq C\left\lVert\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{0}\right\rVert_{Z}=C\lambda^{-d/p}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{Z}.

Let
\gamma>0
, to be chosen later. Now

\mathbf{u}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x},t)\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}\mathbf{u}\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}}{\lambda},\frac{t}{\lambda^{\gamma}}\right),\qquad S_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x},t)\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}\lambda^{1-\gamma}S\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}}{\lambda},\frac{t}{\lambda^{\gamma}}\right)

form a solution of (
21
) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\lambda^{\gamma}T)
with
\mathbf{u}_{\lambda}(\cdot,0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{\lambda}\right\rVert_{L^{r}(0,\lambda^{\gamma}T;L^{s})}=\lambda^{\frac{\gamma}{r}+\frac{d}{s}}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{r}(0,T;L^{s})}\leq C\lambda^{\frac{\gamma}{r}+\frac{d}{s}-\frac{d}{p}}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{Z},

\displaystyle\left\lVert S_{\lambda}\right\rVert_{L^{r/2}(0,\lambda^{\gamma}T;L^{s/2})}=\lambda^{\frac{2\gamma}{r}+\frac{2d}{s}+1-\gamma}\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{L^{r/2}(0,T;L^{s/2})}\leq C\lambda^{\frac{2\gamma}{r}+\frac{2d}{s}+1-\gamma-\frac{d}{p}}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{Z}.

If
r<\infty
, then
\gamma\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}r(\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{s})>0
by assumption and

(22)

\frac{2\gamma}{r}+\frac{2d}{s}+1-\gamma-\frac{d}{p}=\frac{(1-r)d}{p}+\frac{rd}{s}+1<0.

Therefore, making
\gamma
slightly smaller, we get both (
22
) and
\frac{\gamma}{r}+\frac{d}{s}-\frac{d}{p}<0
. Letting
\lambda\to\infty
we conclude that
(0,0)
is a weak solution of (
21
) with datum
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in Z\setminus\{0\}
. This gives the sought contradiction when
r<\infty
. If
r=\infty
, the same argument works for any
\gamma>\frac{2d}{s}+1-\frac{d}{p}
.

The second claim has an entirely analogous proof, but
(0,T)
is replaced by
(0,\infty)
,
\gamma=r(\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{s})
need not be positive and we let
\lambda\to 0
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

A similar relaxation of the Navier-Stokes equations shows that if
\frac{5}{2r}+\frac{3}{s}<\frac{3}{2}
, then not only weak solutions but also very weak solutions in
\cup_{T>0}L^{r}(0,T;L^{s})
are ruled out for a Baire generic datum
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}
. The improvement relative to Theorem
1.2
is, however, so marginal that we omit the details.

and corollaries

4.1.
The proof of Theorem
1.2

For the proof of Theorem
1.2
we formulate a lemma which was used (even if not stated explicitly) in
[
40
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
X
is a normed space and
\mathcal{T}
is a topology on
X
. Suppose that
\{\tau_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbb{R}}
is a one-parameter group of isometries
X\to X
. If a sequentially
\mathcal{T}
-closed set
Z\subset X
contains a ball
\bar{B}(x,r)
,
\tau_{a}x\to 0
in
\mathcal{T}
as
a\to\infty
and
\tau_{a}Z\subset Z
for every
a\in\mathbb{R}
, then
\bar{B}(0,r)\subset Z
.

Proof.

Let
\left\lVert y\right\rVert_{X}\leq r
. For every
a\in\mathbb{R}
we have
x+\tau_{-a}y\in\bar{B}(x,r)\subset Z

is as in Claim

Suppose Claim

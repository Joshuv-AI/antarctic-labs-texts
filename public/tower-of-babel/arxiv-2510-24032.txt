Self-Covering, Finiteness, Commutativity, And Fibering Over Tori
Lizhen Qin, Yang Su

Abstract
. A topological space is called self-covering if it is a nontrivial cover of itself. We prove that, under mild assumptions, a closed self-covering manifold with an abelian fundamental group fibers over a torus in various senses. As a corollary, if its dimension is above 5 5 and its fundamental group is free abelian, then it is a fiber bundle over a circle. We also construct non-fibering examples when these assumptions are not fulfilled. In particular, one class of examples illustrates that the structure of self-covering manifolds is more complicated when the fundamental groups are nonabelian, and the corresponding fibering problem encounters significant difficulties.



1.
Introduction

A topological space is called self-covering if it is homeomorphic, or more generally homotopy equivalent, to a nontrivial covering space of itself. Self-covering spaces, especially self-covering manifolds, are well-studied in the literature (see e.g.
[
4
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
75
]
and
[
78
]
).

The simplest self-covering closed manifolds are tori
\mathbb{T}^{n}:=(S^{1})^{n}
with a self-covering map
A_{\mathbb{T}}\colon\mathbb{T}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{n}
given by a matrix
A\in\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
with
\det(A)\neq 0
and
\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}A^{k}=0
. Typical examples of self-covering manifolds are constructed by taking the product of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with a closed manifold
N
, with a self-covering map
h=A_{\mathbb{T}}\times\mathrm{id}_{N}
. These examples indicate that, to elucidate the structure of self-covering manifolds with abelian fundamental groups, it is natural to fiber such a manifold either over a torus or with a torus fiber. In this framework, the self-covering property of the manifold is expected to arise directly from a self-covering of the torus.

In
[
64
]
, Qin-Su-Wang studied when a closed self-covering manifold with an abelian fundamental group is a fiber bundle over a circle. As an extension of the previous work, this paper tries to fiber such a manifold over a torus. We work in the category of smooth (DIFF), piecewise-linear (PL) and topological (TOP) manifolds simultaneously. Throughout this paper, the abbreviation CAT stands for any of these categories. All topological spaces are assumed path-connected unless otherwise stated. Motivated by
[
64
]
, we ask the following question.

Question 1.1
.

Suppose
M
is a closed
\mathrm{CAT}
manifold with abelian
\pi_{1}(M)
,
q\colon M^{\prime}\rightarrow M
is a nontrivial covering, and
h\colon M\rightarrow M^{\prime}
is a homotopy equivalence.

Are there a
\mathrm{CAT}
(fiber) bundle projection
p\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{n}
and a linear endomorphism
A_{\mathbb{T}}
of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
such that the following diagram is a homotopy pullback?

(1.1)

\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\scriptstyle{q\circ h}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{\mathbb{T}^{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{A_{\mathbb{T}}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{T}^{n}}

Here
A_{\mathbb{T}}
is required to be defined by a matrix
A\in\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
such that
\det(A)\neq 0
and
\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}A^{k}=0
.

Recall that by a homotopy pullback we mean (
1.1
) commutes up to homotopy and satisfies a certain universal property (see
[
1
, p. 205]
). If the answer to Question
1.1
was affirmative, self-covering phenomenon would be well understood. For closed manifolds with abelian fundamental groups, all self-covering maps essentially would be base changes of fiber bundles resulted from linear endomorphisms of tori. However, it is known that the answer is no in general. Theorems C and D in
[
64
]
provide non-fibering examples using obstructions from algebraic
K
-theory (Wall’s finiteness obstruction and Whitehead torsion). In Theorem
M
below we provide another type of non-fibering examples using tangential data such as the Pontrjagin classes and the Kirby-Siebenmann invariant. Therefore, one usually cannot find such a bundle projection
p\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

Nevertheless, the answer to Question
1.1
is yes in a weaker sense. By Theorem
C
below, one can always get a homotopy fibration
p\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{n}
whose homotopy fiber is a finitely dominated Poincaré Duality Space, and
p
fits (
1.1
) well. (Recall that a Poincaré Duality Space is an imitation of a closed manifold in the homotopy sense.) An interesting consequence is a characterization of topological tori in terms of self-covering properties (Corollary
D
).

To get a positive solution to Question
1.1
in its original sense, we shall try, under additional assumptions, to fiber
M
in various senses, from the weak to the strong, between a homotopy fibration and a fiber bundle. These results are mainly formulated in Subsection
1.4
. Some nontrivial obstructions will appear when we improve a weak solution step by step. They will vanish if certain extra assumptions are fulfilled. Eventually, a desired fiber bundle structure will be achieved.

It’s certainly interesting to obtain some positive solutions to Question
1.1
in the case that
\pi_{1}(M)
is nonabelian. To make broad sense, the
\mathbb{T}^{n}
in Question
1.1
should be now replaced with a general closed aspherical manifold. This motivates us to propose Question
1.12
below. Theorem
O
and
P
show that self-covering manifolds are closely related to self-covering complexes. This allows us to construct self-covering manifolds out of known examples of self-covering complexes with interesting fundamental groups (Theorem
N
). These examples indicate that the case of nonabelian fundamental groups is much more complicated than the abelian case. The answer to Question
1.12
is severely negative even in the weaker sense.

1.1.
CW Complexes

Note that the covering space
\overline{M}
of
M
with
\pi_{1}(\overline{M})=\ker p_{\#}
is homotopy equivalent to the homotopy fiber of
p
in (
1.1
). Therefore a necessary condition for the map
p
to be a bundle projection is that
\overline{M}
is homotopy equivalent to a closed manifold. Thus, to obtain a positive solution to Question
1.1
, we have to show the homotopy finiteness of certain infinite cover of
M
. This is a problem in homotopy theory. So we may work in the wider category of CW complexes.

Recall that a CW complex is of finite type if there are only finitely many cells in each dimension, whereas the dimension of the complex may be infinite; a space is finitely dominated if it is dominated by a finite CW complex.

Suppose
X
is a CW complex,
q\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
a nontrivial covering, and
h\colon X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
is a homotopy equivalence. Since
q_{\#}\colon\pi_{1}(X^{\prime})\rightarrow\pi_{1}(X)
is a subgroup inclusion, for brevity, we abuse the notation
h_{\#}
to denote the composition

(qh)_{\#}\colon\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(X^{\prime})\hookrightarrow\pi_{1}(X).

Then
h_{\#}\colon\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(X)
is a monomorphism whose image is a proper subgroup of
\pi_{1}(X)
. Let
\mathrm{im}h_{\#}^{k}
denote the image of
h_{\#}^{k}\colon\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(X)
.

We obtain the following generalization of
[
64
, Theorem A]
on homotopy finiteness. The theorem serves the geometric foundation of this paper.

Theorem A
.

Let
X
,
X^{\prime}
and
h
be as the above. Suppose further
\pi_{1}(X)
is a finitely generated abelian group. Let
G:=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}h_{\#}^{k}
. Let
\overline{X}
be the cover of
X
with
\pi_{1}(\overline{X})=G
. Then the following conclusion holds:

(1)

\pi_{1}(X)/G\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
for some
n>0
, and
X^{\prime}
is a finite cover of
X
.

(2)

If
X
is homotopy equivalent to a CW complex of finite type (resp. is finitely dominated), then so is
\overline{X}
.

Remark 1.2
.

As pointed in
[
64
, Remark 1.4]
, if
X
is homotopy equivalent to a finite CW complex, then unnecessarily so is
\overline{X}
in Theorem
A
.

Remark 1.3
.

By Theorem
N
, if we drop the assumption that
\pi_{1}(X)
is abelian in Theorem
A
, then its conclusion will be significantly wrong, see also Remark
1.15
.

To get Theorem
A
, we shall actually prove a more general Theorem
5.4
on homotopy finiteness which follows from an even broader Theorem
5.1
on homological finiteness. As a byproduct, we have the following result in group theory.

Corollary B
.

Suppose
\pi
is a finitely generated (unnecessarily abelian) group, and
\varphi\colon\pi\rightarrow\pi
is a monomorphism with
[\pi:\mathrm{im}\varphi]>1
. Suppose
G:=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}\varphi^{k}
is normal in
\pi
and
\pi/G
is abelian. Then
\pi/G\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
for some
n>0
,
[\pi:\mathrm{im}\varphi]<\infty
, and the abelianization
G/[G,G]
is finitely generated.

1.2.
Poincaré Duality Spaces

A Poincaré duality space, or Poincaré space for brevity, is a topological space satisfying Poincaré duality in the strictest sense, i.e. there are Poincaré duality isomorphisms between its homology and cohomology groups for all local coefficient systems. Particularly, a closed TOP
m
-manifold is a Poincaré space with formal dimension
m
. Such a manifold is homotopy equivalent to a finite CW complex, let alone finitely dominated. Poincaré spaces are foundational to the surgery theory of manifolds. For the precise definition and basic properties of Poincaré spaces, see e.g.
[
77
]
and
[
47
]
.

The following theorem gives a positive answer to Question
1.1
in the homotopy sense which is weaker than the original sense.

Theorem C
.

Suppose
X
is a finitely dominated Poincaré space with formal dimension
d
,
\pi_{1}(X)
is abelian, and
q\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
is a nontrivial covering. Suppose
h\colon X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
is a homotopy equivalence. Let
G:=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}h_{\#}^{k}
. Let
\overline{X}
be the cover of
X
with
\pi_{1}(\overline{X})=G
. Let
n:=\mathrm{rank}(\pi_{1}(X)/G)
. Then the following statements hold:

(1)

n>0
,
\pi_{1}(X)/G\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and
X^{\prime}
is a finite cover of
X
.

(2)

There exists a continuous map
p\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{n}
which induces an epimorphism of fundamental groups and the homotopy fiber of
p
is homotopy equivalent to
\overline{X}
.

(3)

\overline{X}
is also a finitely dominated Poincaré space with formal dimension
d-n
. The following diagram is a homotopy pullback.

(1.2)

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\scriptstyle{q\circ h}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{\mathbb{T}^{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{A_{\mathbb{T}}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{T}^{n}}

Here
A_{\mathbb{T}}
is a linear endomorphism of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
induced by a matrix
A\in\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
such that
\det(A)\neq 0
and
\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}A^{k}=0
.

By the classification of low dimensional Poincaré spaces, we obtain Corollaries
6.2
,
6.3
,
6.4
,
6.5
and
6.6
in the case of
d-n\leq 4
. In particular, the following corollary is a characterization of topological tori in terms of self-covering properties.

Corollary D
.

Suppose
M
is a closed TOP
m
-manifold such that
\pi_{1}(M)
is abelian. Suppose
M^{\prime}
is a nontrivial cover of
M
and
h\colon M\rightarrow M^{\prime}
is a homotopy equivalence. Let
G:=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}h_{\#}^{k}
and
n:=\mathrm{rank}(\pi_{1}(M)/G)
. Suppose further
M
satisfies one of the following:

(1)

m-n\leq 1
;

(2)

m-n=2
and
|G|>2
;

(3)

m-n=3
,
G
is not cyclic, and
G\neq\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2
.

Then
M
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{T}^{m}
.

Remark 1.4
.

Even if assume further
M
is a self-covering DIFF (resp. PL) manifold in Corollary
D
, it is not true that
M
is diffeomorphic (resp. PL isomorphic) to
\mathbb{T}^{m}
. Actually, by
[

]
(see also
[

]
), there exists a DIFF closed manifold

such that: (i)

carries a smooth expanding map, which means

is self-covering in a very strong sense; (ii)

is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{T}^{m}
; (iii) but

\mathbb{T}^{m}
.

1.3.
Low Dimensional Manifolds

The categories
\mathrm{TOP}=\mathrm{PL}=\mathrm{DIFF}
for dimensions from
1
to
3
.

The classification of closed self-covering manifolds with dimensions
1
or

is trivial. They are

,

and a Klein bottle.

For dimension

3

For dimension

4

1.5.
Noncommutative Fundamental Groups

To study closed self-covering manifolds with general, i.e. unnecessarily commutative, fundamentals groups, we propose the following question which is a generalization of Question
1.1
. Recall that an aspherical manifold is a manifold with a contractible universal cover.

Question 1.12
.

Suppose
M
is a closed
\mathrm{CAT}
manifold,
q\colon M^{\prime}\rightarrow M
is a nontrivial covering, and
h\colon M\rightarrow M^{\prime}
is a homotopy equivalence preserving base points.

Are there a closed aspherical
\mathrm{CAT}
manifold
B
, a
\mathrm{CAT}
bundle projection
p\colon M\rightarrow B
and a
\mathrm{CAT}
covering map
\psi
such that the following diagram is a homotopy pullback?

(1.3)

\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\scriptstyle{q\circ h}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi}
\textstyle{B}

Here
\psi
is required to preserve base points and to satisfy the condition
\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}\psi_{\#}^{k}=1
.

By “a
\mathrm{CAT}
covering map”, we mean
\psi
is a composition
B\rightarrow B^{\prime}\rightarrow B
, where
B^{\prime}
is a cover of
B
, and
B\rightarrow B^{\prime}
is a
\mathrm{CAT}
isomorphism. Since the answer to Question
1.1
is no in general, the answer to Question
1.12
has to be negative. We still wish to get some positive answers by imposing mild extra assumptions. First of all, we wonder if the answer to Question
1.12
is affirmative in a weaker sense. If there was a
p
fitting (
1.3
) well, then the homotopy fiber of
p
would be homotopy equivalent to the cover
\overline{M}
of
M
such that
\pi_{1}(\overline{M})=\ker p_{\#}
. Since (
1.3
) was a homotopy pullback, we would have
h_{\#}\ker p_{\#}=\ker p_{\#}
. By
\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}\psi_{\#}^{k}=1
, we infer
\ker p_{\#}=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}h_{\#}^{k}
. Again, here we abuse the notation
h_{\#}
to denote the composition

(qh)_{\#}\colon\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(M^{\prime})\hookrightarrow\pi_{1}(M).

This yields an obvious necessary condition:

Question 1.13
.

Let
G:=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}h_{\#}^{k}
. Let
\overline{M}
be the cover of
M
with
\pi_{1}(\overline{M})=G
. Is
G
normal in
\pi_{1}(M)
? And is
\overline{M}
finitely dominated?

If the answer to Question
1.12
was yes in the homotopy sense, similar to Theorem
C
, the answer to Question
1.13
would be positive. In particular,
G
would be a finitely presented normal subgroup of
\pi_{1}(M)
. However, the following Theorem
N
shows this is not true even if
h
is a diffeomorphism. In this theorem,
M
is oriented, the orientation of
M^{\prime}
is induced from that of
M
via the covering map.

Theorem N
.

Let
BS(2,3)=\langle a,t\mid ta^{2}t^{-1}=a^{3}\rangle
be a Baumslag-Solitar group. For each
m\geq 5
, there exist a compact smooth hypersurface
M
(without boundary) in
\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
and a (
C^{\infty}
) diffeomorphism
h\colon M\rightarrow M^{\prime}
satisfying the following properties:

(1)

\pi_{1}(M)=BS(2,3)
, and
M^{\prime}
is a
5
-cover of
M
.

(2)

h
preserves base points and can be arranged to preserve or reverse orientations as one wishes.

(3)

G:=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}h_{\#}^{k}
is not a normal subgroup of
\pi_{1}(M)
.

(4)

G
is a free group with infinite rank, hence it is not finitely generated.

Furthermore, there exist a compact smooth hypersurface
M
in
\mathbb{R}^{5}
and a simple homotopy equivalence
h\colon M\rightarrow M^{\prime}
satisfying the above properties.

Remark 1.14
.

Suppose
q_{1}<q_{2}
are two positive prime numbers, and
s>1
is an integer prime to
q_{1}q_{2}
. Similarly, there is a desired triple
(M,M^{\prime},h)
as the one in Theorem
N
except that
\pi_{1}(M)=BS(q_{1},q_{2})
and
M^{\prime}
is an
s
-cover of
M
. See Remark
10.9
.

Remark 1.15
.

According to the (4) in Theorem
N
, the covering manifold
\overline{M}
of
M
with
\pi_{1}(\overline{M})=G
is far from being finitely dominated. Hence, the conclusion of Theorem
A
would fail dramatically when the fundamental group is nonabelian.

By Theorem
N
, to find any positive answer to Question
1.12
, one has to at least impose the condition that
\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}h_{\#}^{k}
is a finitely presented normal subgroup of
\pi_{1}(M)
. We are not able to make further progress on Question
1.12
at this stage. Nevertheless, we shall raise a program together with a few questions on it in Section
11
.

Theorem
N
follows from Theorems
O
and
P
which are general constructions of self-covering manifolds out of self-covering complexes. They reflect a diversity of the self-covering phenomena in the category of closed manifolds. In these theorems,
\pi_{1}(M)
is identified with
\pi_{1}(X)
by a fixed isomorphism, the subgroups
\pi_{1}(M^{\prime})
and
\pi_{1}(X^{\prime})
are then identified.

Theorem O
.

Suppose
X
is an
r
-dimensional finitely dominated CW complex,
X^{\prime}
is a
k
-cover of
X
for some
k>1
. Suppose
\hat{h}\colon X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
is a homotopy equivalence preserving base points. Then for each integer
m\geq 2r+12
, there exist a compact smooth hypersurface
M
in
\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
and a (
C^{\infty}
) diffeomorphism
h\colon M\rightarrow M^{\prime}
satisfying the following properties:

(1)

\pi_{1}(M)=\pi_{1}(X)
, and
M^{\prime}
is a
k
-cover of
M
with
\pi_{1}(M^{\prime})=\pi_{1}(X^{\prime})
.

(2)

h
preserves base points and
h_{\#}\colon\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(M^{\prime})
equals
\hat{h}_{\#}\colon\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(X^{\prime})
. Additionally,
h
can be arranged to preserve or reverse orientations as one wishes.

Furthermore, if
X
is the underlying space of a finite simplicial complex and
\hat{h}
is a simple homotopy equivalence, then the restriction on the above
m
can be weakened as
m\geq\max\{2r,r+2,5\}
.

Theorem P
.

Suppose
X
is the underlying space of a
2
-dimensional finite simplicial complex,
X^{\prime}
is a
k
-cover of
X
for some
k>1
. Suppose
\hat{h}\colon X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
is a homotopy (resp. simple homotopy) equivalence preserving base points. Then there exist a compact smooth hypersurface
M
in
\mathbb{R}^{5}
and a homotopy (resp. simple homotopy) equivalence
h\colon M\rightarrow M^{\prime}
such that
(M,M^{\prime},h)
satisfies the (1) and (2) in Theorem
O
.

Furthermore, if
\hat{h}
is a simple homotopy equivalence and
\pi_{1}(X)
is good in the sense of
[
30
]
, then
h
is a homeomorphism.

A canonical application of Theorems
O
and
P
is to set
X
as an Eilenberg-MacLane space
K(\pi,1)
.

1.6.
Algebraic Foundation

We convert Theorem
A
to the following algebraic Theorem
Q
on finite generation of modules. This theorem may be of independent interest.

Assumption 1.16
.

Let
A=(a_{ij})_{n\times n}\in\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
, where elements in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
are considered as column vectors and the action of
A
on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is by left multiplication. Suppose
\det(A)\neq 0
and
\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}A^{k}=0
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring and
S=R[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}]
, where
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
are indeterminates.

Assumption 1.17
.

Suppose
\phi\colon S\rightarrow S
is a ring monomorphism such that
\phi(R)=R
. Suppose further,
\forall j
,
\phi(t_{j})=g_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{a_{ij}}
, where
(a_{ij})_{n\times n}=A
and
g_{j}
are units of
R
.

Theorem Q
.

Under the Assumptions
1.16
and
1.17
, assume further
\mathfrak{M}
is a finite
S
-module. Suppose there exists a group isomorphism
\eta\colon\mathfrak{M}\rightarrow\mathfrak{M}
such that,
\forall s\in S
,
\forall x\in\mathfrak{M}
,
\eta(sx)=\phi(s)\eta(x)
. Then
\mathfrak{M}
is a finite
R
-module.

Here we say
\eta
is a
\phi
-twisted
homomorphism of
S
-modules.

2.
Algebraic Finiteness

In this section, we shall prove Theorem
Q
. Our argument is based on Propositions
3.1
and
4.1
which will be proved later.

We introduce a technique of iteration which will be frequently used. For each integer
m>0
, we have
\eta^{m}\colon\mathfrak{M}\rightarrow\mathfrak{M}
is a group isomorphism such that,
\forall x\in\mathfrak{M}
,
\forall s\in S
,
\eta^{m}(sx)=\phi^{m}(s)\eta^{m}(x)
. Obviously,
\phi^{m}\colon S\rightarrow S
is also a ring monomorphism with
\phi^{m}(R)=R
. Furthermore, it’s easy to check that,
\forall j
,
\phi^{m}(t_{j})=g_{m,j}\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{b_{ij}}
, where
(b_{ij})_{n\times n}=A^{m}
and
g_{m,j}
are units of
R
. Clearly,
\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}A^{mk}=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}A^{k}=0
. Therefore,
(\eta^{m},\phi^{m},A^{m})
essentially satisfies the same assumption as
(\eta,\phi,A)
does. To prove Theorem
Q
, we may consider
(\eta^{m},\phi^{m},A^{m})
for some suitable
m>0
.

Let’s assume
\mathfrak{M}\neq 0
. Otherwise, the conclusion of Theorem
Q
is already true. Recall that a prime ideal
Q
of
S
is an associated prime ideal of
\mathfrak{M}
if there exists
x\in\mathfrak{M}
such that the annihilator
\mathrm{Ann}_{S}(x)=Q
. Let
\mathrm{Ass}_{S}(\mathfrak{M})
be the set of prime ideals of
S
associated to
\mathfrak{M}
. Since
\mathfrak{M}
is a finite
S
-module, the set
\mathrm{Ass}_{S}(\mathfrak{M})
is nonempty and finite (cf.
[
53
, (7.B) & (7.G)]
). By
[
53
, (9.A)]
,

(2.1)

\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(\mathfrak{M})=\{Q\cap R\mid Q\in\mathrm{Ass}_{S}(\mathfrak{M})\}.

Thus
\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(\mathfrak{M})
is also nonempty and finite.

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists an integer
m>0
such that
\phi^{-m}(Q)=Q
and
\phi^{-m}(P)=P
for all
Q\in\mathrm{Ass}_{S}(\mathfrak{M})
and
P\in\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(\mathfrak{M})
.

Proof.

Since
\phi\colon S\rightarrow\phi(S)
is a ring isomorphism,
\eta\colon(\mathfrak{M},S)\rightarrow(\mathfrak{M},\phi(S))
is a
\phi
-twisted isomorphism of modules. Thus
\phi^{-1}
induces a bijection
\phi^{-1}\colon\mathrm{Ass}_{\phi(S)}(\mathfrak{M})\rightarrow\mathrm{Ass}_{S}(\mathfrak{M})
. On the other hand,
\phi(S)\subseteq S
, by
[
53
, (9.A)]
,

\mathrm{Ass}_{\phi(S)}(\mathfrak{M})=\{Q\cap\phi(S)\mid Q\in\mathrm{Ass}_{S}(\mathfrak{M})\}.

Since
\mathrm{Ass}_{S}(\mathfrak{M})
is finite, then
\phi^{-1}
induces a bijection
\phi^{-1}\colon\mathrm{Ass}_{S}(\mathfrak{M})\rightarrow\mathrm{Ass}_{S}(\mathfrak{M})
, and
\phi^{-m}
is the identity on
\mathrm{Ass}_{S}(\mathfrak{M})
for some
m>0
. In other words,
\phi^{-m}(Q)=Q
for all
Q\in\mathrm{Ass}_{S}(\mathfrak{M})
. By (
2.1
),
\phi^{-m}(P)=P
for all
P\in\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(\mathfrak{M})
.
∎

We firstly prove a special and important case of Theorem
Q
.

Proposition 2.2
.

In Theorem
Q
, assuming further
R=K
for some field
K
, then the conclusion holds, i.e.
\dim_{K}\mathfrak{M}
is finite.

Proof.

Let’s assume
\mathfrak{M}\neq 0
. By Proposition
2.4
below, it suffices to show
Q
is maximal for all
Q\in\mathrm{Ass}_{S}(\mathfrak{M})
. Fix a
Q\in\mathrm{Ass}_{S}(\mathfrak{M})
. By Lemma
2.1
, replacing
\phi
with its power if necessary, we may assume
\phi^{-1}(Q)=Q
. Let
\kappa(Q):=\mathrm{Quot}(S/Q)
be the residue field of
S
at
Q
. Then
\phi
induces a field embedding
\phi^{\prime\prime}\colon\kappa(Q)\rightarrow\kappa(Q)
.

We prove
Q
is maximal by contradiction. Suppose not, taking the
\phi^{\prime}
in Proposition
4.1
as
\phi
here, we would have
[\kappa(Q):\mathrm{im}\phi^{\prime\prime}]>1
. Note that
\kappa(Q)\otimes_{S}\mathfrak{M}
is finite dimensional over
\kappa(Q)
because
\mathfrak{M}
is a finite
S
-module. On one hand, by Lemma
2.3
below,

(2.2)

\dim_{\mathrm{im}\phi^{\prime\prime}}\mathrm{im}\phi^{\prime\prime}\otimes_{\phi(S)}\mathfrak{M}\geq[\kappa(Q):\mathrm{im}\phi^{\prime\prime}]\cdot\dim_{\kappa(Q)}\kappa(Q)\otimes_{S}\mathfrak{M}.

On the other hand, by assumption,
\phi\colon S\rightarrow\phi(S)
is a ring isomorphism,
\eta\colon\mathfrak{M}\rightarrow\mathfrak{M}
is a group isomorphism, and
\eta(sx)=\phi(s)\eta(x)
for all
s\in S
and
x\in\mathfrak{M}
. Thus

\phi^{\prime\prime}\otimes\eta\colon\kappa(Q)\otimes_{S}\mathfrak{M}\rightarrow\mathrm{im}\phi^{\prime\prime}\otimes_{\phi(S)}\mathfrak{M}

is a
\phi^{\prime\prime}
-twisted isomorphism, which implies

(2.3)

\dim_{\kappa(Q)}\kappa(Q)\otimes_{S}\mathfrak{M}=\dim_{\mathrm{im}\phi^{\prime\prime}}\mathrm{im}\phi^{\prime\prime}\otimes_{\phi(S)}\mathfrak{M}.

Combining (
2.2
), (
2.3
), and the fact
[\kappa(Q):\mathrm{im}\phi^{\prime\prime}]>1
, we infer
\dim_{\kappa(Q)}\kappa(Q)\otimes_{S}\mathfrak{M}=0
which contradicts the fact
Q\in\mathrm{Ass}_{S}(\mathfrak{M})
(cf.
[
53
, (7.D)]
).
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

The inequality (
2.2
) holds.

Proof.

Firstly, we claim that a basis of
\kappa(Q)\otimes_{S}\mathfrak{M}
over
\kappa(Q)
can be chosen as
1\otimes x_{1},\dots,1\otimes x_{m}
for some
x_{j}\in\mathfrak{M}
. Actually, if
y_{1},\dots,y_{m}
is a basis, then

y_{j}=\sum_{h}\tfrac{n_{h}}{d_{h}}\otimes z_{h}

for some
n_{h}
and
d_{h}
in
S/Q
and
z_{h}\in\mathfrak{M}
. Multiplying
y_{j}
by a nonzero element in
S/Q
, we may assume each
d_{h}=1
and each
n_{h}
can be represented by an
r_{h}\in S
. Then

y_{j}=\sum_{h}n_{h}\otimes z_{h}=\sum_{h}1\otimes r_{h}z_{h}=1\otimes\sum_{h}r_{h}z_{h}=1\otimes x_{j}.

Secondly, suppose
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}
are a family of elements of
\kappa(Q)
which are linear independent over
\mathrm{im}\phi^{\prime\prime}
. To finish the proof, it suffices to verify
\dim_{\mathrm{im}\phi^{\prime\prime}}\mathrm{im}\phi^{\prime\prime}\otimes_{\phi(S)}\mathfrak{M}\geq km
.

Multiplying the whole family
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}
by a nonzero element in
\kappa(Q)
if necessary, we may assume they are represented by
s_{1},\dots,s_{k}
in
S
. We claim that
1\otimes s_{i}x_{j}
(
1\leq i\leq k
,
1\leq j\leq m
) are linear independent in
\mathrm{im}\phi^{\prime\prime}\otimes_{\phi(S)}\mathfrak{M}
over
\mathrm{im}\phi^{\prime\prime}
. Actually, suppose

\sum_{i,j}l_{ij}\cdot 1\otimes_{\phi(S)}s_{i}x_{j}=0

in
\mathrm{im}\phi^{\prime\prime}\otimes_{\phi(S)}\mathfrak{M}
for some
l_{ij}\in\mathrm{im}\phi^{\prime\prime}
. Since
\phi(S)\subseteq S
, we see

\sum_{j}\left(\sum_{i}l_{ij}\alpha_{i}\right)\cdot 1\otimes_{S}x_{j}=\sum_{i,j}l_{ij}\cdot 1\otimes_{S}s_{i}x_{j}=0

in
\kappa(Q)\otimes_{S}\mathfrak{M}
, which implies
\sum_{i}l_{ij}\alpha_{i}=0
in
\kappa(Q)
for all
j
and hence all
l_{ij}=0
. Therefore,
\dim_{\mathrm{im}\phi^{\prime\prime}}\mathrm{im}\phi^{\prime\prime}\otimes_{\phi(S)}\mathfrak{M}\geq km
, which completes the proof.
∎

Now we prove Theorem
Q
.

Proof of Theorem
Q
.

Again, we assume
\mathfrak{M}\neq 0
. By
[
64
, Theorem G]
, it suffices to verify two facts: firstly,
\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{M}
is finite dimensional over
\kappa(P)
for all
P\in\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(\mathfrak{M})
; secondly, the eigenvalues of the actions of
t_{i}^{\pm 1}
on
\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{M}
are integral over
R/P
for all
P\in\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(\mathfrak{M})
and
1\leq i\leq n
. Here
\kappa(P)
is the residue field of
R
at
P
.

We firstly prove
\dim_{\kappa(P)}\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{M}
is finite. By Lemma
2.1
again, replacing
\phi
with its power if necessary, we may assume
\phi^{-1}(P)=P
. Since
\phi|_{R}\in\mathrm{Aut}(R)
, it induces an automorphism
\sigma
of
R/P
. This
\sigma
extends to an automorphism of
\kappa(P)
which is still denoted by
\sigma
. Clearly,

\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}S=\kappa(P)[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}],

where
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
are indeterminates over
\kappa(P)
. Since
\mathfrak{M}
is a finite
S
-module,
\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{M}
is a finite
\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}S
-module. By Assumption
1.17
, we see

\sigma\otimes\phi\colon\ \kappa(P)\otimes_{R}S\rightarrow\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}S

is a ring monomorphism such that
(\sigma\otimes\phi)|_{\kappa(P)}=\sigma
and
\forall j
,
\sigma\otimes\phi(t_{j})=g_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{a_{ij}}
, where we use the same notation
g_{j}
to denote its image in
\kappa(P)
. Similarly,

\sigma\otimes\eta\colon\ \kappa(P)\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{M}\rightarrow\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{M},

is a
\sigma\otimes\phi
-twisted isomorphism of
\kappa(P)
-modules. Taking the
(\mathfrak{M},K,\phi,\eta)
in Proposition
2.2
as
(\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{M},\kappa(P),\sigma\otimes\phi,\sigma\otimes\eta)
here, we infer
\dim_{\kappa(P)}\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{M}
is finite.

It remains to show that the eigenvalues of the action of
t_{j}^{\pm 1}
on
\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{M}
are integral over
R/P
. By the assumption that
\eta(sx)=\phi(s)\eta(x)
, we have the equation of actions

\sigma\otimes\eta\cdot t_{j}=g_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{a_{ij}}\cdot\sigma\otimes\eta

or

(2.4)

t_{j}=(\sigma\otimes\eta)^{-1}\cdot g_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{a_{ij}}\cdot\sigma\otimes\eta=\sigma^{-1}(g_{j})\cdot(\sigma\otimes\eta)^{-1}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{a_{ij}}\cdot\sigma\otimes\eta

on
\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{M}
. Here the action of
t_{j}
is actually the action of
1\otimes t_{j}
, and the one of
g_{j}
is the multiplication with
1\otimes g_{j}
or
g_{j}\otimes 1
(here
g_{j}
is identified with its image in
\kappa(P)
). Note that the actions of
t_{j}
and
g_{j}
are
\kappa(P)
-linear, while
\sigma\otimes\eta
is only
\sigma
-twisted
\kappa(P)
-linear. To remedy this
\sigma
-twisted trouble, we argue as follows. Choosing a basis,
\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{M}
is identified
\kappa(P)^{m}
for some
m>0
. Consider
t_{j}
,
g_{j}
,
\sigma\otimes\eta
as maps on
\kappa(P)^{m}
. Define

\Phi_{\sigma}\colon\kappa(P)^{m}\to\kappa(P)^{m},\ \ \ (z_{1},\dots,z_{m})^{T}\mapsto(\sigma(z_{1}),\dots,\sigma(z_{m}))^{T},

where
(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})^{T}
is the transpose of
(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})
. By (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{\sigma}t_{j}\Phi_{\sigma}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{j}\cdot\left(\Phi_{\sigma}(\sigma\otimes\eta)^{-1}\right)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{a_{ij}}\cdot\left((\sigma\otimes\eta)\Phi_{\sigma}^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{j}\cdot\left(\Phi_{\sigma}(\sigma\otimes\eta)^{-1}\right)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{a_{ij}}\cdot\left(\Phi_{\sigma}(\sigma\otimes\eta)^{-1}\right)^{-1}.

Now
t_{i}
,
\Phi_{\sigma}t_{j}\Phi_{\sigma}^{-1}
, and
\Phi_{\sigma}(\sigma\otimes\eta)^{-1}
are invertible
\kappa(P)
-linear transformations on
\kappa(P)^{m}
and hence can be identified with invertible matrices over
\kappa(P)
. If
t_{j}
is represented by a matrix

, then
\Phi_{\sigma}t_{j}\Phi_{\sigma}^{-1}
is represented by
\sigma(B_{j})
. Note also that

is a unit in

, it hence represents a unit in
R/P
. Taking the

,
B_{j}
,

The following proposition is a general and elementary fact in commutative algebra.

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose
K
is a field,
K\subseteq S^{\prime}
and
S^{\prime}
is a finitely generated
K
-algebra. Suppose
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
is a nonzero finite
S^{\prime}
-module. Then
\dim_{K}\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
is finite if and only if
Q
is maximal for each
Q\in\mathrm{Ass}_{S^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

“
\Leftarrow
” By primary decomposition, we have
0=\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{M}_{i}
, where
\mathfrak{M}_{i}
is a
Q_{i}
-primary submodule of
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
, i.e.
\mathrm{Ass}_{S^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}/\mathfrak{M}_{i})=Q_{i}
, and
\mathrm{Ass}_{S^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime})=\{Q_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq k\}
(see
[
53
, (8.G)]
). Then there is a diagonal monomorphism of
S^{\prime}
-module

\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}/\mathfrak{M}_{i}.

Thus it suffices to show
\dim_{K}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}/\mathfrak{M}_{i})<\infty
for all
i
. We may, therefore, assume
\mathrm{Ass}_{S^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime})=\{Q\}
. Then
\sqrt{\mathrm{Ann}_{S^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime})}=Q
(see
[
54
, Theorem 6.6]
). Since
Q
is maximal, we infer
S^{\prime}/Q
is a finite algebraic extension of
K
(cf.
[
58
, I. §1]
). By
[
3
, Proposition 8.6]
,
S^{\prime}/\mathrm{Ann}_{S^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime})
is an Artinian local ring with the maximal ideal
Q/\mathrm{Ann}_{S^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime})
, which further implies that
S^{\prime}/\mathrm{Ann}_{S^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime})
is a finite dimensional
K
-algebra. On the other hand,
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
is a finite
S^{\prime}/\mathrm{Ann}_{S^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime})
-module, which implies the conclusion.

“
\Rightarrow
” Suppose

. There exists

such that
\mathrm{Ann}_{S^{\prime}}(x)=Q
. Since

is an

-submodule of
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
, we have
\dim_{K}S^{\prime}x\leq\dim_{K}\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}<\infty
. On the other hand,
S^{\prime}x\cong S^{\prime}/Q
. So
S^{\prime}/Q
is an algebraic extension of

, which means

is maximal.
∎

Let

In this section, we shall prove the non-fibering Theorem

Lemma 9.1
.

Suppose

is a closed
\mathrm{TOP}
manifold homotopy equivalent to
\mathbb{T}^{n}\times S^{2}
or
\mathbb{T}^{n}\times S^{3}
. If

is a
\mathrm{TOP}
bundle over
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, then

is smoothable and the rational Pontryagin class

.

Proof.

By the assumption and the solution to Poincaré conjecture,
M
is a
\mathrm{TOP}

S^{i}
-bundle over
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, where
i=2
or
3
.

We know the inclusion
\mathrm{O}(i+1)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Homeo}(S^{i})
is a homotopy equivalence, where
\mathrm{Homeo}(S^{i})
is the group of self-homeomorphims of
S^{i}
. For
i=2
, this fact had been proved by Kneser
[
48
]
(see also
[
31
]
). For
i=3
, this is a consequence of the combination of Cerf’s
[
11
, 3.2.1]
and Hatcher’s
[
40
]
.

Therefore,
M
is homeomorphic to the sphere bundle associated to a smooth vector bundle over
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with fiber
\mathbb{R}^{i+1}
, let alone it is smoothable.

Since rational Pontryagin classes are topological invariants (see
[
61
]
and the Theorem 5.3 in
[
46
, p. 317]
), to analyze
p_{2}(M)
, we may assume
M
itself is a smooth bundle
p\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{n}
with fiber
S^{i}
. The tangent bundle
TM
of
M
has a decomposition

TM\cong\zeta\oplus p^{*}T\mathbb{T}^{n},

where
\zeta
is the vertical vector bundle over
M
whose fiber is tangent to the fiber of
p
. Since
T\mathbb{T}^{n}
is trivial,
p_{2}(M)=p_{2}(TM)=p_{2}(\zeta)
. Since the fiber of
\zeta
is
\mathbb{R}^{i}
with

, we infer
p_{2}(\zeta)=0
and hence
p_{2}(M)=0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
M
.

Let’s firstly assume
n\geq 4
. The Spivak normal fibration of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is trivial. Consider
\mathrm{TOP}
surgery problems with target
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Each element of
[\mathbb{T}^{n},\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{TOP}]
is represented by a surgery problem

(f,b)\colon\ \ (N,\nu N)\rightarrow(\mathbb{T}^{n},\xi),

where
N
is a closed
\mathrm{TOP}
manifold with stable normal bundle
\nu N
,
\xi
is a
\mathrm{TOP}
Euclidean space bundle over
\mathbb{T}^{n}
whose image in
B\mathrm{G}
is trivial,
f\colon N\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{n}
is a degree
1
map, and

is a bundle map covering
f
(see e.g.
[
67
, Proposition 9.43]
). By the Theorem 15.1 in
[
46
, p. 328]
, there is an epimorphism

\Delta_{*}\colon\ \ [\mathbb{T}^{n},\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{TOP}]\rightarrow H^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{Z}/2)

such that
\Delta_{*}
maps the element represented by the above
(f,b)
to the Kirby-Siebenmann invariant
\mathrm{ks}(\xi)
of
\xi
. We have

since

. Thus we may choose a surgery problem
(f,b)
with
\mathrm{ks}(\xi)\neq 0
.

By the product formula for surgery obstruction (see e.g.
[
57
, Theorem IV.1.1]
), the surgery obstruction of

(f\times 1,b\times 1)\colon(N\times S^{i},\nu(N\times S^{i}))\rightarrow(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times S^{i},\xi\times\nu S^{i})

vanishes, where

or

. Thus we obtain a homotopy equivalence
\Phi\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{n}\times S^{i}
such that
M
is a closed
\mathrm{TOP}
manifold with
\nu M\cong\Phi^{*}(\xi\times\nu S^{i})
. Since
\nu S^{i}
is trivial and
\Phi
induces an isomorphism
\Phi^{*}
of cohomologies, we have

\mathrm{ks}(\nu M)=\Phi^{*}(\mathrm{ks}(\xi\times\nu S^{i}))=\Phi^{*}(\mathrm{ks}(\xi)\times 1)\neq 0.

So this

is not smoothable. By Lemma

, it is not a
\mathrm{TOP}
bundle over
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. We obtain the desired

for

.

Now let’s assume

. The desired

needs to be a
\mathrm{DIFF}
manifold. The argument almost duplicates the above. We consider
\mathrm{DIFF}
surgery problems and analyze rational Pontryagin classes. The Pontryagin character provides a well-known isomorphism (which follows immediately from the property of the Chern character, cf.
[
2
, p. 19]
)

[\mathbb{T}^{n},B\mathrm{O}]\otimes\mathbb{Q}\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\infty}H^{4j}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{Q}).

Since

, there is a vector bundle
\eta
over
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with rational Pontryagin classes

and
p_{2}(\eta)\neq 0
. We also have the isomorphism (see e.g.
[
67
, § 9.2]
)

[\mathbb{T}^{n},\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{O}]\otimes\mathbb{Q}\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}[\mathbb{T}^{n},B\mathrm{O}]\otimes\mathbb{Q}

which is induced by the inclusion
\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{O}\rightarrow B\mathrm{O}
. Each element of
[\mathbb{T}^{n},\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{O}]
is represented by a
\mathrm{DIFF}
surgery problem. So we can get a
\mathrm{DIFF}
surgery problem with target
(\mathbb{T}^{n},\xi)
such that

is a vector bundle and

in
[\mathbb{T}^{n},B\mathrm{O}]
for some integer

. Similar to the above, we obtain a homotopy equivalence

such that

is a closed
\mathrm{DIFF}
manifold with

By Lemma

again,

is not a
\mathrm{TOP}
bundle over
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.
∎

Let
\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{6}]=\{\frac{m}{6^{n}}\mid m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}

The following lemma is well-known and easy.

Neither

is an algebraic integer. Taking the

∎

Lemma 10.4
.

There exists a monomorphism
f\colon Q(2,3)\rightarrow Q(2,3)
such that
f(x,y)=(-5x,y)
and
[Q(2,3):\mathrm{im}f]=5
. Furthermore,
\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}f^{k}=0\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

By a straightforward calculation, we see
f
is a well-defined monomorphism and
\mathrm{im}f=5\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{6}]\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
.

To prove
[Q(2,3):\mathrm{im}f]=5
, it suffices to show
[\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{6}]:5\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{6}]]=5
. By

\tfrac{1}{6^{n}}-\tfrac{1}{6^{n-1}}=-\tfrac{5}{6^{n}},

we infer
\tfrac{m}{6^{n}}+5\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{6}]=m+5\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{6}]
and hence
[\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{6}]:5\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{6}]]\leq 5
. Since
5
is prime to
6
, we see integers
i
, for
0\leq i\leq 4
, are in distinct cosets of
\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{6}]/5\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{6}]
. Thus
[\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{6}]:5\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{6}]]=5
.

The fact
\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}f^{k}=0\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
is also because
5
is prime to
6
.
∎

Lemma 10.5
.

Suppose
\psi\colon G_{1}\rightarrow G_{2}
is an epimorphism of groups,
H_{i}\leq G_{i}
,
\psi(H_{1})=H_{2}
and
[G_{2}:H_{2}]<\infty
. Then
[G_{1}:H_{1}]=[G_{2}:H_{2}]
if and only if
\psi^{-1}(H_{2})=H_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
H_{3}=\psi^{-1}(H_{2})
, then
H_{1}\leq H_{3}
. Since
\psi
is surjective, it induces a bijection
G_{1}/H_{3}\rightarrow G_{2}/H_{2}
. So

[G_{1}:H_{1}]\geq[G_{1}:H_{3}]=[G_{2}:H_{2}].

It’s easy to see the conclusion holds.
∎

Lemma 10.6
.

The following diagram commutes.

\textstyle{BS(2,3)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\hat{h}_{\#}}
\textstyle{BS(2,3)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{Q(2,3)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{Q(2,3)}

Furthermore, for all integers
k>0
,
\varphi(\mathrm{im}\hat{h}_{\#}^{k})=\mathrm{im}f^{k}
, and
\ker\varphi\leq\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}\hat{h}_{\#}^{k}
.

Proof.

The commutativity is trivial. By Lemma
10.2
,
\varphi
is surjective,
\varphi\mathrm{im}\hat{h}_{\#}^{k}=\mathrm{im}f^{k}
. By Proposition
10.1
and Lemma
10.4
,

[BS(2,3):\mathrm{im}\hat{h}_{\#}]=[Q(2,3):\mathrm{im}f]=5.

By Lemma
10.5
, we have
\varphi^{-1}(\mathrm{im}f)=\mathrm{im}\hat{h}_{\#}
. Thus
\ker\varphi\leq\mathrm{im}\hat{h}_{\#}
.

Suppose
x\in\ker\varphi
. Then there is a
y\in BS(2,3)
such that
x=\hat{h}_{\#}(y)
. By the above diagram,
f\varphi(y)=\varphi\hat{h}_{\#}(y)=\varphi(x)=0
. By Lemma
10.4
,
f
is injective, we see
\varphi(y)=0
, i.e.
y\in\ker\varphi\leq\mathrm{im}\hat{h}_{\#}
. Thus
x\in\mathrm{im}\hat{h}_{\#}^{2}
. By induction, we infer
\ker\varphi\leq\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}\hat{h}_{\#}^{k}
.
∎

Proposition 10.7
.

We have
\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}\hat{h}_{\#}^{k}=\varphi^{-1}(0\rtimes\mathbb{Z})=\ker\varphi\rtimes\langle t\rangle
, and it is not a normal subgroup of
BS(2,3)
.

Proof.

By Lemmas
10.4
and
10.6
, we have

\varphi\left(\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}\hat{h}_{\#}^{k}\right)\leq\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\varphi(\mathrm{im}\hat{h}_{\#}^{k})=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}f^{k}=0\rtimes\mathbb{Z}.

Clearly,
\langle t\rangle\leq\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}\hat{h}_{\#}^{k}
and
\varphi(\langle t\rangle)=0\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
. Thus
\varphi(\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}\hat{h}_{\#}^{k})=0\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
.

By Lemma
10.6
,
\ker\varphi\leq\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}\hat{h}_{\#}^{k}
, we infer
\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}\hat{h}_{\#}^{k}=\varphi^{-1}(0\rtimes\mathbb{Z})
. Since
\ker\varphi
is normal in
BS(2,3)
,
\varphi|_{\langle t\rangle}
is injective, and
\varphi\langle t\rangle=0\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
, we see
\varphi^{-1}(0\rtimes\mathbb{Z})=\ker\varphi\rtimes\langle t\rangle
. Since

is not normal in
Q(2,3)
, nor is
\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}\hat{h}_{\#}^{k}
in

.
∎

Proposition 10.8
.

The group
\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{im}\hat{h}_{\#}^{k}
is a free group with infinite rank.

Proof.

Taking the
(x,y)
in
[

as
(0,1)
, we have

It remains to show

Let

and

Conjecture 11.7

Every finitely dominated aspherical Poincaré spaces is homotopy equivalent to a closed

Suppose

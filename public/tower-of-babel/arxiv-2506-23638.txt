Simple Approximations for General Spanner Problems
Fritz Bökler fritz.boekler@uos.de, Markus Chimani markus.chimani@uos.de, Henning Jasper henning.jasper@uos.de

Abstract
Consider a (possibly directed) graph with n n nodes and m m edges, independent edge weights and lengths, and arbitrary distance demands for node pairs. The spanner problem asks for a minimum-weight subgraph that satisfies these demands via sufficiently short paths w.r.t. the edge lengths. For multiplicative α \alpha -spanners (where demands equal α \alpha times the original distances) and assuming that each edge’s weight equals its length, the simple Greedy heuristic by Althöfer et al. (1993) is known to yield strong solutions, both in theory and practice. To obtain guarantees in more general settings, recent approximations typically abandon this simplicity and practicality. Still, so far, there is no known non-trivial approximation algorithm for the spanner problem in its most general form. We provide two surprisingly simple approximations algorithms. In general, our AugmentedGreedy achieves the first unconditional approximation ratio of m m , which is non-trivial due to the independence of weights and lengths. Crucially, it maintains all size and weight guarantees Greedy is known for, i.e., in the aforementioned multiplicative α \alpha -spanner scenario and even for additive + β +\beta -spanners . Further, it generalizes some of these size guarantees to derive new weight guarantees. Our second approach, RandomizedRounding , establishes a graph transformation that allows a simple rounding scheme over a standard multicommodity flow LP. It yields an 𝒪 ⁡ ( n ​ log ⁡ n ) \mathcal{O}(n\log n) -approximation, assuming integer lengths and polynomially bounded distance demands. The only other known approximation guarantee in this general setting requires several complex subalgorithms and analyses, yet we match it up to a factor of 𝒪 ⁡ ( n 1 / 5 − ε ) \mathcal{O}(n^{1/5-\varepsilon}) using standard tools. Further, on bounded-degree graphs, we yield the first 𝒪 ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) \mathcal{O}(\log n) approximation ratio for constant-bounded distance demands (beyond undirected multiplicative 2 2 -spanners in unit-length graphs).

Preliminaries

Generally,
shortest
means minimum length.
In undirected settings, unordered terminal pairs
K\subseteq\binom{V}{2}
suffice.
With
OPT
we denote the optimum solution value of the considered instance.
We define the maximum length
\bar{\ell}\coloneqq\max_{e\in E}\ell(e)
, weight
\bar{w}\coloneqq\max_{e\in E}w(e)
, distance demand
\bar{\delta}\coloneqq\max_{(u,v)\in K}\delta(u,v)
and (out)degree
\Delta\coloneqq\max_{q\in V}|\{(q,t)\mid(q,t)\in E\}|
.
We can assume w.l.o.g.
\bar{\ell}\leq\bar{\delta}\leq n\bar{\ell}
, as longer edges cannot be used to satisfy any distance demands and larger demands are trivially satisfied in connected graphs.
For a function
f\colon E\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}
, we use the shorthands
f(u,v)\coloneqq f((u,v))
for
(u,v)\in E
,
f(X)\coloneqq\sum_{e\in X}f(e)
for
X\subseteq E
, and
f(G^{\prime})\coloneqq f(E^{\prime})
for
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})\subseteq G
.
Let
\text{LP}(x,y,f)
denote the running time to solve an LP with
\Theta(x)
variables and
\Theta(y)
constraints;
thereby the maximum encoding-length of any coefficient does not exceed that of the largest rational in the image of function
f
.

Enforcing distance demands
\delta(u,v)=\alpha\cdot d_{G}^{\ell}(u,v)
, for all
adjacent
node pairs
(u,v)\in E=K
guarantees feasible multiplicative
\alpha
-spanners
[
43
]
.
Further, it is folklore that non-metric edges can be removed from
coupled

\alpha
-spanner instances, as they cannot be part of an optimum solution.
However, in decoupled settings, non-metric edges
cannot
be removed:
Consider an undirected triangle, where two of the edges have unit weight and length, while the third has weight

and (non-metric) length

The optimum
4

,

Yet, the following Observation allows us to disregard non-metric distance demands in decoupled freeform spanner problems.

In the
demand graph

,
two nodes are adjacent if there is a distance demand between them. Then
d_{D}^{\delta}(u,v)
is the distance between nodes
u
and
v
in
D
w.r.t. the distance demands
\delta
.
We define the
metric
terminal pairs as those whose distance demands (strictly) satisfy the triangle inequality, i.e.,
K^{\prime}\coloneq\{(u,v)\in K\mid d_{D-(u,v)}^{\delta}(u,v)>\delta(u,v)\}
.

Observation 2
.

A subgraph
H=(V,E^{\prime})
is a feasible (un)directed decoupled freeform spanner of
G=(V,E)
, if and only if
d_{H}^{\ell}(u,v)\leq\delta(u,v)
for all metric terminal pairs
(u,v)
.

Proof.

Necessity follows from
K^{\prime}\subseteq K
.
Conversely, assume
d_{H}^{\ell}(u,v)\leq\delta(u,v)
for all
(u,v)\in K^{\prime}
.
Let
(s,t)\in K\setminus K^{\prime}
. Let
P\subseteq K
be a minimum
st
-path in
D
w.r.t.
\delta
;
w.l.o.g.
P\subseteq K^{\prime}
. Consequently,
d_{H}^{\ell}(s,t)\leq\sum_{(i,j)\in P}d_{H}^{\ell}(i,j)\leq\sum_{(i,j)\in P}\delta(i,j)\leq\delta(s,t)
.∎

Dodis and Khanna’s Algorithm.

Going from unit-length to general decoupled settings is non-trivial.
In
[
21
]
, Dodis and Khanna give a
\mathcal{O}(n\log\alpha)
-approximation for directed unit-length multiplicative
\alpha
-spanners.
They use a multicommodity flow LP, that, for every edge
e\in E
, sends flow through a layered graph.
Randomized rounding yields a subgraph
H
, such that
(w.h.p.)
d_{H}^{\ell}(u,v)\leq\alpha\cdot d_{G}^{\ell}(u,v)
, for every
G
-adjacent node pair
(u,v)\in E
.

At the end of the paper, they briefly suggest how to generalize this algorithm to the decoupled setting with polynomially bounded lengths.
However, their approach unfortunately does not work:
In short, they transform the given instance into a unit-length instance where demands are only given for some node pairs (in particular not all adjacent node pairs) and want to solve it using the previous algorithm.
However, that algorithm requires that distance demands are enforced for
all adjacent
node pairs. Since this is not the case here, it cannot be expected to yield feasible solutions in general. See
Appendix

A
for an explicit example.

Nonetheless, our
RandomizedRounding
algorithm in
Section

4
will leverage their idea of multicommodity flow in layered graphs to, in fact, yield approximations even for more general decoupled freeform (not only multiplicative) spanner problems.

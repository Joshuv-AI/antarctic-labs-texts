Non-parametric Causal Inference in Dynamic Thresholding Designs
Aditya Ghosh, Stefan Wager

Abstract
Consider a setting where we regularly monitor patients’ fasting blood sugar, and declare them to have prediabetes (and encourage preventative care) if this number crosses a pre-specified threshold. The sharp, threshold-based treatment policy suggests that we should be able to estimate the long-term benefit of this preventative care by comparing the health trajectories of patients with blood sugar measurements right above and below the threshold. A naive regression-discontinuity analysis, however, is not applicable here, as it ignores the temporal dynamics of the problem where, e.g., a patient just below the threshold on one visit may become prediabetic (and receive treatment) following their next visit. Here, we study thresholding designs in general dynamic systems, and show that simple reduced-form characterizations remain available for a relevant causal target, namely a dynamic marginal policy effect at the treatment threshold. We develop a local-linear-regression approach for estimation and inference of this estimand, and demonstrate promise of our approach in numerical experiments.

1.1
Regression Discontinuities as Marginal Policy Effects

As background for our results on causal inference in dynamic threshold designs, we first
briefly review standard regression discontinuity (RD) designs (or, what could be called
cross-sectional thresholding designs) and how the resulting estimand can be interpreted
as a marginal policy effect. Following
Imbens and
Lemieux 2008
, assume that we have data on
IID-sampled pairs
(Z_{i},\,Y_{i})
for units
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
,
where
Z_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
is the running variable and

Y_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
is the outcome of interest.

The sharp RD design assumes that
there exists a cutoff
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that treatment is assigned as
A_{i}=\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{i}\geq c\right\}
.
We posit potential outcomes
\{Y_{i}(0),\,Y_{i}(1)\}
such that
Y_{i}=Y_{i}(A_{i})
, and
define the conditional average treatment effect (CATE) as
\tau(z)=\mathbb{E}[Y_{i}(1)-Y_{i}(0)\,|\,Z_{i}=z]
.
Then, the sharp RD design enables us to estimate
\tau(z)
, i.e., the CATE at the cutoff,
as a discontinuity in the conditional response surface at
Z_{i}=c

(
Hahn
et al. 2001
)
,

\tau_{\,\mathrm{RD}}:=\tau(c)=\lim_{z\,\downarrow\,c}\,\mathbb{E}[Y_{i}\,|\,Z_{i}=z]-\lim_{z\,\uparrow\,c}\,\mathbb{E}[Y_{i}\,|\,Z_{i}=z],

(1)

provided that the
\mu_{a}(z)=\mathbb{E}[Y_{i}(a)\,|\,Z_{i}=z]
are continuous in
z
and that the
running variable has continuous support around
c
.

The classical interpretation of the RD estimand as the CATE for units whose
running variable
Z_{i}
straddles the cutoff relies crucially on
Z_{i}
being causally
prior to any actions induced by our thresholding policy. In dynamic thresholding
designs, however, past thresholding actions can affect future values of the running
variable; and, as argued in
Frangakis and
Rubin 2002
, conditioning on observed variables
whose value may be affected by treatment generally precludes causal interpretation
of resulting estimands.
To avoid this issue, we find it helpful to re-interpret the classical RD estimand
as a marginal policy effect in the sense of
Carneiro
et al. 2010
, i.e.,
as essentially the answer to a cost-benefit analysis. Given a threshold
c
, the sharp RD design with threshold introduces a treatment policy
\pi_{c}(Z_{i})=\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{i}\geq c\right\}
with associated policy value

V(\pi_{c}):=\mathbb{E}[Y_{i}(\pi_{c}(Z_{i}))]=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[Y_{i}].

Then, provided the running variable is exogenous to the policy cutoff,
\tau_{\,\mathrm{RD}}
can be
interpreted as the policy gradient of lowering the cutoff (i.e., of treating more units),
divided by the corresponding increase in the number of units treated as we lower the cutoff.
Further results on policy counterfactuals in thresholding designs are given in
Dong and
Lewbel 2015
.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that the distribution of
\{Y_{i}(0),\,Y_{i}(1),\,Z_{i}\}
is exogenous to the chosen cutoff
c
.
Suppose furthermore that the running variable
Z_{i}
has density
f(\cdot)
which is continuous and positive at
c
, and that the conditional response functions
\mu_{a}(z):=\mathbb{E}[Y(a)\mid Z=z]

(a=0,1)
are continuous at
c
. Then,

\tau_{\,\mathrm{RD}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial c}V(\pi_{c})\,\bigg/\,\frac{\partial}{\partial c}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[A_{i}],\ \ \ \ \frac{\partial}{\partial c}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[A_{i}]=-f(c).

(2)

In other words, provided the running variables
Z_{i}
are exogenous to the thresholding
policy, and if the cost of providing treatment to a unit is
\lambda
, then a social
planner could achieve cost-adjusted welfare benefits by reducing
c
(and thus marginally
increasing the treatment rate) if and only if
\tau_{\,\mathrm{RD}}>\lambda
. As we move to a
multi-period setting, we will find this alternative characterization of the RD estimand
as the solution to a cost-benefit analysis to be remarkably resilient to challenges
induced by treatment dynamics.

1.2
Modeling Dynamics

Now consider a setting where units are observed at times
t=0,\,1,\,2,\,\ldots,\,T
,
where the horizon
T
may be either finite or infinite
1
1

1

In the infinite-horizon setting, we use
T=\infty
to define the population quantity of interest. However, the methods we propose for estimation and inference on this estimand are designed to operate in the realistic setting where the observed trajectories are finite but long (i.e., when
T
is sufficiently large for our asymptotic results to provide useful approximations).
.
At each time period
t
we observe a running variable
Z_{i,t}\in\mathbb{R}\,\cup\,\{-\infty\}
,
2
2

2

We
allow for the case
Z_{i,t}=-\infty
to account the possibility that the running variable may not be observed
in every time period
(
Hsu and
Shen 2024
)
. For example, in the case of yearly health checkups, it’s possible
a patient misses their health checkup one year and so no health measurements are taken. We assume that
units are not treated in periods where the running variable is unobserved.

take a thresholding action

A_{i,t}=\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{i,t}\geq c\right\}
for some threshold
c\in\mathbb{R}
, and observe an outcome
Y_{i,t}\in\mathbb{R}
.
The causal structure of dynamic problems is considerably richer than in cross-sections ones:
In addition to affecting outcomes
Y_{i,t}
, actions
A_{i,t}
taken at time
t
can affect state—and
thus also actions—at all times
t^{\prime}>t
.

The induced potential outcomes then acquire a tree-like branching structure indexing over all possible
past treatment sequences
(
Robins 1986
)
. This branching makes a direct reduced-form approach to dynamic
thresholding designs intractable—or, at the very least, subject to an exponential blow-up in dimensionality
as the time horizon (and thus action space) grows. Instead, it is usually more fruitful to proceed
via what Robins refers to as the
g
-formula which provides a useful factorization for the observed-data
distribution under natural temporal consistency assumptions. The probability factorization in Robins’
g
-formula
is equivalent to what arises in the study of Markov decision processes
(
Sutton and
Barto 2018
)
; recent textbook
discussions are given in
Hernán and
Robins 2020
and
Wager 2024
. Throughout, we will assume
that conditions required for the
g
-formula to hold are satisfied.

Assumption 1
.

We observe data collected under a dynamic thresholding policy
\pi_{c}
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
, i.e.,
actions are taken according to
A_{i,t}=\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{i,t}\geq c\right\}
.

Assumption 2
.

Under policy
\pi_{c}
, observation sequences for each unit
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
are sampled IID
from a distribution
\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{c}}
which factors according to the
g
-formula,

\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{c}}\left[Z_{i,0},\,Y_{i,0},\,\ldots,\,Z_{i,T},\,Y_{i,T}\right]=\prod_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{P}\left[Z_{i,t}\,\big|\,S_{i,t}\right]\mathbb{P}\left[Y_{i,t}\,\big|\,S_{i,t},\,Z_{i,t},\,A_{i,t}=\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{i,t}\geq c\right\}\right],

where
S_{i,t}=\{Z_{i,0},\,A_{i,0},\,Y_{i,0},\,\ldots,\,Z_{i,t-1},\,A_{i,t-1},\,Y_{i,t-1}\}
denotes observation history up
to time
t
and
S_{i,0}=\emptyset
,
and we emphasize that all conditional probabilities on the right-hand side of the
g
-formula are
policy-independent.

Our main question of interest is how treatment—as determined by dynamic thresholding as in
Assumption
1
—affects net-present expected welfare and treatment frequency,

V(\pi_{c}):=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma^{t}\,Y_{i,t}\right],\ \ \ \ V^{A}(\pi_{c}):=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma^{t}\,A_{i,t}\right],

(3)

where
0<\gamma\leq 1
is a discount rate (if
T=\infty
then we must have
\gamma<1
).
Because of the branching structure of potential outcomes a direct analogue to (
1
)
does not immediately enable meaningful program evaluation. However, perhaps surprisingly,
we will find that marginal policy effect characterizations of the form (
2
) remain
useful: An RD estimand defined as

\tau_{\,\mathrm{RD}}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial c}V(\pi_{c})\,\bigg/\,\frac{\partial}{\partial c}V^{A}(\pi_{c})

(4)

can still be effectively estimated in a sharp RD design—and can still be used to resolve
policy-relevant cost-benefit tradeoffs.

1.3
Related Work

The modern literature on regression discontinuity designs goes back to
Hahn
et al. 2001
;
influential contributions to this literature include
Imbens and
Lemieux 2008
,

Imbens and
Kalyanaraman 2012
,
Calonico
et al. 2014
and
Armstrong and
Kolesár 2018
.
Most of the existing methodological literature on regression discontinuity designs, however, is focused
on the cross-sectional setting where treatment is only assigned once. And, when faced with
the longitudinal setting, empirical researchers have reduced the problem to a cross-sectional
setting by simply considering various reduced-form regression discontinuities, e.g., by running
a standard RDD of
Y_{t}
on
Z_{t}
or of
Y_{t+1}
on
Z_{t}
. This is, for example, the strategy
taken in the original analysis of
Iizuka
et al. 2021
. Such reduced form analyses
can be of considerable substantive interest in applications; however, they do not capture
full treatment dynamics (e.g., how actions taken in one period may change the running
variable—and thus actions—taken in subsequent ones), and are thus not directly interpretable
as policy-relevant treatment effects
(
Heckman
et al. 2016
)
.

One notable exception is
Cellini
et al. 2010
, who use regression discontinuities to
identify a type of treatment on the treated (ATT) effect in dynamic designs. They then use
their estimator to identify the effect of local school spending via public bonds on house
prices in California by comparing outcomes in school districts where bond measures are just
barely accepts vs. rejected by voters; and their estimator allows them to formally consider
the fact that approving bonds in the past makes it less likely that additional bonds will
be approved in the future. The approach of
Cellini
et al. 2010
, however, makes crucial
use of a linear parametric model whereby

Y_{i,t}=\sum_{t^{\prime}\leq t}\theta_{t-t^{\prime}}A_{i,t^{\prime}}+\varepsilon_{i,t},

(5)

i.e., treatment effects are homogeneous and decay uniformly over time. And, as shown by

Hsu and
Shen 2024
, their approach no longer recovers an ATT if we allow for treatment
heterogeneity.
Hsu and
Shen 2024
propose an alternative analysis that avoids (
5
).
But they in turn require a strong conditional mean independence assumption (CIA) which, e.g., in
the 2-period case requires that the time-2 control potential outcomes be independent of the time-2 running
variable for all units whose time-1 running variable
is near the cutoff.
3
3

3

See Assumption 3.1.2 of
Hsu and
Shen 2024
for a precise
statement. This assumption is substantive, and would not hold in generic dynamic thresholding
designs. In particular, the CIA assumption will generally not hold if control potential outcomes
and the running variable both vary smoothly with some time-varying latent confounder; e.g., in our
motivating example, it would generally not hold if FBS
and health outcomes both vary smoothly with unobserved and time-varying health-seeking behaviors.

To the best of our knowledge our paper is the first to provide results on non-parametric
causal inference for dynamic thresholding designs with generality that’s comparable to standard
results in the cross-sectional setting following
Hahn
et al. 2001
.

Our flexible potential-outcomes based model for dynamic causal inference goes back to
Robins 1986
.
This model is widely used in biostatistics in the context of, e.g., marginal structural models
(
Robins
et al. 2000
)

and optimal treatment regimes
(
Robins 2004
)
. To the best of our knowledge, this model has not been
previously used in the context of dynamic thresholding designs—the one exception being
Hsu and
Shen 2024
,
who pair the model of
Robins 1986
with their potentially restrictive CIA assumption to make progress.
Our approach is motivated by results from the reinforcement learning literature
(
Sutton and
Barto 2018
)
, and
especially the policy-gradient theorem. The policy-gradient theorem is widely used for optimizing reinforcement-learning
systems via first-order algorithms
(
Sutton
et al. 1999
)
, and has recently been deployed for estimating global treatment effects in nonstationary Markovian A/B tests with temporal interference
(
Johari
et al. 2025
)
. However, we are not aware of previous uses of policy-gradient
theorems for observational study causal inference in settings of the type we consider here.

2
Characterizing the Dynamic RD Estimand

We work under the general dynamic model introduced in
Section

1.2
. Our first goal will be to provide a reduced-form characterization of the policy gradient in dynamic thresholding designs and connect it to an RD estimand that can be interpreted as a marginal policy effect. This characterization will later serve as the foundation for developing a tractable method for estimation and inference in dynamic thresholding designs.

To meaningfully evaluate how changing the threshold
c
affects the value function
V(\pi_{c})
defined in (
3
), we seek to characterize the negative policy gradient
-\partial V(\pi_{c})/\partial c
, which represents the marginal effect on total discounted welfare of infinitesimally lowering the cutoff (i.e., treating slightly more units). As in
Lemma

1
for the cross-sectional case, we show that this gradient can be expressed in terms of conditional response functions and the density of the running variable at the cutoff. However, the dynamic setting introduces an additional complexity: The relevant conditional response functions must now account for all future treatment dynamics induced by changing the treatment assignment for the current period.
To formalize this, we introduce the
Q
-function (or action-value function), which plays a central role in reinforcement learning and dynamic treatment regime analysis:

Q_{c,\,t}\left(s_{t},\,z_{t},\,a_{t}\right):=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\sum_{j=0}^{T-t}\gamma^{j}\,Y_{i,t+j}\,\bigg|\,S_{i,t}=s_{t},\,Z_{i,t}=z_{t},\,A_{i,t}=a_{t}\right].

(6)

In words,
Q_{c,\,t}(s_{t},\,z_{t},\,a_{t})
is the expected discounted sum of future rewards starting with history
s_{t}
, running variable
z_{t}
and action
a_{t}
at time
t
.

To characterize the policy gradient, all we need in addition to the basic model from Section
1.2

is that the running variable have a density around the cutoff
c
conditionally on past state, and that relevant
conditional-response functions vary smoothly with the running variable. We note that both assumption
will hold whenever there is non-trivial (continuously distributed and exogenous) noise in the running variable

(
Lee 2008
;
Eckles
et al. 2025
)
.

Assumption 3
.

Conditional on the history
S_{i,t}
, the running variable
Z_{i,t}
has a density
f_{t}(\,\cdot\mid S_{i,t})
that is continuous and strictly positive at
c
for almost every
S_{i,t}
and for each
t\geq 0
.

Assumption 4
.

For each
t\geq 0
, the
Q
-functions
Q_{c,\,t}(s_{t},\,z_{t}\,,\,a_{t})

(a_{t}=0,\,1)
are continuous at
z_{t}=c
for almost every
s_{t}
and for
a_{t}=0,\,1
.

Under the above assumptions, the following result explicitly characterizes the policy gradient in our dynamic thresholding setting. Although it is conceptually similar to the standard policy-gradient theorem
(
Sutton
et al. 1999
)
, we note that it is not a direct corollary: The standard policy gradient quantifies the effect of changing action probabilities under overlap conditions (i.e., where treatment and control actions can both occur with positive probability in all states), whereas here we consider the effect of changing the treatment cutoff in a setting without overlap.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
1
,
2
,
3
and
4
hold, and assume furthermore that the
following intergrability conditions hold: For some
\eta>0
,

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\geq 0}\,\sup_{s_{t}}\,\sup_{|c^{\prime}-c|\leq\eta}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c^{\prime}}}\left[\sum_{j=0}^{T-t}\gamma^{j}\,|Y_{i,t+j}|\,\bigg|\,S_{i,t}=s_{t}\right]<\infty,\\
&\sup_{t\geq 0}\,\sup_{s_{t}}\,\sup_{|z-c|\leq\eta}\max\left\{\left|Q_{c,\,t}(s_{t},\,z,\,1)-Q_{c,\,t}(s_{t},\,z,\,0)\right|,1\right\}f_{t}(z\mid s_{t})<\infty.\end{split}

(7)

Then, the gradient of the total discounted reward under
the threshold-based policy
\pi_{c}
with respect to the threshold parameter
c
is given by

-\frac{\partial}{\partial c}V(\pi_{c})=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\left(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,1)-Q_{c,\,t}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,0)\right)f_{t}(c\mid S_{i,t})\right],

provided that either
\gamma<1
or
T<\infty
.

The above result establishes that the negative gradient of the value function
V(\pi_{c})
with respect to the threshold parameter
c
equals a discounted sum of the
Q
-function differences at the threshold across all future time periods, weighted by the conditional density at the threshold in each period. In addition to providing a unified expression for both finite and infinite horizon settings,
Theorem

2
organically handles two core challenges that arise specifically in dynamic thresholding designs:

1.

Carryover effects
: The
Q
-function differences
Q_{c,\,t}(S_{i,t},c,1)-Q_{c,\,t}(S_{i,t},c,0)
automatically incorporate downstream effects on all future outcomes; we do not need to separately model how treatment decision at time
t
affects the outcomes at times
t^{\prime}>t
.

2.

Dynamic threshold proximity
: The density factors
f_{t}(c\mid S_{i,t})
combined with the temporal discounting
\gamma^{t}
accounts for the evolving frequency with which units appear near the threshold over time, thereby accommodating the circumstances where individuals may repeatedly switch between treatment and control groups.

We also define the
Q
-function for the treatment indicators as:

Q_{c,\,t}^{A}\left(s_{t},\,z_{t},\,a_{t}\right):=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\sum_{j=0}^{T-t}\gamma^{j}\,A_{i,t+j}\,\bigg|\,S_{i,t}=s_{t},\,Z_{i,t}=z_{t},\,A_{i,t}=a_{t}\right].

(8)

In words,
Q_{c,\,t}^{A}(s_{t},\,z_{t},\,a_{t})
is the expected number of future periods in which the unit is exposed to the treatment starting with history
s_{t}
, running variable
z_{t}
and action
a_{t}
at time
t
.

Assumption 5
.

For each
t\geq 0
, the
Q
-functions
Q_{c,\,t}^{A}(s_{t},\,z_{t}\,,\,a_{t})

(a_{t}=0,\,1)
are continuous at
z_{t}=c
for almost every
s_{t}
and for
a_{t}=0,\,1
. Furthermore, assume that

\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma^{t}\,(Q_{c,t}^{A}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,1)-Q_{c,t}^{A}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,0))f_{t}(c\mid S_{i,t})\right]>0.

An immediate application of
Theorem

2
to both the numerator and
denominator of
\tau_{\,\mathrm{RD}}
as defined in (
4
) then yields the following characterization.

Corollary 3
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
2
as well as Assumption
5
, the dynamic marginal policy effect parameter
\tau_{\,\mathrm{RD}}
introduced in (
4
) can be equivalently expressed as

\tau_{\,\mathrm{RD}}=\frac{\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,1)-Q_{c,\,t}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,0))f_{t}(c\mid S_{i,t})\right]}{\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[(Q^{A}_{c,\,t}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,1)-Q^{A}_{c,\,t}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,0))f_{t}(c\mid S_{i,t})\right]},

(9)

provided that either
\gamma<1
or
T<\infty
.

3
Inference via Local Linear Regression

The expression for the causal parameter
\tau_{\,\mathrm{RD}}
given in
Corollary

3

is explicit—but at first glance may appear unwieldy to operationalize because of
its dependence on the growing-dimensional state
S_{i,t}
. Perhaps
surprisingly, however, it turns out that the quantity
\tau_{\,\mathrm{RD}}
can be
estimated using a carefully designed local linear regression procedure.
Although we here consider sharp thresholding designs our estimator has a ratio
form typically associated with fuzzy RD designs
(
Imbens and
Lemieux 2008
)
.
The ratio form is explained by the fact that, in the dynamic design, there is
some uncertainty on how moving
c
will affect the treatment frequency (since
we may not get to observe realizations of
Z_{i,t}
for
t\geq 1
for counterfactual
thresholding policies); and, as argued in
Sun
et al. 2021
, cost-benefit
analyses under cost uncertainty induce statistical structure resembling that encountered
in instrumental-variable analyses.

Corollary

3
provides a unified characterization of the dynamic marginal policy effect across both finite and infinite horizons. Our estimation and inference procedures, however, will differ slightly between these two cases. The key difference lies in how one can approximate the
Q
-functions: In finite-horizon settings, we can directly use the cumulative discounted sum of rewards observed from time
t
onward, whereas infinite-horizon problems demand separate attention to the fact that the observed trajectories in any real-world data are necessarily truncated. We first present results for the finite-horizon case.

3.1
Finite-horizon Case

Consider the dynamic thresholding design introduced in
Section

1.2
with
T<\infty
. Define the discounted sum of future outcomes and treatment assignments from time
t
onward as

G_{i,t}=\sum_{j=0}^{T-t}\gamma^{j}\,Y_{i,\,t+j},\ \ \ \ H_{i,t}=\sum_{j=0}^{T-t}\gamma^{j}\,A_{i,\,t+j}

(10)

where
t=0,1,\dots,T
, and
i=1,2,\dots,n
.
Motivated by the twice-discounted structure of the expression in
Corollary

3
, where discounting appears both within the
Q
-functions and in the outer summation, we propose below a twice-discounted local linear regression procedure for estimating the causal parameter
\tau_{\,\mathrm{RD}}
. We choose a small bandwidth
h=h(n)
(where
h(n)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
), a weighting
function
K:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
and run the following weighted linear regression on each side
of the threshold:

\begin{split}&\widehat{\theta}(h):=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{\theta=(\alpha,\,\tau_{G},\,\beta_{0},\,\beta_{1})}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma^{t}\,K\left(\frac{|Z_{i,t}-c|}{h}\right)\left(G_{i,t}-r(Z_{i,t})^{\top}\theta\right)^{2},\\[5.69054pt]

, it holds for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{c}}\left(\tau_{\,\mathrm{RD}}\in\left[\widehat{\tau}_{\,\mathrm{RD}}\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\,\widehat{V}_{\,\mathrm{RD},\,n}^{1/2}(nh)^{-1/2}\right]\right)=1-\alpha.

4
Numerical Experiments

In this section, we validate our theoretical results using numerical experiments.
Consider a simple simulation setting where we generate data from the following autoregressive process:

\begin{split}Z_{t+1}&=\delta+Z_{t}-(1-\rho)(Z_{t}-\mu_{0})-\tau\,A_{t}\,(Z_{t}-\mu_{0})_{+}+4\varepsilon_{t},\\
A_{t}&=\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\},\\
Y_{t}&=-Z_{t+1},\quad t=0,1,\dots,T-1.\end{split}

(18)

We consider a finite horizon
T=12
, and generate the noise
\varepsilon_{t}
as i.i.d. from
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
. We use the treatment threshold
c=110
, the baseline mean
\mu_{0}=100
, the autocorrelation coefficient
\rho=0.9
, the treatment intensity parameter
\tau=0.1
, and consider two choices for the drift parameter
\delta
, namely
\delta=0
(Setting 1) and
\delta=1
(Setting 2). We initialize the autoregressive process with
Z_{0}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,4/(1-\rho^{2})^{1/2})
. Note that this is not a stationary initialization, even when
\delta=0
, because of the treatment-dependent drift
\tau\neq 0
.

To improve precision, we augment the local linear regression (LLR) specification in (
11
) with time fixed effects. Specifically, we run the discounted LLR in (
11
) with the following regressors:

r(z)=(1,\mathbf{1}\left\{z\geq c\right\},z-c,(z-c)\mathbf{1}\left\{z\geq c\right\},d_{1},\dots,d_{T-1})^{\top},

(19)

where
d_{t}
is a dummy variable for the time period
t
. In our simulations, we find that including these dummy variables substantially reduces the variance of our estimator (as well as that of the baseline methods we discuss below), and we plan to investigate formal properties of this modified estimator in a later draft.
We contrast our proposed procedure with the following baseline methods:

1.

Baseline 1: Run the standard LLR of
Y_{i,t}
on
Z_{i,t}
, i.e.,

\widehat{\tau}_{\mathrm{LLR}}:=e_{2}^{\top}\mathop{\rm argmin}_{\theta=(\alpha,\,\tau_{G},\,\beta_{0},\,\beta_{1})}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{t=0}^{T}K\left(\frac{|Z_{i,t}-c|}{h}\right)\left(Y_{i,t}-r(Z_{i,t})^{\top}\theta\right)^{2},

(20)

where the regressors
r(z)
are as defined in (
19
), and
e_{2}=(0,1,0,0)^{\top}
.

2.

Baseline 2: Consider a naive long-run LLR approach where
we collapse each trajectory to the first-period net-present outcome
G_{i,0}
and treatment exposure
H_{i,0}
(as defined in (
10
)), and run separate standard LLRs of
G_{i,0}
and
H_{i,0}
on the first-period running variable
Z_{i,0}
. Precisely,

\begin{split}&\widehat{\tau}_{G,\,\text{naive}}(h):=e_{2}^{\top}\mathop{\rm argmin}_{\theta=(\alpha,\,\tau_{G},\,\beta_{0},\,\beta_{1})}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}K\left(\frac{|Z_{i,0}-c|}{h}\right)\left(G_{i,0}-r(Z_{i,0})^{\top}\theta\right)^{2},\\[5.69054pt]
&\widehat{\tau}_{H,\,\text{naive}}(h):=e_{2}^{\top}\mathop{\rm argmin}_{\theta=(\alpha,\,\tau_{H},\,\beta_{0},\,\beta_{1})}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}K\left(\frac{|Z_{i,0}-c|}{h}\right)\left(H_{i,0}-r(Z_{i,0})^{\top}\eta\right)^{2},\\[5.69054pt]
&\widehat{\tau}_{\mathrm{RD},\,\text{naive}}(h):=\widehat{\tau}_{G,\,\text{naive}}(h)\,\big/\,\widehat{\tau}_{H,\,\text{naive}}(h).\end{split}

(21)

where the regressors
r(z)
are as defined in (
19
), and
e_{2}=(0,1,0,0)^{\top}
.

The first baseline procedure is expected to recover the short-run, partial equilibrium effect (i.e., the one-period RD jump at the threshold), which in this case is given by
\tau_{\text{partial eq.}}=\tau(c-\mu_{0})=1
. This parameter can be very different from the marginal policy effect parameter
\tau_{\,\mathrm{RD}}
. For example, in Setting 1 (where drift
\delta=0
), we find that for discount factor
\gamma=0.8
,
\tau_{\,\mathrm{RD}}\approx 3
(obtained using
n=5{,}000{,}000
monte-carlo simulations), which is three times the partial equilibrium effect.

The second baseline procedure tries to naively incorporate long-run dynamics by first aggregating all future outcomes and treatment decisions into the net-present quantities
G_{i,0}
and
H_{i,0}
, and then running a single cross-sectional RD at time
t=0
. In the limit,
\widehat{\tau}_{\mathrm{RD},\,\text{naive}}(h)
targets
the marginal effect of infinitesimally lowering the threshold at the initial period on discounted outcomes per additional discounted treatment generated by that initial perturbation. However, this estimand only uses information from the first time the running variable is near the cutoff and treats the future evolution of
Z_{t}
as fixed. As a result, this naive long-run LLR generally does not target the dynamic marginal policy effect and can be biased whenever the treatment policy has strong feedback effects on the future state trajectory.

demonstrate that our proposed confidence intervals for the dynamic marginal effect parameter

provide nominal coverage across all sample sizes and discount factors. In contrast, standard LLR confidence intervals severely undercover when used to make inference on
\tau_{\,\mathrm{RD}}
, with coverage rates dropping to near zero even for moderate sample sizes. This is expected since the standard LLR targets the partial equilibrium effect
\tau_{\,\text{partial eq.}}
, not the dynamic marginal policy effect
\tau_{\,\mathrm{RD}}
. Indeed,
Tables

1
and
2
illustrate that the standard LLR confidence intervals provide nominal coverage for the parameter
\tau_{\,\text{partial eq.}}
.

We also observe that our confidence intervals are substantially wider than those from standard LLR, with widths increasing as the discount factor approaches 1. This is expected from our asymptotic theory in
Theorems

9
and
5
: The asymptotic variance of our estimator
\widehat{\tau}_{\,\mathrm{RD}}
aggregates uncertainty across all future time periods, and the effective number of periods contributing to this variance grows as the discount factor increases. More fundamentally, the problem of estimating the long-term marginal policy effect
\tau_{\,\mathrm{RD}}
is intrinsically more difficult than estimating the short-run partial equilibrium effect
\tau_{\,\text{partial eq.}}
, because
\tau_{\,\mathrm{RD}}
incorporates spillovers and dynamic treatment switching across all future periods, each contributing additional uncertainty.

Our proposed method also outperforms the naive long-run LLR (Baseline 2) in terms of both coverage and width. For smaller discount factors and moderate sample sizes, this naive baseline method provides near-nominal coverage with confidence intervals that are substantially wider than our proposed ones. On the other hand, for discount factor
\gamma=1
and larger sample sizes, its coverage deteriorates substantially, dropping below 90%. This breakdown is expected since this naive method only exploits the first-period threshold proximity and ignores how changing the threshold affects the frequency with which units cross the threshold in future periods. In contrast, our method maintains nominal coverage across all settings by correctly pooling information from all time periods using the twice-discounted weighting scheme.

Main Proofs

A.1
Proof of
Lemma

1

Proof.

First observe that the value function under policy
\pi_{c}
is given by

V(\pi_{c})=\mathbb{E}[Y_{i}(\pi_{c}(Z_{i}))]=\int_{c}^{\infty}\mu_{1}(z)\,f(z)\,dz+\int_{-\infty}^{c}\mu_{0}(z)\,f(z)\,dz,

where
\mu_{a}(z):=\mathbb{E}[Y(a)\mid Z=z]
. Differentiating with respect to
c
using the Leibniz rule, we obtain

\frac{\partial}{\partial c}V(\pi_{c})=-(\mu_{1}(c)-\mu_{0}(c))f_{t}(c).

Similarly,

\frac{\partial}{\partial c}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[A_{i}]=\frac{\partial}{\partial c}\int_{c}^{\infty}f(z)\,dz=-f_{t}(c).

Combining the last two displays we arrive at the desired conclusion.

\square

A.2
Proof of
Theorem

2

Proof.

Since units
i=1,\dots,n
are identically distributed, we drop the unit index throughout this proof. Denote by
P_{t}^{\pi_{c}}
the distribution of
S_{t}
under the policy
\pi_{c}
. Define

V_{c,\,t}(P_{t})=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\sum_{j=0}^{T-t}\gamma^{j}\,Y_{t+j}\,\,\bigg|\,\,S_{t}\sim P_{t}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{S_{t}\sim P_{t}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,z,\,\pi_{c}(z))f_{t}(z\mid S_{t})\,dz,

where

Q_{c,\,t}(s,\,z,\,a)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\,\sum_{t=0}^{T-t}\gamma^{t}\,Y_{t}\,\,\bigg|\,\,S_{t}=s,\,Z_{t}=z,\,A_{t}=a\right].

Assume without loss of generality that
h>0
(the case
h<0
follows similarly) and
h<\eta
where
\eta
is as in (
7
). Since
S_{0}=\{\emptyset\}
, we can write

\frac{V(\pi_{c+h})-V(\pi_{c})}{h}=\frac{V_{c+h,\,0}(P_{0}^{\pi_{c}})-V_{c,\,0}(P_{0}^{\pi_{c}})}{h}.

(A.1)

Observe now that for any distribution
P_{t}
for
S_{t}
,

\displaystyle\frac{V_{c+h,\,t}(P_{t})-V_{c,\,t}(P_{t})}{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}\,\mathbb{E}_{S_{t}\sim P_{t}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\{Q_{c+h,\,t}(S_{t},\,z,\,\pi_{c+h}(z))-Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,z,\,\pi_{c}(z))\right\}f_{t}(z\mid S_{t})dz

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}\,\mathbb{E}_{S_{t}\sim P_{t}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\{Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,z,\,\pi_{c+h}(z))-Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,z,\,\pi_{c}(z))\right\}f_{t}(z\mid S_{t})dz

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{h}\,\mathbb{E}_{S_{t}\sim P_{t}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\{Q_{c+h,\,t}(S_{t},\,z,\,\pi_{c+h}(z))-Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,z,\,\pi_{c+h}(z))\right\}f_{t}(z\mid S_{t})dz

\displaystyle=-M_{t}(h;P_{t})+R_{t}(h;P_{t}),

(A.2)

where

M_{t}(h;P_{t}):=\mathbb{E}_{S_{t}\sim P_{t}}\left[\frac{1}{h}\int_{c}^{c+h}\left(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,z,\,1)-Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,z,\,0)\right)f_{t}(z\mid S_{t})\,dz\right],

and

R_{t}(h;P_{t}):=\mathbb{E}_{S_{t}\sim P_{t}}\left[\frac{1}{h}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(Q_{c+h,\,t}\left(S_{t},z,\pi_{c+h}(z)\right)-Q_{c,\,t}\left(S_{t},z,\pi_{c+h}(z)\right)\right)f_{t}\left(z\mid S_{t}\right)dz\right].

Next, define
\mu_{t}(s,\,z,\,a):=\mathbb{E}[Y_{t}\mid S_{t}=s,\,Z_{t}=z,\,A_{t}=a]
and recall that the Bellman equation reads

Q_{c^{\prime},\,t}(s,\,z,\,a)=\mu_{t}(s,\,z,\,a)+\gamma V_{c^{\prime},\,t+1}(P_{t+1}^{\,s,\,z,\,a}),

for any
c^{\prime}
, where
P_{t+1}^{\,s,\,z,\,a}
is the law after one step transition from
(S_{t}=s,\,Z_{t}=z,\,A_{t}=a)
.
Consequently,

Q_{c+h,\,t}(s,\,z,\,a)-Q_{c,\,t}(s,\,z,\,a)=\gamma\left(V_{c+h,\,t+1}(P_{t+1}^{\,s,\,z,\,a})-V_{c,\,t+1}(P_{t+1}^{\,s,\,z,\,a})\right).

(A.3)

This combined with the definition of
R_{0}(h)
yields that

\displaystyle R_{t}(h;P_{t}^{\pi_{c}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{S_{t}\sim P_{t}^{\pi_{c}}}\left[\frac{1}{h}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(Q_{c+h,\,t}\left(S_{t},z,\pi_{c+h}(z)\right)-Q_{c,\,t}\left(S_{t},z,\pi_{c+h}(z)\right)\right)f_{t}\left(z\mid S_{t}\right)dz\right]

(by definition)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{S_{t}\sim P_{t}^{\pi_{c}}}\left[\frac{1}{h}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(Q_{c+h,\,t}\left(S_{t},z,\pi_{c}(z)\right)-Q_{c,\,t}\left(S_{t},z,\pi_{c}(z)\right)\right)f_{t}\left(z\mid S_{t}\right)dz\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\mathbb{E}_{S_{t}\sim P_{t}^{\pi_{c}}}\left[\frac{1}{h}\int_{c}^{c+h}\left((Q_{c+h,\,t}-Q_{c,\,t})(S_{t},z,1)-(Q_{c+h,\,t}-Q_{c,\,t})(S_{t},z,0)\right)f_{t}(z\mid S_{t})dz\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{S_{t}\sim P_{t}^{\pi_{c}}}\left[\frac{1}{h}\int\gamma\left(V_{c+h,\,1}\left(P_{t+1}^{\,S_{t},\,z,\,\pi_{c}(z)}\right)-V_{G,\,1}\left(P_{t+1}^{\,S_{t},\,z,\,\pi_{c}(z)}\right)\right)f_{t}\left(z\mid S_{t}\right)dz\right]

(using (
A.3
))

\displaystyle\quad-\mathbb{E}_{S_{t}\sim P_{t}^{\pi_{c}}}\left[\frac{1}{h}\int_{c}^{c+h}\left((Q_{c+h,\,t}-Q_{c,\,t})(S_{t},z,1)-(Q_{c+h,\,t}-Q_{c,\,t})(S_{t},z,0)\right)f_{t}(z\mid S_{t})dz\right]

\displaystyle=\gamma\,\frac{V_{c+h,\,t+1}(P_{t+1}^{\pi_{c}})-V_{c,\,t+1}(P_{t+1}^{\pi_{c}})}{h}-\Delta_{t}(h),

(by definition)

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{t}(h)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}_{S_{t}\sim P_{t}^{\pi_{c}}}\left[\frac{1}{h}\int_{c}^{c+h}\left((Q_{c+h,\,t}-Q_{c,\,t})(S_{t},z,1)-(Q_{c+h,\,t}-Q_{c,\,t})(S_{t},z,0)\right)f_{t}(z\mid S_{t})dz\right].

Plugging the above expression for
R_{t}(h)
into (
A.2
), we arrive at the following recursion.

\frac{V_{c+h,\,t}(P_{t}^{\pi_{c}})-V_{c,\,t}(P_{t}^{\pi_{c}})}{h}=-M_{t}(h)+\gamma\,\frac{V_{c+h,\,1}(P_{t+1}^{\pi_{c}})-V_{G,\,1}(P_{t+1}^{\pi_{c}})}{h}-\Delta_{t}(h).

Using the above recursion, we deduce from (
A.1
) that

\frac{V(\pi_{c+h})-V(\pi_{c})}{h}=-\sum_{t=0}^{T^{\prime}}\gamma^{t}\,(M_{t}(h)+\Delta_{t}(h))+\gamma^{T^{\prime}+1}\frac{V_{c+h,\,T^{\prime}+1}(P_{T^{\prime}+1}^{\pi_{c}})-V_{c,\,T^{\prime}+1}(P_{T^{\prime}+1}^{\pi_{c}})}{h}.

(A.4)

For the finite-horizon (
T<\infty
) case, we use
T^{\prime}=T
and
V_{c^{\prime},\,T+1}(P_{T+1}^{\pi_{c}})=0
to deduce that

\frac{V(\pi_{c+h})-V(\pi_{c})}{h}=-\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma^{t}\,(M_{t}(h)+\Delta_{t}(h)).

(A.5)

For the infinite-horizon (
T=\infty
) case, we claim that the above display also holds, in a limiting sense: We obtain (
A.5
) by letting
T^{\prime}\to\infty
in (
A.4
). To prove this, derive a uniform upper bound on
(V_{c+h,\,t}(P_{t})-V_{c,\,t}(P_{t}))/h
so that the last term in (
A.4
) goes to
0
as
T^{\prime}\to\infty
, for any fixed
h>0
.
The following chain of inequalities fits the bill. For any
t\geq 0
and any distribution
P_{t}
for
S_{t}
,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{V_{c+h,\,t}(P_{t})-V_{c,\,t}(P_{t})}{h}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{h}\sup_{|c^{\prime}-c|\leq\eta}\left|V_{c^{\prime},\,t}(P_{t})\right|

(since
|h|<\eta
)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{h}\sup_{|c^{\prime}-c|\leq\eta}\mathbb{E}_{S_{t}\sim P_{t}}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c^{\prime}}}\left[\sum_{j=0}^{T-t}\gamma^{j}\left|Y_{t+j}\right|\,\bigg|\,S_{t}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{h(1-\gamma)}\sup_{t\geq 0}\,\sup_{s_{t}}\sup_{|c^{\prime}-c|\leq\eta}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c^{\prime}}}\left[\sum_{j=0}^{T-t}\gamma^{j}\left|Y_{t+j}\right|\,\bigg|\,S_{t}=s_{t}\right]<\infty.

The above bound combined with
\gamma^{T^{\prime}+1}\to 0
as
T^{\prime}\to\infty
yields (
A.5
) from (
A.4
).

Having shown (
A.5
), we now consider taking limits as
h\to 0
. Since
z\mapsto Q_{c,\,t}(s,\,z,\,a)
and
z\mapsto f_{t}(z\mid s)
are continuous at
c
for a.e.
s
and for every
t\geq 0
, and it follows from the fundamental theorem of Calculus that

\lim_{h\to 0}\left[\frac{1}{h}\int_{c}^{c+h}(Q_{c,\,t}(s_{t},\,z,\,1)-Q_{c,\,t}(s_{t},\,z,\,0))f_{t}\left(z\mid s_{t}\right)dz\right]=\left(Q_{c,\,t}(s_{t},\,c,\,1)-Q_{c,\,t}(s_{t},\,c,\,0)\right)f_{t}(c\mid s_{t}),

for a.e.
s_{t}
and for every
t\geq 0
. Since
\sup_{t}\sup_{s_{t}}\sup_{|z-c|\leq\eta}|Q_{c,\,t}(s_{t},\,z,\,1)-Q_{c,\,t}(s_{t},\,z,\,0)|f_{t}(z\mid s_{t})<\infty
, we can apply the dominated convergence theorem to conclude that

\lim_{h\to 0}M_{t}(h)=\mathbb{E}_{S_{t}\sim P_{t}^{\pi_{c}}}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,c,\,1)-Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,c,\,0))\,f_{t}(c\mid S_{t}),

for each
t\geq 0
. The geometric weights allow us to pass the limit under the sum and write the following.

\lim_{h\to 0}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma^{t}\,M_{t}(h)=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,c,\,1)-Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,c,\,0))f_{t}(c\mid S_{t}).

(A.6)

In view of (
A.5
), it only remains to show that

\lim_{h\to 0}\,\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma^{t}\,\Delta_{t}(h)=0.

(A.7)

Toward proving the above display, we first note that

\displaystyle\left|R_{t}(h;P_{t})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}_{S_{t}\sim P_{t}^{\pi_{c}}}\left[\frac{1}{h}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(Q_{c+h,\,t}\left(S_{t},z,\pi_{c+h}(z)\right)-Q_{c,\,t}\left(S_{t},z,\pi_{c+h}(z)\right)\right)f_{t}\left(z\mid S_{t}\right)dz\right]\right|

(by definition)

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}_{S_{t}\sim P_{t}^{\pi_{c}}}\left[\frac{1}{h}\int\gamma\left(V_{c+h,\,t+1}\left(P_{t+1}^{\,S_{t},\,z,\,\pi_{c+h}(z)}\right)-V_{c,\,t+1}\left(P_{t+1}^{\,S_{t},\,z,\,\pi_{c+h}(z)}\right)\right)f_{t}\left(z\mid S_{t}\right)dz\right]\right|

(using (
A.3
))

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\,\sup_{P_{t+1}}\left|\frac{V_{c+h,\,t+1}(P_{t+1})-V_{c,\,t+1}(P_{t+1})}{h}\right|

(by triangle inequality)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\,\sup_{t\geq 0}\sup_{P_{t}}\left|\frac{V_{c+h,\,t}(P_{t})-V_{c,\,t}(P_{t})}{h}\right|.

Note that the above bound is finite, due to (
A.7
). Using the above bound and (
A.2
), we deduce that

\left|\frac{V_{c+h,\,t}(P_{t})-V_{c,\,t}(P_{t})}{h}\right|\leq\left|M_{t}(h;P_{t})\right|+\gamma\,\sup_{t\geq 0}\sup_{P_{t}}\left|\frac{V_{c+h,\,t}(P_{t})-V_{c,\,t}(P_{t})}{h}\right|

This, in turn, implies that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\,\sup_{P_{t}}\left|\frac{V_{c+h}(P_{t})-V_{c}(P_{t})}{h}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sup_{t\geq 0}\,\sup_{P_{t}}|M_{t}(h\,;P_{t})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sup_{t\geq 0}\,\sup_{P_{t}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{t}\sim P_{t}}\left[\frac{1}{h}\int_{c}^{c+h}\left|Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,z,\,1)-Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,z,\,0)\right|f_{t}(z\mid S_{t})\,dz\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sup_{t\geq 0}\,\sup_{s_{t}}\sup_{|z-c|\leq\eta}\left|Q_{c,\,t}(s_{t},\,z,\,1)-Q_{c,\,t}(s_{t},\,z,\,0)\right|f_{t}(z\mid s_{t}),

where the last quantity is finite, by assumption. This implies that for all
|h|<\eta
, for any
t\geq 0
and any distribution
P_{t}
for
S_{t}
,

|V_{c+h,\,t}(P_{t})-V_{c,\,t}(P_{t})|\leq Ch,

for some universal constant
C\in(0,\infty)
. We can therefore invoke (
A.3
) once again to conclude that

\displaystyle\left|\Delta_{t}(h)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}_{S_{t}\sim P_{t}^{\pi_{c}}}\left[\frac{1}{h}\int_{c}^{c+h}\left(\left(Q_{c+h,\,t}-Q_{c,\,t}\right)(S_{t},z,1)-\left(Q_{c+h}-Q_{c}\right)(S_{t},z,0)\right)f_{t}\left(z\mid S_{t}\right)dz\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\mathbb{E}_{S_{t}\sim P_{t}^{\pi_{c}}}\left[\frac{1}{h}\int_{c}^{c+h}\gamma\left(\left(V_{c+h,\,t+1}-V_{c,\,t+1}\right)(P_{t+1}^{S_{t},\,z,\,1})-\left(V_{c+h,\,t+1}-V_{c,\,t+1}\right)(P_{t+1}^{S_{t},\,z,\,0})\right)f_{t}\left(z\mid S_{t}\right)dz\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{S_{t}\sim P_{t}^{\pi_{c}}}\left[\frac{1}{h}\int_{c}^{c+h}\gamma\cdot 2Ch\cdot f_{t}\left(z\mid S_{t}\right)dz\right]

\displaystyle=2C\gamma\,\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{c}}\left(Z_{t}\in[c,c+h]\right).

Finally, the dominated convergence theorem gives

\lim_{h\to 0}\,\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{c}}\left(Z_{t}\in[c,c+h]\right)=0,

which completes the proof of (
A.7
). Combining (
A.5
), (
A.6
) and (
A.7
) we conclude that

-\frac{\partial}{\partial c}V(\pi_{c})=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma^{t}\,(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,c,\,1)-Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,c,\,0))f_{t}\left(c\mid S_{t}\right)\right],

as desired to show.

\square

Proof of

Proof.

\begin{split}\sqrt{nh(n)}\left(\widehat{\tau}_{G,\,n}-\tau_{G,\,n}^{*}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\bar{f}_{c})^{-1}(e_{1}^{\top}M_{+}^{-1}\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+}-e_{1}^{\top}M_{-}^{-1}\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,-})+o_{p}(1),\\
\sqrt{nh(n)}\left(\widehat{\tau}_{H,\,n}-\tau_{H,\,n}^{*}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\bar{f}_{c})^{-1}(e_{1}^{\top}M_{+}^{-1}\psi_{H,\,n,\,i,\,+}-e_{1}^{\top}M_{-}^{-1}\psi_{H,\,n,\,i,\,-})+o_{p}(1),\end{split}

(C.1)

where
\bar{f}_{c}:=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]
,
M_{\pm}:=(((\pm 1)^{i+j-1}\kappa_{i+j}))_{0\leq i,\,j\leq 1}
, and the
\psi
-functions are given by

\displaystyle\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+}:=\sqrt{h}\,\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}\,K_{h}(Z_{i,t})\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{i,t}\geq c\right\}\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})\left(G_{i,t}(\ell)-\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})^{\top}p_{+}(\theta_{n}^{*})\right),

\displaystyle\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,-}:=\sqrt{h}\,\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}\,K_{h}(Z_{i,t})\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{i,t}<c\right\}\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})\left(G_{i,t}(\ell)-\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})^{\top}p_{-}(\theta_{n}^{*})\right),

\displaystyle\psi_{H,\,n,\,i,\,+}:=\sqrt{h}\,\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}\,K_{h}(Z_{i,t})\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{i,t}\geq c\right\}\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})\left(H_{i,t}(\ell)-\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})^{\top}p_{+}(\eta_{n}^{*})\right),

\displaystyle\psi_{H,\,n,\,i,\,-}:=\sqrt{h}\,\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}\,K_{h}(Z_{i,t})\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{i,t}<c\right\}\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})\left(H_{i,t}(\ell)-\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})^{\top}p_{-}(\eta_{n}^{*})\right),

where for a vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
,
p_{-}(v):=(v_{1},v_{3})
and
p_{+}(v):=(v_{1}+v_{2},v_{3}+v_{4})
. In other words,
p_{-}(\theta_{n}^{*})
and
p_{+}(\theta_{n}^{*})
respectively recover the coefficients from local linear regressions similar to (
A.11
) that are fitted separately on either side of the threshold. The desired joint asymptotic normality is established by combining (
C.1
) with
Lemma

C.8
. To illustrate the key argument, we present the derivation for a single marginal distribution below.

It follows from the normal equations that
\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,\pm}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[\psi_{H,\,n,\,i,\,\pm}]=0
. We show in
Lemma

C.8
that

{\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}\left(\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+}\right)\to\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{2t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\left(\sigma^{G,2}_{t}(S_{t},\,c,\,1)+(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,c,\,1)-\mu_{G,\,1}(c))^{2}\right)\,f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]\Lambda_{+},

and

{\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}\left(\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,-}\right)\to\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{2t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\left(\sigma^{G,2}_{t}(S_{t},\,c,\,0)+(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,c,\,0)-\mu_{G,\,0}(c))^{2}\right)\,f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]\Lambda_{-},

where
\Lambda_{\pm}=(((\pm 1)^{i+j-1}\rho_{i+j}))_{0\leq i,\,j\leq 1}
,
\rho_{j}=\int_{0}^{1}u^{j}\,K^{2}(u)\,du
,
j\geq 0
, and
\sigma^{G,\,2}_{t}
denotes the conditional variance function:

\sigma^{G,\,2}_{t}(s,\,z,\,a):={\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}\left(G_{i,t}\mid S_{i,t}=s,\,Z_{i,t}=z,\,A_{i,t}=a\right).

Note that

e_{1}^{\top}M_{\pm}^{-1}\Lambda_{\pm}M_{\pm}^{-1}e_{1}=(\kappa_{2}^{2}\rho_{0}-2\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\rho_{1}+\kappa_{0}^{2}\rho_{2})/(\kappa_{0}\kappa_{2}-\kappa_{1}^{2})^{2}=\xi_{2},

and that
{\rm Cov}_{\pi_{c}}(\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+},\,\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+})=0
. Thus,

{\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}(e_{1}^{\top}M_{+}^{-1}\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+}-e_{1}^{\top}M_{-}^{-1}\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,-})\\[5.69054pt]
\to\sum_{a=0,1}\sum_{t\geq 0}\gamma^{2t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\left(\sigma^{G,2}_{t}(S_{t},\,c,\,a)+(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,c,\,a)-\mu_{G,\,a}(c))^{2}\right)f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right].

It is elementary to note that the above summands can be simplified as follows.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\left(\sigma^{G,2}_{t}(S_{t},\,c,\,a)+(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,c,\,a)-\mu_{G,\,a}(c))^{2}\right)f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\left(\mathbb{E}[G_{t}^{2}\mid S_{t},\,Z_{t}=c,\,A_{t}=a]-Q_{c,\,t}^{2}(S_{t},\,c,\,a)+(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,c,\,a)-\mu_{G,\,a}(c))^{2}\right)f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\left(G_{t}-\mu_{G,\,a}(c)\right)^{2}\mid S_{t},\,Z_{t}=c,\,A_{t}=a\right]f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right].

(C.2)

We further show in
Lemma

C.8
that for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\left\|\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,\pm}\right\|^{2}\mathbf{1}\left\{\left\|\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,\pm}\right\|>\varepsilon\sqrt{n}\right\}\right]\to 0.

The above conditions taken together allow us to apply the Lindeberg-Feller CLT and conclude that

\sqrt{nh}\,(\widehat{\tau}_{G,\,n}-\tau_{G,\,n}^{*})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\bar{f}_{c})^{-1}(e_{1}^{\top}M_{+}^{-1}\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+}-e_{1}^{\top}M_{-}^{-1}\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,-})+o_{p}(1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}(0,V_{G}),

where

is as defined in (
13
).
The joint asymptotic normality of
\widehat{\tau}_{G,\,n}
and
\widehat{\tau}_{H,\,n}
follows from (
C.1
) using arguments similar to above.

\square

Lemma C.2

(Kernel localization with discounting)
.

Suppose that
1
,
2
and
3
hold, and that
h=h(n)\to 0
,
\ell(n)\to\infty
,
T(n)-\ell(n)\to\infty
and
\gamma^{\ell(n)}=o(h(n))
. Define the the discounted sum of future outcomes
G_{i,t}
as in (
10
) with
T=\infty
, and define its truncated version
G_{i,t}(\ell(n))
as in (
16
). Assume furthermore that,

(i)

K:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
is bounded, symmetric, supported on
[-1,1]
, and integrable,

(ii)

z\mapsto f_{t}(z\mid s)
is continuous at
c
for almost every
s
.

(iii)

\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is
L_{\psi}
-Lipschitz for some
L_{\psi}>0
.

(iv)

For each
t\geq 0
,
z\mapsto\mu^{\psi}_{t}(s,\,z,\,1)
is right-continuous at
c
and
z\mapsto\mu^{\psi}_{t}(s,\,z,\,0)
is left-continuous at
c
for almost every
s
, where
\mu^{\psi}_{t}(s,\,z,\,a):=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[\psi(G_{i,t})\mid S_{i,t}=s,\,Z_{i,t}=z,\,A_{i,t}=a]
.

(v)

For some
\eta>0
, there exist envelopes
B_{f,\,t}
and
B_{\mu,\,t}
such that
\sup_{c<z<c+\eta}|\mu^{\psi}_{t}(s,\,z,\,1)|\vee 1\leq B_{\mu,\,t}(s)
as well as
\sup_{c-\eta<z<c}|\mu^{\psi}_{t}(s,\,z,\,0)|\vee 1\leq B_{\mu,\,t}(s)
, and
\sup_{|z-c|\leq\eta}f_{t}(z\mid s)\vee 1\leq B_{f,\,t}(s)
for a.e.
s
. Moreover, assume that
\sum_{t\geq 0}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[B_{\mu,\,t}(S_{i,t})\,B_{f,\,t}(S_{i,t})]<\infty
.

(vi)

\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}|Y_{t}|<\infty
.

Define
K_{h}(z):=h^{-1}K(|z-c|/h)
,
\phi_{j,h}(z)=((z-c)/h)^{j}
and
\kappa_{j}:=\int_{0}^{1}u^{j}K(u)du
,
j\geq 0
. Then for each
j\geq 0
, it holds that

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\,\sum_{t=0}^{T(n)-\ell(n)}\gamma^{t}K_{h(n)}(Z_{i,t})\,\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{i,t}\geq c\right\}\,\phi_{j,h(n)}(Z_{i,t})\,\psi(G_{i,t}(\ell(n)))\right]\\
=\kappa_{j}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\mu^{\psi}_{t}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,1)\,f_{t}(c\mid S_{i,t})\right],\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\,\sum_{t=0}^{T(n)-\ell(n)}\gamma^{t}K_{h(n)}(Z_{i,t})\,\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{i,t}<c\right\}\,\phi_{j,h(n)}(Z_{i,t})\,\psi(G_{i,t}(\ell(n)))\right]\\
=(-1)^{j}\kappa_{j}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\mu^{\psi}_{t}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,0)\,f_{t}(c\mid S_{i,t})\right].\end{split}

Proof.

We establish the first claim; the second follows by analogous reasoning. Since units are identically distributed, we drop the unit index (
i
) throughout the proof. Our approach relies on a standard three-way error decomposition as follows.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\,\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{t})\,\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}\phi_{j,h}(Z_{t})\,\psi(G_{t}(\ell))\right]-\kappa_{j}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\mu^{\psi}_{t}(S_{t},\,c,\,1)\,f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\,\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{t})\,\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}\phi_{j,h}(Z_{t})\,(\psi(G_{t}(\ell))-\psi(G_{t}))\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\,\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{t})\,\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}\phi_{j,h}(Z_{t})\,\psi(G_{t})\right]-\kappa_{j}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\mu^{\psi}_{t}(S_{t},\,c,\,1)\,f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\kappa_{j}\sum_{t=T-\ell+1}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\mu^{\psi}_{t}(S_{t},\,c,\,1)\,f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]

\displaystyle=:E_{1}+E_{2}+E_{3}.

To tackle the error term
E_{1}
, note that

\sup_{z}\left|K_{h}(z)\mathbf{1}\left\{z>c\right\}\phi_{j,h}(z)\right|\leq\frac{\|K\|_{\infty}}{h}\sup_{c<z<c+h}|\phi_{j,h}(z)|\leq\frac{\|K\|_{\infty}}{h}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|E_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{t})\,\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}|\phi_{j,h}(Z_{t})|\,|\psi(G_{t}(\ell))-\psi(G_{t})|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}\frac{\|K\|_{\infty}}{h}L_{\psi}|G_{t}(\ell)-G_{t}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|K\|_{\infty}L_{\psi}}{h}\,\gamma^{\ell}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}\left|G_{t+\ell}\right|

(since
G_{t}-G_{t}(\ell)=\gamma^{\ell}G_{t+\ell}
)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|K\|_{\infty}L_{\psi}}{h}\cdot\frac{\gamma^{\ell}}{1-\gamma}\left(\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}|G_{t}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|K\|_{\infty}L_{\psi}}{h}\cdot\frac{\gamma^{\ell}}{1-\gamma}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\left(\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}|Y_{t}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\frac{\gamma^{\ell}}{h},

for some universal constant
C_{1}\in(0,\infty)
. We can derive a similar upper bound for the error term
E_{3}
, as follows. Since
\sup_{c<z<c+\eta}|\mu^{\psi}_{t}(s,\,z,\,1)|\leq B_{\mu,\,t}(s)
and
\sup_{c<z<c+\eta}f_{t}(z\mid s)\leq B_{f,\,t}(s)
with
\sum_{t\geq 0}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[B_{\mu,\,t}(S_{t})B_{f,\,t}(S_{t})]<\infty
, we deduce that

\displaystyle|E_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{j}\sum_{t=T-\ell+1}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\left|\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\mu^{\psi}_{t}(S_{t},\,c,\,1)f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]\right|\leq\kappa_{j}\sum_{t=T-\ell+1}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[B_{\mu,\,t}(S_{t})\,B_{f,\,t}(S_{t})\right]=o(1).

The second error term (
E_{2}
) can be handled similarly to the proof of
Lemma

B.2
. We first simplify it as follows.

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[K_{h}(Z_{t})\,\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}\phi_{j,h}(Z_{t})\,\psi(G_{t})\right]-\kappa_{j}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\mu^{\psi}_{t}(S_{t},\,c,\,1)\,f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[K_{h}(Z_{t})\,\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}\phi_{j,h}(Z_{t})\,\mu^{\psi}_{t}(S_{t},\,Z_{t},\,1)\right]-\kappa_{j}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\mu(S_{t},\,c,\,1)\,f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]\right)

(tower property)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\Delta_{t}(h\,;S_{t}),

where

\Delta_{t}(h\,;s):=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[K_{h}(Z_{t})\,\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}\phi_{j,h}(Z_{t})\,\mu^{\psi}_{t}(s,\,Z_{t},\,1)\mid S_{t}=s\right]-\kappa_{j}\mu^{\psi}_{t}(s,\,c,\,1)\,f_{t}(c\mid s).

Since
Z_{t}\mid S_{t}\sim f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[K_{h}(Z_{t})\,\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}\phi_{j,h}(Z_{t})\,\mu^{\psi}_{t}(s,Z_{t},1)\mid S_{t}=s\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{c}^{c+h}K_{h}(z)\phi_{j,h}(z)\mu^{\psi}_{t}(s,z,1)f_{t}(z\mid s)\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}u^{j}K(u)\mu^{\psi}_{t}(s,c+hu,1)f_{t}(c+hu\mid s)\,du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}u^{j}K(u)g_{t}(c+hu\,;s)\,du,

where

g_{t}(z\,;s)=\mu^{\psi}_{t}(s,\,z,\,1)f_{t}(z\mid s).

On the other hand, we can write
\displaystyle\kappa_{j}\mu^{\psi}_{t}(s,\,c,\,1)\,f_{t}(c\mid s)=\int_{0}^{1}u^{j}K(u)g_{t}(c+\,;s)\,du
, and thus by the triangle inequality for integrals,

\left|\Delta_{t}(h\,;s)\right|\leq\int_{0}^{1}u^{j}K(u)\left|g_{t}(c+hu\,;s)-g_{t}(c\,;s)\right|du\leq\kappa_{j}M_{t}(h\,;s),

where
M_{t}(h\,;s)
is the modulus of continuity for
z\mapsto g_{t}(z\,;s)
at
c
, defined as

M_{t}(h\,;s):=\sup_{u\in[0,1]}\left|g_{t}(c+hu\,;s)-g_{t}(c+\,;s)\right|.

It follows from the smoothness assumptions on
\mu^{\psi}_{t}
and
f
that
M_{t}(h\,;s)\to 0
as
h\to 0
for a.e.
s
. In particular, if
\mu^{\psi}_{t}(s,\cdot\,,1)
is
L_{\mu}(s)
-Lipschitz and
f(\,\cdot\mid s)
is
L_{f}(s)
-Lipschitz, then
M_{t}(h\,;s)\leq ChL_{f}(s)L_{\mu}(s)
for some universal constant
C
. Next,
\sup_{c<z<c+\eta}|\mu^{\psi}_{t}(s,\,z,\,a)|\vee 1\leq B_{\mu,\,t}(s)
(
a=0,1
) and
\sup_{|z-c|\leq\eta}f_{t}(z\mid s)\vee 1\leq B_{f,\,t}(s)
imply that

M_{t}(h\,;s)\leq C_{2}\,B_{f,\,t}(s)\,B_{\mu,\,t}(s),

for some universal constant
C_{2}
, uniformly for
|h|\leq\eta
. Since
\sum_{t\geq 0}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[B_{f,\,t}(S_{t})\,B_{\mu,\,t}(S_{t})]<\infty
, we can apply the dominated convergence theorem to conclude that

|E_{2}|\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}|\Delta_{t}(h\,;S_{t})|\leq\kappa_{j}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}M_{t}(h\,;S_{t})\to 0,

as
h\to 0
. This completes the proof.

\square

Lemma C.3

(Invariance under rescaling kernel and regressors)
.

Define the rescaled kernel

and the rescaled regressor

Proof.

Using the explicit formula for the solution of the weighted least squares problem (

(C.3)

\displaystyle\widehat{A}_{n}(\phi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{i,t})\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{i,t}\geq c\right\}\phi(Z_{i,t})\,G_{i,t}(\ell),

\displaystyle\widehat{B}_{n}(\phi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{i,t})\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{i,t}\geq c\right\}\phi(Z_{i,t})^{\otimes 2},

\displaystyle\widehat{C}_{n}(\phi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{i,t})\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{i,t}<c\right\}\phi(Z_{i,t})\,G_{i,t}(\ell),

\displaystyle\widehat{D}_{n}(\phi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{i,t})\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{i,t}<c\right\}\phi(Z_{i,t})^{\otimes 2}.

Note that the rescaling

worked out above due to a cancellation in (

Similarly we can write

\tau_{G,\,n}^{*}=e_{1}^{\top}(B_{n}^{*}(\phi))^{-1}A_{n}^{*}(\phi)-e_{1}^{\top}(D_{n}^{*}(\phi))^{-1}C_{n}^{*}(\phi),

where

\displaystyle A_{n}^{*}(\phi)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{t})\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}\phi(Z_{t})\,G_{t}(\ell),\quad B_{n}^{*}(\phi)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{t})\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}\phi(Z_{t})^{\otimes 2},

and
C_{n}^{*}
and
D_{n}^{*}
are defined in an analogous manner.
It only remains to show that we can replace
\phi
with
\phi_{h}
, where
\phi_{h}(z):=(1,(z-c)/h)
. We observe the following.

\circ

for
v\in\{\widehat{A}_{n},\,A_{n}^{*},\,\widehat{C}_{n},\,C^{*}_{n}\}

, where

,

\circ

.

Consequently, for

This finishes the proofs for
\widehat{\tau}_{G,\,n}
and

\square

Lemma C.4

(Consistency of the population-level optimizer)
.

Define the population-level optimizer
\theta_{n}^{*}
and
\eta_{n}^{*}
as in (
A.11
), and denote

Under the conditions of

, it holds that

and

C.2

C.2
with

Lemma C.5

(Concentration of empirical optimizer around population-level optimizer)
.

Define the population-level optimizer
\theta_{n}^{*}
and
\eta_{n}^{*}
as in (
A.11
). Under the conditions of
Theorem

8
, we have

e_{2}^{\top}\widehat{\theta}(h(n))-e_{2}^{\top}\theta^{*}_{n}=o_{p}(1),\quad\text{and}\quad e_{2}^{\top}\widehat{\eta}(h(n))-e_{2}^{\top}\eta^{*}_{n}=o_{p}(1),\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Proof.

We use the expressions for
\widehat{\tau}_{G,\,n}=e_{2}^{\top}\widehat{\theta}(h(n))
and
\tau_{G,\,n}^{*}=e_{2}^{\top}\theta^{*}_{n}
as stated in
Lemma

C.3
.
We first show that
\widehat{A}_{n}-A_{n}^{*}=o_{p}(1)
. Recall the definitions

\displaystyle\widehat{A}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{i,t})\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{i,t}\geq c\right\}\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})\,G_{i,t}(\ell),

\displaystyle A_{n}^{*}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{t})\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}\phi_{h}(Z_{t})\,G_{t}(\ell),

where
K_{h}(z)=h^{-1}K((z-c)/h)
and
\phi_{h}(z)=(1,(z-c)/h)^{\top}
.
Since each entry of
\phi_{h}(\cdot)
is bounded by
1
on the support of the Kernel term
K_{h}(\cdot)
, we derive using Chebyshev inequality and independence across the units that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|e_{j}^{\top}(\widehat{A}_{n}-{A}_{n}^{*})|>\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{-2}{\rm Var}\left(e_{j}^{\top}\widehat{A}_{n}\right)\leq\varepsilon^{-2}n^{-1}\|U_{n,1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where

U_{n,i}:=\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{i,t})\,\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{i,t}\geq c\right\}G_{i,t}(\ell).

Note that

\displaystyle\left\|K_{h}(Z_{t})\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}(G_{t}-G_{t}(\ell))\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|K_{h}\|_{\infty}\|G_{t}-G_{t}(\ell)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{h}\|K\|_{\infty}\,\gamma^{\ell}\|G_{t+\ell}\|_{L^{2}}

(since
G_{t}-G_{t}(\ell)=\gamma^{\ell}G_{t+\ell}
)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{h}\|K\|_{\infty}\,\gamma^{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{j}\|Y_{t+\ell+j}\|_{L^{2}}

(Minkowski’s inequality)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma^{\ell}}{h}\|K\|_{\infty}\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\left(\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[Y_{t}^{2}]\right)^{1/2}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\left\|K_{h}(Z_{t})\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}G_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[K_{h}(Z_{t})^{2}\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}m_{2}(S_{t},\,Z_{t},\,1)\mid S_{t}\right]

(tower property)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\frac{1}{h^{2}}\int_{c}^{c+h}K\left(\frac{z-c}{h}\right)^{2}f\left(z\mid S_{t}\right)\,m_{2}(S_{t},\,z,\,1)\,dz\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\frac{1}{h}\int_{0}^{1}K(u)^{2}f_{t}(c+hu\mid S_{t})\,m_{2}(S_{t},\,c+hu,\,1)\,du\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{h}\|K\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[f\left(c\mid S_{t}\right)m_{2}(S_{t},\,c,\,1)\right]+\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\delta(h\,;S_{t})\right]\right),

where

\delta(h\,;s):=\sup_{u\in[0,1]}\left|f_{t}(c+hu\mid s)\,m_{2}(s,\,c+hu,\,1)-f_{t}(c\mid s)\,m_{2}(s,\,c,\,1)\right|.

The smoothness assumptions on
f
and
m_{2}
yield that
\delta(h\,;s)\to 0
as
h\to 0
. Therefore, by the dominated convergence theorem (as used in the proof of
Lemma

C.2
), we have
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\delta(h\,;S_{t})\to 0
as
h\to 0
.
We can now use the triangle inequality to write

\displaystyle\left\|U_{n,1}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}\left\|K_{h}(Z_{t})\pi_{c}(Z_{t})G_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}\left\|K_{h}(Z_{t})\pi_{c}(Z_{t})(G_{t}-G_{t})(\ell)\right\|_{L^{2}}=O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}+\frac{\gamma^{\ell}}{h}\right),

which in turn implies that
{\rm Var}(e_{j}^{\top}\widehat{A}_{n})=O((nh)^{-1})
. Since
nh\to\infty
, we can conclude that

\widehat{A}_{n}-A_{n}^{*}=O_{p}((nh)^{-1/2})=o_{p}(1).

The same argument applies entrywise to
\widehat{C}_{n}
.
For
\widehat{B}_{n}
and
\widehat{D}_{n}
replace
G_{i,t}(\ell)
by
\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})
. Note that for any
j,k\in\{0,1\}
,
{\rm Var}(e_{j}^{\top}\widehat{B}_{n}e_{k})\leq n^{-1}\|V_{n,1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
where

V_{n,i}:=\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}\left(Z_{i,t}\right)\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{i,t}\geq c\right\}.

It follows by a calculation similar to above that

\left\|V_{n,1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ch^{-1/2}
. Consequently,

\widehat{B}_{n}-B_{n}^{*}=O_{p}((nh)^{-1/2})=o_{p}(1).

To justify the convergence of the inverses, we apply an eigenvalue argument.
Recall that
Lemma

C.2
yields

B_{n}^{*}\to\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})]\begin{bmatrix}\kappa_{0}&\kappa_{1}\\
\kappa_{1}&\kappa_{2}\end{bmatrix}.

Denote the above limiting matrix by
\Sigma
. We can use continuity to claim that

\lambda_{\min}(B_{n}^{*})\to\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma),

where
\lambda_{\min}(A)
denotes the smallest eigenvalue for a real symmetric matrix
A
. Consequently, we can deduce using Weyl’s inequality that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}(\widehat{B}_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{\min}(B_{n}^{*})-\|\widehat{B}_{n})-B_{n}^{*}\|\geq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma),

with probability tending to
1
. This tells us that
\widehat{B}_{n}
is invertible w.h.p., and thus we can use the identity

\widehat{B}_{n}^{-1}-(B_{n}^{*})^{-1}=(B_{n}^{*})^{-1}\left(B_{n}^{*}-\widehat{B}_{n}\right)\widehat{B}_{n}^{-1},

to conclude that

\left\|\widehat{B}_{n}^{-1}-(B_{n}^{*})^{-1}\right\|\leq\left\|(B_{n}^{*})^{-1}\right\|\left\|\widehat{B}_{n}-B_{n}^{*}\right\|\left\|\widehat{B}_{n}^{-1}\right\|=o_{p}(1).

An identical argument applies to
\widehat{D}_{n}
. Putting everything together,

\widehat{\tau}_{G,\,n}-\tau_{G,\,n}^{*}={e_{1}^{\top}(\widehat{B}_{n}^{-1}-B_{n}^{*-1})\,\widehat{A}_{n}}+{e_{1}^{\top}B_{n}^{*-1}(\widehat{A}_{n}-A_{n}^{*})}\\
-{e_{1}^{\top}(\widehat{D}_{n}^{-1}-D_{n}^{*-1})\,\widehat{C}_{n}}-{e_{1}^{\top}D_{n}^{*-1}(\widehat{C}_{n}-C_{n}^{*})}=o_{p}(1),

as desired to show. The proof of
\widehat{\tau}_{H,\,n}-\tau_{H,\,n}^{*}=o_{p}(1)
is analogous.

\square

Lemma C.6

(Second-order bias expansion)
.

Define the population-level optimizer
\theta_{n}^{*}
and
\eta_{n}^{*}
as in (
A.11
), and their respective limits
\tau_{G}^{*}
and
\tau_{H}^{*}
as in (
A.12
). Define
\tau_{G,\,n}^{*}=e_{2}^{\top}\theta_{n}^{*}
and
\tau_{H,\,n}^{*}=e_{2}^{\top}\eta_{n}^{*}
.
Under the assumptions of
Theorem

9
, we have that

\tau_{G,\,n}^{*}-\tau_{G}^{*}=\frac{h^{2}}{2}\,\xi_{1}\Delta\mu^{\prime\prime}_{G}(c)+{o}(h^{2}),\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{H,\,n}^{*}-\tau_{H}^{*}=\frac{h^{2}}{2}\,\xi_{1}\Delta\mu^{\prime\prime}_{H}(c)+{o}(h^{2}),

where
\xi_{1}:=(\kappa_{2}^{2}-\kappa_{1}\kappa_{3})/(\kappa_{0}\kappa_{2}-\kappa_{1}^{2})
with
\kappa_{j}:=\int_{0}^{1}u^{j}K(u)du
, and the functions
\Delta\mu_{G}
and
\Delta\mu_{H}
are as defined in
Lemma

C.1
.

Proof.

Here we essentially sharpen
Lemma

C.4
by leveraging higher-order smoothness (bounded second derivatives). Recall from
Lemma

C.3
that
\tau_{G,\,n}^{*}=e_{1}^{\top}(B_{n}^{*})^{-1}A_{n}^{*}-e_{1}^{\top}(D_{n}^{*})^{-1}C_{n}^{*}
, where

A_{n}^{*}=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{t})\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}\phi_{h}(Z_{t})\,G_{t}(\ell),\quad B_{n}^{*}=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{t})\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}\phi_{h}(Z_{t})^{\otimes 2},

C_{n}^{*}=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{t})\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}<c\right\}\phi_{h}(Z_{t})\,G_{t}(\ell),\quad D_{n}^{*}=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{t})\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}<c\right\}\phi_{h}(Z_{t})^{\otimes 2},

with
\phi_{h}(z):=(1,(z-c)/h)
. Define
\phi_{j,h}(z)=((z-c)/h)^{j}
,
j\geq 0
. It follows from the proof of

Lemma

C.2
with
\psi(x)\equiv x
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\,\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{t})\,\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}\phi_{j,h}(Z_{t})\,G_{t}(\ell)\right]-\kappa_{j}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}\left[Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,c,\,1)\,f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\,\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{t})\,\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}\phi_{j,h}(Z_{t})\,G_{t}\right]-\kappa_{j}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,c,\,1)\,f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\,\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{t})\,\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}\phi_{j,h}(Z_{t})\,(G_{t}(\ell)-G_{t})\right]

(this is
O(\gamma^{\ell}/h)
)

\displaystyle\qquad+\kappa_{j}\sum_{t=T-\ell+1}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,c,\,1)\,f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]

(this is
O(\gamma^{T-\ell})
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}K_{h}(Z_{t})\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}\phi_{j,h}(Z_{t})\,G_{t}-\kappa_{j}\,Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,c,\,1)\,f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]+o(h^{2})

(since
\gamma^{\ell}/h+\gamma^{T-\ell}=o(h^{2})
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\Delta_{j}(h\,;S_{t})+o(h^{2}),

(C.4)

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}(h\,;s)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[K_{h}(Z_{t})\,\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{t}\geq c\right\}\phi_{j,h}(Z_{t})\,Q_{c,\,t}(s,\,Z_{t},\,1)\mid S_{t}=s\right]-\kappa_{j}Q_{c,\,t}(s,\,c,\,1)\,f_{t}(c\mid s)

\displaystyle=\int_{c}^{c+h}K_{h}(z)\phi_{j,h}(z)Q_{c,\,t}(s,\,z,\,1)f_{t}(z\mid s)dz

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}K(u)\,u^{j}\,{Q_{c,\,t}(s,c+hu,1)\,f_{t}(c+hu\mid s)}\,du.

(C.5)

Define
g(s,\,z):=Q_{c,\,t}(s,\,z,\,1)f_{t}(z\mid s)
. Since
Q_{c,\,t}(s,\,\cdot\,,\,1)
and
f(\,\cdot\mid s)
both are twice continuously differentiable at
c
with their second derivatives locally bounded by an integrable envelopes
B_{Q^{\prime\prime}}(s)
and
B_{f^{\prime\prime}}(s)
respectively, it follows that
g(s,\,\cdot\,)
is also twice continuously differentiable at
c
with its second derivative locally bounded by an integrable envelope
B_{g^{\prime\prime}}(s)
.
We thus have the following Taylor expansion.

g(s,\,c+hu)=g(s,\,c)+g^{\prime}_{z}(s,\,c)\,hu+\frac{1}{2}(g^{\prime\prime}_{zz}(s,\,c)+R_{g}(s,\,h))\,h^{2}u^{2},

where the remainder
R_{g}(s,\,h)\to 0
as
h\to 0
, since
g^{\prime\prime}_{zz}(s,\,\cdot)
is continuous at
c
. Moreover,
g^{\prime\prime}_{zz}(s,\,\cdot)
is bounded around
c
by an integrable envelope, which implies that
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}R_{g}(h,\,S_{t})\to 0
by the dominated convergence theorem. We can therefore continue from (
C.5
) to say that

\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\Delta_{j}(h\,;S_{t})=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\left(\kappa_{j}g(S_{t},\,c)+\kappa_{j+1}\,h\,g^{\prime}_{z}(S_{t},\,c)+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\kappa_{j+2}\,g^{\prime\prime}_{zz}(S_{t},\,c)\right)+o(h^{2}).

This in conjunction with (
C.4
) yields

A_{n}^{*}=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\left(g(S_{t},\,c)\begin{bmatrix}\kappa_{0}\\
\kappa_{1}\end{bmatrix}+h\,g^{\prime}_{z}(S_{t},\,c)\begin{bmatrix}\kappa_{1}\\
\kappa_{2}\end{bmatrix}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}g^{\prime\prime}_{zz}(S_{t},\,c)\begin{bmatrix}\kappa_{2}\\
\kappa_{3}\end{bmatrix}\right)+o(h^{2}).

(C.6)

A similar argument with
\phi_{h}(Z_{t})^{\otimes 2}
instead of
\phi_{h}(Z_{t})\,G_{t}
gives us the following.

\displaystyle B_{n}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}\,\int_{0}^{1}K(u)\begin{bmatrix}1&u\\
u&u^{2}\end{bmatrix}f_{t}(c+hu\mid s)\,du+o(h^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}\,\int_{0}^{1}K(u)\begin{bmatrix}1&u\\
u&u^{2}\end{bmatrix}\left(f_{t}(c\mid s)+huf^{\prime}_{z}(c\mid s)\,du+\frac{h^{2}u^{2}}{2}f^{\prime\prime}_{zz}(c\mid s)\right)\,du+o(h^{2})

\displaystyle=M_{0}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})+h\,M_{1}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}f^{\prime}_{z}(c\mid S_{t})+\frac{h^{2}}{2}M_{2}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}f^{\prime\prime}_{zz}(c\mid S_{t})+o(h^{2}),

(C.7)

where

M_{j}=\begin{bmatrix}\kappa_{j}&\kappa_{j+1}\\
\kappa_{j+1}&\kappa_{j+2}\end{bmatrix},\quad j=0,1,2.

Before we deduce the corresponding expansion for
(B_{n}^{*})^{-1}
from above, we introduce some notation. Define, for
j=0,1,2
,

S^{(j)}_{f}(z):=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial z^{j}}f_{t}(z\mid S_{t}),\quad\text{and}\quad S^{(j)}_{g}(z):=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial z^{j}}g(S_{t},\,z).

With this notation, we can rewrite (
C.6
) and (
C.7
) compactly as follows.

A_{n}^{*}=S^{(0)}_{g}(c)\begin{bmatrix}\kappa_{0}\\
\kappa_{1}\end{bmatrix}+h\,S^{(1)}_{g}(c)\begin{bmatrix}\kappa_{1}\\
\kappa_{2}\end{bmatrix}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}S^{(2)}_{g}(c)\begin{bmatrix}\kappa_{2}\\
\kappa_{3}\end{bmatrix}+o(h^{2}),

(C.8)

and

B_{n}^{*}=M_{0}S^{(0)}_{f}(c)+hM_{1}S^{(1)}_{f}(c)+\frac{h^{2}}{2}M_{2}S^{(2)}_{f}(c)+o(h^{2}).

(C.9)

We now use a standard Taylor expansion of the inverse map
A\mapsto A^{-1}
(or equivalently, matching orders in Neumann series) to arrive at the following.

N(h)=N_{0}+hN_{1}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}N_{2}+o(h^{2})\\
\implies N(h)^{-1}=N_{0}^{-1}-h\,N_{0}^{-1}N_{1}N_{0}^{-1}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\!\left(2N_{0}^{-1}N_{1}N_{0}^{-1}N_{1}N_{0}^{-1}-N_{0}^{-1}N_{2}N_{0}^{-1}\right)+o(h^{2}).

The above combined with (
C.9
) yields

(B_{n}^{*})^{-1}=\frac{1}{S^{(0)}_{f}(c)}M_{0}^{-1}-h\,\frac{S^{(1)}_{f}(c)}{S^{(0)}_{f}(c)^{2}}M_{0}^{-1}M_{1}M_{0}^{-1}+h^{2}\,\frac{S^{(1)}_{f}(c)^{2}}{S^{(0)}_{f}(c)^{3}}M_{0}^{-1}M_{1}M_{0}^{-1}M_{1}M_{0}^{-1}\\
-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\frac{S^{(2)}_{f}(c)}{S^{(0)}_{f}(c)^{2}}M_{0}^{-1}M_{2}M_{0}^{-1}+o(h^{2})=:(\mathrm{I})-(\mathrm{II})+(\mathrm{III})-(\mathrm{IV})+o(h^{2}).

(C.10)

The above display in conjunction with (
C.8
) helps us derive an expansion for
e_{1}^{\top}(B_{n}^{*})^{-1}A_{n}^{*}
upto
o(h^{2})
, as follows.

\displaystyle e_{1}^{\top}(\mathrm{I})A_{n}^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{S^{(0)}_{g}(c)}{S^{(0)}_{f}(c)}e_{1}^{\top}M_{0}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}\kappa_{0}\\
\kappa_{1}\end{bmatrix}+h\,\frac{S^{(1)}_{g}(c)}{S^{(0)}_{f}(c)}e_{1}^{\top}M_{0}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}\kappa_{1}\\
\kappa_{2}\end{bmatrix}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\frac{S^{(2)}_{g}(c)}{S^{(0)}_{f}(c)}e_{1}^{\top}M_{0}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}\kappa_{2}\\
\kappa_{3}\end{bmatrix}+o(h^{2})

Lemma C.8

(Limiting variances and Lindeberg condition)
.

Under the conditions of
Theorem

9
, it holds that

{\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}\left(\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+}\right)\to\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{2t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\left(\sigma^{G,2}_{t}(S_{t},\,c,\,1)+(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,c,\,1)-\mu_{G,\,1}(c))^{2}\right)\,f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]\Lambda_{+},

and

{\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}\left(\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,-}\right)\to\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{2t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\left(\sigma^{G,2}_{t}(S_{t},\,c,\,0)+(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,c,\,0)-\mu_{G,\,0}(c))^{2}\right)\,f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]\Lambda_{-},

where
\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+}
and
\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,-}
are as defined in
Lemma

C.7
, and
\Lambda_{\pm}=(((\pm 1)^{i+j-1}\rho_{i+j}))_{0\leq i,\,j\leq 1}
where
\rho_{j}=\int_{0}^{1}u^{j}\,K^{2}(u)\,du
, and
\sigma^{G,\,2}_{t}
denotes the conditional variance function:

\sigma^{G,\,2}_{t}(s,\,z,\,a):={\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}\left(G_{i,t}\mid S_{i,t}=s,\,Z_{i,t}=z,\,A_{i,t}=a\right).

Moreover, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\left\|\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,\pm}\right\|^{2}\mathbf{1}\left\{\left\|\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,\pm}\right\|>\varepsilon\sqrt{n}\right\}\right]=0.

Proof.

We only prove the claims for
\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+}
; the proofs of the corresponding claims for
\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,-}
are analogous.
Write

\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+}=\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t}

where

\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t}:=\sqrt{h}\,\gamma^{t}\,K_{h}(Z_{i,t})\,\mathbf{1}\left\{Z_{i,t}\geq c\right\}\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})\,(G_{i,t}(\ell)-\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})^{\top}p_{+}(\theta_{n}^{*})).

We first show that

\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}{\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}\left(\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t}\mid S_{i,t},\,Z_{i,t},\,A_{i,t}\right)\to\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{2t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\sigma^{G,2}_{t}(S_{t},\,c,\,1)\,f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]\Lambda_{+}.

(C.11)

Define
\mathcal{H}_{i,t}:=\sigma(\{S_{i,t},\,Z_{i,t},\,A_{i,t}\})
,
t\geq 0
.
It is elementary that

\displaystyle\sigma^{G,2}_{t}(S_{i,t},\,Z_{i,t},\,A_{i,t})-\sigma^{G,2}_{t,\,\ell}(S_{i,t},\,Z_{i,t},\,A_{i,t})

\displaystyle={\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}(G_{i,t}\mid\mathcal{H}_{i,t})-{\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}(G_{i,t}(\ell)\mid\mathcal{H}_{i,t})

(by definition)

\displaystyle={\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}(G_{i,t}-G_{i,t}(\ell)\mid\mathcal{H}_{i,t})+2\,{\rm Cov}_{\pi_{c}}(G_{i,t}(\ell),\,G_{i,t}-G_{i,t}(\ell)\mid\mathcal{H}_{i,t})

\displaystyle=\gamma^{2\ell}\,{\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}(G_{i,t+\ell}\mid\mathcal{H}_{i,t})+2\gamma^{\ell}\,{\rm Cov}_{\pi_{c}}(G_{i,t}(\ell),\,G_{i,t+\ell}\mid\mathcal{H}_{i,t}).

(C.12)

where in the last step we used
G_{i,t}-G_{i,t}(\ell)=\gamma^{\ell}G_{i,t+\ell}
.
Observe now that with
\phi_{j,h}(z)=(z-c)^{j}/h^{j}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{2t}\,h\,K^{2}_{h}(Z_{i,t})\,A_{i,t}\,\phi_{j,h}(Z_{i,t})\,{\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}(G_{i,t+\ell}\mid\mathcal{H}_{i,t})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|K\|_{\infty}^{2}}{h}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{2t}\,{\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}(G_{i,t+\ell}\mid\mathcal{H}_{i,t})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|K\|_{\infty}^{2}}{h}\,\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{2t}\,\sup_{t\geq 0}\,{\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}(G_{i,t+\ell})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|K\|_{\infty}^{2}}{h}\,\frac{1}{(1-\gamma^{2})(1-\gamma)}\,\sup_{t\geq 0}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[Y_{i,t}^{2}]\leq\frac{C}{h}.

(C.13)

Similarly,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{2t}\,h\,K^{2}_{h}(Z_{i,t})\,A_{i,t}\,\phi_{j,h}(Z_{i,t})\,{\rm Cov}_{\pi_{c}}(G_{i,t}(\ell),\,G_{i,t+\ell}\mid\mathcal{H}_{i,t})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|K\|_{\infty}^{2}}{h}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{2t}\,{\rm Cov}_{\pi_{c}}(G_{i,t}(\ell),\,G_{i,t+\ell}\mid\mathcal{H}_{i,t})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|K\|_{\infty}^{2}}{h}\,\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{2t}\,\sup_{t\geq 0}\,{\rm Cov}_{\pi_{c}}(G_{i,t}(\ell),\,G_{i,t+\ell})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|K\|_{\infty}^{2}}{h}\,\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{2t}\,\sup_{t\geq 0}\,{\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}^{1/2}(G_{i,t}(\ell))\,\,{\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}^{1/2}(G_{i,t+\ell})

(Cauchy-Schwarz)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|K\|_{\infty}^{2}}{h}\,\frac{1}{(1-\gamma^{2})(1-\gamma)}\,\sup_{t\geq 0}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[Y_{i,t}^{2}]\leq\frac{C}{h}.

(C.14)

We can combine (
C.12
), (
C.13
) and (
C.14
) to deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{2t}\,h\,K^{2}_{h}(Z_{i,t})\,A_{i,t}\,\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})^{\otimes 2}(\sigma^{G,2}_{t}(S_{i,t},\,Z_{i,t},\,A_{i,t})-\sigma^{G,2}_{t,\,\ell}(S_{i,t},\,Z_{i,t},\,A_{i,t}))

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{\gamma^{\ell}}{h}\right)=o(1),

which in turn implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}{\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}\left(\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t}\mid S_{i,t},\,Z_{i,t},\,A_{i,t}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{2t}\,h\,K^{2}_{h}(Z_{i,t})\,A_{i,t}\,\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})^{\otimes 2}\,\sigma^{G,2}_{t,\,\ell}(S_{i,t},\,Z_{i,t},\,A_{i,t})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{2t}\,h\,K^{2}_{h}(Z_{i,t})\,A_{i,t}\,\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})^{\otimes 2}\,\sigma^{G,2}_{t}(S_{i,t},\,Z_{i,t},\,A_{i,t})+o(1)

C.5

C.6

We next show that

\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}{\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left(\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t}\mid S_{i,t},\,Z_{i,t},\,A_{i,t}\right)\to\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{2t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{t},\,c,\,1)-\mu_{G,\,1}(c))^{2}\,f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]\Lambda_{+}.

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}{\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left(\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t}\mid S_{i,t},\,Z_{i,t},\,A_{i,t}\right)

=\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}h\,\gamma^{2t}\,{\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}\left(K_{h}(Z_{i,t})\,A_{i,t}\,\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})\left(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{i,t},\,Z_{i,t},\,1)-\mu_{G,\,1}(c)\right)\right)+o(1).

Next, a standard Taylor expansion gives

Q_{c,\,t}(s,\,c+hu,\,1)=Q_{c,\,t}(s,\,c,\,1)+\left(\partial_{z}Q_{c,t}(s,\,c,\,1)+R(s,h)\right)hu,

where the remainder
R(s,h)
tends to
0
as
h\to 0
and is pointwise dominated by an integrable envelope (due to the assumptions we impose on
\partial_{zz}^{2}Q_{c}
). We can therefore continue from (
Appendix C
) to write

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}{\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left(\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t}\mid S_{i,t},\,Z_{i,t},\,A_{i,t}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}h\,\gamma^{2t}\,{\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}\left(w_{h}(Z_{i,t})\left(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,1)-\mu_{G,\,1}(c)\right)\right)

(
w_{h}(z):=K_{h}(z)\,\mathbf{1}\left\{z\geq c\right\}\,\phi_{h}(z)
)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}h^{2}\,\gamma^{2t}\,{\rm Cov}_{\pi_{c}}\,\bigg(w_{h}(Z_{i,t})\left(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,1)-\mu_{G,\,1}(c)\right),

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\,w_{h}(Z_{i,t})\left(\partial_{z}Q_{c,t}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,1)+R(S_{i,t},h)\right)\frac{(Z_{i,t}-c)}{h}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}h^{3}\,\gamma^{2t}\,{\rm Var}_{\pi_{c}}\left(w_{h}(Z_{i,t})\left(\partial_{z}Q_{c,t}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,1)+R(S_{i,t},h)\right)\frac{(Z_{i,t}-c)}{h}\right)+o(1)

\displaystyle=:U_{n}+h\,V_{n}+h^{2}\,W_{n}+o(1).

Note that once we show
U_{n}=O(1)
and
W_{n}=O(1)
, it follows via Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that
V_{n}=O(1)
and thus the above display is
U_{n}+O(h)
. Towards that,

\displaystyle U_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{2t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,1)-\mu_{G,\,1}(c))^{2}\,h\,\int_{c}^{c+h}K^{2}_{h}(z)\,\phi_{h}(z)^{\otimes 2}\,f_{t}(z\mid S_{i,t})\,dz\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{2t}\,h\left[\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left((Q_{c,\,t}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,1)-\mu_{G,\,1}(c))\,\int_{c}^{c+h}K_{h}(z)\,\phi_{h}(z)\,f_{t}(z\mid S_{i,t})\,dz\right)\right]^{\otimes 2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{2t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,1)-\mu_{G,\,1}(c))^{2}\,\int_{0}^{1}K^{2}(u)\,\begin{bmatrix}1\\
u\end{bmatrix}^{\otimes 2}\,f_{t}(c+hu\mid S_{i,t})\,du\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{2t}\,h\left[\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left((Q_{c,\,t}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,1)-\mu_{G,\,1}(c))\,\int_{0}^{1}K(u)\,\begin{bmatrix}1\\
u\end{bmatrix}\,f_{t}(c+hu\mid S_{i,t})\,du\right)\right]^{\otimes 2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{2t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,1)-\mu_{G,\,1}(c))^{2}\,f_{t}(c\mid S_{i,t})\right]\begin{bmatrix}\rho_{0}&\rho_{1}\\
\rho_{1}&\rho_{2}\end{bmatrix}+O(h),

where the last equality is due to another Taylor expansion (namely, for
f_{t}(c+hu\mid s)
, as in the proof of
Lemma

C.2
) followed by an application of the monotone convergence theorem. On the other hand,

\displaystyle V_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}h\,\gamma^{2t}\,{\rm Cov}_{\pi_{c}}\bigg(w_{h}(Z_{i,t})\left(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,1)-\mu_{G,\,1}(c)\right),

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\,w_{h}(Z_{i,t})\left(\partial_{z}Q_{c,t}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,1)+R(S_{i,t},h)\right)\frac{(Z_{i,t}-c)}{h}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{2t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[(Q^{\prime}_{c}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,1)+R(S_{i,t},\,h))^{2}\,h\,\int_{c}^{c+h}K^{2}_{h}(z)\,\phi_{h}(z)^{\otimes 2}\,\frac{(z-c)}{h}\,f_{t}(z\mid S_{i,t})\,dz\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{2t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[(Q^{\prime}_{c}(S_{i,t},\,c,\,1)+R(S_{i,t},\,h))^{2}\,f_{t}(c\mid S_{i,t})\,\int_{0}^{1}K^{2}(u)\,\begin{bmatrix}u&u^{2}\\
u^{2}&u^{3}\end{bmatrix}\,du\right]

\displaystyle=O(1),

where in the second last step we used the Taylor expansion for
f_{t}(c+hu\mid s)
once again.
This completes the proof of (
C.15
).

We next prove the following.

\sum_{0\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq T-\ell}{\rm Cov}_{\pi_{c}}\left(\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t},\,\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t^{\prime}}\right)\to 0.

(C.18)

Recall the notation
\mathcal{H}_{i,t}=\sigma(\{S_{i,t},\,Z_{i,t},\,A_{i,t}\})
. The tower property for covariances gives

\begin{split}{\rm Cov}_{\pi_{c}}\left(\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t},\,\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t^{\prime}}\right)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}{\rm Cov}_{\pi_{c}}\left(\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t},\,\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t^{\prime}}\mid\mathcal{H}_{i,\,t\,\vee\,t^{\prime}}\right)\\
+\,{\rm Cov}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\,\overline{\psi}_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t},\,\,\overline{\psi}_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t^{\prime}}\right],\end{split}

where
\overline{\psi}_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t}:=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t}\mid\mathcal{H}_{i,t}]
. To address the contributions from the first term above, note that

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq T-\ell}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}{\rm Cov}_{\pi_{c}}\left(\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t},\,\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t^{\prime}}\mid\mathcal{H}_{i,\,t\,\vee\,t^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{0\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq T-\ell}h\,\gamma^{t+t^{\prime}}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}K_{h}(Z_{i,t})K_{h}(Z_{i,t^{\prime}})A_{i,t}A_{i,t^{\prime}}\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t^{\prime}})^{\top}{\sigma(S_{i,t},\,Z_{i,t},\,1)}{\sigma(S_{i,t^{\prime}},\,Z_{i,t^{\prime}},\,1)}

\displaystyle\leq h\,\sum_{t<t^{\prime}}\gamma^{t+t^{\prime}}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}{B_{m_{2}}^{1/2}(S_{i,t})}{B_{m_{2}}^{1/2}(S_{i,t^{\prime}})}B_{f}(S_{i,t})B_{f}(S_{i,t^{\prime}})\int_{[0,1]^{2}}K(u)K(u^{\prime})\begin{bmatrix}1\\
u\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&u^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}\,du\,du^{\prime}

\displaystyle=O(h).

(C.19)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq T-\ell}{\rm Cov}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\,\overline{\psi}_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t}\,\,\overline{\psi}_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq T-\ell}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t}\mid\mathcal{H}_{i,t}]\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t^{\prime}}\mid\mathcal{H}_{i,t^{\prime}}]\right)

\displaystyle\leq h\,\sum_{0\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq T-\ell}\gamma^{t+t^{\prime}}\,{\rm Var}^{1/2}_{\pi_{c}}(\zeta_{h}(S_{i,t},\,Z_{i,t}))\,{\rm Var}^{1/2}_{\pi_{c}}(\zeta_{h}(S_{i,t^{\prime}},\,Z_{i,t^{\prime}})),

(Cauchy-Schwarz)

where

\zeta_{h}(S_{i,t},\,Z_{i,t}):=K_{h}(Z_{i,t})\,A_{i,t}\,\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})(\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[G_{i,t}(\ell)\mid S_{i,t},\,Z_{i,t},\,A_{i,t}]-\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})^{\top}p_{+}(\theta_{n}^{*})).

Following the proof of (
C.15
) above, we can replace
G_{i,t}(\ell)
with
G_{i,t}
and
p_{+}(\theta_{n}^{*})
with its limit
(\mu_{G,\,1}(c),0)
at
o(1)
cost. We thus conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq T-\ell}{\rm Cov}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\,\overline{\psi}_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t}\,\,\overline{\psi}_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq h\,\sum_{0\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq T-\ell}\gamma^{t+t^{\prime}}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}w_{h}(Z_{i,t})\,w_{h}(Z_{i,t^{\prime}})^{\top}(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{i,t},\,Z_{i,t},\,1)-\mu_{G,\,1}(c))(Q_{c,\,t}(S_{i,t^{\prime}},\,Z_{i,t^{\prime}},\,1)-\mu_{G,\,1}(c))\right]^{1/2}

(where
w_{h}(z)=K_{h}(z)\mathbf{1}\left\{z\geq c\right\}\phi_{h}(z)
)

\displaystyle\leq h\,\sum_{0\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq T-\ell}\gamma^{t+t^{\prime}}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\int K(u)K(u^{\prime})\begin{bmatrix}1\\
u\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&u^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}\Xi(u,\,u^{\prime}\mid S_{i,t},\,S_{i,t^{\prime}})\,du\,du^{\prime}\right]^{1/2}

(C.20)

where

\Xi(u,u^{\prime}\mid s,s^{\prime}):=(Q_{c,\,t}(s,\,c+hu,\,1)-\mu_{G,\,1}(c))(Q_{c,\,t}(s^{\prime},\,c+hu^{\prime},\,1)-\mu_{G,\,1}(c))f_{t}(c+hu\mid s)\,f_{t}(c+hu^{\prime}\mid s^{\prime}).

It follows from the locally bounded assumptions via Cauchy-Schwarz that

\sup_{u,u^{\prime}\in[0,1]}\left|\Xi(u,u^{\prime}\mid s,s^{\prime})\right|\leq B_{\Xi}(s,s^{\prime}):=4\,B_{m_{2}}^{1/2}(s)\,B_{m_{2}}^{1/2}(s^{\prime})\,B_{f,\,t}(s)\,B_{f,\,t}(s^{\prime}).

We can therefore continue to write from (
C.20
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq T-\ell}{\rm Cov}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\,\overline{\psi}_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t}\,\,\overline{\psi}_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+,\,t^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq h\,\sum_{0\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq T-\ell}\gamma^{t+t^{\prime}}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[B_{\Xi}(S_{i,t},\,S_{i,t^{\prime}})\,\int K(u)K(u^{\prime})\begin{bmatrix}1\\
u\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&u^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}\,du\,du^{\prime}\right]+o(1)\right]^{1/2}=O(h).

The above combined with (
C.19
) gives us (
C.18
).

Finally, we turn to showing the following condition for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
.

\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\left\|\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+}\right\|^{2}\mathbf{1}\left\{\left\|\psi_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+}\right\|>\varepsilon\sqrt{n}\right\}\right]\to 0.

Notice first that using the arguments used above to prove (
C.11
) and (
C.15
), we can replace
G_{i,t}(\ell)
with
G_{i,t}
and
p_{+}(\theta_{n}^{*})
with its limit
(\mu_{G,\,1}(c),0)
. In other words, it suffices to prove that

\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\|\widetilde{\psi}_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}\left\{\|\widetilde{\psi}_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+}\|>\varepsilon\sqrt{n}\right\}\right]\to 0,

(C.21)

where

\widetilde{\psi}_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+}:=\sqrt{h}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}\,K_{h}(Z_{i,t})\,A_{i,t}\,\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})(G_{i,t}-\mu_{G,\,1}(c)).

Fix a truncation level
b_{n}\to\infty
and
b_{n}=o(\sqrt{nh})
(e.g.,
b_{n}=n^{1/5}
works under
h=O(n^{-1/5})
). Denote
U_{i,t}:=G_{i,t}-\mu_{G,\,1}(c)
,
U_{i,t}^{\,\leq b_{n}}:=U_{i,t}\mathbf{1}\left\{|U_{i,t}|\leq b_{n}\right\}
, and
U_{i,t}^{\,>b_{n}}:=U_{i,t}-U_{i,t}^{\,\leq b_{n}}
. Define the corresponding split for
\widetilde{\psi}
as follows.

\widetilde{\psi}_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+}=\widetilde{\psi}_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+}^{\,\leq b_{n}}+\widetilde{\psi}_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+}^{\,>b_{n}},\quad\text{where}\quad\widetilde{\psi}_{G,\,n,\,i,\,+}^{\,\leq b_{n}}:=\sqrt{h}\sum_{t=0}^{T-\ell}\gamma^{t}\,K_{h}(Z_{i,t})\,A_{i,t}\,\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})\,U_{i,t}^{\,\leq b_{n}}.

Using
\sum_{t\geq 0}\gamma^{t}\leq(1-\gamma)^{-1},\left\|\phi_{h}(z)\right\|\leq 2
, and
\left\|K_{h}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\|K\|_{\infty}/h
, we obtain that

\left\|\widetilde{\psi}_{n,\,i,\,+}^{\,\leq b_{n}}\right\|\leq\sqrt{h}\sum_{t\geq 0}\gamma^{t}\frac{\|K\|_{\infty}}{h}\cdot 2\cdot b_{n}\leq C\frac{b_{n}}{\sqrt{h}}.

By choice of
b_{n}=o(\sqrt{nh})
, the right-hand side above is
o(\sqrt{n})
. Therefore, for all large
n
,

\mathbf{1}\left\{\left\|\widetilde{\psi}_{n,\,i,\,+}^{\,\leq b_{n}}\right\|>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sqrt{n}\right\}=0\text{ a.s.}\implies\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\left\|\widetilde{\psi}_{n,\,i,\,+}^{\,\leq b_{n}}\right\|^{2}\mathbf{1}\left\{\left\|\widetilde{\psi}_{n,\,i,\,+}^{\,\leq b_{n}}\right\|>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sqrt{n}\right\}\right]=0.

We therefore conclude that, for all large
n
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\left|\widetilde{\psi}_{n,\,i,\,+}\right\|^{2}\mathbf{1}\left\{\left\|\widetilde{\psi}_{n,\,i,\,+}\right\|>\varepsilon\sqrt{n}\right\}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\left\|\widetilde{\psi}_{n,\,i,\,+}^{\,>b_{n}}\right\|^{2}\mathbf{1}\left\{\left\|\widetilde{\psi}_{n,\,i,\,+}^{\,>b_{n}}\right\|>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sqrt{n}\right\}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left\|\widetilde{\psi}_{n,\,i,\,+}^{\,>b_{n}}\right\|^{2}.

On the other hand,

\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left\|\widetilde{\psi}_{n,\,i,\,+}^{\,>b_{n}}\right\|^{2}\leq\sum_{t\geq 0}\gamma^{2t}\,a_{n,\,i,\,t},\quad a_{n,\,i,\,t}:=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[h\,K^{2}_{h}(Z_{i,t})\,A_{i,t}\|\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})\|^{2}\,(U_{i,t}^{\,>b_{n}})^{2}\right].

Observe that since
b_{n}\to\infty
,
h\,K_{h}^{2}(Z_{i,t})\,A_{i,t}\,\|\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})\|^{2}\,(U_{i,t}^{\,>b_{n}})^{2}\to 0
a.s., and that

h\,K_{h}^{2}(Z_{i,t})\,A_{i,t}\,\|\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})\|^{2}\,(U_{i,t}^{\,>b_{n}})^{2}\leq h\,K_{h}^{2}(Z_{i,t})\,A_{i,t}\,\|\phi_{h}(Z_{i,t})\|^{2}\,U_{i,t}^{2}\in L^{1},

since
\sum_{t\geq 0}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[B_{m_{2}}(S_{t})\,B_{f,\,t}(S_{t})]<\infty
.
Therefore, it follows from the dominated convergence theorem that
a_{n,\,i,\,t}\to 0
, and thus
\sum_{t\geq 0}\gamma^{2t}a_{n,\,i,\,t}=o(1)
. This completes the proof.

\square

Lemma C.9
.

In addition to the conditions of
Theorem

9
, assume that:

(i)

The conditional second moments of future outcomes are uniformly bounded at the threshold, i.e., there exists an
M\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
t\geq 0
and
j\geq 0
,
\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[Y_{t+j}^{2}\mid S_{t},\,Z_{t}=c,\,A_{t}=a]\leq M
almost surely, for both
a=0,1
.

(ii)

The conditional densities
f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})
are uniformly bounded and bounded away from zero, i.e.,
0<\underline{f}\leq f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\leq\overline{f}<\infty
for some constants
\underline{f}
and
\overline{f}
.

Then, the quantities
V_{G}
,
V_{H}
and
V_{GH}
defined in (
13
) are all bounded by
C_{0}(1-\gamma)^{-1}
, where the constant
C_{0}
does not depend on
\gamma
. Consequently,
the asymptotic
variance
V_{\mathrm{RD}}
in
Theorem

9
satisfies

V_{\mathrm{RD}}=O\left((1-\gamma)^{-1}\frac{(1+\tau_{\,\mathrm{RD}})^{2}}{(\Delta\mu_{H}(c))^{2}}\right),

(C.22)

where

\Delta\mu_{H}(c)=\frac{\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[\left(Q_{c,\,t}^{A}(S_{t},\,c,\,1)-Q_{c,\,t}^{A}(S_{t},\,c,\,0)\right)f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})]}{\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})]}.

If, in addition to above,
Q_{c,\,t}^{A}(s_{t},\,c,\,1)-Q_{c,\,t}^{A}(s_{t},\,c,\,0)\geq\underline{q}>0
for a.e.
s_{t}
and for all
t\geq 0
and

|\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[Y_{t+j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t,\,1}]-\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[Y_{t+j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t,\,0}]|\leq L\cdot(\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[A_{t+j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t,\,1}]-\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[A_{t+j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t,\,0}])
almost surely
for all
t\geq 0
and
j\geq 0
, where
\mathcal{F}_{t,\,a}:=\sigma(S_{t},\,Z_{t}=c,\,A_{t}=a)
and
L\in(0,\infty)
is a constant, then we have

V_{\mathrm{RD}}=O((1-\gamma)^{-1}).

Proof.

Fix
a\in\{0,1\}
and define
\mathcal{F}_{t,\,a}:=\sigma(S_{t},\,Z_{t}=c,\,A_{t}=a)
. It follows from the conditional Minkowski’s inequality (applied to the partial sums, then taking limit) that

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[G_{t}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t,\,a}\right]\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{j}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[Y_{t+j}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t,\,a}\right]\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{j}\cdot M^{1/2}

(using the bounded conditional moments assumption)

\displaystyle=(1-\gamma)^{-1}M^{1/2}.

Squaring both sides, we arrive at the conclusion that

\sup_{t\geq 0}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[G_{t}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t,\,a}\right]\leq(1-\gamma)^{-2}M.

(C.23)

On the other hand, note that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\left|Q_{c,t}(S_{t},\,c,\,a)\right]f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\left(\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[G_{t}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t,\,a}\right]\right)^{1/2}f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]

(conditional Cauchy-Schwarz)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,(1-\gamma)^{-1}M^{1/2}\overline{f}=(1-\gamma)^{-2}{M}^{1/2}\overline{f}.

This, combined with the lower bound
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]\geq(1-\gamma)^{-1}\underline{f}
, yields the following.

|\mu_{G,\,a}(c)|\leq(1-\gamma)^{-1}\frac{M^{1/2}\overline{f}}{\underline{f}}.

(C.24)

In view of (
C.23
) we conclude that

\displaystyle V_{G}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=0,1}\,\xi_{2}\,\frac{\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{2t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[\left(G_{t}-\mu_{G,\,a}(c)\right)^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t,\,a}\right]f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]}{\left(\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]\right)^{2}}

(by definition)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{a=0,1}\,\xi_{2}\,\frac{\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{2t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[(1-\gamma)^{-2}f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]}{\left(\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[f_{t}(c\mid S_{t})\right]\right)^{2}}

(using (
C.23
) and (
C.24
))

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{(1-\gamma)^{-2}(1-\gamma^{2})^{-1}\overline{f}}{(1-\gamma)^{-2}\underline{f}^{2}}=O((1-\gamma)^{-1}).

Similarly for
H_{t}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\gamma^{j}A_{t+j}
, we can use the natural upper bound
\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[A^{2}_{t+j}\mid S_{t},\,Z_{t}=c,\,A_{t}=a]\leq 1
and apply the same argument as above to conclude that

\sup_{t\geq 0}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}\left[H_{t}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t,\,a}\right]\leq(1-\gamma)^{-2},\quad\text{and}\quad|\mu_{H,\,a}(c)|\leq(1-\gamma)^{-1}{\overline{f}}/{\underline{f}}.

(C.25)

Using these we can show that
V_{H}=O((1-\gamma)^{-1})
, using the same arguments as above. Moreover, (
C.25
) in conjunction with (
C.23
) and (
C.24
) imply that
V_{GH}=O((1-\gamma)^{-1})
, again by the same argument as above. This completes the proof of (
C.22
).

The second claim follows from (
C.22
) since the condition
Q_{c,\,t}^{A}(s_{t},\,c,\,1)-Q_{c,\,t}^{A}(s_{t},\,c,\,0)\geq\underline{q}>0
implies that
\Delta\mu_{H}(c)
is bounded away from zero, and the condition
|\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[Y_{t+j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t,\,1}]-\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[Y_{t+j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t,\,0}]|\leq L\cdot(\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[A_{t+j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t,\,1}]-\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{c}}[A_{t+j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t,\,0}])
implies that
|Q_{c,\,t}(s_{t},\,c,\,1)-Q_{c,\,t}(s_{t},\,c,\,0)|\leq L\cdot(Q_{c,\,t}^{A}(s_{t},\,c,\,1)-Q_{c,\,t}^{A}(s_{t},\,c,\,0))
, which in turn implies that
\tau_{\,\mathrm{RD}}
is bounded.

\square

Appendix Appendix D
Auxiliary Results

Lemma D.1
.

Suppose that
h=h(n)\to 0
such that
h=O(n^{-1/5})
. Suppose that
X_{n}
and
Y_{n}
are real-valued random variables such that

X_{n}=x+b_{x}h^{2}+\frac{U_{n}}{\sqrt{nh}}+r_{x,n},\quad Y_{n}=y+b_{y}h^{2}+\frac{V_{n}}{\sqrt{nh}}+r_{y,n},

where
(U_{n},V_{n})
converges weakly to a zero-mean bivariate Gaussian
(U,V)
,
r_{x,n}=o_{p}(\sqrt{nh})
and
r_{y,n}=o_{p}(\sqrt{nh})
, and
y\neq 0
. Then,

\sqrt{nh}\left(\frac{X_{n}}{Y_{n}}-\frac{x}{y}-{h^{2}}\left(\frac{b_{x}}{y}-\frac{xb_{y}}{y^{2}}\right)\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}\left(0,\,\frac{1}{y^{2}}{\rm Var}(U)+\frac{x^{2}}{y^{4}}{\rm Var}(V)-\frac{2x}{y^{3}}{\rm Cov}(U,V)\right).

Proof.

The proof is a straightforward application of the identity
\hat{x}\hat{y}-xy=y(\hat{x}-x)+x(\hat{y}-y)-(\hat{x}-x)(\hat{y}-y)
. Using this identity we can write

\displaystyle\frac{X_{n}}{Y_{n}}-\frac{x}{y}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{y}(X_{n}-x)+x\left(\frac{1}{Y_{n}}-\frac{1}{y}\right)+\underbrace{(X_{n}-x)\left(\frac{1}{Y_{n}}-\frac{1}{y}\right)}_{=\,o_{p}(\sqrt{nh})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{y}\left(b_{x}h^{2}+\frac{U_{n}}{\sqrt{nh}}\right)-\frac{x}{y^{2}}\left(b_{y}h^{2}+\frac{V_{n}}{\sqrt{nh}}\right)+o_{p}(\sqrt{nh})

\displaystyle=h^{2}\left(\frac{b_{x}}{y}-\frac{xb_{y}}{y^{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{nh}}\left(\frac{1}{y}U_{n}-\frac{x}{y^{2}}V_{n}\right)+o_{p}(\sqrt{nh}).

The above display, combined with Slutsky’s lemma and multivariate delta method completes the proof.

\square

Lemma D.2

(Law of large numbers for a triangular array of matrices)
.

Suppose that for each
n\geq 1
,
X_{n,\,1},\dots,X_{n,\,n}
are i.i.d.
\mathbb{R}^{p\times q}
-valued random matrices, where
p,q\geq 1
are fixed. Assume that
\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\left\|X_{n,\,1}\right\|<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}\left[\|X_{n,\,1}\|\mathbf{1}\left\{\|X_{n,\,1}\|>\varepsilon n\right\}\right]\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, for every
\varepsilon>0
, where
\|\cdot\|
is any norm that satisfies
|A_{i,\,j}|\leq\|A\|
for all
i\leq p
and
j\leq q
. Then,

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{n,i}-\mathbb{E}\left[X_{n,\,1}\right]\ {\rightarrow}_{p}\ 0.

Proof.

This result essentially follows from
Durrett 2019
and the accompanying discussion. We present here the proof for
p=q=1
; the extension to arbitrary
p,q\geq 1
follows by applying the same reasoning componentwise, noting that the required assumptions are satisfied for each entry
(i,j)
with
i\leq p
and
j\leq q
, because
\left|X_{n,\,1,\,i,\,j}\right|\leq\|X_{n,\,1}\|
and
\left|X_{n,\,1,\,i,\,j}\right|\mathbf{1}\left\{\left|X_{n,\,1,\,i,\,j}\right|>\varepsilon n\right\}\leq\left\|X_{n,\,1}\right\|\mathbf{1}\left\{\|X_{n,\,1}\|>\varepsilon n\right\}
.

In view of
Durrett 2019
, it suffices to show that (a)
n\,\mathbb{P}(\left|X_{n,\,1}\right|>n)\to 0
, and (b)
n^{-1}\,\mathbb{E}[X_{n,\,1}^{2}\mathbf{1}\left\{\left|X_{n,\,1}\right|\leq n\right\}]\to 0
. The first condition follows from the following argument:

n\,\mathbb{P}(\left|X_{n,\,1}\right|>n)=n\,\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{1}\left\{\left|X_{n,\,1}\right|>n\right\}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left|X_{n,\,1}\right|\mathbf{1}\left\{\left|X_{n,\,1}\right|>n\right\}\right]\to 0.

To prove (b), note that

\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[X_{n,\,1}^{2}\mathbf{1}\left\{\left|X_{n,\,1}\right|\leq n\right\}\right]=\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}2y\,\mathbb{P}(\left|X_{n,\,1}\right|>y)\,dy=\int_{0}^{1}2\varepsilon n\,\mathbb{P}(\left|X_{n,\,1}\right|>\varepsilon n)\,d\varepsilon.

Define
g_{n}(\varepsilon):=2\varepsilon n\,\mathbb{P}(\left|X_{n,\,1}\right|>\varepsilon n)
. Note that
g_{n}(\varepsilon)\to 0
pointwise as
n\to\infty
, because

g_{n}(\varepsilon)=2\varepsilon n\,\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{1}\left\{\left|X_{n,\,1}\right|>\varepsilon n\right\}\right]\leq 2\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|X_{n,\,1}\right|\mathbf{1}\left\{\left|X_{n,\,1}\right|>\varepsilon n\right\}\right]\to 0.

Furthermore,
g_{n}(\varepsilon)\leq 2\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\left|X_{n,\,1}\right|<\infty
for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Therefore, we can apply the dominated convergence theorem to conclude that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}g_{n}(\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon=0
. This completes the proof, thanks to
Durrett 2019
.

\square

Macroscopic assembly of 2D materials for energy storage and seawater desalination
Authors: Chenpeng Zhao, Rui Wang, Biao Fang, Han Liang, Ruqing Li, Shuaifei Li, Yuhui Xiong, Yuye Shao, Biyuan Ni, Ruyi Wang, Biao Xu, Songyang Feng, Runwei Mo
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
SummarySince the discovery of graphene in 2004, two-dimensional (2D) materials have attracted widespread attention due to their excellent physical and chemical properties in the fields of energy, environment, catalysis, and optoelectronics. However, there are still many key problems in the process of practical application. To further promote the potential of 2D materials for practical applications, macroscopic assembly of 2D materials is crucial for the continued development of 2D materials, especially in the fields of energy storage and seawater desalination. Therefore, this review focuses on the latest progress and current status related to the macroscopic assembly of 2D materials, including 1D fibers, 2D films, and 3D architectures. In addition, the application of macroscopic bodies assembled based on 2D materials in the fields of energy storage and seawater desalination is also introduced. Finally, future directions for the macroscopic assembly of 2D materials and their applications are prospected.Subject areas: Materials chemistry, Energy materials

Introduction

In recent years, the rapid development of nanotechnology and micro-nano manufacturing has promoted the innovation of material development, device manufacturing, and system integration methods, which have greatly improved the performance of devices including energy, environmental protection, and catalysis.1,2,3,4 In 2004, Prof. Geim and Novoselov from the University of Manchester prepared single-layer graphene for the first time by using adhesive tape to peel off highly oriented pyrolytic graphite.5 Due to its unique two-dimensional (2D) structure of single atomic layer, graphene exhibits excellent mechanical, electrical, thermal, and optical properties, which has aroused extensive interest of researchers. Since then, 2D materials have been rapidly developed, such as graphene, germanene, silicene, graphene oxide, black phosphor, MXenes, and hexagonal boron nitride (h-BN).6,7,8,9,10,11 It has been found that these 2D materials have good transparency, high surface area, and excellent mechanical and electronic properties, which make them exhibit broad application prospects in the fields of energy storage, seawater desalination, electromagnetic shielding, nanogenerators, nanoelectromechanical systems, flexible electronics, and sensors.12,13,14,15,16 More importantly, it is discovered that 2D materials have a unique phenomenon of quantum confinement. This phenomenon shows a surprising band structure and charge transport, which enables 2D materials to exhibit excellent optoelectronic and electrical properties.17,18 In addition, the properties of 2D materials can be optimized through structural design and interface regulation, which can improve the comprehensive performance of the device. Therefore, the rational design of 2D materials is crucial to realize their applications in the fields of energy storage and seawater desalination.

So far, 2D materials have accumulated a lot of research work in the fields of energy storage and seawater desalination. However, 2D materials are usually unsatisfactory for practical applications due to the problems of restacking and irreversible aggregation. At the same time, the single atomic layer thickness and the lateral size structure of tens of microns make the electrical and thermal properties of 2D materials anisotropic in the plane and vertical directions. The existence of the factors mentioned earlier has greatly affected the excellent performance of 2D materials, which also hindered their practical applications in the fields of energy storage and seawater desalination. Therefore, the challenge in the practical application of 2D materials is to design functionally rich micro-nanostructures while maintaining the advantages of 2D materials.19 To solve the aforementioned problems, it is of great significance to use the elementary structural properties of 2D materials to assemble and construct macroscopic structures with controllable structures and excellent properties for their future practical applications in the fields of energy storage and seawater desalination.

In order to promote the practical application of 2D materials, the vast majority of 2D materials appear in the form of macroscopic volumes. Its properties not only depend on the structural properties of 2D material but also are affected by the interaction between 2D material sheets.20 In particular, the macroscopic bulk structure of 2D materials, including 1D fibers, 2D films, and 3D architectures, has developed rapidly. As shown in Figure 1, the macroscopic bodies of these 2D materials show excellent mechanical and electrical properties, exhibiting great application potential in the fields of energy storage and seawater desalination. This review introduces the macroscopic assembly methods of 2D materials and their recent progress and status in the fields of energy storage and seawater desalination. We also further analyzed the influence of structural design and interface properties on the performance of energy storage and seawater desalination. Finally, prospects and discussions are made on the challenges of future development.

Macroscopic assembly of 2D materials

1D fibers

1D fiber material is a continuous or discontinuous multifunctional material made by spinning 2D materials. The application performance of single-layer or few-layer 2D materials is limited. Therefore, it is of great significance to promote the practical application of 2D materials by constructing macroscopic assemblies with 1D fiber structures to realize the excellent properties of 2D material nanosheets. Wet spinning is one of the main methods to prepare 1D fibers. The method firstly disperses the 2D material in water or organic solvent to form liquid crystal or non-liquid crystal structure spinning solution. Then, a series of post-treatments and reductions (chemical reduction, high-temperature reduction, etc.) are used to prepare 1D fibers using a suitable coagulation bath. Graphene is a class of single-layer two-dimensional materials with sp2-hybridized carbon atoms, which have excellent mechanical, electrical, and thermal properties.21 Assembling individual graphene sheets into fibers is expected to extend its excellent properties from the microscopic to the macroscopic level. In 2011, Xu et al. used sodium hydroxide/methanol solution as a coagulation bath to prepare graphene fibers through wet spinning technology and chemical reduction.22 The fiber has good tensile strength (140 MPa) and electrical conductivity (2. 5 × 104 S m−1). Chen et al. used a high-concentration non-liquid crystal graphene oxide aqueous solution as the spinning solution to prepare graphene fibers through wet spinning technology and chemical reduction.23 The tensile strength and electrical conductivity of the fiber are 208 MPa and 1.53 S m−1, respectively. To further refine the size of 1D fibers to improve graphene alignment and fiber compactness, Xu et al. developed a strategy to introduce stretching throughout wet spinning process (Figure 2).24 Equally important, in order to minimize possible defects in graphene fibers at all levels from the atomic scale to the macroscopic scale, a comprehensive collaborative defect engineering approach is also proposed during subsequent processing. The as-prepared graphene fibers exhibited high mechanical tensile strength of 1.45 GPa and high stiffness of 282 GPa. The electrical conductivity and mechanical properties of graphene fibers can be greatly improved by selecting different coagulation baths and increasing the sheet size of graphene oxide.25

In 2020, Zhang et al. innovatively proposed a method to prepare a multifunctional Ti3C2Tx fiber without adding any additives, as shown in Figure 3.26 The main principle is to induce Ti3C2Tx nanosheets to completely transform into symmetrical fibers in the coagulation bath with mechanical stretching force under different coagulation bath conditions. The properties of Ti3C2Tx fibers assembled under different spinning conditions are different. Both acetic acid and chitosan coagulation baths can produce fibers with strong toughness. However, there was a large difference in the density and diameter of porous fibers in the two coagulation baths. Analyzing the reason, it is found that acetic acid is more likely to replace water in the spinning solution, and this fast solvent exchange with acetic acid "freezes" the Ti3C2Tx flakes, resulting in the formation of a loose Ti3C2Tx flake network in the fiber. In contrast, the dense packing of Ti3C2Tx sheets in the fibers was caused by larger chitosan polymer chains. Finally, Ti3C2Tx fibers with high density and low cross-sectional area are formed. The results showed that the denser Ti3C2Tx fibers exhibited higher tensile strength. Furthermore, the diversity of surface groups of Ti3C2Tx provides a high degree of tunability of its properties.27,28

In addition to wet spinning, researchers have developed a confined hydrothermal method to prepare 1D fiber materials in recent years.29,30,31 In this method, a certain concentration of 2D material solution is injected into a specific tubular reaction vessel for hydrothermal treatment to prepare 1D fibers. Sheng et al. successfully prepared graphene fibers by confined hydrothermal method.29 First, the dispersion of graphene oxide (GO) was sprayed into liquid nitrogen to obtain a graphene oxide dispersion (IGOR) with interconnected sheets. Then, the homogeneous mixture of GO and IGOR was injected into the quartz capillary and sealed and treated at 230°C for 2 h. Finally, it was dried in air for 12 h to obtain graphene fibers. Dong et al. successfully prepared graphene fibers by injecting graphene oxide dispersion into glass tubes and hydrothermally treating them at 230°C for 2 h.30 The tensile strength and electrical conductivity of the fiber are 180 MPa and 10 S cm−1, respectively. Jia et al. synthesized graphene fibers using a simple low-temperature-induced self-assembly method, as shown in Figure 4.31 First, graphene oxide and ascorbic acid were uniformly mixed and packaged in glass tubes of specific diameters. Then, graphene fibers with hierarchical porous structure were obtained by treating at 90°C and 120°C for 2 h, respectively. The graphene fiber has a tensile strength of 236 MPa and an electrical conductivity of 1.3 × 104 S m−1 after heat treatment. In addition, the graphene fibers also exhibit excellent flexibility, which can be conveniently woven into textiles. In recent years, the preparation of 1D fibers by adopting confined hydrothermal method has been rapidly developed. However, the size and length of 1D fibers prepared by this method depend on the tubular container, which still limits its practical application to some extent.

2D films

2D materials have the characteristics of easy film formation, which makes the 2D films obtained by macroscopic assembly have great application potential in many fields.

Vacuum filtration is a low-cost and high-efficiency method commonly used in laboratories to prepare 2D films. Due to the continuous pressure difference during the preparation process of 2D films, the sheets will assemble layer by layer on the filter membrane under the appropriate concentration. The precise control of the thickness of 2D film can be achieved by adjusting the concentration and volume of 2D material solution. Dikin et al. successfully prepared a graphene oxide film with a thickness of 10 μm by vacuum filtration method (Figure 5).32 The film has a dense interlayer structure, where single regular graphene oxide flakes are locked together in a near-parallel fashion. It is noteworthy that there is a large interaction force between these graphene oxide nanosheets, which leads to excellent stretchability. Inspired by the “mother of pearl” structure, Liu et al. prepared a cellulose nanocrystal (CNC)/Ti3C2Tx composite film by vacuum filtration.33 There is a strong hydrogen bond between the CNC and Ti3C2Tx nanosheets. When subjected to external force, CNC acts as an adhesive to form a physical barrier, which improves the mechanical properties. The 2D film prepared by vacuum filtration method has high film quality, uniform thickness, and smooth surface. It is a commonly used method for producing high-performance 2D films. However, this method also has some shortcomings, such as the small size and high cost, which largely limit its industrial application.

In order to solve the shortcomings of the vacuum filtration method, the researchers developed a new strategy for preparing 2D films by casting.34,35 Specifically, the 2D material is uniformly dispersed in the solvent by vigorous stirring. The solution is then cast onto a clean substrate, and the solvent is evaporated by heat or vacuum. After drying, self-supporting 2D films can be prepared by peeling off the substrate. To prepare high-quality 2D films, Ahn et al. pretreated the glass substrate before casting MXene films (Figure 6).34 Specifically, the glass substrate was sequentially immersed in acetone and isopropanol and sonicated for 10 min, respectively. The substrate was then surface treated with UV-ozone for 10 min to protect the material from interfacial oxidation. The MXene solution was subsequently cast on a glass substrate. Finally, the films were prepared by drying in a N2 glove box under room temperature. This treatment not only protects the surface of MXene film but also improves the performance of MXene film. Recently, it has been reported that the casting method has been extended to the preparation of the ternary film system.35 Wang et al. successfully prepared a composite film of MXene/Ni/polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) by casting method.35 Firstly, MXene, Ni, and PVDF were mixed into a homogeneous solution. Then, this solution was cast in a clean glass dish and evacuated in a vacuum oven under room temperature for 30 min to remove air bubbles. Subsequently, the solution was placed in a dry box and heated at 90°C for 12 h to evaporate the solvent. Finally, the composite film was prepared by soaking in deionized water for 24 h. It is found that the three-dimensional crosslinked network composed of Ni and MXene plays a key role in enhancing the mechanical properties of the composite film. During stretching, stress can be transferred between the substrate and the 3D network, which can provide frictional energy dissipation to resist the sliding effect. The interwoven structure of MXene and Ni can further resist sliding against each other in the PVDF matrix, which accelerates the energy dissipation under tension.

In addition, researchers have also developed a layer-by-layer self-assembly (LBL) to prepare 2D films, which utilizes electrostatic or hydrogen-bonding interactions to create functionalized films with controllable thickness and structure.36,37 Yun et al. used the LBL method to successfully prepare composite films with MXene flakes and polyaniline nanofibers (PNF) based on the principle of electrostatic interaction (Figure 7).37 First, the cleaned substrate was immersed in the PNF dispersion for 15 min. Subsequently, the glass substrates were rinsed in three separate deionized water baths for 2 min, 1 min, and 1 min to remove loosely adhered PNF. Then, the basement film was immersed in the MXene dispersion for 15 min. Finally, a single-layer composite film was prepared by performing washing and drying steps. A composite film with a multilayer structure can be prepared by repeating the above steps. The vacuum filtration is widely used due to its simple preparation process and low experimental requirements. However, it is difficult to prepare thin films in a large area, which limits its industrialization process. As a large-scale method for preparing thin films, the casting method has good industrialization prospects. However, the equipment requirements are high, and the properties of as-prepared films are not uniform. The film prepared by LBL method has the characteristics of good uniformity. However, the preparation steps are relatively complicated, and the requirements for materials are relatively high. Therefore, how to prepare 2D films with uniform properties on a large scale is an urgent problem to be solved in the future.

3D architectures

The 3D architectures constructed from 2D materials possess the advantages of superelasticity, ultralow density, and high specific surface area, which are the intrinsic reasons for their diverse potential applications. In recent years, techniques for the synthesis of 3D architectures based on 2D materials have developed rapidly. Self-assembly method is an effective method to prepare 3D architectures from 2D materials as units. The self-assembly method mainly uses 2D materials as the main raw material to realize the construction of multiple ordered structures by using the non-covalent bonds between the assembled monomers and the metal-ligand covalent bonds. For example, GO can be used as raw material to prepare 3D graphene architectures through self-assembly method.38 Specifically, GO dispersions were prepared based on the amphiphilic properties of GO. Then, a suitable reducing agent was added to reduce the oxygen-containing functional groups in GO to enhance the hydrophobic properties, which enabled the assembly of graphene sheets to obtain 3D graphene hydrogels. It is worth noting that the reducing agent can not only determine the reduction degree and reduction time of the reaction but also affect the network structure of 3D graphene architectures. Hu et al. used GO as the precursor and ascorbic acid as the reducing agent to successfully prepare graphene/graphene nanoribbon aerogels with 3D architecture by self-assembly method.38 It was found that as-prepared aerogel exhibited high specific surface area and large porosity. In addition, Li et al. used Ti3C2Tx nanosheets as raw materials and ethylenediamine as a weak reducing agent to synthesize 3D ultralight Ti3C2Tx aerogels by self-assembly under hydrothermal conditions.39 In order to speed up the reaction rate of self-assembly, the researchers developed an electrostatic-force-induced self-assembly method.40 In this preparation process, the gelation of the 2D material aqueous solution is mainly promoted by the electrostatic interaction force, inducing the self-assembly of 2D material to form a 3D architecture. Kim et al. successfully prepared 3D graphene aerogels by electrostatic force-induced self-assembly method (Figure 8).40 First, graphene suspensions were prepared by using graphene flakes with large surface areas. Subsequently, the concentration was increased by evaporating the solvent. The van der Waals force between graphene flakes acts as the force to interconnect and self-assemble into 3D graphene gels. Finally, the moisture of the gel was removed by freeze-drying to form 3D graphene aerogel.

Template method is also a common method to prepare 3D architectures. This method can adjust the pore structure of as-prepared material according to the characteristics of the template, which is conducive to the preparation of 3D architectures with controllable morphology and size. Template methods can be divided into hard template methods and soft template methods. Commonly used hard templating agents are mainly polyethylene microspheres or polyurethane foam. For example, Mo et al. successfully synthesized reduced graphene oxide foam using Ni foam as a hard template.41 First, the graphene nanosheets were assembled into a 3D network structure by dipping the nickel foam into the graphene suspension through a layer-by-layer dip-coating process. The nickel foam is then removed by acid etching. Finally, the reduced graphene oxide foam was prepared by freeze-drying. The 3D architectures prepared by the hard template method have good mechanical properties. However, its pore structure is mainly macroporous, which reduces the specific surface area. More importantly, the post-removal method of hard template is mainly heat treatment, which causes obvious damage to 3D architecture. Compared with the hard template method, the soft template method is relatively easy to remove the template, which does not cause damage to 3D architecture.42,43 Yue et al. prepared Ti3C2Tx/reduced graphene oxide composite aerogel by ice crystal template method, as shown in Figure 9.44 In the preparation process, the microstructure of the aerogel material can be adjusted by controlling the growth of ice crystals. Finally, the ice crystals are removed by drying. The whole preparation process is fast, simple, economical, and green. However, the process of synthesizing soft templates is complex and costly, which hinders the large-scale production of soft templates.

In recent years, 3D printing technology has been rapidly developed, which can be used to efficiently prepare 3D architectures. 3D printing technology appeared in the 1990s, which is an advanced technology in the manufacturing industry. 3D printing technology actually uses technologies such as light curing and paper lamination to achieve rapid prototyping, which is basically the same as the working principle of ordinary printers. The printer contains “printing materials” in a liquid or powder state. After connecting with the computer, the “printing materials” are superimposed layer by layer through computer control. Finally, the blueprint on the computer is turned into a real object. Compared with traditional mechanical manufacturing, 3D printing technology can save raw materials to prepare the complex 3D architecture. In order to improve the physical and chemical properties of 3D architecture, the pore structure of 3D architecture can be adjusted through steps such as gelation, washing, freeze-drying, and thermal reduction. The current 3D printing technologies are mainly divided into direct-write molding technology and freeze 3D printing technology.45,46 Garcia-Tunon et al. used 3D printing technology to prepare 3D architectures, as shown in Figure 10.47 First, a branched copolymer surfactant (BCS) was added to the graphene oxide water-based ink. Then, the linkage between graphene and BCS is controlled by adjusting the pH of the solution, which can regulate the viscosity of the ink. Finally, a 3D graphene architecture can be prepared after extrusion molding according to a certain shape. In addition, Masud et al. also used 3D printing technology to prepare graphene-dopamine-bovine serum albumin (G-PDA-BSA) aerogel.48 First, the GO dispersion with a pH of 8.5 was heated at 60°C for 6 h to allow dopamine (PDA) to self-polymerize. Then, BSA was dispersed into G-PDA and thermally cured in an oven at 85°C for 2 h to form viscoelastic G-PDA-BSA ink. Subsequently, the free-standing cylindrical hydrogel was obtained by 3D printing. Finally, aerogels with hierarchical porous structures were prepared after freeze-drying. The 3D structure prepared by 3D printing has the characteristics of good uniformity, fast prototyping, and structural designability. However, the requirements for materials are also relatively high. Self-assembly methods have been widely studied due to their simple preparation process and low experimental requirements. However, it is difficult to achieve precise control of the microstructure of 2D materials, which limits its industrialization process. As a method for large-scale preparation of 3D structures, the template method has good industrial prospects. However, the preparation process is complicated, and the cost is high. Therefore, how to efficiently prepare 3D structures with uniform properties is an urgent problem to be solved in the future.

Physical and chemical characteristics of macroscopic assembly of 2D materials for energy storage and seawater desalination applications

Macroscopic bodies of 2D materials, including structures that can be classified into 1D fibers, 2D films, and 3D structures, exhibit excellent mechanical, optical, thermal, and electronic properties, which have attracted widespread interest from the scientific and industrial communities. 2D materials can achieve various forms of assembly, which is mainly attributed to the interactions between 2D materials, such as electrostatic attraction, coordination bonds, covalent bonds, hydrogen bonds, van der Waals forces, etc. For macroscopic bodies of 2D materials, their performance largely depends on the basic parameters during the assembly process of 2D materials, including crystallinity, size, arrangement, interface defects, and compactness. 2D materials can be assembled into 1D fibers, 2D films, and 3D structures from their inherent geometric shapes through a variety of assembly principles, which are accomplished through surface tension gradients, liquid crystallinity, electrochemical processes, etc. In addition, the interface properties between 2D material assembly structures have a very important impact on their physical and chemical properties. Among them, heteroatom doping and defect engineering at the 2D material interface can effectively control the surface energy of 2D materials, which greatly expands its application fields. On the one hand, the chemically inert surface of 2D materials can be improved through atomic or molecular doping, which facilitates their applications in the field of energy storage. On the other hand, the surface energy of 2D materials can be improved by building micro-defects such as edges and pores, which is beneficial for applications in the field of seawater desalination.

The macroscopic body of 2D materials has excellent physical and chemical properties, which are summarized as follows: (1) the large specific surface area can effectively load active substances and prevent the agglomeration of 2D materials, which can effectively improve the utilization rate of 2D materials; (2) for 2D materials, atomic doping and defect engineering at the material interface can significantly increase the surface active sites of 2D materials, which can promote the formation of reactive active centers; (3) this network structure provides an excellent ion and electron transport interface, which can significantly improve the kinetic properties of the reaction; (4) the excellent mechanical property facilitates the manufacturing of a variety of devices, which can also ensure the stability of long-term cycle performance. Therefore, macroscopic bodies of 2D materials have great potential in energy storage and seawater desalination applications.

Energy storage applications

The application of 2D materials has been greatly expanded by assembling single-layer or few-layer 2D materials into interconnected macroscopic bulk structures, which has taken a key step in practical applications. In recent years, with the rapid development of electronics and electric vehicles, the demand for energy storage has been increasing. In this subsection, we focus on the latest research progress in the field of energy storage with the macroscopic bulk structure of 2D materials.

Alkali metal-ion batteries

Alkali metal-ion batteries, as rechargeable batteries, such as lithium-ion batteries, sodium-ion batteries, etc., are currently one of the most important energy storage devices. It has attracted widespread attention in recent years due to its advantages such as high energy density, high working voltage, no "memory effect," small self-discharge, green, and pollution-free. The structural design of electrode materials is very important to improve the electrochemical performance of alkali metal-ion batteries. Therefore, the development of electrode materials with high specific capacity and stable structure is the key to promote the development of alkali metal-ion batteries. 2D materials have been widely used in alkali-metal-ion batteries due to favorable physicochemical properties and structural diversity. In recent years, lithium-ion batteries (LIBs) have become the main commercial power source for electronic products.49,50 To realize high-performance batteries, it is critical to design electrode materials with excellent electrochemical performance and good mechanical stability. Graphite is currently the most widely used anode materials in LIBs. However, the low theoretical capacity of graphite limits its further development.51 Compared with graphite electrodes, MXene has become the promising electrode material to replace graphite in LIBs due to its unique layered structure, larger theoretical capacity, and lower Li+ diffusion barrier.52,53 Lu et al. successfully prepared high-performance MXene thin films by using MXene to remove −F and convert −OH to −O under H2 atmosphere.54 It is noteworthy that the specific capacity of the electrode has been significantly improved. However, the aggregation and stacking phenomena of MXene film electrodes are inevitable during the charge-discharge cycle, which is mainly attributed to the existence of van der Waals forces and hydrogen bonds between MXene nanosheets. These phenomena severely hinder the access of ions into the active sites, which leads to poor structural stability during cycling.

Graphene is a two-dimensional nanomaterial composed of carbon atoms in sp2 hybrid orbitals, which has attracted extensive attention due to its unique properties such as high electrical conductivity (103–104 S m−1), large specific surface area (2630 m2 g−1), high chemical stability, and high mechanical strength.55 However, graphene materials are prone to stacking and agglomeration, which significantly reduces their electrochemical performance. To solve the problems discussed earlier, compounding 2D materials with other materials is a common method to improve the electrochemical performance.2,56 The composite of graphene and MXene can effectively prevent the self-stacking phenomenon, which accelerates the diffusion of electrolyte ions and improves the electrochemical performance.57,58 Ma et al. prepared the MXene/rGO composite film with a porous structure, as shown in Figure 11.59 The composite film can be used as a binder-free electrode. The addition of rGO transformed the dense MXene nanosheets into a porous structure, which effectively facilitated the diffusion of lithium ions in the composite electrode. The MXene/rGO electrode still exhibits high capacity and excellent long-term cycle stability under high current density conditions, which is much better than that of pure MXene electrodes. In addition, Liu et al. synthesized Ti3C2/NiCo-MOF composite films based on the interlayer hydrogen bond interaction between Ti3C2 and NiCo-MOF nanosheets by vacuum-assisted filtration technology.60 The composite film not only effectively suppresses the self-stacking of Ti3C2 and MOF sheets but also significantly accelerates the transport of ions. The electrode has a reversible capacity of 402 mA h g−1 and a capacity retention rate of 85.7% after 300 charge-discharge cycles. Equally important, it exhibits excellent rate performance with a reversible capacity of 256 mA h g−1 at a high current density of 1 A g−1. To further reduce the loss of lithium ions and electrolytes, Liu et al. designed a rolled and reduced graphene/Si bilayer nanofilm with a sandwich structure, as shown in Figure 12.61 The internal voids and excellent mechanical properties of the nanofilm significantly reduce the loss of lithium ions and electrolytes during lithiation and delithiation. The alternately stacked conductive graphene layers can avoid the formation of excessive SEI films on Si layers. When the rGO/Si double-layer nanofilm is used as the anode material of LIBs, the decay rate of its capacity per 100 cycles is only 3.3% at a current density of 3 A g−1. Transition metal sulfides (such as SnS2, MoS2, Bi2S3, WS2, etc.) have become very promising embedded anode materials due to the graphene-like sheet structure.62 In addition, the composite of cathode material and graphene can also greatly improve its electrochemical performance. Mo et al. designed the graphene self-assembled mesoporous amorphous FePO4 composite by using the improved hydrothermal method.63 In this hybrid structure, the mesoporous amorphous FePO4 nanoparticles supported by 3D porous graphene framework has a large specific surface area and high electrical conductivity. When this material is used as the cathode of LIBs, the specific capacity can reach 156 mA h g−1 at 0.5 C.

3D printing technology is one of the current advanced manufacturing technologies. As an advanced and forward-looking emerging technology, it is promoting a huge change in traditional industrial manufacturing and traditional production processes.64 It is worth noting that this technology is suitable for rapid manufacturing and personalized customization of complex structures. Compared with the disadvantages of traditional battery manufacturing methods, such as numerous technological processes and complicated operations, the emergence of 3D printing technology has broken through the limitations of traditional manufacturing processes.

3D printing technology can easily manufacture many structures that cannot be achieved by traditional processes, which effectively solves the problems in the large-scale preparation and personalized manufacturing of batteries. Therefore, 3D printing is expected to become a key manufacturing technology for future battery breakthroughs. Fu et al. successfully prepared a sheet-shaped electrode interleaved lithium-ion battery with a size of 7 mm × 3 mm by 3D printing, as shown in Figure 13.65 During the preparation process, it is necessary to formulate GO-based electrode inks with high viscosity and optimal viscoelasticity, including LiFePO4/graphene oxide composite slurry and Li4Ti5O12/graphene oxide composite slurry. 2D-material-based composites exhibit excellent electrochemical performance in alkali metal-ion batteries, which can be attributed to the following three points: (1) the 2D material can promote the contact between nanoparticles, which improves the overall conductivity of the composite material. (2) The 2D material is an excellent diffusion barrier, which may effectively prevent interfacial mixing between electrodes and current collectors. (3) The formation of unstable SEI film is suppressed on the surface of 2D materials, which improves the electrical contact and cycle performance. In addition, by adjusting the functional groups, interlayer distance, and microstructure morphology of 2D materials, the actual capacity of 2D material electrodes can be closer to the theoretical capacity. Combining 2D materials with other materials to construct macroscopic structures can further improve the electrochemical performance of alkali metal-ion batteries.

Lithium-sulfur batteries

With the large-scale development of new energy sources and electric vehicles, it is imperative to develop high-energy and low-cost electrochemical energy storage systems.66,67 The theoretical energy density of lithium-sulfur batteries is as high as 2600 W h kg−1, which is more than five times the energy density of commercial lithium-ion batteries. Moreover, sulfur also has the advantages of abundant reserves, low cost, and non-toxicity. Therefore, lithium-sulfur batteries are considered to be a new generation of battery systems with great development potential and application prospects.68,69 At present, the research work on lithium-sulfur batteries is mainly focused on the design and synthesis of high-performance sulfur cathode materials. The common strategy to optimize the performance of lithium-sulfur battery cathodes is to use 2D materials as supports for the active material sulfur.70,71 An ideal sulfur carrier material should have the following characteristics: (1) the material has a high specific surface area and a well-developed pore structure, which can provide sufficient storage space for sulfur and its discharge products; (2) the material has excellent electrical conductivity, which may provide good electrochemical contact for the active material; (3) the material has excellent structural stability, which can relieve the structural strain of the electrode during charge and discharge; and (4) it is desirable to have a strong adsorption and catalytic conversion of lithium polysulfides, which accelerates the positive redox reaction kinetics and suppresses the shuttling effect.

In recent years, 2D materials have great application potential as sulfur supports in lithium-sulfur batteries.72,73 However, the open two-dimensional planar structure of 2D materials cannot effectively restrain the diffusion of lithium polysulfides, which leads to a rapid decline in the capacity of lithium-sulfur batteries. Moreover, 2D materials are prone to stacking phenomenon, which greatly reduces their actual specific surface area. In recent years, it has been found that the assembly of sheet-layer 2D materials into a macroscopic bulk structure can provide a relatively closed storage space for sulfur and the discharge products, which can inhibit the shuttle effect to a certain extent and improve the electrochemical performance of lithium-sulfur batteries.74,75 For example, Mo et al. successfully prepared hierarchical graphene with a 3D network structure through a space confinement strategy.74 The loose stacking between graphene sheets forms an interconnected 3D macroporous structure. Then, sulfur was encapsulated in the aforementioned porous conductive network to obtain a porous graphene/sulfur composite. The composite has a capacity of 1,362 and 796 mA h g−1 at rates of 0.8 and 16 A g−1, respectively. Li et al. successfully prepared Ti3C2Tx/graphene hybrid fibers as cathodes for lithium-sulfur batteries by wet spinning assembly method (Figure 14).75 It was found that sulfur was uniformly distributed and tightly packed between graphene and Ti3C2Tx nanosheets. The material exhibits large specific capacity, high rate capability, and excellent cycling stability. More importantly, a reversible capacity of 5 mA h cm−2 can be achieved under high sulfur loading (5.6 mg cm−2), showing its great potential for practical applications.

In addition, heteroatom doping of graphene can enhance the adsorption capacity of substrates for lithium polysulfides, which improves the electrochemical performance of lithium-sulfur batteries. Xie et al. prepared three-dimensional boron-doped graphene aerogels by a one-step hydrothermal method.76 It is found that the doped boron atoms can effectively improve the conductivity of the material and the adsorption capacity of lithium polysulfide. The material exhibits good electrochemical performance, which has a specific capacity of 600 mA h g−1 after 200 cycles at a rate of 2 C. Xu et al. prepared three-dimensional nitrogen and sulfur co-doped porous graphene by activating polypyrrole-functionalized graphene with K2CO3.77 The incorporation of pyridinic nitrogen and thiophenic sulfur in the carbon lattice can regulate the local electronic configuration, which increases the binding ability of the graphene-based substrate to lithium polysulfide. The material combined with sulfur as a cathode for lithium-sulfur batteries exhibits excellent rate performance, which can still reach a specific capacity of 651 mA h g−1 at a rate of 5 C.

The wide application of flexible and wearable electronic devices puts more demands on the design of electrochemical energy storage devices. Compared with traditional battery systems, flexible batteries need to maintain their original electrochemical performance after being bent, folded, stretched, and squeezed. Jin et al. synthesized a flexible self-supporting graphene/sulfur composite film (GS/S) by vacuum filtration.78 First, sulfur nanoparticles were uniformly deposited on the graphene layer through the chemical reaction between sodium thiosulfate and hydrochloric acid. Then, the GS/S film can be obtained by vacuum filtration. After 100 cycles at a rate of 0.1C, the GS/S flexible electrode still maintains a specific capacity of 600 mA h g−1. Cao et al. also prepared a flexible reduced graphene oxide/sulfur composite film (rGO-S) and applied it to the cathode of lithium-sulfur batteries (Figure 15).79 First, sulfur nanoparticles were deposited on the surface of graphene oxide using a chemical reaction deposition strategy. Then, the Zn foil was dipped into the GO-S dispersion, which reduced GO to rGO by utilizing the reduction potential difference between rGO/GO and Zn/Zn2+. During the reduction process, electrons are easily transferred from the surface of the zinc foil to the GO sheet, resulting in the ionization of the surface of the zinc foil. The generated Zn2+ was able to adsorb more negatively charged GO sheets, which induced their further reduction and assembly. The exfoliated rGO-S film has outstanding flexibility and mechanical strength. The initial specific capacities of the electrodes are 1,187 and 1,360 mA h g−1 at a rate of 0.1 C, respectively. More importantly, the capacity remains stable after bending. Similar to the application of graphene in conventional lithium-sulfur battery cathodes, various other types of carbon materials, conductive polymers, and polar materials have also been introduced into flexible graphene-based electrodes. Graphene has fully exploited its advantages of high flexibility and high conductivity in these composite electrodes.80 Ghosh et al. prepared polyaniline-/sodium-alginate-bonded S/Mn3O4/graphene flexible films by in situ polymerization of aniline.81 As a binder, polyaniline/sodium alginate has not only high mechanical strength and electronic/ionic conductivity but also a certain chemical adsorption effect on lithium polysulfide. As a lithium-sulfur battery cathode, the flexible film has an initial specific capacity of 1,015 mA h g−1 at a current density of 5 A g−1.

Metal-air batteries

With the rapid development of electronic products, electric vehicles, and smart grids, higher requirements are placed on the energy density of energy storage devices. Due to the ultrahigh theoretical energy density, metal-air batteries (MABs) have attracted extensive interest in recent years. Among them, when lithium metal is used as the metal electrode, the theoretical energy density of the battery can be as high as 11,680 W h·kg−1, which is even comparable to petroleum. In lithium-air batteries, the quality of the cathode material largely determines the electrochemical performance of the battery. After a long cycle test, it is easy to accumulate a large amount of discharge by-products at one end of the cathode material, which greatly reduces the electrochemical performance of lithium-air batteries. Analysis of the reasons found that on the one hand, it is easy to lead to the problem of electrode passivation. On the other hand, it can reduce the gas transport efficiency of the cathode material. In order to solve the aforementioned problems, it is necessary to rationally design the cathode materials. The macroscopic assembly structure based on 2D materials is an ideal catalyst substrate for cathode materials, which is mainly attributed to large specific surface area, multi-level pore structure, and interconnected network architecture. Recently, Wu et al. successfully prepared a 3D graphene-based N-doped carbon nitride composite material, which can be used as a cathode material for lithium-air batteries (Figure 16).82 It is worth noting that the composite shows outstanding electrochemical properties. When the current density is 1,000 mA g−1, the reversible capacity and overpotential of the composite are 8,892 mA h g−1 and 430 mV, respectively. On the one hand, 3D graphene has an interconnected network structure, which provides enough reaction space for active materials. On the other hand, the introduction of N atoms increases the number of active sites, which significantly improves the kinetic rate of electrochemical reactions.

According to different electrolytes, MABs can be divided into organic electrolyte systems and aqueous electrolyte systems. The lithium-air battery mentioned earlier belongs to the organic electrolyte system. Compared with organic electrolytes, MABs based on aqueous electrolyte systems have the advantages of low cost and high safety, which has attracted extensive research. Among them, when zinc metal is used as the metal electrode, the aqueous electrolyte system has the characteristics of low cost, high safety, and high theoretical energy density (1,086 W h kg−1), which has become a research hotspot in the field of MABs. In recent years, the research work on Zn-air batteries has been widely reported. However, there are still some key issues to be resolved for Zn-air batteries. The cathode materials still have the problems of low catalytic activity and poor cycle stability, which greatly reduces the electrochemical performance. In order to solve the aforementioned problems, it is considered an effective way to design single-atom catalysts in cathode materials through interface engineering. The macroscopic assemblies based on 2D materials have large surface areas and interconnected network structures, which provide effective supports for single-atom catalysts. Fu et al. innovatively developed a hydrogel strategy to prepare Ni-MnO/3D rGO composites that can be used as cathode materials for zinc-air batteries.83 The Ni-MnO/3D rGO composites show excellent electrochemical performance, which is mainly attributed to the uniform dispersion of Ni-MnO in 3D rGO. More importantly, the onset and half-wave units of this composite are 0.78 V and 0.94 V, respectively, which is even close to commercial Pt/C. The composite material exhibits a connected network structure, good electrical conductivity, and stable active sites, which are conducive to improving the electrochemical performance. This study provides theoretical and technical guidance for the rational design of graphene-based macroscopic assemblies, which provides an effective way to achieve high-performance MABs. In addition, it also opens up new ideas for the research of other 2D materials in the field of MABs.

Seawater desalination applications

In the 21st century, the increasing demand for industrial and agricultural water and the explosive growth of population have caused a global water resource crisis. The shortage of fresh water resources has become a common challenge facing the world. Therefore, seawater desalination technology has become a research hotspot all over the world, which is an important direction for the development of marine resources. The promotion of this technology will provide a good solution to the shortage of water resources such as offshore operations, agricultural irrigation, and drinking.

Capacitive deionization

With rapid development in the past decade, capacitive deionization (CDI) has become an emerging technology in the field of seawater desalination. Compared with other desalination technologies, CDI has the following advantages: (1) the equipment is simple and easy to operate and has low maintenance cost and long service life; (2) it will not cause secondary pollution to the water body, which is a clean and efficient desalination technology; (3) the applied voltage across the CDI electrodes is low, generally no more than 1.6 V. Especially in the treatment of low-salinity saline water (salt concentration <3,000 mg L−1) it has a unique advantage; and (4) the operating voltage of the CDI module is low, which can be combined with clean energy technologies such as solar energy. At the same time, the energy released during the electrode regeneration process can be collected and reused. CDI is a new seawater desalination technology based on the electrochemical double layer theory. The basic principle is to apply an electric field to both ends of two parallel and opposite porous electrodes, and the anions and cations in the solution move to the two poles under the action of the electric field. An electric double layer is formed at the interface between the electrode and the electrolyte and stored in the electrode to achieve the purpose of removing charged ions in the solution. When the adsorption reaches saturation, short-circuit the electrode or apply a reverse voltage to achieve the purpose of returning the ions to the solution to achieve the regeneration of the electrode.84 The CDI performance largely depends on the electrode material. Based on the principle of CDI, the adsorption capacity depends on the total capacitance after the inner layer capacitance and the diffusion layer capacitance are connected in series between the electrode and the solution. Therefore, an ideal electrode material needs to have good conductivity, high specific surface area, narrow pore size distribution, and good hydrophilicity. On the one hand, good electrical conductivity and high specific surface area can facilitate electron conduction and provide more active sites. On the other hand, the narrow pore size distribution and good hydrophilicity can quickly transport electrolytes.

Graphene is a single-atom-thick 2D nanomaterial with high specific surface area (2,600 m2 g−1), outstanding mechanical properties, and electrical conductivity (7,200 S m−1), which has been applied in the field of seawater desalination. In 2009, Li et al. first prepared CDI electrodes from graphene synthesized by the Hummers method and reduced by hydrazine hydrate.85 However, the agglomeration of graphene greatly reduces the specific surface area of the electrode, which seriously affects the CDI performance of the electrode. In order to solve this problem, Shi et al. prepared a three-dimensional graphene electrode through a hydrothermal reduction self-assembly strategy. It was found that three-dimensional graphene with nanopores etched by hydrogen peroxide showed excellent performance, which was attributed to the increase of specific surface area and reduction of ion transmission distance.86 In addition, the abundant oxygen-containing functional groups on the surface of graphene provide a bridge for compounding with other materials. Zhou et al. prepared Na4Ti9O10/rGO composite electrodes with a three-dimensional structure by hydrothermal self-assembly.87 Na4Ti9O10 is uniformly dispersed on the surface of graphene, which effectively increases the specific surface area. In NaCl solution of 250 mg L−1, it exhibited twice the CDI performance at 1.4 V than that of the Na4Ti9O10/activated carbon composite.

In the past few years, it has been found that low-cost 2D layered materials can also be used as electrode materials for CDI, such as MoS2, MXene, etc. The surface of the material is injected with negative charges during the chemical exfoliation process to facilitate the adsorption of ions, which exhibits good electrochemical activity. Srimuk et al. studied the desalination performance of Ti3C2Tx-based electrode materials.88 It was found that the interlayers of MXene can rapidly intercalate anions or cations. The overall ion removal capacity reaches 13 mg g−1 with little capacity fading after 30 cycles. However, the stacking effect between MXene layers reduces the utilization of its electrochemically active sites. To solve this problem, Bao et al. successfully prepared MXene aerogels with 3D porous structure by freeze-drying method, as shown in Figure 17.89 Its specific surface area of 290 m2 g−1 is 7.3 times that of ordinary MXene. The binder-free electrode was tested in symmetric CDI, which exhibited an ultrahigh electrosorption capacity of 45 mg g−1 and good cycling stability. As far as the current work on CDI is concerned, the improvement of CDI performance can be divided into three categories: (1) designing new CDI electrode materials; (2) combining 2D materials with other materials or performing heteroatom doping (such as nitrogen, sulfur, etc.); and (3) optimizing the hierarchical structure of electrode materials.

Membrane deionization

Membrane deionization technology is based on the selective permeability of the membrane to block the ions in the solution, which does not involve the phase change process. Therefore, this method has the advantages of low energy consumption, simplicity, and convenience, which has attracted widespread attention. Graphene oxide (GO), as a derivative of graphene, is a thin film with a thickness of tens to hundreds of nanometers stacked by multiple layers of oxidized graphene sheets. GO nanosheets were first prepared from oxidizing graphite through the Hummers method. Then it was made into water dispersion by ultrasonic vibration. Finally, a thin film is formed by vacuum filtration method, spin coating method, etc.90,91,92 Therefore, the emergence of graphene oxide has brought new possibilities for the practical use of 2D materials in seawater desalination systems. Prof. Nair and Geim from the University of Manchester have made a series of breakthroughs in graphene-based separation membranes.93,94,95 Due to the good hydrophilicity of the traditional graphene oxide membrane, its layer spacing (∼1.35 nm) cannot achieve the screening effect on water and ions. By adjusting the different humidity environments of the graphene oxide film, the graphene oxide layer spacing (0. 64–0. 98 nm) can be effectively adjusted, which can effectively screen the ions. The NaCl filtration rate of as-prepared graphene oxide film can reach 97%. The emergence of this work proves that the selective permeability of graphene oxide can be introduced into the field of seawater desalination.

In 2014, Joshi et al. investigated the separation effect of graphene oxide membranes on solutions containing different solutes by permeating solutions in U-shaped tubes separated by GO membranes.96 It was found that the screening effect of GO membrane on solutes in solution depends on the relationship between the size of solute particles and graphene oxide channels. When the ion hydration radius or molecular size in the solution is larger than the interlayer size of the graphene oxide channel, the GO film exhibits complete barrier properties. Only when the size of solute particles is appropriate, they can enter the nanochannels between GO layers for transport. Sun et al. conducted an in-depth analysis of the permeation process of various salt ions in GO membranes. It was found that the GO membrane has an excellent screening effect on ions, which is mainly attributed to the electrostatic force of the negative charge on the surface of graphene oxide on the cations in the solution. Therefore, GO membranes with different valence states may be separated according to their charge to different degrees.97,98 It was demonstrated that the confinement of GO membranes through channels can be used to selectively sieve ions, which can be effectively used in desalination of seawater by membrane separation.99

The directional screening of different situations from organic macromolecules to small ions can be realized by adjusting the relevant parameters of GO membranes. The flow of solutions in GO membranes is often closely related to the flow behavior in the interlayer channels of graphene oxide. Therefore, the sieving performance of GO membranes for different solutes can be largely controlled by adjusting the distance between GO membrane stack layers. Mi et al. mixed graphene oxide aqueous solution with substances of different sizes to prepare a composite film of GO sheets and nanoparticles.100 In this composite film, the nanoparticles and GO sheets can be closely connected by covalent bonds or electrostatic interactions. It is found that adjusting the size of intercalated nanoparticles in GO sheet can realize the control of interlayer spacing. In addition, the interlayer spacing of untreated GO membranes in aqueous solution is generally around 1 nm, whereas the hydrated diameter of many salt ions is below this scale, such as 0.78 nm for sodium ions. Therefore, ordinary GO membranes have poor screening performance for small-sized ions such as sodium and potassium. In order to achieve the effect of high salt resistance in small-sized ionic solutions, many research works on reducing the interlayer spacing of graphene oxide have been carried out. In addition to the aforementioned methods, the GO reduction method is also an effective method to regulate the interlayer spacing.95,101 Reduced graphene oxide (RGO) films can be prepared by reducing the GO aqueous dispersion with a reducing agent and then performing suction filtration or directly reducing the GO film by heating. After reduction, the RGO film will reduce the number of functional groups on the sheet, which will weaken the swelling effect caused by the water absorption of GO. Therefore, the interlayer spacing of the reduced RGO film will decrease accordingly. Liu et al. prepared RGO films by reducing the vacuum-filtered graphene oxide films with hydroiodic acid vapor (Figure 18).101 The water flux and ion screening rate of sodium chloride solution passing through RGO membrane, GO membrane, and commercial forward osmosis membrane were measured by forward osmosis experiment. The as-prepared RGO membrane has a much higher ion rejection rate during the forward osmosis filtration process than the GO membrane before reduction due to the reduced layer spacing after reduction. At the same time, it also ensures good water flux, which has surpassed the commercial forward osmosis membrane of cellulose triacetate.

In addition, Chen et al. found that different ions can also play a precise regulation role between graphene oxide layers.102 According to the first-principles calculations and ultraviolet absorption spectra, the cations in the GO channels mainly exist in the places where the oxidized groups and aromatic rings coexist on the GO sheets. Therefore, the cations combine with the large π bonds on the two sheets of the GO channel to form stable ionic bonds, which inhibits the swelling of the GO membrane in solution. Shao et al. realized the precise regulation of the interlayer spacing through the interaction of GO membranes with different hydrated cations.103 The GO membrane is combined with an ion solution with a small hydration diameter, which can effectively prevent the passage of larger hydrated ions. Therefore, precise control of the interlayer of GO membranes can be achieved through cation control, which achieves the purpose of selective filtration of water molecules and hydrated ions. This study provides theoretical and technical guidance for the interlayer regulation of graphene oxide, which shows great application prospects in seawater desalination and other fields. More importantly, this also opens up new ideas for the research of other 2D materials in the field of separation membranes.103

Membrane separation technology is a separation technology that uses the selective permeability of a separation membrane and uses potential difference, concentration difference, pressure difference, temperature difference, etc. as driving forces to separate, concentrate, and purify different components in the feed liquid. In recent years, researchers have used electrospinning methods to prepare membrane materials based on 2D materials, which have been successfully applied in seawater desalination.104 The microstructure and interfacial properties of this membrane material can be designed and customized, which is attributed to the versatility of the electrospinning process. In addition to seawater desalination, scientists have discovered that applying new two-dimensional nanomaterials and their composite materials to water treatment technology can effectively purify impurities such as organic pollutants, heavy metal ions, and dyes in water.105,106,107,108,109,110,111 Functional membrane materials based on 2D materials have controllable separation performance, which can achieve a breakthrough in the trade-off effect. At the same time, 2D materials give new possibilities to membrane materials with their unique lamellar structure, catalytic performance, and modifiability, which show great potential in the preparation and application of functional membranes.

Summary and outlook

The macroscopic assembly of 2D materials shows great potential in the fields of energy storage and seawater desalination. Compared with other types of materials, 2D materials offer superior advantages for assembly due to their ultrathin-thickness sheet structures and excellent physicochemical properties. As mentioned earlier, we introduce a variety of representative macroscopic assembly structures, including 1D fibers, 2D films, and 3D architectures. The precise control of the macroscopic assembly structure can effectively avoid the stacking phenomenon of 2D materials, which demonstrates the excellent characteristics of 2D materials. With the rapid development of society, human beings have put forward higher requirements for energy and environmental issues. Therefore, here we focus on applications in the fields of energy storage and seawater desalination. In addition to the range of applications mentioned earlier, macroscopic assemblies of 2D materials are expected to be valuable for other critical problems, such as air purification filters, catalysis, and gas separation.

Despite the many important breakthroughs have been achieved in the technology of macroscopic assemblies based on 2D materials, many challenges still remain. It mainly includes the following three aspects: (1) the current macroscopic assembly structure is mainly formed by stacking multiple layers of 2D materials. It is necessary to develop new preparation processes and methods to assemble single-layer 2D materials into macroscopically assembled structures. (2) The properties of macroscopic assembly structures depend largely on the shape and pore size. Therefore, it is very important to precisely control the shape and pore size of its structure. 3D printing can be precisely controlled in structure and size according to its working principle. However, the existing technology is still in its infancy, and there are still many key scientific and technical problems to be solved urgently. (3) The macroscopic assembly structure has shown great potential application value in energy storage and seawater desalination. However, it is very important to further understand the working mechanism of macroscopically assembled structures in these applications. It is believed that in the future, the research community and the industry will continue to focus on the preparation of macroscopic assembly structures based on 2D materials and the development of their applications in various fields.

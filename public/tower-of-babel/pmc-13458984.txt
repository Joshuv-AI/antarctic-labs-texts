Endobronchial Myoepithelioma With Fluorodeoxyglucose Uptake
Authors: Taro Abe, Keitaro Nakamoto, Eriko Ieki, Miku Oda, Yoshifumi Hirata, Isao Kataoka, Kazuharu Suda, Aya Isomura, Junji Shibahara, Yutaka Suzuki
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTMyoepitheliomas of the respiratory organs are extremely rare. We report a case of endobronchial myoepithelioma in a 43‐year‐old woman presenting to hospital with a cough. Chest computed tomography (CT) showed consolidation in the lingular segment of her left upper lobe. She was initially diagnosed as having pneumonia and treated with antibiotics, but her symptoms persisted. We then performed diagnostic bronchoscopy and observed a smooth, elevated lesion obstructing the lingular bronchus. Positron emission tomography/CT revealed a nodule in the lingular bronchus of the left upper lobe with fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) uptake, while the consolidation had disappeared. Left upper lobectomy was performed, and on the basis of the pathological findings including immunostaining, she was finally diagnosed as having myoepithelioma. There has been no recurrence during an 18‐month follow‐up. Although myoepithelioma is a rare disease, it should be considered in the differential diagnosis of nodules with FDG uptake.

Introduction

Myoepitheliomas of the respiratory organs are extremely rare tumours arising from the submucosal glands of the tracheobronchial tree. According to the World Health Organization classification, myoepitheliomas are categorised as one of six subtypes of salivary gland‐type tumours (primary pulmonary salivary gland‐type tumours) [1]. These tumours are found as endoluminal lesions or in the peripheral parenchyma of the lung. Herein, we report the case of a patient with endobronchial myoepithelioma initially diagnosed as pneumonia that was finally diagnosed through bronchoscopy and was surgically resected.

Case Report

A 43‐year‐old woman presented to our hospital with a cough. She had a medical history of appendicitis and post‐cholelithiasis surgery, and a 24‐pack‐year history of cigarette smoking. She was initially diagnosed as having bronchitis and received symptomatic treatment, but her symptoms did not improve. On examination at a follow‐up appointment at a later date, her vital signs were: temperature 36.6°C, SpO2 99% on room air, blood pressure 102/66 mmHg, pulse rate 90/min and respiratory rate 18/min. Chest‐X‐ray showed an opacity in the middle left lung field (Figure 1A). Chest computed tomography (CT) also showed a consolidation in the lingular segment of the left upper lobe (Figure 1B). Laboratory findings showed elevated neutrophils (75.8%, normal range 40%–70%) and C‐reactive protein level (1.16 mg/dL, normal range 0.00–0.14 mg/dL). She was diagnosed as having pneumonia and was treated with azithromycin. However, she experienced little improvement, and sputum was also observed. Further blood tests revealed that tumour markers CEA, SCC, NSE and ProGRP were normal. Although a serum antiglycopeptidolipid core IgA antibody test was negative, an interferon gamma release assay was positive. We then performed diagnostic bronchoscopy that revealed a smooth, elevated lesion obstructing the lingular bronchus and biopsied the lesion (Figure 1C). The biopsy suggested a myoepithelial tumour, but a definitive diagnosis could not be reached. After bronchoscopy, further positron emission tomography (PET)/CT (approximately 80 days after chest CT) revealed a nodule in the lingular bronchus of the left upper lobe in which fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) was accumulating (standard uptake value max 12.9) (Figure 1D). The consolidation in the lingular segment of the left upper lobe had disappeared. These results indicated the possibility of malignancy. Various treatment options including argon plasma coagulation, high‐frequency snare resection and lobectomy were considered. However, following consultation with the patient, a left upper lobectomy was performed at a later date. Gross pathologic findings revealed a 10 mm nodule at the entrance to the lingual subsegment, which was continuous with the bronchus (Figure 2A). No necrosis or haemorrhage was seen in the tumour. Pathological findings at the nodule site showed solid proliferation of spindle‐shaped epithelial cells with inconspicuous dysplasia (Figure 2B). Immunostaining showed positivity for S100, vimentin and cytokeratin AE1AE3, with Ki‐67 expressed at a low level of approximately 1%–2% (Figure 2C–F). On the basis of these results, she was finally diagnosed as having a myoepithelioma. She has attended regular follow‐up appointments since the surgery, and no recurrence has been observed.

Discussion

We reported a rare case of endobronchial myoepithelioma with FDG uptake. Myoepithelial cells are contractile cells that originate from the ectoderm [2]. Several normal tissues, such as the salivary glands, sweat glands, prostate, lung and nasopharynx that have secretory functions have myoepithelial cells. A common stem cell with a bidirectional differentiation into epithelial or myoepithelial cell is hypothesised to be the cell of origin of this tumour. The varied histological types (spindle, plasmacytoid or hyaline, epitheloid, clear and oncocytic) exhibited by myoepitheliomas can be attributed to the various stages of differentiation from a cell that has the potential to differentiate into epithelial cells [3]. Myoepitheliomas represent less than 1% of all the salivary gland tumours and occur in adults, with a peak occurrence between the fourth and fifth decades of life [2, 4]. No sex prediction is noted. Moreover, the occurrence of that in the respiratory system is extremely rare. Therefore, past reports on myoepithelioma of the lung have mainly consisted of case reports and small series. Consequently, knowledge on them remains limited.

Imaging findings of myoepithelioma often describe well‐defined, circular nodules on CT when the tumour is located in the lung parenchyma [5, 6]. However, if the lesion is located in the central rather than peripheral areas, findings such as breathlessness, wheezing, obstructive pneumonia and atelectasis may be present. In the present case, consolidation was initially observed on CT, so the patient was initially diagnosed and treated for pneumonia. As the consolidation had improved by the time of PET/CT, we thought that the nodule, which had been hidden by the treatment for pneumonia, had become clear.

Generally, PET/CT is useful for evaluating benign and malignant lung tumours [7]. According to a report by Kourda et al. only one case of PET/CT in a rare benign myoepithelioma has been reported, in which FDG uptake was negative [6]. However, in our patient, FDG uptake was observed at the nodule site. As there are reports of FDG uptake in malignant myoepithelial carcinoma, it may be difficult to differentiate between a benign and malignant tumour based solely on PET/CT results. Therefore, surgical intervention was necessary for a definitive diagnosis.

Myoepithelioma cells exhibit diverse morphologies, but spindle cell types are frequently described. Immunostaining is also important, and past reports have shown positive results for S100, vimentin, SMA, cytokeratin and others [5, 6, 8]. In the present case, solid proliferation of spindle‐shaped epithelial cells with minimal dysplasia was observed. Furthermore, low expression of cell proliferation marker Ki‐67 led to a final diagnosis of myoepithelioma.

Treatment with surgical therapy has been frequently reported in the past [5, 6, 8], and our patient also underwent surgery. The prognosis is often good, and in follow‐up of our patient over 18 months post‐surgery, no recurrence has been observed to date. Nevertheless, metastasis has been reported in past cases, so careful monitoring is necessary going forward.

In conclusion, we experienced a rare presentation of myoepithelioma of the lung with FDG uptake. Although the possibility of a malignancy was suggested, the diagnosis was made through excision of the lesion and comprehensive pathological evaluation. Reports on the clinical characteristics and prognosis of myoepithelioma are limited, making future follow‐up important, and the accumulation of cases is expected.

Endovascular Treatment of Symptomatic Carotid Web Assessed by Intravascular Ultrasound
Authors: Diego Soto V, Gabriel Cassorla J, Sebastián Morales Z, Gonzalo Velez M, José Segundo R, Julián Tique E, Ángel Flores H
Published: 2026

Carotid web (CW) is a rare non-atherosclerotic cause of stroke, characterized by an intraluminal projection at the carotid bulb (CB) [1]. Here, we present two cases in which the complementary use of intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) provided real-time dynamic information.

Case 1: A 29-year-old woman with morbid obesity and insulin resistance presented with an ischemic stroke 1 week before intervention. Case 2: A 45-year-old woman with hypertension and active smoking presented with recurrent amaurosis fugax, including two episodes in the preceding month. Computed tomography angiography (CTA) showed intimal thickening along the posterior wall of the CB in both patients (Fig. 1). Duplex ultrasonography showed only nonspecific posterior wall thickening. Preoperative workup (electrocardiography, echocardiography, thoracic CTA, and hypercoagulability tests) excluded alternative thromboembolic etiologies. Following multidisciplinary review [2] and shared decision-making with both patients [3], an endovascular approach was selected because both patients were symptomatic [4]. Dual-antiplatelet therapy with aspirin and clopidogrel was initiated before intervention.

Preoperative oblique sagittal computed tomography angiography (CTA) and intraoperative angiographic findings. (A) In Case 1, CTA showed a characteristic shelf-like intraluminal projection at the carotid bulb (arrowhead). (B) In Case 2, the corresponding finding was also present but less prominent (arrowhead). Both carotid webs involved the right carotid artery. Intraoperative angiography in Case 1 (C) and Case 2 (D) showed contrast stagnation along the posterior wall of the carotid bulb and at the origin of the internal carotid artery (arrows).

Through a transfemoral approach and a distal filter protection placed before IVUS, angiography revealed contrast stagnation along the posterior wall of the CB in both cases (Fig. 1). IVUS confirmed posterior wall thickening and demonstrated the dynamic behavior of the CW through the cardiac cycle (Figs. 2, 3). Following precise localization of the CW and healthy landing zones using the IVUS catheter distance markers, dual-mesh carotid stents were deployed to prevent intraluminal prolapse of the CW through the stent struts [5]. IVUS confirmed full wall apposition and no residual protrusion of the CW (Fig. 4). The distal protection filters showed no embolic debris following removal. The postoperative course was uneventful. At 2-year follow-up, neither patient showed stent restenosis, and both remained asymptomatic (Fig. 5).

Intravascular ultrasound images demonstrated posterior wall thickening at the carotid bulb and proximal internal carotid artery (ICA). Posterior wall thickening was less pronounced in the 29-year-old patient (A) than in the 45-year-old patient (B).

Intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) assessment of the dynamic changes of the carotid web (CW) during systole and diastole. (A) Fluoroscopy showed IVUS-based localization of the carotid bifurcation, CW, and distal healthy internal carotid artery (ICA) before stent deployment. In the 29-year-old patient, systolic images (B, C) showed expansion of the ICA lumen (yellow) and displacement of the CW (red) toward the posterior wall, while a contrast stagnation area remained between the CW and posterior wall of the ICA (blue). During diastole (D, E), the ICA lumen decreased because of the highly mobile CW, with a corresponding increase in the contrast stagnation area. In the 45-year-old patient (F, G), the CW (red) was also identified but showed less mobility, with little change in the ICA lumen between systole and diastole. (H) A dual-mesh carotid stent was deployed following precise identification of the anatomical structures of interest with IVUS.

Post-stenting angiographic and intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) findings. Completion angiography showed adequate stent positioning and patency, with no residual contrast stagnation along the posterior wall of the carotid bulb and internal carotid artery in either patient ([A] 29-year-old patient, [B] 45-year-old patient). IVUS confirmed adequate stent expansion and wall apposition, with no residual intraluminal protrusion of the carotid web ([C] 29-year-old patient, [D] 45-year-old patient).

Two-year follow-up imaging. (A) Computed tomography angiography of the 29-year-old patient showed adequate stent expansion, positioning, and patency. (B) Carotid duplex ultrasonography of the 45-year-old patient showed a patent stent without hemodynamically significant restenosis.

These cases highlight the added value of IVUS in selected CW cases with adequate embolic protection by demonstrating dynamic morphological features not be fully appreciated with conventional imaging alone.

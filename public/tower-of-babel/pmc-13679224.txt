Timing of corticosteroid initiation and severe in-hospital outcomes in pediatric Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia: a propensity score-matched cohort study
Authors: Lei Yang, Hu Liu, Jiangang Leng, Jiali Tong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAmong hospitalized children with Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (MPP) who received systemic corticosteroids, the association between treatment timing and subsequent in-hospital outcomes remains uncertain. We evaluated whether earlier initiation was associated with a more favorable hospital course.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective propensity score-matched cohort study at a tertiary pediatric center in China between January 2023 and August 2025. Children aged 1–18 years with confirmed MPP who received systemic corticosteroids 3–14 days after symptom onset were included. Symptom onset was adjudicated by chart abstraction from caregiver-reported histories documented in the electronic medical record. All included patients received macrolide-based first-line antibiotics. Patients were grouped by corticosteroid initiation timing as early (3–7 days) or late (> 7 days). The propensity model used prespecified pre-treatment demographic, clinical-course, admission respiratory, inflammatory, radiographic, comorbidity, and resistance variables. The primary outcome was a severe composite endpoint; secondary outcomes included fever duration after corticosteroid initiation, hospital stay, oxygen therapy, ICU admission, pleural effusion, and antibiotic escalation. Exploratory analyses compared 3–5, 6–7, and > 7 days, with initiation day additionally modeled continuously for trend.ResultsAmong 902 eligible patients, 482 received early corticosteroids and 420 received late corticosteroids. After 1:1 matching, 299 pairs were analyzed; 183 early initiators and 121 late initiators were unmatched and excluded from the matched cohort. Early initiation was associated with lower odds of the severe composite outcome than late initiation (12.0% vs. 19.4%; OR 0.57, 95% CI 0.36–0.89; P = 0.018), less oxygen therapy (8.7% vs. 13.7%; OR 0.59, 95% CI 0.35–0.99), less antibiotic escalation (9.4% vs. 15.7%; OR 0.56, 95% CI 0.34–0.91; P = 0.019), shorter fever after corticosteroid initiation (3 [IQR 2–4] vs. 4 [3–5] days), and shorter hospitalization (7 [6–9] vs. 9 [7–11] days; both P < 0.001). In exploratory timing analyses, only initiation on days 3–5 was associated with lower odds of severe outcomes (OR 0.35, 95% CI 0.18–0.68), whereas initiation on days 6–7 was not (OR 0.83, 95% CI 0.48–1.43). Continuous modeling suggested a graded association between later initiation day and worse outcomes (P for trend = 0.021).ConclusionAmong corticosteroid-treated hospitalized children with MPP, earlier corticosteroid initiation—particularly on days 3–5 after symptom onset—was associated with a more favorable in-hospital course. Because residual confounding, potential collider bias, and time-dependent bias cannot be excluded, these findings should be interpreted as associative and require prospective confirmation.Keywords: Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia, Corticosteroids, Timing of treatment, Pediatric respiratory infection

Introduction

Mycoplasma pneumoniae is a leading cause of community-acquired pneumonia in children and accounts for a substantial proportion of pediatric hospital admissions due to lower respiratory tract infections [1, 2]. While the majority of cases follow a self-limiting course, a significant subset may progress to severe or refractory pneumonia, often requiring prolonged hospitalization and supportive care [3, 4]. The immunopathogenesis of Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (MPP) is characterized not only by direct microbial damage but also by an exaggerated host immune response [5]. In this context, systemic corticosteroids have emerged as a therapeutic adjunct aimed at modulating inflammation and preventing pulmonary complications, particularly in children with more severe clinical presentations [6].

Despite their widespread use in pediatric practice, the optimal timing of corticosteroid initiation in MPP remains inadequately defined [7]. In real-world settings, the decision to initiate corticosteroids is frequently influenced by clinical judgment, severity markers, or institutional protocols, resulting in considerable variability across patients [8]. Some children receive corticosteroids within the first few days of illness onset, while others receive them beyond one week, raising concerns about both overtreatment and missed therapeutic windows [9, 10]. The underlying assumption that earlier administration may attenuate inflammatory injury and improve outcomes is biologically plausible, but robust empirical data to guide this timing are lacking. Existing literature is limited by small sample sizes, heterogeneity in study designs, lack of confounder control, and an absence of precise time-based stratification in corticosteroid initiation.

Given that corticosteroids exert their effects on the immunologic phase of disease rather than the infectious phase, their efficacy is likely time-sensitive [9]. However, no consensus exists regarding the temporal threshold beyond which corticosteroid therapy may lose efficacy or even result in harm [11]. Furthermore, previous studies rarely differentiate between early and ultra-early administration, making it difficult to determine whether there exists a critical window during which corticosteroids confer maximal benefit [12]. Addressing this question requires rigorous evaluation in real-world cohorts, ideally with analytical strategies capable of reducing indication bias and baseline confounding [13].

To address this evidence gap, we conducted a large-scale, retrospective cohort study using real-world clinical data from a tertiary pediatric hospital in China. Our primary objective was to investigate whether earlier versus later corticosteroid initiation among hospitalized children already selected to receive systemic corticosteroids for MPP was associated with different in-hospital outcomes. By applying propensity score matching and exploratory timing analyses, we aimed to identify whether initiation within a defined early window—particularly days 3–5 after symptom onset—was associated with a more favorable clinical course. These data were not intended to address the overall efficacy of corticosteroids versus no corticosteroids in unselected MPP, but rather the timing question within the treated population.

Methods

Study design and setting

This investigation was conducted as a retrospective, observational cohort study at a high-volume tertiary pediatric medical center in China. The institution serves as a regional referral hospital for pediatric respiratory infections, admitting a large number of MPP cases annually. The study was designed to evaluate how variations in the timing of systemic corticosteroid initiation influenced clinical outcomes during hospitalization. The observational period extended from January 1, 2023, to August 31, 2025, capturing real-world clinical practices over three consecutive years.

Clinical data were extracted from the hospital’s integrated electronic medical record (EMR) system, encompassing inpatient charts, laboratory databases, pharmacy records, and radiology archives. All patient identifiers were removed prior to analysis to ensure confidentiality. The protocol received formal approval from the institutional ethics review board, and the requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study. The study adhered to the STROBE guidelines to ensure methodological rigor and transparency.

Participants and eligibility criteria

Patients were eligible if they were 1–18 years of age, were hospitalized during the study period, had confirmed MPP, and received systemic corticosteroids 3–14 days after symptom onset. To permit accurate exposure classification, inclusion required a documented symptom-onset date and complete corticosteroid treatment records, defined as available information on corticosteroid agent, route, start date, initial daily dose, and treatment duration. Because this study was designed to evaluate timing among children who received corticosteroids, patients who never received systemic corticosteroids were not included.

Exclusion criteria included corticosteroid administration ≤ 2 days or > 14 days after symptom onset, severe chronic pulmonary diseases likely to independently influence the hospital course (e.g., bronchiectasis, bronchiolitis obliterans, interstitial lung disease, cystic fibrosis, or chronic home oxygen dependence), known immunodeficiency, chronic systemic immunosuppressive therapy, or missing exposure, outcome, or prespecified covariate data. Mild asthma or allergic disease without chronic oral corticosteroid use was not exclusionary and was retained as a baseline comorbidity. Of 1,203 screened hospitalized MPP cases, 902 met all eligibility criteria and comprised the analytic cohort.

Diagnosis and clinical definitions

The diagnosis of MPP was established through a combination of clinical symptoms (persistent cough, fever), radiographic findings (lobar or segmental consolidation, interstitial infiltrates), and microbiological confirmation using polymerase chain reaction (PCR) or serologic assays demonstrating a fourfold rise in anti-Mycoplasma pneumoniae antibody titers. During the study period, first-line antibacterial therapy at our center was macrolide-based, most commonly azithromycin administered orally or intravenously according to contemporary Chinese pediatric expert guidance [14]. There was no rigid hospital protocol mandating a specific corticosteroid start day; rather, the decision to initiate corticosteroids was made by the attending physician on the basis of persistent fever, hypoxemia, radiographic progression, inflammatory markers, and the overall clinical trajectory. Macrolide resistance (MRMP) was determined by 23 S rRNA mutation testing when available or, when molecular testing was unavailable, inferred clinically from persistent fever and/or radiographic non-improvement after ≥ 72 h of macrolide therapy.

Symptom onset was adjudicated retrospectively by chart abstraction from caregiver-reported histories documented in emergency, admission, and inpatient progress notes. When multiple dates were recorded, the earliest concordant date was used; patients with missing or irreconcilable onset dates were excluded. The primary outcome, a severe composite endpoint, was defined as the occurrence of any of the following during the index hospitalization: admission to the intensive care unit (ICU), need for supplemental oxygen, escalation to second-line or broader-spectrum antibiotics, or development of pleural effusion. Fever duration was calculated from parental reports corroborated by the electronic medical record, and time to corticosteroid initiation was defined as the interval between symptom onset and the first dose of systemic corticosteroids.

Exposure and group definitions

The principal exposure variable was the timing of systemic corticosteroid administration relative to symptom onset. Based on clinical practice and previous literature, patients were dichotomized into an early initiation group (corticosteroids started on days 3–7 after symptom onset) and a late initiation group (corticosteroids started after day 7). Children treated before day 3 or after day 14 were excluded from the analytic cohort.

In routine practice, methylprednisolone was the predominant systemic corticosteroid used. The usual starting regimen at our hospital was intravenous methylprednisolone 1–2 mg/kg/day, with escalation to 4–6 mg/kg/day in children with persistent fever, hypoxemia, progressive radiographic involvement, or marked inflammatory activation; after clinical improvement, corticosteroids were tapered, usually over a total course of 3–7 days, and oral therapy was used mainly during tapering or in children able to tolerate enteral medication [15]. For exploratory analyses of the therapeutic window, the early group was further stratified into optimal early initiation (3–5 days) and standard early initiation (6–7 days). Group classification was anchored to symptom onset rather than admission date in order to better reflect the clinical stage of illness.

Data collection and variables

A standardized data extraction protocol was used to retrieve demographic characteristics (age, sex, weight, body mass index), clinical-course variables (duration of fever before admission), admission vital signs (temperature, respiratory rate, room-air oxygen saturation), radiographic findings (bilateral lung involvement and pleural effusion), baseline comorbidities, and initial laboratory values including white blood cell count, neutrophil proportion, C-reactive protein (CRP), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), serum creatinine, and N-terminal pro-BNP. Treatment variables included macrolide resistance status, first-line antibiotic class, corticosteroid timing, route, initial dose, duration, IVIG use, and subsequent antibiotic escalation.

Because no validated pediatric MPP severity score was routinely documented in our electronic medical record, and adult scores such as PSI, CURB-65, and SOFA are not applicable to most children with MPP, we prespecified available pre-treatment variables that could plausibly influence both clinician choice of earlier corticosteroid initiation and the risk of adverse outcomes. These variables captured illness duration before admission, admission respiratory status, inflammatory burden, radiographic extent, resistance status, concomitant immunomodulation, and major comorbidities. Missing data were handled by complete-case analysis; patients with missing exposure timing, primary outcome ascertainment, or prespecified covariate data were excluded before matching. All data were cross-validated by two independent reviewers to ensure abstraction reliability.

Propensity score matching

To address baseline imbalances and reduce confounding by indication, 1:1 propensity score matching (PSM) was performed using a logistic regression model. The propensity model was constructed to capture variables likely to influence treatment timing and prognosis, rather than only variables previously validated as standalone prognostic markers. Prespecified covariates were age, sex, body mass index, fever duration before admission, admission temperature, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, white blood cell count, neutrophil proportion, CRP, LDH, ALT, AST, N-terminal pro-BNP, bilateral lung involvement, pleural effusion, macrolide resistance status, IVIG use, and baseline comorbidities. Oxygen therapy, ICU admission, and antibiotic escalation were not included in the propensity model because they were study outcomes or could occur after treatment assignment. Matching was implemented by nearest-neighbor method with a caliper width of 0.2 standard deviations of the logit of the propensity score, without replacement (Fig. 1).

Flowchart of patient selection and study cohort formation

Post-matching balance was evaluated using standardized mean differences (SMD), with a threshold of < 0.1 indicating adequate covariate balance. Covariate balance was summarized using Love plots (Fig. 2), and propensity score distributions were visually inspected to assess common support. Of 482 early initiators and 420 late initiators in the unmatched cohort, 299 patients from each group were retained after matching; therefore, 183 early initiators (38.0%) and 121 late initiators (28.8%) were discarded because no suitable match was available. The retained matched sample was considered acceptable for comparative analyses, although some loss of generalizability is unavoidable.

Ethical approval

The current study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Funan County People’s Hospital (approval number FPH202511135). All patient data were anonymized prior to analysis, and no identifiable personal information was retained. Due to the retrospective nature of the study and minimal risk to subjects, the requirement for informed consent was waived. The study conformed to the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and followed institutional data governance regulations.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and proportions and compared using Chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests, as appropriate. Continuous variables were assessed for distributional normality; non-normally distributed variables were presented as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs) and compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. Adjusted ORs with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were estimated using multivariable logistic regression models, controlling for prespecified confounders identified a priori.

Subgroup analyses were conducted to evaluate effect heterogeneity across predefined strata including age (< 6 vs. ≥6 years), LDH level, macrolide resistance status, and bilateral lung involvement. Interaction terms were tested within logistic models to assess statistical significance (P for interaction). For the exploratory therapeutic-window analysis, timing was examined both categorically (3–5, 6–7, and > 7 days) and continuously by day of corticosteroid initiation, so that a graded timing-response association in outcome risk could be evaluated (P for trend). Because the matched cohort contained a limited number of outcome events, additional post hoc subgroup analyses based on CRP or neutrophil proportion were not performed in order to avoid unstable estimates. All analyses were conducted using R (version 4.5.0), and two-tailed P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics and covariate balance before and after matching

A total of 1,203 pediatric patients hospitalized with Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia between January 1, 2023 and August 31, 2025 were retrospectively screened. After application of the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, 902 eligible patients remained in the analytic cohort. All included patients had received macrolide-based first-line antibiotics before corticosteroid initiation. Of these patients, 482 (53.4%) received corticosteroids on days 3–7 after symptom onset (early initiation group), whereas 420 (46.6%) initiated corticosteroids after day 7 (late initiation group). Propensity score matching yielded 299 well-balanced pairs, retaining 62.0% of early initiators and 71.2% of late initiators for the matched analysis.

Before matching, several clinically and statistically significant differences were present between the early and late initiation groups (Table 1). Patients in the early group had higher median CRP levels (80 vs. 67 mg/L, P < 0.001), higher LDH levels (450 vs. 390 U/L, P < 0.001), a greater neutrophil proportion (70% vs. 67%, P < 0.001), shorter fever duration before admission (5 vs. 6 days, P < 0.001), and more frequent bilateral pulmonary involvement (41% vs. 33%, P = 0.01). The proportion of macrolide-resistant M. pneumoniae was high in both groups (87% vs. 89%, P = 0.29). After matching, all SMD values were < 0.1, indicating adequate covariate balance (Fig. 2). Importantly, baseline severity surrogates available at admission—including oxygen saturation, respiratory rate, bilateral lung involvement, and pleural effusion—were also well balanced in the matched cohort.

Comparison of in-hospital outcomes between early and late corticosteroid initiation

After propensity score matching, early corticosteroid initiation (within 3–7 days of symptom onset) was associated with more favorable in-hospital outcomes than late initiation (> 7 days) (Table 2; Fig. 3A). The incidence of the severe composite outcome was lower in the early group than in the late group (12.0% vs. 19.4%; OR 0.57, 95% CI 0.36–0.89; P = 0.018). Among secondary outcomes, early initiation was also associated with lower rates of antibiotic escalation (9.4% vs. 15.7%; OR 0.56, 95% CI 0.34–0.91; P = 0.019) and oxygen therapy (8.7% vs. 13.7%; OR 0.59, 95% CI 0.35–0.99; P = 0.046). Differences in ICU admission (1.3% vs. 3.0%; P = 0.151) and pleural effusion (3.7% vs. 6.4%; P = 0.132) were directionally similar but not statistically significant.

In addition to lower complication rates, early corticosteroid initiation was associated with a shorter clinical course. Fever after corticosteroid initiation lasted a median of 3 days (IQR 2–4) in the early group versus 4 days (IQR 3–5) in the late group (P < 0.001). Likewise, length of hospital stay was shorter in the early group (median 7 days, IQR 6–9) than in the late group (median 9 days, IQR 7–11; P < 0.001) (Fig. 3B).

Subgroup analyses of early corticosteroid initiation

To assess the consistency of the treatment association across clinically relevant strata, subgroup analyses were performed according to age, macrolide resistance status, baseline LDH level, and bilateral lung involvement. The overall association between early corticosteroid initiation and lower odds of severe outcomes remained directionally consistent across most subgroups (overall OR 0.57, 95% CI 0.36–0.89) (Fig. 4).

Subgroup analyses of early corticosteroid initiation versus late initiation for risk of severe in-hospital outcomes

In age-based stratification, the ORs were 0.58 (95% CI 0.32–1.05) for patients < 6 years and 0.56 (95% CI 0.29–1.08) for those ≥ 6 years, with no significant interaction (P for interaction = 0.86). Among patients with macrolide-resistant Mycoplasma pneumoniae, early treatment was associated with lower odds of severe outcomes (OR 0.55, 95% CI 0.35–0.88), whereas estimates were imprecise in the macrolide-sensitive subgroup (OR 1.05, 95% CI 0.14–7.80), likely because of very low event counts; no statistical interaction was observed (P = 0.52).

A similar pattern was observed in the subgroup stratified by LDH. In patients with elevated LDH (≥ 400 U/L), early corticosteroid use was associated with lower odds of severe outcomes (OR 0.54, 95% CI 0.31–0.93), whereas the estimate in children with LDH < 400 U/L was weaker and not statistically significant (OR 0.63, 95% CI 0.30–1.34), with no significant interaction (P = 0.12). Bilateral lung involvement likewise did not significantly modify the association (P = 0.35), although the point estimate favored early initiation more strongly in children with bilateral disease (OR 0.48, 95% CI 0.25–0.90) than in those without bilateral involvement (OR 0.67, 95% CI 0.36–1.26). These subgroup analyses should be interpreted as exploratory.

Stratified analysis by initiation timing suggests a therapeutic window at 3–5 days after symptom onset

To further explore the therapeutic window for corticosteroid initiation, we stratified the early group into two subgroups: optimal early initiation (3–5 days) and standard early initiation (6–7 days). Late initiation (> 7 days) served as the reference group. Among the 598 patients included in this analysis, 155 (25.9%) received corticosteroids at 3–5 days, 144 (24.1%) at 6–7 days, and 299 (50.0%) at > 7 days (Table 4). The incidence of severe in-hospital outcomes increased with delayed corticosteroid initiation: 12/155 (7.7%) in the 3–5 day group, 24/144 (16.7%) in the 6–7 day group, and 58/299 (19.4%) in the late group. After adjustment for age, macrolide resistance, baseline LDH level, and bilateral lung involvement, only the 3–5 day group showed significantly lower odds of severe outcomes compared with late initiation (OR 0.35, 95% CI 0.18–0.68), whereas the 6–7 day group did not (OR 0.83, 95% CI 0.48–1.43). Continuous modeling of initiation day also supported a graded association between later treatment and worse outcomes (P for trend = 0.021) (Fig. 5).

Trend analysis of corticosteroid initiation timing and severe composite outcomes

Hospital stay duration followed a similar pattern. Median length of hospitalization was 6 days (IQR 5–8) in the 3–5 day group, 7 days (IQR 6–9) in the 6–7 day group, and 9 days (IQR 7–11) in the late group. The timing-response trend for length of stay was statistically significant (P for trend = 0.003) (Table 4). Together, these exploratory findings suggest that the clearest association with improved short-term outcomes was observed when corticosteroids were initiated on days 3–5 after symptom onset.

Discussion

In this propensity score-matched cohort of hospitalized children who all received systemic corticosteroids for MPP, earlier initiation was associated with a more favorable in-hospital course than later initiation. However, the exploratory timing analysis indicates that this association was driven primarily by children who started corticosteroids on days 3–5 after symptom onset; initiation on days 6–7 was not clearly associated with lower odds of severe outcomes compared with later treatment. Accordingly, our data support a narrow early-treatment window rather than a uniform benefit across the entire 3–7 day interval.

This pattern is biologically plausible. In pediatric MPP, worsening clinical manifestations are thought to reflect not only microbial burden but also an evolving host inflammatory response [16–18]. Corticosteroids may be most effective when introduced during the period in which immune-mediated lung injury is becoming clinically apparent but has not yet fully progressed. Initiation too late in the disease course may miss this modifiable inflammatory window, whereas initiation on days 3–5 after symptom onset may better coincide with the stage at which immunomodulation can still meaningfully influence short-term outcomes [11, 19, 20].

The subgroup analyses provide cautious supportive context rather than definitive effect modification. Directionally favorable estimates were seen across age strata and in children with elevated LDH or bilateral lung involvement, findings that are broadly consistent with the idea that earlier immunomodulation may be particularly relevant in patients with greater inflammatory burden [7, 21–23]. However, no formal interaction was statistically significant, and the small number of events in some strata—especially the macrolide-sensitive subgroup—limits interpretability. These results should therefore be considered hypothesis-generating.

The clinical implications should be interpreted within the context of local practice. During the study period, management at our center was informed by contemporary Chinese pediatric expert guidance, but there was no rigid protocol mandating a specific corticosteroid start day; initiation remained a physician-level decision based on persistent fever, hypoxemia, radiographic progression, inflammatory markers, and overall clinical trajectory [14]. Our findings therefore reflect real-world decision-making and suggest that, among children already selected to receive corticosteroids, consideration of treatment on days 3–5 may be associated with the most favorable short-term course. They should not be interpreted as evidence that all hospitalized children with MPP require corticosteroids.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, as a retrospective single-center study, residual confounding and confounding by indication remain possible despite propensity score matching. Second, because the study was restricted to children who received corticosteroids, it does not estimate the effect of corticosteroids versus no corticosteroids in unselected MPP, but rather the association between earlier and later initiation within the treated population. Third, time-dependent bias cannot be fully excluded because treatment groups were defined by initiation time after symptom onset, and children classified into the late group necessarily remained hospitalized long enough to receive later treatment; accordingly, these results should not be interpreted causally. Fourth, duration of fever before admission was included as a prespecified pre-treatment covariate because it may influence clinician timing decisions, but adjustment for this variable could introduce collider bias. Fifth, no validated pediatric MPP severity score was routinely documented in our electronic medical record; although we incorporated available admission severity surrogates such as oxygen saturation, respiratory rate, bilateral lung involvement, and pleural effusion, unmeasured differences in baseline severity may persist. Finally, missing data were handled by complete-case analysis, and the high regional prevalence of macrolide resistance may limit generalizability to settings with different resistance epidemiology.

In conclusion, among corticosteroid-treated hospitalized children with MPP, initiation on days 3–5 after symptom onset was associated with the most favorable in-hospital outcomes, whereas initiation on days 6–7 was not clearly different from later treatment. These findings support the hypothesis of a narrow early therapeutic window and warrant prospective validation before informing causal or practice-changing recommendations.

Conclusion

In this propensity score-matched cohort study of corticosteroid-treated hospitalized children with Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia, initiation on days 3–5 after symptom onset was associated with lower odds of severe in-hospital outcomes and shorter hospitalization than later initiation. Because residual confounding, time-dependent bias, and collider bias cannot be exclude.

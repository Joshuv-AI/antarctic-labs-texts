A Rule-Aware Prompt Framework for Structured Numeric Reasoning in Cyber-Physical Systems
Yichen Liu, Hongyu Wu, Bo Liu

Abstract
Many cyber-physical systems (CPS) rely on high-dimensional numeric telemetry and explicit operating rules to maintain safe and efficient operation. Recent large language models (LLMs) are increasingly considered as decision-support components in such systems, yet most deployments focus on textual inputs and do not directly address rule-grounded reasoning over numeric measurements. This paper proposes a rule-aware prompt framework that systematically encodes CPS domain context, numeric normalization, and decision rules into a modular prompt architecture for LLMs. The framework decomposes prompts into five reusable modules, including role specification, CPS domain context, numeric normalization, rule-aware reasoning, and output schema, and exposes an interface for plugging in diverse rule sets. A key design element is separating rule specification from the representation of normalized numeric deviations, which enables concise prompts that remain aligned with domain rules. We analyze how different normalization strategies and prompt configurations influence rule adherence, interpretability, and token efficiency. The framework is model-agnostic and applicable across CPS domains. To illustrate its behavior, we instantiate it on numeric anomaly assessment in an IEEE 118-bus electric power transmission network and evaluate several prompting and adaptation regimes. The results show that rule-aware, z-score-based value blocks and a hybrid LLM-detector architecture can substantially improve consistency with CPS rules and anomaly detection performance while reducing token usage, providing a reusable bridge between numeric telemetry and general-purpose LLMs.

Introduction

Cyber-physical systems (CPS), including electric power grids, industrial automation, and transportation networks, are engineered around structured numeric telemetry and explicit operating rules. Measurements such as voltages, flows, temperatures, and pressures are collected at high frequency from heterogeneous sensors and are interpreted against statistical limits, safety envelopes, and physical invariants. Extracting actionable decisions from this data typically relies on application-specific models and carefully tuned pipelines, which can be difficult to adapt as system conditions and sensing infrastructure evolve.

In parallel, large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated strong capabilities in natural language understanding, code generation, and tool-augmented decision support
MinaeeLLM2025
;
HanPretrained2021
;
HuynhCodeGen2025
. Built primarily on the Transformer architecture
Vaswani2017
, these models can support diverse tasks through prompting, in-context learning, and lightweight fine-tuning. Recent surveys and position papers highlight the emerging role of LLMs as agentic decision-support tools across domains
PlaatAgentic2025
. In electric power systems, for example, LLMs are being explored for system analysis, market operations, and operator support
ZhangPowerAgent2024
;
MajumderEnergyLLM2024
.

However, most existing CPS applications remain predominantly text-centric, relying on logs, alerts, or operator messages rather than directly operating on high-dimensional numeric telemetry governed by explicit rules. When numeric data are naively serialized as tokens, LLM behavior can become sensitive to representation choices and may drift away from the underlying decision rules, even if those rules are available to the model in textual form. This is particularly problematic in safety-critical CPS domains, where decisions must remain traceable to well-understood statistical or physical criteria
ZaboliADGrid2024
;
XuLRMSurvey2025
.

A central challenge is therefore:
How should prompts be structured so that LLMs can reason over numeric CPS telemetry in a way that is faithful to domain rules, interpretable to operators, and efficient in token usage?
Addressing this challenge requires more than selecting a particular backbone model or tuning strategy. It calls for a systematic way to encode sensor semantics, normalization, and rule logic into prompts that can be reused across CPS tasks and domains.

Our earlier work investigated the performance of LLMs on numeric anomaly detection (AD) in a small power-system test case under several prompting and adaptation regimes, using a hand-crafted prompt template
LiuAD2025
. That study focused on benchmarking detection performance and understanding the trade-offs among zero-shot prompting, in-context learning, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, and hybrid LLM-deep-learning detectors. In contrast, this paper takes a
framework-oriented
perspective: we aim to design and analyze a general prompt architecture for numeric reasoning in CPS, with anomaly assessment as one illustrative task rather than the primary contribution.

In this work, we propose a
rule-aware prompt framework
for structured numeric reasoning in CPS. Instead of treating numeric telemetry as generic tokens, the framework encodes domain context, measurement semantics, and decision rules into a modular prompt architecture. A key design choice is the explicit separation of (i) a module that specifies how raw measurements are normalized into descriptors suitable for rule-based reasoning and (ii) a module that encodes the rules themselves. This separation enables concise value blocks that are tightly aligned with domain rules while remaining agnostic to the choice of LLM backbone.

Concretely, we represent each prompt as

P=R\oplus C\oplus N\oplus S\oplus V\oplus O,

where
R
specifies the model’s role,
C
the CPS domain context,
N
the numeric normalization strategy,
S
the rule-aware reasoning instructions,
V
the value block containing normalized telemetry, and
O
the output schema. While we instantiate
S
with a standard three-sigma statistical rule in our case study, the design is intended to accommodate richer CPS rule sets without changing the overall architecture.

To demonstrate how this framework behaves in practice, we apply it to numeric anomaly assessment in an electric transmission network, using GPT-OSS-20B as a representative open-weight LLM. Within this case study, we examine how different normalization strategies and supervision levels (e.g., zero-shot prompts, few-shot and in-context learning, lightweight adaptation, and hybrid integration with traditional detectors) interact with the framework to affect rule adherence, interpretability, and token efficiency. The case study is not positioned as an exhaustive benchmark for anomaly detection, but as a concrete instantiation that reveals the impact of prompt design choices in a realistic CPS setting.

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

•

We propose a modular, rule-aware prompt framework that decomposes CPS prompts into role, context, normalization, rule-reasoning, value-block, and output-schema modules, enabling reusable designs for numeric reasoning across CPS tasks and domains.

•

We provide a principled treatment of numeric normalization and rule encoding, formalizing how normalized descriptors and explicit rule modules can be combined so that LLMs operate on deviations that are directly aligned with domain rules instead of raw measurements.

•

Using a power-system case study, we analyze how different normalization strategies and levels of supervision influence rule adherence, interpretability, and token efficiency under the framework.

•

We present a reusable template for integrating LLMs into CPS analytics, separating domain-specific rules and sensor semantics from generic prompt structure so that different backbone models and adaptation techniques can be plugged in with minimal changes.

Background and Related Work

2.1
Large Language Models and Prompt Engineering

Modern LLMs are typically built on the Transformer architecture
Vaswani2017
, which uses self-attention mechanisms to capture long-range dependencies in sequences. Pretrained on large text corpora, these models can support diverse tasks through prompting, in-context learning, and lightweight fine-tuning
MinaeeLLM2025
;
HanPretrained2021
. Surveys and tutorials summarize their capabilities across natural language, code generation, and decision support
HuynhCodeGen2025
;
PlaatAgentic2025
.

Prompt engineering has emerged as a practical strategy to steer LLM behavior without modifying model parameters, by carefully designing instructions, exemplars, and input formats
AgarwalPromptWizard2024
. Early efforts primarily focused on natural language tasks such as question answering and summarization. More recent work explores structured tasks, including table question answering, tool use, and planning, often relying on zero-shot, few-shot, and in-context learning paradigms
XianZeroShot2019
;
ParnamiFewShot2022
;
DongICL2024
. Parameter-efficient adaptation methods such as LoRA
HuLoRA2021
further extend the design space by allowing inexpensive tuning on downstream tasks.

Despite these advances, numeric reasoning remains a challenging capability. LLMs can exhibit sensitivity to numeric formats, rounding, and ordering, and may produce outputs that are fluent but not numerically grounded
XuLRMSurvey2025
. This motivates designs that expose explicit rules or constraints within prompts, to guide the model toward rule-consistent outputs.

2.2
Numeric Telemetry and Anomaly Detection in CPS

CPS telemetry often follows well-understood physical or statistical patterns. For example, measurement distributions may be approximately stationary around nominal operating points, with deviations governed by known error characteristics or fault signatures. Traditional approaches rely on statistical process control, hypothesis testing, and model-based residual analysis to detect deviations and support decision making. These methods typically use explicit thresholds or confidence intervals that are directly interpretable by domain experts.

In electric power systems, numeric AD on multivariate telemetry (e.g., bus voltages, nodal power injections, and line flows) plays a key role in maintaining stability and resilience. Recent works have surveyed the potential of LLMs in the electric energy sector, emphasizing high-level decision support, integration with simulation tools, and orchestration frameworks
ZhangPowerAgent2024
;
MajumderEnergyLLM2024
. Other studies have begun to explore generative-AI-based anomaly detection in grid communication and monitoring
ZaboliADGrid2024
.

While these efforts highlight opportunities for LLMs in the power sector, they largely focus on textual or message-level inputs. The application of LLMs to structured numeric telemetry remains limited. Liu
et al.
present a comprehensive evaluation of GPT-OSS-20B for numeric AD on an IEEE 14-bus system, using a standardized prompt and multiple prompting and adaptation strategies
LiuAD2025
. That study demonstrates that LLMs can achieve competitive detection performance under carefully designed prompts, but it does not systematically analyze the structure of the prompt or the role of numeric normalization.

2.3
Rule-Grounded LLM Reasoning

Recent studies have begun to investigate how LLMs respond to prompts that encode symbolic rules, logical constraints, or mathematical procedures
XuLRMSurvey2025
. Techniques such as chain-of-thought prompting, self-consistency, and tool-augmented reasoning have improved performance on arithmetic and logic benchmarks. Prompt optimization frameworks
AgarwalPromptWizard2024
and surveys on large reasoning models
XuLRMSurvey2025
emphasize the importance of explicit guidance for controlling model behavior.

However, relatively few works have focused on high-dimensional numeric telemetry in CPS, where many variables and rules must be considered simultaneously. Our work contributes to this line of research by designing a modular, rule-aware prompt framework tailored to CPS numeric telemetry, and by validating it on a concrete AD task in a power system. The framework leverages a standard statistical rule, i.e., the three-sigma criterion, as an anchor for interpretability and consistency, while still allowing the LLM to provide natural-language rationales. The power-system case study illustrates how such a framework behaves in practice, building on the evaluation setup in
LiuAD2025
but emphasizing the design and impact of the prompt architecture itself.

3
Rule-Aware Prompt Framework

This section presents the proposed prompt framework for structured numeric reasoning in CPS. The design goal is to transform multivariate numeric telemetry into a textual format that (i) exposes domain semantics, (ii) encodes explicit decision rules, and (iii) maintains a concise and regular structure amenable to LLM inference.

3.1
Design Objectives

We consider the following design objectives:

1.

Modularity:
The prompt should be composed of reusable modules (e.g., role, context, rule, values, output schema) that can be combined and adapted across CPS applications.

2.

Rule Awareness:
Domain rules, such as statistical thresholds or physical limits, should be explicitly stated and referenced in the reasoning process.

3.

Numeric Normalization:
High-dimensional numeric inputs should be normalized or summarized to reduce token redundancy while retaining essential information.

4.

Output Structure:
The desired output format should be clearly specified to support parseable, machine-actionable responses.

5.

Generalizability:
The framework should require minimal changes to apply to different CPS domains and sensor sets, beyond updating the domain context and value block definition.

3.2
Modular Architecture

The proposed framework decomposes a prompt into five modules:

•

Role Instruction Module (R):
Defines the model’s role and high-level task, e.g., “You are a CPS analyst. Your task is to assess whether the following numeric telemetry is consistent with normal operation according to a given statistical rule.”

•

Domain Context Module (C):
Provides a concise description of the CPS domain, measurement types, and feature semantics. This helps the LLM associate numeric quantities with physical meaning (e.g., bus voltage magnitudes or line power flows in a power grid).

•

Numeric Normalization Module (N):
Specifies how raw measurements are transformed into normalized quantities, such as z-scores, to reduce scale sensitivity and token redundancy.

•

Rule-Aware Reasoning Module (S):
Encodes the decision rule (e.g., three-sigma criterion) and the required reasoning steps. The LLM is instructed to apply the rule explicitly when forming its judgment.

•

Output Schema Module (O):
Constrains the response to a small number of fields, such as a categorical label and a short explanation, for downstream use.

A generic prompt instance can be represented as

P=R\oplus C\oplus N\oplus S\oplus V\oplus O,

(1)

where
V
denotes the value block containing the numeric telemetry representation, and
\oplus
denotes concatenation. In the case study,
R
,
C
,
S
, and
O
are held fixed across experiments, while
N
and the design of
V
are varied to assess the impact of different numeric representations.

3.3
Three-Sigma Statistical Rule

To provide a concrete and interpretable rule for numeric reasoning, we use the three-sigma criterion. Let
x_{i}
denote the value of the
i
-th measurement, and let
\mu_{i}
and
\sigma_{i}
denote its nominal mean and standard deviation estimated from historical data. The normalized z-score is

z_{i}=\frac{x_{i}-\mu_{i}}{\max(\sigma_{i},\epsilon)},

(2)

where
\epsilon
is a small positive constant to avoid division by zero. A simple rule for flagging deviations is

\text{flag}_{i}=\begin{cases}1,&|z_{i}|\geq\tau,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(3)

where
\tau
is a threshold, commonly set to
3.0
in three-sigma control charts.

In the prompt, we encode the rule in natural language and provide normalized values (e.g., absolute z-scores) in the value block. The LLM is instructed to determine whether the overall sample should be considered nominal or abnormal based on the rule, and to briefly justify its decision. While the three-sigma rule is convenient and well known, it may be less appropriate for strongly non-Gaussian or heavy-tailed distributions; in such cases, alternative rule modules could be substituted without changing the overall framework.

3.4
Value Block Representation

The value block
V
presents the numeric telemetry in a tabular or list format. To manage token length, we focus on concise numeric descriptors and identifiers. In the case study, we consider several variants:

•

raw values only;

•

raw values plus means and standard deviations (std);

•

raw values, means, std, and absolute z-scores;

•

absolute z-scores only.

All variants share the same role, context, and natural-language description of the three-sigma rule; only the numeric representation changes. The
z-score only
design corresponds directly to the normalized deviation representation emphasized by the framework, and is the configuration used in all non-ablation experiments.

For example, the z-score only value block at a specific time instance can be written as:

Sensor 1: abs_z = 0.8
Sensor 2: abs_z = 1.2
...
Sensor 255: abs_z = 3.5

This representation omits raw values and statistics, relying instead on normalized deviations that are directly aligned with the decision rule. In our experiments, this reduction in numeric verbosity significantly decreases token count while preserving the information required for rule-based reasoning.

3.5
Output Schema

To facilitate consistent and machine-actionable responses, the output schema module
O
prescribes a simple format:

1.

A single-word label indicating the overall assessment, such as “normal” or “anomaly”.

2.

A short explanation summarizing which measurements, if any, exceeded the threshold and why the rule implies the chosen label.

By constraining the output, we reduce variability and simplify downstream parsing, while still allowing the model to provide interpretable rationales. In the case study, the explanation is not directly used for classification but is useful for assessing whether the model’s reasoning appears aligned with the three-sigma rule.

4
Experimental Setup

This section instantiates the proposed rule-aware prompt framework on a numeric anomaly detection task in a representative CPS, namely an AC power network. The goal is to assess how the modular prompt design, and in particular the three-sigma rule and normalized value block, affect anomaly detection performance, rule adherence, and token efficiency.

4.1
CPS Case Study and Dataset

We consider the IEEE 118-bus AC power system as a representative CPS, following the setup in
LiuAD2025
. The system is instrumented with sensors measuring nodal active and reactive power injections, line power flows, and bus voltage magnitudes. Each telemetry snapshot is represented as a 255-dimensional vector of real-valued measurements, corresponding to

•

active/reactive power injections
(P,Q)
at buses,

•

active/reactive line flows
(P_{f},Q_{f})
on transmission lines, and

•

voltage magnitudes
V
at buses.

Historical operating data are generated by mapping hourly load profiles from the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT) to the system’s load buses
ERCOT
;
ACPFData
and solving AC power flow for each operating point. A subset of snapshots is treated as nominal and used to estimate the mean
\mu_{i}
and std
\sigma_{i}
for each measurement. New samples are then constructed by combining nominal operating points with synthetic perturbations: anomalous states are created by injecting a
15\%
deviation into three randomly selected sensor values in each snapshot
LiuAD2025
. Each sample is labeled as nominal or anomalous according to the three-sigma criterion introduced in Section
3
: a sample is labeled anomalous if at least one measurement satisfies
|z_{i}|\geq 3.0
and nominal otherwise. In this setup, the ground-truth labels coincide with the rule outcome, so classification accuracy directly reflects rule adherence. For experiments that require supervised training (e.g., LoRA fine-tuning), we use a balanced subset containing
600
nominal and
600
anomalous samples for training. An additional
400
labeled samples are evenly split into validation and test sets, consistent with
LiuAD2025
. Unless otherwise specified, all reported metrics are averaged over the held-out test set.

4.2
LLM Backbone and Prompting Modes

The framework is instantiated on GPT-OSS-20B, an open-weight, decoder-only Transformer language model with approximately
24
billion parameters
GPTOSS20B
. GPT-OSS-20B is pretrained using an autoregressive language modeling objective on a large-scale multilingual and domain-diverse corpus and is publicly available via the Hugging Face platform. This choice allows us to evaluate LLM performance on structured numeric AD in power systems under realistic resource constraints. All experiments are conducted on a workstation equipped with two NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada GPUs with 48 GB each.

We evaluate the prompt framework under five configurations that represent increasing levels of supervision and adaptation, mirroring the regimes considered in
LiuAD2025
:

•

Zero-shot:
The model receives a single telemetry sample and the rule-aware prompt, but no labeled examples, following the classical notion of zero-shot learning
XianZeroShot2019
.

•

Few-shot:
The prompt includes two labeled exemplars (one nominal, one anomalous) before the test sample, providing minimal supervision in the prompt
ParnamiFewShot2022
.

•

In-context learning (ICL):
The prompt includes five diverse labeled examples that cover different anomaly patterns, exploiting the model’s ability to infer tasks from in-context examples
DongICL2024
.

•

LoRA fine-tuning:
Parameter-efficient low-rank adaptation is applied to the attention projections, supervising the model to generate both the anomaly label and a brief rationale
HuLoRA2021
.

•

Hybrid LLM + detector:
The LLM is used as a rule-aware feature selector and explanation engine; its filtered features are then passed to a traditional deep learning (DL) anomaly detector for the final decision
LiuAD2025
.

In all configurations, the same modular prompt architecture
P=R\oplus C\oplus N\oplus S\oplus V\oplus O
is used, as described in Section
3
. The role instruction (
R
), domain context (
C
), and rule-aware reasoning module (
S
) are held fixed. The primary experimental degrees of freedom are (i) the content of the numeric value block
V
and (ii) the level of supervision (zero-/few-shot, ICL, LoRA, hybrid).

4.3
Value Block Variants and Evaluation Metrics

To study the effect of the numeric normalization module and the amount of statistical context exposed to the model, we consider four value-block designs:

1.

Setup 1 (Value only):
Each sensor is represented only by its raw value.

2.

Setup 2 (Mean + std + value):
Each row contains the raw value together with its nominal mean and standard deviation.

3.

Setup 3 (Mean + std + value + z-score):
Each row additionally includes the computed z-score.

4.

Setup 4 (Z-score only - proposed):
Each sensor is represented only by its absolute z-score
\lvert z_{i}\rvert
, as in the framework description in Section
3
.

All four variants retain the same role, context, and natural-language description of the three-sigma rule; only the numeric representation in
V
changes. The
Z-score only
variant corresponds exactly to the rule-aware normalized value block described in Section
3
, and is the default choice for all non-ablation experiments.

We report standard classification metrics: accuracy (overall correctness), recall (fraction of anomalies correctly detected), precision (fraction of predicted anomalies that are correct), and F1-score (harmonic mean of precision and recall). Because labels are derived directly from the three-sigma rule, these metrics simultaneously capture detection performance and adherence to the encoded rule. In addition, we qualitatively assess token efficiency by comparing prompt compactness across value-block designs.

6
Conclusion and Future Work

This paper presented a rule-aware prompting framework for structured numeric reasoning in cyber-physical systems. The framework decomposes prompts into modular components, including role specification, domain context, numeric normalization, rule-aware logic, value block, and output schema, so that large language models can process high-dimensional telemetry under explicit decision rules and generate machine-actionable outputs. We instantiated the framework on anomaly detection in an IEEE 118-bus power system with a 255-dimensional telemetry vector, using GPT-OSS-20B as the backbone model.

The experiments highlight three main findings. First, the design of the numeric value block is crucial: compared with a raw-value representation, the proposed z-score-only design substantially improves zero-shot anomaly detection performance, raising the F1-score from roughly
50\%
to almost
78\%
while keeping the prompt compact. This confirms that aligning the numeric representation with the underlying three-sigma rule is more effective than simply exposing more unstructured measurements. Second, with the z-score-only value block fixed, progressively stronger prompting and adaptation strategies (few-shot, in-context learning, and LoRA fine-tuning) further enhance performance, with LoRA achieving very high recall at the cost of additional complexity. Third, a hybrid architecture that combines rule-aware LLM reasoning with a traditional deep-learning detector achieves the best overall trade-off, reaching an F1-score above
90\%
with perfect precision in our case study, which illustrates the complementarity between LLM-based reasoning and established CPS analytics. These results suggest that clarity and rule alignment in prompt and input design, rather than sheer input volume, are key to scalable and interpretable LLM-based CPS applications. The modular framework also supports reuse: practitioners can instantiate a shared template with task-specific context and rules, and then plug in different backbone models or supervision levels as needed. Future work will incorporate additional types of domain rules such as physical invariants and logical constraints, and explore applications beyond power systems, including industrial process control and transportation.

An Egorov Theorem for Wasserstein Distances
Jordan Cotler, Felipe Hernández

Abstract
. We prove a new version of Egorov’s theorem formulated in the Schrödinger picture of quantum mechanics, using the p p -Wasserstein metric applied to the Husimi functions of quantum states. The special case p = 1 p=1 corresponds to a “low-regularity” Egorov theorem, while larger values p > 1 p>1 yield progressively stronger estimates. As a byproduct of our analysis, we prove an optimal transport inequality analogous to the main result of Golse and Paul [ GP17 ] in the context of mean-field many-body quantum mechanics.



1.
Introduction

Our universe obeys the laws of quantum mechanics, although our everyday experience portrays a classical world obeying Newtonian laws. Egorov’s theorem forms a mathematical bridge between these two descriptions by demonstrating that quantum observables evolving by the Schrödinger equation are well-approximated by observables evolving by
classical equations of motion.
More precisely, Egorov’s theorem (see
[
HJ00
,
SB02
,
BR02
]
, also the textbook reference
[
Zwo12
, Theorem 11.1]
) states that for sufficiently smooth observable functions

a_{0}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2D})
, one has

e^{i\hat{H}T/\hbar}\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(a_{0})e^{-i\hat{H}T/\hbar}=\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(a_{T})+O(e^{CT}\hbar)\,,

where
\hat{H}=\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(H)
is the Weyl quantization of a smooth Hamiltonian
H\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2D})
and
a_{t}

solves Hamilton’s equation

\partial_{t}a=\{H,a\}_{\rm PB}\,,

where
\{f,g\}_{\rm PB}
is the Poisson bracket.

This quantum-classical correspondence naturally aligns with the Heisenberg picture, which focuses on the evolution of
observables
rather than
states
as in the Schrödinger picture. In this paper we prove a new version of Egorov’s theorem that uses optimal transport metrics to compare the evolution of quantum and classical states.

1.1
.

Let
H\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2D})
be an admissible Hamiltonian as in Definition
2.2
.
For any
1\leq p<\infty
there exists a constant
C_{p}
, depending only on
p
,
D
and
H
, such that for all density matrices
\rho
and all
T\geq 0
,

(1.1)

d_{W_{p}}\big({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\rho}\,,\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho}\big)\leq e^{C_{p}T}\,\hbar^{1/2},

where
{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho}
is the Husimi function defined in (
2.6
),
W_{p}
is the

p
-Wasserstein metric,
\mathcal{U}_{T}\rho:=e^{-i\hat{H}T/\hbar}\rho\,e^{i\hat{H}T/\hbar}
is the quantum evolution of
\rho
, and
\Phi_{T}
is the classical Hamiltonian flow map.

This theorem articulates that, after coarse–graining at the natural phase space scale
\sqrt{\hbar}
(the Husimi smoothing), a quantum state evolves almost classically. Theorem
1.1
is remarkable in that it holds for
arbitrary
quantum states
\rho
, including those lacking good semiclassical descriptions (such as mixtures of coherent states).

To understand the meaning of Theorem
1.1
, it is helpful to unpack it in the case
p=1
.
Invoking Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality yields

\left|\int\varphi\,{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\rho}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha-\int\varphi\,\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\right|\leq\mathrm{Lip}(\varphi)\,e^{C_{1}T}\,\hbar^{1/2}\quad\text{for all $1$-Lipschitz }\varphi,

so expectation values of all Lipschitz classical observables computed from the quantum Husimi density track their classical counterparts with an
O(\sqrt{\hbar})
error.
Along these lines, letting
\mathcal{S}_{\delta}
be the standard symbol class (defined in (
2.1
) below), we have the corollary:

1.2
.

Let
G\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2D})
be an observable satisfying
\partial_{\alpha_{j}}G\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\frac{1}{2}}
for all
j
. Then for all
T\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

(1.2)

\left\|\,{\mathcal{U}}_{T}(\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(G))-\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(\Phi_{T}G)\right\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq e^{C(D,H)T}\hbar^{1/2}.

The bound (
1.2
) above is a kind of “low-regularity” Egorov theorem (since only one derivative of
G
is quantitatively controlled).
To our knowledge, a bound of this form was not previously known.

Moreover, we comment that the bounds of Theorem
1.1
are of increasing strength as
p\geq 1
is taken large. That is, simply by Hölder’s inequality and the primal formulation of the
W_{p}
distance, one has for any
p\geq 1
the inequality

(1.3)

\displaystyle d_{W_{1}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\rho},\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho})\leq d_{W_{p}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\rho},\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho}).

Thus Theorem
1.1
is
strictly stronger
than Corollary
1.2
above, although a natural dual formulation in the Heisenberg picture is not presently clear.

1.1.
Relation to work on mean-field limits

The use of optimal transport metrics to compare quantum and classical evolutions first appeared in the work of Golse and Paul
[
GP17
]
, following the pioneering paper of Golse, Mouhot, and Paul
[
GMP16
]
that introduced quantum optimal transport metrics in many-body quantum physics. (See also
[
NS81
,
PP09
,
Gra+03
]
for earlier works on semiclassical mean field limits.)
In
[
GMP16
]
, an optimal transport metric
{\mathcal{E}}_{\hbar}(\rho,\mu)
is defined to compare quantum states to probability densities, and which is comparable (up to an additive
\hbar^{1/2}
error) to the metric
d_{W_{2}}({\mathrm{H}}_{\rho},\mu)
that we use.
The objective of
[
GP17
]
was to compare many-body quantum dynamics to many-body classical dynamics, and an optimal transport approach is necessary to obtain bounds that are uniform as the number of particles
N\to\infty
.
In our setting we take
D=Nd
where
N
is the number of particles and
d
is the spatial dimension of each particle.

Our main result, Theorem
1.1
, is unsuitable for the purpose of understanding semiclassical
mean-field limits because it does not provide dimension-independent bounds.
Nevertheless, as a byproduct of our proof we can obtain a result with quantitative bounds that is
comparable to the main result of
[
GP17
]
.

1.3
.

Let
K,V\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{D})
and
\hat{H}=K(\hat{p}_{1},...,\hat{p}_{D})+V(\hat{x}_{1},...,\hat{x}_{D})
be a Hamiltonian operator on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{D})
. Then
for states
\rho
of the form

\rho=\int p(\alpha)|\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha

where
p
is a probability density in
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2D})
and
|\alpha\rangle
is the standard coherent state, we have

\frac{1}{2D}\,d_{W_{2}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\rho},\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho})^{2}\leq(1+2e^{\Lambda T})\hbar,

where

\Lambda:=\frac{1}{2}(\|\nabla K\|_{\rm Lip}+\|\nabla V\|_{\rm Lip})\,.

For Hamiltonians of the form

\hat{H}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2m_{j}}\,\hat{\vec{p}}_{j}^{\,\,2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}V_{0}(\hat{\vec{x}}_{j})+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}V_{1}(\hat{\vec{x}}_{j}-\hat{\vec{x}}_{k})

arising in mean-field many-particle dynamics with two-particle interactions, we have that
\Lambda
is uniformly
bounded as
N\to\infty
.

Theorem
1.3
is still not directly comparable to the main result of
[
GP17
]
because it is not
adapted to the bosonic setting, and as stated it does not provide additional information about
n
-particle
marginals for
n\leq N
. Such a result can be obtained with our methods, but would take us further afield from the proof of our main result Theorem
1.1
which we take to be the focus of this paper.

Our approach to proving Theorem
1.3
is to track the phase-space localization of the evolution of
coherent states, as measured by re-centered isotropic Sobolev norms. That is, we consider the quantity

(1.4)

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{1}_{\alpha}}^{2}:=\sum_{j=1}^{2D}\|(\hat{\alpha}_{j}-\alpha_{j})\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

This quantity is the quantum analogue of the 2-Wasserstein distance between
\psi
and a fictitious state perfectly localized at
\alpha
. Indeed, for a probability measure
\mu
on phase space,

W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\delta_{\alpha})=\int|z-\alpha|^{2}\,\mu(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z)\,,

so (
1.4
) provides the operator-theoretic version of the 2-Wasserstein distance to a point mass at
\alpha
. Since the Sobolev norm (
1.4
) is particularly tractable, we can prove Theorem
1.3
via a Gronwall inequality applied to the quantity

Q(t):=\|e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\mathinner{|{\alpha_{0}}\rangle}\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{1}_{\alpha(t)}}^{2},

where
\alpha(t)=\Phi_{t}(\alpha_{0})
tracks the classical flow. This calculation is done in
Section
3
. Fundamentally, this proof does not differ much from the argument of
[
GP17
]
.
However, because the calculation is simple and leads nicely into the proof of our main result, we include it
for completeness.

1.2.
Proof outline

There are two key technical challenges addressed in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The first, more minor, challenge is that the phase space
{\mathbb{R}}^{2D}
is not compact.
In particular, one does not have a bound of the form
d_{W_{p}}({\mathrm{H}}_{\rho_{1}},{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho_{2}})\leq\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{\rm tr}
, so it does not suffice to control
{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\rho=e^{-iHT/\hbar}\rho e^{iHT/\hbar}
in trace norm. One needs instead to control the growth
of tails of
{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\rho
in phase space.
This necessitates working not with
L^{2}
but with isotropic Sobolev spaces

{\mathsf{H}}^{k}
, defined by

{\mathsf{H}}^{k}:=\{\psi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{D})\,:\,\hat{\alpha}^{k}\psi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{D})\}.

Above we use
\hat{\alpha}=(\hat{x}_{1},...,\hat{x}_{D},\hat{p}_{1},...,\hat{p}_{D})

as the phase space operator, and
\hat{\alpha}^{k}
is to be interpreted as a
tuple of possible monomials applied to
\psi
. It is useful to introduce a continuous family of equivalent norms on
{\mathsf{H}}^{k}
parametrized by the center
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2D}
,

\|\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}_{\alpha}^{k}}:=\|(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)^{k}\psi\|_{L^{2}}.

We show in Section
3
that the localized Sobolev norm of a solution to the Schrödinger equation with admissible Hamiltonian grows at most
exponentially if
\alpha(t)
tracks the classical Hamiltonian flow:

1.4
Propagation of localization.

Let
H
be an admissible Hamiltonian as in Definition
2.2
,

\hat{H}=\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(H)
, and
\alpha(T)
be a trajectory of Hamilton’s equations. Then for any
k
there exists
C=C(k,D,H)

such that for all
\psi\in{\mathsf{H}}^{k}({\mathbb{R}}^{D})
,

(1.5)

\|e^{-i\hat{H}T/\hbar}\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha(T)}}\leq e^{CT}\|\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha(0)}}.

Moreover, if
H
has the form
H=K(p_{1},...,p_{D})+V(x_{1},...,x_{D})
with
K,V\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{D})
, then one more precisely has the bound

(1.6)

\Big(\sum_{j}\|(\hat{\alpha}_{j}-\alpha_{j}(T))e^{-i\hat{H}T/\hbar}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)\leq e^{2\Lambda_{H}T}\Big(\sum_{j}\|(\hat{\alpha}_{j}-\alpha_{j}(0))\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big).

where

\Lambda_{H}:=\frac{1}{2}(\|\nabla K\|_{\rm Lip}+\|\nabla V\|_{\rm Lip}).

Note that above there is no formal connection between the trajectory
\alpha(t)

and the solution
\psi
, but in practice one obtains the best bound if
\alpha(0)

minimizes
\|\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha}}
.
As a corollary of Proposition
1.4
, we obtain a bound for initial data that are mixtures of coherent states.

1.5
.

Let
p\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2D})
be a probability density function and

\hat{\rho}=\int p(\alpha)\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha}|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha
be a density matrix that is a mixture of coherent states according to
p(\alpha)
.
Then for general admissible Hamiltonians,

(1.7)

d_{W_{p}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\rho},\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho})\leq e^{C_{p}T}\hbar^{1/2}.

If instead
\hat{H}=K(\hat{p}_{1},...,\hat{p}_{D})+V(\hat{x}_{1},...,\hat{x}_{D})
we have the bound

(1.8)

\frac{1}{2D}\,d_{W_{2}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\rho},\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho})^{2}\leq(1+2e^{\Lambda_{H}T})\hbar.

The derivation of Corollary
1.5
from Proposition
1.4
is done
in Section
3.3
. Note that Corollary
1.5
already implies Theorem
1.3
.

The second, and more serious, challenge addressed in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is that one must handle

arbitrary
states
\rho
. Indeed, in general density matrices
\rho
can encode long-range superpositions that manifest in
high-frequency oscillations in the Wigner function
{\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}
. These oscillations are not robustly encoded in the Husimi function
{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho}
, so part of the content of Theorem
1.1
is the conclusion that long-range superpositions in the initial data do not immediately affect the dynamics of the coarse-grained phase
space distribution.

To overcome this more serious difficulty we first make a small reduction. We use the noising channel
(referring the reader to Section
2.4
for the definition of the coherent state
\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}
)

(1.9)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]:=(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}\int{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho}(\alpha)\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha}|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha,

which can alternatively be described as a mixture of phase-space translations of
\rho
,

(1.10)

{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]=\int{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha}[\rho]\,\gamma_{\hbar}(\alpha)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha.

Above,
{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha}
is the phase-space translation operator defined in (
2.4
) and
\gamma_{\hbar}
is a Gaussian of variance
\hbar
.
By definition,
{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]
has the form required in Corollary
1.5
. That is, we have

d_{W_{p}}(\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]},{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\circ{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]})\leq e^{CT}\hbar^{1/2}.

By the triangle inequality, what remains is to bound

d_{W_{p}}(\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]},\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho})+d_{W_{p}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}[\rho]},{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\circ{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]}).

The first term is bounded by
e^{CT}\hbar^{1/2}
because
\Phi_{T}
is
e^{CT}
-Lipschitz.
The second term, on the other hand, is a genuinely quantum-mechanical quantity. By convexity of the
W_{p}
transport
distance and the identity (
1.10
), we have

d_{W_{p}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}[\rho]},{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\circ{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]})^{p}\leq\int\gamma_{\hbar}(\alpha)d_{W_{p}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}[\rho]},{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\circ{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha}[\rho]})^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\,.

Trivially we have
{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\circ{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha}[\rho]={\mathcal{U}}_{T}\circ{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha}\circ{\mathcal{U}}_{-T}[\,{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\rho]
. That is, we need the following bound for general density matrices
\nu
:

d_{W_{p}}({\mathrm{H}}_{\nu},{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\circ{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha}\circ{\mathcal{U}}_{-T}[\nu]})\leq e^{CT}|\alpha|\,.

Note that the operator
{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\circ{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha}\circ{\mathcal{U}}_{-T}[\nu]
has the form

{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\circ{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha}\circ{\mathcal{U}}_{-T}[\nu]=W_{T,\alpha}\,\nu\,W_{T,\alpha}^{\dagger}\,,

with

W_{T,\alpha}:=e^{i\hat{H}T/\hbar}\tau_{\alpha}e^{-i\hat{H}T/\hbar}.

As a consequence of Proposition
1.4
, this operator is well-localized in phase space in the sense that
\mathinner{\langle{\beta|W_{T,\alpha}|\beta^{\prime}}\rangle}
is rapidly decaying in
|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|
.

Then the main result will follow from the following general result:

1.6
Transport by local unitaries, informally.

Let
W
be a unitary operator that is well-localized in phase space. Then for
arbitrary states
\rho
,

d_{W_{p}}({\mathrm{H}}_{\rho},{\mathrm{H}}_{W\rho W^{\dagger}})\leq C(p,W)\hbar^{1/2}.

Proposition
1.6
is stated more precisely and proven in Section
4
.
The proof uses the Kantorovich duality
for
W_{p}
. After some simple manipulations, one can reduce Proposition
1.6
to the statement
that the operator

\mathrm{Op}^{w}(f\ast\gamma_{\hbar})+W\mathrm{Op}^{w}(g\ast\gamma_{\hbar})W^{\dagger}+C(p,W)\hbar^{1/2}

(where
\mathrm{Op}^{w}
is defined in (
4.5
)) is positive-semidefinite for
f
and
g
satisfying

f(\alpha)+g(\beta)\leq|\alpha-\beta|^{p}.

To prove this we develop a functional-analytic framework for proving positive semi-definiteness based on Lemma
4.6
, as explained in Section
4.2
.

1.3.
Organization of the paper

In Section
2
we recall notation and preliminary results from semiclassical analysis.
Our results about growth of isotropic Sobolev norms are proved in Section
3
, including the proof of Proposition
1.4
and Corollary
1.5
. Then in Section
4

1.6
. Finally in Section
5

1.1
and Corollary
1.2
.

2.
Notation and Preliminaries

This Section introduces our notation for quantum mechanics and summarizes standard semiclassical analysis results.

2.1.
Phase space notation

Points in phase space
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2D}
are represented by Greek letters. We write
\alpha=(\alpha_{x},\alpha_{p})
where

\alpha_{x},\alpha_{p}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D}
. The standard symplectic form on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2D}
is

\omega=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathrm{Id}_{D}\\
-\mathrm{Id}_{D}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Phase space variables are indexed by
a\in[2D]=\{1,...,2D\}
, with the indices
1\leq a\leq D
denoting position variables and
D+1\leq a\leq 2D
momentum variables. Accordingly, we write
\partial_{a}
for the corresponding phase space partial derivative.

The symplectic form is used to define the Poisson bracket of two functions
f,g\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2D})
:

\{f,g\}_{\text{PB}}:=\sum_{j,k}\partial_{j}f\,\omega_{jk}\,\partial_{k}g=(\nabla f)^{\intercal}\cdot\omega\cdot\nabla g\,.

2.2.
Phase space operators and moments

We denote the position operators on
\mathbb{R}^{D}
by
\hat{x}=(\hat{x}_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{D})
, where
\hat{x}_{j}
acts as multiplication by
x_{j}
. The momentum operators are
\hat{p}=(\hat{p}_{1},\ldots,\hat{p}_{D})
with
\hat{p}_{j}=-i\hbar\partial_{j}
. For convenience, we introduce the phase space operator
\hat{\alpha}=(\hat{x},\hat{p})
with components
\hat{\alpha}_{a}
indexed by
a\in[2D]
:

\hat{\alpha}_{a}:=\begin{cases}\hat{x}_{a},&1\leq a\leq D\\
\hat{p}_{a-D},&D+1\leq a\leq 2D\end{cases}.

We also need to work with moments of the phase space operators. A
monomial
of phase space operators is specified by
a tuple
\vec{m}=(m_{1},...,m_{k})\in[2D]^{k}
, and we write

\hat{\alpha}_{\vec{m}}=\hat{\alpha}_{m_{1}}\hat{\alpha}_{m_{2}}\cdots\hat{\alpha}_{m_{k}}.

Given a point in phase space
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2D}
we also use the centered monomials
(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)_{\vec{m}}

which simply replace each
\hat{\alpha}_{a}
above by
\hat{\alpha}_{a}-\alpha_{a}
.

The
order
of a monomial, written
n(\vec{m})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2D}
, is the tuple recording the number of appearances
of each index in
\vec{m}
. For example in
D=2
we have
n((1,3,4,3))=(1,0,2,1)
, since in
(1,3,4,3)
the number ‘
1
’ appears one time, ‘
2
’ appears zero times, ‘
3
’ appears two times, and ‘
4
’ appears one time. We write
|n|(\vec{m})
for the
total degree
|n|(\vec{m}):=\sum_{a}n_{a}(\vec{m})
.

The phase space operators satisfy the commutation relations

[\hat{\alpha}_{a},\hat{\alpha}_{b}]=i\hbar\omega_{ab}\,.

Commutators against phase space operators are common enough that this also has a special notation. In particular, given
a\in[2D]

we write

\mathrm{ad}_{a}[\hat{A}]:=[\hat{\alpha}_{a},\hat{A}]\,.

Given a multi-index
I\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2D}
we write the power

\mathrm{ad}^{I}=\prod_{a=1}^{2D}\mathrm{ad}_{a}^{I_{a}}\,.

Note that there is no operator ordering issue above because
\mathrm{ad}_{a}\mathrm{ad}_{b}=\mathrm{ad}_{b}\mathrm{ad}_{a}
for our phase space operators.

2.3.
Quantization and Symbol classes

Given a smooth function
A\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2D})
and
\hbar>0
we define the Weyl quantization
\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(A)
to be the operator given by the formula

\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(A)f(x)=(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}\int e^{ip\cdot(x-y)/\hbar}A(\frac{x+y}{2},p)f(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}p.

Note that with this definition,
\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(x^{k})=\hat{x}^{k}
and
\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(p^{k})=\hat{p}^{k}
. We sometimes abuse notation and
simply write
\hat{A}=\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(A)
. We make use of the identity

\mathrm{ad}_{a}[\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(A)]=i\hbar\sum_{b}\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(\omega_{ab}\,\partial_{b}A).

We will often quantize functions with certain smoothness and decay conditions, which we encapsulate by symbol classes. The standard
symbol class
{\mathcal{S}}_{\delta}
is given by

(2.1)

{\mathcal{S}}_{\delta}:=\{f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2D})\,:\,\|\partial^{j}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{j}\hbar^{-\delta j}\}.

For
0\leq\delta\leq\frac{1}{2}
, symbols in
{\mathcal{S}}_{\delta}
correspond to uniformly bounded operators by the Calderón-Vaillancourt theorem.
In particular, we use the following fact about such symbols:

2.1
.

Let
0\leq\delta\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
A\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\delta}
. Then
\hat{A}
is bounded on
L^{2}
and for any multi-index
I\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2D}
,

(2.2)

\|\mathrm{ad}^{I}[\hat{A}]\|_{\rm op}\leq C_{|I|}\hbar^{(1-\delta)|I|}.

Beals’ lemma
[
Zwo12
, Theorem 8.3]
shows that bounds of the form (
2.2
)
characterize
the symbol class
{\mathcal{S}}_{\delta}
,
but we do not use this fact.

We define admissible Hamiltonians in terms of the symbol class
{\mathcal{S}}_{\delta}
.

2.2
Admissible Hamiltonians.

A Hamiltonian
H\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2D})
is admissible
if
\partial_{ab}H\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\frac{1}{2}}
for every second order partial derivative. That is,
H
is
admissible if for every multi-index
J
of order
|J|\geq 2
,

\|\partial_{\alpha}^{J}H\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{J}\hbar^{1-|J|/2}.

2.4.
Wigner and Husimi functions

To each operator
A:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{D})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{D})
with Schwartz kernel
K_{A}
we can associate a tempered distribution
{\mathcal{W}}_{A}
(which is a bounded function when
A
is trace-class) by

{\mathcal{W}}_{A}(x,p)=(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}\int e^{ip\cdot(x-y)/\hbar}K_{A}(x+y/2,x-y/2)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

Given an operator
A
and a function
f
we have the identities

\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}({\mathcal{W}}_{A})=(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}A.

We therefore write
\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}^{-1}=(2\pi\hbar)^{D}{\mathcal{W}}_{A}
. Similarly

{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(f)}=(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}f,

so we write
{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}:=(2\pi\hbar)^{D}\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}
. With these definitions we have the identity

(2.3)

\operatorname*{trace}[\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(f)A]=\langle f,{\mathcal{W}}_{A}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2D})},

which along with the identity
\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(1)=\mathrm{Id}
implies that
\operatorname*{trace}[\rho]=\int{\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}(x,p)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}p
.

For each point in phase space,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2D}
, we write

\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{D})
to denote the isotropic coherent state centered at
\alpha
. For concreteness,
\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}
is
chosen such that

\langle x|\alpha\rangle=(2\pi\hbar)^{-D/4}\exp(i\alpha_{p}\cdot(x-\alpha_{x}/2)/\hbar)\exp(-|x-\alpha_{x}|^{2}/(2\hbar)).

With this choice, one has
\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}=\tau_{\alpha}\mathinner{|{0}\rangle}
where
\tau_{\alpha}
is the phase space translation operator

\tau_{\alpha}:=\exp(i(\alpha_{p}\cdot\hat{x}-\alpha_{x}\cdot\hat{p})/\hbar)
and
\mathinner{|{0}\rangle}
is the ground state of the quantum harmonic oscillator with unit mass in
D
dimensions. We then define the
translation
{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha}
operator for
states
by

(2.4)

{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha}[\rho]=\tau_{\alpha}\,\rho\,\tau_{-\alpha}.

Moreover, the states
\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}
decompose the identity as follows:

\mathrm{Id}=(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2D}}|\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha|\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\,.

The Wigner function of the pure state
\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha}|}
is also a Gaussian, given by

(2.5)

{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha}|}}(x,p)=(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}\exp(-|(x,p)-\alpha|^{2}/(2\hbar)).

Given any quantum state
\rho
we define the Husimi function

(2.6)

{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho}(\alpha):=(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|\rho|\alpha}\rangle}\,,

which is a probability distribution over
\mathbb{R}^{2D}
. Combining (
2.3
) with (
2.5
) we obtain the identity

{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho}={\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}\ast\gamma_{\hbar\,\mathrm{Id}},

where
\gamma_{\sigma}
is the
L^{1}
-normalized Gaussian on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2D}
with covariance
\sigma
.

We also will make use of the noising superoperator
{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]:=(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}\int{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho}(\alpha)\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha}|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha
that we defined in (
1.9
). We note that the noising operator satisfies the identity

(2.7)

{\mathcal{W}}_{{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]}={\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}\ast\gamma_{2\hbar\,\mathrm{Id}}\,.

3.
Propagation of Local States

In this Section we prove Proposition
1.4
. There are two claims in this proposition,
one pertaining to general Hamiltonians
H
with
\partial_{ab}H\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\frac{1}{2}}
, and the other to
Hamiltonians of the form
H=K(p)+V(x)
. First in Section
3.1
below we handle the latter case
and prove as a consequence (
1.6
). Then in Section
3.2
we handle the former
case and prove (
1.5
).

3.1.
Hamiltonians of the form
K(p)+V(x)

First we prove Proposition
1.4
in the case that
H=K(p)+V(x)
. In this case we can prove
a quantitative bound in the isotropic Sobolev space
{\mathsf{H}}^{1}
. Let
\psi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{D})
and

\alpha(t)
be a trajectory of Hamilton’s equations in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2D}
. Then we define the quantity

Q(t):=\|e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{1}_{\alpha(t)}}^{2}:=\|(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha(t))e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{a=1}^{2D}\|(\hat{\alpha}_{a}-\alpha_{a}(t))e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

The bound (
1.6
) will follow from the estimate

\frac{d}{dt}Q(t)\leq(\|\nabla K\|_{\rm Lip}+\|\nabla V\|_{\rm Lip})Q(t).

Without loss of generality it suffices to prove this bound at
t=0
(as otherwise we just conjugate by the
time evolution, redefining
\psi
to be the time evolution up to time
t
). To compute the time derivative first
we observe, writing
\hat{A}(t):=e^{i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\hat{A}e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}
, that

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\hat{x}_{j}(t)\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\partial_{j}K(\hat{p})

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\hat{p}_{j}(t)\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{j}V(\hat{x}).

The idea is to use a first order Taylor approximation for each of these equations, as described in the following lemma.

3.1
.

If
F\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{D})
then

(3.1)

F(x)=F(0)+\sum_{j}x_{j}R_{j}(x)

with

(3.2)

R_{j}(x)=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{j}F(sx)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\,.

Proof.

This is simply the fundamental theorem of calculus.
∎

Therefore, for any
x_{0},p_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D}
there are functions
R_{jk;p_{0}}^{K}
and
R_{jk;x_{0}}^{V}
such that

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\hat{p}_{j}(t)\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\partial_{j}K(p_{0})+\sum_{k}(\hat{p}_{k}-(p_{0})_{k})R_{jk;p_{0}}^{K}(\hat{p})

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\hat{p}_{j}(t)\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{j}V(x_{0})+\sum_{k}(\hat{x}_{k}-(x_{0})_{k})R_{jk;x_{0}}^{V}(\hat{x})\,.

Then writing
\alpha(t)=(x(t),p(t))
, using the equations of motion for
x(t)
and
p(t)
, and
applying (
3.1
) with
F=\partial_{j}V
, we have

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}(\hat{x}_{j}(t)-x_{j}(t))\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{D}(\hat{p}_{k}-p_{k})R^{K}_{jk;p}(\hat{p})

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}(\hat{p}_{j}(t)-p_{j}(t))\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{D}(\hat{x}_{k}-x_{k})R^{V}_{jk;x}(\hat{x}).

We can make this more compact by using matrix notation, writing
R^{K}
and
R^{V}
for matrix-valued functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{D}
:

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}(\hat{x}(t)-x(t))\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=R^{K}(\hat{p})\cdot(\hat{p}-p)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}(\hat{p}(t)-p(t))\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=-R^{V}(\hat{x})\cdot(\hat{x}-x).

The operators
R^{K}(\hat{p})
and
R^{V}(\hat{x})
acting on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{D})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{D}
satisfy the operator norm bounds

\displaystyle\|R^{K}(\hat{p})\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{D})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{D}\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{D})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{D}}

\displaystyle\!=\!\sup_{p\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D}}\|R^{K}(p)\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{D}}\!\leq\!\sup_{p\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D}}\|\operatorname*{Hess}K\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{D}}=\|\nabla K\|_{\rm Lip}

\displaystyle\|R^{V}(\hat{x})\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{D})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{D}\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{D})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{D}}

\displaystyle\!=\!\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D}}\|R^{V}(x)\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{D}}\!\leq\!\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D}}\|\operatorname*{Hess}V\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{D}}=\|\nabla V\|_{\rm Lip}\,.

Here we bounded
R^{K}
by
\operatorname*{Hess}K
using the triangle inequality over the integral (
3.2
), and similarly for
R^{V}
. Observing that
\|(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha(t))e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\psi\|_{L^{2}}=\|(\hat{\alpha}(t)-\alpha(t))\psi\|_{L^{2}}
,
we can now compute

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}Q(t)\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=2\langle(\hat{x}-x)\psi,R^{K}(\hat{p})(\hat{p}-p)\psi\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{D})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{D}}+2\langle(\hat{p}-p)\psi,R^{V}(\hat{x})(\hat{x}-x)\psi\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{D})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{D}}

\displaystyle\leq 2(\|\nabla K\|_{\rm Lip}+\|\nabla V\|_{\rm Lip})\|(\hat{x}-x)\psi\|\|(\hat{p}-p)\psi\|

\displaystyle\leq(\|\nabla K\|_{\rm Lip}+\|\nabla V\|_{\rm Lip})(\|(\hat{x}-x)\psi\|^{2}+\|(\hat{p}-p)\psi\|^{2})

\displaystyle=(\|\nabla K\|_{\rm Lip}+\|\nabla V\|_{\rm Lip})Q\,,

as desired.

3.2.
Higher order moments

Now we generalize the argument of the previous Section to more general Hamiltonians on phase space
{\mathbb{R}}^{2D}
and to higher order moments.

To work with
\hat{H}
we first repurpose Lemma
3.1
to apply to functions in
{\mathcal{S}}_{\frac{1}{2}}
.

3.2
.

Let
F\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
with
\partial_{j}F\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Then there exist functions
G_{j}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\frac{1}{2}}

such that

F(x)=F(0)+\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}G_{j}(x)\,.

Proof.

Again by the fundamental theorem of calculus we have

G_{j}:=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{j}F(sx)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\,.

The integrand is uniformly in
{\mathcal{S}}_{\frac{1}{2}}
and the integral is over a bounded interval, so it follows that
G_{j}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\frac{1}{2}}
.
∎

As a consequence of Lemma
3.2
we have the following.

3.3
.

Let
H
be an admissible Hamiltonian as in Definition
2.2
. Then
for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2D}
we have the following “Taylor expansion”

(3.3)

-\frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{\alpha}_{a},\hat{H}]=\left(\sum_{b}\omega_{ab}\partial_{b}H(\alpha)\right)\mathrm{Id}+\sum_{b}\hat{R}_{ab;\alpha}\cdot(\hat{\alpha}_{b}-\alpha_{b}\mathrm{Id})\,,

where
\hat{R}_{ab;\alpha}=\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(R_{ab;\alpha})
with
R_{ab;\alpha}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\frac{1}{2}}
. In particular,

\hat{R}_{ab;\alpha}
satisfies the bounds (
2.2
) as in Proposition
2.1
.

Before stating our assumptions relating

H
and
\hat{H}
, we introduce some notation. We first define the
k
-th order localization norms
around
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2D}
,

\|\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha}}:=\sum_{|J|=k}\|(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)_{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}}.

A first observation is that the
{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha}
norm is bounded in terms of the
{\mathsf{H}}^{1}_{\alpha}
norm due to the uncertainty
principle. In particular, the Heisenberg uncertainty principle

\|(\hat{x}-x_{0})\psi\|_{L^{2}}\|(\hat{p}-p_{0})\psi\|_{L^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\,\hbar

implies, by the AM-GM inequality, the bound

\|(\hat{x}-x_{0})\psi\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\hat{p}-p_{0})\psi\|_{L^{2}}\geq\sqrt{\hbar}\,.

Therefore, we have by induction the consequence

(3.4)

\|\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha}}\geq\sqrt{\hbar}\,\|\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k-1}_{\alpha}}.

Before we are ready to prove the bound (
1.5
) we need one more lemma.

3.4
.

Let
\vec{m}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k}
and
\vec{m}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k^{\prime}}
,
R\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\frac{1}{2}}
, and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2D}
. Then for

\hat{R}=\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(R)
,

(3.5)

\|(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)_{\vec{m}}\,\hat{R}\,(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)_{\vec{m}^{\prime}}\,\psi\|_{L^{2}}{\,\lesssim\,}\|\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k+k^{\prime}}_{\alpha}}.

Proof.

First we prove (
3.5
) with
R=1
. In this case we may take
k^{\prime}=0
without loss of generality. The proof
is by induction on
k
, and the result clearly holds for
k=0,1
. For
k>1
we observe that monomials can be written in the form

(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)_{\vec{m}}=(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)_{n(\vec{m})}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\hbar^{j}P_{k-j}(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha),

where
P_{j}
are (non-commutative) homogeneous polynomials of degree
j
, with coefficients independent of
\hbar
. Therefore by induction
we have

\displaystyle\|(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)_{\vec{m}}\psi\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha}}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\hbar^{j}\|\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k-j}_{\alpha}}.

The result now follows from the uncertainty principle in the form (
3.4
).

Now to prove (
3.5
) in the general case, we observe that

(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)_{\vec{m}}\,\hat{R}=\sum_{S\subset[k]}\mathrm{ad}^{n(\vec{m}_{S})}[\hat{R}](\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)_{\vec{m}_{[k]\setminus S}}.

Above
\vec{m}_{S}
is the substring of
\vec{m}
obtained by keeping only the entries specified by the set
S
. Therefore,
and again using (
3.4
) we have

\displaystyle\|(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)_{\vec{m}}\hat{R}\,(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)_{\vec{m}^{\prime}}\psi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{S\subset[k]}\|\mathrm{ad}^{n(\vec{m}_{S})}[\hat{R}](\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)_{\vec{m}_{[k]\setminus S}+\vec{m}^{\prime}}\psi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle{\,\lesssim\,}\sum_{S\subset[k]}\hbar^{|S|/2}\|\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k+k^{\prime}-|S|}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle{\,\lesssim\,}\|\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k+k^{\prime}}_{\alpha}}.

∎

We are now ready to complete the proof of Equation (
1.5
) of Proposition
1.4
,
which follows from the estimate

(3.6)

\frac{d}{dt}\|U(t)\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha(t)}}\leq C_{k}\|U(t)\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha(t)}}\,,

where as usual
U(t)=e^{-iHt/\hbar}
.

Using (
3.3
) we have for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2D}

\left.\frac{d}{dt}(\hat{\alpha}_{a}(t)-\alpha_{a}(t))\right|_{t=0}=\sum_{b}\hat{R}_{ab;\alpha}(\hat{\alpha}_{b}-\alpha_{b})\,.

Therefore, for any multi-index
J
we have that the derivative
\frac{d}{dt}(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)_{J}
is a finite sum of
terms of the form
(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)_{\vec{m}_{1}}\,\hat{R}_{ab;\alpha}\,(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)_{\vec{m}_{2}}
for some monomial
strings
|\vec{m}_{1}|+|\vec{m}_{2}|=k
, and the result then follows from Lemma
3.4
.

3.3.
Proof of Corollary
1.5

For convenience of the reader we recall the statement of Corollary
1.5

Corollary 1.5
.

Let

d_{W_{p}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\rho},\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho})\leq e^{C_{p}T}\hbar^{1/2}.

(3.8)

\frac{1}{2D}\,d_{W_{2}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\rho},\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho})^{2}\leq(1+2e^{\Lambda_{H}T})\hbar.

Here we will use
W_{q}
instead of
W_{p}

p(\alpha)

\displaystyle d_{W_{q}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\rho},\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho})^{q}\leq\int p(\alpha)d_{W_{q}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}[\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha}|}]},\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha}|}})^{q}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha.

Let
\delta_{\alpha}
be the Dirac delta measure located at
\alpha
. Then we can bound the above
integrand by

\displaystyle d_{W_{q}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}[\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha}|}]},\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha}|}})

\displaystyle\leq d_{W_{q}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}[\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha}|}]},\delta_{\Phi_{T}\alpha})+d_{W_{q}}(\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha}|}},\delta_{\Phi_{T}\alpha})\,.

We treat each term above differently depending on the assumptions about
H
.

Case I: General Hamiltonians.

Without loss of generality (by the monotonicity of
d_{W_{q}}
in
q
) we can assume that
q=2k
for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Then we compute

\displaystyle\int|\beta-\alpha|^{2k}{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\psi}|}}(\beta)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta

\displaystyle=\int|\beta-\alpha|^{2k}|\!\mathinner{\langle{\beta|\psi}\rangle}\!|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta

\displaystyle{\,\lesssim\,}_{k,D}\sum_{a=1}^{2D}\int|\beta_{a}-\alpha_{a}|^{2k}|\!\mathinner{\langle{\beta|\psi}\rangle}\!|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta

\displaystyle{\,\lesssim\,}_{k,D}\|\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha}}.

Taking

, this proves

(3.9)

d_{W_{q}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}[\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha}|}]},\delta_{\Phi_{T}\alpha}){\,\lesssim\,}e^{CT}\hbar^{1/2}.

Moreover,
\Phi_{T}
is

-Lipschitz, so that

\displaystyle d_{W_{q}}(\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha}|}},\delta_{\Phi_{T}\alpha})

\displaystyle{\,\lesssim\,}e^{CT}d_{W_{p}}({\mathrm{H}}_{\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha}|}},\delta_{\alpha})

\displaystyle{\,\lesssim\,}e^{CT}\hbar^{1/2}.

Combining these bounds completes the proof of (
1.7
).

Case II: Hamiltonians of the form

.

An elementary calculation shows that in this case
\Phi_{T}
is
e^{\Lambda_{H}T}
-Lipschitz
1
1

1

In fact, one can use the bound (
1.6
) in the limit

with

to see that

.
and that

It therefore suffices to estimate

\displaystyle\int|\beta-\alpha|^{2}{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\psi}|}}(\beta)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta

The operator in brackets can alternatively be written as follows:

4.
Transport by localized unitaries

In this Section we state a precise version of Proposition

and prove it.
The first step is to quantify what it means for a local unitary to be well localized in phase space,
and to show that the operator

(4.1)

W_{T,\alpha}:=e^{i\hat{H}T/\hbar}\tau_{\alpha}e^{-i\hat{H}T/\hbar}.

is well-localized. This is done in Section
4.1
. Then in Section

elementary bounds, and in Sections

4.1.
Local unitary operators

We measure the localization of a unitary operator in phase space using centered isotropic Sobolev norms applied
to coherent states. To this end we define

\|W\|_{Z^{k}}:=\sup_{\alpha}\|W\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha}}.

Note that
\|W\|_{Z^{k}}\geq\hbar^{1/2}\|W\|_{Z^{k-1}}
by (
3.4
).
This norm bounds the off-diagonal matrix elements of the operator
W
.

4.1
.

For any operator
W
, and
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2D}
,

|\!\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|W|\beta}\rangle}\!|\leq C_{k,D}|\alpha-\beta|^{-k}\|W\|_{Z^{k}}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can suppose that
|\alpha-\beta|\leq C_{D}|\alpha_{1}-\beta_{1}|
.
Define
f(x)=(\hbar+(x-\beta_{1})^{2})^{k/2}
and
g(x)=\frac{1}{f(x)}
. Then

\displaystyle|\!\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|W|\beta}\rangle}\!|

\displaystyle=|\!\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|g(\hat{x}_{1})f(\hat{x}_{1})W|\beta}\rangle}\!|

\displaystyle\leq\|(\hbar+(\hat{x}_{1}-\beta_{1})^{2})^{-k/2}\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\|_{L^{2}}\|(\hbar+(\hat{x}_{1}-\beta_{1})^{2})^{k/2}W\mathinner{|{\beta}\rangle}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq|\alpha_{1}-\beta_{1}|^{-k}\|W\|_{Z^{k}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{k,D}|\alpha-\beta|^{-k}\|W\|_{Z^{k}}.

∎

Our goal is to bound
\|W_{T,\alpha}\|_{Z^{k}}
, where
W_{T,\alpha}=e^{i\hat{H}T/\hbar}\tau_{\alpha}e^{-i\hat{H}T/\hbar}
involves a phase space shift operator
\tau_{\alpha}
. We therefore need to know how the isotropic Sobolev norms are affected under such shifts, and this is recorded in the following lemma:

4.2
.

Let
\psi\in{\mathsf{H}}^{k}
and
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2D}
. Then

\|\tau_{\beta}\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha}}\leq C_{k}(1+|\beta|\hbar^{-1/2})^{k}\|\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha}}.

Proof.

The result for
k=0
follows from the boundedness of the shift operator
(since
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{0}_{\alpha}}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}
. Now suppose
k\geq 1
, and let
\vec{m}\in[2D]^{k}
be a tuple
specifying some monomial of degree
k
.
We use the identity
\hat{\alpha}_{j}\tau_{\beta}=\tau_{\beta}\hat{\alpha}_{j}+\beta_{j}\tau_{\beta}

and write
\vec{m}^{\prime}\in[2D]^{k-1}
for the
k-1
subtuple of
\vec{m}

to estimate

\displaystyle\|(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)_{\vec{m}^{\prime}}\tau_{\beta}\psi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)_{\vec{m}^{\prime}}\tau_{\beta}(\hat{\alpha}_{m_{k}}-\alpha_{m_{k}})\psi\|_{L^{2}}+|\beta_{m_{k}}|\|(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)^{\vec{m}^{\prime}}\tau_{\beta}\psi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\tau_{\beta}(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k-1}_{\alpha}}+|\beta_{m_{k}}|\|\tau_{\beta}\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k-1}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle{\,\lesssim\,}(1+|\beta|\hbar^{-1/2})^{k-1}\|(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k-1}_{\alpha}}+|\beta_{m_{k}}|(1+|\beta|\hbar^{-1/2})^{k-1}\|\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k-1}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle{\,\lesssim\,}(1+|\beta|\hbar^{-1/2})^{k-1}\|\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha}}+\hbar^{-1/2}|\beta_{m_{k}}|(1+|\beta|\hbar^{-1/2})^{k-1}\|\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle{\,\lesssim\,}(1+|\beta|\hbar^{-1/2})^{k}\|\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha}}.

Above we have used the bound
\|\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha}}\leq\hbar^{-1/2}\|\psi\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k-1}_{\alpha}}

(see (
3.4
)) and the inductive hypothesis.
∎

We can now use Proposition
1.4
to bound the phase-space localization of
W_{T,\alpha}
.

4.3
.

Let
H
be an admissible Hamiltonian in the sense that
\partial_{ab}H\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\frac{1}{2}}
for all
a,b\in[2D]
.
Then for any
T\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2D}
the operator
W_{T,\alpha}
defined in (
4.1
)
satisfies

\|W_{T,\alpha}\|_{Z^{k}}\leq e^{C_{k}T}(\hbar^{k/2}+|\alpha|^{k})\,.

Proof.

Fix some
\alpha_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2D}
and let
\alpha(T)
be the classical evolution at time
T
.
We use Proposition
1.4
and Lemma
4.2
to estimate

\displaystyle\|e^{i\hat{H}T/\hbar}\tau_{\alpha}e^{-i\hat{H}T/\hbar}\mathinner{|{\alpha_{0}}\rangle}\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha_{0}}}

\displaystyle{\,\lesssim\,}e^{C_{k}T}\|\tau_{\alpha}e^{-i\hat{H}T/\hbar}\mathinner{|{\alpha_{0}}\rangle}\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha(T)}}

\displaystyle{\,\lesssim\,}e^{C_{k}T}(1+|\alpha|^{k}\hbar^{-k/2})\|e^{-i\hat{H}T/\hbar}\mathinner{|{\alpha_{0}}\rangle}\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha(T)}}

\displaystyle{\,\lesssim\,}e^{C_{k}T}(1+|\alpha|^{k}\hbar^{-k/2})\|\mathinner{|{\alpha_{0}}\rangle}\|_{{\mathsf{H}}^{k}_{\alpha_{0}}}

\displaystyle{\,\lesssim\,}e^{C_{k}T}(1+|\alpha|^{k}\hbar^{-k/2})\hbar^{k/2}.

∎

4.2.
Bound on transport by local unitaries

We are now ready to state the main result concerning the transport distance between
\rho
and
W\rho W^{\dagger}
.

4.4
.

Let
r>\hbar^{1/2}
and
W
be a unitary operator satisfying
\|W\|_{Z^{k}}<\infty
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
1\leq p<\infty
. Then there is some
M=M(p,D)
such that

d_{W_{p}}({\mathrm{H}}_{\rho},{\mathrm{H}}_{W\rho W^{\dagger}})\leq Cr(\hbar^{-2D}r^{4D})(1+r^{-M}\|W\|_{Z^{M}})^{2}.

In particular, taking
W=e^{i\hat{H}T/\hbar}\tau_{\alpha}e^{-i\hat{H}T/\hbar}
for an admissible Hamiltonian

\hat{H}
, we have, taking
r=\hbar^{1/2}+|\alpha|
,

(4.2)

d_{W_{p}}({\mathrm{H}}_{\rho},{\mathrm{H}}_{W\rho W^{\dagger}})\leq Ce^{C_{M}(H)T}(\hbar^{1/2}+|\alpha|)(1+|\alpha|^{4D}\hbar^{-2D}).

The term involving
\hbar^{-2D}r^{4D}
is possibly unnecessary, and represents some inefficiency
in the argument. Moreover, one might expect that one can take
M
independent of
D
, whereas
the proof given below can be made to work only if
M>4D+p+2
.
The quantity
1+r^{-M}\|W\|_{Z^{M}}
appears repeatedly throughout the proof, so we use the more convenient
notation

\|W\|_{Z^{M}_{r}}:=1+r^{-M}\|W\|_{Z^{M}}.

The proof of Theorem
4.4
goes through Kantorovich duality, which gives
the identity

d_{W_{p}}(\mu,\nu)^{p}=\sup_{f(x)+g(y)\leq|x-y|^{p}}\int f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu+\int g\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu.

In the supremum above, no regularity is imposed on
f
and
g
aside from measurability.
On the other hand, when we take
\mu={\mathrm{H}}_{\rho}
and
\nu={\mathrm{H}}_{W\rho W^{\dagger}}
, it will
be helpful to have some regularity for
f
and
g
. This can be handled by performing a
mild mollification.

4.5
.

Let
\mu
and
\nu
be probability measures and let
\kappa
be some convolution kernel satisfying

\int|x|^{p}\kappa(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x:=r_{p}(\kappa)^{p}<\infty
. Then we have

d_{W_{p}}(\mu,\nu)^{p}\leq r_{p}(\kappa)^{p}+\sup_{f(x)+g(y)\leq|x-y|^{p}}\int f\ast\kappa\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu+\int g\ast\kappa\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu\,.

Proof.

We have
d_{W_{p}}(\mu,\mu\ast\kappa)\leq r_{p}(\kappa)
, so by the triangle inequality

d_{W_{p}}(\mu,\nu)\leq d_{W_{p}}(\mu\ast\kappa,\nu\ast\kappa)+r_{p}(\kappa).

Then we apply Kantorovich duality to the first term.
∎

We will need to use a specific convolution kernel with polynomial decay.
To this end, we fix
K=2D+10+2\lceil p\rceil
and define

\varphi(\alpha):=C(1+|\alpha|^{2})^{-K/2}
, with
C=C_{K}
chosen such that
\int\varphi=1
. The exponent is chosen to be large enough that
\int|\alpha|^{p}\varphi(\alpha)<\infty
.
We use also the scaled version
\varphi_{R}(x):=R^{-D}\varphi(x/R)
, where we take

R=Q\ell
with
Q
an as-yet unspecified large constant and
\ell
given by

(4.3)

\ell:=r(\hbar^{-2D}r^{4D})\|W\|_{Z^{K+2D+1}_{r}}^{2}.

Theorem
4.4
will then follow if we show that for arbitrary measurable

f,g
satisfying
f(\alpha)+g(\beta)\leq|\alpha-\beta|^{p}
,

(4.4)

\int(f\ast\varphi_{R})(\alpha){\mathrm{H}}_{\rho}(\alpha)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha+\int(g\ast\varphi_{R})(\alpha){\mathrm{H}}_{W\rho W^{\dagger}}(\alpha)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\leq QR^{p}\,.

For convenience we define
\delta:=QR^{p}
to be the term on the right-hand side.

Using the wavepacket quantization

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Op}^{w}(f):=(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}\int f(\alpha)\,|\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha|\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha,

we have the identity

\int{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho}(\alpha)f(\alpha)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha=\operatorname*{trace}[\rho\,\mathrm{Op}^{w}(f)]\,.

Therefore, the bound (
4.4
) is equivalent to the bound

\operatorname*{trace}[\rho(\mathrm{Op}^{w}(f\ast\varphi_{R})+W\mathrm{Op}^{w}(g\ast\varphi_{R})W^{\dagger})]\leq\delta.

Since
\rho
is arbitrary, proving the above bound requires the following operator inequality:

\mathrm{Op}^{w}(f\ast\varphi_{R})+W\mathrm{Op}^{w}(g\ast\varphi_{R})W^{\dagger}\preceq\delta\,\text{Id}\,.

Now define
D(\alpha):=-(f(\alpha)+g(\alpha))
, which is a nonnegative function because

f(\alpha)+g(\alpha)\leq|\alpha-\alpha|^{p}=0
. We can then rearrange the above inequality into

(4.6)

0\preceq\mathrm{Op}^{w}(D\ast\varphi_{R})+\delta+[\mathrm{Op}^{w}(g\ast\varphi_{R})-W\mathrm{Op}^{w}(g\ast\varphi_{R})W^{\dagger}].

One can think of the error term

\mathrm{Op}^{w}(g\ast\varphi_{R})-W\mathrm{Op}^{w}(g\ast\varphi_{R})W^{\dagger}
on the right as expressing how much
g
changes when
it is wiggled slightly by
W
. The point
of the inequality above is that the function
D
controls the regularity of
g
, acting as a sort of modulus of continuity. Indeed, adding
D(\alpha)
to both sides of

f(\alpha)+g(\beta)\leq|\alpha-\beta|^{p}

yields

g(\beta)-g(\alpha)\leq|\alpha-\beta|^{p}+D(\alpha).

By symmetry, we therefore have

(4.7)

|g(\alpha)-g(\beta)|\leq|\alpha-\beta|^{p}+D(\alpha)+D(\beta).

This inequality justifies the notion that
D
acts as a sort of “modulus of continuity” for
g
.

Although the reasoning above gives some heuristic justification for the operator inequality (
4.6
), it is unclear however how one would establish
positivity for this operator by direct means.
For this we use the following abstract
result.

4.6
.

Let
A\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a bounded self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
. Suppose
that
A=M+E
where
M\succeq{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{Id}
for some
{\varepsilon}>0
and
E
is compact. Moreover suppose that there exists a Banach space
X
and a continuous inclusion
\iota:{\mathcal{H}}\to X
such that
M^{-1}E
is a contraction on
X
.
Then
A
is positive semi-definite.

Proof.

First note that the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M}
defined by

\langle f,g\rangle_{M}:=\langle f,Mg\rangle

is equivalent to the standard inner product on
{\mathcal{H}}
because the operator
M
is a continuous bijection on
{\mathcal{H}}
with inverse bounded by
{\varepsilon}^{-1}
. The operator
B:=M^{-1}E
is self-adjoint with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M}
, and is also
compact because
E
is compact.
The positivity of
A
is then equivalent to the positivity of
\mathrm{Id}+B

with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M}
. This follows if the spectrum
\sigma(B)
satisfies
\sigma(B)\subset[-1,1]
.

Since
B
is compact, we
simply need to show that every eigenvalue

\lambda_{k}
of
B
satisfies
|\lambda_{k}|\leq 1
.
Let
\psi_{k}\in{\mathcal{H}}
be an eigenvector of
B
with eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
, so that
B\psi_{k}=\lambda_{k}\psi_{k}
.
Normalize
\psi_{k}
so that
\|\psi_{k}\|_{X}=1
. Now we use that
B
is a contraction on
X
to see that

|\lambda_{k}|=\|B_{\varepsilon}\psi_{k}\|_{X}\leq\|\psi_{k}\|_{X}=1,

as desired.
∎

To establish (
4.6
) we invoke Lemma
4.6
with

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{Op}^{w}(D\ast\varphi_{R})+\delta

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{Op}^{w}(g\ast\varphi_{R})-W\mathrm{Op}^{w}(g\ast\varphi_{R})W^{\dagger}.

The strict positivity of
M
follows because
\delta>0
and
D\succeq 0
.
Moreover, we can have compactness of
E
if we additionally require that
g
is compactly supported
(which of course can be done without changing the supremum in the Kantorovich duality).

It therefore suffices to find some norm
X
with an inclusion
{\mathcal{H}}\hookrightarrow X

for which
M^{-1}E
is a contraction. This norm can be explicitly defined:

\|\psi\|_{X}:=\sup_{\alpha}|\!\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|\psi}\rangle}\!|.

In our proof we also use the auxiliary norm

\|\psi\|_{Y}:=\sup_{\alpha}(D\ast\varphi_{R}(\alpha)+\delta)^{-1}|\!\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|\psi}\rangle}\!|\,.

The main result Theorem
4.4
then follows from the pair of lemmas:

4.7
.

Let
M
be as above (including all implicit assumptions about
f,g,D
, etc.). Then

(4.8)

\|M^{-1}\|_{Y\to X}\leq C.

4.8
.

Let
E
be as above (including all implicit assumptions about
f,g,D
, etc.). Then

(4.9)

\|E\|_{X\to Y}\leq CQ^{-1}.

Above recall that
R=Q\ell
. Then combining Lemma
4.7
and
Lemma
4.8
we have

\|M^{-1}E\|_{X\to X}\leq CQ^{-1},

so that choosing
Q
large enough we have that
M^{-1}E
is a contraction. This implies
the positivity of the operator (
4.6
) by Lemma
4.6
, and therefore the desired bound (
4.4
) follows and with it the proof of Theorem
4.4
.

The remainder of this Section is dedicated to the proofs of Lemmas
4.7
and
4.8
.
Before we can proceed with their proofs, we first need to take a step back to derive some
elementary facts about the convolution kernel
\varphi
.

4.3.
Elementary lemmas concerning the convolution kernel
\varphi

In this Section we collect some elementary bounds involving
\varphi_{R}
which will be used in the proofs of
Lemma
4.7
and Lemma
4.8
below.

Recall that

\varphi(\alpha):=C_{K}(1+|\alpha|^{2})^{-K/2},

with
C_{K}
chosen so that
\int\varphi=1
. The actual exponent
K>10D
is kept implicit throughout (so constants will depend on
K
). We will work with the scaled function
\varphi_{R}(x):=R^{-D}\varphi(x/R)
.

We collect some pointwise bounds on
\varphi_{R}
below.

4.9
.

The convolution kernel
\varphi_{R}
satisfies the following estimates for any
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2D}
:

(4.10)

\displaystyle|\nabla\varphi_{R}(\alpha)|

\displaystyle\leq CR^{-1}(1+|\alpha/R|)^{-1}\varphi_{R}(\alpha)

(4.11)

\displaystyle|\varphi_{R}(\alpha+\beta)|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|\beta/R|^{K})\varphi_{R}(\alpha)

(4.12)

\displaystyle|\varphi_{R}(\alpha+\beta)-\varphi_{R}(\alpha)|

\displaystyle\leq CR^{-1}|\beta|(1+|\beta/R|^{K})\varphi_{R}(\alpha)\,.

Proof.

The bound (
4.10
) follows from rescaling the elementary inequality

|\nabla\varphi(\alpha)|\leq C(1+|\alpha|)^{-1}\varphi(\alpha)\,.

The bound (
4.11
) simply follows because the right hand side is bounded from below by a constant, and the left hand side is bounded by a constant.

To prove (
4.12
) we use the fundamental theorem of calculus:

|\varphi_{R}(\alpha+\beta)-\varphi_{R}(\alpha)|\leq|\beta|\int_{0}^{1}|(\nabla\varphi_{R})(\alpha+t\beta)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,.

To bound the integrand we first use (
4.10
) and then (
4.11
)

\displaystyle|\nabla\varphi_{R}(\alpha+\beta^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq CR^{-1}(1+|(\alpha+\beta^{\prime})/R|)^{-1}\varphi_{R}(\alpha+\beta^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq CR^{-1}(1+|(\alpha+\beta^{\prime})/R|)^{-1}(1+|\beta^{\prime}/R|^{K})\varphi_{R}(\alpha)\,.

We can drop the term
(1+|(\alpha+\beta^{\prime})/R|)^{-1}
which is bounded by
1
, and then integrate to conclude
the proof of (
4.12
).
∎

The pointwise bounds in Lemma
4.9
can then be used to prove bounds for
functions of the form
f_{R}=f\ast\varphi_{R}
where
f\geq 0
is a positive measurable function.

4.10
.

Let
\gamma
be a positive convolution kernel satisfying
\int|\alpha|\gamma(\alpha)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha=M_{1}<\infty

and
\int|\alpha|^{K+1}\gamma(\alpha)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha=M_{K+1}<\infty
and
f\geq 0
be a positive measurable function.
Then

\int|f_{R}(\alpha)-f_{R}(\alpha+\beta)|\gamma(\beta)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta\leq CR^{-1}(M_{1}+R^{-K}M_{K+1})f_{R}(\alpha)\,.

Proof.

Write out the difference
f_{R}(\alpha)-f_{R}(\alpha+\beta)
as an integral and then apply (
4.12
)
to obtain

\displaystyle|f_{R}(\alpha)-f_{R}(\alpha+\beta)|

\displaystyle\leq\int f(\zeta)|\varphi_{R}(\alpha-\zeta)-\varphi_{R}(\alpha+\beta-\zeta)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\zeta

\displaystyle\leq CR^{-1}|\beta|(1+|\beta/R|^{K})f_{R}(\alpha)\,.

∎

4.4.
Proof of Lemma
4.7

For simplicity we set
\widetilde{D}:=D\ast\varphi_{R}+\delta
, which implicitly depends on the
parameters
R=Q\ell
with
\ell>r
defined in (
4.3
)
and
\delta=QR^{p}
. With this notation, we have that
M=\mathrm{Op}^{w}(\widetilde{D})
.

To work with
M^{-1}
we first observe the identity

\displaystyle\mathrm{Op}^{w}(\widetilde{D}^{-1})\mathrm{Op}^{w}(\widetilde{D})

\displaystyle=(2\pi\hbar)^{-2D}\int\widetilde{D}^{-1}(\alpha)\widetilde{D}(\beta)\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|\beta}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\beta}|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Id}+(2\pi\hbar)^{-2D}\int\widetilde{D}^{-1}(\alpha)(\widetilde{D}(\beta)-\widetilde{D}(\alpha))\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|\beta}\rangle}\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\beta}|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta

\displaystyle=:\mathrm{Id}+T\,.

Rearranging, this gives

\mathrm{Op}^{w}(\widetilde{D})^{-1}=(\mathrm{Id}+T)^{-1}\mathrm{Op}^{w}(\widetilde{D}^{-1})\,.

To prove (
4.8
) it suffices to prove both

(4.13)

\|T\|_{X\to X}\leq\frac{1}{2}

and

(4.14)

\|\mathrm{Op}^{w}(\widetilde{D}^{-1})\|_{Y\to X}\leq C\,.

First we prove (
4.13
). We have the bounds

\displaystyle\|T\|_{X\to X}

\displaystyle{\,\lesssim\,}(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}\sup_{\xi}\int|\widetilde{D}^{-1}(\xi)(\widetilde{D}(\eta)-\widetilde{D}(\xi))||\!\mathinner{\langle{\xi|\eta}\rangle}\!|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle{\,\lesssim\,}CQ^{-1}(\hbar^{1/2}\ell^{-1})\,.

To get to the last line, we applied
Lemma
4.10
to the right hand side with convolution kernel

\gamma(\beta):=(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}|\!\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|\alpha+\beta}\rangle}\!|
and
f=D+\delta
. Thus
by choosing
Q
large enough we have (
4.13
), as desired (since
\ell>\hbar^{1/2}
).

The bound (
4.14
) almost follows by definition of
\|\cdot\|_{Y}
:

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{Op}^{w}(\widetilde{D}^{-1})\psi\|_{X}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\alpha}|\!\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|\mathrm{Op}^{w}(\widetilde{D}^{-1})\psi}\rangle}\!|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\alpha}(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}\int\widetilde{D}^{-1}(\beta)|\!\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|\beta}\rangle}\!\,\!\mathinner{\langle{\beta|\psi}\rangle}\!|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi\|_{Y}\,\sup_{\alpha}\int(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}|\!\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|\beta}\rangle}\!|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta

\displaystyle\leq C\|\psi\|_{Y}.

4.5.
Proof of Lemma
4.8

Recall that

E=\mathrm{Op}^{w}(g\ast\varphi_{R})-W\mathrm{Op}^{w}(g\ast\varphi_{R})W^{\dagger}.

For simplicity we write
g_{R}:=g\ast\varphi_{R}
.

We compute

\displaystyle\|E\psi\|_{Y}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\xi}\widetilde{D}(\xi)^{-1}|\!\mathinner{\langle{\xi|E\psi}\rangle}\!|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\xi}\widetilde{D}(\xi)^{-1}\Big|(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}\int g_{R}(\alpha)\mathinner{\langle{\xi|\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|\psi}\rangle}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha-(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}\int g_{R}(\alpha)\mathinner{\langle{\xi|W|\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|W^{\dagger}|\psi}\rangle}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\Big|.

Note that, by the identity
\mathrm{Id}=(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}\int\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha}|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha
and the unitarity of
W
we have

g_{R}(\xi)\mathinner{\langle{\xi|\psi}\rangle}=(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}\int g_{R}(\xi)\mathinner{\langle{\xi|\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|\psi}\rangle}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha=(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}\int g_{R}(\xi)\mathinner{\langle{\xi|W|\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|W^{\dagger}|\psi}\rangle}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\,,

so that

\displaystyle\|E\psi\|_{Y}

\displaystyle{\,\lesssim\,}\sup_{\xi}\widetilde{D}(\xi)^{-1}\hbar^{-D}\Big[\int|g_{R}(\alpha)-g_{R}(\xi)||\!\mathinner{\langle{\xi|\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|\psi}\rangle}\!|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int|g_{R}(\alpha)-g_{R}(\xi)||\!\mathinner{\langle{\xi|W|\alpha}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|W^{\dagger}|\psi}\rangle}\!|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\Big].

We will prove a bound on the second term, the first term having the same form but with
W=\mathrm{Id}

and
\|\mathrm{Id}\|_{Z^{M}}\leq\|W\|_{Z^{M}}
.
First we use Lemma
4.1
and a decomposition of the identity to estimate

\displaystyle|\!\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|W^{\dagger}|\psi}\rangle}\!|

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}\int|\!\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|W^{\dagger}|\beta}\rangle}\!||\!\mathinner{\langle{\beta|\psi}\rangle}\!|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi\|_{X}\|W\|_{Z^{2D+1}_{r}}(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}\int(1+r^{-1}|\alpha-\beta|)^{-2D-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta

\displaystyle{\,\lesssim\,}(\hbar^{-1/2}r)^{2D}\|\psi\|_{X}\|W\|_{Z^{2D+1}_{r}}.

So to prove (
4.9
) it suffices to show that

(4.15)

(2\pi\hbar)^{-D}\int|g_{R}(\alpha)-g_{R}(\xi)||\!\mathinner{\langle{\xi|W|\alpha}\rangle}\!|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\leq CQ^{-1}(\hbar^{1/2}r^{-1})^{2D}\|W\|_{Z^{2D+1}_{r}}^{-1}\widetilde{D}(\xi)\,.

We have again by Lemma

|\!\mathinner{\langle{\xi|W|\alpha}\rangle}\!|{\,\lesssim\,}r^{2D}\chi_{r}(\xi-\alpha)\|W\|_{Z^{2K}_{r}},

where we have defined
\chi_{r}(\xi):=r^{-2D}(1+|\xi|/r)^{-K-2D-1}
to be the same kind of

\varphi_{r}

Upon changing variables, the integral we need to bound has the form

Now we bound the difference in the first term by writing out the convolution:

\displaystyle|g_{R}(\xi+\alpha)-g_{R}(\xi)|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int g(\beta)(\varphi_{R}(\xi+\alpha-\beta)-\varphi_{R}(\xi-\beta))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int(g(\beta)-g(\xi+(R/r)\alpha))(\varphi_{R}(\xi+\alpha-\beta)-\varphi_{R}(\xi-\beta))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int|g(\beta)-g(\xi+(R/r)\alpha)||\varphi_{R}(\xi+\alpha-\beta)-\varphi_{R}(\xi-\beta)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta

\displaystyle\leq\int\Big(|\beta-\xi-(R/r)\alpha|^{p}+D(\beta)+D(\xi+(R/r)\alpha)\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\,\times|\varphi_{R}(\xi+\alpha-\beta)-\varphi_{R}(\xi-\beta)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta\,.

We therefore have

\int|g_{R}(\xi+\alpha)-g_{R}(\xi)|\chi_{r}(\alpha)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\leq{\rm I}+{\rm II}+{\rm III}\,,

where

(4.16)

\displaystyle{\rm I}

\displaystyle:=\int\int|\beta-\xi-(R/r)\alpha|^{p}|\varphi_{R}(\xi+\alpha-\beta)-\varphi_{R}(\xi-\beta)|\chi_{r}(\alpha)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha

\displaystyle{\rm II}

\displaystyle:=\int\int D(\beta)|\varphi_{R}(\xi+\alpha-\beta)-\varphi_{R}(\xi-\beta)|\chi_{r}(\alpha)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha

\displaystyle{\rm III}

\displaystyle:=\int\int D(\xi+(R/r)\alpha)|\varphi_{R}(\xi+\alpha-\beta)-\varphi_{R}(\xi-\beta)|\chi_{r}(\alpha)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha.

To bound the first integral (

), which appropriately rescaled becomes

Therefore, using also

\displaystyle{\rm I}

\displaystyle\leq CR^{-1}\int\Big(\int|\beta-\xi|^{p}\varphi_{R}(\xi-\beta)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta\Big)|\alpha|(1+|\alpha/R|^{K})\chi_{r}(\alpha)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha

\displaystyle\qquad+CR^{-1}\int\Big(\int\varphi_{R}(\xi-\beta)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta\Big)(R/r)^{p}|\alpha|^{p}|\alpha|(1+|\alpha/R|^{K})\chi_{r}(\alpha)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha

\displaystyle\leq C(D\ast\varphi_{R})(\xi)(r/R)\,.

For the third term (

\displaystyle{\rm III}

\displaystyle\leq(rR^{-1})\int D(\xi+\beta)(R^{-1}|\beta|)\chi_{R}(\beta)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta

\displaystyle{\,\lesssim\,}(rR^{-1})(D\ast\varphi_{R})(\xi)\,.

Combining our bounds for (

\int|g_{R}(\alpha)-g_{R}(\xi)||\!\mathinner{\langle{\xi|W|\alpha}\rangle}\!|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\leq C(r/R)(\hbar^{-1/2}r)^{2D}\|W\|_{Z^{M}_{r}}(D\ast\varphi_{R}(\xi)+R^{p})\,,

and this implies (
4.15
) by the definition of
R=Q\ell
with

\ell=r\hbar^{-2D}r^{4D}\|W\|_{Z^{2K}_{r}}^{2}
.

5.
Proofs of main results

In this Section we complete the proofs of the main results.

5.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Following the argument outlined in Section
1.2
, we use the triangle inequality to bound

\displaystyle d_{W_{p}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\rho},\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho})\leq d_{W_{p}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\rho},{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\circ{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]})+d_{W_{p}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\circ{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]},\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]})+d_{W_{p}}(\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]},\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho})\,,

where
{\mathcal{N}}
is the noising superoperator. By Corollary
1.5
(proved in Section
3.3
) we have

d_{W_{p}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\circ{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]},\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]})\leq e^{C_{p}T}\hbar^{1/2}.

Then because
\Phi_{T}
is
e^{CT}
-Lipschitz we have

d_{W_{p}}(\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]},\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho})\leq e^{CT}d_{W_{p}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]},{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho})\leq C_{p}e^{CT}\hbar^{1/2},

the second inequality following as a consequence of the identity

{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]}={\mathrm{H}}_{\rho}\ast\gamma_{2\hbar}\,.

For the remaining term we use the identity

{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]=\int{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha}[\rho]\,\gamma_{\hbar}(\alpha)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\,.

Thus by convexity of the
W_{p}
distance and defining
\nu:={\mathcal{U}}_{T}\rho
we have

\displaystyle d_{W_{p}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\rho},{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\circ{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]})^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\int\gamma_{\hbar}(\alpha)d_{W_{p}}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\rho},{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\circ{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha}\rho})^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha

\displaystyle=\int\gamma_{\hbar}(\alpha)d_{W_{p}}({\mathrm{H}}_{\nu},{\mathrm{H}}_{W_{T,\alpha}\nu W_{T,\alpha}^{\dagger}})^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{C_{p}T}\int\gamma_{\hbar}(\alpha)((\hbar^{1/2}+|\alpha|)(1+|\alpha|^{4D}\hbar^{-2D}))^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha

\displaystyle{\,\lesssim\,}Ce^{C_{p}T}\hbar^{p/2}.

In the second to last line we have used the bound (
4.2
) from Theorem
4.4
.

5.2.
Proof of Corollary
1.2
from Theorem
1.1

Let
G
satisfy
\partial_{a}G\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\frac{1}{2}}
for all
a\in[2D]
.
It suffices to show that for any density matrix
\rho
,

(5.1)

|\operatorname*{trace}[\rho({\mathcal{U}}_{T}(\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(G))-\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(\Phi_{T}G))]|\leq e^{CT}\hbar^{1/2}.

In the special case
p=1
, Theorem
1.1
combined with Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality implies

\Big|\int G({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}\rho}-\Phi_{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\Big|\leq e^{CT}\hbar^{1/2}.

Using Wigner-Weyl duality and the fact that
{\mathrm{H}}_{\rho}={\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}\ast\gamma_{\hbar}
, this inequality
implies

|\operatorname*{trace}[\rho({\mathcal{U}}_{T}(\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(G\ast\gamma_{\hbar}))-\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}((\Phi_{T}G)\ast\gamma_{\hbar}))]|\leq e^{CT}\hbar^{1/2}.

Thus to prove (
5.1
) it suffices to show that

\|\,{\mathcal{U}}_{T}[\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(G\ast\gamma_{\hbar}-G)]\|_{\rm op}+\|\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(\Phi_{T}G\ast\gamma_{\hbar}-\Phi_{T}G)\|_{\rm op}\leq e^{CT}\hbar^{1/2}.

Observe that
{\mathcal{U}}_{T}
preserves the operator norm, so a bound on each of the terms on the left hand side
will then follow from the following estimate (which is a slightly stronger form of the Calderón-Vaillancourt theorem):

\|\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(F)\|_{\rm op}{\,\lesssim\,}\|F\|_{C^{0}}+\hbar^{(2D+1)/2}\|\partial^{2D+1}F\|_{C^{0}}\,.

From the Lipschitz bound on
G
we have

\|G-G\ast\gamma_{\hbar}\|_{C^{0}}{\,\lesssim\,}\hbar^{1/2}\|\partial G\|_{C^{0}}\,,

and in general for higher order derivatives we have

\|\partial^{k}G-\partial^{k}G\ast\gamma_{\hbar}\|_{C^{0}}{\,\lesssim\,}\hbar^{1/2}\|\partial^{k+1}G\|_{C^{0}}{\,\lesssim\,}\hbar^{1/2}\hbar^{-k/2}.

Thus, for example, it follows that

\|\,{\mathcal{U}}_{T}[\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(G\ast\gamma_{\hbar}-G)]\|_{\rm op}=\|\mathrm{Op}_{\hbar}(G\ast\gamma_{\hbar}-G)\|_{\rm op}{\,\lesssim\,}\hbar^{1/2}.

A similar estimate applies to
\Phi_{T}G-\Phi_{T}G\ast\gamma_{\hbar}
, up to a loss of
e^{(2D+1)CT}
.

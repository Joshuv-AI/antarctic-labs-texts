Transcriptome analysis reveals the mechanism of anthocyanin accumulation induced by leaf breakage in foxtail millet
Authors: Yaqiong Li, Huixia Feng, Meng Li, Mingzhen Yang, Xiaoxia Meng, Ming Duan, Hongying Li, Yaofei Zhao, Bin Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAnthocyanin is a class of water-soluble pigments with antioxidant activity which enhances the stress tolerance of plants. The destruction of vascular bundles may lead to the accumulation of anthocyanin; however, the response mechanism on this phenomenon in foxtail millet is not well understood.ResultsIn this study, a foxtail millet variety Hong Miao Qi Tou Hong (HMQTH) which displayed increasing anthocyanin accumulation after leaf breakage was investigated. Through transcriptome analysis the gene expression pattern induced by leaf breakage in HMQTH was identified. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were mainly enriched in processes such as stimulus response, antioxidant function and flavonoid biosynthesis. Both the results of RNA-seq and qRT-PCR showed a total of 18 differential genes related to stress response and anthocyanin biosynthesis pathways were identified, including 7 genes involved in flavonoid and anthocyanin synthesis, 6 genes which were related to stress response and 5 genes regulating phytohormone synthesis and ABA signaling.ConclusionThis study uncovered the relationship between plant response to wounding and anthocyanin accumulation in foxtail millet, and provided genetic resources for the molecular breeding of crops with high anthocyanin content.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-025-07806-x.

Background

Anthocyanin, a group of water-soluble natural pigments found in many plant tissues, is believed to have health-promoting effects, such as preventing cardiovascular diseases [1–3], and relieving oxidative stress [4, 5]. For plants, anthocyanin also performs significant roles. They can provide flowers and fruits with blue, purple, and red colors that attract animals to pollinate and disperse seed [6]. In addition, the synthesis of anthocyanin can protect plants against many biotic or abiotic stresses. For example, pathogen infection, drought, salt, cold, wound and high light intensity can affect anthocyanin accumulation in many plant species [7–16]. The synthesis of anthocyanin has been well elucidated. Anthocyanin is biosynthesized via a series of enzymes, such as chalcone synthase (CHS), chalcone isomerase (CHI), flavanone 3-hydroxylase (F3H), dihydroflavonol 4-reductase (DFR), anthocyanidin synthase (ANS), and UDP-3-O-glucosyltransferases (UFGT/3GT) [17–19]. In addition, anthocyanin biosynthetic genes are transcriptionally regulated by the MBW complexes consisting of three protein families, basic-helix-loop-helix (bHLH), R2R3-MYB, and WD-repeat (WDR) proteins [20, 21].

Plants are continually exposed to mechanical damage, which can be caused by various factors such as herbivore feeding, wind, and rain. After wounding, plants trigger multiple signals, such as systemin [22], oligosaccharides [23], ROS [24], jasmonates [25], ethylene [26], and abscisic acid [27] to repair the wound, activate defense mechanisms, and regulate plant growth [28]. Leaf breakage can lead to the destruction of vascular bundles, resulting in the obstruction of sugar transport. Sugar not only serves as an energy source in plants, but also acts as a signaling molecule to regulate gene expression, especially in the biosynthesis pathways of flavonoids and anthocyanin [29, 30]. In Arabidopsis, disruption of the sucrose transporter AtSUC1 abolishes exogenous sucrose-induced anthocyanin accumulation, and intracellular sucrose sensing is essential for both sucrose-induced AtSUC1 expression and subsequent anthocyanin biosynthesis. This supports that sucrose accumulation triggers anthocyanin production as an alternative metabolic sink when sucrose efflux is impaired [31, 32]. In maize, carbohydrate distribution deficiency mutants such as cpd28, cpd47, Cpd1, tdy1, and sxd1 exhibit enhanced accumulation of anthocyanin because of soluble sugar accumulation due to the blockage of sucrose export from mature leaves [33–36]. Anthocyanin has antioxidant activity, and its accumulation may play an important role in responding to wounding. A few previous reports suggested that wounding stress can also affect anthocyanin accumulation [37, 38]. MdWRKY40 has been shown to be an important factor modulating wounding-induced anthocyanin accumulation through interaction with MdMYB1 and enhancement of its binding to the promoters of anthocyanin biosynthetic genes in apple [39]. RrMYB5 and RrMYB10 were found to regulate anthocyanin and proanthocyanidin accumulation in response to wounding in Rosa rugosa and tobacco [40].

Foxtail millet (Setaria italica) is an important cereal and forage crop widely cultivated in Asia. It has become a C4 model plant due to its short life cycle, self-pollination, extensive genetic diversity, highly efficient transformation and a compact genome [41, 42]. In addition, foxtail millet has excellent drought tolerance and good adaptability. However, the wound induced response remains unclear, and very little is known about the function of anthocyanin in adaptation in foxtail millet. In our previous study, we found that a purple-leaved variety of foxtail millet that contained more anthocyanin than Yugu1 with green leaves, had a higher antioxidant capacity and accumulated more soluble sugar, which implied some relationship between anthocyanin and stress responses in foxtail millet (Zhao et al., 2022). In the present study, two representative varieties YG1 (short for Yugu1, a modern cultivar in China) and HMQTH (short for Hong Miao Qi Tou Hong, a Chinese landrace) were selected to analyze the wounding-induced anthocyanin accumulation, physiological indexes, and transcriptome sequencing analysis. We found that anthocyanin content in HMQTH increased after wounding stress caused by leaf breakage, and showed stronger ability of ROS scavenging compared to YG1. Further analysis revealed that leaf breakage induced the expression of genes related to anthocyanin biosynthesis, ABA signaling and stress responding. Collectively, our results provide insights into the mechanism of anthocyanin accumulation upon wounding-stress in foxtail millet.

Methods

Plant materials, growth conditions and treatments

Two foxtail millet varieties HMQTH and YG1 used in this study were provided by the Institute of Agricultural Bioengineering, Shanxi Agricultural University. HMQTH and YG1 were sown in the experimental plot located at 37 °N, 112 °E. During the ripening stage, the second leaf from the top was gently creased every 2 cm from the base to the tip using fingers to induce breakage of the vascular bundles, and samples were collected at different time points after treatment. Each sample only requires one leaf. Grind leaves with liquid nitrogen into powder, and weigh the corresponding mass of the ground tissue according to experimental requirements.

Measurement of pigment content

For anthocyanin content measurement, 0.5 g leaves tissue of each biological replicate was extracted with 10 mL acidic methanol (0.1% hydrochloric acid) and the extraction and measurement of anthocyanin were performed by method described previously [43]. The standard curve and regression equations are shown in Supplementary Fig. 1.

Measurements of photosynthetic pigment content were performed by the method described previously [44]. 0.2 g leaves tissue of each biological replicate was sampled and grinded with liquid nitrogen. To each sample, 10 mL 95% ethanol was added and shaken (160 rpm) until the tissue turned white in an opaque shaker. After centrifugation (Eppendorf 5804R, 8000 rpm, 10 min), the supernatant was taken, and the absorption values were determined at 470 nm, 649 nm and 665 nm using 95% ethanol solution as reference. Physiological indexes assays

The content MDA, proline, total soluble sugar, ROS and the activity of SOD and POD were measured according to kit instructions. These kits were produced by BOXBIO (Beijing China; https://www.boxbio.cn/) and the product number of these kits were as follows.

Malondialdehyde (MDA) Content Assay Kit: AKFA013M.

Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) Assay Kit: AKCE002.

Superoxide Dismutase (SOD) Activity Assay Kit: AKAO001M.

Peroxidase (POD) Activity Assay Kit: AKAO005M.

Proline (Pro) Content Assay Kit: AKAM003M.

Plant Soluble Sugar Content Assay Kit: AKPL008M.

Transcriptome sequencing and analyses

Three biological replicates of each time point before or after leaf breakage of HMQTH were used in RNA-sequencing. The quality control of raw data was performed as in a previous study [45]. The clean data were aligned to the foxtail millet genome (Setaria italica v2.2: https://phytozome-next.jgi.doe.gov/info/Sitalica_v2_2) using HISAT2 software [46], and the mapped reads were analyzed by String Tie software [47]. DEGs identification and enrichment analysis were performed as described in a previous study [48]. Differential expression analysis of two conditions/groups (two biological replicates per condition) was performed using the DESeq2 R package (1.20.0). DESeq2 provide statistical routines for determining differential expression in digital gene expression data using a model based on the negative binomial distribution. The resulting P-values were adjusted using the Benjamini and Hochberg’s approach for controlling the false discovery rate. Genes with an adjusted P ≤ 0.05 found by DESeq2 were assigned as differentially expressed.

Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes was implemented by the clusterProfiler R package, in which gene length bias was corrected. GO terms with corrected P value less than 0.05 were considered significantly enriched by differentially expressed genes. KEGG is a database resource for understanding high-level functions and utilities of the biological system, such as the cell, the organism and the ecosystem, from molecular-level information, especially large-scale molecular datasets generated by genome sequencing and other high-through put experimental technologies (http://www.genome.jp/kegg/). We used clusterProfiler R package to test the statistical enrichment of differentially expressed genes in KEGG pathways.

Quantitative real-time PCR analysis

Total RNA extraction was performed with an Ultrapure RNA Kit (CWBio, CW0581). First-strand cDNA from 2 mg of total RNA was synthesized using a FastQuant RT Kit (Tiangen, KR118). Real-time PCR assays were performed as previously described [49]. The 2^-ΔΔCt method was used to calculate the expression level of the genes. Primers used in this study are displayed in Supplementary Table 4.

Statistical analysis

Significance analysis and correlation analysis were performed with IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows version 19.0 (IBM Corp, Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

Anthocyanin accumulation was induced with different degrees by leaf breakage in foxtail millet varieties

In previous studies, we observed that leaves, stems and panicles of different foxtail millet varieties displayed multiple colors. Notably, certain varieties showed purple tissues attributed to the accumulation of anthocyanin. Anthocyanin is not only a class of secondary metabolites with high nutritional value, but also can be utilized in antioxidant activities by scavenging excess reactive oxygen species (ROS) for improving plant resistance to cold and other abiotic stresses. Crops cultivated in open field are susceptible to wounding stress such as leaf breakage caused by wind or animals; therefore, we wondered whether anthocyanin accumulated after wounding stress in foxtail millet.

To explore this question, we selected two representative varieties YG1 and HMQTH to analyze the variation of wounding-induced accumulation of anthocyanin. At the maturation stages, the second leaf of foxtail millet was creased to cause damage to the vascular elements. After treatment, the leaf of HMQTH gradually changed from green to purple above the crease, and the whole leaf turned purple after treatment for 42 h. However, there was no obvious change in the leaf color of YG1 (Fig. 1A). To uncover whether the alteration in leaf color resulted from variations in anthocyanin accumulation, we measured anthocyanin content at different time points after injury treatment. The results showed that the anthocyanin content in HMQTH was significantly higher than that of YG1 at each time point after treatment (Fig. 1B). On the other hand, when the leaves were not subjected to breakage, there is no significant difference in anthocyanin content between the two varieties within 42 h (Supplementary Fig. 2).

Wounding affected the content of photosynthetic pigment in foxtail millet

There are a variety of pigments in plants including anthocyanin, and photosynthetic pigments that hold paramount importance for plant growth and development. Following wounding treatment, HMQTH accumulated more anthocyanin than YG1, prompting an investigation into whether there is a concomitant difference in photosynthetic pigment levels between the two foxtail millet varieties. To investigate this question, we measured the contents of chlorophyll and carotenoid in YG1 and HMQTH. The results revealed a consistent variation pattern of chlorophyll a/b and carotenoid contents of the two varieties. It was observed that the contents of photosynthetic pigments showed an initial decrease followed by an increase after wounding treatment. There was no significant difference in the contents of all photosynthetic pigments in the two varieties before treatment. After wounding treatment, the contents of chlorophyll a/b and carotenoid were decreased significantly in the early stage (Fig. 2). All photosynthetic pigments in HMQTH were accumulated less than that in YG1 at 12 h after wounding. Subsequently, the contents of these pigments gradually increased. At 24 h after wounding, the contents of chlorophyll a and carotenoid in HMQTH were significantly higher than that in YG1. HMQTH accumulated much more chlorophyll b than YG1 at 36 h after wounding treatment, and both the contents of chlorophyll a and chlorophyll b in HMQTH were significantly higher than those in YG1 at 42 h after wounding treatment.

Wounding affected the antioxidant and stress-resistant indexes

An increase in anthocyanin content has been shown to enhance plant tolerance to abiotic stresses, and HMQTH exhibited a substantial accumulation of anthocyanin. We wondered whether there were differences in other stress resistance indexes between these two varieties, in addition to anthocyanin content. Malondialdehyde (MDA) is the final product of cell membrane lipid peroxidation which is toxic to cells by triggering the polymerization and crosslinking of proteins and other biological macromolecules [50]. We found the MDA content of HMQTH was significantly lower than that of YG1 at all time points except the difference was not obvious at 18 h after treatment (Fig. 3A).

Reactive oxygen species (ROS) play a crucial role in plant signal transduction; however, the timely removal of a substantial amount of ROS generated under stress conditions is pivotal [51]. Therefore, the efficient elimination of ROS serves as an important indicator to gauge the robustness of plant stress resistance. We found the ROS in YG1 increased gradually after treatment, while the ROS in HMQTH increased first and then decreased. Although the level of ROS was initially higher in comparison to YG1, HMQTH exhibited a reduced accumulation of ROS after 24 h of treatment (Fig. 3B). As peroxidase (POD) and superoxide dismutase (SOD) are key enzymes in the ROS scavenging system [52], we detected the activities of these two enzymes in foxtail millet. The results showed that the activity of POD and SOD in HMQTH were significantly higher than those in YG1 (Fig. 3C and D).

Proline and soluble sugar play pivotal roles in plant cells against stresses by bolstering their capacity to withstand osmotic stress. The results revealed that the contents of proline and soluble sugar in HMQTH were significantly higher than that in YG1 before and after wounding treatment (Fig. 3E and F).

RNA-seq analysis reveals differentially expressed genes (DEGs) induced by wounding in HMQTH

To further reveal the causes of anthocyanin accumulation and tolerance enhancement induced by wounding stress, the leaf tissues from HMQTH before treatment (0 h) and 12, 18, 24, 36, 42 h after treatment were collected for RNA extraction and transcriptome sequencing. At each time point, we sampled three biological replicates. Through RNA sequencing, we obtained at least 22 million clean reads containing 6.7 Gb nucleotides for each sample after filtration. The mapping rate of each sample was more than 80% and the clean reads which reached the Q30 level exceeded 91.36% (Supplementary Table 1).

Next, we conducted a comparative analysis of transcriptome data at 12 h, 18 h, 24 h, 36 h and 42 h after leaf breakage with the data before treatment (0 h). As shown in Fig. 4A, there were 2595, 2621, 776, 3433, and 2668 up-regulated DEGs and 2607, 2831, 674, 3404 and 3145 down-regulated DEGs respectively in each group. All functionally annotated DEGs were detailed in Supplementary Table 5. Furthermore, we discovered the common genes among these five DEG groups and the Venn diagram showed 779 genes were included in all five DEG groups (Fig. 4B). Meanwhile, we also performed cluster analysis to uncover the differences in gene expression patterns of the five DEG groups (Fig. 4C).

Functional analysis of DEGs by enrichment analysis

To further analyze the function of DEGs and discover the key genes related to anthocyanin accumulation and stress tolerance, we conducted enrichment analysis. First, enrichment analysis of DEGs was performed based on Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis. In the comparisons of 12 h vs. 0 h, 18 h vs. 0 h, 24 h vs. 0 h, 36 h vs. 0 h, and 42 h vs. 0 h, 1,612, 1,692, 446, 2,253, and 1,859 differentially expressed genes were annotated, respectively. These genes were involved in 122, 121, 102, 124, and 121 metabolic pathways, respectively (Supplementary Table 6). As shown in Fig. 5, the significant enrichment mainly included alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism, phenylalanine metabolism, phenylalanine, tyrosine and tryptophan biosynthesis, arginine and proline metabolism, flavonoid biosynthesis (anthocyanin synthesis regulatory genes are included in the regulation of flavonoid biosynthesis pathways), carotenoid biosynthesis, porphyrin and chlorophyll metabolism, photosynthesis-antenna proteins, photosynthesis, plant hormone signal transduction, amino sugar and nucleotide sugar metabolism, and diterpenoid biosynthesis. Beyond damage response, gene expression dynamics in foxtail millet were concurrently modulated by environmental variables including temperature fluctuations, light alterations, and circadian regulation. Crucially, the 24 h vs. 0 h comparison exhibited the fewest DEGs. With the exception of the 24 h vs. 0 h comparison (Fig. 5C), all temporal contrasts showed significant enrichment in circadian rhythm pathways (Figs. 5A, B, D, E). This distinct profile suggests that the DEGs identified specifically in the 24 h vs. 0 h comparison experience minimal confounding influences from periodic biological oscillators. Consequently, this temporally isolated gene set warrants prioritized investigation as it may disproportionately harbor key regulators underlying phenotypic divergence between the studied varieties. In addition, we also performed enrichment analysis of DEGs based on Gene Ontology (GO) analysis. Among the 22 biological process categories, differentially expressed genes were mainly enriched in metabolic process, cellular process, single-organism process, response to stimulus, and biological regulation. Among the 15 cell component categories, the proportion of the DEGs in cell part and cell was the highest; among the 14 molecular function categories, the proportion of antioxidant activity was 0.90% for 37 genes, 1.13% for 49 genes, 0.96% for 11 genes, 1.15% for 63 genes, and 0.93% for 43 genes (Supplementary Fig. 3).

Screening and validation of key candidate genes related to anthocyanin accumulation and stress responses

By combining the results of functional enrichment analysis and expression patterns of DEGs, we identified 22 candidate genes associated with stress response as well as anthocyanin synthesis and accumulation (Supplementary Tables 2 and 3). To validate the precision of the transcriptome data, we detected the expression patterns of candidate genes through qRT-PCR. First, we detected the expression patterns of candidate genes encoding key enzymes of flavonoid and anthocyanin synthesis pathway including SiPAL1 (Seita.1G240200), SiPAL2 (Seita.7G168700), SiCYP450 (Seita.3G327400), SiDFR (Seita.5G237900), SiLDOX (Seita.4G054600), SiGT (Seita.2G324600) and Si4CL (Seita.9G537200). The results showed all these candidate genes were significantly upregulated within 24 h after treatment (Fig. 6).

Discussion

High photosynthetic efficiency and excellent adaptability to the environment make foxtail millet an ideal model system to investigate the mechanism of stress resistance. Some studies indicated that leaves turned red or purple in response to cold, salinity because foliar anthocyanin accumulated rapidly, and the degree of abiotic stress tolerance can often be predicted based on the abundance of anthocyanin [63, 64]. The research on anthocyanin in foxtail millet mainly focus on the synthesis regulation [65], and the relationship between anthocyanin accumulation and stress tolerance is seldom studied. HMQTH is a Chinese landrace, while YG1 is a modern breeding variety. During the breeding process, YG1 may have lost some key genes regulating anthocyanin accumulation. In the future, constructing hybrid populations and performing gene cloning may help further elucidate the genetic basis of phenotypic differences between the two varieties.

ROS are common by-products produced under stresses and leads to the accumulation of anthocyanin [66]. The production of excess ROS is one of the major damages induced by wounding and anthocyanin with natural antioxidant activity might participate in plant responses to wounding like leaf breakage. Consistent with our hypothesis, HMQTH, a foxtail millet variety displayed purple leaves due to the accumulation of large amounts of anthocyanin, showed stronger ability of ROS scavenging compared with YG1 which accumulated less anthocyanin. Meanwhile, our previous study also indicated antioxidant capacity of another purple-leaved foxtail millet variety was higher than green-leaved variety [48]. ROS attack unsaturated fatty acids on the cell membrane, triggering lipid peroxidation reactions that produce MDA. Anthocyanin, as antioxidants, can reduce the accumulation of peroxides and reduce the attack of peroxides on the cell membrane, thereby reducing the accumulation of MDA. In our study, HMQTH can accumulate more anthocyanin than YG1 after leaf breakage, while the MDA content is lower than that of YG1. In summary, we propose that rapid anthocyanin accumulation under stress may represent an adaptive strategy of foxtail millet to cope with complex environments. Additionally, sucrose accumulation caused by vascular bundle damage serves as a key substrate for anthocyanin biosynthesis. We observed that the HMQTH cultivar accumulated higher levels of soluble sugars following leaf injury. The rapid increase in anthocyanin content post-damage in this variety may be associated with dysregulation of the sugar signaling pathway.

Under stress conditions, anthocyanin plays a necessary role in scavenging excess ROS which is beneficial to plant growth [67]. For example, upregulation of genes encoding transcription factors or biosynthesis genes involved in anthocyanin biosynthesis enhanced the cold tolerance by reducing ROS in Brassica rapa and Arabidopsis thaliana [10, 68]. It was also reported the salt stress induced expression of the anthocyanin biosynthesis genes [69]. Mechanical breakage caused by abiotic or biotic factors is a common phenomenon which leads to growth inhibition of plants. However, the study of underlying mechanisms in plant responding to wounding focuses on plant-insect interaction. Our results showed leaf breakage induced the expression level of biosynthesis genes like DFR and PAL to produce more anthocyanin for ROS homeostasis. In addition, we also found purple-leaved variety accumulated more photosynthetic pigment after leaf breakage and it was indicated by other studies that anthocyanin could prevent chlorophyll degradation to maintain photosynthetic capacity [66]. HMQTH, which can accumulate more anthocyanins, exhibits higher content in photosynthetic pigment after leaf breakage. Therefore, scavenging excess ROS and maintaining photosynthetic pigment stability depending on anthocyanin accumulation might be a strategy for the improvement of mechanical wounding resistance of foxtail millet.

Leaf breakage could promote anthocyanin accumulation, and we hope to reveal the specific pathways in which stress signals induce anthocyanin accumulation. As our study was performed in field conditions, not in a greenhouse, it is important to note that gene expression levels are influenced by ambient light, temperature, and the plant’s inherent circadian rhythms in addition to the changes in gene expression caused by leaf breakage. After 24 h, these factors are most similar to those before treatment, and factors other than damage have the least impact on gene expression patterns. This may be the reason for the minimum number of DEGs in 24 h.

The results of KEGG analysis indicated many DEGs enriched in plant hormone signaling at almost every time point we detected and some studies have proved that plant hormones were involved in anthocyanin synthesis. Auxin negatively regulates anthocyanin synthesis in some plants. For example, auxin inhibits anthocyanin accumulation by degrading the MdIAA26 protein and ethylene response factor (ERF) MdERF1B which promote anthocyanin accumulation by regulating MdMYB9 and MdMYB11 in apple [70, 71]. These transcription factors involved in anthocyanin biosynthesis are themselves subject to precise regulation. For example, in Arabidopsis, the inhibitory effect of MYB30 on MYB75 can be relieved by MYB75-induced RHA2b expression, thereby ensuring the self-activation of MYB75 when anthocyanin synthesis is required [72]. Transcriptomic analysis in this study identified SiMYBS3 (Seita.5G216800), a differentially expressed gene encoding a MYB transcription factor, whose expression exhibited significant downregulation following leaf damage (Supplementary Fig. 5). While these findings suggest SiMYBS3 may play a regulatory role in anthocyanin-related gene expression, definitive confirmation of its function requires further validation through transgenic approaches.

Abscisic acid (ABA) is an essential plant hormone involved in stress responses and ABA treatment has positive regulatory effects on anthocyanin synthesis [73, 74]. In this study, we observed that genes related to ABA synthesis and signal transduction were upregulated following leaf breakage, while SiUGT74E2, which functions in auxin synthesis, was downregulated. These results might indicate that mechanical wounding activated ABA signaling and inhibited auxin signaling, thereby promoting anthocyanin accumulation to improve plant tolerance to stress. On the other hand, wounding caused by insect biting activate the jasmonic acid (JA) signaling pathway and JA can directly ubiquitinate JAZ protein to regulate transcription factors in anthocyanin metabolism pathway [75]. But few DEGs associated with JA pathway were detected which might indicate that different types of wounds activate different defense pathways.

The carbohydrates produced by plant photosynthesis need to be transported in a timely manner. If the vascular bundle develops abnormally or the leaves are damaged, the carbohydrates will accumulate in the leaves, interfering with the normal physiological activities of the plant. Previous studies have shown that plants usually convert excess sugar into anthocyanin [34, 36, 76]. Our research also shows that HMQTH can rapidly produce and accumulate anthocyanin under leaf damage conditions. This may be due to the rapid activation of the responsive conversion reaction by HMQTH, and the rapid upregulation of anthocyanin synthesis genes. This variety can be used as a good research material for studying sugar conversion.

Conclusion

Taken together, we described the dynamic transcriptome regulatory network of HMQTH, a foxtail millet with high ability of ROS scavenging leaf breakage by promoting accumulating anthocyanin. Over-accumulation of sugar or other unknown signals promotes the accumulation of anthocyanin, which can improve the antioxidant capacity of plants (a working model is displayed in Supplementary Fig. 6). Our results provide further insight into the mechanism of responses to stress in foxtail millet.

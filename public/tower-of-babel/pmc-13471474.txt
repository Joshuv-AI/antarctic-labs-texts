Comprehensive multi-omics pan-cancer analysis revealed that ANTXR1 is a potential biomarker for diagnosis and immunotherapy
Authors: Yue Qiu, Zhu Yu, Weikun Lai, Wenqian Xu, Jian Yang, Sijiang Zhou, Junqiang Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAnthrax toxin receptor 1 (ANTXR1, TEM8) has been implicated in tumor angiogenesis and progression, yet its pan-cancer immunological role remains incompletely defined.MethodsWe conducted an integrative pan-cancer analysis using multi-omics and clinical data from TCGA, GTEx, and public databases to evaluate the expression, prognostic and diagnostic value, genomic features, immune associations, and drug sensitivity of ANTXR1. Single-cell and spatial transcriptomic analyses were performed to define its cellular and spatial distribution. Functional roles were validated in gastric cancer (GC) using in vitro and in vivo models.ResultsANTXR1 was aberrantly expressed across multiple tumor types and significantly associated with tumor stage and unfavorable prognosis, particularly in GC. Diagnostic analyses demonstrated high accuracy of ANTXR1 in several malignancies. Elevated ANTXR1 expression correlated with genomic instability, stromal enrichment, M2 macrophage infiltration, and reduced CD8⁺ T cell abundance, indicating an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. Single-cell and spatial analyses identified fibroblasts as the primary source of ANTXR1 with co-localization to extracellular matrix components. Functionally, ANTXR1 knockdown suppressed GC cells proliferation, migration, invasion, and tumor growth, inhibited M2 macrophage polarization, and enhanced CD8⁺ T cell activity, partly through PI3K/AKT pathway inhibition.ConclusionANTXR1 functions as a stromal-associated immunomodulatory driver of tumor progression and immune suppression, representing a promising biomarker and therapeutic target in stromal-rich cancers.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13062-026-00856-7.

Introduction

Cancer remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide, with approximately 20 million new cases and 9.7 million deaths reported in 2022 [1]. Tumor initiation and progression are complex processes driven by multiple factors, including intrinsic malignant evolution and dynamic interactions with the tumor microenvironment (TME) [2–4]. Extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling, angiogenesis, and immune cell infiltration play pivotal roles in promoting tumor growth, invasion, and metastasis [5, 6].

Anthrax Toxin Receptor 1 (ANTXR1), also known as Tumor Endothelial Marker 8 (TEM8), is a highly conserved type I transmembrane protein encoded by the TEM8 gene [7]. Recent studies have highlighted its multifunctional role in tumor angiogenesis, invasion, cancer stemness, and immune regulation, positioning ANTXR1 at the center of tumor biology research [8–10].

Elevated ANTXR1 expression has been reported in multiple malignancies, including breast, gastric, colorectal, and lung cancers, and is associated with enhanced invasiveness, metastasis, therapeutic resistance, and poor prognosis [11–14]. In triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), ANTXR1 is expressed in both tumor cells and tumor vasculature, and its knockdown inhibits tumor-induced angiogenesis [8]. In colorectal cancer (CRC), tumor-associated fibroblast-derived lactate promotes ANTXR1 transcription and lactylation, activating the RhoC/ROCK1/Smad5 pathway to enhance stemness and oxaliplatin resistance [13]. In gastric cancer (GC), ANTXR1 accelerates progression via the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway [12].

Emerging evidence indicates that ANTXR1 also modulates the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) [10, 15]. It can serve as a target for immune redirection strategies. For example, tri-specific killer engagers recognizing ANTXR1, tumor/stromal components, and T/NK cells significantly enhance cytotoxicity against ANTXR1-positive tumors [16]. CAR-T therapies targeting ANTXR1 have shown potent anti-tumor effects in TNBC models [11], and structural studies of ANTXR1 binding with oncolytic virus SVV-001 suggest potential for tumor-selective viral therapies [17].

Overall, ANTXR1 has evolved from a classical tumor vascular marker to a key immune-related molecule with roles in immune infiltration, immune evasion, vascular normalization, and immunotherapy.

In this study, we performed a comprehensive pan-cancer bioinformatics analysis of ANTXR1, assessing its expression, clinical relevance, mutation profile, prognostic value, drug sensitivity, functional enrichment, and immune interactions. Subsequent in vitro and in vivo experiments validated its impact on GC progression and M2 macrophage polarization, providing insights into its diagnostic, prognostic, and therapeutic potential across cancers.

Methods

Data collection

RNA-seq data and clinical information were obtained from UCSC Xena [18], and normal tissue expression from GTEx. ANTXR1 expression in immune cells was evaluated using TISCH2 [19]. Gastric adenocarcinoma single-cell RNA-seq datasets (GSE167297, GSE134520, GSE26899, GSE54129) were downloaded from GEO [20, 21].

Expression and clinical correlation

ANTXR1 expression across 33 cancers was assessed via TIMER 2.0 [22]. Integrated TCGA and GTEx datasets were analyzed using SangerBox to evaluate differential expression and correlations with TNM stage [23].

Survival prognosis analysis

OS, DSS, and PFI were analyzed using SangerBox [23]. ROC curves for pan-cancer diagnostic performance were generated using the R pROC package.

Genetic alterations and RNA modification

Mutation and copy number alteration data were obtained from cBioPortal, covering 10,967 tumors from 10,593 patients [24]. DNA damage repair, RNA methylation (m1A, m5C, m6A), and stemness were analyzed via CancerSEA and SangerBox [23, 25]. Promoter methylation was assessed using UALCAN [26].

Immune infiltration and drug sensitivity

Relationships with immune modulators, Stromal/Immune/ESTIMATE scores, and immune cell populations were evaluated via SangerBox [23]. Drug sensitivity was analyzed using oncoPredict, stratifying samples by median ANTXR1 expression.

Single-cell and spatial analysis

TISCH2 was used to profile ANTXR1 expression across cell types [19], and STOmics DB was employed for spatial co-localization with COL1A1, COL1A2, and FN1 [27, 28].

Co-expression and functional enrichment

Top correlated genes were identified via LinkedOmics and visualized with heatmaps [29]. GO and KEGG enrichment, along with PPI network construction, were performed using STRING [30].

Clinical samples

Clinical samples used in this study were archived, de-identified specimens obtained from the institutional tissue biobank of the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University. These samples were originally collected during routine clinical procedures between March and September 2025. Due to anonymization and data availability limitations, detailed clinicopathological characteristics were not available for all cases. Samples were frozen at − 80 °C or formalin-fixed and paraffin-embedded. Ethical approval and informed consent were obtained.

Cell culture

AGS, MKN-28, and THP-1 cells were maintained in appropriate culture media supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin/streptomycin at 37 °C in 5% CO₂. And 0.05 mM β-mercaptoethanol will be additionally added to the THP-1 cells culture medium.

Western blot

Cells were lysed using RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, China) supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitors. Protein concentrations were determined using a BCA Protein Assay Kit (Beyotime, China). Equal amounts of protein (15–20 µg per lane) were separated by SDS–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and transferred onto PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked with 3% BSA in TBST for 1 h at room temperature and then incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against ANTXR1, GAPDH, AKT, p-AKT, PI3K, and p-PI3K. The list of relevant antibodies is presented in Table S1. After washing with TBST, membranes were incubated with appropriate HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) detection kit (Tanon, China) and imaged with a gel imaging system.

Functional assays

Wound healing, Transwell migration/invasion, and CCK-8 proliferation assays were performed following standard protocols.

Flow cytometry experiments

THP-1 cells were treated with PMA (150 ng/mL) for 24 h to induce macrophage differentiation, followed by co-culture with conditioned media from GC cells for 48 h. Cells were then collected, blocked with Fc Receptor Blocking Solution (BioLegend, USA), and stained with antibodies against CD14, CD68, CD163, and CD206. Macrophage polarization was analyzed using a BD FACSVerse flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, USA). For T cell functional assays, peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated using Lymphocyte Separation Medium (Solarbio, China) and used directly without further purification. Cells were stimulated with anti-CD3 (2 µg/mL) and anti-CD28 (1 µg/mL) for 48 h, followed by co-culture with conditioned media for an additional 48 h. Cells were then subjected to Fc receptor blocking and stained with antibodies against CD45, CD3, and CD8. After fixation and permeabilization (Invitrogen, USA), intracellular IFN-γ was stained and analyzed by flow cytometry using a BD FACSVerse system (BD Biosciences, USA). CD8⁺ T cells were identified based on surface marker expression during flow cytometric analysis. The list of relevant antibodies is presented in Table S1. Flow cytometry data were analyzed using FlowJo software (version 10.8).

In vivo xenografts

Male C57BL/6 mice (6 weeks old) were obtained from Henan Sikebeis Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Henan, China), a licensed laboratory animal supplier. All animals were housed under specific pathogen-free (SPF) conditions with controlled temperature and humidity and a 12-h light/dark cycle, with ad libitum access to food and water. ANTXR1-silenced YTN-16 cells were injected subcutaneously into C57BL/6J mice. Mice were humanely euthanized at the experimental endpoint. Prior to euthanasia, mice were deeply anesthetized with pentobarbital sodium (50 mg/kg, intraperitoneal injection) until complete loss of consciousness was confirmed by the absence of reflex responses. Euthanasia was then performed by cervical dislocation, in accordance with institutional and national guidelines for the care and use of laboratory animals. Tumor growth, weight, and immune cell composition were measured.

Bioinformatics and statistical analysis

All analyses were performed using R software (version 4.5.3). Differential expression analysis was conducted using the limma package. ROC analysis was performed using pROC. Correlation analyses between gene expression and immune infiltration were assessed using the psych package (version 2.1.6). Survival analysis was performed using the survival package (version 3.2-7) with the Cox proportional hazards model (coxph function). Drug sensitivity prediction was conducted using oncoPredict, with limma used for preprocessing. Data visualization was performed using ggplot2and ggpubr. Data are presented as mean ± SD. Comparisons between two groups were performed using Student’s t-test, and multiple group comparisons were conducted using ANOVA. Correlations were evaluated using Pearson or Spearman coefficients. A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Pan-cancer expression and clinical relevance of ANTXR1

Pan-cancer analyses based on TIMER 2.0 and TCGA/GTEx datasets demonstrated that ANTXR1 was aberrantly expressed across multiple tumor types, with significant upregulation in gastrointestinal, head and neck, and renal cancers, and downregulation in subsets of urogenital and endocrine malignancies (Fig. 1A–C). Further clinicopathological analysis revealed that ANTXR1 expression was significantly associated with TNM stage in several cancers, including esophageal carcinoma (ESCA), stomach and esophageal carcinoma (STES), stomach adenocarcinoma (STAD), pan-kidney cohort (KIPAN), Thymoma (THYM), and bladder urothelial carcinoma (BLCA) (Fig. 1D), indicating a potential role in tumor progression.

Prognostic value of ANTXR1 in pan-cancer

Cox regression analyses revealed that elevated ANTXR1 expression predicted unfavorable overall survival, disease-specific survival, and progression-free interval in multiple malignancies, particularly STAD, BLCA, thyroid carcinoma (THCA), uveal melanoma (UVM), pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD) and adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC) (Fig. 2A–C). In contrast, low ANTXR1 expression was associated with poor prognosis in glioma (GBMLGG), suggesting tumor-specific prognostic heterogeneity.

Diagnostic value of ANTXR1 in pan-cancer analysis

ROC curve analysis demonstrated that ANTXR1 exhibited significant diagnostic performance across multiple cancer types, including cervical squamous cell carcinoma and endocervical adenocarcinoma (CESC), cholangiocarcinoma (CHOL), colon adenocarcinoma (COAD), esophagus adenocarcinoma (ESAD), GBMLGG, glioblastoma multiforme (GBM), head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSC), kidney chromophobe (KICH), kidney renal papillary cell carcinoma (KIRC), skin cutaneous melanoma (SKCM), STAD, THCA, and THYM (Fig. 3A–N). Notably, ANTXR1 showed excellent diagnostic accuracy in CHOL, GBMLGG, and GBM, with AUC values exceeding 0.90, highlighting its potential as a diagnostic biomarker in these malignancies.

Genetic alterations of ANTXR1 in pan-cancer

Genomic analysis revealed that mutation was the predominant form of ANTXR1 alteration, with R397C representing the most frequent variant (Fig. 4A–B). Missense mutations were the dominant SNV type, particularly in gastrointestinal tumors (Fig. 4C–D). The pan-cancer analysis of copy number variations (CNVs) revealed distinct CNV patterns of ANTXR1 across different tumor types, including recurrent amplifications and gain, highlighting its potential role in tumor heterogeneity (Fig. 4E). ANTXR1 expression showed significant correlations with Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB), Microsatellite Instability (MSI), Homologous Recombination Deficiency (HRD), and Loss of Heterozygosity (LOH) across multiple cancers, indicating a close association with genomic instability (Fig. 4F).

ANTXR1 is associated with epigenetic regulation and tumor stemness-related features

Comprehensive analysis revealed that ANTXR1 was broadly involved in multiple tumor-related functional states (Fig. 5A), suggesting a potential role in tumor progression. We next explored the relationship between ANTXR1 and epigenetic regulatory mechanisms. Notably, ANTXR1 expression showed a predominantly positive correlation with RNA methylation regulators, including m¹A, m⁵C, and m⁶A modification-related genes across most cancer types (Fig. 5B), indicating that ANTXR1 may participate in post-transcriptional regulatory processes that influence tumor cell behavior. In addition, promoter methylation analysis demonstrated that ANTXR1 exhibited decreased methylation levels in 9 cancer types and increased methylation in 4 cancer types compared with normal tissues (Fig. 5C), suggesting that epigenetic regulation may contribute to the dysregulated expression of ANTXR1 in tumors. Furthermore, stemness analysis showed that ANTXR1 expression was negatively correlated with DNAss and RNAss in the majority of tumors, while positive correlations were observed in a limited number of cancer types (Fig. 5D–E). These findings imply that ANTXR1 may be involved in modulating tumor cell differentiation states, although its role appears to be context-dependent. Collectively, these results indicate that ANTXR1 is closely associated with epigenetic modification and tumor stemness-related features, highlighting its potential role in tumor heterogeneity and progression.

Association between ANTXR1 and the tumor immune microenvironment

ANTXR1 expression showed broad positive correlations with immune regulatory genes across cancers (Fig. 6A). Stromal, Immune and ESTIMATE score analyses consistently demonstrated that elevated ANTXR1 expression was associated with enhanced stromal and immune infiltration in multiple tumor types (Fig. 6B–D). Furthermore, ANTXR1 expression was positively correlated with both inhibitory and stimulatory immune checkpoint molecules (Fig. 6E). Immune cell deconvolution analysis indicated that ANTXR1 expression was positively associated with M2 macrophages and resting CD4 memory T cells, but negatively correlated with CD8⁺ T cells, suggesting an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (Fig. 6F). Subsequently, we further used the MCP-counter, xCell, EPIC, and QUANTISEQ algorithms for immune infiltration analysis. The results indicated that, similar to the previous analysis results, in various tumors, the expression of ANT1XR was positively correlated with M2 macrophages and negatively correlated with CD8⁺ T cells (Fig. S1).

Functional enrichment analysis of ANTXR1 in gastric cancer

GC was selected as the representative model for downstream functional validation because ANTXR1 exhibited consistent overexpression, strong prognostic significance, and robust associations with immune infiltration and stromal remodeling in STAD across multiple datasets. Co-expression analysis using LinkedOmics identified genes positively and negatively correlated with ANTXR1 expression, with the top 50 genes in each category displayed as heatmaps (Fig. 7A–B), and additional correlation data shown in Fig. 7C. A protein–protein interaction network constructed using STRING revealed extensive interactions among ANTXR1-associated genes (Fig. 7D). Gene Ontology enrichment analysis indicated that these genes were primarily involved in extracellular matrix organization, extracellular structure organization, and structural molecule activity (Fig. 7E). KEGG pathway analysis further demonstrated significant enrichment in focal adhesion, PI3K–Akt signaling pathway, and ECM–receptor interaction (Fig. 7F).

Association between ANTXR1 expression and drug sensitivity in gastric cancer

To evaluate the therapeutic relevance of ANTXR1, drug sensitivity analysis was performed in GC using the oncoPredict algorithm. The results showed that tumors with low ANTXR1 expression exhibited increased sensitivity to multiple agents, including YK-4-279, AT13148, Sorafenib, Osimertinib, PD-0325901, Dinaciclib, Taselisib, IGF1R-3801, PCI-34,051, OTX015, Talazoparib, IAP_5620. In contrast, high ANTXR1 expression was associated with enhanced sensitivity to 5-Fluorouracil, Temozolomide, NVP-ADW742, LY2109761, Obatoclax, OF-1 and GDC0810 (Fig. 8A–S), indicating that ANTXR1 expression may influence therapeutic response and drug stratification in GC.

Single-cell and spatial transcriptomic characterization of ANTXR1 in gastric cancer

Single-cell RNA sequencing data from two independent STAD datasets (GSE167297 and GSE134520) were analyzed using TISCH2. UMAP-based clustering revealed distinct cellular populations within the tumor microenvironment (Fig. 9A–C, E–F). Violin plot analysis demonstrated that ANTXR1 expression was predominantly enriched in fibroblasts, whereas its expression was markedly lower in CD8⁺ T cells (Fig. 9D, G). Spatial transcriptomic analysis using STOmics DB further revealed spatial co-localization of ANTXR1 with fibroblast markers COL1A1, COL1A2, and FN1, suggesting coordinated expression within the stromal compartment of GC (Fig. 9H).

ANTXR1 protein expression and localization in gastric cancer

Protein-level expression and subcellular localization of ANTXR1 were evaluated using the Human Protein Atlas database. The results indicated that ANTXR1 protein was primarily localized in vesicular structures (Fig. 10A–B). At the transcriptomic level, consistent upregulation of ANTXR1 in GC was further validated in two independent GEO cohorts (GSE26899 and GSE54129) (Fig. 10C), supporting the robustness of its aberrant expression in clinical samples. Consistent with these findings, ANTXR1 expression was elevated in multiple GC cell lines compared with the normal gastric epithelial cell line GES-1, with cell line–specific heterogeneity observed (Fig. 10D). Analysis of paired GC tissues further demonstrated that ANTXR1 protein expression was increased in tumor tissues compared with adjacent non-tumor tissues, as confirmed by both western blotting and immunohistochemistry (Fig. 10E-F).

ANTXR1 knockdown suppresses gastric cancer cell proliferation, migration, and invasion in vitro

Efficient silencing of ANTXR1 was achieved in AGS and MKN-28 cells (Fig. 11A). Functional assays demonstrated that ANTXR1 knockdown significantly impaired wound closure, indicating reduced migratory capacity (Fig. 11B). Transwell assays further revealed that both cell migration and invasion were markedly suppressed following ANTXR1 depletion (Fig. 11C–D). In addition, CCK-8 assays showed that cell proliferation was significantly reduced in ANTXR1-silenced cells compared with controls (Fig. 11E).

ANTXR1 knockdown inhibits M2 macrophage polarization and enhances CD8⁺ T cell function in vitro

Based on the observed association between ANTXR1 expression and immune cell infiltration, the effects of ANTXR1 on macrophage polarization and T-cell function were further investigated. Conditioned medium derived from ANTXR1-silenced GC cells significantly reduced the expression of M2 macrophage markers CD206 and CD163, indicating suppressed M2 polarization (Fig. 12A–B). Moreover, co-culture of peripheral blood–derived CD8⁺ T cells with conditioned medium from ANTXR1-knockdown cells resulted in increased IFN-γ expression, suggesting enhanced cytotoxic T-cell activity (Fig. 12C). Consistent with the pathway enrichment analysis, functional experiments further demonstrated that ANTXR1 regulates the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway in GC cells. Specifically, ANTXR1 knockdown significantly decreased the phosphorylation levels of PI3K and AKT, whereas ANTXR1 overexpression led to increased levels of p-PI3K and p-AKT in GC cells (Fig. 12D). These results collectively indicate that ANTXR1 positively regulates activation of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway. Given the established role of the PI3K/AKT pathway in macrophage polarization and T cell dysfunction, these findings suggest that ANTXR1 may promote immunosuppressive remodeling of the gastric cancer microenvironment at least partly through PI3K/AKT signaling.

ANTXR1 knockdown suppresses tumor growth and modulates the immune microenvironment in vivo

To validate the in vitro findings, a subcutaneous xenograft model was established using murine YTN-16 cells. Tumors derived from ANTXR1-silenced cells exhibited significantly reduced growth, as reflected by decreased tumor volume and weight compared with negative control tumors (Fig. 13A–C). Flow cytometric analysis of tumor-infiltrating immune cells demonstrated that ANTXR1 knockdown suppressed M2 macrophage polarization and enhanced IFN-γ expression in CD8⁺ T cells within the tumor microenvironment (Fig. 13D–E), confirming the in vivo immunomodulatory effects of ANTXR1. Moreover, immunofluorescence analysis also confirmed that ANTXR1 knockdown suppressed M2 macrophage polarization (Fig. 13F).

Discussion

Tumor progression is increasingly understood as a dynamic and context-dependent process driven by continuous interactions between malignant cells and the tumor immune microenvironment rather than by tumor-intrinsic alterations alone [31, 32]. In this study, we performed a systematic pan-cancer analysis of ANTXR1 to delineate its expression landscape, prognostic relevance, genomic features, and immune-associated characteristics across multiple tumor types. Our findings extend the conventional perception of ANTXR1 as a tumor endothelial marker and instead support a broader functional role in stromal remodeling and immune regulation. By integrating bulk multi-omics analyses with single-cell and spatial transcriptomic data, we demonstrate that ANTXR1 is preferentially enriched in fibroblast populations and closely associated with extracellular matrix organization. Importantly, functional experiments in GC further substantiate a causal role for ANTXR1 in promoting tumor progression through the establishment of an immunosuppressive microenvironment characterized by enhanced M2 macrophage polarization and impaired CD8⁺ T-cell function, at least partly mediated by activation of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway. Together, these results position ANTXR1 as an active immunomodulatory molecule rather than a passive stromal marker.

ANTXR1 (TEM8) has previously been implicated in angiogenesis, stromal remodeling, and tumor progression in several malignancies. Earlier studies showed that targeting ANTXR1 suppresses pathological angiogenesis and tumor growth [8], and elevated ANTXR1 expression has been associated with poor clinical outcomes and increased stromal infiltration in bladder and gastric cancers [15, 33, 34]. In addition, TEM8-directed CAR T-cell strategies have demonstrated antitumor activity in triple-negative breast cancer by simultaneously targeting tumor cells and tumor-associated vasculature [11]. Mechanistically, ANTXR1 has been linked to Wnt/β-catenin signaling in lung adenocarcinoma and to PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway activation in GC [12, 35]. These pathways represent central oncogenic programs that cooperatively regulate proliferation, survival, metabolic adaptation, stemness, and epithelial–mesenchymal transition, while also contributing to immune evasion and therapeutic resistance when aberrantly activated [36–38]. However, most prior studies were restricted to single tumor contexts or relied largely on correlative evidence, leaving unresolved whether ANTXR1 exerts a conserved immunological function across cancers.

By contrast, our study integrates pan-cancer transcriptomic and prognostic analyses with immune infiltration profiling, single-cell and spatial transcriptomics, and experimental validation, thereby providing multi-level evidence that ANTXR1 actively participates in shaping an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. Notably, our data indicate that ANTXR1 promotes M2 macrophage polarization and suppresses CD8⁺ T-cell effector function through PI3K/AKT signaling. This integrative framework reduces the likelihood that ANTXR1 merely reflects stromal abundance and instead supports a functional role in modulating immune cell behavior within the tumor niche.

Consistent with this interpretation, functional assays demonstrate that ANTXR1 knockdown significantly impairs GC cells proliferation, migration, and invasion in vitro and markedly reduces tumor growth in vivo. These phenotypic effects are accompanied by decreased phosphorylation of PI3K and AKT, indicating attenuation of pathway activity following ANTXR1 silencing. In parallel, suppression of ANTXR1 limits M2 macrophage polarization while enhancing CD8⁺ T-cell cytotoxicity, providing mechanistic insight into how ANTXR1 links stromal signaling to immune suppression. While angiogenesis-related functions of ANTXR1 have been previously reported, our findings expand its biological scope by directly connecting ANTXR1 to immune regulation and tumor–immune interactions.

Single-cell and spatial transcriptomic analyses further strengthen this conclusion by revealing that ANTXR1 expression is predominantly localized to fibroblast populations and spatially co-distributed with extracellular matrix components such as COL1A1, COL1A2, and FN1. This spatial pattern provides a cellular basis for ANTXR1-mediated stromal–immune crosstalk and offers a mechanistic explanation for its influence on macrophage polarization and T-cell dysfunction. Importantly, the concordance between computational predictions and experimental validation supports the robustness of our conclusions and mitigates concerns regarding dataset-specific bias. The immunomodulatory role of ANTXR1 identified here has direct therapeutic implications. Our results indicate that ANTXR1 contributes to immune evasion by reinforcing M2 macrophage polarization and limiting CD8⁺ T-cell activity, consistent with emerging evidence that stromal–immune interactions critically determine tumor progression and response to therapy [39–41]. Notably, ANTXR1-targeted approaches, including CAR T cells and tri-specific immune engagers, have shown promising preclinical efficacy by simultaneously targeting tumor cells and the stromal compartment [11, 16]. By demonstrating that ANTXR1 functions as a conserved immunomodulatory node across multiple tumor types, our study provides a strong rationale for further exploration of ANTXR1-directed therapies, either alone or in combination with immune checkpoint blockade, particularly in tumors characterized by extensive stromal remodeling and immune suppression.

Beyond therapeutic relevance, our pan-cancer analyses also underscore the prognostic and diagnostic value of ANTXR1. Elevated ANTXR1 expression is consistently associated with adverse overall survival, disease-specific survival, and progression-free intervals in several cancers, while ROC analyses reveal high diagnostic performance in CHOL, GBMLGG, and GBM. Integration of genomic alterations, DNA methylation patterns, and RNA modification signatures further refines the context-dependent roles of ANTXR1 and supports its potential utility in precision oncology.

Several limitations merit consideration. First, although functional validation in GC provides mechanistic support, additional studies are required to determine whether similar immune-regulatory mechanisms operate in other tumor types. Second, while our analyses integrate multi-omics, single-cell, and spatial data, in vivo evaluation of ANTXR1-targeted interventions remains limited to preclinical models. Third, due to incomplete clinicopathological data in the analyzed cohorts, multivariate Cox regression analysis could not be reliably performed, and therefore the independent prognostic value of ANTXR1 requires further validation in well-annotated clinical datasets. Finally, the upstream regulators of ANTXR1 and the precise molecular circuitry linking ANTXR1 to PI3K/AKT-mediated immune modulation warrant further investigation, including potential crosstalk with other oncogenic and immune-related pathways.

In summary, our study establishes ANTXR1 as a pan-cancer immunomodulatory molecule that integrates stromal remodeling with immune suppression and demonstrates clear functional relevance in GC. By combining large-scale computational analyses with experimental validation, we provide a robust conceptual and mechanistic framework that supports ANTXR1 as a promising biomarker and therapeutic target, thereby laying the groundwork for future translational and immunotherapeutic studies.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study presents a comprehensive pan-cancer characterization of ANTXR1 and establishes its central role in coordinating stromal remodeling and immune suppression within the tumor microenvironment. Integrative analyses incorporating multi-omics datasets, single-cell sequencing, and spatial transcriptomics reveal that ANTXR1 is predominantly expressed in fibroblasts and closely associated with extracellular matrix organization.

Clinically, elevated ANTXR1 expression correlates with unfavorable prognosis, increased stromal infiltration, enhanced M2 macrophage polarization, and impaired CD8⁺ T-cell activity across multiple cancer types. Functional experiments in GC further demonstrate that ANTXR1 promotes tumor growth and invasive behavior while attenuating antitumor immunity, at least in part through activation of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway.

Collectively, these findings reposition ANTXR1 from a conventional tumor endothelial marker to a key stromal-associated immunomodulatory molecule and underscore its potential utility as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker, as well as a therapeutic target, particularly in tumors characterized by pronounced stromal enrichment and immune evasion.

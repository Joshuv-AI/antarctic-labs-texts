Adelic descent for continuous localizing invariants
Grigorii Konovalov

Abstract
. We prove a version of adelic descent for continuous localizing invariants.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{Z}
. Recall from
[
1
]
,
Theorem 4.2.4, that solid quasi-coherent sheaves on
X
satisfy adelic descent. Namely, for any tuple
0\leq k_{0}<\ldots k_{m}\leq d
,
where
d
denotes the dimension of
X
, we have the corresponding adelic ring

\begin{split}L_{k_{0}}\ldots L_{k_{m}}\mathcal{O}_{X}\;\simeq\;\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}_{m}\in X\\
\mathrm{dim}\overline{\{\mathfrak{p}_{m}\}}=k_{m}\end{subarray}}{\mathrel{{\mathop{\text{\phantom{a}}}\limits}}\!\!}\>(\ldots\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}_{1}\in\overline{\{\mathfrak{p}_{2}\}}\\
\mathrm{dim}\overline{\{\mathfrak{p}_{1}\}}=k_{1}\end{subarray}}{\mathrel{{\mathop{\text{\phantom{a}}}\limits}}\!\!}\>(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}_{0}\in\overline{\{\mathfrak{p}_{1}\}}\\
\mathrm{dim}\overline{\{\mathfrak{p}_{0}\}}=k_{0}\end{subarray}}{\mathrel{{\mathop{\text{\phantom{a}}}\limits}}\!\!}\>\mathcal{O}_{X,\mathfrak{p}_{0}})_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}\ldots)_{\mathfrak{p}_{m}}\;\in\;\mathrm{CAlg}(\text{QCoh}(X_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}))\;,\end{split}

where
{\mathrel{{\mathop{\text{\phantom{a}}}\limits}}\!\!}\>(-)_{\mathfrak{p}}
denotes the functor given by localizing at the prime
\mathfrak{p}\in X
first and then competing at it.
In fact, the formation of adelic rings defines a functor

\textstyle{[0<1]^{d}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{CAlg}(\text{QCoh}(X_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}))}
\textstyle{0\leq k_{0}<\ldots k_{m}\leq d\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrel{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\in$}}}
\textstyle{L_{k_{0}}\ldots L_{k_{m}}\mathcal{O}_{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrel{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\in$}}}
.

In particular, we get a symmetric monoidal functor

(1.0.0.1)

\text{QCoh}(X_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}})\longrightarrow\mathrel{{\mathop{\lim}\limits}}L_{k_{0}}\ldots L_{k_{m}}\mathcal{O}_{X}-\mathrm{Mod}_{\text{QCoh}(X_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}})}\;,

which happens to be an equivalence—see Theorem 4.2.4 of
[
1
]
.

All of the categories appearing in (
1.0.0.1
) are compactly generated, but they are not suitable for
studying localizing invariants because the category
\text{QCoh}(X_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}})^{\omega}
satisfies a version of the Eilenberg swindle. Namely,
for any fixed object
P\in\text{QCoh}(X_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}})^{\omega}
, there exists a countable product
\prod_{\mathbb{N}}P\in\text{QCoh}(X_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}})^{\omega}
.
Nevertheless, Efimov showed (
[
2
]
)
that any localizing invariant uniquely extends from the category of compactly generated stable categories to
the category of presentable dualizable stable categories, and
Clausen and Scholze defined (8th lecture in
[
3
]
, 13th lecture in
[
4
]
)
the category of nuclear modules over an analytic ring
(more generally, nuclear objects in a symmetric monoidal category), which is a presentable stable dualizable category and is
expected to produce correct localizing invariants in many situations including those coming from formal geometry.
For a certain nice class of localizing invariants such as K-theory, that expectation was proven in

[
5
]
(Theorem 7.9) in the case of a ring completion.
It therefore should be reasonable to study localizing invariants of the adeles using the nuclear modules of Clausen-Shcolze.

In this paper, we prove that the diagram

\textstyle{[0<1]^{d}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{dual}}_{\mathrm{st}})}
\textstyle{0\leq s_{0}<s_{1}<\ldots<s_{m}\leq d\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrel{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\in$}}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Nuc}(L_{s_{0}}\ldots L_{s_{m}}\mathcal{O}_{X},X_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrel{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\in$}}}
,

which we basically get from the diagram (
1.0.0.1
) by passing to nuclear objects,
is mapped to a limit diagram by any stable localizing invariant, see Theorem
3.0.1
.
We also prove that this diagram is a limit diagram
in both
\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{st}})
and
\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{dual}}_{\mathrm{st}})
, see Theorem
3.4.1
.
The latter in fact follows directly from
Theorem 4.2.4 of
[
1
]
by passing to rigidification, but the argument
presented in this paper is in a sense independent of that theorem.

We note that versions of adelic descent for localizing invariants have already been recorded in the literature,
see
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
. In the former paper, the author
studies localizing invariants of perfect modules over the adeles and proves the adelic descent in that context. In the latter
paper, the author does all the algebra within the category
\text{QCoh}(X)-\mathrm{Mod}(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{dual}}_{\mathrm{st}})
and proves a version
of adelic descent for localizing invariants of dualizable categories. Namely, for a category
\mathcal{C}\in\text{QCoh}(X)-\mathrm{Mod}(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{dual}}_{\mathrm{st}})
,

[
7
]
constructs a diagram consisting of products iterated completed localizations of
\mathcal{C}

computed inside
\text{QCoh}(X)-\mathrm{Mod}(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{dual}}_{\mathrm{st}})
and proves the descent statement for it, see Theorem 1.4 of loc. cit..
On the other hand, our approach is to do all the algebra inside
\text{QCoh}(X_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}})

(which has already been done in
[
1
]
)
and see what kind statement about localizing invariants we can get as an output.

At the categorical level, the difference between our version and Kim’s version (
[
7
]
)
is in a sense similar to the difference between Clausen-Scholze’s category of nuclear modules and Efimov’s category of
nuclear modules in the case of a ring completion,
which is well explained in the introduction to
[
5
]
.
That difference may disappear after passing
to a nice localizing invariant—like it does in the case of a ring completion,
see Theorem 7.9 in
[
5
]
—but that seems to need a proof.

The paper is structured as follows.

In the second section, we summarize some results about nuclear objects and derive some relevant corollaries about nuclear modules
over the adelic rings. In the thrid section, we state and prove out main theorem.

1.1.
Notation and conventions

1.1.1.

In this paper, we work in the setting of
\infty
-categories. We adopt the usual convention that the word category means

(\infty,1)
-category, and all the standard categorical notions and constructions, such as functors and (co)limits, should be
understood in this context. In particular, we shall typically refer to derived completion simply as completion.

1.1.2.

We will denote by
\Pr^{L}_{\mathrm{st}}
the
(\infty,1)
-category of stable presentable categories and continuous
(colimit preserving) functors.
We will denote by
\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{dual}}_{\mathrm{st}}
the
(\infty,1)
-category of stable presentable dualizable categories and strongly continuous functors.

1.1.3.

By default, all limits of (stable presentable) categories are computed inside
\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{st}}
. Limits computed inside

\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{dual}}_{\mathrm{st}}
will be denoted by
\mathrel{{\mathop{\lim}\limits}}
.

1.1.4.

Because we need the results of Appendix A,
[
1
]
,
we work in finite type over
\mathbb{Z}
. It is also possible to work in finite type over a countable field
using the ultrasolid vector spaces over the field as the base category.

1.1.5.

We use notation of
[
1
]
for completions. It is summarized in the next four points.

1.1.6.

Let
Z\subseteq\spec(A)
be a closed subset defined by an ideal
I\subset A
. We will use
A^{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\wedge$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\wedge$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$}}}}}_{I}

to denote the (derived) completion of
A
at the ideal
I
.
The same goes for the completion of a (solid) module over
A
.
We note that the functor of completion
(-)^{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\wedge$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\wedge$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$}}}}}_{I}
at the ideal
I\subset A
only depends on the closed subset
Z\subseteq\spec(A)
, but
not on the ideal
I
cutting out
Z
. To emphasize that, and for other reasons, we will often call the functor of completion
at
I
the functor of completion at
Z
and denote it
(-)^{\overset{\sim}{\!\!\phantom{\ldots}}}_{Z}
.

1.1.7.

More generally, for a specialization closed subset
T\subseteq\spec(A)
(which can be thought of as a subset equal to the union
of all its subsets which are closed in
\spec(A)
), we will define a completion at
T
and denote it
(-)^{\overset{\sim}{\!\!\phantom{\ldots}}}_{T}
,
see section 1.4 in
[
1
]
.

1.1.8.

Similarly, for a generalization closed subset
E\subset\spec(A)
, we will define a completion at
E
and denote it

(-)^{\overset{\sim}{\!\!\phantom{\ldots}}}_{E}
, see section 1.7 in
[
1
]
.
This will turn out to be equivalent to the completion
with respect to the ultrasolid analytic ring structure on the ring

\Gamma(E,\mathcal{O})\;\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\;\mathrel{{\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}\limits}}\Gamma(U,\mathcal{O})\;,

where the colimit is taken over all Zariski open subsets
U\subseteq\spec(A)
containing
E
and ordered by (opposite) inclusion.
See Definition 1.1.5.2 in
[
1
]

for the definition of the ultrasolid analytic ring structure.

1.1.9.

For a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subset A
, we will denote by
{\mathrel{{\mathop{\text{\phantom{a}}}\limits}}\!\!}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
the local ring
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
completed at the maximal ideal.
Similarly, for any (solid)
A
-module
M
,
{\mathrel{{\mathop{\text{\phantom{a}}}\limits}}\!\!}\>M_{\mathfrak{p}}
denotes the localization of
M
at
\mathfrak{p}
followed by the (derived) completion
at the maximal ideal of
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

1.1.10.

We use Lurie’s notation for categories of modules over an algebra as opposed to the traditional derived category notation. For example,

\mathrm{Mod}_{A}
means the whole stable
(\infty,1)
-category of modules over the algebra
A
. Likewise,
\mathrm{Mod}_{A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}}
denotes
the stable
(\infty,1)
-category of solid
A
-modules.

This also applies to categories of quasi-coherent sheaves over a scheme:
\text{QCoh}(X)
means the stable
(\infty,1)
-category of
quasi-coherent complexes over
X
;
\text{QCoh}(X_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}})
means the stable
(\infty,1)
-category of solid quasi-coherent sheaves over
X
.

1.1.11.

For concreteness, we work in light setting of the condensed mathematics, see
[
8
]
, Lecture ??.
Specifically, this means that,
for a commutative algebra
A
of finite type over
\mathbb{Z}
, the category of solid
A
-modules
\mathrm{Mod}_{A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}}
is compactly generated
by a single compact generator

\prod_{\mathbb{N}}A\;\in\;\mathrm{Mod}^{\omega}_{A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}}\;.

1.1.12.

Given an analytic ring
\mathcal{B}
, we will use both
\mathcal{B}-\mathrm{Mod}
and
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{B}}
to denote the category
of complete
\mathcal{B}
-modules. There should usually be no room for confusion.

1.1.13.

To make our notation less cumbersome,
given a commutative algebra object
B\in\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Mod}_{A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}})
in the symmetric monoidal category
\mathrm{Mod}_{A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}}
, we will
often use
B-\mathrm{Mod}_{A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}}
(as opposed to something like
B-\mathrm{Mod}(\mathrm{Mod}_{A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}})
) to denote the category of

-modules inside

\mathrm{Mod}_{A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}}
.

1.1.14.

Given an analytic ring
\mathcal{B}
, we will denote the category of nuclear modules over
\mathcal{B}
by
\mathrm{Nuc}(\mathcal{B})
,
see Lecture 8 in
[
3
]
for the definition. Given a finite type scheme

and a ring

2.
Preliminaries

This section contains some technical preliminaries, mostly dealing with categories of nuclear modules over the adelic rings.

2.1.

Here we summarize part of the discussion from
[
5
]
, section 1.5.

2.1.1.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a compactly generated stable symmetric monoidal category whose monoidal unit is compact.
We now recall from
[
3
]
, Lecture 8, the definition of the full subcategory
\mathrm{Nuc}(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathcal{C}
of nuclear objects.
Recall that a map
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)
is called trace-class if it lies in the image of the natural map

\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(1_{\mathcal{C}},x^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathcal{C}}y)\;\longrightarrow\;\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)\;.

An object
M\in\mathcal{C}
is called nuclear if any map
f\colon P\longrightarrow M
from a compact object
P\in\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
is trace-class.
The full subcategory
\mathrm{Nuc}(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathcal{C}
is defined as consisting of nuclear objects. It follows from the definition that the
subcategory of nuclear objects is closed under colimits. Moreover, by Proposition 13.13 of
[
4
]
and
Theorem 2.39 of
[
9
]
, the category
\mathrm{Nuc}(\mathcal{C})
is
\omega_{1}
-presentable
and dualizable. Finally, in case the subcategory of compact objects
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\subset\mathcal{C}
is closed under the tensor product,
the category
\mathrm{Nuc}(\mathcal{C})
inherits the symmetric monoidal structure from
\mathcal{C}
(e.g. follows from Proposition 13.13 of
[
4
]
).

2.1.2.

We will also make a slight use of the functor of rigidification
(-)^{\mathrm{rig}}

(see Construction 4.75 and Theorem 4.77 of
[
10
]
),
which is the right adjoint to the fully faithful inclusion
\mathrm{CAlg}^{\mathrm{rig}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{st},\kappa})
for an uncountable
\kappa
.
It is easily seen (Propositions 1.23 and 1.27 of
[
5
]
)
that, under the hypothesis of section
2.1.1
, the rigidification
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rig}}
is a naturally a full subcateory in
\mathrm{Nuc}(\mathcal{C})
.
The following proposition summarizes part of the discussion from
[
5
]
, section 1.5,
which will provide a description for the category of nuclear modules over certain analytic rings.

Proposition 2.1.3

(
[
5
]
, Propositions 1.28-1.34;
[
11
]
, Lemma 3.32)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a small symmetric monoidal stable category such that, for every
P\in\mathcal{D}
, the object
P^{\mathrm{op},\vee}\in\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}})

is nuclear. Then the following holds:

(1)

the inclusion

\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}})^{\mathrm{rig}}\;\subseteq\;\mathrm{Nuc}(\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}}))

is an equivalence;

(2)

the inclusion

\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{D})^{\mathrm{rig}}\;\subseteq\;\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{D})

admits a continuous symmetric monoidal right adjoint, and the composite

\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{D})\;\longrightarrow\;\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{D})^{\mathrm{rig}}\;\simeq\;\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}})^{\mathrm{rig}}\;\hookrightarrow\;\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}})

maps an object
P\in\mathcal{D}
to
P^{\mathrm{op},\vee}\in\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}})
;

(3)

the inclusion

\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}})^{\mathrm{rig}}\;\subseteq\;\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}})

also admits a symmetric monoidal continuous right adjoint.

Corollary 2.1.4
.

Under the hypothesis of the previous proposition, the category
\mathrm{Nuc}(\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}}))
is generated under colimits by objects of the form

P^{\mathrm{op},\vee}
with
P
varying over the objects of
\mathcal{D}
.

2.2.

Fix a commutative ring
A
of finite type over
\mathbb{Z}
. Recall from
[
1
]
,
section 1.4.5, that whenever we have a specialization closed subset
T\subseteq\spec(A)
, we can define the functor

(-)^{\overset{\sim}{\!\!\phantom{\ldots}}}_{T}\colon\mathrm{Mod}_{A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}}\;\longrightarrow\;\mathrm{Mod}_{A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}}

Likewise, whenever we have a generalization closed subset

, we have the functor

(-)^{\overset{\sim}{\!\!\phantom{\ldots}}}_{E}\colon\mathrm{Mod}_{A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}}\;\longrightarrow\;\mathrm{Mod}_{A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}}

of completion at
E
, see section 1.7 in
[
1
]
.
The latter is isomorphic to the functor

-\otimes_{A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}}\Gamma(E,\mathcal{O})_{\bullet}\;,

where
\Gamma(E,\mathcal{O})_{\bullet}
denotes the ultrasolid analytic ring structure on the ring

\Gamma(E,\mathcal{O})\;\simeq\;\mathrel{{\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}\limits}}\Gamma(U,\mathcal{O})\;,

see section 1.7.3 in
[
1
]
.

Now we would like to study nuclear modules over the solid commutative ring
A^{\overset{\sim}{\!\!\phantom{\ldots}}}_{T,E}\in\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Mod}_{A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}})
.
We note that it is idempotent over
A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}

\begin{split}A^{\overset{\sim}{\!\!\phantom{\ldots}}}_{T,E}\otimes_{A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}}A^{\overset{\sim}{\!\!\phantom{\ldots}}}_{T,E}&\;\simeq\;\bigl(A^{\overset{\sim}{\!\!\phantom{\ldots}}}_{E}\otimes_{A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}}A^{\overset{\sim}{\!\!\phantom{\ldots}}}_{E}\bigr)^{\overset{\sim}{\!\!\phantom{\ldots}}}_{T}\;\simeq\\
&\;\simeq\;\bigl(\Gamma(E,\mathcal{O})\otimes_{A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}}\Gamma(E,\mathcal{O})\bigr)^{\overset{\sim}{\!\!\phantom{\ldots}}}_{T}\;\simeq\;\\
&\;\simeq\;\bigl(\Gamma(E,\mathcal{O})\bigr)^{\overset{\sim}{\!\!\phantom{\ldots}}}_{T}\;=\;A^{\overset{\sim}{\!\!\phantom{\ldots}}}_{T,E}\;,\end{split}

where we use Corollary A.2.7 of
[
1
]
to deal with the completion at
T
.
The next lemma claims that the hypothesis of Proposition
2.1.3
holds for the category
A^{\overset{\sim}{\!\!\phantom{\ldots}}}_{T,E}-\mathrm{Mod}_{A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}}
.

Lemma 2.2.1
.

Fix a specialization closed subset
T\subseteq\spec(A)
and a generalization closed subset
E\subseteq\spec(A)
.
The object

\uhom_{A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}}\Bigl(\prod_{\mathbb{N}}A,A^{\overset{\sim}{\!\!\phantom{\ldots}}}_{T,E}\Bigr)\;\simeq\;\Bigl(\bigoplus_{\mathbb{N}}A\Bigr)^{\overset{\sim}{\!\!\phantom{\ldots}}}_{T,E}\;\in\;A^{\overset{\sim}{\!\!\phantom{\ldots}}}_{T,E}-\mathrm{Mod}_{A_{\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\blacksquare$}}}}}}

is nuclear.

Proof.

,

Because we a have commutative diagram

3.5.
General filtration by specializing subsets

In fact, the relevant part of the argument almost without modification applies to the following more general situation.
Given a filtration

Theorem 3.5.1
.

The cube (
3.5.0.1
) is a limit cube in both
\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{st}}

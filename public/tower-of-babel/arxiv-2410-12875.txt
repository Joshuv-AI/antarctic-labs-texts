Abstract
. We revisit the method of a a -contraction with shifts used for long-time behavior of barotropic Navier-Stokes flows perturbed from a Riemann shock. For the usage of the method of a a -contraction with shifts, we do not employ the effective velocity h h variable even for higher order estimates. This approach would be important when handling the barotropic Navier-Stokes system with other effects, for example, such as capillary effect and boundary effect.

1.
Introduction

The method of
a
-contraction (with shifts) has been extensively used for orbital stability of shock waves measured by the weighted (by
a
) relative entropy up to dynamical shifts.
This method was developed by the second author and Vasseur
[
9
,
23
]
for the contraction property of perturbations of extremal shocks in the hyperbolic system of conservation laws, especially for the Euler system. That was extended to the viscous hyperbolic conservation laws: first for the scalar equation in 1D case
[
10
]
(for more general case
[
7
]
), and then in multi-D case
[
14
,
8
]
. As the first extension of the method to the Navier-Stokes system, Kang-Vasseur
[
11
]
prove the contraction of any large disturbances for a single viscous shock. It is shown in
[
12
]
that this contraction estimate is uniform in the strength of viscosity, and ensures the existence of inviscid limits on which the associated Riemann shock is orbitally stable (see also
[
13
]
).
Surprisingly, the method of
a
-contraction with shifts has been used in studies of models in other contexts
[
1
,
2
,
5
]
.

The
a
-contraction method is also used for time-asymptotic stability of Navier-Stokes (NS) flows slightly perturbed in
H^{s}(s\geq 1)
from Riemann data (generating Riemann shock).
The result
[
15
]
shows the long-time behavior of the barotropic NS system towards composition of shock and rarefaction, and the result by Wang-Wang
[
24
]
handles the behavior of multi-D perturbation towards a single shock. Its extension to the case of generic Riemann solution for the Navier-Stokes-Fourier system was studied in
[
16
]
.

In this paper, we revisit the
a
-contraction method used in the proof for the long-time behavior towards a viscous shock for the one-dimensional barotropic Navier-Stokes (NS) equations, written in the Lagrangian mass coordinates (for simplicity):

(1.1)

\displaystyle v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle u_{t}+p(v)_{x}=\left(\mu(v)\frac{u_{x}}{v}\right)_{x},

where the unknown functions
v=v(t,x)
and
u=u(t,x)
represent the specific volume and velocity of fluid, respectively.
The pressure
p=p(v)
is assumed to satisfy the
\gamma
-law, that is,

p(v)=bv^{-\gamma},\quad b>0,\quad\gamma>1.

The initial data of the NS system (
1.1
) is given by
(v_{0},u_{0})
, whose far-field states are prescribed as constants:

(1.2)

\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}(v_{0}(x),u_{0}(x))=(v_{\pm},u_{\pm}).

Based on hueristic argument in
[
17
]
, the time-asymptotic profile of the NS equations is related to the Riemann solution to the Euler equations:

(1.3)

\displaystyle v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle u_{t}+p(v)_{x}=0,

subject to the Riemann initial data

(1.4)

(v(0,x),u(0,x))=\begin{cases}(v_{-},u_{-}),&x<0,\\
(v_{+},u_{+}),&x>0.\end{cases}

We focus on the case when the end states
(v_{\pm},u_{\pm})
are connected by a single Hugoniot curve. Without loss of generality, we only handle the case of a 2-shock curve. In other words, for a given right-end state
(v_{+},u_{+})
, we consider the left-end state
(v_{-},u_{-})
satisfying the following Rankine-Hugoniot conditions:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\sigma(v_{+}-v_{-})-(u_{+}-u_{-})=0,\\
-\sigma(u_{+}-v_{-})-(p(v_{+})-p(v_{-}))=0,\end{cases}\quad\sigma:=\sqrt{-\frac{p(v_{+})-p(v_{-})}{v_{+}-v_{-}}}>0,

and the entropy condition
v_{-}<v_{+},\quad u_{-}>u_{+}
.

Then the Riemann solution
(\overline{v},\overline{u})
to the Euler equations (
1.3
)–(
1.4
) is given by 2-shock wave

(1.6)

(\overline{v}(t,x),\overline{u}(t,x))=\begin{cases}(v_{-},u_{-})&\text{if}\quad x<\sigma t,\\
(v_{+},u_{+})&\text{if}\quad x>\sigma t.\end{cases}

For the case of NS equations (
1.1
), the counterpart of the Riemann solution (
1.6
) is a viscous shock, as a traveling wave
(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{u})(x-\sigma t)
solution to (
1.1
), that satisfies the following ODEs:

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &-\sigma\widetilde{v}^{\prime}-\widetilde{u}^{\prime}=0,\\
&-\sigma\widetilde{u}^{\prime}+p(\widetilde{v})^{\prime}=\left(\frac{\widetilde{u}^{\prime}}{\widetilde{v}}\right)^{\prime},\\
&(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{u})(\pm\infty)=(v_{\pm},u_{\pm}).\end{aligned}

In previous works
[
4
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
15
,
24
]
on the barotropic NS system, the
a
-contraction method has been used in showing that perturbations of a viscous shock, measured by the weighted relative entropy as the zeroth order estimate, is not increasing in time up to a dynamical shift. For that, the effective velocity
h:=u-\frac{\mu(v)}{v}v_{x}
(corresponding to the Bresch-Desjardins entropy) was used to transform the original system (
1.1
) into a new system that has a viscous term on
v
variable only. Using the new system with
h
variable seems to make the
a
-contraction method to be applied easily, because of the following two reasons: (i) the hyperbolic part of the system is linear in
u
(or
h
) but nonlinear in
v
due to the pressure; (ii) the main terms
\int a^{\prime}(p-p(\widetilde{v}))(h-\tilde{h})dx-\frac{\sigma}{2}\int a^{\prime}(h-\tilde{h})^{2}dx
can be maximized explicitly (without lower order terms), contrary to the maximization process for the original case (
1.1
) as in Section 4.2.

However, when handling the long-time behavior for a slightly complicated problem than (
1.2
), using the
h
variable for the zeroth order estimate may not be helpful and even could make the usage of the method to be complicated.
For example, for the Navier-Stokes system of Korteweg type as in
[
5
]
, the associated new system (represented by
h
variable) would be far more complicated than the original system since the Korteweg term (or capillarity term) is substantially related to the third derivatives of
v
variable.
For the boundary-initial value problem of barotropic Navier-Stokes as in
[
6
]
, handling the original system is easier than employing the
h
variable because of no boundary condition for the derivative of
v
variable.

The goal of the paper is to present a way of applying the
a
-contraction method to the NS system (
1.1
) without using the
h
variable for all estimates, in order for the long-time behavior toward a viscous shock.

For simplicity, we assume (
1.1
) with a constant viscosity coefficient
\mu>0
as a normalized coefficient
\mu=1
and also
b=1
.
However, out result still holds for (
1.1
) with a general pressure
p(v)>0
satisfying
p^{\prime}(v)<0,p^{\prime\prime}(v)>0
, and smooth viscosity
\mu=\mu(v)
for
v>0
,
since we consider small
H^{1}
-perturbations for
v
variables.

Our main theorem reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For a given state
(v_{+},u_{+})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
, there exist positive constants
\delta_{0}
, and
\varepsilon_{0}
such that the following holds:

For any
(v_{-},u_{-})
on the 2-shock curve
S_{2}(v_{+},u_{+})
, satisfying the Rankine-Hugoniot condition (
1.5
) with
|v_{+}-v_{-}|<\delta_{0}
, consider the 2-viscous shock
(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{u})(x-\sigma t)
defined in (
1.7
). Let
(v_{0},u_{0})
be any initial data such that

\sum_{\pm}\left(\|(v_{0}-v_{\pm},u_{0}-u_{\pm})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}\right)+\|(v_{0x},u_{0x})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}<\varepsilon_{0},

where
\mathbb{R}_{-}:=-\mathbb{R}_{+}=(-\infty,0)
. Then, the Navier-Stokes system (
1.1
) admits a unique global-in-time solution
(v,u)
. Moreover, there exists a Lipschitz continuous shift
X(t)
such that

\displaystyle(v(t,x)-\widetilde{v}(x-\sigma t-X(t))\in C(0,\infty;H^{1}(\mathbb{R})),

\displaystyle u(t,x)-\widetilde{u}(x-\sigma t-X(t))\in C(0,\infty;H^{1}(\mathbb{R})),

\displaystyle u_{xx}(t.x)-\widetilde{u}_{xx}(x-\sigma t-X(t))\in L^{2}(0,\infty;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))

In addition, we have

(1.8)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|(v,u)(t,x)-(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{u})(x-\sigma t-X(t))\right|=0

and

(1.9)

\lim_{t\to\infty}|\dot{X}(t)|=0.

Remark 1.1
.

Since (
1.9
) implies

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{X(t)}{t}=0,

the shift function
X(t)
grows at most sub-linearly as
t\to\infty
. Thus, the shifted wave
\widetilde{U}(x-\sigma t-X(t))
tends to the original wave
\widetilde{U}(x-\sigma t)
time-asymptotically.

Remark 1.2
.

There are a lot of previous literatures on the time asymptotic behavior of the NS equations. Among the numerous results on the time-asymptotic stability of the NS equations (
1.1
), we refer to
[
3
,
4
,
15
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
, although the list is not exhaustive. In particular, Matsumura-Nishihara
[
19
]
used the anti-derivative method for the time-asymptotic stability of viscous shock wave, for which the zero-mass condition for the initial perturbation is crucially imposed. Our result does not require the zero-mass condition.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we provide several preliminaries, such as technical estimates on the relative quantities or the properties of the viscous shock (
1.7
). Section
3

a
-contraction with shift, and then we obtain the estimates on the high-order terms in Section

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we present several preliminary estimates on the relative quantities for the pressure and the internal energy. We also provide the existence and properties of viscous shock in this section. Finally, we introduce several
O(1)
-constants and related estimates on them.

2.1.
Estimates on the relative quantities

We present several upper and lower bounds on the relative quantities that will be used in estimating the relative entropy. For any function
F:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
and
v,w\in(0,\infty)
, we define the relative quantity
F(v|w)
as

F(v|w):=F(v)-F(w)-F^{\prime}(w)(v-w).

In particular, when
F
is convex, then the relative quantity is always positive. In the following lemma, we present several lower and upper bounds on the relative quantities for the pressure
p(v)=v^{-\gamma}
and the internal energy
Q(v)=\frac{v^{1-\gamma}}{\gamma-1}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\gamma>1
and
v_{+}
be given constants. Then, there exists constants
C,\delta_{*}
such that the following assertions hold:

(1)

For any
v,\bar{v}
satisfying
0<\bar{v}<2v_{+}
and
0<v<3v_{+}
,

|v-\bar{v}|^{2}\leq CQ(v|\bar{v}),\quad|v-\bar{v}|^{2}\leq Cp(v|w).

(2)

For any
v,\bar{v}
satisfying
v,\bar{v}>v_{+}/2
,

|p(v)-p(\bar{v})|\leq C|v-\bar{v}|.

(3)

For any
0<\delta<\delta_{*}
and any
(v,\bar{v})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
satisfying
|p(v)-p(\bar{v})|<\delta
and
|p(\bar{v})-p(v_{+})|<\delta,

\displaystyle p(v|\bar{v})\leq\left(\frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma}\frac{1}{p(\bar{v})}+C\delta\right)|p(v)-p(\bar{v})|^{2},

\displaystyle Q(v|\bar{v})\geq\frac{p(\bar{v})^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}-1}}{2\gamma}|p(v)-p(\bar{v})|^{2}-\frac{1+\gamma}{3\gamma^{2}}p(\bar{v})^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}-2}(p(v)-p(\bar{v}))^{3},

\displaystyle Q(v|\bar{v})\leq\left(\frac{p(\bar{v})^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}-1}}{2\gamma}+C\delta\right)|p(v)-p(\bar{v})|^{2}.

Proof.

Since the proofs are duplicates of those of
[
11
, Lemma 2.4, 2.5, and 2.6]
, we omit the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For a given right-end state
(v_{+},u_{+})

(v_{-},u_{-})\in S_{2}(v_{+},u_{+})

|v_{+}-v_{-}|\sim|u_{+}-u_{-}|=:\delta<\delta_{0}

(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{u})(\xi)
to (

\widetilde{v}(0)=\frac{v_{-}+v_{+}}{2}
. Moreover, the following estimates hold:

(2.1)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}^{\prime}<0,\quad\widetilde{v}^{\prime}>0,\quad^{\prime}=\dfrac{d}{d\xi},\;\xi=x-\sigma t,

\displaystyle C^{-1}\widetilde{v}^{\prime}(\xi)\leq\widetilde{u}^{\prime}(\xi)\leq C\widetilde{v}^{\prime}(\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R},

2.3.
Useful
O(1)
-constants

In the later analysis, we will use the following
O(1)
-constants defined as

(2.2)

\sigma_{\ell}:=\sqrt{-p^{\prime}(v_{-})},\quad\alpha_{\ell}:=\frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma\sigma_{\ell}p(v_{-})}=\frac{p^{\prime\prime}(v_{-})}{2|p^{\prime}(v_{-})|^{2}\sigma_{\ell}}.

These constants are indeed independent of the small shock strength
\delta
since
v_{+}/2\leq v_{-}\leq v_{+}
. Then, the following estimates on the

(2.3)

|\sigma-\sigma_{\ell}|=\left|\sqrt{-\frac{p(v_{+})-p(v_{-})}{v_{+}-v_{-}}}-\sqrt{-p^{\prime}(v_{-})}\right|\leq C\delta.

Moreover, thanks to Lemma
2.2
, the shock profile is monotone for the weak shock, and therefore
v_{-}\leq\widetilde{v}(x-\sigma t)\leq v_{+}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. This yields the following estimates

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\sigma_{\ell}^{2}+p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v})\|_{L^{\infty}}=\|p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v})-p^{\prime}(v_{-})\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\delta,\\
&\left\|\frac{1}{\sigma_{\ell}^{2}}-\frac{p(\widetilde{v})^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}-1}}{\gamma}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}=\left\|\frac{(v_{-})^{\gamma+1}}{\gamma}-\frac{p(\widetilde{v})^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}-1}}{\gamma}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\delta.\end{aligned}

Throughout the paper,
C
denotes a positive
O(1)
-constant which may change from line to line, but which is independent of the small constants like
\delta,\varepsilon_{1}
and the lifespan
T
given in Proposition
3.3

Local existence

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\underline{v}
and

\underline{v}(x)=v_{\pm},\quad\underline{u}(x)=u_{\pm},\quad\mbox{for}\quad\pm x\geq 1.

,
\underline{\kappa}_{0}

\overline{\kappa}_{0}
,
\underline{\kappa}_{1}

(v_{0},u_{0})

3.2.
Construction of weight function

We first construct the weight function
a=a(t,x)
as

(3.1)

a(t,x):=1+\frac{u_{-}-\widetilde{u}(x-\sigma t)}{\sqrt{\delta}},

where
\delta:=|u_{-}-u_{+}|
denotes the shock strength. It follows from the definition of the weight function

that
1\leq a\leq 1+\sqrt{\delta}<\frac{3}{2}
and

(3.2)

\partial_{x}a=-\frac{\partial_{x}\widetilde{u}}{\sqrt{\delta}}=\frac{\sigma\partial_{x}\widetilde{v}}{\sqrt{\delta}}>0,\quad\mbox{and therefore},\quad|a_{x}|\sim\frac{|\partial_{x}\widetilde{v}|}{\sqrt{\delta}}

where we used (
1.7
)
1
.

3.3.
Construction of shift

Next, we introduce the shift
X:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
as a solution to the following ODE:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{X}(t)&=-\frac{M}{\delta}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\left(x-\sigma t-X(t)\right)\widetilde{u}_{x}\left(x-\sigma t-X(t)\right)\big(u-\widetilde{u}(x-\sigma t-X(t)\big)\,dx\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(x-\sigma t-X(t))\partial_{x}p\big(\widetilde{v}(x-\sigma t-X(t))\big)\big(u-\widetilde{u}(x-\sigma t-X(t))\big)\,dx\Bigg),\\
X(0)&=0,\end{aligned}

where
M=\frac{5\sigma_{\ell}^{3}\alpha_{\ell}}{4}
. Then, the standard existence theorem for the ODE can be applied to guarantee the existence of the shift.

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}>0
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that the following is true. For any

, and any function

the ODE (
3.3
) has a unique Lipschitz continuous solution
X
on

|X(t)|\leq Ct,\quad t\in[0,T].

As the name implies, the constructed shift

will play an important role in the theory of
a
-contraction with shift. In the following, we use the following abbreviated notation for the shifted function. For any function
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

g^{X}(\cdot):=g(\cdot-X(t)),\quad t\geq 0.

to (
1.1
) and the viscous shock
(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{u})
defined as in (
1.7
).

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a positive constant
C
such that for all
t\in[0,T],

(4.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(|u-\widetilde{u}|^{2}+|v-\widetilde{v}|^{2}\big)\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\delta|\dot{X}|^{2}+G_{1}+G^{S}+D\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(|u_{0}-\widetilde{u}|^{2}+|v_{0}-\widetilde{v}|^{2}\big)\,dx,

where
G_{1}
,
G^{S}
, and
D_{u_{1}}
are defined in (
3.6
).

4.1.
Relative entropy method

To prove Lemma
4.1
, we rely on the relative entropy method. To this end, we rewrite the NS system (
1.1
) in the following abstract form:

(4.2)

\partial_{t}U+\partial_{x}A(U)=\partial_{x}(M(U)\partial_{x}D\eta(U)),

where

U:=\begin{pmatrix}v\\
u\end{pmatrix},\quad A(U):=\begin{pmatrix}-u\\
p(v)\end{pmatrix},\quad D\eta(U):=\begin{pmatrix}-p(v)\\
u,\end{pmatrix},

and

M(U):=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&\frac{1}{v}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Similarly, the shifted viscous wave
\widetilde{U}

(4.3)

\widetilde{U}(t,x):=\widetilde{U}(x-\sigma t-X(t)),

satisfies a similar system as

\partial_{t}\widetilde{U}+\partial_{x}A(\widetilde{U})=\partial_{x}\left(M(\widetilde{U})\partial_{x}D\eta(\widetilde{U})\right)-\dot{X}\partial_{x}\widetilde{U}.

Now, we define the relative entropy between
U=(v,u)
and
\overline{U}=(\overline{v},\overline{u})
as

\eta(U|\overline{U}):=\eta(U)-\eta(\overline{U})-D\eta(\overline{U})(U-\overline{U}),

and the relative flux
A(U|\overline{U})

A(U|\overline{U})=A(U)-A(\overline{U})-DA(\overline{U})(U-\overline{U}).

Finally, let
G(U;\overline{U})
be the relative entropy flux definded as

G(U;\overline{U})=G(U)-G(\overline{U})-D\eta(\overline{U})(A(U)-A(\overline{U})),

where
G
is the entropy flux for
\eta
satisfying the condition
D_{i}G(U)=\sum_{k=1}^{2}D_{k}\eta(U)D_{i}A_{k}(U)
. In the case of NS system, we consider
G(U)=p(v)u
, and therefore, we can compute
A(U|\overline{U})
and
G(U;\overline{U})
as

\eta(U|\overline{U})=\frac{|u-\overline{u}|^{2}}{2}+Q(v|\overline{v}),\quad A(U|\overline{U})=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
p(v|\overline{v})\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{and}\quad G(U;\overline{U})=(p(v)-p(\overline{v}))(u-\overline{u}).

Here, the relative internal energy
Q(v|\overline{v})
and the relative pressure
p(v|\overline{v})
are defined as

Q(v|\overline{v})=Q(v)-Q(\overline{v})-Q^{\prime}(\overline{v})(v-\overline{v})\quad\mbox{and}\quad p(v|\overline{v})=p(v)-p(\overline{v})-p^{\prime}(\overline{v})(v-\overline{v}).

In order to obtain
L^{2}
-perturbation estimate in Lemma
4.1
, we focus on estimating the weighted relative entropy between the solution
U
and the shifted viscous shock wave
\widetilde{U}
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(t,x)\eta\left(U(t,x)|\widetilde{U}(t,x)\right)\,dx,\quad\mbox{where}\quad a(t,x):=a(t,x-X(t)).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
a
be the weight function defined by (
3.1
) and
X:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
be any Lipschitz continuous function. Let
U
be a solution to (
4.2
), and
\widetilde{U}
be the shifted viscous shock wave defined in (
4.3
). Then

(4.4)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(t,x)\eta(U(t,x))|\widetilde{U}(t,x))\,dx=\dot{X}(t)Y(U)+\mathcal{J}^{\textup{bad}}(U)-\mathcal{J}^{\textup{good}}(U),

where the terms
Y(U)
,
\mathcal{J}^{\textup{bad}}
, and
\mathcal{J}^{\textup{good}}
are defined as

\displaystyle Y(U)

\displaystyle:=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}\eta(U|\widetilde{U})\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}aD^{2}\eta(\widetilde{U})\widetilde{U}_{x}^{X}(U-\widetilde{U})\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\textup{bad}}(U)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))(u-\widetilde{u})\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\widetilde{u}_{x}^{X}p(v|\widetilde{v})\,dx

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}\frac{(u-\widetilde{u})\partial_{x}(u-\widetilde{u})}{v}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}\frac{(u-\widetilde{u})(v-\widetilde{v})\partial_{x}\widetilde{u}}{v\widetilde{v}}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\partial_{x}(u-\widetilde{u})\frac{(v-\widetilde{v})}{v\widetilde{v}}\partial_{x}\widetilde{u}\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\textup{good}}(U)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\sigma}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}|u-\widetilde{u}|^{2}\,dx+\sigma\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}Q(v|\widetilde{v})\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\frac{|\partial_{x}(u-\widetilde{u})|^{2}}{v}\,dx.

Proof.

Since the system (
4.2
) is written in a general hyperbolic system, we may use the same computations as in
[
11
, Lemma 2.3]
to estimate the time derivative of the weighted relative entropy as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\eta(U|\widetilde{U})\,dx

\displaystyle=\dot{X}(t)Y(U)-\sigma\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}\eta(U|\widetilde{U})\,dx+\sum_{i=1}^{5}I_{1i},

where

\displaystyle I_{11}

\displaystyle:=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\partial_{x}G(U;\widetilde{U})\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))(u-\widetilde{u})\,dx,

\displaystyle I_{12}

\displaystyle:=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\partial_{x}D\eta(\widetilde{U})A(U|\widetilde{U})\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\widetilde{u}_{x}^{X}p(v|\widetilde{v})\,dx,

\displaystyle I_{13}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\left(D\eta(U)-D\eta(\widetilde{U})\right)\partial_{x}\left(M(U)\partial_{x}\left(D\eta(U)-D\eta(\widetilde{U})\right)\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\ =\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(u-\widetilde{u})\partial_{x}\left(\frac{\partial_{x}(u-\widetilde{u})}{v}\right)dx

\displaystyle\ =-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\frac{|\partial_{x}(u-\widetilde{u})|^{2}}{v}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}(u-\widetilde{u})\frac{\partial_{x}(u-\widetilde{u})}{v}dx,

\displaystyle I_{14}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\left(D\eta(U)-D\eta(\widetilde{U})\right)\partial_{x}\left((M(U)-M(\widetilde{U}))\partial_{x}D\eta(\widetilde{U})\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(u-\widetilde{u})\partial_{x}\left(\left(\frac{1}{v}-\frac{1}{\widetilde{v}}\right)\partial_{x}\widetilde{u}\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}\frac{(v-\widetilde{v})(u-\widetilde{u})\partial_{x}\widetilde{u}}{v\widetilde{v}}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\partial_{x}(u-\widetilde{u})\frac{(v-\widetilde{v})\partial_{x}\widetilde{u}}{v\widetilde{v}}\,dx,

\displaystyle I_{15}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(D\eta)(U|\widetilde{U})\partial_{x}\left(M(\widetilde{U})\partial_{x}D\eta(\widetilde{U})\right)\,dx=0.

Thus, combining all the estimates on
I_{1i}
above, we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

4.2.
Maximization on
p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})

Among the terms in
\mathcal{J}^{\text{bad}}
, a primary bad term is

\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))(u-\widetilde{u})\,dx

where the perturbations for
p(v)
and
u
are coupled. In order to exploit the parabolic term on
u
-variable and hence use the Poincaré-type inequality, we separate
u-\widetilde{u}
from
p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})
by using the quadratic structure of
p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})
. We first obtain the following estimates on several terms in
\mathcal{J}^{\textup{bad}}(U)
and
\mathcal{J}^{\textup{good}}(U)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

[
5
, Lemma 4.3]
For any
\delta>0
small enough, let
C_{*}
be the constant as

(4.5)

C_{*}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{\ell}}-\sqrt{\delta}\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma}\frac{1}{p(v_{-})}\right),\quad\sigma_{\ell}=\sqrt{-p^{\prime}(v_{-})}.

Then, we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\widetilde{u}_{x}p(v|\widetilde{v})\,dx-\sigma\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}Q(v|\widetilde{v})\,dx\\
&\leq-C_{*}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})|^{2}\,dx+C\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}\big|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})\big|^{2}\,dx+C\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}\big|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})\big|^{3}\,dx.\end{aligned}

Using Lemma
4.3
and quadratic structure with respect to
p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})
, we can derive an upper bound of
\mathcal{J}^{\textup{bad}}-\mathcal{J}^{\textup{good}}(U)
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For
\mathcal{J}^{\textup{bad}}
and
\mathcal{J}^{\textup{good}}(U)
defined in (
4.4
), we have

\mathcal{J}^{\text{bad}}-\mathcal{J}^{\text{good}}\leq\mathcal{B}-\mathcal{G}.

Here,
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{G}
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(U)=\sum_{i=1}^{6}\mathcal{B}_{i}(U),

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(U)=\mathcal{G}_{1}(U)+\mathcal{G}_{2}(U)+\mathcal{D}(U).

where
\mathcal{B}_{i}
,
\mathcal{G}_{i}
, and
\mathcal{D}
are given by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}:=\frac{1}{4C_{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}|u-\widetilde{u}|^{2}dx,\quad\mathcal{B}_{2}:=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}\frac{(u-\widetilde{u})\partial_{x}(u-\widetilde{u})}{v}dx,\quad\mathcal{B}_{3}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}\frac{(u-\widetilde{u})(v-\widetilde{v})\partial_{x}\widetilde{u}}{v\widetilde{v}}dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{4}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\frac{(v-\widetilde{v})\partial_{x}(u-\widetilde{u})\partial_{x}\widetilde{u}}{v\widetilde{v}}dx,\quad\mathcal{B}_{5}:=C\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}\big|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})\big|^{2}dx,\quad\mathcal{B}_{6}:=C\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}\big|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})\big|^{3}dx,

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}:=C_{*}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})-\frac{u-\widetilde{u}}{2C_{*}}\right|^{2}\,dx,\quad\mathcal{G}_{2}:=\frac{\sigma}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}|u-\widetilde{u}|^{2}\,dx,\quad\mathcal{D}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\frac{|\partial_{x}(u-\widetilde{u})|^{2}}{v}\,dx.

Proof.

We use Lemma
4.3
to derive a quadratic structure in terms of
p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})
as follows:

(4.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))(u-\widetilde{u})\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\widetilde{u}_{x}p(v|\widetilde{v})\,dx-\sigma\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}Q(v|\widetilde{v})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))(u-\widetilde{u})\,dx-C_{*}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle+C\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}\big|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})\big|^{2}\,dx+C\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}\big|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})\big|^{3}\,dx.

Then, the first two terms in the right-hand side of (
4.7
) can be rewritten by using the quadratic structure of
p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})
as

(4.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))(u-\widetilde{u})\,dx-C_{*}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}\left(-C_{*}\left((p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}))-\frac{u-\widetilde{u}}{2C_{*}}\right)^{2}+\frac{(u-\widetilde{u})^{2}}{4C_{*}}\right)dx.

Since the other terms in
\mathcal{J^{\text{bad}}}
and
\mathcal{J}^{\text{good}}
are unchanged, we obtain the desired inequality.
∎

The estimate (
4.4
) in Lemma
4.2
can be further bounded as

(4.9)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\eta(U|\widetilde{U})\,dx\leq\dot{X}(t)Y(U)+\mathcal{B}(U)-\mathcal{G}(U),

On the other hand, since
Y(U)
is expanded as

\displaystyle Y(U)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}\eta(U|\widetilde{U})\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}aD^{2}\eta(\widetilde{U})(\widetilde{U})^{X}_{x}(U-\widetilde{U})\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}\left(\frac{|u-\widetilde{u}^{X}|^{2}}{2}+Q(v|\widetilde{v}^{X})\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\widetilde{u}_{x}(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}ap^{\prime}(\widetilde{v}^{X})\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})\,dx,

we decompose
Y
as

Y=\sum_{i=1}^{4}Y_{i},

where

\displaystyle Y_{1}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\widetilde{u}_{x}(u-\widetilde{u})\,dx,

\displaystyle Y_{2}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}}ap^{\prime}(\widetilde{v})\widetilde{v}_{x}(u-\widetilde{u})\,dx,

\displaystyle Y_{3}

\displaystyle:=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}ap^{\prime}(\widetilde{v})\widetilde{v}_{x}\left(v-\widetilde{v}+\frac{(u-\widetilde{u})}{\sigma}\right)\,dx,

\displaystyle Y_{4}

\displaystyle:=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}\left(\frac{|u-\widetilde{u}^{X}|^{2}}{2}+Q(v|\widetilde{v}^{X})\right)\,dx

We now define a shift function
X(t)
so that it satisfies the following ODE:

(4.10)

\dot{X}=-\frac{M}{\delta}(Y_{1}+Y_{2}),\quad X(0)=0.

With this choice of shift
X
, the term
\dot{X}(t)Y(U)
in (
4.9
) can be written as

\dot{X}(t)Y(U)=-\frac{\delta}{M}|\dot{X}|^{2}+\dot{X}\sum_{i=3}^{4}Y_{i}.

To summarize, we decompose the right-hand side of (
4.9
) as

(4.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\eta(U|\widetilde{U})\,dx&=\underbrace{-\frac{\delta}{2M}|\dot{X}|^{2}+\mathcal{B}_{1}-\mathcal{G}_{2}-\frac{3}{4}\mathcal{D}}_{=:\mathcal{R}_{1}}\\
&\quad\underbrace{-\frac{\delta}{2M}|\dot{X}|^{2}+\dot{X}\sum_{i=3}^{4}Y_{i}+\sum_{i=2}^{6}\mathcal{B}_{i}-\mathcal{G}_{1}-\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{D}}_{=:\mathcal{R}_{2}}.\end{aligned}

In the following subsections, we estimate the terms in
\mathcal{R}_{1}
and
\mathcal{R}_{2}
respectively.

4.3.
Estimate of
\mathcal{R}_{1}

Estimation of
\mathcal{R}_{1}
is the most important part in the proof of Lemma
4.1
, in which the Poincaré-type inequality is crucially used. For a fixed
t\geq 0
, we define an auxiliary variable
y
as

y:=\frac{u_{-}-\widetilde{u}(x-\sigma t-X(t))}{\delta}.

Then it follows from the definition that the map
x\mapsto y=y(x)
is one-to-one and

\frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{1}{\delta}\widetilde{u}_{x}>0,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lim_{x\to-\infty}y=0,\quad\lim_{x\to\infty}y=1.

Using the new variable
y
, we will apply the following Poincarè-type inequality:

Lemma 4.5
.

[
11
, Lemma 2.9]
For any
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
with
\int_{0}^{1}y(1-y)|f^{\prime}|^{2}\,dy<\infty
,

\int_{0}^{1}\left|f-\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy\right|^{2}dy\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}y(1-y)|f^{\prime}|^{2}\,dy.

We apply Lemma
4.5
to the perturbation
f
of the following form:

f:=\left(u(t,\cdot)-\widetilde{u}(\cdot-\sigma t-X(t)\right)\circ y^{-1}.

Therefore, the goal of this subsection is to represent
\mathcal{R}_{1}
in terms of
f
and then use Lemma
4.5
to estimate it. In the following, we estimate the terms in
\mathcal{R}_{1}
separately.

\bullet
(Estimate of
\frac{\delta}{2M}|\dot{X}|^{2}
):
We use the definition of
Y_{1}
and change of variables for
y
to observe that

Y_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\widetilde{u}_{x}^{X}(u-\widetilde{u})\,dx=-\delta\int_{0}^{1}af\,dy.

Then, using (
2.3
) and
\|a-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})}\leq\sqrt{\delta}
, we have

\left|Y_{1}+\delta\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy\right|\leq\delta\int_{0}^{1}|a-1||f|dy\leq\delta^{3/2}\int_{0}^{1}|f|\,dy.

To estimate
Y_{2}
, we first use the relation
\sigma\widetilde{v}_{x}=-\widetilde{u}_{x}
and change of variables for
y
to yield

Y_{2}=-\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}ap^{\prime}(\widetilde{v})\widetilde{u}_{x}(u-\widetilde{u})\,dx=\frac{\delta}{\sigma^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}ap^{\prime}(\widetilde{v})f\,dy.

This, together with the estimates (
2.3
), (
2.4
), and
\|a-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})}\leq\sqrt{\delta}
, implies

\displaystyle\left|Y_{2}+\delta\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy\right|\leq\delta\int_{0}^{1}\left|\frac{ap^{\prime}(\widetilde{v})}{\sigma^{2}}+1\right||f|dy\leq C\delta(\sqrt{\delta}+\delta)\int_{0}^{1}|f|\,dy.

Since
\dot{X}=-\frac{M}{\delta}(Y_{1}+Y_{2})
, we combine the estimates for
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
to obtain

\displaystyle\left|\dot{X}-2M\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy\right|\leq\frac{M}{\delta}\left(\left|Y_{1}+\delta\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy\right|+\left|Y_{2}+\delta\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy\right|\right)\leq C(\sqrt{\delta}+\delta)\int_{0}^{1}|f|\,dy,

which implies

\displaystyle\left(\left|2M\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy\right|-|\dot{X}|\right)^{2}\leq C(\sqrt{\delta}+\delta)^{2}\int_{0}^{1}|f|^{2}\,dy\leq C\delta\int_{0}^{1}|f|^{2}\,dy.

We use an elementary inequality
\frac{p^{2}}{2}-q^{2}\leq(p-q)^{2}
for
p,q\in\mathbb{R}
to obtain

\displaystyle 2M^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy\right)^{2}-|\dot{X}|^{2}\leq C\delta\int_{0}^{1}|f|^{2}\,dy,

and by rearranging the terms, we obtain

(4.12)

-\frac{\delta}{2M}|\dot{X}|^{2}\leq-M\delta\left(\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy\right)^{2}+C\delta\int_{0}^{1}|f|^{2}\,dy.

\bullet
(Estimates of
\mathcal{B}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}
):
Recall that
\mathcal{B}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}
are

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}:=\frac{1}{4C_{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}|u-\widetilde{u}|^{2}\,dx\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathcal{G}_{2}:=\frac{\sigma}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}|u-\widetilde{u}|^{2}\,dx.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}-\mathcal{G}_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{4C_{*}}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}|u-\widetilde{u}|^{2}\,dx=-\left(\frac{1}{4C_{*}}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\delta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{u}_{x}^{X}|u-\widetilde{u}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\delta}\left(\frac{1}{4C_{*}}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}\,dy.

where
C_{*}
defined in (
4.5
) can be written as

C_{*}=\frac{1}{2\sigma_{\ell}}-(\sqrt{\delta}+\delta)\alpha_{\ell}\sigma_{\ell},

with simple notation
\alpha_{\ell}=\frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma\sigma_{\ell}p(v_{-})}
in (
2.2
). On the other hand, using (
2.3
), (
2.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sqrt{\delta}\left(\frac{1}{4C_{*}}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sigma_{\ell}}{2}\frac{\sqrt{\delta}}{1-2(\sqrt{\delta}+\delta)\sigma_{\ell}^{2}\alpha_{\ell}}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\sqrt{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{\delta}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{1-2(\sqrt{\delta}+\delta)\sigma_{\ell}^{2}\alpha_{\ell}}(\sigma_{\ell}-\sigma)+\sigma\left(\frac{1}{1-2(\sqrt{\delta}+\delta)\sigma_{\ell}^{2}\alpha_{\ell}}-1\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{3/2}+\delta\sigma_{\ell}^{3}\alpha_{\ell}.

Therefore, we have

(4.13)

\mathcal{B}_{1}-\mathcal{G}_{2}\leq C\delta^{3/2}\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}\,dy+\sigma_{\ell}^{3}\alpha_{\ell}\delta\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}\,dy.

\bullet
(Estimate of
\mathcal{D}(U)
):
First, using
a\geq 1
and change of variables, we estimate the diffusion term
\mathcal{D}
in terms of
f
:

\mathcal{D}\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{v}|\partial_{x}(u-\widetilde{u})|^{2}dx=\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{y}f|^{2}\frac{1}{v}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)dy.

Similar to the previous result in
[
15
, Lemma 4.5]
, there exists
C>0
such that the following estimate holds:

(4.14)

\left|\frac{1}{y(1-y)}\frac{1}{\widetilde{v}}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)-\frac{\sigma}{2\sigma_{\ell}}\frac{\delta v^{\prime\prime}(p_{-})}{|v^{\prime}(p_{-})|^{2}}\right|\leq C\delta^{2}.

On the other hand, since
C^{-1}\leq v\leq C
, we have

(4.15)

\left|\frac{\widetilde{v}}{v}-1\right|\leq C\left|\widetilde{v}-v\right|\leq C\varepsilon.

Then, using (
4.14
) and (
4.15
), we obtain the lower bound for
\mathcal{D}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{y}f|^{2}\frac{\widetilde{v}}{v}\frac{1}{\widetilde{v}}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)dy

\displaystyle\geq(1-C\varepsilon)\left(\frac{\sigma}{2\sigma_{\ell}}\frac{\delta v^{\prime\prime}(p_{-})}{|v^{\prime}(p_{-})|^{2}}-C\delta^{2}\right)\int_{0}^{1}y(1-y)\left|\partial_{y}f\right|^{2}dy.

Finally, since

\displaystyle\sigma_{\ell}^{3}\alpha_{\ell}=\frac{1}{2}(1+\gamma)\frac{1}{v_{-}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{v^{\prime\prime}(p_{-})}{|v^{\prime}(p_{-})|^{2}},

we obtain

(4.16)

\mathcal{D}\geq\sigma_{\ell}^{3}\alpha_{\ell}\delta(1-C(\delta_{0}+\varepsilon))\int_{0}^{1}y(1-y)|\partial_{y}f|^{2}\,dy.

\bullet
(Estimate of
\mathcal{R}_{1}
): We now combine the estimates (
4.13
) and (
4.16
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}-\mathcal{G}_{2}-\frac{3}{4}\mathcal{D}\leq\sigma_{\ell}^{3}\alpha_{\ell}\delta\left((1+C\sqrt{\delta})\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}\,dy-\frac{3}{4}(1-C(\delta_{0}+\varepsilon))\int_{0}^{1}y(1-y)|\partial_{y}f|^{2}\,dy\right),

which together with the smallness of
\delta_{0},\varepsilon
yields

\mathcal{B}_{1}-\mathcal{G}_{2}-\frac{3}{4}\mathcal{D}\leq\sigma_{\ell}^{3}\alpha_{\ell}\delta\left(\frac{9}{8}\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}\,dy-\frac{5}{8}\int_{0}^{1}y(1-y)|\partial_{y}f|^{2}\,dy\right).

Then, using Lemma
4.5
with the identity

\int_{0}^{1}|f-\overline{f}|^{2}\,dy=\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}\,dy-\overline{f}^{2},\quad\overline{f}:=\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy,

we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}-\mathcal{G}_{2}-\frac{3}{4}\mathcal{D}\leq-\frac{\sigma_{\ell}^{3}\alpha_{\ell}\delta}{8}\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}\,dy+\frac{5\sigma_{\ell}^{3}\alpha_{\ell}\delta}{4}\left(\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy\right)^{2}.

Finally, using (

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigg|\frac{1}{v}(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{xx}+\widetilde{u}^{X}_{xx}\left(\frac{1}{v}-\frac{1}{\widetilde{v}^{X}}\right)-\frac{1}{v^{2}}(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})_{x}(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{x}

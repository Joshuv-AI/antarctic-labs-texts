Global well-posedness and large-time behavior for three-dimensional magneto-micropolar equations with horizontal dissipation
Peng Lu, Yuanyuan Qiao

Abstract
This paper is concerned with the stability and large-time behavior for 3D magneto-micropolar equations with horizontal dissipation. The global well-posedness of the aforementioned system is established, with the initial data and its vertical derivatives required to be sufficiently small in L 2 L^{2} space. Moreover, we obtain the optimal decay rates for the H 1 H^{1} -norm of the solution. The proofs of our main results rely on the special structure of the equations and anisotropic Sobolev-type inequalities.

Introduction

The magneto-micropolar equations were introduced in
[
1
]
to describe the motion of an incompressible, electrically conducting micropolar fluids in the presence of an arbitrary magnetic field, such as salt water, ester, fluorocarbon, etc., which is of great importance in practical and mathematical applications. The three-dimensional viscous and resistive magneto-micropolar system is written as

\begin{cases}u_{t}+(u\cdot{\nabla})u-\mu\Delta u-\chi\Delta u+{\nabla}P=(B\cdot{\nabla})B+2\chi{\nabla}\times w,\\
B_{t}+(u\cdot{\nabla})B-\nu\Delta B=(B\cdot{\nabla})u,\\
w_{t}+(u\cdot{\nabla})w-\gamma\Delta w-\kappa\nabla(\nabla\cdot w)+4\chi w=2\chi{\nabla}\times u,\\
{\nabla}\cdot u={\nabla}\cdot B=0,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
is the velocity,
B=(B_{1},B_{2},B_{3})
is the magnetic field,
w=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})
is the micro-rotational velocity, and
P
is the pressure.
\mu
,
\chi
and
\nu
are positive constants, representing the kinematic viscosity, the vortex viscosity, and the magnetic diffusivity. The positive constants
\gamma
and
\kappa
are the spin viscosity.

As is pointed out in
[
2
,
21
]
, in geophysical fluids, meteorologist often modelize turbulent diffusion by putting a viscosity of the form
\mu_{h}(\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{2}^{2})+\mu_{3}\partial_{3}^{2}
instead of the classical viscosity
\mu\Delta
, where
\partial_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}
for
j=1,2,3
, and
\mu_{3}
is usually much smaller than
\mu_{h}
. We refer to J. Pedlovsky
[
22
, Chapter 4]
for a more complete discussion. In this paper, we focus on the following Cauchy problem of magneto-micropolar equations with horizontal dissipation.

\begin{cases}u_{t}+(u\cdot{\nabla})u-\mu\Delta_{h}u-\chi\Delta u+{\nabla}P=(B\cdot{\nabla})B+2\chi{\nabla}\times w,\\
B_{t}+(u\cdot{\nabla})B-\nu\Delta_{h}B=(B\cdot{\nabla})u,\\
w_{t}+(u\cdot{\nabla})w-\gamma\Delta_{h}w-\kappa\nabla(\nabla\cdot w)+4\chi w=2\chi{\nabla}\times u,\\
{\nabla}\cdot u={\nabla}\cdot B=0,\end{cases}

(1.2)

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and
t\geq 0
denote the spatial coordinate and time coordinate, and
\Delta_{h}=\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{2}^{2}
denotes the horizontal Laplacian. The initial data is given by

(u,B,w)(0,x)=(u_{0},B_{0},w_{0})(x),

(1.3)

satisfying
{\nabla}\cdot u_{0}=0
and
{\nabla}\cdot B_{0}=0
in the sense of distribution.

Recently, Shang and Liu
[

\displaystyle L^{p}=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),~W^{k,p}=W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),~H^{k}=W^{k,2},

\displaystyle\dot{H}^{-\sigma}_{h}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{3})~\big|~\Lambda_{h}^{-\sigma}f\in L^{2}\right\},\quad\text{with}\quad\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{-\sigma}_{h}}=\|\Lambda_{h}^{-\sigma}f\|_{L^{2}}.

We also write

(x_{1},x_{2})

\mathbb{R}^{2}

Our main results can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
(u_{0},B_{0},w_{0})\in H^{1}
with
{\nabla}\cdot u_{0}=0
and
{\nabla}\cdot B_{0}=0
. Then there exists a positive constant
\varepsilon>0
, such that if

\|(u_{0},B_{0},w_{0})\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{3}u_{0},\partial_{3}B_{0},\partial_{3}w_{0})\|_{L^{2}}\leq\varepsilon,

(1.4)

then (
1.2
) admits a unique global solution
(u,B,w)
satisfying

\sup\limits_{t\geq 0}\Big(\|(u,B,w)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{3}u,\partial_{3}B,\partial_{3}w)\|_{L^{2}}\Big)\leq C\varepsilon,

(1.5)

and

\sup\limits_{t\geq 0}\|(u,B,w)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\|(\nabla_{h}u,\nabla_{h}B,\nabla_{h}w)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}{\mathrm{d}}t\leq C\|(u_{0},B_{0},w_{0})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

(1.6)

Moreover, if
(u_{0},B_{0},w_{0})\in H^{k}
for some
k\geq 2
, then the above global solution

(u,B,w)
satisfies

\displaystyle\|(u,B,w)(t)\|_{H^{k}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|(\nabla_{h}u,\nabla_{h}B,\nabla_{h}w)\|_{H^{k}}^{2}{\mathrm{d}}\tau

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle C\|(u_{0},B_{0},w_{0})\|_{H^{k}}^{2}\exp\left\{C\int_{0}^{t}\|(\nabla_{h}u,\nabla_{h}B,\nabla_{h}w)\|_{H^{k-1}}^{2}{\mathrm{d}}\tau\right\},

(1.7)

for any
t\geq 0
, where
\nabla_{h}=(\partial_{1},\partial_{2})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
(u_{0},B_{0},w_{0})\in H^{k}
for
k\geq 4
with (
1.4
), and

(u_{0},B_{0},w_{0},\partial_{3}u_{0},\partial_{3}B_{0},\partial_{3}w_{0})\in\dot{H}_{h}^{-\sigma},

(1.8)

for some
\frac{k-1}{2(k-2)}<\sigma<1
. Then the global solution
(u,B,w)
of (
1.2
) satisfies

\displaystyle\big\|(u,B,w,\partial_{3}u,\partial_{3}B,\partial_{3}w)(t)\big\|_{\dot{H}_{h}^{-\sigma}}\leq~

\displaystyle C,

\displaystyle\big\|(u,B,w,\partial_{3}u,\partial_{3}B,\partial_{3}w)(t)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq~

\displaystyle C(1+t)^{-\sigma},

\displaystyle\big\|(\nabla_{h}u,\nabla_{h}B,\nabla_{h}w)(t)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq~

\displaystyle C(1+t)^{-(1+\sigma)},

(1.9)

for any
t>0
.

Now we outline the proof of our main results. The first part is to prove the stability of the solution. Denote
U=(u,B,w)^{\top}
. A key observation from
[
26
]
is that, by making a suitable linear combination of the
a priori
estimates of
\|U\|_{L^{2}}
,
\|\partial_{3}U\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|{\nabla}_{h}U\|_{L^{2}}
, we are able to derive estimates of the following type

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}t}\|(U,\partial_{3}U)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|({\nabla}_{h}U,{\nabla}_{h}\partial_{3}U)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq~

\displaystyle C\|\partial_{3}U\|_{L^{2}}\|({\nabla}_{h}U,{\nabla}_{h}\partial_{3}U)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}t}\|U\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+C\|{\nabla}_{h}U\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq~

\displaystyle C\|(U,\partial_{3}U)\|_{L^{2}}\|{\nabla}_{h}U\|_{H^{1}}^{2},

see (
3.1
) and (
3.1
) for details. Therefore, through a standard bootstrap argument, one finds that we only need the
L^{2}
-norm of the initial data and its vertical derivatives to be small to establish the
H^{1}
-stability of the solution. This improves the corresponding result in
[
29
]
, where the authors need the initial data to be sufficiently small in
H^{1}
-space to obtain the stability of 3D anisotropic MHD equations. Next, through an inductive process, we further prove that the
H^{k}
-norm of the solution is uniformly bounded via its
H^{k-1}
-norm. As a result, we establish the
H^{k}
-stability of the solution without assuming the higher-order derivatives of the initial data to be small.

The second part is to establish decay estimates of the solution. Inspired by
[
11
]
, we make the ansatz that the following estimate holds for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\big\|(\Lambda_{h}^{-\sigma}U,\Lambda_{h}^{-\sigma}\partial_{3}U)(t)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2E_{0},

where
T>0
is a finite time guaranteed by the local well-posedness theory, and

E_{0}:=\big\|(\Lambda_{h}^{-\sigma}U_{0},\Lambda_{h}^{-\sigma}\partial_{3}U_{0})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<\infty.

Here we note that we do not need
E_{0}
to be small. With the help of the above assumption, we are able to derive some decay estimates of
\|U\|_{L^{2}}
,
\|\partial_{3}U\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|{\nabla}_{h}U\|_{L^{2}}
on the time interval
[0,T]
, see Lemma
4
and Lemma
4
for details. Next, by invoking various anisotropic inequalities (see (
4
)), we obtain a better upper bound of
\|(\Lambda_{h}^{-\sigma}U,\Lambda_{h}^{-\sigma}\partial_{3}U)\|_{L^{2}}
. Therefore, a standard bootstrap argument implies that the decay estimates hold for any
t>0
.

3

4

Preliminaries

In this section, we list some basic lemmas to be used later. The first lemma provides anisotropic estimates for the integral of a triple product (see
[

Lemma 2.1
.

The following estimates hold when the right-hand sides are all bounded,

\displaystyle\int|fgh|{\mathrm{d}}x\leq~

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{1}f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{2}g\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|h\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}h\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\partial_{1}f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\partial_{2}f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\partial_{1}\partial_{2}f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}g\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|h\|_{L^{2}}.

The second lemma provides an estimate for the
L^{q}
-norm of a one-dimensional function, which serves as a basic ingredient for anisotropic estimates (see
[
38
]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
q\in[2,\infty]
and
s>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}
. Then there exists a positive constant

such that for any
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, it holds that

\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{1-\frac{1}{s}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}\|\Lambda^{s}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{s}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}.

In particular, if
q=\infty
and

, then any

satisfies

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

\displaystyle 4\chi\int w\cdot({\nabla}\times u){\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\chi\|{\nabla}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+4\chi\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle+\int\left(\partial_{1}u_{1}+\partial_{2}u_{2}\right)|\partial_{3}B|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x-\int\partial_{3}u_{h}\cdot{\nabla}_{h}w\cdot\partial_{3}w{\mathrm{d}}x+\int\left(\partial_{1}u_{1}+\partial_{2}u_{2}\right)|\partial_{3}w|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x

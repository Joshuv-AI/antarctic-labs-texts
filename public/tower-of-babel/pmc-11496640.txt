Acute copper stress showed toxic effects on the physiological metabolism of Ulva lactuca, a common green macroalgae
Authors: Weimin Chen, Manying Sun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The pollution by heavy metals in coastal waters has gradually intensified due to industrial development. In this study, physiological responses of Ulva lactuca, one of the most common green seaweeds with important ecological and economic value in the global intertidal zone, to acute copper stress were investigated. Results showed that an increase in copper ions concentration significantly inhibited photosynthetic activity and inorganic nitrogen utilization by U. lactuca but, increased its respiration. Copper stress limited the activity and gene expression of enzymes related to carbon and nitrogen assimilation of U. lactuca. Under moderate copper stress, U. lactuca had higher soluble carbohydrate and soluble protein contents, whereas under high copper stress, these components decreased sharply. Copper stress increased malonaldehyde content, and relative electrical conductivity in U. lactuca, but changes in antioxidant enzyme activities were not significant and even slightly decreased. Moreover, the contents of 8-hydroxy-deoxyguanosine and polyADP ribose polymerase in U. lactuca increased by high Cu2+ concentration culture, indicating that oxidative damage caused by high Cu2+ level involved its DNA damage and interfered with DNA repair in the alga. Copper stress seemed to be more damaging to the carbon assimilation process of U. lactuca, resulting in weakened resistance to copper stress and lower growth rate. This reflected the threat of coastal high copper stress to intertidal biodiversity. This provided ecological reference for the assessment of offshore heavy metal pollution represented by copper.

Introduction

With the continuous development of coastal cities, the process of industrialization has been accelerating daily, resulting in the serious pollution of coastal waters. Heavy metal pollution of the marine environment has become a serious problem, and it even indirectly affects human health through the food chain1–4.

Some heavy metals, such as lead (Pb), copper (Cu), and cadmium (Cd), are the most common pollutants found in industrial and municipal sewage3,5. When heavy metals exceed a certain concentration, they harm algae, and the impact on different algae varies with the degree of pollution6. These effects are mainly manifested in algal metabolism, cell membrane permeability, photosynthesis, and respiration and even change the structure of the algal community in the natural environment3,7,8. The damage to macroalgae by heavy metals causes the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), which leads to changes in the level of soluble proteins in activities of enzymes9.

What’s more, excessive ROS such as OH- and O2- attack the 8th carbon atom of guanine base in DNA molecules and generate 8-hydroxy-deoxyguanosine (8-OHdG), leading to changes in DNA structure and function10,11. To prevent the effects of ROS, macroalgae activate their antioxidant systems, and the activities of SOD, CAT and POD gradually increase. polyADP ribose polymerase (PARP) is also involved in the repair of DNA single strand damage12. However, when the heavy metal content is too high, the activities of CAT, POD, and superoxide dismutase (SOD) begin to decrease13–16. Thus, ability to tolerate heavy metals is limited.

Copper is an essential trace element for the normal growth of algae, and it plays a dual role in metabolism and other physiological processes13,17,18. Appropriate concentrations of copper can promote photosynthesis and respiration19. However, excessive copper inhibits the growth, photosynthetic respiration, protein synthesis, antioxidant enzymes, and nitrate reductase (NR) activity in algae20,21.

Ulva species are the most abundant macroalgae in marine ecosystems worldwide, adapted to wide range of temperature and salinity changes, and have a strong tolerance to dry exposure. Due to its global distribution, high growth rate, and ability to adapt to various environmental conditions22–24, the algae of this genus are the main contributors to intertidal seaweed beds and have an important ecological significance in enriching offshore communities. Ulva species are rich in carbohydrates, crude fiber, minerals, fat, vitamins, etc., and they have good economic and medicinal value, they are collected or farmed in many countries and regions25–30.

Ulva species have a strong ability to accumulate copper, cadmium, and cause algae to be negatively affected by excessive heavy metal stress. For example, when heavy metals such as copper were enriched too much, they will replace Mg in chlorophyll, which will affect the photosynthesis of plants3,4,31,32. A lower concentration of copper promoted the absorption of nitrate by Ulva algae, but a higher concentration inhibited its absorption of nitrate33,34. Thus, the effect of Cu on the physiological metabolism of Ulva algae changes with its concentration. In this study, Ulva lactuca L. was selected as the research model to investigate the physiological response characteristics of this species under acute Cu stress. As one of the most typical green algae in the global intertidal zone, the study on this alga will provide theoretical basis and reference for the assessment of coastal heavy metal pollution represented by copper and the use of this species as an indicator for coastal heavy metal pollution.

Materials and methods

Sample collection and experimental treatments

U. lactuca were collected from Dongtou Island in South China and transferred to the laboratory after the visible epiphytes and accumulated sediments were washed away. Then they were cultured in sterile seawater with 200 µmol L–1 nitrate and 10 µmol L–1 phosphate and aerated with ambient air. The culture medium is updated every other day. Environmental conditions provided in a plant incubator (NLF90FPQ-300CY, Ningbo Laifu, China) included a temperature of 20.3 (± 1) ℃, a density of 100 µmol photons m− 2 s− 1, and a photocycle of 12:12 (light: dark).

After 3 d preculture, U. lactuca were cultured at three Cu2+ levels, natural seawater as a control (Cu2+ < 0.001 mmol L–1) and enriched with 0.02 mmol L–1 (MCu) and 0.04 mmol L–1 (HCu) of copper17,18. 30 g of U. lactuca (fresh tissue) were cultivated in 10 L of seawater for each culture under same conditions as above, and the culture medium was renewed every other day. Each copper level had three cultures. After 5 d of indoor culture, physiological and biochemical tests were performed.

Photosynthetic activity

Chlorophyll a fluorescence was determined during the light period by using a portable pulse amplitude modulated (PAM) fluorometer (JUNIOR-PAM; Walz, Germany). Each sample was subjected to 30 min of dark adaptation. A rapid light curves (RLC) of electron transfer rate (ETR) was obtained by exposing the sample to 20 s blue light in eight incremental steps of 0–820 µmol photon m− 2 s− 126. Clark- type oxygen electrode (YSI Model 5300; Yellow Springs Instrument Co., OH, USA)) was used to determine the maximum net photosynthetic O2 evolution rate (NPRm) and dark respiration rate (Rd) of each sample. The water jacket of the oxygen electrode was connected to a cooling circulator (Cole Parmer, USA), which was used to maintain the temperature at the culture level. 0.10 g (fresh weight, Fw) of algae was introduced into a culture chamber containing 8 mL of medium they were exposed to during the trial, and magnetically stirred. After domestication for 10 min, NPRm was determined at saturation light intensity of 400 µmol photons m− 2 s− 135. The light intensity was provided by halogen lamps (Essential G4, Philips). The Rd value was obtained by measuring the consumed dark O2, and TPRm was the sum of the NRPm value and the consumed dark O2 value. All photosynthetic rates were expressed as µmol O2 g− 1 Fw h− 1.

The contents of Chl a, Chl b and carotenoids (Caro) in seaweed were estimated according to Porra37 and Parsons and Strickland38, respectively. A sample of about 0.1 g (Fw) was ground in 10 mL of absolute methanol and extracted in 4 °C darkness for 24 h. After centrifugation at 10,000 × g for 10 min, UV spectrophotometer (UV1800 Shimadzu, Japan) was used to determine its absorbance value at the wavelength of 480 nm, 510 nm, 630 nm, 645 nm, 652 nm, 665 nm, and 750 nm, to calculated the contents of Chl a, Chl b and Caro in the supernatant.

Nitrate absorption rate

We used the zinc-cadmium reduction method to measure nitrate concentrations in seawater after each water exchange during culture39. The rate of nitrate absorption by algae is expressed as µmol NO3–1 g–1 h–1.

Nitrate reductase activity

The activity of nitrate reductase (NR) was determined during one cycle of water exchange, according to the method described by Corzo et al.40. Approximately 0.1 g of algae and 10 mL of the reaction medium were added to the reaction flask. The reaction medium was consisted of 0.1 mol L–1 NaH2PO4 (buffer, pH 7.5), 0.5 mmol L–1 EDTA, 0.1% (v/v) propanol, 0.01 mmol L–1 glucose, and 50 mmol L–1 NaNO3. After three minutes of injecting nitrogen into the reaction solution, the reaction flask was then maintained in the dark at temperature of 30 °C for 30 min. Then 5 mL of the solution was induced into tube, and 2 mL of 1% sulfonate reagent and 2 mL of 0.1% N-(1-naphthyl ethylenediamine hydrochloride) were added into the tube. The absorbance of the sample was then measured at 543 nm. NR activity was expressed in µmol NO2–1 g–1 h–1.

Malonaldehyde and activities of antioxidant related enzymes

Malondialdehyde (MDA) in algae was determined by thiobarbituric acid (TBA) reaction to indicate lipid peroxidation41. Absorbance was measured at 532 nm and non-specific turbidity was corrected by subtracting the value at 600 nm. The extinction coefficient of 155 mmol L–1 cm–1 was used to calculate MDA concentration.

The activities of total superoxide dismutase (T-SOD), peroxidase (POD) and catalase (CAT) were analyzed using a colorimetric assay kit (Jianjieng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Determination of relative electrical conductivity

The relative electrical conductivity (REC) of seaweed was determined by mainly referring to the method of Wu et al.42. About 0.1 g of fresh seaweed samples were placed into a centrifuge tube containing 20 mL of deionized water, and the electrical conductivity (EC1) was measured after 2 h of oscillation. The sample was then placed in a boiling water bath for 10 min, cooled to room temperature, shaken for 10 min, and the electrical conductivity (EC2) was measured. Assessment of DNA damage under copper stress

The content of 8-OHdG in algae was determined by the method modified from Yin et al.43. After 0.1 g sample was ground with liquid nitrogen, 9 times the volume of PBS buffer (mixed with 0.01 mol L–1 Na2HPO4 and 0.0018 mol L–1 KH2PO4, pH = 7.4) was added to homogenate according to the ratio of weight (g): volume (ml) = 1:9. After centrifugation at 10,000 × g for 10 min, the supernatant was taken and OD values of each hole were measured at 450 nm wavelength by double antibody sandwich method.

The determination of PARP in algal cells was mainly based on the method of Wei et al.44 and improved. After about 0.2 g of algal body was ground and homogenized, the supernatant and antibody reaction were detected, and the color depth was positively correlated with the PARP in the sample. The absorbance of the sample was measured at 450 nm wavelength by microplate reader and the PARP activity was calculated.

Changes of gene expression related to carbon and nitrogen assimilation metabolism

After 3d indoor culture, the treated seaweed samples were washed with sterile seawater, then rinsed with deionized water (ddH2O) for several times and dried with absorbent paper. The samples were rapidly frozen with liquid nitrogen, and total RNA was extracted using the M5 plant Rneasy Complex Mini Kit. After DNase I digestion, the concentration and purity of the extracted RNA were determined by NanoDrop 2000 (Thermo Fisher) and agarose gel electrophoresis. cDNA was obtained by reverse transcription using Takara-primeScript™ RT reagent Kit (Code No. RR047A, Japan).

The details of quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) were as follows. Reaction at 95 °C for 30 s for predenaturation. PCR reaction: Reaction at 95 °C for 5 s, then at 60 °C for 20 s, a total of 40 cycles was performed. Melting curve analysis procedure: Reaction at 65 °C for 15 s. The primer premier 6 software (v6.24) was used to design specific primers for target genes. Two key enzymes in the assimilation process of photosynthetic carbon, 6-phosphogluconate dehydrogenase (6PD), GDP-mannose 3.5-epimerase 2 (GDPM), and light harvesting protein (LhcSR) were used as one of the indicators of photosynthetic activity. Three enzymes, nitrite reductase (NiR), glutamine synthetase (GS) and glutamate synthase (GOGAT), were selected to characterize the nitrogen assimilation activity of algal bodies (Table 1). The target gene and reference gene 18 S were analyzed by qRT-PCR. All reactions were performed on a real-time polymerase chain reaction system, and gene expression was calculated using 2−ΔΔCt.

Content of soluble carbohydrates and proteins

About 0.1 g of seaweed (Fw) was ground with 10 mL of distilled water. The crude extract was then centrifuged at 3000 × g for 10 min. The content of soluble carbohydrate (SC) in the supernatant was determined by phenol-sulfuric acid method using glucose as the standard substance45. Soluble protein (SP) was determined with Coomath bright Blue G-250 dye, using bovine albumin as the standard46.

Statistical analysis

The data were initially organized in Excel (office365) and then analyzed by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) and Tukey’s tests using a statistical package Origin 9.0 (OriginLab, USA), to analyze differences between treatments and control (p ≤ 0.05). The regression analysis of the data was carried out by the least square method of SPSS 24.0 software and the Graphpad prism 9. All data were presented as mean ± SD values for at least three measurements.

NPRm and TPRm values of U. lactuca decreased significantly with increasing copper concentration (p < 0.01), Rd also increased significantly under high copper concentration (p < 0.01). However, Rd of the algae in high concentration was significantly lower than that in medium concentration (Fig. 1d; p < 0.05).

Discussions

Low concentrations of copper (approximately below 0.01 mmol L–1) are essential for macroalgae photosynthesis and growth, but higher concentrations of this metal generally inhibit their photosynthesis13,17–19. In this experiment, the electron transport rates and photosynthetic rates of U. lactuca were significantly inhibited by the increase in copper concentration. In photosynthetic organisms, copper is an essential component of proteocyanin, a protein involved in electron transport in chloroplasts13. The photosynthetic activity of Ulva algae had been shown to be inhibited by high concentrations of copper and zinc ions and others21,33,47. Excess copper binds to the D1 protein and destroys both the donor and acceptor sides of PSII, thereby inhibiting electron donation in primary photochemical reactions48,49. Therefore, we speculated that, as in other plants, excess copper wis likely to interfere with photosynthetic activity in U. lactuca by disrupting its photosynthetic system.

Although the respiratory intensities of U. lactuca grown at high copper concentrations increased, Rd was significantly lower at high concentrations than at moderate concentrations. Algae were thought to require more energy to maintain normal metabolism because the protective mechanisms for copper were energy-intensive48,50. Although Ulva algae can enhance their respiratory intensity under a certain amount of heavy metal stress47,51, high concentrations of heavy metals have a toxic effect on the respiration chain.

The effect of high concentrations of copper on the photosynthesis of algae was also related to changes in chloroplast pigment composition and ultrastructure and decrease in the net photosynthetic rate52. Our study showed that the decrease in Chl a content and Chl a/(a + b) in U. lactuca at moderate copper concentrations was not significant, whereas the Caro content was slightly increased. This suggest that U. lactuca has a tolerance mechanism for a certain range of copper stress. Under heavy metal stress, maintenance of a relatively high Caro content can effectively remove reactive oxygen species (ROS), prevent membrane lipid peroxidation, and maintain membrane structure stability in algae53. However, high copper stress significantly reduced the content of photosynthetic pigments in U. lactuca. Copper can affect photosynthesis by affecting membrane permeability, interfering with ion distribution, inhibiting enzyme activity and chloroplast function, and even leading to the death of the algae16,54. In the present study, high copper stress decreased the gene expression of 6PD and GDPM, enzymes related to U. lactuca carbon assimilation. Combined with the inhibited photosynthetic activities, algae growth is inhibited due to the lack of sufficient carbon sequestration efficiency.

In this study, U. lactuca had higher SC and SP contents under moderate copper stress. These findings have been reported in many seaweeds16. Under heavy metal stress, algae reduce the water potential of cells by increasing the content of solutes or osmotic regulatory substances to maintain cell water content55. In addition, exclusion relies primarily on the adsorption of metals to cell walls, surface organisms, and exudated organic materials such as polysaccharides and phenolic compounds56. Previous studies have shown that the cell wall structure and physiological characteristics of algae provide the first defense barrier against heavy metal pollution. Polysaccharides, proteins, lipids, and other polymeric complexes on the cell wall of algae provide many functional groups (such as amino, sulfur, rho, and pthol) that can bind to metal ions3,7. For instance, at high copper concentrations, the fixation of Ulva rigida on copper was proportional to the content of uronic acid and the participation of sulfate in the binding process3,31. Therefore, at a certain concentration, the stimulus was formed, and the algal body synthesizes more protein to make the related substance. Once heavy metals penetrate into the cell, metal-binding proteins or polypeptides such as metallothionein, phytochelating proteins, and oligomers of glutathione begin to control their bioavailability by chelating free metals17,50. However, protein and carbohydrate syntheses are energy-intensive processes, indicating increased respiration under high copper levels in this study.

As the copper concentration increased in the seawater, both nitrogen utilization and NR activity in U. lactuca were inhibited. Inhibition of NR activity by high copper concentrations has also been shown in other Ulva species33. Although the gene expression of NiR was not affected by copper stress, the gene expressions of GS and GOGAT related to nitrogen assimilation were significantly inhibited. Although U. lactuca can resist the effects of copper stress by increasing the content of SC and SP through enhancing energy consumption, the damage effects caused by high concentration of copper stress were still high. This can also be observed from the increase in MDA content and REC values, and the changes in the antioxidant enzyme system activity of U. lactuca. Many previous reports had shown that seaweed accumulated MDA when subjected to extreme stress2,14,57.

Under high copper stress, the adaptation of Ulva species to heavy metal-induced oxidative stress apparently is an effective enzymatic and non-enzymatic antioxidant device to maintain ROS levels under a fitted control and increasing the ability to produce non-enzymatic LPX, H2O2, and AsA and enzymatic lipoperoxides, hydrogen peroxide and ascorbate antioxidant compounds as a defense system58,59. Copper acts as a cofactor for enzymes such as SOD, cytochrome c oxidase, and ascorbate oxidase in macroalgae17. In this study, we observed that medium copper concentration significantly increased the MDA content and activated the antioxidant system of U. lactuca and enhanced the activities of antioxidant enzymes including CAT, SOD, POD. These defense strategies help macroalgae prevent or at least reduce oxidative impairment and buffer ROS levels. However, the high content of MDA and REC indicated that the antioxidative mechanism of U. lactuca did not work well in the presence of high concentration of Cu2+. We detected significantly increased intracellular 8-OHDG and PARP levels in the algae. This suggested that the oxidative damage caused by high copper ions involved damage to the DNA in the algae and interfered with its DNA repair mechanism60,61. Although PARP can be involved in the repair of DNA single strand damage by activating the C-terminal catalytic domain and hydrolyzing NAD+ to generate PAR polymers62, the generated 8-OHDG can pair with any base, and when the increase of 8-OHdG caused organelle dysfunction, the DNA, proteins, and lipids are permanently damaged, leading to cell death63. This further possibly aggravated the inhibiting effect of carbon and nitrogen assimilation under copper stress.

In the present study, at excessively high copper concentrations, U. lactuca had a higher MDA content, but the changes in the antioxidant enzyme activity were not significant and even decreased slightly. Under high copper stress, excessive O2−‧ catalyzed by copper, which cannot be cleared by the antioxidant enzyme system in time, causing damage to algal cells, especially membrane lipid peroxidation64. The SC and SP contents of U. lactuca decreased significantly under high copper stress. Under high copper stress, the photosynthetic activity and nitrogen uptake capacities of U. lactuca were seriously insufficient. Moreover, due to the damage of the membrane, the enzyme activities were inhibited, resulting in insufficient carbon and nitrogen assimilation capacities of this algae, thereby inhibiting the production of SC and SP, which were served as osmoregulatory substances65. In addition, proteins can be directly inactivated due to amino acid-metal interactions16,18, the protein activity was restricted and destroyed by heavy metals; this resulted in a sharply weakened resistance to high copper concentrations, and the negative effects of copper stress on the algae were intensified. This further lead to increased inactivation of proteins such as the antioxidant system in U. lactuca. In addition, the concentration of copper exceeds the defense capacity of the macroalgae, and its macromolecules such as proteins, lipids, and DNA deteriorate, ultimately leading to cell death66.

Conclusion

As one of the most typical green algae in the global intertidal zone, U. lactuca’s carbon and nitrogen utilization were inhibited by acute copper stress. Copper stress increased malonaldehyde content, and REC in U. lactuca, and these oxidative damages aused by high Cu2+ involved its DNA damage and interfered with DNA repair in the alga. Although U. lactuca showed a certain resistance to copper stress, photosynthesis of U. lactuca was sharply inhibited at a high copper concentration. High Cu2+ also limited the activities and gene expressions of enzymes associated with carbon and nitrogen assimilation of U. lactuca. Copper stress seemed to be more damaging to the carbon assimilation process of U. lactuca, resulting in weakened resistance to copper stress and lower growth rate. As one of the most typical species in the construction of intertidal habitats, the growth of U. lactuca is inhibited or even disappeared due to copper stress, which poses a serious threat to intertidal biodiversity. This provided ecological reference for the assessment of offshore heavy metal pollution represented by copper.

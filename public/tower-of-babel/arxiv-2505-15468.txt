Fourier decay in parabolic C 1 + α C^{1+\alpha} systems with overlaps Thanks: The authors are all supported by the Research Council of Finland’s Academy Research Fellowship “Quantum chaos of large and many body systems” , grant Nos. 347365, 353738.
Gaétan Leclerc, Sampo Paukkonen, Tuomas Sahlsten

Abstract
. We establish power Fourier decay for equilibrium states of parabolic C 1 + α C^{1+\alpha} iterated function systems with overlaps satisfying a multiscale nonlinearity condition. This class includes the Lyons conductance measures ν t \nu_{t} , 0 < t < 1 0<t<1 , associated to Galton-Watson trees with equal weights yielding advance towards a conjecture of Lyons on the absolute continuity of ν t \nu_{t} for small t t . Further applications include Patterson-Sullivan measures for cusped hyperbolic surfaces, extending the work of Bourgain and Dyatlov to parabolic settings, conformal measures for Manneville-Pommeau and Lorenz-type maps, and the construction of the first genuinely C 1 + α C^{1+\alpha} IFSs whose attractors have positive Fourier dimension but are not C 1 C^{1} -conjugate to linear IFSs. The proof combines the Bourgain-Dyatlov sum-product strategy with a multiscale induction approach that bypasses the use of spectral gaps for twisted transfer operators needed in several other works in the area.

1.2.
QNL and power Fourier decay

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a finite or countable alphabet and let

\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}=\{(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots)\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}:\ A(a_{i},a_{i+1})=1\}
be a subshift of finite type with admissible words

\Sigma^{n}
and
\Sigma^{*}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\Sigma^{n}
.
Let
\{I_{a}\}_{a\in\mathcal{A}}
be open intervals in
[0,1]
and for
each admissible pair
ab
let

g_{ab}:I_{b}\to I_{a}
be a
C^{1+\alpha}
-diffeomorphic contraction, defining

g_{\mathbf{a}}:=g_{a_{1}a_{2}}\circ\cdots\circ g_{a_{n-1}a_{n}}
for

\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n}
. Assume uniform
\kappa_{+}
-contraction

\kappa_{-}(ab)\leq|g_{ab}^{\prime}(x)|\leq\kappa_{+}<1
and bounded

(C,\alpha)
-distortion

|\log|g_{ab}^{\prime}(x)|-\log|g_{ab}^{\prime}(y)||\leq C|x-y|^{\alpha}
for all
x,y
. Then there exists attractor

F=F_{\Phi}:=\bigcap_{n\geq 1}\ \bigcup_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n}}\overline{g_{\mathbf{a}}(I_{a_{n}})}
.
Let
\phi
be a normalised potential with summable variations and let

\mu_{\phi}
be the associated Gibbs measure. In the countable-branch
case we additionally assume the light-tail condition: there exists

\gamma_{0}>0
such that

\sum_{ab\in\Sigma^{2}}\ \sup_{x\in I_{b}}e^{\phi(g_{ab}(x))}\,|g_{ab}^{\prime}(x)|^{-\gamma_{0}}<\infty.

For
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\Sigma^{n}
and
x,y\in F
define

\Delta_{n}((\mathbf{a},x),(\mathbf{b},y)):=(\log g_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}(x)-\log g_{\mathbf{b}}^{\prime}(x))-(\log g_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}(y)-\log g_{\mathbf{b}}^{\prime}(y)).

We say that
\Phi
satisfies
(QNL)
if there exist

\rho,\Theta\in(0,1)
such that for any interval

I\subset\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sum_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}\in\Sigma^{n}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}})\mu(I_{\mathbf{b}})\mu(I_{\mathbf{c}})\mu(I_{\mathbf{d}})\,\mathbf{1}_{I}\!\left(\Delta_{n}((\mathbf{a},x_{\mathbf{c}}),(\mathbf{b},x_{\mathbf{d}}))\right)\lesssim|I|^{\Theta}+\rho^{n}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Phi
be a
C^{1+\alpha}
IFS as above, satisfying uniform

\kappa_{+}
-contraction and bounded
(C,\alpha)
-distortion. Let
\phi

be a normalised potential with summable variations satisfying the
light-tail condition, and let
\mu_{\phi}
be the associated Gibbs
measure without atoms. If
(QNL)
holds, then there exists
\beta>0
such that

\Big|\int\chi(x,\xi)\,e^{i\psi(x,\xi)}\,d\mu_{\phi}(x)\Big|\ \lesssim\ |\xi|^{-\beta},\qquad|\xi|\to\infty,

for every pair
\chi:I\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\psi:I\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with

\|\chi(\cdot,\xi)\|_{C^{\alpha}}\lesssim|\xi|^{\varepsilon_{\chi}},\qquad\|\log\partial_{x}\psi(\cdot,\xi)\|_{C^{\alpha}}\lesssim|\xi|^{1+\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\psi}},\qquad\inf_{x}|\partial_{x}\psi(x,\xi)|\gtrsim|\xi|^{1-\varepsilon_{\psi}},

for some sufficiently small

\varepsilon_{\chi},\varepsilon_{\psi},\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\psi}>0
.

To make Theorem
1.1
usable in applications, we seek
checkable sufficient conditions for
(QNL)
. In the
C^{2}

uniformly hyperbolic setting, Fourier decay is typically obtained by
establishing spectral gaps for twisted transfer operators via
Dolgopyat-type estimates (e.g.
[
47
,
67
,
7
,
13
,
11
]
). A dynamical formulation of the required
non-concentration is the
Uniform Non-Integrability (UNI)

condition introduced by Chernov and Dolgopyat
[
31
]
. Defining cocycle

\tau(g_{\mathbf{a}}(x)):=-\log|g_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}(x)|
and fluctuation

X_{\mathbf{a}}(x,y):=\tau(g_{\mathbf{a}}(x))-\tau(g_{\mathbf{a}}(y))
, we say
that
\Phi
satisfies
(UNI)
if there exist
c_{0}>0
and
infinite
N
such that for every
c\in\mathcal{A}
there exist

\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\Sigma^{N}
with
b(\mathbf{a})=b(\mathbf{b})=c
satisfying

|X_{\mathbf{a}}(x,y)-X_{\mathbf{b}}(x,y)|\geq c_{0}|x-y|^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }x\neq y\in F\cap I_{c}.

However, in genuinely
C^{1+\alpha}
setting verifying UNI on the entire attractor becomes difficult leading to many difficulties e.g. in smooth dynamics
[
76
,
42
]
. In
these cases one instead seeks a statistical form of non-concentration
along the equilibrium state. Letting
\delta=\dim_{H}F
be the Hausdorff dimension and
\mu
be the
equilibrium state for
\delta\tau
, we say that the IFS
\Phi
satisfies the

Measure Non-Linearity (MNL)
condition if there exists

C_{\mathrm{(MNL)}}>0
such that for all
N
there are

\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\Sigma^{N}
with

\forall x_{0}\in F,\ \forall I\subset\mathbb{R},\qquad\big(X_{\mathbf{a}}(\cdot,x_{0})-X_{\mathbf{b}}(\cdot,x_{0})\big)_{*}\mu(I)\ \leq\ C_{\mathrm{(MNL)}}\,|I|.

This replaces the uniform lower bound in
(UNI)
by a
measure-theoretic non-concentration property along the symbolic tree,
in the spirit of the coupling of geometric and symbolic directions in

[
76
]
.

Under UNI or MNL, we then have the needed QNL for Theorem
1.1
:

Theorem 1.2
.

If infinite
C^{1+\alpha}
IFS has

(UNI)
, or in finite branch case
(MNL)
, then
(QNL)
holds.

Note here we allow arbitrary overlaps. The UNI part in finite branch case can be verified using spectral gaps for twisted transfer operators as in
[
67
]
and in overlapping case a co-cycle version of the Dolgopyat method
was needed and indeed developed in
[
7
,
13
]
via average contraction of random Dolgopyat operators. Our strategy avoids these constructions and replaces it by the multiscale induction allowing us to access parabolic and
C^{1+\alpha}
cases.

Let us now discuss applications of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
reducing power Fourier decay to UNI or MNL type statements.

1.3.
Smoothness of effective conductance of Galton-Watson trees.

Let
(p_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be a probability distribution on
\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and let
T
be the random genealogical tree obtained from the associated
Galton-Watson branching process
begun with one individual
\varnothing
, in which each vertex has
k
children with probability
p_{k}
, independently of all other vertices. Adjoining a parent vertex
v
to
\varnothing
and connecting it to
\varnothing
by an edge, we may view the resulting graph
T\cup\{v\}
as an electrical network in which each edge has unit conductance. Writing
\Theta
for the effective conductance of
T\cup\{\mathbf{v}\}
from
v
to infinity, we know that
\Theta
equals to the probability of a simple random walk starting from
v
and returning to
v

[
55
,
54
,
25
]
. Then the effective conductance is a random continued fraction
\Theta=\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{X_{1}+\frac{1}{1+\cdots}}}
for i.i.d.
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots
. In
[
53
]
, Lyons considered the special case where
X_{i}\in\{0,t\}
i.i.d. with equal probabilities. Thus in this case
\Theta
is obtained as the infinite random
composition
\Theta=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{X_{1}}\circ f_{X_{2}}\circ\cdots f_{X_{n}}(0)
for
f_{0}(x)=\frac{x}{1+x}
and
f_{t}(x)=\frac{x+t}{1+x+t}
so if we denote by
\nu_{t}
the law of
\Theta
, it is an equilibrium state for the overlapping IFS
\{f_{0},f_{t}\}
, which is parabolic as
f_{0}^{\prime}(0)=1
.

On the smoothness of
\nu_{t}
, Lyons
[
53
]
proved
\nu_{t}
is singular for any
t>t_{0}\approx 0.2689
and conjectured
\nu_{t}
should to be absolutely continuous for all small
t
. This was motivated by numerics and it being a nonlinear analogue of the Bernoulli convolution
\mu_{\lambda}
(distribution of
\sum\pm\lambda^{n}
), where a similar conjecture is widely open despite much recent progress
[
70
,
37
,
77
]
and for higher dimensional analogues of Bernoulli convolutions
[
50
]
. As with Bernoulli convolutions, absolute continuity and density information of
\nu_{t}
are known for almost every
t
by transversality method
[
72
,
73
,
75
,
16
]
. In
[
70
]
Shmerkin related the absolute continuity of
\mu_{\lambda}
to the power Fourier decay of Bernoulli convolutions associated to powers
\lambda^{k}
, but it remains a very challenging problem prove this, where the Erdös-Kahane method can be applied for almost every
\lambda
(see e.g.
[
74
,
70
]
).

By inducing the parabolic Lyons IFS
[
57
]
, as a consequence of Theorem
1.1
we have:

Theorem 1.3
.

For all
0<t<1
, the Lyons conductance measure
\nu_{t}
has power Fourier decay.

Now Shmerkin’s strategy
[
70
]
cannot be directly used with Theorem
1.3
to prove Lyons’s conjecture due to the lack of convolution structure of
\nu_{t}
. However, as in the work of Lindenstrauss and Varjú on the higher dimensional setting
[
50
]
, which is very different from the one dimensional case due to non-Abelian nature of
SO(d)
instead a spectral gap assumption on the underlying transfer operator was crucial in the proof of absolute continuity via a power Fourier decay property of the self-similar measure. Given that our proof gives a non-concentration for the Lyons IFS typically needed for spectral gaps
[
13
]
, one may try to adapt more directly the spectral gaps of twisted transfer operators associated to
\nu_{t}
as in
[
50
]
to try to upgrade Theorem
1.3
to a proof of Lyons conjecture.

1.4.
Patterson–Sullivan measures on parabolic limit sets.

Fourier decay for Patterson–Sullivan measures associated to convex co-compact hyperbolic surfaces was established by Bourgain and Dyatlov
[
18
]
as a tool to bound the resonance free regions for scattering resonances by proving improved the Fractal Uncertainty Principle bounds. Extension of this to convex co-compact subgroups of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
was done in
[
47
]
using twisted transfer operators and to higher dimensions in
[
12
]
adapting an
L^{2}
-flattening approach of Khalil
[
40
]
. We will now see that Theorem
1.1
allows us to expand these to geometrically finite case where there are parabolic elements in the group.

Let
X=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}
be a non-compact hyperbolic surface with cusps, where
\Gamma<\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
is torsion-free and geometrically finite with parabolic elements acting on
\mathbb{H}\cup\dot{\mathbb{R}}
(the extended real line, identified with
\mathbb{S}^{1}
in the disk model). Denote the limit set by
\Lambda_{\Gamma}\subset\dot{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\mu_{\Gamma}
be the Patterson–Sullivan measure centered at
i\in\mathbb{H}
. If
\delta>0
is the critical exponent of
\Gamma
, then
\mu_{\Gamma}
is
\delta
–conformal and satisfies, for all bounded Borel
f:\dot{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{C}
and all
\gamma\in\Gamma
,

\int_{\Lambda_{\Gamma}}f(x)\,d\mu_{\Gamma}(x)=\int_{\Lambda_{\Gamma}}f(\gamma(x))\,|\gamma^{\prime}(x)|_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}^{\,\delta}\,d\mu_{\Gamma}(x),

where
|\gamma^{\prime}(x)|_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}=\frac{1+x^{2}}{1+\gamma(x)^{2}}\,|\gamma^{\prime}(x)|
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Gamma<\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
be torsion-free, geometrically finite, with parabolic elements. Then there exists
\beta>0
such that

\Big|\int\chi(x,\xi)\,e^{i\psi(x,\xi)}\,d\mu_{\Gamma}(x)\Big|\ \lesssim\ |\xi|^{-\beta},\qquad|\xi|\to\infty,

for any
\chi:I\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\psi:I\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with

\|\chi(\cdot,\xi)\|_{C^{\alpha}}\lesssim|\xi|^{\varepsilon_{\chi}},\quad\|\log\partial_{x}\psi(\cdot,\xi)\|_{C^{\alpha}}\lesssim|\xi|^{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\psi}},\quad\inf_{x}|\partial_{x}\psi(x,\xi)|\gtrsim|\xi|^{1-\varepsilon_{\psi}},

for some sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{\chi},\varepsilon_{\psi},\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\psi}>0
.

Bourgain–Dyatlov
[
18
]
used Fourier decay to prove a Fractal Uncertainty Principle (FUP) for convex co-compact groups (
\delta_{\Gamma}<1/2
), with consequences for the distribution of scattering resonances. In the parabolic case one has
\delta_{\Gamma}\geq 1/2
, and the strategy in
[
18
]
does not apply directly. It would be interesting to investigate whether Theorem
1.4
has implications for the meromorphic continuation of the resolvent on geometrically finite manifolds with cusps (cf.
[
36
]
).

1.5.
Manneville–Pommeau and Lorenz maps.

Our axioms in Theorem
1.1
were designed with Young tower models in mind, obtained by inducing parabolic systems such as Lorenz maps (arising from the Lorenz flow)
[
52
,
29
]
and, in higher dimensions, the Chernov–Markarian–Zhang structure for slowly mixing billiards (e.g. the Bunimovich stadium)
[
23
,
56
,
19
]
. We give two applications: Manneville–Pommeau maps and Lorenz maps; the assumptions can be verified in broader induced settings.

(a)

Manneville–Pommeau maps.

We use the Liverani–Saussol–Vaienti parametrization
[
51
]
:

T_{\alpha}(x)=\begin{cases}x\big(1+2^{\alpha}x^{\alpha}\big),&x<\tfrac{1}{2},\\
2x-1,&x\geq\tfrac{1}{2},\end{cases}

with
T^{\prime}_{\alpha}(0)=1
(parabolic fixed point). Let
f_{0},f_{1}
be the inverse branches (with
f_{1}
the left, nonlinear one, and
f_{0}(x)=(x+1)/2
), set
x_{0}=1
,
x_{1}=\tfrac{1}{2}
,
x_{n+1}=f_{1}(x_{n})
, and
I=[\tfrac{1}{2},1]
,
I_{n}=[x_{n+1},x_{n})
. This gives a Markov partition of
[0,1]=T_{\alpha}(I)
with
I_{n-1}=T_{\alpha}(I_{n})
. Let
R:I\to\mathbb{N}
be the first return time to
I
, and define the induced map
T_{A}:I\to I
by
T_{A}(x)=T_{\alpha}^{R(x)}(x)
. Then
T_{A}
is an infinitely branched expanding Markov map with inverse branches
g_{a}:I\to A_{a}
,
a\geq 0
(where
A_{0}=[3/4,1]
and
A_{a}:=(T_{\alpha}^{-1}I_{a})\cap I
).

(b)

Lorenz maps.

Consider the map
f:J\setminus\{0\}\to J
,
J=[-1/2,1/2]
, defined by
[
8
, §3]

f(x)=\begin{cases}-a(-x)^{\alpha}+\tfrac{1}{2},&x<0,\\
ax^{\alpha}-\tfrac{1}{2},&x>0,\end{cases}\qquad\alpha:=-\lambda_{3}/\lambda_{1}\in(0,1),\ 0<a\leq 2^{\alpha},

arising from the Poincaré map of the geometric Lorenz flow (
\lambda_{2}<\lambda_{3}<0<-\lambda_{3}<\lambda_{1}
). The derivative of
f
blows up near
0
, and inverse branches have zero derivative there. Diaz-Ordaz
[
29
, Thm. 2]
constructs an induced expanding Markov map
T_{A}:I\to I
(for some neighborhood
I\subset[-1,1]
of
0
) with bounded distortion and first return time
R:I\to\mathbb{N}
having exponential tails, under uniform contraction (1) and bounded distortion (2).

Given a base potential
\phi_{0}
for the original system (e.g.
\phi_{0}=-\delta\log|T^{\prime}_{\alpha}|
for Manneville–Pommeau or
\phi_{0}=-\delta\log|f^{\prime}|
for Lorenz), define the induced potential on
I
by
\phi(x):=S_{R(x)}\phi_{0}(x)
. For our results we require
summable variations
for
\phi
:

\sum_{n\geq 1}V_{n}(\phi)<\infty
, where
V_{n}(\phi):=\sup_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n}}\ \sup_{x,y\in I_{\mathbf{a}}}|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|,

which ensures that the Sarig theory applies to the countable Markov map
T_{A}

[
69
,
68
]
. A sufficient condition is
\sum_{n}n\,V_{n}(\phi_{0})<\infty

[
20
, §2.4]
. Let
\mu_{\phi}
be a Gibbs measure for the induced map
T_{A}
. Define the projected measure on the parabolic system by

\nu_{\phi}(E):=\sum_{a=0}^{\infty}\ \sum_{k=0}^{a}\mu_{\phi}\big(T_{\alpha}^{-k}(E)\cap A_{a}\big),\qquad E\subset I,

and normalize by
\int R\,d\mu_{\phi}
when finite. This is the standard way (e.g. for SRB/conformal measures) to transfer statistical properties from the Young tower to the base system; see
[
20
,
27
]
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume
\phi:I\to\mathbb{R}
has summable variations and, for some
\gamma_{0}>0
,

\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\ \sup_{x\in I}w_{a}(x)\,|g_{a}^{\prime}(x)|^{-\gamma_{0}}<\infty,

where
w_{a}(x)=e^{\phi(g_{a}(x))}
. Let
\mu_{\phi}
be the Gibbs measure for
T_{A}
arising from the Manneville–Pommeau map
T_{\alpha}
or Lorenz map
f
. Then the projected parabolic measure
\nu_{\phi}
for
T_{\alpha}
(or Lorenz map
f
respectively) satisfies, for some
\beta>0
,

\Big|\int\chi(x)\,e^{i\xi\psi(x)}\,d\nu_{\phi}(x)\Big|\ \lesssim\ |\xi|^{-\beta},

for any
\chi,\psi:I\to\mathbb{R}
with
\operatorname{supp}\chi
disjoint from the parabolic fixed point,
\|\chi\|_{C^{\alpha}}<\infty
,
\|\psi\|_{C^{1+\alpha}}<\infty
, and
\inf|\psi^{\prime}|>0
.

For Manneville–Pommeau maps
T_{\alpha}
with
0<\delta<1
and
0<\alpha<1
, the choice
\phi_{0}=-\delta\log T^{\prime}_{\alpha}
yields an induced potential
\phi=S_{R}\phi_{0}
with summable variations and a light-tail condition for any
\gamma_{0}>0
(see
[
20
,
27
]
); hence Theorem
1.5
gives that the
\delta
–conformal measure of
T_{\alpha}
has positive Fourier dimension. The same argument applies to Lorenz maps once (UNI) is verified for the induced model (see Section
9
for examples of parameters, where this holds).

Remark
.

The assumption that
\operatorname{supp}\chi
avoids the parabolic fixed point can be relaxed to a local decay condition; we keep the support condition for clarity of statement.

An intriguing direction is to relax the light-tail assumption. Because Theorem
1.1
is formulated in terms of exponential moment conditions, it is well-suited to being adapted to heavier tails, where one might expect sub-polynomial (e.g. polylogarithmic) Fourier decay. In the context of Furstenberg measures, such log–log decay has already been demonstrated in
[
30
]
. Extending our framework to this regime would provide a natural bridge between strong polynomial decay and much weaker forms of Fourier decay, potentially revealing new structures in dynamical systems with weaker hyperbolicity.

1.6.
C^{1+\alpha}
iterated function systems.

Finally, many dynamically defined measures of interest arise from systems that admit only
C^{1+\alpha}
regularity, for instance due to rough holonomies in codings of hyperbolic diffeomorphisms or in nonlinear cocycle models such as circle extensions and the Fibonacci Hamiltonian. In such settings the associated temporal distance functions are typically non-smooth, and the classical Fourier decay mechanisms based on Dolgopyat-type spectral gap estimates are not available.

Our framework applies in this genuinely low-regularity regime. In particular, we construct examples of
C^{1+\alpha}
iterated function systems whose conformal measures admit power Fourier decay despite not being
C^{1}
-conjugate to linear models, that is, there is no
C^{1}
map
h
such that
\{hfh^{-1}:f\in\Phi\}
consists of maps with constant derivative.

Theorem 1.6
.

There exist
C^{1+\alpha}
iterated function systems with finitely many branches whose attractors support equilibrium states with power Fourier decay, and which are not
C^{1}
-conjugate to linear IFS.

In particular, these examples provide conformal measures of positive Fourier dimension arising from genuinely
C^{1+\alpha}
dynamics. The construction proceeds by coupling the symbolic and geometric oscillations of the distortion function through a novel fixed-point argument in the moduli space of IFS parametrised by equilibrium states. This allows us to verify the multiscale nonlinearity condition required by Theorem
1.1
without relying on higher regularity or smooth symbolic holonomies.

The condition on MNL is quite restrictive as it requires oscillations at right dimension on the fractal. This suggests the possibility of developing a
C^{1+}
theory of Fourier decay, spectral gaps and fractal uncertainty principles in settings where the distortion functions exhibit only autosimilar or fractal-type oscillations (e.g. Brownian/Weierstrass-type as in
[
76
,
42
]
), rather than smooth curvature.

Finally, let us remark on the opposite case when the system is conjugated to a linear IFS. The analytic and
C^{2}
settings (e.g.
[
7
,
13
,
10
,
1
,
15
,
5
]
), power Fourier decay is typically classified through the presence of non-linear branches with non-vanishing second derivative or non-flatness condition of the map
[
1
]
. In our rough
C^{1+\alpha}
framework,
autosimilarity
of the distortion function might play the role of the second derivative giving the necessary non-flatness property needed in
[
1
]
. Establishing such a replacement would move the theory toward a genuine characterisation of power Fourier decay at the
C^{1+\alpha}
regularity threshold.

2.1.
Symbolic coding, Gibbs measures and transfer operators

Recall the notation from Section
1.2
, where
\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}:=\{(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots)\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}:\ A(a_{i},a_{i+1})=1\ \text{for all }i\}
is the countable Markov shift
determined by a matrix
A\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}}
and
\Sigma^{n}
is the set of admissible words of length
n
and by
\Sigma^{*}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\Sigma^{n}
. Then a word
\mathbf{a}:=a_{1}\dots a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
satisfying
A(a_{i},a_{i+1})=1
for all
i
is called
admissible
and cylinder
[\mathbf{a}]\subset\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
is the subtree of infinite words with initial
\mathbf{a}
. We assume there is a collection
\{I_{a}\}_{a\in\mathcal{A}}
of possibly overlapping open intervals in
[0,1]
and for each admissible pair
ab\in\Sigma^{2}
we are given a
C^{1+\alpha}
contraction

g_{ab}:I_{b}\to I_{a},

which form an
iterated function system

\Phi=\{g_{ab}:ab\in\mathcal{A}^{2}\ \text{admissible}\}
.
For an admissible word
\mathbf{a}=a_{1}\cdots a_{n}\in\Sigma^{n}
we define
g_{\mathbf{a}}:=g_{a_{1}a_{2}}\circ\cdots\circ g_{a_{n-1}a_{n}}.
Recall that the notation
\mathbf{a}\to x
simply means that
g_{\mathbf{a}}
is defined at
x
, i.e.
x\in I_{b\left(\mathbf{a}\right)}
with
b\left(\mathbf{a}\right)
denoting the last letter of
\mathbf{a}
.

We suppose the IFS
\Phi=\{g_{ab}:ab\in\mathcal{A}^{2}\ \text{admissible}\}
satisfies the conditions (1)-(3) from Section
1.2
, though in this section we only need uniform
\kappa_{+}
contraction (1) and bounded
(C,\alpha)
-distortions (2). The uniform contraction of the maps
g_{ab}
guarantees that there exists a unique attractor
F\subset I
associated to
\Phi
, that is, for
\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n+1}=a_{0}\dots a_{n}
the map
g_{\mathbf{a}}:=g_{a_{0}a_{1}}\circ\dots\circ g_{a_{n-1}a_{n}}:I_{a_{n}}\rightarrow I_{a_{0}}
, and the interval
I_{\mathbf{a}}:=g_{\mathbf{a}}(I_{a_{n}})
, then
F
is the compact set given by
F=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n}}\overline{I_{\mathbf{a}}}
. This also gives us a projection
\pi:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to F
.

Since the IFS
\Phi
we work on in Theorem
1.1
has overlaps, the map
\pi
can be very badly away from being injective, we will need to consider thermodynamical formalism and the associated measures
\mu
as projections of measures on the symbolic space
\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
. Formally, if
\phi:F\to\mathbb{R}
is a potential, we can consider
\phi
also as a map
\phi\circ\pi:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{R}
. Then if we let
\nu_{\phi}
be the (symbolic) Gibbs measure associated to
\phi
on
\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
, we mean that if is a probability measure satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}}f(\mathbf{a})\,d\nu_{\phi}(\mathbf{a})=\sum_{ab\in\Sigma^{2}}\int_{[b]}e^{\phi(g_{ab}(\mathbf{a}))}f(g_{ab}(\mathbf{a}))\,d\nu_{\phi}(\mathbf{a})

for all continuous
f:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{C}
. Here
b(\mathbf{a})
is the last letter of a word. Thus this implies the projected Gibbs measure
\mu_{\phi}:=\pi\mu_{\phi}
on the attractor
F
satisfies the
\phi
-conformality condition

\displaystyle\int_{F}f(x)\,d\mu_{\phi}(x)=\sum_{ab\in\Sigma^{2}}\int_{I_{b}}e^{\phi(g_{ab}(x))}f(g_{ab}(x))\,d\mu_{\phi}(x)

for all continuous
f:F\to\mathbb{C}
and note by the definition of
b(\mathbf{a})
we can write
g_{\mathbf{a}}:I_{b(\mathbf{a})}\rightarrow I_{\mathbf{a}}
. So from now on when we write
\mu_{\phi}
is a Gibbs measure, we assume
\mu_{\phi}
comes as a projection of a symbolic measure
\nu_{\phi}
defined on
\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
. We also assume
\mu_{\phi}
satisfies the light
(C_{\phi,\gamma_{0}},\gamma_{0})
-tail condition (4) from Section
1.2
.

Now the
\phi
-conformality condition can be iterated to yields the formula:

\forall f\in C^{0}(F,\mathbb{C}),\ \int_{F}fd\mu=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n+1}}\int_{b(\mathbf{a})}e^{S_{n}\phi(g_{\mathbf{a}}(x))}f(g_{\mathbf{a}}(x))d\mu(x),

where
S_{n}\phi(g_{\mathbf{a}}(x)):=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\phi(g_{\mathbf{a}|k}(x))
is a Birkhoff sum. It is then convenient to denote
w_{\mathbf{a}}(x):=e^{S_{n}\phi(g_{\mathbf{a}}(x))}
. The invariance formula can then be rewritten as

\int fd\mu=\int\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}(f)d\mu,

where
\mathcal{L}_{\phi}:f\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{\phi}f
is the
transfer operator
associated to
\phi
, given by the formula

\mathcal{L}_{\phi}(f)(x):=\underset{I_{b}\ni x}{\sum_{ab\in\Sigma^{2}}}w_{ab}(x)f(g_{ab}(x)).

Iterating yields

\forall x\in I_{b},\ \mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{n}(f)(x):=\underset{b(\mathbf{a})=b}{\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n+1}}}w_{\mathbf{a}}(x)f(g_{\mathbf{a}}(x)).

Notice that the normalisation assumption on
\phi
implies that
\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{n}(1)=1
. Notice further that the
\phi
-conformality assumption (and summable variations of
\phi
) implies the following
Gibbs estimate
: there exists a
Gibbs constant

C_{\phi}\geq 1
such that

\forall\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n},\forall x\in I_{b(\mathbf{a})},\ C_{\phi}^{-1}w_{\mathbf{a}}(x)\leq p_{\mathbf{a}}:=w_{\mathbf{a}}(x_{b(\mathbf{a})})\leq C_{\phi}w_{\mathbf{a}}(x).

Troughout the text well will use the notation
a_{n}\lesssim b_{n}
if there exists some constant
C\geq 1
such that
a_{n}\leq Cb_{n}
. We will write
a_{n}\simeq b_{n}
if
a_{n}\lesssim b_{n}\lesssim a_{n}
. Gibbs estimates are then rewritten
w_{\mathbf{a}}(x)\simeq p_{\mathbf{a}}
for all
x
.

2.2.
Moment bounds from the tail condition

By Birkhoff ergodic theorem, we know that the natural scale for the contractions
|g_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}(x)|
is given by
e^{-\lambda n}
for
\mu
-almost every
x
, where
\lambda
is the
Lyapunov exponent
of
\mu
, is defined by

\lambda:=-\int\tau(x)\,d\mu(x),

where
\tau:I\to\mathbb{R}
is the geometric potential
\tau(g_{\mathbf{a}}(x))=\ln|g_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}(x)|
,
x\in I
,
\mathbf{a}\to x
. Proposition 2.1 quantifies deviation from this scale of contraction using properties of the
Gurevich pressure

P(\phi+t(\tau-\lambda))
, which is defined for a potential
\psi
as

\displaystyle P(\psi):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n}}\sup_{x\in I_{b(\mathbf{a})}}e^{S_{n}\psi(g_{\mathbf{a}}(x))}.

Notice that since
\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{n}(1)=1
, we find that our normalized potential
\phi
satify
P(\phi)=0
. It is also known that
\frac{d}{dt}P(\phi+t\tau)_{|t=0}=\int\tau d\mu
. The differentiability properties of the pressure allows us to bound the spreading of the random variable
e^{\lambda n}|g_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}(x)|
in the following way:

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose
\mu
satisfies the
(C_{\phi,\gamma_{0}},\gamma_{0})
-light tail condition. Then there exists
t_{\gamma_{0}}\in(0,1)
,
\varepsilon_{\gamma_{0}}>0
such that:

\displaystyle\forall t\in\left(-t_{\gamma_{0}},t_{\gamma_{0}}\right),\exists n_{0}(t),\forall n\geq n_{0}(t),\forall x\in I,\ \sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n}\atop\mathbf{a}\rightarrow x}w_{\mathbf{a}}(x)(e^{\lambda n}|g_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}(x)|)^{t}\leq 2C_{\phi}e^{\varepsilon_{\gamma_{0}}nt^{2}},

where

\varepsilon_{\gamma_{0}}:=\sup_{|t|\leq t_{\gamma_{0}}}\frac{|P(\phi+t(\tau-\lambda))|}{t^{2}}<\infty.

The optimal choice of
\varepsilon_{\gamma_{0}}
comes from the second order term

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}P(\phi+t(\tau-\lambda))|_{t=0}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int(S_{n}\tau)^{2}\,d\mu

of the Taylor approximation for
t\mapsto P(\phi+t(\tau-\lambda))
near
0
. The formula given by the limit of Birkhoff sums of
\tau
is proved e.g. in
[
62
]
.

The proof of Proposition
2.1
can be deduced from the proof leading to large deviation principle, e.g.
[
38
]
and the references there-in. However, as our result is stated quantitatively in terms of moments, we included a direct proof using Sarig’s results from
[
69
]
for the countable Markov shift for completeness.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Let
-\gamma_{0}\leq t<0
. Then if write
p_{a}:=w_{a}(x_{b}(a))
and recalling that
\kappa_{-}(a)\leq|g_{a}^{\prime}(x)|\leq\kappa_{+}
for all
x\in I_{b}
, where we have supressed the prefix notation of
b
for admissibility, then by the light
(C_{\phi,\gamma_{0}},\gamma_{0})
-tail property and using the Gibbs constant
C_{\phi}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n}\atop\mathbf{a}\rightarrow x}w_{\mathbf{a}}(x)|e^{\lambda n}g_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}(x)|^{t}\leq C_{\phi}\Big(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}p_{a}(e^{\lambda}\kappa_{-}(a))^{t}\Big)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\phi}e^{t\lambda n}\Big(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}p_{a}\kappa_{-}(a)^{-t}\Big)^{n}\leq C_{\phi}e^{t\lambda n}C_{\phi,\gamma_{0}}^{n}=C_{\phi}e^{(t\lambda+\log C_{\phi,\gamma_{0}})n}

Moreover, if
t\geq 0
, then we can simply bound

\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n}}w_{\mathbf{a}}(x)|e^{\lambda n}g_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}(x)|^{t}\leq C_{\phi}\Big(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}p_{a}(e^{\lambda}\kappa_{+})^{t}\Big)^{n}=C_{\phi}(e^{\lambda}\kappa_{+})^{tn}=C_{\phi}e^{t(\lambda+\log\kappa_{+})n},

where note that by uniform
\kappa_{+}
-contraction the Lyapunov exponent of
\mu
satisfies
\lambda\geq-\int\log\kappa_{+}\,d\mu(x)=-\log\kappa_{+}>1
as
\kappa_{+}<1
, so the exponent on is non-negative. Since

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\ln\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n}\atop\mathbf{a}\to x}w_{\mathbf{a}}(x)|e^{\lambda n}g_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}(x)|^{t}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}P(\varphi+t(\tau-\lambda))

this proves that for all
t\in[-\gamma_{0},\infty)
the pressure is finite:

P(\varphi+t(\tau-\lambda))\leq\begin{cases}t\lambda+\log C_{\phi,\gamma_{0}},&-\gamma_{0}\leq t<0;\\
t(\lambda+\log\kappa_{+}),&t\geq 0.\\
\end{cases}

Since
\tau
is
\alpha
-Hölder by the bounded distortion property and as the pressure
P(\varphi+t(\tau-\lambda))
is finite for all
|t|\leq\gamma_{0}
, we know by
[
69
, Theorem 4]
that there exists
0<t_{\gamma_{0}}^{\prime}<\gamma_{0}
such that the function
f:t\in(-t_{\gamma_{0}}^{\prime},t_{\gamma_{0}}^{\prime})\mapsto P(\varphi+t(\tau-\lambda))
is real analytic in
(-t_{\gamma_{0}}^{\prime},t_{\gamma_{0}}^{\prime})
and satisfies that
f(0)=P(\varphi)=0
and
f^{\prime}(0)=\int_{I}(\tau-\lambda)d\mu=0
. Taylor estimate near
0
then gives some
t_{\gamma_{0}}^{\prime\prime}\in(0,1)
such that

|f(t)|\leq\tfrac{3}{4}f^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}

for
|t|\leq t_{\gamma_{0}}^{\prime\prime}
. Thus choosing
0<t_{\gamma_{0}}<t_{\gamma_{0}}^{\prime\prime}
small enough, it follows that, for all
|t|\leq t_{\gamma_{0}}
, there exists
n_{0}(t)\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\forall n\geq n_{0}(t),\ \sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n}}w_{\mathbf{a}}(x)|e^{\lambda n}g_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}(x)|^{t}\leq e^{f^{\prime\prime}(0)nt^{2}}.

and we get the claim for
\varepsilon_{\gamma_{0}}=f^{\prime\prime}(0)
as
\varepsilon_{\gamma_{0}}t_{\gamma_{0}}^{2}\geq|f(t_{\gamma_{0}})|
. We get a bound uniform in
x
because of Gibbs estimates and bounded variations.
∎

2.3.
Upper regularity of the Gibbs measure from the tail

We now prove the Frostman condition under the hypothesis that has the light-tail condition. I We note that if one would relax the light tail assumption to heavier tail assumptions, the upper regularity of
\mu_{\phi}
is likely to fail. The result here is similar to that for overlapping self-similar measures by Feng and Lau
[
33
]
but generalised to the conformal setting and with infinite branches.

Proposition 2.2

(Upper regularity)
.

Suppose that the attractor to the IFS
F
is not a single point. Then assume one of the following:

•

the IFS has no overlaps, or

•

the IFS has overlaps but the potential
\phi:\Sigma^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is locally constant in the sense that
\omega_{a}(x)=p_{a}
, with
\sum_{a}p_{a}=1

Then exists
C_{reg}\geq 1
,
s_{\mu}\in(0,1)
such that:

\displaystyle\forall I\text{ interval},\ \mu(I)\leq C_{reg}|I|^{s_{\mu}}.

We note that all the examples we apply Theorem
1.1
satisfy either of the assumptions (e.g. the induced overlapping infinite IFS associated to the Lyons IFS in Theorem
1.3
satisfies the locally constancy).

To prove Proposition
2.2
, we first need a folklore result about the absence of atoms of equilibrium states under the assumptions in Proposition
2.2
:

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
F
is not a single point.

•

If the IFS have no overlaps, then equilibrium states have no atoms.

•

If the IFS have overlaps, and if the potential
\phi:\Sigma^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is locally constant in the sense that
\omega_{a}(x)=p_{a}
, with
\sum_{a}p_{a}=1
, then
\mu_{\phi}
have no atoms.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mu_{\phi}
have atoms. Consider a maximal atom
x_{0}\in F
of mass
M>0
. Then, for all
n
:

M=\mu(x_{0})=\int_{F}\mathbf{1}_{x_{0}}d\mu(x)=\int_{F}\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}(\mathbf{1}_{x_{0}})d\mu=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n}}\int_{F}\omega_{\mathbf{a}}(y)\mathbf{1}_{x_{0}}(g_{\mathbf{a}}(y))d\mu(y)

=\underset{I_{\mathbf{a}}\ni x_{0}}{\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n}}}\int_{F}\omega_{\mathbf{a}}(y)\mathbf{1}_{g_{\mathbf{a}}^{-1}x_{0}}(y)d\mu(y)=\underset{I_{\mathbf{a}}\ni x_{0}}{\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n}}}\omega_{\mathbf{a}}(g_{\mathbf{a}}(x_{0}))\mu({g_{\mathbf{a}}^{-1}x_{0}})\leq M\underset{I_{\mathbf{a}}\ni x_{0}}{\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n}}}\omega_{\mathbf{a}}(g_{\mathbf{a}}(x_{0}))\mu({g_{\mathbf{a}}^{-1}x_{0}}).

Hence, we get the key bound:

1\leq\underset{I_{\mathbf{a}}\ni x_{0}}{\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n}}}\omega_{\mathbf{a}}(g_{\mathbf{a}}(x_{0})).

Notice that
\omega_{\mathbf{a}}(x)<e^{-\varepsilon|a|}
for some
\varepsilon
, hence this inequality can not hold if the IFS doesn’t have overlaps, thus proving our first point. In the case where
F
have overlaps, if we further suppose that
\omega_{\mathbf{a}}(y)
are constants, we find:

1\leq\underset{I_{\mathbf{a}}\ni x_{0}}{\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n}}}\omega_{\mathbf{a}}\leq{\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n}}}\omega_{\mathbf{a}}=1

and hence
\forall n,\forall\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n},\ I_{\mathbf{a}}\ni x_{0}
. The fact that
F=\bigcap_{n}\bigcup_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n}}\overline{I_{\mathbf{a}}}
and that
|I_{\mathbf{a}}|\leq e^{-\varepsilon n}
for some
\varepsilon>0
implies that
F=\{x_{0}\}
.
∎

We now apply Lemma
2.3
to get upper regularity via an induction-by-scales strategy inspired by Feng and Lau
[
33
]
. Firstly we need the following definition and a technical lemma.

Definition 2.4
.

Define

\text{Reg}(F):=\{h:K\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\ |\ \forall x,y\in F,\forall\mathbf{b}\in\Sigma^{*},\omega_{\mathbf{b}}(x)h(g_{\mathbf{b}}(x))\leq C_{\phi}\omega_{\mathbf{b}}(y)h(g_{\mathbf{b}}(y))\},

where
C_{\phi}
is the Gibbs constant. Notice that
1\in\text{Reg}(F)
by Gibbs estimates.

Lemma 2.5
.

There exists
\sigma_{sep}>0
and
N_{sep}\geq 2
such that, for all
h\in\text{Reg}(F)
, we have, for any interval
I
of size
|I|\leq\sigma_{sep}
:

\forall n\geq N,\forall y\in F,\ \mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{n}(h\mathbf{1}_{I})(y)\leq\frac{h(y)}{4C_{\phi}}.

Proof.

Since
\mu
has no atoms and is compactly supported, its cumulative distribution function is uniformly continuous. It follows that there exists a function
\varepsilon:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
with
\varepsilon(0)=0
, continuous at zero, such that
\mu(I)\leq\varepsilon(|I|)
. Define
\sigma_{sep}>0
so small
\varepsilon(2\sigma_{sep})\leq\frac{1}{4C_{\phi}^{2}}
. Now choose
h\in\text{Reg}(F)
,
|I|\leq\sigma_{sep}
. We can then bound:

\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{n}(h\mathbf{1}_{I})\leq C_{\phi}h(y)\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{n}(\mathbf{1}_{I})\leq C_{\phi}h(y)\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{n}(\chi).

Where
\chi
is smooth, takes value in
[0,1]
and is supported in an interval of size
\sigma_{sep}
. By Perron-Frobenius-Ruelle theorem
[
14
]
, we find, for
n\geq N
where
N
is some constant that only depends on the
IFS
:
\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{n}(\chi)\leq\frac{1}{8C_{\phi}^{2}}+\int\chi d\mu\leq\frac{1}{4C_{\phi}^{2}}
. The desired bound follows.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Define the
N
-th exponential moment function by
\mathcal{M}_{N}(t):=\sup_{x,y}\sum_{\mathbf{\mathbf{a}}\in\Sigma^{N}}\omega_{\mathbf{a}}(x)g_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}(y)^{-t}
, which is finite for small enough
t>0
by hypothesis, and satisfies
\mathcal{M}_{N}(0)=1
. Choose
s_{\mu}\in(0,1)
such that
\mathcal{M}_{N_{sep}}(s_{\mu})\leq 2
. Define further
C_{reg}:=2C_{\phi}\sigma_{sep}^{-1}
from Lemma
2.5
. Then for all
h\in\text{Reg}(F)
, for all interval
I
, we have:

\forall y,\ \mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{n}(h\mathbf{1}_{I})(y)\leq h(y)\cdot C_{reg}\Big(|I|^{s_{\mu}}+\frac{1}{2^{n/N_{sep}}}\Big)

Proof.

The proof is done by induction on
n\geq 0
. If
n\leq N_{sep}
, then the bound is trivial, since
\varphi
is normalized, and since
h\in\text{Reg}(F)
:

\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{n}(h\mathbf{1}_{I})\leq C_{\phi}h(y)\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{n}(\mathbf{1}_{F})\leq 2C_{\phi}\Big(|I|^{s_{\mu}}+\frac{1}{2^{n/N}}\Big)
.

Suppose then that
n\geq N_{reg}+1
. If
\sigma>\sigma_{sep}/2
, the bound is also trivial. So suppose that
\sigma<\sigma_{sep}/2
.

We have:

\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{n}(h\mathbf{1}_{I})(y)=\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\Sigma^{n}}\omega_{\mathbf{c}}(y)h(g_{\mathbf{c}}y)\mathbf{1}_{I}(g_{\mathbf{c}}(y))

=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n-N}}\sum_{\mathbf{b}\in\Sigma^{N}}\omega_{\mathbf{b}}(g_{\mathbf{a}}x)\omega_{\mathbf{a}}(x)h(g_{\mathbf{b}\mathbf{a}}y)\mathbf{1}_{I}(g_{\mathbf{b}\mathbf{a}}y).

Now notice that
\mathbf{1}_{I}(g_{\mathbf{b}\mathbf{a}}y)=\mathbf{1}_{I_{\mathbf{b}}}(g_{\mathbf{a}}y)\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{I}}(g_{\mathbf{b}}(y))

where
\tilde{I}
is an interval of size
|\tilde{I}|<\sigma_{reg}
, since
N_{reg}
is large, and where
I_{\mathbf{b}}
is an interval of size
|g_{\mathbf{b}}^{\prime}|^{-1}|I|
. In particular:

\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{n}(h\mathbf{1}_{I})(y)=\sum_{\mathbf{b}\in\Sigma^{N}}\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma^{n-N}}\omega_{\mathbf{a}}(y)\omega_{\mathbf{b}}(g_{\mathbf{a}}(y))h(g_{\mathbf{b}}g_{\mathbf{a}}(y))\mathbf{1}_{I_{\mathbf{b}}}(g_{\mathbf{a}}(y))\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{I}}(g_{\mathbf{b}}(y))

=\sum_{\mathbf{b}\in\Sigma^{N}}\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{I}}(g_{\mathbf{b}}(y))\mathcal{L}^{n-N}_{\phi}(h_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{1}_{I_{\mathbf{b}}})(y)

\leq\sum_{\mathbf{b}\in\Sigma^{N}}\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{I}}(g_{\mathbf{b}}(y))\omega_{\mathbf{b}}(y)h(g_{\mathbf{b}}y)C_{reg}\Big(|I_{\mathbf{b}}|^{s_{\mu}}+2^{-(n-N)/N}\Big)

\leq C_{reg}\Big(\sum_{\mathbf{b}}\omega_{\mathbf{b}}h\circ g_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{I}}\circ g_{\mathbf{b}}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{\mathbf{b}}\omega_{\mathbf{b}}h\circ g_{\mathbf{b}}|g_{\mathbf{b}}^{\prime}|^{-2s_{\mu}}\Big)^{1/2}|I|^{s_{\mu}}+C_{reg}h(y)2^{-n/N}

\leq C_{reg}\Big(\frac{h(y)^{1/2}}{2C_{\phi}^{1/2}}(C_{\phi}h(y))^{1/2}M_{N_{sep}}(2s_{\mu})|I|^{s_{\mu}}+h(y)2^{-n/N}\Big)

\leq h(y)\cdot C_{reg}\Big(|I|^{s_{\mu}}+2^{-n/N}\Big).

∎

We can now prove Proposition
2.2
using Lemma
2.6
.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

By Lemma
2.6
, we have, for all
n
:

\mu(I)=\int_{F}\mathbf{1}_{I}d\mu=\int_{F}\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}^{n}(\mathbf{1}_{I})d\mu\leq C_{reg}\Big(|I|^{s_{\mu}}+2^{-n/N_{sep}}\Big).

Taking the limit in
n\rightarrow\infty
gives
\mu(I)\leq C_{reg}|I|^{s_{\mu}}
.
∎

3.
Discretised sum-product bounds under light tails

Our strategy is to reduce the Fourier decay bound to exponential sums of multiplicative random variables coming from products of the derivatives
g_{a}^{\prime}(x)
of the IFS. To bound this, as in Bourgain and Dyatlov in
[
18
]
, one can reduce this to a multiscale non-concentration bound via the
discretised sum-product phenomenon

[
17
]
coming from additive combinatorics. However, to gain the multiscale non-concentration, previous works require smoothness from the underlying dynamics such as in the form of spectral gaps of twisted transfer operators
[
67
,
13
,
11
,
43
,
47
]
, which we can avoid.

Next we give the necessary consequences of the sum-product theorem that we need in our setting that are similar to Bourgain and Dyatlov
[
18
]
. Since we also work with parabolic systems, the derivatives
g_{a}^{\prime}
in the infinitely branched systems may have arbitrarily small values, we need to formulate the bounds using moments of the multiplicative random variables, which we can link to the light tail condition by the previous Section
2
.

Firstly, let us recall a quantitative version of this sum-product phenomenon, where this version is a consequence of
[
60
, Theorem 1.14]
, see also
[
18
]
:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\gamma_{4}>0
. Then there exists
\varepsilon_{2}\in(0,1)
such that for any
k\geq C\gamma_{4}^{-1}
the following hold (where
C
denotes an absolute constant). Let
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
,
|\eta|\geq 1
, let
C_{0}\geq 1
, and let
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{k}
be Borel measures supported on
[C_{0}^{-1},C_{0}]
such that
\mu(\mathbb{R})\leq C_{0}
so that

\displaystyle\forall a\in\mathbb{R},\forall\sigma\in[|\eta|^{-1},|\eta|^{-\varepsilon_{2}}],\ \mu_{j}([a-\sigma,a+\sigma])\leq C_{0}\sigma^{\gamma_{4}}.

Then, for some constant
C_{1}
that only depends on
C_{0},\gamma
:

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{[C_{0}^{-1},C_{0}]^{k}}e^{i\eta x_{1}\dots x_{k}}d\mu_{1}(x_{1})\dots d\mu_{k}(x_{k})\Big|\leq C_{1}|\eta|^{-\exp(-C\gamma_{4}^{-1})}.

We will generalise this result for the case where
\mu
is supported on
\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
, under a polynomial moment condition.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\gamma_{3}>0
. Then there exists
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,1)
and
k\geq 1
such that the following holds: Let
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
,
|\eta|\geq 1
and let
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{k}
be Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
such that:

\displaystyle\forall j\in\{1,\dots,k\},\ \forall a\in\mathbb{R},\ \forall\sigma\in[|\eta|^{-2},|\eta|^{-\varepsilon_{1}}],\ (\ln_{*}\mu_{j})([a-\sigma,a+\sigma])\leq 2\sigma^{\gamma_{3}}.

Then, for some constant
c
that only depends on
\gamma_{3}
, we find:

\displaystyle\forall t\in(0,\varepsilon_{1}),\ \Big|\int_{(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*})^{k}}e^{i\eta x_{1}\dots x_{k}}d\mu_{1}(x_{1})\dots d\mu_{k}(x_{k})\Big|\leq c|\eta|^{-\varepsilon_{1}}+|\eta|^{-\frac{t^{3}}{2\sqrt{k}}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}M_{j}(t^{2}),

where
M_{j}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}}e^{t|\ln x|}d\mu_{j}(x)
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma_{3}\in(0,1)
. Define
\gamma_{4}:=\gamma_{3}/2
. Then Theorem
3.1
gives us some
k\geq 1
and some
\varepsilon_{2}\in(0,1)
(associated to
\gamma_{4})
. Taking
\varepsilon_{2}
smaller if necessary, let us assume
\varepsilon_{2}\leq e^{-C/\gamma_{4}}
. We will define
\varepsilon_{1}:=\big(\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{4k}\big)^{6}
. Now, let
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
|\eta|\geq 1
. Let
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{k}
be measures that satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
3.2
with respect to the quantities
(\gamma_{3},k,\varepsilon_{1},\eta)
. We wish to show that the bound on the integral holds.

The idea is to do a dyadic decomposition of the measures
\mu_{j}
. For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define
I_{n}:=[2^{n},2^{n+1}[
, and we define
\mu_{j,n}:=(\mu_{j})\mid_{I_{n}}
. We find

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*})^{k}}e^{i\eta x_{1}\dots x_{k}}d\mu_{1}(x_{1})\dots d\mu_{k}(x_{k})=\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}\int_{I_{\vec{n}}}e^{i\eta x_{1}\dots x_{k}}d\mu_{1,n_{1}}(x_{1})\dots d\mu_{k,n_{k}}(x_{k}),

where
\vec{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})
and
I_{\vec{n}}=I_{n_{1}}\times\dots\times I_{n_{k}}
. We then cut the sum in two depending on if
|\vec{n}|_{\infty}:=\max_{j}|n_{j}|
is large or small. Fix some
t\in(0,\varepsilon_{1})
. We have:

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*})^{k}}e^{i\eta x_{1}\dots x_{k}}d\mu_{1}(x_{1})\dots d\mu_{k}(x_{k})=

\displaystyle\underset{|\vec{n}|_{\infty}<t\log_{2}|\eta|}{\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}}\int_{I_{\vec{n}}}e^{i\eta x_{1}\dots x_{k}}d\mu_{1,n_{1}}(x_{1})\dots d\mu_{k,n_{k}}(x_{k})\ +\underset{|\vec{n}|_{\infty}\geq t\log_{2}|\eta|}{\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}}\int_{I_{\vec{n}}}e^{i\eta x_{1}\dots x_{k}}d\mu_{1,n_{1}}(x_{1})\dots d\mu_{k,n_{k}}(x_{k}).

The sum for large
|\vec{n}|_{\infty}
gives

\displaystyle\Big|\underset{|\vec{n}|_{\infty}\geq t\log_{2}|\eta|}{\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}}\int_{I_{\vec{n}}}e^{i\eta x_{1}\dots x_{k}}d\mu_{1,n_{1}}(x_{1})\dots d\mu_{k,n_{k}}(x_{k})\Big|\leq\underset{|\vec{n}|_{\infty}\geq t\log_{2}|\eta|}{\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}}\mu_{1}(I_{n_{1}})\dots\mu_{k}(I_{n_{k}}).

The moment condition on the measures
\mu_{j}
yields

\displaystyle\forall j,\ \forall n\in\mathbb{Z},\ \mu_{j}(I_{n})\leq\int_{[2^{n},2^{n+1}[}2^{-t^{2}|n|}e^{t^{2}|\ln x|}d\mu_{j}(x)\leq 2^{-t^{2}|n|}M_{j}(t^{2}),

which gives, injecting into the previous sum (denoting by
d\sigma
the Lebesgue measure on the sphere,
M(t):=M_{1}(t)\dots M_{k}(t)
and
|\vec{n}|_{p}:=(\sum_{j}|n_{j}|^{p})^{1/p}
):

\displaystyle\Big|\underset{|\vec{n}|_{\infty}\geq t\log_{2}|\eta|}{\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}}\int_{I_{\vec{n}}}e^{i\eta x_{1}\dots x_{k}}d\mu_{1,n_{1}}(x_{1})\dots d\mu_{k,n_{k}}(x_{k})\Big|\leq M(t^{2})\underset{|\vec{n}|_{\infty}\geq t\log_{2}|\eta|}{\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}}2^{-t^{2}|\vec{n}|_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq M(t^{2})\underset{|\vec{n}|_{2}\geq t\log_{2}|\eta|}{\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}}2^{-\frac{t^{2}|\vec{n}|_{2}}{\sqrt{k}}}\lesssim M(t^{2})\int_{\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{k},\ \|x\|_{2}\geq t\log_{2}|\eta|\}}2^{-\frac{t^{2}\|x\|_{2}}{\sqrt{k}}}dx

\displaystyle=M(t^{2})\int_{\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}\int_{t\log_{2}|\eta|}^{\infty}2^{-\frac{t^{2}r}{\sqrt{k}}}r^{k-1}drd\sigma\lesssim M(t^{2})\int_{t\log_{2}|\eta|}^{\infty}2^{-\frac{t^{2}r}{2\sqrt{k}}}dr\lesssim M(t^{2})\eta^{-\frac{t^{3}}{2\sqrt{k}}}.

To conclude, it now suffices to bound the sum where
|\vec{n}|_{\infty}
is small. We can rewrite it as

\displaystyle\underset{|\vec{n}|_{\infty}<t\log_{2}|\eta|}{\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}}\int_{I_{\vec{n}}}e^{i\eta x_{1}\dots x_{k}}d\mu_{1,n_{1}}(x_{1})\dots d\mu_{k,n_{k}}(x_{k})=\underset{|\vec{n}|_{\infty}<t\log_{2}|\eta|}{\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}}\int_{[1,2]^{k}}e^{i\eta_{\vec{n}}x_{1}\dots x_{k}}d\nu_{1,n_{1}}(x_{1})\dots d\nu_{k,n_{k}}(x_{k}),

where
\nu_{j,n}
are the measures on
[1,2]
defined by the relation
(m_{n})_{*}\nu_{j,n}=\mu_{j,n}
, for
m_{n}(x):=2^{n}x
, and where we denoted
\eta_{\vec{n}}:=\eta 2^{n_{1}+\dots n_{k}}
. Now fix some
\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
with
|\vec{n}|_{\infty}\leq t\log_{2}|\eta|
. We will check that the measures
\nu_{1,n_{1}},\dots,\nu_{k,n_{k}}
satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
3.1
for the quantities
(\gamma_{4},\varepsilon_{2},k,\eta_{\vec{n}})
. Notice that
|\eta|^{1/2}\leq|\eta_{\vec{n}}|\leq|\eta|^{2}
since
t<\varepsilon_{1}<\frac{1}{(4k)^{4}}
. We check the following conditions:

•

Total mass condition:

\displaystyle\forall j,\ \nu_{j,n_{j}}(\mathbb{R})=\mu_{j}(I_{n_{j}})\leq 1.

•

Support condition:

\displaystyle\forall j,\ \text{supp}(\nu_{j,n_{j}})\subset[1,2].

•

Non-concentration condition:

\forall j,\ \forall a\in[1,2],\ \forall\sigma\in[|\eta_{\vec{n}}|^{-1},|\eta_{\vec{n}}|^{-\varepsilon_{2}}]\subset[\eta^{-2},\eta^{-\varepsilon_{2}/2}]:

\nu_{j,n_{j}}([a-\sigma,a+\sigma])\leq\nu_{j,n_{j}}(\{x\in[1,2],\ \ln x\in[\ln a-2\sigma,\ln a+2\sigma]\})

=\mu_{j}(\{x\in I_{n_{j}},\ \ln x\in[n_{j}\ln(2)+\ln(a)-2\sigma,n_{j}\ln(2)+\ln(a)+2\sigma]\})\leq 4\sigma^{\gamma_{3}}

by the non-concentration hypothesis on
\ln_{*}\mu_{j}
.

Hence the conclusion of Theorem
3.1
apply in this setting. That is, for some constant
c>0
that only depends on
\gamma_{3}
, we find the following bound:

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{[1,2]^{k}}e^{i\eta_{\vec{n}}x_{1}\dots x_{k}}d\nu_{1,n_{1}}(x_{1})\dots d\nu_{k,n_{k}}\Big|\leq c|\eta_{\vec{n}}|^{-\varepsilon_{2}}\leq c|\eta|^{-\varepsilon_{2}/2}.

We then inject this estimate in the sum, and find:

\displaystyle\Big|\underset{|\vec{n}|_{\infty}<t\log_{2}|\eta|}{\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}}\int_{I_{\vec{n}}}e^{i\eta x_{1}\dots x_{k}}d\mu_{1,n_{1}}(x_{1})\dots d\mu_{k,n_{k}}(x_{k})\Big|\leq c(t\log_{2}|\eta|)^{k}|\eta|^{-\varepsilon_{2}/2}\lesssim_{\gamma_{3}}|\eta|^{-\varepsilon_{2}/4}\lesssim_{\gamma_{3}}\eta^{-\varepsilon_{1}},

thus concluding the proof.
∎

We will apply the previous Theorem to the special case where the measures are countable sums of Dirac mass, which correspond to our present setting.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\gamma_{2}\in(0,1)
. Then there exists
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,1)
and
k\geq 1
such that the following holds. Let
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
,
|\eta|\geq 1
. Let
\mathcal{Z}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{Z}_{k}
be a finite or countable sets. Choose, for each
j
, some nonnegative weights
(w_{\mathbf{b}}^{(j)})_{\mathbf{b}\in\mathcal{Z}_{j}}
that sums to one, and some maps
\zeta_{j}:\mathcal{Z}_{j}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
. Suppose that for all
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, the following non-concentration condition holds:

\displaystyle\forall\sigma\in[\eta^{-2},\eta^{-\varepsilon_{1}}],\ \sum_{(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c})\in\mathcal{Z}_{j}^{2}}w_{\mathbf{b}}^{(j)}w_{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)}\mathbf{1}_{[-\sigma,\sigma]}(\ln\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{b})-\ln\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{c}))\leq\sigma^{\gamma_{2}}.

Then, there exists a constant
c>0
that only depends on
\gamma
such that

\displaystyle\forall t>0,\ \Big|{{\sum_{\mathbf{b}_{1}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}}}}\dots{{\sum_{\mathbf{b}_{k}\in\mathcal{Z}_{k}}}}w_{\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{(1)}\dots w_{\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{(k)}e^{i\eta\zeta_{1}(\mathbf{b}_{1})\dots\zeta_{k}(\mathbf{b}_{k})}\Big|\leq c|\eta|^{-\varepsilon_{1}^{2}}+\eta^{-\frac{t^{3}}{2\sqrt{k}}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}M_{j}(t^{2}),

where
M_{j}(t):=\sum_{\mathbf{b}\in\mathcal{Z}_{j}}w_{\mathbf{b}}^{(j)}e^{t|\ln\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{b})|}.

Proof.

Let
\gamma_{2}>0
. We then fix
\gamma_{3}:=\gamma_{2}/2
and let
\varepsilon_{1}
and
k\geq 1
be given by Theorem
3.2
associated to
\gamma_{3}
. Let
\mathcal{Z}_{j}
,
(w_{\mathbf{b}}^{(j)})_{\mathbf{b}\in\mathcal{Z}_{j}}
and
\zeta_{j}:\mathcal{Z}_{j}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
be such that they satisfy the hypothesis of Theorem
3.3
. Define some measures
\mu_{j}
by

\displaystyle\mu_{j}:=\sum_{\mathbf{b}\in\mathcal{Z}_{j}}w_{\mathbf{b}}^{(j)}\delta_{\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{b})}.

Let us check the non-concentration condition:

\displaystyle\ln_{*}\mu_{j}([a-\sigma,a+\sigma])=\sum_{\mathbf{b}\in\mathcal{Z}_{j}}w_{\mathbf{b}}^{(j)}\mathbf{1}_{[a-\sigma,a+\sigma]}(\ln\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{b}))

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{Z}_{j}}w_{\mathbf{b}}^{(j)}w_{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)}\mathbf{1}_{[a-\sigma,a+\sigma]}(\ln\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{b}))\mathbf{1}_{[a-\sigma,a+\sigma]}(\ln\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{c}))\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{Z}_{j}}w_{\mathbf{b}}^{(j)}w_{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)}\mathbf{1}_{[2\sigma,2\sigma]}(\ln\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{b})-\ln\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{c}))\Big)^{1/2}\leq(2\sigma)^{\gamma_{2}/2}.

The conclusion follows by applying the previous Theorem.
∎

4.
Discretisation to exponential sums of random products

We finally start to prove Theorem
1.1
. The proof is separated in two parts. The first part is to reduce the Fourier decay bound to the sum-product phenomenon (Theorem
3.3
) for some well chosen maps
(\zeta_{j})
. This is the content of Section
4
. The second part is to check that the non-concentration condition is actually satisfied for these maps
\zeta_{j}
under (QNL). This is the content of Section
5
.

4.1.
Iterating into exponential sums of phase differences

Recall from Section
2
that the transfer operator associated to the potential
\phi
is defined by

\displaystyle\forall x\in I_{b},\ \mathcal{L}_{\phi}f(x)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A},ab\in\Sigma^{2}}e^{\phi(g_{ab}(x))}f(g_{ab}(x)),f:I\to\mathbb{C}.

Let
\mu=\mu_{\phi}
be the Gibbs measure associated to
\phi
, supported on the attractor
F
of our IFS. If
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
with
|\xi|
large enough, choose the unique
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle e^{((2k+1)\lambda+\varepsilon_{0})n}\leq|\xi|<e^{((2k+1)\lambda+\varepsilon_{0})(n+1)},

where
\varepsilon_{0}>0
is a small constant that we will chose later (see Definition 4.1), the point being that
\xi e^{-(2k+1)\lambda n}\simeq e^{\varepsilon_{0}n}
is a term that we will chose to be slowly growing.

We will iterate
(2k+1)
times the invariance relation
\int f\,d\mu=\int\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{n}f\,d\mu
. Because of the relation
g_{ab}\circ g_{bc}=g_{abc}
, it is convenient, for a given word
a_{1}\dots a_{n+1}
, to define the notation
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}:=a_{1}\dots a_{n}
. We can then write
g_{\mathbf{a^{\prime}b}}=g_{\mathbf{a}}\circ g_{\mathbf{b}}
. Iterating the relation then gives

\displaystyle\Big|\int\chi(x)e^{i\xi\psi(x)}\,d\mu(x)\Big|^{2}=\Big|\sum\limits_{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}}\int_{I_{b(\mathbf{A})}}w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x)\chi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x))e^{i\xi\psi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x))}\,d\mu(x)\Big|^{2},

where the sum is over blocks
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{a}_{1}\dots\mathbf{a}_{k+1}\in\mathcal{A}^{n(k+1)},\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{b}_{1}\dots\mathbf{b}_{k}\in\mathcal{A}^{nk}
of words
\mathbf{a}_{j},\mathbf{b}_{j}\in\Sigma^{n}
such that the concatenation

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}:=\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{a}_{2}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{2}^{\prime}\dots\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{a}_{k+1}.

is admissible (i.e. element of
\Sigma^{(2k+1)n}
). Recall that we use the weight notation

\displaystyle w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x):=e^{S_{(2k+1)n}\phi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x)))}.

We fix from now on some choices of reference points
x_{a}\in I_{a}
. We can then multiply and divide to get a form:

\displaystyle\Big|\int\chi(x)e^{i\xi\psi(x)}\,d\mu(x)\Big|^{2}=\Big|\sum\limits_{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}}w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})\int_{I_{b(\mathbf{A})}}\frac{w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x)}{w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})}\chi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x))e^{i\xi\psi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x))}\,d\mu(x)\Big|^{2},

which by Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and Gibbs property of
\mu
is bounded by a constant multiple of

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}}w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})\Big|\int_{I_{b(\mathbf{A})}}\frac{w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x)}{w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})}\chi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x))e^{i\xi\psi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x))}\,d\mu(x)\Big|^{2}.

Since
\chi
is Hölder with
\|\chi\|_{\beta_{\chi}}\lesssim|\xi|^{\varepsilon_{\chi}}
, we have

\displaystyle|\chi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x))-\chi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})}))|\leq C|\xi|^{\varepsilon_{\chi}}|g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x)-g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})|^{\beta_{\chi}}\leq C|\xi|^{\varepsilon_{\chi}}\kappa_{+}^{\beta_{\chi}(2k+1)n},

so by the Gibbs property
\frac{w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x)}{w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})}\lesssim 1
we obtain a bound

(4.1)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}}w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})\chi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})}))^{2}\Big|\int_{I_{b(\mathbf{A})}}\frac{w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x)}{w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x_{0})}e^{i\xi\psi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x))}\,d\mu(x)\Big|^{2}+C^{\prime}|\xi|^{\varepsilon_{\chi}}\kappa_{+}^{2\beta_{\chi}(2k+1)n}.

Since
\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{a}_{2}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{2}^{\prime}\dots\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{a}_{k+1}
, we know that for some
t_{j}\in F
:

\displaystyle w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})=w_{\mathbf{a}_{1}}(t_{1})w_{\mathbf{b}_{1}}(t_{2})w_{\mathbf{a}_{2}}(t_{3})w_{\mathbf{b}_{2}}(t_{4})\dots w_{\mathbf{a}_{k}}(t_{2k-1})w_{\mathbf{b}_{k}}(t_{2k})w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(t_{(2k+1)}).

Thus we can apply Gibbs property applied
2k+1
times to obtain

\displaystyle w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})\leq C^{2k+1}\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}_{1}})\mu(I_{\mathbf{b}_{1}})\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}_{2}})\mu(I_{\mathbf{b}_{2}})\dots\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}_{k}})\mu(I_{\mathbf{b}_{k}})\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}).

Writing
p_{\mathbf{A}}=\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}_{1}})\dots\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}})
and
p_{\mathbf{B}}=\mu(I_{\mathbf{b}_{1}})\dots\mu(I_{\mathbf{b}_{k}})
, we can bound (
4.1
) by

\displaystyle C^{2k+1}\|\chi\|_{\infty}^{2}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{A}}p_{\mathbf{A}}\sum_{\mathbf{B}:\mathbf{A}\ast\mathbf{B}\in\Sigma^{(2k+1)n}}p_{\mathbf{B}}\Big|\int_{I_{b(\mathbf{A})}}\frac{w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x)}{w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})}e^{i\xi\psi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x))}\,d\mu(x)\Big|^{2}+C^{\prime}|\xi|^{\varepsilon_{\chi}}\kappa_{+}^{2\beta_{\chi}(2k+1)n}.

Here as
|\xi|\lesssim e^{(2k+1)\lambda n+\varepsilon_{0}n}
and
e^{-\lambda}\leq\kappa_{+}
, we have
|\xi|^{\varepsilon_{\chi}}\kappa_{+}^{2\beta_{\chi}(2k+1)n}\lesssim|\xi|^{-\beta^{\prime}}
for some
\beta^{\prime}>0
as long as
\varepsilon_{\chi}>0
is small enough. Thus, fixing
\mathbf{A}
, we just need to have decay for

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{B}}\Big|\int_{b(\mathbf{A})}\frac{w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x)}{w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})}e^{i\xi\psi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x))}\,d\mu(x)\Big|^{2}.

Writing
\mathbf{A}\sharp\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{a}_{2}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{2}^{\prime}\dots\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}
, we have
\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}=(\mathbf{A}\sharp\mathbf{B})^{\prime}\mathbf{a}_{k+1}
. We have that at any
x\in I_{b(\mathbf{A})}
:

\displaystyle g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}^{\prime}(x)=g_{\mathbf{A}\sharp\mathbf{B}}^{\prime}(g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x))g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}^{\prime}(x),

and

\displaystyle w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x)=w_{\mathbf{A}\sharp\mathbf{B}}(g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x))w_{{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}}(x).

By Gibbs property
\frac{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)}{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})}\lesssim 1
, the Hölder property of
\phi
gives us

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x)}{w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})}-\frac{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)}{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})}\Big|=\Big|\frac{w_{\mathbf{A}\sharp\mathbf{B}}(g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x))}{w_{\mathbf{A}\sharp\mathbf{B}}(g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})}))}-1\Big|\frac{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)}{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|S_{2kn}\phi(g_{\mathbf{A}\sharp\mathbf{B}}(g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)))-S_{2kn}\phi(g_{\mathbf{A}\sharp\mathbf{B}}(g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})))|

\displaystyle\leq C|g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)-g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})|^{\beta}\lesssim\kappa_{+}^{\beta n}.

Thus we obtain a bound for some universal
C^{\prime}>0
:

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{B}}\Big|\int_{I_{b(\mathbf{A})}}\frac{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)}{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})}e^{i\xi\psi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x))}\,d\mu(x)\Big|^{2}+C^{\prime}\kappa_{+}^{\beta n},

so we just need to study

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{B}}\Big|\int_{I_{b(\mathbf{A})}}\frac{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)}{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})}e^{i\xi\psi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x))}\,d\mu(x)\Big|^{2}.

Expand the square so we just need to bound

\displaystyle\iint_{I_{b(\mathbf{A})}^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{B}}\frac{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)}{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})}\frac{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(y)}{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})}p_{\mathbf{B}}e^{i\xi(\psi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x))-\psi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(y)))}\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\iint_{I_{b(\mathbf{A})}^{2}}\frac{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)}{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})}\frac{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(y)}{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})}\sum_{\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{B}}e^{i\xi(\psi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x))-\psi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(y)))}\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y).

4.2.
Linearisation of the phase

Let us now linearise the phase. By the mean value theorem, we have, for some
t=t_{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}}(x,y)
:

\displaystyle\psi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x))-\psi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(y)))=(\psi\circ g_{\mathbf{A}\sharp\mathbf{B}})^{\prime}(t)(g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)-g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(y)).

By the chain rule, we can then find
t_{1}\in I_{\mathbf{a}_{1}},t_{2}\in I_{\mathbf{a}_{2}},\dots,t_{k+1}\in I_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}
(depending on
t
) such that

\displaystyle(\psi\circ g_{\mathbf{A}\sharp\mathbf{B}})^{\prime}(t)=\psi^{\prime}(t_{1})g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(t_{2})\dots g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(t_{k+1}).

We can then rewrite our sum of interest as

\sum_{\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{B}}\Big|\int_{I_{b(\mathbf{A})}}\frac{w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x)}{w_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})}e^{i\xi\psi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x))}\,d\mu(x)\Big|^{2}

=\iint_{I_{b(\mathbf{A})}^{2}}\frac{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)}{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})}\frac{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(y)}{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})}\sum_{\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{B}}e^{i\xi(\psi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(x))-\psi(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}(y)))}\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)

=\iint_{I_{b(\mathbf{A})}^{2}}\frac{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)}{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})}\frac{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(y)}{w_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})}\sum_{\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{B}}e^{i\xi\psi^{\prime}(t_{1})g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(t_{2})\dots g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(t_{k+1})(g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)-g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(y))}\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y).

To gain space, denote
W_{\mathbf{a}}(x,y):=\frac{w_{\mathbf{a}}(x)}{w_{\mathbf{a}}(x_{b(\mathbf{a})})}\frac{w_{\mathbf{a}}(y)}{w_{\mathbf{a}}(x_{b(\mathbf{a})})}

=\iint_{I_{b(\mathbf{A})}^{2}}W_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x,y)\sum_{\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{B}}\cos\Big({\xi\psi^{\prime}(t_{1})g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(t_{2})\dots g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(t_{k+1})(g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)-g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(y))}\Big)d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)

=\iint_{I_{b(\mathbf{A})}^{2}}W_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x,y)\sum_{\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{B}}\cos\Big({\xi|\psi^{\prime}(t_{1})||g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(t_{2})|\dots|g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(t_{k+1})||g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)-g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(y)|}\Big)d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y).

And then triangle inequality and the fact that
|\text{Re}(z)|\leq|z|
yields:

\leq\iint_{I_{b(\mathbf{A})}^{2}}W_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x,y)\Big|\sum_{\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{B}}\cos\Big({\xi|\psi^{\prime}(t_{1})||g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(t_{2})|\dots|g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(t_{k+1})||g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)-g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(y)|}\Big)\Big|d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)

\leq\iint_{I_{b(\mathbf{A})}^{2}}W_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x,y)\Big|\sum_{\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{B}}e^{i\xi\psi^{\prime}(t_{1})|g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(t_{2})|\dots|g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(t_{k+1})||g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)-g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(y)|}\Big|d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)

\lesssim\iint_{I_{b(\mathbf{A})}^{2}}\Big|\sum_{\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{B}}e^{i\xi\psi^{\prime}(t_{1})|g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(t_{2})|\dots|g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(t_{k+1})||g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)-g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(y)|}\Big|d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y),

where the last inequality comes from Gibbs estimates. Let us now renormalize the phases and get rid of the dependencies of the
(t_{j})_{j}
. Denote
x_{\mathbf{a}}:=g_{\mathbf{a}}(x_{b(\mathbf{a})})\in I_{\mathbf{a}}
some reference points. For all
j\geq 1

(C,\alpha)

z_{\mathbf{a}_{j+1}}\in I

\displaystyle e^{-C\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n}}\leq\left|\frac{g_{\mathbf{a}_{j}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{j}}^{\prime}(t_{j+1})}{g_{\mathbf{a}_{j}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{j}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{j+1}})}\right|\leq e^{C\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n}}.

Similarly, by hypothesis we have that

\displaystyle|\log\psi^{\prime}(x)-\log\psi^{\prime}(y)|\leq C_{\psi}|\xi|^{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\psi}}|x-y|^{\alpha},

for some small enough

\displaystyle e^{-C_{\psi}\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n/2}}\leq e^{-C_{\psi}|\xi|^{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\psi}}\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n}}\leq\left|\frac{\psi^{\prime}(t_{1})}{\psi^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{1}})}\right|\leq e^{C_{\psi}|\xi|^{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\psi}}\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n}}\leq e^{C_{\psi}\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n/2}}.

\displaystyle e^{-\tilde{C}\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n/2}}\leq e^{-kC\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n}-C_{\psi}\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n/2}}\leq\Big|\frac{\psi^{\prime}(t_{1})g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(t_{2})\dots g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(t_{k+1})}{\psi^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{1}})g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{2}})\dots g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}})}\Big|\leq e^{kC\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n}+C_{\psi}\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n/2}}\leq e^{\tilde{C}\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n/2}}.

Since by mean value theorem
|e^{x}-1|\leq|x|

\displaystyle\Bigg|\frac{|\psi^{\prime}(t_{1})g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(t_{2})\dots g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(t_{k+1})|}{|\psi^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{1}})g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{2}})\dots g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}})|}-1\Bigg|\leq\tilde{C}\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n/2}.

then by the mean value theorem for some

we have

\displaystyle|\xi\psi^{\prime}(t_{1})|g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(t_{2})|\dots|g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(t_{k+1})||g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)-g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(y)|-\eta_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1},\mathbf{a}_{1}}(x,y)\zeta_{1,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{b}_{1})\dots\zeta_{k,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{b}_{k})|

\displaystyle=|\xi||g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)-g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(y)|\left|\psi^{\prime}(t_{1})|g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(t_{2})|\dots|g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(t_{k+1})|-\psi^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{1}})g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{2}})\dots g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\xi\right|\left|\left|\psi^{\prime}\right|\right|_{\infty}|g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{2}})\dots g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}})g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}^{\prime}(z)|\left|\frac{\psi^{\prime}(t_{1})g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(t_{2})\dots g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(t_{k+1})}{\psi^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{1}})g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{2}})\dots g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}})}-1\right|

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\left|\xi\right|\left|\left|\psi^{\prime}\right|\right|_{\infty}|g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{2}})\dots g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}})g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}^{\prime}(z)|\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n/2}

\displaystyle\leq|\xi|\ |g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{2}})\dots g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}})g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}^{\prime}(z)|\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n/3}.

for
n
large enough, if
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\psi}
is chosen so small that
\left|\left|\psi^{\prime}\right|\right|_{\infty}\lesssim\kappa_{+}^{-\alpha n/10}
. Recall now that we fixed the relation
|\xi|\simeq e^{(2k+1)\lambda n+\varepsilon_{0}n}
. If we suppose that
\varepsilon_{0}
is chosen so small that
\varepsilon_{0}<\alpha|\ln\kappa_{+}|/6

\lesssim e^{(2k+1)\lambda n}g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}^{\prime}(z)\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n/6}.

This is not a decaying term for all choice of words, but it is for most choices of words. More precisely, using the

x\mapsto e^{ix}
and Proposition
2.1
, we find:

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{A}}p_{\mathbf{A}}\Big|\sum_{\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{B}}e^{i\xi\psi^{\prime}(t_{1})|g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(t_{2})|\dots|g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(t_{k+1})||g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)-g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(y)|}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathbf{A}}p_{\mathbf{A}}\Big|\sum_{\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{B}}e^{i\eta_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1},\mathbf{a}_{1}}(x,y)\zeta_{1,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{b}_{1})\dots\zeta_{k,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{b}_{k})}\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{A}}p_{\mathbf{B}}\Big|e^{i\xi\psi^{\prime}(t_{1})|g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{\prime}(t_{2})|\dots|g_{\mathbf{a}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{\prime}(t_{k+1})||g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)-g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(y)|}-e^{i\eta_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1},\mathbf{a}_{1}}(x,y)\zeta_{1,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{b}_{1})\dots\zeta_{k,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{b}_{k})}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathbf{A}}p_{\mathbf{A}}\Big|\sum_{\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{B}}e^{i\eta_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1},\mathbf{a}_{1}}(x,y)\zeta_{1,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{b}_{1})\dots\zeta_{k,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{b}_{k})}\Big|+2\sum_{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{A}}p_{\mathbf{B}}\mathbf{1}(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}^{\prime}(x)e^{(2k+1)\lambda n}\geq\kappa_{+}^{-\alpha n/20})

\displaystyle\qquad+C\sum_{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{A}}p_{\mathbf{B}}\mathbf{1}(g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}^{\prime}(x)e^{(2k+1)\lambda n}\leq\kappa_{+}^{-\alpha n/20})e^{(2k+1)\lambda n}g_{\mathbf{A}*\mathbf{B}}^{\prime}(z)\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n/6}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathbf{A}}p_{\mathbf{A}}\Big|\sum_{\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{B}}e^{i\eta_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1},\mathbf{a}_{1}}(x,y)\zeta_{1,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{b}_{1})\dots\zeta_{k,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{b}_{k})}\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad+C\sum_{\mathbf{C}\in\Sigma^{(2k+1)n}}p_{\mathbf{C}}\Big(e^{(2k+1)\lambda n}g_{\mathbf{C}}^{\prime}(z)\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n/20}\Big)^{t}+C\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n/10}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathbf{A}}p_{\mathbf{A}}\Big|\sum_{\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{B}}e^{i\eta_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1},\mathbf{a}_{1}}(x,y)\zeta_{1,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{b}_{1})\dots\zeta_{k,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{b}_{k})}\Big|+C\kappa_{+}^{\alpha tn/20}e^{\varepsilon_{\gamma_{0}}nt^{2}}+C\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n/10}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathbf{A}}p_{\mathbf{A}}\Big|\sum_{\mathbf{B}}p_{\mathbf{B}}e^{i\eta_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1},\mathbf{a}_{1}}(x,y)\zeta_{1,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{b}_{1})\dots\zeta_{k,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{b}_{k})}\Big|+C(e^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}|\ln\kappa_{+}|^{2}}{\varepsilon_{\gamma_{0}}800}n}+\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n/10})

by choosing

Thus, to conclude Fourier decay of the measure

where

4.3.
Applying the sum-product bound under non-concentration of derivatives

In the notation in Theorem
3.3
, we can write our random variable as

\displaystyle X_{x,y}(\mathbf{A})=\Big|{{\sum_{\mathbf{b}_{1}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}}}}\dots{{\sum_{\mathbf{b}_{k}\in\mathcal{Z}_{k}}}}w_{\mathbf{b}_{1}}^{(1)}\dots w_{\mathbf{b}_{k}}^{(k)}e^{i\eta\zeta_{1}(\mathbf{b}_{1})\dots\zeta_{k}(\mathbf{b}_{k})}\Big|,

where the map

\displaystyle\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{b}):=\zeta_{j,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{b})=e^{2\lambda n}|g_{\mathbf{a}_{j}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{j+1}})|,

defined on the sets
\mathcal{Z}_{j}:=\mathcal{Z}_{j,\mathbf{A}}=\{\mathbf{b}:\mathbf{a}_{j}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}^{\prime}\mathbf{a}_{j+1}\ \text{is admissible}\}
and

\displaystyle w_{\mathbf{b}_{j}}^{(j)}:=\mu(I_{\mathbf{b}_{j}})\in[0,1]\quad\text{and}\quad\eta:=\eta_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1},\mathbf{a}_{1}}(x,y).

Now, by Theorem
3.3

0<\gamma<1

\varepsilon_{1}>0

for all

(4.2)

\displaystyle\forall\sigma\in[|\eta_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1},\mathbf{a}_{1}}(x,y)|^{-2},|\eta_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1},\mathbf{a}_{1}}(x,y)|^{-\varepsilon_{1}}],\ \sum_{(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c})\in\mathcal{Z}_{j,\mathbf{A}}^{2}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{b}})\mu(I_{\mathbf{c}})\mathbf{1}_{[-\sigma,\sigma]}(\zeta_{j,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{b})-\zeta_{j,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{c}))\leq\sigma^{\gamma},

Then, there exists a constant
c>0
that only depends on

\displaystyle\forall t>0,\ X_{x,y}(\mathbf{A})\leq c|\eta_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1},\mathbf{a}_{1}}(x,y)|^{-\varepsilon_{1}^{2}}+|\eta_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1},\mathbf{a}_{1}}(x,y)|^{-\frac{t^{3}}{2\sqrt{k}}}.\prod_{j=1}^{k}M_{j,\mathbf{A}}(t^{2}),

\displaystyle M_{j,\mathbf{A}}(t):=\sum_{\mathbf{b}\in\mathcal{Z}_{j,\mathbf{A}}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{b}})e^{t|\ln\zeta_{j,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{b})|}.

We will now define the sets of

Definition 4.1

(Choices of parameters)

Denote by

3.3

\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,1)

With these fixed, we can now define

(Well-distributed words)

2.2
together with the bound
X_{x,y}(\mathbf{A})\leq 1
due to
|e^{ix}|=1
and that
\mu
is a probability measure.

Firstly, by the non-concentration Proposition
5.1
and Gibbs property, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{A}\notin\mathcal{W}}p_{\mathbf{A}}\lesssim e^{-\alpha_{reg}n},

for some
\alpha_{reg}>0
, which gives the first term.

Secondly, by Proposition
2.1
, we have for any small enough
t>0
that

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{A}}p_{\mathbf{A}}\iint_{I_{b(\mathbf{A})}^{2}}X_{x,y}(\mathbf{A})\mathrm{\mathbf{1}}(\mathbf{a}_{k+1}:C_{\psi}e^{\lambda n}\kappa_{+}(\mathbf{a}_{k+1})>e^{\varepsilon_{0}n})\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\psi}^{t}e^{-\varepsilon_{0}tn}\sum_{\mathbf{a}_{1}\dots\mathbf{a}_{k}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}_{1}})\dots\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}_{k}})\sum_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}})(e^{\lambda n}\kappa_{+}(\mathbf{a}_{k+1}))^{t}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\psi}^{t}e^{-\varepsilon_{0}tn}2C_{\phi}e^{\varepsilon_{\gamma_{0}}t^{2}n}=C_{\psi}^{t}2C_{\phi}e^{-(\varepsilon_{0}t-\varepsilon_{\gamma_{0}}t^{2})n},

where
\varepsilon_{\gamma_{0}}t^{2}<\varepsilon_{0}t
as long as
t>0
is small enough.

Finally, we can use the
s_{\mu}
-upper regularity of
\mu
(Proposition
2.2
) and that
\mu
is a probability measure, and that
|e^{ix}|=1
to get a bound:

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{A}}p_{\mathbf{A}}\iint_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{A}}^{c}}X_{x,y}(\mathbf{A})\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathbf{A}}p_{\mathbf{A}}\mu^{2\otimes}\big((x,y):|x-y|\leq\exp(-(\lambda-\varepsilon_{\psi}c_{0})n)\kappa_{-}({\mathbf{a}_{k+1}})^{-1}\exp(-\varepsilon_{0}n/2)\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\mathbf{A}}p_{\mathbf{A}}\exp(-s_{\mu}(\lambda-\varepsilon_{\psi}c_{0})n)\kappa_{-}({\mathbf{a}_{k+1}})^{-s_{\mu}}\exp(-s_{\mu}\varepsilon_{0}n/2)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-ns_{\mu}(\lambda-\varepsilon_{\psi}c_{0}+\varepsilon_{0}/2))\exp(-t\lambda n)\sum_{\mathbf{a}_{1}\dots\mathbf{a}_{k}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}_{1}})\dots\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}_{k}})\sum_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}})e^{t\lambda n}\kappa_{-}({\mathbf{a}_{k+1}})^{-t}

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-ns_{\mu}(\lambda-\varepsilon_{\psi}c_{0}+\varepsilon_{0}/2))\exp(-t\lambda n)2C_{\phi}\exp(\varepsilon_{\gamma_{0}}t^{2}n),

as
\mu
is a probability measure by Proposition
2.1
as long as
0<t<\min\{t_{\gamma_{0}},s_{\mu}\}
. Thus choosing
t>0
and
\varepsilon_{\psi}>0
small enough that

\displaystyle s_{\mu}(\lambda-\varepsilon_{\psi}c_{0}+\varepsilon_{0}/2)+t\lambda-\varepsilon_{\gamma_{0}}t^{2}>0,

we have exponential decay for this term. ∎

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{W}\cap\tilde{\mathcal{W}}
and
(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{A}}
. Then, since
c_{0}=(2k+1)\lambda+\varepsilon_{0}
, we can bound from below:

\displaystyle|\eta_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1},\mathbf{a}_{1}}(x,y)|

\displaystyle=|e^{-2\lambda nk}\xi(g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(x)-g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}(y))\psi^{\prime}(g_{\mathbf{a}_{1}}(x_{0}))|

\displaystyle\geq\exp((\lambda+\varepsilon_{0})n)(\inf|\psi^{\prime}|)\kappa_{-}({\mathbf{a}_{k+1}})|x-y|

\displaystyle\geq\exp((\lambda+\varepsilon_{0})n)\exp(-\varepsilon_{\psi}c_{0}n)\kappa_{-}({\mathbf{a}_{k+1}})|x-y|

\displaystyle\geq\exp(\varepsilon_{0}n)\exp((\lambda-\varepsilon_{\psi}c_{0})n)\kappa_{-}({\mathbf{a}_{k+1}})|x-y|,

\displaystyle\geq e^{\varepsilon_{0}n/2},

and from above as
\mathbf{A}\in\tilde{\mathcal{W}}
and
|x-y|\leq|I|
:

\displaystyle|\eta_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1},\mathbf{a}_{1}}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-2\lambda nk}|\xi|(\sup|\psi^{\prime}|)(\sup|g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}^{\prime}|)|x-y|

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-2\lambda nk}e^{(2k+1)\lambda n+\varepsilon_{0}n}(\sup|\psi^{\prime}|)(\sup|g_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}^{\prime}|)|x-y|

\displaystyle\leq e^{\varepsilon_{0}n}C_{\psi}e^{\lambda n}\kappa_{+}({\mathbf{a}_{k+1}})

\displaystyle\leq e^{2\varepsilon_{0}n}.

Thus by (
4.3
) we know that for all
t>0
:

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{W}\cap\tilde{\mathcal{W}}}p_{\mathbf{A}}\sup_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{A}}}X_{x,y}(\mathbf{A})\leq ce^{-\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon_{1}^{2}n/2}+\sum_{\mathbf{A}}p_{\mathbf{A}}(\exp(\varepsilon_{0}n/2))^{-\frac{t^{3}}{2\sqrt{k}}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}M_{j,\mathbf{A}}(t^{2})

so we have reduced Theorem
1.1
to the moment bound for
M_{j,\mathbf{A}}(t^{2})
. If
t\in(0,\varepsilon_{1})
, since
|\ln\zeta_{j,\mathbf{A}}\left(\mathbf{b}\right)|=|2\lambda n-\ln|g_{\mathbf{a}_{j}\mathbf{b}}^{\prime}\left(g_{\mathbf{a}_{j+1}}\left(x_{0}\right)\right)|
, we have by the chain rule and Gibbs property

\displaystyle\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}_{j}})\mu(I_{\mathbf{b}})e^{t^{2}|\ln\zeta_{j,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{b})|}

\displaystyle\lesssim w_{\mathbf{a}_{j}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{a}_{j+1}})\Big(e^{2\lambda n}g_{\mathbf{a}_{j}\mathbf{b}}^{\prime}\left(x_{\mathbf{a}_{j+1}}\right)\Big)^{t^{2}}

Thus by Proposition
2.1
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{a}_{1}\dots\mathbf{a}_{k}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}_{1}})\dots\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}_{k}})\prod_{j=1}^{k}M_{j,\mathbf{A}}(t^{2})

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{\mathbf{a}_{j}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}_{j}})M_{j,\mathbf{A}}(t^{2})

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{\mathbf{a}_{j}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}_{j}})\sum_{\mathbf{b}\in\mathcal{Z}_{j,\mathbf{A}}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{b}})e^{t|\ln\zeta_{j,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{b})|}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{\mathbf{a}_{j}}\sum_{\mathbf{b}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}_{j}})\mu(I_{\mathbf{b}})e^{t^{2}|\ln\zeta_{j,\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{b})|}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(2C_{\phi}e^{2\varepsilon_{\gamma_{0}}nt^{4}}\Big)^{k}.

Thus Proposition
2.1
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{A}}p_{\mathbf{A}}(\exp(\varepsilon_{0}n/2))^{-\frac{t^{3}}{2\sqrt{k}}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}M_{j,\mathbf{A}}(t^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}})(\exp(\varepsilon_{0}n/2))^{-\frac{t^{3}}{2\sqrt{k}}}\sum_{\mathbf{a}_{1}\dots\mathbf{a}_{k}}\mu\left(I_{\mathbf{a}_{1}}\right)\cdots\mu\left(I_{\mathbf{a}_{k}}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{k}M_{j,\mathbf{A}}(t^{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}_{k+1}})(\exp(\varepsilon_{0}n/2))^{-\frac{t^{3}}{2\sqrt{k}}}\Big(2C_{\phi}e^{2\varepsilon_{\gamma_{0}}nt^{4}}\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=(2C_{\phi})^{k}\exp\Big(-n(\frac{t^{3}}{4\sqrt{k}}\varepsilon_{0}-2k\varepsilon_{\gamma_{0}}t^{4})\Big).

Now we see that

\displaystyle\frac{t^{3}}{4\sqrt{k}}\varepsilon_{0}-2k\varepsilon_{\gamma_{0}}t^{4}>0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad 0<t<\frac{1}{8k^{3/2}\varepsilon_{\gamma_{0}}}\varepsilon_{0},

which is possible if
t>0
is chosen small enough. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

5.
Reducing multiscale non-concentration to larger probability space

We are now left with proving Proposition
4.3
. Recall its formulation:

Proposition 5.1
.

Define
\alpha_{reg}:=\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon_{1}\gamma_{2}>0
. Then most blocks are well-distributed:

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{A}\notin\mathcal{W}_{n}^{k+1}}w_{\mathbf{A}}(x_{b(\mathbf{A})})\lesssim e^{-\alpha_{reg}n},

recall Definition
4.2
for the Definition of well-distributed blocks of words.

This estimate will be proved using by a sequence of reductions, adapting arguments found e.g. in
[
18
,
67
,
43
,
13
]
. For clarity, we will drop the conditions on admissiblity of words in the sums, they are implicit. We denote
w_{\mathbf{a}}:=w_{\mathbf{a}}(x_{b(\mathbf{a})})
.

The first step is to reduce this estimate to an easier bound in a larger probability, using Markov’s inequality.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose that, for
n
large enough and for every
\sigma\in[e^{-4\varepsilon_{0}n},e^{-\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon_{1}n/2}]
:

\displaystyle\sum_{(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d})\in(\Sigma^{n})^{4}}w_{\mathbf{a}}w_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{c}}w_{\mathbf{d}}\chi_{[-\sigma,\sigma]}(\ln g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{d}})-\ln g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{c}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{d}}))\leq\sigma^{2\gamma_{2}}.

Then the conclusion of Proposition
5.1
holds.

Proof.

First of all, notice that well-distributed blocks are concatenations of well-distributed couples. In other words:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{n}^{k+1}=\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}\left\{\mathbf{A}\in\Sigma^{(k+1)n}\mid\ (\mathbf{a}_{j-1},\mathbf{a}_{j})\in\mathcal{W}_{n}^{2}\right\}.

We then find (using Gibbs estimates
w_{\mathbf{a}}\simeq\mu(I_{\mathbf{a}})
and
w_{\mathbf{A}}\simeq w_{\mathbf{a}_{1}}\dots w_{\mathbf{a}_{k}}
):

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{A}\in\Sigma^{n(k+1)}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{n}^{k+1}}w_{\mathbf{A}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\underset{(\mathbf{a}_{j-1},\mathbf{a}_{j})\notin\mathcal{W}_{n}^{2}}{\sum_{\mathbf{A}\in\Sigma^{n(k+1)}}}w_{\mathbf{A}}\lesssim{\sum_{(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c})\in(\Sigma^{n})^{2}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{n}^{2}}}w_{\mathbf{a}}w_{\mathbf{c}}.

If we define, for all
l\in\mathbb{N}
, the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{n,l}^{2}:=\Big\{(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{d})\in(\Sigma^{n})^{2},\ \sum_{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}\in\Sigma^{n}}w_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{c}}\chi_{[-e^{-l},e^{-l}]}(\ln g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{d}})-\ln g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{c}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{d}}))\leq e^{-(l+1)\gamma_{2}}\Big\},

then we have the inclusion

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{n}^{2}\supset\bigcap_{\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon_{1}n/2\leq l\leq 4\varepsilon_{0}n}\mathcal{W}_{n,l}^{2},

which gives, by Markov’s inequality:

\displaystyle{\sum_{(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c})\in(\Sigma^{n})^{2}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{n}^{2}}}w_{\mathbf{a}}w_{\mathbf{c}}\leq\underset{\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon_{1}n/2\leq l\leq 4\varepsilon_{0}n}{\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}}}{\sum_{(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{d})\in(\Sigma^{n})^{2}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{n,l}^{2}}}w_{\mathbf{a}}w_{\mathbf{d}}

\displaystyle\leq\underset{\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon_{1}n/2\leq l\leq 4\varepsilon_{0}n}{\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}}}e^{(l+1)\gamma_{2}}{\sum_{(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d})\in(\Sigma^{n})^{4}}}w_{\mathbf{a}}w_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{c}}w_{\mathbf{d}}\ \chi_{[-e^{-l},e^{-l}]}(e^{2\lambda n}g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{d}})-e^{2\lambda n}g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{c}}^{\prime}(x_{\mathbf{d}}))

\displaystyle\leq\underset{\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon_{1}n/2\leq l\leq 4\varepsilon_{0}n}{\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}}}e^{(l+1)\gamma_{2}}e^{-2l\gamma_{2}}\lesssim e^{-\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon_{1}\gamma_{2}n}.

∎

Recall that we can write
\ln g_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}(x)=-S_{n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}}
. The bound in the hypothesis of the previous Lemma can then be rewritten in terms of Birkhoff sums. We check in the next Lemma that the desired bound holds under (QNL).

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose (QNL). Then for every
\sigma\in[e^{-4\varepsilon_{0}n},e^{-\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon_{1}n/2}]
:

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}\in\Sigma^{n}}w_{\mathbf{a}}w_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{c}}w_{\mathbf{d}}\ \chi_{[-\sigma,\sigma]}(S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{d}})-S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{c}}(x_{\mathbf{d}}))\lesssim\sigma^{\Theta/2}.

Proof.

We have, by Cauchy-Schwarz applied to the sum on
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}
:

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}\in\Sigma^{n}}w_{\mathbf{a}}w_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{c}}w_{\mathbf{d}}\ \chi_{[-\sigma,\sigma]}(S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{d}})-S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{c}}(x_{\mathbf{d}}))\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}\in\Sigma^{n}}w_{\mathbf{a}}w_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{c}}\Big(\sum_{\mathbf{d}\in\Sigma^{n}}w_{\mathbf{d}}\chi_{[-\sigma,\sigma]}(S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{d}})-S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{c}}(x_{\mathbf{d}}))\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\mathbf{a,b,c,d,e}}w_{\mathbf{a}}w_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{c}}w_{\mathbf{d}}w_{\mathbf{e}}\chi_{[-\sigma,\sigma]}(S_{2n}\tau(g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{d}}))-S_{2n}\tau(g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{c}}(x_{\mathbf{d}})))\chi_{[-\sigma,\sigma]}(S_{2n}\tau(g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{e}}))-S_{2n}\tau(g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{c}}(x_{\mathbf{e}})))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathbf{a,b,c,d,e}}w_{\mathbf{a}}w_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{c}}w_{\mathbf{d}}w_{\mathbf{e}}\chi_{[-2\sigma,2\sigma]}(S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{d}})-S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{c}}(x_{\mathbf{d}})-S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{e}})+S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{c}}(x_{\mathbf{e}})).

Notice then that

\displaystyle S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{d}})=S_{n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}}(g_{\mathbf{b}}x_{\mathbf{d}})+S_{n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{d}}).

By bounded
(C,\alpha)
-distortions

\displaystyle S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{d}})-S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{e}})=S_{n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{d}})-S_{n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{e}})+\mathcal{O}(\text{diam}(I_{\mathbf{b}})^{\alpha}),

which gives, since
\text{diam}(I_{\mathbf{b}})^{\alpha}\lesssim\kappa_{+}^{\alpha n}\leq e^{-8\varepsilon_{0}n}\leq\sigma^{2}
as
\varepsilon_{0}\leq\alpha|\ln\kappa_{+}|/8
:

\displaystyle S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{d}})-S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{c}}(x_{\mathbf{d}})-S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{e}})+S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\mathbf{c}}(x_{\mathbf{e}})

\displaystyle=S_{n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{d}})-S_{n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{c}}(x_{\mathbf{d}})-S_{n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{e}})+S_{n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{c}}(x_{\mathbf{e}})+\mathcal{O}(\sigma^{2}).

In particular, for
n
large enough, we find:

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{a,b,c,d,e}}w_{\mathbf{a}}w_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{c}}w_{\mathbf{d}}w_{\mathbf{e}}\chi_{[-2\sigma,2\sigma]}(S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{d}})-S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}\mathbf{c}}(x_{\mathbf{d}})-S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{e}})+S_{2n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}\mathbf{c}}(x_{\mathbf{e}}))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathbf{a,b,c,d,e}}w_{\mathbf{a}}w_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{c}}w_{\mathbf{d}}w_{\mathbf{e}}\chi_{[-3\sigma,3\sigma]}(S_{n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{d}})-S_{n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{c}}(x_{\mathbf{d}})-S_{n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{e}})+S_{n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{c}}(x_{\mathbf{e}}))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathbf{b,c,d,e}}w_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{c}}w_{\mathbf{d}}w_{\mathbf{e}}\chi_{[-3\sigma,3\sigma]}(S_{n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{d}})-S_{n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{c}}(x_{\mathbf{d}})-S_{n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{b}}(x_{\mathbf{e}})+S_{n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{c}}(x_{\mathbf{e}}))

Under (QNL), this last sum is bounded by a multiple of
\sigma^{\Theta}+\rho^{n}\leq 2\sigma^{\Theta}

since
\sigma
is slowly decaying comparing to
\rho
:
\sigma^{\Theta}\geq e^{-4\varepsilon_{0}\Theta n}\geq\rho^{n}
since
\varepsilon_{0}\leq|\ln(\rho)|/4
.
This gives the desired bound since
\sigma^{\Theta/2}=\sigma^{2\gamma_{2}}
. ∎

This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
. In the next section, we show how to check (QNL), first under (MNL) in a truly
C^{1+\alpha}
IFS setting with finite branches, and then under (UNI) in a
C^{2}
-setting with infinite branches and the exponential moment condition.

6.
Proofs of the non-concentration estimates

6.1.
C^{1+\alpha}
IFSs: Using (MNL) for non-concentration

This subsection is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.2
, that is, the proof that (MNL) implies (QNL) in the setting where
|\mathcal{A}|<\infty
. To simplify, suppose that the dynamics is also fully branched: the subshift of finite type setting is similar.

Let us denote
0<\kappa_{+},\kappa_{-}<1
such that for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
,
\kappa_{+}<g_{a}^{\prime}<\kappa_{-}
. Recall that we suppose that
\mu
is the equilibrium state associated with
\delta\tau
for
\delta:=\dim_{H}(F_{\Phi})
, so recall that in particular we have bounds:

\forall\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{A}^{*},\ \mu(I_{\mathbf{c}})\simeq|I_{\mathbf{c}}|^{\delta}

and, by our one-dimensional setting:

\forall\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{A}^{*},\ |I_{\mathbf{c}}|\simeq|g_{\mathbf{c}}^{\prime}(0)|\quad;\quad\forall\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathcal{A}^{*},\ |I_{\mathbf{ab}}|\simeq|I_{\mathbf{a}}|\cdot|I_{\mathbf{b}}|.

Recall we defined for
\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
and
\mathbf{a}\to x,y
,
X_{\mathbf{a}}(x,y):=S_{n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}}(x)-S_{n}\tau\circ g_{\mathbf{a}}(y)
.
Notice the relation:

X_{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}\widehat{\mathbf{a}}}(x,y)=S_{n_{1}+n_{2}}\tau\circ g_{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}\widehat{\mathbf{a}}}(x)-S_{n_{1}+n_{2}}\tau\circ g_{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}\widehat{\mathbf{a}}}(y)

=(S_{n_{1}}\tau\circ g_{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}})(g_{\widehat{\textbf{a}}}x)-(S_{n_{1}}\tau\circ g_{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}})(g_{\widehat{\textbf{a}}}y)+S_{n_{2}}\tau\circ g_{\widehat{\mathbf{a}}}(x)-S_{n_{2}}\tau\circ g_{\widehat{\mathbf{a}}}(y)

=X_{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}(g_{\widehat{\mathbf{a}}}x,g_{\widehat{\mathbf{a}}}y)+X_{\widehat{\mathbf{a}}}(x,y).

Notice further that since
\tau\in C^{\delta}
, we have, uniformly in
\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
:

|X_{\mathbf{a}}(x,y)|\lesssim|x-y|^{\delta}.

For the duration of this subsection 6.1, we will suppose that (MNL) holds. This is intuitively a very strong nonconcentration/spreading statement in
x
of the function
X_{\mathbf{a}}(\cdot,x_{0})-X_{\mathbf{b}}(\cdot,x_{0})
, since the pushforward by this function of the measure
\mu
flattens it to an absolutely continuous measure. (MNL) implies in particular a more familliar “UNI” statement
everywhere and at every scales
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For all
N\geq 1
, there exists
\widehat{\mathbf{a}},\widehat{\mathbf{b}}\in\mathcal{A}^{N}
such that for all
\textbf{c}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
and for all
y\in I_{\mathbf{c}}\cap F
, there exists
x\in I_{\mathbf{c}}\cap F
such that

|X_{\widehat{\mathbf{a}}}(x,y)-X_{\widehat{\mathbf{b}}}(x,y)|\geq\frac{1}{3C_{\text{(MNL)}}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{c}}).

Proof.

Let
N\geq 1
and let
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}
be such that (MNL) is satisfied. Fix any
y\in F
. Now consider
I:=[-\frac{1}{3C_{\text{(MNL)}}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{c}}),\frac{1}{3C_{\text{(MNL)}}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{c}})]
. Then

\mu(x\in F,\ X_{\mathbf{a}}(x,y)-X_{\mathbf{b}}(x,y)\in I)\leq C_{\text{(MNL)}}|I|=\frac{2}{3}\mu(I_{\mathbf{c}}).

This ensure that the set
\{x\in F,\ X_{\mathbf{a}}(x,y)-X_{\mathbf{b}}(x,y)\in I\}
can not contain
I_{\mathbf{c}}\cap F
.
∎

Thanks to this seperation condition, we can show that we can always find a word
\widehat{\mathbf{a}}\in\mathcal{A}^{N}
such that
X_{\widehat{\mathbf{a}}}
can avoid some given small enough intervals.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
y\in F
. Consider some function
t:F\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

Then for all
N\geq 1
, for all
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
, there exists
\widehat{\mathbf{a}}\in\mathcal{A}^{N}
such that:

\exists x\in I_{\mathbf{c}}\cap F,\ |X_{\widehat{\mathbf{a}}}(x,y)-t(x)|\geq\frac{1}{10C_{\text{(MNL)}}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{c}}).

Proof.

Let
y\in F
, and
t:F\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Let
N\geq 1
and let
\widehat{\mathbf{a}},\widehat{\mathbf{b}}\in\mathcal{A}^{N}
be given by the previous Lemma. Let
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
. We then know that there exists
x\in I_{\mathbf{c}}
such that
|X_{\widehat{\mathbf{a}}}(x,y)-X_{\widehat{\mathbf{b}}}(x,y)|\geq\frac{1}{3C_{\text{(MNL)}}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{c}}).

It follows that
|X_{\widehat{\mathbf{a}}}(x,y)-t(x)|\geq\frac{1}{10C_{\text{(MNL)}}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{c}})
or
|X_{\widehat{\mathbf{b}}}(x,y)-t(x)|\geq\frac{1}{10C_{\text{(MNL)}}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{c}})
.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
A\geq 1
. There exists
N:=N(A)\geq 1
large enough such that the following holds.

Let
t\in C^{\delta}(F,\mathbb{R})
such that
\|t\|_{C^{\delta}(F,\mathbb{R})}\leq A
. For all
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
and
y\in I_{\mathbf{c}}
:

\exists(\widehat{\mathbf{a}},\widehat{\mathbf{c}})\in(\mathcal{A}^{N})^{2},\ \forall x\in I_{\mathbf{c}\widehat{\mathbf{c}}},\ \forall\tilde{\mathbf{a}}\in\mathcal{A}^{*},\ |X_{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}\widehat{\mathbf{a}}}(x,y)-t(x)|\geq\frac{1}{100C_{\text{(MNL)}}}\mu(I_{\mathbf{c}}).

Proof.

Let
A\geq 1
. Let
N\geq 1
, we will fix it large enough during the proof depending on
A
. Let
t\in C^{\delta}
be such that
\|t\|_{C^{\delta}}\leq A
. Let
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
, and let
y\in I_{\mathbf{c}}
.

We know already that there exists

Lemma 6.8

9.
Proof of Theorem

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem

To be able to use Theorem

for Lorenz-maps but for Manneville-Pommeau maps we can for example see that

10.
Proof of Theorem
1.6
on examples of
C^{1+\alpha}
IFSs

We now prove Theorem
1.6
by constructing
C^{1+\alpha}
IFSs satisfying MNL, yielding fractal equilibrium states with power Fourier decay. The construction proceeds via fixed point theory in the moduli space of IFSs parametrized by equilibrium states, ensuring the required non-concentration to implement the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The key input is the existence of a branch whose log-derivative has
\alpha
–Hölder
lower
regularity, as furnished by a Cantor-type function with jumps precisely on the attractor.

Let us first define the space of Frostman measures
\mu
in
[-1,1]
for given constants
C\geq 1
and
\varepsilon>0
by

\mathcal{M}_{C,\varepsilon}:=\Big\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}([-1,1])\ :\ \mu(I)\leq C|I|^{\varepsilon}\ \text{for all intervals }I\text{ with }|I|\leq\tfrac{1}{100}\Big\}.

Given
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{C,\varepsilon}
, we can define an IFS
\Phi_{\mu}
depending on
\mu
with branches

g_{\mu,1}(x):=\kappa\int_{0}^{x}\exp(\mu(-\infty,t])\,dt+b_{1},\qquad g_{\mu,0}(x):=\kappa_{0}x+b_{0},\qquad x\in[0,1],

where
0<\kappa_{0}\leq\kappa<1/10
and
b_{0},b_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
are chosen so that
\Phi_{\mu}=\{g_{\mu,0},g_{\mu,1}\}
satisfies the strong separation condition. By the Frostman property,
\Phi_{\mu}
is
C^{1+\alpha}
for any
\alpha\leq\varepsilon
. Let
T_{\mu}
be the associated expanding map on
F_{\Phi_{\mu}}
and write
\tau_{\mu}(x):=-\log|T_{\mu}^{\prime}(x)|
as the
C^{\alpha}
roof function.

Theorem 10.1
.

For any
C>0
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{C,\varepsilon}
and
0<\delta_{\mu}<1
such that the Gibbs measure associated to the potential
-\delta_{\mu}\tau_{\mu}
for the (truly)
C^{1+\varepsilon}
IFS
\Phi_{\mu}
has power Fourier decay.

The proof performs a fixed-point analysis for “
\mu
–controlled” transfer operators

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}f(x):=\sum_{i}|g^{\prime}_{\mu,i}(x)|^{\delta_{\mu}}\,f(g_{\mu,i}(x)),

which depend on the equilibrium state
\mu
, where
\delta_{\mu}\in(0,1)
is chosen as the unique number such that

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{*}(\mu)(\mathbb{R})=1,

which exists by the intermediate value theorem as
\mu
is a probability measure. Then this gives us an operator
\Psi:\mathcal{M}_{C,\varepsilon}\to\mathcal{M}_{C,\varepsilon}
defined by

\Psi(\mu):=\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{*}(\mu),\quad\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{C,\varepsilon}.

Now the strategy to prove Theorem
10.1
is to construct a fixed point
\mu
for the operator
\Psi
, that is, a measure satisfying

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{*}(\mu)=\mu.

Therefore, the pressure
P(\phi^{\mu})=0
for
\phi^{\mu}:=\delta_{\mu}\tau_{\mu}
, which implies, as
\Phi_{\mu}
is
C^{1+\alpha}
, that
\delta^{\mu}=\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathrm{supp}\,\mu)
and that
\mu
is
\delta^{\mu}
Ahlfors-David regular. This then allows us to have a convenient form for the geometric potential
\tau_{\mu}
: for some
c_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\log g_{1}^{\prime}(x)=c_{1}+\mu(-\infty,x)\in C^{\delta_{\mu}}.

We can find this fixed point
\mu
by adapting Schauder’s fixed point theorem to the operator
\Psi:\mathcal{M}_{C,\varepsilon}\to\mathcal{M}_{C,\varepsilon}
, which is possible by the following Lemma:

Lemma 10.2
.

(0)

As a map from
\mathcal{M}_{C,\varepsilon}
, the maps
\mu\mapsto g_{\mu,i}^{\prime}
and
\mu\mapsto g_{\mu,i}
are continuous

(1)

The choice of
\delta^{\mu}
is well-defined and continuous as a map of
\mu
, and that
\delta^{\mu}\geq 3\varepsilon
for all
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{C,\varepsilon}

(2)

\Psi
is well-defined:
\Psi(\mathcal{M}_{C,\varepsilon})\subset\mathcal{M}_{C,\varepsilon}

(3)

\Psi
is continuous on
\mathcal{M}_{C,\varepsilon}
in the weak-
\ast

(4)

\mathcal{M}_{C,\varepsilon}
is closed and convex

We postpone the proof of these properties the end of the section. Now, the fixed point property then allows us to prove Theorem
10.1
by following Lemma:

Lemma 10.3

(Measure Non-Linearity)

The IFS
\Phi_{\mu}
and the fixed point
\mu

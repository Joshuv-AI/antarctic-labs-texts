On Unitary 2-Group Symmetries
Thomas Bartsch

Abstract
Global internal symmetries act unitarily on local observables or states of a quantum system. In this note, we aim to generalise this statement to extended observables by considering unitary actions of finite global 2-group symmetries 𝒢 \mathcal{G} on line operators. We propose that the latter transform in unitary 2-representations of 𝒢 \mathcal{G} , which we classify up to unitary equivalence. Our results recover the known classification of ordinary 2-representations of finite 2-groups, but provide additional data interpreted as a type of reflection anomaly for 𝒢 \mathcal{G} .

3.2.3
Equivalences

Having established the notion of intertwiners for unitary 2-representations, we can now discuss equivalences between them. Two unitary 2-representations
\rho=(n,\sigma,\chi,c,s)
and
\rho^{\prime}=(n^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime},\chi^{\prime},c^{\prime},s^{\prime})
are equivalent if there exist an invertible intertwiner
\eta:\rho\Rightarrow\rho^{\prime}
between them. Now let
\eta
be specified by data
\eta=(V,\varphi)
as before. Invertibility of
\eta
can then be reduced to the following:

•

As
V
is an invertible
(n^{\prime}\!\times\!n)
-matrix of Hermitian spaces, we must have that
n=n^{\prime}
with
V
being of the form
V_{ij}=\delta_{i,\tau(j)}\cdot\mathbb{C}_{z_{i}}
for some permutation
\tau\in S_{n}
and some
z\in(\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{n}
. Furthermore, since
V_{ij}=0
unless
\chi_{i}^{\prime}=\chi_{j}
, we must have that
\chi^{\prime}={}^{\tau\!}\chi
, where
({}^{\tau\!}\chi)_{i}=\chi_{\tau^{-1}(i)}
.

•

As
\varphi
provides isomorphisms
\varphi(g)_{ij}:V_{ij}\to V_{\sigma^{\prime}_{g}(i),\sigma_{g}(j)}
for each
g\in G
, we must have that
\sigma^{\prime}_{g}=\tau\circ\sigma_{g}\circ\tau^{-1}
for all
g\in G
. Furthermore, since the entries of
V
are one-dimensional, the above linear maps need to be of the form

\varphi(g)_{ij}\;=\;\delta_{i,\tau(j)}\cdot\vartheta_{g\hskip 1.0pt\triangleright\hskip 0.7pti}(g)

(137)

for some multiplicative phases
\vartheta_{i}(g)\in U(1)
. Plugging this into the composition rule (
95
) then yields

(d\vartheta)_{i}(g,h)\;\,\equiv\;\,\frac{\vartheta_{g^{-1}\hskip 1.0pt\triangleright\hskip 0.7pti}(h)\cdot\vartheta_{i}(g)}{\vartheta_{i}(gh)}\;\,=\;\,\frac{c^{\prime}_{i}(g,h)}{c_{\tau^{-1}(i)}(g,h)}\;,

(138)

which implies that
[c^{\prime}/\,{}^{\tau\hskip-1.0pt}c]=1\in H^{2}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(G,U(1)^{n})
. In addition, the conjugation rule (
96
) yields

(dz)_{i}(g)\;\equiv\;\frac{z_{(g^{-1})\hskip 0.7pt\triangleright\hskip 0.7pti}}{z_{i}}\;=\;\frac{s^{\prime}_{i}(g)}{s_{\tau^{-1}(i)}(g)}\;,

(139)

which implies that
[s^{\prime}]=[{}^{\hskip 1.0pt\tau\hskip-1.0pt}s]\in H^{1}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(G,(\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{n})
.

Note that with
\eta=(V,\varphi)
as above, the inverse of
\eta
is given by its adjoint
\eta^{\dagger_{1}}=(V^{\dagger_{1}},\varphi^{\dagger_{1}})
. This shows that
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
are in fact unitarily equivalent.

In summary, two unitary 2-representations
\rho=(n,\sigma,\chi,c,s)
and
\rho^{\prime}=(n^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime},\chi^{\prime},c^{\prime},s^{\prime})
are (unitarily) equivalent if they have the same dimension
n=n^{\prime}
and there exists a permutation
\tau\in S_{n}
such that

\sigma^{\prime}={}^{\tau\!}\sigma\;,\qquad\chi^{\prime}={}^{\tau\!}\chi\;,\qquad[\hskip 0.5ptc^{\prime}/\,{}^{\tau\hskip-1.0pt}c\hskip 1.0pt]=1\;,\qquad[s^{\prime}]=[{}^{\hskip 1.0pt\tau\hskip-1.0pt}s]\;.

(140)

Now suppose that
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
are irreducible unitary 2-representations, so that we can label them by data
\rho=(H,\lambda,u,p)
and
\rho^{\prime}=(H^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime},u^{\prime},p^{\prime})
as before. By similar reasoning as above,
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
are then (unitarily) equivalent if there exists an
x\in G
such that

H^{\prime}={}^{x\!}H\;,\qquad\lambda^{\prime}={}^{x\!}\lambda\;,\qquad\left[\frac{u^{\prime}}{{}^{x}u}\cdot\braket{\lambda,\gamma_{x}(\alpha)}\right]=1\;,\qquad p^{\prime}\,=\,{}^{x}p\;.

(141)

Note that under the canonical 2-functor
\mathcal{F}:(H,\lambda,u,p)\mapsto(H,\lambda,u)
, this reproduces the known notion of equivalence of ordinary irreducible 2-representations. Two equivalent unitary 2-representations are hence equivalent as ordinary 2-representations as well. The converse, however, is not true, since equivalence as unitary 2-representations additionally requires the associated group homomorphisms
p^{\prime}
and
{}^{x}p
to agree.

3.3
Positivity

Having classified unitary 2-representation of a finite 2-group

, we now turn to the special case of

-2-functors

To begin with, the classification of positive unitary 2-representations

, so that any such

with

and

,

for each

satisfying

\varphi(g)_{h\,\triangleright\hskip 1.0pt(ij)}\ \circ\,\varphi(h)_{ij}\,\;=\,\;\frac{c^{\prime}_{gh\,\triangleright i}(g,h)}{c_{gh\,\triangleright j}(g,h)}\,\cdot\,\varphi(g\cdot h)_{ij}\;.

The duals and adjoints of such an intertwiner are as in (
98
), (
99
) and (

130
). Moreover, asking

to invertible reveals that two positive unitary 2-representations

and

and there exists a permutation

\sigma^{\prime}={}^{\tau\!}\sigma\;,\qquad\chi^{\prime}={}^{\tau\!}\chi\;,\qquad[\hskip 0.5ptc^{\prime}/\,{}^{\tau\hskip-1.0pt}c\hskip 1.0pt]=1\;,\qquad s^{\prime}={}^{\tau\hskip-1.5pt}s\;.

Importantly, comparing with (
140
), an equivalence between two positive unitary 2-repre-sentations
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
requires
s^{\prime}
and
{}^{\tau\hskip-1.5pt}s
to agree as 1-cocycles in
Z^{1}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(G,(\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{n})
, and not just as cohomology classes in
H^{1}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(G,(\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{n})
, as was the case for unitary (non-positive) 2-representations. In particular, this implies that when classifying irreducible positive unitary 2-representations by subgroups
H\subset G
in analogy to subsection
3.1.1
, we can only employ the isomorphism

Z^{1}\big(G,(\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{G/H}\big)\;\cong\;C^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{H}\;,

(146)

where the group of (normalised)
H
-covariant 1-cochains on
G
is given by

C^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{H}\;:=\;\big\{\hskip 1.0ptq:G\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}\;\big|\;q(h\cdot g)=q(h)\cdot q(g)\;\;\forall\;h\in H,\;g\in G\big\}\;.

(147)

As a result, the irreducible positive unitary 2-representations of
\mathcal{G}=A[1]\rtimes_{\alpha}G
can be labelled by quadruples
\rho=(H,\lambda,u,q)
consisting of

1.

a subgroup
H\subset G
,

2.

a
H
-invariant character
\lambda\in A^{\vee}
,

3.

a 2-cochain
u\in C^{2}(H,U(1))
satisfying
du=\braket{\lambda,\alpha|{}_{H}}
,

4.

a
H
-covariant 1-cochain
q\in C^{1}\hskip-1.0pt(G,\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{H}
.

Two such positive unitary 2-representations
\rho=(H,\lambda,u,q)
and
\rho^{\prime}=(H^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime},u^{\prime},q^{\prime})
are considered equivalent if there exists an
x\in G
such that

H^{\prime}={}^{x\!}H\;,\qquad\lambda^{\prime}={}^{x\!}\lambda\;,\qquad\left[\frac{u^{\prime}}{{}^{x\!}u}\cdot\braket{\lambda,\gamma_{x}(\alpha)}\right]=1\;,\qquad q^{\prime}\,=\,{}^{x}q\;.

(148)

The canonical 2-functor
\mathcal{E}:\text{2Rep}^{\dagger}_{+}(\mathcal{G})\to\text{2Rep}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{G})
sends

(H,\lambda,u,q)\;\mapsto\;(H,\lambda,u,q|_{H})

(149)

and is essentially surjective. Two equivalent positive unitary 2-representations are hence equivalent as unitary 2-representations as well. The converse, however, is not true, since equivalence as positive unitary 2-representations additionally requires the associated 1-cochains
q^{\prime}
and
{}^{x}q
to agree.

The irreducible intertwiners between two given irreducible positive unitary 2-representa-tions

(150)

Decoding Algorithms for Two-dimensional Constacyclic Codes over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q}
Vidya Sagar ∗ ID, Shikha Patel ID and Shayan Srinivasa Garani ID

Abstract
We derive the spectral domain properties of two-dimensional (2-D) ( λ 1 , λ 2 ) (\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}) -constacyclic codes over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} using the 2-D finite field Fourier transform (FFFT). Based on the spectral nulls of 2-D ( λ 1 , λ 2 ) (\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}) -constacyclic codes, we characterize the structure of 2-D constacyclic coded arrays. The proposed 2-D construction has flexible code rates and works for any code areas, be it odd or even area. We present an algorithm to detect the location of 2-D errors. Further, we also propose decoding algorithms for extracting the error values using both time and frequency domain properties by exploiting the sparsity that arises due to duality in the time and frequency domains. Through several illustrative examples, we demonstrate the working of the proposed decoding algorithms.



I
Introduction

Cyclic codes are one of the most important classes of linear codes due to their rich algebraic structure. These codes represent a significant category of codes for two primary reasons. First, cyclic codes can be effectively encoded with shift registers, which is why they are favored for their application in engineering. Second, from a theoretical point of view, cyclic codes are readily identified as the ideals of the quotient ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
.
It was first studied in 1957 by Prange
[
1
]
. Later, it was generalized as constacyclic codes by Berlekamp
[
2
]
. These codes can correct both random errors and burst errors. Cyclic codes based on Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem (BCH) and Reed-Solomon (RS) designs have made a tremendous impact in data storage systems, such as hard disk drives and flash memories. Hence, it is natural to consider two-dimensional (2-D) generalizations of 1-D cyclic codes for investigation with applications to multidimensional data storage devices, bar code readers, etc.

In a seminal work done fifty years ago, Imai
[
3
]
introduced a theory for binary 2-D cyclic (TDC) codes of odd area. These binary TDC codes were derived using the idea of a common zero (CZ) set, i.e., zeros common to all codewords. Further, parity-check tensors, parity-check positions, and dual of TDC codes were characterized using the CZ set. TDC codes can be practically implemented using simple 2-D linear feedback shift registers and combinatorial logics. As a result, the examination of these codes over finite fields has received considerable attention from other researchers in the recent past. In
[
4
]
, the authors explored two-dimensional skew cyclic codes over a finite field. Two-dimensional cyclic codes corresponding to the ideals of the ring
\mathbb{F}[x,y]/\langle x^{l}-1,y^{2^{k}}-1\rangle
were explored by the authors in
[
5
]
. Later, repeated root two-dimensional constacyclic codes of different lengths were explored by the authors in
[
6
,
7
]
. The structural behavior of 2-D skew cyclic codes over the ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}+u\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
u^{2}=1
was explored by Sharma et al.
[
8
]
. Recently, Patel and Prakash
[
9
]
have extended this work to
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
-constacyclic codes.

In almost all of the aforementioned works, the authors have used the polynomial decomposition method and not the common zero sets approach as in Imai’s work
[
3
]
. Characterization of CZ sets is useful in the encoding and decoding of these codes. Taking into account these practical code design considerations such as code rates, flexible code areas, etc., we generalized Imai’s theory on TDC codes to 2-D
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
-constacyclic codes using the CZ set approach with the motivation that these codes can get a better minimum distance compared to 2-D cyclic codes with the same code areas and code rates, thus improving the error correction ability in
[
10
]
. An encoding scheme for 2-D
constacyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
was also provided in that work
[
10
]
.

Frequency domain (image under the 2-D finite field Fourier transform) coding is also called transform domain coding or spectral domain coding. Transform domain coding techniques for finite fields were originally introduced by Blahut
[
11
,
12
]
, who first examined cyclic BCH codes and their decoding using syndrome computations
[
13
]
. Transform domain encoders and decoders are simple to implement. Further, the syndromes can be readily identified by non-zero spectral values in the parity positions. Inspired by the transform domain approach, several researchers have explored coding techniques in this area; the readers can refer to the following papers
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
and references therein.

Decoding of 2-D BCH codes was originally introduced by Madhusudana and Siddiqui by extending Blahut’s 1-D approach to the 2-D case using row and column decoders. This approach was made more efficient by exploiting the conjugacy properties of finite fields along with parallel architectures for the optimization of the encoding and decoding algorithms towards low-latency and high-throughput hardware architectures in
[
19
]
. Further, transform domain coding for handling specific 2-D error patterns was explored by Yoon and Moon by extending Imai’s approach using the disjoint syndrome criterion for handling error patterns. The reader must note that, in general, it is difficult to endow a 2-D code with all the properties, such as correction of random errors, cluster errors, and specific error patterns. For example, Abdel-Ghaffar, McEliece and Van Tilborg
[
20
]
have specifically investigated the construction of 2-D codes for rectangular cluster error identification. The interested author can refer to
[
20
]
and references therein.

Our approach in this article is not in the line of specific codes for burst identification, but rather focused on 2-D random errors, since specific burst patterns can be treated as random errors by designing codes over higher dimensions in appropriate field extensions, following the constructions in
[
10
]
. In this article, we focus on characterizing the 2-D code properties of constacyclic codes and constructing decoding algorithms to handle random errors. The central ideas behind our approach are as follows:

(a) Based on the common zero (CZ) set, we characterize the code properties of 2-D constacyclic codes. Further, we derive several useful spectral domain properties of 2-D contacyclic coded arrays, useful during decoding.

(b) We identify the error locations by computing the syndromes over the row and column locations, and identify the corresponding bi-index of the error locations.

(c) We propose decoding algorithms for both low rate and high rate 2-D constacyclic codes by exploiting the sparsity due to duality in the time and frequency domains based on the CZ sets and spectral nulls, respectively.

Here are the major contributions of our work:

1.

We discuss properties of 2-D FFFT, 2-D constacyclic codes in frequency domain, and spectral nulls of 2-D constacyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

2.

We present an algorithm for error-detection in 2-D constacyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Further, we propose a method for finding the locations of errors.

3.

We propose two efficient decoding methods for correcting random errors in 2-D constacyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, exploiting the sparsity resulting from the duality of finite field Fourier transform.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section

V

\mathbb{F}_{q}
followed by a method to find the locations of errors. In addition, we propose efficient decoding methods for correcting random errors in 2-D constacyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

VI

Definition 1

-linear code

Recall that for

Let

Let the set of all

\Omega=\{(i,j)|\ 0\leq i\leq M-1,0\leq j\leq N-1\}

(1)

and let

\mathcal{P}[\Omega]=\Big\{\sum_{(i,j)\in\Omega}p_{i,j}x^{i}y^{j}\ |\ p_{i,j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\Big\}.

(2)

For an array

(3)

The array

Theorem 3
.

(Conjugate Symmetry Property)
Let
A_{j}
for
j=0,1,\dots,n-1
take elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
and
n|(q^{m}-1)
. Then,
a_{i}=0
for
i=0,1,\dots,n-1
are all elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if the following
equations are satisfied:

A_{j}^{q}=A_{qj\mod(n)}
,
j=0,1,\dots,n-1
.

Theorem 4
.

(Reversal Preserving Property)
Let
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{n-1})
and
\boldsymbol{b}=(b_{0},b_{1},\dots,b_{n-1})
be two vectors over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

\boldsymbol{A}=(A_{0},A_{1},\dots,A_{n-1})

for all

.

IV-A

Two-dimensional finite field Fourier transform

Two-dimensional finite field Fourier transform (2-D FFFT)
[
22
]
is an extension of the 1-D finite field Fourier transform (1-D FFFT). Instead of a row vector, we consider an array of size
M\times N
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Consider a 2-D array of size
M\times N
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
as

c=\begin{pmatrix}c_{0,0}&c_{0,1}&\dots&c_{0,N-1}\\
c_{1,0}&c_{1,1}&\dots&c_{1,N-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
c_{M-1,0}&c_{M-1,1}&\dots&c_{M-1,N-1}\end{pmatrix}.

(12)

From Definition
9
, we define the 2-D FFFT as follows. Let
\gamma,\beta
be primitive
M^{\rm th}
and
N^{\rm th}
roots of
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, respectively, and let
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}
be
M^{\rm th}
and
N^{\rm th}
roots of unity, respectively, in an extended field, say
\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
, that is,
\zeta_{1}^{M}=1
,
\zeta_{2}^{N}=1
, and
\gamma^{M}=\lambda_{1}
,
\beta^{N}=\lambda_{2}
.
Then 2-D FFFT is a mapping from
\mathbb{M}_{M\times N}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\longrightarrow\mathbb{M}_{M\times N}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}})
defined by

{\rm FFFT}(c)=C,

(13)

where

C=\begin{pmatrix}C_{0,0}&C_{0,1}&\dots&C_{0,N-1}\\
C_{1,0}&c_{1,1}&\dots&C_{1,N-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
C_{M-1,0}&C_{M-1,1}&\dots&C_{M-1,N-1}\end{pmatrix}

(14)

and

C_{\theta,\phi}=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_{i,j}{(\gamma{\zeta_{1}}^{\theta})}^{i}{(\beta{\zeta_{2}}^{\phi})}^{j}

(15)

for
(\theta,\phi)\in\Omega
.
We may replace the double summation
{\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}}{\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}}
by
{\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in\Omega}}
, where

\Omega=\{(i,j)|0\leq i\leq M-1\,\textnormal{ and }0\leq j\leq N-1\}.

(16)

Remark 2
.

Note that FFFT defined by Eq. (
13
) is an injective function, but not surjective.

Hence, the inverse finite field Fourier transform (2-D IFFFT) of an element in the range of Eq. (
13
) is defined as

{\rm IFFFT}(C)=c,

(17)

where

c_{i,j}=\frac{1}{M(\text{mod }p)\cdot N(\text{mod }p)}\sum_{\theta=0}^{M-1}\sum_{\phi=0}^{N-1}C_{\theta,\phi}{(\gamma{\zeta_{1}}^{\theta})}^{-i}{(\beta{\zeta_{2}}^{\phi})}^{-j}\ \text{ for }(i,j)\in\Omega.

(18)

The following result establishes a connection between 2-D constacyclic codes in the time-domain and its corresponding FFFT images in the frequency-domain.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\gamma,\beta
be primitive
M^{\rm th}
and
N^{\rm th}
roots of
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, respectively, and let
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}
be
M^{\rm th}
and
N^{\rm th}
roots of unity, respectively, in an extended field, say,
\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be 2-D
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
-constacyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then a codeword in
\mathcal{C}
and its corresponding spectrum array over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
are related by:

\begin{split}C_{\theta,\phi}=&\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_{i,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{j}\ \text{ for }(\theta,\phi)\in\Omega,\\
c_{i,j}=&\frac{1}{M(\text{\rm mod }p)\cdot N(\text{\rm mod }p)}\sum_{\theta=0}^{M-1}\sum_{\phi=0}^{N-1}C_{\theta,\phi}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{-i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{-j}\ \text{ for }(i,j)\in\Omega.\end{split}

(19)

Proof.

From Eq. (
6
), we rewrite

\begin{split}x^{M}-\gamma^{M}=&(x-\gamma)(x^{M-1}+\gamma x^{M-2}+\cdots+\gamma^{M-2}x+\gamma^{M-1})\\
=&(x-\gamma)\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\gamma^{i}x^{M-1-i}\bigg).\end{split}

(20)

From the definition of
\zeta_{1}
,
\gamma\zeta_{1}^{r}
is a root of the above polynomial, where
r=0,1,...,M-1
. Hence,
\gamma\zeta_{1}^{r}
is a root of the last term in RHS of Eq. (
20
) for all
r\neq 0\,(\text{mod }M)
. This can also be written as

\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\gamma^{i}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{r})^{M-1-i}=(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{r})^{M-1}\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\zeta_{1}^{-ri}=0,\textnormal{ for }r\neq 0\,(\text{mod }M).

(21)

If
r=0
, then

\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{r})^{M-1}\zeta_{1}^{-ri}=\gamma^{M-1}M\,(\text{mod }p).

(22)

This is not zero if
M
is not a multiple of the field characteristic
p
.

Similarly, we have

\begin{split}y^{N}-\beta^{N}=(y-\beta)\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\beta^{i}y^{N-1-i}\bigg).\end{split}

(23)

From the definition of
\zeta_{2}
,
\beta\zeta_{2}^{r}
is a root of the above polynomial, where
r=0,1,...,N-1
. Hence,
\beta\zeta_{2}^{r}
is a root of the last term in RHS of Eq. (
23
) for all
r\neq 0\ (\text{mod }N)
. This can also be written as

\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\beta^{i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{r})^{N-1-i}=(\beta\zeta_{2}^{r})^{N-1}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\zeta_{2}^{-ri}=0,\textnormal{ for }r\neq 0\,(\text{mod }N).

(24)

If
r=0
, then

\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{r})^{N-1}\zeta_{2}^{-ri}=\beta^{N-1}N\,(\text{mod }p).

(25)

This is not zero if
N
is not a multiple of the field characteristic
p
.

Now, we have

\begin{split}\sum_{\theta=0}^{M-1}\sum_{\phi=0}^{N-1}C_{\theta,\phi}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{-i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{-j}=&\sum_{\theta=0}^{M-1}\sum_{\phi=0}^{N-1}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{-i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{-j}\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}c_{k,k^{\prime}}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{k}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{k^{\prime}}\\
=&\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}c_{k,k^{\prime}}\sum_{\theta=0}^{M-1}\sum_{\phi=0}^{N-1}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{(k-i)}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{(k^{\prime}-j)}\\
=&\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}c_{k,k^{\prime}}\gamma^{k-i}\beta^{k^{\prime}-j}\Big(\sum_{\theta=0}^{M-1}\zeta_{1}^{\theta(k-i)}\Big)\Big(\sum_{\phi=0}^{N-1}\zeta_{2}^{\phi(k^{\prime}-j)}\Big).\\
\end{split}

(26)

Using Eqs. (
21
), (
24
) in Eq. (
26
), we have

\begin{split}\sum_{\theta=0}^{M-1}\sum_{\phi=0}^{N-1}C_{\theta,\phi}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{-i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{-j}=c_{i,j}\,M\,(\text{mod}\,{p})\cdot N(\text{mod}\,{p}).\end{split}

(27)

Since
M,N
are not multiple of
p
,
M\,(\text{mod}\,{p})\neq 0,N\,(\text{mod}\,{p})\neq 0
, proving the lemma.
∎

IV-B

Properties of two-dimensional finite field Fourier transform

In this section, we derive some important properties of the 2-D finite field Fourier transform.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\textbf{a},\textbf{b}\in\mathbb{M}_{M\times N}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and let
\textbf{A}=\text{FFFT}(\textbf{a}),\textbf{B}=\text{FFFT}(\textbf{b})\in\mathbb{M}_{M\times N}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}})
.

1.

(Column
\lambda_{1}
-constacyclic shift property in the transform domain) If
b
is the column
\lambda_{1}
-constacyclic shift of
a
, i.e.,
b_{0,j}=\lambda_{1}a_{M-1,j}
and
b_{i,j}=a_{i-1,j}
for
1\leq i\leq M-1
,
0\leq j\leq N-1
, then
B_{\theta,\phi}=\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta}A_{\theta,\phi}
.

2.

(Row
\lambda_{2}
-constacyclic shift property in the transform domain) If
b
is the row
\lambda_{2}
-constacyclic shift of
a
, that is,
b_{i,0}=\lambda_{2}a_{i,N-1}
and
b_{i,j}=a_{i,j-1}
for
0\leq i\leq M-1
,
1\leq j\leq N-1
, then
B_{\theta,\phi}=\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi}A_{\theta,\phi}
.

Proof.

Let
\textbf{A}=\text{FFFT}(\textbf{a}),\textbf{B}=\text{FFFT}(\textbf{b})
. Then

\begin{split}B_{\theta,\phi}&=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}b_{i,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{j}\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}b_{0,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{0}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}b_{i,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{j}\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\lambda_{1}a_{M-1,j}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}a_{i-1,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{j}\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}a_{M-1,j}(\lambda_{1}\zeta_{1}^{\theta M})(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}a_{i-1,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{j}\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}a_{M-1,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{M}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}a_{i-1,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{j}\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}a_{i,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{i+1}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{j}\\
&=\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta}A_{\theta,\phi}.\end{split}

One can prove part (2) similarly.
∎

Theorem 6
.

(Convolutional Property)
Let
\textbf{a},\textbf{b},\textbf{c}\in\mathbb{M}_{M\times N}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
\textbf{A},\textbf{B}
, and
C
be their corresponding FFFT, respectively.

1.

(Multiplication to convolution) Then
c_{i,j}=a_{i,j}b_{i,j}
for all
(i,j)\in\Omega
if and only if

C_{\theta,\phi}=\frac{1}{M(\textnormal{ mod }p)\cdot N(\textnormal{ mod }p)}\sum_{\theta^{\prime}=0}^{M-1}\sum_{\phi^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{A}_{\theta-\theta^{\prime},\phi-\phi^{\prime}}B_{\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}}

for all
(\theta,\phi)\in\Omega
, where
\mathcal{A}_{\theta-\theta^{\prime},\phi-\phi^{\prime}}
denotes the finite field Fourier transform of cyclic codes (that is, when
\gamma=1,\beta=1
).

2.

(Convolution to multiplication) Then
C_{\theta,\phi}=A_{\theta,\phi}B_{\theta,\phi}
for all
(\theta,\phi)\in\Omega
if and only if

c_{i,j}=\sum_{i^{\prime}=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}a_{i-i^{\prime},j-j^{\prime}}b_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}

for all
(i,j)\in\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
c_{i,j}=a_{i,j}b_{i,j}
for all
(i,j)\in\Omega
. Then

\begin{split}C_{\theta,\phi}&=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_{i,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{j}\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}a_{i,j}b_{i,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{j}\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}a_{i,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{j}\cdot\frac{1}{M(\textnormal{ mod }p)\cdot N(\textnormal{ mod }p)}\sum_{\theta^{\prime}=0}^{M-1}\sum_{\phi^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}B_{\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta^{\prime}})^{-i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi^{\prime}})^{-j}\\
&=\frac{1}{M(\textnormal{ mod }p)\cdot N(\textnormal{ mod }p)}\sum_{\theta^{\prime}=0}^{M-1}\sum_{\phi^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}a_{i,j}\zeta_{1}^{i(\theta-\theta^{\prime})}\zeta_{2}^{j(\phi-\phi^{\prime})}\Big)B_{\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}}\\
&=\frac{1}{M(\textnormal{ mod }p)\cdot N(\textnormal{ mod }p)}\sum_{\theta^{\prime}=0}^{M-1}\sum_{\phi^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{A}_{\theta-\theta^{\prime},\phi-\phi^{\prime}}B_{\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}}.\end{split}

The converse follows from the uniqueness of FFFT.

Part (2) can be proved similarly.
∎

Theorem 7
.

(Conjugate Symmetry Property)
Let
\textbf{C}\in\mathbb{M}_{M\times N}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}})
and
\textbf{c}\in\mathbb{M}_{M\times N}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Then
C
is the finite field Fourier transform of
c
if and only if

C_{\theta,\phi}^{q}=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_{i,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{iq}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{jq}

(28)

for all
(\theta,\phi)\in\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
C
be the FFFT of
c
. Then by the definition of FFFT, we have

C_{\theta,\phi}=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_{i,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{j}.

(29)

We know that
(a+b)^{q}=a^{q}+b^{q}
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, and
d^{q}=d
for all
d\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. From Eq. (
29
), we have

\begin{split}C_{\theta,\phi}^{q}=&\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_{i,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{j}\Big)^{q}\\
=&\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_{i,j}^{q}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{iq}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{jq}\\
=&\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_{i,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{iq}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{jq}.\end{split}

Conversely, for all
(\theta,\phi)\in\Omega
, let

C_{\theta,\phi}^{q}=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_{i,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{iq}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{jq}.

We have

\begin{split}\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_{i,j}^{q}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{iq}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{jq}=&\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_{i,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{iq}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{jq}.\end{split}

By the uniqueness of the FFFT, we have
c_{i,j}^{q}=c_{i,j}
for all
(i,j)\in\Omega
. Thus each
c_{i,j}
is a root of
x^{q}-x=0\implies c_{i,j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
∎

IV-C

Spectral nulls of a 2-D
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
-constacyclic code

We will derive the relationship between the roots of an array in the time-domain (respectively, in frequency domain) and its corresponding array in the frequency domain (respectively, in time-domain).

Definition 10
.

For a given 2-D linear code, the locations
(\theta,\phi)
at which the frequency domain components are zero, i.e.,
C_{\theta,\phi}=0
, we refer to them as the spectral nulls.

Consider a 2-D array
c
of area
M\times N
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then its polynomial representation is

c(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_{i,j}x^{i}y^{j}.

Now, the root of this polynomial at
\left(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta},\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi}\right)

is

c(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta},\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_{i,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{j}.

From Eq. (
15
), we have

c(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta},\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})=C_{\theta,\phi}.

(30)

Thus, the frequency domain components at the coordinate
(\theta,\phi)
are values of the 2-D (or bi-variate) polynomial in the time domain at
(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta},\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})
.

Now, we have the following result.

Theorem 8
.

Let
c(x,y)
be a polynomial in the time-domain and its spectrum polynomial
{\rm FFFT}(c(x,y))=C(x,y)
. Then we have the following:

1.

The polynomial
c(x,y)
has a root at
(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta},\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})
if and only if
C_{\theta,\phi}
equals zero.

2.

The polynomial
C(x,y)
has a root at
(\zeta_{1}^{-i},\zeta_{2}^{-j})
if and only if
c_{i,j}
equals zero.

Proof.

From Eq. (
19
), we have the following connection between the coordinates of an array and their corresponding spectrum

\begin{split}C_{\theta,\phi}=&\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_{i,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{j},\\
c_{i,j}=&\frac{1}{M(\text{\rm mod }p)\cdot N(\text{\rm mod }p)}\sum_{\theta=0}^{M-1}\sum_{\phi=0}^{N-1}C_{\theta,\phi}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{-i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{-j}.\end{split}

(31)

1.

Since
c(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_{i,j}x^{i}y^{j}
, we have

\displaystyle c(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta},\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_{i,j}(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta})^{i}(\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})^{j}=C_{\theta,\phi}.

(32)

This proves the required result.

2.

Since
C(x,y)=\sum_{\theta=0}^{M-1}\sum_{\phi=0}^{N-1}C_{\theta,\phi}x^{\theta}y^{\phi}
, we have

C(\zeta_{1}^{-i},\zeta_{2}^{-j})=\sum_{\theta=0}^{M-1}\sum_{\phi=0}^{N-1}C_{\theta,\phi}(\zeta_{1}^{-i})^{\theta}(\zeta_{2}^{-j})^{\phi}=MN\cdot\gamma^{i}\beta^{j}c_{i,j},

proving the required result.

∎

For a given 2-D
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
-constacyclic code with its CZ set
V_{c}
, we have the following result. We refer the readers to
[
10
]
for more details.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a 2-D
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
-constacyclic code with its CZ set
V_{c}
. If
(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta},\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})\in V_{c}
, then the value at the location
(\theta,\phi)
of the spectrum array
C
in the frequency domain is zero, that is,
C_{\theta,\phi}=0
for each codeword
c\in\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a 2-D
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
-constacyclic code with its CZ set

Systematic encoding of constacyclic arrays can be done in the time domain based on the generator tensor derived from the parity check tensor of the 2-D constacyclic code. For more details on the encoder design, the reader is referred to Section 5 of our recent paper

By rearranging the equations of the system (

), we can always make the first
\rho

linearly independent. So for the sake of simplicity, we assume that the first
\rho
rows of the matrix

, namely,
\{A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{\rho}\}
, are linearly independent, and the last

rows of

are in
{\rm Span}(A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{\rho})
, that is,
k^{\rm th}
row of
A
can be expressed as

A_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{\rho}z_{i}A_{i},

(47)

where
\rho+1\leq k\leq s
and
z_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then by using Eqs. (
45
), (
46
) and (
47
), for the
k^{\rm th}
-row, we have

\begin{split}e(a_{k},b_{k})=&\sum_{i=1}^{\rho}z_{i}e(a_{i},b_{i}),\\
\implies r(a_{k},b_{k})=&\sum_{i=1}^{\rho}z_{i}r(a_{i},b_{i}),\end{split}

(48)

where
\rho+1\leq k\leq s
. Then

\tilde{A_{k}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\rho}z_{i}\tilde{A_{i}},

(49)

where
\rho+1\leq k\leq s
. Then from Eq. (
49
), we have
{\rm rank}(A|B)\leq\rho
, that is,
\rho\leq{\rm rank}(A|B)\leq\rho
. Hence,
{\rm rank}(A)={\rm rank}(A|B)
.

Now we will prove that
{\rm rank}(A)={\rm rank}(A|B)=t
.

We have
{\rm rank}(A)\leq t
. If possible, assume that
{\rm rank}(A)<t

14
(2), the system (

, the number of unknowns. This implies that the system (

∎

Thus, the system of equations (

) has a unique solution, leading to a unique error pattern

V-D

Decoding using finite field Fourier transforms: Method III

This subsection presents another decoding method using FFFT. In this process, we determine the codeword spectrum
C(x,y)
, which subsequently leads to the recovery of the desired codeword
c(x,y)
by taking the inverse Finite Field Fourier Transform (IFFFT). We can cleverly exploit
sparsity
due to
duality
while dealing with frequency-domain properties of the code.

Advantage of
Method III
:
When
|\Omega\setminus\Pi|<<|\Pi|
, then
Method:III
will work more efficiently over
Method: II
, as we need to deal with a non-homogeneous system of linear equations with a lesser number of unknowns.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a 2-D constacyclic code of area
M\times N
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
r
be a received array of area
M\times N
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Here
r=c+e
, where
c\in\mathcal{C}
, and
e
is an error pattern outside
\mathcal{C}
, that is, it is a detectable error. Then its image under FFFT is

\begin{split}{\rm FFFT}(r)&={\rm FFFT}(c)+{\rm FFFT}(e)\\
R&=C+E\end{split}

(50)

Note that all the arrays
R
,
C
and
E
are of the same area
M\times N
, but over an extended field.

We can write it in bi-variate polynomial form

R(x,y)=C(x,y)+E(x,y).

(51)

Using Lemma
5
, we have
C_{\theta,\phi}=0
for each pair
(\theta,\phi)
when
(\gamma\zeta_{1}^{\theta},\beta\zeta_{2}^{\phi})\in V_{c}
. We collect all such pairs
(\theta,\phi)
and construct the set
\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}
. The set
\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}
, where the spectrum
C
is nulling, is completely determined by Lemma
5
. So we have

C_{\theta,\phi}=0\quad\forall\quad(\theta,\phi)\in\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}.

(52)

Observe that
|\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}|=|\Pi|=|V_{c}|\implies|\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}^{c}|=MN-|V_{c}|=s^{\prime}
(say).

Let

\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}^{c}=\{(\theta_{k},\phi_{k})\,|\,k=0,1,\dots,s^{\prime}\}.

(53)

Now the problem of finding the spectrum polynomial
C
boils down to find the entries of
C
only at
(\theta_{k},\phi_{k})\in\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}^{c}
.

Let the spectrum polynomial over the extended field be

C(x,y)=\sum_{k=1}^{s^{\prime}}C_{\theta_{k},\phi_{k}}x^{\theta_{k}}y^{\phi_{k}}.

(54)

Let
|\mathbb{E}^{c}|=t^{\prime}
. Note that

e_{i,j}=0\quad\forall\quad(i,j)\in\mathbb{E}^{c}=\Omega\setminus\mathbb{E},

(55)

where the set
\mathbb{E}
of error locations of the error pattern
e(x,y)
is given by Eq. (
42
). Let

\mathbb{E}^{c}=\{(i_{l},j_{l})\,|\,l=1,2,\dots,t^{\prime}\}.

(56)

Consequently, for spectrum error polynomial
E(x,y)
, we have

E(\zeta_{1}^{-i_{l}},\zeta_{2}^{-j_{l}})=0\quad\forall\quad l=1,2,\dots,t^{\prime},

(57)

by Theorem
8
(2).
Here, we have
|\mathbb{E}|=t
,
|\mathbb{E}^{c}|=t^{\prime}
,
|V_{c}|=s
,
|\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}^{c}|=MN-|V_{c}|=s^{\prime}
and from Eq. (
44
)

\begin{split}&t\leq\Big\lfloor\frac{|V_{c}|}{2}\Big\rfloor\leq|V_{c}|\\
\implies&MN-|V_{c}|\leq MN-t\\
\implies&|\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}^{c}|\leq|\mathbb{E}^{c}|\\
\implies&s^{\prime}\leq t^{\prime}.\end{split}

(58)

By Eqs. (
51
), (
54
) and (
57
), we have the following non-homogeneous system of linear equations

\begin{split}R(\zeta_{1}^{-i_{l}},\zeta_{2}^{-j_{l}})&=C(\zeta_{1}^{-i_{l}},\zeta_{2}^{-j_{l}})+E(\zeta_{1}^{-i_{l}},\zeta_{2}^{-j_{l}})\\
&=C(\zeta_{1}^{-i_{l}},\zeta_{2}^{-j_{l}}),\end{split}

(59)

for all
(i_{l},j_{l})\in\mathbb{E}^{c}
, where
l=1,2,\dots,t^{\prime}
. Equivalently, we have the following non-homogeneous system of linear equations (by an abuse of notation we are using the same symbol as we use in Eq. (
46
))

AX=B,

(60)

where

A=\begin{pmatrix}{\zeta_{1}}^{-i_{1}\theta_{1}}{\zeta_{2}}^{-j_{1}\phi_{1}}&{\zeta_{1}}^{-i_{1}\theta_{2}}{\zeta_{2}}^{-j_{1}\phi_{2}}&\cdots&{\zeta_{1}}^{-i_{1}\theta_{s^{\prime}}}{\zeta_{2}}^{-j_{1}\phi_{s^{\prime}}}\\

\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
{\zeta_{1}}^{-i_{t^{\prime}}\theta_{1}}{\zeta_{2}}^{-j_{t^{\prime}}\phi_{1}}&{\zeta_{1}}^{-i_{t^{\prime}}\theta_{2}}{\zeta_{2}}^{-j_{t^{\prime}}\phi_{2}}&\cdots&{\zeta_{1}}^{-i_{t^{\prime}}\theta_{s^{\prime}}}{\zeta_{2}}^{-j_{t^{\prime}}\phi_{s^{\prime}}}\\
\end{pmatrix},

X=\begin{pmatrix}C_{\theta_{1},\phi_{1}}\\
C_{\theta_{2},\phi_{2}}\\
\vdots\\
C_{\theta_{s^{\prime}},\phi_{s^{\prime}}}\\
\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{ and }\quad B=\begin{pmatrix}R(\zeta_{1}^{-i_{1}},\zeta_{2}^{-j_{1}})\\
R(\zeta_{1}^{-i_{2}},\zeta_{2}^{-j_{2}})\\
\vdots\\

The non-homogeneous system of linear equations (

Proof.

The result directly follows from the arguments presented in the proof of Theorem

∎

VI
Conclusions

We derived the spectral domain properties of 2-D constacyclic coded arrays over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
by viewing them through the lens of common zero sets, generalizing the results for binary cyclic codes originally proposed by Imai. We also proposed efficient decoding methods for correcting random errors within the design distance of 2-D constacyclic codes using syndrome computations in both the time and frequency domains. Our proposed ideas are practically useful for next-generation 2-D bar codes and in data storage devices that work with native 2-D codes without requiring rastering of 1-D codes and other overheads.

Demo: Multi-Modal Seizure Prediction System
Ali Saeizadeh, Pietro Brach del Prever, Douglas Schonholtz, Raffaele Guida, Emrecan Demirors, Jorge M. Jimenez, Pedram Johari, Tommaso Melodia

Abstract




Abstract.
This demo presents SeizNet, an innovative system for predicting epileptic seizures benefiting from a multi-modal sensor network and utilizing
Deep Learning (DL)
techniques. Epilepsy affects approximately 65 million people worldwide, many of whom experience drug-resistant seizures. SeizNet aims at providing highly accurate alerts, allowing individuals to take preventive measures without being disturbed by false alarms. SeizNet uses a combination of data collected through either invasive (
intracranial electroencephalogram (iEEG)
) or non-invasive (
electroencephalogram (EEG)
and
electrocardiogram (ECG)
) sensors, and processed by advanced
DL
algorithms that are optimized for real-time inference at the edge, ensuring privacy and minimizing data transmission. SeizNet achieves
\geq
97% accuracy in seizure prediction while keeping the size and energy restrictions of an implantable device.

Our proposed system fundamentally comprises four interconnected wearable and implantable devices: (1) an

Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS)
whose parameters can be remotely adjusted (personalized per patient) thorough gateway.

The gateway receives and combines the
DL

Node Architecture.

Each node consists of: (1) Processing Unit (PU): Executes
DL
algorithms and processes communication messages; (2) Ultrasonic Communication Unit (UCU): Implements physical and MAC layer protocols; (3) Memory Unit (MU): Stores sensor data and
DL
model parameters (each
DL
model needs 34KB of data); (4) Energy Management Unit (EMU): Powers the node with a rechargeable battery.

We implement the
EEG
and
ECG
seizure classification algorithms on a Xilinx KV260 that provides sufficient computational and memory resources, as well as the required analog-to-digital converter to read the sensor data. The KV260 has 256K logic cells, 1.2K DSP slices, and 4GB DDR while its hardware form factor allows to embed it on a wearable electronic device being the chip only 60mm x 77mm x 27mm. Results.
We demonstrate that the SeizNet prototype passes the necessary communication & processing thresholds to perform high accuracy seizure prediction (up to 99% sensitivity, 93% specificity, and 97% accuracy) while keeping the size and energy restrictions of an implantable device.

A proof of the soliton resolution conjecture for the Benjamin–Ono equation
Louise Gassot, Patrick Gérard, Peter D. Miller

Abstract
. We give a proof of the soliton resolution conjecture for the Benjamin–Ono equation, namely every solution with sufficiently regular and decaying initial data can be written as a finite sum of soliton solutions with different velocities up to a radiative remainder term in the long–time asymptotics. We provide a detailed correspondence between the spectral theory of the Lax operator associated to the initial data and the different terms of the soliton resolution expansion. The proof is based on a new use of a representation formula of the solution due to the second author, and on a detailed analysis of the distorted Fourier transform associated to the Lax operator.

1.
Introduction and main result

This paper is devoted to the long-time behavior of solutions of the Benjamin–Ono equation,

(1.1)

\partial_{t}u-\partial_{x}|D_{x}|u+\partial_{x}(u^{2})=0\ ,\ u(0,x)=u_{0}(x).

Equation (
1.1
) was introduced in 1967
[
3
]
in order to model long, unidirectional internal gravity waves
in a two-layer fluid with infinite depth, see e.g. the book
[
24
]
and
[
26
]
for a recent derivation. Here
u=u(t,x)
is a real–valued function and
|D|
denotes the Fourier multiplier associated to the symbol
|\xi|
acting on functions on the real line. Alternatively,
|D|=H\partial_{x}
, where
H
denotes the Hilbert transform, normalized as

(1.2)

Hf(x):=\frac{1}{\pi}\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{f(y)}{x-y}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Global wellposedness theory of the initial–value problem (
1.1
) in Sobolev spaces was the object of intensive research from
[
27
]
to
[
21
]
, where it is shown that (
1.1
) is solved by a continuous flow map on every Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s>-1/2
, so that, if
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
u(t)
is bounded in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
as
t\to\infty
. We refer to
[
21
]
for more references on this topic. In this paper, we will be only interested in the case
s\geq 0
, see also
[
25
]
.

A very natural question is the description of the long–time dynamics of such solutions
u
. In order to be able to answer this question,
one must have in mind a class of special solutions of (
1.1
), which were the focus of Benjamin’s original paper, namely solutions of the form

(1.3)

u(t,x)=R_{p}(x-c_{p}t),\ R_{p}(y):=\frac{2\mathrm{Im}p}{|y+p|^{2}},\ c_{p}:=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Im}p},

where
p\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
is a complex number with a positive imaginary part. A theorem by Amick and Toland
[
1
]
establishes that all solutions of (
1.1
) of the form
u_{0}(x-ct)
are given by (
1.3
). Such solutions are called
soliton solutions
of the Benjamin–Ono equation and are expected to be the building blocks of the whole dynamics. More precisely, the soliton resolution conjecture for this equation — see e.g.
[
16
]
— roughly claims that every solution to (
1.1
) behaves as
t\to\pm\infty
like a sum of such soliton solutions and a radiation term, namely a solution of the linearized equation of (
1.1
) at
u=0
:

(1.4)

\partial_{t}w=\partial_{x}|D|w,

which we shall denote by
w(t):=\mathrm{e}^{t\partial_{x}|D|}w(0).

The purpose of this paper is to prove this conjecture for a large class of initial data
u_{0}
, including finite linear combinations of soliton solutions
R_{p}
and, say, Schwartz functions. Our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
be a real–valued function such that
xu_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Assume moreover that there exist
c_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
v_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that

(1.5)

\forall x\in\mathbb{R},\ x^{2}u_{0}(x)=c_{0}+v_{0}(x).

Then there exist a nonnegative integer
N
, elements
p_{1},\dots,p_{N}
of
\mathbb{C}_{+}
, with
\mathrm{Im}(p_{1})<\dots<\mathrm{Im}p_{N}
, and real–valued functions
u_{\infty}^{\pm}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that

(1.6)

u(t,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{N}R_{p_{j}}(x-c_{p_{j}}t)-\mathrm{e}^{t\partial_{x}|D|}u_{\infty}^{\pm}\longrightarrow 0

in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
as
t\to\pm\infty
.

Notice that the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
on
u_{0}
are satisfied by any finite linear combination of solitons, and more generally by any real–valued rational function
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that
xu_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and also, of course, by any function in the Schwartz class. In the special case of a multi–soliton solution, namely if
u_{0}
is a finite sum of functions
R_{p}
, Theorem
1.1
was already known, see
[
20
]
,
[
29
]
, with no radiation term,
u_{\infty}^{\pm}=0
. On the other hand, Theorem
1.1
was more recently established in
[
4
]
, in the special case
u_{0}=-R_{p}
, where
N=0
, so that the solution is purely radiative as
t\pm\infty
. A more precise formulation of Theorem
1.1
is given in Section
2
and the strategy of proof is presented in Section
3
.

Before coming to Section
2
, let us mention related works dealing with soliton resolution for other Hamiltonian partial differential equations.
The soliton resolution property seems to have been first observed in the case of the Korteweg–de Vries equation in
[
12
]
, where the integrable structure is used through inverse scattering transform and the Gelfand–Levitan–Marchenko integral equation. Later, these asymptotics were revisited in
[
9
]
, using the Riemann–Hilbert formulation of the inverse scattering transform and the associated Deift–Zhou nonlinear steepest descent method. This approach was also used in
[
8
]
to study the asymptotic stability of multi–soliton solutions to the defocusing cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation with non–zero boundary conditions at infinity, in
[
5
]
to study the long time asymptotics of the focusing cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation, and in
[
6
]
for the focusing modified Korteweg–de Vries equation. Finally, the case of derivative nonlinear Schrödinger equation was studied in

[
19
]
using again inverse scattering tools. Notice that the Benjamin–Ono equation (
1.1
) is also an integrable equation. However, the proof of Theorem
1.1
is very different from the above references, since inverse spectral theory is replaced by an explicit representation formula discovered by the second author in
[
14
]
, see Theorem
3.1
below. Moreover, the upshot is also very different, since in Theorem
1.1
the parameters
p_{j}
are the same as
t\to+\infty
and
t\to-\infty
, while this is not the case in the above references, where some non–trivial scattering map relates the soliton parameters as
t\to\pm\infty
. The difference also holds for the radiation term, since it is expressed as a free scattering term in Theorem
1.1
, while it may be expressed as a modified scattering term in the above references.

The soliton resolution property was recently established for some non–integrable Hamiltonian PDE, such as the energy critical focusing nonlinear wave equation with radial symmetry
[
11
]
,
[
17
]
, or the energy–critical wave map with equivariant symmetry
[
10
]
,
[
18
]
. Let us stress several differences of these two cases with the integrable ones. Firstly, the soliton solutions are no longer obtained by translation, but by dilation of the space variable. Secondly, global existence does not always hold, hence the asymptotics hold either as
t\to\pm\infty
or as
t
tends to the blow–up time. Thirdly, the methods of proof are very different, using modulation and energy criticality, as well as energy channels for the wave equation. Such a modulation approach seems hopeless for the Benjamin–Ono equation, which is
H^{-1/2}
–critical, with no conservation law at this regularity level.

Let us conclude by mentioning an equation which may serve as a bridge between the above two sets of equations, namely the Calogero–Moser derivative nonlinear Schrödinger equation
[
13
]
,
[
22
]
, which is mass critical and admits an integrable structure allowing an explicit formula very close to the Benjamin–Ono one. For this equation, a weak soliton resolution property was proved in
[
23
]
in the focusing case by means of modulation methods, while for the defocusing case scattering theory was very recently established in
[
7
]
using the integrable approach.

2.
A more precise formulation of the main result

2.1.
The Hardy space and the Lax operator

Let us now introduce the spectral data that will be relevant parameters in the long-time asymptotics stated in Theorem
1.1
. We will use the integrable structure for the Benjamin–Ono equation, for which we refer e.g. to
[
15
]
.
We denote by
L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
the set of functions
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
whose Fourier transform
\widehat{f}
is supported in the half-line
[0,+\infty)
. An element
f
in the Hardy space
L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
extends as a holomorphic function on the upper half-plane
\mathbb{C}_{+}
satisfying

\sup_{y>0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x+\mathrm{i}y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x<+\infty,

in which case
f
can be identified with the limit in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
of
f(\cdot+\mathrm{i}y)
as
y\to 0^{+}
.
We denote by
\Pi
the orthogonal projector from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
onto
L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
. The Toeplitz operator
T_{b}
associated to
b\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is then given by the formula

\forall f\in L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R}),\quad T_{b}f:=\Pi(bf).

The Lax operator for the Benjamin-Ono equation is given by
L_{u_{0}}=D-T_{u_{0}}
, where
D:=-\mathrm{i}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}x}
.

Finally, we define the operator
X^{*}
by its domain

D(X^{*})=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\colon\widehat{f}|_{(0,\infty)}\in H^{1}(0,\infty)\},

and the formula

\widehat{X^{*}f}(\xi):=\mathrm{i}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}\widehat{f}(\xi),\quad\xi>0.

Applying the inverse Fourier transform, we find

X^{*}f(x)=xf(x)+\frac{I_{+}(f)}{2\mathrm{i}\pi},

where
I_{+}(f):=\widehat{f}(0^{+})
. The operator
X^{*}
can also be seen as the adjoint on
L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
of multiplication by
x
, see
[
15
]
.

Next we define soliton parameters. We denote by
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}
the — simple and negative — eigenvalues of the Lax operator
L_{u_{0}}=D-T_{u_{0}}
on the Hardy space
L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
, and by
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{N}
the corresponding eigenfunctions, normalized in
L^{2}
. The finiteness of
N
has been established in
[
30
, Theorem 1.2]
. For every
j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, we set

(2.1)

p_{j}=-\langle X^{*}\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j}\rangle.

Recall that, according to Wu’s identities
[
30
, Lemma 2.5 and (2.23)]
— see also
[
2
]
if
u
is less regular—,

(2.2)

|\langle\varphi_{j},\Pi u_{0}\rangle|^{2}=-2\pi\lambda_{j}\ ,\ \lambda_{j}I_{+}(\varphi_{j})=-\langle\varphi_{j},\Pi u_{0}\rangle,

and consequently, expressing
\langle X^{*}\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j}\rangle
on the Fourier side and integrating by parts, we find

(2.3)

\mathrm{Im}\,p_{j}=\frac{|I_{+}(\varphi_{j})|^{2}}{4\pi}=\frac{1}{2|\lambda_{j}|}>0.

Now we define generalized eigenfunctions and radiation profiles.
For every
\lambda>0
, we denote by
m_{\pm}=m_{\pm}(x,\lambda)
the unique solution in the space
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
of the equation

(2.4)

(L_{u_{0}}-\lambda)m_{\pm}=0\ ,\ \lim_{x\to\pm\infty}{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x}m_{\pm}(x,\lambda)}=1.

For every
f\in L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
, we denote by
\widetilde{f}^{\pm}
the distorted Fourier transform of
f
defined by

(2.5)

\widetilde{f}^{\pm}(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{m_{\pm}(x,\lambda)}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Finally, we define real–valued functions
u_{\infty}^{\pm}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
by

(2.6)

\forall\lambda>0\ ,\ \widehat{u}_{\infty}^{\pm}(\lambda)=\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}^{\mp}(\lambda).

We recall that the existence and uniqueness of
m_{-}
was proven in
[
4
, Theorem 26]
; the analogous result for
m_{+}
can be proved similarly. Moreover, the following distorted Plancherel identity was proven in
[
4
, Theorem 26]
:

(2.7)

\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\langle f,\varphi_{j}\rangle|^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}|\widetilde{f}^{\pm}(\lambda)|^{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}{2\pi}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

2.2.
The main result in more detail

The following result relates the parameters of Theorem
1.1
to the spectral quantities introduced above.

Theorem 2.1

(Soliton resolution and spectral data)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, the function
r^{\pm}
defined by

r^{\pm}(t,x):=u(t,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{N}R_{p_{j}}(x-c_{p_{j}}t)-\mathrm{e}^{t\partial_{x}|D_{x}|}u_{\infty}^{\pm}(x)

satisfies

(2.8)

\|r^{\pm}(t,\diamond)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|r^{\pm}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}r^{\pm}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\rightarrow\pm\infty}}}}0.

Corollary 2.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
,

•

Purely radiative asymptotics —
N=0
— holds if and only if the operator
L_{u_{0}}
is positive. This is in particular the case if
u_{0}\leq 0
.

•

Given a positive integer
N
, a
N
–soliton asymptotics holds for
t\to+\infty
—
u_{\infty}^{+}=0
— or for
t\to-\infty
—
u_{\infty}^{-}=0
— if and only if
u_{0}
is a sum of
N
soliton profiles, namely there exist
\tilde{p}_{1},\dots,\tilde{p}_{N}\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
such that

u_{0}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}R_{\tilde{p}_{j}}(x).

Let us briefly indicate how to deduce Corollary
2.2
from Theorem
2.1
. The first statement is a consequence of the already–quoted results of
[
30
]
, namely that, if
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\cap L^{2}
and
xu_{0}\in L^{2}
,
L_{u_{0}}
has no eigenvalue if and only if it is a positive operator. The particular case
u_{0}\leq 0
is then obvious.
As for the second statement, we observe that the distorted Plancherel formula (
2.7
) applied to
f=\Pi u_{0}
implies that
u_{\infty}^{\pm}=0
if and only if
\Pi u_{0}
is a linear combination of eigenfunctions of
L_{u_{0}}
. Then the conclusion is a direct consequence of Theorem 4.8 of
[
29
]
, where Theorem
2.1
is also proved in the particular case of multi–solitons.

Remark 2.3
.

(1)

Notice that, with the notation of
[
4
]
, see in particular Remark 15, we have
\hat{u}_{\infty}^{+}(\lambda)=\alpha(\lambda)
and
\hat{u}_{\infty}^{-}(\lambda)=-\mathrm{i}\beta(\lambda)
. In view of
[
4
, Proposition 30]
, we observe that there exists a function
\ell(\lambda)=\ell_{+}(m_{-},\lambda)
of modulus
1
such that

(2.9)

\forall\lambda>0,\quad\hat{u}_{\infty}^{+}(\lambda)=\ell(\lambda)\hat{u}_{\infty}^{-}(\lambda),

so that the scattering operator is a unitary Fourier multiplier.

(2)

A careful inspection of our proof — see in particular Remarks
3.3
and
6.3
below— suggests that the strong convergence of
r^{\pm}(t,.)
to
0
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, as well as the conclusions of Corollary
2.2
, are guaranteed by relaxed assumptions on
u_{0}
, say

u_{0},xu_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for some
s>0
, and (
1.5
). In particular, the soliton resolution holds in
L^{2}
if
u_{0}(x)=c{\bf 1}_{(-1,1)}(x)
for every
c\in\mathbb{R}
, with pure radiative asymptotics if and only if
c\leq 0
.

3.
Strategy of proof

In this section, we sketch the proof of Theorem
2.1
. We shall make use of the explicit formula for
u
from
[
14
]
.

Theorem 3.1

(Explicit formula
[
14
]
)
.

The solution
u\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R},H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
of the Benjamin-Ono equation (
1.1
) with initial datum
u(0)=u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is given by
u(t,x)=\Pi u(t,x)+\overline{\Pi u(t,x)}
, with

(3.1)

\forall t\in\mathbb{R},\ \forall z\in\mathbb{C}_{+},\quad\Pi u(t,z)=\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}\pi}I_{+}((X^{*}-2tL_{u_{0}}-z)^{-1}\Pi u_{0}).

3.1.
Reduction to the case
t\to+\infty

We observe that, if
u
is the solution of (
1.1
), and if
u^{*}(t,x):=u(-t,-x)
, then
u^{*}
still satisfies the Benjamin–Ono equation with initial datum
u^{*}_{0}(x):=u_{0}(-x)
. Notice that, since
u_{0}
is real valued,
\hat{u}^{*}_{0}(\xi)=\overline{\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)}
for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
. As a consequence, if
\varphi_{j}
is an eigenfunction of
L_{u_{0}}
associated to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}
, the function
\varphi_{j}^{*}\in H^{1}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
defined by

\widehat{\varphi_{j}^{*}}(\xi)=\overline{\varphi_{j}(\xi)}

is an eigenfunction of
L_{u_{0}^{*}}
with the same eigenvalue, and

\langle X^{*}\varphi_{j}^{*},\varphi_{j}^{*}\rangle=-\overline{\langle X^{*}\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j}\rangle}.

Similarly, if the generalized eigenfunctions
m_{\pm}
are associated to
u_{0}
by Definition
2.4
, then the generalized eigenfunctions
m^{*}_{\pm}
associated to
u_{0}^{*}
are given by

m_{\pm}^{*}(x,\lambda)=\overline{m_{\mp}(-x,\lambda)}.

Consequently, the scattering profiles
u_{\infty}^{*,\pm}
associated to
u_{0}^{*}
by Definition
2.4
are given by

\forall\lambda>0,\quad\hat{u}_{\infty}^{*,\pm}(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Pi u_{0}^{*}(x)\overline{m_{\mp}^{*}(x,\lambda)}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{\Pi u_{0}(-x)}m_{\pm}(-x,\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}x=\overline{\hat{u}_{\infty}^{\mp}(\lambda)},

or equivalently
u_{\infty}^{*,\pm}(x)=u_{\infty}^{\mp}(-x)
. Hence the statement of Theorem
2.1
for
t\to+\infty
and
u^{*}
is exactly the statement of Theorem
2.1
for
t\to-\infty
and
u
.

Therefore we may restrict ourselves to the case
t\to+\infty
, which we shall do from now on, and we will simplify the notation by setting

\tilde{f}(\lambda):=\tilde{f}^{-}(\lambda),\ u_{\infty}:=u_{\infty}^{+},\ r:=r^{\pm}.

3.2.
Sufficient conditions based on weak convergence

By a functional–analytic argument using the distorted Plancherel formula (
2.7
), we are reduced to establishing the following two limits.

Lemma 3.2

(Sufficient conditions for Theorem
2.1
)
.

Theorem
2.1
holds if the following two properties are satisfied.

•

Soliton limit. For every
j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, for every
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
,

(3.2)

\Pi u(t,z-2t\lambda_{j})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}}}}\frac{\mathrm{i}}{z-\langle X^{*}\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j}\rangle}.

•

Radiation limit. For every
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
with
|z|
small enough,

(3.3)

(2t)^{1/2}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\lambda^{2}}\Pi u(t,z-2t\lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\rightharpoonup}\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}}}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\pi/4}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda z}\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}({\lambda}),

in the sense of weak convergence in
L^{2}(0,+\infty)
in the
\lambda
variable.

Proof.

First step. Reformulation of the radiation limit.
Assume the radiation limit (
3.3
) holds for
\mathrm{Im}z
small enough. Then we claim that it also holds for
z=0
. Indeed, notice that, since
\Pi u(t,.)
is bounded in
L^{2}
, an elementary change of variable implies that, for every
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
,

(3.4)

F_{t,z}(\lambda):=(2t)^{1/2}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\lambda^{2}}\Pi u(t,z-2t\lambda)

is bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, since
\partial_{z}\Pi u(z)=\Pi(\partial_{x}u)(z)
, the
H^{1}
conservation law implies that
\partial_{z}F_{t,z}(\lambda)
is also bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Assume that (
3.3
) holds for
0<\mathrm{Im}z\leq\delta
, fix
\varphi
a test function on
(0,\infty)
, and set, for
y\in(0,\delta]
,

(3.5)

g_{t}(y):=\int_{0}^{\infty}F_{t,\mathrm{i}y}(\lambda)\varphi(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,\ g_{\infty}(y):=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\pi/4}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda y}\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}({\lambda})\varphi(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Then we have
g_{t}(y)\to g_{\infty}(y)
as
t\to+\infty
for every
y\in(0,\delta]
, and
g^{\prime}_{t}(y)
is uniformly bounded on
(0,\delta]
as
t\to\infty
. Consequently,
the convergence of
g_{t}
to
g_{\infty}
is uniform, thus
g_{t}(0^{+})\to g_{\infty}(0^{+})
as
t\to+\infty
, which, since
\varphi
is arbitrary, exactly means (
3.3
) for
z=0
, namely

(3.6)

(2t)^{1/2}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\lambda^{2}}\Pi u(t,-2t\lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\rightharpoonup}\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}}}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\pi/4}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}({\lambda}),

in the sense of weak convergence in
L^{2}(0,+\infty)
in the
\lambda
variable. At this stage we can reformulate (
3.6
) as

(3.7)

\mathrm{e}^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\Pi u(t,\diamond)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\rightharpoonup}\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}}}}\Pi u_{\infty}.

Indeed, it is well known that, for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\partial_{x}^{2}}f=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{x^{2}}{4t}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\pi/4}}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}\hat{f}\left(\frac{-x}{2t}\right)+o(1),

as
t\to+\infty
, in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, so that, for every
f\in L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\partial_{x}^{2}}\Pi u(t,\diamond),f\rangle

\displaystyle=(2t)^{1/2}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\pi/4}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Pi u(t,-2t\lambda)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\lambda^{2}}\overline{\hat{f}(\lambda)}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda+o(1)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}}}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\hat{u}_{\infty}(\lambda)\overline{\hat{f}(\lambda)}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda=\langle\Pi u_{\infty},f\rangle,

in view of (
3.6
).

Remark 3.3
.

Notice that the above step can be made under a weaker assumptions on
u_{0}
, say
u_{0}\in H^{s}
for some
s>0
. Indeed, in that case the conservation laws imply that
\Pi u(t,\diamond)
is uniformly bounded in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, so that, if
0<y<y^{\prime}\leq\delta
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|F_{t,\mathrm{i}y}(\lambda)-F_{t,\mathrm{i}y^{\prime}}(\lambda)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}(\mathrm{e}^{-y\xi}-\mathrm{e}^{-y^{\prime}\xi})^{2}|\hat{u}(t,\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\lesssim(y^{\prime}-y)^{2s}.

Hence the family
g_{t}
defined in (
3.5
) is uniformly Hölder continuous, and
g_{t}(0^{+})\to g_{\infty}(0^{+}),
whence (
3.6
).

Second step. Strong convergence in
L^{2}
.
Next, if
r=r^{+}
is the function defined in Theorem
2.1

In this section, we prove property (

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f\in L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, then the function
\Omega_{t}f
defined by

\forall z\in\mathbb{C}_{+},\quad\Omega_{t}f(z):=\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}\pi}I_{+}((X^{*}-2tL_{u_{0}}-z)^{-1}f)

belongs to
L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
, and
\|\Omega_{t}f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}}
. In particular, for every
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
, we have

|\Omega_{t}f(z)|\leq\frac{\|f\|_{L^{2}}}{2\sqrt{\pi\mathrm{Im}z}}.

Proof.

If
u_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, the proof of the explicit formula in
[
14
]
gives

\Omega_{t}=U(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tL_{u_{0}}^{2}},

where
U(t)
is the — unitary— solution of the following linear ODE in the space of bounded operators on
L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
,

U^{\prime}(t)=B_{u(t)}U(t),\quad U(0)=\mathrm{Id},

and
B_{u}=\mathrm{i}(T_{|D|u}-T_{u}^{2})
is the second part of the Benjamin–Ono Lax pair. The general case follows by mollifying
u_{0}
. As for the last inequality, it follows from the inverse Fourier transform applied to
g=\Omega_{t}f
,

\forall z\in\mathbb{C}_{+},\quad g(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}z\xi}\widehat{g}(\xi)\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{2\pi},

and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality combined with the Plancherel identity complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and
f\in L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
be such that
f\perp\varphi_{j}
. Then, with the notation of Lemma
4.1
, for every
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
,

\Omega_{t}f(z-2\lambda_{j}t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}}}}0.

Proof.

By functional calculus for the selfadjoint operator
L_{u_{0}}
, there exists
h\in L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
such that

(L_{u_{0}}-\lambda_{j})h=f.

First assume that
f\in D(X^{*})
, and let us prove that
h\in D(X^{*})
. Using the — standard — Fourier transform, we have

\forall\xi\in]0,+\infty[,\quad(\xi-\lambda_{j})\widehat{h}(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\widehat{u}_{0}*\widehat{h}(\xi).

Since
f\in D(X^{*})
,
\widehat{f}\in H^{1}(0,+\infty)
. Moreover, since
xu_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
\widehat{u}_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, hence
\widehat{u}_{0}*\widehat{h}
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}
and its derivative is bounded on
\mathbb{R}
. Consequently,

\widehat{h}(\xi)=\frac{\widehat{f}(\xi)}{\xi-\lambda_{j}}+\frac{\widehat{u}_{0}*\widehat{h}(\xi)}{2\pi(\xi-\lambda_{j})}

belongs to
H^{1}(0,\infty)
, namely
h\in D(X^{*})
. Let us complete the proof. We have

\displaystyle(X^{*}-2t(L_{u_{0}}-\lambda_{j})-z)^{-1}f

\displaystyle=(X^{*}-2t(L_{u_{0}}-\lambda_{j})-z)^{-1}(L_{u_{0}}-\lambda_{j})h

\displaystyle=-\frac{h}{2t}+\frac{1}{2t}(X^{*}-2t(L_{u_{0}}-\lambda_{j})-z)^{-1}(X^{*}h-zh).

Consequently, using Lemma
4.1
,

\Omega_{t}f(z-2t\lambda_{j})=-\frac{I_{+}(h)}{4\mathrm{i}\pi t}+\frac{\Omega_{t}(X^{*}h-zh)(z-2t\lambda_{j})}{2t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}}}}0.

Finally, for a general
f\in\varphi_{j}^{\perp}
, the result follows from the uniform bound on
\Omega_{t}f(z)
provided by Lemma
4.1
, and from the elementary fact that, since
D(X^{*})
is dense in
L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
,
D(X^{*})\cap\varphi_{j}^{\perp}
is dense in
\varphi_{j}^{\perp}
.
∎

We also need to know a little more about the eigenfunction
\varphi_{j}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

We have
\varphi_{j}\in D(X^{*})
.

Proof.

Using again the Fourier transform, we have, for every
\xi>0
,

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}_{j}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\widehat{u}_{0}*\widehat{\varphi}_{j}(\xi)}{2\pi(\xi-\lambda_{j})},

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}_{j}^{\prime}(\xi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\widehat{u}_{0}*\widehat{\varphi}_{j}(\xi)}{2\pi(\xi-\lambda_{j})^{2}}+\frac{(\widehat{u}_{0})^{\prime}*\widehat{\varphi}_{j}(\xi)}{2\pi(\xi-\lambda_{j})},

and the right hand side in each line clearly belongs to
L^{2}(0,+\infty)
.
∎

Let us now prove the soliton limit (
3.2
).

Proof of the soliton limit (
3.2
).

First we write

\Pi u_{0}=\langle\Pi u_{0},\varphi_{j}\rangle\varphi_{j}+f_{j},

where
f_{j}\in\varphi_{j}^{\perp}
. From Lemma
4.2
, we infer, for every
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
,
\Omega_{t}f_{j}(z-2t\lambda_{j})\to 0
as
t\to\infty
. Next we set

\psi_{j}:=\frac{\varphi_{j}}{\langle X^{*}\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j}\rangle-z}

and we observe that

(X^{*}-2t(L_{u_{0}}-\lambda_{j})-z)\psi_{j}=\varphi_{j}+g_{j}\ ,\quad g_{j}:=\frac{X^{*}\varphi_{j}-\langle X^{*}\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j}\rangle\varphi_{j}}{\langle X^{*}\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j}\rangle-z}.

Consequently,

(X^{*}-2t(L_{u_{0}}-\lambda_{j})-z)^{-1}\varphi_{j}=\psi_{j}-(X^{*}-2t(L_{u_{0}}-\lambda_{j})-z)^{-1}g_{j},

and

\Omega_{t}(\varphi_{j})(z-2t\lambda_{j})=\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}\pi}I_{+}(\psi_{j})-\Omega_{t}(g_{j})(z-2t\lambda_{j}).

Since
g_{j}\in\varphi_{j}^{\perp}
, we again infer from Lemma
4.2
that

\Omega_{t}(\varphi_{j})(z-2t\lambda_{j})\to\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}\pi}I_{+}(\psi_{j})
as
t\to\infty
.
Finally, we get

\Omega_{t}(\Pi u_{0})(z-2t\lambda_{j})\to\frac{\langle\Pi u_{0},\varphi_{j}\rangle}{2\mathrm{i}\pi}I_{+}(\psi_{j})=\frac{\langle\Pi u_{0},\varphi_{j}\rangle I_{+}(\varphi_{j})}{2\mathrm{i}\pi(\langle X^{*}\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j}\rangle-z)}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{z-\langle X^{*}\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j}\rangle},

where the last equality is obtained thanks to Wu’s identities (
2.2
). This is exactly the soliton limit (
3.2
).
∎

Making the distorted Fourier transform appear

Coming back to the explicit formula and to (
5.5
), we must study the limit as
t\to\infty
of

(5.8)

(2t)^{1/2}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\lambda^{2}}\Pi u(t,z-2t\lambda)=\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}\pi}h_{t,z,\lambda}(0^{+})=(2t)^{1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\eta-\lambda)^{2}+\mathrm{i}z\eta}\widehat{q}_{t,z,\lambda}(\eta)\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\eta}{2\pi}.

We first localize near the critical point
\lambda
the domain of the oscillatory integral in the right–hand side, by using (
5.1
).

Now, using the following equation defining the generalized eigenfunction
m_{-}
of
L_{u_{0}}
,

(5.9)

\forall\eta>0,\quad m_{-}(x,\eta)=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\eta x}+\mathrm{i}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\eta(x-y)}T_{u_{0}}m_{-}(y,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}y,

we get

\widehat{q}_{t,z,\lambda}\left(\eta\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}q_{t,z,\lambda}(x)\overline{m_{-}}\left(x,\eta\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\mathrm{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}q_{t,z,\lambda}(x)\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\eta(x-y)}\overline{T_{u_{0}}m_{-}}\left(y,\eta\right)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

Using the equation (
5.7
) satisfied by
q_{t,z,\lambda}
, we finally obtain

(5.10)

\widehat{q}_{t,z,\lambda}\left(\eta\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Pi u_{0}(x)\overline{m_{-}}\left(x,\eta\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
-\mathrm{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}q_{t,z,\lambda}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{T_{u_{0}}m_{-}}\left(x-\sigma,\eta\right)\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda\sigma-\mathrm{i}\sigma(2z+\sigma-2x)/(4t)}-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\eta\sigma}\right]\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x.

Notice that, in the first integral of the right–hand side,
\Pi
can be dropped since
m_{-}\in L^{\infty}_{+}
.
In the second integral of the right–hand side, we make the change of variable
x-\sigma=y
and
\xi=\lambda+\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}}.

Hence the decomposition (
5.10
) transforms (
5.8
) into

\displaystyle\sqrt{2t}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\lambda^{2}}\Pi u(t,z-2t\lambda)=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\xi-\lambda)^{2}+\mathrm{i}z\xi}\widehat{q}_{t,z,\lambda}(\xi)(1-\chi(\xi))\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{2\pi}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{2t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\xi-\lambda)^{2}+\mathrm{i}z\xi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi(\xi)u_{0}(x)\overline{m_{-}}(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}x\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{2\pi}

\displaystyle+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda z}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha^{2}/2+\mathrm{i}\frac{z\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi\left(\lambda+\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}}\right)q_{t,z,\lambda}(x)\overline{r}_{t,z,\lambda}(x,\alpha)\,\mathrm{d}x\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\alpha}{2\pi},

where
r_{t,z,\lambda}(x,\alpha)
satisfies (
5.4
).
We are therefore reduced to proving (
5.1
), (
5.2
) and (
5.3
). This completes the proof of Proposition
5.1
.

6.
A priori estimates

In this section, we derive a priori estimates on
f_{t,z,\lambda}=(X^{*}-2t(L_{u_{0}}-\lambda)-z)^{-1}\Pi u_{0}
and
q_{t,z,\lambda}=\Pi u_{0}-2tT_{u_{0}}f_{t,z,\lambda}
. The Fourier transform of
f_{t,z,\lambda}
solves

(6.1)

\forall\xi>0,\quad\left(\mathrm{i}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}-2t\xi+2t\lambda-z\right)\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}(\xi)+\frac{t}{\pi}\widehat{u_{0}}\ast\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}(\xi)=\widehat{u}_{0}(\xi).

6.1.
L^{2}
bounds

Lemma 6.1
.

There is
C>0
such that when
\operatorname{Im}z+|\operatorname{Re}(z)|/t
is small enough, then

\sup_{t\geq 1}t\|f_{t,z,\lambda}\|_{L^{2}_{x,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}\times\Lambda)}^{2}\leq C.

Proof.

We integrate identity (
6.1
) against
\overline{(\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda})^{\prime}}
and take the real part. We find that

\operatorname{Re}\langle\mathrm{i}(\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda})^{\prime},{(\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda})^{\prime}}\rangle=0.

Then, by integration by parts,

\operatorname{Re}\langle-2t\xi\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda},{(\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda})^{\prime}}\rangle=-t\left[\xi|\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}|^{2}\right]_{0}^{+\infty}+t\|\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Since
\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}\in H^{1}(0,+\infty)
, it is continuous at
\xi=0^{+}
. Moreover, since
\xi\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}
and
(\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda})^{\prime}
belong to
L^{2}(0,+\infty)
, using that
\xi|\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}|^{2}=-\operatorname{Re}\int_{\xi}^{\infty}(2\eta\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}(\eta)\overline{(\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda})^{\prime}(\eta)}+|\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}(\eta)|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}\eta
, we deduce that the term under brackets also vanishes at
\xi=+\infty
:

\operatorname{Re}\langle-2t\xi\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda},{(\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda})^{\prime}}\rangle=t\|\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Next, we have by direct integration that

\operatorname{Re}\langle(2t\lambda-\operatorname{Re}(z))\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda},{(\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda})^{\prime}}\rangle=-(t\lambda-\operatorname{Re}(z)/2)|I_{+}(f_{t,z,\lambda})|^{2}.

We note that thanks to the explicit formula,

|I_{+}(f_{t,z,\lambda})|=2\pi|\Pi u(t,z-2t\lambda)|,

hence the conservation of the
L^{2}
norm for the Benjamin-Ono equation implies that

(6.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|I_{+}(f_{t,z,\lambda})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda=(2\pi)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|\Pi u(t,z-2t\lambda)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\leq\frac{C}{t}.

We now focus on

2t\operatorname{Re}\langle\widehat{u_{0}}\ast\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda},{(\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda})^{\prime}}\rangle=\left[t(\widehat{u_{0}}\ast\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda})\overline{\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}}\right]_{0}^{+\infty},

where we used integration by parts and the fact that
u_{0}
is real-valued. Hence we get the upper bound

\displaystyle\left|2t\operatorname{Re}\langle\widehat{u_{0}}\ast\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda},{(\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda})^{\prime}}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq Ct|\langle u_{0},f_{t,z,\lambda}\rangle||I_{+}(f_{t,z,\lambda})|

\displaystyle\leq t(\epsilon|\langle u_{0},f_{t,z,\lambda}\rangle|^{2}+C_{\epsilon}|I_{+}(f_{t,z,\lambda})|^{2}).

To conclude, integrating (
6.1
) against
\overline{(\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda})^{\prime}}
and taking the real part, we have shown that for large
t
, if
\operatorname{Re}(z)/t
stays bounded,

(6.3)

\int_{\Lambda}\left|t\|\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\operatorname{Im}(z)\operatorname{Im}\langle\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda},{(\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda})^{\prime}}\rangle\right|\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\leq C_{\epsilon,\Lambda}+t\epsilon\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\int_{\Lambda}\|f_{t,z,\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

If
\operatorname{Im}(z)=0
, we can absorb the second term in the right-hand side by the left-hand side.

In the general case, we need to deal with the term
\operatorname{Im}(z)\operatorname{Im}\langle\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda},{(\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda})^{\prime}}\rangle
separately.
For this we integrate (
6.1
) against
\overline{\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}}
and take the imaginary part: we find

\operatorname{Im}\langle\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda},{(\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda})^{\prime}}\rangle-\operatorname{Im}(z)\|\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{t}{\pi}\operatorname{Im}\langle\widehat{u_{0}}\ast\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda},\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}\rangle=\operatorname{Im}\langle\widehat{u_{0}},\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}\rangle.

We note that

t|\langle\widehat{u_{0}}\ast\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda},\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}\rangle|\leq Ct\|\widehat{u_{0}}\|_{L^{1}}\|\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
\widehat{u_{0}}\in L^{1}
if
u_{0}
since
u_{0}\in H^{1}
. Hence when
|\operatorname{Im}(z)|\leq\epsilon
we find

|\operatorname{Im}(z)\operatorname{Im}\langle\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda},(\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda})^{\prime}\rangle|\leq C\epsilon(t\|\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+1),

so we can still absorb this term in (
6.3
) to find an upper bound on
t\|\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda,\xi}(\Lambda\times(0,\infty))}^{2}
.
∎

From the
L^{2}
bound on
f_{t,z,\lambda}
we deduce a
L^{2}
bound on
X^{*}q_{t,z,\lambda}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

We have

(6.4)

\|X^{*}q_{t,z,\lambda}\|_{L^{2}_{x,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}\times\Lambda)}\leq C\sqrt{t}.

Proof.

Combining Lemma
6.1
with (
5.6
) leads to

(6.5)

\|q_{t,z,\lambda}\|_{L^{2}_{x,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}\times\Lambda)}\leq C\sqrt{t}.

Furthermore, since
X^{*}T_{u_{0}}f=T_{xu_{0}}f
and
xu_{0}\in H^{1}\subset L^{\infty}
, Lemma
6.1
and (
5.6
) lead to the upper bound on
\|X^{*}q_{t,z,\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\Lambda)}
.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

The above proof of Lemma
6.2
is the only place in the proof where we use specifically the assumption
xu_{0}\in H^{1}
, and only in the weaker form
xu_{0}\in L^{\infty}
. In particular the case of the initial datum
u_{0}(x)=c{\bf 1}_{(-1,1)}(x)
can be treated.

6.2.
Weak convergence

Lemma 6.4
.

The following properties hold.

(1)

For every bounded family
(\psi_{t})_{t}\in L^{\infty}(\Lambda)
, for every function
\varphi\in L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\Lambda}\sqrt{t}f_{t,z,\lambda}(x)\varphi(x)\psi_{t}(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}}}}0.

(2)

For every
\psi\in L^{2}(\Lambda)
,

\int_{\Lambda}I_{+}(q_{t,z,\lambda})\psi(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda=o(\sqrt{t}).

Proof.

Let us prove the first property. We already know from (
6.1
) that the family
(\sqrt{t}\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda})_{t}
is bounded in
L^{2}_{x,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}\times\Lambda)
, so that
F_{t,z,\lambda}:=\psi_{t}\sqrt{t}\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}
is bounded as well. Hence up to a subsequence, we can assume that
F_{t,z,\lambda}\rightharpoonup F_{\infty,z,\lambda}
in
L^{2}_{x,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}\times\Lambda)
as
t\to+\infty
. Then passing to the weak limit in (
6.1
) after dividing by
\sqrt{t}
, we find that
\widehat{F}_{\infty,z,\lambda}
is solution to

-2(\xi-\lambda)\widehat{F}_{\infty,z,\lambda}+\frac{1}{\pi}\widehat{u_{0}}\ast\widehat{F}_{\infty,z,\lambda}=0.

We recognize the equation
L_{u_{0}}F_{\infty,z,\lambda}=\lambda F_{\infty,z,\lambda}
. Taking the distorted Fourier transform of both sides, we find that in
L^{2}_{\lambda,\eta}({\Lambda}\times(0,\infty))
,

\eta\widetilde{F}_{\infty,z,\lambda}(\eta)=\lambda\widetilde{F}_{\infty,z,\lambda}(\eta).

This implies that
\widetilde{F}_{\infty,z,\lambda}=0
outside the diagonal
\lambda=\eta
. Since
\widetilde{F}_{\infty,z,\lambda}\in L^{2}_{\lambda,\eta}({\Lambda}\times(0,\infty))
, we conclude that
\widetilde{F}_{\infty,z,\lambda}=0
, hence
F_{\infty,z,\lambda}=0
and (1) follows.

Then we write

I_{+}(q_{t,z,\lambda})=I_{+}(\Pi u_{0})-2tI_{+}(T_{u_{0}}f_{t,z,\lambda})=I_{+}(\Pi u_{0})-2\sqrt{t}\langle\sqrt{t}f_{t,z,\lambda},\Pi u_{0}\rangle,

and (2) follows. The proof is complete.
∎

7.
Proof of Properties (
5.1
) and (
5.2
)

7.1.
Proof of the localization Property (
5.1
)

The first step will be to show that the contribution (
5.1
) from the integral away from
\xi=\lambda
vanishes as
t\to\infty
.

We recall that
\widehat{q}_{t,z,\lambda}/\sqrt{t}
is bounded in
L^{2}_{\lambda}(\Lambda,H^{1}_{\xi}(0,\infty))
thanks to Lemma
6.2
, in particular it is bounded in
L^{2}_{\lambda}(\Lambda,L^{\infty}_{\xi}(0,\infty))
.
By integration by parts, we have

(7.1)

\sqrt{2t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\xi-\lambda)^{2}+\mathrm{i}z\xi}\widehat{q}_{t,z,\lambda}(\xi)(1-\chi(\xi))\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{2\pi}\\
=-\mathrm{i}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\lambda^{2}}I_{+}(q_{t,z,\lambda})}{2\pi\sqrt{2t}(\lambda-z/2t)}-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\sqrt{2t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\xi-\lambda)^{2}+\mathrm{i}z\xi}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}\left(\frac{\widehat{q}_{t,z,\lambda}(\xi)(1-\chi(\xi))}{\xi-\lambda+z/2t}\right)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{2\pi}.

The first term in the right-hand side goes to zero in
L^{2}_{w}(\Lambda)
as
t\to+\infty
thanks to Lemma
6.4
. Concerning the second term in the right-hand side, we split the integral depending on where the derivative falls. For instance, we detail the upper bound on

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\xi-\lambda)^{2}+\mathrm{i}z\xi}\frac{(\widehat{q}_{t,z,\lambda})^{\prime}(\xi)(1-\chi(\xi))}{\xi-\lambda+z/2t}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{2\pi}.

We decompose
\widehat{q}_{t,z,\lambda}=\widehat{\Pi u_{0}}-2t\widehat{T_{u_{0}}f_{t,z,\lambda}}
. The term corresponding to
\widehat{\Pi u_{0}}
tends to zero thanks to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\xi-\lambda)^{2}+\mathrm{i}z\xi}\frac{(\widehat{\Pi u_{0}})^{\prime}(\xi)(1-\chi(\xi))}{\xi-\lambda+z/2t}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{2\pi}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2t}}\left\|\frac{(1-\chi(\xi))}{\xi-\lambda+z/2t}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}\|(\widehat{\Pi u_{0}})^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}},

where
\|(\widehat{\Pi u_{0}})^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}
is finite since
xu_{0}\in L^{2}_{x}
and
\left\|(1-\chi(\xi))/(\xi-\lambda+z/2t)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}
is finite since
\xi
is located away from
\lambda\in\Lambda
on the support of
(1-\chi)
. The term corresponding to
2t\widehat{T_{u_{0}}f_{t,z,\lambda}}
can be written as

(7.2)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\xi-\lambda)^{2}+\mathrm{i}z\xi}\frac{(\sqrt{2t}\widehat{T_{u_{0}}f_{t,z,\lambda}})^{\prime}(\xi)(1-\chi(\xi))}{\xi-\lambda+z/2t}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{2\pi}\\
=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\xi-\lambda)^{2}+\mathrm{i}z\xi}\frac{(1-\chi(\xi))}{\xi-\lambda+z/2t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{2t}\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}(\eta)(\widehat{u_{0}})^{\prime}(\xi-\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\eta\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{2\pi}.

Thanks to the weak convergence of
\sqrt{2t}f_{t,z,\lambda}
to zero in
L^{2}_{x,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}\times\Lambda)
, we know that pointwise in
\xi
, there holds
\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{2t}\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}(\eta)(\widehat{u_{0}})^{\prime}(\xi-\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\eta\rightharpoonup 0
in
L^{2}(\Lambda)
as
t\to+\infty
. Moreover, we have from the Young inequality

\left|\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\xi-\lambda)^{2}+\mathrm{i}z\xi}(1-\chi(\xi))}{\xi-\lambda+z/2t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{2t}\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}(\eta)(\widehat{u_{0}})^{\prime}(\xi-\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\eta\right|\leq C\left|\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}z\xi}(1-\chi)(\xi)}{\xi-\lambda}\right|\|\sqrt{2t}\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}\|(\widehat{u_{0}})^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}},

where
\|\sqrt{2t}\widehat{f}_{t,z,\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}
is bounded and
\left|\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}z\xi}(1-\chi)(\xi)}{\xi-\lambda}\right|\in L^{1}_{\xi}
thanks to the fact that
\mathrm{Im}(z)>0
. We can therefore apply the dominated convergence theorem and get that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\xi-\lambda)^{2}+\mathrm{i}z\xi}\frac{(\sqrt{2t}\widehat{T_{u_{0}}f_{t,z,\lambda}})^{\prime}(\xi)(1-\chi(\xi))}{\xi-\lambda+z/2t}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{2\pi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\rightharpoonup}\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}}}}0

in
L^{2}_{w}(\Lambda)
.
In the second term of the right-hand side of (
7.1
), the other terms obtained when the derivative falls either on
(1-\chi)
or on
\frac{1}{\xi-\lambda+z/2t}
are treated similarly.

7.2.
Proof of Property (
5.2
)

In this paragraph, we focus on the second term (
5.2
). From now on, we shall make an extensive use of stationary phase asymptotics, which we recall in the following lemma, see e.g.
[
28
]
.

Lemma 7.1

(The van der Corput estimate and stationary phase asymptotics)
.

There exists a universal constant
\kappa_{0}
such that, for every open interval
I
, for every
C^{2}
function
\phi:I\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\phi^{\prime\prime}\geq 1
on
I
, for every continuous function
b\in L^{1}(I)
such that
b^{\prime}\in L^{1}(I)
,

(7.3)

\forall\tau\geq 1,\ \forall x_{0}\in I,\ \left|\int_{I}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau\phi(x)}b(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\kappa_{0}\tau^{-1/2}\left(|b(x_{0})|+\int_{I}|b^{\prime}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\right).

Furthermore, if
\phi
has a critical point
x_{c}
in
I
and if moreover
b\in H^{1}(I)
is compactly supported,

(7.4)

\int_{I}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau\phi(x)}b(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\left(\frac{2\pi}{\tau\phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{c})}\right)^{1/2}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\pi/4}(b(x_{c})+o(1)),\quad\tau\to\infty.

In the integral (
5.2
), we first recognize the distorted Fourier transform of
\Pi u_{0}
in the integral over
x
:

\sqrt{2t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0}(x)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\xi-\lambda)^{2}+\mathrm{i}z\xi}\chi(\xi)\overline{m}_{-}\left(x,\xi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{2\pi}=\sqrt{2t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\xi-\lambda)^{2}+\mathrm{i}z\xi}\chi(\xi)\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}\left(\xi\right)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{2\pi}.

In order to apply the stationary phase lemma, we use the fact that
\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}\in H^{1}_{\xi}(\epsilon,\infty)
for every
\epsilon>0
(see Lemma
A.1
below), and in particular it is continuous on
(0,\infty)
.
Since
\chi
is supported on
[\Lambda_{-}/4,4\Lambda_{+}]
, we can apply (
7.4
) from Lemma
7.1
and deduce that

\sqrt{2t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0}(x)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\xi-\lambda)^{2}+\mathrm{i}z\xi}\chi(\xi)\overline{m}_{-}\left(x,\xi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{2\pi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}}}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\pi/4}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda z}\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}(\lambda).

The convergence is clearly uniform in
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

8.
Proof of Property (
5.3
)

In this section, we prove the remainder term estimate (
5.3
), which turns out to be the most delicate one. Let us set

p_{t,z,\lambda}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}-\mathrm{i}\frac{\overline{z}\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}}}\chi\left(\lambda+\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}}\right)r_{t,z,\lambda}(x,\alpha)\,\mathrm{d}\alpha.

Our goal is to prove that the following quantity tends to
0
in
L^{2}_{w}(\Lambda)
as
t\to\infty
,

(8.1)

\langle q_{t,z,\lambda},p_{t,z,\lambda}\rangle=I_{1}(t,z,\lambda)-I_{2}(t,z,\lambda),

where

(8.2)

\displaystyle I_{1}(t,z,\lambda)

\displaystyle:=\langle\Pi u_{0},p_{t,z,\lambda}\rangle,

(8.3)

\displaystyle I_{2}(t,z,\lambda)

\displaystyle:=2t\langle T_{u_{0}}f_{t,z,\lambda},p_{t,z,\lambda}\rangle.

8.1.
Decomposition of
p_{t,z,\lambda}
.

First, we derive a new expression for
p_{t,z,\lambda}
.
The starting point is the following observation, coming from the fact that
m_{-}
is a generalized eigenfunction of
L_{u_{0}}
:

T_{u_{0}}m_{-}(y,\eta)=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\eta y}Da(y,\eta),

where we set
a(y,\eta):=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\eta y}m_{-}(y,\eta).
Then
a(y,\eta)\to 1
as
y\to-\infty
.
Plugging this identity into the definition (
5.4
) of
r_{t,z,\lambda}(x,\alpha)
, and integrating by parts in
y
, we obtain

\displaystyle r_{t,z,\lambda}(x,\alpha)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda x}Da\left(y,\lambda+\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}}\right)\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\overline{z}\frac{(x-y)}{2t}+\mathrm{i}\frac{(x^{2}-y^{2})}{4t}+\mathrm{i}\frac{\alpha y}{\sqrt{2t}}}-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\alpha x}{\sqrt{2t}}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda x}\left(a\left(y,\lambda+\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}}\right)-1\right)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\overline{z}\frac{(x-y)}{2t}+\mathrm{i}\frac{(x^{2}-y^{2})}{4t}+\mathrm{i}\frac{\alpha y}{\sqrt{2t}}}\left[\frac{-\overline{z}}{2t}+\frac{y}{2t}-\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}}\right]\,\mathrm{d}y.

Plugging this identity into the definition of
p_{t,z,\lambda}(x)
, and integrating by parts in
\alpha
, we observe a cancellation leading to

(8.4)

p_{t,z,\lambda}(x)=-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda x}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}-\mathrm{i}\frac{\overline{z}\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}}-\mathrm{i}\overline{z}\frac{(x-y)}{2t}+\mathrm{i}\frac{(x^{2}-y^{2})}{4t}+\mathrm{i}\frac{\alpha y}{\sqrt{2t}}}b\left(y,\lambda+\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}}\right)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\alpha}{2t}.

where

(8.5)

b(y,\eta):=\chi^{\prime}(\eta)(a(y,\eta)-1)+\chi(\eta)\partial_{\eta}a(y,\eta).

Identity (
8.4
) is a crucial step in our proof. According to Lemma
A.2
below, we isolate the term with no decay as
y\to-\infty
in the expression of
b
, so that we define

(8.6)

b_{0}(y,\eta):=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\eta y}}{\eta}\overline{\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}}(\eta)\chi(\eta)\quad\text{ and }\quad b_{1}(y,\eta):=b(y,\eta)-b_{0}(y,\eta).

The part of (
8.4
) corresponding to
b_{0}
decouples the variables
y
and
\alpha
, so that we define

(8.7)

\displaystyle J^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}-\mathrm{i}\frac{\overline{z}\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}}}\frac{\overline{\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}}(\eta)}{\eta}\chi(\eta)_{|\eta=\lambda+\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\alpha}{2\pi},

(8.8)

\displaystyle G^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\overline{z}\frac{(x-y)}{2t}-\mathrm{i}\frac{y^{2}}{4t}-\mathrm{i}\lambda y}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{\sqrt{2t}}.

Finally, we denote the part corresponding to
b_{1}
by

(8.9)

P^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\Phi_{t,z}(\alpha,x,y)}b_{1}\left(y,\lambda+\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}}\right)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}},

where for further reference, we introduce the phase function in the integral in the right hand side of (
8.4
):

(8.10)

\Phi_{t,z}(\alpha,x,y):=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}+\frac{\overline{z}\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}}+\frac{\overline{z}(x-y)}{2t}-\frac{x^{2}-y^{2}}{4t}-\frac{\alpha y}{\sqrt{2t}}.

We have proven the following proposition.

Proposition 8.1

(Decomposition of
p_{t,z,\lambda}
)
.

We have a decomposition

(8.11)

\displaystyle p_{t,z,\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=p^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\lambda^{2}}+p^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}(x),

(8.12)

\displaystyle p^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\lambda^{2}+\mathrm{i}\lambda x+\mathrm{i}\frac{x^{2}}{4t}}J^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}G^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)/\sqrt{2t},

(8.13)

\displaystyle p^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda x}P^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)/\sqrt{2t}.

The integrals
J^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}
and
G^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}
enjoy the following properties.

Lemma 8.2

(Properties of
J^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}
and
G^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}
)
.

The integral
J^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}
is uniformly bounded in the variable
\lambda\in\Lambda
as
t\to+\infty
, and convergent to

J^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\pi/4}\frac{\overline{\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}}(\lambda)}{\sqrt{2\pi}\lambda}.

The function
G^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}
is uniformly bounded in the variables
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda\in\Lambda
, moreover, we have the pointwise convergence result

(8.14)

\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\lambda^{2}}G^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}}}}\sqrt{2\pi}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\pi/4}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\overline{z}\lambda}.

Proof.

Since
\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}
belongs to
H^{1}([\Lambda_{-}/4,\infty))
, the stationary phase formula (
7.4
) leads to the result for
J^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}
.

We now focus on
G^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}
. For fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we set
y=2ts
in the
y
integral and we get

(8.15)

G^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)=\sqrt{2t}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{\overline{z}x}{2t}}\int_{-\infty}^{\frac{x}{2t}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\overline{z}s-\mathrm{i}ts^{2}-2\mathrm{i}t\lambda s}\,\mathrm{d}s,

and, after localization near the critical point
s=-\lambda
, the stationary phase formula (
7.4
) yields (
8.14
).
Then, writing
\sigma=\sqrt{2t}(s+\lambda)
in (
8.15
), we have

G^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\lambda^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\sqrt{2t}(\lambda+\frac{x}{2t})}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\overline{z}(\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2t}}-\frac{x}{2t}-\lambda)}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma,

and the boundedness follows from integration by parts.
∎

8.2.
Study of
I_{2}
.

Next we study the limit in
L^{2}_{w}(\Lambda)
as
t\to\infty
of

I_{2}(t,z,\lambda)=\langle\sqrt{2t}f_{t,z,\lambda},\sqrt{2t}u_{0}p_{t,z,\lambda}\rangle.

We set

g_{t,z,\lambda}(x)=\sqrt{2t}\,u_{0}(x)\,p_{t,z,\lambda}(x).

In view of the first statement of Lemma
6.4
, the following proposition implies that this limit is
0
.

Proposition 8.3
.

The two families

g^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)=\sqrt{2t}u_{0}(x)p^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)\quad\text{ and }\quad g^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)=\sqrt{2t}u_{0}(x)p^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)

belong to a compact subset of
L^{2}_{\lambda,x}(\Lambda\times\mathbb{R})
, and

g_{t,z,\lambda}(x)=g^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\lambda^{2}}+g^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}(x).

The next two paragraphs are devoted to the proof of Proposition
8.3
: we show that
g^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}
and
g^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}
belong to a compact subset of
L^{2}_{\lambda,x}(\Lambda\times\mathbb{R})
.

8.2.1.
Study of
g^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}
.

We have

g^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\lambda^{2}+\mathrm{i}\lambda x+\mathrm{i}\frac{x^{2}}{4t}}u_{0}(x)J^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}G^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}(x).

Thanks to Lemma
8.2
, we know that

g^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}}}}-\mathrm{i}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda x}u_{0}(x)\frac{\overline{\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}}(\lambda)}{\lambda}.

Moreover,
J^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}
and
G^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)
are uniformly bounded, hence there exists
g^{*}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\forall t\in\mathbb{R},\forall(x,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}\times\Lambda,\quad|g^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)|\leq g^{*}(x).

In order to conclude to compactness of
(x,\lambda)\mapsto g^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)
in
L^{2}_{x,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}\times\Lambda)
, it only remains to apply the Lebesgue theorem.

8.2.2.
Study of
g^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}
.

In this paragraph, we prove that the functions

(x,\lambda)\mapsto g^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)=-u_{0}(x)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda x}P^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)

belong to a compact subset of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\Lambda)
as
t\to\infty
. As a first step, we deal with the contribution of
x\leq-R
for
R>\!\!>1
.

Lemma 8.4
.

There holds

\sup_{x\leq-R}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\left|P^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)\right|=O(R^{-1/2}),R\to+\infty.

Proof.

We recall that
P^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}
was defined in (
8.9
). According to Lemma
A.2
below, the contribution from
b_{1}
(defined in (
8.6
)) as
y\to-\infty
is of the form

b_{1}(y,\eta)=-\mathrm{i}\chi^{\prime}(\eta)\frac{c_{0}}{y+\mathrm{i}}-\kappa(\eta)\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\eta y}}{y+\mathrm{i}}+O\left(\frac{1}{|y|^{3/2}}\right)

where

(8.16)

\kappa(\eta):=\mathrm{i}\chi^{\prime}(\eta)\frac{\overline{\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}}(\eta)}{2\pi\eta}+\chi(\eta)\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0}(s)n(s,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}s}{2\pi\eta}.

The contribution from
\kappa(\eta)\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\eta y}}{y+\mathrm{i}}
decouples the variables of integration
y
and
\alpha
, so that we set

(8.17)

G^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}(x):=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\overline{z}}{2t}(y-x)-\mathrm{i}\frac{y^{2}}{4t}-\mathrm{i}\lambda y}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{(y+\mathrm{i})}.

For the contribution of
-\mathrm{i}\chi^{\prime}(\eta)\frac{c_{0}}{y+\mathrm{i}}
, we write
\alpha:=\beta+\frac{y}{\sqrt{2t}}
and use the identity

(8.18)

\Phi_{t,z}(\alpha,x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha-\frac{y}{\sqrt{2t}}\right)^{2}+\frac{\overline{z}}{\sqrt{2t}}\left(\alpha-\frac{y}{\sqrt{2t}}\right)+\frac{\overline{z}x}{2t}-\frac{x^{2}}{4t}.

Hence we find that as
x\to-\infty
,

(8.19)

P^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)=-\mathrm{i}c_{0}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{x^{2}}{4t}-\mathrm{i}\frac{\overline{z}x}{2t}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}-\mathrm{i}\frac{\overline{z}\beta}{\sqrt{2t}}}\chi^{\prime}\left(\lambda+\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2t}}+\frac{y}{2t}\right)d\beta\right]\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{\sqrt{2t}(y+\mathrm{i})}\\
-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{x^{2}}{4t}}G^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}-\mathrm{i}\frac{\overline{z}\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}}}\kappa\left(\lambda+\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}}\right)\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}}+O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{1/2}}\right).

Denote by
P^{1,1}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)
and
P^{1,2}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)
the two terms of the right–hand side of (
8.19
).

Integrating by parts in
\beta
, and observing that

\left|\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2t}}+\frac{y}{2t}\right|\lesssim 1,

we obtain

(8.20)

|P^{1,1}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)|\lesssim\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{Im}z\frac{(y-x)}{2t}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{\sqrt{2t}(1+|y|)}=\frac{O(\log|x|+\log|t|)}{\sqrt{t}}.

Next we estimate
P^{1,2}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)
. We claim that

(8.21)

|P^{1,2}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)|=O\left(\frac{1}{|x|}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\right).

Indeed, since
\kappa
is a bounded compactly supported function, the integral in
\alpha
is bounded, and we just have to estimate
G^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)
.
We decompose

G^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\overline{z}}{2t}(y-x)-\mathrm{i}\frac{y^{2}}{4t}-\mathrm{i}\lambda y}\left(1-\chi\left(\frac{-y}{2t}\right)\right)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{(y+\mathrm{i})}+\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\overline{z}}{2t}(y-x)-\mathrm{i}\frac{y^{2}}{4t}-\mathrm{i}\lambda y}\chi\left(\frac{-y}{2t}\right)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{(y+\mathrm{i})}.

In the first integral, the derivative
\lambda+y/2t-\overline{z}/2t
of the phase function is away from
0
, therefore an integration by parts shows that these integrals are

O(|x|^{-1}+t^{-1})
.
In the second integral, setting
y=2ts
and applying (
7.3
) leads to a bound
O(t^{-1/2})
.

Finally, estimates (
8.19
), (
8.20
) and (
8.21
) complete the proof of Lemma
8.4
.
∎

Remark 8.5
.

Using Lemma
8.4
, we infer

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|g^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(x<-R)}=O(R^{-1/2}).

Taking
x=-R
in Lemma
8.4
and using that

\mathrm{Im}(\Phi_{t,z}(\alpha,x,y)-\Phi_{t,z}(\alpha,-R,y))=-\mathrm{Im}z(x+R)\leq 0,

we also infer that

\sup_{x\geq-R}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{-\infty}^{-R}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Phi_{t,z}(\alpha,x,y)}b_{1}\left(y,\lambda+\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}}\right)\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}}\right|=O(R^{-1/2}),R\to+\infty.

Lemma 8.6
.

We set, for
R>0
, and
x\geq-R
,

g^{1}_{t,z,\lambda,R}(x):=-{\bf 1}_{x\geq-R}u_{0}(x)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda x}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-R}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\Phi_{t,z}(\alpha,x,y)}b_{1}\left(y,\lambda+\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2t}}\right)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\alpha}{2t}.

Then for every
R>0
, the family of functions
(x,\lambda)\mapsto g^{1}_{t,z,\lambda,R}(x)
belongs to a compact subset of
L^{2}([-R,\infty)\times\Lambda)
as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

For
x\geq-R
, let us rewrite
g^{1}_{t,z,\lambda,R}(x)
as

g^{1}_{t,z,\lambda,R}(x)=-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda x}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\overline{z}x}{2t}+\mathrm{i}\frac{x^{2}}{4t}}u_{0}(x)\int_{-R}^{x}G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}(y)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{\sqrt{2t}},

with

(8.22)

G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}(y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}-\mathrm{i}\frac{\overline{z}\beta}{\sqrt{2t}}}b_{1}\left(y,\lambda+\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2t}}+\frac{y}{2t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\beta.

Since
b_{1}
is uniformly bounded thanks to Lemma
A.2
, and since the domain of the integral in
\beta
has a length of order
\sqrt{2t}
, we infer that

G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}(y)/\sqrt{2t}
is uniformly bounded:

(8.23)

|G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}(y)/\sqrt{2t}|\leq C\mathrm{e}^{(\mathrm{Im}z)y/(2t)}.

As a consequence, using
y\leq x
and
|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\overline{z}x/2t}|\leq\mathrm{e}^{-(\mathrm{Im}z)x/(2t)}
, a crude estimate yields

|g^{1}_{t,z,\lambda,R}(x)|\leq C|x+R||u_{0}(x)|.

Furthermore, recall that the function
(y,\eta)\mapsto b_{1}(y,\eta)
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)
. Hence the function
(x,\lambda)\mapsto\int_{-R}^{x}G_{t,z,\lambda}(y)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{\sqrt{2t}}
is uniformly equicontinuous on
[-R,L]\times\Lambda
and, up to extracting a subsequence in
t
,
g^{1}_{t,z,\lambda,R}(x)
is pointwise convergent in
x,\lambda
.
By the dominated convergence theorem, we infer that
g^{1}_{t,z,\lambda,R}
belongs to a compact subset of
L^{2}([-R,\infty)\times\Lambda)
.
∎

Combining Lemma
8.4
, Remark
8.5
and Lemma
8.6
, we have shown that
g^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}
belongs to a compact subset of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\Lambda)
.
This completes the proof of Proposition
8.3
.

8.3.
Study of
I_{1}
.

In comparison to
I_{2}(t,z,\lambda)
studied in the previous paragraph,
I_{1}(t,z,\lambda)
has an extra factor
t^{-1/2}
but the decay of the factor
\Pi u_{0}
is slower, since we can only have the decomposition

(8.24)

\Pi u_{0}(x)=\frac{\Pi((\diamond+\mathrm{i})u_{0})(x)}{x+\mathrm{i}}+\frac{\mathrm{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s}{2\pi(x+\mathrm{i})}=v_{1}(x)+\frac{c_{1}}{x+\mathrm{i}},

where
v_{1}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. We recall that
I_{1}(t,z,\lambda)=\langle\Pi u_{0},p_{t,z,\lambda}\rangle
where
p_{t,z,\lambda}
is also decomposed into two parts according to (
8.11
).

8.3.1.
The contribution of
p^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}
.

We already saw in Lemma
8.2
that
J^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}
is uniformly bounded, and that
G^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}
is uniformly bounded and pointwise convergent.
Hence, by (
8.12
) and the dominated convergence theorem, there holds the strong convergence in
L^{2}(\Lambda)

\langle v_{1},p^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}}}}0.

Let us come to the study of
\langle(x+\mathrm{i})^{-1},p^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}\rangle
. We have

\langle(x+\mathrm{i})^{-1},p^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}\rangle=\overline{J^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\lambda^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x-\mathrm{i}\frac{x^{2}}{4t}}}{x+\mathrm{i}}\overline{G^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}(x)}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}x}{\sqrt{2t}}.

Again, we extract the contribution of the critical point
x=-2t\lambda
by introducing the cutoff function
\chi(-x/2t)
. In the integral containing
1-\chi(-x/2t)
, we integrate once by parts in
x
, and we observe that this integral tends to
0
as
t\to\infty
. As for the integral containing
\chi(-x/2t)
, we set
y=2ts
in the definition (
8.8
) of
G^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}
and
x=-2t\mu
: we observe that this integral is bounded by

O(1)\sup_{\Lambda_{-}/4\leq\mu\leq 4\Lambda_{+}}\left|\int_{-\infty}^{-\mu}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}z(\mu+s)+\mathrm{i}t(s^{2}+2\lambda s)}\,\mathrm{d}s\right|=O(t^{-1/2}),

applying again the van der Corput estimate (
7.3
).

Summing up, we have proved that
\langle\Pi u_{0},p^{0}_{t,z,\lambda}\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}}}}0
strongly in
L^{2}(\Lambda)
.

8.3.2.
The contribution of
p^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}
.

We first study the most delicate contribution in the decomposition (
8.24
) of
\Pi u_{0}
, which is the one of
c_{1}/(x+\mathrm{i})
. We have, using again (
8.18
) in the expression (
8.9
) of
P^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}
,

(8.25)

\langle(x+\mathrm{i})^{-1},p^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}\rangle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x-\mathrm{i}\frac{x^{2}}{4t}+\mathrm{i}\frac{zx}{2t}}}{x+\mathrm{i}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\overline{G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}}(y)\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{{2t}},

where
G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}
was defined in (
8.22
). Let us improve the bound (
8.23
).
Integrating by parts in
\beta
, and observing that, on the domain of the integral defining
G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}
,

\left|\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2t}}+\frac{y}{2t}\right|\lesssim 1,

so that we get

(8.26)

|G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}(y)|\lesssim\mathrm{e}^{\frac{y\mathrm{Im}z}{2t}},

and consequently

(8.27)

\left|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{zx}{2t}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\overline{G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}}(y)\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{{2t}}\right|=O(1).

Next, as in the previous paragraph, we extract the contribution of the critical point
x=-2t\lambda
by introducing the cutoff function
\chi(-x/2t)
. Then in the integral containing
1-\chi(-x/2t)
, we integrate by parts in
x
. We get

(8.28)

\mathrm{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x-\mathrm{i}\frac{x^{2}}{4t}+\mathrm{i}\frac{zx}{2t}}}{x+\mathrm{i}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\overline{G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}}(y)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{2t}=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4},

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(1-\chi\left(-\frac{x}{2t}\right)\right)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x-\mathrm{i}\frac{x^{2}}{4t}+\mathrm{i}\frac{zx}{2t}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}x}\left(\frac{1}{(x+\mathrm{i})\left(\lambda+\frac{x-z}{2t}\right)}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{x}\overline{G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}}(y)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{2t},

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi^{\prime}\left(-\frac{x}{2t}\right)\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x-\mathrm{i}\frac{x^{2}}{4t}+\mathrm{i}\frac{zx}{2t}}}{(x+\mathrm{i})\left(\lambda+\frac{x-z}{2t}\right)}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\overline{G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}}(y)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{(2t)^{2}},

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(1-\chi\left(-\frac{x}{2t}\right)\right)\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x-\mathrm{i}\frac{x^{2}}{4t}+\mathrm{i}\frac{zx}{2t}}}{(x+\mathrm{i})\left(\lambda+\frac{x-z}{2t}\right)}\overline{G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}}(x)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}x}{2t},

\displaystyle J_{4}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi\left(-\frac{x}{2t}\right)\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x-\mathrm{i}\frac{x^{2}}{4t}+\mathrm{i}\frac{zx}{2t}}}{x+\mathrm{i}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\overline{G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}}(y)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{2t}.

Study of
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
.
In view of (
8.27
) and of the inequality
|x|\lesssim|x+2\lambda t|
on the support of the integrand, the integrand in
J_{1}
is uniformly bounded by
(1+x^{2})^{-1}
. Furthermore, one can use the uniform estimate in
\eta\in[\Lambda_{-}/4,4\Lambda_{+}]
, as
y\to-\infty
,

|b_{1}(y,\eta)|\lesssim\frac{1}{1+|y|},

from Lemma
A.2
to improve (
8.26
) into

(8.29)

|{G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}}(y)|\lesssim(1+|y|)^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{y\mathrm{Im}z}{2t}},

as
y\to-\infty
. We conclude that, as
t\to\infty
, for fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

(8.30)

\left|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{zx}{2t}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\overline{G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}}(y)\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{2t}\right|=O\left(\frac{\log t}{t}\right).

Hence, by dominated convergence,
J_{1}\to 0
uniformly in
\lambda\in\Lambda
as
t\to\infty
. A similar argument leads to
J_{2}\to 0
, since, on the support of the integrand,
t\simeq|x|
.

Study of
J_{3}
.
As for the third integral
J_{3}
, we perform again a partial integration,

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}J_{3}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(1-\chi\left(-\frac{x}{2t}\right)\right)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x-\mathrm{i}\frac{x^{2}}{4t}+\mathrm{i}\frac{zx}{2t}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}x}\left(\frac{1}{(x+\mathrm{i})\left(\lambda+\frac{x-z}{2t}\right)^{2}}\right)\overline{G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}}(x)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}x}{2t}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi^{\prime}\left(-\frac{x}{2t}\right)\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x-\mathrm{i}\frac{x^{2}}{4t}+\mathrm{i}\frac{zx}{2t}}}{(x+\mathrm{i})\left(\lambda+\frac{x-z}{2t}\right)^{2}}\overline{G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}}(x)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}x}{(2t)^{2}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(1-\chi\left(-\frac{x}{2t}\right)\right)\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x-\mathrm{i}\frac{x^{2}}{4t}+\mathrm{i}\frac{zx}{2t}}}{(x+\mathrm{i})\left(\lambda+\frac{x-z}{2t}\right)^{2}}\partial_{x}\overline{G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}}(x)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}x}{2t}.

Using (
8.26
), the first two integrals are
O(t^{-1})
.

For the third integral, we use finer properties of
b_{1}
, that we decompose as

(8.31)

b_{1}(y,\eta)=(a(y,\eta)-1)\chi^{\prime}(\eta)+a^{\sharp}(y,\eta)\chi(\eta),

where
a^{\sharp}
is defined in (
A.10
). Lemmas
A.2
and
A.3
, then (
A.1
) combined with (
A.7
) imply that

(8.32)

\|\partial_{y}b_{1}(\diamond,\eta)\|_{\mathscr{F}}+\|\partial_{\eta}b_{1}(\diamond,\eta)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C,

where
\mathscr{F}
is the space of functions
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
such that

f(x)=f_{1}(x)+f_{2}(x),

where
f_{2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
(1+|x|)f_{1}(x)
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}
, equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathscr{F}}:=\inf_{f=f_{1}+f_{2}}\|(1+|\diamond|)f_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+\|f_{2}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

We deduce the estimate

\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{zx}{2t}}\partial_{x}\overline{G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}}(x)=R_{1}(x)+R_{2}(x),\ \|R_{1}\|_{\mathscr{F}}\lesssim\sqrt{t},\ \|R_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2t}},

which follows from the estimates (
8.32
) and of a crude estimate of the integral in
\beta
, since the support of integrand has a length
\lesssim\sqrt{t}
. Using again that
|x|\lesssim|x+2\lambda t|
on the support of the integrand, we conclude that this integral is
O(t^{-1/2})
.

Study of
J_{4}
.
Finally, let us study the integral
J_{4}
involving the localization
\chi(-x/2t)
.

We set
x=-2t\mu
and
y=2ts
, so that

J_{4}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi(\mu)\frac{2t}{2\mathrm{i}t\mu+1}\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}t\lambda\mu-\mathrm{i}t\mu^{2}-\mathrm{i}z\mu}\mathcal{B}_{t,z}(\mu)\,\mathrm{d}\mu,

with

\mathcal{B}_{t,z}(\mu):=\int_{-\infty}^{-\mu}\overline{G^{2}_{t,z,\lambda}}(2ts)\,\mathrm{d}s.

In view of the support of
\chi
, the support of the integrand is away from
\mu=0
, and the amplitude
\frac{2t}{2\mathrm{i}t\mu+1}
is bounded with all its derivatives.
Moreover, in view of the bounds (
8.26
) and (
8.27
), we have uniformly in the support of
\chi
,

|\mathcal{B}_{t,z}(\mu)|=O(1),\ |\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{B}_{t,z}(\mu)|=O(1).

Applying the van der Corput estimate (
7.3
), we infer

J_{4}=O(t^{-1/2}).

Summing up, we have proved that
\langle(x+\mathrm{i})^{-1},p^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}}}}0
, uniformly in
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

The contribution of the other term

\langle v_{1},p^{1}_{t,z,\lambda}\rangle

coming from (
8.24
) is easier to handle because
v_{1}
is integrable, so, from (
8.27
), the integrand is then bounded by a fixed integrable function. Furthermore, we have already noticed in (
8.30
) that the integrand is poinwise convergent to
0
. Therefore we can conclude using the dominated convergence theorem.

Appendix A
Properties of the distorted Fourier transform

In this section, we derive some properties of the distorted Fourier transform and of the function
a
defined as
a(x,\lambda)=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x}m_{-}(x,\lambda)
.

A.1.
Regularity of the distorted Fourier transform

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Then
\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}\in H^{1}(\epsilon,\infty)
.

Proof.

We already know that
\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}\in L^{2}(0,\infty)
, hence we focus on the derivative

\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}^{\prime}(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0}(x)\overline{\partial_{\lambda}m_{-}}(x,\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}x.

We make the change of functions
m_{-}(x,\lambda)=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda x}a(x,\lambda)
and write

\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}^{\prime}(\lambda)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{i}xu_{0}(x)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x}\overline{m_{-}}(x,\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0}(x)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x}\overline{\partial_{\lambda}a}(x,\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
xu_{0}\in L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R})
, the first term in the right-hand side is the distorted Fourier transform of a
L^{2}
function, hence it belongs to
L^{2}_{\lambda}(0,\infty)
. Concerning the second term in the right-hand side, since
u_{0}\in L^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R})
, it is enough to show that

\|\partial_{\lambda}a(\cdot,\lambda)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\in L^{2}_{\lambda}(\epsilon,\infty).

We use the equation (
5.9
) satisfied by
m_{-}
to get that
a
is solution to

(A.1)

a(y,\lambda)-1=\mathrm{i}\int_{-\infty}^{y}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x}T_{u_{0}}m_{-}(x,\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}x.

We write
a(x,\cdot)
as the limit in
L^{\infty}(\Lambda)
as
\delta\to 0
of the following absolutely convergent integral,

a(x,\lambda)-1=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\mathrm{i}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda y}T_{u_{0}}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda\diamond}a(\diamond,\lambda))(y)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{(1-\mathrm{i}\delta y)^{2}}.

Then
\partial_{\lambda}a
is the limit in the sense of distributions of

(A.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}a(x,\lambda)=

\displaystyle-\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda y}[T_{u_{0}},y](\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda\diamond}a(\diamond,\lambda))(y))\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{(1-\mathrm{i}\delta y)^{2}}

\displaystyle+\lim_{\delta\to 0}\mathrm{i}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda y}T_{u_{0}}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda\diamond}\partial_{\lambda}a(\diamond,\lambda))(y)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{(1-\mathrm{i}\delta y)^{2}}.

Now we note that when
T_{u_{0}}f\in\mathrm{Dom}(X^{*})
, there holds

(A.3)

X^{*}T_{u_{0}}f(y)=T_{u_{0}}(yf)\quad\text{and}\quad X^{*}T_{u_{0}}f(y)=yT_{u_{0}}f(y)+\frac{I_{+}(T_{u_{0}}f)}{2\mathrm{i}\pi}.

This formula is valid when
f=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda\diamond}a(\diamond,\lambda)
since
u_{0}f\in L^{2}
and
xu_{0}f\in L^{2}
by assumption.
Hence
[T_{u_{0}},y](\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda\diamond}a(\diamond,\lambda))
is a constant function of
y
. More precisely,

[T_{u_{0}},y](\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda\diamond}a(\diamond,\lambda))=\frac{I_{+}(\Pi(u_{0}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda\diamond}a(\cdot,\lambda)))}{2\mathrm{i}\pi}=\frac{\overline{\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}}(\lambda)}{2\mathrm{i}\pi}.

Finally, by integration by parts, we can show that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda\in\Lambda

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda y}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{(1-\mathrm{i}\delta y)^{2}}=\mathrm{i}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x}}{\lambda}.

In the second term in the right-hand side in the expression of
\partial_{\lambda}a
, we recall that
[
4
, Lemma 32]

K_{u_{0},\lambda}m(x):=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda(x-y)}T_{u_{0}}m(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

defines a compact operator
K_{u_{0},\lambda}:L^{\infty}_{+}\to L^{\infty}_{+}
. Hence

\widetilde{K}_{u_{0},\lambda}b(x):=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda y}T_{u_{0}}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda\diamond}b)(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

also defines a compact operator
\widetilde{K}_{u_{0}}:L^{\infty}_{+}\to L^{\infty}_{+}
. Therefore we can pass to the limit
\delta\to 0
in (
A.2
) and find that
\partial_{\lambda}a
is solution to

(A.4)

(1-\mathrm{i}\widetilde{K}_{u_{0},\lambda})\partial_{\lambda}a=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x}}{\lambda}\overline{\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}}(\lambda),

where the right-hand side belongs to
L^{\infty}_{+}
and
(1-\mathrm{i}\widetilde{K}_{u_{0},\lambda})
is an invertible operator
L^{\infty}_{+}\to L^{\infty}_{+}
. The decay of the right-hand side as
\lambda\to\infty
is then enough to infer
\|\partial_{\lambda}a\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\in L^{2}_{\lambda}((\epsilon,\infty))
.
∎

A.2.
Asymptotics of
a
and
\partial_{\lambda}a
.

At this stage we are going to make use of assumption (
1.5
).

Lemma A.2
.

Functions
a
and
\partial_{\lambda}a
are bounded on
\mathbb{R}\times[\lambda_{0},\infty)
for every
\lambda_{0}>0
. Furthermore, as
y\to-\infty

(A.5)

a(y,\lambda)-1=-\mathrm{i}\frac{c_{0}}{y+\mathrm{i}}-\mathrm{i}\frac{\overline{\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}}(\lambda)}{2\pi\lambda}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda y}}{(y+\mathrm{i})}+O\left(\frac{1}{|y|^{3/2}}\right),

and

(A.6)

\partial_{\lambda}a(y,\lambda)=-\frac{\overline{\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}}(\lambda)}{2\pi\lambda}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda y}-\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0}(s)n(s,\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}s}{2\pi\lambda}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda y}}{y+\mathrm{i}}+O\left(\frac{1}{|y|^{3/2}}\right).

Moreover, the remainder terms are uniform for
(y,\lambda)\in(-\infty,-y_{0}]\times[\lambda_{0},\infty)

Proof.

The boundedness of
a
and
\partial_{\lambda}a
have already been established in the proof of Lemma
A.1
. Let us prove (
A.5
) and (
A.6
).
From (
1.5

A.3

X^{*}

(A.7)

T_{u_{0}}m_{-}(x,\lambda)=c_{0}\frac{m_{-}(x,\lambda)}{(x+\mathrm{i})^{2}}+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\frac{\overline{\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}}(\lambda)}{x+\mathrm{i}}+\frac{c_{1}(\lambda)}{(x+\mathrm{i})^{2}}+\frac{w_{1}(x,\lambda)}{(x+\mathrm{i})^{2}},

where

c_{1}(\lambda):=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{v_{0}(x)+(2\mathrm{i}x-1)u_{0}(x)}{x+\mathrm{i}}m_{-}(x,\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}x

. Notice that, if

c_{1}(\lambda)
is uniformly bounded and

is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}
. Next we recall that
a

A.1
)
so that, from (

a(y,\lambda)-1

We proceed similarly for the estimate of

\partial_{\lambda}a(y,\lambda)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda y}}{\lambda}\overline{\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}}(\lambda)+\mathrm{i}\int_{-\infty}^{y}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda x}T_{u_{0}}n(x,\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where

Then we write, similarly to (

T_{u_{0}}n(x,\lambda)=c_{0}\frac{n(x,\lambda)}{(x+\mathrm{i})^{2}}+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0}(s)n(s,\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}s}{x+\mathrm{i}}+\frac{c_{2}(\lambda)}{(x+\mathrm{i})^{2}}+\frac{w_{2}(x,\lambda)}{(x+\mathrm{i})^{2}},

Since we know that

∎

A.3.
Decaying part in
\partial_{\lambda}a

Let us define

(A.10)

a^{\sharp}(y,\lambda):=\partial_{\lambda}a(y,\lambda)+\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda y}\overline{\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}}(\lambda)}{2\pi\lambda}=\mathrm{i}\int_{-\infty}^{y}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x}T_{u_{0}}n(x,\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where we recall that
n(x,\lambda)=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda x}\partial_{\lambda}a(x,\lambda)

We introduce the space
\mathscr{F}
of functions
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})

where
f_{2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})

Lemma A.3
.

For
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}>0

\|\partial_{y}a^{\sharp}(\diamond,\lambda)\|_{\mathscr{F}}+\|\partial_{\lambda}a^{\sharp}(\diamond,\lambda)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C.

Proof.

We have

\partial_{y}a^{\sharp}(y,\lambda)=\mathrm{i}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda y}T_{u_{0}}n(y,\lambda),

and the bound follows from (
A.9
). As for the second estimate, we proceed as in the proof of (

\displaystyle(1-\mathrm{i}\widetilde{K}_{u_{0},\lambda})\partial_{\lambda}a^{\sharp}=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0}(s)n(s,\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}s}{2\pi\lambda}-\mathrm{i}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}\left(\frac{\overline{\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}}(\lambda)}{2\pi\lambda}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{y}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x}\Pi u_{0}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\frac{\overline{\widetilde{\Pi u_{0}}}(\lambda)}{2\pi\lambda}\int_{-\infty}^{y}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda x}\Pi(\diamond\,u_{0})(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

We recall that the decomposition (

∎

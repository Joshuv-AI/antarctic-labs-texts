Explicit computation of the generic degree of the generalized Verschiebung in rank two
Yuki Kondo, Yasuhiro Wakabayashi

Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is to apply previous work on dormant opers to the study of the moduli space of stable bundles in positive characteristic. We affirmatively resolve the rank 2 2 case of a conjecture proposed by the second author, which predicts a direct relationship between the number of higher-level dormant PGL 2 \mathrm{PGL}_{2} -opers and the generic degree of the generalized Verschiebung map for rank 2 2 stable bundles induced by Frobenius pull-back. As a consequence, we obtain a procedure for explicitly determining these generic degrees in the previously unexplored range of genera by counting certain combinatorial objects.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Counting problem for the generalized Verschiebung map

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>2
and
X
a smooth projective curve over
k
of genus
g>1
.
For a positive integer
N
,
we denote by
X^{(N)}
the
N
-th Frobenius twist of
X
over
k
, i.e.,
the base-change of
X
along the
N
-th iterate of the Frobenius automorphism of
\mathrm{Spec}(k)
.

Pulling-back stable bundles on
X^{(N)}
via the
N
-th relative Frobenius morphism
F^{(N)}_{X/k}:X\rightarrow X^{(N)}
gives rise to a rational map

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ver}_{N}^{n}:SU_{X^{(N)}}^{n}\dashrightarrow SU_{X}^{n}

between the moduli spaces
SU_{X^{(N)}}^{n}
and
SU_{X}^{n}

of rank
n>1
stable bundles with trivial determinant on
X^{(N)}
and
X
, respectively.
We refer to this rational map as the
(
N
-th) generalized Verschiebung map
.
It captures the dynamics of stable bundles under Frobenius pull-back and
has been studied from various perspectives in, e.g.,

[
BrKa
]
,
[
dSa
]
,
[
Gie
]
,
[
JoPa
]
,
[
JRXY
]
,
[
Las
]
,

[
LasPa1
]
,
[
LasPa2
]
,

[
Li1
]
, and
[
Oss2
]
.
See also
[
DuMe
]
,
[
LanPa
]
,
[
Li2
]
,
[
Li3
]
,
[
Li4
]
,
[
Oss1
]
, and
[
Yan
]

for studies on the geometric structure of
\mathrm{Ver}^{n}_{1}
itself.

It is known that
\mathrm{Ver}^{n}_{N}
is generically finite, which allows us to define its generic degree
\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{N}^{n})
, a quantity that reflects the complexity of this rational map.
Although the Frobenius action on the cohomology ring of the moduli stack of vector bundles has been investigated in
[
CaNe
]
and
[
NeSt
]
, not much is known about
\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{N}^{n})
.
It therefore arises as a natural question to know how much this value is explicitly.

Note that
\mathrm{Ver}^{n}_{N}
factors as a composite of
N
rational maps obtained as Frobenius twists of
\mathrm{Ver}^{n}_{1}
:

(1.2)

\displaystyle SU_{X^{(N)}}^{n}\dashrightarrow SU_{X^{(N-1)}}^{n}\dashrightarrow\cdots\dashrightarrow SU_{X^{(1)}}^{n}\dashrightarrow SU_{X}^{n}.

This implies the identity
\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{N}^{n})=\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}^{n}_{1})^{N}
, which reduces the problem to the case
N=1
.

As far as the authors are aware,
the case
(n,g)=(2,2)
is the only one for which the value of the generic degree has been explicitly determined so far.
For a genus-
2
curve
X
,
the rational map
\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{1}
is relatively more accessible for analysis.
In fact, the moduli space
SU_{X}^{2}
(as well as
SU_{X^{(1)}}^{2}
) admits a natural compactification by semistable bundles, which is canonically isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, and its boundary can be identified with the Kummer surface associated to
X
.
Moreover, the base locus of
\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{1}

coincides with the moduli space of certain flat bundles known as
dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
-opers
.
By this observation, an explicit computation of the total number of such flat bundles, established in
[
Moc
]
, leads to the following formula:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{1}^{2})=\frac{1}{3}\cdot(p^{3}+2p)

See also
[
Oss1
, Theorem 1.3]
,
[
LanPa
, Corollary]
for this computation.

For arbitrary genus
g
,
the main result of
[
HoWa
]
provides an upper bound for
\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{1})
, based on the idea of K. Joshi involving a correspondence with certain Quot schemes (cf.
[
Jos
]
,
[
JoPa
]
).
However, this upper bound has shown to be suboptimal (cf. the remark preceding
[
HoWa
, Corollary 3.2.4]
), and
the overall structure of
\mathrm{Ver}^{n}_{1}
remains mysterious (even in the case
n=2
) despite its fundamental significance.
In order to advance the discussion beyond this point,
we propose a new approach that connects the problem to the enumeration of certain combinatorial objects, via the theory of higher-level dormant opers developed in
[
Wak7
]
.

1.2.
Comparison with dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
-opers of higher level

In the remainder of this introduction,

A central object in our discussion is

-oper

-destabilized

Let

Moreover, for each

with
N^{\prime}\leq N
,
the following square diagram commutes:

\textstyle{T_{a}SU^{2}_{X^{(N)}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\eta_{N}}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\scriptstyle{d\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{N\Rightarrow N^{\prime}}|_{a}}
\textstyle{T_{b}\mathcal{O}p_{N,g}^{{}^{\mathrm{Zzz...}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

In other words,

) is fully compatible with that of the sequence

(1.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}p_{N,g}^{{}^{\mathrm{Zzz...}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}p_{N-1,g}^{{}^{\mathrm{Zzz...}}}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\mathcal{O}p_{2,g}^{{}^{\mathrm{Zzz...}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}p_{1,g}^{{}^{\mathrm{Zzz...}}}

established in

, Theorem C, (i)]
, leads to

Theorem A

(cf. Corollary
4.11
)
.

Let
\Pi_{N}
denote
the natural projection
\mathcal{O}p_{N,g}^{{}^{\mathrm{Zzz...}}}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
, which is
finite and faithfully flat (cf.

(1.11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(\Pi_{N})\geq\frac{\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{1}^{2})^{N-1}\cdot p^{g-1}}{2^{2g-1}}\cdot\sum_{\theta=1}^{p-1}\frac{1}{\sin^{2g-2}\big(\frac{\pi\cdot\theta}{p}\big)}.

Moreover, if
X
is general (cf. Section
1.4
), then this inequality “
\geq
” becomes an equality “
=
”.
In particular, the resulting formula gives an affirmative solution to a strong form (for
n=2
) of the conjecture stated in
[
Wak7
, Conjecture 10.26]
(cf. Remark
4.12
).

1.3.
Explicit computations via edge numberings on a trivalent graph

A key point of the preceding theorem is that the left-hand side of (
1.11
) can be computed explicitly by applying results from
[
Wak7
]
.
In fact, the decomposition of the degree
\mathrm{deg}(\Pi_{N})
with respect to various clutching morphisms on moduli spaces of curves is collectively described through a
2
d TQFT (= a
2
-dimensional topological quantum field theory).
This
2
d TQFT provides a combinatorial procedure for computing
\mathrm{deg}(\Pi_{N})
by reducing
the problem to the case where the underlying curve is totally degenerate.
In this degenerate case, each component of its normalization is isomorphic to a
3
-pointed projective line, and dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}^{(N)}
-opers on such curves are determined entirely by combinatorial data associated with their
radii
(cf.
[
Moc
, Chapter I, Definition 4.1]
).

Let us fix a finite connected trivalent graph
\mathbb{G}
of genus
g
and a totally degenerate curve
X_{\mathbb{G}}
whose dual graph coincides with
\mathbb{G}
(cf. the discussion preceding Definition
5.2
).
By a
balanced
(p,N)
-edge numbering
on
\mathbb{G}
, we mean a collection of nonnegative integers
(a_{e})_{e}
, indexed by
the edges
e

of
\mathbb{G}
, such that for every triple of edges
(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})
(with multiplicity) incident to a common vertex, the triple
(a_{e_{1}},a_{e_{2}},a_{e_{3}})
satisfies certain prescribed conditions, including specific types of triangle inequalities (cf. Definition
5.2
for the precise definition).
We denote the set of balanced
(p,N)
-edge numberings on
\mathbb{G}
by

(1.12)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ed}_{p,N,\mathbb{G}}

(cf. (
5.3
)).
This set is finite, and one can explicitly determine which combinations of nonnegative integers belong to it.

According to the theory of diagonal reduction/lifting and Gauss hypergeometric differential operators in characteristic
p^{N}
, as developed in
[
Wak7
]
,
dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}^{(N)}
-opers on
X_{\mathbb{G}}

correspond bijectively to elements of
\mathrm{Ed}_{p,N,\mathbb{G}}
.
(This correspondence generalizes results for
N=1
, discussed in
[
LiOs
]
,
[
Moc
]
,
[
Wak2
]
, and
[
Wak5
]
.)
In particular, Theorem
A
implies the following result, which constitutes the second main result of this paper.

Theorem B

(cf. Theorems
5.3
and
5.5
)
.

Suppose that
X
is a general smooth projective curve classified by
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
.
Then, the following assertions hold:

(i)

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a finite connected trivalent graph of genus
g
.
Then, the following equality holds:

(1.13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{1})=\frac{\sharp(\mathrm{Ed}_{p,2,\mathbb{G}})}{\sharp(\mathrm{Ed}_{p,1,\mathbb{G}})}\left(=\frac{\sharp(\mathrm{Ed}_{p,2,\mathbb{G}})\cdot 2^{2g-1}}{p^{g-1}}\cdot\left(\sum_{\theta=1}^{p-1}\frac{1}{\sin^{2g-2}\big(\frac{\pi\cdot\theta}{p}\big)}\right)^{-1}\right).

(ii)

There exists a quasi-polynomial
Q(t)
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
of degree
3g-3
(depending only on

) that satisfies

(1.14)

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{1}^{2})=Q(p).

Moreover, the leading coefficient of

coincides with
\frac{(-1)^{g}\cdot 2^{3g-4}\cdot B_{2g-2}}{(2g-2)!}
, where

-th Bernoulli number.

As a consequence, the equality (

-edge numberings for

Suppose that the genus of

equals

Then, the generic degree
\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{1})

(1.15)

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{1})=\frac{1}{45}\cdot\left(2p^{6}+5p^{4}+38p^{2}\right).

, a prime number

, and an algebraically closed field

.
Also, fix a proper, smooth, and connected curve

of genus

over

.
Denote by

the sheaf of Kähler differentials (i.e.,
1
-forms) on
X
over
k

.

Let

denote the absolute Frobenius endomorphism of

.
For a positive integer
N
, the
N
-th Frobenius twist
of
X
over
k
is, by definition, the base-change

of
X
along the
N
-th iterate of the absolute Frobenius endomorphism of

.
The morphism

is called the

of

over

.
For convenience, we write

,

, and

.

We shall write
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathrm{SL}_{2})\left(=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathrm{PGL}_{2})\right)
, i.e., the Lie algebra of the special linear group

over

(identified with that of the projective linear group

).
Given a rank
2

\mathcal{E}
on a curve

(such as

or

for some

),
we shall write
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}}
for the vector bundle associated to
\mathcal{E}
via the adjoint representation
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Thus,

\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}}
is identified with the sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-linear endomorphisms of
\mathcal{E}
with vanishing trace.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{g}
denote the moduli stack of proper, smooth, and connected curves of genus
g
over
k
, and let
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
denote its Deligne-Mumford compactification, which parametrizes stable curves of genus

.
Since
\mathcal{M}_{g}
is
an irreducible

(cf.
[

, Section 5]
),
it makes sense to speak of a “

\mathcal{M}_{g}
that lies outside a fixed closed substack not equal to
\mathcal{M}_{g}
itself.

For a rational map
f:Y\dashrightarrow Z
between schemes with

we write
\mathrm{Dom}(f)
for the domain of definition of

, i.e., the largest open subscheme of
Y

f
is defined.

Any morphism of sheaves of abelian groups

can be regarded as a complex of sheaves concentrated at degrees

and
1
;
we denote this complex by
\mathcal{K}^{\bullet}[\nabla]
.
For each

, we denote by
\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\mathcal{K}^{\bullet}[\nabla])
the

-th hypercohomology group of
\mathcal{K}^{\bullet}[\nabla]
.
Given an integer
n
and a sheaf
\mathcal{E}
, we define the complex
\mathcal{E}[n]
to be
\mathcal{E}
(considered as a complex concentrated at degree

) shifted down by

, so that
\mathcal{E}[n]^{-n}=\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}[n]^{i}=0
(

).

2.
Generalized Verschiebung maps

In this section, we recall the definition of the generalized Verschiebung map between moduli spaces of stable bundles and describe its local structure in terms of cohomology groups.

2.1.
Moduli space of stable bundles

For a nonnegative integer
N
, we denote by

(2.1)

\displaystyle SU^{2}_{X^{(N)}}

the moduli space of rank
2
stable bundles on
X^{(N)}
with trivial determinant.
It is known that
SU^{2}_{X^{(N)}}
is represented by a smooth irreducible variety over
k
of dimension
3g-3
(cf.
[
MeSu
, Lemma A]
).

Given another nonnegative integer
N^{\prime}\leq N
,
the pull-back
\mathcal{E}\mapsto F^{(N-N^{\prime})*}_{X^{(N^{\prime})}/k}(\mathcal{E})
by
F^{(N-N^{\prime})}_{X^{(N^{\prime})}/k}
gives rise to a rational map

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{N\Rightarrow N^{\prime}}:SU^{2}_{X^{(N)}}\dashrightarrow SU^{2}_{X^{(N^{\prime})}}

over
k
.
The domain of definition
\mathrm{Dom}(\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{N\Rightarrow N^{\prime}})
classifies vector bundles
\mathcal{E}\in SU^{2}_{X^{(N)}}
whose pull-back
by
F^{(N-N^{\prime})}_{X^{(N^{\prime})}/k}
remain stable (cf.
[
Oss1
, Theorem A.6]
).
Since a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
on
X^{(N^{\prime})}
is stable whenever its pull-back
F^{(N^{\prime}-N^{\prime\prime})*}_{X^{(N^{\prime})}/k}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
(for
N^{\prime\prime}\leq N^{\prime}
) is stable,
we obtain the following chain of inclusions:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dom}(\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{N\Rightarrow 0})\subseteq\mathrm{Dom}(\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{N\Rightarrow 1})\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathrm{Dom}(\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{N\Rightarrow N-1})\subseteq\mathrm{Dom}(\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{N\Rightarrow N})=SU^{2}_{X^{(N)}}.

For simplicity, we write
\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{N}:=\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{N\Rightarrow 0}
.

It is well-known that
\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{N\Rightarrow N^{\prime}}
is generically finite, so one may consider its generic degree, denoted by
\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{N\Rightarrow N^{\prime}})
.
To be precise, there exists a dense open subscheme
U_{\mathrm{ff}}
of
SU_{X^{(N^{\prime})}}^{2}
such that
\mathrm{Ver}_{N\Rightarrow N^{\prime}}^{2}
restricts to a finite and faithfully flat morphism
(\mathrm{Ver}_{N\Rightarrow N^{\prime}}^{2})^{-1}(U_{\mathrm{ff}})\rightarrow U_{\mathrm{ff}}
(cf.
[
Har
, Chapter II, Exercise 3.7]
).
Moreover, if we assume that
X
is general in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
, then
\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{N\Rightarrow N^{\prime}}
is verified to be generically étale (cf.
[
MeSu
, Corollary 2.1.1]
).
Under this assumption, we can find a dense open subscheme
U_{\text{f\'{e}}}
of
U_{\mathrm{ff}}
such that
\mathrm{Ver}_{N\Rightarrow N^{\prime}}^{2}
restricts to a finite and étale morphism
(\mathrm{Ver}_{N\Rightarrow N^{\prime}}^{2})^{-1}(U_{\text{f\'{e}}})\rightarrow U_{\text{f\'{e}}}
.

2.2.
Differential of the generalized Verschiebung map

Let
N
and
N^{\prime}
be nonnegative integers with
N^{\prime}\leq N
, and let

a
be a
k
-rational point of
\mathrm{Dom}(\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{N\Rightarrow N^{\prime}})
.
This point determines a stable bundle
\mathcal{E}
, whose pull-back
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}:=F^{(N-N^{\prime})*}_{X^{(N^{\prime})}}(\mathcal{E})
by
F^{(N-N^{\prime})}_{X^{(N^{\prime})}}
remains stable.
Denote by
T_{a}SU^{2}_{X^{(N)}}
(resp.,
T_{a^{\prime}}SU_{X^{(N^{\prime})}}
) the tangent space of
SU^{2}_{X^{(N)}}
(resp.,
SU^{2}_{X^{(N^{\prime})}}
) at
a
(resp.,
a^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Ver}_{N\Rightarrow N^{\prime}}^{2}(a)
).
From general deformation theory of vector bundles,
we have
a natural isomorphism of
k
-vector spaces

(2.4)

\displaystyle\tau_{su}:T_{a}SU^{2}_{X^{(N)}}\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{1}(X^{(N)},\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}})\ \left(\text{resp.,}\ \tau^{\prime}_{su}:T_{a^{\prime}}SU^{2}_{X^{(N^{\prime})}}\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{1}(X^{(N^{\prime})},\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}})\right).

Moreover,
if
d\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{N\Rightarrow N^{\prime}}|_{a}
denotes the
k
-linear morphism

T_{a}SU^{2}_{X^{(N)}}\rightarrow T_{a^{\prime}}SU^{2}_{X^{(N^{\prime})}}
obtained by differentiating
\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{N\Rightarrow N^{\prime}}
at
a
,
then the following square diagram commutes:

\textstyle{T_{a}SU^{2}_{X^{(N)}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tau_{su}}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\scriptstyle{d\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{N\Rightarrow N^{\prime}}|_{a}}
\textstyle{H^{1}(X^{(N)},\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F^{(N-N^{\prime})*}_{X^{(N^{\prime})}}}
\textstyle{T_{a^{\prime}}SU_{X^{(N^{\prime})}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\scriptstyle{\tau^{\prime}_{su}}
\textstyle{H^{1}(X^{(N^{\prime})},\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}),}

where the right-hand vertical arrow is the morphism induced from pull-back by
F^{(N-N^{\prime})}_{X^{(N^{\prime})}}
.
It follows that
\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{N\Rightarrow N^{\prime}}
is étale at the point
a
if and only if the right-hand vertical arrow of this square diagram is an isomorphism.

3.
Dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
-opers of higher level

This section deals with dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
-opers of higher level, which were introduced in
[
Wak4
]
and
[
Wak7
]
as dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
-opers enhanced by actions of differential operators of finite level in the sense of P. Berthelot.
We also review a cohomological description of their deformation spaces, established in
[
Wak7
]
(cf. Proposition
3.5

3.1.
\mathcal{D}
-modules of higher level

Fix a positive integer
N
.
Following
[

]
,
the sheaf of differential operators
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}:=\mathcal{D}_{X/k}^{(N-1)}
on
X/k
of level
N-1
is defined, where
\mathrm{Spec}(k)
is equipped with the trivial
(N-1)
-PD structure.

For each

, we write
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}_{\leq j}
for the subsheaf of
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
consisting of differential operators of order at most

, where
\mathcal{D}_{\leq\infty}^{(N-1)}:=\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
.
In particular, we have
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\mathcal{D}_{\leq j}^{(N-1)}
.
Note that
\mathcal{D}_{\leq j}^{(N-1)}
carries two distinct
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module structures, i.e., one as given by left multiplication, where we denote this
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module by
{{}^{L}}\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}_{\leq j}
, and the other given by right multiplication, where we denote this
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module by
{{}^{R}}\mathcal{D}_{\leq j}^{(N-1)}
.
For an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{E}
, we equip the tensor product
\mathcal{D}_{\leq j}^{(N-1)}\otimes\mathcal{E}:={{}^{R}}\mathcal{D}_{\leq j}^{(N-1)}\otimes\mathcal{E}
with the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module structure given by left multiplication.

A
(left)
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
-module structure
on
\mathcal{E}
is a left
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
-action
\nabla:{{}^{L}}\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}nd_{k}(\mathcal{E})
on
\mathcal{E}
extending its
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module structure.
When it is necessary to emphasize the level “
N-1
”, we write
\nabla^{(N-1)}
instead of
\nabla
.
A
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
-bundle
is defined as a pair
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
consisting of a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
, and a
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
-module structure
\nabla
on
\mathcal{E}
.
For example, the pair
(\mathcal{O}_{X},\nabla_{\mathrm{triv}}^{(N-1)})
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
and the natural
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
-module structure
\nabla^{(N-1)}_{\mathrm{triv}}
on it forms a (line)
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
-bundle.
In the case of
N=1
, giving a
\mathcal{D}^{(0)}
-module structure on a
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{E}
amounts to giving a connection on
\mathcal{E}
, i.e, a
k
-linear morphism
\nabla:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\Omega_{X}\otimes\mathcal{E}
satisfying the Leibniz rule
\nabla(a\cdot v)=da\otimes v+a\cdot\nabla(v)
for any
a\in\mathcal{O}_{X}
and
v\in\mathcal{E}
.
(Since
X
is of dimension
1
, any such connection is automatically flat, i.e., has vanishing curvature.)

Now, let
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
be
a
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
-bundle.
It is known that the natural

\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules

[

, Section 2.5]

The composite

(3.1)

\displaystyle\psi(\nabla):\mathcal{T}^{\otimes p^{N}}_{X}\xrightarrow{\psi_{X}}\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}_{\leq p^{N}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{inclusion}}\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}\xrightarrow{\nabla}\mathcal{E}nd_{k}(\mathcal{E}),

is called the

-curvature
of

(cf.
[
GLQ
]
,
[
LeQu
, Definition 3.1.1]
).

Denote by

the subsheaf of
\mathcal{E}
on which
\mathcal{D}_{+}^{(N-1)}
acts as zero via
\nabla
, where
\mathcal{D}_{+}^{(N-1)}

\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}

This sheaf can be regarded as an
\mathcal{O}_{X^{(N)}}
-submodule of

via the underlying homeomorphism of

.

For a vector bundle
\mathcal{G}
on

(or more generally, an
\mathcal{O}_{X^{(N)}}
-module),
there exists a canonical

\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
-module structure

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathcal{G},\mathrm{can}}^{(N-1)}:{{}^{L}}\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}nd_{k}(F_{X/k}^{(N)*}(\mathcal{G}))

on the pull-back

[

The resulting assignments

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}\text{the category of}\\
\text{vector bundles on $X^{(N)}$}\end{matrix}\right)\xrightarrow{\sim}\left(\begin{matrix}\text{the category of $\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}$-bundles}\\
\text{with vanishing $p^{N}$-curvature}\end{matrix}\right)

[
LeQu
, Corollary 3.2.4]

\oplus
”, tensor product “
\otimes
”, determinant “
\mathrm{det}
”, and internal Hom “
\mathcal{H}om
”.

3.2.
Dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}^{(N)}
-opers and their moduli stack

Recall from
[

, Definitions 5.6, 5.7]
that a
dormant
\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{(N)}
-oper
on

is
a triple

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}^{\heartsuit}:=(\mathcal{F},\nabla,\mathcal{L})

consisting of a
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
-bundle
(\mathcal{F},\nabla)
of rank
2
and a line subbundle
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{F}
satisfying the following conditions:

•

\nabla
has vanishing
p^{N}
-curvature;

•

The composite

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathrm{KS}_{\mathscr{F}^{\heartsuit}}:\mathcal{D}_{\leq 1}^{(N-1)}\otimes\mathcal{L}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{inclusion}}\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}\otimes\mathcal{F}\xrightarrow{\nabla}\mathcal{F}

is an isomorphism.

We refer to
\mathcal{L}
as the

of
\mathscr{F}^{\heartsuit}
.
Also, a
dormant
\mathrm{SL}_{2}^{(N)}
-oper
is defined as a dormant
\mathrm{GL}_{n}^{(N)}
-oper equipped with an isomorphism of line
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
-bundles
(\mathrm{det}(\mathcal{F}),\mathrm{det}(\nabla))\xrightarrow{\sim}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\nabla^{(N-1)}_{\mathrm{triv}})
,
where
\mathrm{det}(\nabla)
denotes the
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
-module structure on the determinant
\mathrm{det}(\mathcal{F})
induced naturally from
\nabla
.

The notion of an isomorphism between dormant
\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{(N)}
-opers (resp., dormant
\mathrm{SL}_{2}^{(N)}
-opers) is formulated in a natural manner.
Hence, one obtains the category of dormant
\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{(N)}
-opers (resp., dormant
\mathrm{SL}_{2}^{(N)}
-opers) on
X
.

Remark 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{F},\nabla)
be a
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
-bundle of rank
2
, and suppose that there exists a line subbundle
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
such that the triple
(\mathcal{F},\nabla,\mathcal{L})
forms a dormant
\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{(N)}
-oper.
The isomorphism
\mathrm{KS}_{\mathscr{F}^{\heartsuit}}:\mathcal{D}_{\leq 1}^{(N-1)}\otimes\mathcal{L}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{F}

implies

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{L})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{deg}((\mathcal{D}_{\leq 1}^{(N-1)}\otimes\mathcal{L})/(\mathcal{D}_{\leq 0}^{(N-1)}\otimes\mathcal{L}))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{T}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{L})

\displaystyle=2-2g+\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{L})

\displaystyle<\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{L}).

This strict inequality shows that such a line subbundle
\mathcal{L}
is uniquely determined.
Therefore, the category of dormant
\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{(N)}
-opers can be regarded as a full subcategory of
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
-bundles.

Two dormant
\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{(N)}
-opers
\mathscr{F}^{\heartsuit}_{i}:=(\mathcal{F}_{i},\nabla_{i},\mathcal{L}_{i})
(
i=1,2
) on
X
are said to be
equivalent

if there exists a line
\mathcal{D}^{(N-1)}
-bundle
\mathscr{N}:=(\mathcal{N},\nabla_{\mathcal{N}})
with vanishing
p^{N}
-curvature such that the twisted dormant
\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{(N)}
-oper

(\mathscr{F}_{1}^{\heartsuit})_{\otimes\mathscr{N}}:=(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{N},\nabla\otimes\nabla_{\mathcal{N}},\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{N})
is isomorphic to
\mathscr{F}^{\heartsuit}_{2}
.
This binary relation forms an equivalence relation on the set of dormant
\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{(N)}
-opers on
X
, and we define a
dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}^{(N)}
-oper
as an equivalence class
\mathscr{F}^{\spadesuit}:=[\mathscr{F}^{\heartsuit}]
of a dormant
\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{(N)}
-oper
\mathscr{F}^{\heartsuit}
.

Remark 3.2

The notion of a dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}^{(N)}
-oper can be extended, within the framework of logarithmic geometry, to the case where the underlying space is a (pointed) stable curve (cf.

However, for simplicity of our discussion, we omit the details of the generalized formulation in this paper.

4.1.
Maximally Frobenius-destabilized bundles

Let

be a positive integer and

The

(4.1)

\displaystyle s_{1}(\mathcal{E}):=\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{E})-2\cdot\mathrm{max}\left\{\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{L})\,|\,\text{$\mathcal{L}$ is a line subbundle of $\mathcal{E}$}\right\}.

This assumption implies

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{L}\otimes(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{L})^{\vee})=2\cdot\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{L})-\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{E})=-s_{1}(\mathcal{E})>2g-2.

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L})\cong H^{1}(X,\mathcal{L}\otimes(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{L})^{\vee})=0,

is étale.
Hence, the second assertion follows from Theorem
4.5
, (ii), and the comment in Remark

.

Moreover, by Proposition
4.6
,
each connected component of

contains at least one point mapped to
a
.
By the étalneness stated in the second assertion,

\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{N\Rightarrow N^{\prime}}
induces an isomorphism between the formal completion at this point and

.
It follows that

decomposes as the disjoint union of finitely many copies of

, and the number of its components coincides with
\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{N\Rightarrow N^{\prime}})
.
This completes the proof of the first assertion.
∎

4.4.
Degrees of

and

One can apply Proposition

established above in order to relate the generic degree of the generalized Verschiebung map and the degree of the projection

.
As a result, we obtain
the following theorem.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
N
and
N^{\prime}
be positive integers with

.
Then, the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(\Pi_{N\Rightarrow N^{\prime}})\geq\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{N\Rightarrow N^{\prime}})\left(=\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{1})^{N-N^{\prime}}\right).

If, moreover,

Proof.

As explained in

) with “

=
” under the assumption that

In this case,

Hence, under the correspondences (

) and (

5.1.
Balanced
(p,N)
-edge numberings

For each positive integer
N
,
we shall set

(5.1)

\displaystyle{{}^{\dagger}}C_{N}

(cf.
[
Wak7
, Section 10.2]
) to be the set of triples of nonnegative integers
(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
satisfying the following conditions:

•

\sum_{i=1}^{3}s_{i}\leq p^{N}-2
and
|s_{2}-s_{3}|\leq s_{1}\leq s_{2}+s_{3}
;

•

For every positive integer
N^{\prime}<N
, there exists a triple
(s^{\prime}_{1},s^{\prime}_{2},s^{\prime}_{3})
with
s^{\prime}_{i}\in\{[s_{i}]_{N^{\prime}},p^{N^{\prime}}-1-[s_{i}]_{N^{\prime}}\}
for
i=1,2,3
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{3}s^{\prime}_{i}\leq p^{N^{\prime}}-2
and
|s^{\prime}_{2}-s^{\prime}_{3}|\leq s^{\prime}_{1}\leq s^{\prime}_{2}+s^{\prime}_{3}
.

Here, for each nonnegative integer
a
, we set
[a]_{N^{\prime}}

to be the remainder obtained by dividing
a
by
p^{N^{\prime}}
.
Note that the second condition can be equivalently expressed as requiring that, for every integer
N^{\prime}<N
,
the triple
(\hat{s}_{1},\hat{s}_{2},\hat{s}_{3})
satisfies

\sum_{i=1}^{3}\hat{s}_{i}\leq p^{N^{\prime}}-2
and
|\hat{s}_{2}-\hat{s}_{3}|\leq\hat{s}_{1}\leq\hat{s}_{2}+\hat{s}_{3}
, where

(5.2)

\displaystyle\hat{a}:=\frac{p^{N^{\prime}}-1}{2}-\left|a-p^{N^{\prime}}\cdot\left\lfloor\frac{a}{p^{N^{\prime}}}\right\rfloor-\frac{p^{N^{\prime}}-1}{2}\right|

for each nonnegative integer
a
.

Remark 5.1
.

In
[
Wak7
]
(and
[
Moc
]
for
N=1
), a characterization
of dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}^{(N)}
-opers on a
3
-pointed projective line was provided in terms of specific combinations of nonnegative integers.
This characterization is derived by examining the radii (i.e., the eigenvalues of the residue matrices) of such opers at the marked points.
As a consequence of the theory of diagonal reduction/lifting and Gauss hypergeometric differential operators in characteristic
p^{N}
,
we obtain a bijective correspondence between dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}^{(N)}
-opers on this curve and elements of
{{}^{\dagger}}C_{N}
(cf.
[
Wak7
, Theorem 10.11]
).

Next, let us fix
a finite connected trivalent graph
\mathbb{G}
(without open edges), in the sense of
[
Wak7
, Definition 6.5, (i)-(iii)]
;
this consists of a vertex set
V
and an edge set
E
, each of whose element has cardinality
2
(with
e\cap e^{\prime}=\emptyset
if
e\neq e^{\prime}
), and a map
\zeta:\coprod_{e\in E}e\rightarrow V
.
In particular, we have
\sharp(V),\sharp(E)<\infty
.
We refer to any element
b
of
e\in E
as a branch of
e
.
For each vertex
v\in V
, we shall write
B_{v}:=\zeta^{-1}(v)
, which is the set of branches
incident to
v
(hence
\sharp(B_{v})=3
).
Assume further that
\mathbb{G}
has genus
g
, i.e., is of type
(g,0)
, in the sense of
[
Wak7
, Definition 6.5, (iv)]
.
That is, when we regard
\mathbb{G}
as a topological space in the usual way (cf.
[
Wak3
, Remark 7.3]
), this assumption requires that the first Betti number
b_{1}(\mathbb{G}):=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{Q}}(H_{1}(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{Q}))
coincides with
g
.

Definition 5.2
.

A

(p,N)
-edge numbering
on
\mathbb{G}
is a collection

(a_{e})_{e\in E}

of nonnegative integers indexed by the elements of

such that, for each vertex
v\in V
, the triple
(a_{\zeta(b)})_{b\in B_{v}}
belongs to
{{}^{\dagger}}C_{N}
.

We denote the set of all balanced
(p,N)
-edge numberings on
\mathbb{G}
by

(5.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ed}_{p,N,\mathbb{G}}.

This set contains finitely many elements, and its cardinality can be computed by explicitly enumerating balanced
(p,N)
-edge numberings.
For example, when
\mathbb{G}
has genus
3

(5.4)

\displaystyle\sharp(\mathrm{Ed}_{3,2,\mathbb{G}})=49,\ \ \ \sharp(\mathrm{Ed}_{5,2,\mathbb{G}})=11775,\ \ \ \sharp(\mathrm{Ed}_{7,2,\mathbb{G}})=542626,\ \ \ \sharp(\mathrm{Ed}_{11,2,\mathbb{G}})=107098915,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\sharp(\mathrm{Ed}_{3,3,\mathbb{G}})=2401,\ \ \ \sharp(\mathrm{Ed}_{5,3,\mathbb{G}})=9243375,\ \ \ \sharp(\mathrm{Ed}_{7,3,\mathbb{G}})=3004520162,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\sharp(\mathrm{Ed}_{3,4,\mathbb{G}})=117649,\ \ \ \sharp(\mathrm{Ed}_{5,4,\mathbb{G}})=7256049375.

(For details of the following discussion, we refer to
[
Wak7
, Section 10.5]

Recall that a stable curve is said to be

if it is obtained by gluing together finitely many copies of a
3
-pointed projective line, along their marked points to form nodal points (cf.
[
Wak7
, Definition 6.25]
for the precise definition).
One can find a totally degenerate curve
X_{\mathbb{G}}
of genus
g
whose dual graph is given by
\mathbb{G}
.
In particular, the vertices in
\mathbb{G}
represent irreducible components, and the edges represent nodal points.

Since the projection
\Pi_{N}
is étale over all the points of totally degenerate curves (cf.
[

, Theorem C, (i)]
),
the degree
\mathrm{deg}(\Pi_{N})
coincides with the total number of dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}^{(N)}
-opers on

.
Moreover, each such dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}^{(N)}
-oper can be constructed by gluing dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}^{(N)}
-opers on the components of its normalization (which is isomorphic to a
3
-pointed projective line)
in accordance with the attachment of underlying curves
at the marked points.
As noted in Remark
5.1
,
these componentwise opers are classified by their radii, which are identified with elements of
{{}^{\dagger}}C_{N}
.
Therefore, a global dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}^{(N)}
-opers on
X_{\mathbb{G}}
corresponds to an edge numberings such that, for every vertex, the numbers on incident edges form a triple in

{{}^{\dagger}}C_{N}
.
This gives a bijective correspondence between dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}^{(N)}
-opers on
X_{\mathbb{G}}
and elements of
\mathrm{Ed}_{p,N,\mathbb{G}}
.
Consequently, we obtain the following equality:

(5.7)

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(\Pi_{N})=\sharp(\mathrm{Ed}_{p,N,\mathbb{G}})

(cf.
[
Wak7
, Theorem E]
).
This fact
and Corollary
4.11

together imply the following assertion.

Theorem 5.3

(cf. Theorem
B
, (i))
.

Let us keep the above notation, and
suppose that
X
is general in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
.
Then,
the following equality holds:

(5.8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{1})=\frac{\sharp(\mathrm{Ed}_{p,N+1,\mathbb{G}})}{\sharp(\mathrm{Ed}_{p,N,\mathbb{G}})}.\left(=\frac{\sharp(\mathrm{Ed}_{p,2,\mathbb{G}})\cdot 2^{2g-1}}{p^{g-1}}\cdot\left(\sum_{\theta=1}^{p-1}\frac{1}{\sin^{2g-2}\big(\frac{\pi\cdot\theta}{p}\big)}\right)^{-1}\right).

5.2.
Explicit computation for

In this final subsection, we consider a polynomial expression for
\mathrm{Ver}_{1}^{2}
.
According to
[
Wak7

\mathrm{Ed}_{p,N,\mathbb{G}}
correspond to the lattice points in a certain rational polytope
embedded in an
\mathbb{R}
-vector space.
In particular, one may apply Ehrhart’s theory of lattice-point enumeration for rational polytopes to this setting.
As shown in
[
Wak7

there exists a quasi-polynomial

of degree
(3g-3)\cdot N
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
whose minimum period divides

\displaystyle\sharp(\mathrm{Ed}_{p,N,\mathbb{G}})=H_{N}(p)

for every odd prime
p
.

Since
\sharp(\mathrm{Ed}_{p,1,\mathbb{G}})\left(=H_{1}(p)\right)
has been already computed in prior work,
it follows from Theorem

that determining
\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{1}^{2})
reduces to

.
To this end,

(p,2)
-edge numberings on
\mathbb{G}
for some values of

The following two assertions may slightly reduce the computational burden

Proposition 5.4
.

The leading coefficient of
H_{1}(t)
(resp.,
H_{2}(t)
) coincides with

\frac{(-1)^{g}\cdot B_{2g-2}}{2\cdot(2g-2)!}
(resp.,
\frac{2^{3g-5}\cdot B^{2}_{2g-2}}{((2g-2)!)^{2}}

Let us consider the rational polytope

\displaystyle 0\leq x,\ \ \ 0\leq y,\ \ \ 0\leq z,\ \ \ x+y+z\leq 1,\ \ \text{and}\ \ \ |y-z|\leq x\leq y+z.

In particular,

Denote by

on the edge set

(3g-3)

forms its lattice.
Each element of
\mathbb{R}^{E}
(resp.,
\mathbb{Z}^{E}

(resp.,

).

We now define

\mathcal{P}
to be the subset of

such that, for each

the triple

\mathcal{P}_{0}
.
As established in
[

]

By the discussion in

Next,

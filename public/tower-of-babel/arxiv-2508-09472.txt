Planar Hall effects in X X -wave magnets with X = p , d , g , f , i X=p,d,g,f,i
Motohiko Ezawa

Abstract
The planar Hall effect is a phenomenon that the Hall conductivity emerges perpendicular to the electric field in the presence of an in-plane magnetic field. We investigate the planar Hall effect in two-dimensional higher symmetric X X -wave magnets with X = p , d , f , g , i X=p,d,f,g,i , where those with X = d , g , i X=d,g,i are altermagnets. The X X -wave magnet is characterized by the number N X N_{X} of the nodes in the band structure, where N X = 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 6 N_{X}=1,2,3,4,6 corresponding to X = p , d , f , g , i X=p,d,f,g,i . We demonstrate that the Hall conductivities are almost half quantized and well approximated by the formula σ x ​ y = ± ( e 2 / 2 ​ h ) \sigma_{xy}=\pm(e^{2}/2h) sgn ( J sin N X Φ ) \left(J\sin N_{X}\Phi\right) , where J J is the coefficient of the coupling between the X X -wave magnet and the electrons, and Φ \Phi is the direction of the applied magnetic field. Hence, the Hall conductivity has the periodicity with respect to Φ \Phi , and the periodicity is equal to the number N X N_{X} of the nodes. This property may be used to confirm that the target material is indeed an X X -wave magnet. Furthermore, the sign of J J may be used as a bit for antiferromagnetic spintronics.

II.2
Hall conductivity

The two-band Hamiltonian (
2
) is summarized in the form of

H=h_{0}+\sum_{j=x,y,z}h_{j}\sigma_{j}.

(9)

We define a normalized vector

\mathbf{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\equiv\mathbf{h}/|\mathbf{h}|=\left(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta\right).

(10)

The Berry curvature is expressed in terms of the solid angle of the vector

;

;

\Omega\left(\mathbf{k}\right)=-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{n}\cdot\left(\partial_{x}\mathbf{n}\times\partial_{y}\mathbf{n}\right).

The integration of the Berry curvature over whole Brillouin zone gives the
Chern number

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\text{BZ}}\Omega\left(\mathbf{k}\right)d\mathbf{k}=C.

(12)

It is interpreted as the wrapping number of the vector

known as
the Pontryagin number.

According to the Thouless-Kohmoto-Nightingale-den Nijs formula, the Hall
conductivity is related to the Berry curvature as

\sigma_{xy}=\frac{e^{2}}{2\pi h}\sum_{n}\int d^{2}k\,f\left(E_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right)\Omega_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right),

(13)

where
f\left(E_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right)=1/[\exp\frac{E_{n}(\mathbf{k})-\mu}{k_{\text{B}}T}+1]
is the Fermi distribution function, and

\mu
is the chemical potential. In an insulator at zero temperature, the
Hall conductivity is proportional to the Chern number,

\sigma_{xy}=\frac{e^{2}}{h}C.

(14)

In the Dirac system, the Chern number is half quantized if the chemical
potential is within the Dirac gap. Hence, the Hall conductivity per Dirac cone is given by

\sigma_{xy}=\pm\frac{e^{2}}{2h}.

(15)

This quantization formula is exact only for insulators.

IV
Planar Hall effects

The Dirac point shifts in the presence of the in-plane magnetic field. The
shifted Dirac point is given by
(k_{x}^{\prime},k_{y}^{\prime})=0
, where

k_{x}^{\prime}=k_{x}+\frac{B_{y}}{\lambda},\qquad k_{y}^{\prime}=k_{y}-\frac{B_{x}}{\lambda},

(26)

by solving the equation

\lambda\left(k_{x}\sigma_{y}-k_{y}\sigma_{x}\right)+B_{x}\sigma_{x}+B_{y}\sigma_{y}=0.

(27)

In terms of the shifted momentum, the Hamiltonian (
2
) is given by

H=H_{\text{kine}}^{\prime}+H_{\text{R}}^{\prime}+H_{\text{mass}}^{\prime},

(28)

up to the first order of
k_{x}^{\prime}
and
k_{y}^{\prime}
, where
H_{\text{kine}}^{\prime}
is the kinetic energy at the shifted Dirac point,

H_{\text{kine}}^{\prime}=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\left[\left(\frac{B}{\lambda}\right)^{2}-\frac{2B_{y}}{\lambda}k_{x}^{\prime}+\frac{2B_{x}}{\lambda}k_{y}^{\prime}\right],

(29)

H_{\text{R}}^{\prime}
is the Rashba interaction term,

H_{\text{R}}^{\prime}=\lambda\left(k_{x}^{\prime}\sigma_{y}-k_{y}^{\prime}\sigma_{x}\right),

(30)

and
H_{\text{mass}}^{\prime}
is the Dirac mass term,

H_{\text{mass}}^{\prime}=J\left(\Delta+g_{x}k_{x}^{\prime}+g_{y}k_{y}^{\prime}\right)\sigma_{z},

(31)

with

\Delta\equiv f_{X}\left(k_{x}^{\prime}=0,k_{y}^{\prime}=0\right),

(32)

and

g_{x}\equiv\frac{\partial f_{X}\left(k_{x}^{\prime},k_{y}^{\prime}\right)}{\partial k_{x}^{\prime}},\quad g_{y}\equiv\frac{\partial f_{X}\left(k_{x}^{\prime},k_{y}^{\prime}\right)}{\partial k_{y}^{\prime}}.

(33)

Then, the Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle H=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\left[\left(\frac{B}{\lambda}\right)^{2}-\frac{2B_{y}}{\lambda}k_{x}^{\prime}+\frac{2B_{x}}{\lambda}k_{y}^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle+\lambda\left(k_{x}^{\prime}\sigma_{y}-k_{y}^{\prime}\sigma_{x}\right)

\displaystyle+J\left(\Delta+g_{x}k_{x}^{\prime}+g_{y}k_{y}^{\prime}\right)\sigma_{z}.

(34)

The Berry curvature (
11
) is calculated as

\Omega=-\frac{J\Delta\lambda^{2}}{2\left(\lambda^{2}k^{2}+\left(J\Delta+Jg_{x}k_{x}^{\prime}+Jg_{y}k_{y}^{\prime}\right)^{2}\right)^{3/2}}

(35)

for the bottom band.

The integral of the Berry curvature over the whole Brillouin zone gives an
exactly half-quantized value if the chemical potential is within the gap.
This is the case for the insulating system. However, the present system is
metallic due to the presence of the kinetic term. Then, the integration is
done only within the Fermi surface. It gives a non half-quantized value of
the Hall conductivity, which is slightly smaller than the half-quantized
value. The accuracy of the half-quantization depends on how the Berry
curvature localizes. If the Dirac gap is small, the Berry curvature is
sharply localized and the Hall conductivity is almost half-quantized. On the
other hand, if the Dirac gap is large, the Berry curvature widely spreads
over the Brillouin zone, and the Hall conductivity is not half-quantized at
all. We will verify these based on the tight-binding model in the following
subsections devoted to each
X
-wave magnets.

Here, we make a simple approximation that the Fermi surface is a circle with
the radius
k_{\text{c}}
. We integrate the Berry curvature with a cutoff
k_{\text{c}}
as

\int_{0}^{k_{\text{c}}}kdk\int d\phi\Omega=-\frac{1}{2}\text{sgn}J\Delta+\int d\phi W(k_{\text{c}},\phi),

(36)

where

W(k_{\text{c}},\phi)\equiv-\frac{J\Delta+k_{\text{c}}g_{\parallel}\left(\phi\right)}{2\sqrt{\lambda^{2}k_{\text{c}}^{2}+\left(J\Delta+k_{\text{c}}g_{\parallel}\left(\phi\right)\right)^{2}}},

(37)

with

g_{\parallel}\left(\phi\right)\equiv g_{x}\cos\phi+g_{y}\sin\phi.

(38)

First, we evaluate Eq.(
36
) in the limit
k_{\text{c}}\rightarrow\infty
, where it is given by

W\left(k_{\text{c}},\phi\right)=-\frac{g_{\parallel}\left(\phi\right)}{2\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+g_{\parallel}^{2}\left(\phi\right)}}.

(39)

Eq.(
39
) does not contribute to Eq.(
36
) because
W\left(k_{\text{c}},\phi\right)=-W\left(k_{\text{c}},\phi+\pi\right)
. Then, there is
no contribution of
g_{x}
and
g_{y}
to the Chern number,

C=-\frac{1}{2}\text{sgn}J\Delta.

(40)

The Hall conductivity is given by

\sigma_{xy}=\frac{e^{2}}{h}C=-\frac{e^{2}}{h}\frac{1}{2}\text{sgn}J\Delta.

(41)

Next, we integrate the Berry curvature for a finite
k_{\text{c}}
. For
small
J
, the Fermi surface is calculated as

k_{\text{c}}=\sqrt{2m}\sqrt{\mu+m\lambda^{2}+\sqrt{\Delta^{2}+2\mu m\lambda^{2}+m^{2}\lambda^{4}}}.

(42)

The Hall conductivity is given by

\sigma_{xy}=-\frac{e^{2}}{h}\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{B}{\sqrt{B^{2}+\lambda^{2}k_{\text{c}}^{2}}}\right)\text{sgn}J\Delta.

(43)

It follows from Eq.(
43
) that

\left|\sigma_{xy}\right|<\frac{e^{2}}{h}\frac{1}{2}.

(44)

The Hall conductivity is not half-quantized for a metallic system.

V
Conclusion

We have studied planar Hall effects in the
X
-wave magnets. The main result
is that the magnitude of the Hall conductivity induced by in-plane magnetic
field is proportional to sgn
\left(J\sin N_{X}\Phi\right)
. It is
possible to determine
N_{X}
by measuring the
\Phi
dependence. On the
other hand, the Dirac gap is given by
\Delta\propto B^{N_{X}}
. Then, the
fine tuning of the chemical potential is necessary to adjust the chemical
potential within the Dirac gap for large
N_{X}
and for small
B
.

The Hall conductivity is almost half quantized for small
B
. This is
because the Berry curvature is sharply confined within the vicinity of the
Dirac point. The deviation from the half-quantized Hall conductivity
originates in the fact that the integration is not over the whole Brillouin
zone due to the presence of the kinetic term.

The absolute value of the Hall conductivity takes the maximum value at
\Phi=\left(\pm\pi/2+2n\pi\right)/N_{X}
with
n=0,\cdots,N_{x}-1
in the
X
-wave magnet. In particular, we have studied the Hall conductivity as a
function of magnetic field
B
by fixing
\Phi=\pi/\left(2N_{X}\right)
and
\pi/\left(2N_{X}\right)+\pi
in Figs.3(c), 5(c), 6(c), 7(c) an
8(c) by the following reason. External magnetic field is introduced by applying
current to a solenoid. By changing the direction of the applied current
continuously, the angle
\Phi=\pi/\left(2N_{X}\right)
is continuously
flipped to
\Phi=\pi/\left(2N_{X}\right)+\pi
. The Hall conductivity is
odd for
\Phi=\pi/\left(2N_{X}\right)
and
\pi/\left(2N_{X}\right)+\pi
for the
p
-wave magnet and the
f
-wave magnet. On the other hand,
it is even for the
d
-wave altermagnet, the
g
-wave altermagnet and the
i
-wave magnet.

Adaptive Radiation Therapy in the 0-7-21 Regimen: A Retrospective Study of Tumor Volumetric Response
Authors: Revadhi C. Chelvarajah, Dana D. Bartolucci, Joanna Javor, Edward Taylor, Zhihui Amy Liu, Xiang Y. Ye, Monica Serban, Bernard Cummings, Laura Dawson, Dana Keilty, Ezra Hahn, C. Jillian Tsai, Philip Wong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeA hypofractionated palliative radiation regimen referred to as “0-7-21,” which involves 1500 to 2400 cGy prescribed in 3 fractions delivered over 3 weeks (ie, days 0, 7, and 21), has been well documented in literature as an effective way to alleviate symptoms in solid malignancies with minimal toxicity and logistics. Given the time between fractions, this study aimed to identify predictive factors of on-treatment tumor response in patients treated with the 0-7-21 regimen to help select palliative patients who may benefit from adaptive therapy.Methods and MaterialsThis single-institution retrospective review of patients treated with 0-7-21 from February 2017 to June 2022. Gross tumor volumes from cone-beam computed tomography at days 0 and 21 were collected. The calculated percent change in lesion volume was grouped into quartiles. Groups showing the greatest reduction (25th percentile) versus the greatest growth or least reduction (75th percentile) were analyzed. Univariate and multivariable linear regression models identified factors associated with the percent change in tumor volume.ResultsA total of 105 patients with 193 lesions were included. The mean age was 77 years, the majority were men (n = 57, 54.3%), and several were nonsmokers (n = 42, 40%). Head and neck primaries (n = 125, 64.8%), head and neck anatomic sites (n = 160, 82.9%), and squamous cell carcinoma (n = 148, 76.7%) were the most common. Nine lesions (4.7%) were treated during concurrent systemic therapy. From days 0 to 21, the mean percent volume change was −9.9% (SD, 19.94). On multivariable analysis, Merkel cell histology (−24.24% [95% CI, −45.4 to −3.06]; P = .03) and smoking history (6.10 [95% CI, −0.22 to −10.84]; P = .047) were associated with tumor volume reduction and growth, respectively. Merkel cell histology was the only factor associated with the group with the largest volume reduction (25th percentile) on multivariable analysis (odds ratio, 15.34; 95% CI, 1.52-154.41; P = .02).ConclusionsOnline adaptive radiation therapy may benefit selected 0-7-21 palliative regimen patients with Merkel cell histology and a smoking history, warranting consideration in future studies and resource allocation.

Introduction

The “0-7-21” (1500-2400 cGy in 3 fractions over 3 weeks, ie, at days 0, 7, and 21) has been a well-described hypofractionated radiation regimen, designed to treat solid malignancies in the palliative setting. It was first conceived to deliver multiples of the well-known 8Gy in one fraction over multiple weeks to provide durable palliative symptom control and was found most effective in head and neck, skin, and gynecological cancers.1, 2, 3, 4 The extended time between each fraction allows for normal tissue repair and hence improves tolerability for patients who may struggle with toxicities yet still provides effective and sustained symptomatic relief.1, 2, 3, 4 It has an added benefit of convenience for patients and caregivers of 3 treatments spread over 3 weeks rather than the logistics required for daily radiation treatment regimes and hence is usually reserved for patients who have poor performance status and a shorter life expectancy.1, 2, 3, 4

Short-term radiological and clinical responses of tumors treated with 0-7-21 have been described in several tumor types. In nonmelanoma skin cancers, clinical response was seen as early as 21 days after the initial fraction, with a mean percentage reduction in tumor diameter of 33% and a median decrease from 5 to 3 cm.1 Another study in head and neck cancer observed a 31% complete radiological response rate within 3 months of radiation therapy (RT).5

This study highlighted that, for a subset of patients, clinically important tumor volume changes may occur between the extended fraction intervals in the 0-7-21 regimen that may require adaptation of treatment volumes and radiation plan to optimize dose to the tumor and/or normal tissue(s). This information is particularly important in the palliative setting, given its focus on symptom management and minimizing toxicity.

Online adaptive RT has been shown to allow improvements in target volume dose coverage and safe margin reduction, resulting in reduced normal tissue irradiation for curative-intent treatment in vulvar,6 prostate,7 and head and neck cancer.8 However, the clinical utility of using online adaptive RT for patients treated with multi-fraction palliative radiation has not been described. There is also a lack of research on potential predictive factors of rapid tumor changes in the palliative setting to guide patient selection for online adaptive RT.

The aim of our study was to identify patient, tumor, and treatment factors predictive of tumor volume responses between days 0 and 21 in patients treated with the 0-7-21 regimen.

Methods and Materials

Study population

We conducted a single-institution retrospective review of patients treated with 0-7-21 palliative hypofractionated RT from February 2017 to June 2022. We obtained an exemption from the institutional research ethics board for this quality improvement study. Our institutional practice for delivery of 0-7-21 palliative hypofractionated RT involves a variable dose of 1500 to 2400 cGy given in 3 equal fractions once a week, spread over a 3 to 4 week period (equivalent to maximum biologically effective dose 10 [for acute/tumor effects] of 43.2 Gy and biologically effective dose 3 [for late effects/connective tissue] of 88 Gy). All primary tumors and disease sites were included. Exclusion criteria consisted of incomplete treatment, overall treatment time less than 18 days or more than 28 days, lack of a computed tomography (CT) simulation scan (SIM) and/or no gross tumor volume (GTV) contours on SIM, lack of daily cone-beam CT (CBCT) image verification, or requirement of a treatment replan.

Patient demographics, tumor and treatment characteristics, and GTV assessment were obtained from our institutional database and our institutional cancer registry. Other than patient demographics, all other factors used were collected per lesion. Primary diseases were categorized as “disease category groups” and grouped into head and neck, skin, and other, while the targeted anatomic disease site, categorized as “disease site group,” was separated into bone, head and neck (which included unilateral and bilateral neck), skin, soft tissue, and other. Histology was categorized as squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), Merkel cell carcinoma, and other. Human papillomavirus (HPV) grouping was categorized as positive, negative, not tested, and not applicable (for diseases not known for HPV association).

GTV assessment

CBCTs at the first and third fractions were imported into the Raystation (RaySearch Laboratories AB) planning system. GTV for each lesion were contoured on CBCT day 0 and day 21 (first and third fractions) by a radiation oncology fellow, with volumes reviewed by a radiation oncologist. CT SIM GTV contours helped guide contouring of GTV_CBCT Day 0 and Day 21. Volumetric measurements (cm3) of GTV_CTSIM, GTV_CBCTDay0, and GTV_CBCTDay21 were recorded, and the percent change in the volume (GTV) during treatment was defined as the volume difference of GTV_CBCTDay21 minus GTV_CBCTDay0 over the baseline volume of GTV_CBCTDay0:GTVCBCTDay21−GTV_CBCTDay0GTV_CBCTDay0

To further describe GTV change during treatment, we applied volumetric definitions based on the principles of the response evaluation criteria in solid tumors guideline criteria.9 A partial response was defined as a 65% volume reduction (equivalent to a 30% reduction in a sphere’s diameter), and progressive disease was defined as a 73% volumetric increase, which is the volumetric equivalent of a 20% increase in diameter. Of note, no clinical target volume or planning target volume was collected or analyzed, and no dose calculations were completed.

Symptom response assessment

Symptom response was defined as “improved,” “stable,” “worsened,” or “not recorded” for the specific lesion treated compared with baseline pre-treatment symptomatology. Most patients did not have a formal follow-up at a predefined interval postradiation; therefore, symptom response was obtained by a review of any follow-up notes from a physician and/or nursing staff within our institution at least 2 weeks after radiation treatment.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to describe baseline patient and tumor characteristics, where the patient-specific characteristics (ie, age, sex, and smoking status) were summarized per patient, while all other characteristics were summarized per lesion. The association of the percent change in GTV with the patient and tumor characteristics was examined using univariate linear logistic regression models with the generalized estimating equations approach to account for the correlation of data because of the clustering of lesions within patients. To further determine the association, the final multivariable linear logistic regression model derived by backward variable selection procedures with a stay criterion of P < .1, with the generalized estimating equations approach, was applied. The covariates associated with the primary outcome identified in the univariate analysis were included in the full model.

We also classified the lesions into 2 groups: those with/without a percent change in GTV ≤25th percentile to compare lesions with the best response to others. Conversely, to compare patients with the worst response with others, we classified the lesions into those with/without a percent change in GTV ≥75th percentile. The patient and lesion characteristics were compared between the 2 groups using a χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables and a Student t test or Wilcoxon rank-sum test as appropriate for continuous variables. The association of the patient and lesion level characteristics with the outcome percent change in GTV ≤25th percentile was further determined using a multivariable logistic regression model.

The overall survival (OS), defined as the time from the end of RT to death or last follow-up when alive (censored), was estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method. Data management and all statistical analyses were performed using SAS 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc). A 2-sided P-value of <.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinical characteristics

A total of 166 patients completed palliative intent 0-7-21 radiation treatment at our institution from February 2017 to June 2022, with a treatment duration of between 18 and 28 days. Of these, 36 patients were excluded because a clinical markup technique was used, and no simulation CT or CBCT was acquired. Another 25 patients were excluded because there was no GTV contoured at CT SIM and/or day 0 CBCT for comparison. In total, 105 patients with 193 lesions were included in the analyses. The mean age of patients was 77 years old; the majority were men (n = 57, 54.3%) and nonsmokers (n = 42, 40%) (Table 1). Lesions located within the head and neck anatomy consisted of the most common disease site treated (82.9%) (Table 2). Within this site (n = 160), 98 treated lesions were nodal diseases, 56 were primaries, and the remainder were metastases. Other anatomical disease sites treated included skin beyond the head and neck (n = 14, 7.3%), bone (n = 6, 3.1%), pelvis (n = 8, 4.1%), and other (n = 10, 5.2%) (Table 2).

Fifteen lesions (7.8%) received less than 2400 cGy. Concurrent systemic therapy, including chemotherapy, immunotherapy, targeted therapy, and/or endocrine therapy, was given to 9 lesions (4.7%).

Differences in GTV from CT SIM and Day 0 of treatment

There was no significant change in GTV between CT SIM (mean volume 78.11 [SD, 151.47] cm3) and day 0 of treatment (mean volume, 78.85 [SD, 151.47] cm3), with a mean difference of 0.74 (SD, 6.07) cm3. The average time between CT SIM and day 0 of treatment was 1 day.

Factors associated with percent changes in GTV from days 0 to 21 of treatment

There was a mean percentage volume reduction of −9.9% (SD 19.94) in the GTV between days 0 and 21 of treatment.

On univariate linear regression analysis, Merkel cell histology compared with SCC (−25.68 [95% CI, −48.4 to −2.93]; P = .027), nodal GTV compared with primary GTVs (−6.43 [95% CI, −12.75 to −0.12]; P = .046), and those undergoing concurrent systemic therapy (−2.31 [95% CI −2.33 to −2.30], P < .001) were observed to having a significant reduction in GTV (Table 3). Lesions treated in the head and neck regions showed significantly less growth in GTV compared with bone (7.68 [95% CI, 4.35-1.02]; P < .001) and soft tissue (8.04 [95% CI, 4.89-1.20]; P < .001) (Table 2). There was a significant growth in GTV for ever-smokers (current or ex-smokers) compared with nonsmokers (6.64 [95% CI, 0.38-12.90], P = .04) and GTV from metastases compared with primary diseases (17.98 [95% CI, 17.87-18.08], P < .001) (Table 3). Factors associated with GTV with the greatest reduction or growth between days 0 and 21

Change in GTV from days 0 to 21 of treatment was categorized into a 25th percentile group (ie, lesions that showed the greatest reduction in GTV) (n = 48, 24.9%) and a 75th percentile group (ie, lesions that showed the least reduction or growth during treatment) (n = 33, 17.1%). The change in volume from days 0 to 21 of lesions within the 25th percentile group ranged from −89.82 to −14.48%, whereas the change in volume in the 75th percentile group ranged from 0.00 to 50.30%.

Among the lesions that responded volumetrically the most to radiation (25th percentile group), there were more lesions belonging to male patients, skin primaries, Merkel cell histology, and non-HPV-related cancers. Among current and previous smokers, diseases originating from the head and neck were less common among responders. Dose, treatment duration, and the use of concurrent systemic therapy were not associated with lesions that best responded to radiation (Table E1).

On multivariable analysis of the 25th percentile group, Merkel cell histology was the only factor that maintained significance in having the greatest reduction in GTV (OR, 15.54; 95 % CI, 1.54-156.81; P = .02) (Table 4 and Fig. 2). Although univariate analysis observed lesions from past and current smokers to be more likely to grow during radiation, this observation was not statistically significant on multivariable analysis (OR, 0.46; 95% CI, 0.18-1.19; P = .11). OS and symptom response

Median OS was 7.2 months (95% CI, 3.7-17.1), which was consistent with patient selection for this radiation regimen. It was encouraging to note that 63% of patients experienced symptomatic improvements to their treated lesions, given that the priority for this patient population is to balance symptom control, treatment toxicity, and treatment burden in this frail population. Furthermore, we found that lesions with the greatest volumetric reduction (percent change in GTV ≤25th percentile) had a significantly higher chance of symptom improvement (82% vs 66.4%, P = .045) compared with those with a percent change in GTV >25th percentile following their radiation treatments (Table E1).

Discussion

Our study retrospectively evaluated tumor volume response in patients who underwent the 0-7-21 radiation regimen with a goal of identifying which patients may benefit most from adaptive RT within this palliative radiation regimen. From days 0 to 21, a mean percentage reduction of −9.9% of the GTV was identified. Merkel cell carcinomas demonstrated the greatest reduction of GTV, while current smokers and ex-smokers were at risk of GTV growth during the course of their treatments. Palliative treatment involves a holistic, multidisciplinary approach that focuses on the personalized needs of each patient.10 Palliative RT regimens vary widely and are tailored to factors such as disease site, tumor histology, symptom burden, and patient performance status. Across these categories, a wide range of dose and fractionation schedules are used, from single-fraction treatments to multi-fraction regimens. Patients with poor performance status or limited prognosis are often treated with shorter, hypofractionated courses to minimize treatment burden. A common and well-established palliative regimen is 0-7-21, whereby patients can be reassessed for interfraction response and tolerability. This approach balances the need for symptom control and preserves patient quality of life.11 The clinical motivation for adaptive treatment is to improve the conformality of treated disease while reducing unnecessary doses to normal tissue(s) and hence toxicity. The 0-7-21 regimen, because of its interfraction timing, provides a unique opportunity for studying adaptive RT in a palliative setting, as there is sufficient time between treatments for tumor changes to occur and be observed. This is an opportunity not afforded by shorter daily fractionation schedules, where tumor response would not be observed within the treatment period. Multivariable analysis of our patient cohort indicates that those with Merkel cell histology were most likely to experience a large reduction in GTV during 0-7-21. This observation aligns with established knowledge, as Merkel cell carcinoma is recognized to have high radiation response rates.12 Iyer et al13 found that 94% of Merkel cell carcinoma tumors demonstrated a measured response to a single fraction of 8 Gy, and 45% demonstrated a complete response, including bulky tumors.13 Given that Merkel cell carcinomas most frequently affect sun-exposed areas of the body,12 such as the head and neck, adaptive RT in this region is of particular value in minimizing toxicity to the delicate normal tissue structures in the head and neck. Conversely, lesions in current and ex-smokers were more likely to grow during 0-7-21, suggesting that consideration of adaptive RT for patients with either of these factors may be appropriate. However, a smoking history alone was insufficient to predict the least radiation-responsive lesions. Despite the short lifespan of this patient population, symptomatic response was observed in 63%, with a higher likelihood of symptomatic improvement (82%) among the most radiation-responsive lesions (≤25th percentile).

Among the examined factors, neither HPV-positive tumors nor SCC histology was associated with volumetric tumor response in our study. Although HPV-positive disease is clinically expected to be radiosensitive, this may not correlate with radiological response, given the short time interval of 0-7-21. This finding is consistent with the literature in the curative setting, where Huang et al. demonstrated that although HPV-positive disease involutes more quickly than HPV-negative disease, this difference is not observed until 36 weeks posttreatment.14

Although not statistically significant, a trend was observed in which nodal disease appeared to respond more favorably to 0-7-21 compared with primary tumors or metastatic sites. This suggests that lymph node involvement may be more amenable to adaptive RT, though further studies are needed to confirm this observation. Although limited, the available literature supports the clinical importance of nodal response, with studies such as the Mao et al15 study highlighting that nodal disease, particularly small nodal volumes, had a significantly higher shrinkage rate than primary disease. Merlino et al16 also highlighted increased therapeutic response in nodes compared with primary disease, though these findings were derived from patients treated with neoadjuvant immunotherapy.

Several key limitations should be acknowledged. First, the small sample size restricts statistical power and may limit the generalizability of the findings. There is also an uneven distribution of patients across disease sites and histology, particularly SCC being a significantly larger histology group than others, hence introducing potential bias. Notably, our study does not collect toxicity data, meaning it is unclear whether patients with the greatest tumor volume reduction experienced increased adverse effects and thus might have benefited most from adaptive planning. A considered limitation to our study was the choice to have one consistent reviewer to contour all lesions so that the review of lesional change and the approach to contouring remained consistent, hence minimizing interobserver variability. The use of a second reviewer was for quality control to ensure consistency of this method between each lesion. However, it is acknowledged that this may be a confounder in itself, with variations in one’s contouring that may contribute to perceived volume changes, and hence we recognized that having multiple investigators contouring and an aggregate volume used may strengthen the data used. Finally, a major limitation of our study is that, with the lack of dose-of-the-day calculations evaluating the dosimetric impact on organs-at-risk with a reduction or growth in GTV and further its clinical impact on patient toxicity, it is hard to make a convincing argument to promote adaptive planning just based on tumor volume change. This study helped identify cases that may show changes in tumor volume and, hence, help rationalize resources and guide further prospective studies on which palliative cases may have the most clinically meaningful impact if adaptive planning were to be used.

In summary, our analysis of the 0-7-21 regimen demonstrates that Merkel cell carcinoma exhibits the largest reduction in GTV, whereas smokers and ex-smokers are at risk for GTV growth. Given the established benefits of the 0-7-21 regimen in symptom control and local disease management and the opportunities for interfraction assessment and dosimetric optimization, there is potential for integrating adaptive RT techniques into palliative care pathways, particularly for patients with Merkel cell carcinomas or a smoking history, while rationalizing these resources for cases that may not have significant benefit. Future research will focus on selecting patients based on our findings for adaptive 0-7-21 palliative RT and prospectively validate the identified predictive factors of tumor response while assessing organs-at-risk dosimetric impacts and their correlation with patient-reported outcomes for symptoms and toxicities.

Guaranteeing Higher Order Convergence Rates for Accelerated Wasserstein Gradient Flow Schemes
Raymond Chu, Matt Jacobs

Abstract
In this paper, we study higher-order-accurate-in-time minimizing movements schemes for Wasserstein gradient flows. We introduce a novel accelerated second-order scheme, leveraging the differential structure of the Wasserstein space in both Eulerian and Lagrangian coordinates. For sufficiently smooth energy functionals, we show that our scheme provably achieves an optimal quadratic convergence rate. Under the weaker assumptions of Wasserstein differentiability and λ \lambda -displacement convexity (for any λ ∈ ℝ \lambda\in{\mathbb{R}} ), we show that our scheme still achieves a first-order convergence rate and has strong numerical stability. In particular, we show that the energy is nearly monotone in general, while when the energy is L L -smooth and λ \lambda -displacement convex (with λ > 0 \lambda>0 ), we prove the energy is non-increasing and the norm of the Wasserstein gradient is exponentially decreasing along the iterates. Taken together, our work provides the first fully rigorous proof of accelerated second-order convergence rates for smooth functionals and shows that the scheme performs no worse than the classical scheme JKO scheme for functionals that are λ \lambda -displacement convex and Wasserstein differentiable.



Keywords:
Wasserstein gradient flows, minimizing movement schemes, higher–order time discretizations, optimal quantitative convergence analysis.

Introduction

Given an energy functional
\phi:\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{+\infty\}
, the continuity equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho(t,x)-\nabla\cdot\Bigl(\rho(t,x)\nabla\Bigl(\tfrac{\delta\phi}{\delta\mu}\bigl(\rho(t,\cdot),x\bigr)\Bigr)\Bigr)=0\quad\text{on }(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[6.0pt]
\rho(0,\cdot)=\rho_{0}(\cdot)\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

(1.1)

can be interpreted formally as the gradient flow of
\phi
on the Wasserstein space
(\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\mathbf{W}_{2})
.
This
class of equations models various important physical phenomena such as fluid flow, heat transfer, aggregation-diffusion, and crowd motion
[
30
,
28
]
to name a few. In general, these equations are both stiff and non-linear making them challenging to solve numerically. Perhaps the most well-known
stable
numerical method for solving these equations is the celebrated JKO scheme
[
24
]
, an unconditionally energy stable variational scheme, well-known to be first-order-accurate-in-time with respect to the time step
\tau>0

[
2
]
. Recently, there has been a great deal of interest in formulating new versions of the JKO scheme, which achieve higher order accuracy in time, while maintaining favorable stability properties
[
26
,
27
,
5
,
32
,
10
,
23
,
20
,
7
]
. However, to the best of our knowledge, none of these methods to date have been able to rigorously prove a second (or higher) order convergence rate in any metric, even for smooth functionals, despite promising numerical evidence. Indeed, the only rigorous rates that have appeared in the literature for these accelerated methods are
O(\sqrt{\tau})
, worse than the
O(\tau)
rate of the simpler JKO scheme. The goal of this manuscript is to remedy this situation. We provide a new energy stable scheme that we rigorously demonstrate is second-order-in-time for smooth energy functionals and at worst first-order-accurate-in-time for
\lambda
-displacement convex and Wasserstein differentiable functionals (see Section
2
for the precise definition of Wasserstein differentiability and (
1.10
) for the main model functionals that we consider).

To construct our scheme, we exploit both the Eulerian (Wasserstein) and Lagrangian
L^{2}
gradient flow structures of the equation to construct a novel second-order-accurate-in-time numerical scheme for solving (
1.1
). To motivate this Lagrangian
L^{2}
perspective, let us assume that the velocity field
v=-\nabla\bigl(\frac{\delta\phi}{\delta\mu}\bigr)
is sufficiently regular, so that the solution
\rho(t,x)
can be expressed as the push-forward of the initial data
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by the Lagrangian flow
X(t,x)
, i.e.

\rho_{t}(x)=(X(t,\cdot)_{\#}\rho_{0})(x).

(1.2)

where

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}X(t,x)=-\nabla\left[\frac{\delta\phi}{\delta\mu}(X(t,\cdot)_{\#}\rho_{0},x)\right]\quad\text{on }[0,\infty),\\
X(0,x)=x,\end{cases}

(1.3)

As we will see below, the Lagrangian flow (
1.3
) is a gradient flow of the lifted energy functional
X\mapsto\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X)
over the Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\rho_{0})
where

\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X):=\phi(X_{\#}\rho_{0}).

(1.4)

That is, if
\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X)
denotes the
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\rho_{0})
Fréchet derivative of the map
X\mapsto\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X)
, then

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}X(t,x)=-\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X(t,x))\quad\text{on }[0,\infty),\\
X(0,\cdot)=\textnormal{Id}.\end{cases}.

(1.5)

Although the lifted energy
X\mapsto\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X)
is a good deal more complicated than the original energy
\rho\mapsto\phi(\rho)
, the advantage of this perspective is that we can much more readily generalize high-order-accurate numerical discretizations of Euclidean differential equations to the Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\rho_{0})
, rather than needing to search for their correct analogue over the Wasserstein space.
Indeed, from a theoretical standpoint, it is often preferable to work with the lifted functional because its higher order derivatives encode higher order geometric information such as geodesic convexity (see
[
22
, Lemma 3.6]
and Theorem
2.13
). By contrast, the Wasserstein hessian alone does not capture geodesic convexity: for instance, the potential energy is a linear functional, so its Wasserstein hessian vanishes identically. Indeed, (
2.8
) shows that the second time derivative of the energy functional along geodesics involves not only the Wasserstein hessian, but also the spatial gradient of the Wasserstein gradient.

To obtain a second-order-in-time discretization of (
1.1
), we apply the second order trapezoidal finite difference scheme to (
1.5
). In particular, we consider

\begin{cases}X^{\tau}_{n+1}\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\xi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\rho_{0})}\left(\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\xi)+\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n}),\xi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\rho_{0})}\bigr)+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\xi-X^{\tau}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\rho_{0})}^{2}\right),\\
\rho^{\tau}_{n+1}:=(X^{\tau}_{n+1})_{\#}\rho_{0},\end{cases}

(1.6)

where the initial condition
X^{\tau}_{0}
is prescribed.
The optimizers
X^{\tau}_{n+1}
of (
1.6
) admits the following trapezoidal finite difference scheme:

X^{\tau}_{n+1}=X^{\tau}_{n}-\frac{\tau}{2}\bigl(\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})+\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})\bigr).

(1.7)

We note that, unlike the JKO scheme, the trapezoidal update maps

T^{\tau}_{n+1}:=X^{\tau}_{n+1}\circ(X^{\tau}_{n})^{-1}

are not, in general, optimal transport maps between

\rho_{n}^{\tau}
and
\rho_{n+1}^{\tau}
, since they need not be gradients of convex functions.

1.1
Background and previous results

We mentioned earlier, the Lagrangian flow (
1.3
) can also be realized as a gradient flow in the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\rho_{0})
of the
lifted
energy functional (
1.4
), which was introduced in mean-field games.
As shown in mean-field games
[
22
,
21
,
9
,
8
]
, the convexity and differentiability properties of the lift
\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}
on
L^{2}
are closely related to notions of convexity and differentiability of
\phi
over
\mathbf{W}_{2}
. In particular, for a large class of probability measures
\rho_{0}
, the differentiability of
\phi
in the Wasserstein sense at the measure
\xi_{\#}\rho_{0}
is equivalent to the Fréchet differentiability of
\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}
at the function
\xi
(see
[
22
, Corollary 3.22]
). In this case, we have

\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\xi)=\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\xi_{\#}\rho_{0},\xi),

where
\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}
denotes the Wasserstein gradient, which when
\phi
and
\rho
are sufficiently smooth (see
[
4
, Lemma 4.12]
), can be characterized as

\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\rho)(x)=\nabla\frac{\delta\phi}{\delta\mu}(\rho,x).

Thus, formally, the Lagrangian flow (
1.3
) can be viewed as the gradient flow in the Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\rho_{0})
described by (
1.5
). The implicit Euler discretization of (
1.5
) and its connection to Wasserstein gradient flows (
1.1
) has been studied in

[
25
,
11
,
19
,
3
]
. However, to obtain quantatitive error rates,
[
25
]
had to assume uniform
C^{5}
bounds on the iterates.

Gradient flows on Hilbert spaces, such as (
1.5
), are well understood when
\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}
is
\lambda
-convex. The general theory of gradient flows for
\lambda
-convex functionals in Hilbert spaces (see
[
1
, Chapters 11 and 12]
) ensures existence and uniqueness of strong Hilbert-Space-valued solutions to (
1.5
). In this setting, solutions can be constructed via the implicit Euler scheme,

\begin{cases}X^{\tau}_{n+1}=X^{\tau}_{n}-\tau\,\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}\bigl(X^{\tau}_{n+1}\bigr),\\
\rho^{\tau}_{n+1}:=\bigl(X^{\tau}_{n+1}\bigr)_{\#}\,\rho_{0},\end{cases}

(1.8)

which achieves the optimal convergence rate of
O(\tau)
, where
\tau
is the time step size, when the initial data lies in the subdifferential of
\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}

[
2
]
.
The convexity of
\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}
over
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\rho_{0})
is closely related to the (displacement) convexity of
\phi
over
\mathbf{W}_{2}
. In particular, for the class of
continuous
energy functionals
\phi
,
[
21
, Lemma 3.6]
shows that
\lambda
-displacement convexity of
\phi
in
\mathbf{W}_{2}
is equivalent to
\lambda
-convexity of its lift
\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\rho_{0})
. In more general settings, where
\phi
may be discontinuous, one can still obtain poly-convexity of
\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}
from the displacement convexity of
\phi

[
11
]
.

By the theory described above, one can construct solutions to (
1.5
) for continuous and
\lambda
-displacement convex energy functionals using the implicit Euler scheme. We now examine its connection to the JKO scheme. In
[
3
]
, under suitable conditions, it was shown that the iterates defined by (
1.8
) coincide with those of the JKO scheme. Moreover, under some assumptions, the update map

T^{\tau}_{n+1}:=X^{\tau}_{n+1}\circ\bigl(X^{\tau}_{n}\bigr)^{-1}

is the optimal transport map from
\rho^{\tau}_{n}
to
\rho^{\tau}_{n+1}
. In particular, composing both sides of the Lagrangian coordinates of (
1.8
) with
\bigl(X^{\tau}_{n}\bigr)^{-1}
yields

T^{\tau}_{n+1}=\textnormal{Id}-\tau\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho^{\tau}_{n}}(T^{\tau}_{n+1}),

showing that the optimal transport map corresponds to a single implicit Euler step for the
L^{2}
-gradient flow

\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{X}(t,x)=-\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho^{\tau}_{n}}(\tilde{X}(t,x)),

with initial condition
\tilde{X}(0,\cdot)=\textnormal{Id}
.

We now focus on higher-order schemes, i.e., schemes that formally converge faster than
O(\tau)
. A key difficulty in extending higher-order schemes to dimensions
d>1
is that in Eulerian coordinates it can be difficult to correctly approximate the Wasserstein space by a Hilbert space to greater than first order accuracy, while in Lagrangian coordinates, the composition of flow map iterates
X_{n+1}^{\tau}\circ(X_{n}^{\tau})^{-1}
is in general not an optimal transport map between
\rho_{n}^{\tau}
and
\rho_{n+1}^{\tau}
, creating new difficulties. Note that the failure of
X_{n+1}^{\tau}\circ(X_{n}^{\tau})^{-1}
to be an optimal transport map is due to Brenier’s theorem, which states that optimal transport maps must be gradients of convex functions, a structure not naturally preserved by higher-order Lagrangian schemes in dimensions
d>1
.
Nonetheless, many higher-order stable schemes for Wasserstein gradient flows have been proposed for (
1.1
) (see, e.g.,
[
26
,
27
,
5
,
32
,
10
,
23
,
20
,
7
]
). However, as we noted earlier, these approaches have so far only established convergence without quantitative rates, or at best, have achieved a suboptimal
O(\sqrt{\tau})
convergence rate by extending the methods in
[
2
]
.

Our scheme (
1.6
) appears to be the first to identify smoothness conditions on the energy functional that guarantee an
O(\tau^{2})
convergence rate for both the Lagrangian coordinates and measure iterates. Moreover, to our knowledge, it is the first to leverage the differential structure of
\mathbf{W}_{2}
to derive error estimates for Wasserstein gradient flows. We expect this calculus-based framework to extend naturally to explicit schemes and other higher-order schemes. Although we only verify the case
p=2
below, we expect that an
O(\tau^{p})
accurate (explicit or implicit) scheme for (
1.1
) can be obtained by applying an
O(\tau^{p})
accurate finite difference scheme to (
1.5
). In addition, even for less regular functionals, where such calculus based techniques do not apply, we prove our scheme (
1.6
) converges with an intermediate
O(\tau)
convergence rate.

1.2
Main results

Our first result establishes an
O(\tau^{2})
error rate for the scheme (
1.6
) under suitable smoothness conditions on the lifted energy functional. Our strategy is to extend the classical finite-dimensional proof of the
O(\tau^{2})
convergence of the trapezoid scheme for ODEs on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to the infinite-dimensional Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\rho_{0})
. Consequently, our assumptions on convergence mirror those in the finite-dimensional setting.

Assumption 1

(Smoothness Assumption)
.

Let
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be given, and define the Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\rho_{0})
.
We assume that the lifted energy functional

X\mapsto\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X)

belongs to
C^{1,1}(\mathbb{H};\mathbb{H})
.
In addition, we assume that the unique strong solution
X:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{H}
of (
1.5
) with
X_{0}=\mathrm{Id}
satisfies

X\in C^{2,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty);\mathbb{H}).

As our numerical Lagrangian flow,
X^{\tau}_{n+1}
arises from a finite difference scheme, we are able to extend the classical finite-dimensional analysis of such schemes to our infinite-dimensional setting, thereby obtaining:

Theorem 1.1

(
O(\tau^{2})
Convergence: Theorem
4.4
, Theorem
5.14
, Theorem
5.15
)
.

Fix a terminal time
T>0
,
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and assume that the smoothness Assumption
1
holds.
Define the Lipschitz constants

L(\phi):=\sup_{\xi_{1}\neq\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{H}}\frac{\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\xi_{1})-\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\xi_{2})\|_{\mathbb{H}}}{\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}}},\quad L(T,\ddot{X}):=\sup_{t\neq s\in[0,T]}\frac{\|\dot{X}_{t}-\dot{X}_{s}-(t-s)\ddot{X}_{s}\|_{\mathbb{H}}}{|t-s|}.

Then, for any time step
\tau\leq 1/L(\phi)
and integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
n\tau\leq T
, one has

W_{2}(\rho_{n\tau},\rho_{n}^{\tau})\leq\|X(n\tau)-X_{n}^{\tau}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq e^{2L(\phi)T}\|X_{0}^{\tau}-\textnormal{Id}\|_{\mathbb{H}}+2\frac{L(T,\ddot{X})}{L(\phi)}(e^{2L(\phi)T}-1)\cdot\tau^{2},

where
\rho^{\tau}_{n}
and
X^{\tau}_{n}
are defined by (
1.6
). Here,
\rho_{t}:=(X(t))_{\#}\rho_{0}
is a weak solution of

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho-\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\rho,x))=0\text{ on }(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\\
\rho(0,\cdot)=\rho_{0}(\cdot)\text{ on }{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\end{cases}.

(1.9)

If instead one has
X\in C^{2,\alpha}_{\textnormal{loc}}([0,\infty);\mathbb{H})
the convergence rates becomes
O(\tau^{1+\alpha})
.

We now provide an example of an energy functional satisfying our assumptions. Consider the functional (see Example

U(\rho):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\bigl((\rho\star\chi)(x)\bigr)\,d\rho(x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x)\,d\rho(x)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}W(x-y)\,d\rho(x)\,d\rho(y),

where
V,W\in C^{2,1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
f\in C^{2,1}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
, and
\chi\in C^{2,1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with

and
\eta
being even functions. Here,

denotes convolution. This functional satisfies the assumptions of Theorem

when
\rho_{0}
has no atoms or when
\rho=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{j}}
. The latter case is useful for numerical simulations as the energy and its gradient will be fully discrete. Numerical simulations of this scheme for this energy functional can be found in Section

[

,
17
,

Even under weaker regularity assumptions on
\phi
, namely, that

convergence rate by adapting the

Assumption 2
.

We fix a measure
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
\mathbb{H}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\rho_{0})
.

Given a functional
\phi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
, we assume that its lifted functional
\phi_{\rho_{0}}^{\#}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{R}
, defined by (

)
is Fréchet differentiable over
\mathbb{H}
and is
\lambda
-convex over
\mathbb{H}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
Additionally, we assume that the lifted energy functional is proper, i.e.,

\inf_{\xi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\rho_{0})}\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\xi)>-\infty.

We further assume that in (
1.6
) the time step parameter satisfies

\lambda/2+1/\tau>0.

Finally, we suppose that the initial iterates satisfy

\lim_{\tau\downarrow 0}\|X_{0}^{\tau}-\mathrm{Id}\|_{\mathbb{H}}=0,\qquad\sup_{\tau>0}\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{0}^{\tau})\|_{\mathbb{H}}<\infty.

Remark 1.2
.

We expect that the Fréchet differentiablity assumption can be weakened to just lower semi-continuity of the lift, but we impose this condition to simplify some of our arguments.

Remark 1.3
.

Note that the condition of the lift being
\lambda
-convex is rather strong and different from a functional being
\lambda
-displacement convex in the Wasserstein sense. In particular, the
\lambda
-convexity of the lift does not hold for the internal energy functional
\rho\mapsto\int f(\rho)
even when
f
is convex. Nonetheless, if the energy is continuous with respect to the Wasserstein metric, then
\lambda
-convexity of the lifted energy is equivalent to
\lambda
-convexity in the Wasserstein sense (see the next remark).

Remark 1.4

(Convexity and Differentiability Assumptions)
.

Let
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be atomless and
\phi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous. Then
\lambda
-displacement convexity (see Definition
2.11

\lambda
-convexity of the lifted functional
\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}
(see Theorem

[

).

Furthermore, if
\phi\in C^{1}(\mathbf{W}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d});\mathbb{R})

\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\xi)(x)=\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi\big(\xi_{\#}\rho_{0},\,\xi(x)\big).

These results can be useful for verifying the convexity and smoothness assumptions we require.

We will also frequently use the quantity

\lambda_{\tau}:=\frac{1}{2\tau}\log\!\left(\frac{1+\lambda\tau}{1-\lambda\tau}\right),

(1.11)

which satisfies
\lambda_{\tau}\to\lambda

Our approach to Theorem
1.5
is based on the EVI characterization of
\lambda
-convex gradient flows. For example, in Hilbert spaces
[
1
]
, a curve
x(t)
solves the gradient flow of a
\lambda
-convex functional
f
if and only if it satisfies the evolution variational inequality

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|x(t)-y\|^{2}\right)+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|x(t)-y\|^{2}+f(x(t))\leq f(y)\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in(0,\infty),

for all
y
in the domain of
f
. The key idea is to show that suitable interpolations of the numerical Lagrangian flow
X^{\tau}_{n}
satisfy an approximate EVI with an error term of order
O(\tau^{2})
. Then the
O(\tau)
convergence rate is obtained by using a version of Grönwall’s inequality from
[
2
]
.

The approximate EVI we derived for our trapezoid-rule method is in Theorem
5.5
and follows from our Discrete EVI derived in Lemma
5.2
. Deriving this discrete EVI with the correct
O(\tau^{2})
error term was a key technical challenge in our analysis. Compared to the implicit Euler and JKO schemes in
[
2
]
, our scheme’s variational problem (
1.6
) included the term

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho^{\tau}_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n}),\xi\rangle d\rho_{0}(x).

To control this term we had to carefully use both the convexity properties of
\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}
and the trapezoid scheme (
1.7
) (see Lemma
5.1
).

Another difficulty in the trapezoidal rule setting, compared to the implicit Euler scheme, is the lower bounds of
\|X^{\tau}_{n+1}-X^{\tau}_{n}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}
in terms of the gradient. In the implicit Euler case, this quantity simplifies to
\tau^{2}\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}
. However, in the trapezoidal rule case, it becomes

\frac{\tau^{2}}{4}\left\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})+\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})\right\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},

which prevents us from establishing a lower bound of the form
\|X^{\tau}_{n+1}-X^{\tau}_{n}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\geq C\tau^{2}\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}
without assuming that
\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}
is sufficiently smooth. As a result, we were unable to recover exponentially decaying constants in the error estimate (
1.12
) when
\lambda>0
without a smoothness assumption.

Beyond convergence rates, our scheme exhibits the following numerical stability properties:

Theorem 1.6

(Numerical Stability: Lemma
3.5
, Lemma
3.6
, Lemma
6.2
)
.

Under the notation and assumptions of Assumption
2
. Then the energy is
almost decreasing
, in the sense that

\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})-\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})\leq\frac{\tau}{4}\left(\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}-\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\right).

Moreover, the gradient norm satisfies:

\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\mathcal{C}(\lambda)\cdot\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},

where

\mathcal{C}(\lambda):=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }\lambda\geq 0,\\
e^{2|\lambda_{\tau}|\tau},&\text{if }\lambda<0,\end{cases}

and
\lambda_{\tau}
is defined in (
1.11
). In particular, when
\lambda\geq 0
, the gradient norm is non-increasing across iterations.

Furthermore, if
\lambda\geq 0
and
\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}
is
L
-
Lipschitz
, then for
\tau\leq 1/L
, the energy is
non-increasing
:

\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})+\frac{\lambda}{2}||X^{\tau}_{n+1}-X^{\tau}_{n}||_{\mathbb{H}}+\tau\left(1-\frac{L\tau}{2}\right)\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n}),

and the
gradient norm decays exponentially
:

\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\leq e^{-2\lambda_{\tau,L}\tau}\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},

where
\lambda_{\tau,L}
is defined in Lemma
6.2
.

We also have obtained a classical stability result of our scheme in Lemma
3.7
and refined it in the
L
-Lipschitz setting in Lemma
6.3
.

Preliminaries on the Differential Structure of
\mathbf{W}_{2}

2.1
Notation

Given any measure
\mu
, we define the Hilbert space

L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\mu):=\left\{\xi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\xi(x)|^{2}d\mu(x)<\infty\right\},

where the inner product is given by

\langle\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\mu)}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\langle\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\rangle d\mu(x),

where
\langle x,y\rangle
denotes the dot product in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and we let
||\xi||_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\mu)}
represent the norm on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\mu)
induced by the
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\mu)
inner product.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be the space of probability measures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with finite second moments.

We frequently consider the
push-forward
of probability measures under (Borel) measurable maps. Given a measurable map
\xi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and a probability measure
\mu
, the push-forward of
\mu
by
\xi
is defined as

\xi_{\#}\mu(A):=\mu(\xi^{-1}(A))\quad\text{for all Borel sets }A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

If
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
\xi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\mu)
, then

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|x|^{2}d(\vec{\xi}_{\#}\mu)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\xi(x)|^{2}d\mu(x)<\infty.

Thus, we conclude that
\xi_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
whenever
\xi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\mu)
and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

We also frequently use the following composition rule for push-forwards: given any Borel measurable maps
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

(\xi_{1}\circ\xi_{2})_{\#}\mu=(\xi_{1})_{\#}\big((\xi_{2})_{\#}\mu\big).

The Wasserstein-2 metric space
\mathbf{W}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is given by
(\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),W_{2})
, where the Wasserstein-2 metric is

W_{2}(\mu,\nu):=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi[\mu,\nu]}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|x-y|^{2}d\pi(x,y),

(2.1)

where
\Pi[\mu,\nu]
denotes the set of probability measures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with left marginal
\mu
and right marginal
\nu
(see
[
31
,
28
]
).

We now establish some useful and well-known inequalities for this metric.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\xi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be
L
-Lipschitz, meaning that

|\xi(x)-\xi(y)|\leq L|x-y|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then, for any
(\mu,\nu)\in(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{2}
, we have

W_{2}(\xi_{\#}\mu,\xi_{\#}\nu)\leq LW_{2}(\mu,\nu).

Proof.

This follows from the observation that if
\pi\in\Pi[\mu,\nu]
, then
(\xi,\xi)_{\#}\pi\in\Pi[\xi_{\#}\mu,\xi_{\#}\nu]
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Fix
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)
. Then,

W_{2}^{2}((\xi_{1})_{\#}\mu,(\xi_{2})_{\#}\mu)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi_{1}(x)-\xi_{2}(x)|^{2}\,d\mu(x)=\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}.

Proof.

By assumption, both measures
\xi_{1}{}_{\#}\mu
and
\xi_{2}{}_{\#}\mu
belong to
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The result then follows by observing that the coupling
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})_{\#}\mu
belongs to
\Pi[\xi_{1}{}_{\#}\mu,\xi_{2}{}_{\#}\mu]
, and applying the definition of the Wasserstein-2 distance via (
2.1
).
∎

2.2
Derivatives on Wasserstein Space

We present two notions of derivatives for
\phi
, following the definitions in
[
8
, Section 2]
. The concept of the Wasserstein derivative used here coincides with their notion of the intrinsic derivative.

Definition 2.3

(First Variation)
.

Let
\phi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
. We say that
\phi\in C^{1}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d});\mathbb{R})
if there exists a function

\frac{\delta\phi}{\delta\mu}:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}

satisfying the following conditions:

1.

The function
\frac{\delta\phi}{\delta\mu}
is jointly continuous on
\mathbf{W}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

2.

For any
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the function
x\mapsto\frac{\delta\phi}{\delta\mu}(\nu,x)
has at most quadratic growth.

3.

For any
\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\phi((1-\varepsilon)\rho+\varepsilon\rho^{\prime})-\phi(\rho)}{\varepsilon}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\delta\phi}{\delta\mu}(\rho,x)\,d(\rho^{\prime}-\rho)(x).

(2.2)

The function
\frac{\delta\phi}{\delta\mu}
is called the first variation of
\phi
.

Since the first variation is defined only up to an additive constant, we impose the normalization

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{\delta\phi}{\delta\mu}(\rho,y)d\rho(y)=0,\quad\forall\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

The first variation also satisfies the fundamental theorem of calculus property (see
[
8
]
):

\phi(\rho^{\prime})-\phi(\rho)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{\delta\phi}{\delta\mu}((1-s)\rho+s\rho^{\prime},x)d(\rho^{\prime}-\rho)(x)ds.

(2.3)

Next, we define the Wasserstein gradient, which extends the classical gradient notion to the metric space
\mathbf{W}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Definition 2.4

(Wasserstein Gradient)
.

We say that
\phi\in C^{1}(\mathbf{W}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d});\mathbb{R})
if:

1.

\phi\in C^{1}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d});\mathbb{R})
.

2.

The first variation
\frac{\delta\phi}{\delta\mu}(\rho,x)
is differentiable in
x
for all
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

3.

The gradient
\nabla_{x}\frac{\delta\phi}{\delta\mu}(\rho,x)
is jointly continuous on
\mathbf{W}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

4.

For any
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, the mapping
y\mapsto\nabla_{x}\frac{\delta\phi}{\delta\mu}(\rho,y)
has at most quadratic growth.

The Wasserstein gradient of
\phi
is defined as

\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\rho,x):=\nabla_{x}\frac{\delta\phi}{\delta\mu}(\rho,x).

Bounds on the Wasserstein gradient provide Lipschitz control for
\phi
. Indeed, in
[
8
, Section 2]
, the dual formulation of the
W_{1}
metric was used to establish the following Lipschitz property.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\phi\in C^{1}(\mathbf{W}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d});{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and assume that

L:=\sup_{(\rho,x)\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\rho,x)|<\infty.

Then, for any
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
p\geq 1
, we have

|\phi(\mu)-\phi(\nu)|\leq LW_{p}(\mu,\nu).

(2.4)

The definition of Wasserstein differentiability at a point
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be found in
[
22
, Definition 3.11]
. When
\phi\in C^{1}(\mathbf{W}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d});\mathbb{R})
and its Wasserstein gradient
\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi
satisfies certain mild growth conditions, then
\phi
is Wasserstein differentiable on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(see Proposition
2.14
and
[
22
, Corollary 3.22]
). The growth condition bounds on
\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi
are used used to control the associated error terms arising in the differentiation.

Next, we introduce the notion of hessians in Wasserstein space.

Definition 2.6

(Wasserstein hessian)
.

We say that
\phi\in C^{2}(\mathbf{W}_{2};\mathbb{R})
if
\phi\in C^{1}(\mathbf{W}_{2};\mathbb{R})
and, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the mapping

\rho\mapsto\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\rho,x)

belongs to
C^{1}(\mathbf{W}_{2};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, componentwise. Moreover, we assume that the Wasserstein hessian

\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{W}}\phi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},

defined as the Wasserstein gradient of
\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\rho,x)
with respect to
\rho
, is jointly continuous on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

In particular, one has that

\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\rho,x,x^{\prime})=\left[\nabla_{x^{\prime}}\frac{\delta}{\delta\mu}\nabla_{x}\frac{\delta}{\delta\mu}\left(\phi\right)\right](\rho,x,x^{\prime})

By Lemma
2.5
, if
\phi\in C^{2}(\mathbf{W}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d});{\mathbb{R}})
and
\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{W}}\phi
is uniformly bounded in
\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, then the Wasserstein gradient satisfies the Lipschitz property

\sup_{\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\mu,x)-\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\nu,x)|}{W_{p}(\mu,\nu)}\leq C,

(2.5)

for all
p\geq 1
where
C>0
is independent of
x
and
p
. We will see later that the Wasserstein hessian will allow us to control the gradient of the lifted energy functional. See in particular Example
2.16
.

Remark 2.7
.

A
positive semi definite
Wasserstein hessian does
not
guarantee that an energy functional is
\lambda
–displacement convex. Indeed, as we will see from Remark
2.12
to obtain
\lambda
-geodesic convexity, one needs control on both
\nabla_{x}\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi
and
\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{W}}\phi
(see in particular (
2.8
)).

Now we show an energy example functional that is of class
C^{2}(\mathbf{W}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d});{\mathbb{R}}
). The functional is well suited for numerical approximation, since both the energy and its gradients can be computed in a fully discrete form when the probability measure is a finite sum of Dirac masses.

Example 2.8
.

Fix
f\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
V\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
W\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
\chi\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Further assume that
W
and
\eta
are even. Define
U:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
by

U(\rho):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f((\rho\star\chi)(x))\,d\rho(x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x)\,d\rho(x)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}W(x-y)\,d\rho(x)\,d\rho(y),

where
\star
denotes convolution. Then, the following properties hold:

1.

U\in C^{2}(\mathbf{W}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d});\mathbb{R})
.

2.

The Wasserstein gradient
\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}U(\rho,x)
is uniformly Lipschitz in
x
, i.e.,

\sup_{\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}U(\rho,x)-\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}U(\rho,y)|}{|x-y|}<\infty.

3.

The Wasserstein hessian
\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{W}}U
is uniformly bounded, i.e.,

\sup_{(\rho,x)\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{W}}U(\rho,x)|<\infty.

Proof.

We first establish that
U\in C^{1}\left(\mathbf{W}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d});\mathbb{R}\right)
by computing its Wasserstein gradient. First we compute its first variation. For the convolved internal energy, we let

\mathcal{F}(\rho):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f((\rho\star\chi)(x))d\rho(x).

Fix
\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and define
\sigma:=\rho^{\prime}-\rho
. For
\varepsilon\neq 0
, set
\rho_{\varepsilon}:=\rho+\varepsilon\sigma
. Then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{F}(\rho_{\varepsilon})-\mathcal{F}(\rho)}{\varepsilon}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f((\rho\star\chi)(x))d\sigma(x)+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left(f^{\prime}(\rho\star\chi)\rho\star\chi\right)d\sigma(x)

Here we are using the notation for any
g\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

\left((g\rho)\star\chi\right)(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\chi(x-y)g(y)d\rho(y)

Hence, by standard identities of the first variations in Wasserstein space for the potential and interaction energy (see, e.g.,
[
29
]
), we conclude that

\frac{\delta U}{\delta\rho}(\rho,x)=f((\rho\star\chi)(x))+\left[f^{\prime}\left(\rho\star\chi\right)\rho\star\chi\right](x)+V(x)+(W\star\rho)(x).

Now to obtain the Wasserstein gradient, we differentiate in
x
to see that

\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}U(\rho,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\!\!\bigl[f^{\prime}\bigl((\rho*\chi)(x)\bigr)+f^{\prime}\bigl((\rho*\chi)(z)\bigr)\bigr]\nabla\chi(x-z)\,d\rho(z)+\nabla V(x)+(\nabla W\star\rho)(x).

(2.6)

The joint continuity of
\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}U
follows from the smoothness of
f^{\prime}
,
\nabla\chi
,
\nabla V
, and
\nabla W
along with the observation that
\rho\mapsto(\chi\star\rho)
and
\rho\mapsto(\nabla\chi\star\rho)
are Lipschitz continuous on the
\mathbf{W}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
metric uniformly in
x
due the dual problem formulation of the
\mathbf{W}_{1}
metric.

Property
(2)
follows from the assumptions
\nabla^{2}V,\nabla^{2}W,\nabla^{2}\chi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
f^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, and the compact support of
\chi
.

For the Wasserstein hessian, we first compute the first variation of (
2.6
) with
x
fixed. For the internal energy term, we set
\rho_{\chi}:=\rho\star\chi
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{\varepsilon},x)-\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\mathcal{F}(\rho,x)}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\!\!\bigl[f^{\prime}(\rho_{\chi}(x))+f^{\prime}(\rho_{\chi}(z))\bigr]\nabla\chi(x-z)\,d\sigma(z)

\displaystyle\quad+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\!\!\bigl[f^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{\chi}(x))\,\chi(x-v)+f^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{\chi}(z))\,\chi(z-v)\bigr]\nabla\chi(x-z)\,d\rho(z)\,d\sigma(v).

For the interaction energy term,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{(\nabla W\star\rho_{\varepsilon})(x)-(\nabla W\star\rho)(x)}{\varepsilon}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla W(x-x^{\prime})\,d\sigma(x^{\prime}).

Hence, the first variation of the Wasserstein gradient is

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\,\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}U}{\delta\mu}(\rho;x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\nabla W(x-x^{\prime})+\bigl[f^{\prime}(\rho_{\chi}(x))+f^{\prime}(\rho_{\chi}(x^{\prime}))\bigr]\,\nabla\chi(x-x^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\!\!\bigl[f^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{\chi}(x))\,\chi(x-x^{\prime})+f^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{\chi}(z))\,\chi(z-x^{\prime})\bigr]\nabla\chi(x-z)\,d\rho(z).

Differentiating in
x^{\prime}
gives the
(i,j)
-th entry of the Wasserstein hessian:

\displaystyle-[\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{W}}U]_{i,j}(\rho,x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=[\nabla^{2}W(x-x^{\prime})]_{i,j}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\!\!\bigl[f^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{\chi}(z))\,\partial_{j}\chi(z-x^{\prime})+f^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{\chi}(x))\,\partial_{j}\chi(x-x^{\prime})\bigr]\partial_{i}\chi(x-z)\,d\rho(z)

\displaystyle+\bigl[f^{\prime}(\rho_{\chi}(x))+f^{\prime}(\rho_{\chi}(x^{\prime}))\bigr]\partial^{2}_{i,j}\chi(x-x^{\prime})-f^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{\chi}(x^{\prime}))\,\partial_{i}\chi(x-x^{\prime})\,(\rho*\partial_{j}\chi)(x^{\prime}).

So to check Property (2), it suffices to check joint continuity of
\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{W}}U
. This follows from the smoothness of
\chi
,
W
, and
f^{\prime\prime}
Property (3) follows from the boundedness of all the first and second derivative terms.
∎

2.3
Convexity and Differentiability of the Lifted Functional

In this section, we study the differential and convexity properties of the lifted functionals
\phi_{\mu}^{\#}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)\to\mathbb{R}
arising in Mean Field Games. The results of
[
21
,
22
,
9
]
establish that the convexity and differentiability of
\phi
in the Wasserstein space
\mathbf{W}_{2}
are closely linked to the convexity and differentiability of
\phi_{\mu}^{\#}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)
.

Definition 2.9

(Lifted Energy Functional)
.

Let
\phi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The
lift
of
\phi

by

\mu
is the functional
\phi^{\#}_{\mu}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(\xi):=\phi(\xi_{\#}\mu).

Furthermore, if
\phi^{\#}_{\mu}
is Fréchet differentiable at
\xi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)
, we denote its Fréchet derivative in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)
by
\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}
.

We begin by stating the connection between convexity
\phi^{\#}_{\mu}
and
\phi
. We first recall the standard definition of
\lambda
-convexity for functions over Hilbert spaces.

Definition 2.10

(Flat Convexity)
.

Given any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we say that
\phi^{\#}_{\mu}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)\to\mathbb{R}
, as defined in Definition
2.9
, is
\lambda
-convex if

\phi^{\#}_{\mu}((1-t)\xi_{1}+t\xi_{2})\leq(1-t)\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(\xi_{1})+t\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(\xi_{2})-\frac{\lambda}{2}t(1-t)\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}

for all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)
and
t\in[0,1]
.

Let us also recall the notion of displacement convexity.

Definition 2.11

(Displacement Convexity)
.

A functional
\phi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
\lambda
-
displacement convex
if, for any
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and any
constant-speed geodesic

(\gamma_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
with
\gamma_{0}=\mu
and
\gamma_{1}=\nu
, we have

\phi(\gamma_{t})\leq(1-t)\phi(\mu)+t\phi(\nu)-\frac{\lambda}{2}t(1-t)W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu),\quad\forall t\in[0,1].

Now we formally derive the connection between the Wasserstein hessian and displacement convexity:

Remark 2.12

(Displacement Convexity and Wasserstein Derivatives)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure.
Fix
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and let
T
denote the optimal transport map from
\mu
to
\nu
. Then the constant-speed geodesic
(\gamma_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
from
\mu
to
\nu
is given by

\gamma_{t}:=\big((1-t)\mathrm{Id}+tT\big)_{\#}\mu.

Assume the energy functional
\phi:\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to{\mathbb{R}}
is sufficiently smooth. Then, denoting the velocity field by
v(x):=T(x)-x
and
T_{t}(x)=(1-t)x+tT(x)
, we have the following expressions for the first and second derivatives of
\phi
along the geodesic:

\frac{d}{dt}\phi(\gamma_{t})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\gamma_{t},T_{t}(x)),\,v(x)\right\rangle d\mu(x),

(2.7)

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\phi(\gamma_{t})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left\langle\nabla_{x}\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\gamma_{t},T_{t}(x))v(x),v(x)\right\rangle\,d\mu(x)+\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}\phi(\gamma_{t},T_{t}(x),T_{t}(z))v(z),v(x)\right\rangle d\mu(z)d\mu(x).

(2.8)

Observe from (
2.8
) that if the operator norm of
\nabla_{x}\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\rho,x,z)
and
\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\rho,x,z)
are uniformly bounded by
C
, then
\phi
is
-2C
displacement convex and
2C
displacement concave when the initial measure is absolutely continuous. Indeed, by Cauchy-Schwarz

\left|\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\phi(\gamma_{t})\right|\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|v(x)|^{2}d\mu(x)+C\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|v(z)|\cdot|v(x)|d\mu(z)d\mu(x).

So as
W_{2}(\mu,\nu)=||v(x)||_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\mu)}
, we conclude from Jensen’s inequality that

\left|\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\phi(\gamma_{t})\right|\leq CW_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)+C||v(x)||_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\mu)}^{2}\leq 2CW_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu).

We now focus on formally computing these derivatives. To compute the first time derivative, we use (
2.3
) to see that

\phi(\gamma_{t+h})-\phi(\gamma_{t})\approx\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{\delta\phi}{\delta\mu}(\gamma_{t},x)d(\gamma_{t+h}-\gamma_{t})(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left(\frac{\delta\phi}{\delta\mu}(\gamma_{t},T_{t+h})-\frac{\delta\phi}{\delta\mu}(\gamma_{t},T_{t})\right)d\mu.

Then by Taylor expanding in the spatial coordinate, we obtain that

\approx h\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\gamma_{t},T_{t}(x)),v(x)\rangle d\mu(x),

which shows the equality of the first time derivative.

To see the second time derivative equality, let
F(\gamma_{t}):=\frac{d}{dt}\phi(\gamma_{t}),
then by (
2.7
)

F(\gamma_{t+h})-F(\gamma_{t})=(I)+(II)

=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\gamma_{t+h},T_{t+h}(x))-\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\gamma_{t+h},T_{t}(x)),v(x)\rangle d\mu(x)+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\gamma_{t+h},T_{t}(x))-\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\gamma_{t},T_{t}(x)),v(x)\rangle d\mu(x).

Then observe that by Taylor expanding in the spatial coordinate

(I)\approx h\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\langle\nabla_{x}\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\gamma_{t},T_{t}(x))v(x),v(x)\rangle d\mu(x)

and by using (
2.3
)

(II)\approx\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\langle\frac{\delta\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi}{\delta\mu}(\gamma_{t},T_{t}(x),z),v(x)\rangle d(\gamma_{t+h}-\gamma_{t})(z)d\mu(x)

=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\langle\frac{\delta\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi}{\delta\mu}(\gamma_{t},T_{t}(x),T_{t+h}(z))-\frac{\delta\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi}{\delta\mu}(\gamma_{t},T_{t}(x),T_{t}(z)),v(x)\rangle d\mu(z)d\mu(x),

so by Taylor expanding in the last spatial argument, we see that

\approx h\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\langle\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\gamma_{t},T_{t}(x),T_{t}(z))v(z),v(x)\rangle d\mu(z)d\mu(x).

This implies the formula for the second time derivative.

The notion of geodesic convexity of
\phi
and convexity of the lift
\phi^{\#}_{\mu}
are equivalent when
\phi
is continuous, as established in
[
21
]
.

Theorem 2.13
.

[
21
, Lemma 3.6]

Let
\phi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
be
continuous
and assume that
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
has no atoms. Then the following statements are equivalent:

1.

\phi
is
\lambda
-displacement convex on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

2.

\phi_{\mu}^{\#}
is
\lambda
-convex on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)
.

Proof.

Let
m
be the uniform probability measure on
[0,1]^{d}
. The result was proven in
[
21
, Lemma 3.6]
for
m
. To extend this to a general
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with no atoms, we claim that
\phi^{\#}_{m}
is
\lambda
-convex on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};m)
if and only if
\phi^{\#}_{\mu}
is
\lambda
-convex on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)
.

We first show that
\lambda
-convexity of
\phi^{\#}_{\mu}
implies
\lambda
-convexity of
\phi^{\#}_{m}
. Since
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is atomless and
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a Polish space, there exists a measurable map
T
such that
T_{\#}\mu=m
(see
[
9
, Page 379]
). Because
m\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we have that
T\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)
. For
i\in\{1,2\}
, fix
\xi_{i}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};m)
and note that
\xi_{i}\circ T\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)
. Since
\phi^{\#}_{\mu}
is
\lambda
-convex, we obtain

\phi^{\#}_{\mu}\big((1-t)\xi_{1}\circ T+t\xi_{2}\circ T\big)\leq(1-t)\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(\xi_{1}\circ T)+t\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(\xi_{2}\circ T)-\frac{\lambda}{2}t(1-t)\|\xi_{1}\circ T-\xi_{2}\circ T\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}.

Since
T_{\#}\mu=m
, this simplifies to

\phi^{\#}_{m}\big((1-t)\xi_{1}+t\xi_{2}\big)\leq(1-t)\phi^{\#}_{m}(\xi_{1})+t\phi^{\#}_{m}(\xi_{2})-\frac{\lambda}{2}t(1-t)\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};m)},

which is precisely the definition of
\lambda
-convexity for
\phi^{\#}_{m}
.

The reverse implication follows by the same argument with the roles of
\mu
and
m
interchanged.
∎

Now we focus on derivatives of
\phi^{\#}_{\mu}
. Under mild growth conditions on
\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi
, the lift is differentiable on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\mu)
:

Proposition 2.14
.

[
9
, Proposition 5.48]

Assume that
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
have no atoms. Suppose
\phi\in C^{1}(\mathbf{W}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d});\mathbb{R})
is such that for any bounded
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

1.

x\mapsto\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\rho,x)
has at most linear growth, uniformly for
\rho\in\mathcal{K}
.

2.

x\mapsto\frac{\delta\phi}{\delta\mu}(\rho,x)
has at most quadratic growth, uniformly for
\rho\in\mathcal{K}
.

Then the lift
\phi^{\#}_{\mu}
is Fréchet differentiable on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)
, with

\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(\xi)(x)=\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\xi_{\#}\mu,\xi(x))\quad\mu\textnormal{ almost everywhere}.

Proof.

We apply
[
9
, Proposition 5.48]
to the atom-less probability space
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mu)
, using the fact that for this probability space, the law of a random variable
X
is given by
X_{\#}\mu
. ∎

In
[
22
, Corollary 3.22]
the Fréchet differentiability of the lift at
\xi
is shown to be equivalent to the Wasserstein differentiability of
\phi
at
\xi_{\#}\mu
. In this case, we have that

\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(\xi)(x)=\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\xi_{\#}\mu,\xi(x))\quad\mu\text{ almost everywhere.}

We refer to
[
22
]
for the general definition of the Wasserstein gradient in terms of the sub and super differential of
\phi
.

We can now use Proposition
2.14
to find the hessian of the lift. By Theorem
2.13
, the hessian of the lift is closely related to
\lambda
-geodesic convexity.

Now we link notions of
C^{1,1}
of
\phi
with its lift.

Definition 2.15

(
C^{1,\alpha}(X;X)
Functions)
.

Let
X
be a Hilbert space and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. A functional
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
is said to belong to the class
C^{1,\alpha}(X;X)
if it is Fréchet differentiable everywhere, and there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
x,y\in X
,

\big\|\nabla u(x)-\nabla u(y)\big\|_{X}\leq C\big\|x-y\big\|_{X}^{\alpha}.

We will say
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{b}(X;X)
if
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(X;X)
and

\sup_{x\in X}|u(x)|+|\nabla u(x)|<\infty.

Example 2.16
.

Under the notation and assumptions of Example
2.8
, if
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is atomless, then the lifted functional
U^{\#}_{\mu}
belongs to
C^{1,1}(\mathbb{H}_{\mu};\mathbb{H}_{\mu})
for
\mathbb{H}_{\mu}:=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\mu)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.14
, we have that

\nabla U^{\#}_{\mu}(\xi)=\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}U(\xi_{\#}\mu,\xi).

Hence, for
\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{H}_{\mu}
, the triangle inequality implies

||\nabla U^{\#}_{\mu}(\xi_{1})-\nabla U^{\#}_{\mu}(\xi_{2})||_{\mathbb{H}_{\mu}}\leq

||\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}U((\xi_{1})_{\#}\mu,\xi_{1})-\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}U((\xi_{1})_{\#}\mu,\xi_{2})||_{\mathbb{H}_{\mu}}+||\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}U((\xi_{1})_{\#}\mu,\xi_{2})-\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}U((\xi_{2})_{\#}\mu,\xi_{2})||_{\mathbb{H}_{\mu}}.

Now by properties (2) and (3) of Example
2.8
, there exists a
C>0
such that

\leq C(||\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}||_{\mathbb{H}_{\mu}}+W_{2}((\xi_{1})_{\#}\mu,(\xi_{2})_{\#}\mu))\leq 2C||\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}||_{\mathbb{H}_{\mu}}.

∎

3
The Lagrangian Trapezoidal Scheme: Definition and Numerical Stability

In this section, we define a higher-order implicit method for the gradient flow of
\phi
and establish stability properties of the energy functional and its Wasserstein gradient. Our scheme corresponds to the implicit trapezoidal rule applied to the Lagrangian Flow (
1.5
). We begin by defining the variational problem associated with the scheme and then prove its well-posedness.

Definition 3.1

(Lagrangian Trapezoidal
Scheme)
.

Fix a reference measure
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and assume that
\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}
is Fréchet differentiable on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\rho_{0})
. Fix a time step
\tau>0
and an initial condition
X_{0}^{\tau}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\rho_{0})
. Then, for
n\geq 0
, define the discrete Lagrangian flows
X_{n+1}^{\tau}
via:

X_{n+1}^{\tau}\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\xi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\rho_{0})}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\xi)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})(x),\xi(x)\rangle\,d\rho_{0}(x)+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\xi-X_{n}^{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\rho_{0})}^{2}\right\}.

(3.1)

Finally, define the discrete trapezoidal rule solutions as the measures

\rho_{n}^{\tau}:=(X_{n}^{\tau})_{\#}\rho_{0}.

Now, under appropriate assumptions on
\phi
, we show that the scheme is well-defined for sufficiently small
\tau
.

Lemma 3.2

(Existence and Uniqueness of the Minimizer)
.

Given a
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we assume that
\phi^{\#}_{\mu}
is Fréchet differentiable on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\mu)
. We further assume that
\phi^{\#}_{\mu}
is
\lambda
-convex on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\mu)
for some
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Further, let
\tau>0
satisfy
(\lambda/2+1/\tau)>0
and fix an
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)
. Define the operator
\Phi_{\tau,\mu,v}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)\to\mathbb{R}
as

\Phi_{\tau,\mu,v}(\xi):=\frac{1}{2}\left(\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(\xi)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(v(x)),\xi(x)\rangle\,d\mu(x)\right)+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\xi-v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}.

Additionally, assume that

\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(v)(x)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}<\infty,

(3.2)

and that the energy functional
\phi
is proper, i.e.,
\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\phi(\mu)>-\infty
. Then, there exists a unique minimizer of
\Phi_{\tau,\mu,\eta}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)
.

Proof.

From our assumptions it follows that
\Phi_{\tau,\mu,v}(\xi)
is
(\lambda/2+1/\tau)
-convex in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)
, ensuring at most one minimizer.

The existence of a minimizer follows from the continuity and convexity of
\Phi_{\tau,\mu,v}(\xi)
. Define the constant

\beta:=\inf_{\xi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}\Phi_{\tau,\mu,v}(\xi)>-\infty,

where the lower bound holds due to (
3.2
) and the properness of
\phi
.

Now, consider a minimizing sequence
\{\xi_{n}\}
for
\Phi_{\tau,\mu,v}
. We claim that the
(\lambda/2+1/\tau)
-convexity of
\Phi_{\tau,\mu,v}
implies that
\{\xi_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)
. Defining the geodesic
\gamma(t):=(1-t)\xi_{n}+t\xi_{m}
implies from convexity that

\beta\leq\Phi_{\tau,\mu,v}\left(\gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{\tau,\mu,v}(\xi_{n})+\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{\tau,\mu,v}(\xi_{m})-\frac{1}{8}\underbrace{(\lambda/2+1/\tau)}_{>0}\|\xi_{n}-\xi_{m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}.

Since
\{\xi_{n}\}
is a minimizing sequence, we have
\Phi_{\tau,\mu,v}(\xi_{n})\to\beta
. This implies
\{\xi_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence, as
\|\xi_{n}-\xi_{m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}
must tend to 0 as
n,m\to\infty
. The continuity of
\Phi_{\tau,\mu,v}
ensures that the limit of
\xi_{n}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)
is the unique minimizer of
\Phi_{\tau,\mu,v}
.
∎

Now, we characterize the minimizer of
\Phi_{\tau,\mu,v}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Under the same notation and assumptions as Lemma
3.2
, the unique minimizer
\xi^{*}
of
\Phi_{\tau,\mu,v}
is the unique solution over
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\mu)
to the implicit equation:

\xi^{*}(x)=\eta(x)-\frac{\tau}{2}\left(\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(\xi^{*})(x)+\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(v)(x)\right).

Proof.

Since
\Phi_{\tau,\mu,v}
is strictly convex and Fréchet differentiable, its unique minimizer is also its unique critical point. The implicit equation relationship then follows from

\nabla\Phi_{\tau,\mu,v}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(\xi)+\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(v)\right)+\frac{1}{\tau}(\xi-v).

∎

Next, we will derive a useful estimate on the gradient of the minimizer of
\Phi_{\tau,\mu,\eta}
that ensures the gradient norm is bounded along our trapezoid iterates.

Lemma 3.4

(Gradient Estimate)
.

Under the assumptions and notation of Lemma
3.2
, define

C(\lambda,\tau):=\begin{cases}1,&\lambda\geq 0,\\
e^{2|\lambda_{\tau}|\tau},&\lambda<0,\end{cases}\quad\textnormal{where}\quad\lambda_{\tau}:=\frac{1}{2\tau}\log\left(\frac{1+\lambda\tau}{1-\lambda\tau}\right).

Then the unique minimizer
\xi^{*}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)
of
\Phi_{\tau,\mu,v}
satisfies

\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(\xi^{*})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}\leq C(\lambda,\tau)\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(v)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}.

Proof.

By
\lambda
-convexity, we have that for any
\eta\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)

\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(\xi^{*})-\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(v),\xi^{*}-v\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}\geq\lambda\|\xi^{*}-v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}^{2}.

Using Lemma
3.3
, we have that the above expression is

\frac{\tau}{2}\left(\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(v)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}-\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(\xi^{*})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}\right)\geq\lambda\|\xi^{*}-v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}.

(3.3)

This implies the claim for
\lambda\geq 0
.

Now, consider the case when
\lambda<0
. Applying Lemma
3.3
to (
3.3
), we express
\lambda\|\xi^{*}-v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}
as

\lambda\frac{\tau^{2}}{4}\left(\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(v)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}+\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(\xi^{*})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}+2\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(v),\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(\xi^{*})\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}\right).

(3.4)

Since
\lambda<0
, we apply Cauchy-Schwarz and the inequality
ab\leq\frac{a^{2}}{2}+\frac{b^{2}}{2}
to obtain

\lambda\|\xi^{*}-v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}\geq\lambda\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}\left(\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(v)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}+\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(\xi)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}\right).

(3.5)

Thus, from (
3.3
), we obtain the desired inequality of

\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(v)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}\geq\frac{(1+\lambda\tau)}{(1-\lambda\tau)}\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\mu}(\xi^{*})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)}.

∎

Now we focus on properties of our scheme (Definition
3.1
). We recall the following quantity, which will be frequently used

\mathcal{C}(\lambda,\tau):=\begin{cases}1,&\lambda\geq 0,\\
e^{2|\lambda_{\tau}|\tau},&\lambda<0,\end{cases}=\max\{1,e^{2\lambda_{\tau}\tau}\}\quad\textnormal{where}\quad\lambda_{\tau}:=\frac{1}{2\tau}\log\left(\frac{1+\lambda\tau}{1-\lambda\tau}\right).

(3.6)

Now Assumption
2
, Lemmas
3.2
,
3.3
, and
3.4
implies the following for our trapezoid scheme:

Theorem 3.5

(Trapezoid Rule and Gradient Estimate)
.

Under Assumption
2
and using its notation, there exists a unique minimizer in
\mathbb{H}
of (
3.1
) satisfying, for
\rho_{0}
-a.e.
x
,

X^{\tau}_{n+1}(x)=X^{\tau}_{n}(x)-\frac{\tau}{2}\left(\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})(x)+\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})(x)\right).

(3.7)

Additionally, the lifted gradient along the iterates satisfies the estimate:

\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n+1}^{\tau})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\mathcal{C}(\lambda,\tau)\cdot\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

(3.8)

Beyond the gradient estimate (
3.8
), we establish additional stability properties.

Lemma 3.6

(Energy is Almost Decreasing)
.

Under Assumption
2
and using its notation, with
X_{n}^{\tau}
and
\rho_{n}^{\tau}
defined as in Definition
3.1
, we have the following estimate:

\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})-\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})\leq\frac{\tau}{4}\left(\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n}^{\tau})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}-\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n+1}^{\tau})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\right).

(3.9)

Proof.

Using the competitor
X_{n}^{\tau}
in (
3.1
), we obtain:

\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})-\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})\leq\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n}^{\tau}),X_{n}^{\tau}-X_{n+1}^{\tau}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}-\frac{1}{\tau}\|X_{n+1}^{\tau}-X_{n}^{\tau}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

(3.10)

Applying equation (
3.7
), the right-hand side simplifies to:

\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n}^{\tau}),X_{n}^{\tau}-X_{n+1}^{\tau}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}-\frac{1}{\tau}\|X_{n+1}^{\tau}-X_{n}^{\tau}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}=\frac{\tau}{2}\left(\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n}^{\tau})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n}^{\tau}),\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n+1}^{\tau})\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}\right)-\frac{1}{\tau}\|X_{n+1}^{\tau}-X_{n}^{\tau}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

Using Theorem
3.5
on the last term, we find that the above expression further simplifies to:

\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n}^{\tau}),X_{n}^{\tau}-X_{n+1}^{\tau}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}-\frac{1}{\tau}\|X_{n+1}^{\tau}-X_{n}^{\tau}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}=\frac{\tau}{4}\left(\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n}^{\tau})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}-\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n+1}^{\tau})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\right).

Using the above display in (
3.10
) implies (
3.9
).
∎

To conclude this section, we establish a stability estimate similar to the classical one for the JKO scheme, along with a uniform Lipschitz time estimate for the numerical solutions.

Lemma 3.7

(Stability Estimate)
.

Under Assumption
2
and using its notation, with
X_{n}^{\tau}
and
\rho_{n}^{\tau}
defined as in Definition
3.1
, we have the following estimate for any
N
with
N\tau\leq T
:

\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{W_{2}^{2}(\rho^{\tau}_{j},\rho^{\tau}_{j+1})}{\tau}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{\|X_{j+1}^{\tau}-X_{j}^{\tau}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}{\tau}\leq T\cdot\tilde{\mathcal{C}}(\lambda,\tau,T)\cdot\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{0}^{\tau})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

(3.11)

Also for any
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
n,m\leq N
, we have the Lipschitz in time estimate:

W_{2}(\rho_{n}^{\tau},\rho_{m}^{\tau})\leq\|X^{\tau}_{n}-X^{\tau}_{m}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\tau\cdot|n-m|\cdot\sqrt{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}(\lambda,\tau,T)}\cdot\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{0})\|_{\mathbb{H}}.

(3.12)

where

\tilde{\mathcal{C}}(\lambda,\tau,T)=\max\{1,e^{-2\lambda_{\tau}T}\}.

Proof.

First, observe that for any
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
:

W_{2}^{2}(\rho_{n}^{\tau},\rho_{m}^{\tau})=W_{2}^{2}((X_{n}^{\tau})_{\#}\rho_{0},(X_{m}^{\tau})_{\#}\rho_{0})\leq\|X^{\tau}_{n}-X^{\tau}_{m}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},

where the final inequality follows from Lemma
2.2
. Thus, it suffices to show the bound on
X^{\tau}_{n}
.

For (
3.11
), we apply Theorem
3.5
to obtain:

\|X_{j+1}^{\tau}-X_{j}^{\tau}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}=\frac{\tau^{2}}{4}\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{j+1})+\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{j})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

By expanding out the square and applying Cauchy-Schwartz, we see that

\|X_{j+1}^{\tau}-X_{j}^{\tau}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}\left(\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{j+1})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{j})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\right).

Then, applying the gradient estimate in Theorem
3.5
, we obtain:

\|X_{j+1}^{\tau}-X_{j}^{\tau}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\tau^{2}\tilde{\mathcal{C}}(\lambda,\tau,T)\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{0}^{\tau})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

By summing, we obtain the bound (
3.11
).

To obtain (
3.12
), observe that for
n\geq m
the implicit equation in Theorem
3.5
implies:

X^{\tau}_{n}-X^{\tau}_{m}=\sum_{j=m}^{n-1}(X^{\tau}_{j+1}-X^{\tau}_{j})=-\frac{\tau}{2}\sum_{j=m}^{n-1}\left(\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{j+1})+\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{j})\right).

Using Theorem
3.5
, we conclude:

\|X^{\tau}_{n}-X^{\tau}_{m}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq|n-m|\cdot\tau\cdot\sqrt{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}(\lambda,\tau,T)}\cdot\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{0})\|_{\mathbb{H}}.

∎

4
O(\tau^{2})
Convergence via Differential Methods

We now establish the higher-order convergence of the Lagrangian Trapezoidal Scheme under the assumption that both
\phi
and the limiting velocity field are sufficiently smooth by extending the finite dimensional proofs to
\mathbb{H}
. First we begin by establishing existence of (
1.5
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}\in C_{b}^{1,1}(\mathbb{H};\mathbb{H})
. Then for any
\eta\in\mathbb{H}
, there exists a unique map
X\in L^{2}_{\textnormal{loc}}([0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d};\rho_{0})
such that

\begin{cases}\dot{X}(t)=-\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X(t))\quad\text{on }[0,\infty),\\
X(0)=\eta.\end{cases}

Proof.

This follows from a standard Banach’s Fixed Point theorem argument because
\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}\in C_{b}^{1,1}(\mathbb{H};\mathbb{H})
.
∎

To ensure the initial measure is
\rho_{0}
, we will often take as initial data
\eta=\text{Id}\in\mathbb{H}
. In addition, to ensure higher order convergence of our trapezoid scheme, we will also need to make a
C^{2,\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{H})
assumption on the Lagrangian flow.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
T>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
, and
X
be a Hilbert space. A function
u:[0,T]\to X
belongs to
C^{1,\alpha}([0,T];X)
if it is Fréchet differentiable and there exists a constant
C>0
such that its Fréchet derivative
\dot{u}:[0,T]\to X
satisfies

\|u(t+h)-u(t)-h\dot{u}(t)\|_{X}\leq C|h|^{1+\alpha},\quad\forall\,0\leq t,t+h\leq T.

(4.1)

We say
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\textnormal{loc}}([0,\infty);X)
if
u\in C^{1,\alpha}([0,T];X)
for every
T>0
.

Assumption 3

(Higher-Order Convergence)
.

Fix
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and define
\mathbb{H}:=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\rho_{0})
. Assume
\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}\in C_{b}^{1,1}(\mathbb{H};\mathbb{H})
, and define the Lipschitz constant of its gradient by

L(\phi):=\sup_{\xi_{1}\neq\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{H}}\frac{\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\xi_{1})-\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\xi_{2})\|_{\mathbb{H}}}{\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}}}<\infty.

Let
X\in L^{2}_{\textnormal{loc}}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\rho_{0})
be the unique solution to (
1.5
). We further assume there exists an
\alpha\in(0,1]
such that
\dot{X}(t,\cdot)\in C_{\textnormal{loc}}^{1,\alpha}([0,\infty);\mathbb{H})
. For each
T>0
, define

L_{\alpha}(T,\ddot{X}):=\sup_{t\neq s\in[0,T]}\frac{\|\dot{X}_{t}-\dot{X}_{s}-(t-s)\ddot{X}_{s}\|_{\mathbb{H}}}{|t-s|^{\alpha}}<\infty.

(4.2)

We begin by extending the trapezoidal quadrature rule to the Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Fix
T>0
,
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and suppose
u\in C^{1,\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{H})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Let
L
be the smallest constant for which
u
satisfies (
4.1
). Then, for any
0\leq a\leq b\leq T
, we have

\left\|\frac{b-a}{2}\left(u(b,x)+u(a,x)\right)-\int_{a}^{b}u(s,x)\,ds\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq 2L|b-a|^{2+\alpha}.

(4.3)

Proof.

We first observe that Jensen’s inequality and Fubini’s theorem imply for any
f

\left\|\int_{a}^{b}f(t,x)\,dt\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\leq(b-a)\int_{a}^{b}\|f(t,x)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\,dt.

(4.4)

We begin by decomposing the integrand in (
4.3
). Observe that the integrand decomposes as

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{2}\left[u(s,x)-u(b,x)\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left[u(s,x)-u(a,x)\right]\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{a}^{b}(s-b)\dot{u}(b,x)\,ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{a}^{b}(s-a)\dot{u}(a,x)\,ds+\mathcal{R},

(4.5)

where the remainder term is

\mathcal{R}=\frac{1}{2}\underbrace{\int_{a}^{b}[u(s,x)-u(b,x)-(s-b)\dot{u}(b,x)]\,ds}_{\mathcal{R}_{1}}+\frac{1}{2}\underbrace{\int_{a}^{b}[u(s,x)-u(a,x)-(s-a)\dot{u}(a,x)]\,ds}_{\mathcal{R}_{2}}.

We first bound the
\mathbb{H}
norm of the remainder term. Specifically, we will show that

\|\mathcal{R}_{i}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\frac{L}{\sqrt{2\alpha+3}}(b-a)^{2+\alpha},\quad\text{for }i\in\{1,2\}.

Since the argument is analogous for
\mathcal{R}_{2}
, we only provide the proof for
\mathcal{R}_{1}
.
Using (
4.4
) and Definition
4.2
, we have

\|\mathcal{R}_{1}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\leq(b-a)\int_{a}^{b}\|u(s,x)-u(b,x)-(s-b)\dot{u}(b,x)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\,ds\leq L^{2}(b-a)\int_{a}^{b}|s-b|^{2(1+\alpha)}\,ds=\frac{L^{2}}{2\alpha+3}(b-a)^{2\alpha+4}.

This gives the desired bound on
\mathcal{R}_{1}
.

Next, we control the remaining terms in (
4.5
). Notice that

\int_{a}^{b}(s-b)\dot{u}(b,x)+(s-a)\dot{u}(a,x)\,ds=\frac{(a-b)^{2}}{2}(\dot{u}(a,x)-\dot{u}(b,x)).

Thus,

\left\|\int_{a}^{b}(s-b)\dot{u}(b,x)+(s-a)\dot{u}(a,x)\,ds\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}=\frac{(b-a)^{2}}{2}\|\dot{u}(a,x)-\dot{u}(b,x)\|_{\mathbb{H}}.

Since
\dot{u}(t,x)
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous on
\mathbb{H}
with Hölder constant
2L
, we have

\left\|\int_{a}^{b}(s-b)\dot{u}(b,x)+(s-a)\dot{u}(a,x)\,ds\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq L(b-a)^{2+\alpha}.

Combining this with our bound on
\mathcal{R}
, we conclude from (
4.5
):

\left\|\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{2}\left[u(s,x)-u(b,x)\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left[u(s,x)-u(a,x)\right]\,ds\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq 2L|b-a|^{2+\alpha}.

∎

We now have the tools needed to establish the higher-order convergence of our scheme.

Theorem 4.4

(Higher Order Convergence)
.

Suppose Assumption
3
holds and the time step satisfies
0<\tau\leq\frac{1}{L(\phi)}
. Given a terminal time
T>0
, let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
(n+1)\tau\leq T
. Then, the iterates
X_{n}^{\tau}
of the Lagrangian trapezoid scheme (Definition
3.1
) satisfy

W_{2}(\rho_{n\tau},\rho_{n}^{\tau})\leq\|X_{n}^{\tau}-X(n\tau)\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq e^{2L(\phi)T}\|X_{0}^{\tau}-\textnormal{Id}\|_{\mathbb{H}}+2\frac{L(T,\ddot{X})}{L(\phi)}(e^{2L(\phi)T}-1)\,\tau^{1+\alpha},

where
X(t)
is the unique solution to (
1.5
) in
L^{2}_{\textnormal{loc}}([0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d};\rho_{0})
, and we define
\rho_{t}:=(X_{t})_{\#}\rho_{0}
and
\rho_{j}^{\tau}:=(X_{j}^{\tau})_{\#}\rho_{0}
.

Proof.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define the error iterates

e_{n+1}:=\|X_{n+1}^{\tau}-X((n+1)\tau)\|_{\mathbb{H}},

along with the trapezoid approximation of
X((n+1)\tau)
:

Y_{n+1}^{\tau}:=X(n\tau)-\frac{\tau}{2}\left(\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X(n\tau))+\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X((n+1)\tau))\right).

The triangle inequality yields

e_{n+1}\leq\underbrace{\|Y_{n+1}^{\tau}-X((n+1)\tau)\|_{\mathbb{H}}}_{\mathcal{I}_{n+1}^{(1)}}+\underbrace{\|Y_{n+1}^{\tau}-X^{\tau}_{n+1}\|_{\mathbb{H}}}_{\mathcal{I}_{n+1}^{(2)}}.

To control
\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{n+1}
, recall from (
1.5
) that for any
s<t
,

X(t)=X(s)-\int_{s}^{t}\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X(a))\,da,\quad\rho_{0}\text{-a.e.}

(4.6)

Then by choosing
s=n\tau
and
t=(n+1)\tau
, we see by Lemma
4.3
and Assumption
3
that

\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{n+1}\leq 2L_{\alpha}(T,\ddot{X})\,\tau^{2+\alpha}.

Next, to control
\mathcal{I}^{(2)}_{n+1}
, use (
4.6
) to write

Y_{n+1}^{\tau}-X^{\tau}_{n+1}=(X(n\tau)-X_{n}^{\tau})-\frac{\tau}{2}\left(\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X(n\tau))-\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n}^{\tau})\right)-\frac{\tau}{2}\left(\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X((n+1)\tau))-\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n+1}^{\tau})\right).

Thus, the triangle inequality and Assumption
3
yield

\mathcal{I}^{(2)}_{n+1}\leq e_{n}+\tau\frac{L(\phi)}{2}(e_{n}+e_{n+1}).

Combining these estimates, we have

e_{n+1}\leq\frac{2L_{\alpha}(T,\ddot{X})\tau^{2+\alpha}}{1-\frac{L(\phi)\tau}{2}}+\frac{(1+\frac{L(\phi)\tau}{2})e_{n}}{1-\frac{L(\phi)\tau}{2}}.

Since we assumed
\tau\leq\frac{1}{L(\phi)}
, it follows that
L(\phi)\tau\leq 1
. Hence, we have that
1-\frac{L(\phi)\tau}{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Hence,

e_{n+1}\leq 4L_{\alpha}(T,\ddot{X})\tau^{2+\alpha}+\frac{(1+\frac{L(\phi)\tau}{2})e_{n}}{1-\frac{L(\phi)\tau}{2}}.

Now by using the inequality with
x=L(\phi)\tau/2

\frac{1+x}{1-x}\leq 1+4x,\quad\text{for }x\in[0,\tfrac{1}{2}],

we obtain

e_{n+1}\leq 4L_{\alpha}(T,\ddot{X})\tau^{2+\alpha}+(1+2L(\phi)\tau)e_{n}.

(4.7)

By iterating (
4.7
), we arrive at

e_{n}\leq(1+2L(\phi)\tau)^{n}e_{0}+4L_{\alpha}(T,\ddot{X})\tau^{2+\alpha}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(1+2L(\phi)\tau)^{j}.

Thus, for
(n+1)\tau\leq T
, we have

e_{n}\leq e^{2L(\phi)T}e_{0}+2\frac{L_{\alpha}(T,\ddot{X})}{L(\phi)}(e^{2L(\phi)T}-1)\tau^{1+\alpha}.

∎

In particular, if
\dot{X}\in C^{1,1}_{\textnormal{loc}}([0,T];\mathbb{H})
, we obtain an
O(\tau^{2})
convergence rate.

4.1
Examples of Functionals Satisfying Higher-Order Convergence

Now we focus on deriving examples that satisfy our higher order convergence theorem.

Theorem 4.5
.

Assume that
\rho_{0}
is a non-atomic measure such that
\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}\in C^{1,1}([0,\infty);{\mathbb{R}}^{d})

[\nabla_{x}\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\rho,\cdot)]_{C^{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+[\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\rho,\cdot,\cdot)]_{C^{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq C

Moreover, assume that the Wasserstein hessian of
\phi
satisfies the Hölder-condition:

\sup_{\mu\neq\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\frac{\|\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\mu,x,y)-\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\nu,x,y)\|}{W^{\alpha}_{1}(\mu,\nu)}\leq C.

(4.8)

Then,
\dot{X}\in C^{1,\alpha}([0,\infty);\mathbb{H})
, and its Fréchet derivative is given by:

\ddot{X}(t,x)=\bigl[\nabla_{x}\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi\bigl((X(t))_{\#}\rho_{0},X(t,x)\bigr)\bigr]\dot{X}(t,x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigl(\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{W}}\phi\bigl((X(t))_{\#}\rho_{0},X(t,x),X(t,z)\bigr)\bigr)\dot{X}(t,z)\,d\rho_{0}(z).

Proof.

Because of our assumptions, we have that
\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\xi)(x)=\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\xi_{\#}\rho_{0},\xi(x))
for
\rho_{0}
-a.e.
x
.

Step 1:
X(t)\in C^{1,1}([0,\infty);\mathbb{H})
and Lipschitz Regularity in Time

Because
\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\rho,x)
is uniformly bounded on
{\rm{spt}}(\rho_{0})
, we immediately obtain that
\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\xi)
is uniformly bounded on
\mathbb{H}
. This implies from (
1.5
) that
X(t)
is Lipschitz continuous on
\mathbb{H}
. Then as
\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\xi)
is Lipschitz on
\mathbb{H}
, (
1.5
) then implies
X(t)\in C^{1,1}([0,\infty);\mathbb{H})
.

Now using (
1.5
), we have that for a.e.
x\in\text{spt}(\rho_{0})

X(t+h,x)-X(t,x)=-\int_{t}^{t+h}\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(X(s)_{\#}\rho_{0},X(s,x))ds.

From our assumption that the Wasserstein gradient is uniformly bounded, we obtain for a.e.
x\in\text{spt}(\rho_{0})

|X(t+h,x)-X(t,x)|\leq C|h|

(4.9)

Step 2 :
\dot{X}_{t}\in C^{1,\alpha}([0,\infty);\mathbb{H})

For notational simplicity, we will let
F(\rho,x):=\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\rho,x)
and
\rho_{t}:=(X_{t})_{\#}\rho_{0}
.

Using (
1.5
), we obtain for
\rho_{0}
-a.e.
x
:

\dot{X}(t,x)-\dot{X}(t+h,x)=F(\rho_{t},X(t,x))-F(\rho_{t+h},X(t+h,x)).

=\underbrace{\left[F(\rho_{t},X(t,x))-F(\rho_{t},X(t+h,x))\right]}_{(I)}+\underbrace{[F(\rho_{t},X(t+h,x))-F(\rho_{t+h},X(t+h,x))]}_{(II)}

Bounding Term (I).
As
x\mapsto\nabla_{x}F(\rho,x)
is Hölder continuous, we obtain from Taylor’s Theorem

(I)=-h\nabla_{x}F(\rho_{t},X(t,x))\cdot(\frac{X(t+h,x)-X(t,x)}{h})+\tilde{\mathcal{R}}

where
|\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(t,x)|\leq C^{\prime}|X(t+h,x)-X(t,x)|^{1+\alpha}
, which implies from (
4.9
) that
||\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(t,\cdot)||_{\mathbb{H}}\leq C^{\prime\prime}|h|^{1+\alpha}
.

Simplifying further, we have that

(I)=-\left(\nabla_{x}F(\rho_{t},X(t,x))\right)\dot{X}(t,x)+\mathcal{R}_{1},

where

\mathcal{R}_{1}=\tilde{\mathcal{R}}-\underbrace{h\left(\nabla_{x}F(\rho_{t},X(t,x)\right)\cdot\left(\frac{X(t+h,x)-X(t,x)}{h}-\dot{X}(t,x)\right)}_{\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{2}}.

From
X(t)\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{H};\mathbb{H})
and
\nabla_{x}F
being uniformly bounded, we see that
||\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{2}||_{\mathbb{H}}=O(|h|^{2})
. In particular, we have that
||\mathcal{R}_{1}||_{\mathbb{H}}=O(|h|^{1+\alpha})
.

Bounding Term (II).
Applying (
2.3
), we obtain:

(II)=-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mu}\bigl((1-s)\rho_{t}+s\rho_{t+h},X(t+h,x),z\bigr)d(\rho_{t+h}-\rho_{t})(z)ds.

Using Taylor’s Theorem with
y\mapsto\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\rho,x,y)
is uniformly
C^{\alpha}

=-h\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}F((1-s)\rho_{t}+s\rho_{t+h},X(t+h,x),X(t,z))\cdot\frac{(X(t,z)-X(t+h,z))}{h}d\rho_{0}(z)ds+\mathcal{R}_{2},

where
||\mathcal{R}_{2}(t,\cdot)||_{\mathbb{H}}=O(h^{1+\alpha})
.

Because
X\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{H};\mathbb{H})
and the boundedness of
\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}F
, we obtain:

(II)=h\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}F((1-s)\rho_{t}+s\rho_{t+h},X(t+h,x),X(t,z))\cdot\dot{X}(t,z)d\rho_{0}(z)ds+\mathcal{R}_{3},

where

\|\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{3}\|_{\mathbb{H}}=O(h^{2})
.

Now we use that
x\mapsto\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}F(\rho,x,y)
is uniformly

and (
4.9

(II)=h\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}F((1-s)\rho_{t}+s\rho_{t+h},X(t,x),X(t,z))\cdot\dot{X}(t,z)d\rho_{0}(z)ds+\mathcal{R}_{4},

Finally, using the Hölder condition on the measure position of

||\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi((1-s)\rho_{t}+s\rho_{t+h},X(t,x),X(t,z))-\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\phi(\rho_{t},X(t,x),X(t,z))||_{\mathbb{H}}

.

(II)=h\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}F(\rho_{t},X(t,x),X(t,z))\cdot\dot{X}(t,z)d\rho_{0}(z)+\mathcal{R}_{5},

, completing the proof.
∎

Example 4.6
.

Suppose
U(\rho)
is the functional from Example

, and additionally assume that
V,W\in C^{2,1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

.

By the gradient formulas in Example
2.8

4.8
). We now check this term by term for the Wasserstein hessian formula in Example

. Throughout the proof, we set
\mu_{\chi}:=\mu\star\chi

for some constant

.

The difference in first term of the Wasserstein hessian formula at the

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f^{\prime\prime}(\mu_{\chi}(z))\partial_{j}\chi(z-x^{\prime})\partial_{i}\chi(x-z)d\mu(z)-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f^{\prime\prime}(\nu_{\chi}(z))\partial_{j}\chi(z-x^{\prime})\partial_{i}\chi(x-z)d\nu(z)

|f^{\prime}(\mu_{\chi}(z))-f^{\prime}(\nu_{\chi}(z))|\lesssim\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\chi(z-x)d(\mu-\nu)(x)\right|\lesssim W_{1}(\mu,\nu).

Hence, one sees that

The difference in the fourth and final term of the Wasserstein Hessian at the

th component is

(f^{\prime\prime}(\nu_{\chi}(x^{\prime}))-f^{\prime\prime}(\mu_{\chi}(x^{\prime})))\cdot\partial_{i}\chi(x-x^{\prime})(\nu\star\partial_{j}\chi)(x^{\prime})+f^{\prime\prime}(\mu_{\chi}(x^{\prime}))\cdot\partial_{i}\chi(x-x^{\prime})\cdot((\nu-\mu)\star\partial_{j}\chi)(x^{\prime})=(V)+(VI)

Now because
\nabla\chi\in L^{\infty}
and
f^{\prime\prime}
is Lipschitz, we have from the dual formula of the
W_{1}
metric

|(V)|\lesssim|\nu_{\chi}(x^{\prime})-\mu_{\chi}(x^{\prime})|=\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\chi(x^{\prime}-z)d(\nu-\mu)(z)\right|\lesssim W_{1}(\mu,\nu).

Finally, one has that from
f^{\prime\prime},\nabla\chi\in L^{\infty}
and
\partial_{j}\chi
is Lipschitz that from the dual formula of the
W_{1}
metric.

|(VI)|\lesssim|(\nu-\mu)\star\partial_{j}\chi(x^{\prime})|=\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\partial_{j}\chi(x^{\prime}-z)d(\nu-\mu)(z)\right|\lesssim W_{1}(\mu,\nu).

Therefore, one has that

|\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}U(\mu,x,x^{\prime})-\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}U(\nu,x,x^{\prime})|\lesssim W_{1}(\mu,\nu),

which is (
4.8
).
∎

5
O(\tau)
convergence via Discrete EVI

5.1
Discrete EVI

In this section, we show that the maps
X^{\tau}_{n}
for less regular functionals converge in
\mathbb{H}
at a rate of
O(\tau)
and characterize the limit in terms of an Evolution Variational Inequality (EVI).
To achieve these goals, we utilize the
discrete evolution variational inequality
. Our approach is inspired by the methods in
[
4
]
. Before deriving the discrete EVI, we first present a useful inequality.

Lemma 5.1
.

Under the same notation and assumptions as in Theorem
3.5
, we have that for any
\xi\in\mathbb{H}
:

\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n}^{\tau}),\xi-X_{n+1}^{\tau}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\left(\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\xi)-\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n+1}^{\tau})\right)+\frac{\tau}{2}\big(\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n}^{\tau})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}-\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n+1}^{\tau})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\big)-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|X^{\tau}_{n}-X^{\tau}_{n+1}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\xi-X^{\tau}_{n}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

Proof.

We first decompose the inner product as follows:

\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n}^{\tau}),\xi-X_{n+1}^{\tau}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}=\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n}^{\tau}),\xi-X_{n}^{\tau}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}+\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n}^{\tau}),X_{n}^{\tau}-X_{n+1}^{\tau}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}=(I)+(II).

(5.1)

To bound
(I)
, we use the tangent line inequality:

(I)\leq\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\xi)-\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\xi-X^{\tau}_{n}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

To bound
(II)
, we use the implicit equation in Theorem
3.5
to obtain:

(II)=\frac{\tau}{2}\left(\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n}),\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}\right).

The implicit equation in Theorem
3.5
also implies that

\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n}),\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}=-\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\frac{2}{\tau}\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1}),X^{\tau}_{n}-X^{\tau}_{n+1}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}.

Using the tangent line inequality on the inner product term implies

(II)\leq\frac{\tau}{2}\big(\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}-\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\big)+\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})-\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})-\frac{\lambda}{2}||X^{\tau}_{n}-X^{\tau}_{n+1}||^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

Combining the bounds on
(I)
and
(II)
allows us to conclude.
∎

Lemma 5.2

(Discrete EVI)
.

Under Assumption
2
, for any
\xi\in\mathbb{H}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have:

\frac{1}{2\tau}\left(\|\xi-X^{\tau}_{n+1}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}-\|\xi-X^{\tau}_{n}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\right)+\frac{\lambda}{4}\left(\|\xi-X^{\tau}_{n}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\|\xi-X^{\tau}_{n+1}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\right)

\leq\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\xi)-\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})+\frac{\tau}{4}\left(\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n}^{\tau})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}-\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\right)-\left(\frac{1}{2\tau}+\frac{\lambda}{4}\right)\|X^{\tau}_{n}-X^{\tau}_{n+1}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

Proof.

Following the arguments of
[
2
]
for the JKO scheme, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the functional

\Phi(\xi):=\frac{1}{2}\left(\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\xi)+\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n}^{\tau}),\xi\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}\right)+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\xi-X_{n}^{\tau}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

By Assumption
2
,
\Phi
is
(\lambda/2+1/\tau)
-convex. Since
X^{\tau}_{n+1}
minimizes
\Phi
due to Definition
3.1
, we obtain, for the linear interpolation
\gamma(t):=(1-t)X^{\tau}_{n+1}+t\xi
that

\Phi(X^{\tau}_{n+1})\leq\Phi(\gamma(t))\leq(1-t)\Phi(X^{\tau}_{n+1})+t\Phi(\xi)-\frac{t(1-t)}{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}+\frac{1}{\tau}\right)\|\xi-X^{\tau}_{n+1}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\quad\forall t\in(0,1].

In particular, this implies by dividing by
t
and then letting
t\rightarrow 0
gives

0\leq\Phi(\xi)-\Phi(X^{\tau}_{n+1})-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}+\frac{1}{\tau}\right)\|\xi-X^{\tau}_{n+1}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

(5.2)

Applying Lemma
5.1
to (
5.2
) implies the claim.
∎

5.2
Interpolations and Differential Inequalities

We recall that gradient flows of
\lambda
-convex functionals are uniquely characterized by the Evolution Variational Inequality (EVI) (see
[
2
]
). Our approach to obtaining an
O(\tau)
error rate is to consider linear interpolations of the discrete streamline and energy functional, and to show that the discrete EVI (Lemma
5.2
) implies that these interpolations satisfy an approximate EVI with a controlled error term. This combined with Grönwall’s inequality will allow us to obtain our error rate. Our methods are similar to those in
[
2
]
with adaptations to handle our more complicated case.

Definition 5.3

(Interpolations)
.

Fix a time step size
\tau>0
. Then we define

\ell_{\tau}(t):=\frac{t-n\tau}{\tau},\quad\overline{X}^{\tau}_{t}:=X^{\tau}_{n+1},\text{ and }\underline{X}^{\tau}_{t}:=X^{\tau}_{n},\quad\text{for }t\in[n\tau,(n+1)\tau).

The linearly interpolated numerical Lagrangian flow and energy functional are given by

\tilde{X}^{\tau}_{t}:=(1-\ell_{\tau}(t))\underline{X}^{\tau}_{t}+\ell_{\tau}(t)\overline{X}^{\tau}_{t},\quad\phi_{\tau}(t):=(1-\ell_{\tau}(t))\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\underline{X}^{\tau}_{t})+\ell_{\tau}(t)\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\overline{X}^{\tau}_{t}).

For
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{H}
, we define the metric

d(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}):=\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}},

and for any
\xi\in\mathbb{H}
, the linearly interpolated distance functional is

d^{2}_{\tau}(t;\xi):=(1-\ell_{\tau}(t))d^{2}(\xi,\underline{X}^{\tau}_{t})+\ell_{\tau}(t)d^{2}(\xi,\overline{X}^{\tau}_{t}).

Define the constant interpolation gradient error term as

G^{\tau}_{t}:=\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}-\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},\quad t\in[n\tau,(n+1)\tau).

and the distance between iterates as

\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau}:=d(\underline{X}^{\tau}_{t},\overline{X}^{\tau}_{t}).

Now with the notation of Definition
5.3
, we see that the discrete EVI (Lemma
5.2
) implies the following differential inequality for a.e.
t\geq 0

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}d^{2}_{\tau}(t;\xi)+\frac{\lambda}{4}\left(d^{2}(\xi,\underline{X}^{\tau}_{t})+d^{2}(\xi,\overline{X}^{\tau}_{t}))\right)\leq\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\xi)-\phi_{\tau}(t)+\frac{\tau}{4}G^{\tau}_{t}-\frac{\lambda}{4}(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})^{2}+\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\tau}.

(5.3)

Here, the remainder term
\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\tau}
is given by:

\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\tau}:=\left(\phi_{\tau}(t)-\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\overline{X}^{\tau}_{t})-\frac{1}{2\tau}(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})^{2}\right).

Expanding this term, we obtain:

\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\tau}=(1-\ell_{\tau}(t))\left(\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\underline{X}^{\tau}_{t})-\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\overline{X}^{\tau}_{t})-\frac{1}{\tau}(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\ell_{\tau}(t)-\frac{1}{2}\right)(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})^{2}.

(5.4)

When
\lambda\neq 0
, as in
[
2
]
, it is more convenient to modify the differential inequality (
5.3
) so that the left-hand side contains terms of the form
d^{2}_{\tau}(t;\xi)
instead of
d^{2}(\xi,\overline{X}^{\tau}_{t})
and
d^{2}(\xi,\underline{X}^{\tau}_{t})
. To achieve this, we use the following inequality:

Lemma 5.4
.

With the notation of Definition
5.3
, we have the bounds

d^{2}(\xi,\underline{X}_{t}^{\tau})+d^{2}(\xi,\overline{X}^{\tau}_{t})\leq 2d^{2}_{\tau}(t;\xi)+(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})^{2}+2(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})\cdot d_{\tau}(t;,\xi).

(5.5)

and

2d^{2}_{\tau}(t;\xi)-2(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})d_{\tau}(t;\xi)\leq d^{2}(\xi,\underline{X}_{t}^{\tau})+d^{2}(\xi,\overline{X}^{\tau}_{t})+3(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})^{2}.

(5.6)

Proof.

First observe that the concavity of the square root function implies that

(1-\ell_{\tau}(t))\cdot d(\underline{X}_{t}^{\tau},\xi)+\ell_{\tau}(t)\cdot d(\overline{X}^{\tau}_{t},\xi)\leq d_{\tau}(t;\xi).

(5.7)

Using triangle inequality and (
5.7
) we see that

d(\underline{X}_{t}^{\tau},\xi)=(1-\ell_{\tau}(t))d(\underline{X}_{t}^{\tau},\xi)+\ell_{\tau}(t)d(\underline{X}_{t}^{\tau},\xi)\leq d_{\tau}(t;\xi)+\ell_{\tau}(t)(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau}).

(5.8)

Similarly, we also have that

d(\overline{X}_{t}^{\tau},\xi)\leq d_{\tau}(t;\xi)+(1-\ell_{\tau}(t))(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau}).

(5.9)

Squaring these two inequalities and adding them imply (
5.5
).

Now for (
5.6
), observe that from the triangle inequality

d^{2}_{\tau}(t;\xi)=d^{2}(\underline{X}_{t}^{\tau},\xi)+\ell_{\tau}(t)\left(d^{2}(\overline{X}_{t}^{\tau},\xi)-d^{2}(\underline{X}_{t}^{\tau},\xi)\right)\leq d^{2}(\underline{X}_{t}^{\tau},\xi)+\ell_{\tau}(t)((\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})^{2}+2d(\underline{X}_{t}^{\tau},\xi)(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})).

So now using (
5.8
), we obtain that

d^{2}_{\tau}(t;\xi)\leq d^{2}(\underline{X}_{t}^{\tau},\xi)+2\ell_{\tau}(t)(d_{\tau}(t;\xi)(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau}))+\ell_{\tau}(t)(1+2\ell_{\tau}(t))(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})^{2}.

Similarly, we have the bound

d^{2}_{\tau}(t;\xi)\leq d^{2}(\overline{X}_{t}^{\tau},\xi)+2(1-\ell_{\tau}(t))(d_{\tau}(t;\xi)(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau}))+(1-\ell_{\tau}(t))(1+2(1-\ell_{\tau}(t)))(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})^{2}.

Adding these two bounds and maximizing over
t
, gives (
5.6
). ∎

By combining (
5.3
) with Lemma
5.4
, we obtain the following differential inequality:

Theorem 5.5

(Approximate EVI)
.

Under Assumption
2
and the notation introduced therein, as well as the notation from Definition
5.3
, the following differential inequalities holds:

\frac{d}{dt}d^{2}_{\tau}(t;\xi)+\lambda d^{2}_{\tau}(t;\xi)\leq 2(\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\xi)-\phi_{\tau}(t))+\frac{\tau}{2}G^{\tau}_{t}+2\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\tau}+|\lambda|(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})\cdot((\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})+d_{\tau}(t;\xi)).

(5.10)

Now we want to use Theorem
5.5
to obtain a differential inequality for
d^{2}(\underline{X}^{\tau}_{t},\underline{X}^{\eta}_{t})
with two different time steps
\tau,\eta>0
. To do so, we follow
[
2
]
and introduce another interpolation function

Definition 5.6
.

Using the notation from Definition
5.3
, let
\tau,\eta>0
be two time steps. We define the further interpolated distance functional as

d^{2}_{\tau,\eta}(t;s):=(1-\ell_{\tau}(t))[(1-\ell_{\eta}(s))\cdot d^{2}(\underline{X}^{\eta}_{s},\underline{X}^{\tau}_{t})+\ell_{\eta}(s)\cdot d^{2}(\overline{X}^{\eta}_{s},\underline{X}^{\tau}_{t})]+\ell_{\tau}(t)\left((1-\ell_{\eta}(s))d^{2}(\underline{X}^{\eta}_{s},\overline{X}^{\tau}_{t})+\ell_{\eta}(s)d^{2}(\overline{X}^{\eta}_{s},\overline{X}^{\tau}_{t})\right)

=(1-\ell_{\eta}(s))\cdot d^{2}_{\tau}(t;\underline{X}^{\eta}_{s})+\ell_{\eta}(s)\cdot d^{2}_{\tau}(t;\overline{X}^{\eta}_{s}).

Theorem 5.7

(Differential Inequality)
.

Fix two time steps
\tau,\eta>0
satisfying

\frac{\lambda}{2}+\min\left\{\frac{1}{\tau},\frac{1}{\eta}\right\}>0.

Using the assumptions and notation from Theorem
5.5
and from Definition
5.6
, we have

\frac{d}{dt}d^{2}_{\tau,\eta}(t;t)+2\lambda d^{2}_{\tau,\eta}(t;t)\leq\frac{\tau}{2}G^{\tau}_{t}+\frac{\eta}{2}G^{\eta}_{t}+2(\mathcal{R}^{\tau}_{t}+\mathcal{R}^{\eta}_{t})+|\lambda|\left((\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})^{2}+(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\eta})^{2}\right)+|\lambda|(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau}+\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\eta})d_{\tau,\eta}(t;t).

Proof.

First, we note the symmetry property of
d^{2}_{\tau,\eta}(t;s)
:

d^{2}_{\tau,\eta}(t;s)=d^{2}_{\eta,\tau}(s;t).

(5.11)

Next, by Definition
5.6
, Theorem
5.5
, and (
5.7
), we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}d^{2}_{\tau,\eta}(t;s)+\lambda d^{2}_{\tau,\eta}(t;s)\leq\phi_{\eta}(s)-\phi_{\tau}(t)+\frac{\tau}{4}G^{\tau}_{t}+\mathcal{R}^{\tau}_{t}+\frac{|\lambda|}{2}\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau}\left(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau}+d_{\tau,\eta}(t;s)\right).

By symmetry (
5.11
), we have
\frac{\partial}{\partial s}d^{2}_{\tau,\eta}(t;s)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}d^{2}_{\eta,\tau}(s;t)
, which gives

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}d^{2}_{\tau,\eta}(t;s)+\lambda d^{2}_{\tau,\eta}(t;s)\leq\phi_{\tau}(t)-\phi_{\eta}(s)+\frac{\eta}{4}G^{\eta}_{s}+\mathcal{R}^{\eta}_{s}+\frac{|\lambda|}{2}\mathcal{D}_{s}^{\eta}\left(\mathcal{D}_{s}^{\eta}+d_{\tau,\eta}(t;s)\right).

Summing these two inequalities at
s=t
implies the desired bound.
∎

5.3
O(\tau+\eta)
Convergence of
d_{\tau,\eta}(t;t)
for
\lambda\geq 0

The next step for obtaining convergence rates is to obtain bounds for the remainder terms
D^{\tau}_{t}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\tau}_{t}
in Theorem
5.7
.

Lemma 5.8

(Bounds on
\mathcal{R}^{\tau}_{t}
)
.

Under Assumption
2
and the notation of Theorem
5.5
, the following bound holds:

\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\tau}\leq\left(1-\ell_{\tau}(t)\right)\left(\frac{\tau}{4}G^{\tau}_{t}-\frac{\lambda}{2}(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\ell_{\tau}(t)-\frac{1}{2}\right)(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})^{2}.

Proof.

By (
5.4
), it suffices to show that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})-\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})-\frac{1}{\tau}\|X^{\tau}_{n+1}-X^{\tau}_{n}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\frac{\tau}{4}G^{\tau}_{n\tau}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|X^{\tau}_{n}-X^{\tau}_{n+1}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

Because
\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}
is
\lambda
-convex on
\mathbb{H}
, the tangent line inequality implies

\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})-\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})-\frac{1}{\tau}\|X^{\tau}_{n+1}-X^{\tau}_{n}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n}),X^{\tau}_{n}-X^{\tau}_{n+1}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}-\frac{1}{\tau}\|X^{\tau}_{n+1}-X^{\tau}_{n}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|X^{\tau}_{n}-X^{\tau}_{n+1}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

Using (
3.7
), we obtain

\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n}),X^{\tau}_{n}-X^{\tau}_{n+1}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}-\frac{1}{\tau}\|X^{\tau}_{n+1}-X^{\tau}_{n}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}=\frac{\tau}{4}G^{\tau}_{n\tau}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Now we have enough to obtain an error estimate on
d_{\tau,\eta}(t;t)
when
\lambda\geq 0
.

Corollary 5.9

(Error Bound on
d_{\tau,\eta}(t;t)
for
\lambda\geq 0
)
.

Under the assumptions and notation of Theorem
5.7
, the following bound holds for
\lambda\geq 0
:

d^{2}_{\tau,\eta}(t;t)\leq\|X_{0}^{\tau}-X_{0}^{\eta}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\frac{7}{4}\left(\tau^{2}\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{0}^{\tau})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\eta^{2}\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{0}^{\eta})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\right).

Proof.

Because
\phi
is
\lambda
-convex with
\lambda\geq 0
, we can use Theorem
5.7
with
\lambda=0
to obtain:

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}d^{2}_{\tau,\eta}(t;\xi)\leq\frac{\tau}{4}(G^{\tau}_{t}+G^{\eta}_{t})+\mathcal{R}^{\tau}_{t}+\mathcal{R}^{\eta}_{t}.

(5.12)

For any
t\geq 0
, let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be the smallest integer such that
t\leq N\tau
.
Since
\lambda\geq 0
, it follows from (
3.8
) that
G^{\tau}_{t}\geq 0
. Hence, by integrating in time, we obtain:

\int_{0}^{t}G^{\tau}_{s}ds\leq\int_{0}^{N\tau}G^{\tau}_{s}ds=\tau\sum_{j=0}^{N}\left(\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{j})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}-\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{j+1})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\right)\leq\tau\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{0}^{\tau})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

(5.13)

Similarly,

\int_{0}^{t}G^{\eta}_{s}ds\leq\eta\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{0}^{\eta})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

(5.14)

Next, we handle the term
\mathcal{R}^{\tau}_{t}
. Observe that for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
:

\int_{j\tau}^{(j+1)\tau}\left(\ell_{\tau}(s)-\frac{1}{2}\right)ds=0,\quad\int_{j\tau}^{(j+1)\tau}(1-\ell_{\tau}(s))ds=\frac{\tau}{2}.

Applying Lemma
5.8
with
\lambda\geq 0
gives:

\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{R}^{\tau}_{s}ds\leq\frac{\tau^{2}}{8}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\left(\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{j}^{\tau})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}-\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{j+1}^{\tau})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\right)-\frac{\|\overline{X}^{\tau}_{t}-\underline{X}^{\tau}_{t}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}{\tau}\int_{(N-1)\tau}^{t}\left(\ell_{\tau}(s)-\frac{1}{2}\right)ds.

\leq\frac{\tau^{2}}{8}\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{0})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\frac{1}{2}\|\overline{X}^{\tau}_{t}-\underline{X}^{\tau}_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

By applying Lemma
3.7
along with the above display, we see that

\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{R}^{\tau}_{s}ds\leq\frac{5\tau^{2}}{8}\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{0})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

(5.15)

Applying the same argument for
\eta
, we obtain:

\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{R}^{\eta}_{s}ds\leq\frac{5\eta^{2}}{8}\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\eta}_{0})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

Finally, integrating (
5.12
) in time and applying the estimates (
5.13
) and (
5.15
), we obtain the desired bound.
∎

5.4
O(\tau+\eta)
Convergence of
d_{\tau,\eta}(t;t)
for
\lambda<0

We now derive convergence rates for
\lambda<0
. Since
\lambda_{\tau}\leq\lambda
(see Assumption
2
), any
\lambda
-convex function
\phi
is also
\lambda_{\tau}
-convex. Applying Theorem
5.7
with
\lambda_{\tau}
and using Gronwall’s Inequality
[
2
, Lemma 4.1.8]
, we obtain the bound
for any
T>0
:

d_{\tau,\eta}(T;T)\leq e^{-\lambda_{\tau}T}\left(d^{2}_{\tau,\eta}(0;0)+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\lambda_{\tau}s}a(s)ds\right)^{1/2}+e^{-\lambda_{\tau}T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\lambda_{\tau}s}b(s)ds.

(5.16)

Here, the functions
a(t)
and
b(t)
are given by

a(t):=\frac{\tau}{2}G^{\tau}_{t}+\frac{\eta}{2}G^{\eta}_{t}+2(\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\tau}+\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\eta})+|\lambda_{\tau}|\big((\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})^{2}+(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\eta})^{2}\big),\quad b(t):=|\lambda_{\tau}|\cdot(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau}+\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\eta}).

To estimate the integrals in (
5.16
), we will estimate each individual term in
a(t)
.

Lemma 5.10
.

Under the notation and assumptions of Theorem
5.7
, and with the additional assumptions that
\lambda<0
, we have the following estimates for any
T\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\lambda_{\tau}t}(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{2}(T+\tau)e^{-2\lambda_{\tau}\tau}\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{0}^{\tau})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},

(5.17)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\lambda_{\tau}t}G_{t}^{\tau}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\tau\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{0}^{\tau})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\lambda_{\tau}t}\mathcal{R}^{\tau}_{t}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{2}\left(\frac{1}{8}+e^{-2\lambda_{\tau}\tau}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{3}\lambda_{\tau}(T+\tau)\right)\right)\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{0})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{\lambda_{\tau}t}(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\tau(T+\tau)e^{-\lambda_{\tau}\tau}\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{0}^{\tau})\|_{\mathbb{H}}.

Proof.

Choose the smallest integer
N
such that
T\leq N\tau
.

Step 1: Bounding

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\lambda_{\tau}t}(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})^{2}\,dt
.

We first observe that

\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\lambda_{\tau}t}d^{2}(\underline{X}_{t}^{\tau},\overline{X}^{\tau}_{t})\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{N\tau}e^{2\lambda_{\tau}t}d^{2}(\underline{X}_{t}^{\tau},\overline{X}^{\tau}_{t})\,dt=\underbrace{\frac{e^{2\lambda_{\tau}\tau}-1}{2\lambda_{\tau}}}_{\geq 0}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}e^{2\lambda_{\tau}j\tau}d^{2}(X^{\tau}_{j},X^{\tau}_{j+1}).

(5.18)

By applying (
3.12
), we obtain

d^{2}(X^{\tau}_{j},X^{\tau}_{j+1})\leq e^{-2\lambda_{\tau}(j+1)\tau}\tau^{2}\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{0}^{\tau})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

(5.19)

Substituting this into (
5.18
) and using the inequalities
(e^{2\lambda_{\tau}\tau}-1)\geq 2\lambda_{\tau}\tau

Step 2: Bounding
\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\lambda_{\tau}t}G^{\tau}_{t}dt

Because

Where
(x)_{+}:=\max\{x,0\}

\alpha_{n}:=||\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n}^{\tau})||^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},

(5.20)

. Since
\lambda_{\tau}<0

=(I)+(II).

In the above inequality, we used

\int_{0}^{(N-1)\tau}\frac{e^{2\lambda_{\tau}t}}{\tau}(\frac{1}{2}-\ell_{\tau}(t))\cdot d^{2}(\overline{X}^{\tau}_{t},\underline{X}_{t}^{\tau})dt\leq-\frac{\lambda_{\tau}\tau}{6}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}e^{2\lambda_{\tau}j\tau}\cdot d^{2}(X^{\tau}_{j},X^{\tau}_{j+1})\leq-\frac{\lambda_{\tau}\tau^{2}}{6}e^{-2\lambda_{\tau}\tau}\cdot T\cdot||\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{0})||^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

(II)\leq\tau^{2}e^{-2\lambda_{\tau}\tau}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{3}\lambda_{\tau}(T+\tau)\right)\cdot||\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{0})||^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

Theorem 5.15

6

In this section, we show that when

The key to upgrading our previous result is that when
\lambda\geq 0
and
\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}
is
L
-Lipschitz, we can obtain a lower bound for
||X_{n}^{\tau}-X_{n+1}^{\tau}||^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}
in terms of
||\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})||^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

Lemma 6.1
.

Under Assumption
2
and its notation, suppose
\lambda\geq 0
and there exists a constant
L\geq 0
such that

\sup_{\xi_{1}\neq\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{H}}\frac{\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\xi_{1})-\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(\xi_{2})\|_{\mathbb{H}}}{\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}}}\leq L.

Then if
\tau\leq\frac{1}{L}
, we have the following estimate for any
n\in\mathbb{N}

\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n}),\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}\geq(1-L\tau)||\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})||_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\textnormal{ and }\|X_{n}^{\tau}-X_{n+1}^{\tau}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\geq\tau^{2}(1-\frac{L\tau}{2})\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n+1}^{\tau})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

(6.1)

Proof.

We observe that the inner product inequality implies the other inequality because it along with using Lemma
3.5
with
\lambda\geq 0
implies

||X^{\tau}_{n}-X^{\tau}_{n+1}||^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}=\frac{\tau^{2}}{4}\left(||\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})||_{\mathbb{H}}+||\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})||_{\mathbb{H}}+2\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n}),\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}\right)\geq\tau^{2}(1-\frac{L\tau}{2})||\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})||_{\mathbb{H}},

Therefore, it suffices to show
\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n}),\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}\geq(1-L\tau)\cdot||\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})||_{\mathbb{H}}
. To see this, observe that

\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1}),\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}=\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})-\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n}),\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}.

Applying Cauchy-Schwarz, we obtain

\geq\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}-L\|X^{\tau}_{n+1}-X^{\tau}_{n}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\cdot\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})\|_{\mathbb{H}}\geq(1-L\tau)\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}.

(6.2)

In the second last inequality, we used
\lambda\geq 0
along with (
3.8
) to obtain that

\|X^{\tau}_{n+1}-X^{\tau}_{n}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\tau^{2}\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

Then the inner product inequality follows from (
6.2
),
(1-L\tau)\geq 0
, and using the gradient norms are non-increasing in
n
(see (
3.8

\lambda\geq 0

Lemma 6.2

.

Under the notation and assumptions of Lemma

\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\geq e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}\tau}\|\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

5.2
) with
\lambda\geq 0

\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}+\frac{2}{\tau}\right)\|X^{\tau}_{n}-X^{\tau}_{n+1}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1})\leq\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})+\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{n}^{\tau}),X^{\tau}_{n}-X^{\tau}_{n+1}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}.

3.7

.

For the exponential decay of the gradient, we use

\langle\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n})-\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X^{\tau}_{n+1}),X^{\tau}_{n}-X^{\tau}_{n+1}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}\geq\lambda\|X^{\tau}_{n}-X^{\tau}_{n+1}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

Then, using Lemma
6.1

Lemma 6.3

(6.5)

Proof.

3.7

6.2

To conclude this section, we refine the constants in Theorem

for
\lambda\geq 0
. Since
\lambda_{\tau,L}\leq\lambda
,
\phi
remains
\lambda_{\tau,L}
-convex. Applying Theorem
5.7
with
\lambda_{\tau,L}\geq 0
, we can estimate
d_{\tau,\eta}(t;t)
using Gronwall’s Inequality
[
2
, Lemma 4.1.8]
. Indeed, we have the bound

d_{\tau,\eta}(T;T)\leq e^{-\lambda_{\tau}T}\left(d^{2}_{\tau,\eta}(0;0)+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}s}\tilde{a}(s)ds\right)^{1/2}+e^{-\lambda_{\tau}T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\lambda_{\tau}s}\tilde{b}(s)ds,

(6.6)

where

\tilde{a}(t):=\frac{\tau}{2}G^{\tau}_{t}+\frac{\eta}{2}G^{\eta}_{t}+2(\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\tau}+\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\eta})+\lambda_{\tau,L}((\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})^{2}+(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\eta})^{2}),\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{b}(t):=\lambda_{\tau,L}(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau}+\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\eta}).

Now let us estimate the integral terms:

Lemma 6.4
.

Under the notation and assumptions of Lemma
6.3
, then the following estimates hold for any
T\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}t}G^{\tau}_{t}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\tau\cdot\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\tau,L}}\cdot(1+2\lambda(T+\tau))\cdot||\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{0}^{\tau})||^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}t}\mathcal{R}^{\tau}_{t}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\tau,L}}\cdot\left[\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\lambda}{2}(T+\tau)\right]+\frac{1}{2}e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}\tau}\right)\cdot||\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{0}^{\tau})||^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}t}(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\tau,L}}\tau^{2}(T+\tau)||\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{0}^{\tau})||^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{\lambda_{\tau,L}t}(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})\,dt

\displaystyle\leq(T+\tau)\tau\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\tau,L}}}||\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{0}^{\tau})||_{\mathbb{H}}.

Proof.

Choose
N\in\mathbb{N}
to be the smallest integer such that
T\leq N\tau
.

Step 1:

\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}t}(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})^{2}dt
.

Observe that

\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}t}(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})^{2}dt\leq\int_{0}^{N\tau}e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}t}d^{2}(\overline{X}_{t}^{\tau}\underline{X}_{t}^{\tau})dt=\frac{e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}\tau}-1}{2\lambda_{\tau,L}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}||X^{\tau}_{j}-X^{\tau}_{j+1}||^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\cdot e^{2j\lambda_{\tau,L}\tau}.

In addition, the definition
\lambda_{\tau,L}
implies that
e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}\tau}-1\leq 2\lambda\tau
, so using the above bound with (
6.5
) implies the desired bound.

Step 2:

\int_{0}^{T}e^{\lambda_{\tau,L}t}(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})dt
.

The bound follows directly from Holder’s inequality and the bound from Step 1.

Step 3:

\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}t}G^{\tau}_{t}\,dt

We will use the notation
\alpha_{j}
from (
5.20
). Then as
G^{\tau}_{t}\geq 0
because
\lambda\geq 0
from (
3.8
), we see that

\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}t}G^{\tau}_{t}dt\leq\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{j+1})\int_{j\tau}^{(j+1)\tau}e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}t}dt=\frac{e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}\tau}-1}{2\lambda_{\tau,L}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{j+1})e^{2j\lambda_{\tau,L}\tau}.

(6.7)

To control the sum, we observe that

\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{j+1})e^{2j\lambda_{\tau,L}\tau}=\left(\alpha_{0}-\alpha_{N}e^{2N\lambda_{\tau,L}\tau}\right)+\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\alpha_{j+1}(e^{2(j+1)\lambda_{\tau,L}\tau}-e^{2j\lambda_{\tau,L}\tau})\leq\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{0}(e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}\tau}-1)N.

In the last inequality we used Lemma
6.2
. Then again the definition of
\lambda_{\tau,L}
implies that
e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}\tau}-1\leq 2\lambda\tau
, so we see that from (
6.7
)

\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}t}G_{t}^{\tau}dt\leq\tau\cdot\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\tau,L}}\cdot(1+2\lambda(T+\tau))\cdot||\nabla\phi^{\#}_{\rho_{0}}(X_{0}^{\tau})||^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

Step 4:

\int_{0}^{t}e^{\lambda t}\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\tau}dt

By using Lemma
5.8
and that
\lambda\geq 0
, we have that

\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}t}\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\tau}\leq\frac{\tau}{4}\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}t}G_{t}^{\tau}dt-\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}t}(\ell_{\tau}(t)-\frac{1}{2})(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\tau})^{2}dt=(I)+(II).

We can control
(I)
by using the error bound derived in Step 3. To control
(II)
, observe that

\int_{j\tau}^{(j+1)\tau}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\ell_{\tau}(t)\right)e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}t}dt\leq 0.

Hence, we see from Lemma
6.3

(II)\leq\frac{1}{2\tau}\cdot d^{2}(X_{N-1}^{\tau},X_{N}^{\tau})\cdot\int_{(N-1)\tau}^{T}e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}t}\leq\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}||\nabla\phi(X^{\tau}_{N})||^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\cdot e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}(N+1)\tau}

\leq\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}e^{2\lambda_{\tau,L}\tau}\cdot||\nabla\phi(X_{0}^{\tau})||^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

The bounds on
(I)
and
(II)
allows us to conclude.

∎

Numerical Experiments

In this section, we present numerical experiments for our trapezoidal time discretization scheme applied to the the energy functional
U
from Example
2.8
. We will take
\chi_{\sigma}
to be the density of a
\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}\textnormal{Id})
random variable. When
\rho=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{j}}
is an empirical measure, the computations from Example
2.8
implies

\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\mathcal{E}(\rho,X_{i})=\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\mathcal{F}(\rho,X_{i})+\nabla V(X_{i})+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\nabla W(X_{i}-X_{j}).

(7.1)

where

\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\mathcal{F}(\rho,X_{i})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Big(f^{\prime}\big((\rho\ast\chi_{\sigma})(X_{i})\big)+f^{\prime}\big((\rho\ast\chi_{\sigma})(X_{j})\big)\Big)\,\nabla\chi_{\sigma}(X_{i}-X_{j}).

This formulation is particularly convenient for numerical implementation,
as both the energy
\mathcal{E}(\rho)
and its Wasserstein gradient

\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}\mathcal{E}(\rho)
can be evaluated explicitly from the particle positions

\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
. In practice, this allows for efficient computation of the energy and its gradients without any need for grid-based interpolation or density estimation.

Moreover, push-forwards are especially simple in this particle representation:
if
\rho=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{i}}
and
T:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a map, then

T_{\#}\rho=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{T(X_{i})}.

Hence both the evaluation of
\mathcal{E}
and the application of transport maps
can be implemented directly at the level of the particle positions.

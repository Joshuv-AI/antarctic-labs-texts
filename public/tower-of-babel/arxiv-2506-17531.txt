1.
Introduction

Let
G
be the Lie group
\mathbb{R}_{+}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{n}
endowed with the product: for all
(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in G
,

\displaystyle(x,y)\,(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=(xx^{\prime},y+xy^{\prime}).

The group
G
is called an
ax+b
group. Clearly,
e=(1,0)
is the identity of
G
. We endow
G
with the left-invariant Riemannian metric
\mathbf{g}=x^{-2}(\mathrm{d}x^{2}+\mathrm{d}y^{2})
, and denote by
d
the corresponding metric. This coincides with the metric on the
(n+1)
-dimensional hyperbolic space. The right Haar measure on
G
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\rho(x,y)=x^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

The space
(G,d,\mathrm{d}\rho)
is a solvable Lie group with exponential volume growth. Throughout the article, we will use the right Haar measure
\mathrm{d}\rho
in notions such as
L^{p}(G)
for all
1\leq p<\infty
.

A basis for the Lie algebra of left-invariant vector fields is given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle X=x{\partial\over\partial x},\quad Y_{j}=x{\partial\over\partial y_{j}},\quad 1\leq j\leq n.

We define the distinguished left-invariant Laplacian to be the second order differential operator

(1.2)

\displaystyle L=-X^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}Y_{j}^{2}.

The distinguished Laplacian
L
has a self-adjoint extension in
L^{2}(G)
(
[
15
]
), and thus we can use spectral calculus to define the operators

\displaystyle e^{it\sqrt{L}}m(\sqrt{L}),

where the multiplier
m
will lie in a suitable symbol class.

Let
u=u(t,(x,y))
be the solution of the Cauchy problem

(1.3)

\displaystyle{\partial^{2}\over\partial t^{2}}u-\left(X^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}Y_{j}^{2}\right)\!u=0,\quad u|_{t=0}=f,\quad\left.{\partial\over\partial t}u\right|_{t=0}=g.

For
f,g
in
L^{2}(G)
the solution
u
is given by

\displaystyle u=\cos(t\sqrt{L})(f)+{\sin(t\sqrt{L})\over\sqrt{L}}(g).

In
[
13
, Corollary 8.2]
, Müller and Thiele studied Sobolev estimates for solutions of the wave equation (
1.3
) and showed that for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p<\infty
,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq C_{p}\left((1+|t|)^{2|1/p-1/2|}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha_{0}}\hskip-0.85355pt(G)}+(1+|t|)\,\|g\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha_{1}}\hskip-1.42271pt(G)}\right)

provided that

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}>n\left|{1\over p}-{1\over 2}\right|\quad\mbox{and}\quad\alpha_{1}>n\left|{1\over p}-{1\over 2}\right|-1.

Here the adapted Sobolev norm
is given by

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha}(G)}:=\|(\mathrm{Id}+L)^{\alpha/2}\varphi\|_{L^{p}(G)}.

Later on, Müller and Vallarino proved the result (
1.4
) for solutions of the wave equation on Damek-Ricci spaces, see
[
14
, Theorem 6.1]
.

In this article, we investigate the endpoint estimate of (
1.4
) and show that the estimate (
1.4
) holds for
\alpha_{0}=n|{1/p}-{1/2}|
and
\alpha_{1}=n|{1/p}-{1/2}|-1
with
1<p<\infty
, as pointed out in
[
13
, Remark 8.1]
. This is the counterpart to the corresponding results by Peral
[
16
]
and Miyachi
[
12
]
in the Euclidean setting. Our result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be the group
\mathbb{R}_{+}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{n}
which is of
ax+b
type, and
L
be the distinguished left invariant Laplacian on
G
. Assume
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

(i)

For
1<p<\infty
, the solution
u=u(t,(x,y))
of the wave equation (
1.3
) satisfies

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq C_{p}\,\Big((1+|t|)^{2|1/p-1/2|}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha_{0}}\hskip-0.85355pt(G)}+(1+|t|)\,\|g\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha_{1}}\hskip-1.42271pt(G)}\Big)

if and only if

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}\geq n\left|{1\over p}-{1\over 2}\right|\quad\mbox{and}\quad\alpha_{1}\geq n\left|{1\over p}-{1\over 2}\right|-1.

(ii)

For
p=1
, we have

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(G)}\leq C\,(1+|t|)\big(\|(\mathrm{Id}+L)^{\alpha_{0}/2}f\|_{H^{1}(G)}+\|(\mathrm{Id}+L)^{{\alpha_{1}/2}}g\|_{H^{1}(G)}\big).

if and only if

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}\geq{n\over 2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\alpha_{1}\geq{n\over 2}-1.

Here
H^{1}(G)
is the Hardy space on
G
, see Subsection
2.3
below.

Note that the exponent
2|1/p-1/2|
of
1+|t|
in (i) of Theorem
1.1
is independent of
n
, in contrast to the corresponding estimate by Peral
[
16
]
and Miyachi
[
12
]
for the Euclidean case, where it agrees with
n|1/p-1/2|
.

To prove the endpoint estimate for
\alpha_{0}=n|{1/p}-{1/2}|
and
\alpha_{1}=n|{1/p}-{1/2}|-1
of (
1.4
) in Theorem
1.1
, we use an approach inspired by
[
16
]
and
[
21
]
. We will factorize the wave propagator
(\mathrm{Id}+L)^{-n/4}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{L}}
into an operator given by convolution with some weighted measure on the sphere of radius
|t|
, and a multiplier of order
0
. Although the group
G
is of exponential growth, we can still get polynomial growths with respect to
|t|
in the estimates in Theorem
1.1
. This phenomenon is due to the fact that the
L^{1}
–
L^{1}
-norm of the operator given by convolution with the weighted measure on the sphere of radius
|t|
mentioned above brings an additional exponential decay on
|t|
, which can
perfectly
eliminate the exponential growth. The proof relies heavily on the explicit asymptotic expansions of the spherical functions
\varphi_{\lambda}(t)
on
G
, the spherical Fourier transform of the normalized measure on the sphere of radius
|t|
as well as its derivative
\varphi_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)
on
t
. To show the sharpness of the Sobolev indices in Theorem
1.1
, we use the fact that for some
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
and some
m
belonging to the symbol class of order
\sigma
, the kernel of the wave propagator
m(\sqrt{L})\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{L}}
concentrates near the sphere of radius
|t|
. Notice that
m(\sqrt{L})\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{L}}(f)
concentrates near the identity whenever
f
is a function supported near the sphere of radius
|t|
. We then construct a family of such functions to obtain the sharpness of the Sobolev indices in Theorem
1.1
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we present basic facts about the Fourier theory as well as the Hardy space
H^{1}(G)
on
G
. In Section
3
we derive explicit asymptotic expansions of the spherical function
\varphi_{\lambda}(t)
and its derivative
\varphi_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)
on
t
, which will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. In Section
4
we will prove the sufficiency part of Theorem
1.1
. In Section
5
we will obtain the necessity part of Theorem
1.1
.

2.1.
The hyperbolic space

The space
G
endowed with the left-invariant Riemannian metric given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}=x^{-2}(\mathrm{d}x^{2}+\mathrm{d}y^{2})

is the
(n+1)
-dimensional hyperbolic space. The gradient induced by the Riemannian metric
\mathbf{g}
reads

(2.2)

\displaystyle\nabla f(x,y)=x^{2}\left({\partial f\over\partial x}(x,y)\,{\partial\over\partial x}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\partial f\over\partial y_{j}}(x,y)\,{\partial\over\partial y_{j}}\right).

The geodesic distance
d
is a left-invariant distance on
G
. For all
(x,y)
in
G

(2.3)

\displaystyle d((x,y),e)=\mathrm{arcosh}{x^{2}+1+|y|^{2}\over 2x},

where
e
is the identity on
G
and
|\cdot|
is the
n
-dimensional Euclidean norm. For simplicity in the sequel we denote
R(x,y)=d((x,y),e)
for
(x,y)\in G
. The measure
\mathrm{d}V
induced by the Riemannian metric
\mathbf{g}
is a left Haar measure on
G
and is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}V(x,y)=x^{-(n+1)}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y_{1}\dots\mathrm{d}y_{n}.

In particular,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}V(x,y)=\delta(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\rho(x,y),

where the modular function is given by

(2.5)

\displaystyle\delta(x,y)=x^{-n}.

Following
[
17
, p. 21 and p. 136]
and
[
7
, Exercise 3.18]
, we can adopt polar coordinates

\displaystyle(x,y)=(x(r,\omega),y(r,\omega)),\quad(r,\omega)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{n},

where
r=R(x,y)\geq 0
,
\omega=(\omega^{\prime},\omega_{n+1})\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{1+n}
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle x(r,\omega)={1\over(\omega_{n+1}+\coth r)\,\sinh r}\quad\mbox{and}\quad y(r,\omega)={\omega^{\prime}\over\omega_{n+1}+\coth r},

Under these coordinates, the right Haar measure becomes

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\rho(x,y)=\big((\omega_{n+1}+\coth r)\,\sinh r\big)^{-n}(\sinh r)^{n}\,\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\omega.

We now introduce a simple lemma that will be repeatedly used throughout this article.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
r>0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\big((\omega_{n+1}+\coth r)\,\sinh r\big)^{-n/2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\Big|\partial_{r}\!\Big[\big((\omega_{n+1}+\coth r)\,\sinh r\big)^{-n/2}\Big]\Big|\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle\leq C\times\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if }0<r\leq 1,\\
r\,\mathrm{e}^{-nr/2}&\mbox{if }r>1.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let us first consider the case
0<r\leq 1
. Note that

\displaystyle\big((\omega_{n+1}+\coth t)\,\sinh r\big)^{-n/2}=(\omega_{n+1}\sinh r+\cosh r)^{-n/2}\leq C

for
0<r\leq 1
and
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, so

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\big((\omega_{n+1}+\coth r)\,\sinh r\big)^{-n/2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\leq C.

Also, since

(2.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\!\Big[(\omega_{n+1}\sinh r+\cosh r)^{-n/2}\Big]=\big((\omega_{n+1}+\coth r)\,\sinh r\big)^{-n/2-1}(\omega_{n+1}+\tanh r)\,\cosh r,

for
0<r\leq 1
and
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, the right hand side is bounded, and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\Big|\partial_{r}\!\Big[\big((\omega_{n+1}+\coth r)\,\sinh r\big)^{-n/2}\Big]\Big|\,\mathrm{d}\omega\leq C.

These estimates finish the proof of the case
0<r\leq 1
.

Now we suppose
r\geq 1
. Recalling
\delta(u,v)=u^{-n}
, by
[
6
, §D.2]
we can write

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\big((\omega_{n+1}+\coth r)\,\sinh r\big)^{-n/2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,\mathrm{e}^{-nr/2}\int_{-1}^{1}(\omega_{n+1}+\coth r)^{-n/2}(1-\omega_{n+1}^{2})^{(n-2)/2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega_{n+1}

and by (
2.8
),

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\Big|\partial_{r}\!\Big[\big((\omega_{n+1}+\coth r)\,\sinh r\big)^{-n/2}\Big]\Big|\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{n\over 2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\big((\omega_{n+1}+\coth r)\,\sinh r\big)^{-n/2-1}|\omega_{n+1}+\tanh r|\,\cosh r\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,\mathrm{e}^{-nr/2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\omega_{n+1}+\coth r)^{-n/2-1}\big((\omega_{n+1}+\coth r)+|\tanh r-\coth r|\big)\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,\mathrm{e}^{-nr/2}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}(\omega_{n+1}+\coth r)^{-n/2}(1-\omega_{n+1}^{2})^{(n-2)/2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega_{n+1}\right.

\displaystyle\quad+\left.(\coth r-\tanh r)\int_{-1}^{1}(\omega_{n+1}+\coth r)^{-(n/2+1)}(1-\omega_{n+1}^{2})^{(n-2)/2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega_{n+1}\right).

By a simple calculation, for
r>1
,

\displaystyle\coth r-\tanh r\leq C\,\mathrm{e}^{-2r}

and

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}(\omega_{n+1}+\coth r)^{-\delta}(1-\omega_{n+1}^{2})^{(n-2)/2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega_{n+1}\leq C\times\begin{cases}r&\mbox{if }\delta=n/2,\\[3.0pt]
1+\mathrm{e}^{(2\delta-n)r}&\mbox{if }\delta\neq n/2.\end{cases}

These estimates yield the desired result for
r>1
.
∎

Recall that a function
f(x,y)
is said to be radial on
G
, if it depends only on
R(x,y)
. For such a function
f
, we have the following integration formula, which is a consequence of (
2.5
)–(
2.7
) and Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For every radial function
f
,

\displaystyle\int_{G}\delta^{1/2}(x,y)\,f(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\rho(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(r)\,A(r)\,\mathrm{d}r,

where

\displaystyle A(r)\leq C\times\begin{cases}r^{n}&\mbox{if }0<r\leq 1,\\
r\,\mathrm{e}^{nr/2}&\mbox{if }r>1.\end{cases}

2.2.
Harmonic analysis on
G

One can define the spherical Fourier transform of a radial function
f
by

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}f(\lambda)=\nu_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(r)\,\varphi_{\lambda}(r)\,(\sinh r)^{n}\,\mathrm{d}r,

where
\nu_{n}
is the surface area of the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
\varphi_{\lambda}
denotes the elementary spherical function given by

(2.10)

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}(\lambda)={2^{n/2-1}\Gamma((n+1)/2)\over\sqrt{\uppi}\,\Gamma(n/2)}(\sinh t)^{1-n}\int_{-t}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda s}(\cosh t-\cosh s)^{n/2-1}\,\mathrm{d}s,

see
[
2
, (3.1.4)]
. We also have the inverse formula of the above Fourier transform:

\displaystyle f(r)={2^{n}\uppi^{(n-1)/2}\over\Gamma((n+1)/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathscr{F}f(\lambda)\,\varphi_{\lambda}(r)\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,

where
\mathbf{c}
denotes the Harish-Chandra function given by

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathbf{c}(\lambda)={2^{n-1}\Gamma((n+1)/2)\over\sqrt{\uppi}}{\Gamma(\mathrm{i}\lambda)\over\Gamma(n/2+\mathrm{i}\lambda)},

see
[
2
, (3.1.3)]
.

Now we introduce the spectral multipliers on
G
. The distinguished Laplacian
L
given by (
1.2
) has a special relationship with the (positive definite) Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta
on
G
. Indeed, in view of

, p. 177]
,

has a continuous spectrum
[n^{2}/4,\infty)
on

. If we let

be the shifted operator

, it follows from

that

\displaystyle L(f)(x,y)=\delta^{1/2}(x,y)\,\Delta_{n}(\delta^{-1/2}f)(x,y).

This combined with the spectral theorem gives

(2.12)

\displaystyle\psi(\sqrt{L})(f)(x,y)=\delta^{1/2}(x,y)\,\psi(\sqrt{\Delta_{n}})(\delta^{-1/2}f)(x,y).

Let
k_{\psi}
and
\kappa_{\psi}
denote the convolution kernel of
\psi(\sqrt{L})
and
\psi(\sqrt{\Delta_{n}})
respectively, i.e.,

\displaystyle\psi(\sqrt{L})(f)=f*k_{\psi}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\psi(\sqrt{\Delta_{n}})(f)=f*\kappa_{\psi},

where “

” denotes the convolution on
G
defined by

(2.13)

\displaystyle f*k(g)=\int_{G}f(h)\,k(h^{-1}g)\,\delta(h)\,\mathrm{d}\rho(h).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\psi
be a Borel function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Then

is radial and is given by

\displaystyle\kappa_{\psi}(x,y)=\mathscr{F}^{-1}(m)(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(\lambda)\,\varphi_{\lambda}(R(x,y))\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Proof.

See
[
2
, Section 4]
.
∎

In view of Lemma
2.3
and (

),

(2.14)

\displaystyle k_{\psi}(x,y)=\delta^{1/2}(x,y)\,\kappa_{\psi}(x,y)=\delta^{1/2}(x,y)\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(\lambda)\,\varphi_{\lambda}(R(x,y))\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Following

, the above formula (

\displaystyle k_{\psi}(x,y)=\delta^{1/2}(x,y)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(\lambda)\,F_{R(x,y)}(\lambda)\,\lambda\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

for

where

is any integer satisfying

. Moreover, the function

and its derivatives on

have the following asymptotic expansions. Recall that the symbol class

\displaystyle\Big\{b\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}):\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}\,(1+\lambda^{2})^{(-\sigma+k)/2}|b^{(k)}(\lambda)|<\infty\mbox{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}.

.

2.3.
Hardy spaces on

.

Let us first recall the definition of Calderón-Zygmund sets on

Definition 2.6

(

A

Let

In
[

]

•

For every

\displaystyle B((x_{R},y_{R}),r_{R})\subset R\subset B((x_{R},y_{R}),c_{0}r_{R}),

is the ball in

centered at

of radius

.

•

For every
R\in\mathscr{R}
, its dilated set is defined by
R^{*}:=\{(x,y)\in G:d((x,y),R)<r_{R}\}
, which satisfies

\displaystyle\rho(R^{*})\leq c_{1}\rho(R).

•

For every
R\in\mathscr{R}
there exist mutually disjoint sets
R_{1},\dots,R_{k}\in\mathscr{R}
, with
k=2
or
2^{n}
, such that
R=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{k}R_{i}
and
\rho(R_{i})=\rho(R)/k
for
i=1,\cdots,k
.

Now we are in a position to introduce the Hardy space
H^{1}(G)
defined in
[
23
]
. By replacing balls with Calderón-Zygmund sets in the classical definition of atoms, we say that a function

is an
(1,\infty)
-atom
if it satisfies the following properties.

(1)

a
is supported in a Calderón-Zygmund set
R
.

(2)

\|a\|_{\infty}\leq(\rho(R))^{-1}
.

(3)

\int a\,\mathrm{d}\rho=0
.

Observe that an
(1,\infty)
-atom is in
L^{1}(G)
and it is normalized in such a way that its
L^{1}(G)
-norm does not exceed
1
.

Definition 2.7

(
[
23
, Definition 2.2]
)
.

The Hardy space
H^{1}(G)
is the space of all functions
h
in
L^{1}(G)
such that
h=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
, where
a_{j}
are
(1,\infty)
-atoms and
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\sum_{j}|\lambda_{j}|<\infty
. We denote by
\|h\|_{H^{1}(G)}
the infimum of
\sum_{j}|\lambda_{j}|
over such decompositions.

We have the following singular integral theorem and multiplier theorem as well as the interpolation theorem.

Lemma 2.8

(
[
23
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

Let
T
be a linear operator which is bounded on
L^{2}(G)
and admits a locally integrable Schwartz kernel
K
with respect to
\mathrm{d}\rho
off the diagonal which satisfies the condition

\displaystyle\sup_{R\in\mathscr{R}}\sup_{h,h^{\prime}\in R}\int_{(R^{*})^{c}}|K(g,h)-K(g,h^{\prime})|\,\mathrm{d}\rho(g)<\infty.

Then
T
extends to a bounded operator from
H^{1}(G)
to
L^{1}(G)
.

Lemma 2.9

(
[
23
, Proposition 4.2]
)
.

Suppose that
s_{0}>3/2
and
s_{\infty}>\max\{3/2,(n+1)/2\}
. Let
\beta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
\mathrm{supp}\beta\subset[1/4,4]
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\beta(2^{-j}\lambda)\equiv 1\quad\mbox{for all }\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

If

satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{0<\tau\leq 1}\|m(\tau\cdot)\,\beta(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{s_{0}}(\mathbb{R})}+\sup_{\tau>1}\|m(\tau\cdot)\,\beta(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{s_{\infty}}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty,

then the operator
m(\sqrt{L})
is bounded from
H^{1}(G)
to
L^{1}(G)
, and is bounded on
L^{p}(G)
for all
1<p<\infty
.

Lemma 2.10

(
[
11
, Theorem 5.3]
)
.

Denote by
\Sigma
the closed strip

. Suppose that

such that the map

\displaystyle\|T_{\zeta}f\|_{L^{1}(G)}\leq A_{1}\|f\|_{H^{1}(G)}\quad\mbox{for all }f\in L^{2}(G)\cap H^{1}(G)\mbox{ and }\mathrm{Re}\zeta=0,

\displaystyle\|T_{\zeta}f\|_{L^{2}(G)}\leq A_{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(G)}\quad\mbox{for all }f\in L^{2}(G)\mbox{ and }\mathrm{Re}\zeta=1,

\displaystyle\|T_{\vartheta}f\|_{L^{q_{\vartheta}}}\leq A_{1}^{1-\vartheta}A_{2}^{\vartheta}\|f\|_{L^{q_{\vartheta}}}\quad\mbox{for all }f\in L^{2}(G)\cap L^{q_{\vartheta}}.

The following lemma is needed in the proof of the sufficiency part of Theorem

3.
Explicit asymptotic expansions of spherical functions

Let
\mathrm{d}\sigma_{t}
be the normalized spherical measure of radius
t
on
G
, i.e.

\displaystyle\int_{G}f\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{t}={1\over\nu_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}f(g(t,\omega))\,\mathrm{d}\omega,

where by
g(r,\omega)
we denote the tuple
(x(r,\omega),y(r,\omega))
for
x(r,\omega)
and
y(r,\omega)
as in (
2.6
). It follows that the spherical function
\varphi_{\lambda}(t)
given by (
2.10
) is the spherical Fourier transform of
\mathrm{d}\sigma_{t}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}(\mathrm{d}\sigma_{t})(\lambda)=\varphi_{\lambda}(t),

which implies

\displaystyle\varphi_{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}(t)(f)(x,y)=f*\mathrm{d}\sigma_{t}(x,y)={1\over\nu_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}f((x,y)\cdot g(t,\omega))\,\mathrm{d}\omega

according to Lemma
2.3
. By (
2.12
), we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{\sqrt{L}}(t)(f)(x,y)

\displaystyle=\delta^{1/2}(x,y)\,\varphi_{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}(t)(\delta^{-1/2}f)(x,y)

(3.1)

\displaystyle={1\over\nu_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\delta^{-1/2}(g(t,\omega))\,f((x,y)\cdot g(t,\omega))\,\mathrm{d}\omega.

We now give the explicit asymptotic expansions of
\varphi_{\lambda}(t)
and
\varphi_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)
for large
\lambda
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose
t>0
and
\lambda\geq\max\{1,t^{-1}\}
.

(a)

For
\varphi_{\lambda}(t)
we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda}(t)=2^{n/2}\Gamma\!\left({n+1\over 2}\right)(\sinh t)^{-n/2}\lambda^{-n/2}\cos(t\lambda-n\uppi/4)+\mathfrak{r}_{t}(\lambda),

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}_{t}(\lambda)=\begin{cases}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\lambda}a_{t}(t\lambda)+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\lambda}a_{t}(-t\lambda)&\mbox{if }0<t\leq 1\mbox{ and }t\lambda\geq 1,\\
\mathrm{e}^{-nt/2}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\lambda}a_{t}(\lambda)+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\lambda}a_{t}(-\lambda)\right)&\mbox{if }t>1\mbox{ and }\lambda\geq 1\end{cases}

for some
a_{t}\in S^{-1-n/2}
uniformly in
t>0
.

(b)

For
\varphi_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)
we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)=-2^{n/2}\Gamma\!\left({n+1\over 2}\right)(\sinh t)^{-n/2}\lambda^{1-n/2}\sin(t\lambda-n\uppi/4)+\mathfrak{s}_{t}(\lambda),

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}_{t}(\lambda)=\begin{cases}t^{-1}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\lambda}b_{t}(t\lambda)+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\lambda}b_{t}(-t\lambda)\right)&\mbox{if }0<t\leq 1\mbox{ and }t\lambda\geq 1,\\
\mathrm{e}^{-nt/2}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\lambda}b_{t}(\lambda)+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\lambda}b_{t}(-\lambda)\right)&\mbox{if }t>1\mbox{ and }\lambda\geq 1\end{cases}

for some
b_{t}\in S^{-n/2}
uniformly in
t>0
.

Proof.

(a) follows directly from the proof of
[
22
, Lemma 2.2]
and the argument on
[
20
, pp. 355–357]
.

We now show (b). We use (
2.10
) to write

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{C_{n}\,(\sinh t)^{1-n}\,\partial_{t}\!\left[\int_{-t}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda s}(\cosh t-\cosh s)^{n/2-1}\,\mathrm{d}s\right]}_{\mathcal{I}_{t}(\lambda)}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{(1-n){\cosh t\over\sinh t}C_{n}\,(\sinh t)^{1-n}\int_{-t}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda s}(\cosh t-\cosh s)^{n/2-1}\,\mathrm{d}s}_{\mathcal{J}_{t}(\lambda)},

where

\displaystyle C_{n}={2^{n/2-1}\Gamma((n+1)/2)\over\sqrt{\uppi}\,\Gamma(n/2)}

is the constant in (
2.10
). In comparison with (
2.10
), we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{t}(\lambda)=(1-n){\cosh t\over\sinh t}\varphi_{\lambda}(t),

which by (a) is of form
\mathfrak{s}_{t}(\lambda)
.

It remains to show that
\mathcal{I}_{t}(\lambda)
enjoys the desired asymptotic behavior. If
n\geq 3
we directly write

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{t}(\lambda)={n-1\over\sinh t}C_{n-2}\,(\sinh t)^{1-(n-2)}\int_{-t}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda s}(\cosh t-\cosh s)^{(n-2)/2-1}\,\mathrm{d}s.

In comparison with (
2.10
) and by (a) we obtain the desired result.

The case
n=1,2
is much trickier. We set an even function
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
with
\mathrm{supp}\chi\subset[-1/2,1/2]
such that
\chi\equiv 1
on
[-1/4,1/4]
. For
0<t\leq 1
we write

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{t}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=C_{n}\underbrace{(\sinh t)^{1-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda s}\partial_{t}\!\left[\chi\!\left({s\over t}\right)(\cosh t-\cosh s)^{n/2-1}\right]\,\mathrm{d}s}_{\mathcal{I}_{1,t}(\lambda)}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{n}\underbrace{(\sinh t)^{1-n}\,\partial_{t}\!\left[\int_{-t}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda s}\left(1-\chi\!\left({s\over t}\right)\right)(\cosh t-\cosh s)^{n/2-1}\,\mathrm{d}s\right]}_{\mathcal{I}_{2,t}(\lambda)}.

Integrating by parts
N
times for
N=1,2,\dots,
, we have

\displaystyle|\partial^{k}_{\lambda}\mathcal{I}_{1,t}(\lambda)|\leq C_{N}\,t^{-1}(t\lambda)^{-N}\quad\mbox{for all }k=0,1,2,\dots.

In the meantime, by integration by parts,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2,t}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\,\lambda\,(\sinh t)^{2-n}\int_{-t}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda s}{1-\chi(s/t)\over\sinh s}(\cosh t-\cosh s)^{n/2-1}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+{2\mathrm{i}\over n}\lambda\,(\sinh t)^{1-n}\int_{-t}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda s}{s\,\chi^{\prime}(s/t)\over t^{2}\sinh s}(\cosh t-\cosh s)^{n/2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle-(\sinh t)^{2-n}\int_{-t}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda s}\left({\chi^{\prime}(s/t)\over t\,\sinh s}+{(1-\chi(s/t))\,\cosh s\over(\sinh s)^{2}}\right)(\cosh t-\cosh s)^{n/2-1}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle-{2\over n}(\sinh t)^{1-n}\int_{-t}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda s}\left({s\,\chi^{\prime\prime}(s/t)-t\,\chi^{\prime}(s/t)\over t^{3}\sinh s}+{s\,\chi^{\prime}(s/t)\,\cosh s\over t^{2}(\sinh s)^{2}}\right)(\cosh t-\cosh s)^{n/2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Following the proof of
[
22
, Lemma 2.2]
and the argument on
[
20
, pp. 355–357]
, we obtain the desired asymptotic expansion.

For
t>1
we write

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{t}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=C_{n}\underbrace{(\sinh t)^{1-n}\int_{-t}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda s}\partial_{t}\!\left[(1-\chi(|s|-t))\,(\cosh t-\cosh s)^{n/2-1}\right]\,\mathrm{d}s}_{\mathcal{I}_{1,t}(\lambda)}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{n}\underbrace{(\sinh t)^{1-n}\,\partial_{t}\!\left[\int_{-t}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda s}\chi(|s|-t)\,(\cosh t-\cosh s)^{n/2-1}\,\mathrm{d}s\right]}_{\mathcal{I}_{2,t}(\lambda)}.

Integrating by parts
N
times for
N=1,2,\dots
, we have

\displaystyle|\partial^{k}_{\lambda}\mathcal{I}_{1,t}(\lambda)|\leq C_{N}\,\mathrm{e}^{-nt/2}\lambda^{-N}\quad\mbox{for all }k=0,1,2,\dots.

In the meantime, one can use the above argument dealing with
\mathcal{I}_{2,t}(\lambda)
for
0<t\leq 1
to show that for
t>1

\mathcal{I}_{2,t}(\lambda)
possesses the desired asymptotic expansion.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem

: the sufficiency part

In this section we show the sufficiency part of Theorem
1.1
. For the purpose we prove the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G
be the group
\mathbb{R}_{+}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{n}
which is of
ax+b
type, and
L
be the distinguished left invariant Laplacian on
G
. Assume

.

(i)

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\alpha_{0}=n|1/p-1/2|
and
\alpha_{1}=n|1/p-1/2|-1
. The solution

of the wave equation (

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq C_{p}\,\Big((1+|t|)^{2|1/p-1/2|}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha_{0}}\hskip-0.85355pt(G)}+(1+|t|)\,\|g\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha_{1}}\hskip-1.42271pt(G)}\Big).

(ii)

Let
\alpha_{0}=n/2
and
\alpha_{1}=n/2-1
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(G)}\leq C\,(1+|t|)\big(\|(\mathrm{Id}+L)^{\alpha_{0}/2}f\|_{H^{1}(G)}+\|(\mathrm{Id}+L)^{{\alpha_{1}/2}}g\|_{H^{1}(G)}\big).

The following proposition plays an essential role in the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

and

be an even symbol.

(a)

If

, then

\displaystyle\|m(\sqrt{L})\,\cos(t\sqrt{L})\|_{H^{1}(G)\to L^{1}(G)}\leq C\,(1+|t|)

for some constant

independent of

.

(b)

If

\displaystyle\left\|m(\sqrt{L})\,{\sin(t\sqrt{L})\over\sqrt{L}}\right\|_{H^{1}(G)\to L^{1}(G)}\leq C\,(1+|t|)

for some constant
C>0
independent of
t
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we will assume that
t>0
. To prove (a), we suppose that

is an even function,
and consider two cases:
t>1
and

.
Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})

on

\displaystyle\|\eta(\sqrt{L})\,m(\sqrt{L})\,\cos(t\sqrt{L})\|_{L^{1}(G)\to L^{1}(G)}\leq C\,(1+|t|)

for some constant

independent of

. Therefore it remains to show that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|(1-\eta(\sqrt{L})\,m(\sqrt{L})\,\cos(t\sqrt{L})\|_{H^{1}(G)\to L^{1}(G)}\leq C\,(1+|t|).

\displaystyle\cos(t\lambda)

Proposition 4.3
.

Let

\displaystyle\|m_{1}(\sqrt{L})\,\cos(t\sqrt{L})\|_{L^{p}(G)\to L^{p}(G)}\leq C\,(1+|t|)^{2|1/p-1/2|}

and

\displaystyle\|m_{2}(\sqrt{L})\,\sin(t\sqrt{L})/\sqrt{L}\|_{L^{p}(G)\to L^{p}(G)}\leq C\,(1+|t|).

Proof.

By duality, it suffices to assume

. We set

\displaystyle m^{\zeta}_{1}(\lambda):=\mathrm{e}^{(\zeta-2+2/p)^{2}}m_{1}(\lambda)\,(1+\lambda^{2})^{n|1/p-1/2|/2-n(1-\zeta)/4}

and

\displaystyle m^{\zeta}_{2}(\lambda):=\mathrm{e}^{(\zeta-2+2/p)^{2}}m_{2}(\lambda)\,(1+\lambda^{2})^{n|1/p-1/2|/2-1-(n-2)(1-\zeta)/4}.

Note that

and
m^{\zeta}_{2}\in S^{-(n-2)(1-\mathrm{Re}\zeta)/2}
uniformly in

\displaystyle T^{\zeta}_{1,t}:=m^{\zeta}_{1}(\sqrt{L})\,\cos(t\sqrt{L})\quad\mbox{and}\quad T^{\zeta}_{2,t}:=m^{\zeta}_{2}(\sqrt{L}){\sin(t\sqrt{L})\over\sqrt{L}}.

\displaystyle\|T^{\zeta}_{j,t}\|_{H^{1}(G)\to L^{1}(G)}\leq C\,(1+|t|)\quad\mbox{for }j=1,2,\ \mathrm{Re}\zeta=0,

\displaystyle\|T^{\zeta}_{1,t}\|_{L^{2}(G)\to L^{2}(G)}\leq C\mbox{ and }\|T^{\zeta}_{2,t}\|_{L^{2}(G)\to L^{2}(G)}\leq C\,|t|\quad\mbox{for }\mathrm{Re}\zeta=1.

Therefore, by Lemma
2.10

\displaystyle\|T^{2(1-1/p)}_{1,t}\|_{L^{p}(G)\to L^{p}(G)}\leq C\,(1+|t|)^{2(1/p-1/2)}\mbox{ and }\|T^{2(1-1/p)}_{2,t}\|_{L^{2}(G)\to L^{2}(G)}\leq C\,(1+|t|).

But

and
T^{2(1-1/p)}_{2,t}=m_{2}(\sqrt{L})\,\sin(t\sqrt{L})/\sqrt{L}

5.
Proof of Theorem

In this section we show the necessity part of Theorem

.
In the following, we fix a

, and assume that
g=0

We consider three cases:

Case 1:

Case 2:

. In this case, we will show that
\alpha_{0}\geq n|1/p-1/2|=n/2-1/4

be an even function such that

and
\eta\equiv 1
on

[

, Theorem 8.1]
that for all

and all

, the operator
\cos(t\sqrt{L})\,(1-\eta(\sqrt{L}))\,(\mathrm{Id}+L)^{-\alpha_{0}/2}

. To consider the operator
\cos(t\sqrt{L})\,\eta(\sqrt{L})\,(\mathrm{Id}+L)^{-\alpha_{0}/2}

that its

as

. So, it reduces to showing that if the operator
\cos(t\sqrt{L})\,\eta(\sqrt{L})\,m(\sqrt{L})
is bounded on

for some

as above, then we must have
\alpha_{0}\geq n/2-1/4
.

To show it, we construct a family of functions
\{f^{(\varepsilon)}_{t}\}
as follows:

\displaystyle f^{(\varepsilon)}_{t}(x,y):=\delta^{1/2}(x,y)\,\mathbf{1}_{E^{(\varepsilon)}_{t}}(x,y),

where

for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small. Since the modular function
\delta
is a continuous function, obviously

(5.1)

\displaystyle\|f^{(\varepsilon)}_{t}\|_{L^{4n}(G)}\leq C(t)\,\varepsilon^{1/(4n)}.

Next we need to estimate

(5.2)

\displaystyle\|\cos(t\sqrt{L})\,\eta(\sqrt{L})\,m(\sqrt{L})(f^{(\varepsilon)}_{t})\|_{L^{4n}(G)}.

We denote by
\kappa_{t}
the convolution kernel of
\cos(t\sqrt{\Delta_{n}})\,\eta(\sqrt{\Delta_{n}})\,m(\sqrt{\Delta_{n}})

is the shifted Laplace-Beltrami operator defined in Subsection
2.2
. In view of Lemma
2.3
and Proposition
3.1
(a), we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle\kappa_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos(t\lambda)\,\eta(\lambda)\,m(\lambda)\,\varphi_{\lambda}(R(x,y))\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{n}\underbrace{(\sinh R(x,y))^{-n/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos(t\lambda)\,\eta(\lambda)\,m(\lambda)\,\lambda^{-n/2}\cos(\lambda R(x,y)-n\uppi/4)\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}_{\widetilde{\kappa}_{t}(x,y)}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos(t\lambda)\,\eta(\lambda)\,m(\lambda)\,\mathfrak{r}_{R(x,y)}(\lambda)\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}_{\mathfrak{R}_{t}(x,y)},

where
\mathbf{c}
is the Harish-Chandra function given by (
2.11
) and
\mathfrak{r}_{t}(\lambda)
is as in Proposition
3.1
(a). By (
2.11
) and the basic properties of gamma function
[
5
, (2), (5) and (7) on p. 3]
, we have that
|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\sim\lambda^{n}
as

. We set
m(\lambda):=|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{2}\lambda^{n}\lambda^{-\alpha_{0}}
.

We first estimate
\widetilde{\kappa}_{t}(x,y)
in (

). Note that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\kappa}_{t}(x,y)=(\sinh R(x,y))^{-n/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos(t\lambda)\,\eta(\lambda)\,\lambda^{n/2-\alpha_{0}}\cos(\lambda R(x,y)-n\uppi/4)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

In view of
[

, for
R>0
and
\gamma\neq 2K+1

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos(\lambda R)\,\lambda^{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda={\Gamma((\gamma+1)/2)\over\Gamma(-\gamma/2)}2^{\gamma}\sqrt{\uppi}R^{-1-\gamma}.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sin(\lambda R)\,\lambda^{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda=-{\Gamma((\gamma+1)/2)\over\Gamma(-\gamma/2)}\cot\!\left({\uppi\gamma\over 2}\right)2^{\gamma}\sqrt{\uppi}\,R^{-1-\gamma}.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos(t\lambda)\,\eta(\lambda)\,\lambda^{\gamma}\cos(\lambda r-n\uppi/4)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda=C_{\gamma}\,(r-t)^{-\gamma-1}+O(1)

with

, as

. We substitute (

) to obtain that for

(5.7)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\kappa}_{t}(x,y)=(\sinh R(x,y))^{-n/2}\big(C_{\alpha_{0}}\,(R(x,y)-t)^{\alpha_{0}-n/2-1}+O(1)\big)

, we apply Lemma

to obtain that

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{R}_{t}(x,y)|\leq C(R(x,y))\,(R(x,y)-t)^{\alpha_{0}-n/2},

, where

Now we apply (

) and (

.

holds.

.

. From

, we see that by duality, the condition

|\kappa_{m}^{\prime}(r)|\leq C_{N}\,r^{-N}\mathrm{e}^{-nr/2}
,
|\kappa_{m}(r)|\leq C_{N}\,r^{-N}\mathrm{e}^{-nr/2}
, while for
0<r\leq 1
,

\displaystyle|\kappa_{m}^{\prime\prime}(r)|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(r^{-n/2+1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}m(\lambda)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda r}b_{n/2+2}(\lambda,r)\,\lambda\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\right|+r^{-n-1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}m(\lambda)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda r}b_{0}(\lambda,r)\,\lambda\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\,r^{-n-2},

and similarly,
|\kappa_{m}^{\prime}(r)|\leq C\,r^{-n-1}
,
|\kappa_{m}(r)|\leq C\,r^{-n}
. Substituting these estimates into (
A.2
) gives the desired estimates.
∎

Now we are in a position to show Lemma
2.11
.

Proof of Lemma
2.11
.

Note that

(A.3)

\displaystyle|\nabla m(\sqrt{L})(f)|_{\mathbf{g}}\sim|Xm(\sqrt{L})(f)|+\sum_{j=1}^{n}|Y_{j}m(\sqrt{L})(f)|.

Therefore, it suffices to show the operators
Xm(\sqrt{L})
and
Y_{j}m(\sqrt{L})\ (j=1,2,\dots,n)
are bounded from
H^{1}(G)
to
L^{1}(G)
.

It follows from
[
9
, Theorem 6.4]
that the Riesz transform
\nabla L^{-1/2}
is bounded from
L^{2}(G,|\cdot|_{\mathbf{g}})
to
L^{2}(G)
. Thus by the spectral theorem

\displaystyle\||\nabla m(\sqrt{L})(f)|_{\mathbf{g}}\|_{L^{2}(G)}

\displaystyle=\||(\nabla L^{-1/2})\sqrt{L}m(\sqrt{L})(f)|_{\mathbf{g}}\|_{L^{2}(G)}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|\sqrt{L}m(\sqrt{L})(f)\|_{L^{2}(G)}\leq C\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(G)}.

By (
A.3
) and Lemma
2.8
, it suffices to show that for each
j=0,1\dots,n
,

(A.4)

\displaystyle\sup_{R\in\mathscr{R}}\sup_{h,h^{\prime}\in R}\int_{(R^{*})^{c}}|K_{j}(g,h)-K_{j}(g,h^{\prime})|\,\mathrm{d}\rho(g)<\infty.

For fixed
j=0,1,\dots,n
,
R\in\mathscr{R}
and
h,h^{\prime}\in R
, by Lemma
A.1
we have

\displaystyle\int_{(R^{*})^{c}}|K_{j}(g,h)-K_{j}(g,h^{\prime})|\,\mathrm{d}\rho(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{(R^{*})^{c}}\left|\int_{0}^{d(h,h^{\prime})}\partial_{\vartheta}[K_{j}(g,\gamma_{h,h^{\prime}}(\vartheta))]\,\mathrm{d}\vartheta\right|\mathrm{d}\rho(g)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{d(h,h^{\prime})}\left(\int_{(R^{*})^{c}}|\langle(\nabla K_{i}(g,\cdot))(\gamma_{h,h^{\prime}}(\vartheta)),\dot{\gamma}_{h,h^{\prime}}(\vartheta)\rangle_{\mathbf{g}}|\,\mathrm{d}\rho(g)\right)\mathrm{d}\vartheta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d(h,h^{\prime})\sup_{\widetilde{h}\in R}\int_{(R^{*})^{c}}|(\nabla K_{i}(g,\cdot))(\widetilde{h})|_{\mathbf{g}}\,\mathrm{d}\rho(g)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{N}\,r_{R}\sup_{\widetilde{h}\in R}\int_{(B(\widetilde{h},r_{R}))^{c}}\delta^{1/2}(g\widetilde{h})\,\varrho_{N}(d(g,\widetilde{h}))\,\mathrm{d}\rho(g),

where
r_{R}
is as in (
2.16
),
\gamma_{h,h^{\prime}}
is the arc-lengthed geodesic connecting
h
and
h^{\prime}
,
\varrho_{N}
is as in (
A.1
), and we have used the fact that for
h,h^{\prime}\in R
and
\vartheta\in[0,d(h,h^{\prime})]
,
\gamma_{h,h^{\prime}}(\vartheta)\in R
. Using Lemma
2.2
, if
0<r_{R}\leq 1
, we write

\displaystyle r_{R}\int_{(B(\widetilde{h},r_{R}))^{c}}\delta^{1/2}(g\widetilde{h})\,\varrho_{N}(d(g,\widetilde{h}))\,\mathrm{d}\rho(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{R}\int_{(B(e,r_{R}))^{c}}\delta^{1/2}(g)\,\varrho_{N}(R(g))\,\mathrm{d}\rho(g)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,r_{R}\left(\int_{r_{R}}^{2}r^{-n-2}r^{n}\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{2}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-nr/2}r^{-N}r\,\mathrm{e}^{nr/2}\,\mathrm{d}r\right)\leq C,

while if
r_{R}>1
, we write

\displaystyle r_{R}\int_{(B(\widetilde{h},r_{R}))^{c}}\delta^{1/2}(g\widetilde{h})\,\varrho_{N}(d(g,\widetilde{h}))\,\mathrm{d}\rho(g)\leq C_{N}\,r_{R}\left(\int_{r_{B}}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-nr/2}r^{-N}r\,\mathrm{e}^{nr/2}\,\mathrm{d}r\right)\leq C_{N}.

From these estimates (
A.4
) is valid.
∎

On the continuity of derivations over locally regular Banach algebras
Felipe I. Flores

Abstract
We study the problem of continuity of derivations over Banach algebras. More specifically, we consider a class of Banach algebras that contain a dense ‘ C ∗ C^{*} -like’ subalgebra. We discuss applications to L p L^{p} -crossed products and symmetrized L p L^{p} -crossed products. As an example, our results imply that every derivation over the L p L^{p} -crossed product F p ​ ( G , X , α ) F^{p}(G,X,\alpha) is continuous, provided that G G is infinite, finitely generated, has polynomial growth, and acts freely on the compact space X X .



1
Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be a Banach algebra and let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach
\mathfrak{B}
-bimodule. A derivation is a linear map
D:\mathfrak{B}\to\mathcal{X}
that satisfies the Leibniz rule:

D(ab)=D(a)b+aD(b),\quad\text{ for all }a,b\in\mathfrak{B}.

The study of derivations on Banach algebras is a central topic in Banach algebra theory. Indeed, many structural properties of the algebra are expressed using derivations. Some examples of this are given by amenability, weak amenability, or Hochschild cohomology (see
[
26
]
). The study of automatic continuity of derivations also serves as a base case for the general theory of automatic continuity.

Indeed, a central question about derivations is whether they are automatically continuous. It is known, and due to Ringrose
[
25
]
, that every derivation over a
C^{*}
-algebra is automatically continuous. But more general results are hard to obtain. In fact, it is an open question to characterize the groups
G
such that all derivations of
L^{1}(G)
are continuous
[
10
, Question 22]
. Positive results in this direction were obtained for abelian and compact groups by Jewell
[
18
]
, for ‘factorizable’ groups by Willis
[
29
]
and for groups of polynomial growth by Runde
[
27
]
.

In
[
15
]
, the author extended Runde’s approach to provide a large class of (generalized) convolution algebras of the form
L^{1}_{\alpha}(G,\mathfrak{A})
with the property that every derivation over them is continuous. These algebras are associated with compactly generated groups of polynomial growth. In
[
14
]
however, the condition of compact generation was lifted for many of these examples.

In the more general setting, the technique introduced by Ringrose was extended by Bade and Curtis
[
3
]
and Longo
[
21
]
, to produce results for abstract Banach algebras.

The specific case where
\mathcal{X}=\mathfrak{A}
has received special attention (see
[
28
,
19
]
). But it seems to be mostly solved by Johnson and Sinclair
[
20
]
, who proved that every derivation
D:\mathfrak{B}\to\mathfrak{B}
is continuous if
\mathfrak{B}
is semisimple. We remark, however, that the
L^{p}
-crossed product algebras introduced and studied in the last section of the paper are not known to be semisimple, except for the trivial cases (that is, when
p\in\{1,2,\infty\}
), so the results in
[
20
]
do not cover them.

The present article has a purpose to extend Longo’s
[
21
]
approach to automatic continuity of derivations in order to obtain new examples of algebras
\mathfrak{B}
with the property that every derivation over
\mathfrak{B}
is continuous. In fact, in doing so, we also extend some of the results obtained in
[
15
]
in a manner that will be explained in the next paragraphs. An interesting fact about the algebras we study is that we do not demand them to be involutive, but we do ask for a suitable ‘
C^{*}
-like’ dense subalgebra. The key point is that Longo requires an everywhere defined, very strong functional calculus (see condition FC in
[
21
, pag. 22]
) but the works
[
27
,
15
]
prove that one can get results about automatic continuity just with a densely defined smooth functional calculus. This motivates us to introduce the notion of ‘locally regular’ inclusions
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{B}
(see Definition
2.2
and Remark
2.3
, for the historical motivation). The idea is to demand
\mathfrak{A}
to be an
A^{*}
-algebra where every element
b\in\mathfrak{A}
generates a regular Banach function algebra, so that all of the arguments can be carried over in a dense manner through
\mathfrak{A}
.

Our first main theorem of the article provides sufficient conditions (on
\mathfrak{A}
) so that every derivation of
\mathfrak{B}
is continuous.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{B}
be a locally regular inclusion and let
D:\mathfrak{B}\to\mathcal{X}
be a derivation into a Banach
\mathfrak{B}
-bimodule. Further suppose that
\mathfrak{A}
is unital and that
{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})
has no proper closed two-sided ideals of finite codimension. Then
D
is continuous.

Our techniques also suffice to prove the following theorem, which is about derivations where the target module is a special completion of the algebra in question. In particular, they apply to the notable cases
\mathcal{X}=\mathfrak{B}
and
\mathcal{X}={\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{B})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{C}
be Banach algebras such that
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{C}\subset{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})
and such that
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{B}
is a locally regular inclusion. Then every derivation
D:\mathfrak{B}\to\mathfrak{C}
is continuous.

The above-mentioned theorems have a wide range of applications. For example, one could take
\mathfrak{B}=L^{1}(G\mid\mathscr{C})
, the algebra of integrable cross-sections on the Fell bundle
\mathscr{C}\to G
. Then the results in
[
15
, Proposition 4.10]
provide us with a locally regular inclusion
\mathfrak{E}\subset L^{1}(G\mid\mathscr{C})
, as soon as
G
is locally finite or compactly generated and has polynomial growth. In this case, an application of Theorem
1.1
yields a generalization of Theorem
[
15
, Theorem 4.16]
, as the result no longer requires the assumption of symmetry.

In the same setting, Theorem
1.2
implies that every derivation
D:L^{1}(G\mid\mathscr{C})\to{\rm C^{*}}(G\mid\mathscr{C})
is continuous. A result of this generality is unachievable with the methods in
[
15
]
.

The applications that we do want to discuss more at length come from the theory of
L^{p}
-operator algebras, so we proceed to mention them now. For the general theory of
L^{p}
-operator algebras and
L^{p}
-crossed products, we recommend
[
22
,
23
,
17
]
. Let us also mention the recent developments
[
7
,
9
,
11
]
. Symmetrized
L^{p}
-crossed products have the advantage of having an involution. These have been studied in
[
24
,
2
,
12
,
4
]
, often under the name of ‘algebras of pseudofunctions’. In what follows,
F^{p}(G,X,\alpha)
and
F^{p}_{*}(G,X,\alpha)
will denote, respectively, the
L^{p}
-crossed product and symmetrized
L^{p}
-crossed product associated with a continuous action
G\overset{\alpha}{\curvearrowright}X
and a parameter
p\in[1,\infty]
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and let
G
be a countable infinite group, which is either locally finite or finitely generated and of polynomial growth. Let
\alpha
be a continuous action of
G
on the compact space
X
such that the restriction of
\alpha
to any invariant subset is topologically free. Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach
F^{p}(G,X,\alpha)
-bimodule. Then every derivation
D:F^{p}(G,X,\alpha)\to\mathcal{X}
is continuous.

The theorem above remains valid if one wishes to change
F^{p}(G,X,\alpha)
by its symmetrized counterpart
F^{q}_{*}(G,X,\alpha)
(maintaining the assumptions on
(G,X,\alpha)
). However,
F^{q}_{*}(G,X,\alpha)
is also an
A^{*}
-algebra, so we can also apply Theorem
1.2
to it. Doing so yields the following.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
p,q\in[1,2]
and let
G
be either a countable locally finite group or a compactly generated locally compact group with polynomial growth. Let
\alpha
be a continuous action of
G
on the locally compact space
X
. Then every derivation
D:F^{p}_{*}(G,X,\alpha)\to F^{q}_{*}(G,X,\alpha)
is continuous.

The result above seems particularly interesting in the cases where
p=1
,
q=2
, and
p=q
. That is, for derivations of the form
L^{1}_{\alpha}(G,C_{0}(X))\to F^{q}_{*}(G,X,\alpha)
,
F^{p}_{*}(G,X,\alpha)\to C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{\alpha}G
, or
F^{p}_{*}(G,X,\alpha)\to F^{p}_{*}(G,X,\alpha)
.

2
Main results

If
\mathfrak{B}
is a Banach algebra,
\mathfrak{B}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
denotes the closed subalgebra of
\mathfrak{B}
generated by the elements
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\in\mathfrak{B}
. We set
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}
to denote the smallest unitization of
\mathfrak{B}
(it coincides with
\mathfrak{B}
when
\mathfrak{B}
is already unital). As usual,

{\rm Spec}_{\mathfrak{B}}(b)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\mid b-\lambda 1\textup{ is not invertible in }\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}\}

will denote the spectrum of an element
b\in\mathfrak{B}
.

If
\mathfrak{B}
has an involution,
\mathfrak{B}_{\rm sa}
denotes the set of self-adjoint elements in
\mathfrak{B}
, that is, of all
b\in\mathfrak{B}
such that
b^{*}=b
. A Banach
∗
-algebra admitting a
C^{*}
-norm is called an
A^{*}
-algebra.

If
\mathfrak{B}
is a commutative Banach algebra with spectrum
\Delta_{\mathfrak{B}}
, then
\hat{b}\in C_{0}(\Delta_{\mathfrak{B}})
denotes the Gelfand transform of
b\in\mathfrak{B}
. If the Gelfand transform is injective,
\mathfrak{B}
is called a Banach function algebra.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be a Banach function algebra with spectrum
\Delta_{\mathfrak{B}}
.
\mathfrak{B}
is called
regular
if for every closed set
X\subset\Delta_{\mathfrak{B}}
and every point
\omega\in\Delta_{\mathfrak{B}}\setminus X
, there exists an element
b\in\mathfrak{B}
such that
\hat{b}(\varphi)=0
for all
\varphi\in X
and
\hat{b}(\omega)\not=0
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be a Banach algebra that contains a dense Banach subalgebra
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{B}
which is an
A^{*}
-algebra and such that
\mathfrak{A}(b)
is a regular Banach function algebra, for all
b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\rm sa}
. In such a case, we say that the inclusion
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{B}
is
locally regular
.

Remark 2.3
.

The terminology used in Definition
2.2
is inspired by the terminology of B. Barnes. In
[
5
, Definition 4.1]
, Barnes defines local regularity for an
A^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
by requiring that
\mathfrak{A}(a)
is a regular Banach function algebra, for all
a
in a dense subset of
\mathfrak{A}_{\rm sa}
. In some sense, this definition is weaker than ours, but it does not generalize to Banach algebras without involutions.

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be a Banach algebra. A Banach space
\mathcal{X}
which is also a
\mathfrak{B}
-bimodule is called a
Banach
\mathfrak{B}
-bimodule
if the maps

\mathfrak{B}\times\mathcal{X}\ni(b,\xi)\mapsto b\xi\in\mathcal{X}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{X}\times\mathfrak{B}\ni(\xi,b)\mapsto\xi b\in\mathcal{X}

are jointly continuous.

Example 2.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{B}\subset\mathfrak{C}
be an inclusion of Banach algebras. Then
\mathfrak{C}
is naturally a Banach
\mathfrak{B}
-bimodule under the actions

\mathfrak{B}\times\mathfrak{C}\ni(b,c)\mapsto bc\in\mathfrak{C}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathfrak{C}\times\mathfrak{B}\ni(c,b)\mapsto cb\in\mathfrak{C}.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be a Banach algebra and let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach
\mathfrak{B}
-bimodule. A linear map
D:\mathfrak{B}\to\mathcal{X}
is called a
derivation
if for each
a,b\in\mathfrak{B}
, one has

D(ab)=D(a)b+aD(b).

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be a Banach algebra and let
D:\mathfrak{B}\to\mathcal{X}
be a derivation. Then

\mathscr{I}(D)=\{b\in\mathfrak{B}\mid\text{the maps }a\mapsto D(ba)\text{ and }a\mapsto D(ab)\text{ are continuous }\forall a\in\mathfrak{B}\}

is the
continuity ideal
of
D
.

The following lemma is an immediate application of well-known results by Barnes that can be found in
[
6
, Theorem 2.1, Theorem 2.2]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{B}
be a locally regular inclusion and let
J
be a closed two-sided ideal of
{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})
. Then

{\rm Spec}_{\mathfrak{A}/(J\cap\mathfrak{A})}(a+J\cap\mathfrak{A})={\rm Spec}_{{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})/J}(a+J),

for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\rm sa}
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{B}
be a locally regular inclusion and let
D:\mathfrak{B}\to\mathcal{X}
be a derivation into a Banach
\mathfrak{B}
-bimodule. Then the ideal
\overline{\mathscr{I}(D)\cap\mathfrak{A}}^{{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})}\cap\mathfrak{A}
is closed and has finite codimension in
\mathfrak{A}
.

Proof.

For simplicity, set
J=\overline{\mathscr{I}(D)\cap\mathfrak{A}}^{{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})}\subset{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})
and
K=J\cap\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{A}
. Note that
K
is a closed
∗
-ideal. Let
\pi:{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})\to{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})/J
and
\pi^{\prime}:\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{A}/K
be the quotient maps and
\iota:\mathfrak{A}\to{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})
the canonical inclusion. Then there exists a continuous
∗
-homomorphism
\varphi:\mathfrak{A}/K\to{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})/J
that makes the following diagram commute

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{A}/K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})/J.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

Since
\varphi
maps
\mathfrak{A}/K
injectively into the
C^{*}
-algebra
{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})/J
, it is necessary for
\mathfrak{A}/K
to be an
A^{*}
-algebra.

Now, for the sake of contradiction, we will assume that
\mathfrak{A}/K
is infinite-dimensional. That makes
\mathfrak{A}/K
an infinite-dimensional
A^{*}
-algebra and so, by
[
1
, Corollary 5.4.3]
, there exists
a\in\mathfrak{A}
such that the spectrum
\Sigma={\rm Spec}_{\mathfrak{A}/K}(\pi^{\prime}(a))
is infinite and we have
\Sigma={\rm Spec}_{{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})/J}(\pi(a))\subset\mathbb{R}
, due to Lemma
2.7
. Using the regularity of
\mathfrak{A}(a)
, we can find a sequence of functions
f_{n}\in C(\mathbb{R})
which are nonzero in
\Sigma
, have disjoint supports and such that the sequence
a_{n}=f_{n}(a)
belongs to
\mathfrak{A}
and satisfies

a_{n}a_{m}=f_{n}(a)f_{m}(a)=(f_{n}\cdot f_{m})(a)=0,\quad\text{ for }n\not=m.

It also satisfies

a_{n}^{2}\not\in K,\quad\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N},

since

\pi^{\prime}(a_{n}^{2})=\pi^{\prime}(f_{n}^{2}(a))=\varphi^{-1}(f_{n}^{2}(\pi(a)))\not=0.

Replacing the
a_{n}
’s by appropriate scalar multiples, we can assume that
\lVert a_{n}\rVert_{\mathfrak{B}}\leq 1
. Note that
a_{n}^{2}\not\in\mathscr{I}(D)
, as the opposite situation would mean that
a_{n}^{2}\in\mathscr{I}(D)\cap\mathfrak{A}\subset K
. Thus, one can find
b_{n}\in\mathfrak{B}
such that

\lVert b_{n}\rVert_{\mathfrak{B}}\leq 2^{-n}\quad\text{ and }\quad\lVert D(a^{2}_{n}b_{b})\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}\geq M\lVert D(a_{n})\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}+n,

where
M
satisfies
\lVert b\xi\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}\leq M\lVert b\rVert_{\mathfrak{B}}\lVert\xi\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}
. Now consider the element
c=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a_{n}b_{n}\in\mathfrak{B}
. It satisfies
\lVert c\rVert_{\mathfrak{B}}\leq 1
and
a_{n}c=a_{n}^{2}b_{n}
, for all the
n
such that
b_{n}
exists. Hence

\displaystyle M\lVert D(c)\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}\geq\lVert a_{n}D(c)\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}=\lVert D(a_{n}c)-D(a_{n})c\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}\geq\lVert D(a_{n}^{2}b_{n})\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}-M\lVert D(a_{n})\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}\geq n,

which is absurd. Hence, the quotient
\mathfrak{A}/K
has to be finite-dimensional.
∎

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{B}
be a locally regular inclusion and let
D:\mathfrak{B}\to\mathcal{X}
be a derivation into a Banach
\mathfrak{B}
-bimodule. Further suppose that
\mathfrak{A}
is unital and that
{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})
has no proper closed ideals of finite codimension. Then
D
is continuous.

Proof.

An application of Lemma
2.8
tells us that the ideal
\overline{\mathscr{I}(D)\cap\mathfrak{A}}^{{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})}\cap\mathfrak{A}
has finite codimension in
\mathfrak{A}
. From this, it is easy to observe that
\overline{\mathscr{I}(D)\cap\mathfrak{A}}^{{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})}
has finite codimension in
{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})
and, because of the assumptions, it follows that
1\in\overline{\mathscr{I}(D)\cap\mathfrak{A}}^{{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})}
. Appealing to basic spectral theory, we note that
\mathscr{I}(D)\cap\mathfrak{A}
must contain an invertible element
a
, whose inverse
a^{-1}
necessarily lies in
\mathfrak{A}
(Lemma
2.7
). Therefore
1\in\mathscr{I}(D)
and the map

\mathfrak{B}\ni b\mapsto D(b1)=D(b)\in\mathcal{X}

is continuous.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

The above-proved theorem should be compared with
[
15
, Theorem 3.6]
. Whereas
[
15
, Theorem 3.6]
applies to a bigger class of maps (and not just derivations), it possesses more assumptions, making it more restrictive in the setting that concerns us here. The most obvious difference is that
\mathfrak{B}
is required to have an involution. Hence, when restricted to the setting of derivations into Banach bimodules, the theorem presented here is far more general.

The following lemma is an immediate application of a classic result of Barnes on the uniqueness of
C^{*}
-norms. See, for example,
[
5
, Theorem 4.2]
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{B}
be a locally regular inclusion. Let
\{0\}\not=I\subset{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})
be a closed two-sided ideal. Then
I\cap\mathfrak{A}\not=\{0\}
.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{C}
be Banach algebras such that
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{C}\subset{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})
and such that
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{B}
is a locally regular inclusion. Then every derivation
D:\mathfrak{B}\to\mathfrak{C}
is continuous.

Proof.

Again, for the sake of simplicity, set
J=\overline{\mathscr{I}(D)\cap\mathfrak{A}}^{{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})}
and
K=J\cap\mathfrak{A}
. Because of Lemma
2.8
, we have that
K
has finite codimension in
\mathfrak{A}
and
J
has finite codimension in
{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})
. Now consider the set

S=\{c\in\mathfrak{C}\mid ct=0,\,\forall t\in\mathscr{I}(D)\}.

If we show that
S=\{0\}
, then the theorem is proven and the reason is the following. Suppose that
S=\{0\}
and, by means of the closed graph theorem, let us show that
D
is continuous. In order to achieve that purpose, take a sequence
a_{n}\in\mathfrak{B}
and an element
c\in\mathfrak{C}
such that

\lVert a_{n}\rVert_{\mathfrak{B}}\to 0\quad\text{ and }\quad\lVert D(a_{n})-c\rVert_{\mathfrak{C}}\to 0.

Observe that
D(a_{n}t)\to 0
for all
t\in\mathscr{I}(D)
. On the other hand, one sees that

D(a_{n}t)=a_{n}D(t)+D(a_{n})t\to ct.

Hence
ct=0
, for all
t\in\mathscr{I}(D)
, so
c\in S=\{0\}
, which proves the continuity of
D
.

Now, it remains to show that
S=\{0\}
. In order to do so, first note that
S
is a two-sided ideal of
\mathfrak{C}
. Note also that
I=\overline{S}^{{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})}
and
J
are two-sided ideals of
{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{A})
and hence
C^{*}
-algebras; in particular, they contain bounded approximate identities. It follows that
IJ=I\cap J
(one can appeal to
[
8
, Theorem 11.10]
). But then, one can easily check that
IJ=\{0\}
and hence

(I\cap\mathfrak{A})\cap K\subset I\cap J=\{0\}.

Since
K
has finite codimension, this forces
I\cap\mathfrak{A}
to be finite-dimensional. But
I\cap\mathfrak{A}
is a closed
∗
-ideal of
\mathfrak{A}
and therefore a finite-dimensional
A^{*}
-algebra.

In order to achieve a contradiction, suppose now that
I\cap\mathfrak{A}\not=\{0\}
. Then, up to renorming,
I\cap\mathfrak{A}
must be a nontrivial finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra and so it must contain a unit
p\in I\cap\mathfrak{A}
. It is easy to see that
p
is a central element in
\mathfrak{A}
and it satisfies
I\cap\mathfrak{A}=\mathfrak{A}p
. Using this, we note that the map

\mathfrak{A}\ni a\mapsto ap\in I\cap\mathfrak{A}

has finite-dimensional range and factorizes the map

\mathfrak{B}\ni a\mapsto D(ap)\in\mathfrak{C},

making the latter continuous, so
p\in\mathscr{I}(D)\cap\mathfrak{A}\subset K
. It follows that
p\in(I\cap\mathfrak{A})\cap K=\{0\}
, a contradiction. Hence
I\cap\mathfrak{A}=\{0\}

Finally, Lemma
2.11
allows us to conclude that
S\subset I=\{0\}
from the fact that
I\cap\mathfrak{A}=\{0\}
, so the theorem is proven. ∎

3
Applications to
L^{p}
-crossed products and symmetrized
L^{p}
-crossed products

As mentioned before, the purpose of this section is to apply the previous results to
L^{p}
-crossed products and symmetrized
L^{p}
-crossed products. For that purpose, we will fix a
C^{*}
-dynamical system
(G,C_{0}(X),\alpha)
, where
G
is an amenable locally compact group,
X
a locally compact Hausdorff space,
\alpha
a continuous action of
G
on
X
. In what follows, we consider that
G
is always endowed with a Haar measure
{\rm d}x
.

We shall abuse the notation and call
\alpha
both the action on
X
and the induced action on
C_{0}(X)
. That is

\alpha_{x}(f)(z)=f\big(\alpha_{x^{-1}}(z)),

where
x\in G,z\in X,f\in C_{0}(X)
.

Then the generalized convolution algebra
L^{1}_{\alpha}(G,C_{0}(X))
is given by all Bochner integrable functions
\Phi:{\sf G}\to\mathfrak{A}
, endowed with the product

\Phi*\Psi(x)=\int_{G}\Phi(y)\alpha_{y}[\Psi(y^{-1}x)]{\rm d}y

and the involution

\Phi^{*}(x)=\Delta(x^{-1})\alpha_{x}[\Phi(x^{-1})^{*}],

making
L^{1}_{\alpha}(G,C_{0}(X))
a Banach
∗
-algebra under the norm
\lVert\Phi\rVert_{1}=\int_{G}\lVert\Phi(x)\rVert_{\mathfrak{A}}{\rm d}x
.

It is known that the algebra
L^{1}_{\alpha}(G,C_{0}(X))
admits
C^{*}
-norms, so it is an
A^{*}
-algebra. Its enveloping
C^{*}
-algebra is denoted
C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{\alpha}G
and we call it the
crossed product
of
C_{0}(X)
by
G
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty],
and let
(G,C_{0}(X),\alpha)
be a
C^{*}
-dynamical system as above. Let
(Y,\mathcal{B},\mu)
be a measure space. A
covariant representation
of
(G,C_{0}(X),\alpha)
on
L^{p}(Y,\mu)
is a pair
(v,\pi)
consisting of a map
g\mapsto v_{g}
from
G
to the (invertible) isometries on
L^{p}(Y,\mu)
that is continuous in the sense that
g\mapsto v_{g}\xi
is continuous for all
\xi\in L^{p}(Y,\mu)
, and a non-degenerate, contractive representation
\pi:C_{0}(X)\to\mathbb{B}(L^{p}(Y,\mu)),
such that

v_{x}v_{y}=v_{xy},\qquad v_{x}\pi(f)v_{x}^{-1}=\pi(\alpha_{x}(f)),

for all
x,y\in G
and
f\in C_{0}(X)
.

Given a covariant representation
(v,\pi)
on
L^{p}(Y,\mu)
, we define the associated
integrated
representation

\pi\rtimes v:L^{1}_{\alpha}(G,C_{0}(X))\to\mathbb{B}(L^{p}(Y,\mu))
by

(\pi\rtimes v)(\Phi)(\xi)=\int_{G}\pi(\Phi(x))(v_{x}(\xi)){\rm d}x,

for all
\Phi\in L^{1}_{\alpha}(G,C_{0}(X))
and all
\xi\in L^{p}(Y,\mu)
.

It is well known that every representation of
C_{0}(X)
induces a covariant representation of
(G,C_{0}(X),\alpha)
. We proceed to describe this process. Indeed, let
\pi_{0}:C_{0}(X)\to\mathbb{B}(L^{p}(Y,\mu))
be a contractive representation and let
\nu
denote the Haar measure on
G
. Then the following formulas define representations
v
of
G
and
\pi
of
C_{0}(X)
on
L^{p}(G,\,L^{p}(Y,\mu))\cong L^{p}(G\times Y,\nu\times\mu)
:

v_{y}(\xi)(x)=\xi(y^{-1}x),\quad\text{ and }\quad\big(\pi(f)\xi\big)(x)=\pi_{0}(\alpha_{x}^{-1}(f))\big(\xi(x)\big),

for all
x,y\in G
,
\xi\in C_{\rm c}(G,\,L^{p}(Y,\mu))
, and
f\in C_{0}(X)
.

It is easy to see that the pair
(v,\pi)
constructed above is a covariant representation. It is usually called the
regular covariant representation
associated with
\pi_{0}

[
23
, Definition 2.12]
. If
\pi_{0}
is the point-evaluation representation

\pi_{0}:C_{0}(X)\to\mathbb{C},\quad\text{ given by }\pi_{0}(f)=f(z),

then we use the notation
(v,\pi_{z})
for the associated regular covariant representation. In that case, the integrated representation acquires the following form:

(\pi_{z}\rtimes v)(\Phi)\xi(x)=\int_{G}\Phi(y)(xz)\xi(y^{-1}x){\rm d}y,

for all
x\in G,\Phi\in L^{1}_{\alpha}(G,C_{0}(X))
and all
\xi\in L^{p}(G)
.

With these definitions at hand, we can introduce the following norms on
L^{1}_{\alpha}(G,C_{0}(X))
.

\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{p,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\|(\pi\rtimes v)(\Phi)\|\mid(v,\pi)\text{ is a regular covariant pair}\},

\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{p,r}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\|(\pi_{z}\rtimes v)(\Phi)\|\mid(v,\pi_{z})\text{ is a regular covariant pair and }z\in X\}.

The completion
F^{p}(G,X,\alpha):=\overline{L^{1}_{\alpha}(G,C_{0}(X))}^{\|\cdot\|_{p,\lambda}}
is usually called the reduced
L^{p}
-crossed product associated with
(G,C_{0}(X),\alpha)
. It is easily seen to be a Banach algebra and it coincides with
L^{1}_{\alpha}(G,C_{0}(X))
when
p=1
or
p=\infty
, and with
C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{\alpha}G
when
p=2
. It is commonly understood as a non-involutive analog of the
C^{*}
-crossed product.

We also mention that there is a ‘full’ crossed product construction, which is obtained using all representations into
L^{p}
-spaces and not just the regular ones, but in our setting (the groups are amenable), these two constructions are known to coincide
[
4
, Theorem 3.5]
, so we avoid introducing it.

On the other hand, the norms
\|\cdot\|_{p,r}
can and have been used to introduce symmetrized versions of the
L^{p}
-crossed product (cf.
[
2
]
). These are involutive versions of the
L^{p}
-crossed product with desirable properties, so we introduce them now. Indeed, we let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
be Hölder duals and define
F^{q}_{*}(G,X,\alpha)
to be the completion of
L^{1}_{\alpha}(G,C_{0}(X))
under the norm

\lVert\Phi\rVert_{q,*}=\max\{\|\Phi\|_{p,r},\|\Phi\|_{q,r}\}.

The point is that
F^{q}_{*}(G,X,\alpha)
is naturally a Banach
∗
-algebra that sits between
L^{1}_{\alpha}(G,C_{0}(X))
and
C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{\alpha}G
, so it fits the setting of Theorem
1.2
perfectly. Indeed, the fact that this algebra has a continuous involution comes from the fact that, for all
\Phi\in L^{1}_{\alpha}(G,C_{0}(X)),\xi\in L^{p}(G),\eta\in L^{q}(G)
, one has

\langle(\pi_{z}\rtimes v)(\Phi)\xi,\eta\rangle=\langle\xi,(\pi_{z}\rtimes v)(\Phi^{*})\eta\rangle,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the duality pairing between
L^{p}(G)
and
L^{q}(G)
. On the other hand, the inclusions
L^{1}_{\alpha}(G,C_{0}(X))\subset F^{q}_{*}(G,X,\alpha)\subset C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{\alpha}G
follow from complex interpolation.

We remit the reader to
[
2
, Section 3]
or
[
4
, Sections 2,3]
for a careful discussion of the algebra
F^{q}_{*}(G,X,\alpha)
and of these properties that we just mentioned.

In any case, let us describe our source of locally regular inclusions that involve
L^{p}
-crossed products.

Definition 3.2
.

A weight on the locally compact group
G
is a measurable, locally bounded function
w:G\to[1,\infty)
satisfying

w(xy)\leq w(x)w(y)\,,\quad w(x^{-1})=w(x)\,,\quad\forall\,x,y\in G.

During the rest of the section,
\mathfrak{E}_{w}
will denote the subalgebra of
L^{1}_{\alpha}(G,C_{0}(X))
consisting of all the functions
\Phi
such that
\lVert\Phi\rVert_{w}<\infty
, where the norm in question is given by

\lVert\Phi\rVert_{w}=\max\Big\{{\rm essup}_{x\in G}\lVert\Phi(x)\rVert_{C_{0}(X)},\int_{G}\lVert\Phi(x)\rVert_{C_{0}(X)}w(x){\rm d}x\Big\}.

It is not hard to see that
\mathfrak{E}_{w}
is a dense Banach
∗
-subalgebra of
L^{1}_{\alpha}(G,C_{0}(X))
. Furthermore, the following lemma was proven in
[
15
, Proposition 4.10]
, using some results from
[
16
]
. Note that the algebra
\mathfrak{E}_{w}
was first introduced in
[
16
]
.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
15
]
)
.

Let
G
be either a countable locally finite group or a compactly generated locally compact group with polynomial growth. Then there exists a weight function
w:G\to[1,\infty)
such that
\mathfrak{E}_{w}(\Phi)
is a regular Banach function algebra, for every
\Phi=\Phi^{*}\in\mathfrak{E}_{w}
.

The above lemma implies that, for any Banach algebra completion
\mathfrak{B}
of
\mathfrak{E}_{w}
, the inclusion
\mathfrak{E}_{w}\subset\mathfrak{B}
is locally regular, so our theorems are applicable to
\mathfrak{B}
. In particular, the
L^{p}
-crossed products we just described are Banach algebra completions of
L^{1}_{\alpha}(G,C_{0}(X))
, hence completions of
\mathfrak{E}_{w}
. The following corollary is then just a simple application of Theorem
2.12
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
p,q\in[1,2]
and let
G
be either a countable locally finite group or a compactly generated locally compact group with polynomial growth. Let
\alpha
be a continuous action of
G
on the locally compact space
X
. Then every derivation
D:F^{p}_{*}(G,X,\alpha)\to F^{q}_{*}(G,X,\alpha)
is continuous.

In order to also apply Theorem
2.9
, we also need to guarantee that
{\rm C^{*}}(\mathfrak{E}_{w})=C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{\alpha}G
lacks proper closed two-sided ideals of finite codimension. But this is known to be the case when
G
is infinite and the restriction of
\alpha
to any invariant subset is topologically free (see
[
13
, Theorem 29.9]
).

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and let
G
be a countable infinite group, which is either locally finite or finitely generated and of polynomial growth. Let
\alpha
be a continuous action of
G
on the compact space
X
such that the restriction of
\alpha
to any invariant subset is topologically free. Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach
F^{p}(G,X,\alpha)
-bimodule. Then every derivation
D:F^{p}(G,X,\alpha)\to\mathcal{X}
is continuous.
